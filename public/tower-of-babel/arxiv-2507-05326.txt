Residues and Gorenstein Contractions of Genus One Curves
Adrian Neff

Abstract
. Let C C be a genus one nodal curve over a local artinian base and let E E be a proper subcurve of genus one. We define residues for curves over local artinian rings, then define generalized residues with values in line bundles over the local artinian ring that arise from tropical data on the curve. We then use these residues to construct a contraction of C C that collapses E E to a Gorenstein genus one singularity.



1.
Introduction

Given a genus one nodal curve
C
and a proper subcurve of genus one,
E
, we wish to construct a contraction of
C
collapsing
E
to a Gorenstein genus one singularity. Our construction begins with a family of centrally aligned genus one log curves
\pi:C\to S=\mathrm{Spec}(A)
, where
A
is a local artinian ring. One may think of the central alignment as a semistable modification
\tilde{C}\to C
that “orders the components” inside the subcurve
E
. Using this data, we produce a residue for singular curves valued in
A
, then use this to construct the contraction
C\to\widebar{C}
.

Specifically, let
C\to\mathrm{Spec}(A)
be a family of curves over a local artinian ring
A
and let
p\in C_{0}
be a closed point in the fiber. We first construct the residue of a meromorphic differential
\varphi
along a branch
X
at
p
, denoted by
\mathrm{Res}_{p,X}(\varphi)
, that satisfies the following two properties:

(i)

if
p
is a node with branches
X
and
Y
, then

\mathrm{Res}_{p,X}(\varphi)=-\mathrm{Res}_{p,Y}(\varphi);

(ii)

if
C
is smooth, then

\sum_{p\in C}\mathrm{Res}_{p,C}(\varphi)=0.

One may think of this as an algebraic analogue of integration over a vanishing cycle in the complex analytic setting.

After defining our notion of residue, we construct the contraction over
\mathrm{Spec}(A)
via generalized residues with values in line bundles over
\mathrm{Spec}(A)
coming from tropical data, denoted
\mathrm{Res}^{m}
, as follows. There are
n
rational curves
R_{1},...,R_{n}
connecting the subcurve
E
to the rest of
C
. Let
p_{i}
be the node connecting
R_{i}
to
E
. The condition for a function
f
in a neighborhood of
E
to descend to the contraction is

\sum_{p_{i}}\mathrm{Res}^{m}_{p_{i},R_{i}}(f\varphi)=0,

where
\varphi
is a specially chosen differential.

We then replace
S
with the spectrum of a local noetherian ring
R
with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
. Setting
S_{n}=\mathrm{Spec}(R/\mathfrak{m}^{n+1})
and
C_{n}=C\times_{S}S_{n}
, we have a diagram of cartesian squares
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C_{0}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C_{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S_{0}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S_{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}

and a contraction for each

,
\tau_{i}:C_{i}\to\bar{C}_{i}
. We then have the following characterization of the functions in
\mathcal{O}_{\widebar{C}}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
U\subset\bar{C}_{n}
be an open subset,
V=\tau_{n}^{-1}(U)
, and

. The following are equivalent:

(i)

is in the image of

for all

;

(ii)

there exist compatible lifts

of

for all

;

(iii)

is in
\mathcal{O}_{\bar{C}_{n}}(U)
;

satisfies the residue condition:
\sum_{p_{i}}\mathrm{Res}^{m}_{p_{i},R_{i}}(f_{n}\varphi_{n}|_{V})=0\in\mathcal{O}_{S}(-\delta)

Here the
\varphi_{n}
are specially chosen differentials on the

.

1.1.
Relation to Other Works and Future Directions

Contractions of genus one curves have been studied previously, for example, by Smyth in
[
17
]
, Ranganathan, Santos–Parker, and Wise in
[
16
]
, and Bozlee in
[
6
]
. Comparing approaches, the constructions in
[
17
]
and
[
16
]
both rely on putting the curve in a smoothing family and performing the contraction in that family, and therefore struggle to identify the contraction for a fixed curve. The construction in
[
6
]
remedies this by constructing the ring for the contraction by hand, but the viewpoint therein does not make clear the connections between the contraction and other related problems, such as smoothing of differentials.

Centrally aligned curves were introduced in
[
16
]
in order to give a modular desingularization of
\widebar{\mathcal{M}}_{1,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)
. By giving a more explicit construction of the contraction used in that paper, it makes clear why factorization through the contraction is related to smoothing stable maps, in terms of smoothing functions pulled back from projective space. We should also be able to use our viewpoint on the contraction to give a novel proof of the splitting of the virtual class of
\widebar{\mathcal{M}}_{1,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)
over the main and boundary components.

The technique for the contraction constructed within should also generalize to higher genera by considering residues of functions paired with multiple independent differentials on a curve. Such contractions are vital to constructing modular desingularizations of the moduli spaces of stable maps to projective space, see, e.g., the aforementioned construction in
[
16
]
and the similar construction of Battistella and Carocci in
[
4
]
. Extending this approach even to just genus two presents interesting challenges and questions, such as the presence of non-reduced structures on the contraction seen in
[
4
]
, coming from degenerating curves with a double cover of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
.

Lastly, by making the connection between the contraction and residues of differentials explicit, we hope to be able to give a new proof of the differential smoothing conjecture (valid in any characteristic) posed by Ranganathan and Wise. This conjecture was stated by Battistella and Bozlee in
[
3
]
and proven in characteristic 0 by Chen and Chen in
[
8
]
. In the process, we should also be able to give an algebraic proof of the global residue condition for smoothing of differentials introduced in
[
5
]
.

2.
Background

2.1.
Genus One Singularities

We work over an algebraically closed field
k
throughout.

Let
C
be a reduced curve over
k
and let
\nu:\tilde{C}\to C
be the normalization at a closed point
p\in C
. Let
\{p_{1},...,p_{n}\}
be the preimages of
p
under the map
\nu
. We define two quantities:

(i)

the
number of branches of the singularity at
p
is
m(p)=n
;

(ii)

the
\delta
-invariant
is
\delta(p)=\text{dim}_{k}(\nu_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\tilde{C}}/\mathcal{O}_{C})
.

We then define the
genus of the singularity at
p
to be

g(p)=\delta(p)-m(p)+1.

We will be concerned with Gorenstein genus one singularities, which have been characterized by Smyth (
[
17
]
Proposition A.3) as follows.

Proposition 2.1
.

For each
n\geq 1
, there is a unique Gorenstein genus one singularity with
n
branches. Specifically, these singularities are the following:

(i)

n=1
: the cusp
V(y^{2}-x^{3})
;

(ii)

n=2
: the tacnode
V(y^{2}-yx^{2})
;

(iii)

n\geq 3
: the union of
n
general lines through the origin in
\mathbb{A}^{n-1}
.

2.2.
Tropical Curves

We refer the reader to
[
9
]
for the details of the following approach to tropical curves.

Let
M
be a finitely generated, integral, saturated, sharp monoid. We define a tropical curve with edge lengths in
M
.

Definition 2.2
.

An
n
-marked tropical curve
\Gamma
with edge lengths in
M
is a finite graph with vertex set
V
, edge set
E
, and half-edge set
L
along with:

(i)

a bijection
\{1,...,n\}\to L
;

(ii)

a genus function
g:V\to\mathbb{N}
;

(iii)

a length function
l:E\to M
.

The genus of
\Gamma
is the sum of the genera of each vertex plus the first Betti number of the underlying graph.

We will need the notion of a piecewise linear function on a tropical curve.

Definition 2.3
.

A
piecewise linear function

f
on a tropical curve
\Gamma
is the assignment of a value
f(v)\in M
for each
v\in V
and a slope
m(h)\in\mathbb{N}
for each
h\in L
such that if
e\in E
is an edge between vertices
v,w\in V
, then
f(v)-f(w)
is an integer multiple of
l(e)
.

2.3.
Log Curves

We refer the reader to
[
13
]
for background on logarithmic geometry.

Definition 2.4
.

A
log curve
over an fs log scheme
S
is a log smooth, integral, and proper map
\pi:C\to S
of fs log schemes such that each geometric fiber is a reduced and connected curve.

A more useful characterization is the following, due to F. Kato (
[
14
]
1.8). This formulation of the statement is borrowed from
[
16
]
(Theorem 2.3.1).

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
C\to S
be a log curve and
p\in C
a geometric point with image
s\in S
. Then there are étale neighborhoods
V
of
p
and
U
of
s
so that
V\to U
has a strict ètale map to a model
V^{\prime}\to U
with one of the following forms:

(i)

(smooth germ)
V^{\prime}=\mathbb{A}^{1}_{U}\to U
, and the log structure on
V^{\prime}
is pulled back from the base;

(ii)

(germ of a marked point)
V^{\prime}=\mathbb{A}^{1}_{U}\to U
, and the log structure on
V^{\prime}
is pulled back from the toric log structure on
\mathbb{A}^{1}
;

(iii)

(node)
V^{\prime}=\mathrm{Spec}\,\mathcal{O}_{U}[x,y]/(xy-t)
for some
t\in\mathcal{O}_{U}
, and the log structure on
V^{\prime}
is pulled back from the multiplication map
\mathbb{A}^{2}\to\mathbb{A}^{1}
of toric varieties along the map
U\to\mathbb{A}^{1}
of log schemes induced by
t
.

We call the image of
t\in M_{S}
in
\widebar{M}_{S}
a
smoothing parameter of the node
.

Using this characterization, we can define the tropical curve
\Gamma
associated to a log curve
C\to S
by defining the underlying graph of
\Gamma
to be the dual graph of
C
, and the length of an edge
e\in E
is the smoothing parameter
t\in\widebar{M}_{S}
associated to the node, i.e.,
l(e)=t
.

We will also need the notion of the line bundle associated to a piecewise linear function, which we now review. Let
f
be a piecewise linear function on the tropicalization of
C\to S
. It is shown in
[
9
]
(Remark 7.3) that one can view this as a section of
\widebar{M}_{C}
. Then from the short exact sequence

0\to\mathcal{O}_{C}^{*}\to M_{C}^{\text{gp}}\to\widebar{M}_{C}^{\text{gp}}\to 0

we get a map
H^{0}(C,\widebar{M}_{C}^{\text{gp}})\to H^{1}(C,\mathcal{O}_{C}^{*})
. This is the association of a line bundle
\mathcal{O}(-f)
to a piecewise linear function
f
, where
f
is viewed as a section of
\widebar{M}_{C}^{\text{gp}}
. The following proposition from
[
16
]
(Proposition 2.4.1) will be used throughout.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
\pi:C\to S
be a log curve such that
\widebar{M}_{S}
and
\Gamma
are constant over
S
. If
f
is a piecewise linear function on
\Gamma
and
v
is a vertex corresponding to an irreducible component
C_{v}
of
C
, then we have

\mathcal{O}_{C}(f)|_{C_{v}}=\mathcal{O}_{C_{v}}\left(\sum s(f,e_{q})q\right)\otimes\pi^{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(f(v)),

where
s(f,e_{q})
is the outgoing slope of

along the edge corresponding to

.

2.4.
Centrally Aligned Curves

We can now define the notion of a central alignment, as introduced in
[
16
]
(Definition 4.6.2.1). Let
\pi:C\to S
be a genus one log curve such that
S=\mathrm{Spec}(k)
and let
\Gamma
be the tropicalization of
C
.

Definition 2.7
.

The
core
of
C
is the minimal genus one subcurve with respect to inclusion.

We can now define a piecewise linear function
\lambda
as follows. For any vertex
v
, there is a unique path from the core to
v
, say
e_{1},e_{2},...,e_{k}
. We then set

\lambda(v)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}l(e_{i})\in\widebar{M}_{S}.

It is shown in
[
16
]
(in the paragraphs following Lemma 3.3.2) that this can be extended to have
S
be an arbitrary base.

Now we can put a partial order on
\widebar{M}_{S}^{gp}
by saying
f\leq g
if
g-f\in\widebar{M}_{S}
. With this we can make the following definition:

Definition 2.8
.

A genus one log curve
\pi:C\to S
is
radially aligned
if for every geometric point
s\in S
and every pair of vertices
v,w
in
\Gamma_{s}
,
\lambda(v)
and
\lambda(w)
are comparable under the partial order defined above.

We can now define the related notion of a (stable) central alignment.

Definition 2.9
.

Let
\pi:C\to S
be a genus one log curve with tropicalization
\Gamma
. A
(stable) central alignment
of
C
is the existence of
v
in
\Gamma
such that, setting
\lambda(v)=\delta
, the following two conditions hold:

(i)

the interior of the circle of radius
\delta
around the core is radially aligned;

(ii)

the subgraph of
\Gamma
where
\lambda<\delta
is a stable curve.

A curve with a central alignment is a
centrally aligned curve
.

Given a central alignment, there is a semistable modification
\tilde{C}\to C
obtained by the following procedure. For every vertex
v
and edge
e
between vertices
w
and
u
contained in the aligned circle, if
\lambda(w)<\lambda(v)<\lambda(u)
, then we subdivide the edge
e
to introduce a new vertex a distance of
\lambda(v)
away from the core. Throughout the remainder of this paper, given a centrally aligned curve
C
, we will implicitly work on
\tilde{C}
.

Example 2.10
.

Suppose we have two edges
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
connecting vertices
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
to the core, with
\lambda(v_{1})<\lambda(v_{2})
. We then subdivide the edge
e_{2}
at a distance of
\lambda(v_{1})
from the core. See Figure
1
.

Figure 1.

Let

be a local artinian ring with maximal ideal

and residue field

\pi:C\to S=\mathrm{Spec}(A)

\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{C,p}\to\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{C_{0},p}

Proposition 3.1
.

If

is a minimal prime ideal of
\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{C_{0},p}
, then there is an isomorphism
(\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{C_{0},p})_{q_{0}}\simeq k(\!(x)\!)
.

Proof.

Denote
\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{C_{0},p}
and
\mathcal{O}_{C_{0},p}
by
\hat{R}
and
R
, respectively, throughout.

Let
\{q_{1},...,q_{n}\}
be the minimal prime ideals of
\hat{R}
. If we denote by
R^{\prime}
the integral closure of
R
in
Q(R)
(its total ring of fractions), then the number of maximal ideals of

is
n
(
[
18
]
Tags 0C37 and 0C2E). Denote these by
\{m_{1},...,m_{n}\}
. Let
\hat{R^{\prime}}
be the completion of
R^{\prime}
at the ideal
I=m_{1}\cdots m_{n}
, which is equivalent to completing
R^{\prime}
at the maximal ideal
m
of

. Since

is semi-local, we have an isomorphism (
[
15
]

\hat{R^{\prime}}\simeq\hat{R^{\prime}}_{m_{1}}\times\cdots\times\hat{R^{\prime}}_{m_{n}},

where
\hat{R^{\prime}}_{m_{i}}
is the completion of the local ring

at its maximal ideal.

Now since
R^{\prime}
is finite over

, we also have an isomorphism

\hat{R^{\prime}}\simeq\hat{R}\otimes_{R}R^{\prime}.

R
is a localization of a finitely generated algebra over a field, so it is an excellent ring. In particular, it is a G-ring, so it has normal formal fibers, and from (
[
11
]

\hat{R}\otimes_{R}R^{\prime}
is the integral closure of
\hat{R}
in

. Thus, we can write

\hat{R^{\prime}}\simeq\hat{R}_{1}\times\cdots\times\hat{R}_{n},

where
\hat{R}_{i}

(
[
18
]

\hat{R}_{i}\simeq\hat{R^{\prime}}_{m_{i}}
. In particular,
\hat{R}_{i}
is a regular, complete, local noetherian ring of dimension one over

.
∎

Definition 3.4
.

The
residue of
\varphi
at
p
along the branch
X
is the image of
(\varphi_{p})|_{X}
under the map
A(\!(x)\!)\,dx\to A
. We denote this by
\mathrm{Res}_{p,X}(\varphi)
.

This induces a map
\pi_{*}\omega_{C}\to\mathcal{O}_{S}
, which we call the
residue map
.

We must now show that this is well-defined, i.e., that the residue of an element of
A(\!(x)\!)\,dx
is invariant under any continuous automorphism of
A(\!(x)\!)
. This follows from
[
10
]
Corollary 5.4. This statement is more powerful than needed, as it deals with Laurent series in
n
variables. In order to keep this self-contained, we provide a proof in Appendix
A
of the case
n=1
that we are considering.

3.1.
Facts about Residues

Remark 3.5
.

We note that, by definition, if we have an expansion

(1)

\displaystyle(\varphi_{p})|_{X}=\sum a_{i}x^{i}\,dx\in A(\!(x)\!)\,dx,

then the residue is just the term
a_{-1}
in (
1
). We will use this fact heavily throughout.

Remark 3.6
.

When
p
is a smooth point, there is only a single branch and we recover the usual definition of residue on a smooth curve over a local artinian ring (see, e.g., Chapter VII of
[
12
]
).

As mentioned in the Introduction, one may think of this residue over a local artinian ring as an algebraic analogue of integration around a vanishing cycle in the complex analytic setting. The following lemma lends credence to that idea.

Lemma 3.7
.

If
p\in C
is a node with branches
X
and
Y
, then

\mathrm{Res}_{p,X}(\varphi)=-\mathrm{Res}_{p,Y}(\varphi).

Proof.

Assume we have local coordinates
x
and
y
for
X
and
Y
, respectively, such that
xy=t
for some
t\in\mathfrak{m}
. We first write out

\varphi_{p}|_{X}=\left(\sum_{j=N}^{\infty}a_{j}x^{j}\right)dx,

so
\mathrm{Res}_{p,X}(\varphi)=a_{-1}
. We now split into the cases
t=0
and
t\neq 0
.

First assume
xy=t
, where
t\neq 0
. When we restrict to the complement of
X
,
y
will be invertible and we can write
x=ty^{-1}
, and
dx=-t\frac{dy}{y^{2}}
. The
-1
term for the residue in this expansion on
Y
will come from
a_{-1}x^{-1}=a_{-1}(ty^{-1})^{-1}
, due to the
1/y^{2}
in the
dx
conversion. Multiplying
-t\frac{dy}{y^{2}}
by this term shows

\mathrm{Res}_{p,Y}(\varphi)=-a_{-1}=-\mathrm{Res}_{p,X}(\varphi).

Now assume
t=0
. Over
\mathrm{Spec}(k)
, we have that

\omega_{C/k}\subset\omega_{X\setminus p/k}\times\omega_{Y\setminus p/k}\simeq\Omega_{X\setminus p/k}\times\Omega_{Y\setminus p/k}

and we can identify this with the multiples of
\left(\frac{dx}{x},-\frac{dy}{y}\right)
(see, e.g., Rosenlicht duality in Altman–Kleinman
[
1
]
Prop. VIII.1.16). Over an artinian base
S
we still have

\omega_{C/S}\subset\omega_{X\setminus p/S}\times\omega_{Y\setminus p/S}\simeq\Omega_{X\setminus p/S}\times\Omega_{Y\setminus p/S}

and it will still be generated by one element. Using Nakayama’s Lemma, one shows that
\left(\frac{dx}{x},-\frac{dy}{y}\right)
still generates, since it generates over
k
. Thus, since every element of
\omega_{C/S}
is a multiple of
\left(\frac{dx}{x},-\frac{dy}{y}\right)
and no element of
\mathcal{O}_{C}
will have different constant value on
X
and
Y
(as they agree at the node), we see that the residues of
\varphi
along
X
and
Y
still differ by a sign.
∎

\displaystyle\tilde{\varphi}_{p_{i}}|_{R_{i}}

\displaystyle=[u^{2}][(\gamma_{-2}^{i}+\alpha^{i}u+\beta^{i}u^{2})x_{i}^{-2}+\cdots]\,dx_{i}

for some

with the nodes removed. Lifting to the rational components is always possible, so the only condition can come from lifting to

Computing and summing the residues of

where

and

are sums containing the other coefficients. Now since

Thus for

which is equivalent to

\gamma_{-2}^{1}c_{1}^{1}+\gamma_{-2}^{2}c_{1}^{2}=0,

since if

to descend to
\widebar{C_{0}}
.

Theorem 4.10
.

Let

be a local noetherian ring complete with respect to its maximal ideal

,

, and

. Let

be a family of centrally aligned genus one curves and set

. Assume

, and

f_{n}

\mathcal{O}_{C_{m}}(V)\to\mathcal{O}_{C_{n}}(V)
for all

;

(ii)

there exist compatible lifts

Proof.

We clearly have (ii) implies (i), and (iii) and (iv) are equivalent by definition of

. We will first show (i) implies (iv), then finish by showing (iv) implies (ii).

We begin with (i) implies (iv). If
\tau_{n}(E)\not\in U
, then
\mathcal{O}_{\widebar{C_{n}}}(U)=\mathcal{O}_{C_{n}}(V)
, so (i) clearly implies (iv) in this case.

Now assume
\tau_{n}(E)\in U
. Let

\sum_{p_{i}}\mathrm{Res}^{m}_{p_{i},R_{i}}(f_{n}\varphi_{n}|_{V})=[t]a

for some

.
Let

be the smallest integer such that

, but
ta\not\in\mathfrak{m}^{N+1}
. Note that we could have

. From Lemma

, we know that

\sum_{p_{i}}\mathrm{Res}^{m}_{p_{i},R_{i}}(f_{N}\varphi_{N}|_{V})=[t]\tilde{a}+[t]G,

where

and

is a lift of

to

. Then we have

since

. Now applying Lemma

with

as in said lemma, we have

\sum_{q_{i}}\mathrm{Res}_{q_{i},L_{0}}(f_{N}\varphi_{N}|_{V})=-t\tilde{a},

and since this is the total residue of

on

, we must have

, from which we deduce that
\tilde{a}=0
, since if

, then
\tilde{a}\not\in\text{Ann}(t)
. Restricting back to

, we conclude that

, so

, as desired.

We now address (iv) implies (ii). Let

. Since

is an infinitesimal deformation of

, we have a short exact sequence

By flatness of the maps

, we see that

. Now this sequence induces a long exact sequence in cohomology, and we see that the obstruction to lifting

to a function

is found in

. When

,

is a collection of rational curves, so

and we always have a lift. We can then repeat this on

to find a compatible lift to

, then repeat to get a lift to any

for

.

Now we examine the case when
\tau_{n}(E)\in U
. We will approach this by showing that if

satisfies (iv), then we can find a lift

that also satisfies (iv). We can then inductively lift

to

also satisfying (iv), and so forth to any order.

We first make a reduction to the problem. If we let

be a component of

with nodes

, then we claim that finding a lift of

to

is equivalent to finding compatible lifts of

and

. This follows from Zariski gluing, since viewing

as a function in a suitably small neighborhood

of

, we see that

and

form a cover of

. Now we can always find lifts to

, since

is affine, and

is surjective, so we can also find a lift to
(\mathcal{O}_{C_{n+1}})_{r_{i}}
. However, these lifts must be compatible, and the lift to

can potentially force the lift to

to have a zero or pole at

. Thus we see that finding a lift is equivalent to finding a rational function on

with prescribed zeroes or poles at the nodes. As we saw above, this can always be done when the component is rational. Furthermore, since

has genus one, the dual graph of

minus the vertices corresponding to the core forms a tree. Thus if we think of finding a lift as first lifting to layer

, then layer

, and so forth, we see that the only obstruction to lifting arises from lifting to

. This is the obstruction in

previously discussed.

Let us now compute this obstruction and show that we can always find a lift when

. In a similar fashion to the proof of Lemma

, we compute in local coordinates. At each

, with

the local coordinate for

, we write (with

a unit)

(f_{n})_{p_{i}}=c^{i}+c_{1}^{i}x_{i}+\cdots

from which we compute

\mathrm{Res}^{m}_{p_{i},R_{i}}(f_{n}\varphi_{n}|_{V})=[t]\gamma_{-2}^{i}c_{1}^{i}.

Again as in Lemma

, since

and
\varphi_{n+1}
are lifts of

and
\varphi_{n}
, respectively, we can write

(\varphi_{n+1})_{p_{i}}|_{R_{i}}=[t][(\gamma_{-2}^{i}+v_{-2}^{i})x_{i}^{-2}+(\gamma_{0}^{i}+v_{0}^{i})+\cdots]\,dx_{i}

(f_{n+1})_{p_{i}}=c^{i}+c_{1}^{i}x_{i}+\cdots+u^{i}(d^{i}+d_{1}^{i}x_{i}+\cdots)

where

. From this we compute

\displaystyle\mathrm{Res}^{m}_{p_{i},R_{i}}(f_{n+1}\varphi_{n+1}|_{V})

\displaystyle=[t](\gamma_{-2}^{i}c_{1}^{i}+u^{i}d_{1}^{i}\gamma_{-2}^{i}+v_{-2}^{i}c_{1}^{i})

\displaystyle=[t]\gamma_{-2}^{i}c_{1}^{i}+t(u^{i}d_{1}^{i}\gamma_{-2}^{i}+v_{-2}^{i}c_{1}^{i}).

Now using Lemma

, we can compute

\sum_{q_{i}}\mathrm{Res}_{q_{i},L_{0}}(f_{n+1}\varphi_{n+1}|_{V})=-t\sum_{p_{i}}\gamma_{-2}^{i}c_{1}^{i},

where the other terms in the residue vanish since

. We then have principal parts on

and want to know if these principal parts come from a rational function – this is the Mittag-Leffler problem. Thus to find our rational function on

we only need

, which is satisfied by assumption. Therefore, we can find a lift
f_{n+1}
. Furthermore, we can choose

to satisfy

for each

and setting

, so we can choose the lift

to satisfy (iv). Hence we can find compatible lifts of

to

for any

.
∎

Appendix A
Invariance of Residue (by Adrian Neff and Jonathan Wise)

In order to show that Definition
3.4
is invariant of the choice of local coordinate
x
, we will need to split into the cases of positive and zero characteristic. Before doing that, we begin with some generalities on continuous automorphisms of
A(\!(x)\!)
.

A.1.
Automorphisms of Laurent Series

We begin by classifying continuous automorphisms of
A(\!(x)\!)
.

Let
\varphi:A(\!(x)\!)\to A(\!(x)\!)
be a continuous automorphism. Since
x\in A(\!(x)\!)^{*}
, we must have
\varphi(x)\in A(\!(x)\!)^{*}
. Thus, we must classify the group of units
A(\!(x)\!)^{*}
. We introduce the following subset of
A(\!(x)\!)
:

A(\!(x)\!)^{nil}=\left\{\sum a_{i}x^{i}\,|\,a_{0}\in A^{*},a_{n}\text{ is nilpotent for }n<0\right\}.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
A
be a local artinian ring. Then
A(\!(x)\!)^{*}
is in bijection with
\mathbb{Z}\times A(\!(x)\!)^{nil}
.

Proof.

Indeed, since
\mathrm{Spec}(A)
is connected, a unit in
A(\!(x)\!)
is of the form
\sum a_{i}x^{i}
with
a_{N}\in A^{*}
for some
N
, and
a_{n}
nilpotent for
n<N
, which can be written as
x^{N}(\sum a_{i}x^{i-N})
with
\sum a_{i}x^{i-N}\in A(\!(x)\!)^{nil}
(see, e.g., Lemme 0.7 of
[
7
]
). We then define the bijection by

x^{N}\left(\sum a_{i}x^{i-N}\right)\mapsto\left(N,\sum a_{i}x^{i-N}\right).

∎

Following the notation of
[
10
]
, for a unit
u\in A(\!(x)\!)^{*}
, denote
\nu(u)=N
, where this is the associated
N
from Lemma
A.1
. Explicitly, if

u=\sum a_{i}x^{i},

then
\nu(u)
is the
N
such that
a_{N}\in A^{*}
and
a_{n}
is nilpotent for
n<N
. It is shown in
[
10
]
(Theorem 4.7 with
n=m=1
) that every continuous endomorphism
\varphi:A(\!(x)\!)\to A(\!(x)\!)
satisfies
\nu(\varphi(x)\!)>0
. We now classify which of these are automorphisms.

Proposition A.2
.

A continuous endomorphism
\varphi:A(\!(x)\!)\to A(\!(x)\!)
is an automorphism if and only if
\nu(\varphi(x)\!)=1
.

Proof.

This follows immediately from Theorem 6.8 (with
n=1
) of
[
10
]
. However, we provide a proof for this case here.

As explained above,
\varphi
must satisfy
\nu(\varphi(x)\!)>0
. Let us examine the image of
\varphi(x)
under the map
A(\!(x)\!)\to k(\!(x)\!)
induced by
A\to A/\mathfrak{m}\simeq k
. Denote this image by
\overline{\varphi(x)}
. Since
\nu(\varphi(x)\!)>0
, we see that
\overline{\varphi(x)}=\overline{a_{1}}x+\overline{a_{2}}x^{2}+\cdots\in xk[\![x]\!]
. On the other hand, if
\overline{\varphi(x)}\in x^{2}k[\![x]\!]
, i.e., if
\overline{a_{1}}=0
, then this will not be an invertible map, as it will not be surjective (
x
will not be in the image). Thus, we see that
\varphi
is an automorphism if and only if
\nu(\varphi(x)\!)=1
.
∎

A.2.
Characteristic Zero Residue

Assume that
k
has characteristic zero.

Lemma A.3
.

We have an isomorphism

A(\!(x)\!)\,dx/dA(\!(x)\!)\simeq A\,x^{-1}\,dx=A\,d\log x.

Proof.

To see this, we define a surjective map
A(\!(x)\!)\,dx\to A\,x^{-1}\,dx
by
\sum a_{i}x^{i}\,dx\mapsto a_{-1}x^{-1}\,dx
. We claim that the kernel is exactly
dA(\!(x)\!)
. Indeed, if we have an element
\sum a_{i}x^{i}\,dx\in A(\!(x)\!)\,dx
with
a_{-1}=0
, then this element is the image under
d
of
\sum a_{i}x^{i+1}/(i+1)\,dx
.
∎

Proposition A.4
.

The isomorphism
A(\!(x)\!)\,dx/dA(\!(x)\!)\simeq A\,d\log x
is invariant under a continuous automorphism of
A(\!(x)\!)
.

Proof.

From Proposition
A.2
, any continuous automorphism must send
x
to
xu
, where
u\in A(\!(x)\!)^{nil}
. We must show that
d\log(xu)\equiv d\log(x)\pmod{dA(\!(x)\!)}
. Since
d\log(xu)=d\log(x)+d\log(u)
, this amounts to showing that
d\log(u)\in dA(\!(x)\!)
.

First, up to scaling by an element of
A^{*}
, we can write
u=1+v
, where
v=\sum a_{i}x^{i}
with
a_{0}=0
and
a_{n}
nilpotent for
n<0
. Now let
F(x)=\sum_{i>0}(-1)^{i+1}x^{i}/i
be the power series of
\log(1+x)
. We claim that
F(v)
is a well-defined formal Laurent series. To see this, note that we can write
v=v^{nil}+v^{\prime}
, where
v^{nil}=\sum_{i<0}a_{i}x^{i}
(with each
a_{i}
nilpotent) and
v^{\prime}=\sum_{i>0}a_{i}x^{i}
. Since
v^{nil}
is nilpotent, say with
(v^{nil})^{N}=0
, we see that for any
i>0
,
v^{i}
is a sum of
(v^{nil})^{j}(v^{\prime})^{k}
with
j+k=i
and
i\geq k>i-N
. Thus for any
n
, there will only be finitely many
i
such that
v^{i}
contributes a nonzero coefficient of
x^{n}
.

Next,
d(F(x)\!)=\sum_{i>0}(-1)^{i}x^{i}=(1+x)^{-1}
, and since
F(v)
is defined, we have

d(F(v)\!)=dF(v)\cdot dv=(1+v)^{-1}dv=d(1+v)/(1+v)=du/u=d\log(u).

Therefore,
d\log(u)
is the image of
F(v)
under

, so

, as desired.
∎

A.3.
Positive Characteristic Residue

Now assume
k
has characteristic
p\neq 0
.

One might be tempted to replicate the proof from characteristic zero, but an issue quickly arises: Lemma
A.3
fails to be true when
k
has positive characteristic. This is due to the fact that in characteristic
p
,
d(x^{p^{n}})=p^{n}x^{p^{n}-1}\,dx=0
for all
n
. Thus, the quotient
A(\!(x)\!)\,dx/dA(\!(x)\!)
will be too large, as it will contain terms of the form
x^{p}
,
x^{p^{2}}
, etc.

However, we can still recover the idea from characteristic zero as follows. The goal is to quotient
A(\!(x)\!)\,dx
and have a free rank
1

A
-module. Attempting to quotient by just
dA(\!(x)\!)
fails, as just discussed, so we will try to quotient by something larger. Specifically, we saw that the terms of the form
x^{p^{n}}
are what cause the issue with the quotient, so we will try to construct a submodule that contains these terms, leaving us with just
d\log x
after quotienting.

For each
n
, we define a derivation

p^{-n}d:A(\!(x^{p^{n}})\!)\to A(\!(x)\!)\>dx

by the following formula:

p^{-n}d(x^{p^{n}})=x^{p^{n}-1}dx=x^{p^{n}}d\log x.

This extends uniquely to a continuous derivation. Equivalently,
p^{-n}d
is the composition of the derivation

d:A(\!(x^{p^{n}})\!)\to A(\!(x^{p^{n}})\!)d(x^{p^{n}})

that sends
x^{p^{n}}
to
d(x^{p^{n}})=x^{p^{n}}d\log(x^{p^{n}})
with the homomorphism

A(\!(x^{p^{n}})\!)d\log(x^{p^{n}})\to A(\!(x^{p^{n}})\!)d\log x\subset A(\!(x)\!)d\log x

that sends the basis element
d\log x^{p^{n}}
to
d\log x
(or, equivalently, sends
d(x^{p^{n}})
to
x^{p^{n}-1}dx
).

Our goal will now be to show that
\sum_{n}p^{-n}dA(\!(x^{p^{n}})\!)
is the submodule that we should quotient by.

Lemma A.5
.

Let
A
be a
p
-torsion-free local ring with residue field of characteristic
p
. Let

be an integer. Let

be a Laurent series with the property that
\mathrm{ord}_{p}(a_{n})+\mathrm{ord}_{p}(n)\geq r
for all
n
. Then

has the property that
\mathrm{ord}_{p}(b_{n})+\mathrm{ord}_{p}(n)\geq r+1
for all

.

Proof.

Suppose that
p^{k}\mid n
and
p^{k+1}\nmid n
. Then
b_{n}
is a sum of terms

(6)

a_{i_{1}}\cdots a_{i_{p}}x^{i_{1}+\cdots+i_{p}}

where

. Since

is not a multiple of

must not be a multiple of

. Therefore

so
\mathrm{ord}_{p}(a_{i_{1}}\cdots a_{i_{p}})+\mathrm{ord}_{p}(n)\geq r-k+k=r
. But if

are not all the same then the term (

) appears a multiple of

times in

, so (

) and all of its permutations contribute a term

to

with

.

On the other hand, if

then

does not divide

does not divide

.
∎

Lemma A.6

Let

∎

It follows from Lemma

Lemma A.7
.

The quotient

Lemma A.8
.

Let

After multiplication by a unit, we can write

We argue that there is a power series

c_{3}\\

Let

. Since
J
is nilpotent, this process will eventually terminate and reduce the matrix to the identity.

Now since
g
is a unit of
A(\!(x)\!)
, we can now write
u
as
(1+v)(1+w)
where
v\in x^{-1}A[x^{-1}]
and has nilpotent coefficients and
w\in xA[\![x]\!]
. Since
d\log(u)=d\log(1+v)+d\log(1+w)
, we can therefore treat the two cases
u=1+v
and
u=1+w
separately.

We consider the case where
u\in 1+xA[\![x]\!]
first. Replacing
A
by
\mathbf{Z}_{(p)}[[b_{1},b_{2},\ldots]]
, it is sufficient to assume that
A
is
p
-torsion-free.
Since
d\log u=du/u=\sum c_{n}x^{n}d\log(x)
has a power series expansion with coefficients in
A
, it is possible to write a power series
\log u
with coefficients in
A[p^{-1}]
,

\log u=\sum\frac{c_{n}}{n}x^{n},

where all
c_{n}
are in
A
. We group the terms by the power of
p
appearing in
n
, so that

\log u=\sum_{\mathrm{ord}_{p}n=0}\frac{c_{n}}{n}x^{n}+p^{-1}\sum_{\mathrm{ord}_{p}n=1}\frac{c_{n}}{n/p}(x^{p})^{n/p}+p^{-2}\sum_{\mathrm{ord}_{p}n=2}\frac{c_{n}}{n/p^{2}}(x^{p^{2}})^{n/p^{2}}+\cdots.

The first sum lies in
A(\!(x)\!)
, the second in
A(\!(x^{p})\!)
, the third in
A(\!(x^{p^{2}})\!)
, etc. Applying
d
, therefore,

d\log u=d\sum_{\mathrm{ord}_{p}n=0}\frac{c_{n}}{n}x^{n}+p^{-1}d\sum_{\mathrm{ord}_{p}n=1}\frac{c_{n}}{n/p}(x^{p})^{n/p}+p^{-2}d\sum_{\mathrm{ord}_{p}n=2}\frac{c_{n}}{n/p^{2}}(x^{p^{2}})^{n/p^{2}}+\cdots\\
\in dA(\!(x)\!)+p^{-1}dA(\!(x^{p})\!)+p^{-2}dA(\!(x^{p^{2}})\!)+\cdots

Next we consider
u=1+v
where
v\in x^{-1}A[x^{-1}]
has nilpotent coefficients. But
u(x^{-1})\in 1+xA[x]
, so by the above,

d\log u(x^{-1})\in dA[x]+p^{-1}dA[x^{p}]+p^{-2}dA[x^{p^{2}}]+\cdots

Applying the homomorphism
x\mapsto x^{-1}
from
A[x]
to
A(\!(x)\!)
, we conclude that

d\log u\in dA(\!(x)\!)+p^{-1}dA(\!(x^{p})\!)+p^{-2}dA(\!(x^{p^{2}})\!)+\cdots

as required.
∎

Proposition A.9
.

The isomorphism

A(\!(x)\!)\>dx\Big/\bigl(dA(\!(x)\!)+p^{-1}dA(\!(x^{p})\!)+p^{-2}dA(\!(x^{p^{2}})\!)+\cdots\bigr)\simeq Ad\log x

is invariant under any continuous automorphism of
A(\!(x)\!)
.

Proof.

As in the proof of Proposition
A.4
, assume that the automorphism sends
x
to
xu
with
u\in A(\!(x)\!)^{nil}
. Then we must show that
d\log(xu)\equiv d\log x
modulo
dA(\!(x)\!)+p^{-1}dA(\!(x^{p})\!)+p^{-2}dA(\!(x^{p^{2}})\!)+\cdots
, and since
d\log(xu)=d\log x+d\log u
, we must show that
d\log u\in dA(\!(x)\!)+p^{-1}dA(\!(x^{p})\!)+p^{-2}dA(\!(x^{p^{2}})\!)+\cdots
. This is exactly the statement of Lemma
A.8
.
∎

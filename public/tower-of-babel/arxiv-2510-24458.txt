Efficient Network Reconfiguration by Randomized Switching
Samuel Talkington,Dmitrii M. Ostrovskii, Daniel K. Molzahn

Abstract
We present an algorithm that efficiently computes nearly-optimal solutions to a class of combinatorial reconfiguration problems on weighted, undirected graphs. Inspired by societally relevant applications in networked infrastructure systems, these problems consist of simultaneously finding an unreweighted sparsified graph and nodal potentials that satisfy fixed demands, where the objective is to minimize some congestion criterion, e.g., a Laplacian quadratic form. These are mixed-integer nonlinear programming problems that are NP-hard, in general. To circumvent these challenges, instead of solving for a single best configuration, the proposed randomized switching algorithm seeks to design a distribution of configurations that, when sampled, ensures that congestion concentrates around its optimum. We show that the proposed congestion metric is a generalized self-concordant function in the space of switching probabilities, which enables the use of efficient and simple conditional gradient methods. We implement our algorithm and show that it outperforms a state-of-the-art commercial mixed-integer second-order cone programming (MISOCP) solver by orders of magnitude over a large range of problem sizes.

Introduction

Consider an undirected weighted graph
G=(N,E,\bm{w})
with
n=\left|N\right|
nodes and
m=\left|E\right|

switchable edges
which can be opened or closed. The edge weights
\bm{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}
are fixed. We are given a fixed vector of nodal demands
\bm{d}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
that satisfies
\bm{d}^{\top}\bm{1}=0
. A
network reconfiguration problem
is to find edge
switching statuses

\bm{s}\in S\subseteq\left\{0,1\right\}^{m}

and
nodal potentials
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
that minimize a
congestion criterion
, subject to various engineering constraints. For example, such constraints may include ensuring that the graph is connected and that the nodal potentials satisfy network physics for the given demands over the switched graph. A network reconfiguration problem can be formulated as the following parametric mixed-integer nonlinear program (MINLP):

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}(\bm{d}):\qquad\min_{\bm{s},\bm{x}}

\displaystyle\qquad\bm{x}^{\top}\bm{\Gamma}_{\bm{s}}\bm{x}

(Congestion)

\displaystyle\operatorname{\sf s.t.}

\displaystyle\qquad\bm{L}_{\bm{s}}\bm{x}=\bm{d}

(Physics)

\displaystyle\qquad\lambda_{2}(\bm{L}_{\bm{s}})\geq\lambda_{\star}

(Connectivity)

\displaystyle\qquad\bm{s}\in S

(Switching)

\displaystyle\qquad\bm{x}\perp\bm{1},

(Kirchhoff)

where
\bm{L}_{\bm{s}}:=\sum_{i<j}\bm{E}_{ij}w_{ij}s_{ij}
is the
model Laplacian
of the switched graph
G_{\bm{s}}:=(N,E,\bm{w}\circ\bm{s})
, and
\bm{\Gamma}_{\bm{s}}:=\sum_{i<j}\bm{E}_{ij}\gamma_{ij}s_{ij}
is a
target Laplacian
for a graph with the same connectivity, but potentially different edge weights. The matrix
\bm{E}_{ij}:=\left(\bm{e}_{i}-\bm{e}_{j}\right)\left(\bm{e}_{i}-\bm{e}_{j}\right)^{\top}
is an elementary Laplacian describing a two-node graph. The constraint on the second-smallest eigenvalue
\lambda_{2}(\bm{L}_{\bm{s}})\geq\lambda_{\star}
ensures the graph remains connected.

There are several particularly relevant cases for the objective (
Congestion
) and the target Laplacian
\bm{\Gamma}_{\bm{s}}
, with natural connections to engineering applications, such as in electric power systems:

1.

the original
model Laplacian
, i.e.,
\gamma_{ij}=w_{ij}
for all
(i,j)\in E
. In this case, if
w_{ij}
represents the
conductance
of edge
(i,j)\in E
, the objective becomes the
total electric power dissipated
across all nodes in the network
\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}x_{i}:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}
.

2.

a
capacitated Laplacian
, e.g.
\gamma_{ij}=w_{ij}^{2}/c_{ij}^{2}
, for all
(i,j)\in E
. In this case, the objective function becomes the sum of the normalized line flows
\sum_{i<j}f_{ij}^{2}/c_{ij}^{2}
.

3.

a
risk
,
fairness
, or
robustness
functional, which measures the importance of an edge with respect to some metric. For instance,
\gamma_{ij}
could represent the probability that a wildfire ignites at a line in an electrical network, or the criticality of the edge in socioeconomic factors.

These cases can be derived in a straightforward manner from the general framework of electrical networks and their corresponding graph representations.

1.1
Motivation

The program
\mathcal{P}
is inspired by more complex network reconfiguration problems that are of great current interest to electric power system researchers; see
[
4
,
20
,
51
]
for recent examples. Such problems are known as

,

, or

in the relevant literature. The constraint (
Physics
) is identical to
DC power flow

is NP-hard in general (see
[

,
22
]

A natural first attempt to solve the problem is to relax the switching variables
\bm{s}
to be continuous, i.e.,
\bm{s}\in[0,1]^{m}
, and then solve the resulting optimization problem. However, this approach does not yield a feasible solution to the original problem, since the relaxed solution need not be integral. Moreover, the relaxed problem is still a challenging problem, as (
Physics
) involves bilinear equality constraints.

Applications of interest.

The present paper proposes a new primitive blending randomized network design and mixed-integer quadratic optimization. The proposed model has direct applications to widely used approximate models for electric power flow and water flow in real-world infrastructure networks, both of which obey the conditions of the proposed theory. Moreover, this primitive is directly inspired by the electric power transmission network reconfiguration problem, where transmission lines are switched on and off to improve the stability of the network. The algorithm is designed to be usable for the
DC power flow approximation
of the nonlinear AC power flow equations, a canonical linearization that appears frequently in practice; see
[
46
]
for a salient review. This approximation has the attractive problem of being
identical
to the Poisson problem. In essence, we are given a
net power injection vector

\bm{p}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\bm{p}^{\top}\bm{1}=0
and we want to find a
voltage phase angle
vector
\bm{\theta}\in(-\pi,\pi]^{n}
such that

\bm{p}=\bm{L}\bm{\theta},\qquad\bm{L}:=-\bm{B},\qquad\bm{\theta}^{\top}\bm{1}=0,

where
\bm{B}\preceq 0
is a weighted graph Laplacian matrix corresponding to an undirected graph with negative edge weights
b:N\times N\to\mathbb{R}_{-}
, known as
susceptances
.

While the proposed theory is a general graph algorithm, it is also applicable to electric power engineering, which has thus far taken largely empirical and deterministic mixed-integer-based approaches. By targeting approximate preservation of Poisson solutions with controllable energy reduction through randomized edge selection, we provide a new, truly interdisciplinary contribution to this problem.

2
Preliminaries

The following parameterized positive-semidefinite (PSD) Laplacian matrix functional is the central object of study.

Definition 2.1

(Switched Laplacian)
.

For a multigraph
G=(N,E,\bm{w})
, let
\bm{A}\in\left\{-1,0,1\right\}^{m\times n}
be the node-to-edge incidence matrix for
G
. Let
\bm{s}\in\left\{0,1\right\}^{m}
be
switching variables
corresponding to a reconfiguration, and let
\bm{L}_{(\cdot)}:\left\{0,1\right\}^{m}\to\mathbb{S}^{n\times n}
be a
switched Laplacian matrix
functional, where

\bm{L}_{\bm{s}}:=\bm{A}^{\top}\bm{W}^{1/2}\bm{S}\bm{W}^{1/2}\bm{A},

(1)

corresponds to the switched graph
G_{\bm{s}}:=(N,E,\bm{w}\circ\bm{s})
,
with
\bm{S}:=\operatorname{diag}(\bm{s})
.

Def.
2.1
is also known as an
unweighted sparsifier

[
2
]
. From here, we can naturally define corresponding effective resistance and leverage score functionals.

Definition 2.2

(Effective resistance and leverage)
.

The effective resistance
\varrho_{ij}:\left\{0,1\right\}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
of a multi-edge
ij\in E
under switching strategy
\bm{s}
is defined as

\varrho_{ij}(\bm{s}):=\bm{e}_{ij}^{\top}\bm{L}_{\bm{s}}^{\dagger}\bm{e}_{ij}.

Moreover, let
w_{ij}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
be the weight of the edge
ij
. The
leverage
of the edge
ij
is defined as the effective resistance scaled by the weight:

\ell_{ij}(\bm{s}):=w_{ij}\cdot\varrho_{ij}(\bm{s})\leq 1.

Note that one has
\sum_{ij\in E}\ell_{ij}(\bm{s})=n-1
. This is also known as Foster’s theorem
[
15
]
.

2.1
Relaxed problem formulation

Throughout, we work under the following simplifying assumptions. Assumption
1
is without loss of generality, while Assumption
2
is a somewhat restrictive assumption that provides a clean way to handle connectivity constraints.

Assumption 1
.

The target edge weights are the same as the model weights,
y_{e}=w_{e}
for all
e\in E
; equivalently,
\bm{\Gamma}_{\bm{s}}=\bm{L}_{\bm{s}}
for any
\bm{s}\in\left\{0,1\right\}^{m}
.

Assumption 2
.

There exists a connected template graph
S_{0}\subseteq E
such that
s_{e}=1
always for all
e\in S_{0}
.

Relaxation to switching probabilities.

The key step is to relax the binary variables to lie in the unit cube
\bm{s}\in\left[0,1\right]^{m}
. This choice has two benefits. From an optimization perspective, the relaxed reconfiguration program becomes much more tractable. Moreover, fractional solutions of the relaxed reconfiguration problem can be viewed as
switching probabilities
, through the framework of randomized rounding
[
36
]
.
Under Assumptions
1
and
2
, by primal feasibility, the reconfiguration problem can be written as

\displaystyle\min_{\bm{s}}\qquad

\displaystyle\bm{d}^{\top}\bm{L}_{\bm{s}}^{\dagger}\bm{d}\;=:\varphi(\bm{s})

\displaystyle\operatorname{\sf s.t.}\qquad

\displaystyle s_{e}=1\quad\forall e\in S_{0},

\displaystyle\left|\left|\bm{s}\right|\right|_{1}\leq q.

The relaxed congestion objective
\varphi:\left[0,1\right]^{m}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
can be written as

\varphi(\bm{s})=\bm{d}^{\top}\bm{L}_{\bm{s}}\bm{d},\qquad\bm{L}_{\bm{s}}=\bm{A}^{\top}\operatorname{diag}(\bm{w}\circ\bm{s})\bm{A},

(2)

and it has notable structural properties, which we now investigate.

2.2
First-order information

The gradient of the relaxed congestion (
2
) can be computed in closed form. To obtain this, we first show how to differentiate a related function, the
total effective resistance
or
Kirchhoff index
, which was studied by Ghosh and Boyd in
[
19
]
.

Lemma 1
.

Let
R(\bm{s})=\sum_{i<j}\varrho_{ij}(\bm{s})=\frac{1}{2}\bm{1}^{\top}\bm{R}_{\bm{s}}\bm{1}
be the total effective resistance of the graph
G_{\bm{s}}=(N,E,\bm{w}\circ\bm{s})
. Then,

\frac{\partial}{\partial s_{ij}}R(\bm{s})=-nw_{ij}\bm{e}_{ij}^{\top}\bm{L}^{2\dagger}_{\bm{s}}\bm{e}_{ij},\qquad ij\in E.

In particular, in our setting of fixed demands, we generalize the above result to the following.

Lemma 2

(Congestion gradient)
.

For a fixed demand
\bm{d}\perp\bm{1}
, the gradient of the relaxed congestion (
2
) is given elementwise for each edge
e\in E
as

\frac{\partial}{\partial s_{e}}\,\varphi(\bm{s})=-w_{e}\left|\left\langle\bm{a}_{e},\bm{L}_{\bm{s}}^{\dagger}\bm{d}\right\rangle\right|^{2}:=-w_{e}\Delta_{e}^{2}\leq 0,

(3)

where
\Delta_{e}=\hat{x}_{i}-\hat{x}_{j}
is the voltage difference solution map across edge
e=(i,j)\in E
.

Using the gradient entries of the relaxed congestion (
3
), we will show that the relaxed congestion (
2
) inherits many of the favorable properties of the total effective resistance.

2.3
Second-order information and Hessian

To analyze the performance of our algorithms, we need to understand the spectral content of the Hessian of the congestion function
\varphi(\bm{s})
with respect to the switching variables
\bm{s}
. We will first state and prove the following technical lemma.

Lemma 3

(Cauchy–Schwarz for voltage differences)
.

Let
\bm{d}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a vector of demands, and let
\bm{x}=\bm{L}_{\bm{s}}^{\dagger}\bm{d}
be the voltages induced by the demands under switching strategy
\bm{s}\in\left\{0,1\right\}^{m}
. Then, for any edge
ij\in E
and switching strategy
\bm{s}
, we have

\Delta_{ij}^{2}=\left|\bm{a}_{ij}^{\top}\bm{x}\right|^{2}\leq\varrho_{ij}\cdot\varphi(\bm{s})\qquad\text{for all }(i,j)\in E.

(4)

Lemma
3
says that the squared voltage differences in electrical networks can never exceed the product of the effective resistance of the edge and the total congestion in the network. This is a consequence of the fact that
\bm{L}_{\bm{s}},\bm{L}_{\bm{s}}^{\dagger}\succeq 0
, and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality applied to the voltages induced by the demands
\bm{d}
on the switched graph
G_{\bm{s}}=(N,E,\bm{w}\circ\bm{s})
.

Lemma 4

(Operator norm bound on the Hessian)
.

Given a switching strategy set
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\left[0,1\right]^{m}
and demands
\bm{d}
, let
\bm{H}(\cdot):\mathcal{S}\to\mathbb{S}^{m}_{+}
be the Hessian of the congestion function
\varphi(\cdot)
. The operator norm of the Hessian
\bm{H}(\cdot)
is bounded as follows:

\displaystyle\left|\left|\bm{H}\right|\right|_{\operatorname{op}}

\displaystyle\leq L:=2\max_{\bm{s}\in\mathcal{S}}\varphi(\bm{s})\leq 2\left|\left|\bm{d}\right|\right|_{\bm{L}_{\bm{s}_{0}}}^{2}\leq\frac{2\left|\left|\bm{d}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}}{\lambda_{2}\left(\bm{L}_{\bm{s}_{0}}\right)}.

Moreover, if
\left|\left|\bm{d}\right|\right|_{2}\leq 1
always, and the template is such that
\lambda_{2}(\bm{L}_{\bm{s}_{0}})\geq 1
, then it holds that

\left|\left|\bm{H}\right|\right|_{\operatorname{op}}\leq 2
.

The proof appears in the supplementary material.

2.4
Structural properties of congestion

The goal of this section is to outline useful properties about the relaxed congestion (
2
).

Remark
.

Set
\bm{1}_{T}\in\{0,1\}^{m}
as the indicator of a spanning tree
T\subseteq E
of
G
. In the feasible set

\mathcal{D}_{T}\;:=\;\{\,\bm{s}\in[0,1]^{m}:s_{e}=1\text{ for all }e\in T\},

the graph remains connected for all
\bm{s}\in\mathcal{D}_{T}
because the tree edges have switching value one.

Furthermore, we have the following useful property, which is reminiscent of
[
19
]
.

Lemma 5
.

The relaxed congestion (
2
) is a convex, homogeneous function of degree
-1
, namely,

\varphi(\bm{s})=-\left\langle\nabla\varphi(\bm{s}),\bm{s}\right\rangle\qquad\forall\bm{s}\in\mathcal{D}_{T}.

The proof appears in Appendix
A.6
.

Theorem 1

(Generalized self–concordance)
.

Let
T\subseteq E
. For
\bm{s}\in\mathcal{D}_{T}
define the relaxed congestion
\varphi(\bm{s})=\bm{d}^{\top}\bm{L}_{\bm{s}}^{\dagger}\bm{d}
. Let
\bm{\varrho}_{T}:=\operatorname{diag}(\bm{A}\bm{L}_{T}^{\dagger}\bm{A}^{\top})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
denote the vector of effective resistances in
T
, i.e.,
\bm{\varrho}_{T}(e)=\bm{a}_{e}^{\top}\bm{L}_{T}^{\dagger}\bm{a}_{e}
where
\bm{L}_{T}=\bm{A}^{\top}\operatorname{diag}(\bm{w}\odot\bm{1}_{T})\bm{A}
is the Laplacian corresponding to
T
and
\bm{L}_{T}^{\dagger}
its pseudoinverse. Then
\varphi
is
(M,\nu)
–generalized self–concordant on
\mathcal{D}_{T}
with

\nu=2\quad\text{and}\quad M\;=\;3\,\left|\left|\bm{w}\odot\bm{\varrho}_{T}\right|\right|_{2}.

(5)

That is, for all
s\in\mathcal{D}_{T}
and all directions
\bm{u},\bm{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
one has

\bigl|D^{3}\varphi(s)[\bm{v},\bm{u},\bm{u}]\bigr|\;\leq\;3\,\bigl\|\bm{w}\odot\bm{\varrho}_{T}\bigr\|_{2}\,\|\bm{v}\|_{2}\,\|\bm{u}\|_{\nabla^{2}\varphi(\bm{s})}^{2}.

(6)

In particular, the generalized self–concordance constant
M
is finite and independent of
\bm{s}
on the entire feasible domain
\mathcal{D}_{T}
.

The proof appears in Appendix
A.8
.

Main Results

3.1
Approximation Primitives

As shown in Thm.
1
,
\varphi
is a generalized self-concordant function in the space of
switching probabilities

\bm{s}\in\mathcal{D}_{T}
. This makes a particularly simple variant of the Frank-Wolfe algorithm a good choice to design a distribution of configurations; see the recent works of
[
14
,
34
,
8
]
. We aim for our random output switching vector to be such that, when we sample edges without replacement (that is,

), it is likely to produce a connected subgraph with at most
O(q)
edges, and to satisfy

, where we desire
\alpha
to be as small as possible. To achieve an efficient implementation of this algorithm, in this section, we will present the key ingredients that leverage fast Laplacian solvers.

3.1.1
Fast Laplacian solver with switching

Below is a simple corollary of the result in
[
23
, Thm. 1.2.1.]
, which proves useful in our subsequent analysis.

Corollary 1.1

(
[
23
]
)
.

Fix a scalar
\delta<1/n^{100}
and a switching strategy
\bm{s}
with at most
q
non-zero entries, and let
\bm{L}_{\bm{s}}\succeq\bm{0}
be as in Def.
2.1
. There exists an algorithm that returns a random Cholesky factor
\bm{C}_{\bm{s}}
such that, with probability at least

,

\tfrac{1}{2}\bm{L}_{\bm{s}}\preceq\bm{C}_{\bm{s}}\bm{C}_{\bm{s}}^{\top}\preceq\tfrac{3}{2}\bm{L}_{\bm{s}}.

This algorithm runs in time
O\left(q\log^{2}\left(1/\delta\right)\log(n)\right)
.

Consequently, given demands
\bm{d}\perp\bm{1}
and an error tolerance
\varepsilon>0
there is an algorithm
\mathsf{Solve}(\bm{L}_{\bm{s}},\bm{d},\delta,\varepsilon)
that returns an approximate voltage solution
\hat{\bm{x}}(\bm{s})
such that with probability at least
1-O(\delta)
,

\left|\left|\hat{\bm{x}}(\bm{s})-\bm{L}^{\dagger}_{\bm{s}}\bm{d}\right|\right|_{\bm{L}_{\bm{s}}}\leq\varepsilon\left|\left|\bm{L}_{\bm{s}}^{\dagger}\bm{d}\right|\right|_{\bm{L}_{\bm{s}}},

where
\left|\left|\bm{x}\right|\right|_{\bm{L}}:=\sqrt{\bm{x}^{\top}\bm{L}\bm{x}}
for
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
The algorithm runs in
O(q\log^{2}(1/\delta)\log(n)\log(1/\varepsilon))
time.

Access to a near-linear time Laplacian solver immediately provides a simple, near-linear time algorithm for
simultaneously
determining the gradient of the congestion criterion
\nabla\varphi\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, and pairwise voltage differences across all edges,
\Delta\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, which we present in Section
3.1.3
.

3.1.2
Switching strategies and edge budget

A useful reconfiguration problem is to select a subset of edges
S\subseteq E
such that the graph remains connected, i.e., the algebraic connectivity
\lambda_{2}(\bm{L}_{S})>0
, the number of edges in
S
is at most
K
, and the congestion (graph energy) does not grow too much. There are two such ways we can model this constraint set.

Throughout, we treat all switching variables as the parameters of independent Bernoulli random variables, i.e., we assume that each edge
e\in E
is switched with probability
s_{e}\in[s_{\sf min},1]
, with the lower bound to be defined later, and the switching decisions are independent. We want to show that
\bm{s}
is a good
approximately optimal switching strategy
, in the following sense: if we sample a
random
configuration
\bm{\tilde{s}}
with
\bm{\tilde{s}}_{e}\sim\mathsf{Ber}(\bm{s}_{e})
, independently for each edge
e\in E
, then the congestion of the resulting graph does not grow too much. We will define this notion precisely in Theorem
2
.

A natural constraint is to impose a budget on the number of edges that can be switched, that is, we require that
\left|\left|\bm{s}\right|\right|_{1}\leq q
, where
q
is the edge budget. This is a natural constraint in many applications, such as electrical networks, where we may not be able to switch all edges due to physical limitations or operational costs.

3.1.3
Fast gradients

We now study the algorithm
ApproxDiff
, presented in Alg.
1
, which updates the congestion gradient and the voltage differences for a given switching strategy

3.2
Frank-Wolfe with randomized rounding

We apply a simple Frank-Wolfe procedure in Alg.
2
to optimize the relaxed congestion function. After the iterative phase of the algorithm, we obtain a fractional switching vector
\bm{s}_{t}\in[0,1]^{m}
satisfying
\|\bm{s}_{t}\|_{1}\leq q
and
(\bm{s}_{t})_{e}=1
for all backbone edges
e\in T
. To convert
\bm{s}_{t}
into an integral solution while preserving the edge weights
\bm{w}
, we adopt the following procedure. Fix a failure probability
\delta\in(0,1)
and choose a baseline probability, e.g.
p_{\min}\;=\;C\,\log(n/\delta)/n
with a universal constant
C>0
. Define

\bar{s}_{e}=\begin{cases}\max\{(\bm{s}_{t})_{e},\,p_{\min}\}&e\in E\setminus T\\
1&e\in T.\end{cases}

(7)

Sample a random vector
\tilde{\bm{s}}\in\{0,1\}^{m}
by including each edge
e
independently with probability
\bar{s}_{e}
. If
\|\tilde{\bm{s}}\|_{1}>q
, remove edges in increasing order of
(\bm{s}_{t})_{e}
until at most
q
edges remain; if
\|\tilde{\bm{s}}\|_{1}<q
, add edges in decreasing order of
(\bm{s}_{t})_{e}
until
q
edges are selected. Another approach is to sample multiple draws of
\bm{\tilde{s}}
until
\|\bm{\tilde{s}}\|_{1}\leq q
, without ensuring the constraint is tight. Scalar Bernoulli concentration can show that this will take at most a logarithmic number of draws. Denote by
\bm{L}_{\bm{\tilde{s}}}=\sum_{e}\tilde{s}_{e}w_{e}\bm{a}_{e}\bm{a}_{e}^{\top}
the Laplacian of the sampled configuration.

4.1
Baseline formulation

The network reconfiguration studied in the present paper can be formulated as a mixed-integer second-order cone program (MISOCP). This structure is compatible with some commercial solvers, like Gurobi, to ostensibly solve the problem efficiently. Introducing a change of variable, we obtain the program

\displaystyle\min_{\bm{u},\bm{s},\bm{f}}\quad

\displaystyle 2\sum_{e\in E}w_{e}^{-1}u_{e}

(11a)

\displaystyle\operatorname{\sf s.t.}\quad

\displaystyle\bm{u}\geq\bm{0},\ \bm{s}\in\left\{0,1\right\}^{m},\ \bm{f}\in\mathbb{R}^{m},

(11b)

\displaystyle u_{e}s_{e}\geq\frac{1}{2}f_{e}^{2},\quad\forall e\in E,

(11c)

\displaystyle s_{e}=1\quad\forall e\in S_{0},

(11d)

\displaystyle\left|\left|\bm{s}\right|\right|_{1}\leq q,

(11e)

\displaystyle\bm{A}^{\top}\bm{f}=\bm{d}.

(11f)

The program (
11
) is a mixed-integer second-order cone program (MISOCP). The objective function is a linearization of the congestion objective
\varphi(s)
, where
u_{e}
is an upper bound on the congestion of edge
e
. The constraints (
11c
) are second-order cone constraints (condtional on
s_{e}
) that enforce
u_{e}\geq\frac{1}{2}\frac{f_{e}^{2}}{s_{e}}
when
s_{e}=1
and
u_{e}\geq 0
when
s_{e}=0
. The constraint (
11e
) enforces the power constraint. Finally, the constraint
\bm{A}^{\top}\bm{f}=\bm{d}
enforces flow conservation.

5
Conclusion

We presented an approximation scheme to efficiently solve network reconfiguration problems. We proposed a particular instance of such an algorithm,
RandReconfig
, based on a conditional gradient procedure with randomized rounding. We demonstrated that our method outperforms a commercial MINLP solver by orders of magnitude on a relevant problem. Inclusion of line flow limits (edge capacities) is an essential component for future work, in addition to allowing some entries of the demand vector to be continuous decision variables (i.e., controllable generation).

\displaystyle\overset{(1)}{\leq}2\cdot\sup_{\bm{s}\in\mathcal{S}}\ \left|\left|\operatorname{diag}(\bm{\zeta})\right|\right|_{\operatorname{op}}^{2}\cdot\left|\left|\bm{P}\right|\right|_{\operatorname{op}}

\displaystyle\overset{(2)}{\leq}2\cdot\sup_{\bm{s}\in\mathcal{S}}\ \varphi(\bm{s})

\displaystyle=2\sup_{\bm{s}\in\mathcal{S}}\left|\left|\bm{d}\right|\right|_{\bm{L}_{\bm{s}}}^{2}

\displaystyle\overset{(3)}{\leq}\frac{2\left|\left|\bm{d}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}}{\inf_{\bm{s}\in\mathcal{S}}\lambda_{2}(\bm{L}_{\bm{s}})}

\displaystyle\overset{(4)}{\leq}\frac{2\left|\left|\bm{d}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}}{\lambda_{2}(\bm{L}_{\bm{s}_{0}})}=:L,

where step (1) follows from the fact that
\left|\left|\bm{P}\right|\right|_{\operatorname{op}}=1
with multiplicity
n-1
, step (2) follows from Lemma
3
, and step (3) follows from the fact that
\varphi(\bm{s})\leq\frac{1}{\lambda_{2}(\bm{L}_{\bm{s}})}\left|\left|\bm{d}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}
. Lastly, step (4) is due to the fact that
1/\lambda_{2}(\bm{L}_{\bm{s}})
is montonically increasing as
\bm{s}\to\bm{0}
; hence, the graph corresponding to
\bm{s}_{0}
; that is, the graph with the least number of edges in
\mathcal{S}
, attains the maximal value of
1/\lambda_{2}(\cdot).
Finally, under certain connectivity assumptions, we can maintain
\lambda_{2}\geq 1
. Further, by assumption that
\left|\left|\bm{d}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\leq 1
, we have that
L=2
.
∎

Proof of Lemma

Proof of Lemma
5
.

Reminiscient of
[
19
]
, note that the congestion
\varphi(\cdot)
can be rewritten as:

\varphi(\bm{s})=\bm{d}^{\top}\bm{L}_{\bm{s}}^{+}\bm{d}=\bm{x}(\bm{s})^{\top}\bm{L}_{\bm{s}}\bm{x}(\bm{s})=\sum_{e\in E}s_{e}w_{e}\left|\left\langle\bm{a}_{e},\bm{x}(\bm{s})\right\rangle\right|^{2}=-\sum_{e\in E}s_{e}\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial s_{e}}\varphi(\bm{s})=-\left\langle\nabla\varphi(\bm{s}),\bm{s}\right\rangle

for any
\bm{s}\in\mathcal{D}_{T}
. Thus, we have shown that
\varphi
is a homogeneous function of degree
-1
, as desired. What remains is to show convexity. As remarked by
[
19
]
, a highly related result, shown in
[
39
]
, is that the effective resistance between two electrical points
i
and
j
:

\varrho_{ij}(\bm{s})=\left(\bm{e}_{i}-\bm{e}_{j}\right)^{\top}\bm{L}_{\bm{s}}^{\dagger}\left(\bm{e}_{i}-\bm{e}_{j}\right),

is a concave function in
r_{ij}:=1/(s_{ij}w_{ij})
for
w_{ij},s_{ij}\geq 0
, but this is not sufficient to imply convexity in
\varphi
; moreover, the technique of
[
19
]
does not yield a straightforward path. We will take a different path to show that
\varphi
is
convex
in
\bm{s}
, but not strictly so.

To establish this, recall that
\operatorname{tr}\bm{X}^{-1}
is a convex function over positive semi-definite matrices
\bm{X}=\bm{X}^{\top}\succeq\bm{0}
. Then, recall that the pseudoinverse of the switched Laplacian operator is

\bm{L}_{\bm{s}}^{\dagger}=\left(\bm{L}_{\bm{s}}+\bm{1}\bm{1}^{\top}/n\right)^{-1}=\left(\bm{A}^{\top}\bm{W}^{1/2}\bm{S}\bm{W}^{1/2}\bm{A}+\bm{1}\bm{1}^{\top}/n\right)^{-1}.

Thus, because the affine mapping
\bm{s}\mapsto\bm{L}_{\bm{s}}+\bm{1}\bm{1}^{\top}/n
is one-to-one, the function
\operatorname{tr}\left(\bm{L}_{\bm{s}}^{\dagger}\right)
is convex over
\bm{s}\in\left[0,1\right]^{m}
. Consequently, we can show that the congestion

\varphi(\bm{s})=\bm{d}^{\top}\bm{L}_{\bm{s}}^{\dagger}\bm{d}=\operatorname{tr}\left(\bm{d}\bm{d}^{\top}\bm{L}_{\bm{s}}^{\dagger}\right)=\operatorname{tr}\left(\bm{d}\bm{d}^{\top}\left(\bm{L}_{\bm{s}}+\bm{1}\bm{1}^{\top}/n\right)^{-1}\right)

is convex in
\bm{s}
for
\bm{d}\perp\bm{1}
.

First, when
\bm{M}=\bm{d}\bm{d}^{\top}
, we have the variational identity

\varphi(\bm{s})=\bm{d}^{\top}\bm{L}_{\bm{s}}^{\dagger}\bm{d}=\sup_{\bm{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\;2\bm{d}^{\top}\bm{z}-\bm{z}^{\top}\bm{L}_{\bm{s}}\bm{z},

(14)

which holds because, by the non-negativity of the norm,

\bm{z}^{\top}\bm{L}_{\bm{s}}\bm{z}-2\bm{d}^{\top}\bm{z}+\bm{d}^{\top}\bm{L}_{\bm{s}}^{\dagger}\bm{d}=\left(\bm{z}-\bm{L}_{\bm{s}}^{\dagger}\bm{d}\right)^{\top}\bm{L}_{\bm{s}}\left(\bm{z}-\bm{L}_{\bm{s}}^{\dagger}\bm{d}\right)=\left|\left|\bm{z}-\bm{L}_{\bm{s}}^{\dagger}\bm{d}\right|\right|_{\bm{L}_{\bm{s}}}\geq 0,

with equality at
\bm{z}=\bm{L}_{\bm{s}}^{\dagger}\bm{d}
, which is the unique maximizer of (
14
).
Since for each fixed
\bm{z}
, the mapping
\bm{L}_{\bm{s}}\mapsto-\bm{z}^{\top}\bm{L}_{\bm{s}}\bm{z}
is affine in
\bm{L}_{\bm{s}}
, and indeed, affine in
\bm{s}
, we have that
\varphi(\bm{s})
is a pointwise supremum of a family of affine functions of
\bm{s}
, and hence, convex.
∎

Remark

A stronger statement can be proved—the function
\operatorname{tr}\left(\bm{M}\bm{L}_{\bm{s}}^{\dagger}\right)

Proof of Lemma

Proof of Lemma
6
.

A single call to
\mathsf{Solve}(\bm{L}_{\bm{s}},\bm{d},\delta,\varepsilon)
yields
\hat{\bm{x}}
in at most
O(m\log^{c}n\,\log(1/\varepsilon))
time; by Lemma
2
, each gradient entry is
-w_{e}(\bm{a}_{e}^{\top}\hat{\bm{x}})^{2}
, so one linear pass over edges suffices.

Set
\bm{x}=\bm{L}_{\bm{s}}^{\dagger}\bm{d}
and
\bm{z}:=\hat{\bm{x}}-\bm{x}
. The solver guarantee gives, with probability
\geq 1-O(\delta)
,

\|\bm{z}\|_{\bm{L}_{\bm{s}}}\leq\varepsilon\|\bm{x}\|_{\bm{L}_{\bm{s}}}
.
Let
\Delta:=\bm{A}\bm{x}
,
\widehat{\Delta}:=\bm{A}\hat{\bm{x}}
, and
\delta\Delta:=\bm{A}\bm{z}
.
Define the vectors

\bm{\zeta}:=\sqrt{\bm{w}}\odot\Delta,\qquad\beta:=\sqrt{w}\odot\delta\Delta,

so that, by Lemma
2
,

\nabla\varphi(\bm{s})=-\,\bm{\zeta}\odot\bm{\zeta}

and

\widehat{\nabla}\varphi(\bm{s})=-\,(\bm{\zeta}+\bm{\beta})\odot(\bm{\zeta}+\bm{\beta}).

Hence the error is

h:=\widehat{\nabla}\varphi(\bm{s})-\nabla\varphi(\bm{s})\;=\;-\,2\,\bm{\zeta}\odot\bm{\beta}\;-\;\bm{\beta}\odot\bm{\beta}.

From the Hessian factorization proved in Section
A.5
,

\nabla^{2}\varphi(\bm{s})\;=\;2\,\operatorname{diag}(\bm{\zeta})\,\bm{P}\,\operatorname{diag}(\bm{\zeta}),\quad\bm{P}:=\bm{W}^{1/2}\bm{A}\,\bm{L}_{\bm{s}}^{\dagger}\bm{A}^{\top}\bm{W}^{1/2},

where
\bm{W}=\operatorname{diag}(\bm{w})
and
\bm{P}
is an orthogonal projector on
\operatorname{span}(\bm{W}^{1/2}\bm{A})
with
\|\bm{P}\|_{\mathrm{op}}=1
.
Therefore, for any
\bm{u}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,

\|\bm{u}\|_{\nabla^{2}\varphi(\bm{s})^{\dagger}}=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\;\big\|\bm{P}^{\dagger/2}\,\operatorname{diag}(\bm{\zeta})^{-1}\bm{u}\big\|_{2}.

Applying this to
u=h
and using the triangle inequality,

\|\bm{h}\|_{\nabla^{2}\varphi(\bm{s})^{\dagger}}\leq\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\Big(2\,\|\bm{P}^{\dagger/2}\bm{\beta}\|_{2}+\big\|\bm{P}^{\dagger/2}\,\operatorname{diag}(\bm{\zeta})^{-1}(\bm{\beta}\odot\bm{\beta})\big\|_{2}\Big).

Because
\bm{\beta}=\bm{W}^{1/2}\bm{A}\bm{z}
lies in the range of
\bm{P}
(so
\bm{P}^{\dagger}=\bm{P}
on that range),

\|\bm{P}^{\dagger/2}\bm{\beta}\|_{2}^{2}\;=\;\bm{\beta}^{\top}\bm{P}^{\dagger}\bm{\beta}\;=\;\bm{z}^{\top}\bm{L}_{\bm{s}}\bm{z}\;=\;\|\bm{z}\|_{\bm{L}_{\bm{s}}}^{2}.

For the quadratic term, use

\|\bm{P}^{\dagger/2}\operatorname{diag}(\bm{\zeta})^{-1}(\bm{\beta}\odot\bm{\beta})\|_{2}\leq\|\bm{P}^{\dagger/2}\bm{\beta}\|_{2}\cdot\max_{e}|\beta_{e}|/|\zeta_{e}|.

By Lemma 3 (Cauchy–Schwarz for voltage differences),

|\delta\Delta_{e}|\leq\sqrt{\varrho_{e}}\,\|\bm{z}\|_{\bm{L}_{\bm{s}}}
and
|\Delta_{e}|\leq\sqrt{\varrho_{e}}\,\|\bm{x}\|_{\bm{L}_{\bm{s}}}
,
so whenever
\zeta_{e}\neq 0
,

\frac{|\beta_{e}|}{|\zeta_{e}|}=\frac{\sqrt{w_{e}}\,|\delta\Delta_{e}|}{\sqrt{w_{e}}\,|\Delta_{e}|}\leq\frac{\|\bm{z}\|_{\bm{L}_{\bm{s}}}}{\|\bm{x}\|_{\bm{L}_{\bm{s}}}}.

Thus,

\|\bm{h}\|_{\nabla^{2}\varphi(\bm{s})^{\dagger}}\leq\Big(\sqrt{2}\,\frac{\|\bm{z}\|_{\bm{L}_{\bm{s}}}}{\|\bm{x}\|_{\bm{L}_{\bm{s}}}}+\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,\frac{\|\bm{z}\|_{\bm{L}_{\bm{s}}}^{2}}{\|\bm{x}\|_{\bm{L}_{\bm{s}}}^{2}}\Big)\,\|\bm{x}\|_{\bm{L}_{\bm{s}}}.

Finally, observe that

\|\nabla\varphi(\bm{s})\|_{\nabla^{2}\varphi(\bm{s})^{\dagger}}=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\|\bm{P}^{\dagger/2}\bm{\zeta}\|_{2}=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\|\bm{x}\|_{\bm{L}_{\bm{s}}}

(since
\bm{\zeta}=\bm{W}^{1/2}\bm{A}\bm{x}
).
With
\|\bm{z}\|_{\bm{L}_{\bm{s}}}\leq\varepsilon\|\bm{x}\|_{\bm{L}_{\bm{s}}}
this yields

\|h\|_{\nabla^{2}\varphi(\bm{s})^{\dagger}}\leq(2\varepsilon+\varepsilon^{2})\,\|\nabla\varphi(\bm{s})\|_{\nabla^{2}\varphi(\bm{s})^{\dagger}}\ \leq\ 3\varepsilon\,\|\nabla\varphi(\bm{s})\|_{\nabla^{2}\varphi(\bm{s})^{\dagger}}

for
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
, completing the proof.
∎

A.8
Proof of Theorem

Proof of Theorem
1
.

Our proof follows the general pattern of
[
47
]
and relies on explicit formulas for the derivatives of
\varphi
. First, recall the definition of a
(M,\nu)
–generalized self–concordant function from
[
8
, Definition 1.3]
: a closed convex function
f\in C^{3}(\mathrm{dom}(f))
with open domain is
(M,\nu)
–generalized self–concordant if for all
x
and all directions
u,w
,

\bigl|\langle D^{3}f(x)[w]u,u\rangle\bigr|\;\leq\;M\,\|u\|_{\nabla^{2}f(x)}^{2}\;\|w\|_{2}^{\nu-2}\,\|w\|_{2}^{3-\nu},

where
D^{3}f(x)[w]
is the third Fréchet derivative of
f
and
\|v\|_{\nabla^{2}f(x)}^{2}=v^{\top}\nabla^{2}f(x)\,v
is the local norm. Setting
\nu=2
yields the bound
M\,\|u\|_{\nabla^{2}f(x)}^{2}\,\|w\|_{2}
.

We now derive expressions for the derivatives of
\varphi
. Define
x=L(s)^{-1}d\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, which satisfies
L(s)x=d
and
x\perp\mathbf{1}
. Let
V(h)=A^{\top}\operatorname{diag}(\bm{w}\odot h)A
for
h\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
so that
DL(s)[h]=V(h)
. Differentiating
x
gives
Dx[s][h]=-L(s)^{-1}V(h)x
. A standard computation using these identities (see, e.g., our earlier derivation or
[
8
, Section 1]
) yields the first three directional derivatives of
\varphi
:

\displaystyle D\varphi[s][h]

\displaystyle=-\,x^{\top}V(h)\,x,

\displaystyle D^{2}\varphi[s][u,v]

\displaystyle=2\,x^{\top}V(u)\,L(s)^{-1}V(v)\,x,

\displaystyle D^{3}\varphi[s][w,u,u]

\displaystyle=-6\,x^{\top}V(u)\,L(s)^{-1}V(w)\,L(s)^{-1}V(u)\,x.

Since
V(h)
is symmetric, the second derivative can be written more compactly as
\nabla^{2}\varphi(s)=2\,\operatorname{diag}(\zeta)\,P\,\operatorname{diag}(\zeta)
, where
\zeta_{e}=\sqrt{w_{e}}\,a_{e}^{\top}x
and
P=\omega^{1/2}A\,L(s)^{-1}A^{\top}\omega^{1/2}
is an orthogonal projector (its square equals itself) as described in
[
8
]
. Therefore
\|u\|_{\nabla^{2}\varphi(s)}^{2}=2\,(\operatorname{diag}(\zeta)u)^{\top}P\,\operatorname{diag}(\zeta)u\geq 0
.

To bound the third derivative, we whiten the expression. Set
y=L(s)^{-1/2}V(u)x
and
B=L(s)^{-1/2}V(w)\,L(s)^{-1/2}
. The matrix
B
is symmetric positive semidefinite. With this notation,

D^{3}\varphi[s][w,u,u]\;=\;-6\,y^{\top}B\,y.

Since
\|y\|_{2}^{2}=y^{\top}y=x^{\top}V(u)\,L(s)^{-1}V(u)\,x=\tfrac{1}{2}\,u^{\top}\nabla^{2}\varphi(s)\,u
, we obtain

\bigl|D^{3}\varphi[s][w,u,u]\bigr|\;\leq\;6\,\|B\|_{\mathrm{op}}\,\|y\|_{2}^{2}\;=\;3\,\|B\|_{\mathrm{op}}\,\|u\|_{\nabla^{2}\varphi(s)}^{2}.

(15)

It remains to bound the operator norm
\|B\|_{\mathrm{op}}
uniformly over
s\in\mathcal{D}_{T}
. For any vector
y\perp\mathbf{1}
we have the Rayleigh quotient

\frac{y^{\top}V(w)\,y}{y^{\top}L(s)\,y}\;=\;\frac{\sum_{e=1}^{m}|w_{e}|\,w_{e}\,(a_{e}^{\top}y)^{2}}{y^{\top}L(s)\,y}.

Because
s_{e}=1
for all
e\in T
, one has
L(s)\succeq L_{T}
uniformly on
\mathcal{D}_{T}
. For every edge
e\in E
, Cauchy–Schwarz in the
L_{T}
–energy inner product yields

(a_{e}^{\top}y)^{2}\;=\;\langle L_{T}^{\dagger}a_{e},\,y\rangle_{L_{T}}^{2}\;\leq\;\|L_{T}^{\dagger}a_{e}\|_{L_{T}}^{2}\,\|y\|_{L_{T}}^{2}\;=\;\varrho_{T}(e)\,\bigl(y^{\top}L_{T}\,y\bigr)\;\leq\;\varrho_{T}(e)\,y^{\top}L(s)\,y.

Summing over
e
leads to

y^{\top}V(w)\,y\;\leq\;\Bigl(\sum_{e=1}^{m}|w_{e}|\,w_{e}\,\varrho_{T}(e)\Bigr)y^{\top}L(s)\,y\;\leq\;\bigl\|\bm{w}\odot\bm{\varrho}_{T}\bigr\|_{2}\cdot\|w\|_{2}\;y^{\top}L(s)\,y.

Taking the supremum over all
y\perp\mathbf{1}
gives

\bigl\|L(s)^{-1/2}V(w)\,L(s)^{-1/2}\bigr\|_{\mathrm{op}}\;\leq\;\bigl\|\bm{w}\odot\bm{\varrho}_{T}\bigr\|_{2}\;\|w\|_{2}

uniformly for all
s\in\mathcal{D}_{T}
. Substituting this bound into (
15
) yields

\bigl|D^{3}\varphi[s][w,u,u]\bigr|\;\leq\;3\,\bigl\|\bm{w}\odot\bm{\varrho}_{T}\bigr\|_{2}\,\|w\|_{2}\,\|u\|_{\nabla^{2}\varphi(s)}^{2},

which exactly matches the definition of
(M,\nu)
–generalized self–concordance with
\nu=2
and
M=3\|\bm{w}\odot\bm{\varrho}_{T}\|_{2}
. This bound is independent of
s
, completing the proof.
∎

t
by induction. The base case is true since
\bm{s}_{0}
is a feasible point. Now, for the induction step, suppose that
\sum_{e}s_{e}^{(t)}\leq q
. Then, at iteration
t+1
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{e}s_{e}^{(t+1)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{e}\left((1-\gamma_{t})s_{e}^{(t)}+\gamma_{t}v_{e}^{\star}(s^{(t)})\right)

\displaystyle=(1-\gamma_{t})\sum_{e}s_{e}^{(t)}+\gamma_{t}\sum_{e}v_{e}^{\star}(s^{(t)})

\displaystyle\leq(1-\gamma_{t})q+\gamma_{t}q

\displaystyle=q.

Thus, by induction, we have that
\sum_{e}s_{e}^{(t)}\leq q
for all
t
.

By Theorem
2
and the stated assumptions, we have

\varepsilon\leq\sqrt{\frac{3R\log\left((n-1)/\delta\right)}{\lambda_{2}(\bm{L}_{\bm{s}_{t}})}}\leq\sqrt{3R\log((n-1)/\delta)}\lesssim\sqrt{\log(n)/\delta},

where the second inequality is by assumption that
\lambda_{2}(\bm{L}_{\bm{s}_{0}})\geq 1
, so
\lambda_{2}(\bm{L}_{\bm{s}_{t}})\geq 1
for all
t\geq 1
, also.

Finally, note that each FW step costs one Laplacian solve + one
O(m)
pass over the edges + top-
q
selection, for
t=\Theta(q/\alpha)
iterations. Thus, the total running time is
\tilde{O}(mq/\alpha).
This completes the proof.
∎

Appendix B
Additional Analyses

In this section we present further analyses that we were unable to fit in the main submission.

B.1
Satisfying the budget with high probability

Here, we provide rigorous modifications of the Bernoulli parameters to ensure that the constraint
\left|\left|\bm{\tilde{s}}\right|\right|_{1}\leq q
is satisfied with high probability after rounding.

Proposition 1
.

Let
\bm{s}_{0}\in\left\{0,1\right\}^{m}
be a connected template and let
\bm{s}\in S_{q}(\bm{s}_{0})
be any feasible switching distribution. Fix a failure probability
\delta\in(0,1)
and
0<\gamma\leq q-\left|T\right|.
Define the shrinkage parameter

\theta:=\begin{cases}1&\mathsf{if}\ \sum_{e\in E\setminus T}s_{e}\leq q-\left|T\right|-\gamma\\
\frac{q-\left|T\right|-\gamma}{\sum_{e\notin T}s_{e}}&\operatorname{\sf otherwise},\end{cases}

and set
s_{e}^{\prime}=1
for
e\in T
and
s_{e}^{\prime}=\theta s_{e}
otherwise. Then, if
\gamma\geq\sqrt{2\left(q-\left|T\right|\right)\log(1/\delta)},
and we sample a rounded vector
\bm{\tilde{s}}\in\left\{0,1\right\}^{m}
as a vector Bernoulli random variables with
\left(\bm{\tilde{s}}\right)_{e}\sim\mathsf{Ber}(s_{e}^{\prime}),
we have that

\Pr\left(\left|\left|\bm{\tilde{s}}\right|\right|_{1}\leq q\right)\geq 1-\delta.

Proof.

Let
Y:=\sum_{e}\tilde{s}_{e}=\sum_{e\in T}\tilde{s}_{e}+\sum_{e\in E\setminus T}\tilde{s}_{e}.
For
e\in T,

s_{e}^{\prime}=1
, so
\sum_{e\in T}\tilde{s}_{e}=\left|T\right|.
Moreover, for
e\notin T
,
\operatorname{E}\tilde{s}_{e}=s_{e}^{\prime}
, and by construction
\mu:=\operatorname{E}\left[\sum_{e\notin T}\tilde{s}_{e}\right]=\sum_{e\notin T}s_{e}^{\prime}\leq q-\left|T\right|-\gamma.
Apply the scalar Bernstein inequality to
S:=\sum_{e\notin T}\left(\tilde{s}_{e}-\operatorname{E}\tilde{s}_{e}\right),
and, for any
t>0
, we have

\Pr\left(S\geq t\right)\leq\exp\left(\frac{-t^{2}}{2\left(\mu+t/3\right)}\right).

With
t=\gamma,
we get

\Pr\left(Y\geq\left|T\right|+\mu+\gamma\right)\leq\exp\left(-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2\left(\mu+\gamma/3\right)}\right)\leq\exp\left(-\frac{3\gamma^{2}}{8\left(q-\left|T\right|\right)}\right),

where the final inequality is due to the fact that
\mu\leq q-\left|T\right|-\gamma.
Choose
\gamma\geq\sqrt{2\left(q-\left|T\right|\right)\log(1/\delta)}\implies\mu+\gamma/3\leq q-\left|T\right|
to get the bound
\Pr\left(Y\geq\left|T\right|+\mu+\gamma\right)\leq\exp\left(-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2\left(q-\left|T\right|\right)}\right).
That is, choosing
\gamma\geq\sqrt{2\left(q-\left|T\right|\right)\log(1/\delta)}
yields
\Pr\left(Y\geq q\right)\leq\delta.

∎

B.2
Alternative complexity analysis in
\ell_{1\to\infty}
space

The runtime analysis in Appendix
A.12
yields the optimal asymptotic rate; we can achieve the same asymptotic complexity in a different geometry, which may have certain advantages. For a fixed budget
q\in\mathbb{N}_{+},
define the norm

\left|\left|\cdot\right|\right|_{\hexagon}:=\max\left\{\left|\left|\cdot\right|\right|_{1},\,q\left|\left|\cdot\right|\right|_{\infty}\right\};

with corresponding norm ball
\mathcal{B}_{\hexagon}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}
as

\mathcal{B}_{\hexagon}:=\left\{\bm{u}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\;:\;\left|\left|\bm{u}\right|\right|_{\hexagon}\leq 1\right\}=\left\{\bm{u}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\;:\;\max\left\{\left|\left|\bm{u}\right|\right|_{1},\;q\left|\left|\bm{u}\right|\right|_{\infty}\right\}\leq 1\right\}.

This family of norms describes a polytopal geometry
[
12
]
; a subset of the unit hypercube with cut corners, which is related to Eulerian numbers.

Proposition 2
.

Let
\bm{u}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, and let
\left|u\right|_{[1]}\geq\left|u\right|_{[2]}\geq\dots\geq\left|u\right|_{[m]}
denote the magnitude of the entries of
\bm{u}
sorted in non-increasing order. For a fixed
q\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
, the dual norm of
\left|\left|\cdot\right|\right|_{\hexagon}
is given as

\left|\left|\bm{u}\right|\right|_{\hexagon,\star}=\sup_{\left|\left|\bm{y}\right|\right|_{1}\leq 1,\;\left|\left|\bm{y}\right|\right|_{\infty}\leq 1/q}\;\left\langle\bm{u},\bm{y}\right\rangle=\frac{1}{q}\sum_{i\in q}\left|u\right|_{[i]},

i.e., the average of the
q
largest absolute coordinates.

Proof.

By definition, for any
\bm{u}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
the dual norm of
\left|\left|\cdot\right|\right|_{\hexagon}
is

\left|\left|\bm{u}\right|\right|_{\star}:=\sup_{\bm{y}:\left|\left|\bm{y}\right|\right|_{\hexagon}\leq 1}\;\left\langle\bm{y},\bm{u}\right\rangle.

This is equivalent to the constrained optimization problem

\left|\left|\bm{u}\right|\right|_{\hexagon,\star}=\sup_{\bm{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}\;\left\langle\bm{y},\bm{u}\right\rangle\quad\operatorname{\sf s.t.}\quad\sum_{k=1}^{m}y_{k}\leq 1,\quad 0\leq y_{k}\leq 1/q,\ k=1,\ldots,m.

This is knapsack problem with identical bounds; the optimal solution is clearly
y_{i}=1/q
for indices
i=1,\ldots,q
corresponding to
\left|u\right|_{[1]}\geq\left|u\right|_{[2]}\geq\ldots\geq\left|u\right|_{[q]}.

∎

Now, we relate our choice of norm to
\left|\left|\cdot\right|\right|_{2}
.

Lemma 8
.

We have

\left|\left|\bm{u}\right|\right|_{\hexagon,\star}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{q}}\left|\left|\bm{u}\right|\right|_{2}.

Proof.

By Cauchy-Schwarz,

\left|\left|\bm{u}\right|\right|_{\hexagon,\star}=\frac{1}{q}\sum_{i=1}^{q}\left|u\right|_{[i]}=\frac{1}{q}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left|u\right|_{[i]}\cdot\mathds{1}\left\{i\leq q\right\}=\frac{1}{q}\left\langle\bm{1}_{i\leq q},\bm{u}\circ\operatorname{sign}(\bm{u})\right\rangle\overset{(1)}{\leq}\frac{1}{\sqrt{q}}\left|\left|\bm{u}\right|\right|_{2},

where in step (1) we applied norm equivalence, namely
\left|\left|\bm{u}\right|\right|_{1}\leq\sqrt{m}\left|\left|\bm{u}\right|\right|_{2}
for
\bm{u}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}.

∎

We immediately yield the following result.

Lemma 9
.

Fix an integer
q\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
, and a connected template
\bm{s}_{0}
. Define

S_{q}(\bm{s}_{0})=\left\{\bm{s}\in\left[0,1\right]^{m}\;\left|\left|\bm{s}\right|\right|_{1}\leq q,\;s_{e}=1\;\forall e\in\mathsf{supp}(\bm{s}_{0})\right\}.

Assume that
\varphi(\cdot)
is
L
-smooth with respect to
\left|\left|\cdot\right|\right|_{2}
. Then, for fixed demands
\bm{d}\perp\bm{1}
, the relaxed congestion
\varphi(\bm{s})=\bm{d}^{\top}\bm{L}_{\bm{s}}^{\dagger}\bm{d}
is
\beta
-smooth w.r.t.
\left|\left|\cdot\right|\right|_{\hexagon}
, i.e.,

\left|\left|\nabla\varphi(\bm{s})-\nabla\varphi(\bm{s}^{\prime})\right|\right|_{\hexagon,\star}\leq\beta\left|\left|\bm{s}-\bm{s}^{\prime}\right|\right|_{\hexagon}\qquad\forall\bm{s},\bm{s}^{\prime}\in S_{q}(\bm{s}_{0}),

with

\beta\leq L/q.

Proof.

By applying Lemma
8
twice, we have

\displaystyle\left|\left|\nabla\varphi(\bm{s})-\nabla\varphi(\bm{s}^{\prime})\right|\right|_{\hexagon,\star}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{q}}\left|\left|\nabla\varphi(\bm{s})-\nabla\varphi(\bm{s}^{\prime})\right|\right|_{2}\leq\frac{L}{\sqrt{q}}\left|\left|\bm{s}-\bm{s}^{\prime}\right|\right|_{2}\leq\frac{L}{q}\left|\left|\bm{s}-\bm{s}^{\prime}\right|\right|_{\hexagon}.

This completes the proof.
∎

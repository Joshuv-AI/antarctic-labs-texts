Integrated bulk and single-cell transcriptomics identifies shared and specific immune signatures in Takayasu Arteritis
Authors: Kexin Xu, Taotao Li, Na Gao, Yaxin Zhang, Yi Yang, Honglei Zhao, Longfei Wang, Junming Zhu, Jian Liu, Lili Pan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundTakayasu arteritis (TAK) is a rare, chronic large-vessel vasculitis. Although CD4⁺ and CD8⁺ T cells are acknowledged drivers of vascular injury in TAK, the gene networks that confer their pathogenicity remain incompletely mapped.ObjectivesThis study aimed to integrate bulk RNA-sequencing of peripheral-blood T-cell subsets with single-cell RNA-sequencing of aortic tissue to find mechanistic biomarkers and therapeutic targets for TAK.MethodsWe performed bulk RNA-sequencing on peripheral-blood CD4⁺ and CD8⁺ T cells from eight treatment-naïve TAK patients and three age matched healthy controls. In parallel, single-cell RNA-sequencing was applied to aortic tissue from three additional TAK patients and three atherosclerotic controls. DEGs were defined at false-discovery rate < 0.05. Functional enrichment used Gene Ontology, KEGG and Reactome. STRING constructed protein–protein interaction networks, and Cell Chat inferred intercellular ligand–receptor communication.ResultsBulk profiling identified 851 DEGs in CD4⁺ and 1 645 DEGs in CD8⁺ T cells. CD4⁺ DEGs were enriched for inflammation, angiogenesis and platelet-activation pathways; CD8⁺ DEGs concentrated on cytokine synthesis, notably interleukin-1 signaling. Both subsets shared enrichment in complement cascade, focal adhesion and extracellular-matrix organization, indicating convergent pro-inflammatory programs. Single-cell analyses delineated dense CD4⁺– CD8⁺ crosstalk within TAK aorta and, relative to atherosclerotic controls, heat-shock protein binding and ubiquitin-ligase activity—hallmarks of heightened protein-homeostasis stress. Four transcriptional regulators—EGR1, KLF4, RHOB and ATF3—were consistently up-regulated in both blood and tissue; EGR1 showed the strongest fold-change and occupied a central hub in protein-interaction and ligand–receptor networks. In peripheral cells EGR1 co-clustered with cytokine-biosynthetic modules, while in tissue its profile mirrored the composite CD4⁺ and CD8⁺ signature, underscoring a unifying role in systemic and local inflammation.ConclusionsIntegrated bulk and single-cell transcriptomics reveal both shared and subset-specific signaling landscapes for CD4⁺ and CD8⁺ T cells in TAK. The consistent prominence of EGR1 across compartments nominates this factor as a pivotal molecular switch and attractive therapeutic target. These data furnish a mechanistic framework for precision immune modulation in large-vessel vasculitis.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13075-025-03673-x.

Introduction

Takayasu Arteritis (TAK) is a chronic, rare inflammatory disease that primarily affects the aorta and its branches [1]. This disease predominantly affects Asian women under the age of 40, with an incidence rate of 40 per 100,000 in Japan and 10.7 per 100,000 in Turkey [2]. There is no specific data on the incidence of TAK in China. TAK leads to vascular wall narrowing, fibrosis, occlusion, and may progress to aneurysm formation [3]. In addition, many TAK patients exhibit coronary artery involvement, and myocardial ischemia is the leading cause of death in these patients [4]. Currently, glucocorticoids are the first-line treatment for TAK, but their use is often limited due to the increased risk of diseases such as diabetes and osteoporosis [5]. Therefore, understanding the potential pathogenesis of TAK and finding more effective treatment strategies has become an urgent task.

In TAK patients, a large number of immune cells, particularly T cells and macrophages, have been observed infiltrating the media and adventitia of the affected vessels [6, 7]. Among these, CD4+ T cells and CD8+ T cells make up the highest proportion in the lesion tissues [8]. CD4+ T cells can differentiate into multiple subtypes, such as Th1 and Th17, which play crucial roles in the immune response and inflammation processes in TAK [9]. These subtypes produce pro-inflammatory cytokines such as IFN-γ and IL-17, which are significantly elevated in both the serum and aortic tissues of patients [10]. Apart from CD4+ T cells, CD8+ T cells also play an important role in TAK. They kill target cells directly by secreting perforin and granzyme, further contributing to tissue damage. Previous studies from our group have confirmed that granzyme B expression is significantly elevated in CD8+ T cells in TAK patients and decreases significantly after treatment, correlating with a reduction in disease activity [11]. Moreover, CD8+ T cells are closely related to the recurrence of TAK [12].

Although the role of T cells in TAK is somewhat understood, research on specific gene pathways related to CD4+ T cells and CD8+ T cells is still limited. Some studies have identified the JAK/STAT pathway as one of the important pathways in the pathogenesis of TAK. Inhibition of this pathway can reduce Th1, Th17, and related inflammatory factors [13]. Additionally, this inhibitor can also lower CRP levels, NIH scores, and glucocorticoid dosage in patients [14].

Apart from the JAK/STAT signaling pathway, studies focusing on other key genes and associated pathways involved in TAK remain relatively limited. In this study, we integrated bulk RNA sequencing and single-cell RNA sequencing to comprehensively analyze the characteristics of CD4⁺ T cells and CD8⁺ T cells in both peripheral blood and aortic tissues from TAK patients and healthy individuals. The objective was to identify genes and signaling pathways that are significantly upregulated in TAK, and to further compare the shared and distinct molecular features of CD4⁺ and CD8⁺ T cells in the context of TAK.

Methods

Patients and ethic approval

Our study enrolled ten patients with TAK who were treated at Anzhen Hospital, Capital Medical University, and one patient treated at Aerospace Center Hospital. All patients fulfilled the 2022 classification criteria for TAK established by the American College of Rheumatology (ACR) [15]. At the same time, the study also included 3 healthy individuals and 3 atherosclerosis patients for comparison. Peripheral blood CD4 + T cells and CD8 + T cells from 8 TAK patients and 3 healthy controls underwent Bulk RNA sequencing. Additionally, aortic tissues from another 3 TAK patients and 3 atherosclerosis patients were subjected to single-cell sequencing. Transcriptomic profiling of CD4 + T cells and CD8 + T cells

Whole blood was drawn from eight patients diagnosed with TAK and three matched healthy donors, with heparin applied to prevent coagulation. CD4⁺ and CD8⁺ T lymphocyte subsets were enriched using a magnetic bead-based isolation technique. For bulk RNA-seq, libraries were constructed using the NEBNext® Ultra™ Directional RNA Library Prep Kit for Illumina (NEB, USA) according to the manufacturer’s protocol, generating strand-specific RNA-seq libraries. Sequencing was performed on the Illumina NovaSeq platform, producing paired-end 150 bp reads. For each sample, an average of about 43 million clean reads (21–23 million read pairs) was obtained after quality control, with a range of 38.5–45.9 million clean reads across samples, corresponding to 5.8–6.9 Gb of high-quality data per sample. Sequencing quality was consistently high, with Q20 ≥ 96% and Q30 ≥ 92% for all libraries. Reads were then aligned to the human genome using HISAT2 (v2.0.5), transcripts were assembled with StringTie (v1.3.3b), and read counts were quantified with featureCounts (v1.5.0-p3). Differential expression analysis was performed with DESeq2 (v1.20.0) or edgeR (v3.22.5), with multiple testing correction by the Benjamini–Hochberg method [16].

Single-Cell transcriptomic analysis

For scRNA-seq, aortic tissues from three TAK patients and three individuals with atherosclerosis were enzymatically digested to obtain single-cell suspensions. Following red blood cell lysis and cell quality assessment, dead cells were removed when necessary, and viable cells were resuspended in RPMI-1640 supplemented with 2% FBS. Single-cell RNA-seq libraries were then prepared using the SeekOne MM kit. Cells and barcoded magnetic beads were loaded into microwells, where RNA was captured, reverse-transcribed, and converted into barcoded cDNA. Libraries were subsequently amplified, purified, quality-checked, and sequenced on the Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform (paired-end 150 bp). Quality control involved filtering cells (< 200 or > 6000 detected genes, > 10% mitochondrial reads) and doublet removal with DoubletFinder (5% expected rate). Downstream analyses were performed in R (v4.4.0) with Seurat (v5.3.0), including LogNormalize, selection of the top 2000 variable genes (vst), PCA (30 PCs), clustering (Louvain algorithm, resolutions 0.3/0.5), and UMAP visualization. Differentially expressed genes were identified by the Wilcoxon rank-sum test with Benjamini–Hochberg correction, and cell types were annotated using canonical markers with reference to the CellMarker database.

Differential gene expression and pathway enrichment analysis

For the selection of DEGs in bulk RNA-seq, we applied thresholds of p-value < 0.05 and |log2FC| ≥ 1.0, whereas subsequent pathway enrichment analyses were performed using padj < 0.05 with FDR correction. In the subsequent single-cell transcriptomic analysis, given the sufficient cell numbers and higher statistical power, we applied the Wilcoxon rank-sum test with Benjamini–Hochberg correction, using padj < 0.05 and |log2FC| ≥ 0.25 as the criteria for defining DEGs.

DEGs were subjected to functional enrichment analysis using Gene Ontology (GO), Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG), and the Reactome pathway database. Analyses were performed in R(v4.4.0) with the packages clusterProfiler (v4.12.6), Reactome (v1.40.8), DOSE (v3.30.5), and org.Hs.eg.db (v3.19.1). GO enrichment was conducted separately for Biological Process (BP), Molecular Function (MF) categories and Cellular Component(CC). KEGG and Reactome enrichment analyses were performed based on Entrez gene IDs converted from gene symbols. The Benjamini–Hochberg method was used to adjust for multiple testing, with p.adjust < 0.05 considered significant. Visualization of enriched terms and pathways was generated using the enrichplot and ggplot2 packages.

Construction and analysis of the Protein-Protein Interaction (PPI) network

The STRING database is a comprehensive resource designed to aggregate, assess, and synthesize publicly available PPI datasets, while also incorporating computationally predicted interactions. Cytoscape, a robust network analysis tool, enables the visualization and interpretation of biological networks in a two-dimensional (2D) format. In this investigation, the STRING database was employed to construct a PPI network to identify crucial regulatory genes with a confidence score exceeding 0.4. We specifically concentrated on the overlapping subset of hub genes linked to clinical phenotypes, as determined through DEGs. This intersected gene set was then uploaded into the STRING database to assess the interrelationship between gene expression profiles and their corresponding protein interactions. The final network analysis was conducted using Cytoscape, facilitating the identification of candidate hub genes for further investigation.

Results

Differential gene expression in CD4+ and CD8+ T cells of Takayasu Arteritis patients reveals key immune response regulators

We performed Bulk-RNA sequencing analysis on CD4+ and CD8+ T cells from 8 TAK patients and 3 healthy controls, revealing significant differentially expressed genes. The results showed that, compared to the healthy control group, 851 genes were significantly differentially expressed in CD4+ T cells from the TAK group, with 708 genes upregulated and 143 genes downregulated (Fig. 1A). In CD8+ T cells, a total of 1645 genes were significantly differentially expressed, with 1130 genes upregulated and 515 genes downregulated (Fig. 1B).

Further analysis revealed the top 10 significantly upregulated genes in CD4+ T cells, which were XIST, KLF10, TP53INP2, PRKAR2B, EGR1, KLF6, MIR181A1HG, FOS, ADM, and KLF11. For CD8+ T cells, the top 10 significantly upregulated genes were AC245128.3, AQP9, ATF3, C4orf50, HLA-DQB1-AS1, DUSP5, MGAM, SLC24A4, S100A8, and DENND3.

We then performed protein-protein interaction network analysis for the differential genes in CD4+ T cells and CD8+ T cells. In CD4+ T cells, the most interacting proteins were FOS, IFNG, and EGR1 (Fig. 1C). In CD8+ T cells, the most interacting proteins were SYK, FCGR3B, FVGR3A, TYROBP, and ITGAM (Fig. 1D).

Distinct GO enrichment profiles of CD4+ and CD8+ T cells in Takayasu Arteritis highlight their roles in immune regulation, chemokine production, and platelet interaction

Next, we performed GO pathway enrichment analysis on the Bulk RNA results from CD4+ and CD8+ T cells of TAK patients. The top 10 enriched pathways for BP, MF, and CC were summarized. For BP, CD4+ T cells were most significantly enriched in processes related to the regulation of the inflammatory response. They were also highly enriched in processes related to the response to extracellular stimuli, including responses to antibodies, oxidative substances, and hydrogen peroxide (Fig. 2A). Additionally, BP enrichment results indicated that CD4+ T cells in TAK patients were closely associated with angiogenesis processes. For CC, CD4+ T cells were primarily localized to platelet α-granules and α-granule lumen (Fig. 2B). In MF, CD4+ T cells in TAK patients were significantly enriched in pathways related to cytokines, chemokines, and antibodies, such as cytokine activity, platelet-derived growth factor receptor binding, and CXCR chemokine receptor binding (Fig. 2C).

The GO enrichment results for CD8+ T cells were somewhat different from those for CD4+ T cells. In BP, CD8+ T cells were most significantly enriched in the leukocyte differentiation process. We also found that CD8+ T cells in TAK patients were primarily involved in pathways related to cytokine synthesis, chemokine production, and metabolism, such as IL-1 production and chemokine generation (Fig. 2D). For CC, CD8+ T cells were predominantly concentrated in membrane-related structures and secretory granules, such as secretory granule membranes, tertiary granules, and endocytic vesicles (Fig. 2E). In MF, CD8+ T cells were significantly enriched in pathways related to substance binding and cytokine binding, including binding to actin, Wnt proteins, integrins, and growth factors (Fig. 2F).

Shared and distinct KEGG pathways enriched in CD4+ and CD8+ T cells from Takayasu Arteritis patients

We conducted KEGG pathway enrichment analysis on the Bulk RNA sequencing results of CD4+ T cells from TAK patients, selecting the top 20 most significantly enriched pathways (Fig. 3A). The results showed that CD4+ T cells were significantly enriched in several important pathways related to immune responses, signal transduction, and cell function. The pathways closely associated with inflammatory responses and immune regulation included complement and coagulation cascades, cytokine-cytokine receptor interactions, chemokine signaling pathways, and TNF signaling pathways. Pathways related to signal transduction included the Rap1 signaling pathway, MAPK signaling pathway, and AGE-RAGE signaling pathway. In terms of cell function, CD4+ T cells were involved in processes such as cytoskeleton regulation, platelet activation, and cell adhesion.

Similarly, we also analyzed the top 20 KEGG pathways enriched in CD8+ T cells from TAK patients (Fig. 3B). Interestingly, there were 9 pathways that were significantly enriched in both CD4+ and CD8+ T cells, including complement and coagulation cascades, focal adhesion, Leishmania infection, and neutrophil extracellular trap formation. Additionally, CD8+ T cells were primarily involved in pathways related to cell adhesion, such as cell adhesion molecules, focal adhesion, and ECM receptor activation. CD8+ T cells were also enriched in cardiovascular disease-related pathways, such as hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, dilated cardiomyopathy, and lipid and atherosclerosis pathways.

Reactome pathway enrichment analysis reveals CD4+ and CD8+ T cell involvement in platelet activation and immune signaling in Takayasu Arteritis

We then performed Reactome pathway enrichment analysis on the Bulk RNA sequencing results for CD4+ and CD8+ T cells. The top 20 enriched pathways for CD4+ T cells were primarily related to platelets and inflammatory cytokines. These included “platelet activation, signaling, and aggregation,” “platelet degranulation,” “complement activation,” “interleukin 4 and interleukin 13 signaling pathways,” and “chemokine receptor binding” (Fig. 3C). In contrast, CD8+ T cells were primarily enriched in “neutrophil degranulation,” “extracellular matrix organization,” “cell-cell interactions at the vascular wall,” “non-integrin membrane-ECM interactions,” “ECM proteoglycans,” and “TLR signaling regulating endogenous ligands” (Fig. 3D).

Distinct immune cell interactions involving CD4⁺ and CD8⁺ T cells characterize TAK aortic lesions

We performed scRNA-seq analysis on cells derived from the aortic tissue of patients with TAK and from atherosclerosis donor aortic tissue. Initially, unsupervised clustering analysis was conducted to identify distinct cell populations (Fig. 4A). Subsequently, cell type annotation was performed based on reference to two authoritative databases—Monaco Immune Data and Human Primary Cell Atlas. A total of eight cell types, closely associated with the pathogenesis of TAK, were identified (Fig. 4B), including CD4⁺ T cells, CD8⁺ T cells, B cells, fibroblasts, endothelial cells, macrophages, dendritic cells, and smooth muscle cells.We observed slight variations in the number of cell types across different samples; however, the overall distribution patterns of the major cell populations, such as smooth muscle cells, fibroblasts, and endothelial cells, remained largely consistent among samples (Fig. 4C).

Based on these findings, we further performed cell-cell interaction analyses focusing on CD4⁺ and CD8⁺ T cells within the TAK group. The interaction network analysis demonstrated that CD4⁺ T cells exhibited the strongest interactions with CD8⁺ T cells (Fig. 4D), suggesting potential crosstalk and synergistic involvement of these T cell subsets in the immune response of TAK. In addition, CD4⁺ T cells showed notable interactions with macrophages, B cells, dendritic cells, and endothelial cells, with macrophages displaying particularly prominent interactions. Similarly, interaction analysis of CD8⁺ T cells revealed strong communication with both macrophages and endothelial cells. Furthermore, interactions were also observed between CD8⁺ T cells and B cells, dendritic cells, as well as CD4⁺ T cells (Fig. 4E). Notably, we found no significant interactions between either CD4⁺ or CD8⁺ T cells and fibroblasts or smooth muscle cells in TAK patients.

Single-cell transcriptomic analysis reveals immune and metabolic pathway enrichment in CD4⁺ and CD8⁺ T cells from aortic tissues in TAK

Subsequently, GO functional categorization and KEGG pathway enrichment analyses were performed on DEGs in CD4⁺ and CD8⁺ T cells isolated from aortic tissues of TAK patients and atherosclerosis controls.

In the BP category, DEGs in CD4⁺ T cells were significantly enriched in multiple immune-related pathways (Fig. 5A), including immune response activation, T cell differentiation, and lymphocyte differentiation. Additionally, these genes were enriched in pathways involved in protein homeostasis regulation, post-translational modification, and ribonucleoprotein complex biogenesis. In the CC category (Fig. 5B), CD4⁺ T cell DEGs were predominantly enriched in structural components associated with RNA metabolism, protein synthesis, and mitochondrial function, such as cytoplasmic ribonucleoprotein granule, ribosome, and respirasome. Moreover, enrichment was also observed in structures related to cell adhesion and migration, including focal adhesion and cell-substrate junction. In the MF category (Fig. 5C), DEGs were primarily enriched in functions such as structural constituents of ribosomes, heat shock protein binding, ubiquitin protein ligase binding, and protein folding, implicating roles in protein synthesis, stress response, and transcriptional regulation. Enrichment in cadherin binding and other adhesion-related functions further suggests that CD4⁺ T cells may undergo altered regulation of protein homeostasis and cellular interactions in TAK.

A similar enrichment pattern was observed for CD8⁺ T cells. In the BP category (Fig. 5D), CD8⁺ T cell DEGs were also significantly enriched in pathways closely related to immune regulation and RNA/protein processing, including T cell differentiation, RNA splicing, translation, post-translational modification, and ribonucleoprotein complex biogenesis. In the CC category (Fig. 5E), DEGs were most significantly enriched at the cytoplasmic side of the membrane and were also enriched in components related to protein synthesis, ribosomal structures, and cell adhesion, such as ribosome, ribonucleoprotein granule, cell-substrate junction, and focal adhesion. In the MF category (Fig. 5F), CD8⁺ T cell DEGs were enriched in structural constituents of ribosomes, ubiquitin protein ligase binding, protein folding, and heat shock protein binding. Additionally, enrichment was noted in transcriptional corepressor activity, histone modification, and cadherin binding, indicating potential involvement in transcriptional regulation and cell adhesion processes relevant to the pathogenesis of TAK.

Based on the KEGG pathway enrichment analysis, DEGs in CD4⁺ T cells were significantly enriched in multiple infection- and immune-related pathways (Fig. 5G), including Coronavirus disease (COVID-19), T cell receptor signaling, Th17 cell differentiation, and Th1/Th2 cell differentiation, suggesting their potential involvement in dysregulated immune responses in TAK. In addition, enrichment was also observed in ribosome and oxidative phosphorylation pathways, indicating alterations in protein synthesis and cellular metabolism. The KEGG enrichment profile of CD8⁺ T cells showed partial overlap with that of CD4⁺ T cells, with significant enrichment in infection- and immunity-related pathways (Fig. 5H), such as the T cell receptor signaling pathway, HIV infection, and HTLV-1 infection. Moreover, DEGs in CD8⁺ T cells were also enriched in pathways related to cytoskeletal regulation, ubiquitin-mediated proteolysis, and ribosome function, highlighting their potential roles in immune modulation and metabolic processes in TAK.

Common differentially expressed genes in CD4+ and CD8+ T cells between TAK patients and healthy controls: insights from bulk RNA and single-cell sequencing analysis

Based on the Bulk-RNA data, we further analyzed the top 300 most significantly differentially expressed genes in CD4+ and CD8+ T cells of TAK patients compared to healthy controls. We further validated these findings by analyzing single-cell sequencing data of arterial tissue from TAK patients and healthy controls. The results showed that, except for CPNE5, ITGA2B, RELT, and NOG, the other 22 genes that were differentially expressed in peripheral blood also exhibited significant differential expression in tissue, confirming our Bulk-RNA findings. Among these, ATF3, EGR1, RHOB, and KLF4 were significantly highly expressed in the arterial tissue of TAK patients compared to normal controls (Fig. 6C-F). Notably, PDGFRB, SASH1, and SAMD4A showed inverse expression patterns; they were upregulated in the peripheral blood of TAK patients, but in tissue single-cell sequencing, their expression was significantly higher in healthy controls (Supplementary Fig. 1).

Further analysis revealed that among the four genes—ATF3, EGR1, RHOB, and KLF4—only EGR1 exhibited significantly differential expression in both CD4⁺ and CD8⁺ T cells from arterial tissues (Fig. 7A–B). In contrast, the other three genes did not show significant differential expression in either cell type.

We also performed GO-BP pathway enrichment analysis on the common differentially expressed genes ATF3, EGR1, RHOB, and KLF4 in CD4+ and CD8+ T cells of TAK patients. In TAK patients, KLF4 was commonly enriched in pathways related to inflammation and angiogenesis in both CD4+ and CD8+ T cells (Fig. 8A). ATF3 was enriched in ERK1 and ERK2 cascade reactions and regulation (Fig. 8B). EGR1 was enriched in leukocyte differentiation、cytokine generation pathways and T cell differention patyways (Fig. 8C). In the cytokine generation pathway, EGR1 was an upstream regulator of KLF4.

Subsequently, based on the single-cell sequencing-derived DEGs, we investigated the functional enrichment of EGR1 in arterial tissues of TAK patients. Notably, the enriched pathways associated with EGR1 showed a high degree of overlap with the GO enrichment profiles of both CD4⁺ and CD8⁺ T cells.

For CD4⁺ T cells, EGR1-related genes were significantly enriched in multiple immune-regulatory and T cell function-related BP (Fig. 9A). In the CC category (Fig. 9B), these genes were predominantly localized to the ribosome, cell adhesion structures, and ribonucleoprotein granules. In the MF category (Fig. 9C), EGR1-associated genes were enriched in functions such as ribosomal structural constituents, transcription factor binding, histone modification, and protein folding, indicating involvement in transcriptional regulation and stress response.

In CD8⁺ T cells, EGR1-related genes were similarly enriched in immune-related and T cell differentiation pathways within the BP category (Fig. 9D), including the immune response–activating signaling pathway, T cell receptor signaling, lymphocyte differentiation, as well as RNA and protein processing pathways such as cytoplasmic translation and RNA splicing. Discussion

In this study, we first performed Bulk-RNA sequencing to analyze the differential gene expression profiles and related pathway enrichment in CD4⁺ and CD8⁺ T cells from the peripheral blood of patients with TAK compared with healthy controls. Subsequently, single-cell RNA sequencing was employed to further investigate the differential gene expression and pathway enrichment of CD4⁺ and CD8⁺ T cells within the aortic tissues of TAK patients and those with atherosclerosis. We observed some signaling pathways in CD4⁺ and CD8⁺ T cells from peripheral blood and aortic tissues of TAK patients. In peripheral blood, both T cell subsets were predominantly associated with cytokine and chemokine production pathways. In contrast, within aortic tissues, these cells were additionally enriched in pathways related to protein modification and molecular binding processes. Notably, EGR1 was consistently and significantly upregulated in both CD4⁺ and CD8⁺ T cells across peripheral blood and vascular tissue. Furthermore, in aortic tissues, EGR1 was involved in multiple critical pathways in both T cell subsets. These findings highlight EGR1 as a potential central regulator in the immunopathogenesis of TAK and nominate it as a promising therapeutic target for future intervention strategies. In the Bulk-RNA analysis of peripheral blood, we found that CD4⁺ T cells from TAK patients exhibited a strong association with platelets, showing significant enrichment in platelet-related structures and functions such as “platelet activation, signaling, and aggregation” and “platelet degranulation.” Platelets are known to play a critical role in the pathogenesis of various autoimmune diseases, including systemic lupus erythematosus, systemic sclerosis, and rheumatoid arthritis [17, 18]. They store a large number of cytokines and chemokines, such as IL-1β and CXCL4, within their α-granules and dense granules, contributing strongly to the inflammatory response [19, 20]. Moreover, platelets are a major source of circulating TGF-β, a cytokine closely associated with the progression of tissue fibrosis [21]. Our previous work has identified elevated platelet counts as a potential predictor of refractory TAK [22]. The findings of the present study further suggest that during the pathogenesis of TAK, CD4⁺ T cells may exacerbate vascular wall inflammation and promote fibrotic progression through interactions with platelets. In addition, CD4 + T cells were linked to the production of inflammatory cytokines such as IL-4 and IL-13. IL-4 induces Th0 cells to differentiate into Th2 cells, with a positive feedback loop leading to increased IL-4 production, while IL-13 has a similar function [23]. Our previous research also found that serum IL-4 and IL-13 levels were significantly higher in TAK patients compared to healthy controls, with a notable increase in peripheral Th2 cell populations [24]. These findings indicate that Th2 cells may play an important role in TAK. In terms of specific signaling pathways, CD4 + T cells were mainly enriched in the Rap-1, MAPK, and AGE-RAGE pathways. Research has shown that the Rap-1 pathway can promote integrin activation on CD4 + T cells, thereby enhancing their adhesion [25], which may be critical in TAK pathogenesis. The MAPK pathway promotes CD4 + T cell differentiation into Th1 cells [26]. For CD8 + T cells, we found that these cells were mainly enriched in pathways related to leukocyte differentiation. Regarding inflammatory factors, CD8 + T cells were associated with the production of IL-1β. IL-1β has a strong pro-inflammatory effect, inducing the production of a large number of cytokines and promoting interactions between endothelial cells and leukocytes [27]. Additionally, IL-1β can promote the migration and proliferation of vascular smooth muscle cells, leading to vascular narrowing and restricted blood flow [28]. However, in the context of TAK, IL-6 and IL-17 have been more widely studied and recognized for their roles in inflammation and fibrosis [29, 30], while studies on IL-1β are less frequent, suggesting that IL-1β could represent a novel avenue for research. Subsequently, through single-cell RNA sequencing analysis of aortic tissues from TAK patients, we observed that CD4⁺ T cells exhibited the strongest interaction with CD8⁺ T cells, while CD8⁺ T cells showed more prominent interactions with macrophages and endothelial cells. Although direct evidence of CD4⁺ T cell–CD8⁺ T cell interactions in TAK patients has not yet been reported, studies in other autoimmune diseases have demonstrated that CD4⁺ T cells can promote the proliferation and differentiation of CD8⁺ T cells through the secretion of cytokines such as IL-2 and through co-stimulatory interactions including CD40L–CD40 signaling [31]. Whether a similar interaction occurs in TAK requires further investigation. The interaction between CD8⁺ T cells and macrophages in TAK has been previously confirmed. CD8⁺ T cells are recognized as a major source of interferon-gamma (IFN-γ) in TAK patients, and IFN-γ, as a potent pro-inflammatory cytokine, can activate macrophages and enhance their inflammatory responses [32]. Additionally, CD8⁺ T cells can exert cytotoxic effects on macrophages and other vascular wall cells through the secretion of perforin and granzymes, thereby contributing to vascular injury [33]. Our findings provide novel evidence supporting the interaction between CD4⁺ and CD8⁺ T cells in TAK. The pathways enriched in differentially expressed genes of CD4⁺ and CD8⁺ T cells within the aortic tissues showed some differences compared to those identified in peripheral blood. Although both CD4⁺ and CD8⁺ T cells were significantly enriched in immune-related pathways, including T cell differentiation, immune activation, and antigen recognition, they were also notably associated with pathways involved in protein translation, heat shock protein binding, and ubiquitin protein ligase binding. Previous studies have demonstrated the expression of HSP65 in the aortic tissues of TAK patients. It has been proposed that unknown antigens may stimulate the expression of HSP65 in the aorta, which in turn induces the synthesis of MHC class I-related chain A (MICA). MICA can then be recognized by T cells and NK cells, thereby amplifying the inflammatory response [34]. KEGG pathway analysis of aortic tissue revealed that CD4⁺ T cells were closely related to pathways such as T cell receptor signaling, Th17 cell differentiation, and Th1/Th2 cell differentiation, further suggesting that Th1, Th2, and Th17 cells play important roles in the inflammatory response and cytokine network within the tissues of TAK patients. Furthermore, we found that the genes EGR1, ATF3, KLF4, and RHOB were significantly overexpressed in both peripheral blood and arterial tissues from TAK patients. ATF3 plays a crucial role in cardiovascular remodeling, and it can also promote vascular smooth muscle cell proliferation, leading to intimal hyperplasia and vascular calcification [35, 36]. KLF4 facilitates angiogenesis by activating the VEGF signaling pathway [37]. EGR1 is similarly important in angiogenesis and tissue fibrosis. High expression of EGR1 has been observed in patients with systemic sclerosis, particularly in damaged skin and lung biopsy tissues [38, 39]. In vascular smooth muscle cells, EGR1 promotes the expression of Id2 (DNA-binding inhibitor 2), which further stimulates smooth muscle cell proliferation and vascular fibrosis [40]. EGR1 also promotes IL-4 production in Th2 cells [41]. Interestingly, further analysis of CD4 + and CD8 + T cells in arterial tissues from TAK patients revealed that only EGR1 was highly expressed in both cell types. This suggests that EGR1 may play a more central role in TAK pathogenesis and could serve as a potential therapeutic target.

We also observed an inverse expression pattern for some genes, such as PDGFRB, SASH1, and SAMD4A. Although these genes were upregulated in the peripheral blood of TAK patients, their expression decreased in the tissue, reflecting the complexity of TAK, where immune responses in peripheral blood circulation and the vascular tissue microenvironment may be regulated by different mechanisms. Additionally, we found that in the ERK cascade pathway, ATF3 regulates KLF4, and in the IL-1β and IL-6 pathways, EGR1 regulates KLF4. These findings suggest that these four genes may interact, which warrants further investigation.

Despite providing valuable insights into the pathogenesis of TAK, this study has several limitations. Firstly, the sample size was relatively small, which could affect the statistical power of our findings. Another limitation is that the bulk RNA-seq analysis included 8 TAK patients but only 3 healthy controls, rather than a fully balanced design. Although controls were carefully matched for baseline characteristics to minimize confounding, the smaller control group may limit the robustness of the comparisons. Future studies could expand the cohort, such as through multi-center research, to improve the reliability of the results. Secondly, a further limitation of our study is that bulk RNA sequencing was applied to peripheral T cells, whereas single-cell RNA sequencing was performed on aortic tissue, preventing direct one-to-one comparison of transcriptional profiles between circulating and tissue-resident T cells. As a result, our findings cannot provide definitive conclusions about their differences, and future studies using harmonized sequencing platforms with paired blood–tissue samples will be needed to directly address this issue. Moreover, although we identified important genes related to TAK pathogenesis (EGR1, ATF3, KLF4, RHOB), further validation at the protein level is necessary. Additionally, the precise molecular pathways regulated by these genes need to be explored to confirm their role in disease progression and evaluate their potential as therapeutic targets.

Conclusions

Integrated bulk and single-cell transcriptomics reveal both shared and subset-specific signaling landscapes for CD4⁺ and CD8⁺ T cells in TAK. The consistent prominence of EGR1 across compartments nominates this factor as a pivotal molecular switch and attractive therapeutic target. These data furnish a mechanistic framework for precision immune modulation in large-vessel vasculitis.

Stochastic Optimal Linear Quadratic Controls with A Recursive Cost Functional
Lin Li and Jiongmin Yong

Abstract




(
In the Memory of Professor Thomas I. Seidman
)

Abstract.
This paper is concerned with a stochastic linear quadratic (LQ, for short) control problem with a recursive cost functional. It involves BSDEs in
L^{1}
whose well-posedness is a subtle issue. A suitable framework has been adopted so that the corresponding LQ problem is correctly formulated. Open-loop and closed-loop solvability of such an LQ problem have been investigated and characterized by the solvability of an FBSDE and that of Riccati differential equation.

Keywords.
Optimal linear quadratic control, recursive cost functional, forward-backward stochastic differential equation, Riccati equation, backward differential equation in
L^{1}
space.

Introduction.

Let
(\Omega,{\cal F},\mathbb{P})
be a complete probability space, with
{\cal N}
being the set of all
\mathbb{P}
-null sets, on which a standard Brownian motion is defined. Let
T>0
be the given time horizon and let
t\in[0,T]
be given. Define

{\cal F}^{t}_{s}=\sigma\Big(\{W(\tau^{\prime})-W(\tau)\bigm|t\leqslant\tau<\tau^{\prime}\leqslant s\}\vee{\cal N}\Big).

Denote
\mathbb{F}^{t}=\{{\cal F}^{t}_{s}\}_{s\geqslant t}
. In what follows, we will denote
\mathbb{E}_{s}^{t}[\,\cdot\,]=\mathbb{E}^{t}[\,\cdot\,|{\cal F}^{t}_{s}]
(and thus
\mathbb{E}^{t}_{t}[\,\cdot\,]=\mathbb{E}^{t}[\,\cdot\,|{\cal F}^{t}_{t}]=\mathbb{E}[\,\cdot\,]
) Now, in
(\Omega,{\cal F},\mathbb{F}^{t},\mathbb{P})
, we consider the following controlled linear stochastic equation:

\left\{\negthinspace\negthinspace\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle dX(s)=[A(s)X(s)+B(s)u(s)+b(s)]ds+[C(s)X(s)+D(s)u(s)+\sigma(s)]dW(s),\quad s\in[t,T],\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle X(t)=x.\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

For the above state equation, we introduce the following hypothesis:

(H1)
The maps
A,C:[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
,
B,D:[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
, and
b,\sigma:[0,T]\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfy the following:

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle A(\cdot),C(\cdot)\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n})\equiv\Big\{A:[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}\bigm|A(\cdot)\hbox{ is measurable and bounded}\Big\},\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle B(\cdot),D(\cdot)\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{n\times m})\equiv\Big\{B:[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}\bigm|B(\cdot)\hbox{ is measurable and bounded}\Big\},\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle b(\cdot)\in L^{1}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{n})\equiv\Big\{b:[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{n}\bigm|b(\cdot)\hbox{ is measurable and }\int_{t}^{T}|b(s)|ds<\infty\Big\},\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\sigma(\cdot)\in L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{n})\equiv\Big\{\sigma:[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{n}\bigm|\sigma(\cdot)\hbox{ is measurable and }\int_{t}^{T}|\sigma(s)|^{2}ds<\infty\Big\},\end{array}

Note that in the above, we have assumed
b(\cdot)
and
\sigma(\cdot)
to be deterministic because they are required to be
\mathbb{F}^{t}
-progressively measurable for all
t\in[0,T]
which leads to
b(\cdot)
and
\sigma(\cdot)
to be
\mathbb{F}^{t}
-progressively measurable for each
t\in[0,T]
, it implies that
b(t)
and
\sigma(t)
are
\sigma({\cal N})
-measurable, for each
t\in[0,T]
. Thus, it is almost surely that
b(t)
and
\sigma(t)
are deterministic vectors for each
t\in[0,T]
. Hence, we assume that
b(\cdot)
and
\sigma(\cdot)
are deterministic, for convenience. Next, we introduce the set of admissible controls:

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\mathscr{U}^{p}[t,T]=\Big\{u:[t,T]\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{m}\bigm|u(\cdot)\hbox{ is $\mathbb{F}$-progressively meaurble, }\mathbb{E}\Big(\int_{t}^{T}|u(s)|^{2}ds\Big)^{p\over 2}<\infty\Big\}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\equiv L^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;L^{2}(t,T;\mathbb{R}^{m})),\qquad p\geqslant 1.\end{array}

Then, under (H1), for any initial pair
(t,x)\in[0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
u(\cdot)\in\mathscr{U}^{p}[t,T]
, there exists a unique solution

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle X(\cdot)=X(\cdot\,;t,x,u(\cdot))\in L^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;C[t,T];\mathbb{R}^{n}))\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\equiv\Big\{X(\cdot)\in L^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;L^{1}(t,T;\mathbb{R}^{n}))\bigm|X(\cdot)\hbox{ has continuous paths, }\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{s\in[t,T]}|X(s)|^{p}\Big]<\infty\Big\},\end{array}

to the state equation (
1.1
). Moreover, the following holds:

\mathbb{E}\Big(\sup_{s\in[t,T]}|X(s)|^{p}\Big)\leqslant K\Big[|x|^{p}+\Big(\int_{t}^{T}|b(s)|ds\Big)^{p}+\Big(\int_{t}^{T}|\sigma(s)|^{2}ds\Big)^{p\over 2}+\mathbb{E}\Big(\int_{t}^{T}|u(s)|^{2}ds\Big)^{p\over 2}\Big],

(1.2)

which implies
X(T)\in L^{p}_{{\cal F}_{T}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
. In what follows, we let
K>0
to be a generic constant, which could be different from line to line. Further, we have the following stability estimate: If
u^{\prime}(\cdot)\in\mathscr{U}^{p}[t,T]
is another control with
X^{\prime}(\cdot)
being the corresponding state process. Then

\mathbb{E}\Big(\sup_{s\in[t,T]}|X(s)-X^{\prime}(s)|^{p}\Big)\leqslant K\mathbb{E}\Big(\int_{t}^{T}|u(s)-u^{\prime}(s)|^{2}ds\Big)^{p\over 2}.

(1.3)

To measure the performance of the controls, we introduce the following cost functional of the recursive form

J(t,x;u(\cdot))=Y(t),

(1.4)

where
(Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot))
is the adapted solution of the following stochastic backward differential equation (BSDE, for short):

\left\{\negthinspace\negthinspace\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle dY(s)=-[E(s)Y(s)+F(s)Z(s)+f(s)\rangle]ds+Z(s)dW(s),\qquad s\in[t,T],\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle Y(T)=\xi,\end{array}\right.

(1.5)

where

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle f(s)=\langle Q(s)X(s),X(s)\rangle+2\langle S(s)X(s),u(s)\rangle+\langle R(s)u(s),u(s)\rangle+2\langle q(s),X(s)\rangle+2\langle r(s),u(s)\rangle,\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad s\in[t,T],\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\xi=\langle GX(T),X(T)\rangle+2\langle g,X(T)\rangle,\end{array}

(1.6)

for some deterministic bounded functions
E(\cdot)
and
F(\cdot)
. Note that by assuming
Q(s)
,
S(s)
and
R(s)
to be deterministic and bounded,
q(s)
and
r(s)
to be deterministic square integrable processes, and
g\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
(The argument for
q(\cdot),r(\cdot)
and
g
to be deterministic is similar to that for
b(\cdot)
and
\sigma(\cdot)
)

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}|f(s)|ds\leqslant K\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}\Big(|X(s)|^{2}+|X(s)||u(s)|+|u(s)|^{2}+|X(s)|+|u(s)|\Big)ds\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\leqslant K\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}\Big(|X(s)|^{2}+|u(s)|^{2}+|q(s)|^{2}+|r(s)|^{2}\Big)ds\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\leqslant K\Big[|x|^{2}+\Big(\int_{t}^{T}|b(s)|ds\Big)^{2}+\int_{t}^{T}\Big(|\sigma(s)|^{2}+|q(s)|^{2}+|r(s)|^{2}\Big)ds+\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}|u(s)|^{2}ds\Big]<\infty,\end{array}

and further assuming
\begin{pmatrix}Q(s)&S(s)^{\top}\\
S(s)&R(s)\end{pmatrix}
to be uniformly positive definite, which leads to, for some
\delta>0
,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}|f(s)|ds\geqslant\delta\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}\Big(|X(s)|^{2}+|u(s)|^{2}\Big)ds-K\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}\Big(|X(s)||u(s)|+|q(s)||X(s)|+|r(s)|u(s)|\Big)ds\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\geqslant{\delta\over 2}\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}\Big(|X(s)|^{2}+|u(s)|^{2}\Big)ds-K\int_{t}^{T}\Big(|q(s)|^{2}+|r(s)|^{2}\Big)ds\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\geqslant{\delta\over 2}\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}|u(s)|^{2}ds-K\int_{t}^{T}\Big(|q(s)|^{2}+|r(s)|^{2}\Big)ds.\end{array}

Combining the above, we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle{\delta\over 2}\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}|u(s)|^{2}ds-K\int_{t}^{T}\Big(|q(s)|^{2}+|r(s)|^{2}\Big)ds\leqslant\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}|f(s)|ds\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\leqslant K\Big[|x|^{2}+\Big(\int_{t}^{T}|b(s)|ds\Big)^{2}+\int_{t}^{T}\Big(|\sigma(s)|^{2}+|q(s)|^{2}+|r(s)|^{2}\Big)ds+\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}|u(s)|^{2}ds\Big].\end{array}

(1.7)

Thus, essentially,
\|f(\cdot)\|_{L_{\mathbb{F}}^{1}(\Omega;L^{1}(t,T:\mathbb{R}^{n}))}
and
\|u(\cdot)\|_{\mathscr{U}^{2}[t,T]}
are of the same orders. Next, by assuming square integrability of
g
, we get

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|\xi|\leqslant K\mathbb{E}\Big(|X(T)|^{2}+|g|^{2}\Big)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\leqslant K\mathbb{E}\Big[|x|^{2}+\Big(\int_{t}^{T}|b(s)|ds\Big)^{2}+\int_{t}^{T}|\sigma(s)|^{2}ds+\int_{t}^{T}|u(s)|^{2}ds+|g|^{2}\Big]<\infty.\end{array}

Further, let
G
to be positive definite and

B(s)=I,\quad D(s)=0,\quad b(s)=\sigma(s)=0,\qquad s\in[t,T],\qquad x=0.

For
k\geqslant 1
, we let

u_{k}(s)={1\over(T-t_{k})^{1\over 2}}{\bf 1}_{[t_{k},T]}(s)u_{0},\qquad s\in[t,T],

for some fixed
u_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus\{0\}
. Then

X_{k}(T)=\int_{t_{k}}^{T}\Psi(\tau,t_{k})u_{k}(\tau)d\tau,\qquad\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}|u_{k}(s)|^{2}ds=|u_{0}|^{2}.

with
\Psi(\cdot\,,\cdot)
solving the following

\left\{\negthinspace\negthinspace\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle d\Psi(t,t_{k})=A(s)\Psi(s,t_{k})ds+C(s)\Psi(s,t_{k})dW(s),\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\Psi(t_{k},t_{k})=I.\end{array}\right.

Hence,

\mathbb{E}|\xi|\geqslant\mathbb{E}\Big(\delta|X_{k}(T)|^{2}-|g|^{2}\Big)\geqslant{\delta\over T-t_{k}}\mathbb{E}\Big|\int_{t_{k}}^{T}\Psi(\tau,t_{k})u_{0}d\tau\Big|^{2}=\delta\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}|u_{k}(s)|^{2}ds+o(1).

Combing the above, in general, we should have

\delta\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace|u_{k}(s)|^{2}ds+o(1)\leqslant\mathbb{E}|\xi|\leqslant K\mathbb{E}\Big[|x|^{2}+\Big(\int_{t}^{T}\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace|b(s)|ds\Big)^{2}+\int_{t}^{T}\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace|\sigma(s)|^{2}ds+\int_{t}^{T}\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace|u_{k}(s)|^{2}ds+|g|^{2}\Big].

(1.8)

Thus, in general,
\|\xi\|_{L^{1}_{{\cal F}_{T}}(\Omega:\mathbb{R}^{n})}
and
\|u(\cdot)\|_{\mathscr{U}^{2}[t,T]}
are of the same orders. The above shows that, in general, (
1.5
) is a linear nonhomogeneous BSDE with the nonhomogeneous term being an
L^{1}
process and the terminal term being an
L^{1}
random variable. Thus, the well-posedness of such a BSDE is very subtle (see
[
1
,
6
,
5
,
7
]
among many papers). We will see that for
u(\cdot)\in\mathscr{D}(J(t,x;\cdot))
, a dense subset in
\mathscr{U}^{2}[t,T]
, the BDSE (
1.5
) is well-psosed, and the cost functional (
1.4
) is well-defined which is called a
recursive functional
. The recursive functional was firstly introduced by Duffie–Epstein in 1992 (
[
2
,
3
]
) (which was called differential utility). People found that this was nothing but some special cases of BSDEs. Peng then firstly studied some general nonlinear optimal control problems with recursive cost functional in 1992 (
[
13
]
). A little later, Peng introduced the so-called
g
-expectation notion (a generalized expectation, which could nonlinear, but is time-consistent) in 1997 from general theory of BSDEs (
[
14
]
). The notion of differential utility was extended by Quenez–Lazrak
[
9
]
, and Lazrak
[
8
]
in the beginning of 2000s to a general BSDE framework. In 2008, Yong studied LQ problems under (nonlinear)
g
-expectation introduced in
[
14
]
with the framework of
L^{p\over 2}
space (
p>2
)
[
17
]
). Thus, the linear structure was partially ruined (since the expectation was nonlinear) and moreover, it could not cover the case of
p=2
. Because of that, the Riccati type differential equation was derived only for a very special case in
[
17
]
. In the current paper, with a recursive (in
L^{1}
, and linear) cost functional, defined through the idea of
[
14
]
, we are able to pose the following optimal control problem for which all the linear-quadratical structure is kept.

Problem (LQR).
For given initial pair
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
, find a control
\bar{u}(\cdot)\in\mathscr{D}(J(t,x;\cdot))\subseteq\mathscr{U}^{2}[t,T]
such that

J(t,x;\bar{u}(\cdot))=\inf_{u(\cdot)\in\mathscr{D}(J(t,x;\cdot))}J(t.x;u(\cdot))=V(t,x).

(1.9)

Note that when
E(s),F(s)\equiv 0
, the cost reads

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle J(t,x;u(\cdot))=\mathbb{E}\Big[\langle GX(T),X(T)\rangle+2\langle g,X(T)\rangle+\int_{t}^{T}\Big(\langle Q(s)X(s),X(s)\rangle+2\langle S(s)X(s),u(s)\rangle\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\langle R(s)u(s),u(s)\rangle+2\langle q(s),X(s)\rangle+2\langle r(s),u(s)\rangle\Big)ds\Big].\end{array}

(1.10)

In this case, we actually have
\mathscr{D}(J(t,x;\cdot))=\mathscr{U}[t,T]^{2}
. Then Problem (LQR) is well-posed for
p=2
and the above gives a classical LQ cost functional. Therefore, our results will cover the classical LQ theory presented in, say,
[
15
]
. Now, we highlight the main features of the current paper.

\bullet
Developping the BSDE in
L^{1}
space (which is an extension of
[
14
]
), we correctly formulate the stochastic Problem (LQR) with
L^{1}
recursive cost functional.

\bullet
Revealing a relation of Problem (LQR) and the classical LQ problem. It turns out that the former under a transformation leads to a classical LQ problem. But the latter is not a simple extension of the former, as far as the cost functionals (together with their domains) are concerned.

\bullet
Introduce the open-loop solvability of Problem (LQR) and its equivalent conditions, which is the solvability of an FBSDE, satisfying the maximum condition. This will be achieved by the idea of
[
12
]
. We will find the Fréchet derivative and the Hessian of the recursive cost functional.

\bullet
Introduce the closed-loop solvability of Problem (LQR) and its equivalent conditions, which is the solvability of a Riccati differential equation. The basic approach is the completing the squares.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows, Section 2 is to present some relevant results of BSDEs in
L^{1}
, together with the formulation of our LQ problem, and open-loop, and closed-loop solvability of the problem. By making some transformation, it seems that a classical LQ is derived. But, this LQ problem is not simple extension of our Problem (LQR). A concrete relation will be revealed in Section 3. In Section 4, we present the equivalence between open-loop solvability and that of an FBSDE. Closed-loop solvability of the Problem (LQ) turns out to be equivalent to that of Riccati equation. This will be presented in Section 5. Finally, some conclusion remarks are collected in Section 6.

We first introduce the following definitions.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\beta\in(0,1)
. Define:

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle{\cal S}^{\beta}[t,T]=\Big\{Y:[t,T]\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}\bigm|Y(\cdot)\hbox{ is $\mathbb{F}$-progressively measurable,}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\|Y(\cdot)\|_{{\cal S}^{\beta}[t,T]}\equiv\Big[\mathbb{E}\Big(\sup_{s\in[t,T]}|Y(s)|^{\beta}\Big)\Big]^{1\over\beta}<\infty\Big\},\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle M^{\beta}[t,T]=\Big\{Z:[t,T]\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}\bigm|Z(\cdot)\hbox{ is $\mathbb{F}$-progressively measurable,}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\|Z\|_{M^{\beta}}=\Big[\mathbb{E}\Big(\int_{t}^{T}|Z(s)|^{2}ds\Big)^{\beta\over 2}\Big]^{1\over\beta}<\infty\Big\}.\end{array}

Clearly,
{\cal S}^{\beta}[t,T]
and
M^{\beta}[t,T]
are complete linear metric spaces.

Definition 2.2
.

Process
Y:[t,T]\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
is of class (D) if it is
\mathbb{F}
-adapted, and

\{Y(\tau)\bigm|\tau\hbox{ is a stopping time valued in $[t,T]$ }\}

is uniformly integrable.

Consider the following general BSDE:

\left\{\negthinspace\negthinspace\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle dY(s)=-g(s,Y(s),Z(s))ds+Z(s)dW(s),\qquad s\in[t,T],\\

(2.1)

We assume the following.

(H2)
Let

\mathbb{E}\Big[|\xi|+\int_{0}^{T}|g(s,0,0)|ds\Big]<\infty,

(2.2)

and for some constant
K>0
and
\alpha\in(0,1)

|g(s,y_{1},z_{1})-g(s,y_{2},z_{2})|\leqslant K\Big(|y_{1}-y_{2}|+|z_{1}-z_{2}|^{\alpha}\Big),\qquad y_{1},y_{2},z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathbb{R},

(2.3)

for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
.

According to

]

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
(H2)
hold. Then BSDE admits a unique adapted solution
(Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot))
, with

in the class (D) and such that

\mathbb{E}\Big(\sup_{s\in[0,T]}|Y(s)|^{\beta}\Big)^{1\over\beta}+\mathbb{E}\Big(\int_{t}^{T}|Z(s)|^{2}ds\Big)^{\beta\over 2}<\infty,\qquad\forall\beta\in(0,1).

(2.4)

Note that the uniqueness follows the following comparison: If
\xi^{\prime}\geqslant\xi
are two proper random variables, and let
(Y^{\prime}(\cdot),Z^{\prime}(\cdot))
and

In what follows, for any

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle L^{p_{1}}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;L^{p_{2}}(t,T;\mathbb{H}))=\Big\{\varphi:[t,T]\times\Omega\to\mathbb{H}\bigm|\varphi(\cdot)\hbox{ is $\mathbb{F}$-progressively measurable, }\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\mathbb{E}\Big(\int_{t}^{T}|\varphi(s)|^{p_{2}}ds\Big)^{p_{1}\over p_{2}}<\infty\Big\}.\end{array}

(2.5)

L^{p_{1}}_{{\cal F}_{T}}(\Omega;\mathbb{H})=\Big\{\xi:\Omega\to\mathbb{H}\bigm|\xi\hbox{ is ${\cal F}_{T}$-measurable, }\mathbb{E}|\xi|^{p_{1}}<\infty\Big\}.

(2.6)

We define

L^{p_{1}+}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;L^{p_{2}+}(t,T;\mathbb{R}))=\bigcup_{p_{1}^{\prime}>p_{1},p_{2}^{\prime}>p_{2}}L^{p_{1}^{\prime}}(\Omega;L^{p_{2}^{\prime}}(t,T;\mathbb{R})),\qquad L^{p_{1}+}_{{\cal F}_{T}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}))=\bigcup_{p_{1}^{\prime}>p_{1}}L^{p_{1}^{\prime}}_{{\cal F}_{T}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}),

(2.7)

\mathscr{U}^{2+}[t,T]=\bigcup_{p>2}\mathscr{U}^{p}[t,T].

(2.8)

(they do not have to be forms (

1.5
) is a linear BSDE with

f(\cdot)\in L^{1}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;L^{1}(t,T;\mathbb{R})),\qquad\xi\in L^{1}_{{\cal F}_{T}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}).

(2.9)

It is known that the well-posedness of BSDE in
L^{1}

to approach the problem. The following lemma will play an essential role below.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
E(\cdot),F(\cdot)\in L^{\infty}(t,T;\mathbb{R})
. Let
\varPhi(\cdot\,,t)

Lemma 2.5
.

Let

. Then for any

\mathbb{E}\Big|\mathbb{E}^{t}_{s}\Big[e^{\int_{t}^{T}F(\theta)dW(\theta)}\Big]\Big|^{p}\leqslant e^{{p^{2}\over 2}\int_{s}^{T}F(\theta)^{2}d\theta}.

Proof.

[

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big|\mathbb{E}^{t}_{s}\Big[e^{\int_{t}^{T}F(\theta)dW(\theta)}\Big]\Big|^{p}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}{p^{k}\over k!}\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{s}^{T}F(\theta)dW(\theta)\Big]^{k}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}{p^{2k}\over(2k)!}{(2k)!\over 2^{k}k!}\Big(\int_{s}^{T}|F(\theta)|^{2}d\theta\Big)^{k}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}{1\over k!}\Big({p^{2}\over 2}\int_{s}^{T}|F(\theta)|^{2}d\theta\Big)^{k}=e^{{p^{2}\over 2}\int_{s}^{T}|F(\theta)|^{2}d\theta}.\end{array}

Then, our conclusion follows.

Consequently, for
p>2
, and for

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big|\mathbb{E}_{s}^{t}\Big[e^{\int_{t}^{T}F(\theta)dW(\theta)}\xi\Big]\Big|\leqslant\Big(\mathbb{E}\Big[e^{\int_{t}^{T}F(\theta)dW(\theta)}\Big]^{p\over p-2}\Big)^{p-2\over p}\Big(\mathbb{E}|\xi|^{p\over 2}\Big)^{2\over p}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\leqslant\Big\{\mathbb{E}\Big[e^{{p^{2}\over 2(p-2)^{2}}\int_{s}^{T}F(\theta)^{2}d\theta}\Big]\Big\}^{p-2\over p}\Big(\mathbb{E}|\xi|^{p\over 2}\Big)^{2\over p}\leqslant K\Big(\mathbb{E}|\xi|^{p\over 2}\Big)^{2\over p}.\end{array}

3
A Relation to the Classical Case.

In this section, we are transforming Problem (LQR) to a classical one, and reveal a relation of our Problem (LQR) with the classical Problem (LQ). First of all, let

\Lambda(s,t)=e^{\int_{t}^{s}[-{1\over 2}F(\theta)^{2}+E(\theta)]d\theta},\qquad 0\leqslant s\leqslant t\leqslant T.

(3.1)

Thus, the following type group property holds:

\Lambda(s,t)=\Lambda(s,\tau)\Lambda(\tau,t),\qquad 0\leqslant s,\tau,t\leqslant T.

When
F(\cdot)
and
E(\cdot)
are bounded,
\Lambda(\cdot\,,\cdot)
is bounded as well. Then

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle Y(s)=\mathbb{E}_{s}^{t}\Big[\varPhi(T,s)\Big(\xi+\int_{s}^{T}\varPhi(\tau,T)e^{\int_{T}^{\tau}E(\theta)d\theta}f(\tau)d\tau\Big)\Big]\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad=\mathbb{E}^{t}_{s}\Big[e^{\int_{s}^{T}F(\theta)dW(\theta)+\int_{s}^{T}[-{1\over 2}F(\theta)^{2}+E(\theta)]d\theta}\Big(\xi+\int_{s}^{T}e^{\int_{T}^{\tau}F(\theta)dW(\theta)+\int_{T}^{\tau}[-{1\over 2}F(\theta)^{2}+E(\theta)]d\theta}f(\tau)d\tau\Big)\Big]\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad=\mathbb{E}^{t}_{s}\Big[e^{\int_{s}^{T}F(\theta)dW(\theta)}\Lambda(T,s)\Big(\xi+\int_{s}^{T}e^{\int_{T}^{\tau}F(\theta)dW(\theta)}\Lambda(\tau,T)f(\tau)d\tau\Big)\Big]\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad=\Lambda(T,s)\mathbb{E}_{s}^{t}\Big(e^{\int_{s}^{T}F(\theta)dW(\theta)}\xi+\int_{s}^{T}e^{\int_{s}^{\tau}F(\theta)dW(\theta)}\Lambda(\tau,T)f(\tau)d\tau\Big).\end{array}

(3.2)

Thus,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle J(t,x;u(\cdot))=Y(t)=\Lambda(T,t)\mathbb{E}\Big[e^{\int_{t}^{T}F(\theta)dW(\theta)}\Big(\langle GX(T),X(T)\rangle+2\langle g,X(T)\rangle\Big)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\int_{t}^{T}\Lambda(\tau,T)e^{\int_{t}^{\tau}F(\theta)dW(\theta)}\Big(\langle Q(\tau)X(\tau),X(\tau)\rangle+2\langle S(\tau)X(\tau),u(\tau)\rangle\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\langle R(\tau)u(\tau),u(\tau)\rangle+2\langle q(\tau),X(\tau)\rangle+2\langle r(\tau),u(\tau)\rangle\Big)d\tau\Big]\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle=\Lambda(T,t)\mathbb{E}\Big[\langle G\widehat{X}(T),\widehat{X}(T)\rangle+2\langle\widehat{g},\widehat{X}(T)\rangle+\int_{t}^{T}\Lambda(\tau,T)\Big(\langle Q(\tau)\widehat{X}(\tau),\widehat{X}(\tau)\rangle\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad+2\langle S(\tau)\widehat{X}(\tau),\widehat{u}(\tau)\rangle+\langle R(\tau)\widehat{u}(\tau),\widehat{u}(\tau)\rangle+2\langle\widehat{q}(\tau),\widehat{X}(\tau)\rangle+2\langle\widehat{r}(\tau),\widehat{u}(\tau)\rangle\Big)d\tau\Big]\end{array}

(3.3)

where

\left\{\negthinspace\negthinspace\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\widehat{g}=e^{{1\over 2}\int_{t}^{T}F(\theta)dW(\theta)}g,\quad\widehat{q}(\tau)=e^{\int_{t}^{\tau}{1\over 2}F(\theta)dW(\theta)}q(\tau),\quad\widehat{r}(\tau)=e^{\int_{t}^{\tau}{1\over 2}F(\theta)dW(\theta)}r(\tau),\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\widehat{X}(\tau)=e^{{1\over 2}\int_{t}^{\tau}F(\theta)dW(\theta)}X(\tau),\quad\widehat{u}(\tau)=e^{{1\over 2}\int_{t}^{\tau}F(\theta)dW(\theta)}u(\tau),\end{array}\right.

(3.4)

Applying the Itô’s formula to the map
\tau\mapsto e^{{1\over 2}\int_{t}^{\tau}F(\theta)dW(\theta)}X(\tau)
, one has

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle d\widehat{X}(\tau)=\Big[e^{{1\over 2}\int_{t}^{\tau}F(\theta)dW(\theta)}{1\over 2}F(\tau)dW(\tau)\Big]X(\tau)+e^{{1\over 2}\int_{t}^{\tau}F(\theta)dW(\theta)}\Big([A(\tau)X(\tau)+B(\tau)u(\tau)+b(\tau)]d\tau\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+[C(\tau)X(\tau)+D(\tau)u(\tau)+\sigma(\tau)]dW(\tau)\Big)+{1\over 2}F(\tau)e^{{1\over 2}\int_{t}^{\tau}F(\theta)dW(\theta)}[C(\tau)X(\tau)+D(\tau)u(\tau)+\sigma(\tau)]d\tau\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\quad={1\over 2}\widehat{X}(\tau)F(\tau)dW(\tau)+[A(\tau)\widehat{X}(\tau)+B(\tau)\widehat{u}(\tau)+e^{{1\over 2}\int_{t}^{\tau}F(\theta)dW(\theta)}b(\tau)]d\tau\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+[C(\tau)\widehat{X}(\tau)+D(\tau)\widehat{u}(\tau)+e^{{1\over 2}\int_{t}^{\tau}F(\theta)dW(\theta)}\sigma(\tau)]dW(\tau)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+{1\over 2}F(\tau)[C(\tau)\widehat{X}(\tau)+D(\tau)\widehat{u}(\tau)+e^{{1\over 2}\int_{t}^{\tau}F(\theta)dW(\theta)}\sigma(\tau)]d\tau\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\quad=\Big([A(\tau)+{1\over 2}F(\tau)C(\tau)]\widehat{X}(\tau)+[B(\tau)+{1\over 2}F(\tau)D(\tau)]\widehat{u}(\tau)+e^{{1\over 2}\int_{t}^{\tau}F(\theta)dW(\theta)}[b(\tau)+{1\over 2}F(\tau)\sigma(\tau)]\Big)d\tau\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\Big([C(\tau)+{1\over 2}F(\tau)I]\widehat{X}(\tau)+D(\tau)\widehat{u}(\tau)+e^{{1\over 2}\int_{t}^{\tau}F(\theta)dW(\theta)}\sigma(\tau)\Big)dW(\tau)\end{array}

Then, we have a classical LQ problem with the state equation:

\left\{\negthinspace\negthinspace\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle d\widehat{X}(\tau)=\Big(\widehat{A}(\tau)\widehat{X}(\tau)+\widehat{B}(\tau)\widehat{u}(\tau)+\widehat{b}(\tau)\Big)d\tau\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\Big(\widehat{C}(\tau)\widehat{X}(\tau)+\widehat{D}(\tau)\widehat{u}(\tau)+\widehat{\sigma}(\tau)\Big)dW(\tau),\quad\tau\in[t,T],\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\widehat{X}(t)=x,\end{array}\right.

(3.5)

and with cost functional:

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\widehat{J}(t,x;\widehat{u}(\cdot))=\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\langle G\widehat{X}(T),\widehat{X}(T)\rangle+2\langle\widehat{g},\widehat{X}(T)\rangle\Big)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\int_{t}^{T}\Lambda(\tau,T)\Big(\langle Q(\tau)\widehat{X}(\tau),\widehat{X}(\tau)\rangle+2\langle S(\tau)\widehat{X}(\tau),\widehat{u}(\tau)\rangle\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\langle R(\tau)\widehat{u}(\tau),\widehat{u}(\tau)\rangle+2\langle\widehat{q}(\tau),\widehat{X}(\tau)\rangle+2\langle\widehat{r}(\tau),\widehat{u}(\tau)\rangle\Big)d\tau\Big].\end{array}

(3.6)

In the above, we have used

\left\{\negthinspace\negthinspace\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\widehat{A}(\tau)=A(\tau)+{1\over 2}F(\tau)C(\tau),\qquad\widehat{B}(\tau)=B(\tau)+{1\over 2}F(\tau)D(\tau),\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\widehat{C}(\tau)=C(\tau)+{1\over 2}F(\tau)I,\qquad\widehat{D}(\tau)=D(\tau),\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\widehat{b}(\tau)=e^{{1\over 2}\int_{t}^{\tau}F(\theta)dW(\theta)}\Big(b(\tau)+{1\over 2}F(\tau)\sigma(\tau)\Big),\qquad\widehat{\sigma}(\tau)=e^{{1\over 2}\int_{t}^{\tau}F(\theta)dW(\theta)}\sigma(\tau).\end{array}\right.

(3.7)

Also,

J(t,x;u(\cdot))=Y(t)=\Lambda(T,t)\widehat{J}(t,x;\widehat{u}(\cdot)).

(3.8)

Hence, under transformation (
3.4
), we obtain the following classical LQ problem.

Problem (LQ).
For given
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
, find
\overline{\widehat{u}}(\cdot)\in\mathscr{U}^{2}[t,T]
such that

\widehat{J}(t,x;\overline{\widehat{u}}(\cdot))=\inf_{\widehat{u}(\cdot)\in\mathscr{U}^{2}[t,T]}\widehat{J}(t,x;\widehat{u}(\cdot)).

(3.9)

Problem (LQR) seems to be transformed to the above classical Problem (LQ). But, we might overlook one point. Function
u(\cdot)\mapsto J(t,x;u(\cdot))
is defined on
\mathscr{D}(J(t,x;\cdot))
which is dense in
\mathscr{U}^{2}[t,T]
, whereas

is defined on

In this section, we are going to look at the open-loop solvability of Problem (LQR). Let us first present a general result, which is similar to that in

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
{\bf H}
be a Hilbert space and
{\bf F}:\mathscr{D}(F)\subseteq{\bf H}\to\mathbb{R}
be a quadratic function, i.e.,

{\bf F}(u)=\langle\varPhi_{2}u,u\rangle+2\langle\varPhi_{1},u\rangle+\varPhi_{0},\qquad u\in\mathscr{D}({\bf F})\equiv\mathscr{D}(\varPhi_{2}),

(4.1)

with
\varPhi_{2}:\mathscr{D}(\varPhi_{2})\subseteq{\bf H}\to{\bf H}
being self-adjoint,
\varPhi_{1}\in{\bf H}
and
\varPhi_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
. Let
u\in\mathscr{D}(\varPhi_{2})
attend a minimum of
{\bf F}
. Then

\varPhi_{2}\geqslant 0,

(4.2)

and

D_{u}{\bf F}(u)=2\big(\varPhi_{2}u+\varPhi_{1}\big)=0,

(4.3)

admits a solution
\bar{u}\in\mathscr{D}(\varPhi_{2})
. Conversely. if (
4.2
) holds and
\bar{u}\in\mathscr{D}(\varPhi_{2})
solves equation (
4.3
), then
\bar{u}
is a minimum of
{\bf F}
over
\mathscr{D}(\varPhi_{2})\subseteq{\bf H}
. In this case.

{\bf F}(u)=\langle\varPhi_{2}(u-\bar{u}),u-\bar{u}\rangle+\varPhi_{0}-\langle\varPhi_{2}^{\dagger}\varPhi_{1},\varPhi_{1}\rangle,

(4.4)

where
\varPhi_{2}^{\dagger}
is the pseudo-inverse of
\varPhi_{2}\geqslant 0
. In the case that
\varPhi_{2}
is uniformly positive definite, the minimum uniquely exists.

Proof.
Let
\bar{u}\in\mathscr{D}(\varPhi_{2})\subseteq{\bf H}
such that

{\bf F}(\bar{u})=\min_{u\in\mathscr{D}({\bf F})\subseteq{\bf H}}{\bf F}(u)=\min_{u\in\mathscr{D}(\varPhi_{2})\subseteq{\bf H}}\Big[\langle\varPhi_{2}u,u\rangle+2\langle\varPhi_{1},u\rangle+\varPhi_{0}\Big]>-\infty.

Then we claim (
4.2
) holds. In fact, if it were not true. Then, there exists a
u_{0}\in\mathscr{D}(\varPhi_{2})
such that

\langle\varPhi_{2}u_{0},u_{0}\rangle<0.

Then,

{\bf F}(\lambda u_{0})=\lambda^{2}\langle\varPhi_{2}u_{0},u_{0}\rangle+2\lambda\langle\varPhi_{1},u_{0}\rangle+\varPhi_{0}\to-\infty,

which is a contradiction. Hence, (
4.2
) is true.

Next, suppose
{\bf F}(\cdot)
attends a minimum at
\bar{u}\in\mathscr{D}(\varPhi_{2})\subseteq{\bf H}
. Then, by Fermat’ Theorem, we see that
\bar{u}
is a solution of equation (
4.3
). Conversely, if (
4.2
) holds and
\bar{u}\in\mathscr{D}(\varPhi_{2})\subseteq{\bf H}
solves equation (
4.3
), then (note
\varPhi_{1}=-\varPhi_{2}\bar{u}
)

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle{\bf F}(u)=\langle\varPhi_{2}u,u\rangle+2\langle\varPhi_{1},u\rangle+\varPhi_{0}=|\varPhi_{2}^{1\over 2}u|^{2}-2\langle\varPhi_{2}\bar{u},u\rangle+\varPhi_{0}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad=|\varPhi_{2}^{1\over 2}u|^{2}-2\langle\varPhi_{2}^{1\over 2}u,\varPhi_{2}^{1\over 2}\bar{u}\rangle+\varPhi_{0}=|\varPhi_{2}^{1\over 2}(u-\bar{u})|^{2}+\varPhi_{0}-|\varPhi_{2}^{1\over 2}\bar{u}|^{2}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad=\langle\varPhi_{2}(u-\bar{u}),u-\bar{u}\rangle+\varPhi_{0}-\langle\varPhi_{2}\bar{u},\bar{u}\rangle=\langle\varPhi_{2}(u-\bar{u}),u-\bar{u}\rangle+\varPhi_{0}-\langle\varPhi_{2}^{\dagger}\varPhi_{1},\varPhi_{1}\rangle.\end{array}

(4.5)

since
\varPhi_{2}\varPhi_{2}^{\dagger}\varPhi_{2}=\varPhi_{2}
. Now, if
\varPhi_{2}
is uniformly positive definite.
Then for any minimizing sequence
u_{k}
,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle{\bf F}(u_{k})=\langle\varPhi_{2}u_{k},u_{k}\rangle+2\langle\varPhi_{1},u_{k}\rangle+\varPhi_{0}=|\varPhi_{2}^{1\over 2}u_{k}|^{2}+\langle\varPhi_{2}^{1\over 2}u_{k},\varPhi_{2}^{-{1\over 2}}\varPhi_{1}\rangle+\varPhi_{0}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\quad=|\varPhi_{2}^{1\over 2}(u_{k}-\varPhi_{2}^{-1}\varPhi_{1})|^{2}+\varPhi_{0}-\langle\varPhi_{2}^{-1}\varPhi_{1},\varPhi_{1}\rangle.\end{array}

Hence,
u_{k}
is bounded. Then, we may assume
u_{k}
goes to some
\bar{u}
weekly. Then by the lower semi-continuity of
{\bf F}(\cdot)
, we have

{\bf F}(\bar{u})\leqslant\mathop{\underline{\rm lim}}_{k\to\infty}{\bf F}(u_{k}).

Thus,
\bar{u}
is a minimum of
{\bf F}
. Thus, by (
4.5
),

\min_{u\in{\bf H}}{\bf F}(u)=\varPhi_{0}-\langle\varPhi_{2}^{-1}\varPhi_{1},\varPhi_{1}\rangle={\bf F}(u^{\prime})=\langle\varPhi_{2}(u^{\prime}-\bar{u}),u^{\prime}-\bar{u}\rangle+\varPhi_{0}-\langle\varPhi_{2}^{-1}\varPhi_{1},\varPhi_{1}\rangle,

leading to

\delta|u^{\prime}-\bar{u}|^{2}\leqslant\langle\varPhi_{2}(u^{\prime}-\bar{u}),u^{\prime}-\bar{u}\rangle=0.

This gives the uniqueness.

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\gamma=[(A+EI+FC)^{\top}-({\bf S}(P)^{\top}+FPD){\bf R}(P)^{\dagger}[(B+FD)^{\top}]\widetilde{\eta}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\{(C+FI)^{\top}-[{\bf S}(P)^{\top}+FPD]{\bf R}(P)^{\dagger}D^{\top}\}\widetilde{\zeta}+Pb+q\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\{(C+FI)^{\top}-[{\bf S}(P)^{\top}+FPD]{\bf R}(P)^{\dagger}D^{\top}\}P\sigma-[{\bf S}(P)^{\top}+FPD]{\bf R}(P)^{\dagger}r.\end{array}

(5.32)

Now, since (
5.3
) is a BSDE with deterministic coefficients and deterministic terminal state, we must have
\widetilde{\zeta}=0
. Thus,
(
5.32
) reads

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\gamma=[(A+EI+FC)^{\top}-({\bf S}(P)^{\top}+FPD){\bf R}(P)^{\dagger}[(B+FD)^{\top}]\widetilde{\eta}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad+\{(C+FI)^{\top}-[{\bf S}(P)^{\top}+FPD]{\bf R}(P)^{\dagger}D^{\top}\}P\sigma-[{\bf S}(P)^{\top}+FPD]{\bf R}(P)^{\dagger}r+Pb+q.\end{array}

(5.33)

Then, combining the above with the uniqueness, we obtain ODE (
5.3
).

5.2
Sufficient conditions for closed-loop solvability

In this subsection, we are going to give a sufficient condition for the closed-loop solvability of Problem (LQR). The ideas come from the so-called the Four Step-Scheme found in
[
10
,
11
]
for FBSDEs. Our main result is the following.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
(H1), (H3)–(H4)
hold. Let the Riccati differential equation (
5.2
) (or (
5.27
)) admit a unique solution
P(\cdot)
, and let
\eta(\cdot)
be the solution of the terminal value problem (
5.3
) (or
(
5.31
)–(
5.32
)), with range conditions (
5.4
). Then Problem
(LQR)
is closed-loop solvable at any
t\in[0,T]
with the optimal closed-loop strategy given by (
5.1
).

Proof.
Let
(\bar{\Psi}(\cdot),\bar{v}(\cdot))\in\boldsymbol{\Psi}[t,T]\times\mathscr{U}^{2}[t,T]
be any given strategy. For any initial state
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, let

\bar{u}(\cdot)=\bar{\Psi}(\cdot)\bar{X}(\cdot)+\bar{v}(\cdot)

be a corresponding outcome of this strategy (with
\bar{X}(\cdot)
being the corresponding state process). Also, let

\left\{\negthinspace\negthinspace\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\bar{Y}(s)=P(s)\bar{X}(s)+\eta(s),\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\bar{Z}(s)=P(s)\Big[C(s)\bar{X}(s)+D(s)\bar{u}(s)+\sigma(s)\Big],\end{array}\right.\qquad s\in[t,T],

(5.34)

with
P(\cdot)
being the solution to the Riccati dyifferential equation (
5.2
) and
\eta(\cdot)
being the solution of terminal value problem (
5.3
).
Then, we claim that
(\bar{X}(\cdot),\bar{u}(\cdot),\bar{Y}(\cdot),\bar{Z}(\cdot))
is the adapted solution of the following FBSDE:

\left\{\negthinspace\negthinspace\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle d\bar{X}(s)=\Big[A(s)\bar{X}(s)+B(s)\bar{u}((s)+b(s)\Big]ds+\Big[C(s)\bar{X}(s)+D(s)\bar{u}(s)+\sigma(s)\Big]dW(s),\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle d\bar{Y}(s)=-\Big[\Big(A(s)+E(s)I+F(s)C(s)\Big)^{\top}\bar{Y}(s)+\Big(C(s)+F(s)I\Big)^{\top}\bar{Z}(s)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+Q(s)\bar{X}(s)+S(s)^{\top}\bar{u}(s)+q(s)\Big]ds+\bar{Z}(s)dW(s),\qquad s\in[t,T],\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\bar{X}(t)=x,\quad\bar{Y}(T)=G\bar{X}(T)+g,\end{array}\right.

(5.35)

with the stationary condition

[B(s)+F(s)D(s)]^{\top}\bar{Y}(s)+D(s)^{\top}\bar{Z}(s)+S(s)\bar{X}(s)+R(s)\bar{u}(s)+r(s)=0.

(5.36)

To solve (
5.35
)–(
5.36
) (by means of Four Step Scheme
[
10
,
11
]
), we set

\bar{Y}(s)=\bar{P}(s)\bar{X}(s)+\bar{\eta}(s),\qquad s\in[t,T].

Then

\bar{Y}(T)=G\bar{X}(T)+g.

Next, we need

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle-[(A+EI+FC)^{\top}\bar{Y}+(C+FI)^{\top}\bar{Z}+Q\bar{X}+S^{\top}\bar{u}+q]ds+\bar{Z}dW\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle=d\bar{Y}=\dot{P}\bar{X}ds+P\Big[\Big(A\bar{X}+B\bar{u}+b\Big)ds+\Big(C\bar{X}+D\bar{u}+\sigma\Big)dW\Big]+\dot{\eta}ds.\end{array}

Thus, the requirement for
\bar{Z}(s)
in (
5.34
) holds. Next, we require the drifts on the both sizes of (
5.34
) are equal:

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle 0=\dot{P}X+P(A\bar{X}+B\bar{u}+b)+\dot{\eta}+(A+EI+FC)^{\top}\bar{Y}+(C+FI)^{\top}\bar{Z}+Q\bar{X}+S^{\top}\bar{u}+q\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\quad=\dot{P}X+P(A\bar{X}+B\bar{u}+b)+\dot{\eta}+(A+EI+FC)^{\top}(P\bar{X}+\eta)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad+(C+FI)^{\top}P(C\bar{X}+D\bar{u}+\sigma)+Q\bar{X}+S^{\top}\bar{u}+q\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\quad=[\dot{P}+PA+A^{\top}P+C^{\top}PC+Q+EP+F(C^{\top}P+PC)]\bar{X}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad+(PB+C^{\top}PD+S^{\top}+FPD)\bar{u}+Pb+\dot{\eta}+(A+EI+FC)^{\top}\eta+(C+FI)^{\top}P\sigma+q\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\quad=[\dot{P}+{\bf Q}(P)+EP+F(C^{\top}P+PC)]\bar{X}+[{\bf S}(P)^{\top}+FPD]\bar{u}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad+(A+EI+FC)^{\top}\eta+(C+FI)^{\top}P\sigma+Pb+q+\dot{\eta}\end{array}

Note we also need:

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle 0=(B+FD)^{\top}\bar{Y}+D^{\top}\bar{Z}+S\bar{X}+R\bar{u}+r\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\quad=(B+DF)^{\top}(P\bar{X}+\eta)+D^{\top}P(C\bar{X}+D\bar{u}+\sigma)+S\bar{X}+R\bar{u}+r\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\quad=(B^{\top}P+D^{\top}PC+S+FD^{\top}P)\bar{X}+(R+D^{\top}PD)\bar{u}+(B+FD)^{\top}\eta+D^{\top}P\sigma+r\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\quad=[{\bf S}(P)+FD^{\top}P]\bar{X}+{\bf R}(P)\bar{u}+(B+FD)^{\top}\eta+D^{\top}P\sigma+r.\end{array}

Under the range conditions (
5.4
), we have

\bar{u}=-{\bf R}(P)^{\dagger}[{\bf S}(P)+FD^{\top}P]\bar{X}-{\bf R}(P)^{\dagger}[(B+FD)^{\top}\eta+D^{\top}P\sigma+r].

With such a control, we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle 0=[\dot{P}+{\bf Q}+EP+F(CP^{\top}+PC)]\bar{X}-[{\bf S}(P)^{\top}+FPD]{\bf R}(P)^{\dagger}[{\bf S}(P)+FD^{\top}P]\bar{X}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad-{\bf R}(P)^{\dagger}[{\bf S}(P)^{\top}+FPD][(B+FD)^{\top}\eta+D^{\top}P\sigma+r]\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad+(A+EI+FC)^{\top}\eta+(C+FI)^{\top}P\sigma+Pb+q+\dot{\eta}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\quad=\{(A+EI+FC)^{\top}-{\bf R}(P)^{\dagger}[{\bf S}(P)^{\top}+FPD][(B+FD)^{\top}\}\eta\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad+\{(C+FI)^{\top}-{\bf R}(P)^{\dagger}[{\bf S}(P)^{\top}+FPD]D^{\top}\}P\sigma+D^{\top}P\sigma+r]\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad-{\bf R}(P)^{\dagger}r+Pb+q+\dot{\eta}.\end{array}

Thus, (
5.35
)–(
5.36
) holds. This shows that the FBSDE
(
5.8
) is decoupled, for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then, we obtain the open-loop solvability of Problem (LQR) for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, under the same strategy
(\Psi(\cdot),v(\cdot))\in\boldsymbol{\Psi}[t,T]\times\mathscr{U}^{2}[t,T]
given by (
5.1
).
Hence, Problem (LQR) is closed-loop solvable at
t\in[0,T]
.

6
Conclusions

We formulated a stochastic LQ problem with recurve cost functional. It is naturally involved BSDE in
L^{1}
, which leads to some technicality in the correct formulation of Problem (LQ). We defined the open-loop and closed-loop solvability and their characteristics. It is proved that for Problem (LQR), its open-loop solvable at
(t,x)
if and only if the corresponding FBSDE (together with the stationary condition) is solvable on
[t,T]
; and its closed-loop solvable at
t
if and only if the Riccati differential equation admits a solution, and a terminal value problem is solvable.

We expect that by assuming uniform convexity conditions, Problem (LQR) is both open-loop and closed-loop solvable. We are not able to prove that for the time being. Thus, it is left open.

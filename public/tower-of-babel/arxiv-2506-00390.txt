A large scaling property of level sets for degenerate p p -Laplacian equations with logarithmic BMO matrix weights
Thanh-Nhan Nguyen, Minh-Phuong Tran

Abstract
In this study, we deal with generalized regularity properties for solutions to p p -Laplace equations with degenerate matrix weights. It has been already observed in previous interesting works [ 5 , 4 ] that gaining Calderón-Zygmund estimates for nonlinear equations with degenerate weights under the so-called log \log - BMO \mathrm{BMO} condition and minimal regularity assumption on the boundary. In this paper, we also follow this direction and extend general gradient estimates for level sets of the gradient of solutions up to more subtle function spaces. In particular, we construct a covering of the super-level sets of the spatial gradient | ∇ u | |\nabla u| with respect to a large scaling parameter via fractional maximal operators. Keywords. Calderón-Zygmund type estimates; Elliptic problems; p p -Laplacian type; Matrix weights; Fractional maximal operator. 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification. 35B65; 35J70; 35R05; 46E30.

Introduction

1.1.
Motivation and setting of the problems.

In this paper, we study the generalized regularity of weak solutions to the following non-homogeneous Dirichlet boundary value problem

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\mathrm{div}\left(\mathcal{L}_{p}(x,\nabla u)\right)&=\ -\mathrm{div}\left(\mathcal{L}_{p}(x,\mathbf{F})\right),\quad\mbox{in}\ \Omega,\\
{\hskip 28.45274pt}u&=\ \mathsf{g},\quad\mbox{on}\ \partial\Omega,\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a domain (=open subset) with non-smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
, for
n\geq 2
;
\mathbf{F}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a given vector field,
\mathsf{g}:\overline{\Omega}\to\mathbb{R}
is a measurable boundary datum. Further, we will motivate our interest in the specific case
\mathcal{L}_{p}(x,\zeta):=|\mathbb{P}(x)\zeta|^{p-2}\mathbb{P}^{2}(x)\zeta
, for
1<p<\infty
, that represents the elliptic operator driven by
p
-Laplacian involving the imposed matrix-valued weight
\mathbb{P}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\mathrm{sym}^{+}}
that is symmetric, positive definite and satisfies

\displaystyle|\mathbb{P}(x)|\cdot|\mathbb{P}^{-1}(x)|\leq\Lambda,\quad x\in\Omega,

(1.2)

for some
\Lambda\geq 1
and
|\cdot|
denotes the default matrix norm induced by the Euclidean vector norm. Let us further define a scalar weight
\omega:\Omega\to[0,\infty)
as follows

\displaystyle\omega(x)=|\mathbb{P}(x)|,\quad x\in\Omega,

(1.3)

and the condition (
1.2
) can be rewritten by

\displaystyle\Lambda^{-1}\omega(x)\mathrm{Id}_{n}\leq\mathbb{P}(x)\leq\omega(x)\mathrm{Id}_{n},\ \mbox{ or }\ \Lambda^{-1}\omega(x)|\zeta|\leq|\mathbb{P}(x)\zeta|\leq\omega(x)|\zeta|,

(1.4)

for all
x\in\Omega
and
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Here,
\mathrm{Id}_{n}
denotes the
n\times n
identity matrix. Suppose that
\omega^{p}
belongs to the class of Muckenhoupt weights
\mathrm{A}_{p}
, it is possible to consider the corresponding weighted Lebesgue space in the multiplicative sense
L^{p}_{\omega}(\Omega):=L^{p}(\Omega,\omega^{p}dx)
and the corresponding Sobolev space
W^{1,p}_{\omega}(\Omega)
(Muckenhoupt weights behave in a multiplicative form, see Section
2
for detailed definitions). Considering
\mathbf{F}\in L^{p}_{\omega}(\Omega)
and
\mathsf{g}\in W^{1,p}_{\omega}(\overline{\Omega})
, we say that a weak solution to such problem (
1.1
) is a map
u\in\mathsf{g}+W^{1,p}_{0,\omega}(\Omega)
satisfying the weak formulation

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\mathbb{P}(x)\nabla u|^{p-2}\mathbb{P}(x)\nabla u\cdot\mathbb{P}(x)\nabla\varphi dx=\int_{\Omega}|\mathbb{P}(x)\mathbf{F}|^{p-2}\mathbb{P}(x)\mathbf{F}\cdot\mathbb{P}(x)\nabla\varphi dx,

(1.5)

for all
\varphi\in W^{1,p}_{0,\omega}(\Omega)
.

Equation (
1.1
) appears naturally in different contexts as well as in variational models for many problems from mathematical physics. When
\mathbb{P}\equiv\mathrm{Id}_{n}
, the equation (
1.1
) reads a non-homogeneous
p
-harmonic function, and to our knowledge, this type of operator appears a lot in physics, especially in the radiation of heat, glaciology, rheology, plastic molding, etc. Otherwise, in the case when
p=n
and
\mathbf{F}=0
, the equation plays a crucial role in the theory of quasiconformal mappings, an important subject in complex analysis, as well as in physics and engineering. Note that by rewriting (
1.1
) as

\displaystyle-\mathrm{div}\left((\mathbb{P}^{2}(x)\nabla u\cdot\nabla u)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\mathbb{P}^{2}(x)\nabla u\right)=-\mathrm{div}\left(|\mathbb{P}(x)\mathbf{F}|^{p-2}\mathbb{P}^{2}(x)\mathbf{F}\right),

it emphasizes that the latter is the Euler-Lagrange equation of minimizers of the functional

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(w):=\int_{\Omega}{|\mathbb{P}(x)\nabla w|^{p}dx}-p\int_{\Omega}{|\mathbb{P}(x)\mathbf{F}|^{p-2}\mathbb{P}(x)\mathbf{F}\cdot(\mathbb{P}(x)\nabla w)dx}.

During the last few years, there have been extensive mathematical investigations of both solvability and regularity theory for various classes of problems whose nonlinearity is connected with a matrix weight. For instance, a lot of authors have been executing their research to analyze the linear case when
\mathcal{L}_{p}(x,\nabla u)\equiv\mathbb{A}(x)\nabla u
, where
\mathbb{A}:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\text{sym}}
is a uniformly elliptic weight, i.e.

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}|\zeta|^{2}\leq\langle\mathbb{A}(x)\zeta,\zeta\rangle\leq\lambda_{2}|\zeta|^{2},\quad\forall x\in\Omega,\quad\forall\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(1.6)

In particular, we refer the reader to

(1.7)

Assumption
\mathbf{A_{1}}

(Small

-
\mathrm{BMO}

Let
\mathbb{P}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\mathrm{sym}^{+}}

). For given

, we define the

\mathrm{BMO}

\displaystyle|\log\mathbb{P}|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\sup_{0<r\leq R}\fint_{B(z,r)}|\log\mathbb{P}(x)-\langle\mathbb{P}\rangle_{B(z,r)}^{\log}|dx},

(1.8)

where

is the logarithm average of
\mathbb{P}
over the ball

, given as

\displaystyle\langle\mathbb{P}\rangle_{B}^{\log}:=\exp\left(\fint_{B}\log\mathbb{P}(x)dx\right).

(1.9)

Here, for the sake of brevity, in case the ball
B
covers
\Omega

when no confusion arises. For given

-

\mathrm{BMO}

-small-

Assumption

(The

1.3.
Statements of main results.

2
Standard definitions and basic properties

This section recalls several real analysis definitions and tools that are needed in the paper. We also focus our attention in the
new
matrix weights and logarithms discussed in
[
5
,
4
]
. We first recall here the definition of
\mathrm{A}_{p}
-Muckenhoupt class of weights.

Definition 2.1

Let
1\leq q\leq\infty
and a locally integrable function
\mu:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,\infty)
. We say that this function belongs to the class of Muckenhoupt weights, i.e.
\mu\in\mathrm{A}_{q}
for
q\in(1,\infty)
, if and only if the term
[\mu]_{\mathrm{A}_{q}}
is finite, where

\displaystyle[\mu]_{\mathrm{A}_{q}}:=\displaystyle\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\varrho>0}\left(\fint_{B(y,\varrho)}[\mu(\zeta)]^{-\frac{1}{q-1}}d\zeta\right)^{q-1}\left(\fint_{B(y,\varrho)}\mu(\zeta)d\zeta\right).

We say that
\mu\in\mathrm{A}_{1}
if and only if there exists a constant
C
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{\varrho>0}\fint_{B(x,\varrho)}\mu(\zeta)d\zeta\leq C\mu(x),\mbox{ for a.e. }x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

In this case, we define by
[\mu]_{\mathrm{A}_{1}}
the smallest value of
C
for which this inequality holds. On the other hand, we say that
\mu\in\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
if and only if there exist
C>0
and
\nu
such that the following estimate holds

\displaystyle\mu(\mathcal{O})\leq C\left({|\mathcal{O}|}/{|B|}\right)^{\nu}\mu(B),

for all measurable subset
\mathcal{O}
of any ball
B
. It also remarks that if
\mu\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{+})
, we shall denote

\displaystyle\mu(\mathcal{O}):=\int_{\mathcal{O}}\mu(x)dx,

for some measurable set
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Furthermore, when
\mu\equiv 1
, it is known that
\mu(\mathcal{O})\equiv|\mathcal{O}|
.

Remark 2.2

It is worth mentioning here that
\mathrm{A}_{1}\subset\mathrm{A}_{q}\subset\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
for any
1<q<\infty
and
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}:=\cup_{q\geq 1}\mathrm{A}_{q}
. Moreover,
\mu\in\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
if and only if there exist
c_{1},c_{2}
and
\nu_{1},\nu_{2}>0
such that

\displaystyle c_{1}\left[{|\mathcal{O}|}/{|B|}\right]^{\nu_{1}}\mu(B)\leq\mu(\mathcal{O})\leq c_{2}\left[{|\mathcal{O}|}/{|B|}\right]^{\nu_{2}}\mu(B).

(2.1)

In this case, we shall write
[\mu]_{\mathrm{A}_{\infty}}=(c_{1},c_{2},\nu_{1},\nu_{2})
for the sake of brevity.

Remark 2.3

For any ball
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have another interesting property:
\mu^{q}\in\mathrm{A}_{q}
if and only if two following inequalities hold true

\displaystyle\left(\fint_{B}[\mu(x)]^{q}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq C_{1}\langle\mu\rangle_{B}^{\log},\quad\text{and}\quad\left(\fint_{B}[\mu(x)]^{-q^{\prime}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\leq C_{2}\frac{1}{\langle\mu\rangle_{B}^{\log}},

where one further notices that

\displaystyle\langle 1/\mu\rangle_{B}^{\log}=\exp\left(-\fint_{B}\log\mu(x)dx\right)=\frac{1}{\langle\mu\rangle_{B}^{\log}}.

As shown in
[
4
]
, one concludes that if
|\log\mathbb{P}|_{\mathrm{BMO}}
is small enough then
|\mathbb{P}|^{q}
belongs to Muckenhoupt class
\mathrm{A}_{q}
. We restate it in the next lemma and the detailed proof can be found in
[
5
]
.

Lemma 2.4

Let
1<q<\infty
,
\mathbb{P}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\mathrm{sym}^{+}}
be a matrix-valued weight function and
\mu=|\mathbb{P}|
. Then,
\mu^{q}\in\mathrm{A}_{q}
when
\mathbb{P}
satisfies the small
\log
-
\mathrm{BMO}
condition. It means that, there exists a constant
\kappa=\kappa(q)>0
such that if
|\log\mathbb{P}|_{\mathrm{BMO}}\leq\kappa
then
\mu^{q}\in\mathrm{A}_{q}

Lemma 3.2

Let
p>1
and two functions
\Psi:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
,
\mathbb{V}_{p}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
be defined by

\displaystyle\Psi(t):=\frac{1}{p}t^{p},\ t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\ \mbox{ and }\ \mathbb{V}_{p}(\zeta):=|\zeta|^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\zeta,\ \zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(3.2)

If
p\geq 2
then there exists a constant
C=C(p)>0
such that

\displaystyle\Psi(|\zeta_{1}-\zeta_{2}|)\leq C|\mathbb{V}_{p}(\zeta_{1})-\mathbb{V}_{p}(\zeta_{2})|^{2},\quad\forall\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(3.3)

Otherwise, if
1<p<2
, for every
\epsilon\in(0,1)
there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\Psi(|\zeta_{1}-\zeta_{2}|)\leq\epsilon\Psi(|\zeta_{1}|)+C{\epsilon}^{1-\frac{2}{p}}|\mathbb{V}_{p}(\zeta_{1})-\mathbb{V}_{p}(\zeta_{2})|^{2},\quad\forall\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(3.4)

Proof.
Let us first recall the shifted
N
-function associated to
\Psi
as below

\displaystyle\Psi_{a}(t):=\int_{0}^{t}\frac{s\Psi^{\prime}(\max\{a;s\})}{\max\{a;s\}}ds,\quad a,t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}.

For every
a,t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
, by a simple computation, we can show that

\displaystyle\Psi_{a}(t)\simeq(\max\{a,t\})^{p-2}t^{2}.

That means there exist two positive constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that

\displaystyle C_{1}\Psi_{a}(t)\leq(\max\{a,t\})^{p-2}t^{2}\leq C_{2}\Psi_{a}(t),\quad\mbox{ for all }a,t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}.

(3.5)

The proof of (
3.3
) is very simple for the first case
p\geq 2
. Indeed, by (
3.5
) one has

\displaystyle\Psi(t)=\frac{1}{p}t^{p}=\frac{1}{p}t^{p-2}t^{2}\leq\frac{1}{p}\big(\max\{a,t\}\big)^{p-2}t^{2}\leq\frac{1}{p}C_{2}\Psi_{a}(t).

(3.6)

Moreover, for all
\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, it is well-known that

|\mathbb{V}_{p}(\zeta_{1})-\mathbb{V}_{p}(\zeta_{2})|^{2}\simeq\Psi_{|\zeta_{2}|}(|\zeta_{1}-\zeta_{2}|),

which means there exist
C_{3},C_{4}>0
such that

\displaystyle C_{3}\Psi_{|\zeta_{2}|}(|\zeta_{1}-\zeta_{2}|)\leq|\mathbb{V}_{p}(\zeta_{1})-\mathbb{V}_{p}(\zeta_{2})|^{2}\leq C_{4}\Psi_{|\zeta_{2}|}(|\zeta_{1}-\zeta_{2}|).

(3.7)

Therefore, one may obtain (
3.3
) from (
3.6
) and (
3.7
). It is worth mentioning that all constants
C_{1},C_{2},C_{3},C_{4}
in (
3.5
) and (
3.7
) only depend on
p
.

We now show (
3.4
) for the remain case
1<p<2
. For all
\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we may use the decomposition

\displaystyle|\zeta_{1}-\zeta_{2}|^{p}

\displaystyle=\left(\big(|\zeta_{1}|+|\zeta_{2}|\big)^{p-2}|\zeta_{1}-\zeta_{2}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\big(|\zeta_{1}|+|\zeta_{2}|\big)^{\frac{(2-p)p}{2}}.

Combining two following basic inequalities

\displaystyle\max\{|\zeta_{1}-\zeta_{2}|;|\zeta_{2}|\}\leq|\zeta_{1}|+|\zeta_{2}|\mbox{ and }(|\zeta_{1}|+|\zeta_{2}|)^{p}\leq 4^{p}\big(|\zeta_{1}-\zeta_{2}|^{p}+|\zeta_{1}|^{p}\big),

one gets that

\displaystyle|\zeta_{1}-\zeta_{2}|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\big(\max\{|\zeta_{1}-\zeta_{2}|;|\zeta_{2}|\}\big)^{p-2}|\zeta_{1}-\zeta_{2}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\big(|\zeta_{1}|^{p}+|\zeta_{1}-\zeta_{2}|^{p}\big)^{\frac{2-p}{2}}.

This inequality is equivalent to

\displaystyle\Psi(|\zeta_{1}-\zeta_{2}|)\leq C\big[\Psi(|\zeta_{1}|)+\Psi(|\zeta_{1}-\zeta_{2}|)\big]^{\frac{2-p}{2}}\left[\Psi_{|\zeta_{2}|}(|\zeta_{1}-\zeta_{2}|)\right]^{\frac{p}{2}}.

(3.8)

For every
\epsilon\in(0,1)
, let us apply Young’s inequality on the right-hand side of (
3.8
), it follows that

\displaystyle\Psi(|\zeta_{1}-\zeta_{2}|)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\epsilon}{2}\big[\Psi(|\zeta_{1}|)+\Psi(|\zeta_{1}-\zeta_{2}|)\big]+C\epsilon^{1-\frac{2}{p}}\Psi_{|\zeta_{2}|}(|\zeta_{1}-\zeta_{2}|)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\Psi(|\zeta_{1}-\zeta_{2}|)+\frac{\epsilon}{2}\Psi(|\zeta_{1}|)+C\epsilon^{1-\frac{2}{p}}\Psi_{|\zeta_{2}|}(|\zeta_{1}-\zeta_{2}|),

which allows us to conclude (
3.4
) by combining with (
3.7
).

Lemma 3.6

Let

,

\displaystyle\left[\mathbf{M}\chi_{B(y,\varrho)}\right](x)\leq 2^{-(j-1)n},\quad\mbox{ for all }x\in\Omega_{j}^{\varrho}(y),

where
\Omega_{j}

\displaystyle\Omega_{j}^{\varrho}(y)=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\ 2^{j}\varrho\leq|x-y|<2^{j+1}\varrho\right\},\quad j\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}.

(3.20)

Proof.
Let

and

\displaystyle\left[\mathbf{M}\chi_{B(y,\varrho)}\right](x)

\displaystyle=\sup_{r>0}\fint_{B(x,r)}\chi_{B(y,\varrho)}(z)dz=\sup_{r>0}\mathcal{R}((y,\varrho);(x,r)),

where the ratio
\mathcal{R}((y,\varrho);(x,r))
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}((y,\varrho);(x,r)):=\frac{|B(y,\varrho)\cap B(x,r)|}{|B(x,r)|}.

Since

|z-x|\geq|x-y|-|y-z|>2^{j}\varrho-\varrho\geq 2^{j-1}\varrho,

Similarly, one can check that

\displaystyle\sup_{r\geq 2^{j+2}\varrho}\mathcal{R}((y,\varrho);(x,r))=\sup_{r\geq 2^{j+2}\varrho}{|B(x,r)|^{-1}}{|B(y,\varrho)|}=2^{-(j+2)n}.

\displaystyle\sup_{2^{j-1}\varrho<r<2^{j+2}\varrho}\mathcal{R}((y,\varrho);(x,r))

\displaystyle\mu(\mathbb{D})\leq C\varepsilon\mu(\mathbb{E}),

then (

(4.3)

Assume that
[\mu]_{\mathrm{A}_{\infty}}=(c_{1},c_{2},\nu_{1},\nu_{2})

2.7

-Lipschitz domain for some
r_{0}>0
. Here,
C
is a positive constant depending on
\mathfrak{q},\upsilon,\mathtt{data}
.

Proof of Theorem
4.3
.

Let
y\in\Omega
and
0<\varrho<D_{0}
, we consider
\mathbb{K}
as

\displaystyle\mathbb{K}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{\psi(y,\varrho)}\int_{\Omega(y,\varrho)}[\mathbf{M}_{\alpha}(|\mathbb{P}(x)\nabla u|^{p})]^{\mathfrak{q}}dx.

Since
\chi_{B(y,\varrho)}(x)\leq\left[\mathbf{M}\chi_{B(y,\varrho)}\right](x)\leq\left[\mathbf{M}\chi_{B(y,\varrho)}\right]^{\sigma}(x)\leq 1
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
a.e. and for all
\sigma\in(0,1)
, then it follows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{K}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\psi(y,\varrho)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}[\chi_{\Omega}(x)\mathbf{M}_{\alpha}(|\mathbb{P}(x)\nabla u|^{p})]^{\mathfrak{q}}\left[\mathbf{M}\chi_{B(y,\varrho)}\right]^{\sigma}(x)dx.

Thanks to
[
13
, Proposition 2]
, we conclude that
\left[\mathbf{M}\chi_{B(y,\varrho)}\right]^{\sigma}\in\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
. Hence, it is possible to apply Theorem
1.1
with
\mathfrak{s}=\mathfrak{q}
and
\mu=\left[\mathbf{M}\chi_{B(y,\varrho)}\right]^{\sigma}
. Then, we get that

\displaystyle\mathbb{K}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\psi(y,\varrho)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left[\chi_{\Omega}(x)\mathbf{M}_{\alpha}(|\mathbb{P}(x)\mathbf{G}|^{p})\right]^{\mathfrak{q}}\left[\mathbf{M}\chi_{B(y,\varrho)}\right]^{\sigma}(x)dx.

(4.27)

Using the notation in (
3.20
), we have the following decomposition

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{n}=B(y,2\varrho)\cup\left(\bigcup_{j=1}^{\infty}\Omega_{j}^{\varrho}(y)\right).

We may rewrite (
4.27
) as follows

\displaystyle\mathbb{K}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\psi(y,\varrho)}\int_{B(y,2\varrho)}\left[\chi_{\Omega}(x)\mathbf{M}_{\alpha}(|\mathbb{P}(x)\mathbf{G}|^{p})\right]^{\mathfrak{q}}\left[\mathbf{M}\chi_{B(y,\varrho)}\right]^{\sigma}(x)dx

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{C}{\psi(y,\varrho)}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega_{j}^{\varrho}(y)}\left[\chi_{\Omega}(x)\mathbf{M}_{\alpha}(|\mathbb{P}(x)\mathbf{G}|^{p})\right]^{\mathfrak{q}}\left[\mathbf{M}\chi_{B(y,\varrho)}\right]^{\sigma}(x)dx.

Applying condition (
4.25
) and inequality (
3.19
) in Lemma
3.6
, one obtains that

\displaystyle\mathbb{K}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\frac{2^{\upsilon}}{\psi(y,2\varrho)}\int_{B(y,2\varrho)}\left[\chi_{\Omega}(x)\mathbf{M}_{\alpha}(|\mathbb{P}(x)\mathbf{G}|^{p})\right]^{\mathfrak{q}}dx\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+C\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}2^{-(j-1)n\sigma}\left(\frac{2^{(j+1)\upsilon}}{\psi(y,2^{j+1}\varrho)}\int_{B(y,2^{j+1}\varrho)}\left[\chi_{\Omega}(x)\mathbf{M}_{\alpha}(|\mathbb{P}(x)\mathbf{G}|^{p})\right]^{\mathfrak{q}}dx\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\upsilon+n\sigma}C\left[1+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left(2^{\upsilon-n\sigma}\right)^{j}\right]\|\mathbf{M}_{\alpha}(|\mathbb{P}(x)\mathbf{G}|^{p})\|_{\mathrm{M}^{\mathfrak{q},\psi}(\Omega)}^{\mathfrak{q}}.

(4.28)

Since
\upsilon<n
, it is possible to fix
\sigma\in(0,1)
in (
4
) such that

2^{\upsilon-n\sigma}<1
. It implies that the series
\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left(2^{\upsilon-n\sigma}\right)^{j}
is finite. The proof of (
4.26
) is now complete.

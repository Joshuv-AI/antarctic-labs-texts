Asymptotic flocking dynamics of Relativistic-Cucker-Smale particles immersed in incompressible Navier-Stokes equations
Shenglun Yan, Weiyuan Zou

Abstract
In this paper, we propose a coupled system describing the interaction between the Relativistic Cucker-Smale model and the incompressible Navier-Stokes equations via a drag force, and establish a global existence theory as well as the time-asymptotic behavior of the proposed model in 𝕋 3 \mathbb{T}^{3} . It is shown that the coupled system exhibits an exponential alignment under some specific assumptions, and that weak solutions exist globally for general initial data.



College of Mathematics and Physics,
Beijing University of Chemical Technology, Beijing 100029, P. R. China

Key Words.
Relativistic-Cucker-Smale model, incompressible Navier-Stokes equation, asymptotic stability, weak solution.

x\in\mathbb{T}^{3}
and

\small{\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}f+\hat{v}(w)\cdot\nabla_{x}f+\nabla_{w}\cdot(F[f,u]f)=0,\\
&\partial_{t}u+(u\cdot\nabla_{x})u+\nabla_{x}p-\mu\Delta_{x}u=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}F_{d}[u]f\mathrm{d}w,\\
&\nabla_{x}\cdot u=0,(t,x,w)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3},\hskip 9.24994ptF[f,u]:=L[f]+F_{d}.\end{aligned}\right.}

(1.1)

subject to initial data:

(f(0,x,w),u(0,x))=(f^{\text{in}}(x,w),u^{\text{in}}(x)),\quad(x,w)\in\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3},

and the periodic boundary conditions on

u^{\text{in}}|_{\Omega_{j}}=u^{\text{in}}|_{\Omega_{j+3}},\;\nabla_{x}u^{\text{in}}|_{\Omega_{j}}=\nabla_{x}u^{\text{in}}|_{\Omega_{j+3}},\;f^{\text{in}}|_{\Omega_{j}}=f^{\text{in}}|_{\Omega_{j+3}},

with
\Omega_{j}:=\partial\mathbb{T}^{3}\cap\{x_{j}=0\}

\Omega_{j+3}:=\partial\mathbb{T}^{3}\cap\{x_{j}=1\}(j=1,2,3)
. Here, the relativistic velocity
w

\hat{w}:\ B_{c}\left(0\right)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}

w=\hat{w}\left(v\right)=\frac{v}{\sqrt{1-\frac{|v|^{2}}{c^{2}}}}+\frac{v}{c^{2}-|v|^{2}}.

The meaning of

is reversible. We denote the inverse mapping as
\hat{v}

L\left[f\right]\left(t,x,w\right):=-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}{\phi}\left(|x-x_{*}|\right)\left(\hat{v}\left(w\right)-\hat{v}\left(w_{*}\right)\right)f\left(t,x_{*},w_{*}\right)\mathrm{d}x_{*}\mathrm{d}w_{*},

Here

F_{d}

\mathbb{R}_{+}

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we first construct the RCS-NS model using friction theory. Then, we will display some known results. These results will play an important role in the research of the RCS-NS model. Finally, we will give the conservation laws and energy dissipation of RCS-NS model.

2.1
Construction of RCS-NS model

We again define the map
\hat{w}:B_{c}(0)\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
by

\hat{w}(v):=F(v)v=\Gamma(v)\biggl(1+\frac{\Gamma(v)}{c^{2}}\biggr)v,\quad\Gamma(v):=\frac{c}{\sqrt{c^{2}-|v|^{2}}}.

Then, it was verified in
[
12
]
that
\hat{w}
is one-to-one, and it has an inverse function
\hat{v}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to B_{c}(0).
Furthermore, we consider the map
g:|v|\mapsto|\hat{w}(v)|
defined by

g(|v|):=\frac{c|v|}{\sqrt{c^{2}-|v|^{2}}}+\frac{|v|}{c^{2}-|v|^{2}}

and its inverse
g^{-1}:|w|\mapsto|\hat{v}(w)|.
As long as there is no confusion, we also suppress “hat” notations in
\hat{v},\hat{w}
notations and simply write
w
and
v
instead of
\hat{w}
and
\hat{v}
, respectively. We also note that

w_{i}:=F(v_{i})v_{i}.

Observe that it is straightforward to see that
g
and
g^{-1}
are increasing functions.

The authors has provided the form of Relativistic Cucker-Smale model in
[
10
]
, as following,

\small{\left\{\begin{aligned} &\displaystyle{\frac{\mathrm{d}x_{i}}{\mathrm{d}t}=v_{i},\hskip 9.24994pti\in\left[N\right]:=\left\{1,2,...,N\right\},\hskip 9.24994ptt>0,}\\
&\displaystyle{\frac{\mathrm{d}w_{i}}{\mathrm{d}t}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}{\phi}\left(|x_{i}-x_{j}|\right)\left(v_{j}-v_{i}\right).}\end{aligned}\right.}

When the number of particle is to large, it is too complex if we continue using ODE to describe its moving. In a mean-field regime
N\gg 1
, the RCS model can be effectively approximated by the corresponding mean-field kinetic equation, namely, the relativistic Kinetic Cucker-Smale (RKCS) equation for one-particle distribution function
f=f(t,x,w)
,

\small{\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}f+\hat{v}(w)\cdot\nabla_{x}f+\nabla_{w}\cdot(L[f]f)=0,\\
&L[f](t,x,w):=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\phi(|x_{*}-x|)(\hat{v}(w)-\hat{v}(w_{*}))f(t,x_{*},w_{*})\mathrm{d}x_{*}\mathrm{d}w_{*},\\
&f(0,x,w)=f^{\mathrm{in}}(x,w).\end{aligned}\right.}

The mean-field limit has been proved strictly in
[
11
]
.

Next, we will consider the fluid part. Because the fluid considered in this study is macroscopic and bulk, we still use the incompressible Navier-Stokes equations without considering relativistic effects. This consideration is relatively rational in physics and can appropriately reduce the difficulty of mathematical processing. The famous incompressible Navier-Stokes equations has the following form,

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u+(u\cdot\nabla)u+\nabla p-\mu\Delta u=0,\\
\nabla\cdot u=0.\end{cases}

Finally, we will consider the interaction between particles and fluid. From
[
26
,
27
]
, we know that the interaction is through friction forces. Hence, we shall add

-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(u(t,x)-w)f(t,x,w)\mathrm{d}w

on the R.H.S of the momentum part of Navier-Stokes equations, and add

\nabla_{w}\cdot((u-w)f)

on the L.H.S of the RCS part. So far, we have finished the construction of the RCS-NS model (
1.1
).

2.4
Conservation laws and energy dissipation

The following lemmas will describe the change rule of the mass, momentum and energy of the RCS-NS system.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
[f(t,x,w),u(t,x)]
be a smooth solution to (
1.1
), and let
f(t,x,w)
decay to zero sufficiently fast in the
w
-variable. Then, we have the following assertions.

(1) The conservation of mass:

\displaystyle{\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}f(t,x,w)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w=0},

(2) The conservation of momentum:

\displaystyle{\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}wf(t,x,w)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}u\mathrm{d}x\right)=0,}

(3) The estimate of total energy:

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}Ef(t,x,w)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right)+\mu\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|\nabla u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}(u-\hat{v}(w))\cdot(u-w)f\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{6}\times\mathbb{R}^{6}}\phi(|x-x_{*}|)|\hat{v}(w_{*})-\hat{v}(w)|^{2}f(t,x,w))f(t,x_{*},w_{*})\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w\mathrm{d}x_{*}\mathrm{d}w_{*}.

(2.8)

Proof.

(1) For the conservation of mass, we integrate the first equation (
1.1
) over
\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
to obtain

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}f(t,x,w)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}f(t,x,w)\nabla_{x}\cdot\hat{v}(w)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w=0

(2.9)

(2) For the conservation of momentum, we multiply the first equation (
1.1
) by
w
and integrate the resulting equation over
\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
to obtain

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}wf(t,x,w)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w=-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}w\nabla_{w}\cdot[L[f]+F_{d})f]\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}(L[f]+F_{d})f\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w.

(2.10)

Since

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}L[f]f\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{6}\times\mathbb{R}^{6}}\phi(|x-x_{*}|)(\hat{v}(w_{*})-\hat{v}(w))f(t,x_{*},w_{*})f(t,x,w)\mathrm{d}x_{*}\mathrm{d}w_{*}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{6}\times\mathbb{R}^{6}}\phi(|x-x_{*}|)(\hat{v}(w)-\hat{v}(w_{*}))f(t,x,w)f(t,x_{*},w_{*})\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w\mathrm{d}x_{*}\mathrm{d}w_{*}

\displaystyle\quad\text{by }(x,w)\leftrightarrow(x_{*},w_{*})

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}L[f]f\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w,

(2.11)

this implies

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}L[f]f\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w=0.

(2.12)

Therefore, it follows from (
2.10
) that

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}wf(t,x,w)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}F_{d}f\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}(u(t,x)-w)f\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w.

(2.13)

On the other hand, it follows from the second equation in (
1.1
) that

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}u\mathrm{d}x=-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}(u(t,x)-w)f\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w.

(2.14)

Hence, we deduce from (
2.13
) and (
2.14
) to obtain the conservation of momentum.

(3)For the estimate of energy, we multiply the first equation (
1.1
) by
E(v)
and integrate the resulting equation over
\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
to obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}Ef(t,x,w)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}E\nabla_{w}\cdot[L[f]+F_{d})f]\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla_{w}E\cdot(L[f]+F_{d})f\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\hat{v}(w)\cdot(L[f]+F_{d})f\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w.

(2.15)

Since

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\hat{v}(w)\cdot L[f]f\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{6}\times\mathbb{R}^{6}}\phi(|x-x_{*}|)\hat{v}(w)\cdot(\hat{v}(w_{*})-\hat{v}(w))f(t,x_{*},w_{*})f(t,x,w)\mathrm{d}x_{*}\mathrm{d}w_{*}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{6}\times\mathbb{R}^{6}}\phi(|x-x_{*}|)\hat{v}(w_{*})\cdot(\hat{v}(w_{*})-\hat{v}(w)f(t,x,w))f(t,x_{*},w_{*})\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w\mathrm{d}x_{*}\mathrm{d}w_{*}

\displaystyle\quad\text{by }(x,w)\leftrightarrow(x_{*},w_{*})

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{6}\times\mathbb{R}^{6}}\phi(|x-x_{*}|)|\hat{v}(w_{*})-\hat{v}(w)|^{2}f(t,x,w))f(t,x_{*},w_{*})\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w\mathrm{d}x_{*}\mathrm{d}w_{*},

(2.16)

then we have

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}Ef(t,x,w)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\hat{v}(w)\cdot(L[f]+F_{d})f\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{6}\times\mathbb{R}^{6}}\phi(|x-x_{*}|)|\hat{v}(w_{*})-\hat{v}(w)|^{2}f(t,x,w))f(t,x_{*},w_{*})\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w\mathrm{d}x_{*}\mathrm{d}w_{*}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\hat{v}(w)\cdot(u(t,x)-w)f\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w.

(2.17)

On the other hand, it follows from the second equation in (
1.1
) that

\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x+\mu\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|\nabla u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x=-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}u\cdot(u-w)f\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w.

(2.18)

Hence, we deduce from (
2.17
) and (
2.18
) the dissipative structure of the energy.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

Unlike non-relativistic situations, the energy estimate (
2.8
) does not imply the monotonicity of the total energy as it is. More precisely, it is not clear to get that the total energy is not increasing.

3
Asymptomatic flocking estimate

If the fluid velocity is larger than the particle velocity, i.e.
u(x,t)>w
, then
\displaystyle{-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}F_{d}f\mathrm{d}x_{*}\mathrm{d}w_{*}}

is negative; hence, the fluid will decelerate to match the particle’s relativistic velocity. In contrast, if the fluid velocity is smaller than the relativistic velocity of the particle, then the drag force makes the fluid accelerate, so alignment is achieved. Therefore, we can view the drag force as a kind of an alignment force.

By conducting a detailed analysis of the Lyapunov functional, we demonstrate the exponential stability of the RCS-NS system in this subsection.

Lemma 3.1

(Time-variation estimate for
\mathcal{E}^{w}
)
.

Let
[f(t,x,w),u(t,x)]
be a smooth solution to (
1.1
) satisfying

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}f^{\text{in}}(x,w)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w=1,\quad\bigcup\limits_{t\geq 0}\Omega_{w}(f,t)\subset B_{W_{0}}(0)\quad\text{for some}\quad W_{0}>0,

(3.1)

and
f(t,x,w)
decays to zero sufficiently fast in the
w
-variable. Then, we have

\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathcal{E}^{w}(t)}{\mathrm{d}t}\leq-2C(c,W_{0})\phi(\sqrt{3})\mathcal{E}^{w}(t)+2\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}(w-w_{c}(t))\cdot(u-w)f\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w.

(3.2)

Proof.

By direct calculation, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathcal{E}^{w}(t)}{\mathrm{d}t}

\displaystyle=-2w_{c}(t)\cdot\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}(w-w_{c}(t))f(t,x,w)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}|w-w_{c}(t)|^{2}\partial_{t}f(t,x,w)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w

\displaystyle=:\mathcal{I}_{1}(t)+\mathcal{I}_{2}(t).

(3.3)

To estimate
\mathcal{I}_{1}
, we use the definition of
w_{c}(t)
and unit mass assumption to obtain

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}(w-w_{c}(t))f(t,x,w)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w=0.

(3.4)

Therefore, we have
\mathcal{I}_{1}(t)=0
.

To estimate
\mathcal{I}_{2}
, we use

\partial_{t}f=-\hat{v}(w)\cdot\nabla_{x}f-\nabla_{w}\cdot[(L[f]+F_{d})f],

(3.5)

and integration by parts to find

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{2}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}|w-w_{c}(t)|^{2}\partial_{t}f\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w

\displaystyle=2\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}(w-w_{c}(t))\cdot[(L[f]+F_{d})f]\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w

\displaystyle=2\int_{\mathbb{T}^{6}\times\mathbb{R}^{6}}\phi(|x-x_{*}|)(w-w_{c}(t))\cdot(\hat{v}(w_{*})-\hat{v}(w))f(t,x_{*},w_{*})f(t,x,w)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w\mathrm{d}x_{*}\mathrm{d}w_{*}

\displaystyle\quad+2\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}(w-w_{c}(t))\cdot(u-w)f\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w

\displaystyle=:\mathcal{I}_{21}(t)+\mathcal{I}_{22}(t)

(3.6)

Since for
\forall w_{1},w_{2}\in B_{W_{0}}(0)
, the map
\hat{w}:B_{c}(0)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfies

|w_{1}-w_{2}|=|\hat{w}(v_{1})-\hat{w}(v_{2})|\leq|\nabla_{v}\hat{w}|_{\text{op}}|v_{1}-v_{2}|

(3.7)

where
|\nabla_{v}\hat{w}|_{\text{op}}
denotes the operator norm of the matrix
\nabla_{v}\hat{w}
. Since
\nabla_{v}\hat{w}
is asymmetric positive definite matrix,the operator norm is
nothing but its largest eigenvalue, which is
\lambda_{2}(v)
in our case. Therefore, one has

|w_{1}-w_{2}|=|\hat{w}(v_{1})-\hat{w}(v_{2})|\leq|\nabla_{v}\hat{w}|_{\text{op}}|v_{1}-v_{2}|\leq\left(\sup\limits_{v\in B_{\hat{v}(W_{0})}(0)}\lambda_{2}(v)\right)|v_{1}-v_{2}|=:C_{0}(c,W_{0})|v_{1}-v_{2}|.

(3.8)

The first term
\mathcal{I}_{21}
can be preliminarily estimated as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{21}(t)

\displaystyle=2\int_{\mathbb{T}^{6}\times\mathbb{R}^{6}}\phi(|x-x_{*}|)(w-w_{c}(t))\cdot(\hat{v}(w_{*})-\hat{v}(w))f(t,x_{*},w_{*})f(t,x,w)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w\mathrm{d}x_{*}\mathrm{d}w_{*}

\displaystyle=2\int_{\mathbb{T}^{6}\times\mathbb{R}^{6}}\phi(|x-x_{*}|)w\cdot(\hat{v}(w_{*})-\hat{v}(w))f(t,x_{*},w_{*})f(t,x,w)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w\mathrm{d}x_{*}\mathrm{d}w_{*}

\displaystyle=-2\int_{\mathbb{T}^{6}\times\mathbb{R}^{6}}\phi(|x-x_{*}|)w_{*}\cdot(\hat{v}(w_{*})-\hat{v}(w))f(t,x_{*},w_{*})f(t,x,w)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w\mathrm{d}x_{*}\mathrm{d}w_{*}

\displaystyle\quad\text{by }(x,w)\leftrightarrow(x_{*},w_{*})

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{6}\times\mathbb{R}^{6}}\phi(|x-x_{*}|)(w_{*}-w)\cdot(\hat{v}(w_{*})-\hat{v}(w))f(t,x_{*},w_{*})f(t,x,w)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w\mathrm{d}x_{*}\mathrm{d}w_{*}

\displaystyle\leq-\phi(\sqrt{3})\int_{\mathbb{T}^{6}\times\mathbb{R}^{6}}\frac{c^{2}+1}{c^{2}}|\hat{v}(w_{*})-\hat{v}(w)|^{2}f(t,x_{*},w_{*})f(t,x,w)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w\mathrm{d}x_{*}\mathrm{d}w_{*}

\displaystyle\leq-\phi(\sqrt{3})\int_{\mathbb{T}^{6}\times\mathbb{R}^{6}}\frac{c^{2}+1}{c^{2}[C_{0}(c,W_{0})]^{2}}|w_{*}-w|^{2}f(t,x_{*},w_{*})f(t,x,w)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w\mathrm{d}x_{*}\mathrm{d}w_{*}

(3.9)

By applying the definition of
w_{c}(t)
, we have

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{6}\times\mathbb{R}^{6}}(w_{*}-w_{c})\cdot(w-w_{c})f(t,x_{*},w_{*})f(t,x,w)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w\mathrm{d}x_{*}\mathrm{d}w_{*}=\left|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}(w-w_{c})f(t,x,w)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w\right|^{2}=0.

(3.10)

Then, by applying the triangle equality

|w_{*}-w|^{2}=|w_{*}-w_{c}|^{2}+|w-w_{c}|^{2}+2(w_{*}-w_{c})\cdot(w-w_{c})
, the first term
\mathcal{I}_{21}
can be finally estimated as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{21}(t)

\displaystyle\leq-\phi(\sqrt{3})\int_{\mathbb{T}^{6}\times\mathbb{R}^{6}}\frac{c^{2}+1}{c^{2}[C_{0}(c,W_{0})]^{2}}(|w_{*}-w_{c}|^{2}+|w-w_{c}|^{2})f(t,x_{*},w_{*})f(t,x,w)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w\mathrm{d}x_{*}\mathrm{d}w_{*}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2(c^{2}+1)}{c^{2}[C_{0}(c,W_{0})]^{2}}\phi(\sqrt{3})\mathcal{E}^{w}(t)=:-2C(c,W_{0})\phi(\sqrt{3})\mathcal{E}^{w}(t).

(3.11)

On the other hand, we split term
\mathcal{I}_{22}
as two terms:

\mathcal{I}_{22}=2\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}(w-w_{c}(t))\cdot(u-w)f\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w=2\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}w\cdot(u-w)f\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w-2\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}w_{c}(t)\cdot(u-w)f\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w.

(3.12)

We finally combine (
3.11
) and (
3.12
) to obtain the desired result.
∎

Lemma 3.2

Lemma 3.3

(Time-variation estimate for
\mathcal{D}
)
.

Let

be a smooth solution to (

(1)
\displaystyle{\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}f^{\text{in}}(x,w)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w=1,\quad\bigcup\limits_{t\geq 0}\Omega_{w}(f,t)\subset B_{W_{0}}(0)\quad\text{for some}\quad W_{0}>0}
.

(2)
\displaystyle{||\rho_{f}||_{L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{T}^{3}\right)}<\infty,\quad\text{where}\quad\rho_{f}(t,x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f(t,x,w)\mathrm{d}w}
and
f(t,x,w)
decay to zero sufficiently fast in the
w
-variable. Then, we have

\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{L}(t)\leq C(\rho_{f},\mu)\mathcal{D}(t)

(3.22)

Proof.

By direct calculation and using Young’s inequality, for
\forall\varepsilon>0
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u-w|^{2}f\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u-u_{c}+u_{c}-w_{c}+w_{c}-w|^{2}f\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w

\displaystyle=|u_{c}-w_{c}|^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u-u_{c}|^{2}f\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}|w_{c}-w|^{2}f\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w+2\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}(u-u_{c})\cdot(u_{c}-w)f\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w

\displaystyle\geq|u_{c}-w_{c}|^{2}+\left(1-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u-u_{c}|^{2}f\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}|w_{c}-w|^{2}f\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w-\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u_{c}-w|^{2}f\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w

\displaystyle=(1-\varepsilon)|u_{c}-w_{c}|^{2}+\left(1-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u-u_{c}|^{2}f\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w+(1-\varepsilon)\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}|w_{c}-w|^{2}f\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w.

(3.23)

We set
\displaystyle{\varepsilon=\frac{1}{2}}
to obtain

\frac{1}{2}|u_{c}-w_{c}|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}|w_{c}-w|^{2}f\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w\leq\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\rho_{f}(t,x)|u-u_{c}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u-w|^{2}f\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w.

(3.24)

Subsequently, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{L}(t)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}(\frac{1}{2}+\rho_{f})|u-u_{c}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u-w|^{2}f\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w+C(c,W_{0})\phi(\sqrt{3})\mathcal{E}^{w}(t)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1}{2}+||\rho_{f}||_{L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{T}^{3}\right)}\right)\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|u-u_{c}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u-w|^{2}f\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w+C(c,W_{0})\phi(\sqrt{3})\mathcal{E}^{w}(t)

\displaystyle\leq C(\rho_{f})\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|\nabla u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u-w|^{2}f\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w+C(c,W_{0})\phi(\sqrt{3})\mathcal{E}^{w}(t)

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{1,\frac{C(\rho_{f})}{\mu}\right\}\mathcal{D}(t)=:C(\rho_{f},\mu)\mathcal{D}(t),

(3.25)

where we have used the
Poincar\acute{e}^{\prime}s
inequality

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|u-u_{c}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\leq C_{P}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|\nabla u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x.

(3.26)

Here

is the
Poincar\acute{e}^{\prime}s
constant.
∎

Proof of Theorem 1.1
.

By recalling Lemma
3.2
and Lemma
3.3
, we have

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{L}(t)+2\mathcal{D}(t)\leq 0,\quad\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{L}(t)\leq C(\rho_{f},\mu)\mathcal{D}(t).

(3.27)

Therefore, we can easily obtain that

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{L}(t)+\frac{1}{C(\rho_{f},\mu)}\mathcal{L}(t)\leq\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{L}(t)+2\mathcal{D}(t)\leq 0.

(3.28)

By applying
Gr\ddot{o}nwall^{\prime}s
inequality, we reach the conclusion

\mathcal{L}(t)\leq\mathcal{L}(0)\exp\{-\frac{1}{C(\rho_{f},\mu)}t\}.

(3.29)

Remark 3.1
.

From Theorem
1.1
, we know that
\mathcal{E}^{w}(t)\leq\mathcal{L}(t)\rightarrow 0
. This denotes the formation of velocity alignment in probability. It can be seen from the Chebyshev inequality as follows. For any
\sigma>0
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{w}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}f|w_{c}(t)-w|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w\geq\int_{\{|w_{c}(t)-w|>\sigma\}}f|w_{c}(t)-w|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w

\displaystyle\geq\sigma^{2}\int_{\{|w_{c}(t)-w|>\sigma\}}f\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w=\sigma^{2}\mathbb{P}[|w_{c}(t)-w|>\sigma].

This implies the weak flocking estimate,

\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{P}[|w_{c}(t)-w|>\sigma]\leq\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathcal{E}^{w}(t)=0.

4
A global existence of weak solutions

In this section, we will construct the form of weak solutions for the RCS-NS system and prove their existence. Fortunately, Reference
[
29
]
has already provided the form of weak solutions for the coupling structure of Vlasov-type equations and incompressible Navier-Stokes equations and provided ideas and techniques for proving the existence of weak solutions. Therefore, the work in this section will draw on the ideas of Reference
[
29
]
and, if necessary, directly use the estimates from
[
29
]

[
29
]
before reading this section.

First, we introduce some function spaces as follows:

\mathcal{H}:=\{w\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})\mid\nabla\cdot w=0\},\quad\mathcal{V}:=\{w\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3})\mid\nabla\cdot w=0\},

and we denote the dual space of
\mathcal{V}
by
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}.
Next, without loss of generality, we set the viscosity
\mu=1
and define the concept of the weak solutions to system (
1.1
) as follows.

Definition 4.1
.

For a given
T\in(0,\infty)
, the pair
(f,u)
is a weak solution of (
1.1
) on the time interval
[0,T)
if and only if the pair satisfies the following conditions:

(1)
f\in L^{\infty}(0,T;(L^{\infty}\cap L_{+}^{1})(\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}))
,
|w|^{2}f\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})).

(2)
u\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathcal{H})\cap L^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{V})\cap\mathcal{C}^{0}([0,T],\mathcal{V}^{\prime}).

(3) For all
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}^{1}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})
with compact support in
w
, such that

\varphi(T,\cdot,\cdot)=0
,

-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}f\left(\partial_{t}\varphi+\hat{v}(w)\cdot\nabla\varphi+F[f,u]\cdot\nabla_{w}\varphi\right)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w\mathrm{d}s=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}f^{\mathrm{in}}\varphi(0,\cdot,\cdot)\>\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w.

(4) For all
\psi\in\mathcal{C}^{1}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{3})
such that
\nabla\cdot\psi=0
,for a.e.
t\in[0,T]

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}u(t)\cdot\psi(t)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\left(-u\cdot\partial_{t}\psi+(u\cdot\nabla)u\cdot\psi+\nabla u:\nabla\psi\right)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}(u-w)\cdot\psi f\>\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w\mathrm{d}s+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}u^{\mathrm{in}}\cdot\psi(\cdot,0)\>\mathrm{d}x.

Now, we are ready to state our first main result on the global existence of weak solutions as follows.

4.1
A brief introduction to a regularized system

Let
\varepsilon>0

m\geq 0,\quad m\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),\quad\mathrm{supp~}m\subset B_{1}(0),\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}m(x)\mathrm{d}x=1.

(4.1)

The existence of
m
can be ensured. For example,

\displaystyle m\left(x\right)=\begin{cases}C\exp\left(\frac{1}{\left|x\right|^{2}-1}\right),\text{ if}\,\,\left|x\right|<1\\
0,\text{ if}\,\,\left|x\right|\geq 1\\
\end{cases},\quad\text{ where }C:=\left(\int_{B_{1}(0)}\exp\left(\frac{1}{\left|x\right|^{2}-1}\right)\mathrm{d}x\right)^{-1}.

(4.2)

and we set a family of smooth mollifiers:

m_{\varepsilon}(x):=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{3}}m(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}),\;\varepsilon>0.

(4.3)

Furthermore, we also introduce a family of smooth cut-off functions

\mathrm{supp~}\gamma_{\varepsilon}\subset B_{{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}}(0),\quad 0\leq\gamma_{\varepsilon}\leq 1,\quad\gamma_{\varepsilon}=1\mathrm{~on~}B_{{\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}}}(0),\quad\gamma_{\varepsilon}\to 1\mathrm{~as~}\varepsilon\to 0.

(4.4)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}f_{\varepsilon}+\hat{v}(w)\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{\varepsilon}+\nabla_{w}\cdot(F[f_{\varepsilon},u_{\varepsilon}]f_{\varepsilon})=0,\\
&\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon}+((m_{\varepsilon}\ast u_{\varepsilon})\cdot\nabla)u_{\varepsilon}+\nabla p_{\varepsilon}-\mu\Delta u_{\varepsilon}=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}F_{d}[u_{\varepsilon}]f_{\varepsilon}\gamma_{\varepsilon}(w)\mathrm{d}w,\\
&\nabla\cdot u_{\varepsilon}=0,(t,x,w)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3},\end{aligned}\right.

(4.5)

subject to regularized initial data:

f_{\varepsilon}(0,x,w)=f_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{in}}(x,w),\quad u_{\varepsilon}(0,x)=u_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{in}}(x),

(4.6)

where
\ast
denotes the convolution with respect to the spatial variable
x

F[f_{\varepsilon},u_{\varepsilon}](t,x,w)=F[f_{\varepsilon}](t,x,w)+m_{\varepsilon}*u_{\varepsilon}(t,x)-w,

and
f_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{in}}
and
u_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{in}}
are
C^{\infty}
approximations of
f^{\mathrm{in}}
and
u^{\mathrm{in}}
such that
\displaystyle{\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{in}}}
d
z=1
for any
\varepsilon>0
,
f_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{in}}
converges to
f^{\mathrm{in}}
strongly in

for all
p\in[1,\infty)
, and weakly

,
M_{2}f_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{in}}
is uniformly bounded with respect to
\varepsilon

M_{2}f^{\mathrm{in}}
in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
and
(u_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{in}})
strongly converges to
u^{\mathrm{in}}
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}).
Furthermore, we assume that
f_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{in}}
has a compact support in
w
.

Similarly to Definition
4.1
, we define the weak solution of the regularization system (
4.5

Definition 4.2
.

For a given

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{in}}(t)\varphi(t,\cdot,\cdot)\>\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{\varepsilon}\left(\partial_{t}\varphi+\hat{v}(w)\cdot\nabla\varphi+F[f_{\varepsilon},u_{\varepsilon}]\cdot\nabla_{w}\varphi\right)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon}(t)\cdot\psi(t)\mathrm{d}x

4.2
The solvability of the regularized system

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}f+\hat{v}(w)\cdot\nabla f+\nabla_{w}\cdot(F[f,u]f)=0,\quad(x,w)\in\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3},\\

(4.10)

We use the method of characteristics to prove the existence of classical solution and

\begin{cases}\displaystyle{\frac{\mathrm{d}x(t)}{\mathrm{d}t}=\hat{v}(w(t))=:v(t),\quad t>0},\\

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi(|x^{n-1}(s)-x_{*}|)[v^{n-1}(s)-v^{n}]f^{n}\mathrm{d}x_{*}\mathrm{d}w_{*}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi(|x^{n-1}(s)-x_{*}|)(\hat{v}(w_{*})-v^{n-1}(s))[f^{n}(s)-f^{n-1}(s)]\mathrm{d}x_{*}\mathrm{d}w_{*}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=:\mathcal{K}_{61}(t)+\mathcal{K}_{62}(t)+\mathcal{K}_{63}(t).

(4.31)

We now estimate the terms
\mathcal{K}_{6i},i=1,2,3
as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{61}(t)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\left(\|z^{n}(s)-z^{n-1}(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v_{*}|f^{n}\mathrm{d}x_{*}dv_{*}\right)\mathrm{d}s+C(\varepsilon)\int_{0}^{t}|v^{n}(s)|\left\|z^{n}(s)-z^{n-1}(s)\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\left(\|z^{n}(s)-z^{n-1}(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}|w_{*}|f^{n}\mathrm{d}x_{*}dv_{*}\right)\mathrm{d}s+C(\varepsilon)\int_{0}^{t}|v^{n}(s)|\left\|z^{n}(s)-z^{n-1}(s)\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq C(\varepsilon)\int_{0}^{t}\|z^{n}(s)-z^{n-1}(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}\mathrm{d}s,

(4.32)

and

\mathcal{K}_{62}(t)\leq C(\varepsilon)\int_{0}^{t}\|z^{n}(s)-z^{n-1}(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}\mathrm{d}s,

(4.33)

\mathcal{K}_{63}(t)\leq C(\varepsilon)\int_{0}^{t}\|f^{n}(s)-f^{n-1}(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}\mathrm{d}s.

(4.34)

where we used the uniform boundedness of
v^{n}
.

We combine the above estimates to obtain the desired result (
4.28
). We now consider the sum
\displaystyle{\eqref{eq4.23}+\frac{1}{\eta}\eqref{eq4.28}}
with
\displaystyle{0<\eta\ll 1}
to obtain

\displaystyle\|f^{n}(t)-f^{n-1}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\left(\frac{1}{\eta}-C(T)\right)\|z^{n}(t)-z^{n-1}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C(\varepsilon,T)}{\eta}\biggl[\int_{0}^{t}\|\omega^{n}(s)\|_{L^{2}}\>\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\|z^{n}(s)-z^{n-1}(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}\>\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\|f^{n}(s)-f^{n-1}(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}\>\mathrm{d}s\biggr].

(4.35)

We choose
\eta\ll 1
sufficiently small such that

\frac{1}{\eta}-C(T)\geq 1.

This together with setting
\Delta^{n}(t):=\|f^{n}(t)-f^{n-1}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|z^{n}(t)-z^{n-1}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}
gives

\Delta^{n}(t)\leq\frac{C(\varepsilon,T)}{\eta}\bigg[\int_{0}^{t}\|\omega^{n}(s)\|_{L^{2}}\>\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\Delta^{n}(s)\>\mathrm{d}s\bigg],

Then the standard
Gr\ddot{o}nwall^{\prime}s
Lemma yields

\Delta^{n}(t)\leq C(\varepsilon,T)\int_{0}^{t}\|\omega^{n}(s)\|_{L^{2}}\>\mathrm{d}s.

(4.36)

For the estimate of
\omega^{n}
, we use the estimates of
[
29
]
. In fact, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\|\omega^{n+1}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla\omega^{n+1}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(\varepsilon,T)(\|f^{n}(t)-f^{n-1}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+\|\omega^{n+1}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\omega^{n}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}).

Please refer to subsection 3.1.3 in
[
29
]
for a detailed derivation process. Then, we now use (
4.36
) to find

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\|\omega^{n+1}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla\omega^{n+1}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C(\varepsilon,T)\biggl(\int_{0}^{t}\|\omega^{n}(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\>\mathrm{d}s+\|\omega^{n+1}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\omega^{n}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\biggr).

Therefore, by integrating the previous inequality with respect to

and using

\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{s}\|\omega^{n}(r)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}^{2}dr\mathrm{d}s\leq T\int_{0}^{t}\|\omega^{n}(r)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}^{2}dr,

we can reach the desired result.

Lemma 4.7
.

The unique solution sequence
[f^{n},u^{n}]
obtained in Lemma
4.5
strongly converges in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\times(0,T))\times L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))
towards the weak solution of (
4.5
).

Proof.

The proof simply follows from Lemma
4.6

4.6

that

\|u^{n}-u^{n-1}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))}^{2}\leq\frac{K^{n}T^{n}}{n!},

for some

This gives the strong convergence of

in

, and subsequently, this also yields

\|f^{n}(t)-f^{n-1}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|z^{n}(t)-z^{n-1}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\frac{(\tilde{K})^{n}T^{n}}{n!},

for some

The convergence of

in

∎

4.4
Proof of Theorem 1.2

So far, we have already proved that the sequence
[f^{n}_{\varepsilon},u^{n}_{\varepsilon}]

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
\left[f_{\varepsilon},u_{\varepsilon}\right]
be a solution to (
4.5

T^{*}\in(0,T]

\displaystyle(1)\quad\|f_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\times(0,T))}\leq C(T)\|f^{\mathrm{in}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})},

\displaystyle(2)\quad\|u_{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(s)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}^{2}\mathrm{d}s+M_{2}f_{\varepsilon}(t)\leq C,\quad\mathrm{a.e.}\>t\leq T^{*},

Since
\|f_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{in}}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|f^{\mathrm{in}}\|_{L^{\infty}}

\|f_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq{C(T)}\|f^{\mathrm{in}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})},

where we have used Lemma
4.5
(1).

(2) We use Proposition
2.2
to estimate

as follows.

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}M_{2}f_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=2\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}w\cdot F[f_{\varepsilon},u_{\varepsilon}]f_{\varepsilon}\>\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w

\displaystyle=2\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}w\cdot(m_{\varepsilon}*u_{\varepsilon})f_{\varepsilon}\>\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w+2\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}w\cdot F[f_{\varepsilon}]f_{\varepsilon}\>\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w-2M_{2}f_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|m_{\varepsilon}*u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{5}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\|m_{1}f_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{5/4}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}-2M_{2}f_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{5}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}(M_{2}f_{\varepsilon})^{4/5}-2M_{2}f_{\varepsilon},

where we used

m_{1}f_{\varepsilon}\leq C(m_{2}f_{\varepsilon})^{\frac{4}{5}}\Rightarrow\|m_{1}f_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{5/4}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\leq C(M_{2}f_{\varepsilon})^{4/5},

and

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}w\cdot F[f_{\varepsilon}]f_{\varepsilon}\>\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}w=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{6}\times\mathbb{R}^{6}}\phi(|x-x_{*}|)w\cdot(\hat{v}(w_{*})-\hat{v}(w)f_{\varepsilon}(t,x,w)f_{\varepsilon}(t,x_{*},w_{*})\>\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}x_{*}\mathrm{d}w\mathrm{d}w_{*}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{6}\times\mathbb{R}^{6}}\phi(|x-x_{*}|)(w_{*}-w)\cdot(\hat{v}(w_{*})-\hat{v}(w)f_{\varepsilon}(t,x,w)f_{\varepsilon}(t,x_{*},w_{*})\>\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}x_{*}\mathrm{d}w\mathrm{d}w_{*}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{C(c)}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{6}\times\mathbb{R}^{6}}\phi(|x-x_{*}|)|\hat{v}(w_{*})-\hat{v}(w)|^{2}f_{\varepsilon}(t,x,w)f_{\varepsilon}(t,x_{*},w_{*})\>\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}x_{*}\mathrm{d}w\mathrm{d}w_{*}\leq 0.

C_{1}(\|f^{\mathrm{in}}\|_{L^{\infty}})>0

5
Conclusion

In this article, we propose a coupled model of the relativistic Cucker-Smale equation and the incompressible Navier-Stokes equation based on the theory of friction. We also analyze its asymptotic stability and the existence of weak solutions. According to our knowledge, there have been no previous studies on this coupled model.

Firstly, we have proved that the smooth solution of the RCS-NS equation exhibits exponential stability under certain conditions. Physically, this means that the particle velocity will converge with the fluid’s velocity. In addition, we also proposed a weak solution form for the RCS-NS equation and proved its existence under conditions of large initial values. This provides a potential theoretical basis for designing its numerical algorithms.

In the future, we will continue to investigate the existence and uniqueness of strong solutions in the periodic region of the RCS-NS model, as well as analyze the behavior of solutions in the whole domain
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. In addition, further exploration is needed on the coupling structure between RCS equation and compressible Navier-Stokes equations, relativistic Euler equations, Stokes equation and other fluid equations.

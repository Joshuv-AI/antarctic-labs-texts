Abstract
. Affine Cartesian codes were first discussed by Geil and Thomsen in 2013 in a broader framework and were formally introduced by López, Rentería-Márquez and Villarreal in 2014. These are linear error-correcting codes obtained by evaluating polynomials at points of a Cartesian product of subsets of the given finite field. They can be viewed as a vast generalization of Reed-Muller codes. In 1970, Delsarte, Goethals and MacWilliams gave a formula for the minimum weight codewords of Reed-Muller codes. Carvalho and Neumann in 2020 considered affine Cartesian codes in a special setting where the subsets in the Cartesian product are nested subfields of the given finite field, and gave a characterization of their minimum weight codewords. We use this to give an explicit formula for the number of minimum weight codewords of affine Cartesian codes in the case of nested subfields. This is seen to unify the known formulas for the number of minimum weight codewords of Reed-Solomon codes and Reed-Muller codes.



1.
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{F}_{q}
denote the finite field with
q
elements, where
q
is the power of some prime
p
. For a positive integer
m
, consider nonempty subsets
A_{1},\ldots,A_{m}
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of size
d_{1},\ldots,d_{m}
respectively. Without loss of generality, we may assume that

1<d_{1}\leq\cdots\leq d_{m}\leq q.

Define the Cartesian product

\mathcal{A}:=A_{1}\times\cdots\times A_{m}\subseteq\mbox{$\mathbb{F}$}_{q}^{m}\quad\text{and}\quad n:=|\mathcal{A}|=d_{1}\cdots d_{m}.

Denote by
\mathcal{S}
, the polynomial ring in
m
variables
X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Let
{\mathrm{X}}=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{m})
. Then

\mathcal{S}:=\mbox{$\mathbb{F}_{q}$}[{\mathrm{X}}]=\mbox{$\mathbb{F}_{q}$}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}].

For a nonnegative integer
u
, define
\mathcal{S}_{\leq u}({\mathcal{A}})
to be the vector subspace of
\mathcal{S}
having polynomials
f
such that
\deg f\leq u
and
\deg_{X_{i}}(f)<d_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq m
, i.e.,

\mathcal{S}_{\leq u}({\mathcal{A}}):=\{f({\mathrm{X}})\in\mathcal{S}:\deg f\leq u,\deg_{X_{i}}(f)<d_{i}\text{ for }1\leq i\leq m\}.

Denote, by convention,
\mathcal{S}_{\leq-1}({\mathcal{A}})=\{\bf{0}\}
, the zero subspace of
\mathcal{S}
. Since
|{\mathcal{A}}|=n
, we write
{\mathcal{A}}=\{P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}\}
. Define the evaluation map

\mathrm{Ev}:\mathcal{S}_{\leq u}({\mathcal{A}})\rightarrow{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n}\qquad\text{given by}\qquad f\mapsto{\bf{c}}_{f}:=(f(P_{1}),\ldots,f(P_{n})).

We note that
\mathrm{Ev}
is a linear injective map and its image is a linear code, called the
affine Cartesian code of order
u
and is denoted by
\mathrm{AC}_{q}(u,{\mathcal{A}})
. Thus,
\mathrm{AC}_{q}(u,{\mathcal{A}})=\mathrm{Ev}(\mathcal{S}_{\leq u}({\mathcal{A}}))
is a linear code of length
n=d_{1}\cdots d_{m}
. Moreover,
\mathrm{Ev}
is a surjective map if
u\geq K:=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}(d_{i}-1)
.

Affine Cartesian codes were first defined by Geil and Thomsen
[
13
]
in 2013 in a more general setting where they consider weighted Reed-Muller codes on a Cartesian product of subsets of the given finite field. Independently, these codes were formally introduced by López, Rentería-Márquez and Villarreal in 2014
[
18
]
; in this work, they determine the basic parameters of these codes using the theory of complete intersections and Hilbert functions. Among the different evaluation codes that exist in the literature, Reed-Solomon codes and Reed-Muller codes are widely studied and can be seen as special cases of affine Cartesian codes. In fact,

•

If m=1, then
\mathrm{AC}_{q}(u,{\mathcal{A}})=\mathrm{RS}_{q}(u+1,d_{1})
, the Reed-Solomon code of length
d_{1}
and dimension
u+1
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

•

If
A_{1}=\cdots=A_{m}=\mbox{$\mathbb{F}_{q}$}
, then
{\mathcal{A}}={\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{m}
and hence
\mathrm{AC}_{q}(u,{\mathcal{A}})=\mathrm{RM}_{q}(u,m)
, the Reed-Muller code of order
u
in
m
variables over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

Reed-Muller codes were first introduced by Muller
[
20
]
in 1954, and a decoding algorithm was later developed by Reed
[
21
]
. These codes were initially defined over the binary field
\mbox{$\mathbb{F}$}_{2}
and were later extended in 1968 by Kasami, Lin, and Peterson to generalized Reed-Muller codes, which are defined over a finite field
\mbox{$\mathbb{F}$}_{q}
. Generalized Reed-Muller codes, denoted by
\mathrm{RM}_{q}(u,m)
, are obtained by evaluating polynomials of degree at most
u
in the polynomial ring
\mbox{$\mathbb{F}$}_{q}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}]
at all points in the affine space
\mbox{$\mathbb{A}$}^{m}(\mbox{$\mathbb{F}$}_{q})
. An extensive literature on binary and
q
-ary Reed-Muller codes can be found in
[
1
,
22
]
. Due to their nice algebraic properties, these codes have been extensively studied and are still an active area of research. It were the seminal papers by Kasami, Lin, Peterson in 1968
[
16
]
where they determined the basic parameters of these codes and by Delsarte, Goethals, MacWilliams in 1970
[
10
]
where they gave a characterization of minimum weight codewords in terms of product of linear polynomials that significantly advanced the study of Reed-Muller codes. Other prominent works on Reed-Muller codes include the determination of their automorphism groups by Berger, Charpin
[
4
]
, their generalized Hamming weights by Heijnen, Pellikaan
[
14
]
, and later by Beelen
[
2
]
. A lot of work has also been done towards the determination of higher Hamming weights and next-to-minimum weight codewords of Reed-Muller codes. One can refer to
[
6
, Section 1]
and references within for more details on higher weights of Reed-Muller codes.

Affine Cartesian codes, being a generalization of Reed-Muller codes have always been under active consideration. Other than the defining works by López, Rentería-Márquez and Villareal
[
18
]
, some major works include determination of their duals and generalized Hamming weights by Beelen, Datta
[
3
]
, their complimentary duals by Lopez, Manganiello, Matthews
[
19
]
and relative generalized Hamming weights by Datta
[
9
]
. In a special case, Carvalho and Neumann
[
6
]
gave a characterization of minimum weight codewords. They also determined second Hamming weights and next-to-minimal weight codewords of affine Cartesian codes. One can refer to
[
5
,
7
,
8
]
for more details.

Delsarte, Goethals, MacWilliams in
[
10
]
gave a characterization of minimum weight codewords for Reed-Muller codes. They showed that for
0\leq u\leq m(q-1)
, if

u=t(q-1)+s

for some uniquely determined integers
t,s
such that
0\leq t\leq m
and
0\leq s<q-1
, every minimum weight codeword of the Reed-Muller code
\mathrm{RM}_{q}(u,m)
is obtained by evaluation of
{\bf{c}}_{f}
of polynomials
f\in\mathcal{S}_{\leq u}({\mathcal{A}})
, given by

f(X_{1},\ldots,X_{m})={\gamma}\prod_{i=1}^{t}(1-(L_{i}+b_{i})^{q-1})\prod_{j=1}^{s}(L_{t+1}-{\omega}_{j}),

where
{\gamma}\in\mbox{$\mathbb{F}$}_{q}^{*}
,
L_{1},\ldots,L_{t+1}\in\mbox{$\mathbb{F}_{q}$}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}]
are linearly independent linear homogeneous polynomials,
b_{1},\ldots,b_{t}\in\mbox{$\mathbb{F}_{q}$}
and
\Omega:=\{w_{1},\ldots,{\omega}_{s}\}\subseteq\mbox{$\mathbb{F}_{q}$}
. This result showed that all the minimum weight codewords lie on a special type of hyperplane arrangement. Moreover, they also enumerated the minimum weight codewords and showed that if
\mathrm{N}_{q}(u,m)
denotes the set of minimum weight codewords of
\mathrm{RM}_{q}(u,m)
, then

\left|\mathrm{N}_{q}(u,m)\right|=\begin{cases}(q-1)q^{t}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{m}{t}_{q}&{\text{ if }s=0},\\[5.0pt]
(q-1)q^{t}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{m}{t}_{q}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{m-t}{1}_{q}\binom{q}{s}&{\text{ if }s\neq 0},\\
\end{cases}

where
\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{m}{t}_{q}
denotes the
q
-binomial coefficient and is defined to be the number of
t
-dimensional linear subspaces of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{m}
. In 2012, Leducq in
[
17
]
gave an alternative proof for the above result of Delsarte, Goethals and MacWilliams
[
10
]
using the concept of ‘reduced’ polynomials. A generalization of this result to affine Cartesian codes has proven to be a difficult problem due to arbitrary nature of subsets of the Cartesian product under consideration. However, some work has recently been done by Geil
[
12
]
in this direction where he showed that not all minimum weight codewords are obtained by polynomials which can be expressed as a product of linear factors. Extending the work of Delsarte Goethals and MacWilliams
[
10
]
, Carvalho and Neumann
[
6
]
in 2020 considered a special case of affine Cartesian codes and gave a similar characterization for its minimum weight codewords. They assumed subsets
A_{1},\ldots,A_{m}
of the Cartesian product
{\mathcal{A}}
to be subfields of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Moreover, they further assumed that

A_{1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq A_{m}\subseteq\mbox{$\mathbb{F}_{q}$}.

We address the problem of enumerating the minimum weight codewords in this specific context. We write

{\mathcal{A}}=F_{1}\times\cdots\times F_{1}\times\cdots\cdots\times F_{\lambda}\times\cdots\times F_{\lambda}\subseteq\mbox{$\mathbb{F}$}_{q}^{m},

where
F_{1}\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq F_{\lambda}\subseteq\mbox{$\mathbb{F}_{q}$}
are distinct subfields of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and for
1\leq t\leq\lambda
, let
F_{t}
is repeated
\mu_{t}
times in
{\mathcal{A}}
. Then, it follows that

\mu_{1}+\cdots+\mu_{\lambda}=m.

Define
s_{0}=0
, and

s_{t}:=\mu_{1}+\cdots+\mu_{t}\quad\text{ for }\quad 1\leq t\leq\lambda.

Since
\mu_{t}>0
for
1\leq t\leq\lambda
, we get

0<s_{1}<s_{2}<\cdots<s_{\lambda}=m.

Then, it follows from the division algorithm that for every
k
such that
1\leq k\leq m
, there exists a unique integer
t=t_{k}
such that
s_{t_{k}-1}<k\leq s_{t_{k}}
.

Denoting by
\mbox{$\mathcal{N}$}_{q}(u,{\mathcal{A}})
, the set of minimum weight codewords of
\mathrm{AC}_{q}(u,{\mathcal{A}})
, we completely determine
\left|\mbox{$\mathcal{N}$}_{q}(u,{\mathcal{A}})\right|
for all values of
u
under consideration and is given by the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
m\geq 1
and
{\mathcal{A}}=F_{1}\times\cdots\times F_{1}\times\cdots\times F_{\lambda}\times\cdots\times F_{{\lambda}}=F_{1}^{\mu_{1}}\times\cdots\times F_{{\lambda}}^{\mu_{{\lambda}}}\subseteq\mbox{$\mathbb{F}$}_{q}^{m}
as before. Then

\left|\mbox{$\mathcal{N}$}_{q}(0,{\mathcal{A}})\right|=q-1.

For
1\leq u\leq K=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}(d_{i}-1)
, write

u=\sum_{i=1}^{j}(d_{i}-1)+\ell,

where
j,\ell
are uniquely determined integers such that
0\leq j<m
and
0<\ell\leq d_{j+1}-1
. Then

\left|\mbox{$\mathcal{N}$}_{q}(u,{\mathcal{A}})\right|=\begin{cases}(q-1)\bigg(\prod\limits_{\underset{i\neq k}{i=1}}^{j+1}d_{i}\bigg)\displaystyle{\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{\mu_r}{j+1-s_{r-1}}_{d_{j+1}}}&\text{ if }\ell=d_{j+1}-1,\\
\left|\mbox{$\mathcal{N}$}_{q}^{(j+1)}(u,{\mathcal{A}})\right|+\sum\limits_{t=t_{k_{0}}}^{r-1}\left|\mbox{$\mathcal{N}$}_{q}^{(s_{t})}(u,{\mathcal{A}})\right|&\text{ if }\ell<d_{j+1}-1,\\
\end{cases}

where
r=t_{j+1}
,
1\leq k_{0}\leq j+1
is the least integer
k
such that
d_{k}\geq d_{j+1}-\ell
and
|\mbox{$\mathcal{N}$}_{q}^{(k)}(u,{\mathcal{A}})|
is given by

•

If
t_{k}=r
,

\left|\mbox{$\mathcal{N}$}_{q}^{(k)}(u,{\mathcal{A}})\right|=(q-1)\bigg(\prod\limits_{\underset{i\neq k}{i=1}}^{j+1}d_{i}\bigg)\displaystyle{\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{\mu_r}{j-s_{r-1} }_{d_{j+1}}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{s_{r }-j}{1}_{d_{k}}}\binom{d_{k}}{d_{j+1}-\ell}.

•

If
t_{k}<r
,

\left|\mbox{$\mathcal{N}$}_{q}^{(k)}(u,{\mathcal{A}})\right|=(q-1)\bigg(\prod\limits_{\underset{i\neq k}{i=1}}^{j+1}d_{i}\bigg)\bigg(\prod\limits_{i=s_{t_{k}}+1}^{j+1}d_{i}\bigg)\displaystyle{\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{\mu_r}{j+1-s_{r-1} }_{d_{j+1}}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{\mu_{t_k}}{1}_{d_{k}}}\binom{d_{k}}{d_{j+1}-\ell}.

This article is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we give some preliminary results pertaining to basics of finite fields, group theory and coding theory that will be used later. In Section

2.
Preliminaries

Let
m
and
\mathbb{F}_{q}
be as defined in Section
1
and remain fixed throughout the article. We begin with an elementary yet crucial property related to elements of finite fields.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
15
]
, Lemma 2.21)
.

Let
s
be an integer and
0\leq s\leq q-1
. Then

\sum_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}a^{s}=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if $0\leq s\leq q-2$},\\
-1&\text{ if $s=q-1$}.\\
\end{cases}

(2.1)

Denote by
\mathrm{GL}(m,\mbox{$\mathbb{F}_{q}$})
, the group of
m\times m
non-singular matrices over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

Definition 2.2
.

The
affine general linear group
of degree
m
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is

\mathrm{AGL}(m,\mathbb{F}_{q})=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}A&b\\
0&1\\
\end{bmatrix}:A\in\mathrm{GL}(m,\mathbb{F}_{q}),b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{m}\right\}.

It can also be described as follows.

\mathrm{AGL}(m,\mbox{$\mathbb{F}_{q}$}):=\{\sigma_{A,b}:\mbox{$\mathbb{F}$}_{q}^{m}\rightarrow\mbox{$\mathbb{F}$}_{q}^{m}|\sigma_{A,b}({\mathrm{X}})=A{\mathrm{X}}+b\text{ for some }A\in\mathrm{GL}(m,\mbox{$\mathbb{F}_{q}$}),b\in\mbox{$\mathbb{F}$}_{q}^{m}\}.

(2.2)

Note that
\mathrm{AGL}(m,\mbox{$\mathbb{F}_{q}$})
is a group with respect to composition of maps, i.e.,

\sigma_{A^{\prime},b^{\prime}}\circ\sigma_{A,b}({\mathrm{X}})=\sigma_{A^{\prime},b^{\prime}}(A{\mathrm{X}}+b)=(A^{\prime}A){\mathrm{X}}+(A^{\prime}b+b^{\prime}),

which is same as matrix multiplication

\begin{bmatrix}A^{\prime}&b^{\prime}\\
0&1\\
\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}A&b\\
0&1\\
\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}A^{\prime}A&A^{\prime}b+b^{\prime}\\
0&1\\
\end{bmatrix}.

Observe that
|\mathrm{AGL}(m,\mbox{$\mathbb{F}_{q}$})|=\left|{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{m}\right|\cdot\left|\mathrm{GL}(m,\mbox{$\mathbb{F}_{q}$})\right|=q^{m}\prod\limits_{i=0}^{m-1}(q^{m}-q^{i})
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
(G,*)
be a group with identity
e
and let
X
be a set. A
group action

\Phi
of
G
on
X
is a map

\Phi:G\times X\rightarrow X,

such that for all
g_{1},g_{2}\in G,x\in X
, it satisfies

(1)

\Phi(e,x)=x
.

(2)

\Phi(g_{1}*g_{2},x)=\Phi(g_{1},\Phi(g_{2},x))
.

One can refer to
[
11
, Section 1.7]
and
[
11
, Chapter 4]
for more details on group actions.

Definition 2.4
.

The
orbit
and
stabilizer
of an element
x\in X
w.r.t. the action
\Phi
of
G
on
X
are defined respectively by

\mathrm{orb}_{G}(x):=\{\Phi(g,x):g\in G\}\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathrm{stab}_{G}(x):=\{g\in G:\Phi(g,x)=x\}.

The orbit and stabilizer of an element are closely related and this relation is given by the following result, called the
orbit-stabilizer theorem
.

Theorem 2.5

(
[
11
]
, Chapter 4, Proposition 2)
.

Let
\Phi
be an action of a group
G
on a finite set
X
. Then for any
x\in X
,

|\mathrm{orb}_{G}(x)|=\frac{|G|}{|\mathrm{stab}_{G}(x)|}.

Let
0\leq s\leq q-1
. Denote by
{\mathcal{P}}_{s}(\mbox{$\mathbb{F}_{q}$})
the collection of all subsets of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
having exactly
s
elements, i.e.,

{\mathcal{P}}_{s}(\mbox{$\mathbb{F}_{q}$}):=\{\Omega\subseteq\mbox{$\mathbb{F}_{q}$}:|\Omega|=s\}.

Therefore,

|{\mathcal{P}}_{s}(\mbox{$\mathbb{F}_{q}$})|=\binom{q}{s}.

Consider the group action
\Phi:\mathrm{AGL}(1,\mbox{$\mathbb{F}_{q}$})\times{\mathcal{P}}_{s}(\mbox{$\mathbb{F}_{q}$})\rightarrow{\mathcal{P}}_{s}(\mbox{$\mathbb{F}_{q}$})
given by

\Phi(\sigma_{a,b},\Omega)=a\Omega+b:=\{a\omega_{1}+b,\ldots,a\omega_{s}+b\}\text{ for }\Omega=\{\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{s}\}.

Note that
\Phi
is a well-defined group action. Denote by
[\Omega]
, the orbit of
\Omega
under the group action
\Phi
, i.e.,

[\Omega]:=\{\Phi(\sigma_{a,b},\Omega):\sigma_{a,b}\in\mathrm{AGL}(1,\mbox{$\mathbb{F}_{q}$})\}.

We know that any two orbits are either disjoint or identical. Since
{\mathcal{P}}_{s}(\mbox{$\mathbb{F}_{q}$})
is a finite set, there will be finitely many disjoint orbits. Suppose there are
\eta
disjoint orbits and let
\Omega_{1},\ldots,\Omega_{\eta}
be their representatives. Then, it must follow that

|{\mathcal{P}}_{s}(\mbox{$\mathbb{F}_{q}$})|=\sum_{i=1}^{\eta}\left|\right[\Omega_{i}]|.

But, Theorem
2.5
tells us that for
\Omega\in{\mathcal{P}}_{s}(\mbox{$\mathbb{F}_{q}$})
,

\left|[\Omega]\right|=\frac{|\mathrm{AGL}(1,\mbox{$\mathbb{F}_{q}$})|}{|\Delta_{\Omega}|},

where

\Delta_{\Omega}:=\{\sigma_{a,b}\in\mathrm{AGL}(1,\mbox{$\mathbb{F}_{q}$}):a\Omega+b=\Omega\},

(2.3)

is the stabilizer of
\Omega
with respect to the group action
\Phi.
Therefore,

|{\mathcal{P}}_{s}(\mbox{$\mathbb{F}_{q}$})|=\sum_{i=1}^{\eta}\frac{|\mathrm{AGL}(1,\mbox{$\mathbb{F}_{q}$})|}{|\Delta_{\Omega_{i}}|},

which implies that

\sum_{i=1}^{\eta}\frac{1}{|\Delta_{\Omega_{i}}|}=\frac{1}{q(q-1)}\binom{q}{s}.

(2.4)

2.1.
Affine Cartesian codes

Let
A_{1},\ldots,A_{m}
be nonempty subsets of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and let
|A_{i}|=d_{i}
. For
1\leq i\leq m
, we write
A_{i}=\{\gamma_{i,1},\gamma_{i,2},\ldots,\gamma_{i,d_{i}}\}
. Without loss of generality, we may assume that

1<d_{1}\leq d_{2}\leq\cdots\leq d_{m}\leq q.

Define the cartesian product

\mathcal{A}=A_{1}\times A_{2}\times\cdots\times A_{m}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{m}.

Recall, from Section
1
,

n=\prod_{i=1}^{m}d_{i}\quad\text{ and }\quad K=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}(d_{i}-1).

Then
|{\mathcal{A}}|=n
and we write
{\mathcal{A}}=\{P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}\}
. Define

\mathcal{I}({\mathcal{A}}):=\{f\in\mathcal{S}:f(P)=0\text{ for all }P\in{\mathcal{A}}\}.

Note that
{\mathcal{I}}({\mathcal{A}})
is an ideal of
\mathcal{S}
, called
the vanishing ideal of
{\mathcal{A}}
. Moreover,
{\mathcal{I}}({\mathcal{A}})
is given by

{\mathcal{I}}({\mathcal{A}})=\left\langle g_{1},\ldots,g_{m}\right\rangle,

where the generators
g_{1},\ldots,g_{m}
of
{\mathcal{I}}({\mathcal{A}})
are univariate polynomials defined by

g_{i}(X_{i})=\prod_{j=1}^{d_{i}}(X_{i}-\gamma_{i,j})\text{ for }1\leq i\leq m.

(2.5)

We know that for a subset
Y\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{m}
, the function
f:Y\rightarrow\mbox{$\mathbb{F}_{q}$}
can always be expressed by some polynomial
\mathrm{p}\in\mathcal{S}
. Let
\mathcal{S}({\mathcal{A}})
be the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-algebra of functions defined on
{\mathcal{A}}
. Consider the map

\psi:\frac{\mbox{$\mathbb{F}_{q}$}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}]}{\mathcal{I}({\mathcal{A}})}\rightarrow\mathcal{S}({\mathcal{A}})\text{ given by }f\mapsto f\pmod{{\mathcal{I}}({\mathcal{A}})}.

Note that
\psi
is an isomorphism and hence, for every
f\in\mathcal{S}({\mathcal{A}})
, there exists a unique polynomial
\mathrm{p}\in\mathcal{S}
such that
\deg_{X_{i}}(\mathrm{p})<d_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq m
and
\psi(\mathrm{p}+{\mathcal{I}}({\mathcal{A}}))=f
. We say that
\mathrm{p}
is the
reduced
polynomial associated to
f
and define the degree of
f
as being the degree of
\mathrm{p}
. Therefore, without loss of generality, we will identify elements of
\mathcal{S}({\mathcal{A}})
with their reduced polynomials, i.e.,

\mathcal{S}({\mathcal{A}})=\frac{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{I}({\mathcal{A}})}=\{f\in\mathcal{S}:\deg_{X_{i}}(f)<d_{i}\}.

For any integer
u\geq 0
, denote by
\mathcal{S}_{\leq u}(\mathcal{A})
, the vector subspace of
\mathcal{S}({\mathcal{A}})
consisting of polynomials
f
with
\deg(f)\leq u
and
\deg_{X_{i}}(f)<d_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq m
. Then, we can write

\mathcal{S}_{\leq u}(\mathcal{A})=\{f\in\mbox{$\mathbb{F}_{q}$}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}]:\deg(f)\leq u,\deg_{X_{i}}(f)<d_{i}\text{ for }i=1,\ldots,m\}.

(2.6)

By convention, we denote
\mathcal{S}_{-1}({\mathcal{A}})=\{0\}
.

Definition 2.6
.

For
f\in\mathcal{S}({\mathcal{A}})
, we define the set

\mathrm{Supp}_{{\mathcal{A}}}(f):=\{P\in{\mathcal{A}}:f(P)\neq 0\},

and is called the
{\mathcal{A}}
-support
of a function
f
. Further, cardinality of the set
\mathrm{Supp}_{{\mathcal{A}}}(f)
is called
Hamming weight
of
f
and is denoted by
|f|_{{\mathcal{A}}}
.

Definition 2.7
.

We denote the
zero set
of
f\in S({\mathcal{A}})
by
\mathrm{Z}_{{\mathcal{A}}}(f)
and is defined by

\mathrm{Z}_{{\mathcal{A}}}(f):=\{P\in{\mathcal{A}}:f(P)=0\}={\mathcal{A}}\setminus\mathrm{Supp}_{{\mathcal{A}}}(f).

Then,
|\mathrm{Z}_{{\mathcal{A}}}(f)|=|{\mathcal{A}}|-|\mathrm{Supp}_{{\mathcal{A}}}(f)|=n-|f|_{{\mathcal{A}}}
.

Definition 2.8
.

For
u\geq 0
, the
evaluation map
\mathrm{Ev}
is defined as follows:

\mathrm{Ev}:\mathcal{S}_{\leq u}(\mathcal{A})\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}\text{ given by }f\mapsto{\bf{c}}_{f}\text{ where }{\bf{c}}_{f}:=(f(P_{1}),f(P_{2}),\ldots,f(P_{n})).

(2.7)

Then,
\mathrm{Ev}
is a linear injective map and its image is a linear subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
. Thus, for
u\geq 0
,
\mathrm{AC}_{q}(u,\mathcal{A})=\mathrm{Ev}(\mathcal{S}_{\leq u}(\mathcal{A}))
is a linear code of length
n=d_{1}d_{2}\cdots d_{m}
and is called the
affine Cartesian code of order
u
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Furthermore, note that the map
\mathrm{Ev}
is surjective if
u\geq K
. By convention, we denote by
\mathrm{AC}_{q}(-1,{\mathcal{A}})=\{0\}
, the zero subspace of
\mbox{$\mathbb{F}$}_{q}^{n}
.

Remark 2.9
.

If m=1, then
\mathrm{AC}_{q}(u,{\mathcal{A}})=\mathrm{RS}_{q}(u+1,n)
, the Reed-Solomon code of length
n
and dimension
u+1
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

Remark 2.10
.

Reed-Muller code,
\mathrm{RM}_{q}(u,m)
is a particular case of affine Cartesian code
\mathrm{AC}_{q}(u,{\mathcal{A}})
when
A_{1}=\cdots=A_{m}=\mbox{$\mathbb{F}_{q}$}
, i.e.,

{\mathcal{A}}=\mbox{$\mathbb{F}$}_{q}^{m}\text{ implies }\mathrm{RM}_{q}(u,m)=\mathrm{AC}_{q}(u,{\mathcal{A}}).

The next theorem gives the basic parameters of affine Cartesian codes.

Theorem 2.11
.

For
0\leq u\leq K
, the affine Cartesian code
\mathrm{AC}_{q}(u,{\mathcal{A}})
has the following parameters:

•

length, n :

n=d_{1}\cdots d_{m}.

•

dimension,
\rho_{q}(u,{\mathcal{A}})
[
[
18
]
, Theorem 3.1]:

\rho_{q}(u,{\mathcal{A}})=\binom{m+u}{u}-\sum_{1\leq i\leq m}\binom{m+u-d_{i}}{u-d_{i}}+\sum_{i<j}\binom{m+u-(d_{i}+d_{j})}{u-(d_{i}+d_{j})}\\
-\sum_{i<j<k}\binom{m+u-(d_{i}+d_{j}+d_{k})}{u-(d_{i}+d_{j}+d_{k})}+\ldots+(-1)^{m}\binom{m+u-(d_{1}+\ldots+d_{m})}{u-(d_{1}+\ldots+d_{m})}.

•

minimum distance,
{\delta}_{q}(u,{\mathcal{A}})
[
[
18
]
, Theorem 3.8]:

\delta_{q}(0,{\mathcal{A}})=n,

and for
1\leq u\leq K
,

\delta_{q}(u,{\mathcal{A}})=(d_{j+1}-\ell)d_{j+2}\cdots d_{m},

where
0\leq j<m
and
0<\ell\leq d_{j+1}-1
are uniquely determined integers such that
u=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{j}(d_{i}-1)+\ell
.

•

dual,
\mathrm{AC}_{q}(u,\mathcal{A})^{\perp}
[
[
3
]
, Theorem 5.7]:

\mathrm{AC}_{q}(u,\mathcal{A})^{\perp}=\{(w_{1}c_{1},w_{2}c_{2},\ldots,w_{n}c_{n}):(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})\in\mathrm{AC}_{q}(K-u-1,\mathcal{A})\},

where
\frac{1}{w_{j}}=\prod\limits_{i=1}^{m}g_{i}^{\prime}(P_{j})
for
1\leq j\leq n
and
g_{i}^{\prime}s
are given by equation (
2.5
).

3.
Affine Cartesian codes for Nested Subfields

We consider a special family of affine Cartesian codes in which the subsets
A_{1},\ldots,A_{m}
are subfields of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Furthermore, suppose

A_{1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq A_{m}\subseteq\mbox{$\mathbb{F}_{q}$}.

Then, we can write

{\mathcal{A}}=F_{1}\times\cdots\times F_{1}\times\cdots\cdots\times F_{{\lambda}}\times\cdots\times F_{{\lambda}},

(3.1)

where
F_{1}\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq F_{{\lambda}}\subseteq\mbox{$\mathbb{F}_{q}$}
are distinct subfields of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. For
1\leq t\leq\lambda
, let
F_{t}
is repeated
\mu_{t}
times in
{\mathcal{A}}
. Then

\sum_{t=1}^{{\lambda}}\mu_{t}=m.

Let
|F_{t}|=p^{r_{t}}
, then
r_{1}\mid r_{2}\mid\cdots\mid r_{{\lambda}}
. Therefore,

n=|{\mathcal{A}}|=\prod_{t=1}^{{\lambda}}(p^{r_{t}})^{\mu_{t}}=p^{\left(\sum\limits_{t=1}^{{\lambda}}r_{t}\mu_{t}\right)}

Let
{\alpha}_{t}
be a primitive element of
F_{t}
for
1\leq t\leq\lambda
. Then,
F_{t}^{*}=\langle{\alpha}_{t}\rangle
. Define
s_{0}=0
and for
1\leq t\leq\lambda
,

s_{t}:=\mu_{1}+\ldots+\mu_{t}=s_{t-1}+\mu_{t}.

Note that
s_{\lambda}=m
. Recall, for each
1\leq k\leq m
, there exists a unique integer
t=t_{k}\in\{1,\ldots,\lambda\}
such that
s_{t_{k}-1}<k\leq s_{t_{k}}
. If
{\mathcal{A}}=A_{1}\times\cdots\times A_{m}
with
|A_{i}|=d_{i}
. Then, upon comparing with equation
(
3.1
)
, we get

A_{i}=F_{t_{i}}\text{ for }s_{{t_{i}}-1}<i\leq s_{t_{i}}.

and hence

d_{i}=p^{r_{t_{i}}}\text{ whenever }s_{{t_{i}}-1}<i\leq s_{t_{i}}.

In this case, the ideal
{\mathcal{I}}({\mathcal{A}})=\{f\in\mathcal{S}({\mathcal{A}}):f(P)=0\text{ for all }P\in{\mathcal{A}}\}
can be rewritten as

{\mathcal{I}}({\mathcal{A}})=\langle X_{1}^{d_{1}}-X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}^{d_{m}}-X_{m}\rangle.

(3.2)

Recall, the affine General Linear group
\mathrm{AGL}(m,\mbox{$\mathbb{F}_{q}$})
is the group of transformations of
\mbox{$\mathbb{F}$}_{q}^{m}
of the form
{\mathrm{X}}\mapsto A{\mathrm{X}}+b
for some
A\in\mathrm{GL}(m,\mbox{$\mathbb{F}_{q}$})
and
b\in\mbox{$\mathbb{F}$}_{q}^{m}
. Carvalho and Neumann
[
5
]
characterized the minimum weight codewords of
\mathrm{AC}_{q}(u,{\mathcal{A}})
when
{\mathcal{A}}
is a Cartesian product of nested subfields of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Before their main result, they define
the affine group associated to
{\mathcal{A}}
, which is given below.

Definition 3.1
.

The
affine group associated to
{\mathcal{A}}
, denoted by
{\text{Aff}}({\mathcal{A}})
is given by

{\text{Aff}}({\mathcal{A}})=\{\phi:{\mathcal{A}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{A}}\text{ given by }\phi=\sigma|_{{\mathcal{A}}}\text{ for some }\sigma\in\mathrm{AGL}(m,\mbox{$\mathbb{F}_{q}$})\text{ and }\phi({\mathcal{A}})={\mathcal{A}}\}.

(3.3)

Further, they say that polynomials
f,g\in\mathcal{S}({\mathcal{A}})
are said to be
{\mathcal{A}}-
equivalent
if there exists
\phi\in{\text{Aff}}({\mathcal{A}})
such that
f=g\circ\phi
.

Theorem 3.2

(
[
5
]
, Theorem 3.5)
.

For
1\leq u\leq K
, let

u=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{j}(d_{i}-1)+\ell,\text{ for some }0\leq j<m\text{ and }0<\ell\leq d_{j+1}-1.

The minimum weight codewords of
\mathrm{AC}_{q}(u,{\mathcal{A}})
are given by evaluations of
f\in\mathcal{S}_{\leq u}({\mathcal{A}})
where
f
is
{\mathcal{A}}
-equivalent to the functions of the form

h_{k}^{\Omega}({\mathrm{X}})={\gamma}\prod_{\underset{i\neq k}{i=1}}^{j+1}(1-X_{i}^{d_{i}-1})\prod_{{\alpha}=1}^{d_{k}-(d_{j+1}-\ell)}(X_{k}-{\omega}_{{\alpha}}),

for some
{\gamma}\in\mbox{$\mathbb{F}$}_{q}^{*}
,
1\leq k\leq j+1
such that
d_{k}\geq d_{j+1}-\ell
, and
\Omega:=\{{\omega}_{1},\ldots,{\omega}_{d_{k}-(d_{j+1}-\ell)}\}\subseteq~A_{k}
.

3.1.
A Characterization of the Affine Group

In this section, we characterize the affine group w.r.t
{\mathcal{A}}
given in definition
3.1
in terms of nonsingular matrices and hence determine the size of this affine group.

Definition 3.3
.

We define a subset
\mathrm{G}\subseteq\mathrm{GL}(m,\mbox{$\mathbb{F}_{q}$})
by

\mathrm{G}:=\{A\in\mathrm{GL}(m,\mbox{$\mathbb{F}_{q}$}):A=(a_{i,j})_{m\times m}\text{ given by }a_{i,j}\in F_{t}\text{ whenever }s_{{t_{i}}-1}<i\leq s_{t_{i}},\\
\text{ and }a_{i,j}=0\text{ for }j>s_{t_{i}},\text{ for }1\leq t_{i}\leq\lambda\}.

(3.4)

Note that elements of
\mathrm{G}
are block lower triangular matrices. Thus, an element
A\in\mathrm{G}
can be written as

A=\begin{bmatrix}A_{11}&\bf{0}&\cdots&\bf{0}\\
A_{21}&A_{22}&\cdots&\bf{0}\\
\vdots&&\ddots&\vdots\\
A_{{\lambda}1}&A_{{\lambda}2}&\cdots&A_{{\lambda}{\lambda}}\\
\end{bmatrix},

where
A_{ij}\in M_{\mu_{i}\times\mu_{j}}(F_{i})
and
A_{ii}\in\mathrm{GL}(\mu_{i},F_{i})
for
i=1,\ldots,{\lambda}
. It is easy to observe that
\mathrm{G}
is a subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}(m,\mbox{$\mathbb{F}_{q}$})
. The next result gives a characterization of the affine group associated to
{\mathcal{A}}
in terms of nonsingular matrices.

Proposition 3.4
.

The affine group associated to
{\mathcal{A}}
is given by

{\text{Aff}}({\mathcal{A}})=\big\{\phi:{\mathcal{A}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{A}}\text{ given by }\hskip 3.61371pt\phi=\sigma_{A,b}|_{{\mathcal{A}}}\text{ where }\sigma_{A,b}\in\mathrm{AGL}(m,\mbox{$\mathbb{F}_{q}$})\text{ for }A\in\mathrm{G},b\in{\mathcal{A}}\big\}.

Proof.

Let the right hand side be denoted by
{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathcal{A}}}
, i.e.,

{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathcal{A}}}:=\big\{\phi:{\mathcal{A}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{A}}\big|\hskip 3.61371pt\phi=\sigma_{A,b}|_{{\mathcal{A}}}\text{ where }\sigma_{A,b}\in\mathrm{AGL}(m,\mbox{$\mathbb{F}_{q}$}),\text{ for some }A\in\mathrm{G},b\in{\mathcal{A}}\big\}.

(3.5)

Without loss of generality, we consider elements of
{\mathcal{A}}
to be column vectors. We first show that
{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathcal{A}}}\subseteq{\text{Aff}}({\mathcal{A}})
. To see this, let
\phi\in{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathcal{A}}}
. Then
\phi:{\mathcal{A}}\rightarrow\mbox{$\mathbb{F}$}_{q}^{m}
is given by
\phi({\mathrm{X}})=A{\mathrm{X}}+b
for some
A\in\mathrm{G}
,
b\in{\mathcal{A}}
. We need to show that
\phi
is a bijection. Since
{\mathcal{A}}
is a finite set, it suffices to show that
\phi
is injective and
\phi({\mathcal{A}})\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}
. Let
{\mathrm{X}}=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{m})\in{\mathcal{A}}
. It implies
X_{i}\in F_{t_{i}}
for
s_{{t_{i}}-1}<i\leq s_{t_{i}}
and for
1\leq t_{i}\leq\lambda
. For
1\leq i\leq m
, let
{\mathrm{X}}_{i}
denotes the
i^{th}
component of
{\mathrm{X}}=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{m})
. Then,

(\phi({\mathrm{X}}))_{i}=(A{\mathrm{X}}+b)_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_{i,j}X_{j}+b_{i}.

Since
A\in\mathrm{G}
, we have
a_{i,j}=0
for
j>s_{t_{i}}
for
1\leq t_{i}\leq\lambda
. Therefore,

(\phi({\mathrm{X}}))_{i}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{s_{t_{i}}}a_{i,j}X_{j}+b_{i}\in F_{t_{i}}.

Hence,
\phi({\mathrm{X}})\in{\mathcal{A}}
. Now, we need to show that
\phi
is injective. Let
\phi({\mathrm{X}})=\phi({\mathrm{Y}})
for some
{\mathrm{X}},{\mathrm{Y}}\in{\mathcal{A}}
. Therefore, it follows that
A{\mathrm{X}}+b=A{\mathrm{Y}}+b
which implies
A({\mathrm{X}}-{\mathrm{Y}})=0
, but
A\in\mathrm{GL}(m,\mbox{$\mathbb{F}_{q}$})
and hence
{\mathrm{X}}={\mathrm{Y}}
. Thus,
\phi:{\mathcal{A}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{A}}
is a bijection and therefore,
\phi\in{\text{Aff}}({\mathcal{A}})
.

On the other hand, to show
{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathcal{A}}}\subseteq{\text{Aff}}({\mathcal{A}})
, let
\phi\in{\text{Aff}}({\mathcal{A}})
, i.e.,
\phi:{\mathcal{A}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{A}}
is given by
\phi({\mathrm{X}})=A{\mathrm{X}}+b
where
A\in\mathrm{GL}(m,\mbox{$\mathbb{F}_{q}$})
and
b\in\mbox{$\mathbb{F}$}_{q}^{m}
. We need to show that
\phi\in{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathcal{A}}}
. Let
C_{1},\ldots,C_{m}
denotes the columns of matrix
A
. We consider two cases:

Case 1:

\underline{b=0}:

In this case,
\phi:{\mathcal{A}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{A}}
is given by
\phi({\mathrm{X}})=A{\mathrm{X}}
. Let
e_{1},\ldots,e_{m}
denote the standard basis vectors of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{m}
. Clearly,
e_{i}\in{\mathcal{A}}
for all
1\leq i\leq m
, and hence
\phi(e_{i})=C_{i}\in{\mathcal{A}}
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
. Thus, every column of matrix
A
is an element of
{\mathcal{A}}
. It follows that for
1\leq t_{i}\leq\lambda
such that
s_{{t_{i}}-1}<i\leq s_{t_{i}}
, we get
a_{i,j}\in F_{t_{i}}
for all
1\leq j\leq m
. Now, it remains to show that for
1\leq t_{i}\leq\lambda
,
a_{i,j}=0
for
j>s_{t_{i}}
. We prove the result by using induction on
\lambda
. If
\lambda=1
, there is nothing to prove. Let
\lambda=2
. Then
s_{2}=\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}=m
. Let

A=\begin{bmatrix}A_{11}&A_{12}\\
A_{21}&A_{22}\\
\end{bmatrix}\in\mathrm{GL}(m,\mbox{$\mathbb{F}_{q}$}),

be a block matrix where
A_{ij}
is a matrix of size
\mu_{i}\times\mu_{j}
with entries from
F_{i}
for
i,j\in\{1,2\}
. We need to show
A_{12}=0
, i.e.,
a_{i,j}=0
for
1\leq j\leq\mu_{1}
,
j>\mu_{1}
. Let
{\mathrm{X}}^{(1)}=(\underbrace{{\alpha}_{1},\ldots,{\alpha}_{1}}_{\text{m-1 times}},{\alpha}_{2})^{T}\in{\mathcal{A}}
. Then
\phi({\mathrm{X}}^{(1)})=A{\mathrm{X}}^{(1)}\in{\mathcal{A}}
. But, for
1\leq i\leq m
, we can write

(A{\mathrm{X}}^{(1)})_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_{i,j}{\mathrm{X}}^{(1)}_{j}=\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}a_{i,j}{\alpha}_{1}+a_{i,m}{\alpha}_{2}.

If
1\leq i\leq\mu_{1}
, then
\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m-1}a_{i,j}{\alpha}_{1}+a_{i,m}{\alpha}_{2}\in F_{1}
, but
a_{i,j},{\alpha}_{1}\in F_{1}
which implies that
a_{i,m}{\alpha}_{2}\in F_{1}
. If
a_{i,m}\neq 0
for some
i
, then
{\alpha}_{2}\in F_{1}
, which is a contradiction. Hence,
a_{i,m}=0
for
1\leq i\leq\mu_{1}
. Now let
{\mathrm{X}}^{(2)}=(\underbrace{{\alpha}_{1},\ldots,{\alpha}_{1}}_{\text{m-2 times}},{\alpha}_{2},{\alpha}_{2})^{T}\in{\mathcal{A}}
. Then for
1\leq i\leq\mu_{1}
,
(A{\mathrm{X}}^{(2)})_{i}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m-2}a_{i,j}{\alpha}_{1}+a_{i,(m-1)}{\alpha}_{2}\in F_{1}
. Thus,
a_{i,(m-1)}{\alpha}_{2}\in F_{1}
, which is true if and only if
a_{i,(m-1)}=0
for
1\leq i\leq\mu_{1}
. Thus, proceeding inductively, we get
A_{12}=\bf{0}
.

Now let us assume the result holds for
\lambda-1
.
Consider
{\mathrm{X}}^{(1)}=(\underbrace{{\alpha}_{1},\ldots,{\alpha}_{1}}_{\text{m-1 times}},{\alpha}_{{\lambda}})^{T}\in{\mathcal{A}}
. Then
\phi({\mathrm{X}}^{(1)})=A{\mathrm{X}}^{(1)}\in{\mathcal{A}}
. Therefore,

(A{\mathrm{X}}^{(1)})_{i}\in F_{{\lambda}-1}
for
s_{{\lambda}-2}<i\leq s_{{\lambda}-1}
. It implies
\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m-1}a_{i,j}{\alpha}_{1}+a_{i,m}{\alpha}_{{\lambda}}\in F_{{\lambda}-1}
. But, for
s_{{t_{i}}-1}<i\leq s_{t_{i}}
,
(A{\mathrm{X}}^{(1)})_{i}\in F_{t_{i}}\subseteq F_{{\lambda}-1}
for all
t_{i}\leq{\lambda}-1
. Thus, we get
a_{i,m}{\alpha}_{{\lambda}}\in F_{{\lambda}-1}
which is possible if and only if
a_{i,m}=0
for all
1\leq i\leq s_{{\lambda}-1}
. Continuing as in the case for
{\lambda}=2
, we get
a_{i,j}=0
for all
1\leq i\leq s_{{\lambda}-1}
and
j>s_{{\lambda}-1}
. Hence, we can write the matrix
A
as

A=\begin{bmatrix}A^{\prime}_{11}&\bf{0}\\
A^{\prime}_{21}&A^{\prime}_{22}\\
\end{bmatrix},

where
A^{\prime}_{11}\in\mathrm{GL}(s_{{\lambda}-1},F_{{\lambda}-1})
,
A^{\prime}_{22}\in\mathrm{GL}(\mu_{{\lambda}},F_{{\lambda}})
and
A^{\prime}_{21}\in M_{\mu_{{\lambda}}\times s_{{\lambda}-1}}(F_{{\lambda}})
.
Consider

{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}=F_{1}\times\cdots\times F_{1}\times\cdots\cdots\times F_{{\lambda}-1}\times\cdots\times F_{{\lambda}-1}\subseteq\mbox{$\mathbb{F}$}_{q}^{s_{{\lambda}-1}}.

For
{\mathrm{X}}\in{\mathcal{A}}
, Let
{\mathrm{X}}^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}
obtained from
{\mathrm{X}}
by truncating last
\mu_{{\lambda}}
components. Since
A{\mathrm{X}}\in{\mathcal{A}}
for all
{\mathrm{X}}\in{\mathcal{A}}
, we obtain
A^{\prime}_{11}{\mathrm{X}}^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}
for all
{\mathrm{X}}^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}
. Then, by induction hypothesis,
A^{\prime}_{11}
has the required form, i.e.,

A^{\prime}_{11}=\begin{bmatrix}B_{11}&\bf{0}&\ldots&\bf{0}\\
B_{21}&B_{22}&\ldots&\bf{0}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
B_{({\lambda}-1)1}&B_{({\lambda}-1)2}&\ldots&B_{({\lambda}-1)({\lambda}-1)}\\
\end{bmatrix},

where
B_{ij}\in M_{\mu_{i}\times\mu_{j}}(F_{i})
and
\det(B_{ii})\neq 0
. Hence,

A=\begin{bmatrix}B_{11}&\bf{0}&\ldots&\bf{0}&\bf{0}\\
B_{21}&B_{22}&\ldots&\bf{0}&\bf{0}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
B_{(\lambda-1)1}&B_{(\lambda-1)2}&\ldots&B_{(\lambda-1)(\lambda-1)}&\bf{0}\\
A_{\lambda 1}&A_{\lambda_{2}}&\ldots&A_{\lambda(\lambda-1)}&A_{\lambda\lambda}\\
\end{bmatrix},

where
A^{\prime}_{21}=\begin{bmatrix}A_{\lambda 1}&A_{\lambda_{2}}&\ldots&A_{\lambda(\lambda-1)}\\
\end{bmatrix}
and
A^{\prime}_{22}=A_{\lambda\lambda}
. Hence,
\phi\in{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathcal{A}}}
and thus,
{\text{Aff}}({\mathcal{A}})\subseteq{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathcal{A}}}
.

Case 2:

\underline{b\neq 0}:

Let
\phi\in{\text{Aff}}({\mathcal{A}})
, i.e.,
\phi:{\mathcal{A}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{A}}
is a bijection given by
\phi({\mathrm{X}})=A{\mathrm{X}}+b
where
A\in\mathrm{GL}(m,\mbox{$\mathbb{F}_{q}$})
,
b\in\mbox{$\mathbb{F}$}_{q}^{m}
. We need to show that
A\in\mathrm{G}
and
b\in{\mathcal{A}}
. Note that
{\mathrm{X}}_{0}=(0,0,\ldots,0)^{T}\in{\mathcal{A}}
and by the given hypothesis,
\phi({\mathrm{X}}_{0})\in{\mathcal{A}}
, but
\phi({\mathrm{X}}_{0})=AX_{0}+b=b
. Thus,
b\in{\mathcal{A}}
. Now, let
{\mathrm{Y}}=A{\mathrm{X}}+b
, then
{\mathrm{Y}}\in{\mathcal{A}}
for all
{\mathrm{X}}\in{\mathcal{A}}
. Hence,
A{\mathrm{X}}={\mathrm{Y}}-b\in{\mathcal{A}}
for all
{\mathrm{X}}\in{\mathcal{A}}
. Consider
\psi:{\mathcal{A}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{A}}
given by
\psi({\mathrm{X}})=A{\mathrm{X}}
. Then,
\psi
is a bijection and hence by Case 1,
A\in\mathrm{G}
. Thus, we have proved that
A\in\mathrm{G}
,
b\in{\mathcal{A}}
. Hence,
\phi\in{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathcal{A}}}
.
∎

Corollary 3.5
.

With
{\mathcal{A}}
as defined in equation
(
3.1
)
, we have

|{\text{Aff}}({\mathcal{A}})|=\prod_{i=1}^{\lambda}\left(d_{s_{i}}^{{}^{\mu_{i}(s_{i-1}+1)}}\prod_{t=0}^{\mu_{i}-1}(d_{s_{i}}^{{}^{\mu_{i}}}-d_{s_{i}}^{{}^{t}})\right).

(3.6)

Proof.

We know that
{\text{Aff}}({\mathcal{A}})=\{\sigma_{A,b}\in\mathrm{AGL}(m,\mbox{$\mathbb{F}_{q}$}):A\in\mathrm{G},b\in{\mathcal{A}}\}
. Thus,

\begin{split}\left|{\text{Aff}}({\mathcal{A}})\right|&=|{\mathcal{A}}|\cdot|\mathrm{G}|\\
&=|{\mathcal{A}}|\cdot\left(\prod_{i=1}^{\lambda}\left|\mathrm{GL}(\mu_{i},F_{i})\right|\right)\left(\prod_{t=1}^{\lambda}\left|M_{\mu_{t}\times s_{t-1}}(F_{t})\right|\right)\\
&=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{\lambda}d_{s_{i}}^{{}^{\mu_{i}}}\right)\left(\prod_{i=1}^{\lambda}\left\{\prod_{t=0}^{\mu_{i}-1}(d_{s_{i}}^{{}^{\mu_{i}}}-d_{s_{i}}^{{}^{t}})\right\}\right)\left(\prod_{i=1}^{\lambda}d_{s_{i}}^{{}^{\mu_{i}s_{i-1}}}\right)\\
&=\prod_{i=1}^{\lambda}\left(d_{s_{i}}^{{}^{\mu_{i}(s_{i-1}+1)}}\prod_{t=0}^{\mu_{i}-1}(d_{s_{i}}^{{}^{\mu_{i}}}-d_{s_{i}}^{{}^{t}})\right)\end{split}

∎

4.
Enumeration of minimum weight codewords

We have seen from Theorem
3.2
that minimum weight codewords of
\mathrm{AC}_{q}(u,{\mathcal{A}})
are given by evaluations of polynomials of
\mathcal{S}_{\leq u}({\mathcal{A}})
of the form
f({\mathrm{X}})={\gamma}\cdot h_{k}^{\Omega}(\sigma({\mathrm{X}}))
for some
{\gamma}\in\mbox{$\mathbb{F}$}_{q}^{*},\sigma\in{\text{Aff}}({\mathcal{A}})
and
h_{k}^{\Omega}({\mathrm{X}})
is given by

h_{k}^{\Omega}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{m})=\prod_{\underset{i\neq k}{i=1}}^{j+1}(1-X_{i}^{d_{i}-1})\prod_{{\alpha}=1}^{d_{k}-(d_{j+1}-\ell)}(X_{k}-{\omega}_{{\alpha}}),

(4.1)

for some
1\leq k\leq j+1
such that
d_{k}\geq d_{j+1}-\ell
and
\Omega:=\{{\omega}_{1},\ldots,{\omega}_{d_{k}-(d_{j+1}-\ell)}\}\subseteq A_{k}
. Using the characterization of the affine group
{\text{Aff}}({\mathcal{A}})
from Proposition
3.4
, we can write the polynomial
f({\mathrm{X}})
as follows:

f(X_{1},\ldots,X_{m})={\gamma}\prod_{\underset{i\neq k}{i=1}}^{j+1}(1-(L_{i}+b_{i})^{d_{i}-1})\cdot\prod_{{\alpha}=1}^{d_{k}-(d_{j+1}-\ell)}(L_{k}+b_{k}-{\omega}_{{\alpha}}),

(4.2)

for some
{\gamma}\in\mbox{$\mathbb{F}$}_{q}^{*}
,
1\leq k\leq j+1
such that
d_{k}\geq d_{j+1}-\ell
, and linearly independent linear homogeneous polynomials
L_{1},\dots,L_{j+1}\in\mbox{$\mathbb{F}_{q}$}[X_{1},\dots,X_{m}]
, and scalars
b_{i},\omega_{i}\in\mbox{$\mathbb{F}_{q}$}
such that
\Omega:=\{{\omega}_{1},\ldots,{\omega}_{d_{k}-(d_{j+1}-\ell)}\}\subseteq A_{k}
and

L_{i}\in F_{t_{i}}[X_{1},\dots,X_{s_{t_{i}}}]\text{ and }b_{i}\in F_{t_{i}}\quad\text{whenever }s_{{t_{i}}-1}<i\leq s_{t_{i}}\text{ for }1\leq t_{i}\leq\lambda.

Thus, the support of a minimum weight codeword of
\mathrm{AC}_{q}(u,{\mathcal{A}})
is given by

\mathrm{Supp}_{{\mathcal{A}}}({\bf{c}}_{f})=\bigg(\bigcap_{\underset{i\neq k}{i=1}}^{j+1}Z_{{\mathcal{A}}}\left(L_{i}+b_{i}\right)\bigg)\bigcap\bigg(\bigcup_{{\omega}\in A_{k}\setminus\Omega}Z_{{\mathcal{A}}}(L_{k}+b_{k}-{\omega})\bigg).

Throughout the article, we will use the notations of equation
(
4.2
)
to describe polynomials corresponding to minimum weight codewords. Let
\mbox{$\mathcal{N}$}_{q}(u,{\mathcal{A}})
denotes the set of minimum weight codewords of
\mathrm{AC}_{q}(u,{\mathcal{A}})
. Then

\mbox{$\mathcal{N}$}_{q}(u,{\mathcal{A}})=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\\
d_{k}\geq d_{j+1}-\ell\end{subarray}}^{j+1}\mbox{$\mathcal{N}$}_{q}^{(k)}(u,{\mathcal{A}}),

where

\mbox{$\mathcal{N}$}_{q}^{(k)}(u,{\mathcal{A}}):=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Omega\subseteq A_{k}\\
|\Omega|=d_{k}-(d_{j+1}-\ell)\end{subarray}}\mbox{$\mathcal{N}$}_{q}^{(k,\Omega)}(u,{\mathcal{A}}),

(4.3)

and for a fixed
k
and
\Omega
,
N_{q}^{(k,\Omega)}(u,{\mathcal{A}})
is given by

\mbox{$\mathcal{N}$}_{q}^{(k,\Omega)}(u,{\mathcal{A}}):=\left\{{\bf{c}}_{f}:f({\mathrm{X}})=\gamma\cdot h_{k}^{\Omega}(\sigma({\mathrm{X}}))\text{ for some }{\gamma}\in\mbox{$\mathbb{F}$}_{q}^{*}\text{ and }\sigma\in{\text{Aff}}({\mathcal{A}})\right\}.

(4.4)

Therefore,

\mbox{$\mathcal{N}$}_{q}(u,{\mathcal{A}})=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\\
d_{k}\geq d_{j+1}-\ell\end{subarray}}^{j+1}\left(\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Omega\subseteq A_{k}\\
|\Omega|=d_{k}-(d_{j+1}-\ell)\end{subarray}}\mbox{$\mathcal{N}$}_{q}^{(k,\Omega)}(u,{\mathcal{A}})\right).

(4.5)

We determine
\left|\mbox{$\mathcal{N}$}_{q}(u,{\mathcal{A}})\right|
for
-1\leq u\leq K
. We know, by convention
\mathrm{AC}_{q}(-1,{\mathcal{A}})=\{\bf{0}\}
. Therefore,
\mbox{$\mathcal{N}$}_{q}(-1,{\mathcal{A}})=\emptyset
, and hence

\left|\mbox{$\mathcal{N}$}_{q}(-1,{\mathcal{A}})\right|=0.

If
u=0
, then
\mathrm{AC}_{q}(u,{\mathcal{A}})=\{(\lambda,\ldots,\lambda):\lambda\in\mbox{$\mathbb{F}_{q}$}\}
. Note that
{\delta}_{q}(0,{\mathcal{A}})=n
. Therefore,

\mbox{$\mathcal{N}$}_{q}(u,{\mathcal{A}})=\left|\{\lambda(1,\ldots,1):\lambda\in\mbox{$\mathbb{F}$}_{q}^{*}\}\right|
, and hence

\left|\mbox{$\mathcal{N}$}_{q}(0,{\mathcal{A}})\right|=q-1.

(4.6)

Now, consider
1\leq u\leq K
. Write

u=\sum_{i=1}^{j}(d_{i}-1)+\ell,\qquad 0\leq j<m,\quad 0<\ell\leq d_{j+1}-1.

(4.7)

Define

\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{A}}:=\mbox{$\mathbb{F}$}_{q}^{*}\times{\text{Aff}}({\mathcal{A}})=\{({\gamma},\sigma_{A,b}):{\gamma}\in\mbox{$\mathbb{F}$}_{q}^{*},\sigma_{A,b}\in{\text{Aff}}({\mathcal{A}})\text{ for some }A\in\mathrm{G},b\in{\mathcal{A}}\},

(4.8)

where
\mathrm{G}
is given by definition
3.3
. Then,
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{A}}
is a group with respect to component-wise operation. Moreover,

|\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{A}}|=|\mbox{$\mathbb{F}$}_{q}^{*}|\cdot|{\text{Aff}}({\mathcal{A}})|=(q-1)\prod_{i=1}^{\lambda}\left(d_{s_{i}}^{{}^{\mu_{i}(s_{i-1}+1)}}\prod_{t=0}^{\mu_{i}-1}(d_{s_{i}}^{{}^{\mu_{i}}}-d_{s_{i}}^{{}^{t}})\right).

(4.9)

Fix
k,\Omega
where
1\leq k\leq j+1
such that
d_{k}\geq d_{j+1}-\ell
and
\Omega\subseteq A_{k}
of size
d_{k}-(d_{j+1}-\ell)
. Consider the group action
\Phi
of the group
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{A}}
acting on the set
\mbox{$\mathcal{N}$}_{q}^{(k,\Omega)}(u,{\mathcal{A}})
given by

\Phi:\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{A}}\times\mbox{$\mathcal{N}$}_{q}^{(k,\Omega)}(u,{\mathcal{A}})\rightarrow\mbox{$\mathcal{N}$}_{q}^{(k,\Omega)}(u,{\mathcal{A}})\quad\text{ given by }\quad(({\gamma},\sigma_{A,b}),{\bf{c}}_{f})\mapsto{\gamma}\cdot{\bf{c}}_{g},

(4.10)

where
g({\mathrm{X}})=f(\sigma_{A,b}({\mathrm{X}})).
Note that it is a well-defined group action and moreover, it is easy to observe that

\mbox{$\mathcal{N}$}_{q}^{(k,\Omega)}(u,{\mathcal{A}})=\{\Phi(({\gamma},\sigma_{A,b}),{\bf{c}}_{h_{k}^{\Omega}}):({\gamma},\sigma_{A,b})\in\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{A}}\}.

Therefore, we can say

\mathcal{N}_{q}^{(k,\Omega)}\left(u,\mathcal{A}\right)=\mathrm{orb}_{\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{A}}}({\bf{c}}_{h_{k}^{\Omega}}).

To determine
\left|\mbox{$\mathcal{N}$}_{q}^{(k,\Omega)}(u,{\mathcal{A}})\right|
for all possible choices for
k
and
\Omega
, it suffices to determine
\left|\mathrm{stab}_{\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{A}}}({\bf{c}}_{h_{k}^{\Omega}})\right|
where

\mathrm{stab}_{\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{A}}}({\bf{c}}_{h_{k}^{\Omega}}):=\left\{({\gamma},\sigma_{A,b})\in\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{A}}:{\gamma}\cdot h_{k}^{\Omega}(A{\mathrm{X}}+b)=h_{k}^{\Omega}({\mathrm{X}})\right\}.

(4.11)

Recall that for
1\leq i\leq j+1
,
t_{i}
is the unique integer such that
s_{t_{i}-1}<i\leq s_{t_{i}}
. Define
r:=t_{j+1}
. The following lemmas provide some fundamental conclusions
\mathrm{stab}_{\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{A}}}({\bf{c}}_{h_{k}^{\Omega}})
.

Lemma 4.1
.

For
1\leq u\leq K
, let
u=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{j}(d_{i}-1)+\ell
for unique
j,\ell
such that
0\leq j<m
and
0<\ell\leq d_{j+1}-1
. Let
k,\Omega,\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{A}}
be as defined before. If

({\gamma},\sigma_{A,b})\in\mathrm{stab}_{\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{A}}}({\bf{c}}_{h_{k}^{\Omega}})
, then the following holds:

(i)

a_{i,k}=0
for
1\leq i\leq j+1,i\neq k
.

(ii)

b_{i}=0
for
1\leq i\leq j+1,i\neq k
.

(iii)

a_{k,k}\neq 0
and
{\gamma}=(a_{k,k})^{-s}
where
s=d_{k}-(d_{j+1}-\ell)
.

Proof.

Let
({\gamma},\sigma_{A,b})\in\mathrm{stab}_{\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{A}}}({\bf{c}}_{h_{k}^{\Omega}})
. Then it must follow that
{\gamma}\cdot h_{k}^{\Omega}(A{\mathrm{X}}+b)=h_{k}^{\Omega}({\mathrm{X}})
, where
A=(a_{i,j})\in\mathrm{G}
and
b=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{m})^{T}\in{\mathcal{A}}
. Since
A\in\mathrm{G}
, we get
a_{i,j}=0
whenever
j>s_{t_{i}}
for
1\leq t_{i}\leq\lambda
. Hence, for
\Omega=\{{\omega}_{1},\ldots,{\omega}_{s}\}
where
s=d_{k}-(d_{j+1}-\ell)
, we have

{\gamma}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq k\end{subarray}}^{j+1}\left(1-(L_{i}+b_{i})^{d_{i}-1}\right)\prod_{{\alpha}=1}^{s}(L_{k}+b_{k}-\omega_{{\alpha}})\pmod{{\mathcal{I}}({\mathcal{A}})}=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq k\end{subarray}}^{j+1}\left(1-X_{i}^{d_{i}-1}\right)\prod_{{\alpha}=1}^{s}(X_{k}-\omega_{{\alpha}}),

where
L_{1},\ldots,L_{j+1}
are linearly independent linear homogeneous polynomials such that
L_{i}\in F_{s_{t}}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{s_{t}}]
is given by

L_{i}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{s_{t_{i}}})=\sum_{\zeta=1}^{s_{t_{i}}}a_{i,\zeta}X_{\zeta},

and
b_{i}\in F_{s_{t_{i}}}
for
1\leq i\leq j+1
. Substituting
X_{1}=\cdots=X_{k-1}=X_{k+1}=\cdots=X_{s_{r}}=0
, we get

{\gamma}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq k\end{subarray}}^{j+1}\left(1-(a_{i,k}X_{k}+b_{i})^{d_{i}-1}\right)\prod_{{\alpha}=1}^{s}(a_{k,k}X_{k}+b_{k}-\omega_{{\alpha}})\pmod{(X_{k}^{d_{k}}-X_{k})}=\prod_{{\alpha}=1}^{s}(X_{k}-\omega_{{\alpha}}).

Now, if
\ell<d_{j+1}-1
, then
s\leq d_{k}-2
. If we assume
a_{k,k}=0
, then upon comparing the degrees, we get

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=s_{t_{k}-1}+1\\
i\neq k\\
a_{i,k}\neq 0\end{subarray}}^{j+1}\left(d_{i}-1\pmod{d_{k}}\right)=s,

which is not possible since
\left(d_{i}-1\pmod{d_{k}}\right)=d_{k}-1
for all
s_{t_{k}-1}+1\leq i\leq j+1
. Therefore, we must have
a_{k,k}\neq 0
which further implies that
a_{i,k}=0
for all
i\in\{s_{t_{k}-1}+1,\ldots,j+1\}\setminus\{k\}
. On the other hand, if
\ell=d_{j+1}-1
, then
s=d_{k}-1
and hence, upon comparing degrees, we get

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=s_{t_{k}-1}+1\\
a_{i,k}\neq 0\end{subarray}}^{j+1}\left(d_{i}-1\pmod{d_{k}}\right)=d_{k}-1,

which holds true if and only if there exists a unique
i
such that
s_{t_{k}-1}<i\leq s_{t_{k}}
and
a_{i,k}\neq 0
. Without loss of generality, we assume that
i=k
. Note that
a_{i,k}=0
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,s_{t_{k-1}}\}
since
A\in\mathrm{G}
. Thus, it follows that
a_{i,k}=0
for
1\leq i\leq j+1,i\neq k
. Now, comparing the coefficient of
X_{k}^{s}
on both sides, we get

{\gamma}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq k\end{subarray}}^{j+1}\left(1-(b_{i})^{d_{i}-1}\right)a_{k,k}^{s}=1.

Since
b_{i}\in A_{i}=\mbox{$\mathbb{F}$}_{d_{i}}
, Lemma
2.1
tells us that
(1-(b_{i})^{d_{i}-1})\neq 0
if and only if
b_{i}=0
. Therefore, we must have
b_{i}=0
for
1\leq i\leq j+1,i\neq k
and
{\gamma}=(a_{k,k})^{-s}
. Hence, the proof is complete.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

With notations as before, let
({\gamma},\sigma_{A,b})\in\mathrm{stab}_{\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{A}}}({\bf{c}}_{h_{k}^{\Omega}})
. Then
a_{i,\zeta}=0
for
s_{r-1}<i\leq j+1
and
j+2\leq\zeta\leq s_{r}.

Proof.

Let
({\gamma},\sigma_{A,b})\in\mathrm{stab}_{\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{A}}}({\bf{c}}_{h_{k}^{\Omega}})
. Let
s=d_{k}-(d_{j+1}-\ell)
. Then, it follows that

{\gamma}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq k\end{subarray}}^{j+1}\left(1-(\sum_{\zeta=1}^{s_{t_{i}}}a_{i,\zeta}X_{\zeta}+b_{i})^{d_{i}-1}\right)\prod_{{\alpha}=1}^{s}\left(\sum_{\zeta=1}^{s_{t_{k}}}a_{k,\zeta}X_{\zeta}+b_{k}-\omega_{{\alpha}}\right)\pmod{{\mathcal{I}}({\mathcal{A}})}=\\
\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq k\end{subarray}}^{j+1}\left(1-X_{i}^{d_{i}-1}\right)\prod_{{\alpha}=1}^{s}(X_{k}-\omega_{{\alpha}}),

where
1\leq t_{i}\leq r
is the unique integer such that
s_{t_{i}-1}<i\leq s_{t_{i}}
and
\Omega=\{\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{s}\}\subseteq A_{k}
. Using Lemma
4.1
, the above equation reduces to

{\gamma}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq k\end{subarray}}^{j+1}\left(1-\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\zeta=1\\
\zeta\neq k\end{subarray}}^{s_{t_{i}}}a_{i,\zeta}X_{\zeta}\right)^{d_{i}-1}\right)\prod_{{\alpha}=1}^{s}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\zeta=1\\
\zeta\neq k\end{subarray}}^{s_{t_{k}}}a_{k,\zeta}X_{\zeta}+a_{k,k}X_{k}+b_{k}-\omega_{{\alpha}}\right)\pmod{{\mathcal{I}}({\mathcal{A}})}=\\
\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq k\end{subarray}}^{j+1}\left(1-X_{i}^{d_{i}-1}\right)\prod_{{\alpha}=1}^{s}(X_{k}-\omega_{{\alpha}}),

where
a_{k,k}\neq 0
.
Since
s=d_{k}-(d_{j+1}-\ell)
and
0<\ell\leq d_{j+1}-1
, it follows that
0\leq s\leq d_{k}-1
and hence
A_{k}\setminus\{\Omega\}\neq\emptyset
. Let
\widehat{\omega}\in A_{k}\setminus\{\Omega\}
. We first consider the case when
s>0
. If
a_{\rho,\eta}\neq 0
for some
\rho\in\{s_{r-1}+1,\ldots,j+1\}
and
\eta\in\{j+2,\ldots,s_{r}\}
, then evaluating the above equation at
P=(p_{1},\ldots,p_{m})\in{\mathcal{A}}
given by

p_{\zeta}=\begin{cases}1&{\text{ if }\zeta=k},\\
\frac{1}{a_{\rho,\eta}}\left(\omega_{1}-b_{k}-a_{k,k}\widehat{\omega}\right)&{\text{ if }\zeta=\eta},\\
0&{\text{ otherwise, }}\\
\end{cases}

we obtain that
LHS=0
whereas
RHS\neq 0
, which is a contradiction. On the other hand, if
s=0
, then the equation becomes

{\gamma}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq k\end{subarray}}^{j+1}\left(1-\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\zeta=1\\
\zeta\neq k\end{subarray}}^{s_{t_{i}}}a_{i,\zeta}X_{\zeta}\right)^{d_{i}-1}\right)\pmod{{\mathcal{I}}({\mathcal{A}})}=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq k\end{subarray}}^{j+1}\left(1-X_{i}^{d_{i}-1}\right).

If
a_{\rho,\eta}\neq 0
for some
\rho\in\{s_{r-1}+1,\ldots,j+1\}
and
\eta\in\{j+2,\ldots,s_{r}\}
, then evaluating the above equation at
e_{\eta}=(0,\ldots,0,1,0,\ldots,0)
, where
1
occurs at
\eta^{\textrm{th}}
position, then
LHS=0
, whereas
RHS=1
, which again is a contradiction. Hence, we must have

a_{i,\zeta}=0
for all
i\in\{s_{r-1}+1,\ldots,j+1\}
and
\zeta\in\{j+2,\ldots,s_{r}\}
.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

With notations as before, let
({\gamma},\sigma_{A,b})\in\mathrm{stab}_{\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{A}}}({\bf{c}}_{h_{k}^{\Omega}})
. Consider the matrix
A^{\prime}=(a_{ij}^{{}^{\prime}})\in\mathrm{G}
where

a^{\prime}_{i,j}=\begin{cases}a_{i,j}&\text{ if }i\neq k,\\
a_{k,j}&\text{ if }i=k\text{ and }j\in\{s_{t_{k}}+1,\ldots,m\}\cup\{k\}.\\
\end{cases}

Then
({\gamma},\sigma_{A^{\prime},b})\in\mathrm{stab}_{\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{A}}}({\bf{c}}_{h_{k}^{\Omega}})
.

Proof.

We first note that
A^{\prime}
differs from
A
only in some entries of
k^{th}
-row. Since
A^{\prime}\in\mathrm{G}
, we have
({\gamma},\sigma_{A^{\prime},b})\in\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{A}}
. Let
g({\mathrm{X}})\in\mbox{$\mathbb{F}_{q}$}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}]
be given by
g({\mathrm{X}})={\gamma}\cdot h_{k}^{\Omega}(A^{\prime}{\mathrm{X}}+b)
. Then
{\bf{c}}_{g}\in\mbox{$\mathcal{N}$}^{(k,\Omega)}(u,{\mathcal{A}})
. To show
({\gamma},\sigma_{A^{\prime},b})\in\mathrm{stab}_{\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{A}}}({\bf{c}}_{h_{k}^{\Omega}})
, it suffices to show that
f(P)=g(P)=h_{k}^{\Omega}(P)
for all
P\in{\mathcal{A}}
where
f({\mathrm{X}})={\gamma}\cdot h_{k}^{\Omega}(A{\mathrm{X}}+b)
. Since
({\gamma},\sigma_{A,b})\in\mathrm{stab}_{\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{A}}}({\bf{c}}_{h_{k}^{\Omega}})
, Lemma
4.1
gives
a_{i,k}=0=b_{i}
for all
i\in[j+1]\setminus\{k\}
,
a_{k,k}\neq 0
and
{\gamma}=(a_{k,k})^{-s}
where
s=d_{k}-(d_{j+1}-\ell)
. Therefore, We can write

\begin{split}g({\mathrm{X}})&={\gamma}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq k\end{subarray}}^{j+1}\left(1-\left(\sum_{\zeta=1}^{s_{t_{i}}}a^{\prime}_{i,\zeta}X_{\zeta}\right)^{d_{i}-1}\right)\prod_{{\alpha}=1}^{s}\left(\sum_{\zeta=1}^{s_{t_{k}}}a^{\prime}_{k,\zeta}X_{\zeta}+b_{k}-\omega_{{\alpha}}\right)\\
&=({\gamma}a_{k,k}^{s})\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq k\end{subarray}}^{j+1}\left(1-\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\zeta=1\\
\zeta\neq k\end{subarray}}^{s_{t_{i}}}a_{i,\zeta}X_{\zeta}\right)^{d_{i}-1}\right)\prod_{{\alpha}=1}^{s}\left(\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\zeta=1\\
\zeta\neq k\end{subarray}}^{s_{t_{k}}}\frac{a^{\prime}_{k,\zeta}}{a_{kk}}X_{\zeta}\right)+X_{k}-\omega_{{\alpha}}\right),\\
\end{split}

where
{\gamma}a_{k,k}^{s}=1
. We know that
({\gamma},\sigma_{A,b})\in\mathrm{stab}_{\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{A}}}({\bf{c}}_{h_{k}^{\Omega}})
which implies
{\bf{c}}_{f}={\bf{c}}_{h_{k}^{\Omega}}
and hence
f(P)=h_{k}^{\Omega}(P)
for all
P\in{\mathcal{A}}
. Let
S=\mathrm{Supp}({\bf{c}}_{h_{k}^{\Omega}})
. Then

S=\{P=(p_{1},\ldots,p_{m})\in{\mathcal{A}}:p_{i}=0\text{ for all }i\in[j+1]\setminus\{k\},p_{k}\in A_{k}\setminus\Omega\}.

Now, if
P\in S
, then

g(P)=\prod_{{\alpha}=1}^{s}(p_{k}-\omega_{{\alpha}})=h_{k}^{\Omega}(P)=f(P).

If
P\in{\mathcal{A}}\setminus S
, we must have
h_{k}^{\Omega}(P)=f(P)=0
since

{\mathcal{A}}\setminus S=\{(0,\ldots,0,p_{k},0,\ldots,0,p_{j+2},\ldots,p_{m}):p_{k}\in\Omega\}\\
\bigcup\{(p_{1},\ldots,p_{m}):(p_{1},\ldots,p_{k-1},p_{k+1},\ldots,p_{j+1})\neq(0,\ldots,0)\}.

Therefore, it suffices to show that
g(P)=0
for all
P\in{\mathcal{A}}\setminus S
. Let there exists
P\in{\mathcal{A}}\setminus S
such that
g(P)\neq 0
. But, it holds true if and only if
\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\zeta=1\\
\zeta\neq k\end{subarray}}^{s_{t_{i}}}a_{i,\zeta}p_{\zeta}=0
for
1\leq i\leq j+1,i\neq k
and
\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\zeta=1\\
\zeta\neq k\end{subarray}}^{s_{t_{k}}}\frac{a^{\prime}_{k,\zeta}}{a_{k,k}}p_{\zeta}\neq p_{k}-\omega_{{\alpha}}
for all
1\leq{\alpha}\leq s
. It follows from Lemma
4.2
that

a_{i,\zeta}=0
for
s_{r-1}<i\leq j+1
and
j+2\leq\zeta\leq s_{r}
. Therefore, the system of equations
\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\zeta=1\\
\zeta\neq k\end{subarray}}^{s_{t}}a_{i,\zeta}X_{\zeta}=0
is a homogeneous system of
j
linearly independent equations in
j
variables, namely
X_{1},\ldots,X_{k-1},X_{k+2},\ldots,X_{j+1}
. Hence, it admits a trivial solution, i.e.,
p_{\zeta}=0
for
1\leq\zeta\leq j+1,{\zeta}\neq k
. Now, substituting these values in
g(P)
, we get

g(P)=\prod_{{\alpha}=1}^{s}(p_{k}-\omega_{{\alpha}})=0,

since
P\in{\mathcal{A}}\setminus S
and
p_{i}=0
for all
i\in[j+1]\setminus\{k\}
, which is a contradiction. Hence,
g(P)=0=h_{k}^{\Omega}(P)
for all
P\in{\mathcal{A}}
. Therefore,
({\gamma},\sigma_{A^{\prime},b})\in\mathrm{stab}_{\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{A}}}({\bf{c}}_{h_{k}^{\Omega}})
.
∎

We now determine
\left|\mbox{$\mathcal{N}$}_{q}^{(k,\Omega)}(u,{\mathcal{A}})\right|
for all possible choices of
k
and
\Omega
and consider different cases accordingly.

4.1.
Case
\ell=d_{j+1}-1

For
1\leq u\leq K
, we write

u=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{j}(d_{i}-1)+\ell
, for uniquely determined integers
j,\ell
such that
0\leq j<m
and
0<\ell\leq d_{j+1}-1
. In this section, we consider the case when
\ell=d_{j+1}-1
. Then, we can write

u=\sum_{i=1}^{j+1}(d_{i}-1).

Hence, for
\Omega=\{{\omega}_{1},\ldots,{\omega}_{d_{k}-1}\}
, we have

h_{k}^{\Omega}({\mathrm{X}})=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq k\end{subarray}}^{j+1}(1-X_{i}^{d_{i}-1})\prod_{{\alpha}=1}^{d_{k}-1}(X_{k}-{\omega}_{{\alpha}}).

Let
\Omega^{\prime}=\{{\omega}^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,{\omega}^{\prime}_{d_{k}-1}\}\subseteq A_{k}
such that
\Omega\neq\Omega^{\prime}
. Then there exist
a_{k,k},b_{k}\in A_{k}
such that
a_{k,k}\neq 0
and
\Omega^{\prime}=a_{k,k}\Omega+b_{k}=\{a_{k,k}{\omega}_{i}+b_{k}:1\leq i\leq d_{k}-1\}
. Indeed, define

a_{k,k}=1,\quad b_{k}={\omega}^{\prime}_{0}-{\omega}_{0},

where
\{{\omega}_{0}\}=A_{k}\setminus\Omega
and
\{{\omega}^{\prime}_{0}\}=A_{k}\setminus\Omega^{\prime}
. Then, it follows that for
{\gamma}=1,A=I_{m}
and
b=(0,\ldots,0,b_{k},0\ldots,0)
, we get
{\gamma}\cdot h_{k}^{\Omega}(A{\mathrm{X}}+b)=h_{k}^{\Omega^{\prime}}({\mathrm{X}})
and hence

\mbox{$\mathcal{N}$}_{q}^{(k,\Omega)}(u,{\mathcal{A}})=\mbox{$\mathcal{N}$}_{q}^{(k,\Omega^{\prime})}(u,{\mathcal{A}}).

Therefore, without loss of generality, let
\Omega=A_{k}\setminus\{0\}
and since
A_{k}
is a subfield of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of size
d_{k}
, it follows that

\prod_{{\alpha}=1}^{d_{k}-1}(X_{k}-{\omega}_{{\alpha}})=1-X_{k}^{d_{k}-1}.

Hence, we can write

h_{k}^{\Omega}({\mathrm{X}})=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq k\end{subarray}}^{j+1}(1-X_{i}^{d_{i}-1})\prod_{{\alpha}=1}^{d_{k}-1}(X_{k}-{\omega}_{{\alpha}})=\prod_{i=1}^{j+1}(1-X_{i}^{d_{i}-1}).

Note that
h_{k}^{\Omega}({\mathrm{X}})
is independent of
k
and
\Omega
. Thus, every minimum weight codeword
{\bf{c}}_{f}
of
\mathrm{AC}_{q}(u,{\mathcal{A}})
is given by
{\bf{c}}_{f}={\gamma}\cdot{\bf{c}}_{h_{j+1}\circ\sigma}
for some
{\gamma}\in\mbox{$\mathbb{F}$}_{q}^{*}
and
\sigma\in{\text{Aff}}({\mathcal{A}})
and
h_{j+1}({\mathrm{X}})\in\mathcal{S}_{\leq u}({\mathcal{A}})
is given by

h_{j+1}({\mathrm{X}})=\prod_{i=1}^{j+1}(1-X_{i}^{d_{i}-1}).

(4.12)

Thus,

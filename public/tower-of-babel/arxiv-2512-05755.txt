The Commuting Graphs of Small Dimensional Solvable Lie Algebras
Hieu V. Ha, Vu A. Le, Tuan A. Nguyen, Tuyen T. M. Nguyen, Hoa D. Quang

Abstract
Let Z ⁡ ( ℒ ) Z(\cal L) be the center of a noncommutative Lie algebra ℒ \cal L with Lie bracket [ ⋅ , ⋅ ] [\cdot,\cdot] . The commuting graph of ℒ \cal L is then defined by the simple undirected graph Γ ⁡ ( ℒ ) = ( V ℒ , E ℒ ) \Gamma({\cal L})=(V_{\cal L},E_{\cal L}) having the set of vertices V ℒ = ℒ ∖ Z ⁡ ( ℒ ) V_{\mathcal{L}}=\mathcal{L}\setminus Z(\mathcal{L}) and the set of edges E ℒ = { { x , y } ∣ [ x , y ] = 0 } E_{\cal L}=\left\{\{x,y\}\mid[x,y]=0\right\} . This paper explicitly aims to determine the commuting graphs of all solvable Lie algebras of dimension up to 4 over any field 𝔽 \mathbb{F} .

Introduction

In recent years, exploring connections between graph theory and other mathematical structures, such as group theory and ring theory, has gained significant attention. Numerous studies have investigated these connections (see, for example,
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
]
). Consider a non-empty subset
\cal S
of a noncommutative ring
(\mathcal{R},\cdot)
with its centralizer
Z(\mathcal{S}):=\left\{x\mid x\cdot s=s\cdot x,\,\forall s\in\mathcal{S}\right\}
, the commuting graph of
\cal S
, denoted by
\Gamma(\cal S)
, is then defined as a simple undirected graph with the vertex set
V=\mathcal{S}\setminus Z(\cal S)
, where two vertices
u,v\in V
are adjacent if and only if
u\cdot v=v\cdot u

[
3
]
. The connectedness of commuting graphs for certain important subsets of the matrix algebra
M_{n}(\mathbb{F})
has been studied
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
7
,
12
]
. It is also well established that a matrix algebra
M_{n}(\mathbb{F})
can be viewed as a Lie algebra, with the Lie bracket defined by
[A,B]=AB-BA
. Based on this connection, Wang and Xia extended the concept of commuting graphs to Lie algebras and determined the diameters of commuting graphs of all simple Lie algebras
[
17
]
.

This paper first examines the connectedness of the commuting graphs of a class of 1-step solvable Lie algebras over a field
\mathbb{F}
in Section
3
, and then explicitly determines the commuting graphs of all solvable Lie algebras of dimension up to 4 (Sections
4
,
5
). Specifically, we will prove that if the derived algebra
\mathcal{L}^{1}
of a finite-dimensional Lie algebra
\cal L
satisfies:
\mathcal{L}^{1}\cap Z(\mathcal{L})=\{0\}
and
\dim\left(\mathcal{L}^{1}+Z(\mathcal{L})\right)=\dim\mathcal{L}-1
, then its commuting graph is disconnected. Moreover, this commuting graph contains
|\mathcal{L}^{1}|+1
connected components, and each component is a complete graph (Theorem
5
). In addition, we also show that any non-commutative solvable Lie algebra of dimension up to 3 has disconnected commuting graph, and its connected components are complete graphs (see Table
3
). Finally, we observe that all Lie algebras of dimension 4, except the direct sum of two copies of a 2-dimensional Lie algebras, have disconnected commuting graphs. Moreover, their connected components are either a complete graph, a generalized windmill graph, or a union of complete graphs (see Table
4
).

Preliminaries

We first recall some basic concepts in graph theory. We refer the reader to
[
6
]
for more details. A
simple undirected graph
is represented as an ordered pair,
G=(V,E)
, of vertex set
V\neq\emptyset
and edge set

E\subseteq\left\{\{x,y\}\mid x,y\in V\text{ and }x\neq y\right\}.

(1)

Two distinct vertices
u
and
v
are said to be
adjacent
if
\{u,v\}
belongs to
E
. As usual, a
path
(joining
v_{1}
and
v_{k}
) of
G
is a sequence of pair-wise distinct vertices
v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{k}
such that
\{v_{i},v_{i+1}\}\in E
for every
i=1,2,\ldots,k-1
. We will denote this path as
\{v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{k}\}
. If
U
is a nonempty subset of
V
then the subgraph of
G
induced by
U
is defined as the graph having the vertex set
U
and the edge set
\left\{\{x,y\}\in E\mid x,y\in U\right\}
.
If every pair of distinct vertices of
G
is joined by a path, we say that
G
is connected. Otherwise, it is said to be disconnected. The subgraph of
G
induced by a maximal collection of vertices such that every pair of vertices is connected by a path is known as a
connected component
of
G
. The vertex sets of all the connected components of
G
form a partition of the vertex set of
G
.
G
is said to be
complete
if every pair of distinct vertices is adjacent. We will denote the complete graph on
n
vertices by
K_{n}
, and on a set of vertices
S\subseteq V
by
K_{S}
.
G
is a so-called
(K_{S},v)
-
windmill graph
if
G
is the graph obtained by joining some copies of the complete graph
K_{S}
at a shared vertex
v
. If a complete graph of a vertex set
U
replaces
v
, then the graph is called a generalized windmill graph of type 1
[
9
]
. More generally, we will denote by
W(K_{S},U,t)
a graph obtained by joining
t
copies of the complete graph
K_{S}
to every vertex of the complete graph
K_{U}
(
t
is not necessary to be finite).
If
G^{\prime}=(V^{\prime},E^{\prime})
and
G^{\prime\prime}=(V^{\prime\prime},E^{\prime\prime})
are two subgraphs of
G
then the union of
G^{\prime}
and
G^{\prime\prime}
, denoted as
G^{\prime}\cup G^{\prime\prime}
, is the graph which has vertex set
V^{\prime}\cup V^{\prime\prime}
and edge set
E^{\prime}\cup E^{\prime\prime}
.

We then recall some basic concepts in Lie algebras. We refer the reader to
[
16
]
for more details. Let
(\mathcal{L},[\cdot,\cdot])
be a Lie algebra defined over a field
\mathbb{F}
.
Then its
center
,
Z(\cal L)
, is defined as:

Z(\mathcal{L}):=\{x\in\mathcal{L}\mid[x,y]=0\text{ for every }y\in\cal L\}.

(2)

For two subsets
\mathcal{L}_{1}
and
\mathcal{L}_{2}
of
\mathcal{L}
, we define

\mathcal{L}_{1}+\mathcal{L}_{2}:=\{x_{1}+x_{2}\mid x_{1}\in\mathcal{L}_{1},x_{2}\in\mathcal{L}_{2}\}.

(3)

In the case
\mathcal{L}_{1}
contains only one element, says
x
, then we define
x+\mathcal{L}_{2}
as
\{x\}+\mathcal{L}_{2}
. That is,

x+\mathcal{L}_{2}=\{x+y\mid y\in\mathcal{L}_{2}\}.

(4)

If
\mathcal{L}_{1}
and
\mathcal{L}_{2}
are two Lie subalgebras of
\mathcal{L}
such that
\mathcal{L}_{1}+\mathcal{L}_{2}=\mathcal{L}
,
\mathcal{L}_{1}\cap\mathcal{L}_{2}=[\mathcal{L}_{1},\mathcal{L}_{2}]=\{0\}
, then
\mathcal{L}
is said to be decomposed into the direct sum of
\mathcal{L}_{1}
and
\mathcal{L}_{2}
. We will denote this direct sum as
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{L}_{2}
.

It is well known that any finite-dimensional Lie algebra can be uniquely determined via its structure constants
[
10
]
. More precisely, the Lie bracket of any pair of elements
x
,
y
in an
n
-dimensional Lie algebra, spanned by
\{e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{n}\}
, can be recovered from the structure constants
\{(c_{ij})^{k}\mid 1\leq i<j\leq n,1\leq k\leq n\}
defined as follows:

[e_{i},e_{j}]=c_{ij}^{1}e_{1}+c_{ij}^{2}e_{2}+\cdots+c_{ij}^{n}e_{n}.

(5)

Therefore, throughout this paper, we will use the following notation:

\mathcal{L}=\left<e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{n}\right>\colon[e_{i},e_{j}]=c_{ij}^{1}e_{1}+c_{ij}^{2}e_{2}+\cdots+c_{ij}^{n}e_{n};\,(i,j)\in M

(6)

to indicate the
n
-dimensional Lie algebra
\mathcal{L}
spanned by
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}\}
and having their structure constants:

[e_{i},e_{j}]=c_{ij}^{1}e_{1}+c_{ij}^{2}e_{2}+\cdots+c_{ij}^{n}e_{n};\,\forall(i,j)\in M.

(7)

for some
M\subseteq\left\{\{i,j\}\mid i\neq j\text{ and }1\leq i<j\leq n\right\}
. For ease of use, we constantly set
[e_{r},e_{t}]=0
for any
(r,t)\notin M
.

Definition 1
.

[
17
]

Let
\cal L
be a noncommutative Lie algebra over a field
\mathbb{F}
with center
Z(\cal L)
. Then the commuting graph of
\cal L
, denoted by
\Gamma(\mathcal{L})=(V_{\mathcal{L}},E_{\mathcal{L}})
is the simple undirected graph which have

i)

the vertex set
V_{\cal L}:=\mathcal{L}\setminus Z(\cal L)
, and

ii)

the edge set
E_{\cal L}:=\left\{\{x,y\}\,|\,x\neq y,\text{ and }[x,y]=0\right\}
.

For convenience, from now on, we will denote by
\text{char}(\mathbb{F})
,
\mathrm{CC}_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathcal{L})
, and
S^{\#}
the characteristic of the field
\mathbb{F}
, the set of all connected components of
\Gamma(\mathcal{L})
, and the set of all non-zero elements of a subset
S\subseteq\cal L
, respectively.

Since
[x,\lambda x+z]=0
for every
\lambda\in\mathbb{F},\,x\in\mathcal{L},\,z\in Z(\mathcal{L})
, we easily obtain the following result about connected components of commuting graphs.

Lemma 2
.

Let
\Gamma(\mathcal{L})
be the commuting graph of a noncommutative Lie algebra
\cal L
over a field
\mathbb{F}
. If
C_{\mathcal{L}}
is a connected component of
\Gamma(\mathcal{L})
then
\lambda x+z\in C_{\mathcal{L}}
for every
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}^{*}
,
x\in C_{\mathcal{L}}
, and
z\in Z(\mathcal{L})
.

The following corollary is directly implied by the above lemma.

Corollary 3
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a Lie algebra. Then the subgraph of
\Gamma(\mathcal{L})
induced by any nonempty subset of
\{\lambda s\mid\lambda\in\mathbb{F}^{*},s\in V_{\cal L}\}
is complete.

To close this section, we shall note here that the commuting graphs of two isomorphic Lie algebras are isomorphic. However, two non-isomorphic Lie algebras might have the same commuting graph.
This is seen in the following example.

Example 4
.

Let
\mathcal{N}_{3,1}
and
\mathcal{N}_{3,2,1}
be the following Lie algebras:

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{3,1}=\left<x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}\right>\colon

\displaystyle[x_{3},x_{1}]=x_{1},\,[x_{3},x_{2}]=x_{2};

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{3,2,1}=\left<y_{1},y_{2},y_{3}\right>\colon

\displaystyle[y_{3},y_{1}]=y_{2},\,[y_{3},y_{2}]=y_{2}+y_{1}.

According to the classification in Table
1
,
\mathcal{N}_{3,1}
is not isomorphic to
\mathcal{N}_{3,2,1}
. The centers of these Lie algebras are determined by:

Z(\mathcal{N}_{3,1})=Z(\mathcal{N}_{3,2,1})=\{0\}.

Therefore,
\mathcal{N}_{3,1}^{\#}
and
\mathcal{N}_{3,2,1}^{\#}
are the vertex sets of
\Gamma(\mathcal{N}_{3,1})
and
\Gamma(\mathcal{N}_{3,2,1})
, respectively. Now, it is easy to verify that

\displaystyle[a_{1}x_{1}+a_{2}x_{2}+a_{3}x_{3},b_{1}x_{1}+b_{2}x_{2}+b_{3}x_{3}]=0\iff a_{1}b_{3}-a_{3}b_{1}=a_{2}b_{3}-a_{3}b_{2}=0,

(8)

\displaystyle[a_{1}y_{1}+a_{2}y_{2}+a_{3}y_{3},b_{1}y_{1}+b_{2}y_{2}+b_{3}y_{3}]=0\iff a_{1}b_{3}-a_{3}b_{1}=a_{2}b_{3}-a_{3}b_{2}=0,

(9)

for every
x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}\in\mathcal{N}_{3,1}
,
y_{1},y_{2},y_{3}\in\mathcal{N}_{3,2,1}
, and
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},b_{1},b_{2},b_{3}\in\mathbb{F}
. Note that

a_{1}b_{3}-a_{3}b_{1}=a_{2}b_{3}-a_{3}b_{2}=0\iff\left[\begin{aligned} &a_{3}=b_{3}=0\\
&\left\{\begin{aligned} &a_{3}\neq 0,\\
&(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3})=\dfrac{b_{3}}{a_{3}}(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})\end{aligned}\right.\end{aligned}\right.

(10)

It follows that

1.

the subgraph of
\Gamma(\mathcal{N}_{3,1})
induced by
\left<x_{1},x_{2}\right>^{\#}
is a complete graph and a connected component of
\Gamma(\mathcal{N}_{3,1})
, and

2.

the subgraph of
\Gamma(\mathcal{N}_{3,1})
induced by a set of the form
\left\{\lambda(x_{3}+\mu_{1}x_{1}+\mu_{2}x_{2})\mid\lambda\in\mathbb{F}^{*}\right\}
is a complete graph and a connected component of
\Gamma(\mathcal{N}_{3,1})
for every
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\in\mathbb{F}
.

Therefore, the collection of the connected components of
\Gamma(\mathcal{N}_{3,1})
is

\mathrm{CC}_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathcal{N}_{3,1})=\left\{K_{\left<x_{1},x_{2}\right>^{\#}},K_{\left<x_{3}+\mu_{1}x_{1}+\mu_{2}x_{2}\right>^{\#}}\mid\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\in\mathbb{F}\right\}.

(11)

Similarly,

\mathrm{CC}_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathcal{N}_{3,2,1})=\left\{K_{\left<y_{1},y_{2}\right>^{\#}},K_{\left<y_{3}+\mu_{1}y_{1}+\mu_{2}y_{2}\right>^{\#}}\mid\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\in\mathbb{F}\right\}.

3
Commuting graphs of solvable Lie algebras

This section will study some interesting properties of the commuting graph of solvable Lie algebras. These results are useful to study commuting graphs of solvable Lie algebras of dimension up to 4 in next sections. Recall that a Lie algebra is said to be 1-step solvable if the derived algebra
\mathcal{L}^{1}:=[\cal L,L]
is commutative.

Theorem 5
.

Let
\cal L
be a 1-step solvable Lie algebra of dimension
n
such that
\mathcal{L}^{1}\cap Z(\mathcal{L})=\{0\}
and
\dim\left(\mathcal{L}^{1}+Z(\mathcal{L})\right)=\dim\mathcal{L}-1
. Then the commuting graph of
\cal L
is disconnected. Moreover, for every
u\in\mathcal{L}\setminus\left(\mathcal{L}^{1}+Z(\mathcal{L})\right)
, we have

\mathrm{CC}_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathcal{L})=\left\{K_{(\mathcal{L}^{1})^{\#}+Z(\mathcal{L})},K_{\left<u+v\right>^{\#}+Z(\mathcal{L})}\mid v\in\mathcal{L}^{1}\right\}.

(13)

In particular, if
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{F}_{q}
, then
\Gamma(\mathcal{L})
has
\left(1+q^{\dim\mathcal{L}^{1}}\right)
connected components, including the complete graph
K_{q^{n-1}-q^{\dim Z(\mathcal{L})}}
and
q^{\dim\mathcal{L}^{1}}
complete graphs
K_{(q-1)q^{\dim Z(\mathcal{L})}}
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{L}
is 1-step solvable and
\dim\left(\mathcal{L}^{1}+Z(\mathcal{L})\right)=\dim\mathcal{L}-1
, there is a basis
\{e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{n}\}
of
\cal L
such that

1.

\mathcal{L}^{1}=\left<e_{1},\ldots,e_{k}\right>
is commutative for some
1\leq k\leq n-1
;

2.

Z(\mathcal{L})=\left<e_{k+1},\ldots,e_{n-1}\right>
; and

3.

the restriction of
\mathrm{ad}_{e_{n}}
on
\mathcal{L}^{1}
, denoted by
(\mathrm{ad}_{e_{n}})_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}
, is an isomorphism on
\mathcal{L}^{1}
.

It follows that the subgraph of
\Gamma(\mathcal{L})
induced by vertices contained in
\mathcal{L}^{1}+Z(\mathcal{L})
is a connected component of
\Gamma(\mathcal{L})
. Note that the set of these vertices coincides with
\left(\mathcal{L}^{1}\right)^{\#}+Z(\mathcal{L})
, because
\mathcal{L}^{1}\cap Z(\mathcal{L})=\{0\}
. In addition, this connected component is a complete graph on
(\mathcal{L}^{1})^{\#}+Z(\mathcal{L})
because
\mathcal{L}^{1}+Z(\mathcal{L})
is commutative.

Moreover, since
(\mathrm{ad}_{e_{n}})_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}
is an isomorphism, the following equivalences hold for every
\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}^{*}
, and
\mu_{1},\mu_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,\mu_{n-1},\mu_{n-1}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}
:

\begin{array}[]{ll}&\left[\lambda(e_{n}+\mu_{1}e_{1}+\cdots+\mu_{n-1}e_{n-1},\lambda^{\prime}(e_{n}+\mu_{1}^{\prime}e_{1}+\cdots+\mu_{n-1}^{\prime}e_{n-1}\right]=0\\
\iff&\lambda\lambda^{\prime}\mathrm{ad}_{e_{n}}\left((\mu_{1}^{\prime}-\mu_{1})e_{1}+\cdots+(\mu_{k}^{\prime}-\mu_{k})e_{k}\right)=0\\
\iff&\mu_{1}^{\prime}=\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu^{\prime}_{k}=\mu_{k}.\end{array}

Therefore, for each
\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{k}
, the subgraph of
\Gamma(\mathcal{L})
induced by vertices in

T_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\ldots,\mu_{k}}:=\{\lambda(e_{n}+\mu_{1}e_{1}+\cdots+\mu_{k}e_{k})\mid\lambda\in\mathbb{F}^{*}\}+Z(\mathcal{L})

is a connected component of
\Gamma(\mathcal{L})
, and this connected component is a complete graph. Note that

\left\{\{T_{\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{k}}\mid\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{k}\in\mathbb{F}\}+Z(\mathcal{L})\right\}=\{\left<u+v\right>^{\#}+Z(\mathcal{L})\mid v\in\mathcal{L}^{1}\}

for every
u\in\mathcal{L}\setminus(\mathcal{L}^{1}+Z(\mathcal{L}))
.
Furthermore, for every
u\in\mathcal{L}\setminus(\mathcal{L}^{1}+Z(\mathcal{L}))
, the sets
(\mathcal{L}^{1})^{\#}+Z(\mathcal{L})
and
\left\{\left<u+v\right>^{\#}+Z(\mathcal{L})\colon v\in\mathcal{L}^{1}\right\}
form a partition of
V_{\cal L}=\mathcal{L}\setminus Z(\mathcal{L})
, the vertex set of
\Gamma(\mathcal{L})
. This proves Equation (
13
).

In the case where
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{F}_{q}
is a finite field, we easily see that

\displaystyle\left|(\mathcal{L}^{1})^{\#}+Z(\mathcal{L})\right|=(q^{\dim\mathcal{L}^{1}}-1)q^{\dim Z(\mathcal{L})}=q^{n-1}-q^{\dim Z(\mathcal{L})},

\displaystyle\left|T_{\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{k}}\right|=(q-1)q^{\dim Z(\mathcal{L})},

\displaystyle\left|\left\{T_{\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{k}}\mid\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{k}\in\mathbb{F}\right\}\right|=q^{k}=q^{\dim\mathcal{L}^{1}}.

The above equations complete the proof.
∎

Corollary 6
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be an
n
-dimensional Lie algebra having
(n-1)
-dimensional commutative derived algebra. Then the commuting graph of
\mathcal{L}
is disconnected. Moreover, for every
e_{n}\in\mathcal{L}\setminus\mathcal{L}^{1}
, we have

\mathrm{CC}_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathcal{L})=\left\{K_{(\mathcal{L}^{1})^{\#}},K_{\left<z+e_{n}\right>^{\#}}\mid z\in\mathcal{L}^{1}.\right\}

(14)

In particular, if
\mathbb{F}
is finite, then
\Gamma(\mathcal{L})
has
(|\mathbb{F}|^{n-1}+1)
connected components: one complete graph
K_{|\mathbb{F}|^{n-1}-1}
, and
|\mathbb{F}|^{n-1}
complete graphs
K_{|\mathbb{F}|-1}
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{L}^{1}
is commutative and of dimension
n-1
, there is a basis
\{e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{n}\}
of
\cal L
such that
\mathcal{L}^{1}=\left<e_{1},\ldots,e_{n-1}\right>
and the restriction of
\mathrm{ad}_{e_{n}}
in
\mathcal{L}^{1}
is an isomorphism. Thus,
Z(\mathcal{L})=\{0\}
. The proof is then followed directly from Theorem
5
.

∎

We now consider the case of decomposable Lie algebras. The following result is simple but useful to calculate the connected components of the commuting graph of such a Lie algebra.

Lemma 7
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a noncommutative Lie algebra, and let
k
be any positive integer. Then the number of connected components of
\Gamma(\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathbb{F}^{k})
is the same as the number of connected components of
\Gamma(\mathcal{H})
. That is,

|\mathrm{CC}_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathcal{H})|=|\mathrm{CC}_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathbb{F}^{k})|

(15)

Moreover, each connected component of
\Gamma(\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathbb{F}^{k})
is the sum of a connected component of
\Gamma(\mathcal{H})
with
\mathbb{F}^{k}
. That is,

\mathrm{CC}_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathbb{F}^{k})=\left\{C+\mathbb{F}^{k}\mid C\in\mathrm{CC}_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathcal{H})\right\}.

(16)

Proof.

The proof is direct from the fact that
Z(\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathbb{F}^{k})=Z(\mathcal{H})\oplus\mathbb{F}^{k}
and that

\displaystyle[x+h_{1},y+h_{2}]=0\iff[x,y]=0

(17)

for every
x,\,y\in\mathcal{H}
, and
h_{1},\,h_{2}\in\mathbb{F}^{k}
.
∎

Lemma 10
.

Let
\mathcal{L}=\left<e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{n}\right>
be a Lie algebra over a field
\mathbb{F}
and let

be a vertex in
\Gamma(\cal L)
such that the following equivalence holds for every

\left[u,x_{1}e_{1}+\cdots+x_{n}e_{n}\right]=0\iff\exists\lambda\in\mathbb{F},v\in Z(\mathcal{L})\colon x_{1}e_{1}+x_{2}e_{2}+\cdots+x_{n}e_{n}=\lambda u+v.

(20)

Then the subgraph induced by

is a connected component of
\Gamma(\mathcal{L})
. Moreover, this connected component is a complete graph.

Proof.

Let
w
be a vertex in
\Gamma(\mathcal{L})
such that
w
is adjacent to a vertex in
\left<u\right>^{\#}+Z(\mathcal{L})
. Then there exist

and

such that

which follows that
[w,u]=0
. According to the equivalence (

), we observe that

. Since
w
is a vertex in
\Gamma(\mathcal{L})
, we get

.

It remains to prove that the subgraph induced by
\left<u\right>^{\#}+Z(\mathcal{L})
is a complete graph. Indeed, for every pair of vertices

and

\left<u\right>^{\#}+Z(\mathcal{L})
is a complete graph. This completes the proof.

Conclusion

In this paper, we have completely determined the commuting graphs of a class of 1-step solvable Lie algebras, and of all solvable Lie algebras of dimension up to 4 over any field. We also give formulas to calculate the number of all connected components in their commuting graphs. All solvable Lie algebras of dimensions up to 3 are disconnected and their connected components are given in Table
2
. All solvable Lie algebras of dimension 4 are disconnected, except
\mathcal{N}_{4,7}
, and their connected components are presented in Table
4
. It can be seen that the connected components are either complete graphs, windmill graphs, or a union of complete graphs.

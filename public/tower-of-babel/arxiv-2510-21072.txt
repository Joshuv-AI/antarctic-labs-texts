Matching conditions at null infinity in the presence of logarithms: the role of advanced and retarded radiation
Matías Briceño, Hernán A. González, Marc Henneaux, and Alfredo Pérez

Abstract
We provide a new perspective on the general matching conditions between the future of past null infinity and the past of future null infinity, emphasizing the impact of dominant logarithmic terms in the asymptotic expansion of the fields near null infinity. We explicitly consider the cases of a massless scalar field and of electromagnetism. Key in our derivation is the identification of the physical origin of these logarithms, which are associated with advanced and retarded radiation saturating the finite energy flux condition at null infinity (in a space of functions which is made precise). The matching conditions arise then from the requirement of Coulombic (i.e., 1 / r 1/r ) behaviour at spatial infinity.

\mathscr{I}^{-}
from the advanced field yields the conditions

\displaystyle\lim_{v\rightarrow\pm\infty}\frac{d^{l}}{dv^{l}}g_{lm}^{\text{adv}}\left(v\right)

\displaystyle=g_{lm}^{\pm\text{adv}}\log\left(\pm v\right)+G_{lm}^{\pm\text{adv}}+\dots\,,

(45)

\displaystyle\lim_{v\rightarrow\pm\infty}\frac{d^{l}}{dv^{l}}h_{lm}^{\text{adv}}\left(v\right)

\displaystyle=h_{lm}^{\pm\text{adv}}\log\left(\pm v\right)+H_{lm}^{\pm\text{adv}}+\dots\,,

(46)

from which one gets

\displaystyle\frac{d^{l-k}}{dv^{l-k}}g_{lm}^{\text{adv}}\left(v\right)

\displaystyle\underset{v\rightarrow+\infty}{=}g_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}\left(\frac{v^{k}}{k!}\log\left(v\right)-\frac{1}{k!}H_{k}v^{k}\right)+G_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}\frac{v^{k}}{k!}+\dots\,,

(47)

\displaystyle\frac{d^{l-k}}{dv^{l-k}}h_{lm}^{\text{adv}}\left(v\right)

\displaystyle\underset{v\rightarrow+\infty}{=}h_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}\left(\frac{v^{k}}{k!}\log\left(v\right)-\frac{1}{k!}H_{k}v^{k}\right)+H_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}\frac{v^{k}}{k!}+\dots\,.

(48)

3.3
Asymptotic behaviour near
i^{0}

Further conditions arise from the requirement that the field strength involves no logarithm at spatial infinity. These relate the advanced functions to the retarded functions and will be key in the derivation of the matching conditions below.

We shall work out the components
\tilde{F}_{\mu\nu}
of the electromagnetic tensor in
\left(t,r,x^{A}\right)
coordinates from the components in retarded coordinates. One has

\tilde{F}_{tr}=F_{ur}\,,\qquad\qquad\tilde{F}_{tA}=F_{uA}\,,\qquad\qquad\tilde{F}_{rA}=F_{rA}-F_{uA}\,,\qquad\qquad\tilde{F}_{AB}=F_{AB}\,.

Since our goal is to determine the conditions that enforce the absence of
\log r
terms at spatial infinity, we trace only the
\log u
and
\log v
terms in the field strength, since these are the only source of
\log r
terms.

Let us first focus on the component
\tilde{F}_{tr}=F_{ur}
. Using the relations
u=t-r
and
v=t+r
as well as the expressions for the retarded and advanced functions resulting from the finite radiated/incoming energy conditions, we find (keeping only the logs)

\tilde{F}_{tr}=\sum_{l,m}\sum_{k=0}^{l}\frac{k}{2^{k-1}k!r^{k+1}}\frac{\left(l+k\right)!}{\left(l-k\right)!}\left(-g_{lm}^{-\text{ret}}\frac{k\left(t-r\right)^{k-1}}{k!}\log\left(r-t\right)-\right.\\
\left.\left(-1\right)^{k}g_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}k\frac{\left(t+r\right)^{k-1}}{k!}\log\left(t+r\right)\right)Y_{lm}\\
+\sum_{l,m}\sum_{k=0}^{l}\frac{k\left(k+1\right)}{2^{k}k!r^{k+2}}\frac{\left(l+k\right)!}{\left(l-k\right)!}\left(\left(-1\right)^{k}g_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}\frac{\left(t+r\right)^{k}}{k!}\log\left(t+r\right)-g_{lm}^{-\text{ret}}\frac{\left(t-r\right)^{k}}{k!}\log\left(r-t\right)\right)Y_{lm}.

Therefore, in the limit when
r\rightarrow\infty
, with
t
held
fixed, we find

\left.\tilde{F}_{tr}\right|_{i^{0}}=\frac{\log r}{r^{2}}\sum_{l,m}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{l}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{k}k\left(k-1\right)}{2^{k}\left(k!\right)^{2}}\frac{\left(l+k\right)!}{\left(l-k\right)!}\right)\left(g_{lm}^{-\text{ret}}-g_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}\right)Y_{lm}+\cdots\,.

Expressing the sum in terms of the derivatives of the Legendre polynomials
P_{l}(x)
evaluated at zero (see (
95
)) we can write

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{l}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{k}k\left(k-1\right)}{2^{k}\left(k!\right)^{2}}\frac{\left(l+k\right)!}{\left(l-k\right)!}=P^{\prime\prime}_{l}(0).

(49)

Notice that the above relation vanishes for odd values of
l
, and is non-zero for even values
of
l
.
Let us now compute
\tilde{F}_{tA}=F_{uA}
.
Following the same rules as for
\tilde{F}_{tr}
, we find

\displaystyle\tilde{F}_{tA}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l,m}\sum_{k=0}^{l}\frac{1}{2^{k}k!r^{k}}\frac{\left(l+k\right)!}{\left(l-k\right)!}\left(g_{lm}^{-\text{ret}}\frac{k\left(t-r\right)^{k-1}}{k!}\log\left(r-t\right)\right.

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;+\left.\left(-1\right)^{k}g_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}k\frac{\left(t+r\right)^{k-1}}{k!}\log\left(t+r\right)\right)\partial_{A}Y_{lm}

\displaystyle+\sum_{l,m}\sum_{k=0}^{l}\frac{k}{2^{k}k!r^{k+1}}\frac{\left(l+k\right)!}{\left(l-k\right)!}\left(g_{lm}^{-\text{ret}}\frac{\left(t-r\right)^{k}}{k!}\log\left(r-t\right)\right.

\displaystyle\left.\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;-\left(-1\right)^{k}g_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}\frac{\left(t+r\right)^{k}}{k!}\log\left(t+r\right)\right)\partial_{A}Y_{lm}

\displaystyle+\sum_{l,m}\sum_{k=0}^{l}\frac{1}{2^{k}k!r^{k}}\frac{\left(l+k\right)!}{\left(l-k\right)!}\left(h_{lm}^{-\text{ret}}\frac{\left(t-r\right)^{k-1}}{k!}\log\left(r-t\right)\right.

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\left.+\left(-1\right)^{k}h_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}k\frac{\left(t+r\right)^{k-1}}{k!}\log\left(t+r\right)\right)\sqrt{\gamma}\epsilon_{AB}\partial^{B}Y_{lm}.

Therefore, taking the limit when
r\rightarrow\infty
, keeping
t

fixed, one finds

\displaystyle\tilde{F}_{tA}

\displaystyle=\frac{\log r}{r}\left[\sum_{l,m}\sum_{k=0}^{l}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{k}k}{2^{k}\left(k!\right)^{2}}\frac{\left(l+k\right)!}{\left(l-k\right)!}\left(h_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}-h_{lm}^{-\text{ret}}\right)\sqrt{\gamma}\epsilon_{AB}\partial^{B}Y_{lm}\right]+\dots\,.

Expressing the sum in terms of the derivatives of the Legendre polynomials evaluated at zero (see equation (
94
) for a derivation),

\sum_{k=0}^{l}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{k}k}{2^{k}\left(k!\right)^{2}}\frac{\left(l+k\right)!}{\left(l-k\right)!}=-2P^{\prime}_{l}(0)\,,

(50)

which now vanishes for even values of
l
, we obtain

\left.\tilde{F}_{tA}\right|_{i^{0}}=\frac{\log r}{r}\left[-2\sum_{l,m}P^{\prime}_{l}(0)\left(h_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}-h_{lm}^{-\text{ret}}\right)\sqrt{\gamma}\epsilon_{AB}\partial^{B}Y_{lm}\right]+\dots\,.

Similar considerations yield
\tilde{F}_{rA}=F_{rA}-F_{uA}
,

\displaystyle\tilde{F}_{rA}=\sum_{l,m}\Bigg[\sum_{k=0}^{l}\frac{1}{2^{k}k!\,r^{k}}\frac{(l+k)!}{(l-k)!}\Bigg(-g_{lm}^{-\text{ret}}\frac{k\,(t-r)^{k-1}}{k!}\log(r-t)

\displaystyle\hskip-113.81102pt+(-1)^{k}g_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}\frac{k\,(t+r)^{k-1}}{k!}\log(t+r)\Bigg)\Bigg]\partial_{A}Y_{lm}

\displaystyle+\sum_{l,m}\sum_{k=0}^{l}\frac{1}{2^{k}k!\,r^{k}}\frac{(l+k)!}{(l-k)!}\Bigg(-h_{lm}^{-\text{ret}}\frac{k\,(t-r)^{k-1}}{k!}\log(r-t)

\displaystyle\hskip-113.81102pt+(-1)^{k}h_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}\frac{k\,(t+r)^{k-1}}{k!}\log(t+r)\Bigg)\sqrt{\gamma}\,\epsilon_{AB}\,\partial^{B}Y_{lm}

\displaystyle-\sum_{l,m}\Bigg[\sum_{k=0}^{l}\frac{k}{2^{k}k!\,r^{k+1}}\frac{(l+k)!}{(l-k)!}\Bigg(h_{lm}^{-\text{ret}}\frac{(t-r)^{k}}{k!}\log(r-t)

\displaystyle\hskip-113.81102pt+(-1)^{k}h_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}\frac{(t+r)^{k}}{k!}\log(t+r)\Bigg)\Bigg]\sqrt{\gamma}\,\epsilon_{AB}\,\partial^{B}Y_{lm}\,.

After using (
50
), and taking the limit
r\rightarrow\infty
with
t
kept constant, reduces to

\displaystyle\left.\tilde{F}_{rA}\right|_{i^{0}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\log r}{r}\sum_{l,m}\left[-2P_{l}^{\prime}(0)\left(g_{lm}^{-\text{ret}}+g_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}\right)\right]\partial_{A}Y_{lm}+\dots\,.

Finally, let us turn to
\tilde{F}_{AB}=F_{AB}
.
We find this time by performing similar manipulations

F_{AB}=\sum_{l,m}\sum_{k=0}^{l}\frac{\left(l+k\right)!}{\left(l-k\right)!}\left(h_{lm}^{-\text{ret}}\frac{\left(t-r\right)^{k}}{k!^{2}\left(2r\right)^{k}}\log\left(r-t\right)+h_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{k}\left(t+r\right)^{k}}{k!^{2}\left(2r\right)^{k}}\log\left(t+r\right)\right)\\
\times\left(\epsilon_{BC}\partial_{A}\partial^{C}Y_{lm}-\epsilon_{AC}\partial_{B}\partial^{C}Y_{lm}\right).

Therefore, in the limit when
r\rightarrow\infty
keeping
t
constant,
we obtain

F_{AB}=\log r\sum_{l,m}\left[P_{l}(0)\left(h_{lm}^{-\text{ret}}+h_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}\right)\right]\left(\epsilon_{BC}\partial_{A}\partial^{C}Y_{lm}-\epsilon_{AC}\partial_{B}\partial^{C}Y_{lm}\right),

where we used the relation (
93
)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{l}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{(k!)^{2}}\frac{(l+k)!}{(l-k)!}=P_{l}(0).

(51)

In order to eliminate the leading
\log r
terms, we must impose the following requirements

P_{l}^{\prime}(0)\left(h_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}-h_{lm}^{-\text{ret}}\right)=0,\quad P_{l}(0)\left(h_{lm}^{-\text{ret}}+h_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}\right)=0,

(52)

and

P^{\prime}_{l}(0)\left(g_{lm}^{-\text{ret}}+g_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}\right)=0,\quad P^{\prime\prime}_{l}(0)\left(g_{lm}^{-\text{ret}}-g_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}\right)=0.

(53)

These have the same form as in the scalar case discussed in (
72
) and (
73
). They lead to the following conditions,

g_{lm}^{-\text{ret}}=\left(-1\right)^{l}g_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}\,,\qquad\qquad h_{lm}^{-\text{ret}}=-\left(-1\right)^{l}h_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}\,.

(54)

These relations between the advanced and retarded fields are essential for establishing the matching conditions connecting past and future null infinity for the logarithmic terms.

Note that, as in the scalar field case, the contributions to the leading logarithmic term at
i^{0}
arise from all powers of
r
appearing in the electromagnetic tensor expressed in retarded or advanced coordinates. Accordingly, it becomes necessary to extend the near-
\mathcal{I^{\pm}}
expansion into the bulk in order to derive the conditions (
54
) and determine the leading orders in the vicinity of
i^{0}
.

3.4
Matching conditions

The matching conditions of
He:2014cra
;
Kapec:2015ena
;
Strominger:2017zoo
relate the leading order of the radial electric and magnetic fields at the past of
\mathscr{I}^{+}
to the leading order of the corresponding fields at the future of
\mathscr{I}^{-}
. They were derived assuming no logarithmic terms at null infinity. To derive them in the general case, let us thus examine the behaviour of the fields near null infinity, starting with the electromagnetic potential.

3.4.1
Expansion of the electromagnetic potential near
\mathscr{I}^{+}

The advanced solution is by definition the
v
-dependent part of the electromagnetic potential, i.e.,

\displaystyle A_{u}^{\text{adv}}=

\displaystyle\sum_{l,m}\sum_{k=0}^{l}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{k}k}{2^{k}k!r^{k+1}}\frac{\left(l+k\right)!}{\left(l-k\right)!}\frac{d^{l-k}}{dv^{l-k}}g_{lm}^{\text{adv}}\left(v\right)Y_{lm}\,,

\displaystyle A_{A}^{\text{adv}}=

\displaystyle\sum_{l,m}\sum_{k=0}^{l}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{k}}{2^{k}k!r^{k}}\frac{\left(l+k\right)!}{\left(l-k\right)!}\left(\frac{d^{l-k}}{dv^{l-k}}g_{lm}^{\text{adv}}\left(v\right)\right)\partial_{A}Y_{lm}

\displaystyle+\sum_{l,l}\sum_{k=0}^{l}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{k}}{2^{k}k!r^{k}}\frac{\left(l+k\right)!}{\left(l-k\right)!}\left(\frac{d^{l-k}}{dv^{l-k}}h_{lm}^{\text{adv}}\left(v\right)\right)\sqrt{\gamma}\epsilon_{AB}\partial^{B}Y_{lm}\,.

When expanded near future null infinity, it brings
\log r
terms, as in the scalar case, which we now determine.

Using the expressions (
45
)-(
48
) for
g_{lm}^{\text{adv}}
and
h_{lm}^{\text{adv}}
, the advanced solution becomes

\displaystyle A_{u}^{\text{adv}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l,m}\sum_{k=0}^{l}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{k}k}{2^{k}k!^{2}r^{k+1}}\frac{\left(l+k\right)!}{\left(l-k\right)!}\left[g_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}\left(v^{k}\log\left(v\right)-H_{k}v^{k}\right)+G_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}v^{k}+\dots\right]Y_{lm}\,,

\displaystyle A_{A}^{\text{adv}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l,m}\sum_{k=0}^{l}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{k}}{2^{k}k!^{2}r^{k}}\frac{\left(l+k\right)!}{\left(l-k\right)!}\left[g_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}\left(v^{k}\log\left(v\right)-H_{k}v^{k}\right)+G_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}v^{k}+\dots\right]\partial_{A}Y_{lm}

\displaystyle+\sum_{l,m}\sum_{k=0}^{l}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{k}}{2^{k}k!^{2}r^{k}}\frac{\left(l+k\right)!}{\left(l-k\right)!}\left[h_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}\left(v^{k}\log\left(v\right)-H_{k}v^{k}\right)+H_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}v^{k}+\dots\right]\sqrt{\gamma}\epsilon_{AB}\partial^{B}Y_{lm}.

Substituting
v=u+2r
, and expanding for large values of
r
,
i.e., near future null infinity, we find

\displaystyle A_{u}^{\text{adv}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\log r}{r}\left[\sum_{l,m}g_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{l}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{k}k}{\left(k!\right)^{2}}\frac{\left(l+k\right)!}{\left(l-k\right)!}\right)Y_{lm}\right]+

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\frac{1}{r}\sum_{l,m}\sum_{k=0}^{l}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{k}k}{\left(k!\right)^{2}}\frac{\left(l+k\right)!}{\left(l-k\right)!}\left[g_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}\log 2-g_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}H_{k}+G_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}\right]Y_{lm}+\dots\,,

\displaystyle A_{A}^{\text{adv}}

\displaystyle=\log r\,\sum_{l,m}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{l}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{k}}{\left(k!\right)^{2}}\frac{\left(l+k\right)!}{\left(l-k\right)!}\right)\left[g_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}\partial_{A}Y_{lm}+h_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}\sqrt{\gamma}\epsilon_{AB}\partial^{B}Y_{lm}\right]

\displaystyle+\sum_{l,m}\sum_{k=0}^{l}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{k}}{\left(k!\right)^{2}}\frac{\left(l+k\right)!}{\left(l-k\right)!}\left[g_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}\log 2-g_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}H_{k}+G_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}\right]\partial_{A}Y_{lm}

\displaystyle+\sum_{l,m}\sum_{k=0}^{l}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{k}}{\left(k!\right)^{2}}\frac{\left(l+k\right)!}{\left(l-k\right)!}\left[h_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}\log 2-h_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}H_{k}+H_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}\right]\sqrt{\gamma}\epsilon_{AB}\partial^{B}Y_{lm}

\displaystyle+\frac{\log r}{r}\left[\sum_{l,m}\sum_{k=0}^{l}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{k}k}{2\left(k!\right)^{2}}\frac{\left(l+k\right)!}{\left(l-k\right)!}u\left(g_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}\partial_{A}Y_{lm}+h_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}\sqrt{\gamma}\epsilon_{AB}\partial^{B}Y_{lm}\right)\right]+\dots\,.

The above expressions can be simplified using the identities (
96
) and (
97
)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{l}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{k}}{\left(k!\right)^{2}}\frac{\left(l+k\right)!}{\left(l-k\right)!}=\left(-1\right)^{l}\,,\qquad\sum_{k=0}^{l}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{k}k}{\left(k!\right)^{2}}\frac{\left(l+k\right)!}{\left(l-k\right)!}=\left(-1\right)^{l}l\left(l+1\right)\,,

(55)

which imply

\displaystyle\left.A_{u}^{\text{adv}}\right|_{\mathscr{I}^{+}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\log r}{r}\left[\sum_{l,m}\left(-1\right)^{l}l\left(l+1\right)g_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}Y_{lm}\right]+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r}\sum_{l,m}\left[\left(-1\right)^{l}l\left(l+1\right)\left(g_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}\log 2+G_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}\right)-\sum_{k=0}^{l}g_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{k}k}{\left(k!\right)^{2}}\frac{\left(l+k\right)!}{\left(l-k\right)!}H_{k}\right]Y_{lm}\,,

\displaystyle\left.A_{A}^{\text{adv}}\right|_{\mathscr{I}^{+}}

\displaystyle=\log r\sum_{l,m}\left[\left(-1\right)^{l}g_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}\partial_{A}Y_{lm}+\left(-1\right)^{l}h_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}\sqrt{\gamma}\epsilon_{AB}\partial^{B}Y_{lm}\right]

\displaystyle+\sum_{l,m}\left[\left(-1\right)^{l}\left(g_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}\log 2+G_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}\right)-g_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}\sum_{k=0}^{l}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{k}}{\left(k!\right)^{2}}\frac{\left(l+k\right)!}{\left(l-k\right)!}H_{k}\right]\partial_{A}Y_{lm}+

\displaystyle+\sum_{l,m}\left[\left(-1\right)^{l}\left(h_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}\log 2+H_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}\right)-h_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}\sum_{k=0}^{l}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{k}}{\left(k!\right)^{2}}\frac{\left(l+k\right)!}{\left(l-k\right)!}H_{k}\right]\sqrt{\gamma}\epsilon_{AB}\partial^{B}Y_{lm}

\displaystyle+\frac{\log r}{r}\left[\sum_{l,m}\frac{u}{2}\left(-1\right)^{l}l\left(l+1\right)\left(g_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}\partial_{A}Y_{lm}+h_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}\sqrt{\gamma}\epsilon_{AB}\partial^{B}Y_{lm}\right)\right]+\dots\,.

Therefore, the full solution (retarded + advanced) near
\mathscr{I}^{+}
is given by

\displaystyle A_{u}

\displaystyle=\frac{\log r}{r}\left[\sum_{l,m}\left(-1\right)^{l}l\left(l+1\right)g_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}Y_{lm}\right]+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r}\sum_{l,m}\left[\left(-1\right)^{l}l\left(l+1\right)\left(g_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}\log 2+G_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}\right)-g_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}\sum_{k=0}^{l}\left(\frac{\left(-1\right)^{k}k}{\left(k!\right)^{2}}\frac{\left(l+k\right)!}{\left(l-k\right)!}H_{k}\right)\right]

(56)

\displaystyle\left.-\sum_{l,m}\sum_{k=1}^{l}l\left(l+1\right)\left(\frac{d^{l}}{du^{l}}g_{lm}^{\text{ret}}\left(u\right)\right)+Q\right]Y_{lm}+\dots\,,

and

\displaystyle A_{A}

\displaystyle=\log r\Bigg[\sum_{l,m}(-1)^{l}g_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}\,\partial_{A}Y_{lm}+\sum_{l,m}(-1)^{l}h_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}\,\sqrt{\gamma}\,\epsilon_{AB}\,\partial^{B}Y_{lm}\Bigg]

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{l,m}\Bigg[\frac{d^{l}}{du^{l}}\,g_{lm}^{\text{ret}}(u)+(-1)^{l}\!\left(g_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}\log 2+G_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}\right)

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,-g_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}\!\sum_{k=0}^{l}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{(k!)^{2}}\frac{(l+k)!}{(l-k)!}\,H_{k}\Bigg]\partial_{A}Y_{lm}

(57)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{l,m}\Bigg[\frac{d^{l}}{du^{l}}\,h_{lm}^{\text{ret}}(u)+(-1)^{l}\!\left(h_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}\log 2+H_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}\right)

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,-h_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}\!\sum_{k=0}^{l}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{(k!)^{2}}\frac{(l+k)!}{(l-k)!}\,H_{k}\Bigg]\sqrt{\gamma}\,\epsilon_{AB}\,\partial^{B}Y_{lm}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\log r}{r}\Bigg[\sum_{l,m}\frac{u}{2}(-1)^{l}l(l+1)\big(g_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}\,\partial_{A}Y_{lm}+h_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}\,\sqrt{\gamma}\,\epsilon_{AB}\,\partial^{B}Y_{lm}\big)\Bigg]+\dots\,.

3.4.2
Electromagnetic tensor near
\mathscr{I}^{+}

Let us compute the electromagnetic tensor near
\mathscr{I}^{+}
, focusing on the radial electric field
F_{ur}
and the radial magnetic field
F_{AB}
occurring in the matching conditions of
He:2014cra
;
Kapec:2015ena
;
Strominger:2017zoo
. These components are scalars under the changes of coordinates
(u,r)\leftrightarrow(t,r)\leftrightarrow(v,r)
. We can also derive the matching conditions for the other components since we have all the expressions, but because there is nothing we can compare them with in the literature, we shall not do so here.

From the asymptotic developments of the vector potential in Eqs. (
56
) and (
57
), one gets

\displaystyle F_{ur}

\displaystyle=\frac{\log r}{r^{2}}\left[\sum_{l,m}\left(-1\right)^{l}l\left(l+1\right)g_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}Y_{lm}\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\sum_{l,m}\left[\left(-1\right)^{l}l\left(l+1\right)(g_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}\left(\log 2-1\right)+G_{lm}^{+\text{adv}})-\sum_{k=0}^{l}g_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{k}H_{k}k\left(l+k\right)!}{\left(k!\right)^{2}\left(l-k\right)!}\right.

\displaystyle\left.-\sum_{k=1}^{l}l\left(l+1\right)\left(\frac{d^{l}}{du^{l}}g_{lm}^{\text{ret}}\left(u\right)\right)\right]Y_{lm}+\frac{Q}{r^{2}}+\dots\,,

(58)

and

\displaystyle F_{AB}

\displaystyle=\log r\sum_{l,m}\left(-1\right)^{l}h_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}\sqrt{\gamma}\left(\epsilon_{BC}\partial_{A}\partial^{C}Y_{lm}-\epsilon_{AC}\partial_{B}\partial^{C}Y_{lm}\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{l,m}\left[\frac{d^{l}}{du^{l}}h_{lm}^{\text{ret}}\left(u\right)+\left(-1\right)^{l}\left(h_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}\log 2+H_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}\right)-h_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{l}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{k}}{\left(k!\right)^{2}}\frac{\left(l+k\right)!}{\left(l-k\right)!}H_{k}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\times\sqrt{\gamma}\left(\epsilon_{BC}\partial_{A}\partial^{C}Y_{lm}-\epsilon_{AC}\partial_{B}\partial^{C}Y_{lm}\right)+\dots\,.

In both components of the field strength, there is a leading radial logarithmic contribution arising from the advanced wave. In particular, the leading term of
F_{ur}
is entirely determined by the “electric” mode
g_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}
of the advanced wave, while the leading term of
F_{AB}
depends only on the “magnetic” advanced mode
h_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}
of the electromagnetic wave.

3.4.3
Electromagnetic tensor near
\mathscr{I}^{-}

Similar developments, in which one inserts
u=v-2r
into the retarded solution, yield the asymptotic expansion of the field strength near
\mathscr{I}^{-}
,

\displaystyle F_{vr}

\displaystyle=\frac{\log r}{r^{2}}\sum_{l,m}\left(-1\right)^{l+1}l\left(l+1\right)g_{lm}^{-\text{ret}}Y_{lm}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{r^{2}}\sum_{l,m}\left[-l\left(l+1\right)\frac{d^{l}}{dv^{l}}g_{lm}^{\text{adv}}\left(v\right)Y_{lm}+\left(-1\right)^{l}l\left(l+1\right)\left(g_{lm}^{-\text{ret}}\left(\log 2-1\right)+G_{lm}^{-\text{ret}}\right)\right.

(59)

\displaystyle\left.-\left(\sum_{k=0}^{l}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{k}k}{\left(k!\right)^{2}}\frac{\left(l+k\right)!}{\left(l-k\right)!}H_{k}\right)g_{lm}^{-\text{ret}}Y_{lm}\right]+\dots\,,

and

\displaystyle F_{AB}

\displaystyle=\log r\sum_{l,m}\left(-1\right)^{l}h_{lm}^{-\text{ret}}\sqrt{\gamma}\left(\epsilon_{BC}\partial_{A}\partial^{C}Y_{lm}-\epsilon_{AC}\partial_{B}\partial^{C}Y_{lm}\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{l,m}\left[\frac{d^{l}}{dv^{l}}h_{lm}^{\text{adv}}\left(v\right)+\left(-1\right)^{l}\left(h_{lm}^{-\text{ret}}\log 2+H_{lm}^{-\text{ret}}\right)-\left(\sum_{k=0}^{l}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{k}}{\left(k!\right)^{2}}\frac{\left(l+k\right)!}{\left(l-k\right)!}H_{k}\right)h_{lm}^{-\text{ret}}\right]

\displaystyle\times\sqrt{\gamma}\left(\epsilon_{BC}\partial_{A}\partial^{C}Y_{lm}-\epsilon_{AC}\partial_{B}\partial^{C}Y_{lm}\right)+\dots\,.

3.4.4
Matching conditions

The matching conditions on the field strength follow immediately from the conditions relating the advanced and retarded fields obtained at
i^{0}
in Eq. (
54
).
Indeed, from Eqs. (
58
) and (
59
), the leading
logarithmic terms of
F_{ur}
and
F_{vr}
are given

\left.F_{ur}^{\text{log}}\left(\hat{x}\right)\right|_{\mathscr{I}_{-}^{+}}=\sum_{l,m}\left(-1\right)^{l}l\left(l+1\right)g_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}Y_{lm}\left(\hat{x}\right),

\left.F_{vr}^{\text{log}}\left(\hat{x}\right)\right|_{\mathscr{I}_{+}^{-}}=\sum_{l,m}\left(-1\right)^{l+1}l\left(l+1\right)g_{lm}^{-\text{ret}}Y_{lm}\left(\hat{x}\right).

Therefore, using (
54
), we find the following matching
condition:

\left.F_{vr}^{\text{log}}\left(-\hat{x}\right)\right|_{\mathscr{I}_{+}^{-}}=-\left.F_{ur}^{\text{log}}\left(\hat{x}\right)\right|_{\mathscr{I}_{-}^{+}}.

Similarly,

\displaystyle\left.F_{AB}^{\text{log}}\left(\hat{x}\right)\right|_{\mathscr{I}_{-}^{+}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l,m}\left(-1\right)^{l}h_{lm}^{+\text{adv}}\sqrt{\gamma}\left(\epsilon_{BC}\partial_{A}\partial^{C}Y_{lm}-\epsilon_{AC}\partial_{B}\partial^{C}Y_{lm}\right)\,,

and

\displaystyle\left.F_{AB}^{\text{log}}\left(\hat{x}\right)\right|_{\mathscr{I}_{+}^{-}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l,m}\left(-1\right)^{l}h_{lm}^{-\text{ret}}\sqrt{\gamma}\left(\epsilon_{BC}\partial_{A}\partial^{C}Y_{lm}-\epsilon_{AC}\partial_{B}\partial^{C}Y_{lm}\right)\,.

Thus,

\left.F_{AB}^{\text{log}}\left(-\hat{x}\right)\right|_{\mathscr{I}_{+}^{-}}=\left.F_{AB}^{\text{log}}\left(\hat{x}\right)\right|_{\mathscr{I}_{+}^{-}}\,.

In this case, we must take into account that the magnetic sector has
an extra minus sign in the parity coming from the Levi-Civita symbol,
i.e.,

\sqrt{\gamma}\epsilon_{AB}\partial^{B}Y_{lm}\left(-\hat{x}\right)=\left(-1\right)^{l+1}\sqrt{\gamma}\epsilon_{AB}\partial^{B}Y_{lm}\left(\hat{x}\right)

As shown in
Fuentealba:2025ekj
, the matching conditions for the coefficients of the leading (logarithmic) terms have opposite sign compared with those of
He:2014cra
;
Kapec:2015ena
;
Strominger:2017zoo
. If one assumes that the leading logarithmic terms are absent, the matching conditions for the coefficients of the next terms, which become leading, obey the matching conditions with the sign of
He:2014cra
;
Kapec:2015ena
;
Strominger:2017zoo
characteristic of the
P
-branch. The derivation proceeds as in the scalar case.

4
Conclusions

In this work, we have studied the role of advanced and retarded radiation
in determining the asymptotic structure of massless scalar and electromagnetic
fields that exhibit radial logarithmic terms near
\mathscr{I}^{\pm}
.
Assuming that the total incoming energy flux across
\mathscr{I}^{-}

is finite, we have shown that the final advanced wave, characterized
by an asymptotic
\log v
dependence at late times, leaves a distinctive
imprint at
\mathscr{I}^{+}
in the form of
\log r
terms. Conversely,
the first retarded wave completely determines the radial logarithmic
behavior in the vicinity of
\mathscr{I}^{-}
. This analysis offers
both a novel perspective and a coherent physical interpretation of
the origin of the radial logarithmic terms. Furthermore, the results
obtained through this approach coincide with those in Refs.
Fuentealba:2024lll
;
Fuentealba:2025ekj
,
where a spatial-infinity-based route was used.

Remarkably, this advanced/retarded wave approach enables the determination of the matching conditions for the radial logarithmic terms between the future of past null infinity and the past of future null infinity. This is achieved by evaluating the fields in the vicinity of
i^{0}
and imposing that their asymptotic behaviour be of the Coulombic type, free of
\log r
contributions at spatial infinity. The matching conditions derived using this approach are in exact agreement with those in Refs.
Fuentealba:2024lll
;
Fuentealba:2025ekj
.

As we just stressed, the assumption that there is no logarithmic term at spatial infinity plays a key role in our derivation of the matching conditions. This assumption is motivated by the asymptotic behaviour of the elementary solution of the Poissson equation (and of superpositions of such solutions). However, this assumption can be relaxed while preserving finiteness of Poincaré charges and of the energy fluxes through past and future infinity. For instance
\log r/r
terms with coefficients having definite parity at spatial infinity could be contemplated. It is beyond the scope of this paper to investigate the most liberal asymptotic behaviour at spatial infinity that is consistent with the above physical requirements, since the assumed
1/r
behaviour is sufficient for our purposes.

It is natural to ask to what extent these results can be extended to the gravitational case. For linearized gravity, one may naturally expect a behaviour closely analogous to that of the scalar and electromagnetic fields discussed in this work. However, in the fully nonlinear theory, the situation is considerably more subtle. This is because, as observed for the scalar and electromagnetic fields discussed above, all subleading terms in the radial expansion near
\mathscr{I}^{-}
contribute to the leading
\log r
term at
\mathscr{I}^{+}
. In General Relativity, it is extremely challenging to control all subleading terms in the radial expansion due to the inherent nonlinearities of the theory. However, since Bondi’s energy-loss formula
Bondi:1960jsa
has a similar form to that of the scalar and electromagnetic cases, the requirement of finite total energy implies that the shear tensor at late advanced times must take the form
C_{AB}\underset{v\rightarrow\infty}{=}C_{AB}^{+}\log v+\dots
. Consequently, when it is reexpressed in retarded coordinates, it will generically give rise to
\log r
contributions in the asymptotic expansion near
\mathscr{I}^{+}
. How to connect these logarithmic terms to the polyhomogeneous solutions involving dominant logarithmic terms presented for example, in Refs.
Winic:1985
;
Chrusciel:1993hx
;
Andersson:1994ng
remains an interesting question for future investigation.

Appendix A
General analysis of the massless scalar field

A.1
Retarded and advanced waves

In this section, we extend the scalar field analysis of spherical and dipole waves
to the general solution of the wave equation. As for the dipole case, subleading terms in the asymptotic radial expansion must be taken into account, as they play a fundamental role.

The general solution of the wave equation in a source-free region
is expressed again as the sum of retarded and advanced terms.

\phi=\phi_{R}+\phi_{A},

with

\displaystyle\phi_{R}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l,m}\sum_{k=0}^{l}\frac{1}{2^{k}k!r^{k+1}}\frac{\left(l+k\right)!}{\left(l-k\right)!}\frac{d^{l-k}}{du^{l-k}}a_{l,m}\left(u\right)Y_{lm}\left(\theta,\phi\right),

(60)

\displaystyle\phi_{A}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l,m}\sum_{k=0}^{l}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{k}}{2^{k}k!r^{k+1}}\frac{\left(l+k\right)!}{\left(l-k\right)!}\frac{d^{l-k}}{dv^{l-k}}b_{l,m}\left(v\right)Y_{lm}\left(\theta,\phi\right).

(61)

Here,
a_{l,m}\left(u\right)
and
b_{l,m}\left(v\right)

are arbitrary functions of the retarded and advanced times, respectively.
It is noteworthy that the solution above is expressed purely
as a power-law expansion in the radial coordinate, with no logarithmic
terms.

In particular, the leading-order term at large
r
takes the form:

\phi_{R}=\frac{\bar{\phi}_{R}\left(u,\hat{x}\right)}{r}+O\left(r^{-2}\right)\,,\quad\phi_{A}=\frac{\bar{\phi}_{A}\left(v,\hat{x}\right)}{r}+O\left(r^{-2}\right)\,,

where

\displaystyle\bar{\phi}_{R}\left(u,\hat{x}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l,m}\frac{d^{l}}{du^{l}}a_{l,m}\left(u\right)Y_{lm},

\displaystyle\bar{\phi}_{A}\left(v,\hat{x}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l,m}\frac{d^{l}}{dv^{l}}b_{l,m}\left(v\right)Y_{lm}.

The total energy flux across
\mathscr{I}^{\pm}
takes exactly the
same form as in Eqs. (
10
) and (
12
).
Consequently, for the
l
-th spherical harmonic, the requirement
of finite energy flux across
\mathscr{I}^{\pm}
implies that

\displaystyle\lim_{u\rightarrow\pm\infty}\frac{d^{l}}{du^{l}}a_{l,m}\left(u\right)

\displaystyle=\phi_{R\left(l,m\right)}^{\pm}\log\left(\pm u\right)+\varphi_{R\left(l,m\right)}^{\pm}+\dots\,,

\displaystyle\lim_{v\rightarrow\pm\infty}\frac{d^{l}}{dv^{l}}b_{l,m}\left(v\right)

\displaystyle=\phi_{A\left(l,m\right)}^{\pm}\log\left(\pm v\right)+\varphi_{A\left(l,m\right)}^{\pm}+\dots\,,

assuming again integer power law behaviour for
\partial_{u}\bar{\phi}_{R}
(
\partial_{v}\bar{\phi}_{A}
).

Let us examine the contribution of the advanced wave to the asymptotic
behavior of the field near
\mathscr{I}^{+}
. This requires integrating
the following equation

\frac{d^{l}}{dv^{l}}b_{l,m}\left(v\right)\underset{v\rightarrow+\infty}{=}\phi_{A\left(l,m\right)}^{+}\log\left(v\right)+\varphi_{A\left(l,m\right)}^{+}+\dots\,.

To carry out the integration, we will make use of the following integral
identity

\alpha\underbrace{\int\cdots\int}_{k}\log x\,dx+\beta\underbrace{\int\cdots\int}_{k}\,dx=\alpha\left(\frac{x^{k}}{k!}\log\left(x\right)-\frac{1}{k!}H_{k}x^{k}\right)+\beta\frac{x^{k}}{k!}+\sum_{n=0}^{k-1}c_{k-n}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}\,.

(62)

Here,
c_{k-n}
denotes the integration constants, and
H_{k}=\sum_{n=1}^{k}\frac{1}{n}

represents the harmonic numbers.
This identity can be readily proven by induction, starting from its differential form,

\frac{d^{k}}{dx^{k}}\left[\alpha\left(\frac{x^{k}}{k!}\log\left(x\right)-\frac{1}{k!}H_{k}x^{k}\right)+\beta\frac{x^{k}}{k!}+\sum_{n=0}^{k-1}c_{k-n}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}\right]=\alpha\log\left(x\right)+\beta\,,

and using the recursive property of the harmonic numbers,
H_{k+1}=H_{k}+\frac{1}{k+1}
.

Thus,

\displaystyle\frac{d^{l-k}}{dv^{l-k}}b_{l,m}\left(v\right)\underset{v\rightarrow\infty}{=}

\displaystyle\phi_{A\left(l,m\right)}^{+}\left(\frac{v^{k}}{k!}\log\left(v\right)-\frac{1}{k!}H_{k}v^{k}\right)+\varphi_{A\left(l,m\right)}\frac{v^{k}}{k!}+\sum_{n=0}^{k-1}c_{A\left(k-n\right)}^{\left(l,m\right)}\frac{v^{n}}{n!}\,.

(63)

The term with
k=0
in the above expression can be properly defined
by setting
H_{0}=0
. Substituting Eq. (
63
) into Eq. (
61
)
one finds that the advanced solution at large
v
can then be written
as

\phi_{A}\underset{v\rightarrow\infty}{=}\sum_{l,m}\sum_{k=0}^{l}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{k}}{2^{k}k!r^{k+1}}\frac{\left(l+k\right)!}{\left(l-k\right)!}\left[\phi_{A\left(l,m\right)}^{+}\left(\frac{v^{k}}{k!}\log\left(v\right)-\frac{1}{k!}H_{k}v^{k}\right)+\varphi_{A\left(l,m\right)}\frac{v^{k}}{k!}+\dots\right]Y_{lm}.

(64)

We omit the terms containing the integration constants
c_{A(k-n)}^{(l,m)}
, as they contribute only subleading corrections that do not affect the present analysis.

The key step is to express the advanced coordinate in terms of the
retarded one using
v=u+2r
. This allows one to write

\phi_{A}=\sum_{l,m}\sum_{k=0}^{l}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{k}}{2^{k}k!r^{k+1}}\frac{\left(l+k\right)!}{\left(l-k\right)!}\left[\phi_{A\left(l,m\right)}^{+}\left(\frac{\left(u+2r\right)^{k}}{k!}\log\left(u+2r\right)-\frac{1}{k!}H_{k}\left(u+2r\right)^{k}\right)\right.\\
\left.+\varphi_{A\left(l,m\right)}\frac{\left(u+2r\right)^{k}}{k!}+\dots\right]Y_{lm}.

In the limit
r\rightarrow\infty
with
u
held constant, the leading
terms are given by

\phi_{A}=\left[\sum_{l,m}\phi_{A\left(l,m\right)}^{+}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{l}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{k}}{\left(k!\right)^{2}}\frac{\left(l+k\right)!}{\left(l-k\right)!}\right)Y_{lm}\right]\frac{\log r}{r}\\
+\left[\sum_{l,m}\sum_{k=0}^{l}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{k}}{\left(k!\right)^{2}}\frac{\left(l+k\right)!}{\left(l-k\right)!}\left[\phi_{A\left(l,m\right)}^{+}\left(\log 2-H_{k}\right)+\varphi_{A\left(l,m\right)}\right]Y_{lm}\right]\frac{1}{r}+\dots\,.

The above expression can be simplified using the identity (
96
)

\sum_{k=0}^{l}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{k}}{\left(k!\right)^{2}}\frac{\left(l+k\right)!}{\left(l-k\right)!}=\left(-1\right)^{l}\,.

Consequently, if one considers the sum of the advanced and retarded
solutions, the full solution near
\mathscr{I}^{+}
becomes:

\displaystyle\left.\phi\right|_{\mathscr{I}^{+}}

\displaystyle=\left[\sum_{l,m}\left(-1\right)^{l}\phi_{A\left(l,m\right)}^{+}Y_{lm}\right]\frac{\log r}{r}

\displaystyle+\left[\sum_{l,m}\left(\frac{d^{l}}{du^{l}}a_{l,m}\left(u\right)+\left(-1\right)^{l}\left(\phi_{A\left(l,m\right)}^{+}\log 2+\varphi_{A\left(l,m\right)}\right)\right.\right.

(65)

\displaystyle-\left.\left.\left(\sum_{k=0}^{l}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{k}}{\left(k!\right)^{2}}\frac{\left(l+k\right)!}{\left(l-k\right)!}H_{k}\right)\phi_{A\left(l,m\right)}^{+}\right)Y_{lm}\right]\frac{1}{r}+\dots\,.

Note that a
\log r/r
term appears, arising exclusively from the
advanced wave. This term is time-independent and typically includes
all spherical harmonics. Due to its time-independence, it does not
contribute to the energy flux across
\mathscr{I}^{+}
. Additionally,
the advanced wave also contributes a time-independent term at order

r^{-1}
.

Let us now determine the contribution of the retarded wave to the
asymptotic expansion near
\mathscr{I}^{-}
. The requirement of finite
total radiated energy implies that

\frac{d^{l}}{du^{l}}a_{l,m}\left(u\right)\underset{u\rightarrow-\infty}{=}\phi_{R\left(l,m\right)}^{-}\log\left(-u\right)+\varphi_{R\left(l,m\right)}^{-}+\dots\,.

Integrating the previous equation using the identity along the same lines of (
62

\alpha\underbrace{\int\cdots\int}_{k}\log\left(-x\right)\,dx+\beta\underbrace{\int\cdots\int}_{k}\,dx=\alpha\left(\frac{x^{k}}{k!}\log\left(-x\right)-\frac{1}{k!}H_{k}x^{k}\right)+\beta\frac{x^{k}}{k!}+\sum_{n=0}^{k-1}c_{k-n}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}\,,

one obtains

\displaystyle\frac{d^{l-k}}{du^{l-k}}a_{l,m}\left(u\right)\underset{v\rightarrow-\infty}{=}

\displaystyle\phi_{R\left(l,m\right)}^{-}\left(\frac{u^{k}}{k!}\log\left(-u\right)-\frac{1}{k!}H_{k}u^{k}\right)+\varphi_{R\left(l,m\right)}^{-}\frac{u^{k}}{k!}+\sum_{n=0}^{k-1}c_{R\left(k-n\right)}^{\left(l,m\right)}\frac{u^{n}}{n!}\,.

Substituting this result into Eq. (
60

(66)

By expressing the retarded time
u

v

\displaystyle\phi_{R}=\sum_{l,m}\sum_{k=0}^{l}\frac{1}{2^{k}k!r^{k+1}}\frac{\left(l+k\right)!}{\left(l-k\right)!}\left[\phi_{R\left(l,m\right)}^{-}\left(\frac{\left(v-2r\right)^{k}}{k!}\log\left(2r-v\right)-\frac{1}{k!}H_{k}\left(v-2r\right)^{k}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\varphi_{R\left(l,m\right)}^{-}\frac{\left(v-2r\right)^{k}}{k!}+\dots\right]Y_{lm}.

Therefore, in the limit
r\rightarrow\infty

held constant,
the leading terms are:

\displaystyle\phi_{R}=

\displaystyle\left[\sum_{l,m}\phi_{R\left(l,m\right)}^{-}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{l}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{k}}{\left(k!\right)^{2}}\frac{\left(l+k\right)!}{\left(l-k\right)!}\right)Y_{lm}\right]\frac{\log r}{r}

\displaystyle+\left[\sum_{l,m}\sum_{k=0}^{l}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{k}}{\left(k!\right)^{2}}\frac{\left(l+k\right)!}{\left(l-k\right)!}\left[\phi_{R\left(l,m\right)}^{-}\left(\log 2-H_{k}\right)+\varphi_{R\left(l,m\right)}^{-}\right]Y_{lm}\right]\frac{1}{r}+\cdots\,.

Consequently, the full solution near
\mathscr{I}^{-}

\displaystyle\left.\phi\right|_{\mathscr{I}^{-}}

\displaystyle=\left[\sum_{l,m}\left(-1\right)^{l}\phi_{R\left(l,m\right)}^{-}Y_{lm}\right]\frac{\log r}{r}

\displaystyle+\left[\sum_{l,m}\left(\left(\frac{d^{l}}{dv^{l}}b_{l,m}\left(v\right)\right)+\left(-1\right)^{l}\left(\phi_{R\left(l,m\right)}^{-}\log 2+\varphi_{R\left(l,m\right)}^{-}\right)\right.\right.

(67)

\displaystyle\left.\left.-\left(\sum_{k=0}^{l}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{k}}{\left(k!\right)^{2}}\frac{\left(l+k\right)!}{\left(l-k\right)!}H_{k}\right)\phi_{R\left(l,m\right)}^{-}Y_{lm}\right)\right]\frac{1}{r}+\cdots\,.

\displaystyle+\left[\sum_{l,m}\sum_{k=0}^{l}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{k}}{2^{k}\left(k!\right)^{2}}\frac{\left(l+k\right)!}{\left(l-k\right)!}\left(\phi_{R\left(l,m\right)}^{-}\left(1+kH_{k}\right)-k\varphi_{R\left(l,m\right)}^{-}\right)Y_{lm}\right]\frac{1}{r^{2}}+\dots\,.

This expression can be simplified by applying the following identity (

\sum_{k=0}^{l}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{k}k}{2^{k}\left(k!\right)^{2}}\frac{\left(l+k\right)!}{\left(l-k\right)!}=-P_{l}^{\prime}(0),

Thus, the combined contribution of the retarded and advanced components
of the momentum near

\displaystyle\left.\pi\right|_{i^{0}}

\displaystyle=-\left[\sum_{l,m}P_{l}^{\prime}(0)\left(\phi_{A\left(l,m\right)}^{+}-\phi_{R\left(l,m\right)}^{-}\right)Y_{lm}\right]\frac{\log r}{r^{2}}

\displaystyle+\left[\sum_{l,m}\left(P_{l}(0)\left(\phi_{A\left(l,m\right)}^{+}+\phi_{R\left(l,m\right)}^{-}\right)-P^{\prime}_{l}(0)\left(\varphi_{A\left(l,m\right)}-\varphi_{R\left(l,m\right)}^{-}\right)\right.\right.

(71)

\displaystyle+\left.\left.\left(\sum_{k=0}^{l}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{k}}{2^{k}\left(k!\right)^{2}}\frac{\left(l+k\right)!}{\left(l-k\right)!}kH_{k}\right)\left(\phi_{R\left(l,m\right)}^{-}-\phi_{A\left(l,m\right)}^{+}\right)\right)Y_{lm}\right]\frac{1}{r^{2}}+\dots\,

As noted in
Fuentealba:2024lll
, terms of order
\log r/r

in
\left.\phi\right|_{i^{0}}
and
\log r/r^{2}
in
\left.\pi\right|_{i^{0}}

are not allowed. Therefore, from Eqs. (

) and (
71
),
we obtain the following conditions

\displaystyle\sum_{l,m}P_{l}(0)\left(\phi_{A\left(l,m\right)}^{+}+\phi_{R\left(l,m\right)}^{-}\right)Y_{lm}

\displaystyle=0\,,

(72)

\displaystyle\sum_{l,m}P^{\prime}_{l}(0)\left(\phi_{A\left(l,m\right)}^{+}-\phi_{R\left(l,m\right)}^{-}\right)Y_{lm}

\displaystyle=0\,.

(73)

There are two cases depending if

is odd or even. Thanks to the parity properties of the Legendre polynomials, we have that half of the equations are always satisfied. So, for
l
even, only the first equation imposes a condition on the field coefficients. In contrast, for
l
odd only the second relation yields a condition on the difference of the coefficients
\phi^{\pm}_{(l,m)}
.

Consequently, the
condition of not having logarithmic terms in (
70

(

\phi_{A\left(l,m\right)}^{+}
and
\phi_{R\left(l,m\right)}^{-}
that can be summarized as

\phi_{A\left(l,m\right)}^{+}=-\left(-1\right)^{l}\phi_{R\left(l,m\right)}^{-}\,.

(74)

The above expression establishes a relation between the leading logarithmic
contributions of the field at
\mathscr{I}_{-}^{+}
and
\mathscr{I}_{+}^{-}
.
Using the definitions of the radial logarithmic terms given in Eqs.(
68
)
and (
69

),
one obtains:

\displaystyle\Psi\left(\hat{x}\right)+\Psi^{\prime}\left(-\hat{x}\right)

\displaystyle=2\sum_{l,m}\left(-1\right)^{l}\left(\phi_{A\left(l,m\right)}^{+}Y_{lm}\left(\hat{x}\right)+\phi_{R\left(l,m\right)}^{-}Y_{lm}\left(-\hat{x}\right)\right)\,,

\displaystyle=2\sum_{l,m}\left(-1\right)^{l}\left(\phi_{A\left(l,m\right)}^{+}+\left(-1\right)^{l}\phi_{R\left(l,m\right)}^{-}\right)Y_{lm}\left(\hat{x}\right)\,,

\displaystyle=0\,.

Here, we have used the parity property of the spherical harmonics,

Y_{lm}\left(-\hat{x}\right)=\left(-1\right)^{l}Y_{lm}\left(\hat{x}\right)
.
As a result, we obtain the relation

\Psi\left(\hat{x}\right)=-\Psi^{\prime}\left(-\hat{x}\right),

which corresponds precisely to the matching condition established
in
Fuentealba:2024lll
.

If we now assume that there is no leading logarithmic term (
\Psi(\hat{x})=\Psi^{\prime}(\hat{x})=0
), the solution reduces at leading order to the

,
v\rightarrow\infty
), which obeys the “standard” parity condition of
Campiglia:2017dpg

Appendix B
General solution of Maxwell’s equations in retarded coordinates

The wave equation obeyed by the electromagnetic potential is

\left(\boxempty A_{\mu}-\partial_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}A^{\nu}\right)=0.

Writing this solution in terms of retarded coordinates, and imposing
for simplicity the gauge
A_{r}=0
, one finds the following equations:

\displaystyle\partial_{r}^{2}A_{u}+\frac{2}{r}\partial_{r}A_{u}-\partial_{u}\partial_{r}A_{u}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\left(D^{2}A_{u}-\partial_{u}\left(D^{B}A_{B}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=0\,,

(75)

\displaystyle\partial_{r}^{2}A_{u}+\frac{2}{r}\partial_{r}A_{u}-\frac{1}{r^{2}}\partial_{r}\left(D^{B}A_{B}\right)

\displaystyle=0\,,

(76)

\displaystyle\partial_{r}^{2}A_{A}-2\partial_{u}\partial_{r}A_{A}+\partial_{A}\partial_{r}A_{u}+\frac{D^{B}D_{B}A_{A}-D_{A}D_{C}A^{C}-A_{A}}{r^{2}}

\displaystyle=0\,.

(77)

To solve these equations, it is convenient to introduce the following separation of variables

\displaystyle A_{u}

\displaystyle=f_{lm}\left(u,r\right)Y_{lm}\,,

\displaystyle A_{A}

\displaystyle=g_{lm}\left(u,r\right)\partial_{A}Y_{lm}+h_{lm}\left(u,r\right)\sqrt{\gamma}\epsilon_{AB}\partial^{B}Y_{lm}\,.

Thus, from Eq. (
75
) one finds

\partial_{r}^{2}f_{lm}+\frac{2}{r}\partial_{r}f_{lm}-\partial_{u}\partial_{r}f_{lm}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}l\left(l+1\right)\left(\partial_{u}g_{lm}-f_{lm}\right)=0.

From Eq. (
76
) one obtains

\partial_{r}^{2}f_{lm}+\frac{2}{r}\partial_{r}f_{lm}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}l\left(l+1\right)\partial_{r}g_{lm}=0\,.

From Eq. (
77
) one finds

\left(\partial_{r}^{2}g_{lm}+\partial_{r}f_{lm}-2\partial_{u}\partial_{r}g_{lm}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}l\left(l+1\right)g_{lm}-\frac{1}{r^{2}}g_{lm}\right)\partial_{A}Y_{lm}\\
+\left(\partial_{r}^{2}h_{lm}-2\partial_{u}\partial_{r}h_{lm}-\frac{1}{r^{2}}h_{lm}\right)\sqrt{\gamma}\epsilon_{AB}\partial^{B}Y_{lm}\\
+\frac{1}{r^{2}}g_{lm}D^{C}D_{A}D_{C}Y_{lm}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}h_{lm}\sqrt{\gamma}\epsilon_{AB}D^{C}D^{B}D_{C}Y_{lm}=0.

Using the identity
D^{B}D_{A}D_{B}\Lambda=D_{A}D^{2}\Lambda+D_{A}\Lambda
,
one obtains

\left(\partial_{r}^{2}g_{lm}+\partial_{r}f_{lm}-2\partial_{u}\partial_{r}g_{lm}\right)\partial_{A}Y_{lm}+\\
\left(\partial_{r}^{2}h_{lm}-2\partial_{u}\partial_{r}h_{lm}-\frac{l\left(l+1\right)}{r^{2}}h_{lm}\right)\sqrt{\gamma}\epsilon_{AB}\partial^{B}Y_{lm}=0\,.

(78)

This gives two equations

0=\partial_{r}^{2}g_{lm}+\partial_{r}f_{lm}-2\partial_{u}\partial_{r}g_{lm}\,,

0=\partial_{r}^{2}h_{lm}-2\partial_{u}\partial_{r}h_{lm}-\frac{l\left(l+1\right)}{r^{2}}h_{lm}\,.

In summary, Maxwell equations reduce to the following system of differential equations

Equations for
f
and
g
:

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\partial_{r}^{2}f_{lm}+\frac{2}{r}\partial_{r}f_{lm}-\partial_{u}\partial_{r}f_{lm}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}l\left(l+1\right)\left(\partial_{u}g_{lm}-f_{lm}\right)\,,

(79)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\partial_{r}^{2}f_{lm}+\frac{2}{r}\partial_{r}f_{lm}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}l\left(l+1\right)\partial_{r}g_{lm}\,,

(80)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\partial_{r}^{2}g_{lm}+\partial_{r}f_{lm}-2\partial_{u}\partial_{r}g_{lm}\,.

(81)

Equation for
h

0=\partial_{r}^{2}h_{lm}-2\partial_{u}\partial_{r}h_{lm}-\frac{l\left(l+1\right)}{r^{2}}h_{lm}\,.

(82)

We shall assume, as in the scalar case, that the functions
f_{lm}
,
g_{lm}
and
h_{lm}
, can be decomposed as the sum of retarded and advanced waves.
The retarded part is assumed to have an asymptotic expansion in inverse powers of

B.1
Solution of the magnetic sector

By performing the change of variables

B.2
Solution of the electric sector

The equations that must be solved are given by the following system

\displaystyle 0

Appendix C
Legendre polynomials properties

In this article, multiple sums appeared, some of which have known results. In this section, we will show how to compute them.

There are two types of sums: those appearing at null infinity, which yield the matching conditions, and those arising when evaluating the field at spatial infinity. Both can be expressed in terms of Legendre polynomials and their derivatives, evaluated at specific points, either
x=-1
or
x=0
.

C.1
Values at
x=0

An interesting representation of the Legendre polynomials is given by

\displaystyle P_{l}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{l}\frac{(x-1)^{k}}{2^{k}(k!)^{2}}\frac{(l+k)!}{(l-k)!};\quad\text{with}\quad P_{l}(x)=(-1)^{l}P_{l}(-x).

(92)

This representation is useful because several of the relevant sums can be written as Legendre polynomials and their derivatives evaluated at zero:

\displaystyle P_{l}(0)=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{l}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{2^{k}(k!)^{2}}\frac{(l+k)!}{(l-k)!};\quad P_{l}(0)=0\text{ for }l\text{ odd}

(93)

\displaystyle-P^{\prime}_{l}(0)=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{l}\frac{k(-1)^{k}}{2^{k}(k!)^{2}}\frac{(l+k)!}{(l-k)!}\quad P^{\prime}_{l}(0)=0\text{ for }l\text{ even}

(94)

\displaystyle P^{\prime\prime}_{l}(0)=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{l}\frac{k(k-1)(-1)^{k}}{2^{k}(k!)^{2}}\frac{(l+k)!}{(l-k)!}\quad P^{\prime\prime}_{l}(0)=0\text{ for }l\text{ odd}.

(95)

Each of these sums arises when evaluating the fields at
i^{0}
. The appearance of these polynomials is not surprising, as they naturally occur in
Fuentealba:2024lll
;
Fuentealba:2025ekj
.

C.2
Values at
x=\pm 1

The other type of sums in this article are those related to the field evaluated at null infinity (either future or past). In general, they can always be expressed as Legendre polynomials evaluated at
x=-1
. From the same representation in (
92
), we can write

\displaystyle P_{l}(-1)=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{l}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{(k!)^{2}}\frac{(l+k)!}{(l-k)!}

(96)

\displaystyle-2P^{\prime}_{l}(-1)=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{l}\frac{k(-1)^{k}}{(k!)^{2}}\frac{(l+k)!}{(l-k)!}.

(97)

Due to the parity condition and the representation of the Legendre polynomials in (
92
) we can easily determine the value of
P_{l}(-1)
and their derivatives

\displaystyle P_{l}(-1)=(-1)^{l}P_{l}(1)=(-1)^{l}\,,\quad-2P^{\prime}_{l}(-1)=2(-1)^{l}P^{\prime}_{l}(1)=(-1)^{l}l(l+1).

(98)

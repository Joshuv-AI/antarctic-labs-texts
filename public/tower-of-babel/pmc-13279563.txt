Efficacy and Safety of Reteplase Versus Alteplase in Acute Ischemic Stroke Based on Fibrinogen Levels: The RAISE Trial Subgroup
Authors: Jiaqing Wang, Dandan He, Hongqiu Gu, Zixiao Li, Yilong Wang, Xingquan Zhao, Yongjun Wang, Shuya Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe effects of intravenous thrombolytic agents on fibrinogen differ due to structural differences among the agents. Using data from the RAISE (Reteplase Versus Alteplase for Acute Ischemic Stroke) trial, we aimed to investigate the impact of differences in baseline plasma fibrinogen levels on the efficacy and safety of reteplase versus alteplase within 4.5 hours of acute ischemic stroke symptom onset.MethodsThis post hoc subgroup analysis of the multicenter RAISE trial categorized participants by baseline fibrinogen levels: low (<2 g/L), normal (2–4 g/L), and high (>4 g/L). The primary efficacy outcome was excellent functional outcome at 90 days (modified Rankin scale score of 0 or 1). The primary safety outcome was symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage within 36 hours.ResultsA total of 1373 patients with acute ischemic stroke were included. Ninety‐two in the low fibrinogen group (<2 g/L), 1178 in the normal fibrinogen group (2–4 g/L), and 103 in the high fibrinogen group (>4 g/L). Adjusted risk ratios of primary efficacy outcome were 1.13 (95% CI, 0.97–1.32) for the low fibrinogen group, 1.13 (95% CI, 1.04–1.23) for the normal fibrinogen group, and 1.09 (95% CI, 0.84–1.42) for the high fibrinogen group. The primary safety outcome showed no difference between reteplase and alteplase in the 3 fibrinogen subgroups.ConclusionsAmong patients with acute ischemic stroke who were treated with either reteplase or alteplase within 4.5 hours after symptom onset, there was no difference observed in the relative efficacy and safety between the 2 groups across the 3 fibrinogen subgroups. However, these findings should be interpreted cautiously and require validation in larger, adequately powered prospective studies.REGISTRATION: URL: https://www.clinicaltrials.gov; Unique identifier: NCT05295173.Keywords: acute ischemic stroke (AIS), alteplase, fibrinogen, functional outcome, hemorrhagic transformation, reteplase, thrombolysisSubject Categories: Ischemic Stroke

Acute ischemic stroke (AIS) remains a leading cause of disability and death worldwide, 1 Intravenous thrombolysis is a standard reperfusion therapy for patients within 4.5 hours of symptom onset. 2 , 3 , 4 However, thrombolysis may be complicated by hemorrhagic transformation, particularly symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage, which could increase the risk of poor functional outcomes and death in patients with stroke. 5 Intravenous alteplase is the globally approved standard thrombolytic agent for AIS. 2 , 3 , 4 Reteplase, a nonglycosylated variant of alteplase, 6 has emerged as an alternative thrombolytic option. 4 In the RAISE (Reteplase Versus Alteplase for Acute Ischemic Stroke) trial, patients experiencing AIS receiving intravenous thrombolysis within 4.5 hours of symptom onset, reteplase was superior to alteplase in achieving excellent functional outcomes, but associated with a higher incidence of hemorrhagic events. 7

Fibrinogen is a critical biomarker of hemostasis, serves as a substrate for thrombin, and also contributes to platelet activation. 8 Elevated baseline fibrinogen levels in patients with ischemic stroke or transient ischemic attack have been associated with unfavorable functional outcomes and dependence. 9 Moreover, among patients with AIS treated with intravenous alteplase, fibrinogen played a role in predicting the risk of hemorrhagic complications 10 , 11 , 12 , 13 and unfavorable functional outcomes. 14 , 15 However, evidence on the impact of fibrinogen levels in patients with AIS who received reteplase treatment are limited, and it remains unclear whether baseline fibrinogen modifies the comparative efficacy and safety of reteplase versus alteplase. In this post hoc analysis of the RAISE trial, we evaluated the efficacy and safety of intravenous reteplase versus intravenous alteplase in patients with AIS within 3 baseline fibrinogen strata (low, <2 g/L; normal, 2–4 g/L; and high, >4 g/L).

Methods

The data that support the findings of this study are available from the corresponding author upon reasonable request.

Trial Design and Participants

This study is a post hoc subgroup analysis based on the RAISE trial, conducted in accordance with the Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials reporting guideline. Detailed information about the study's rationale, design, and primary results has been previously published. 7 The RAISE trial was a multicenter, open‐label, randomized controlled study conducted at 47 tertiary hospitals across China between March 21, 2022, and June 22, 2023. Eligible participants were adults aged 18 to 80 years who presented with AIS within 4.5 hours of symptom onset, had a baseline modified Rankin Scale (mRS) score of 0 to 1, and a National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale score between 4 and 25. Patients who had previously undergone or were scheduled to undergo endovascular thrombectomy were excluded. After obtaining informed consent, participants were randomized to receive either intravenous reteplase or alteplase in a 1:1 ratio using a centralized interactive web response system with variable block length, and treatment allocation was concealed until randomization. Intravenous thrombolytic treatment was administered in an open‐label manner, whereas all end points were evaluated by trained independent investigators who were blinded to treatment assignment. In this study, we included all patients with available baseline fibrinogen data.

Stratification by Fibrinogen Levels

For the present analysis, patients were stratified according to their baseline fibrinogen levels measured before thrombolytic administration using the standardized Clauss method. The plasma fibrinogen concentration typically ranges between 2 and 4 g/L under normal physiological conditions. 16 Accordingly, patients were categorized into 3 groups on the basis of baseline fibrinogen levels: low (<2 g/L), normal (2–4 g/L), and high (>4 g/L).

Treatment Protocol

Patients in the reteplase group received a total dose of 36 mg, administered as 2 intravenous boluses of 18 mg each, given 30 minutes apart. In the alteplase group, patients received the standard dose of 0.9 mg/kg (maximum, 90 mg) administered over a 60‐minute infusion, with an initial 10% bolus over 1 minute. Both treatments were administered within 4.5 hours of symptom onset, as per standard guidelines.

Efficacy and Safety Assessments

The primary efficacy outcome was the percentage of patients with an excellent functional outcome (defined as an mRS score of 0–1 at 90 days). The secondary efficacy outcomes included a good functional outcome (defined as an mRS score of 0–2 at 90 days), the ordinal distribution of the mRS score at 90 days, early dramatic recovery (defined as a decrease of ≥4 points in National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale score or a National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale score of ≤1 at 24 hours and 7 days after treatment), and a Barthel Index score of at least 95 at 90 days.

The safety outcomes included symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage, as defined by the ECASS III (European Cooperative Acute Stroke Study III), within 36 hours and 7 days 17 ; parenchymal hemorrhage type 2 defined by the SITS‐MOST (Safe Implementation of Thrombolysis in Stroke—Monitoring Study) within 36 hours 18 ; any intracranial hemorrhage; major hemorrhage and clinically relevant nonmajor hemorrhage (defined by the International Society on Thrombosis and Hemostasis criteria) 19 , 20 death from all causes within 7 and 90 days; and serious adverse events occurring within 90 days. 21

We also evaluated the dynamic changes of fibrinogen levels at baseline, 24 hours after thrombolysis, and 72 hours after thrombolysis in the alteplase and reteplase groups.

Statistical Analysis

This post hoc exploratory subgroup analysis of the RAISE trial was conducted using complete data without imputation for missing data. Descriptive statistics were used to summarize baseline characteristics, with categorical variables presented as frequencies and percentages, and continuous variables as medians and interquartile ranges. Categorical variables were compared across different fibrinogen groups and treatment groups using the χ2 test or Fisher's exact test, while continuous variables were analyzed using ANOVA or the Kruskal–Wallis H test for comparisons across fibrinogen groups, and Student's t test, Welch's t test, or the Mann–Whitney U test for comparisons between treatment groups.

The analyses of efficacy and safety outcomes were conducted in the modified intention‐to‐treat population (all randomized patients who received study treatment and had confirmed AIS) 7 and the safety population, respectively. For the primary efficacy outcome, risk ratio (RR) and corresponding 95% CI comparing reteplase with alteplase were estimated using univariate and multivariable robust Poisson regression models, with trial center treated as a random effect. 19 The interaction between treatment and fibrinogen was evaluated within the regression models, with fibrinogen treated as both continuous and categorical variables. Multivariable analyses were adjusted for age and baseline National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale score, consistent with the prespecified covariate adjustment strategy in the statistical analysis plan of the RAISE study. 7 The subgroup analyses were performed according to stratification variables including age, sex, onset‐to‐treatment time, and baseline comorbidities (hypertension, diabetes, hyperlipidemia, coronary heart disease). Additionally, fibrinogen level was treated as a continuous variable in tests of interaction with treatment. For dichotomous secondary efficacy outcomes and safety outcomes, the same model analysis was used. Ordinal logistic regression was used to analyze ordinal secondary efficacy outcomes, with results reported as common odds ratios with 95% CIs. Temporal changes in fibrinogen levels at baseline, 24 hours, and 72 hours after treatment were plotted by line graphs for each treatment group.

All analyses were performed using SAS version 9.4 (SAS Institute Inc., Cary, NC). As this was a post hoc exploratory subgroup analysis, no adjustments were made for multiple comparisons. Two‐sided P values <0.05 were considered statistically significant.

Ethical Considerations

This trial was approved by the ethics committees of Beijing Tiantan Hospital and all participating institutes in China. All patients or their representatives provided written informed consent before participation.

Results

Baseline Characteristics

A total of 1412 patients with AIS were enrolled in RAISE trial and assigned to receive either reteplase (n=707) or alteplase (n=705) (Figure 1). The modified intention‐to‐treat population included 698 patients assigned to the reteplase group and 698 patients in the alteplase group. After excluding 23 patients with missing admission fibrinogen data, 1373 (98.4%) patients were analyzed in this subgroup analysis (Figure 1). Baseline characteristics of patients based on the fibrinogen group are shown in Table S1. There were 92 (6.7%) patients in the low group (median, 1.82 g/L [interquartile range, 1.67–1.93]), 1178 (85.8%) patients in the normal group (median, 2.83 g/L [interquartile range, 2.49–3.25]), and 103 (7.5%) patients in the high group (median, 4.36 g/L [interquartile range, 4.16–4.80]). Patients with higher fibrinogen levels were older, had higher proportions of women, and a higher prevalence of diabetes (all P<0.05). Among the 3 fibrinogen groups, demographic and clinical characteristics were balanced across treatment groups (Table 1).

Efficacy Outcomes

Overall, an excellent functional outcome (mRS score, 0–1) was reported in 80.0% of the patients in the reteplase group and in 72.0% of those in the alteplase group (adjusted RR, 1.12 [95% CI, 1.04–1.22]; P=0.005; Table 2). The adjusted RRs of mRS score of 0 to 1 at 90 days for the patients in the low, normal, and high fibrinogen group were 1.13 (95% CI, 0.97–1.32; P=0.120), 1.13 (95% CI, 1.04–1.23; P=0.005), and 1.09 (95% CI, 0.84–1.42; P=0.518), respectively. No interaction association between fibrinogen level and treatment was observed. The subgroup analyses for the primary efficacy outcome are provided in Figures S1 and S2.

Safety Outcomes

In the safety population, symptomatic intracranial hemorrhages within 36 hours occurred in 17 of 688 patients (2.5%) in the reteplase group and in 14 of 688 patients (2.0%) in the alteplase group (adjusted RR, 1.19 [95% CI, 0.54–2.59]; P=0.667). Symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage within 36 hours occurred in 0 of 42 patients treated with reteplase and 2 (3.9%) of 51 patients treated with alteplase in the low fibrinogen group, in 16 (2.7%) of 591 patients treated with reteplase, and 12 (2.0%) of 589 patients treated with alteplase (adjusted RR, 1.31 [95% CI, 0.66–2.59]; P=0.446) in the normal fibrinogen group, and in 1 (1.8%) of 55 treated with reteplase and 0 of 48 patients treated with alteplase in the high fibrinogen group (Table 3).

DISCUSSION

This post hoc study, based on the RAISE trial, showed that baseline fibrinogen levels did not associate with the differences in the efficacy or safety outcomes between reteplase and alteplase, among patients with AIS treated with intravenous thrombolysis within 4.5 hours of symptom onset.

The efficacy of intravenous thrombolysis tends to be fibrinogen dependent. A previous study showed that higher baseline fibrinogen levels in patients with ischemic stroke or transient ischemic attack are associated with poor functional outcomes and greater dependency. 9 In addition, multiple studies have also indicated that fibrinogen may predict both functional outcomes 14 , 15 and the risk of hemorrhagic complications 10 , 11 , 12 , 13 in patients with AIS undergoing intravenous thrombolysis with alteplase. However, data on the prognostic value of fibrinogen in patients with AIS who received reteplase treatment remain limited. The RAISE trial, the first phase III randomized clinical trial comparing reteplase and alteplase in AIS treated within 4.5 hours of symptom onset, demonstrated superior 90‐day functional outcomes with reteplase. 7 This subgroup study further revealed no significant differences between the 2 agents across fibrinogen strata. Notably, a fibrinogen‐dependent trend was observed: As fibrinogen levels increased, the therapeutic efficacy of both thrombolytics tended to decline.

Regarding safety, fibrinogen depletion has been linked to hemorrhagic risk. Previous studies have indicated that after patients receive intravenous thrombolysis, reteplase induces a more significant depletion of fibrinogen than alteplase, 6 , 22 and those fibrinogen depletions are associated with an elevated hemorrhagic risk, 23 which is consistent with our findings showing higher rates of any intracranial hemorrhage and serious adverse events in the reteplase group. Moreover, no differences in the relative safety profile between reteplase and alteplase were observed across fibrinogen strata. Concurrently, reteplase showed greater fibrinogen depletion at 24 hours after thrombolysis compared with alteplase across all fibrinogen subgroups.

This phenomenon may be attributed to the dual binding properties of reteplase and alteplase. Both agents can bind thrombus‐associated fibrin and activate plasminogen to plasmin. Plasmin subsequently cleaves fibrin into fibrin degradation products, thereby lysing the thrombus and achieving vascular recanalization. 6 , 24 However, its affinity is not limited to thrombus fibrin; it also binds to circulating fibrinogen, leading to fibrinogen depletion. 6 , 24 , 25 It can be speculated that high levels of circulating fibrinogen may compete with thrombus‐bound fibrin for binding sites on the thrombolytic agent, potentially reducing the efficacy of recanalization. During fibrinogen depletion, fibrinogen molecules are cleaved into fibrin degradation products, including X, Y, D, and E fragments, some of which possess anticoagulant properties, such as impairing fibrin polymerization or disrupting platelet function, 26 , 27 thereby increasing hemorrhagic risk. Reteplase exhibits lower affinity for fibrin compared with alteplase due to the absence of the finger domain, 6 consequently inducing greater fibrinogen depletion.

From a clinical perspective, these findings suggest that baseline fibrinogen levels alone might not provide sufficient evidence for guiding thrombolytic decisions between reteplase and alteplase in patients with AIS treated within 4.5 hours of symptom onset.

Limitations

This study has several limitations. First, this study adopted an open‐label design, which might introduce potential bias despite randomization and blinded outcome assessment. Second, the study population was limited to Chinese Han patients aged <80 years, restricting the generalizability of results. Also, as a post hoc exploratory analysis, the study may be underpowered for subgroup or interaction analyses, and the lack of adjustments for multiple comparisons could increase the risk of type I error. Therefore, these results should be interpreted as hypothesis generating rather than confirmatory. Furthermore, larger‐scale real‐world studies are warranted to further validate the comparative safety and efficacy of the 2 drugs across different fibrinogen levels, and future investigations should integrate both clinical characteristics and biological markers to refine patient‐specific thrombolytic strategies.

Conclusions

In conclusion, baseline fibrinogen level alone may have limited value for guiding the choice between reteplase and alteplase in patients with AIS within 4.5 hours of symptom onset. Further studies are needed to explore individualized thrombolytic strategies or biomarker‐guided therapy.

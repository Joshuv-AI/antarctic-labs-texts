Telehealth in Cancer Care: A Qualitative Study of Nurses’ and Healthcare Professionals’ Perspectives in Indonesia
Authors: Cecep Eli Kosasih, Siti Ulfah Rifa’atul Fitri, Eka Afrima Sari, Hesti Platini
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundCancer care requires long-term, continuous, and coordinated services. Telehealth has emerged as a promising strategy to enhance continuity of care; however, its implementation in oncology nursing within developing countries remains limited. This study aimed to explore nurses’ and health managers’ perspectives on the needs, challenges, and potential development of telehealth in cancer care in Indonesia.MethodsA qualitative descriptive design with thematic analysis was employed. The participants in this study were eight people consisting of one head doctor of the health service, three hospital managers, and four nurse managers. In-depth semi-structured interviews were conducted with oncology nurses, community health nurses, and health managers involved in cancer care services. Data were analyzed using Braun and Clarke’s six-phase thematic analysis framework.ResultsSeven major themes were identified: (1) telehealth as a strategy for continuity of cancer care; (2) gaps in communication and patient monitoring systems; (3) limited integration of clinical data and documentation; (4) strengthening education and psychosocial support through digital platforms; (5) the need for therapeutic notification systems to enhance adherence; (6) system readiness and interprofessional collaboration; and (7) socioeconomic and digital literacy barriers to implementation.ConclusionTelehealth plays a strategic role in strengthening continuity, education, and psychosocial support in cancer care. Effective implementation requires integration with health information systems, supportive policies, adequate infrastructure, and improved digital literacy among patients and healthcare professionals.

Background

Cancer represents a major global public health challenge, contributing significantly to morbidity and mortality worldwide.1,2 Patients with cancer require prolonged, continuous, and coordinated care to ensure optimal outcomes. Continuity of care has been recognized as a key determinant of quality and safety in chronic disease management.3

Cancer services are largely centralized in referral hospitals, creating challenges for patients who live far from healthcare facilities. Communication between patients and nurses is often conducted through informal platforms such as WhatsApp or telephone calls, which lack structured documentation, systematic monitoring, and data integration. Furthermore, patients with cancer frequently experience significant psychological distress, which remains inadequately addressed due to limited mental health resources.

The rapid digital transformation of health services has positioned telehealth as a promising solution to improve access, communication, and monitoring of cancer patients.4–6 Telehealth enables remote clinical consultations, therapy monitoring, patient education, and psychosocial support, particularly in geographically diverse and resource-constrained settings.

In Indonesia and other developing countries, telehealth adoption in oncology nursing remains suboptimal due to infrastructure limitations, regulatory barriers, and variations in digital literacy among patients and health professionals. Understanding nurses’ and health managers’ perspectives is critical for developing effective and context-appropriate telehealth strategies.

Despite growing evidence that telemedicine enhances access, patient satisfaction, and continuity of cancer care particularly following the COVID-19 pandemic most recent studies remain concentrated on clinical effectiveness and patient-reported outcomes in high-income settings, with limited attention to the complexities of implementation in low- and middle-income countries (LMICs). Existing tele-oncology research treats telehealth as a standalone technological intervention, overlooking its role within a broader, integrated care system requiring robust clinical data integration, interprofessional coordination, and sustained psychosocial support. Consequently, there is a critical lack of qualitative, context-sensitive evidence capturing how telehealth is operationalized in routine oncology practice, especially in settings characterized by centralized services, infrastructure constraints, and reliance on informal communication platforms. This study addresses these gaps by providing an in-depth exploration of telehealth implementation from the perspectives of nurses and health managers in Indonesia, identifying key system-level requirements including integrated health information systems, proactive patient monitoring, therapeutic notification mechanisms, and digitally enabled education and psychosocial support. In doing so, this study advances the field by reframing telemedicine from a technology-driven solution into a comprehensive, patient-centered, and system-integrated model of oncology care, offering empirically grounded insights for developing sustainable telehealth strategies in resource-constrained settings.

Understanding the real needs, challenges, and system readiness for telehealth from the perspectives of frontline nurses and health managers is essential for developing an effective and sustainable telehealth model for cancer care. However, empirical qualitative evidence in this area remains scarce. Therefore, this study aimed to explore the needs, challenges, and potential development of telehealth in cancer care from the perspectives of nurses and healthcare managers in Indonesia.

Methods

Design

A qualitative descriptive design with a thematic analysis approach was used to explore participants’ experiences and perspectives regarding telehealth in cancer care.

Participants and Setting

A total of eight participants included oncology nurses, community health nurses, and health managers involved in cancer care services in Indonesia. Participants were recruited using purposive sampling based on the following criteria: (1) Direct involvement in cancer care services, (2) Minimum of two years of working experience, and (3) Willingness to participate in the study. The study was conducted in a public referral hospital and a district-level health office in Indonesia.

Data Collection

Data were collected through semi-structured in-depth interviews using open-ended questions exploring telehealth utilization, challenges, and expectations. Each interview lasted approximately 45–90 minutes and was conducted in a private setting to ensure confidentiality. Interviews were audio-recorded with participants’ consent and transcribed verbatim. Transcripts were coded independently and categories and themes were generated through iterative discussion among researchers. Data collection continued until data saturation was achieved, indicated by redundancy of information and no emergence of new themes. The researchers served as the primary instruments of data collection and maintained a neutral, non-judgmental stance during in-depth interviews to facilitate open and rich discussions. Reflexivity was applied throughout the research process to minimize potential bias.

Data Analysis

Data were analyzed using Braun and Clarke’s six-phase thematic analysis framework: familiarization with the data, generation of initial codes, searching for themes, reviewing themes, defining and naming themes, and producing the report.7 Coding and theme development were conducted iteratively by the research team to ensure analytical rigor. Transcripts were repeatedly reviewed to achieve familiarization, followed by systematic manual coding, identification of initial codes, and iterative development of themes. Themes were reviewed, refined, and defined through regular discussions among the research team to ensure credibility and consistency. All data analysis was conducted using manual thematic coding without the use of qualitative data analysis software, allowing for in-depth engagement with the data and careful interpretation of participants’ perspectives.

Trustworthiness

Trustworthiness was ensured through member checking and peer debriefing. These strategies enhanced the credibility and dependability of the findings.

Ethical Considerations

Ethical approval was obtained from the institutional ethics committee. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants. Anonymity and confidentiality were maintained throughout the study. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants prior to data collection. The consent process included permission for the use and publication of anonymized responses and direct quotations for research purposes.

Theme and Subthemes

Theme 1: Telehealth as a Strategy for Continuity of Cancer Care

Participants consistently described telehealth as essential for maintaining continuity of cancer care, particularly for follow-up, symptom monitoring, and medication adherence. Telehealth facilitated regular nurse–patient communication when geographical and financial barriers limited hospital visits. Nurses highlighted the value of sustained communication to prevent loss to follow-up.

Participants consistently highlighted telehealth as a promising solution to improve continuity of care, particularly in maintaining communication and follow-up with patients outside hospital settings. Telehealth was perceived as a tool to bridge gaps in access and facilitate ongoing monitoring: It’s very good… if there is an application, it is good to communicate.

It’s very good… if there is an application, it is good to communicate.

Participants also emphasized that without continuous communication, patients are at risk of being lost to follow-up: If they don’t communicate with us, later they can be lost.

If they don’t communicate with us, later they can be lost.

This finding suggests that telehealth can function as a critical mechanism to ensure continuity and patient retention in oncology care pathways.

Theme 2: Gaps in Communication and Patient Monitoring Systems

Existing communication and monitoring systems were perceived as insufficient. Participants reported reliance on informal communication platforms, delayed responses, lack of 24-hour monitoring, and reactive rather than proactive care, increasing the risk of delayed intervention.

Despite the availability of basic communication tools (eg, hotline or messaging applications), participants reported significant limitations in real-time communication and systematic monitoring. Delayed responses and inconsistent contact were common challenges: The communication was a bit stuck… it’s difficult to contact the patient. We cannot monitor patients 24 hours… sometimes responses are delayed.

The communication was a bit stuck… it’s difficult to contact the patient.

We cannot monitor patients 24 hours… sometimes responses are delayed.

Monitoring systems were described as passive and dependent on patient-initiated contact, indicating the absence of structured, proactive follow-up mechanisms.

Theme 3: Limited Integration of Clinical Data and Documentation

A lack of integrated health information systems emerged as a major barrier. Participants noted that patient complaints were not systematically documented, and clinical data across healthcare facilities were poorly integrated, limiting longitudinal evaluation of patient outcomes.

A major barrier identified was the fragmentation of clinical data systems across healthcare settings. Participants described difficulties in accessing and integrating patient data: Hospital data cannot be accessed here… it is difficult to integrate.

Hospital data cannot be accessed here… it is difficult to integrate.

Additionally, documentation of patient complaints and monitoring data was often incomplete or inconsistent: The data is not yet available… we often have to repeat it again.

The data is not yet available… we often have to repeat it again.

These findings highlight the need for integrated health information systems to support continuity and decision-making in cancer care.

Theme 4: Strengthening Education and Psychosocial Support Through Digital Platforms

Telehealth was viewed as a valuable medium for providing continuous education on symptom management, chemotherapy side effects, nutrition, pain control, and emergency signs. Participants also emphasized the importance of psychosocial support, including counselling to address anxiety, fear, and emotional distress experienced by patients and families.

Participants emphasized that cancer patients require comprehensive education and psychosocial support, particularly regarding disease management, treatment side effects, and lifestyle modifications: Psychosocial support is indispensable. Diet is the most important thing… patients don’t know what to eat.

Psychosocial support is indispensable.

Diet is the most important thing… patients don’t know what to eat.

Education was viewed not only as informational but also as a form of emotional support, helping patients cope with the psychological burden of cancer.

Theme 5: The Need for a Notification System as a Reinforcement of Therapeutic Adherence

Digital notification systems were considered critical for reinforcing adherence to long-term cancer therapy. Participants highlighted reminders for chemotherapy schedules, laboratory tests, and medication intake as mechanisms to prevent treatment delays.

The absence of structured reminder systems was identified as a key contributor to non-adherence and delayed treatment. Participants highlighted the importance of automated notifications: Medication notifications are important because patients often forget. If they forget medication, chemotherapy can be delayed.

Medication notifications are important because patients often forget.

If they forget medication, chemotherapy can be delayed.

This underscores the role of telehealth in enhancing adherence through timely reminders and alerts.

Theme 6: System Readiness and Interprofessional Collaboration

Health managers reported that basic digital infrastructure, including electronic medical records, was already available. However, effective telehealth implementation required stronger interprofessional collaboration and integration across hospitals, primary care, and community services.

Participants acknowledged that certain components of system readiness, such as electronic medical records, were already available, but not yet optimally utilized: Medical records and lab data are already integrated.

Medical records and lab data are already integrated.

However, effective telehealth implementation requires stronger coordination among healthcare providers and across sectors.

Theme 7: Socioeconomic and Digital Literacy Barriers

Participants identified socioeconomic constraints, limited device ownership, unstable internet connectivity, and low digital literacy—particularly among elderly patients—as major barriers to telehealth utilization. Dependence on family members sometimes led to information distortion.

Barriers related to socioeconomic status and digital literacy were frequently reported. Patients often relied on others to communicate with healthcare providers, leading to miscommunication: Complaints are often delivered by others, so the information is not accurate.

Complaints are often delivered by others, so the information is not accurate.

Limited access to technology and varying levels of digital literacy further constrained the effectiveness of existing communication approaches.

Synthesis of Research Findings

The synthesis of all interviews indicates that telehealth is perceived as a strategic solution in ensuring the continuity of cancer patient care, particularly in facilitating patients to convey their complaints, obtain education, and receive psychosocial support while at home. Telehealth is considered capable of bridging the limitations of face-to-face healthcare services, which have long been a major barrier in cancer care.

However, the communication and monitoring systems currently in use remain conventional, relying primarily on WhatsApp and hotlines with limited responsiveness, the absence of 24-hour readiness, and a reactive nature. This condition increases the risk of delayed intervention and the potential for information distortion, as patient complaints are often conveyed through third parties.

From an information systems perspective, significant limitations remain in the integration of clinical data across healthcare facilities. The documentation of patient complaints has not yet been structured and has not formed a longitudinal database that could support a comprehensive evaluation of the clinical progression of cancer patients.

Education emerged as a primary need for cancer patients, particularly regarding diet and nutrition, chemotherapy side effects, pain management, recognition of emergency symptoms, and understanding disease stage and progression. The education currently provided remains sporadic and has not yet been integrated into a continuous and structured patient learning system.

Psychological problems constitute the dominant burden among cancer patients. Anxiety, fear, emotional stress, and the need for someone to share their experiences with, as well as continuous counseling, consistently emerged across all interviews. These findings indicate that psychosocial support is an essential component in the development of cancer telehealth services. In addition, therapeutic notification systems are perceived as crucial for maintaining patients’ adherence to chemotherapy schedules, laboratory examinations, and medication intake. Notifications function as behavioral reinforcements in long-term cancer therapy adherence.

From the perspective of system readiness, healthcare services are considered to already possess basic infrastructure in the form of electronic medical records and hospital information systems, enabling further development of more integrated telehealth services. Nevertheless, the implementation of telehealth continues to face major challenges, including patients’ socioeconomic limitations, low levels of digital literacy, limited access to technological devices, and a preference for simpler information displays.

Overall, the findings of this study emphasize that the development of cancer telehealth services must be comprehensively designed by integrating communication, clinical monitoring, medical data integration, education, psychosocial support, and therapeutic notification systems, while also considering patients’ socioeconomic barriers and levels of digital literacy.

Discussion

This study demonstrates that telehealth plays a strategic role in strengthening continuity of cancer care, particularly in facilitating long-term monitoring, follow-up, and communication between nurses and patients. This finding is consistent with the continuity of care framework, which emphasizes longitudinal relationships, information continuity, and coordinated care across health services.3 Telehealth enables nurses to maintain sustained interactions with patients beyond hospital visits, supporting symptom monitoring and treatment adherence, which are critical in oncology care.4,8

The first theme highlights telehealth as a strategy for ensuring continuity of cancer care. Similar findings have been reported in previous studies showing that telehealth improves access to care, reduces travel burden, and enhances patient satisfaction, especially among cancer survivors.4,8,9 In low- and middle-income countries (LMICs), telehealth also serves as a compensatory mechanism for limited oncology specialists and centralized cancer services.10 Therefore, telehealth is not merely a technological innovation but a structural solution to health system inequities in cancer care.

The second theme emphasizes the role of telehealth in strengthening education and psychosocial support. Telehealth-based education supports patient self-management by enhancing knowledge, skills, and confidence in managing cancer-related symptoms and therapy side effects. This aligns with Orem’s Self-Care Deficit Nursing Theory, which positions patient education as a core nursing responsibility to promote independence and adaptive functioning.11 Previous studies confirm that telehealth and mobile health interventions significantly improve health-related behaviors and self-management in chronic illnesses, including cancer.10,12,13

Psychological distress, including anxiety, fear of recurrence, and emotional burden, is highly prevalent among cancer patients. Telehealth facilitates continuous psychosocial support, counseling, and family involvement, which are essential components of holistic oncology nursing care. This finding is consistent with psycho-oncology literature, which emphasizes that psychological interventions are as important as biomedical treatment in cancer care.14 Digital platforms also enable peer support and emotional exchange, which have been shown to improve coping and quality of life among cancer survivors.15

The third theme describes system readiness and interprofessional collaboration as prerequisites for effective telehealth implementation. Successful telehealth integration requires interoperable electronic medical records (EMRs), clear referral pathways, and strong collaboration between hospital-based oncology services, primary care, and community nursing services. This finding supports the digital health implementation framework proposed by Greenhalgh et al, which emphasizes that digital health adoption is a complex, adaptive process influenced by organizational capacity, leadership, and system integration.13

Interprofessional collaboration in telehealth-based cancer care enhances care coordination, reduces information fragmentation, and improves clinical decision-making. This is consistent with the Institute of Medicine’s framework on health system quality, which emphasizes that safe and effective care requires well-coordinated multidisciplinary teamwork.16 Nurses, in this context, play a pivotal role as care coordinators, educators, and digital facilitators in telehealth services. The World Health Organization emphasizes that effective interprofessional collaborative practice enhances health outcomes, improves care coordination, reduces medical errors, and increases patient and healthcare provider satisfaction. IPC involves healthcare professionals from different disciplines working together through effective communication, shared decision-making, and mutual understanding of professional roles and responsibilities.17,18

The fourth theme reveals that structural and digital literacy barriers remain major obstacles to telehealth implementation. Limited internet connectivity, inadequate devices, lack of institutional telehealth policies, and low digital literacy—particularly among elderly cancer patients—significantly hinder optimal utilization. These findings align with previous reviews showing that infrastructure limitations and lack of technology acceptance are the most significant barriers to telehealth adoption in developing countries.9,19

Digital inequality exacerbates health disparities by limiting access to telehealth among socio-economically vulnerable populations. This reflects the broader concept of the “digital divide,” where unequal access to technology creates inequities in healthcare delivery. Without targeted interventions to improve digital literacy and infrastructure, telehealth risks reinforcing, rather than reducing, existing health inequities.10,19

This study provides novel, context-sensitive insights into telehealth implementation in oncology care, extending beyond the dominant focus of prior research on technological effectiveness and patient satisfaction. Existing evidence consistently shows that telemedicine improves access, quality of life, and psychological outcomes in cancer care.20 However, our findings advance this knowledge by demonstrating that telehealth should not be conceptualized merely as a technological intervention, but rather as a complex, system-integrated model of care that requires alignment across communication systems, clinical workflows, and patient support mechanisms.

A key contribution of this study is the identification that implementation challenges are primarily systemic rather than technological. While previous studies highlight barriers related to technical usability and infrastructure,21 our findings emphasize deeper issues such as fragmented communication, lack of integrated data systems, and absence of structured monitoring protocols. This aligns with recent evidence indicating that tele-oncology adoption is influenced by multi-level factors—including organizational, managerial, and data management barriers—rather than technology alone.21 Importantly, this study extends these insights by providing empirical evidence from a real-world clinical context, highlighting how these barriers manifest in daily oncology nursing practice.

Another important contribution is the reconceptualization of telehealth as a proactive care and behavioral support system. Recent clinical studies demonstrate that telemedicine can effectively reduce symptom burden and improve patient satisfaction in oncology settings.22 However, our findings go further by identifying therapeutic notifications and continuous monitoring as essential components for ensuring adherence and preventing treatment delays. This suggests that telehealth should incorporate structured, automated support mechanisms, shifting from reactive communication toward anticipatory and preventive care models.

Furthermore, this study underscores the central role of oncology nurses in telehealth delivery, reinforcing emerging evidence that telehealth can serve as an innovative model for nurse-led follow-up and care coordination.23 Unlike many existing telemedicine models that prioritize physician-led consultations, our findings highlight the importance of nursing-driven interventions, particularly in delivering continuous education, symptom monitoring, and psychosocial support. This aligns with recent literature emphasizing the expanding role of nurses in tele-oncology and the need for nurse-led digital care models.

The findings also contribute to the growing body of literature on health equity in digital oncology care. Recent studies have highlighted persistent inequities in telehealth utilization, particularly related to socioeconomic status, geographic disparities, and digital literacy.24 Consistent with this, our study reveals that reliance on intermediaries (eg, family members) and limited patient engagement with digital tools can compromise communication accuracy and continuity of care. This extends current knowledge by illustrating how digital inequities operate at the micro-level of patient-provider interaction in LMIC contexts.

Overall, this study confirms that telehealth has substantial potential to transform cancer nursing care by enhancing continuity, education, psychosocial support, and system integration. However, technological readiness alone is insufficient. Sustainable telehealth implementation requires strong governance, institutional policies, continuous training for nurses, and patient-centered digital literacy programs. Nursing leadership is therefore essential in translating telehealth innovations into routine oncology practice.

Limitations

This study has several limitations that should be considered when interpreting the findings. First, the study was conducted within a specific regional context and involved a relatively small number of participants, which may limit the generalizability of the findings to other settings with different healthcare infrastructures or patient populations. Second, the data were primarily derived from healthcare providers’ perspectives, particularly nurses and health managers, without direct inclusion of patient experiences, which may result in a partial understanding of telehealth implementation in oncology care. Third, as a qualitative study relying on self-reported data, the findings may be subject to recall bias and social desirability bias. In addition, the use of manual thematic analysis, while allowing in-depth engagement with the data, may introduce potential subjectivity in coding and interpretation despite efforts to enhance rigor through team discussions and reflexivity. Finally, this study focused on perceived needs and challenges rather than evaluating actual implementation outcomes, thus future research is needed to test the effectiveness, feasibility, and scalability of the proposed telehealth model in real-world oncology settings.

Conclusions

Telehealth serves as a strategic innovation for improving continuity of cancer care, enhancing education, and strengthening psychosocial support. To optimize implementation, health systems in developing countries must address infrastructure readiness, regulatory frameworks, and digital literacy. Nursing leadership is essential in driving sustainable telehealth integration. By integrating recent empirical evidence with context-specific findings, this study provides a strong foundation for designing telehealth models that are not only technologically feasible but also clinically meaningful, equitable, and sustainable.

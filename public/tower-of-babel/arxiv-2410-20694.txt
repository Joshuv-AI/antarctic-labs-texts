Asymptotics of discrete Okounkov bodies and thresholds
Chenzi Jin, Yanir A. Rubinstein, Gang Tian

Abstract
This article initiates the study of discrete Okounkov bodies and higher-dimensional Weierstrass gap phenomena, with applications to asymptotic analysis of stability and global log canonical thresholds.



1
Introduction

This article has three main goals. First, to initiate the study of
discrete Okounkov bodies

and their asymptotic behavior. Second, to
study asymptotics of algebraic geometric invariants that are fundamental in the study of K-stability and
canonical Kähler metrics, generally referred to as
stability thresholds
and
global
log canonical thresholds
.
The second goal is achieved partly as an application of the first.
Third, to point out that, surprisingly,
estimates on thresholds follow from a new
higher-dimensional generalization of classical Weierstrass gap theory.

Towards these goals we introduce several new ideas to the study of asymptotic
invariants in algebraic geometry: volume quantiles from statistics to study the big cone, joint large
(k,m(k))
-limits
using Grassmannians in linear systems to unify the study of
global
log canonical thresholds and stability thresholds,
a notion of collapsing Okounkov bodies inspired by Riemannian convergence theory,
and discrete versions of tangent cones and blow-up estimates for collapsing discrete Okounkov bodies.
This also leads to new valuative criteria for stability on the big cone.

1.1
Asymptotics of stability thresholds

Stability thresholds provide sufficient and/or necessary conditions
for K-stability and Chow stability. They have been studied extensively in the past
decade. These invariants can also be characterized analytically as
coercivity thresholds
for the corresponding energy functionals whose critical points are Kähler–Einstein or balanced metrics (or their
logarithmic generalizations).
Despite the absolute centrality of these invariants to moduli problems in algebraic geometry
and to PDE arising in complex geometry, it seems very little is known about their asymptotic behavior in
k
. Here,
k
is the power of the line bundle, with
1/k
playing the rôle of
Planck’s constant
\hbar
in geometric quantization.

Let
L
be a big line bundle on a projective variety
X
with
klt singularities.
Stability thresholds, known as
\delta_{k}
-invariants
(or still as
basis log canonical thresholds), were introduced by Fujita–Odaka in 2016 to measure the complex singularity exponent of the “most singular” basis-type divisor in
|kL|

[
31
]
. By a theorem of Rubinstein–Tian–Zhang these invariants coincide with the analytic coercivity thresholds of the quantized
Ding functional and characterize existence of
k
-balanced metrics

[
54
]
.

According to a theorem of Blum–Jonsson the limit in
k
of these invariants exists: it is the celebrated Fujita–Odaka
\delta
-invariant with
the Fujita–Li valuative criterion

\delta>1
characterizing uniform
K/Ding-stability
[
46
,
30
,
11
]
,
[
31
, Theorem 0.3]
,
[
8
, Theorems B, 4.4]
,

[
5
]
(see
also
[
4
]
).
Beyond the foundational results of Fujita–Odaka and Blum–Jonsson, it seems almost nothing is known about
the asymptotic behavior of these invariants in
k
.
In addition, the work of Blum–Liu has shown that
asymptotics of
\delta_{k}
-invariants is crucial for results on lower-semi-continuity
of the
\delta
-invariant, i.e., openness of K-(semi)stability
[
9
,
10
]
.

Problem 1.1
.

Determine the asymptotics of
\delta_{k}
in
k
.

Recently, two of us solved Problem
1.1

in the toric and ample case
[
40
]
. In this article, using
completely different methods, we give the first general result on
Problem
1.1
, i.e.,
for an arbitrary big line bundle on a general variety.
It seems new already for surfaces.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
X
be a normal
projective variety
with klt singularities, and
L
a big line bundle on
X
.
If
\delta
is computed by a divisorial valuation (Definition
5.10
),

\delta_{k}=\delta+O(1/k).

By results in the toric case
[
40

,
the rate
O(1/k)
in Theorem

is optimal.
The condition “
\delta
is computed by a divisorial valuation”
(Definition
5.10
)
is a natural
one in the literature (e.g., the Fujita–Li valuative criterion for

\delta
is phrased only in terms
of divisorial valuations
[
46
,
30
,
11
]
,
[
47
, Theorem 2.9]
), and there
are no known examples where it does not hold.
By a deep theorem of Liu–Xu–Zhuang it holds in rather great generality,
when
L=-K_{X}-D
is ample,
(X,D)
is klt, and
\delta<\frac{n+1}{n}

[
47
, Theorem 1.2]
.
In fact, the proof of Theorem

only requires this condition

Theorem

is related to a seminal result of Blum–Jonsson who
showed that

is always computed by a valuation when

is ample
[
8
, Theorem E]
.
A key step in their proof is to modify the

-graded linear series on
L

(Definition

2.16
) by certain multiplier ideals in such a way that it can be ‘lifted’ to a
birational model over
X

O(1/k)

estimates for a twisted expected vanishing order
[
8
, Theorem 5.3]
, and, ultimatley, through
a tour de force involving deep facts on multiplier ideal sheaves they are
able to obtain the necessary compactness in the space of valuations and guarantee a
minimizing valuation exists. Roughly, Theorem
1.2
is a sort of converse
(in the more general big case but under the more restrictive divisorial assumption),
starting by assuming that a minimizing valuation exists and showing the optimal

O(1/k)
asymptotics for the expected vanishing order
on the
actual

v
-graded linear series.

1.2
Asymptotics of global log canonical thresholds

Our approach to Problem
1.1

allows us to tackle
another central open problem, concerning another classical family of
invariants, the
k
-th
global log canonical
thresholds

capturing the “most singular” divisor

studied by Shokurov and Cheltsov in the algebraic geometry literature starting in the 1990’s.
By a theorem of Demailly, these invariants coincide with
Tian’s analytic

-invariants introduced in 1988 in the context
of the Kähler–Einstein problem
[

,
58
,
59

Problem 1.3
.

Determine the asymptotics of
\alpha_{k}
in
k

It is also very loosely related to the

that stipulates (roughly) that the set of all

[

[

Finally, Demailly–Kollár studied a

\big|\mathrm{lct}_{x}(f)-\mathrm{lct}_{x}(f_{k})\big|\leq\frac{n}{k+1},

Surprisingly, there are still

According to a theorem of Demailly and Shi

[

In the non-exceptional log
Fano setting (i.e.,

The toric Fano case is the only setting where the stabilization problem is completely solved

(in addition, the case of smooth del Pezzo surfaces

A beautiful and difficult theorem of Blum–Jonsson is the only general result on Problem

Theorem 1.4
.

(Blum–Jonsson)

Let
X
be a normal
projective variety
with klt singularities, and

an ample line bundle on
X

Then

\alpha_{k}=\alpha+O(1/k).

Our second main result addresses Problem

1.3
in the general big case.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
X
be a normal
projective variety of dimension
n
with klt singularities, and
L
a big line bundle on
X
.
If
\alpha
is computed by a divisorial valuation (Definition
5.10
),

\alpha_{k}=\alpha+O(1/k^{1/n}).

If for some
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
\alpha=\alpha_{\ell}
, then
\alpha_{k}=\alpha+O(1/k)
.

The convergence rate of Theorem
1.5
is sometimes
slower than the one in the ample setting. We do not know if this
can be improved. The proof involves delicate a priori estimates (Theorem
1.17
)
inspired by blow-up analysis of tangent cones in Riemannian convergence
theory, and adapted to collapsing
discrete
Okounkov bodies, introduced in
§
1.4
. In a precise sense, these estimates generalize
Weierstrass gap theory from the setting of curves, and this is explained in
§
1.4
–

.
The condition “
\alpha
is computed by a divisorial valuation”
is once again natural. It is implied by the existence of an
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}

such that
\alpha=\alpha_{\ell}
(Lemma
4.2
, Corollary
4.10
), and this latter
condition holds by Birkar’s theorem in even greater generality than for
\delta

whenever
L=-K_{X}-D
is big and nef,
(X,D)
is klt, and
\alpha\leq 1
(i.e.,
(X,D)
is non-exceptional in the sense of Shokurov;
according to Höring–Śmiech any smooth (or even canonical
singularities) Fano

with

should fall into this class (and this would
follow from the effective nonvanishing conjecture),
and in dimension up to 5 it is also the case
[
36
]
).
It is interesting
to note that
\alpha\leq 1
is a weaker assumption than
\delta<\frac{n+1}{n}

but that is not necessarily relevant to whether
\delta

or
\alpha
are computed by divisors. E.g., for all smooth del Pezzo
surfaces
\alpha\leq 1
, while
\delta\geq 3/2
for cubic surfaces
with an Eckardt point, generic 7-point blow-ups of
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
,
and 8-point blow-ups of
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}

[
3
, Table 2.1]
.
Regardless, both

and

are always computed
by a divisor in this dimension
[
17
,
3
]
.

1.3
A unified approach to thresholds:
volume quantile, Grassmannians, and joint asymptotics

The
volume
function on the big cone is a very central notion in algebraic geometry.
One of the innovations of this article is the idea of introducing
volume
quantiles
. This is done in two completely different ways: using Grassmannians
in spaces of sections, and using valuations. Theorem
1.7
shows
that these coincide in an appropriate
joint limit
, i.e., the former
is a quantization of the latter. A subtle point compared to many quantization
results in that here the limit is not simply a so-called large
k
limit,
but rather a joint one in
(k,m)
with
m=m(k)
the Grassmannian parameter.
It follows from our analysis that the notion of a compatible
m
-basis is precisely
the quantization of the volume quantile.

As an important application, this allows us
to unify the study of
\alpha_{k}
and
\delta_{k}
.
To that end, a family of invariants
\delta_{k,m}

that include both as extreme cases is introduced. Theorem

1.2

is then obtained as a special case of Theorem
1.7
below.
Theorem
1.5
can be considered as a limiting degenerate
case of
Theorem
1.7
. As such it requires careful additional treatment and does not follow from Theorem
1.7
.

Definition 1.6
.

Let
d_{k}:=\dim H^{0}\left(X,kL\right)
, and

{\mathbb{N}}(L):=\{k\in{\mathbb{N}}\,:\,d_{k}>0\}
.
For
k\in{\mathbb{N}}(L)
and
m\in\{1,\ldots,d_{k}\}
, let (see Definition

2.6
)

\delta_{k,m}:=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\left\{s_{\ell}\right\}_{\ell=1}^{m}\subset H^{0}\left(X,kL\right)\\
\mbox{\smlsev linearly independent}\end{subarray}}km\,\mathrm{lct}\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\left(s_{\ell}\right)\right).

(1)

We call the
\delta_{k,m}

Grassmannian stability thresholds
.
They are inspired by the invariants
\alpha_{k,m}

[
59
,
39
]
but are quite different. By definition,
\delta_{k,1}=\alpha_{k}

while
\delta_{k,d_{k}}=\delta_{k}
(Definition
4.3
).
We study their

joint asymptotics
in
(k,m(k)=m_{k})
. This allows to study
the asymptotics of
\alpha_{k}
and
\delta_{k}
simultaneously.
It also yields a 1-parameter family of new invariants
whose endpoints are
\alpha
and
\delta
, depending on the
volume of the linear subsystem, with parameter being the
volume quantile
(Definition
4.11
)

\tau:=\lim_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}(L)}\frac{m(k)}{d_{k}}\in[0,1].

(2)

Importantly, this idea allows us to realize

\alpha_{k}
as invariants associated to a
collapsing

family of Okounkov bodies. Finally, it motivates
the study of discrete Okounkov bodies, as described in §
1.4
.

The following generalizes Theorem
1.2
.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
X
be a normal
projective variety
with klt singularities, and
L
a big line bundle on
X
.
For any sequence
\{m_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}(L)}
,
with
\lim_{k\to\infty}{m_{k}}/{d_{k}}=\tau\in[0,1],

the limit

\bm{\delta}_{\tau}:=\lim_{k\to\infty}\delta_{k,m_{k}}

exists and is independent of the choice of
\{m_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}(L)}
. In particular,
\bm{\delta}_{0}=\alpha
,
\bm{\delta}_{1}=\delta
.

Moreover, there exists
C>0
independent of
k
such that

\delta_{k,m_{k}}\geq\left(1-\frac{C}{k}\right)\min\left\{\frac{m_{k}}{\tau d_{k}},1\right\}\bm{\delta}_{\tau}.

If
\tau>0
and
\bm{\delta}_{\tau}
is computed by a divisorial valuation (Definition
5.10
), then

\delta_{k,m_{k}}\leq\left(1+\frac{C}{k}\right)\max\left\{\frac{m_{k}}{\tau d_{k}},1\right\}\bm{\delta}_{\tau}.

One idea in the proof of Theorem
1.7

is to construct a
rooftop Okounkov body
as well
as its discrete analogues above the ones
associated to
(X,L)
and a flag associated to the divisorial
valuation that computes
\bm{\delta}_{\tau}
. This generalizes
a well-known construction of Donaldson
[
26
]
from the toric setting,
that was generalized by Witt Nyström
[
61
]
.
For toric
X
, we previously discretized Donaldson’s construction
to obtain asymptotics of
\delta_{k}
using Ehrhart theory
[
40
]
. Here,
Ehrhart theory does not apply so instead we prove certain
uniform

estimates on lattice point counts. Another idea is to use notions from probability theory
to control the convergence rates of the
\delta_{k,m_{k}}
by interpreting
the associated invariants
S_{k,m_{k}}
as barycenters of tail distributions
arising from the discrete Okounkov body
\Delta_{k}
via discrete and continuous ccdfs
and volume
quantiles. This makes contact with the beautiful construction of Bouckson–Chen
of 1-parameter filtered families of Okounokv bodies
[
12
]
(in some sense, it is
its quantization). A third idea is to
introduce the notion of
idealized expected vanishing order
and
idealized jumping function
to get useful estimates on the
S_{k,m_{k}}
.

It is natural to ask
whether the
new invariants
\bm{\delta}_{\tau}
are related to K-stability.
A model result, going back to Tian in 1987, and generalized over the years
by Odaka–Sano, Fujita, and Blum–Jonsson

[

, Theorem 2.1]
,
[

, Theorem 1.4]
,
[

, Proposition 2.1]
,
[

, Theorem A]

is the following.

Theorem 1.8
.

(Tian, Odaka–Sano)

Let
X
be a normal

with klt singularities and
-K_{X}
ample.
Then

\alpha\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\geq)}}{{>}}\frac{n}{n+1}

Despite multiple generalizations, it has been an open problem whether

1.8
extends to big classes since all proofs
use ampleness crucially.
In the spirit of §
1.1
–§

it is natural to ask:

Problem 1.9
.

Does Tian–Odaka–Sano’s criterion generalize to the big cone?

A major motivation for Problem

is that

is one of the main tools for constructing smooth and singular Kähler–Einstein metrics.
Thus, a positive

Via the work of Darvas–Zhang
[

In a sequel

we show how our work here, together with new estimates

and, in the spirit of §

(not just

).
We state the result next in order to showcase this application
of Theorem

and the circle of ideas involved in its proof.
A key ingredient, in addition to Theorem

, is the realization, inspired by convex geometry, that
while

are defined on the big cone, they

Note that in the next definition
\bm{\tilde{\delta}}_{1}=\delta
always, while on the nef cone,

\bm{\tilde{\delta}}_{\tau}=\bm{\delta}_{\tau}
and

.

Definition 1.10
.

Let
\sigma:{\operatorname{Val}}_{X}^{\operatorname{div}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}_{+}

the minimal vanishing order (Definition

\bm{\tilde{\delta}}_{\tau}:=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\inf_{v\in{\operatorname{Val}}_{X}^{\operatorname{div}}}\frac{A}{{\mathcal{S}}_{0}+\sigma/n}=:\tilde{\alpha},&\tau=0,\\
\\

These new invariants yield a

]
.

Theorem 1.11
.

Let

be a normal

with klt singularities, and

.

\bm{\tilde{\delta}}_{\tau}\leq\begin{cases}\frac{n}{n+1}\delta,&\tau=0;\\
\tau\left(1-\left(1-\tau\right)^{\frac{n+1}{n}}\right)^{-1}\delta,&0<\tau\leq 1.\end{cases}

In particular,
\delta\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\geq)}}{{>}}1

\displaystyle\bm{\tilde{\delta}}_{\tau}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\geq)}}{{>}}\frac{n}{n+1-\root n \of{\tau}}

1.4
Discrete Okounkov bodies and their gap asymptotics

To prove our main results on asymptotics of thresholds (Theorems
1.5
and
1.7
)
requires in an essential way to introduce the concept of discrete Okounkov bodies
and study their asymptotics.
As we explain next, these provide the natural generalization of Ehrhart
theory and monomial sections from the toric setting. In §
1.5
we explain how
they generalize Weierstrass gap theory to any dimension.

Indeed, results in the toric case on the asymptotics of
\delta_{k}

[
40
]
pointed
out a fascinating link
between Ehrhart theory of asymptotics of lattice points
in lattice polytopes and K-stability theory.
Such asymptotics have been lacking for the vastly
more general Okounkov bodies.
This is one of the inspirations for this article, aiming
to develop quantitative lattice point
estimates in Okounkov bodies. Aside from their crucial
application to asymptotics of thresholds, we believe some
of the techniques developed in this article will be
important for further study of quantitative asymptotics in
the theory of Okounkov bodies.

First, recall the definition of the Okounkov body
[
45
, §1]
,

[
42
]

associated
to a big line bundle
L\rightarrow X
over an
n
-dimensional irreducible variety and a choice of admissible flag

Y_{\bullet}:\ X=Y_{0}\supset Y_{1}\supset\cdots\supset Y_{n}=\{p\},

on
X
(i.e.,
each
Y_{i}
is an irreducible subvariety of codimension
i
regular at
p
).
Choose a holomorphic coordinate chart
f:U\to V\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
around
p
such that

Y_{i}\cap U=f^{-1}\left(\left\{x\in V\,:\,x_{j}=0\text{ for }1\leq j\leq i\right\}\right).

By shrinking
U
we may assume
L|_{U}
is trivial. Fix a trivialization on
U
. Let
\nu
denote the lexicographic valuation, i.e., for a non-zero power series
s=\sum_{I}a_{I}x^{I}
,

\nu\left(s\right)=\min\left\{I\,:\,a_{I}\neq 0\right\},

where we take the lexicographic order on the space of multi-indices.
Let
V_{\bullet}
be a graded linear series belonging to
L
. The Okounkov body is the compact convex set

\Delta:=\overline{\co\left(\bigcup_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}{\textstyle\frac{1}{k}}\,\nu\left(V_{k}\setminus\left\{0\right\}\right)\right)}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n},

where
\co
denotes the convex hull.

In this article we define and initiate the study of the discrete analogues of the Okounkov body:

Definition 1.12
.

For
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, the
k
-th discrete Okounkov body
is

\Delta_{k}:={\textstyle\frac{1}{k}}\,\nu\left(V_{k}\setminus\left\{0\right\}\right)\subset{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}/k.

(3)

A hallmark of Ehrhart theory of a convex lattice polytope
P
is that
the
Ehrhart function

E(k):=\#(P\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}/k)
is a polynomial
of degree
n
, with its coefficients intensely studied (though still
quite mysterious)
[
32
]
. For a general convex body
K
the Ehrhart function
is rather mysterious beyond the leading term
|K|k^{n}
.
Rather recently it was determined for irrational planar
triangles
[
20
]
. An old, but quite different problem, known as the generalized
Gauss Circle Problem is to determine the sub-leading asymptotics for a
real

dilation parameter
r>0
. Namely, to determine the asymptotics of
\big|\#(rK\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{n})-|K|r^{n}\big|
.
This is still wide open even for
K
the unit disc in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
(see, e.g.,
[
38
]
).

These classical problems are hallmarks of Discrete Geometry and Geometry of Numbers.
In the context of Okounkov bodies we pose a
somewhat different, but related, set of problems, inspired by Problems
1.1
and
1.3
. Namely, we are interested to quantify to what
extent do the
\Delta_{k}
approximate
\Delta
. In this setting the asymptotics of

\#(\Delta_{k})=d_{k}
are completely known (at least when
L
is ample): this is the Hilbert polynomial of
L

(recall Definition
1.6
). Thus, instead
of comparing
\Delta
to the
\Delta_{k}
the natural problem is to consider

gaps
in
\Delta\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}/k
. To make this precise, recall the
notion of a valuative point of
\Delta\cap{\mathbb{Q}}^{n}
, i.e.,
a point in the image of
\nu_{k}
for
some

k\in{\mathbb{N}}(L)

[
44
, Definition 1.2.44]
. We extend this to discrete Okounkov bodies:

Definition 1.13
.

A point in
\Delta\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}/k
is
k
-valuative
if it is in
\Delta_{k}
,
and is a
k
-gap point
otherwise. The
k
-gap set of
\Delta
is

(\Delta\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}/k)\setminus\Delta_{k}
.

Problem 1.14
.

Determine the asymptotics of

2.1
Singularities, valuations, log canonical thresholds

Let
X
be a normal projective variety and
L
a line bundle over
X
.
For
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
denote

R_{k}:=H^{0}\left(X,kL\right),

(8)

(here
R_{0}=\mathbb{C}
) and consider the graded ring

R:=\bigoplus_{k=0}^{\infty}R_{k}.

Let

d_{k}:=\dim R_{k}.

(9)

Recall the equality
[
45
, Lemma 1.4]
,

\#\left(\Delta_{k}\right)=\dim R_{k}.

(10)

We always assume
L
is big, i.e., its volume

\mathrm{Vol}\,L:=n!\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{d_{k}}{k^{n}}

is positive, and
{\mathbb{N}}(L)
(Definition
1.6
) contains all sufficiently large integers.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
K
be a field,
K^{*}=K\setminus\{0\}
its multiplictive group, and
\Gamma
an abelian totally ordered group. A map
v:K\to\Gamma\cup\{\infty\}
is called a
valuation
if
v(0)=\infty
and
v|_{K^{*}}:K^{*}\to\Gamma
is a group homomorphism satisfying

v\left(a+b\right)\geq\min\left\{v\left(a\right),v\left(b\right)\right\},

with equality if
v(a)\neq v(b)
. We say
v
is trivial if
v|_{K^{*}}\equiv 0
.

Let
\mathbb{C}(X)
denote the function field of
X
, i.e., the field of rational functions on
X
. Denote

\operatorname{Val}_{X}:=\{v\,:\,\hbox{$v$ is a non-trivial valuation on $\mathbb{C}(X)$ trivial on
$\mathbb{C}\subset\mathbb{C}(X)$}\}.

(11)

Example 2.2
.

Any prime divisor
F
on
X
determines a discrete valuation (i.e., integer valued)
\mathrm{ord}_{F}
on
\mathbb{C}(X)
, called the
vanishing order
along
F
. See
[
34
, §II.6]
.

Note that for any section
s\in R_{k}
, we can make sense of
v(s)
(
v(D)
, respectively) by choosing a trivialization and expressing
s
(the defining function of
D
, respectively) locally. More generally, by homomorphism property,
v(kD)=kv(D)
, allowing us to define
v
on
{\mathbb{Q}}
-divisors.

Definition 2.3
.

For any proper birational morphism
\pi:Y\to X
where
Y
is normal, a prime divisor
F
on
Y
is called a
divisor over
X
.
Let
\mathrm{ord}_{F}
denote the vanishing order along
F
.
A valuation
v
is called
divisorial
if it is in the form of
Cv_{F,\pi}:=C\,\mathrm{ord}_{F}\circ\pi^{*}
for some
C>0
. Specifically, for a section
s\in R_{k}
,

v\left(s\right):=C\,\mathrm{ord}_{F}\left(\pi^{*}s\right).

Let

{\operatorname{Val}}_{X}^{\operatorname{div}}\subset\operatorname{Val}_{X}

(12)

denote the set of all divisorial valuations.

Example 2.4
.

Consider
X={\mathbb{P}}^{2}
.
Let
(z_{1},z_{2})
be the coordinate on some chart

\mathbb{C}^{2}\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
. For a holomorphic function

f\left(z\right)=\sum_{I=(i_{1},i_{2})}a_{I}z_{1}^{i_{1}}z_{2}^{i_{2}}

around
0
, define
v(0):=\infty
and

v\left(f\right):=\min_{a_{I}\neq 0}\left(i_{1}+\sqrt{2}\,i_{2}\right).

This defines a valuation. Indeed, define an order on the multi-indices by
I\leq I^{\prime}
if

i_{1}+\sqrt{2}\,i_{2}\leq i_{1}^{\prime}+\sqrt{2}\,i_{2}^{\prime}.

Let

\deg:f\mapsto\min_{a_{I}\neq 0}\left(i_{1},i_{2}\right)

denote the multi-degree with respect to this order. Then

\deg fg=\deg f+\deg g,

and

\deg\left(f+g\right)\geq\min\left\{\deg f,\deg g\right\},

with equality if
\deg f\neq\deg g
.
However,

v(z_{1})=1
and
v(z_{2})=\sqrt{2}
, while for any divisorial valuation
w_{F,\pi}
and
f_{1},f_{2}\in\mathbb{C}({\mathbb{P}}^{2})^{*}
,
\frac{w_{F,\pi}(f_{1})}{w_{F,\pi}(f_{2})}=\frac{\mathrm{ord}_{F}(f_{1}\circ\pi)}{\mathrm{ord}_{F}(f_{2}\circ\pi)}\in{\mathbb{Q}}

by Example
2.2
. Thus,
v\not\in\hbox{\rm Val}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{2}}^{\operatorname{div}}
.

Remark 2.12
.

The following is well-known; we record it since it will
be used several times below in other instances.
Recall Fekete’s Lemma: let
\{c_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
be sub-additive (super-additive, respectively), i.e.,

c_{i+j}\leq c_{i}+c_{j}\quad(c_{i+j}\geq c_{i}+c_{j},\hbox{\rm respectively}).

Then

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{c_{k}}{k}

equals

\inf_{k}\frac{c_{k}}{k}
(
\sup_{k}\frac{c_{k}}{k}
, respectively).
Let
v\in\operatorname{Val}_{X}
. For
k,k^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
s\in R_{k}
,
s\in R_{k^{\prime}}
,
s\otimes s^{\prime}\in R_{k+k^{\prime}}
and (recall the homomorphism property in Definition
2.1
)

v\left(s\otimes s^{\prime}\right)=v\left(s\right)+v\left(s^{\prime}\right).

Consequently,

kS_{k,1}\left(v\right)+k^{\prime}S_{k^{\prime},1}\left(v\right)\leq\left(k+k^{\prime}\right)S_{k+k^{\prime},1}\left(v\right),

(17)

so (
15
) can be improved to

\lim_{k\to\infty}S_{k,1}\left(v\right)=\sup_{k}S_{k,1}\left(v\right)={\mathcal{S}}_{0}\left(v\right).

Notice that
kS_{k,m}
is the average of the largest
m
jumping numbers, so by (
14
),

m\leq m^{\prime}\quad\Rightarrow\quad S_{k,m}\geq S_{k,m^{\prime}}.

(18)

Going back to our example of
v
associated to
D\subset X
,
suppose one is tasked with finding

of

such that their average vanishing order along

That maximum is achieved and equal

. This is the content of the next lemma for general
valuations (this generalizes
[

, Lemma 2.2]
,
[

, Lemma 3.5]

Lemma 2.13
.

Let

and

.
For

kmS_{k,m}\left(v\right)=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\left\{s_{\ell}\right\}_{\ell=1}^{m}\subset R_{k}\\
\mbox{\smlsev linearly independent}\end{subarray}}v\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\left(s_{\ell}\right)\right).

Proof.

We may assume

∎

To start, recall the classical definition. For a curve
C

,

is a

of

if there is no meromorphic function on
C
that is holomorphic away from
p
and has a pole of order
N
at
p

[
60
]
.
Practically, the gaps are determined as follows.
Consider the map
{\mathcal{W}}:{\mathbb{N}}\longmapsto\{0,1\}

i\;\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\mathcal{W}}}}{{\longmapsto}}\;h^{0}(ip)-h^{0}\big((i-1)p\big).

Using Riemann–Roch

h^{0}(kp)-h^{1}(kp)=k+1-g

,
together with
g=h^{1}\geq h^{1}(p)\geq h^{1}(2p)\geq\cdots\geq h^{1}((2g-1)p)=h^{0}(K_{C}-(2g-1)p)=0

implies that

. The set of Weierstrass gaps
is
{\mathcal{W}}^{-1}(0)=\{N_{1},\ldots,N_{g}\}\subset\{1,\ldots,2g-1\}
:
if

, i.e.,

, the

vanishes at

, so

is a Weierstrass gap, and vice versa.
It follows that there are precisely
g

1=N_{1}<\cdots<N_{g}\leq 2g-1
.

This can be translated
to the language of discrete Okounkov bodies as follows.
First observe that
N
is a Weierstrass gap of

precisely when there is no section

. Equivalently,

where
\Delta_{k}
is as in the statement. This proves (ii).
Note that (iii) is a restatement of (ii).
The point

is called a
Weierstrass point
if
{\mathcal{W}}^{-1}(0)\not=\{1,\ldots,g\}

and so (iv) follows from (ii),
and so does (v).

While (i) follows from (ii), we prove (i) directly by induction to illustrate how
the

can
be computed in practice (see Figure
4
). Note that
\Delta_{1}=\{1\}
, so

.
Suppose (i) holds for

for some

.
Note that
\Delta=[0,1]
so

Thus,

Second, if the new point is contained in

3.2
Idealized jumping function

In this subsection we introduce the idealized jumping function and compare it with the usual one.

Definition 3.3
.

Recall Definition
2.21
. Let

D_{k}:=\#\left(\Delta\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}/k\right).

(32)

The

R

(33)

Similarly to (
14
),

i_{k,1}\geq\cdots\geq i_{k,D_{k}}.

(34)

Lemma 3.4
.

For
k\in{\mathbb{N}}(L)
and
\ell\in\{1,\ldots,d_{k}\},\;

j_{k,\ell}\left(v\right)\leq i_{k,\ell}\left(v\right).

Proof.

Recall the equality
[
45
, Lemma 1.4]
,

\#\left((\Delta^{t}_{v})_{k}\right)=V_{v,k}^{t},

(35)

and that the latter equals
{\mathcal{F}}_{v}^{kt}R_{k}
by
Definition
2.21
.
By (
35
) and Definition
3.3
, for any
a>i_{k,\ell}(v)

\dim{\mathcal{F}}_{v}^{a}R_{k}=\#\left(\Delta_{v,k}^{{a}/{k}}\right)\leq\#\left(\Delta_{v}^{{a}/{k}}\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}/k\right)<\ell.

By (

) (note the maximum is attained),

j_{k,\ell}\left(v\right)<a.

Hence,

j_{k,\ell}\left(v\right)\leq i_{k,\ell}\left(v\right).

∎

A reverse inequality holds for divisorial valuations:

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
v\in{\operatorname{Val}}_{X}^{\operatorname{div}}
. For
k\in{\mathbb{N}}(L)
and
\ell\in\{1,\ldots,d_{k}\},\;

j_{k,d_{k}+1-\ell}\left(v\right)\geq i_{k,D_{k}+1-\ell}\left(v\right).

Proof.

Follow the notation in the proof of Lemma
2.22

for the Okounkov body associated to the flag (
28
).
Recall Definition
3.3
. By slight abuse we will use interchangeably the same
notation for sequences and finite sets in
{\mathbb{R}}
. The non-decreasing sequence
\{i_{k,D_{k}+1-\ell}(v)\}_{\ell=1}^{D_{k}}
is equal to
p_{1}(k\Delta\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{n})

onto their first coordinate. Since the sequence of jumping numbers
\{j_{k,d_{k}+1-\ell}(v)\}_{\ell=1}^{d_{k}}
consists of the projection of (part of) the lattice points, namely of

(

), it
is a subsequence of

(by the monotone arrangement (

for

∎

3.3
Idealized expected vanishing

So far we have associated various quantities to the discrete

\Delta_{k}

In the spirit of §

quantities to the idealized discrete Okounkov bodies

Definition 3.6
.

The

is the function

Example 3.7
.

Let

be a smooth projective curve of genus

, and fix a point

, yielding the flag
C\supset\{P\}
. Let

be a line bundle over

with

, and

[
45
, Example 1.3]
. For a very general choice of

(36)

On

(37)

Proof.

By Definition

\displaystyle S_{k,m_{k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{km_{k}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{m_{k}}j_{k,\ell}=\frac{1}{km_{k}}\sum_{\ell=d_{k}+1-m_{k}}^{d_{k}}j_{k,d_{k}+1-\ell}

4.1
Valuative characterization of Grassmannian stability thresholds

First, a straightforward generalization of the valuative

(

\delta_{k}
(
m=d_{k}
)

[

, Propositions 4.1 and 4.3]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

For
k\in{\mathbb{N}}(L)
and
m\in\{1,\ldots,d_{k}\}
,

\displaystyle\delta_{k,m}=\inf_{v\in{\operatorname{Val}}_{X}^{\operatorname{div}}}{A}/{S_{k,m}}=\inf_{v\in{\operatorname{Val}}_{X}^{\operatorname{fin}}}{A}/{S_{k,m}}.

Proof.

By (
1
) and Theorem
2.7
,

\displaystyle\delta_{k,m}

\displaystyle=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\left\{s_{\ell}\right\}_{\ell=1}^{m}\subset R_{k}\\
\mbox{\smlsev linearly independent}\end{subarray}}km\mathrm{lct}\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\left(s_{\ell}\right)\right)=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\left\{s_{\ell}\right\}_{\ell=1}^{m}\subset R_{k}\\
\mbox{\smlsev linearly independent}\end{subarray}}\inf_{v\in{\operatorname{Val}}_{X}^{\operatorname{div}}}km\frac{A\left(v\right)}{v\left(\sum\limits_{\ell=1}^{m}\left(s_{\ell}\right)\right)}

\displaystyle=\inf_{v\in{\operatorname{Val}}_{X}^{\operatorname{div}}}km\frac{A\left(v\right)}{\sup\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\left\{s_{\ell}\right\}_{\ell=1}^{m}\subset R_{k}\\
\mbox{\smlsev linearly independent}\end{subarray}}v\left(\sum\limits_{\ell=1}^{m}\left(s_{\ell}\right)\right)}=\inf_{v\in{\operatorname{Val}}_{X}^{\operatorname{div}}}\frac{A\left(v\right)}{S_{k,m}\left(v\right)}.

The same argument applies to

.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

For
k\in{\mathbb{N}}(L)
,
\alpha_{k}=\delta_{k,1}
is computed by a divisorial valuation, i.e.,

\delta_{k,1}=\frac{A(v)}{S_{k,1}(v)}\quad\hbox{for some $v\in{\operatorname{Val}}_{X}^{\operatorname{div}}$}.

Proof.

By (
1
),

\delta_{k,1}=\inf_{s\in R_{k}}k\mathrm{lct}\left(s\right).

By the lower semi-continuity of complex singularity exponents
[

such that

\delta_{k,1}=k\mathrm{lct}\left(s\right).

By Theorem
2.7
, there is

such that

\mathrm{lct}\left(s\right)={A\left(v\right)}/{v\left(s\right)}.

By Lemma

v\left(s\right)\leq kS_{k,1}\left(v\right).

Combining these four equations,

\frac{A\left(v\right)}{S_{k,1}\left(v\right)}\leq\delta_{k,1}.

By Lemma
4.1

∎

4.2
End-point convergence cases

In this subsection we prove Theorem

.

.

The

The
basis log canonical threshold (
\delta
-invariant,
or

\delta:=\lim_{k\to\infty}\delta_{k}

(the limit exists
[
8
, Theorem A]
), where

\delta_{k}=kd_{k}\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\left\{s_{\ell}\right\}_{\ell=1}^{d_{k}}\\
\mbox{\smlsev basis of }R_{k}\end{subarray}}\mathrm{lct}\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^{d_{k}}\left(s\right)\right).

The next result generalizes a theorem of Demailly
[
18
, Theorem A.3]
and Shi
[
56
, Theorem 2.2]
.

Theorem 4.4
.

Suppose
m_{k}\in\{1,\ldots,d_{k}\}
for
k\in{\mathbb{N}}(L)
.
If
m_{k}=o(k^{n})
, then

\lim_{k\to\infty}\delta_{k,m_{k}}=\inf_{k}\delta_{k,m_{k}}=\alpha.

If

m_{k}=d_{k}-o(k^{n})
,
then
\lim_{k\to\infty}\delta_{k,m_{k}}=\delta.

The proof relies on Proposition
4.6
and Lemma

4.5
proved below.

Proof.

For a sequence
\{m_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}(L)}
such that
1\leq m_{k}=o(k^{n})
, we rely on to Proposition
4.6
and its proof. Namely, Lemma
4.1
implies that for any fixed
v_{0}\in{\operatorname{Val}}_{X}^{\operatorname{fin}}
,

\limsup_{k\to\infty}\delta_{k,m_{k}}=\limsup_{k\to\infty}\inf_{v\in\operatorname{Val}_{X}}\frac{A\left(v\right)}{S_{k,m_{k}}\left(v\right)}\leq\limsup_{k\to\infty}\frac{A\left(v_{0}\right)}{S_{k,m_{k}}\left(v_{0}\right)}=\frac{A\left(v_{0}\right)}{\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}S_{k,m_{k}}\left(v_{0}\right)}=\frac{A\left(v_{0}\right)}{{\mathcal{S}}_{0}\left(v_{0}\right)}.

Since
v_{0}
is arbitrary, by Remark
2.12
and Lemma
4.1
(see also
[
8
, Theorem C]
),

\limsup_{k\to\infty}\delta_{k,m_{k}}\leq\inf_{v\in{\operatorname{Val}}_{X}^{\operatorname{fin}}}\frac{A\left(v\right)}{{\mathcal{S}}_{0}\left(v\right)}=\alpha.

On the other hand, by Lemma
4.1
, (
18
), and Remark
2.12
,

\delta_{k,m_{k}}=\inf_{v\in{\operatorname{Val}}_{X}^{\operatorname{fin}}}\frac{A\left(v\right)}{S_{k,m_{k}}\left(v\right)}\geq\inf_{v\in{\operatorname{Val}}_{X}^{\operatorname{fin}}}\frac{A\left(v\right)}{{\mathcal{S}}_{0}\left(v\right)}=\alpha.

In conclusion,

\limsup_{k\to\infty}\delta_{k,m_{k}}\leq\alpha\leq\inf_{k}\delta_{k,m_{k}}\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\delta_{k,m_{k}}.

For a sequence
\{m_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}(L)}
such that

, by (
39
),

\frac{m_{k}}{d_{k}}\delta_{k}\leq\delta_{k,m_{k}}\leq\delta_{k}.

By Definition
4.3
this completes the proof.
∎

.

For any sequence
\{m_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}(L)}

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}S_{k,m_{k}}={\mathcal{S}}_{1}.

By (

S_{k,d_{k}}\leq S_{k,m_{k}}=\frac{1}{km_{k}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{m_{k}}j_{k,\ell}\leq\frac{1}{km_{k}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{d_{k}}j_{k,\ell}=\frac{d_{k}}{m_{k}}S_{k,d_{k}}.

(39)

Taking the limit in

completes the proof.
∎

Proposition 4.6
.

For any sequence
\{m_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}(L)}

\lim_{k\to\infty}S_{k,m_{k}}=\sup_{k}S_{k,m_{k}}={\mathcal{S}}_{0}.

For

k_{1}

\displaystyle m_{k}\leq\frac{\varepsilon^{n}\mathrm{Vol}\,L}{2n!}k^{n}.

By Claim

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{n!}{k^{n}}\dim{\mathcal{F}}_{v}^{k\left(1-\varepsilon\right){\mathcal{S}}_{0}\left(v\right)}R_{k}=\mathrm{Vol}\,V_{v,\bullet}^{\left(1-\varepsilon\right){\mathcal{S}}_{0}\left(v\right)}\geq\varepsilon^{n}\mathrm{Vol}\,L.

Hence there is

\dim{\mathcal{F}}_{v}^{k\left(1-\varepsilon\right){\mathcal{S}}_{0}\left(v\right)}R_{k}\geq\frac{\varepsilon^{n}\mathrm{Vol}\,L}{2n!}k^{n}.

(In particular,

By (
18
),

S_{k,m_{k}}\left(v\right)=\frac{1}{km_{k}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{m_{k}}j_{k,\ell}\left(v\right)\geq\frac{1}{k}j_{k,m_{k}}\left(v\right)\geq\left(1-\varepsilon\right){\mathcal{S}}_{0}\left(v\right).

Since
\varepsilon
is arbitrary,

\liminf_{k\to\infty}S_{k,m_{k}}\left(v\right)\geq{\mathcal{S}}_{0}\left(v\right).

(40)

On the other hand, by (
15
) and (
18
),

S_{k,m_{k}}\left(v\right)\leq S_{k,1}\left(v\right)\leq{\mathcal{S}}_{0}\left(v\right).

(41)

Combining (
40
)–(
41
),

\limsup_{k\to\infty}S_{k,m_{k}}\left(v\right)\leq\sup_{k}S_{k,m_{k}}\left(v\right)\leq{\mathcal{S}}_{0}\left(v\right)\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}S_{k,m_{k}}\left(v\right).

∎

4.3
Joint monotonicity

This subsection collects basic monotonicity properties of
S_{k,m}
and
\delta_{k,m}
.

Lemma 4.7
.

For
k,k^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{N}}(L)
,

\left(k+k^{\prime}\right)S_{k+k^{\prime},m}\geq kS_{k,m}+k^{\prime}S_{k^{\prime},1}.

Proof.

For
v\in\operatorname{Val}_{X}
, let
s\in H^{0}\left(X,k^{\prime}L\right)
be such that
v(s)=k^{\prime}S_{k^{\prime},1}\left(v\right)
(recall Lemma
2.13
). The map

{\mathcal{F}}_{v}^{\lambda}R_{k}\xrightarrow{\otimes s}{\mathcal{F}}_{v}^{\lambda+k^{\prime}S_{k^{\prime},1}\left(v\right)}R_{k+k^{\prime}}

is an injection. In particular,

\displaystyle j_{k,\ell}\left(v\right)

\displaystyle=\max\left\{\lambda\,:\,\dim{\mathcal{F}}_{v}^{\lambda}R_{k}\geq\ell\right\}\leq\max\left\{\lambda\,:\,\dim{\mathcal{F}}_{v}^{\lambda+k^{\prime}S_{k^{\prime},1}\left(v\right)}R_{k+k^{\prime}}\geq\ell\right\}

\displaystyle=\max\left\{\lambda\,:\,\dim{\mathcal{F}}_{v}^{\lambda}R_{k+k^{\prime}}\geq\ell\right\}-k^{\prime}S_{k^{\prime},1}\left(v\right)=j_{k+k^{\prime},\ell}\left(v\right)-k^{\prime}S_{k^{\prime},1}\left(v\right).

It follows that

S_{k,m}=\frac{1}{km}\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}j_{k,\ell}\leq\frac{1}{km}\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}j_{k+k^{\prime},\ell}-\frac{k^{\prime}}{k}S_{k^{\prime},1}=\frac{k+k^{\prime}}{k}S_{k+k^{\prime},m}-\frac{k^{\prime}}{k}S_{k^{\prime},1}.

∎

Lemma 4.8
.

For
k\in{\mathbb{N}}(L)
,
m\in\{1,\ldots,d_{k}\}
, and
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

S_{k,m}\leq S_{\ell k,m}.

Proof.

Let
k_{\ell}=(\ell-1)k
. Notice that for any
s\in R_{k}
,

s^{\otimes(\ell-1)}\in R_{k_{\ell}}
. In particular, for
v\in\operatorname{Val}_{X}
,

\max_{s\in R_{k_{\ell}}}v\left(s\right)\geq\left(\ell-1\right)\max_{s\in R_{k}}v\left(s\right).

By (
18
),

S_{k,m}\left(v\right)\leq S_{k,1}\left(v\right)=\frac{1}{k}\max_{s\in R_{k}}v\left(s\right)\leq\frac{1}{k_{\ell}}\max_{s\in R_{k_{\ell}}}v\left(s\right)=S_{k_{\ell},1}\left(v\right).

By Lemma
4.7
and (
18
),

S_{k,m}\left(v\right)\leq\frac{k+k_{\ell}}{k}S_{k+k_{\ell},m}\left(v\right)-\frac{k_{\ell}}{k}S_{k_{\ell},1}\left(v\right)\leq\frac{k+k_{\ell}}{k}S_{k+k_{\ell},m}\left(v\right)-\frac{k_{\ell}}{k}S_{k,m}\left(v\right),

i.e.,

S_{k,m}\left(v\right)\leq S_{k+k_{\ell},m}\left(v\right).

∎

Lemma
4.8
together with
Lemma
4.1
and (
18
)
imply:

Corollary 4.9
.

Let
k\in{\mathbb{N}}(L)
,
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
m,m^{\prime}\in\{1,\ldots,d_{k}\}
with
m\leq m^{\prime}
.
Then

\delta_{k,m}\leq\delta_{k,m^{\prime}},

and

\delta_{\ell k,m}\leq\delta_{k,m}.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.1
and (
18
),

\displaystyle\delta_{k,m}=\inf_{v\in\operatorname{Val}_{X}}\frac{A\left(v\right)}{S_{k,m}\left(v\right)}\leq\inf_{v\in\operatorname{Val}_{X}}\frac{A\left(v\right)}{S_{k,m^{\prime}}\left(v\right)}=\delta_{k,m^{\prime}}.

By Lemma
4.8
and Lemma
4.1
,

\displaystyle\delta_{\ell k,m}=\inf_{v\in\operatorname{Val}_{X}}\frac{A\left(v\right)}{S_{\ell k,m}\left(v\right)}\leq\inf_{v\in\operatorname{Val}_{X}}\frac{A\left(v\right)}{S_{k,m}\left(v\right)}=\delta_{k,m}.

∎

Corollary 4.10
.

Recall Definition
4.3
. Suppose
\alpha=\alpha_{\ell}
for some
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}(L)
. Then

0\leq\alpha_{k}-\alpha=O\left(1/k\right),k\in{\mathbb{N}}(L).

Proof.

By Lemma
4.2
, there is
w\in{\operatorname{Val}}_{X}^{\operatorname{div}}
such that

\alpha_{\ell}=\frac{A\left(w\right)}{S_{\ell,1}\left(w\right)}.

By Remark
2.12
and Lemma
4.1
,

\alpha=\inf_{k}\alpha_{k}=\inf_{v\in{\operatorname{Val}}_{X}^{\operatorname{div}}}\frac{A\left(v\right)}{{\mathcal{S}}_{0}\left(v\right)}.

Recall (
15
). Combining,

\alpha_{\ell}=\frac{A\left(w\right)}{S_{\ell,1}\left(w\right)}\geq\frac{A\left(w\right)}{{\mathcal{S}}_{0}\left(w\right)}\geq\inf_{v\in{\operatorname{Val}}_{X}^{\operatorname{div}}}\frac{A\left(v\right)}{{\mathcal{S}}_{0}\left(v\right)}=\alpha.

Since
\alpha_{\ell}=\alpha
,

S_{\ell,1}\left(w\right)={\mathcal{S}}_{0}\left(w\right),

(42)

and

\alpha=\frac{A\left(w\right)}{{\mathcal{S}}_{0}\left(w\right)}.

(43)

Pick
\ell_{0}
sufficiently large so that for any
k\geq\ell_{0}
,
k\in{\mathbb{N}}(L)
. Using Euclidean division, for
k\geq\ell_{0}
,

k-\ell_{0}=q\left(k\right)\ell+r\left(k\right),

where
q(k)\geq 0
and
0\leq r(k)<\ell
. By Remark
2.12
, Lemma
4.8
(with
m=1
), and (
42
),

\displaystyle kS_{k,1}\left(w\right)

\displaystyle\geq q\left(k\right)\ell S_{q\left(k\right)\ell,1}\left(w\right)+\left(\ell_{0}+r\left(k\right)\right)S_{\ell_{0}+r\left(k\right),1}\left(w\right)

\displaystyle\geq q\left(k\right)\ell S_{\ell,1}\left(w\right)+\left(\ell_{0}+r\left(k\right)\right)S_{\ell_{0}+r\left(k\right),1}\left(w\right)

\displaystyle\geq q\left(k\right)\ell S_{\ell,1}\left(w\right)=\left(k-r\left(k\right)-\ell_{0}\right){\mathcal{S}}_{0}\left(w\right)>\left(k-\ell-\ell_{0}\right){\mathcal{S}}_{0}\left(w\right).

By (
43
), for
k>\ell+\ell_{0}
,

\alpha_{k}\leq\frac{A\left(w\right)}{S_{k,1}\left(w\right)}<\frac{k}{k-\ell-\ell_{0}}\frac{A\left(w\right)}{{\mathcal{S}}_{0}\left(w\right)}=\frac{k}{k-\ell-\ell_{0}}\alpha,

i.e.,

\displaystyle\alpha_{k}-\alpha<\frac{\ell+\ell_{0}}{k-\ell-\ell_{0}}\alpha=O\left(k^{-1}\right).

∎

4.4
The ccdf, quantile, and tail distribution associated to a valuation

In this subsection we introduce the ccdf, quantile, and tail distribution associated to a valuation.

Definition 4.11
.

Let
v\in{\operatorname{Val}}_{X}^{\operatorname{fin}}
.
The
complementary cumulative distribution function (ccdf)

of
v
is (recall (
23
))

F_{v}(t):=\mu_{v}\big([t,{\mathcal{S}}_{0}(v)]\big),\quad t\in[0,{\mathcal{S}}_{0}(v)].

The
quantile function associated to
v
is its
generalized inverse

Q_{v}:=\sup F_{v}^{-1},\quad\hbox{\ on }[0,1],

where
F_{v}^{-1}
is a set-valued map and

Q_{v}(\tau)

equals the supremum of the set
F_{v}^{-1}(\tau)\subset{\mathbb{R}}
.
The
\tau
-tail distribution associated to
v
is

\bm{1}_{[Q_{v}(\tau),{\mathcal{S}}_{0}(v)]}\mu_{v}
.

The barycenter of a measure
\nu
on
{\mathbb{R}}
is

b\mskip 1.0mu(\nu):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}td\nu(t)\big/\int_{\mathbb{R}}d\nu.

Proposition 4.12
.

Suppose
m_{k}\in\{1,\ldots,d_{k}\}
for
k\in{\mathbb{N}}(L)
,
and

\lim_{k\to\infty}{m_{k}}/{d_{k}}=\tau\in\left[0,1\right].

The limit

{\mathcal{S}}_{\tau}:=\lim_{k\to\infty}S_{k,m_{k}}

exists on
{\operatorname{Val}}_{X}^{\operatorname{fin}}
and is independent of the choice of
\{m_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}(L)}
. Moreover,
{\mathcal{S}}_{\tau}

is the barycenter of the
\tau
-tail distribution of
v
(recall (
23
)
and Definition
4.11
),

{\mathcal{S}}_{\tau}=b\mskip 1.0mu\left(\bm{1}_{[Q_{v}(\tau),{\mathcal{S}}_{0}(v)]}\mu_{v}\right).

In particular,
{\mathcal{S}}_{\tau}
is non-increasing with respect to
\tau
.

The key for the proof of Proposition
4.12
is to associate quantum analogues of the ccdf and tail distribution to
v

and prove some asymptotic estimates for them that guarantee that the quantum barycenters converge to the classical ones.

Definition 4.13
.

Let
v\in{\operatorname{Val}}_{X}^{\operatorname{fin}}
,
k\in{\mathbb{N}}(L)
and
t\in[0,S_{k,1}(v)]
.
The
k
-th quantum ccdf of
v
is (recall (
22
))

F_{v,k}(t):=\mu_{v,k}\big([t,S_{k,1}(v)]\big)\in\{j/d_{k}\}_{j=0}^{d_{k}},\quad t\in[0,S_{k,1}(v)].

The
k
-th quantum quantile function of
v
is its inverse

Q_{v,k}(\tau):=\sup F_{v,k}^{-1}\left(\lfloor\tau d_{k}\rfloor/d_{k}\right),\quad\hbox{\ on }[0,1].

The
k
-th quantum
\tau
-tail distribution associated to
v
is

\bm{1}_{[Q_{v,k}(\tau),S_{k,1}(v)]}\mu_{v,k}
.

Note that
by Theorem
2.18
and Definition
4.11
,

\lim_{k}F_{v,k}=F_{v},

and hence also

\lim_{k}Q_{v,k}=Q_{v}.

In fact, there is a useful expression for the quantum ccdf:

Lemma 4.14
.

Let
v\in{\operatorname{Val}}_{X}^{\operatorname{fin}}
,
k\in{\mathbb{N}}(L)
and
t\in[0,S_{k,1}(v)]
.
Then,
\displaystyle F_{v,k}(t)=\frac{1}{d_{k}}\dim{\mathcal{F}}_{v}^{kt}R_{k}
.

Set

\iota_{v,k}\left(t\right):=\left\{1\leq\ell\leq d_{k}\,:\,j_{k,\ell}\left(v\right)\geq kt\right\}.

(44)

Proof.

First, we record a useful expression for
\iota_{v,k}
(
44
).
By (
13
),
j_{k,\ell}(v)\geq kt
if and only if
\dim{\mathcal{F}}_{v}^{kt}R_{k}\geq\ell
. Therefore (recall (
44
)),

\displaystyle\iota_{v,k}\left(t\right)

\displaystyle=\left\{1\leq\ell\leq d_{k}\,:\,j_{k,\ell}\left(v\right)\geq kt\right\}=\left\{1\leq\ell\leq d_{k}\,:\,\dim{\mathcal{F}}_{v}^{kt}R_{k}\geq\ell\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{1,\ldots,\dim{\mathcal{F}}_{v}^{kt}R_{k}\right\}.

(45)

Now, note that by Definition
4.11
and (
44
),

F_{v,k}=\frac{1}{d_{k}}\max\iota_{v,k}=\frac{1}{d_{k}}\#\left(\iota_{v,k}\right),

(46)

and the lemma follows.
∎

Towards the proof of Proposition
4.12
we prove first a
quantum analogue:

Lemma 4.15
.

For
k\in{\mathbb{N}}(L),m_{k}\in d_{k}F_{v,k}([0,S_{k,1}(v)])
,

S_{k,m_{k}}\left(v\right)=b\mskip 1.0mu\left(\bm{1}_{\left[Q_{v,k}(m_{k}/d_{k}),S_{k,1}\left(v\right)\right]}\mu_{v,k}\right).

Proof.

By (
46
),
the statement is equivalent to showing that

S_{k,\#\left(\iota_{v,k}\left(s\right)\right)}\left(v\right)=b\mskip 1.0mu\left(\bm{1}_{\left[s,{\mathcal{S}}_{0}\left(v\right)\right]}\mu_{v,k}\right),\quad\hbox{\ for $s\in[0,S_{k,1}(v)]\subset[0,{\mathcal{S}}_{0}(v)]$}.

(47)

By
(
22
) and (
44
),

\int_{\left[s,{\mathcal{S}}_{0}\left(v\right)\right]}td\mu_{v,k}=\frac{1}{kd_{k}}\sum_{\ell\in\iota_{v,k}\left(s\right)}j_{k,\ell}\left(v\right),

and by (
46
)

\int_{\left[s,{\mathcal{S}}_{0}\left(v\right)\right]}d\mu_{v,k}=F_{v,k}(s)=\frac{1}{d_{k}}\#\left(\iota_{v,k}\left(s\right)\right).

Thus,

S_{k,\#\left(\iota_{v,k}\left(s\right)\right)}\left(v\right)=\frac{1}{k\#\left(\iota_{v,k}\left(s\right)\right)}\sum_{\ell\in\iota_{v,k}\left(s\right)}j_{k,\ell}\left(v\right)=\frac{\int_{\left[s,{\mathcal{S}}_{0}\left(v\right)\right]}td\mu_{v,k}}{\int_{\left[s,{\mathcal{S}}_{0}\left(v\right)\right]}d\mu_{v,k}},

(48)

proving (
47
).
∎

4.5
General double limits: tail expectations

We are now in position to prove Proposition
4.12
.

Proof of Proposition
4.12
.

When
\tau=0,1
, this is Lemma
4.5
and Proposition
4.6
.
Thus, suppose
m_{k}\in\{1,\ldots,d_{k}\}
for
k\in{\mathbb{N}}(L)
,
and

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{m_{k}}{d_{k}}=\tau\in\left(0,1\right).

Note that (recall (
23
) and Definition
4.11
)

\displaystyle Q_{v}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\sup\left\{t\leq{\mathcal{S}}_{0}\left(v\right)\,:\,\mu_{v}\left(\left[t,{\mathcal{S}}_{0}\left(v\right)\right]\right)\geq\tau\right\}

(49)

\displaystyle=\sup\left\{t\leq{\mathcal{S}}_{0}\left(v\right)\,:\,\mathrm{Vol}\,V_{v,\bullet}^{t}\geq\tau\mathrm{Vol}\,L\right\}\in\left(0,{\mathcal{S}}_{0}\left(v\right)\right].

(50)

Lemma 4.16
.

For
\tau\in[0,1)
and
t\in[0,Q_{v}(\tau))
,
\mathrm{Vol}\,V_{v,\bullet}^{t}>\tau\mathrm{Vol}\,L
.

Proof.

Fix
\tau\in[0,1)
and
t\in[0,Q_{v}(\tau))
, and suppose

\mathrm{Vol}\,V_{v,\bullet}^{t}\leq\tau\mathrm{Vol}\,L.

(51)

Pick

s\in\left(t,Q_{v}\left(\tau\right)\right).

(52)

By Claim
2.20
(whose assumptions are fulfilled by (
51
)
as
\tau<1
),

\mathrm{Vol}\,V_{v,\bullet}^{s}<\mathrm{Vol}\,V_{v,\bullet}^{t}\leq\tau\mathrm{Vol}\,L.

By (
50
),
Q_{v}(\tau)\leq s
, contradicting (
52
).
∎

By (
21
) and Lemma
4.16
,

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{k^{n}}\dim{\mathcal{F}}_{v}^{k\left(Q_{v}\left(\tau\right)-\varepsilon\right)}R_{k}=\frac{1}{n!}\mathrm{Vol}\,V_{v,\bullet}^{Q_{v}\left(\tau\right)-\varepsilon}>\frac{\tau{\mathrm{Vol}\,L}}{n!}=\frac{\mathrm{Vol}\,L}{n!}\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{m_{k}}{d_{k}}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{m_{k}}{k^{n}},\quad\hbox{for any $\varepsilon\in(0,Q_{v}(\tau)]$}.

Therefore,

m_{k}\leq\dim{\mathcal{F}}_{v}^{k\left(Q_{v}\left(\tau\right)-\varepsilon\right)}R_{k},\quad\hbox{\ for sufficiently large $k$}.

By (
45
),

\displaystyle\#\Big(\iota_{v,k}\big(Q_{v}(\tau)-\varepsilon\big)\Big)

\displaystyle=\#\left\{\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}\,:\,1\leq\ell\leq\dim{\mathcal{F}}_{v}^{k\left(Q_{v}\left(\tau\right)-\varepsilon\right)}R_{k}\right\}

\displaystyle=\dim{\mathcal{F}}_{v}^{k\left(Q_{v}\left(\tau\right)-\varepsilon\right)}R_{k}\geq m_{k}.

By (
18
), (
48
), and Theorem
2.18
,

\displaystyle\liminf_{k\to\infty}S_{k,m_{k}}\left(v\right)

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{k\to\infty}S_{k,\#\left(\iota_{v,k}\left(Q_{v}\left(\tau\right)-\varepsilon\right)\right)}\left(v\right)

\displaystyle=\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{\int_{\left[Q_{v}\left(\tau\right)-\varepsilon,{\mathcal{S}}_{0}\left(v\right)\right]}td\mu_{v,k}\left(t\right)}{\int_{\left[Q_{v}\left(\tau\right)-\varepsilon,{\mathcal{S}}_{0}\left(v\right)\right]}d\mu_{v,k}\left(t\right)}=\frac{\int_{\left[Q_{v}\left(\tau\right)-\varepsilon,{\mathcal{S}}_{0}\left(v\right)\right]}td\mu_{v}\left(t\right)}{\int_{\left[Q_{v}\left(\tau\right)-\varepsilon,{\mathcal{S}}_{0}\left(v\right)\right]}d\mu_{v}\left(t\right)}.

Since
\varepsilon\in(0,Q_{v}(\tau)]
is arbitrary,

\liminf_{k\to\infty}S_{k,m_{k}}\left(v\right)\geq\frac{\int_{\left[Q_{v}\left(\tau\right),{\mathcal{S}}_{0}\left(v\right)\right]}td\mu_{v}\left(t\right)}{\int_{\left[Q_{v}\left(\tau\right),{\mathcal{S}}_{0}\left(v\right)\right]}d\mu_{v}\left(t\right)}.

(53)

Now, there are two cases to consider.

First, suppose
\tau\leq\mu_{v}(\{{\mathcal{S}}_{0}(v)\})
. By (
49
),
Q_{v}(\tau)={\mathcal{S}}_{0}(v)
. By (
53
) (and as
\tau>0
),

\liminf_{k\to\infty}S_{k,m_{k}}\left(v\right)\geq\frac{\int_{\left\{{\mathcal{S}}_{0}\left(v\right)\right\}}td\mu_{v}\left(t\right)}{\int_{\left\{{\mathcal{S}}_{0}\left(v\right)\right\}}d\mu_{v}\left(t\right)}={\mathcal{S}}_{0}\left(v\right).

By (
15
) and (
18
),

S_{k,m_{k}}(v)\leq S_{k,1}(v)\leq{\mathcal{S}}_{0}(v).

Altogether,

\lim_{k\to\infty}S_{k,m_{k}}\left(v\right)={\mathcal{S}}_{0}\left(v\right).

Second, suppose
\tau>\mu_{v}(\{{\mathcal{S}}_{0}(v)\})
. By (
24
), there exists
s<{\mathcal{S}}_{0}(v)
such that

\mathrm{Vol}\,V_{v,\bullet}^{s}<\tau\mathrm{Vol}\,L.

By (
50
),
Q_{v}(\tau)\leq s<{\mathcal{S}}_{0}(v)
. Again by (
50
), for
\varepsilon\in(0,{\mathcal{S}}_{0}(v)-Q_{v}(\tau))
,
\mathrm{Vol}\,V_{v,\bullet}^{Q_{v}(\tau)+\varepsilon}<\tau\mathrm{Vol}\,L
, so,

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{d_{k}}\dim{\mathcal{F}}_{v}^{k\left(Q_{v}\left(\tau\right)+\varepsilon\right)}R_{k}=\frac{\mathrm{Vol}\,V_{v,\bullet}^{Q_{v}\left(\tau\right)+\varepsilon}}{\mathrm{Vol}\,L}<{\tau}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{m_{k}}{d_{k}}.

Therefore for sufficiently large
k
,

\dim{\mathcal{F}}_{v}^{k\left(Q_{v}\left(\tau\right)+\varepsilon\right)}R_{k}\leq m_{k}.

By (
45
),

\displaystyle\#\Big(\iota_{v,k}\big(Q_{v}\left(\tau\right)+\varepsilon\big)\Big)=\dim{\mathcal{F}}_{v}^{k\left(Q_{v}\left(\tau\right)+\varepsilon\right)}R_{k}\leq m_{k}.

By (
18
) and (
48
),

\displaystyle\limsup_{k\to\infty}S_{k,m_{k}}\left(v\right)

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{k\to\infty}S_{k,\#\left(\iota_{v,k}\left(Q_{v}\left(\tau\right)+\varepsilon\right)\right)}\left(v\right)

\displaystyle=\limsup_{k\to\infty}\frac{\int_{\left[Q_{v}\left(\tau\right)+\varepsilon,{\mathcal{S}}_{0}\left(v\right)\right]}td\mu_{v,k}\left(t\right)}{\int_{\left[Q_{v}\left(\tau\right)+\varepsilon,{\mathcal{S}}_{0}\left(v\right)\right]}d\mu_{v,k}\left(t\right)}=\frac{\int_{\left[Q_{v}\left(\tau\right)+\varepsilon,{\mathcal{S}}_{0}\left(v\right)\right]}td\mu_{v}\left(t\right)}{\int_{\left[Q_{v}\left(\tau\right)+\varepsilon,{\mathcal{S}}_{0}\left(v\right)\right]}d\mu_{v}\left(t\right)}.

Since
\varepsilon
is arbitrary,

\limsup_{k\to\infty}S_{k,m_{k}}\left(v\right)\leq\frac{\int_{\left[Q_{v}\left(\tau\right),{\mathcal{S}}_{0}\left(v\right)\right]}td\mu_{v}\left(t\right)}{\int_{\left[Q_{v}\left(\tau\right),{\mathcal{S}}_{0}\left(v\right)\right]}d\mu_{v}\left(t\right)}.

Combining with (
53
),

\lim_{k\to\infty}S_{k,m_{k}}\left(v\right)=\frac{\int_{\left[Q_{v}\left(\tau\right),{\mathcal{S}}_{0}\left(v\right)\right]}td\mu_{v}\left(t\right)}{\int_{\left[Q_{v}\left(\tau\right),{\mathcal{S}}_{0}\left(v\right)\right]}d\mu_{v}\left(t\right)}

also in this case.
Finally, monotonicity of
{\mathcal{S}}_{\tau}
follows from (
18
),
concluding the proof of Proposition
4.12
.
∎

Remark 4.17
.

4.6
Uniform estimates on the Ehrhart function in convex sub-bodies

In this subsection we prove estimates on lattice points in a convex body
K
.
Specifically, we are interested to show estimates that
hold
uniformly for any convex body inside
K
and simultaneously
for any integer dilation of
K
.
Lemma
4.19

can be thought of as a one-sided generalized Euler–Maclaurin type formula
(a typical Euler-Maclaurin formula applies to a
fixed
body

[
43
,
52
]
).
Both Lemma
4.19

and Proposition
4.20
can be thought of
as
uniform
versions of estimates on the

discrepancy
(also called
rest
) or

Ehrhart function
. These estimates seem to be new even in the case
of convex lattice polytopes, though in that case one can
derive much more refined estimates using Ehrhart theory
[
40
]
.

The main result of this subsection is:

Theorem 4.18
.

For any convex body
K\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
\nu\in(0,|K|]
, there exists
C(\nu,K)>0
such that for any convex body

Lemma 5.4
.

For
t\in[a,b)
,

\frac{b-a}{b-t}{\mathcal{C}}_{v}^{a,b,V}(t)

is a translation of
{\mathcal{C}}_{v}^{a,b,V}
.

Proof.

The cones
{\mathcal{C}}_{v}^{a,b,V}(t)
all have the same tip
V
and by construction
are all similar. More precisely,

{\mathcal{C}}_{v}^{a,b,V}(t)-V=\frac{b-t}{b-a}\left({\mathcal{C}}_{v}^{a,b,V}-V\right).

I.e.,

\displaystyle\frac{b-a}{b-t}{\mathcal{C}}_{v}^{a,b,V}(t)={\mathcal{C}}_{v}^{a,b,V}+\frac{t-a}{b-t}V.

∎

Claim 5.5
.

For a convex body
K
,
\#(K\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}/k)=\#(\frac{k}{\ell}K\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}/\ell)

Proof.

Observe that

\#(K\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}/k)=\#(\frac{k}{\ell}(K\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}/k))=\#(\frac{k}{\ell}K\cap\frac{k}{\ell}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}/k))
.
∎

Lemma 5.6
.

Suppose that
t\in[a,b-\frac{\ell}{k}(b-a)]
. Then,

\#\left({\mathcal{C}}_{v}^{a,b,V}(t)\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}/k\right)\geq\min_{x\in\left[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right]^{n}}\#\left(({\mathcal{C}}_{v}^{a,b,V}+x)\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}/\ell\right).

Proof.

By inclusion, it suffices to prove the statement for
t=b-\frac{\ell}{k}(b-a)
.
By Claim
5.5
, the number of

{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}/k
lattice
points of
{\mathcal{C}}_{v}^{a,b,V}(t)
coincides with the number
of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}/\ell
lattice

\frac{k}{\ell}{\mathcal{C}}_{v}^{a,b,V}(t)
.
In turn, Lemma
5.4
identifies the latter cone
with a translation of
{\mathcal{C}}_{v}^{a,b,V}
. Thus,

\#\left({\mathcal{C}}_{v}^{a,b,V}(t)\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}/k\right)\geq\min_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\#\left(({\mathcal{C}}_{v}^{a,b,V}+x)\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}/\ell\right).

The result follows since
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}/\ell

∎

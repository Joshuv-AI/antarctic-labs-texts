Delta operation at height 2
Noam Nissan

Abstract
We compute the delta power operation for morava E-theory of height 2 at the prime 3. The delta power operation was defined using the notion of higher semi additivity by Shachar Carmeli, Tomer M. Schlank and Lior Yanovski. We briefly survey the basic definitions in higher semi-additivity. Using explicit formulas for moduli spaces of elliptic curves and computations done by Y. Zhu for the total power operation we provide an explicit formula for the delta power operation. We obtain the numerical result that the delta operation of a polynomial is a polynomial which can be explained by a certain connection which was described by M. Hopkins, C. Rezk and later N. Stapleton between similar operations and the Hecke action on the functions on the moduli space of elliptic curves.

Introduction

In recent years, the notion of higher semiadditivity has become central in chromatic homotopy theory. The first nontrivial example of a higher semiadditive
\infty
-category is the category
\textbf{Sp}_{K(n)}
of
K(n)
-local spectra, as shown in
[
5
]
.

Let
k
be a field of characteristic
p
(we will consider the case
k=\mathbb{F}_{q}
), and let
G
be a formal group of height
n
over
k
. There are two important homotopy commutative spectra associated to this data, denoted
K(n)
and
E_{n}
. The ring
\pi_{0}(E_{n})
is local, and its residue field is canonically isomorphic to
\pi_{0}(K(n))=\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Both spectra are periodic. Explicitly,

\pi_{*}(E_{n})=\pi_{0}(E_{n})[u^{\pm 1}]\quad\text{and}\quad\pi_{*}(K(n))=\pi_{0}(K(n))[v_{n}^{\pm 1}].

Multiplication by
u
and
v_{n}
induces equivalences

E_{n}\cong\Sigma^{2}E_{n}\quad\text{and}\quad K(n)\cong\Sigma^{2(p^{n}-1)}K(n),

respectively.

This spectra are complex oriented. The most important feature of these spectra is their associated Quillen formal group. We have

\pi_{*}K(n)^{\mathbb{CP}^{\infty}}=\pi_{*}(K(n))[[t]]=\mathbb{F}_{q}[v_{n}^{\pm 1}][[t]]\quad\text{and}\quad\pi_{*}E_{n}^{\mathbb{CP}^{\infty}}=\pi_{*}(E_{n})[[t]].

Both carry Hopf algebra structures induced by the group structure on
\mathbb{CP}^{\infty}
. It follows that

\spf\pi_{0}K(n)^{\mathbb{CP}^{\infty}}\quad\text{and}\quad\spf\pi_{0}E_{n}^{\mathbb{CP}^{\infty}}

are formal groups. The formal group
\spf K(n)^{*}\mathbb{CP}^{\infty}
is the original formal group, while
\spf E_{n}^{*}\mathbb{CP}^{\infty}
is its universal deformation. The
K(n)
-local category
\textbf{Sp}_{K(n)}
is the full subcategory of spectra spanned by objects that receive no nonzero maps from
K(n)
-acyclic spectra.

Higher semiadditivity yields a notion of integration over
\pi
-finite spaces for
K(n)
-local spectra. Moreover, the
K(n)
-local category is
\infty
-semiadditively symmetric monoidal. This implies a distributivity relation between multiplication and integration on
K(n)
-local ring spectra. This distributivity follows from the fact that the (completed) tensor product on
\textbf{Sp}_{K(n)}
preserves colimits indexed by
\pi
-finite spaces, as well as all other colimits.

In
[
7
]
, Carmeli, Schlank, and Yanovski define a power operation for commutative ring spectra in
\infty
-semiadditively symmetric monoidal
\infty
-categories. They use this operation to show that the

-local category is also
\infty
-semiadditively symmetric monoidal. In Section
2

\delta

The goal of this project is to compute the
\delta

in the category

-local

In Sections

The first main result we use is Zhu’s computation in
[

The second tool is a special case of the computation of Barthel and Stapleton in

2
Ambidextirety and the definition of
\delta

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a category with small limits and colimits. We say that
\mathcal{C}
is
-2
semiadditive without any requirements.

Suppose we have a
-2
semiadditive category, then any object
X\in\mathcal{C}
is represented by a functor
\left\{*\right\}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}
.
We have a canonical map
\nm_{\left\{*\right\}}:X=\colim_{\left\{*\right\}}X\rightarrow\lim_{\left\{*\right\}}X=X
which is homotopic to the identity. We also have a canonical map, homotopic to the identity as well, given by the composition

\int_{\left\{*\right\}}\id_{X}:X\xrightarrow{\Delta}\lim_{\left\{*\right\}}X\xrightarrow{\nm_{\left\{*\right\}}^{-1}}\colim_{\left\{*\right\}}X\xrightarrow{\nabla}X.

If
\mathcal{C}
is
-2
semiadditive then there is a canonical map
\nm_{\emptyset}:\emptyset\rightarrow*
from the initial object to the terminal object obtained from their universal properties.
We say that
\mathcal{C}
is
-1
semiadditive if
\nm_{\emptyset}
is an isomorphism. Such a category is also occasionally called ”pointed” in the literature.

Suppose we have a
-1
semiadditive category
\mathcal{C}
. This allows to define a special morphism between any two objects
X,Y\in C
called the zero map, defined as

\int_{\emptyset}:X\rightarrow*\xrightarrow{\sim}\emptyset\rightarrow Y.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be
-1
and let
A
be a finite set. One defines for any
A
indexed collection of objects
X_{a}\in\mathcal{C}
for
a\in A
, a natural map
\coprod_{a\in A}X_{a}\rightarrow\prod_{a\in A}X_{a}
. Giving such a map is the same as giving a matrix indexed by
A^{2}
of morphisms
X_{a}\to X_{b}
for
(a,b)\in A^{2}
. For this matrix we take the identity matrix

\left(\delta^{i}_{j}\right)_{(i,j)\in A^{2}},\delta^{i}_{j}=\begin{cases}\int_{\left\{*\right\}}\id_{X}&i=j\\
\int_{\emptyset}&i\neq j\end{cases}

and obtain a map which we will call
\nm_{A}
.

We say that
\mathcal{C}
is
0
-semiadditive (or just semiadditive) if for any finite set
A
,
\nm_{A}
is a natural isomorphism. This enables one to define an integration map
\int_{A}
which takes a map
\phi:A\rightarrow\hom(X,Y)
and returns
\int_{A}\phi:X\rightarrow Y
defined by the composition

X\xrightarrow{\Delta}\prod_{a\in A}X\xrightarrow{\prod_{a\in A}\phi(a)}\prod_{a\in A}Y\xrightarrow{\nm^{-1}}\coprod_{a\in A}Y\xrightarrow{\nabla}Y.

This integration over finite sets endows the mapping spaces in
\mathcal{C}
with a structure of a commutative monoid where the unit is
\int_{\emptyset}
. This structure allows to define, given an action of a finite group
G
on an object
X\in\mathcal{C}
, a morphism
\nm_{BG}:X_{G}\rightarrow X^{G}
. A category where all norm maps
\nm_{BG}
are equivalances is called
1

This story is part of an inductive sequence of definitions one can make. Given an
(n-1)
-semiadditive category, one has integration over
(n-1)
-finite spaces, which allows one to define an
n
-norm map on
n
-finite spaces and then define a notion of an
n
-semiadditive category, proceeding ad infinitum. We will explicate on this in subsection §
2.1
.

Given a
1
semiadditive symmetric monoidal category
\mathcal{C}
, a commutative coalgebra
C
and a commutative algebra

one can define a natural transformation
\alpha:\pi_{0}\map(C,A)\rightarrow\pi_{0}\map(C,A)
which is dependent on both the algebraic structures on

and on the notion of higher semiadditivity of
\mathcal{C}
. Stating this definition and going over some of
\alpha

2.2
.

Another thing one can do with an
\infty
-semiadditive category is to define a notion of cardinality of
\pi
-finite spaces. Given a
\pi
-finite space
A
and an object
X\in\mathcal{C}
, one can define
X^{A}=\lim_{A}X,X_{A}=\colim_{A}X
. Then we get a natural transformation of the identity functor in
\mathcal{C}
by the composition
\left|A\right|_{X}=\int_{A}\id_{X}:X\rightarrow X^{A}\xrightarrow{\nm_{A}^{-1}}X_{A}\rightarrow X
. We call this natural transformation the

of

2.2
The
\alpha

\infty
-semiadditivity allows one to sum over
\pi
-finite families of morphisms. In algebra, we are often interested in algebras, and in particular in multiplication operations. A basic requirement is that multiplication distributes over addition.

In higher algebra, the standard setting for this compatibility is a commutative algebra object in a symmetric monoidal stable category whose tensor product preserves colimits in each variable.

In the previous chapter, we defined a notion of integration over
\pi
-finite spaces. It is natural to seek a notion of algebra in which multiplication distributes over these integration maps, in the sense that

b\cdot\int_{A}a=\int_{A}b\cdot a.

This compatibility is formalized in the following definition.

Definition 2.2.1
.

An
m
-semiadditively symmetric monoidal category
(\mathcal{C},\otimes)
is a symmetric monoidal
m
-semiadditive category
(\mathcal{C},\otimes)
such that the tensor product
\otimes
preserves
m
-finite colimits in each variable.

Remark 2.2.2
.

By

-semiadditivity, this condition is equivalent to requiring that the tensor product
\otimes
preserves
m

\displaystyle=(\tau\otimes\id)\left(\sum_{g_{1}+g_{2}=h}e_{g_{1}}\otimes e_{g_{2}}\right)=\sum_{g_{1}+g_{2}=h}c_{g_{1}}e_{g_{2}},

\displaystyle(\tau\otimes u)(e_{h})

\displaystyle=\sum_{g\in G}c_{h}\,e_{g}.

Comparing coefficients of the basis

shows that all

are equal.

It remains to compute

. Since

it suffices to compute

. By definition,

is the cardinality

\left|BC_{p}\right|\colon E\xrightarrow{1}E^{BC_{p}}\xrightarrow{\sim}E\otimes BC_{p}\to E,

which equals

by

, Lemma 5.3.4]
. Since

, we conclude that

, and hence

for all

∎

Proposition 3.3.3
.

Let
\widetilde{\psi^{C_{p}}}:\pi_{0}E\rightarrow\pi_{0}E^{BC_{p}}
be the total power operation of

, then
\alpha=\frac{1}{p}\tr\circ\;\widetilde{\psi^{C_{p}}}
.

Proof.

By

we have that the map

is the trace.
\alpha
was defined as the composition of this map with the total power operation.
∎

4
The character of the total power operation

We now have most of the ingredients needed to state a formula for

\pi_{0}E
at height

The operation

In this section, we prove the following result.

Proposition 4.0.1

There is a natural isomorphism

on the first factor, and by the rationalization of

To establish this decomposition, we use HKR character theory. HKR character theory provides a description of the rationalized Morava

4.1
HKR character theory

Let
G
be a finite group.

Definition 4.1.1
.

Let

denote the set of
n
-tuples of pairwise commuting
p
-torsion elements of
G
. Equivalently,

This set carries a natural action of
G
by conjugation.

Definition 4.1.2
.

The height

generalized class functions of
G
with coefficients in a ring

are the
C
-algebra of functions on the quotient

\hom(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n},G)/G,

where

acts by conjugation. We denote this algebra by

. If

is equipped with the trivial

-action, then

\hom_{G\text{-}\textbf{Set}}(G_{n},R)=\cl_{n}(G,R).

Example 4.1.3
.

Since
C_{p}
is an abelian
p
-group, conjugation is trivial. Therefore
\cl_{2}(C_{p})
is a free module of rank

\left|\hom(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{2},C_{p})\right|=\left|C_{p}^{2}\right|=p^{2}.

Indeed, for any ordered pair of elements of

there is a unique group homomorphism

\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{2}\to C_{p}

sending
(1,0)
and
(0,1)
to the given elements.

Note that this agrees with the rank of
\pi_{0}E^{BC_{p}}
in height
n=2
.

Example 4.1.4
.

The
C
-module
\cl_{2}(\Sigma_{p})
has rank
p+2
. Indeed, to specify a homomorphism

\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{2}\to\Sigma_{p}

we first choose the image of
(1,0)
. This element may be trivial or nontrivial.

If
(1,0)
is trivial, then
(0,1)
is either trivial (one possibility) or a
p
-cycle (one additional possibility, unique up to conjugation).

If
(1,0)
is nontrivial, then by conjugation we may assume it is the cycle
(1\,2\,\dots\,p)
. In this case
(0,1)
must be sent to a power of this cycle, giving
p
distinct possibilities.

Altogether, this yields

conjugacy classes, and hence
\cl_{2}(\Sigma_{p})
has rank
p+2
.

Note that this also agrees with the rank of
\pi_{0}E^{B\Sigma_{p}}
in height
n=2
.

HKR character theory identifies a suitable base change of
\pi_{0}E^{BG}
with
\cl_{n}(G,C)
, providing a powerful computational tool. We now formulate this theory precisely.

Over an algebraically closed field of characteristic
0
, all formal groups are étale. Thus there exists a faithfully flat
\pi_{0}(E)
-algebra over which the Quillen formal group

becomes isomorphic to
(\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p})^{n}
. There is a universal algebra carrying such an isomorphism in the following sense.

Definition 4.1.5
.

[
13
, Corollary 6.8(i)]

There exists a rational
\pi_{0}(E)
-algebra
C_{0}
, called the splitting algebra, such that for any rational
\pi_{0}(E)
-algebra
R
there is a natural identification

\hom_{\pi_{0}(E)\text{-}\textbf{Alg}}(C_{0},R)=\iso\left((\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p})^{n},\;Q\times\spf R\right).

Proposition 4.1.6
.

The algebra
C_{0}
carries a natural left action of
Gl_{n}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
.

Proof.

By the Yoneda lemma, giving a left action of
Gl_{n}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
on

is equivalent to giving a right action on the functor

R\longmapsto\iso\left((\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p})^{n},\;Q\times\spf R\right).

By the Yoneda lemma again, this is equivalent to giving a left action of
Gl_{n}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
on
(\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p})^{n}
. Such an action is canonical, since

(\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p})^{n}\cong\hom(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n},\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}),

and
Gl_{n}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
acts on
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n}
by change of basis.
∎

Proposition 4.1.7
.

[
13
, Corollary 6.8(iii)]

The subring of

-fixed points of
C_{0}
is equal to
\pi_{0}(E)\otimes\mathbb{Q}
.

Thus there is a natural

-action on

, given by the evident action on

together with the action on scalars coming from Proposition

This allows us to formulate the main theorem of HKR character theory.

Theorem 4.1.8
.

[
13
, Theorem C]

Let
E
be a Morava
E
-theory of height
n
. There is a natural ring homomorphism

for any finite group

Via the natural

-action on

-invariants.

The remainder of this subsection is devoted to recalling the definition of the character map
\chi
.

To define the map

\chi\colon\pi_{0}E^{BG}\to\cl_{n}(G,C_{0}),

it suffices to specify, for each conjugacy class
[f]
with

, a ring homomorphism

\pi_{0}E^{BG}\to C_{0},

since
\cl_{n}(G,C_{0})
is canonically a product of copies of

indexed by the

.

Let

\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n}\to\im f

is a finite abelian

-group. Although conjugation in

changes the inclusion

, the induced maps

are homotopic, since the corresponding maps of classifying spaces are homotopic. Thus, to define

, it suffices to construct a map

\pi_{0}E^{B\im f}\to C_{0}.

Proposition 4.1.9
.

The ring
C_{0}
admits a canonical map from
\pi_{0}E^{B(\mathbb{Z}/p^{r})^{n}}
.

Proof.

The ring
\pi_{0}E^{B(\mathbb{Z}/p^{r})^{n}}
classifies

in the Quillen formal group

(\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p})^{n}\cong Q\times\spf C_{0},

we obtain

-torsion points corresponding to the elements

There is a unique ring homomorphism

\pi_{0}E^{B(\mathbb{Z}/p^{r})^{n}}\to C_{0}

classifying these points.
∎

Since
\im f
is finite, there exists
r
sufficiently large such that the map

\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n}\to\im f

factors through

. This yields a morphism

\pi_{0}E^{B\im f}\to\pi_{0}E^{B(\mathbb{Z}/p^{r})^{n}}.

We therefore obtain a composite

\chi_{[f]}\colon\pi_{0}E^{BG}\to\pi_{0}E^{B\im f}\to\pi_{0}E^{B(\mathbb{Z}/p^{r})^{n}}\to C_{0}.

It remains to check that this construction is independent of the choice of

. For

On points, this map sends a

-torsion point

to

, so the above composite is independent of

.

Definition 4.1.10
.

The HKR character map is the ring homomorphism

\chi\colon\pi_{0}E^{BG}\to\cl_{n}(G,C_{0}).

On the factor corresponding to the conjugacy class of

it is given by the composite

\chi_{[f]}\colon\pi_{0}E^{BG}\to\pi_{0}E^{B\im f}\to\pi_{0}E^{B(\mathbb{Z}/p^{r})^{n}}\to C_{0}.

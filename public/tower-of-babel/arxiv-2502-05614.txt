Resolvent bounds for repulsive potentials
Andrés Larraín-Hubach, Yulong Li, Jacob Shapiro, Joseph Tiller

Abstract
. We prove limiting absorption resolvent bounds for the semiclassical Schrödinger operator with a repulsive potential in dimension n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 , which may have a singularity at the origin. As an application, we obtain time decay for the weighted energy of the solution to the associated wave equation with a short range repulsive potential and compactly supported initial data.



1.
Introduction and statement of results

The goal of this paper is to establish limiting absorption resolvent bounds for the semiclassical Schrödinger operator with a repulsive potential in dimensions three and higher. The dimension one case was studied in
[
ChDa21
, Section 2]
. As an application, we obtain time decay of a weighted energy for the solution to the associated wave equation with a short range repulsive potential and compactly supported initial data.

We begin with some notation and conventions. Let
\Delta\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\partial^{2}_{j}\leq 0
be the Laplacian on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We use
(r,\theta)=(|x|,x/|x|)\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
for polar coordinates on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
. Throughout, we equip
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
with the standard Borel measure
d\theta
such that the product measure
r^{n-1}dr\times d\theta
gives Lebesgue measure on
(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. Put
B(0,r_{0})\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|x|<r_{0}\}
. For a function
u
defined on a subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we write
u(r,\theta)\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=u(r\theta)
, and denote the radial derivative by
u^{\prime}\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\partial_{r}u
. If
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a Borel set,
\mathbf{1}_{E}
stands for its indicator function.

Our Schrödinger operator takes the form

(1.1)

P(h)\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=-h^{2}\Delta+V(x):L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

where
h>0
is the semiclassical parameter. The conditions we place on the Borel measurable potential
V:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
are as follows. We suppose
V=V^{+}-V^{-}
with
V^{+}=\max(V,0)
,
V^{-}=-\min(V,0)
, and

(1.2)

\displaystyle V^{-}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

(1.3)

\displaystyle r^{\rho(n)}V(x)\text{ is bounded for $r\leq 1$,}

(1.4)

\displaystyle V(x)\text{ is bounded for $r\geq 1$,}

(1.5)

for each
\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
,
(0,\infty)\ni r\mapsto V(r,\theta)\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=V(r\theta)
has bounded variation.

Here,
\rho(n)>0
depends on the dimension
n
:

(1.6)

\rho(n)<\begin{cases}\frac{3}{2}&n=3,\\
2&n\geq 4.\end{cases}

Recall that a function
f
of locally bounded variation on an interval
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
has distributional derivative equal to a locally finite signed Borel measure, which we denote by
df
. In particular

(1.7)

\int\varphi df=-\int f\varphi^{\prime}dx,\qquad\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(I),

where the
dx
on the right side denotes Lebesgue measure on
I
;
df
further satisfies,

(1.8)

\int_{(a,b]}df=f^{R}(b)-f^{R}(a),

for any interval
(a,b]
contained in the interior of
I
, where
f^{R}(x)\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\lim_{\delta\to 0^{+}}f(x+\delta)
.

The last condition we impose on
V
is that there exists
C_{V}>0
so that for all
\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
and every bounded Borel set
E\subseteq(0,\infty)
,

(1.9)

\int_{E}dV(r,\theta)\leq-C_{V}\int_{E}(r+1)^{-1}{V^{+}(r,\theta)}dr.

We emphasize that since the inequality (
1.9
) is one sided, each measure
dV(\cdot,\theta)
is allowed to have negative point masses. Moreover, because only the positive part
V^{+}
appears on the right side, the potential is allowed to approach a negative constant as
r\to\infty
. A prototype potential satisfying the above conditions is

V(r,\theta)=g(\theta)\big(\mathbf{1}_{B(0,1)}r^{-\rho(n)}-2^{-1}\mathbf{1}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B(0,1)}(r^{-\delta}-2^{-1})\big)

for some
\delta>0
and
g\geq 0
a bounded measurable function on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. Note that for
V\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n};[0,\infty))
, (
1.9
) implies each
V(\cdot,\theta)
is repulsive in sense of classical mechanics, i.e., that
V(r,\theta)>0
implies
V^{\prime}(r,\theta)<0
. The local bound (
1.3
) allows for mild singularities at the origin, most notably the repulsive Coulomb behavior.

For a real valued potential
V\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})+L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
p\geq 2
,
p>n/2
,
P(h)
is self-adjoint if one takes the Sobolev space
H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
as the domain
[
Ne64
, Theorem 8]
. The conditions (
1.2
), (
1.3
), and (
1.4
) imply that
V\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})+L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for some such

. Our main results are the following weighted limiting absorption resolvent bounds for

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose

and

satisfies (

) and (

by (

In case that

we prove stronger estimates.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose

and

satisfies (
1.2

.

From (

and

. The dependence of

Section

is devoted to the proof of Theorem

. In particular, Christiansen and Datchev obtained (

. The corresponding problem in dimension two appears to be delicate, and to our knowledge remains open; we comment below on a possible approach.

Remark 1.4
.

In Appendix
D
, we recall how for the case
V=0
and

, the conditions on

and

in Theorem

Remark 1.5
.

The weighted estimates underlying (
1.10
), (
1.11
), and (
1.12
) hold under a condition weaker than (
1.3
), namely that
|r^{n-1}V(r,\theta)|\to 0
as
r\to 0
, see (
2.8
) (the factor
r^{n-1}
reflects the volume element of Lebesgue measure in polar coordinates). These estimates are derived for test functions in
C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and are transferred to resolvent bounds via the density argument in Appendix
C
. This step uses that the operator domain is
H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and that
C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is dense in
H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, which is why we impose the stronger hypothesis (
1.3
). We note, however, that self-adjoint realizations of
-\Delta+V
exist under weaker local assumptions on
V
. For example, if
V\in L^{2}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
V\geq 0
, then
-\Delta+V
is essentially self-adjoint on
C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

[
ReSi75
, Theorem X.28]
; essential self-adjointness on
C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
holds under inverse square lower bounds
[
ReSi75
, Theorem X.30]
. It would be interesting to formulate the weighted estimates in such frameworks, which would allow more singular potentials. However, for the wave decay results discussed below, a restriction comes from the need to control
rV
near the origin. We thus adopt the more streamlined approach here.

We prove Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
using the
spherical energy method
, a widely used technique for deriving weighted estimates for Schrödinger operators. The approach is based on separation of variables and the classical identity

(1.13)

r^{\frac{n-1}{2}}(-\Delta)r^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}=-\partial^{2}_{r}+r^{-2}\Lambda,

where

(1.14)

\Lambda\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}+\frac{(n-1)(n-3)}{4},

and
\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}
denotes the negative Laplace-Beltrami operator on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. The repulsivity condition is sufficiently advantageous to allow the use of a relatively simple weight–specifically, the same weight employed in
[
ChDa21
]
–to obtain (
2.11
) and (
2.22
). For more general potentials, it is usually necessary to instead conjugate the Laplacian by
e^{\varphi/h}r^{(n-1)/2}
(see, e.g.,
[
CaVo02
,
Da14
,
GaSh22
]
) for a suitable phase
\varphi
. This results in a Carleman estimate with exponential losses as
h\to 0^{+}
.

We use in a crucial way that
\Lambda\geq 0
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
, see (
2.6
). This is why our approach does not cover the case
n=2
where the effective potential
-1/(4r^{2})
has a strong negative singularity as
r\to 0
. We expect that repulsive potentials in dimension two can be treated by adapting the Mellin transform methods used in
[
DGS23
,
Ob24
]
.

As an application of (

), we prove weighted energy decay for the solution to the wave equation

(1.15)

\begin{cases}(\partial^{2}_{t}-\Delta+V(x))u(t,x)=0&(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n},\,n\geq 3,\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\,\partial_{t}u(0,x)=u_{1}(x)&x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\end{cases}

where
u_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and

have compact support. The potential obeys

, (

), (
1.5
) with

, (

), and the extra short range condition

\mathbf{1}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B(0,1)}V\leq C(r+1)^{-\delta(n)},

for some
C>0
and
\delta(n)>0
such that

(1.17)

\delta(n)>\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}+\frac{n+3}{4}&n\neq 8,\\
\frac{1}{2}+3&n=8.\end{cases}

Since
P\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=P(1)=-\Delta+V
is self-adjoint (and nonnegative) under such conditions, we may use the spectral theorem for self-adjoint operators to represent the solution to (

(1.18)

u(t,\cdot)=\cos(t\sqrt{P})u_{0}+\frac{\sin(t\sqrt{P})}{\sqrt{P}}u_{1}.

For

to (

E_{s}[u](t)=E_{s}(t)\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\langle x\rangle^{-2s}(|\partial_{t}u(t,x)|^{2}+|\nabla u(t,x)|^{2}+|u(t,x)|^{2})dx.

Set also

E(0)\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\|\nabla u_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|u_{1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.

Suppose

Since

.

Remark 1.8

We expect that, by adapting the arguments of
[
Vo04b

, the assumption of compact support on the initial data can be relaxed. Specifically, the decay should continue to hold for initial conditions lying in an appropriate weighted Sobolev spaces. However, to keep the presentation technically streamlined, we suppose

Remark 1.9
.

The condition (
1.17
) has a quirk in dimension eight compared to other dimensions. This appears to be an artifact of our approach, which analyzes the low-frequency behavior of the resolvent kernel in terms of Hilbert–Schmidt norms (Appendix
E
).

For smooth, nonnegative potentials of compact support, the local energy

E_{r_{0}}(t)\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\int_{B(0,r_{0})}|\partial_{t}u(t,x)|^{2}+|\nabla u(t,x)|^{2}+|u(t,x)|^{2}dx,\qquad r_{0}>0,

obeys

(1.21)

E_{r_{0}}(t)=\begin{cases}O(e^{-ct})\text{ for some }c>0&n\geq 3\text{ odd},\\
O(t^{-2n})&n\geq 4\text{ even}.\end{cases}

Indeed, Vainberg
[
Va75
]
showed (
1.21
) for compactly supported perturbations of the Laplacian satisfying the so-called Generalized Huygens Principle (as defined in
[
Vo04c
]
). A result of Melrose and Sjöstrand on the propagation of singularities
[
MeSj78
,
MeSj82
]
implies that this principle holds for a broad class of nontrapping perturbations of the Laplacian, which includes smooth, nonnegative, compactly supported potentials. The study of energy decay for nontrapping perturbations has a long history, tracing back to the works of Lax, Morawetz, and Phillips
[
LMP63
,
Mo66
,
Mo75
]
.

On the other hand, bounds similar to (
1.20
) were obtained in previous works for various classes of short range potentials. In
[
Za04
]
, Zappacosta considered potentials
V\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3};(0,\infty))
with
\partial^{\alpha}_{x}V=O(\langle x\rangle^{-\delta-|\alpha|})
for all
0\leq|\alpha|\leq 1
and some
\delta>2
. For each
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, the bound

\|\chi\sqrt{V}(\sin(t\sqrt{P})/\sqrt{P})\sqrt{V}\chi\|^{2}_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}=O(t^{-2})
was proved. In
[
Vo04a
]
, Vodev showed
E_{r_{0}}(t)=O(t^{-2})
in dimension
n\geq 3
, where
V\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n};[0,\infty))
obeys

(1.22)

\displaystyle V=O(\langle x\rangle^{-\delta_{0}})\qquad\text{for some $\delta_{0}>2$, and}

(1.23)

\displaystyle 2V+r\partial_{r}V\leq C\langle x\rangle^{-\delta}\qquad\text{for some $C>0$ and some $\delta>1$.}

Additionally, it is assumed that
V
has no resonance at zero energy, a condition closely related to the validity of a bound such as (
1.12
) for
|z|\ll 1
. Vodev also obtained weighted energy decay for a class of long-range, nontrapping perturbations of the Laplacian that includes perturbations by a nonnegative long-range potential, provided the initial conditions are spectrally localized away from
[0,a]
for
a>0
sufficiently large
[
Vo04b
]
. We note that, in our approach, the positivity of the potential appears crucial for obtaining (
1.12
). Indeed, the constant
\mathfrak{C}(E,s,\varepsilon,\|V^{-}\|_{L^{\infty}})
in (
2.16
) blows up as
E\to 0^{+}
unless
\|V^{-}\|_{L^{\infty}}=0
.

We note the connection between (
1.23
) and our repulsiveness condition. If

V\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n};(0,\infty))
satisfies (
1.9
), then
\partial_{r}(\log V(\cdot,\theta))\leq-C_{V}(r+1)^{-1}
uniformly in
\theta
. Integration in
r
yields
0\leq V(r,\theta)\leq C(r+1)^{-C_{V}}
, so
2V+r\partial_{r}V\leq 2V\leq C(r+1)^{-C_{V}}
, which is (
1.23
) provided
C_{V}>1
.

In the absence of a bound like (
1.12
), or a condition excluding a resonance or eigenvalue at zero, decay of wave equation solutions generally cannot be expected. Thus, in the present work the positivity of the potential serves as a sufficient condition to rule out threshold obstructions. Other sufficient conditions have been obtained in previous works; for example, smallness of the potential in the Rollnik and global Kato norms
[
RoSc04
]
. For related discussions and further references, see
[
JeNe01
,
ChDa25
,
CDY25
]
.

The proof of Theorem
1.6
follows the strategy of
[
Vo04a
, Section 3]
, with modifications to account for the possible singularity of

at the origin. The key step is to establish

(1.24)

t^{2}E_{s}(t)\leq Ct^{2}\int_{t}^{\infty}E_{s}(\tau)d\tau\leq CE(0),\qquad t\geq 1.

Using Duhamel’s formula and the Fourier transform (
t
dual to
\lambda
), the Fourier transform of

can be expressed in terms of

, see (

). Because the initial data are compactly supported, finite speed of propagation allows insertion of a cutoff function
\eta
, and Plancherel’s theorem then reduces control of
E_{s}(t)
, to bounds on
\langle x\rangle^{-s}(P-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\eta
, which comes from (
1.12
), see also Lemma

.

However, the factor
t^{2}
in (
1.24
) corresponds to differentiation with respect to
\lambda
, so it is also necessary to control
\langle x\rangle^{-s}\tfrac{d}{d\lambda}(P-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\eta
. This is achieved under the stronger assumptions (
1.3
) with
\rho=1
(compare with (
1.6
)) and (
1.16
). In particular, the restriction
\rho=1
arises from the need for a uniform bound on
\lambda V(-\Delta-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\langle x\rangle^{-s}
for

, see (

). It would be interesting to determine whether more singular potentials could be accommodated by controlling the
\lambda

We expect that for certain potentials with mild spatial decay depending on the dimension,

is the optimal decay for the local energy. A sharper description should depend on a low-frequency expansion of the resolvent around
\lambda=0
(see, e.g.,
[

), rather than a bound alone.

Another energy studied is the quantity

E^{(1)}_{K}[u](t)\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\int_{K}|\partial_{t}u(t,x)|^{2}+|\nabla u(t,x)|^{2}+V(x)|u(t,x)|^{2}dx,

2.
Proof of Theorem

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.2
. Throughout this section, we take
P(h)
as in (
1.1
), and assume the potential

satisfies (
1.2

1.5

By (

(2.1)

\begin{split}P^{\pm}(h)&\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=r^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\left(P(h)-E\pm i\varepsilon\right)r^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}\\

where we let

F[u](r)

(2.2)

F(r)=F[u](r)\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\|hu^{\prime}(r,\cdot)\|^{2}-\langle(h^{2}r^{-2}\Lambda+V^{R}(r,\cdot)-E)u(r,\cdot),u(r,\cdot)\rangle,

where
\|\cdot\|

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denote the norm and inner product on

. We take complex conjugation to occur in the first argument of
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle

V^{R}(r,\theta)
is the measurable function defined by
V^{R}(r,\theta)\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\lim_{k\to\infty}V((r+(1/k))\theta)
for

The function

\begin{split}C^{\infty}_{0}(0,\infty)\ni\varphi&\mapsto\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi(r)\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}V(r,\theta)2\mathop{\rm Re}(\overline{u}u^{\prime})d\theta dr\\

Note that, aside from the term in

involving

Using (

\begin{split}(wF)^{\prime}&=wF^{\prime}+w^{\prime}F\\

First we show (

w(r)\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=1-\frac{C_{V}}{C_{V}+\delta}(1+r)^{-\delta}.

For such

Therefore, on the one hand

Thus, the last two estimates and (

\begin{split}\int\varphi d(wF)&=-\int(wF)\varphi^{\prime}dr\\

Note that in the first line of (

1.7

\int_{r_{0}}^{\infty}Ew^{\prime}\|u\|^{2}+w^{\prime}\|hu^{\prime}\|^{2}dr+w(r_{0})F^{R}(r_{0})\leq\int_{r_{0}}^{\infty}2w\mathop{\rm Re}\langle P^{\pm}(h)u,u^{\prime}\rangle\mp 2\varepsilon w\mathop{\rm Im}\langle u,u^{\prime}\rangle dr.

Since

(2.8)

\begin{split}F^{R}(r_{0})&=\|hu^{\prime}(r_{0},\cdot)\|^{2}+r_{0}^{n-3}\langle h^{2}\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}v(r_{0},\cdot),v(r_{0},\cdot)\rangle\\

We rewrite the term in (

Therefore,

(2.9)

\begin{split}r_{0}^{n-3}&\langle h^{2}\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}v(r_{0},\cdot),v(r_{0},\cdot)\rangle\\
&=h^{2}r_{0}^{n-1}\langle(\Delta v)(r_{0},\cdot),v(r_{0},\cdot)\rangle\\
&-h^{2}r_{0}^{n-1}\langle(\partial^{2}_{r}v)(r_{0},\cdot),v(r_{0},\cdot)\rangle-h^{2}(n-1)r_{0}^{n-2}\langle(\partial_{r}v)(r_{0},\cdot),v(r_{0},\cdot)\rangle.\end{split}

We can express the differential operators

(2.10)

\partial_{r}=r^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}\partial_{x_{j}},\qquad\partial^{2}_{r}=r^{-2}\sum_{k=1}^{n}x_{k}\sum_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}\partial_{x_{k}}\partial_{x_{j}}.

Thus by (

\lim_{r_{0}\to 0}w(r_{0})F(r_{0})=w(0)F(0)=\begin{cases}\omega_{n-1}w(0)|v(0)|^{2}&n=3,\\
0&n\geq 4,\end{cases}

Thus in view of (

) and
0<w\leq 1
,

(2.11)

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{\infty}Ew^{\prime}\|u\|^{2}&+w^{\prime}\|hu^{\prime}\|^{2}dr\\

&+\frac{2\varepsilon}{h}(\int_{0}^{\infty}\|u\|^{2}dr)^{1/2}(\int_{0}^{\infty}\|hu^{\prime}\|^{2}dr)^{1/2}.\end{split}

We now estimate,

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{\infty}\|hu^{\prime}\|^{2}dr&=\mathop{\rm Re}\int_{0}^{\infty}\langle u,-h^{2}u^{\prime\prime}\rangle dr\\
&=\mathop{\rm Re}\big(\int_{0}^{\infty}\langle u,P^{\pm}(h)u\rangle dr+\int_{0}^{\infty}\langle u,(E-V-h^{2}r^{-2}\Lambda)u\rangle dr\mp i\varepsilon\int^{\infty}_{0}\|u\|^{2}dr\big)\\
&=\mathop{\rm Re}\int_{0}^{\infty}\langle u,P^{\pm}(h)u\rangle dr+\int_{0}^{\infty}\langle u,(E-V-h^{2}r^{-2}\Lambda)u\rangle dr\\
&\leq\big(\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{w^{\prime}}\|P^{\pm}(h)u\|^{2}dr\big)^{1/2}\big(\int_{0}^{\infty}w^{\prime}\|u\|^{2}dr\big)^{1/2}+(E+\|V^{-}\|_{L^{\infty}})\int_{0}^{\infty}\|u\|^{2}dr,\end{split}

and

\begin{split}\varepsilon\int_{0}^{\infty}\|u\|^{2}dr&=\varepsilon\|v\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\\
&=|\mathop{\rm Im}\langle(P(h)-E\pm i\varepsilon)v,v\rangle_{L^{2}}|\\
&=\big|\mathop{\rm Im}\int_{0}^{\infty}\langle P^{\pm}(h)u,u\rangle dr\big|\\
&\leq\big(\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{w^{\prime}}\|P^{\pm}(h)u\|^{2}dr\big)^{1/2}\big(\int_{0}^{\infty}w^{\prime}\|u\|^{2}dr\big)^{1/2}.\end{split}

Combining these gives

\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{h^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\|u\|^{2}dr\cdot\int_{0}^{\infty}\|hu^{\prime}\|^{2}dr\leq(E+\varepsilon+\|V^{-}\|_{L^{\infty}})\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{h^{2}w^{\prime}}\|P^{\pm}(h)u\|^{2}dr\cdot\int_{0}^{\infty}w^{\prime}\|u\|^{2}dr.

Plugging this into (
2.11
) yields

(2.12)

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{\infty}Ew^{\prime}\|u\|^{2}&+w^{\prime}\|hu^{\prime}\|^{2}dr\\
&\leq 2\big(\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{h^{2}w^{\prime}}\|P^{\pm}(h)u\|^{2}dr\big)^{1/2}\\
&\cdot\big(\big(\int_{0}^{\infty}w^{\prime}\|hu^{\prime}\|^{2}dr\big)^{1/2}+(E+\varepsilon+\|V^{-}\|_{L^{\infty}})^{1/2}\big(\int_{0}^{\infty}w^{\prime}\|u\|^{2}dr\big)^{1/2}\big).\end{split}

Now restrict

and complete the square in (
2.12
) to find,

(2.13)

\begin{split}\big(E^{1/2}\big(\int_{0}^{\infty}&w^{\prime}\|u\|^{2}dr\big)^{1/2}-\frac{(E+\varepsilon+\|V^{-}\|_{L^{\infty}})^{1/2}}{E^{1/2}}\big(\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{h^{2}w^{\prime}}\|P^{\pm}(h)u\|^{2}dr\big)^{1/2}\big)^{2}\\

Dropping the second term on the left side of (

) implies, for all

and
\varepsilon\geq 0
,

(2.14)

\begin{split}E^{1/2}\big(&\int_{0}^{\infty}w^{\prime}\|u\|^{2}dr\big)^{1/2}\\
&\leq\big(\frac{(E+\varepsilon+\|V^{-}\|_{L^{\infty}})^{1/2}}{E^{1/2}}+\frac{(2E+\varepsilon+\|V^{-}\|_{L^{\infty}})^{1/2}}{E^{1/2}}\big)\big(\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{h^{2}w^{\prime}}\|P^{\pm}(h)u\|^{2}dr\big)^{1/2}.\end{split}

Recall that
w^{\prime}=C_{V}\delta(C_{V}+\delta)^{-1}(r+1)^{-1-\delta}
and

. From (

) and the density argument in Appendix

we get, rewriting

, that

\|(1+r)^{-2s}(P-z)^{-1}(1+r)^{-2s}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\frac{\mathfrak{C}(E,s,\varepsilon,\|V^{-}\|_{L^{\infty}})}{h}\,

where

\mathfrak{C}(E,s,\varepsilon,\|V^{-}\|_{L^{\infty}})=\dfrac{C_{V}+\delta}{E\delta C_{V}}\left((E+\varepsilon+\|V^{-}\|_{L^{\infty}})^{1/2}+(2E+\varepsilon+\|V^{-}\|_{L^{\infty}})^{1/2}\right).

From this point on, we assume
V^{-}=0
. Consider the sector
\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|\mathop{\rm Im}z|<\alpha\mathop{\rm Re}z\}
for

. From (

), we get for all

,
E\pm i\varepsilon\in\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|\mathop{\rm Im}z|<\alpha\mathop{\rm Re}z\}

\begin{split}|(E&\pm i\varepsilon)^{1/2}|\big(\int_{0}^{\infty}(r+1)^{-2s}\|u\|^{2}dr\big)^{1/2}\\

Here, our branch of the complex square root is chosen so that

\begin{split}\|z^{1/2}(r+1)^{-s}(P(h)&-z)^{-1}(r+1)^{-s}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\\

on

and for any

[

]
in the following way. For

Then

in analytic in

|U(z)|\leq h^{-1}(1+\alpha^{2})^{1/4}\big(\frac{1}{\delta}+\frac{1}{C_{V}}\big)((1+\alpha)^{1/2}+(2+\alpha)^{1/2})\|u\|_{L^{2}}\|v\|_{L^{2}}.

On the other hand, in
\Omega_{\alpha}
, we have the standard bound

|U(z)|\leq\frac{|z|^{1/2}\|u\|_{L^{2}}\|v\|_{L^{2}}}{\mathop{\rm dist}(z,[0,\infty))}=\begin{cases}\frac{\|u\|_{L^{2}}\|v\|_{L^{2}}}{|z|^{1/2}}&\mathop{\rm Re}z<0,\\
\frac{|z|^{1/2}\|u\|_{L^{2}}\|v\|_{L^{2}}}{|\mathop{\rm Im}z|}&\mathop{\rm Re}z\geq 0,\,z\in\Omega_{\alpha},\end{cases}

where we used

\frac{1}{\mathop{\rm dist}(z,[0,\infty))}=\frac{1}{\inf_{r\geq 0}((\mathop{\rm Re}z-r)^{2}+(\mathop{\rm Im}z)^{2})^{1/2}}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{|z|}&\mathop{\rm Re}z<0,\\
\frac{1}{|\mathop{\rm Im}z|}&\mathop{\rm Re}z\geq 0,\,z\in\Omega_{\alpha}.\end{cases}

Finally, define,
g(z)=e^{i(z^{-1})^{1/2}}
, where our branch of the square root is as above. In

,

for some

depending on
\alpha
. It follows because, from the definition of

, there exists

so that any

takes the form

with

. Whence

. Combining with (
2.20

(2.21)

\limsup_{z\to 0,\,z\in\Omega_{\alpha}}|g(z)|^{\sigma}|U(z)|=0,\qquad\sigma>0.

Therefore, from (
2.19
) and (
2.21
), the Phragmén Lindelöf Theorem (Theorem

in Appendix
B
) implies that (
2.18
) holds for all

too. Sending

completes the proof of (

To prove (
1.12

) and drop the first term on the left hand side. Still working on

\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}w^{\prime}\|hu^{\prime}\|^{2}\,dr\right)^{1/2}\leq(1+\sqrt{2+\alpha})\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{h^{2}w^{\prime}}\|P^{\pm}(h)u\|^{2}dr\right)^{1/2}.

By integration by parts,

which implies

\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}(r+1)^{-3-\delta}\|u\|^{2}\,dr\right)^{1/2}\leq h^{-1}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}(r+1)^{-1-\delta}\|hu^{\prime}\|^{2}\,dr\right)^{1/2}.

From (

) and

,

Using again the density argument in Appendix
C
, for

,

(2.24)

\|(1+r)^{-\frac{3+\delta}{2}}(P(h)-z)^{-1}(1+r)^{-\frac{1+\delta}{2}}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\leq h^{-2}\left(\delta^{-1}+C_{V}^{-1}\right)(1+\sqrt{2+\alpha}),

on
\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|\mathop{\rm Im}z|<\alpha\mathop{\rm Re}z\}
. Then, as above, (

), the Phragmén Lindelöf Theorem, and sending

, imply

Since the norm of an operator and its adjoint coincide,

\|(1+r)^{-\frac{1+\delta}{2}}(P(h)-z)^{-1}(1+r)^{-\frac{3+\delta}{2}}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\leq h^{-2}\left(\delta^{-1}+C_{V}^{-1}\right)(1+\sqrt{2}),\qquad z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus[0,\infty).

The three lines lemma then says that for fixed

\|(1+r)^{-\frac{3+\delta}{2}+\theta}(P(h)-z)^{-1}(1+r)^{-\frac{1+\delta}{2}-\theta}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\leq h^{-2}\left(\delta^{-1}+C_{V}^{-1}\right)(1+\sqrt{2}),\qquad\theta\in[0,1].

2.25
), to finish, we need to see that we can choose

and

appropriately to arrive at (

). That is, we need to attain the more general weights characterized by

,

. However, because we have the restrictions

and

3.

Lemma 3.1

(3.1)

Proof.

Since
((P-\lambda^{2})^{-1})^{*}=(P-(\overline{\lambda})^{2})^{-1}
, to prove (

First, we treat the case

1.11

1.12

(3.2)

\|\langle x\rangle^{-s_{1}}(P-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\langle x\rangle^{-s_{2}}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\leq C(1+|\mathop{\rm Re}\lambda|)^{-1},\qquad 0<\mathop{\rm Im}\lambda\leq 1,

Recall from standard elliptic theory that for all
f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and all
\gamma>0
,

(3.3)

\begin{gathered}\|f\|_{H^{2}}\leq C(\|f\|_{L^{2}}+\|\Delta f\|_{L^{2}}),\\
\|f\|^{2}_{H^{1}}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}}\|f\|_{H^{2}}\leq C(\gamma^{-1}\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\gamma\|\Delta f\|^{2}_{L^{2}}).\end{gathered}

Therefore, for any
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

\begin{split}\|\langle x\rangle^{-s_{1}}&(P-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\langle x\rangle^{-s_{2}}f\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\\
&\leq C(\|\langle x\rangle^{-s_{1}}(P-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\langle x\rangle^{-s_{2}}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|(-\Delta)\langle x\rangle^{-s_{1}}(P-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\langle x\rangle^{-s_{2}}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})})\\
&\leq C(\|\langle x\rangle^{-s_{1}}(P-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\langle x\rangle^{-s_{2}}f\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|\langle x\rangle^{-s_{1}}(-\Delta)(P-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\langle x\rangle^{-s_{2}}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})})\\
&\leq C(\gamma^{-1}+|\mathop{\rm Re}\lambda|^{2})\|\langle x\rangle^{-s_{1}}(P-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\langle x\rangle^{-s_{2}}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\\
&+C\gamma\|\Delta\langle x\rangle^{-s_{1}}(P-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\langle x\rangle^{-s_{2}}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\\
&+C\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.\end{split}

Selecting
\gamma
sufficiently small depending on
C
, and applying (
3.2
) yields

(3.4)

\|\langle x\rangle^{-s_{1}}(P-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\langle x\rangle^{-s_{2}}f\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C(1+|\mathop{\rm Re}\lambda|)\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

as desired. This confirms (
3.1
) for
|\alpha_{1}|=2
. For
|\alpha_{1}|=1
(still with
|\alpha_{2}|=0
), combine (
3.2
) and (
3.4
) via the second line of (
3.3
).

If
|\alpha_{2}|>0
, let
f\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and put
u=\langle x\rangle^{-s_{1}}(P-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\langle x\rangle^{-s_{2}}\partial^{\alpha_{2}}_{x}f
. We need to show

\|u\|_{H^{|\alpha_{1}|}}\leq C(1+|\mathop{\rm Re}\lambda|)^{|\alpha_{1}|+|\alpha_{2}|-1}\|f\|_{L^{2}},\qquad H^{0}\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

If
|\alpha_{1}|=0
, we use self-adjointness and that we have already showed
\|\langle x\rangle^{-s_{2}}(P-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\langle x\rangle^{-s_{1}}f\|_{H^{j}}\leq C(1+|\mathop{\rm Re}\lambda|)^{j-1}\|f\|_{L^{2}}
,
j\in\{0,1,2\}
, to get

\begin{split}\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}}&=\langle u,\langle x\rangle^{-s_{1}}(P-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\langle x\rangle^{-s_{2}}\partial^{\alpha_{2}}_{x}f\rangle_{L^{2}}\\
&\leq\|\partial^{\alpha_{2}}_{x}\langle x\rangle^{-s_{2}}(P-(\overline{\lambda})^{2})^{-1}\langle x\rangle^{-s_{1}}u\|_{L^{2}}\|f\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\leq C(1+|\mathop{\rm Re}\lambda|)^{|\alpha_{2}|-1}\|u\|_{L^{2}}\|f\|_{L^{2}}.\end{split}

If
|\alpha_{1}|=1
, we recognize that
(P-\lambda^{2})u=\langle x\rangle^{-s_{1}-s_{2}}\partial^{\alpha_{2}}_{x}f+[-\Delta,\langle x\rangle^{-s_{1}}]\langle x\rangle^{s_{1}}u
. Then multiply by
\overline{u}
, integrate over
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and integrate by parts as appropriate

\begin{split}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}&=\int(\lambda^{2}-V)|u|^{2}-\int\partial^{\alpha_{2}}_{x}(\langle x\rangle^{-s_{1}-s_{2}}\overline{u})f+\int\overline{u}[-\Delta,\langle x\rangle^{-s_{1}}]\langle x\rangle^{s_{1}}u\\
&\leq\lambda^{2}\int|u|^{2}-\int\partial^{\alpha_{2}}_{x}(\langle x\rangle^{-s_{1}-s_{2}}\overline{u})f+\int\overline{u}[-\Delta,\langle x\rangle^{-s_{1}}]\langle x\rangle^{s_{1}}u.\end{split}

Because both

[-\Delta,\langle x\rangle^{-s_{1}}]\langle x\rangle^{s_{1}}=(-\Delta\langle x\rangle^{-s_{1}})\langle x\rangle^{s_{1}}-2(\nabla\langle x\rangle^{-s_{1}})\cdot\nabla\langle x\rangle^{s_{1}},

and
\partial^{\alpha_{2}}_{x}\langle x\rangle^{-s_{1}-s_{2}}
are first order differential operators with bounded coefficients, we conclude, for all
\gamma>0
,

\begin{split}\|\nabla u\|^{2}_{L^{2}}&\leq C_{\gamma}((1+|\mathop{\rm Re}\lambda|)^{2}\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}})+\gamma\|\nabla u\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\\
&\leq C_{\gamma}(1+|\mathop{\rm Re}\lambda|)^{2}\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\gamma\|\nabla u\|^{2}_{L^{2}},\end{split}

for some
C_{\gamma}>0
depending on
\gamma
.

Fixing
\gamma
small enough, we absorb the second term on the right side into the left side, confirming (
3.1
) when
|\alpha_{1}|=|\alpha_{2}|=1
.

∎

-derivative of the weighted resolvent, which requires the extra short range conditions (

) and (

Lemma 3.2
.

Let

. Suppose

satisfies (

Remark 3.3
.

In

]
, the estimate (

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
n\geq 3
and suppose
s
is as in (
1.19
). Assume
V
obeys (
1.2
) through (
1.5
) and (
1.9
), as well as (
1.16
) and (
1.17
). There exists
C>0
so that for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
with
0<|\mathop{\rm Im}\lambda|\leq 1
, and any
j\in\{0,1\}
and multiindex
\alpha
such that
j+|\alpha|\leq 1
,

(3.6)

\|\tfrac{d}{d\lambda}\langle x\rangle^{-s}\lambda^{j}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}(P-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\langle x\rangle^{-s}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we take
s
sufficiently close to (but larger than)
(n+3)/4
when
n\neq 8
, or sufficiently close to (but larger than)
3
when
n=8
, so that by (
1.17
) we may fix
s^{\prime}>1/2
so that
s+s^{\prime}<\delta
. We note also that with these choices we have
s^{\prime}+s>2
, so we are permitted to apply (
3.1
) as the need arises.

We begin from the resolvent identity

(3.7)

(P-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\langle x\rangle^{-s}(I+K(\lambda))=R_{0}(\lambda)\langle x\rangle^{-s},

where
K(\lambda)\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=V(x)\langle x\rangle^{s+s^{\prime}}\langle x\rangle^{-s^{\prime}}R_{0}(\lambda)\langle x\rangle^{-s}
and
R_{0}(\lambda)\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=(-\Delta-\lambda^{2})^{-1}
.

It is well known that
\langle x\rangle^{-s^{\prime}}R_{0}(\lambda)\langle x\rangle^{-s}:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
has a continuous extension from either half-plane (
\pm\mathop{\rm Im}\lambda>0
) to
\mathbb{R}

[
GiMo74
, Proposition 2.4]
. Let us denote this extension by
R^{\pm}_{0,s^{\prime},s}(\lambda)
and put
K^{\pm}(\lambda)=V(x)\langle x\rangle^{s+s^{\prime}}R^{\pm}_{0,s^{\prime},s}(\lambda)

We now show that
K^{\pm}(\lambda)
is a compact operator
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. To see this, observe that we may write
K^{\pm}(\lambda)
as the sum

K^{\pm}(\lambda)=(\chi\langle x\rangle^{s+s^{\prime}}V)R^{\pm}_{0,s^{\prime},s}(\lambda)+((1-\chi)V\langle x\rangle^{\delta})\langle x\rangle^{s+s^{\prime}-\delta}R^{\pm}_{0,s^{\prime},s}(\lambda).

where
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n};[0,1])
is identically one near the origin in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and supported in
B(0,1)
. The second operator on the right side is compact by
[
DyZw19
, Theorem B.4]
). The first operator on the right side is compact as follows: it may be viewed as the composition of bounded
R^{\pm}_{0,s^{\prime},s}(\lambda):L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
followed by multiplication by
\chi\langle x\rangle^{s+s^{\prime}}V
. Due to (
1.3
) and Lemma
F.1
, we have
\|\chi\langle x\rangle^{s+s^{\prime}}Vu\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|u\|_{H^{1}(B(0,1))}
for some
C>0
and all
u\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. By the Kondrachov embedding theorem the inclusion
H^{2}(B(0,1))\to H^{1}(B(0,1))
is compact. So compactness of
(\chi\langle x\rangle^{s+s^{\prime}}V)R^{\pm}_{0,s^{\prime},s}(\lambda)
holds as desired.

We claim further that
I+K^{\pm}(\lambda)
is invertible
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
\lambda
with
\pm\mathop{\rm Im}\lambda\geq 0
. By compactness of
K^{\pm}(\lambda)
and the Fredholm alternative
[
ReSi80
, Theorem VI.14]
, we have that
I+K^{\pm}(\lambda)
is invertible if we can show that for
g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
(I+K^{\pm}(\lambda))g=0
implies
g=0
. To this end, put
u\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\langle x\rangle^{s^{\prime}}R^{\pm}_{0,s^{\prime},s}(\lambda)g
, which belongs to
\langle x\rangle^{s^{\prime}}H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. If we can show
u=0
, then in fact
g=0
. This is because
(-\Delta-\lambda^{2})u=\langle x\rangle^{-s}g
in the distributional sense.

Now let us show
u=0
. If
\lambda^{2}\in\mathbb{C}\setminus[0,\infty)
(so that
K^{\pm}(\lambda)=K(\lambda)
), this follows immediately from
(P-\lambda^{2})u=\langle x\rangle^{-s}g+VR_{0}(\lambda)\langle x\rangle^{-s}g=\langle x\rangle^{-s}(I+K(\lambda))g=0.
If
\lambda^{2}\in[0,\infty)
, the idea is the same, but we incorporate a limiting step. Set
u_{\pm,\varepsilon}=(-\Delta-\lambda^{2}\pm i\varepsilon)^{-1}\langle x\rangle^{-s}g
. We have that
[
GiMo74
, Proposition 2.4]
implies that
\langle x\rangle^{-s^{\prime}}u_{\pm,\varepsilon}
converges to
\langle x\rangle^{-s^{\prime}}u
in
H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
. We also have that

\begin{split}u_{\pm,\varepsilon}&=(-\Delta-\lambda^{2}\pm i\varepsilon)^{-1}\langle x\rangle^{-s}g\\
&=(P-\lambda^{2}\pm i\varepsilon)^{-1}(P-\lambda^{2}\pm i\varepsilon)(-\Delta-\lambda^{2}\pm i\varepsilon)^{-1}\langle x\rangle^{-s}g\\
&=(P-\lambda^{2}\pm i\varepsilon)^{-1}\langle x\rangle^{-s}(I+V\langle x\rangle^{s}(-\Delta-\lambda^{2}\pm i\varepsilon)^{-1}\langle x\rangle^{-s})g.\end{split}

Therefore, by (
1.12
),

\begin{split}\|\langle x\rangle^{-s^{\prime}}u\|_{L^{2}}&=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\|\langle x\rangle^{-s^{\prime}}u_{\pm,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\leq C\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\|(I+V\langle x\rangle^{s}(-\Delta-\lambda^{2}\pm i\varepsilon)^{-1}\langle x\rangle^{-s})g\|_{L^{2}}\\
&=\|(I+K^{\pm}(\lambda))g\|_{L^{2}}=0.\end{split}

Thus we have demonstrated that
I+K^{\pm}(\lambda)
is invertible for
\pm\mathop{\rm Im}\lambda\geq 0
. As
\lambda\to\infty
,
\|K(\lambda)\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\to 0
thanks to (
3.1
), hence we can compute
(I+K^{\pm}(\lambda))^{-1}
by a Neumann series, thanks to (
3.1
). Therefore

(3.8)

\|(I+K^{\pm}(\lambda))^{-1}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\leq C.

Now for
0<|\mathop{\rm Im}\lambda|\leq 1
take the
\lambda
-derivative of (
3.7
),

(3.9)

\begin{split}\big(\tfrac{d}{d\lambda}\langle x\rangle^{-s}&\lambda^{j}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}(P-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\langle x\rangle^{-s}\big)(I+K(\lambda))\\
&=\tfrac{d}{d\lambda}\langle x\rangle^{-s}\lambda^{j}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}R_{0}(\lambda)\langle x\rangle^{-s}\\
&-2\langle x\rangle^{-s}\lambda^{j}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}(P-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\langle x\rangle^{-s^{\prime}}V\langle x\rangle^{s+s^{\prime}}\lambda\langle x\rangle^{-s}(-\Delta-\lambda^{2})^{-2}\langle x\rangle^{-s},\end{split}

where we used

(3.10)

\tfrac{d}{d\lambda}\langle x\rangle^{-s^{\prime}}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}R_{0}(\lambda)\langle x\rangle^{-s}=2\lambda\langle x\rangle^{-s^{\prime}}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}(-\Delta-\lambda^{2})^{-2}\langle x\rangle^{-s},\qquad j\in\{0,1\}.

The operator norm
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
of the term in the second line of (
3.9
) is bounded above by a constant due to (
3.5
) and (
3.10
). As for the third line,
\|\langle x\rangle^{-s}\lambda^{j}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}(P-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\langle x\rangle^{-s^{\prime}}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\leq C
by (
3.1
). Moreover

\begin{split}\|V&\langle x\rangle^{s+s^{\prime}}\lambda\langle x\rangle^{-s}(-\Delta-\lambda^{2})^{-2}\langle x\rangle^{-s}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\\
&\leq C\|\lambda\langle x\rangle^{-s}(-\Delta-\lambda^{2})^{-2}\langle x\rangle^{-s}\|_{H^{1}\to L^{2}}\end{split}

since multiplication by
V\langle x\rangle^{s+s^{\prime}}
is a bounded operator
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
(see (
1.3
) and Lemma
F.1
). Finally, because
\|\lambda\langle x\rangle^{-s}(-\Delta-\lambda^{2})^{-2}\langle x\rangle^{-s}\|_{H^{1}\to L^{2}}\leq C
by (
3.5
), the proof of (
3.6
) is complete.

∎

. As before we use the notation
P=-\Delta+V:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

In several steps below, we use that for all

Lemma 4.1
.

If

and
V

) through (

1.19
) and

\phi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})

near

,
\phi(t)=1
near
[1,\infty)
. Since

, where

On the other hand,

(P-(\lambda-i\varepsilon)^{2})^{-1}=\int^{\infty}_{0}e^{-it(\lambda-i\varepsilon)}\frac{\sin(t\sqrt{P})}{\sqrt{P}}dt,\qquad\varepsilon>0.

It follows from the last two identities that the Fourier transform
\widehat{\phi u}
of

By finite propagation speed for the wave equation, and because

is compactly supported in

,

, and thus also

, is contained in some compact subset of

independent of

. Choose

near

. By (

\begin{gathered}\langle x\rangle^{-s}\widehat{\phi u}(\lambda-i\varepsilon)=\langle x\rangle^{-s}(P-(\lambda-i\varepsilon)^{2})^{-1}\eta\hat{v}(\lambda-i\varepsilon),\\

Therefore, by (
3.1
), for

and
V
obeying (
1.2
) through (
1.9

C>0
independent of
\lambda
and
\varepsilon
, so that for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
,

, we have

(4.6)

\begin{split}\Big\|\frac{d^{k}}{d\lambda^{k}}&\langle x\rangle^{-s}\widehat{\partial_{t}(\phi u)}(\lambda-i\varepsilon)\Big\|_{L^{2}}+\Big\|\frac{d^{k}}{d\lambda^{k}}\langle x\rangle^{-s}\nabla\widehat{\phi u}(\lambda-i\varepsilon)\Big\|_{L^{2}}\\

for

. If in addition we suppose

) and (

.

Next, by (

) and Plancherel’s theorem, there exist
C_{1},C_{2},C_{3},C>0

(4.7)

\begin{split}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}&(\|\langle x\rangle^{-s}\partial_{t}(\phi u)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\langle x\rangle^{-s}\nabla(\phi u)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\langle x\rangle^{-s}\phi u\|_{L^{2}}^{2})e^{-2\varepsilon t}dt\\
&=C_{1}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}(\|\langle x\rangle^{-s}\widehat{\partial_{t}(\phi u)}(\lambda-i\varepsilon)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\langle x\rangle^{-s}\nabla\widehat{\phi u}(\lambda-i\varepsilon)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\langle x\rangle^{-s}\widehat{\phi u}(\lambda-i\varepsilon)\|_{L^{2}}^{2})d\lambda\\

In this appendix we recall the Phragmén Lindelöf Theorem. Let

be a holomorphic function in a domain

of the complex plane with boundary

. We say that

does not exceed a number

.

(Phragmén Lindelöf Theorem
[
EM
]
)
.

, and

analytic on

does not exceed

. Suppose also there is a function

with the following properties:

(2)

\begin{split}\lim_{k\to\infty}\langle g,\langle&x\rangle^{-s}R_{0}(\lambda)(r\partial_{r})\tilde{u}_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}}\\
&=\lim_{k\to\infty}\langle(\partial_{r})^{*}rR_{0}(\overline{\lambda})\langle x\rangle^{-s}g,\tilde{u}_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}}\\
&=\langle(\partial_{r})^{*}rR_{0}(\overline{\lambda})\langle x\rangle^{-s}g,\langle x\rangle^{-s}f\rangle_{L^{2}}\\
&=\langle g,\langle x\rangle^{-s}R_{0}(\lambda)r\partial_{r}\langle x\rangle^{-s}f\rangle_{L^{2}}.\end{split}

as desired. Taken together, (
E.4
) and (
E.5
) confirm (
E.2
).

Now we turn to the case
|\lambda|\leq 1
, and utilize the integral kernel of the free resolvent, which is given by
[
JeNe01
, Section 3]
,

(E.6)

(-\Delta-\lambda^{2})^{-1}(|x-y|)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\left(\frac{-i\lambda}{2\pi|x-y|}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}-1}K_{\frac{n}{2}-1}(-i\lambda|x-y|),\qquad\mathop{\rm Im}\lambda>0,

where
K_{\nu}(z)
is the Macdonald function of order
\nu

[
DLMF
, 10.27.4, 10.27.5]
. Now, if
n=3
, then the integral kernel of
\langle x\rangle^{-s}\frac{d}{d\lambda}(-\Delta-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\langle x\rangle^{-s}
is given by
i(4\pi)^{-1}\langle x\rangle^{-s}e^{i\lambda|x-y|}\langle y\rangle^{-s}
, which has Hilbert-Schmidt norm bounded uniformly in
|\lambda|\leq 1
provided
s>3/2.
Moving on to
n\geq 4
, by
[
DLMF
, 10.29.2]
,

\frac{d}{d\lambda}\left(\frac{-i\lambda}{2\pi|x-y|}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}-1}K_{\frac{n}{2}-1}(-i\lambda|x-y|)=\frac{-(-i)^{\frac{n}{2}}\lambda^{\frac{n}{2}-1}}{(2\pi)^{\frac{n}{2}-1}|x-y|^{\frac{n}{2}-2}}K_{\frac{n}{2}-2}(-i\lambda|x-y|).

The Macdonald function satisfies
[
DLMF
, 10.25.3, 10.30.2, 10.30.3]

(E.7)

|K_{\nu}(z)|\leq\begin{cases}C|z|^{-\nu}&0<|z|\leq 1,\,\nu>0,\\
C|\ln|z||&0<|z|\leq 1,\,\nu=0,\\
C|z|^{-1/2}&|z|\geq 1,\,\mathop{\rm Re}z\geq 0,\,\nu\geq 0,\end{cases}

for
C>0
a constant independent of
z
.
Therefore, for
C>0
independent of
\lambda
,

(E.8)

\begin{split}\Big|&\frac{\lambda^{\frac{n}{2}-1}\langle x\rangle^{-s}\langle y\rangle^{-s}}{|x-y|^{\frac{n}{2}-2}}K_{\frac{n}{2}-2}(-i\lambda|x-y|)\Big|\\
&\leq\begin{cases}C|\lambda|\langle x\rangle^{-s}\langle y\rangle^{-s}|\ln(|\lambda||x-y|)|\mathbf{1}_{\{|\lambda||x-y|\leq 1\}}+C\frac{|\lambda|^{\frac{n}{2}-\frac{3}{2}}\langle x\rangle^{-s}\langle y\rangle^{-s}}{|x-y|^{\frac{n}{2}-\frac{3}{2}}}\mathbf{1}_{\{|\lambda||x-y|>1\}}&n=4,\\
C\frac{|\lambda|\langle x\rangle^{-s}\langle y\rangle^{-s}}{|x-y|^{n-4}}\mathbf{1}_{\{|\lambda||x-y|\leq 1\}}+C\frac{|\lambda|^{\frac{n}{2}-\frac{3}{2}}\langle x\rangle^{-s}\langle y\rangle^{-s}}{|x-y|^{\frac{n}{2}-\frac{3}{2}}}\mathbf{1}_{\{|\lambda||x-y|>1\}}&n>4.\end{cases}\end{split}

As preparation for the conclusions we draw in the next paragraph, we observe that the first term in line three of (
E.8
) has the bound, for
|\lambda|\leq 1
and
0<\alpha\leq 1
,

(E.9)

\frac{|\lambda|\langle x\rangle^{-s}\langle y\rangle^{-s}}{|x-y|^{n-4}}\mathbf{1}_{\{|\lambda||x-y|\leq 1\}}=\frac{|\lambda||x-y|^{\alpha}\langle x\rangle^{-s}\langle y\rangle^{-s}}{|x-y|^{n-4+\alpha}}\mathbf{1}_{\{|\lambda||x-y|\leq 1\}}\leq\frac{\langle x\rangle^{-s}\langle y\rangle^{-s}}{|x-y|^{n-4+\alpha}}\mathbf{1}_{\{|\lambda||x-y|\leq 1\}}.

In what follows we make repeated use of Lemma
F.3
to verify that a given kernel is Hilbert-Schmidt. In (
E.8
), the second term in line two and the second term in line three are are uniformly bounded in Hilbert-Schmidt norm for
|\lambda|\leq 1
, provided
s>(n+3)/4
. This also holds for the first term in line two of (
E.8
) if
s>3/2
. In addition, to address the first term in line three of (
E.8
), we utilize (
E.9
) in combination with Lemma
F.3
. Taken together they give that
\langle x\rangle^{-s}\langle y\rangle^{-s}|x-y|^{-n+4-\alpha}
is Hilbert-Schmidt

\begin{gathered}\text{when $n=5$ if $\alpha=1$ and $s>3/2$,}\\
\text{when $n=6$ if $0<\alpha<1$ and $s>2-(\alpha/2)$, and}\\
\text{when $n=7$ if $0<\alpha<1/2$ and $s>2-(\alpha/2)$.}\end{gathered}

In particular, when
n=6
it is enough to take
s>3/2
, while when
n=7
,
s>7/4
suffices.

Finally, if
n\geq 8
, the first term in line three is uniformly bounded
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
|\lambda|\leq 1
provided
s>3
. This is due to (
E.9
) with
\alpha=1
and the Schur test, see Lemma
F.2
.

We finish by resolving the
|\alpha|=1
case for (
3.5
). By (
3.5
) in the
|\alpha|=0
case, and by (
3.3
), we need to show
\|\lambda\langle x\rangle^{-s}(-\Delta-\lambda^{2})^{-2}\langle x\rangle^{-s}f\|_{H^{2}}\leq O(1+|\lambda|)\|f\|_{L^{2}}
. According to (
F.1
) below,

\begin{split}\|\lambda&\langle x\rangle^{-s}(-\Delta-\lambda^{2})^{-2}\langle x\rangle^{-s}f\|_{H^{2}}\\
&\leq C\|\lambda\langle x\rangle^{-s}(-\Delta-\lambda^{2})^{-2}\langle x\rangle^{-s}f\|_{L^{2}}+C\|\lambda\langle x\rangle^{-s}(-\Delta)(-\Delta-\lambda^{2})^{-2}\langle x\rangle^{-s}f\|_{L^{2}}\\
&=C\|f\|_{L^{2}}+C\|\lambda\langle x\rangle^{-s}(-\Delta)(-\Delta-\lambda^{2})^{-2}\langle x\rangle^{-s}f\|_{L^{2}}.\end{split}

Then use

\begin{split}\langle x\rangle^{-s}&(-\Delta)(-\Delta-\lambda^{2})^{-2}\langle x\rangle^{-s}f\\
&=\langle x\rangle^{-s}(-\Delta-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\langle x\rangle^{-s}f+\lambda^{2}\langle x\rangle^{-s}(-\Delta-\lambda^{2})^{-2}\langle x\rangle^{-s}f,\end{split}

which in combination with (
3.1
), as well as (
3.5
) in the
|\alpha|=0
case yields

\|\lambda\langle x\rangle^{-s}(-\Delta)(-\Delta-\lambda^{2})^{-2}\langle x\rangle^{-s}f\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(1+|\lambda|)\|f_{L^{2}},

completing the proof.

∎

Appendix F
Useful lemmas

Lemma F.1

(
[
Fa67
, Proposition 6]
)
.

Let
n\geq 3
. Then,

\|r^{-1}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\leq\Big(\frac{2}{n-2}\Big)^{2}\|\nabla u\|^{2}_{L^{2}},\qquad u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Lemma F.2

(Schur’s test
[
DyZw19
, Section A.5]
)
.

Suppose that
K(x,y)
is measurable on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
and

\sup_{x}\int|K(x,y)|dy,\,\sup_{y}\int|K(x,y)|dx\leq C.

Then the linear operator

Tf(x)=\int K(x,y)f(y)dy,

obeys the estimate

\|Tf\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}}.

Lemma F.3

(
[
Pe24
]
)
.

The necessary and sufficient conditions for

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\langle x\rangle^{-s}\langle y\rangle^{-t}|x-y|^{-p}dxdy<\infty,

are

s+p>n,\quad t+p>n,\quad s+p+t>2n,\quad p<n.

Lemma F.4
.

Suppose
T:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a bounded operator. For any
s>0
, there exists
C>0
so that

(F.1)

\|\langle x\rangle^{-s}T\|_{L^{2}\to H^{2}}\leq C(\|\langle x\rangle^{-s}T\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}+\|\langle x\rangle^{-s}\Delta T\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}).

Proof.

Let
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and put
u=Tf
. By the first line of (
3.3
), there exists
C>0
, whose precise value may change from line to line, so that

(F.2)

\|\langle x\rangle^{-s}u\|_{H^{2}}\leq C\|\langle x\rangle^{-s}u\|_{L^{2}}+C\|\Delta\langle x\rangle^{-s}u\|_{L^{2}},\qquad\tilde{u}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Then use the second line of (
3.3
),

\begin{split}\|\Delta\langle x\rangle^{-s}u\|_{L^{2}}&\leq\|[\Delta,\langle x\rangle^{-s}]u\|_{L^{2}}+\|\langle x\rangle^{-s}\Delta u\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\leq C\|\langle x\rangle^{-s}u\|_{H^{1}}+\|\langle x\rangle^{-s}\Delta u\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\leq C\gamma^{-1}\|\langle x\rangle^{-s}u\|_{L^{2}}+C\gamma\|\Delta\langle x\rangle^{-s}u\|_{L^{2}})+\|\langle x\rangle^{-s}\Delta u\|_{L^{2}},\qquad\gamma>0.\end{split}

Fixing
\gamma
small enough yields,

\|\Delta\langle x\rangle^{-s}u\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(\|\langle x\rangle^{-s}u\|_{L^{2}}+\|\langle x\rangle^{-s}\Delta u\|_{L^{2}}),

which in combination with (
F.2
) implies (
F.1
).

∎

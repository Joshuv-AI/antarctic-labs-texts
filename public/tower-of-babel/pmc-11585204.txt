Quality of life and associated characteristics in long-term care residents with advanced dementia in Macao: a cross-sectional study
Authors: Jianwei Wu, Kuai In Tam, Han Wang, Mingxia Zhu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe quality of life (QoL) of long-term care residents with advanced dementia represents a critical concern. However, empirical data on this topic especially within Chinese society remains relatively scarce.AimsThis study aimed to investigate the QoL in this population and identify the key factors affecting it.MethodsA cross-sectional study employing cluster sampling methodology was carried out in three long-term care facilities in Macao, involving a sample of 81 residents. The participants were assessed using the Functional Assessment Staging Test (FAST) and the Quality of Life in Late-Stage Dementia (QUALID) scale. Other variables recorded included Behavioral and Psychological Symptoms of Dementia (BPSD), comorbidities, PAINAD scores, and Waterlow scores.ResultsThe majority of participants were female (65.4%), and 70.1% were classified at Stage 7 on the FAST scale. The mean score on the QUALID was 23.94 ± 7.58, suggesting a relatively lower QoL in Macao, particularly in items of smiling, interaction, and touch. Multiple linear regression analysis indicated that higher PAINAD scores, presence of BPSD, and higher FAST stage were significantly associated with lower quality of life among long-term care residents with advanced dementia, explaining 65.9% of the total variance (F = 36.639, p < 0.001).ConclusionsThe findings indicated that residents with advanced dementia in Macao experience relatively lower quality of life, particularly in aspects such as smiling, interaction, and touch. Pain, BPSD, and advanced dementia stage are the three major associated factors of QoL among long-term care residents with advanced dementia. Comprehensive and individually tailored care strategies focusing on pain management and interventions targeting BPSD are suggested to enhance QoL in this population.Keywords: Quality of life, Long-term care, Advanced dementia, Macao

Introduction

Dementia is an umbrella term for a group of progressive neurodegenerative diseases affecting approximately 55 million people [1]. The prevalence of dementia is projected to increase to 75 million by 2030 and 132 million by 2050 [2], establishing it as a significant global health issue. As the seventh leading cause of death and a major cause of disability and dependency among the elderly worldwide [3], dementia affects individuals, their families, and communities. Advanced dementia is characterized by a substantial decline in awareness and cognitive function. Nevertheless, research suggests that individuals with severe cognitive impairments may retain some level of complex awareness. They can recognize pain, comment on their quality of life(QoL), and provide feedback on the care they receive [4]. QoL includes physical and mental health, autonomy, social interaction, and the person-environment relationship [5]. People with advanced dementia are at higher risk of experiencing frequent and severe behavioral and psychological symptoms, which contribute to a decline in quality of life, underscoring the need for focused care and attention [6]. Because there’s no cure for dementia, the research and management focus in long-term care facilities is primarily on improving the QoL for people living with dementia. To improve QoL for people with advanced dementia, the characteristics associated with QoL need to be identified.

Although several studies emphasized the necessity of a quality-of-life framework for people with dementia [7, 8]. Research on the quality of life in advanced dementia is relatively sparse, contrasting with the extensive focus on the early stages of the disease [9–13]. However, research indicated that the need for symptomatic relief in people with moderate to advanced dementia is comparable to those dying from cancer [14, 15]. And their care demands are often overlooked and their needs unmet, eg., cognitive, behavioral, and psychological symptoms are frequently experienced but not routinely addressed [16, 17]. Managing these symptoms becomes increasingly challenging as the disease progresses, complicating care delivery by healthcare professionals [18]. Upon reviewing literature related to the quality of life for people with advanced dementia [19–22], most of the studies were conducted in the developed Western countries, such as in Norway, Netherlands and Sweden. These studies confirm functional impairment, neuropsychiatric symptoms, and pain as prevalent determinants influencing QoL in dementia. Although a national survey on health-related quality of life in long-term care residents with dementia was recently conducted in Taiwan [23] and some other studies related to quality of life among nursing home residents conducted in Hong Kong [24, 25], there is still limited evidence on the characteristics and associated factors of quality of life in long-term care residents with advanced dementia in Chinese society.

Macao, a Special Administrative Region of China, has a population of 672,000, with 13.3% aged 65 years or older as of 2022 [26]. Approximately 6,000 individuals in Macao are living with dementia, of whom 3,000 have been formally diagnosed [27, 28]. A significant number of individuals with undiagnosed dementia reside within the community. In response to this public health challenge, the Macao SAR government initiated a dementia policy in 2016, which includes a 10-year strategic framework aimed at establishing Dementia-Friendly Communities (DFCs) [29]. Considering that age is a significant risk factor for dementia, the aging population in Macao is anticipated to lead to a rise in dementia cases. This trend presents considerable challenges to Macao's public and healthcare systems. However, there is a paucity of evidence regarding the quality of life for individuals with advanced dementia in long-term care facilities. Therefore, this study aimed to fill this knowledge gap by investigating the quality of life and characteristics of individuals with advanced dementia residing in long-term care facilities in Macao. The findings will provide valuable insights to support care processes and improve the quality of life for people with advanced dementia.

Materials and methods

Research design

A cross-sectional survey was conducted to assess the quality of life of people with advanced dementia in Macao using structured assessment tools.

Participants and procedure

The target population in this study comprised people living with advanced dementia in Macao. There were a total of 65 long-term care facilities throughout Macao. Participants were recruited from these settings using a two-step sampling approach to ensure representativeness and minimize bias. Firstly, three different long-term care facilities (one each for large, medium, and small accommodation capacities) were purposely selected from different districts. Secondly, all residents who had reached advanced dementia of 6d level according to the Functional Assessment Staging Test (FAST) [30] were invited to participate in the study. Considering adults with advanced dementia, assent was gained from the next-of-kin or the agreed family carer(s) of the participants. All next-of-kins or the agreed family carer(s) of participants were then thoroughly informed about the purpose of the study and provided written informed consent prior to participation. If there was no family carer(s) available, the research team sought assent from the agreed healthcare professional/healthcare institution responsible for making decisions for the participants. Exclusion criteria for individuals with advanced dementia include: those with severe hearing impairments, and those who may experience levels of distress that could make participation overwhelming.

Twelve health professionals from these facilities were recruited and trained as investigators. Training included a two-hour session on the assessment instruments, followed by a simulated case assessment to ensure consistency and accuracy. Data collection occurred from July to October 2020.

Measurements

For each individual with advanced dementia, demographic data, including age, gender, diagnosis date, comorbidities, and duration of stay in the facility, were collected. Additionally, four assessments were conducted.

The Functional Assessment Staging Scale (FAST) was employed to evaluate the functional performance of individuals who have progressed into the advanced stages of dementia. Due to the severity of the disease, information from FAST can be obtained through caregivers involved in their care. Ranging from normally functioning adult (stage 1) to severe dementia (stage 7), FAST assesses the individual's ability to perform multiple and complex daily activities, including handling personal finances, organizational capacity, ability to choose appropriate clothing, continence, and other mobility [30]. For this study, FAST was used to collect information regarding the functional level of participants with advanced dementia.

The Waterlow score was employed to assess the risk of pressure ulcer development [31]. People with advanced dementia are likely to develop skin injuries due to poor personal care in long-term care facilities [32]. The assessment includes risk factors such as body mass index, continence, tissue integrity, sex, age, mobility, and nutritional status. Individuals receive points from each risk factor category and are stratified into three risk categories: "At risk" (score of 10–14), "High risk" (score of 15–19), and "Very high risk" (score of 20 and above) [31].

The Pain Assessment in Advanced Dementia (PAINAD) scale is a tool designed to evaluate pain in individuals with advanced dementia who are incapable of verbally expressing their pain experiences. The validity and reliability of PAINAD in measuring pain among non-communicative individuals with advanced dementia were verified by Warden et al. [33]. The instrument comprises five items: breathing, negative vocalization, facial expression, body language, and consolability. Each item is scored on a scale from 0 to 2, resulting in a total score ranging from 0 to 10. Pain scores are interpreted as follows: 1 to 3 indicates mild pain, while 4 to 6 indicates moderate pain; and 7 to 10 indicates severe [33]. Other pre-existing conditions that may contribute to pain should also be taken into account, and information pertaining to the PAINAD tool must be acquired from the healthcare professionals responsible for the participants' care.

The Quality of Life in Late Stage Dementia Scale (QUALID) was employed to assess the quality of life among participants with dementia [34]. The QUALID scale comprises 11 items, each rated on a 5-point scale that evaluates the frequency of specific behaviors or conditions. The scale demonstrates robust reliability and internal consistency. The cumulative score ranges from 11 to 55, with a score of 11 indicating the highest quality of life. In accordance with the guidelines proposed by Weiner et al. [34], the QUALID assessments were completed by healthcare professionals who had 30 or more hours of exposure to the resident over the past week.

Statistical analysis

Data were checked for errors before double-entry input into SPSS 28. Descriptive statistics, including mean, standard deviation, frequency distribution, and percentage, were calculated. One-way ANOVA compared living conditions among participants, with the Brown-Forsythe test applied if variances were unequal. Variance Inflation Factor (VIF) assessed collinearity in multiple linear regression analysis, addressing collinearity if VIF exceeded 10. Multiple regression analysis identified the effects of independent variables on QUALID scores. Statistical significance was determined by a p-value ≤ 0.05 in two-tailed tests.

Results

A total of 81 individuals residing in three long-term care facilities and diagnosed with advanced dementia at a 6d level using the Functional Assessment Staging Test (FAST) were included in the study. Characteristics of quality of life in long-term care residents with advanced dementia in Macao

The present study assessed the quality of life of individuals with dementia using the Quality of Life in Late-Stage Dementia (QUALID) scale. The results of the QUALID scale ranged from 12 to 41, with a mean score of 23.94 ± 7.58. Among the 11 items of the QUALID scale, the top three items associated with higher quality of life were "Cries," "Is irritable or aggressive," and "Appears calm and comfortable," while the top three items associated with lower quality of life were "Smiles," "Enjoys interacting with others," and "Enjoys touching/being touched."

Moreover, One-way ANOVA was used to assess the quality of life among long-term care residents with advanced dementia. The results presented in Table 2 indicate that individuals exhibiting BPSD and those at Stage 7 on the FAST had significantly higher scores on the QUALID scale (F = 16.789, p < 0.001; F = 8.123, p = 0.006). Additionally, Pearson correlation analysis was performed between QUALID scores and other continuous variables such as age, duration of diagnosis, duration of residency in long-term care facilities, Waterlow score, and PAINAD score. As shown in Table 3, individuals with higher Waterlow scores or PAINAD scores were found to have higher QUALID scores (r = 0.303, p = 0.006; r = 0.753, p < 0.001). The findings suggest that individuals residing in long-term care facilities with advanced dementia, experiencing pain, exhibiting behavioral and psychological symptoms of dementia (BPSD), at a higher risk of pressure ulcers, and with more severe dementia likely have a diminished quality of life.

Factors associated with quality of life in long-term care residents with advanced dementia

Multiple linear regression was conducted to identify factors associated with the quality of life in long-term care residents with advanced dementia, with the QUALID score as the dependent variable. And four significant variables resulting from one-way ANOVA and Pearson correlation analysis (ie. Waterlow score, PAINAD score, BPSD, and FAST stage) were included as independent variables. As illustrated in Table 4, the PAINAD score, BPSD, and FAST stage were significantly associated with the quality of life, explaining 65.9% of the total variance (F = 36.639, p < 0.001). This analysis indicates that higher PAINAD scores, presence of BPSD, and higher FAST stage are significantly associated with lower quality of life among long-term care residents with advanced dementia. Other factors, including Waterlow score, gender, age, duration of diagnosis, duration of residency, and existing comorbidities, were not significantly associated with the quality of life in this population.

Discussion

Lower QoL in smiling, interaction, and touch among advanced dementia residents in Macao's long-term care facilities

As shown in Table 1, a significant portion of the population (39.5%) exhibited Behavioral and Psychological Symptoms of Dementia (BPSD), and all participants had comorbidities. The mean QUALID score of 23.94 ± 7.58 indicates a moderate level of quality of life (QoL) impairment. Comparatively, larger studies have reported lower QUALID scores, such as 19.8 ± 7.2 in a Norwegian nursing home study [19] and 22.34 ± 5.76 in a study of Hong Kong nursing home residents with dementia [25]. These differences suggest a poorer QoL in Macao. Additionally, the item-specific analysis of the QUALID scale revealed that the lower QoL was correlated with behaviors like smiling, enjoying interaction, and enjoying touch. While residents with advanced dementia may find some comfort in certain behaviors, their inability to engage socially or express joy significantly diminishes their quality of life [35].These findings underscore the necessity of implementing strategies aimed at enhancing behaviors such as smiling, enjoying interaction, and enjoying touch among long-term care residents with advanced dementia in Macao.

The high prevalence of BPSD and comorbidities among long-term care residents with advanced dementia in Macao could contribute to the observed lower QoL scores. Livingston et al. [36] pointed out that increased social interaction can significantly reduce agitation and improve QoL, highlighting the importance of social engagement in managing BPSD and enhancing overall well-being in dementia patients. As mentioned earlier, the item-specific analysis of the QUALID scale revealed that the lower QoL was correlated with behaviors like smiling, interaction, and touch. This suggests that limited interaction opportunities for residents in Macao might exacerbate symptoms like irritability and aggression, further diminishing their QoL. Integrating the findings from these seminal studies, it becomes clear that the QoL of long-term care residents with advanced dementia in Macao is relatively poorer and may be affected by various factors. The high prevalence of BPSD and comorbidities, coupled with inadequate social interaction, likely contribute to the lower QoL observed in this population.

Pain, BPSD, and functional stages associated with quality of life in long-term care residents with advanced dementia

The study highlighted the significant roles of pain, BPSD, and functional stages in predicting the QoL among long-term care residents with advanced dementia in Macao. These findings align with extant literature, which identifies functional impairment, neuropsychiatric symptoms, and pain as prevalent determinants influencing QoL in dementia [21, 37–40]. Participants experiencing severe pain reported a diminished quality of life (QoL), underscoring the imperative for effective pain management to improve overall well-being. This observation corroborates previous research indicating that pain and restraint are linked to reduced QoL [41] and highlighted the frequently underdiagnosed and undertreated nature of pain in dementia patients [42]. Additionally, residents displaying behavioral and psychological symptoms of dementia (BPSD), such as agitation or aggression, also reported lower QoL. Some studies found these symptoms are frequently experienced but not routinely addressed [16, 17]. This finding underscores the need for comprehensive strategies to manage these symptoms within care settings. The study further identified that individuals at more advanced stages of dementia (FAST Stage 7) exhibited diminished QoL. This observation highlights the progressive nature of dementia and the escalating difficulties in sustaining satisfactory QoL as the disease progresses. Prior research has demonstrated a correlation between greater functional impairment and reduced QoL [10, 43], although some studies propose an indirect relationship [37, 44]. These studies underscored the importance of functional performance in influencing QoL among long-term care residents with advanced dementia.

The finding that Pain, BPSD, and functional stages associated with quality of life in long-term care residents with advanced dementia can provide valuable insights to help educators and practitioners anticipate future interventions. As dementia progresses and residents become more frail, healthcare professionals will face greater challenges in delivering care. Tailoring educational training for nursing and care staff to improve pain assessment and BPSD management could be beneficial. Combining the above findings and related literature [36, 45]. Interventions should also focus on reducing BPSD and enhancing QoL through improved social interaction, comfort, and joy in activities.

Implications

The findings of this study have both theoretical and practical implications. This was the first study to describe the characteristics and associated factors of the quality of life in long-term care residents with advanced dementia in Macao, contributing to the limited research in the Chinese context. These results can help enhance the quality of life for long-term care residents with advanced dementia in Macao and other society. The research emphasizes the importance of a holistic approach that addresses both physical and psychological symptoms, including pain and BPSD. Additionally, individualized interventions for managing emotional and behavioral symptoms, such as training care staff in person-centered care and communication techniques, have proven integral in improving the overall state of residential care residents [46].

Enhancing social engagement through activities that promote social interaction and physical contact is critical for promoting emotional health [36]. Regular assessment of QoL using tools such as QUALID for persons living with dementia can assist in monitoring changes and, together with caregivers and health professionals, modifying care plans. Culturally appropriate strategies for interventions that focus on improving emotional and behavioral problems are more likely to have a greater impact on overall QoL. Effective interventions may include improved communication skills, training in person-centered care, or even training on dementia care mapping for caregivers, as proposed by Livingston et al. [35], to provide staff with insights into what residents with advanced dementia need.

Furthermore, there is evidence suggesting the need for policy changes related to implementing guidelines for pain management, BPSD interventions, and caregiver training programs. Long-term care settings can optimize quality-of-life improvements for residents with advanced dementia by addressing its wide-ranging consequences through holistic, person-centered, and culturally sensitive care approaches.

Limitations and future research

While this study has yielded numerous valuable insights, several limitations must be acknowledged. Firstly, the cross-sectional design precludes the establishment of causal relationships between variables. Future longitudinal studies are recommended to further elucidate the dynamics of quality of life in long-term care residents with advanced dementia. Additionally, the sample size(n = 81) in this research was relatively small and exclusively comprised care facilities in Macao, which may limit the generalizability of the findings to other regions or cultural contexts. Future research should investigate these factors within larger and more diverse populations to enhance the generalizability of the findings. Additionally, while pain, behavioral and psychological symptoms of dementia (BPSD), and functional stage were identified as significant associated factors of quality of life, other variables highlighted in prior studies, such as caregiver burden and social support, were not comprehensively assessed [47, 48]. Incorporating these variables in subsequent studies may yield a more holistic understanding of the determinants of quality of life in this demographic.

Conclusions

This study offers significant insights into the quality of life and associated characteristics of long-term care residents with advanced dementia, thereby contributing to the limited body of research on this topic within a Chinese context. The findings indicated that residents with advanced dementia in Macao experience relatively lower quality of life, particularly in aspects such as smiling, interaction, and touch. Furthermore, the study underscored the critical importance of effective pain management, attention to behavioral and psychological symptoms of dementia (BPSD), and consideration of functional stages to enhance the well-being of this vulnerable population. Specifically, residents experiencing higher levels of pain, exhibiting BPSD, and being in more advanced stages of the disease reported a diminished quality of life, thereby necessitating targeted interventions to mitigate these distressing conditions. The findings underscored the importance of a person-centered, holistic approach in care delivery, which addresses the physical, psychological, and social needs of individuals with advanced dementia, to optimize their quality of life across various care settings.

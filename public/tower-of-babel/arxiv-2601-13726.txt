Central Limit Theorems in Multiplicative Diophantine Approximation
Michael Björklund, Reynold Fregoli, Alexander Gorodnik

Abstract
. We investigate the number of integer solutions to a multiplicative Diophantine approximation problem and show that the associated counting function converges in distribution to a normal law. Our approach relies on the analysis of correlations of measures on homogeneous spaces, together with estimates for Siegel transforms restricted to subspaces.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary: 11K60, Secondary: 37A25, 37A44

1.
Introduction

For a pair of real numbers
\underline{x}=(x_{1},x_{2})
, we consider the products

q\,\|qx_{1}\|\,\|qx_{2}\|,\qquad q\in\mathbb{N},

where
\|\cdot\|
denotes the distance to the nearest integer. Our interest lies in small values of these products. Accordingly, for
b\in(0,1)
and
T>0
, we introduce the counting function

N_{b,T}(\underline{x}):=\big|\{q\in\mathbb{N}:\ q\,\|qx_{1}\|\,\|qx_{2}\|<b,\ 1\leq q<T\}\big|.

Throughout the paper, both
b
and
T
are treated as varying parameters. For example, in Theorem
1.1
below one may take
b=T^{-\eta}
with
\eta<6/5
.

The asymptotic behavior of this and related counting functions has been studied in

[
18
,
19
,
1
]
. It is convenient to interpret this problem in terms of lattice
point counting. Indeed, consider the domains

(1.1)

\Omega_{b,T}:=\Big\{(x_{1},x_{2},y)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:\ |x_{1}|\,|x_{2}|\,y<b,\ |x_{1}|,|x_{2}|\leq\tfrac{1}{2},\ 1\leq y<T\Big\}.

When
\underline{x}
has irrational coordinates,
N_{b,T}(\underline{x})
coincides
with the number of integer triples
(p_{1},p_{2},q)\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}
such that

(qx_{1}+p_{1},qx_{2}+p_{2},q)\in\Omega_{b,T}.

It was shown in
[
1
]
that, under suitable conditions on the parameters
b
and

T
, for almost every
\underline{x}
,

N_{b,T}(\underline{x})\sim\Vol(\Omega_{b,T})\qquad\text{as }T\to\infty.

The main objective of the present paper is to study the distributional behavior of
the functions
N_{b,T}
. In particular, we establish the following central limit theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that

(\ln T)^{-6/5+\theta}\leq b<1\qquad\text{for some }\theta>0.

Then the normalized functions

D_{b,T}(\underline{x}):=\frac{N_{b,T}(\underline{x})-\Vol(\Omega_{b,T})}{\Vol(\Omega_{b,T})^{1/2}},\qquad\underline{x}\in[0,1)^{2},

converge in distribution, as
T\to\infty
, to a non-degenerate normal law. More precisely, for every

\xi\in\mathbb{R}
,

\Vol\Big(\big\{\underline{x}\in[0,1)^{2}:\ D_{b,T}(\underline{x})<\xi\big\}\Big)\longrightarrow\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma}\int_{-\infty}^{\xi}e^{-t^{2}/(2\sigma^{2})}\,dt,

where

\sigma^{2}:=\sum_{p,q\in\mathbb{N}}\Vol\big(p^{-1}\cdot\Delta_{\infty}\cap q^{-1}\cdot\Delta\big)>0,

with

\displaystyle\Delta_{\infty}

\displaystyle:=\big\{(x_{1},x_{2},y)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:\ 0<|x_{1}x_{2}|\,y\leq 1\big\},

\displaystyle\Delta

\displaystyle:=\big\{(x_{1},x_{2},y)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:\ 0<|x_{1}x_{2}|\,y\leq 1,\ (2e)^{-1}<|x_{1}|,|x_{2}|\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\big\}.

More generally, fix
c\in(0,1)
. For parameters
0\leq a<b<1
and
T>1
, we consider
the counting function

(1.2)

N_{a,b,T}(\underline{x}):=\left|\left\{(p_{1},p_{2},q)\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}:\ \begin{array}[]{l}a<|qx_{1}-p_{1}|\,|qx_{2}-p_{2}|\,q<b,\\[2.0pt]
|qx_{1}-p_{1}|,\,|qx_{2}-p_{2}|\leq c,\quad 1\leq q<T\end{array}\right\}\right|.

Associated to this counting function is the family of domains

(1.3)

\Omega_{a,b,T}:=\left\{(x_{1},x_{2},y)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:\ a<|x_{1}|\,|x_{2}|\,y<b,\ |x_{1}|,|x_{2}|\leq c,\ 1\leq y<T\right\}.

We establish the following generalization of Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume that

a\leq b\,(\ln\ln T)^{-4-\theta}\qquad\text{and}\qquad(\ln T)^{-6/5+\theta}\leq b<1

for some
\theta>0
. Then the normalized functions

D_{a,b,T}(\underline{x}):=\frac{N_{a,b,T}(\underline{x})-\Vol(\Omega_{a,b,T})}{\Vol(\Omega_{a,b,T})^{1/2}},\qquad\underline{x}\in[0,1)^{2},

converge in distribution, as
T\to\infty
, to a normal law with variance

\sigma_{c}^{2}:=\sum_{p,q\in\mathbb{N}}\Vol\big(p^{-1}\cdot\Delta_{\infty}\cap q^{-1}\cdot\Delta_{c}\big),

where

(1.4)

\Delta_{c}:=\left\{(x_{1},x_{2},y)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:\ 0<|x_{1}x_{2}|\,y\leq 1,\ e^{-1}c<|x_{1}|,|x_{2}|\leq c\right\}.

In the one-dimensional setting, the distribution of the counting function for the
number of integer solutions
(p,q)
to the inequalities

|qx-p|<\psi(q),\qquad 1\leq q<T,

was studied in
[
12
,
13
,
14
,
8
]
using continued fraction techniques, where a
central limit theorem was established.

In higher dimensions, a central limit theorem was proved in

[
6
,
3
]
for the number of integer solutions
(p_{1},\ldots,p_{n},q)

to the system of inequalities

|qx_{1}-p_{1}|<b_{1}q^{-w_{1}},\ \ldots,\ |qx_{n}-p_{n}|<b_{n}q^{-w_{n}},\qquad 1\leq q<T,

with fixed constants
b_{i}
and exponents satisfying

w_{1}+\cdots+w_{n}=1
.

While the present paper follows the general approach developed in

[
3
]
, the multiplicative setting considered here presents several
new challenges, which we outline in the next section. A related
multiplicative Diophantine approximation problem involving products of
linear forms was studied in our earlier work
[
5
]
. That work relied
on a different tessellation construction and the method was effective
only in dimensions greater than nine.

2.
Outline of the Proof Strategy

Our approach relies on analysis on the space
X
of unimodular lattices in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
The connection with the original counting problem is provided by the Siegel transform.
For a compactly supported function
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}
, its Siegel transform

\widehat{f}\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
is defined by

\widehat{f}(\Lambda):=\sum_{\underline{\lambda}\in\Lambda\setminus\{0\}}f(\underline{\lambda}),\qquad\Lambda\in X.

To simplify notation, we suppress the dependence on the parameters
a,b,T
and write

\Omega:=\Omega_{a,b,T}
for the domain defined in (
1.1
).
The counting function (
1.2
) can then be interpreted as a function on
X
:

N_{a,b,T}(\underline{x})=|\Lambda_{\underline{x}}\cap\Omega|=\widehat{\chi}_{\Omega}(\Lambda_{\underline{x}}),

where

\Lambda_{\underline{x}}:=\big\{(qx_{1}+p_{1},qx_{2}+p_{2},q):\ p_{1},p_{2},q\in\mathbb{Z}\big\}\in X.

To prove our main results, we analyze the moments of
N_{a,b,T}
.

In Section
3
, we introduce a tessellation of
\Omega
of the form

\Omega=\bigsqcup_{n\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}}a(n)^{-1}\Delta_{\Omega,n},

constructed using the diagonal transformations

a(t):=\operatorname{diag}\big(e^{t_{1}},e^{t_{2}},e^{-t_{1}-t_{2}}\big),\qquad t=(t_{1},t_{2})\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}.

This decomposition allows us to write

\widehat{\chi}_{\Omega}(\Lambda_{\underline{x}})=\sum_{n\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}}\widehat{\chi}_{\Delta_{\Omega,n}}\big(a(n)\Lambda_{\underline{x}}\big),

and hence to express the moments as

\int_{[0,1)^{2}}N_{a,b,T}(\underline{x})^{r}\,d\underline{x}=\sum_{(n_{1},\ldots,n_{r})\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}^{r}}\int_{[0,1)^{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{r}\widehat{\chi}_{\Delta_{\Omega,n_{i}}}\big(a(n_{i})\Lambda_{\underline{x}}\big)\,d\underline{x}.

In practice, we work with smooth compactly supported approximations of the functions

\widehat{\chi}_{\Delta_{\Omega,n_{i}}}
, constructed in Section
7
.
The above formula shows that the analysis of moments of
N_{a,b,T}
reduces,
to a large extent, to understanding the convergence of certain measures on the product space
X^{r}
.
Let
\nu
denote the measure on
X
supported on the family of lattices
\Lambda_{\underline{x}}
,

\underline{x}\in[0,1)^{2}
, and let
\nu_{r}^{\Delta}
be its image under the diagonal embedding into
X^{r}
.
The problem then becomes the study of the push-forward measures

(a(t_{1}),\ldots,a(t_{r}))_{*}\nu_{r}^{\Delta},\qquad t_{1},\ldots,t_{r}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2},

on
X^{r}
.

This question was investigated in our companion paper
[
2
]
, and the relevant results are summarized in Section
4
.
In contrast to the one-parameter setting considered in
[
3
]
, this family of measures
admits many accumulation points supported on proper homogeneous subspaces of
X^{r}
(see Theorem
4.2
).
Excluding the basins of attraction of these limiting measures—where correlations can be analyzed using Theorem
4.2
—still leaves a large number of terms whose total contribution is not
o\big(\Vol(\Omega)^{1/2}\big)
.

A key new ingredient is a decorrelation estimate (Theorem
4.1
),
in which the error term depends only on the mutual distances
|t_{i}-t_{j}|
.
This estimate plays a crucial role in the analysis of correlation sums and ultimately allows us
to exploit cancellations in cumulants, making it possible to control higher-order moments.

The presence of limiting measures supported on proper homogeneous subspaces leads to an additional difficulty:
to obtain effective bounds, we also need estimates for the
L^{p}
-norms of Siegel transforms restricted to these subspaces.
This analysis is carried out in Section
5
.
The resulting estimates are independent of the rest of the paper and may be of interest for other problems in multiplicative Diophantine approximation.

Using these Siegel transform bounds, we analyze the variance in Sections
6
and
8
.
In Section
6
, we treat the contribution coming from basins of attraction of proper limiting measures,
while Section
8
deals with the remaining terms.
Higher-order cumulants are handled in Section
9
.
Finally, the proof of the main theorem is completed in Section
10
.
Combining the variance and cumulant estimates obtained in these sections,
we deduce convergence to the normal law under suitable assumptions on the parameters.

Our method requires an additional condition of the form
a\geq(\ln T)^{-K}
for some
K>0
.
The complementary case is handled by an application of a Borel–Cantelli argument (see Lemma
10.1
).

3.
Tessellations

Throughout the paper, we work with the sets

\Omega:=\Omega_{a,b,T}
introduced in (
1.3
)
depending on
0<a<b<1
,
0<c<1
, and
T>1
.
While
c
is fixed through the paper,
a,b,T
are viewed as varying parameter. A direct computation gives that

V_{\Omega}:=\Vol(\Omega)=2(\ln T)^{2}(b-a)+4(\ln T)\left((b-a)+b\ln\left(b^{-1}c^{2}\right)-a\ln\left(a^{-1}c^{2}\right)\right)+O_{c}(1).

In particular, it follows that

(3.1)

V_{\Omega}:=\Vol(\Omega)\sim 2(\ln T)^{2}(b-a)\quad\hbox{as $T\to\infty$}

when

a\gg(\ln T)^{-K}\;\;\hbox{for some $K>0$}\quad\textrm{and}\quad(\ln T)^{2}(b-a)\to\infty.

A crucial ingredient of our argument is the tessellation constructed in our previous work
[
1
, Lemma 3.1]
:

(3.2)

\Omega=\bigsqcup_{n\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}}a({n})^{-1}\Delta_{\Omega,{n}},

where

\displaystyle\Delta_{\Omega,{n}}

\displaystyle:=\left\{(x_{1},x_{2},y)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:\;\begin{tabular}[]{l}$a<|x_{1}x_{2}|\,y\leq b,\;e^{-1}c<|x_{1}|,\,|x_{2}|\leq c$\\
$e^{-n_{1}-n_{2}}\leq y<Te^{-n_{1}-n_{2}}$\end{tabular}\right\},

(3.5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}

\displaystyle:=\left\{n\in\mathbb{N}_{o}^{2}\,:\,\alpha_{\Omega}\leq n_{1}+n_{2}<\beta_{\Omega}\right\}

with

(3.6)

\alpha_{\Omega}:=\ln\left(e^{-2}b^{-1}c^{2}\right)\quad\textrm{and}\quad\beta_{\Omega}:=\ln\left(Ta^{-1}c^{2}\right).

We have

(3.7)

|\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}|\leq(\beta_{\Omega}+1)(\beta_{\Omega}-\alpha_{\Omega}+1)\ll_{c}(\ln T)^{2}.

One can check (see
[
1
, Lemma 3.1]
) that

(3.8)

\Delta_{\Omega,{n}}\subset\left[-c,c\right]^{2}\times\left({a}{c^{-2}},e^{2}{b}{c^{-2}}\right]\quad\hbox{for all $n$.
}

We use a certain regularity property of the sets
\Delta_{\Omega,{n}}
, which is the content of

[
1
, Lemma 4.3]
. For
\varepsilon>0
, let
V_{\varepsilon}
denote
the symmetric open neighborhood of the identity in
\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{R})

defined by

(3.9)

V_{\varepsilon}:=\{g\in\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{R}):\|g-\textup{id}\|_{\textup{op}},\|g^{-1}-\textup{id}\|_{\textup{op}}<\varepsilon\},

where
\|\cdot\|_{\textup{op}}
stands for the operator norm of a matrix induced by the supremum norm on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
For
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}
, we write

E^{y}:=\{(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\,:\,(x_{1},x_{2},y)\in E\}.

We are interested in comparing the sets
\Delta_{\Omega,{n}}
and
g^{-1}\Delta_{\Omega,{n}}
with
g\in V_{\varepsilon}
.
In view of (
3.8
), the sets
\Delta_{\Omega,{n}}
satisfy the conditions of

[
1
, Lemma 4.3]
with
M=e^{2}c^{-2}
and
\gamma=ac^{-2}
.
Therefore, we deduce from this lemma that for every
\varepsilon\in(0,O_{c}(a))
, there exist
sets
E_{s}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
,
s=1,\ldots,24
, such that

(3.10)

\left(g^{-1}\Delta_{\Omega,{n}}\setminus\Delta_{\Omega,{n}}\right)\sqcup\left(\Delta_{\Omega,{n}}\setminus g^{-1}\Delta_{\Omega,{n}}\right)\subset\bigcup_{s}E_{s}\quad\hbox{for all $g\in V_{\varepsilon}$, }

where each of these sets satisfies

(3.11)

E_{s}\subset\left[-(e^{2}c^{-2}+1),e^{2}c^{-2}+1\right]^{2}\times\left(ac^{-2}/2,e^{2}c^{-2}+1\right]

and

(3.12)

\Vol(E_{s}^{y})\ll_{c}\max\left(\varepsilon,-\frac{\varepsilon}{a}\ln\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{a}\right)\right)\quad\hbox{for all $y$,}

or there is an interval
[\alpha,\beta]
such that

(3.13)

E_{s}\subset\left[-(e^{2}c^{-2}+1),e^{2}c^{-2}+1\right]^{2}\times[\alpha,\beta],\quad\beta-\alpha\ll_{c}\varepsilon,\quad\alpha\geq ac^{-2}/4.

In particular, it follows from (
3.12
)–(
3.13
) that

(3.14)

\Vol(E_{s})\ll_{c}\max\left(\varepsilon,-\frac{\varepsilon}{a}\ln\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{a}\right)\right).

We also note that the sets
E_{s}
constructed in
[
1
, Lemma 4.3]
are given by finitely many linear inequalities.

4.
Correlations on homogeneous spaces

Our analysis is carried out on the space of unimodular three-dimensional lattices

X\simeq G/\Gamma,\quad\hbox{where $G:=\hbox{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{R})$ and $\Gamma:=\hbox{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{Z})$,}

equipped with the probability invariant measure
\mu
.
We consider the homogeneous subspace

(4.1)

Y:=U\Gamma\subset X,\quad\hbox{where $U:=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&*\\
0&1&*\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix}$},

equipped with the probability invariant measure
\nu
.
The crucial question that we will need to solve for the present work and which was explored in details in our companion paper
[
2
]

is the limiting behavior of the translated measures
a(t)_{*}\nu
with
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
and their correlations.
In particular, we use the following decorrelation estimate for these measures, where
|\cdot|
denotes
the
\max
-norm on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
\|\cdot\|_{C^{\ell}}
stands for the
C^{\ell}
-norm on the space of smooth functions on
X
.

Theorem 4.1

(
[
2
]
,Theorem 1.4)
.

Let
r\in\mathbb{N}
. There exist
\ell\geq 1
and
\eta_{r}>0
such that for any

t_{1},\dotsc,t_{r}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
and

\varphi_{1},\dotsc,\varphi_{r}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(X)
,

\left|\int_{Y}\prod_{i=1}^{r}\varphi_{i}\circ a(t_{i})\,d\nu-\prod_{i=1}^{r}\int_{Y}\varphi_{i}\circ a(t_{i})\,d\nu\right|\ll_{r}e^{-\eta_{r}\min_{i\neq j}|t_{i}-t_{j}|}\prod_{i=1}^{r}\|\varphi_{i}\|_{C^{\ell}}.

Depending on the parameter
t
, the measures
a(t)_{*}\nu
converge to measures supported on different homogeneous subspaces. The following homogeneous subspaces come into play:

(4.2)

Y_{1}:=G_{1}\Gamma\;\;\hbox{and}\;\;Y_{2}:=G_{2}\Gamma\quad\hbox{ with
$G_{1}:=\begin{pmatrix}*&0&*\\
*&1&*\\
*&0&*\end{pmatrix}$
\;\;\hbox{and}\;\; $G_{2}:=\begin{pmatrix}1&*&*\\
0&*&*\\
0&*&*\end{pmatrix}$
}.

We denote by
\nu_{1}
and
\nu_{2}
the invariant probability measures supported on
Y_{1}
and
Y_{2}
respectively.
One can check that

a(t_{1},0)_{*}\nu\to\nu_{1},\quad a(0,t_{2})_{*}\nu\to\nu_{2},\quad a(t_{1},t_{2})_{*}\nu\to\mu

as
t_{1},t_{2}\to\infty
.
Theorem
4.2
below gives
a generalization of this property.
For
I\subset\{1,2\}
, we set

Y_{I}:=\begin{cases}Y\quad\mbox{if }I=\emptyset,\\
Y_{i}\quad\mbox{if }I=\{i\}\mbox{ with }i=1,2,\\
X\quad\mbox{if }I=\{1,2\}.\end{cases}

and denote by
\nu_{I}
the corresponding measures supported on
Y_{I}
.
For
t\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we write
\lfloor t\rfloor_{I}:=\min\{t_{i}:i\in I\}
.

Theorem 4.2

(
[
2
]
, Theorem 6.1)
.

Let
r\in\mathbb{N}
. There exist
\ell\geq 1
and
\gamma_{r}>0
such that
for any
I_{1},\ldots,I_{r}\subset\{1,2\}
,
t_{1},\dotsc,t_{r}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
with
(t_{i})_{k}=0
for
k\notin I_{i}
,
and
\varphi_{1},\dotsc,\varphi_{r}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(X)
,

(4.3)

\left|\int_{Y}\prod_{i=1}^{r}\varphi_{i}\circ a(t_{i})\,d\nu-\prod_{i=1}^{r}\int_{Y_{I_{i}}}\varphi_{i}\,d\nu_{I_{i}}\right|\\
\ll_{r}e^{-\gamma_{r}\min\big(\min_{i}\lfloor t_{i}\rfloor_{I_{i}},\,\min_{i\neq j}|t_{i}-t_{j}|\big)}\prod_{i=1}^{r}\|\varphi_{i}\|_{C^{\ell}}.

In the rest of the paper, we work with fixed sufficiently large
\ell
such that the above estimates apply.

5.
Moments of Siegel transforms

5.1.
Basic estimates

We start by recalling the following classical formulas
for the Siegel transform.

Theorem 5.1

(Siegel
[
17
]
)
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}
be a bounded Riemann-integrable function with compact support.
Then
\widehat{f}\in L^{1}(X)
and

\int_{X}\widehat{f}\,d\mu=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f(\underline{z})\,d\underline{z}.

Theorem 5.2

(Rogers
[
15
]
)
.

Let
f,g:\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}
be nonnegative Borel functions with compact support.
Then
\widehat{f}\cdot\widehat{g}\in{L}^{1}(X)
and

\displaystyle\int_{X}\widehat{f}\cdot\widehat{g}\,d\mu=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f(\underline{z})\,d\underline{z}\right)\cdot\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}g(\underline{z})\,d\underline{z}\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{p,q\in\mathbb{\mathbb{N}}}\zeta(3)^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f(p\underline{z})\cdot g(q\underline{z})\,d\underline{z}+\sum_{p,q\in\mathbb{\mathbb{N}}}\zeta(3)^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f(p\underline{z})\cdot g(-q\underline{z})\,d\underline{z}.

In particular, using the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we get a following estimate:

Corollary 5.3
.

For a nonnegative Borel function
f:\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}
with compact support,

\int_{X}\widehat{f}^{2}\,d\mu\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f(\underline{z})\,d\underline{z}\right)^{2}+2\zeta(3)^{-1}\zeta(3/2)^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f(\underline{z})^{2}\,d\underline{z}.

Further, we recall that Siegel transforms can be bounded above in terms of the successive minima of a lattice. To be precise, let
\{\underline{e}_{1},\underline{e}_{2},\underline{e}_{3}\}
denote the standard (ordered) basis of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. We extend the
\max
-norm with respect to this basis to the second exterior power
\mathbb{R}^{3}\wedge\mathbb{R}^{3}
as follows. For
\underline{u},\underline{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
and

\underline{w}:=\underline{u}\wedge\underline{v}=w_{12}\,\underline{e}_{1}\wedge\underline{e}_{2}+w_{13}\,\underline{e}_{1}\wedge\underline{e}_{3}+w_{23}\,\underline{e}_{2}\wedge\underline{e}_{3},

we set
\|\underline{w}\|_{\infty}:=\max(|w_{12}|,|w_{13}|,|w_{23}|)
.
For a (not necessarily unimodular) lattice
\Lambda
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
the successive minima are defined as:

\displaystyle s_{1}(\Lambda)

\displaystyle:=\min\big\{\|\underline{\lambda}\|_{\infty}\,:\,\underline{\lambda}\in\Lambda\setminus\{0\}\big\},

\displaystyle s_{1}^{*}(\Lambda)

\displaystyle:=\min\big\{\|\underline{\lambda}_{1}\wedge\underline{\lambda}_{2}\|_{\infty}\,:\,\underline{\lambda}_{1}\wedge\underline{\lambda}_{2}\neq 0,\enskip\underline{\lambda}_{1},\underline{\lambda}_{2}\in\Lambda\setminus\{0\}\big\},

\displaystyle d(\Lambda)

\displaystyle:=\min\big\{|\alpha|\,:\,\alpha\,\underline{e}_{1}\wedge\underline{e}_{2}\,\wedge\underline{e}_{3}=\underline{\lambda}_{1}\wedge\underline{\lambda}_{2}\wedge\underline{\lambda}_{3}\neq 0,\enskip\underline{\lambda}_{1},\underline{\lambda}_{2},\underline{\lambda}_{3}\in\Lambda\setminus\{0\}\big\},

Then we define the height function as

(5.1)

\height(\Lambda):=\min\big(s_{1}(\Lambda),s_{1}^{*}(\Lambda),d(\Lambda)\big)^{-1}.

In terms of these notations, we have the following estimate:

Lemma 5.4

(W. Schmidt
[
16
]
)
.

For a Borel function
f:\mathbb{R}^{3}\to[0,1]
with compact support,

\widehat{f}\ll_{\supp(f)}\height.

We further recall (see, for instance,
[
7
, Lemma 3.10]
)

Lemma 5.5
.

For
p<3
,
\height\in L^{p}(\mu)
In particular, for every
L>0
and
\delta>0
,

\mu\big(\{\height\geq L\}\big)\ll_{\delta}L^{-3+\delta}.

5.2.
Estimates on homogeneous subspaces

To carry out our argument, we will require upper bounds for the moments of Siegel transforms on the spaces
Y,Y_{1},Y_{2}
, and
X
. The following four propositions provide the required bounds.
The proofs are independent of the rest of the paper and can be skipped for the first reading.

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{3}\to[0,1]
be a measurable function whose support is contained in
[-c,c]^{2}\times[\xi,\beta]
for some
c>0
and
0<\xi<\beta
.
Then for any
n\in\mathbb{N}^{2}_{o}
,

\displaystyle\int_{Y}\widehat{f}\circ a({n})\,d\nu\ll_{c}\beta.

Moreover, if we assume that the function

A(y):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f(x_{1},x_{2},y)\,dx_{1}dx_{2}

is
piecewise
C^{1}
with discontinuities in at most
s
points
and satisfies

(5.2)

|A|\leq M\quad\textrm{and}\quad|A^{\prime}|\leq M^{\prime}\quad\;\;\hbox{for some $M,M^{\prime}>0$},

then

\int_{Y}\widehat{f}\circ a({n})\,d\nu=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f\,d\underline{x}+O_{s,\beta}\Big((M+M^{\prime})e^{-n_{1}-n_{2}}\Big).

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\int_{Y}\widehat{f}\circ a({n})\,d\nu

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,1)^{2}}\sum_{(p_{1},p_{2},q)\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}}f\big(e^{n_{1}}(\alpha_{1}q+p_{1}),e^{n_{2}}(\alpha_{2}q+p_{2}),e^{-n_{1}-n_{2}}q\big)\,d\underline{\alpha}.

For fixed positive
q
, applying a change of variables, we deduce that

\displaystyle\int_{[0,1)^{2}}\sum_{(p_{1},p_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}f\big(e^{n_{1}}(\alpha_{1}q+p_{1}),e^{n_{2}}(\alpha_{2}q+p_{2}),e^{-n_{1}-n_{2}}q\big)\,d\underline{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{[0,1)^{2}}\sum_{h_{1},h_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{k_{1},k_{2}=0}^{q-1}\int_{[0,1)^{2}}f\big(e^{n_{1}}((\alpha_{1}+h_{1})q+k_{1}),e^{n_{2}}((\alpha_{2}+h_{2})q+k_{2}),e^{-n_{1}-n_{2}}q\big)\,d\underline{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k_{1},k_{2}=0}^{q-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f\big(e^{n_{1}}(\alpha_{1}q+k_{1}),e^{n_{2}}(\alpha_{2}q+k_{2}),e^{-n_{1}-n_{2}}q\big)\,d\underline{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,e^{-n_{1}-n_{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f\big(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},e^{-n_{1}-n_{2}}q\big)\,d\underline{\alpha}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\int_{Y}\widehat{f}\circ a({n})\,d\nu

\displaystyle=e^{-n_{1}-n_{2}}\sum_{q\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f\big(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},e^{-n_{1}-n_{2}}q\big)\,d\underline{\alpha}

When
e^{n_{1}+n_{2}}\beta<1
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{Y}\widehat{f}\circ a({n})\,d\nu=0,

and when
e^{n_{1}+n_{2}}\beta\geq 1
, the number of terms in the sum is at most

\lfloor e^{n_{1}+n_{2}}\beta\rfloor
, so that

\displaystyle\int_{Y}\widehat{f}\circ a({n})\,d\nu\ll_{c}\beta,

which verifies the first bound.
The second part of the proposition follows readily from the following lemma.
∎

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
A:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a piecewise
C^{1}
-function supported on the interval
[\xi,\beta]
with discontinuities at most
s
points
which satisfies (
5.2
).
Then for every
T\geq 1
,

{T}^{-1}\sum_{q\in\mathbb{Z}}A\left({q}/{T}\right)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}A(y)\,dy+O\Big((sM+(\beta-\xi+2)M^{\prime})T^{-1}\Big).

Proof.

We pick
q_{1},q_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
, so that
q_{1}/T\leq\xi<(q_{1}+1)/T
and
q_{2}/T\leq\beta<(q_{2}+1)/T
. Then

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}A(y)\,dy=\int_{q_{1}/T}^{(q_{2}+1)/T}A(y)\,dy=\sum_{q=q_{1}}^{q_{2}}\int_{q/T}^{(q+1)/T}A(y)\,dy.

When
A
is differentiable on
[{q/T},{(q+1)/T}]
,
it follows from the Mean Value Theorem that

\int_{q/T}^{(q+1)/T}A(y)\,dy=A(q/T)/T+O(M^{\prime}T^{-2}).

This bound applies to all but
2s
intervals, so that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}A(y)\,dy

\displaystyle=\sum_{q=q_{1}}^{q_{2}}A(q/T)/T+O\Big(4sM/T+(T(\beta-\xi)+2)M^{\prime}T^{-2}\Big)

\displaystyle=T^{-1}\sum_{q\in\mathbb{Z}}A(q/T)+O\Big((sM+(\beta-\xi+2)M^{\prime})T^{-1}\Big),

which proves the lemma.
∎

a(n)_{*}\nu

Lemma 5.8
.

For every
L>1
and
n\in\mathbb{N}^{2}_{o}
,

\nu\Big(\{\textup{ht}\circ a(n)\geq L\}\Big)\ll L^{-3}+L^{-2}e^{-\lfloor n\rfloor}.

Proof.

We have

\{\underline{\alpha}\in[0,1)^{2}:\textup{ht}(a(n)\Lambda_{\alpha})\geq L\}\subset B(n,L^{-1})\cup B^{*}(n,L^{-1}),

where

\displaystyle B(n,\eta)

\displaystyle:=\{\underline{\alpha}\in[0,1)^{2}:s_{1}(a(n)\Lambda_{\alpha})\leq\eta\},

\displaystyle B^{*}(n,\eta)

\displaystyle:=\{\underline{\alpha}\in[0,1)^{2}:s^{*}_{1}(a(n)\Lambda_{\alpha})\leq\eta\}.

Let
\eta\in(0,1)
.
We observe that
\underline{\alpha}\in B(n,\eta)
iff there exists
(p_{1},p_{2},q)\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}\backslash\{0\}
such that

(5.3)

|q\alpha_{1}-p_{1}|\leq\eta e^{-n_{1}},\;\;|q\alpha_{2}-p_{2}|\leq\eta e^{-n_{2}},\;\;|q|\leq\eta e^{n_{1}+n_{2}}.

Since
\eta<1
,
q=0
gives no solutions
\underline{\alpha}
for (
5.3
) and moreover, if (
5.3
) has a solution
\underline{\alpha}
for a given
q
, there are at most
O(q^{2})
possibilities
(p_{1},p_{2})
.
Therefore, the number of
(p_{1},p_{2},q)
such that
(
5.3
) has a solution
\underline{\alpha}
is
O(q^{2}\eta e^{n_{1}+n_{2}})
.
On the other hand, for fixed
(p_{1},p_{2},q)
the measure of the set of
solutions
\underline{\alpha}
for (
5.3
) is
O(q^{-2}\eta^{2}e^{-n_{1}-n_{2}})
.
Therefore, we conclude that

\Vol(B(n,\eta))\ll\eta^{3}.

Similarly, we also have that

\underline{\alpha}\in B^{*}(n,\eta)
iff there exist linearly independent vectors
(p_{1},p_{2},q),(p^{\prime}_{1},p^{\prime}_{2},q^{\prime})\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}\backslash\{0\}
such that
for

\displaystyle\underline{v}(\underline{\alpha}):=

\displaystyle(q\alpha_{1}-p_{1},q\alpha_{2}-p_{2},q)\wedge(q^{\prime}\alpha_{1}-p^{\prime}_{1},q^{\prime}\alpha_{2}-p^{\prime}_{2},q^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big((p_{2}q^{\prime}-qp_{2}^{\prime})\alpha_{1}+(p_{1}q^{\prime}-qp_{1}^{\prime})\alpha_{2}+(p_{1}p_{2}^{\prime}-p_{2}p_{1}^{\prime})\big)\underline{e}_{1}\wedge\underline{e}_{2}

\displaystyle+(qp_{2}^{\prime}-p_{2}q^{\prime})\underline{e}_{2}\wedge\underline{e}_{3}+(qp_{1}^{\prime}-p_{1}q^{\prime})\underline{e}_{1}\wedge\underline{e}_{3},

one has

|\underline{v}(\underline{\alpha})_{12}|\leq\eta e^{-n_{1}-n_{2}},\;\;|\underline{v}(\underline{\alpha})_{23}|\leq\eta e^{n_{1}},\;\;|\underline{v}(\underline{\alpha})_{13}|\leq\eta e^{n_{2}}.

This implies that there exists
(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}\backslash\{0\}
such that

(5.4)

|u_{1}\alpha_{1}+u_{2}\alpha_{2}+u_{3}|\leq\eta e^{-n_{1}-n_{2}},\;\;|u_{1}|\leq\eta e^{n_{1}},\;\;|u_{2}|\leq\eta e^{n_{2}}.

Since
\eta<1
, when
u_{1}=u_{2}=0
, no solutions
\underline{\alpha}
of (
5.4
)
exist, and for given
(u_{1},u_{2})
, there are
O\big(\max(|u_{1}|,|u_{2}|)\big)
integers
u_{3}
such that solutions
\underline{\alpha}
of (
5.4
) exist.
For fixed
(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})
with
(u_{1},u_{2})\neq 0
, the measure of the set of solutions
\underline{\alpha}

satisfying (
5.4
) is
O\big(\max(|u_{1}|,|u_{2}|)^{-1}\eta e^{-n_{1}-n_{2}}\big)
.
We consider the cases:

•

When
\eta e^{n_{1}}<1
and
\eta e^{n_{2}}<1
, one gets
u_{1}=u_{2}=0
,
so that (
5.4
) has no solutions.

•

When
\eta e^{n_{1}}\geq 1
and
\eta e^{n_{2}}<1
,
u_{2}=0
and there are

O(\eta e^{n_{1}})
possibilities for
u_{1}
, so that
the measure of the set of solutions
\underline{\alpha}

is
O\big(\eta^{2}e^{-n_{2}}\big)
.

•

When
\eta e^{n_{1}}<1
and
\eta e^{n_{2}}\geq 1
,
similarly the measure of the set of solutions
\underline{\alpha}

is
O\big(\eta^{2}e^{-n_{1}}\big)
.

•

When
\eta e^{n_{1}}\geq 1
and
\eta e^{n_{2}}\geq 1
,
there are

O(\eta^{2}e^{n_{1}+n_{2}})
possibilities for
(u_{1},u_{2})
, so that
the measure of the set of solutions
\underline{\alpha}

is
O\big(\eta^{3}\big)
.

Ultimately, we conclude that

\Vol(B^{*}(n,\eta))\ll\eta^{3}+\eta^{2}e^{-\lfloor n\rfloor},

which completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Proposition 5.9
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{3}\to[0,1]
be a measurable function whose support is contained in the set
[-c,c]^{2}\times[\xi,\beta]
for some
c>0
and
0<\xi<\beta\ll 1
.
Then, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}^{2}_{o}
,

\int_{Y}\widehat{f}^{2}\circ a({n})\,d\nu\ll_{c}\beta\cdot\max\big(1,\ln(\beta/\xi)\big)^{2}\cdot\max\left(1,|n|e^{-\lfloor{n}\rfloor}\right).

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\int_{Y}\widehat{f}^{2}\circ a({n})\,d\nu=\sum_{q_{1},q_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{\underline{p}_{1},\underline{p}_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\int_{[0,1)^{2}}f\circ a({n})(q_{1}\underline{\alpha}+\underline{p}_{1},q_{1})f\circ a({n})(q_{2}\underline{\alpha}+\underline{p}_{2},q_{2})\,d\underline{\alpha}.

Let
\Delta_{n}:=[-e^{-n_{1}}c,e^{-n_{1}}c]\times[-e^{-n_{2}}c,e^{-n_{2}}c]
.
Then when
q_{1},q_{2}\in[\xi e^{n_{1}+n_{2}},\beta e^{n_{1}+n_{2}}]
,

\displaystyle\int_{[0,1)^{2}}f\circ a({n})(q_{1}\underline{\alpha}+\underline{p}_{1},q_{1})f\circ a({n})(q_{2}\underline{\alpha}+\underline{p}_{2},q_{2})\,d\underline{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{[0,1)^{2}}\chi_{\Delta_{n}}(\underline{\alpha}q_{1}+\underline{p}_{1})\chi_{\Delta_{n}}(\underline{\alpha}q_{2}+\underline{p}_{2})\,d\underline{\alpha}=\Vol\left(q_{1}^{-1}(\Delta_{n}-\underline{p}_{1})\cap q_{2}^{-1}(\Delta_{n}-\underline{p}_{2})\cap[0,1)^{2}\right).

For the complementary range of
q_{1},q_{2}
, this integral is zero.
In particular, we may assume that

\beta e^{n_{1}+n_{2}}\geq 1.

We note that there are at most
\lfloor\beta e^{n_{1}+n_{2}}\rfloor^{2}

possibilities for
(q_{1},q_{2})
that give a non-trivial contribution.

We write
q_{1}=dq_{1}^{\prime}
and
q_{2}=dq_{2}^{\prime}
with
d:=\gcd(q_{1},q_{2})
.
Then

q_{2}\underline{p}_{1}-q_{1}\underline{p}_{2}=d\underline{k}
for
\underline{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
.
We group the summands according to the parameter
\underline{k}
.
We note that all the solutions
(\underline{x},\underline{y})
of the equation

q_{2}\underline{x}-q_{1}\underline{y}=d\underline{k}
are of the form

\underline{x}=x_{\underline{k}}+q_{1}^{\prime}\underline{l},\;\;\underline{y}=y_{\underline{k}}+q_{2}^{\prime}\underline{l}\quad\hbox{with $\underline{l}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2},$}

where
(x_{\underline{k}},y_{\underline{k}})
is a fixed solution of this equation.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{\underline{p}_{1},\underline{p}_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\Vol\left(q_{1}^{-1}(\Delta_{n}-\underline{p}_{1})\cap q_{2}^{-1}(\Delta_{n}-\underline{p}_{2})\cap[0,1)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\underline{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\sum_{\underline{l}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\Vol\left(\left(q_{1}^{-1}(\Delta_{n}-x_{\underline{k}})-{\underline{l}}/d\right)\cap\left(q_{2}^{-1}(\Delta_{n}-y_{\underline{k}})-{\underline{l}}/{d}\right)\cap[0,1)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\underline{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\sum_{\underline{l}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\Vol\left(q_{1}^{-1}(\Delta_{n}-x_{\underline{k}})\cap q_{2}^{-1}(\Delta_{n}-y_{\underline{k}})\cap([0,1)^{2}+\underline{l}/d)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\underline{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}d^{2}\Vol\left(q_{1}^{-1}(\Delta_{n}-x_{\underline{k}})\cap q_{2}^{-1}(\Delta_{n}-y_{\underline{k}})\right)=\sum_{\underline{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\Vol\left({q^{\prime}_{1}}^{-1}\Delta_{n}\cap{q^{\prime}_{2}}^{-1}(\Delta_{n}+\underline{k}/{q^{\prime}_{1}})\right).

We note that in the above sum only vectors

(5.5)

\underline{k}\in q_{2}^{\prime}\Delta_{n}-q_{1}^{\prime}\Delta_{n}\subset 2\max(q_{1}^{\prime},q_{2}^{\prime})\Delta_{n}

give non-trivial
contributions, and

\Vol\left({q^{\prime}_{1}}^{-1}\Delta_{n}\cap{q^{\prime}_{2}}^{-1}(\Delta_{n}+\underline{k}/{q^{\prime}_{1}})\right)\ll_{c}\max(q^{\prime}_{1},q^{\prime}_{2})^{-2}e^{-n_{1}-n_{2}}.

Finally, we have to sum the above bounds over
q_{1},q_{2}\in[\xi e^{n_{1}+n_{2}},\beta e^{n_{1}+n_{2}}]
to estimate the Siegel transform. Let as consider several cases:

•

When
\max(q^{\prime}_{1},q^{\prime}_{2})\geq\max(e^{n_{1}},e^{n_{2}})
, there are

O_{c}(\max(q^{\prime}_{1},q^{\prime}_{2})^{2}e^{-n_{1}-n_{2}})
possibilities for
\underline{k}
satisfying (
5.5
), so that

\sum_{\underline{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\Vol\left({q^{\prime}_{1}}^{-1}\Delta_{n}\cap{q^{\prime}_{2}}^{-1}(\Delta_{n}+\underline{k}/{q^{\prime}_{1}})\right)\ll_{c}(e^{-n_{1}-n_{2}})^{2},

and the sum over
q_{1},q_{2}\in[\xi e^{n_{1}+n_{2}},\beta e^{n_{1}+n_{2}}]
gives
O_{c}(\beta^{2})
.

•

When
\max(q^{\prime}_{1},q^{\prime}_{2})\leq\min(e^{n_{1}},e^{n_{2}})
,
there are

O_{c}(1)
possibilities for
\underline{k}
satisfying (
5.5
), so that

\sum_{\underline{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\Vol\left({q^{\prime}_{1}}^{-1}\Delta_{n}\cap{q^{\prime}_{2}}^{-1}(\Delta_{n}+\underline{k}/{q^{\prime}_{1}})\right)\ll_{c}\max(q^{\prime}_{1},q^{\prime}_{2})^{-2}e^{-n_{1}-n_{2}},

and we obtain the sum

\displaystyle\sum_{d\leq\beta e^{n_{1}+n_{2}}}\left(\sum_{\xi e^{n_{1}+n_{2}}/d\leq q_{1}^{\prime},q_{2}^{\prime}\leq\beta e^{n_{1}+n_{2}}/d}\max(q^{\prime}_{1},q^{\prime}_{2})^{-2}e^{-n_{1}-n_{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,e^{-n_{1}-n_{2}}\sum_{d\leq\beta e^{n_{1}+n_{2}}}\left(\sum_{\xi e^{n_{1}+n_{2}}/d\leq q_{1}^{\prime},q_{2}^{\prime}\leq\beta e^{n_{1}+n_{2}}/d}{q^{\prime}_{1}}^{-1}{q^{\prime}_{2}}^{-1}\right)\ll\beta\cdot\max\left(1,\ln({\beta}/{\xi})\right)^{2}.

•

When
e^{n_{2}}\leq\max(q^{\prime}_{1},q^{\prime}_{2})\leq e^{n_{1}}
,
there are

O_{c}\big(\max(q_{1}^{\prime},q_{2}^{\prime})e^{-n_{2}}\big)
possibilities for
\underline{k}
satisfying (
5.5
), so that

\sum_{\underline{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\Vol\left({q^{\prime}_{1}}^{-1}\Delta_{n}\cap{q^{\prime}_{2}}^{-1}(\Delta_{n}+\underline{k}/{q^{\prime}_{1}})\right)\ll_{c}\max(q^{\prime}_{1},q^{\prime}_{2})^{-1}e^{-n_{1}-2n_{2}}.

In this case the greatest common divisor
d=q_{i}/q_{i}^{\prime}
satisfies
d\leq\beta e^{n_{1}}
.
Thus, we obtain the sum

\displaystyle\sum_{d\leq\beta e^{n_{1}}}\left(\sum_{\xi e^{n_{1}+n_{2}}/d\leq q_{1}^{\prime},q_{2}^{\prime}\leq\beta e^{n_{1}+n_{2}}/d}\max(q^{\prime}_{1},q^{\prime}_{2})^{-1}e^{-n_{1}-2n_{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,e^{-n_{1}-2n_{2}}\sum_{d\leq\beta e^{n_{1}}}\frac{\beta e^{n_{1}+n_{2}}}{d}\left(\sum_{\xi e^{n_{1}+n_{2}}/d\leq q_{1}^{\prime},q_{2}^{\prime}\leq\beta e^{n_{1}+n_{2}}/d}{q^{\prime}_{1}}^{-1}{q^{\prime}_{2}}^{-1}\right)

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle\,\beta\cdot\max\left(1,\ln({\beta}/{\xi})\right)^{2}\cdot n_{1}e^{-n_{2}}.

•

The remaining case is handled similarly, and we obtain the bound

\ll_{c}\beta\cdot\max\left(1,\ln({\beta}/{\xi})\right)^{2}\cdot n_{2}e^{-n_{1}}.

Combining these cases, we get the proposition.
∎

Proposition 5.10
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{3}\to[0,1]

[-c,c]^{2}\times[-\beta,\beta]

c\in(0,1)

p\in[1,2)

Y_{2}

\xi=\xi(c)

\chi

\int_{G_{1}}f\,dm_{G_{1}}=\int_{[0,2\pi)\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}f({\sf k}_{\theta}{\sf a}_{s}{\sf n}_{\underline{x}})\,e^{2s}d\theta dsd\underline{x},\quad f\in C_{c}(G_{1}).

\sf b(\beta/\xi)

\sf b^{\prime}(\beta/\xi)

\int_{Y_{1}}\widehat{\chi}^{p}\circ{\sf b}^{\prime}\circ a(n)\,d\nu_{1}\ll_{c}\int_{Y_{1}}\hbox{ht}^{p}\circ{\sf b}^{\prime}(\beta/\xi)\circ a(n)\cdot\omega\circ{\sf b}^{\prime}(\beta/\xi)\circ a(n)\,d\nu_{1},

\theta

{\sf k}_{\theta}\cdot\big([-c,c]^{2}\times[-\xi,\xi])\subset\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:\,\|x\|_{\infty}\leq r\},

\omega\circ{\sf k}_{\theta}\leq\tilde{\omega},

s^{*}_{1}\leq s_{1}\leq r
in the above integral.
We similarly estimate the corresponding integral with the help of Lemma
5.12
below, but
we have to consider separately the sums with

2^{-k}<\beta^{-1/2}e^{m/2+s}\quad\textrm{and}\quad 2^{-k}\geq\beta^{-1/2}e^{m/2+s}.

In the first case, Lemma
5.12
applies, and as above for
p<2
we get the bound

\displaystyle\leq\int_{-\infty}^{s_{0}}\left(\sum_{k=\lfloor\log_{2}r\rfloor-1}^{\infty}2^{kp}\Vol\big(B^{*}(n,s,\beta,2^{-k})\big)\right)e^{2s}ds

\displaystyle\ll_{p}\beta^{2}+\beta^{5/2}e^{-n_{2}/2}+\beta^{3/2}e^{-3n_{2}/2}

\displaystyle\ll\beta^{3/2}.

In the second case, we use just the trivial bound on the volume.
When
\beta^{1/2}e^{-m/2-s}\leq r/2
, the sum over
k
gives zero contribution.
When
\beta^{1/2}e^{-m/2-s}>r/2
,
we get for
p<2
,

\displaystyle\leq\int_{-\infty}^{s_{0}}\left(\sum_{r/2\leq 2^{k}\leq\beta^{1/2}e^{-m/2-s}}2^{kp}\right)e^{2s}ds\ll_{p}\beta^{p/2}e^{-pm/2}\leq\beta^{p/2}.

This bounds applies in both situations and proves Proposition
5.10
.
∎

In the following two lemmas, we establish the volume bounds for
B(n,s,\beta,\eta)
and
B^{*}(n,s,\beta,\eta)
used in the proof of the previous proposition.

Lemma 5.11
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
\beta,\eta>0
, and
n=(-m/2,m)
with
m\in\mathbb{N}_{o}
,
m>\ln\eta
. Then for every
\varrho\in[0,1/2]
,

\Vol\big(B(n,s,\beta,\eta)\big)\ll\eta^{3}\beta+\eta^{3-2\varrho}\beta^{1-\varrho}e^{-\rho m-2(1-\varrho)s}

uniformly on
\varrho
.

Proof.

Since for
\underline{v}=(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
,

{\sf b}^{\prime}(\beta)a({n}){\sf a}_{s}{\sf n}_{\underline{x}}v=\big(\beta^{-1/2}e^{-m/2}e^{s}(v_{1}+x_{1}v_{3}),e^{m}(x_{2}v_{1}+v_{2}+x_{3}v_{3}),\beta^{-1/2}e^{-m/2}e^{-s}v_{3}\big),

the set
B(n,s,\beta,\eta)
consists of
\underline{x}\in[-1/2,1/2]^{3}
for which
there exists
\underline{v}\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}\backslash\{0\}
satisfying

(5.6)

\displaystyle|v_{1}+x_{1}v_{3}|\leq\eta\beta^{1/2}e^{m/2-s},\;\;|v_{2}+x_{2}v_{1}+x_{3}v_{3}|\leq\eta e^{-m},\;\;|v_{3}|\leq\eta\beta^{1/2}e^{m/2+s}.

If (
5.6
) has a solution
\underline{x}\in[-1/2,1/2]^{3}
, then

(5.7)

|v_{1}|\leq|v_{3}|/2+\eta\beta^{1/2}e^{m/2-s},\;\;|v_{2}|\leq|v_{1}|/2+|v_{3}|/2+1,\;\;|v_{3}|\leq\eta\beta^{1/2}e^{m/2+s}.

Let us first consider the case when
|v_{3}|\geq\eta\beta^{1/2}e^{m/2-s}
.
Then also
|v_{1}|\leq 3|v_{3}|/2
.
The volume of the set of
\underline{x}\in[-1/2,1/2]^{3}
satisfying (
5.6
) is estimated as

\ll|v_{3}|^{-1}\eta\beta^{1/2}e^{m/2-s}\cdot\max(|v_{1}|,|v_{3}|)^{-1}\eta e^{-m}\leq|v_{3}|^{-2}\eta^{2}\beta^{1/2}e^{-m/2-s}.

Summing these volume bounds over
\underline{v}
satisfying (
5.7
) and
|v_{1}|\leq 3|v_{3}|/2
,
we obtain

\ll\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|v_{3}|\leq\eta\beta^{1/2}e^{m/2+s}\\[0.28453pt]
|v_{1}|\leq 3|v_{3}|/2,\,|v_{2}|\leq 2|v_{3}|+1\end{subarray}}|v_{3}|^{-2}\eta^{2}\beta^{1/2}e^{-m/2-s}\ll\eta^{3}\beta.

Next, we consider the case when
1\leq|v_{3}|<\eta\beta^{1/2}e^{m/2-s}
.
The volume of the set of
\underline{x}\in[-1/2,1/2]^{3}
satisfying (
5.6
) is estimated as

\ll\max(|v_{1}|,|v_{3}|)^{-1}\eta e^{-m}.

Summing these volume bounds over
\underline{v}
satisfying (
5.7
),
we obtain

\ll\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|v_{1}|<2\eta\beta^{1/2}e^{m/2-s},\,|v_{3}|\leq\eta\beta^{1/2}e^{m/2+s}\\[0.28453pt]
|v_{2}|\leq\max(|v_{1}|,|v_{3}|)+1\end{subarray}}\max(|v_{1}|,|v_{3}|)^{-1}\eta e^{-m}\ll\eta^{3}\beta.

Finally, we are left to consider the cases when
v_{3}=0
. Let us assume first that
v_{1}\neq 0
.
The volume of the set of
\underline{x}\in[-1/2,1/2]^{3}
satisfying (
5.6
) is estimated as

O\left(|v_{1}|^{-1}\eta e^{-m}\right)
.
Let
\varrho\in[0,1/2]
. Summing these bounds, we obtain

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq|v_{1}|\leq\eta\beta^{1/2}e^{m/2-s}\\[0.28453pt]
|v_{2}|\leq|v_{1}|/2+1\end{subarray}}|v_{1}|^{-1}\eta e^{-m}

\displaystyle\ll\eta e^{-m}\sum_{1\leq|v_{1}|\leq\eta\beta^{1/2}e^{m/2-s}}|v_{1}|^{1-2\varrho}

\displaystyle\ll\eta^{3-2\varrho}\beta^{1-\varrho}e^{-\rho m-2(1-\varrho)s}

uniformly on
\varrho
.

Since
m>\ln\eta
, when
v_{1}=v_{3}=0
, we also have
v_{2}=0
, so that all the cases have been covered.
∎

Lemma 5.12
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
\beta,\eta>0
, and
n=(-m/2,m)
with
m\in\mathbb{N}_{o}

satisfying

\eta<\beta^{-1/2}e^{m/2+s}.

Then

\Vol\big(B^{*}(n,s,\beta,\eta)\big)\ll\eta^{3}\beta^{2}+\eta^{4}\beta^{5/2}e^{-m/2+s}+\eta^{3}\beta^{3/2}e^{-3m/2+|s|}.

Proof.

The set
B^{*}(n,s,\beta,\eta)
consists of
\underline{x}\in[-1/2,1/2]^{3}

such that there exist linearly independent
v,v^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}

satisfying

(5.8)

\left\|{\sf b}^{\prime}(\beta)a({n}){\sf a}_{s}{\sf n}_{\underline{x}}(v\wedge v^{\prime})\right\|_{\infty}\leq\eta.

Since

\displaystyle{\sf n}_{\underline{x}}(v\wedge v^{\prime})=

\displaystyle\Big((v_{1}v_{2}^{\prime}-v_{2}v_{1}^{\prime})+(x_{3}-x_{1}x_{2})(v_{1}v_{3}^{\prime}-v_{3}v_{1}^{\prime})+x_{1}(v_{3}v_{2}^{\prime}-v_{2}v_{3}^{\prime})\Big)\underline{e}_{1}\wedge\underline{e}_{2}

\displaystyle+\Big((v_{2}v_{3}^{\prime}-v_{3}v_{2}^{\prime})+x_{2}(v_{1}v_{3}^{\prime}-v_{3}v_{1}^{\prime})\Big)\underline{e}_{2}\wedge\underline{e}_{3}+\Big(v_{1}v_{3}^{\prime}-v_{3}v_{1}^{\prime}\Big)\underline{e}_{1}\wedge\underline{e}_{3}

and

\displaystyle{\sf b}^{\prime}a({n}){\sf a}_{s}(\underline{e}_{1}\wedge\underline{e}_{2})

\displaystyle=\beta^{-1/2}e^{m/2+s}\,\underline{e}_{1}\wedge\underline{e}_{2},

\displaystyle{\sf b}^{\prime}a({n}){\sf a}_{s}(\underline{e}_{2}\wedge\underline{e}_{3})

\displaystyle=\beta^{-1/2}e^{m/2-s}\,\underline{e}_{2}\wedge\underline{e}_{3},

\displaystyle{\sf b}^{\prime}a({n}){\sf a}_{s}(\underline{e}_{1}\wedge\underline{e}_{3})

\displaystyle=\beta^{-1}e^{-m}\,\underline{e}_{1}\wedge\underline{e}_{3}.

The condition (
5.8
) implies existence of
\underline{u}\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}\backslash\{0\}
such that

(5.9)

|u_{1}+(x_{3}-x_{1}x_{2})u_{2}+x_{1}u_{3}|\leq\eta\beta^{1/2}e^{-m/2-s},\;|u_{3}+x_{2}u_{2}|\leq\eta\beta^{1/2}e^{-m/2+s},\;|u_{2}|\leq\eta\beta e^{m}.

Let us denote by
E_{\underline{u}}
the set of
\underline{x}\in[-1/2,1/2]^{3}
satisfying
(
5.9
) and by

F_{\underline{u}}

the set of
\underline{y}\in[-1,1]^{3}
satisfying

(5.10)

|u_{1}+y_{3}u_{2}+y_{1}u_{3}|\leq\eta\beta^{1/2}e^{-m/2-s},\;|u_{3}+y_{2}u_{2}|\leq\eta\beta^{1/2}e^{-m/2+s},\;|u_{2}|\leq\eta\beta e^{m}.

It follows from a change of variables that

\Vol(E_{\underline{u}})\leq\Vol(F_{\underline{u}}).

Now we estimate
\sum_{\underline{u}\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}\backslash\{0\}}\Vol(F_{\underline{u}})
. We note that if
F_{\underline{u}}\neq 0
, then

u_{1},u_{2},u_{3}
satisfy the conditions

(5.11)

|u_{1}|\leq|u_{2}|+|u_{3}|+\eta\beta^{1/2}e^{-m/2-s},\;\;|u_{3}|\leq|u_{2}|+\eta\beta^{1/2}e^{-m/2+s},\;\;|u_{2}|\leq\eta\beta e^{m},

which we assume from now on. Further, we note that when
u_{2}=u_{3}=0
, it follows from the assumption on
\eta

that also
u_{1}=0
, which is not possible,
so that
(u_{2},u_{3})\neq 0
.
We analyze several cases separately.

When
u_{2}=0
and
u_{3}\neq 0
, we have

|u_{3}|\leq\eta\beta^{1/2}e^{-m/2+s},\quad|u_{1}|\leq\eta\beta^{1/2}e^{-m/2}(e^{s}+e^{-s}),

and

\displaystyle\Vol(F_{\underline{u}})

\displaystyle\ll|u_{3}|^{-1}\eta\beta^{1/2}e^{-m/2-s}.

Summing these volume estimates, we obtain

(5.12)

\ll\eta\beta^{1/2}e^{-m/2+s}\cdot\eta\beta^{1/2}e^{-m/2}(e^{s}+e^{-s})\cdot\eta\beta^{1/2}e^{-m/2-s}\ll\eta^{3}\beta^{3/2}e^{-3m/2+|s|}.

Now it remains to consider the cases when
u_{2}\neq 0
.
In this situation, we obtain

\displaystyle\Vol(F_{\underline{u}})

\displaystyle\ll\max(|u_{2}|,|u_{3}|)^{-1}\eta\beta^{1/2}e^{-m/2-s}\cdot|u_{2}|^{-1}\eta\beta^{1/2}e^{-m/2+s}

\displaystyle=\max(|u_{2}|,|u_{3}|)^{-1}|u_{2}|^{-1}\eta^{2}\beta e^{-m}.

Our assumption also implies that
|u_{1}|\leq 3\max(|u_{2}|,|u_{3}|)
, so that
summing the above volume bounds, we obtain

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{1\leq|u_{2}|\leq\eta\beta e^{m}}(|u_{2}|+\eta\beta^{1/2}e^{-m/2+s})|u_{2}|^{-1}\eta^{2}\beta e^{-m}

(5.13)

\displaystyle\ll\eta^{3}\beta^{2}+\eta^{3}\beta^{3/2}e^{-3m/2+s}\sum_{1\leq|u_{2}|\leq\eta\beta e^{m}}|u_{2}|^{-1}\ll\eta^{3}\beta^{2}+\eta^{4}\beta^{5/2}e^{-m/2+s}.

Combining (
5.12
) and (
5.13
), we obtain the lemma.
∎

Proposition 5.13
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{3}\to[0,1]
be a function supported on
[-c,c]^{2}\times[-\beta,\beta]
for some
c>0
and
0<\beta\ll 1
.
Then for
p<3
,

\int_{X}\widehat{f}^{p}\,d\mu\ll_{c,p}\beta^{p/3}.

Proof.

As in the previous proof,

\int_{X}\widehat{f}^{p}\,d\mu\leq\int_{X}\widehat{\chi}^{p}\circ{\sf b}(\beta)\,d\mu\ll_{c}\int_{X}\hbox{ht}^{p}\circ{\sf b}(\beta)\,d\mu.

where

\chi
is the characteristic function of
[-c,c]^{2}\times[-1,1]
and
{\sf b}(\beta):=\hbox{diag}(1,1,\beta^{-1})
.
By invariance of
\mu
,

\int_{X}\hbox{ht}^{p}\circ{\sf b}(\beta)\,d\mu=\int_{X}\hbox{ht}^{p}\circ{\sf b}^{\prime\prime}(\beta)\,d\mu,

where
{\sf b}^{\prime\prime}(\beta):=\hbox{diag}(\beta^{-1/3},\beta^{-1/3},\beta^{-1/3})
.
We have

s_{1}\circ{\sf b}^{\prime\prime}(\beta)=\beta^{-1/3}s_{1},\quad s^{*}_{1}\circ{\sf b}^{\prime\prime}(\beta)=\beta^{-2/3}s^{*}_{1},\quad d\circ{\sf b}^{\prime\prime}(\beta)=\beta^{-1}d,

so that

\hbox{ht}\circ{\sf b}^{\prime\prime}(\beta)\ll\beta^{1/3}\,\hbox{ht}
.
Since
ht
is
L^{p}
-integrable on
X
, this implies the proposition.
∎

6.
Estimating the variance: first step

In this section, we proceed with analyzing the variance of averages
of functions over the sets
\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}
, defined in (
3.5
).
This general result will be subsequently used in Sections
7
–
8
. Recall that
b\in(0,1)
denotes a parameter in the definition
of the set
\Omega
.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
\phi_{n}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(X)
,
n\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}
, be such that uniformly on
n
,

(i)

\int_{Y}\phi_{n}\circ a(n)\,d\nu=0
,

(ii)

\int_{Y}\phi_{n}^{2}\circ a(n)\,d\nu=O_{\delta}\left((\ln T)^{\delta}e^{\delta|n|}\right)
for any
\delta>0
,

(iii)

for any
p\in[1,2)
and
i=1,2
,
\int_{Y_{i}}|\phi_{n}|^{p}\circ a(n)\,d\nu_{i}=O_{p}\left(b^{p/2}\right)
,

(iv)

\|\phi_{n}\|_{C^{\ell}}=O\big((\ln T)^{\sigma}\big)
for some fixed
\sigma>0
.

Then for any
B\geq B(\sigma)>0
,
B_{1}\geq B_{1}(B,\sigma)>0
and

B_{0}\geq B_{0}(B,B_{1},\sigma)>0

\displaystyle\int_{Y}\left(\sum_{n\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}}\phi_{n}\circ a(n)\right)^{2}\,d\nu=\sum_{(n,k)\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}^{\triangledown}}

\displaystyle\int_{X}\phi_{n}\circ a(n)\cdot\phi_{k}\circ a(k)\,d\mu

\displaystyle+O_{c,\delta}\Big((1+(\ln T+\ln(b/a))b^{1-\delta})(\ln T)^{\delta}\Big)

for all
\delta>0
,
where
\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}^{\triangledown}=\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}^{\triangledown}(B,B_{0},B_{1})
denotes the subset of
(n,k)\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}\times\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}

satisfying

(6.1)

|n-k|<B\ln\ln T,\quad|n|\geq B_{0}\ln\ln T,\quad\lfloor n\rfloor\geq{B_{1}}\ln\ln T.

Proof.

Throughout the proof, we use the bound (
3.7
) which, in particular, implies that
the number of terms in the sums below is
O_{c}\left((\ln T)^{4}\right)
.

For a parameter
B>0
to be specified later, we write

(6.2)

\left(\sum_{n\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}}\phi_{n}\circ a(n)\right)^{2}=\Phi^{+}+\Phi^{-},

where
\Phi^{+}
denotes the sum over the products
\phi_{n}\circ a(n)\cdot\phi_{k}\circ a(k)

with
n,k\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}
satisfying

(6.3)

|n-k|<B\ln\ln T,

and
\Phi^{-}
denotes
the remaining sum with

(6.4)

|n-k|\geq B\ln\ln T.

Since
\int_{Y}\phi_{n}\circ a(n)\,d\nu=0
, it follows from Theorem
4.1
and (iv) that
when (
6.4
) holds

\displaystyle\left|\int_{Y}\phi_{n}\circ a({n})\cdot\phi_{k}\circ a(k)\,d\nu\right|

\displaystyle\ll\|\phi_{n}\|_{C^{\ell}}\|\phi_{k}\|_{C^{\ell}}(\ln T)^{-\eta_{2}B}\ll(\ln T)^{2\sigma-\eta_{2}B},

so that

\left|\int_{Y}\Phi^{-}\,d\nu\right|\ll|\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}|^{2}(\ln T)^{2\sigma-\eta_{2}B}\ll_{c}(\ln T)^{4+2\sigma-\eta_{2}B}.

This contribution is negligible when the parameter
B
is chosen sufficiently large depending on
\sigma
.

It remains to deal with
\Phi^{+}
, which we write as

(6.5)

\Phi^{+}=\Phi^{(1)}+\Phi^{(2)}+\Phi^{(3)},

where
\Phi^{(1)}

is the subsum of
\Phi^{+}
given by the condition

(6.6)

|n|<B_{0}\ln\ln T,

\Phi^{(2)}

is the subsum of
\Phi^{+}
given by the condition

(6.7)

|n|\geq B_{0}\ln\ln T\quad\textrm{and}\quad\lfloor n\rfloor<{B_{1}}\ln\ln T,

and

\Phi^{(3)}

is the subsum given by the condition

(6.8)

|n|\geq B_{0}\ln\ln T\quad\textrm{and}\quad\lfloor n\rfloor\geq{B_{1}}\ln\ln T,

where
B_{0}
and
B_{1}
are constants yet to be chosen.

To estimate
\Phi^{(1)}
, we observe that

\left|\int_{Y}\phi_{n}\circ a(n)\cdot\phi_{k}\circ a(k)\,d\nu\right|\leq\big\|\phi_{n}\circ a(n)\big\|_{L^{2}(\nu)}\cdot\big\|\phi_{k}\circ a(k)\big\|_{{L}^{2}(\nu)}.

\Phi^{(2)}
. Without loss of generality we only consider the part of the sum with

,
so that

.
The other part of the sum can be analyzed similarly.

4.2
with

,

(6.9)

\displaystyle\int_{Y}\phi_{n}\circ a(n)\cdot\phi_{k}\circ a(k)\,d\nu=

It follows from conditions (

\displaystyle\big\|\phi_{n}\circ a(0,{n}_{2})\cdot\phi_{k}\circ a(k-(n_{1},0))\big\|_{C^{\ell}}

\displaystyle\ll\big\|\phi_{n}\circ a(0,{n}_{2})\big\|_{C^{\ell}}\cdot\big\|\phi_{k}\circ a(k-(n_{1},0))\big\|_{C^{\ell}}

\displaystyle\ll(\ln T)^{\sigma^{\prime}}

On the other hand, also

Therefore, taking

\sigma^{\prime}

Finally, we proceed with estimating

(n,k)
in the sum satisfy (

) and (

In this case, by Theorem
4.2
with

(6.11)

\displaystyle\int_{Y}\phi_{n}\circ a({n})\cdot\phi_{k}\circ a(k)\,d\nu=

Approximation by smooth compactly-supported functions

In view of the tesselation (

and their Siegel transforms

\widehat{\chi}_{\Delta_{\Omega,{n}}}
. We also introduce their smoothened versions (cf.
[
2
, Section 8]
).
Let
\rho_{\varepsilon}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(G)
with
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
be a family of bump-functions satisfying

(7.1)

\rho_{\varepsilon}\geq 0,\quad\hbox{supp}(\rho_{\varepsilon})\subset V_{\varepsilon},\quad\int_{G}\rho_{\varepsilon}\,dm=1,\quad\|\rho_{\varepsilon}\|_{C^{\ell}}\ll_{\ell}\varepsilon^{-\sigma_{\ell}}

for some
\sigma_{\ell}>0
.
Taking a decreasing function
\varepsilon_{T}\to 0
as

T\rightarrow\infty
(to be specified later), we define

f_{\Omega,{n}}(v):=\rho_{\varepsilon_{T}}*\chi_{\Delta_{\Omega,{n}}}(v)=\int_{G}\rho_{\varepsilon_{T}}(g)\chi_{\Delta_{\Omega,{n}}}(g^{-1}v)\,dm(g),\quad v\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.

We note that
0\leq f_{\Omega,{n}}\leq 1
, and it follows from (
3.8
)
that

(7.2)

\supp(f_{\Omega,{n}})\subset\left[-(1+\varepsilon_{T})c,(1+\varepsilon_{T})c\right]^{2}\times\left((1-\varepsilon_{T}){a}{c^{-2}},(1+\varepsilon_{T})e^{2}{b}{c^{-2}}\right].

Also it follows from Lemma
5.4
that

(7.3)

\displaystyle\widehat{f}_{\Omega,{n}}\ll_{c}\hbox{ht}.

However, the Siegel transform
\widehat{f}_{\Omega,{n}}
is not uniformly bounded. We will need more precise control on its
L^{p}
-norms.

Lemma 7.1
.

For every
n\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}
,

\left\|\widehat{f}_{\Omega,n}\right\|_{L^{1}(\mu)}\ll_{c}b\quad\textrm{and}\quad\left\|\widehat{f}_{\Omega,n}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}\ll_{c}b^{1/2}.

Proof.

We have

\left\|\widehat{f}_{\Omega,n}\right\|_{L^{1}(\mu)}=\int_{X}\widehat{\chi}_{\Delta_{\Omega,n}}\,d\mu,

and

\left\|\widehat{f}_{\Omega,n}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}\leq\left\|\rho_{\varepsilon_{T}}\right\|_{L^{1}(m)}\cdot\left\|\widehat{\chi}_{\Delta_{\Omega,n}}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}=\left(\int_{X}\widehat{\chi}_{\Delta_{\Omega,n}}^{2}\,d\mu\right)^{1/2}.

Therefore, the above estimates follow from Theorem
5.1
and Corollary
5.3
respectively, taking (
3.8
) into account.
∎

The following result is a simplified version of
[
1
, Lemma 8.5]
.

Lemma 7.2
.

When
\varepsilon_{T}=O_{c}(a)
, for every
n\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}
,

\left\|\left(\widehat{\chi}_{\Delta_{\Omega,n}}-\widehat{f}_{\Omega,n}\right)\circ a(n)\right\|_{L^{1}(\nu)}\\
\ll_{c}b

and moreover,

\left\|\left(\widehat{\chi}_{\Delta_{\Omega,n}}-\widehat{f}_{\Omega,n}\right)\circ a(n)\right\|_{L^{1}(\nu)}\\
\ll_{c}\max\left(\varepsilon_{T},-\frac{\varepsilon_{T}}{a}\ln\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{T}}{a}\right)\right)+e^{-n_{1}-n_{2}}.

Proof.

It follows from the properties of
\rho_{\varepsilon}
stated in (
7.1
) that

\displaystyle\left\|\left(\widehat{\chi}_{\Delta_{\Omega,n}}-\widehat{f}_{\Omega,n}\right)\circ a(n)\right\|_{L^{1}(\nu)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{Y}\left|\widehat{\chi}_{\Delta_{\Omega,n}}(a(n)x)-\int_{G}\rho_{\varepsilon_{T}}(g)\widehat{\chi}_{\Delta_{\Omega,n}}(g^{-1}a(n)x)\,dm(g)\right|\,d\nu(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{Y}\left|\int_{G}\rho_{\varepsilon_{T}}(g)\left(\widehat{\chi}_{\Delta_{\Omega,n}}(a(n)x)-\widehat{\chi}_{\Delta_{\Omega,n}}(g^{-1}a(n)x)\right)\,dm(g)\right|d\nu(x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{Y}\int_{G}\rho_{\varepsilon_{T}}(g)\left|\widehat{\chi}_{\Delta_{\Omega,n}}(a(n)x)-\widehat{\chi}_{\Delta_{\Omega,n}}(g^{-1}a(n)x)\right|\,dm(g)d\nu(x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{g\in V_{\varepsilon_{T}}}\left\|\left(\widehat{\chi}_{\Delta_{\Omega,n}}-\widehat{\chi}_{g\Delta_{\Omega,n}}\right)\circ a(n)\right\|_{L^{1}(\nu)}\leq\left\|\widehat{\chi}_{\partial\Delta_{\Omega,n}}\circ a(n)\right\|_{L^{1}(\nu)},

where

\partial\Delta_{\Omega,n}:=\left(\cup_{g\in V_{\varepsilon_{T}}}g\Delta_{\Omega,n}\right)\backslash\left(\cap_{g\in V_{\varepsilon_{T}}}g\Delta_{\Omega,n}\right).

It follows from (
3.8
) that

(7.4)

\partial\Delta_{\Omega,{n}}\subset\left[-(1+\varepsilon_{T})c,(1+\varepsilon_{T})c\right]^{2}\times\left[(1-\varepsilon_{T}){a}{c^{-2}},(1+\varepsilon_{T})e^{2}{b}{c^{-2}}\right].

Then, the first assertion follows from the first part of Proposition
5.6
.
We further note that the set
\partial\Delta_{\Omega,{n}}
is contained in the union of the sets
E_{s}
given in (
3.10
).
Since these sets are defined by finitely many linear inequalities,
the second part of Proposition
5.6

applies to the functions
\chi_{E_{s}}
.
Therefore, the second estimate follows from
Proposition
5.6
and (
3.14
).
∎

Similar argument also gives

Lemma 7.3
.

When
\varepsilon_{T}=O_{c}(a)
, for every
n\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}
,

\left\|\widehat{\chi}_{\Delta_{\Omega,n}}-\widehat{f}_{\Omega,n}\right\|_{L^{1}(\mu)}\\
\ll_{c}\max\left(\varepsilon_{T},-\frac{\varepsilon_{T}}{a}\ln\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{T}}{a}\right)\right).

Proof.

Indeed, as in Lemma
7.2
, with
\partial\Delta_{\Omega,{n}}
defined in (
7.4
),

\displaystyle\left\|\widehat{\chi}_{\Delta_{\Omega,n}}-\widehat{f}_{\Omega,n}\right\|_{L^{1}(\mu)}\leq\left\|\widehat{\chi}_{\partial\Delta_{\Omega,n}}\right\|_{L^{1}(\mu)},

and

\left\|\widehat{\chi}_{\partial\Delta_{\Omega,n}}\right\|_{L^{1}(\mu)}\leq\sum_{s}\left\|\widehat{\chi}_{E_{s}}\right\|_{L^{1}(\mu)}\ll_{c}\max\left(\varepsilon_{T},-\frac{\varepsilon_{T}}{a}\ln\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{T}}{a}\right)\right),

where the last estimate follows from Theorem
5.1
and (
3.14
).
∎

Lemma 7.4
.

When
\varepsilon_{T}=O_{c}(a)
, for every
n\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}
,

\left\|\widehat{f}_{\Omega,{n}}-\widehat{\chi}_{\Delta_{\Omega,{n}}}\right\|_{{L}^{2}(\mu)}\ll_{c}\max\left(\varepsilon_{T},-\frac{\varepsilon_{T}}{a}\ln\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{T}}{a}\right)\right)^{1/2}.

Proof.

It follows from the Jensen inequality that

\displaystyle\left\|\widehat{f}_{\Omega,{n}}-\widehat{\chi}_{\Delta_{\Omega,{n}}}\right\|^{2}_{{L}^{2}(\mu)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{X}\left(\int_{G}\rho_{\varepsilon_{T}}(g)\cdot\big(\widehat{\chi}_{\Delta_{\Omega,{n}}}(g^{-1}x)-\widehat{\chi}_{\Delta_{\Omega,n}}(x)\big)\,dm(g)\right)^{2}\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{X}\int_{G}\rho_{\varepsilon_{T}}(g)\cdot\big(\widehat{\chi}_{\Delta_{\Omega,{n}}}(g^{-1}x)-\widehat{\chi}_{\Delta_{\Omega,n}}(x)\big)^{2}\,dm(g)\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{g\in V_{\varepsilon_{T}}}\left\|\widehat{\chi}_{\Delta_{\Omega,n}}-\widehat{\chi}_{g\Delta_{\Omega,n}}\right\|^{2}_{{L}^{2}(\mu)}\leq\left\|\widehat{\chi}_{\partial\Delta_{\Omega,n}}\right\|^{2}_{{L}^{2}(\mu)},

where
\partial\Delta_{\Omega,{n}}
is defined in (
7.4
). Then as in the previous argument,

\left\|\widehat{\chi}_{\partial\Delta_{\Omega,n}}\right\|_{{L}^{2}(\mu)}\leq\sum_{s}\big\|\widehat{\chi}_{E_{s}}\big\|_{{L}^{2}(\mu)}\ll_{c}\max\left(\varepsilon_{T},-\frac{\varepsilon_{T}}{a}\ln\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{T}}{a}\right)\right)^{1/2},

where we used Corollary
5.3
and (
3.14
) in the last estimate.
∎

We use a family of smooth functions
\eta_{L}:X\rightarrow[0,1]
with
L>1
(cf.
[
3
, Lemma 4.11]
) such that

(7.5)

\{\height\leq L/2\}\subset\{\eta_{L}=1\}\subset\supp(\eta_{L})\subset\{\height\leq 2L\},

where
\height
is the height function on
X
defined in (
5.1
), and

(7.6)

\|\eta_{L}\|_{C^{\ell}}\ll_{\ell}1,\quad\textrm{for all $L\geq 1$}.

We use a parameter
L_{T}\to\infty
as
T\to\infty
, to be specified later,
and introduce the following functions that will be used to
construct approximations for the discrepancy:

(7.7)

\displaystyle\psi_{\Omega,{n}}

\displaystyle:=\widehat{\chi}_{\Delta_{\Omega,{n}}}-\int_{X}\widehat{\chi}_{\Delta_{\Omega,n}}\,d\mu=\widehat{\chi}_{\Delta_{\Omega,{n}}}-\Vol(\Delta_{\Omega,{n}}),

(7.8)

\displaystyle\varphi_{\Omega,n}

\displaystyle:=\widehat{f}_{\Omega,n}\cdot\eta_{L_{T}}-\int_{Y}\left(\widehat{f}_{\Omega,n}\cdot\eta_{L_{T}}\right)\circ a(n)\,d\nu.

It follows from (
7.3
) and (
7.5
) that

(7.9)

|\varphi_{\Omega,n}|\ll_{c}L_{T}.

We note that our definition of the function
\varphi_{\Omega,n}
here
differs from the definition used in
[
1
, Sec. 8.2]
by
the constant

\int_{X}\widehat{f}_{\Omega,{n}}\cdot\eta_{L_{T}}\,d\mu-\int_{Y}\left(\widehat{f}_{\Omega,{n}}\cdot\eta_{L_{T}}\right)\circ a({n})\,d\nu.

Since the supports of the functions
f_{\Omega,{n}}

are uniformly bounded (cf. (
7.2
)), it follows from Theorem
5.1

that the first integral is uniformly bounded.
By Proposition
5.6
, the second integral is also
uniformly bounded. Therefore, the estimate of

[
1
, Lemma 8.4]
still applies:

(7.10)

\big\|\widehat{f}_{\Omega,{n}}\cdot\eta_{L_{T}}\big\|_{C^{\ell}},\,\|\varphi_{\Omega,n}\|_{C^{\ell}}\ll_{\ell}\varepsilon_{T}^{-\sigma_{\ell}}\cdot L_{T}

uniformly on
n
and sufficiently large
T
with some
\sigma_{\ell}>0
.

We consider the corresponding sums

(7.11)

\displaystyle\Psi_{\Omega}:=\sum_{n\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}}\psi_{\Omega,{n}}\circ a({n})\quad\textrm{and}\quad\Phi_{\Omega}:=\sum_{n\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}}\varphi_{\Omega,{n}}\circ a({n}).

It follows from (
3.2
) that for
\underline{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,

\displaystyle N_{a,b,T}(\underline{x})=|\Omega\cap\Lambda_{\underline{x}}|=\sum_{n\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}}\widehat{\chi}_{\Delta_{\Omega,{n}}}(a(n)\Lambda_{\underline{x}}),

and

V_{\Omega}:=\Vol(\Omega)=\sum_{n\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}}\int_{X}\widehat{\chi}_{\Delta_{\Omega,{n}}}\,d\mu,

so that

(7.12)

N_{a,b,T}(\underline{x})-\Vol(\Omega)=\Psi_{\Omega}(\Lambda_{\underline{x}}).

We recall from (
3.1
) that

V_{\Omega}\sim 2(\ln T)^{2}(b-a)\quad\textrm{as $T\rightarrow\infty$}.

The following proposition shows that the sums

\Psi_{\Omega}
and
\Phi_{\Omega}
have the same asymptotic behavior on the scale of order
V_{\Omega}^{-1/2}
:

Proposition 7.5
.

Suppose that some
K_{0},K_{1},K_{2},\theta>0
,

(7.13)

\displaystyle a\leq b/2,\quad(\ln T)^{-K_{0}}\leq\varepsilon_{T}\leq a(\ln T)^{-K_{1}},

\displaystyle b^{-1/3}(\ln T)^{\theta}\leq L_{T}\leq(\ln T)^{K_{2}},\;\;b\geq(\ln T)^{-2\min(1,K_{1}-1)+\theta}.

Then

\big\|\Psi_{\Omega}-\Phi_{\Omega}\big\|_{{L}^{1}(\nu)}=o\left(V_{\Omega}^{1/2}\right)\quad\mbox{as }T\to\infty.

Proof.

Let

\widehat{f}_{\Omega,{n}}^{(L_{T})}:=\widehat{f}_{\Omega,{n}}\cdot(1-\eta_{L_{T}})\quad\textrm{and}\quad g_{\Omega,{n}}^{(L_{T})}:=\widehat{f}_{\Omega,{n}}^{(L_{T})}-\int_{Y}\widehat{f}_{\Omega,{n}}^{(L_{T})}\circ a(n)\,d\nu.

We start by observing (cf. (
7.7
) and (
7.8
)) that

\displaystyle(\psi_{\Omega,{n}}-\varphi_{\Omega,{n}})\circ a({n})=

\displaystyle\left(\widehat{\chi}_{\Delta_{\Omega,{n}}}-\widehat{f}_{\Omega,{n}}\right)\circ a(n)+\widehat{f}_{\Omega,{n}}^{(L_{T})}\circ a({n})

\displaystyle-\int_{X}\widehat{\chi}_{\Delta_{\Omega,{n}}}\,d\mu+\int_{Y}\widehat{\chi}_{\Delta_{\Omega,{n}}}\circ a({n})\,d\nu

\displaystyle-\int_{Y}\widehat{\chi}_{\Delta_{\Omega,{n}}}\circ a({n})\,d\nu+\int_{Y}\widehat{f}_{\Omega,{n}}\circ a({n})\,d\nu

\displaystyle-\int_{Y}\widehat{f}_{\Omega,{n}}^{(L_{T})}\circ a({n})\,d\nu,

so that

\displaystyle\left\|\Psi_{\Omega}-\Phi_{\Omega}\right\|_{L^{1}(\nu)}=

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{n\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}}(\psi_{\Omega,{n}}-\varphi_{\Omega,{n}})\circ a({n})\right\|_{L^{1}(\nu)}

(7.14)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,2\sum_{n\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}}\left\|\left(\widehat{\chi}_{\Delta_{\Omega,{n}}}-\widehat{f}_{\Omega,{n}}\right)\circ a(n)\right\|_{L^{1}(\nu)}

(7.15)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{n\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}}\left|\int_{X}\widehat{\chi}_{\Delta_{\Omega,{n}}}\,d\mu-\int_{Y}\widehat{\chi}_{\Delta_{\Omega,{n}}}\circ a({n})\,d\nu\right|

(7.16)

\displaystyle\quad+\left\|\sum_{n\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}}g_{\Omega,{n}}^{(L_{T})}\circ a(n)\right\|_{L^{1}(\nu)}.

We estimate each of these terms separately.
We recall (cf. (
3.7
) and (
3.1
)) that

|\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}|\ll_{c}(\ln T)^{2}\quad\textrm{and}\quad V_{\Omega}\gg(\ln T)^{2}b,

since
a\leq b/2
and
b\geq(\ln T)^{-2+\theta}
.

To estimate (
7.14
),
we observe that by Lemma
7.2
,

\left\|\left(\widehat{\chi}_{\Delta_{\Omega,{n}}}-\widehat{f}_{\Omega,{n}}\right)\circ a({n})\right\|_{{L}^{1}(\nu)}\ll_{c}b.

We use this bound only when
|n|\leq B\ln\ln T

for a parameter
B>0
. This gives
O_{B}\big((\ln\ln T)^{2}\big)
summands.
On the other hand, when
|n|>B\ln\ln T
,
we use the second part of Lemma
7.2
to deduce that

\displaystyle\left\|\left(\widehat{\chi}_{\Delta_{\Omega,{n}}}-\widehat{f}_{\Omega,{n}}\right)\circ a({n})\right\|_{L^{1}(\nu)}

\displaystyle\ll_{c}\max\left(\varepsilon_{T},-\frac{\varepsilon_{T}}{a}\ln\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{T}}{a}\right)\right)+e^{-n_{1}-n_{2}}

\displaystyle\ll_{K_{1}}(\ln T)^{-K_{1}}\ln\ln T+(\ln T)^{-B},

where used that
\varepsilon_{T}\leq a(\ln T)^{-K_{1}}
.
Therefore, we conclude that

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}}\left\|\left(\widehat{\chi}_{\Delta_{\Omega,{n}}}-\widehat{f}_{\Omega,{n}}\right)\circ a({n})\right\|_{L^{1}(\nu)}\ll_{c,K_{1},B}(\ln\ln T)^{2}b+(\ln T)^{2-K_{1}}\ln\ln T+(\ln T)^{2-B},

which is
o(V_{\Omega}^{1/2})
, when
B
is sufficiently large
due to our assumption
b\geq(\ln T)^{-2(K_{1}-1)+\theta}.

To estimate (
7.15
), we note that by Theorem
5.1
,

\int_{X}\widehat{\chi}_{\Delta_{\Omega,{n}}}\,d\mu=\Vol(\Delta_{\Omega,{n}})\ll_{c}b.

We apply Proposition
5.6
to
{\chi}_{\Delta_{\Omega,{n}}}
. In order to do so, we need to show that
uniformly in
n

the function

A_{n}(y):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\chi_{\Delta_{\Omega,n}}(x_{1},x_{2},y)\,dx_{1}dx_{2}

is bounded and
C^{1}
except at finitely many points, and has bounded derivative. From (
3
), for
y\in[e^{-(n_{1}+n_{2})},Te^{-(n_{1}+n_{2})})
,

A_{n}(y)=4\int_{ce^{-1}}^{c}\left|[ce^{-1},c]\cap\left[ax_{1}^{-1}y^{-1},bx_{1}^{-1}y^{-1}\right]\right|\,dx_{1},

and
A_{n}(y)=0
otherwise. This function is clearly piecewise
C^{1}
, and

|A_{n}^{\prime}|\ll_{c}1\quad\textrm{and}\quad|A_{n}^{\prime}(y)|\ll_{c}b\cdot y^{-2}\quad\hbox{uniformly on $n$.}

We note that also
A_{n}(y)=0
when
y\leq c^{-2}a
, so that
since
a\geq(\ln T)^{2(K_{1}-K_{0})}
,
we conclude that

|A_{n}^{\prime}(y)|\ll_{c}b\cdot a^{-2}\leq(\ln T)^{2(K_{1}-K_{0})}\quad\hbox{uniformly on $n$ and $y$. }

Therefore, it follows from Proposition
5.6
that

\left|\int_{Y}\widehat{\chi}_{\Delta_{\Omega,{n}}}\circ a({n})\,d\nu-\Vol(\Delta_{\Omega,{n}})\right|\ll_{c}(\ln T)^{2(K_{1}-K_{0})}e^{-n_{1}-n_{2}}.

In any case, by the first part of Proposition
5.6
, we also have

\left|\int_{Y}\widehat{\chi}_{\Delta_{\Omega,{n}}}\circ a({n})\,d\nu-\Vol(\Delta_{\Omega,{n}})\right|\ll_{c}b.

Considering the cases when
|n|<B\ln\ln T
and when
|n|\geq B\ln\ln T
, we obtain that

\sum_{n\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}}\left|\int_{Y}\widehat{\chi}_{\Delta_{\Omega,{n}}}\circ a({n})\,d\nu-\Vol(\Delta_{\Omega,n})\right|\ll_{B}(\ln\ln T)^{2}b+(\ln T)^{2+2(K_{1}-K_{0})-B}

for any
B>0
. When
B
is sufficiently large, this gives
o(V_{\Omega}^{1/2})
because of our assumption on
b
.

Finally, we deal with (
7.16
).
Let us introduce a new parameter
M_{T}:=(\ln T)^{K_{2}^{\prime}}\geq L_{T}
for some
K_{2}^{\prime}>K_{2}

to be chosen later and set

\widehat{f}_{\Omega,{n}}^{(L_{T},M_{T})}:=\widehat{f}_{\Omega,{n}}\cdot(\eta_{M_{T}}-\eta_{L_{T}})\quad\textrm{and}\quad g_{\Omega,{n}}^{(L_{T},M_{T})}:=\widehat{f}_{\Omega,{n}}^{(L_{T},M_{T})}-\int_{Y}\widehat{f}_{\Omega,{n}}^{(L_{T},M_{T})}\circ a(n)\,d\nu.

Then

g_{\Omega,{n}}^{(L_{T})}=g_{\Omega,{n}}^{(M_{T})}+g_{\Omega,{n}}^{(L_{T},M_{T})}.

We introduce the subsets

B_{n,k}:=\left\{\Lambda\in Y:\,2^{k}\leq\textup{ht}(a(n)\Lambda)<2^{k+1}\right\}.

In view of (
7.3
) and (
7.5
),

\widehat{f}_{\Omega,{n}}^{(M_{T})}\circ a({n})\ll_{c}2^{k}\quad\;\hbox{on $B_{n,k}$}.

Therefore, from Lemma
5.8
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\int_{Y}\widehat{f}_{\Omega,{n}}^{(M_{T})}\circ a({n})\,d\nu

\displaystyle\ll_{c}\sum_{k:\,2^{k+1}\geq M_{T}/2}2^{k}\cdot\nu(B_{n,k})

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{k:\,2^{k+1}\geq M_{T}/2}2^{k}\left(2^{-3k}+2^{-2k}\cdot e^{-\lfloor n\rfloor}\right)

\displaystyle\ll M_{T}^{-2}+M_{T}^{-1}\cdot e^{-\lfloor n\rfloor}.

Then

(7.17)

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{n\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}}g_{\Omega,{n}}^{(M_{T})}\circ a(n)\right\|_{L^{1}(\nu)}

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{n\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}}\int_{Y}\widehat{f}_{\Omega,{n}}^{(M_{T})}\circ a({n})\,d\nu

\displaystyle\ll M_{T}^{-2}(\ln T)^{2}+M_{T}^{-1}\ln T.

This gives

o(V_{\Omega}^{1/2})=o(b^{1/2}\ln T)
when
we take
M_{T}=(\ln T)^{K_{2}^{\prime}}
with sufficiently large
K_{2}^{\prime}
due
to our assumption on
b
.

Now it remains to estimate the
L^{1}
-norm of

\sum_{n\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}}g_{\Omega,{n}}^{(L_{T},M_{T})}\circ a(n)
.
We use that

\left\|\sum_{n\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}}g_{\Omega,{n}}^{(L_{T},M_{T})}\right\|_{L^{1}(\nu)}\leq\left\|\sum_{n\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}}g_{\Omega,{n}}^{(L_{T},M_{T})}\right\|_{L^{2}(\nu)}

and apply Proposition
6.1
to the functions
g_{\Omega,{n}}^{(L_{T},M_{T})}
.
Since
0\leq\eta_{L_{T}}\leq 1
,

\displaystyle\left\|g_{\Omega,{n}}^{(L_{T},M_{T})}\circ a(n)\right\|_{{L}^{2}(\nu)}

\displaystyle\leq 2\left\|\widehat{f}_{\Omega,n}\circ a(n)\right\|_{{L}^{2}(\nu)},

\displaystyle\left\|g_{\Omega,{n}}^{(L_{T},M_{T})}\circ a({n})\right\|_{{L}^{2}(\nu_{i})}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\widehat{f}_{\Omega,{n}}\circ a({n})\right\|_{{L}^{2}(\nu_{i})}+\left\|\widehat{f}_{\Omega,n}\circ a({n})\right\|_{{L}^{1}(\nu)}.

Recalling (
7.2
), we deduce from Proposition
5.9
that since
a\geq(\ln T)^{K_{1}-K_{0}}
,

(7.18)

\left\|\widehat{f}_{\Omega,n}\circ a(n)\right\|_{{L}^{2}(\nu)}\ll_{c}\max(1,\ln(b/a))\max(1,|n|)^{1/2}\ll_{K_{0},K_{1}}(\ln\ln T)\max(1,|n|)^{1/2}.

This verifies Condition (ii) of Proposition
6.1
.
Condition (iii) follow from Proposition
5.10
and
the first part of Proposition
5.6
using (
7.2
).
Using that
L_{T},M_{T}\leq(\ln T)^{K_{2}^{\prime}}
and
\varepsilon_{T}\geq(\ln T)^{-K_{0}}
,
we deduce that condition (iv) follows from (
7.10
).
Now applying Proposition
6.1
, we obtain that
for all
\delta>0
,

\displaystyle\int_{Y}\left(\sum_{n\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}}g_{\Omega,{n}}^{(L_{T},M_{T})}\circ a({n})\right)^{2}\,d\nu=

\displaystyle\sum_{(n,k)\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}^{\triangledown}}\int_{X}g_{\Omega,{n}}^{(L_{T},M_{T})}\circ a({n})\cdot g_{\Omega,{k}}^{(L_{T},M_{T})}\circ a({k})\,d\mu

(7.19)

\displaystyle\;\;+O_{c,\delta}\left((1+(\ln T)b^{1-\delta})(\ln T)^{\delta}\right),

where we additionally used that
a\geq(\ln T)^{-(K_{0}-K_{1})}
.
By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality,

\int_{X}g_{\Omega,{n}}^{(L_{T},M_{T})}\circ a({n})\cdot g_{\Omega,{k}}^{(L_{T},M_{T})}\circ a({k})\,d\mu\leq\left\|g_{\Omega,{n}}^{(L_{T},M_{T})}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}\cdot\left\|g_{\Omega,{k}}^{(L_{T},M_{T})}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)},

and

\left\|g_{\Omega,{n}}^{(L_{T},M_{T})}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}\leq\left\|\widehat{f}_{\Omega,{n}}^{(L_{T},M_{T})}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}+\left\|\widehat{f}_{\Omega,{n}}^{(L_{T},M_{T})}\circ a(n)\right\|_{L^{1}(\nu)}.

Since
\lfloor n\rfloor\geq B_{1}\ln\ln T

when
(n,k)\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}^{\triangledown}
, it follows from Theorem
4.2
that

\int_{Y}\widehat{f}_{\Omega,{n}}^{(L_{T},M_{T})}\circ a({n})\,d\nu=\int_{X}\widehat{f}_{\Omega,{n}}^{(L_{T},M_{T})}\,d\mu+O\left(\left\|\widehat{f}_{\Omega,{n}}^{(L_{T},M_{T})}\right\|_{C^{\ell}}(\ln T)^{-\gamma_{1}B_{1}}\right).

Further, since
L_{T},M_{T}\leq(\ln T)^{K_{2}^{\prime}}
and
\varepsilon_{T}\geq(\ln T)^{-K_{0}}
, it follows from (
7.10
) that

\left\|\widehat{f}_{\Omega,{n}}^{(L_{T},M_{T})}\right\|_{C^{\ell}}\ll_{\ell}(\ln T)^{\sigma-\gamma_{1}B_{1}}

for some
\sigma=\sigma(K_{0},K_{2}^{\prime})>0
.
Therefore, we conclude that

\left\|g_{\Omega,{n}}^{(L_{T},M_{T})}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}\ll\left\|\widehat{f}_{\Omega,{n}}^{(L_{T},M_{T})}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}+(\ln T)^{\sigma-\gamma_{1}B_{1}}.

Using Lemma
5.5
and
Proposition
5.13
,
we deduce from Hölder’s Inequality that
for
p<3/2

\displaystyle\left\|\widehat{f}_{\Omega,{n}}^{(L_{T},M_{T})}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\{\hbox{\tiny ht}\geq L_{T}/2\}}\widehat{f}_{\Omega,{n}}^{2}\,d\mu\leq\left(\int_{X}\widehat{f}_{\Omega,{n}}^{2p}\,d\mu\right)^{1/p}\left(\int_{X}\chi_{\{\hbox{\tiny ht}\geq L_{T}/2\}}\,d\mu\right)^{(p-1)/p}

\displaystyle\ll_{c,p,\delta}b^{2/3}L_{T}^{-(3-\delta)(p-1)/p}.

This implies that for any
\delta>0
,

\displaystyle\left\|g_{\Omega,{n}}^{(L_{T},M_{T})}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}\ll_{c,\delta}b^{1/3}L_{T}^{-1/2+\delta}+(\ln T)^{\sigma-\gamma_{1}B_{1}}.

Therefore, since

(7.20)

|\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}^{\triangledown}|\ll_{B}|\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}|(\ln\ln T)^{2}\ll_{c}(\ln T)^{2}(\ln\ln T)^{2},

we obtain from (
7.19
) that

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{n\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}}g_{\Omega,{n}}^{(L_{T},M_{T})}\circ a({n})\right\|_{L^{1}(\nu)}\ll_{c,\delta,B}

\displaystyle\Big(\big(b^{1/3}L_{T}^{-1/2+\delta}+(\ln T)^{\sigma-\gamma_{1}B_{1}}\big)^{2}(\ln T)^{2}(\ln\ln T)^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad+(1+(\ln T)b^{1-\delta})(\ln T)^{\delta}\Big)^{1/2}

for any
\delta>0
.
We need to ensure that this bound is
o(V_{\Omega}^{1/2})=o(b^{1/2}\ln T)
, which follows
by taking
B_{1}
sufficiently large and using that

L_{T}\geq b^{-1/3}(\ln T)^{\theta}
and
b\geq(\ln T)^{-2+\theta}
.
∎

8.
Estimating the variance: second step

In this section we analyze the variance with respect to the measure
\nu
of the sums
\Phi_{\Omega}
defined in (
7.11
).
We assume that for some
K_{0},K_{1},K_{2}>0
and
\theta>0
,

(8.1)

\displaystyle a\leq b/2,\;\;(\ln T)^{-K_{0}}\leq\varepsilon_{T}\leq a(\ln T)^{-K_{1}},

\displaystyle b^{-1/3}(\ln T)^{\theta}\leq L_{T}\leq(\ln T)^{K_{2}},\;\;b\geq(\ln T)^{-\min(2,K_{1})+\theta}.

Throughout this section, we use that

|\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}|\ll_{c}(\ln T)^{2}\quad\textrm{and}\quad V_{\Omega}\gg(\ln T)^{2}b,

which follows from (
3.7
) and (
3.1
),
since
a\leq b/2
and
b\geq(\ln T)^{-2+\theta}
.

We apply Proposition
6.1
to the functions
\varphi_{\Omega,n}
(cf. (
7.8
)).
Since
0\leq\eta_{L_{T}}\leq 1
,

\displaystyle\big\|\varphi_{\Omega,n}\circ a(n)\big\|_{{L}^{2}(\nu)}

\displaystyle\leq 2\left\|\widehat{f}_{\Omega,n}\circ a(n)\right\|_{{L}^{2}(\nu)},

\displaystyle\big\|\varphi_{\Omega,{n}}\circ a({n})\|_{{L}^{p}(\nu_{i})}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\widehat{f}_{\Omega,n}\circ a({n})\right\|_{{L}^{p}(\nu_{i})}+\left\|\widehat{f}_{\Omega,n}\circ a({n})\right\|_{{L}^{1}(\nu)}.

Thus, Condition (ii) follows from (
7.18
),
and Condition (iii) follows from Proposition
5.10

and the first part of Propositions
5.6
.
In view of our assumptions on
\varepsilon_{T}
and
L_{T}
,
Condition (iv) follows from (
7.10
).
Applying Proposition
6.1
and noting that
b\geq(\ln T)^{-2+\theta}
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\int_{Y}\Phi_{\Omega}^{2}\,d\nu

\displaystyle=\sum_{(n,k)\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}^{\triangledown}}\int_{X}\varphi_{\Omega,n}\circ a(n)\cdot\varphi_{\Omega,k}\circ a(k)\,d\mu+O_{c,\delta}\Big((1+(\ln T)b^{1-\delta})(\ln T)^{\delta}\Big)

for all
\delta>0
, so that

\displaystyle\int_{Y}\Phi_{\Omega}^{2}\,d\nu

\displaystyle=\sum_{(n,k)\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}^{\triangledown}}\int_{X}\varphi_{\Omega,n}\circ a(n)\cdot\varphi_{\Omega,k}\circ a(k)\,d\mu+o(V_{\Omega}).

Next, we show that

\sum_{(n,k)\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}^{\triangledown}}\int_{X}\varphi_{\Omega,n}\circ a(n)\cdot\varphi_{\Omega,k}\circ a(k)\,d\mu=\sum_{(n,k)\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}^{\triangledown}}\int_{X}\psi_{\Omega,n}\circ a(n)\cdot\psi_{\Omega,k}\circ a(k)\,d\mu+o(V_{\Omega}).

We have

(8.2)

\displaystyle\int_{X}\varphi_{\Omega,n}\circ a({n})\cdot\varphi_{\Omega,k}\circ a(k)\,d\mu=

\displaystyle\int_{X}\psi_{\Omega,n}\circ a({n})\cdot\psi_{\Omega,k}\circ a(k)\,d\mu

\displaystyle+\int_{X}(\varphi_{\Omega,n}-\psi_{\Omega,n})\circ a({n})\cdot\varphi_{\Omega,k}\circ a(k)\,d\mu

\displaystyle+\int_{X}\psi_{\Omega,n}\circ a({n})\cdot(\varphi_{\Omega,k}-\psi_{\Omega,k})\circ a(k)\,d\mu.

By the Cauchy–Schwartz inequality,

\left|\int_{X}(\varphi_{\Omega,n}-\psi_{\Omega,n})\circ a({n})\cdot\varphi_{\Omega,k}\circ a(k)\,d\mu\right|\leq\left\|\varphi_{\Omega,n}-\psi_{\Omega,n}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}\cdot\left\|\varphi_{\Omega,k}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}.

Since
0\leq\eta_{L_{T}}\leq 1
, it follows from Lemma
7.1
and Proposition
5.6
that

\left\|\varphi_{\Omega,k}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}\leq\left\|\widehat{f}_{\Omega,k}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}+\left\|\widehat{f}_{\Omega,k}\right\|_{L^{1}(\nu)}\ll_{c}b^{1/2}.

The other factor is estimated as

\displaystyle\left\|\varphi_{\Omega,n}-\psi_{\Omega,n}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\widehat{f}_{\Omega,{n}}\cdot\eta_{L_{T}}-\widehat{\chi}_{\Delta_{\Omega,{n}}}\right\|_{{L}^{2}(\mu)}

\displaystyle+\left|\int_{Y}\left(\widehat{f}_{\Omega,{n}}\cdot\eta_{L_{T}}\right)\circ a({n})\,d\nu-\int_{X}\widehat{\chi}_{\Delta_{\Omega,{n}}}\,d\mu\right|.

Since
\lfloor n\rfloor\geq B_{1}\ln\ln T
, it follows from Theorem
4.2
that

(8.3)

\int_{Y}\left(\widehat{f}_{\Omega,{n}}\cdot\eta_{L_{T}}\right)\circ a({n})\,d\nu=\int_{X}\widehat{f}_{\Omega,{n}}\cdot\eta_{L_{T}}\,d\mu+O\left(\left\|\widehat{f}_{\Omega,{n}}\cdot\eta_{L_{T}}\right\|_{C^{\ell}}(\ln T)^{-\gamma_{1}B_{1}}\right).

Since
L_{T}\leq(\ln T)^{K_{2}}
and
\varepsilon_{T}\geq(\ln T)^{-K_{0}}
,
it follows from (
7.10
) that

\left\|\widehat{f}_{\Omega,{n}}\cdot\eta_{L_{T}}\right\|_{C^{\ell}}\ll_{\ell}(\ln T)^{\sigma-\gamma_{1}B_{1}}

for some
\sigma=\sigma(K_{0},K_{2})>0
.
Therefore, the sum coming from the second terms in (
8.3
) gives a negligible contribution when
B_{1}
is chosen sufficiently large. Further,

\left|\int_{X}\widehat{f}_{\Omega,{n}}\cdot\eta_{L_{T}}\,d\mu-\int_{X}\widehat{\chi}_{\Delta_{\Omega,{n}}}\,d\mu\right|\leq\left\|\widehat{f}_{\Omega,{n}}\cdot\eta_{L_{T}}-\widehat{\chi}_{\Delta_{\Omega,{n}}}\right\|_{{L}^{2}(\mu)},

so that it remains to deal with these
L^{2}
-norms, which we estimate as

\left\|\widehat{f}_{\Omega,{n}}\cdot\eta_{L_{T}}-\chi_{\Delta_{\Omega,{n}}}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}\leq\left\|\widehat{f}_{\Omega,{n}}-\widehat{\chi}_{\Delta_{\Omega,{n}}}\right\|_{{L}^{2}(\mu)}+\left\|(1-\eta_{L_{T}})\cdot\widehat{\chi}_{\Delta_{\Omega,{n}}}\right\|_{{L}^{2}(\mu)}

using that
0\leq\eta_{T}\leq 1
.
According to Lemma
7.4
,

\left\|\widehat{f}_{\Omega,{n}}-\widehat{\chi}_{\Delta_{\Omega,{n}}}\right\|_{{L}^{2}(\mu)}\ll_{c}\max\left(\varepsilon_{T},-\frac{\varepsilon_{T}}{a}\ln\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{T}}{a}\right)\right)^{1/2}.

To estimate the last term, we apply the Hölder inequality with

1/p+1/q=1/2
,
q<3
:

\left\|(1-\eta_{L_{T}})\cdot\widehat{\chi}_{\Delta_{\Omega,{n}}}\right\|_{{L}^{2}(\mu)}\leq\mu\Big(\{\height\geq L_{T}/2\}\Big)^{1/p}\left\|\widehat{\chi}_{\Delta_{\Omega,{n}}}\right\|_{{L}^{q}(\mu)}\ll_{c,q}L_{T}^{-q/p}b^{1/3},

where we used Lemma
5.5
and Proposition
5.13
.

Combining the above estimates, we deduce that

\displaystyle\int_{X}(\varphi_{\Omega,n}-\psi_{\Omega,n})\circ a({n})

\displaystyle\cdot\varphi_{\Omega,k}\circ a(k)\,d\mu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,O_{c,q}\left(b^{1/2}\max\left(\varepsilon_{T},-\frac{\varepsilon_{T}}{a}\ln\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{T}}{a}\right)\right)^{1/2}+L_{T}^{-q/p}b^{5/6}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,O_{c,K_{1},\delta}\left(b^{1/2}(\ln T)^{-K_{1}/2}(\ln\ln T)^{1/2}+L_{T}^{-1/2+\delta}b^{5/6}\right)

for any
\delta>0
.
The last term in (
8.2
) can be estimated similarly. Using (
7.20
), we conclude that

\displaystyle\sum_{(n,k)\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}^{\triangledown}}\int_{X}\varphi_{\Omega,n}\circ a(n)\cdot\varphi_{\Omega,k}\circ a(k)\,d\mu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{(n,k)\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}^{\triangledown}}\int_{X}\psi_{\Omega,n}\circ a({n})\cdot\psi_{\Omega,k}\circ a(k)\,d\mu

\displaystyle\quad\quad+O_{c,B,K_{1},\delta}\left(b^{1/2}(\ln T)^{2-K_{1}/2}(\ln\ln T)^{5/2}+(\ln T)^{2}(\ln\ln T)^{2}L_{T}^{-1/2+\delta}b^{5/6}\right)

for every
\delta>0
.
Since

L_{T}\geq b^{-1/3}(\ln T)^{\theta}
and
b\geq(\ln T)^{-K_{1}+\theta}
, the last term is
o(V_{\Omega})
as
T\to\infty
.
Therefore, we conclude that

\int_{Y}\Phi_{\Omega}^{2}\,d\nu={\sum}_{(n,k)\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}^{\triangledown}}\int_{X}\psi_{\Omega,n}\circ a({n})\cdot\psi_{\Omega,k}\circ a(k)\,d\mu+o(V_{\Omega})\quad\hbox{as $T\to\infty$.}

Recalling (
7.7
) and using Theorem
5.2
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{X}\psi_{\Omega,n}\cdot\psi_{\Omega,k}\,d\mu

\displaystyle=\int_{X}\widehat{\chi}_{a(-n)\Delta_{\Omega,{n}}}\cdot\widehat{\chi}_{a(-k)\Delta_{\Omega,{n}}}\,d\mu-\Vol(\Delta_{\Omega,{n}})\cdot\Vol(\Delta_{\Omega,k})

\displaystyle=\zeta(3)^{-1}\sum_{p,q\in\mathbb{N}}\Vol\left(p^{-1}\cdot a(-n)\Delta_{\Omega,n}\cap q^{-1}\cdot a(-k)\Delta_{\Omega,k}\right).

The above computation verifies the following lemma:

Lemma 8.1
.

Under the assumptions (
8.1
),
there exist constants
B,B_{0},B_{1}>0
, depending only on
K_{0},K_{1},K_{2}
such that

\int_{Y}\Phi_{\Omega}^{2}\,d\nu=\zeta(3)^{-1}\sum_{(n,k)\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}^{\triangledown}}\sum_{p,q\in\mathbb{N}}V_{p,q}(n,k)+o(V_{\Omega})\quad\hbox{as $T\to\infty$},

where
\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}^{\triangledown}=\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}^{\triangledown}(B,B_{0},B_{1})
is defined in (
6.1
) and

V_{p,q}(n,k):=\Vol\left(p^{-1}\cdot a(-n)\Delta_{\Omega,{n}}\cap q^{-1}\cdot a(-k)\Delta_{\Omega,k}\right).

The following lemma complements the above result:

Lemma 8.2
.

Assume that for some
K_{4}>0
and
\varepsilon>0
.

(8.4)

(\ln T)^{-K_{4}}\leq a\leq b(\ln\ln T)^{-4-\varepsilon}.

Then

V_{\Omega}^{-1}\cdot\sum_{(n,k)\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}^{\triangledown}}\sum_{p,q\in\mathbb{N}}V_{p,q}(n,k)\longrightarrow\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\sum_{p,q\in\mathbb{N}}V_{p,q}(m)\quad\hbox{ as $T\to\infty$,}

where
\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}^{\triangledown}
is defined in (
6.1
)
and

V_{p,q}(m):=\Vol\left(p^{-1}\cdot a(m)\Delta_{c}\cap q^{-1}\cdot\Delta_{c}\right)

with
\Delta_{c}
defined in (
1.4
).

Proof.

We note that from (
3.1
) and our assumption (
8.4
),

(8.5)

V_{\Omega}\sim(\ln T)^{2}b\quad\hbox{as $T\to\infty$}

and from (
3.8
),

a(-m)\Delta_{\Omega,m}\subset\big(e^{-m_{1}-1}c,e^{-m_{1}}c\big]\times\big(e^{-m_{2}-1}c,e^{-m_{2}}c\big]\times\big(e^{m_{1}+m_{2}}ac^{-2},e^{m_{1}+m_{2}+2}bc^{-2}\big].

Let
K_{p,q}(n):=\{k:\,V_{p,q}(n,k)\neq 0\}
.
Then when
k\in K_{p,q}(n)
,

(8.6)

\frac{e^{-n_{i}-1}}{p}\leq\frac{e^{-k_{i}}}{q}\quad\textrm{and}\quad\frac{e^{-k_{i}-1}}{q}\leq\frac{e^{-n_{i}}}{p}\quad\quad\hbox{for $i=1,2$.}

This gives at most three possibilities for
k_{i}

when the other parameters are fixed, and it follows that

|{K_{p,q}(n)}|\leq 9.

Therefore, it follows from the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality that

(8.7)

\displaystyle\sum_{(n,k)\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}^{\triangledown}}\sum_{p,q\in\mathbb{N}}V_{p,q}(n,k)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,\sum_{n\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}}\sum_{p,q\in\mathbb{N}}\sum_{k\in K_{p,q}(n)}\Vol\left(p^{-1}\cdot a(-n)\Delta_{\Omega,n}\right)^{1/2}\cdot\Vol\left(q^{-1}\cdot a(-k)\Delta_{\Omega,k}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,\sum_{n\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}}\sum_{p,q\in\mathbb{N}}\sum_{k\in K_{p,q}(n)}p^{-3/2}\Vol\left(\Delta_{\Omega,n}\right)^{1/2}\cdot q^{-3/2}\Vol\left(\Delta_{\Omega,k}\right)^{1/2}\ll_{c}\,|\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}|b\ll V_{\Omega}.

We refine this computation to show that the normalized above sum converges.
Let us introduce the following super-sets of

\Delta_{\Omega,{n}}
:

(8.8)

\Delta_{\Omega}:=\left\{(x_{1},x_{2},y)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\,:\,\begin{array}[]{c}a<|x_{1}x_{2}|\,y\leq b\\[8.5359pt]
e^{-1}c<|x_{1}|,|x_{2}|\leq c\end{array}\right\}\subset[-c,c]^{2}\times[ac^{-2},e^{2}bc^{-2}].

We observe that
\Delta_{\Omega}=\Delta_{\Omega,{n}}

unless

(8.9)

e^{2}bc^{-2}\geq Te^{-(n_{1}+n_{2})}\quad\hbox{or}\quad ac^{-2}\leq e^{-(n_{1}+n_{2})}.

The first condition in (
8.9
) gives a subset of
n\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}
with

\ln\big(Te^{-2}b^{-1}c^{2}\big)\leq n_{1}+n_{2}<\beta_{\Omega}=\ln\big(Ta^{-1}c^{2}\big),

and the number of such
n
is estimated as

(8.10)

\leq\left(\ln\big(Ta^{-1}c^{2}\big)+1\right)\cdot\left(\ln\big(e^{2}ba^{-1}\big)+1\right)\ll_{K_{4}}\ln T\cdot\ln\ln T.

Similarly, the second condition in (
8.9
) gives a subset of
n\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}
with

\alpha_{\Omega}=\ln\big(e^{-2}b^{-1}c^{2}\big)\leq n_{1}+n_{2}\leq\ln\big(a^{-1}c^{2}\big),

and the number of such
n
is estimated as

(8.11)

\leq\left(\ln\big(a^{-1}c^{2}\big)+1\right)\left(\ln\big(e^{2}ba^{-1}\big)+1\right)\ll_{K_{4}}(\ln\ln T)^{2}.

Let
\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}^{\diamond}
denote the subset of
(n,k)\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}^{\triangledown}

where both
n
and
k
do not satisfy (
8.9
).
Then arguing as in (
8.7
), we conclude that

\sum_{(n,k)\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}^{\triangledown}\setminus\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}^{\diamond}}\sum_{p,q\in\mathbb{N}}V_{p,q}(n,k)\ll_{c}\ln T\cdot\ln\ln T\cdot b=o(V_{\Omega}).

Therefore, it remains to analyze the sum

\sum_{(n,k)\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}^{\diamond}}\sum_{p,q\in\mathbb{N}}V^{\prime}_{p,q}(n,k),

where

V^{\prime}_{p,q}(n,k):=\Vol\left(p^{-1}\cdot a(-n)\Delta_{\Omega}\cap q^{-1}\cdot a(-k)\Delta_{\Omega}\right).

Further, we consider the sets

(8.12)

\widetilde{\Delta}_{\Omega}:=\left\{(x_{1},x_{2},y)\,:\,\begin{array}[]{c}0<|x_{1}x_{2}|\cdot y\leq b\\[8.5359pt]
e^{-1}c<|x_{1}|,|x_{2}|\leq c\end{array}\right\}

and define

\displaystyle\widetilde{V}_{p,q}(n,k):=

\displaystyle\Vol\left(p^{-1}\cdot a(-n)\widetilde{\Delta}_{\Omega}\cap q^{-1}\cdot a(-k)\widetilde{\Delta}_{\Omega}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,b\cdot\Vol\left(p^{-1}\cdot a(-n)\Delta_{c}\cap q^{-1}\cdot a(-k)\Delta_{c}\right)=b\cdot V_{p,q}(n-k).

It follows from our assumption (
8.4
) that

\Vol\left(\widetilde{\Delta}_{\Omega}\setminus\Delta_{\Omega}\right)\ll_{c}a\leq b\cdot(\ln\ln T)^{-4-\varepsilon}.

We shall use the following estimate: for subsets
\Delta_{1}\subset\widetilde{\Delta}_{1}
and
\Delta_{2}\subset\widetilde{\Delta}_{2}
of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
,

\displaystyle\Vol(\widetilde{\Delta}_{1}\cap\widetilde{\Delta}_{2})-\Vol(\Delta_{1}\cap\Delta_{2})\leq

\displaystyle\Vol(\widetilde{\Delta}_{1}\setminus\Delta_{1})^{1/2}\Vol(\widetilde{\Delta}_{2})^{1/2}

\displaystyle\quad+\Vol(\Delta_{1})^{1/2}\Vol(\widetilde{\Delta}_{2}\setminus\Delta_{2})^{1/2},

which follows from the Cauchy–Schwarz Inequality.
This gives

\widetilde{V}_{p,q}(n,k)=V_{p,q}^{\prime}(n,k)+O_{c}\left(p^{-3/2}q^{-3/2}\cdot b\cdot(\ln\ln T)^{-2-\varepsilon/2}\right),

so that in view of (
7.20
) and (
8.5
)

\sum_{(n,k)\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}^{\diamond}}\sum_{p,q\in\mathbb{N}}\widetilde{V}_{p,q}(n,k)=\sum_{(n,k)\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}^{\diamond}}\sum_{p,q\in\mathbb{N}}V^{\prime}_{p,q}(n,k)+o(V_{\Omega})\quad\hbox{as $T\to\infty$.}

Now it remains to compute the limit

\lim_{T\to\infty}(\ln T)^{-2}\sum_{(n,k)\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}^{\diamond}}\sum_{p,q\in\mathbb{N}}V_{p,q}(n-k).

It follows from the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality that

(8.13)

V_{p,q}(m)\ll_{c}p^{-3/2}q^{-3/2}\quad\hbox{uniformly on $m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}$.}

We recall that pairs
(n,k)\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}^{\triangledown}
satisfy

|n-k|<B\,\ln\ln T
, so that taking (
8.10
) and (
8.11
) into account,
we obtain that

|\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}^{\triangledown}\setminus\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}^{\diamond}|\ll_{B,K_{4}}\ln T\cdot(\ln\ln T)^{3}.

Therefore, because of (
8.13
),

\sum_{(n,k)\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}^{\diamond}}\sum_{p,q\in\mathbb{N}}V_{p,q}(n-k)=\sum_{(n,k)\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}^{\triangledown}}\sum_{p,q\in\mathbb{N}}V_{p,q}(n-k)+o\big((\ln T)^{2}\big).

Let
\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}^{\square}
consist of
(n,k)\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}\times\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}
such that

|n-k|<B\,\ln\ln T
. We note that the number of pairs

(n,k)\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}^{\square}
such that either

|n|<B_{0}\ln\ln T
or
\lfloor n\rfloor<{B_{1}}\ln\ln T

is
O_{B,B_{0},B_{1},c}\big(\ln T(\ln\ln T)^{3}\big)
.
Thus, because of (
8.13
),

\sum_{(n,k)\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}^{\triangledown}}\sum_{p,q\in\mathbb{N}}V_{p,q}(n-k)=\sum_{(n,k)\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}^{\square}}\sum_{p,q\in\mathbb{N}}V_{p,q}(n-k)+o\big((\ln T)^{2}\big).

We observe that when
V_{p,q}(n-k)\neq 0
,

p^{-1}\cdot a(-n)\Delta_{c}\cap q^{-1}\cdot a(-k)\Delta_{c}\neq\emptyset

and

pe^{n_{i}}\asymp_{c}qe^{k_{i}}
for
i=1,2
, so that
\max(p,q)\gg_{c}e^{|n-k|}
. Hence, using (
8.13
),

\displaystyle\sum_{(n,k)\in(\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}\times\mathcal{F}_{\Omega})\setminus\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}^{\square}}\sum_{p,q\in\mathbb{N}}V_{p,q}(n-k)

\displaystyle\ll_{c}|\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}|^{2}\sum_{p,q\in\mathbb{N}:\,\max(p,q)\gg_{c}(\ln T)^{B}}p^{-3/2}q^{-3/2}

\displaystyle=o\big((\ln T)^{2}\big)

when
B
is sufficiently large.
Finally,

\displaystyle(\ln T)^{-2}\sum_{n,k\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}}\sum_{p,q\in\mathbb{N}}V_{p,q}(n-k)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\sum_{p,q\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{|\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}\cap(m+\mathcal{F}_{\Omega})|}{(\ln T)^{2}}\cdot V_{p,q}(m).

We observe that when
V_{p,q}(m)\neq 0
, we have
pe^{m_{i}}\asymp_{c}q
for
i=1,2
, and it follows that
\max(p,q)\gg_{c}e^{|m|}
. Using (
8.13
), we conclude that

\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\sum_{p,q\in\mathbb{N}}V_{p,q}(m)\ll_{c}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}e^{-|m|/2}<\infty.

Since

|\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}|\ll_{c}(\ln T)^{2}\quad\textrm{and}\quad|\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}\cap(m+\mathcal{F}_{\Omega})|\sim(\ln T)^{2}\;\;\hbox{as $T\to\infty$},

it follows from the Dominated Convergence Theorem that

\displaystyle(\ln T)^{-2}\sum_{n,k\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}}\sum_{p,q\in\mathbb{N}}V_{p,q}(n-k)\longrightarrow\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}{\sum}_{p,q\in\mathbb{N}}V_{p,q}(m)\quad\hbox{as $T\to\infty$},

which completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Combining Lemmas
8.1
and
8.2
, we obtain the following

Proposition 8.3
.

Under the assumptions (
8.1
) and (
8.4
),

V_{\Omega}^{-1}\cdot\int_{Y}\Phi_{\Omega}^{2}\,d\nu\to\zeta(3)^{-1}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\sum_{p,q\in\mathbb{N}}V_{p,q}(m)\quad\hbox{ as $T\to\infty$.}

9.
Estimating higher-order cumulants

The goal of this section is to analyze the higher moments
or equivalently cumulants of the functions
\Phi_{\Omega}
defines in (
7.11
).
Before stating the main result, let us first introduce relevant notations.
Given bounded measurable functions
\phi_{i}:X\to\mathbb{R}
,
i=1,\dotsc,r
,
the cumulant with respect to the measure
\nu
is defined as

(9.1)

\textup{Cum}(\phi_{1},\dotsc,\phi_{r}):=\sum_{\mathcal{P}}(-1)^{|\mathcal{{P}}|-1}(|\mathcal{P}|-1)!\prod_{I\in\mathcal{P}}\int_{Y}{\prod}_{i\in I}\phi_{i}\,d\nu,

where the sum ranges over all partitions
\mathcal{P}
of the set
\{1,\dotsc,r\}
. Further, for a single bounded function
\phi
, one defines

\textup{Cum}_{r}(\phi):=\textup{Cum}(\phi,\dotsc,\phi).

The main result is the following:

Proposition 9.1
.

Assume that for some
K_{0}>0
and
\theta>0
,

(9.2)

a\leq b/2,\quad(\ln T)^{-K_{0}}\leq\varepsilon_{T},\quad L_{T}\leq V_{\Omega}^{1/2},\quad b\geq(\ln T)^{-2+\theta}.

Then for all
r\geq 3
,

\textup{Cum}_{r}\left(\Phi_{\Omega}\right)=o(V_{\Omega}^{r/2})\quad\hbox{as $T\to\infty$.}

Let
\mathcal{Q}
be a partition of
\{1,\dotsc,r\}
.
For
i,j\in\{1,\dotsc,r\}
we write
i\sim_{\mathcal{Q}}j
to indicate that
i
and
j
lie in the same atom of the partition
\mathcal{Q}
.
For
0\leq\alpha<\beta
, we define

\displaystyle\Delta_{\mathcal{Q}}(\alpha,\beta)

\displaystyle:=\left\{(n_{1},\dotsc,n_{r})\in(\mathbb{N}_{o}^{2})^{r}:\begin{tabular}[]{ll}$|n_{i}-n_{j}|\leq\alpha$&if $i\sim_{\mathcal{Q}}j$,\\

\displaystyle\textup{Cum}_{r}\left(\Phi_{\Omega}\right)=

\displaystyle\sum_{({n}_{1},\dotsc,{n}_{r})\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}^{r}}\textup{Cum}\big(\varphi_{\Omega,{n}_{1}}\circ a(n_{1}),\dotsc,\varphi_{\Omega,{n}_{r}}\circ a(n_{r})\big)

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}\sum_{|\mathcal{Q}|\geq 2}\sum_{({n}_{1},\dotsc,{n}_{r})\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}^{r}\cap\Delta_{\mathcal{Q}}(3\beta_{j},\beta_{j+1})}\left|\textup{Cum}\big(\varphi_{\Omega,{n}_{1}}\circ a(n_{1}),\dotsc,\varphi_{\Omega,{n}_{r}}\circ a(n_{r})\big)\right|.

Lemma 9.2
.

Under the assumptions (
9.2
),

\displaystyle\sum_{({n}_{1},\dotsc,{n}_{r})\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}^{r}\cap\Delta(\beta_{r})}\Big|\textup{Cum}\big(\varphi_{\Omega,{n}_{1}}\circ a(n_{1}),\dotsc,\varphi_{\Omega,{n}_{r}}\circ a(n_{r})\big)\Big|=o\left(V_{\Omega}^{r/2}\right)\quad\hbox{as $T\to\infty$.}

Proof.

We note that by (

) and (

\phi_{\Omega,n}\ll_{c}L_{T}.

Now it remains to estimate the products

\prod_{I\in\mathcal{Q}}\int_{Y}\prod_{i\in I}\phi_{\Omega,{n}_{i}}\circ a(n_{i})\,d\nu

for partitions
\mathcal{Q}
of
\{1,\ldots,r\}
.
Since
({n}_{1},\dotsc,{n}_{r})\in\Delta(\beta_{r})
, for some
K_{1}>0

(9.5)

|n_{i}-n_{j}|\leq K_{1}\,\ln\ln T\quad\hbox{for all $i,j$.}

We distinguish several possibilities.
First, we analyze the case when

\lfloor n_{1}\rfloor\geq B_{1}\,\ln\ln T

for a constant

to be specified later.
We pick

From (

Also in view of (

Ultimately, we conclude that

The case when

\displaystyle\int_{Y}\prod_{i\in I}\phi_{\Omega,{n}_{i}}\circ a(n_{i})\,d\nu

Lemma 9.3
.

Let
M,M^{\prime}\geq 0
and
\mathcal{Q}
be a non-trivial partition
of
\{1,\ldots,r\}
.
Then under the assumptions (
9.2
), for all
(n_{1},\dotsc,n_{r})\in\Delta_{\mathcal{Q}}\big(M\,\ln\ln T,M^{\prime}\,\ln\ln T\big)
,

\Big|\textup{Cum}(\varphi_{\Omega,{n}_{1}}\circ a(n_{1}),\dotsc,\varphi_{\Omega,{n}_{r}}\circ a(n_{r}))\Big|\ll(\ln T)^{r(K_{0}\sigma_{\ell}+1)+r\theta_{\ell}M-\eta_{r}M^{\prime}}.

Proof.

For bounded measurable functions
\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{r}:X\to\mathbb{R}
,
one defines the conditional cumulant with respect to a partition
\mathcal{Q}
as

\textup{Cum}(\phi_{1},\dotsc,\phi_{r}|\mathcal{Q}):=\sum_{\mathcal{P}}(-1)^{|\mathcal{{P}}|-1}(|\mathcal{P}|-1)!\prod_{I\in\mathcal{P}}\prod_{J\in\mathcal{Q}}\int_{X}{\prod}_{i\in I\cap J}\phi_{i}\,d\nu.

Here the product over an empty set is understood to be
1
.
It is well-known (see, for instance,
[
4
, Proposition 8.1]
) that

\textup{Cum}(\phi_{1},\dotsc,\phi_{r}|\mathcal{Q})=0

for every non-trivial partition
\mathcal{Q}
.
Therefore, it will be sufficient to check that

\textup{Cum}(\varphi_{\Omega,{n}_{1}}\circ a(n_{1}),\dotsc,\varphi_{\Omega,{n}_{r}}\circ a(n_{r}))\approx\textup{Cum}(\varphi_{\Omega,{n}_{1}}\circ a(n_{1}),\dotsc,\varphi_{\Omega,{n}_{r}}\circ a(n_{r})|\mathcal{Q})

with the above error term. In fact, we show that
for any partition
\mathcal{P}
,

\prod_{I\in\mathcal{P}}\int_{X}{\prod}_{i\in I}\varphi_{\Omega,n_{i}}\circ a(n_{i})\,d\nu\approx\prod_{I\in\mathcal{P}}\prod_{J\in\mathcal{Q}}\int_{X}{\prod}_{i\in I\cap J}\varphi_{\Omega,n_{i}}\circ a(n_{i})\,d\nu.

When
I\cap J\neq\emptyset
, we pick
i_{J}\in I\cap J
and set

F_{I,J}:={\prod}_{i\in I\cap J}\varphi_{\Omega,{n}_{i}}\circ a(n_{i}-n_{i_{J}}).

Since
(n_{1},\dotsc,n_{r})\in\Delta_{\mathcal{Q}}\big(M\,\ln\ln T,M^{\prime}\,\ln\ln T\big)
,

|n_{i}-n_{i_{J}}|\leq M\ln\ln T,

so that it follows from (
7.10
) that for some
\theta_{\ell}>0
,

\displaystyle\|F_{I,J}\|_{C^{\ell}}

\displaystyle\ll_{\ell}{\prod}_{i\in I\cap J}\|\varphi_{\Omega,{n}_{i}}\circ a(n_{i}-n_{i_{J}})\|_{C^{\ell}}\ll_{\ell}{\prod}_{i\in I\cap J}\|\varphi_{\Omega,{n}_{i}}\|_{C^{\ell}}e^{\theta_{\ell}|n_{i}-n_{i_{J}}|}

\displaystyle\ll_{\ell}(\varepsilon_{T}^{-\sigma_{\ell}}L_{T}e^{\theta_{\ell}M\ln\ln T})^{|I\cap J|}\leq(\ln T)^{|I\cap J|(K_{0}\sigma_{\ell}+\theta_{\ell}M)}L_{T}^{|I\cap J|}.

Applying Theorem
4.1
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\int_{Y}{\prod}_{i\in I}\varphi_{\Omega,n_{i}}\circ a(n_{i})\,d\nu=

\displaystyle\int_{Y}{\prod}_{J\in\mathcal{Q}}F_{I,J}\circ a(n_{i_{J}})d\nu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\prod}_{J\in\mathcal{Q}}\int_{Y}F_{I,J}\circ a(n_{i_{J}})d\nu

\displaystyle\quad\quad+O_{r}\left(e^{-\eta_{r}\min_{J_{1}\neq J_{2}}|n_{i_{J_{1}}}-n_{i_{J_{2}}}|}{\prod}_{J\in\mathcal{Q}}\|F_{I,J}\|_{C^{\ell}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{J\in\mathcal{Q}}\int_{Y}{\prod}_{i\in I\cap J}\varphi_{\Omega,n_{i}}\circ a(n_{i})\,d\nu

(9.13)

\displaystyle\quad\quad+O_{r}\Big((\ln T)^{|I|(K_{0}\sigma_{\ell}+\theta_{\ell}M)-\eta_{r}M^{\prime}}L_{T}^{|I|}\Big).

For subsets
I\subset\{1,\ldots,r\}
, we also have the bound

(9.14)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{Y}{\prod}_{i\in I}\varphi_{\Omega,n_{i}}\circ a(n_{i})\,d\nu\right|\leq L_{T}^{|I|},

which follows from (
7.9
).
Since
L_{T}\leq V_{\Omega}^{1/2}\ll\ln T
,
combining (
9.13
) and (
9.14
), we conclude that

\displaystyle\prod_{I\in\mathcal{P}}\int_{X}{\prod}_{i\in I}\varphi_{\Omega,n_{i}}\circ a(n_{i})\,d\nu=

\displaystyle\prod_{I\in\mathcal{P}}\prod_{J\in\mathcal{Q}}\int_{X}{\prod}_{i\in I\cap J}\varphi_{\Omega,n_{i}}\circ a(n_{i})\,d\nu

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad+O_{r}\Big((\ln T)^{r(K_{0}\sigma_{\ell}+1)+r\theta_{\ell}M-\eta_{r}M^{\prime}}\Big).

This implies the lemma.
∎

using the decomposition (

10.
Completion of proof

Now we finish the proof of Theorem
1.2
.
We combine Proposition
7.5
, Proposition
8.3
, and Proposition
9.1
.
Since

\int_{Y}\Phi_{\Omega}\,d\nu=0,\quad V_{\Omega}^{-1}\int_{Y}\Phi_{\Omega}^{2}\,d\nu\to\sigma^{2}>0,\quad\textup{Cum}_{r}\left(\Phi_{\Omega}\right)=o(V_{\Omega}^{r/2})\quad\hbox{for $r\geq 3$},

it follows that the random variable
V_{\Omega}^{-1/2}\Phi_{\Omega}
converges as
T\to\infty
in distribution to the Normal Law
with variance
\sigma^{2}
. Furthermore, since

\big\|\Psi_{\Omega}-\Phi_{\Omega}\big\|_{{L}^{1}(\nu)}=o\left(V_{\Omega}^{1/2}\right),

the random variable
V_{\Omega}^{-1/2}\Psi_{\Omega}
has the same limiting distribution.
In view of (
7.12
), this implies the theorem.

It remains to verify that the assumptions of these propositions can be simultaneously satisfied, namely, the conditions (
7.13
),
(
8.1
), (
8.4
), (
9.2
).
We take
L_{T}=V_{\Omega}^{1/2}
and
\varepsilon_{T}=(\ln T)^{-K_{0}}
with
K_{0}>0
.
Then we are required to have

\displaystyle(\ln T)^{-(K_{0}-K_{1})}\leq a\leq b(\ln\ln T)^{-4-\varepsilon},\;\;b^{-1/3}(\ln T)^{\theta}\leq V_{\Omega}^{1/2},\;\;b\geq(\ln T)^{-2\min(1,K_{1}-1)+\theta}

for some
0<K_{1}<K_{0}
and
\theta,\varepsilon>0
.
We recall (cf. (
3.1
)) that
V_{\Omega}\sim(\ln T)^{2}b
as
T\to\infty
, so that
these conditions can be arranged when

(\ln T)^{-K}\leq a\leq b(\ln\ln T)^{-4-\varepsilon}\quad\textrm{and}\quad b\geq(\ln T)^{-6/5+\theta}

for some
K,\varepsilon,\theta>0
. This gives the main theorem under the additional assumption that

a\geq(\ln T)^{-K}\quad\hbox{for some $K>0$.}

Finally, we show that solutions with small products give negligible contributions.
For this purpose, we use the following lemma:

Lemma 10.1
.

Let

\Theta_{T}:=\left\{(x_{1},x_{2},y)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:\;|x_{1}x_{2}|\,y\leq(\ln T)^{-K},\;|x_{1}|,\,|x_{2}|\leq c,\;1\leq y<T\right\}.

Then when
K>2
, for a.e.
\underline{x}\in[0,1)^{2}
,

\left|\bigcup_{T>1}\Theta_{T}\cap\Lambda_{\underline{x}}\right|<\infty.

Proof.

The set in question consists of vectors
(qx_{1}-p_{1},qx_{2}-p_{2},q)
with
(p_{1},p_{2},q)\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}
such that

|qx_{1}-p_{1}|\cdot|qx_{2}-p_{2}|\cdot q\leq(\ln T)^{-K},\;\;\;|qx_{1}-p_{1}|,|qx_{2}-p_{2}|\leq c,\;\;\;1\leq q<T

for some
T>1
. If this set is infinite, it follows the inequalities

|qx_{1}-p_{1}|\cdot|qx_{2}-p_{2}|\leq(\ln q)^{-K}q^{-1},\;\;\;|qx_{1}-p_{1}|,|qx_{2}-p_{2}|\leq c

have integral solutions for infinitely many
q\in\mathbb{N}
.
In other words,

\left|\bigcup_{T\geq 1}\Theta_{T}\cap\Lambda_{\underline{x}}\right|=\infty\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\underline{x}\in\limsup A_{q},

where

A_{q}:=\left\{\underline{x}\in[0,1)^{2}:\,\exists p_{1},p_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}:(qx_{1}-p_{1},qx_{2}-p_{2})\in\Upsilon\left((\ln q)^{-K}q^{-1}\right)\right\}

and

\Upsilon(\varepsilon):=\left\{(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:\;|x_{1}x_{2}|\leq\varepsilon,\;|x_{1}|,|x_{2}|\leq c\right\}.

One checks that

\Vol(\Upsilon(\varepsilon))\ll_{c}\varepsilon\ln(1/\varepsilon).

for sufficiently small
\varepsilon
, and consequently

\Vol(A_{q})\ll_{c}(\ln q)^{1-K}q^{-1}.

Hence, the claim follows from the Borel–Cantelli lemma.
∎

Now we are ready to complete the proof of the main theorem.
Let us write

\Omega=\Omega^{\prime}\sqcup\Omega^{\prime\prime},

where the set

\Omega^{\prime}
consists of the points of
\Omega
satisfying
additionally
|x_{1}x_{2}|\,y>(\ln T)^{-K}
with fixed sufficiently large
K
,
and
\Omega^{\prime\prime}
is its complement. Then

|\Omega\cap\Lambda_{\underline{x}}|-\Vol(\Omega)=\Big(|\Omega^{\prime}\cap\Lambda_{\underline{x}}|-\Vol(\Omega^{\prime})\Big)+\Big(|\Omega^{\prime\prime}\cap\Lambda_{\underline{x}}|-\Vol(\Omega^{\prime\prime})\Big).

We have already established that

\Vol(\Omega^{\prime})^{-1/2}\big(|\Omega^{\prime}\cap\Lambda_{\underline{x}}|-\Vol(\Omega^{\prime})\big)
,
\underline{x}\in[0,1)^{2}
,
converges in distribution to the Normal Law,
because
\Omega^{\prime}
can be viewed as the set (
1.3
) with
the lower bound
a^{\prime}:=\max(a,(\ln T)^{-K})
.
Further, we note that

\Vol(\Omega^{\prime\prime})\leq\int_{1}^{T}\Vol\left(\Upsilon\big(y^{-1}(\ln T)^{-K}\big)\right)\,dy\ll_{K}(\ln T)^{1-K}\ln\ln T=o\big(\Vol(\Omega)^{1/2}\big)

as
T\to\infty
. Therefore,

\Vol(\Omega)^{-1/2}\big(|\Omega^{\prime}\cap\Lambda_{\underline{x}}|-\Vol(\Omega^{\prime})\big)

also converges in distribution to the same limit.
Further, it follows from Lemma
10.1
that

\Vol(\Omega)^{-1/2}\big(|\Omega^{\prime\prime}\cap\Lambda_{\underline{x}}|-\Vol(\Omega^{\prime\prime})\big)\to 0
for a.e.
\underline{x}\in[0,1)^{2}
,
and consequently this quantity also converges to zero in measure.
Therefore,
\Vol(\Omega)^{-1/2}\big(|\Omega\cap\Lambda_{\underline{x}}|-\Vol(\Omega)\big)

converges to the same Normal Law.

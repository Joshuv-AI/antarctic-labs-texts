Strong time regularity and decay of 𝐋 ∞ \mathbf{L}^{\infty} solutions to 2 × 2 2\times 2 systems of conservation laws
Luca Talamini

Abstract
. We consider 𝐋 ∞ \mathbf{L}^{\infty} solutions to 2 × 2 2\times 2 systems of conservation laws. For genuinely nonlinear systems we prove that finite entropy solutions (in particular entropy solutions, if a uniformly convex entropy exists) belong to C 0 ​ ( ℝ + , 𝐋 l ​ o ​ c 1 ​ ( ℝ ) ) C^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{+};\mathbf{L}^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R})) . 1.
Introduction

In this paper we study
\mathbf{L}^{\infty}
entropy solutions
\boldsymbol{u}:\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
to
2\times 2
systems of conservation laws in one space dimension

(1.1)

\partial_{t}\,\boldsymbol{u}(t,x)+\partial_{x}\,f(\boldsymbol{u}(t,x))=0,\qquad\text{in $\mathscr{D}^{\prime}_{t,x}$}\qquad\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathcal{U}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2},\quad f:\mathcal{U}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}.

Entropy solutions are weak solutions to (
1.1
) that in addition satisfy the entropy inequality

(1.2)

\partial_{t}\eta(\boldsymbol{u})+\partial_{x}q(\boldsymbol{u})\leq 0\qquad\text{in $\mathscr{D}^{\prime}_{t,x}$}

for every entropy-entropy flux pair
(\eta(\boldsymbol{u}),q(\boldsymbol{u}))\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}
such that

\nabla q(\boldsymbol{u})=\nabla\eta(\boldsymbol{u})Df(\boldsymbol{u}),\qquad\text{$\eta$ convex.}

It is by now a classical result that, under
a priori

\mathbf{L}^{\infty}
and energy bounds on a sequence of approximate solutions
\boldsymbol{u}^{\varepsilon}
, the method of compensated compactness
[
DiP83a
,
Tar79
]
allows to prove the strong compactness of the family
\{\boldsymbol{u}^{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
, under standard nonlinearity assumptions on the flux
f
, known as
genuine nonlinearity
(see Definition
2.5
), thus proving existence of solutions in some important cases (see e.g.
[
LPT96
]
). A general existence result is lacking, and the assumptions allowing to apply the method must be checked each time. For an account on this topic we refer to
[
Ser00
, Chapter 9]
,
[
Daf16
]
.

A long standing problem is the one of understanding the regularity and other qualitative properties of solutions constructed with the compensated compactness method (see e.g. the open questions in
[
Per02
, Section 1.13]
). It is known that merely continuous weak solutions to (
1.1
) can be quite wild, in particular they are non-unique
[
CVY24
]
; however entropic solutions may still exhibit better regularity or stability properties. It is conjectured that uniqueness holds at least in some intermediate spaces (see
[
ABB23
,
BMG25
,
ABM25
]
for some recent results), in between small
BV
(where uniqueness is known
[
BB05
,
BDL23
,
CKV22
]
) and small
\mathbf{L}^{\infty}
(where existence of solutions with good decay/regularity properties is known
[
GL70
,
BCM10
,
Gla24
]
, but nothing is known about uniqueness). A few special exceptions for which some results about the structure and regularity of large solutions are available are systems of Temple class
[
AC05
]
(systems for which rarefaction and shock curves coincide) and
2\times 2
systems with a specific structure on the eigenvalues (i.e. the eigenvalue
\lambda_{i}
depends only on the
i
-th Riemann invariant), for which a notable example is the system of isentropic gas dynamics with
\gamma=3
(
[
LPT94b
]
,
[
Gol23
]
,
[
Tal24
]
,
[
Vas99
]
).

For general genuinely nonlinear systems, in
[
AMT25
]
it is shown that finite entropy solutions to (
1.1
) satisfy a kinetic equation (based on a family of discontinuous entropies constructed in
[
PT00
]
,
[
Tza03
]
), and a Liouville-type result for isentropic solutions is obtained. In particular this implies the existence of a set
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{J}}
(the candidate jump set) with
\mathrm{dim}_{\mathscr{H}}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{J}})\leq 1
, such that every point outside of
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{J}}
is of vanishing mean oscillation. Let us point out that the
1
-rectifiability of
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{J}}
is an open problem at the time of writing.

The aim of this paper is to further exploit the kinetic formulation of
[
AMT25
]
and prove some qualitative properties of solutions.

1.1.
Strong Time regularity

Our first and main contribution is to show that entropy solutions (or more generally finite entropy solutions, see Proposition
2.3
) are strongly time continuous.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\boldsymbol{u}:[0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathcal{U}
be a finite entropy solution to (
1.1
) (see Definition
2.2
). Assume that the system is strictly hyperbolic and genuinely nonlinear. Then
\boldsymbol{u}\in C^{0}([0,+\infty),\mathbf{L}^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}))
.

By Proposition
2.3
, if there exists a uniformly convex entropy
E:\mathcal{U}\to\mathbb{R}
, entropy solutions are in particular also finite entropy solutions, so that the result applies.
To the best of our knowledge, the only available result concerning time regularity for
2\times 2
systems of conservation laws in the
\mathbf{L}^{\infty}

setting is due to
[
Vas99
]
. There, the author considers the isentropic Euler system with
\gamma=3
, and establishes time regularity by leveraging the specific structure of the system through novel blow-up techniques. We point out that Theorem
1.1
also applies to entropy solutions of the isentropic Euler system for a general exponent
\gamma
, provided that the solutions remain uniformly away from vacuum (i.e.,
\rho>c>0
) (however this assumption was not needed in
[
Vas99
]
). This condition ensures the uniform validity of the hyperbolicity assumption (
2.1
), which is used throughout the paper.

Remark 1.2
.

The existence of a uniformly convex entropy is a standard assumption which is satisfied in all physical cases. Moreover, under mild conditions on the Riemann invariants (see
[
Daf16
, Section 12.2]
), the Lax entropies, which are entropies of the following form:

\eta^{1}_{k}(w,z):=e^{kw}\left[\phi(w,z)+\frac{1}{k}\chi(w,z)+\mathcal{O}(1/k^{2})\right]

\eta^{2}_{k}(w,z):=e^{kz}\left[\alpha(w,z)+\frac{1}{k}\beta(w,z)+\mathcal{O}(1/k^{2})\right]

are uniformly convex for big enough
k
.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
is based on some Lagrangian techniques, first introduced in the scalar case
[
BBM17
,
Mar19
]
, and adapted to systems of conservation laws in
[
AMT25
]
, and on blow up techniques, introduced for the first time in
[
Vas99
]
(see also
[
DOW03
]
)

1.2.
A decay property for vanishing viscosity solutions

Our second result, which is of a different nature but whose proof is still related to the kinetic formulation, is the following dispersive-type estimate.

Theorem 1.3
.

Assume that the system (
1.1
) is genuinely nonlinear, strictly hyperbolic, and that there exists a uniformly convex entropy
E:\mathcal{U}\to\mathbb{R}
. Let
\boldsymbol{u}
be a vanishing viscosity solution to (
1.1
) (see Definition
2.4
). Assume that for some
\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}\in\mathcal{U}
we have
\boldsymbol{u}(0,x)-\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}\in\mathbf{L}^{1}
. Then there is a constant
C
depending only on
f
and
E
such that

(1.3)

\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\boldsymbol{u}(t,x)-\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}|^{4}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\leq C\Big(\|\boldsymbol{u}(0,\cdot)-\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{1}}+\|\boldsymbol{u}(0,\cdot)-\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{1}}^{2}\Big).

The result is restricted to vanishing viscosity solutions, as these exhibit additional structure in the associated dissipation measures—a property that is not a priori guaranteed for general entropy solutions (see Theorem
4.1
).

Similar dispersive estimates have been derived for the Euler equations of gas dynamics in
[
LPT94b
]
, where a kinetic formulation was introduced and used to establish decay estimate for weak entropy solutions.
Other estimates of dispersive nature, analogous in spirit to the ones we consider here, were established for the multidimensional Burgers equation by employing the framework of compensated integrability, as developed in
[
Ser18a
,
Ser19
]
. There, it was shown that initial data belonging to
\mathbf{L}^{1}
are instantaneously regularized to higher integrability spaces
\mathbf{L}^{p}
for all positive times. Here that framework cannot be directly applied because the kinetic formulation available in the setting of
2\times 2
systems
[
AMT25
]
does not involve positive kinetic functions, i.e. they can change sign. In
[
SS19
]
the authors managed to obtain an even stronger decay, deriving
\mathbf{L}^{\infty}
estimates. We remark that a stronger decay is to be expected for systems of
2\times 2
conservation laws, although establishing it appears to be out of reach with the methods introduced in this paper. In particular, it remains an open problem to establish a stronger
\mathbf{L}^{\infty}
decay, analogously to what happens in the small total variation setting.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we outline the general setting and we recall some preliminary results. In Section
3
we prove Theorem
1.1
. Finally, in Section
4
we prove Theorem
1.3
.

2.
Preliminary Results

2.1.
General Setting

Throughout the paper, we assume that the system (
1.1
) is strictly hyperbolic in
\mathcal{U}
, so that the matrix
\mathrm{D}f
has distinct real eigenvalues

(2.1)

\lambda_{1}(\boldsymbol{u})<\lambda_{2}(\boldsymbol{u})\qquad\forall\;\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathcal{U}

with corresponding eigenvectors
r_{1}(\boldsymbol{u}),r_{2}(\boldsymbol{u})
.
We also let
\ell_{1},\ell_{2}
be the corresponding left eigenvectors, normalized so that

\ell_{i}(\boldsymbol{u})\cdot r_{i}(\boldsymbol{u})=\delta_{i,j}\qquad\forall\;\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathcal{U}.

Being a system of two equations, (
1.1
) admits a coordinate system of
Riemann invariants

\phi_{1},\phi_{2}
. The latter are smooth invertible functions
\phi=(\phi_{1},\phi_{2}):\mathcal{U}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
defined by

(2.2)

\nabla\phi_{1}(\boldsymbol{u})=\ell_{1}(\boldsymbol{u}),\qquad\nabla\phi_{2}(\boldsymbol{u})=\ell_{2}(\boldsymbol{u})\qquad\forall\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathcal{U}.

We let
\mathcal{W}\doteq(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})(\mathcal{U})\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Since we consider bounded solutions, here
\mathcal{U}
is assumed to be a rectangle in Riemann invariants, i.e. there holds

\mathcal{W}=[\underaccent{\bar}{w},\bar{w}]\times[\underaccent{\bar}{z},\bar{z}].

A function
g
can be expressed in terms of the state vector
\boldsymbol{u}
or in terms of the Riemann invariants
(w,z)
, according to

g(\boldsymbol{u})=\hat{g}(\phi^{-1}(\boldsymbol{u})),\qquad\partial_{w}\hat{g}=r_{1}\cdot\nabla g,\quad\partial_{z}\hat{g}=r_{2}\cdot\nabla g.

From now on, relying on a common abuse of notation, we will use the same symbol
g
for both expressions.
A pair of Lipschitz functions
\eta,q:\mathcal{U}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
is called an
entropy-entropy flux pair
for (
1.1
) if

(2.3)

\nabla\eta(\boldsymbol{u})\cdot\mathrm{D}f(\boldsymbol{u})=\nabla q(\boldsymbol{u})\qquad\text{for almost every $\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathcal{U}$}.

Admissible (entropy) solutions of (
1.1
) are the ones that dissipate the family of
convex
entropies.

Definition 2.1
.

A function
\boldsymbol{u}:\Omega\to\mathcal{U}
is called an
entropy weak solution
of (
1.1
) if it satisfies

(2.4)

\mu_{\eta}:=\partial_{t}\eta(\boldsymbol{u})+\partial_{x}q(\boldsymbol{u})\leq 0\qquad\text{in}\;\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(\Omega)

for all entropy-entropy flux pairs
(\eta,q)
with
\eta
a convex function.

Given an open set
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we denote by
\mathscr{M}(A)
the space of Radon measures in
A
. A more general notion of solution is the following.

Definition 2.2
.

We say that a weak solution of (
1.1
)
\boldsymbol{u}
is a
finite entropy solution
to (
1.1
) if
\mu_{\eta}\in\mathscr{M}(\Omega)
for every entropy-entropy flux pair
(\eta,q)
of class
C^{2}
. Moreover,
\boldsymbol{u}
is
isentropic
if
\mu_{\eta}=0
for every
(\eta,q)
in the same class.

Notice that here a sign on
\mu_{\eta}
is not imposed, and the convexity of
\eta
is not required. It is a simple but crucial observation that, in the presence of a uniformly convex entropy
E:\mathcal{U}\to\mathbb{R}
, entropy solutions are also finite entropy solutions.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\boldsymbol{u}:\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathcal{U}
be an entropy solution, and assume that there exists a uniformly convex entropy
E:\mathcal{U}\to\mathbb{R}
. Then
\boldsymbol{u}
is also a finite entropy solution.

Proof.

Let
\eta
be any
C^{2}
entropy, and let
\kappa>0
big enough such that
\eta+\kappa E
is convex. Then we have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}E(\boldsymbol{u})+\partial_{x}G(\boldsymbol{u})=\mu_{E},

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\eta+\kappa E)(\boldsymbol{u})+\partial_{x}(q+\kappa G)(\boldsymbol{u})=\mu_{\eta+\kappa E}

where
G
is the entropy flux of
E
, and
\mu_{E}
,
\mu_{\eta+\kappa E}
are locally finite negative measures, because
\boldsymbol{u}
is entropic. Then

\partial_{t}\eta(\boldsymbol{u})+\partial_{x}q(\boldsymbol{u})=\mu_{\eta+\kappa E}-\kappa\mu_{E}

which proves the result.
∎

As it is well known, the viscous approximations to (
1.1
)

(2.5)

\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{u}^{\epsilon}(t,x)+\partial_{x}f(\boldsymbol{u}^{\epsilon}(t,x))=\epsilon\partial^{2}_{xx}\boldsymbol{u}^{\epsilon},\qquad(t,x)\in\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R},\qquad\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathcal{U}

produce entropy admissible weak solutions of (
1.1
) in the limit
\epsilon\to 0^{+}
.

Definition 2.4
.

We say that
\boldsymbol{u}:\Omega\to\mathcal{U}
is a
vanishing viscosity solution
to (
1.1
) if there exists a sequence
\epsilon_{i}\to 0^{+}
and solutions
\boldsymbol{u}^{\epsilon_{i}}:\Omega\to\mathcal{U}
to (
2.5
) such that
{\boldsymbol{u}}^{\epsilon_{i}}\to\boldsymbol{u}
in
\mathbf{L}^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
.

We conclude by recalling the definition of
genuine nonlinearity
.

Definition 2.5
.

We say that the eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}
(
\lambda_{2}
) is genuinely nonlinear (GNL) if there is
\bar{c}>0
such that

\partial_{w}\lambda_{1}(\boldsymbol{u})\geq\bar{c}\quad\Big(\partial_{z}\lambda_{2}(\boldsymbol{u})\geq\bar{c}\Big)\qquad\forall\;\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathcal{U}.

2.2.
Kinetic formulation

We recall the construction in
[
AMT25
]
that leads to a kinetic formulation. It is based on the singular entropies of
[
PT00
]
,
[
Tza03
]
.
Let
g,h
be

\displaystyle g(w,z)

\displaystyle=\exp\left[\int_{{\underaccent{\bar}{z}}}^{z}-\frac{\lambda_{1z}(w,y)}{\lambda_{1}(w,y)-\lambda_{2}(w,y)}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\right]

\displaystyle h(w,z)

\displaystyle=\exp\left[\int_{{\underaccent{\bar}{w}}}^{w}\frac{\lambda_{2w}(y,z)}{\lambda_{1}(y,z)-\lambda_{2}(y,z)}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\right].

Definition 2.6
.

We denote by
\boldsymbol{\Theta}[\xi]
the entropy constructed as the unique solution to the Goursat-boundary value problem

\begin{cases}\boldsymbol{\Theta}_{wz}=\frac{g_{z}}{g}\boldsymbol{\Theta}_{w}+\frac{h_{w}}{h}\boldsymbol{\Theta}_{z},&\text{in}\;\mathcal{W}\\
\\
\boldsymbol{\Theta}(w,{\underaccent{\bar}{z}})=1,&\forall\;w\in[{\underaccent{\bar}{w}},\bar{w}]\\
\\
\boldsymbol{\Theta}(\xi,z)=g(\xi,z)&\forall\;z\in[{\underaccent{\bar}{z}},\bar{z}].\end{cases}

We call
\boldsymbol{\Xi}
the entropy flux of
\boldsymbol{\Theta}
.
Up to an additive constant in the entropy flux we can assume that (see
[
AMT25
]
or
[
PT00
]
)

(2.6)

\boldsymbol{\Xi}(\xi,z)=\lambda_{1}(\xi,z)\boldsymbol{\Theta}(\xi,z)\qquad\forall\;z\in[{\underaccent{\bar}{z}},\bar{z}].

We define distributional entropies (see
[
PT00
]
)

(2.7)

\boldsymbol{\chi}[\xi]\doteq\boldsymbol{\Theta}[\xi]\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\{w\geq\xi\}},\qquad\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\chi}}[\xi]\doteq\boldsymbol{\Theta}[\xi]\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\{w\leq\xi\}}

and entropy fluxes

(2.8)

\boldsymbol{\psi}[\xi]\doteq\boldsymbol{\Xi}[\xi]\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\{w\geq\xi\}}\qquad\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\psi}}[\xi]\doteq\boldsymbol{\Xi}[\xi]\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\{w\leq\xi\}}.

For a proof of the following Proposition see e.g.
[
AMT25
]
.

Proposition 2.7
.

There exists positive
\bar{r},c>0
such that, for every
\xi,w\in[\underaccent{\bar}{w},\bar{w}]
and
z\in[\underaccent{\bar}{z},\bar{z}]
such that
\xi\leq w\leq\xi+\bar{r}
, the following holds:

(1)

Local strict positivity of the entropies:

\boldsymbol{\chi}[\xi](w,z)\geq c>0

(2)

If
\lambda_{1}
is genuinely nonlinear, then we have the monotonicity of the kinetic speed:

\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\xi}\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{1}[\xi](w,z)\geq c>0

where

(2.9)

\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{1}[\xi](w,z)\doteq\frac{\boldsymbol{\psi}[\xi](w,z)}{\boldsymbol{\chi}[\xi](w,z)}\qquad\forall\;\xi\leq w\leq\xi+\bar{r}.

A completely symmetric statement holds for the entropies
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\chi}}
.

A symmetric construction can be made for the entropies that can be cut along the second Riemann invariant; for these entropies, for
\zeta\in[{\underaccent{\bar}{z}},\bar{z}]
, we let
\boldsymbol{\upsilon}[\zeta](w,z)
be entropy corresponding to
\boldsymbol{\chi}[\xi](w,z)
, and
\boldsymbol{\varphi}[\zeta](w,z)
for the respective entropy flux, corresponding to
\boldsymbol{\psi}[\xi](w,z)
.

In the following, given a function
\boldsymbol{u}:\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathcal{U}
, we define

(2.10)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\chi}_{\boldsymbol{u}}(t,x,\xi)\doteq\boldsymbol{\chi}[\xi](\boldsymbol{\boldsymbol{u}}(t,x))\qquad\forall\;(t,x,\xi)\in\Omega\times(\underaccent{\bar}{w},\bar{w}),

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\upsilon}_{\boldsymbol{u}}(t,x,\zeta)\doteq\boldsymbol{\upsilon}[\zeta](\boldsymbol{\boldsymbol{u}}(t,x))\qquad\forall\;(t,x,\zeta)\in\Omega\times(\underaccent{\bar}{z},\bar{z})

(2.11)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\psi}_{\boldsymbol{u}}(t,x,\xi)\doteq\boldsymbol{\psi}[\xi](\boldsymbol{\boldsymbol{u}}(t,x))\qquad\forall\;(t,x,\xi)\in\Omega\times(\underaccent{\bar}{w},\bar{w}),

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{\boldsymbol{u}}(t,x,\zeta)\doteq\boldsymbol{\varphi}[\zeta](\boldsymbol{\boldsymbol{u}}(t,x))\qquad\forall\;(t,x,\zeta)\in\Omega\times(\underaccent{\bar}{z},\bar{z}).

Notice that the support of
\boldsymbol{\chi}_{\boldsymbol{u}},\boldsymbol{\psi}_{\boldsymbol{u}}
is contained in the hypograph of the first Riemann invariant
w=\phi_{1}(\boldsymbol{u})
, while the support of
\boldsymbol{\upsilon}_{\boldsymbol{u}}
,
\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{\boldsymbol{u}}
is contained in the hypograph of the second Riemann invariant
z=\phi_{2}(\boldsymbol{u})
.

Proposition 2.8

(
[
AMT25
]
)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be an open set and let
\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathbf{L}^{\infty}(\Omega,\,\mathcal{U})
. Then
\boldsymbol{u}
is a finite entropy solution to (

) if and only if there are locally finite measures
\mu_{1},\mu_{0}\in\mathscr{M}(\Omega\times(\underaccent{\bar}{w},\bar{w}))

(2.12)

\;\;\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\chi}_{\boldsymbol{u}}(t,x,\xi)+\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{\psi}_{\boldsymbol{u}}(t,x,\xi)=\partial_{\xi}\mu_{1}+\mu_{0}\qquad\text{in $\mathscr{D}^{\prime}$}\big(\Omega\times({\underaccent{\bar}{w}},\bar{w})\big)

(2.15)

\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\upsilon}_{\boldsymbol{u}}(t,x,\zeta)+\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{\boldsymbol{u}}(t,x,\zeta)=0\qquad\text{in $\mathscr{D}^{\prime}$}\big(\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\big)

.

Defining

(2.16)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\chi}}_{\boldsymbol{u}}(t,x,\xi)\doteq\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\chi}}[\xi](\boldsymbol{u}(t,x)),\quad\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\psi}}_{\boldsymbol{u}}(t,x,\xi)\doteq\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\psi}}[\zeta](\boldsymbol{u}(t,x)),\quad\forall\;(t,x,\xi)\in\Omega\times(\underaccent{\bar}{w},\bar{w})

3.
Strong Time Regularity

In this section we prove Theorem
1.1
. We need some preliminary tools from
[
AMT25
]
, which we recall in the following Subsection.

3.1.
Lagrangian tools

Let
\boldsymbol{u}
be an isentropic solution to (
1.1
) defined in
\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}
and let
w=\phi_{1}(\boldsymbol{u})
.
From now on, all the results will be stated for the first Riemann invariant
w=\phi_{1}(\boldsymbol{u})
, but it is clear that symmetric statements hold for the other
z=\phi_{2}(\boldsymbol{u})
.
Define
w_{\max}\doteq\esssup_{t,x}w
and
w_{\min}\doteq\essinf_{t,x}w.

Following
[
AMT25
, Section 4]
, for
r>0
sufficiently small we define

(3.1)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\chi}^{\max}(t,x,\xi)\doteq\boldsymbol{\chi}_{\boldsymbol{u}}(t,x,\xi)\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\{(t,x,\xi)\;|\;w_{\max}-r\leq\xi\leq w_{\max}\}}(t,x,\xi)\geq 0

(3.2)

Lemma 3.6
.

Assume that
\bar{\sigma}_{x}:(t_{1},t_{2})\subset\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is a Lipschitz curve and that for

-a.e.

\lim_{\delta\to 0}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{\bar{\sigma}_{x}(t)-\delta}^{\bar{\sigma}_{x}(t)+\delta}|w(t,x)-w(t,\bar{\sigma}_{x}(t))|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t=0.

In the scalar case
f(u)=u^{2}
, if a solution

is isentropic, then

must be an increasing function for all

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
\boldsymbol{u}:\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathcal{U}
be an isentropic solution and let

.
Then for every

(3.11)

\lim_{M\to-\infty}\Bigg(\esssup_{(t,x)\in(0,T)\times(-\infty,M)}w\Bigg)\;=\;w_{\min}

and

(3.12)

\lim_{M\to+\infty}\Bigg(\essinf_{(t,x)\in(0,T)\times(M,+\infty)}w\Bigg)\;=\;w_{\max}

Proof.

Let
\Gamma^{\min},\Gamma^{\max}
be the set of curves of Proposition
3.1
for the solution
\boldsymbol{u}
, and
\boldsymbol{\chi}^{\max}
,
\boldsymbol{\chi}^{\min}
defined as in (

). Take any
\bar{\sigma}\in\Gamma^{\min}
and define

\Omega_{\bar{\sigma}}:=\{(t,x)\;|\;x\leq\bar{\sigma}_{x}(t)\},\quad\boldsymbol{v}:=\boldsymbol{u}_{\mid\Omega_{\bar{\sigma}}}.

By contradiction, assume that

(3.13)

w^{\ell}_{\max}:=\esssup_{\Omega_{\bar{\sigma}}}\phi_{1}(\boldsymbol{v})>\bar{\sigma}_{\xi}.

Let

(3.14)

\boldsymbol{\chi}_{\ell}^{\max}(t,x,\xi):=\boldsymbol{\chi}_{\boldsymbol{v}}(t,x,\xi)\mathbf{1}_{\{(t,x,\xi)\;|\;w^{\ell}_{\max}-\widetilde{r}\leq\xi\leq w^{\ell}_{\max}\}}

where

, where we recall that

\boldsymbol{\chi}_{\boldsymbol{v}}=(\boldsymbol{\chi}_{\boldsymbol{u}})_{\mid\Omega_{\bar{\sigma}}}.

Since

is a Lebesgue point of

Next, we claim that

To prove the claim, we first notice that since

satisfies (
3.3
), then by definition (
3.14
) we have

(3.15)

\int\int_{\Omega_{\bar{\sigma}}}\varphi_{t}\boldsymbol{\chi}_{\ell}^{\max}+\varphi_{x}\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{1}[\xi](\boldsymbol{u})\boldsymbol{\chi}_{\ell}^{\max}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\xi=0\qquad\forall\;\varphi\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega_{\bar{\sigma}}),\quad\mathrm{supp}\,\varphi\subset\{t>0\}.

To conclude the proof we need to extend (
3.15

\varphi\in C^{1}_{c}({\overline{\Omega}}_{\bar{\sigma}})
with
\mathrm{supp}\,\varphi\subset\{t>0\}
. Let
\varphi
be such a function and consider a smooth cutoff

of the following form:

(3.16)

\begin{cases}h_{\varepsilon}(t,x)=1&\qquad\text{in $\{(t,x)\;|\;x\leq\bar{\sigma}_{x}(t)-2\varepsilon\}$}\\

\varphi^{\varepsilon}:=\varphi h_{\varepsilon}

(3.17)

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\Bigg[

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\bar{\sigma}}}\int h_{\varepsilon}\Big(\varphi_{t}\boldsymbol{\chi}_{\ell}^{\max}+\varphi_{x}\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{1}[\xi](\boldsymbol{u})\boldsymbol{\chi}_{\ell}^{\max}\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\xi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega_{\bar{\sigma}}}\int\varphi\Big(h^{\varepsilon}_{t}\boldsymbol{\chi}_{\ell}^{\max}+h^{\varepsilon}_{x}\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{1}[\xi](\boldsymbol{u})\boldsymbol{\chi}_{\ell}^{\max}\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\xi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\Bigg]=0

(3.18)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega_{\bar{\sigma}}}\int\varphi\Big(h^{\varepsilon}_{t}\boldsymbol{\chi}_{\ell}^{\max}+h^{\varepsilon}_{x}\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{1}[\xi](\boldsymbol{u})\boldsymbol{\chi}_{\ell}^{\max}\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\xi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\right|

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\bar{\sigma}_{x}(t)-2\varepsilon}^{\bar{\sigma}_{x}(t)-\varepsilon}\int\boldsymbol{\chi}_{\ell}^{\max}(t,x,\xi)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\xi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\bar{\sigma}_{x}(t)-2\varepsilon}^{\bar{\sigma}_{x}(t)-\varepsilon}|w(t,x)-w(t,\bar{\sigma}_{x}(t))|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t.

Finally, the second term above goes to zero thanks to Lemma
3.6
, and this proves our claim.
This proves our claim.

By Remark
3.3
, we have thus proved that for every

, there holds

\esssup\boldsymbol{u}_{\mid\{(t,x)\,|\;x\leq\bar{\sigma}_{x}(t)\}}\leq\bar{\sigma}_{\xi}

for some uniform positive constant

\esssup\boldsymbol{u}_{\mid\{(t,x)\,|\;x\leq\bar{\sigma}_{x}(T)-LT,\hskip 7.97224ptt\in[0,T]\}}\leq\bar{\sigma}_{\xi}

The proof is concluded by taking a sequence of curves

Lemma 3.9

is an isentropic solution in
\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}
satisfying (
3.23
) with
\boldsymbol{u}_{0}=\boldsymbol{u}(\bar{t},\bar{x})
, and therefore by Lemma
3.8
we obtain that
\boldsymbol{u}_{\infty}^{\bar{x}}\equiv\boldsymbol{u}(\bar{t},\bar{x})
is constant.
In particular, the limiting constant
\boldsymbol{u}^{\bar{x}}_{\infty}
does not depend on the subsequence, therefore we actually obtain the full convergence (
3.20
).

2.
It follows from (
3.20
) that

(3.26)

\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{-1}^{1}|\boldsymbol{u}(\bar{t}+sr,\bar{x}+yr)-\boldsymbol{u}(\bar{t},\bar{x})|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s=0\qquad\text{for a.e. $\bar{x}\in\mathbb{R}$}.

Let
\omega:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
the
\mathbf{L}^{1}
modulus of continuity of
\boldsymbol{u}(\bar{t},\cdot)
on the compact set
[-M-1,M+1]
, i.e.

(3.27)

\int_{-M-1}^{M+1}|\boldsymbol{u}(\bar{t},z)-\boldsymbol{u}(\bar{t},z+\delta)|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z\leq\omega(|\delta|)

By Fubini’s theorem we obtain

(3.28)

\displaystyle\int_{-M}^{M}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{-1}^{1}|\boldsymbol{u}(\bar{t}+sr,\bar{x}+yr)-\boldsymbol{u}(\bar{t},\bar{x})|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\bar{x}

\displaystyle=\int_{[-1,1]}\int_{-M}^{M}\int_{0}^{1}|\boldsymbol{u}(\bar{t}+sr,\bar{x}+yr)-\boldsymbol{u}(\bar{t},\bar{x})|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\bar{x}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\int_{[-1,1]}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{-M+yr}^{M+yr}|\boldsymbol{u}(\bar{t}+sr,z)-\boldsymbol{u}(\bar{t},z-yr)|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq\int_{[-1,1]}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{-M}^{M}|\boldsymbol{u}(\bar{t}+sr,z)-\boldsymbol{u}(\bar{t},z)|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y+4r\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{\infty}}+\int_{-1}^{1}\omega(|y|r)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=2\fint_{0}^{r}\int_{-M}^{M}|\boldsymbol{u}(\bar{t}+s,z)-\boldsymbol{u}(\bar{t},z)|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+o(1)

where

goes to zero as

From (

), (
3.28
) and the dominated convergence theorem, we deduce that

\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\fint_{0}^{r}\int_{-M}^{M}|\boldsymbol{u}(\bar{t}+s,z)-\boldsymbol{u}(\bar{t},z)|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s=\frac{1}{2}\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\int_{-M}^{M}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{-1}^{1}|\boldsymbol{u}(\bar{t}+sr,\bar{x}+yr)-\boldsymbol{u}(\bar{t},\bar{x})|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\bar{x}=0

∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Notice that the limit

(3.29)

\boldsymbol{u}(\bar{t},\cdot)=\text{weak${}^{*}$}-\lim_{t\to\bar{t}}\boldsymbol{u}(t,\cdot)

exists at every time
\bar{t}
, as well as the one sided limits

(3.30)

\text{weak${}^{*}$}-\lim_{t\to\bar{t}^{\pm}}\eta(\boldsymbol{u}(t,\cdot))

Therefore we only have to prove that the limit in (
3.29
) is strong, in which case the one sided limits (
3.30
) coincide.
If
\boldsymbol{u}(t,x)
is a finite entropy solution,
\boldsymbol{v}(t,x):=\boldsymbol{u}(T-t,-x)
is again a finite entropy solution in
[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}
. Therefore, up to this change of variable, to prove Theorem
1.1
it is enough to prove that
\boldsymbol{u}(t,\cdot)\longrightarrow\boldsymbol{u}(\bar{t},\cdot)
strongly for
t\to\bar{t}^{+}
.

1.
Thanks to Lemma
3.10
, for every
M>0
, we deduce that there exists a sequence
t_{j}\to\bar{t}^{+}
such that

\lim_{j\to+\infty}\int_{-M}^{M}|\boldsymbol{u}(t_{j},x)-\boldsymbol{u}(\bar{t},x)|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x=0.

Let
(\eta,q)
be any entropy-entropy flux pair.
Computing the limit along the specific sequence
t_{j}
we find that

(3.31)

\text{weak${}^{*}$}-\lim_{t\to\bar{t}^{\pm}}\eta(\boldsymbol{u}(t,\cdot))=\eta(\text{weak${}^{*}$}-\lim_{t\to\bar{t}}\boldsymbol{u}(t,\cdot))=\eta(\boldsymbol{u}(\bar{t},\cdot)).

If a uniformly convex entropy exists, then taking
\eta
as that entropy the proof can be immediately concluded by a standard argument, using that the weak limit commutates with a uniformly convex function. In the more general case, a bit more work is required, and is the content of the next step.

2.
Let now
\{t_{j}\}
be a sequence converging to
\bar{t}
for which there exists a Young measure
\alpha:\mathbb{R}\to\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{U})
,
x\mapsto\alpha_{x}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{U})
, such that

\boldsymbol{u}(t_{j},\cdot)\longrightarrow\alpha\qquad\text{in the sense of Young measures as $j\to+\infty$}.

We recall that the latter convergence means that for every continuous function
\psi:\mathcal{U}\to\mathbb{R}
there holds

\psi(\boldsymbol{u}(t_{j},\cdot))\rightharpoonup^{\ast}f_{\psi}(x):=\int\psi(\boldsymbol{v})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\alpha_{x}(\boldsymbol{v})\qquad\text{weakly${}^{\ast}$ in $\mathbf{L}^{\infty}$ as $j\to+\infty$.}

Since every bounded sequence in
\mathbf{L}^{\infty}
admits a subsequence converging in the sense of Young measures, it is clear that to conclude the proof it is enough to show that
\alpha
is a Dirac Young measure for every such sequence, i.e. that
\alpha_{x}=\delta_{\boldsymbol{v}(x)}
, for some
\boldsymbol{v}(x)\in\mathcal{U}
.

We first notice that (
3.31
), applied to the sequence
\{t_{j}\}
, means (taking
\psi=\eta
, where
\eta
is a smooth entropy):

(3.32)

\int_{\mathcal{U}}\eta(\boldsymbol{v})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\alpha_{x}(\boldsymbol{v})=\eta\left(\int_{\mathcal{U}}\boldsymbol{v}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\alpha_{x}(\boldsymbol{v})\right)\qquad\text{for a.e. $x\in\mathbb{R}$}

Let
x
be a point where (
3.32
) holds, and by contradiction assume that
\alpha_{x}
is not a Dirac mass. Then, without loss of generality, we can assume that
\alpha_{x}
is not supported in a single curve
S^{1}_{\xi}:=\{\phi_{1}(\boldsymbol{u})=\xi\}
(otherwise, it is not supported in a single curve of the second Riemann invariant, for which the same analysis can be applied).
Define the barycenter of
\alpha_{x}

\boldsymbol{b}_{x}:=\int_{\mathcal{U}}\boldsymbol{w}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\alpha_{x}(\boldsymbol{w}).

Then at least one of the following holds:

(3.33)

\alpha_{x}\big(\{\boldsymbol{w}\;|\;\phi_{1}(\boldsymbol{w})<\phi_{1}(\boldsymbol{b}_{x})\}\big)>0,\qquad\alpha_{x}\big(\{\boldsymbol{w}\;|\;\phi_{1}(\boldsymbol{w})>\phi_{1}(\boldsymbol{b}_{x})\}\big)>0.

Without loss of generality assume that the first one holds.
Let
\rho
be a smooth function
\rho:[\underaccent{\bar}{w},\bar{w}]\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
and define

\eta_{\rho}(\boldsymbol{w}):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\boldsymbol{\chi}[\xi](\boldsymbol{w})\rho(\xi)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\xi.

Since
\boldsymbol{\chi}[\xi]
are distributional entropies, it is immediate to check that
\eta_{\rho}
is a smooth entropy (see
[
PT00
]
,
[
AMT25
]
). Therefore by (
3.32
) there holds

(3.34)

\int_{\mathcal{U}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\boldsymbol{\chi}[\xi](\boldsymbol{w})\rho(\xi)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\xi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\alpha_{x}(\boldsymbol{w})=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\boldsymbol{\chi}[\xi]\left(\boldsymbol{b}_{x}\right)\rho(\xi)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\xi

But now let
\rho
be strictly positive in a thin strip

\{\boldsymbol{w}\in\mathcal{U}\;|\;\phi_{1}(\boldsymbol{w})\in(\xi_{\min}-\varepsilon,\xi_{\min}+\varepsilon)\}

and zero outside, where

\xi_{\min}:=\inf\{\phi_{1}(\boldsymbol{w})\;|\;\boldsymbol{w}\in\mathrm{supp}\;\alpha_{x}\},\qquad\varepsilon<\min\{\bar{r}/2,\,\mathrm{dist}(\xi_{\min},\phi_{1}(\boldsymbol{b}_{x})\}

and
\bar{r}
is as in Proposition
2.7
. Then we have

0<\int_{\mathcal{U}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\boldsymbol{\chi}[\xi](\boldsymbol{w})\rho(\xi)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\xi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\alpha_{x}(\boldsymbol{w})=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\boldsymbol{\chi}[\xi]\left(\boldsymbol{b}_{x}\right)\rho(\xi)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\xi=0

where in the first inequality we used that
\boldsymbol{\chi}[\xi](\boldsymbol{w})>c>0
if

\xi,\phi_{1}(\boldsymbol{w})\in(\xi_{\min}-\varepsilon,\xi_{\min}+\varepsilon),\qquad\xi\leq\phi_{1}(\boldsymbol{w})

by Proposition
2.7
, and in the last inequality we used
\boldsymbol{\chi}[\xi](\boldsymbol{b}_{x})=0
for all
\xi\in\mathrm{supp}\,\rho
.
∎

We conclude the section with a simple corollary of Theorem
1.1
.

Corollary 3.11
.

Let
\boldsymbol{u}
be a finite entropy solution to (
1.1
). Then for every entropy-entropy flux
\eta,q
, the dissipation measure
\mu_{\eta}
satisfies

|\mu_{\eta}|\big(\{t=\bar{t}\}\big)=0\qquad\forall\;\bar{t}>0.

In particular
\boldsymbol{\nu}
defined by (
2.19
) satisfies
\boldsymbol{\nu}(\{t=\bar{t}\})=0
for all
\bar{t}>0
.

Proof.

Let

h^{\varepsilon}(t):=\begin{cases}\varepsilon^{-1}(t-\bar{t}+\varepsilon)&\text{if $\bar{t}-\varepsilon<t<\bar{t}$},\\
\varepsilon^{-1}(\bar{t}+\varepsilon-t)&\text{if $\bar{t}<t<\bar{t}+\varepsilon$},\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

It is enough to show that for every smooth
\varphi\in C^{1}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
there holds

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\iint\varphi(x)h^{\varepsilon}(t)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{\eta}(t,x)=0.

By definition of
\mu_{\eta}
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\iint\varphi(x)h^{\varepsilon}(t)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{\eta}(t,x)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\iint\varphi(x)h^{\varepsilon}_{t}(t)\eta(\boldsymbol{u})+q(\boldsymbol{u})\varphi^{\prime}(x)h^{\varepsilon}(t)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\fint_{\bar{t}}^{\bar{t}+\varepsilon}\eta(\boldsymbol{u})\varphi(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x-\fint_{\bar{t}-\varepsilon}^{\bar{t}}\eta(\boldsymbol{u})\varphi(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\right|+C\varepsilon.

where
C
depends on
\varphi
and
\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{\infty}}
. The first term goes to zero as
\varepsilon\to 0
since
\eta
is Lipschitz and
\boldsymbol{u}(t,\cdot)\to\boldsymbol{u}(\bar{t},\cdot)
strongly in
\mathbf{L}^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R})
, and this concludes the proof.
∎

4.
Proof of the decay estimate

4.1.
Idea of the proof

The proof is based on the dispersive properties of the transport equation (
2.12
), (
2.13
). Let us sketch the proof of the decay for the first Riemann invariant
w
. To convey the main idea, assume for the moment that
\boldsymbol{u}
is a finite entropy solution and that (1) and (2) of Proposition
2.7
hold for every
\xi\in[\underaccent{\bar}{w},\bar{w}]
and
(w,z)\in[\underaccent{\bar}{w},\bar{w}]\times[\underaccent{\bar}{z},\bar{z}]
, and that
\mu_{0},\mu_{1}=0
in some time interval
[0,T]
, which means entropy has not been dissipated yet. Let
\boldsymbol{u}
be an entropy solution whose initial datum
\boldsymbol{u}(0,x)
is in
\mathbf{L}^{1}
with respect to a constant
\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}\in\mathcal{U}
, i.e.
\boldsymbol{u}(0,x)-\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}\in\mathbf{L}^{1}
. Assume also
w(t,x)>\widehat{w}
, where
\widehat{w}=\phi_{1}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}})
(the general case would follow by considering the positive and negative part separately).

Here (
2.12
) can be rewritten as a transport equation for
\boldsymbol{\chi}_{\boldsymbol{u}}
:

\;\;\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\chi}_{\boldsymbol{u}}(t,x,\xi)+\partial_{x}\Big(\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{1}[\xi](\boldsymbol{u}(t,x))\boldsymbol{\chi}_{\boldsymbol{u}}(t,x,\xi)\Big)=0\qquad\text{in $\mathscr{D}^{\prime}$}\big(\Omega\times({\underaccent{\bar}{w}},\bar{w})\big)

where
\boldsymbol{\chi}_{\boldsymbol{u}}\equiv\boldsymbol{\chi}_{\boldsymbol{u}}\mathbf{1}_{\xi>\widehat{w}}(\xi)

and where
\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{1}
is as in (
2.9
) where here we have considered
\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{1}[\xi](\boldsymbol{u})
as a function of
\boldsymbol{u}
or of the Riemann invariants up to the invertible change of variables
(\phi_{1},\phi_{2}):\mathcal{U}\to\mathcal{W}
. Now, by our simplifying assumptions, for fixed
\boldsymbol{u}
we have

\partial_{\xi}\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{1}[\xi](\boldsymbol{u})>c>0,\qquad\boldsymbol{\chi}_{\boldsymbol{u}}(t,x,\xi)>c>0.

This means that particles at height
\xi
will typically cross from the left particles at height
\xi^{\prime}<\xi
, and once they cross they will never meet again. This basic dispersive mechanism is formalized in the introduction of an interaction functional

\mathcal{I}(t):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\boldsymbol{\chi}_{u}(t,x,\xi)\boldsymbol{\chi}_{u}(t,x^{\prime},\xi^{\prime})\mathbf{1}_{\xi>\xi^{\prime}}(\xi,\xi^{\prime})\mathbf{1}_{x<x^{\prime}}(x,x^{\prime})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\xi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\xi^{\prime}.

In this simple case, at least formally, by differentiating we obtain

\dot{\mathcal{I}}(t):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathbf{1}_{\xi>\xi^{\prime}}(\xi,\xi^{\prime})\Big(\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{1}[\xi^{\prime}](\boldsymbol{u}(t,y))-\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{1}[\xi](\boldsymbol{u}(t,y))\Big)\,\boldsymbol{\chi}_{\boldsymbol{u}}(t,y,\xi)\boldsymbol{\chi}_{\boldsymbol{u}}(t,y,\xi^{\prime})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\xi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\xi^{\prime}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y

and therefore since

\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{1}[\xi^{\prime}](\boldsymbol{u}(t,y))<-c(\xi-\xi^{\prime})+\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{1}[\xi](\boldsymbol{u}(t,x))

we obtain

\dot{\mathcal{I}}(t)\leq-C\int_{\mathbb{R}}|w(t,x)-\widehat{w}|^{3}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x

where
\widehat{w}=\phi_{1}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}})
, and by integrating we obtain the desired estimate

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|w(t,x)-\widehat{w}|^{3}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\leq\frac{1}{C}\mathcal{I}(0).

Since
\mathcal{I}(0)<+\infty
, in this case this argument would also yield an upper bound on the time
T
, thus providing an upper bound for the time of shock formation.

In the general case, one has to take into account the fact that (1), (2) of Proposition
2.7
hold only locally in
\xi\sim\phi_{1}(\boldsymbol{u})(t,x)
. This will be achieved by localizing the kinetic equation in strips between two values
\mathbb{R}^{+}_{t}\times\mathbb{R}_{x}\times[\ell,\ell+r]
, where
0<r<\bar{r}
is sufficiently small (Lemma
4.2
), by proving a decay estimate in each strip (Proposition
4.3
) and finally concluding by an iterative procedure which allows to pass from one strip to the next one (Theorem
4.4
). Moreover, due to the presence of the term
\partial_{\xi}\mu_{1}
, one has to add a weight of the form
(\xi-\xi^{\prime})
into the definition of the functional, which in turn allows to control only the
\mathbf{L}^{4}
norm of the solution.

For vanishing viscosity solutions, additional structure on the dissipation measures can be obtain, see
[
AMT25
]
.

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}}\varphi_{\xi}\mathbf{1}_{\{\ell<\xi<\ell+r\}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{1}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}}\varphi\mathbf{1}_{\{\ell<\xi<\ell+r\}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{0}.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
\boldsymbol{u}:\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathcal{U}
be a bounded vanishing viscosity solution to (
1.1
) with an initial datum
\boldsymbol{u}_{0}
such that
\boldsymbol{u}_{0}-\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}\in\mathbf{L}^{1}
for some constant
\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}\in\mathcal{U}
, with
\widehat{w}=w(\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}})
. Assume that the eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}
is genuinely nonlinear.
Then, it holds

(4.33)

\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big(w(t,x)-\widehat{w}\big)^{4}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\leq\mathcal{O}(1)\cdot\Big(\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{0}}-\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{1}}+\big(\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{0}}-\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{1}}\big)^{2}\Big).

Proof.

We first prove that

(4.34)

\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big([w(t,x)-\widehat{w}]^{+}\big)^{4}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\leq\mathcal{O}(1)\cdot\Big(\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{0}}-\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{1}}+\big(\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{0}}-\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{1}}\big)^{2}\Big).

The estimate for the negative part is entirely symmetric and is accordingly omitted.
Define
k\in\mathbb{N},r>0
by

k\doteq\left\lceil\frac{\bar{w}-\widehat{w}}{\bar{r}}\right\rceil,\qquad r\doteq\frac{\bar{w}-\widehat{w}}{k}

where for
a\in\mathbb{R}
, the ceiling function
\lceil a\rceil
denotes the smallest integer greater then
a
.
Then
r\cdot k=\bar{w}-\widehat{w}
and
0<r\leq\bar{r}
. Define points
\ell_{0},\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{2(k-1)}
by

\ell_{0}\doteq\widehat{w},\qquad\ell_{i}\doteq\widehat{w}+i\cdot\frac{r}{2},\qquad i=1,\ldots,2(k-1).

Proposition
4.3
yields, by setting
\ell=\ell_{i}
,

(4.35)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big(g^{r,\ell_{i}}(t,x)\big)^{4}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{O}(1)\cdot\Big(\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{0}}-\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{1}}+\big(\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{0}}-\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{1}}\big)^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle+\mathcal{O}(1)\cdot\mathscr{L}^{2}\Big(\big\{(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}\;|\;w(t,x)\geq\ell_{i}+r\big\}\Big).

Notice that

(4.36)

w(t,x)\geq\ell_{i}+r\quad\Longrightarrow\quad g^{r,\ell_{i+1}}(t,x)\geq\frac{r}{2}.

Now, for every
i=0,\ldots,2(k-1)-1
, using (
4.36
) and Markov’s inequality, we estimate

(4.37)

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}^{2}\Big(\big\{(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}\;|\;w(t,x)\geq\ell_{i}+r\big\}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\mathscr{L}^{2}\Big(\big\{(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}\;|\;g^{r,\ell}\geq r/2\big\}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{16}{r^{4}}\cdot\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big(g^{r,\ell_{i+1}}(t,x)\big)^{4}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

Then, starting from
i=0
, and applying the estimates above repeatedly, we obtain

(4.38)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big(g^{r,\ell_{0}}(t,x)\big)^{4}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq 2(k-1)\cdot\mathcal{O}(1)\cdot\Big(\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{0}}-\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{1}}+\big(\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{0}}-\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{1}}\big)^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle+\mathcal{O}(1)\cdot\mathscr{L}^{2}\Big(\big\{(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}\;|\;w(t,x)\geq\ell_{2(k-1)}+r\big\}\Big).

But now notice that
\ell_{2(k-1)}+r=\bar{w}
, and by assumption
w(t,x)\leq\bar{w}
for a.e.
(t,x)
, therefore

\mathscr{L}^{2}\Big(\big\{(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}\;|\;w(t,x)\geq\ell_{2(k-1)}+r\big\}\Big)=0.

Moreover, we clearly have

\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big([w(t,x)-\widehat{w}]^{+}\big)^{4}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\leq\mathcal{O}(1)\cdot\frac{1}{r^{4}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big(g^{r,\ell_{0}}(t,x)\big)^{4}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t.

Therefore we conclude that

\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big([w(t,x)-\widehat{w}]^{+}\big)^{4}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\leq\mathcal{O}(1)\cdot\Big(\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{0}}-\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{1}}+\big(\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{0}}-\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{1}}\big)^{2}\Big)

and this proves the result.
∎

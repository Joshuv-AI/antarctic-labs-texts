Quaternionic families of Heegner points and p p -adic L L -functions
M. Longo, P. Magrone, E. R. Walchek

Abstract
. Following up a previous article of the authors which studies the interpolation of certain anticyclotomic p p -adic L L -functions associated to quaternionic modular forms in a Hida family, we extend the work of F. Castella on the interpolation and specialization of big Heegner points to the quaternionic setting. We prove an explicit reciprocity law relating the big p p -adic L L -function to the big Heegner points in this quaternionic setting.



August 10, 2026

1.
Introduction

This note arises with the aim of generalizing some of the results contained in the beautiful papers
[
Cas13
,
Cas20
]
by Castella to the setting of indefinite quaternion algebras. The main result of these two papers is the description of the specialization of big Heegner points introduced by Howard
[
How07
]

at certain arithmetic morphisms of a given Hida family in terms of generalized Heegner cycles. The approach of Castella, following previous works by Darmon–Rotger
[
DR17
]
, is to use density of weight
2
primes in Hida family, and the explicit description of big Heegner points as limits of Heegner points (which therefore, by construction, are directly related to the specializations at weight
2
of big Heegner points). The relation between higher weight specializations of big Heegner points and generalized Heegner cycles is obtained using families of
p
-adic
L
-functions as a bridge between the two, and the comparison is made possible by the density of weight
2
specializations.

In the quaternionic setting, Howard’s big Heegner points have been introduced by Fouquet
[
Fou13
]
(even over totally real number fields) and one of the authors of this paper in collaboration with Vigni
[
LV11
]
. The natural question is to what extent the techniques and approach in
[
Cas13
,
Cas20
,
DR17
]
can be adapted to the quaternionic setting. It should be noticed that the
p
-adic
L
-function
which appears as a bridge in the approach of
[
Cas20
]
is a
p
-adic variation of the
p
-adic
L
-function constructed in
[
CH18
]
following the approach of Brakočević
[
Bra11
]
(and Bertolini–Darmon–Prasanna
[
BDP13
]
). This approach makes use of Serre–Tate expansions of modular forms, and a part of it can be adapted to
the quaternionic setting: this portion of the work has been done in
[
LMW25
]
. The goal of this work is to accomplish the comparison between higher weight specialization of big Heegner points in the quaternionic setting and generalized Heegner cycles, using again the
p
-adic family of
p
-adic
L
-functions and weight
2
specializations as a bridge.

Our main result proves an equality (up to units,
cf.
Theorem
6.3
)

(1.1)

\mathscr{L}_{\mathbb{I},\boldsymbol{\xi}}^{\mathrm{an}}=\mathscr{L}_{\mathbb{I},\boldsymbol{\xi}}^{\mathrm{alg}}

of two
p
-adic
L
-functions attached to a quaternionic Hida family
\mathbb{I}
and a
p
-adic family of Hecke characters
\boldsymbol{\xi}
of a quadratic imaginary extension
K/\mathbb{Q}
.
Here
\mathbb{I}
is a primitive branch of a Hida family passing through a fixed
p
-stabilized newform
f\in S_{k_{0}}(\Gamma_{0}(Np))
of trivial character and even weight
k_{0}\equiv 2\mod{2(p-1)}
, and we assume throughout that the
restriction of the residual
p
-adic representation attached to
f
to a decomposition group at
p
is
p
-distinguished and irreducible; further the discriminant
-D_{K}
of
K
is assumed to be coprime with
Np
,
p
is assumed to be split in
K
, and
N
is assumed to factor as
N=N^{+}N^{-}
with
(N^{+},N^{-})=1
,
\ell\mid N^{+}
(respectively,
\ell\mid N^{-}
) if and only if
\ell
is split (respectively, inert) in
K
with

and

a product of an even number of distinct primes.
(The reader is referred to Section

for a more detailed discussion on the hypothesis of this paper and the terminology used in the previous lines.)
Equality (
1.1
) holds in

\widetilde{\mathbb{I}}[[\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\infty}]]
where
\widetilde{\mathbb{I}}=\mathbb{I}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\mathrm{unr}}
and

\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\infty}
is the Galois group of the union of the ring class fields of
K
of conductors
cp^{n}
for all integers

, where

is a fixed integer coprime with
D_{K}Np
,
which depends on
\boldsymbol{\xi}
.
Here
\mathscr{L}_{\mathbb{I},\boldsymbol{\xi}}^{\mathrm{an}}
is the family of
p
-adic
L
-functions constructed in
[
LMW25
]
and alluded to before, while

is the

-function which arises as the evaluation of a big Perrin-Riou logarithmic map at the quaternionic big Heegner point of tame conductor

As mentioned before, Equality (

) is obtained by an explicit comparison of the weight
2
specializations of the two sides.
This allows us to prove (

) that quaternionic big Heegner points are non-torsion, as predicted in
[

Since

specializes at higher weights to generalized Heegner cycles by a result of one of the authors of this paper in

However, at the moment our result is not completely satisfactory, for the following reason. The construction of

[

]
to obtain the aforementioned result).

2.1.
Quaternion algebras

Let

, defined over
\mathbb{Q}
, let

be the maximal order of

.
For primes

, we fix isomorphisms

Take the sequence of Eichler orders

has level
N^{+}p^{m}
and the image of

is equal to the order of upper triangular matrices modulo

coming from the splitting at

\mathbb{Q}
-algebras

\iota_{K}\colon K\hookrightarrow B

\iota_{K}(x)

From Section
4
on, to obtain a clear description of CM points, it will be convenient to choose the isomorphisms
i_{\ell}
and
i_{\infty}
(and, consequently, the Eichler orders
R_{m}
) as follows. Take the
\mathbb{Q}
-basis
\{1,\theta\}
of
K
, where
\theta=\frac{D^{\prime}+\sqrt{-D_{K}}}{2}
, being
D^{\prime}=D_{K}
if
2\nmid D_{K}
and
D^{\prime}=D_{K}/2
if
2\mid D_{K}
. For each place
v\mid N^{+}p\infty
of
\mathbb{Q}
, we may assume the isomorphism

to satisfy

i_{v}(\theta)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathrm{T}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\theta)&-\mathrm{N}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\theta)\\
1&0\end{array}\right).

Define the orthogonal idempotents
e
and
\bar{e}
in
K\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}K
to be:

e=\frac{1\otimes{\theta}-\theta\otimes 1}{(\theta-\bar{\theta})\otimes 1}\hskip 15.0pt\text{and}\hskip 15.0pt\bar{e}=\frac{\theta\otimes 1-1\otimes\bar{\theta}}{(\theta-\bar{\theta})\otimes 1}.

A simple computation shows that
e+\bar{e}=1
.
Let
\ell\mid N^{+}p
be a prime number. Then
\ell
splits in
K
as
\ell=\mathfrak{l}\bar{\mathfrak{l}}
, where

\mathfrak{l}
is the prime ideal corresponding to the chosen embedding
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\hookrightarrow\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
, so

splits as the direct sum
\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}e\oplus\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}\bar{e}
of two copies of
\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
. We have a canonical map

j_{\ell}\colon K\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}K\mbox{\;$\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow$\;}K\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}\mbox{\;$\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow$\;}B_{\ell}\overset{i_{\ell}}{\longrightarrow}\M_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})

and one may verify that
j_{\ell}(e)=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)
and
j_{\ell}(\bar{e})=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}0&0\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)
.

Denote by
i\colon K\hookrightarrow\M_{2}(\mathbb{Q})
the
\mathbb{Q}
-linear map which takes
\theta
to
\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}\mathrm{T}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\theta)&-\mathrm{N}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\theta)\\
1&0\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)
. Then we have

and we obtain a map

j\colon K\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}K\mbox{\;$\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow$\;}\M_{2}(K)

defined by

, and one verifies again that
j(e)=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)
and
j(\bar{e})=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}0&0\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)
.

2.2.
Shimura curves

Let
X_{m}
denote the Shimura curve of
V_{1}(N^{+}p^{m})
-level structure attached to the indefinite quaternion algebra
B
. By the action of
B^{\times}
via fractional linear transformations through the embedding

on
\mathcal{H}^{\pm}=\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}
, often identified with
\Hom_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbb{C},B_{\infty})
, we have, for any integer

,

(2.1)

X_{m}(\mathbb{C})=B^{\times}\backslash(\mathcal{H}^{\pm}\times\widehat{B}^{\times})/U_{m},

where

is the subgroup of
\widehat{R}_{m}^{\times}
consisting of elements whose

\ell^{\mathrm{ord}_{\ell}(N^{+}p^{m})}

\ell\nmid N^{-}
. We will write
[(x,g)]
for a point in

.
Define

and

\Ta_{p}=\mathop{\varprojlim}\limits\Ta_{p}(J_{m})

).
The
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module
\Ta_{p}(J_{m})
is equipped with a continuous action of the absolute Galois group of
\mathbb{Q}
and an action of Hecke operators

for primes

\ell\nmid Np
and
U_{\ell}
for primes
\ell\mid N^{+}p
attached to the indefinite quaternion algebra

(
[

, §6.2]
); denote

the Hecke algebra generated by these operators. Taking the projective limit of these Hecke algebras one defines a big Hecke algebra

acting on

; we may define a Hida ordinary idempotent

2.3.
Moduli spaces

The Shimura curve
X_{m}
has a model
\mathcal{X}_{m}
over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
which is constructed by means of naïve level

structures and Drinfeld level structures at

. For a subring
\mathcal{O}\subseteq\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
, and assuming that

-rational point of

is a quadruplet

We call

(A,\iota)
is a QM abelian surface,
\alpha
is a level
V_{1}(N^{+})

A
and
\beta
is a Drinfeld level

A

-algebra

,
satisfying a base-change compatibility condition. Equivalently, a modular form

can be viewed as a rule that assigns to each test object

over an
\mathcal{O}
-algebra

for any choice of section
\omega

If

and

is a field, one can check that this notion coincides with the notion of modular forms as global sections of

is the

where

2.4.
Hida families

Let

.

Assumption 2.1
.

We suppose that

•

is

;

•

The modular form

. Denote

(the

of
\nu
) and an integer

(the

of
\nu
); the pair

is the

of
\nu
. Let

be the finite extension of

whose valuation ring is

, which contains

. Then there exists a power series

is the maximum between

.

.
One may also write

and

\nu
.
We assume throughout that

Assumption 2.2
.

There is
\nu_{0}
of weight

and trivial wild character such that

Note that specializations

of
\mathbb{I}

3.
Galois representations

The goal of this section is to collect the results on families of Galois representations that will be used in this paper.

3.1.
Modular forms

We let
V_{{f}_{\nu}}
denote the
p
-adic Galois

-representation attached to
{f}_{\nu}
and determined by the property that the characteristic polynomial of the

3.2.
Critical characters

We now introduce critical characters. Let

and
\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}\colon G_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}

Factor

Let

denote the inclusions of group-like elements

We also need some variants of this character, that we now introduce. Write
\boldsymbol{\theta}\colon\mathbb{Q}^{\times}\backslash\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\times}\rightarrow\mathbb{I}^{\times}

Since

If

\mathbf{N}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}\colon\mathbb{A}_{K}^{\times}\rightarrow\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\times}
the adelic norm map, by
\mathbf{N}_{\mathbb{Q}}\colon\mathbb{A}^{\times}_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
the adelic absolute value and let

\mathbf{N}_{K}\colon\mathbb{A}^{\times}_{K}\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
denote the composition
\mathbf{N}_{K}=\mathbf{N}_{\mathbb{Q}}\circ\mathbf{N}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}
.
Define
the
character
{\boldsymbol{\chi}}\colon K^{\times}\backslash\widehat{K}^{\times}\rightarrow\mathbb{I}^{\times}

by
{\boldsymbol{\chi}}=\boldsymbol{\theta}\circ\mathbf{N}^{-1}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}.

For an arithmetic morphism
\nu
, define

\hat{\chi}_{\nu}=\nu\circ\boldsymbol{\chi}
.
Since
\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}\circ\mathrm{rec}_{\mathbb{Q}}
is the

-adic avatar of the adelic absolute value

On the other hand, since

The Learning Curve – A Neurosurgeon’s Memoir
Authors: Venkataraman Sundaram
Published: 2024

The book is written in the author’s own words, reflecting the metamorphosis of an ambitious student from the prestigious Armed Forces Medical College (AFMC) to an illustrious neurosurgeon of the Indian Army Medical Corps (AMC). He had been a bright performer during his undergraduate and postgraduate days through AFMC.

The narrative begins with his joining AFMC in 1974. He was a bright undergraduate student. After passing his MBBS, he was posted to Military Hospital (MH) Jabalpur in 1978 for internship and was finally commissioned in the AMC as a medical officer in 1979. His skill in surgery was spotted by HOD Surgery Department, who recommended him for training in postgraduation in surgery. The officer too decided to become a surgeon after he experienced seeing a case of traumatic cerebrospinal fluid leak while on duty as a duty medical officer.

While he was deployed in a field medical unit in Punjab, he was attracted by paratroopers’ duty after seeing them in an adjoining place. He offered to join a Para Regiment. He was posted as the Regimental Medical Officer of a Parachute Regiment in May 1982. Later, he had a chance to serve in the President’s Bodyguards (PBG). It is during this posting that he cleared PG entrance examination for postgraduation in surgery (Delhi University). He got training in surgery from Army Hospital Delhi Cantt. He passed MS (General Surgery) of Delhi University in 1986 while still being a medical officer of PBG.

He was posted to Leh for some time, during which he got experience of treating high-altitude casualties besides general surgical work. He realized the need of special skills to treat neurosurgical casualties of peace and war and decided to specialize in neurosurgery. He successfully cleared the entrance examination for admission to Postgraduate Institute of Medical Education and Research and was trained as a neurosurgeon from Jan 13, 1990 to Jan 12, 1992. Here he came under the influence of Prof. Vijay Kak, Chief of Neurosurgery, Prof. Virender Khosla, and Prof. Suresh Narain Mathurya and was mentored by all of them in different skills of neurosurgery.

He worked in various army hospitals as a neurosurgeon and had treated many craniospinal injuries during anti-militancy operations in Jammu and Kashmir. His meticulous record keeping and writing capabilities resulted in an earlier book “Cranio-spinal Missile injuries,” followed by the present one.

In July 2007, he was deputed with three others – another army neurosurgeon, a navy radiation oncologist, and a physicist – by the Director General (DG) AFMS to undergo Gamma Knife surgery training at University of Pittsburgh Medical Centre, Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania. The team underwent a 2-week orientation course in Gamma Knife neurosurgery under Dale Lunsford and Douglas Kondizolka. Upon return to India (Army hospital research and referral [AHRR], New Delhi), the group of four got together to kickstart GK neurosurgery at AHRR.

The present work is an excellent record to read by all young students of surgery aspiring to be neurosurgeons – in both civil and armed forces alike. His way of writing the professional aspects mixed with personal life accounts with accurate anecdotes is indeed commendable.

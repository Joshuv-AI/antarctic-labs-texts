The hyperbolic lattice counting problem in large dimensions
Christos Katsivelos

Abstract
. For n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 and Γ \Gamma a cocompact lattice acting on the hyperbolic space ℍ n \mathbb{H}^{n} , we investigate the average behaviour of the error term in the circle problem. First, we explore the local average of the error term over compact sets of Γ \ ℍ n {\Gamma\backslash{\mathbb{H}}}^{n} . Our upper bound depends on the quantum variance and the spectral exponential sums appearing in the study of the Prime geodesic theorem. We also prove Ω \Omega -results for the mean value and the second moment of the error term.

Primary 11F72; Secondary 37C35, 37D40

1.
Introduction

For
n\geq 2
let
\mathbb{H}^{n}
denote the
n
-dimensional hyperbolic space and let
\Gamma\subset{\hbox{SO}^{+}(1,n)}
be a discrete cofinite group of isometries acting on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. The quotient space
{\Gamma\backslash{\mathbb{H}}}^{n}
is a
n
-dimensional geometrically finite orbifold of constant curvature
K=-1
. Assume that
{\Gamma\backslash{\mathbb{H}}}^{n}
is compact. Then the Laplace-Beltrami operator
-\Delta=-\Delta_{\Gamma}
is a symmetric, non-negative operator on
{\Gamma\backslash{\mathbb{H}}}^{n}
and has discrete spectrum in
{\hbox{L}^{2}}({\Gamma\backslash{\mathbb{H}}}^{n})
:

\displaystyle 0=\lambda_{0}\leq\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq...\leq\lambda_{j}\leq...

with
\lambda_{j}\to\infty
.
We write
\phi_{j}
for the
{\hbox{L}^{2}}
-normalized eigenfunction of
-\Delta
corresponding to
\lambda_{j}
, i.e.

\displaystyle(\Delta+\lambda_{j})\phi_{j}(z)=0,\quad z\in{\Gamma\backslash{\mathbb{H}}}^{n}.

The study of the behaviour of Laplace eigenfunctions
{\phi}_{j}
and the corresponding Laplace eigenvalues
\lambda_{j}
is a central problem in Mathematical Physics and Number theory. For the rest of the paper we fix the notation
{\lambda}_{j}=s_{j}(n-1-s_{j})
. Notice that
\lambda_{j}\geq(\frac{n-1}{2})^{2}
if and only if
\Re(s_{j})=\frac{n-1}{2}
. The eigenvalues satisfying
\lambda_{j}<(\frac{n-1}{2})^{2}
are called
small
(or
exceptional
) eigenvalues. We also use the standard notation

\displaystyle\lambda_{j}=\left(\frac{n-1}{2}\right)^{2}+t_{j}^{2}

with
t_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
or
0<|it_{j}|\leq\frac{n-1}{2}
, with
t_{j}
imaginary. If
{\Gamma\backslash{\mathbb{H}}}^{n}
is not compact but has finite hyperbolic volume the operator
-\Delta
has also continuous spectrum, the interval
[(n-1)^{2}/4,\infty)
, spanned by non-holomorphic Eisenstein series.

For two points
z,w\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
let
d(z,w)
denote their hyperbolic distance. The hyperbolic lattice counting problem (also known as
the circle problem
in
2
dimensions) asks to estimate the growth of
{\Gamma}
-orbits in
{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
, i.e. the counting function

\displaystyle N_{\Gamma,r}(z,w)=\#\{\gamma\in\Gamma:d(z,\gamma w)\leq r\},\,\text{as}\quad r\to\infty.

This problem can be regarded as the hyperbolic analogue of the classical lattice counting problem on the Euclidean space, which dates back to Gauss. After the change of variable
X=2\cosh r\asymp e^{r}
we denote the quantity
N_{\Gamma,r}(z,w)
by
N_{\Gamma}(X;z,w)
. The study of pointwise estimates for
N_{\Gamma}(X;z,w)
reduces to estimating the spectral expansion of an appropriately defined automorphic kernel using the pre-trace formula. Günther
[
23
]
, Lax and Phillips
[
35
]
and Levitan
[
36
]
(for the cocompact case), generalizing previous work of Selberg
[
47
]
, Huber
[
24
]
, Patterson
[
42
]
, Good
[
20
]
for
n=2
and Fricker
[
18
]
for
n=3
, proved the following main theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that
\Gamma
is a cofinite group acting on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. Then the asymptotic behaviour of
N_{\Gamma}(X;z,w)
as
X\to\infty
is given by the formula:

\displaystyle N_{\Gamma}(X;z,w)=M_{\Gamma}(X;z,w)+E_{\Gamma}(X;z,w),

where the main term
M_{\Gamma}(X;z,w)
is given by a finite sum over the small eigenvalues
\lambda_{j}<(\frac{n-1}{2})^{2}
and the eigenvalue
\lambda_{j}=\left(\frac{n-1}{2}\right)^{2}
; moreover the error term
E_{\Gamma}(X;z,w)
satisfies the bound

(1.1)

\displaystyle E_{\Gamma}(X;z,w)=O_{\Gamma}\left(X^{n-2+\frac{2}{n+1}}(\log X)^{\frac{3}{n+1}}\right).

Remark 1.1
.

All the
O
-estimates throughout the paper will depend on the group
\Gamma
. Moreover, whenever the
O
-bound depends on
\epsilon>0
, the implied constant will also depend on
\epsilon
. For brevity reasons, from now on we will not indicate explicitly these dependencies.

For the exact description of the main term
M_{\Gamma}(X;z,w)
, which is a well understood quantity, we refer to subsection
2.1
. The main goal of the lattice counting problem in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
is to understand the exact asymptotic behaviour of the error term
E_{\Gamma}(X;z,w)
, which contains only the contribution of the large eigenvalues
\lambda_{j}>(n-1)^{2}/4
. This problem remains far open, most famously in dimensions
2
and
3
.

1.1.
Local average over compact sets, quantum variance and exponential sums

An intriguing relationship between the lattice counting problem and a quantum variance estimate was explored by Petridis-Risager for the case of the modular group
\Gamma={\hbox{PSL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})}
in
[
43
]
. In fact, in the
2
-dimensional case Selberg proved the upper bound

\displaystyle E_{\Gamma}(X;z,w)=O(X^{2/3}),

which is
X^{\epsilon}
-stronger than the upper bound (
1.1
).
Improving this bound amounts to detect thin cancellation in a spectral exponential sum involving the Laplace eigenfunctions
\phi_{j}
. Selberg’s bound was recently improved for the first time in
[
13
]
for the modular group and for Heegner points
z,w
of different discriminants.

One may hope for stronger results when averaging the error term over the surface. The main result of Petridis and Risager in
[
43
]
is the following.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
{\Gamma}={\hbox{PSL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})}
and
f
a smooth, compactly supported function on the modular surface
{\Gamma\backslash{\mathbb{H}}}^{2}
. Then

\displaystyle\int_{{\Gamma\backslash{\mathbb{H}}}^{2}}f(z)E_{{\Gamma}}(X;z,z)d\mu(z)=O_{f}\left(X^{7/12+\epsilon}\right),

for every
\epsilon>0
, where
d\mu
is the hyperbolic volume measure on
{\Gamma\backslash{\mathbb{H}}}^{2}
.

Recently Cherubini-Katsivelos in
[
14
]
announced the following analogous conditional result for the Picard manifold.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
{\Gamma}=\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[i])
and
let
f
a smooth compactly
supported function on the Picard manifold
{\Gamma\backslash{\mathbb{H}}}^{3}
.
Then, for every
\epsilon>0
, we have

\int_{{\Gamma\backslash{\mathbb{H}}^{3}}}f(z)E_{\Gamma}(X,z,z)\,d\mu(z)=O_{f,\theta,q}\left(X^{\frac{6-4\theta}{5-4\theta}+\epsilon}+X^{\frac{2q}{q+1}+\epsilon}\right),

where
d\mu
is the hyperbolic volume measure on
{\Gamma\backslash{\mathbb{H}}}^{3}
,
\theta
is any subconvexity exponent
for quadratic Dirichlet
L
-functions over the Gaussian integers and
q
is an exponent subject to a conjectural statement for the Quantum variance on
{\Gamma\backslash{\mathbb{H}}}^{3}
.

Remark 1.2
.

For more details on the exponents
\theta
and
q
in the statement of Theorem
1.3
see Hypothesis STX and Hypothesis QV in
[
14
]
.

An estimate of this type is called a
local average
estimate over the manifold. In order to prove their result, Petridis-Risager and Cherubini-Katsivelos used strong input from the Quantum unique ergodicity problem.

Assume
\mathcal{M}
is a compact Riemannian manifold with volume measure
d\mu
and Laplace eigenfunctions
\phi_{j}
(assumed
{\hbox{L}^{2}}
-normalized) corresponding to eigenvalues
\lambda_{j}\geq 0
. Define the measures

(1.2)

\,d\mu_{j}(z)=|\phi_{j}(z)|^{2}\,d\mu(z).

The Quantum ergodicity (QE) theorem states that if the geodesic flow on the unit cotangent bundle is ergodic then there exists a density one subsequence
\lambda_{j_{k}}
such that the measures
d\mu_{j_{k}}
converge weakly to the volume measure:

\,d\mu_{j_{k}}(z)\xrightarrow{j_{k}\to\infty}\frac{1}{\hbox{vol}(\mathcal{M})}\,d\mu(z),

i.e.

(1.3)

\int_{\mathcal{M}}f(z)\,d\mu_{j_{k}}(z)\xrightarrow{j_{k}\to\infty}\bar{f}:=\frac{1}{\hbox{vol}(\mathcal{M})}\int_{\mathcal{M}}f(z)\,d\mu(z),

for every smooth and compactly supported function
f
on
\mathcal{M}
(for more details, see
[
37
,
38
]
and references therein).
The Quantum unique ergodicity (QUE) conjecture of Rudnick and Sarnak
[
45
]
states that for compact manifolds of negative curvature the full sequence
d\mu_{j}
converges to the volume measure.
This has been proven to be the case for compact arithmetic Riemann surfaces by Linderstrauss
[
37
]
and for the cofinite case by Soundararajan
[
48
]
.
In fact, Luo and Sarnak
[
38
]
have proved the following strong quantum variance estimate.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\Gamma={\hbox{PSL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})}
,
f
a smooth compactly supported function on
\mathcal{M}={\Gamma\backslash{\mathbb{H}}}^{2}
and
\phi_{j}(z)
an orthonormal basis of Hecke cusp forms. Then, for every
\epsilon>0

\sum_{|t_{j}|\leq T}\left|\int_{{\Gamma\backslash{\mathbb{H}}}^{2}}f(z)\,d\mu_{j}(z)-\bar{f}\right|^{2}=O_{f}\left(T^{1+\epsilon}\right).

This quantum variance estimate was later refined by Zhao
[
49
]
and Sarnak–Zhao
[
50
]
, whereas for the holomorphic case the quantum variance was worked out by Luo–Sarnak
[
39
]
.
In higher dimensions the QUE conjecture remains far open. However, Zelditch
[
51
,
52
]
proved a more general quantum variance estimate for any
n
-dimensional compact Riemannian manifold with negative curvature (not necessarily constant), but with a worse upper bound (see also
[
49
,
53
]
for more details on quantum variance estimates).

For
{\Gamma\backslash{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}
compact, Weyl’s law (Theorem
2.1
) implies that the size of the (necessarily discrete) spectrum of the hyperbolic Laplacian on
\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}^{n}
is of order
\#\{j:|t_{j}|\leq T\}\sim c_{\Gamma}T^{n}.

The following conjecture is a weak version of the quantum variance problem, see
[
40
, p. 2]
for further details.

Conjecture 1.1
.

Assume
f
is a smooth, compactly supported function on
\Gamma\backslash{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
, with
\Gamma
cocompact. Then

(1.4)

\sum_{|t_{j}|\leq T}\bigg|\int_{\Gamma\setminus\mathbb{H}^{n}}f(z)\,d\mu_{j}(z)-\bar{f}\bigg|^{2}=O_{f}\big(T^{q_{n}}\big),

with
q_{n}=q_{n}({\Gamma})=1+\epsilon
for every
n\geq 2
.

Notice that any upper bound of the form
o(T^{n})
in (
1.4
) implies QE for the orbifold
{\Gamma\backslash{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}
, and indeed this is what Zelditch proved. Although Conjecture
1.1
is far open, we will see that a weaker version of this conjecture suffices to prove our result.

For a compact manifold
{\Gamma\backslash{\mathbb{H}}}^{n}
with eigenvalues
\lambda_{j}
we define the spectral exponential sums

(1.5)

S_{\Gamma}(T,X)=S(T,X):=\sum_{|t_{j}|\leq T}X^{it_{j}},

for
X,T\geq 1
. For arithmetic Riemann surfaces this exponential sum has been studied in
[
2
,
3
,
26
,
32
,
38
]
, whereas for the Picard manifold the analogous sum has been investigated in
[
1
,
4
,
12
,
29
,
30
,
33
]
. We are interested in upper bounds of the form

(1.6)

S(T,X)\ll X^{a}T^{b},

for exponents
a=a({\Gamma}),\,b=b({\Gamma})
. Weyl’s law yields the trivial estimate
a=0
,
b=n
, for every
n
. For the case of the modular surface
{\hbox{PSL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})}\backslash{\mathbb{H}}^{2}
it was conjectured in
[
43
]
that (
1.6
) holds with
a=\epsilon,

b=1+\epsilon
, for any
\epsilon>0
. For the Picard manifold
{\hbox{PSL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}}[i])}\backslash{\mathbb{H}}^{3}
, it is conjectured in
[
29
, Conjecture 5.1]
that (
1.6
) holds with
a=\epsilon,

b=2+\epsilon
. It is thus justified to formulate the following conjecture, which may be well known to the experts but we have not found it anywhere explicitly stated in the literature.

Conjecture 1.2
.

Let
n\geq 2
and let
\Gamma
be an essentially cuspidal cofinite lattice in
{\hbox{SO}^{+}(1,n)}
Then, for
X,T\to\infty
and for every
\epsilon>0

we have

S(T,X)=O\left(X^{\epsilon}T^{n-1+\epsilon}\right)

We will only use this conjecture for the case of cocompact lattices, nevertheless it makes sense to state the conjecture for any essentially cuspidal group
\Gamma
(see
[
46
]
for the definition of an essentially cuspidal group).

1.2.
Our local average result

Our first result is a local average for the error term
E_{{\Gamma}}(X;z,w)
, with
{\Gamma}
a cocompact lattice acting on
{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
(with
n\geq 3
). In this case our result is conditional on (a weaker version of) Conjecture
1.1
and Conjecture
1.2
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
n\geq 3
and let
{\Gamma}\subset{\hbox{SO}^{+}(1,n)}
be a cocompact group and
f
a smooth, compactly supported function on
{\Gamma\backslash{\mathbb{H}}}^{n}
.
Assume Conjecture
1.1
with the weaker exponent
q_{n}=n-2+\epsilon
and Conjecture
1.2
. Then

\displaystyle\int_{{\Gamma\backslash{\mathbb{H}}}^{n}}f(z)E_{{\Gamma}}(X;z,z)\,d\mu(z)=O_{f}\left(X^{n-2+\epsilon}\right),

where
d\mu
is the hyperbolic volume measure on
{\Gamma\backslash{\mathbb{H}}}^{n}
.

It should be mentioned that any stronger assumption on the value of
q_{n}
does not improve our result. Notice also that for
n=3
our result is essentially optimal.

Remark 1.3
.

Conjecture
1.1
is analogous to Hypothesis QV in
[
14
]
and Conjecture
1.2
is analogous to Hypothesis STX in
[
14
]
.

Remark 1.4
.

The local average has been also studied by Biró
[
5
]
where he proved that for any cofinite group
\Gamma\subset{\hbox{PSL}_{2}({\mathbb{R}})}
we can obtain the local average bound
O_{f,\epsilon}\left(X^{5/8+\epsilon}\right)
. Even though his exponent is worse than the exponent of Petridis-Risager (by a margin of
1/24
), he covers any cofinite Fuchsian group
\Gamma
and his proof needs no arithmetic input. In particular Biró used his generalization of the Selberg trace formula from
[
6
]
. More recently, in his recent preprint
[
7
]
, Biró also studied a second moment local average for any cofinite Fuchsian group, obtaining a slightly worse exponent.

Remark 1.5
.

The lattice counting problem has also been studied in the higher rank case
[
17
,
21
]
. Recently, Blomer and Lutsko
[
8
]
improved previous pointwise bounds for the group
\hbox{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
and studied a version of the local average of the error term for any rank.

1.3.
\Omega
-results for the mean value of the error term

One would like to expect square root cancellation for the error term:

(1.7)

\displaystyle E_{\Gamma}(X;z,w)=O\left(X^{\frac{n-1}{2}+\epsilon}\right)

for any
z
and
w
. However, Phillips and Rudnick proved that this is not always true. In
[
44
]
they investigated the mean behaviour of the error term
E_{\Gamma}(X;z,z)
in the radial parameter
r\asymp\log X
, as well as
\Omega
-results for the error. For
n\geq 4
, they constructed an explicit arithmetic lattice
\Gamma\subset{\hbox{SO}^{+}(1,n)}\cap{\hbox{SL}_{n+1}({\mathbb{Z}})}
such that for a special fixed point
z_{0}
we have

\displaystyle E_{\Gamma}(X;z_{0},z_{0})=\Omega(X^{n-2}).

Thus for large dimensions one cannot always expect square root cancellation for the error term.
Their argument is purely arithmetic, as they embedded a Euclidean lattice counting error term in
E_{\Gamma}(X;z_{0},z_{0})
. However, for a general lattice
\Gamma\subset{\hbox{SO}^{+}(1,n)}
, using the almost periodicity of the normalized error term in the
r
-variable they only proved the following much weaker
\Omega
-result.

Theorem 1.6
.

If
\Gamma
is cocompact or congruence group, then

E_{\Gamma}(X;z,z)=\Omega\left(X^{\frac{n-1}{2}}(\log\log X)^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2n}-\epsilon}\right)

for all
\epsilon>0
.

For a cofinite group
\Gamma
they also prove the existence of the mean value in the
r
-variable:

(1.8)

\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{1}{R}\int_{0}^{R}\frac{E_{\Gamma}(e^{r};z,z)}{e^{(n-1)r/2}}dr=c

where
c=c({\Gamma})
is a constant depending only on
\Gamma
(and
c=0
for
\Gamma
cocompact).

The almost periodicity of the normalized error term in the
r
-variable is crucial (see
[
15
]
), but this property fails for the mean value in the
X
-variable.
Although the error term attains large growth when
n\geq 4
, for
n=2
and
3
the square root cancellation (
1.7
) is far open and difficult. As in the Euclidean case (see
[
31
]
), one may expect that (
1.7
) holds for a ‘generic’ lattice
\Gamma\subset{\hbox{SO}^{+}(1,n)}
and for ‘most’ points
z
and
w
.

Let
{\Gamma}
be cocompact and
e(T,z)
denote the following modified mean average of the error term in the
X
-variable:

(1.9)

e(T,z)=e_{{\Gamma}}(T,z):=\frac{1}{T}\int_{\sqrt{T}}^{T}\frac{E_{\Gamma}(x;z,z)}{x^{(n-1)/2}}dx.

This normalized error term is no longer almost periodic in the
X
-variable (see
[
11
]
), hence we expect a less normal behaviour for
e(T,z)
. In
2
dimensions we have
e(T,z)=O(1)
, but Chatzakos
[
11
]
proved that the limit

\lim_{T\to\infty}e(T,z)

may not exist (see also Remark
1.6
).

We study the behaviour of the modified mean value
e(T,z)
for
n\geq 3
.
We prove that the normalized error term
e(T,z)
does not have finite mean value for large dimensions, which can be compared with the mean value result (
1.8
) of Phillips and Rudnick in the
r
-variable.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
\Gamma\subset{\hbox{SO}^{+}(1,n)}
be a cocompact lattice acting on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. Then:

(a) for
n=3
we have

e_{{\Gamma}}(T,z)=\Omega\left(\log\log\log T\right),

b) for
n\geq 4
we have

e_{{\Gamma}}(T,z)=\Omega\left((\log\log T)^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{3}{2n}-\epsilon}\right)

for all
\epsilon>0
.

Remark 1.6
.

In
[
11
]
Chatzakos studied the behaviour of the average of the mean error term in the full interval
[2,T]
instead of
[\sqrt{T},T]
. For
n=2
there are no technical differences between the two average terms. However, for
n\geq 3
, we choose to average the error in
[\sqrt{T},T]
since this simplifies our calculations. In particular, studying the full interval
[2,T]
yields a factor that does not get absorbed in the error term, whereas studying the dyadic interval
[T,2T]
yields oscillatory factors, with alternating sign for arbitrarily large
t
. Our average does not affect our result in the sense that we work in an interval of asymptotic length
T
.

1.4.
\Omega
-results for the second moment

In the classical Gauss circle problem Cramér
[
16
, (eq.) 5]
proved an asymptotic formula for the second moment of the error term with a power saving.
Cramér’s result indicates a very regular behaviour of the Euclidean error term, as the extra
X^{\epsilon}
-factors disappear in the main asymptotic of the second moment. An average result of this kind is called a
Cramér-type result
.
For analogous results in higher Euclidean dimensions
see
[
19
,
28
]
.

For
{\Gamma\backslash{\mathbb{H}}}^{2}
, Phillips and Rudnick studied the second moment in the
r
-variable. They proved that

(1.10)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{R}\int_{0}^{R}\left|\frac{E_{\Gamma}(e^{r};z,z)}{e^{r/2}}\right|^{2}dr=\Omega(1),

and speculated that a Cramér-type result in the
r
-variable may hold. For the second moment in the
X
-variable, the
\Omega
-results of
[
11
]
and the large sieve of Chamizo
[
9
,
10
]
imply that

(1.11)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\int_{2}^{T}\left|\frac{E_{\Gamma}(x;z,z)}{x^{1/2}}\right|^{2}dx

is
\Omega\left(1\right)
and
O(\log^{2}T)
. The upper bound was subsequently improved by Cherubini
[
15
]
to
O(\log T)
using the almost periodicity of the error term. If (
1.11
) is
O(1)
, then (
1.10
) is also
O(1)
. However, although we expect a Cramér-type result in the
r
-variable to be true, the boundedness of (
1.11
) is doubtful.

For
{\Gamma\backslash{\mathbb{H}}}^{n}
, with
n\geq 3
, the large sieve inequalities give worse upper bounds for the second moment than in
2
dimensions, due to the abundance of eigenvalues (see Laaksonen
[
34
]
for the case
n=3
).
As a straightforward corollary of Theorem
1.7
, we prove the following
\Omega
-results.

Corollary 1.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a cocompact lattice acting on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. Then:

a) for
n=3
we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\int_{2}^{T}\left|\frac{E_{\Gamma}(x;z,z)}{x}\right|^{2}dx=\Omega\left((\log\log\log T)^{2}\right),

b) for
n\geq 4
we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\int_{2}^{T}\left|\frac{E_{\Gamma}(x;z,z)}{x^{(n-1)/2}}\right|^{2}dx=\Omega\left((\log\log T)^{1-\frac{3}{n}-\epsilon}\right)

for all
\epsilon>0
.

Remark 1.7
.

The most interesting case of Corollary
1.1
is part (a), where we expect the upper bound
O(X^{1+\epsilon})
to be true. Hill and Parnovski
[
25
]
studied a (spatial) variance of the error term
E_{\Gamma}(X;z,w)
, where they fix the one point and take the average over the other. They deduced an explicit formula for the second moment where the error term has a saving of the extra
X^{\epsilon}
-factors. Their results
[
25
, Th. 1, Cor. 1]
can be compared with the work of Laaksonen
[
34
]
and our Corollary
1.1
.

2.
Background

2.1.
The contribution of the main term
M_{\Gamma}(X;z,w)
.

For
\Gamma
a cofinite group acting on
{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
we can decompose the main term
M_{\Gamma}(X;z,w)
in the asymptotic formula of Theorem
1.1
as

(2.1)

\displaystyle M_{\Gamma}(X;z,w)=\sum_{\frac{n-1}{2}\leq s_{j}\leq n-1}\pi^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\frac{\Gamma\left(s_{j}-\frac{n-1}{2}\right)}{\Gamma(s_{j}+1)}\phi_{j}(z)\overline{\phi_{j}(w)}X^{s_{j}}+A_{\Gamma}(X;z,w),

where following
[
44
]
, the term
A_{\Gamma}(X;z,w)
is a well understood quantity. More precisely, for
n\geq 3
it can be split as a finite sum

\displaystyle A_{\Gamma}(X;z,w)=\sum_{\frac{n-1}{2}\leq s_{j}\leq n-1}a(X;s_{j},z,w),

where for every
s_{j}>(n-1)/2
the term
a(X;s_{j},z,w)
contains the secondary contributions of the eigenvalue
\lambda_{j}<(n-1)^{2}/4
(if this eigenvalue exists) and satisfies

\displaystyle a(X;s_{j},z,w)\ll X^{s_{j}-2}+o(X^{(n-1)/2})

[
44
, eq. (4.3)]
. The term
a(X;(n-1)/2,z,w)
includes the total contribution of the eigenvalue
\lambda_{j}=(n-1)^{2}/4
and satisfies

\displaystyle a\left(X;\frac{n-1}{2},z,w\right)=O(X^{(n-1)/2+\epsilon}).

Overall, we conclude that the total contribution of the secondary error terms is estimated as

(2.2)

\displaystyle A_{{\Gamma}}(X;z,w)=O(X^{n-3}+X^{\frac{n-1}{2}+\epsilon}).

We refer to
[
44
, sect. 4]
or
[
25
, Prop.3, p. 479]
for a detailed explanation of this term (the two references do not agree because of a typo in
[
44
, p.103 eq. (4.3)]
).

2.2.
Weyl’s law

We write
s_{j}=(n-1)/2+it_{j}
, hence
\lambda_{j}=(n-1)^{2}/4+t_{j}^{2}
. We will refer to the following two main results describing the asymptotic behaviour of the eigenvalues
\lambda_{j}
and of the eigenfunctions
\phi_{j}(z)
as
j\to\infty
. The first result is Weyl’s law for
\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}^{n}
.

Theorem 2.1

(Weyl’s law)
.

Let
{\Gamma\backslash{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}
be a
n
-dimensional compact manifold. Then as
T\to\infty
we have

\displaystyle\#\left\{j:|t_{j}|\leq T\right\}\sim\frac{\vol(\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}^{n})}{(4\pi)^{n/2}\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}+1\right)}T^{n}.

The local version of Theorem
2.1
is the so-called
local Weyl’s law
for the space
{\hbox{L}^{2}}(\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}^{n})
(see
[
44
, p. 103, eq. (4.7)]
). For our purposes it suffices to state the result in the cocompact case.

Theorem 2.2

(Local Weyl’s law)
.

Let
{\Gamma}
be a cocompact group. Then, for every
z\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
, as
T\to\infty
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{|t_{j}|\leq T}|\phi_{j}(z)|^{2}\sim cT^{n},

where the constant
c=c(z)
depends only on the point
z
and the dimension.

2.3.
The Selberg/Harish-Chandra transform

We choose the kernel
k
depending on the hyperbolic distance between two points to be the characteristic function

\displaystyle k_{X}(u(z,w))=k_{X}(u)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}1,&\mbox{for}&u\leq(X-2)/4,\\
0,&\mbox{for}&u>(X-2)/4,\end{array}\right.

where
u(z,w)
is the standard point-pair invariant function satisfying
2u(z,w)+1=\cosh d(z,w)

The
n
-dimensional Selberg/Harish-Chandra transform
h_{X}(t)
of the kernel defined in (
2.3
) is given by the formula

(2.6)

h_{X}(t)=c_{n}\int_{-r}^{r}(\cosh r-\cosh u)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}e^{itu}\,du,

where
c_{n}
is a specific constant depending only on the dimension
n
(see
[
44
, p. 102]
).
From now on, we will denote by
c_{n}
any multiple of this constant (not necessarily the same) depending only on the dimension
n
.

As before, let us set
X=2\cosh r
. Using
[
22
, p. 962, eq. (8.715.1)]
we can write
h_{X}(t)
in the form

\displaystyle h_{X}(t)=c_{n}(\sinh r)^{n/2}P_{-1/2+it}^{-n/2}(\cosh r),

where
P_{\nu}^{\mu}
is the associated Legendre function.
By formula
[
22
, p. 971,eq. (8.776.1)]
we calculate, for
X\gg 1
:

(2.7)

h_{X}(t)=c_{n}\Re\left(\frac{{\Gamma}(it)}{{\Gamma}\left(\frac{n+1}{2}+it\right)}X^{it}\right)\left(X^{\frac{n-1}{2}}+O\left(X^{\frac{n-5}{2}}\right)\right).

Stirling’s formula
[
22
, p. 895, eq. (8.328.1)]
reads
\lim_{|t|\to+\infty}|\Gamma(x+it)|\sim\sqrt{2\pi}e^{-\pi|t|/2}|t|^{x-1/2}
, thus for
X,|t|\gg 1
we have the asymptotic

(2.8)

h_{X}(t)=c_{n}\frac{X^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}{|t|^{\frac{n+1}{2}}}\Re(\theta_{n}X^{it})+O\left(\frac{X^{(\frac{n-5}{2})}}{|t|^{\frac{n+3}{2}}}\right),

where
\theta_{n}
is an angular constant depending only on the dimension
n
. Additionaly using formula
[
22
, p. 963,eq. (8.723.1)]
we can express
h_{X}(t)
as a sum of conjugates in the form

(2.9)

h_{X}(t)=\tilde{A}(X,t)e^{itr}+\tilde{B}(X,t)e^{-itr},

where in terms of the Gamma and Hypergeometric functions we have

(2.10)

\tilde{A}(X,t)=c_{n}(\sinh r)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\frac{\Gamma(it)}{\Gamma(\frac{n+1}{2}+it)}F\left(\frac{1-n}{2},\frac{n+1}{2},1-it,\frac{1}{1-e^{2r}}\right)

and
\tilde{B}(X,t)=\tilde{A}(X,-t).

On the other hand in the spirit of
[
10
, Lemma 2.4]
for any
t\in\mathbb{C}
we have the uniform upper bound:

(2.11)

h_{X}(t)=O(X^{\frac{n-1}{2}+|\Im t|}\log X).

3.
Local average for the lattice counting

3.1.
The automorphic kernel and heuristics for the lattice point counting

Let us recall heuristically the spectral approach to the hyperbolic lattice point counting. Assume
\Gamma
is a cocompact lattice in
{\hbox{SO}^{+}(1,n)}
. We need to apply the pre-trace formula (see for instance
[
41
]
) for
{\Gamma\backslash{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}
to the automorphic kernel

\displaystyle K(z,w)=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}k(z,\gamma w),

where
k
is a suitable kernel depending on the hyperbolic distance
d(z,\gamma w)
. Then, the pre-trace formula allows us to express
K(z,w)
as a spectral expansion

\displaystyle K(z,w)=\sum_{\lambda_{j}}h(t_{j})\phi_{j}(z)\overline{\phi_{j}(w)}

over the Laplacian eigenvalues
\lambda_{j}=(n-1)^{2}/4+t_{j}^{2}
. Here,
h(t)
is the
n-
dimensional Selberg/Harish-Chandra transform of the kernel
k
. If we could choose the kernel
k_{X}(u(z,w))
defined in (
2.3
), in that case we can easily verify that

(3.1)

\displaystyle N_{\Gamma}(X;z,w)=K_{X}(u(z,w))=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}k_{X}(u(z,\gamma w)).

Applying the pre-trace formula directly to (
3.1
), setting
z=w
and subtracting the contribution of the small eigenvalues (see Theorem
1.1
), we would get a spectral expansion for the error term
E_{\Gamma}(X;z,z)
over the real, non-zero Satake parameters
t_{j}
:

\displaystyle E_{\Gamma}(X;z,z)\sim\sum_{|t_{j}|>0}h_{X}(t_{j})|\phi_{j}(z)|^{2}.

However, the characteristic kernel is not an admissible kernel in order to apply the pre-trace formula, hence we need to consider smooth approximations.

For the local average result, we will define appropriate smooth approximations
k_{\pm}(u)
of
k(u)
satisfying the inequality

(3.2)

k_{-}(u)\leq k_{X}(u)\leq k_{+}(u).

Then the corresponding automorphic kernels
N^{\pm}_{{\Gamma}}
defined as

(3.3)

N^{\pm}_{{\Gamma}}(X;z,w):=\sum_{\gamma\in{\Gamma}}k_{\pm}(u(z,\gamma w))

also satisfy an inequality similar to (
3.2
):

(3.4)

N^{-}_{{\Gamma}}(X;z,w)\leq N_{{\Gamma}}(X;z,w)\leq N^{+}_{{\Gamma}}(X;z,w).

Therefore in order to prove Theorem
1.5
it suffices to bound the local average

\int_{{\Gamma\backslash{\mathbb{H}}}^{n}}f(z)E_{{\Gamma}}^{\pm}(X;z,z)\,d\mu(z),

where

(3.5)

E_{{\Gamma}}^{\pm}(X;z,w)=N_{{\Gamma}}^{\pm}(X;z,w)-M_{{\Gamma}}^{\pm}(X;z,w)

and
M_{{\Gamma}}^{\pm}(X;z,w)
are sufficiently good approximations of the main term
M_{{\Gamma}}(X;z,w)
defined by (
2.1
). If we set
z=w
in Selberg’s pre-trace formula for the automorphic kernel defined in (
3.3
), subtract
M_{{\Gamma}}^{\pm}(X;z,z)
and integrate against
f
we obtain

(3.6)

\int_{\Gamma\setminus\mathbb{H}^{n}}f(z)E_{\Gamma}^{\pm}(X;z,z)\,d\mu(z)=\bar{f}\sum_{|t_{j}|>0}h_{\pm}(t_{j})+\sum_{|t_{j}|>0}h_{\pm}(t_{j})\left(\int_{\Gamma\setminus\mathbb{H}^{n}}f(z)\,d\mu_{j}(z)-\bar{f}\right),

where
f
is a smooth compactly supported function on
\mathcal{M}={\Gamma\backslash{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}
and
\bar{f}
as defined in (
1.3
).
Here
h_{\pm}
is the Selberg/Harish-Chandra transform of kernel
k_{\pm}
, which we will describe in detail in subsection
3.2
.
Moreover
\,d\mu_{j}(z)
are the measures defined in (
1.2
) for the Riemannian manifold
\mathcal{M}=\Gamma\backslash{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
. Inequality (
3.4
) together with the definition of
E_{\Gamma}^{\pm}
will lead to the proof of Theorem
1.5
.

3.2.
Smooth approximations

For smoothing purposes we will convolute the kernel
k_{X}
, with the family of test functions

(3.7)

k_{\delta}(u)=\frac{1}{\hbox{vol}(B_{n}(\delta))}\cdot\chi_{[0,\frac{\cosh\delta-1}{2}]}(u),

for
0<\delta<1
a (sufficiently small) parameter, which will be later chosen as a negative power of
X
, and

(3.8)

\hbox{vol}\left(B_{n}(r)\right)=\frac{2\pi^{n/2}}{\Gamma(n/2)}\int_{0}^{r}(\sinh t)^{n-1}\,dt

is the volume of the
n
-dimensional hyperbolic ball of radius
r
. Even though expression (
3.8
) can be computed in terms of special functions (see
[
25
, Corollary 2]
) we will only need the asymptotics for small values of
r
, that is

\hbox{vol}\left(B_{n}(r)\right)\sim\frac{2\pi^{n/2}}{n{\Gamma}(n/2)}r^{n}.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
k_{\delta}(u)
be the kernel defined in (
3.7
) and
h_{\delta}(t)
its Selberg/Harish-Chandra transform given by (
2.6
). Then for
t\geq 1
and
\delta\to 0
, we have that

h_{\delta}(t)=O\left(\min(1,(\delta t)^{-(\frac{n+1}{2})})\right)

and also

h^{\prime}_{\delta}(t)=O\left(\frac{1}{t}\min(1,(\delta t)^{-(\frac{n-1}{2})})\right).

Proof.

Using the identity
\cosh\delta=2\sinh^{2}(\delta/2)+1
and with the change of variable
u=\delta y
we can rewrite
h_{\delta}(t)
as

h_{\delta}(t)=c_{n}2^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\delta^{1-n}\left(\sinh\frac{\delta}{2}\right)^{n-1}\int_{-1}^{1}\left(1-\left(\frac{\sinh(\delta y/2)}{\sinh(\delta/2)}\right)^{2}\right)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}e^{it\delta y}\,dy.

Applying dominated convergence we have that if
\delta\to 0
and
\delta t\to 0
then

h_{\delta}(t)\sim c_{n}2^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\delta^{1-n}\left(\sinh\frac{\delta}{2}\right)^{n-1}\int_{-1}^{1}(1-y^{2})^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\,dy,

where the integral can be explicitly computed in terms of the Gamma function but for our purposes it suffices to note that it is a constant depending only on
n
. Also if
\delta\to 0
and
\delta t\in(0,+\infty)
we have that

h_{\delta}(t)\sim c_{n}2^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\delta^{1-n}\left(\sinh\frac{\delta}{2}\right)^{n-1}\int_{-1}^{1}(1-y^{2})^{\frac{n-1}{2}}e^{it\delta y}\,dy,

where by
[
22
, eq. 8.411.10]
we can evaluate the integral in terms of Bessel functions and deduce

h_{\delta}(t)\sim c_{n}2^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\delta^{1-n}\left(\sinh\frac{\delta}{2}\right)^{n-1}\sqrt{\pi}2^{n/2}{\Gamma}\left(\frac{n+1}{2}\right)\frac{J_{n/2}(\delta t)}{(\delta t)^{n/2}}.

Applying the bound
J_{n}(z)=O(\min(z^{n},z^{-1/2}))
for the Bessel function (see
[
27
, Appendix B4]
), the first part of the lemma follows. As for the second part, concerning the derivative of
h_{\delta}(t)
in (
2.6
), differentiating with respect to
t
and applying similar arguments as above, the result follows.
∎

Setting
\cosh Y=X/2
,
it is straightforward that
u\leq(X-2)/4\iff d\leq Y
. Let
h_{Y\pm\delta}
be the Selberg/Harish-Chandra transform for the characteristic function

(3.9)

\chi_{\left[0,\frac{\cosh(Y\pm\delta)-1}{2}\right]}

and define the convolution

k_{\pm}:=\chi_{\left[0,\frac{(\cosh(Y\pm\delta)-1)}{2}\right]}\ast k_{\delta}.

It follows that inequality (
3.2
) holds for the kernels
k_{-},k_{X},k_{+}
and as for the Selberg/Harish-Chandra transform of
k_{\pm}
we have by the convolution identity (see
[
10
, 4. p.323]
, we omit the proof in
{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
) that

(3.10)

h_{\pm}=h_{Y\pm\delta}\cdot h_{\delta}.

3.3.
Bounding the contribution of the small eigenvalues

In this section we bound the difference of the main term
M_{\Gamma}(X;z,z)
and its approximation
M_{{\Gamma}}^{\pm}(X;z,z)
(see subsection
3.1
). We begin by subtracting
M_{{\Gamma}}^{\pm}(X;z,w)
from inequality (
3.4
) and using (
3.5
) we deduce that for
z=w
, we have

E_{{\Gamma}}(X;z,z)\ll E_{{\Gamma}}^{\pm}(X;z,z)+O\left(M_{{\Gamma}}^{\pm}(X;z,z)-M_{{\Gamma}}(X;z,z)\right).

The term
M_{{\Gamma}}^{\pm}(X;z,z)
is the main term of the spectral expansion of
N_{{\Gamma}}^{\pm}(X;z,z)
and is given by

M_{{\Gamma}}^{\pm}(X;z,z)=\sum_{\frac{n-1}{2}\leq s_{j}\leq n-1}h_{\pm}(it_{j})|\phi_{j}(z)|^{2},

with
s_{j}=\frac{n-1}{2}+it_{j}
. By the definition of
h_{\pm}
we need to estimate
h_{Y\pm\delta}
and
h_{\delta}
for imaginary and bounded argument. Using
[
44
, eq. 4.3]
and
the fact that

e^{Y\pm\delta}=X+O(X\delta),

we have that

(3.11)

h_{Y\pm\delta}(it)=c_{n}X^{|t|+\frac{n-1}{2}}+O(X^{|t|+\frac{n-1}{2}}\delta+X^{|t|+\frac{n-5}{2}}+X^{\frac{n-1}{2}}),

for
|t_{j}|>0
and
0<\delta<1
, with
\delta>X^{-1}
. As for
t_{j}=0
using equation (
2.11
), we have that
h_{Y\pm\delta}(0)=O(X^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\log X)
.
To estimate
h_{\delta}(it)
we recall
[
25
, Prop. 2, Coro. 2]
and we have

\displaystyle h_{\delta}(it)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\hbox{vol}(B_{n}(\delta))}\frac{\pi^{\frac{n-1}{2}}{\Gamma}(\frac{n+1}{2})}{{\Gamma}(n+1)}(2\sinh\delta)^{n}e^{(it-\frac{n+1}{2})\delta}

\displaystyle\times\,\,{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\frac{n+1}{2}-it,\frac{n+1}{2},n+1,1-e^{-2\delta}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{(it-\frac{n-1}{2})\delta}\,\times\,\frac{{}_{2}F_{1}(\frac{n+1}{2}-it,\frac{n+1}{2},n+1,1-e^{-2\delta})}{{}_{2}F_{1}(1,\frac{n+1}{2},n+1,1-e^{-2\delta})},

for all
\delta>0
and all
t\in\mathbb{C}.

Using the power series definition for the hypergeometric function (see
[
22
, eq. 9.100]
) it is straightforward that

(3.12)

h_{\delta}(it)=1+O(\delta),

for
0<\delta<1
and
0\leq it\leq(n-1)/2.

For
M_{{\Gamma}}(X;z,z)
we recall definition (
2.1
). Note that the constant in expression (
2.1
) agrees with the constant
c_{n}
in equation (
3.11
) and therefore the leading terms of
M_{\Gamma}
and
M_{{\Gamma}}^{\pm}
cancel each other out.
Using (
2.1
), (
2.2
) (
3.11
), (
3.12
) and
summing over all
0\leq it_{j}\leq(n-1)/2
we obtain a bound of the form

M_{{\Gamma}}(X;z,z)-M_{{\Gamma}}^{\pm}(X;z,z)=O\left(X^{n-1}\delta+X^{n-3}+X^{\frac{n-1}{2}+\epsilon}\right).

Therefore for the error term we have the asymptotic

(3.13)

E_{{\Gamma}}(X;z,z)\ll E_{{\Gamma}}^{\pm}(X;z,z)+O(X^{n-1}\delta+X^{n-3}+X^{\frac{n-1}{2}+\epsilon}).

Thus, in order to estimate the local average of the error term we are left with the task to estimate the local average of
E_{{\Gamma}}^{\pm}(X;z,z)
.

3.4.
Proof of Theorem
1.5

We begin this section by proving the following two Lemmas.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
h_{\pm}(t)
be as in (
3.10
) and
0<\delta<1
. We can express
h_{\pm}(t)
in the form

h_{\pm}(t)=A(X,t,\delta,n)e^{it(Y\pm\delta)}+B(X,t,\delta,n)e^{-it(Y\pm\delta)},

where for
t>1
the following bounds hold

\begin{gathered}A(X,t,\delta,n),\,\,B(X,t,\delta,n)\ll\frac{X^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}{|t|^{\frac{n+1}{2}}}\min\left(1,(\delta t)^{-(\frac{n+1}{2})}\right),\\
\partial_{t}A(X,t,\delta,n),\,\,\partial_{t}B(X,t,\delta,n)\ll\frac{X^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}{|t|^{\frac{n+3}{2}}}\min\left(1,(\delta t)^{-(\frac{n-1}{2})}\right).\end{gathered}

Proof.

We apply (
2.9
) and (
2.10
) for
h_{Y\pm\delta}(t)
defined as the Selberg/Harish-Chandra transform of the characteristic function defined in (
3.9
). Then we have an expression for
h_{Y\pm\delta}
as a sum of conjugates, with an explicit form on the coefficients similar to (
2.10
) and
Y\pm\delta
in place of
r
. Having in mind that
e^{Y\pm\delta}\asymp X
, using Stirling’s formula for the part involving the Gamma factors and the power series definition of the Hypergeometric function (
[
22
, eq. 9.100]
) we derive bounds for the coefficients of the sum of conjugates expression for
h_{Y\pm\delta}
and also for their derivatives with respect to
t
. From the above discussion and Lemma
3.1
the result follows.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
n\geq 3
and assume Conjecture
1.1
with
q_{n}=n-2+\epsilon
. Then the following estimate holds:

\sum_{|t_{j}|>0}h_{\pm}(t_{j})\left(\int_{\Gamma/\mathbb{H}^{n}}f(z)\,d\mu_{j}(z)-\bar{f}\right)=O_{f}\left(X^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\delta^{\frac{3-n}{2}-\epsilon}\right).

Proof.

For
|t_{j}|<1
using (
2.11
), (
3.10
) and Lemma
3.1
, the contribution is
O(X^{(n-1)/2}\log X)
and as for
|t_{j}|\geq 1
by Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and assuming Conjecture
1.1
, we have over dyadic intervals that

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{T\leq|t_{j}|<2T}h_{\pm}(t_{j})\left(\int_{\Gamma/\mathbb{H}^{n}}f(z)\,d\mu_{j}(z)-\bar{f}\right)\right|

(3.14)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{T\leq|t_{j}|<2T}|h_{\pm}(t_{j})|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{T\leq|t_{j}|<2T}\left|\int_{\Gamma/\mathbb{H}^{n}}f(z)\,d\mu_{j}(z)-\bar{f}\right|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=O_{f}\left(T^{n-1+\epsilon}\max_{T<|t|\leq 2T}|h_{\pm}(t)|\right).

Using Lemma
3.2
, we have that (
3.4
) is

O_{f}\left(X^{(n-1)/2}T^{\frac{n-3}{2}+\epsilon}\min(1,(\delta T)^{-(n+1)/2}\right).

Splitting cases for
T<\delta^{-1}
and
T\geq\delta^{-1}
, with
T=2^{k}
and summing for all
k\geq 0
we have that

\sum_{|t_{j}|\geq 1}h_{\pm}(t_{j})\left(\int_{\Gamma\setminus\mathbb{H}^{n}}f(z)\,d\mu_{j}(z)-\bar{f}\right)=O_{f}\left(X^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\delta^{\frac{3-n}{2}-\epsilon}\right).

As we mentioned earlier, the parameter
0<\delta<1
will be chosen as a negative power of
X\to\infty
, hence without loss of generality we may assume that the above error term dominates over the error
O(X^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\log X)
coming from
0<|t_{j}|<1
.
∎

We now want to estimate the term

\sum_{|t_{j}|>0}h_{\pm}(t_{j})
in (
3.6
).
We have seen that for the contribution arising from
|t_{j}|<1
every term is
O(X^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\log X)
and since there are finitely many such terms, it suffices to estimate the sum

\sum_{|t_{j}|\geq 1}h_{\pm}(t_{j}).

Summing over dyadic intervals and using Lemma
3.2
we get

\sum_{T\leq|t_{j}|<2T}h_{\pm}(t_{j})\ll\sum_{T\leq|t_{j}|<2T}A(X,t_{j},\delta,n)e^{(Y\pm\delta)it_{j}},

where by partial summation and definition (
1.5
) this is equal to

(3.15)

\displaystyle A(X,2T,\delta,n)S(2T,e^{Y\pm\delta})-A(X,T,\delta,n)S(T,e^{Y\pm\delta})-

\displaystyle\int_{T}^{2T}A^{\prime}(X,u,\delta,n)S(u,e^{Y\pm\delta})\,du.

Recalling Conjecture
1.2
and using Lemma
3.2
we can estimate the above expression. The first two terms of (
3.15
) are bounded by

\displaystyle O\left(X^{\frac{n-1}{2}+\epsilon}T^{\frac{n-3}{2}+\epsilon}\min\left(1,(\delta T)^{-(\frac{n+1}{2})}\right)\right),

whereas the integral is bounded by

\displaystyle O\left(X^{\frac{n-1}{2}+\epsilon}T^{\frac{n-3}{2}+\epsilon}\min\left(1,(\delta T)^{-(\frac{n-1}{2})}\right)\right).

We conclude that

\sum_{T\leq|t_{j}|<2T}h_{\pm}(t_{j})=O\left(X^{\frac{n-1}{2}+\epsilon}T^{\frac{n-3}{2}+\epsilon}\min\left(1,(\delta T)^{-(\frac{n-1}{2})}\right)\right).

Using again dyadic decomposition with
T=2^{k},
splitting for
T<\delta^{-1}
and
T\geq\delta^{-1}
and summing for
k\geq 0
we deduce that

(3.16)

\sum_{|t_{j}|\geq 1}h_{\pm}(t_{j})=O\left(X^{\frac{n-1}{2}+\epsilon}\delta^{\frac{3-n}{2}-\epsilon}\right).

By equation (
3.6
), the bound (
3.16
) and Lemma
3.3
, we get the local average upper bound for the smooth error terms

\int_{\Gamma\setminus\mathbb{H}^{n}}f(z)E_{{\Gamma}}^{\pm}(X;z,z)\,d\mu(z)=O_{f}(X^{\frac{n-1}{2}+\epsilon}\delta^{\frac{3-n}{2}-\epsilon}).

Recalling (
3.13
) for the relation between the error term and the smooth error terms, we obtain

\int_{\Gamma\setminus\mathbb{H}^{n}}f(z)E_{{\Gamma}}(X;z,z)\,d\mu(z)=O_{f}(X^{n-1}\delta+X^{n-3}+X^{\frac{n-1}{2}+\epsilon}+X^{\frac{n-1}{2}+\epsilon}\delta^{\frac{3-n}{2}-\epsilon}).

Choosing
\delta=X^{-1+\epsilon}
completes the proof of Theorem
1.5
.

4.
Proof of the
\Omega
-result

Before giving the proof of Theorem
1.7
, we can make some comments for the method of the proof. As in section
3
we cannot apply the pre-trace formula directly to the kernel
k_{X}
as it is not of rapid decay. For

, convergence problems disappear when we apply the pre-trace formula to the smoothed error

given by (

), however for
n\geq 3

(4.1)

\displaystyle e(T,z)=\sum_{t_{j}>0}|\phi_{j}(z)|^{2}\frac{1}{T}\int_{\sqrt{T}}^{T}\frac{h_{x}(t_{j})}{x^{(n-1)/2}}dx.

Applying local Weyl’s law for
{\hbox{L}^{2}}(\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}^{n})
and using estimate (
2.8
) we deduce that the principal series in (
4.1
) does not converge. For this reason, we need to smooth out the error term further. We do so by mollifying
e(T,z)
with an appropriately chosen family of test functions. We can now proceed to the proof of our first
\Omega
-result.

Proof.

(of Theorem
1.7
) Let
\psi\geq 0
be a smooth, even and compactly supported function in
[-1,1]
, such that

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\psi(x)\,e^{-itx}dx:=\hat{\psi}(t)\geq 0

and
\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\psi(x)dx=1
. We also define the family of functions
\psi_{\epsilon}(x)=\epsilon^{-1}\psi(x/\epsilon)
, for every
0<\epsilon<1
. We have
0\leq\hat{\psi}_{\epsilon}(t)\leq 1
and
\hat{\psi}_{\epsilon}(0)=1
.
Using integration by parts it is straightforward that

(4.2)

\hat{\psi}_{\epsilon}(t)=O_{k}\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon^{k}(1+|t|)^{k}}\right)

for any
k\in\mathbb{Z}
and
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
We mollify
e(T,z)
with the family
\{\psi_{\epsilon}\}
, i.e. we consider the convolution

\displaystyle e_{{\Gamma},\epsilon}^{*}(R,z):=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\psi_{\epsilon}(R-Y)\,e(e^{Y},z)dY.

Our
\Omega
-result for
e(T,z)
will follow from a
\Omega
-result for
e_{{\Gamma},\epsilon}^{*}(R,z)
. Applying the pre-trace formula and using expression (
2.7
) for the Selberg/Harish-Chandra transform we get the expansion

\displaystyle e_{{\Gamma},\epsilon}^{*}(R,z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{|t_{j}|>0}c_{n}|\phi_{j}(z)|^{2}\Re\left(\frac{{\Gamma}(it_{j})}{{\Gamma}\left(\frac{n+1}{2}+it_{j}\right)}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{\psi_{\epsilon}(R-Y)}{e^{Y}}\int_{e^{Y/2}}^{e^{Y}}x^{it_{j}}dxdY\right)

\displaystyle+O\left(\sum_{|t_{j}|>0}|\phi_{j}(z)|^{2}\Re\left(\frac{{\Gamma}(it_{j})}{{\Gamma}\left(\frac{n+1}{2}+it_{j}\right)}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{\psi_{\epsilon}(R-Y)}{e^{Y}}\int_{e^{Y/2}}^{e^{Y}}x^{it_{j}-2}dxdY\right)\right).

The compact support of
\psi(x)
implies that we integrate for
R\asymp Y
. Further, using the properties of
\psi(x)
, applying Stirling’s asymptotic formula and (
4.2
) we compute

(4.3)

\displaystyle e_{{\Gamma},\epsilon}^{*}(R,z)=

\displaystyle\sum_{|t_{j}|>0}c_{n}|\phi_{j}(z)|^{2}\hat{\psi}_{\epsilon}(t_{j})\Re\left(\frac{{\Gamma}(it_{j})}{{\Gamma}\left(\frac{n+1}{2}+it_{j}\right)(1+it_{j})}e^{it_{j}R}\right)

\displaystyle+O_{k}\left(e^{-R/2}\sum_{|t_{j}|>0}\frac{\epsilon^{-k}}{(1+|t_{j}|)^{\frac{n+3}{2}+k}}|\phi_{j}(z)|^{2}\right).

One can easily verify that for every
\epsilon>0
the principal series in (
4.3
) converges for
k>(n-3)/2
. For such a
k
and
for
A\gg 1
, we truncate the principal series in (
4.3
); the tail of the series in the main term for
t_{j}>A
is bounded by

\displaystyle\sum_{t_{j}>A}\frac{\hat{\psi}_{\epsilon}(t_{j})}{(1+|t_{j}|)^{\frac{n+3}{2}}}|\phi_{j}(z)|^{2}=O_{k}\left(\epsilon^{-k}A^{\frac{n-3}{2}-k}\right)

Similarly, the error term of (
4.3
) is bounded by
O_{k}\left(\epsilon^{-k}e^{-R/2}\right)
. The initial part of the series for
t_{j}\leq A
can be handled using the Dirichlet’s box principle.

Lemma 4.1

(Dirichlet’s box principle,
[
44
]
)
.

Let
r_{1},r_{2},...,r_{m}
be
m
distinct real numbers and
M>0
,
N>1
. Then, there is an
R
,
M\leq R\leq MN^{m}
, such that

\displaystyle|e^{ir_{j}R}-1|<\frac{1}{N}

for all
j=1,...,m
.

Applying Lemma
4.1
(Dirichlet’s box principle) in the initial part of (
4.3
) for a sufficiently large
N
(we will choose it later) and for
t_{j}\leq A
(hence
m\asymp A^{n}
) and using local Weyl’s law we find a large
R\leq N^{A^{n}}
such that

(4.4)

\displaystyle e_{{\Gamma},\epsilon}^{*}(R,z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{t_{j}\leq A}c_{n}|\phi_{j}(z)|^{2}\hat{\psi}_{\epsilon}(t_{j})\Re\left(\frac{{\Gamma}(it_{j})}{{\Gamma}\left(\frac{n+1}{2}+it_{j}\right)(1+it_{j})}\right)

\displaystyle+O_{k}\left(N^{-1}A^{\frac{n-3}{2}+o(1)}+\epsilon^{-k}A^{\frac{n-3}{2}-k}+\epsilon^{-k}e^{-R/2}\right),

(where for the first term in the error we simply use
|\hat{\psi}_{\epsilon}(t_{j})|\leq 1
).
Using the balance
N=A^{n/2}
and
\epsilon^{-k}=A^{k-\frac{n-3}{2}}
(then we get
\epsilon^{-k}\ll e^{R/2}
), the error term in (
4.4
) is bounded by
O(1)
. The bound
R\leq N^{A^{n}}
implies

(4.5)

\displaystyle\log R\ll n\epsilon^{-\frac{2kn}{2k-(n-3)}}\log(\epsilon^{-1}).

Further, there exists a
\tau\in(0,1)
such that
\hat{\psi}(x)\geq 1/2
whenever
|x|\leq\tau
. Using Stirling’s asymptotics and the relation
\hat{\psi}_{\epsilon}(t_{j})=\hat{\psi}(\epsilon t_{j})
, from (
4.4
) we deduce there are arbitrarily large values of
R
satisfying the bound

\displaystyle e_{{\Gamma},\epsilon}^{*}(R,z)\gg\sum_{t_{j}\leq\tau/\epsilon}\frac{|\phi_{j}(z)|^{2}}{(1+|t_{j}|)^{\frac{n+3}{2}}}.

Using local Weyl’s law and partial summation, we get for
n=3
the lower bound

\displaystyle\sum_{t_{j}\leq\tau/\epsilon}\frac{|\phi_{j}(z)|^{2}}{(1+|t_{j}|)^{\frac{n+3}{2}}}\gg\log(\epsilon^{-1}),

and the balance (
4.5
) gives
e_{{\Gamma},\epsilon}^{*}(R,z)=\Omega(\log\log R)
, hence
e(T,z)=\Omega(\log\log\log T)
. Similarly for
n\geq 4
we deduce

\displaystyle\sum_{t_{j}\leq\tau/\epsilon}\frac{|\phi_{j}(z)|^{2}}{(1+|t_{j}|)^{\frac{n+3}{2}}}\gg\epsilon^{-\frac{(n-3)}{2}}

and the balance (
4.5
) gives
e_{{\Gamma},\epsilon}^{*}(R,z)=\Omega\left((\log R)^{\frac{n-3}{2n}-\delta}\right)
for every
\delta>0
, hence
e(T,z)=\Omega\left((\log\log T)^{\frac{n-3}{2n}-\delta}\right)
.
∎

We can now prove the
\Omega
-result for the second moment of the normalized error term.

Proof.

(of Corollary
1.1
) This is an immediate application of Cauchy-Schwarz inequality: for any
n\geq 2
we get

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\sqrt{T}}^{T}\frac{E_{\Gamma}(x;z,z)}{x^{(n-1)/2}}dx\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\sqrt{T}}^{T}1dx\right)\left(\int_{\sqrt{T}}^{T}\left|\frac{E_{\Gamma}(x;z,z)}{x^{(n-1)/2}}\right|^{2}dx\right)

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle T\left(\int_{\sqrt{T}}^{T}\left|\frac{E_{\Gamma}(x;z,z)}{x^{(n-1)/2}}\right|^{2}dx\right),

hence

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{T}\int_{\sqrt{T}}^{T}\frac{E_{\Gamma}(x;z,z)}{x^{(n-1)/2}}dx\right)^{2}\ll\frac{1}{T}\int_{\sqrt{T}}^{T}\left|\frac{E_{\Gamma}(x;z,z)}{x^{(n-1)/2}}\right|^{2}dx\leq\frac{1}{T}\int_{2}^{T}\left|\frac{E_{\Gamma}(x;z,z)}{x^{(n-1)/2}}\right|^{2}dx.

The statement follows from Proposition
1.7
.
∎

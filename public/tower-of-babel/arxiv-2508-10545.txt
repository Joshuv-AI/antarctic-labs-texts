Homogeneous hypersurfaces of the four-dimensional Thurston geometry Sol 0 4 {\rm Sol_{0}^{4}} Thanks: Z. Yao is the corresponding author
Guoxin Wei and Zeke Yao and Xi Zhang

Abstract
. In this paper, we classify hypersurfaces with constant principal curvatures in the four-dimensional Thurston geometry Sol 0 4 {\rm Sol_{0}^{4}} under certain geometric assumptions. As an application of the classification result, we give a complete classification of homogeneous hypersurfaces in Sol 0 4 {\rm Sol_{0}^{4}} , which solves a problem raised by Erjavec and Inoguchi (Problem 6.4 of [J. Geom. Anal. 33, Art. 274, (2023)]).

Primary 53C42; Secondary 53C40, 53C30

1.
Introduction

A hypersurface
M
of a Riemannian manifold
\widetilde{M}
is called
homogeneous

if there exists a closed subgroup
G
of the isometry group
{\rm Iso}(\widetilde{M})

such that
M=G_{p}=\{g\cdot p\mid g\in G\}
for some point
p\in M
.
Homogeneous hypersurfaces have constant principal curvatures and
play a fundamental role in the study of isoparametric theory.
The classification of such hypersurfaces constitutes a classical problem in submanifold geometry.
Remarkably, the classification of homogeneous hypersurfaces up to isometric congruence is equivalent to the classification of cohomogeneity one actions up to orbit equivalence.
Here, an isometric action of a connected Lie group on

\widetilde{M}
is said to be of cohomogeneity one if it admits a cohomogeneity one orbit.
Two such actions on
\widetilde{M}
are orbit equivalent if there exists an isometry of

\widetilde{M}
that maps the orbits of one action onto those of the other.
Furthermore, cohomogeneity one actions serve as a powerful tool for constructing special geometric structures, such as Einstein metrics and metrics with special holonomies.

The classification of homogeneous hypersurfaces in Euclidean space and real hyperbolic space

\mathbb{R}H^{n}
follows from the classification of isoparametric hypersurfaces in these spaces, established by Seger
[
24
]
and Cartan
[
2
]
, respectively. For the unit sphere

\mathbb{S}^{n}
, Hsiang-Lawson
[
18
]
gave the classification of homogeneous hypersurfaces, while Takagi-Takahashi
[
27
]
computed their principal curvatures. Recently, Li-Ma-Wang-Wang
[
22
]
extended this work by classifying Möbius homogeneous hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{S}^{n}
, which include the classical homogeneous examples.
In the 1970s, Takagi
[
26
]
classified homogeneous hypersurfaces in complex projective space

\mathbb{C}P^{n}
, whereas the analogous classification for complex hyperbolic space

\mathbb{C}H^{n}
was completed much later by Berndt-Tamaru
[
1
]
. The four simply connected rank one symmetric spaces of compact type (
\mathbb{S}^{n}
,
\mathbb{C}P^{n}
, the quaternionic projective space
\mathbb{H}P^{n}
and the Cayley projective plane
\mathbb{O}P^{2}
) have been thoroughly studied, homogeneous hypersurfaces in

\mathbb{H}P^{n}
and
\mathbb{O}P^{2}
were classified in
[
4
,
19
,
20
]
. More broadly, Kollross
[
21
]
classified cohomogeneity one actions on irreducible symmetric spaces of compact type up to orbit equivalence.
For rank one symmetric spaces of noncompact type (
\mathbb{R}H^{n}
,

\mathbb{C}H^{n}
, the quaternionic hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}H^{n}

and the Cayley hyperbolic plane
\mathbb{O}H^{2}
), homogeneous hypersurfaces in

\mathbb{H}H^{n}
and
\mathbb{O}H^{2}
were classified in
[
9
,
10
]
. Additionally, Díaz-Ramos, Domínguez-Vázquez and Otero
[
8
]
developed a structural framework for cohomogeneity one actions on (not necessarily irreducible) symmetric spaces of noncompact type and arbitrary rank.
More recently, Solonenko and Sanmartín-López
[
25
]
completed the classification of isometric cohomogeneity one actions on all symmetric spaces of noncompact type
up to orbit equivalence.
In reducible symmetric spaces, there are also some studies on homogeneous hypersurfaces.
For example, Urbano
[
29
]
, Gao-Ma-Yao
[
16
]
, Domínguez-Vázquez and Manzano
[
12
]
,
de Lima and Pipoli
[
5
]

classified homogeneous hypersurfaces and isoparametric hypersurfaces of
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}
,
\mathbb{H}^{2}\times\mathbb{H}^{2}
,

\mathbb{S}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{H}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
(
n\geq 2
), respectively.
Gao-Ma-Yao
[
15
]
gave the classification of isoparametric hypersurfaces
in product space
M_{\kappa_{1}}^{2}\times M_{\kappa_{2}}^{2}
of two-dimensional space forms for

\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2}\in\{-1,0,1\}
with
\kappa_{1}\neq\kappa_{2}
.
For further developments on homogeneous hypersurfaces in symmetric spaces and advances in isoparametric theory, we refer to
[
3
,
9
,
17
]
.

In the latter half of the last century, Thurston
[
28
]
raised the well known geometrization conjecture: Every compact three-manifold has a canonical decomposed into pieces modeled on one of
eight distinct geometries. These fundamental geometries are:

\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
\mathbb{S}^{3}
,
\mathbb{H}^{3}
,

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
The geometric structure on
{\rm Sol_{0}^{4}}

In this subsection, we review some basic materials from
[
7
,
13
]
.
The underlying manifold of
{\rm Sol_{0}^{4}}
is the following
connected solvable Lie group

(2.1)

G:=\left\{(x,y,z,t):=\left.\left(\begin{matrix}e^{t}&0&0&x\\
0&e^{t}&0&y\\
0&0&e^{-2t}&z\\
0&0&0&1\\
\end{matrix}\right)\right|\ x,y,z,t\in\mathbb{R}\right\}.

Equivalently, the underlying manifold is

\mathbb{R}^{4}=\{(x,y,z,t)|\,x,y,z,t\in\mathbb{R}\}
with group operator

(2.2)

(\tilde{x},\tilde{y},\tilde{z},\tilde{t})\cdot(x,y,z,t)=(\tilde{x}+e^{\tilde{t}}x,\tilde{y}+e^{\tilde{t}}y,\tilde{z}+e^{-2\tilde{t}}z,\tilde{t}+t).

Here, we use
(x,y,z,t)
to denote Cartesian coordinates on
\mathbb{R}^{4}
.
The inverse of
(x,y,z,t)
is given by

(x,y,z,t)^{-1}=(-e^{-t}x,-e^{-t}y,-e^{2t}z,-t).

At any
p=(x,y,z,t)\in{\rm Sol_{0}^{4}}
, there are four left invariant vector fields defined by

(2.3)

\displaystyle E_{1}=e^{t}\tfrac{\partial}{\partial x}=(e^{t},0,0,0),\ \ E_{2}=e^{t}\tfrac{\partial}{\partial y}=(0,e^{t},0,0),

\displaystyle E_{3}=e^{-2t}\tfrac{\partial}{\partial z}=(0,0,e^{-2t},0),\ \ E_{4}=\tfrac{\partial}{\partial t}=(0,0,0,1).

Then one can check the following commutation relations:

(2.4)

\displaystyle[E_{1},E_{2}]=[E_{1},E_{3}]=[E_{2},E_{3}]=0,

\displaystyle[E_{1},E_{4}]=-E_{1},\ \ [E_{2},E_{4}]=-E_{2},\ \ [E_{3},E_{4}]=2E_{3}.

The Riemannian metric
g
on
{\rm Sol_{0}^{4}}
is determined by

g=e^{-2t}(dx^{2}+dy^{2})+e^{4t}dz^{2}+dt^{2}.

Then the frame field
\{E_{i}\}_{i=1}^{4}

is orthonormal with respect to
g
.

According to
[
6
,
7
,
30
]
, the isometry group
{\rm Iso(Sol_{0}^{4})}
of
{\rm Sol_{0}^{4}}
is
the semi-direct product
{\rm Sol_{0}^{4}}\rtimes({\rm O(2)}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2})
,
where
{\rm Sol_{0}^{4}}
acts on itself by left
translations,
{\rm O(2)}
acts by rotations and
reflections in the
xy
-plane and
\mathbb{Z}_{2}

acts by reflections in the
z
-coordinate. Moreover,
the identity component of the isometry group of
{\rm Sol_{0}^{4}}
is given by

(2.5)

{\rm Sol_{0}^{4}}\rtimes{\rm SO(2)}=\left\{\left.\left(\begin{matrix}e^{t}\cos\theta&-e^{t}\sin\theta&0&x\\
e^{t}\sin\theta&e^{t}\cos\theta&0&y\\
0&0&e^{-2t}&z\\
0&0&0&1\end{matrix}\right)\right|x,\,y,\,z,\,t\in\mathbb{R},\,0\leq\theta<2\pi\right\}.

Let
\tilde{\nabla}
be the Levi-Civita connection of
g
.
By using (
2.4
) and Koszul’s formula, we obtain

(2.6)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \tilde{\nabla}_{E_{1}}E_{1}&=E_{4},\ \ \tilde{\nabla}_{E_{1}}E_{2}=0,\ \ \tilde{\nabla}_{E_{1}}E_{3}=0,\ \ \tilde{\nabla}_{E_{1}}E_{4}=-E_{1},\\
\tilde{\nabla}_{E_{2}}E_{1}&=0,\ \ \tilde{\nabla}_{E_{2}}E_{2}=E_{4},\ \ \tilde{\nabla}_{E_{2}}E_{3}=0,\ \ \tilde{\nabla}_{E_{2}}E_{4}=-E_{2},\\
\tilde{\nabla}_{E_{3}}E_{1}&=0,\ \ \tilde{\nabla}_{E_{3}}E_{2}=0,\ \ \tilde{\nabla}_{E_{3}}E_{3}=-2E_{4},\ \ \tilde{\nabla}_{E_{3}}E_{4}=2E_{3},\\
\tilde{\nabla}_{E_{4}}E_{1}&=0,\ \ \tilde{\nabla}_{E_{4}}E_{2}=0,\ \ \tilde{\nabla}_{E_{4}}E_{3}=0,\ \ \tilde{\nabla}_{E_{4}}E_{4}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

On
{\rm Sol_{0}^{4}}
, there are two complex structures defined by

(2.7)

\left\{\begin{aligned} J_{1}E_{1}&=E_{2},\ \ J_{1}E_{2}=-E_{1},\ \ J_{1}E_{3}=E_{4},\ \ J_{1}E_{4}=-E_{3},\\
J_{2}E_{1}&=E_{2},\ \ J_{2}E_{2}=-E_{1},\ \ J_{2}E_{3}=-E_{4},\ \ J_{2}E_{4}=E_{3},\end{aligned}\right.

which are compatible and integrable with respect to
g
.
Note that, none of these structures is Kähler.
Besides, we have also an important tensor
P
on
{\rm Sol_{0}^{4}}
defined by

(2.8)

PX=g(X,E_{4})E_{4},\ \ \forall X\in T({\rm Sol_{0}^{4}}).

The covariant derivatives of
J_{1}
,
J_{2}
and
P
are given by:

(2.9)

\displaystyle G_{1}(X,Y):=(\tilde{\nabla}_{X}J_{1})Y=-g(J_{1}Y,E_{4})X+g(Y,E_{4})J_{1}X+g(J_{1}Y,X)E_{4}-g(Y,X)J_{1}E_{4},

(2.10)

\displaystyle G_{2}(X,Y):=(\tilde{\nabla}_{X}J_{2})Y=-g(J_{2}Y,E_{4})X+g(Y,E_{4})J_{2}X+g(J_{2}Y,X)E_{4}-g(Y,X)J_{2}E_{4},

(2.11)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} G_{3}(X,Y):=(\tilde{\nabla}_{X}P)Y=&\tfrac{1}{2}\big(g(Y,X)E_{4}+g(Y,E_{4})X\big)-4g(Y,PX)E_{4}\\
&+\tfrac{3}{2}\big(g(Y,J_{1}J_{2}X)E_{4}+g(Y,E_{4})J_{1}J_{2}X\big),\ \ \forall X,Y\in T({\rm Sol_{0}^{4}}).\end{aligned}

We also consider the
4
-dimensional Euclidean space
(\mathbb{R}^{4},g^{E})
, where
g^{E}
is the Euclidean metric.
Let
\tilde{\nabla}^{E}
be the usual Euclidean connection, with
respect to the frame
\{E_{i}\}_{i=1}^{4}
, we get

(2.12)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \tilde{\nabla}^{E}_{E_{i}}E_{j}&=0,\ \ 1\leq i\leq 3,\ \ 1\leq j\leq 4,\\
\tilde{\nabla}^{E}_{E_{4}}E_{1}&=E_{1},\ \ \tilde{\nabla}^{E}_{E_{4}}E_{2}=E_{2},\ \ \tilde{\nabla}^{E}_{E_{4}}E_{3}=-2E_{3},\ \ \tilde{\nabla}^{E}_{E_{4}}E_{4}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

Now, on
\mathbb{R}^{4}
, with respect to the connections
\tilde{\nabla}^{E}
and
\tilde{\nabla}
, we define the difference tensor
K
by

K(X,Y):=\tilde{\nabla}^{E}_{X}Y-\tilde{\nabla}_{X}Y,\ \ \forall X,Y\in T\mathbb{R}^{4}.

Then we have the following Lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

On
\mathbb{R}^{4}
, the difference tensor
K
is given by

(2.13)

\displaystyle K(X,Y)

\displaystyle=-J_{1}G_{3}(X,J_{1}PY)-J_{2}G_{3}(X,J_{2}PY)+J_{1}PJ_{1}G_{3}(Y,X)

\displaystyle+\tfrac{1}{2}\big[G_{1}(X,J_{1}Y)+G_{2}(X,J_{2}Y)+J_{1}G_{3}(X,J_{1}Y)+J_{2}G_{3}(X,J_{2}Y)

\displaystyle-J_{1}G_{1}(Y,PX)-J_{1}G_{2}(Y,PX)\big].

Proof.

Note that both sides of the expression (
2.13
) are tensorial.
Then under the frame
\{E_{i}\}_{i=1}^{4}
, by using the equations (
2.6
)–(
2.12
),
one can directly verify that the equation (
2.13
) holds.
∎

Finally, the Riemannian curvature tensor
\tilde{R}
of
{\rm Sol_{0}^{4}}

with the Riemannian metric
g
is given by

(2.14)

\displaystyle\tilde{R}(X,Y)Z=

\displaystyle 2\Big(g(Y,Z)X-g(X,Z)Y\Big)

\displaystyle-\tfrac{1}{2}\Big(g(J_{1}Y,Z)J_{1}X-g(J_{1}X,Z)J_{1}Y+2g(X,J_{1}Y)J_{1}Z\Big)

\displaystyle-\tfrac{1}{2}\Big(g(J_{2}Y,Z)J_{2}X-g(J_{2}X,Z)J_{2}Y+2g(X,J_{2}Y)J_{2}Z\Big)

\displaystyle-3\Big(g(PY,Z)X+g(Y,Z)PX-g(PX,Z)Y-g(X,Z)PY\Big),

where
X,Y,Z\in T({\rm Sol_{0}^{4}})
.

2.2.
Hypersurfaces of
{\rm Sol_{0}^{4}}

Let
M
be a hypersurface of
{\rm Sol_{0}^{4}}
with
N
a unit normal
vector field and still
g
the induced metric on
M
. We put

N=aE_{1}+bE_{2}+cE_{3}+dE_{4},

where
\{E_{i}\}_{i=1}^{4}
is defined by (
2.3
), and

a,b,c,d
are called angle functions satisfying

a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}+d^{2}=1
. Then it follows that

(2.15)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &T_{1}=bE_{1}-aE_{2}+dE_{3}-cE_{4},\\
&T_{2}=cE_{1}-dE_{2}-aE_{3}+bE_{4},\\
&T_{3}=dE_{1}+cE_{2}-bE_{3}-aE_{4},\\
\end{aligned}\right.

forms an orthonormal frame field of
M
.

Let
\nabla
be the induced connection on
M
. The Gauss and Weingarten formulae state that

\tilde{\nabla}_{X}Y=\nabla_{X}Y+g(AX,Y)N,\ \ \tilde{\nabla}_{X}N=-AX,\ \ \forall\ X,Y\in TM,

where
A
is the shape operator of
M
.

Let
R
denote the Riemannian curvature tensor of
M
. The Gauss and Codazzi equations
of
M
are given by

(2.16)

\displaystyle R(X,Y)Z=

\displaystyle 2\Big(g(Y,Z)X-g(X,Z)Y\Big)

\displaystyle-\tfrac{1}{2}\Big(g(J_{1}Y,Z)(J_{1}X)^{\top}-g(J_{1}X,Z)(J_{1}Y)^{\top}+2g(X,J_{1}Y)(J_{1}Z)^{\top}\Big)

\displaystyle-\tfrac{1}{2}\Big(g(J_{2}Y,Z)(J_{2}X)^{\top}-g(J_{2}X,Z)(J_{2}Y)^{\top}+2g(X,J_{2}Y)(J_{2}Z)^{\top}\Big)

\displaystyle-3\Big(g(PY,Z)X+g(Y,Z)(PX)^{\top}-g(PX,Z)Y-g(X,Z)(PY)^{\top}\Big)

\displaystyle+g(AY,Z)AX-g(AX,Z)AY,

(2.17)

\displaystyle g((\nabla_{X}A)

\displaystyle Y-(\nabla_{Y}A)X,Z)

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{1}{2}\Big(g(J_{1}Y,Z)g(J_{1}X,N)-g(J_{1}X,Z)g(J_{1}Y,N)+2g(X,J_{1}Y)g(J_{1}Z,N)\Big)

\displaystyle-\tfrac{1}{2}\Big(g(J_{2}Y,Z)g(J_{2}X,N)-g(J_{2}X,Z)g(J_{2}Y,N)+2g(X,J_{2}Y)g(J_{2}Z,N)\Big)

\displaystyle-3\Big(g(Y,Z)g(PX,N)-g(X,Z)g(PY,N)\Big),

where
X,Y,Z\in TM
, and
\cdot^{\top}
means the tangential part.

3.
Examples of homogeneous hypersurfaces of
{\rm Sol_{0}^{4}}

In this section, we introduce some canonical examples of hypersurfaces in
{\rm Sol_{0}^{4}}

which are characterized by Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
.

Example 3.1
.

For any given
r>0
, we define the hypersurface

M_{1,r}:=\{(e^{x_{3}}\cos x_{1}\tanh r,e^{x_{3}}\sin x_{1}\tanh r,x_{2},{x_{3}}+\ln({\rm sech}\,r))\in{\rm Sol_{0}^{4}}\mid x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}\in\mathbb{R}\}.

Let
H_{1}:=\{(0,0,z,t)\in{\rm Sol_{0}^{4}}\mid z,t\in\mathbb{R}\}\rtimes{\rm SO(2)}
.
Then for
M_{1,r}
, we have the following proposition.

Proposition 3.1
.

As a hypersurface of
{\rm Sol_{0}^{4}}
,

M_{1,r}
has the following properties:

(1)

The unit normal vector field is given by

(3.1)

N=-\cos{x_{1}}\,{\rm sech}\,rE_{1}-\sin{x_{1}}\,{\rm sech}\,rE_{2}+\tanh rE_{4},

where
\{E_{i}\}_{i=1}^{4}
is defined by (
2.3
);

(2)

It has three distinct constant principal curvatures
\coth r
,
\tanh r
and
-2\tanh r
;

(3)

It is an orbit of subgroup
H_{1}
which passes through the point

(\tanh r,0,0,\ln({\rm sech}\,r))
.
Therefore
M_{1,r}
is a homogeneous hypersurface;

(4)

It is a tube of radius
r>0
over the focal manifold

\{(0,0,z,t)\in{\rm Sol_{0}^{4}}\mid z,t\in\mathbb{R}\}
.

Proof.

First, at any
p\in M_{1,r}
, we consider the following frame field

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let

{\rm Sol_{0}^{4}}
. Up to isometries of

,

{\rm Sol_{0}^{4}}\rtimes{\rm SO(2)}

of the isometry group
{\rm Iso(Sol_{0}^{4})}
. For any given
\phi\in{\rm Sol_{0}^{4}}\rtimes{\rm SO(2)}
,
there is an isometric map

\phi:{\rm Sol_{0}^{4}}\rightarrow{\rm Sol_{0}^{4}}

. By (
2.3
) and (
2.5
), it can be checked that

From the assumption that

,

\phi\in{\rm Sol_{0}^{4}}\rtimes{\rm SO(2)}

\phi\left(M\right)=M

\phi\left(p_{0}\right)=p

It follows that
N_{p}=\pm d\phi_{p_{0}}\left(N_{p_{0}}\right)

\displaystyle c(p)

\displaystyle=g(N_{p},E_{3}|_{p})=g(\pm d\phi_{p_{0}}\left(N_{p_{0}}\right),d\phi_{p_{0}}(E_{3}|_{p_{0}}))=\pm c\left(p_{0}\right),

\displaystyle d(p)

\displaystyle=g(N_{p},E_{4}|_{p})=g(\pm d\phi_{p_{0}}\left(N_{p_{0}}\right),d\phi_{p_{0}}(E_{4}|_{p_{0}}))=\pm d\left(p_{0}\right).

By the fact that

,

In conclusion, we have completed the proof of Theorem

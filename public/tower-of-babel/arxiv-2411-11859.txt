Introduction

For
n=1,2,3,4
, the explicit formulas for the sum of powers of natural numbers

S_{n}=\sum_{x=1}^{m}x^{n}

were well-known already in the ancient times. In 1613, Faulhaber published a paper, where some properties of the function
S_{n}(m)
were studied and, moreover, the explicit formulas for high odd values of
n
were given
[
4
]
. Nowadays, for calculating
S_{n}
, there are various recurrent formulas, geometric and matrix methods, as well as formulas that are valid for arbitrary
n
, but contain Bernoulli numbers (see, e.g.,
[

]
,

). In
[

, the explicit formulas for
S_{n}
are given for the cases
n=1,2,...,7
.

In the present work, we give an explicit formula for
S_{n}
, for an arbitrary natural
n
; this formula does not contain Bernoulli numbers. To this end, we first find the explicit formula for the function

g(m)=\sum_{x=1}^{m}f(x),

where
f(x)
is a polynomial. The found formula implies that
g(m)
is a polynomial and is divisible by
m
.

Applying again the found formula for
g(m)
, the explicit formula for the sum of the
n
th powers of natural numbers is obtained:

\sum_{x=1}^{m}x^{n}=(-1)^{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}m(m+1)(m+2)...(m+i),

(1.1)

where

a_{i}=\frac{1}{i+1}\sum_{k=1}^{i}\frac{(-1)^{k}k^{n}}{k!(i-k)!},

(1.2)

(i=1,2,...,n)
. Note that this formula does not contain Bernoulli numbers. Note also that, for the same sum, the following formula was given without proof in our earlier paper
[
10
]
:

\sum_{x=1}^{m}x^{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{i}\frac{(-1)^{k+n}k^{n}m(m+1)(m+2)...(m+i)C_{i}^{k}}{(i+1)!},

where
C_{i}^{k}
denotes the binomial coefficient
{\begin{pmatrix}i\\
k\\
\end{pmatrix}}
.

Taking the fact that the principal term of the polynomial

S_{n}(m)=\sum_{x=1}^{m}x^{n}

is
\frac{1}{n+1}m^{n+1}

[
8

,
[
1
]
, the calculation of
a_{n}
can be simplified. Namely, we obtain that

a_{n}=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n+1}.

(1.3)

The above-mentioned formulas imply that if
n\geq 3
, then

\sum_{x=1}^{m}x^{n}=(-1)^{n}m(m+1)(-\frac{1}{2}+\sum_{i=2}^{n}a_{i}(m+2)(m+3)...(m+i)),

where
a_{i}
is given by (1.2), for
i=2,3,...,n-1
, while
a_{n}
is given by (1.3).

2.
The sum of powers of natural numbers

Below we give the formula for the sum of the values of a polynomial
f(x)
in the points
x=1,2,...,m
. To this end, we first give the following lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

If the degree of a polynomial
f(x)
is less than or equal to
n
, then the following equality holds:

f(x)=f(0)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}x(x+1)(x+2)...(x+i-1),

(2.1)

where

d_{i}=\sum_{k=o}^{i}\frac{(-1)^{k}f(-k)}{k!(i-k)!}\;\;\;\;(i=1,2,...,n).

(2.2)

Proof.

Consider the polynomial

h(x)=l_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}l_{i}x(x+1)(x+2)...(x+i-1),

(2.3)

and choose coefficients
l_{0}
,
l_{1}
, …,
l_{n}
in such a way that the values of the polynomials
f(x)
and
h(x)
coincide in the
(n+1)
points:

f(-j)=h(-j)\;\;\;\;(j=0,1,2,...,n).

Then

f(x)\equiv h(x),

whence

f(-j)=l_{0}-l_{1}j+l_{2}j(j-1)+...+(-1)^{j}l_{j}j(j-1)(j-2)\cdot...\cdot 2\cdot 1.

(2.4)

(j=0,1,2,...,n)
.
Applying the well-known formula

C_{k}^{0}-C_{k}^{1}+C_{k}^{2}+...+(-1)^{k}C_{k}^{k}=0,

(
k\in\mathbb{N}
) where
C_{i}^{j}
denotes the binomial coefficient
{\begin{pmatrix}j\\
i\\
\end{pmatrix}}
, it is not hard to verify that the solution of the system (2.4) is

l_{0}=f(0),\;\;\;l_{i}=\sum_{k=0}^{i}\frac{(-1)^{k}f(-k)}{k!(i-k)!}\;\;\;(i=1,2,...,n).

This implies formulas (2.1) and (2.2).
∎

Consider the function

g(m)=\sum_{x=1}^{m}f(x).

As is well-known
[
13
]
,

\sum_{x=1}^{m}x(x+1)(x+2)...(x+i-1)=\frac{1}{i+1}m(m+1)(m+2)...(m+i).

(2.5)

Therefore,
Lemma 2.1 implies

Theorem 2.2
.

\sum_{x=1}^{m}f(x)=mf(0)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{d_{i}}{i+1}m(m+1)(m+2)...(m+i),

(2.6)

where
d_{i}
(
i=1,2,...,n)
is given by formula (2.2).

Theorem 2.2 implies

Corollary 2.3
.

The function
g(m)
is a polynomial, and is divisible by
m
.

Corollary 2.3 implies the well-known fact that the function
S_{n}(m)=\sum_{x=1}^{m}x^{n}
is a polynomial
[
4
]
.

Theorem 2.2 implies the explicit formula for
S_{n}(m)
, for any natural
n
. Note that it does not contain Bernoulli numbers.

Theorem 2.4
.

For any natural
n
, one has the equality

\sum_{x=1}^{m}x^{n}=(-1)^{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}m(m+1)(m+2)...(m+i),

(2.7)

where

a_{i}=\frac{1}{i+1}\sum_{k=1}^{i}\frac{(-1)^{k}k^{n}}{k!(i-k)!},

(2.8)

(i=1,2,...,n)
.

From formula (2.7) it follows the well-known fact that
the polynomial

S_{n}(m)=\sum_{x=1}^{m}x^{n}

is divisible by
m(m+1)
, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}

[
4
]
.

Formula (2.8) implies that

a_{1}=-\frac{1}{2}.

(2.9)

Taking formula (2.7) and the fact that the principal term of the polynomial
S_{n}(m)
is
\frac{1}{n+1}m^{n+1}

[
8
]
,
[
1
]
, the calculation of
a_{n}
can be simplified. Namely, we obtain that

a_{n}=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n+1}.

(2.10)

From Theorem 2.4, formulas (2.9) and (2.10), we obtain

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
n\geq 3
. Then one has

\sum_{x=1}^{m}x^{n}=(-1)^{n}m(m+1)(-\frac{1}{2}+\sum_{i=2}^{n}a_{i}(m+2)(m+3)...(m+i)),

where
a_{i}
is given by formula (2.8) for
i=2,3,...,n-1
, while
a_{n}
is given by (2.10).

Finally note that since formula (2.8) implies that

a_{n}=\frac{1}{(n+1)!}\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k}C_{n}^{k}k^{n},

formula (2.10) implies the following combinatorial identity.

Proposition 2.6
.

For any natural number
n
, one has

\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k}C_{n}^{k}k^{n}=(-1)^{n}n!.

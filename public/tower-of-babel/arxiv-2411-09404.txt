Dirac Operators, Dirac Cohomology and Unitarity for A ⁡ ( m | n ) A(m|n)
Steffen Schmidt

Abstract
. Dirac operators and Dirac cohomology for Lie superalgebras of Riemannian type, introduced by Huang and Pandžić, provide an effective tool for the study of unitarizable supermodules. In this article, we study these objects for Lie superalgebras of type A A and relate them systematically to unitarity. In the first part, we establish the basic structure of the theory in this setting. We relate unitarity to the Dirac operator, derive the corresponding Dirac inequality, and show that Dirac cohomology determines unitarizable supermodules. We also determine explicitly the Dirac cohomology of unitarizable simple supermodules. In the second part, we turn to applications. We obtain a new characterization of unitarity, establish a relation with Kostant’s cohomology, derive a formula for formal characters, and introduce a Dirac index.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Vue d’ensemble

Dirac operators entered representation theory through the work of Parthasarathy and Vogan. Parthasarathy used them in the construction of discrete series representations of semisimple Lie groups
[
3
,
37
]
, while Vogan introduced Dirac cohomology for
({\mathfrak{g}},K)
-modules and conjectured that it determines the infinitesimal character. This conjecture was proved by Huang and Pandžić
[
18
]
.

Since then, Dirac cohomology has become an important invariant in representation theory, with strong links to other cohomological theories. For highest weight modules it is closely related to
\mathfrak{n}
-cohomology, and for Vogan–Zuckerman modules
A_{\mathfrak{q}}(\lambda)
it is closely related to
({\mathfrak{g}},K)
-cohomology
[
15
,
16
,
35
]
. At the same time, it is often computationally more accessible than
({\mathfrak{g}},K)
-cohomology while retaining substantial information about the representation.

In
[
20
]
, Huang and Pandžić extended the theory of Dirac operators and Dirac cohomology to Lie superalgebras of Riemannian type. Their construction requires a non-degenerate invariant supersymmetric bilinear form
B
on
{\mathfrak{g}}
and a decomposition of
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}
into complementary Lagrangian subspaces. Under these assumptions, one obtains a natural Dirac operator
\operatorname{D}
on tensor products of
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules with the oscillator module
M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
for the Weyl algebra of
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}
, whose square is the sum of two quadratic Casimir operators and a constant. The associated Dirac cohomology of a
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule
M
is defined by

(1.1)

\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}(M)\coloneqq\ker\operatorname{D}/(\ker\operatorname{D}\cap\operatorname{Im}\operatorname{D}).

Huang and Pandžić proved a superalgebra analogue of Vogan’s conjecture: if
\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}(M)\neq 0
, then
\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}(M)
determines the infinitesimal character of
M

[
19
]
.

An important link with other cohomological invariants was established in
[
8
]
, where Dirac cohomology was related to Kostant’s
{\mathfrak{g}}_{+1}
-cohomology for finite-dimensional
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules, assuming that
{\mathfrak{g}}
admits a
\mathbb{Z}
-grading compatible with the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-grading. For unitarizable
{\mathfrak{gl}}(m|n)
-supermodules, Xiao later showed that Dirac cohomology is, up to a twist, isomorphic to
\mathrm{H}^{*}({\mathfrak{g}}_{+1},M)

[
42
]
.

Explicit computations of Dirac cohomology in the super setting are still missing, and its relation to unitarity has not been investigated systematically. In this article, we address both problems for Lie superalgebras of type
A
, denoted by
{\mathfrak{g}}=A(m-1|n-1)
: we compute Dirac cohomology explicitly and relate it to unitarity.

1.2.
Results

The results are divided into two parts. The first develops the structural relations between unitarity, the Dirac operator, and Dirac cohomology. The second gives explicit applications, including a new characterization of unitarity, relations with Kostant’s cohomology, the construction of a Dirac index and explicit formulas for the formal character.

We begin with the first part. We realize the Dirac operator
\operatorname{D}
of Huang and Pandžić as a distinguished element of the algebra of
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-invariants
\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}})
in the quantum Weil algebra

{\mathcal{W}}({\mathfrak{g}})\coloneqq{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes{\operatorname{Cl}}({\mathfrak{g}}).

This construction extends to arbitrary quadratic Lie superalgebras and provides a general framework for the study of
\operatorname{D}
. In particular, viewed as an element of
{\mathcal{W}}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}})={\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes{\operatorname{Cl}}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
, the Dirac operator
\operatorname{D}
acts naturally on
M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
, where
M
is a
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule and
M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
denotes the simple
{\operatorname{Cl}}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
-module, that is, the oscillator module.

In this article, we are primarily interested in unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules, that is,
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules
M
admitting a positive definite Hermitian form with respect to which the action of
{\mathfrak{g}}
is contravariant for some conjugate-linear anti-involution
\omega
. Such anti-involutions are in bijection with real forms of
{\mathfrak{g}}
. For Lie superalgebras of type
A
, non-trivial simple unitarizable supermodules exist only for a restricted class of real forms, and in the cases considered here they are necessarily highest weight supermodules.

To relate unitarity to the Dirac operator, one must work with a basis adapted not to a
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-compatible
{\mathbb{Z}}
-grading, but to the real form defining the unitary structure. More precisely, for every real form admitting non-trivial unitarizable supermodules, we construct a basis of
{\mathfrak{g}}
compatible both with the even non-degenerate supersymmetric invariant bilinear form
B
and with the conjugate-linear anti-involution
\omega
. This basis is naturally adapted to the corresponding maximal compact subalgebra. In terms of it, the Hermitian structure on a supermodule and the Dirac operator become directly comparable, and one obtains the following characterization of contravariance (
cf.
Theorem
61
).

Theorem 1
.

Let
M
be a simple
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule equipped with a positive definite Hermitian form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{M}
, such that
M_{\bar{0}}
and
M_{\bar{1}}
are mutually orthogonal. Assume that
M
is
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-semisimple. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

(M,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{M})
is a unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule.

(ii)

The Dirac operator
\operatorname{D}
is selfadjoint with respect to
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}
.

As a direct consequence of selfadjointness, one obtains a Dirac inequality, which becomes a basic tool in the study of unitarizable supermodules. The following combines Proposition
60
and Proposition
74
from the main text.

Proposition 2
.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}
be a unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule. Then

\langle\operatorname{D}^{2}v,v\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}\geq 0\qquad\text{for all }v\in{\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}).

In particular, if
{\mathcal{H}}
is simple of highest weight
\Lambda
, and if
V
is a
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-highest weight submodule of
{\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
with highest weight
\mu
, then

(\mu+2\rho,\mu)\geq(\Lambda+2\rho,\Lambda).

Turning to Dirac cohomology, one observes that, for general supermodules, it does not admit an adjoint functor, although it satisfies a six-term exact sequence.
A modified Dirac cohomology is therefore introduced; it admits a right adjoint and agrees with the usual Dirac cohomology on unitarizable supermodules.

For unitarizable supermodules, the Dirac cohomology simplifies to

\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}(M)=\operatorname{ker}\operatorname{D}.

Moreover, if
M
is a simple unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule, then
\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}(M)
is a unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-module and decomposes as a direct sum of simple unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-modules (neglecting parity). We show that these
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-modules are precisely those arising from the relative holomorphic discrete series of the universal covering group associated with
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
. Combined with the superalgebra analogue of Vogan’s conjecture, this leads to an explicit determination of the Dirac cohomology of all unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules. The following statements correspond to Theorem
76
and Theorem
80
in the main text.

Theorem 3
.

Let
L(\Lambda)
be a non-trivial unitarizable highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule with highest weight
\Lambda
. Then

\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}(L(\Lambda))\cong L_{0}(\Lambda-\rho_{\bar{1}}).

Consequently, two unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules
{\mathcal{H}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{H}}_{2}
are equivalent if and only if

\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}({\mathcal{H}}_{1})\cong\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}({\mathcal{H}}_{2})
as
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}
-modules.

We extend this result to the Dirac cohomology of highest weight supermodules, which possess Jordan–Hölder filtrations with unitarizable quotients.

The second part of this article concerns several applications of Dirac operators and Dirac cohomology. The first result gives a criterion for unitarity in terms of the
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-structure of a highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule. This is non-trivial in both directions: semisimplicity over
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
with unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-constituents does not by itself imply unitarity, and induction from
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
to
{\mathfrak{g}}
does not in general preserve it. The missing condition is provided by the Dirac inequality.

Indeed, if
M
is a highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule of highest weight
\Lambda
, then
M
admits a
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-filtration with simple highest weight subquotients
L_{0}(\Lambda-\gamma)
, where
\gamma
is a sum of distinct positive odd roots. In the unitarizable case,
M
is completely reducible over
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
, and the Dirac inequality is strict on every non-trivial
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-constituent. This suggests that unitarity should be characterized by the unitarizability of the highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-constituent together with the strict Dirac inequality on all remaining
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-composition factors. We show that this is indeed the case. The following is Theorem
86
in the main text.

Theorem 4
.

Let
M
be a highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule with highest weight
\Lambda
. Then
M
is unitarizable if and only if the following two conditions are satisfied:

a)

\Lambda
is the highest weight of a unitarizable highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-module.

b)

If
L_{0}(\mu)
is a simple
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-composition factor in a
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-filtration of
M
with highest weight
\mu
, then

(\mu+2\rho,\mu)>(\Lambda+2\rho,\Lambda).

Next, we relate Dirac cohomology to Kostant’s cohomology. The selfadjointness condition for the Dirac operator fixes a positive system on
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}
. As a consequence, Kostant’s cohomology is naturally defined only as a module over the maximal compact subalgebra
{\mathfrak{k}}
of the even part of the chosen real form. In this setting, we obtain the following comparison theorem which is Theorem
90
in the main text.

Theorem 5
.

For any unitarizable simple
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule
{\mathcal{H}}
, there is a
{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}
-module isomorphism

\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}({\mathcal{H}})\cong\mathrm{H}^{\ast}({\mathfrak{g}}_{+1},{\mathcal{H}})\otimes{\mathbb{C}}_{-\rho_{\bar{1}}}.

We then consider the Euler characteristic of Dirac cohomology.
For this purpose, we introduce the
Dirac index
of a
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule
M
, defined as the virtual
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}
-module

(1.2)

\operatorname{I}(M)\coloneqq M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}})_{\bar{0}}-M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}})_{\bar{1}}.

The Dirac index coincides with the Euler characteristic of Dirac cohomology (
cf.
Proposition
91
),
i.e.
,

(1.3)

\operatorname{I}(M)=\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}^{+}(M)-\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}^{-}(M).

Moreover, it commutes with tensoring by finite-dimensional
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules, in analogy with the classical case.

Finally, we obtain two formulas for the formal
{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}
-character. For unitarizable simple supermodules, the first is obtained from Kostant cohomology. For
\operatorname{D}^{2}
-semisimple Harish-Chandra supermodules
{\mathcal{H}}
, that is, those satisfying

(1.4)

{\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})\cong\operatorname{ker}\operatorname{D}^{2}\oplus\operatorname{im}\operatorname{D}^{2},

the second is obtained from the Dirac index. The main results are Theorems
94
and
97
.

Theorem 6
.

Let
F^{\nu}
be the simple
{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}
-module of highest weight
\nu\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}
, and set
N(\mu)\coloneqq\bigwedge({\mathfrak{n}}_{\bar{1}}^{-})\otimes F^{\mu}
.

a)

Let
{\mathcal{H}}
be a unitarizable simple
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule. Then

\operatorname{ch}_{{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}}({\mathcal{H}})=\sum_{\mu}\sum_{k\geq 0}(-1)^{k}[\mathrm{H}^{k}({\mathfrak{g}}_{+1},{\mathcal{H}}):F^{\mu}]\,\operatorname{ch}_{{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}}\bigl(N(\mu)\bigr).

b)

Let
{\mathcal{H}}
be a
\operatorname{D}^{2}
-semisimple Harish-Chandra supermodule. If
\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}^{+}({\mathcal{H}})=\sum_{\mu}F^{\mu}
and
\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}^{-}({\mathcal{H}})=\sum_{\nu}F^{\nu}
, then

\operatorname{ch}_{{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}}({\mathcal{H}})=\sum_{\mu}\operatorname{ch}_{{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}}\bigl(N(\mu+\rho_{\bar{1}})\bigr)-\sum_{\nu}\operatorname{ch}_{{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}}\bigl(N(\nu+\rho_{\bar{1}})\bigr).

1.3.
Structure of the paper

Section
2
introduces the structure theory of Lie superalgebras
A(m|n)
and their real forms
{\mathfrak{su}}(p,q|r,s)/\mathfrak{psu}(p,q|r,s)
.

Section
3
introduces unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules and analyzes the consequences of unitarity.
Harish-Chandra supermodules are defined, and it is shown that every unitarizable highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule arises as a quotient of a Verma supermodule, leading to the Shapovalov form as the canonical contravariant Hermitian form.
We further examine the
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}
-constituents of unitarizable highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules and introduce a distinguished subclass, the relative holomorphic discrete series modules.

Section
4
provides an overview of Dirac operators and Dirac cohomology in the language of the quantum Weil algebra and constructs a Dirac induction functor that is left adjoint to Dirac cohomology on unitarizable supermodules.

Section
5
examines the interaction between Dirac operators, Dirac cohomology, and unitarity.
In particular, the Dirac operator is shown to detect unitarity, and the Dirac cohomology of unitarizable simple
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules is computed.

Finally, Section
6
discusses further applications of the Dirac operator and Dirac cohomology.
These include a new characterization of unitarity, a relation with Kostant’s cohomology, the introduction of a Dirac index and formulas for formal
{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}
-characters.

1.4.
Notation

We denote by
{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
the set of positive integers. Let
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\coloneqq{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
be the ring of integers modulo
2
. We denote the elements of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
by
\overline{0}
(the residue class of even integers) and
\overline{1}
(the residue class of odd integers). The ground field is
{\mathbb{C}}
, unless otherwise stated.

If
V\coloneqq V_{\bar{0}}\oplus V_{\bar{1}}
is a super vector space and
v\in V
is a homogeneous element, then
p(v)
denotes the parity of
v
, meaning
p(v)=\bar{0}
if
v\in V_{\bar{0}}
and
p(v)=\bar{1}
if
v\in V_{\bar{1}}
. For any two super vector spaces
V
and
W
, define the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-graded tensor product
V\otimes W
by

(V\otimes W)_{\bar{0}}\coloneqq(V_{\bar{0}}\otimes W_{\bar{0}})\oplus(V_{\bar{1}}\otimes W_{\bar{1}}),\quad(V\otimes W)_{\bar{1}}\coloneqq(V_{\bar{0}}\otimes W_{\bar{1}})\oplus(V_{\bar{1}}\otimes W_{\bar{0}}).

The assignment
(V,W)\mapsto V\otimes W
is additive in each variable. Moreover,
\otimes
is associative, and the map
V\otimes W\to W\otimes V
,
v\otimes w\mapsto(-1)^{p(v)p(w)}w\otimes v
, is an isomorphism. If
V
and
W
are superalgebras, the product in
V\otimes W
is
(v_{1}\otimes w_{1})(v_{2}\otimes w_{2})=(-1)^{p(w_{1})p(v_{2})}(v_{1}v_{2}\otimes w_{1}w_{2})
for any homogeneous
v_{1},v_{2}\in V
and
w_{1},w_{2}\in W
.

For a given Lie superalgebra
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}\oplus{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}
, we denote its universal enveloping superalgebra by
{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})
. The universal enveloping algebra of the Lie subalgebra
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
is denoted by
{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}})
. The centers are denoted by
\mathfrak{Z}({\mathfrak{g}})
and
\mathfrak{Z}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}})
, respectively.

By a (left)
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule, we mean a super vector space
M=M_{\bar{0}}\oplus M_{\bar{1}}
equipped with a graded linear (left) action of
{\mathfrak{g}}
, such that
[X,Y]v=X(Yv)-(-1)^{p(X)p(Y)}Y(Xv)
for all homogeneous
X,Y\in{\mathfrak{g}}
and
v\in M
. Analogously, a
{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})
-supermodule is defined. A morphism
f:M\to N
of
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules is a linear map such that
f(M_{\overline{i}})\subset N_{\overline{i}}
and
f(Xv)=Xf(v)
for all
X\in{\mathfrak{g}}
and
v\in M
. We write
{\mathfrak{g}}\textbf{-smod}
for the category of all (left)
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules. This category is a
{\mathbb{C}}
-linear abelian category, equipped with an endofunctor
\Pi
, the
parity reversing functor
. The parity reversing functor is defined by
\Pi(M)_{\bar{0}}=M_{\bar{1}}
and
\Pi(M)_{\bar{1}}=M_{\bar{0}}
. Moreover,
\Pi(M)
is viewed as a
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule with the induced action
X\cdot v\coloneqq(-1)^{p(X)}Xv
for any
X\in{\mathfrak{g}}
and
v\in M
. In particular, a
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule
M
is not necessarily isomorphic to
\Pi(M)
.

We denote by
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}\textbf{-mod}
the category of (left)
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-modules. When
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
is considered as a purely even Lie superalgebra, the category
{\mathfrak{g}}\textbf{-smod}
is simply the direct sum of two copies of
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}\textbf{-mod}
. We view any
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-module as a
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-supermodule concentrated in a single parity. Additionally, if we disregard parity, any
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule
M
can be viewed as a
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-module, also denoted by
M|_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}
. In what follows, the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-grading of
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-supermodules will often be left implicit and will be indicated only when it is relevant.

We will not make an explicit distinction between the categories
{\mathfrak{g}}\textbf{-smod}
and
{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})
-smod
or
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}\textbf{-mod}
and
{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}})
-mod
, which are the categories of all (left)
{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})
-supermodules and left
{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}})
-modules, respectively.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we introduce linear Lie superalgebras
A(m|n)
, present their structure theory, and describe the real forms
{\mathfrak{su}}(p,q|r,s)
and
\mathfrak{psu}(p,q|r,s)
.

2.1.
Lie superalgebras
A(m|n)

The
general linear Lie superalgebra

{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}
is the Lie superalgebra consisting of all block matrices

(2.1)

X=\left(\begin{array}[]{@{}c|c@{}}A&B\\
\hline\cr C&D\end{array}\right),

where
A
is an
m\times m
complex matrix,
B
an
m\times n
complex matrix,

C
an
n\times m
complex matrix, and
D
an
n\times n
complex matrix.
The
special linear Lie superalgebra

{\mathfrak{sl}(m|n)}
is the Lie subsuperalgebra of
{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}
consisting of all matrices
X
with zero supertrace, that is,

(2.2)

\operatorname{str}(X)=\operatorname{tr}(A)-\operatorname{tr}(D)=0.

This is a codimension-1 ideal in
{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}
. The even part of
{\mathfrak{sl}(m|n)}
is
{\mathfrak{sl}(m|n)}_{\bar{0}}\cong\mathfrak{sl}(m)\oplus\mathfrak{sl}(n)\oplus\mathbb{C}E_{m|n}
, where
E_{m|n}
denotes the diagonal matrix with
A=nE_{m}
and
D=mE_{n}
, and where
E_{m},E_{n}
are identity matrices of the indicated sizes. Furthermore,
{\mathfrak{sl}(m|n)}
is simple if and only if
m\neq n
and
m+n>2
. When
m=n
and
m+n>2
,
\mathfrak{sl}(n|n)
contains the nontrivial center
\mathbb{C}E_{n|n}
, and the quotient
\mathfrak{psl}(n|n)\coloneqq\mathfrak{sl}(n|n)/\mathbb{C}E_{n|n}
is a simple Lie superalgebra. The Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{sl}(1|1)
is nilpotent. For the remainder of this article, we assume
m+n>2
.

In order to treat simultaneously the cases
m=n
and
m\neq n
, we define the simple Lie superalgebras
A(m|n)
by

(2.3)

A(m|n)\coloneqq\begin{cases}\mathfrak{sl}(m+1|n+1),&\text{if }m\neq n,\;m,n\geq 0,\\
\mathfrak{psl}(n+1|n+1),&\text{if }m=n,\;n>0.\end{cases}

In the sequel we write
{\mathfrak{g}}\coloneqq A(m-1|n-1)
.

We denote by
{\mathfrak{d}}\coloneqq\{H=\operatorname{diag}(h_{1},\ldots,h_{m+n})\}
the abelian Lie subalgebra of diagonal matrices
in
{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}
. We choose the subspace of diagonal matrices with vanishing supertrace, denoted by
{\mathfrak{h}}
, as a Cartan subalgebra of
{\mathfrak{sl}(m|n)}
. The standard basis of the dual space
{\mathfrak{d}}^{\ast}
of
{\mathfrak{d}}
is

(\epsilon_{1},\dotsc,\epsilon_{m},\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{n})
where
\epsilon_{i}(H)=h_{i},\ \delta_{k}(H)=h_{k+m}
for
i=1,\dotsc,m
and
k=1,\dotsc,n
, for any
H\in{\mathfrak{d}}
. We use the symbols for the induced basis of
\mathfrak{h}^{*}
of
\mathfrak{h}
. Namely, we identify weights
\lambda\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}
for
{\mathfrak{g}}

with tuples
(\lambda_{1},\dotsc,\lambda_{m}|\lambda^{\prime}_{1},\dotsc,\lambda^{\prime}_{n})
via the expansion

\lambda=\lambda_{1}\epsilon_{1}+\dotsc+\lambda_{m}\epsilon_{m}+\lambda^{\prime}_{1}\delta_{1}+\dotsc+\lambda^{\prime}_{n}\delta_{n},

keeping in mind that shifts by
(1,\ldots,1|{-}1,\ldots,-1)
do not change the weight.

The Cartan subalgebra
{\mathfrak{h}}
of
{\mathfrak{g}}
is, in particular, a Cartan subalgebra for
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
, and hence acts semisimply on
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
. However,
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}
is a completely reducible
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-module under the adjoint action induced by the matrix supercommutator
[\cdot,\cdot]
, so that we obtain a
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-graded root space decomposition of
{\mathfrak{g}}
:

(2.4)

{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{h}}\oplus\bigoplus_{\alpha\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}}{\mathfrak{g}}^{\alpha},\qquad{\mathfrak{g}}^{\alpha}\coloneqq\{X\in{\mathfrak{g}}\ :\ [H,X]=\alpha(H)X\ \text{for all}\ H\in{\mathfrak{h}}\}.

We call
\alpha\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}
a
root
if
\alpha\neq 0
and
{\mathfrak{g}}^{\alpha}\neq\{0\}
. Elements of
{\mathfrak{g}}^{\alpha}
are called
root vectors
, and
{\mathfrak{g}}^{\alpha}
is called the
root space
corresponding to the root
\alpha
. Let
\Delta
denote the set of all roots. Each root space
{\mathfrak{g}}^{\alpha}
has either superdimension
(1|0)
or
(0|1)
. A root
\alpha\in\Delta
is called
even
if
{\mathfrak{g}}^{\alpha}\cap{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}\neq\{0\}
, and
odd
if
{\mathfrak{g}}^{\alpha}\cap{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}\neq\{0\}
. The associated sets of roots are denoted by
\Delta_{\bar{0}}
and
\Delta_{\bar{1}}
, respectively. Altogether, the set of roots of
{\mathfrak{g}}
is given by
\Delta=\Delta_{\bar{0}}\sqcup\Delta_{\bar{1}}
, where

(2.5)

\begin{split}\Delta_{\bar{0}}&=\{\pm(\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}),\pm(\delta_{k}-\delta_{l}):1\leq i<j\leq m,\ 1\leq k<l\leq n\},\\
\Delta_{\bar{1}}&=\{\pm(\epsilon_{r}-\delta_{s}):1\leq r\leq m,\ 1\leq s\leq n\}.\end{split}

The set of even roots
\Delta_{\bar{0}}
is the disjoint union of the root systems of
\mathfrak{sl}(m)
and
\mathfrak{sl}(n)
.

On
\Delta_{\bar{0}}
, we fix the standard positive system for the remainder of this article:

(2.6)

\Delta_{\bar{0}}^{+}\coloneqq\{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j},\ \delta_{k}-\delta_{l}\ :\ 1\leq i<j\leq m,\ 1\leq k<l\leq n\},

such that the root vectors for
\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}
,
i<j
are strictly upper triangular
matrices of
{\mathfrak{sl}}(m)
, and the root vectors for
\delta_{k}-\delta_{l}
,
k<l
, are strictly upper
triangular matrices of
{\mathfrak{sl}}(n)
, both diagonally embedded in
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
. A positive system for
\Delta_{\bar{1}}^{+}
will be specified in Section
2.2.1
. Then the set of positive roots is
\Delta^{+}=\Delta_{\bar{0}}^{+}\sqcup\Delta_{\bar{1}}^{+}
. With respect to a choice of
\Delta^{+}
, the Lie superalgebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
has a triangular decomposition

(2.7)

{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{n}}^{-}\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}\oplus{\mathfrak{n}}^{+},\qquad{\mathfrak{n}}^{\pm}\coloneqq\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta^{+}}{\mathfrak{g}}^{\pm\alpha},

with associated
Borel subalgebra

{\mathfrak{b}}={\mathfrak{h}}\oplus{\mathfrak{n}}^{+}
. Restricting the triangular decomposition of
{\mathfrak{g}}
to
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
yields a triangular decomposition of
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
, namely
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}={\mathfrak{n}}_{\bar{0}}^{-}\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}\oplus{\mathfrak{n}}_{\bar{0}}^{+}
, with
{\mathfrak{n}}_{\bar{0}}^{\pm}\coloneqq\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta_{\bar{0}}^{+}}{\mathfrak{g}}^{\pm\alpha}
.

For a fixed positive system
\Delta^{+}
, we define the
fundamental system

\Uppi\subset\Delta^{+}
to be the set of all
\alpha\in\Delta^{+}
which cannot be written as the sum of two roots in
\Delta^{+}
. Elements of
\Uppi
are called
simple
roots. For
\Uppi=\{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{r}\}
, any
\alpha\in\Delta
can be uniquely represented as a linear combination

(2.8)

\alpha=\sum_{i=1}^{r}k_{i}\alpha_{i},

where either all
k_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
or all
k_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\leq 0}
.

The general linear Lie superalgebra
{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}
is an example of a quadratic Lie superalgebra, that is, it carries a non-degenerate, even, supersymmetric, and invariant bilinear form
(\cdot,\cdot):{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}\times{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
given by

(2.9)

(X,Y)\coloneqq\operatorname{str}(XY)

for any
X,Y\in{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}
. Here, invariance means that
([X,Y],Z)=(X,[Y,Z])
for all
X,Y,Z\in{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}
, while supersymmetry means that
(\cdot,\cdot)
is symmetric on
{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}_{\bar{0}}
, skew-symmetric on
{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}_{\bar{1}}
, and that
{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}_{\bar{0}}
and
{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}_{\bar{1}}
are orthogonal to each other. The restriction of
(\cdot,\cdot)
to
{\mathfrak{sl}(m|n)}
is non-degenerate only when
m\neq n
. If
m=n
, the one-dimensional center of
\mathfrak{sl}(n|n)
becomes the radical of
(\cdot,\cdot)
. Furthermore, the restriction to
{\mathfrak{d}}
is non-degenerate while the restriction to
{\mathfrak{h}}
is non-degenerate if
m\neq n
. The bilinear form induced
on the dual spaces is denoted by the same symbol. On the standard basis, we have for all

1\leq i,j\leq m
and
1\leq k,l\leq n
:

(2.10)

(\epsilon_{i},\epsilon_{j})=\delta_{ij},\qquad(\delta_{k},\delta_{l})=-\delta_{kl},\qquad(\epsilon_{i},\delta_{k})=0.

For any root
\alpha\in\Delta
, we associate a unique element
h_{\alpha}\in{\mathfrak{h}}
through the condition
\alpha(H)=(H,h_{\alpha})
for all
H\in{\mathfrak{h}}
. This element
h_{\alpha}
is called the
coroot
associated with
\alpha
. If
m=n
, we fix the coroots as elements of
{\mathfrak{h}}
by requiring
\alpha(H)=(H,h_{\alpha})
for all
H\in{\mathfrak{d}}
.
However, the bilinear form on
{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}
, given by the linear extension of
(\alpha,\beta)\coloneqq(h_{\alpha},h_{\beta})
for
\alpha,\beta\in\Delta
, remains
non-degenerate only when
m\neq n
. In addition, it follows from Equation
2.10
that the odd roots are all isotropic, that is,

(\alpha,\alpha)=0
for all
\alpha\in\Delta_{\bar{1}}
.

The root system
\Delta
admits a natural action by the Weyl group
W
associated with the even part
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
of the Lie superalgebra. This group
W
is isomorphic to the direct product of symmetric groups
S_{m}\times S_{n}
, acting on
m
and
n
elements, respectively. It is generated by reflections with respect to the even roots, given by

(2.11)

R_{\alpha}(\beta)\coloneqq\beta-2\frac{(\alpha,\beta)}{(\alpha,\alpha)}\alpha,\qquad\alpha\in\Delta_{\bar{0}},\ \beta\in\Delta.

This action extends linearly to the dual space
{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
and preserves the bilinear form
(\cdot,\cdot)
. The
Weyl group of
{\mathfrak{g}}
is defined to be the Weyl group of
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
. The
Weyl vector

\rho
is
\rho=\rho_{\bar{0}}-\rho_{\bar{1}}
where

(2.12)

\rho_{\bar{0}}\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta_{\bar{0}}^{+}}\alpha=\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}(m-2i+1)\epsilon_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}(n-2j+1)\delta_{j}\right),\qquad\rho_{\bar{1}}\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\beta\in\Delta_{\bar{1}}^{+}}\beta.

We define the
dot action
of
W
on
{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}
by

(2.13)

w\cdot\lambda\coloneqq w(\lambda+\rho)-\rho

for any
\lambda\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}
and
w\in W
. We say that
\lambda,\mu\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}
are
W
-linked
if there exists some
w\in W
such that
\mu=w\cdot\lambda
. This gives an equivalence relation on
{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}
, and the Weyl orbit
\{w\cdot\lambda:w\in W\}
of
\lambda
under the dot action is called the
W
-linkage class
of
\lambda
.

Finally, the Lie superalgebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
of type
A
is an example of an
involutive Lie superalgebra
, that is, a Lie superalgebra endowed with a conjugate-linear anti-involution
\omega:{\mathfrak{g}}\to{\mathfrak{g}}
. A conjugate-linear map
\omega:{\mathfrak{g}}\to{\mathfrak{g}}
is called a
conjugate-linear anti-involution
if
\omega|_{{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0},\bar{1}}}\neq\operatorname{id}_{{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0},\bar{1}}}
,
\omega^{2}=\operatorname{id}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}
, and

(2.14)

\omega([x,y])=[\omega(y),\omega(x)],\qquad\forall x,y\in{\mathfrak{g}}.

The conjugate-linear anti-involutions
\omega
are in one-to-one correspondence with real forms of
{\mathfrak{g}}

[
36
,
41
]
, and we will see that a specific choice of
\omega
is essential when studying unitarizable supermodules over
{\mathfrak{g}}
.

2.2.
Special unitary Lie superalgebras
\mathfrak{(p)su}(p,q|r,s)

The
special unitary (indefinite) Lie superalgebras

\mathfrak{(p)su}(p,q|r,s)
are real forms of
{\mathfrak{g}}=A(m-1|n-1)
, where
p+q=m
and
r+s=n
. We construct the real Lie superalgebras
\mathfrak{(p)su}(p,q|r,s)
using Hermitian forms rather than super Hermitian forms, following
[
22
, Section 2]
. For that, we consider
V={\mathbb{C}}^{m|n}
,
i.e.
, the complex super vector space of superdimension
(m|n)
. For any fixed
p,q,r,s\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
with
p+q=m
and
r+s=n
, we equip
V
with the non-degenerate Hermitian form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:V\times V\to{\mathbb{C}}
given by

(2.15)

\langle v,w\rangle\coloneqq\overline{v}^{T}J_{(p,q|r,s)}w,\qquad J_{(p,q|r,s)}\coloneqq\left(\begin{array}[]{@{}c|c@{}}I_{p,q}&0\\
\hline\cr 0&I_{r,s}\end{array}\right).

Here,
\overline{\ \cdot\ }
means complex conjugation, and we consider any element of
{\mathbb{C}}^{m|n}
as a column vector. The matrix
I_{k,l}
is the diagonal matrix whose first
k
entries are
1
and whose last
l
entries are
-1
. This Hermitian form is
consistent
, that is,
\langle V_{\bar{0}},V_{\bar{1}}\rangle=0
.

We define the
(indefinite) unitary Lie superalgebra

\mathfrak{u}(p,q|r,s)=\mathfrak{u}(p,q|r,s)_{\bar{0}}\oplus\mathfrak{u}(p,q|r,s)_{\bar{1}}
as the Lie superalgebra preserving the Hermitian form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
:

(2.16)

\mathfrak{u}(p,q|r,s)_{\bar{k}}\coloneqq\{X\in{\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)}_{\bar{k}}:\langle Xv,w\rangle+\langle v,Xw\rangle=0\ \text{for all}\ v,w\in V\}.

The
(indefinite) special unitary Lie superalgebra
is defined as
\mathfrak{su}(p,q|r,s)\coloneqq\mathfrak{u}(p,q|r,s)\cap A(m-1|n-1)
if
m\neq n
and
\mathfrak{psu}(p,q|r,s)\coloneqq\mathfrak{u}(p,q|r,s)\cap A(n-1|n-1)
if
m=n
. To treat both cases uniformly, we write

(2.17)

\mathfrak{(p)su}(p,q|r,s)\coloneqq\begin{cases}\mathfrak{su}(p,q|r,s),\qquad&\text{if}\ m\neq n,\\
\mathfrak{psu}(p,q|r,s)\qquad&\text{if}\ m=n.\end{cases}

In our standard realization of
{\mathfrak{g}}
,
\mathfrak{(p)su}(p,q|r,s)
can be described explicitly as

(2.18)

\mathfrak{(p)su}(p,q|r,s)=\left\{X\in{\mathfrak{g}}:J_{(p,q|r,s)}^{-1}X^{\dagger}J_{(p,q|r,s)}=-X\right\}

with
(\cdot)^{\dagger}
denoting complex conjugate-transpose. The associated conjugate-linear anti-involution on
{\mathfrak{g}}
defining the real form
\mathfrak{(p)su}(p,q|r,s)
is

(2.19)

\omega(X)=J_{(p,q|r,s)}^{-1}X^{\dagger}J_{(p,q|r,s)},\qquad X\in{\mathfrak{g}}.

Of particular interest are the real forms
\mathfrak{(p)su}(p,q|n,0)
and
\mathfrak{(p)su}(p,q|0,n)
for
p,q\neq 0
, as they are the only real forms of
{\mathfrak{g}}
admitting non-trivial unitarizable supermodules (see Theorem
12
).

2.2.1.
Lie superalgebras
\mathfrak{(p)su}(p,q|n,0)
and
\mathfrak{(p)su}(p,q|0,n)

To simplify notation, we restrict attention to the real forms
\mathfrak{su}(p,q|n,0)
and
\mathfrak{su}(p,q|0,n)
for
m\neq n
, which are real forms of
{\mathfrak{sl}}(m|n)
for
p+q=m
; the cases
\mathfrak{psu}(p,q|n,0)
and
\mathfrak{psu}(p,q|0,n)
are obtained by factoring out the center.

Assume
m\neq n
. We fix as a real form either
{\mathfrak{su}}(p,q|n,0)
or
{\mathfrak{su}}(p,q|0,n)
. Then the even subalgebra of
{\mathfrak{g}}
is
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}\cong(\mathfrak{su(p,q)\oplus{\mathfrak{su}}(n)}\oplus{\mathfrak{u}}(1))^{{\mathbb{C}}}
to emphasize the real form. This leads to a natural refinement of the set of even roots
\Delta_{\bar{0}}
. The Lie subalgebra of the real form has a maximal compact subalgebra, namely

(2.20)

{\mathfrak{k}}\coloneqq{\mathfrak{su}}(p)\oplus{\mathfrak{su}}(q)\oplus{\mathfrak{u}}(1)\oplus{\mathfrak{su}}(n)\oplus{\mathfrak{u}}(1)

diagonally embedded in
{\mathfrak{g}}
. The subalgebra
{\mathfrak{k}}
satisfies the
equal rank condition

(2.21)

{\mathfrak{h}}\subset{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}\subset{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}\subset{\mathfrak{g}},

i.e.
,
{\mathfrak{h}}
is a Cartan subalgebra for
{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
and
{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}
. We say a root
\alpha\in\Delta_{\bar{0}}
is
compact
if the associated root vector lies in
{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}
; otherwise, the root is referred to as
non-compact
. The compact roots make up precisely the root system of
({\mathfrak{h}},{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}})
and can be identified with the subset

(2.22)

\Delta_{c}\coloneqq\{\pm(\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}):1\leq i<j\leq p,\ p+1\leq i<j\leq m\}\cup\{\pm(\delta_{i}-\delta_{j}):1\leq i<j\leq n\}.

The
non-compact roots
will be denoted by
\Delta_{nc}\coloneqq\Delta_{\bar{0}}\setminus\Delta_{c}
, such that

(2.23)

\Delta_{\bar{0}}=\Delta_{c}\sqcup\Delta_{nc}.

The fixed positive system on
\Delta_{\bar{0}}
induces a positive system on
\Delta_{c,nc}
, namely,
\Delta_{c,nc}^{+}\coloneqq\Delta^{+}\cap\Delta_{c,nc}
, and in analogy to Section
2.1
, we set

(2.24)

{\mathfrak{n}}_{\bar{0},c}^{+}\coloneqq\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Delta_{c}^{+}}{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}^{\alpha},\quad\text{and}\quad{\mathfrak{n}}_{\bar{0},nc}^{+}\coloneqq\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Delta_{nc}^{+}}{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}^{\alpha}.

The associated Weyl vectors are denoted by
\rho_{c}
and
\rho_{nc}
, respectively.

This refinement of the even structure allows for an explicit characterization of the possible positive odd root systems in
\Delta_{\bar{1}}
compatible with the chosen real form. In general, we distinguish two cases: the case where either
p=0
or
q=0
, and the case where
p,q\neq 0
. In the former case, the real form under consideration is the compact real form of
{\mathfrak{g}}
satisfying
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}\cong{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}
.

We first consider the case in which either
p=0
or
q=0
, that is, we consider the real Lie superalgebras
{\mathfrak{su}}(m,0|n,0)\cong{\mathfrak{su}}(0,m|0,n)
or
{\mathfrak{su}}(m,0|0,n)\cong{\mathfrak{su}}(0,m|n,0)
whose Lie subalgebra is isomorphic to
{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}
. The odd part
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}
is a completely reducible
{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}
-module under the adjoint action, and it decomposes as

(2.25)

{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}=\left\{\left(\begin{array}[]{@{}c|c@{}}0&B\\
\hline\cr 0&0\end{array}\right)\ :\ B\in{\text{Mat}}(m\times n;{\mathbb{C}})\right\}\oplus\left\{\left(\begin{array}[]{@{}c|c@{}}0&0\\
\hline\cr C&0\end{array}\right)\ :\ C\in{\text{Mat}}(n\times m;{\mathbb{C}})\right\}.

Consequently, there are exactly two conjugate-linear anti-involutions compatible with the standard ordering, which produce the even real forms
{\mathfrak{su}}(m)\oplus{\mathfrak{su}}(n)
on
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
:

(2.26)

\omega_{\pm}\left(\begin{array}[]{@{}c|c@{}}A&B\\
\hline\cr C&D\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{@{}c|c@{}}A^{\dagger}&\pm C^{\dagger}\\
\hline\cr\pm B^{\dagger}&D^{\dagger}\end{array}\right).

The real Lie superalgebra
{\mathfrak{su}}(m,0|n,0)\cong{\mathfrak{su}}(0,m|0,n)
corresponds to the conjugate-linear anti-involution
\omega_{+}
, while the real Lie superalgebra
{\mathfrak{su}}(m,0|0,n)\cong{\mathfrak{su}}(0,m|n,0)
corresponds to the conjugate-linear anti-involution
\omega_{-}
.
There are essentially two choices for the odd positive roots, namely

(2.27)

\begin{split}\Delta_{\bar{1},\text{st}}^{+}\coloneqq\{\epsilon_{i}-\delta_{j}\ :\ 1\leq i\leq m,\ 1\leq j\leq n\},\qquad\Delta_{\bar{1},\text{-st}}^{+}\coloneqq\{-\epsilon_{i}+\delta_{j}\ :\ 1\leq i\leq m,\ 1\leq j\leq n\},\end{split}

which differ by a sign. We fix
\Delta_{\bar{1},\text{st}}^{+}
as our positive system, so that
{\mathfrak{n}}^{+}
consists precisely of the upper block matrices in
{\mathfrak{g}}
. We denote this system simply by
\Delta^{+}
.

We now describe the conjugate-linear anti-involutions compatible with the standard ordering that produce the real forms
{\mathfrak{su}}(p,q)\oplus{\mathfrak{su}}(n)
in the semisimple part of
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
for
p,q\neq 0
. In this case, the odd part decomposes under
{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}
as

{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}={\mathfrak{q}}_{1}\oplus{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}\oplus{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}\oplus{\mathfrak{p}}_{2},

where explicitly

(2.28)

\begin{split}{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}&=\left\{\left(\begin{array}[]{@{}cc|c@{}}0&0&P_{1}\\
0&0&0\\
\hline\cr 0&0&0\end{array}\right):P_{1}\in{\text{Mat}}(p\times n;{\mathbb{C}})\right\},\qquad{\mathfrak{p}}_{2}=\left\{\left(\begin{array}[]{@{}cc|c@{}}0&0&0\\
0&0&P_{2}\\
\hline\cr 0&0&0\end{array}\right):P_{2}\in{\text{Mat}}(q\times n;{\mathbb{C}})\right\},\\
{\mathfrak{q}}_{1}&=\left\{\left(\begin{array}[]{@{}cc|c@{}}0&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
\hline\cr Q_{1}&0&0\end{array}\right):Q_{1}\in{\text{Mat}}(n\times p;{\mathbb{C}})\right\},\qquad{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}=\left\{\left(\begin{array}[]{@{}cc|c@{}}0&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
\hline\cr 0&Q_{2}&0\end{array}\right):Q_{2}\in{\text{Mat}}(n\times q;{\mathbb{C}})\right\}.\end{split}

Given this decomposition of
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}
, we express a general element
X\in{\mathfrak{g}}
as

(2.29)

X=\left(\begin{array}[]{@{}c|c@{}}\begin{matrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{matrix}&\begin{matrix}P_{1}\\
P_{2}\end{matrix}\\
\hline\cr\begin{matrix}Q_{1}&Q_{2}\end{matrix}&E\end{array}\right),

where
P_{i}\in{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}
,
Q_{j}\in{\mathfrak{q}}_{j}
for
1\leq i,j\leq 2
,
\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in{\mathfrak{su}}(p,q)^{{\mathbb{C}}}
,
E\in{\mathfrak{su}}(n)^{{\mathbb{C}}}
and
\operatorname{tr}\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}-\operatorname{tr}E=0
.
Then, there are exactly two conjugate-linear anti-involutions compatible with the standard ordering, whose restriction to the semisimple part of
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
yields the real form
{\mathfrak{su}}(p,q)\oplus{\mathfrak{su}}(n)

[
22
, Lemma 5.1]
:

(2.30)

\omega_{(-,+)}(X)=\left(\begin{array}[]{@{}c|c@{}}\begin{matrix}a^{\dagger}&-c^{\dagger}\\
-b^{\dagger}&d^{\dagger}\end{matrix}&\begin{matrix}-Q_{1}^{\dagger}\\
Q_{2}^{\dagger}\end{matrix}\\
\hline\cr\begin{matrix}-P_{1}^{\dagger}&P_{2}^{\dagger}\end{matrix}&\begin{matrix}E^{\dagger}\end{matrix}\end{array}\right),\qquad\omega_{(+,-)}(X)=\left(\begin{array}[]{@{}c|c@{}}\begin{matrix}a^{\dagger}&-c^{\dagger}\\
-b^{\dagger}&d^{\dagger}\end{matrix}&\begin{matrix}Q_{1}^{\dagger}\\
-Q_{2}^{\dagger}\end{matrix}\\
\hline\cr\begin{matrix}P_{1}^{\dagger}&-P_{2}^{\dagger}\end{matrix}&\begin{matrix}E^{\dagger}\end{matrix}\end{array}\right).

The real Lie superalgebra
{\mathfrak{su}}(p,q|0,n)
corresponds to
\omega_{(-,+)}
while
{\mathfrak{su}}(p,q|n,0)
corresponds to
\omega_{(+,-)}
. We conclude that essentially three positive systems are relevant for the odd part. These are uniquely determined by

(2.31)

{\mathfrak{n}}_{\bar{1},\text{st}}^{+}\coloneqq{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}\oplus{\mathfrak{p}}_{2},\quad{\mathfrak{n}}_{\bar{1},\text{-st}}^{+}\coloneqq{\mathfrak{q}}_{1}\oplus{\mathfrak{q}}_{2},\quad{\mathfrak{n}}_{\bar{1},\text{nst}}^{+}\coloneqq{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}\oplus{\mathfrak{q}}_{2},

referred to as
standard
,
minus standard
, and
non-standard
, respectively. We will fix the non-standard choice and denote it by
\Delta^{+}
, as this choice is preferred in the context of the Dirac operator, as explained in Remark
34
. In this case, we have

(2.32)

\rho_{\bar{1}}=\frac{1}{2}\left(n\sum_{i=1}^{p}\epsilon_{i}-n\sum_{j=p+1}^{m}\epsilon_{j}+(q-p)\sum_{k=1}^{n}\delta_{k}\right).

For convenience, we set
{\mathfrak{n}}_{\bar{1}}^{+}\coloneqq\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Delta_{\bar{1}}^{+}}{\mathfrak{g}}^{\alpha}
and
{\mathfrak{n}}_{\bar{1}}^{-}\coloneqq\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Delta_{\bar{1}}^{+}}{\mathfrak{g}}^{-\alpha}
, leading to the decomposition of super vector spaces
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}={\mathfrak{n}}_{\bar{1}}^{-}\oplus{\mathfrak{n}}_{\bar{1}}^{+}
and
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{n}}_{\bar{1}}^{-}\oplus{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}\oplus{\mathfrak{n}}_{\bar{1}}^{+}
. However,
[{\mathfrak{n}}_{\bar{1}}^{\pm},{\mathfrak{n}}_{\bar{1}}^{\pm}]\neq\{0\}
, indicating that this
\mathbb{Z}
-grading is not compatible with the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-grading since
{\mathfrak{n}}_{\bar{1}}^{\pm}
are invariant under
{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}
but not under
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
. The following lemma summarizes the important commutation relations.

Lemma 7
.

The following commutation relations hold in
{\mathfrak{g}}
for
1\leq k,l\leq 2
:

[{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}},{\mathfrak{p}}_{k}]\subset{\mathfrak{p}}_{k},\qquad[{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}},{\mathfrak{q}}_{l}]\subset{\mathfrak{q}}_{l},\qquad[{\mathfrak{n}}_{\bar{1}}^{+},{\mathfrak{n}}_{\bar{1}}^{+}]\subset{\mathfrak{n}}_{\bar{0},nc}^{+}.

For the remainder of this article, unless otherwise stated, we assume
m\leq n
and
p,q\neq 0
. The results for the finite-dimensional case are then obtained by setting
p=0
or
q=0
and utilizing
{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}={\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
.

3.
Unitarizable supermodules

In this section, we present unitarizable supermodules over
{\mathfrak{g}}
, examine their properties and algebraic realization, and explain how they relate to Harish-Chandra supermodules.

3.1.
Unitarizable supermodules

There are two equivalent approaches to defining unitarizable supermodules over
{\mathfrak{g}}
, based on the fact that Hermitian forms and super Hermitian forms on a complex super vector space can be transformed into one another. In this paper, we work with Hermitian forms, viewing them as complex-valued maps that are conjugate-linear in the first variable and linear in the second. For a more thorough discussion comparing Hermitian and super Hermitian forms, see
[
22
,
40
]
.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}={\mathcal{H}}_{\bar{0}}\oplus{\mathcal{H}}_{\bar{1}}
be a complex super vector space equipped with a positive definite Hermitian form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
such that
\langle{\mathcal{H}}_{\bar{0}},\,{\mathcal{H}}_{\bar{1}}\rangle=0
.
We refer to such a structure as a
pre-super Hilbert space
. Given this structure, we can now define unitarizable supermodules. These supermodules are defined with respect to real forms of the Lie superalgebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
, which are in one-to-one correspondence with conjugate-linear anti-involutions
\omega

[
36
,
41
]
(see Section
2.2.1
for examples), such that the Hermitian form of the pre-super Hilbert space is
contravariant
with respect to
\omega
.

Definition 8

(
[
22
, Definition 2.3]
)
.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}
be a
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule, and let
\omega
be a conjugate-linear anti-involution on
{\mathfrak{g}}
. The supermodule
{\mathcal{H}}
is called an
\omega
-unitarizable

{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule
if
{\mathcal{H}}
is a super pre-Hilbert space such that for all
v,w\in{\mathcal{H}}
and all
X\in{\mathfrak{g}}
, the Hermitian form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is
\omega
-contravariant:

\langle Xv,w\rangle=\langle v,\omega(X)w\rangle.

If we instead consider
{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})
-supermodules, we extend
\omega
to
{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})
in the obvious way, keeping the same notation. In this setting, a
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule
{\mathcal{H}}
is unitarizable precisely when it is a
Hermitian supermodule
over
({\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}}),\omega)
, that is, when

\langle Xv,w\rangle=\langle v,\omega(X)w\rangle

holds for all
v,w\in{\mathcal{H}}
and
X\in{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})
. Whenever
\omega
is clear from the context, we will simply say “unitarizable”.

Direct consequences of this definition are collected in the following proposition.

Proposition 9
.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}
be a
\omega
-unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule. Then the following assertions hold:

a)

{\mathcal{H}}
is completely reducible; that is, for any invariant subsuperspace, its orthogonal complement is also an invariant subsuperspace.

b)

As a
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}
-supermodule,
{\mathcal{H}}
is
\omega|_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}
-unitarizable. In particular, it is completely reducible as a
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-supermodule.

We will see that unitarizability is a very strong condition for supermodules since only a specific but central class of supermodules can satisfy it: the
highest
or
lowest weight supermodules
.

Definition 10
.

A
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule
M
is called a
highest weight

{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule with highest weight

\Lambda\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}
if there exists a nonzero vector
v_{\Lambda}\in M
such that:

a)

Xv_{\Lambda}=0
for all
X\in{\mathfrak{n}}^{+}
,

b)

Hv_{\Lambda}=\Lambda(H)v_{\Lambda}
for all
H\in{\mathfrak{h}}
, and

c)

{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})v_{\Lambda}=M
.

The vector
v_{\Lambda}
is referred to as the highest weight vector of
M
.
Lowest weight

{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules
are defined analogously.

Remark 11
.

We fixed in Section
2.2
the non-standard odd positive system
\Delta^{+}_{\bar{1}}\coloneqq\Delta^{+}_{\bar{1},\text{nst}}
for this article, which is not a substantial restriction. Indeed, set
\Delta_{\text{nst}}^{+}\coloneqq\Delta_{\bar{0}}^{+}\sqcup\Delta_{\bar{1},\text{nst}}^{+}
and
\Delta_{\text{st}}^{+}\coloneqq\Delta_{\bar{0}}^{+}\sqcup\Delta_{\bar{1},\text{st}}^{+}
. If
L(\Lambda;\Delta_{\text{nst}}^{+})
is a simple highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule with respect to
\Delta_{\text{nst}}^{+}
, then there exists a
\Lambda^{\prime}\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}
such that either
L(\Lambda;\Delta_{\text{nst}}^{+})\cong L(\Lambda^{\prime};\Delta_{\text{st}}^{+})
or
L(\Lambda;\Delta_{\text{nst}}^{+})\cong\Pi L(\Lambda^{\prime};\Delta_{\text{st}}^{+})
, with
\Lambda^{\prime}+\rho_{\text{st}}=\Lambda+\rho_{\text{nst}}
, where
\rho_{\text{nst}}
and
\rho_{\text{st}}
are the Weyl vectors for
\Delta^{+}_{\text{nst}}
and
\Delta^{+}_{\text{st}}
, respectively. This is because the associated fundamental systems can be transformed into each other via a sequence of odd reflections, as explained in
[
7
, Section 1.4]
, using
\Delta_{\text{st}}^{+}\cap\Delta_{\bar{0}}=\Delta_{\bar{0}}^{+}=\Delta_{\text{nst}}^{+}\cap\Delta_{\bar{0}}
, and the multiple application of
[
7
, Lemma 1.40]
.

The central theorem concerning unitarizable supermodules is the following:

Theorem 12

(
[
13
,
29
]
)
.

The Lie superalgebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
of type
A
admits non-trivial unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules only with respect to the real forms
\mathfrak{(p)su}(p,q|n,0)
and
\mathfrak{(p)su}(p,q|0,n)
. In particular, any such simple supermodule is either a lowest weight or a highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule.

It suffices to consider highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules, as the case of lowest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules follows by the same arguments.

Recall the anti-involutions
\omega_{(-,+)}
and
\omega_{(+,-)}
of (
2.30
); the real forms
\mathfrak{(p)su}(p,q|0,n)
and
\mathfrak{(p)su}(p,q|n,0)
correspond respectively to
\omega_{(-,+)}
and
\omega_{(+,-)}

{\mathfrak{g}}
admits non-trivial unitarizable highest weight supermodules only with respect to

\omega_{(+,-)}
corresponds to unitarizable lowest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules (see
[

,

\mathfrak{(p)su}(p,q|0,n)
and, for notational convenience, write
\mathfrak{(p)su}(p,q|n)
instead. In what follows, we omit the prefix
\omega_{(-,+)}
and refer simply to unitarizable highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules.

Next, we collect further algebraic properties of unitarizable highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules. We remind the reader of our assumptions that, unless otherwise stated,
m\leq n
and
p,q\neq 0
.

3.2.
Harish-Chandra supermodules

Unitarizable highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules constitute a class of
Harish-Chandra supermodules
; that is, they carry an intrinsic structure induced by the decomposition relative to a maximal compact subalgebra of a chosen real form.

Fix a real form
\mathfrak{(p)su}(p,q|n)
of
{\mathfrak{g}}
, and let
{\mathfrak{k}}
be the maximal compact subalgebra of
\mathfrak{(p)su}(p,q|n)_{\bar{0}}
introduced in Section
2.2
, such that the following relation holds:

(3.1)

{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}\subset{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}\subset{\mathfrak{g}}.

Any
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule
M
can be viewed as a
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-supermodule, and any
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-supermodule can be regarded as a
{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}
-supermodule. However, when viewing a
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule as a
{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}
-supermodule, we ignore the parity and treat it simply as a
{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}
-module.

If
M
is unitarizable, the action of
{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}
is contravariant by the definition of the Hermitian form, and
M
can be decomposed under
{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}
, which is equivalent to decomposing
M|_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}
under
{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}
. This naturally leads to the definition of a
({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}})
-supermodule or a
({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}},{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}})
-module.

Definition 13
.

A complex
({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}})
-supermodule (
({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}},{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}})
-module) is a complex
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule (
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-module)
M
which, as a
{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}
-module, decomposes as a direct sum of simple
{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}
-modules,
i.e.
,
M
is
{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}
-semisimple.

An important class of
({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}},{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}})
-modules consists of those arising from unitary irreducible representations of the universal covering Lie group of the Lie group
S(U(p,q)\times U(n))
, associated with
\mathfrak{(p)su}(p,q|n)_{\bar{0}}
. These modules exhibit an additional property that leads to the definition of Harish-Chandra (super)modules.

Definition 14
.

A complex
({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}})
-supermodule (
({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}},{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}})
-module) is called a
Harish-Chandra supermodule (Harish-Chandra module)
if it is finitely generated and is locally finite as a
{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}
-module.

For highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules, the conditions of local finiteness over
{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}
and finite generation are redundant.

Proposition 15

(
[
5
, Proposition 2.8]
)
.

Let
M
be a highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule with highest weight
\Lambda
and highest weight vector
v_{\Lambda}
. The following assertions are equivalent:

a)

\dim({\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}})v_{\Lambda})<\infty
.

b)

M
is a
({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}})
-supermodule.

c)

M
is a Harish-Chandra supermodule.

If these assertions hold, then
{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}})v_{\Lambda}
is a simple
{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}
-module.

We show that any unitarizable highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule is a Harish-Chandra supermodule. Recall that any unitarizable highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule is
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-semisimple, and each
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-constituent is a unitarizable highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-module (disregarding parity), for which we have the following well-known lemma.

Lemma 16

(
[
31
, Lemma IX.3.10]
)
.

Any unitarizable highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-module is a Harish-Chandra module.

To establish that unitarizable simple
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules are Harish-Chandra supermodules, we need the following lemma, which is straightforward but useful.

Lemma 17
.

A (complex) finitely generated
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule
M
is a
({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}})
-supermodule if and only if
M|_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}
is a
({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}},{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}})
-module.

Proof.

The statement follows immediately, as
{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})
is a finitely generated
{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}})
-module.
∎

We now obtain the following proposition.

Proposition 18
.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}
be a unitarizable highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule. Then
{\mathcal{H}}
is a Harish-Chandra supermodule.

The category of Harish-Chandra (super)modules is the full subcategory whose objects are the Harish-Chandra (super)modules and whose morphisms are the even
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-module and
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule homomorphisms, respectively. In this article, all (super)modules are understood to lie in this category.

3.3.
Infinitesimal characters

Unitarizable simple
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules are of highest weight type, and hence admit infinitesimal characters. These are algebra homomorphisms
\chi:\mathfrak{Z}({\mathfrak{g}})\to{\mathbb{C}}
, which can be explicitly described using the Harish-Chandra homomorphism for Lie superalgebras. To construct this homomorphism, we use the following isomorphism of super vector spaces:

(3.2)

\mathfrak{U}({\mathfrak{g}})\cong\mathfrak{U}({\mathfrak{h}})\oplus({\mathfrak{n}}^{-}\mathfrak{U}({\mathfrak{g}})+\mathfrak{U}({\mathfrak{g}}){\mathfrak{n}}^{+}),

which follows directly from the PBW Theorem for
{\mathfrak{g}}
. The decomposition is stable under
\omega
, extended to
{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})
. The associated projection
\operatorname{pr}:\mathfrak{U}({\mathfrak{g}})\to\mathfrak{U}({\mathfrak{h}})
is called the
Harish-Chandra projection
, whose restriction to
\mathfrak{Z}({\mathfrak{g}})
defines an algebra homomorphism:

(3.3)

\operatorname{pr}\big|_{\mathfrak{Z}({\mathfrak{g}})}:\mathfrak{Z}({\mathfrak{g}})\to\mathfrak{U}({\mathfrak{h}})\cong\operatorname{S}({\mathfrak{h}}).

The
Harish-Chandra homomorphism

\operatorname{HC}:\mathfrak{Z}({\mathfrak{g}})\to S({\mathfrak{h}})
is given by the composition of
\operatorname{pr}\big|_{\mathfrak{Z}({\mathfrak{g}})}
with the twist
\zeta:\operatorname{S}({\mathfrak{h}})\to\operatorname{S}({\mathfrak{h}})
, defined by

\lambda(\zeta(f))\coloneqq(\lambda-\rho)(f)
for all
f\in\operatorname{S}({\mathfrak{h}})
and
\lambda\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
, where
\rho
denotes the Weyl vector corresponding to the chosen positive system
\Delta^{+}
. For any
\Lambda\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}
, the associated map

(3.4)

\chi_{\Lambda}(z)\coloneqq(\Lambda+\rho)(\text{HC}(z))

defines an algebra homomorphism
\chi_{\Lambda}:\mathfrak{Z}({\mathfrak{g}})\to{\mathbb{C}}
.
A
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule
M
is said to admit an
infinitesimal character
if there exists
\Lambda\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}
such that every
z\in\mathfrak{Z}({\mathfrak{g}})
acts on
M
by scalar multiplication with
\chi_{\Lambda}(z)\in{\mathbb{C}}
. The map
\chi_{\Lambda}
is referred to as the
infinitesimal character
of
M
.

Highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules admit an infinitesimal character by Proposition
20
. More precisely, the following holds:

Lemma 19

(
[
28
, Lemma 8.5.3]
)
.

Let
M
be a highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule with highest weight
\Lambda
. Then
M
admits an infinitesimal character
\chi_{\Lambda}
. Furthermore, the quadratic Casimir element
\Omega_{{\mathfrak{g}}}\in\mathfrak{Z}({\mathfrak{g}})
acts by the scalar

(\Lambda+2\rho,\Lambda).

The Harish-Chandra homomorphism is an injective ring homomorphism. To describe its image, we define

(3.5)

\operatorname{S}({\mathfrak{h}})^{W}\coloneqq\{f\in\operatorname{S}({\mathfrak{h}})\mid w(\lambda)(f)=\lambda(f)\ \text{for all}\ w\in W,\lambda\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}\},

and for any
\lambda\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
, we introduce

(3.6)

A_{\lambda}\coloneqq\{\alpha\in\Delta_{\bar{1}}^{+}\mid(\lambda+\rho,\alpha)=0\}.

The image of
\operatorname{HC}
is then given by
[
14
,
23
]
:

(3.7)

\operatorname{im}(\operatorname{HC})=\bigl\{f\in\operatorname{S}({\mathfrak{h}})^{W}\mid(\lambda+t\alpha)(f)=\lambda(f)\ \text{for all $t\in{\mathbb{C}}$, $\lambda\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}$, and $\alpha\in A_{\lambda-\rho}$}\bigr\}.

As an immediate consequence, one obtains
\chi_{\Lambda}=\chi_{\Lambda^{\prime}}
whenever

(3.8)

\Lambda^{\prime}=w\biggl(\Lambda+\rho+\sum_{i=1}^{k}t_{i}\alpha_{i}\biggr)-\rho,

where
w\in W
,
t_{i}\in{\mathbb{C}}
, and
\alpha_{1},\dotsc,\alpha_{k}\in A_{\Lambda}
are linearly independent odd isotropic roots satisfying
(\Lambda+\rho,\alpha_{i})=0
. This leads to the concept of
atypicality
. A weight
\Lambda\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}
is called
typical
if
A_{\Lambda}=\emptyset
,
i.e.
,

(\Lambda+\rho,\alpha)\neq 0
for
all
\alpha\in\Delta_{\bar{1}}^{+}
. Otherwise
\Lambda
is called
atypical
.

3.4.
Algebraic realization and Shapovalov form

In this section, we realize unitarizable highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules as simple quotients of Verma supermodules to specify their structure and to construct a natural contravariant Hermitian form on them.

3.4.1.
Verma supermodules

We consider the fixed involutive Lie superalgebra
({\mathfrak{g}},\omega=\omega_{(-,+)})
, and the fixed
Borel subalgebra

(3.9)

{\mathfrak{b}}={\mathfrak{h}}\oplus{\mathfrak{n}}^{+},\qquad{\mathfrak{n}}^{+}\coloneqq\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Delta^{+}}{\mathfrak{g}}^{\alpha},

with
\Delta^{+}\coloneqq\Delta^{+}_{\text{nst}}
(see Section
2.2.1
). The Borel satisfies
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{b}}+{\mathfrak{b}}^{\omega}
for

{\mathfrak{b}}^{\omega}\coloneqq\{\omega(X):X\in{\mathfrak{b}}\}.

We consider
{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})
as a right
{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{b}})
-supermodule with respect to right multiplication. For any
\Lambda\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}
, let
{\mathbb{C}}_{\Lambda}
be the one-dimensional
{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{b}})
-supermodule with trivial action of
{\mathfrak{n}}^{+}
and weight
\Lambda
. The
Verma supermodule
associated to
\Lambda\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}
is the induced
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule

(3.10)

M^{{\mathfrak{b}}}(\Lambda)\coloneqq{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes_{{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{b}})}{\mathbb{C}}_{\Lambda}.

The superscript
{\mathfrak{b}}
will be omitted when the Borel subalgebra is fixed.

The well-known properties of Verma supermodules are summarized in the following proposition. The proof of the proposition follows standard arguments similar to those used for reductive Lie algebras and will therefore be omitted.

Proposition 20

(
[
28
, Chapter 8]
)
.

a)

The Verma supermodule
M(\Lambda)
is a highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule with highest weight
\Lambda
, and highest weight vector
[1\otimes 1]
. In particular,
{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{n}}^{-})[1\otimes 1]=M(\Lambda)
.

b)

For each
{\mathfrak{b}}
-eigenvector
v_{\Lambda}
of weight
\Lambda
in a
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule
M
, there exists a uniquely determined surjective
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule morphism
M(\Lambda)\to M
sending
[1\otimes 1]
to
v_{\Lambda}
.

c)

M(\Lambda)
is a
weight supermodule
, that is,
M(\Lambda)=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}}M(\Lambda)^{\lambda}
, where

M(\Lambda)^{\lambda}\coloneqq\{m\in M(\Lambda):Hm=\lambda(H)m\text{ for all }H\in{\mathfrak{h}}\}
. The space
M(\Lambda)^{\lambda}
is
the
weight space
of weight
\lambda
, and
\dim(M(\Lambda)^{\lambda})
its
multiplicity
.

\dim(M(\Lambda)^{\lambda})<\infty
for all
\lambda
, with
\dim(M(\Lambda)^{\Lambda})=1
.

d)

\operatorname{End}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(M(\Lambda))\cong{\mathbb{C}}
.

e)

M(\Lambda)
has a unique maximal subsupermodule and unique simple quotient, denoted by
L(\Lambda)
. In particular,
M(\Lambda)
is indecomposable.

f)

M(\Lambda)
has a finite Jordan–Hölder series.

The properties of Verma supermodules pass, in particular, to highest weight supermodules.
Moreover, all simple highest weight supermodules with the same highest weight and the same parity are isomorphic.

Combining Theorem
12
with Proposition
20
, we conclude that any unitarizable highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule is the unique simple quotient of a Verma supermodule. In general, Verma supermodules admit a natural
\omega
-contravariant Hermitian form, known as the Shapovalov form. It descends to a
\omega
-contravariant form on the unique simple quotient, also referred to as
Shapovalov form
. A highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule is unitarizable if and only if the associated Shapovalov form is positive definite.

3.4.2.
Shapovalov form

Each Verma supermodule carries a natural contravariant Hermitian form if the highest weight
\Lambda\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}
satisfies

(3.11)

\Lambda(\omega(H))=\overline{\Lambda(H)},\qquad\forall H\in{\mathfrak{h}}.

An element
\Lambda\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}
that meets this condition is called
symmetric
. Notably, all highest weights of unitarizable highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules are symmetric. Indeed, let
{\mathcal{H}}
be a unitarizable highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule with highest weight
\Lambda
and highest weight vector
v_{\Lambda}
. Then, for any
H\in{\mathfrak{h}}
,

(3.12)

\langle Hv_{\Lambda},v_{\Lambda}\rangle=\langle v_{\Lambda},\omega(H)v_{\Lambda}\rangle\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\overline{\Lambda(H)}=\Lambda(\omega(H)).

Recall that the decomposition (
3.2
) is stable under
\omega
. Let
\operatorname{pr}:{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})\to{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{h}})
be the Harish-Chandra projection from above. For any
\Lambda\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}
, we define a bilinear map on
{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})
:

(3.13)

(X,Y)_{\Lambda}\coloneqq\Lambda(\operatorname{pr}(\omega(X)Y)),\qquad X,Y\in{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}}).

Lemma 21
.

Let
\Lambda\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}
be symmetric. Then the following assertions hold:

a)

(\cdot,\cdot)_{\Lambda}
is conjugate-linear in the first coordinate and linear in the second coordinate.

b)

(X,Y)_{\Lambda}=\overline{(Y,X)}_{\Lambda}
for all
X,Y\in{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})
.

c)

(ZX,Y)_{\Lambda}=(X,\omega(Z)Y)_{\Lambda}
for any
X,Y,Z\in\mathfrak{U}({\mathfrak{g}})
.

Proof.

a) and c) follow by definition and the fact that
\omega
is conjugate-linear. For b), let
X,Y\in{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})
, and note that
\omega(h)=\overline{h}
for all
h\in{\mathfrak{h}}
using (
2.30
). Using
\omega
-stability of the decomposition and
\overline{\Lambda(H)}=\Lambda(\omega(H))
for all
H\in{\mathfrak{h}}
, one has (
3.2
)

\begin{split}(X,Y)_{\Lambda}=\Lambda(\operatorname{pr}(\omega(X)Y))=\overline{\Lambda(\omega(\operatorname{pr}(\omega(X)Y)))}=\overline{\Lambda(\omega(Y)X)}=\overline{(Y,X)}_{\Lambda}.\qed\end{split}

Since
M(\Lambda)={\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})[1\otimes 1]
, the form
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\Lambda}
induces a well-defined contravariant Hermitian form on
M(\Lambda)
. This form is the
Shapovalov form
, denoted by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{M(\Lambda)}
.

Proposition 22
.

If
\Lambda\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}
is symmetric, then there exists a unique
\omega
-contravariant Hermitian form on
M(\Lambda)
satisfying
\langle[1\otimes 1],[1\otimes 1]\rangle_{M(\Lambda)}=1
. All other contravariant Hermitian forms are real multiples of this form.

Proof.

To complete the argument, we must show uniqueness. Thus it suffices to verify that any contravariant Hermitian form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
on
M(\Lambda)
with
\langle[1\otimes 1],[1\otimes 1]\rangle=0
is necessarily trivial. This follows from the computation below:

\begin{split}\langle M(\Lambda),M(\Lambda)\rangle&=\langle{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})[1\otimes 1],{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})[1\otimes 1]\rangle=\langle[1\otimes 1],{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})[1\otimes 1]\rangle\\
&=\langle[1\otimes 1],{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{b}}^{\omega})[1\otimes 1]\rangle=\langle{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{b}})[1\otimes 1],[1\otimes 1]\rangle\\
&\subset{\mathbb{C}}\langle[1\otimes 1],[1\otimes 1]\rangle=0.\qed\end{split}

By the construction of the Shapovalov form, weight spaces of different weights with respect to
{\mathfrak{h}}
are orthogonal. We conclude the following corollary.

Corollary 23
.

If
\Lambda
is symmetric, the maximal proper subsupermodule of
M(\Lambda)
coincides with the radical
R
of the Shapovalov form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{M(\Lambda)}.
In particular,
L(\Lambda)\cong M(\Lambda)/R
.

Let
M
be a highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule with highest weight
\Lambda
and highest weight vector
v_{\Lambda}
. Assume that
\Lambda
is symmetric. By Proposition
20
, there is a
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule homomorphism
q:M(\Lambda)\to M
that maps
[1\otimes 1]
to
v_{\Lambda}
. Since
\Lambda
is symmetric, the maximal subsupermodule of
M(\Lambda)
is the same as the radical
R
of the Shapovalov form. As a result, the kernel of
q
must lie within
R
. This leads us to the following proposition.

Proposition 24
.

M
carries a nonzero unique
\omega
-contravariant consistent Hermitian form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
such that
\langle v_{\Lambda},v_{\Lambda}\rangle=1
. The form is non-degenerate if and only if
M
is simple, in which case
M\cong L(\Lambda)\coloneqq M(\Lambda)/R
, and the space of
{\mathfrak{b}}
-eigenvectors of weight
\Lambda
in
L(\Lambda)
is one-dimensional.

Proof.

It remains to show that the form is non-degenerate if and only if
M
is simple. It is clear that the Shapovalov form induces a non-degenerate form on
L(\Lambda)=M(\Lambda)/R
.

If, conversely,
M

Lemma 26
.

Let
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}\coloneqq{\mathfrak{g}}_{1}\oplus{\mathfrak{g}}_{2}
be a direct sum of reductive Lie algebras with root space decompositions. Let
L_{0}(\lambda;{\mathfrak{g}}_{1})
and

be two simple highest weight modules with highest weight vectors
v_{\lambda}
and
v_{\mu}
, respectively. Assume that
\lambda
and
\mu
are symmetric. Define the
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-module

. Then the following assertions hold:

a)

The tensor product of the Shapovalov forms on both factors defines a non-degenerate contravariant Hermitian form on
V
.

b)

v_{\lambda}\otimes v_{\mu}
is a primitive element in
V
, that is, a nonzero
{\mathfrak{b}}_{\bar{0}}
-eigenvector.

c)

Proof.

We denote the Shapovalov forms on

and
L_{0}(\mu;{\mathfrak{g}}_{2})

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\lambda}
and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mu}
, respectively. Then
V
has a contravariant Hermitian form

\langle v_{1}\otimes w_{1},v_{2}\otimes w_{2}\rangle=\langle v_{1},v_{2}\rangle_{\lambda}\langle w_{1},w_{2}\rangle_{\mu}.

To see that
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is non-degenerate, consider
x\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{k}v_{j}\otimes w_{j}
with
\langle x,y\rangle=0
for all

are linearly independent. Since

is simple,

, so

For assertions b) and c), note that the set of weights of

and

,

∎

Proposition 27
.

Let
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}\coloneqq{\mathfrak{g}}_{1}\oplus{\mathfrak{g}}_{2}
be the direct sum of Lie algebras with root space decompositions. Let

The positive systems for

We introduce the

Recall the definition of the supertrace form

4.1.

Quadratic Dirac operators and their relative counterparts are defined here in the setting of the supersymmetric analogue of the quantum Weil algebra introduced in

appears as the relative Dirac operator associated with the pair

4.1.1.
Weil algebra, quantum Weil algebra and Weyl algebra

We begin by describing the quantum Weil algebra for a general quadratic Lie superalgebra
{\mathfrak{l}}
and for embeddings of quadratic Lie subsuperalgebras. We then make these constructions explicit for the quadratic pair
({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}})
.

Let
V
be a super vector space with a supersymmetric and non-degenerate bilinear form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
, and let

denote the tensor superalgebra over

, equipped with the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-grading induced by

We endow

with its natural
{\mathbb{Z}}
-grading, and write
T(V)=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}T^{n}(V)
with
T^{0}(V)={\mathbb{C}}
.
The following associative unital superalgebras arise naturally as quotients
T(V)/I
, where
I
is a two-sided homogeneous ideal of
T(V)
. The
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-grading is the one induced by the grading of
V
.

(1)

Exterior superalgebra

\bigwedge(V)
with
I_{\wedge}
generated by the elements

v\otimes w+(-1)^{p(v)p(w)}\,w\otimes v,\qquad v,w\in V.

Its multiplication will be written
\wedge
and called

. Moreover, as the ideal

is homogeneous, we have a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-grading

\bigwedge(V)=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigwedge\nolimits^{n}(V),\qquad\bigwedge\nolimits^{n}(V)\coloneqq T^{n}(V)/(I_{\wedge}\cap T^{n}(V)).

(2)

Symmetric superalgebra

S(V)
with
I_{S}
generated by the elements

v\otimes w-(-1)^{p(v)p(w)}w\otimes v,\qquad v,w\in V.

Similarly to the exterior superalgebra,

has a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-grading
S(V)=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}S^{n}(V)
induced by the
{\mathbb{Z}}
-grading of
T(V)
.

(3)

Clifford superalgebra

{\operatorname{Cl}}(V)
with
I_{{\operatorname{Cl}}}
generated by the elements

v\otimes w+(-1)^{p(v)p(w)}w\otimes v-2\langle v,w\rangle 1_{T(V)},\qquad v,w\in V.

If
({\mathfrak{l}}=V,B)
is a quadratic Lie superalgebra, we define the
Weil algebra
and
quantum Weil algebra
generalizing the notion in
[
2
]
to the super setting.

Definition 31
.

Let
{\mathfrak{l}}
be a quadratic Lie superalgebra. We call
W({\mathfrak{l}})\coloneqq S({\mathfrak{l}})\otimes\bigwedge({\mathfrak{l}})

Weil algebra
and

.

and

are defined with respect to the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-graded tensor product over

The quantum Weil algebra

is generated by the elements
x\otimes 1
and
1\otimes x
, where

. It carries a
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-grading given by

(4.1)

\deg(1\otimes x)=(\bar{1},p(x)),\qquad\deg(x\otimes 1)=(\bar{0},p(x)).

For
\alpha=(\bar{a},\bar{b})
and

\chi(\alpha,\beta)\coloneqq(-1)^{\bar{a}\bar{c}+\bar{b}\bar{d}}.

\begin{split}\partial_{(i-1)n+(j-m)}&=\begin{cases}E_{ij}\ \ \qquad&\text{for}\ 1\leq i\leq p,\ m+1\leq j\leq m+n,\\
E_{ji}\qquad&\text{for}\ p+1\leq i\leq m,\ m+1\leq j\leq m+n,\end{cases}\\
x_{(i-1)n+(j-m)}&=\begin{cases}E_{ji}\qquad&\text{for}\ 1\leq i\leq p,\ m+1\leq j\leq m+n,\\
-E_{ij}\qquad&\text{for}\ p+1\leq i\leq m,\ m+1\leq j\leq m+n.\end{cases}\end{split}

Here, the
x_{k}
’s span the space of negative odd roots
{\mathfrak{n}}_{\bar{1}}^{-}={\mathfrak{p}}_{2}\oplus{\mathfrak{q}}_{1}
and the
\partial_{k}
’s span the space of positive odd roots
{\mathfrak{n}}_{\bar{1}}^{+}={\mathfrak{p}}_{1}\oplus{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}
(see Section
2.2.1
).

Remark 34
.

It is important to note that, for the Lagrangian subspaces
{\mathfrak{l}}^{+}\coloneqq{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}\oplus{\mathfrak{p}}_{2}
and
{\mathfrak{l}}^{-}\coloneqq{\mathfrak{q}}_{1}\oplus{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}
associated with the standard positive system, there does not exist a basis
x_{1},\ldots,x_{mn}
of
{\mathfrak{l}}^{-}
and
\partial_{1},\ldots,\partial_{mn}
of
{\mathfrak{l}}^{+}
that simultaneously satisfies
B(\partial_{i},x_{j})=\frac{1}{2}\delta_{ij}
and
\omega(x_{i})=-\partial_{i}
for all
1\leq i,j\leq mn
.

The
Weyl algebra

{\mathscr{W}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}
is defined as the quotient
T({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})/I_{\mathcal{W}}
, where
I_{\mathcal{W}}
is the two-sided ideal generated by all elements of the form
v\otimes w-w\otimes v-2B(v,w)
for
v,w\in{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}
. As
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}
is odd, we have by definition
{\operatorname{Cl}}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})={\mathscr{W}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}
. With this notation, the Weyl algebra
{\mathscr{W}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}
over
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}
is generated by
\partial_{i}
and
x_{j}
,
i.e.
, it can be identified with the algebra of differential operators with polynomial coefficients in the variables
x_{1},\dotsc,x_{mn}
, by identifying
\partial_{i}
with the partial derivative
\partial/\partial x_{i}
for all
i=1,\dotsc,mn
. In particular, with the canonical Lie bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]_{{\mathcal{W}}}
, the Weyl algebra
{\mathscr{W}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}
is a Lie algebra with the following commutation relations:

(4.10)

[x_{i},x_{j}]_{\mathscr{W}}=0,\qquad[\partial_{i},\partial_{j}]_{\mathscr{W}}=0,\qquad[\partial_{i},x_{j}]_{\mathscr{W}}=\delta_{ij},

for all
1\leq i,j\leq mn
.

Since
{\mathscr{W}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}={\operatorname{Cl}}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
, the Weyl algebra has a natural
{\mathbb{Z}}
-grading induced by the grading of
T({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
. Similarly, the symmetric superalgebra
\operatorname{S}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
has a natural
{\mathbb{Z}}
-grading
\operatorname{S}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}S^{n}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
. As
{\mathbb{Z}}
-graded vector spaces, they are isomorphic, with isomorphism given by the symmetrization map

(4.11)

\text{sym}:\operatorname{S}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})\to{\mathscr{W}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})},\qquad\text{sym}(v_{1}\ldots v_{k})\coloneqq\tfrac{1}{k!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{k}}v_{\sigma(1)}\ldots v_{\sigma(k)}.

In
\operatorname{S}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
, the
symmetric square

S^{2}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
is a Lie algebra spanned by
\{x_{i}x_{j},\partial_{i}\partial_{j}:i\leq j\}\cup\{\partial_{i}x_{j}\}
. Under the isomorphism above, the image of
S^{2}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
is the Lie algebra
\text{sym}(S^{2}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}))
spanned by

(4.12)

\text{sym}(x_{i}x_{j})=x_{i}x_{j},\qquad\text{sym}(\partial_{i}\partial_{j})=\partial_{i}\partial_{j},\qquad\text{sym}(\partial_{i}x_{j})=\partial_{i}x_{j}-\frac{1}{2}\delta_{ij}

in the basis
\{x_{i},\partial_{j}\}
. Now, if
\mathfrak{sp}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
denotes the complex symplectic algebra over the vector space
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}
defined with respect to the symplectic form
B|_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}}(\cdot,\cdot)
, a direct calculation yields an isomorphism of Lie algebras

(4.13)

(\mathfrak{sp}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}),[\cdot,\cdot])\cong(\operatorname{sym}(S^{2}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})),[\cdot,\cdot]_{W}).

This identification yields a natural embedding of
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
into
({\mathscr{W}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})},[\cdot,\cdot]_{W})
.
Indeed, the adjoint action of
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
on
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}
, induced by the super Lie bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]
of
{\mathfrak{g}}
, defines a Lie algebra homomorphism

(4.14)

\nu:{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{sp}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}),

since
B
is invariant.
For
m\neq n
the kernel of
\nu
is trivial, whereas for
m=n
it consists precisely of the scalar multiples of the identity matrix. This proves the following lemma.

Lemma 35
.

There exists a Lie algebra homomorphism
\alpha:{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}\to{\mathscr{W}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}
which is injective for
m\neq n
, and whose kernel is
{\mathbb{C}}E_{m|n}
for
m=n
.

For later use, this Lie algebra homomorphism is given explicitly by (
cf.

[
19
, Equation 10]
):

(4.15)

\alpha(X)=\sum_{i,j=1}^{mn}(B(X,[\partial_{i},\partial_{j}])x_{i}x_{j}+B(X,[x_{i},x_{j}])\partial_{i}\partial_{j})\\
-\sum_{i,j=1}^{mn}2B(X,[x_{i},\partial_{j}])x_{j}\partial_{i}-\sum_{l=1}^{mn}B(X,[\partial_{l},x_{l}])

for any
X\in{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
.

We define a diagonal embedding with respect to
\alpha
(
cf.
(
4.6
)):

(4.16)

{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}\to{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes{\mathscr{W}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})},\qquad X\mapsto X\otimes 1+1\otimes\alpha(X).

We denote the images of
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
,
{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}})
, and
\mathfrak{Z}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}})
by
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0},\Updelta},{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0},\Updelta})
, and
\mathfrak{Z}({\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0},\Updelta})
, respectively. Furthermore, the image of the quadratic Casimir
\Omega_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}
of
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
is denoted by
\Omega_{{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}},\Updelta}
. The quadratic Casimir of
{\mathfrak{g}}
is denoted by
\Omega_{{\mathfrak{g}}}
. The following lemma follows from a direct calculation.

Lemma 36

(
[
18
,
26
]
)
.

Let
\{W_{k}\}
be an orthonormal basis for
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
with respect to
B(\cdot,\cdot)
. Then the following assertions hold:

a)

\Omega_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}=\sum_{k}W_{k}^{2}
.

b)

\Omega_{{\mathfrak{g}}}=\Omega_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}+2\sum_{i}(x_{i}\partial_{i}-\partial_{i}x_{i})
.

c)

\Omega_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}},\Updelta}=\sum_{k}\left(W_{k}^{2}\otimes 1+2W_{k}\otimes\alpha(W_{k})+1\otimes\alpha(W_{k})^{2}\right)
.

d)

C\coloneqq\sum_{k}\alpha(W_{k})^{2}

4.1.2.
Relative quadratic Dirac operators

Our basic example is the quadratic pair
({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}})
with
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}\oplus{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}
and
[{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}},{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}]\subseteq{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
. We therefore begin with a general quadratic pair
({\mathfrak{l}},{\mathfrak{u}})
such that

(4.17)

{\mathfrak{l}}={\mathfrak{u}}\oplus{\mathfrak{p}},\qquad[{\mathfrak{p}},{\mathfrak{p}}]\subseteq{\mathfrak{u}}.

The condition
[{\mathfrak{p}},{\mathfrak{p}}]\subseteq{\mathfrak{u}}
is the algebraic input needed for the relative quadratic Dirac operator. To encode the
{\mathfrak{u}}
-structure inside the Weil algebra, we write
W({\mathfrak{l}},{\mathfrak{u}})
and
{\mathcal{W}}({\mathfrak{l}},{\mathfrak{u}})
for the subalgebras of
{\mathfrak{u}}
-invariants in
W({\mathfrak{l}})
and
{\mathcal{W}}({\mathfrak{l}})
, respectively, where the
{\mathfrak{u}}
-action on
{\mathcal{W}}({\mathfrak{l}})
is induced by (
4.6
). Concretely, the
{\mathfrak{u}}
-action is induced by the adjoint action on both factors, which is for
{\mathcal{W}}({\mathfrak{l}})
precisely
[\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}(x),\cdot]_{{\mathcal{W}}}
for any
x\in{\mathfrak{u}}
.

Choose a homogeneous basis
\{e_{a}\}
of
{\mathfrak{l}}
with
B
-dual basis
\{e^{a}\}
. Then

(4.18)

\mathcal{D}_{{\mathfrak{l}}}\coloneqq\sum_{a}e^{a}\otimes e_{a}\in W({\mathfrak{l}})

is the canonical quadratic Dirac element attached to
{\mathfrak{l}}
. Likewise, for
{\mathfrak{u}}
one has
\mathcal{D}_{{\mathfrak{u}}}\in W({\mathfrak{u}})
. Passing to quantizations using
\mathcal{Q}
(see Theorem
32
) gives the operators
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{l}}}
and
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{u}}}
. Their difference defines the
{\mathfrak{u}}
-relative quadratic Dirac operator
:

(4.19)

\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{l}},{\mathfrak{u}}}\coloneqq j(\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{u}}})-\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{l}}}.

Lemma 37

(
[
24
]
)
.

The operator
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{l}},{\mathfrak{u}}}
satisfies the following properties:

a)

It is independent of the choice of basis.

b)

One has
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{l}},{\mathfrak{u}}}\in{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{l}})\otimes{\operatorname{Cl}}({\mathfrak{p}})
.

c)

It is
{\mathfrak{u}}
-invariant for the induced
{\mathfrak{u}}
-action on
{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{l}})\otimes{\operatorname{Cl}}({\mathfrak{p}})
, that is,

[j(\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}(x)),\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{l}},{\mathfrak{u}}}]_{{\mathcal{W}}}=0\qquad\forall\,x\in{\mathfrak{u}}.

d)

\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{l}},{\mathfrak{u}}}
defines a differential on
{\mathcal{W}}({\mathfrak{l}},{\mathfrak{u}})
by

d^{{\mathcal{W}}}\coloneqq[\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{l}},{\mathfrak{u}}},\cdot]_{{\mathcal{W}}}.

The fact that
d^{\mathcal{W}}

squares to zero means that
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{l}},{\mathfrak{u}}}
squares to a central element.

4.1.3.
The Dirac operator
\operatorname{D}

In this article, we fix a Lie superalgebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
of type
A
. Since
{\mathfrak{g}}
is quadratic and
B|_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}
is symmetric, non-degenerate, and invariant, the pair
({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}})
is quadratic. Thus we may define the
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-relative quadratic Dirac operator

(4.20)

\operatorname{D}\coloneqq j(\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}})-\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}},

which we simply call the
Dirac operator
. This is the operator introduced in
[
19
]
. In what follows, we identify
{\mathcal{W}}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}})
with its image in
{\mathcal{W}}({\mathfrak{g}})
under
j
and suppress
j
from the notation.

Since the definition of
\operatorname{D}
is basis-independent, we choose the basis
\{x_{i},\partial_{i}\}
introduced above, whose
B
-dual basis is
\{-2\partial_{i},2x_{i}\}
. With this choice,

(4.21)

\operatorname{D}=2\sum_{i=1}^{mn}\bigl(\partial_{i}\otimes x_{i}-x_{i}\otimes\partial_{i}\bigr)\in{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes{\mathscr{W}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}.

By Lemma
37
, the operator
\operatorname{D}
is
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-invariant. For completeness, we record the square of
\operatorname{D}
(
cf.

[
19
, Proposition 2]
).

Theorem 38
.

One has

\operatorname{D}^{2}=-\Omega_{{\mathfrak{g}}}\otimes 1+\Omega_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}},\Updelta}+C(1\otimes 1),\qquad C=-\frac{1}{24}\bigl(\operatorname{str}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\operatorname{ad}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\Omega_{{\mathfrak{g}}}))-\operatorname{str}_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}(\operatorname{ad}_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}(\Omega_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}))\bigr)

where
C
is the constant of Lemma
36
.

Remark 39
.

For explicit calculations, it is particularly useful to express the square of the Dirac operator in the following alternative form:

\operatorname{D}^{2}=2\sum_{i,j=1}^{mn}([\partial_{i},\partial_{j}]\otimes x_{i}x_{j}+[x_{i},x_{j}]\otimes\partial_{i}\partial_{j}-2[\partial_{i},x_{j}]\otimes x_{i}\partial_{j})-4\sum_{i=1}^{mn}x_{i}\partial_{i}\otimes 1.

We finally decompose
\operatorname{D}
in appropriate
{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}
-invariant pieces. To this end, recall that
x_{1},\ldots,x_{mn}
span
{\mathfrak{n}}_{\bar{1}}^{-}={\mathfrak{p}}_{2}\oplus{\mathfrak{q}}_{1}
and
\partial_{1},\ldots,\partial_{mn}
span
{\mathfrak{n}}_{\bar{1}}^{+}={\mathfrak{p}}_{1}\oplus{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}
. The spaces
{\mathfrak{p}}_{1,2}
and
{\mathfrak{q}}_{1,2}
(see Section
2.2.1
) are invariant under
{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}
, but not under
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
. Accordingly, we decompose the Dirac operator as
\operatorname{D}\coloneqq\operatorname{D}^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}+\operatorname{D}^{{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}}
, where

(4.22)

\begin{split}{\operatorname{D}^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}}&\coloneqq 2({\mathrm{d}^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}}-{\delta^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}}),\qquad{\mathrm{d}^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}}\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{pn}\partial_{i}\otimes x_{i},\qquad\ \ \ \ {\delta^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}}\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{pn}x_{i}\otimes\partial_{i},\\
{\operatorname{D}^{{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}}}&\coloneqq 2({\mathrm{d}^{{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}}}-{\delta^{{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}}}),\qquad{\mathrm{d}^{{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}}}\coloneqq\sum_{i=pn+1}^{mn}\partial_{i}\otimes x_{i},\qquad{\delta^{{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}}}\coloneqq\sum_{i=pn+1}^{mn}x_{i}\otimes\partial_{i}.\end{split}

Lemma 40
.

The operators
{\mathrm{d}^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}},{\mathrm{d}^{{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}}}
and
{\delta^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}},{\delta^{{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}}}
are
{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}
-invariant, meaning
[\mathrm{d}^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1},{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}},{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}]=0
and
[\delta^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1},{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}},{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}]=0
.

Proof.

We prove the statement only for
{\mathrm{d}^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}}=\sum_{i=1}^{pn}\partial_{i}\otimes x_{i}
; the rest follows analogously.

Let
X\in{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}
. Since
{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}
leaves
{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}
invariant by Lemma
7
, the commutators in the standard basis are given by

[X,x_{i}]=-2\sum_{j=1}^{pn}B(X,[x_{i},\partial_{j}])x_{j},\qquad[X,\partial_{i}]=2\sum_{j=1}^{pn}B(X,[\partial_{i},x_{j}])\partial_{j}

for all
1\leq i\leq pn
. This leads to the following computation:

\begin{split}[\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}(X),{\mathrm{d}^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}}]_{{\mathcal{W}}}&=\sum_{i=1}^{pn}\left([X,\partial_{i}]\otimes x_{i}+\partial_{i}\otimes[X,x_{i}]\right)\\
&=2\sum_{i,j=1}^{pn}B(X,[\partial_{i},x_{j}])\partial_{j}\otimes x_{i}-2\sum_{i,j=1}^{pn}B(X,[x_{i},\partial_{j}])\partial_{i}\otimes x_{j}\\
&=2\sum_{i,j=1}^{pn}\left(-B(X,[x_{i},\partial_{j}])+B(X,[\partial_{j},x_{i}])\right)\partial_{i}\otimes x_{j}\\
&=0,\end{split}

where we used in the last equality
B(X,[\partial_{i},x_{j}])=B(X,[x_{j},\partial_{i}])
for all
1\leq i,j\leq pn
since
x_{i}
and
\partial_{j}
are odd.
∎

4.2.
Dirac cohomology

The Dirac cohomology assigns to any
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule
M
a
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-supermodule
\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}(M)
, utilizing the
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-invariance of the Dirac operator
\operatorname{D}
. To define it, we consider the natural componentwise action of
\operatorname{D}\in{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes{\mathscr{W}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}
on
M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
, where
M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
denotes the oscillator module over
{\mathscr{W}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}
, which we briefly introduce.

4.2.1.
Oscillator module

The Weyl algebra
{\mathscr{W}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}
, identified with the algebra of differential operators with polynomial coefficients in the variables
x_{i}
, where
\partial_{i}
corresponds to the partial derivative
\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}
, acts naturally on
M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})\coloneqq{\mathbb{C}}[x_{1},\dotsc,x_{mn}]
. We refer to
M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
as the
oscillator module
. Any element in
M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
that is annihilated by all
\partial_{i}
is
necessarily constant. We conclude that the maximal proper submodule of
M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
is zero,
and
M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
is a simple module over
{\mathscr{W}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}
.

Proposition 41
.

The oscillator module
M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
is a simple
{\mathscr{W}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}
-module.

We equip
M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
with a
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-grading by declaring
M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})_{\bar{0}}
to be the subspace generated by homogeneous polynomials of even degree, and
M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})_{\bar{1}}
to be the subspace generated by homogeneous polynomials of odd degree.

Additionally,
M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
carries a unique Hermitian form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}
, known as the
Bargmann–Fock Hermitian form
or
Fischer–Fock Hermitian form

[
4
,
11
,
12
]
, such that
\partial_{k}
and
x_{k}
are adjoint to each other and the following orthogonality relations hold:

(4.23)

\begin{split}\langle\prod_{k=1}^{mn}x_{k}^{p_{k}},\prod_{k=1}^{mn}x_{k}^{q_{k}}\rangle_{M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}=\begin{cases}\prod_{k=1}^{mn}p_{k}!\qquad&\text{if}\ p_{k}=q_{k}\ \text{for all}\ k,\\
0\qquad&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}\end{split}

The conjugate-linear anti-involution
\omega
induces a
∗
-structure on
{\operatorname{Cl}}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})={\mathscr{W}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}
, that is, a conjugate-linear involution
(\cdot)^{\ast}:{\mathscr{W}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}\to{\mathscr{W}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}
such that
(xy)^{\ast}=y^{\ast}x^{\ast}
for all
x,y\in{\mathscr{W}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}
.

Lemma 42
.

The conjugate-linear involution
\omega
induces a
∗
-structure on
{\operatorname{Cl}}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})={\mathscr{W}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}
via
x^{\ast}\coloneqq-\omega(x)
such that
x_{i}^{\ast}=\partial_{i}
.

Proof.

We first note that
\omega({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})\subset{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}
.
We extend
\omega
to
T({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
by defining it on simple tensors via

\omega(v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{k})\coloneqq\omega(v_{k})\otimes\cdots\otimes\omega(v_{1}).

This map descends to
{\operatorname{Cl}}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
provided that it preserves the ideal
I_{{\operatorname{Cl}}}
generated by the elements

v\otimes w+(-1)^{p(v)p(w)}w\otimes v-2B(v,w)\,1_{T({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}.

Applying
\omega
shows that this condition is equivalent to

\overline{B(w,v)}=B(\omega(v),\omega(w))\qquad\text{for all }v,w\in{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}.

Recall that
\omega
is defined by

\omega(x)=J_{(p,q|0,n)}^{-1}x^{\dagger}J_{(p,q|0,n)},\qquad x\in{\mathfrak{g}}.

Hence, for all
x,y\in{\mathfrak{g}}
we obtain

B(\omega(x),\omega(y))=\tfrac{1}{2}\operatorname{str}(\omega(x)\omega(y))=\tfrac{1}{2}\operatorname{str}\!\left(J_{(p,q|0,n)}^{-1}x^{\dagger}y^{\dagger}J_{(p,q|0,n)}\right)=\tfrac{1}{2}\overline{\operatorname{str}(yx)}=\overline{B(y,x)}.

Now, set
v^{\ast}\coloneqq-\omega(v)
.
Since
\omega
preserves
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}
and, by construction, satisfies
\omega(vw)=\omega(w)\omega(v)
for all
v,w\in{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}
, the map
(\cdot)^{\ast}:{\mathscr{W}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}\to{\mathscr{W}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}
is a
∗
-structure on
{\mathscr{W}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}
.
∎

We fix the
∗
-structure on
{\mathscr{W}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}
induced by
\omega
.
A
{\mathscr{W}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}
-module
M
is called a
∗
-module if it is equipped with a positive definite Hermitian form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
satisfying

(4.24)

\langle xv,w\rangle=\langle v,x^{\ast}w\rangle\qquad x\in{\mathscr{W}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})},\;v,w\in M.

This yields the following lemma.

Lemma 43
.

The oscillator module
M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
, together with the form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})})
, is a
∗
-module over
{\mathscr{W}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}
. In particular, the generators of
{\mathscr{W}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}
satisfy the following relations for all
1\leq i\leq mn
:

\langle\partial_{i}v,w\rangle_{M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}=\langle v,x_{i}w\rangle_{M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})},\qquad\langle x_{i}v,w\rangle_{M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}=\langle v,\partial_{i}w\rangle_{M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}.

We finally consider
M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
as a
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-module under the Lie algebra homomorphism
\alpha:{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}\to{\mathscr{W}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}
.

Lemma 44
.

As operators on
M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
, one has for any
X\in{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}

\alpha(X)^{\ast}=\alpha(\omega(X)).

Proof.

We first recall
x_{i}^{\ast}=\partial_{i}
, and
\overline{B(x,y)}=B(\omega(y),\omega(x))
. The Lie algebra homomorphism
\alpha
is explicitly given by

\alpha(X)=\sum_{i,j=1}^{mn}(B(X,[\partial_{i},\partial_{j}])x_{i}x_{j}+B(X,[x_{i},x_{j}])\partial_{i}\partial_{j})\\
-\sum_{i,j=1}^{mn}2B(X,[x_{i},\partial_{j}])x_{j}\partial_{i}-\sum_{l=1}^{mn}B(X,[\partial_{l},x_{l}]).

We demonstrate the statement for the first summand
B(X,[\partial_{i},\partial_{j}])x_{i}x_{j}
. The rest follows by a similar line of argument. One has

\displaystyle(B(X,[\partial_{i},\partial_{j}])x_{i}x_{j})^{\ast}

\displaystyle=\overline{B(X,[\partial_{i},\partial_{j}])}x_{j}^{\ast}x_{i}^{\ast}

\displaystyle=B(\omega([\partial_{i},\partial_{j}]),\omega(X))\partial_{j}\partial_{i}

\displaystyle=B([\omega(\partial_{j}),\omega(\partial_{i})],\omega(X))\partial_{j}\partial_{i}

.

The action of

Proof.

First, we recall the commutation relations from Lemma

[\partial_{i},\partial_{j}]\in{\mathfrak{n}}_{\bar{0},nc}^{+},\quad[x_{i},x_{j}]\in{\mathfrak{n}}_{\bar{0},nc}^{-},\quad[x_{i},\partial_{i}]\in{\mathfrak{h}},\quad[{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}},{\mathfrak{n}}_{\bar{0},nc}^{\pm}]\subset{\mathfrak{n}}_{\bar{0},nc}^{\pm}

whenever

We claim that

for any

4.3.
Dirac induction

For general
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules, Dirac cohomology has poor functorial properties. In particular,

Lemma 53
.

Let

be a short exact sequence of
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules admitting an infinitesimal character. Then there exists a six-term exact sequence:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}^{+}(M^{\prime})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}^{+}(M)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}^{+}(M^{\prime\prime})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}^{-}(M^{\prime\prime})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}^{-}(M)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}^{-}(M^{\prime})}

Proof.

We modify
[
15
, Theorem 8.1]
to our setting. We tensor the short exact sequence with
M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
and denote the arrows again by
a
and
b
, which get tensored with the identity on
M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
. As a result, we obtain a right exact sequence

M^{\prime}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})\to M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})\to M^{\prime\prime}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})\to 0.

However,
m^{\prime\prime}\neq 0
, yielding
c=0
. In addition, we have
b(\operatorname{D}\!m)=\operatorname{D}\!b(m)=\operatorname{D}\!m^{\prime\prime}=0
,
i.e.
,
\operatorname{D}\!m
lies in

, and we find some
m^{\prime}\in M
with
\operatorname{D}\!m=a(m^{\prime})
. The element

determines a cohomology class, as

is injective. This class is by definition the image of the class of

under the connecting homomorphism. This defines both vertical arrows. Exactness is verified by a direct calculation, which we omit.
∎

This motivates an alternative definition of Dirac cohomology, which agrees with the classical one on unitarizable supermodules. The construction goes back to
[

]
. Following
[
33
]
, we define a functor, again called

Dirac

on the subcategory of unitarizable supermodules.

Any
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule
M
extends to a
({\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes{\mathscr{W}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})})
-supermodule by
M\mapsto M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
. Accordingly, let
({\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes{\mathscr{W}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})})\textbf{-smod}^{\mathrm{osc}}
be the full subcategory of
({\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes{\mathscr{W}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})})\textbf{-smod}
consisting of supermodules isomorphic to

for some
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule
M
. We call it the

. Conversely, any object
X
of this subcategory defines a
{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})
-supermodule by
X\mapsto\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathscr{W}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}}(M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}),X)
, with the induced
{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})
-action.

Proposition 54
.

There is an equivalence of categories

F:{\mathfrak{g}}\textbf{-smod}\to\left({\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes{\mathscr{W}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}\right)\textbf{-smod}^{\text{osc}},\qquad F(M)\coloneqq M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})

with inverse
G(X)\coloneqq\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathscr{W}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}}(M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}),X)
.

Proof.

It is well known that
{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})
-smod
is equivalent to
{\mathfrak{g}}\textbf{-smod}
. By abuse of notation, we still use
F
for the functor from
{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})\textbf{-smod}
to
({\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes{\mathscr{W}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})})\textbf{-smod}^{\text{osc}}
.

We first show that
F
and
G
are naturally isomorphic on objects. For any
{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})
-supermodule
M
, we have the following isomorphisms:

\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathscr{W}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}}(M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}),M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}))\cong M\otimes\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathscr{W}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}}(M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}),M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}))\cong M,

where the second isomorphism uses the fact that

is a simple
{\mathscr{W}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}
-supermodule (see Proposition
41

and one has

Altogether, we have proved that the assignments

is full and faithful.

Proposition 55

5.
Dirac operators, Dirac cohomology and unitarizable supermodules

In this section we explain how the Dirac operator and Dirac cohomology reflect unitarity.
More precisely, we establish a Parthasarathy–Dirac inequality, show how the Dirac operator detects the unitarity of

(in particular, the selfadjointness of

with respect to

5.1.
Dirac operators and unitarity

Fix a unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule

with the Hermitian form defined by

(5.1)

\langle v\otimes P,w\otimes Q\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}\coloneqq\langle v,w\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}}\cdot\langle P,Q\rangle_{M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})},

for all
v\otimes P,w\otimes Q\in{\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
. If
{\mathcal{H}}
is simple, the Hermitian form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}
is positive definite and unique up to a real scalar, since
{\mathcal{H}}
is a highest weight supermodule and
M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
is a simple
∗
-module over
{\mathscr{W}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}
(
cf.
(
4.24
)).

We extend the conjugate-linear anti-involution
\omega
naturally to
{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})
. Moreover,
\omega
defines a conjugate-linear anti-involution on the Weyl algebra
{\mathscr{W}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}
, and we extend it in the obvious way to a conjugate-linear anti-involution on the tensor product
{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes{\mathscr{W}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}
, denoted by the same symbol.

The following lemma is a direct consequence of Lemma
43
.

Lemma 57
.

For all
X\in{\mathfrak{g}}
and for all generators
\partial_{k},x_{k}
of
{\mathscr{W}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}
, the following identities hold:

\displaystyle\langle(X\otimes x_{k})v,w\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}

\displaystyle=\langle v,(\omega(X)\otimes\partial_{k})w\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})},

\displaystyle\langle(X\otimes\partial_{k})v,w\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}

\displaystyle=\langle v,(\omega(X)\otimes x_{k})w\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})},

for all
v,w\in{\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
.

We decompose the Dirac operator
\operatorname{D}
as
\operatorname{D}={\operatorname{D}^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}}+{\operatorname{D}^{{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}}}
, where
{\operatorname{D}^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}},{\operatorname{D}^{{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}}}\in{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes{\mathscr{W}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}
are
{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}
-invariant elements defined in (
4.22
). Recall that

(5.2)

{\operatorname{D}^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}}=2({\mathrm{d}^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}}-{\delta^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}}),\qquad{\operatorname{D}^{{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}}}=2({\mathrm{d}^{{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}}}-{\delta^{{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}}}).

By Lemma
57
and the identity
\omega(x_{k})=-\partial_{k}
in
{\mathfrak{g}}
for all
k=1,\dots,mn
, the operators
{\mathrm{d}^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}}
and
{\delta^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}}
, as well as
{\mathrm{d}^{{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}}}
and
{\delta^{{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}}}
, are skew-adjoint to each other up to sign with respect to the Hermitian form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}
.

Lemma 58
.

The operators
{\mathrm{d}^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}}
and
{\delta^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}}
, and similarly
{\mathrm{d}^{{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}}}
and
{\delta^{{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}}}
, are skew-adjoint to each other with respect to the Hermitian form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}
,
i.e.
,

\langle\delta^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}v,w\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}=-\langle v,\mathrm{d}^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}w\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})},\qquad\langle\delta^{{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}}v,w\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}=-\langle v,\mathrm{d}^{{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}}w\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}.

In particular, the operators
{\operatorname{D}^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}}
,
{\operatorname{D}^{{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}}}
, and
\operatorname{D}
are selfadjoint on
{\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
.

Corollary 59
.

For all
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
, the operators
\operatorname{D}
,
{\operatorname{D}^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}}
, and
{\operatorname{D}^{{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}}}
satisfy:

\operatorname{ker}\operatorname{D}=\operatorname{ker}\operatorname{D}^{k},\qquad\operatorname{ker}{\operatorname{D}^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}}=\operatorname{ker}\big(\operatorname{D}^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}\big)^{k},\qquad\operatorname{ker}{\operatorname{D}^{{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}}}=\operatorname{ker}\big(\operatorname{D}^{{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}}\big)^{k}.

Another direct consequence of Lemma
58
is an inequality for
\operatorname{D}
, known as
Parthasarathy’s Dirac inequality
, or simply the
Dirac inequality
.

Proposition 60

(Parthasarathy’s Dirac inequality)
.

The square of the Dirac operator satisfies
\operatorname{D}^{2}\geq 0
on
{\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
; that is,

\langle\operatorname{D}^{2}v,v\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}\geq 0\quad\text{for all }v\in{\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}).

Proof.

Let
v\in{\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
be arbitrary. By Lemma
58
, the Dirac operator
\operatorname{D}
is selfadjoint with respect to the Hermitian form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}
. Moreover, this form is positive definite by construction. Consequently,

\langle\operatorname{D}\!v,\operatorname{D}\!v\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}=\langle\operatorname{D}^{2}v,v\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}\geq 0,

which proves the claim.
∎

We will specify this inequality in Proposition
74
for highest weight supermodules.

Let
M
be a simple
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule equipped with a positive definite Hermitian form, and assume that
M
is
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-semisimple. A natural question is: when does the Dirac operator
\operatorname{D}
act as a selfadjoint operator on
M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
?

Theorem 61
.

Let
M
be a simple
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule equipped with a positive definite Hermitian form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{M}
, such that
M_{\bar{0}}
and
M_{\bar{1}}
are mutually orthogonal. Assume that
M
is
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-semisimple. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

(M,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{M})
is a unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule.

(ii)

The Dirac operator
\operatorname{D}
is selfadjoint with respect to
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}
.

Proof.

If
M
is unitarizable with respect to
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{M}
, then
\operatorname{D}
is selfadjoint on
M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
by Lemma
58
.

Conversely, assume
\operatorname{D}
is selfadjoint with respect to
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}
. It suffices to show that the operators
\partial_{k}\otimes 1
and
x_{k}
have adjoints
\omega(\partial_{k})
and
\omega(x_{k})
, since they generate
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}
and
[{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}},{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}]={\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
. A direct calculation shows:

\operatorname{D}(1\otimes\partial_{k})-(1\otimes\partial_{k})\operatorname{D}=-2(\partial_{k}\otimes 1),\qquad(1\otimes x_{k})\operatorname{D}-\operatorname{D}(1\otimes x_{k})=2(x_{k}\otimes 1).

Taking adjoints
(\cdot)^{\dagger}
with respect to
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{M}
yields:

\left(\operatorname{D}(1\otimes\partial_{k})-(1\otimes\partial_{k})\operatorname{D}\right)^{\dagger}=(1\otimes x_{k})\operatorname{D}-\operatorname{D}(1\otimes x_{k}),

using Lemmas
43
and
58
. Consequently, for all
v,w\in M
, we compute:

\displaystyle\langle\partial_{k}v,w\rangle_{M}

\displaystyle=\langle(\partial_{k}\otimes 1)(v\otimes 1),w\otimes 1\rangle_{M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{1}{2}\langle(\operatorname{D}(1\otimes\partial_{k})-(1\otimes\partial_{k})\operatorname{D})(v\otimes 1),w\otimes 1\rangle_{M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{1}{2}\langle v\otimes 1,((1\otimes x_{k})\operatorname{D}-\operatorname{D}(1\otimes x_{k}))(w\otimes 1)\rangle_{M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}

\displaystyle=-\langle v\otimes 1,(x_{k}\otimes 1)(w\otimes 1)\rangle_{M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}

\displaystyle=-\langle v,x_{k}w\rangle_{M}

\displaystyle=\langle v,\omega(\partial_{k})w\rangle_{M}.

Analogously, one shows that
\langle x_{k}v,w\rangle_{M}=\langle v,\omega(x_{k})w\rangle_{M}
.
This completes the proof.
∎

In general, the (Kac) induction of a unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-supermodule is not itself unitarizable. Moreover, it can happen that a simple
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule
M
restricts to a unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-module, even though
M
is not unitarizable as a
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule. The Dirac operator provides a criterion that helps clarify this situation.

Corollary 62
.

Let
M
be a simple
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule. Assume that
M
admits an infinitesimal character, is
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-semisimple and it is equipped with a
\omega|_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}
-contravariant positive definite Hermitian form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{M}
such that
M_{\bar{0}}
and
M_{\bar{1}}
are mutually orthogonal. Then
M
is unitarizable as a
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule if and only if the following conditions hold:

(i)

All eigenvalues of
\operatorname{D}^{2}
on
M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
are non-negative.

(ii)

For each eigenvalue
\lambda^{2}
of
\operatorname{D}^{2}
, and for all
v,w\in\operatorname{ker}(\operatorname{D}^{2}-\lambda^{2})
we have

\langle\operatorname{D}\!v,\operatorname{D}\!w\rangle_{M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}=\lambda^{2}\langle v,w\rangle_{M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}.

Proof.

We adapt the argument from
[
34
, Corollary 2]
. By assumption and Proposition
9
, the
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-module
M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
decomposes completely into simples.

Moreover, by Theorem
38
and Proposition
60
, the operator
\operatorname{D}^{2}
acts on each
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-isotypic component as a positive scalar
\lambda^{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}
.

On each such component,
\operatorname{D}
has eigenvalues
\pm\lambda
, and these components are mutually orthogonal with respect to the Hermitian form. The operator
\operatorname{D}
is selfadjoint on
\operatorname{ker}(\operatorname{D}^{2}-\lambda^{2})
if and only if the
+\lambda
and
-\lambda
eigenspaces are orthogonal. Indeed, for any
v\in\operatorname{ker}(\operatorname{D}^{2}-\lambda^{2})
, the span of
v
and
\operatorname{D}\!v
is
\operatorname{D}
-invariant and contains the eigenvectors
\lambda v\pm\operatorname{D}\!v
with eigenvalues
\pm\lambda
. These eigenspaces are orthogonal if and only if

\langle\lambda v+\operatorname{D}\!v,\lambda w-\operatorname{D}\!w\rangle_{M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}=0

for all
v,w\in\operatorname{ker}(\operatorname{D}^{2}-\lambda^{2})
. A direct computation shows that this is equivalent to

\langle\operatorname{D}\!v,\operatorname{D}\!w\rangle_{M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}=\lambda^{2}\langle v,w\rangle_{M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}.\qed

We decompose the
{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes{\mathscr{W}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}
-supermodule
{\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
, where
{\mathcal{H}}
is a fixed unitarizable simple
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule, with respect to the Dirac operator
\operatorname{D}
. Since
{\mathcal{H}}
is unitarizable and simple, it is completely reducible as both a
{\mathfrak{g}}
- and a
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-supermodule (see Proposition
9
). Moreover, the oscillator module
M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
is also unitarizable and completely reducible as a
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-module, decomposing into a direct sum of unitarizable highest weight constituents (see Remark
46
). Consequently,
{\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
is a completely reducible unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-supermodule.

On any simple
{\mathfrak{g}}
-constituent of
{\mathcal{H}}
, the quadratic Casimir acts as a scalar multiple of the identity. Similarly, on any simple
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-constituent of
{\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
, the even quadratic Casimir acts as a scalar by Dixmier’s Theorem
[
9
, Proposition 2.6.8]
. By Theorem
38
, it follows that
\operatorname{D}^{2}
is a semisimple operator on
{\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
, and we can decompose
{\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
into eigenspaces
({\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}))(c)
corresponding to eigenvalue
c
, that is,

(5.3)

{\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})=\bigoplus_{c}({\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}))(c),

where the direct sum is orthogonal with respect to the Hermitian form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}
.

Each eigenspace is a
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-supermodule since
\operatorname{D}
is
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-invariant (Lemma
37
). In particular, the eigenspace with eigenvalue
c=0
is precisely the kernel of
\operatorname{D}
, as
\operatorname{ker}\operatorname{D}=\operatorname{ker}\operatorname{D}^{k}
for all
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
.

More generally, such a decomposition with respect to
\operatorname{D}^{2}
exists for any
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule
{\mathcal{H}}
of finite length that is
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-semisimple.

Lemma 63
.

Let
M
be a
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule that admits an infinitesimal character and is
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-semisimple. Then, as a
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-supermodule, it decomposes into a direct sum of generalized
\operatorname{D}^{2}
-eigenspaces:

M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})=\bigoplus_{c\in{\mathbb{C}}}(M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}))(c),

where

(M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}))(c)\coloneqq\left\{v\in M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})\mid\exists n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}\text{ such that }(c\cdot\operatorname{id}-\operatorname{D}^{2})^{n}v=0\right\}.

The proof proceeds
mutatis mutandis
as in
[
1
, Corollary 3.3]
.

Lemma 64
.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}
be a unitarizable simple
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule. Then there is an orthogonal decomposition:

{\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})=\operatorname{ker}\operatorname{D}^{2}\oplus\operatorname{im}\operatorname{D}^{2},

where the direct sum is orthogonal with respect to the Hermitian form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}
.

Proof.

First, we recall the eigenspace decomposition with respect to
\operatorname{D}^{2}
:

{\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})=({\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}))(0)\oplus\bigoplus_{c\neq 0}({\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}))(c),

where the zero eigenspace satisfies
({\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}))(0)=\operatorname{ker}\operatorname{D}=\operatorname{ker}\operatorname{D}^{2}
.

Since
\operatorname{D}^{2}
is a semisimple and selfadjoint operator on
({\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}),\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})})
, it follows that
\operatorname{im}\operatorname{D}^{2}=(\operatorname{ker}\operatorname{D}^{2})^{\perp}
,
i.e.
, the image and kernel are orthogonal complements. Hence,

\operatorname{im}\operatorname{D}^{2}=(\operatorname{ker}\operatorname{D}^{2})^{\perp}=({\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}))(0)^{\perp}=\bigoplus_{c\neq 0}({\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}))(c).

This yields the desired orthogonal decomposition.
∎

5.2.
Dirac cohomology and unitarity

The Dirac cohomology functor

(5.4)

\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}(\cdot):{\mathfrak{g}}\textbf{-smod}\to{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0},\Updelta}\textbf{-smod}\cong{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}\textbf{-smod}

assigns to a
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule
M
its Dirac cohomology
\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}(M)\coloneqq\operatorname{ker}\operatorname{D}\big/\left(\operatorname{ker}\operatorname{D}\cap\operatorname{im}\operatorname{D}\right)
. In this section, we investigate the Dirac cohomology of unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules and show that Dirac cohomology provides a complete characterization.

5.2.1.
Basic facts about Dirac cohomology of unitarizable supermodules

For unitarizable supermodules, Dirac cohomology admits a particularly simple description: it coincides with the kernel of the Dirac operator.

Proposition 65
.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}
be a unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule. Then

\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}({\mathcal{H}})=\operatorname{ker}\operatorname{D}.

Proof.

We prove that
\operatorname{ker}\operatorname{D}\cap\operatorname{im}\operatorname{D}=\{0\}
. Let
v\in\operatorname{ker}\operatorname{D}\cap\operatorname{im}\operatorname{D}
, so that
\operatorname{D}\!v=0
and there exists
w\in{\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
with
v=\operatorname{D}\!w
. Since
{\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
is equipped with a positive definite Hermitian form, we compute:

\langle v,v\rangle=\langle\operatorname{D}\!w,v\rangle=\langle w,\operatorname{D}\!v\rangle=0.

Thus
v=0
, and the claim follows.
∎

As an immediate consequence, Dirac cohomology is additive on direct sums of unitarizable supermodules.

Lemma 66
.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}_{1},{\mathcal{H}}_{2}
be unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules. Then

\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}({\mathcal{H}}_{1}\oplus{\mathcal{H}}_{2})=\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}({\mathcal{H}}_{1})\oplus\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}({\mathcal{H}}_{2}).

We now express the Dirac operator as
\operatorname{D}={\operatorname{D}^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}}+{\operatorname{D}^{{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}}}
, where

{\operatorname{D}^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}}=2({\mathrm{d}^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}}-{\delta^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}}),\ {\operatorname{D}^{{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}}}=2({\mathrm{d}^{{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}}}-{\delta^{{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}}}),

and
{\mathrm{d}^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}},{\mathrm{d}^{{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}}},{\delta^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}},{\delta^{{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}}}
are
{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}
-invariant operators. As shown in Lemma
58
, these operators are skew-adjoint to one another with respect to the Hermitian form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}
. This decomposition provides a more explicit understanding of the kernel of
\operatorname{D}
. We now record some structural relations among the constituent operators.

Lemma 67
.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}
be a unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule.

a)

The operators
{\mathrm{d}^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}},{\delta^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}},{\mathrm{d}^{{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}}},{\delta^{{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}}}
square to zero.

b)

The operators
{\mathrm{d}^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}}
and
{\delta^{{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}}}
, and the operators
{\mathrm{d}^{{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}}}
and
{\delta^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}}
commute,
i.e.
,
[{\mathrm{d}^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}},{\delta^{{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}}}]=0
and
[{\mathrm{d}^{{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}}},{\delta^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}}]=0
.

c)

With respect to the form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}
, the following holds:

(i)

\operatorname{im}{\mathrm{d}^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}}
is orthogonal to
\operatorname{ker}{\delta^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}}
and
\operatorname{im}{\delta^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}}
;
\operatorname{im}{\delta^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}}
is orthogonal to
\operatorname{ker}{\mathrm{d}^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}}
.

(ii)

\operatorname{im}{\mathrm{d}^{{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}}}
is orthogonal to
\operatorname{ker}{\delta^{{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}}}
and
\operatorname{im}{\delta^{{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}}}
;
\operatorname{im}{\delta^{{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}}}
is orthogonal to
\operatorname{ker}{\mathrm{d}^{{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}}}
.

d)

\operatorname{ker}({\operatorname{D}^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}})^{2}=\operatorname{ker}{\operatorname{D}^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}}=\operatorname{ker}{\mathrm{d}^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}}\cap\operatorname{ker}{\delta^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}}
, and
\operatorname{ker}({\operatorname{D}^{{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}}})^{2}=\operatorname{ker}{\operatorname{D}^{{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}}}=\operatorname{ker}{\mathrm{d}^{{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}}}\cap\operatorname{ker}{\delta^{{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}}}
.

Proof.

a) This is a direct consequence of the fact that
{\mathfrak{p}}_{1,2}
and
{\mathfrak{q}}_{1,2}
are abelian Lie supersubalgebras of
{\mathfrak{g}}
.

b) This is a direct consequence of
[\partial_{k},x_{l}]=0
unless
k=l
.

c) We only prove that
\operatorname{im}{\mathrm{d}^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}}
and
\operatorname{ker}{\delta^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}}
are orthogonal; the rest can be proved similarly using a) and b). First, let
v\in\operatorname{im}{\mathrm{d}^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}}
and
w\in\operatorname{ker}{\delta^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}}
. Then there exists a non-trivial
v^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
such that
{\mathrm{d}^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}}v^{\prime}=v
, and consequently by Lemma
58

\langle v,w\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}=\langle{\mathrm{d}^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}}v^{\prime},w\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}=-\langle v^{\prime},{\delta^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}}w\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}=0,

i.e.
,
\langle\operatorname{im}{\mathrm{d}^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}},\operatorname{ker}{\delta^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}}\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}=0
.

d) The operators
{\operatorname{D}^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}}
and
{\operatorname{D}^{{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}}}
are selfadjoint by Lemma
58
, and therefore
\operatorname{ker}{\operatorname{D}^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}}=\operatorname{ker}({\operatorname{D}^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}})^{2}
and
\operatorname{ker}{\operatorname{D}^{{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}}}=\operatorname{ker}({\operatorname{D}^{{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}}})^{2}
. We prove that
\operatorname{ker}{\operatorname{D}^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}}=\operatorname{ker}{\mathrm{d}^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}}\cap\operatorname{ker}{\delta^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}}
. Let
v\in\operatorname{ker}{\operatorname{D}^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}}
, then
{\operatorname{D}^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}}v=2({\mathrm{d}^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}}-{\delta^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}})v=0
,
i.e.
,
{\mathrm{d}^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}}v={\delta^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}}v
. By b),
\operatorname{im}{\mathrm{d}^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}}
and
\operatorname{im}{\delta^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}}
are orthogonal to each other, hence
v\in\operatorname{ker}{\delta^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}}\cap\operatorname{ker}{\mathrm{d}^{{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}}}
. The other inclusion is trivial. Analogously, the equality
\operatorname{ker}{\operatorname{D}^{{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}}}=\operatorname{ker}{\mathrm{d}^{{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}}}\cap\operatorname{ker}{\delta^{{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}}}
follows.
∎

In summary, we can describe the Dirac cohomology of unitarizable supermodules in terms of
\operatorname{ker}{\operatorname{D}^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}}
and
\operatorname{ker}{\operatorname{D}^{{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}}}
.

Lemma 68
.

The Dirac cohomology of a unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule
{\mathcal{H}}
is

\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}({\mathcal{H}})=\operatorname{ker}{\operatorname{D}^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}}\cap\operatorname{ker}{\operatorname{D}^{{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}}}.

Proof.

We decompose the Dirac operator as
\operatorname{D}={\operatorname{D}^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}}+{\operatorname{D}^{{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}}}
, such that

\operatorname{ker}\operatorname{D}=\{v\in{\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}):{\operatorname{D}^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}}v=-{\operatorname{D}^{{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}}}v\}.

Here, note that
\left(\operatorname{ker}{\operatorname{D}^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}}\cap\operatorname{ker}{\operatorname{D}^{{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}}}\right)\subset\{v\in{\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}):{\operatorname{D}^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}}v=-{\operatorname{D}^{{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}}}v\}
. Assume
v\coloneqq m\otimes P\in{\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
satisfies
w\coloneqq{\operatorname{D}^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}}v=-{\operatorname{D}^{{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}}}v
. Then

\begin{split}0&\leq\langle w,w\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}\\
&=-\langle{\operatorname{D}^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}}v,{\operatorname{D}^{{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}}}v\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}\\
&=-\sum_{k=1}^{pn}\sum_{l=pn+1}^{mn}\langle(\partial_{k}-x_{k})m,(\partial_{l}-x_{l})m\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}}\langle(x_{k}-\partial_{k})P,(x_{l}-\partial_{l})P\rangle_{M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}.\end{split}

However, for all
1\leq k\leq pn
and
p+1\leq l\leq mn
, we have

\langle(x_{k}-\partial_{k})P\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}},(x_{l}-\partial_{l})P\rangle_{M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}=0

by the construction of the Bargmann–Fock form and
k\neq l
. We conclude
w=0
, as the Hermitian form is positive definite,
i.e.
,
{\operatorname{D}^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}}v=0
and
{\operatorname{D}^{{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}}}v=0
or
v\in(\operatorname{ker}{\operatorname{D}^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}}\cap\operatorname{ker}{\operatorname{D}^{{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}}})
. The assertion now follows from Proposition
65
.
∎

In Section
4.3
, we introduced a left exact functor
\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}^{\prime}(\cdot)
, which we also referred to as Dirac cohomology. It coincides with the Dirac cohomology
\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}(\cdot)
on unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules.

Corollary 69
.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}
be a unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule. Then the Dirac cohomologies
\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}({\mathcal{H}})
and
\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}^{\prime}({\mathcal{H}})
coincide. In particular,
\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}(\cdot)
is left exact in the category of unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules.

Proof.

The Dirac cohomology of a unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule
{\mathcal{H}}
is
\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}({\mathcal{H}})=\operatorname{ker}\operatorname{D}=\operatorname{ker}\operatorname{D}^{2}
by Corollary
59
and Proposition
65
. Moreover,
\operatorname{D}^{2}
commutes with any element of
({\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes{\mathscr{W}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})})^{{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}})}
by Theorem
38
, and it is an element of
\mathscr{I}
, where

\mathscr{I}
is the two-sided ideal in
({\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes{\mathscr{W}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})})^{{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}})}
generated by the Dirac operator
\operatorname{D}
. The Dirac cohomology
\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}^{\prime}({\mathcal{H}})
is defined as the
\mathscr{I}
-invariants in
{\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
. The relation
\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}^{\prime}({\mathcal{H}})\subset\operatorname{ker}\operatorname{D}^{2}
is immediate. Conversely, for any
v\in\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}({\mathcal{H}})
, we have
DXv=0
for any
X\in({\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes{\mathscr{W}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})})^{{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}})}
, as
\operatorname{D}^{2}\!Xv=X\!\operatorname{D}^{2}\!v=0
. This shows
\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}({\mathcal{H}})=\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}^{\prime}({\mathcal{H}})
. The second assertion follows by Proposition
55
.
∎

The Dirac cohomology
\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}({\mathcal{H}})
inherits the structure of a unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-supermodule. Since the oscillator module
M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
is itself a unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-supermodule (Proposition
45
), it follows that
{\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
is also a unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-supermodule. Here the action is induced by the diagonal embedding of
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
into
\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})
. Since the Dirac operator
\operatorname{D}
is
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-invariant, it follows that
\operatorname{ker}\operatorname{D}
likewise carries the structure of a unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-supermodule.

Proposition 70
.

The Dirac cohomology
\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}({\mathcal{H}})
of a unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule
{\mathcal{H}}
is a unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-supermodule. In particular, if
{\mathcal{H}}
is simple, it decomposes completely into unitarizable highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-supermodules.

5.2.2.
Computation of Dirac cohomology

Having established the basic properties of the Dirac cohomology for unitarizable supermodules, we now proceed to compute it explicitly. We begin by noting that general highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules possess non-trivial Dirac cohomology.

Proposition 71
.

Let
M
be a highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule with highest weight
\Lambda
. Then
\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}(M)
contains a highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-supermodule with highest weight
\Lambda-\rho_{\bar{1}}
that occurs with multiplicity one. In particular,
\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}(M)\neq\{0\}
.

Proof.

Let
v_{\Lambda}
be the highest weight vector of
M
, meaning that
{\mathfrak{n}}^{+}v_{\Lambda}=0
, and specifically
{\mathfrak{n}}_{\bar{1}}^{+}v_{\Lambda}=0
. Since
1
is constant and annihilated by
\partial_{k}
, the vector
v_{\Lambda}\otimes 1
lies in the kernel of

\operatorname{D}=2\sum_{k=1}^{mn}(\partial_{k}\otimes x_{k}-x_{k}\otimes\partial_{k}).

Further, we assert that
v_{\Lambda}\otimes 1
generates a highest weight
{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}})
-supermodule.

Any element
X\in{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
acts on
v_{\Lambda}\otimes 1
via the diagonal embedding:

Xv_{\Lambda}\otimes 1+v_{\Lambda}\otimes\alpha(X)1,

and we recall:

\alpha(X)=\sum_{k,j=1}^{mn}(B(X,[\partial_{k},\partial_{j}])x_{k}x_{j}+B(X,[x_{k},x_{j}])\partial_{k}\partial_{j})\\
-\sum_{k,j=1}^{mn}2B(X,[x_{k},\partial_{j}])x_{j}\partial_{k}-\sum_{l=1}^{mn}B(X,[\partial_{l},x_{l}]).

By Lemma
7
, we have the commutation relations
[\partial_{k},\partial_{j}]\in{\mathfrak{n}}_{\bar{0}}^{+}
,
[x_{k},x_{j}]\in{\mathfrak{n}}_{\bar{0}}^{-}
and
[x_{k},\partial_{j}]\in{\mathfrak{h}}
. Let
X\in{\mathfrak{n}}^{+}_{\bar{0}}
. Then the definition of
B(\cdot,\cdot)
forces

Xv_{\Lambda}\otimes 1+v_{\Lambda}\otimes\alpha(X)1=v_{\Lambda}\otimes\sum_{k,j=1}^{mn}B(X,[x_{k},x_{j}])\partial_{k}\partial_{j}1=0,

where we use that
{\mathfrak{n}}_{\bar{0}}^{+}v_{\Lambda}=0
.

Any
H\in{\mathfrak{h}}
acts on
v_{\Lambda}\otimes 1
by

\begin{split}Hv_{\Lambda}\otimes 1+v_{\Lambda}\otimes\alpha(H)v_{\Lambda}&=\Lambda(H)v_{\Lambda}\otimes 1-v_{\Lambda}\otimes\sum_{l=1}^{mn}B(H,[\partial_{l},x_{l}])1\\
&=\Lambda(H)v_{\Lambda}\otimes 1-v_{\Lambda}\otimes\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta_{\bar{1}}^{+}}\frac{1}{2}\alpha(H)1\\
&=(\Lambda-\rho_{\bar{1}})(H)(v_{\Lambda}\otimes 1).\end{split}

Hence,
v_{\Lambda}\otimes 1\in\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}(M)
generates a (simple) highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-supermodule, which is in particular unitarizable by Proposition
70
. This module appears with multiplicity one, as the weight spaces of
v_{\Lambda}
and
1
are one-dimensional.
∎

The following corollary is an immediate consequence of Proposition
9
, Theorem
12
and Proposition
71
.

Corollary 72
.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}
be a unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule. Then
\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}({\mathcal{H}})
is non-trivial.

To compute
\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}({\mathcal{H}})
explicitly, we use Theorem
38
. For that, we relate the constant
C
with the Weyl vector
\rho=\rho_{\bar{0}}-\rho_{\bar{1}}
.

Lemma 73
.

The constant is
C=-(\rho_{\bar{1}}-2\rho_{\bar{0}},\rho_{\bar{1}})
.

Proof.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}
be a non-trivial unitarizable highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule with highest weight
\Lambda
. Such a supermodule exists
[
22
]
. By Proposition
71
, there exists a
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-supermodule in
\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}({\mathcal{H}})
with highest weight
\Lambda-\rho_{\bar{1}}
. As
\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}({\mathcal{H}})=\operatorname{ker}\operatorname{D}=\operatorname{ker}\operatorname{D}^{2}
, we have by Theorem
52
:

0=-(\Lambda+2\rho,\Lambda)+(\Lambda-\rho_{\bar{1}}+2\rho_{\bar{0}},\Lambda-\rho_{\bar{1}})+C,

and a direct calculation yields

(\Lambda-\rho_{\bar{1}}+2\rho_{\bar{0}},\Lambda-\rho_{\bar{1}})=(\Lambda+2\rho,\Lambda)+(\rho_{\bar{1}}-2\rho_{\bar{0}},\rho_{\bar{1}}).

This finishes the proof.
∎

As a direct consequence, using the expression
\operatorname{D}^{2}=-\Omega_{{\mathfrak{g}}}\otimes 1+\Omega_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}},\Delta}-C
, we obtain an explicit form of the Dirac inequality for unitarizable simple supermodules.

Proposition 74
.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}
be a unitarizable simple
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule of highest weight
\Lambda
. If
L_{0}(\mu)
is a
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-constituent of
{\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
of highest weight
\mu
, then

(\mu+2\rho,\mu)\geq(\Lambda+2\rho,\Lambda).

In particular,
L_{0}(\mu)
belongs to
\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}({\mathcal{H}})
if and only if

(\Lambda+2\rho,\Lambda)=(\mu+2\rho,\mu).

Proof.

The square
\operatorname{D}^{2}=-\Omega_{{\mathfrak{g}}}\otimes 1+\Omega_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}},\Delta}+C
acts on
L_{0}(\mu)
by the scalar

-(\Lambda+2\rho,\Lambda)+(\mu-\rho_{\bar{1}}+2\rho_{\bar{0}},\mu-\rho_{\bar{1}})-(\rho_{\bar{1}}-2\rho_{\bar{0}},\rho_{\bar{1}})=-(\Lambda+2\rho,\Lambda)+(\mu+2\rho,\mu).

The first part of the proof follows by Proposition
60
and the second part by
\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}({\mathcal{H}})=\ker\operatorname{D}=\ker\operatorname{D}^{2}
.
∎

We now determine the Dirac cohomology of unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules. Since any unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule is completely reducible and
\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}(\cdot)
is additive, it suffices to consider unitarizable simple
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules. Our computation is based on the following consequence of Vogan’s theorem.

Lemma 75
.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}
be a unitarizable highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule with highest weight
\Lambda
. If the Dirac cohomology
\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}({\mathcal{H}})
contains a nonzero
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-supermodule with even infinitesimal character
\chi_{\lambda}^{\bar{0}}
for some
\lambda\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}
, then

\Lambda-\rho_{\bar{1}}=w(\lambda+\rho_{\bar{0}})-\rho_{\bar{0}}

for some
w\in W
.

Proof.

By Proposition
71
, the supermodule
\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}({\mathcal{H}})
contains a
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-constituent of highest weight
\Lambda-\rho_{\bar{1}}
.
If
\lambda
is the highest weight of another
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-constituent, then by Vogan’s theorem (Theorem
52
) we have

\chi^{\bar{0}}_{\Lambda-\rho_{\bar{1}}}(\zeta(z))=\chi^{\bar{0}}_{\lambda}(\zeta(z)),\qquad z\in\mathfrak{Z}({\mathfrak{g}}).

If
z\in\mathfrak{Z}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}})
, then
\zeta(z)=z
, and therefore the above equality holds for all
z\in\mathfrak{Z}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}})
.
In particular,

\chi^{\bar{0}}_{\Lambda-\rho_{\bar{1}}}=\chi^{\bar{0}}_{\lambda},

which is the case if and only if there exists
w\in W
such that (
cf.
Section
3.3
)

\Lambda-\rho_{\bar{1}}=w\cdot\lambda=w(\lambda+\rho_{\bar{0}})-\rho_{\bar{0}}.\qed

Theorem 76
.

The Dirac cohomology of a non-trivial unitarizable simple
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule
{\mathcal{H}}
with highest weight
\Lambda
is

\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}({\mathcal{H}})\cong L_{0}(\Lambda-\rho_{\bar{1}}).

Proof.

We decompose
\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}({\mathcal{H}})
into its
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-constituents by Proposition
70
. Note that it decomposes discretely since
{\mathcal{H}}
is simple and by Remark
46
. Then the module
L_{0}(\Lambda-\rho_{\bar{1}})
is a simple constituent by Proposition
71
, and unitarizable. Moreover, it is a relative holomorphic
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-supermodule
by Harish-Chandra’s condition:

(\Lambda-\rho_{\bar{1}}+\rho_{\bar{0}},\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{m})=\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{m}+m-1-n<0

as
n\geq m
and
\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{m}\leq 0
,
i.e.
,
\Lambda-\rho_{\bar{1}}\in{\mathscr{D}^{\text{hol}}}
, where
{\mathscr{D}^{\text{hol}}}
denotes the set of all Harish-Chandra parameters of relative holomorphic discrete series
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-modules described in Section
3.5.1
. In particular, as
\Delta_{\bar{0}}^{+}
is fixed,
\Lambda-\rho_{\bar{1}}
is the unique highest weight in its
W
-linkage class of a unitarizable highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-supermodule
29
.

Any simple
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-constituent
L_{0}(\mu)
is a highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-supermodule by Proposition
70
, and the highest weight
\mu
is of the form

w\cdot(\Lambda-\rho_{\bar{1}})=\mu,

by Lemma
75
. Thus, by uniqueness,
w
must be the identity, and
\Lambda-\rho_{\bar{1}}=\mu
. In addition, the multiplicity is one by Proposition
71
. This concludes the proof.
∎

Remark 77
.

The proof of the theorem applies verbatim to any
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}
–semisimple simple highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule
M
whose highest weight
\Lambda
is the highest weight of a unitarizable highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}
–module. In addition, the preceding reasoning extends, without essential modification, to the case where
\Lambda
is regular and dominant integral.

Corollary 78
.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}
be a unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule. Then any
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-constituent in
{\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
belongs to the relative holomorphic discrete series.

Proof.

Any
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-constituent in
{\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
has highest weight

\mu=\Lambda-\rho_{\bar{1}}-\sum_{i}n_{i}\alpha_{i}-\sum_{j}m_{j}\beta_{j},\qquad n_{i},m_{j}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+},\quad\alpha_{i}\in\Delta_{\bar{0}}^{+},\ \beta_{j}\in\Delta_{\bar{1}}^{+}.

A direct calculation yields for all
i,j
:

(\alpha_{i},\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{m})\geq 0,\qquad(\beta_{j},\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{m})\geq 0

which implies
(\mu+\rho_{\bar{0}},\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{m})<0
since
(\Lambda-\rho_{\bar{1}}+\rho_{\bar{0}},\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{m})<0
.
∎

The
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-supermodule generated by
1\in M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
is entirely concentrated in even parity due to the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-grading of
M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
. This leads to the following corollary.

Corollary 79
.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}
be a non-trivial unitarizable simple
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule. Then

\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}({\mathcal{H}})\cong\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}^{+}({\mathcal{H}}).

Furthermore, unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules are uniquely determined by their Dirac cohomology.

Theorem 80
.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{H}}_{2}
be two unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules. Then
{\mathcal{H}}_{1}\cong{\mathcal{H}}_{2}
as
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules if and only if
\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}({\mathcal{H}}_{1})\cong\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}({\mathcal{H}}_{2})
as
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-supermodules.

Proof.

Unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules are completely reducible, and the Dirac cohomology is additive. It therefore suffices to consider unitarizable highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules, say
{\mathcal{H}}_{1}=L(\Lambda_{1})
and
{\mathcal{H}}_{2}=L(\Lambda_{2})
for some
\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2}\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}
.

Then, by Theorem
76
, the associated Dirac cohomology is
\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}({\mathcal{H}}_{1})=L_{0}(\Lambda_{1}-\rho_{\bar{1}})
and
\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}({\mathcal{H}}_{2})=L_{0}(\Lambda_{2}-\rho_{\bar{1}})
. The statement now follows, since two highest weight supermodules with respect to
\Delta_{\bar{0}}^{+}
are isomorphic if and only if they have the same highest weight.
∎

Finally, we apply the preceding results to compute the Dirac cohomology of highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules
M
whose highest weight
\Lambda
is the highest weight of a unitarizable highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-module. For an explicit discussion of these supermodules, we refer to
[
39
]
.

Fix such a supermodule
M
. Then it has a composition series (Proposition
20
):

\{0\}=M_{0}\subset M_{1}\subset\dotsc\subset M_{n}=M

such that each
M_{i+1}/M_{i}
is a simple highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule, say
L(\Lambda_{i})
. In particular, using
[
22
, Theorem 2.5, Corollary 2.7]
, any
\Lambda_{i}
is of the form
\Lambda_{i}=\Lambda-\gamma_{i}
, where
\gamma_{i}\in\Gamma
is a sum of pairwise distinct odd positive roots.

Proposition 81
.

The Dirac cohomology of
M
is given by

\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}(M)=\bigoplus_{i}\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}(L(\Lambda_{i}))\cong\bigoplus_{i}L_{0}(\Lambda_{i}-\rho_{\bar{1}}).

Proof.

Recall that we work in the category of Harish-Chandra (super)modules. In particular, it is well-known that for
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-modules
L_{0}(\Lambda)
and
L_{0}(\Lambda^{\prime})
with
\Lambda,\Lambda^{\prime}\in{\mathscr{D}^{\text{hol}}}
, one has

(5.5)

\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(L_{0}(\Lambda),L_{0}(\Lambda^{\prime}))=0

in the category of Harish-Chandra modules.

We first consider the case
k=2
. The short exact sequence

0\to M_{1}=L(\Lambda_{0})\to M_{2}\to L(\Lambda_{1})\to 0

induces, by Lemma
53
, Corollary
79
, and Theorem
76
, an exact sequence

0\to L_{0}(\Lambda_{0}-\rho_{\bar{1}})\to\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}^{+}(M_{2})\to L_{0}(\Lambda_{1}-\rho_{\bar{1}})\to 0,

where parity is suppressed. By Corollary
78
, both
\Lambda_{0}-\rho_{\bar{1}}
and
\Lambda_{1}-\rho_{\bar{1}}
lie in
{\mathscr{D}^{\text{hol}}}
. Hence
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}\bigl(L_{0}(\Lambda_{1}-\rho_{\bar{1}}),L_{0}(\Lambda_{0}-\rho_{\bar{1}})\bigr)=0
and the sequence splits, so that

\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}^{+}(M_{2})\cong\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}(L(\Lambda_{0}))\oplus\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}(L(\Lambda_{1})).

Moreover, the six-term exact sequence of Lemma
53
gives

\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}^{-}(M_{2})=0,

since
\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}^{-}(L(\Lambda_{0}))=\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}^{-}(L(\Lambda_{1}))=0
. Thus

\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}(M_{2})\cong\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}(L(\Lambda_{0}))\oplus\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}(L(\Lambda_{1})).

The general case follows by induction, using the same argument and the facts that
\Lambda_{i}-\rho_{\bar{1}}\in{\mathscr{D}^{\text{hol}}}
for all
i
, and (
5.5
).
∎

6.
Complementary perspectives

In this section, we consider several complementary viewpoints on Dirac cohomology.
First, we provide a new characterization of unitarity via the Dirac inequality, which in turn yields a novel classification the full set of unitarizable simple
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules.
Next, we introduce an analogue of Kostant’s cohomology and show that, as
{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}
-modules, it agrees with Dirac cohomology.
We then define the Dirac index and establish that it equals the Euler characteristic of Dirac cohomology.
Finally, by means of Kostant’s cohomology and the Dirac index, we obtain
{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}
-character formulas.

6.1.
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-Decomposition and unitarity

For highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules, an important problem is the decomposition under
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
. This passes from the super setting to the reductive Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
, where the representation theory is classical. In general, if
M
is a highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule of highest weight
\Lambda
, then
M
admits a
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-filtration whose composition factors are simple highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-supermodules of the form
L_{0}(\Lambda-\gamma)
, where
\gamma
is a sum of distinct positive odd roots. In what follows, the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-grading of the corresponding modules is left implicit, and we simply refer to them as
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-modules.

For unitarizable supermodules, this picture becomes more rigid. By Proposition
9
, such a module is completely reducible over
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
. At the same time, the Dirac inequality is strict on every
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-constituent of
M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
. In particular,

(6.1)

(\Lambda-\gamma+2\rho,\Lambda-\gamma)>(\Lambda+2\rho,\Lambda)>0\qquad\forall\,\gamma\neq 0

for every highest weight
\Lambda-\gamma
occurring in the
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-decomposition. This suggests that the strict Dirac inequality should detect unitarity.

We now show that this is indeed the case. In Section
3.4
, we proved that every simple highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule
M
carries a unique non-degenerate contravariant Hermitian form, induced from the Shapovalov form on the corresponding Verma supermodule. Thus
M
is unitarizable if and only if this form is positive definite. The main point is that this positivity is equivalent to the strict Dirac inequality induced from every
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-composition factor of
M
different from
L_{0}(\Lambda)
, together with the unitarizability of
L_{0}(\Lambda)
as a
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-module.

In what follows,
M
denotes a simple highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule with highest weight
\Lambda
, where
\Lambda
is the highest weight of a unitarizable highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-module.

We transfer the question of positive definiteness of the Shapovalov form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{M}
on
M
to that of the form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}
on
\langle M\otimes 1\rangle_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}
, where
\langle M\otimes 1\rangle_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}
denotes the
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-submodule of
M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
generated by
M\otimes 1
under the diagonal action induced by (
4.6
). On this module, positive definiteness is equivalent to strictness of the Dirac inequality.

Lemma 82
.

A simple highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule
M
is unitarizable if and only if
\langle M\otimes 1\rangle_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}
is a unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-module with respect to the restriction of
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}
.

Proof.

Assume that
M
is unitarizable. Then
M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
is a unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}}
-module. Hence, under the diagonal action, it is also a unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-module. Therefore its
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-submodule
\langle M\otimes 1\rangle_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}
is unitarizable.

Conversely, assume that
\langle M\otimes 1\rangle_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}
is unitarizable with respect to the restriction of
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}
. It suffices to show that the Shapovalov form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{M}
on
M
is positive definite. For any
0\neq v\in M
one has
v\otimes 1\in\langle M\otimes 1\rangle_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}
. Then

0<\langle v\otimes 1,v\otimes 1\rangle_{M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}=\langle v,v\rangle_{M}\langle 1,1\rangle_{M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}=\langle v,v\rangle_{M}.

Thus
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{M}
is positive definite. Hence
M
is unitarizable.
∎

Let
\Gamma
be the set of all sums of distinct positive odd roots. By
[
28
, Theorem 10.4.5]
, there exists a subset
S(\Lambda)\subseteq\Gamma
such that, regarded as a
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-module,
M
admits a filtration whose simple highest weight subquotients have highest weights
\Lambda-\gamma
with
\gamma\in S(\Lambda)
and they appear with multiplicity
m(\gamma)
in this filtration. This yields the following decomposition.

Lemma 83
.

Let
M
be a simple highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule of highest weight
\Lambda
. Assume that
\Lambda
is the highest weight of a unitarizable highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-module.

a)

There exists a subset

such that

where

denotes the multiplicity of the constituent

.

b)

L_{0}(\Lambda-\rho_{\bar{1}})
appears in
\langle M\otimes 1\rangle_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}
as a
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-constituent with multiplicity one.

c)

\langle M\otimes 1\rangle_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}
is a unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-module.

d)

Considered as a
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-submodule in

,
\operatorname{D}^{2}
acts on each
L_{0}(\Lambda-\rho_{\bar{1}}-\gamma)
as the scalar multiple of
-(\Lambda+2\rho,\Lambda)+(\Lambda-\gamma+2\rho,\Lambda-\gamma)
.

Proof.

By
[
28
, Theorem 10.4.5]
, there exists a subset
S(\Lambda)\subseteq\Gamma
and a
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-filtration

0=M_{0}\subset M_{1}\subset\cdots\subset M_{r}=M

such that each quotient
M_{i+1}/M_{i}
is isomorphic to
L_{0}(\Lambda-\gamma)
for some
\gamma\in S(\Lambda)
. Set
N_{i}\coloneqq\langle M_{i}\otimes 1\rangle_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}
. Then
(N_{i})
is a
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-filtration of
\langle M\otimes 1\rangle_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}
, and each quotient

is generated by the image of

, each such highest weight module is simple, and

Remark 84
.

For
\langle M\otimes 1\rangle_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}\subset M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
, one has

\ker\operatorname{D}^{2}\big|_{\langle M\otimes 1\rangle_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}}=L_{0}(\Lambda-\rho_{\bar{1}}),\qquad\operatorname{im}\operatorname{D}^{2}\big|_{\langle M\otimes 1\rangle_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}}=\bigoplus_{\gamma\in S(\Lambda)\setminus\{0\}}L_{0}(\Lambda-\rho_{\bar{1}}-\gamma).

By Lemma
82
, it suffices to show that
\langle M\otimes 1\rangle_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}
is a unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-module with respect to the restriction of
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}
. By Lemma
83
, one has

(6.2)

\langle M\otimes 1\rangle_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}=\bigoplus_{\gamma\in S(\Lambda)}m(\gamma)L_{0}(\Lambda-\rho_{\bar{1}}-\gamma).

Equipped with the direct sum of the positive-definite Hermitian forms
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{L_{0}(\nu)}
on its simple summands, this becomes a unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-module. On each simple summand
L_{0}(\nu)
, the restriction of
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}
is a nonzero
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-invariant Hermitian form. Hence

(6.3)

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}\big|_{L_{0}(\nu)}=c_{\nu}\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{L_{0}(\nu)}

for some
c_{\nu}\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\{0\}
. It remains to show that
c_{\nu}>0
for every
\nu=\Lambda-\rho_{\bar{1}}-\gamma
with
\gamma\in S(\Lambda)
. Since
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}\big|_{L_{0}(\nu)}
is therefore either positive definite or negative definite, it suffices to evaluate it on a highest weight vector
v_{\nu}
of
L_{0}(\nu)
. If
\langle v_{\nu},v_{\nu}\rangle_{M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}>0
for all
\nu
, then
c_{\nu}>0
for all
\nu
, and the claim follows. For these highest weight vectors, we need the following lemma.

Lemma 85
.

Let
v_{\mu}\otimes 1
be a highest weight vector in the
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-decomposition of
\langle M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})\rangle_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}
in Lemma
83
. Then

((\mu+2\rho,\mu)-(\Lambda+2\rho,\Lambda))\langle v_{\mu}\otimes 1,v_{\mu}\otimes 1\rangle_{M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}=4\sum_{k=1}^{mn}\langle\partial_{k}v_{\mu}\otimes 1,\partial_{k}v_{\mu}\otimes 1\rangle_{M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}.

Proof.

By Lemma
83
,
D^{2}
acts on each
v_{\mu}\otimes 1
as the scalar multiple of
-(\Lambda+2\rho,\Lambda)+(\mu+2\rho,\mu)
. Consequently,

\begin{aligned} ((\mu+2\rho,\mu)-(\Lambda+2\rho,\Lambda))\langle v_{\mu}\otimes 1,v_{\mu}\otimes 1\rangle_{M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}&=\langle\operatorname{D}^{2}(v_{\mu}\otimes 1),v_{\mu}\otimes 1\rangle_{M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}\\
&=-4\sum_{k=1}^{mn}\langle x_{k}\partial_{k}v_{\mu}\otimes 1,v_{\mu}\otimes 1\rangle_{M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}\\
&=4\sum_{k=1}^{mn}\langle\partial_{k}v_{\mu}\otimes 1,\partial_{k}v_{\mu}\otimes 1\rangle_{M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}\\
&=4\sum_{k=1}^{mn}\langle\partial_{k}(v_{\mu}\otimes 1),\partial_{k}(v_{\mu}\otimes 1)\rangle_{M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}\end{aligned},

where we use in the second equality the explicit form of
\operatorname{D}^{2}
given in Remark
39
,
and

\langle 2\sum_{k,l}[\partial_{k},\partial_{l}]v\otimes x_{k}x_{l},v\otimes 1\rangle_{M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}=2\sum_{k,l=1}^{mn}\langle[\partial_{k},\partial_{l}]v,v\rangle_{M}\langle x_{k}x_{l},1\rangle_{M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}=0,

since
\langle x_{k}x_{l},1\rangle_{M{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}=\langle x_{l},\partial_{k}1\rangle_{M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}=0
for all
1\leq k,l\leq mn
. The third equality uses the contravariance of
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}
and the fact that
\omega(x_{k})=-\partial_{k}
for all
1\leq k\leq mn
. The last equality uses

\partial_{k}(v_{\Lambda-\gamma}\otimes 1)=\partial_{k}v_{\Lambda-\gamma}\otimes 1\in\langle M\otimes 1\rangle_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}.\qed

Theorem 86
.

Let
M
be a highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule with highest weight
\Lambda
.
Then
M
is unitarizable if and only if the following two conditions are satisfied:

a)

\Lambda
is the highest weight of a unitarizable highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-module.

b)

If
L_{0}(\mu)
is a simple
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-composition factor in a
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-filtration of
M
with highest weight
\mu
, then

\displaystyle(\mu+2\rho,\mu)>(\Lambda+2\rho,\Lambda).

Proof.

If
M
is unitarizable, then a) and b) follow directly from Proposition
9
and the Dirac inequality in Proposition
74
.

Conversely, following the idea of (
6.3
), we show that
c_{\nu}>0
for every
\nu=\Lambda-\rho_{\bar{1}}-\gamma
with
\gamma\in S(\Lambda)
by the strict Dirac inequality and an induction argument.

We first endow
\langle M\otimes 1\rangle_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}
with a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-grading compatible with the action of the
\partial_{k}
on the highest weight vectors
v_{\Lambda-\gamma}\otimes 1
for
\gamma\in S(\Lambda)
. By definition, each
\gamma\in\Gamma
is a sum of distinct positive odd roots. By our choice of positive system, the number of summands is uniquely determined by
\gamma
. We call it the
length
of
\gamma
and denote it by
\operatorname{len}(\gamma)
. This yields a well-defined grading of the
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}
-constituents
L_{0}(\mu)
by
\operatorname{len}(\gamma)
. Explicitly,

\langle M\otimes 1\rangle_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}=\bigoplus_{k}\langle M\otimes 1\rangle_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}[k],\qquad\langle M\otimes 1\rangle_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}[k]\coloneqq\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in S(\Lambda)\\
\operatorname{len}(\gamma)=k\end{subarray}}m(\gamma)L_{0}(\Lambda-\rho_{\bar{1}}-\gamma).

By construction,
\langle M\otimes 1\rangle_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}[0]=L_{0}(\Lambda-\rho_{\bar{1}})
, and the restriction of the Shapovalov form to this summand is positive definite.

We next show that this grading is compatible with the action of the operators
\partial_{k}
on the vectors
v_{\Lambda-\gamma}\otimes 1
. Since
\partial_{k}(v_{\Lambda-\gamma}\otimes 1)\in\langle M\otimes 1\rangle_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}},

and
\langle M\otimes 1\rangle_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}
is the direct sum of the modules
L_{0}(\Lambda-\rho_{\bar{1}}-\gamma^{\prime})
, every nonzero vector in
\langle M\otimes 1\rangle_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}
must have a weight occurring in one of these summands. Hence such a vector is zero as soon as its weight does not occur in any of them. Recall that every weight of a
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-constituent
L_{0}(\nu)
is of the form

(6.4)

\nu-\sum_{j}s_{j}\beta_{j},\qquad s_{j}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+},\ \beta_{j}\in\Delta_{\bar{0}}^{+}.

Let
v_{\Lambda-\gamma}\otimes 1
be a highest weight vector in the constituent indexed by
\gamma=\gamma_{1}+\cdots+\gamma_{l}
, where the
\gamma_{i}
are distinct positive odd roots. We fix such an arbitrary decomposition of
\gamma
; the same argument applies verbatim to any other one. Then
v_{\Lambda-\gamma}\otimes 1\in\langle M\otimes 1\rangle_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}[l]
. Let
\partial_{k}
be an odd weight vector of positive odd root
\alpha
. We consider
\partial_{k}(v_{\Lambda-\gamma}\otimes 1)
.

The idea is to compare
\alpha_{k}
with each summand
\gamma_{i}
of
\gamma
. Note that
\partial_{k}(v_{\Lambda-\gamma}\otimes 1)
is either trivial or non-trivial and has weight of the form

\Lambda-\rho_{\bar{1}}-\gamma+\alpha_{k}.
Write

A\coloneqq\{\epsilon_{i}-\delta_{j}:1\leq i\leq p,\ 1\leq j\leq n\},\qquad B\coloneqq\{-\epsilon_{i}+\delta_{j}:p+1\leq i\leq m,\ 1\leq j\leq n\}.

For
\alpha\in A
and
\beta\in B
, neither
\alpha-\beta
nor
\beta-\alpha
is a sum of roots. If
\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}\in A
or
\beta,\beta^{\prime}\in B
, then
\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}
and
\beta-\beta^{\prime}
are either
0
, not roots, or even roots of either sign. Hence, for each
i
,

\alpha_{k}-\gamma_{i}=0,\qquad\alpha_{k}-\gamma_{i}\notin\Delta,\qquad\text{or}\qquad\alpha_{k}-\gamma_{i}\in\Delta_{\bar{0}}^{\pm}.

If
\alpha_{k}-\gamma_{i}=0
or
\alpha_{k}-\gamma_{i}\in-\Delta_{\bar{0}}^{+}
, then the resulting weight can be written as
\Lambda-\rho_{\bar{1}}-(\gamma-\alpha_{k})
in the first case and as
\Lambda-\rho_{\bar{1}}-(\gamma-\gamma_{i})-(\gamma_{i}-\alpha_{k})
in the second. Moreover,
\operatorname{len}(\gamma-\alpha_{k})=l-1
and
\operatorname{len}(\gamma-\gamma_{i})=l-1
.
Consequently, if
\partial_{k}(v_{\Lambda-\gamma}\otimes 1)
is non-trivial its weight can occur only in a constituent
L_{0}(\Lambda-\rho_{\bar{1}}-\gamma^{\prime})
with
\operatorname{len}(\gamma^{\prime})\leq l-1
. Indeed, its weight then belongs to the constituent

L_{0}\bigl(\Lambda-\rho_{\bar{1}}-(\gamma-\gamma_{i})\bigr)
or
L_{0}(\Lambda-\rho_{\bar{1}}-(\gamma-\alpha_{k}))
,
which lie in layer
l-1
. If no such
i
exists, then this weight occurs in no even constituent, so the vector is zero. Therefore

\partial_{k}(v_{\Lambda-\gamma}\otimes 1)\in\bigoplus_{r\leq l-1}\langle M\otimes 1\rangle_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}[r].

We are now ready to complete the proof. By assumption, the restriction of the Shapovalov form to
L_{0}(\Lambda-\rho_{\bar{1}})
is positive definite.
On each
\langle M\otimes 1\rangle_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}[l]
, we define the restriction of the Shapovalov form to be
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\langle M\otimes 1\rangle_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}[l]}
. Note that
\langle M\otimes 1\rangle_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}[0]\cong L_{0}(\Lambda-\rho_{\bar{1}})
. It suffices to prove that
\langle v,v\rangle_{M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}>0
for any
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-highest weight module
v
appearing in layer
l
, that is,
v=v_{\Lambda-\gamma}\otimes 1
for some
\gamma\in S(\Lambda)
with
\operatorname{len}(\gamma)=l
. We argue by induction on
l
.

The case
l=0
holds by assumption.
Suppose that the restrictions of the Shapovalov form to
\langle M\otimes 1\rangle_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}[0],\langle M\otimes 1\rangle_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}[1],\ldots,\langle M\otimes 1\rangle_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}[l]
are positive definite.
Let
v\in\langle M\otimes 1\rangle_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}[l+1]\setminus\{0\}
be a highest weight vector of a
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-constituent in layer
l+1
. Assume
v
is of the form
v_{\Lambda-\gamma}\otimes 1
. For every
k=1,\ldots,mn
we have
\partial_{k}v=0
or
\partial_{k}v\in\langle M\otimes 1\rangle_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}[l]
, hence

\langle\partial_{k}v,\partial_{k}v\rangle_{\langle M\otimes 1\rangle_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}[l]}\geq 0,

with equality if and only if
\partial_{k}v=0
. At least one
\partial_{k}v
must be nonzero; otherwise
\mathfrak{n}^{+}_{\bar{1}}v=0
and
v
is
{\mathfrak{b}}
-primitive which contradicts the simplicity of
M
. Consequently, using Lemma
85
, one has

\bigl((\mu+2\rho,\mu)-(\Lambda+2\rho,\Lambda)\bigr)\langle v_{\Lambda-\gamma}\otimes 1,v_{\Lambda-\gamma}\otimes 1\rangle_{\langle M\otimes 1\rangle_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}[l+1]}=4\sum_{k=1}^{mn}\langle\partial_{k}v_{\Lambda-\gamma}\otimes 1,\partial_{k}v_{\Lambda-\gamma}\otimes 1\rangle_{\langle M\otimes 1\rangle_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}[l]}>0.

Since the Dirac inequalities hold strictly, the coefficient on the left is positive, and therefore

\langle v_{\Lambda-\gamma}\otimes 1,v_{\Lambda-\gamma}\otimes 1\rangle_{\langle M\otimes 1\rangle_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}[l+1]}>0.

This completes the induction and the proof.
∎

6.2.
Relation to Kostant’s cohomology

We define an analog of Kostant’s cohomology similarly to
[
8
]
, which captures the
{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}
-module structure of the Dirac cohomology. When we consider a unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule as a
{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}
-module, we neglect parity. We adapt the notation of Section
4.1
.

6.2.1.
Construction of
\mathrm{H}^{\ast}({\mathfrak{g}}_{+1},M)

The Lie superalgebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
of type
A
admits a
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-compatible
{\mathbb{Z}}
-grading. We consider
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}
as a
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-module under the supercommutator. Then
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}
decomposes into a direct sum of two simple
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-modules, namely
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}={\mathfrak{g}}_{-1}\oplus{\mathfrak{g}}_{+1}
, where concretely

(6.5)

{\mathfrak{g}}_{+1}\coloneqq{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}\oplus{\mathfrak{p}}_{2},\qquad{\mathfrak{g}}_{-1}\coloneqq{\mathfrak{q}}_{1}\oplus{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}.

Both are, in particular, abelian Lie subsuperalgebras of
{\mathfrak{g}}
,
i.e.
,
[{\mathfrak{g}}_{\pm 1},{\mathfrak{g}}_{\pm 1}]=0
, and the associated
{\mathbb{Z}}
-grading of
{\mathfrak{g}}
, given by

(6.6)

{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{g}}_{-1}\oplus{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}\oplus{\mathfrak{g}}_{+1},

is then compatible with the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-grading on
{\mathfrak{g}}
.

Fix the complex vector spaces
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\pm 1}
and consider the
{\mathbb{Z}}
-gradings
S({\mathfrak{g}}_{\pm 1})=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}S^{n}({\mathfrak{g}}_{\pm 1})
and
\bigwedge({\mathfrak{g}}_{\pm 1})=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigwedge^{n}({\mathfrak{g}}_{\pm 1})
. Note that
I_{\wedge,S}\cap T^{1}({\mathfrak{g}}_{\pm 1})=\{0\}
, so that we can identify
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\pm 1}
with
\bigwedge^{1}({\mathfrak{g}}_{\pm 1})
and
S^{1}({\mathfrak{g}}_{\pm 1})
.

We identify the dual space
{\mathfrak{g}}_{+1}^{\ast}
with
{\mathfrak{g}}_{-1}
using the bilinear form
2B(\cdot,\cdot)
. This identification is
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-invariant, since
B(\cdot,\cdot)
is invariant. Additionally, we equate
S({\mathfrak{g}}_{+1}^{\ast})
with
S({\mathfrak{g}}_{-1})
, the polynomial algebra in the variables
x_{1},\dotsc,x_{pn},\partial_{pn+1},\dotsc,\partial_{mn}
. The universal enveloping algebra
{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}}_{+1})
can be identified with
\bigwedge({\mathfrak{g}}_{+1})
.

To define the cohomology under consideration, we use the standard free resolution of

\bigwedge({\mathfrak{g}}_{+1})
–modules. Recall that

(6.7)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}^{\mathfrak{p}_{1}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{pn}\partial_{k}\otimes x_{k},

\displaystyle\qquad\delta^{\mathfrak{p}_{1}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{pn}x_{k}\otimes\partial_{k},

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}^{\mathfrak{q}_{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=pn+1}^{mn}\partial_{k}\otimes x_{k},

\displaystyle\qquad\delta^{\mathfrak{q}_{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=pn+1}^{mn}x_{k}\otimes\partial_{k}.

Set
\delta\coloneqq-\mathrm{d}^{\mathfrak{q}_{2}}+\delta^{\mathfrak{p}_{1}},

and consider the complex

(6.8)

\dotsb\xlongrightarrow{\delta}S^{i}({\mathfrak{g}}_{+1})\otimes\bigwedge({\mathfrak{g}}_{+1})\xlongrightarrow{\delta}S^{i-1}({\mathfrak{g}}_{+1})\otimes\bigwedge({\mathfrak{g}}_{+1})\xlongrightarrow{\delta}\dotsb\xlongrightarrow{\delta}{\mathbb{C}}\otimes\bigwedge({\mathfrak{g}}_{+1})\xlongrightarrow{\delta}0.

Here, we use that the symmetric algebra
S({\mathfrak{g}}_{+1})
is the polynomial algebra generated by

\partial_{1},\ldots,\partial_{pn},x_{pn+1},\ldots,x_{mn},

so that
\delta
indeed lowers the polynomial degree, and by Lemma
67
, we have
\delta^{2}=0
. Thus
\delta
defines a differential. Moreover, it is
{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}
-invariant by Lemma
40
.

Fix a
({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}})
-supermodule
M
, and apply the contravariant functor
\operatorname{Hom}_{\bigwedge({\mathfrak{g}}_{+1})}(-,M)

to the above resolution. Then we arrive at the following complex of vector spaces

(6.9)

\dotsc\xlongleftarrow{\mathrm{d}}\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathbb{C}}}(S^{i+1}({\mathfrak{g}}_{+1}),M)\xlongleftarrow{\mathrm{d}}\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathbb{C}}}(S^{i}({\mathfrak{g}}_{+1}),M)\xlongleftarrow{\mathrm{d}}\dotsc\xlongleftarrow{\mathrm{d}}\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathbb{C}}}(S^{0}({\mathfrak{g}}_{+1}),M)\leftarrow 0,

where
\mathrm{d}
is the pullback operator of
\delta
, explicitly given by
\mathrm{d}\coloneqq{\mathrm{d}^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}}-{\delta^{{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}}}
if we identify
\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathbb{C}}}(S^{i}({\mathfrak{g}}_{+1}),M)
with
M\otimes S^{i}({\mathfrak{g}}_{-1})
for any
i
.

M
and
S^{i}({\mathfrak{g}}_{+1})
are
{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}
-modules, where the action on
S^{i}({\mathfrak{g}}_{+1})
is induced by the adjoint action of
{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}
on
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}
. This identifies the spaces

(6.10)

\mathrm{C}^{i}(M)\coloneqq\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathbb{C}}}(S^{i}({\mathfrak{g}}_{+1}),M)\cong M\otimes S^{i}({\mathfrak{g}}_{-1})

as
{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}
-modules. The
{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}
-invariance of
\mathrm{d}
(
cf.
Lemma
40
) makes the complex
\mathrm{C}\coloneqq(\mathrm{C}^{i}(M),\mathrm{d})
into a
{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}
-module complex. The cohomology groups of the complex
\mathrm{C}
will be denoted by
\mathrm{H}^{i}({\mathfrak{g}}_{+1},M)
. These are naturally
{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}
-modules.

6.2.2.
Relation to Dirac cohomology

For a unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule
{\mathcal{H}}
, we regard
\mathrm{d}
and
\delta
as operators on
{\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
, so that
\operatorname{D}=2(\mathrm{d}-\delta)
. The Dirac cohomology of
{\mathcal{H}}
is

\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}({\mathcal{H}})=\operatorname{ker}\operatorname{D},

whereas the analogue of Kostant cohomology is
\operatorname{ker}\mathrm{d}/\operatorname{im}\mathrm{d}
. We compare these two spaces. We begin by recording the basic properties of
\mathrm{d}
and
\delta
.

Lemma 87
.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}
be a unitarizable simple
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule. Then the following assertions hold with respect to
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}
:

a)

\mathrm{d}
and
\delta
are skew-adjoint to each other.

b)

\operatorname{im}\mathrm{d}
is orthogonal to
\operatorname{ker}\delta
and
\operatorname{im}\delta
, while
\operatorname{im}\delta
is orthogonal to
\ker\mathrm{d}
.

Proof.

a) The operators are defined as
\mathrm{d}={\mathrm{d}^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}}-{\delta^{{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}}}
and
\delta=-{\mathrm{d}^{{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}}}+{\delta^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}}
. By Lemma
67
,
{\mathrm{d}^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}}
is skew-adjoint to
{\delta^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}}
and
{\mathrm{d}^{{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}}}
is skew-adjoint to
{\delta^{{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}}}
. This proves a).

b) This proof is analogous to the proofs of parts c) and d) in Lemma

and will be omitted.
∎

Lemma 88
.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}
be a unitarizable simple
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule. Then the following assertions hold with respect to
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})}
:

a)

{\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})=\operatorname{ker}\operatorname{D}\oplus\operatorname{im}\mathrm{d}\oplus\operatorname{im}\delta
.

b)

\ker\mathrm{d}=\operatorname{ker}\operatorname{D}\oplus\operatorname{im}\mathrm{d}
.

Proof.

a) By Lemma
64
and Corollary
59
, we have
{\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})=\operatorname{ker}\operatorname{D}^{2}\oplus\operatorname{im}\operatorname{D}^{2}=\operatorname{ker}\operatorname{D}\oplus\operatorname{im}\operatorname{D}^{2}
. Moreover,
\operatorname{im}\mathrm{d}
and
\operatorname{im}\delta
are orthogonal to each other by Lemma
87
,
\mathrm{d}^{2}=0
, and

. We conclude:

{\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})=\operatorname{ker}\operatorname{D}\oplus\operatorname{im}\operatorname{D}^{2}\subset\operatorname{ker}\operatorname{D}\oplus\operatorname{im}\operatorname{D}\subset\operatorname{ker}\operatorname{D}\oplus\operatorname{im}\mathrm{d}\oplus\operatorname{im}\delta,

and consequently
{\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})=\operatorname{ker}\operatorname{D}\oplus\operatorname{im}\mathrm{d}\oplus\operatorname{im}\delta
.

b) The assertion follows from a) and Lemma
87
once we show
\operatorname{ker}\operatorname{D}=\ker\mathrm{d}\cap\operatorname{ker}\delta
. However, this assertion is clear since
\operatorname{D}=2(\mathrm{d}-\delta)
and
\operatorname{im}\mathrm{d}
and
\operatorname{im}\delta
are orthogonal.
∎

Now Lemma
88
and Proposition
47
allow us to compare the
{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}
-module structures of
\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}({\mathcal{H}})
and
\mathrm{H}^{\ast}({\mathfrak{g}}_{+1},{\mathcal{H}})
. For this, we need the following elementary observation. It follows from the fact that the

{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}
-equivariant nondegenerate pairing between the underlying spaces, and hence between the corresponding symmetric algebras.

Lemma 89
.

The following assertions hold:

a)

One has

{\mathbb{C}}[x_{1},\dotsc,x_{mn}]\cong{\mathbb{C}}[x_{1},\dotsc,x_{pn}]\otimes{\mathbb{C}}[x_{pn+1},\dotsc,x_{mn}]

as

on
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}

The

Theorem 90
.

For any unitarizable simple
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule
{\mathcal{H}}

{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}

\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}({\mathcal{H}})\cong\mathrm{H}^{\ast}({\mathfrak{g}}_{+1},{\mathcal{H}})\otimes{\mathbb{C}}_{-\rho_{\bar{1}}}.

Proof.

First, by Proposition

and Lemma

\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}({\mathcal{H}})=\operatorname{ker}\operatorname{D}\cong\ker\mathrm{d}/\operatorname{im}\mathrm{d}

6.3.
Dirac index

In this section, we introduce the Dirac index. For this, we call a
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule
M

\operatorname{D}^{2}
-semisimple
if
\operatorname{D}^{2}
acts semisimply on
M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
. To every such supermodule we associate a virtual character, called the
Dirac index
. This class includes, in particular, simple and unitarizable supermodules. This index coincides with the Euler characteristic of Dirac cohomology and satisfies several desirable properties. Moreover, for unitarizable simple
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules, the Dirac index agrees with the Dirac cohomology.

For any
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule
M
, the Dirac operator
\operatorname{D}
acts on
M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})_{\bar{0}}
and
M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})_{\bar{1}}
, interchanging these subspaces. The
Dirac index
of
M
is defined as the virtual
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-supermodule

(6.11)

\operatorname{I}(M)\coloneqq M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})_{\bar{0}}-M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})_{\bar{1}}.

This is an element of the Grothendieck group of
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-supermodules. In contrast, the operator
\operatorname{D}:M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})_{\bar{0},\bar{1}}\to M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})_{\bar{1},\bar{0}}
gives rise to a decomposition of the Dirac cohomology
\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}(M)
into even and odd parts:

\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}(M)=\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}^{+}(M)\oplus\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}^{-}(M),

whose
Euler characteristic
is given by the virtual
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-supermodule

\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}^{+}(M)-\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}^{-}(M).

The virtual
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-supermodules
\operatorname{I}(M)
and the Euler characteristic are identical provided that
M
is
\operatorname{D}^{2}
-semisimple.

Proposition 91
.

Let
M
be a
\operatorname{D}^{2}
-semisimple
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule. Then the Dirac index
\operatorname{I}(M)
is equal to the Euler characteristic of the Dirac cohomology
\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}(M)
,
i.e.
,

\operatorname{I}(M)=\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}^{+}(M)-\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}^{-}(M).

Proof.

We decompose
M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
into a direct sum of eigenspaces of
\operatorname{D}^{2}
:

M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})=\big(M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})\big)(0)\oplus\bigoplus_{c\neq 0}\big(M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})\big)(c).

Since
\operatorname{D}^{2}
is even, this decomposition is compatible with the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-grading:

\big(M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})\big)(c)=\big(M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})_{\bar{0}}\big)(c)\oplus\big(M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})_{\bar{1}}\big)(c).

Moreover,
\operatorname{D}
commutes with
\operatorname{D}^{2}
, so it preserves each eigenspace
\big(M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})\big)(c)
. However,
\operatorname{D}
switches parity, inducing isomorphisms

\operatorname{D}(c):\big(M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})_{\bar{0},\bar{1}}\big)(c)\to\big(M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})_{\bar{1},\bar{0}}\big)(c),

with inverses given by
\frac{1}{c}\operatorname{D}(c)
for
c\neq 0
. Consequently, the contributions from nonzero eigenspaces cancel in the index, and we have

M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})_{\bar{0}}-M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})_{\bar{1}}=\big(M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})_{\bar{0}}\big)(0)-\big(M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})_{\bar{1}}\big)(0).

The Dirac operator
\operatorname{D}
restricts to a differential on
\operatorname{ker}(\operatorname{D}^{2})
, and its cohomology is precisely the Dirac cohomology. The result then follows from the Euler–Poincaré principle.
∎

As a direct consequence, for a unitarizable simple
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule, the Dirac index coincides with its Dirac cohomology (see Corollary
79
).

Corollary 92
.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}
be a unitarizable simple
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule. Then as
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-supermodules

\operatorname{I}({\mathcal{H}})\cong\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}({\mathcal{H}}).

Furthermore, the Dirac index commutes with tensoring by finite-dimensional
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules. This compatibility can be used to study the Dirac cohomology of unitarizable supermodules via translation functors.

Lemma 93
.

Let
M
be a
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule, and let
F
be a finite-dimensional
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule. Then there is a canonical isomorphism of
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-supermodules

\operatorname{I}(M\otimes F)\cong\operatorname{I}(M)\otimes F.

Proof.

We compute:

\operatorname{I}(M\otimes F)=M\otimes F\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})_{\bar{0}}-M\otimes F\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})_{\bar{1}},

while

\operatorname{I}(M)\otimes F=\big(M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})_{\bar{0}}-M\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})_{\bar{1}}\big)\otimes F.

Since
F
is finite-dimensional, the tensor product distributes over the direct sum and difference, yielding a canonical isomorphism between the two expressions.
∎

6.4.
Formal characters

We now derive two expressions for the formal character of a unitarizable simple
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule, one in terms of Kostant cohomology and one in terms of the Dirac index.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}
be a unitarizable simple
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule of highest weight
\Lambda
. Since
{\mathcal{H}}
is a highest weight Harish-Chandra supermodule, all
{\mathfrak{h}}
-weight spaces
{\mathcal{H}}^{\mu}
are finite-dimensional. Its formal character is therefore defined by

(6.12)

\operatorname{ch}({\mathcal{H}})\coloneqq\sum_{\mu\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}}\dim{\mathcal{H}}^{\mu}\,e^{\mu},

where
e^{\mu}
denotes the formal exponential of
\mu
. On the other hand,
{\mathcal{H}}
decomposes into
{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}
-types as

(6.13)

{\mathcal{H}}=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in P^{++}_{{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}}}m(\lambda)F^{\lambda},

where
F^{\lambda}
is the simple finite-dimensional
{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}
-module of highest weight
\lambda
and
m(\lambda)<\infty
. Here,
P^{++}_{{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}}
denotes the set of dominant integral weights in
{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}
with respect to
\Delta_{c}^{+}
. Since
{\mathfrak{h}}\subseteq{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}
, each
F^{\lambda}
has a well-defined
{\mathfrak{h}}
-character
\operatorname{ch}_{{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}}(F^{\lambda})
. The
{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}
-type decomposition
therefore yields the expression

(6.14)

\operatorname{ch}_{{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}}({\mathcal{H}})\coloneqq\sum_{\lambda\in P^{++}_{{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}}}m(\lambda)\,\operatorname{ch}(F^{\lambda}),

which, upon restriction to
{\mathfrak{h}}\subset{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}
, is precisely the formal character
\operatorname{ch}({\mathcal{H}})
. Hence the formal
{\mathfrak{h}}
-character of
{\mathcal{H}}
is determined by its
{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}
-type decomposition, and we use the notation
\operatorname{ch}_{{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}}(\cdot)
to emphasize the
{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}
-type decomposition.

6.4.1.
Formal characters and Kostant’s cohomology

We study the relation of
\operatorname{ch}_{{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}}({\mathcal{H}})
and
\mathrm{H}^{\ast}({\mathfrak{g}}_{+1},{\mathcal{H}})
partially using ideas and constructions presented in
[
8
]
.

Fix a unitarizable simple
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule
{\mathcal{H}}
. In Section
6.2
, we introduced the cohomology groups
\mathrm{H}^{k}({\mathfrak{g}}_{+1},{\mathcal{H}})
as the cohomology groups of the
{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}
-complex
\mathrm{C}\coloneqq(\mathrm{C}^{k}({\mathcal{H}}),\mathrm{d})
with
\mathrm{C}^{k}({\mathcal{H}})\coloneqq\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathbb{C}}}(S^{k}({\mathfrak{g}}_{+1}),{\mathcal{H}})\cong{\mathcal{H}}\otimes S^{k}({\mathfrak{g}}_{-1})
. Recall that we treat here
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\pm 1}
as ordinary complex vector spaces. Moreover, let
\mathrm{H}^{k}({\mathfrak{g}}_{+1},{\mathcal{H}})^{\lambda}
be the weight
\lambda
-subspace of
\mathrm{H}^{k}({\mathfrak{g}}_{+1},{\mathcal{H}})
for some weight
\lambda\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}
. As
{\mathcal{H}}
is a Harish-Chandra supermodule (Proposition
18
), we have for fixed weight
\lambda
that
\dim(\mathrm{H}^{k}({\mathfrak{g}}_{+1},{\mathcal{H}})^{\lambda})\neq 0
only for finitely many
k
. The Euler–Poincaré principle then implies that

(6.15)

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\dim(\mathrm{H}^{k}({\mathfrak{g}}_{+1},{\mathcal{H}})^{\lambda})=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\dim(\mathrm{C}^{k}({\mathcal{H}})^{\lambda}),

and considering their formal characters gives

(6.16)

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\operatorname{ch}\left(\mathrm{H}^{k}({\mathfrak{g}}_{+1},{\mathcal{H}})\right)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\operatorname{ch}(\mathrm{C}^{k}({\mathcal{H}}))=\operatorname{ch}({\mathcal{H}})\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\operatorname{ch}(S^{k}({\mathfrak{g}}_{+1}^{\ast})).

Next, as
{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}
-modules, we have
S^{k}({\mathfrak{g}}_{-1})\cong S^{k}({\mathfrak{q}}_{1}\oplus{\mathfrak{p}}_{2})=S^{k}({\mathfrak{n}}_{\bar{1}}^{-})
for any
k
(
cf.
Lemma
89
), and it is well-known that

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\operatorname{ch}(S^{k}({\mathfrak{n}}_{\bar{1}}^{-}))=(\prod_{\gamma\in\Delta_{\bar{1}}^{+}}(1+e^{-\gamma}))^{-1}.

We conclude

(6.17)

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\operatorname{ch}(\mathrm{C}^{k}({\mathcal{H}}))=\frac{1}{D_{1}}\operatorname{ch}({\mathcal{H}}),\qquad D_{1}=\prod_{\gamma\in\Delta_{\bar{1}}^{+}}(1+e^{-\gamma}),

and the formal character of
{\mathcal{H}}
is given by

(6.18)

\operatorname{ch}({\mathcal{H}})=D_{1}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\operatorname{ch}(\mathrm{H}^{k}({\mathfrak{g}}_{+1},{\mathcal{H}})).

We now pass to the
{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}
-type decomposition. Let
[\mathrm{H}^{k}({\mathfrak{g}}_{+1},{\mathcal{H}}):F^{\mu}]
denote the multiplicity of the simple (unitarizable highest weight)
{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}
-module
F^{\mu}
with highest weight
\mu
in
\mathrm{H}^{k}({\mathfrak{g}}_{+1},{\mathcal{H}})
. As
{\mathfrak{k}}
is compact,
\mathrm{H}^{k}({\mathfrak{g}}_{+1},{\mathcal{H}})
is completely reducible as a
{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}
-module, and we can express
\operatorname{ch}_{{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}}(\mathrm{H}^{k}({\mathfrak{g}}_{+1},{\mathcal{H}}))
in terms of the multiplicities:

(6.19)

\operatorname{ch}_{{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}}(\mathrm{H}^{k}({\mathfrak{g}}_{+1},{\mathcal{H}}))=\sum_{\mu\in P^{++}_{{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}}}[\mathrm{H}^{k}({\mathfrak{g}}_{+1},{\mathcal{H}}):F^{\mu}]\operatorname{ch}_{{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}}(F^{\mu}).

Altogether, we have proven the following theorem.

Theorem 94
.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}
be a unitarizable simple
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule. The formal character of
{\mathcal{H}}
is

\operatorname{ch}_{{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}}({\mathcal{H}})=D_{1}\sum_{\mu\in P^{++}_{{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}[\mathrm{H}^{k}({\mathfrak{g}}_{+1},{\mathcal{H}}):F^{\mu}]\operatorname{ch}_{{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}}\bigl(F^{\mu}\bigr).

6.4.2.
Formal characters and Dirac index

We have shown that, for a
\operatorname{D}^{2}
-semisimple supermodule
M
, the Dirac index agrees with Dirac cohomology. We now derive a
{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}
-character formula for
\operatorname{D}^{2}
-semisimple Harish-Chandra supermodules. In what follows, such a supermodule is denoted by
{\mathcal{H}}
. By definition
{\mathcal{H}}
admits a formal
{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}
-character, and we obtain a formula
\operatorname{ch}_{{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}}({\mathcal{H}})
from
\operatorname{I}({\mathcal{H}})
.

Recall from Section
6.3
the definition:

(6.20)

\operatorname{I}({\mathcal{H}})={\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})_{\bar{0}}-{\mathcal{H}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})_{\bar{1}}=\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}^{+}({\mathcal{H}})-\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}^{-}({\mathcal{H}}),

which is the Euler characteristic of
\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}({\mathcal{H}})
by Proposition
91
. In terms of characters, this reads

(6.21)

\operatorname{ch}_{{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}}({\mathcal{H}})\bigl(\operatorname{ch}_{{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}}(M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})_{\bar{0}})-\operatorname{ch}_{{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}}(M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})_{\bar{1}})\bigr)=\operatorname{ch}_{{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}}(\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}^{+}({\mathcal{H}}))-\operatorname{ch}_{{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}}(\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}^{-}({\mathcal{H}})).

The arguments below follow the methods and ideas of
[
17
]
, adapted to the present setting.

We consider the finite-dimensional vector space
{\mathfrak{n}}_{\bar{1}}^{-}
, with basis
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{mn}\}
. We are interested in the free resolution of free
\bigwedge({\mathfrak{n}}_{\bar{1}}^{-})
-modules:

(6.22)

\ldots\xrightarrow{\delta}S^{i}({\mathfrak{n}}_{\bar{1}}^{-})\otimes\bigwedge({\mathfrak{n}}_{\bar{1}}^{-})\xrightarrow{\delta}S^{i-1}({\mathfrak{n}}_{\bar{1}}^{-})\otimes\bigwedge({\mathfrak{n}}_{\bar{1}}^{-})\xrightarrow{\delta}\ldots\xrightarrow{\delta}\mathbb{C}\otimes\bigwedge({\mathfrak{n}}_{\bar{1}}^{-})\xrightarrow{\delta}0,

where the boundary operator is

(6.23)

\delta\coloneqq{\mathrm{d}^{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}}+{\mathrm{d}^{{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}}}=\sum_{i=1}^{mn}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\otimes x_{i}:S({\mathfrak{n}}_{\bar{1}}^{-})\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\bigwedge({\mathfrak{n}}_{\bar{1}}^{-})\longrightarrow S({\mathfrak{n}}_{\bar{1}}^{-})\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\bigwedge({\mathfrak{n}}_{\bar{1}}^{-}).

The boundary operator
\delta
is invariant under the action of
{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}
,
i.e.
,
[{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}},\delta]=0
, as shown in Lemma
40
. Additionally, the proof of the following lemma follows
mutatis mutandis
from
[
20
, Proposition 3.3.5]
.

Lemma 95
.

The following assertion holds:

\operatorname{ker}\delta=\operatorname{im}\delta\oplus{\mathbb{C}}(1\otimes 1).

In particular, the cohomology is generated by
{\mathbb{C}}(1\otimes 1)
.

Lemma 96
.

Let
{\mathbb{C}}_{-\rho_{\bar{1}}}
be the one-dimensional
{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}
-module with weight
-\rho_{\bar{1}}
. Then

\operatorname{ch}_{{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}}(\bigwedge\nolimits{\mathfrak{n}}_{\bar{1}}^{-})\bigl(\operatorname{ch}_{{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}}(M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})_{\bar{0}})-\operatorname{ch}_{{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}}(M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})_{\bar{1}})\bigr)=\operatorname{ch}_{{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}}({\mathbb{C}}_{-\rho_{\bar{1}}}).

Proof.

By Proposition
47
, we have the following isomorphism of
{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}
-modules:

\bigwedge\nolimits{\mathfrak{n}}_{\bar{1}}^{-}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})\cong\bigwedge\nolimits{\mathfrak{n}}_{\bar{1}}^{-}\otimes S({\mathfrak{n}}_{\bar{1}}^{-})\otimes{\mathbb{C}}_{-\rho_{\bar{1}}}.

In particular,
\bigwedge\nolimits{\mathfrak{n}}_{\bar{1}}^{-}\otimes S({\mathfrak{n}}_{\bar{1}}^{-})
is the complex introduced above with
{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}
-invariant boundary operator
\delta
.
By the Euler–Poincaré principle,

\operatorname{ch}_{{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}}(\bigwedge\nolimits{\mathfrak{n}}^{-}_{\bar{1}})\bigl(\operatorname{ch}_{{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}}(M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})_{\bar{0}})-\operatorname{ch}_{{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}}(M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})_{\bar{1}})\bigr)

is the Euler characteristic of this complex. However, by Lemma
95
, the cohomology is generated by
1\otimes 1
. The claim therefore follows from Proposition
47
, since
1\otimes 1
is
{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}
-invariant.
∎

Theorem 97
.

Let
F^{\nu}
denote a simple
{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}
-module of highest weight
\nu\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}
. Define
N(\mu)\coloneqq\bigwedge\nolimits{\mathfrak{n}}^{-}_{\bar{1}}\otimes F^{\mu}
, and assume
\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}^{+}({\mathcal{H}})=\sum_{\mu}F^{\mu}
and
\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}^{-}({\mathcal{H}})=\sum_{\nu}F^{\nu}
. Then

\operatorname{ch}_{{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}}({\mathcal{H}})=\sum_{\mu}\operatorname{ch}_{{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}}(N(\mu+\rho_{\bar{1}}))-\sum_{\nu}\operatorname{ch}_{{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}}(N(\nu+\rho_{\bar{1}})).

Proof.

Combining Lemma
96
, the definition of
N(\mu)
and the definition of the Dirac index, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{ch}_{{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}}({\mathcal{H}})

\displaystyle\bigl(\operatorname{ch}_{{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}}(M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})_{\bar{0}})-\operatorname{ch}_{{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}}(M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})_{\bar{1}})\bigr)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{ch}_{{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}}(\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}^{+}({\mathcal{H}}))-\operatorname{ch}_{{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}}(\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{{D}}}^{-}({\mathcal{H}}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mu}\operatorname{ch}_{{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}}(F^{\mu})-\sum_{\nu}\operatorname{ch}_{{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}}(F^{\nu})

\displaystyle=\bigl(\sum_{\mu}\operatorname{ch}_{{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}}(N(\mu+\rho_{\bar{1}}))-\sum_{\nu}\operatorname{ch}_{{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}}(N(\nu+\rho_{\bar{1}}))\bigr)\bigl(\operatorname{ch}_{{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}}(M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})_{\bar{0}})-\operatorname{ch}_{{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}}(M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})_{\bar{1}})\bigr),

which can be rewritten as

\bigl(\operatorname{ch}_{{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}}({\mathcal{H}})-\sum_{\mu}\operatorname{ch}_{{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}}(N(\mu+\rho_{\bar{1}}))+\sum_{\nu}\operatorname{ch}_{{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}}(N(\nu+\rho_{\bar{1}}))\bigr)\bigl(\operatorname{ch}_{{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}}(M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})_{\bar{0}})-\operatorname{ch}_{{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}}(M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})_{\bar{1}})\bigr)=0.

We claim that the first factor must be trivial. Assume that it is non-trivial,
i.e.
,

\operatorname{ch}_{{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}}(V)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}n_{i}\operatorname{ch}_{{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}}(F^{\mu_{i}})\neq 0,\qquad V\coloneqq{\mathcal{H}}-\sum_{\mu}N(\mu+\rho_{\bar{1}})+\sum_{\nu}N(\nu+\rho_{\bar{1}}).

Assume that
V
contains a
{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}
-type
F^{\xi}
. Then,
\xi=\Lambda-\sum_{j}\beta_{j}-\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}
for positive non-compact roots
\beta_{j}
and positive odd roots
\alpha_{i}
. This follows by Theorem
25
and
[
21
, Proposition 3.6]
. The
\beta_{j}
are not present in
N(\Lambda)
by construction.

We consider the Weyl vector
\rho_{nc}
associated to the set of non-compact positive roots (
cf.
Section
2.2.1
). Recall that the non-compact positive roots are
\epsilon_{k}-\epsilon_{l}
for
1\leq k\leq p
and
p+1\leq l\leq m
, while the odd positive roots are
\{\epsilon_{k}-\delta_{r},-\epsilon_{l}+\delta_{s}:1\leq r,s\leq n,\ 1\leq k\leq p,\ p+1\leq l\leq m\}
. Then, a direct calculation yields

(\beta_{j},\rho_{nc})>0,\qquad(\alpha_{i},\rho_{nc})>0,\qquad\forall i,j.

We conclude
(\xi,\rho_{nc})\leq(\Lambda,\rho_{nc})
.

Without loss of generality, we can assume that
n_{1}\neq 0
and
(\mu_{1},\rho_{nc})\geq(\mu_{i},\rho_{nc})
for all
i
.
Since
F^{\mu_{1}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
contains
F^{\mu_{1}}\otimes 1\cong F^{\mu_{1}-\rho_{\bar{1}}}
with multiplicity one, the character of
F^{\mu_{1}-\rho_{\bar{1}}}
appears in
\operatorname{ch}_{{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}}(F^{\mu_{1}})(\operatorname{ch}_{{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}}(M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})_{\bar{0}})-\operatorname{ch}_{{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}}(M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})_{\bar{1}}))
with coefficient one.

By assumption, the contribution of
\operatorname{ch}_{{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}}(F^{\mu_{1}})(\operatorname{ch}_{{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}}(M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})_{\bar{0}})-\operatorname{ch}_{{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}}(M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})_{\bar{1}}))
must be canceled by the remaining terms in
\operatorname{ch}_{{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}}({\mathfrak{n}}_{\bar{1}}^{-})(\operatorname{ch}_{{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}}(M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})_{\bar{0}})-\operatorname{ch}_{{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}}(M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})_{\bar{1}}))
. Hence
F^{\mu_{1}+\rho_{\bar{1}}}
must occur in
\operatorname{ch}_{{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}}(F^{\mu_{i}})(\operatorname{ch}_{{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}}(M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})_{\bar{0}})-\operatorname{ch}_{{\mathfrak{k}}^{{\mathbb{C}}}}(M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})_{\bar{1}}))
for some
i>1
, equivalently, in
F^{\mu_{i}}\otimes M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
for some
i>1
.

The weights of
M({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}})
are of the form
-\rho_{\bar{1}}-\sum_{j}\beta_{j}-\sum_{k}\alpha_{k}
, where
\beta_{j}
are distinct non-compact positive roots and
\alpha_{k}
are distinct positive odd roots. We conclude that

\mu_{1}=\mu_{i}-\sum_{j}\beta_{j}-\sum_{k}\alpha_{k}.

This leads to a contradiction, as it would imply
(\mu_{1},\rho_{nc})<(\mu_{i},\rho_{nc})
since
(\beta_{j},\rho_{nc})>0
and
(\alpha_{i},\rho_{nc})>0
for all
i,j
. This finishes the proof.
∎

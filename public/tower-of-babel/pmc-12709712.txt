MRI quantitative imaging biomarkers in differentiating brain parenchymal tuberculoma and lung cancer brain metastases
Authors: Anlong Wang, Xiaowei Qiu, Quelu Chen, Yuxuan Qiu, Yijing Wang, Yanan Hu, Jing Wang, Ming Zhan, Hanlin Zhu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundBrain parenchymal tuberculoma (BT) and brain metastases (BM) originating from lung cancer often exhibit overlapping clinical and imaging features, making accurate differentiation challenging. Current diagnostic approaches remain suboptimal.PurposeThis study aimed to develop a radiomics-based MRI model to differentiate BT from BM and enhance model interpretability using Shapley Additive Explanations (SHAP) analysis.Material and methodsThis retrospective study involved 175 patients (97 with BT and 78 with BM) treated at Hangzhou Red Cross Hospital from January 2018 to March 2024, encompassing 1014 lesions (581 in BT and 433 in BM). Patients were randomly divided into training (n = 122) and test sets (n = 53) in a 7:3 ratio. MRI images were segmented and preprocessed for radiomics feature extraction. Feature selection was performed using recursive feature elimination. Logistic regression models were developed based on features from contrast-enhanced T1-weighted imaging (T1WI+C) and fluid-attenuated inversion recovery (FLAIR) sequences. Three predictive models were assessed using the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC): the optimal radiomics model, a combined clinical-radiological model, and an integrated radiomics and multi-clinical model (RMCM).ResultsFollowing feature selection, the FLAIR and T1WI+C models retained six and four essential radiomic features, respectively. At the lesion level, the T1WI+C model achieved superior performance (AUC: 0.932 in the training set; 0.933 in the test set) compared to the FLAIR model (AUC: 0.824 and 0.869, respectively). At the patient level, the RMCM, incorporating eight clinical–radiological features such as CEA, age, and peritumoral edema, showed superior diagnostic performance (AUC: 0.986 in training; 0.958 in testing). SHAP analysis highlighted the radiomics score as the key contributor to its diagnostic value.ConclusionsA radiomics model based on T1WI+C MRI sequences effectively distinguishes BT from BM. Incorporating clinical and radiological features into the RMCM further improves diagnostic accuracy, offering a robust, interpretable tool to aid clinical decision-making.Graphical abstractSupplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s40001-025-03476-5.Keywords: Brain parenchymal tuberculoma, Brain metastases, Radiomics, Magnetic resonance imaging, Explainable artificial intelligence

Introduction

Central nervous system (CNS) tuberculosis results from hematogenous dissemination of Mycobacterium tuberculosis, primarily originating from pulmonary infection. The bacilli may seed the brain parenchyma, meninges, and adjacent structures. When a lesion ruptures and releases bacilli into the subarachnoid space or ventricles, it leads to tuberculous meningitis. In contrast, progressive enlargement of a lesion without rupture forms a brain parenchymal tuberculoma (BT) [1]. According to the World Health Organization’s Global Tuberculosis Report 2024 [2], an estimated 10.8 million new tuberculosis cases occurred worldwide in 2023. Among these, approximately 1% develop CNS involvement, with BT accounting for 10–30% of CNS tuberculosis cases. CNS tuberculosis represents the most severe form of the disease, with over 50% of patients experiencing death or long-term neurological sequelae [3].

In parallel, lung cancer remains one of the most prevalent and lethal malignancies worldwide. Cancer Statistics 2025 predicts lung cancer will have the second-highest incidence and the highest mortality among all cancers in the United States [4]. In China, data from over 700 cancer registries analyzed in China National Cancer Centre, 2022 identified lung cancer as the leading cause of cancer-related morbidity and mortality [5]. Brain metastases (BM) from lung cancer occur more frequently than those from melanoma, breast, renal, or colorectal cancer [6]. It is estimated that 20–65% of patients with lung cancer will develop BM during the course of their illness, making lung cancer the most common primary origin of BM [7–9].

Currently, the diagnosis of BT and BM relies heavily on conventional neuroimaging, with contrast-enhanced MRI being the preferred modality. Previous studies distinguishing brain parenchymal tuberculoma (BT) from brain metastasis (BM) primarily relied on conventional imaging features (such as ring enhancement, peritumoral edema, etc.), but these characteristics are highly overlapping between the two conditions, especially in cases with solitary or small lesions, which can lead to misdiagnosis. Although radiomics studies have been conducted for the classification or genetic profiling of BM, no studies have systematically constructed and validated a radiomics model based on multi-sequence MRI for differentiating BT and BM, and there is a lack of an interpretable combined model integrating both clinical and imaging features. Furthermore, misdiagnosis is common in general hospitals lacking specialized tuberculosis units, where clinicians may have limited experience with CNS tuberculosis and insufficient familiarity with its MRI characteristics. As a result, BT is frequently misdiagnosed as BM. There is a pressing need for novel, rapid, and accurate diagnostic strategies capable of distinguishing BT from BM, particularly in diverse clinical settings and patient populations. Radiomics-based approaches may offer an effective solution by extracting quantitative imaging biomarkers that go beyond visual assessment, providing clinicians with enhanced diagnostic tools for more precise and individualized decision-making.

Recent studies have demonstrated that radiomics, through refined quantitative analysis, can uncover deep, high-dimensional features embedded within medical imaging data. This approach has shown promising potential in the diagnosis and differential diagnosis of both infectious and neoplastic diseases [10, 11], and has been applied to predict gene mutations and the risk of treatment resistance in BM [12, 13]. Furthermore, the emergence of Shapley Additive Explanations (SHAP), a game theory-based interpretability framework, has led to significant advances in explainable machine learning [14]. This has provided a viable solution to the “black-box” nature of radiomics models, thereby laying the groundwork for more personalized diagnostic strategies [15].

In response to the current diagnostic challenges, this study aims to develop an MRI-based radiomics model for brain tuberculosis and brain metastases, characterized by “first discovery, no anti-tuberculosis/anti-tumor treatment, and a maximum axial diameter < 2 cm, either single or multiple lesions.” The SHAP method is utilized for decision visualization, quantifying key imaging features to enhance clinical acceptability. A dual-dimensional framework is constructed: the lesion-level treats each enhanced lesion as an independent sample for training and testing, with only target lesions that meet the size criteria retained in multi-lesion patients; the patient-level, using the individual patient as the unit, combines the radiomics scores of target lesions with clinical-imaging variables to establish an RMCM model for clinical decision support. This approach balances both imaging-assisted diagnosis and personalized treatment planning.

Patients and methods

This study was approved by the Medical Ethics Review Committee of Hangzhou Red Cross Hospital (Approval No. [2024] Research Ethics No. 118-001), and patient informed consent was waived. Furthermore, it adhered to the ethical standards set forth by the World Medical Association’s Declaration of Helsinki.

Study population

A total of 97 patients diagnosed with BT at Hangzhou Red Cross Hospital between January 2018 and March 2024 were retrospectively enrolled as the study group. There were 48 males and 49 females, aged 4–81 years (mean age: 45.8 ± 20.9 years). Among them, 88 patients (90.72%) had coexisting pulmonary tuberculosis, 18 (18.56%) had skeletal or joint tuberculosis, and 13 (13.40%) had tuberculosis in other sites. Additionally, 48 patients (49.48%) presented with concurrent tuberculous meningitis.

A total of 78 patients with BM secondary to lung cancer, meeting the inclusion criteria and diagnosed during the same period, were included as the control group. This group included 40 males and 38 females, aged 39–87 years (mean age: 62.3 ± 11.3 years). The primary lung cancer types included adenocarcinoma (n = 53, 67.95%), squamous cell carcinoma (n = 13, 16.67%), small cell carcinoma (n = 11, 14.10%), and large cell carcinoma (n = 1, 1.28%). Nineteen patients (23.35%) had concurrent leptomeningeal metastases.

MRI protocol and imaging parameters

Further details are included in the supplemental material.

MRI image segmentation

MRI image segmentation was independently performed by two board-certified radiologists (Reader A and Reader B) with 13 and 6 years of experience in central nervous system imaging, respectively. Both radiologists were blinded to the patients’ clinical information during the segmentation process. Segmentation of regions of interest (ROI) was conducted using ITK-SNAP software (version 4.2.0, http://www.itksnap.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php) according to the following procedure: (1) lesions were manually delineated slice-by-slice on axial T1WI, and a three-dimensional (3D) ROI was subsequently generated. In patients with multiple lesions, each lesion was labeled separately using distinct identifiers. (2) The corresponding contrast-enhanced T1-weighted (T1WI+C) images were rigidly registered to the fluid-attenuated inversion recovery (FLAIR) images, followed by non-rigid (deformable) registration for finer alignment. The ROIs were first outlined on the T1WI+C sequence, then the FLAIR images were rigidly registered to the T1WI+C images, allowing the ROIs to be transferred to the registered FLAIR images. To evaluate intra-rater reproducibility, Reader A randomly chose 100 cases and repeated the ROI segmentation after 4 weeks, calculating the intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) for consistency.

MRI image preprocessing and radiomics feature extraction

MRI image preprocessing and radiomics feature extraction were performed using the Python packages PyRadiomics (Version 3.0.1) and SimpleITK (Version 2.2.0). Further details are included in the supplemental material.

Radiomics feature selection

Patients were randomly divided (random seed = 45) into a training set of 122 cases (709 lesions) and a test set of 53 cases (305 lesions) in a 7:3 ratio. In the training set, the inter-observer reliability of radiomics features was evaluated using the ICC, and features with an ICC value less than 0.75 were excluded. Univariate analysis was performed to retain features with statistically significant differences. To further refine the feature set, Recursive Feature Elimination was applied for feature reduction. Through iterative training and cross-validation, features with minimal contributions to predictive performance were removed. Use Spearman’s correlation coefficient to remove radiomic features with a rs > 0.75 to identify optimal features and reduce multicollinearity.

Clinical and radiological assessment

Radiologists A and B retrospectively reviewed the enhanced MRI images of the patients and jointly assessed classical imaging features, including homogeneous whole-tumor enhancement (HTE), homogeneous circumferential enhancement (HCE), circumferential enhancement with wall nodules (CEWN), circumferential enhancement with central punctate or fine line-like enhancement (CEPE), and peritumoral edema (PTE) [19, 20] (Fig. 1). In case of disagreement, Radiologist C, with 20 years of experience in central nervous system imaging, made the final decision.

Prediction model development and validation

In the training set, logistic regression models based on lesion-level features from T1WI+C and FLAIR sequences were developed to evaluate the diagnostic performance of radiomics models in distinguishing between BT and BM. At the patient level, individual radiomics scores from each lesion were summed and averaged to generate a comprehensive radiomics score that reflects the overall disease characteristics of the patient. Based on the optimal radiomics features, clinical-imaging features, and their combination, separate models were established: the Clinical-Imaging Model, and the Radiomics and Multi-Clinical Model (RMCM). The diagnostic performance of these models for identifying BT was evaluated at the patient level and compared with the T1WI+C and FLAIR models. Finally, the SHAP values were used to visualize and interpret the best-performing model. A detailed flowchart of the study process is shown in Fig. 2.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using R (Version 4.3.3) and Python (Version 3.9.13). The Shapiro–Wilk test was applied to assess the normality of continuous variables. For normally distributed variables, the mean ± standard deviation (SD) was used, and group differences were assessed using the t test. Non-normally distributed data were presented as median and interquartile range (IQR), with differences between groups evaluated using the Mann–Whitney U test. Categorical variables were expressed as proportions (%), and group comparisons were made using the Chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test. The receiver operating characteristic curve (ROC) was used to evaluate the diagnostic performance of the models. The calibration curve was used to quantify the accuracy of the model predictions, and the Brier score was used to measure calibration. A Brier score of less than 0.1 was considered excellent, between 0.1 and 0.2 as good, between 0.2 and 0.3 as fair, and above 0.3 as poor calibration performance. Decision curve analysis was conducted to assess the clinical value and benefit of each model in actual clinical practice. The DeLong test was used to compare the differences in area under the curve (AUC) between the models. A P value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Model diagnostic performance evaluation

At the lesion level, a total of 107 radiomic features were extracted from each ROI on FLAIR and T1WI+C images, with 87 features retained after excluding those with an ICC less than 0.75. After feature selection, the FLAIR and T1WI+C models retained 6 and 4 key radiomic features, respectively (Fig. 3). In the training set, the AUCs were 0.824 (95% confidence interval [CI], 0.791–0.856) for the FLAIR model and 0.932 (95% CI, 0.914–0.951) for the T1WI+C model. In the testing set, the AUCs were 0.869 (95% CI, 0.827–0.910) for the FLAIR model and 0.933 (95% CI, 0.907–0.958) for the T1WI+C model (Table 3). The Delong test revealed statistically significant differences in AUC between the FLAIR and T1WI+C models in both the training and testing sets (P < 0.001) (Fig. 4a, b).

The calibration results showed that the Brier scores for the FLAIR model in the training and testing sets were 0.158 and 0.147, respectively, while the T1WI+C model had Brier scores of 0.100 and 0.107, both lower than those of the FLAIR model (Fig. 4c, d). At the patient level, in the training set, the AUC of the clinical-image and RMCM models was 0.903 (95% CI, 0.853–0.953) and 0.986 (95% CI, 0.970–1.000), respectively, while the AUC of the FLAIR and T1WI+C models was 0.808 (95% CI, 0.725–0.892) and 0.932 (95% CI, 0.884–0.980). In the testing set, the AUC of the four models was 0.905 (95% CI, 0.828–0.982), 0.958 (95% CI, 0.903–1.000), 0.785 (95% CI, 0.669–0.900), and 0.930 (95% CI, 0.865–0.995), respectively. The RMCM model consistently outperformed the other three models in diagnosing TB, with specific details provided in Table 4 and Fig. 5a and b. In terms of calibration in the training set, the Brier scores for the Clinical-Image, RMCM, FLAIR, and T1WI+C models ranged from 0.099 to 0.172, while in the testing set, the range was from 0.074 to 0.195. The RMCM model exhibited the lowest Brier scores (Fig. 5c, d). Clinical decision curve analysis demonstrated that the RMCM model achieved better clinical net benefit than the other three models in both the training and testing sets (Fig. 5e, f).

Discussion

BT and BM from lung cancer are among the most common intracranial space-occupying lesions, with a significant number of affected patients. These two conditions exhibit similar clinical characteristics and radiological features but require distinct treatment approaches. This often leads to diagnostic errors, as clinicians face significant challenges in achieving a rapid and accurate differential diagnosis in the early stages of the disease. Currently, the diagnosis of BT primarily relies on microbiological laboratory pathogen detection, with pathogen culture and nucleic acid amplification techniques being considered the gold standard methods. However, the cultivation of pathogens is time-consuming and does not facilitate early diagnosis. Smear staining has a low positivity rate and is prone to contamination, while Polymerase Chain Reaction is constrained by laboratory conditions. In recent years, with the advancement of next-generation sequencing technology, rapid detection of pathogenic microorganisms in cerebrospinal fluid has become possible. However, its high cost hinders its widespread adoption at grassroots hospitals [21–25]. For the diagnosis of BM, the routine approach involves detecting tumor cells in cerebrospinal fluid or using related laboratory tests to exclude other diagnoses. However, the positivity rate of these methods remains low. Therefore, there is an urgent need for accurate and rapid diagnostic and differential diagnostic methods that can be applied across diverse patient populations and healthcare environments.

Recent studies show that radiomic features can distinguish BM from other brain lesions [26]. Artzi et al. analyzed T1-weighted MRI data from 439 patients and developed machine learning models, finding that a support vector machine classifier was most effective, achieving an AUC of 0.96, with 86.0% sensitivity and 85.0% specificity [27]. Deng et al. created a model to identify histological types of brain metastasis in non-small cell lung cancer patients [28], achieving an AUC of 0.850 in internal validation and 0.800 in external validation. These findings highlight the potential of radiomic features in identifying BM. This study focused on using MRI radiomic features to differentiate BM from BT. The T1WI+C model achieved an AUC of 0.933, outperforming the FLAIR model’s 0.869, consistent with previous findings. At the patient level, combining classic radiological features with clinical variables yielded an AUC of 0.905, surpassing the FLAIR model’s 0.785 but not the T1WI+C model’s 0.930. This indicates the T1WI+C model’s superior ability to enhance diagnostic performance through high-dimensional image feature extraction. Traditional radiological features have limitations, such as difficulty in accurately assessing small central septations, reliance on expert knowledge, and susceptibility to subjective biases, leading to inter-observer variability. This restricts their use by non-specialized physicians. The RMCM fusion model outperforms others in diagnostic performance, calibration, and clinical applicability, highlighting the complementary nature of the T1WI+C model and traditional features. By integrating both, RMCM enhances diagnostic accuracy for intracranial tuberculosis, opening new avenues for clinical multimodal diagnosis.

The SHAP value analysis showed that the radiomics score, based on T1WI+C sequences, was more significant than traditional radiological and clinical features. This score includes first-order texture features like kurtosis and mean absolute deviation, and higher-order features such as sum average and run length nonuniformity. Kurtosis measures the distribution shape of lesion signal intensities, while mean absolute deviation indicates the average deviation of these intensities from the mean. Sum average and run length nonuniformity both assess spatial relationships between image pixels or voxels. Sum average indicates the general trend of signal distribution, linked to texture uniformity, while run length nonuniformity assesses the irregularity of gray-level run lengths. These features, which quantify texture, distribution, and complexity, are vital for detecting subtle image differences and were crucial for early intracranial tuberculosis diagnosis in this study.

In the RMCM model, CEA and age have the second and third highest weights, both negatively impacting the likelihood of BT, meaning lower CEA and younger age increase BT chances. Among five classic radiological features, PTE also negatively contributes, suggesting less edema in brain tuberculosis lesions compared to metastasis lesions. While brain tuberculosis can increase vascular permeability, it results in less perilesional edema than BM, where tumor cells disrupt the blood–brain barrier, causing fluid leakage and edema [29]. HCE is a key imaging feature of BT, occurring more frequently than in metastasis. BT lesions often have central caseous necrosis with peripheral inflammatory cells and granulation tissue, leading to ring enhancement on T1WI+C images. BT also typically shows uneven ring enhancement and a higher rate of nodule detection on the inner wall; therefore, CEWN was found to have a negative contribution in differentiating BT from metastases. BT 17.53% combined with CEPE indicators, as BT lesions typically show granulation tissue and densely packed glial cells in the core, leading to spotty or septal-like heterogeneous enhancement on T1WI+C images [30]. HTE had the least weight and an uncertain contribution, suggesting significant overlap in enhancement patterns between early BM (lesions > 2 cm) and BT lesions, making it difficult to differentiate between them without further refinement.

The specific novelty of this study is as follows: (1) the study developed and validated a lesion-level radiomics model based on the T1WI+C sequence, which significantly outperforms the traditional FLAIR sequence. (2) Additionally, a patient-level RMCM model was constructed by integrating radiomics scores with eight clinical-imaging features, achieving an AUC of 0.958, demonstrating excellent generalizability. (3) SHAP analysis was introduced to enhance the model’s interpretability, clearly identifying that the radiomics score is the most critical indicator for differentiating BT from BM. This provides clinicians with an objective, quantifiable, and interpretable auxiliary diagnostic tool. This work contributes to addressing the methodological gap in the precise differentiation between infectious and neoplastic intracranial space-occupying lesions.

Limitations

This study’s limitations include its single-center retrospective design, which may lead to selection and statistical biases, affecting result generalizability. Some BM cases lacked pathological confirmation, relying only on clinical findings. Furthermore, the mechanisms behind varying ring enhancement in BT are unclear. Future research could incorporate pathological or molecular markers, such as immune microenvironment and genetic mutations, to clarify the biological importance of key imaging features, enhancing the integration of imaging, pathology, and clinical data.

Conclusion

The MRI-based radiomics model demonstrated excellent performance in differentiating BT from BM. The T1WI+C model exhibited significantly better diagnostic performance at the lesion level compared to the FLAIR model. Furthermore, the RMCM model, which integrates clinical features, further enhanced diagnostic accuracy. The radiomics feature score demonstrated the greatest significance within the model, offering clinicians an objective and precise supplementary diagnostic tool.

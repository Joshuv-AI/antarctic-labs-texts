Burden and trends of NASH-related liver cancer associated with high fasting plasma glucose in China and globally from 1990 to 2021: a population-based analysis of the global burden of disease study
Authors: Youjian Yao, Caifa Ji, ChenChen Zhou, Mei Gui
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesTo analyse the trends in the burden of non-alcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH)-related liver cancer associated with high fasting plasma glucose (HFPG) globally and in China from 1990 to 2021, and to project the burden over the next 15 years.DesignPopulation-based secondary analysis of data from the Global Burden of Disease (GBD) 2021 study.SettingGlobal and Chinese populations included in the GBD 2021 database.ParticipantsData were derived from individuals aged 20 years and older included in the GBD 2021 study, covering both sexes across all age groups.Primary and secondary outcome measuresThe primary outcomes were mortality and disability-adjusted life years (DALYs) of NASH-related liver cancer associated with HFPG. Secondary outcomes included years lived with disability and years of life lost. Trends were described using the annual percentage change and average annual percentage change. Age-standardised mortality rates and DALY rates were projected to 2036.ResultsIn 2021, the burden of NASH-related liver cancer associated with HFPG was higher in men than in women in both the global and Chinese populations and was predominantly concentrated among older age groups. From 1990 to 2021, the burden substantially increased globally and in China, showing a significant upward trend. Projections indicate that the global burden will continue to rise among men but decline among women, whereas both sexes in China are expected to experience a decline in burden over the next 15 years.ConclusionsFrom 1990 to 2021, the burden of NASH-related liver cancer associated with HFPG increased substantially in both global and Chinese populations. High-risk groups, including men, older individuals, those with elevated fasting plasma glucose and patients with NASH, should be closely monitored. Targeted screening and preventive strategies for these populations may help manage the disease burden. Future studies are needed to determine whether effective blood glucose control can reduce the risk of NASH-related liver cancer.

Introduction

Liver cancer is one of the most common cancers worldwide, ranking sixth in incidence among all cancers and third in mortality. In 2022, there were 865 269 new cases of liver cancer globally, and 757 948 patients with liver cancer died from the disease.1 In 2022, the age-standardised numbers of new liver cancer cases and deaths in China were 367 700 and 316 500, respectively.2

Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) is the most common chronic liver disease worldwide and a major cause of liver cancer and cirrhosis.35 NAFLD can progress to non-alcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH), which may lead to liver fibrosis and liver cancer.69 With the rising prevalence of metabolic disorders such as obesity and diabetes, the incidence of NASH-related liver cancer is increasing globally.1012

High fasting plasma glucose (HFPG) is a metabolic disorder characterised by elevated blood glucose levels beyond the normal range in a fasting state. HFPG induces metabolic disturbances, which are key factors in the development of NAFLD and NASH.13 14 Research has shown that HFPG is positively correlated with the incidence of liver cancer. A 12-year prospective cohort study confirmed this relationship.15 Additionally, metabolic disturbances have been found to be closely associated with a significant increase in liver cancer incidence among men, further emphasising the critical role of HFPG in the progression of liver cancer.16 However, there is limited research on the burden of NASH-related liver cancer associated with HFPG. According to a literature review, there are only two studies on the disease burden of liver cancer associated with HFPG. One study focuses on the disease burden of NASH-related liver cancer associated with HFPG, while the other investigates the disease burden of liver cancer associated with HFPG. However, no research has yet explored the comparison of the disease burden of NASH-related liver cancer associated with HFPG between China and the global context.17 18 As the prevalence of glucose metabolism disorders continues to rise, further epidemiological research is crucial for quantifying the impact of HFPG on NASH-related liver cancer and assessing its burden on public health.19 20

Although NASH-related liver cancer is closely associated with high blood glucose levels, a detailed description of the burden of NASH-related liver cancer associated with HFPG has not been provided for either the global context or China. Furthermore, the burden associated with HFPG may vary by region and age. To understand the status of NASH-related liver cancer burden due to HFPG globally and in China, this study analyses trends in deaths, disability-adjusted life years (DALYs), years lived with disability (YLD) and years of life lost (YLL) associated with HFPG-induced NASH-related liver cancer from 1990 to 2021 across different genders and age groups, based on the Global Burden of Disease Study 2021 (GBD 2021) database. Additionally, a Bayesian age-period-cohort model will be used to predict the burden of NASH-related liver cancer associated with HFPG in the next 15 years, providing evidence to identify high-risk populations and inform targeted prevention strategies.

Methods

Data sources

This study is based on the GBD 2021 database, which evaluates health risks associated with 371 diseases, injuries and 88 risk factors. GBD 2021 provides comprehensive data on disease burden, including mortality, morbidity and risk factors, for different regions and populations.21 This study systematically analyses the burden of NASH-related liver cancer associated with HFPG from 1990 to 2021, both globally and within China. Due to the limitations of the GBD 2021 database, which only includes data for populations aged 25 years and older, the analysis is restricted to individuals within this age group. The population is further stratified into 15 age intervals to assess the disease burden at different stages of adulthood, thereby providing a detailed understanding of the impact of HFPG-related NASH liver cancer across the lifespan. In the GBD framework, HFPG is considered a metabolic risk factor rather than a disease entity. It encompasses both individuals with diabetes mellitus and those with elevated fasting plasma glucose who do not meet the diagnostic threshold for diabetes. This definition enables a broader and more precise quantification of the population-level risk associated with HFPG. This study used publicly available, de-identified data from the GBD 2021 study. All data were obtained from the publicly accessible Global Health Data Exchange (GHDx) Results Tool (https://vizhub.healthdata.org/gbd-results/).22 In this study, ‘liver cancer’ refers specifically to primary liver cancer, defined as hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) according to the GBD 2021 classification. Other primary liver malignancies such as cholangiocarcinoma, as well as secondary (metastatic) liver cancers, were not included.

Disease burden metrics

In this study, the burden of NASH-related liver cancer associated with HFPG from 1990 to 2021 is assessed using key disease burden metrics, including mortality rates, DALYs, YLD and YLL due to premature mortality. Mortality rates quantify the annual number of deaths directly associated with NASH-related liver cancer resulting from HFPG. DALYs, a composite measure, account for both premature mortality (YLL) and YLD, providing an estimate of the total disease burden. Specifically, YLD reflects the years lived in suboptimal health due to NASH-related liver cancer, while YLL represents the years lost due to premature death. These metrics offer a comprehensive understanding of the disease burden, capturing both fatal and non-fatal consequences of NASH-related liver cancer associated with HFPG.

Uncertainty estimation interval

The uncertainty estimation interval (UI) was calculated to address potential heterogeneity resulting from both sampling error and non-sampling variance for NASH-related liver cancer associated with HFPG from 1990 to 2021. The 95% UI was derived by taking 1000 samples from the posterior distribution at each step of the modelling process. The interval was then reported as the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles of each estimate. These intervals were directly obtained through the GHDx and incorporated into the analysis to account for uncertainties in model inputs, ensuring more reliable estimates of disease burden.

Statistical analysis

Given that the GBD 202122 database encompasses different age groups and changes over time, age-standardised rates were used to assess the burden of NASH-related liver cancer associated with HFPG. The indicators analysed in this study include mortality, DALYs, YLDs and YLLs. The trends for these four indicators from 1990 to 2021 in both global and Chinese contexts were described using the annual percentage change (APC) and average annual percentage change (AAPC) derived from the Joinpoint regression model. A Bayesian age-period-cohort (BAPC) predictive model was constructed using the ‘BAPC’ and ‘INLA (Integrated Nested Laplace Approximation)’ packages in R software (V.4.2.3) to predict the age-standardised mortality rates (ASMRs) and age-standardised DALY rates associated with HFPG-related NASH-related liver cancer over the next 15 years for both global and Chinese populations.

Patient and public involvement

Patients and/or the public were not involved in the design, or conduct, or reporting, or dissemination plans of this research.

Results

Disease burden of NASH-related liver cancer associated with HFPG in different age groups in 2021 for global and China

The study results indicate that in 2021, global mortality, DALY, YLD and YLL rates for men were consistently higher than those for women, highlighting a clear gender disparity (figure 1; onlinesupplemental figures 1 2). Specifically, male mortality rates peaked at ages 90–94 years, after which a sharp decline was observed, whereas female mortality rates reached their maximum at ages 85–89 years before decreasing. The DALY rate followed a similar pattern, with men peaking at ages 80–84 years and women at slightly younger ages. In terms of absolute numbers, deaths peaked in the 70–74 years age group for both sexes (men: 887.28; women: 837.76), then declined. DALY losses were highest in the 65–69 years age group (men: 21 549.40; women: 18 583.44), indicating that the middle-aged population bears the greatest overall disease burden globally.

Consistent with global trends, mortality, DALY, YLD and YLL rates for men in China were higher than those for women, demonstrating a pronounced gender disparity (figure 2; onlinesupplemental figures 3 4). Both male and female mortality rates peaked at ages 90–94 years, with subsequent declines. However, the DALY rate for men peaked at ages 90–94 years, while for women, it peaked earlier, at ages 80–84 years, reflecting differential age-related disease burden between genders. The number of deaths peaked in the 65–69 years age group for men (184.31 cases) and 70–74 for women (174.52 cases), followed by decreasing trends. DALY losses were also highest in the 65–69 years age group (men: 184.31; women: 168.27), underscoring that middle-aged adults in China bear the greatest burden of NASH-related liver cancer associated with HFPG.

Long-term trends in NASH-related liver cancer associated with HFPG in global and China

The results indicate that the global number of deaths associated with HFPG in NASH-related liver cancer increased from 2319 in 1990 to 10 021 in 2021. The total DALYs associated with HFPG in NASH-related liver cancer rose from 27 885 years in 1990 to 213 481 years in 2021. The ASMR increased from 0.06 per 100 000 in 1990 to 0.12 per 100 000 in 2021, while the age-standardised DALY rate increased from 1.35 per 100 000 to 2.45 per 100 000 over the same period. Sex-stratified analysis revealed that the number of deaths among males was lower than that among females in 1990 but exceeded that of females in 2021. However, the DALY burden was consistently higher in males than in females in both 1990 and 2021. Among males, the ASMR increased from 0.07 per 100 000 in 1990 to 0.13 per 100 000 in 2021, while the age-standardised DALY rate increased from 1.5 per 100 000 to 2.8 per 100 000. Among females, the ASMR increased from 0.06 per 100 000 in 1990 to 0.11 per 100 000 in 2021, while the age-standardised DALY rate increased from 1.22 per 100 000 to 2.14 per 100 000. Compared with 1990, all indicators demonstrated an upward trend in 2021 (table 1).

The study results show that between 1990 and 2021, the global ASMR increased by 91% and the age-standardised DALY rate rose by 81%. For males, the ASMR grew by 97%, while the DALY rate increased by 86%; in contrast, females experienced a 76% rise in age-standardised DALY rate. In China, the ASMR saw a 20% increase, with a 12% rise in the age-standardised DALY rate. Specifically, for males in China, the ASMR increased by 30% and the DALY rate by 23%, whereas for females, the mortality rate rose by 11% and the DALY rate increased by just 2% (online supplemental table 1).

Annual trends in NASH-related liver cancer associated with HFPG in global and China

The results indicate that, globally, from 1994 to 2000, the highest APC in ASMR and age-standardised DALY rate associated with HFPG-related NASH liver cancer was 4.34% and 4.02%, respectively. The lowest APC values were observed in 2016–2021 for ASMR (0.28%) and in 2000–2006 for the DALY rate (0.34%). Stratified by gender, the highest APC values for ASMR and the DALY rate in males were 4.63% and 4.26%, respectively, while the lowest APC values were recorded in 2000–2006 at 0.57% and 0.32%, respectively. In females, the highest APC values for ASMR and the DALY rate were 4.08% and 3.75% in 1994–2000, while the lowest APC values were observed in 2016–2021 for ASMR (0.12%) and in 2017–2021 for the DALY rate (−0.05%). Except for the period from 2000 to 2006, during which the APC of the age-standardised DALY rate in females was higher than that in males, males exhibited higher APC values than females for both ASMR and the age-standardised DALY rate across all other periods. Notably, only in 2017–2021 did the APC of the age-standardised DALY rate for females exhibit a declining trend, at −0.05%. From a long-term perspective, between 1990 and 2021, the AAPC in ASMR and the DALY rate among males was 2.24% and 2.05%, respectively, both exceeding those of the total population and females. This indicates that males experienced a more pronounced overall increasing trend (table 3).

Future burden of NASH-related liver cancer associated with HFPG in the next 15 years globally and in China

Discussion

With global population ageing and lifestyle changes, the burden of NASH-related liver cancer has been increasing, particularly the burden associated with HFPG. Effectively addressing this public health challenge requires a comprehensive understanding of the disease burden across sex and age groups to inform identification of high-risk populations and guide evidence-based public health strategies. In this study, we provide a detailed analysis of the mortality rate, DALY rate, YLD rate and YLL rate of NASH-related liver cancer associated with HFPG in 2021, stratified by sex and age, both globally and in China. Furthermore, we explore the peak burden across different age groups to offer scientific evidence for public health interventions.

In 2021, the mortality rate, DALY rate, YLD rate and YLL rate of NASH-related liver cancer associated with HFPG were higher in males than in females, both globally and in China. Regarding age distribution, the peak mortality rate among global males occurred in the 90–94 years age group, while for global females, it peaked in the 85–89 years age group. In China, both males and females reached peak mortality in the 90–94 years age group. Globally, the highest absolute number of deaths was observed in the 70–74 years age group, whereas the greatest DALY loss was recorded in the 65–69 years age group. The patterns observed in China were consistent with global trends, indicating that individuals aged 65–74 years may represent a critical window for targeted intervention and prevention strategies. Previous studies have suggested that males have a stronger association with metabolic syndrome compared with females, and metabolic syndrome is often accompanied by hyperglycaemia and insulin resistance, both of which play a crucial role in the progression of diabetes.23 Moreover, evidence suggests that hyperglycaemia serves as a hallmark of diabetes and contributes to the development of hepatic steatosis and fibrosis progression through chronic inflammation, oxidative stress and dysregulation of hepatic insulin signalling, thereby increasing the risk of NASH progression to liver cancer.24 A study further demonstrated that metabolic syndrome is significantly associated with an increased incidence of liver cancer in males,16 which may explain the higher burden of NASH-related liver cancer associated with HFPG in males compared with females. Furthermore, studies have shown that patients with NASH progress by one stage of hepatic fibrosis every 7.1 years on average. Without effective intervention, these patients may progress to liver cirrhosis over several decades, further increasing the risk of liver cancer.25

From 1990 to 2021, the burden of NASH-related liver cancer associated with HFPG increased significantly both globally and in China, with a rising trend in ASMR and DALY rates. Globally, the number of deaths increased 4.3-fold, while DALYs increased 7.7-fold. In China, deaths and DALYs increased 3.2-fold and 5.3-fold, respectively. This upward trend reflects the impact of population ageing, the increasing prevalence of metabolic disorders and the rising incidence of diabetes on the burden of HFPG-attributable NASH-related liver cancer.19 26 The results of this study also indicate that the disease burden was consistently higher in men than in women globally and in China, except for a slightly higher number of HFPG-attributable NASH-related liver cancer cases among women than men in 1990 at the global level. Several factors may contribute to this gender disparity. First, men are at a higher risk of metabolic abnormalities. Studies have shown that men are more susceptible to metabolic syndrome and are more likely to develop insulin resistance and type 2 diabetes, thereby accelerating the progression of NASH to liver cancer.27 Second, lifestyle and behavioural factors play a critical role. Higher rates of smoking and alcohol consumption among men, along with dietary habits and insufficient physical activity, may further exacerbate metabolic dysregulation and liver damage.28 29 Lastly, hormonal protective effects may contribute to the observed sex differences. Oestrogen has been suggested to have a protective role in women by reducing hepatic steatosis, oxidative stress and inflammation, thereby lowering the risk of NASH progression to liver cancer. However, with declining oestrogen levels after menopause, this protective effect may diminish, potentially explaining the higher mortality rate among elderly women.30

This study found that the APC of age-standardised mortality and age-standardised DALY rate was the highest in the world during 1994–2000, which was 4.34% and 4.02%, respectively, while China reached a higher peak during 1995–2000, which was 7.22% and 6.70%, respectively. This phenomenon indicates that the NASH-related liver cancer disease burden associated with HFPG in China increased much faster than the global level during this period, which may be related to a series of economic policies and reforms introduced in China during this period.3134 Economic policies and reforms that promote rapid economic development are closely related to factors that may lead to changes in dietary structure, rising rates of obesity and increasing prevalence of diabetes.35

From a long-term perspective, the ASMR and age-standardised DALY rate associated with HFPG for NASH-related liver cancer have exhibited higher AAPC globally compared with China. In recent years, from 2016 to 2021, the global ASMR and DALY rate associated with HFPG for NASH-related liver cancer have continued to increase, although at a slower pace. In contrast, in China, the ASMR and DALY rate associated with HFPG for NASH-related liver cancer have shown a declining trend from 2014 to 2019, although the magnitude of decline remains relatively modest. This trend is more pronounced when stratified by sex. Specifically, from 2018 to 2021, the ASMR for Chinese men declined by −7.85%, while from 2019 to 2021, the age-standardised DALY rate decreased by −9.12%. Similarly, among Chinese women, the ASMR and age-standardised DALY rate declined by −7.83% and −6.77%, respectively, from 2019 to 2021. Compared with the continued increase observed among both men and women globally, the substantial decline in ASMR and age-standardised DALY rate among Chinese men and women suggests a distinct trend. One possible explanation for this disparity between global and Chinese trends may be the implementation of the ‘Healthy China 2030’ policy in recent years, which aims to strengthen the prevention and control of major diseases, including cardiovascular diseases, cancer, chronic respiratory diseases and diabetes.36

In addition, this study employed the BAPC model to predict the future burden of NASH-related liver cancer associated with HFPG in global and Chinese populations over the next 15 years. The results indicate that globally, ASMR and age-standardised DALY rates for males are expected to continue increasing, while for females, a declining trend is projected. In contrast, predictions for China suggest a different pattern, with both male and female ASMR and age-standardised DALY rates expected to decrease over the next 15 years. Compared with traditional linear regression or time-series forecasting models, the BAPC model more accurately captures the interactions among age effects, temporal trends and cohort effects, thus providing more reliable long-term projections and scientific evidence for the development of targeted intervention strategies.37

This study is the first to use 2021 GBD data to assess the burden of NASH-related liver cancer associated with HFPG by year and age in both global and Chinese populations. However, several limitations exist. Data quality and collection standards vary across regions, and the GBD relies on models and assumptions that may not fully capture the real-world impact of HFPG, especially given the complexity of disease mechanisms. The database primarily provides population-level data, limiting analyses of individual risk factors such as lifestyle or genetics. Since our study focuses on trend analysis and future projections based on aggregated GBD data, it does not aim to assess causal relationships or the effects of specific risk factors. Therefore, individual-level confounders were not considered in the current analysis. We acknowledge that future studies using individual-level data should take into account potential confounding factors to better evaluate the determinants of NASH-related liver cancer. HFPG is only one of multiple contributors to NASH-related liver cancer, so its attributable burden may be underestimated or overestimated, and whether reducing HFPG can mitigate risk remains unconfirmed. Importantly, whether lowering HFPG can reduce the burden of NASH-related liver cancer remains unconfirmed, and current evidence is largely indirect. Disability was assessed using standardised weights reflecting general reductions in physical function, quality of life and daily activity, without detailing specific clinical manifestations. Furthermore, projections using the BAPC model are based on historical trends and current epidemiology and do not account for potential future interventions, such as widespread antiviral therapy for chronic viral hepatitis or emerging treatments like glucagon-like peptide-1 and glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide receptor agonists. The long-term impact of such interventions on NASH-related liver cancer is uncertain, so these projections should be interpreted cautiously as trends expected in the absence of these interventions.

Conclusion

In summary, the burden of NASH-related liver cancer associated with HFPG has shown an upward trend from 1990 to 2021, both globally and in China, with higher burden in males and older age groups. Model predictions suggest that while the burden in China may show a decreasing trend over the next 15 years, the global burden is projected to continue rising. Identifying high-risk populations, including males, older individuals, those with HFPG and patients with NASH, is crucial for monitoring and potential targeted interventions to manage the disease burden. Future studies are needed to evaluate whether controlling blood glucose could reduce the risk of NASH-related liver cancer.

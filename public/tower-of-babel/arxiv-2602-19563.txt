Multigraded Hurwitz forms
Elizabeth Pratt, Luca Sodomaco, Bernd Sturmfels

Abstract
The Hurwitz form of a projective variety characterizes linear spaces of complementary dimension which meet the variety non-transversally. We extend this notion to varieties in a product of projective spaces. This parallels the multigraded Chow forms due to Osserman and Trager. We study the degrees of multigraded Hurwitz forms. An explicit degree formula is given for complete intersections. This offers a new tool for elimination theory that has many applications, ranging from Nash equilibria to Feynman integrals.

Foundations and Examples

Our ambient space is the product of projective spaces
\mathbb{P}\coloneqq\mathbb{P}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{P}^{n_{2}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{P}^{n_{\ell}}
.
Note that
\mathbb{P}
has dimension
N:=n_{1}+n_{2}+\cdots+n_{\ell}
.
The
Chow ring
of
\mathbb{P}
is the truncated polynomial ring

A^{*}(\mathbb{P})\,\,=\,\,\mathbb{Z}[T_{1},T_{2},\ldots,T_{\ell}]/\bigl\langle\,T_{1}^{n_{1}+1},\,T_{2}^{n_{2}+1},\,\ldots,\,T_{\ell}^{n_{\ell}+1}\bigr\rangle.

(4)

The coordinate
ring of
\mathbb{P}
is a polynomial ring

in
N+\ell
variables, graded by the group
\mathbb{Z}^{\ell}
.

We consider an irreducible variety
X
of codimension

in
\mathbb{P}
.
Its defining prime ideal

is
\mathbb{Z}^{\ell}
-homogeneous.
The

of

is its class
[X]
in the Chow ring
A^{*}(\mathbb{P})
.
This is a homogeneous polynomial of degree
c
in the formal variables
T_{1},T_{2},\ldots,T_{\ell}
, written as

\hskip 20.00003pt[X]\,\,=\,\,\sum_{|\alpha|=c}\delta_{\alpha}\,T^{\alpha}\hskip 20.00003pt\in\hskip 10.00002ptA^{*}(\mathbb{P}).

(5)

In
Macaulay2

[
6
]
,
one computes
[X]
from generators of
I_{X}
with the command

multidegree
.

Let
\mathbb{G}(k,n)
denote the Grassmannian of
(k+1)
-dimensional subspaces in
\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
.
For us, points in
\mathbb{G}(k,n)
are linear subspaces of dimension
k
in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
.
In the multigraded setting, where the ambient space is
\mathbb{P}
, the role of linear subspaces is
played by products
L_{1}\times\cdots\times L_{\ell}
, where

L_{i}\in\mathbb{G}(\alpha_{i},n_{i})
for
i
in
[\ell]:=\{1,\ldots,\ell\}
.
This is a subvariety in
\mathbb{P}
of dimension
|\alpha|:=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\alpha_{i}
.

Osserman and Trager

Elements

in
\,\mathbb{P}=\mathbb{P}^{n_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{P}^{n_{\ell}}

\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}_{X}\,\,\,:=\,\,\,\bigl\{(L_{1},\ldots,L_{\ell})\in\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}\,:\,L\,\cap\,X\not=\emptyset\bigr\}.

(7)

A combinatorial criterion for when (

) has codimension

is given in

[

is the defining polynomial of (

in (

If

\geq 2

We now define the multigraded Hurwitz forms of
X
.
Each of them arises from projecting
an incidence correspondence. Fix
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{\ell}
such that
|\alpha|=c
and
\alpha_{i}\leq n_{i}
.
We define

For every subset

The

&\text{$\pi_{S}(L)\cap\pi_{S}(X)_{\rm reg}$ is transverse at $\pi_{S}(p)$ for $S\subsetneq[\ell]$}\end{aligned}\right\}}\,\subset\,X\times\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}\,.

We define the

(Plane curves)

Fix a bivariate polynomial

45
terms.
In the fully mixed setting of Example
2.5
, we would
work on a variety
X
in
(\mathbb{P}^{3})^{4}
, so the mixed discriminant has

10156
terms. If all supports
A_{i}
are equal, then the
system is unmixed, and we are using a toric variety
X

in a single
\mathbb{P}^{n}
. Here
\mathrm{Hu}^{c}_{X}
is the
Hurwitz form of a toric variety, as in

[
17
, Examples 2.3 and 2.7]
.
∎

We now return to the general case. Our aim is to
describe the ideal of the multigraded Hurwitz incidence
\Phi^{\alpha}_{X}
.
Let
I_{X}
be the prime ideal of the irreducible variety
X\subset\mathbb{P}
.
The generators of
I_{X}
are
\mathbb{Z}^{\ell}
-homogeneous polynomials in the ring
R\coloneqq\bigotimes_{i=1}^{\ell}\mathbb{C}[x^{(i)}]
, where
\mathbb{C}[x^{(i)}]
denotes
\mathbb{C}[x_{i,0},\ldots,x_{i,n_{i}}]
. In what follows,
we work in the coordinate ring
R[\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}]
of the product (
6
) over
R
.
Let
J_{i}
be the bilinear ideal that encodes the condition
p_{i}\in L_{i}
. The ideal

K\,\,\coloneqq\,\,I_{X}+J_{1}+\cdots+J_{\ell}\hskip 10.00002pt\subset\,\,R[\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}]

defines the incidence variety
W\subset\mathbb{P}\times\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}

whose points are the pairs
(p,L)
with
p\in L\cap X
.

Let
I_{{\rm sing}(X)}
be the ideal of the singular locus of
X
, and let
A
be the Jacobian matrix of partial derivatives of the generators of
K
with respect to all variables
x_{i,j}
in
R
.
The ideal

J\,\,\coloneqq\,\,\bigl(\,K+\,\langle\text{${\rm codim}(W)$-minors of $A$}\rangle\,\bigr)\,\colon I_{{\rm sing}(X)}^{\infty}

encodes the condition “
L\cap X_{\rm reg}
is not transverse at
p
” in the definition of the variety
\Phi^{\alpha}_{X}
.

It remains to encode the requirement “
\pi_{S}(L)\cap\pi_{S}(X)_{\rm reg}
is transverse at
p
”.
For
S\subsetneq[\ell]
, the projection
\pi_{S}(X)
of our variety
X
to
\prod_{i\in S}\mathbb{P}^{n_{i}}
is defined by the elimination ideal

\mathfrak{I}_{S}\,\,\coloneqq\,\,I_{X}\,\cap\,\bigotimes_{i\in S}\mathbb{C}[x^{(i)}]\,.

Let
W_{S}
denote the incidence variety in
\prod_{i\in S}\mathbb{P}^{n_{i}}\times\prod_{i\in S}\mathbb{G}(\alpha_{i},n_{i})

that is defined by the ideal

K_{S}\,\,\coloneqq\,\,\mathfrak{I}_{S}\,+\,\sum_{i\in S}J_{i}\,.

Let
\mathfrak{I}_{S,\,{\rm sing}}
be the ideal of the singular locus of
\pi_{S}(X)
,
and let
A_{S}
be the Jacobian matrix of partial derivatives of the generators of
K_{S}
with respect to
the variables
x_{i,j}
with
i\in S
.

The locus we wish to remove in the definition of
\Phi^{\alpha}_{X}
is defined by the saturation ideal

\hskip 20.00003ptT_{S}\,\,\coloneqq\,\,\bigl(\,K_{S}\,+\langle\text{${\rm codim}(W_{S})$-minors of $A_{S}$}\rangle\bigr)\,\colon\mathfrak{I}_{S,\,{\rm sing}}^{\infty}\hskip 20.00003pt{\rm for}\,\,\,S\subsetneq[\ell]\,.

Indeed, the points in
T_{S}
describe scenarios where

\pi_{S}(L)
intersects
\pi_{S}(X)_{\rm reg}
non-transversally.

Proposition 2.7
.

The ideal that defines the multigraded Hurwitz incidence
\Phi^{\alpha}_{X}
equals

J\,\colon\,\biggl(\,\prod_{S\subsetneq[\ell]}T_{S}\biggr)^{\!\infty}.

(10)

The ideal of the Hurwitz locus
\mathcal{H}_{X}^{\alpha}

is obtained from (
10
)
by eliminating all the variables
x_{i,j}
in
R
.
If this elimination ideal is principal then its generator is the Hurwitz form

\mathrm{Hu}^{\alpha}_{X}
.

Proof.

The ideals
J,\mathfrak{I}_{S},K_{S},T_{S}
in
R[\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}]

represent a translation into algebra of our geometric definition of

\Phi^{\alpha}_{X}
in (
8
). The projection from
\Phi^{\alpha}_{X}
onto
\mathcal{H}_{X}^{\alpha}

corresponds to the final elimination step, where we intersect (
10
)
with
\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}]
. This ring is where the Hurwitz form lives.
∎

Proposition
2.7

provides an algorithm for computing multigraded Hurwitz forms.
When implementing this, it is important to be
clever about all saturation steps, especially that
in (
10
). In practice, it is advantageous to
perform elimination prior to saturating, and to
extract
\mathrm{Hu}^{\alpha}_{X}
as the main factor from the output.
Here is an illustration for a small example.

Example 2.8
.

Let
X
be the surface in
\mathbb{P}^{3}\times\mathbb{P}^{3}

\displaystyle\begin{split}&y_{1}y_{2}-y_{0}y_{3},\quad x_{2}y_{2}+x_{3}y_{3},\quad x_{2}y_{0}+x_{3}y_{1},\\
&x_{1}y_{1}+x_{3}y_{3},\quad x_{1}y_{0}+x_{3}y_{2},\quad x_{0}y_{2}+x_{1}y_{3},\\
&x_{0}y_{1}+x_{2}y_{3},\quad x_{0}y_{0}-x_{3}y_{3},\quad x_{1}x_{2}-x_{0}x_{3}\,.\end{split}

(11)

This is the conormal variety of the quadratic surface

\mathbb{P}^{3}

The dual variety

Geometrically, the surface

The multidegree is

We compute the locus
\mathcal{H}_{X}^{\alpha}
for

The Hurwitz incidence
\Phi^{\alpha}_{X}

(\mathbb{P}^{3})^{4}

[y_{0}:y_{1}:y_{2}:y_{3}]

\mathbb{P}^{3}

.
Once checks that

is a prime ideal. The variety it defines has codimension
6
in
(\mathbb{P}^{3})^{4}
.

The Jacobian matrix of the
11

with respect to

A\,\,\,=\,\,\footnotesize\begin{bmatrix}0&0&0&0&-y_{3}&y_{2}&y_{1}&-y_{0}\\
0&0&y_{2}&y_{3}&0&0&x_{2}&x_{3}\\
0&0&y_{0}&y_{1}&x_{2}&x_{3}&0&0\\
0&y_{1}&0&y_{3}&0&x_{1}&0&x_{3}\\
0&y_{0}&0&y_{2}&x_{1}&0&x_{3}&0\\
y_{2}&y_{3}&0&0&0&0&x_{0}&x_{1}\\
y_{1}&0&y_{3}&0&0&x_{0}&0&x_{2}\\
y_{0}&0&0&-y_{3}&x_{0}&0&0&-x_{3}\\

After computing the ideal

or

\begin{matrix}T_{1}&=&\langle x_{1}x_{2}-x_{0}x_{3},a_{0}x_{0}+\cdots+a_{3}x_{3}\rangle&+&\langle\text{$2\times 2$ minors of $A_{1}$}\rangle,\\

\,b_{0}&b_{1}&b_{2}&b_{3}\end{bmatrix}

\,a_{0}&a_{1}&a_{2}&a_{3}\end{bmatrix}

The ideal of
\Phi^{\alpha}_{X}
is

.
Eliminating

and

from the previous ideal yields the Hurwitz form

\begin{matrix}{\rm Hu}^{(2,2)}_{X}\,&=&a_{0}^{2}b_{0}^{2}+a_{1}^{2}b_{1}^{2}+a_{2}^{2}b_{2}^{2}+a_{3}^{2}b_{3}^{2}+4\,a_{1}a_{2}b_{0}b_{3}+4\,a_{0}a_{3}b_{1}b_{2}+2\,a_{0}a_{1}b_{0}b_{1}\\
&&+\,2\,a_{0}a_{2}b_{0}b_{2}-2\,a_{0}a_{3}b_{0}b_{3}-2\,a_{1}a_{2}b_{1}b_{2}+2\,a_{1}a_{3}b_{1}b_{3}+2\,a_{2}a_{3}b_{2}b_{3}.\end{matrix}

This is the
hyperdeterminant
of the
2\times 2\times 2
tensor
formed by the eight coordinates of
\mathbb{G}_{(2,2)}
.
Note that the nine quadrics in (

) also define the
Segre embedding of
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
in
\mathbb{P}^{7}
.
∎

Conormal varieties provide an interesting family to test
our theory. Here is the general definition.
Given any irreducible variety
Z\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
, its
conormal variety
is
N_{Z}=\overline{N_{Z}^{\circ}}
, where

N_{Z}^{\circ}\,\coloneqq\,\bigl\{\,(p,H)\in\mathbb{P}^{n}\times(\mathbb{P}^{n})^{*}\mid\text{
$H$ is tangent to $Z$ at
$p\in Z_{\rm reg}$}\bigr\}\,.

It is known that
N_{Z}
is irreducible of codimension
n+1
in
\mathbb{P}^{n}\times(\mathbb{P}^{n})^{*}
. Its multidegree is

[N_{Z}]\,\,=\,\,\delta_{0}\,T_{1}^{n}T_{2}\,+\,\delta_{1}\,T_{1}^{n-1}T_{2}^{2}\,+\,\cdots\,+\,\delta_{n-1}\,T_{1}T_{2}^{n}\,,

where the coefficients
\delta_{i}
are the
polar degrees
of
Z
. Reading from left to right, the first nonzero coefficient
\delta_{i}
is the degree of the dual variety
Z^{\vee}
which has codimension
i-1
in
(\mathbb{P}^{n})^{*}
.
In Example
2.8

we studied

for the surface
Z
defined by the

2\times 2
determinant.

3
Degree Formula

In this section we present a formula for the degree of the multigraded Hurwitz form.
To state our result, we need some definitions. As before, we fix an irreducible
variety
X
of codimension
c\,
in
\,\mathbb{P}=\mathbb{P}^{n_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{P}^{n_{\ell}}
.
The
multisectional genus
of
X
is a polynomial

g(X)\,\,\,\,\coloneqq\,\,\sum_{|\beta|=c+1}\!\!g_{\beta}\,T^{\beta}.

(12)

The coefficient
g_{\beta}
is the geometric genus of the curve
X\cap(L_{1}\times\cdots\times L_{\ell})
,
where
L_{1}\in\mathbb{G}(\beta_{1},n_{1}),\,\ldots,\,L_{\ell}\in\mathbb{G}(\beta_{\ell},n_{\ell})
are generic.
If that intersection is not a curve, then
g_{\beta}\coloneqq 0
.

Example 3.1
.

We consider three subvarieties in
\mathbb{P}=\mathbb{P}^{3}\times\mathbb{P}^{3}
.
First, let
X_{2}
be the conormal surface of the Fermat cubic
C=\{x_{0}^{3}+x_{1}^{3}+x_{2}^{3}+x_{3}^{3}=0\}
.
This has multidegree
12T_{1}^{3}T_{2}+6T_{1}^{2}T_{2}^{2}+3T_{1}T_{2}^{3}
, and its ideal has
22
minimal
generators. The multisectional genus
records the sectional genera of the dual surface
C^{\vee}
of degree
12
and
of the given cubic surface
C
:

\,g(X_{2})\,\,=\,\,4T_{1}^{3}T_{2}^{2}\,+\,T_{1}^{2}T_{2}^{3}.

Let
X_{3}
be the intersection of
three generic hypersurfaces of degrees
(1,2),(1,2),(2,1)
.
The multisectional genus of the threefold
X_{3}
can be computed using the adjunction formula:

\,g(X_{3})\,\,=\,\,4T_{1}^{3}T_{2}\,+\,16T_{1}^{2}T_{2}^{2}\,+\,11T_{1}T_{2}^{3}.

The middle coefficient
16
is the genus of
X_{3}\cap(P_{1}\!\times\!P_{2})
where

P_{1},P_{2}
are generic planes in
\mathbb{P}^{3}
.

Finally, let
X_{4}
be the toric
4
-fold in Example
2.1
.
Its multisectional genus equals

g(X_{4})\,\,=\,\,T_{1}^{2}T_{2}\,+\,T_{1}T_{2}^{2}.

(13)

This means that the curve in
\mathbb{P}
defined by three of the
four equations in (
1
) has genus one.
∎

For arbitrary subvarieties
X
of
\mathbb{P}
, we compute
the multisectional genus
g(X)
as follows.
From the ideal of the curve
C=X\cap(L_{1}\times\cdots\times L_{\ell})
, we compute the multigraded Hilbert series

\frac{K(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{\ell})}{(1-z_{1})^{n_{1}+1}(1-z_{2})^{n_{2}+1}\,\cdots\,(1-z_{\ell})^{n_{\ell}+1}}\hskip 10.00002pt=\hskip 10.00002pt\sum_{u\in\mathbb{N}^{\ell}}c_{u_{1},u_{2},\cdots,u_{\ell}}z_{1}^{u_{1}}z_{2}^{u_{2}}\cdots z_{\ell}^{u_{\ell}}.

Next, we examine the sequence of diagonal coefficients

c_{m,m,\ldots,m}
for
m=1,2,3,\ldots
.
For
m\gg 0
, this sequence is a linear function of
m
, namely it is the
Hilbert polynomial of the Segre embedding
of
C\subset\mathbb{P}
into a projective space of
dimension
\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}(n_{i}+1)-1
. Therefore,

\hskip 10.00002ptc_{m,m,\ldots,m}\,\,=\,\,{\rm degree}(C)\cdot m\,+\,(1-{\rm genus}(C))\hskip 20.00003pt{\rm for}\,\,\,\,m\gg 0.

(14)

If
C
is smooth then this gives us the correct geometric genus for
the coefficient of
T^{\beta}
in
g(X)
.
If
C
is nodal then we subtract the number of nodes.
Otherwise, we just get a bound.

We now come to the second ingredient for our degree formula
in Theorem
3.4
.
The following definition
allows us to weaken the hypothesis, made in
[
17
, Theorem 1.1]
,
that the singular locus of
X
has codimension at least
2
.
A curve
C\subset\mathbb{P}
is said to be
polynodal
if, for any subset
S\subseteq[\ell]
, the projection of
C
to
\prod_{i\in S}\mathbb{P}^{n_{i}}
is a curve with at most nodal singularities. Our variety
X\subset\mathbb{P}
is called
polynodal
if, for any generic linear variety
L_{1}\times\cdots\times L_{\ell}

of dimension
c+1
in
\mathbb{P}
, the curve
X\cap(L_{1}\times\cdots\times L_{\ell})
is polynodal,
whenever this is a curve.

Note that to check whether a curve is polynodal, we do not have to check the projection to every subproduct of projective spaces.
It suffices to check that the projections to every
\mathbb{P}^{n_{i}}
with
n_{i}\geq 2
and every
\mathbb{P}^{n_{i}}\times\mathbb{P}^{n_{j}}
with
n_{i}=n_{j}=1
have at most nodal singularities.

Remark 3.2
.

Consider the case
\ell=1
when
X

is a variety of degree
d
in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
. Then
X
being polynodal means that
the generic curve section of
X
has at most nodal singularities.
This ensures that the Hurwitz form

{\rm Hu}^{c}_{X}
has the expected degree
2d+2g-2
, where
g
is the
geometric genus of that curve. In
[
17
, Theorem 1.1]
,
the stronger assumption was made that
X
is regular in codimension one, in
which case geometric genus equals arithmetic genus.

Example 3.3
.

Conormal varieties are generally not polynodal.
For instance, the conormal curve
C\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{2}

of any smooth curve of degree
\geq 3
in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
is not polynodal
because the inflection points of the given curve correspond to cusps in its dual curve. The conormal surface
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{3}\times\mathbb{P}^{3}
in Example

2.8
is polynodal because it is derived
from a quadric.
∎

We now state our main result. It generalizes
[
17
, Theorem 1.1]
to the multigraded case.
For the irreducibility result, the hypothesis on the dimensions
n_{i}
is needed by
Remark
2.3
.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
X
be an irreducible variety of codimension
c
in
\mathbb{P}
.
Fix
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{\ell}
with
|\alpha|=c
, and

\delta_{\alpha}\geq 2
in (
5
),
and
n_{i}\geq{\rm max}(2,1+\alpha_{i})

for
i\in[\ell]
.
Then the multigraded Hurwitz locus
\mathcal{H}^{\alpha}_{X}
is an irreducible variety in
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
, and
the degree
u
of the Hurwitz form
{\rm Hu}^{\alpha}_{X}
satisfies

u_{i}\,\,\leq\,\,2(g_{\alpha+e_{i}}+\delta_{\alpha}-1)\hskip 20.00003pt\hbox{for}\,\,\,i\in[\ell].

(15)

All inequalities in (
15
) are equalities if the variety
X
is polynodal.

Example 3.5

(Toric
4
-fold)
.

We revisit our running example,
which is the
4
-fold in
\mathbb{P}^{3}\times\mathbb{P}^{3}
defined by the

2\times 2
minors of the matrix (
3
).
From Example
2.1
and equation (
13
) we know
the multidegree

[X]=2T_{1}^{2}+4T_{1}T_{2}+2T_{2}^{2}

and the multisectional genus
g(X)=T_{1}^{2}T_{2}\,+\,T_{1}T_{2}^{2}
.
According to Theorem
3.4
, the expected degree of the
Hurwitz form
{\rm Hu}_{X}^{(1,1)}
equals

\bigl(\,2(g_{(2,1)}+\delta_{(1,1)}-1),\,2(g_{(1,2)}+\delta_{(1,1)}-1)\,\bigr)\,\,=\,\,\bigr(\,8\,,\,8\,\bigr).

(16)

However, the degree of
{\rm Hu}_{X}^{(1,1)}
is
(6,6)
, as shown by the explicit formula in the Introduction.

The explanation for this discrepancy is that
X
fails to be polynodal.
For generic lines
L
and planes
P
in
\mathbb{P}^{3}
, the curve

C=X\cap(L\times P)
is smooth of genus one in
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{2}
.
Its projection into
\mathbb{P}^{2}
is a curve of degree four
which has geometric genus one. It has two singular points, both of which are
cusps. Hence
C
is not polynodal.
In the Riemann-Hurwitz formula, we must subtract one
whenever a node is replaced by a cusp. Hence the degree of
{\rm Hu}_{X}^{(1,1)}

in the second factor of
\mathbb{G}_{(1,1)}
is
6
instead of
8
. The same
holds for the first factor.

It is instructive to modify this example by replacing (
3
) with nearby matrices, such as

\begin{bmatrix}\,x_{0}y_{3}&x_{1}x_{2}&x_{3}-x_{2}\,\,\\
\,y_{1}y_{2}&x_{3}y_{0}&y_{3}\,\,\end{bmatrix}\hskip 20.00003pt{\rm or}\hskip 20.00003pt\begin{bmatrix}\,x_{0}y_{3}&x_{1}x_{2}&x_{3}-x_{2}\,\,\\
\,y_{1}y_{2}&x_{3}y_{0}&y_{3}+y_{1}\,\,\end{bmatrix}.

Here,
[X]
and
g(X)
are unchanged, but
the Hurwitz forms now have degrees
(7,8)
and
(8,8)
.
On the left, one projected curve
has one node and one cusp.
On the right,
X
is polynodal.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.4
.

We first show the irreducibility of
the multigraded Hurwitz locus
\mathcal{H}_{X}^{\alpha}
. For every subset
S\subsetneq[\ell]
,
consider the following Zariski closed subset of the given variety
X
:

V_{S}\,\,\coloneqq\,\,\overline{\left\{p\in X_{\rm reg}\ \bigg|\ \text{$\exists L\in\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}$ such that $\pi_{S}(L)\cap\pi_{S}(X)_{\rm reg}$ is not transverse at $\pi_{S}(p)$}\right\}}\,.

Let
\mathcal{U}\coloneqq X_{\rm reg}\cap\bigcup_{S\subsetneq[\ell]}(X\setminus V_{S})
.
If
\mathcal{U}=\emptyset
, then
\Phi^{\alpha}_{X}=\emptyset
and there is nothing to prove.
Otherwise
\mathcal{U}
is an open dense subset of
X
, since each

V_{S}
is a closed subvariety. Let
\mathrm{pr}_{1}
denote the projection of
X\times\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}

onto the first factor
X
. We have

\Phi^{\alpha}_{X}=\overline{\mathrm{pr}_{1}^{-1}(\mathcal{U})}
, and all fibers of
\mathrm{pr}_{1}
over
\mathcal{U}
are multilinear of the same dimension. Moreover, the generic fiber is irreducible.
This ensures that
\Phi^{\alpha}_{X}
is irreducible. Since
\mathcal{H}_{X}^{\alpha}
is the projection of
\Phi^{\alpha}_{X}
to
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
, we conclude that
\mathcal{H}_{X}^{\alpha}
is irreducible. Hence
the Hurwitz form
{\rm Hu}_{X}^{\alpha}
is an irreducible polynomial.

We now prove the statement about the degree of
{\rm Hu}_{X}^{\alpha}
.
In the case
\ell=1
this follows from
[
17
, Theorem 1.1]
.
From now on, we assume
\ell\geq 2
.
Fix
i\in[\ell]
and set
L=L_{1}\times\cdots\times L_{\ell}\subset\mathbb{P}
,
where the factors are

L_{i}\in\mathbb{G}(\alpha_{i}+1,n_{i})
and

L_{j}\in\mathbb{G}(\alpha_{j},n_{j})
for
j\not=i
. These
linear spaces are assumed to be generic.
Our hypothesis
\delta_{\alpha}\geq 2
ensures
that the following intersection, defined by
a generic hyperplane
H_{i}\subset\mathbb{P}^{n_{i}}
, is non-empty and
zero-dimensional:

X\,\cap\,\bigl(L_{1}\times\cdots\times(L_{i}\cap H_{i})\times\cdots\times L_{\ell}\bigr)\,\,\,=\,\,\,(X\cap L)\,\cap\,\bigl(\mathbb{P}^{n_{1}}\times\cdots\times H_{i}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{P}^{n_{\ell}}\bigr).

Looking at the right-hand side of the equation above, we conclude that the multidegree
[X\cap L]
contains the nonzero term
\delta_{\alpha}\,T_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots T_{i}^{n_{i}-1}\cdots T_{\ell}^{n_{\ell}}
.
This means that
X\cap L
is a curve.

We next argue that the curve
X\cap L
is irreducible.
Consider the projection
\pi_{1}
from
\mathbb{P}
onto its first factor
\mathbb{P}^{n_{1}}
. Since
X
is polynodal,
the curve
X\cap L
is polynodal. In particular,
\pi_{1}(X\cap L)
is a curve.
Furthermore, our hypotheses ensure that
\dim X\geq\ell\geq 2
.

We now show that
\dim\pi_{1}(X)\geq 2
. Suppose for contradiction that
\dim\pi_{1}(X)<2
. Then
\dim\pi_{1}(X\cap L)\leq\dim\pi_{1}(X)\cap L_{1}\leq 0
because
L_{1}
is generic of dimension at most
n_{1}-1
. This contradicts the fact that
\pi_{1}(X\cap L)
is a curve. Applying the version of Bertini’s theorem stated in
[
11
, Théorème 6.3(4)]
, we have that
X^{\prime}\coloneqq\pi_{1}^{-1}(L_{1})\cap X
is irreducible. We repeat the previous argument, but with
X^{\prime}
instead of
X
.
If
\ell=2
and
X^{\prime}
is a curve, then necessarily
L_{2}=\mathbb{P}^{n_{2}}
and then
X^{\prime}=X\cap L
, hence
X\cap L
is irreducible. Otherwise,
\dim X^{\prime}\geq 2
. Repeating the previous argument with the projection
\pi_{2}
from
\mathbb{P}
to
\mathbb{P}^{n_{2}}
, we obtain
find that
X^{\prime\prime}\coloneqq\pi_{2}^{-1}(L_{2})\cap X^{\prime}=\pi_{2}^{-1}(L_{2})\cap\pi_{1}^{-1}(L_{1})\cap X
is irreducible.
Repeating the argument inductively, we conclude that
X\cap L
is
an irreducible curve.

Let
H
be a generic hyperplane in
\mathbb{P}^{n_{i}}
.
We consider the divisor
H^{\prime}\coloneqq\mathbb{P}^{n_{1}}\times\cdots\times H\times\cdots\times\mathbb{P}^{n_{\ell}}

in
\mathbb{P}
.
The Hurwitz locus
\mathcal{H}^{\alpha}_{X}
parametrizes subvarieties
L\cap H^{\prime}

which intersect
X
non-transversally, but such that
\pi_{S}(X)\cap\pi_{S}(L\cap H^{\prime})
is transverse for all
S\subsetneq[\ell]
. This implies that the hyperplane
H
intersects the projected curve
\pi_{i}(X\cap L)
non-transversally.
The curves
X\cap L
and
\pi_{i}(X\cap L)
are both irreducible of
the same degree
\delta_{\alpha}\geq 2
and the same geometric genus
g_{\alpha+e_{i}}
.
The dual variety of
\pi_{i}(X\cap L)
is an irreducible hypersurface, defined by an irreducible
polynomial in
H
. The degree of this irreducible polynomial is bounded above by

2(g_{\alpha+e_{i}}+\delta_{\alpha}-1)
, and equality holds when
all singularities of
\pi(X\cap L)
are nodes.

In the previous paragraph we assumed that
L
was generic but fixed.
We now vary
L
and we view
L\cap H^{\prime}
as a point in
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
.
The multigraded Hurwitz locus
\mathcal{H}^{\alpha}_{X}
is irreducible because its
restriction to
L
was seen to be irreducible. Moreover,
\mathcal{H}^{\alpha}_{X}
is a hypersurface
in
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
, defined by an irreducible polynomial
{\rm Hu}^{\alpha}_{X}
.
The degree of this hypersurface in the Plücker coordinates
of the
i
th factor
\mathbb{G}(\alpha_{i},n_{i})
equals the
degree of the aforementioned dual hypersurface. That degree is bounded
above by
2(g_{\alpha+e_{i}}+\delta_{\alpha}-1)
.
Equality holds for all
i
if
X
is polynodal.
∎

4
Complete Intersections and Software

In this section we study a generic complete intersection
X
in

\mathbb{P}=\mathbb{P}^{n_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{P}^{n_{\ell}}
. We shall derive an explicit
degree formula for the Hurwitz forms
{\rm Hu}^{\alpha}_{X}
.
This rests on
combinatorial expressions for the multidegree
[X]
and the multisectional genus
g(X)
.
In the second half of this section we present an
implementation in
Macaulay2
,
called
MultiHurwitz.m2
, for computing the numerical invariants studied in this paper.
Our code is posted on
Zenodo

[
14
]
.

We write the degrees of the polynomials that generate
I_{X}
in a matrix
B=(b_{ij})\in\mathbb{N}^{c\times\ell}
.
This means that
X
is the variety defined by
c
generic multihomogeneous polynomials,
where the
i
th polynomial has degree
b_{ij}
in the coordinates of the

j
th projective space
\mathbb{P}^{n_{j}}
. Equivalently, the
i
th divisor class equals

D_{i}=b_{i1}T_{1}+\cdots+b_{i\ell}T_{\ell}
.
The multidegree of
X
is

\hskip 10.00002pt[X]\,\,=\sum_{|\alpha|=c}\delta_{\alpha}\,T^{\alpha}\,\,\,\,=\,\,\,\,D_{1}D_{2}\cdots D_{c}\,\,=\,\,\prod_{i=1}^{c}\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}b_{ij}T_{j}\,\hskip 10.00002pt\in\,\,\,A^{*}(\mathbb{P}).

(17)

What follows is our main theoretical result in Section
4
.
We write
\hat{\delta}_{ij}
for Kronecker’s delta.

Theorem 4.1
.

For any vector
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{\ell}
with
|\alpha|=c
and
\delta_{\alpha}\geq 2
,
the multigraded Hurwitz form
{\rm Hu}^{\alpha}_{X}
of the generic complete intersection
X

is a polynomial of degree
(u_{1},\ldots,u_{\ell})
, where

\hskip 10.00002ptu_{i}\,\,\,=\,\,\,2\delta_{\alpha}\,\,+\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in[\ell]\\
\alpha_{j}+\hat{\delta}_{ij}>0\end{subarray}}\!\delta_{\alpha+e_{i}-e_{j}}\cdot\biggl(-\alpha_{j}-1-\hat{\delta}_{ij}+\sum_{p=1}^{c}b_{pj}\biggr)\,\hskip 10.00002pt\hbox{for all}\,\,\,\,i\in[\ell].

(18)

Proof.

The generic complete intersection
X
is polynodal. Indeed,
consider any generic curve section
C=X\cap(L_{1}\times\cdots\times L_{\ell})
.
Then
C
is polynodal becauses its projections into
\prod_{i\in S}\mathbb{P}^{n_{i}}
for
S\subseteq[\ell]

are curves with at most nodal singularities. Therefore, by Theorem
3.4
, the Hurwitz form

{\rm Hu}_{X}^{\alpha}
has degree
2(g_{\alpha+e_{i}}+\delta_{\alpha}-1)

in the Plücker coordinates of the
i
th Grassmannian

\mathbb{G}(\alpha_{i},n_{i})
. We must show that

2(g_{\alpha+e_{i}}+\delta_{\alpha}-1)
equals
the expression for
u_{i}
given in (
18
).

We shall use the adjunction formula.
Consider a curve in
\mathbb{P}
that is the intersection of

N-1
generic divisors of degrees
D_{1},D_{2},\ldots,D_{N-1}
.
The genus
g
of this curve satisfies

2g-2\,\,=\,\,D_{1}D_{2}\cdots D_{N-1}\cdot(D_{1}+\cdots+D_{N-1}+K_{\mathbb{P}})\,.

(19)

Here
K_{\mathbb{P}}
denotes the canonical divisor of
\mathbb{P}
, which is known to be

K_{\mathbb{P}}\,\,=\,\,\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}(-n_{i}-1)\cdot T_{i}.

Let
D_{1},\,\ldots,\,D_{c}
be the divisors that define
X
, and
let
D_{c+1},\ldots,D_{N-1}
be the divisors that define
the multilinear variety
L_{1}\times\cdots\times L_{\ell}
which intersects
X

in a curve of genus
g_{\alpha+e_{i}}
.
Let
n
denote the vector
(n_{1},\,\ldots,\,n_{\ell})
.
Then
D_{c+1}\cdots D_{N-1}=T^{n-\alpha}/T_{i}
, and therefore

D_{1}\cdots D_{c}D_{c+1}\cdots D_{N-1}\,\,=\,\,[X]\cdot D_{c+1}\cdots D_{N-1}\,\,=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in[\ell]\\
\alpha_{j}+\hat{\delta}_{ij}>0\end{subarray}}\delta_{\alpha+e_{i}-e_{j}}T^{n-e_{j}}.

(20)

The rightmost factor in (
19
) is the divisor class

D_{1}+\cdots+D_{N-1}+K_{\mathbb{P}}\,\,\,=\,\,\sum_{k=1}^{\ell}\left(-\alpha_{k}-\hat{\delta}_{ik}-1+\sum_{p=1}^{c}b_{pk}\right)T_{k}.

(21)

We multiply (
20
) with (
21
). Because we are working in the Chow ring
A^{*}(\mathbb{P}),
the
j
th and
k
th terms in each sum multiply to zero unless
j=k.
The
resulting simplified expression is

2g_{\alpha+e_{i}}-2\,\,=\,\,\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\delta_{\alpha+e_{i}-e_{j}}\biggl(-\alpha_{j}-1-\hat{\delta}_{ij}+\sum_{p=1}^{c}b_{pj}\biggr).

By adding
2\delta_{\alpha}
to this expression, we obtain the
formula for
u_{i}
that is asserted in (
18
).
∎

Example 4.2

(

be the surface in

5
Polytopes and Nash Equilibria

This section is motivated by game theory, namely by the
study of totally mixed Nash equilibria.
These are defined by semi-mixed systems of multilinear equations
whose parameters are the payoff tables of the players
[
16
, Chapter 6]
.
The Nash discriminant
[
1
]
is a polynomial
in the payoff tables which vanishes when two equilibria come together.
We will show that these can be interpreted as
multigraded Hurwitz forms for a certain class of toric varieties.

Before getting to Nash equilibria, we take a step back
and revisit the mixed discriminants in Examples
2.5
and
2.6
.
Fix
d\geq\ell
and subsets
A_{1},\ldots,A_{\ell}
that span
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, and set

P_{i}={\rm conv}(A_{i})
for
i\in[\ell]
. Let
X
be the
associated
d
-dimensional toric variety in
\mathbb{P}=\mathbb{P}^{n_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{P}^{n_{\ell}}
.
Here we abbreviate
n_{i}=|A_{i}|-1
for
i\in[\ell]
.
The codimension of
X
is
c=n_{1}+\cdots+n_{\ell}-d
.

With the tuple of polytopes
P_{1},\ldots,P_{\ell}
one associates the following
volume polynomial

V(T)\,\,=\,\,{\rm vol}\bigl(T_{1}P_{1}+T_{2}P_{2}+\cdots+T_{\ell}P_{\ell}\bigr)\,\,\,=\,\,\,\sum_{|\gamma|=d}\mu_{\gamma}\,T^{\gamma}.

(22)

Here “vol” is the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
It is known that (
22
) is a Lorentzian polynomial,
which means that it has desirable combinatorial properties
[
9
]
.
According to the following formula from
[
9
, Section 2.1]
,
the coefficients of the volume polynomial are mixed volumes:

MV(P_{i_{1}},P_{i_{2}},\ldots,P_{i_{d}})\,\,=\,\,\frac{\partial}{\partial T_{i_{1}}}\frac{\partial}{\partial T_{i_{2}}}\cdots\frac{\partial}{\partial T_{i_{d}}}V(T)\hskip 20.00003pt\hbox{for any}\,\,i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{d}\,\in\,[\ell].

By Bernstein’s Theorem, this mixed volume is the number of
solutions to a square polynomial system with Newton polytopes

P_{i_{1}},P_{i_{2}},\ldots,P_{i_{d}}
. And, that number is precisely
the coefficient complementary to
T_{i_{1}}T_{i_{2}}\cdots T_{i_{d}}
in the
multidegree of the toric variety
X
associated with the subsets
A_{1},A_{2},\ldots,A_{\ell}
of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.
These considerations directly imply the following result.

Lemma 5.1
.

The multidegree of the toric variety
X\subset\mathbb{P}
is read off from (
22
) as follows:

[X]\,\,\,=\,\,\,\sum_{|\gamma|=d}\mu_{\gamma}\,\gamma_{1}!\,\gamma_{2}!\,\cdots\,\gamma_{\ell}!\cdot T_{1}^{n_{1}-\gamma_{1}}T_{2}^{n_{2}-\gamma_{2}}\,\cdots\,T_{\ell}^{n_{\ell}-\gamma_{\ell}}.

Hence, in the notation of (
5
), the coefficients in the multidegree of
X
are

\delta_{\alpha}\,\,=\,\,\mu_{n_{1}-\alpha_{1},\ldots,n_{\ell}-\alpha_{\ell}}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}(n_{i}-\alpha_{i})!.

We next determine
the multisectional genus
g(X)
. This rests on Khovanskii’s theorem
[
12
, Theorem 1]
.
Namely, we compute each coefficient
g_{\beta}

by counting lattice points in Newton polytopes.
To state the theorem, we fix
\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{\ell}
with
|\beta|=c+1
.
We need to find the genus
g_{\beta}
of the curve

\,C=X\,\cap\,(L_{1}\times\cdots\times L_{\ell})
,
where
L_{i}
is generic in
\mathbb{G}(\beta_{i},n_{i})
for
i\in[\ell]
.
The subspace
L_{i}
is defined by
n_{i}-\beta_{i}
linear equations
on the
i
th factor
\mathbb{P}^{n_{i}}
, and these pull back to
n_{i}-\beta_{i}

Laurent polynomial equations in
d
variables with Newton polytope
P_{i}
.
For any vector
\mu\in\mathbb{N}^{\ell}
with
\mu\leq\beta

in the coordinatewise order, we consider the Minkowski sum

P_{\mu}\,=\,\mu_{1}P_{1}+\mu_{2}P_{2}+\cdots+\mu_{\ell}P_{\ell}\hskip 20.00003pt\subset\,\,\mathbb{R}^{d}.

We compute (
12
) by counting
the interior lattice points in each of these polytopes.

Theorem 5.2

(Khovanskii)
.

The
coefficients of the multisectional genus
g(X)
are

g_{\beta}\,\,\,=\,\,\,\sum_{\gamma\leq\beta}\,(-1)^{|\beta-\gamma|}\cdot\bigl|\,{\rm int}(P_{\gamma})\,\cap\,\mathbb{Z}^{d}\,\bigr|.

We illustrate Lemma
5.1
and Theorem
5.2
for our running example.

Example 5.3

(Toric
4
-fold)
.

Here
d=4
,
\ell=2
, and the supports are
A_{1}=\{e_{1}+e_{2},e_{3},e_{4},0\}
and
A_{2}=\{e_{3}+e_{4},e_{1},e_{2},0\}
.
So,
P_{1},P_{2}
are tetrahedra in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
. The volume polynomial equals

V(T)\,\,=\,\,{\rm vol}(T_{1}P_{1}+T_{2}P_{2})\,\,\,=\,\,\,\frac{1}{3}T_{1}^{3}T_{2}\,+\,T_{1}^{2}T_{2}^{2}\,+\,\frac{1}{3}T_{1}T_{2}^{3}.

Lemma
5.1
yields
[X]=2T_{1}^{2}+4T_{1}T_{2}+2T_{2}^{2}
.
Theorem
5.2
implies that
the multisectional genus is
g(X)=T_{1}^{2}T_{2}+T_{1}T_{2}^{2}
.
Indeed, the
4
-polytope

P_{(2,1)}=2P_{1}+P_{2}
has precisely one interior lattice point, while none
of the polytopes
P_{i}+P_{j}
has an interior lattice point.
Hence the system of equations with Newton polytopes
P_{1},P_{1},P_{2}

defines a curve of genus one.
∎

We now turn to game theory, following
[

]
.
First consider
3
players with binary choices.

Example 5.4
.

The
Nash equilibrium variety
of a

game is the locus of points
([x_{0}:x_{1}],[y_{0}:y_{1}],[z_{0}:z_{1}])\in\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
that satisfy the three bilinear equations

\displaystyle\begin{split}a_{00}y_{0}z_{0}+a_{01}y_{0}z_{1}+a_{10}y_{1}z_{0}+a_{11}y_{1}z_{1}&=0,\\

c_{00}x_{0}y_{0}+c_{01}x_{0}y_{1}+c_{10}x_{1}y_{0}+c_{11}x_{1}y_{1}&=0.\end{split}

(23)

This system has two solutions when the matrices
A=(a_{ij})
,
B=(b_{ij})
,
C=(c_{ij})
are generic.
The Nash discriminant, which identifies double roots,
is the symmetric
6\times 6

\,A^{T}&0&C\,\\
\,B^{T}&C^{T}&0\,\end{bmatrix}.

(24)

The toric variety
X
is
the mixed Segre embedding of
\mathbb{P}^{1}\!\times\!\mathbb{P}^{1}\!\times\!\mathbb{P}^{1}
into

\mathbb{P}^{3}\!\times\!\mathbb{P}^{3}\!\times\!\mathbb{P}^{3}
, given by

\bigl([x_{0}:x_{1}],[y_{0}:y_{1}],[z_{0}:z_{1}]\bigr)\,\,\mapsto\,\,\bigl([y_{0}z_{0}:\cdots:y_{1}z_{1}],[x_{0}z_{0}:\cdots:x_{1}z_{1}],[x_{0}y_{0}:\cdots:x_{1}y_{1}]\bigr)\,.

Here
d=\ell=3
,
\,P_{1},P_{2},P_{3}
are facets of the cube
[0,1]^{3}
, and

V(T)=(T_{1}+T_{2})(T_{1}+T_{3})(T_{2}+T_{3})
.
By applying Lemma
5.1
, we find that the multidegree of the toric threefold

equals

[X]\,\,=\,\,2\,T_{1}^{3}T_{2}^{2}T_{3}+2\,T_{1}^{3}T_{2}T_{3}^{2}+2\,T_{1}^{2}T_{2}^{3}T_{3}+{\bf 2}\,T_{1}^{2}T_{2}^{2}T_{3}^{2}+2\,T_{1}^{2}T_{2}T_{3}^{3}+2\,T_{1}T_{2}^{3}T_{3}^{2}+2\,T_{1}T_{2}^{2}T_{3}^{3}.

The Hurwitz form in (
24
) has degree
(2,2,2)
because
the multisectional genus equals

g(X)\,\,=\,\,-1\cdot(T_{1}^{3}T_{2}^{3}T_{3}+T_{1}^{3}T_{2}T_{3}^{3}+T_{1}T_{2}^{3}T_{3}^{3})\,+\,0\cdot(T_{1}^{3}T_{2}^{2}T_{3}^{2}+T_{1}^{2}T_{2}^{3}T_{3}^{2}+T_{1}^{2}T_{2}^{2}T_{3}^{3}).

The coefficient
0
of
T_{1}^{3}T_{2}^{2}T_{3}^{2}
arises because
P_{2}+P_{3}
is

3
-dimensional but has no interior lattice point. The coefficient
-1
in front of

T_{1}^{3}T_{2}^{3}T_{3}
indicates that
P_{3}+P_{3}
is not full-dimensional.
∎

We now turn to games with
\ell
players of format

(k_{1}+1)\times\cdots\times(k_{\ell}+1)
. We assume
1\leq k_{1}\leq\cdots\leq k_{\ell}
.
The Nash discriminant variety was defined in
[
1
, Section 3]
.
It has codimension one if
k_{\ell}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\ell-1}k_{i}
by
[
1
, Theorem 3.7]
, but there may be
extraneous components of lower dimensions.
This was shown in
[
1
, Example 3.27]
for
games of boundary format
2\times 2\times 3
.

To model the general case using polytopes, we set
d=k_{1}+k_{2}+\cdots+k_{\ell}
,
and we consider the product of simplices
\Delta:=\Delta_{k_{1}}\times\cdots\times\Delta_{k_{\ell}}
,
where
\Delta_{k_{i}}={\rm conv}\{0,e_{1},\ldots,e_{k_{i}}\}
denotes the unit simplex.
We fix the following
\ell
distinguished facets of the
d
-dimensional polytope
\Delta
:

A_{i}\,\,:=\,\,\Delta_{k_{1}}\times\cdots\times\Delta_{k_{i-1}}\times\{0\}\times\Delta_{k_{i+1}}\times\cdots\times\Delta_{k_{\ell}}\hskip 20.00003pt{\rm for}\,\,\,i\in[\ell].

See
[
16
, Section 6.4]
. These polytopes define a toric variety
X

in
\mathbb{P}=\mathbb{P}^{n_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{P}^{n_{\ell}}
, where

\,n_{i}\,\,=\,\,(k_{1}+1)\cdots(k_{i-1}+1)(k_{i+1}+1)\cdots(k_{\ell}+1)\,-\,1\hskip 20.00003pt{\rm for}\,\,\,i\in[\ell].

Just like in Example
5.4
, the variety
X
is an embedding of the

\mathbb{P}^{k_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{P}^{k_{\ell}}
.
The volume polynomial equals

V(T)=\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}(\sum_{j\not=i}T_{j})^{k_{i}}
,
and from this we can read off the multidegree
[X]
.
Set
\alpha=(n_{1}-k_{1},n_{2}-k_{2},\ldots,n_{\ell}-k_{\ell})
.
The coefficient
\delta_{\alpha}
in
[X]
is the
number of totally mixed Nash equilibria.
A combinatorial rule for
\delta_{\alpha}
is given in
[
16
, Theorem 6.8]
.
We seek conditions on the payoff tables
that force two equilibria to come together.

In the following result, the upper bound of Theorem
3.4

is made more explicit for
Nash equilibria.
Our formula is computed in the Chow ring of
\Sigma\coloneqq\mathbb{P}^{k_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{P}^{k_{\ell}}
, that is

A^{*}(\Sigma)\,\,=\,\,\mathbb{Z}[H_{1},H_{2},\ldots,H_{\ell}]/\bigl\langle\,H_{1}^{k_{1}+1},\,H_{2}^{k_{2}+1},\,\ldots,\,H_{\ell}^{k_{\ell}+1}\bigr\rangle\,.

As is customary in intersection theory,
the integral sign
\int_{\Sigma}
will be used to mean that we extract the coefficient of
H_{1}^{k_{1}}H_{2}^{k_{2}}\cdots H_{\ell}^{k_{\ell}}

in the argument. In particular, we have the identity

\delta_{\alpha}\,\,=\,\,\int_{\Sigma}H\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\text{where}\,\,\,H\coloneqq\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}\hat{H}_{i}^{k_{i}}\ \,\,\text{and}\,\,\,\hat{H}_{i}\coloneqq\sum_{j\neq i}H_{j}\,\,\,\text{for all $i\in[\ell]$.}

(25)

Here, the relevant degree is
\alpha=(n_{1}-k_{1},n_{2}-k_{2},\ldots,n_{\ell}-k_{\ell})
.

Proposition 5.5
.

If
\,\mathrm{Hu}_{X}^{\alpha}
is a hypersurface, then its degree
(u_{1},\ldots,u_{\ell})
satisfies

u_{i}\,\,\,\leq\,\,\int_{\Sigma}\,\,2\,H+\frac{H}{\hat{H}_{i}}\left(-\hat{H}_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\left[k_{j}\hat{H}_{j}-(k_{j}+1)H_{j}\right]\right)\,.

(26)

The upper bound in (
26
) is an equality provided
the toric variety
X
is polynodal.

We conjecture that
X
is polynodal, and
\mathrm{Hu}_{X}^{\alpha}
is a hypersurface, so
equality holds in (
26
).

Proof.

Assume that
\mathrm{Hu}_{X}^{\alpha}
is a hypersurface. By Theorem
3.4
, its degree

(u_{1},\ldots,u_{\ell})
satisfies

u_{i}\,\,\leq\,\,2(\delta_{\alpha}+g_{\alpha+e_{i}}-1)\,.

(27)

The coefficient
\delta_{\alpha}
in
[X]
satisfies (
25
).
To determine the upper bound explicitly, it remains to compute the coefficient
g_{\alpha+e_{i}}
in the multisectional genus
g(X)
for all
i\in[\ell]
. This is the genus of the curve
X\cap(L_{1}\times\cdots\times L_{\ell})
, where
\dim(L_{j})=\alpha_{j}+\hat{\delta}_{ij}
. Viewing
X
as a Segre embedding of
\Sigma
, we pull back
L_{1}\times\cdots\times L_{\ell}
to obtain the generic complete intersection
Z_{1}\times\cdots\times Z_{\ell}
in
\Sigma
. Each
Z_{j}
is cut out by
k_{j}-\hat{\delta}_{ij}
generic multihomogeneous polynomials of degrees
(1,\ldots,1)-e_{j}
in the
\ell
sets of variables. Overall we are considering a generic complete intersection of
k-1\coloneqq k_{1}+\cdots+k_{\ell}-1
divisors in
\Sigma
. We denote these by

D_{1},\ldots,D_{k-1}
. The genus
g_{\alpha+e_{i}}

can thus be derived using the adjunction formula as in Theorem
4.1
, namely

2\,g_{\alpha+e_{i}}-2\,\,=\,\,\int_{\Sigma}D_{1}\cdots D_{k-1}\left(D_{1}+\cdots+D_{k-1}+K_{\Sigma}\right).

(28)

Here
K_{\Sigma}
is the canonical divisor of
\Sigma
.
We next substitute the following relations into
(
28
):

\displaystyle D_{1}\cdots D_{k-1}\,=\,\frac{H}{\hat{H}_{i}}\,,\hskip 10.00002ptD_{1}+\cdots+D_{k-1}\,=\,-\hat{H}_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}k_{j}\hat{H}_{j}\,,\hskip 10.00002ptK_{\Sigma}\,=\,-\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}(k_{j}+1)H_{j}.

By applying (
27
), we obtain the upper bound stated in (
26
).
∎

6
Grassmannians and Particle Physics

In this section we discuss an application of multigraded Hurwitz forms
to Landau analysis for Feynman integrals in particle physics
[
7
,
8
]
.
We begin by describing the general setting.
We fix
n_{1}=n_{2}=\cdots=n_{\ell}=5
, so our ambient product of projective spaces is

\mathbb{P}=(\mathbb{P}^{5})^{\ell}
. The six coordinates on the
i
th factor
\mathbb{P}^{5}
are
denoted
x^{(i)}=(x^{(i)}_{12},x^{(i)}_{13},x^{(i)}_{14},x^{(i)}_{23},x^{(i)}_{24},x^{(i)}_{34})
.
In each factor we consider the Grassmannian of lines in
\mathbb{P}^{3}
in its Plücker embedding. We obtain

{\mathbb{G}}(1,3)^{\ell}\,\,=\,\,\bigl\{\,(x^{(1)},\,x^{(2)},\,\ldots,\,x^{(\ell)})\in\mathbb{P}\,:\,x^{(i)}_{12}x^{(i)}_{34}-x^{(i)}_{13}x^{(i)}_{24}+x^{(i)}_{14}x^{(i)}_{23}\,=\,0\,\,\,\,\hbox{for}\,\,i\in[\ell]\,\bigr\}.

(29)

This is a complete intersection
with degree vectors
2e_{1},2e_{2},\ldots,2e_{\ell}
, so its
multidegree equals

\bigl[\mathbb{G}(1,3)^{\ell}\bigr]\,\,=\,\,2^{\ell}\,T_{1}T_{2}\cdots T_{\ell}.

We shall view
\mathbb{G}(1,3)^{\ell}
as a generic complete intersection, given by a quadric in each factor.

Our next ingredient is a graph
G
with vertex set
[\ell]=\{1,2,\ldots,\ell\}
.
We identify
G
with its set of edges, which is a subset of
\binom{[\ell]}{2}
.
For each edge
ij
of
G
we consider the bilinear form

B_{ij}(x)\,\,:=\,\,x^{(i)}_{12}x^{(j)}_{34}-x^{(i)}_{13}x^{(j)}_{24}+x^{(i)}_{14}x^{(j)}_{23}+x^{(i)}_{23}x^{(j)}_{14}-x^{(i)}_{24}x^{(j)}_{13}+x^{(i)}_{34}x^{(j)}_{12}.

(30)

The equation
B_{ij}(x)=0

holds if and only if the
i
th line intersects the
j
th line in
\mathbb{P}^{3}
.

The graph
G
specifies the incidence variety

V_{G}\,\,=\,\,\bigl\{\,x\in\mathbb{G}(1,3)^{\ell}\,:\,B_{ij}(x)=0\,\,\,\,{\rm for}\,\,\,ij\in G\,\bigr\}.

(31)

This variety was introduced in
[
7
]
and is applied to
Feynman integrals in
[
8
]
. Its points are
configurations of
\ell
lines in
\mathbb{P}^{3}
which are required to satisfy
pairwise incidence relations.

Objects of main interest in
[
8
]
are the
LS discriminants
of the graph
G
.
The acronym LS refers to the
leading singularities
of the integrand in a Feynman integral.
Analyzing such singularities is a key point of Landau analysis.
The connection to the present work arises because the LS discriminants are the multigraded Hurwitz forms of the incidence variety
V_{G}
.

Example 6.1

(
\ell=1
)
.

Here
G
is the graph with one vertex and no edge,
and
V_{G}
is simply the Grassmannian
\mathbb{G}(1,3)
in
\mathbb{P}^{5}
.
We are thus in the classical setting of
[
17
]
,
with only one projective space. We have
c=1
,
the sectional genus is
g=0
, and we set
x_{jk}=x^{(1)}_{jk}
.
The Hurwitz form of
\mathbb{G}(1,3)
is a hypersurface in
\mathbb{G}(1,5)
.
Its points are the lines
\mathfrak{L}
in
\mathbb{P}^{5}
for which the intersection
\mathbb{G}(1,3)\cap\mathfrak{L}
is
a double point. We represent
\mathfrak{L}
as the row space of a matrix

\begin{bmatrix}u_{12}&u_{13}&u_{14}&u_{23}&u_{24}&u_{34}\\
v_{12}&v_{13}&v_{14}&v_{23}&v_{24}&v_{34}\end{bmatrix}.

(32)

Writing
[ij]
for the
2\times 2
minors of the matrix in (
32
), the Hurwitz form of
\mathbb{G}(1,3)
equals

{\rm Hu}_{\mathbb{G}(1,3)}^{(4)}\,=\,[16]^{2}+[25]^{2}+[34]^{2}+2[16][25]-2[16][34]+2[25][34]+4[14][36]-4[15][26]-4[24][35].

In our physics application, the line
\mathfrak{L}
is the intersection of four Schubert hyperplanes in
\mathbb{P}^{5}

\begin{matrix}a^{(1)}_{34}x_{12}-a^{(1)}_{24}x_{13}+a^{(1)}_{23}x_{14}+a^{(1)}_{14}x_{23}-a^{(1)}_{13}x_{24}+a^{(1)}_{12}x_{34}&=&0,\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\

a^{(4)}_{34}x_{12}-a^{(4)}_{24}x_{13}+a^{(4)}_{23}x_{14}+a^{(4)}_{14}x_{23}-a^{(4)}_{13}x_{24}+a^{(4)}_{12}x_{34}&=&0.\end{matrix}

(33)

The
i
th hyperplane being Schubert means that its coefficient vector

(a^{(i)}_{12},a^{(i)}_{13},\ldots,a^{(i)}_{34})
lies in the Grassmannian
\mathbb{G}(1,3)
.
We write
{\rm Hu}_{\mathbb{G}(1,3)}^{1}
in the

24
Plücker coordinates
a^{(i)}_{jk}
by replacing

[ij]
with the signed
4\times 4
minor of the coefficient matrix in (
33
) indexed by
[6]\backslash\{i,j\}
.
What results is the most basic discriminant in Schubert calculus.
Given four lines in
\mathbb{P}^{3}
, there are two lines incident to all four.
Our Hurwitz form vanishes when these two lines coincide.
∎

The geometry studied in

[

,

\ell=1
to

Example 6.2

(
\ell=2
)
.

Here
G
is the graph with two vertices and one edge.
The variety
V_{G}
is irreducible. It is defined by one bilinear equation
(
30
) in
\mathbb{G}(1,3)\times\mathbb{G}(1,3)
.

V_{G}
is a complete intersection of
type
(2,0),(0,2),(1,1)
in
\mathbb{P}^{5}\times\mathbb{P}^{5}
.
So, its codimension equals
c=3
.

To proceed, we replace
V_{G}
with
a generic complete intersection
in
\mathbb{P}^{5}\times\mathbb{P}^{5}
, of the same type

(2,0),(0,2),(1,1)
, to be denoted

.

The multigraded Hurwitz form
{\rm Hu}^{(1,2)}_{U_{G}}
is an irreducible polynomial
of degree
(8,4)
in the Plücker coordinates of

and a plane

The intersection

consists of four points, and

We obtain the Hurwitz form of our incidence variety

by degenerating the

{\rm Hu}^{(1,2)}_{U_{G}}
of the generic

. The

decomposes into two irreducible factors, of degrees
(4,4)
and
(4,0)
.

{\rm Hu}^{(1,2)}_{V_{G}}
.
The second is an extraneous factor, namely it is the

6.1
, taken
in the left factor of
\mathbb{G}(1,5)\times\mathbb{G}(2,5)
.

In conclusion, the variety
V_{G}
has two multigraded Hurwitz forms

{\rm Hu}^{(1,2)}_{V_{G}}
and
{\rm Hu}^{(2,1)}_{V_{G}}
. Both of
these are irreducible polynomials of degree
(4,4)
in pairs of
Plücker coordinates. For the application of
[
8
]
,
these are replaced by expressions in the
42
coefficients

that specify
7=4+3
Schubert hyperplanes in
\mathbb{P}^{5}
,

\mathbb{G}(1,5)
, as in (

\mathbb{G}(2,5)
.
∎

for

is a complete intersection of type

for the generic complete intersection

in

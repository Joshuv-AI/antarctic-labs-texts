Shell model description of the N = 82 N=82 isotonic chain with a new effective interaction
Y. X. Yu, Q. Y. Chen, Chong Qi, G. J. Fu

Abstract
In this work, we present a systematic study of low-lying states and electromagnetic properties of the semi-magic N = 82 N=82 isotonic chain with proton number Z = 51 Z=51 -77, using the full configuration interaction shell model with a newly developed high-quality effective interaction. The calculations are performed in a large model space that includes all proton orbitals between Z = 50 Z=50 and 82: 0 ​ g 7 / 2 0g_{7/2} , 1 ​ d 5 / 2 1d_{5/2} , 1 ​ d 3 / 2 1d_{3/2} , 2 ​ s 1 / 2 2s_{1/2} , and 0 ​ h 11 / 2 0h_{11/2} . The effective interaction is derived through the principal component analysis approach, starting from 160 two-body matrix elements and 5 single-particle energies and considering up to 30 degrees of freedom. Those are optimized by fitting to 204 available experimental energy levels. The resulting root-mean-square deviation is as low as 102 keV. The new interaction successfully reproduces the binding energies, low-lying spectra, electric quadrupole transition probabilities B ⁡ ( E ​ 2 ) B(E2) , and magnetic dipole moments across both even-even and odd-mass isotones, with consistent agreement with experimental values. The nuclear structure of low-lying states is analyzed in detail. Additionally, predictions are made for several more proton-rich nuclei beyond current experimental reach, including Ta 155 {}^{155}\mathrm{Ta} , W 156 {}^{156}\mathrm{W} , Re 157 {}^{157}\mathrm{Re} , Os 158 {}^{158}\mathrm{Os} , and Ir 159 {}^{159}\mathrm{Ir} .



I
INTRODUCTION

The full-configuration-interaction shell model, more commonly known as the nuclear shell model, is one of the most successful and versatile microscopic approaches for studying atomic nuclei.
Large-scale calculations within a defined model space, using either realistic (e.g., renormalized
G
-matrix or in-medium similarity renormalization group methods) or phenomenological [e.g., optimized two-body matrix elements (TBMEs) or monopole terms] effective interactions, have yielded reliable and predictive results
Talmi 1993
;
Talmi 2005
;
Brown 2001
;
Caurier et al. 2005
;
Liu and Yuan 2023
.
In the phenomenological case, excellent agreement with experiment can often be achieved with minimal modifications to realistic interactions, offering a practical tool for both theoretical studies and experimental interpretation.
For nuclei in the light- and medium-mass regions, shell model calculations have proven effective in explaining the properties of low-lying states. For instance, the empirical Hamiltonians USDA and USDB accurately reproduce low-lying energy levels, binding energies, and electromagnetic properties for nuclei in the
sd
shell
Brown and Richter 2006
. Similarly, the GXPF1
Honma et al. 2002a
and KB3G
Poves et al. 2001
interactions have been highly successful in describing a wide range of properties in
pf
-shell nuclei. The JUN45 interaction, derived from the realistic Bonn-C nuclear force and empirically optimized to match experimental data, has been widely used in
f_{5}pg_{9}
-shell calculations and effectively reproduces excitation energies and magnetic moments of nuclei up to
N=Z=50
, particularly those involving the
g_{9/2}
orbital
Honma et al. 2009
.

There has been significant recent experimental and theoretical interest in the structure of nuclei with proton or neutron numbers between 50 and 82, corresponding to the
g_{7}dsh_{11}
shell
Ortner et al. 2024
;
Cederlöf et al. 2023
;
Storbacka and Qi 2024
;
Doncel et al. 2017
;
Cheng et al. 2016
;
Doncel et al. 2015
;
Jiang et al. 2013
;
Bäck et al. 2013
;
Procter et al. 2013
;
Qi and Xu 2012
;
Qi 2016
;
Faestermann et al. 1979
;
Bhoy and Srivastava 2022
.
Particular attention has been devoted to the extended chains of Sn and Te isotopes, as well as the
N=80
and 82 isotonic chains.
These studies focus on the robustness of the
Z=50
and
N=82
shell closures and the subshell effects at
Z=64
(or
N=64
), which arise due to the energy gap separating the
0g_{7/2},1d_{5/2}
orbitals from the
1d_{3/2},2s_{1/2},0h_{11/2}
orbitals.
Increasing experimental and theoretical attention has also been directed toward proton-rich nuclei near or beyond the drip line, such as
{}^{155}\mathrm{Ta}
and
{}^{150}\mathrm{Yb}
.
Reliable theoretical predictions are essential for understanding nuclear structure in this region and provide valuable guidance for future experimental studies.
Despite the exponential increase in model space dimensions, large-scale shell model calculations remain computationally feasible for nuclei near neutron or proton major shell closures.

Several effective interactions have been developed for the
N=82
isotonic chain, where the valence protons are active in the
g_{7}dsh_{11}
shell.
Notable examples include the JJ56PNA
Brown and Rae 2014
and KH5082
Kuo and Herling 1971
;
Warburton and Brown 1991
interactions.
The KH5082 interaction, developed by Kuo, Herling
et

al.
, starts from a free nucleon-nucleon potential and incorporates renormalizations to account for the contributions from outside model space. The CW5082 interaction
Chou and Warburton 1992
was subsequently derived from KH5082 by replacing the proton TBMEs with those from the effective interaction proposed by Kruse and Wildenthal
Kruse and Wildenthal 1982
.
The region from
Z=52
to 63 has been explored using interactions based on meson-exchange potential models
Holt et al. 1997
;
Suhonen et al. 1998
. Previous work has also investigated low-spin excitation energies in the
N=82
isotonic chain using time-dependent degenerate linked-diagram perturbation theory
Coraggio et al. 2009
.

In this work, we present a detailed and systematic investigation of the low-lying states in the
N=82
isotones through an optimization of the effective Hamiltonian using the principal component analysis (PCA) approach.
A preliminary version of the monopole and multipole optimized effective interaction was introduced in Ref.
Chen et al. 2024
, which reasonably reproduces the systematic trend of the
2^{+}
excitation energies.
In the present work, we achieve excellent agreement with experimental data and resolve previous systematic discrepancies, including consistent overestimations of the
3^{-}
excitation energies and deviations in the predicted ground-state spins and parities of the heavier odd-
A
isotones.
With the newly optimized interaction, we systematically investigate binding energies, low-lying spectra, electric quadrupole reduced transition probabilities
B(E2)
, and magnetic dipole moments
\mu
.
We further analyze the dominant configurations that characterize the structure of these low-lying states.
The paper is organized as follows. Sec. II introduces the model space and the PCA-based optimization of the effective Hamiltonian.
Sec. III presents the calculated results, compares them with available experimental data, and discusses the underlying structures of the low-lying states.
Finally, Sec. IV briefly summaries the results and provides an outlook.

II
Theoretical framework

To describe the
N=82
isotones, we consider valence protons in the
g_{7}dsh_{11}
shell, which includes the
0g_{7/2}
,
1d_{5/2}
,
1d_{3/2}
,
2s_{1/2}
, and
0h_{11/2}
orbitals, above the doubly magic nucleus
132
Sn taken as the inert core.
The low-lying states are calculated using the shell model code BIGSTICK
Johnson et al. 2013
;
Johnson et al. 2018
.
The largest
M
-scheme dimension encountered in this work is for
148
Dy, reaching
1.6\times 10^{7}
for even-parity states.
This model space enables minimal modifications of the effective Hamiltonian within a manageable computational budget.

The shell model Hamiltonian is given by

\displaystyle\hat{H}=\sum_{a}\varepsilon_{a}\hat{n}_{a}+

\displaystyle\sum_{a\leq b,c\leq d}\sum_{JT}V_{JT}(abcd)

(1)

\displaystyle\times\sum_{MT_{z}}\hat{A}^{(JT)\dagger}_{MT_{z}}(ab)\hat{A}^{(JT)}_{MT_{z}}(cd),

where
\hat{n}_{a}
is the single-particle number operator for the orbit
a
with quantum numbers
(n_{a},l_{a},j_{a})
, and

\displaystyle\hat{A}^{(JT)\dagger}_{MT_{z}}(ab)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\delta_{ab}}}(\hat{a}_{a}^{\dagger}\times\hat{a}_{b}^{\dagger})^{(JT)}_{MT_{z}}

(2)

is a normalized two-particle creation operator coupled to good spin and isospin.
In this work, the Hamiltonian involves 5 proton single-particle energies (SPEs)
\varepsilon_{a}
and 160 proton-proton TBMEs
V_{JT}(abcd)
with
T=1
.
We apply the standard mass scaling factor
(A/134)^{-1/3}
to the TBMEs, which is frequently adopted in shell model calculations.
The effect of the Coulomb interaction is included in the Hamiltonian.
For compact notation, the Hamiltonian in Eq. (
1
) can be rewritten in the following form:

\hat{H}=\sum_{i=1}^{t}a_{i}\hat{O}_{i},

(3)

where
\hat{O}_{i}
represents either the particle number operator
\hat{n}_{a}
or the two-body operator
\hat{A}^{(JT)\dagger}_{MT_{z}}(ab)\hat{A}^{(JT)}_{MT_{z}}(cd)
, and the coefficients
\vec{a}
are the corresponding parameters, i.e.,
\varepsilon_{a}
and
V_{JT}(abcd)
.

We optimize the parameters
\vec{a}
using the least square fitting, combined with a PCA method
Pearson 1901
;
Hotelling 1933
to truncate the parameter space.
We use
N
to denote the number of data points to be fitted, and
t
the total number of input parameters.
The experimental value of the
k
-th data is denoted by
y^{k}
, and the corresponding theoretical formula by
f^{k}=f(k;\vec{a})
.
At the
s
-th iteration, the parameters are given by
\vec{a}^{(s)}
.
We minimize the weighted least-square loss function:

\displaystyle\nabla_{\Delta\vec{a}}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\omega_{k}(y^{k}-f^{k})^{2}=0,

(4)

\displaystyle f^{k}\approx f^{k(s)}+\vec{g}^{k(s)}\cdot\Delta\vec{a},

(5)

where the gradient
\vec{g}^{k(s)}
is defined by
g^{k(s)}_{i}\equiv\partial f^{k}/\partial a_{i}|_{\vec{a}^{(s)}}
,

\Delta\vec{a}
represents the change of the parameters,
and
\omega_{k}
is the weight associated with the
k
-th data point.
All weights are set to 1, except for the binding energies of
{}^{152}\textrm{Yb}
and
{}^{153}\textrm{Lu}
, which are both down-weighted to
0.5
due to their large experimental uncertainties.
Combining Eqs. (
4
) and (
5
) yields the linear equations:

\displaystyle A\Delta\vec{a}=\vec{b},

(6)

with

\displaystyle A_{ij}=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\omega_{k}g^{k(s)}_{i}g^{k(s)}_{j},

(7)

\displaystyle\vec{b}=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\omega_{k}(y^{k}-f^{k(s)})\vec{g}^{k(s)}.

(8)

The standard least square fitting would typically stop at this stage. But to implement the PCA, we proceed by diagonalizing the error matrix
D=A^{-1}
:

{\rm diag}(d_{1},d_{2},\dots,d_{t})=PDP^{T},

(9)

where the eigenvalues
d_{i}
are sorted in ascending order.
Using the transformation
P
, Eq. (
6
) becomes

\displaystyle\Delta c_{i}=d_{i}e_{i},

(10)

where
\Delta\vec{c}\equiv P\Delta\vec{a}
, and
\vec{e}\equiv P\vec{b}
.
The components
\Delta\vec{c}
represent the change along the principal direction of the parameter space, referred to as the principal parameters.
These principal parameters are linear combinations of the original parameters
\vec{a}
and are selected according to their sensitivity in reducing the residuals of the fit.
The most important variations are associated with the components having the smallest uncertainties, i.e., the principal directions corresponding to the smallest eigenvalues of
D
.
The PCA is applied by retaining only the
q
components associated with the smallest eigenvalues, where
q
defines the dimension of the truncated parameter space.
This truncation is implemented as follows:

\Delta c^{\prime}_{i}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}d_{i}e_{i},&\text{if}~~i\leqslant q,\\
0,&\text{otherwise},\end{array}\right.

(11)

where
\Delta c^{\prime}_{i}
represents the truncated changes of the principal parameters.
The components with larger
d_{i}
are neglected, ensuring the fit focuses on the most important parameters.
The parameters
\vec{a}
are then updated using

\vec{a}^{(s+1)}=\vec{a}^{(s)}+P^{T}\Delta\vec{c}^{\prime}.

(12)

The above procedures are repeated iteratively until convergence is achieved.
Similar parameter space truncation approaches have been widely used to optimize shell model interactions
Arima et al. 1968
;
Richter et al. 1991
;
Honma et al. 2002b
;
Honma et al. 2002a
;
Lisetskiy et al. 2004
;
Brown and Richter 2006
;
Honma et al. 2009
.

In this work, we restrict the parameter space to
q=30
, which effectively mitigates potential overfitting issues.
Our dataset comprises 204 experimental data points for the
N=82
isotones, including 21 nuclear binding energies, 101 excitation energies in even-even nuclei (where available, we focus on the first
2^{+},4^{+},6^{+},8^{+},10^{+},12^{+}
states, as well as the
0_{2}^{+},2_{2}^{+},4_{2}^{+},6_{2}^{+},8_{2}^{+},10_{2}^{+},3_{1}^{-},5_{1}^{-}
, and
7_{1}^{-}
states), and 82 excitation energies in odd-
A
nuclei (for example, in
149
Ho, states include
11/2^{-}
,
15/2^{-}
,
19/2^{-}
,
23/2^{-}
,
27/2^{-}
,
1/2^{+}
,
3/2^{+}
,
5/2^{+}
,
7/2^{+}
,
19/2^{+}
, and
23/2^{+}
).
As the starting point, we adopt the initial parameter values from Ref.
Chen et al. 2024
. The optimization, as described above, converges in only three iterations, indicating the stability and efficiency of the fitting procedure.
The root-mean-square deviation (RMSD) between the experimental values and the shell model results for the 204 data points is 102 keV.

III.1
Binding energies and separation energies

The nuclear masses, denoted by
M_{\rm N}
, are derived using the following equation:

M_{\rm N}=M_{\rm A}-Z\cdot M_{e}+B_{e},

(13)

where
M_{\rm A}
is the atomic mass, taken from the AME2020 database
Wang et al. 2021
,

M_{e}
is the mass of an electron, and
B_{e}
is the total binding energy of electrons in the atom, calculated by

B_{e}=14.4381Z^{2.39}+1.55468\times 10^{-6}Z^{5.35}\text{ eV.}

(14)

The nuclear binding energy, denoted by
B(Z,N)
, is defined by

B(Z,N)\equiv Z\cdot M_{\rm p}+N\cdot M_{\rm n}-M_{\rm N},

(15)

where
M_{\rm p}
and
M_{\rm n}
are masses of a proton and a neutron, respectively.
Using the shell model, we calculate the binding energy of a nucleus relative to the doubly-magic core
132
Sn, i.e.,
B(Z,N)-B(^{132}\text{Sn})
.
Fig.
1
(a) compares the binding energies from AME2020
Wang et al. 2021
with the calculated result.
For the isotones
134
Te–
151
Tm, the shell model reproduces the experimental values with high accuracy, yielding an RMSD of 59 keV.
For
152
Yb and
153
Lu, the experimental atomic mass excesses carry large uncertainties, reported as
-46.270(150)
MeV and
-38.375(150)
MeV, respectively. These uncertainties may partially account for the relatively larger deviations from the shell model predictions, which are
-45.977
MeV and
-38.146
MeV, respectively.
Experimental values for the atomic mass excesses of
154
Hf and
155
Ta are not available, but the 2020 Audi-Wapstra extrapolation (labeled AW2020)
Wang et al. 2021
provides estimates of
-32.730(300)
MeV and
-23.988(300)
MeV, while the shell model predicts
-32.490
MeV and
-23.859
MeV, respectively.
The shell model further provides predictions for the atomic mass excesses of the more proton-rich isotones
156
W–
159
Ir, with values of
-17.335
MeV,
-8.032
MeV,
-0.523
MeV, and
9.393
MeV, respectively.

We also calculate the one-proton and two-proton separation energies, defined by:

\displaystyle S_{\rm p}(Z,N)=

\displaystyle B(Z,N)-B(Z-1,N),

(16)

\displaystyle S_{\rm 2p}(Z,N)=

\displaystyle B(Z,N)-B(Z-2,N).

In Fig.
1
(b), experimental data for the one-proton and two-proton separation energies are available up to
155

159

153

In contrast, both the one-proton and two-proton separation energies for
154

For
155

Uusitalo et al. 1999
;
Page et al. 2007
.
For the recently observed nucleus
156

-0.606

This indicates the possibility of two-proton emission; however, experimental results show that this decay mode is strongly suppressed, with
\beta^{+}
decay being dominant

;
Briscoe et al. 2023
.
For the experimentally unmeasured nucleus
158

-0.235
MeV and a two-proton separation energy of
-2.264
MeV. This implies that while single-proton emission is hindered, two-proton emission could be a favored decay channel.
In the cases of
157

159

-2.029

-2.643

-1.279

All these values are negative, indicating that both nuclei are unbound with respect to one- and two-proton emission.

III.3
Low-lying structures of even-even nuclei

In this subsection, we discuss the low-lying spectra of the

We begin with the systematics of the first

As shown in Fig.

\displaystyle g_{j}=\begin{cases}g_{l}+\frac{g_{s}-g_{l}}{2j},\quad~\text{if }j=l+\frac{1}{2},\\

IV
summary and outlook

In this work, we have performed comprehensive shell model calculations for the
N=82
isotonic chain, based on a newly optimized proton-proton effective interaction.
We use the PCA method to optimize the effective interaction by fitting to 204 experimental data points. We consider in the optimization up to 30 degrees of freedom for linear combinations among the 165 TBMEs and SPEs of the effective Hamiltonian, which allows us to achieve a root-mean-square deviation of 102 keV.
The resulting interaction successfully reproduces binding energies, as well as one- and two-proton separation energies, electromagnetic properties, and low-lying spectra, and provides reliable predictions for nuclei near or beyond the proton drip line, including
{}^{155}\mathrm{Ta}
,
{}^{156}\mathrm{W}
,
{}^{157}\mathrm{Re}
,
{}^{158}\mathrm{Os}
, and
{}^{159}\mathrm{Ir}

We have studied the
B(E2)
values and
g
-factors which can provide a sensitive probe for low-lying structures across the even-even isotones, with particular focus on the seniority pattern.
The systematics of odd-parity states (
3^{-}
,
5^{-}
, and
7^{-}
) were analyzed. The shell model calculations correctly reproduces the experimental minimum of the
3^{-}
energy at
{}^{146}\textrm{Gd}
.
We also calculated
B(E3)
values and compared them with the available data, including those for
{}^{140}\textrm{Ce}
and
{}^{146}\textrm{Gd}
.

For the odd-
A
nuclei of concern, our shell-model calculation reproduces all known ground-state spins and parities, offering an explanation for the
1/2^{+}
ground state of
{}^{147}\textrm{Tb}
and the
3/2^{+}
ground states of
{}^{155}\textrm{Ta}
and
{}^{157}\textrm{Re}
, beyond the scope of the mean-field approximation.
We present systematic study for
g
-factors of low-lying
5/2^{+}
,
7/2^{+}
, and
11/2^{-}
states, and analyzed their underlying configurations.
In particular, we suggest that the low-lying
5/2^{+}
states in
{}^{135}\textrm{I}
exhibit strong admixtures of seniority-one and weak-coupling configurations, shedding light on their complex structure.

We hope that the new proton-proton effective interaction developed in this work provides a solid foundation for future studies of atomic nuclei near
N=82
.

On the hull-variation problem of equivalent vector rank metric codes
Duy Ho, Trygve Johnsen

Abstract
The intersection of a linear code with its dual is called the hull of the code. It is known that, for classical linear codes under the Hamming metric, the dimension of the hull can be reduced up to equivalence. This phenomenon leads to the so-called hull-variation problem formulated by Hao Chen in 2023. In this paper, we consider the analogous problem for vector rank metric codes, along with their associated matrix codes and extended block codes. We also discuss the implications in the context of ( q , m ) (q,m) -polymatroids.



Keywords:
Vector rank metric codes, matrix codes, extended block codes, hull dimensions,
(q,m)
-polymatroids.

MSC (2020):
Primary 94B05; Secondary 94B60, 94B25.

1
Introduction

A classical linear code
C
is a subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
with the Hamming distance used as the metric.
The hull of a classical linear code is defined as the intersection of the code with its dual code.
The concept of the hull has gained significant interest, particularly in applications such as quantum error correction and cryptography.

Classical linear codes with trivial hulls are known as linear codes with complementary duals (LCD codes). These codes were first introduced by Massey in
[
23
]
.
In
[
31
]
, Sendrier proved that LCD codes are asymptotically good and used them in relation with equivalence testing of linear codes in
[
32
]
.
Recently, LCD codes became an attractive research interest as they offer solutions to many cryptographic problems, for example against side-channel attacks and fault non-invasive attacks, see
[
4
]
.
In
[
5
]
, it was shown that any linear code over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
(
q>3
) is equivalent to a Euclidean LCD code and any linear code over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
(
q>2
) is equivalent to a Hermitian LCD code.

The latter result was generalized recently in
[
7
]
and
[
22
]
, where it was shown that any classical linear code
C
over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
(
q>2
) and with Hermitian hull dimension
\ell
, one can find a new code which is equivalent to
C
with Hermitian hull dimension not greater than
\ell
. This phenomenon leads to the so-called
hull-variation problem formulated by Hao Chen in 2023. The problem is stated as follows.

Hull-Variation Problem:
When a linear code
C
is transformed to an equivalent linear code
C^{\prime}
, how is its Euclidean or Hermitian hull changed?

Apart from the Hamming distance, various distance metrics are used in coding theory for different applications.
One notable example that we will consider here is the rank metric.
The class of rank metric codes was first considered by Delsarte
[
9
]
in the late 1970s. In 1985, Gabidulin
[
10
]
contributed significantly to the development
of error-correcting codes in the rank metric, establishing several important properties of rank metric codes.
In the past decade, rank metric codes have become an important research topic with various applications, especially in network coding, cryptography, and error correction in random linear network coding. For recent surveys on the theory and applications of rank metric code, we refer the reader to
[
1
]
and
[
12
]
. For a description of applications of vector rank metric codes, where it is important that the codes are LCD, see
[
18
, pp. 107–110]
.

Based on the previous results in the literature, in this paper we consider the (Euclidean) hull-variation problem of equivalent rank metric codes, along with their associated matrix codes and extended block codes.
We show that when
q>3
, the dimension of the hull can also be reduced up to equivalence.
We provide examples that when
q=2
or
q=3
, the hull dimension of vector rank metric codes can also be varied up to equivalence.
These examples demonstrate that the behavior of the hulls in the context of vector rank metric codes is different from that of classical Hamming metric codes. Our results also imply that the hull dimension of a matrix code is not an invariant captured by the associated
(q,m)
-polymatroids.

The content of the paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we recall preliminary results for linear codes. In Section 3, we consider the hull-variation problem of vector rank metric and their associated codes when
q>3
. In Section 4, we consider the special cases
q=2
and
q=3
not covered by Section 3. In Section 5, we discuss the implications in the context of
(q,m)
-polymatroids.

2
Preliminaries

We follow some material from
[
2
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
13
]
,
[
24
]
,
[
29
]
, and
[
30
]
.
Let
q
be a prime power, and let
m,n,k
be positive integers.
Let
\mathbb{F}_{q}
be the finite field with
q
elements.
Let
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n\times m}
be the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-vector space of matrices of size
n\times m
with entries in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

2.1
Hamming metric codes and vector rank metric codes

An
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-linear code

C
of length
n
is a subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{n}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
. Let
k
be the dimension of
C
over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
. The code
C
is often described in terms of a
generator matrix

G\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{k\times n}
, which is a full-rank matrix whose rows generate
C
.
Let
u=(u_{1},\dots,u_{n})
and
v=(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})
be in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{n}
.
The
dual
of
C
is the
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-linear code

C^{\perp}:=\{v\in\mathbb{F}^{n}_{q^{m}}\mid\langle v,w\rangle=0\text{ for all $w\in C$}\},

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is the standard inner product of
\mathbb{F}^{n}_{q^{m}}
.

We now define two different metrics used for the
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-linear code
C
.

1. The classical
Hamming distance

d_{H}(u,v)
between
u
and
v
is defined to be the number of coordinates in which they differ. If
C
is equipped with the Hamming distance, then it is called a
Hamming metric code
and is denoted by
[n,k]_{q^{m}}
.

2. For each
1\leq i\leq n
, let
u_{i}=(u_{i,1},\dots u_{i,m})
and
v_{i}=(v_{i,1},\dots v_{i,m})
, with respect to some fixed basis
\mathcal{G}
of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Let

R_{u,v}=\begin{bmatrix}u_{1,1}-v_{1,1}&u_{2,1}-v_{2,1}&\dots&u_{n,1}-v_{n,1}\\
u_{1,2}-v_{1,2}&u_{2,2}-v_{2,2}&\dots&u_{n,2}-v_{n,2}\\
\dots&\dots&\dots&\dots\\
u_{1,m}-v_{1,m}&u_{2,m}-v_{2,m}&\dots&u_{n,m}-v_{n,m}\\
\end{bmatrix}.

The
rank distance

d_{R}(u,v)
between
u
and
v
is defined to be the rank of
R_{u,v}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
If
C
is equipped with the rank distance, then it is called a
vector rank metric
code and is denoted by
[n,k]_{q^{m}/q}
.

2.2
Matrix codes

A
matrix code
of size
n\times m
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear subspace
C\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n\times m}
.
If
C
is of dimension
k
, then
C
is called an
[n\times m,k]_{q}
matrix code over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
Given a matrix
M\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n\times m}
, we write
\text{Tr}(M)
for the trace of
M
, and
M^{T}
for the transpose of
M
.
The
trace product
of matrices
M,N\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n\times m}
is denoted and defined by

\langle M,N\rangle:=\text{Tr}(MN^{T}).

The dual of
C
is the matrix code defined as

C^{\perp}:=\{N\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n\times m}\mid\langle M,N\rangle=0\text{ for all }M\in C\}.

It can be checked that
C^{\perp}
is an
[n\times m,nm-k]_{q}
matrix code.

We now define the metric used for matrix codes. The
rank weight
of a matrix
M\in C
is the rank of
M
and is denoted
\operatorname{wt}_{R}(M)
. The
rank distance
between two matrices
M,N\in C
is the rank of their difference
M-N
, that is,

d_{R}(M,N)=\operatorname{wt}_{R}(M-N).

2.3
Extended block codes

In order to easily handle matrix codes, we transform matrix codes to linear block codes. For a matrix
X=[x_{ij}]\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n\times m}
, the
extended row vector
corresponding to
X
is the vector

\rho(X)=(x_{11},x_{12},\dots,x_{1m},x_{21},x_{22},\dots,x_{2m},\dots,x_{n1},x_{n2},\dots,x_{nm})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{nm},

formed by concatenating the rows of the
n\times m
matrix
X
.
If
C\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n\times m}
is a matrix code, then the
extended block code
of
C
is given by

\rho(C)=\{\rho(X)\mid X\in C\}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{nm}.

The map
\rho
defines a natural correspondence between matrix codes and linear block codes. For an
[n\times m,k]_{q}
matrix code
C
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, there is a corresponding
[nm,k]_{q}
linear block code

C^{\prime}=\rho(C)=\{\rho(A):A\in C\}

over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. It can be verified that

\langle X,Y\rangle=\text{Tr}(XY^{T})=\rho(X)\cdot\rho(Y)

and so

\rho(C^{\perp})=\rho(C)^{\perp}.

(1)

Thus, there is also a natural correspondence between the duals of matrix codes and the
duals of extended block codes.

A generator matrix
G
of
\rho(C)
will also be called a
generator matrix
of
C
.
Each row of
G
is the image of the generators of the matrix code
C
under
\rho
.
This generator matrix is of size
k\times nm
.
We note that a generator matrix does not determine a unique matrix code and it is important to indicate the size of the matrix code whenever a generator matrix is mentioned.

We note that although the dual codes coincide, the distance metrics used
for block codes are different from those for matrix codes. Throughout, we consider
only the Hamming distance for block codes and the rank distance for matrix codes.

2.4
Codes associated with vector rank metric codes

Let
\mathcal{G}=\{\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{m}\}
be a basis of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
The matrix associated with a vector
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{n}
with respect to
\mathcal{G}
is the
n\times m
matrix
M_{\mathcal{G}}(\alpha)
with entries in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
defined by

\alpha_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}M_{\mathcal{G}}(\alpha)_{ij}\gamma_{j}\quad\text{for all }i=1,\dots,n.

The
matrix code associated with a vector rank metric code
C\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{n}
with respect to the basis
\mathcal{G}
is

\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{G}}(C)=\{M_{\mathcal{G}}(\alpha):\alpha\in C\}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n\times m}.

Although for a fixed basis
\mathcal{G}
, the duality notions for matrix and vector rank metric codes do not coincide in general, in
[
29
]
it was shown that there is a simple relation between them via dual bases of finite fields.

Let
\text{Trace}:\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}\to\mathbb{F}_{q}
be the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear trace map given by

\text{Trace}(\alpha):=\alpha+\alpha^{q}+\cdots+\alpha^{q^{m-1}}\quad\forall\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}.

Two bases
\mathcal{G}=\{\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{m}\}
and
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}=\{\gamma^{\prime}_{1},\dots,\gamma^{\prime}_{m}\}
of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
are said to be
dual
(or
complementary
) if

\text{Trace}(\gamma_{i}\gamma^{\prime}_{j})=\delta_{i,j}\quad\text{for all }i,j\in\{1,\dots,m\}.

Here,
\delta_{\cdot,\cdot}
is the Kroneker delta function. It is well known that for every basis
\mathcal{G}
of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, there exists a unique dual basis
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
, see
[
19
]
and
[
26
]
. A basis is
self-dual
if it is dual with itself.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
C\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{k}
be a vector rank metric code, and let
\mathcal{G},\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
be dual bases of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Then

C_{\mathcal{G}^{\prime}}(C^{\perp})=C_{\mathcal{G}}(C)^{\perp}.

As described in the previous subsection, for each matrix code, we have a corresponding extended block code from the map
\rho
. The
extended block code associated with a vector rank metric code
C\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{n}
with respect to the basis
\mathcal{G}
is

\rho(\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{G}}(C))=\{\rho(M_{\mathcal{G}}(\alpha)):\alpha\in C\}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{nm}.

2.5
Equivalent codes

We now describe different notions of equivalent codes.

1.
Hamming metric codes
. A
monomial matrix
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is a square matrix having exactly one nonzero element of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
in each row and column.
Let
C
and
C^{\prime}
be a pair of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-linear codes.
We say that
C
and
C^{\prime}
are equivalent with respect to the Hamming metric if there exists a monomial matrix
M\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{n\times n}
and a pair of generator matrices
G,G^{\prime}
of
C,C^{\prime}
, respectively, satisfying
G^{\prime}=GM
.

2.
Vector rank metric codes
. Let
C
and
C^{\prime}
be a pair of
[n,k]_{q^{m}/q}
vector rank metric codes. We say that
C
and
C^{\prime}
are equivalent with respect to the rank metric if there exists
A\in\text{GL}_{n}(q)
and a pair of generator matrices
G,G^{\prime}
of
C,C^{\prime}
, respectively, satisfying

G^{\prime}=GA.

3.
Matrix codes
. An
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear isometry
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n\times m}
is an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear homomorphism

\varphi:\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n\times m}\to\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n\times m}

such that

\text{rank}(\varphi(M))=\text{rank}(M)\quad\text{for every }M\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n\times m}.

As shown by Hua
[
17
]
for fields of odd characteristic and by Wan
[
35
]
for fields of characteristic 2, an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear isometry
\varphi
can be characterized as follows.

(a) If
m\neq n
, then there exist matrices
A\in\text{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
and
B\in\text{GL}_{m}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
such that

\varphi(M)=AMB\quad\text{for all }M\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n\times m}.

(b) If
m=n
, then there exist matrices
A,B\in\text{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
such that either

\varphi(M)=AMB\quad\text{for all }M\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n\times n},

or

\varphi(M)=AM^{T}B\quad\text{for all }M\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n\times n}.

We say that two matrix codes
C,C^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n\times m}
are equivalent if there is an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear isometry

\varphi:\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n\times m}\to\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n\times m}

such that
\varphi(C)=D
.

4.
Extended block codes
. We follow the setting in
[
24
]
and
[
25
]
.
Since the generator matrix of a matrix code
C\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n\times m}
is expressed as the generator matrix of its extended block code
\rho(C)
, we can translate the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear isometries described above as maps acting on the generator matrix of
C
. In particular, we determine these maps as
right multiplication by elements of
\text{GL}_{nm}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
acting on the extended row vectors.

We say that
f:\mathbb{F}_{q}^{nm}\to\mathbb{F}_{q}^{nm}
is a
linear
n\times m
matrix-equivalence map on extended row vectors
if there exists an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear isometry
g:\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n\times m}\to\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n\times m}
such that, for all
A\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n\times m}
,

f(\rho(A))=\rho(g(A)),

i.e.,
f=\rho\circ g\circ\rho^{-1}
.
We denote the collection of linear
n\times m
matrix-equivalence maps on extended row vectors by
{Equiv}_{\text{Vec}}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n\times m})
.
The group
{Equiv}_{\text{Vec}}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n\times m})
satisfies

{Equiv}_{\text{Vec}}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n\times m})=\begin{cases}\{S^{i}(N^{T}\otimes M)\mid i\in\{0,1\},N,M\in\text{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\}&\text{if }n=m,\\
\{N^{T}\otimes M\mid N\in\text{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q}),M\in\text{GL}_{m}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\}&\text{if }n\neq m,\end{cases}

where
S=[E_{ji}]_{ij}
is the matrix for transposition. The group structure is given by

{Equiv}_{\text{Vec}}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n\times m})\cong\begin{cases}\mathbb{Z}_{2}\ltimes(\text{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\times\text{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))/H&\text{if }n=m,\\
(\text{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\times\text{GL}_{m}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))/H&\text{if }n\neq m,\end{cases}

where
H=\{(\lambda I_{n},\lambda^{-1}I_{m})\mid\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}\}\leq\text{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\times\text{GL}_{m}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
.

Let
C
and
C^{\prime}
be two matrix codes. We say that
\rho(C)
and
\rho(C^{\prime})
are
linear
n\times m
matrix-equivalent
if there exists
A\in{Equiv}_{\text{Vec}}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n\times m})
and a pair of generator matrices
G,G^{\prime}
of
\rho(C),\rho(C^{\prime})
, respectively, satisfying

G^{\prime}=GA.

5.
Codes associated with vector rank metric codes
.
In
[
24
]
, we have the following.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
C
and
C^{\prime}
be two vector rank metric codes. If
C
and
C^{\prime}
are equivalent, then
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{G}}(C)
and
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{G}}(C^{\prime})
are equivalent.

Also from the setting above, we have the following.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
C
and
C^{\prime}
be two vector rank metric codes. If
C
and
C^{\prime}
are equivalent, then
\rho(\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{G}}(C))
and
\rho(\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{G}}(C^{\prime}))
are linear
n\times m
matrix-equivalent.

2.6
The hull of codes

We have defined the duality notions for Hamming metric codes, vector rank metric codes, matrix codes, and extended block codes. If
C
is one such code, then the
(Euclidean) hull
of
C
is the intersection of
C
and
C^{\perp}
. We denote the hull of
C
by
H(C)
.

Let
C
be an
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-linear code, equipped with either the Hamming distance or the rank distance. If
C\cap C^{\perp}=\{\mathbf{0}\}
, then we say that
C
is a
linear code with complementary dual
. We will abbreviate such a code as an
(Euclidean) LCD code
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
C
be either an
[n,k]_{q^{m}}
Hamming metric code or an
[n,k]_{q^{m}/q}
vector rank metric code. Let
G
be a generator matrix of
C
over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
. Then

\dim(H(C))=k-\mathrm{rank}(GG^{T})
.

Proof.

A vector
v
is an element of
H(C)
if and only if
\textbf{v}\in C
and
\textbf{v}\in C^{\perp}
. The first condition implies that
\textbf{v}=\textbf{u}G
for some
\textbf{u}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{k}
. The second condition implies that
\textbf{v}G^{T}=\textbf{0}
.
Then

H(C)=\{\textbf{u}G\mid\textbf{u}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{k}\text{ and }\textbf{u}GG^{T}=\textbf{0}\}.

The proof now follows from the rank-nullity theorem.
∎

We are interested in investigating the hulls of matrix codes and extended block codes associated with vector rank metric codes. For this purpose, we assume that
\mathcal{G}
is a self-dual basis of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, that is,
\mathcal{G}
is dual with itself. Such a self-dual basis exists if and only if
q
is even or both
q
and
m
are odd, see
[
15
,
33
]
. In this case, we have the following result from
[
20
]
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
q
be even or both
q
and
m
be odd, and let

\mathcal{G}
be a self-dual basis of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Let
C
be a
[n,k]_{q^{m}/q}
vector rank metric code. Then
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{G}}(C\cap C^{\perp})=\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{G}}(C)\cap\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{G}}(C)^{\perp}
.

3
On the hull-variation problem of vector rank metric and their associated codes

It was shown in
[
5
, Corollary 5.15]
that the following holds.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
q>3
be a prime power and let
C
be an
[n,k]_{q}
code. Then there exists an equivalent
[n,k]_{q}
code
C^{\prime}
which is LCD.

In
[
7
, Corollary 2.2]
and
[
22
, Theorem 6]
, the previous Theorem
3.1
was generalized in the setting of Hermitian hulls, with applications in constructing entanglement assisted quantum error-correcting codes. This result can be adapted to the case of Euclidean hulls, which we state as follows.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
q>3
be a prime power and let
C
be an
[n,k]_{q}
code with
\dim(H(C))=h
. Then there exists an equivalent
[n,k]_{q}
code
C^{\prime}

with
\dim(H(C^{\prime}))=l
for each
l\in\{0,1,\dots,h\}
.

3.1
Vector rank metric codes

Inspired by the proof of
[
22
, Theorem 6]
, we show that the Euclidean hull dimension of vector rank-metric codes and their associated codes can also be reduced under equivalence.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
q>3
be a prime power and let
C
be an
[n,k]_{q^{m}/q}
vector rank metric code with
\dim(H(C))=h
.
Then there exists an equivalent
[n,k]_{q^{m}/q}
code
C^{\prime}
with

\dim(H(C^{\prime}))=l
for each
l\in\{0,1,\dots,h\}
.

Proof.

Since
H(C)
is a
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-subspace of
C
, we can regard
H(C)
as an
[n,h]_{q^{m}/q}
vector rank metric code with generator matrix
G_{0}
.
We note that applying elementary row operations on
G_{0}
with multiplication by scalars from
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
does not change the code
H(C)
, so we can assume that
G_{0}
has the standard form
G_{0}=[I_{h}\hskip 5.0ptA]
after a possible permutation of the coordinates of the codewords of
C
. The same standard form would have been achieved if we had performed the same permutation of the coordinates of the code
C
we had started with. The hull dimension does not change after such a permutation, so we may indeed assume that
G_{0}
is on the standard form. (Permuting the coordinates is given by
G\mapsto GA,
for
A
a permutation matrix, and hence vector rank metric codes differing only by a permutation of the coordinates are equivalent as such codes).

1. Let
G
be a generator matrix of
C
. Under suitable row operations on
G
,
we can then assume that
G
is of the form

G=\begin{bmatrix}I_{h}&A\\
\mathbf{0}&B\end{bmatrix}.

Let
\mathbf{g}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{g}_{k}
be the rows of
G
. Since
\mathbf{g}_{i}\in H(C)
for
1\leq i\leq h
, we have that
\langle\mathbf{g}_{i},\mathbf{g}_{j}\rangle=0
if
1\leq i\leq h
or
1\leq j\leq h
.
Hence,
GG^{T}
has the form

GG^{T}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{0}&\mathbf{0}\\
\mathbf{0}&BB^{T}\end{bmatrix}.

In particular,
AA^{T}+I_{h}=\mathbf{0}
, and
AB^{T}=\mathbf{0}
. From Lemma
2.4
,
\text{rank}(BB^{T})=k-h
.

2. Let
M=\text{diag}(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{h-l},1,\dots,1)
be an
n\times n
diagonal matrix, where
a_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
and
a_{i}^{2}\neq 1
for
1\leq i\leq h-l
. These conditions can always be met
for
q>3
.

We now consider the vector rank metric code
C^{\prime}
with the generator matrix

G^{\prime}=GM
. Since
M\in\text{GL}_{n}(q)
, it follows that
C^{\prime}
is equivalent to
C
.
We have

\displaystyle G^{\prime}G^{\prime T}

\displaystyle=GMM^{T}G^{T}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}I_{h}&A\\
\mathbf{0}&B\end{bmatrix}\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}\begin{matrix}a_{1}^{2}&&&&&\\
&\ddots&&&&\\
&&a_{h-l}^{2}&&&\\
&&&1&&\\
&&&&\ddots&\\
&&&&&1\end{matrix}&\mathbf{0}\\
\hline\cr\mathbf{0}&\begin{matrix}1&&\\
&\ddots&\\
&&1\end{matrix}\end{array}\right]\begin{bmatrix}I_{h}&\mathbf{0}\\
A^{T}&B^{T}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}\begin{matrix}a_{1}^{2}&&&&&\\
&\ddots&&&&\\
&&a_{h-l}^{2}&&&\\
&&&1&&\\
&&&&\ddots&\\
&&&&&1\end{matrix}&A\\
\hline\cr\mathbf{0}&B\end{array}\right]\begin{bmatrix}I_{h}&\mathbf{0}\\
A^{T}&B^{T}\end{bmatrix}.

Let
T
be the diagonal matrix
T=\text{diag}(a_{1}^{2}-1,a_{2}^{2}-1,\dots,a_{h-l}^{2}-1)
.
Then

G^{\prime}G^{\prime T}=\begin{bmatrix}T&\mathbf{0}&\mathbf{0}\\
\mathbf{0}&\mathbf{0}&\mathbf{0}\\
\mathbf{0}&\mathbf{0}&BB^{T}\end{bmatrix},

and
\mathrm{rank}(G^{\prime}G^{\prime T})=k-l
. Then by Lemma
2.4
,
\dim(H(C^{\prime}))=l
and the proof now follows.
∎

3.2
Codes associated with vector rank metric codes

In this subsection, we assume the additional condition that
q
is even or both
q
and
m
are odd. Then there exists a self-dual basis of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, see Subsection 2.6.
From Theorem
3.3
, we have the following corollary for matrix codes associated with vector rank metric codes.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
q>3
be even or both
q
and
m
are odd. Let
\mathcal{G}
be a self-dual basis of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
Let
C
be an
[n,k]_{q^{m}/q}
vector rank metric code with
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}(H(C))=h
.
Let
D:=\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{G}}(C)
be the matrix code associated with
C
.
Then there exists a matrix code
D^{\prime}
equivalent to D with

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(H(D^{\prime}))=ml
for each
l\in\{0,1,\dots,h\}
.

Proof.

Let
l\in\{0,1,\dots,h\}
. By Theorem
3.3
, there exists a vector rank metric code
C^{\prime}
equivalent to
C
with
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}(H(C^{\prime}))=l
.
Let
D^{\prime}:=\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{G}}(C^{\prime})
be the matrix code associated with
C^{\prime}
.
Then by Lemma
2.5
, we have that

H(D^{\prime})=\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{G}}(C^{\prime})\cap\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{G}}(C^{\prime})^{\perp}=\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{G}}(C\cap C^{\perp})=\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{G}}(H(C^{\prime})),

and so

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(H(D^{\prime}))=m\cdot\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}(H(C^{\prime}))=ml.

Furthermore,
D^{\prime}
is equivalent to
D
by Lemma
2.2
, and this completes the proof.
∎

We now consider the hull-variation problem for extended block codes associated with vector rank metric codes.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
C
and
C^{\prime}
be two matrix codes in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n\times m}
. Then
\rho(C\cap C^{\prime})=\rho(C)\cap\rho(C^{\prime})
.

Proof.

It can be readily checked that
\rho(C\cap C^{\prime})\subseteq\rho(C)\cap\rho(C^{\prime})
. For the converse, let
c\in\rho(C)\cap\rho(C^{\prime})
. Then
\rho^{-1}(c)\in C
and
\rho^{-1}(c)\in C^{\prime}
, so that
\rho^{-1}(c)\in C\cap C^{\prime}
. Then,
c\in\rho(C\cap C^{\prime})
.
∎

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
q>3
be even or both
q
and
m
are odd. Let
C
be an
[n,k]_{q^{m}/q}
vector rank metric code with
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}(H(C))=h
. Let
D:=\rho(\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{G}}(C))
be the extended block code associated with
C
. Then there exists an extended block code
D^{\prime}
which is linearly
n\times m
matrix-equivalent to
D
with

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(H(D^{\prime}))=ml
for each
l\in\{0,1,\dots,h\}
.

Proof.

Fix
l\in\{0,1,\dots,h\}
. By Theorem
3.3
, there exists a vector rank metric code
C^{\prime}
equivalent to
C
with
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}(H(C^{\prime}))=l
. Let
D^{\prime}:=\rho(\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{G}}(C^{\prime}))
be the extended block code associated with
C^{\prime}
. By Lemma
2.2
, the matrix codes
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{G}}(C)
and
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{G}}(C^{\prime})
are equivalent. Then by Lemma
2.3
,
D^{\prime}
is linearly
n\times m
matrix-equivalent to
D
.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle H(D^{\prime})=D^{\prime}\cap D^{\prime\perp}

\displaystyle=\rho(\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{G}}(C^{\prime}))\cap\rho(\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{G}}(C^{\prime}))^{\perp}

\displaystyle=\rho(\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{G}}(C^{\prime}))\cap\rho(\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{G}}(C^{\prime})^{\perp})

(by (
1
))

\displaystyle=\rho(\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{G}}(C^{\prime}))\cap\rho(\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{G}}(C^{\prime\perp}))

(by Lemma
2.1
)

\displaystyle=\rho(\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{G}}(C^{\prime})\cap\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{G}}(C^{\prime\perp}))

(by Lemma
3.5
)

\displaystyle=\rho(\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{G}}(C^{\prime}\cap C^{\prime\perp}))

(by Lemma
2.5
)

\displaystyle=\rho(\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{G}}(H(C^{\prime}))).

From the proof of Corollary
3.4
, we have that
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{G}}(H(C^{\prime})))=ml
. It follows that
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}H(D^{\prime})=ml,
which completes the proof.
∎

4
Special cases:
q=2
and
q=3

The results in Section 3 only applies to the case
q>3
. In this section, we consider some special cases when
q=2
and
q=3
to demonstrate that the hull dimension of vector rank metric codes can also be varied up to equivalence.

We first consider the following scenario when
q
is an arbitrary prime power, including the cases
q=2
and
q=3
. Let
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
be two
[n,k]_{q^{m}/q}
vector rank metric codes, with generator matrices
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
, respectively, such that both
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
have all their entries in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
Then there exists
A\in\text{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
such that
G_{1}A=G_{2}
, see
[
26
, Definition 13.3.14]
. Hence, all such codes
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
are equivalent vector rank vector codes.

Moreover, let
C_{0}
be the
[n,k]_{q^{m}/q}
vector rank vector code with generator matrix
G_{0}=[I_{k}|0]
. Then
H(C_{0})=\{\mathbf{0}\},
and so
C_{0}
is an LCD code. We then have the following.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
q
be a prime power and let
C
be an
[n,k]_{q^{m}/q}
vector rank metric code.
If
C
has a generator matrix over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
with all of its entries in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, then
C
is equivalent to an LCD code.

We describe an example for binary case.

Example 1
.

Let
q=2^{e}
for some natural number
e
, and let
m
be an arbitrary natural number.
We give an example of two vector rank metric codes, which are equivalent but with different hull dimension.
Let

G=\begin{bmatrix}1&0&1\\
0&1&0\end{bmatrix}

and let
C
be the
[3,2]_{q^{m}/q}
vector rank metric code generated by
G
over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
. A parity-check matrix of
C
is

H=\begin{bmatrix}1&0&1\end{bmatrix}.

The hull
H(C)
is equal to
C^{\perp}
, and so
C
is a dual-containing code. In particular,
\dim(H(C))=1
. Let

A=\begin{bmatrix}1&0&0\\
1&0&1\\
1&1&1\end{bmatrix},

and let
G^{\prime}=GA
. Explicitly,

G^{\prime}=\begin{bmatrix}0&1&1\\
1&0&1\end{bmatrix}.

Let
D
be the linear code generated by
G
over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
. A parity-check matrix of
D
is

H^{\prime}=\begin{bmatrix}1&1&1\end{bmatrix}.

In this case,
H(D)=\{\mathbf{0}\}
and so
D
is an LCD code.

Remark 4.1
.

If
e\geq 2
, the previous example serves as an illustration of Theorem
3.3
. However, it also applies when

e=1
; in other words, two equivalent
[n,k]_{2^{m}/2}
vector rank metric codes can have different hull dimension.

We now consider an example for the ternary case.

Example 2
.

Let
q=3^{e}
for some natural number
e
, and let
m
be an arbitrary natural number. Let
C
be the
[4,2]_{q^{m}/q}
vector rank metric code generated by

G=\begin{bmatrix}1&1&0&1\\
1&2&1&0\end{bmatrix}.

Then
C
is a self-orthogonal code with
\dim(H(C))=2
. Let

A=\begin{bmatrix}1&0&0&0\\
1&1&0&0\\
1&1&1&0\\
1&1&1&1\end{bmatrix}.

Let
G^{\prime}=GA
and let
D
be the
[4,2]_{q^{m}/q}
vector rank metric code generated by
G^{\prime}
. Then

G^{\prime}=\begin{bmatrix}0&2&1&1\\
1&0&1&0\end{bmatrix}.

and
\dim(H(D))=0
.

Unlike Examples
1
and
2
, in the following example we construct vector rank metric codes with generator matrices whose entries do not lie in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Hence, this case is not covered by Proposition
4.1
.

Example 3
.

Let
q
be a prime power of
2
and
m
an arbitrary natural number. Let
\alpha
be a primitive element of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
. We consider the
[q^{m},2]_{q^{m}/q}
vector rank metric code
C
with generator matrix

G=\begin{bmatrix}0&1&\alpha&\alpha^{2}&\cdots&\alpha^{q^{m}-2}\\
1&0&0&0&\cdots&0\end{bmatrix}.

We have

GG^{T}=\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\
0&1\end{bmatrix},

so that
\dim(H(C))=2-\mathrm{rank}(GG^{T})=1
. Let
A
be the lower bidiagonal matrix with all nonzero entries are 1, that is,

A=\begin{bmatrix}1&0&0&\cdots&0\\
1&1&0&\cdots&0\\
0&1&1&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&\cdots&0&1&1\end{bmatrix}.

Letting
G^{\prime}=GA,
we obtain

G^{\prime}G^{\prime T}=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\
1&1\end{bmatrix},

which is nonsingular. And so the code
C^{\prime}
generated by
G^{\prime}
is LCD and equivalent to
C
.

5
Matroids

In this section, we discuss some implications of the results in previous sections in the context of matroids,
q
-matroids and
(q,m)
-polymatroids. First of all, we recall some definitions.

1.
Matroids
. Let
E
be a finite set and let
r:2^{E}\to\mathbb{Z}
be a function.
An ordered pair
\mathcal{M}=(E,r)
is called a
matroid
if the following conditions hold:

(a)

If
X\subseteq E
, then
0\leq r(X)\leq|X|
.

(b)

If
X,Y\subseteq E
and
X\subseteq Y
, then
r(X)\leq r(Y)
.

(c)

If
X,Y\subseteq E
, then

r(X\cup Y)+r(X\cap Y)\leq r(X)+r(Y).

A matroid isomorphism is a bijection
\phi
of
E
, such that
r(\phi(X))=r(X),
for all
X\subset E.

2.
(q,m)
-polymatroids
. Let
\mathcal{E}
be the vector space
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
. We denote by
\Sigma(\mathcal{E})
the set of all subspaces of
\mathcal{E}.

A
(q,m)
-polymatroid
is an ordered pair
\mathcal{P}=(\mathcal{E},\varrho)
, where
\varrho:\Sigma(\mathcal{E})\to\mathbb{Z}
is a function satisfying the following properties:

(a)

If
A\in\Sigma(\mathcal{E})
, then
0\leq\varrho(A)\leq m\cdot\dim A
.

(b)

If
A,B\in\Sigma(\mathcal{E})
and
A\subseteq B
, then
\varrho(A)\leq\varrho(B)
.

(c)

If
A,B\in\Sigma(\mathcal{E})
, then
\varrho(A+B)+\varrho(A\cap B)\leq\varrho(A)+\varrho(B).

For
(q,m)
-matroids
\mathcal{P}_{i}=(\mathcal{E},\varrho_{i})
, for
i=1,2
, a matroid isomorphism is a rank preserving element of the linear group
\text{GL}_{n}(q)
, for
\mathcal{E}=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}.

3. A
q
-matroid
is a
(q,1)
-polymatroid.

5.1
Matroid-like structures from codes

For an
[n,k]_{q}
Hamming metric code
C
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and any subset
X\subseteq E:=\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
, define

r(X):=\dim C-\dim C(E-X),

where

C(Y):=\{x\in C:\operatorname{supp}(x)\subseteq Y\},

for any
Y\subseteq E
. Then
\mathcal{M}_{C}:=(E,r)
is a matroid.

We recall the well known result, which is proved in
[
3
]
for
q=2
and
[
14
]
for
q=3
(see also
[
5
, Section 3]
).

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
C
be a Hamming metric code over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
or
\mathbb{F}_{3}
. Then the dimension of
H(C)
is determined by the matroid isomorphism class of
\mathcal{M}_{C}
. Hence equivalent codes for the Hamming metric have identical hull dimensions, for
q=2,3
.

It is straightforward that equivalent Hamming metric codes induce isomorphic matroids.
For a matroid
\mathcal{M}=(E,r)
, its Tutte polynomial is defined as

t_{\mathcal{M}}(X,Y)=\Sigma_{A\subset E}(X-1)^{r(E)-r(A)}(Y-1)^{|A|-r(A)}.

It is known that for a binary Hamming metric code
C
one has

\dim(H(C))=\log_{2}^{|t_{M_{C}}(-1,-1)|}.

Moreover, for a Hamming metric ternary code
C
we have

\dim(H(C))=\log_{\sqrt{3}}^{|t_{M_{C}}(j,j^{2})|},

where
j
is a primitive third root of unity of the
complex numbers, and
|z|
is the modulus of the complex number
z
.
We refer the reader to
[
3
]
and
[
14
]
for expressions of hull dimensions in terms of Tutte polynomials.

This result stands in contrast to Theorem
3.2
, which states that for
q\geq 4
, given any linear code with hull dimension
h
, and any
l\in\{0,1,\dots,h\}
, there exists an equivalent code with hull dimension
l
.
Hence Proposition
5.1
shows that the situation for Hamming metric binary and ternary codes is fundamentally different from that of Hamming metric
q
-ary codes for
q\geq 4
.
(In short: For
q\geq 4
equivalent Hamming metric codes may have different hull dimensions, for
q=2,3
this is impossible).

For vector rank metric codes, Theorem
3.3
provides the analogue of Theorem
3.2
for
q\geq 4
. It is then natural to look for a result analogous to Proposition
5.1
for vector rank metric codes over

\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}/\mathbb{F}_{2}
and

\mathbb{F}_{3^{m}}/\mathbb{F}_{3}
.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
\mathcal{E}=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
and let
C
be an
[n,k]_{q^{m}/q}
vector rank metric code. Let
\varrho:\Sigma(\mathcal{E})\to\mathbb{Z}
be defined by

\varrho(X)=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}C-\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}C(X^{\perp}),

where
X^{\perp}
is the orthogonal complement of
X
in
\mathcal{E}
with respect to the standard Euclidean product, and
C(Y)
denotes the subset of
C
consisting of those words, whose column spaces are contained in
Y
, for a subspace
Y
of
\mathcal{E}
(after converting words to
n\times m
-matrices using a fixed basis of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
).
We define
\mathcal{P}_{C}:=(\mathcal{E},\varrho)
.

It was shown in
[
16
]
that
\mathcal{P}_{C}
is a
q
-matroid. See also
[
27
, Example 26]
for another equivalent definition.

From Examples
1
and
2
, in the cases when
e=1
and
q=2,3
,
with
C
a vector rank metric code over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}/q}
, we see that it is not possible to express the hull dimension of
C
in terms of the
q
-matroid
\mathcal{P}_{C}.

One sees directly that each of the two examples, involving the right multiplication of generator matrices by elements of
\text{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, gives two isomorphic
q
-matroids
\mathcal{P}_{C}
from two equivalent vector rank metric codes with different hull dimensions. Hence no general formula for the hull dimension in terms of a Tutte-invariant respecting equivalence of vector rank metric codes is possible, not even in the cases
q=2,3
. We sum up by concluding the following.

Corollary 5.2
.

For all prime powers
q
, in particular for
q=2
and
q=3
, we may find a pair
\{C,D\}
of equivalent vector rank metric codes, and then also with isomorphic
q
-matroids
\mathcal{P}_{C}
and
\mathcal{P}_{D}
, but with different hull dimensions (over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
).

We now turn to matrix codes over a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

Definition 5.2
.

Let
\mathcal{E}_{1}=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
and
\mathcal{E}_{2}=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{m}
. Let
C
be a matrix code of size
n\times m
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
Let
p_{1}:\Sigma(\mathcal{E}_{1})\to\mathbb{Z}
be defined by

p_{1}(X)=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}C-\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}C(X^{\perp}),

where
X^{\perp}
and
C(X^{\perp})
are as described in Definition
5.1
.
We define
\mathcal{P}_{1,C}:=(\mathcal{E}_{1},p_{1})
.

Furthermore, let
p_{2}:\Sigma(\mathcal{E}_{2})\to\mathbb{Z}
be defined by

p_{2}(X)=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}C-\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}C(X^{\perp}),

where
C(Y)
here denotes the subset of
C
, whose row space is contained in
Y
, for all subspaces
Y\subset\mathcal{E}_{2}.
We define

\mathcal{P}_{2,C}:=(\mathcal{E}_{2},p_{2})
.

It was shown in
[
34
, Proposition 3]
that for
\mathcal{P}_{1,C}
is a
(q,m)
-polymatroid. A similar argument can be used to show that
\mathcal{P}_{2,C}
is a
(q,n)
-polymatroid.
This also means that, for the extended block code
\rho(C)\in\mathbb{F}^{nm}
, we have two associated
(q,m)
-polymatroid
\mathcal{P}_{1,C}
and
(q,n)
-polymatroid
\mathcal{P}_{2,C}
.

Corollary 5.3
.

For all prime powers
q
, in particular for
q=2
and
q=3
, we may, for some
m
and
n
, find a pair
\{C,D\}
of equivalent matrix codes, and then also with
\mathcal{P}_{1,C}
isomorphic to
\mathcal{P}_{1,D}
, and
\mathcal{P}_{2,C}
isomorphic to
\mathcal{P}_{2,D},

but with different hull dimensions (over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
).

The analogous statement is true for extended block codes.

Proof.

In Examples 1 and 2 we have given examples of equivalent vector rank metric codes with different hull dimensions, for
q=2
and
q=3
, respectively. For all
q\geq 4
such examples arise by Theorem
3.3
. If we choose self-dual bases for
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{m}/\mathbb{F}_{q}
, we also, by Lemmas
2.2
and
2.5
, derive two equivalent matrix codes with different hull dimensions in both these examples. It was shown in
[
13
, Proposition 6.7]
that equivalent matrix codes give rise, both to isomorphic
(q,m)
-polymatroids, and
(q,n)
-polymatroids if
n\neq m
(In
[
13
]
one phrases this result in terms of
q
-polymatroids, and not
(q,m)
-polymatroids and
(q,n)
-polymatroids, like we do, but this is just a matter of scaling the rank fuctions down by
m
and
n
respectively.) In Examples 1 and 2 we have
n=3,4,
respectively, so choosing any other
m
gives our desired examples, provided that
m
is odd when
q
is odd (to ensure the existence of a self-dual basis).
The statement about extended codes follows automatically.
∎

6
Final remarks

Remark 6.1
.

Most of the results given in the previous sections can be accompanied by analogous results, using Hermitian inner products, rather than the usual (Euclidean) products, which we have used. This preassumes that
q
, or at least
q^{m}
, is a square, so that conjugations of type
\overline{a}=a^{q}
or

\overline{a}=a^{\sqrt{q^{m}}}
are possible.

Remark 6.2
.

We have found it intriguing that by passing to extended codes, one obtains usual block codes in
\mathbb{F}^{mn}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, with the same, usual inner (scalar) product induced from the matrix trace-product, as the one which is used when treating the codes in
\mathbb{F}^{mn}
with the Hamming metric. Hence the hulls are defined in the same ways; only the metrics, and thereby the equivalence relations, and matroid/polymatroid structures, are different.

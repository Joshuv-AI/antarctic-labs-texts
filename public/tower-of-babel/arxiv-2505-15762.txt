Definition 1.1
.

We say that a countable set

-covering

In cases of

We say that an entire function

1.2.
Discretization Theorems

Let
B
be a vector space of measurable functions on a
measurable set
S\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{m}

and let
q\in[1,{\infty}]
.
Discretization theorems
for
B
state that there exist a finite (for a bounded
S
)
or countable (for an unbounded
S
) set of knots

\Omega=\left\{X_{\nu}\right\}_{\nu\in\Delta}\subset S

and constants

C_{1}=C_{1}(B,\Omega,S,q,m)\geq C_{2}=C_{2}(B,\Omega,S,q,m)

such that for all
f\in B
,

C_{2}\|f\|_{L_{q}(S)}\leq\left(\sum_{\nu\in\Delta}\left|f\left(X_{\nu}\right)\right|^{q}\right)^{1/q}\leq C_{1}\|f\|_{L_{q}(S)}.

(1.4)

For
q={\infty}
, the right inequality of (
1.4
) is trivial
with
C_{1}=1
, and
the left one with
C_{2}\in(0,1)
can be written in the form

\|f\|_{L_{\infty}(S)}\leq(1+{\gamma})\sup_{\nu\in\Delta}\left|f\left(X_{\nu}\right)\right|,

(1.5)

where
{\gamma}>0
.
In case of a compact set
S,\,q\in[1,{\infty})
,
and
\mathrm{card}(\Omega)={\Lambda}
, inequalities
(
1.4
) are often written in the following form
of so-called Marcinkiewicz-type theorems
(see, e.g.,
[
19
, Eqn. (1.2)]
):

C_{2}\|f\|_{L_{q}(S)}\leq\left(\frac{1}{{\Lambda}}\sum_{\nu=1}^{\Lambda}\left|f\left(X_{\nu}\right)\right|^{q}\right)^{1/q}\leq C_{1}\|f\|_{L_{q}(S)},

(1.6)

where
{\Lambda}={\Lambda}(B,\Omega,S,q,m)
and

C_{1}=C_{1}(q,m)\geq C_{2}=C_{2}(q,m)
.
In certain cases, the set
\Omega
can be explicitly
identified.
”Good” estimates of
{\Lambda}
are needed for each
discretization theorem with a compact
S
.
In this paper, we often call inequalities
(
1.4
), (
1.5
),
and (
1.6
) discretization theorems.

Discretization theorems had been initiated
in the 1930s–1940s
by Bernstein (1931 and 1948), Cartwright (1936),
Marcinkiewicz (1936), Marcinkiewicz
and Zygmund (1937),
Duffin and Shaeffer (1945), and others.
Influenced by problems of metric entropy,
numerical integration, and interpolation,
this topic has revisited in the 1990s–2020s
(see detailed surveys by Lubinsky
[
40
]
,
Schmeisser and Sickel
[
53
]
,
Bos et al.
[
12
]
, Dai et al.
[
19
]
,
Kroó
[
35
]
,
recent papers by Temlyakov
[
57
]
,
Dai et al.
[
18
]
, Kroó
[
36
]
,
and the references therein).

In most publications, containing discretization theorems,
the space
B
is one of the following spaces:
real- or complex-valued trigonometric or algebraic
polynomials, EFETs, and exponential polynomials.

1.2.1.
Trigonometric Polynomials

The story begins, like many others in
approximation theory, with Bernstein in 1931
who proved
[
3
, Eqns. (6),(22)]

the following inequalities for
S=[0,2\pi],\,B={\mathcal{T}}_{n},\,q={\infty},\,N\in{\mathbb{N}},\,N>n
, and
T_{n}\in{\mathcal{T}}_{n}
:

\displaystyle\left\|T_{n}\right\|_{L_{\infty}([0,2\pi])}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{2N+1}{2N-2n+1}}\max_{0\leq\nu\leq 2N}\left|T_{n}\left(\frac{2\nu\pi}{2N+1}\right)\right|

(1.7)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1+O(n/N))\max_{0\leq\nu\leq 2N}\left|T_{n}\left(\frac{2\nu\pi}{2N+1}\right)\right|,

\displaystyle\left\|T_{n}\right\|_{L_{\infty}([0,2\pi])}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\cos\frac{n\pi}{2N}\right)^{-1}\max_{0\leq\nu\leq 2N-1}\left|T_{n}\left(\frac{\nu\pi}{N}\right)\right|

(1.8)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1+O(n/N))\max_{0\leq\nu\leq 2N-1}\left|T_{n}\left(\frac{\nu\pi}{N}\right)\right|,

as
n/N\to 0
, where inequality (
1.8
) is sharp for

n|N
. Note that (
1.5
)
immediately follows from (
1.7
) and (
1.8
)
for a large enough

and, in addition,

{\Lambda}=2N
for (
1.8
).

A version of (
1.7
) for polynomials
on the unit circle was obtained by Sheil-Small

[
55
, Theorem 1]
.
Dubinin
[
22
, Theorem 1]
and Kalmykov

[
31
, Theorems 1 and 2]

extended (

) for polynomials
on the unit circle and on a circular arc,
respectively.

Discretization theorem (
1.6
)
for
S=[0,2\pi]
and

was proved by
Marcinkiewicz
[
41
, Theorems 9 and 10]

(see also
[

and

(see also

{\Lambda}=\lceil 2(1+{\varepsilon})n\rceil

{\varepsilon}>0

and all

These results were extended to

,
and multivariate
polynomials from

In particular,

1.2.3.
EFETs

Unlike polynomials, the discretization of EFETs on
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}

obviously requires an infinite set of knots.

Discretization theorems for EFETs in case of
q={\infty}
(i.e., inequalities
in the form of (
1.5
)) have been known since 1936.
The celebrated result proved by Cartwright

[
15
]
(see also
[
58
, Sect. 4.3.3]
)
states that for any
p>{\sigma}>0
and every

f\in B_{\sigma},\,\|f\|_{L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{1})}\leq C(p,{\sigma})\sup_{\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{1}}\left|f\left(\nu\pi/p\right)\right|
.
Duffin and Schaeffer
[
23
, Theorem 1]

strengthened this result by proving that for every
sequence
\Omega=\left\{X_{\nu}\right\}_{\nu=-{\infty}}^{\infty}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{1}
,
satisfying the conditions

\sup_{\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{1}}\left|X_{\nu}-\nu\pi/p\right|<{\infty},\qquad\inf_{\nu\neq\mu}\left|X_{\nu}-X_{\mu}\right|>0,

(1.10)

the following inequality holds:

\|f\|_{L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{1})}\leq C(p,{\sigma},\Omega)\sup_{\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{1}}\left|f\left(X_{\nu}\right)\right|
.
The careful analysis of the proofs of these results shows that

\lim_{p\to{\infty}}C(p,{\sigma})=\lim_{p\to{\infty}}C(p,{\sigma},\Omega)=1
,
so (
1.5
) is valid for
S={\mathbb{R}}^{1},\,B=B_{\sigma}
,
and
\Delta={\mathbb{Z}}^{1}
.

Bernstein
[
9
, Theorem 1]
introduced a weakened condition
(compared with (
1.10
))

0<X_{\nu+1}-X_{\nu}\leq\pi/p,\qquad\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{1},\quad p>{\sigma},

(1.11)

that guarantees the validity of
the following nonperiodic version of (
1.8
):

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{1})}\leq\left(\cos\frac{{\sigma}\pi}{2p}\right)^{-1}\sup_{\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{1}}\left|f\left(X_{\nu}\right)\right|

for every
f\in B_{\sigma}
of
at most polynomial growth on
{\mathbb{R}}^{1}
.
More univariate Cartwright-type theorems can be found in

[
11
]
and in the references therein.

Logvinenko
[
39
, Theorem 1]

proved a multivariate Cartwright-type theorem,
replacing univariate conditions
(
1.10
) and (
1.11
)
with the condition that

\left\{X_{\nu}\right\}_{\nu=1}^{\infty}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
is a

{\delta}
-covering net for
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
.
His result states that if

{\delta}{\sigma}<(2(\lceil em\rceil+1)^{-1}
, then for every

f\in B_{{\sigma},m}
,

\|f\|_{L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{m})}\leq e^{\delta}(1-{\delta}{\sigma})^{-1}\sup_{\nu\in{\mathbb{N}}}\left|f\left(X_{\nu}\right)\right|.

(1.12)

Thus (
1.5
) holds for
S={\mathbb{R}}^{m},\,B=B_{{\sigma},m}
,
and
\Delta={\mathbb{N}}
.
Earlier versions of this result
with a stronger condition
were obtained in Theorem 1 of

[
37

38
]
.
Note that a
{\delta}
-covering net is defined differently in

[
37
,
38

the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}

The celebrated univariate discretization theorem

\displaystyle C_{2}\|f\|_{L_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{1})}\leq\left(\sum_{\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{1}}\left|f\left(\pi\nu/{\sigma}\right)\right|^{q}\right)^{1/q}\leq C_{1}\|f\|_{L_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{1})}.

Its extension to

Multivariate discretization theorems

C_{2}\|f\|_{L_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{m})}\leq\left(\sum_{\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{1}}\left|f\left(X_{\nu}\right)\right|^{q}\right)^{1/q}\leq C_{1}\|f\|_{L_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{m})}

(1.13)

were discussed
by Pesenson
[
45
, Theorem 3.1]
and
by Zhai et al. in recent publication
[
59
, Theorem 4.1]
.

In particular, Pesenson proved that there exists

\Omega=\left\{X_{\nu}\right\}_{\nu=1}^{\infty}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}

such that for all functions

f\in B_{{\sigma},m}\cap L_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{m}),\,q\in[1,{\infty}]
,
inequalities (
1.13
) are held.
Note that Pesenson actually proved a more general result,
replacing
f\left(X_{\nu}\right)
in (
1.13
) with special
compactly supported distributions

\Phi_{\nu}(f)
.

Zhai et al. proved (
1.13
)
for
f\in B_{{\sigma},m,S}\cap L_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{m}),\,q\in(0,{\infty})
,
and special sets

\Omega=\left\{X_{\nu}\right\}_{\nu=1}^{\infty}
.
Note that the authors actually proved a weighted version of (
1.13
).
Certain necessary conditions on a set
\Omega
are discussed in

[
59
, Lemma 4.3]
as well.
The detailed comparison of our and the authors’ results
are given in Remark

.

A general approach to discretization theorems in various Banach and quasi-Banach spaces was developed by Kolomoitsev and Tikhonov in recent preprint
[
32
]
. In particular, the authors obtained discretization theorems
for EFETs from
L_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{m})
and other spaces (see
[
32
, Theorem 6.2 and Example 6.4 (i)]
).

Note that the major difference between
discretization theorems from
[
46
,
45
,
59
,
32
]
described above
and our result (see Theorem
1.6
below) is that we do not include the condition

f\in L_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{m})
. The absence of this condition, on the one hand, strengthens discretization theorem (
1.13
) but, on the other hand, it makes the proof of the left-hand side of (
1.13
) more complicated.

While discretization theorems for general EFETs on
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
have been known since 1936,
the corresponding results on compact subsets of
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
are unknown. Certainly, they are known for some special classes, e.g., for trigonometric polynomials on parallelepipeds (see Sect.
1.2.1
). One more special class of EFETs is discussed below.

1.2.4.
Exponential Polynomials

Kroó
[
36
, Theorems 6 and 7]

established discretization theorems in the form of
(
1.5
) for every real-valued multivariate
exponential polynomial

E(w,{\alpha})=\sum_{l=1}^{\mathcal{N}}\boldsymbol{c}_{l}e^{({\lambda}_{l},w)}

with the separation condition

\left|{\lambda}_{l}-{\lambda}_{j}\right|\geq{\alpha}>0,\,l\neq j
.
The discretization theorems are proved for
convex polytopes and convex polyhedral cones
S
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}

with

{\Lambda}=\mathrm{card}(\Omega)\leq C(S,m)\left(\frac{{\mathcal{N}}}{\sqrt{{\gamma}}}\right)^{m}\log^{m}\left(\frac{A_{{\mathcal{N}}}}{{\alpha}{\gamma}}\right),\qquad A_{{\mathcal{N}}}:=\max_{1\leq l\leq{\mathcal{N}}}\left|{\lambda}_{l}\right|.

(1.14)

Since the exponential type
A_{\mathcal{N}}
of
E(w,{\alpha})

appears only in the logarithmic term of inequality
(
1.14
), the main part of the bound is
provided by
{\mathcal{N}}^{m}
.

In addition, Kroó
[
36
, p. 72]
remarked
that if for every exponential polynomial

E_{N}^{*}(w)=\sum_{\langle k\rangle\leq N}c_{k}e^{(k,w)},\qquad c_{k}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{1},\quad k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}_{+}\cap O^{m}_{N},

(1.15)

of ”total degree”
N
, the inequality

\left\|E_{N}^{*}\right\|_{L_{\infty}(Q^{m}_{b})}\leq(1+{\gamma})\max_{1\leq j\leq{\Lambda}}\left|E_{N}^{*}(X_{j}\right)|

holds,
where
S\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
is a compact set with

|S|_{m}>0
and

\Omega=\left\{X_{\nu}\right\}_{\nu=1}^{\Lambda}\subset S

is a discrete set, then

\Lambda=\mathrm{card}(\Omega)\geq C(S,m)\left(N/\sqrt{{\gamma}}\right)^{m}

(1.16)

(see also
[
36
, Theorem 4]
for
m=1
).
Since for polynomials (
1.15
)

{\mathcal{N}}\sim N^{m}
and
A_{\mathcal{N}}\sim N
, estimate
(
1.14
) is sharp with respect
to
{\mathcal{N}}
up to the logarithmic term for
m=1
.
However, for
m>1
the main part of the bound
(
1.14
) for polynomials (
1.15
)
is
N^{m^{2}}
versus the lower estimate

CN^{m}
of (
1.16
).

Main Results and Remarks

We first discuss discretization results for EFETs on

{\mathbb{R}}^{m}

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
{\delta}_{1}>0,\,{\delta}>0,\,q\in[1,{\infty}]

In addition, let

{\mathbb{R}}^{m}

(b) Let
\Omega
be a
{\delta}
-covering net
for
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
(see Definition

) with

{\delta}
, satisfying the condition

{\delta}{\sigma}\leq C(m,q)

If

\left(\sum_{\nu=1}^{\infty}\left|f\left(X_{\nu}\right)\right|^{q}\right)^{1/q}\geq C_{2}\|f\|_{L_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{m})},

\displaystyle C_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{2}({\delta},{\sigma},m,q)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(4{\delta})^{-m/q}2^{1/q-1}\left(1-C(m,q)\max\left\{({\delta}{\sigma})^{q},({\delta}{\sigma})^{dq}\right\}\right)^{1/q}>0.

(c) Let
\Omega
be a
{\delta}
-covering net
for
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
with

{\delta}
, satisfying the condition

11m^{3/2}{\delta}{\sigma}\leq 1
.

.

A simplified version of Theorem
1.6

presented below immediately follows from
Theorem
1.6
.

Let

(a) If

Next, we discuss discretization results for
entire functions of high exponential type
on the cube
Q^{m}_{b}
. Let us define the function

\psi(\tau):=\frac{\sqrt{1+\tau^{2}}}{\tau}-\log\left(\tau+\sqrt{1+\tau^{2}}\right),\qquad\tau\in(0,{\infty}),

(1.26)

with the unique positive zero

{\gamma}_{0}=1.5088\ldots
; note that
\psi(\tau)<0
for
\tau>{\gamma}_{0}
(see also Sect.
3.1.2
).

Theorem 1.10
.

Let
{\mathfrak{S}}:=\{n(N)\}_{N=1}^{\infty}
be
an increasing sequence of
positive integers, and
let fixed numbers
b>0
and
\tau>{\gamma}_{0}

be independent of a given
n=n(N)\in{\mathfrak{S}}
.
In addition, let
f(\cdot)=f(N,\cdot)

be an entire function,
satisfying the inequality

|f(w)|\leq D_{n}\|f\|_{L_{\infty}(Q^{m}_{b})}\exp\left({\sigma}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left|w_{j}\right|\right),\qquad n\in{\mathfrak{S}},\quad w\in{\mathbb{C}}^{m},

(1.27)

where
{\sigma}={n}/{(mb\tau)}
and

D_{n}=D_{n}(b,\tau,m)
are constants.
If

\lim_{N\to{\infty}}D_{n(N)}e^{n(N)\psi(\tau)}=0,

(1.28)

then for any
{\gamma}>0
, there exist a constant

C=C(b,\tau,m,{\gamma})
,
an integer
n_{0}=n_{0}(b,\tau,m,{\gamma})\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
and a finite set
\left\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{\Lambda}\right\}\subset Q^{m}_{b}

with
{\Lambda}\leq Cn^{m}
such that

\|f\|_{L_{\infty}(Q^{m}_{b})}\leq(1+{\gamma})\max_{1\leq j\leq{\Lambda}}\left|f(X_{j}\right)|.

(1.29)

for every entire
f
, satisfying (
1.27
) for
n\geq n_{0}

and (
1.28
).

It is easy to verify conditions (
1.27
) and
(
1.28
) for the following class of EFETs.

Example 1.11
.

Let
F_{0}
be an entire function of one variable,
satisfying the inequality

\left|F_{0}(\xi)\right|\leq Ce^{|\xi|},\,\xi\in{\mathbb{C}}^{1},
and, in addition,

F_{0}\geq 0
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\setminus\{0\}
and
F_{0}(0)>0
.
Then any function of the form

f(w):=\int_{Q^{m}_{\sigma}}F_{0}((w,y))\,d\mu(y),\qquad w\in{\mathbb{C}}^{m},

(1.30)

where
{\sigma}>0
and
\mu
is a positive measure on
Q^{m}_{\sigma}
,
is entire, and, moreover,

\displaystyle|f(w)|\leq\int_{Q^{m}_{\sigma}}d\mu(y)\max_{y\in Q^{m}_{\sigma}}\left|F_{0}((w,y))\right|\leq C(f(0)/F_{0}(0))\exp\left({\sigma}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left|w_{j}\right|\right),\qquad w\in{\mathbb{C}}^{m}.

Hence for any function (
1.30
),
inequality (
1.27
) holds with

D_{n}=C/F_{0}(0),\,n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and obviously (
1.28
)
is valid as well. Note that here,
{\sigma}>0
is any number,
i.e.,
{\sigma}
is not necessarily equal to
n/(mb\tau)

as assumed in Theorem
1.10
.

In particular,
the set of all exponential polynomials

\sum_{l=1}^{\mathcal{N}}\boldsymbol{c}_{l}e^{({\lambda}_{l},w)}

with nonnegative
coefficients is a subset of the family of all functions
(
1.30
) with
F_{0}(\xi)=e^{\xi},\,\xi\in{\mathbb{C}}^{1}
, and
all positive discrete measures

\mu
on
Q^{m}_{\sigma}
.
Therefore, Theorem
1.10
holds for this set of
exponential polynomials by Example
1.11
.
For exponential polynomials with real or
complex coefficients, the problem of verifying

1.4.
Outline of the Proofs

The proofs of Theorems
1.6
,
1.9
,
1.10
,
and
1.12
and Corollary
1.8
are presented in Section
4
.
They are based, firstly, on certain geometric results and
properties of algebraic polynomials
and EFETs given in Section
2
and, secondly,
on approximation of EFETs by polynomials and
other EFETs discussed in Section
3
.

Major ingredients of the proofs of many
discretization theorems are Markov-Bernstein
type inequalities for polynomials and/or EFETs.
We need them as well, and three
Bernstein-type inequalities
are presented in Lemma
2.8
.
In particular, the proof of Theorem
1.6

is based on a technical discretization estimate
of Lemma
2.9
that uses a
Bernstein-type inequality for EFETs
from Lemma
2.8
(b).

While statement (a) of Theorem
1.6

follows from Lemmas
2.2
(a) and
2.9

(the former one is a certain result from
combinatorial geometry of independent interest),
the proof of statement (b) is more complicated
because the premises of the statement
do not include
the assumption of
f\in L_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{m})

that prevents us from using
Bernstein-type inequalities and
Lemma
2.9
. That is why we approximate
f

by special EFETs
f_{n}\in L_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{m}),\,n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and
study their properties (see Sect.
3.2
). Then we prove
statement (b) of Theorem
1.6
for functions
f_{n}
by using
geometric Lemmas
2.1
and
2.2
and
the discretization estimate of Lemma
2.9
.
Passing to the limit as
n\to{\infty}
completes the
proof of this statement.
Statement (c) of Theorem
1.6
is proved similarly
but with fewer technicalities.

Note that the idea of using similar functions belongs to
Logvinenko
[
37
,
38
,
39
]

2.
Properties of Sets, Polynomials, and Entire Functions

In this section we discuss certain geometric results
and some properties of
polynomials and entire functions of exponential type.

2.1.
Geometric Lemmas

First, we discuss properties of
{\delta}
-covering
and
{\delta}_{1}
-packing nets for
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}

(see Definitions
1.1
and
1.2
).

Lemma 2.1
.

(a)

\Omega=\left\{X_{\nu}\right\}_{\nu=1}^{\infty}

is a
{\delta}
-covering net for
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
if and only if

\bigcup_{\nu=1}^{\infty}\mathring{Q}_{\delta}^{m}\left(X_{\nu}\right)={\mathbb{R}}^{m}

for the family of open cubes

\left\{\mathring{Q}_{\delta}^{m}\left(X_{\nu}\right)\right\}_{\nu=1}^{\infty}
.

(b)

\Omega=\left\{X_{\nu}\right\}_{\nu=1}^{\infty}

is a
{\delta}_{1}
-packing net for
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
if and only if
the family of open cubes

\left\{\mathring{Q}_{{\delta}_{1}/2}^{m}\left(X_{\nu}\right)\right\}_{\nu=1}^{\infty}

is pairwise disjoint.

(c) If
\Omega=\left\{X_{\nu}\right\}_{\nu=1}^{\infty}

is a
{\delta}
-covering net for
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
,
then there exists a subset

\Omega^{*}=\left\{Z_{\mu}\right\}_{\mu=1}^{\infty}

of
\Omega
such that
\Omega^{*}
is
a
{\delta}
-packing and a
2{\delta}
-covering net for
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
.

Proof.

The proofs of statements (a) and (b)
are simple and left as an exercise to
the reader.

To prove statement (c), we first need certain
inductive constructions.
Let us set
Z_{1}:=X_{1}
and assume that we can construct
a set

\Omega^{*}_{n}:=\left\{Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{n}\right\}\subset\Omega

such that the following relations hold:

\displaystyle\inf_{1\leq\nu,\mu\leq n;\nu\neq\mu}\left\|Z_{\nu}-Z_{\mu}\right\|_{{\infty}}\geq{\delta},

(2.1)

\displaystyle\left\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\right\}\subset\bigcup_{\mu=1}^{n}\mathring{Q}_{{\delta}}^{m}\left(Z_{\mu}\right).

(2.2)

Note that relations (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) are
trivially valid for
n=1
.
The set
\Omega_{n}:=\Omega\setminus\bigcup_{\mu=1}^{n}\mathring{Q}_{{\delta}}^{m}\left(Z_{\mu}\right)\neq\emptyset

is a subsequence of
\Omega
,
and we choose
Z_{n+1}
as the element of
\Omega_{n}
with
the smallest index.
Then taking account of (
2.1
), we see that
(
2.1
) holds for

n
replaced with
n+1

since
Z_{n+1}\in\Omega_{n}
.
In addition, if
X_{n+1}\notin\bigcup_{\mu=1}^{n}\mathring{Q}_{{\delta}}^{m}\left(Z_{\mu}\right)
,
then
X_{n+1}=Z_{n+1}\in\mathring{Q}_{{\delta}}^{m}\left(Z_{n+1}\right)

by (
2.2
) and by the choice of
Z_{n+1}
.
Therefore, (
2.2
) holds for

n
replaced with
n+1
.
Thus by induction, for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, elements of

\Omega^{*}_{n}
satisfy relations (
2.1
) and
(
2.2
).

Next, setting
\Omega^{*}:=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}\Omega^{*}_{n}
,
we see by (
2.1
) that
\Omega^{*}
is a

{\delta}
-packing net for
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
.
Finally, since

\Omega\subset\bigcup_{\mu=1}^{\infty}\mathring{Q}_{{\delta}}^{m}\left(Z_{\mu}\right)

by (
2.2
),
for each
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}

there exist elements
X_{\nu}\in\Omega

and
Z_{\mu}\in\Omega^{*}
, such that

\left\|x-X_{\nu}\right\|_{{\infty}}<{\delta}
and

\left\|X_{\nu}-Z_{\mu}\right\|_{{\infty}}<{\delta}
.
Therefore,
\Omega^{*}
is
a
2{\delta}
-covering net for
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
.
∎

Next, we discuss a certain result from
combinatorial geometry.

Lemma 2.2
.

(a) Let
G:=\left\{S_{1},S_{2},\ldots\right\}

be a finite or countable family
of sets from
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
.
Assume that there exists a number
N=N(G)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{1}_{+}

such that any set from
G
has
the nonempty intersection with no more than
N

sets from
G
, not counting the set itself.
Then
there exists a partition

\left\{G_{j}\right\}_{j=1}^{N+1}
of
G

with pairwise disjoint sets in each subfamily

G_{j},\,1\leq j\leq N+1

(certain subfamilies can be empty).

(b) Let
\Omega=\left\{X_{\nu}\right\}_{\nu=1}^{\infty}
be a

{\delta}_{1}
-packing net for
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
.
Then any cube from a family

G:=\left\{Q_{\delta}^{m}\left(X_{\nu}\right)\right\}_{\nu=1}^{\infty}

of closed cubes with
{\delta}_{1}<2{\delta}
has
the nonempty intersection with no more than

N=\left\lfloor 2^{m}\left(\left({4{\delta}}/{{\delta}_{1}}\right)^{m}-1\right)\right\rfloor-1

(2.3)

cubes from
G
, not counting the cube itself.

(c) In addition to statement (b),
there exists a partition
\left\{G_{j}\right\}_{j=1}^{N+1}
of
G

with pairwise disjoint cubes in each subfamily
G_{j},\,1\leq j\leq N+1

(certain subfamilies can be empty), where
N
is defined by (
2.3
).

Proof.

(a) We first note that if
\mathrm{card}(G)<N+1
,
then the statement trivially holds true
with certain empty subfamilies.
So assume that
\mathrm{card}(G)\geq N+1
.

We start an inductive process of constructing a partition by setting

G_{j}(1):=\left\{S_{j}\right\},\,1\leq j\leq N+1
. Obviously,

\left\{G_{j}(1)\right\}_{j=1}^{N+1}
is a partition of

G(1):=\left\{S_{1},\ldots,S_{N+1}\right\}
.

Assume that we can construct a partition
\left\{G_{j}(k)\right\}_{j=1}^{N+1}
of

G(k):=\left\{S_{1},\ldots,S_{N+k}\right\}

with pairwise disjoint sets in each
G_{j}(k),\,k\geq 1,\,1\leq j\leq N+1
.
Recall that
S_{N+k+1}
has
the nonempty intersection with no more than
N
sets from
G(k)
.
Therefore by the pigeonhole principle, there exists
G_{j_{0}}(k),\,1\leq j_{0}\leq N+1
,
whose elements have the empty intersection with
S_{N+k+1}
. Finally, setting

\displaystyle G_{j}(k+1):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}G_{j}(k),&j\neq j_{0},\\
G_{j_{0}}(k)\cup\left\{S_{N+k+1}\right\},&j=j_{0},\end{array}\right.,1\leq j\leq N+1,

we obtain the needed partition

\left\{G_{j}(k+1)\right\}_{j=1}^{N+1}
of

G(k+1):=\left\{S_{1},\ldots,S_{N+k+1}\right\}
.

If
G
is finite, then the inductive process stops after

\mathrm{card}(G)-N
steps.
If
G
is countable, then setting

G_{j}=\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}G_{j}(k),\,1\leq j\leq N+1,
we arrive at
statement (a).
In particular, the construction shows that certain subfamilies

G_{j},\,1\leq j\leq N+1,
could be empty only in case

\mathrm{card}(G)<N+1
.

(b) Note first that if
Q_{\delta}^{m}(X_{\mu})\in G
and

Q_{\delta}^{m}(X_{\nu})\in G
for
\nu\neq\mu
,
then
Q_{\delta}^{m}(X_{\mu})\cap Q_{\delta}^{m}(X_{\nu})\neq\emptyset

if and only if

{\delta}_{1}\leq\left\|X_{\mu}-X_{\nu}\right\|_{{\infty}}\leq 2{\delta}

(recall that
{\delta}_{1}<2{\delta}
).
Indeed, if
x\in Q_{\delta}^{m}(X_{\mu})\cap Q_{\delta}^{m}(X_{\nu})
, then

\left\|X_{\mu}-X_{\nu}\right\|_{{\infty}}\leq 2{\delta}
by the triangle inequality.
If
\left\|X_{\mu}-X_{\nu}\right\|_{{\infty}}\leq 2{\delta}
,
then the intervals
\left[X_{\mu,j}-{\delta},X_{\mu,j}+{\delta}\right]
and

\left[X_{\nu,j}-{\delta},X_{\nu,j}+{\delta}\right]
have at least one joint point

x_{j},\,1\leq j\leq m
. Thus
x\in Q_{\delta}^{m}(X_{\mu})\cap Q_{\delta}^{m}(X_{\nu})
.

Hence the number
N_{1}
of cubes
Q_{\delta}^{m}(X_{\nu})\in G
that have
the nonempty intersection with a fixed
Q_{\delta}^{m}(X_{\mu})\in G,\,\nu\neq\mu
,
can be estimated by the maximal number
N_{2}
of points

X_{\nu}\in Q^{\prime}:=Q_{2{\delta}}^{m}(X_{\mu})\setminus\mathring{Q}_{{\delta}_{1}}^{m}(X_{\mu})

with the mutual ”distance” of
{\delta}_{1}
.
Let us also set
Q^{\prime\prime}:=Q_{2{\delta}}^{m}(X_{\mu})\setminus\mathring{Q}_{{\delta}_{1}/2}^{m}(X_{\mu})
.
Since

\Omega
is a
{\delta}_{1}
-packing net for
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
,
the sets
\mathring{Q}_{{\delta}_{1}/2}^{m}(X_{\nu})\cap Q^{\prime\prime}

are pairwise disjoint for

X_{\nu}\in Q^{\prime}\cap\Omega
.
In addition, if
x\in Q^{\prime}
, then

\left|\mathring{Q}_{{\delta}_{1}/2}^{m}(x)\cap Q^{\prime\prime}\right|_{m}\geq({\delta}_{1}/2)^{m}
.
Taking account of these two facts, we obtain

\displaystyle N_{1}\leq N_{2}\leq\frac{\left|Q^{\prime\prime}\right|_{m}}{\inf_{x\in Q^{\prime}}\left|\mathring{Q}_{{\delta}_{1}/2}^{m}(x)\cap Q^{\prime\prime}\right|_{m}}\leq 2^{m}\left(\left(\frac{4{\delta}}{{\delta}_{1}}\right)^{m}-1\right).

Thus statement (b) is established.

(c) The statement immediately follows from
statements (a) and (b).
∎

Different versions of Lemma
2.2
were obtained by
Brudnyi and Kotlyar
[
14
]

and Dol’nikov
[
21
, Theorem 1]
.

2.2.
Properties of Polynomials

We first prove a general result that reduces numerous
Markov-Bernstein-Nikolskii type inequalities for
complex-valued functions to real-valued ones.

Lemma 2.3
.

For a compact set
K\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
,
let
B
be a subspace of
C(K)

with a basis of real-valued functions
and
B_{{\mathbb{R}}}:=B\cap C_{{\mathbb{R}}}(K)
.
Next, let
L

be a bounded linear operator on

B
such that
L:B\to C(K)

and
L:B_{{\mathbb{R}}}\to C_{{\mathbb{R}}}(K)
.
In addition, let
\|\cdot\|
be
a monotone norm on
B

(i.e., if
g\in B,\,h\in B
, and

|g(x)|\leq|h(x)|
for
x\in K
,
then
\|g\|\leq\|h\|
). Then

\sup_{h\in B\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\|L(h)\|_{C(K)}}{\|h\|}=\sup_{g\in B_{{\mathbb{R}}}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\|L(g)\|_{C_{{\mathbb{R}}}(K)}}{\|g\|}.

(2.5)

Proof.

Every nonzero element
h\in B
can be represented
in the form
h=h_{1}+ih_{2}
, where
h_{j}\in B_{{\mathbb{R}}},\,j=1,2
.
Then there exists
x_{0}\in K
such that

\|L(h)\|_{C(K)}=|L(h)(x_{0})|
.
Assuming that
L(h)(x_{0})\neq 0
,
let us define
{\gamma}\in[0,2\pi)
by the equality

e^{i{\gamma}}=L(h)(x_{0})/|L(h)(x_{0})|
.
Then the element

g:=\cos{\gamma}\,h_{1}+\sin{\gamma}\,h_{2}
belongs to
B_{{\mathbb{R}}}
and
satisfies the relations

\displaystyle|g(x)|\leq|h(x)|,\quad x\in K;\qquad|L(g)(x_{0})|=|L(h)(x_{0})|.

Hence

\frac{\|L(h)\|_{C(K)}}{\|h\|}\leq\frac{|L(g)(x_{0})|}{\|g\|}\leq\frac{\|L(g)\|_{C_{\mathbb{R}}(K)}}{\|g\|}.

(2.6)

Thus (
2.5
) is established.
∎

In particular, a linear operator
in Lemma
2.3
can be replaced
by a linear functional; in this case
the norms
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{C(K)}
and

\left\|\cdot\right\|_{C_{\mathbb{R}}(K)}
in (
2.5
)
and (
2.6
)
can be replaced by
|\cdot|
.
Special cases of Lemma
2.3

for differential operators
L

and for various
sets
B
of EFETs and
algebraic or trigonometric polynomials
were discussed in

[
47
, p. 567]
,
[
25
, p. 30]
,

[
30
, Theorem 1.1]
,

[
29
, Remark 4.2]
, and others.
In the following two lemmas we apply Lemma
2.3

to algebraic polynomials and special
linear functionals
L
.
Two properties of the Chebyshev polynomial are discussed
below.

Lemma 2.4
.

(a) For any
u\in{\mathbb{R}}^{1}\setminus[-b,b],\,b>0
,
and
P_{n}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{n}
,

\left|P_{n}^{(l)}(u)\right|\leq b^{-l}\left|T_{n}^{(l)}(u/b)\right|\left\|P_{n}\right\|_{C([-b,b])},\qquad l=0,\ldots,n.

(2.7)

(b) For any
u\geq 1
and
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

T_{n}(u)\leq 2^{n-1}u^{n}.

(2.8)

Proof.

Statement (a) is well-known for
polynomials with real coefficients and
b=1

(see, e.g.,
[
49
, Eqn. (2.37)]
).
Inequality (
2.7
) for a real-valued
P_{n}

follows from this case by a linear substitution.
If
P_{n}
is a complex-valued polynomial,
then (
2.7
) follows
from Lemma
2.3
for

K=[-b,b],\,B={\mathcal{P}}_{n},\,\|\cdot\|=\|\cdot\|_{C([-b,b])}
, and
the linear functional

L(h)(y):=h^{(l)}(u)
, where
h\in{\mathcal{P}}_{n},\,y\in[-b,b]
, and

u\in{\mathbb{R}}^{1}\setminus[-b,b]
is a fixed point.

To prove (
2.8
), we set

u=1/\sin{\beta},\,{\beta}\in(0,\pi/2]
. Then
for
n\geq 1
,

\displaystyle 2u^{-n}T_{n}(u)=(1+\cos{\beta})^{n}+(1-\cos{\beta})^{n}=2^{n}\left(\cos^{2n}({\beta}/2)+\sin^{2n}({\beta}/2)\right)\leq 2^{n}.

∎

Two multivariate versions of Lemma
2.4
(a)
are presented
in the following lemma.

Lemma 2.5
.

(a) For any
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\setminus V
and
P\in{\mathcal{P}}_{n,m}
,

\left|P(x)\right|\leq T_{n}\left(\frac{2|x|}{w(V)}\right)\left\|P\right\|_{C(V)}.

(2.9)

(b) For any
{\lambda}>0,\,k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}_{+},\,x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
with

\left|x_{1}\right|>{\lambda},\ldots,\left|x_{m}\right|>{\lambda}
,
and
P\in{\mathcal{Q}}_{n,m}
,

\left|D^{k}P(x)\right|\leq{\lambda}^{-\langle k\rangle}\left|T_{n}^{\left(k_{1}\right)}\left(x_{1}/{\lambda}\right)\ldots T_{n}^{\left(k_{m}\right)}\left(x_{m}/{\lambda}\right)\right|\left\|P\right\|_{C\left(Q^{m}_{\lambda}\right)}.

(2.10)

Proof.

(a) The restriction of
P
to any straight line
{\mathcal{L}}
,
passing through the origin,
is a univariate polynomial
P_{n}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{n}
.
Then applying
(
2.7
) for
l=0
to
P_{n}
, we have

\displaystyle\left|P(x)\right|\leq\max\left\{\left|P_{n}(|x|)\right|,\left|P_{n}(-|x|)\right|\right\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|T_{n}\left(\frac{\pm 2|x|}{|V\cap{\mathcal{L}}|_{1}}\right)\right|\left\|P_{n}\right\|_{C(V\cap{\mathcal{L}})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle T_{n}\left(\frac{2|x|}{w(V)}\right)\left\|P\right\|_{C(V)}.

Thus (
2.9
) holds.

(b) We first apply
Lemma
2.3
for

K=Q^{m}_{\lambda},\,B={\mathcal{Q}}_{n,m},\,\|\cdot\|=\|\cdot\|_{C\left(Q^{m}_{\lambda}\right)}
, and
the linear functional

L(h)(y):=D^{k}h(x)
,
where
h\in{\mathcal{Q}}_{n,m},\,y\in Q^{m}_{\lambda}
, and
a fixed point
x
satisfies the conditions of statement (b).
Therefore, it suffices to prove (
2.10
) for real-valued
polynomials
P
.

To prove the statement for real-valued polynomials,
we need the following proposition
whose proof by induction is simple and left as an exercise to the reader.

Proposition 2.6
.

If
{\varphi}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{n}
is a polynomial with real coefficients
and all zeros of
{\varphi}
lie in
(-1,1)
, then
for any
u>1
and any

d=0,\ldots,n,\,\mathrm{sgn}\,{\varphi}^{(d)}(u)=\mathrm{sgn}\,{\varphi}(1)
.

Setting
{\lambda}=1
for simplicity, we prove statement (b) by contradiction.
If the statement is invalid, then there exist a multi-index

k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}_{+}\cap Q^{m}_{n}
, a point

x^{*}=\left(x_{1}^{*},\ldots,x_{m}^{*}\right)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}

with
x_{1}^{*}>1,\ldots,x_{m}^{*}>1
, and
a polynomial
P^{*}\in{\mathcal{Q}}_{n,m}

with
\left\|P^{*}\right\|_{C\left(Q^{m}_{1}\right)}\leq 1

such that

D^{k}P^{*}(x^{*})=MT_{n}^{\left(k_{1}\right)}\left(x_{1}^{*}\right)\ldots T_{n}^{\left(k_{m}\right)}\left(x_{m}^{*}\right),

(2.11)

where
M>1
.
Let us set

U(x):=P^{*}(x)-MT_{n}(x_{1})\ldots T_{n}(x_{m}),\qquad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}.

(2.12)

Then
U\in{\mathcal{Q}}_{n,m}
and

D^{k}U(x^{*})=0

(2.13)

by (
2.11
).
We prove below that

D^{k}U(x^{*})<0

(2.14)

which contradicts (
2.13
).

To prove (
2.14
), we first note that for all

l\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}_{+}\cap Q^{m}_{n}
,

U\left(\cos\frac{l_{1}\pi}{n},\ldots,\cos\frac{l_{m}\pi}{n}\right)=P^{*}\left(\cos\frac{l_{1}\pi}{n},\ldots,\cos\frac{l_{m}\pi}{n}\right)+(-1)^{1+\sum_{j=1}^{m}l_{j}}M

(2.15)

by (
2.12
).
Then the polynomial

{\varphi}_{0,n}(x_{1}):=U\left(x_{1},\cos\frac{l_{2}\pi}{n},\ldots,\cos\frac{l_{m}\pi}{n}\right)

belongs to
{\mathcal{P}}_{n}
and satisfies the condition

\mathrm{sgn}\left({\varphi}_{0,n}\left(\cos\frac{l_{1}\pi}{n}\right)\right)=(-1)^{1+\sum_{j=1}^{m}l_{j}}

by (
2.15
), since
\left\|P^{*}\right\|_{C(Q^{m}_{1})}\leq 1
and

M>1
.
Therefore,
{\varphi}_{0,n}
changes its signs at points

x_{1}=\cos\frac{l_{1}\pi}{n},\,0\leq l_{1}\leq n
,
and
\mathrm{sgn}\left({\varphi}_{0,n}\left(1\right)\right)=(-1)^{1+\sum_{j=2}^{m}l_{j}}
.
Moreover, since all zeros of
{\varphi}_{0,n}
lie in
(-1,1)
, we see that

\mathrm{sgn}\left({\varphi}^{(k_{1})}_{0,n}\left(x_{1}^{*}\right)\right)=(-1)^{1+\sum_{j=2}^{m}l_{j}}
by Proposition
2.6
.
Thus

\mathrm{sgn}\left(\frac{\partial^{k_{1}}}{\partial x_{1}^{k_{1}}}U\left(x_{1}^{*},\cos\frac{l_{2}\pi}{n},\ldots,\cos\frac{l_{m}\pi}{n}\right)\right)=(-1)^{1+\sum_{j=2}^{m}l_{j}}.

(2.16)

Assume that for a fixed
p\in{\mathbb{N}},\,1\leq p\leq m-1
,

\mathrm{sgn}\left(D^{k}U\left(x_{1}^{*},\ldots,x_{p}^{*},\cos\frac{l_{p+1}\pi}{n},\ldots,\cos\frac{l_{m}\pi}{n}\right)\right)=(-1)^{1+\sum_{j=p+1}^{m}l_{j}}.

(2.17)

For
p=1
(
2.17
) is valid by (
2.16
).
Then assumption (
2.17
) shows that
the polynomial

\displaystyle{\varphi}_{p,n}\left(x_{p+1}\right):=D^{k}U\left(x_{1}^{*},\ldots,x_{p}^{*},x_{p+1},\cos\frac{l_{p+2}\pi}{n},\ldots,\cos\frac{l_{m}\pi}{n}\right)

from
{\mathcal{P}}_{n}

changes its signs at points

x_{p+1}=\cos\frac{l_{p+1}\pi}{n},\,0\leq l_{p+1}\leq n
,
and

\mathrm{sgn}\left({\varphi}_{p,n}\left(1\right)\right)=\linebreak(-1)^{1+\sum_{j=p+2}^{m}l_{j}}.

Moreover, since all zeros of
{\varphi}_{p,n}
lie in
(-1,1)
, we see that

\mathrm{sgn}\left({\varphi}^{(k_{p+1})}_{p,n}\left(x_{p+1}^{*}\right)\right)=\linebreak(-1)^{1+\sum_{j=p+2}^{m}l_{j}}

by Proposition
2.6
. Therefore, (
2.17
) holds true
with
p
replaced by
p+1
. Thus by induction, (
2.17
) is valid for every
p\in{\mathbb{N}},\,1\leq p\leq m
. In particular, for
p=m
(
2.17
) is equivalent to (
2.14
).
This proves inequality (
2.10
).
∎

Different versions of Lemma
2.5
(a) were discussed by Rivlin and Shapiro

[
50
, Problem 3]
and by Brudnyi and the author
[
13
, Eqn. (
2^{\prime}
)]
.
In addition, Lemma
2.5
(b) for
k=0

was proved by Bernstein
[
7
, Theorem 2]
. The proof of statement (b)
is based on an idea from
[
7
]
.

The following property is a corollary of Lemma
2.5
(b).

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
U(y)=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}_{+}\cap Q^{m}_{n}}c_{k}\,y^{k}\in{\mathcal{Q}}_{n,m}
.

(a) If
[A,B]\subset(0,{\infty})
, then

\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}_{+}\cap Q^{m}_{n}}\left|c_{k}\right|\leq\left[T_{n}\left(\frac{B+A+2}{B-A}\right)\right]^{m}\|U\|_{C\left([A,B]^{m}\right)}.

(2.18)

(b) If
b>0
, then

\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}_{+}\cap Q^{m}_{n}}\left|c_{k}\right|\leq\left(\frac{e^{b/4}+e^{-b/4}}{e^{b/4}-e^{-b/4}}\right)^{mn}\|U\|_{C\left(\left[e^{-b},e^{b}\right]^{m}\right)}.

(2.19)

Proof.

(a) Applying Lemma
2.5
(b) to the polynomial

P(x):=U\left(\frac{B+A}{2}-x_{1},\ldots,\frac{B+A}{2}-x_{m}\right)\in{\mathcal{Q}}_{n,m}

for
{\lambda}=(B-A)/2
and
x=\left((B+A)/{2},\ldots,(B+A)/2\right)
,
we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}_{+}\cap Q^{m}_{n}}\left|c_{k}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}_{+}\cap Q^{m}_{n}}\frac{1}{\prod_{j=1}^{m}k_{j}!}\left|D^{k}P(x)\right|_{x_{1}=\ldots=x_{m}=(B+A)/2}

(2.20)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}_{+}\cap Q^{m}(n)}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\frac{1}{k_{j}!}\left(\frac{B-A}{2}\right)^{-k_{j}}T_{n}^{\left(k_{j}\right)}\left(\frac{B+A}{B-A}\right)\left\|P\right\|_{C\left(Q^{m}_{(B-A)/2}\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[T_{n}\left(\frac{B+A}{B-A}+\frac{2}{B-A}\right)\right]^{m}\left\|U\right\|_{C\left([A,B]^{m}\right)}.

Note that the last equality in (
2.20
) follows from Taylor’s formula.
Thus (
2.18
) is established.

(b) Since

\displaystyle T_{n}\left(\frac{e^{b}+e^{-b}+2}{e^{b}-e^{-b}}\right)=T_{n}\left(\frac{e^{b/2}+e^{-b/2}}{e^{b/2}-e^{-b/2}}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\left(\frac{e^{b/4}+e^{-b/4}}{e^{b/4}-e^{-b/4}}\right)^{n}+\left(\frac{e^{b/4}-e^{-b/4}}{e^{b/4}+e^{-b/4}}\right)^{n}\right),

inequality (
2.19
) follows from (
2.18
).
∎

2.3.
Properties of EFETs

We first need several Bernstein-type inequalities.

Lemma 2.8
.

(a) If
f\in B_{{\sigma},m}\cap L_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{m}),\,q\in[1,{\infty}]
, then

\left\|D^{k}f\right\|_{L_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{m})}\leq{\sigma}^{\langle k\rangle}\|f\|_{L_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{m})}.

(2.21)

(b) If
f\in B_{{\sigma},m}\cap L_{q}\left({\mathbb{R}}^{m}\right),\,q\in[1,{\infty}),\,d\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and
h>0
, then

\displaystyle I_{h}(f):=\left(\sum_{r\leq\langle k\rangle\leq d+r}h^{\langle k\rangle q}\left\|D^{k}f\right\|_{L_{q}\left({\mathbb{R}}^{m}\right)}^{q}\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\binom{m+d+r}{m}-r\right)^{1/q}\max\left\{(h{\sigma})^{r},(h{\sigma})^{d+r}\right\}\|f\|_{L_{q}\left({\mathbb{R}}^{m}\right)},\qquad r=0,\,1.

(2.22)

(c) If
f\in B_{{\sigma},m}\cap L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{m})
, then

\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left|\frac{\partial f(x)}{\partial x_{j}}\right|\right\|_{L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{m})}\leq m{\sigma}\|f\|_{L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{m})}.

(2.23)

Proof.

Statement (a) is a multivariate version of Bernstein’s inequality
(see, e.g.,
[
44
, Eqn. 3.2.2(8)]
) and
(c) follows directly from (a).
Next, using (
2.21
), we have

I_{h}(f)\leq\left(\sum_{l=r}^{d+r}(h{\sigma})^{lq}\binom{m+l-1}{m-1}\right)^{1/q}\|f\|_{L_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{m})}.

(2.24)

Then statement (b) follows from (
2.24
) and the known identity

\sum_{0\leq l\leq d+r}\binom{m+l-1}{m-1}=\binom{m+d+r}{m},

(2.25)

where the right-hand side of (
2.25
) coincides with the dimension of the space
{\mathcal{P}}_{d+r,m}

(see, e.g.,
[
48
, Eqn. (3.8)]
) .
∎

Note that more general
inequalities than (
2.23
) were recently proved in

[
29
, Theorem 2.1 and Corollary 2.4]
.
Next, we discuss a certain technical discretization inequality
that plays an important role in the proof of Theorem
1.6
.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
\left\{Q_{h}^{m}\left(X_{\nu}\right)\right\}_{\nu=1}^{\infty}

be a family of closed cubes with the pairwise disjoint interiors
and let
Y_{\nu}\in Q_{h}^{m}\left(X_{\nu}\right),\,\nu\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
In addition, let
d
be defined by (
1.17
).
If
f\in B_{{\sigma},m}\cap L_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{m}),\,q\in[1,{\infty})
, then

\left|\left(\sum_{\nu=1}^{\infty}\left|f\left(X_{\nu}\right)\right|^{q}\right)^{1/q}-\left(\sum_{\nu=1}^{\infty}\left|f\left(Y_{\nu}\right)\right|^{q}\right)^{1/q}\right|\leq C(m,q)h^{-m/q}\max\left\{h{\sigma},(h{\sigma})^{d}\right\}\|f\|_{L_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{m})}.

(2.26)

Proof.

We first prove
two estimates for the modulus of continuity
of differentiable functions on a cube.
Let
F
be a
d+1
times continuously differentiable
function on
Q^{m}_{h}\left(X_{0}\right)
, where
X_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
.
Then for any

X\in Q^{m}_{h}\left(X_{0}\right)
and
Y\in Q^{m}_{h}\left(X_{0}\right)
,
the following estimate is valid
by the Sobolev embedding theorem with
dq>m
(see, e.g.,
[
1
, Theorem 5.4, Eqn. (8)]
):

\left|F(X)-F(Y)\right|\leq 2\|F\|_{C\left(Q^{m}_{h}\left(X_{0}\right)\right)}\leq C(m,q)h^{-m/q}\left(\sum_{0\leq\langle k\rangle\leq d}h^{\langle k\rangle q}\left\|D^{k}F\right\|_{L_{q}\left(Q^{m}_{h}\left(X_{0}\right)\right)}^{q}\right)^{1/q}.

(2.27)

On the other hand, using again the embedding theorem, we have

\displaystyle\left|F(X)-F(Y)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{m}|X-Y|\sup_{\langle k\rangle=1}\left\|D^{k}F\right\|_{C\left(Q^{m}_{h}\left(X_{0}\right)\right)}

(2.28)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(m,q)h^{-m/q}\left(\sum_{1\leq\langle k\rangle\leq d+1}h^{\langle k\rangle q}\left\|D^{k}F\right\|_{L_{q}\left(Q^{m}_{h}\left(X_{0}\right)\right)}^{q}\right)^{1/q}.

Next, using Minkowski’s inequality for sums and estimates (
2.27
) and (
2.28
)
for
F=f
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\left(\sum_{\nu=1}^{\infty}\left|f\left(X_{\nu}\right)\right|^{q}\right)^{1/q}-\left(\sum_{\nu=1}^{\infty}\left|f\left(Y_{\nu}\right)\right|^{q}\right)^{1/q}\right|\leq\left(\sum_{\nu=1}^{\infty}\left|f\left(X_{\nu}\right)-f\left(Y_{\nu}\right)\right|^{q}\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle\leq C(m,q)h^{-m/q}\min_{r\in\{0,1\}}\left(\sum_{r\leq\langle k\rangle\leq d+r}h^{\langle k\rangle q}\left\|D^{k}F\right\|_{L_{q}\left({\mathbb{R}}^{m}\right)}^{q}\right)^{1/q}.

(2.29)

Finally, inequality (
2.26
) follows from (
2.29
) and
Bernstein-type inequality (
2.22
).
∎

3.
Approximation of EFETs by Polynomials and Entire Functions

In this section we discuss approximation of univariate EFETs by polynomials
on compacts from
{\mathbb{C}}
and approximation of multivariate EFETs by polynomials
on the octahedron and the cube. In addition, we also study unconventional approximation of
EFETs by other EFETs.

3.1.
Approximation of EFETs by Polynomials

Approximation of EFETs by algebraic polynomials was
initiated by Bernstein
[
5
,
6
]
and independently
it was
discussed by Logvinenko
[
37
,
38
]
.
Various univariate and multivariate versions
of these results were obtained
by the author
[
26
,
27
,
28
]
. Most of
approximation theorems from these publications have discussed
EFETs either bounded or of polynomial growth on
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
.
However, in this paper we need estimates of univariate and
mutivariate polynomial approximation
for general EFETs. Some of these results are based on
estimates of Chebyshev coefficients.

3.1.1.
Estimates of Chebyshev coefficients

Let
\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}_{+}}c_{k,m}(f,b)\prod_{j=1}^{m}T_{k_{j}}\left(x_{j}/b\right),\,x\in Q^{m}_{b},\,b>0,

be the multivariate Fourier-Chebyshev series of a function

f\in L_{\infty}\left(Q^{m}_{b}\right)
with the coefficients

\displaystyle c_{k,m}(f,b)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\frac{(2/\pi)^{m}}{2^{r_{m}(k)}}\mathop{\text{\LARGE$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle Q^{m}_{b}$}}$}}\nolimits f(x)\prod_{j=1}^{m}\frac{T_{k_{j}}\left(x_{j}/b\right)}{\sqrt{b^{2}-x_{j}^{2}}}dx

(3.1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(2/\pi)^{m}}{2^{r_{m}(k)}}\int_{[0,\pi]^{m}}f\left(b\cos t_{1},\ldots,b\cos t_{m}\right)\prod_{j=1}^{m}\cos k_{j}t_{j}\,dt,\qquad k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}_{+}.

Here,
r_{m}(k)
is the number of zero components in a vector
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}_{+}
.

In addition, let

\Gamma_{R}^{m}(b):=\left\{w=x+iy\in{\mathbb{C}}^{m}:\left(\frac{x_{j}}{R/b+b/R}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{y_{j}}{R/b-b/R}\right)^{2}\leq(b/2)^{2},\,1\leq j\leq m\right\}

(3.2)

be the direct product of the sets encircled by the corresponding ellipses
in
{\mathbb{C}}^{1}
with foci at the ends of
[-b,b]
and with the sum of its semi-axes
equal to
R>b
.

Lemma 3.1
.

If
f
is a holomorphic and bounded function on the interior of
\Gamma_{R}^{m}(b)
,
then

\left|c_{k,m}(f,b)\right|\leq 2^{m-r_{m}(k)}(b/R)^{\langle k\rangle}\|f\|_{L_{\infty}\left(\Gamma_{R}^{m}(b)\right)},\qquad k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}_{+}.

(3.3)

Proof.

Assume for simplicity that
b=1
, and let
f
satisfy the conditions of the lemma.
Note that the univariate estimate

\left|c_{k_{1},1}(f,1)\right|\leq 2^{1-r_{1}(k_{1})}R^{-k_{1}}\|f\|_{L_{\infty}\left(\Gamma_{R}^{1}(1)\right)},\qquad k_{1}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{1}_{+},

(3.4)

is well known (see
[
4
, Sect. 2.1, Lemma 1]
and
[
58
, Sect. 3.7.3]
).
Let us set

\displaystyle{\varphi}_{l}\left(z_{1},\ldots,z_{l}\right):=f\left(z_{1},\ldots,z_{l},z_{l+1},\ldots,z_{m}\right)

with fixed parameters
z_{l+1},\ldots,z_{m},\,1\leq l\leq m
.
To prove the lemma, it suffices to establish the inequality

\left|c_{k,p}\left({\varphi}_{p},1\right)\right|\leq 2^{p-r_{p}(k)}R^{-\langle k\rangle}\left\|{\varphi}_{p}\right\|_{L_{\infty}\left(\Gamma_{R}^{p}(1)\right)},\qquad k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{p}_{+},\quad 1\leq p\leq m,

(3.5)

by induction in
p
. For
p=1
(
3.5
) follows from (
3.4
). Next, assume
that (
3.5
) is valid for
p=l,\,k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{l}_{+},\,1\leq l\leq m-1
.
Since
c_{k,l}\left({\varphi}_{l},1\right)

holomorphic and bounded function in
z_{l+1}
on the interior of
\Gamma_{R}^{1}(1)
,
we can use (
3.4
) and also (
3.5
) for
p=l
to obtain
the following relations for
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{l+1}_{+}
and
k(l):=\left(k_{1},\ldots,k_{l}\right)
,

\displaystyle\left|c_{k,l+1}\left({\varphi}_{l+1},1\right)\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\mathop{\text{\LARGE$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\kern-2.04861pt-1$}}^{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle 1$}}$}}\nolimits\frac{c_{k(l),l}\left({\varphi}_{l},1\right)T_{k_{l+1}}(x_{l+1})}{\sqrt{1-x^{2}_{l+1}}}\,dx_{l+1}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{1-r_{1}\left(k_{l+1}\right)}R^{-k_{l+1}}\sup_{z_{l+1}\in\Gamma_{R}^{1}(1)}\left|c_{k_{(l)},l}\left({\varphi}_{l},1\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{l+1-r_{l+1}(k)}R^{-\langle k\rangle}\sup_{z_{l+1}\in\Gamma_{R}^{1}(1)}\left\|{\varphi}_{l}\right\|_{L_{\infty}\left(\Gamma_{R}^{l}(1)\right)}.

Therefore, (
3.5
) holds true for
p=l+1
. Thus (
3.3
) is valid.
∎

3.1.2.
Univariate approximation.

Here, we discuss univariate approximation on a symmetric (with respect to the origin)
compact
K\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{1}
.

Let
z(w)=w+\sqrt{w^{2}-1}
denote the conformal map of the ellipse
\Gamma_{R}^{1}(1)

defined in (
3.2
) onto the circle
\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}^{1}:|z|=R\}
.
Let us set

{\alpha}={\alpha}(K):=\max_{w\in K}\left|w+\sqrt{w^{2}-1}\right|=\min_{R\in(1,{\infty})}\{R:K\subseteq\Gamma^{1}_{R}(1)\}=\left|w_{0}+\sqrt{w_{0}^{2}-1}\right|,

(3.6)

where
w_{0}\in K\cap\Gamma^{1}_{R_{0}}(1)
and
R_{0}
is the extremal value in (
3.6
).
Since
K
is symmetric, the next estimate immediately follows from (
3.6
) and (
1.3
):

\max_{w\in bK}|T_{k}(w/b)|\leq{\alpha}^{k}.

(3.7)

In addition, let us set

\displaystyle\psi({\gamma},K):=\frac{\sqrt{1+{\gamma}^{2}}}{{\gamma}}-\log\left(\frac{{\gamma}+\sqrt{1+{\gamma}^{2}}}{{\alpha}}\right)=\psi({\gamma})+\log{\alpha},\qquad{\gamma}\in(0,{\infty}),

where
\psi({\gamma})=\psi({\gamma},[-1,1])
is defined by (
1.26
).
Since
\psi
is a strictly decreasing function in
{\gamma}

on
(0,{\infty})
, there exists the unique solution

{\gamma}_{0}={\gamma}_{0}({\alpha})\in(0,{\infty})
to the equation
\psi({\gamma},K)=0
, and, in addition,

\psi({\gamma},K)<0
for
{\gamma}>{\gamma}_{0}
and
{\gamma}_{0}+\sqrt{1+{\gamma}_{0}^{2}}>{\alpha}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Given
{\sigma}>0,\,b>0,
and
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
let us denote

\tau:=\frac{n}{{\sigma}b}
.
In addition, let

f\in B_{\sigma}
satisfy the condition

|f(\xi)|\leq Ae^{{\sigma}|\xi|},\qquad\xi\in{\mathbb{C}}^{1},

(3.8)

where
A>0
is a constant.
If
\tau>{\gamma}_{0}({\alpha})
, then there exists a polynomial
U_{n}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{n}
such that

\max_{w\in bK}\left|f(w)-U_{n}(w)\right|=\max_{w\in\frac{n}{{\sigma}\tau}K}\left|f(w)-U_{n}(w)\right|\leq C(K)Ae^{n\psi(\tau,K)}.

(3.9)

Proof.

It follows from (
3.8
) that for any
{\delta}>0
,

|f(w)|\leq Ae^{{\sigma}b\sqrt{1+{\delta}^{2}}},\qquad w\in\Gamma^{1}_{R}(b),

(3.10)

where
R=b\left({\delta}+\sqrt{1+{\delta}^{2}}\right)
.

Next, following Bernstein
[
6
]
(see also
[
58
, Sect. 5.4.4]
),
we approximate
f
by the partial Fourier-Chebyshev sum

U_{n}(w):=\sum_{k=0}^{n}c_{k,1}T_{k}(w/b)
, where

c_{k,1}=c_{k,1}(f,b),\,k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{1}_{+}
, is defined
by (
3.1
).
Then the estimate

\left|c_{k,1}\right|\leq\frac{2Ae^{{\sigma}b\sqrt{1+{\delta}^{2}}}}{\left({\delta}+\sqrt{1+{\delta}^{2}}\right)^{k}},\qquad k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{1}_{+},

(3.11)

follows from (
3.10
) and (
3.3
) for
m=1
(see also (
3.4
)).

Since
f
is an entire function, we see that

f(w)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}c_{k,1}T_{k}(w/b)
for
w\in\Gamma_{R}^{1}(b)

(see
[
56
, Theorem 9.1.1]
).
Therefore, if
{\alpha}<{\delta}+\sqrt{1+{\delta}^{2}}
, then using (
3.11
) and (
3.7
), we obtain

\displaystyle\max_{w\in bK}\left|f(w)-U_{n}(w)\right|\leq\sum_{k=n+1}^{\infty}\left|c_{k,1}\right|{\alpha}^{k}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2A}{1-{\alpha}/({\delta}+\sqrt{1+{\delta}^{2}})}\exp\left[{\sigma}b\sqrt{1+{\delta}^{2}}-n\log\left(\frac{{\delta}+\sqrt{1+{\delta}^{2}}}{{\alpha}}\right)\right].

(3.12)

Note that if
{\delta}=\tau=\frac{n}{{\sigma}b}>{\gamma}_{0}({\alpha})
, then

{\alpha}<{\gamma}_{0}+\sqrt{1+{\gamma}_{0}^{2}}<{\delta}+\sqrt{1+{\delta}^{2}}
. Then choosing

{\delta}=\tau
in (
3.12
), we arrive at (
3.9
) with

\displaystyle C(K)\leq\frac{2}{1-{\alpha}/\left({\gamma}_{0}+\sqrt{1+{\gamma}_{0}^{2}}\right)}.

∎

Examples of
K,\,{\alpha},\,w_{0}
, and
{\gamma}_{0}({\alpha})
are given below.

Example 3.3
.

(a)
K=[-1,1],\,{\alpha}=1,\,{\gamma}_{0}({\alpha})=1.5088\ldots
;

(b)
K=\Gamma_{R}^{1}(1),\,{\alpha}=R;

(c)
K=\left\{w\in{\mathbb{C}}^{1}:|w|\leq M\right\},\,{\alpha}=M+\sqrt{M^{2}+1},\,w_{0}=iM
;

(d)
K=\left\{w\in{\mathbb{C}}^{1}:|w|\leq 1\right\},\,{\alpha}=1+\sqrt{2},\,{\gamma}_{0}({\alpha})=3.3541\ldots;

(e)

.

Example
3.3
(a) is trivial, while examples (b), (c), (d), and (e)
follow from relations (
3.6
).

Remark 3.4
.

3.2
will be used in the following two situations:

is independent of

and

is proportional to

.

is independent of

and

from Example

(a),

]

with the Taylor polynomial

and an integer

, while inequality
(

) is valid only for

.
The author
[

3.2

for

and
the square

from Example

(e).

.

,

, and a fixed

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to{\infty}}\left(\inf_{U_{n}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{n}}\max_{w\in\left[-\frac{n}{{\sigma}\tau},\frac{n}{{\sigma}\tau}\right]}\left|e^{{\sigma}w}-U_{n}(w)\right|\right)^{1/n}=e^{\psi(\tau)}.

),

.

3.1.3.
Multivariate approximation.

Here, we discuss two multivariate versions of
Lemma
3.2
.
The first one is an extension of Case 1 for polynomials
from
{\mathcal{P}}_{n,m}

and the second one is an extension of Case 2 for
polynomials from

{\mathcal{Q}}_{n,m}
.

Lemma 3.6
.

For
f\in B_{{\sigma},m}
and for a fixed
\tau\geq 4
there exists
a polynomial

P_{n}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{n,m}
such that

\left\|f-P_{n}\right\|_{L_{\infty}\left((n/\tau)O_{1/{\sigma}}^{m}\right)}\leq C_{4}e^{-an},

(3.13)

where
C_{4}=C_{4}(f,\tau,{\sigma},m)
and
a=a(\tau)>0
are
independent of
n
.

Proof.

First, let us set

W:=\left\{x+iy\in{\mathbb{C}}^{m}:x\in Q_{\sigma}^{m},y\in Q_{\sigma}^{m}\right\}
.
In addition, let
U_{n}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{n}
be a polynomial from
Lemma
3.2

for
f(\xi)=e^{\xi}
, and let
K_{1}
be the square from
Example
3.3
(e).

Then it follows from (
3.9
) and Example
3.3

(e) that for any

{\varepsilon}>0
and
\tau/(1+{\varepsilon})>3.9896\ldots
, the following
inequalities hold:

\displaystyle\max_{t\in(n/\tau)O_{1/{\sigma}}^{m}}\max_{w\in(1+{\varepsilon})W}\left|e^{(t,w)}-U_{n}((t,w))\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max_{\xi\in((1+{\varepsilon})n/\tau)K_{1}}\left|e^{\xi}-U_{n}(\xi)\right|

(3.14)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(K_{1})e^{n\psi(\tau/(1+{\varepsilon}),K_{1})}.

Next, for any
z=x+iy\in{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
,

\displaystyle{\sigma}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left|z_{j}\right|\leq{\sigma}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left(\left|x_{j}\right|+\left|y_{j}\right|\right)=\sup_{w\in W}\mathrm{Re}(z,w):=H_{W}(z).

Lemma 3.7

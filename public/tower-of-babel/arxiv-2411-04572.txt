A notion of homotopy for directed graphs and their flag complexes
Thomas Chaplin, Heather A. Harrington, Ulrike Tillmann

Abstract
Directed graphs can be studied by their associated directed flag complex. The homology of this complex has been successful in applications as a topological invariant for digraphs. Through comparison with path homology theory, we derive a homotopy-like equivalence relation on digraph maps such that equivalent maps induce identical maps on the homology of the directed flag complex. Thus, we obtain an equivalence relation on digraphs such that equivalent digraphs have directed flag complexes with isomorphic homology. With the help of these relations, we can prove a generic stability theorem for the persistent homology of the directed flag complex of filtered digraphs. In particular, we show that the persistent homology of the directed flag complex of the shortest-path filtration of a weighted directed acyclic graph is stable to edge subdivision. In contrast, we also discuss some important instabilities that are not present in persistent path homology. We also derive similar equivalence relations for ordered simplicial complexes at large. Since such complexes can alternatively be viewed as simplicial sets, we verify that these two perspectives yield identical relations.

Introduction

Digraphs appear naturally in numerous applications domains, including neuroscience
[
28
]
, traffic network analysis
[
3
]
and social network analysis
[
27
]
.
Frequently these digraphs support a dynamical system such as brain activity, traffic flow and (dis)information spread.
A common hypothesis is that the structure of the digraph is critical in determining the evolution of the dynamical system.
In order to assess this hypothesis, there is a need for interpretable and informative summaries of digraphs, that are amenable to statistical analysis.

A common approach is to associate a combinatorial object to the digraph
built out of substructures in the digraph which are relevant to the application in hand.
For example, one could study the set of all paths
[
18
]
, all tournaments
[
16
]
or all directed cliques
[
26
]
in the digraph. The interest to us is that each of these combinatorial objects carries sufficient structure to build a chain complex,
the homology of which can be used as an algebraic invariant of the underlying digraph.
These invariants are most useful (such as in the context of persistence) when they are functorial, in the sense that there is a class of digraph maps which induce maps on homology.

In order to assess the discriminative power of these homologies and hence their utility in applications, some natural questions arise:
When do two digraphs give rise to isomorphic homology?
When do two digraph maps induce
the same
maps on homology?

In this work, we make progress towards these two questions in the case of the directed flag complex.
This is a simplicial set in which the
k
-simplies are the directed
(k+1)
-cliques, i.e.

(k+1)
-tuples of distinct vertices
v_{0}\dots v_{k}
such that there is an edge
v_{i}\to v_{j}
whenever
i<j
.
We focus on the directed flag complex because it has seen particular success in the field of neuroscience. The homology of the directed flag complex was proposed as a digraph invariant by
[
26
]

[
26
]

and, shortly thereafter,
the Betti numbers of the complex
associated to the activation graph of numerically simulated neocortical microcircuitry
was shown to
exhibit significant temporal patterns in response to stimuli
[
28
]
.
Further applications may be enabled by the incorporation of persistence,
and the presence of highly performant software for its computation
[
25
]
,
which can be used to capture the topological organisation of directed structures, across a range of scales
[
5
]
.

In contrast, the directed flag complex has received comparatively little theoretical treatment.
Here, we describe an equivalence relation
\simeq_{\dFl}
for digraph maps such that if two maps are
\simeq_{\dFl}
-equivalent then they induce identical maps on the homology of the directed flag complex.
In particular, this induces an equivalence relation
\simeq_{\dFl}
on digraphs themselves, such that if two digraphs are
\simeq_{\dFl}
-equivalent then their directed flag complexes have isomorphic homology.
To illustrate the utility of these relations in the context of persistence, we use the relation
\simeq_{\dFl}
to show that the persistent homology of the directed flag complex applied to a filtration of digraphs is stable in certain circumstances.

There are numerous homotopy theories for both directed and undirected graphs.
The relation we will develop is primarily derived from path homotopy theory, which was introduced by
[
19
]
in a series of papers
[
17
,
18
,
19
]
.
Initially developed for digraphs, path homotopy generalises a prior homotopy theory for undirected graphs
[
1
,
2
]
, viewing undirected graphs as a full subcategory of digraphs.
This theory was further generalised to path complexes in later work
[
20
]
.

Summary

Given a digraph
G
, we denote the associated directed flag complex by
\dFl(G)
.
The directed flag complex is a particular example of a more general class of objects called ordered simplicial complexes (Definition
2.4
).
In this work, we primarily view an ordered simplicial complex as a special case of a regular path complex (Definition
2.3
).
The latter were first introduced in
[
18
]
as an invariant of directed graphs.
To any regular path complex one can associate a chain complex and moreover this can be made into a functor

\Omega:\bm{\mathrm{WkRPC}}\to\bm{\mathrm{Ch}}
,
where
\bm{\mathrm{WkRPC}}
is a category of regular path complexes and
\bm{\mathrm{Ch}}
is the category of chain complexes of vector spaces over some background field.
Taking the full subcategory of
\bm{\mathrm{WkRPC}}
, in which objects are restricted to ordered simplicial complexes, yields a category
\bm{\mathrm{TcOSC}}
.
We fully characterise the morphisms in this category as weak simplicial morphisms that never map a directed
3
-clique to a reciprocal pair of edges (Lemma
2.10
).
Pre-composing with the functor which takes digraphs to their directed flag complex, we obtain a sequence of functors

\bm{\mathrm{TcDgr}}\xrightarrow{\dFl}\bm{\mathrm{TcOSC}}\xhookrightarrow{\iota}\bm{\mathrm{WkRPC}}\xrightarrow{\Omega}\bm{\mathrm{Ch}}

(1.1)

where the morphisms in
\bm{\mathrm{TcDgr}}
are defined so that
\dFl
becomes a full and faithful functor. We show that this is a factorisation of the chain complex of
\dFl(G)
(Lemma
2.24
).
This factorisation is the main perspective through which we construct our equivalence relation,
\simeq_{\dFl}
, by comparison with a similar relation for the well-studided category
\bm{\mathrm{WkRPC}}
.

In particular, there is a notion of homotopy equivalence,
\simeq
, between the morphisms of
\bm{\mathrm{WkRPC}}
such that if
f\simeq g
then
\Omega(f)
and
\Omega(g)
are chain homotopic (see
[
20
]
).
The equivalence relation for digraph maps that we will construct is a pull-back of this equivalence relation, i.e.

f\simeq_{\dFl}g\iff(\iota\circ\dFl)(f)\simeq(\iota\circ\dFl)(g).

(1.2)

Our main contribution is an ‘intrinsic’ characterisation of this pull-back (Section
5.2
).

To be more precise,
[
20
]
describes a system of one-step homotopies between morphisms in
\bm{\mathrm{WkRPC}}
.
This is essentially a binary relation on the morphisms of
\bm{\mathrm{WkRPC}}
, which is then completed to the equivalence relation
\simeq
.
As above, we can pull back this binary relation to one for the morphisms of
\bm{\mathrm{TcDgr}}
.
Given two morphisms
f,g\in\bm{\mathrm{TcDgr}}
,
we can provide simple, edge-based conditions that are equivalent to the pair
(f,g)
belonging to this pull-back binary relation (Corollary

).
In particular, we relate
(f,g)
if

1.

x\mathbin{\hbox{\hskip 3.8889pt\hskip-2.5pt\hbox{\hbox{\hskip 2.5pt\hskip 0.0pt\hbox{}\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-2.5pt\raisebox{0.5pt}{\hbox{$\to$}}\hskip-2.5pt\hskip 2.5pt}}\hskip-2.5pt\hskip-3.8889pt\raisebox{-4.16875pt}{\hbox{$=$}}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip 3.8889pt}}y\implies f(x)\mathbin{\hbox{\hskip 3.8889pt\hskip-2.5pt\hbox{\hbox{\hskip 2.5pt\hskip 0.0pt\hbox{}\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-2.5pt\raisebox{0.5pt}{\hbox{$\to$}}\hskip-2.5pt\hskip 2.5pt}}\hskip-2.5pt\hskip-3.8889pt\raisebox{-4.16875pt}{\hbox{$=$}}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip 3.8889pt}}g(y)
; and

2.

x\to y
and
f(x)=g(y)

\implies

f(x)=f(y)=g(x)=g(y)
,

where
\mathbin{\hbox{\hskip 3.8889pt\hskip-2.5pt\hbox{\hbox{\hskip 2.5pt\hskip 0.0pt\hbox{}\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-2.5pt\raisebox{0.5pt}{\hbox{$\to$}}\hskip-2.5pt\hskip 2.5pt}}\hskip-2.5pt\hskip-3.8889pt\raisebox{-4.16875pt}{\hbox{$=$}}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip 3.8889pt}}
indicates that either the vertices coincide or are joined by a directed edge.
The relation
\simeq_{\dFl}
can then be constructed by completing this to an equivalence relation.

As a first step, in Section
4
, we study the pull-back binary relation for
\bm{\mathrm{TcOSC}}
along the inclusion functor
\iota:\bm{\mathrm{TcOSC}}\hookrightarrow\bm{\mathrm{WkRPC}}
.
Again, we aim to describe the resulting relation ‘intrinsically’ with the category
\bm{\mathrm{TcOSC}}
.
Corollary
4.9
achieves this by
describing related morphisms in terms of a cylinder functor for this category.
For an alternative view,
\bm{\mathrm{TcOSC}}
can also be fully and faithfully embedded in the category of simplicial sets, which itself has a notion of homotopy equivalence.
In Section
4.3
, we verify that the pull-back relation along this embedding coincides with the pull-back along
\iota
.

Finally, in Section
6
, we employ the equivalence relation
\simeq_{\dFl}
to obtain stability results for the persistent homology of the directed flag complex of a filtration of digraphs.
Typically, such stability results take the form of a bound on the interleaving distance.
The relation
\simeq_{\dFl}
allow us to construct interleavings of digraph filtrations up to
\simeq_{\dFl}
, facilitating the derivation of such bounds.
We summarise this approach with Corollary
6.4
.
In Proposition
6.10
, we apply this result to the shortest-path filtration of a weighted directed acyclic graph (DAG), showing that its persistent homology is stable to edge subdivision.
However, persistent homology pipelines using the directed flag complex have some notable instabilities.
We discuss some important examples in Section
6.3
, namely edge subdivision of a non-DAG and the addition of an appendage edge.

Given a triangle-collapsing simplicial morphism

Given two simplicial complexes,

Lemma 2.10

A weak simplicial morphism

Suppose

2.2

.
We call

We denote the sets of vertices and edges by

In particular,

,
\bm{\mathrm{TcDgr}}\to\bm{\mathrm{TcOSC}}
and
\bm{\mathrm{StDgr}}\to\bm{\mathrm{StOSC}}
.

Proof.

Suppose

G\to H
.
Take any simplex

.
Then for every
i\leq j
, there is an edge

in
G
.
Since
f
is a weak digraph map
f(v_{i})\mathbin{\hbox{\hskip 3.8889pt\hskip-2.5pt\hbox{\hbox{\hskip 2.5pt\hskip 0.0pt\hbox{}\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-2.5pt\raisebox{0.5pt}{\hbox{$\to$}}\hskip-2.5pt\hskip 2.5pt}}\hskip-2.5pt\hskip-3.8889pt\raisebox{-4.16875pt}{\hbox{$=$}}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip 3.8889pt}}f(v_{j})
for every

Either these are all edges, in which case

, or there is an equality in which case

is non-simplicial.
Hence,

\dFl(G)\to\dFl(H)
and so certainly induces a weak simplicial morphism

for any

.

Now suppose
f
induces a weak simplicial morphism
\Sk_{1}(\dFl(G))\to\dFl(H)

Take an edge

Note there is a simplex

.
Hence,

G\to H

A similar argument shows the second point and the third point follows immediately from definitions.
∎

Chain complexes from regular path complexes

We now construct a chain complex which algebraically represents the combinatorial structure in a regular path complex.
This construction is due to
[
18
]

[
18
]
.
It is possible to repeat this construction for any path complex
P
, by first applying the regularisation functor,
\toreg:\bm{\mathrm{WkPathC}}\to\bm{\mathrm{WkRPC}}
.

To begin, we choose an arbitrary field
\mathbb{F}
and
let
{\color[rgb]{0.5,0,1}\bm{\mathrm{Ch}}}
denote the category of non-negatively graded chain complexes of vector spaces over
\mathbb{F}
.
Given a regular path complex
P
, generate the following free
\mathbb{F}
-vector spaces:

\displaystyle\Lambda_{k}(P)

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{F}\left\langle\{p=v_{0}\dots v_{k}\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}p\text{ is an elementary }k\text{-path on }V(P)\}\right\rangle,

(2.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{k}(P)

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{F}\left\langle\{p=v_{0}\dots v_{k}\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}p\text{ is a regular }k\text{-path on }V(P)\}\right\rangle,

(2.5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{k}(P)

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{F}\left\langle\{p=v_{0}\dots v_{k}\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}p\text{ is an irregular }k\text{-path on }V(P)\}\right\rangle,

(2.6)

\displaystyle C_{k}(P)

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{F}\left\langle\{p=v_{0}\dots v_{k}\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}p\in P\}\right\rangle.

(2.7)

We will not distinguish between paths and their corresponding generators in these free groups.
We drop
P
from notation when it is clear from context.

Remark 2.21
.

1.

The vector spaces
\Lambda_{k}(P)
,
\mathcal{R}_{k}(P)
and
\mathcal{I}_{k}(P)
each depend only on
V(P)
.

2.

There is always a direct sum decomposition
\Lambda_{k}=\mathcal{R}_{k}\oplus\mathcal{I}_{k}
.

3.

Since we only consider regular path complexes, there is a subspace relation,
C_{k}(P)\subseteq\mathcal{R}_{k}(P)
.

We can define a
non-regular boundary map
amongst the
\Lambda_{\bullet}
in the usual fashion:
given a standard basis element
v_{0}\dots v_{k}\in\Lambda_{k}
, define

\partial^{\mathrm{nr}}_{k}(v_{0}\dots v_{k}):=\sum_{i=0}^{k}(-1)^{i}v_{0}\dots\hat{v_{i}}\dots v_{k}

(2.8)

and then extend linearly to a map
\partial^{\mathrm{nr}}_{k}:\Lambda_{k}\to\Lambda_{k-1}
.
As usual,
\partial^{\mathrm{nr}}_{k-1}\circ\partial^{\mathrm{nr}}_{k}=0

[
18
, Lemma 2.3]
.

Next, once can verify that
\partial_{k}(\mathcal{I}_{k})\subseteq\mathcal{I}_{k-1}

[
18
, Lemma 2.9]
and hence
\partial^{\mathrm{nr}}_{k}
passes to the quotient to define the
regular boundary map

\partial_{k}:\mathcal{R}_{k}\to\mathcal{R}_{k-1}
.
Intuitively, this boundary map can be computed by following the usual formula (
2.8
) and then dropping any summands corresponding to irregular paths.
The chain complex equation passes to the quotient to give
\partial_{k-1}\circ\partial_{k}=0
.

Unfortunately, this boundary map does not restrict to a map
C_{k}\to C_{k-1}
because, a priori,
P
is not a simplicial complex.
Instead, we must pass to a further subspace

\Omega_{k}(P):=\left\{p\in C_{k}(P)\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}\partial_{k}(p)\in C_{k-1}(P)\right\}.

(2.9)

Notice that the chain complex equation implies that
\partial_{k}
restricts to a boundary map
\partial_{k}:\Omega_{k}\to\Omega_{k-1}
.
Therefore,
{\{\Omega_{k},\partial_{k}\}}_{k\geq 0}
forms a chain complex, which we call the
regular chain complex of
P
.

In general, elements of
\Omega_{k}(P)
are
sums
of paths in
P
.
Indeed, there may not even be a basis of
\Omega_{k}(P)
for which each basis element is supported on a single path.
Computing a basis for
\Omega_{k}(\mathcal{A}(G))
is often non-trivial
[
12
]
, especially for

.

Theorem 2.22

(
[
19
, Theorem 2.10]
)
.

The regular chain complex of a regular path complex can be made into a functor

\Omega:\bm{\mathrm{WkRPC}}\to\bm{\mathrm{Ch}}
.

Proof.

Let

be a weak path morphism of regular path complexes.
We will define
{f}_{\#}:\Omega(P_{1})\to\Omega(P_{2})
on each component.
First we define maps

on the standard basis.
Given an arbitrary path

, let

\mathcal{R}_{k}(f)(p):=\begin{cases}f(p)&\text{if }f(p)\text{ is regular,}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

(2.10)

One can verify that this yields a chain map and moreover, since

is a weak path morphism,
\mathcal{R}_{k}(f)(\Omega_{k}(P_{1}))\subseteq\Omega_{k}(P_{2})

We let
{f}_{\#}
denote the restriction of
\mathcal{R}_{k}(f)
to
\Omega_{k}(P_{1})\to\Omega_{k}(P_{2})

The construction of
\mathcal{R}_{k}(f)
is clearly functorial and hence so is the restriction.
∎

3
Systems of one-step homotopies

All the homotopies presented herein are generated by a class of directional, one-step homotopies.
The one-step homotopies determine a binary relation on the morphisms, which is subsequently completed to an equivalence relation by identifying ‘zig-zags’ of one-step homotopies.
In this section, we present a small framework for developing these systems of one-step homotopies and relating them via functors.

Definition 3.1
.

Fix a locally small category
\bm{\mathcal{C}}
.

1.

A
system of one-step homotopies

{\mathcal{S}}
for
\bm{\mathcal{C}}
is a choice of digraph
{\mathcal{S}}(C_{1},C_{2})
on the vertex set
\mathrm{Mor}_{\bm{\mathcal{C}}}(C_{1},C_{2})
, for every pair of objects
C_{1},C_{2}\in C
.

2.

We call an edge
f\to g
in
{\mathcal{S}}(C_{1},C_{2})
a
one-step
{\mathcal{S}}
-homotopy from
f
to
g
.
A priori, this may not necessarily correspond to a specific morphism in
\bm{\mathcal{C}}
, but this will often be the vase.

3.

If there is an edge
f\to g
or
g\to f
in
{\mathcal{S}}(C_{1},C_{2})
then we say
f
and
g
are
one-step
{\mathcal{S}}
-homotopic
and write

f\simeq_{{\mathcal{S}},1}g
.

4.

We say two morphisms
f,g
are

{\mathcal{S}}
-homotopic
, and write

f\simeq_{{\mathcal{S}}}g
, if they belong to the same weak path component of
{\mathcal{S}}(C_{1},C_{2})
, i.e. if there is a sequence of morphisms

f=f_{0},f_{1},\dots,f_{m-1},f_{m}=g

(3.1)

such that
f_{i-1}\simeq_{{\mathcal{S}},1}f_{i}
for every
i
.
We refer to such a sequence of morphisms as a
multi-step
{\mathcal{S}}
-homotopy from
f
to
g
.

For brevity, we drop the subscript
{\mathcal{S}}
from the notation when it is clear from context.

Remark 3.2
.

By construction,
\simeq_{{\mathcal{S}}}
is an equivalence relation on
\mathrm{Mor}_{\bm{\mathcal{C}}}(C_{1},C_{2})
.

A system of one-step homotopies can be pulled back through a functor.
Under sufficient conditions this pull-back can induce ‘the same’ equivalence relation on the morphisms.

Definition 3.3
.

Given a functor
\mu:\bm{\mathrm{C}}\to\bm{\mathrm{D}}
and a system of one-step homotopies
{\mathcal{S}}
for
\bm{\mathcal{D}}
, the

pull-back
is the system of one-step homotopies

\mu^{\ast}{\mathcal{S}}
for
\bm{\mathcal{C}}
given by

f\to g\text{ in }\mu^{\ast}{\mathcal{S}}(C_{1},C_{2})\iff\mu(f)\mathbin{\hbox{\hskip 3.8889pt\hskip-2.5pt\hbox{\hbox{\hskip 2.5pt\hskip 0.0pt\hbox{}\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-2.5pt\raisebox{0.5pt}{\hbox{$\to$}}\hskip-2.5pt\hskip 2.5pt}}\hskip-2.5pt\hskip-3.8889pt\raisebox{-4.16875pt}{\hbox{$=$}}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip 3.8889pt}}\mu(g)\text{ in }{\mathcal{S}}(\mu(C_{1}),\mu(C_{2})).

(3.2)

Remark 3.4
.

Given a pair of functors
\mu:\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{0}\to\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{1}
,
\nu:\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{1}\to\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{2}
and a system of one-step homotopies
{\mathcal{S}}
for
\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{2}
, one can check that
(\nu\circ\mu)^{\ast}{\mathcal{S}}=\mu^{\ast}\nu^{\ast}{\mathcal{S}}
.

For every pair of objects,
\mu
defines a weak digraph map
\mu^{\ast}{\mathcal{S}}(C_{1},C_{2})\to{\mathcal{S}}(\mu(C_{1}),\mu(C_{2}))
.
Indeed, an alternative definition of
\mu^{\ast}{\mathcal{S}}
is that
\mu^{\ast}{\mathcal{S}}(C_{1},C_{2})
is the largest digraph on
\mathrm{Mor}_{\bm{\mathcal{C}}}(C_{1},C_{2})
such that
\mu
induces such a weak digraph map.
If
\mu
is faithful then each of these vertex maps is injective and hence forms a strong digraph map.
If
\mu
is full and faithful then each map is bijective and hence forms an isomorphism of digraphs.

Lemma 3.5
.

Given a functor
\mu:\bm{\mathrm{C}}\to\bm{\mathrm{D}}
and a system of one-step homotopies
{\mathcal{S}}
for
\bm{\mathcal{D}}
then

f\simeq_{\mu^{\ast}{\mathcal{S}}}g\implies\mu(f)\simeq_{{\mathcal{S}}}\mu(g).

(3.3)

If
\mu
is a full functor then

f\simeq_{\mu^{\ast}{\mathcal{S}}}g\iff\mu(f)\simeq_{{\mathcal{S}}}\mu(g).

(3.4)

Proof.

If
f\simeq_{\mu^{\ast}{\mathcal{S}}}g
then there is a sequence of morphisms

f=f_{0},f_{1},\dots,f_{m-1},f_{m}=g

(3.5)

such that
f_{i-1}\to f_{i}
in
\mu^{\ast}{\mathcal{S}}(C_{1},C_{2})
for every
i
.
Hence,

\mu(f)=\mu(f_{0}),\mu(f_{1}),\dots,\mu(f_{m-1}),\mu(f_{m})=\mu(g)

(3.6)

is a sequence of morphisms, such that for every
i
either
\mu(f_{i-1})=\mu(f_{i})
or
\mu(f_{i-1})\to\mu(f_{i})
in
{\mathcal{S}}(\mu(C_{1}),\mu(C_{2}))
.
After removing duplicate morphisms, we see
\mu(f)\simeq_{{\mathcal{S}}}\mu(g)
.

If
\mu
is full then, by definition,
\mu
is surjective as a vertex map
\mathrm{Mor}_{\bm{\mathcal{C}}}(C_{1},C_{2})\to\mathrm{Mor}_{\bm{\mathcal{D}}}(\mu(C_{1}),\mu(C_{2}))
.
Hence, if
\mu(f)\simeq_{{\mathcal{S}}}\mu(g)
then there is a sequence of morphisms as in equation (

)
such that
\mu(f_{i-1})\to\mu(f_{i})
in
{{\mathcal{S}}}(\mu(C_{1}),\mu(C_{2}))
for every
i
.
By the construction of the pull-back

f=f_{0},f_{1},\dots,f_{m-1},f_{m}=g

(3.7)

is a sequence of morphisms showing that
f\simeq_{\mu^{\ast}{\mathcal{S}}}g
.
∎

This equivalence relation for morphisms can induce an equivalence relation on the objects of
\bm{\mathcal{C}}
, in the standard way.
However, we must first check that the relations between the morphisms compose transitively.

{{\mathcal{S}}}

g\circ f\simeq_{{\mathcal{S}}}\mathrm{id}_{C_{1}}\quad\text{and}\quad f\circ g\simeq_{{\mathcal{S}}}\mathrm{id}_{C_{2}}.

(3.8)

For brevity, we drop the subscript
{{\mathcal{S}}}

.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
{{\mathcal{S}}}

\bm{\mathcal{C}}

If
{{\mathcal{S}}}

Corollary 3.9
.

Given a full functor
\mu:\bm{\mathrm{C}}\to\bm{\mathrm{D}}
and a system of one-step homotopies
{{\mathcal{S}}}
for
\bm{\mathcal{D}}

Corollary 3.10
.

Given a functor
\mu:\bm{\mathrm{C}}\to\bm{\mathrm{D}}

{{\mathcal{S}}}

\bm{\mathcal{D}}

One way to construct a system of one-step homotopies is via a choice of cylinder functor (see
[
24
]
for an introduction).

Definition 3.11
.

A

for a category
\bm{\mathcal{C}}
is a functor

\Cyl:\bm{\mathcal{C}}\to\bm{\mathcal{C}}

(3.14)

equipped with three natural transformations

(3.15)

satisfying

With such a cylinder functor in hand, one can construct a system of one-step homotopies

Given

, a

{{\mathcal{S}}}

(3.16)

This determines the system

by including an arrow

in
{\mathcal{S}}(C_{1},C_{2})
whenever such a one-step
{\mathcal{S}}
-homotopy exists.
In a slight abuse of terminology, we use the term ‘one-step
{\mathcal{S}}
-homotopy’ to refer interchangeably between the edge in
{\mathcal{S}}(C_{1},C_{2})
and a morphism that implies its existence.
We refer to
{\mathcal{S}}
as the system of one-step homotopies associated to

.

Lemma 3.12

(
[
24
, Lemma 2.3]
)
.

The system of one-step homotopies associated to a cylinder functor is transitive.

In this work, we opt for slightly more generality than cylinder functors in order to emphasise the important properties of the resultant systems.
Moreover, in Section

, we will see that it is not clear how to construct an appropriate cylinder functor in

4.2
Ordered simplicial complexes as path complexes

Due to Lemma
2.10
, there is an inclusion of full subcategories
\bm{\mathrm{TcOSC}}
,
\iota:\bm{\mathrm{TcOSC}}\hookrightarrow\bm{\mathrm{WkRPC}}
.
Therefore, the system of one-step homotopies described in Section
4.1
restricts to a system for ordered simplicial complexes, by taking the pull-back
\iota^{\ast}{\mathcal{S}_{\bm{\mathrm{WkRPC}}}}
.
In this section, we seek to characterise this system intrinsically within
\bm{\mathrm{TcOSC}}
, i.e. without reference to path complexes or morphisms.
First, we require the notion of the simplicial closure of a path complex.

Definition 4.5
.

A path complex,
P
, is called
pre-simplicial
if all of its constituent paths are simplicial.
Given a pre-simplicial path complex
P
, its
simplicial closure
,
{\color[rgb]{0.5,0,1}\overline{P}}
is the smallest simplicial complex containing
K
.

Remark 4.6
.

1.

A pre-simplicial path complex is necessarily regular.

2.

The simplicial closure of a pre-simplicial path complex is well-defined because the set of all simplicial complexes is closed under arbitrary intersections.

It will be important to understand the structure of
\overline{\Cyl(K)}
, for a simplicial complex
K
, because a homotopy of path complexes has
\Cyl(K)
as its domain.
First we list the additional simplices in the closure of a cylinder.

Lemma 4.7
.

Given any simplicial complex
K
,
\Cyl(K)
is pre-simplicial.
Moreover,

\overline{\Cyl(K)}\setminus\Cyl(K)=\left\{(v_{0},0)\dots(v_{i},0)(v_{i+1},1)(v_{k},1)\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}v_{0}\dots v_{k}\in K,0\leq i<k\right\}.

(4.12)

Proof.

It is clear to see that all paths in the cylinder of
K
are simplicial.
Then, for every
v_{0}\dots v_{k}\in K
and
0\leq i<k
,
\Cyl(K)
contains the path

(v_{0},0)\dots(v_{i},0)(v_{i+1},0)(v_{i+1},1)\dots(v_{k},1).

(4.13)

Therefore, in order to be closed under taking ordered subsets,
\overline{\Cyl(K)}
must contain the path

(v_{0},0)\dots(v_{i},0)(v_{i+1},1)\dots(v_{k},1).

(4.14)

Finally, note that adding these paths to
\Cyl(K)
yields a simplicial complex.
∎

With these, we are able to characterise the homotopies between morphisms in
\bm{\mathrm{TcOSC}}
without reference to path complexes or morphisms.

Theorem 4.8
.

Given two triangle-collapsing simplicial morphisms
f,g:K_{1}\to K_{2}
,
a vertex map
F:V(K_{1})\times\{0,1\}\to V(K_{2})
induces a one-step
{\mathcal{S}_{\bm{\mathrm{WkRPC}}}}
-homotopy from
f
to
g

if and only if
F
induces a triangle-collapsing simplicial morphism
\overline{\Cyl(K_{1})}\to K_{2}
such that

F\circ\iota_{0}=f\quad\text{and}\quad F\circ\iota_{1}=g.

(4.15)

Proof.

First, note that it suffices to show
F
induces a weak path morphism
\Cyl(K_{1})\to K_{2}
if and only if
F
induces a triangle-collapsing simplicial morphism
\overline{\Cyl(K_{1})}\to K_{2}
, under the assumption that
F\circ\iota_{0}=f
and
F\circ\iota_{1}=g
.
Also note that
f
and
g
are weak path morphisms
K_{1}\to K_{2}
, by Lemma
2.10
.

One direction is straight-forward: if
F
induces a triangle-collapsing simplicial morphism
\overline{\Cyl(K_{1})}\to K_{2}
then, by Lemma
2.10
,
F
induces a weak path morphism
\overline{\Cyl(K_{1})}\to K_{2}
.
Then, because
\Cyl(K_{1})\subseteq\overline{\Cyl(K_{1})}
,
F
must also induce a weak path morphism
\Cyl(K_{1})\to K_{2}
.

For the opposite direction, suppose
F
induces a weak path morphism
\Cyl(K_{1})\to K_{2}
.
It remains to show that for

F(p)\in K_{2}
or
F(p)
is irregular.
By Lemma
4.7
, we can assume that

takes the form

(v_{0},0)\dots(v_{i},0)(v_{i+1},1)\dots(v_{k},1)

and

Consider the cylinder path

\displaystyle F(p)

\displaystyle=F\big((v_{0},0)\dots(v_{i-1},0)(v_{i},0)(v_{i+1},1)(v_{i+2},1)\dots(v_{k},1)\big)

\displaystyle F(p)

for some

.
Since

is simplicial, the only repeated vertex is

The final point follows immediately from the third.
∎

Lemma 4.11
.

Given two simplicial complexes
K_{1}
,
K_{2}
and a weak simplicial morphism

,

.

and

are both triangle-collapsing simplicial morphisms

,

(4.27)

Proof.

First, suppose
F
is triangle-collapsing.
Then take

such that

.
This implies that
F(x,0)=F(y,1)
.
Now note
(x,0)(y,0)(y,1)\in\overline{\Cyl(K_{1})}

so

and hence

.
Likewise,
(x,0)(x,1)(y,1)\in\overline{\Cyl(K_{1})}

so
F(x,1)=F(x,0)
and hence

.

Now suppose

satisfies condition (
4.27
).
Take a simplex

.
By Lemma
4.10
.
4
, we may assume
v_{0}\neq v_{2}
.
If

for some

and

then

because

and

are both triangle-collapsing.
It remains to consider the case where

.
Let us assume that

First, note the condition

becomes

.

and

.
We split into two cases.

Suppose

.

.

(4.28)

.

∎

4.3
Ordered simplicial complexes as simplicial sets

Those unfamiliar with path complexes may prefer to view an ordered simplicial complex as a simplicial set.
For completeness, we consider this alternative perspective here.
The remainder of this work is not dependent on this section.

First, we describe a full and faithful embedding
\sigma:\bm{\mathrm{TcOSC}}\to\bm{\mathrm{sSet}}
, into the category of simplicial sets.
To each simplicial set, one can assign a chain complex, called its normalised Moore complex, via a functor

(see
[
14
, § 2]

From a homological point of view, these two perspectives are identical, in the sense that
\Omega
and

are naturally isomorphic as functors
\bm{\mathrm{TcOSC}}\to\bm{\mathrm{Ch}}

The category of simplicial sets is equipped with a system of one-step homotopies, via its ‘left homotopies’, which we denote

In Corollary

, we show that
\sigma^{\ast}{\mathcal{S}_{\bm{\mathrm{sSet}}}}={\mathcal{S}_{\bm{\mathrm{TcOSC}}}}

We begin with definitions of the simplex category and of simplicial sets, which facilitate the construction of

.

There is an inclusion of subcategories

The proof that

Essentially, this amounts to showing that,

Since an ordered simplicial complex contains no ‘degenerate’ simplices, one may prefer to view an ordered simplicial complex as a semi-simplicial set (sometimes called a

We choose to focus here on simplicial sets since they are better-studied in the literature and the two perspectives are compatible (see

Finally, we note that there is a notion of homotopy theory for directed topological spaces, from the field of directed algebraic topology

To the authors’ knowledge, this theory has not yet been connected to the various homotopy and homology theories of directed graphs.
One could choose instead to view the directed flag complex as a directed topological space; this perspective requires further study.

M\Rightarrow M[\delta]
, given by
\mathcal{T}(M,\delta)(t):=M(t\leq t+\delta)
.

3.

A

\delta
-interleaving
between
M
and
N
is a pair of natural transformations

f:M\Rightarrow N[\delta]\quad\text{and}\quad g:N\Rightarrow M[\delta],

(6.3)

such that
g[\delta]\circ f=\mathcal{T}(M,2\delta)
and
f[\delta]\circ g=\mathcal{T}(N,2\delta)
.
If such an interleaving exists, we say
M
and
N
are

\delta
-interleaved (in
\bm{\mathcal{C}}
)
.

4.

Finally, the
interleaving distance
between
M
and
N
is

{\color[rgb]{0.5,0,1}d_{I}(M,N)}:=\left\{\delta\geq 0\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}M\text{ and }N\text{ are }\delta\text{-interleaved}\right\}\in[0,\infty].

(6.4)

The interleaving distance is an extended pseudo-metric on persistent
\bm{\mathcal{C}}
-objects
[
4
]
.
Moreover, the interleaving distance behaves well with respect to functors.

Lemma 6.2
.

Any functor
F:\bm{\mathcal{C}}\to\bm{\mathcal{D}}
induces a functor
F:\textbf{[}\bm{\mathrm{R}},\,\bm{\mathcal{C}}\textbf{]}\to\textbf{[}\bm{\mathrm{R}},\,\bm{\mathcal{D}}\textbf{]}
.
Endowing these functor categories with the interleaving distance,

is a
1
-Lipschitz map, i.e.

(6.5)

for all

.

Proof.

The functor is defined in the obvious way:
given
M\in\textbf{[}\bm{\mathrm{R}},\,\bm{\mathcal{C}}\textbf{]}
,
we define

and
F(M)(s\leq t):=F(M(s\leq t))
.
If
f,g
constitute a
\delta
-interleaving between
M,N\in\textbf{[}\bm{\mathrm{R}},\,\bm{\mathcal{C}}\textbf{]}
then
F(f),F(g)
constitute a
\delta
-interleaving between
F(M)
and
F(N)
.
∎

6.2
Stability theorems

Since
{\mathcal{S}_{\dFl}}
is a pull-back of
{\mathcal{S}_{\bm{\mathrm{WkPathC}}}}
, it is a transitive system.
Consequently, the composition operator for morphisms in
\bm{\mathrm{TcDgr}}
passes to the quotient to define a composition operator between
\simeq_{\dFl}
equivalence classes of triangle-collapsing digraph maps.
This allows us to define a new category.

Definition 6.3
.

The
naive
\dFl
-homotopy category
,

\bm{\mathrm{h}}_{\dFl}\bm{\mathrm{TcDgr}}
is the category whose objects are simple digraphs and morphisms are
\simeq_{\dFl}
equivalence classes of triangle-collapsing digraph maps.
We use the notation

[\bullet]_{\dFl}
to denote objects and morphisms in this category.

Note that the objects of
\bm{\mathrm{h}}_{\dFl}\bm{\mathrm{TcDgr}}
are the same as
\bm{\mathrm{TcDgr}}
, but the morphisms are collapsed, according to the
\simeq_{\dFl}
equivalence relation.
Corollary
4.3
automatically yields the following factorisation and hence a stability theorem.

Corollary 6.4
.

The directed flag complex homology functor
H\circ\dFl:\bm{\mathrm{TcDgr}}\to\bm{\mathrm{grVec}}
factors through
\bm{\mathrm{h}}_{\dFl}\bm{\mathrm{TcDgr}}
.
Therefore,
given
M,N\in\textbf{[}\bm{\mathrm{R}},\,\bm{\mathrm{TcDgr}}\textbf{]}
,

d_{I}((H\circ\dFl)(M),(H\circ\dFl)(N))\leq d_{I}({[M]}_{\dFl},{[N]}_{\dFl})\leq d_{I}(M,N).

(6.6)

More explicitly, if there are natural transformations

f:M\Rightarrow N[\delta]\quad\text{and}\quad g:N\Rightarrow M[\delta],

(6.7)

such that
g[\delta]\circ f\simeq_{\dFl}\mathcal{T}(M,2\delta)(t)

and
f[\delta]\circ g\simeq_{\dFl}\mathcal{T}(N,2\delta)(t)

for every
t\in\bm{\mathrm{R}}

then,

d_{I}\big((H\circ\dFl)(M),(H\circ\dFl)(N)\big)\leq\delta.

(6.8)

Essentially, this tells us that it suffices to
\delta
-interleave
M
and
N
, up to
\simeq_{\dFl}
equivalence.
In general, this is a significantly easier task, especially when

and
N
are on different vertex sets.
Note that we only require a homotopy at each
t\in\bm{\mathrm{R}}
; the homotopies need not be natural, i.e. they need not commute with the structure maps of
M
and
N
.
In the sequel, we construct explicit examples of such interleavings.

A
filtration of digraphs on
V
is a family of digraphs
{\{G_{t}\}}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
such that
V(G_{t})=V
is constant and if

.
Connecting these graphs via the natural inclusions, we can view a filtration as a functor

.
Equivalently, we can view such a filtration as a function
\mathrm{ET}(G_{\bullet}):V\times V\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{\pm\infty\}
, which records the entrance time of each edge:

\mathrm{ET}(G_{\bullet})(v,w):=\inf\left\{t\in\mathbb{R}\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}(v,w)\in E(G_{t})\right\}.

(6.9)

Proposition 6.5
.

Let
G_{\bullet},G^{\prime}_{\bullet}
be two filtrations of digraphs on a common vertex set
V
.
Then

d_{I}\big((H\circ\dFl)(G_{\bullet}),(H\circ\dFl)({G^{\prime}}_{\bullet})\big)\leq|\!|\mathrm{ET}(G_{\bullet})-\mathrm{ET}({G^{\prime}}_{\bullet})|\!|_{\infty}.

(6.10)

Proof.

Take arbitrary
\epsilon>0
and denote
\delta:=|\!|\mathrm{ET}(G_{\bullet})-\mathrm{ET}(G^{\prime}_{\bullet})|\!|_{\infty}

Using the identity vertex map in both directions, it is straightforward to show that

and

Proposition 6.10
.

Let

.

, it suffices to show that

Let

is triangle-collapsing.

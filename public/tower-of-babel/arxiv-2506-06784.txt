Caterpillar GNN: Replacing Message Passing with Efficient Aggregation
Marek Černý

Abstract
Message-passing graph neural networks (MPGNNs) dominate modern graph learning, typically prioritizing maximal expressive power. In contrast, we introduce an efficient aggregation mechanism, deliberately trading off some expressivity for stronger and more structured aggregation capabilities. Our approach allows seamless scaling between classical message-passing and simpler methods based on colored or plain walks. We rigorously characterize the expressive power at each intermediate step using homomorphism counts from a hierarchy of generalized caterpillar graphs . Based on this foundation, we propose the Caterpillar GNN , whose robust graph-level aggregation enables it to successfully tackle synthetic graph-level task specifically designed to be challenging for classical MPGNNs. Moreover, we demonstrate that, on real-world datasets, the Caterpillar GNN achieves comparable predictive performance while significantly reducing the number of nodes in the hidden layers of the computational graph.

Introduction

Graphs are a powerful and expressive structure,
capable of representing complex relational information across various domains
such as biology, chemistry, databases or social sciences.
Unlike data with a given finite-dimensional structure such as text and images,
graph structured data brings into a consideration the additional variability
of its relations.
To let deep learning methods process a signal represented as graph, Message-Passing Graph Neural Networks (MPGNNs) have emerged as a default approach.
MPGNN iteratively aggregates information from a tree structure of node neighborhoods in a graph.
However, in scenarios where strict invariance to vertex ordering is not required,
alternative aggregation schemes based on applying deep learning to sequences of nodes visited by random walks have emerged.
Recently, these two schemes combined
Chen et al. 2024
;
Tönshoff et al. 2023

shown significant improvements.

Deep neural networks rely on stacking multiple feedforward layers,
progressively abstracting information.
For instance, convolutional neural networks for images
downscale after every given block of layers,
gradually reducing the spatial dimension
to accommodate for higher-level compact representations.
In contrast, MPGNNs traditionally
copy
the input graph structure
across all layers to perform repetitive
message-passing
.
As a consequence, the structure and complexity of their computational graphs vary significantly depending on the properties of the input graph, some of which negatively influence the
performance
Vitvitskyi et al. 2025
.
Meanwhile,
random walk-based processing
typically abandons explicit replication of node neighborhoods altogether, as shown
Chen et al. 2024
.

In this work, we simultaneously challenge and exploit both paradigms above
by introducing a novel, parametric and vertex ordering invariant aggregation scheme that enables controlled, gradual reduction of the structure of the tree of neighborhoods in the feedforward computational graph at each parameter step.
Our approach,
efficient aggregation
explicitly bridges the classical
message-passing and the expressive power of plain walk counting, thereby achieving a structured and efficient option for “downscaling” within graph neural networks—an innovation we found previously absent in graph learning.

Conclusion

In this paper, we introduced
efficient aggregation
, that scales between classical message-passing graph neural networks and simpler graph-learning methods based on colored and plain walks.
Our approach explicitly balances expressive power and computational complexity, leading to the proposed
Caterpillar GNN
architecture.
Through rigorous theoretical analysis, we characterized the expressivity of efficient aggregation via homomorphism counts from generalized caterpillar graphs, revealing that rather
intermediate expressivity
and more organized aggregation could provide more optimized balance.
Experimentally, we validated these insights on both synthetic and real-world tasks.
In our real-world comparison benchmark, Caterpillar GNN achieved comparable or superior predictive performance to GCNs, consistently utilizing fewer computational nodes.
In the extreme case of unattributed COLLAB, this reduction reached
94\%
of nodes,
but typically, according to our observations, reductions range between thirty and sixty percent.

Next Steps.

A promising next step is to further investigate the practical analogy between efficient aggregation’s (caterpillar height) parameter
h
and traditional hyperparameters like batch size or learning rate, possibly to develop adaptive, data-driven selection strategies.
Understanding the optimization dynamics behind the observed double-descent phenomenon could also enhance practical applicability.
Lastly, extending applications of our efficient graph matrices to other GNN variants or heterogeneous graph structures provides additional fruitful directions for future exploration.

Lemma B.1

For every integer

We can instead take logarithm of

B.1

In this subsection, we extend the proof of

to the case of generalized caterpillars

Similarly to the previous case in

where we counted possible mappings of each leg of the folklore caterpillar,
we can adapt a more general approach by replacing star graphs with

-labeled graphs.
Finally, we replace the counts of all possible mappings of star graphs
by the homomorphism counts of linear combinations of

-labeled graphs, called quantum

-labeled graphs.

Labeled graphs.

We follow the algebraic approach to quantum graphs from Lovász and Szegedy

, instantiating for the
1
-labeled case.
A
1
-labeled graph
, or simply

,

is a graph

with one distinguished vertex

, called a

, denoted by

.

Let
{\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}}
be a class of labeled graphs,

F^{\bullet}{\text{ in }}{\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}}
labeled,
and

another labeled graph
then we define vector
\mathsf{hom}({\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}},G^{\bullet}){\text{ in }}\mathbb{N}^{\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}}
entry-wise: each its entry
\mathsf{hom}({\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}},G^{\bullet})[F^{\bullet}]

is the number of graph homomorphisms
\varphi\colon V(G)\to V(H)

that, moreover, preserve the label, i.e.,

\displaystyle\varphi(\mathsf{lab}(F^{\bullet}))=\mathsf{lab}(G^{\bullet}).

In cases where we need to explicitly indicate the labeled vertex of
G^{\bullet}
, we write

for

Next, a

of two labeled graphs

is the graph created by identification of
\mathsf{lab}(G^{\bullet}_{1})
and
\mathsf{lab}(G^{\bullet}_{2})

in the disjoint union of

and
G^{\bullet}_{2}

A
quantum graph
is a formal linear combination of finitely many graphs.
A
1
-labeled quantum graph
, or simply
labeled quantum graph
, is a formal linear combination (with real coefficients) of finitely many
1
-labeled graphs.
The homomorphism counting extends linearly to quantum graphs:

\displaystyle\mathsf{hom}({\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}},\,G^{\bullet})\Big[\sum_{i=1}^{d}\alpha_{i}F^{\bullet}_{i}\Big]\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{d}\alpha_{i}\,\mathsf{hom}({\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}},G^{\bullet})[F^{\bullet}_{i}],

for the coefficients

, and the
1
-labeled graphs

for

.

Moreover, quantum graphs

can be naturally combined by sum, product, and exponentiation operations.

is the sum of their linear combinations.
A

is the product of their linear combinations, where

with itself.
We will use a standard identity for

-labeled, possibly quantum, graphs:

\displaystyle\mathsf{hom}({\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}},G^{\bullet})[(F^{\bullet})^{k}]=\left(\mathsf{hom}({\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}},G^{\bullet})[F^{\bullet}]\right)^{k}.

(23)

C.3
Efficiency of Aggregation

Our proposed efficient aggregation scheme significantly reduces computational complexity during training compared to full message-passing (MP) graph convolutional networks (GCNs), while maintaining or even improving predictive performance.
Experiments on accuracy-based classification datasets (
Table 2
) and regression-based datasets evaluated by mean absolute error (
Table 3
) demonstrate that using lower-height caterpillar aggregations substantially reduces the size of computation graphs.
That is, up to approximately 93.8% fewer nodes compared to MP for
unattributed
dataset IMDB-BINARY,
and up to 82.2% for
categorically attributed
dataset such as PROTEINS.
This reduction translates into improved computational efficiency, memory usage, and scalability.
In our experiments without additional hyperparameter optimization we observed even a positive impact on predictive accuracy or regression performance across datasets.
These results underscore the practical value and scalability potential of our efficient aggregation method in graph-based machine learning tasks.

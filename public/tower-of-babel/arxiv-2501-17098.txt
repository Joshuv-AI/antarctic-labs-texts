Automorphism groups of measures on the Cantor space. Part I: Good measures and Rokhlin properties Thanks: Research partially supported by the National Center of Science, Poland under the Weave-UNISONO call in the Weave programme [grant no 2021/03/Y/ST1/00072]. The first-named author was supported by the GAČR project 22-07833K and by the Czech Academy of Sciences (RVO 67985840).
Michal Doucha, Dominik Kwietniak, Maciej Malicki, Piotr Niemiec

Abstract
. We study criteria for the existence of a dense or comeager conjugacy class in the automorphism group of a given measure on the Cantor space. We concentrate on good measures, defined by Akin [ Trans. Amer. Math. Soc. 357 (2005), no. 7, 2681–2722], which we characterize as a particular subclass of ultrahomogeneous measures. We determine good measures with rational values on clopen sets whose automorphism group admits a comeager conjugacy class. Our approach uses the Fraïssé theory.

Primary 37A15, 28D15; Secondary 28D05, 03E15

1.
Introduction

We study automorphism groups of measures of Cantor spaces.
The Cantor space
2^{\mathbb{N}}
is homeomorphically unique, but it carries multiple non-homeomorphic measures (here, all measures are always Borel and probabilistic). The automorphism group of a measure
\mu
is the collection
\mbox{Homeo}(2^{\mathbb{N}},\mu)
of all homeomorphisms of
2^{\mathbb{N}}
that preserve
\mu
. It is a closed subgroup of
\mbox{Homeo}(2^{\mathbb{N}})
.

A topological invariant for a full (positive on nonempty open sets), nonatomic measure
\mu
on the Cantor space is its clopen values set
V_{\mu}
(the set of values attained on clopen subsets). Good measures on
2^{\mathbb{N}}
are full measures
\mu
satisfying the Subset Condition: if
\mu(U)<\mu(V)
for some clopen sets
U
and
V
, then
V
contains a clopen subset
W
with
\mu(W)=\mu(U)
. All good measures are nonatomic (see Proposition
3.1
). Good measure
\mu
is up to a homeomorphism determined by its clopen values set
V_{\mu}
. Glasner and Weiss
[
8
]
proved that if
\mu
is a unique invariant measure for some minimal homeomorphism on
2^{\mathbb{N}}
then it is good. Akin
[
2
]
proved the converse, that is, he proved that every good measure is preserved by a uniquely ergodic homeomorphism of
2^{\mathbb{N}}
. He
[
2
]
also proved that
\mbox{Homeo}(2^{\mathbb{N}},\mu)
contains a comeager
conjugacy class when
V_{\mu}+\mathbb{Z}
is a
{\mathbb{Q}}
-vector subspace of
{\mathbb{R}}
, a criterion later recovered by Ibarlucía and Melleray
[
9
]
.

Ibarlucía and Melleray
[
9
]
studied existence of dense conjugacy class in the group
\mbox{Homeo}(2^{\mathbb{N}},\mu)
of all homeomorphisms preserving a given good measure
\mu
. They used a Fraïssé theoretic approach to recover a result of Akin which provides a criterion phrased in terms of
V_{\mu}
and describing when the group
\mbox{Homeo}(2^{\mathbb{N}},\mu)
admits a comeager conjugacy class. See also
[
10
,
14
]
for further research on the topic.

We apply the Fraïssé theoretic approach to good measure themselves, an idea used in particular cases already in
[
12
]
and also in
[
5
]
, to obtain the following. We refer to Section
3
for undefined notions.

Theorem A

(see Theorem
3.4
)
.

Good measures on the Cantor space are precisely those full ultrahomogeneous measures that are maximal.

As ultrahomogeneity is
closely related to Fraïssé classes, it is quite natural to study
the automorphism groups of all ultrahomogeneous measures in terms of properties
typical in dynamical systems (such as transitivity or minimality of the action,
Rokhlin properties, etc.). These are the topic of the second part of the paper

[
6
]
, whereas here in this article we will focus mainly on good measures (as they are the most common examples from the aforementioned class).
Among our results on the automorphism groups of good measures we highlight the following.

Theorem B

(see Theorem
5.7
)
.

Let
\mu
be a good measure on
2^{\mathbb{N}}
whose clopen values set is contained in
{\mathbb{Q}}
. The following conditions are equivalent:

•

\mbox{Homeo}(2^{\mathbb{N}},\mu)
admits a dense conjugacy class.

•

\mbox{Homeo}(2^{\mathbb{N}},\mu)
admits a comeager conjugacy class.

•

V_{\mu}+\mathbb{Z}
is a subring of
{\mathbb{R}}
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we recall
the notion of a group-like sets and introduce general concepts from category
theory that will be used by us in the sequel. Section
3
is devoted
to the concept of ultrahomogeneity for measures. We give there two
characterizations of good measures: one in terms of Fraïssé limits (of certain
countable structures) and the other as precisely those ultrahomogeneous measures
that are maximal (see, respectively, Proposition
3.3
and
Theorem
3.4
below). We also give illustrative examples of
ultrahomogeneous measures that are not good, and show that good measures are (in
a certain quite natural sense) generic among all measures whose clopen values
set is contained in a given group-like set. Section
4
discusses Rokhlin
properties of the automorphism groups of good measures. Among other things, we
give there an equivalent condition for the aforementioned group to have
the Rokhlin property (see Theorem
4.8
below) as well as a sufficient
condition for this group to have a non-meager conjugacy class (consult
Theorem
4.9
below). The latter is also an equivalent condition but we omit the proof of the other direction as we do not need it.
Finally, in Section
5
we apply the results to fully classify (in Theorem
5.7
) good measures with clopen values
set contained in
{\mathbb{Q}}
whose automorphism groups have the Rokhlin property and show that for such measures this property is actually equivalent to the strong Rokhlin property. The same equivalence (between strong and standard Rokhlin properties) holds also for good measures whose clopen values set contains
{\mathbb{Q}}\cap[0,1]
(as shown by
Lemma
5.9
).

Notation and terminology

In this paper
{\mathbb{N}}=\{1,2,\ldots\}
, all topological spaces are Polish (that is, they are completely metrisable and separable), apart from the space
\operatorname{Full}(V)
introduced in the paragraph preceding Proposition
3.7
. All measures considered here are Borel probability measures. A Cantor space is a zero-dimensional, perfect,
nonempty, compact Polish space. We write
2^{\mathbb{N}}
to denote the countable
topological product of the discrete spaces
\{0,1\}
. It is standard that
2^{\mathbb{N}}
is a Cantor space. A measure is
full
if it is positive on all nonempty
open sets (has full support). A measure is
nonatomic
if it vanishes on all
countable subsets. A homeomorphism
h\colon X\to X
is
minimal
if every orbit is dense. For any measure
\mu
on
X
we use
h^{*}(\mu)
to denote
the
transport
of
\mu
via
h
; that is,
h^{*}(\mu)(B)=\mu(h^{-1}(B))
for any Borel set
B\subseteq X
. The measure
\mu
is
invariant
under
h
(or, equivalently,
h

preserves

\mu
)
if
h^{*}(\mu)=\mu
.
Given a measure
\mu
on
2^{\mathbb{N}}
we write
\mbox{Homeo}(2^{\mathbb{N}},\mu)
for the Polish group of all homeomorphisms of
2^{\mathbb{N}}
preserving
\mu
.

Following e.g.
[
2
]
, we say a Polish group
G
has
Rokhlin property
(resp.
strong Rokhlin property
) if it admits a dense (resp. dense
\mathscr{G}_{\delta}
) conjugacy class.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Group-like sets.

Recall that a set
V
is
group-like

if
\{0,1\}\subseteq V\subseteq[0,1]
and
V+\mathbb{Z}
is an additive subgroup of
\mathbb{R}
. In other words,

V\subseteq[0,1]
is group-like if and only if
\{0,1\}\subseteq V
and
there is an additive subgroup
G
of
\mathbb{R}
such that
V=[0,1]\cap G
.
In this paper, all group-like sets are, by definition, (in addition) countably infinite
. We say that a group-like set
V
is
{\mathbb{Q}}
-like
(respectively,
ring-like
) if
V+\mathbb{Z}
is a rational vector space (respectively, a subring of
\mathbb{R}
). It is easy to see that every group-like set must be dense in
[0,1]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
V
be group-like. Let
x^{0}_{i}\in V
(
1\leq i\leq k
),
x^{1}_{j}\in V
(
1\leq j\leq l
), and
z\in V
be such that

x^{0}_{1}+\ldots+x^{0}_{k}=x^{1}_{1}+\ldots+x^{1}_{l}=z.

Then there exist
m\geq 1
and
z_{i}\in V
(
1\leq i\leq m
) such that

z_{1}+\ldots+z_{m}=z,

and
\{1,\ldots m\}

can be partitioned in two ways: into the sets
X^{0}_{i}
(
1\leq i\leq k
) and into sets
X^{1}_{j}
(
1\leq j\leq l
) in such a way that for
1\leq i\leq k
and
1\leq j\leq l
we have

x^{0}_{i}=\sum_{s\in X^{0}_{i}}z_{s}\quad\text{and}\quad x^{1}_{j}=\sum_{s\in X^{1}_{j}}z_{s}.

Proof.

The proof goes by induction on
n=k+l
. If
k=1
or
l=1
, then the lemma is obviously true. Assume that
k,l\geq 2
and that the lemma holds whenever
k^{\prime}+l^{\prime}<k+l
. Without loss of generality we can assume
x^{0}_{k}\geq x^{1}_{l}
. If
x^{0}_{k}=x^{1}_{l}
, we apply the inductive assumption to
x^{0}_{1},\ldots,x^{0}_{k-1}
,
x^{1}_{1},\ldots,x^{1}_{l-1}
and
\bar{z}=z-x^{1}_{l}
to obtain
m
and
z_{s}\in V

(1\leq s\leq m)
. Clearly,
z_{1},\ldots,z_{m},z_{m+1}=x^{1}_{l}
are as required. Otherwise, we slightly modify the first sequence setting
\bar{x}^{0}_{1}=x^{0}_{1},\ldots,\bar{x}^{0}_{k-1}=x^{0}_{k-1}
,
\bar{x}^{0}_{k}=(x^{0}_{k}-x^{1}_{l})
, and then apply the inductive assumption to
\bar{x}^{0}_{1},\ldots,\bar{x}^{0}_{k}
,
x^{1}_{1},\ldots,x^{1}_{l-1}
, and
\bar{z}=z-x^{1}_{l}
to obtain
m
and
z_{s}\in V

(1\leq s\leq m)
. Again,
z_{1},\ldots,z_{m},z_{m+1}=x^{1}_{l}
are as required.
∎

2.2.
Categories

Let
{\mathcal{K}}
be a category. Writing
A\in{\mathcal{K}}
we mean that “
A
is an object of
{\mathcal{K}}
.” We say that
{\mathcal{K}}^{\prime}
is a subcategory of
{\mathcal{K}}
and write
{\mathcal{K}}^{\prime}\subseteq{\mathcal{K}}
if
{\mathcal{K}}^{\prime}
is a category such that each
object of
{\mathcal{K}}^{\prime}
is an object of
{\mathcal{K}}
and each morphism arrow of
{\mathcal{K}}^{\prime}
is also an arrow of
{\mathcal{K}}
(with the same domain and co-domain).

For
A,B\in{\mathcal{K}}
and a morphism
p\colon B\rightarrow A
that is a surjective mapping, we say that
p
projects
B
onto
A
, and that
B
is a
lift
(or, more precisely, a
p
-lift
) of
A
. For
A\in{\mathcal{K}}
, we write
{\mathcal{K}}^{A}\subseteq{\mathcal{K}}
for the subcategory of all lifts of
A
.

We say that
{\mathcal{K}}
is
directed
if every two objects have a common lift, that is, for any
A_{i}\in{\mathcal{K}}
(
i=0,1
), there is
B\in{\mathcal{K}}
and there are morphisms
r_{i}\colon B\rightarrow A_{i}
. The category
{\mathcal{K}}

has amalgamation
if for any
A\in{\mathcal{K}}
and any morphisms
r_{i}\colon B_{i}\rightarrow A
(
i=0,1
), there is
C\in{\mathcal{K}}
and there are morphisms
s_{i}\colon C\rightarrow B_{i}
such that
r_{0}\circ s_{0}=r_{1}\circ s_{1}
. A subcategory
{\mathcal{K}}^{\prime}\subseteq{\mathcal{K}}
is
cofinal
in
{\mathcal{K}}
if every object from
{\mathcal{K}}
has a lift from
{\mathcal{K}}^{\prime}
(with an arrow from
{\mathcal{K}}
).
{\mathcal{K}}
has
cofinal amalgamation
if there is a cofinal
{\mathcal{K}}^{\prime}\subseteq{\mathcal{K}}
with amalgamation. Finally, the category
{\mathcal{K}}
has
weak amalgamation
if for any
A\in{\mathcal{K}}
there is a morphism
p\colon A^{\prime}\rightarrow A
such that for any morphisms
r_{i}\colon B_{i}\rightarrow A^{\prime}
(
i=0,1
), there is
C\in{\mathcal{M}}
and there are morphisms
s_{i}\colon C\rightarrow B_{i}
such that
p\circ r_{0}\circ s_{0}=p\circ r_{1}\circ s_{1}
. We call any
A^{\prime}
as above an
amalgamation basis for
A
.

We denote by
\sigma{\mathcal{K}}
the space of all
chains
from
{\mathcal{K}}
, i.e., sequences
(A_{n},p_{n})
such that
A_{n}\in{\mathcal{K}}
and
p_{n}\colon A_{n+1}\rightarrow A_{n}
are morphisms. We will denote by
p^{j}_{i}
,
i<j
, the appropriate compositions of
p_{n}
, and write
(A_{n})
for
(A_{n},p_{n})
if there is no risk of ambiguity. (Iso)morphisms of chains are defined in a natural way (see
[
13
]
for details).
We say that a chain
(A_{n},p_{n})
from
{\mathcal{K}}
is
Fraïssé
if
\{A_{n}\}
is cofinal in
{\mathcal{K}}
, and for every
i
,
A\in{\mathcal{K}}
, and
\pi\colon A\rightarrow A_{i}
there are
j\geq i
and
r\colon A_{j}\rightarrow A
such that
\pi\circ r=p^{j}_{i}
. Any two Fraïssé chains are isomorphic. It is the cornerstone of Fraïssé theory that if the category
{\mathcal{K}}
is countable, it is directed and has amalgamation then
\sigma{\mathcal{K}}
admits a Fraïssé chain. We refer to
[
13
]
for details.

3.
Good measures are ultrahomogeneous

In the existing literature good measures are
always
defined as full,
nonatomic and satisfying the subset condition. To our best
knowledge, the observation that being nonatomic is redundant in this definition is new.

Proposition 3.1
.

A full measure on
2^{\mathbb{N}}
satisfying the subset condition is good.

Proof.

Let
\mu
be a full measure on
2^{\mathbb{N}}
satisfying the subset condition. It remains to show that
\mu
is nonatomic. Assume that

\mu(\{x\})=c>0
for some
x\in 2^{\mathbb{N}}
. Since there exists
y\in 2^{\mathbb{N}}
such that
\mu(\{y\})=0
, there are clopen sets

U,V
with
x\in U
and
y\in V
such that
0<\mu(V)<c
and
\mu(U)<c+\mu(V)
. Hence,
\mu(U)\geq c>\mu(V)
. The subset condition yields
a clopen set
W\subseteq U
with
\mu(W)=\mu(V)
. We infer
that
x\notin W
(as
\mu(W)<c
). Hence
\mu(W)\leq\mu(U\setminus\{x\})=\mu(U)-c<\mu(V)
, which contradicts our choice of
W
,
and finishes the proof.
∎

Let
{\mathcal{P}}
be the category of all finite partitions of the Cantor space
2^{\mathbb{N}}
into clopen sets (henceforth we refer to these as
clopen partitions
), with surjections given by the refinement relations as morphisms. Observe that
{\mathcal{P}}
has amalgamation. In the sequel, the letter
\pi
will always refer to morphisms in
{\mathcal{P}}
, e.g.,
\pi^{V}_{U}\colon V\rightarrow U
. If there is no risk of confusion, we will write
\pi
for
\pi^{V}_{U}
. In particular, a
\pi
-lift
is a lift by some refining partition.

Let
V
be a group-like set. We define
\mathcal{F}_{V}
to be the category of pairs
(P,\mu)
, where
P\in{\mathcal{P}}
and
\mu
is a probability distribution on
P
such that for every
x\in P
we have
\mu(x)\in V
and
\mu(x)>0
.

In
\mathcal{F}_{V}
, an
\mathcal{F}_{V}
-morphism
between
(P_{\nu},\nu)
and
(P_{\mu},\mu)
is a surjection
p\colon P_{\nu}\to P_{\mu}
in
{\mathcal{P}}
such that for every
x\in P_{\mu}
we have
\mu(x)=\sum_{y\in p^{-1}(\{x\})}\nu(y)
. Clearly, every chain
((P_{\mu_{n}},\mu_{n}),\pi_{n})
in
\mathcal{F}_{V}
(i.e.,
\pi_{n}
are morphisms in
{\mathcal{P}}
that are also morphisms in
\mathcal{F}_{V}
) determines a full measure
\mu
such that
\mu
is defined on the inverse limit of
(P_{\mu_{n}},\pi_{n})
(which is a zero-dimensional compact metrisable space) and the set of clopen values of
\mu
(the set of values that
\mu

attains on clopen sets) is a subset of
V
. Conversely, any full measure on
2^{\mathbb{N}}
(or more generally, on a zero-dimensional compact metrisable space) can be represented as such a chain. For a full measure
\mu
on
2^{\mathbb{N}}
, and
P,R\in{\mathcal{P}}
, we say that a surjection
p\colon R\to P
is a
\mu
-morphism
if it is an
\mathcal{F}_{V}
-morphism for restrictions of
\mu
to
P
and
R
. Note that for every
\mu
, every morphism
\pi
in
{\mathcal{P}}
determines
\mu
-morphism, but the converse is false.

A measure
\mu
on
2^{\mathbb{N}}
satisfies subset condition if and only if it is
V_{\mu}
-homogeneous as defined in
[
5
]
.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
\mu
be a full measure on
2^{\mathbb{N}}
and let
V=V_{\mu}
be the clopen values set of
\mu
. We say that
\mu
is
ultrahomogeneous
if for any
P,R\in{\mathcal{P}}
such that there is a bijection
f\colon P\to R
satisfying
\mu(x)=\mu(f(x))
for
x\in P
(for simplicity, we will call each such a function
f
a
partial
\mu
-isomorphism
), there exists a homeomorphism
\varphi\in\mbox{Homeo}(2^{\mathbb{N}},\mu)
such that for every
x\in P
it holds
\varphi[x]=f(x)
where
\varphi[x]
denotes the image of
x
via
\varphi
.

We say that a measure
\mu
on the Cantor space
2^{\mathbb{N}}
is
maximal
if for every tuple
v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}
of non-zero elements of
V(\mu)
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{i}=1
there exists a clopen partition
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
of
2^{\mathbb{N}}
such that
\mu(x_{i})=v_{i}
(
1\leq i\leq n
), and moreover for every
v,w\in V(\mu)
we have
|v-w|\in V(\mu)
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
V
be a group-like set. Then
\mathcal{F}_{V}
is countable, directed, and has amalgamation. In particular, there exists a maximal and ultrahomogeneous measure on
2^{\mathbb{N}}
with clopen values set equal to
V
.

Proof.

Clearly,
\mathcal{F}_{V}
is countable. Since directedness follows in this case from amalgamation, we only prove the latter.

Fix
(E_{1},\mu_{1})
,
(E_{2},\mu_{2})
,
(F,\nu)
in
\mathcal{F}_{V}
and consider morphisms
f_{i}\colon(E_{i},\mu_{i})\to(F,\nu)
,
(i=1,2)
. Enumerate
F
as
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{l}\}
. Fix
1\leq t\leq l
. Let

f_{1}^{-1}(x_{t})=\{y^{t}_{1},\ldots,y^{t}_{n(t)}\}\subseteq E_{1}\quad\text{and}\quad f_{2}^{-1}(x_{t})=\{z^{t}_{1},\ldots,z^{t}_{m(t)}\}\subseteq E_{2}.

By the assumption, we have

\nu(x_{t})=\sum_{i=1}^{n(t)}\mu_{1}(y^{t}_{i})=\sum_{j=1}^{m(t)}\mu_{2}(z^{t}_{j}).

Since
V
is group-like, by Lemma
2.1
there are
k(t)
and
w^{t}_{1},\ldots,w^{t}_{k(t)}\in V
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{k(t)}w^{t}_{i}=\nu(x_{t})
and there are partitions
\{Y^{t}_{i}:1\leq i\leq n(t)\}
and
\{Z^{t}_{j}:1\leq j\leq m(t)\}
of
\{1,\ldots,k(t)\}
so that for
1\leq i\leq n(t)
and
1\leq j\leq m(t)
it holds

\mu_{1}(y^{t}_{i})=\sum_{l\in Y^{t}_{i}}w^{t}_{l},\quad\text{and}\quad\mu_{2}(z^{t}_{j})=\sum_{l\in Z^{t}_{j}}w^{t}_{l}.

We take any clopen partition
G
of
2^{\mathbb{N}}
with
k(1)+\ldots+k(l)
elements. Then we enumerate elements of
G
so that
G
can be written as a disjoint union

G=\bigcup_{1\leq t\leq l}\{g^{t}_{1},\ldots,g^{t}_{k(t)}\}.

Finally, we set
(G,\mu_{G})\in\mathcal{F}_{V}
so that
\mu_{G}(g^{t}_{i})=w^{t}_{i}
, for
1\leq t\leq l
and
1\leq i\leq k(t)
. We define maps
p_{i}\colon(G,\mu_{G})\to(E_{i},\mu_{i})

(i=1,2)
, by setting

p_{1}(w^{t}_{l})=y_{i},\text{ where $1\leq i\leq n(t)$ is such that }l\in Y^{t}_{i}

and

p_{2}(w^{t}_{l})=z_{j},\text{ where $1\leq j\leq m(t)$ is such that }l\in Z^{t}_{j}.

We leave to the reader the straightforward verification that
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
are well-defined
\mathcal{F}_{V}
-morphisms and
f_{1}\circ p_{1}=f_{2}\circ p_{2}
, thus the amalgamation is verified.

Let
((P_{n},\mu_{n}),\pi_{n})
be a Fraïssé chain in
\sigma\mathcal{F}_{V}
. We claim that the inverse limit of
((P_{n},\mu_{n}),\pi_{n})
is a measure on
2^{\mathbb{N}}
that is full and moreover maximal and ultrahomogeneous. It is straightforward that the inverse limit of
P_{n}
’s is infinite and has no isolated points, thus we identify it with
2^{\mathbb{N}}
. Moreover, the inverse limit
\mu
of
\mu_{n}
’s defines a measure on
2^{\mathbb{N}}
. By the definition of
\mathcal{F}_{V}
,
\mu
is positive on every nonempty clopen set, therefore
\mu
is full. Since
V
is group-like, to check maximality, it suffices to check that for every tuple
v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}
of non-zero elements of
V
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{i}=1
there exists a clopen partition
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
of
2^{\mathbb{N}}
such that
\mu(x_{i})=v_{i}
(
1\leq i\leq n
). To see this, take
(P_{\nu},\nu)\in\mathcal{F}_{V}
, such that
P_{\nu}=\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}\}
and
\nu(z_{i})=v_{i}

(1\leq i\leq n)
. Since
((P_{n},\mu_{n}),\pi_{n})
is a Fraïssé chain in
\sigma\mathcal{F}_{V}
we find
j
such that there is a morphism
p\colon(P_{\mu_{j}},\mu_{j})\to(P_{\nu},\nu)
. Now, set
x_{i}=p^{-1}(z_{i})
(
1\leq i\leq n
).

Finally, the fact that
\mu
is ultrahomogeneous immediately follows from the fact that the Fraïssé chain
((P_{n},\mu_{n}),\pi_{n})
is homogeneous in the sense of
[
13
, Section 5]
.
∎

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
\mu
be a full measure on the Cantor space. Then
\mu
is maximal and ultrahomogeneous if and only if it is good.

Proof.

Suppose first that
\mu
is maximal and ultrahomogeneous. We need to check that
\mu
satisfies the subset condition. Let
V\subseteq[0,1]
be the clopen values set of
\mu
. Let
A
and
B
be clopen subsets of
2^{\mathbb{N}}
such that
\mu(A)<\mu(B)
. We may suppose that
\mu(B)<1
, otherwise
B=2^{\mathbb{N}}
and there is nothing to prove. Since
\mu
is maximal there exists a clopen partition
\{P_{1},P_{2},P_{3}\}
of
2^{\mathbb{N}}
such that
\mu(P_{1})=\mu(A)
,
\mu(P_{2})=\mu(B)-\mu(A)
, and
\mu(P_{3})=1-\mu(B)
. Define
f\colon\{P_{1}\cup P_{2},P_{3}\}\to\{B,2^{\mathbb{N}}\setminus B\}
by
f(P_{1}\cup P_{2})=B
and
f(P_{3})=2^{\mathbb{N}}\setminus B
. Since
f
is a morphism and
\mu
is ultrahomogeneous there exists a homeomorphism
\varphi\in\mbox{Homeo}(2^{\mathbb{N}},\mu)
such that
\varphi[P_{1}\cup P_{2}]=B
. Set
A^{\prime}:=\phi[P_{1}]
. Then
A^{\prime}\subseteq B
and
\mu(A^{\prime})=\mu(A)
. This shows that
\mu
satisfies the subset condition. Since
\mu
is a full measure by assumption,
\mu
must be nonatomic, hence it is good.

Conversely, suppose that
\mu
is good. Then by
[
2
, Proposition 2.4]
the clopen values set
V
of
\mu
is group-like. So by Proposition
3.3
there exists a maximal and ultrahomogeneous measure
\nu
with clopen values set
V
. By the first part of this proof,
\nu
is good. Since
\mu
and
\nu
are two good measures with the same clopen values set they must be conjugate (that is, there is
\varphi\in\mbox{Homeo}(2^{\mathbb{N}})
such that
\varphi^{*}(\mu)=\nu
) by
[
2
, Theorem 2.9 (a)]
(see also,
[
3
, Theorem 2.3 (d)]
). It is plain that a measure homeomorphic to a maximal and ultrahomogeneous measure is again maximal and ultrahomogeneous, and so is
\mu
.
∎

In view of Theorem
3.4
, ultrahomogeneous measures are a priori a weaker notion than good measures if they are not assumed to be maximal. We confirm this by providing examples of ultrahomogeneous measures that are not maximal, therefore they are not good. It may be interesting to study such measures and homeomorphisms preserving them in a similar fashion as for good measures. In a subsequent paper (that is, in the second part of the present article), we will propose a general approach to this issue. Among other things, we will give there an example of a full measure
\mu
on the Cantor space such that
\mu
is ultrahomogeneous and not good. Moreover, the action of
G=\mbox{Homeo}(2^{\mathbb{N}},\mu)
on
2^{\mathbb{N}}
is minimal and
G
has the Rokhlin property.

Example 3.5
.

Let
V_{1}
be the set of
3
-adic rationals inside
[0,1]
. Moreover, set
V_{2}:=\{0,1\}\cup\{n\cdot\alpha\;(\text{mod }1)\colon n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
, where
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
is some irrational number. Clearly, both
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
are group-like sets, and therefore there exist corresponding good measures
\mu^{\prime}_{i}
with clopen values set being equal to
V_{i}
, for
i=1,2
.

Write now the Cantor space as a disjoint union of two nonempty clopen sets
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
. Let
\mu_{1}
be the measure supported on
C_{1}
that is equivalent to
1/3\mu^{\prime}_{1}
via some homeomorphism between
2^{\mathbb{N}}
and
C_{1}
. Similarly, let
\mu_{2}
be the measure supported on
C_{2}
that is equivalent to
2/3\mu^{\prime}_{2}
via some homeomorphism between
2^{\mathbb{N}}
and
C_{2}
. Finally, set
\mu:=\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}
. Clearly,
\mu
is a full measure on the Cantor space. We claim it is ultrahomogeneous, but not maximal. For ultrahomogeneity, take two clopen partitions
\mathcal{P}_{1}
and
\mathcal{P}_{2}
such that there is a bijection
f:\mathcal{P}_{1}\to\mathcal{P}_{2}
satisfying
\mu(P)=\mu(f(P))
, for every
P\in\mathcal{P}_{1}
. Without loss of generality,
we may assume that every
P\in\mathcal{P}_{i}
, for
i=1,2
, is either a subset of
C_{1}
or a subset of
C_{2}
. Indeed, if
A_{i},A^{\prime}_{i}\subseteq C_{i}
,
i=1,2
, are clopen sets such that
\mu(A_{1})+\mu(A_{2})=\mu(A^{\prime}_{1})+\mu(A^{\prime}_{2})
then
\mu(A_{i})=\mu(A^{\prime}_{i})
for
i=1,2
, since otherwise
\mu(A_{1})-\mu(A^{\prime}_{1})
would be an irrational number, a contradiction.

If
P\in\mathcal{P}_{1}
is a subset of
C_{1}
then
\mu(P)
is rational, therefore also
f(P)\subseteq C_{1}
. Analogously, if
P\in\mathcal{P}_{2}
is a subset of
C_{2}
then also
f(P)\subseteq C_{2}
. Since
\mu^{\prime}_{i}
,
i=1,2
, are ultrahomogeneous there exist measure preserving homeomorphisms
\phi_{i}\colon C_{i}\to C_{i}
,
i=1,2
, such that for every
P\in\mathcal{P}_{1}
a subset of
C_{1}
, resp. a subset of
C_{2}
, we have
\phi_{1}(P)=f(P)
, resp.
\phi_{2}(P)=f(P)
. These two homeomorphisms together form the desired measure preserving homeomorphism on
2^{\mathbb{N}}
.

Finally, we show that
\mu
is not maximal, therefore not good. The clopen values set
V
of
\mu
is
1/3V_{1}+2/3V_{2}
. Therefore
1/3\in V
. If
\mu
were maximal there would exist a clopen partition
\{P_{1},P_{2},P_{3}\}
such that
\mu(P_{i})=1/3
, for
i=1,2,3
. It is clear that by the definition of
\mu
such a partition does not exist. Note also that
\mbox{Homeo}(2^{\mathbb{N}},\mu)
is quite rich, but its action is not transitive.

Example 3.6
.

Although ultrahomogeneity has a quite natural definition (introduced in
Definition
3.2
) which may lead to
a supposition that the automorphism group of an ultrahomogeneous measure is
rich, actually this property does not even imply that this group is nontrivial.
To convince oneself of that, observe that there exists a full non-atomic measure

\mu
on the Cantor space such that on the algebra of all clopen subsets of

2^{\mathbb{N}}
it is both rational-valued and one-to-one. Then each partial
\mu
-isomorphism coincides with the identity map and hence
\mu
is ultrahomogeneous. Hence,
\mbox{Homeo}(2^{\mathbb{N}},\mu)
is trivial, as the only homeomorphism preserving
\mu
is the identity. This measure cannot be maximal, as then it would be good, so it would be preserved by a non-trivial homeomorphism.

(It is worth mentioning here that Akin

[
1
]
constructed an uncountable family
\mathcal{M}^{*}
of full non-atomic
measures on the Cantor space
2^{\mathbb{N}}
such that if for some continuous map
h\colon 2^{\mathbb{N}}\to 2^{\mathbb{N}}
and
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\in\mathcal{M}^{*}
we have
h_{*}(\mu_{1})=\mu_{2}
, then

\mu_{1}=\mu_{2}
and
h
is the identity map. This is in sharp contrast with
a classical theorem of Oxtoby and Ulam
[
15
]
stating that any two full
non-atomic measures on a Euclidean ball are homeomorphic.)

General ultrahomogeneous measures are studied in detail in the forthcoming second part of
the present article
[
6
]
. We focus there on those of them whose automorphism groups act transitively on the Cantor space.

For the purpose of the next result, we recall classical notions and introduce
a few auxiliary ones. Let
\operatorname{Prob}(2^{\mathbb{N}})
stand for the simplex of all
(probability) measures on the Cantor space, equipped with the standard
weak
∗

topology (that is,
\operatorname{Prob}(2^{\mathbb{N}})
is a metrisable compact convex set in
which measures
\mu_{n}
tend to
\mu
iff the integrals
\int_{2^{\mathbb{N}}}f\,\textup{d}\mu_{n}
tend to
\int_{2^{\mathbb{N}}}f\,\textup{d}\mu
for any real-valued
continuous function on
2^{\mathbb{N}}
; equivalently,
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu_{n}=\mu

iff
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu_{n}(U)=\mu(U)
for all clopen sets
U\subseteq 2^{\mathbb{N}}
.) For a fixed group-like set
V
denote by

\operatorname{Full}(V)
the set of all full measures whose clopen values set is
contained in
V
. We consider
\operatorname{Full}(V)
with the weak
∗
topology (that
is, the topology induced from the weak
∗
one of
\operatorname{Prob}(2^{\mathbb{N}})
), and with
strong
topology, that is, the topology of pointwise convergence on clopen sets w.r.t. the discrete topology of
V
. More precisely, the strong topology of
\operatorname{Full}(V)
is a metrisable topology in which measures
\mu_{n}\in\operatorname{Full}(V)
tend to
\mu\in\operatorname{Full}(V)
iff for any clopen set
U

the sequence
\mu_{n}(U)
is eventually constant and equal to
\mu(U)
. Note that
\operatorname{Full}(V)
with the weak
∗
topology is non-Polish in general.

Below we consider
\operatorname{Full}(V)
with the natural action of
\mbox{Homeo}(2^{\mathbb{N}})
.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
V
and
\mu
be, respectively, a group-like set and a good measure whose clopen values set coincides with
V
.
Further, set
H
to be the conjugacy class of
\mu
, that is,
H=\{h^{*}(\mu)\colon h\in\mbox{Homeo}(2^{\mathbb{N}})\}
. Then:

(a)

The strong topology is Polish and stronger than the
weak
∗
topology on
\operatorname{Full}(V)
.

(b)

In the weak* topology,
\operatorname{Full}(V)
is an
\mathscr{F}_{\sigma\delta}
subset of

\operatorname{Prob}(2^{\mathbb{N}})
.

(c)

In the weak
∗
topology,
H
is a dense
\mathscr{F}_{\sigma\delta}

subset of
\operatorname{Full}(V)
.

(d)

In the strong topology,
H
is a unique dense
\mathscr{G}_{\delta}
conjugacy class in

\operatorname{Full}(V)
.

Proof.

Part (a) is standard and left to the reader (recall that
V
is countable). To
show (b), observe that
\operatorname{Full}(V)
coincides with
\bigcap_{U}\bigcup_{v}K(U,v)
where
U
and
v
run over all, respectively, nonempty clopen
subsets of
2^{\mathbb{N}}
and positive numbers from
V
, and
K(U,v)
is
the (compact) set of all
\nu\in\operatorname{Prob}(2^{\mathbb{N}})
satisfying
\nu(U)=v
.
Now we pass to both (c) and (d). Note that a measure
\lambda\in\operatorname{Full}(V)
belongs to
H
iff
\lambda
is good and its clopen values
set is
V
, or—equivalently—if for any nonempty clopen set
U
and each

v\in V
that is less than
\lambda(U)
there exists a clopen set
W\subseteq U

such that
\lambda(W)=v
. In other words,
H
coincides with

\bigcap_{U,v}\bigcup_{W}E(U,v,W)
where
U
and
W
run over all
nonempty clopen sets such that
W\subseteq U
, and
v
runs over all
positive numbers from
V
, and
E(U,v,W)
consists of all measures
\nu\in\operatorname{Full}(V)
for which
\nu(U)\leq v
or
\nu(W)=v
. It is readily
seen that
E(U,v,W)
is closed in the weak
∗
topology and open in the strong
one. So, it remains to show that
H
is dense in the strong topology (thanks
to (a); note also that uniqueness in part (d) follows from the Baire category
theorem). To this end, fix any measure
\nu\in\operatorname{Full}(V)
and observe
that the sets of the form

B(\nu,P)=\{\rho\in\operatorname{Full}(V)\colon\ \rho(p)=\nu(p)\ (\forall p\in P)\},

where
P
is a clopen partition of
2^{\mathbb{N}}
, form a basis of neighbourhoods of

\nu
in the strong topology. But if
P
is a clopen partition and
\nu\in\operatorname{Full}(V)
, then it
follows from both ultrahomogeneity and maximality of
\mu
that there exists

h\in\mbox{Homeo}(2^{\mathbb{N}})
such that
h^{*}(\mu)
extends the restriction of
\nu

to
P
, hence
h^{*}(\mu)\in B(\nu,P)
and we are done.
∎

4.
Amalgamation and conjugacy classes

An
equi-summed
matrix is a real-valued matrix
A=(a_{p,p^{\prime}})_{p,p^{\prime}\in P}
with nonnegative entries, where
P
, denoted also by
P_{A}
, is a finite set, such that

(1)

\sum_{p^{\prime}\in P}a_{p,p^{\prime}}=\sum_{p^{\prime}\in P}a_{p^{\prime},p}\qquad\text{ for all }p\in P.

An equi-summed matrix is
balanced
if it satisfies

(2)

\sum_{p,p^{\prime}\in P}a_{p,p^{\prime}}=1.

We routinely identify
A=(a_{p,p^{\prime}})_{p,p^{\prime}\in P}
with the corresponding labelled directed graph
G(A)
. That is,
G(A)
is the graph whose vertex set is the subset of
P_{A}
corresponding to non-zero rows of
A
and labelled directed edges of
G(A)
correspond to non-zero entries of
A
meaning that
a_{p,p^{\prime}}>0
if and only if there is an edge in
G(A)
from
p
to
p^{\prime}
labelled by
a_{p,p^{\prime}}
and denoted
p\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a_{p,p^{\prime}}}}{{\rightarrow}}p^{\prime}
(or simply
p\to p^{\prime}
if the label is irrelevant).
Note that (
1
) says that
A
is equi-summed if for every vertex
p
of
G(A)
the sum of labels of all edges terminating at
p
is equal to the sum of labels of edges outgoing from
p
.

Let
A=(a_{p,p^{\prime}})_{p,p^{\prime}\in P}
,
B=(b_{q,q^{\prime}})_{q,q^{\prime}\in Q}
be balanced matrices. A
balanced morphism

f
from
B
to
A
is a surjection
f\colon Q\to P
such that for every
p,p^{\prime}\in P
we have

a_{p,p^{\prime}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\in f^{-1}(p),\\
q^{\prime}\in f^{-1}(p^{\prime})\end{subarray}}b_{q,q^{\prime}}.

A
cycle matrix
is an equi-summed matrix
C
such that
G(C)
is a single directed cycle. Since
C
is equi-summed, all non-zero entries in
C
are equal, and we call this number the
weight
of
C
. The following seems to be a folklore fact:

Lemma 4.1
.

Every equi-summed matrix
A
is a sum of cycle matrices, whose weights belong to the additive subgroup of
\mathbb{R}
generated by the entries of
A
.

Proof.

The proof goes by induction on the number
m
of non-zero entries of
A
. For
m=1
, this is obvious. Suppose the lemma is true for some
m
, and let
A
be an equi-summed matrix with
m+1
non-zero entries. Observe that (
1
) ensures that there there is a cycle in
G(A)
, i.e., there are
k\geq 0
and
i_{0},i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}=i_{0}\in P_{A}
such that
i_{m}\neq i_{n}
for
0\leq m<n<k
and

a_{i_{0},i_{1}},a_{i_{1},i_{2}},\ldots,a_{i_{k-1},i_{k}}=a_{i_{k-1},i_{0}}>0.

Let
a=\min\{a_{i_{j},i_{j+1}}:0\leq j<k\}
, and let
C
be the cycle matrix corresponding to the cycle

i_{0}\to i_{1}\to i_{2}\to\ldots\to i_{k-1}\to i_{k}=i_{0}

with weight
a
. Then
A^{\prime}=A-C
has at most
m
non-zero entries, and it is equi-summed. Clearly, all the entries in
A^{\prime}
are in the subgroup generated by the entries of
A
. By the inductive assumption,
A^{\prime}
is a sum of cycle matrices with weights in the subgroup generated by the entries of
A
.
∎

Let
V
be a group-like set, let
((P_{n},\mu_{n}),\pi_{n})
be a Fraïssé chain from
\mathcal{F}_{V}
, and let
\mu_{V}
be the good measure on
2^{\mathbb{N}}
determined by
((P_{n},\mu_{n}),\pi_{n})
. Let
{\mathcal{M}}_{V}
be the category of all
V
-valued balanced matrices
A=(a_{p,p^{\prime}})_{p,p^{\prime}\in P}
such that
P
is a clopen partition of
2^{\mathbb{N}}
and
A
is
compatible with
\mu_{V}
meaning that

(3)

\sum_{p^{\prime}\in P}a_{p,p^{\prime}}=\mu_{V}(p)\qquad(\forall p\in P).

If
P
and
Q
are different partitions of
2^{\mathbb{N}}
, then two matrices
A=(a_{p,p^{\prime}})_{p,p^{\prime}\in P}
and
B=(b_{q,q^{\prime}})_{q,q^{\prime}\in Q}
in
{\mathcal{M}}_{V}
are considered different, although it may happen that there is a bijection
\psi\colon P\to Q
such that

a_{p,p^{\prime}}=b_{\psi(p),\psi(p^{\prime})}\qquad(\forall p,p^{\prime}\in P).

Since we consider only clopen partitions with nonempty cells, all rows of
A=(a_{p,p^{\prime}})_{p,p^{\prime}\in P}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{V}
are non-zero.

Morphisms in
{\mathcal{M}}_{V}
are balanced morphisms that are also
\mu_{V}
-morphisms between compatible partitions. In other words, a balanced morphism
f
between matrices
B
and
A
in

{\mathcal{M}}_{V}
is a morphism in
{\mathcal{M}}_{V}
if the transformation between the sets of indices
P_{B}\to P_{A}
that determines

is given by an
\mu_{V}
-morphism (see Section
3
). Observe that if
B\in{\mathcal{M}}_{V}
and

, where

, is a
\mu_{V}
-morphism, then there is
A=(a_{p,p^{\prime}})_{p,p^{\prime}\in P}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{V}
such that
A
is compatible with
P
and and

becomes an
{\mathcal{M}}_{V}
-morphism that maps
B
onto
A
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}_{V}\subseteq{\mathcal{M}}_{V}
be the subcategory consisting of balanced matrices
A\in{\mathcal{M}}_{V}
such that there is a single non-zero entry for each row of
A
.
For
A\in{\mathcal{C}}_{V}
we easily see that
G(A)
consists of disjoint cycles, that is, one can partition vertices of
G(A)
into disjoint sets
\{S_{1},\ldots,S_{d}\}
so that

restricted to

is a single directed cycle (of size

).

Let
A=(a_{p,p^{\prime}})_{p,p^{\prime}\in P}
be a balanced
V
-valued square matrix with non-zero rows. Since
\mu_{V}
is maximal, using (
2
) we easily find a partition

such that the elements of

are in a one-to-one correspondence with indices of
P
of

and (
3
) holds.

Therefore every
V
-valued balanced matrix
A=(a_{p,p^{\prime}})_{p,p^{\prime}\in P}
with non-zero rows is
\mu_{V}
compatible with some clopen partition of
2^{\mathbb{N}}
. We adopt a convention that for

we write

to denote a
\mu_{V}
-compatible partition of

that is the set of indices of

.

Because
\mu_{V}
is maximal, Lemma
4.1
immediately gives:

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
V
be group-like. Then
{\mathcal{C}}_{V}
is cofinal in
{\mathcal{M}}_{V}
.

Given
R,Q\in{\mathcal{P}}
and
\mu_{V}
-morphism

, we set

[p]=\{f\in\mbox{Homeo}(2^{\mathbb{N}},\mu_{V}):f[r]\subseteq p(r)\,(\forall r\in R)\}.

In other words,
[p]
consists of those
f\in\mbox{Homeo}(2^{\mathbb{N}},\mu_{V})
that send each cell

of the partition

into the cell

of the partition

.

The collection
[p]
where
p
runs over all
\mu_{V}
-morphisms is a basis for the compact-open topology on
\mbox{Homeo}(2^{\mathbb{N}},\mu_{V})
. It is a standard fact in the theory of Fraïssé limits (see
[
13
]
), that every morphism extends to an automorphism of the limit, i.e.,
[p]\neq\emptyset
for every
\mu_{V}
-morphism

.

We call
\varphi\in\mbox{Homeo}(2^{\mathbb{N}},\mu_{V})

compatible
with
A=(a_{p,p^{\prime}})_{p,p^{\prime}\in P}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{V}
if
\mu_{V}(\varphi[p]\cap p^{\prime})=a_{p,p^{\prime}}
for all

.
For every

we set

[A]=\{\varphi\in\mbox{Homeo}(2^{\mathbb{N}},\mu_{V}):\varphi\text{ is compatible with $A$}\}.

We distinguish a special class among all
{\mathcal{M}}_{V}
-morphism.
We say that
B
is a
\pi
-lift
of

if it is a
\pi^{P_{B}}_{P_{A}}
-lift of

, that is,

is a lift of

via
{\mathcal{M}}_{V}
morphism determined by
\pi^{P_{B}}_{P_{A}}
. Clearly,
[A]\supseteq[B]
if

-lift of

.

Proposition 4.3
.

The family
\{[A]:A\in{\mathcal{M}}_{V}\}
is a basis for the compact-open topology on
\mbox{Homeo}(2^{\mathbb{N}},\mu_{V})
. Moreover, for every
A\in{\mathcal{M}}_{V}
, the family

\{[B]:B\text{ is a $\pi$-lift of }A\}

is a basis for
[A]
.

Proof.

Clearly, for every
A\in{\mathcal{M}}_{V}
the set
[A]
is clopen in
\mbox{Homeo}(2^{\mathbb{N}},\mu_{V})
. Fix
f\in\mbox{Homeo}(2^{\mathbb{N}},\mu_{V})
. Let

be such that

. Choose a common refinement

of

and

. Define

. It is clear that

and

(actually only the pairs
(q,q^{\prime})
of indices for which

is non-zero matter). The other statement is proved in the same way.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
A\in{\mathcal{C}}_{V}
, and, for some
R\in{\mathcal{P}}
, let
p\colon R\rightarrow P_{A}
be a
\mu_{V}
-morphism. There is
B\in{\mathcal{M}}_{V}
such that
P_{B}=R
and

is an
{\mathcal{M}}_{V}
morphism. from

onto

.

Proof.

It is enough to consider each cycle separately.
Suppose
x_{0}\to\ldots\to x_{m}\in P_{A}
is a cycle in
G(A)
(i.e.,
x_{0}=x_{m})
, where
m\geq 1
with weight
1/m
.

Fix
0\leq k<m
and set
Y^{0}=p^{-1}(x_{k})
and
Y^{1}=p^{-1}(x_{k+1})
(recall that
x_{0}=x_{m}
). Take

. For each
y\in Y^{i}\subseteq R
define
x^{i}_{y}=\mu_{V}(y)\in V
.
Note that

\sum_{y\in Y^{i}}x^{i}_{y}=\frac{1}{m}.

Use Lemma
2.1
to find the set
\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}\}\subseteq V
and two partition
X^{0}
and
X^{1}
of
\{1,\ldots,n\}
such that for

and every

there is the unique cell

x^{i}_{y}=\mu_{V}(y)=\sum_{j\in Z^{i}_{y}}z_{j}.

For every
y\in Y^{0}
and

we set

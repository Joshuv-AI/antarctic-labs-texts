Sections and projections of the outer and inner regularizations of a convex body
Natalia Tziotziou

Abstract
We establish new geometric inequalities comparing the volumes of sections and projections of a convex body, whose barycenter or Santaló point is at the origin, with those of its inner and outer regularizations. We also provide functional extensions of these inequalities to the setting of log-concave functions. Our approach relies on the recent optimal M M -estimate of Bizeul and Klartag for isotropic convex bodies.



1
Introduction

The purpose of this work is to establish new geometric inequalities comparing
the volumes of sections and projections of a convex body—whose barycenter or
Santaló point lies at the origin—with those of its inner and outer regularizations.
Our method highlights the role of isotropic normalization as a unifying
tool bridging symmetric and general convex bodies.
These results extend naturally to the functional setting,
yielding analogous inequalities for log-concave functions that satisfy
volume-type bounds similar to those for convex bodies.
A key ingredient in our approach is the recent optimal
M
-estimate
of Bizeul and Klartag for isotropic convex bodies, which provides the
quantitative foundation for our main results.

Throughout, let
K
be a convex body in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
0\in{\rm int}(K)
. We define the
outer
and
inner regularizations
of
K
by

K_{\mathrm{out}}={\rm conv}(K,-K)\hskip 9.24994pt\text{and}\hskip 9.24994ptK_{\mathrm{in}}=K\cap(-K).

Clearly,
K_{\mathrm{in}}\subseteq K\subseteq K_{\mathrm{out}}
, and both
K_{\mathrm{out}}
and
K_{\mathrm{in}}
are origin-symmetric. We shall frequently use the duality relations

(1.1)

(K_{\mathrm{out}})^{\circ}=(K^{\circ})_{\mathrm{in}}\hskip 9.24994pt\text{and}\hskip 9.24994pt(K_{\mathrm{in}})^{\circ}=(K^{\circ})_{\mathrm{out}},

which follow from the identities

({\rm conv}(A\cup B))^{\circ}=A^{\circ}\cap B^{\circ}\hskip 9.24994pt\text{and}\hskip 9.24994pt(A\cap B)^{\circ}={\rm conv}(A^{\circ}\cup B^{\circ})

valid for convex bodies
A,B
with
0\in{\rm int}(A\cap B)
. Here
A^{\circ}
denotes the polar body of
A
.

We write
{\rm bar}(A)
for the barycenter

{\rm bar}(A)=\frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}_{n}(A)}\int_{A}x\,dx

of a convex body
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
{\rm vrad}(A)
for its
volume radius
,

{\rm vrad}(A)=\left(\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{n}(A)}{\mathrm{vol}_{n}(B_{2}^{n})}\right)^{1/n},

where
B_{2}^{n}
denotes the Euclidean unit ball and
\mathrm{vol}_{n}
stands for
n
-dimensional volume.

Our first result compares the volumes of the projections of
K_{\mathrm{out}}
and
K_{\mathrm{in}}
onto a
k
-dimensional subspace
H
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
K
be a convex body in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with either
{\rm bar}(K)=0
or
{\rm bar}(K^{\circ})=0
. Then, for every
1\leqslant k\leqslant n-1
and any
H\in G_{n,k}
,

{\rm vrad}(P_{H}(K_{\mathrm{out}}))\leqslant\frac{ng(n)}{k}\,{\rm vrad}(P_{H}(K_{\mathrm{in}})),

where
g(n)\leqslant C(\ln n)^{3}
.

A related estimate was obtained by Vritsiou in
[
43
, Corollary 11]
, who proved that if
{\rm bar}(K)=0
then

{\rm vrad}(P_{H}(K_{\mathrm{out}}))\leqslant\left(\frac{n}{k}\right)^{5}(\ln(en/k))^{2}\,{\rm vrad}(P_{H}(K_{\mathrm{in}}))

for all
1\leqslant k\leqslant n-1
and
H\in G_{n,k}
. Theorem
1.1
thus yields an estimate that is nearly linear in
n/k
.

Our second result concerns sections of
K_{\mathrm{out}}
and
K_{\mathrm{in}}
by a
k
-dimensional subspace
H\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
K
be a convex body in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with either
{\rm bar}(K)=0
or
{\rm bar}(K^{\circ})=0
.
Then, for every
1\leqslant k\leqslant n-1
and any
H\in G_{n,k}
,

{\rm vrad}(K_{\mathrm{out}}\cap H)\leqslant\frac{ng(n)}{k}\,{\rm vrad}(K_{\mathrm{in}}\cap H),

where
g(n)\leqslant C(\ln n)^{3}
.

It is instructive to compare Theorem
1.2
with an inequality due to Rudelson
[
38
]
, who showed that if
K
is a convex body in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then for all
1\leqslant k\leqslant n-1
and all
H\in G_{n,k}
,

{\rm vrad}((K-K)\cap H)\leqslant c\,\min\{n/k,\sqrt{k}\}\,\max_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\rm vrad}(K\cap(x+H)),

where
c>0
is an absolute constant. Fradelizi further proved in
[
15
]
that

\max_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\rm vrad}(K\cap(x+H))\leqslant\frac{n+1}{k+1}\,{\rm vrad}(K\cap({\rm bar}(K)+H))^{1/k}.

Combining these results yields

(1.2)

{\rm vrad}((K-K)\cap H)\leqslant c\left(\frac{n}{k}\right)^{2}{\rm vrad}(K\cap({\rm bar}(K)+H))

for all
1\leqslant k\leqslant n-1
and
H\in G_{n,k}
. Since
K_{\mathrm{in}}\subseteq K
and
K-K\subseteq 2K_{\mathrm{out}}
, Theorem
1.2
immediately implies a version of (
1.2
) with improved dependence on
n/k
.

Combining Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
yields the following Blaschke–Santaló type inequality for projections of possibly non-symmetric convex bodies.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
K
be a convex body in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that either
{\rm bar}(K)=0
or
{\rm bar}(K^{\circ})=0
. Then, for every
1\leqslant k\leqslant n-1
and
H\in G_{n,k}
,

{\rm vrad}(P_{H}(K))\,{\rm vrad}(K^{\circ}\cap H)\leqslant\frac{ng(n)}{k},

where
g(n)\leqslant C(\ln n)^{3}
.

We include the proof in Section
5
to highlight that the three theorems above are closely related. A stronger version of Theorem
1.3
was proved by Vritsiou
[
43
]
, who established that

(1.3)

{\rm vrad}(P_{H}(K))\,{\rm vrad}(K^{\circ}\cap H)\leqslant\frac{cn}{k},

for some absolute constant
c>0
. Moreover, (
1.3
) is sharp: as shown in
[
43
]
, if
S_{n}
is a regular simplex of edge length
\sqrt{2}
with barycenter at the origin, then the affine hull of any
k
of its vertices, together with the average of the remaining
n+1-k
vertices, forms a subspace
H_{k}\in G_{n,k}
for which

{\rm vrad}(P_{H_{k}}(S_{n}^{\circ}))\,{\rm vrad}(S_{n}\cap H_{k})\approx\frac{n}{k}.

For the proofs of the above theorems, we may assume without loss of generality that
K
(or
K^{\circ}
) is isotropic (see
below and in Section
2
for precise definitions and background). Under this assumption, stronger estimates can
be obtained for a random subspace
H\in G_{n,k}
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
K
be an isotropic convex body in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then, for any
1\leqslant k\leqslant n-1
, a random subspace
H\in G_{n,k}
satisfies

(1.4)

{\rm vrad}(K_{\mathrm{out}}\cap H)\leqslant\gamma_{n}\,{\rm vrad}(K_{\mathrm{in}}\cap H),

with probability greater than
1-e^{-k}
, where
\gamma_{n}\leqslant C(\ln n)^{2}
. Moreover, with the same probability, one also has

(1.5)

{\rm vrad}(K_{\mathrm{out}}\cap H)\leqslant\delta_{n}\,{\rm vrad}(K_{\mathrm{in}}\cap H),

where
\delta_{n}\leqslant C\ln n
.

We also establish functional analogues of Theorems
1.2
and
1.1
for the class
\mathcal{L}^{n}
of geometric log-concave integrable functions. These are the centered log-concave functions
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,\infty)
satisfying
f(0)=\|f\|_{\infty}
and
0<\int f<\infty
. For any
f\in\mathcal{L}^{n}
, define

\Delta_{\mathrm{out}}f(x)=\sup\Big\{\sqrt{f(x_{1})f(-x_{2})}:x=\tfrac{1}{2}(x_{1}+x_{2})\Big\}\hskip 9.24994pt\text{and}\hskip 9.24994pt\Delta_{\mathrm{in}}f(x)=\min\{f(x),f(-x)\}.

If
f=\mathds{1}_{K}
for some convex body
K
, then
\Delta_{\mathrm{out}}f=\mathds{1}_{\frac{1}{2}(K-K)}
and
\Delta_{\mathrm{in}}f=\mathds{1}_{K\cap(-K)}
. Moreover, for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, one has
\Delta_{\mathrm{in}}f(x)\leqslant f(x)
and
\Delta_{\mathrm{in}}f(x)\leqslant\Delta_{\mathrm{out}}f(x)
. The following theorem extends Theorem
1.2
to this functional setting.

Theorem 1.5
.

For any
f\in\mathcal{L}^{n}
, any
1\leqslant k\leqslant n-1
, and any
H\in G_{n,k}
,

(1.6)

\left(\int_{H}\Delta_{\mathrm{out}}f(x)\,dx\right)^{1/k}\leqslant C(n/k)^{2}g(n)\left(\int_{H}\Delta_{\mathrm{in}}f(x)\,dx\right)^{1/k},

and

(1.7)

c\left(\int_{H}f(x)\,dx\right)^{1/k}\leqslant\left(\int_{H}\Delta_{\mathrm{out}}f(x)\,dx\right)^{1/k},

where
c>0
and
C>1
are absolute constants.

Given a nonnegative measurable function
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,\infty)
and
H\in G_{n,k}
, the orthogonal projection
of
g
onto
H
is defined by

(P_{H}g)(z)=\sup\{g(y+z):y\in H^{\perp}\}.

With this definition, we obtain the following functional analogue of Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 1.6
.

For any
f\in\mathcal{L}^{n}
, any
1\leqslant k\leqslant n-1
, and any
H\in G_{n,k}
,

(1.8)

\left(\int_{H}P_{H}(\Delta_{\mathrm{out}}f)(x)\,dx\right)^{1/k}\leqslant C(n/k)^{2}g(n)\left(\int_{H}P_{H}(\Delta_{\mathrm{in}}f)(x)\,dx\right)^{1/k},

where
C>0
is an absolute constant.

To prove Theorems
1.5
and
1.6
, we employ the family of K. Ball’s bodies
K_{p}(f)
associated with a log-concave function
f
, allowing us to transfer the problem to the setting of convex bodies and apply Theorems
1.2
and
1.1
accordingly.

The foundation of our results lies in recent advances concerning isotropic convex bodies and, in particular, in the sharp
MM^{\ast}
-estimate. We denote by
p_{K}(x)=\inf\{t>0:x\in tK\}
the Minkowski functional of
K
, and by
h_{K}(y)=\max\{\langle x,y\rangle:x\in K\}
its support function. The parameters

M(K)=\int_{S^{n-1}}p_{K}(x)\,d\sigma(x)\hskip 9.24994pt\text{and}\hskip 9.24994ptM^{\ast}(K)=\int_{S^{n-1}}h_{K}(x)\,d\sigma(x),

where
\sigma
denotes the rotationally invariant probability measure on the unit sphere
S^{n-1}
, play a central role in the asymptotic theory of finite-dimensional normed spaces. It is well known that
M(K)M^{\ast}(K)\geqslant 1
for every convex body
K
with
0\in{\rm int}(K)
.

A classical result of Figiel–Tomczak-Jaegermann
[
14
]
, Lewis
[
23
]
, and Pisier
[
32
]
asserts that for any symmetric convex body
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
there exists
T\in GL_{n}
such that

M(TK)M^{\ast}(TK)\leqslant c\ln n,

where
c>0
is an absolute constant. Without assuming symmetry, it is natural to consider

E(K)=\inf M(TK)M^{\ast}(TK),

where the infimum runs over all invertible affine transformations
T
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfying
0\in{\rm int}(TK)
. It turns out that the isotropic position yields a sharp upper bound for
E(K)
.

A convex body
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is called
isotropic
if it has volume
1
, its barycenter is at the origin, and there exists a constant
L_{K}>0
such that

\int_{K}\langle x,\xi\rangle^{2}\,dx=L_{K}^{2}\hskip 9.24994pt\text{for all }\xi\in S^{n-1}.

Every convex body
K
admits an isotropic affine image, unique up to orthogonal transformations (see
[
28
]
). Using this canonical position, one defines the isotropic constant
L_{K}
, an affine invariant of
K
.

A central question in asymptotic convex geometry, posed by Bourgain
[
8
]
, asked whether there exists an absolute constant
C>0
such that

L_{n}:=\max\{L_{K}:K\text{ isotropic convex body in }\mathbb{R}^{n}\}\leqslant C

for all
n\geqslant 1
. This was recently resolved affirmatively by Klartag and Lehec
[
21
]
, following major progress by Guan
[
18
]
, and soon afterward an alternative proof was given by Bizeul
[
5
]
.

E. Milman proved in
[
25
]
that if
K
is isotropic in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, then

(1.9)

M^{\ast}(K)\leqslant C\sqrt{n}(\ln n)^{2}L_{K}\leqslant c_{1}\sqrt{n}(\ln n)^{2},

where the second inequality uses the boundedness of
L_{n}
. The dependence on
n
is optimal up to the logarithmic term.

The dual estimate for
M(K)
in the isotropic position was obtained recently by Bizeul and Klartag
[
6
]
, who showed that

(1.10)

M(K)\leqslant c_{2}\frac{\log n}{\sqrt{n}}.

Combining (
1.9
) and (
1.10
) gives

(1.11)

E(K)\leqslant c_{3}(\ln n)^{3}

for every convex body
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Since
M(K)M^{\ast}(K)\geqslant 1
holds universally, (
1.11
) provides a sharp upper bound for
E(K)
up to a factor of
(\ln n)^{3}
.

In Section
2
we review these results in more detail, and in Section
3
we collect classical geometric inequalities relevant to our setting.

Our approach exploits these refined
M
- and
M^{\ast}
-estimates to derive strong regularity estimates for covering numbers of isotropic convex bodies. These, in turn, allow a careful comparison of sections and projections of the inner and outer symmetrizations
K_{\mathrm{in}}
and
K_{\mathrm{out}}
, leading directly to Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
. In particular, the isotropic position serves not only as a convenient normalization but also as a tool for transferring results from the symmetric to the general, non-symmetric setting. This strategy underpins the functional extensions presented in Theorems
1.5
and
1.6
, showing that the geometric inequalities naturally extend to the broader class of log-concave functions.

2
Notation and background

We work in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, equipped with the standard inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
.
The corresponding Euclidean norm is denoted by
|\cdot|
, the Euclidean unit ball by
B_{2}^{n}
, and the unit sphere by
S^{n-1}
.
Volume in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is denoted by
\mathrm{vol}_{n}
, while
\omega_{n}=\mathrm{vol}_{n}(B_{2}^{n})
stands for the volume of the Euclidean unit ball.

We denote by
\sigma
the rotationally invariant probability measure on
S^{n-1}
and by
\nu
the Haar measure on
O(n)
.
The Grassmann manifold
G_{n,k}
of
k
-dimensional subspaces of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is equipped with the Haar probability measure
\nu_{n,k}
.
For each integer
1\leqslant k\leqslant n-1
and every
H\in G_{n,k}
, we denote by
P_{H}
the orthogonal projection from
\mathbb{R}^{n}
onto
H
, and set

B_{H}:=B_{2}^{n}\cap H,\hskip 18.49988ptS_{H}:=S^{n-1}\cap H.

The letters
c,c^{\prime},c_{1},c_{2},\ldots
denote absolute positive constants whose value may change from line to line.
Whenever we write
a\approx b
, we mean that there exist absolute constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0
such that
c_{1}a\leqslant b\leqslant c_{2}a
.
Similarly, for convex bodies
K,C\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we write
K\approx C
if there exist absolute constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0
such that

c_{1}K\subseteq C\subseteq c_{2}K
.

A
convex body
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a compact convex subset
K
with nonempty interior.
We say that
K
is
symmetric
if
K=-K
, and
centered
if its barycenter
{\rm bar}(K)
is at the origin.

The
radial function
of a convex body
K
with
0\in{\rm int}(K)
is

\rho_{K}(x)=\max\{t>0:tx\in K\},\hskip 18.49988ptx\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\},

and the
support function
of
K
is defined for
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
by

h_{K}(y)=\max\{\langle x,y\rangle:x\in K\}.

The
radius
of
K
is
R(K)=\max\{|x|:x\in K\}
, and the
volume radius
is

{\rm vrad}(K)=\left(\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K)}{\mathrm{vol}_{n}(B_{2}^{n})}\right)^{1/n}.

The
polar body

K^{\circ}
of a convex body
K
with
0\in{\rm int}(K)
is given by

K^{\circ}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\langle x,y\rangle\leqslant 1\text{ for all }y\in K\}.

An absolutely continuous Borel probability measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is called
log-concave
if its density
f_{\mu}
is of the form

f_{\mu}=e^{-\varphi}
with
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
convex.
The uniform probability measure on any convex body is log-concave.

The barycenter of
\mu
is

{\rm bar}(\mu):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}xf_{\mu}(x)\,dx,

and its
isotropic constant
is the affine-invariant quantity

(2.1)

L_{\mu}:=\left(\frac{\|f_{\mu}\|_{\infty}}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f_{\mu}(x)\,dx}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}\det({\rm Cov}(\mu))^{\frac{1}{2n}},

where

{\rm Cov}(\mu):=\int x\otimes x\,d\mu(x)-\left(\int x\,d\mu(x)\right)\otimes\left(\int x\,d\mu(x)\right)

is the covariance matrix of
\mu
.
A log-concave probability measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is called
isotropic
if
{\rm bar}(\mu)=0
and
{\rm Cov}(\mu)=I_{n}
.
A convex body
K
of volume
1
is isotropic if and only if the log-concave probability measure with density
L_{K}^{n}\mathds{1}_{K/L_{K}}
is isotropic.

K. Ball
[
3
]
showed that, in every dimension
n
,

\sup_{\mu}L_{\mu}\leqslant C\sup_{K}L_{K},

where the suprema are taken over all log-concave probability measures
\mu
and all convex bodies
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, respectively.
Around 1985–86 (published in 1990), Bourgain
[
9
]
obtained the bound
L_{n}\leqslant cn^{1/4}\ln n
, later improved by Klartag
[
19
]
to
L_{n}\leqslant cn^{1/4}
.
These estimates remained the best known until 2020.
In a breakthrough, Chen
[
12
]
proved that for every
\varepsilon>0
, one has
L_{n}\leqslant n^{\varepsilon}
for all sufficiently large
n
.
This development initiated a series of works culminating in the final affirmative solution of Bourgain’s problem
by Klartag and Lehec
[
21
]
, following an important contribution by Guan
[
18
]
.
Shortly thereafter, Bizeul
[
5
]
provided another proof of the conjecture.

The study of
E(K)
in the non-symmetric case began with the work of Banaszczyk, Litvak, Pajor, and Szarek
[
4
]
, who showed that if
K
is a convex body in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
in John’s position (i.e., its maximal-volume inscribed ellipsoid is the Euclidean unit ball), then

M^{\ast}(K)\leqslant c\sqrt{n}\sqrt{\ln n}.

Since
K\supseteq B_{2}^{n}
in John’s position, we also have the trivial bound
M(K)\leqslant M(B_{2}^{n})=1
, hence
E(K)\leqslant c\sqrt{n}\sqrt{\ln n}
.
Rudelson
[
39
]
improved this to

E(K)\leqslant cn^{1/3}(\ln n)^{b}

for some absolute constant
b>0
.
This remained the best known bound until the recent work of Bizeul and Klartag.

After earlier estimates of order
n^{3/4}L_{K}
for
M^{\ast}(K)
in the isotropic position (see
[
11
, Chapter 9]
),
E. Milman
[
25
]
proved that if
K
is a symmetric isotropic convex body in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then

M^{\ast}(K)\leqslant c_{1}\sqrt{n}(\ln n)^{2}L_{K},

and the same bound extends to non-symmetric isotropic convex bodies.
For the dual problem, estimating
M(K)
in isotropic position, the first nontrivial results appeared in
[
17
]
.
The best known bound in the symmetric case, due to Giannopoulos and E. Milman
[
16
]
, was

M(K)\leqslant\frac{C(n\ln n)^{1/3}}{\sqrt{n}},

while in the non-symmetric case, Vritsiou
[
43
]
obtained the estimate

M(K)\leqslant\frac{cn^{5/11}(\ln n)^{5/22}}{\sqrt{n}}.

For background on isotropic convex bodies and log-concave measures, we refer to
[
11
]
;
for general information on the local theory of normed spaces, see
[
1
,
2
,
34
]
.

3
Geometric inequalities

In this section, we review several classical geometric inequalities that will be used in the sequel, beginning with the Blaschke–Santaló inequality.

Let
K
be a convex body in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The function
\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K)\,\mathrm{vol}_{n}((K-z)^{\circ})
,
defined on
\operatorname{int}(K)
, is strictly convex and attains a unique minimum at the
Santaló point

s(K)
of
K
. The Blaschke–Santaló inequality asserts that

\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K)\,\mathrm{vol}_{n}((K-s(K))^{\circ})\leqslant\omega_{n}^{2}.

Moreover, if
\operatorname{bar}(K)=0
, then
s(K^{\circ})=0
. Since
(K^{\circ})^{\circ}=K
, we obtain

\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K)\,\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K^{\circ})=\mathrm{vol}_{n}((K^{\circ}-s(K^{\circ}))^{\circ})\,\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K^{\circ})\leqslant\omega_{n}^{2}.

Hence we have the following classical result.

Theorem 3.1

(Blaschke–Santaló)
.

Let
K
be a convex body in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that either
\operatorname{bar}(K)=0
or
s(K)=0
. Then,

\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K)\,\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K^{\circ})\leqslant\omega_{n}^{2}.

A refinement of this inequality was given by Meyer and Pajor
[
24
]
.
For
\lambda\in(0,1)
, a hyperplane

F=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\langle x,u_{F}\rangle=\alpha_{F}\},

where
u_{F}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
and
\alpha_{F}\in\mathbb{R}
, is said to be
\lambda
-separating
for
K
if

\mathrm{vol}_{n}(\{x\in K:\langle x,u_{F}\rangle\geqslant\alpha_{F}\})=\lambda\,\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K).

Note that a
\lambda
-separating hyperplane necessarily intersects the interior of
K
.

Theorem 3.2

(Meyer–Pajor)
.

Let
K
be a convex body in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
F
a
\lambda
-separating hyperplane for
K
, where
\lambda\in(0,1)
. Then, there exists
z\in\operatorname{int}(K)\cap F
such that

\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K)\,\mathrm{vol}_{n}((K-z)^{\circ})\leqslant\frac{\omega_{n}^{2}}{4\lambda(1-\lambda)}.

Moreover,
z
is the unique point in
\operatorname{int}(K)\cap F
such that

\operatorname{bar}((K-z)^{\circ})\in\{tu_{F}:t\in\mathbb{R}\}.

In the opposite direction, the Bourgain–Milman inequality
[
10
]
provides a universal lower bound.

Theorem 3.3

(Bourgain–V. Milman)
.

Let
K
be a convex body in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
0\in\operatorname{int}(K)
. Then,

\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K)\,\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K^{\circ})\geqslant\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K)\,\mathrm{vol}_{n}((K-s(K))^{\circ})\geqslant c^{n}\omega_{n}^{2},

where
c>0
is an absolute constant.

Classical results of Rogers–Shephard
[
35
]
and V. Milman–Pajor
[
29
]
compare the volume of a convex body with those of its inner and outer regularizations.

Theorem 3.4

(Rogers–Shephard / V. Milman–Pajor)
.

Let
K
be a convex body in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then,

2^{n}\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K)\leqslant\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K-K)\leqslant\binom{2n}{n}\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K)\leqslant 4^{n}\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K).

Moreover, if
0\in\operatorname{int}(K)
, then

\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K_{\mathrm{out}})=\mathrm{vol}_{n}(\operatorname{conv}(K,-K))\leqslant 2^{n}\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K),

and if
\operatorname{bar}(K)=0
, then

\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K_{\mathrm{in}})=\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K\cap(-K))\geqslant 2^{-n}\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K).

If
s(K)=0
, then
\operatorname{bar}(K^{\circ})=0
, and since
(K_{\mathrm{in}})^{\circ}=(K^{\circ})_{\mathrm{out}}
, the Bourgain–Milman inequality yields

2^{n}\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K_{\mathrm{in}})\,\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K^{\circ})\geqslant\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K_{\mathrm{in}})\,\mathrm{vol}_{n}((K^{\circ})_{\mathrm{out}})\geqslant c^{n}\omega_{n}^{2}\geqslant c^{n}\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K)\,\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K^{\circ}),

and hence

\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K_{\mathrm{in}})\geqslant(c/2)^{n}\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K),

an observation due to Rudelson
[
39
]
.

The following inequality estimates the product of the volumes of a projection of a convex body and the corresponding orthogonal section
(see
[
36
]
for the first and
[
41
]
for the second claim).

Theorem 3.5

(Rogers–Shephard / Spingarn)
.

Let
K
be a convex body in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
0\in\operatorname{int}(K)
. Then, for every
1\leqslant k\leqslant n-1
and any
H\in G_{n,k}
,

\mathrm{vol}_{k}(P_{H}(K))\,\mathrm{vol}_{n-k}(K\cap H^{\perp})\leqslant\binom{n}{k}\,\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K).

If
\operatorname{bar}(K)=0
, then also

\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K)\leqslant\mathrm{vol}_{k}(P_{H}(K))\,\mathrm{vol}_{n-k}(K\cap H^{\perp}).

Rudelson’s inequality
[
38
]
compares the volume of a central section of
K-K
with that of the maximal corresponding section of
K
.

Theorem 3.6

(Rudelson)
.

Let
K
be a convex body in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then, for every
1\leqslant k\leqslant n-1
and any
H\in G_{n,k}
,

\mathrm{vol}_{k}((K-K)\cap H)^{1/k}\leqslant c\min\left\{\sqrt{k},\frac{n}{k}\right\}\max_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathrm{vol}_{k}(K\cap(x+H))^{1/k}.

An inequality of Fradelizi
[
15
]
compares the maximal section of a convex body with the section passing through its barycenter.

Theorem 3.7

(Fradelizi)
.

Let
K
be a convex body in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then, for every
1\leqslant k\leqslant n-1
and any
H\in G_{n,k}
,

\max_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathrm{vol}_{k}(K\cap(x+H))^{1/k}\leqslant\frac{n+1}{k+1}\,\mathrm{vol}_{k}(K\cap(\operatorname{bar}(K)+H))^{1/k}.

Combining these two results yields

(3.1)

\mathrm{vol}_{k}((K-K)\cap H)^{1/k}\leqslant c\left(\frac{n}{k}\right)^{2}\mathrm{vol}_{k}(K\cap(\operatorname{bar}(K)+H))^{1/k}

for every
1\leqslant k\leqslant n-1
and
H\in G_{n,k}
.
A direct proof of (
3.1
) with improved dependence on
n/k
will be given in Theorem
1.2
(Section
5
).

Let
K
be a convex body in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
0\in\operatorname{int}(K)
.
Recall that for
1\leqslant k\leqslant n-1
and
H\in G_{n,k}
, the projection
P_{H}(K)
is the polar body of
K^{\circ}\cap H
in
H
.
If
K
is symmetric, then
P_{H}(K)
and
K^{\circ}\cap H
are polar symmetric convex bodies in
H
, so that

{\rm vrad}(P_{H}(K))\,{\rm vrad}(K^{\circ}\cap H)\leqslant 1

by the Blaschke–Santaló inequality.
Vritsiou
[
43
]
extended this to non-symmetric convex bodies.

Theorem 3.8

(Vritsiou)
.

Let
K
be a convex body in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that either
\operatorname{bar}(K)=0
or
s(K)=0
. Then, for every
1\leqslant k\leqslant n-1
and any
H\in G_{n,k}
,

{\rm vrad}(P_{H}(K))\,{\rm vrad}(K^{\circ}\cap H)\leqslant c\,\frac{n}{k},

where
c>0
is an absolute constant.

The proof of Theorem
3.8
combines the Meyer–Pajor refinement of the Blaschke–Santaló inequality (Theorem
3.2
) with Grünbaum-type inequalities due to Stephen, Zhang, and Myroshnychenko
[
42
,
30
]
.

4
Covering numbers of isotropic convex bodies

Recall that if
A
and
B
are two convex bodies in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the
covering number

N(A,B)
of
A
by
B
is the least
integer
N
for which there exist
N
translates of
B
whose union covers
A
:

N(A,B)=\min\Big\{N\in\mathbb{N}:\exists\,x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\ \text{such that}\ A\subseteq\bigcup_{j=1}^{N}(x_{j}+B)\Big\}.

The Sudakov and dual Sudakov inequalities (see
[
1
, Chapter 4]
) state that if
K
is a symmetric convex body in

\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then for every
t>0
,

N(K,tB_{2}^{n})\leqslant\exp\!\big(cnM^{\ast}(K)^{2}/t^{2}\big)\hskip 9.24994pt\text{and}\hskip 9.24994ptN(B_{2}^{n},tK)\leqslant\exp\!\big(cnM(K)^{2}/t^{2}\big),

where
c>0
is an absolute constant.

V. Milman proved in
[
27
]
that there exists an absolute constant
\beta>0
such that every centered convex body
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
admits a linear image
\tilde{A}
satisfying
\mathrm{vol}_{n}(\tilde{A})=\mathrm{vol}_{n}(B_{2}^{n})
and

(4.1)

\max\Bigl\{N(\tilde{A},B_{2}^{n}),N(B_{2}^{n},\tilde{A}),N(\tilde{A}^{\circ},B_{2}^{n}),N(B_{2}^{n},\tilde{A}^{\circ})\Bigr\}\leqslant\exp(\beta n).

A convex body
A
satisfying this estimate is said to be in
M
-position
with constant
\beta
.

Pisier
[
33
]
proposed a different approach, allowing one to construct an entire family of
M
-positions and to obtain
more precise quantitative information on the corresponding covering numbers.

Theorem 4.1

(Pisier)
.

For every
0<\alpha<2
and every symmetric convex body
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, there exists
a linear image
\tilde{A}
of
A
such that

\max\Bigl\{N(\tilde{A},tB_{2}^{n}),N(B_{2}^{n},t\tilde{A}),N(\tilde{A}^{\circ},tB_{2}^{n}),N(B_{2}^{n},t\tilde{A}^{\circ})\Bigr\}\leqslant\exp\!\big(c(\alpha)n/t^{\alpha}\big)

for every
t\geqslant c(\alpha)^{1/\alpha}
, where
c(\alpha)
depends only on
\alpha
and satisfies

c(\alpha)=O\!\big((2-\alpha)^{-\alpha/2}\big)
as
\alpha\to 2^{-}
.

A convex body
A
satisfying the estimate in Theorem
4.1
is said to be in
\alpha
-regular
M
-position
with constant
c(\alpha)
.

The following proposition, a simple consequence of the
M^{\ast}
estimate (
1.9
) and the
M
estimate (
1.10
),
shows that every isotropic convex body
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is (almost) in
2
-regular
M
-position.
Below, we set
r_{n}=\omega_{n}^{-1/n}
; note that
r_{n}\approx\sqrt{n}
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
K
be an isotropic convex body in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then, for every
t>0
,

(4.2)

\displaystyle\max\Bigl\{N(K,tr_{n}B_{2}^{n}),\;N(B_{2}^{n},tr_{n}K^{\circ})\Bigr\}

\displaystyle\leqslant\exp\!\Big(\frac{\gamma_{n}^{2}n}{t^{2}}\Big),

(4.3)

\displaystyle\max\Bigl\{N(r_{n}B_{2}^{n},tK),\;N(r_{n}K^{\circ},tB_{2}^{n})\Bigr\}

\displaystyle\leqslant\exp\!\Big(\frac{\delta_{n}^{2}n}{t^{2}}\Big),

where
\gamma_{n}\leqslant c_{1}(\ln n)^{2}
and
\delta_{n}\leqslant c_{2}\ln n
.

Proof.

Let
K_{\rm in}
and
K_{\rm out}
denote the inner and outer regularizations of
K
. Observe that

(4.4)

\displaystyle M(K_{\rm in})

\displaystyle=M^{\ast}\big((K^{\circ})_{\rm out}\big)\leqslant M^{\ast}(K^{\circ}-K^{\circ})=2M^{\ast}(K^{\circ})=2M(K),

(4.5)

\displaystyle M^{\ast}(K_{\rm out})

\displaystyle\leqslant M^{\ast}(K-K)=2M^{\ast}(K).

Since
K_{\rm in}\subseteq K\subseteq K_{\rm out}
, it is clear that

M(K_{\rm out})\leqslant M(K)
and
M^{\ast}(K_{\rm in})\leqslant M^{\ast}(K)
.

The Sudakov inequality, combined with (
4.5
), gives

(4.6)

\displaystyle N(K,tr_{n}B_{2}^{n})

\displaystyle\leqslant N(K_{\rm out},tr_{n}B_{2}^{n})\leqslant\exp\!\big(cnM^{\ast}(K_{\rm out})^{2}/(r_{n}t)^{2}\big)

\displaystyle\leqslant\exp\!\big(4cnM^{\ast}(K)^{2}/(r_{n}t)^{2}\big)\leqslant\exp\!\big(\gamma_{n}^{2}n/t^{2}\big).

Similarly, using (
4.4
) we obtain

(4.7)

\displaystyle N(r_{n}K^{\circ},tB_{2}^{n})

\displaystyle\leqslant N(r_{n}(K_{\rm in})^{\circ},tB_{2}^{n})\leqslant\exp\!\big(cnr_{n}^{2}M^{\ast}((K_{\rm in})^{\circ})^{2}/t^{2}\big)

\displaystyle=\exp\!\big(cnr_{n}^{2}M(K_{\rm in})^{2}/t^{2}\big)\leqslant\exp\!\big(4cnr_{n}^{2}M(K)^{2}/t^{2}\big)\leqslant\exp\!\big(\delta_{n}^{2}n/t^{2}\big).

Applying the dual Sudakov inequality and (
4.4
) yields

(4.8)

\displaystyle N(r_{n}B_{2}^{n},tK)

\displaystyle\leqslant N(r_{n}B_{2}^{n},tK_{\rm in})\leqslant\exp\!\big(cnr_{n}^{2}M(K_{\rm in})^{2}/t^{2}\big)

\displaystyle\leqslant\exp\!\big(4cnr_{n}^{2}M(K)^{2}/t^{2}\big)\leqslant\exp\!\big(\delta_{n}^{2}n/t^{2}\big),

and, using (
4.5
), we similarly have

(4.9)

\displaystyle N(B_{2}^{n},tr_{n}K^{\circ})

\displaystyle\leqslant N(B_{2}^{n},tr_{n}(K_{\rm out})^{\circ})\leqslant\exp\!\big(cnM((K_{\rm out})^{\circ})^{2}/(r_{n}t)^{2}\big)

\displaystyle\leqslant\exp\!\big(cnM^{\ast}(K_{\rm out})^{2}/(r_{n}t)^{2}\big)=\exp\!\big(4cnM^{\ast}(K)^{2}/(r_{n}t)^{2}\big)\leqslant\exp\!\big(\gamma_{n}^{2}n/t^{2}\big).

Combining (
4.6
)–(
4.9
) completes the proof.
∎

5
Projections and sections of the outer and inner regularizations

In this section we compare the volumes of sections and projections of a convex body with those of its inner and outer
regularizations. We begin with the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Assume first that
{\rm bar}(K)=0
. Since
T(K_{\rm in})=(TK)_{\rm in}
and
T(K_{\rm out})=(TK)_{\rm out}
for every
T\in GL_{n}
, we may assume that
K
is isotropic.
From (
4.6
) we know that
N_{t}=N(K_{\rm out},tr_{n}B_{2}^{n})\leqslant\exp(\gamma_{n}^{2}n/t^{2})
for every
t>0
. Thus, there exist

x_{1},\ldots,x_{N_{t}}
such that

K_{\rm out}\subseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^{N_{t}}(x_{i}+tr_{n}B_{2}^{n}).

Projecting onto a
k
-dimensional subspace
H
, we obtain

P_{H}(K_{\rm out})\subseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^{N_{t}}(P_{H}(x_{i})+tr_{n}B_{H}),

where
B_{H}
denotes the Euclidean unit ball in
H
. Hence,

\mathrm{vol}_{k}(P_{H}(K_{\rm out}))\leqslant N_{t}\,\mathrm{vol}_{k}(tr_{n}B_{H})\leqslant\exp(\gamma_{n}^{2}n/t^{2})(tr_{n})^{k}\omega_{k}.

Choosing
t=\gamma_{n}\sqrt{n/k}
minimizes the right-hand side and gives

\mathrm{vol}_{k}(P_{H}(K_{\rm out}))\leqslant e^{k}r_{n}^{k}\gamma_{n}^{k}(n/k)^{k/2}\omega_{k},

and therefore,

(5.1)

{\rm vrad}(P_{H}(K_{\rm out}))\leqslant er_{n}\gamma_{n}\sqrt{n/k}.

On the other hand, (
4.8
) shows that

N^{\prime}_{t}=N(r_{n}B_{2}^{n},tK_{\rm in})\leqslant\exp(\delta_{n}^{2}n/t^{2})
for every
t>0
.
Proceeding in the same way, we obtain

\mathrm{vol}_{k}(r_{n}B_{H})\leqslant N^{\prime}_{t}\,\mathrm{vol}_{k}(tP_{H}(K_{\rm in}))\leqslant\exp(\delta_{n}^{2}n/t^{2})t^{k}\mathrm{vol}_{k}(P_{H}(K_{\rm in})).

Choosing
t=\delta_{n}\sqrt{n/k}
, we get

r_{n}^{k}\mathrm{vol}_{k}(B_{H})\leqslant e^{k}\delta_{n}^{k}(n/k)^{k/2}\mathrm{vol}_{k}(P_{H}(K_{\rm in})),

and hence,

(5.2)

r_{n}\leqslant e\delta_{n}\sqrt{n/k}\,{\rm vrad}(P_{H}(K_{\rm in})).

Combining (
5.1
) and (
5.2
), we find that

(5.3)

{\rm vrad}(P_{H}(K_{\rm out}))\leqslant e^{2}\delta_{n}\gamma_{n}(n/k){\rm vrad}(P_{H}(K_{\rm in})).

The first claim of the theorem follows with
g(n)=e^{2}\delta_{n}\gamma_{n}\leqslant c(\ln n)^{3}
.

Next, assume that
{\rm bar}(K^{\circ})=0
, in which case
s(K)=0
.
We may assume that
K^{\circ}
is isotropic.
Replacing
K
by
K^{\circ}
in (
4.7
) and using (
1.1
), we get

N(r_{n}K_{\rm out},tB_{2}^{n})\leqslant\exp(\delta_{n}^{2}n/t^{2}).

Applying the argument of the first part of the proof, we obtain

(5.4)

r_{n}{\rm vrad}(P_{H}(K_{\rm out}))\leqslant e\delta_{n}\sqrt{n/k}.

Replacing
K
by
K^{\circ}
in (
4.9
) and using again (
1.1
), we find that

N(B_{2}^{n},tr_{n}K_{\rm in})\leqslant\exp(\gamma_{n}^{2}n/t^{2})
, and by the same reasoning,

(5.5)

1\leqslant er_{n}\gamma_{n}\sqrt{n/k}\,{\rm vrad}(P_{H}(K_{\rm in})).

Combining (
5.4
) and (
5.5
), we obtain

(5.6)

{\rm vrad}(P_{H}(K_{\rm out}))\leqslant e^{2}\delta_{n}\gamma_{n}(n/k){\rm vrad}(P_{H}(K_{\rm in})),

and the second claim of the theorem follows with
g(n)=e^{2}\delta_{n}\gamma_{n}\leqslant c(\ln n)^{3}
.
∎

Theorem
1.2
follows easily from Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Since
P_{H}((K^{\circ})_{\rm in})
is symmetric and its polar body in
H
is
K_{\rm out}\cap H
,
the Blaschke–Santaló inequality implies

(5.7)

{\rm vrad}(K_{\rm out}\cap H)\,{\rm vrad}(P_{H}((K^{\circ})_{\rm in}))\leqslant 1.

Similarly, since
P_{H}((K^{\circ})_{\rm out})
is symmetric and its polar body in
H
is
K_{\rm in}\cap H
,
the Bourgain–Milman inequality gives

(5.8)

c_{1}\leqslant{\rm vrad}(K_{\rm in}\cap H)\,{\rm vrad}(P_{H}((K^{\circ})_{\rm out})).

Assuming that either
{\rm bar}(K)=0
or
{\rm bar}(K^{\circ})=0
, we may apply Theorem
1.1
to
K^{\circ}
and obtain

{\rm vrad}(P_{H}((K^{\circ})_{\rm out}))\leqslant c_{2}\frac{ng(n)}{k}\,{\rm vrad}(P_{H}((K^{\circ})_{\rm in})).

Combining this estimate with (
5.7
) and (
5.8
), we get

\displaystyle c_{1}

\displaystyle\leqslant{\rm vrad}(K_{\rm in}\cap H)\,{\rm vrad}(P_{H}((K^{\circ})_{\rm out}))

\displaystyle\leqslant c_{2}\frac{ng(n)}{k}\,{\rm vrad}(K_{\rm in}\cap H)\,{\rm vrad}(P_{H}((K^{\circ})_{\rm in}))

\displaystyle\leqslant c_{2}\frac{ng(n)}{k}\,{\rm vrad}(K_{\rm in}\cap H)\,{\rm vrad}(K_{\rm out}\cap H)^{-1}.

Hence,

{\rm vrad}(K_{\rm out}\cap H)\leqslant c_{3}\frac{ng(n)}{k}\,{\rm vrad}(K_{\rm in}\cap H),

where
c_{3}=c_{2}/c_{1}
.
∎

We now turn to the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

We have
{\rm vrad}(P_{H}(K))\leqslant{\rm vrad}(P_{H}(K_{\rm out}))
, and by the Blaschke–Santaló inequality applied to
P_{H}(K_{\rm in})
in
H
,

{\rm vrad}(K^{\circ}\cap H)\leqslant{\rm vrad}((K_{\rm in})^{\circ}\cap H)\leqslant{\rm vrad}(P_{H}(K_{\rm in}))^{-1}.

Therefore,

{\rm vrad}(P_{H}(K))\,{\rm vrad}(K^{\circ}\cap H)\leqslant\frac{{\rm vrad}(P_{H}(K_{\rm out}))}{{\rm vrad}(P_{H}(K_{\rm in}))}\leqslant\frac{ng(n)}{k},

in view of Theorem
1.1
.
∎

In the case where
K
is isotropic, we can show that for a random
k
-dimensional subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
the volume radii of the corresponding sections or projections of
K_{\rm out}
and
K_{\rm in}
are of the same order,
up to a logarithmic factor in the dimension.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Let
K
be an isotropic convex body in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Paouris and Pivovarov
[
31
]
proved that for every symmetric convex body
C\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

and every
1\leqslant k\leqslant n-1
,

(5.9)

\Phi_{k}(C):=\mathrm{vol}_{n}(C)^{-1/n}\left(\int_{G_{n,k}}\mathrm{vol}_{k}(P_{H}(C))^{-n}\,d\nu_{n,k}(H)\right)^{-\frac{1}{kn}}\geqslant c_{1}\sqrt{\frac{n}{k}},

where
c_{1}>0
is an absolute constant.
Moreover, E. Milman and Yehudayoff
[
26
]
established the sharp lower bound

\Phi_{k}(B_{2}^{n})\leqslant\Phi_{k}(C)
, together with a characterization of equality:
ellipsoids are the only local minimizers with respect to the Hausdorff metric, for all
1\leqslant k\leqslant n-1
.

From (
5.9
) and Markov’s inequality, it follows that

{\rm vrad}(P_{H}(C))\geqslant c_{1}t^{-1}\sqrt{n}\,\mathrm{vol}_{n}(C)^{1/n}

with probability greater than
1-t^{-kn}
. Applying this result to
K_{\rm in}
and taking into account the inequality of
V. Milman and Pajor, which ensures that
\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K_{\rm in})^{1/n}\geqslant\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K)^{1/n}=\frac{1}{2}
, we obtain

(5.10)

{\rm vrad}(P_{H}(K_{\rm in}))\geqslant c_{2}\sqrt{n}

with probability greater than
1-e^{-kn}
, where
c_{2}>0
is an absolute constant.

Next, we use a well-known consequence of the Aleksandrov inequalities
(see
[
40
, Section 6.4]
).
For every convex body
C\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the sequence

Q_{k}(C)=\left(\frac{1}{\omega_{k}}\int_{G_{n,k}}\mathrm{vol}_{k}(P_{H}(C))\,d\nu_{n,k}(H)\right)^{1/k}

is decreasing in
k
. In particular, for any
1\leqslant k\leqslant n-1
,

Q_{k}(C)\leqslant Q_{1}(C),

which can be written equivalently as

\left(\frac{1}{\omega_{k}}\int_{G_{n,k}}\mathrm{vol}_{k}(P_{H}(C))\,d\nu_{n,k}(H)\right)^{1/k}\leqslant M^{\ast}(C).

Applying this to
K_{\rm out}
and using that

M^{\ast}(K_{\rm out})\leqslant 2M^{\ast}(K)\leqslant c\sqrt{n}(\ln n)^{2}

by E. Milman’s inequality, we obtain

(5.11)

{\rm vrad}(P_{H}(K_{\rm out}))\leqslant\gamma_{n}\sqrt{n}

with probability greater than
1-e^{-2k}
, where
\gamma_{n}\leqslant c_{3}(\ln n)^{2}
.
Combining (
5.10
) and (
5.11
), we conclude that

{\rm vrad}(P_{H}(K_{\rm out}))\leqslant\gamma_{n}\,{\rm vrad}(P_{H}(K_{\rm in}))

with probability greater than
1-e^{-k}
.

We now turn to the proof of (
1.5
).
Repeating the above reasoning for the polar body
K^{\circ}
, we obtain

(5.12)

{\rm vrad}(P_{H}((K^{\circ})_{\rm in}))\geqslant c_{2}\sqrt{n}\,\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K^{\circ})^{1/n}

and

(5.13)

{\rm vrad}(P_{H}((K^{\circ})_{\rm out}))\leqslant 2M^{\ast}(K^{\circ})=2M(K)\leqslant\frac{\delta_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}

for a random
H\in G_{n,k}
, where
\delta_{n}\leqslant c\,\ln n
.
It follows that

{\rm vrad}(P_{H}((K^{\circ})_{\rm out}))\leqslant\delta_{n}\,{\rm vrad}(P_{H}((K^{\circ})_{\rm in}))

with probability greater than
1-e^{-k}
.

Finally, repeating the proof of Theorem
1.2
,
and using the Blaschke–Santaló inequality for the symmetric convex bodies

K_{\rm out}\cap H
and
K_{\rm in}\cap H

(whose polars are
P_{H}((K^{\circ})_{\rm in})
and
P_{H}((K^{\circ})_{\rm out})
, respectively),
we obtain

{\rm vrad}(K_{\rm out}\cap H)\leqslant\delta_{n}\,{\rm vrad}(K_{\rm in}\cap H)

for every
H\in G_{n,k}
satisfying (
5.12
) and (
5.13
).
∎

6
Functional inequalities

We consider the class
\mathcal{L}^{n}
of
geometric log-concave integrable functions
:
these are the centered log-concave functions
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
that satisfy

f(0)=\|f\|_{\infty}
and
0<\int f<\infty
. We say that
f
is centered if
\int xf(x)\,dx=0
.
For any
f,g\in\mathcal{L}^{n}
we define

(f\star g)(x)=\sup\{f(x_{1})g(x_{2}):x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\ x=x_{1}+x_{2}\}.

Roysdon
[
37
]
obtained a functional version of Rudelson’s theorem on the sections of the difference body.
He considered
\overline{f}(x)=f(-x)
and defined
\Delta_{0}f=f\star\overline{f}
, i.e.

(\Delta_{0}f)(x)=\sup\{f(x_{1})f(x_{2}):x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\ x=x_{1}-x_{2}\}.

Note that if
f=\mathds{1}_{K}
for some convex body
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then

\Delta_{0}f=\mathds{1}_{K-K}
.
Thus,
\Delta_{0}f
may be viewed as a functional analogue of the difference body.
With this definition, Roysdon’s inequality reads as follows.

Theorem 6.1

(Roysdon)
.

For any
f\in\mathcal{L}^{n}
, any
1\leqslant k\leqslant n-1
, and any
H\in G_{n,k}
we have

(6.1)

\left(\int_{H}\Delta_{0}f(x)\,dx\right)^{1/k}\leqslant C\max\!\left\{\sqrt{k},\frac{n}{k}\right\}\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\frac{1}{\|f|_{y+H}\|_{\infty}}\int_{y+H}f(x)\,dx\right)^{1/k},

where
f|_{y+H}
denotes the restriction of
f
to
y+H
. Moreover,

c\left(\int_{H}f(x)\,dx\right)^{1/k}\leqslant\left(\int_{H}\Delta_{0}f(x)\,dx\right)^{1/k},

where
c>0
and
C>1
are absolute constants.

For any
f\in\mathcal{L}^{n}
we define the functions
\Delta_{\rm out}f
and
\Delta_{\rm in}f
by

\Delta_{\rm out}f(x)=\sup\!\left\{\sqrt{f(x_{1})f(-x_{2})}:x=\tfrac{1}{2}(x_{1}+x_{2})\right\},\hskip 18.49988pt\Delta_{\rm in}f(x)=\min\{f(x),f(-x)\}.

This definition is consistent with the notion of the
\alpha
-difference function

\Delta_{\alpha}f
of a function
f
, introduced by Colesanti in
[
13
]

for any
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,\infty]
and any
\alpha\in[-\infty,0]
.
It is shown in
[
13
]
that if
f
is
\alpha
-concave, then
\Delta_{\alpha}f
is also
\alpha
-concave.
Difference functions have been studied as functional analogues of the difference body in a number of works;
see, e.g.,
[
13
,
20
,
7
]
.
One can easily check that the functions
\Delta_{\mathrm{out}}f
and
\Delta_{\mathrm{in}}f

considered here coincide with
\Delta_{0}f
and
\Delta_{-\infty}f
, respectively.
In particular, if
f\in\mathcal{L}^{n}
, then
\Delta_{\mathrm{out}}f
is log-concave and

\Delta_{\mathrm{in}}f
is quasi-concave.

Note that if
f=\mathds{1}_{K}
for some convex body
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then

\Delta_{\rm out}f=\mathds{1}_{\frac{1}{2}(K-K)}
and

\Delta_{\rm in}f=\mathds{1}_{K\cap(-K)}
.
Moreover, it is clear that
\Delta_{\rm in}f(x)\leqslant f(x)
and

\Delta_{\rm in}f(x)\leqslant\Delta_{\rm out}f(x)
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
;
for the latter inequality we choose
x_{1}=x_{2}=x
and note that

\Delta_{\rm out}f(x)\geqslant\sqrt{f(x)f(-x)}\geqslant\min\{f(x),f(-x)\}
.

We now establish the following functional version of Theorem
1.2
.

Theorem 6.2
.

For any
f\in\mathcal{L}^{n}
, any
1\leqslant k\leqslant n-1
, and any
H\in G_{n,k}
we have

(6.2)

\left(\int_{H}\Delta_{\rm out}f(x)\,dx\right)^{1/k}\leqslant C(n/k)^{2}g(n)\left(\int_{H}\Delta_{\rm in}f(x)\,dx\right)^{1/k}

and

(6.3)

c\left(\int_{H}f(x)\,dx\right)^{1/k}\leqslant\left(\int_{H}\Delta_{\rm out}f(x)\,dx\right)^{1/k},

where
c>0
and
C>1
are absolute constants.

For the proof we employ two families of convex bodies associated with each
f\in\mathcal{L}^{n}
.
The first, introduced by K. Ball
[
3
]
, is given for every
p>0
by

K_{p}(f)=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{p-1}f(rx)\,dr\geqslant\frac{f(0)}{p}\right\}.

From the definition it follows that the radial function of
K_{p}(f)
satisfies

(6.4)

\varrho_{K_{p}(f)}(x)=\left(\frac{1}{f(0)}\int_{0}^{\infty}pr^{p-1}f(rx)\,dr\right)^{1/p}\hskip 9.24994pt\text{for }x\neq 0.

Moreover, for every
0<p<q
one has

(6.5)

\frac{\Gamma(p+1)^{1/p}}{\Gamma(q+1)^{1/q}}K_{q}(f)\subseteq K_{p}(f)\subseteq K_{q}(f).

A proof of these inclusions is given in
[
11
, Proposition 2.5.7]
.
We also consider the family
\{R_{p}(f)\}_{p>1}
defined by

R_{p}(f)=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:f(x)\geqslant e^{-(p-1)}f(0)\}.

We will use several relations between these two families; their proofs are provided in
Subsection
6.1
.
Lemma
6.5
shows that

(6.6)

c_{2}K_{p}(f)\subseteq R_{p}(f)\subseteq c_{1}K_{p}(f)

for all
p\geqslant 2
, where
c_{1},c_{2}>0
are absolute constants.
Furthermore, Lemma
6.6
states that

(6.7)

K_{p}(\Delta_{\rm out}f)\approx K_{p}(f)_{{\rm out}}\hskip 9.24994pt\hbox{and}\hskip 9.24994ptK_{p}(\Delta_{\rm in}f)\approx K_{p}(f)_{{\rm in}}

for all
p\geqslant 2
.

Since (
6.2
) is homogeneous, we may assume that
f(0)=\|f\|_{\infty}=1
.
It is well known that for any
H\in G_{n,k}
,

\int_{H}f(x)\,dx=\mathrm{vol}_{k}(K_{k}(f)\cap H).

Indeed, integrating in polar coordinates gives

\displaystyle\mathrm{vol}_{k}(K_{k}(f)\cap H)

\displaystyle=\int_{H}\mathds{1}_{K_{k}(f)}(x)\,dx=\int_{S^{n-1}\cap H}\int_{0}^{\rho_{K_{k}(f)}(\xi)}r^{k-1}\,dr\,d\xi

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k}\int_{S^{n-1}\cap H}\rho_{K_{k}(f)}(\xi)^{k}\,d\xi=\frac{1}{k}\int_{S^{n-1}\cap H}k\int_{0}^{\infty}f(r\xi)r^{k-1}\,dr\,d\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{H}f(z)\,dz.

Proof of Theorem
6.2
.

We begin with the convex body
K_{k}(\Delta_{\rm out}f)
.
Note that
\Delta_{\rm out}f(0)=\|\Delta_{\rm out}f\|_{\infty}=\|f\|_{\infty}=1
.
We have

\int_{H}\Delta_{\rm out}f(x)\,dx=\mathrm{vol}_{k}(K_{k}(\Delta_{\rm out}f)\cap H)\leqslant\mathrm{vol}_{k}(K_{n+1}(\Delta_{\rm out}f)\cap H),

since
K_{k}(\Delta_{\rm out}f)\subseteq K_{n+1}(\Delta_{\rm out}f)
.
By Lemma
6.6
,

K_{n+1}(\Delta_{\rm out}f)\subseteq c\,K_{n+1}(f)_{{\rm out}}

for some absolute constant
c>0
.
As
K_{n+1}(f)
is centered, we deduce that

(6.8)

\mathrm{vol}_{k}\big(K_{n+1}(f)_{{\rm out}}\cap H\big)\leqslant\left(\frac{ng(n)}{k}\right)^{k}\mathrm{vol}_{k}\big(K_{n+1}(f)_{{\rm in}}\cap H\big).

Moreover,

K_{n+1}(f)_{{\rm in}}\subseteq\frac{cn}{k}\,K_{k}(f)_{{\rm in}},

and hence

\mathrm{vol}_{k}(K_{n+1}(f)_{{\rm in}}\cap H)\leqslant\left(\frac{cn}{k}\right)^{k}\mathrm{vol}_{k}(K_{k}(f)_{{\rm in}}\cap H).

Lemma
6.6
implies that

K_{k}(\Delta_{\rm in}f)\approx K_{k}(f)_{{\rm in}}
, therefore

\big(\mathrm{vol}_{k}(K_{k}(f)_{{\rm in}}\cap H)\big)^{1/k}\approx\big(\mathrm{vol}_{k}(K_{k}(\Delta_{\rm in}f)\cap H)\big)^{1/k}=\left(\int_{H}\Delta_{\rm in}f(x)\,dx\right)^{1/k}.

Combining the above estimates gives

\left(\int_{H}\Delta_{\rm out}f(x)\,dx\right)^{1/k}\leqslant(n/k)^{2}g(n)\left(\int_{H}\Delta_{\rm in}f(x)\,dx\right)^{1/k},

which completes the proof of (
6.2
).

For (
6.3
), observe first that

K_{k}(f|_{H})\subseteq c_{1}(K_{k}(f)\cap H)

for some absolute constant
c_{1}>0
.
Indeed,

\displaystyle K_{k}(f|_{H})

\displaystyle\subseteq c_{2}R_{k}(f|_{H})=c_{2}\{x\in H:f(x)\geqslant(f|_{H})(0)e^{-(k-1)}\}

\displaystyle=c_{2}\{x\in H:f(x)\geqslant e^{-(k-1)}\}=c_{2}\big(\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:f(x)\geqslant e^{-(k-1)}\}\cap H\big)

\displaystyle=c_{2}(R_{k}(f)\cap H)\subseteq c_{1}(K_{k}(f)\cap H),

by (
6.6
).
Then, combining Lemma
6.6
with the Brunn–Minkowski inequality, we obtain

(6.9)

\displaystyle\mathrm{vol}_{k}(K_{k}(f|_{H}))

\displaystyle\leqslant c_{1}^{k}\mathrm{vol}_{k}(K_{k}(f)\cap H)\leqslant c_{1}^{k}2^{-k}\mathrm{vol}_{k}(K_{k}(f)_{{\rm out}}\cap H)

\displaystyle\leqslant c_{3}^{k}\mathrm{vol}_{k}(K_{k}(\Delta_{\rm out}f)\cap H)=c_{3}^{k}\int_{H}\Delta_{\rm out}f(x)\,dx.

Thus,

\int_{H}f(x)\,dx\leqslant c_{3}^{k}\int_{H}\Delta_{\rm out}f(x)\,dx,

and taking
k
th roots completes the proof of (
6.3
).
∎

Next, we establish a functional analogue of Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 6.3
.

For any
f\in\mathcal{L}^{n}
, any
1\leqslant k\leqslant n-1
, and any
H\in G_{n,k}
we have

(6.10)

\left(\int_{H}P_{H}(\Delta_{\rm out}f)(x)\,dx\right)^{1/k}\leqslant C(n/k)^{2}g(n)\left(\int_{H}P_{H}(\Delta_{\rm in}f)(x)\,dx\right)^{1/k},

where
C>0
is an absolute constant.

Proof.

Recall that if
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,\infty)
is a nonnegative measurable function and
H\in G_{n,k}
,
the orthogonal projection of
g
onto
H
is the function
P_{H}g:H\to[0,\infty)
defined by

(P_{H}g)(z)=\sup\{g(y+z):y\in H^{\perp}\}.

It is straightforward to verify that

R_{p}(P_{H}g)=P_{H}(R_{p}(g))\hskip 9.24994pt\text{for every }p>1.

Moreover, if
g=\mathds{1}_{K}
for some compact set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then
P_{H}g=\mathds{1}_{P_{H}(K)}
.

We shall also use the fact that if
g(0)=\|g\|_{\infty}=1
, then

\displaystyle\|P_{H}g\|_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{H}P_{H}g(x)\,dx=\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{vol}_{k}(\{x\in H:P_{H}g(x)\geqslant t\})\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{1}^{\infty}e^{-(p-1)}\mathrm{vol}_{k}(\{x\in H:P_{H}g(x)\geqslant e^{-(p-1)}\})\,dp

\displaystyle=\int_{1}^{\infty}e^{-(p-1)}\mathrm{vol}_{k}(R_{p}(P_{H}g))\,dp=\int_{1}^{\infty}e^{-(p-1)}\mathrm{vol}_{k}(P_{H}(R_{p}(g)))\,dp.

Let
f\in\mathcal{L}^{n}
with
f(0)=\|f\|_{\infty}=1
.
By the right-hand side inclusion of Lemma
6.5
we have

(6.11)

\displaystyle\int_{H}P_{H}(\Delta_{\rm out}f)(x)\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{1}^{\infty}e^{-(p-1)}\mathrm{vol}_{k}(P_{H}(R_{p}(\Delta_{\rm out}f)))\,dp

\displaystyle\leqslant c_{1}^{k}\int_{1}^{\infty}e^{-(p-1)}\mathrm{vol}_{k}(P_{H}(K_{p}(\Delta_{\rm out}f)))\,dp.

From (
6.5
) we know that

K_{p}(\Delta_{\rm out}f)\subseteq K_{n+1}(\Delta_{\rm out}f)
for all
1\leqslant p\leqslant n+1
,
and we also know that
K_{p}(\Delta_{\rm out}f)\subseteq\frac{cp}{n+1}K_{n+1}(\Delta_{\rm out}f)
for all
p>n+1
.
Therefore,

(6.12)

\displaystyle\int_{H}P_{H}(\Delta_{\rm out}f)(x)\,dx

\displaystyle\leqslant c_{1}^{k}\Bigg(\int_{1}^{n+1}e^{-(p-1)}\mathrm{vol}_{k}(P_{H}(K_{n+1}(\Delta_{\rm out}f)))\,dp

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\int_{n+1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{c_{2}p}{n+1}\right)^{k}e^{-(p-1)}\mathrm{vol}_{k}(P_{H}(K_{n+1}(\Delta_{\rm out}f)))\,dp\Bigg)

\displaystyle=c_{1}^{k}\,\mathrm{vol}_{k}(P_{H}(K_{n+1}(\Delta_{\rm out}f)))\left(\int_{1}^{n+1}e^{-(p-1)}\,dp+\int_{n+1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{c_{2}p}{n+1}\right)^{k}e^{-(p-1)}\,dp\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant c_{1}^{k}\,\mathrm{vol}_{k}(P_{H}(K_{n+1}(\Delta_{\rm out}f)))\left(\int_{1}^{\infty}e^{-(p-1)}\,dp+\frac{e\,c_{2}^{k}}{(n+1)^{k}}\int_{0}^{\infty}p^{k}e^{-p}\,dp\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant c_{1}^{k}\,\mathrm{vol}_{k}(P_{H}(K_{n+1}(\Delta_{\rm out}f)))\left(1+\frac{e\,c_{2}^{k}k!}{(n+1)^{k}}\right)\leqslant c_{3}^{k}\,\mathrm{vol}_{k}(P_{H}(K_{n+1}(\Delta_{\rm out}f))).

On the other hand,

(6.13)

\displaystyle\int_{H}P_{H}(\Delta_{\rm in}f)(x)\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{1}^{\infty}e^{-(p-1)}\mathrm{vol}_{k}(P_{H}(R_{p}(\Delta_{\rm in}f)))\,dp

\displaystyle\geqslant c_{4}^{k}\int_{2}^{\infty}e^{-(p-1)}\mathrm{vol}_{k}(P_{H}(K_{p}(\Delta_{\rm in}f)))\,dp

\displaystyle\geqslant c_{4}^{k}\int_{\max\{k,2\}}^{n+1}e^{-(p-1)}\left(\frac{c_{5}k}{n+1}\right)^{k}\mathrm{vol}_{k}(P_{H}(K_{n+1}(\Delta_{\rm in}f)))\,dp

\displaystyle\geqslant\left(\frac{c_{6}k}{n+1}\right)^{k}\mathrm{vol}_{k}(P_{H}(K_{n+1}(\Delta_{\rm in}f))).

Since
K_{n+1}(f)
is centered, Theorem
1.1
gives

\mathrm{vol}_{k}(P_{H}(K_{n+1}(\Delta_{\rm out}f)))^{1/k}\leqslant c_{7}(n/k)g(n)\mathrm{vol}_{k}(P_{H}(K_{n+1}(\Delta_{\rm in}f)))^{1/k}.

Combining this estimate with (
6.12
) and (
6.13
) yields

\int_{H}P_{H}(\Delta_{\rm out}f)(x)\,dx\leqslant\left(\frac{c_{8}n\sqrt{g(n)}}{k}\right)^{2k}\int_{H}P_{H}(\Delta_{\rm in}f)(x)\,dx,

or equivalently,

\left(\int_{H}P_{H}(\Delta_{\rm out}f)(x)\,dx\right)^{1/k}\leqslant c_{8}(n/k)^{2}g(n)\left(\int_{H}P_{H}(\Delta_{\rm in}f)(x)\,dx\right)^{1/k}.

This completes the proof.
∎

6.1
Auxiliary lemmas

In this subsection we present variants of several technical lemmas from
[
22
]

(see also
[
37
]
).

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
g:[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
be an increasing convex function with
g(0)=0
.
For any
p>1
define

M_{p}=\sup\{e^{-g(t)}t^{p-1}:t>0\},

and let
t_{p}>0
denote the unique point satisfying
M_{p}=e^{-g(t_{p})}t_{p}^{p-1}
.
Then,

(6.14)

\frac{M_{p}t_{p}}{p}\leqslant\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{p-1}e^{-g(t)}\,dt\leqslant c\,\frac{M_{p}t_{p}}{\sqrt{p-1}},

where
c>0
is an absolute constant. Moreover,

(6.15)

g(2t_{p})\geqslant p-1\geqslant g(t_{p}).

Proof.

Since
g
is increasing, we have

\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{p-1}e^{-g(t)}\,dt\geqslant\int_{0}^{t_{p}}t^{p-1}e^{-g(t)}\,dt\geqslant e^{-g(t_{p})}\int_{0}^{t_{p}}t^{p-1}\,dt=\frac{e^{-g(t_{p})}t_{p}^{p}}{p}=\frac{M_{p}t_{p}}{p}.

For the upper bound, set
\varphi(t)=g(t)-(p-1)\ln t
.
Note that
\varphi
is convex and has a unique critical point at
t_{p}
. Since
\varphi^{\prime}_{-}(t_{p})\leqslant 0\leqslant\varphi^{\prime}_{+}(t_{p})
, it follows that

g(t)\geqslant g(t_{p})+\frac{p-1}{t_{p}}(t-t_{p})\hskip 9.24994pt\text{for all }t>0.

Hence,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{p-1}e^{-g(t)}\,dt

\displaystyle\leqslant e^{(p-1)-g(t_{p})}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{p-1}e^{-\frac{(p-1)t}{t_{p}}}\,dt

\displaystyle=e^{(p-1)-g(t_{p})}\left(\frac{t_{p}}{p-1}\right)^{p}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{p-1}e^{-t}\,dt

\displaystyle=e^{-g(t_{p})}t_{p}^{p-1}\frac{e^{p-1}(p-1)!}{(p-1)^{p-1}}\frac{t_{p}}{p-1}\approx M_{p}\frac{t_{p}}{\sqrt{p-1}}.

Finally, if
0<t<t_{p}
then
g^{\prime}_{+}(t)\leqslant(p-1)/t_{p}
, which implies

g(t_{p})\leqslant g(0)+\int_{0}^{t_{p}}\frac{p-1}{t_{p}}\,dt=p-1,

while

g(2t_{p})\geqslant g(t_{p})+\frac{p-1}{t_{p}}(2t_{p}-t_{p})\geqslant p-1.

This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{L}^{n}
with
f(0)=\|f\|_{\infty}=1
. For any
p\geqslant 2
we have

c_{1}K_{p}(f)\subseteq R_{p}(f)\subseteq c_{2}K_{p}(f),

where
c_{1},c_{2}>0
are absolute constants. The right-hand side inclusion holds for all
p>1
.

Proof.

Fix
\xi\in S^{n-1}
and consider the convex function
g(t)=-\ln f(t\xi)
.
Let
M_{p}=\sup\{e^{-g(t)}t^{p-1}:t>0\}
, and suppose that

e^{-g(t_{p})}t_{p}^{p-1}=f(t_{p})t_{p}^{p-1}=M_{p}
.
Then, by Lemma
6.4
,

\frac{M_{p}t_{p}}{p}\leqslant\int_{0}^{\infty}f(t)t^{p-1}\,dt\leqslant c\,\frac{M_{p}t_{p}}{\sqrt{p-1}}.

By the definition of
K_{p}(f)
, this implies

(M_{p}t_{p})^{1/p}\leqslant\varrho_{K_{p}(f)}(\xi)\leqslant(M_{p}t_{p})^{1/p}\left(\frac{cp}{\sqrt{p-1}}\right)^{1/p}\leqslant c_{1}(M_{p}t_{p})^{1/p}

for all
p\geqslant 2
, where
c_{1}>0
is an absolute constant. The left-hand side inequality holds for all
p>1
.

Since
M_{p}t_{p}=f(t_{p}\xi)t_{p}^{p}\leqslant t_{p}^{p}
, and from Lemma
6.4
we have

g(t_{p})\leqslant p-1
, it follows that

M_{p}t_{p}=f(t_{p}\xi)t_{p}^{p-1}t_{p}\geqslant e^{-(p-1)}t_{p}^{p}\geqslant e^{-p}t_{p}^{p}.

Thus,

c_{2}t_{p}\leqslant\varrho_{K_{p}(f)}(\xi)\leqslant c_{1}t_{p}.

Moreover, since
g(t_{p})\leqslant p-1\leqslant g(2t_{p})
, we also have

f(2t_{p}\xi)\leqslant e^{-(p-1)}\leqslant f(t_{p}\xi)
, implying

t_{p}\leqslant\varrho_{R_{p}(f)}(\xi)\leqslant 2t_{p}.

Combining these estimates yields the desired inclusion.
∎

Lemma 6.6
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{L}^{n}
with
f(0)=\|f\|_{\infty}=1
.
For any
p\geqslant 2
we have

K_{p}(\Delta_{\rm out}f)\approx K_{p}(f)_{{\rm out}}\hskip 18.49988ptK_{p}(\Delta_{\rm in}f)\approx K_{p}(f)_{{\rm in}}.

Proof.

Let
x\in K_{p}(\Delta_{\rm out}f)
.
By Lemma
6.5
,
c_{1}x\in R_{p}(\Delta_{\rm out}f)
.
Hence there exist
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

c_{1}x=\tfrac{1}{2}(x_{1}+x_{2})
and

f(x_{1})f(-x_{2})\geqslant e^{-2(p-1)}
.
Since
\|f\|_{\infty}=1
, we obtain

f(x_{1})\geqslant e^{-2(p-1)}
and
f(-x_{2})\geqslant e^{-2(p-1)}
,
that is,
x_{1}\in R_{2p-1}(f)
and
x_{2}\in R_{2p-1}(\overline{f})
.
Applying Lemma
6.5
again gives

c_{1}x\in\tfrac{1}{2}\big(R_{2p-1}(f)+R_{2p-1}(\overline{f})\big)\subseteq c_{2}\big(K_{2p-1}(f)-K_{2p-1}(f)\big)\approx K_{2p-1}(f)_{{\rm put}}\approx K_{p}(f)_{{\rm out}},

where we also used
K_{p}(\overline{f})=-K_{p}(f)
and (
6.5
).
Thus,

K_{p}(\Delta_{\rm out}f)\subseteq c_{3}K_{p}(f)_{{\rm out}}.

Conversely, if
x_{1},x_{2}\in K_{p}(f)
, then
c_{1}x_{1}\in R_{p}(f)
and

-c_{1}x_{2}\in R_{p}(\overline{f})
. Hence,

(\Delta_{\rm out}f)(c_{1}(x_{1}-x_{2}))\geqslant\sqrt{f(c_{1}x_{1})f(-c_{1}x_{2})}\geqslant e^{-(p-1)},

which implies

x_{1}-x_{2}\in c_{1}^{-1}R_{p}(\Delta_{\rm out}f)\subseteq(c_{2}/c_{1})K_{p}(\Delta_{\rm out}f).

Therefore,

K_{p}(f)_{{\rm out}}\subseteq K_{p}(f)-K_{p}(f)\subseteq c_{4}K_{p}(\Delta_{\rm out}f).

For the second assertion, observe that

\displaystyle R_{p}(\Delta_{\rm in}f)

\displaystyle=\{x:\min\{f(x),\overline{f}(x)\}\geqslant e^{-(p-1)}\}

\displaystyle=\{x:f(x)\geqslant e^{-(p-1)}\}\cap\{x:\overline{f}(x)\geqslant e^{-(p-1)}\}=R_{p}(f)\cap R_{p}(\overline{f}),

and thus, by Lemma
6.5
,

K_{p}(\Delta_{\rm in}f)\approx K_{p}(f)\cap K_{p}(\overline{f})=K_{p}(f)\cap(-K_{p}(f))=K_{p}(f)_{{\rm in}}.

∎

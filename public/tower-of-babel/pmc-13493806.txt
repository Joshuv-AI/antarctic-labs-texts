Research on aerodynamic characteristics prediction of folding-wing aircraft based on improved PointNet++
Authors: Zhiqiang Chen, Chaoyang Zhai, Xiang Li, Xiaolu Wang, Xinzhe Zhang, Daijun Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractFolding-wing aircraft enhance flight performance across diverse mission profiles, representing a key development trend for next-generation variable-configuration aircraft. However, current research predominantly focuses on structural design and aeroelastic vibrations, leaving the prediction of their aerodynamic characteristics in its infancy. Engineering typically relies on computational fluid dynamics (CFD) for analysis, but high-fidelity CFD simulations demand substantial computational resources and time, creating a bottleneck for rapid design iterations. To address this, a method for fast aerodynamic performance prediction is crucial. This study constructs an aerodynamic database using Fluent’s dynamic mesh technology. It integrates the PointNet ++ model to build an intelligent mapping from the aircraft’s dynamic shape to its surface pressure field distribution, achieving near real-time (second-level) prediction. The model’s accuracy is enhanced by processing CFD-calculated surface pressure data through techniques like random sampling and grouping. Compared to CFD results, the predicted lift coefficient error remains within 6%, demonstrating good accuracy and promising application value for improving aircraft design efficiency.Keywords: Folding-wing aircraft, Surface pressure prediction, Data-driven, Neural network, Rapid predictionSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

A morphing aircraft is an aircraft that can change its own configuration to adapt to different flight environments and optimize its aerodynamic performance, so as to complete various mission requirements. As a type of morphing aircraft, folding-wing aircraft offer performance benefits accompanied by specific technical challenges. Compared with traditional fixed-configuration aircraft, due to the introduction of a folding mechanism in folding-wing variant aircraft, their aerodynamic shape changes dynamically with the folding state, resulting in the need to consider multi-dimensional parameters such as folding angle and folding position simultaneously in the analysis of aerodynamic characteristics, and the calculation process is more complex and time-consuming. Therefore, for folding-wing variant aircraft, accurately predicting their aerodynamic characteristics is a key link to ensure the stability of the folding process and improve flight control performance. Among them, accurately predicting the surface pressure coefficient of the aircraft is particularly important, which is specifically reflected in the following two aspects: (1) By analyzing the distribution and change of surface pressure, the high-pressure and low-pressure areas on the wing under different folding states can be accurately identified, especially providing optimization guidance for phenomena such as airflow separation that are likely to occur at the folding connection part; (2) The surface pressure distribution is the core basis for evaluating the comprehensive aerodynamic performance of folding-wing aircraft. Based on the pressure coefficient, key parameters such as lift, drag, and pitching moment can be further deduced, thereby providing data support for the structural design of the folding mechanism and the optimization of flight control strategies.

In recent years, the rapid development of artificial intelligence and data science technologies has opened up new paths for their in-depth application in the aerospace field. In the aspect of two-dimensional wing aerodynamic prediction, accurately and efficiently predicting airfoil aerodynamic coefficients is crucial for improving aircraft performance. For this purpose, Chen et al.1 proposed a multi-task learning framework for calculating two-dimensional airfoil aerodynamic parameters. This framework contains two key subtasks: pressure coefficient prediction and airfoil flow field prediction. Pressure coefficient prediction focuses on providing local features and mainly predicts the pressure distribution on the airfoil surface to accurately calculate the lift coefficient and drag coefficient; airfoil flow field prediction focuses on the global pressure and velocity changes on the airfoil surface and around the flow field to capture the overall flow characteristics. Tang et al.2 proposed an improved Fourier neural operator for flow field prediction. It addresses the high computational cost of traditional aerodynamic optimization and the poor accuracy and generalization of existing deep learning-based surrogate models. The operator achieves significantly reduced prediction errors (by 70–90%), accurate prediction of supercritical airfoils (with a drag coefficient error of approximately 1 count on the validation set), and better generalization performance.

Due to the late start of the aerodynamic performance prediction of three-dimensional wings, the high cost of three-dimensional aerodynamic numerical simulation, and the lack of flexibility of traditional parameterization methods, it has always been a core bottleneck problem. Li et al.3 proposed a non-parametric deep learning meta-model method based on point clouds and the PointNet architecture. This method can directly learn and predict the aerodynamic characteristics from the point cloud data representing the object surface end-to-end. The verification with the ONERA-M6 wing as an example shows that this method is more consistent with the experimental data than the prediction results of the deep neural network based on parametric learning, achieving efficient and accurate prediction of the aerodynamic characteristics of three-dimensional geometries. Zhang et al.4 proposed a multi-task deep learning framework customized for the rapid prediction of the velocity field and aerodynamic coefficients of airfoils with trailing-edge flaps, successfully solving the problems of high computational cost and long time consumption of traditional computational fluid dynamics (CFD) simulation methods when analyzing airfoils with trailing-edge flaps. In a specific case study, the prediction time of this model is only 1/7214 of the time required for CFD simulation.

In recent years, with the rapid development of artificial intelligence technology, aerodynamic modeling based on deep learning has gradually become a research hotspot. Sekar et al.5 proposed a data-driven method for predicting the steady flow field of incompressible laminar flow around airfoils, which combines deep convolutional neural networks (CNN) and deep multi-layer perceptrons (MLP). This method addresses the time-consuming issue in traditional approaches where solutions to the airfoil flow field are obtained by solving the Navier-Stokes (NS) equations. Ultimately, it achieves efficient and accurate airfoil flow field prediction results: after training the network using a database generated by the OpenFOAM solver, there is no need to solve the NS equations. Instead, only the airfoil geometric parameters extracted by CNN, Reynolds number, and angle of attack need to be input into the MLP network, and the flow field around the airfoil can be obtained within a few seconds. Wu et al.6 used a deep belief network as a surrogate model for aerodynamic coefficient prediction, predicted the aerodynamic parameters of the wing with it, and finally adopted an improved particle swarm algorithm for rapid optimization, and applied it to the design of a general aircraft wing. Under the condition of ensuring that the drag does not increase, the lift of multiple working conditions was significantly improved, verifying the efficiency and superiority of the proposed method. Du et al.7 proposed a multi-fidelity deep neural network model integrating the particle swarm algorithm and its double-threshold update strategy, solving the problems of insufficient prediction accuracy and high computational cost of traditional multi-fidelity models in aerodynamic shape optimization, and finally obtaining an efficient optimization framework that can not only ensure the reliability of the optimization results but also halve the number of high-fidelity data evaluations.

In view of the nonlinearity and complexity of aerodynamic data, many scholars have used data reduction methods to process and analyze it. Pan et al.8. proposed a novel reduced-order model based on a deep sequence generation model, namely the Long Sequence-to-Sequence (Lseq2seq) model, for the effective prediction of unsteady aerodynamic forces. They also explored a prediction-correction hybrid method called GRU-Lseq2seq to predict the flutter response of airfoils. Peng et al.9 propose a neural network-based reduced-order model (using a CNN as the encoder, paired with a fully connected (FC) decoder and a deconvolutional (DC) decoder respectively) to solve the problem of predicting the unsteady flow field around single/multiple cylinders. In particular, the deconvolutional decoder enhances the ability to capture and reconstruct the spatial relationships of low-rank feature spaces, enabling fast and accurate prediction of the velocity field under different flow field complexities.

On the basis of existing research, this paper further improves the PointNet++ algorithm, integrates it with the established aerodynamic database, and constructs an intelligent mapping model from the dynamic shape to the surface pressure field distribution. This model aims to achieve fast and high-precision prediction of the surface pressure distribution of folding-wing aircraft during the dynamic change process.

In the undeformed configuration of the wing, the pressure peak at the leading edge is concentrated, the spanwise pressure gradient is steep, the profile drag is large but the high aspect ratio results in low induced drag. As the folding angle increases, the leading edge peak weakens, the pressure distribution becomes more uniform, and the concentration of aerodynamic loads is mitigated; the effective projected area of the wing decreases, the maximum theoretical lift coefficient decreases, and the reduction of the aspect ratio intensifies the wing tip effect, and the low-pressure area on the upper surface expands. When the folding angle reaches, the high pressure at the leading edge of the wing is sharply concentrated, and the low-pressure area at the rear of the upper surface is vast; the increase in the sweep angle inhibits the flow separation and improves the static stability at low speeds and large angles of attack, but at the cost of a significant increase in the maximum lift and induced drag.

Figure 7 shows the relationship between lift coefficient, moment coefficient, folding angle, and angle of attack. Under the same flight conditions (same angle of attack and same altitude), as the wing folding angle increases, the lift coefficient of the wing decreases. This is mainly because the folding of the wingtips reduces the overall wingspan and wing area of the aircraft. The curves at all folding angles are approximately a set of parallel lines in the small angle-of-attack region. This indicates that the change in the folding angle does not significantly affect the lift curve slope of the wing. All the curves approximately intersect at ≈-3°and Cl = 0. This once again proves that the change in the folding angle does not change the basic properties of the airfoil. In addition, although the aerodynamic parameters are calculated only within a small angle-of-attack range in this paper, it can be seen that the derivative of the curve is decreasing, that is, the growth rate of the lift coefficient with the increase of becomes significantly slower and is no longer a strictly linear relationship. It can be predicted that when the angle of attack continues to increase, the lift coefficient will remain unchanged or even decrease because the aircraft reaches the stall critical angle of attack, resulting in a significant reduction in the lift of the aircraft.

Modeling process design

For the prediction of the overall aircraft pressure distribution, constructing a single comprehensive prediction model can more fully consider the aerodynamic interaction effects between regions such as the wing and fuselage, thus directly realizing the rapid and high-precision prediction of the oncoming flow conditions and aerodynamic shape parameters to the surface pressure distribution of the whole aircraft. Compared with the traditional surrogate models that only predict the integrated aerodynamic force or local points, this model can more effectively capture and characterize the spatial correlation between different grid nodes through its internal grid region division and boundary information interaction mechanism, thus showing better performance in the prediction of the overall aircraft aerodynamic pressure distribution.

The goal of this work is to predict the pressure coefficient based on the geometric features of the point cloud transformed from the surface mesh vertices, namely coordinates and surface normal vectors12. The proposed framework uses PointNet++ as the deep learning architecture and processes the data before and after the deep learning module, including subset division, subset reordering and multiple samplings. The PointNet++ model architecture is shown in Fig. 8, and its core is divided into two parts: an encoder (Set Abstraction, SA) and a decoder (Feature Propagation, FP). The input point cloud first undergoes feature extraction and downsampling through three SA layers: each SA layer selects the center points by farthest point sampling, groups the neighboring points using the nearest neighbor algorithm, extracts local features through a shared MLP and max pooling, and finally concatenates the coordinates and features to capture multi-scale information. After passing through the SA layers, the point cloud resolution gradually decreases and the feature dimension increases, finally extracting the global features. Subsequently, the data enters the FP layer for feature propagation and upsampling: the low-resolution features are mapped to the high-resolution space through interpolation (such as inverse distance weighted interpolation), the skip connections fuse the features of the corresponding SA layers, and then high-resolution features are generated through MLP transformation. After passing through the FP layer, the point cloud resolution returns to the input scale, and the feature dimension is adjusted according to the task requirements. Finally, the features are output through a linear layer to obtain the pressure coefficient of each point. This structure realizes the effective modeling of complex geometric features through multi-scale feature extraction and fusion.

PointNet ++ model architecture.

The Set Abstraction (SA) layer is the core encoder module of PointNet++. It extracts features and downsamples point clouds through multi-level cascading. Its core process consists of four key steps: First, it selects center points through Farthest Point Sampling (FPS). This algorithm iteratively selects the point with the maximum distance from the selected point set, ensuring that the sampled points evenly cover the entire point cloud and effectively retaining geometric boundary information. Subsequently, it uses the ball query or k-NN algorithm to construct a local neighborhood for each center point. Then, it extracts local features through shared MLP and max pooling operations. Finally, it concatenates the sampled point coordinates and local features to form an enhanced feature representation containing spatial position information. The SA layer gradually reduces the number of points layer by layer (such as from ), while expanding the receptive field and integrating multi-scale context information, laying the foundation for subsequent feature propagation and task prediction. The FPS algorithm iteratively selects the point farthest from the selected point set, ensuring that the sampled points are evenly distributed in space, thus better retaining the geometric features of the point cloud.

The Feature Propagation (FP) layer, as the decoder module of PointNet++, restores the point cloud resolution and fuses multi-scale information through feature propagation and upsampling mechanisms: First, it uses inverse distance weighted interpolation or nearest neighbor interpolation to map low-resolution features to a high-resolution space, generating high-precision initial features; Subsequently, it splices the interpolated features with the features of the corresponding encoder-stage SA layer through skip connections to achieve deep fusion of local details and global context; Finally, it uses a multi-layer perceptron (MLP) to perform a non-linear transformation on the fused features, generating a high-resolution feature representation with both spatial fineness and semantic richness. While making the output point cloud scale return to the original size, the feature dimension can be flexibly adjusted according to task requirements.

For traditional PointNet++ algorithm datasets, the number of input and output point clouds is 1000–2000 data points each time. By adjusting the model input and output, etc., it is finally decided to process the data in chunks. The overall data is randomly chunked into point clouds. Conclusions

This study constructs an aerodynamic data calculation framework based on PointNet++. First, we collect a large amount of aerodynamic experimental data containing the point cloud data of various folding-wing aircraft and their corresponding parameters such as lift coefficients. Subsequently, the point cloud data is preprocessed to improve the data quality and consistency. The preprocessed data is input into the PointNet++ model for training, and by continuously adjusting the model parameters, it can accurately predict the aerodynamic parameters.

Through a large number of experiments and optimizations, this research has achieved phased results in the calculation of aerodynamic data based on PointNet++. From the perspective of prediction accuracy, the overall performance of the model is good, and it can capture the variation law of aerodynamic data to a certain extent. Although there are still certain errors in some data, the prediction error of the overall lift coefficient is controlled within 6%, which proves that the proposed network model can better predict the aerodynamic performance of the wing and provides a technical basis for the development of a large model for subsequent aerodynamic performance prediction. In terms of algorithm optimization, although the PointNet++ model shows good characteristics in processing point cloud data, there is still room for further improvement. In the future, more advanced network structures or training strategies can be explored, such as introducing attention mechanisms, designing more complex loss functions, etc., to improve the prediction accuracy and stability of the model. In addition, other deep learning models or traditional aerodynamic calculation methods14–17 can also be combined to construct a hybrid model and integrate the advantages of different methods to further improve the overall performance of aerodynamic data calculation.

Enhancing maternal autonomy in choosing birthing positions: an exploratory pre-post intervention study protocol (MAM-Care)
Authors: Mi-Ran Okumu, Lissa Haid-Schmallenberg, Niklas Sand, Arno Stöcker, Anna Volkert, Nadine Scholten
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionAlthough evidence on whether particular birthing positions offer advantages over others remains inconclusive, the majority of women give birth in the supine position. Earlier research associates maternal satisfaction with autonomy and active participation in choosing positions, irrespective of the clinical outcome. In practice, however, autonomy may be limited by the dynamics of labour, insufficient information and sociocultural norms. Against this background, the intervention aims to enhance maternal autonomy in childbirth through the provision of activating information materials and psychological nudges. Women shall be encouraged to explore various positions enabling them to autonomously choose positions that best align with their individual needs.Methods and analysisUsing an exploratory pre-post design, this controlled intervention is carried out as a multicentre study in six German obstetrics departments. The intervention will include various ways to inform and nudge women to move and try out various positions autonomously during childbirth. Due to its minimally invasive character, it should easily integrate into organisational processes and everyday care. The primary endpoint of the study is the proportion of mothers who indicate that their final birthing position felt appropriate. Secondary endpoints include the proportion of upright positions as final birthing positions and maternal and neonatal health outcomes among others. Data sources include maternal longitudinal data, birth companions’ and midwives’/physicians’ perspectives as well as clinical sheets and aggregated comparisons.Ethics and disseminationThis study obtained ethical approval from the Ethics Committee of the Medical Faculty of the University of Bonn and the Medical Chamber of Nordrhein (2025–325 BO). Introduction

Upright birthing positions (sitting, squatting, standing and hands-and-knees) as well as maternal mobility and positional changes have been cited as facilitating physiological labour, reducing the need for clinical interventions and shortening the second stage of labour.1,7 However, the majority of women give birth in the supine position while evidence as to whether particular birthing positions offer advantages over others remains inconclusive.14 8,11 Against this background, national and international guidelines advise that women should position themselves in ways they find most comfortable while suggesting that they avoid the supine position during the second stage of labour.8 9 12 13 This shows emphasis on women’s autonomy but also draws certain boundaries. The latter requires information on the potential disadvantages of the supine position while also reassuring that it remains an acceptable option if preferred by the woman.

The choice of birthing positions during the first and second stages of labour offers a meaningful lens to examine women’s autonomy and participation during childbirth.14,17 Autonomy or self-determination regards patients as active consumers.18 19 In this regard, shared decision-making forms a key component, and caregivers have the responsibility to support patients in exercising their self-determination.20 Indeed, most parturients wish to be involved in decision-making during childbirth.21 However, in a cross-sectional survey across Germany, 30.8% of women responded that they did not choose their birthing position voluntarily, with the highest proportion (22.5%) relating to the supine position.11 Alongside clinically indicated interventions, factors limiting women’s autonomy in choosing birthing positions include the preferences of caregivers, insufficient information and a lack of encouragement to actively select or try different positions.22,26 Earlier research indicates that maternal satisfaction is less affected by the birthing position itself but is associated with autonomy and active participation in choosing positions, irrespective of the clinical outcome.1617 20 26,31 This highlights the central role of autonomy.

However, supporting autonomy during labour may be challenging20 28 32: first, labour is a dynamic process. Birthing positions may feel different from those anticipated, and interventions may require other positions than planned.22 Decision-making during childbirth is a continuous, non-linear and dynamic process; therefore, ongoing active involvement throughout birth seems crucial compared with simply using the preferred and anticipated position.14 33 This requires flexibility and a combination of clinical assessment with parturients’ preferences and reactions,24 highlighting the need for a person-centred approach.28 34 Second, the perceived autonomy also depends on the availability of information.15 17 25 34 Information includes both knowledge of birthing positions and equipment use, as well as awareness of the option to move freely. Third, preferences and prior knowledge are often shaped by sociocultural influences which vary across time and culture35: in Western societies, the supine position predominates and is the most familiar position.17 In fact, according to earlier research, the majority of women give birth while lying on their backs.11 23 26 36 Therefore, De Jonge et al23 call for education of women, midwives, physicians and the public in general so that alternative positions become a viable option. Furthermore, in the delivery room, parturients may lack confidence or assurance to move and change positions themselves.14 17 19 This underscores the importance of providing comprehensive information on birth positions and empowering women to autonomously choose their preferred position. Such support may enhance parturients’ capacity to recognise and articulate their preferences while participating in shared decision-making.

To address this need, the newly created intervention seeks to strengthen parturients’ autonomy through structured information and targeted psychological nudges. The low-threshold approach is designed to reach diverse populations, particularly groups with low levels of education and non-native speakers. The minimally invasive design can easily be integrated into daily routine practice. As the intervention is expected to produce changes at the organisational level, a pre-post design was chosen to compare birth experiences before and after implementation.

Objectives

The primary objective is to enhance maternal autonomy in childbirth through the provision of activating information materials and psychological nudges. Women will be encouraged to explore different birthing positions and autonomously select those that best meet their individual needs while ensuring maternal and neonatal safety is not compromised.

Methods and analysis

This study protocol was written in line with the template for intervention description and replication (TIDieR).37 The Standard Protocol Items: Recommendations for Interventional Trials (SPIRIT)38 guidelines were followed to ensure transparency in reporting. As the study does not employ randomisation, the corresponding SPIRIT section was omitted. Detailed information on the implementation of TIDieR and SPIRIT is provided in the online supplemental material.

Study design

The non-randomised pre-post intervention study is conducted across six German obstetrics departments. As the intervention targets organisational processes, individual-level randomisation is not feasible. Selective application would interfere with routine care, compromising its minimally invasive integration into daily practice. Therefore, a pre-post design was chosen. The study includes a 6-month control period, followed by the implementation and an intervention phase of 5 months during which all mothers who give birth vaginally will receive the intervention. The intervention phase is designed to be shorter than the control phase, as higher participation is expected, allowing for sufficient data to be collected within a shorter period. To assess both baseline conditions and the potential effects of the intervention, written/online surveys will be conducted during the control period and during the intervention period. The surveys will address mothers and their companions during childbirth, as well as midwives and physicians.

Both during the control and intervention phases, mothers after vaginal birth will be invited to participate in the survey. For mothers, the study consists of two surveys: the first is conducted immediately after delivery, and the second is 3 months post partum. Birth companions will be invited to participate in a one-time online survey directly after childbirth. The data of the mothers and birth companions will be matched pseudonymously with the data of the hospital’s maternity record sheets, containing clinical data on birth and its outcomes. In the context of a formative evaluation, a survey of midwives and physicians of the participating obstetrics departments will be conducted at two time points (at the end of the control phase and at the end of the intervention phase). Study setting

The study will be carried out in six German obstetrics departments of different sizes, ranging from about 600 to 2850 deliveries per year, with different ownership structures (university hospital, municipal hospital and non-profit hospital). The departments are located in the northern and western parts of Germany and draw their patients from urban areas as well as from the rural surroundings. The departments were recruited via an announcement on Instagram and selected according to the criteria of heterogeneity. Each participating department signed a cooperation agreement and received financial compensation for participating in the study.

Data sources will include pseudonymised survey data of the mothers and their birth companions, as well as anonymous survey data of the midwives and physicians. Mothers can consent to the linkage of survey data from their birth companions and clinical parameters. If the mother consents to the linkage of survey data and clinical parameters, the following information will be extracted from the clinical documentation in pseudonymised form: induction of labour, amniotomy, mode of fetal heart monitoring, prolonged second stage of labour, episiotomy, amount of blood loss, birth weight, status of the newborn infant (APGAR score), epidural and further medication, gravidity and parity, epidural and further medication, gravidity and parity, body mass index (BMI) before pregnancy, birthing position, fetal position, birth injuries and mode of delivery (vaginal birth or assisted vaginal birth). At the same time, these data are also compared anonymously at the organisational level for the control and intervention periods (complete survey). In addition, the proportion of caesarean sections to vaginal births is compared using the aggregated department data.

The participants’ personal data will be handled sensitively and confidentially. To guarantee the segregation of research data and personal identifying data (such as full names, pseudonyms and email addresses needed for the follow-up survey), an administrative study office will be created within the project. Administrative study management staff will only access administrative data, while scientific staff will only access the pseudonymised research data. Any data transfer outside the project will occur exclusively in an anonymised form. All study procedures will adhere to relevant data protection regulations. Survey participation will be voluntary. For the pseudonymous survey with mothers and birth companions, written consent will be obtained (see online supplemental material). Withdrawal of consent will be possible at any time without repercussions. Informed consent of participants will be obtained by the administrative study office.

Intervention

The rationale for the intervention stems from the comprehensive assessment of the status quo of obstetric care in German maternity departments conducted in Phase I of MAM-Care (maternal involvement, centredness, safety and care in obstetrics), supported by the Federal Ministry of Research, Technology and Space (BMFTR) under grant number 01GY2110. The intervention materials were created in accordance with scientific evidence and the German obstetric guideline ‘Vaginal Birth at Term’.8 The intervention components were derived from a preceding qualitative interview study involving mothers (n=17). Analysis was conducted against the Theoretical Domains Framework and the Capability, Opportunity, Motivation-Behaviour model.

The intervention will include various ways to encourage women to move and take positions autonomously during childbirth. The intervention is designed to be minimally invasive, that is, it should affect organisational processes in the hospital as little as possible and thus be easily integrated into everyday care. At the same time, obstetric departments have the option of selecting which materials they would like to use, and materials can be tailored to individual departmental circumstances. The materials will be made publicly available on the project website from the start of the intervention phase. The information materials include the following:

No criteria for discontinuing or modifying the allocated intervention are anticipated, as the study involves no clinical or pharmacological procedures. Strategies to improve adherence to the intervention are not applicable. Usual obstetric care may continue without restriction throughout the study. Harms will be defined as health-related outcomes affecting the mother or newborn and will be systematically assessed by reviewing clinical documentation, comparing the control and intervention groups.

Sample size

The conservative sample size estimation is based on the annual number of births in the six obstetrics departments. Assuming an even distribution of births over the year, with a caesarean section rate of 33%, we expect 2900 vaginal births during the control and intervention phases, respectively. The expected response rate is 33% post partum and again 25% for the follow-up survey. In Phase I of MAM-Care, the maternal survey achieved a response rate of 28% without incentives. For the companion survey, we expect a high response rate of 75%, based on mothers’ prior engagement and the likelihood that motivated companions in close social relationships will participate. Mothers’ participation in the survey will be incentivised with €5 per survey in the form of a gift certificate.

The estimated sample size of midwives and physicians is based on full-time equivalent (FTE) positions across the six study departments in 2023: 117.76 FTE positions for midwives and 146.26 FTE positions for physicians. Due to the high proportion of part-time staff, the absolute number of employees is higher. Based on self-selection of the departments and the use of incentives (gift baskets during the control and intervention periods), we anticipate an increased response rate of approximately 50%.

Recruitment

In all participating departments, mothers after vaginal birth will receive the study information, declaration of consent and questionnaire. The participants will also receive a postcard to pass on to their birth companions. The postcard provides a QR code to an online questionnaire. Arabic-speaking and Russian-speaking mothers have the option to receive the documents in the corresponding language and to take part online via a QR code. Respective birth companions will not be surveyed.

Study information and questionnaires for midwives and physicians will be provided in the wards.

Data collection and analysis

Different modes of data collection will be used depending on the target group, including online and paper questionnaires. The same formats will be used during the control and intervention phases. All survey instruments will consist of both self-developed sets of questions and validated scales. The latter include the following: Satisfaction with Childbirth and Perceived Control in Childbirth,39 Childbirth Experience Questionnaire 2,40,42 Multidimensional Assessment of Interoceptive Awareness, V.2,43 44 Experience of Shame45 46 and Short Scale for Measuring General Self-efficacy Beliefs (ASKU).47 While the core questionnaire will be consistent across both phases, additional intervention-related items will be added to the survey during the intervention phase.

The questionnaire for mothers will focus on the birth experience using patient-reported experience measures (PREMs) and patient-reported outcome measures (PROMs), their sources of information regarding birthing positions, personality traits and general personal information. German-speaking mothers will first receive a paper questionnaire, while the follow-up survey will be administered online.

In the online survey, birth companions will be asked about their perception of the delivery room and their role during childbirth.

Midwives and physicians will receive paper questionnaires. The questions will target clinical practices, personal attitudes towards childbirth, general personal information and the intervention material (only during the intervention phase).

All questionnaires will be pre-tested by the respective target group.

Data will be collected through an online survey, using the platform www.limesurvey.de,www.limesurvey.dewhile paper questionnaires will be digitised using the TeleForm software. All entries will be checked for implausible or inconsistent values to ensure data quality. Data coding, entry and storage will follow secure procedures, with access restricted to authorised study personnel.

To analyse the primary and secondary endpoints, identify relevant influencing factors and estimate their effect sizes, inferential statistical methods will be conducted. At an aggregate level, the before/after data will be examined for significant differences.

Comparative analyses between mothers in the control and intervention groups will be conducted to evaluate statistically significant differences in primary and secondary outcomes. Appropriate inferential statistical tests for group comparisons, as well as regression analyses to examine associations, will be applied. Data linkage and analyses will be performed in accordance with informed consent. Missing data will not be imputed. Pre-specified subgroup analyses will be conducted for non-German-speaking mothers and selected obstetric outcomes. Sensitivity analyses will be performed to assess the robustness and reliability of the findings.

A Data Monitoring Committee (DMC) is not required for this study, as it involves no clinical or pharmacological interventions and poses minimal risk to participants. No interim analyses or stopping guidelines are planned as the study does not involve interventions beyond standard obstetric care. No formal monitoring of trial conduct is required for the same reason.

Patient and public involvement

In the sense of a participatory approach, the development of the intervention materials involved consultations and polls over social media, addressing both mothers and healthcare professionals. Among others, this included participants from the interview study that informed the intervention development.

Ethics and dissemination

A positive ethical vote was obtained by the Ethics Committee of the Medical Faculty of the University of Bonn and the Medical Chamber of Nordrhein (2025–325 BO). Following the inclusion of an additional obstetric department, an amendment of the original approval was obtained. The amended ethical approval covers all six participating clinics.

Public dissemination of project information, intervention materials and results will take place through the project’s homepage and social media. Apart from general project information, there will be specific content tailored to the target groups of mothers and medical staff. The project’s Instagram page shall facilitate dissemination at a low threshold. Study results will further be published in peer-reviewed journals and presented during scientific conferences.

De-identified participant data and statistical codes will be made available in online repositories linked to the content referenced by the peer-reviewed journal articles.

No protocol amendments are currently planned. No special provisions for ancillary or post-trial care are planned, as postpartum care is covered by standard care, and no new clinical or pharmacological interventions will be introduced. Participants will be informed through the study materials about available psychological support services.

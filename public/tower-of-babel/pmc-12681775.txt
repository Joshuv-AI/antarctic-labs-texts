Protocol to establish rice root aphid colonies and experiments in greenhouse settings
Authors: Nuris M. Acosta, Jonathan Lee-Rodriguez, Luis Canas
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
SummaryThe rice root aphid (RRA) Rhopalosiphum rufiabdominalis (Sasaki) is a cosmopolitan species that attacks the root system of many plants grown in fields and greenhouses, including hydroponic production systems. Here, we present a protocol to establish consistent RRA colonies used to infest plants and to set up experiments in greenhouse settings. We describe steps for researching and understanding RRA colonies and establishing and maintaining the colony. We then provide suggestions for conducting studies in a greenhouse setting.

Before you begin

Make sure that you confirm with your institution that you comply with the introduction of an insect that is not common in your state and how to follow protocols to do such research. The introduction of insects from another state is regulated by APHIS, and appropriate permits need to be secured.

In this protocol, we explain step by step how to establish a colony of RRA. Establishing a new colony requires important knowledge to be successful and adapt techniques to your own needs. It is important to 1. Do research to understand the aphid’s biology and ecology. 2. Apply that knowledge to establish a new colony. 3. Be observant to detect problems while performing the colony care. The main objective to maintain a colony is to perform research. Here we share the best management methods that worked for us when we ran an efficacy experiment in a greenhouse.

Innovation

A crucial aspect of insect research is the availability of colonies that provide the necessary insects. This is of foremost importance for species that are difficult to maintain. Here we provide an innovative approach to initiating and maintaining an RRA colony. This protocol provides insight into how to use the RRA lifecycle and biology to select an appropriate plant host. Detailed guidance on RRA transfer and counting plus innovative cage design ensures colony stability both in the controlled environment room and in the greenhouse.

Planning the experiment

Timeline for greenhouse experiment of Rice root aphid (RRA).

Expected outcomes

Rice root aphid is a very finicky colony to maintain, because if you do not keep a close eye on it, the plants might be growing but the aphids in the roots system might be dead. We reviewed previous protocols and established our own based on previous colonies’ experience. We succeeded in establishing and maintaining the colony, producing enough aphids to run our experiments as well as shipping aphids to other scientists to establish their colony. We gave suggestions on how to infest, count, and do final assessments on the number of aphids.

Limitations

Setting up experiments using RRA are limited by the growth of the aphid colony. It is important to start early enough to have enough aphids for the experiments. Proper facilities, including growth chambers and greenhouses with excellent environmental controls are needed. Furthermore, the staff managing the colonies need to receive adequate training to keep the colonies alive and prevent any contamination.

Troubleshooting

Problem 1

Finding suitable plant hosts (related to optimal host, step 3b):

To keep a colony of RRA and even to do research, you need a suitable plant host where the aphids will reproduce and thrive, if not your colony will not establish, and you will not be able to run experiments.

Potential solution

•Check the literature for plants that have been used before, or other plants within the family that are easy to find and grow in your setting.•It is important to test several cycles, until you observe high population of aphids.

•Check the literature for plants that have been used before, or other plants within the family that are easy to find and grow in your setting.

Check the literature for plants that have been used before, or other plants within the family that are easy to find and grow in your setting.

•It is important to test several cycles, until you observe high population of aphids.

It is important to test several cycles, until you observe high population of aphids.

Problem 2

Watering (related to maintaining the colony, step 8e):

Soilless media humidity is important. If you water and fertilize too much, algae will form and when you stop watering the algae will dry out and kill the aphids, or make it harder for them to develop. On the other hand, if you do not water enough, the plant will dry out and the aphids will die.

Potential solution

•Check your plants daily, water only as needed, to maintain about 40% RH. If you do not have a soil moisture meter (hydrometer), assess the cup until you get used to the look and weight of the cup. The weight of our 473 ml cup when at 40% RH was 224g.•When the colony is established, you should transition to bigger pots, 15.2 cm diameter. They will hold the humidity better for a longer period.

Problem 3

Too many seeds (related to establishing the host plants, step 6b):

Adding too many seeds to a 473 ml cup will result in the plant forming a dense root system, the aphids will travel to the bottom, making it hard to harvest, and the aphids will die. Also, the cup will dry out too fast.

Potential solution

•Plant no more than 30 seeds per cup. This will result in an adequate root mass that will allow the aphid to move and settle well.•You could thin the seedlings if you planted too many.

Problem 4

Keeping plants for too long (related to maintaining the colony, step 8b):

Keeping plants that are too old is detrimental to the colony, because they may harbor unwanted insects. Also, old or dying plants will not host the aphids well and you risk losing the colony.

Potential solution

•When doing the maintenance of the colony, always discard old plants, because if you have contamination, those plants will harbor unwanted insects.•The best choice is to create a new cage as explained before.•If too busy, trim the old plants down to 3 cm from the soilless media, and add new plants to the cage.

Problem 5

Effect of temperature on RRA life parameters (related to increasing the colony for research, step 9a):

Environmental conditions on the rearing room, especially temperature, can determine the reproductive rate of the female aphids, therefore, it will impact the number of aphids you will need for your experiments.

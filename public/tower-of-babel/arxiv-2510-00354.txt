A posteriori error estimation for weak Galerkin method of the fourth-order singularly perturbed problem Journal: Journal of Computational and Applied Mathematics
Shicheng Liu, Qilong Zhai

Abstract
In this paper, we present a posteriori error estimation for weak Galerkin method applied to fourth order singularly perturbed problem. The weak Galerkin discretization space and numerical scheme are first described. A fully computable residual type error estimator is then constructed. Both the reliability and efficiency of the proposed estimator are rigorously demonstrated. Numerical experiments are provided to validate the theoretical findings.

Introduction

For given a bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
and

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{2}\Delta^{2}u-\Delta u

\displaystyle=f,\quad\text{in}~\Omega,

(1.1)

\displaystyle u=\nabla u\cdot\mathbf{n}

\displaystyle=0,\quad\text{on}~\partial\Omega.

Over recent decades, computation with adaptive grid refinement has established itself as a valuable and efficient methodology in scientific computing. Central to this technique is the design of an accurate a posteriori error estimator, which offers guidance on where and how to refine the grid. An estimator is deemed reliable if it provides a rigorous upper bound for the exact error, and efficient if it furnishes a corresponding lower bound. Upper bounds combined with lower bounds yield error indicators of optimal order, enabling efficient mesh refinement. Computable a posteriori error estimates and adaptive strategies for fourth-order problems have garnered growing interest over the last twenty years. This paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we introduce the Shishkin mesh and the assumptions associated. For any

\displaystyle\left(\nabla_{w,k}v,\mathbf{q}\right)_{T}=-\left(v_{0},\nabla\cdot\mathbf{q}\right)_{T}+\left\langle v_{b},\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{n}\right\rangle_{\partial T},\quad\forall\mathbf{q}\in\left[\mathbb{Q}_{k}(T)\right]^{2}.

(2.2)

For notational simplicity, and when no confusion may arise, we omit the subscript

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{h}u=\left\{\mathcal{Q}_{0}u,\mathcal{Q}_{b}u,\mathbf{Q}_{g}(\nabla u)\right\}.

Lemma 2.1
.

On each cell
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, the following identity holds for all

,

\displaystyle D^{2}_{w}v=\mathbb{Q}_{h}(D^{2}v).

(2.3)

Proof.

For any

\displaystyle\left(D^{2}_{w}v,\widetilde{\varphi}\right)_{T}

\displaystyle=\left(v,\nabla\cdot\nabla\cdot\widetilde{\varphi}\right)_{T}-\left\langle v,\nabla\cdot\widetilde{\varphi}\cdot\mathbf{n}\right\rangle_{\partial T}+\left\langle\nabla v,\widetilde{\varphi}\mathbf{n}\right\rangle_{\partial T}

\displaystyle=\left(D^{2}v,\widetilde{\varphi}\right)_{T}

\displaystyle=\left(\mathbb{Q}_{h}(D^{2}v),\widetilde{\varphi}\right)_{T}.

Since the equality holds for all
\widetilde{\varphi}\in[\mathbb{P}_{k}(T)]^{2\times 2}
, we conclude that

\displaystyle D^{2}_{w}v=\mathbb{Q}_{h}(D^{2}v),

which completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

On each cell
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, the following identity holds for all

,

\displaystyle\nabla_{w}v=\mathbf{Q}_{h}(\nabla v).

(2.4)

Proof.

For any
\mathbf{q}\in[\mathbb{P}_{k}(T)]^{2}
, applying integration by parts yields

\displaystyle\left(\nabla_{w}v,\mathbf{q}\right)_{T}

\displaystyle=-\left(v,\nabla\cdot\mathbf{q}\right)_{T}+\left\langle v,\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{n}\right\rangle_{\partial T}

\displaystyle=\left(\nabla v,\mathbf{q}\right)_{T}

\displaystyle=\left(\mathbf{Q}_{h}\nabla v,\mathbf{q}\right)_{T}.

Since the equality holds for all
\mathbf{q}\in[\mathbb{P}_{k}(T)]^{2}
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\nabla_{w}v=\mathbf{Q}_{h}(\nabla v),

which completes the proof.
∎

3
Numerical algorithm

For notational simplicity, we employ the following conventions

\displaystyle\left(D^{2}_{w}u_{h},D^{2}_{w}v_{h}\right)_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\left(D^{2}_{w}u_{h},D^{2}_{w}v_{h}\right)_{T},

\displaystyle\left\|D^{2}_{w}u_{h}\right\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\left\|D^{2}_{w}u_{h}\right\|_{T}^{2},

\displaystyle\left(\nabla_{w}u_{h},\nabla_{w}v_{h}\right)_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\left(\nabla_{w}u_{h},\nabla_{w}v_{h}\right)_{T},

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla_{w}u_{h}\right\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\left\|\nabla_{w}u_{h}\right\|_{T}^{2}.

The weak Galerkin scheme is then formulated as follows. Find
u_{h}\in V_{h}^{0}
such that

\displaystyle B_{h}(u_{h},v_{h})+A_{h}(u_{h},v_{h})=(f,v_{0}),

\displaystyle\forall v_{h}\in V_{h}^{0},

(3.1)

where

\displaystyle B_{h}(u_{h},v_{h})=\varepsilon^{2}(D^{2}_{w}u_{h},D^{2}_{w}v_{h})_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}+S_{1}(u_{h},v_{h}),

\displaystyle A_{h}(u_{h},v_{h})=(\nabla_{w}u_{h},\nabla_{w}v_{h})_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}+S_{2}(u_{h},v_{h}),

and the stabilizer terms are given by

\displaystyle S_{1}\left(u_{h},v_{h}\right)=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\left(\varepsilon^{2}h_{T}^{-1}\left\langle\nabla u_{0}-\mathbf{u}_{g},\nabla v_{0}-\mathbf{v}_{g}\right\rangle_{\partial T}+\varepsilon^{2}h_{T}^{-3}\left\langle u_{0}-u_{b},v_{0}-v_{b}\right\rangle_{\partial T}\right),

\displaystyle S_{2}\left(u_{h},v_{h}\right)=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\left(h_{T}\left\langle\nabla u_{0}-\mathbf{u}_{g},\nabla v_{0}-\mathbf{v}_{g}\right\rangle_{\partial T}+h_{T}^{-1}\left\langle u_{0}-u_{b},v_{0}-v_{b}\right\rangle_{\partial T}\right).

We endow the WG space
V_{h}
with an
H^{2}
-like seminorm defined by

\displaystyle{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}v_{h}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}^{2}=B_{h}(v_{h},v_{h})+A_{h}(v_{h},v_{h}).

(3.2)

Lemma 3.1
.

The quantity
{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}\cdot{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}
defines a norm in
V^{0}_{h}
, and the weak Galerkin scheme (
3.1
) has a unique solution.

Proof.

To verify that
{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}\cdot{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}
defines a norm on
V_{h}^{0}
, suppose
v_{h}\in V_{h}^{0}
satisfies
{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}v_{h}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}=0
. It follows that
D^{2}_{w}v_{h}=0
and
\nabla_{w}v_{h}=0
on each cell
T
, together with the conditions
v_{0}=v_{b}
and
\nabla v_{0}=\mathbf{v}_{g}
on
\partial T
. Now, for any
\widetilde{\varphi}\in[\mathbb{P}_{k}(T)]^{2\times 2}
, applying definition (
2.1
) along with
D^{2}_{w}v_{h}=0
, we obtain

\begin{split}0&=\left(D^{2}_{w}v_{h},\widetilde{\varphi}\right)_{T}\\
&=\left(v_{0},\nabla\cdot\nabla\cdot\widetilde{\varphi}\right)_{T}-\left\langle v_{b},\nabla\cdot\widetilde{\varphi}\cdot\mathbf{n}\right\rangle_{\partial T}+\left\langle\mathbf{v}_{g},\widetilde{\varphi}\mathbf{n}\right\rangle_{\partial T}\\
&=\left(D^{2}v_{0},\widetilde{\varphi}\right)_{T}+\left\langle v_{0}-v_{b},\nabla\cdot\widetilde{\varphi}\cdot\mathbf{n}\right\rangle_{\partial T}-\left\langle\nabla v_{0}-\mathbf{v}_{g},\widetilde{\varphi}\mathbf{n}\right\rangle_{\partial T}\\
&=\left(D^{2}v_{0},\widetilde{\varphi}\right)_{T},\end{split}

(3.3)

which implies
D^{2}v_{0}=0
on each cell
T
. Hence,
v_{0}
is a linear polynomial on
T
, and
\nabla v_{0}
is constant per cell. Combining this with the condition
\nabla v_{0}=\mathbf{v}_{g}
on
\partial T
, it follows that
\nabla v_{0}
is continuous across the entire domain
\Omega
. Since
\mathbf{v}_{g}=0
on
\partial\Omega
, we conclude that
\nabla v_{0}=0
in
\Omega
and
\mathbf{v}_{g}=0
on every edge. Therefore,
v_{0}
is constant on each cell. Using the condition
v_{0}=v_{b}
on
\partial T
, we deduce that
v_{0}
is continuous throughout
\Omega
. The boundary condition
v_{b}=0
on
\partial\Omega
then implies
v_{0}=0
in
\Omega
and
v_{b}=0
on all edges.

Now, let
u_{h}^{(1)}
and
u_{h}^{(2)}
be two distinct solutions of the WG scheme (
3.1
). Then, the error
u_{h}^{(1)}-u_{h}^{(2)}
belongs to
V_{h}^{0}
and satisfies

\displaystyle B_{h}(u_{h}^{(1)}-u_{h}^{(2)},v_{h})+A_{h}(u_{h}^{(1)}-u_{h}^{(2)},v_{h})=0,\quad\forall v\in V_{h}^{0}.

(3.4)

By choosing
v_{h}=u_{h}^{(1)}-u_{h}^{(2)}
in equation (
3.4
), it follows that

\displaystyle{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}u_{h}^{(1)}=u_{h}^{(2)}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}^{2}=0.

Since
{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}\cdot{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}
defines a norm on
V_{h}^{0}
, the identity
{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}u_{h}^{(1)}=u_{h}^{(2)}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}=0
implies

, and hence
u_{h}^{(1)}=u_{h}^{(2)}
. This establishes the uniqueness of the solution.
∎

4
A posteriori error estimation

In this section, we introduce a residual-based a posteriori error estimator for the singularly perturbed problem and establish its reliability and efficiency. First, we define an energy norm
\|\cdot\|_{\varepsilon}
, balanced with respect to the singular perturbation parameter
\varepsilon
, on the space
H_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
as follows

\displaystyle\|w\|_{\varepsilon}=\left(\varepsilon^{2}|w|_{2}^{2}+|w|_{1}^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

We also define the localized version
\|\cdot\|_{\varepsilon,d}
of this norm over a subdomain
d
, which may be an edge
e
or a cell
T
, as needed in the analysis.

To define an a posteriori error estimator for the singularly perturbed problem, we introduce the following local error indicators. First, define the parameters

\displaystyle\alpha_{T}=\min\{\varepsilon^{-1}h_{T}^{2},h_{T}\},

\displaystyle\alpha_{e,1}=\min\{\varepsilon^{-1}h_{T}^{3/2},h_{T}^{1/2}\},

\displaystyle\alpha_{e,2}=\min\{\varepsilon^{-1}h_{T}^{1/2},h_{T}^{-1/2}\}.

The local residual and jump terms are given by

\displaystyle R_{T}=f_{h}-\nabla\cdot\nabla\cdot\varepsilon^{2}D^{2}_{w}u_{h}+\nabla\cdot\nabla_{w}u_{h},

\displaystyle J_{e,1}=[(\nabla\cdot\varepsilon^{2}D_{w}^{2}u_{h}-\nabla_{w}u_{h})\cdot\mathbf{n}],

\displaystyle J_{e,2}=[\varepsilon^{2}D_{w}^{2}u_{h}\mathbf{n}].

Then, the local error indicators are defined as

\displaystyle\eta_{T,1}^{2}=\alpha_{T}^{2}\left\|f-f_{h}\right\|_{T}^{2},

\displaystyle\eta_{T,2}^{2}=\alpha_{T}^{2}\left\|R_{T}\right\|_{T}^{2},

\displaystyle\eta_{e,1}^{2}=\sum_{e\in\partial T}\alpha_{e,1}^{2}\left\|J_{e,1}\right\|_{e}^{2},

\displaystyle\eta_{e,2}^{2}=\sum_{e\in\partial T}\alpha_{e,2}^{2}\left\|J_{e,2}\right\|_{e}^{2}.

The local and global error estimators are respectively defined as

\displaystyle\eta_{T}=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{2}\left(\eta_{T,i}^{2}+\eta_{e,i}^{2}+S_{i}(u_{h},u_{h})|_{T}\right)\right)^{1/2},

\displaystyle\eta_{h}=\left(\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\eta_{T}^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

Upper bound

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{m}=\left\{v\in C^{1}(T):v|_{\kappa_{i}}\in\mathbb{P}_{m}(\kappa_{i}),i=1,2,\cdots,s\right\}.

Further details can be found in
[
16

Lemma 4.1

There exists an operator

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}|u_{0}-E(u_{0})|_{\alpha,T}^{2}\leq C\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}}\left(\|h_{e}^{1/2-\alpha}[u_{0}]\|_{e}^{2}+\|h_{e}^{3/2-\alpha}[\nabla u_{0}]\|_{e}\right),

for

Let

\displaystyle e_{h}=u-u_{h}=(u-u_{c})+(u_{c}-u_{h})=e_{c}+e_{d}.

(4.2)

Lemma 4.2

Let

\displaystyle l_{1}(u_{h})=

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\left(\varepsilon^{2}\left(D^{2}u,D^{2}e_{c}\right)_{T}-\varepsilon^{2}\left(D_{w}^{2}u_{h},D_{w}^{2}e_{c}\right)_{T}+\left(\nabla u,\nabla e_{c}\right)_{T}-\left(\nabla_{w}u_{h},\nabla_{w}e_{c}\right)_{T}\right)

\displaystyle+S_{1}(u_{h},Ie_{c})+S_{2}(u_{h},Ie_{c})\left.\right)

\displaystyle\left\|e_{c}-Ie_{c}\right\|_{T}\leq\varepsilon^{-1}h_{T}^{2}\varepsilon\left|e_{c}\right|_{2,T},

\displaystyle\left\|e_{c}-Ie_{c}\right\|_{T}\leq h_{T}\left|e_{c}\right|_{1,T}.

which together imply

\displaystyle\left\|e_{c}-Ie_{c}\right\|_{T}\leq\alpha_{T}\left\|e_{c}\right\|_{\varepsilon,T}.

(4.5)

Furthermore, on each edge

, applying the trace inequality and the inverse inequality yields

\displaystyle\left\|e_{c}-Ie_{c}\right\|_{e}\leq\left\|e_{c}-Ie_{c}\right\|_{T_{e}}^{1/2}\left|e_{c}-Ie_{c}\right|_{1,T_{e}}^{1/2}\leq\alpha_{e,1}\left\|e_{c}\right\|_{\varepsilon,T_{e}}.

(4.6)

Similarly,

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla(e_{c}-Ie_{c})\right\|_{e}\leq\left\|\nabla(e_{c}-Ie_{c})\right\|_{T_{e}}^{1/2}\left|\nabla(e_{c}-Ie_{c})\right|_{1,T_{e}}^{1/2}\leq\alpha_{e,2}\left\|e_{c}\right\|_{\varepsilon,T_{e}}.

(4.7)

where

and

are as defined previously.

For the first term of

, applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the interpolation estimate (

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\left(f-f_{h},e_{c}-Ie_{c}\right)_{T}\leq\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\left\|f-f_{h}\right\|_{T}\left\|e_{c}-Ie_{c}\right\|_{T}\leq\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\alpha_{T}\left\|f-f_{h}\right\|_{T}\left\|e_{c}\right\|_{\varepsilon,T}.

For the second term, again using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and (

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\left\|R_{T}\right\|_{T}\left\|e_{c}-Ie_{c}\right\|_{T}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\alpha_{T}\left\|R_{T}\right\|_{T}\left\|e_{c}\right\|_{\varepsilon,T}.

For the third term, applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the edge estimate (

\displaystyle\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}}\left\langle J_{e,1},e_{c}-Ie_{c}\right\rangle_{e}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}}\left\|J_{e,1}\right\|_{e}\left\|e_{c}-Ie_{c}\right\|_{e}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}}\alpha_{e,1}\left\|J_{e,1}\right\|_{e}\left\|e_{c}\right\|_{\varepsilon,T}

and

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{T}\left\|\nabla u_{0}-\mathbf{u}_{g}\right\|_{\partial T}\left(\left\|\nabla(Ie_{c}-Ie_{c}^{+})\right\|_{\partial T}+\left\|\nabla(Ie_{c}-Ie_{c}^{-})\right\|_{\partial T}\right)

Combining all the above estimates, we conclude that

\displaystyle l_{1}(u_{h})\leq

\displaystyle C\eta_{h}\|e_{c}\|_{\varepsilon}.

4.2
Lower bound

In this section, we establish the efficiency of the proposed a posteriori error estimator for guiding adaptive mesh refinement in the singularly perturbed problem. To derive the efficiency bounds, we employ bubble function techniques.

Let

denote the standard interior bubble function on a cell
T

\widehat{T}

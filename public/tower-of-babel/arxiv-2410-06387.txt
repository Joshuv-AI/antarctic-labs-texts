Shafarevich’s conjecture for families of hypersurfaces over function fields
Philip Engel, Alice Lin, Salim Tayou

Abstract
. Given a smooth quasi-projective complex algebraic variety 𝒮 \mathcal{S} , we prove that there are only finitely many Hodge-generic non-isotrivial families of smooth projective hypersurfaces over 𝒮 \mathcal{S} of degree d d in ℙ ℂ n + 1 \mathbb{P}_{{\mathbb{C}}}^{n+1} . We prove that the finiteness is uniform in 𝒮 \mathcal{S} and we give examples where the result is sharp. We also prove similar results for certain complete intersections in ℙ ℂ n + 1 \mathbb{P}_{{\mathbb{C}}}^{n+1} of higher codimension and more generally for algebraic varieties whose moduli space admits a period map that satisfies the infinitesimal Torelli theorem.

1.1.
Main contribution and earlier work

The approach of Parshin and Arakelov for
1.2
uses two ingredients: boundedness and rigidity.
Given
\mathcal{M}
a separated Deligne–Mumford moduli stack which is given as the fine moduli space of algebraic varieties, and
\mathcal{S}
a smooth quasi-projective variety, the space of maps
\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{M})
corresponds to families of algebraic varieties over
\mathcal{S}
that are parameterized by
\mathcal{M}
.

Thus, one would like to prove that the mapping space
\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{M})
is of finite type (boundedness) and zero-dimensional (rigidity).
Kovács and Lieblich have shown in
[
KL10
]
that boundedness holds when
\mathcal{M}
is a weakly bounded compactifiable Deligne–Mumford stack, see
Section

2.3
, and that rigidity holds if the stack satisfies an “infinitesimal rigidity” condition
[
KL10
, Theorems 1.7, 5.2]
.

The main observation of this paper is to recast the two problems in the framework of Hodge theory: the boundedness problem is solved using the recent resolution of the conjecture of Griffiths by Brunebarbe-Bakker-Tsimerman
[
BBT23
]
, and we give a sufficient condition for rigidity in Hodge-theoretic terms and in situations where infinitesimal Torelli holds, directly inspired from earlier results of Peters, see
[
Pet86
,
Pet90
]
.

Our main theorem is the following:

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be smooth quasiprojective algebraic variety and
\mathcal{M}
be a separated, finite type, smooth Deligne–Mumford stack which is a fine moduli space for polarized smooth projective algebraic varieties.
Assume that
{\mathcal{M}}
admits a period map that satisfies the infinitesimal Torelli theorem and such that the associated generic Mumford–Tate group is absolutely simple.

Then Shafarevich finiteness holds for Hodge generic non-isotrivial families over
\mathcal{S}
parameterized by
\mathcal{M}
.
Moreover, the number of such families is bounded uniformly when
\mathcal{S}
is the fiber of a smooth family
{\mathcal{C}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{T}}
.

Remark 1.9
.

Corollary 7.1 in
[
BBT23
]
shows that a complex analytic space
fibered in varieties parameterized by
\mathcal{M}
over a quasiprojective algebraic variety
is actually algebraic. Hence all the previous results hold true if we work
with
analytic families over an algebraic base
{\mathcal{S}}
.

1.2.
Strategy of the proof

As explained in
Section

1.1
, the proof of Theorem
1.4
has the same two ingredients:
we first prove that the space of maps
\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{M})
has finitely many connected components over
{\mathbb{C}}
. We first show that
{\mathcal{M}}
is weakly bounded and compactifiable using the generalized Arakelov inequality recently proved by Brunebarbe
[
Bru20
]
and the ampleness of the Griffiths line bundle on the coarse moduli space associated to
\mathcal{M}
. Then we invoke Kovács–Lieblich’s result to show that
\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{M})
is a finite type stack. As for the rigidity, we use a result of Peters to analyze deformation of period maps and relate that to deformation of maps to
\mathcal{M}
. Under the assumption on the generic Mumford–Tate group being absolutely simple, we prove that these maps are rigid and thus
\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{M})
is zero dimensional at those points.

Organization of the paper

In §

Corollary

1.6
(§

).

Hodge-theoretic background

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a smooth complex algebraic variety,
and let

variation of Hodge structure of weight
k

associated to the local system
{\mathbb{V}}_{\mathbb{Z}}

satisfies Griffiths’ transversality: for all

\nabla({\mathcal{F}}^{p})\subset{\mathcal{F}}^{p-1}\otimes\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{S}}\penalty\ .

For every point

be the Mumford–Tate group of the Hodge structure

which fixes all Hodge tensors of

in
\mathcal{S}
, it is isomorphic to a fixed group

, called the generic Mumford–Tate group of the variation.

If

Let

Arakelov inequality

In this section, we recall Arakelov’s inequality, recently established by Brunebarbe

Let

The Griffiths line bundle

L:=\bigotimes_{p\geq 0}\det{\mathcal{F}}^{p}.

Let

Proposition 2.3
.

Let

be a smooth quasi-projective curve and let

a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-polarized variation of Hodge structure over

. Then the following inequality holds:

\deg_{\overline{\mathcal{S}}}(\overline{L})\leq C({\mathbb{V}})\cdot\left(-\chi(\mathcal{S})\right),

where

2.3.2.
Deligne–Mumford stacks with immersive period maps

In this section, we make the following additional assumption.

Assumption 2.6

We assume that
{\mathcal{M}}
supports a polarized variation of Hodge structure

Remark 2.7

The previous assumption is restrictive on

of moduli spaces of algebraic varieties which do not admit immersive period maps and it is unknown in general what conditions guarantee that the Shafarevich conjecture holds for the algebraic varieties parameterized by these families. However, it will be satisfied in all the cases that we study in this paper.

Theorem 2.8
.

The stack

By

Let

Given an algebraic variety

3.1.
Rigidity of period maps

Let
{\mathbb{V}}
be
{\mathbb{Z}}
-PVHS over a smooth quasiprojective algebraic variety

over

has the structure of a finite dimensional analytic variety

6

6

with possibly infinitely many irreducible components
and its Zariski tangent space at

[

Proposition 3.1
.

The Zariski tangent space of
\mathrm{Hom}^{||}(\mathcal{S},X_{\Gamma})
at

is given by the
(-1,1)
part of the Hodge structure
\mathrm{End}(V_{{\mathbb{C}}})^{\pi_{1}(\mathcal{S},s)}
, where

is the fiber of
{\mathbb{V}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}
at a point

, and

is the

-local system giving rise to the period map

Remark 3.2

Since the monodromy group

\mathrm{End}(V)^{\pi_{1}(\mathcal{S})}=\mathrm{End}(V)^{\overline{\pi_{1}(\mathcal{S})}}\subseteq\mathrm{End}(V)^{H}\penalty\ .

Proposition 3.3
.

Keep the same notation as above. If

Proof.

By

4.1.
Discriminants and Proof of
Corollary

1.6

Let
{\mathcal{S}}
be a quasi-projective curve over
{\mathbb{C}}
. Fix
n,d\geq 1
, and let
{\mathcal{U}}_{n,d}
be the moduli space of smooth hypersurfaces of degree
d
in
{\mathbb{P}}^{n+1}
. A non-isotrivial family of such hypersurfaces over
{\mathcal{S}}
corresponds to a nonconstant map
f:{\mathcal{S}}\to{\mathcal{U}}_{n,d}
.
Recall that
{\mathcal{U}}_{n,d}=[({\mathbb{P}}^{N(n,d)}-D_{n,d})/\PGL_{n+2}]
, where
{\mathbb{P}}^{N(n,d)}
is the parameter space for all degree
d
homogeneous polynomials in
n+2
variables, and
D_{n,d}
is the locus of homogeneous polynomials corresponding to singular hypersurfaces (i.e. the discriminant locus).

Any choice of coordinates on
{\mathbb{P}}^{n+1}
determines a lift
g:{\mathcal{S}}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{N(n,d)}
of
f
. Any two lifts will differ by an
\PGL_{n+2}
-automorphism.

Fix a smooth compactification
{\mathcal{S}}\hookrightarrow\overline{{\mathcal{S}}}
. There is a unique way to extend
g
to
\overline{g}:\overline{{\mathcal{S}}}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{N(n,d)}
. By uniqueness of the extension, the extensions
\overline{g}_{1},\overline{g}_{2}
of any two choices of lifts
g_{1},g_{2}
differing by
\sigma\in\PGL_{n+2}
will also differ by
\sigma
.

Since the
\PGL_{n+2}
-action is invariant on
D_{n,d}
, the following defined quantity is independent of the choice of
g
or
\overline{g}
.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}
be a quasiprojective complex curve with smooth compactification
{\mathcal{S}}\hookrightarrow\overline{{\mathcal{S}}}
. For a family of smooth hypersurfaces given by the map
f:{\mathcal{S}}\to{\mathcal{U}}_{n,d}
, define the
discriminant
of this family as

\disc(f):=\deg_{\overline{{\mathcal{S}}}}(\overline{g}^{*}{\mathcal{O}}(D_{n,d})).

Remark 4.4
.

We can interpret the discriminant of the family
f:{\mathcal{S}}\to{\mathcal{U}}_{n,d}
as counting how many singular fibers there are in the induced family of hypersurfaces over
\overline{{\mathcal{S}}}
.

By Bézout’s theorem,

(1)

\displaystyle\disc(f)

\displaystyle=(\deg\overline{g}(\overline{{\mathcal{S}}}))(\deg D_{n,d})(\deg\overline{g})

where the first term on the right-hand side is the degree of the image of
\overline{g}
as a closed subvariety of
{\mathbb{P}}^{N(n,d)}
, the second term is the degree of
D_{n,d}
as a subvariety of
{\mathbb{P}}^{N(n,d)}
, and the third term is the degree of the finite morphism
\overline{g}
onto its image. Using this formulation of the discriminant, we can prove
Corollary

1.6
.

Proof of
Corollary

1.6
.

The degree of the discriminant locus
D_{n,d}
is classically known to be
(n+2)(d-1)^{n}
(see, e.g.
[

1

and dimension
n

,
Theorem

1.4
implies that there are only finitely many Hodge-generic families of smooth projective hypersurfaces of degree

and dimension

Conditions for Large-Sample Majorization of Pairs of Flat States in Terms of α \alpha - z z Relative Entropies
Frits Verhagen 1 , Marco Tomamichel 1,2 and Erkka Haapasalo 1

Abstract
. In this work, we offer the first operational interpretation of the α \alpha - z z relative entropies, which were introduced by Jakšić et al. [ 11 ] and Audenaert and Datta [ 2 ] , where the α \alpha and z z parameters are truly independent from each other. Namely, we show that these relative entropies appear in the conditions for large-sample or catalytic relative majorization of pairs of flat states and certain generalizations of them. Additionally, the optimal rate of converting one such pair into another may be formulated in terms of the α \alpha - z z relative entropies.



1.
Introduction

The framework of
relative quantum majorization
can be put in the following simple terms: We consider two systems with Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{H}_{\rm in}
and
\mathcal{H}_{\rm out}
and pairs of states (density operators)
(\rho,\sigma)
on
\mathcal{H}_{\rm in}
and
(\rho^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime})
on
\mathcal{H}_{\rm out}
. We want to know if there is a single quantum channel (a completely positive trace-preserving affine map)
\mathcal{C}
such that

(1)

\mathcal{C}(\rho)=\rho^{\prime},\qquad\mathcal{C}(\sigma)=\sigma^{\prime}.

This setting is encountered, e.g., in quantum thermodynamics where we ask if a state
\rho
can be transformed into
\rho^{\prime}
using a Gibbs-preserving map. In this case, we have
\sigma=\sigma^{\prime}=\gamma_{\beta}
, the Gibbs state in the inverse temperature
\beta
.

Instead of the above single-shot case, we are mainly concerned with
large-sample
and
catalytic
relative majorization. For the former, we ask if, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
sufficiently large, there exists a channel
\mathcal{C}_{n}
such that

(2)

\mathcal{C}_{n}\big(\rho^{\otimes n}\big)=(\rho^{\prime})^{\otimes n},\qquad\mathcal{C}_{n}\big(\sigma^{\otimes n}\big)=(\sigma^{\prime})^{\otimes n}.

For catalytic relative majorization, we ask if there exists a pair
(\tau,\omega)
of states (catalysts) and a channel
\mathcal{C}
such that

(3)

\mathcal{C}(\rho\otimes\tau)=\rho^{\prime}\otimes\tau,\qquad\mathcal{C}(\sigma\otimes\omega)=\sigma^{\prime}\otimes\omega.

If the catalyzing states
\tau
and
\omega
were mutually orthogonal, they could catalyze any transformations. This is why we always assume that a pair
(\tau,\omega)
of catalysts are not completely orthogonal (i.e., their fidelity
F(\tau,\omega)>0
). Large-sample relative majorization implies catalytic relative majorization
[
4
]
, but the converse is not typically true
[
6
]
. In addition to the exact scenario above, we will also be interested in
asymptotic
large-sample and catalytic majorization. In this case, we require that the target output states are only approximately reached by a channel, but up to arbitrary precision.

The aim of this work is to identify conditions in the form of inequalities

(4)

\mathbb{D}(\rho\|\sigma)>\mathbb{D}(\rho^{\prime}\|\sigma^{\prime})\text{ or }\mathbb{D}(\rho\|\sigma)\geq\mathbb{D}(\rho^{\prime}\|\sigma^{\prime}),

for certain real-valued maps
\mathbb{D}
on pairs of states, guaranteeing either exact or asymptotic, large-sample or catalytic, relative majorization. It will turn out that the strict inequality above is applicable to the exact scenario, and the non-strict inequality to the asymptotic one. The quantities
\mathbb{D}
should naturally be monotone under majorization, i.e., if
(\rho,\sigma)
majorizes
(\rho^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime})
in the sense of (
1
), then
\mathbb{D}(\rho\|\sigma)\geq\mathbb{D}(\rho^{\prime}\|\sigma^{\prime})
. In other words,
\mathbb{D}
should satisfy the
data-processing inequality
(DPI). Due to the large-sample condition, these quantities should also be
(tensor) additive
or
extensive
in the sense that

(5)

\mathbb{D}(\rho_{1}\otimes\rho_{2}\|\sigma_{1}\otimes\sigma_{2})=\mathbb{D}(\rho_{1}\|\sigma_{1})+\mathbb{D}(\rho_{2}\|\sigma_{2}).

Such quantities
\mathbb{D}
are often called
relative entropies
. Many quantum relative entropies have been presented in the literature. However, while all
classical
relative entropies have been identified
[
13
]
, finding all possible quantum relative entropies is an open problem. Indeed, the non-commutativity of quantum states results in many quantum generalizations of a single classical relative entropy, and finding a complete characterization of all monotone generalizations is currently still intractable.

Our partial solution to this problem is restricting the kinds of pairs of quantum states that we study. Recall that a state
\rho
is a
classical-quantum (cq) state
if there are probabilities
p_{i}
and states
\rho_{i}
such that

(6)

\rho=\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}\outerproduct{i}{i}\otimes\rho_{i}

with fixed orthonormal flags
\ket{i}
, and
n\geq 1
. We will specialize to pairs of such states where additionally all component states
\rho_{i}
are pure, i.e. pairs
(\rho,\sigma)
such that

(7)

\rho=\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}\outerproduct{i}{i}\otimes\outerproduct{\alpha_i}{\alpha_i},\quad\sigma=\sum_{i=1}^{n}q_{i}\outerproduct{i}{i}\otimes\outerproduct{\beta_i}{\beta_i},

with
\ket{\alpha_i},\ket{\beta_i}
normalized vectors. We say that
\rho
and
\sigma
of (
7
)
have some overlap
if there is
i
such that
\langle\alpha_{i}|\beta_{i}\rangle\neq 0
. We also say that
\rho
and
\sigma
are
non-parallel
if
|\innerproduct{\alpha_i}{\beta_i}|<1
for all
i
. Let us point out a particular subset of pairs
(\rho,\sigma)
like those in (
7
): Assume that
\rho=\tr[P]^{-1}P
and
\sigma=\tr[Q]^{-1}Q
where
P
and
Q
are projections. Due to Jordan’s Lemma, this pair
(\rho,\sigma)
has the desired cq-decompositions with pure component states. Naturally, weighted direct sums of these
flat states
can also be decomposed in the same manner. For technical reasons, we will exclusively consider pairs of cq-states with pure components as in (
7
) that have some overlap, and denote this collection of pairs by
\mathcal{F}
. Note that any
(\rho,\sigma)\in\mathcal{F}
as in (
7
) is essentially finite dimensional since
n<\infty
and the vectors
\ket{\alpha_i}
and
\ket{\beta_i}
span a 2-dimensional space for each
i
.

In the above restricted setting, we identify a set of relative entropies
\mathbb{D}
giving sufficient and (almost) necessary conditions as in (
4
) for exact or asymptotic, large-sample or catalytic majorization. These relative entropies turn out to be within the set of the
\alpha
-
z
relative entropies

D_{\alpha,z}
. The
\alpha
-
z
relative entropies for any pair of quantum states
(\rho,\sigma)
, introduced in
[
11
]
and
[
2
]
, are defined as

(8)

D_{\alpha,z}(\rho\|\sigma):=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log\Tr\left[\left(\sigma^{\frac{\alpha-1}{2z}}\rho^{\frac{\alpha}{z}}\sigma^{\frac{\alpha-1}{2z}}\right)^{z}\right],

as well as certain limits
\alpha\rightarrow 1
,
z\rightarrow 0
, of this expression, and with some restrictions on the supports of
\rho
,
\sigma
. The question of what the precise range of
(\alpha,z)
is where the
D_{\alpha,z}
satisfy DPI, has been investigated in a number of papers
[
2
,
9
,
3
]
and conclusively answered in
[
19
]
.

The relative entropies
\mathbb{D}
giving conditions of the form (
4
) for exact or asymptotic large-sample of catalytic majorization of flat states (and their generalizations) are essentially those
D_{\alpha,z}
with parameters
\alpha
and
z
such that

(9)

\alpha\in(0,1)\text{ and }z\geq\max\{\alpha,1-\alpha\},

together with certain extensions to
\alpha=0,1
and
z\rightarrow\infty
. Note that the
D_{\alpha,z}
with
\alpha>1
do not play a role here because they would diverge on the pairs of states we consider, since the component states are pure. To our knowledge, this is the first time an operational interpretation of the
\alpha
-
z
relative entropies, where
\alpha
and
z
are truly independent of each other, has been demonstrated. Let us note however that the special cases of the so-called ‘sandwiched’ quantum relative entropies
[
14
]
(see also a new generalization in
[
10
]
), where
\alpha=z
, and the Petz-type relative entropies
[
15
,
16
]
, where
z=1
, have their own fields of applications and operational interpretations.

For the setting of asymptotic catalytic majorization between pairs of states in
\mathcal{F}
, we show the following result, which gives necessary and sufficient conditions in terms of the
\alpha
-
z
relative entropies.

Theorem 1
.

Let
(\rho,\sigma)
,
(\rho^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime})
be two pairs of states in
\mathcal{F}
(e.g. flat states), where
\rho
and
\sigma
are non-parallel and
\rho^{\prime}
,
\sigma^{\prime}
do not commute. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

D_{\alpha,z}(\rho\|\sigma)\geq D_{\alpha,z}(\rho^{\prime}\|\sigma^{\prime})
when
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
z>\max\{\alpha,1-\alpha\}
.

(ii)

For each
\varepsilon>0
, there exist a state
\rho^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}
such that
F(\rho^{\prime}_{\varepsilon},\rho^{\prime})\geq 1-\varepsilon
, a quantum channel
\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}
, and a catalyzer pair
(\tau_{\varepsilon},\omega_{\varepsilon})\in\mathcal{F}
such that

(10)

\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\rho\otimes\tau)=\rho^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}\otimes\tau_{\varepsilon}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\sigma\otimes\omega_{\varepsilon})=\sigma^{\prime}\otimes\omega_{\varepsilon}.

This result has a counterpart (Theorem
3
), dealing with the asymptotic large-sample case, where we need to introduce an extra condition on
(\rho,\sigma)
. Additionally, we also show similar results giving sufficient conditions for
exact
large-sample and catalytic majorization, see Theorem
2
. These conditions are almost always necessary as well, in a sense that will be made more precise later.

Our results also yield the optimal rate of converting a pair
(\rho,\sigma)
of cq-states with pure components to another such pair
(\rho^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime})
: it is given by the infimum of the ratio
D_{\alpha,z}(\rho\|\sigma)/D_{\alpha,z}(\rho^{\prime}\|\sigma^{\prime})
over all
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
z>\max\{\alpha,1-\alpha\}
.

The techniques we use in proving our results come from the real-algebraic theory of preordered semirings developed in
[
7
,
8
]
. The theorems presented there, known as the
Vergleichsstellensätze
, allow us to identify the relative entropies involved in the conditions for majorization. We also build on previous work in
[
5
,
18
]
.

This paper is organized as follows: In Section
2
, we state the remaining main results that were outlined above. To prepare for the proof of our results, we introduce some notation and necessary mathematical tools in Section
3
. In Section
4
we apply the methodology introduced in Section
3
to our specific setting. We use the technical results of Section
3
in Section
5
to derive our main results on large-sample or catalytic, and exact or asymptotic, relative majorization, and the optimal conversion rate.

2.
The main results

We now present our remaining results. For a pair of states
(\rho,\sigma)\in\mathcal{F}
, and
\alpha\in[0,1]
,
z\geq\max\{\alpha,1-\alpha\}
, define

(11)

\hat{D}_{\alpha,z}(\rho\|\sigma):=-\frac{1}{z+1}\log\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}(p_{i})^{\alpha}(q_{i})^{1-\alpha}|\innerproduct{\alpha_i}{\beta_i}|^{2z}\right),

and

(12)

\hat{D}^{\mathbb{T}}(\rho\|\sigma):=-\log\left(\max_{i=1,\ldots,n}|\innerproduct{\alpha_i}{\beta_i}|^{2}\right).

When
\alpha\in(0,1)
, the
\hat{D}_{\alpha,z}
coincide with the
\alpha
-
z
relative entropies
D_{\alpha,z}
as in (
8
), when evaluated on pairs of states in
\mathcal{F}
, except for a different prefactor in front of the
\log
for technical reasons. Since the
D_{\alpha,z}
satisfy DPI for
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
z\geq\max\{\alpha,1-\alpha\}
, the same holds for
\hat{D}_{\alpha,z}
, where we use the continuity of
\hat{D}_{\alpha,z}
in
\alpha
to establish the DPI when
\alpha=0,1
.
\hat{D}^{\mathbb{T}}
is the point-wise limit
z\rightarrow\infty
of
\hat{D}_{\alpha,z}
, for any
\alpha\in[0,1]
as one may rather easily verify. As a consequence, also
\hat{D}^{\mathbb{T}}
satisfies DPI.

A result by Uhlmann
[
17
]
states that a pair of pure states
(\outerproduct{\alpha}{\alpha},\outerproduct{\beta}{\beta})
majorizes another pair of pure states
(\outerproduct{\alpha'}{\alpha'},\outerproduct{\beta'}{\beta'})
if and only if
|\innerproduct{\alpha}{\beta}|\leq|\innerproduct{\alpha'}{\beta'}|
. Note that in the expressions (
11
) and (
12
), the fidelity between pure states
|\innerproduct{\alpha_i}{\beta_i}|
also makes an appearance.

To state our remaining results, we introduce the following two additional properties for a pair of states
(\rho,\sigma)\in\mathcal{F}
:

(13)

|\innerproduct{\alpha_i}{\beta_i}|<1\quad\forall\;i\in\{1,\ldots,n\},

and

(14)

\exists\;i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\ \text{s.t.}\ \innerproduct{\alpha_i}{\beta_i}=0\ \text{and}\ \ket{\alpha_i},\ket{\beta_i}\neq 0.

Note that (
13
) means that
\rho
and
\sigma
are non-parallel.

The following Theorem gives sufficient and almost necessary conditions for exact large-sample and catalytic majorization. This result is established at the end of Section
4.3
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
(\rho,\sigma)
,
(\rho^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime})
be two pairs of states in
\mathcal{F}
, where additionally
(\rho,\sigma)
satisfies (
14
). If

(15)

\displaystyle\hat{D}_{\alpha,z}(\rho\|\sigma)>\hat{D}_{\alpha,z}(\rho^{\prime}\|\sigma^{\prime})\ \text{when}\ \alpha\in[0,1],\ z\geq\max\{\alpha,1-\alpha\},

(16)

\displaystyle\hat{D}^{\mathbb{T}}(\rho\|\sigma)>\hat{D}^{\mathbb{T}}(\rho^{\prime}\|\sigma^{\prime}),

then
(\rho,\sigma)
majorizes
(\rho^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime})
both in the large-sample setting and in the catalytic setting, with a pair of catalyst states in
\mathcal{F}
. For the catalytic result,
(\rho,\sigma)
does not need to satisfy (
14
).

Conversely, if
(\rho,\sigma)
majorizes
(\rho^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime})
in large samples or catalytically, then the above inequalities (
15
) and (
16
) hold non-strictly.

Theorem
1
on asymptotic catalytic majorization presented in the Introduction can be extended to the following result that also gives conditions for asymptotic large-sample majorization. The proof can be found in Section
5.2
.

Theorem 3
.

Let
(\rho,\sigma)
,
(\rho^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime})
be two pairs of states in
\mathcal{F}
, where
\rho
and
\sigma
satisfy (
13
) and
\rho^{\prime}
,
\sigma^{\prime}
do not commute. Consider the following statements:

(i)

\hat{D}_{\alpha,z}(\rho\|\sigma)\geq\hat{D}_{\alpha,z}(\rho^{\prime}\|\sigma^{\prime})
when
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
z>\max\{\alpha,1-\alpha\}
.

(ii)

For each
\varepsilon>0
, there exist a state
\rho^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}
such that
F(\rho^{\prime}_{\varepsilon},\rho^{\prime})\geq 1-\varepsilon
, and a quantum channel
\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon,n}
for
n
large enough such that
\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon,n}(\rho^{\otimes n})=(\rho^{\prime}_{\varepsilon})^{\otimes n}
and
\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon,n}(\sigma^{\otimes n})=(\sigma^{\prime})^{\otimes n}
.

(iii)

For each
\varepsilon>0
, there exist a state
\rho^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}
such that
F(\rho^{\prime}_{\varepsilon},\rho^{\prime})\geq 1-\varepsilon
, a quantum channel
\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}
, and a catalyzer pair
(\tau_{\varepsilon}
,
\omega_{\varepsilon})\in\mathcal{F}
such that

(17)

\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\rho\otimes\tau_{\varepsilon})=\rho^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}\otimes\tau_{\varepsilon}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\sigma\otimes\omega_{\varepsilon})=\sigma^{\prime}\otimes\omega_{\varepsilon}.

Statements (i) and (iii) are equivalent. If
(\rho,\sigma)
furthermore satisfies (
14
), then (i), (ii) and (iii) are equivalent.

It might be possible to relax the assumption (
14
) in Theorems
2
and
3
, but this requires the study of a slightly different semiring. We will address this issue later in the Discussion. Note that, for the results on large-sample majorization, Theorem
3
requires
(\rho,\sigma)
to satisfy both (
13
) and (
14
), while Theorem
2
only explicitly requires (
14
). However, (
13
) is automatically satisfied for
(\rho,\sigma)
in Theorem
2
since the strict inequality (
16
) implies (
13
).

We say that, given pairs
(\rho,\sigma)
and
(\rho^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime})
,
r\geq 0
is an
achievable conversion rate
if
r\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}m_{n}/n
where
(m_{n})_{n}
is a sequence of natural numbers such that
\big(\rho^{\otimes n},\sigma^{\otimes n}\big)
majorizes
\big((\rho^{\prime})^{\otimes m_{n}},(\sigma^{\prime})^{\otimes m_{n}}\big)
for large enough
n\in\mathbb{N}
. The
optimal conversion rate

r\big((\rho,\sigma)\to(\rho^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime})\big)
, namely the supremum of all the achievable conversion rates, is given by the following result, which is shown in Section
5.3
.

Theorem 4
.

For pairs
(\rho,\sigma)
and
(\rho^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime})
of states in
\mathcal{F}
, the optimal conversion rate from
(\rho,\sigma)
to
(\rho^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime})
is

\displaystyle r\big((\rho,\sigma)\to(\rho^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime})\big)

(18)

\displaystyle\quad=\inf\left\{\,\frac{D_{\alpha,z}(\rho\|\sigma)}{D_{\alpha,z}(\rho^{\prime}\|\sigma^{\prime})}\,\middle|\,\alpha\in(0,1),z>\max\{\alpha,1-\alpha\}\,\right\}

(19)

\displaystyle\quad=\min\left\{\,\frac{\hat{D}_{\alpha,z}(\rho\|\sigma)}{\hat{D}_{\alpha,z}(\rho^{\prime}\|\sigma^{\prime})}\,\middle|\,\alpha\in[0,1],z\geq\max\{\alpha,1-\alpha\}\,\right\}\cup\left\{\frac{\hat{D}^{\mathbb{T}}(\rho\|\sigma)}{\hat{D}^{\mathbb{T}}(\rho^{\prime}\|\sigma^{\prime})}\right\}.

The additional relative entropies
\hat{D}_{\alpha,z}
for
\alpha=0,1
and the limit point
\hat{D}^{\mathbb{T}}
make the set of relative entropies appearing in (
19
) compact in the topology of pointwise convergence
[
7
, Proposition 8.5]
. This is why in (
19
) we are able to take the minimum, instead of taking the infimum when using the original definition of the
\alpha
-
z
relative entropies
D_{\alpha,z}
in (
18
).

3.
Mathematical background

3.1.
The Vergleichsstellensätze

Throughout this work, we follow the convention
\mathbb{N}=\{1,2,\ldots\}
and denote
[n]:=\{1,\ldots,n\}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

To derive Theorems
2
and
4
, we need some mathematical machinery. This comes in a real-algebraic form and involves preordered semirings in particular. The following definitions and results are from
[
7
,
8
]
.

We only study commutative algebraic structures in this work. We say that a tuple
(S,+,\cdot,0,1,\preceq)
is a
preordered semiring
if
(S,+,0)
and
(S,1,\cdot)
are commutative semigroups where the multiplication distributes over the addition and
\preceq
is a preorder (a binary relation which is reflexive and transitive) satisfying

(20)

x\preceq y\ \Rightarrow\ \left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}x+a&\preceq&y+a,\\
xa&\preceq&ya,\end{array}\right.

for all
a\in S
. As above, we usually omit the multiplication dot between elements in the semiring. We denote by
\sim
the equivalence relation generated by
\preceq
, i.e.
x\sim y
if and only if there are
z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}\in S
such that

(21)

x\preceq z_{1}\succeq z_{2}\preceq\cdots\succeq z_{n}\preceq y.

The preordered semiring
S
is of
polynomial growth
if it has a
power universal

u\in S
, i.e.

(22)

x\preceq y\quad\Rightarrow\quad\exists k\in\mathbb{N}:\ y\preceq xu^{k}.

A preordered semiring
S
is a
preordered semidomain
if

(23)

\begin{array}[]{rcl}xy=0&\Rightarrow&x=0\ {\rm or}\ y=0,\\
0\preceq x\preceq 0&\Rightarrow&x=0.\end{array}

Moreover,
S
is
zerosumfree
if
x+y=0
implies
x=0=y
.

Given preordered semirings
S
and
T
, we say that a map
\Phi:S\to T
is a
monotone homomorphism
if

•

\Phi(x+y)=\Phi(x)+\Phi(y)
for all
x,y\in S
(additivity),

•

\Phi(xy)=\Phi(x)\Phi(y)
for all
x,y\in S
(multiplicativity),

•

x\preceq y

\Rightarrow

\Phi(x)\preceq\Phi(y)
(monotonicity), and

•

\Phi(0)=0
and
\Phi(1)=1
.

We say that such a monotone homomorphism is
degenerate
if
x\preceq y
implies
\Phi(x)=\Phi(y)
. Otherwise
\Phi
is
nondegenerate
. For our results we need monotone homomorphisms with values in special semirings. These are the following:

•

\mathbb{R}_{+}
: The half-line
[0,\infty)
equipped with the natural addition, multiplication, and total order.

•

\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\rm op}
: The same as above but with the reversed order. Together
\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\rm op}
are often called
temperate reals
.

•

\mathbb{T}\mathbb{R}_{+}
: The half-line
[0,\infty)
equipped with the natural multiplication, total order, and the tropical sum
x+y=\max\{x,y\}
.

•

\mathbb{T}\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\rm op}
: The same as above but with the reversed order. Together
\mathbb{T}\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
\mathbb{T}\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\rm op}
are often called
tropical reals
.

Suppose that
S
is a preordered semiring and that
\Phi:S\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is a monotone homomorphism. We say that an additive map
\Delta:S\to\mathbb{R}
is a
derivation at
\Phi
or a
\Phi
-derivation
if it satisfies the
Leibniz rule

(24)

\Delta(xy)=\Delta(x)\Phi(y)+\Phi(x)\Delta(y)

for all
x,y\in S
. We are mainly only interested in derivations at degenerate homomorphisms that are also monotone, i.e. satisfy

(25)

x\preceq y\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\Delta(x)\leq\Delta(y).

Several results collectively called the “Vergleichsstellensätze” have been derived in
[
7
]
. Of all of them, we will need the following version:

Theorem 5

(Based on Theorem 8.6 in
[
7
]
)
.

Let
S
be a zerosumfree preordered semidomain with a power universal element
u
. Assume that for some
d\in\mathbb{N}
there is a surjective homomorphism
\|\cdot\|:S\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{d}\cup\{(0,\ldots,0)\}
with trivial kernel and such that

(26)

a\succeq b\ \Rightarrow\ \|a\|=\|b\|\quad{\rm and}\quad\|a\|=\|b\|\ \Rightarrow\ a\sim b.

Denote the component homomorphisms of
\|\cdot\|
by
\|\cdot\|_{(j)}
,
j=1,\ldots,d
. Let
x,y\in S\setminus\{0\}
with
\|x\|=\|y\|
. If

(i)

for every
\mathbb{K}\in\{\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\rm op},\mathbb{T}\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathbb{T}\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\rm op}\}
and every nondegenerate monotone homomorphism
\Phi:S\to\mathbb{K}
with trivial kernel, we have
\Phi(x)>\Phi(y)
, and

(ii)

\Delta(x)>\Delta(y)
for every monotone
\|\cdot\|_{(j)}
-derivation
\Delta:S\to\mathbb{R}
with
\Delta(u)=1
for all component indices
j=1,\ldots,d
,

then

(a)

there exists a nonzero
a\in S
such that
ax\succeq ay
, and

(b)

if additionally
x
is power universal, then
x^{n}\succeq y^{n}
for all sufficiently large
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Conversely if either of these properties holds for any
n
or
a
, then the above inequalities hold non-strictly.

Large-sample ordering as in (a) in the above Theorem implies catalytic ordering as in (b), with a catalyst of the form
a=\sum_{\ell=0}^{n-1}x^{\ell}y^{n-1-\ell}
for a sufficiently large
n\in\mathbb{N}
. This was shown in
[
4
]
when
x,y
are probability vectors, but the proof extends directly to the more abstract setting considered here. The converse implication typically does not holds; Theorem 3 of
[
6
]
provides a recipe for deriving counter examples in a particular setting.

3.2.
Jordan’s Lemma

In our earlier classical results in
[
5
,
18
]
, we were aided by the fact that tuples of classical probability vectors can be jointly decomposed into single-entry tuples. This enabled us to utilize Theorem
5
in a simple manner directly in the classical case in finding the monotone homomorphisms and derivations since these maps can now be decomposed into simpler maps on smaller spaces. This is naturally impossible in the quantum case. However, in some settings we may proceed in a way similar to the classical case. Namely, Jordan’s Lemma
[
12
]
says that we may jointly decompose a pair of projections so that the components are of rank at most 1:

Lemma 6

(Jordan’s Lemma)
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a finite-dimensional linear space and
A,B
projections in
\mathcal{H}
(i.e. two operators on
\mathcal{H}
such that
A^{2}=A=A^{*},B^{2}=B=B^{*}
). Then
A,B
can be simultaneously block-diagonalized with blocks of dimension
1
or
2
. In the case of dimension
2
, the corresponding blocks in
A,B
have rank
1
. More precisely, there exists a decomposition
\mathcal{H}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{H}_{i}
, for some
n\geq 1
, where the
\mathcal{H}_{i}
are at most
2
dimensional linear spaces, such that

(27)

A=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}A_{i},\quad B=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}B_{i},

where the
A_{i}
,
B_{i}
are projections of rank at most
1
in
\mathcal{H}_{i}
.

Naturally also pairs of flat states may be similarly decomposed into at most rank-1 components to obtain a form like in (
27
), up to an overal normalization. This motivates us to construct a preordered semiring out of flat states where we can find all the monotone homomorphisms and derivations and derive Theorems
2
and
4
using Theorem
5
.

4.
Analyzing the semirings

The preceding machinery has been successfully used previously in problems of classical matrix majorization in
[
5
,
18
]
and we will need some results derived also therein. In particular, we will assume the reader is familiar with the algebraic notions introduced in section 2.4 in
[
18
]
.

4.1.
Defining the semirings

We consider the set
V
consisting of all pairs of self-adjoint matrices that are mutually block-diagonalizable into blocks of rank at most
1
. More precisely,
(A,B)\in V
when
A,B
are positive semi-definite operators on a Hilbert space

(28)

\mathcal{H}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{H}_{i},

where
\mathcal{H}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{H}_{n}
,
n\geq 1
, are Hilbert spaces (which may all vary), such that
A,B
admit a decomposition

(29)

A=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}\outerproduct{\alpha_i}{\alpha_i},\quad B=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}\outerproduct{\beta_i}{\beta_i},

where
\ket{\alpha_i},\ket{\beta_i}\in\mathcal{H}_{i}
are vectors (possibly equal to the zero-vector).

We define the set of
minimal restrictions
pairs
V_{\rm m.r.}\subsetneq V
to be those
(A,B)\in V
such that either
A
and
B
are both the
0
-operator in their associated Hilbert space, or
A
and
B
are at least partially overlapping in the sense that

(30)

\innerproduct{\alpha_i}{\beta_i}\neq 0\text{ for some }i\in[n].

Also, we define the set of
everywhere overlapping
pairs
V_{\rm e.o.}\subsetneq V_{\rm m.r.}
to be those
(A,B)\in V_{\rm m.r.}
such that, for any
i\in[n]
, we have either

(31)

\innerproduct{\alpha_i}{\beta_i}\neq 0

or

(32)

\ket{\alpha_i}=0=\ket{\beta_i}.

Note that any pair of flat states or pair of pure states (that are at least partially overlapping) is in
V
.

V
is closed under the
\boxplus
and
\boxtimes
operations, defined by

(33)

(A,B)\boxplus(C,D):=(A\oplus C,B\oplus D),\quad(A,B)\boxtimes(C,D):=(A\otimes C,B\otimes D),

as one easily sees. We say that
(A,B)\in V
with associated Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
majorizes
(C,D)\in V
with associated Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
and write
(A,B)\succeq(C,D)
if and only if there exists a quantum channel
\mathcal{C}:\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})
such that
\mathcal{C}(A)=C
and
\mathcal{C}(B)=D
.

We define the equivalence relation
\approx
as follows: Let
(A,B),(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})\in V
with associated Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{H}
, respectively
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
, and write
(A,B)\approx(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})
if and only if they are equal up to a permutation of the Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{H}_{i}
, adding/removing copies of the zero-row
(0\,0)
, or by transformations of the form

(34)

V_{i}\outerproduct{a_i}{a_i}V_{i}^{*}=W_{i}\outerproduct{a'_i}{a'_i}W_{i}^{*},\quad V_{i}\outerproduct{b_i}{b_i}V_{i}^{*}=W_{i}\outerproduct{b'_i}{b'_i}W_{i}^{*},

where the
V_{i},W_{i}
are isometries. For
(A,B)\in V
, we denote by
[(A,B)]
the
\approx
-equivalence class of
(A,B)
. Define
S_{\rm m.r.}:=V_{\rm m.r.}/\approx
. The operations
\boxplus
and
\boxtimes
induce well-defined operations on
S
that are now also commutative. Also, the preorder
\succeq
induces a preorder on
S
. In conclusion,
(S_{\rm m.r.},\boxplus,0:=[(0\,0)],\boxtimes,1:=[(1\,1)],\succeq)
is a preordered semiring. We may also define the preordered semiring based on
S_{\rm e.o.}=V_{\rm e.o.}/\!\approx
in the same way. In the sequel, we will often treat elements of
V_{\rm m.r.}
or
V_{\rm e.o.}
as elements of the semirings
S_{\rm m.r.}
or
S_{\rm e.o.}
, i.e., we usually ignore the
\approx
-equivalence classes. We will also treat functions (such as the monotone homomorphisms) on the semirings as functions on
V_{\rm m.r.}
or
V_{\rm e.o.}
; we simply assume that they are constant on each equivalence class.

In the rest of this paper, we will usually write a pair
(A,B)\in V
as follows:

(35)

A=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}\outerproduct{\alpha_i}{\alpha_i},\quad B=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}q_{i}\outerproduct{\beta_i}{\beta_i},

where the vectors
\ket{\alpha_i},\ket{\beta_i}
,
i=1,\ldots,n
, are assumed to be normalized, or equal to
0
, and
p_{i},q_{i}\geq 0
,
i=1,\ldots,n
. Note that when
(A,B)
is a pair of quantum states, meaning that
\Tr[A]=1=\Tr[B]
, then
p_{1}+\ldots+p_{n}=1=q_{1}+\ldots+q_{n}
, i.e. the
p_{i}
and the
q_{i}
are both finite probability distributions. One sees that such pairs are precisely those in
\mathcal{F}
, i.e. cq-states with pure components that have some overlap.

We introduce some notation that will make the connection with the previously studied classical semirings in
[
5
,
18
]
, whose elements are written as two-column matrices, more clear. A pair
(A,B)\in V
as in (
35
) can be written as a matrix

(36)

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}p_{1}\outerproduct{\alpha_1}{\alpha_1}&q_{1}\outerproduct{\beta_1}{\beta_1}\\
\vdots&\vdots\\
p_{n}\outerproduct{\alpha_n}{\alpha_n}&q_{n}\outerproduct{\beta_n}{\beta_n}\end{array}\right).

Also, if we have
|\innerproduct{\alpha_i}{\beta_i}|=1
for some
i
, then we may regard this as a “classical row” of the above matrix and we simply omit the corresponding
\outerproduct{\alpha_i}{\alpha_i}
and
\outerproduct{\beta_i}{\beta_i}
in the notation.

In
[
18
]
, Section 3, we discussed a semiring of
d
-tuples of vectors (
d\in\mathbb{N}
) with non-negative entries where the vectors needed to have some common support but they could also have partially disjoint supports. This semiring was there called the minimal restrictions semiring. Our current preordered semirings
S_{\rm m.r.}
and
S_{\rm e.o.}
both contain this semiring in the case
d=2
: it is the subset of those
(A,B)\in V_{\rm m.r.}
where
A
and
B
commute. This means that we know something of the monotone homomorphisms of these semirings already based on our earlier work, and we will use this knowledge in our analysis of
S_{\rm m.r.}
and
S_{\rm e.o.}
.

4.2.
Power universal elements

We will show which elements in the semirings
S_{\rm m.r.}
and
S_{\rm e.o.}
are power universals. Essentially they are precisely those with some quantum component. We first present a useful result due to Uhlmann
[
17
]
:

Proposition 7

(Uhlmann)
.

Let
\ket{\alpha_1},\ket{\beta_1}\in\mathcal{H}_{1}
and
\ket{\alpha_2},\ket{\beta_2}\in\mathcal{H}_{2}
be normalized vectors in some Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2}
. Then

(37)

(\,\outerproduct{\alpha_1}{\alpha_1},\outerproduct{\beta_1}{\beta_1}\,)\succeq(\,\outerproduct{\alpha_2}{\alpha_2},\outerproduct{\beta_2}{\beta_2}\,)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad|\innerproduct{\alpha_1}{\beta_1}|\leq|\innerproduct{\alpha_2}{\beta_2}|.

Using the preceding proposition, we may prove a characterization for power universals first in
S_{\rm e.o.}
.

Proposition 8
.

Let
(A,B)
be a pair of (normalized) quantum states of the form (
35
). Then
(A,B)
is a power universal of
S_{\rm e.o.}
if and only if
0<|\innerproduct{\alpha_i}{\beta_i}|<1
for
i=1,\ldots,n
.

Let us note that the above condition for the power universals of
S_{\rm e.o.}
essentially means that all the components of the power universals are, in a sense, purely quantum.

Proof.

Let us first consider a simpler case where we show that a pair
(\outerproduct{\alpha}{\alpha},\outerproduct{\beta}{\beta})
of pure states such that
0<|\innerproduct{\alpha}{\beta}|<1
is a power universal of
S_{\rm e.o.}
. To this end, let us fix an element
(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})\in V_{\rm e.o.}
such that
\tr[A^{\prime}]=1=\tr[B^{\prime}]
where

(38)

\displaystyle A^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{n^{\prime}}p^{\prime}_{j}\outerproduct{\alpha'_j}{\alpha'_j},

(39)

\displaystyle B^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{n^{\prime}}q^{\prime}_{j}\outerproduct{\beta'_j}{\beta'_j}.

Since we are within
V_{\rm e.o.}
, we may assume that
p^{\prime}_{j},q^{\prime}_{j}>0
for all
j=1,\ldots,n^{\prime}
. We next show that, for this generic (normalized) element
(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})
, we have

(40)

(\outerproduct{\alpha}{\alpha},\outerproduct{\beta}{\beta})^{\boxtimes m}\succeq(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})

for
m\in\mathbb{N}
sufficiently large.

Let us denote
F:=|\innerproduct{\alpha}{\beta}|^{2}
. Let
m_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
F^{m}p^{\prime}_{i}<q^{\prime}_{i}
for all
m\geq m_{0}
. Let
\ket{\alpha_m^\perp}
be a vector in the 2-dimensional Hilbert space
\mathcal{M}_{m}
spanned by
\ket{\alpha}^{\otimes m}
and
\ket{\beta}^{\otimes m}
which is perpendicular to
\ket{\alpha}^{\otimes m}
for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
. It follows that we may define, for all
m\geq m_{0}
, the positive-operator-valued measure
E^{m}=(E^{m}_{j})_{j=1}^{n^{\prime}}
on
\mathcal{M}_{m}
through

(41)

E^{m}_{i}=p^{\prime}_{i}\outerproduct{\alpha}{\alpha}^{\otimes m}+\frac{q^{\prime}_{i}-F^{m}p^{\prime}_{i}}{1-F^{m}}\outerproduct{\alpha_m^\perp}{\alpha_m^\perp}.

In what follows, we write, e.g.,
|\alpha^{\otimes m}\rangle:=|\alpha\rangle^{\otimes m}
. Through a straightforward calculation, we find that

(42)

\left\langle\alpha^{\otimes m}\middle|E^{m}_{i}\middle|\alpha^{\otimes m}\right\rangle=p^{\prime}_{i},\qquad\left\langle\beta^{\otimes m}\middle|E^{m}_{i}\middle|\beta^{\otimes m}\right\rangle=q^{\prime}_{i}.

Let us define the unit vectors
\big|\tilde{\beta}_{i}^{m}\big\rangle=(q^{\prime}_{i})^{-1/2}(E^{m}_{i})^{1/2}\ket{\beta}^{\otimes m}
. It is easily seen that

(43)

\big\langle\alpha^{\otimes m}\big|\tilde{\beta}_{i}^{m}\big\rangle=\sqrt{p^{\prime}_{i}}\innerproduct{\alpha}{\beta}^{m}\to 0

as
m\to\infty
. Thus, there is
m_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
such that, for any
m\geq m_{1}
and
i=1,\ldots,n^{\prime}
, there is a channel
\mathcal{C}^{m}_{i}
such that
\mathcal{C}^{m}_{i}(\outerproduct{\alpha}{\alpha})=\outerproduct{\alpha'_i}{\alpha'_i}
and
\mathcal{C}^{m}_{i}\big(\big|\tilde{\beta}_{i}^{m}\big\rangle\big\langle\tilde{\beta}_{i}^{m}\big|\big)=\outerproduct{\beta'_i}{\beta'_i}
. Thus, when
m\geq\max\{m_{0},m_{1}\}
, we may define the channel
\mathcal{C}_{m}
through

(44)

\mathcal{C}_{m}(\tau)=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n^{\prime}}\mathcal{C}_{i}^{m}\big((E^{m}_{i})^{1/2}\tau(E_{i}^{m})^{1/2}\big).

Whenever
m\geq\max\{m_{0},m_{1}\}
, it is seen through simple calculation that

(45)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{m}(\outerproduct{\alpha}{\alpha})

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n^{\prime}}p^{\prime}_{i}\outerproduct{\alpha'_i}{\alpha'_i}=A^{\prime},

(46)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{m}(\outerproduct{\beta}{\beta})

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n^{\prime}}q^{\prime}_{i}\outerproduct{\beta'_i}{\beta'_i}=B^{\prime}.

Let us now assume that the state pair
(A,B)
is as in (
35
) with
0<|\innerproduct{\alpha_i}{\beta_i}|<1
for
i=1,\ldots,n
. We are free to assume that
|\innerproduct{\alpha_1}{\beta_1}|^{2}
is the greatest of the fidelities
|\innerproduct{\alpha_i}{\beta_i}|^{2}
. According to Proposition
7
, we have channels
\mathcal{D}_{i}
such that
\mathcal{D}_{i}(\outerproduct{\alpha_i}{\alpha_i})=\outerproduct{\alpha_1}{\alpha_1}
and
\mathcal{D}_{i}(\outerproduct{\beta_i}{\beta_i})=\outerproduct{\beta_1}{\beta_1}
for
i=1,\ldots,n
; for
i=1
we may naturally choose the identity map
\mathcal{D}_{1}={\rm id}
. Let
P_{i}
be the projection of the Hilbert space onto the
i
’th component in (
35
) and define the channel
\mathcal{D}
,
\mathcal{D}(\tau)=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{D}_{i}(P_{i}\tau P_{i})
. It follows that
\mathcal{D}(A)=\outerproduct{\alpha_1}{\alpha_1}
and
\mathcal{D}(B)=\outerproduct{\beta_1}{\beta_1}
, so that
(A,B)
majorizes
(\outerproduct{\alpha_1}{\alpha_1},\outerproduct{\beta_1}{\beta_1})
. Since, by assumption,
0<|\innerproduct{\alpha_1}{\beta_1}|<1
, we know from what we have shown thus far that
(\outerproduct{\alpha_1}{\alpha_1},\outerproduct{\beta_1}{\beta_1})^{\boxtimes m}\succeq(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})
for any normalized element
(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})\in V_{\rm e.o.}
for sufficiently large
m\in\mathbb{N}
.
Thus, also for any normalized
(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})\in V_{\rm e.o.}
,

(47)

(A,B)^{\boxtimes m}\succeq(\outerproduct{\alpha_1}{\alpha_1},\outerproduct{\beta_1}{\beta_1})^{\boxtimes m}\succeq(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})

for
m\in\mathbb{N}
large enough

We are finally able to show that the above
(A,B)
is a power universal. For this, consider pairs
(A_{1},B_{1}),(A_{2},B_{2})\in V_{\rm e.o.}
such that
(A_{1},B_{1})\succeq(A_{2},B_{2})
or
(A_{2},B_{2})\succeq(A_{1},B_{1})
. This clearly means especially that
\tr[A_{1}]=\tr[A_{2}]
and
\tr[B_{1}]=\tr[B_{2}]
. Define
A^{\prime}:=\tr[A_{2}]^{-1}A_{2}
and
B^{\prime}:=\tr[B_{2}]^{-1}B_{2}
. Since we have already proven that, for
m\in\mathbb{N}
large enough, we have (
47
), we now have

(48)

\displaystyle(A_{1},B_{1})\boxtimes(A,B)^{\boxtimes m}

\displaystyle=(A_{1}\otimes A^{\otimes m},B_{1}\otimes B^{\otimes m})

(49)

\displaystyle\succeq\big(\tr[A_{1}]A^{\otimes m},\tr[B_{1}]B^{\otimes m}\big)

(50)

\displaystyle=\big(\tr[A_{2}]A^{\otimes m},\tr[B_{2}]B^{\otimes m}\big)

(51)

\displaystyle\succeq\big(\tr[A_{2}]A^{\prime},\tr[B_{2}]B^{\prime}\big)=(A_{2},B_{2})

for
m\in\mathbb{N}
large enough. This finally shows that the
(A,B)
of the claim is a power universal.
∎

Using the above result, we may easily characterize certain power universals of
S_{\rm m.r.}
. We only need a small orthogonal addition to the power universals of
S_{\rm e.o.}
resulting into the conditions of (
13
) and (
14
).

Proposition 9
.

A pair
(A,B)
of (normalized) quantum states of the form (
35
) satisfying (
13
) and (
14
) is a power universal of
S_{\rm m.r.}
.

Proof.

Let
(A,B)
be a pair as that stated in the claim. Possibly by reordering the blocks, we may assume that there is
\ell
,
1\leq\ell<n
, unit vectors
\ket{\alpha_i}
and
\ket{\beta_i}
such that
0<|\innerproduct{\alpha_i}{\beta_i}|<1
,
i=1,\ldots,\ell
, positive probabilities
p_{1},\ldots,p_{\ell}
and
q_{1},\ldots,q_{\ell}
with
P:=p_{1}+\cdots+p_{\ell}<1
and
Q:=q_{1}+\cdots+q_{\ell}<1
, and non-zero vectors
p,q\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n-\ell}
with
\|p\|_{1}+P=1=\|q\|_{1}+Q
such that

(52)

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{\ell}p_{i}\outerproduct{\alpha_i}{\alpha_i}\oplus p,

(53)

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{\ell}q_{i}\outerproduct{\beta_i}{\beta_i}\oplus q.

Due to (
14
), we have that

(54)

{\rm supp}\,q\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}\setminus{\rm supp}\,p,

i.e.,
p
and
q
are disjoint. Let
(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})
be a pair of states within
V_{\rm m.r.}
. Let us assume that this pair has the form of (
38
) and (
39
). Now, however, not all
p^{\prime}_{i}
and
q^{\prime}_{i}
need to be non-zero. However, there needs to be a block
i_{0}
such that
p^{\prime}_{i_{0}},q^{\prime}_{i_{0}}>0
and
\innerproduct{\alpha'_{i_0}}{\beta'_{i_0}}\neq 0
. For simplicity, we may assume that
i_{0}=1
.

It follows that, for each
m\in\mathbb{N}
, there are disjointly supported vectors
p_{m}
and
q_{m}
with non-negative entries such that

(55)

\displaystyle A^{\otimes m}

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}^{\ell}p_{i_{1}}\cdots p_{i_{m}}\outerproduct{\alpha_{i_1}}{\alpha_{i_1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\outerproduct{\alpha_{i_m}}{\alpha_{i_m}}\oplus p_{m},

(56)

\displaystyle B^{\otimes m}

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}^{\ell}p_{i_{1}}\cdots p_{i_{m}}\outerproduct{\alpha_{i_1}}{\alpha_{i_1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\outerproduct{\beta_{i_m}}{\beta_{i_m}}\oplus q_{m}.

This is due to the fact that, after the
\ell^{m}
first blocks, the remaining blocks in
(A,B)^{\boxtimes m}
are disjoint. We may assume that the block
i=1
has the highest fidelity among
|\innerproduct{\alpha_i}{\beta_i}|^{2}
for
i=1,\ldots,\ell
. Hence, there are channels
\mathcal{C}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}
such that

(57)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}\big(\outerproduct{\alpha_{i_1}}{\alpha_{i_1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\outerproduct{\alpha_{i_m}}{\alpha_{i_m}}\big)

\displaystyle=\outerproduct{\alpha_1}{\alpha_1}^{\otimes m}

(58)

\displaystyle C_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}\big(\outerproduct{\beta_{i_1}}{\beta_{i_1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\outerproduct{\beta_{i_m}}{\beta_{i_m}}\big)

\displaystyle=\outerproduct{\beta_1}{\beta_1}^{\otimes m}.

Summing together the first
\ell^{m}
blocks and then the remaining blocks, we have
(A,B)^{\boxtimes m}\succeq(A_{m},B_{m})
where

(59)

\displaystyle A_{m}

\displaystyle=P^{m}\outerproduct{\alpha_1}{\alpha_1}^{\otimes m}\oplus(1-P^{m})\oplus 0,

(60)

\displaystyle B_{m}

\displaystyle=Q^{m}\outerproduct{\beta_1}{\beta_1}^{\otimes m}\oplus 0\oplus(1-Q^{m}).

Let us now upper bound the target
(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})
. We may write
A^{\prime}=A_{1}\oplus A_{2}
and
B^{\prime}=B_{1}\oplus B_{2}
where
(A_{1},B_{1})\in V_{\rm e.o.}
and
A_{2}
and
B_{2}
are completely disjoint. Let
s:=\tr[A_{1}]
and
t:=\tr[B_{1}]
. We now claim that
(\tilde{A},\tilde{B})\succeq(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})
where

(61)

\displaystyle\tilde{A}

\displaystyle=s\outerproduct{\alpha}{\alpha}\oplus(1-s)\oplus 0,

(62)

\displaystyle\tilde{B}

\displaystyle=t\outerproduct{\beta}{\beta}\oplus 0\oplus(1-t)

where
\ket{\alpha}
and
\ket{\beta}
are some non-orthogonal unit vectors. Indeed, from the proof Proposition
8
, we know that (when
|\innerproduct{\alpha}{\beta}|
is sufficiently small)
(\outerproduct{\alpha}{\alpha},\outerproduct{\beta}{\beta})\succeq(\tr[A_{1}]^{-1}A_{1},\tr[B_{1}]^{-1}B_{1})
, and from the remaining orthogonal parts of
(\tilde{A},\tilde{B})
we can easily create
(A_{2},B_{2})
. We next show that there is
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
(A,B)^{\boxtimes m}\succeq(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})
by showing the intermediary
(A_{m},B_{m})\succeq(\tilde{A},\tilde{B})
for sufficiently large
m\in\mathbb{N}
.

Let us denote the 2-dimensional space spanned by
\ket{\alpha_1}^{\otimes m}
and
\ket{\beta_1}^{\otimes m}
by
\mathcal{M}_{m}
and the projection of
\mathcal{M}_{m}\oplus\mathbb{C}\oplus\mathbb{C}
onto
\mathcal{M}_{m}
by
R_{m}
. The projection orthogonal to this we denote by
R_{m}^{\perp}
. Using again Proposition
7
, we find that, for
m\in\mathbb{N}
large enough, there is a channel
\mathcal{D}_{m}
such that
\mathcal{D}_{m}(\outerproduct{\alpha_1}{\alpha_1}^{\otimes m})=\outerproduct{\alpha}{\alpha}
and
\mathcal{D}_{m}(\outerproduct{\beta_1}{\beta_1}^{\otimes m})=\outerproduct{\beta}{\beta}
. Let us assume that
m\in\mathbb{N}
is sufficiently large so that
P^{m}<s
and
Q^{m}<t
.We may now define the channel
\mathcal{E}_{m}
on the two latter blocks of
(A_{m},B_{m})
(technically on a qubit) through

(63)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{m}(1\oplus 0)

\displaystyle=\frac{s-P^{m}}{1-P^{m}}\outerproduct{\alpha}{\alpha}\oplus\frac{1-s}{1-P^{m}}\oplus 0,

(64)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{m}(0\oplus 1)

\displaystyle=\frac{t-Q^{m}}{1-Q^{m}}\outerproduct{\alpha}{\alpha}\oplus 0\oplus\frac{1-t}{1-Q^{m}}.

Define the channel
\mathcal{C}_{m}
through

(65)

\mathcal{C}_{m}(\tau)=\mathcal{D}_{m}(R_{m}\tau R_{m})+\mathcal{E}_{m}(R_{m}^{\perp}\tau R_{M}^{\perp}).

It now follows from a straightforward calculation that
\mathcal{C}_{m}(A_{m})=\tilde{A}
and
\mathcal{C}_{m}(B_{m})=\tilde{B}
. Thus, we may deduce that
(A,B)^{\boxtimes m}\succeq(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})
for
m\in\mathbb{N}
large enough. The fact that
(A,B)
is a power universal follows now through the same simple logic as in the end of the proof of Proposition
8

∎

4.3.
Finding the monotone homomorphisms and derivations

We fix a monotone homomorphism

(\outerproduct{\alpha_1}{\alpha_1},\outerproduct{\beta_1}{\beta_1})\boxtimes(\outerproduct{\alpha_2}{\alpha_2},\outerproduct{\beta_2}{\beta_2})=(\ket{\alpha_1}\otimes\ket{\alpha_2}\bra{\alpha_1}\otimes\bra{\alpha_2},\ket{\beta_1}\otimes\ket{\beta_2}\bra{\beta_1}\otimes\bra{\beta_2})

Thus, by multiplicativity of

5.3.
Optimal Conversion Rate

Recall that, given pairs
(\rho,\sigma)
and
(\rho^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime})
,
r\geq 0
is called an achievable conversion rate if
r\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}m_{n}/n
where
(m_{n})_{n}
is a sequence of natural numbers such that
\big(\rho^{\otimes n},\sigma^{\otimes n}\big)\succeq\big((\rho^{\prime})^{\otimes m_{n}},(\sigma^{\prime})^{\otimes m_{n}}\big)
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
large enough. The optimal conversion rate
r\big((\rho,\sigma)\to(\rho^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime})\big)
is defined as the supremum of all the achievable conversion rates. We now give a proof for Theorem
4
, which gives an expression for the optimal conversion rate in terms of the ratio of relative entropies, analogous to the proof of Corollary 29 in
[
18
]
.

Proof of Theorem
4
.

Define

(104)

\mathcal{D}:=\{\,\hat{D}_{\alpha,z}\,|\,\alpha\in[0,1],z\geq\max\{\alpha,1-\alpha\}\,\}\cup\{\hat{D}^{\mathbb{T}}\}.

We prove that the optimal conversion rate is given by (
19
) by first showing that any achievable conversion rate is upper bounded by
D(\rho\|\sigma)/D(\rho^{\prime}\|\sigma^{\prime})
for any relative entropy
D
(i.e., any tensor additive monotone map on state pairs, not only for
D\in\mathcal{D}
). This proof is completely standard and does not use the earlier results. This shows that

(105)

r((\rho,\sigma)\to(\rho^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime}))\leq\frac{D(\rho\|\sigma)}{D(\rho^{\prime}\|\sigma^{\prime})},\qquad{\rm for\ any\ relative\ entropy}\ D.

Then we show that

(106)

r((\rho,\sigma)\to(\rho^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime}))\geq\min_{D\in\mathcal{D}}\frac{D(\rho\|\sigma)}{D(\rho^{\prime}\|\sigma^{\prime})}

with a simple application of Theorem
5
.

Suppose that
r\geq 0
is
((\rho,\sigma),(\rho^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime}))
-achievable, i.e., there is a sequence
(m_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}
such that
r\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}m_{n}/n
and
\big(\rho^{\otimes n},\sigma^{\otimes n}\big)\succeq\big((\rho^{\prime})^{\otimes m_{n}},(\sigma^{\prime})^{\otimes m_{n}}\big)
for any
n\geq n_{0}
where
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
is some sufficiently large number. We fix, for now, a general relative entropy
D:S_{\rm m.r.}\setminus\{0\}\to\mathbb{R}
, i.e,

(107)

D(\rho\otimes\rho^{\prime}\|\sigma\otimes\sigma^{\prime})=D(\rho\|\sigma)+D(\rho^{\prime}\|\sigma^{\prime})

for all
(\rho,\sigma),(\rho^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime})\in S_{\rm m.r.}
and
(\rho,\sigma)\succeq(\rho^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime})

\Rightarrow

D(\rho\|\sigma)\geq D(\rho^{\prime}\|\sigma^{\prime})
. From the additivity and monotonicity it immediately follows that
m_{n}/n\leq D(\rho\|\sigma)/D(\rho^{\prime}\|\sigma^{\prime})
for all
n\geq n_{0}
. From the definition of
((\rho,\sigma),(\rho^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime}))
-achievability it follows that, for all
\varepsilon>0
, there is
n_{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{N}
which we can choose to be larger than
n_{0}
such that
r-\varepsilon\leq\inf_{n\geq n_{\varepsilon}}m_{n}/n
. But as
n_{\varepsilon}\geq n_{0}
, this means that
r-\varepsilon\leq D(\rho\|\sigma)/D(\rho^{\prime}\|\sigma^{\prime})
. As this holds for all
\varepsilon>0
, we have
r\leq D(\rho\|\sigma)/D(\rho^{\prime}\|\sigma^{\prime})
. Taking the supremum over achievable conversion rates and then the minimum over
D\in\mathcal{D}
, we get (
105
).

Let us then assume that
r=p/q<\min_{D\in\mathcal{D}}D(\rho\|\sigma)/D(\rho^{\prime}\|\sigma^{\prime})
with
p,q\in\mathbb{N}
. We now have, for all
D\in\mathcal{D}
,

(108)

\displaystyle D(\rho^{\otimes q}\|\sigma^{\otimes q})

\displaystyle=qD(\rho\|\sigma)=\frac{p}{r}D(\rho\|\sigma)>pD(\rho\|\sigma)\max_{D^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}}\frac{D^{\prime}(\rho^{\prime}\|\sigma^{\prime})}{D^{\prime}(\rho\|\sigma)}

(109)

\displaystyle\geq pD(\rho^{\prime}\|\sigma^{\prime})=D(\rho^{\prime\otimes p}\|\sigma^{\prime\otimes p}).

Thus, according to (the reformulation of) Theorem
5
, we know that
(\rho,\sigma)^{\boxtimes qn}\succeq(\rho^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime})^{\boxtimes pn}
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
large enough. This means that
r=p/q
is
((\rho,\sigma),(\rho^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime}))
-achievable, so that
r\leq r((\rho,\sigma)\to(\rho^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime}))
. Since this holds for any rational
r<\min_{D\in\mathcal{D}}D(\rho\|\sigma)/D(\rho^{\prime}\|\sigma^{\prime})
, we finally have (
106
). Combining our observations, we arrive at (
19

∎

6.
Discussion

Recall that Proposition
7
gives necessary and sufficient for one-shot majorization between two pairs of pure states. There is a generalization for one-shot majorization between tuples of
d\geq 3
pure states, also due to Uhlmann
[
17
]
. Namely, consider two tuples of
d
pure states
A:=(\outerproduct{\alpha_1}{\alpha_1},\ldots,\outerproduct{\alpha_d}{\alpha_d})
and
B:=(\outerproduct{\beta_1}{\beta_1},\ldots,\outerproduct{\beta_d}{\beta_d})
. The Gramm matrix of
A
is defined as
(G(A))_{i,j}:=\innerproduct{\alpha_i}{\alpha_j}
, and similarly for
B
. Then,
A
majorizes
B
if and only if there exists a positive semi-definite matrix
M
such that
G(A)\circ M=G(B)
, where
\circ
is the Hadamard product (i.e. entry-wise multiplication).

This result leads us to attempt to generalize this paper’s findings on large-sample and catalytic majorization of pairs as in (
7
), to tuples of
d\geq 3
states of this form. Namely, we can consider tuples
(\rho^{(1)},\ldots,\rho^{(d)})
, with
d\geq 3
, where

(110)

\rho^{(k)}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}^{(k)}\outerproduct{i}{i}\otimes\big|\alpha_{i}^{(k)}\big\rangle\big\langle\alpha_{i}^{(k)}\big|

are cq-states with pure components. The case of
d
-tuples of finite probability distributions was studied in
[
5
,
18
]
. The multivariate relative entropies involved there are generalizations of the bipartite Rényi relative entropies. One could try to use these results, in combination with Uhlmann’s result on
d
-tuples of pure states stated above, to derive multivariate quantum relative entropies that give conditions for large-sample and catalytic majorization of
d
-tuples such as (
110
). In this setting, the problem of identifying all monotone homomorphisms reduces to analyzing the values they take on tuples of
d
pure states. In the
d=2
case we have shown these to be
z
-th powers of the fidelity between the two pure states (see Proposition
10
). Our preliminary results suggest that in the general
d
case these will be products of the fidelities between the states in each of the
d(d-1)/2
pairs of pure states in the tuple, raised to a power that can be different for each pair.

It seems to be possible to relax the condition (
14
) required for large-sample majorization, both in the exact and asymptotic setting. This requires a deeper study of the semiring
S_{\rm e.o.}
, which consists precisely of those pairs in
S_{\rm m.r.}
that do not satisfy (
14
). In Section
4.3
we found that the monotone homomorphisms associated to
S_{\rm e.o.}
are those associated to
S_{\rm m.r.}
as well as possibly additional ones for
\alpha=0,1
and
z<1
, their pointwise limit
z\rightarrow-\infty
, and potentially also associated derivations. Currently we are unsure whether these extra homomorphisms are monotone or not. However, the
\alpha
-
z
relative entropies for
\alpha=0,1
,
z<1
have been shown to violate DPI for general pairs of states. The counterexamples found in the literature do not involve pairs of states of the form (
7
) that we study. Hence, the possibility remains that these homomorphisms are in fact monotone within our restricted setting.

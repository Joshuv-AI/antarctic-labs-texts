Enhanced radiation hardness of a copper iodine cluster framework scintillator using a bridging macrocyclic ligand with aggregation-induced emission properties
Authors: Li-Qiang Yue, Xiao-Man Xu, Meng-Han Fu, Yuan-Yuan Li, Ruo-Yu Cao, Qing-He Pan, You-Song Hu, Ruo-Yu Fang, Kai Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAs an emerging class of inorganic-organic hybrid scintillators, copper iodide clusters have garnered significant research interest owing to their numerous advantages, including structural diversity, high X-ray absorption capacity, superior X-ray excited luminescence (XEL) performance, and low toxicity. However, their practical applications are hindered by insufficient stability, particularly low radiation hardness. Constructing a framework material by connecting copper iodide clusters with bridging ligand has been demonstrated as an effective approach to enhance the stability, while improving the radiation hardness of the copper iodide cluster frameworks remains a challenge. In this study, a non-conjugated bridging macrocyclic AIE ligand was introduced to the copper iodide cluster framework scintillator. The obtained framework exhibits good X-ray excited luminescence and successfully used for high-performance X-ray imaging. The non-conjugated bridging structure of ligand endows the copper iodine cluster framework with excellent radiation hardness, providing a new strategy for the construction of high-performance scintillator.Subject terms: Coordination chemistry, Materials for optics

Introduction

X-ray imaging technology has been increasingly applied across a wide range of fields, such as medical diagnostics, industrial non-destructive testing, and resource exploration, thereby attracting significant attention from researchers1–5. A high-performance scintillator serves as a critical material in X-ray imaging technology6–9. As an emerging class of inorganic-organic hybrid scintillators, copper iodide clusters have garnered significant research interest owing to their numerous advantages, including structural diversity, high X-ray absorption capacity, superior X-ray excited luminescence (XEL) performance, and low toxicity10–14. However, their practical applications are hindered by insufficient stability, particularly low radiation hardness15,16. Constructing a framework material by connecting copper iodide clusters with a bridging ligand has been demonstrated as an effective approach to enhance the stability, while improving the radiation hardness of the copper iodide cluster frameworks remains a challenge17–19. As is well recognized, large-conjugated structures with abundant π-electrons exhibit a tendency to undergo chemical transformation under excited states, whereas non-conjugated aliphatic moieties demonstrate relatively higher stability20–22. Thus, in this study, a non-conjugated bridging macrocyclic aggregation-induced emission (AIE) ligand was incorporated into the design of the copper iodide cluster framework scintillator, thereby endowing it with enhanced radiation hardness.

Oxacalix[2]arene[2]pyrazine (OAP) is a kind of newly reported macrocyclic AIEgens that exhibits typical AIE characteristics through a restriction of intramolecular vibration (RIV) process (Fig. 1a)23–25. OAP AIEgens serve as ideal ligands for the construction of luminescent metal-organic frameworks (MOFs) owing to their enhanced emission in the aggregated state and the presence of intrinsic coordination sites of pyrazine groups. In this study, a new OAP macrocyclic AIEgen of oxacalix[2]spirobiindan[2]pyrazine (OSP) was designed and synthesized, which has two twisted spirobiindan moieties connecting two pyrazine groups (Fig. 1b). Based on OSP, a copper iodide cluster framework of Cu4I4-OSP was successfully prepared, which not only exhibits intense luminescence under UV light irradiation but also intense XEL. More importantly, the non-conjugated bridging ligand of OSP endows Cu4I4-OSP with excellent radiation hardness, making it suitable for high-performance X-ray imaging. For comparison, a Cu2I2-ONP, which features a structurally similar (Cu4I4-OSP) but conjugated bridging ligand oxacalix[2]arene[2]triazine (ONP), was also prepared (Fig. S1 and Fig. S2).

OSP can be readily synthesized through a one-step, catalyst-free reaction, as illustrated in Fig. 1b. The molecular structure of OSP was characterized by nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) and high-resolution mass spectrometry (HRMS), as shown in Figs. S3–S4. Single-crystal X-ray diffraction analysis indicates that OSP exhibits a centrosymmetric molecular structure, with two pyrazine moieties oriented in opposite directions, making it a suitable bridging ligand for constructing framework materials (Fig. S5). As a kind of OAP macrocyclic molecule, OSP exhibits typical AIE properties. As shown in Fig. 1c, OSP exhibits barely any emission in pure DMSO. With the increase of water fraction (fw), the emission intensity of OSP dramatically increased, and the αAIE value is calculated to be 17.5 (αAIE = I/I0, I0 is the emission intensity in DMSO, I is the emission intensity in the H2O/DMSO mixture with fw = 99%) (Fig. 1d)26. Besides, a level-off tail in the visible region of the absorption spectrum is clearly observed in the water-dominated solution (Fig. 1e), indicating the formation of aggregate suspensions27,28. Dynamic light scattering (DLS) measurement suggests that OSP in the water-dominated solution has a particle size around 260 nm (Fig. 1f). Analogous with the reported OAP AIEgens, the AIE property of OSP originates from a RIV process, which can be confirmed by the emission change in solvents of different viscosities29. As illustrated in Fig. 1g, h, the luminescence intensity of OSP increases gradually with the increasing glycerol fraction (fg) in the mixed solvents (glycerol/DMSO), demonstrating the intramolecular vibrations of OSP are restricted by the high solvent viscosity.

Results and discussion

Using OSP as bridging ligand, CuI as metal source, a copper iodide cluster framework of Cu4I4-OSP was prepared (Fig. 2a). Single-crystal X-ray diffraction (SCXRD) analysis unambiguously confirmed the crystal structure of Cu4I4-OSP, which crystallizes in the tetragonal system with space group P-4n2. The framework architecture features a three-dimensional network constructed from Cu4I4 secondary building units (SBUs) interconnected by OSP ligands. Within this architecture, each Cu4I4 cluster acts as a tetratopic connector. The four peripheral Cu atoms each coordinate to a pyrazinyl nitrogen atom from one side of an OSP ligand, forming Cu-N bonds. Simultaneously, the pyrazinyl nitrogen atoms on the opposite side of the same OSP ligand coordinate to adjacent Cu4I4 clusters. This bridging connectivity pattern, in which OSP ligands link the copper(I) iodide clusters, extends in three dimensions to generate the overall 3D network structure of the MOF material (Fig. 2b and Fig. S6). Beneficial for the AIE property of OSP and inherent Cu4I4 luminescence center, Cu4I4-OSP shows an intense cyan luminescence with a quantum efficiency (QY) as high as 17% (Fig. S7). Meanwhile, the heavy atom composition of Cu4I4-OSP endows it with good X-ray absorption performance. As shown in Fig. 2c, the X-ray absorption capacity of Cu4I4-OSP was calculated using the XCOM web database30. Compared with that of the ligand of OSP, the X-ray absorption intensity of Cu4I4-OSP is much higher in the whole energy region and higher absorption than Bi4Ge3O12 (BGO) in the lower energy region. As shown in Fig. S8, the attenuation efficiencies of Cu4I4-OSP and BGO were calculated to be 26.8% and 99.1% at a thickness of 0.5 mm, respectively. A comparative analysis with a BGO reference standard, using the integrated area ratio of the emission peaks, yielded a light yield of 20382 photons MeV-1 for Cu4I4-OSP (Fig. S9).

The strong photoluminescence and favorable X-ray absorption properties of Cu4I4-OSP confer its potential for X-ray excited luminescence (XEL). A bright cyan emission can be observed from Cu4I4-OSP under X-ray irradiation, and the corresponding XEL spectra are presented in Fig. 2d. (Dose rate range of 4.5–763.2 μGy s-1, Table S1). Furthermore, a strong linear correlation exists between the XEL intensity of Cu4I4-OSP and the X-ray dose rate. The minimum detection limit, calculated using the 3σ method, is 2.17 μGy s-1 (Fig. 2e), which is below the minimum X-ray dose rate required for medical X-ray imaging (5.5 μGy s-1)31.

More importantly, the stability including thermostability, water-oxygen stability and radiation hardness of Cu4I4-OSP, is extremely outstanding. First, thermogravimetric analysis data shows that there is no weight loss of Cu4I4-OSP below 350 °C (Fig. 3a). Second, the powder X-ray diffraction (PXRD) patterns of Cu4I4-OSP were recorded after the sample was exposed to air or immersed in water for three months. The PXRD patterns are consistent with those of the as-prepared Cu4I4-OSP (Fig. 3b). Furthermore, static water contact angle measurements were performed on the samples. The results show that the sample exhibits a hydrophobic surface with a water contact angle larger than 90° (Fig. S10), which is in good agreement with the excellent stability observed in humid environments in Fig. 3b.

Meanwhile, no emission decrease was observed for the sample after being exposed to air (Fig. 3c), indicating the excellent water-oxygen stability. Third, Cu4I4-OSP was exposed to a high X-ray dose (45.79 mGy min-1) to study its radiation hardness. As illustrated in Fig. 3d, e, the XEL intensity remains stable even after exposure to a high-dose-rate X-ray for 300 cycles or continuous irradiation for 1 h (total dose of 2747.4 mGy), demonstrating its excellent radiation stability.

The excellent stability of Cu4I4-OSP might be attibuted to its rigid framework structure and non-conjugated bridging ligand. To verify this hypothesis, control compound Cu2I2-ONP was prepared according to the reported methods (Fig. 3f)23. Cu2I2-ONP has a framework structure but connected with a conjugated bridging ligand. As shown in Fig. 3e, under the same X-ray irradiation condition, the XEL intensity of Cu2I2-ONP decreased to 94% compared with its original value. These results indicate that the non-conjugated bridging ligand is beneficial for the high radiation hardness of the copper iodide cluster framework.

Methods

A scintillator screen of Cu4I4-OSP was further prepared using a reported method32. In order to verify that the deposition layer retains the crystal structure of MOF, we performed PXRD tests on the thin film samples (Fig. S11). The obtained diffraction peak position is completely matched with the XRD pattern simulated by the MOF crystal structure. In addition, we also measured the emission spectrum of the scintillation screen, and the results show that the emission spectrum is consistent with the emission spectrum of Cu4I4-OSP (Fig. S12). As shown in Fig. S13, Cu4I4-OSP scintillator screen maintains its XEL intensity well after being continuously exposed to high dose X-ray (45.79 mGy min-1) for 60 min, demonstrating excellent scintillation hardness. Furthermore, the XEL intensity of Cu4I4-OSP scintillator screen remained virtually unchanged over a 60-day period.

The equipments for X-ray imaging are shown in Fig. 4a, which contain commercial camera, radiation detector, scintillator screen, sample and X-ray source. The spatial resolution of the scintillator screen was evaluated using a standardized line pair card (Fig. 4b). As illustrated in Fig. 4c, the X-ray imaging of the standardized line pair card demonstrates that the Cu4I4-OSP scintillator screen reaches a minimum resolution of 20 lp/mm. Moreover, the spatial resolution of Cu4I4-OSP scintillator screen was further calculated by the modulation transfer function (MTF) curve via the slanted-edge method33,34. As shown in Fig. 4d, a spatial resolution of 29.8 lp/mm was obtained at MTF = 0.2, which is outstanding compared to the reported cluster scintillators (Table S2), suggesting an excellent imaging property. X-ray imaging of actual samples was performed. Under X-ray irradiation, the internal structures of a capsule with an embedded spring, an SD card, and a microchip were clearly visualized on the scintillator screen (Fig. 4e, f).

X-ray imaging applications of Cu4I4-OSP.

a Photo of the X-ray imaging device. b X-ray image of the standardized X-ray line pair card with Cu4I4-OSP scintillator screen. c The X-ray line pair card profile curve of Cu4I4-OSP scintillator screen plotted via ImageJ software. d MTF curve of Cu4I4-OSP scintillator screen. e–g X-ray imaging of a capsule with an embedded spring, an SD card, and a microchip with a dense built-in circuitry using Cu4I4-OSP scintillator screen.

In conclusion, a new kind of oxacalix[2]arene[2]pyrazine AIEgen of OSP was prepared, which was used in the construction of a copper iodine cluster framework scintillator. The non-conjugated bridging structure of ligand endows the copper iodine cluster framework with excellent radiation hardness, providing a new strategy for the construction of high-performance scintillator.

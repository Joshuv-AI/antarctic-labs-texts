Abstract
. A classification of proper compactifications of topological groups is presented from the point of view of the possibility of continuous extensions of group operations. Ellis’ method allows one to obtain all right topological semigroup compactifications of a group on which its left action on itself (by multiplication on the left) continuously extends from its equivariant compactifications. The presentation of group elements as graphs of maps in the hyperspace with the Vietoris topology allows one to obtain compactifications on which the involution and the left action of a group on itself extend from its equivariant compactifications. Keywords: topological group, compactification, semigroup, involution, enveloping Ellis semigroup, binary relation, hyperspace AMS classification: Primary 22F50, 54D35 Secondary 54H15, 54E15

Introduction

On a topological group
G
there are algebraic structures:

(continuous) binary operation —
multiplication

\cdot:G\times G\to G
,

(continuous) unary operation —
involution
(a self-inverse map
*:G\to G
such that
*(g)=g^{-1}
and
*(gh)=*(h)*(g)
, element
g^{-1}
is an
inverse
of
g
),

multiplication on the left specifies (continuous)
left action

\alpha:G\times G\to G,\ \alpha(g,h)=gh.

multiplication on the left and involution specifies (continuous)
”right” action

\alpha^{*}:G\times G\to G,\ \alpha^{*}(g,h)=hg^{-1}.

It was A. Weil who showed that a topological group
G
is (locally) precompact in the right uniformity iff some (locally) compact group
\bar{G}
contains
G
as a dense topological subgroup (
\bar{G}
is the Weil completion of
G
). Having a system of (continuous) homomorphisms to compact groups separating the elements of
G
, Bohr (non-proper) compactification of a topological group can be obtained (topological embedding is replaced with injectivity). I. M. Gel’fand and D. Raǐkov showed that every locally compact Abelian group has Bohr compactification in this sense. Leaving only continuity from the topological embedding, a ”general” Bohr compactification of a topological group is similarly defined. The Bohr compactification is intimately connected to the finite-dimensional unitary representation theory of topological groups.

WAP-compactification of a group
G
corresponds (using Gelfand–Kolmogoroff theory, which establishes an order-preserving bijective correspondence between Banach unital subalgebras of
C^{*}(G)
and compactifications of
G
) to the algebra of weakly almost periodic functions. It is the maximal semitopological semigroup (non-proper) compactification of
G
. WAP-compactifications are important in understanding the structure and representations of topological groups. M. Megrelishvili
[
22
]
showed that the WAP-compactification may not be a proper compactification of
G
.

In topological dynamics, where the action of a topological group
G
on a compact space
X
is examined, the concept of an enveloping semigroup is a crucial tool for studying orbit closures and, consequently, minimal flows. The enveloping semigroup of a dynamical system was introduced by R. Ellis
[
10
]
. An enveloping semigroup of
G
(see, for instance,
[
26
]
) may be examined as a non-proper semigroup compactification
(S,\varphi)
of
G
:

(a)

S
is a right topological semigroup,

(b)

\varphi:G\to S
is a continuous homomorphism of semigroups and
\varphi(G)
is dense in
S
,

(c)

the extension of left action of
G
on itself to
S
is continuous.

In topological algebra, this approach led to the concept of a semigroup compactification of a semigroup
S
, which is a topological space.
(T,\varphi)
is a semigroup compactification of
S
if

is a compact right topological semigroup,
\varphi:S\to T

\varphi(S)
is dense in

The well-known examples of proper compactifications of a topological group

are the maximal

-compactification
\beta_{G}G
(the greatest ambit) and Roelcke compactification
b_{r}G
. They are Samuel compactifications of

in the right uniformity and Roelcke uniformity on

Considering a topological group

as the transformation group of a compact space

(action is effective), the elements of

can be examined as self-maps of

. Two methods of obtaining compactifications of

are possible. If the topology on

coincides with the topology of pointwise convergence for the action

G\curvearrowright X

) is the proper compactification of

. This method allows one to obtain a proper right topological semigroup compactification of

If the topology on

coincides with the compact-open topology for the action
G\curvearrowright X

as graphs of maps in the hyperspace
2^{X\times X}
with Vietoris topology and take the closure of
G
in the compact space
2^{X\times X}
. The method of graphs allows one to obtain compactifications on which the involution, the left and ”right” action of
G
on itself continuously extend
[
14
]
and
[
25
]
.

In the paper, by a proper compactification of a topological group

we understand a pair
(bG,b)

is a compact space and

is a topological embedding. We show what proper compactifications of topological groups are obtained from equivariant compactifications of

using the above two methods. This yields a possible classification of proper compactifications of topological groups from the point of view of the possibility of extensions of the noted above algebraic operations. A similar approach to (partial) classifications of non-proper compactifications of topological (semi-)groups can be found in

The possibility of applying the Ellis method and the method of graphs to obtain proper compactifications is justified in §

In Section

•

compactifications to which the left action of

•

•

Examining all

-compactifications of a topological group

Part II (of the classification):

•

•

Examining all

In Theorem

Dynamical Formation of Apparent Horizons due to Boundary Effect in Vacuum Einstein Gravity
Puskar Mondal, Shing-Tung Yau

Abstract
. We prove that an apparent horizon can form as a result of Einsteinian evolution in pure vacuum spacetime starting from regular initial data free of apparent horizons due to pure boundary effects. We adapt a Cauchy-double-null framework and use the boundary generalized mean curvature condition for the existence of an interior apparent horizon imposed by the author S-T Yau in [ 38 ] . In particular, we prove that the condition of [ 38 ] can be met dynamically starting from a configuration that does not verify the same through a focusing mechanism. This is the first part of a two-part sequence, and in the sequel, we will focus on explicitly constructing the Cauchy data.

3.1.
Main Ideas and Novelty

The principal analytical difficulty of the present work lies in the
construction of the initial data
, whether prescribed on a characteristic or a Cauchy hypersurface. The heuristics discussed in the preceding section suggest that the nonlinearities arising in the transport equations for the null Ricci coefficients contribute, in a delicate sense, only subdominant effects to the leading dynamics. Arbitrary initial data, however, fail to exhibit such cancellation structures, and in fact, by the celebrated small-data result of Christodoulou–Klainerman
[
17
]
, one already knows that for sufficiently small perturbations of Minkowski data, no apparent horizon or trapped surface can form.

In the large-data regime, the situation is profoundly different: the system is fully nonlinear, no global small parameter exists, and long-time uniform control cannot, in general, be expected in the hyperbolic setting. As a result, one faces the possibility of geometric pathologies, including singularity formation and breakdown of the foliation. A central challenge, therefore, consists in constructing a hierarchy of
large
but
controlled
initial data
(g,k)
that is compatible with a semi-global existence theorem for the Einstein vacuum equations, and whose evolution can be followed up to the onset of an apparent horizon.

From a structural viewpoint, the formation of a trapped surface is governed by the Raychaudhuri equation for the null expansion
\mbox{tr}\chi
along the outgoing null direction:

(45)

\nabla_{4}\mbox{tr}\chi+\tfrac{1}{2}(\mbox{tr}\chi)^{2}=-\,|\widehat{\chi}|^{2}_{\not{g}}-2\omega\,\mbox{tr}\chi.

In the traditional approach (cf.
[
16
,
22
,
1
,
3
]
), one attempts to produce a negative expansion
\mbox{tr}\chi<0
within a finite affine parameter time along the null generators. This mechanism is driven by the largeness of the incoming gravitational shear
|\widehat{\chi}|^{2}
, which acts as a source in (
45
).

By contrast, the present work adopts a complementary perspective. Rather than directly forcing
\mbox{tr}\chi
to become negative, we study the evolution of the
generalized mean curvature
quantity

(46)

c:=H-|\kappa|,

as introduced in Section
1.2
. Our objective is to obtain a strictly positive value of
c
on an appropriate boundary hypersurface, thus triggering the dynamical emergence of an apparent horizon from a previously horizon–free configuration. In the double–null gauge, one has the decomposition

(47)

H-|\kappa|=\tfrac{1}{2}\big(\mbox{tr}\chi-\mbox{tr}\underline{\chi}\big)-\tfrac{1}{2}\big|\mbox{tr}\chi+\mbox{tr}\underline{\chi}\big|.

The guiding principle is therefore to drive the difference
\mbox{tr}\chi-\mbox{tr}\underline{\chi}
to become large and positive while simultaneously suppressing the absolute term

along the incoming null direction.

This strategy is realized by prescribing a large incoming shear
\underline{\hat{\chi}}

\underline{H}_{0}
and an appropriately scaled profile for
\mbox{tr}\chi

\mbox{tr}\underline{\chi}

\mbox{tr}\underline{\chi}

The methodology presented here represents a substantial conceptual departure from all previous works on trapped surface formation. The open set of characteristic data constructed herein accommodates a broader class of large data than those treated in the earlier frameworks of Christodoulou and Klainerman–Rodnianski. The analytical framework developed here constitutes a genuinely new approach to the dynamical formation of apparent horizons and trapped surfaces in vacuum general relativity. To the best of our knowledge, no previous result in the literature establishes the emergence of such geometric features through the control of the generalized mean curvature quantity

Another distinctive aspect of the present analysis is that elliptic estimates are
not
required. By formulating the entire problem at a sufficiently high level of Sobolev regularity within the hyperbolic (double-null) setting, we circumvent any dependence on auxiliary elliptic estimates and maintain full control through transport-type and energy-type arguments (this is well known by now in the context of the characteristic initial value problem). Finally, the structure developed here aligns naturally with the local constructions of Luk

, suggesting that one can generate more general families of initial data on finite domains satisfying Yau’s boundary condition in a dynamically consistent fashion.

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
\mathcal{G},\mathcal{H}\in\Gamma(^{N}\otimes T^{*}S)

\nabla_{3}\mathcal{G}+\lambda_{0}\hskip 1.42262pt\mbox{tr}\underline{\chi}\hskip 1.42262pt\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{H}.

Proof.

The variation of area formula for a scalar function

(105)

\underline{L}\int_{S_{u,\underline{u}}}f=\int_{S_{u,\underline{u}}}\underline{L}f+\Omega\hskip 1.42262pt\mbox{tr}\underline{\chi}\hskip 1.42262ptf=\int_{S_{u,\underline{u}}}\Omega\hskip 1.42262pt\left(e_{3}(f)+\mbox{tr}\underline{\chi}\hskip 1.42262ptf\right).

Plugging in

(106)

\begin{split}&\underline{L}\int_{S_{u,\underline{u}}}\lvert u\rvert^{2\lambda_{1}}\lvert\mathcal{G}\rvert_{\gamma}^{2}\\

(107)

\begin{split}&\frac{-2\lambda_{1}e_{3}(u)}{\lvert u\rvert}+(1-2\lambda_{0})\hskip 1.42262pt\mbox{tr}\underline{\chi}\\

\lVert{\mbox{tr}\underline{\chi}+\frac{2}{\lvert u\rvert}}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(S_{u,\underline{u}})}\leq\frac{\Gamma}{\lvert u\rvert^{2}}

(108)

\begin{split}&\lvert u\rvert^{2\lambda_{1}}\lVert{\mathcal{G}}\rVert_{L^{2}(S_{u,\underline{u}})}\\

(136)

\begin{split}&\int_{u_{\infty}}^{u}\frac{a^{2}}{\lvert u^{\prime}\rvert^{4}}\lVert{a^{\frac{i}{2}}\sum_{i_{1}+i_{2}+i_{3}+i_{4}=i-2}\nabla^{i_{1}}\psi_{g}^{i_{2}+1}\nabla^{i_{3}}(\underline{\hat{\chi}},\mbox{tr}\underline{\chi})\nabla^{i_{4}}\mbox{tr}\underline{\chi}}\rVert_{L^{2}_{(sc)}(S_{u^{\prime},\underline{u}})}\hskip 1.42262pt\text{d}u^{\prime}\\

(137)

by the Sobolev embedding. This will prove useful in the estimates for
\widetilde{\mbox{tr}\underline{\chi}}
. ∎

7.
The Cauchy problem on the slab

and completion of the argument

In this section, we complete the proof of Theorem

7.9.
Constraint map and local deformation

Let

3
–manifold. Its linearization

–adjoint

are overdetermined–elliptic. On weighted Sobolev scales

26

\mathcal{K}\cong\mathbb{R}^{10},

(E,P,J,C)
at infinity. Compactly supported solutions of

exist and yield compactly supported corrections to

\mathcal{K}
–projection of the residual is killed by parameter variation in an admissible model family (Kerr) on the outer end.

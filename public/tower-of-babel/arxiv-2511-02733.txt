Ekedahl-Oort types of 𝐙 / 2 ​ 𝐙 \mathbf{Z}/2\mathbf{Z} -covers in characteristic 2 2
Jeremy Booher, Steven R. Groen, Joe Kramer-Miller

Abstract
. In this article we study the Ekedahl-Oort types of 𝐙 / 2 ​ 𝐙 \mathbf{Z}/2\mathbf{Z} -Galois covers π : Y → X \pi:Y\to X in characteristic two. When the base curve X X is ordinary, we show that the Ekedahl-Oort type of Y Y is completely determined by the genus of X X and the ramification of π \pi . For a general base curve X X , we prove bounds on the Ekedahl-Oort depending on the Ekedahl-Oort type of X X and the ramification of π \pi . Along the way, we develop a theory of enhanced differentials of the second kind . This theory allows us to study algebraic de Rham cohomology in any characteristic by working directly with differentials, in contrast to the standard Čech resolution. Keywords: curve, Jacobian, abelian variety, positive characteristic, Artin-Schreier cover, p p -torsion, moduli space, Ekedahl-Oort strata, de Rham cohomology. 2020 MSC primary: 11G20, 14H10, 14H30, 14H40, 14F40. Secondary: 14G17, 14L15.



1.
Introduction

Let
p
be a prime and
k
be a perfect field of characteristic
p
. If
X,Y
are nice curves over
k
(smooth, projective, and geometrically connected) and
\pi:Y\to X
is a branched
\mathbf{Z}/p\mathbf{Z}
-Galois cover of curves, it is natural to study how “natural properties” of
Y
depend on
X
and the ramification of
\pi
. Most famously, the Riemann-Hurwitz formula relates the genus of
Y
to the genus of
X
and the ramification of
\pi
.
Another well-known example is the Deuring-Shafarevich theorem, which gives a formula for the
p
-rank of
Y
in terms of the
p
-rank of
X
and the ramification
[
Sub75
]
.
More recent work has focused on finer invariants related to
the
p
-power torsion of the Jacobians
\text{Jac}(X)
and
\text{Jac}(Y)
, which reveals a
more nuanced picture.
For instance, there is no analog of the Deuring-Shafarevich theorem for
a
-numbers. Instead, there are bounds on the
a
-number of
Y
in terms
of the
a
-number of
X
and the ramification of
\pi
(see
[
FP13
,
BC20
]
and
[
Gro24
]
for similar results with
higher
a
-numbers). Similarly, there is no Deuring-Shafarevich theorem
for ‘higher slopes’ of the Newton polygon. There is, however,
a ‘Newton-over-Hodge’ type phenomenon, which gives a lower bound
on the Newton polygon of
Y
in terms of the Newton polygon of
X
and
the ramification (see
[
KM21
]
and
[
KMU25
]
).
All of this work can be subsumed into the following goal:
describe the cohomology of
Y
in terms of the cohomology of
X
and the ramification of
\pi
. In this article we work with algebraic de Rham cohomology in characteristic two,
endowed with the structure of a mod-
p
Dieudonné module.
When the base curve
X
is ordinary, we completely determine the
Dieudonné module of
Y
in terms of the Dieudonné module of
X

and the ramification. For a more general base curve we provide
bounds on the Dieudonné module of
Y
in terms of this same information.

Recall that for a nice curve
C
over
k
, the algebraic de Rham cohomology
\HH_{\textrm{dR}}^{1}(C)
is naturally equipped with the semilinear operators Frobenius and Verschiebung,
F
and
V
, subject to the relation
FV=VF=0
. In particular,
\HH_{\textrm{dR}}^{1}(C)
is
a Dieudonné module, i.e., a module over the mod-
p
Dieudonné ring
D_{k}=k[F,V]/(FV)
, where
Fw=w^{p}F
and
Vw^{p}=wV
for
w\in k
.
There is also a symplectic pairing
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
on
\HH_{\textrm{dR}}^{1}(C)
for which
F
and
V
are ‘skew’ adjoint, i.e., they satisfy
\langle Vx,y\rangle^{p}=\langle x,Fy\rangle
.
Furthermore, there is a short exact sequence

0\to H^{0}(C,\Omega^{1}_{C})\to\HH_{\textrm{dR}}^{1}(C)\to H^{1}(C,\mathcal{O}_{C})\to 0

with
H^{0}(C,\Omega^{1}_{C})=\ker F=\operatorname{Im}V
. So knowledge of
\HH_{\textrm{dR}}^{1}(C)
as a
D_{k}
-module gives, among other things, an understanding of the genus of
C
(half the dimension), the
p
-rank (the stable-rank of
V
on
H^{0}(C,\Omega^{1}_{C})
), and the
a
-number (the dimension of the kernel of
V
on
H^{0}(C,\Omega^{1}_{C})
, i.e.
\dim_{k}(\ker F\cap\ker V)
). Furthermore, a result of Oda
[
Oda69
]
shows that
\HH_{\textrm{dR}}^{1}(C)
is naturally isomorphic as a Dieudonné module to the Dieudonné module of
\text{Jac}(C)[p]
, the
p
-torsion in the Jacobian of
C
.
In particular, knowledge of the Dieudonné module
\HH_{\textrm{dR}}^{1}(C)
is equivalent to understanding the
Ekedahl-Oort type
of
C
, which is the isomorphism class of
\text{Jac}(C)[p]
as a finite flat group scheme. See
[
Pri08
]
and Section
4.2
for background on Ekedahl-Oort types and how to combinatorially express them as
final types
.
Thus an equivalent way to state our question is as follows: given a branched
\mathbf{Z}/p\mathbf{Z}
-Galois cover of curves
\pi:Y\to X
, describe the Ekedahl-Oort type of
Y
in terms of the Ekedahl-Oort type of
X
and the ramification of
\pi
.

The only previous work on the question at this level of generality is Cais and Ulmer’s work on unramified covers
[
CU23
]
and Elkin and Pries’s work for covers of
\mathbf{P}^{1}
in characteristic two
[
EP13
]
. In the present paper, we focus on one of the few general situations where we expect the Ekedahl-Oort type of
X
and the ramification of
\pi
to determine the Ekedahl-Oort type of
Y
. In particular, in characteristic
p=2
when the base curve
X
is ordinary we show the ramification of
\pi:Y\to X
determines the Ekedahl-Oort type of
Y
. (Recall
X
being ordinary means that the
p
-rank of
X
equals the genus of
X
, which fully determines the Ekedahl-Oort type.) To state the result more precisely, we must set up some notation.

Fix
p=2
and consider a
\mathbf{Z}/2\mathbf{Z}
-cover
\pi:Y\to X
which is ramified over
m
points
\{P_{i}\}_{i=1,\ldots,m}
of
X
. Let
d_{i}
be the
ramification break
at
P_{i}
, the unique break in the upper ramification filtration above
P_{i}
. The final type is a combinatorial way to describe a polarized mod-
p
Dieudonné module, which we review in Section
4.2
.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
M_{\textrm{ord}}
be the polarized mod-
p
Dieudonné module with final type
[1]
(i.e. the Dieudonné module of an ordinary elliptic curve).
For a positive odd integer
d
, let
M_{d}
be the polarized mod-
p
Dieudonné module with final type
\left[0,1,1,2,2,\ldots,\left\lfloor\frac{d-1}{4}\right\rfloor\right]
. (The last entry occurs once or twice depending on
d
modulo
4
.)

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\pi:Y\to X
be a
\mathbf{Z}/2\mathbf{Z}
-cover of smooth, proper, geometrically connected curves over a perfect field
k
of characteristic two, and suppose
X
is ordinary. Then as Dieudonné modules

D(\text{Jac}(Y)[2])\simeq\HH_{\textrm{dR}}^{1}(Y)\simeq M_{\mathrm{ord}}^{2g_{X}-1+m}\oplus\bigoplus_{i=1}^{m}M_{d_{i}}.

Here
g_{X}
is the genus of
X
, equal to the
p
-rank of
X
; note that
2g_{X}-1+m
is the
p
-rank of
Y
by the Deuring-Shafarevich formula
[
Sub75
]
.

Remark 1.3
.

Theorem
1.2
is a simultaneous generalization of a result of Elkin and Pries, which explicitly treats the case that
X=\mathbf{P}^{1}

[
EP13
]
, and of a result of Voloch which computes the
a
-number of an Artin-Schreier cover of an ordinary curve in characteristic two
[
Vol88
]
.

Remark 1.4
.

The Dieudonné modules in Theorem
1.2
can equivalently be described by giving the
F
and
V
actions: see Lemma
3.1
, Section
4.1
, and in particular the proof of Theorem
4.6
.

By fixing a base curve in characteristic two and varying the cover subject to a specific set of ramification invariants, we can construct positive dimensional families of genus
g
curves with constant Ekedahl-Oort type. For example:

Corollary 1.5
.

For any positive integer
g
, there is a
(g-1)
-dimensional family of smooth curves of genus
g
with constant Ekedahl-Oort type: in particular, the final type is
[0,1,1,2,2,\ldots,\left\lfloor g/2\right\rfloor]
.

For any positive integer
n
, let
d=2^{n}+1
. There is a
(d+1)/2
-dimensional family of smooth curves of genus
(d+3)/2
with constant Ekedahl-Oort type: in particular, the final type is
[0,1,2,3,3,4,4,\ldots,(d-1)/4+1,(d-1)/4+1,(d-1)/4+2]
.

Remark 1.6
.

These families are “unlikely intersections” in the sense that, when
g
is sufficiently large, the codimension of the Ekedahl-Oort strata in the moduli space of principally polarized Abelian varieties of dimension
g
exceeds
3g-3
, the dimension of the moduli space of curves of genus
g
(see for example Remark
4.12
).

When the base curve
X
is not ordinary, the Ekedahl-Oort type of the cover
Y
is no longer
determined entirely by the Ekedahl-Oort type of
X
and the ramification breaks. Instead, we are able to constrain the Ekedahl-Oort type of the cover
Y
based on the ramification breaks. In Section
5
we consider the simplest non-ordinary case, which is a supersingular elliptic curve.
When the cover is ramified at a single point with break
d
, we determine all possible
k[V]
-structures on
H^{0}(Y,\Omega_{Y}^{1})
(see Theorem
5.11
) and determine the codimensions of the strata in the moduli space of such covers (see Theorem
5.15
). The difficulty in extending this analysis to the
full Ekedahl-Oort type lies in understanding the pairing
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
. In particular, in Example
5.19
we exhibit two covers whose
global differentials have the same
k[V]
-structure, but whose Ekedahl-Oort
types differ.

More generally, let
X
be any curve and let
[v_{1},\dots,v_{g_{Y}}]
be the final type of
Y
. In Theorem
6.19
we estimate
v_{l}
with an error that is essentially logarithmic in terms of the ramification breaks. To avoid introducing unnecessary notation, we put off
stating Theorem
6.19
in full generality until Section
6
. Instead, we state
a special case of this theorem for the case where
Y\to X
is branched at one point.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
Y\to X
be a
\mathbf{Z}/2\mathbf{Z}
-cover of smooth, proper, geometrically connected curves over a perfect field
k
of characteristic two,
branched at one point with ramification invariant
d
. Let
f_{Y}=2f_{X}
be the
p
-rank of
Y
and
l_{X}=g_{X}-f_{X}
be the local rank of
X
. Letting
[\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{g_{Y}}]
denote the final type of
Y
:

(1)

For
1\leq l\leq f_{Y}
, we have
\nu_{l}=l
.

(2)

For
f_{Y}<l<f_{Y}+2l_{X},
we have
f_{Y}\leq\nu_{l}\leq l-1
.

(3)

For
f_{Y}+2l_{X}\leq l\leq g_{Y}
, we have

(1)

\left|\nu_{l}-\left(f_{Y}+\left\lfloor\frac{l-f_{Y}}{2}\right\rfloor\right)\right|\leq\begin{cases}\frac{3\lceil\log_{2}(d-1)\rceil}{2}l_{X}&\hbox{if $d\leq 4g_{X}-5$}\\
\lceil\log_{2}(d-1)\rceil l_{X}&\hbox{if $d>4g_{X}-5$.}\end{cases}

In particular, we have an estimate for the final type of
Y

whose error is logarithmic in the ramification invariant.

1.1.
Structure of the paper

The main technical tool we introduce is a generalization of differentials of the second kind which works in positive characteristic.
We develop a theory of
enhanced differentials of the second kind
for smooth proper connected curves over a field in Section
2
, based on a folklore pole-order resolution for the de Rham complex. Concretely, an enhanced differential of the second kind consists
of a classical differential of the second kind
\omega
(i.e. a locally exact differential) together with a choice of antiderivative for the meromorphic part of
\omega
at each of its poles. In characteristic
zero there is only one such antiderivative, and we therefore recover the classical theory of differentials of the second kind.
In contrast, when
k
has characteristic
p
there are many possible
local antiderivatives, since
\frac{d}{dx}f(x)^{p}=0
.
This theory gives a concrete way to represent de Rham cohomology classes: we prove that
\HH_{\textrm{dR}}^{1}(X)
is isomorphic to the space of enhanced differentials
of the second kind modulo those that are globally exact. Note that classical differentials of the second kind have been studied in positive
characteristic by Rosenlicht
[
Ros53
]
, but they fail to compute
de Rham cohomology.
The standard techniques for studying the Cartier operator on regular differentials have natural generalizations to enhanced differentials of the second kind, which inspires our analysis. (While a similar analysis could surely be done using the representation of de Rham cohomology classes coming from the Čech resolution, the generalization of these techniques is far less natural from that perspective.)

In Section
3
, we apply the theory developed in Section
2
to study
\mathbf{Z}/2\mathbf{Z}
-covers
\pi:Y\to X
in characteristic two. We decompose

\HH_{\textrm{dR}}^{1}(Y)=U\oplus Z\oplus L
where
V
is bijective on
U
(and
F
is zero), where
F
is bijective on
Z
(and
V
is zero), and where
F
and
V
are both nilpotent on
L
. The latter space is the most interesting, and we construct a convenient set of enhanced differentials of the second kind
\{\widetilde{\omega}_{i,j}\}
which almost span
L
and for which the action of
V
is particularly simple (see Proposition
3.8
and Definition
3.9
). In particular, these enhanced differentials are built using pullbacks of meromorphic differentials on the base curve
X
: this is the same spirit as Voloch’s argument computing the
a
-number of
Y
using a space of differentials with bounded poles on the ordinary base curve
[
Vol88
]
. For a general base curve, we know a lot about the
V
-action on
L
and the form of the duality pairing on
\HH_{\textrm{dR}}^{1}(Y)
via studying
\{\widetilde{\omega}_{i,j}\}
but not quite enough to determine the Ekedahl-Oort type of
Y
. We expect the ideas in Section
2

and Section
3
will be useful for studying

\HH_{\textrm{dR}}^{1}(X)
for curves in any characteristic.

In Section
4
we study the case when the base curve
X
is ordinary, in which case
\{\widetilde{\omega}_{i,j}\}
forms a basis for
L
and we are able to completely describe the action of
V
. This is enough information to fully determine the Ekedahl–Oort type and establish Theorem
1.2
. We obtain the first family described in Corollary
1.5
by looking at covers of
\mathbf{P}^{1}
ramified at one point with ramification break
d=2g+1
, which is a case covered by Elkin and Pries
[
EP13
]
.

In Section
5
we study the case
when
X
is a supersingular elliptic curve. This is the
simplest non-ordinary case. We first give explicit examples
that demonstrate that the Ekedahl–Oort type of
Y
is
not determined by that of
X
and the ramification of the cover.
This is done by exploring the difference between the span of
\{\widetilde{\omega}_{i,j}\}
and the space
L
.
Next, we consider the
k[V]
-structure of
Y
for covers
branched over one point. We determine all possible

k[V]
-structures that occur. Furthermore, we determine
the codimensions of the
k[V]
-module strata in the moduli
space of curves admitting a
\mathbf{Z}/2\mathbf{Z}
-cover to
X
branched
at a single point with fixed ramification break
d
. Finally,
we prove that the Ekedahl-Oort type is determined by the ramification when
d=2^{n}+1
, which gives the second case of Corollary
1.5
.

In Section
6
, we focus on bounding the Ekedahl-Oort type of
Y
when
X
is not ordinary. The final type is determined by the dimensions of the spaces
w(\HH_{\textrm{dR}}^{1}(Y))
where

w=V^{n_{t}}\bot V^{n_{t-1}}\bot\ldots\bot V^{n_{1}}
. Here
\bot
denotes taking symplectic
complement under the pairing
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
. The uncertainty about the
V
-action and its interaction with the duality pairing introduce additional uncertainty when taking the symplectic complements. As seen in Example
5.19
, this leads to legitimate variation of the Ekedahl-Oort type. Our approach is to bound this uncertainty in terms
of the number of
\bot
’s that occur in a word
w
. Proposition
6.14
gives constants
L(X,\pi,w)
and
U(X,\pi,w)
such that

(2)

L(X,\pi,w)\leq\dim_{k}(w(L))\leq U(X,\pi,w).

These constants are determined inductively on
t
, i.e., the number of
\bot
’s
occurring in the word
w
.
When
t=1
, so that there are no
\bot
’s in
w
, the range in (
2
)
is approximately
l_{X}=g_{X}-f_{X}
, where
f_{X}
is the
p
-rank of
X
. When

w=V^{n_{t+1}}\bot w^{\prime}
and
w^{\prime}
is a word containing
\bot
exactly
t-1
times,
we find that the range of possible values of
\dim_{k}(w(L))
(i.e.
U(X,\pi,w)-L(X,\pi,w)
)
is approximately
2l_{X}
more than the range for
\dim_{k}(w^{\prime}(L))
. In particular,
we determine
\dim_{k}(w(L))
up to an error that is approximately
2l_{X}\cdot t
.
Our main result, Theorem
6.19
, then ascertains the effects of these bounds on the final type of
Y
. Finally, Theorem
1.7
follows
by bounding the total number of
\bot
’s that are necessary to obtain all possible
subspaces

w(\HH_{\textrm{dR}}^{1}(Y))
when
\pi
has only one branch point.

Remark 1.8
.

Our analysis and the Ekedahl-Oort types in Theorems
1.2
and
6.19
exhibit a kind of local-to-global principle. Each point of ramification makes an independent contribution depending on the ramification break, and this combined with the Ekedahl-Oort type of the base gives the overall behavior. This sort of behavior has also been seen in the Riemann-Hurwitz formula, the Deuring-Shafarevich formula, and bounds for
a
-numbers and higher
a
-numbers
[
BC20
,
Gro24
]
. This also appears in work of Garnek, which obtains local-global decompositions of
\HH_{\textrm{dR}}^{1}(Y)
as a
\mathbf{Z}/p\mathbf{Z}
-module
[
Gar23
,
Gar25
]
. (It is unfortunately not compatible with Frobenius and Verschiebung.) However, there is a not a simple local-to-global principle for Ekedahl-Oort types (or even
a
-numbers) of Artin-Schreier curves. Groen gives an example of an Artin-Schreier curve whose
a
-number depends on the location of its branch points, not just the breaks in the ramification filtration
[
Gro23
, Theorem 2.7.1]
.

Remark 1.9
.

Our results are specific to characteristic two for (at least) two reasons. The first is that in odd characteristic even if the base curve is ordinary (i.e.
\mathbf{P}^{1}
) the Ekedahl-Oort type of the cover (or even just the
a
-number) is not determined by the ramification and can take on a wide range of possibilities. So there is no hope of a direct analog of Theorem
1.2
when the base curve is not ordinary.

The second reason is technical, and has to do with the construction of the enhanced differentials
\{\widetilde{\omega}_{i,j}\}
which we essentially understand. The fact that most of
L\subset\HH_{\textrm{dR}}^{1}(Y)
can be constructed with differentials pulled back from
X
is also specific to characteristic two. In contrast, on an Artin-Schreier curve in odd characteristic this is patently false. For example, consider the curve in characteristic three given by
y^{3}-y=f(x)
where
f(x)
is a polynomial of degree
d
with
\gcd(d,3)=1
. Writing
\omega=(g_{0}(x)+g_{1}(x)y+g_{2}(x)y^{2})dx
with
g_{i}(x)
polynomials, we see that
\omega
is regular if and only if
\deg(g_{0})\leq\lceil\frac{2d}{3}\rceil-2
,
\deg(g_{1})\leq\lceil\frac{d}{3}\rceil-2
, and
g_{2}=0

[
BC20
, Lemma 3.7]
. So there are many differentials which involve
y
and cannot be analyzed based on the action of
V
on spaces of differentials with poles on the base.

2.
Enhanced differentials of the second kind and de Rham cohomology

In this section we let
X
be a smooth proper connected curve over a field
k
. We make no assumption on the characteristic of
k
. Let
k(X)
denote the function field of
X
. For any closed point
Q\in X
we let
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{X,Q}
denote the completion of the local ring at
Q
and we let
K_{Q}
denote the fraction field of
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{X,Q}
.

2.1.
Enhanced differentials of the second kind

Differentials of the second kind are a classic concept over the complex numbers. Rosenlicht introduced and studied the concept in arbitrary characteristic
[
Ros53
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

A differential of the second kind on
X
is a meromorphic differential (i.e. a differential that may have poles)
\omega
such that for every
Q\in X
, there exists

f\in K_{Q}
such that
\omega-df\in\Omega^{1}_{\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{X,Q}/k}
.

Rosenlicht requires there be
f\in k(X)
with
\omega-df
regular at
Q
, which is equivalent.

Remark 2.2
.

Note that when
k
has characteristic zero, a differential
\omega
is of the second kind if and only if the residue at
Q
is zero for all
Q\in X
. This fails in characteristic
p
as the derivative of a
p
-th power is zero. In particular, to be of the second kind the local expansion of
\omega
in terms of a uniformizer
t
at
Q
cannot include terms of the form
t^{i}dt
where
i<0
and
i\equiv-1\mod{p}
.

Note that exact differentials are automatically of the second kind.

The classical isomorphism between de Rham cohomology and differentials of the second kind modulo exact differentials breaks down in characteristic
p
. (For an expository account, see the note of Gurski
[
Gur01
]
.)
We introduce the notion of enhanced differentials of the second kind to recover this connection. An enhanced differential of the second kind consists of a differential of the second kind along with a choice of an antiderivative of the “tail” of
\omega
at each
Q
.

Definition 2.3
.

An enhanced differential of the second kind on
X
is a pair
(\omega,(f_{Q})_{Q\in X})
where

(1)

\omega
is a differential of the second kind on
X
;

(2)

for each
Q\in X
,
f_{Q}\in K_{Q}/\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{X,Q}
and if
\tilde{f}_{Q}\in K_{Q}
represents
f_{Q}
then
\omega-d\tilde{f}_{Q}\in\Omega^{1}_{\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{X,Q}/k}
.

Note that almost all the
f_{Q}
’s are automatically in
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{X,Q}
. We will often abuse notation by not distinguishing between the element
\tilde{f}_{Q}
of
K_{Q}
and the equivalence class
f_{Q}
it represents.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
S
be a finite set of closed points in
X
. An
S
-enhanced differential of the second kind on
X
is a pair
(\omega,(f_{Q})_{Q\in S})
such that

(1)

\omega
is a differential of the second kind that is regular away from
S
;

(2)

f_{Q}\in K_{Q}/\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{X,Q}
and
if
\tilde{f}_{Q}
is a lift of
f_{Q}
to
K_{Q}
we have
\omega-d\tilde{f}_{Q}\in\Omega^{1}_{\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{X,Q}/k}
.

If we take
f_{Q}=0
for
Q\in X-S
, an
S
-enhanced differential of the second kind naturally becomes an enhanced differential of the second kind.

Definition 2.5
.

We let
E_{X}
(resp.
E_{X,S}
) denote the
k
-vector space of enhanced differentials (resp.
S
-enhanced differentials) on
X
.
Define

2.3.
Relation to the Čech description of algebraic de Rham
cohomology

We now compare the enhanced differential description of

with the Čech description given in
[

, §5]
(see also

[

). Let
S
be a finite set of points in
X

and let
U_{2}
be an affine open subscheme of

containing

\mathfrak{U}=\{U_{1},U_{2}\}
is a cover of

Z^{1}(\mathfrak{U})=\left\{\left(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},f\right)~\middle|~\begin{array}[]{c}\omega_{i}\in\Omega^{1}_{X}(U_{i})\\
f\in\mathcal{O}_{X}(U_{1}\cap U_{2})\\
\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}=df\end{array}\right\}.

The Čech 1-coboundaries are defined by

B^{1}(\mathfrak{U})=\left\{(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},f)~\middle|~\begin{array}[]{c}\text{There exists }f_{1},f_{2}\text{ with }f_{i}\in\mathcal{O}_{X}(U_{i})\\

Then we have

\HH_{\textrm{dR}}^{1}(X)\cong Z^{1}(\mathfrak{U})/B^{1}(\mathfrak{U}).

We now define a map
\imath:Z^{1}(\mathfrak{U})\to E_{S}
by

\imath(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},f)=\left(\omega_{1},(f\mod\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{X,Q})_{Q\in S}\right).

Note the only poles of

are at

, and since

is regular at the points of

, the relation
\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}=df
means that

\omega_{1}-df\in\Omega^{1}_{\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{X,Q}}
for each

.

Proposition 2.11
.

The map
\imath
induces an isomorphism

(5)

\frac{Z^{1}(\mathfrak{U})}{B^{1}(\mathfrak{U})}\to\frac{E_{S}}{d_{X,S}(A_{S})}.

Proof.

Note that
\imath
factors through the quotient as if

is a Čech 1-coboundary, then

is equal to

.
To see that this map is an isomorphism, we describe the inverse.
Let
(\omega,(f_{Q})_{Q\in S})
be an
S
-enhanced differential.
Let

be a function in
\mathcal{O}_{X}(U_{1}\cap U_{2})
with

for each

Such an

is a Čech 1-cocycle.
A short calculation shows that a different choice of

-cocycle that differs by a

-coboundary.
If

is exact, so equal to

The Čech

-cocycles and

\displaystyle Z^{2}(\mathfrak{U})

\displaystyle\omega

First, viewing

T(\alpha_{\rm odd})=T(\alpha_{\rm even}).

Since

, we must have

.
In particular, we see that

, where

Next, we know from (

and hence

. Again using (

gives

. In particular, we obtain

(11)

\displaystyle t_{i}

\displaystyle=g(u)^{2}+a_{d_{i}+2}u^{d_{i}+2}+O(u^{d_{i}+3}).

As

is a uniformizer, we take
u_{i}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}g(u)

\displaystyle t_{i}

\displaystyle=u_{i}^{2}+c_{i}u_{i}^{d_{i}+2}+O(u_{i}^{d_{i}+3}),

where

.
∎

Corollary 3.3
.

For all

we have

Proof.

From Lemma

. Thus,
\frac{t_{i}^{j}}{u_{i}^{2j}}=1+O(u_{i}^{d_{i}})
(e.g. use the generalized binomial theorem). The corollary follows by multiplying by

∎

\displaystyle\omega_{i,j}

\displaystyle=[u_{i}^{-2j+d_{i}-1}+O(u_{i}^{d_{i}+1})]du_{i}

\displaystyle=[u_{i}^{-2j+d_{i}-1}+O(u_{i}^{-2j+2d_{i}-1})]du_{i}.

3.3.
The action of
F
and
V

Since we wish to understand the Dieudonné module structure of
\HH_{\textrm{dR}}^{1}(Y)
, it is a natural next step to study the action of
F
and
V
on the
S
-enhanced differentials
\widehat{\omega}_{i,j}
.

Using the decomposition of
k[F]
-modules

\HH_{\textrm{dR}}^{1}(Y)=\HH_{\textrm{dR}}^{1}(Y)^{\mathrm{bij}}\oplus\HH_{\textrm{dR}}^{1}(Y)^{\mathrm{nil}}

coming from Fitting’s lemma, for any
\widehat{\omega}\in\HH_{\textrm{dR}}^{1}(Y)
we can write
\widehat{\omega}=\widehat{\omega}^{\mathrm{bij}}+\widehat{\omega}^{\mathrm{nil}}
.

Proposition 3.8
.

There exists a choice of differentials
\widehat{\omega}_{i,j}
as in Proposition
3.6
such that

V(\widehat{\omega}_{i,j}^{\mathrm{nil}})=\begin{cases}\widehat{\omega}_{j/2}^{\mathrm{nil}}+\pi^{*}\eta_{i,j}&\hbox{if $j$ is even}\\
\pi^{*}\eta_{i,j}&\hbox{if $j$ is odd},\end{cases}

where
\eta_{i,j}
is an element of
\HH^{0}(X,\Omega_{X}^{1})
on which
V
acts nilpotently. In particular,
V
acts nilpotently on
\widehat{\omega}_{i,j}^{\mathrm{nil}}
.

Proof.

Note first that
V
kills
\widehat{\omega}_{i,j}^{\mathrm{bij}}
since it is in the image of
F
. Thus it suffices to show the lemma for the
\widehat{\omega}_{i,j}
.

Since
\omega_{i,j}
is regular away from
P_{i}
, so is
V(\omega_{i,j})
. Locally at
P_{i}
, we have

V(w_{i,j})=V((t_{i}^{-j-1}+O(1))dt_{i})=\begin{cases}\left(t_{i}^{-\frac{j}{2}+1}+O(1)\right)dt_{i}&\hbox{if $j$ is even}\\
O(1)dt_{i}&\hbox{if $j$ is odd}.\end{cases}

Pulling back along
\pi
and using Proposition
2.16
, the local expansions show that
V(\widehat{\omega}_{i,j})-\omega_{i,j/2}
is the pullback of a differential which is regular on
X
. Noting that
\widehat{\omega}_{i,j/2}=\widehat{\omega}_{i,j/2}^{\mathrm{nil}}
since a regular differential is killed by
F
, we obtain the formula in the lemma.
It remains to be shown that we may assume that
V
acts nilpotently on
\eta_{i,j}
(and hence on
\widehat{\omega}_{i,j}
). For this, recall the decomposition of
k[V]
-modules from Fitting’s lemma

\HH^{0}(X,\Omega_{X}^{1})=\HH^{0}(X,\Omega_{X}^{1})^{\mathrm{bij}}\oplus\HH^{0}(X,\Omega_{X}^{1})^{\mathrm{nil}}

and write
\eta_{i,j}=\eta_{i,j}^{\mathrm{bij}}+\eta_{i,j}^{\mathrm{nil}}.
There exists a differential
\gamma_{i,j}\in\HH^{0}(X,\Omega_{X}^{1})
such that
V(\gamma_{i,j})=\eta_{i,j}^{\mathrm{bij}}
. Recall that the differential
w_{i,j}
on
X
is chosen up to an element of
\HH^{0}(X,\Omega_{X}^{1})
, so we may replace
w_{i,j}
by
w_{i,j}-\gamma_{i,j}
. Then we have

V(w_{i,j}-\gamma_{i,j})=V(w_{i,j})-V(\gamma_{i,j})=\begin{cases}w_{i,j/2}+\eta_{i,j}^{\mathrm{nil}}&\hbox{if $j$ is odd}\\
\eta_{i,j}^{\mathrm{nil}}&\hbox{if $j$ is even}.\end{cases}

Thus we may choose
w_{i,j}
such that
V
is nilpotent on
w_{i,j}
and therefore
V
is nilpotent on
\widehat{\omega}_{i,j}^{\mathrm{nil}}
.
∎

.

Fixing differentials as in Proposition

, we define

3.4.
The symplectic pairing

We already know that the pairing
U\times Z\to k
is perfect and the spaces
U\oplus Z
and
L
are orthogonal. So the next step is to analyze the symplectic pairing between the
S
-enhanced differentials
\widetilde{\omega}_{i,j}
on
Y
.

Proposition 3.11
.

Let
\widetilde{\omega}_{i,j}
be an
S
-enhanced differential with
1\leq j\leq\frac{d_{i}-1}{2}
. Let
\widetilde{\omega}_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}
be arbitrary. Then the symplectic pairing is given by

\langle\widetilde{\omega}_{i,j},\widetilde{\omega}_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}\rangle=\begin{cases}1&\hbox{if $i=i^{\prime}$ and $j+j^{\prime}=d_{i}$}\\
0&\hbox{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Proof.

Recall that we had defined
\widetilde{\omega}_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}=\widehat{\omega}_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}-\widehat{\omega}_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{bij}}
. Since
j\leq(d_{i}-1)/2
, we have
\widetilde{\omega}_{i,j}=\widehat{\omega}_{i,j}
. By Proposition
3.8
,
V
is nilpotent on
\widehat{\omega}_{i,j}
, so let
r
be such that
V^{r}(\widehat{\omega}_{i,j})=0
. Note there exists
\delta_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}
such that
F^{r}(\delta_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}})=\widehat{\omega}_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{bij}}
. Then observe

\displaystyle\langle\widetilde{\omega}_{i,j},\widetilde{\omega}_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\widehat{\omega}_{i,j},\widehat{\omega}_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}-\widehat{\omega}_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{bij}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\widehat{\omega}_{i,j},\widehat{\omega}_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}\rangle-\langle\widehat{\omega}_{i,j},F^{r}(\delta_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}})\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\widehat{\omega}_{i,j},\widehat{\omega}_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}\rangle-\langle V^{r}(\widehat{\omega}_{i,j}),\delta_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}\rangle^{p^{r}}

\displaystyle=\langle\widehat{\omega}_{i,j},\widehat{\omega}_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}\rangle.

The rest of the proof is devoted to computing
\langle\widehat{\omega}_{i,j},\widehat{\omega}_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}\rangle
making use of Theorem
2.9
.

First assume
i^{\prime}\neq i
. Since
j\leq\frac{d_{i}-1}{2}
, the class
\widehat{\omega}_{i,j}
only consists of a regular differential. If
j^{\prime}\leq\frac{d_{i^{\prime}}}{2}
, then we immediately obtain
\langle\omega_{i,j},\omega_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}\rangle=0
. Otherwise, write
\widehat{\omega}_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}=(\omega_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}},(0,\ldots,u_{i^{\prime}}^{-2j^{\prime}+d_{i^{\prime}}},\ldots,0).
Then the pairing is given by

\langle\widehat{\omega}_{i,j},\widehat{\omega}_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}\rangle=\mathrm{Res}_{Q_{i^{\prime}}}(u^{-2j^{\prime}+d_{i^{\prime}}}\omega_{i,j}).

Now, since
w_{i,j}
is regular at
P_{i^{\prime}}
, it follows from Lemma
3.2
that
\mathrm{ord}_{Q_{i^{\prime}}}(\omega_{i,j})\geq d_{i^{\prime}}+1,
so that

\mathrm{ord}_{Q_{i^{\prime}}}(u^{-2j^{\prime}+d_{i^{\prime}}}\omega_{i,j})\geq-2j^{\prime}+d_{i^{\prime}}+(d_{i^{\prime}}+1)\geq 3,

since
j^{\prime}\leq d_{i^{\prime}}-1
. Therefore the residue vanishes.

Next we compute the shape of the pairing restricted to
W_{i}
. For
j^{\prime}\leq\frac{d_{i}-1}{2}
, we know
\omega_{i,j}
and
\omega_{i,j^{\prime}}
are regular differentials, and thus
\langle\widehat{\omega}_{i,j},\widehat{\omega}_{i,j^{\prime}}\rangle=0
. When
\omega_{i,j^{\prime}}
is not regular, we obtain

\displaystyle\langle\widehat{\omega}_{i,j},\widehat{\omega}_{i,j^{\prime}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Res}_{Q_{i}}(u_{i}^{-2j^{\prime}+d_{i}}\omega_{i,j}).

By Lemma
3.4
we have

u_{i}^{-2j^{\prime}+d_{i}}\omega_{i,j}=[u_{i}^{-2(j+j^{\prime})+2d_{i}-1}+O(u_{i}^{-2j^{\prime}+2d_{i}-1})]du_{i}

Since
j^{\prime}\leq d_{i}-1
we know that
O(u_{i}^{-2j^{\prime}+2d_{i}-1})du_{i}
has zero residue. Thus, we see that
u_{i}^{-2j^{\prime}+d_{i}}\omega_{i,j}
has residue one if
j+j^{\prime}=d_{i}
and zero otherwise.
∎

Lemma 3.15

Assume
\sum_{i=1}^{m}(d_{i}+1)>4g_{X}-4

Proof.

Recall

when

. By modifying the de Rham class representative using these functions (i.e. replacing

\mathrm{ord}_{Q_{i}}(\pi^{*}(f_{i}))=2\mathrm{ord}_{P_{i}}(f_{i})\geq-(d_{i}+1)

, we may write

(15)

f_{i}=\sum_{l=\frac{d_{i}+1}{2}}^{0}c_{l}t_{i}^{-l}+O(1)\quad\text{and}\quad\pi^{*}(f_{i})=\sum_{l=-\frac{d_{i}+1}{2}}^{0}\left(c_{l}u_{i}^{2l}+O\left(u_{i}^{2l+d_{i}}\right)\right).

In particular,
\mathrm{ord}_{Q_{i}}(f_{i})\geq-(d_{i}+1)
.

If

We now show that any

4.2.
Final types

Recall that the Ekedahl-Oort type of a curve
C
is the isomorphism class of the polarized mod-
p
Dieudonné module
\HH_{\textrm{dR}}^{1}(C)
, or equivalently the isomorphism class of the group scheme
\text{Jac}(C)[p]
.
The final type is a combinatorial way to encode the Ekedahl-Oort type, which we will now recall. See Pries
[
Pri08
]
for additional information.

Let
N
be a polarized mod-
p
Dieudonné module (for instance
N=\HH_{\textrm{dR}}^{1}(C)
) with dimension
2g
as a
k
-vector space. A
final filtration
is a filtration

0\subset N_{1}\subset\cdots\subset N_{g}=V(N)\subset N_{g+1}\subset\ldots\subset N_{2g}=N

that is stable under
V
and
\perp
(symplectic complement) satisfying
\dim_{k}(N_{l})=l
. By
[
Oor01
, Lemma 5.2]
, one may require instead that the filtration is stable under
V
and
F^{-1}
, giving an equivalent definition. The
final type
is a string of integers defined by
\nu_{l}=\dim_{k}(V(N_{l}))
. Through the duality coming from the polarization, the first
g
elements of the final type determine the entire final type. Thus we declare the final type
\nu
of
N
to be

\nu=[\nu_{1},\cdots,\nu_{g}].

Oort proves that two polarized mod-
p
Dieudonné modules are isomorphic if and only if they have the same final type
[
Oor01
]
.

Definition 4.4
.

A
simple word
in
V
and
\perp
is a string of
V
’s and
\perp
’s that ends with
V
and does not contain consecutive copies of
\perp
. More concretely, simple words are of the form
V^{n_{t}}\bot V^{n_{t-1}}\ldots V^{n_{2}}\bot V^{n_{1}}
.

A simple word
w=V^{n_{t}}\bot V^{n_{t-1}}\ldots V^{n_{2}}\bot V^{n_{1}}
acts on subspaces
L\subset N
by

w(L)=V^{n_{t}}(\cdots(V^{n_{2}}((V^{n_{1}}(L))^{\perp})^{\perp})\cdots)

This is convenient for studying final types and the canonical filtration as knowing
\dim_{k}w(N)
for all simple words
w
determines the final type.

Remark 4.11
.

We can now prove the first part of Corollary
1.5
: consider covers of
\mathbf{P}^{1}
ramified at infinity with ramification break
d
. The genus of the cover is
g=(d-1)/2
by Riemann-Hurwitz, and the final type is
[0,1,1,2,2,\ldots,\left\lfloor g/2\right\rfloor]
by Theorem
4.6
. The dimension of the family can be recovered from
[

]
, or seen more directly as follows. By Artin-Schreier theory, any such Artin-Schreier cover can be written in the form

where

is a polynomial of degree

, and the extension of function fields is determined by
f(x)
up to adding something of the form
g^{2}+g
. Thus to obtain non-isomorphic extensions we may just restrict to polynomials

for which the coefficient of

is zero for any integer
i
. There are
(d+1)/2
unconstrained coefficients, giving a
(d+1)/2
-dimensional family of Artin-Schreier covers. However, two such covers of
\mathbf{P}^{1}
may be isomorphic without being isomorphic as covers of
\mathbf{P}^{1}
. As there is a two-dimensional family of automorphisms of
\mathbf{P}^{1}
which fix infinity, we obtain a
(d+1)/2-2
-dimensional family of Artin-Schreier curves.

Remark 4.12
.

By
[
Oor01

, the Ekedahl-Oort stratum with final type

inside

6.1.

As before, let

Recall from Section

Lemma 6.1

W_{\bot V^{n}w}\subseteq\pi_{T_{1}\oplus W}(V^{n}(M_{0}\oplus W_{w}^{L})^{\perp})
we see that

\mathrm{codim}_{k}\left(W_{\bot V^{n}w}\cap V^{n}(T_{0})^{\perp}\subseteq W_{\bot V^{n}w}\right)\leq c^{\prime}

Letting
Z=\pi_{T_{1}\oplus W}(V^{n}(M_{0}\oplus T_{0}\oplus W_{w}^{L})^{\perp})
, we estimate

\displaystyle\mathrm{codim}_{k}(W_{\bot V^{n}w}\subseteq W_{\bot V^{n}w}^{U})

\displaystyle\leq\dim_{k}Z-\dim_{k}(W_{\bot V^{n}w}\cap Z)

\displaystyle=\dim_{k}Z-\dim_{k}(W_{\bot V^{n}w}\cap V^{n}(T_{0})^{\perp})

\displaystyle\leq\left(\phi(\bot V^{n}w)+l_{X}+a_{X}^{n}+c-c^{\prime}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt-\left(\phi(\bot V^{n}w)-\mathrm{codim}_{k}\left(W_{\bot V^{n}w}\cap V^{n}(T_{0})^{\perp}\subseteq W_{\bot V^{n}w}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq c+l_{X}+a_{X}^{n}.

This gives the general claim about the codimension.

We now prove the sharper upper bound under the assumption
\sum_{i=1}^{m}(d_{i}+1)>4g_{X}-4
. Under this assumption, Lemma
3.15
yields

M\subseteq(M_{0}\oplus W_{V})^{\perp}\subseteq V^{n}(M_{0}\oplus W_{w}^{L})^{\perp}.

As a result, the inclusion in Equation (
31
) becomes an equality:

V^{n}(M_{0}\oplus W_{w}^{L})^{\perp}=M\oplus T_{0}\oplus\pi_{T_{1}\oplus W}(V^{n}(M_{0}\oplus W_{w}^{L})^{\perp}).

After computing the dimension of the left-hand side in the same way as before, we now get

\displaystyle\dim_{k}(\pi_{T_{1}\oplus W}(V^{n}(M_{0}\oplus W_{w}^{L})^{\perp}))

\displaystyle=\dim_{k}(V^{n}(M_{0}\oplus W_{w}^{L})^{\perp})-\dim_{k}(M\oplus T_{0})

\displaystyle=\dim_{k}(V^{n}(M_{0}\oplus W_{w}^{L})^{\perp})-3l_{X}

\displaystyle\leq a_{X}^{n}+\phi(\bot V^{n}w)+c.

Comparing this to Equation (
32
), the bound has decreased by
l_{X}
using the assumption
\sum_{i=1}^{m}(d_{i}+1)>4g_{X}-4
. From there on, the same argument building on Equation (
32
) shows that

\mathrm{codim}_{k}(W_{\bot V^{n}w}\subseteq W_{\bot V^{n}w}^{U})\leq c+a_{X}^{n}.\qed

Remark 6.6
.

In some cases, this bound can be slightly improved by using Tango’s theorem

]
. For this, let

be the Tango number of

\displaystyle M_{0}\subseteq V^{n}(M_{0}\oplus W_{V})

\displaystyle\subseteq V^{n}(M_{0}\oplus W_{w}^{L})

whence

\displaystyle\dim_{k}(V^{n}(M_{0}\oplus W_{w}^{L}))

In that case the bound on

is reduced by

Proposition 6.7
.

Let
w=\bot V^{n_{t}}\bot\ldots\bot V^{n_{1}}
be a simple word containing
t
instances of
\bot
. There exists

•

a space
W_{w}^{L}\supseteq W_{V}
that has codimension at most
\lfloor\tfrac{3t}{2}\rfloor l_{X}
inside
W_{w}
,

•

and a space
W_{w}^{U}\subseteq T_{1}\oplus W
in which

has codimension at most
\lceil\frac{3t}{2}\rceil l_{X}
,

such that the following holds:

M_{0}\oplus T_{0}\oplus W_{w}^{L}\subseteq w(L)\subseteq M\oplus T_{0}\oplus W_{w}^{U}.

Furthermore, if we additionally assume
\sum_{i=1}^{m}(d_{i}+1)>4g_{X}-4

The proof is by induction on

.

To make the base case easier, we write
\bar{w}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\bot V^{n_{t}}\bot\ldots V^{n_{1}-1}\bot V

Thus for
w=\bot V
we may set

, so both codimensions equal zero.

For the induction step, assume the statement holds for a simple word
w
containing
t
instances of
\bot
, and let
n>0
. Using

, Lemma
6.4
gives a space

.

Analogously, using Lemma

gives a space

as desired. If we additionally assume
\sum_{i=1}^{m}(d_{i}+1)>4g_{X}-4

∎

Lemma 6.8
.

Let
\eta=\omega_{0}+\omega_{1}y
be a (not necessarily regular) differential on

and let

be an integer. Assume
\mathrm{ord}_{P_{i}}(\omega_{0})\geq-n
and

Proof.

This is an adaptation in characteristic

of the proof of
[

, Theorem 8.3]
, with the difference that we no longer require
\eta
to be regular. The decomposition
\eta=\omega_{0}+\omega_{1}y
has implicitly fixed an equation
y^{2}+y=\psi
defining

V(\eta)=V(\omega_{0})+V(\omega_{1}y)=V(\omega_{0})+V(\omega_{1}(y^{2}+\psi))=V(\omega_{0})+V(\psi\omega_{1})+yV(\omega_{1})=0,

so that
V(\omega_{1})=0
and
V(\omega_{0})=V(\psi\omega_{1})

\mathrm{ord}_{P_{i}}(\omega_{0})\geq-n

(34)

\mathrm{ord}_{P_{i}}(V(\psi\omega_{1}))=\mathrm{ord}_{P_{i}}(V(\omega_{0}))\geq-\left\lfloor\frac{n+1}{2}\right\rfloor.

Now, we may pick a uniformizer

such that
\psi=c^{2}z_{i}^{-d_{i}}
for some

. Since

, there exists a function

such that

. Then we obtain

V(\psi\omega_{1})=V(c^{2}f_{1}^{2}z_{i}^{-d_{i}}z_{i})=cf_{1}z_{i}^{-\frac{d_{i}+1}{2}}dz_{i}.

Combining this with Equation (

\displaystyle\mathrm{ord}_{P_{i}}(f_{1})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{d_{i}+1}{2}-\left\lfloor\frac{n+1}{2}\right\rfloor=\left\lceil\frac{d_{i}-n}{2}\right\rceil,

and therefore

\displaystyle\mathrm{ord}_{P_{i}}(\omega_{1})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{ord}_{P_{i}}(f_{1}^{2}dz_{i})\geq 2\left\lceil\frac{d_{i}-n}{2}\right\rceil.\qed

Recall Equation (

Definition 6.10
.

Consider a simple word
w=V^{r}\bot V^{n_{t}}\bot\ldots\bot V^{n_{1}}=V^{r}\bot w^{\prime}

\displaystyle L_{2}(X,\pi,w)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\begin{cases}\phi(w)-\left\lfloor\frac{3t}{2}\right\rfloor l_{X}&\hbox{if $\sum_{i=1}^{m}(d_{i}+1)\leq 4g_{X}-4$ },\\

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}L_{1}(X,\pi,w)+L_{2}(X,\pi,w)+L_{3}(X,\pi,w).

Lemma 6.11
.

Consider a word
w=V^{r}\perp V^{n_{t}}\perp\ldots\perp V^{n_{1}}=V^{r}\perp w^{\prime}.
We have

\dim_{k}(w(L))\geq L(X,\pi,w).

Proof.

We use the lower bound from Proposition
6.7
to the word
\bot w^{\prime}
, which implies

w(L)=V^{r}\left(w^{\prime}(L)^{\perp}\right)\supseteq V^{r}\left(M_{0}\oplus W_{\perp w^{\prime}}^{L}\oplus T_{0}\right)=V^{r}(M_{0}\oplus W_{\perp w^{\prime}}^{L})+V^{r}(T_{0}).

where the subspace

satisfies

\mathrm{codim}_{k}(W_{\perp w^{\prime}}^{L}\subseteq W_{\perp w^{\prime}})\leq\begin{cases}\left\lfloor\frac{3t}{2}\right\rfloor l_{X}&\hbox{if $\sum_{i=1}^{m}(d_{i}+1)\leq 4g_{X}-4$ }\\

so that

6.3
. Note that

[
Tan72
]
and
[
Gro24

so that in either case

Finally, we analyze the contribution from
T_{0}

3.13
we have
\dim_{k}(\pi_{T_{0}}(V^{r}(T_{0})))=l_{X}-a_{X}^{r}
, which contributes to lower bound on

Consider a simple word

.

6.7

\displaystyle V^{r}(M\oplus W_{\perp w^{\prime}})

\displaystyle\dim_{k}(V^{r}(M\oplus W_{\perp w^{\prime}}^{U}))

Corollary 6.15

6.14

, we have

\displaystyle\delta_{L}^{r+1}-\delta_{L}^{r}

\displaystyle=(\delta^{r+1}-e_{1}^{r+1})-(\delta^{r}-e_{1}^{r})=(\delta^{r+1}-\delta^{r})+(e_{1}^{r}-e_{1}^{r+1})

\displaystyle\delta_{U}^{r+1}-\delta_{U}^{r}

\displaystyle=(\delta^{r+1}+e_{2}^{r+1})-(\delta^{r}+e_{2}^{r})=(\delta^{r+1}-\delta^{r})-(e_{2}^{r}-e_{2}^{r+1}).

Lemma 6.17
.

We have

\phi(V^{r+1}\widehat{w})-\phi(V^{r}\widehat{w})-2a_{X}^{r+1}\leq\delta^{r+1}-\delta^{r}\leq\phi(V^{r+1}\widehat{w})-\phi(V^{r}\widehat{w})+a_{X}^{r}.

Proof.

We first prove the inequality

(38)

\phi(V^{r}\widehat{w})+2(l_{X}-a_{X}^{r})\leq\delta^{r}\leq\phi(V^{r}\widehat{w})+2l_{X}.

The proof of the lower bound in Equation (
38
) is analogous to the proof of Lemma
6.11
. Namely, we observe

\displaystyle\delta^{r}=\dim_{k}(V^{r}(M_{0}\oplus T_{0}\oplus W_{\widehat{w}}))

\displaystyle\geq\dim_{k}(\pi_{M_{0}}(V^{r}(M_{0})))+\dim_{k}(\pi_{T_{0}}(V^{r}(T_{0})))+\dim_{k}(\pi_{W}(V^{r}(W_{\widehat{w}})))

\displaystyle=2(l_{X}-a_{X}^{r})+\phi(V^{r}\widehat{w}).

For the upper bound of Equation (
38
), we observe that
V^{r}(M_{0}\oplus W_{\widehat{w}})\subseteq M_{0}\oplus W_{V^{r}\widehat{w}}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\delta^{r}=\dim_{k}(V^{r}(M_{0}\oplus T_{0}\oplus W_{\widehat{w}}))

\displaystyle\leq\dim_{k}(M_{0}\oplus W_{V^{r}\widehat{w}})+\dim_{k}(T_{0})

\displaystyle=2l_{X}+\phi(V^{r}\widehat{w}).

The lower bound in the lemma follows immediately from subtracting Equation (
38
) from its analog for
\delta^{r+1}
. For the upper bound, observe that

\displaystyle\delta^{r+1}

\displaystyle=\dim_{k}(V^{r+1}(M_{0}\oplus W_{\widehat{w}}\oplus T_{0}))

\displaystyle\leq\dim_{k}(V^{r+1}(M_{0}\oplus W_{\widehat{w}}))+l_{X}

\displaystyle=\phi(V^{r+1}\widehat{w})+\dim_{k}(\pi_{M_{0}}(V^{r+1}(M_{0}\oplus W_{\widehat{w}})))+l_{X}

and that

\delta^{r}\geq\phi(V^{r}\widehat{w})+\dim_{k}(\pi_{M_{0}}(V^{r}(M_{0}\oplus W_{\widehat{w}})))+(l_{X}-a_{X}^{r}).

Then the lemma follows as
\dim_{k}(\pi_{M_{0}}(V^{r+1}(M_{0}\oplus W_{\widehat{w}})))\leq\dim_{k}(\pi_{M_{0}}(V^{r}(M_{0}\oplus W_{\widehat{w}})))
.
∎

Proposition 6.18
.

Suppose
w=V^{r}\bot V^{n_{t}}\bot\ldots\bot V^{n_{1}}
and
n=\dim_{k}(w(L))
. Then we have

n+\phi(Vw)-\phi(w)-2a_{X}^{r+1}-e_{2}^{0}\leq\dim_{k}(V(w(L)))\leq n+\phi(Vw)-\phi(w)+a_{X}^{r}+e_{1}^{0}.

Proof.

This follows from Lemmas
6.16
and
6.17
and the observation
0\leq e_{j}^{r}-e_{j}^{r+1}\leq e_{j}^{0}
.
∎

Proposition
6.18
provides bounds on
\dim_{k}(V(w(L))
in terms of
n
and
w
, assuming
n=\dim_{k}(w(L))
. This complements Proposition
6.14
, which gives bounds on
\dim_{k}(w(L))
for any word.

Theorem 6.19
.

Let
Y\to X
be a double cover with ramification invariants
d_{1},\ldots,d_{m}
. Let
w=V^{n_{s}}\bot\ldots\bot V^{n_{1}}
. Let
[\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{g_{Y}}]
be the final type of
Y
and set
l:=f_{Y}+\phi(w)+2l_{X}
. Then we have

\left|\nu_{l}-(f_{Y}+\phi(Vw)+l_{X})\right|\leq\begin{cases}\left\lceil\frac{3s+2}{2}\right\rceil l_{X}&\hbox{if $\sum_{i=1}^{m}(d_{i}+1)\leq 4g_{X}-4$ }\\
(s+1)l_{X}&\hbox{if $\sum_{i=1}^{m}(d_{i}+1)>4g_{X}-4$.}\end{cases}

Proof.

For ease of exposition, we assume
\sum_{i=1}^{m}(d_{i}+1)>4g_{X}-4
, so that Lemma
3.15
applies and the bounds in Proposition
6.14
are sharper. The case
\sum_{i=1}^{m}(d_{i}+1)\leq 4g_{X}-4
works analogously. Throughout this proof, we use the simplification
a_{X}^{r}\leq l_{X}
for every
r
.

Let
n=\dim_{k}(w(L))
and let
t=s-1
. Then Proposition
6.14
implies

\phi(w)-tl_{X}\leq L(X,\pi,w)\leq n=\dim_{k}(w(L))\leq U(X,\pi,w)\leq\phi(w)+(t+2)l_{X}.

Recalling Lemma
6.1
, note that
\nu_{f_{Y}+n}=f_{Y}+\dim_{k}(V(w(L)))
. We consider two cases.

•

Assume
f_{Y}+n\leq l
. Then Proposition
6.14
yields

\nu_{l}\geq\nu_{f_{Y}+n}\geq f_{Y}+L(X,\pi,Vw)\geq f_{Y}+\phi(Vw)-tl_{X}=(f_{Y}+\phi(Vw)+l_{X})-sl_{X},

which proves the lower bound of the theorem. For the upper bound, we use Proposition
6.18
and Equation (
36
):

\displaystyle\nu_{f_{Y}+n}

\displaystyle\leq f_{Y}+n+\phi(Vw)-\phi(w)+l_{X}+tl_{X}.

Now using that
\nu_{j}\leq\nu_{j+1}\leq\nu_{j}+1
we see

\displaystyle\nu_{l}

\displaystyle\leq l+\phi(Vw)-\phi(w)+(t+1)l_{X}.

Finally recalling that
l=f_{Y}+\phi(w)+2l_{X}
yields

\displaystyle\nu_{l}

\displaystyle\leq f_{Y}+\phi(Vw)+(t+3)l_{X}=(f_{Y}+\phi(Vw)+l_{X})+(s+1)l_{X}.

Thus the assertion is proven when
f_{Y}+n\leq l
.

•

Assume
f_{Y}+n>l
. Then Proposition
6.14
immediately yields the upper bound

\nu_{l}\leq\nu_{f_{Y}+n}\leq f_{Y}+U(X,\pi,Vw)\leq f_{Y}+\phi(Vw)+(t+2)l_{X}=(f_{Y}+\phi(Vw)+l_{X})+sl_{X}.

For the lower bound, Proposition
6.18
and Equation (
36
) show

\displaystyle\nu_{f_{Y}+n}

\displaystyle\geq f_{Y}+n+\phi(Vw)-\phi(w)-2l_{X}-(t+1)l_{X}.

Then we conclude that

\displaystyle\nu_{l}

\displaystyle\geq l+\phi(Vw)-\phi(w)-2l_{X}-(t+1)l_{X}

\displaystyle=f_{Y}+\phi(Vw)-(t+1)l_{X}=(f_{Y}+\phi(Vw)+l_{X})-(s+1)l_{X},

which finishes the proof. ∎

Remark 6.20
.

Note that Theorem
4.6
is a special case of Theorem
6.19
in which
X
is ordinary. More precisely, one recovers Corollary
4.10
, which is equivalent to Theorem
4.6
, by substituting
l_{X}=0
in Theorem
6.19
.

6.5.
One point covers

We now apply Theorem
6.19
to double covers that are branched at exactly one point. This leads to Theorem
6.22
. Let
Y\to X
be a double cover branched at one point with ramification break
d
.

One of the inputs of Theorem
6.19
is the word
w
that is needed to construct a space in the canonical filtration of a given size. The bounds are proportional to the number of occurrences of
\perp
in
w
. The following lemma will allow us to bound this number uniformly.

Lemma 6.21
.

Let
d
be a positive integer. Every integer
0\leq m\leq\frac{d-1}{2}
can be written as
m=\phi(d,w)
for some word
w=V^{n_{s}}\bot\ldots\bot V^{n_{1}}
with
s\leq\left\lceil\log_{2}\left(\frac{d-1}{2}\right)\right\rceil
.

Proof.

Let
M_{t}
be the maximal gap between consecutive values of
\phi(d,w)\leq\frac{d-1}{2}
for simple words
w
with at most
t
instances of
\bot
. (By consecutive we mean that there does not exist a word
w_{3}
of the same form such that
\phi(d,w_{1})<\phi(d,w_{3})<\phi(d,w_{2})
.) We will prove that
M_{t}
decreases at the appropriate rate as
t
increases.

Let
i=\phi(d,w_{1})
and
j=\phi(d,w_{2})
. Then recalling Definition
4.8
we see

\left\lfloor\frac{|i-j|}{2^{n}}\right\rfloor\leq|\phi(d,V^{n}\bot w_{1})-\phi(d,V^{n}\bot w_{2})|\leq\left\lceil\frac{|i-j|}{2^{n}}\right\rceil.

This implies
M_{t+1}\leq\left\lceil\frac{M_{t}}{2}\right\rceil
. Note that
M_{0}=\frac{d-1}{2}
, so that
M_{s}=1
when
s\geq\left\lceil\log_{2}\left(\frac{d-1}{2}\right)\right\rceil
.
∎

This lemma can be interpreted as follows: all spaces in the (unique) final filtration of
W
can be constructed using a word containing only a small number of applications of
\perp
. This provides restrictive bounds on the final type of
Y
, as is recorded by the following theorem.

Theorem 6.22
.

Let
Y\to X
be branched at one point with ramification invariant
d
. Let
f_{Y}=2f_{X}
be the
p
-rank of
Y
. Let
l_{X}=g_{X}-f_{X}
be the local rank of
X
. Denote the final type of
Y
by
[\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{g_{Y}}]
. Then the following restrictions hold:

(1)

For
1\leq l\leq f_{Y}
, we have
\nu_{l}=l
.

(2)

For
f_{Y}<l<f_{Y}+2l_{X},
we have
f_{Y}\leq\nu_{l}\leq l-1
.

(3)

For
f_{Y}+2l_{X}\leq l\leq g_{Y}
, we have

(39)

\left|\nu_{l}-\left(f_{Y}+\left\lfloor\frac{l-f_{Y}}{2}\right\rfloor\right)\right|\leq\begin{cases}\frac{3\lceil\log_{2}(d-1)\rceil}{2}l_{X}&\hbox{if $d\leq 4g_{X}-5$}\\
\lceil\log_{2}(d-1)\rceil l_{X}&\hbox{if $d>4g_{X}-5$.}\end{cases}

Proof.

For
1\leq l\leq f_{Y}
, the statement follows immediately from the fact
f_{Y}=\max\{j\;|\;\nu_{j}=j\}
.

This fact also implies
\nu_{f_{Y}+1}=f_{Y}
. Combining this with the rule
\nu_{j}\leq\nu_{j+1}\leq\nu_{j}+1
gives the statement for
f_{Y}<l<f_{Y}+2l_{X}
.

Finally, assume
f_{Y}+2l_{X}\leq l\leq g_{Y}=f_{Y}+2l_{X}+\frac{d-1}{2}
. Let
m:=l-f_{Y}-2l_{X}
, so that
0\leq m\leq\frac{d-1}{2}
. Then Lemma
6.21
implies that there exists a word
w=V^{n_{s}}\perp\ldots\perp V^{n_{1}}
with
s\leq\left\lceil\log_{2}\left(\frac{d-1}{2}\right)\right\rceil
such that
m=\phi(d,w)
and hence
l=f_{Y}+\phi(d,w)+2l_{X}
. We apply Theorem
6.19
to this word
w
, yielding

\left|\nu_{l}-(f_{Y}+\phi(Vw)+l_{X})\right|\leq\begin{cases}\frac{3\lceil\log_{2}(d-1)\rceil}{2}l_{X}&\hbox{if $d\leq 4g_{X}-5$ }\\
\lceil\log_{2}(d-1)\rceil l_{X}&\hbox{if $d>4g_{X}-5$.}\end{cases}

Here we have used
\log_{2}\left(\frac{d-1}{2}\right)+1=\log_{2}(d-1)
. The proof is concluded by observing

\phi(d,Vw)=\left\lfloor\frac{\phi(d,w)}{2}\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor\frac{l-f_{Y}-2l_{X}}{2}\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor\frac{l-f_{Y}}{2}\right\rfloor-l_{X}.\qed

Remark 6.23
.

The aim of Theorem
6.22
is to provide a uniform bound on the possible amount of variation of the final type of
Y
. The bounds will not be optimal for all
l
. For instance, when
l=f_{Y}+2l_{X}+\phi(d,w)
for a word
w=V^{n_{s}}\perp\ldots\perp V^{n_{1}}
with
s
much smaller than
\log_{2}(d-1)
, then Theorem
6.19
prescribes stricter bounds.

Remark 6.24
.

It is interesting to interpret Theorem
6.22
in the context when the base curve
X
is fixed and the ramification invariant
d
increases. The genus
g_{Y}=2g_{X}+\frac{d-1}{2}
grows linearly with
d
, while the interval allowed by the bounds grows logarithmically. Since

\displaystyle\lim_{d\to\infty}\frac{\log_{2}(d-1)l_{X}}{2g_{X}+\frac{d-1}{2}}=0,

it follows that, as
d
grows, the error in approximating
\nu_{l}
by
f_{Y}+\left\lfloor\frac{l-f_{Y}}{2}\right\rfloor
becomes negligible.

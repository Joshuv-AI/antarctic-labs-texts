On polarons and dimerons in the two-dimensional attractive Hubbard model
G. Pascual 1 , J. Boronat 1 , and K. Van Houcke 2

Abstract
A two-dimensional spin-up ideal Fermi gas interacting attractively with a spin-down impurity in the continuum undergoes, at zero temperature, a first-order phase transition from a polaron to a dimeron state. Here we study a similar system on a square lattice, by considering the attractive 2D Fermi-Hubbard model with a single spin-down and a finite filling fraction of spin-up fermions. We study polaron and dimeron quasi-particle properties via variational Ansatz up to one particle-hole excitation. Moreover, we develop a determinant diagrammatic Monte Carlo algorithm for this problem based on expansion in bare on-site coupling U U . This algorithm turns out to be sign-problem free at any filling of spin-up fermions, allowing one to sample very high diagram order (larger than 200 200 in our study) and to do simulations for large U / t U/t (we go up to U / t = − 20 U/t=-20 with t t the hopping strength). Both methods give qualitatively consistent results. With variational Ansatz we go to even larger on-site attraction. At very low spin-up filling fraction, we observe the polaron-to-dimeron transition, in agreement with the continuum case. Upon increasing the filling fraction, however, the transition shifts to higher values of | U | / t |U|/t and the transition disappears beyond a filling fraction of about 20 % 20\% . In this region, the polaron state always gives a lower energy and has a finite quasi-particle residue. Our findings are directly relevant to cold atom experiments with 2D optical lattices: a small and finite density of spin-down impurities in the ground state will form a superfluid at strong coupling at low spin-up filling fraction. Above some critical spin-up filling fraction, the system is expected to remain a normal Fermi liquid, even in the strong-coupling limit.

Model
— We consider the attractive Hubbard model for a 2D square lattice with
N
sites (
N=L\times L
, where
L
is the number of sites per dimension) filled with
N_{\uparrow}
spin-up fermions and a single spin-down impurity. The Hamiltonian reads

\displaystyle\hat{H}=-t\sum_{\sigma}\sum_{\langle\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\rangle}\left(\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{r},\sigma}\hat{c}^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime},\sigma}+h.c.\right)+U\sum_{\mathbf{r}}\hat{n}_{\mathbf{r},\uparrow}\hat{n}_{\mathbf{r},\downarrow}\;,

(1)

where
\sigma=\uparrow,\downarrow
,
\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{r},\sigma}
creates a spin-
\sigma
fermion on site
\mathbf{r}
, and
\hat{n}_{\mathbf{r},\sigma}=\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{r},\sigma}\hat{c}^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{\mathbf{r},\sigma}
the on-site number operator.
The first term describes hopping of fermions between nearest-neighbor sites with amplitude
t
, while the second term describes on-site attraction with amplitude
U<0
.
Note that the repulsive model is mapped onto the attractive one after particle-hole transformation of one spin-component.
We assume periodic boundary conditions and we set the lattice spacing to
1
.

Methods

\begin{split}\ket{P(\mathbf{Q}_{P})}=\>&\alpha_{0}\hat{c}^{{\dagger}}_{\mathbf{Q}_{P},\downarrow}\ket{\text{FS}_{N_{\uparrow}}}+\\

A priori, we wish to compute the energy of quasi-particles with zero center-of-mass momentum, i.e.
\mathbf{Q}_{P}=0

\mathbf{Q}_{M}=0

However, keeping the possibility of setting

By minimizing the energy

\left(E-E_{\mathbf{Q}_{P}}^{(0)}\right)\alpha_{0}=\frac{U}{N}\sum\limits_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{q}}{}^{{}^{\prime}}\alpha_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{q}}\>,

(4)

\left(E-E_{\mathbf{Q}_{P},\mathbf{k},\mathbf{q}}^{(1)}\right)\alpha_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{q}}=\frac{U}{N}\alpha_{0}+\frac{U}{N}\sum\limits_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}{}^{{}^{\prime}}\alpha_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime},\mathbf{q}}-\frac{U}{N}\sum\limits_{\mathbf{q^{\prime}}}{}^{{}^{\prime}}\alpha_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{q^{\prime}}}\>,

Terms in Eqs. (

In addition to the variational calculations, we also performed diagrammatic Monte Carlo simulations for the lattice polaron problem, in order to determine
the effect of higher number of particle-hole excitations.
To study the ground-state properties, we consider the single-particle propagator:

\displaystyle G_{\downarrow}(\mathbf{k},\tau)=-\theta(\tau)\bra{\text{FS}_{N_{\uparrow}}}\hat{c}^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{\mathbf{k},\downarrow}(\tau)\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k},\downarrow}(0)\ket{\text{FS}_{N_{\uparrow}}},

(8)

where operators are written in the imaginary-time Heisenberg picture,
\hat{c}^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{\mathbf{k},\downarrow}(\tau)=e^{\hat{K}\tau}\hat{c}_{\mathbf{k},\downarrow}e^{-\hat{K}\tau}

\hat{K}=\hat{H}-\sum\limits_{\sigma}\mu_{\sigma}\hat{N}_{\sigma}
, and with
\mu_{\sigma}
the chemical potentials for both spin components.
We developed and implemented a polaron determinant (PDet) algorithm
[
69
]
for evaluating the diagrammatic expansion in terms of bare

G_{\downarrow}

The algorithm works in position-imaginary time representation, where the sum of all diagram topologies for a given set of space-time coordinates of the interaction vertices is given by a single determinant
[
70

71

72
]
. For the polaron problem,
all diagrams generated in this way are automatically connected
[
69
]
. The expansion of the polaron propagator in powers of

is written as

\displaystyle G_{\downarrow}(X)

\displaystyle=G_{\downarrow}^{0}(X)+\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{X_{1}}\ldots\int_{X_{n}}U^{n}G_{\downarrow}^{0}(X_{1})

(9)

\displaystyle G_{\downarrow}^{0}(X_{2}-X_{1})\cdots G_{\downarrow}^{0}(X-X_{n})\penalty\ \det(\mathcal{M}^{(n)})\;,

with
\mathcal{M}^{(n)}
an

\mathcal{M}^{(n)}_{i,j}=G^{0}_{\uparrow}(X_{i}-X_{j})
, with
i,j=1,\ldots,n

is the order of the diagrams
We have used the notation
X=(\mathbf{r},\tau)
and
\int_{X}:=\sum\limits_{\mathbf{r}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}d\tau
.
The

are the non-interacting spin-
\sigma

9

stochastically by the Metropolis algorithm
[
73
]

Extracting the quasi-particle energy from
G_{\downarrow}
calculated by Monte Carlo typically requires fitting the propagator at large enough
\tau

[
28
,
29
,
30
]
. We propose here a new direct method to estimate the energy, without fitting and without calculating the self-energy. We follow a standard approach for quantum Monte Carlo calculations: given a trial state
|\psi_{T}\rangle
for the system,
the operator
e^{-\hat{K}\tau}
acts as a projector onto the ground state:

\displaystyle\frac{\bra{\psi_{T}}e^{-\hat{K}\tau/2}\hat{H}e^{-\hat{K}\tau/2}\ket{\psi_{T}}}{\bra{\psi_{T}}e^{-\hat{K}\tau}\ket{\psi_{T}}}\overset{\tau\to+\infty}{=}E\;.

(10)

This will yield the true ground-state energy as long as
\braket{\psi_{T}|\Psi_{0}^{N_{\uparrow}}}\neq 0
, with
|\Psi_{0}^{N_{\uparrow}}\rangle
the ground state of the interacting system.
If we choose the trial state to be
|\psi_{T}\rangle=\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k},\downarrow}|\text{FS}_{N_{\uparrow}}\rangle
, which is an eigenstate of the non-interacting Hamiltonian, then the energy estimator in the l.h.s. of Eq. (
10
) gives the energy

\displaystyle E^{N_{\uparrow}}(\mathbf{k},\tau)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle E^{N_{\uparrow}}_{\text{FS}}+\epsilon_{\mathbf{k},\downarrow}

(11)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{\bra{\text{FS}_{N_{\uparrow}}}\hat{c}^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{\mathbf{k},\downarrow}\hat{V}e^{-\hat{K}\tau}\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k},\downarrow}\ket{\text{FS}_{N_{\uparrow}}}}{\bra{\text{FS}_{N_{\uparrow}}}\hat{c}^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{\mathbf{k},\downarrow}e^{-\hat{K}\tau}\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k},\downarrow}\ket{\text{FS}_{N_{\uparrow}}}}\;,

with
\hat{V}
the on-site Hubbard interaction term and
\mathbf{k}
the total quasi-momentum.
The denominator is proportional to
G_{\downarrow}(\mathbf{k},\tau)
, which is calculated with PDet based on Eq. (
9

To evaluate the numerator, one needs to consider diagrams having an extra interaction vertex at imaginary time
\tau^{-}
. This can be achieved in the Monte Carlo algorithm by extending the configuration space with an extra sector where diagrams have such an extra interaction vertex. The numerator and denominator in Eq. (
11

Upon increasing
\rho_{\uparrow}
the critical
-U/t
shift to higher values and, ultimately, the transition disappears above a critical
\rho_{\uparrow}\simeq 0.2
.

One might ask why the transition disappears in the lattice beyond some filling fraction. An upper bound for the critical filling fraction can be obtained as follows. The dimeron energy is bounded by
E_{D}-U\geq\epsilon_{F}
with
\epsilon_{F}\equiv\epsilon(\mathbf{Q}_{\text{FS}})
the Fermi energy.
In addition, an upper bound to the polaron energy is obtained via the polaron Ansatz with following homogeneous choice for the variational parameters:

\alpha_{0}=\sqrt{\rho_{\uparrow}}
and
\alpha_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{q}}=1/(N\sqrt{\rho_{\uparrow}})
. This gives the upper bound
E_{P}-U\leq-4t\rho_{\uparrow}+(1-\rho_{\uparrow})12t
for the exact polaron energy
E_{P}
. The two bounds are equal for
\rho_{\uparrow}=\frac{3}{4}-\frac{\epsilon_{F}}{16t}\simeq 0.7
, thus showing that there can be no polaron-dimeron transition beyond this filling fraction. The bounds are essentially caused by Pauli-blocking and the reduced available phase space for particle-hole excitations upon increasing
\rho_{\uparrow}
.
Moreover, a particle-hole transformation for the spin-up component with density
\rho_{\uparrow}=1-\eta
maps the attractive model onto the repulsive one with a small density of
\eta
for spin-up. For the repulsive model, there is no dimeron ground state in this dilute regime.

Conclusion and outlook
— We have studied the quasi-particle properties of the Fermi polaron in a 2D lattice. To this end, we have developed a
sign-problem-free
PDet Monte Carlo algorithm based on the diagrammatic expansion in bare
U
, with a direct estimator for the quasi-particle energy.
In addition, we have done calculations based on variational Ansätze.
At low enough filling, we observe the polaron-dimeron transition, similar to the continuum case. However, this transition disappears upon increasing the filling fraction beyond a critical value close to
0.2
.
The absence of the transition is closely related to the increased Pauli-blocking and the reduced available phase space for particle-hole excitations when increasing
\rho_{\uparrow}
.
Our results are relevant for the ground-state phase diagram of the strongly polarized Fermi gas on the lattice. A small and finite density of spin-down impurities in the ground state will form a superfluid at strong coupling when
\rho_{\uparrow}
is low enough. Above some spin-up filling fraction, however, the system will remain a normal Fermi liquid, even in the strong-coupling limit. Determining the precise phase diagram of the highly spin-polarized system would be an interesting topic of future research.
Our findings are thus directly relevant to cold atom experiments with 2D optical lattices
[
74
]
, where impressive progress has been made recently to reach low temperatures
[
75
,
76
,
77
]
. In particular, the stability of the Fermi polaron at high values of
|U|/t
at filling fractions
\rho_{\uparrow}\gtrsim 0.2
could be verified experimentally.
We have also calculated the polaron residue, which remains finite, in agreement with early calculations by Sorella
[
48
]
.
The fact that our PDet algorithm is free of any sign problem was unexpected since there is no obvious symmetry guaranteeing a fixed sign. The implications for simulating a finite spin-down density is a topic we leave for future research.

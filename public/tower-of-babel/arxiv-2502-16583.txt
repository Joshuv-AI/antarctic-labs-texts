Guorong Qu, Jianzhong Lu, Wei Deng

Abstract




Abstract:

This paper investigates the zero-filter limit problem associated with the Camassa-Holm equation. In the work cited as
[
5
]
, it was established that, under the hypothesis of initial data
u_{0}\in B^{s}_{2,r}(\mathbb{R})
with
s>\frac{3}{2}
and
1\leq r<\infty
, the solutions
\mathbf{S}_{t}^{\mathbf{\alpha}}(u_{0})
of the Camassa-Holm equation exhibit convergence in the
L^{\infty}_{T}(B^{s}_{2,r})
norm to the unique solution of the Burgers equation as
\alpha\rightarrow 0
. Contrary to this result, the present study demonstrates that for initial data
u_{0}\in B^{s}_{2,\infty}(\mathbb{R})
the solutions of the Camassa-Holm equation fail to converge strongly in the
L^{\infty}_{T}(B^{s}_{2,\infty})
norm to the Burgers equation as
\alpha\rightarrow 0
.

Keywords:
Camassa-Holm equation; Burgers equation; zero-filter limit.

35Q35.

1
Introduction

In this work, we investigate the Camassa-Holm (CH) equation, which is expressed as

\displaystyle\begin{cases}u_{t}+u\partial_{x}u=-\partial_{x}\left(1-\alpha^{2}\partial_{x}^{2}\right)^{-1}\left(u^{2}+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}(\partial_{x}u)^{2}\right),\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\end{cases}

(1.1)

and can be equivalently reformulated as

\displaystyle\begin{cases}u_{t}+3u\partial_{x}u=-\alpha^{2}\partial^{3}_{x}\left(1-\alpha^{2}\partial_{x}^{2}\right)^{-1}(u^{2})-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}\partial_{x}\left(1-\alpha^{2}\partial_{x}^{2}\right)^{-1}(\partial_{x}u)^{2},\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x).\end{cases}

(1.2)

When the parameter
\alpha
vanishes, the CH equation simplifies to the Burgers equation

\displaystyle\begin{cases}u_{t}+3u\partial_{x}u=0,\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\end{cases}

(1.3)

which can be viewed as the non-viscous counterpart (
\nu=0
) of the well-known Burgers equation:

\partial_{t}u+u\partial_{x}u-\nu\partial_{xx}u=0.

Proposed by Burgers in the 1940s as a simplified model for turbulence, this equation emulates the Navier-Stokes equation through its representation of nonlinear advection and diffusion, albeit in a one-dimensional context without a pressure gradient to drive the flow. For further details, refer to
[
23
,
24
]
. The Burgers equation has been extensively studied since its inception and serves as a fundamental example of a PDE that evolves to produce shocks.

The CH equation was initially introduced in the study of hereditary symmetries as an integrable system
[
15
]
. It was later rediscovered by Camassa and Holm
[
4
]
as a model for shallow water wave dynamics, subsequently becoming widely recognized as the Camassa-Holm equation. The equation is completely integrable, possessing a bi-Hamiltonian structure
[
7
,
15
]
and an infinite number of conservation laws
[
4
,
15
]
. Among its most notable features are the peakon solutions, which take the form
ce^{-|x-ct|}
with
c>0
, and have garnered significant interest in the field of physics
[
9
,
26
]
. Another striking characteristic of the CH equation is the phenomenon of wave breaking, where the solution remains bounded while its slope becomes unbounded within a finite time frame
[
6
,
10
,
11
]
. The existence of global weak solutions and dissipative solutions has been extensively studied
[
2
,
3
,
27
]
, with further results available in the cited references.

Recent literature has focused on the well-posedness of the Camassa-Holm equation in Sobolev and Besov spaces. Li and Olver
[
18
,
25
]
demonstrated that the Cauchy problem (1.1) is locally well-posed for initial data
u_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
with
s>3/2
. Danchin
[
13
]
examined local well-posedness in Besov spaces, establishing well-posedness in
B^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R})
for
s>\max\{1+1/p,3/2\}
and
1\leq p\leq\infty,~1\leq r<\infty
, with the continuous dependence aspect demonstrated by Li and Yin
[
19
]
. For the critical case
s=3/2
, Danchin
[
14
]
confirmed local well-posedness in
B^{3/2}_{2,1}(\mathbb{R})
and ill-posedness in
B^{3/2}_{2,\infty}(\mathbb{R})
. In the critical space
B^{3/2}_{2,2}(\mathbb{R})=H^{3/2}(\mathbb{R})
, Guo et al.
[
17
]
demonstrated norm inflation and consequent ill-posedness in
B^{1+1/p}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R})
for
1\leq p\leq\infty,~1<r\leq\infty
using peakon solutions, thereby resolving the open problem posed by Danchin
[
14
]
.

The investigation into the zero-filter limit of the Camassa-Holm equation was initiated by Gui and Liu in
[
16
]
, who demonstrated that the solutions of the dissipative Camassa-Holm equation converge, at least locally, to those of the dissipative Burgers equation as the filtering parameter
\alpha
approaches zero within Sobolev spaces of lower regularity. More recently, Li et al.
[
21
]
established that, as
\alpha\to 0
, the solutions of the Camassa-Holm equation with fractional dissipation exhibit strong convergence in the space
L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{s}(\mathbb{R}))
toward the inviscid Burgers equation for any initial data
u_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
with
s>3/2
and for some
T>0
. In contrast, the authors of
[
22
]
further revealed that, for initial data
u_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
with
s>3/2
and for some
T>0
, the solutions of the CH equation fail to converge uniformly with respect to the initial data in
L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{s}(\mathbb{R}))
to the inviscid Burgers equation as
\alpha\to 0
. In a recent publication
[
5
]
, these findings were extended to more general Besov spaces
B^{s}_{2,r}(\mathbb{R})
with
s>\frac{3}{2}
and
1\leq r<\infty
. Aligning with this research direction, the aim of this work is to investigate the zero-filter limit for the Camassa-Holm equation with initial data
u_{0}
belonging to the Besov spaces
B^{s}_{2,\infty}(\mathbb{R})
. The primary result of this study is formulated as follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that
s>\frac{3}{2}
and
\alpha\in[0,1]
. Let
\mathbf{S}_{t}^{\mathbf{\alpha}}(v_{0})
be the solutions of (
1.1
) with the initial data
v_{0}\in B^{s}_{2,\infty}(\mathbb{R})
. Then there exist a initial data
u_{0}\in B^{s}_{2,\infty}(\mathbb{R})
and a positive constant
\eta_{0}
such that

\left\|\mathbf{S}_{t_{n}}^{\alpha_{n}}(u_{0})-\mathbf{S}_{t_{n}}^{0}(u_{0})\right\|_{B^{s}_{2,\infty}}\geq\eta_{0},

with
\alpha_{n}\rightarrow 0
and
t_{n}\rightarrow 0
.

It should be noted that this finding underscores the critical dependence of solution convergence on the regularity and structure of the initial data, thereby contributing to a deeper understanding of the limiting behavior of solutions to nonlinear partial differential equations.

Proof of the main theorem

In this section, we aim to demonstrate that the zero-filter limit associated with the Camassa-Holm equation fails to converge with respect to the initial conditions specified in the Besov space
B^{s}_{2,\infty}(\mathbb{R})

\mathbf{S}_{t}^{\mathbf{\alpha}}(v_{0})
, where
v_{0}\in B^{s}_{2,\infty}(\mathbb{R})

\alpha
within the interval
[0,1]

.

For
s>\frac{3}{2}

\alpha\in[0,1]

1.1

\displaystyle||\mathbf{S}_{t}^{\mathbf{\alpha}}(v_{0})||_{L^{\infty}_{T}(B_{2,\infty}^{s})}\leq C||v_{0}||_{B_{2,\infty}^{s}},\quad\forall\alpha\in[0,1].

.

Note that

\displaystyle\mathbf{S}^{\alpha_{n}}_{t}(u_{0})=u_{0}+\underbrace{\mathbf{S}^{\alpha_{n}}_{t}(u_{0})-u_{0}-t\mathbf{E}_{0}(\alpha_{n},u_{0})}_{=:\,\mathbf{I}_{1}}+t\mathbf{E}_{0}(\alpha_{n},u_{0}),

\displaystyle\mathbf{S}^{0}_{t}(u_{0})=u_{0}+\underbrace{\mathbf{S}^{0}_{t}(u_{0})-u_{0}-t\mathbf{E}_{0}(0,u_{0})}_{=:\,\mathbf{I}_{2}}+t\mathbf{E}_{0}(0,u_{0}),

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{0}(\alpha_{n},u_{0})-\mathbf{E}_{0}(0,u_{0})=\underbrace{\alpha^{2}_{n}\partial^{2}_{x}\left(1-\alpha^{2}_{n}\partial_{x}^{2}\right)^{-1}(u_{0}\partial_{x}u_{0})}_{=:\,\mathbf{J}_{1}}-\underbrace{\frac{\alpha^{2}_{n}}{2}\partial_{x}\left(1-\alpha^{2}_{n}\partial_{x}^{2}\right)^{-1}(\partial_{x}u_{0})^{2}}_{=:\,\mathbf{J}_{2}},

we deduce from Proposition
3.2
, Lemma
2.1
and Lemma
2.6
that

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{S}^{\alpha_{n}}_{t}(u_{0})-\mathbf{S}^{0}_{t}(u_{0})\right\|_{B^{s}_{2,\infty}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle~t2^{ns}\|\Delta_{n}[\mathbf{E}_{0}(\alpha_{n},u_{0})-\mathbf{E}_{0}(0,u_{0})]\|_{L^{2}}-2^{ns}\left\|\Delta_{n}\mathbf{I}_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}}-2^{ns}\left\|\Delta_{n}\mathbf{I}_{2}\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle~t2^{ns}\|\Delta_{n}\mathbf{J}_{1}\|_{L^{2}}-t2^{ns}\|\Delta_{n}\mathbf{J}_{2}\|_{L^{2}}-2^{2n}\left\|\mathbf{I}_{1}\right\|_{B^{s-2}_{2,\infty}}-2^{2n}\left\|\mathbf{I}_{2}\right\|_{B^{s-2}_{2,\infty}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle~t2^{ns}\|\Delta_{n}\mathbf{J}_{1}\|_{L^{2}}-||\mathbf{J}_{2}||_{B^{s}_{2,\infty}}-C2^{2n}t^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle~t2^{ns}\|\Delta_{n}\mathbf{J}_{1}\|_{L^{2}}-C\alpha_{n}||(\partial_{x}u_{0})^{2}||_{B^{s-1}_{2,\infty}}-C2^{2n}t^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle~t2^{ns}\|\Delta_{n}\mathbf{J}_{1}\|_{L^{2}}-C2^{-n}-Ct^{2}2^{2n}.

(3.9)

Applying Plancherel’s identity, we deduce the following equivalence:

\displaystyle\left\|\Delta_{n}[\alpha^{2}_{n}\partial^{2}_{x}\left(1-\alpha^{2}_{n}\partial_{x}^{2}\right)^{-1}(u_{0}\partial_{x}u_{0})]\right\|_{L^{2}}\approx\|\Delta_{n}(u_{0}\partial_{x}u_{0})\|_{L^{2}},

which, in conjunction with (
3
), leads to the inequality:

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{S}^{\alpha_{n}}_{t}(u_{0})-\mathbf{S}^{0}_{t}(u_{0})\right\|_{B^{s}_{2,\infty}}\geq ct2^{ns}\|\Delta_{n}(u_{0}\partial_{x}u_{0})\|_{L^{2}}-C2^{-n}-Ct^{2}2^{2n}.

(3.10)

Next, we shall estimate the norm
2^{ns}\|\Delta_{n}(u_{0}\partial_{x}u_{0})\|_{L^{2}}
. By Lemma
2.3
, we have

\displaystyle 2^{ns}\|\Delta_{n}(u_{0}\partial_{x}u_{0})\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=2^{ns}\|u_{0}\partial_{x}\Delta_{n}u_{0}+[\Delta_{n},u_{0}]\partial_{x}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq 2^{{n}s}\|u_{0}\partial_{x}\Delta_{n}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}-2^{{n}s}\|[\Delta_{n},u_{0}]\partial_{x}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq 2^{{n}s}\|u_{0}\partial_{x}\Delta_{n}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}-C||u_{0}||^{2}_{B^{s}_{2,\infty}}.

(3.11)

Since

\displaystyle\Delta_{n}u_{0}(x)

\displaystyle=2^{-ns}\phi(x)\cos\Big(\frac{17}{12}2^{n}x\Big),

we find that

\displaystyle\partial_{x}\Delta_{n}u_{0}

\displaystyle=2^{-ns}\phi^{\prime}(x)\cos\Big(\frac{17}{12}2^{n}x\Big)-\frac{17}{12}2^{n}2^{-ns}\phi(x)\sin\Big(\frac{17}{12}2^{n}x\Big).

Thus,

\displaystyle u_{0}\partial_{x}\Delta_{n}u_{0}

\displaystyle=2^{-ns}u_{0}(x)\phi^{\prime}(x)\cos\Big(\frac{17}{12}2^{n}x\Big)-\frac{17}{12}2^{n}2^{-ns}u_{0}(x)\phi(x)\sin\Big(\frac{17}{12}2^{n}x\Big).

Observe that
u_{0}(x)
is a real-valued and continuous function defined on
\mathbb{R}
. Consequently, there exists some
\delta>0
such that

\displaystyle|u_{0}(x)|\geq\frac{1}{2}|u_{0}(0)|=\frac{1}{2}\phi(0)\sum\limits^{\infty}_{n=0}2^{-ns}=\frac{2^{s}\phi(0)}{2(2^{s}-1)}\quad\text{ for any }x\in B_{\delta}(0).

(3.12)

Therefore, it follows from (
3.12
) that

\displaystyle\|u_{0}\partial_{x}\Delta_{n}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq c2^{n}2^{-ns}\Big\|\phi(x)\sin\Big(\frac{17}{12}2^{n}x\Big)\Big\|_{L^{2}(B_{\delta}(0))}-C2^{-ns}\Big\|\phi^{\prime}(x)\phi(x)\cos\Big(\frac{17}{12}2^{n}x\Big)\Big\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq(c2^{n}-C)2^{-ns}.

By selecting
n
sufficiently large so that
C<c2^{n-1}
, we derive the following lower bound:

\displaystyle\|u_{0}\partial_{x}\Delta_{n}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\geq c2^{-n(s-1)}.

(3.13)

Plugging (
3
) and (
3.13
) into (
3.10
), we have for
t\in[0,T]

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{S}^{\alpha_{n}}_{t}(u_{0})-\mathbf{S}^{0}_{t}(u_{0})\right\|_{B^{s}_{2,\infty}}\geq ct2^{n}-Ct-C2^{-n}-Ct^{2}2^{2n}.

Then, taking large
n>N
such that
c2^{n}\geq 2C
, we obtain the following inequality for
t\in[0,T]

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{S}^{\alpha_{n}}_{t}(u_{0})-\mathbf{S}^{0}_{t}(u_{0})\right\|_{B^{s}_{2,\infty}}\geq ct2^{n}-C2^{-n}-Ct^{2}2^{2n}.

(3.14)

By selecting
t_{n}=\varepsilon 2^{-n}
with a sufficiently small
\varepsilon
, and choosing
n>N
large enough such that
C2^{-n}\leq\varepsilon^{2}
, we obtain from (
3.14
)

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{S}^{\alpha_{n}}_{t_{n}}(u_{0})-\mathbf{S}^{0}_{t_{n}}(u_{0})\right\|_{B^{s}_{2,\infty}}\geq c\varepsilon-C\varepsilon^{2}\geq c_{0}\varepsilon.

This completes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

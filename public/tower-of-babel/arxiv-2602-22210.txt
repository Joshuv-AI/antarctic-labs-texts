Abstract
. On a compact connected Lie group G G , we study the global solvability and the cohomology spaces of the differential complex associated with an essentially real involutive structure that is invariant under left translations. We prove that solvability in the first degree of the complex implies solvability in all other degrees, and furnish a converse for this fact under a certain commutativity hypothesis (that always holds when G G is a torus). Additionally, it is proved that the solvability holds when the structure comes from the Lie algebra of a closed subgroup of G G . We also investigate real tube structures when G G is the base manifold.

1.
Introduction

Let
\Omega
be a smooth manifold and
\mathcal{V}
an involutive subbundle of
\mathbb{C}T\Omega
, the complexified tangent bundle of
\Omega
. Involutivity means that the Lie bracket of any two sections of
\mathcal{V}
(which are, in particular, complex vector fields over
\Omega
) is again a section of
\mathcal{V}
. Such a situation arises in several instances of interest, but particularly in complex geometry: if
\Omega
is a complex manifold, and
\mathcal{V}
is spanned by the antiholomorphic vector fields; or if
\Omega
is a real hypersurface in a complex manifold, and
\mathcal{V}
is spanned by the antiholomorphic vector fields which are tangent to
\Omega
. The latter situation is the object of study of CR geometry, and is concerned with phenomena such as extendability of holomorphic functions across the hypersurface
\Omega
, thus playing a key role in the function theory of the ambient manifold.

Another situation of this kind, more intimately related to our own, arises in the theory of foliations. In PDE terminology, we will assume that
\mathcal{V}
is
essentially real
, meaning simply that
\mathcal{V}
is the complexification of an involutive subbundle of
T\Omega
; i.e., an integrable distribution on
\Omega
. That distribution, in turn, produces, by means of the Frobenius Theorem, a foliation on
\Omega
whose leaves are tangent to
\mathcal{V}
.

In either case, to such an involutive bundle
\mathcal{V}
, a general abstract construction (see
[
8
,
18
]
) gives rise to a complex of vector bundles over
\Omega
and first-order differential operators between them

(1.1)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(\Omega;\underline{\Lambda}^{q-1})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(\Omega;\underline{\Lambda}^{q})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(\Omega;\underline{\Lambda}^{q+1})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{d}^{\prime}}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{d}^{\prime}}

which encodes important information of the underlying geometry. In each of the three cases discussed above, such a construction yields: the Dolbeault complex, in complex geometry; the
\bar{\partial}_{b}
complex, in CR geometry; and the foliated de Rham complex, in foliation theory.

In the present paper, our objects of interest are the cohomology spaces
H^{q}_{\mathrm{d}^{\prime}}(\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(\Omega))
of the complex (
1.1
) when
\mathcal{V}
is essentially real and
\Omega
is compact. Except when
\mathcal{V}=\mathbb{C}T\Omega
– in which case (
1.1

1.1
) is not elliptic. Therefore, one cannot expect
H^{q}_{\mathrm{d}^{\prime}}(\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(\Omega))
to be finite dimensional, so we compromise on a more modest goal. Notice that all the spaces in (

are continuous, hence the

inherit a quotient topology, making them topological vector spaces: our task will be to determine necessary or sufficient conditions for
H^{q}_{\mathrm{d}^{\prime}}(\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(\Omega))
to be Hausdorff.

From the point of view of the global analysis of PDEs, the latter property is central, and is called
global solvability in degree

\mathrm{d}^{\prime}:\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(\Omega;\underline{\Lambda}^{q-1})\to\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(\Omega;\underline{\Lambda}^{q})

Left-invariant structures on compact Lie groups

The case to which we dedicated most of our work is when the manifold is a Lie group and
\mathcal{V}
is invariant under left translations (hence called a
left-invariant subbundle
).
In this context, let
G
be a compact, connected Lie group and
\mathcal{V}\subset\mathbb{C}TG
be an essentially real, left-invariant subbundle. Our first main result is the following:

Theorem 1
.

If

(1.2)

\mathrm{d}^{\prime}:\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(G)\longrightarrow\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(G;\underline{\Lambda}^{1})\quad\text{has closed range}

then

\mathrm{d}^{\prime}:\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(G;\underline{\Lambda}^{q-1})\longrightarrow\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(G;\underline{\Lambda}^{q})\quad\text{has closed range}

for all
1\leq q\leq n\doteq\rank(\mathcal{V})
. Moreover, in that case, we have

(1.3)

H^{q}_{\mathrm{d}^{\prime}}(\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(G))\cong\mathscr{K}(G)\otimes H^{q}_{\mathrm{d}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{C}_{G}),\quad 0\leq q\leq n,

topologically, where:

•

\mathscr{K}(G)
is the space of homogeneous solutions of
\mathcal{V}
: an
f\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(G)
belongs to
\mathscr{K}(G)
if and only if
\mathrm{L}f=0
whenever
\mathrm{L}
is a smooth section of
\mathcal{V}
; and

•

H^{q}_{\mathrm{d}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{C}_{G})
is the space of left-invariant classes
2
in
H^{q}_{\mathrm{d}^{\prime}}(\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(G))
.

Results regarding global solvability at intermediate levels (

) are scarce compared to the first level of the complex. Our result was possible thanks to a decomposition at the level of the cohomology groups done in Section

Properly interpreted, the spirit of Theorem

can be traced back to
[

11

(more on that later on). These two works were the major conceptual inspiration for our results: on a product of tori, by using a normalization process (Section

[

(1)

When
\mathcal{V}
is
globally hypoelliptic
, that is: any distribution

such that
\mathrm{L}f\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(G)

is already smooth to start with. This is the same as global hypoellipticity of the differential operator

When the leaves of the foliation generated by

Our Theorem

[

asserting the existence of left-invariant representatives of de Rham cohomology classes on

When

. In our setting, Theorem

provides a partial converse under the additional assumption that the Lie algebra

Tube structures

Let
M
be a compact, connected manifold, and
\mathbb{T}^{m}\doteq\mathbb{R}^{m}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}^{m}
be the
m
-dimensional torus. There is a particular way to induce an involutive structure on
\Omega\doteq M\times\mathbb{T}^{m}
, called a (corank
m
)
tube structure
, which we now describe. Let
\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{m}
be smooth, closed
1
-forms on
M
, and take
\mathcal{V}\subset\mathbb{C}T(M\times\mathbb{T}^{m})
the subbundle whose sections are vector fields annihilated by all the
1
-forms

\zeta_{k}\doteq\mathrm{d}x_{k}-\omega_{k},\quad k\in\{1,\ldots,m\};

here,
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})
are the usual coordinates on
\mathbb{T}^{m}
. The global solvability of the differential complex (
1.1
) associated with a tube structure is a question of great interest (see for instance
[
2
,
5
,
6
,
13
,
14
]
or
[
10
]
for non-compact manifolds).

If
M
is a Lie group (with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{m}
) then so is
G\doteq M\times\mathbb{T}^{m}
(with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{m}\oplus\mathbb{R}^{m}
), and
\mathcal{V}
is essentially real and left-invariant if and only if
\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{m}
are real-valued and left-invariant. While this is still not quite in the context of Theorem
1
, we prove in Corollary
7.3
that every real tube structure on
G
is equivalent to a left-invariant one by means of a diffeomorphism. Since the differential complex (
1.1
) is an invariant functorially attached to
\mathcal{V}
, by means of this “normalization” we can replace the original tube structure by a much simpler one (of “constant coefficients”, in a certain sense), and take full advantage of the symmetries of
G
to analyze it: for now the whole machinery of Theorem
1
is available.

We exploit this to investigate a question raised in a previous paper
[
2
]
. There, while looking for a certain representation formula for
H^{q}_{\mathrm{d}^{\prime}}(\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(\Omega))
for tube structures (akin to the one presented in
[
11
]
when
M
is a torus), we found an obstruction, which vanishes in certain cases (e.g., the torus), but not always: we then conjectured that the obstruction should vanish when
M
is a Lie group (hence, the said representation formula does hold).

In the present paper, our last main result is the proof of this conjecture (Corollary
7.5
). These questions are explained in details in Section
7.2
, and settled in Section
7.3
via perturbations of the de Rham complex by closed
1
-forms.

in
L^{2}(G)
. Therefore,
\mathscr{K}(G)\perp\bar{\mathscr{R}}(G)
as well: we must prove that their sum is
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(G)
.

Let
\Delta_{G}\doteq\mathrm{d}^{*}\mathrm{d}
be the Laplace-Beltrami operator, acting on functions, associated to our metric on
G
. The following claims regarding its spectral theory are well-known: the spectrum
\sigma(\Delta_{G})
is countable; the eigenspaces
E_{\lambda}\subset\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(G)
associated to eigenvalues
\lambda\in\sigma(\Delta_{G})
are finite dimensional; and

(3.3)

f=\sum_{\lambda\in\sigma(\Delta_{G})}\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}(f)

for every
f\in L^{2}(G)
, where
\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}:L^{2}(G)\to E_{\lambda}
denotes the orthogonal projection. In a Paley-Wiener-like fashion – a consequence of Weyl’s law – smoothness of
f
is characterized by the following condition: for each
s>0
there exists
C_{s}>0
such that

(3.4)

\|\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}(f)\|_{L^{2}(G)}\leq C_{s}(1+\lambda)^{-s},\quad\forall\lambda\in\sigma(\Delta_{G}),

in which case the series (
3.3
) actually converges in
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(G)
.

Since every
\mathrm{X}\in\mathfrak{g}
is skew-symmetric with respect to the
L^{2}
inner product on functions, one easily shows that
\Delta_{G}=\Delta_{\mathfrak{g}}
(for any choice of orthonormal basis of
\mathfrak{g}
), hence
\Delta_{G}
commutes with any
\mathrm{X}\in\mathfrak{g}
(Lemma
2.1
), and then also with
\Delta_{\mathfrak{v}}
. In particular,
\Delta_{\mathfrak{v}}(E_{\lambda})\subset E_{\lambda}
for every
\lambda\in\sigma(\Delta_{G})
: we define

\mathscr{K}_{\lambda}\doteq\ker\left\{\Delta_{\mathfrak{v}}:E_{\lambda}\longrightarrow E_{\lambda}\right\},\quad\mathscr{R}_{\lambda}\doteq\ran\left\{\Delta_{\mathfrak{v}}:E_{\lambda}\longrightarrow E_{\lambda}\right\}.

From (
3.2
) we further infer that
E_{\lambda}=\mathscr{K}_{\lambda}\oplus\mathscr{R}_{\lambda}
as a sum of orthogonal, finite dimensional subspaces.

Given
f\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(G)
, using the
L^{2}
-orthogonality we are entitled to decompose uniquely
\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}(f)=\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}(f)_{\mathscr{K}}+\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}(f)_{\mathscr{R}}
in
\mathscr{K}_{\lambda}\oplus\mathscr{R}_{\lambda}
, for each
\lambda\in\sigma(\Delta_{G})
. It follows from the Paley-Wiener Theorem above that both series

f_{\mathscr{K}}\doteq\sum_{\lambda\in\sigma(\Delta_{G})}\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}(f)_{\mathscr{K}},\quad f_{\bar{\mathscr{R}}}\doteq\sum_{\lambda\in\sigma(\Delta_{G})}\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}(f)_{\mathscr{R}}

converge in
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(G)
. Clearly,
f=f_{\mathscr{K}}+f_{\bar{\mathscr{R}}}
and
f_{\mathscr{K}}\perp f_{\bar{\mathscr{R}}}
. On the one hand, it is clear that

\Delta_{\mathfrak{v}}(f_{\mathscr{K}})=\Delta_{\mathfrak{v}}\Big(\sum_{\lambda\in\sigma(\Delta_{G})}\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}(f)_{\mathscr{K}}\Big)=\sum_{\lambda\in\sigma(\Delta_{G})}\Delta_{\mathfrak{v}}\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}(f)_{\mathscr{K}}=0

i.e.,
f_{\mathscr{K}}\in\mathscr{K}(G)
. On the other hand, by definition of
\mathscr{R}_{\lambda}
, we can solve

\Delta_{\mathfrak{v}}u_{\lambda}=\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}(f)_{\mathscr{R}},\quad u_{\lambda}\in E_{\lambda},

for each
\lambda\in\sigma(\Delta_{G})
, hence

f_{\bar{\mathscr{R}}}=\sum_{\lambda\in\sigma(\Delta_{G})}\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}(f)_{\mathscr{R}}=\lim_{\nu\to\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\in\sigma(\Delta_{G})\\
|\lambda|\leq\nu\end{subarray}}\Delta_{\mathfrak{v}}u_{\lambda}

with convergence in
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(G)
: we conclude that
f_{\bar{\mathscr{R}}}\in\bar{\mathscr{R}}(G)
, since as each truncated sum above belongs to
\mathscr{R}(G)
.

Finally, to check continuity of the projections
\pi_{\mathscr{K}},\pi_{\bar{\mathscr{R}}}
(we endow
\mathscr{K}(G),\bar{\mathscr{R}}(G)
with their subspace topologies inherited from
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(G)
, which turn them into Fréchet spaces), we make use of adapted Sobolev norms: for
f\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(G)
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, let

\|f\|_{\mathscr{H}^{k}(G)}\doteq\|(I+\Delta_{G})^{k}f\|_{L^{2}(G)}=\Big(\sum_{\lambda\in\sigma(\Delta_{G})}(1+\lambda)^{2k}\|\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}(f)\|_{L^{2}(G)}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}};

they generate the topology of
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(G)
and, given
\mathscr{F}\in\{\mathscr{K},\bar{\mathscr{R}}\}
, it is clear that

\|\pi_{\mathscr{F}}(f)\|_{\mathscr{H}^{k}(G)}^{2}\leq\|\pi_{\mathscr{K}}(f)\|_{\mathscr{H}^{k}(G)}^{2}+\|\pi_{\bar{\mathscr{R}}}(f)\|_{\mathscr{H}^{k}(G)}^{2}=\|f\|_{\mathscr{H}^{k}(G)}^{2},

from which continuity of
\pi_{\mathscr{F}}
is immediate.
∎

We extend the previous result to forms:

Corollary 3.2
.

We have
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(G;\underline{\Lambda}^{q})=\mathscr{K}(G;\underline{\Lambda}^{q})\oplus\bar{\mathscr{R}}(G;\underline{\Lambda}^{q})
as a sum of closed subspaces. The associated projections
\pi_{\mathscr{K}},\pi_{\bar{\mathscr{R}}}
are continuous, and commute with
\mathrm{d}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

It is clear that
\mathscr{K}(G;\underline{\Lambda}^{q})\cap\bar{\mathscr{R}}(G;\underline{\Lambda}^{q})=\{0\}
due to their coefficientwise definition. We write
u\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(G;\underline{\Lambda}^{q})
as

u=\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{|J|=q}u_{J}\ \tau_{J}=\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{|J|=q}\left(\pi_{\mathscr{K}}(u_{J})+\pi_{\bar{\mathscr{R}}}(u_{J})\right)\ \tau_{J},

hence
u=\pi_{\mathscr{K}}(u)+\pi_{\bar{\mathscr{R}}}(u)
, where

\pi_{\mathscr{F}}(u)\doteq\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{|J|=q}\pi_{\mathscr{F}}(u_{J})\tau_{J}\in\mathscr{F}(G;\underline{\Lambda}^{q}),\quad\mathscr{F}\in\{\mathscr{K},\bar{\mathscr{R}}\}.

All the claims are now obvious. For the last one, notice that
\mathrm{d}^{\prime}u=\mathrm{d}^{\prime}\pi_{\mathscr{K}}(u)+\mathrm{d}^{\prime}\pi_{\bar{\mathscr{R}}}(u)
, where

\mathrm{d}^{\prime}\pi_{\mathscr{F}}(u)\in\mathscr{F}(G;\underline{\Lambda}^{q+1}),\quad\mathscr{F}\in\{\mathscr{K},\bar{\mathscr{R}}\},

as both
\mathscr{K}(G),\bar{\mathscr{R}}(G)
are
\mathfrak{v}
-invariant spaces. Uniqueness in the direct sum decomposition
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(G;\underline{\Lambda}^{q+1})=\mathscr{K}(G;\underline{\Lambda}^{q+1})\oplus\bar{\mathscr{R}}(G;\underline{\Lambda}^{q+1})
shows that
\mathrm{d}^{\prime}\pi_{\mathscr{F}}(u)=\pi_{\mathscr{F}}(\mathrm{d}^{\prime}u)
.
∎

Proposition 4.1
.

The range of
\mathrm{d}^{\prime}:\mathscr{K}(G;\underline{\Lambda}^{q})\to\mathscr{K}(G;\underline{\Lambda}^{q+1})
is always closed. That is:
H^{q}_{\mathrm{d}^{\prime}}(\mathscr{K}(G))

By means of (

Theorem 5.1

Theorem 5.4
.

Assume that
\mathfrak{v}

The key algebraic aspect of Abelian structures is the simplification of the expression (

\mathrm{d}^{\prime}
thanks to the vanishing of the structural constants
\alpha_{JK}
. Indeed, by (
4.3

\alpha_{JK}=(\mathrm{d}^{\prime}\tau_{J})(\mathrm{L}_{k_{0}},\ldots,\mathrm{L}_{k_{q}})=0

thanks to (
2.4
), the duality relations between
\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{n}
and
\mathrm{L}_{1},\ldots,\mathrm{L}_{n}
, and the fact that the latter is a family of pairwise commuting vector fields (our hypothesis). Therefore, if we write
u\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(G;\underline{\Lambda}^{q})
in its canonical form (
2.1
), then

(5.2)

\mathrm{d}^{\prime}u=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{|J|=q}(\mathrm{L}_{j}u_{J})\ \tau_{j}\wedge\tau_{J}.

The proof of the theorem starts by assuming, otherwise, that the range of
\Delta_{\mathfrak{v}}:\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(G)\to\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(G)
is not closed. By results in
[
1
]
, given
C,\rho>0
there exist
\lambda\in\sigma(\Delta_{G})
and
\phi\in E_{\lambda}\cap\left(\ker\Delta_{\mathfrak{v}}\right)^{\perp}
such that
\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(G)}=1
and

(5.3)

\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\mathrm{L}_{j}\phi\|_{L^{2}(G)}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}<C(1+\lambda)^{-\rho}.

(see the proof of Proposition
3.1
for notation). It follows that for each
\nu\in\mathbb{N}
we can find
0<C_{\nu}\leq 1
,
\lambda_{\nu}\in\sigma(\Delta_{G})
and
\phi_{\nu}\in E_{\lambda_{\nu}}\cap\left(\ker\Delta_{\mathfrak{v}}\right)^{\perp}
with
\|\phi_{\nu}\|_{L^{2}(G)}=1
and

(5.4)

\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\mathrm{L}_{j}\phi_{\nu}\|_{L^{2}(G)}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}<C_{\nu}(1+\lambda_{\nu})^{-\nu},\quad\forall\nu\in\mathbb{N}.

Concerning this construction, below we deduce a few simplifications that we will be able to assume without loss of generality.

First, we may further assume
\{\lambda_{\nu}\}_{\nu\in\mathbb{N}}
strictly increasing. Indeed, putting
C=1
and
\rho=1
in (
5.3
), we find
\lambda_{1}\in\sigma(\Delta_{G})
and
\phi_{1}\in E_{\lambda_{1}}\cap(\ker\Delta_{\mathfrak{v}})^{\perp}
such that

\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\mathrm{L}_{j}\phi_{1}\|_{L^{2}(G)}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}<(1+\lambda_{1})^{-1},

and pick
C_{1}\doteq 1
. Since
\sigma(\Delta_{G})
is discrete and each
E_{\lambda}
is finite dimensional, the set

\mathcal{S}_{\lambda_{1}}\doteq\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\in\sigma(\Delta_{G})\\
\lambda\leq\lambda_{1}\end{subarray}}\left\{\phi\in E_{\lambda}\cap(\ker\Delta_{\mathfrak{v}})^{\perp}\ ;\ \|\phi\|_{L^{2}(G)}=1\right\}

is compact, so by (
2.8
) we have

m_{\lambda_{1}}\doteq\min_{\phi\in\mathcal{S}_{\lambda_{1}}}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\mathrm{L}_{j}\phi\|_{L^{2}(G)}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}>0.

Picking
0<C_{2}<\min\{m_{\lambda_{1}},1\}
and putting
C=C_{2}
and
\rho=2
in (
5.3
), we obtain
\lambda_{2}\in\sigma(\Delta_{G})
, and
\phi_{2}\in E_{\lambda_{2}}\cap(\ker\Delta_{\mathfrak{v}})^{\perp}
with
\|\phi_{2}\|_{L^{2}(G)}=1
such that

\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\mathrm{L}_{j}\phi_{2}\|_{L^{2}(G)}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}<C_{2}(1+\lambda_{2})^{-2}.

Note that
\lambda_{2}>\lambda_{1}
: otherwise,
\phi_{2}\in\mathcal{S}_{\lambda_{1}}
, hence

C_{2}(1+\lambda_{2})^{-2}<m_{\lambda_{1}}\leq\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\mathrm{L}_{j}\phi_{2}\|_{L^{2}(G)}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

leading to a contradiction. Proceeding in this way we obtain the desired sequences.

Fix
\nu\in\mathbb{N}
. Since the vector fields
\mathrm{L}_{1},\ldots,\mathrm{L}_{n}
restrict to
E_{\lambda_{\nu}}
as commuting skew-adjoint linear endomorphisms, we can pick an orthonormal basis

\mathcal{B}_{\nu}\doteq\left\{\varphi_{1}^{(\nu)},\ldots,\varphi_{d_{\nu}}^{(\nu)}\right\}

of
E_{\lambda_{\nu}}
formed by common eigenfunctions of these operators, where
d_{\nu}\doteq\dim E_{\lambda_{\nu}}
. After relabeling, we can assume that

\mathcal{B}_{\nu}^{\prime}\doteq\{\varphi\in\mathcal{B}_{\nu}\ ;\ \Delta_{\mathfrak{v}}\varphi\neq 0\}=\left\{\varphi_{1}^{(\nu)},\ldots,\varphi_{q_{\nu}}^{(\nu)}\right\}

for some
q_{\nu}\in\{1,\ldots,d_{\nu}\}
–
\mathcal{B}_{\nu}^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
since
\phi_{\nu}\in E_{\lambda_{\nu}}
. Noting that
\varphi_{1}^{(\nu)},\ldots,\varphi_{d_{\nu}}^{(\nu)}
are also eigenfunctions of
\Delta_{\mathfrak{v}}
– thanks to its very definition (
2.6
) –, elementary arguments show that
\mathcal{B}_{\nu}^{\prime}
is an orthonormal basis of
E_{\lambda_{\nu}}\cap(\ker\Delta_{\mathfrak{v}})^{\perp}
.

Thus
\phi_{\nu}\in\Span_{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{B}_{\nu}^{\prime}
, but we may also assume that actually
\phi_{\nu}\in\mathcal{B}_{\nu}^{\prime}
. Indeed, otherwise we would have

\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\mathrm{L}_{j}\varphi_{\ell}^{(\nu)}\|_{L^{2}(G)}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\geq C_{\nu}(1+\lambda_{\nu})^{-\nu},\quad\forall\ell\in\{1,\ldots,q_{\nu}\}.

Writing
\mathrm{L}_{j}\varphi_{\ell}^{(\nu)}=\mu_{j,\ell}^{(\nu)}\varphi_{\ell}^{(\nu)}
, we have, for any
\phi\in E_{\lambda_{\nu}}\cap(\ker\Delta_{\mathfrak{v}})^{\perp}
:

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\mathrm{L}_{j}\phi\|_{L^{2}(G)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\Big\|\sum_{\ell=1}^{q_{\nu}}\langle\phi,\varphi_{\ell}^{(\nu)}\rangle_{L^{2}(G)}\ \mathrm{L}_{j}\varphi_{\ell}^{(\nu)}\Big\|_{L^{2}(G)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\Big\|\sum_{\ell=1}^{q_{\nu}}\langle\phi,\varphi_{\ell}^{(\nu)}\rangle_{L^{2}(G)}\ \mu_{j,\ell}^{(\nu)}\varphi_{\ell}^{(\nu)}\Big\|_{L^{2}(G)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{\ell=1}^{q_{\nu}}|\langle\phi,\varphi_{\ell}^{(\nu)}\rangle_{L^{2}(G)}|^{2}\ |\mu_{j,\ell}^{(\nu)}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{\ell=1}^{q_{\nu}}|\langle\phi,\varphi_{\ell}^{(\nu)}\rangle_{L^{2}(G)}|^{2}\ \|\mathrm{L}_{j}\varphi_{\ell}^{(\nu)}\|_{L^{2}(G)}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{\ell=1}^{q_{\nu}}|\langle\phi,\varphi_{\ell}^{(\nu)}\rangle_{L^{2}(G)}|^{2}\ C_{\nu}^{2}(1+\lambda_{\nu})^{-2\nu}

\displaystyle=\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(G)}^{2}C_{\nu}^{2}(1+\lambda_{\nu})^{-2\nu},

which, for
\phi=\phi_{\nu}
, contradicts (
5.4
). Hence, we may assume that
\phi_{\nu}\in\mathcal{B}_{\nu}^{\prime}
, and, after further relabeling, that
\phi_{\nu}=\varphi_{1}^{(\nu)}
.

Finally, we can assume without loss of generality that

(5.5)

\max_{1\leq j\leq n}\|\mathrm{L}_{j}\phi_{\nu}\|_{L^{2}(G)}=\|\mathrm{L}_{1}\phi_{\nu}\|_{L^{2}(G)},\quad\forall\nu\in\mathbb{N}.

Indeed, the maximum in the left-hand side of (
5.5
) must be attained infinitely many times at some
j_{0}\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Relabeling the vector fields so that as to have
j_{0}=1
and extracting the underlying subsequence in
\nu
does the job. In particular,
\mathrm{L}_{1}\phi_{\nu}\neq 0
for all
\nu\in\mathbb{N}
.

Now we set

f\doteq\sum_{\nu=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(\mathrm{L}_{j}\phi_{\nu})\ \tau_{j}\wedge\tau_{2}\wedge\cdots\wedge\tau_{q+1}\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(G;\underline{\Lambda}^{q+1}).

Indeed,
\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}(f)=0
if
\lambda\neq\lambda_{\nu}
for all
\nu\in\mathbb{N}
, whereas

\|\mathcal{F}_{\lambda_{\nu}}(f)\|_{L^{2}(G)}^{2}=\|\mathrm{L}_{1}\phi_{\nu}\|_{L^{2}(G)}^{2}+\sum_{j=q+2}^{n}\|\mathrm{L}_{j}\phi_{\nu}\|_{L^{2}(G)}^{2}<(1+\lambda_{\nu})^{-2\nu},\quad\forall\nu\in\mathbb{N},

by (
5.4
), from which smoothness of
f
follows by (
3.4
), which also entails convergence of the series above in
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(G;\underline{\Lambda}^{q+1})
. Moreover,
f
belongs to the closure of the range of
\mathrm{d}^{\prime}:\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(G;\underline{\Lambda}^{q})\to\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(G;\underline{\Lambda}^{q+1})
: notice that, thanks to (
5.2
),

f=\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathrm{d}^{\prime}\left(\sum_{\nu=1}^{N}\phi_{\nu}\ \tau_{2}\wedge\cdots\wedge\tau_{q+1}\right).

But by hypothesis the said range is closed, so we obtain
u\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(G;\underline{\Lambda}^{q})
such that
\mathrm{d}^{\prime}u=f
. We have, on the one hand,

f(\mathrm{L}_{1},\mathrm{L}_{2},\ldots,\mathrm{L}_{q+1})=\sum_{\nu=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{L}_{1}\phi_{\nu}

by definition of
f
; on the other hand, by (
2.4
) (recalling, once more, that
\mathfrak{v}
is commutative) we have

\displaystyle(\mathrm{d}^{\prime}u)(\mathrm{L}_{1},\mathrm{L}_{2},\ldots,\mathrm{L}_{q+1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{q+1}(-1)^{j+1}\mathrm{L}_{j}\left(u(\mathrm{L}_{1},\ldots,\widehat{\mathrm{L}}_{j},\ldots,\mathrm{L}_{q+1})\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{q+1}(-1)^{j+1}\mathrm{L}_{j}u_{\widehat{J}(j)}

with coefficients
u_{J}
as in (
2.1
), where
\widehat{J}(j)\doteq(1,\ldots,\widehat{j},\ldots,q+1)
. We conclude that

\sum_{j=1}^{q+1}(-1)^{j+1}\mathrm{L}_{j}u_{\widehat{J}(j)}=\sum_{\nu=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{L}_{1}\phi_{\nu}

which we project onto
E_{\lambda_{\nu}}
, obtaining

\displaystyle\mathrm{L}_{1}\varphi_{1}^{(\nu)}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{L}_{1}\phi_{\nu}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{q+1}(-1)^{j+1}\mathcal{F}_{\lambda_{\nu}}(\mathrm{L}_{j}u_{\widehat{J}(j)})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{q+1}(-1)^{j+1}\mathrm{L}_{j}\mathcal{F}_{\lambda_{\nu}}(u_{\widehat{J}(j)})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{q+1}\sum_{\ell=1}^{q_{\nu}}(-1)^{j+1}\langle u_{\widehat{J}(j)},\varphi_{\ell}^{(\nu)}\rangle_{L^{2}(G)}\ \mathrm{L}_{j}\varphi_{\ell}^{(\nu)}.

Recalling that the
\varphi_{\ell}^{(\nu)}
are eigenfunctions of each
\mathrm{L}_{j}
, by the linear independence of
\varphi_{1}^{(\nu)},\ldots,\varphi_{q_{\nu}}^{(\nu)}
it follows that

\mathrm{L}_{1}\varphi_{1}^{(\nu)}=\mathrm{L}_{1}\phi_{\nu}=\sum_{j=1}^{q+1}(-1)^{j+1}\langle u_{\widehat{J}(j)},\varphi_{1}^{(\nu)}\rangle_{L^{2}(G)}\ \mathrm{L}_{j}\varphi_{1}^{(\nu)}

hence computing
L^{2}
norms yields, using (
5.5
),

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{q+1}|\langle u_{\widehat{J}(j)},\varphi_{1}^{(\nu)}\rangle_{L^{2}(G)}|^{2}\ \frac{\|\mathrm{L}_{j}\varphi_{1}^{(\nu)}\|_{L^{2}(G)}^{2}}{\|\mathrm{L}_{1}\varphi_{1}^{(\nu)}\|_{L^{2}(G)}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{q+1}|\langle u_{\widehat{J}(j)},\varphi_{1}^{(\nu)}\rangle_{L^{2}(G)}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{q+1}\|\mathcal{F}_{\lambda_{\nu}}(u_{\widehat{J}(j)})\|_{L^{2}(G)}^{2}.

which is impossible since
u_{\widehat{J}(j)}\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(G)
implies that
\|\mathcal{F}_{\lambda_{\nu}}(u_{\widehat{J}(j)})\|_{L^{2}(G)}
must decay rapidly in
\lambda_{\nu}
(in the sense of (
3.4
)).
∎

6.
Structures whose integral subgroup is closed

Since
\mathfrak{v}
is essentially real, there exists a Lie subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}\subset\mathfrak{g}
whose complexification equals
\mathfrak{v}
: we denote by
H\subset G
its (connected) integral subgroup, which, in this section, we will further assume to be closed. In that case, the space
G/H
of left cosets is a smooth, compact manifold, and the natural projection
\Pi:G\to G/H
is a smooth submersion. We have

(6.1)

\mathscr{K}(G)=\{\Pi^{*}g\ ;\ g\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(G/H)\}\cong\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(G/H).

Theorem 6.1
.

If
H\subset G
is closed then
\mathrm{d}^{\prime}:\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(G)\to\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(G;\underline{\Lambda}^{1})
has closed range.

Proof.

Pick
\mathrm{L}_{1},\ldots,\mathrm{L}_{n}
an orthonormal basis of
\mathfrak{h}
: as in Section
3
we will prove, equivalently, that
\Delta_{\mathfrak{v}}:\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(G)\to\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(G)
has closed range.

We endow
H
with the
\mathrm{ad}
-invariant metric inherited from
G
(which is the same as endowing
\mathfrak{h}\subset\mathfrak{g}
with the induced inner product), and regard
\mathrm{L}_{1},\ldots,\mathrm{L}_{n}
as left-invariant vector fields on
H
as well, in which sense

\Delta_{H}=-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathrm{L}_{j}^{2}

is the Laplace-Beltrami operator on
H
associated to the metric. It follows, since the vector fields
\mathrm{L}_{1},\ldots,\mathrm{L}_{n}
are tangent to
H
, that
(\Delta_{\mathfrak{v}}f)|_{H}=\Delta_{H}(f|_{H})
for every
f\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(G)
. The following estimates for
\Delta_{H}
(which is elliptic) are well-known:

\|h\|_{L^{2}(H)}\leq C\|\Delta_{H}h\|_{L^{2}(H)},\quad\forall h\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(H),\ h\perp_{L^{2}(H)}\mathbb{C}_{H}.

We will also make use of the following Fubini formula
[
16
, Theorem 8.36]
:

\int_{G}f(x)\ \mathrm{d}\mu_{G}(x)=\int_{G/H}\left(\int_{H}f(xy)\ \mathrm{d}\mu_{H}(y)\right)\mathrm{d}\mu_{G/H}(xH),\quad\forall f\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(G),

where
\mu_{G},\mu_{H}
are the respective Haar measures, and
\mu_{G/H}
is a certain
G
-invariant measure on
G/H
. Notice that
x\mapsto\int_{H}f(xy)\mathrm{d}\mu_{H}(y)
descends to
G/H
thanks to the left-invariance of
\mu_{H}
.

Let
f\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(G)
be
L^{2}
-orthogonal to
\mathscr{K}(G)
. By (
6.1
) we have

0=\langle f,\Pi^{*}g\rangle_{L^{2}(G)}=\int_{G/H}\overline{g(xH)}\left(\int_{H}f(xy)\ \mathrm{d}\mu_{H}(y)\right)\mathrm{d}\mu_{G/H}(xH),\quad\forall g\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(G/H),

hence, for every
x\in G
,

\langle(\ell_{x}^{*}f)|_{H},1_{H}\rangle_{L^{2}(H)}=\int_{H}f(xy)\ \mathrm{d}\mu_{H}(y)=0.

This shows that
(\ell_{x}^{\ast}f)|_{H}\perp_{L^{2}(H)}\mathbb{C}_{H}
, and thus

\|(\ell_{x}^{*}f)|_{H}\|_{L^{2}(H)}\leq C\|\Delta_{H}[(\ell_{x}^{*}f)|_{H}]\|_{L^{2}(H)},\quad\forall x\in G.

Now notice that
\Delta_{H}[(\ell_{x}^{*}f)|_{H}]=[\Delta_{\mathfrak{v}}(\ell_{x}^{*}f)]|_{H}=[\ell_{x}^{*}(\Delta_{\mathfrak{v}}f)]|_{H}
, and therefore

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{2}(G)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{G}|f(x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mu_{G}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{G/H}\left(\int_{H}|f(xy)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mu_{H}(y)\right)\mathrm{d}\mu_{G/H}(xH)

\displaystyle=\int_{G/H}\|(\ell_{x}^{*}f)|_{H}\|_{L^{2}(H)}^{2}\ \mathrm{d}\mu_{G/H}(xH)

\displaystyle\leq C^{2}\int_{G/H}\|[\ell_{x}^{*}(\Delta_{\mathfrak{v}}f)]|_{H}\|_{L^{2}(H)}^{2}\ \mathrm{d}\mu_{G/H}(xH)

\displaystyle=C^{2}\|\Delta_{\mathfrak{v}}f\|_{L^{2}(G)}^{2}.

We have proved that

\|f\|_{L^{2}(G)}\leq C\|\Delta_{\mathfrak{v}}f\|_{L^{2}(G)},\quad\forall f\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(G),\ f\perp_{L^{2}(G)}\mathscr{K}(G).

Since
\Delta_{\mathfrak{v}}
is a left-invariant operator, our conclusion follows from results in
[
1
]
.
∎

Corollary 6.2
.

If
H\subset G
is closed then

H^{q}_{\mathrm{d}^{\prime}}(\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(G))\cong\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(G/H)\otimes H^{q}_{\mathrm{dR}}(H),\quad\forall q\in\{0,\ldots,n\}.

Proof.

By Theorems
6.1
and
5.1
, Remark
4.2
and equation (
6.1
), we have

H^{q}_{\mathrm{d}^{\prime}}(\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(G))\cong H^{q}_{\mathrm{d}^{\prime}}(\mathscr{K}(G))\cong\mathscr{K}(G)\otimes H^{q}_{\mathrm{d}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{C}_{G})\cong\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(G/H)\otimes H^{q}_{\mathrm{d}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{C}_{G}),

so the only thing left to prove is that
H^{q}_{\mathrm{d}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{C}_{G})\cong H^{q}_{\mathrm{dR}}(H)
.

Pick
\mathrm{L}_{1},\ldots,\mathrm{L}_{n},\mathrm{M}_{1},\ldots,\mathrm{M}_{m}
a basis of
\mathfrak{g}
such that
\mathfrak{h}=\Span\{\mathrm{L}_{1},\ldots,\mathrm{L}_{n}\}
, and
\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{n},\zeta_{1},\ldots,\zeta_{m}\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
the dual basis; we will regard them as global frames for
TG
and
T^{*}G
, respectively. Let
\imath:H\hookrightarrow G
be the inclusion map: denoting by
\mathrm{L}_{1}^{\bullet},\ldots,\mathrm{L}_{n}^{\bullet}
the vector fields induced on
H
, they form a global frame of
TH
, and
\mathrm{L}_{j}^{\bullet}
is
\imath
-related to
\mathrm{L}_{j}
for each
j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. It follows that
\imath^{*}\zeta_{k}=0
for each
k\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
, since for each
p\in H
we have

\langle\imath^{*}\zeta_{k}|_{p},\mathrm{L}_{j}^{\bullet}|_{p}\rangle=\langle\zeta_{k}|_{\imath(p)},\imath_{*}(\mathrm{L}_{j}^{\bullet}|_{p})\rangle=\langle\zeta_{k}|_{\imath(p)},\mathrm{L}_{j}|_{\imath(p)}\rangle=0,\quad\forall j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}.

A similar argument shows that
\imath^{*}\tau_{1},\ldots,\imath^{*}\tau_{n}
is a global frame for
T^{*}H
, dual to
\mathrm{L}_{1}^{\bullet},\ldots,\mathrm{L}_{n}^{\bullet}
. Therefore, writing an arbitrary
u\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(G;\underline{\Lambda}^{q})
as (
2.1
), we have

\imath^{*}u=\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{|J|=q}(\imath^{*}u_{J})\ \imath^{*}\tau_{J}.

Since
\imath^{*}:\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(G)\to\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(H)
is surjective (because
H\subset G
is a closed embedded submanifold), it follows that
\imath^{*}:\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(G;\underline{\Lambda}^{q})\to\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(H;\wedge^{q}\mathbb{C}T^{*}H)
is also surjective, with kernel

\{u\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(G;\underline{\Lambda}^{q})\ ;\ u_{J}|_{H}=0,\quad\forall J\}.

Moreover, since
\imath^{*}\zeta_{k}=0
for all
k\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
, it follows that
\imath^{*}(\mathrm{d}u-\mathrm{d}^{\prime}u)=0
, thus

\imath^{*}(\mathrm{d}^{\prime}u)=\imath^{*}(\mathrm{d}u)=\mathrm{d}\imath^{*}u,

i.e.,
\imath^{*}:\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(G;\underline{\Lambda}^{q})\longrightarrow\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(H;\wedge^{q}\mathbb{C}T^{*}H)
is a chain map. In particular, it induces maps in cohomology

\imath^{*}:H^{q}_{\mathrm{d}^{\prime}}(\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(G))\longrightarrow H^{q}_{\mathrm{dR}}(H).

By restriction, it is immediate that
\imath^{*}:\mathbb{C}_{G}(G;\underline{\Lambda}^{q})\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}_{H}(H;\wedge^{q}\mathbb{C}T^{*}H)
is an isomorphism (where the target is the space
\Span_{\mathbb{C}}\{\imath^{*}\tau_{J}\ ;\ |J|=q\}
of left-invariant
q
-forms on
H
). Hence,

\imath^{*}:H^{q}_{\mathrm{d}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{C}_{G})\longrightarrow H^{q}_{\mathrm{d}}(\mathbb{C}_{H})

is also an isomorphism, where
H^{q}_{\mathrm{d}}(\mathbb{C}_{H})
stands for left-invariant de Rham cohomology of
H
. Finally,
H^{q}_{\mathrm{d}}(\mathbb{C}_{H})\cong H^{q}_{\mathrm{dR}}(H)
(by
[
9
]
, or our Theorem
5.1
plus Remark
4.2
applied to the de Rham cohomology of
H
).
∎

7.
Tube structures and related equations

In this final section, we address some questions raised in a previous paper
[
2
]
in the context of tube structures.

Let
M
be a smooth
n
-dimensional manifold, whose bundle of
q
-forms we denote by
\Lambda^{q}
; the pullback of the latter to
\Omega\doteq M\times\mathbb{T}^{m}
will be denoted, in this section, by
\underline{\Lambda}^{q}
: the bundle of
(0,q)
-forms
. The choice of
m
smooth, closed
1
-forms on
M
– call them
\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{m}
– induced on
\Omega
an involutive structure
\mathcal{V}\subset\mathbb{C}T\Omega
, often called a
tube structure
on
\Omega
. Its associated differential complex
[
8
,
18
]
can be concretely written as

\mathrm{d}^{\prime}\doteq\mathrm{d}_{t}+\sum_{k=1}^{m}\omega_{k}\wedge\partial_{x_{k}}:\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(\Omega;\underline{\Lambda}^{q})\longrightarrow\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(\Omega;\underline{\Lambda}^{q+1}),\quad q\in\{0,\ldots,n-1\},

with cohomology spaces (based on smooth sections) denoted by
H^{q}_{\mathrm{d}^{\prime}}(\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(\Omega))
.

7.1.
Normalization

Consider on
\Omega
two tube structures
\mathcal{V},\mathcal{V}^{\bullet}\subset\mathbb{C}T\Omega
, associated to
1
-forms
\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{m}
and
\omega_{1}^{\bullet},\ldots,\omega_{m}^{\bullet}
, respectively.

Theorem 7.1
.

Suppose there exist
\psi_{1},\ldots,\psi_{m}\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(M;\mathbb{R})
such that

\omega_{k}^{\bullet}=\omega_{k}+\mathrm{d}\psi_{k},\quad k\in\{1,\ldots,m\}.

Then there exists a diffeomorphism
\Psi:\Omega\to\Omega
such that

(7.1)

\Psi_{*}(\mathcal{V}_{p})=\mathcal{V}^{\bullet}_{\Psi(p)},\quad\forall p\in\Omega.

Proof.

We define
\Psi:M\times\mathbb{T}^{m}\to M\times\mathbb{T}^{m}
by

\Psi(t,x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})\doteq(t,x_{1}+\psi_{1}(t),\ldots,x_{m}+\psi_{m}(t)),

where the sum
x_{k}+\psi_{k}(t)
takes place in
\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}
. This is clearly a diffeomorphism (its inverse has the same form). To prove (
7.1
), we show instead the dual relationship between the annihilator bundles:

\Psi^{*}(\T^{\prime\bullet}_{\Psi(p)})=\T^{\prime}_{p},\quad\forall p\in\Omega.

Let
p=(t,x)\in\Omega
, and pick a neighborhood of
\Psi(p)=(t,x^{\bullet})
of the form
U^{\bullet}\times V^{\bullet}
, where:

•

U^{\bullet}\subset M
is a neighborhood of
t
where one can find
\phi_{1}^{\bullet},\ldots,\phi_{m}^{\bullet}\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(U^{\bullet})
local primitives for
\omega_{1}^{\bullet},\ldots,\omega_{m}^{\bullet}
, respectively; and

•

V^{\bullet}\subset\mathbb{T}^{m}
is a neighborhood of
x^{\bullet}
with standard coordinates
(x_{1}^{\bullet},\ldots,x_{m}^{\bullet})
.

Take now
U\times V\subset\Psi^{-1}(U^{\bullet}\times V^{\bullet})
a neighborhood of
p
, where
U\subset M
is a neighborhood of
t
and
V\subset\mathbb{T}^{m}
is a neighborhood of
x
with standard coordinates
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})
. Using the definition of
\Psi
, one checks at once that
U\subset U^{\bullet}
, hence
\phi_{k}\doteq\phi_{k}^{\bullet}-\psi_{k}
is a primitive for
\omega_{k}
on
U
for each
k\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
.

This means that

\zeta_{k}\doteq\mathrm{d}x_{k}-\mathrm{d}\phi_{k},\quad k\in\{1,\ldots,m\},

span
\T^{\prime}
on
U\times V
, whereas

\zeta_{k}^{\bullet}\doteq\mathrm{d}x_{k}^{\bullet}-\mathrm{d}\phi_{k}^{\bullet},\quad k\in\{1,\ldots,m\},

span
\T^{\prime\bullet}
on
U^{\bullet}\times V^{\bullet}
. We have, on
U\times V
:

\Psi^{*}x_{k}^{\bullet}=x_{k}^{\bullet}\circ\Psi=x_{k}+\psi_{k}+2\pi\nu_{k},\quad\Psi^{*}\phi_{k}^{\bullet}=\phi_{k}^{\bullet}\circ\Psi=\phi_{k}^{\bullet}

(recall that
\phi_{k}^{\bullet}
depends only on the
t
-variables), for some
\nu\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}
fixed. Hence,

\Psi^{*}\zeta_{k}^{\bullet}=\mathrm{d}\Psi^{*}x_{k}^{\bullet}-\mathrm{d}\Psi^{*}\phi_{k}^{\bullet}=\mathrm{d}(x_{k}+\psi_{k}+2\pi\nu_{k})-\mathrm{d}\phi_{k}^{\bullet}=\zeta_{k}

for each
k\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
, thus proving our assertion.
∎

Corollary 7.2
.

Assume further that all
\omega_{k},\omega_{k}^{\bullet}
are real-valued. If
[\omega_{k}]=[\omega_{k}^{\bullet}]
in
H^{1}_{\mathrm{dR}}(M)
for every
k\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
, then there exists a diffeomorphism
\Psi:\Omega\to\Omega
such that (
7.1
) holds.

Diffeomorphisms like
\Psi
above are said to be
compatible
with the involutive structures
\mathcal{V},\mathcal{V}^{\bullet}
. Thanks to (
7.1
), they induce pullback maps
\Psi^{*}
on the level of
(0,q)
-forms that commute with the associated differentials
\mathrm{d}^{\prime},\mathrm{d}^{\prime}_{\bullet}
(i.e.,
\Psi^{*}
is a chain map), hence conjugating the complexes by means of topological isomorphisms, and inducing isomorphism in cohomology:

\Psi^{*}:H^{q}_{\mathrm{d}^{\prime}_{\bullet}}(\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(\Omega))\longrightarrow H^{q}_{\mathrm{d}^{\prime}}(\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(\Omega)).

In particular, all the properties we are concerned with are preserved by it.

Since every closed
1
-form on a compact, connected Lie group
M
admits a left-invariant representative (by
[
9
]
, or our Theorem
5.1
plus Remark
4.2
applied to the de Rham cohomology of
M
), we have the following special case of Corollary
7.2
:

Corollary 7.3
.

When
M
is a compact, connected Lie group, every real tube structure on
G\doteq M\times\mathbb{T}^{m}
is equivalent to one defined by left-invariant
1
-forms.

7.2.
A formula for cohomology

In this section we assume that
M
is a compact, connected
n
-dimensional manifold, and start by recalling some context from
[
2
]
. There, we defined, for each
\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}
and
q\in\{0,\ldots,n-1\}
, a differential operator
\mathrm{d}^{\prime}_{\xi}:\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(M;\Lambda^{q})\to\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(M;\Lambda^{q+1})
by

\mathrm{d}^{\prime}_{\xi}f\doteq\mathrm{d}f+i\sum_{k=1}^{m}\xi_{k}\omega_{k}\wedge f.

It follows that
\mathrm{d}^{\prime}_{\xi}\circ\mathrm{d}^{\prime}_{\xi}=0
, and the associated cohomology spaces

H^{q}_{\xi}(\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(M))\doteq\frac{\ker\{\mathrm{d}^{\prime}_{\xi}:\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(M;\Lambda^{q})\longrightarrow\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(M;\Lambda^{q+1})\}}{\ran\{\mathrm{d}^{\prime}_{\xi}:\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(M;\Lambda^{q-1})\longrightarrow\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(M;\Lambda^{q})\}}

are finite dimensional thanks to ellipticity of that complex, plus compactness of
M
. We further let
\mathscr{S}_{\xi}
be the sheaf of germs of smooth functions on
M
annihilated by
\mathrm{d}^{\prime}_{\xi}
, and considered the group
[
2
, Theorem 3.6]

\Gamma_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}\doteq\{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}\ ;\ \text{$\mathscr{S}_{\xi}$ has a non-vanishing global section}\}.

Then, we turned our attention to the property:

(7.2)

H^{q}_{\xi}(\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(M))=\{0\},\quad\forall\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}\setminus\Gamma_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}.

When (
7.2
) holds (which is always the case, e.g., when
M
is the
2
-sphere, or the
2
-torus), we proved that, for instance
[
2
, Theorem 7.5]
:

\text{$H_{\mathrm{d}^{\prime}}^{q}(\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(\Omega))$ is finite dimensional}\Longleftrightarrow H_{\mathrm{d}^{\prime}}^{q}(\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(\Omega))\cong H^{q}_{\mathrm{dR}}(M).

When
\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{m}
are
real
, a more precise result can be obtained
[
2
, Theorem 5.7]
: if
\mathrm{d}^{\prime}:\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(\Omega;\underline{\Lambda}^{q-1})\to\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(\Omega;\underline{\Lambda}^{q})
has closed range and (
7.2
) holds, then there is a natural isomorphism

H^{q}_{\mathrm{d}^{\prime}}(\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(\Omega))\cong\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{r})\otimes H^{q}_{\mathrm{dR}}(M),

where
r
is the rank of the group
\Gamma_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}
. By the end of the next section, we will have proved the following result:

Theorem 7.4
.

If
M
is a compact, connected
Lie group
and
\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{m}
are closed,
left-invariant
and real-valued then (
7.2
) holds for every
q\in\{0,\ldots,n\}
.

Actually, thanks to Corollary
7.3
, the hypothesis of left-invariance of
\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{m}
is inessential. Indeed, for each
k\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
let
\omega_{k}^{\bullet}\doteq\omega_{k}+\mathrm{d}\psi_{k}
for some
\psi_{k}\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(M;\mathbb{R})
, and consider
\mathrm{d}^{\prime\prime}_{\xi}
the operators associated with the new system
\omega_{1}^{\bullet},\ldots,\omega_{m}^{\bullet}
. That is:

\mathrm{d}^{\prime\prime}_{\xi}f\doteq\mathrm{d}f+i\sum_{k=1}^{m}\xi_{k}\omega_{k}^{\bullet}\wedge f.

A simple computation shows that

\mathrm{d}^{\prime}_{\xi}(e^{i\phi}f)=e^{i\phi}\mathrm{d}^{\prime\prime}_{\xi}f,\quad\text{where $\phi\doteq\sum_{k=1}^{m}\xi_{k}\psi_{k}$},

hence both complexes (and all their associated constructions: sheaves of solutions, cohomologies, etc.) are isomorphic: for real
\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{m}
, condition (
7.2
) also depends only on their de Rham cohomology classes. The conclusions will, then, be summarized as follows:

Corollary 7.5
.

If
M
is a compact, connected Lie group,
\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{m}
are closed and real-valued, and
r
is the rank of the group
\Gamma_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}
, then

H^{q}_{\mathrm{d}^{\prime}}(\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(\Omega))\cong\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{r})\otimes H^{q}_{\mathrm{dR}}(M)

holds for every
q\in\{0,\ldots,n\}
.

7.3.
Perturbations of the de Rham complex

Motivated by the discussion in the previous section, we consider a class of zero-order perturbations of the de Rham complex of
M
. Given
\omega
a smooth, closed
1
-form on
M
, for each
q\in\{0,\ldots,n\}
we define a first-order operator acting on
q
-forms:

\mathrm{D}_{\omega}\doteq\mathrm{d}+i\omega\wedge\cdot:\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(M;\Lambda^{q})\longrightarrow\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(M;\Lambda^{q+1}).

Clearly
\mathrm{D}_{\omega}\circ\mathrm{D}_{\omega}=0
, and the differential complex so constructed is elliptic. Its cohomology spaces are finite dimensional, and denoted by

(7.3)

H_{\omega}^{q}(\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(M))\doteq\frac{\ker\{\mathrm{D}_{\omega}:\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(M;\Lambda^{q})\longrightarrow\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(M;\Lambda^{q+1})\}}{\ran\{\mathrm{D}_{\omega}:\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(M;\Lambda^{q-1})\longrightarrow\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(M;\Lambda^{q})\}}.

We will prove:

Theorem 7.6
.

If
M
is a compact, connected Lie group, and
\omega
is closed, left-invariant and real-valued, then we have two possibilities:

(1)

either

\ker\{\mathrm{D}_{\omega}:\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(M)\longrightarrow\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(M;\Lambda^{1})\}=\{0\},

in which case
H_{\omega}^{q}(\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(M))=\{0\}
for every
q\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
;

(2)

or the kernel of
\mathrm{D}_{\omega}
is non-trivial, in which case
H_{\omega}^{q}(\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(M))\cong H^{q}_{\mathrm{dR}}(M)
for every
q\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.

Remark 7.7
.

The algebraic parts of the proofs below, including the ones in the appendix, are inspired by an argument in
[
9
, pp. 117–118]
.

We start by giving explicit global descriptions of the objects involved. Let
\mathrm{T}_{1},\ldots,\mathrm{T}_{n}
be a basis for the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{m}
of
M
, with dual basis
\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{n}\in\mathfrak{m}^{*}
. We write

\omega=\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}\tau_{j},\quad a_{j}\in\mathbb{R},

due to its left-invariance. An arbitrary
u\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(M;\Lambda^{q})
is written as

(7.4)

u=\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{|J|=q}u_{J}\ \tau_{J},\quad u_{J}\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(M),

hence

\mathrm{D}_{\omega}u=\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{|J|=q}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\mathrm{T}_{j}u_{J}+ia_{j}u_{J}\right)\ \tau_{j}\wedge\tau_{J}+\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{|J|=q}u_{J}\ \mathrm{d}\tau_{J},

that is,
\mathrm{D}_{\omega}
is expressed in global frames as a matrix of left-invariant differential operators on
M
. For that reason, it allows for Fourier analysis.

Take
\Delta_{M}
the Laplace-Beltrami operator associated to some
\mathrm{ad}
-invariant metric on
M
. For each
\mu\in\sigma(\Delta_{M})
, we denote by
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}_{E_{\mu}}(M)
the eigenspace associated to
\mu
, and by
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}_{E_{\mu}}(M;\Lambda^{q})
the space of
q
-forms (
7.4
) such that
u_{J}\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}_{E_{\mu}}(M)
for every
J
. It follows that the entries of
\mathrm{D}_{\omega}
commute with
\Delta_{M}
, hence
\mathrm{D}_{\omega}\mathscr{C}^{\infty}_{E_{\mu}}(M;\Lambda^{q})\subset\mathscr{C}^{\infty}_{E_{\mu}}(M;\Lambda^{q+1})
for every
\mu
.

By finite dimensionality of
H^{q}_{\omega}(\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(M))
, it follows from
[
1
, Corollary 2.9]
that

(7.5)

H^{q}_{\omega}(\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(M))\cong\bigoplus_{\mu\in\sigma(\Delta_{M})}\frac{\ker\{\mathrm{D}_{\omega}:\mathscr{C}^{\infty}_{E_{\mu}}(M;\Lambda^{q})\longrightarrow\mathscr{C}^{\infty}_{E_{\mu}}(M;\Lambda^{q+1})\}}{\ran\{\mathrm{D}_{\omega}:\mathscr{C}^{\infty}_{E_{\mu}}(M;\Lambda^{q-1})\longrightarrow\mathscr{C}^{\infty}_{E_{\mu}}(M;\Lambda^{q})\}}.

In particular, at most finitely many quotients in the right-hand side of (
7.5
) are non-trivial.

Proof of Theorem
7.6
.

First we assume that there is a non-zero
\phi\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(M)
such that
\mathrm{d}\phi=-i\phi\omega
. If
\phi
did vanish at some
t_{0}\in M
, we could take
U\subset M
a connected open neighborhood of
t_{0}
where
\omega
is exact:
\omega|_{U}=\mathrm{d}\psi
for some
\psi\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(U)
. Therefore:

\mathrm{d}(\phi e^{i\psi})=\mathrm{d}\phi\wedge e^{i\psi}+i\phi e^{i\psi}\wedge\mathrm{d}\psi=-i\phi\omega\wedge e^{i\psi}+i\phi e^{i\psi}\wedge\omega=0.

Consequently,
\phi e^{i\psi}
is constant, and since
\phi(t_{0})=0
it follows that
\phi
vanishes on
U
. Since
M
is connected, this leads to a contradiction, so
\phi
cannot vanish anywhere in
M
. Thus we can write

\mathrm{d}(f/\phi)=(1/\phi)\mathrm{D}_{\omega}(f),\quad\forall f\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(M;\Lambda^{q}),

hence multiplication by
\phi^{-1}
yields
H_{\omega}^{q}(\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(M))\cong H^{q}_{\mathrm{dR}}(M)
for every
q\in\{0,\ldots,n\}
.

Next we consider the case where
\mathrm{D}_{\omega}
is injective. Notice that
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}_{E_{\mu}}(M;\Lambda^{q})
is finite dimensional, just as the cohomology spaces

H^{q}_{\omega}(\mathscr{C}^{\infty}_{E_{\mu}}(M))\doteq\frac{\ker\{\mathrm{D}_{\omega}:\mathscr{C}^{\infty}_{E_{\mu}}(M;\Lambda^{q})\longrightarrow\mathscr{C}^{\infty}_{E_{\mu}}(M;\Lambda^{q+1})\}}{\ran\{\mathrm{D}_{\omega}:\mathscr{C}^{\infty}_{E_{\mu}}(M;\Lambda^{q-1})\longrightarrow\mathscr{C}^{\infty}_{E_{\mu}}(M;\Lambda^{q})\}},\quad\mu\in\sigma(\Delta_{M}),

which, as we have seen, vanish except for finitely many
\mu
: thanks to (
7.5
), in order to prove the vanishing of (
7.3
) we must check that
H^{q}_{\omega}(\mathscr{C}^{\infty}_{E_{\mu}}(M))=\{0\}
for
all

\mu
.

On
G\doteq M\times\mathbb{T}^{1}
, we consider
\mathcal{V}\subset\mathbb{C}TG
the corank
1
tube structure determined by the single
1
-form
\mathrm{d}x-\omega
. Since
\omega
is left-invariant, so is
\mathcal{V}
, with associated subalgebra
\mathfrak{v}\subset\mathbb{C}\mathfrak{g}
spanned by the real left-invariant vector fields

\mathrm{L}_{j}\doteq\mathrm{T}_{j}+a_{j}\partial_{x},\quad j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}.

Fix
\mu\in\sigma(\Delta_{M})
and consider the space

\mathscr{F}_{\mu}(G)\doteq\{e^{ix}\phi\ ;\ \phi\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}_{E_{\mu}}(M)\}\subset\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(G),

which is finite dimensional and
\mathfrak{v}
-invariant:

\mathrm{L}_{j}(e^{ix}\phi)=e^{ix}\left(\mathrm{T}_{j}\phi+ia_{j}\phi\right),\quad\forall j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}.

Notice also that

\mathscr{F}_{\mu}(G;\underline{\Lambda}^{q})=\{e^{ix}u\ ;\ u\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}_{E_{\mu}}(M;\Lambda^{q})\}\subset\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(G;\underline{\Lambda}^{q})

is finite dimensional for every
q\in\{0,\ldots,n\}
, and for
u\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}_{E_{\mu}}(M;\Lambda^{q})
we have

\mathrm{d}^{\prime}(e^{ix}u)=(\mathrm{d}_{t}+\omega\wedge\partial_{x})(e^{ix}u)=e^{ix}\mathrm{d}_{t}u+ie^{ix}\omega\wedge u=e^{ix}(\mathrm{D}_{\omega}u).

The map induced by multiplication by
e^{ix}
in cohomology

H^{q}_{\omega}(\mathscr{C}^{\infty}_{E_{\mu}}(M))\longrightarrow H^{q}_{\mathrm{d}^{\prime}}(\mathscr{F}_{\mu}(G))

is thus well-defined, and moreover injective. Indeed, if
[f]\in H^{q}_{\omega}(\mathscr{C}^{\infty}_{E_{\mu}}(M))
is such that
[e^{ix}f]=0
in
H^{q}_{\mathrm{d}^{\prime}}(\mathscr{F}_{\mu}(G))
then there exists
u\in\mathscr{F}_{\mu}(G;\underline{\Lambda}^{q-1})
such that
\mathrm{d}^{\prime}u=e^{ix}f
. As such, there exists
v\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}_{E_{\mu}}(M;\Lambda^{q-1})
such that

, hence

e^{ix}f=\mathrm{d}^{\prime}(e^{ix}v)=e^{ix}(\mathrm{D}_{\omega}v)\Longrightarrow f=\mathrm{D}_{\omega}v

from which
[f]=0
follows. To finish, we prove that
H^{q}_{\mathrm{d}^{\prime}}(\mathscr{F}_{\mu}(G))=\{0\}
.

First, notice that
\Delta_{\mathfrak{v}}:\mathscr{F}_{\mu}(G;\underline{\Lambda}^{q})\longrightarrow\mathscr{F}_{\mu}(G;\underline{\Lambda}^{q})
is a bijection; since
\mathscr{F}_{\mu}(G;\underline{\Lambda}^{q})
is finite dimensional, it suffices to check injectivity. Take
f\in\mathscr{F}_{\mu}(G;\underline{\Lambda}^{q})
such that
\Delta_{\mathfrak{v}}f=0
. We have

f=\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{|J|=q}f_{J}\ \tau_{J},\quad f_{J}\in\mathscr{F}_{\mu}(G)\Longrightarrow\Delta_{\mathfrak{v}}f=\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{|J|=q}(\Delta_{\mathfrak{v}}f_{J})\ \tau_{J},

hence
\Delta_{\mathfrak{v}}f_{J}=0
for each
J
. However, for an arbitrary
e^{ix}\phi\in\mathscr{F}_{\mu}(G)
we have

\displaystyle\Delta_{\mathfrak{v}}(e^{ix}\phi)=0

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\mathrm{L}_{j}(e^{ix}\phi)=0,\quad\forall j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\mathrm{d}^{\prime}(e^{ix}\phi)=0

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\mathrm{D}_{\omega}\phi=0

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\phi=0

by hypothesis. So
f_{J}=0
for all

, i.e.,

.

Finally, let
f\in\mathscr{F}_{\mu}(G;\underline{\Lambda}^{q})
be such that
\mathrm{d}^{\prime}f=0
, and take

such that
\Delta_{\mathfrak{v}}g=f
. Then

0=\mathrm{d}^{\prime}f=\mathrm{d}^{\prime}\Delta_{\mathfrak{v}}g=\Delta_{\mathfrak{v}}(\mathrm{d}^{\prime}g),

which implies that
\mathrm{d}^{\prime}g=0
by the injectivity of
\Delta_{\mathfrak{v}}:\mathscr{F}_{\mu}(G;\underline{\Lambda}^{q+1})\to\mathscr{F}_{\mu}(G;\underline{\Lambda}^{q+1})
shown above. Lemma
2.3
ensures the existence of a

such that
\mathrm{d}^{\prime}v=\Delta_{\mathfrak{v}}g=f
. Since

is arbitrary, we conclude that
H^{q}_{\mathrm{d}^{\prime}}(\mathscr{F}_{\mu}(G))=\{0\}
.
∎

Back to Theorem

and

v\doteq\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathrm{L}_{j}(\imath_{\mathrm{L}_{j}}f)

solves
\mathrm{d}^{\prime}v=-\Delta_{\mathfrak{v}}f
.

Remark A.3
.

For
\mathscr{F}(G)
a
\mathfrak{v}
-invariant subspace,
f\in\mathscr{F}(G;\underline{\Lambda}^{q})
implies
v\in\mathscr{F}(G;\underline{\Lambda}^{q-1})
.

Proof.

We write

(A.4)

\mathrm{d}^{\prime}v=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathrm{d}^{\prime}\mathrm{L}_{j}(\imath_{\mathrm{L}_{j}}f)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\mathrm{d}^{\prime}\mathrm{L}_{j}(\imath_{\mathrm{L}_{j}}f)-\mathrm{L}_{j}\mathrm{d}^{\prime}(\imath_{\mathrm{L}_{j}}f)\right)+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathrm{L}_{j}\mathrm{d}^{\prime}(\imath_{\mathrm{L}_{j}}f),

and focus on the first sum, which we develop by evaluating at
\mathrm{X}_{1},\ldots,\mathrm{X}_{q}\in\mathfrak{v}
. Thanks to (
2.5
), we have

\displaystyle\left(\mathrm{d}^{\prime}\mathrm{L}_{j}(\imath_{\mathrm{L}_{j}}f)-\mathrm{L}_{j}\mathrm{d}^{\prime}(\imath_{\mathrm{L}_{j}}f)\right)(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{X}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{q}(-1)^{k+1}[\mathrm{X}_{k},\mathrm{L}_{j}]\left((\imath_{\mathrm{L}_{j}}f)(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{X}}}_{k})\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{q}(-1)^{k+1}[\mathrm{X}_{k},\mathrm{L}_{j}]f(\mathrm{L}_{j},\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{X}}}_{k}),

hence

\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\mathrm{d}^{\prime}\mathrm{L}_{j}(\imath_{\mathrm{L}_{j}}f)-\mathrm{L}_{j}\mathrm{d}^{\prime}(\imath_{\mathrm{L}_{j}}f)\right)(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{X}})=\sum_{k=1}^{q}(-1)^{k+1}\sum_{j=1}^{n}[\mathrm{X}_{k},\mathrm{L}_{j}]f(\mathrm{L}_{j},\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{X}}}_{k}).

We then apply Lemma
A.1
to the
1
-form
f(\cdot,\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{X}}}_{k})
and
\mathrm{L}=\mathrm{X}_{k}
:

\sum_{j=1}^{n}[\mathrm{X}_{k},\mathrm{L}_{j}]f(\mathrm{L}_{j},\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{X}}}_{k})=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathrm{L}_{j}f([\mathrm{L}_{j},\mathrm{X}_{k}],\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{X}}}_{k}),

which we replace in the previous identity to obtain

\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\mathrm{d}^{\prime}\mathrm{L}_{j}(\imath_{\mathrm{L}_{j}}f)-\mathrm{L}_{j}\mathrm{d}^{\prime}(\imath_{\mathrm{L}_{j}}f)\right)(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{X}})=\sum_{k=1}^{q}(-1)^{k+1}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathrm{L}_{j}f([\mathrm{L}_{j},\mathrm{X}_{k}],\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{X}}}_{k}).

Now we study the last sum in (
A.4
):

\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathrm{L}_{j}\mathrm{d}^{\prime}(\imath_{\mathrm{L}_{j}}f)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathrm{L}_{j}(\mathcal{L}^{\prime}_{\mathrm{L}_{j}}f-\imath_{\mathrm{L}_{j}}(\mathrm{d}^{\prime}f))=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathrm{L}_{j}\mathcal{L}^{\prime}_{\mathrm{L}_{j}}f,

which we again evaluate at
\mathrm{X}_{1},\ldots,\mathrm{X}_{q}\in\mathfrak{v}
:

(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{L}_{j}}^{\prime}f)(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{X}})=\mathrm{L}_{j}\left(f(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{X}})\right)+\sum_{k=1}^{q}(-1)^{k}f([\mathrm{L}_{j},\mathrm{X}_{k}],\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{X}}}_{k}),

hence

(\mathrm{L}_{j}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{L}_{j}}^{\prime}f)(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{X}})=\mathrm{L}_{j}\left((\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{L}_{j}}^{\prime}f)(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{X}})\right)=\mathrm{L}_{j}^{2}\left(f(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{X}})\right)+\sum_{k=1}^{q}(-1)^{k}\mathrm{L}_{j}f([\mathrm{L}_{j},\mathrm{X}_{k}],\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{X}}}_{k}),

and thus

\sum_{j=1}^{n}(\mathrm{L}_{j}\mathcal{L}^{\prime}_{\mathrm{L}_{j}}f)(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{X}})=-(\Delta_{\mathfrak{v}}f)(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{X}})+\sum_{k=1}^{q}(-1)^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathrm{L}_{j}f([\mathrm{L}_{j},\mathrm{X}_{k}],\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{X}}}_{k}).

Summing everything up, all the inconvenient terms cancel out leaving us with

(\mathrm{d}^{\prime}v)(\mathrm{X}_{1},\ldots,\mathrm{X}_{q})=-(\Delta_{\mathfrak{v}}f)(\mathrm{X}_{1},\ldots,\mathrm{X}_{q}),\quad\forall\mathrm{X}_{1},\ldots,\mathrm{X}_{q}\in\mathfrak{v},

which is enough to prove our assertion as forms in
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(G;\underline{\Lambda}^{q})
are completely determined by their action on vectors in
\mathfrak{v}
.
∎

SGA-determined nutritional status predicts cardiac index and protein-energy wasting in peritoneal dialysis patients with end-stage renal disease
Authors: Nuerziye Abulikemu, Renaguli Nuer
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo investigate the relationships between Subjective Global Assessment (SGA)-determined nutritional status and cardiac index (CI), protein-energy wasting (PEW), prognostic factors, and clinical parameters in peritoneal dialysis patients with end-stage renal disease (ESRD).MethodsA total of 80 ESRD patients treated between December 2019 and December 2021 were categorized into well-nourished (n = 36) and malnourished (n = 44) groups based on SGA scoring. Nutritional status, CI, and PEW were assessed. Clinical parameters and prognostic factors, including C-reactive protein (CRP), fetuin-A, and interleukin-6 (IL-6), were measured. Multivariate logistic regression and Spearman’s correlation analyses were employed to identify independent influencing factors and assess variable relationships.ResultsSignificant intergroup differences (p < 0.05) were observed for PEW prevalence, CI, CRP, fetuin-A, IL-6, serum creatinine, and albumin levels. Binary logistic regression identified PEW status, CI, fetuin-A, IL-6, serum creatinine, and albumin levels as independent factors associated with malnutrition. Spearman’s correlation analysis revealed strong positive correlations between SGA scores and PEW prevalence, CI, fetuin-A, IL-6, serum creatinine, and albumin (all p < 0.001). The combined prognostic model demonstrated excellent discriminatory power, with an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.960 (p < 0.05).ConclusionSGA-determined nutritional status significantly influences CI, PEW development, and key clinical biomarkers in ESRD patients. Systematic assessment of these parameters provides valuable prognostic stratification and can guide therapeutic interventions for malnutrition management in this population.Keywords: End-stage renal disease, Subjective Global Assessment, cardiac index, protein-energy wasting, prognosis, clinical parameters

Introduction

End-stage renal disease (ESRD) represents a clinical condition characterized by severely diminished kidney function, typically below 15% of normal capacity [1]. Renal failure manifests in two primary forms: acute kidney injury (AKI), which develops rapidly and may be reversible and chronic kidney disease (CKD), which progresses gradually and is generally irreversible [2]. Clinical manifestations of renal failure encompass diverse symptoms including peripheral edema, fatigue, vomiting, anorexia, and cognitive impairment [3]. Both AKI and CKD present significant complications such as uremia, hyperkalemia, and volume overload [4]. Substantial evidence indicates that hemodialysis treatment in ESRD patients frequently precipitates malnutrition [5]. This malnutrition directly correlates with increased morbidity and mortality, primarily stemming from inadequate nutrient intake, underscoring the critical importance of optimal nutritional support [6]. Multiple validated standards currently exist for assessing nutritional status in hemodialysis-dependent ESRD patients [7]. The Subjective Global Assessment (SGA) serves as a comprehensive nutritional evaluation tool, utilizing criteria including body weight trajectory, dietary changes, gastrointestinal symptoms, and physical examination findings of subcutaneous fat and muscle mass loss [8]. This method integrates key aspects of nutrient intake and body composition, offering several advantages such as operational simplicity, low cost, rapid administration, and strong correlations with diverse nutritional biomarkers [9]. ESRD significantly impacts cardiac index (CI) and contributes to protein-energy wasting (PEW), particularly in patients undergoing peritoneal dialysis [10,11]. Due to the multifactorial etiology of PEW [12], its precise pathophysiological mechanisms remain incompletely understood. This study aims to investigate the relationships of SGA scores with CI levels, PEW development, prognostic factors, and other relevant clinical parameters in ESRD patients receiving peritoneal dialysis.

Materials and methods

General information

A total of 80 patients with ESRD treated in our hospital from December 2019 to December 2021 were enrolled as study subjects. Participants were stratified into two groups according to their SGA scoring: a well-nourished group (n = 36) and a malnourished group (n = 44). The well-nourished group comprised 21 males and 15 females, with an age range of 27–77 years (mean age, 54.32 ± 14.41 years) and a body weight range of 45–70 kg (mean body weight, 59.66 ± 11.78 kg). The malnourished group included 23 males and 21 females, aged 28 to 78 years (mean age, 55.21 ± 13.23 years) with body weights ranging from 44 to 69 kg (mean body weight, 59.12 ± 12.24 kg). No statistically significant differences were observed in baseline characteristics between the two groups (p > 0.05), indicating comparability. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of The First Affiliated Hospital of Xinjiang Medical University (No. 2022017), and written informed consent was obtained from all participants or their legal guardians.

Inclusion criteria: (1) Age between 25 and 80 years; (2) Receiving continuous ambulatory peritoneal dialysis (CAPD) for a minimum duration of three months; (3) Performing 3–5 exchanges per day, with a dwell time of 4–6 h for each exchange; (4) Meeting diagnostic criteria for ESRD as defined by the Chinese Guidelines for Prevention and Treatment of Diabetic Kidney Disease (2021 Edition) [12]; (5) Voluntary participation in this study.

Exclusion criteria: (1) Presence of massive proteinuria (>3.5 g/24 h); (2) Concurrent diagnosis of liver cirrhosis or acute infectious disease; (3) Terminally ill status with a life expectancy of less than six months; and (4) Occurrence of infection, severe heart failure, acute myocardial infarction, gastrointestinal disease, active liver disease, or other critical conditions within the preceding 2 months.

Methods

Nutritional assessment via SGA

Nutritional status was evaluated using the SGA [8], a validated clinical tool incorporating six subjective domains: dietary intake, gastrointestinal symptoms, physical signs, physiological status, among others. Each domain is rated according to a three-tiered classification system: A (well-nourished, scored as 5 points), B (moderately malnourished, 3 points), and C (severely malnourished, 1 point). A composite score exceeding 25 points indicates adequate nutritional status, whereas a score below this threshold identifies patients with malnutrition.

Assessment of other clinical parameters

Following an overnight fast of at least 12 h, study participants underwent standardized measurement of key biochemical parameters. These included hemoglobin, serum creatinine, uric acid, albumin, triglycerides, total cholesterol, high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C), low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C), fasting blood glucose, potassium, calcium, and inorganic phosphate levels, all quantified using established laboratory protocols.

Assessment of prognostic factors

Serum C-reactive protein (CRP) levels were quantified using nephelometry [13], with concentrations ≥ 10 mg/L indicative of systemic inflammation. Fetuin-A (0–019372, Shanghai Jianglai Biotechnology Co., Ltd.) and interleukin-6 (IL-6) (JL14113, Shanghai Jianglai Biotechnology Co., Ltd.) concentrations were determined in serum samples using commercially available enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) kits. All samples not analyzed immediately were stored at −80 °C to preserve analyte integrity.

Observation parameters

Cardiac function assessment

CI was assessed at baseline and after 4 weeks of treatment using an SSD-210F color Doppler ultrasound system, Measurements were performed across three consecutive cardiac cycles to ensure accuracy. CI was calculated as cardiac output (CO) divided by body surface area (BSA), where CO was derived from Doppler measurements of left ventricular outflow tract (LVOT) diameter and velocity–time integral (VTI). CI was expressed in liters per minute per square meter (L/min/m2). A CI value between 2.5 and 4.0 L/min/m2 is generally considered the normal range, with values below this threshold indicating impaired cardiac function and those above suggesting possible high-output states. In the context of ESRD, reduced CI is clinically significant as it reflects compromised cardiovascular performance, which may exacerbate malnutrition and PEW development.

PEW assessment

PEW was diagnosed according to the International Society of Renal Nutrition and Metabolism (ISRNM) criteria [14], requiring fulfillment of at least three out of four principal domains with at least one criterion per domain: (1) serum biochemistry: serum albumin <38 g/L or total cholesterol <100 mg/dL; (2) Body composition: body mass index (BMI) <23.0 kg/m2, >5% weight loss over three months or >10% over six months, or body fat percentage <10%; (3) Muscle mass: >5% muscle mass reduction over three months or >10% over six months, or mid-arm circumference reduction >10% below the population median; (4) Dietary intake: protein intake <0.8 g/kg/day or energy intake <25 kcal/kg/day. Dietary assessments were conducted by certified nutritionists through analysis of 3 consecutive days of food records. Protein intake was calculated and normalized to each patient’s ideal body mass weight (IBW), determined as [height (cm) − 100] for males and [height (cm) − 105] for females. Weight loss was calculated based on serial body weight measurements recorded during routine dialysis visits. Muscle mass was evaluated by mid-arm muscle circumference, derived from mid-arm circumference and triceps skinfold thickness. Body fat percentage was estimated using skinfold calipers (triceps, biceps, subscapular, and suprailiac sites) and calculated according to standard equations. Among the cohort, 64 patients met PEW diagnostic criteria, comprising 25 from the well-nourished group and 39 from the malnourished group.

Statistical analysis

All collected data were systematically organized into dedicated databases and analyzed using SPSS version 26.0. Normally distributed continuous variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (x¯±s) and compared using t-test. Categorical variables were presented as percentages (%) and analyzed with the chi-square (χ2) test. Multivariate logistic regression analysis was employed to identify independent influencing factors, while Spearman’s correlation analysis assessed relationships between variables. A P-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant for all analyses.

Discussion

The therapeutic management of renal failure encompasses pharmacological interventions, surgical procedures, nutritional support, traditional Chinese medicine (TCM), and adjunctive modalities, with nutritional therapy constituting the fundamental cornerstone of comprehensive care [15–17]. Central to nutritional management is the implementation of protein-restricted diets, which demonstrably reduce urinary protein excretion, thereby attenuating the progression of chronic renal impairment [18]. Early diagnosis and timely intervention in renal failure are paramount for minimizing nephron injury and facilitating functional recovery [19]. This necessitates prompt identification and correction of reversible etiologies, maintenance of homeostatic equilibrium, provision of adequate nutritional support, prevention and treatment of complications, and judicious initiation of renal replacement therapies when indicated.

Our analytical findings delineated statistically significant disparities between the well-nourished and malnourished cohorts regarding PEW prevalence, CI, CRP, fetuin-A, IL-6, serum creatinine, and albumin concentrations. Conversely, no statistically significant intergroup differences were observed for other evaluated parameters. Spearman correlation analysis further revealed robust positive associations between SGA scores and PEW status, CI, fetuin-A, IL-6, serum creatinine, and albumin levels in the ESRD population. These results align with established literature demonstrating fetuin-A’s association with nutritional status in ESRD, wherein well-nourished patients exhibit significantly elevated fetuin-A concentrations compared to malnourished counterparts [20]. Concordantly, our data indicated a substantially lower PEW incidence and higher CI values among well-nourished ESRD patients. The observation of elevated serum creatinine in malnourished ESRD subjects corroborates prior investigations [21]. Although PEW and SGA both describe malnutrition and share overlapping domains, they are not identical. SGA is a subjective, clinician-rated assessment integrating medical history and physical findings, whereas PEW follows ISRNM criteria and emphasizes objective biochemical, anthropometric, and dietary intake measures. By analyzing their relationship, our study highlights how objective PEW indicators can complement the clinical judgment of SGA, offering a more comprehensive nutritional assessment framework in ESRD patients.

Compared with previous studies, our work provides several novel insights. Earlier reports primarily emphasized single nutritional or inflammatory markers, such as albumin or CRP, when assessing malnutrition risk in ESRD patients [22,23]. In contrast, our study integrated SGA with multiple biomarkers (fetuin-A, IL-6, serum creatinine, and CI), demonstrating that a combined model has superior prognostic performance (AUC = 0.960). This suggests that SGA-based evaluation not only reflects overall nutritional status but also captures cardiovascular function and systemic inflammation, which earlier investigations rarely addressed in a unified framework. Furthermore, our findings extend previous evidence by identifying fetuin-A and IL-6 as independent predictors alongside traditional measures, thereby adding mechanistic depth to the understanding of PEW development in peritoneal dialysis patients.

Mechanistically, fetuin-A interacts with macrophages, modulating their inactivation capacity while enhancing cationic uptake, thereby mitigating cellular damage inflicted by pro-inflammatory cytokines such as IL-6 [24], which is a finding consistent with our demonstration of significantly lower IL-6 levels in well-nourished patients. Serum albumin, the predominant plasma protein, fulfills two physiologically critical roles: maintaining normative nutritional status and preserving colloid osmotic pressure [25]. Consequently, albumin serves as an indispensable biomarker of nutritional homeostasis. Protracted malnutrition invariably precipitates marked hypoalbuminemia, whereas nutritional rehabilitation typically elevates albumin concentrations [26]. This phenomenon is corroborated by our data showing significantly higher albumin levels in the well-nourished cohort.

This study demonstrated significant associations between SGA-determined nutritional status and CI, PEW, and inflammatory markers IL-6 and CRP in peritoneal dialysis patients with ESRD. Based on these findings, we propose the following clinical strategies: (1) Routine nutritional monitoring: Implement dynamic SGA assessment every 3 months for all peritoneal dialysis patients per the Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes (KDIGO) guidelines [27], focusing on body weight change, dietary intake, and muscle mass. (2) Targeted evaluation for malnutrition: For patients with moderate-to-severe malnutrition (SGA grade B/C), combine SGA with serial monitoring of serum albumin (target ≥38 g/L) and CI (target ≥3.5 L/min/m2) to enable early detection of PEW and cardiac function decline. (3) Nutritional and anti-inflammatory intervention: Recommend a regimen of high biological value protein (1.2–1.5 g/kg/day) combined with ω-3 fatty acid supplementation (1.0 g/day) to improve nutritional status and mitigate inflammation. (4) Inflammation management: Investigate potential infection or occult cardiovascular events in patients with persistently elevated CRP, and consider adjunctive anti-inflammatory therapy if indicated. (5) Multidisciplinary care for high-risk patients: Establish a multidisciplinary team to develop individualized dialysis and nutrition plans for PEW patients with CI < 3.0 L/min/m2. Intensify follow-up frequency (every 1–2 months) and integrate prognostic markers such as N-terminal pro-B-type natriuretic peptide (NT-proBNP) [28].

Notwithstanding these substantive contributions, certain methodological limitations warrant acknowledgment. The relatively constrained sample size potentially limits generalizability across the broader ESRD population. Future investigations should prioritize expanded recruitment to permit more comprehensive exploration of the relationships between SGA-derived nutritional status, CI, peritoneal dialysis-associated PEW, prognostic factors, and other clinically relevant parameters in ESRD. In addition, although our prognostic model achieved a high AUC (0.960), its clinical applicability is limited because it incorporates parameters such as IL-6, serum creatinine, and cardiac index, which require specialized assays or advanced diagnostic technologies that may be time-consuming and costly. By contrast, SGA itself remains a simple, noninvasive, rapid, and inexpensive tool already validated in clinical practice. Therefore, the added value of our model lies mainly in providing mechanistic insights and identifying potential biomarkers for future studies, while subsequent research should focus on developing simplified and clinically feasible models using more readily available indicators.

Conclusion

Clinical stratification of ESRD patients via SGA scoring effectively discriminates between well-nourished and malnourished subgroups, revealing statistically significant differences and positive correlations in PEW prevalence, CI, fetuin-A, IL-6, serum creatinine, and albumin concentrations. Therefore, systematic assessment of these biomarkers may provide clinically actionable insights for prognostic evaluation and therapeutic guidance, meriting broader implementation in nephrology practice.

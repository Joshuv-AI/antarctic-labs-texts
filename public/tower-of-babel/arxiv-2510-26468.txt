Spin-orbit coupled spin-boson model : A variational analysis
Sudip Sinha, S. Sinha, S. Dattagupta

Abstract
The spin-boson (SB) model is a standard prototype for quantum dissipation, which we generalize in this work, to explore the dissipative effects on a one-dimensional spin-orbit (SO) coupled particle in the presence of a sub-ohmic bath. We analyze this model by extending the well-known variational polaron approach, revealing a localization transition accompanied by an intriguing change in the spectrum, for which the doubly degenerate minima evolves to a single minimum at zero momentum as the system-bath coupling increases. For translational invariant system with conserved momentum, a continuous magnetization transition occurs, whereas the ground state changes discontinuously. We further investigate the transition of the ground state in the presence of harmonic confinement, which effectively models a quantum dot–like nanostructure under the influence of the environment. In both the scenarios, the entanglement entropy of the spin-sector can serve as a marker for these transitions. Interestingly, for the trapped system, a cat-like superposition state corresponds to maximum entanglement entropy below the transition, highlighting the relevance of the present model for studying the effect of decoherence on intra-particle entanglement in the context of quantum information processing.

II
Model and variational analysis

We consider a 1D SO coupled particle in the presence of a bosonic bath described by the total
Hamiltonian,
\hat{\mathcal{H}}=\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm SO}+\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm B}+\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm I}
, where,

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm SO}

\displaystyle=\frac{\hat{p}^{2}_{x}}{2m}-q\hbar\hat{\sigma}_{x}\frac{\hat{p}_{x}}{m}+\delta\hat{\sigma}_{z},

(1a)

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm B}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l}\hbar\omega_{l}\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{l}\hat{b}_{l},

(1b)

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm I}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l}\uplambda_{l}(\hat{b}_{l}+\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{l})\hat{\sigma}_{z}.

(1c)

The second term in
\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm SO}
[Eq. (
1a
)] corresponds to the SO coupled particle with tunable SO coupling strength
q
, and the third term with
\delta
represents an additional Rabi coupling
Spielmann2011_SS
.
On the other hand, Eq. (
1b
) represents the bosonic bath, where
\hat{b}_{l}
are the annihilation operators of the bath modes with energy
\hbar\omega_{l}
. The system-bath interaction is described by Eq. (
1c
), where the
z
-component of the spin is linearly coupled to the bath modes with coupling strength
\uplambda_{l}
similar to the SB model
Weiss_book
;
Petruccione_book
;
Leggett_review
. In the rest of this paper, we set
\hbar,\,k_{B},\,m=1
, and for simplicity, we set
\delta=0
.

Notably, the momentum is a conserved quantity here, since
[\hat{\mathcal{H}},\hat{p}_{x}]=0
. Consequently, the momentum eigenstate

\ket{k}
of the SO coupled particle is the suitable basis to analyze this model. For a fixed momentum
k
, the above Hamiltonian
\hat{\mathcal{H}}
reduces to that of the SB model with an effective spin tunneling strength
qk/m
. Next, we analyze this system for a fixed value of
k
using the variational framework, a method that has been extensively employed to investigate the properties of the SB model
Zheng2011
.
This approach can also provide the energy-momentum dispersion relation of the SO coupled particle, thereby capturing the effects of the bosonic bath.

III
Variational Analysis for conserved momentum

To investigate the ground state properties of SO coupled SB model, we employ a variational polaron transformation method, originally developed for the SB model
Silbey_Harris
and later generalized in subsequent studies
Chin2006
;
plenio_prl
;
Zheng2011
;
Tong2011
;
Chin2014_environmental_entanglement
;
Chen2018
.
Following unitary transformation is adopted to derive the effective Hamiltonian for a fixed momentum
k
,

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{U}}=\hat{\mathcal{U}}_{+}\ket{\uparrow}\!\bra{\uparrow}+\hat{\mathcal{U}}_{-}\ket{\downarrow}\!\bra{\downarrow},

(2)

where
\hat{\mathcal{U}}_{\pm}=\exp\left(-\sum_{l}f^{(\pm)}_{l}(\hat{b}_{l}-\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{l})\right)
generates displacement by an amount
f^{(\pm)}_{l}
of the bath oscillators, effectively describing them as coherent states. Here the shifts
f^{(\pm)}_{l}
are considered as variational parameters. The states
\ket{\uparrow}
and
\ket{\downarrow}
denote the spin-up and spin-down states, respectively. By applying the transformation in Eq. (
2
) to the Hamiltonian
\hat{\mathcal{H}}
, we obtain the effective Hamiltonian
\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}
as follows,

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}=\hat{\mathcal{U}}^{\dagger}\hat{\mathcal{H}}\hat{\mathcal{U}}=\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}_{0}+\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}_{1}+\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}_{2},

(3)

where the individual terms for a fixed momentum
k
are given by,

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}_{0}=

\displaystyle\,\frac{k^{2}}{2}\!-\!|k|\tilde{q}\hat{\sigma}_{x}\!+\!B_{+}\ket{\uparrow}\!\bra{\uparrow}\!+\!B_{-}\ket{\downarrow}\!\bra{\downarrow}\!+\!\sum_{l}\omega_{l}\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{l}\hat{b}_{l},

(4a)

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}_{1}=

\displaystyle\sum_{l}(\hat{b}_{l}+\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{l})\,\Big[\!\left(f^{(+)}_{l}\omega_{l}\!+\!\uplambda_{l}\right)\!\ket{\uparrow}\!\bra{\uparrow}\!+\!\left(f^{(-)}_{l}\omega_{l}\!-\!\uplambda_{l}\right)\!\ket{\downarrow}\!\bra{\downarrow}\!\Big]

\displaystyle+\!\dot{\imath}|k|\tilde{q}\hat{\sigma}_{y}\sum_{l}(\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{l}-\hat{b}_{l})\left(f^{(+)}_{l}-f^{(-)}_{l}\right)

(4b)

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}_{2}=

\displaystyle-\!|k|q\hat{\sigma}_{x}\left[\cosh\left(\sum_{l}\left(f^{(+)}_{l}-f^{(-)}_{l}\right)(\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{l}-\hat{b}_{l})\right)-\eta\right]

\displaystyle+\!\dot{\imath}|k|q\hat{\sigma}_{y}\Bigg[\!\sinh\left(\sum_{l}\left(f^{(+)}_{l}-f^{(-)}_{l}\right)(\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{l}-\hat{b}_{l})\right)

\displaystyle-\!\eta\!\sum_{l}\left(f^{(+)}_{l}-f^{(-)}_{l}\right)(\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{l}-\hat{b}_{l})\Bigg].

(4c)

Here,
B_{\pm}\!\!=\!\!\sum_{l}\left(f^{(\pm)2}_{l}\omega_{l}\pm 2f^{(\pm)}\uplambda_{l}\right)
, and the renormalized SO coupling is represented by
\tilde{q}=q\eta
with,

\displaystyle\eta=\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{l}\left(f^{(+)}_{l}-f^{(-)}_{l}\right)^{2}\right),

(5)

which plays a key role in the delocalization to localization transition in the SB model
Silbey_Harris
.
In the above transformed Hamiltonian, we only consider the first two terms and neglect
\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}_{2}
, as it describes two particle and higher order excitation process above the bosonic vacuum state
Zheng2011
.
The Hamiltonian in Eq. (
4a
) can be diagonalized, yielding two energy branches.

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\pm}(k)=\frac{k^{2}}{2}+\frac{\left(B_{+}+B_{-}\right)}{2}\pm\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{(B_{+}-B_{-})^{2}+4k^{2}\tilde{q}^{2}},

(6)

Correspondingly, the ground state wavefunction can be written in the form,

\displaystyle\ket{G}=\left(u_{-}\ket{\uparrow}+v_{-}\ket{\downarrow}\right)\otimes\ket{0}_{b}\otimes\ket{k},

(7)

where
u_{-}(v_{-})
are given by,

\displaystyle u_{-}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{\epsilon}{W}\right)},\quad v_{-}=\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\frac{\epsilon}{W}\right)},

(8)

with
\epsilon=B_{+}-B_{-}
and
W=\sqrt{\epsilon^{2}+4k^{2}\tilde{q}^{2}}
. Furthermore, assuming that the first order perturbations of the ground state vanishes, i.e.
\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{1}^{\prime}\ket{G}=0
, the following conditions are obtained,

\displaystyle\left(\uplambda_{l}+f^{(+)}_{l}\omega_{l}\right)u_{-}+|k|\tilde{q}\left(f^{(+)}_{l}-f^{(-)}_{l}\right)v_{-}

\displaystyle=0

(9a)

\displaystyle\left(\uplambda_{l}-f^{(+)}_{l}\omega_{l}\right)v_{-}+|k|\tilde{q}\left(f^{(+)}_{l}-f^{(-)}_{l}\right)u_{-}

\displaystyle=0.

(9b)

Substituting Eq. (
8
) in the above equations yields the form of the variational parameters,

\displaystyle f^{(\pm)}_{l}=\frac{\uplambda_{l}}{\omega_{l}}\frac{\left(2|k|\tilde{q}\epsilon/W\mp\sqrt{\left(1-\epsilon^{2}/W^{2}\right)}\omega_{l}\right)}{\left(2|k|\tilde{q}+\sqrt{\left(1-\epsilon^{2}/W^{2}\right)}\omega_{l}\right)}.

(10)

Note that, the same form of the variational parameters
f^{(\pm)}_{l}
can also be obtained from minimizing
\mathcal{E}_{-}(k)
.

Since the magnetization of the ground state is defined as,
M=\bra{G}\hat{\sigma}_{z}\ket{G}
, it is easy to see that,

\displaystyle M=u^{2}_{-}-v^{2}_{-}=-\epsilon/W,

(11)

and
B_{\pm}
can be written as,

\displaystyle B_{\pm}=

\displaystyle\sum_{l}\frac{\uplambda^{2}_{l}}{\omega_{l}}\Big(4k^{2}\tilde{q}^{2}M^{2}-\omega^{2}_{l}(1-M^{2})-4|k|\tilde{q}\omega_{l}\sqrt{1-M^{2}}

\displaystyle\mp 8k^{2}\tilde{q}^{2}M\Big)/\left(2|k|\tilde{q}+\omega_{l}\sqrt{1-M^{2}}\right)^{2}.

(12)

To study the effects of the bath on the ground state properties in a systematic manner, we now consider the following spectral function of the bath
Bulla2005
,

\displaystyle J(\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi\sum_{l}\uplambda^{2}_{l}\delta(\omega-\omega_{l})

(13)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\pi\alpha\omega^{1-s}_{c}\omega^{s}\Theta(\omega_{c}-\omega),

where
0<s<1
,
s=1
,
s>1
, denote a sub-ohmic, ohmic, and super-ohmic bath, respectively. Here,
\omega_{c}
is the cut-off frequency of the bath and
\alpha
denotes the coupling constant. For the case of sub-ohmic bath, in the limit
\omega_{c}\!\gg\!kq
, the lowest branch of energy corresponding to the ground state [Eq. (
6
)] in the leading order of
|k|q/\omega_{c}
can be written as,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{-}(k)=

\displaystyle\,\frac{k^{2}}{2}\!-\!2\alpha\frac{\omega_{c}}{s}\!+\!2\alpha\omega_{c}(1+M^{2})\frac{\pi(1-s)}{\sin{\pi s}}\!\left(\frac{2|k|\tilde{q}}{\omega_{c}\sqrt{1-M^{2}}}\right)^{\!s}

\displaystyle\!-\!\frac{1}{2}\left[\left(-8M\alpha\omega_{c}\frac{\pi(1-s)}{\sin{\pi s}}\right)^{\!2}\!\!\left(\frac{2|k|\tilde{q}}{\omega_{c}\sqrt{1-M^{2}}}\right)^{\!2s}\!\!\!\!+4k^{2}\tilde{q}^{2}\right]^{\!\frac{1}{2}}\!\!,

(14)

where the effective SO coupling strength
\tilde{q}
is given by,

\displaystyle\tilde{q}=

\displaystyle\,q\exp\left(-4\alpha\!\!\int^{\omega_{c}}_{0}\!\!\frac{\omega^{1-s}_{c}\omega^{s}(1-M^{2})}{\left(2|k|\tilde{q}+\omega\sqrt{1-M^{2}}\right)^{2}}d\omega\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,q\exp\left(-4\alpha\frac{\pi s}{\sin{\pi s}}\left(\frac{2|k|\tilde{q}}{\omega_{c}\sqrt{1-M^{2}}}\right)^{\!s-1}\right)\exp\left(\frac{4\alpha}{(1-s)}\right).

(15)

The above Equation and Eqs. (
11
,
12
) can be solved self-consistently to yield the ground state energy
\mathcal{E}_{-}(k)
and magnetization
M
for a fixed value of momentum
k
.

Importantly, above self-consistent procedure yields the energy-momentum dispersion of the lowest branch, which undergoes significant change due to the coupling with bosonic bath. In the absence of a bath, the dispersion of SO coupled particle
\mathcal{E}_{-}(k)=\frac{k^{2}}{2}-q|k|
has two minima at
\pm q
and its spin is polarized along the x-axis depending on the direction of the momentum

, with the wavefunction
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\ket{\uparrow}+sgn(k)\ket{\downarrow})
.
With increasing the coupling to bath the dispersion gradually changes and most importantly, above a critical coupling strength this lowest energy branch exhibits only one minimum at momentum
k=0
. as shown in Fig.
1
(a).
Furthermore, the magnetization of the particle also undergoes a continuous transition. This SO coupled particle acquires a magnetized phase with
\langle\sigma_{z}\rangle\neq 0
due to the coupling with bath.

, the magnetization along the

Next, we investigate the entanglement between spin and the environment for different momentum

of the particle. By integrating out the bath degrees of freedom, the reduced density matrix of the SO coupled particle takes the form

, where

denotes the reduced density matrix corresponding to the spin degree of freedom, which is given by,

\hat{\rho}_{s}=|u_{-}|^{2}\ket{\uparrow}\!\bra{\uparrow}+|v_{-}|^{2}\ket{\downarrow}\!\bra{\downarrow}+u_{-}v_{-}\eta\left[\,\ket{\uparrow}\!\bra{\downarrow}+\ket{\downarrow}\!\bra{\uparrow}\,\right]

of the particle. The entanglement entropy increases from

[see Fig.

(d)]. As shown in Fig.

-

, which in absence of bath appear at

, representing doubly degenerate ground states. In the presence of bosonic bath, the strength of SO interaction

is renormalized, resulting in a decrease in momentum at which the doubly degenerate minima appear. As depicted in the top panel of Fig.

IV
Variational analysis in presence of Harmonic trap

In this section, we investigate how the ground state of the SO coupled system in the presence of a harmonic trap modifies due to interaction with the bosonic bath modes.
In the presence of a harmonic trap (with frequency
\omega_{0}
), the SO coupled Hamiltonian in Eq. (
1a
) can be written as,

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm SO}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dot{\iota}q\sqrt{\omega_{0}/2}\,(\hat{a}-\hat{a}^{\dagger})\hat{\sigma}_{x}+\omega_{0}\hat{a}^{\dagger}\hat{a},

(17)

where
\hat{a}=(\hat{x}\sqrt{\omega_{0}/2}+\dot{\imath}\hat{p}_{x}/\sqrt{2\omega_{0}})
denotes the ladder operator. Note that, unlike the previous case, momentum is not a conserved quantity any more in the presence of the trap. It is evident from Eq. (
17
), there are doubly degenerate ground states, given by,
\ket{\mathcal{U}_{\pm q}}\otimes\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}[\ket{\uparrow}\pm\ket{\downarrow}]
, where

\ket{\mathcal{U}_{\pm q}}=\exp\left(\frac{\pm\dot{\imath}q}{\sqrt{2\omega_{0}}}(\hat{a}+\hat{a}^{\dagger})\right)\ket{0}_{a}.

(18)

with
\ket{0}_{a}
representing the vacuum state of the oscillator.
These states correspond to the coherent states with momentum
\pm q
, and the spin being aligned along the
\pm x
-axis, respectively. Due to the presence of the bosonic bath modes, an effective magnetic field
\epsilon=B_{+}-B_{-}
is generated in the single-particle Hamiltonian [see Eq. (
4a
)], which tries to polarize the spins along the
z
-axis. In the presence of an additional polarising term
\epsilon\sigma_{z}
, the degenerate ground states can be written as,

\ket{\phi^{0}_{\pm k}}=\left[\cos{\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)}\ket{\uparrow}\pm\sin{\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)}\ket{\downarrow}\right]\ket{\mathcal{U}_{\pm k}},

(19)

where,the spin orientations, expressed in terms of the
SU(2)
representation, describe a symmetric arrangement of spin vectors along the positive and negative
x
-axes, each making an angle
\theta
with the
z
-axis.

Based on the above observations, we can construct a variational wavefunction in the presence of bath using the polaron transformation and a linear combination of these degenerate states with equal and opposite momentum
k
, that is given by,

|\psi_{G}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathcal{N}}}[\cos{\!\chi}\ket{\phi_{+k}}+\sin{\!\chi}\ket{\phi_{-k}}],

(20)

where,

\ket{\phi_{\pm k}}=\left[\cos{\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)}\ket{\uparrow}\ket{\mathcal{B}_{+}}\pm\sin{\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)}\ket{\downarrow}\ket{\mathcal{B}_{-}}\right]\ket{\mathcal{U}_{\pm k}}.

V
Discussions

We present a spin-orbit (SO) coupled spin-boson (SB) model to explore the effect of dissipation on a particle with SO interaction, for which the spin degree of freedom is coupled to a sub-ohmic heat bath. This system exhibits two distinct types of transitions: a continuous magnetization transition for a free particle with conserved linear momentum, and a discontinuous first-order transition of the ground state as the coupling to the bath increases. Moreover, this transition is accompanied by a striking modification in the energy–momentum dispersion of the SO coupled particle, where the doubly degenerate minima in the spectrum merge into a single minimum at zero momentum with increasing coupling to the bath. From a physical standpoint, the asymmetric displacement of bath oscillators induce an effective magnetic field that plays a crucial role in driving the magnetization transition and lifting the ground-state degeneracy. Moreover, the bath induced effective mass of the particle changes differently across the transition. Such dissipation-driven modifications in the spectrum and magnetic transitions can have a significant impact on transport properties, particularly in spintronic systems

Furthermore, we investigate the entanglement properties of the spin sector, where the entanglement entropy exhibits a sharp peak at the boundary of the magnetized phase, capturing the continuous transition, in contrast to the abrupt jump observed across the first-order transition associated with the change in the ground state of free system.
The presence of a weak trapping potential leads to a superposition of the two degenerate ground states with equal and opposite momenta, rendering them quasi-degenerate with an exponentially small energy gap. Importantly, this generates maximally mixed spin state (with high entanglement) below the transition. However, above a critical bath coupling strength, the quasi-degeneracy associated with the ground state is lifted and entanglement decreases monotonically.
Insights into the behavior of such intra-particle entanglement under environmental influence are highly relevant for quantum information processing

Moreover, the superposition of two degenerate states with equal and opposite momenta generates a ‘cat-like’ non-classical state, with phase-space density concentrated around the two opposite momenta, while the interference effects are suppressed due to the orthogonal alignment of the spins, resulting in a dephased cat state.
The study of single-particle physics provides important understanding of exotic quantum phases that emerge from the interplay of SO coupling and inter-particle interactions, which has recently been explored in SO coupled condensates.
In particular, the superposition of two momentum-carrying states gives rise to an intriguing superstripe-supersolid phase, which has been observed experimentally

In conclusion, we have analyzed a SO coupled spin-boson system that exhibits a dissipation-driven transition accompanied by a modification of the energy spectrum.
Such effects are relevant to electronic systems with strong SO coupling, including Graphene, as well as to cold-atom setups with tunable synthetic SO coupling, which can also find potential applications in quantum information processing.

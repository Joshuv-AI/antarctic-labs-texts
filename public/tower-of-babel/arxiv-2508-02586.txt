The Length of Functional Batch and PIR Codes
Altan B. Kılıç, Alberto Ravagnani, Flavio Salizzoni

Abstract
We consider the problem of computing the minimum length of functional batch and PIR codes of fixed dimension and for a fixed list size, over an arbitrary finite field. We recover, generalize, and refine several results that were previously obtained for binary codes. We present new upper and lower bounds for the minimum length, and discuss the asymptotic behaviour of this parameter. We also compute its value for several parameter sets. The paper also offers insights into the “correct” list size to consider for the Functional Batch Conjecture over non-binary finite fields, and establishes various supporting results.

This paper focuses on the block length of functional batch codes and PIR (private information retrieval) codes. Both types of codes are central objects in the realm of distributed data storage.

2
Problem statement and preliminaries

We introduce the problem statement and establishes the notation for the rest of the paper.

Notation 2.1
.

In the sequel,
q
denotes a prime power and
k,t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
. Let
M\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k\times n}
,
L
be a list of nonzero vectors in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k}
, and
I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}.
Denote by
M^{I}
the set columns of
M
indexed by
I
. If
|I|=1
, say
I=\{i\}
, we simply write
M^{i}
instead of
M^{\{i\}}
. Assume that the list
L
consists of
\ell
different vectors
v_{1},\ldots,v_{\ell}
such that they appear
m_{1},\ldots,m_{\ell}
times in
L
, respectively. In this case, we write

L=\{v_{1}^{m_{1}},\ldots,v_{\ell}^{m_{\ell}}\}.

Next, we formally define what it means for a matrix to “serve” a list of vectors.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
M\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k\times n}
and
v\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k}
. A set
R\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}
is called a
recovery set
for
v
if

v\in\langle M^{R}\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}.

A matrix
M\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k\times n}
can
serve
the list
L=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{t}\}
of nonzero vectors in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k}
if there exist pairwise disjoint
subsets
X_{1},\dots,X_{t}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}
such that

X_{i}
is a recovery set for
v_{i}

for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,t\}
. We equivalently say that
L
is
served by

G
.

Private information retrieval (PIR) codes have been studied extensively due to their property of having mutually disjoint recovery sets for any of the information bits. This concept has been extended to three other families of codes.

Definition 2.3
.

An
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear code
\mathcal{C}
is:

•

a
t
-PIR
code if there exists a generator matrix
M\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k\times n}
of
\mathcal{C}
that can serve the list
L=\{e_{i}^{t}\}
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
;

•

a
t
-batch
code if there exists a generator matrix
M\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k\times n}
of
\mathcal{C}
that can serve any list of the form

L=\{e_{1}^{t_{1}},\ldots,e_{k}^{t_{k}}\}
with
t_{1}+\dots+t_{k}=t
;

•

a
t
-functional PIR
code if there exists a generator matrix
M\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k\times n}
of
\mathcal{C}
that can serve the list
L=\{v^{t}\}
for any nonzero vector
v\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k}
;

•

a
t
-functional batch
code if there exists a generator matrix
M\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k\times n}
of
\mathcal{C}
that can serve any list
L
of
t
nonzero vectors in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k}
.

In this paper we will only work on
t
-functional PIR and
t
-functional batch codes. Note that a
t
-functional batch is a
t
-batch code, and a
t
-functional PIR code is a
t
-PIR code. Moreover, a
t
-functional batch code is a
t
-functional PIR code.

The main problem in studying these codes is to minimize their length given the values
k
and
t
. That is, one wants to find the smallest integer
n
for which there exists a
t
-functional PIR code and a
t
-functional batch code of dimension
k
and length
n
. To this end, in this paper we study the following parameters.

Definition 2.4
.

Let

\displaystyle\textnormal{FP}(k,t,q)

\displaystyle=\min\{n\in\mathbb{N}\mid\mbox{there exists a }k\mbox{-dimensional }t\mbox{-functional PIR code}\},

\displaystyle\textnormal{FB}(k,t,q)

\displaystyle=\min\{n\in\mathbb{N}\mid\mbox{there exists a }k\mbox{-dimensional }t\mbox{-functional batch code}\}.

It follows from the definitions that

\textnormal{FB}(k,t,q)\geq\textnormal{FP}(k,t,q).

The next observation shows that the choice of the generator matrix is irrelevant when computing
the minima defined above.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
G\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k\times k}
be any invertible matrix. We have that
M
achieves
\textnormal{FB}(k,t,q)
, or
\textnormal{FP}(k,t,q)
, if and only if
GM
does.

The next proposition establishes some general properties of the
functions
\textnormal{FP}(k,t,q)
and
\textnormal{FB}(k,t,q)
.

Proposition 2.6
.

The functions
\textnormal{FP}(k,t,q)
and
\textnormal{FB}(k,t,q)
are:

1.

strictly increasing in
k
and
t
;

2.

subadditive in
k
and
t
.

Proof.

The subadditivity of the functions is proven in
[
14
]
, and the strict monotonicity of the functions in
t
is proven in
[
18
]
for
q=2
. The same proofs also work for arbitrary field size. The fact that
\textnormal{FP}(k,t,q)
and
\textnormal{FB}(k,t,q)
are strictly increasing in
k
will be proven in Proposition
4.2
.∎

We conclude this section by stressing that, to the best of our knowledge, the problem considered in this paper
has only been studied for
q=2
. We refer to
[
18
,
17
]
.

Exact values

In this section we compute the exact value of
\textnormal{FB}(k,t,q)
for some choices of the parameters. When

\textnormal{FB}(1,t,q)=t
and
\textnormal{FB}(k,1,q)=k

3.1
Length of 2-dimensional FB codes

We focus on the case

k=2
for any prime power
q
. The final statement is the following.

Theorem 3.1
.

\textnormal{FB}(2,t,q)=\lceil 2(q+1)t/(q+2)\rceil=t+\lceil qt/(q+2)\rceil
.

To prove Theorem
3.1
, we need some preliminary results. The next lemma follows from a straightforward parity analysis.

Lemma 3.2
.

For any positive integer
z
, the following hold.

1.

\lfloor(z+1)/2\rfloor+1=\lceil(z+2)/2\rceil
,

2.

\lceil(z+2)/2\rceil+\lfloor z/2\rfloor=z+1
,

3.

\lfloor(z+1)/2\rfloor=\left\lceil(z\lfloor(z+1)/2\rfloor)/(z+2)\right\rceil=\left\lceil(z\lfloor(z+1)/2\rfloor+z)/(z+2)\right\rceil
,

4.

z=\left\lceil z(z+1)/(z+2)\right\rceil
.

We continue with a lower bound on
\textnormal{FB}(2,t,q)

Lemma 3.3
.

\textnormal{FB}(2,t,q)\geq\left\lceil\frac{2(q+1)t}{q+2}\right\rceil=t+\left\lceil\frac{qt}{q+2}\right\rceil.

Proof.

Let
G
be a matrix that attains
\textnormal{FB}(2,t,q)

G
can be seen as a multiset of points in

Let
v_{0}=(0,1)^{\top}
and
v_{i}=(1,i-1)^{\top}
for

be the multiplicity of

G

If
t<n_{j}
for some

G
cannot attain
\textnormal{FB}(2,t,q)
since a matrix obtained by removing the extra

columns that are equal to

would suffice to serve any list of
t

Therefore we have

for all

Let

To serve
t

\textnormal{FB}(2,t,q)\geq n_{j}+2(t-n_{j}).
Applying this for all

and summing the constraints yields

(q+1)\textnormal{FB}(2,t,q)\geq\textnormal{FB}(2,t,q)+2(q+1)t-2\textnormal{FB}(2,t,q),

which gives the desired result.
∎

We are now ready to prove Theorem

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

We will prove the following statement equivalent to the theorem:
For
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}
with
0\leq x
and
0\leq y<q+2
, we have

\textnormal{FB}(2,x(q+2)+y,q)=2(q+1)x+\left\lceil\frac{2(q+1)y}{q+2}\right\rceil.

By Lemma
3.3
, we have

\displaystyle\textnormal{FB}(2,x(q+2)+y,q)

\displaystyle\geq x(q+2)+y+\left\lceil\frac{q(x(q+2)+y)}{q+2}\right\rceil

\displaystyle=x(q+2)+\left\lceil y+\frac{(q+2)qx+qy}{q+2}\right\rceil

\displaystyle=x(q+2)+qx+\left\lceil y+\frac{qy}{q+2}\right\rceil

\displaystyle=2(q+1)x+\left\lceil\frac{2(q+1)y}{q+2}\right\rceil.

By Proposition
2.6
we have

\textnormal{FB}(2,x(q+2)+y,q)\leq x\textnormal{FB}(2,(q+2),q)+\textnormal{FB}(2,y,q).
By Lemma
3.4
, we have
\textnormal{FB}(2,(q+2),q)=2(q+1)
, which implies that

\textnormal{FB}(2,x(q+2)+y,q)\leq 2(q+1)x+\textnormal{FB}(2,y,q).

Note that if
y=0
the lower and upper bounds coincide, giving the desired result. Therefore for the rest of the proof we assume
1\leq y\leq q+1
.

By Lemma
3.4
, we have

\textnormal{FB}(2,y,q)\leq\begin{cases}2y&\textnormal{ if }1\leq y\leq\lfloor(q+1)/2\rfloor,\\
2y-1&\textnormal{ if }y=\lfloor(q+1)/2\rfloor+1,\\
2\lceil(q+2)/2\rceil-1+2j&\textnormal{ if }y=\lceil(q+2)/2\rceil+j\mbox{ for }j\in\{1,\ldots,\lfloor q/2\rfloor\}.\end{cases}

(1)

Therefore to conclude the proof it suffices to show that

\mbox{``the right-hand side of \eqref{fb2yq}''}=\left\lceil\frac{2(q+1)y}{q+2}\right\rceil=y+\left\lceil qy/(q+2)\right\rceil,

(2)

so that the lower and the upper bound coincide. We analyze each case separately.

1.

Let
1\leq y\leq\lfloor(q+1)/2\rfloor
. If
y=1
, then
2y=y+\left\lceil qy/(q+2)\right\rceil
. If
y=\lfloor(q+1)/2\rfloor
, then
2y=y+\left\lceil qy/(q+2)\right\rceil
by the third equality in Lemma
3.2
.

2.

Let
y=\lfloor(q+1)/2\rfloor+1.
Then,
2y-1=y+\left\lceil qy/(q+2)\right\rceil
by the third equality in Lemma
3.2
.

3.

Let
y=\lceil(q+2)/2\rceil+j\mbox{ for some }j\in\{1,\ldots,\lfloor q/2\rfloor\}.
If
y=\lceil(q+2)/2\rceil+1
, then
2\lceil(q+2)/2\rceil-1+2=y+\left\lceil qy/(q+2)\right\rceil
by the first and the third equality in Lemma
3.2
. Lastly, if
y=\lceil(q+2)/2\rceil+\lfloor q/2\rfloor
, then
2\lceil(q+2)/2\rceil-1+2\lfloor q/2\rfloor=y+\left\lceil qy/(q+2)\right\rceil
by the second and the fourth equality in Lemma
3.2
. ∎

Corollary 3.5

\textnormal{FB}(2,t,2)=t+\lceil t/2\rceil

3.2
Serving any list of two binary vectors

We compute the exact value of
\textnormal{FB}(k,t,q)
when
(t,q)=(2,2).

Theorem 3.6
.

We have

\textnormal{FB}(k,2,2)=\left\lceil\frac{3k}{2}\right\rceil.

Theorem
3.6
will follow from Lemmas
3.8
and
3.9
below. These investigate some base cases that can easily be shown or are small enough to be verified with a computer.

Lemma 3.7
.

We have
\textnormal{FB}(1,2,2)=2
,
\textnormal{FB}(2,2,2)=3
and
\textnormal{FB}(3,2,2)=5
.

Lemma 3.8
.

\textnormal{FB}(k,2,2)\geq\left\lceil\frac{3k}{2}\right\rceil.

Proof.

Let
m
be a positive integer. The lemma is equivalent to
\textnormal{FB}(2m,2,2)\geq 3m
and
\textnormal{FB}(2m+1,2,2)\geq 3m+2
.
We induct on
m
. If
m=1
,
\textnormal{FB}(2,2,2)=3\geq 3
and
\textnormal{FB}(3,2,2)=5\geq 5
by Lemma
3.7
. Assume that
\textnormal{FB}(2m,2,2)\geq 3m
and
\textnormal{FB}(2m+1,2,2)\geq 3m+2
. We want to show that
\textnormal{FB}(2m+2,2,2)\geq 3m+3
and that
\textnormal{FB}(2m+3,2,2)\geq 3m+5
.

We have
\textnormal{FB}(2m+2,2,2)>\textnormal{FB}(2m+1,2,2)\geq 3m+2
by Proposition
2.6
and the induction hypothesis, yielding
\textnormal{FB}(2m+2,2,2)\geq 3m+3
. On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle\textnormal{FB}(2m+3,2,2)\geq\textnormal{FB}(2m,2,2)+\textnormal{FB}(3,2,2)=\textnormal{FB}(2m,2,2)+5\geq 3m+5,

where the first inequality follows from Proposition
2.6
and the last inequality follows from the induction hypothesis.
∎

By Lemma
3.8
, to conclude Theorem
3.6

Lemma 3.9
.

\textnormal{FB}(k,2,2)\leq\left\lceil\frac{3k}{2}\right\rceil.

Proof.

Let

be a positive integer. The lemma is equivalent to show that
\textnormal{FB}(2m,2,2)\leq 3m

\textnormal{FB}(2m+1,2,2)\leq 3m+2
. Consider the matrices

, where

for

. Observe that the sum of the columns in both matrices equal to the zero vector, proving that any vector can be served twice. Thus, we assume that

Case 1:
Let
k=2m
. We will show that

can be used to serve any two vectors proving
\textnormal{FB}(2m,2,2)\leq 3m
. Let

3.3
Explicit computations for other special parameters

In this subsection, we compute the values
\textnormal{FP}(k,t,q)
and
\textnormal{FB}(k,t,q)
for some specific choices of the triple
(k,t,q)
.
In
[
18
, Theorem 7]
, it was shown that
\textnormal{FP}(k,2^{k-1},2)=2^{k}-1
, while in
[
17
, Theorem 4]
the authors showed that
\textnormal{FB}(k,2^{k},2)=2^{k+1}-2
. These two results can be generalized as follows.

Lemma 3.10
.

For every
s
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we have that

\textnormal{FP}(k,2^{k-1}s,2)=(2^{k}-1)s\quad\text{ and }\quad\textnormal{FB}(k,2^{k}s,2)=(2^{k+1}-2)s.

Proof.

Since
\textnormal{FP}(k,2^{k-1},2)=(2^{k}-1)
, by the subadditivity of Proposition
2.6
, we immediately obtain that

\textnormal{FP}(k,2^{k-1}s,2)\leq(2^{k}-1)s.

Let
M\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k\times\textnormal{FP}(k,2^{k-1}s,2)}
be the generator matrix of a
2^{k-1}s
-functional PIR code. Then, by the pigeonhole principle there exists a vector
v\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}
that appears
\ell
times, with
\ell\leq\lfloor\textnormal{FP}(k,2^{k-1}s,2)/(2^{k}-1)\rfloor
, among the columns of
M
. Since
M
can serve
2^{k-1}s
times the vector
v
we obtain the following inequality

\textnormal{FP}(k,2^{k-1}s,2)\geq 2(2^{k-1}s-\ell)+\ell=2(2^{k-1}s)-\ell\geq 2^{k}s-\frac{\textnormal{FP}(k,2^{k-1}s,2)}{2^{k}-1}.

Solving this inequality, we obtain
\textnormal{FP}(k,2^{k-1}s,2)\geq(2^{k}-1)s
, and this concludes the proof of the first equality. Starting from
\textnormal{FB}(k,2^{k},2)=(2^{k+1}-2)
and proceeding in the same way, one can also prove the second inequality.
∎

Lemma
3.10
gives a curious corollary. Note that if the corollary were true for
t
a multiple of
2^{k-1}
instead of
2^{k}
, this would prove the Functional Batch Conjecture.

Corollary 3.11
.

If
t
is a multiple of
2^{k}
, then
\textnormal{FP}(t,k,2)=\textnormal{FB}(t,k,2)
.

Next, we show that the exact value of
\textnormal{FP}(k,t,2)
can be computed recursively provided that one knows the value of
\textnormal{FP}(k,t,2)
for some smaller values of
t
.

Proposition 3.12
.

Let
s,h\in\mathbb{N}
and
t=2^{k-1}s+h
with
0\leq h<2^{k-1}
. If
s+h\geq 2^{k}-1
. We have

\textnormal{FP}(k,t,2)=\textnormal{FP}(k,t-2^{k-1},2)+2^{k}-1.

Proof.

Let
M\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k\times\textnormal{FP}(k,t,2)}
be the generator matrix of a
(2^{k-1}s+h)
-functional PIR code. If
v\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k}
is not a column of
M
, then in order to serve
t
times the vector
v
we need at least
2t
columns. Thus

\displaystyle\textnormal{FP}(k,t,2)

\displaystyle\geq 2(2^{k-1}s+h)=(2^{k}s+2h)=(2^{k}-1)s+(s+h)+h\geq(2^{k}-1)s+(s+h)

\displaystyle\geq(2^{k}-1)s+(2^{k}-1)=(2^{k}-1)(s+1)=\textnormal{FP}(k,2^{k-1}(s+1),2),

where the last inequality follows from the assumption
s+h\geq 2^{k}-1
, and the last equality follows from Lemma
3.10
. This contradicts Proposition
2.6
, as
t<2^{k-1}(s+1)
since
h<2^{k-1}
. Therefore, we can assume that every vector of
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}
appears among the columns of
M
.

Fix
v\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}-\{0\}
. Let
X_{1},\dots,X_{t}
be a partition of the columns of
M
that serves
t
times the vector
v
. For every vector
w\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}
consider the recovery sets
X_{i}=\{w,v+w\}
of
v
for
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}.
Therefore, the matrix
M^{\prime}
, which we obtain by removing from
M
each vector of
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}
exactly once, can still serve at least
t-2^{k-1}
times
v
(we subtract
2^{k-1}
since there were
2^{k-1}=2^{k}/2
different recovery sets of the form
X_{i}
to begin with). Since this is true for every nonzero
v\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}
, we obtain

\textnormal{FP}(k,t,2)\geq\textnormal{FP}(k,t-2^{k-1},2)+2^{k}-1.

We conclude using the subadditivity of
\textnormal{FP}(k,t,2)
. Since
\textnormal{FP}(k,2^{k-1},2)=2^{k}-1
by
[
18
, Theorem 7]
, the results follows from Proposition
2.6
.
∎

We illustrate Proposition
3.12
with an example.

Example 3.13
.

We will compute
\textnormal{FP}(3,19,2)
. Take
(s,h)=(4,3)
in Proposition
3.12
. This gives

\textnormal{FP}(3,19,2)=\textnormal{FP}(3,15,2)+7.

(3)

By
[
18
, Theorem 5]
we know that
\textnormal{FP}(3,16,2)=\textnormal{FP}(3,15,2)+1
and
\textnormal{FP}(3,16,2)=28
. Therefore,
\textnormal{FP}(3,19,2)=34
by equation (
3
).

One of the main contributions of this paper is to identify the natural generalization of
\textnormal{FP}(k,2^{k-1},2)=2^{k}-1=|\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}-\{0\}|
for
q\neq 2.
The next result does that by finding the largest
t
such that
\textnormal{FP}(k,t,q)=q^{k}-1=|\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k}-\{0\}|
.

Lemma 3.14
.

\textnormal{FP}\left(k,\frac{q^{k}+q-2}{2},q\right)=q^{k}-1.

Proof.

Let
M\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k\times(q^{k}-1)}
be a matrix in which every nonzero vector of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k}
appears as a column. Fix a vector
v\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k}
. We will now perform a parity analysis on
q
.

If
q
is even, then
M
can serve
(q^{k}+q-2)/2
times the vector
v
with the partition given by all the sets of the form
\{0,\alpha v\}
with
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
, and all the sets of the form
\{w,v+w\}
with
w\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\setminus\langle v\rangle
. This works because
(v+w)+w=v
when
q
is even. We have

(q-1)+\frac{q^{k}-q}{2}=\frac{q^{k}+q-2}{2},

(4)

where
q-1
is the number of
\alpha
’s,
q^{k}-q
is the number of vectors outside the line
\langle v\rangle_{q}
, and the division by 2 is required to avoid overcounting the sets of the form
\{w,v+w\}
.

If
q
is odd, then
M
can serve
(q^{k}+q-2)/2
times the vector
v
with the partition given by all the sets of the form
\{0,\alpha v\}
with
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
, and all the sets of the form
\{v+w,v-w\}
(this set would be a singleton if
q
were even) with
w\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\setminus\langle v\rangle
. This is a partition since given
w_{1},w_{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\setminus\langle v\rangle
such that
\{v+w_{1},v-w_{1}\}\cap\{v+w_{2},v-w_{2}\}\neq\emptyset
, then either
w_{1}=w_{2}
or
w_{1}=-w_{2}
, and in both cases we have
\{v+w_{1},v-w_{1}\}=\{v+w_{2},v-w_{2}\}
. For the counting, the same reasoning of equation (
4
) works, giving

\textnormal{FP}\left(k,\frac{q^{k}+q-2}{2},q\right)\leq q^{k}-1.

(5)

Let now
M
be a matrix that realizes
\textnormal{FP}(k,(q^{k}+q-2)/2,q)
. By Equation (
5
) we conclude that there exists a vector
v
such that there are
h\leq q-1
columns of
M
that belong to
\langle v\rangle
. Then, we have that

\frac{\textnormal{FP}\left(k,\frac{q^{k}+q-2}{2},q\right)-h}{2}+h\geq\frac{q^{k}+q-2}{2},

hence

\textnormal{FP}\left(k,\frac{q^{k}+q-2}{2},q\right)\geq q^{k}+q-2-h\geq q^{k}-1,

yielding the desired result.
∎

As explained before, taking
q=2
in Lemma
3.14
gives the known result

\textnormal{FP}(k,2^{k}-1,2)=2^{k}-1.

We further generalize Lemma
3.14
as follows, where by taking
s=1
one recovers Lemma
3.14
. Note however that the lemma is used in the proof.

Theorem 3.15
.

For every
s\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, we have

\textnormal{FP}\left(k,\frac{q^{k}+q-2}{2}s,q\right)=s(q^{k}-1).

Proof.

We proceed as in the proof of Lemma
3.10
. By Lemma
3.14
and by the subadditivity established in Proposition
2.6
, we have

\textnormal{FP}\left(k,\frac{q^{k}+q-2}{2}s,q\right)\leq s(q^{k}-1).

Let
M\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k\times\textnormal{FP}(k,\frac{q^{k}+q-2}{2}s,q)}
be the generator matrix of a
(q^{k}+q-2)s/2
-functional PIR code. By the pigeonhole principle there exists a nonzero vector
v\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}
for which there are

h\leq\frac{(q-1)\textnormal{FP}\left(k,\frac{q^{k}+q-2}{2}s,q\right)}{q^{k}-1}

columns of
M
that belong to
\langle v\rangle
. Since
M
can serve
s(q^{k}+q-2)/2
times the vector
v
, we obtain the following inequality:

\frac{\textnormal{FP}(k,\frac{q^{k}+q-2}{2}s,q)-h}{2}+h\geq\frac{(q^{k}+q-2)s}{2}.

This implies
\textnormal{FP}(k,\frac{q^{k}+q-2}{2},2)\geq(q^{k}-1)s
, concluding
the proof.
∎

We continue by recalling an equality that established in
[
17
, Section VI]
:

\textnormal{FB}(k,2^{k},2)=2(2^{k}-1)

(6)

This was proven independently also in
[
7
]
using an algorithm by Hall, see
[

. Similarly to Lemma
3.14
and Theorem
3.15
, one can try to generalize Equation (

) to an arbitrary finite field. To this end, we propose the following open questions.

Open Problem 3.16
.

Prove or disprove that

\textnormal{FB}\left(k,q^{k}+q-2,q\right)=2q^{k}-2.

We also state a “projectivized” version of the previous question.

Open Problem 3.17
.

Prove or disprove that

\textnormal{FB}\left(k,\frac{q^{k}-1}{q-1}+1,q\right)=2\frac{q^{k}-1}{q-1}.

Notice that a positive answer to the latter question would also result in a positive answer to the former one. In the next section, we will give some partial result on Open Problem
3.16
.

4
Bounds and field size

This section is devoted to bounds on
\textnormal{FB}(k,t,q)
and to understand how
\textnormal{FB}(k,t,q)
behaves with respect to
q
. We start with a trivial bound that will be useful later on.

Lemma 4.1
.

We have
t+k-1\leq\textnormal{FP}(k,t,q)\leq\textnormal{FB}(k,t,q)\leq tk.

Proof.

When
t=1
, one needs a full rank matrix. For the lower bound on
\textnormal{FP}(t,k,q)
, we know that
\textnormal{FP}(k,1,q)=k
. Since
\textnormal{FP}(k,t,2)
is strictly increasing in
t
, we have
\textnormal{FP}(k,t,2)\geq k+t-1
. The upper bound on
\textnormal{FB}(k,t,q)
is obtained considering a matrix that is formed by concatenating
t
many identity matrices of size
k
.
∎

In Proposition
2.6
we mentioned that
\textnormal{FP}(k,t,q)
is strictly increasing in
k
. The following proposition gives an lower bound on how much it increase at each step.

Proposition 4.2
.

We have

\left\lceil\frac{(q-1)\textnormal{FB}(k,t,q)}{q^{k}-1}\right\rceil\leq\textnormal{FB}(t,k,q)-\textnormal{FB}(t,k-1,q).

The same inequality holds also for
\textnormal{FP}(k,t,q)
.

Proof.

Let
M
achieve
n=\textnormal{FB}(k,t,q)
. There exists a one-dimensional space
V
for which at least
\smash{\frac{n(q-1)}{q^{k}-1}}
columns of
M
belongs to
V
. By Proposition
2.5
, we can assume without loss of generality that
V
is generated by
e_{1}
. Then, the matrix
M^{\prime}
, obtained from
M
by deleting the first row and all the columns corresponding to vectors that belong to
V
, can serve any sequence of
t
vectors in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k-1}
. Therefore, we obtain

\textnormal{FB}(k,t,q)-\frac{(q-1)\textnormal{FB}(k,t,q)}{q^{k}-1}\geq\textnormal{FB}(k-1,t,q),

and we conclude by noticing that
\textnormal{FB}(k,t,q)-\textnormal{FB}(k-1,t,q)
is an integer. The same proof also works for
\textnormal{FP}(k,t,q).

∎

We now turn to the behavior of
\textnormal{FB}(k,t,q)
with respect to
q
.

Lemma 4.3
.

For all
s_{1},s_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
s_{1}\mid s_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\textnormal{FB}(k,t,q^{s_{2}})\leq\textnormal{FB}\left(k,\left(\frac{s_{2}}{s_{1}}\right)t,q^{s_{1}}\right)\leq\left(\frac{s_{2}}{s_{1}}\right)\textnormal{FB}(k,t,q^{s_{2}}).

The same inequalities hold for
\textnormal{FP}(k,t,q)
.

Proof.

Let
G
be a matrix that achieves
\textnormal{FB}(k,t,q^{s_{2}})
. Fix a basis of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{s_{2}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{s_{1}}
. For each column
g
of
G
we find
s_{2}/s_{1}
vectors that corresponds to the coefficients of
g
with respect to our basis.
The matrix obtained by collecting all these vectors has
s_{2}/s_{1}\textnormal{FB}(k,t,q^{s_{2}})
columns and can serve any list of
\frac{s_{2}}{s_{1}}t
vectors. Therefore, we obtained the inequality on the right-hand side of the lemma. On the other hand, since we are able to serve
\frac{s_{2}}{s_{1}}t
vectors over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{s}_{1}}
, with the same matrix we can also serve
t
vectors over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{s}_{2}}
.
∎

We now provide more precise lower bounds for
\textnormal{FP}(k,t,q)
and
\textnormal{FB}(k,t,q)
.

Lemma 4.4
.

We have

\textnormal{FP}(k,t,q)>\frac{kt}{\log_{q}(et)+1}-t.

Proof.

Let
n=\textnormal{FP}(k,t,q)
. We define
s\in\mathbb{N}
as

s=\max\left\{i\in\mathbb{N}:\binom{it}{i}q^{i}<q^{k}\right\}.

We first prove that
n>st
. Assume towards a contradiction that
n\leq st
. Then

\binom{n}{s}q^{s}\leq\binom{st}{s}q^{s}<q^{k}.

For a given matrix
M
that realizes
\textnormal{FB}(k,t,q)
we can find a vector
v\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k}
that does not belong to any linear space generated by
s
columns of
M
.
Therefore we would obtain
n\geq(s+1)t
, a contradiction. Thus
n>st
.
By the definition of
s
we have

q^{k}\leq\binom{(s+1)t}{s+1}q^{s+1}<\left(\frac{(s+1)te}{s+1}\right)^{s+1}q^{s+1},

and therefore
(s+1)(\log_{q}(et)+1)>k
. We conclude by isolating
s
and multiplying by
t
.
∎

The ideas used in the previous proof
allow us to establish the following result as well.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
1\leq s\leq k-1
. If
q^{k-s}\geq\binom{t(s+1)}{s}
, then
\textnormal{FB}(t,k,q)\geq t(s+1)
.

Proof.

Suppose
\textnormal{FB}(t,k,q)\leq t(s+1)-1
and let
M
achieve
\textnormal{FB}(k,t,q)
. The groups of
s
columns of
M
span at most

\binom{t(s+1)-1}{s}q^{s}
vectors. By assumption, we have

\binom{t(s+1)-1}{s}<\binom{t(s+1)}{s}\leq q^{k-s}.

This means that there exists a vector
v
that needs at least
s+1
columns to be recovered. But then
\textnormal{FB}(t,k,q)\geq t(s+1)
, a contradiction.
∎

Observe that Proposition
4.5
when
s=k-1
says that
\textnormal{FB}(t,k,q)\geq kt.
However, we know by Lemma
4.1
that
\textnormal{FB}(k,t,q)\leq kt
. Theorefore, Proposition
4.5
gives
the following corollary.

Corollary 4.6
.

If
q\geq\binom{kt}{k-1}
, then
\textnormal{FB}(k,t,q)=kt
.

We continue with a bound on
\textnormal{FB}(k,t,q)
that does not assume any constraints on the field size, in contrast to Proposition
4.5
.

Theorem 4.7
.

We have that

(t(q-1)+1)^{\textnormal{FB}(k,t,q)}\geq(q^{k}-1)^{t}.

In particular, for
q\neq 2
we have that

\textnormal{FB}(k,t,q)\geq\frac{tk}{\log_{q-1}(t(q-1)+1)}.

Proof.

Let
G
be a matrix that achieves
\textnormal{FB}(k,t,q)
. We denote by
\mathcal{M}
the set of matrices in
\smash{\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k\times t}}
with all columns different from zero, and by
\mathcal{N}
the set of matrices in
\smash{\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\textnormal{FB}(k,t,q)\times t}}
whose rows are different from zero in at most one entry. We have that
\smash{\lvert\mathcal{M}\rvert=(q^{k}-1)^{t}}
and
\smash{\lvert\mathcal{N}\rvert=(t(q-1)+1)^{\textnormal{FB}(k,t,q)}}
. Since for every matrix
M\in\mathcal{M}
there exists a matrix
N\in\mathcal{N}
such that
GN=M
, we have

(t(q-1)+1)^{\textnormal{FB}(k,t,q)}\geq(q^{k}-1)^{t}.

Moreover, when
q\neq 2
, by taking the logarithm on both sides we obtain

\textnormal{FB}(k,t,q)\geq\frac{t\log_{q-1}(q^{k}-1)}{\log_{q-1}(t(q-1)+1)}\geq\frac{t\log_{q-1}((q-1)^{k})}{\log_{q-1}(t(q-1)+1)}=\frac{tk}{\log_{q-1}(t(q-1)+1)},

which concludes the proof.
∎

By Theorem
3.6
and Proposition
2.6
, for
k
and
t
even we have
\textnormal{FB}(k,t,2)\leq\frac{3}{4}kt
. By Corollary
4.6
, we know that the function
\textnormal{FB}(k,t,q)
for large
q
stabilizes at the value
kt
. However, we do not know if it is locally increasing. It remains an open problem
to describe the behavior of
\textnormal{FB}(k,t,q)
and
\textnormal{FB}(t,k,q)
as
q
varies.

Our next goal is to establish bound related to Open Problem
3.16
. Even though we are not able to give a complete answer, we will prove that the difference between
\textnormal{FB}(k,q^{k}+q-2,q)
and
2(q^{k}-1)
is at most linear in
k
. We start by recalling the following result, which we will need in the proof of Lemma
4.9
.

Theorem 4.8

(see
[
7
, Theorem 4.4]
)
.

Let
(A,+)
be a finite abelian group of cardinality
n
, and let
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}
be
a list in
A
. There exists an ordering
g_{1},\dots,g_{n}
of the elements of
A
such that
g_{1}+a_{1},\dots,g_{n}+a_{n}
is a permutation of the elements of
A
if and only if
a_{1}+\dots+a_{n}=0
.

Lemma 4.9
.

For every
k\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\textnormal{FB}(k,q^{k},q)\leq 2(q^{k}-1).

Proof.

Let
A=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k}
and
n=q^{k}
. Let
G
be a matrix in which every nonzero vector in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k}
appears exactly twice as a column and consider a list
L=\{a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\}
of nonzero elements in
A
. If there exists
a\in A
such that
a=a_{i}
for every
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, then by Theorem
4.8
there exists an ordering
g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}
of the element of
A
such that
a+g_{1},\dots,a+g_{n}
is a permutation of the elements of
A
. Therefore,
G
can serve the vector
a
exactly
n
times by considering the recovery sets
\{g_{i},a+g_{i}\}
for
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.

Now assume that the list has at least two different elements, without loss of generality say
a_{n-1}\neq a_{n}
. Define

x_{L}
as the sum of the first
n-1
elements in the list
L
. We start by showing that one can always assume
x_{L}\neq 0
. If
x_{L}=0
, then
x_{L}-a_{n-1}=-a_{n-1}
. Consider a new list
L^{\prime}=\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n-2},a_{n},a_{n-1}\}
. We have
x_{L^{\prime}}\neq 0
, since
a_{n-1}\neq a_{n}
by assumption. Therefore, for the remainder of the proof we assume that
x_{L}\neq 0
.

Consider the list
L^{\prime}=\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n-1},-x_{L}\}
. By Theorem
4.8
, there exists an ordering
g_{1},\dots,g_{n}
of the elements of
A
such that
g_{1}+a_{1},\dots,g_{n}-x_{L}
is a permutation of the elements of
A
. Then
g_{1}+a_{1}+(x_{L}-g_{n}+a_{n}),\dots,g_{n}-x_{L}+(x_{L}-g_{n}+a_{n})
is a a permutation of the elements of
A
as well. Therefore,
G
can serve the list
L^{\prime}
with the sets

\displaystyle X_{1}

\displaystyle=\{g_{1}+a_{1}+(x_{L}-g_{n}+a_{n}),g_{1}+(x_{L}-g_{n}+a_{n})\},

\displaystyle\,\;\vdots

\displaystyle X_{n-1}

\displaystyle=\{g_{n-1}+a_{n-1}+(x_{L}-g_{n}+a_{n}),g_{n-1}+(x_{L}-g_{n}+a_{n})\},

\displaystyle X_{n}

\displaystyle=\{g_{n}-x_{L}+(x_{L}-g_{n}+a_{n}),g_{n}+(x_{L}-g_{n}+a_{n})\}=\{a_{n},g_{n}+(x_{L}-g_{n}+a_{n})\}.

We conclude that
G
can serve the list
L
with the sets
X_{1},\dots,X_{n-1},\{a_{n}\}
.
∎

Since the function
\textnormal{FB}(k,t,q)
is subadditive in
t
, the previous lemma implies the following bound.

Proposition 4.10
.

2(q^{k}-1)=\textnormal{FP}(k,q^{k}+q-2)\leq\textnormal{FB}(k,q^{k}+q-2,q)\leq 2(q^{k}-1)+(q-2)k.

For large
t
we can refine the previous result as follows.

Proposition 4.11
.

2(q^{k}-1)\frac{q^{2k}+q-2}{q^{k}+q-2}\leq\textnormal{FB}(k,q^{2k}+q-2,q)\leq 2q^{k}(q^{k}-1).

Proof.

Note that
q^{2k}+q-2\geq\frac{q^{k}-1}{q-1}(q^{k}+q-2)
. This implies that in any list of length
q^{2k}+q-2
there are at least
q^{k}+q-2
elements belonging to the same one-dimensional space. Since

q^{2k}+q-2=q^{k}(q^{k}-1)+q^{k}+q-2,

by Lemma
4.9
and Theorem
3.15
for
s=2
we have

\textnormal{FB}(k,q^{2k}+q-2,q)\leq\textnormal{FB}(k,q^{k}(q^{k}-1),q)+\textnormal{FP}(k,q^{k}+q-2,q)\leq 2(q^{k}-1)(q^{k}-1)+2(q^{k}-1),

proving the desired upper bound.

To prove the lower bound, let
n=\textnormal{FB}(k,q^{2k}+q-2,q)
and let
G
be a matrix that achieves
n
. By the pigeonhole principle there exists at least one 1-dimensional space
V
for which there are less than

x=\left\lfloor\frac{n(q-1)}{q^{k}-1}\right\rfloor

columns of
G
belonging to
V
. Therefore, it must hold that

\textnormal{FB}(k,q^{2k}+q-2,2)\geq x+2(q^{2k}+q-2-x)=2(q^{2k}+q-2)-x.

This translates into the following inequality:

\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{n(q^{k}+q-2)}{q^{k}-1}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\left(n+\frac{n(q-1)}{q^{k}-1}\right)\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(n+\left\lfloor\frac{n(q-1)}{q^{k}-1}\right\rfloor\right)\geq q^{2k}+q-2,

from which we obtain

n\geq 2(q^{k}-1)\frac{q^{2k}+q-2}{q^{k}+q-2},

concluding the proof.
∎

It is natural to ask what happens if we switch the parameters
k
and
t
.
By observing the cases where
k
and
t
are very small, one may be tempted to conjecture that
\textnormal{FB}(k,t,q)=\textnormal{FB}(t,k,q)
. However, this is false in general.
In fact, the following holds.

Corollary 4.12
.

For every
q
, there exist
k
and
t
such that
\textnormal{FB}(k,t,q)<\textnormal{FB}(t,k,q)
.

Proof.

By Proposition
4.10
we have
\textnormal{FB}(k,q^{k}+q-2,2)\leq 2(q^{k}-1)+(q-2)k
. By Lemma
4.4
we have

\textnormal{FB}(q^{k}+q-2,k,q)\geq\textnormal{FP}(q^{k}+q-2,k,q)>\frac{(q^{k}+q-2)k}{\log(ek)+1}-k.

This implies

\begin{split}\textnormal{FB}(q^{k}+q-2,k,q)-\textnormal{FB}(k,q^{k}&+q-2,q)\\
&\geq\frac{(q^{k}+q-2)k}{\log_{q}(ek)+1}-k-(2(q^{k}-1)+(q-2)k)\\
&=\frac{(q^{k}+q-2)k-(2(q^{k}-1)+(q-1)k)(\log_{q}(ek)+1)}{\log(ek)+1},\end{split}

from which

\liminf_{k\to\infty}(\textnormal{FB}(q^{k}+q-2,k,q)-\textnormal{FB}(k,q^{k}+q-2,q))\geq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{q^{k}k-2q^{k}\log_{q}(ek)}{\log(ek)}=+\infty.

This establishes the corollary.
∎

Example 4.13
.

It can be checked, for instance, that
\textnormal{FB}(1032,10,2)>\textnormal{FB}(10,1032,2)
.

We conclude this section with an upper bound on
\textnormal{FB}(k,t,q)
, which we will
later in the proof of Theorem
5.3
.

Lemma 4.14
.

We have

\textnormal{FB}(k,t,q)\leq\left\lceil\frac{h}{s}\right\rceil\frac{2(s+q-2+\log_{q}(s))(s-1+(q-2)\log_{q}(s))}{\log_{q}(s)},

where
h=\max\{k,t\}
and
s=\min\{k,t\}
.

Proof.

Let
g=\lceil\log_{q}s\rceil
. We have

\begin{split}\textnormal{FB}(s,s,q)&\leq\textnormal{FB}\left(q^{g}+q-2,q^{g}+q-2,q\right)\leq\left\lceil\frac{q^{g}+q-2}{g}\right\rceil\textnormal{FB}(g,q^{g}+q-2,q)\\
&\leq 2\left\lceil\frac{q^{g}+q-2}{g}\right\rceil\left(q^{g}-1+(q-2)g\right)\leq 2\left(\frac{q^{g}+q-2}{g}+1\right)\left(q^{g}-1+(q-2)g\right)\\
&=\frac{2(s+q-2+\log_{q}(s))(s-1+(q-2)\log_{q}(s))}{\log_{q}(s)},\end{split}

where the third inequality follows from Proposition
4.10
, and the last inequality follows from the fact that
q^{g}=s
. We conclude by noticing that
\textnormal{FB}(k,t,q)\leq\left\lceil\frac{h}{s}\right\rceil\textnormal{FB}(s,s,q)
.
∎

5
Asymptotic behaviour

In Section
4
we discussed what happens for
q
sufficiently large. For
q=2
, the behaviour of
\textnormal{FB}(k,t,2)
and
\textnormal{FP}(k,t,2)
when
t
is fixed has been studied in
[
18
]
. The goal of this section is to study the behaviour of the functions
\textnormal{FP}(k,t,q)
and
\textnormal{FB}(k,t,q)
when
t
goes to infinity. Both functions are strictly increasing in
t
and are therefore not bounded from above. It is natural to ask
how quickly they approach infinity. Since the two functions are subadditive in
t
, by Fekete’s Lemma the limit of the ratio exists and it coincides with the infimum, i.e.,

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\textnormal{FP}(k,t,q)}{t}=\inf_{t\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\textnormal{FP}(k,t,q)}{t}\quad\text{ and }\quad\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\textnormal{FB}(k,t,q)}{t}=\inf_{t\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\textnormal{FB}(k,t,q)}{t}.

We wish to explicitly compute these limits as
k
and
q
range over all possible values. The following proposition addresses the case of functional PIR codes.

Proposition 5.1
.

We have

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\textnormal{FP}(k,t,q)}{t}=\frac{2(q^{k}-1)}{q^{k}+q-2}.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.15
we have

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\textnormal{FP}(k,t,q)}{t}=\lim_{s\to\infty}\frac{\textnormal{FP}\left(k,\frac{q^{k}+q-2}{2}s,q\right)}{\frac{q^{k}+q-2}{2}s}=\lim_{s\to\infty}\frac{s(q^{k}-1)}{\frac{q^{k}+q-2}{2}s}=\frac{2(q^{k}-1)}{q^{k}+q-2},

as desired.
∎

The previous proposition can be easily extended to the case of functional batch codes assuming a positive answer to Open Problem
3.17
. However, with some extra work one can obtain the same result without that assumption.

Theorem 5.2
.

We have

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\textnormal{FB}(k,t,q)}{t}=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\textnormal{FP}(k,t,q)}{t}=\frac{2(q^{k}-1)}{q^{k}+q-2}.

Proof.

Let
N=\frac{(q^{k}+q-2)}{2}
. Observed that if
t>N\frac{q^{k}-1}{q-1}
, then in any list of
t
nonzero vectors there are at least
N
of them that belong to the same one-dimensional vector space. Therefore,
\textnormal{FB}(k,t,q)\leq\textnormal{FB}(k,t-N,q)+\textnormal{FP}(k,N,q)
. By iterating this argument we obtain

\textnormal{FB}\left(k,N\frac{q^{k}-1}{q-1}+sN,q\right)\leq\textnormal{FB}\left(k,N\frac{q^{k}-1}{q-1},q\right)+s\textnormal{FP}\left(k,N,q\right),

(7)

which implies that

\begin{split}\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\textnormal{FB}(k,t,q)}{t}&\leq\lim_{s\to\infty}\frac{\textnormal{FB}\left(k,N\frac{q^{k}-1}{q-1}+sN,q\right)}{N\frac{q^{k}-1}{q-1}+sN}\leq\lim_{s\to\infty}\frac{\textnormal{FB}\left(k,N\frac{q^{k}-1}{q-1}+sN,q\right)}{sN}\\
&\leq\lim_{s\to\infty}\frac{\textnormal{FB}\left(k,N\frac{q^{k}-1}{q-1},q\right)+s\textnormal{FP}\left(k,N,q\right)}{sN}=0+\frac{\textnormal{FP}(k,N,q)}{N}=\frac{2(q^{k}-1)}{q^{k}+q-2}.\end{split}

To conclude the proof, it suffices to use that
\textnormal{FP}(k,t,q)\leq\textnormal{FB}(k,t,q)
and
apply Lemma
3.14
and Proposition
5.1
.
∎

We now turn to the behavior of
\textnormal{FP}(k,t,q)
and
\textnormal{FB}(k,t,q)
when both
k
and
t
go to infinity simultaneously. Since
\textnormal{FB}(k,t,q)
is submodular in both variables
k
and
t
, one can prove that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\textnormal{FB}(k,k,q)}{k^{2}}=\inf_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\textnormal{FB}(k,k,q)}{k^{2}};

see for instance
[
2
]
. Moreover, in analogy with
Theorem
5.2
one obtains

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\textnormal{FP}(k,k,q)}{k^{2}}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\textnormal{FB}(k,k,q)}{k^{2}}=0.

(8)

Our goal is to extend this result to two arbitrary divergent sequences of natural numbers
(k_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(t_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
(k_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(t_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be divergent sequences of natural numbers such that
k_{n}\geq t_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. We have

1\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\textnormal{FP}(k_{n},t_{n},q)\log_{q}(t_{n})}{k_{n}t_{n}}\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\textnormal{FB}(k_{n},t_{n},q)\log_{q}(t_{n})}{k_{n}t_{n}}<4.

Proof.

The left-most inequality follows from Lemma
4.4
. By Lemma
4.14
we have

\begin{split}\textnormal{FB}(k_{n},t_{n},q)&<2\left(\frac{k_{n}}{t_{n}}+1\right)\frac{(t_{n}+q-2+\log_{q}(t_{n}))(t_{n}-1+(q-2)\log_{q}(t_{n}))}{\log_{q}(t_{n})}\\
&\leq 2\left(\frac{2k_{n}}{t_{n}}\right)\frac{(t_{n}+q-2+\log_{q}(t_{n}))(t_{n}-1+(q-2)\log_{q}(t_{n}))}{\log_{q}(t_{n})}.\end{split}

Therefore,

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\textnormal{FB}(k_{n},t_{n},q)\log_{q}(t_{n})}{k_{n}t_{n}}\\
\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}2\left(\frac{2k_{n}}{t_{n}}\right)\frac{(t_{n}+q-2+\log_{q}(t_{n}))(t_{n}-1+(q-2)\log_{q}(t_{n}))}{k_{n}t_{n}}\\
\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}4\frac{(t_{n}+q-2+\log_{q}(t_{n}))(t_{n}-1+(q-2)\log_{q}(t_{n}))}{t_{n}^{2}}=4,

concluding the proof.
∎

The upper bound of the previous theorem can be improved if one has more information about the ratio
between
(k_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(t_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. For instance, when
k_{n}=t_{n}=n
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we obtain the following.

Corollary 5.4
.

We have

1\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\textnormal{FP}(n,n,q)\log_{q}(n)}{n^{2}}\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\textnormal{FB}(n,n,q)\log_{q}(n)}{n^{2}}\leq 2.

The upper bound of Corollary
5.4
follows
from the fact we can skip an
approximation in the proof of Theorem
5.3
. More precisely, in the first line of the proof of Theorem
5.3
, we have “
2
” instead of “
2(k_{n}/t_{n})+1
”.

We conclude the section with a generalization of Equation (
8
) to arbitrary divergent sequences.

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
(k_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(t_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be divergent sequences of natural numbers. We have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\textnormal{FP}(k_{n},t_{n},q)}{k_{n}t_{n}}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\textnormal{FB}(k_{n},t_{n},q)}{k_{n}t_{n}}=0.

Proof.

Let
h_{n}=\max\{k_{n},t_{n}\}
and
s_{n}=\min\{k_{n},t_{n}\}
for all
n
. Since
\textnormal{FB}(k,t,q)
is subadditive in both variables, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\textnormal{FB}(k_{n},t_{n},q)}{k_{n}t_{n}}\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\frac{h_{n}}{s_{n}}+1\right)\frac{\textnormal{FB}(s_{n},s_{n},q)}{h_{n}s_{n}}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\frac{h_{n}+s_{n}}{h_{n}}\right)\frac{\textnormal{FB}(s_{n},s_{n},q)}{s_{n}^{2}}=0,

where the first inequality follows from the fact that
k_{n}t_{n}=h_{n}s_{n}
, and the last equality follows from Corollary
5.4
.
∎

Improved Berezin-Li-Yau inequality and Kröger inequality and consequences 0 0 footnotetext: 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification . Primary 35P15; Secondary 35P20, 42B37. Key words and phrases: Dirichlet eigenvalue, Neumann eigenvalue, Berezin-Li-Yau inequality, Kröger inequality
Zaihui Gan, Renjin Jiang & Fanghua Lin

Abstract




Abstract
. We provide quantitative improvements to the Berezin-Li-Yau inequality and
the Kröger inequality, in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
. The improvement on Kröger’s inequality resolves an open question raised by Weidl from 2006.
The improvements allow us to show that, for any open bounded domains,
there are infinite many Dirichlet eigenvalues satisfying Pólya’s conjecture if
n\geq 3
, and infinite many Neumann eigenvalues satisfying
Pólya’s conjecture if
n\geq 5
and the Neumann spectrum is discrete.

1
Introduction

Given an open bounded domain
\Omega
in
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
,
n\geq 2
. Let

0<\lambda_{1}(\Omega)\leq\lambda_{2}(\Omega)\leq\lambda_{3}(\Omega)\leq\cdots

be eigenvalues to the Dirichlet Laplacian on
\Omega
.
When dealing with the Neumann Laplacian on
\Omega
, we shall assume that
-\Delta_{N}
on
\Omega

has a discrete spectrum as

0=\gamma_{1}(\Omega)\leq\gamma_{2}(\Omega)\leq\gamma_{3}(\Omega)\leq\cdots.

We shall simply write
\lambda_{k},\gamma_{k}

instead of
\lambda_{k}(\Omega),\gamma_{k}(\Omega)
when there is no confusion.

In 1954, Pólya
[
26
]
conjectured that, it should hold on arbitrary domain that

\frac{|\Omega|\omega(n)}{(2\pi)^{n}}\gamma_{k+1}^{n/2}\leq k\leq\frac{|\Omega|\omega(n)}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda_{k}^{n/2},

where
\omega(n)
denotes the volume of unit ball in
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
.
Pólya himself
[
27
]
proved this conjecture for tiling domains (regular tiling domains for the Neumann case) in the plane.
His method extends to high dimensions, and so the conjecture is true for tiling domains in high dimensions too. See Kellner
[
17
]

for removing the regular assumption regarding Neumann eigenvalues and simplification.

The Pólya conjecture has been wide open since then. Other than tiling domains,
Pólya’s conjecture was known to be true on on product domains
\Omega_{1}\times\Omega_{2}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{1}+n_{2}}
,
n_{1}\geq 2
,

n_{2}\geq 1
provided Pólya’s conjecture holds on
\Omega_{1}
, by the work of Laptev
[
21
]
. Filonov et al.
[
5
,
6
]

verified Pólya’s conjecture on balls for Dirichlet eigenvalues for all
n\geq 2
, and for Neumann eigenvalues for
n=2
,
and on annuli for Dirichlet eigenvalues for
n=2
. Recently, it was proved in
[
16
]
that on a Lipschitz domain
\Omega
, for any
\epsilon\in(0,1)
it holds for all

\lambda_{k}\geq\Lambda(\Omega,\epsilon)
that

k\leq(1+\epsilon)\frac{|\Omega|\omega(n)}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda_{k}^{n/2},

where
\Lambda(\Omega,\epsilon)
depending on the geometry of
\Omega
is given explicitly. Moreover,
[
16
]
also provides in all dimensions
n\geq 2
classes of
irregular shaped domains that satisfy Pólya’s conjecture on the Dirichlet eigenvalue.

For general domains
\Omega
, it was known that Pólya’s conjecture holds for the first two Dirichlet eigenvalues and the first two non-trivial Neumann eigenvalues,
see
[
13
]
for the Rayleigh-Faber-Krahn inequality (
\lambda_{1}(\Omega)
), the Krahn-Szegö inequality (
\lambda_{2}(\Omega)
) and the
Szegö-Weinberger inequality (
\gamma_{2}(\Omega)
), and also recent breakthroughs
[
3
,
12
]
for
\gamma_{3}(\Omega)
. In higher dimensions,
n\geq 3
, Freitas
[
10
]
proved that
the first
b(n)
Dirichlet eigenvalues satisfying Pólya conjecture, where
b(n)\geq 4
and grows as dimension increases.

For arbitrary bounded open domains,
Berezin
[
2
]
and Li-Yau
[
24
]
independently proved that

(1.1)

\frac{|\Omega|^{2/n}\omega(n)^{2/n}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}\geq\frac{n}{n+2}k^{\frac{n+2}{n}},

which implies that

(1.2)

\frac{|\Omega|^{2/n}\omega(n)^{2/n}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\lambda_{k}\geq\frac{n}{n+2}k^{\frac{2}{n}}.

Melas
[
25
]
later gave a quantitative improvement of Li-Yau’s estimate
as

(1.3)

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}\geq\frac{n}{n+2}\frac{(2\pi)^{2}}{|\Omega|^{2/n}\omega(n)^{2/n}}k^{\frac{n+2}{n}}+C(n)k\frac{|\Omega|}{I(\Omega)},

where
I(\Omega)
is the moment of inertia of
\Omega
, i.e.,

(1.4)

\displaystyle I(\Omega):=\inf_{a\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}\int_{\Omega}|x-a|^{2}\,dx.

Above
C(n)
can be taken as
\frac{1}{24(n+2)}
, see Cheng et al.
[
4
, p. 36]
, and a better constant for
n=2
,
C(2)=\frac{1}{32}
, from
[
19
, (16)]
.
Melas’s result have been further improved by Kovařík, Vugalter and Weidl
[
19
]
for
n=2
,
where a term of order
k^{\frac{3}{2}-\epsilon}
can be added to the RHS of (
1.3
) on domains with some smoothness.
See also
[
11
,
15
,
29
]
for further generalisations.

Kröger
[
20
]
proved for bounded domains with piecewisely smooth boundary that

(1.5)

\displaystyle\frac{|\Omega|^{2/n}\omega(n)^{2/n}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{k+1}\gamma_{j}\leq\frac{n}{n+2}k^{1+2/n}.

and

(1.6)

\displaystyle\frac{|\Omega|^{2/n}\omega(n)^{2/n}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\gamma_{k+1}\leq\left(\frac{n+2}{2}\right)^{2/n}k^{2/n}.

Li and Tang
[
23
]
showed the above inequality (
1.5
) is strict by multiplying on the right hand side an implicit factor
C(k,n,\Omega)<1
which tends to 1 as
k

goes to infinity.
We note Filonov
[
7
]
recently improved the estimate for Neumann eigenvalues on planar convex domains.

Laptev
[
21
]
showed that it holds for the Riesz means of Dirichlet eigenvalues and Neumann eigenvalues that

(1.7)

\sum_{k:\,\lambda_{k}<\lambda}(\lambda-\lambda_{k})\leq\frac{2}{n+2}\frac{\omega(n)|\Omega|}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda^{1+\frac{n}{2}}

and

(1.8)

\sum_{k:\,\gamma_{k}<\gamma}(\gamma-\gamma_{k})\geq\frac{2}{n+2}\frac{\omega(n)|\Omega|}{(2\pi)^{n}}\gamma^{1+\frac{n}{2}}.

He then used these two inequalities to give a new proof of (
1.2
) and (
1.6
).
Note that Laptev
[
21
]
used the Riesz means to verify Pólya’s conjecture on product domains
\Omega_{1}\times\Omega_{2}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{1}+n_{2}}

provided Pólya’s conjecture holds on
\Omega_{1}
as we recalled above.

In recent works
[
8
,
9
]
, Frank and Larson have launched the study of two term expansion of the Riesz means
for both Dirichlet/Neumann eigenvalues. In particular, asymptotic behavior have been established for Lipschitz domains
by
[
8
,
9
]
. Very recently Frank and Larson
[
8
]
obtained improvements of (
1.7
)
and (
1.8
) via the uncertainty principle, as

(1.9)

\sum_{k:\,\lambda_{k}<\lambda}(\lambda-\lambda_{k})\leq\frac{2}{n+2}\frac{\omega(n)|\Omega|}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda^{1+\frac{n}{2}}\left(1-ce^{-c^{\prime}(\Omega)\sqrt{\lambda}}\right)

and

(1.10)

\sum_{k:\,\gamma_{k}<\gamma}(\gamma-\gamma_{k})\geq\frac{2}{n+2}\frac{\omega(n)|\Omega|}{(2\pi)^{n}}\gamma^{1+\frac{n}{2}}\left(1+ce^{-c^{\prime}(\Omega)\sqrt{\lambda}}\right),

where the constants
c,c^{\prime}
are implicit.

For a given bounded open domain
\Omega
, let

(1.11)

\displaystyle J_{1}:=\frac{|\Omega|^{1/2}}{2|I(\Omega)|^{1/2}},

and
R_{\Omega}>0
be such that
|\Omega|=|B(0,R_{\Omega})|
.
Note that

\displaystyle\frac{n}{n+2}|\Omega|R_{\Omega}^{2}\leq I(\Omega)=\inf_{a\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}\int_{\Omega}|x-a|^{2}\,dx\leq\frac{1}{4}(\mathrm{diam}(\Omega))^{2}|\Omega|,

the quantity
J_{1}
has lower and upper bounds as

(1.12)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)}\leq J_{1}\leq\sqrt{\frac{n+2}{n}}\frac{1}{2R_{\Omega}}.

Adopting the elegant method of using the moment of inertia by Males
[
25
]
, we obtain the following estimates for the Riesz means of
Dirichlet eigenvalues.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded open set in
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
,
n\geq 2
. Then it holds for
\lambda>0
that

(1.13)

\displaystyle\sum_{k:\,\lambda_{k}<\lambda}(\lambda-\lambda_{k})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{n+2}\lambda^{1+\frac{n}{2}}\frac{|\Omega||\omega(n)|}{(2\pi)^{n}}\left(1-C_{1}(n)\lambda^{-1}|J_{1}|^{2}\right)_{+},

where
a_{+}=\max\{0,a\}
and

(1.14)

\displaystyle C_{1}(n)=\begin{cases}\frac{157}{480},\ &n=2,\\
\frac{5n(n+2)}{96}\left(1-\frac{\sqrt{n+2}}{8\sqrt{n}\Gamma(1+\frac{n}{2}))^{2/n}}\right)^{n-1}\left(2-\frac{\sqrt{n+2}}{8\sqrt{n}\Gamma(1+\frac{n}{2}))^{2/n}}\right),\ &n\geq 3.\end{cases}

Theorem
1.1
will follow from a sharper estimate below (Theorem
2.4
). With the aid of Stirling’s formula we see that in
C_{1}(n)

the factor involving Gamma function have a uniformly positive lower bound, about 1.014, for all
n\geq 3
, see Remark
2.6
below.
Theorem
1.1
also implies an eigenvalue estimate (cf. Corollary
2.7
below),
which shows that the constant
C_{1}(n)
improves on previous version of Melas’ improvement (
1.3
).

Recall that Weyl’s conjecture on sharper asymptotic behavior of Dirichlet eigenvalue states for domains with piecewise smooth boundary that

(1.15)

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{D}_{\Omega}(\lambda)=\frac{|\Omega|\omega(n)}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda^{n/2}-\frac{\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial\Omega)}{2^{n+1}\pi^{\frac{d-1}{2}}\Gamma(\frac{n+1}{2})}\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}}+o(\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}}),

where
\mathcal{N}^{D}_{\Omega}(\lambda)
is the counting function

\mathcal{N}^{D}_{\Omega}(\lambda):=\#\{\lambda_{k}(\Omega):\,\lambda_{k}(\Omega)<\lambda\}.

The conjecture has been proved by Ivrii
[
14
]
under the condition that the set of all periodic geodesic billiards in
\Omega
has measure zero, and proved in
[
28
]
for convex analytic domains
and polygons.
From (
1.15
), one might hope to replace
(1-C_{1}(n)\lambda^{-1}|J_{1}|^{2})
in R.H.S. of (
1.13
)
by something like
(1-C_{1}(n)\lambda^{-1/2}|J_{1}|)
. However, since we didn’t assume any regularity of
\partial\Omega
,
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial\Omega)

might be infinite, Weyl’s sharper asymptotic formula cannot hold in such general settings, see however
[
19
, Theorem 2]

for domains with certain smoothness on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
.

Theorem
1.1
implies the following qualitative result.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded open set in
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
,
n\geq 3
. Then there exist infinitely many eigenvalues
\{\lambda_{k_{j}}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfying Pólya’s conjecture, i.e.,

(1.16)

\displaystyle\frac{|\Omega||\omega(n)|}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda_{k_{j}}^{\frac{n}{2}}\geq k_{j}.

The corollary will follow from Theorem
2.8
below, where we in fact show that
for a certain length of interval there is at least one eigenvalue satisfies Pólya’s conjecture.

We have a similar but weaker result for the Neumann case which improves the Kröger inequality from
[
20
]
, quantitatively.
Note that, it is an open question raised by Weidl from 2006

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded open set in
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
,

. Suppose that

has a discrete spectrum

0=\gamma_{1}\leq\gamma_{2}\leq\gamma_{3}\leq\cdots.

Then
it holds for
\gamma>0
that

(1.17)

\displaystyle\sum_{k:\,\gamma_{k}<\gamma}(\gamma-\gamma_{k})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\omega(n)|\Omega|}{(2\pi)^{n}}\frac{2\gamma^{1+\frac{n}{2}}}{n+2}\left(1+\frac{n(n+2)}{3}\frac{J_{1}^{4}}{\gamma^{1/2}(4J_{1}^{2}+\gamma)^{3/2}}\right).

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded open set in
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
,
n\geq 5
. Suppose that
-\Delta_{N}
has a discrete spectrum

0=\gamma_{1}\leq\gamma_{2}\leq\gamma_{3}\leq\cdots.

Then there exist infinitely many eigenvalues
\{\gamma_{k_{j}}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfying Pólya’s conjecture, i.e.,

(1.18)

\displaystyle k_{j}-1\geq\frac{\omega(n)|\Omega|}{(2\pi)^{n}}\gamma_{k_{j}}^{n/2}.

From the technique point of view the improvement on Neumann case is weaker seems somehow nature to us, since

while for the Neumann case, the Fourier transform does not simply transform the Laplacian to polynomial multipliers.

2
Improved Berezin-Li-Yau inequality

In this section we prove Theorem
1.1
and Corollary
1.2
, which will follow from a sharper version, Theorem
2.4
below.
In this section, we assume that
n\geq 2
.

For
x\in\Omega
, let

f_{\lambda}(x)=\sum_{k:\,\lambda_{k}<\lambda}\phi_{k}(x)^{2},

where
\phi_{k}
is the corresponding normalized eigenfunction to
\lambda_{k}
, and for
\xi\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
, let

F_{\lambda}(\xi)=\sum_{k:\,\lambda_{k}<\lambda}|\hat{\phi}_{k}(\xi)|^{2}.

It is well known by using the orthogonal property of eigenfunctions
\{\phi_{k}\}
and viewing
\hat{\phi}_{k}(\xi)
as
the function
(2\pi)^{-n/2}e^{ix\cdot\xi}
projecting to the bases
\{\phi_{k}\}_{\lambda_{k}<\lambda}
(see
[
24
]
for instance) that

F_{\lambda}(\xi)=\sum_{k:\,\lambda_{k}<\lambda}|\hat{\phi}_{k}(\xi)|^{2}\leq(2\pi)^{-n}|\Omega|.

On the other hand, by using the function
(2\pi)^{-n/2}xe^{ix\cdot\xi}
projecting to the bases
\{\phi_{k}\}_{\lambda_{k}<\lambda}
we have

(2.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{k:\,\lambda_{k}<\lambda}|\nabla\hat{\phi}_{k}(\xi)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k:\,\lambda_{k}<\lambda}(2\pi)^{-n}\left|\int_{\Omega}ixe^{-ix\xi}\phi_{k}(x)\,dx\right|^{2}\leq(2\pi)^{-n}\int_{\Omega}|x|^{2}\,dx.

In what follows,
we denote

I(\Omega)=\inf_{a\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}\int_{\Omega}|x-a|^{2}\,dx.

By translation, we see that

(2.2)

\displaystyle\sum_{k:\,\lambda_{k}<\lambda}|\nabla\hat{\phi}_{k}(\xi)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(2\pi)^{-n}I(\Omega).

Note that

(2.3)

\displaystyle I(\Omega)\leq\frac{1}{4}\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)^{2}|\Omega|,

and by the Hardy-Littlewood inequality (cf.
[
18
, Corollary 1.4.1]
), it holds that

(2.4)

\displaystyle I(\Omega)\geq\int_{B(0,R_{\Omega}):|B(0,R_{\Omega})|=|\Omega|}|x|^{2}\,dx=\frac{n}{n+2}R_{\Omega}^{2}|\Omega|.

The Hölder inequality then implies that

(2.5)

\displaystyle|\nabla F_{\lambda}|\leq 2\sum_{k:\,\lambda_{k}<\lambda}|\hat{\phi}_{k}(\xi)||\nabla\hat{\phi}_{k}(\xi)|\leq 2(2\pi)^{-n}|\Omega|^{1/2}|I(\Omega)|^{1/2}.

For
t>0
, we define the non-increasing re-arrangement of
F_{\lambda}(\xi)
as

F_{\lambda}^{\#}(t):=\begin{cases}\sup_{\xi\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}F_{\lambda}(\xi),&t=0,\\
\inf\{s:\,|\{\xi\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}:\,F_{\lambda}(\xi)>s\}|\leq t\},&t>0.\end{cases}

The Schwarz symmetrization of
F_{\lambda}(\xi)
is then defined as

F_{\lambda}^{\ast}(y)=F_{\lambda}^{\#}(\omega(n)|y|^{n}).

Lemma 2.1
.

The function
F_{\lambda}^{\ast}\in W^{1,p}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
for all
p\in[1,\infty]
, and in particular,

\|\nabla F_{\lambda}^{\ast}\|_{L^{p}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}\leq\|\nabla F_{\lambda}\|_{L^{p}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})},\ \forall\,p\in[1,\infty].

Proof.

By definition of
F_{\lambda}
, we have

\displaystyle|\nabla F_{\lambda}(\xi)|\leq 2\sum_{k:\,\lambda_{k}<\lambda}|\hat{\phi}_{k}(\xi)||\nabla\hat{\phi}_{k}(\xi)|\leq 2F_{\lambda}(\xi)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{k:\,\lambda_{k}<\lambda}(2\pi)^{-n}\left|\int_{\Omega}ixe^{-ix\xi}\phi_{k}(x)\,dx\right|^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

Since
\phi_{k}
and
x\phi_{k}(x)
belong to
L^{1}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})\cap L^{\infty}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
, the Plancherel principle and the fact Fourier transform maps
L^{1}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
to
L^{\infty}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
imply that

\displaystyle|\nabla F_{\lambda}(\xi)|\in L^{1}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})\cap L^{\infty}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}).

The Pólya-Szegő inequality then implies for all
p\in[1,\infty)
that

\|\nabla F_{\lambda}^{\ast}\|_{L^{p}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}\leq\|\nabla F_{\lambda}\|_{L^{p}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}.

For the case
p=\infty
, we can follow the proof of
[
18
, Theorem 2.3.2]
in the case of bounded domains.
From the cases
p<\infty
, it follows that
F_{\lambda}^{\ast}\in W^{1,p}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
for all
p\in[1,\infty)
, and then from the Sobolev embedding that

F_{\lambda}^{\ast}
is in the class
C^{\alpha}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
for all
\alpha<1
. We further see that

F_{\lambda}^{\#}
is continuous on
(0,\infty)
.

For
s>0
, set
t=F_{\lambda}^{\#}(s)
. Let

F_{\lambda}^{\#}(s+k)=t-h.

Then it holds that

\displaystyle\int_{\{t-h<F_{\lambda}\leq t\}}|\nabla F_{\lambda}|\,dx\leq\|\nabla F_{\lambda}\|_{L^{\infty}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}\left(\mu(t-h)-\mu(t)\right),

where

\mu(t)=|\{\xi\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}:\,F_{\lambda}(\xi)>t\}|

is the distribution of
F_{\lambda}
.
The coarea formula together with the isoperimetric inequality then implies that

\displaystyle\int_{\{t-h<F_{\lambda}\leq t\}}|\nabla F_{\lambda}|\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{t-h}^{t}P(\{\xi\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}:\,F_{\lambda}(\xi)>\tau\})\,d\tau

\displaystyle\geq n\omega(n)^{1/n}\int_{t-h}^{t}\mu(\tau)^{1-1/n}\,d\tau

\displaystyle\geq n\omega(n)^{1/n}h\mu(t)^{1-1/n}.

This further implies that

\displaystyle F_{\lambda}^{\#}(s)-F_{\lambda}^{\#}(s+k)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|\nabla F_{\lambda}\|_{L^{\infty}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}}{n\omega(n)^{1/n}}ks^{-1+1/n}

and hence,

\displaystyle 0<-\frac{\,dF_{\lambda}^{\#}(s)}{\,ds}\leq\frac{\|\nabla F_{\lambda}\|_{L^{\infty}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}}{n\omega(n)^{1/n}}s^{-1+1/n}.

It follows then for
|x|>|y|
,
F_{\lambda}^{\ast}(x)\leq F_{\lambda}^{\ast}(y)
and

\displaystyle F_{\lambda}^{\ast}(y)-F_{\lambda}^{\ast}(x)=\int_{\omega(n)|y|^{n}}^{\omega(n)|x|^{n}}-\frac{\,dF_{\lambda}^{\#}(s)}{\,ds}\,ds\leq\|\nabla F_{\lambda}\|_{L^{\infty}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}(|x|-|y|).

The proof is complete.
∎

For simplicity of notions we denote by
\psi(r)=\psi(|x|)=F_{\lambda}^{\ast}(x)
.

Lemma 2.2
.

It holds that

\displaystyle\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}(\lambda-|\xi|^{2})F_{\lambda}(\xi)\,d\xi\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}n\omega(n)\left(\frac{\lambda r^{n}}{n}-\frac{r^{n+2}}{n+2}\right)(-\psi^{\prime}(r))\,dr.

Proof.

We first truncate the function
|\xi|^{2}

to

G_{k}(\xi)=\begin{cases}|\xi|^{2},&|\xi|\leq k\\
k^{2},&|\xi|>k.\end{cases}

Then
G_{k}
is in
L^{\infty}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
and is radially non-decreasing.

Since
F_{\lambda}^{\ast}(x)
and
F_{\lambda}(x)
are equi-measurable and belong to
L^{1}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
,
F_{\lambda}^{\ast}(x)
is radially non-increasing,
G_{k}(\xi)
is radially non-decreasing and belongs to
L^{\infty}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
, we deduce from
the Hardy-Littlewood inequality (cf.
[
18
, Corollary 1.4.1]
) that

\displaystyle\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}|\xi|^{2}F_{\lambda}(\xi)\,d\xi\geq\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}G_{k}(\xi)F_{\lambda}(\xi)\,d\xi\geq\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}G_{k}(\xi)F_{\lambda}^{\ast}(\xi)\,d\xi.

Letting
k\to\infty
and using the monotone convergence theorem give that

\displaystyle\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}|\xi|^{2}F_{\lambda}(\xi)\,d\xi\geq\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}|\xi|^{2}F_{\lambda}^{\ast}(\xi)\,d\xi.

We therefore have that

\displaystyle\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}(\lambda-|\xi|^{2})F_{\lambda}(\xi)\,d\xi

\displaystyle\leq\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}(\lambda-|\xi|^{2})F_{\lambda}^{\ast}(\xi)\,d\xi

(2.6)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}(\lambda-r^{2})\psi(r)n\omega(n)r^{n-1}\,dr.

Since
\psi(|x|)=F_{\lambda}^{\ast}(x)
is bounded, non-increasing on
(0,\infty)
,

\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi(r)n\omega(n)r^{n-1}\,dr=\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}F_{\lambda}(\xi)\,d\xi<\infty,

and

\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi(r)\omega(n){n}r^{n+1}\,dr\leq\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}|\xi|^{2}F_{\lambda}(\xi)\,d\xi<\infty,

we see that

\displaystyle\lim_{r\to\infty}\psi(r)r^{n+2}=0.

Therefore for large enough
T
, saying
T>200\sqrt{\lambda}
, we integrate by parts and obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}(\lambda-r^{2})\psi(r)n\omega(n)r^{n-1}\,dr

\displaystyle={\omega(n)}{n}\left(\frac{\lambda r^{n}}{n}-\frac{r^{n+2}}{n+2}\right)\psi(r)\bigg|_{0}^{T}-\int_{0}^{T}n\omega(n)\left(\frac{\lambda r^{n}}{n}-\frac{r^{n+2}}{n+2}\right)(\psi^{\prime}(r))\,dr

\displaystyle={\omega(n)}{n}\left(\frac{\lambda T^{n}}{n}-\frac{T^{n+2}}{n+2}\right)\psi(T)-\int_{0}^{T}n\omega(n)\left(\frac{\lambda r^{n}}{n}-\frac{r^{n+2}}{n+2}\right)(\psi^{\prime}(r))\,dr,

which is equivalent to

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}n\omega(n)\left(\frac{\lambda r^{n}}{n}-\frac{r^{n+2}}{n+2}\right)(\psi^{\prime}(r))\,dr

\displaystyle={\omega(n)}{n}\left(\frac{\lambda T^{n}}{n}-\frac{T^{n+2}}{n+2}\right)\psi(T)-\int_{0}^{T}(\lambda-r^{2})\psi(r)n\omega(n)r^{n-1}\,dr.

Since
\lim_{r\to\infty}\psi(r)r^{n+2}=0
(so
\lim_{r\to\infty}\psi(r)r^{n}=0
too),

\left|\int_{0}^{T}(\lambda-r^{2})\psi(r)n\omega(n)r^{n-1}\,dr\right|\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}(\lambda+r^{2})\psi(r)n\omega(n)r^{n-1}\,dr<\infty,

and the integral
\int_{0}^{T}n\omega(n)\left(\frac{\lambda r^{n}}{n}-\frac{r^{n+2}}{n+2}\right)(\psi^{\prime}(r))\,dr
is increasing on
T\in(\sqrt{\frac{n+2}{n}\lambda},\infty)
,
we see that
\int_{0}^{T}n\omega(n)\left(\frac{\lambda r^{n}}{n}-\frac{r^{n+2}}{n+2}\right)(\psi^{\prime}(r))\,dr
, as a function in
T
, is increasing on
(\sqrt{\frac{n+2}{n}\lambda},\infty)

and uniformly bounded from above.
Therefore the monotone converges theorem guarantees that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}(\lambda-r^{2})\psi(r)n\omega(n)r^{n-1}\,dr

\displaystyle={\omega(n)}{n}\left(\frac{\lambda r^{n}}{n}-\frac{r^{n+2}}{n+2}\right)\psi(r)\bigg|_{0}^{\infty}-\int_{0}^{\infty}n\omega(n)\left(\frac{\lambda r^{n}}{n}-\frac{r^{n+2}}{n+2}\right)(\psi^{\prime}(r))\,dr

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{\infty}n\omega(n)\left(\frac{\lambda r^{n}}{n}-\frac{r^{n+2}}{n+2}\right)(\psi^{\prime}(r))\,dr.

This together with (
2
) completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
n\geq 2
and
\lambda>0
. Suppose that
g\geq 0
on
(0,\infty)
, which satisfies

\int_{0}^{\infty}g(r)\,dr\leq A,\,g(r)\leq B

with
A,B>0
satisfying
A/B<\sqrt{\frac{n+2}{n}\lambda}
.
Then

\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\lambda r^{n}}{n}-\frac{r^{n+2}}{n+2}\right)g(r)\,dr\leq B\left(\frac{\lambda\left[(t+A/B)^{n+1}-t^{n+1}\right]}{n(n+1)}-\frac{\left[(t+A/B)^{n+3}-t^{n+3}\right]}{(n+2)(n+3)}\right),

where
t<\sqrt{\lambda}
is such that
t+A/B>\sqrt{\lambda}
and

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda\left[(t+A/B)^{n}-t^{n}\right]}{n}=\frac{\left[(t+A/B)^{n+2}-t^{n+2}\right]}{(n+2)}.

Proof.

Note that the function

\frac{\lambda r^{n}}{n}-\frac{r^{n+2}}{n+2}

tends to zero when
r\to 0
, and is minus infinite when
r=\infty
.
Moreover, there is only one maximum in
(0,\infty)
, which attains at the point

r=\sqrt{\lambda},

and the function is negative if

\lambda<\frac{n}{n+2}r^{2}\Longleftrightarrow r>\sqrt{\frac{n+2}{n}\lambda}.

So for any
g
satisfies the assumption,

\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\lambda r^{n}}{n}-\frac{r^{n+2}}{n+2}\right)g(r)\,dr\leq\int_{0}^{\sqrt{\frac{n+2}{n}\lambda}}\left(\frac{\lambda r^{n}}{n}-\frac{r^{n+2}}{n+2}\right)g(r)\,dr.

Therefore we may and do assume that
{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,supp\,}}g\subset(0,\sqrt{\frac{n+2}{n}\lambda})
.

Further, note that
\sqrt{\frac{n+2}{n}\lambda}-A/B>0
.
If
\int_{0}^{\sqrt{\frac{n+2}{n}\lambda}}g\,dr<A
, we can find another
\tilde{g}
such that

B\geq\tilde{g}\geq g
and
\int_{0}^{\sqrt{\frac{n+2}{n}\lambda}}\tilde{g}\,dr=A
. It is obvious that

\int_{0}^{\sqrt{\frac{n+2}{n}\lambda}}\left(\frac{\lambda r^{n}}{n}-\frac{r^{n+2}}{n+2}\right)g(r)\,dr\leq\int_{0}^{\sqrt{\frac{n+2}{n}\lambda}}\left(\frac{\lambda r^{n}}{n}-\frac{r^{n+2}}{n+2}\right)\tilde{g}(r)\,dr.

For
t\in(0,\sqrt{\frac{n+2}{n}\lambda}-A/B)
, let us set

g_{t}(r)=\begin{cases}B,&r\in(t,t+A/B)\\
0,&\mbox{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Then

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\sqrt{\frac{n+2}{n}\lambda}}\left(\frac{\lambda r^{n}}{n}-\frac{r^{n+2}}{n+2}\right)g_{t}(r)\,dr

\displaystyle=B\left(\frac{\lambda r^{n+1}}{n(n+1)}-\frac{r^{n+3}}{(n+2)(n+3)}\right)\bigg|_{t}^{t+A/B}

\displaystyle=B\left(\frac{\lambda\left[(t+A/B)^{n+1}-t^{n+1}\right]}{n(n+1)}-\frac{\left[(t+A/B)^{n+3}-t^{n+3}\right]}{(n+2)(n+3)}\right)

\displaystyle=:BG(t).

The last term
G(t)
as a function of
t
, converges to
-\infty
as
t\to\infty
, and when
t=0
, it holds that

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\lambda\left[(A/B)^{n+1}\right]}{n(n+1)}-\frac{\left[(A/B)^{n+3}\right]}{(n+2)(n+3)}\right)

\displaystyle=(A/B)^{n+1}\left(\frac{\lambda}{n(n+1)}-\frac{\left[(A/B)^{2}\right]}{(n+2)(n+3)}\right)

\displaystyle\geq(A/B)^{n+1}\left(\frac{\lambda}{n(n+1)}-\frac{\lambda}{n(n+3)}\right)

\displaystyle=(A/B)^{n+1}\frac{2\lambda}{n(n+1)(n+3)}.

The maximal point of the
G(t)
is attained at the point
t_{0}
such that

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda\left[(t_{0}+A/B)^{n}-t_{0}^{n}\right]}{n}=\frac{\left[(t_{0}+A/B)^{n+2}-t_{0}^{n+2}\right]}{(n+2)}.

Equivalently,

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda(t_{0}+A/B)^{n}}{n}-\frac{\lambda(t_{0}+A/B)^{n+2}}{n+2}=\frac{\lambda t_{0}^{n}}{n}-\frac{t_{0}^{n+2}}{(n+2)}.

Since
\frac{\lambda r^{n}}{n}-\frac{r^{n+2}}{n+2}
is increasing on
(0,\sqrt{\lambda})
and decreasing on
(\sqrt{\lambda},\infty)
, we see that

t_{0}<\sqrt{\lambda}
and
t_{0}+A/B>\sqrt{\lambda}
.

For any other
{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,supp\,}}g\subset(0,\sqrt{\frac{n+2}{n}\lambda})
satisfying
\int_{0}^{\sqrt{\frac{n+2}{n}\lambda}}g\,dr=A
and the assumptions,
suppose that
g
is not fully supported in
[t_{0},t_{0}+A/B]
, i.e.,

\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{0}+A/B}g(t)\,dt<A.

Then

\displaystyle\int_{(0,\sqrt{\frac{n+2}{n}\lambda})\setminus[t_{0},t_{0}+A/B]}\left(\frac{\lambda r^{n}}{n}-\frac{r^{n+2}}{n+2}\right)g(r)\,dr

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{t\in(0,\sqrt{\frac{n+2}{n}\lambda})\setminus[t_{0},t_{0}+A/B]}\left(\frac{\lambda r^{n}}{n}-\frac{r^{n+2}}{n+2}\right)\left(A-\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{0}+A/B}g(t)\,dt\right)

\displaystyle=\inf_{t\in[t_{0},t_{0}+A/B]}\left(\frac{\lambda r^{n}}{n}-\frac{r^{n+2}}{n+2}\right)\left(\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{0}+A/B}\left(g_{t_{0}}(t)-g(t)\right)\,dt\right)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{0}+A/B}\left(\frac{\lambda r^{n}}{n}-\frac{r^{n+2}}{n+2}\right)\left(g_{t_{0}}(t)-g(t)\right)\,dt.

The proof is complete.
∎

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded open set in
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
,
n\geq 2
. Then it holds for
\lambda>0
that

\displaystyle\sum_{k:\,\lambda_{k}<\lambda}(\lambda-\lambda_{k})\begin{cases}=0,&\lambda\leq\frac{(2\pi)^{2}}{|\Omega|^{2/n}\omega(n)^{2/n}},\\
\leq\frac{2n\omega(n)}{(2\pi)^{n}}|\Omega|^{1/2}|I(\Omega)|^{1/2}\left[\frac{\lambda((t_{0}+J_{1})^{n+1}-t_{0}^{n+1})}{n(n+1)}-\frac{(t_{0}+J_{1})^{n+3}-t_{0}^{n+3}}{(n+2)(n+3)}\right],&\lambda>\frac{(2\pi)^{2}}{|\Omega|^{2/n}\omega(n)^{2/n}},\end{cases}

where
t_{0}<\sqrt{\lambda}
is such that
t_{0}+J_{1}>\sqrt{\lambda}
and

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda\left[(t_{0}+J_{1})^{n}-t_{0}^{n}\right]}{n}=\frac{\left[(t_{0}+J_{1})^{n+2}-t_{0}^{n+2}\right]}{(n+2)}.

Proof.

By the definition of
F_{\lambda}
and Lemma
2.2
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k:\,\lambda_{k}<\lambda}(\lambda-\lambda_{k})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k:\,\lambda_{k}<\lambda}\int_{\Omega}(\lambda-\lambda_{k})|\phi_{k}(x)|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=\sum_{k:\,\lambda_{k}<\lambda}\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}(\lambda-|\xi|^{2})|\hat{\phi}_{k}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}(\lambda-|\xi|^{2})F_{\lambda}(\xi)\,d\xi

(2.7)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}n\omega(n)\left(\frac{\lambda r^{n}}{n}-\frac{r^{n+2}}{n+2}\right)(-\psi^{\prime}(r))\,dr,

where
\psi(r)=\psi(|x|)=F_{\lambda}^{\ast}(x)
.

By Lemma
2.1
and (
2.5
), we have that

On the other hand, we have

0\leq\psi(r)\leq(2\pi)^{-n}|\Omega|.

Now set
A=(2\pi)^{-n}|\Omega|
and
B=2(2\pi)^{-n}|\Omega|^{1/2}|I(\Omega)|^{1/2}
in Lemma
2.3
.
Recall from (
2.4
) that

\displaystyle I(\Omega)\geq\int_{B(0,R_{\Omega})}|x|^{2}\,dx=\frac{n}{n+2}R_{\Omega}^{2}|\Omega|,

and we further have

(2.8)

\displaystyle A/B=\frac{(2\pi)^{-n}|\Omega|}{2(2\pi)^{-n}|\Omega|^{1/2}|I(\Omega)|^{1/2}}=\frac{|\Omega|^{1/2}}{2|I(\Omega)|^{1/2}}=J_{1}\leq\frac{\sqrt{n+2}}{2R_{\Omega}\sqrt{n}}.

where

1.11

On the other hand, note that for the first eigenvalue we always have

(2.9)

\lambda_{1}\geq\frac{(2\pi)^{2}}{|\Omega|^{2/n}\omega(n)^{2/n}}=\frac{(2\pi)^{2}}{R_{\Omega}^{2}\omega(n)^{4/n}}.

Therefore for

, it holds that

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{n+2}{n}\lambda}

\displaystyle>\sqrt{\frac{n+2}{n}\lambda_{1}}\geq\sqrt{\frac{n+2}{n}}(2\pi)|\omega(n)|^{-2/n}R_{\Omega}^{-1}

(2.10)

\displaystyle\geq 4(\Gamma(1+\frac{n}{2}))^{2/n}J_{1}=4(\Gamma(1+\frac{n}{2}))^{2/n}A/B,

since

\omega(n)=\frac{\pi^{n/2}}{\Gamma(1+\frac{n}{2})}.

We therefore deduce from Lemma
2.3
that

\displaystyle\sum_{k:\,\lambda_{k}<\lambda}(\lambda-\lambda_{k})

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}n\omega(n)\left(\frac{\lambda r^{n}}{n}-\frac{r^{n+2}}{n+2}\right)(-\psi^{\prime}(r))\,dr

(2.11)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2n\omega(n)}{(2\pi)^{n}}|\Omega|^{1/2}|I(\Omega)|^{1/2}\left[\frac{\lambda((t_{0}+J_{1})^{n+1}-t_{0}^{n+1})}{n(n+1)}-\frac{(t_{0}+J_{1})^{n+3}-t_{0}^{n+3}}{(n+2)(n+3)}\right],

where

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda\left[(t_{0}+J_{1})^{n}-t_{0}^{n}\right]}{n}=\frac{\left[(t_{0}+J_{1})^{n+2}-t_{0}^{n+2}\right]}{(n+2)},

the proof is complete.
∎

Remark 2.5
.

2.8
) and (
2.9

Remark 2.6
.

By Stirling’s formula

\Gamma(1+x)\geq\sqrt{2\pi x}(x/e)^{x},

\Gamma(1+\frac{n}{2}))^{2/n}
behaves as
\frac{n}{2e}
along

.
This implies that

\left(1-\frac{\sqrt{n+2}}{8\sqrt{n}\Gamma(1+\frac{n}{2}))^{2/n}}\right)^{n-1}\to e^{-e/4}\approx 0.507.

So we can find a uniformly positive lower bound for
\left(1-\frac{\sqrt{n+2}}{8\sqrt{n}\Gamma(1+\frac{n}{2}))^{2/n}}\right)^{n-1}\left(2-\frac{\sqrt{n+2}}{8\sqrt{n}\Gamma(1+\frac{n}{2}))^{2/n}}\right)

for all
n\geq 3
. In fact, a calculation shows that the quantity

\left(1-\frac{\sqrt{n+2}}{8\sqrt{n}\Gamma(1+\frac{n}{2}))^{2/n}}\right)^{n-1}\left(2-\frac{\sqrt{n+2}}{8\sqrt{n}\Gamma(1+\frac{n}{2}))^{2/n}}\right)\approx 1.402,1.341,1.248,1.045,1.016,1.014

for
n=3,4,5,100,2000,100000
.

By letting
\lambda=(1+2/n)\lambda_{k}
and dividing both terms in (
1.13
) we have the following improvement
on eigenvalues from
[

]
, see also
[

]
.

(2.13)

\displaystyle k

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded open set in
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
,
n\geq 2
.
For any
\lambda_{j}>\lambda_{1}
, let
\lambda_{j_{1}}
be the smallest eigenvalue that satisfies

\lambda_{j_{1}}\geq\left(\frac{2\lambda_{j}^{1+\frac{n}{2}}}{C_{1}(n)|J_{1}|^{2}}\right)^{2/n}.

Then there exists an eigenvalue
\lambda_{k_{j}}\in[\lambda_{j},\lambda_{j_{1}}]
, that satisfies

\frac{|\Omega||\omega(n)|}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda_{k_{j}}^{\frac{n}{2}}\geq k_{j}-1+\frac{|\Omega||\omega(n)|}{(2\pi)^{n}}\frac{nC_{1}(n)|J_{1}|^{2}}{2(n+2)}\lambda_{k_{j}}^{\frac{n}{2}-1}.

Above
C_{1}(n)
is given by (
1.14
).

Proof.

Let
\{k_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
be a subset of
\mathbb{N}
such that

0<\lambda_{1}=\cdots=\lambda_{k_{1}}<\lambda_{k_{1}+1}=\cdots=\lambda_{k_{2}}<\lambda_{k_{2}+1}\cdots.

Let us set also

F_{D}(\lambda):=\frac{2}{n+2}\lambda^{1+\frac{n}{2}}\frac{|\Omega||\omega(n)|}{(2\pi)^{n}}\left(1-\frac{C_{1}(n)|J_{1}|^{2}}{2\lambda}\right)_{+}-\sum_{k:\,\lambda_{k}<\lambda}(\lambda-\lambda_{k}).

Obviously,
F_{D}(\lambda)
is absolutely continuous on
(0,\infty)
, and smooth in each interval
(\lambda_{k_{j}},\lambda_{k_{j}+1})
.
Since
\lambda_{1}>8|J_{1}|^{2}
by (
2
), for
\lambda>\lambda_{1}
,

F_{D}(\lambda)=\frac{2}{n+2}\lambda^{1+\frac{n}{2}}\frac{|\Omega||\omega(n)|}{(2\pi)^{n}}\left(1-\frac{C_{1}(n)|J_{1}|^{2}}{2\lambda}\right)-\sum_{k:\,\lambda_{k}<\lambda}(\lambda-\lambda_{k}).

For
\lambda\in(\lambda_{k_{j}},\lambda_{k_{j}+1})=(\lambda_{k_{j}},\lambda_{k_{j+1}})
, it holds that

F_{D}(\lambda)=\frac{2}{n+2}\lambda^{1+\frac{n}{2}}\frac{|\Omega||\omega(n)|}{(2\pi)^{n}}\left(1-\frac{C_{1}(n)|J_{1}|^{2}}{2\lambda}\right)-\sum_{i=1}^{j}(k_{i}-k_{i-1})(\lambda-\lambda_{k_{j}}),

where we set
k_{0}=0
.
Moreover, for
\lambda\in(\lambda_{k_{j}},\lambda_{k_{j}+1})
,
j\geq 1
,

\displaystyle F^{\prime}_{D}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\frac{|\Omega||\omega(n)|}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda^{\frac{n}{2}}\left(1-\frac{nC_{1}(n)|J_{1}|^{2}}{2(n+2)\lambda}\right)-k_{j}

(2.14)

\displaystyle=\frac{|\Omega||\omega(n)|}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda^{\frac{n}{2}-1}\left(\lambda-\frac{nC_{1}(n)|J_{1}|^{2}}{2(n+2)}\right)-k_{j},

which implies that
F^{\prime}_{D}(\lambda)
is increasing on each
(\lambda_{k_{j}},\lambda_{k_{j}+1})
since
\lambda_{1}>8|J_{1}|^{2}
.

By Theorem
1.1
, we see that for
\lambda>\lambda_{1}
, it holds that

(2.15)

\displaystyle F_{D}(\lambda)=\frac{2}{n+2}\lambda^{1+\frac{n}{2}}\frac{|\Omega||\omega(n)|}{(2\pi)^{n}}\left(1-\frac{C_{1}(n)|J_{1}|^{2}}{2\lambda}\right)-\sum_{k:\,\lambda_{k}<\lambda}(\lambda-\lambda_{k})>\frac{C_{1}(n)|J_{1}|^{2}}{n+2}\lambda^{\frac{n}{2}}\frac{|\Omega||\omega(n)|}{(2\pi)^{n}}.

For a given
\lambda_{k}
, it equals
\lambda_{k_{j_{0}}}
for some
k_{j_{0}}
.
Let
\lambda_{k_{j_{1}}+1}
be the smallest eigenvalue that satisfies

\lambda_{k_{j_{1}}+1}\geq\left(\frac{2\lambda_{k_{j_{0}}}^{1+\frac{n}{2}}}{C_{1}(n)|J_{1}|^{2}}\right)^{2/n}.

The choice of
\lambda_{k_{j_{0}}}
and
\lambda_{k_{j_{1}}+1}
implies that

F_{D}(\lambda_{k_{j_{1}}+1})>F_{D}(\lambda_{k_{j_{0}}}),

and hence,

F_{D}(\lambda_{k_{j_{1}}+1})-F_{D}(\lambda_{k_{j_{0}}})=\int_{\lambda_{k_{j_{0}}}}^{\lambda_{k_{j_{0}}+1}}F^{\prime}_{D}(\lambda)\,d\lambda=\sum_{j_{0}\leq j\leq j_{1}}\int_{\lambda_{k_{j}}}^{\lambda_{k_{j}+1}}F^{\prime}_{D}(\lambda)\,d\lambda>0.

This together the piecewise smoothness of
F^{\prime}_{D}
implies that
F_{D}
is increasing at least on part of an interval
(\lambda_{k_{j}},\lambda_{k_{j}+1})
,
and consequently,
F^{\prime}_{D}(\lambda)
is positive on part of it. Since
F^{\prime}_{D}(\lambda)
is increasing on each
(\lambda_{k_{i}},\lambda_{k_{i}+1})
,
we see that

F_{D}^{\prime}(\lambda_{k_{j}+1}-\epsilon)=\frac{|\Omega||\omega(n)|}{(2\pi)^{n}}(\lambda_{k_{j}+1}-\epsilon)^{\frac{n}{2}}\left(1-\frac{nC_{1}(n)|J_{1}|^{2}}{2(n+2)(\lambda_{k_{j}+1}-\epsilon)}\right)-k_{j}>0

for any sufficiently small
\epsilon>0
.
Sending
\epsilon
to zero gives

\frac{|\Omega||\omega(n)|}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda_{k_{j}+1}^{\frac{n}{2}}\geq k_{j}+\frac{|\Omega||\omega(n)|}{(2\pi)^{n}}\frac{nC_{1}(n)|J_{1}|^{2}}{2(n+2)}\lambda_{k_{j}+1}^{\frac{n}{2}-1}.

The proof is complete.
∎

3
Improved Kröger inequality

In this section, we provide proofs for Theorem
1.3
.
We assume that
-\Delta_{N}
on
\Omega
has discrete spectrum so that

0=\gamma_{1}\leq\gamma_{2}\leq\cdots.

Let
u_{k}
be the corresponding normalized eigenfunctions to
\gamma_{k}
. Then for each
\xi\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|\hat{u}_{k}(\xi)|^{2}=(2\pi)^{-n}|\Omega|

and

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\gamma_{k}|\hat{u}_{k}(\xi)|^{2}=(2\pi)^{-n}|\xi|^{2}|\Omega|.

The two identity together implies that

(3.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{k:\,\gamma_{k}<\gamma}(\gamma-\gamma_{k})|\hat{u}_{k}(\xi)|^{2}\geq\begin{cases}(2\pi)^{-n}(\gamma-|\xi|^{2})|\Omega|,&|\xi|<\sqrt{\gamma},\\
0,&|\xi|\geq\sqrt{\gamma}.\end{cases}

We can now prove Theorem
1.3
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

To estimate

\sum_{k:\,\gamma_{k}<\gamma}(\gamma-\gamma_{k})=\sum_{k:\,\gamma_{k}<\gamma}\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}(\gamma-\gamma_{k})|\hat{u}_{k}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi,

let us denote by
F_{\lambda}(\xi)
the quantity

F_{\lambda}(\xi)=\left(\sum_{k:\,\gamma_{k}<\gamma}(\gamma-\gamma_{k})|\hat{u}_{k}(\xi)|^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

The function
F_{\lambda}(\xi)
is Lipschitz continuous on
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
which satisfies

\displaystyle|\nabla F_{\lambda}(\xi)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{F_{\lambda}(\xi)}\sum_{k:\,\gamma_{k}<\gamma}(\gamma-\gamma_{k})|\hat{u}_{k}(\xi)|\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n/2}}\left|\int_{\Omega}xe^{-ix\cdot\xi}u_{k}(x)\,dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{k:\,\gamma_{k}<\gamma}(\gamma-\gamma_{k})\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\left|\int_{\Omega}xe^{-ix\cdot\xi}u_{k}(x)\,dx\right|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\gamma}\left(\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{\Omega}x^{2}\,dx\right)^{1/2}.

By suitably moving the position of
\Omega
, we see that it further holds

\displaystyle|\nabla F_{\lambda}(\xi)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sqrt{\gamma}}{(2\pi)^{n/2}}\left(\inf_{x_{0}\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}\int_{\Omega}|x-x_{0}|^{2}\,dx\right)^{1/2}=\frac{\sqrt{\gamma}}{(2\pi)^{n/2}}I(\Omega)^{1/2}.

Let us denote by
F_{\lambda}^{\ast}
the Schwarz symmetrization of
F_{\lambda}
.
Lemma
2.1
tells us that

\displaystyle|\nabla F_{\lambda}^{\ast}(\xi)|

\displaystyle\leq\||\nabla F_{\lambda}^{\ast}\|_{L^{\infty}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}\leq\frac{\sqrt{\gamma}}{(2\pi)^{n/2}}I(\Omega)^{1/2}.

On the other hand, note that it follows from (
3.1
) that

F_{\lambda}(\xi)\geq\frac{|\Omega|^{1/2}}{(2\pi)^{n/2}}\sqrt{(\gamma-|\xi|^{2})_{+}},

which implies that

F_{\lambda}^{\ast}(\xi)\geq\frac{|\Omega|^{1/2}}{(2\pi)^{n/2}}\sqrt{(\gamma-|\xi|^{2})_{+}}.

Set

\varphi_{\gamma}(r):=\frac{|\Omega|^{1/2}}{(2\pi)^{n/2}}\sqrt{(\gamma-r^{2})_{+}}.

Then for
r<\sqrt{\gamma}
,

\varphi^{\prime}_{\gamma}(r)=\frac{|\Omega|^{1/2}}{(2\pi)^{n/2}}\frac{-r}{\sqrt{\gamma-r^{2}}},

which is decreasing on
(0,\sqrt{\gamma})
.

Consider the equation

\frac{|\Omega|^{1/2}}{(2\pi)^{n/2}}\frac{r}{\sqrt{\gamma-r^{2}}}=\frac{\sqrt{\gamma}}{(2\pi)^{n/2}}I(\Omega)^{1/2},

which has a unique positive solution

r_{0}=\frac{\gamma}{\sqrt{4J_{1}^{2}+\gamma}}.

Since

F_{\lambda}^{\ast}(\xi)=F_{\lambda}^{\ast}(|\xi|)\geq\varphi_{\gamma}(|\xi|),\,\&\,|\nabla F_{\lambda}^{\ast}(\xi)|\leq\frac{\sqrt{\gamma}}{(2\pi)^{n/2}}I(\Omega)^{1/2},

we find that

(3.2)

\displaystyle F_{\lambda}^{\ast}(\xi)\geq

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\frac{|\Omega|^{1/2}}{(2\pi)^{n/2}}\sqrt{(\gamma-|\xi|^{2})},&\,|\xi|\leq r_{0},\\
\frac{|\Omega|^{1/2}}{(2\pi)^{n/2}}\frac{2J_{1}\sqrt{\gamma}}{\sqrt{4J_{1}^{2}+\gamma}}-\frac{\sqrt{\gamma}}{(2\pi)^{n/2}}I(\Omega)^{1/2}(|\xi|-r_{0}),&\,r_{0}<|\xi|<r_{0}+\frac{4J_{1}^{2}}{\sqrt{4J_{1}^{2}+\gamma}},\\
0,&\,|\xi|\geq r_{0}+\frac{4J_{1}^{2}}{\sqrt{4J_{1}^{2}+\gamma}}.\end{cases}

Note that

r_{0}+\frac{4J_{1}^{2}}{\sqrt{4J_{1}^{2}+\gamma}}=\sqrt{4J_{1}^{2}+\gamma}=:r_{1},

and so for
r_{0}<|\xi|<r_{1}
,

\displaystyle F_{\lambda}^{\ast}(\xi)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{|\Omega|^{1/2}}{(2\pi)^{n/2}}\frac{2J_{1}\sqrt{\gamma}}{\sqrt{4J_{1}^{2}+\gamma}}-\frac{\sqrt{\gamma}}{(2\pi)^{n/2}}I(\Omega)^{1/2}(|\xi|-r_{0})

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{\gamma}}{(2\pi)^{n/2}}I(\Omega)^{1/2}(r_{1}-|\xi|)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{|\Omega|^{1/2}}{(2\pi)^{n/2}}\sqrt{(\gamma-|\xi|^{2})_{+}}.

We therefore conclude that

\displaystyle\sum_{k:\,\gamma_{k}<\gamma}(\gamma-\gamma_{k})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k:\,\gamma_{k}<\gamma}\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}(\gamma-\gamma_{k})|\hat{u}_{k}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}[F_{\lambda}(\xi)]^{2}\,d\xi=\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}[F_{\lambda}^{\ast}(\xi)]^{2}\,d\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}{n}{\omega(n)}[F_{\lambda}^{\ast}(r)]^{2}r^{n-1}\,dr

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{r_{0}}{n}{\omega(n)r^{n-1}}\frac{|\Omega|}{(2\pi)^{n}}{(\gamma-r^{2})}\,dr

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{r_{0}}^{r_{1}}{n}{\omega(n)r^{n-1}}\frac{|I(\Omega)|\gamma}{(2\pi)^{n}}\left(r_{1}-r\right)^{2}\,dr

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{\sqrt{\gamma}}{n}{\omega(n)r^{n-1}}\frac{|\Omega|}{(2\pi)^{n}}{(\gamma-r^{2})}\,dr

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\sqrt{\gamma}}^{r_{1}}{n}{\omega(n)r^{n-1}}\frac{|I(\Omega)|\gamma}{(2\pi)^{n}}\left(r_{1}-r\right)^{2}\,dr.

For the first integral, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\sqrt{\gamma}}{n}{\omega(n)r^{n-1}}\frac{|\Omega|}{(2\pi)^{n}}{(\lambda-r^{2})}\,dr

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega(n)|\Omega|}{(2\pi)^{n}}\frac{2\gamma^{1+\frac{n}{2}}}{n+2}.

For the second integral, it holds that

\displaystyle\int_{\sqrt{\gamma}}^{r_{1}}{n}{\omega(n)r^{n-1}}\frac{|I(\Omega)|\gamma}{(2\pi)^{n}}\left(r_{1}-r\right)^{2}\,dr

\displaystyle\geq\frac{n|I(\Omega)|\omega(n)\gamma}{3(2\pi)^{n}}\gamma^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\left(r_{1}-\sqrt{\gamma}\right)^{3}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{n|I(\Omega)|\omega(n)\gamma}{3(2\pi)^{n}}\gamma^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\left(\frac{4J_{1}^{2}}{\sqrt{4J_{1}^{2}+\gamma}+\sqrt{\gamma}}\right)^{3}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{n|I(\Omega)|\omega(n)}{3(2\pi)^{n}}\gamma^{\frac{n+1}{2}}\frac{8J_{1}^{6}}{(4J_{1}^{2}+\gamma)^{3/2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{n|\Omega|\omega(n)}{3(2\pi)^{n}}\gamma^{\frac{n+1}{2}}\frac{2J_{1}^{4}}{(4J_{1}^{2}+\gamma)^{3/2}}

Summing up the above two estimates and using the definition of
r_{0},r_{1}
we arrive at

\displaystyle\sum_{k:\,\gamma_{k}<\gamma}(\gamma-\gamma_{k})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\omega(n)|\Omega|}{(2\pi)^{n}}\frac{2\gamma^{1+\frac{n}{2}}}{n+2}+\frac{n|\Omega|\omega(n)}{3(2\pi)^{n}}\gamma^{\frac{n+1}{2}}\frac{2J_{1}^{4}}{(4J_{1}^{2}+\gamma)^{3/2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega(n)|\Omega|}{(2\pi)^{n}}\frac{2\gamma^{1+\frac{n}{2}}}{n+2}\left(1+\frac{n(n+2)}{3}\frac{J_{1}^{4}}{\gamma^{1/2}(4J_{1}^{2}+\gamma)^{3/2}}\right).

The proof is complete.
∎

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded open set in
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
,
n\geq 4
. Suppose that
-\Delta_{N}
has a discrete spectrum

0=\gamma_{1}\leq\gamma_{2}\leq\gamma_{3}\leq\cdots.

For any given
\gamma_{j}\geq 4|J_{1}|^{2}
, let
\gamma_{j_{1}}
be the smallest eigenvalue satisfying

\gamma_{k_{j_{1}}+1}\geq\left(j\frac{(2\pi)^{n}}{\omega(n)|\Omega|}\right)^{\frac{2(n+2)}{n(n-2)}}\left(3\left(\frac{n+2}{2}\right)^{2/n}\frac{2^{3/2}}{nJ_{1}^{4}}\right)^{\frac{2}{n-2}}.

there exists
\gamma_{k_{j}}\in[\gamma_{j},\gamma_{j_{1}}]
satisfying

k_{j}-\frac{J_{1}^{4}}{2^{3/2}}\frac{\omega(n)|\Omega|}{(2\pi)^{n}}\frac{n}{3}\frac{n-2}{2}\gamma_{k_{j}}^{\frac{n}{2}-2}\geq\frac{\omega(n)|\Omega|}{(2\pi)^{n}}\gamma_{k_{j}}^{n/2}.

Proof.

Let
\{k_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
be a subset of
\mathbb{N}
such that

0=\gamma_{1}=\cdots=\gamma_{k_{1}}<\gamma_{k_{1}+1}=\cdots=\gamma_{k_{2}}<\gamma_{k_{2}+1}=\cdots=\gamma_{k_{2}}<\cdots.

For
\gamma>4|J_{1}|^{2}
, set

F_{N}(\gamma):=\sum_{k:\,\gamma_{k}<\gamma}(\gamma-\gamma_{k})-\frac{\omega(n)|\Omega|}{(2\pi)^{n}}\frac{2\gamma^{1+\frac{n}{2}}}{n+2}\left(1+\frac{n(n+2)}{6}\frac{J_{1}^{4}}{2^{3/2}}\gamma^{-2}\right).

Assume that
\gamma_{k_{j_{0}}}>4|J_{1}|^{2}
. For
j_{1}>j_{0}
,
\gamma\in(\gamma_{k_{j_{1}}},\gamma_{k_{j_{1}}+1})=(\gamma_{k_{j_{1}}},\gamma_{k_{{j_{1}}+1}})
, we have

(3.3)

\displaystyle F_{N}(\gamma)=\sum_{j=1}^{j_{1}}(k_{j}-k_{j-1})(\gamma-\gamma_{k_{j}})-\frac{\omega(n)|\Omega|}{(2\pi)^{n}}\frac{2\gamma^{1+\frac{n}{2}}}{n+2}\left(1+\frac{n(n+2)}{6}\frac{J_{1}^{4}}{2^{3/2}}\gamma^{-2}\right),

and hence,

(3.4)

\displaystyle F^{\prime}_{N}(\gamma)=k_{j_{1}}-\frac{\omega(n)|\Omega|}{(2\pi)^{n}}\gamma^{n/2}-\frac{J_{1}^{4}}{2^{3/2}}\frac{\omega(n)|\Omega|}{(2\pi)^{n}}\frac{n}{3}\frac{n-2}{2}\gamma^{\frac{n}{2}-2}.

Obviously,
F_{N}^{\prime}(\gamma)
decreases on
(\gamma_{k_{j_{1}}},\gamma_{k_{j_{1}}+1})
as
n\geq 4
.

By Theorem
1.3
,

F_{N}(\gamma)>\frac{\omega(n)|\Omega|}{(2\pi)^{n}}\frac{n\gamma^{\frac{n}{2}-1}}{3}\frac{J_{1}^{4}}{2^{3/2}}

for
\gamma>4|J_{1}|^{2}
.
For a given
\gamma_{j}>4|J_{1}|^{2}
, let
\gamma_{k_{j_{0}}}=\gamma_{j}
, and
\gamma_{k_{j_{1}}+1}
be the smallest eigenvalue satisfying

\frac{\omega(n)|\Omega|}{(2\pi)^{n}}\frac{n\gamma_{k_{j_{1}}+1}^{\frac{n}{2}-1}}{3}\frac{J_{1}^{4}}{2^{3/2}}\geq j^{1+2/n}\left(\frac{n+2}{2}\right)^{2/n}\frac{(2\pi)^{2}}{|\Omega|^{2/n}\omega(n)^{2/n}},

which is equivalent to

\gamma_{k_{j_{1}}+1}\geq\left(j\frac{(2\pi)^{n}}{\omega(n)|\Omega|}\right)^{\frac{2(n+2)}{n(n-2)}}\left(3\left(\frac{n+2}{2}\right)^{2/n}\frac{2^{3/2}}{nJ_{1}^{4}}\right)^{\frac{2}{n-2}}.

By (
1.6
) together with Theorem
1.3
, we have

F_{N}(\gamma_{k_{j_{1}}+1})>j\gamma_{j}>F_{N}(\gamma_{j})=F_{N}(\gamma_{k_{j_{0}}}).

This implies that there exists
(\gamma_{k_{j}},\gamma_{k_{j}+1})\subset(\gamma_{k_{j_{0}}},\gamma_{k_{j_{1}}+1})
such that it contains a small interval that
F_{N}(\lambda)
is increasing.
By (
3.4
), this means there exists
\epsilon_{j}>0
such that

F_{N}(\lambda)
is increasing on
(\gamma_{k_{j}},\gamma_{k_{j}}+\epsilon_{j}),

and consequently,

k_{j}-\frac{\omega(n)|\Omega|}{(2\pi)^{n}}\gamma_{k_{j_{1}}}^{n/2}-\frac{J_{1}^{4}}{2^{3/2}}\frac{\omega(n)|\Omega|}{(2\pi)^{n}}\frac{n}{3}\frac{n-2}{2}\gamma_{k_{j_{1}}}^{\frac{n}{2}-2}\geq 0.

This is equivalent to

k_{j_{1}}-\frac{J_{1}^{4}}{2^{3/2}}\frac{\omega(n)|\Omega|}{(2\pi)^{n}}\frac{n}{3}\frac{n-2}{2}\gamma_{k_{j_{1}}}^{\frac{n}{2}-2}\geq\frac{\omega(n)|\Omega|}{(2\pi)^{n}}\gamma_{k_{j_{1}}}^{n/2},

which completes the proof.
∎

1.4

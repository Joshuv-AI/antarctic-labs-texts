Magnetic brightening of light-like excitons in a monolayer semiconductor
A. Delhomme, T. Amit, P. Ji, C. Faugeras, S. Refaely-Abramson, J. J. Finley, A. V. Stier

Abstract
Monolayer transition-metal dichalcogenides, such as WSe 2 , are direct gap, multi-valley semiconductors. Long-range electron-hole exchange interactions mix the valleys, yielding dispersion relations for massive ( ∝ Q 2 \propto Q^{2} ) as well as light-like ( ∝ Q \propto Q ) excitons. We report magneto-photoluminescence spectroscopy of excitons in the monolayer semiconductor WSe 2 to B = ± 25 B=\pm 25 T. The magnetic field-dependent line shape of the neutral exciton reveals the emergence of a new blue-detuned emission peak in both field orientations. Analyzing the distinct magnetic field-dependent shifts of both peaks facilitates the identification of the emergent feature as a spin-singlet with a significantly smaller reduced exciton mass as compared to the neutral exciton. The intensity of the emergent feature increases with magnetic field according to ∝ B 2 \propto B^{2} , as expected for a linear dispersion relation. The density-dependent diamagnetic shift ratios of both features follow the expected density dependence of the electron-hole exchange interactions. We interpret our observations within a picture of magnetic-field-induced coupling between the bright massive and quasi dark light-like exciton, leading to its brightening.

The observed density dependence of the rate of brightening as well as the diamagnetic shift shows that only the light-like branch is affected by the excess electron density, as predicted by theory. Our results expand the plethora of excitons in monolayer semiconductors to particles obeying linear dispersion relations driven by electron-hole exchange interactions.

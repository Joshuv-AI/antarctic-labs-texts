Interplay of Zeeman field, Rashba spin-orbit interaction, and superconductivity: spin susceptibility
Chen Pang, Yi Zhou

Abstract
We present a self-consistent theory to calculate the static and uniform spin susceptibility in superconductors under simultaneous Zeeman magnetic fields and Rashba-type spin-orbit coupling (SOC). Employing a single-band Bogoliubov-de Gennes Hamiltonian, we solve the gap equation for both conventional s s -wave spin-singlet and six representative p p -wave spin-triplet pairing states, categorized into opposite-spin-pairing (OSP) and equal-spin-pairing (ESP) classes. The Kubo formula, decomposed into intra- and interband particle-hole and particle-particle channels, provides two key constraints: at zero temperature, only particle-particle terms contribute, while at the critical temperature T c T_{c} , only particle-hole terms remain, ensuring χ ⁡ ( T c − ) = χ N \chi(T_{c}^{-})=\chi_{N} for continuous phase transitions. For s s -wave pairing, a Zeeman field reduces T c T_{c} , whereas Rashba SOC preserves T c T_{c} but yields a residual zero temperature spin susceptibility χ ⁡ ( 0 ) \chi(0) which approaches 2 ​ χ N / 3 2\chi_{N}/3 in the strong SOC limit; combined fields create a Bogoliubov Fermi surface, resulting in a kink in χ ⁡ ( 0 ) \chi(0) . In contrast, p p -wave states exhibit strong anisotropy: OSP states mimic spin-singlet pairing behavior for parallel Zeeman fields and ESP for transverse ones, while ESP states show the opposite, with Rashba SOC potentially changing the quasiparticle nodal structure, lowering T c T_{c} , or causing χ z ​ z ​ ( 0 ) \chi_{zz}(0) divergences. This framework offers quantitative benchmarks for Knight-shift experiments in non-centrosymmetric superconductors like A 2 Cr 3 As 3 (A = Na, K, Rb, and Cs), enabling diagnostics to disentangle pairing symmetry, SOC strength, and Zeeman effects.



I
Introduction

Superconductivity, a quintessential demonstration of macroscopic quantum coherence, has been a cornerstone of modern physics since its discovery by H. Kamerlingh Onnes in 1911. A key aspect of this phenomenon is how superconductors respond to external electric and magnetic fields, characterized by zero electrical resistance and perfect diamagnetism, known as the Meissner-Ochsenfeld effect
Tinkham 2004
. These responses not only define superconductivity but also serve as critical tools for investigating the pairing symmetry of Cooper pairs, which is essential for advancing our understanding of the field.

To probe pairing symmetry, nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) techniques, such as the Knight shift and the spin-lattice relaxation rate
1/T_{1}
, are particularly powerful, as they reveal spin susceptibility and dynamics in superconducting states. For example, in an
s
-wave spin-singlet pairing, the Knight shift becomes isotropic and decreases below the critical temperature
T_{c}
, vanishing at zero temperature
Bardeen et al. 1957
, while a spin-triplet pairing shows anisotropic behavior depending on the magnetic field direction
Leggett 1975
. Similarly, the spin-lattice relaxation rate
1/T_{1}
exhibits a Hebel-Slichter coherence peak at
T=T_{c}
for sign-unchanged
s
-wave pairings, but this peak is diminished or absent in sign-changed pairings like
p
-wave states
Leggett 1975
;
Anderson and Morel 1961
;
Anderson and Brinkman 1973
;
Balian and Werthamer 1963
. These NMR observations highlight the sensitivity of spin dynamics to pairing symmetry.

However, theoretical analyses often rely on perturbation theory, treating external fields as small disturbances to derive linear response properties. In NMR experiments, however, the required magnetic fields are typically strong, with energy scales comparable to or larger than the superconducting gap. This necessitates recalculating the ground state under the field’s influence rather than approximating it as a perturbation, ensuring a more accurate assessment of the system’s responses.

Complicating this picture, the spin-orbit interaction, often present due to broken inversion symmetry in crystals, further modifies the superconducting ground state. This interaction, exemplified by the Dresselhaus or Rashba effects
Dresselhaus 1955
;
Rashba 1960
, arises from structural asymmetries and can be modeled within symmetry-based band theory
Dresselhaus et al. 2007
. Using
\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{p}
perturbation theory, the spin-orbit coupling (SOC) is typically expressed as
H_{\text{SO}}=\mathbf{g}_{\mathbf{k}}\cdot\hat{\sigma}
, with
\mathbf{g}_{\mathbf{k}}
containing odd-powered momentum terms. In this work, we adopt a simplified form,
H_{\text{SO}}=g\mathbf{k}\cdot\hat{\sigma}
, which has been shown to capture essential effects
Bauer and Sigrist 2012
;
Yip 2014
;
Samokhin 2015
;
Smidman et al. 2017
.

Building on early experimental and theoretical insights, the interplay between magnetic fields and spin-orbit coupling has been a focal point since the late 1950s. NMR studies on BCS superconductors revealed minimal changes in the Knight shift for heavy elements like Hg
Reif 1957
and Sn
Androes and Knight 1959
, prompting key theoretical works by Ferrell
Ferrell 1959
, Martin and Kadanoff
Martin and Kadanoff 1959
, Schrieffer
Schrieffer 1959
, and Anderson
Anderson 1959a
. Anderson attributed these observations to strong spin-orbit scattering disrupting spin conservation
Anderson 1959a
;
Anderson 1959b
. Subsequent research, including Appel’s analysis of SOC in non-transition metals
Appel 1965
, Shiba’s work on surface effects
Shiba 1976
, and studies on magnetic field influences
Zhogolev and Glasser 1972
, has expanded this understanding. More recently, attention has shifted to superconductors with broken inversion symmetry
Frigeri et al. 2004a
;
Frigeri et al. 2004b
;
Samokhin 2007
;
Edelstein 2008
;
Zhou et al. 2017
;
Yang et al. 2021
.

Motivated by recent experimental observations in a new family of superconductors, A
2
Cr
3
As
3
(A = Na, K, Rb, and Cs)
Bao et al. 2015
;
Tang et al. 2015a
;
Tang et al. 2015b
;
Mu et al. 2018
;
Yang et al. 2021
, the current authors have theoretically investigated the effects of an external Zeeman field and Rashba spin-orbit interactions on superconductivity, particularly their influence on the superconducting transition temperature and quasiparticle excitations
Pang and Zhou 2025
. In this context, the present paper provides a comprehensive examination of how a Zeeman magnetic field and/or Rashba SOC affect spin susceptibility in both
s
-wave and
p
-wave superconductors.

The remainder of the paper is structured as follows. Section II presents the model Hamiltonian and the Kubo formula for spin susceptibility, along with a brief discussion of results and interpretations. Section III examines
s
-wave spin-singlet pairing superconductors, with a focus on spin susceptibility. Section IV explores various
p
-wave spin-triplet pairing superconductors, highlighting their distinct responses to the Zeeman field and/or Rashba SOC. Section V provides a summary and conclusions.

II
Theoretical formulation

As an extension of our previous work
Pang and Zhou 2025
, we employ a standard mean-field Hamiltonian to describe a superconducting system. This Hamiltonian comprises an unpaired component
H_{0}
and a paired component
H_{\text{SC}}
, and is expressed as:

H=H_{0}+H_{\text{SC}}.

(1)

The unpaired part
H_{0}
is given by:

H_{0}=\sum_{\mathbf{k},\alpha,\beta}c_{\mathbf{k},\alpha}^{\dagger}[H_{0}(\mathbf{k})]_{\alpha\beta}c_{\mathbf{k},\beta},

(2)

where
\alpha,\beta=\uparrow,\downarrow
represent spin indices,
c_{\mathbf{k},\alpha}^{\dagger}
(
c_{\mathbf{k},\alpha}
) denotes the creation (annihilation) operator for an electron with wave vector
\mathbf{k}
and spin
\alpha
, and
H_{0}(\mathbf{k})
is a
2\times 2
Hermitian matrix.

The paired part
H_{\text{SC}}
, which accounts for the superconducting pairing, takes the form:

H_{\text{SC}}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\mathbf{k},\alpha,\beta}\left[c_{\mathbf{k},\alpha}^{\dagger}\Delta_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf{k})c_{-\mathbf{k},\beta}^{\dagger}+\text{h.c.}\right].

(3)

Here, the pairing function
\Delta(\mathbf{k})
is a
2\times 2
matrix characterized by an isotropic component
\Delta
and a d-vector
\mathbf{d}(\mathbf{k})
, and is defined as:

\Delta(\mathbf{k})=i\left[\Delta\sigma_{0}+\mathbf{d}(\mathbf{k})\cdot\hat{\sigma}\right]\sigma_{y},

(4)

where
\sigma_{0}
is the identity matrix, and
\hat{\sigma}
is a vector comprising the three Pauli matrices
Balian and Werthamer 1963
.

In the normal state, the unpaired Hamiltonian
H_{0}
governs the system, incorporating Rashba spin-orbit coupling with strength
g
and an external Zeeman field
\mathbf{H}
, and reads

H_{0}(\mathbf{k})=\xi_{\mathbf{k}}\sigma_{0}+\mu_{B}\mathbf{H}\cdot\hat{\sigma}+g\mathbf{k}\cdot\hat{\sigma},

(5)

where
\xi_{\mathbf{k}}
is the electron energy relative to the chemical potential
\mu
, and
\mu_{B}
is the Bohr magneton.
The eigenvalues of
H_{0}(\mathbf{k})
are given by:

\xi_{\mathbf{k}\pm}=\xi_{\mathbf{k}}\pm\left|\mu_{B}\mathbf{H}+g\mathbf{k}\right|,

(6a)

with the corresponding eigenstates expressed as:

\left|\mathbf{k},+\right\rangle=\left|\mathbf{k}\right\rangle\otimes\begin{pmatrix}\cos\frac{\Theta_{\mathbf{k}}}{2}\\
\sin\frac{\Theta_{\mathbf{k}}}{2}\mathrm{e}^{i\Phi_{\mathbf{k}}}\end{pmatrix},\quad\left|\mathbf{k},-\right\rangle=\left|\mathbf{k}\right\rangle\otimes\begin{pmatrix}\sin\frac{\Theta_{\mathbf{k}}}{2}\\
-\cos\frac{\Theta_{\mathbf{k}}}{2}\mathrm{e}^{i\Phi_{\mathbf{k}}}\end{pmatrix}.

(6b)

The angles
\Theta_{\mathbf{k}}
and
\Phi_{\mathbf{k}}
, which define the direction of the vector
\mu_{B}\mathbf{H}+g\mathbf{k}
, are specified as:

\frac{\mu_{B}\mathbf{H}+g\mathbf{k}}{\left|\mu_{B}\mathbf{H}+g\mathbf{k}\right|}=(\sin\Theta_{\mathbf{k}}\cos\Phi_{\mathbf{k}},\sin\Theta_{\mathbf{k}}\sin\Phi_{\mathbf{k}},\cos\Theta_{\mathbf{k}}).

(7)

Explicitly, these angles are given by:

\begin{split}\cos\Theta_{\mathbf{k}}&=\frac{\mu_{B}H_{z}+g|\mathbf{k}|\cos\theta_{\mathbf{k}}}{\left|\mu_{B}\mathbf{H}+g\mathbf{k}\right|},\\
\tan\Phi_{\mathbf{k}}&=\frac{\mu_{B}H_{y}+g|\mathbf{k}|\sin\theta_{\mathbf{k}}\sin\varphi_{\mathbf{k}}}{\mu_{B}H_{x}+g|\mathbf{k}|\sin\theta_{\mathbf{k}}\cos\varphi_{\mathbf{k}}},\end{split}

(8)

where
\theta_{\mathbf{k}}
and
\varphi_{\mathbf{k}}
are the polar and azimuthal angles of the wave vector
\mathbf{k}
, respectively
Pang and Zhou 2025
.

II.1
Pairing interaction and self-consistent equation

The formation of Cooper pairs in a superconductor is driven by a four-fermion interaction, described by the Hamiltonian:

H_{\text{int}}=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}^{\prime},\alpha,\beta,\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}}V_{\beta\alpha\alpha^{\prime}\beta^{\prime}}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}^{\prime})c_{\mathbf{k},\alpha}^{\dagger}c_{-\mathbf{k},\beta}^{\dagger}c_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime}}c_{-\mathbf{k}^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}},

(9)

where
V_{\beta\alpha\alpha^{\prime}\beta^{\prime}}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}^{\prime})
is the pairing potential matrix element, and
\alpha,\beta,\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}=\uparrow,\downarrow
are spin indices.

Assuming a spherical Fermi surface and rotational symmetry, the matrix element
V(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}^{\prime})
can be expanded as
Leggett 1975
;
Sigrist and Ueda 1991
:

V(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}^{\prime})=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}(2l+1)V_{l}P_{l}\left(\cos\theta_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}\right),

(10)

where
\theta_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}
is the angle between the wave vectors
\mathbf{k}
and
\mathbf{k}^{\prime}
, and
P_{l}(x)
are Legendre polynomials. A positive
V_{l}
indicates an attractive interaction; specifically, the
l=0
channel with
V_{0}>0
corresponds to
s
-wave pairing, while the
l=1
channel with
V_{1}>0
leads to
p
-wave pairing.

Our model is defined by the pairing potential
V(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}^{\prime})
or its channel-specific parameters
V_{l}
from Eq. (
10
). Applying a mean-field decomposition to the interaction
H_{\text{int}}
in the pairing channel yields the superconducting Hamiltonian
H_{\text{SC}}
as given in Eq. (
3
). The pairing function
\Delta(\mathbf{k})
is then determined self-consistently using the following equation
Sigrist and Ueda 1991
:

\Delta_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf{k})=-\sum_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}}V_{\beta\alpha\alpha^{\prime}\beta^{\prime}}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\langle c_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime}}c_{-\mathbf{k}^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}}\rangle.

(11)

To solve this, we employ the Bogoliubov transformation, detailed in Appendix
A
, which is expressed as:

c_{\mathbf{k},\alpha}=\sum_{s=\pm}\left(u^{\alpha s}_{\mathbf{k}}\psi_{\mathbf{k},s}+v_{\mathbf{k}}^{\alpha s}\psi_{-\mathbf{k},s}^{\dagger}\right).

(12)

This transformation diagonalizes the mean-field Hamiltonian
H
from Eq. (
1
), allowing us to iteratively solve Eq. (
11
). Here,
\psi_{\mathbf{k},s}
and
\psi_{\mathbf{k},s}^{\dagger}
are Bogoliubov quasiparticle operators, with
s=\pm
denoting the quasiparticle band index. This index differs from that in the normal state (as defined in Eq. (
6b
)). Additionally, the quasiparticle energy in the superconducting state is denoted as
E_{\mathbf{k}\pm}
, to distinguish it from the normal state energy in Eq. (
6a
).

II.2
Spin susceptibility in superconducting states

After self-consistently solving for the pairing function
\Delta(\mathbf{k})
from Eq. (
11
), the temperature-dependent spin susceptibility
\chi_{\mu\nu}(T)
is calculated using the Kubo formula, with details provided in Appendix
B
. For clarity,
\chi_{\mu\nu}
is decomposed into different channels:

\chi_{\mu\nu}=\chi_{\mu\nu}^{ph}+\chi_{\mu\nu}^{pp},

(13a)

where
\chi_{\mu\nu}^{ph}
and
\chi_{\mu\nu}^{pp}
represent contributions from particle-hole (
ph
) and particle-particle/hole-hole (
pp
) processes, respectively. These can be further broken down as

\chi_{\mu\nu}^{ph}=\chi_{\mu\nu}^{ph+}+\chi_{\mu\nu}^{ph-}\quad\text{and}\quad\chi_{\mu\nu}^{pp}=\chi_{\mu\nu}^{pp+}+\chi_{\mu\nu}^{pp-},

(13b)

with the superscripts
+
and
-
indicating intraband and interband contributions.

Then we focus on the diagonal components, which are explicitly given by:

\displaystyle\chi_{\mu\mu}^{ph+}(T)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mu_{B}^{2}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\sum_{s=\pm}\left[(u_{\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}\sigma_{\mu}u_{\mathbf{k}})^{ss}-(v_{-\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}\sigma_{\mu}v_{-\mathbf{k}})^{ss}\right]^{2}\frac{df(E_{\mathbf{k}s})}{dE_{\mathbf{k}s}},

(13c)

\displaystyle\chi_{\mu\mu}^{ph-}(T)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\mu_{B}^{2}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\left|(u_{\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}\sigma_{\mu}u_{\mathbf{k}})^{+-}-(v_{-\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}\sigma_{\mu}v_{-\mathbf{k}})^{-+}\right|^{2}\frac{f(E_{\mathbf{k}+})-f(E_{\mathbf{k}-})}{E_{\mathbf{k}+}-E_{\mathbf{k}-}},

(13d)

\displaystyle\chi_{\mu\mu}^{pp+}(T)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mu_{B}^{2}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\sum_{s=\pm}\left|(u_{\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}\sigma_{\mu}v_{\mathbf{k}})^{ss}-(u_{-\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}\sigma_{\mu}v_{-\mathbf{k}})^{ss}\right|^{2}\frac{f(E_{\mathbf{k}s})+f(E_{-\mathbf{k}s})-1}{E_{\mathbf{k}s}+E_{-\mathbf{k}s}},

(13e)

\displaystyle\chi_{\mu\mu}^{pp-}(T)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mu_{B}^{2}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\sum_{s=\pm}\left|(u_{\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}\sigma_{\mu}v_{\mathbf{k}})^{s\bar{s}}-(u_{-\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}\sigma_{\mu}v_{-\mathbf{k}})^{\bar{s}s}\right|^{2}\frac{f(E_{\mathbf{k}s})+f(E_{-\mathbf{k}\bar{s}})-1}{E_{\mathbf{k}s}+E_{-\mathbf{k}\bar{s}}},

(13f)

where
f(E)
is the Fermi-Dirac distribution function, and
u_{\mathbf{k}}
and
v_{\mathbf{k}}
are Bogoliubov transformation matrices.
All the four components,
\chi_{\mu\mu}^{ph(pp)+(-)}
, are semi-positive definite, as
df(E)/dE<0
and
f(0)=1/2
. Additionally, the coefficients satisfy a sum rule:

\begin{split}\sum_{s_{1},s_{2}}&\left[\left|(u_{\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}\sigma_{\mu}u_{\mathbf{k}})^{s_{1}s_{2}}-(v_{-\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}\sigma_{\mu}v_{-\mathbf{k}})^{s_{2}s_{1}}\right|^{2}\right.\\
&\left.+\left|(u_{\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}\sigma_{\mu}v_{\mathbf{k}})^{s_{1}s_{2}}-(u_{-\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}\sigma_{\mu}v_{-\mathbf{k}})^{s_{2}s_{1}}\right|^{2}\right]=2,\end{split}

(14)

with the derivation provided in Appendix
A
.

\displaystyle=0,

To ensure a robust framework, we restrict our analysis to unitary states, where

\displaystyle E_{\mathbf{k}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\xi_{\mathbf{k}}^{2}+|\mathbf{d}(\mathbf{k})|^{2}}.

For unitary states,

When both a Zeeman field and Rashba SOC are present, deriving an analytical expression for the quasiparticle energy dispersion is highly challenging and often not feasible. In contrast, analytical solutions can be obtained when only one of these fields is applied

Moving to the classification of pairing states,

-wave and spin-triplet superconductors are divided into opposite-spin pairing (OSP) and equal-spin pairing (ESP) states

. This categorization depends on the spin quantization axis and can vary accordingly. For clarity, details are provided in Ref.
Pang and Zhou 2025
. In this work, unless otherwise stated, the spin quantization axis is set along the
z
-axis, either in the absence of an external magnetic field or aligned with the Zeeman field direction.

In OSP states, the superconducting condensate minimally responds to a Zeeman field along the quantization axis
\hat{\alpha}
(e.g.,
\hat{x}
,
\hat{y}
, or
\hat{z}
), avoiding Zeeman energy loss. Thus, only quasiparticle excitations contribute to magnetization within the Pauli limit, resulting in a reduced spin susceptibility
\chi_{\alpha\alpha}(T)
below
T_{c}
. In ESP states, however, the condensate behaves similarly to the normal state under a Zeeman field along
\hat{\alpha}
, maintaining a constant spin susceptibility
\chi_{\alpha\alpha}(T)=\chi_{N}
across
T_{c}

Leggett 1975
.

\displaystyle Y(T)

\displaystyle=\int\frac{d\Omega_{\mathbf{k}}}{4\pi}\int_{-\Lambda}^{\Lambda}d\xi_{\mathbf{k}}\frac{\beta}{4}\left(\cosh\frac{\beta E_{\mathbf{k}}}{2}\right)^{-2},

(27)

where the integrand depends on
\theta_{\mathbf{k}}

In contrast to the

V
Summary and Discussions

In this work, we have carried out a comprehensive study of the static spin susceptibility of superconductors that experience both a Zeeman magnetic field and Rashba-type SOC. The analysis, valid for arbitrary field strength and SOC magnitude, covers isotropic
s
-wave spin-singlet as well as several representative
p
-wave spin-triplet pairing states, and may be viewed as a non-perturbative extension of classical linear-response treatments.

General Framework.

Starting from a single-band Bogoliubov-de Gennes Hamiltonian [Eq. (
1
)] that contains the Zeeman term
\mu_{B}\mathbf{H}\cdot\hat{\sigma}
and the Rashba term
g\,\mathbf{k}\cdot\hat{\sigma}
, we derived (i) self-consistent gap equations for arbitrary pairing channels and (ii) a compact Kubo expression for uniform susceptibility, decomposed into intra-/inter-band particle-hole and particle-particle contributions [Eqs. (
13
)]. Two universal results follow:
(i) only particle-particle channels survive at
T=0
, and (ii) only particle-hole channels survive at
T=T_{c}
, so that
\chi(T_{c}^{-})=\chi(T_{c}^{+})=\chi_{N}
for a continuous superconducting transition. These identities are indifferent to the pairing symmetry and constitute useful benchmarks for future numerical or experimental work.

s
-wave and Spin-singlet Pairing.

For conventional BCS pairing, we recover the Yosida temperature suppression when both Zeeman field and Rashba SOC are absent. A pure Zeeman field lowers
T_{c}
towards the Pauli limit and produces a first-order transition when
T_{c}/T_{c0}<0.556
[Fig.
2
], whereas pure SOC leaves
T_{c}
untouched but generates a residual susceptibility
\chi(0)\to 2\chi_{N}/3
in the strong SOC limit [Fig.
3
]. When both terms are present, the competition is highly non-trivial:
(i)
\chi_{zz}(T)
always decreases below
T_{c}
but never vanishes; (ii) for sufficiently large
g
a Bogoliubov Fermi surface appears between two Zeeman critical fields, producing kinks in
\chi_{zz}(0)
[Fig.
5
].

p

The spin-triplet pairing states divide naturally into OSP and ESP classes (Table

•

Our results offer valuable predictions for the Knight shift in non-centrosymmetric and strongly anisotropic superconductors, where Zeeman energies and SOC splittings are comparable to the superconducting gap. Outlook.

The current single-band, unitary framework offers a foundation for broader applications and can be extended to multi-orbital materials and non-unitary triplet states, where intrinsic breaking of time-reversal symmetry plays a key role. Additionally, incorporating Fermi surface anisotropy would enable the study of quasi-one-dimensional or quasi-two-dimensional systems, capturing more realistic material properties. Furthermore, extending the theory to finite wave-vector susceptibility could facilitate a microscopic analysis of NMR relaxation rates and collective modes under strong-field and strong-SOC conditions. Such advances hold promise for deepening our understanding of the intricate interplay between symmetry, topology, and correlations in unconventional superconductors.

Appendix A
Bogoliubov Transformation and the Sum Rule in Eq. (
14
)

The Hamiltonian for a superconducting system can be expressed as:

H=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}C^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}}H(\mathbf{k})C_{\mathbf{k}},

(33)

where
C_{\mathbf{k}}=(c_{\mathbf{k}\uparrow},c_{\mathbf{k}\downarrow},c_{-\mathbf{k}\uparrow}^{\dagger},c_{-\mathbf{k}\downarrow}^{\dagger})^{T}
, and

H(\mathbf{k})=\begin{pmatrix}H_{0}(\mathbf{k})&\Delta(\mathbf{k})\\
\Delta^{\dagger}(\mathbf{k})&-H_{0}(-\mathbf{k})^{T}\end{pmatrix}.

(34)

To diagonalize this Hamiltonian, we introduce the Bogoliubov-transformed operators
\Psi_{\mathbf{k}}=(\psi_{\mathbf{k}+},\psi_{\mathbf{k}-},\psi_{-\mathbf{k}+}^{\dagger},\psi_{-\mathbf{k}-}^{\dagger})^{T}
, related to the original operators by
C_{\mathbf{k}}=U_{\mathbf{k}}\Psi_{\mathbf{k}}
, with the transformation matrix

U_{\mathbf{k}}=\begin{pmatrix}u_{\mathbf{k}}&v_{\mathbf{k}}\\
v_{-\mathbf{k}}^{*}&u_{-\mathbf{k}}^{*}\end{pmatrix}.

(35)

This transformation diagonalizes the Hamiltonian, yielding

U_{\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}H(\mathbf{k})U_{\mathbf{k}}=\mbox{diag}(E_{\mathbf{k}+},E_{\mathbf{k}-},-E_{-\mathbf{k}+},-E_{-\mathbf{k}-}).

(36)

Since
U_{\mathbf{k}}
is unitary, it satisfies
U_{\mathbf{k}}U_{\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}=U_{\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}U_{\mathbf{k}}=\mathbb{I}
, which implies the following conditions:

\displaystyle u_{\mathbf{k}}u_{\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}+v_{\mathbf{k}}v_{\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{I},

(37)

\displaystyle u_{\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}u_{\mathbf{k}}+v_{-\mathbf{k}}^{T}v_{-\mathbf{k}}^{*}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{I},

\displaystyle u_{\mathbf{k}}v_{-\mathbf{k}}^{T}+v_{\mathbf{k}}u_{-\mathbf{k}}^{T}

\displaystyle=0.

Using these properties, the sum rule in Eq. (
14
) can be verified as follows:

\displaystyle\sum_{s_{1},s_{2}}\left|(u_{\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}\sigma_{\mu}u_{\mathbf{k}})^{s_{1}s_{2}}-(v_{-\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}\sigma_{\mu}v_{-\mathbf{k}})^{s_{2}s_{1}}\right|^{2}+\left|(u_{\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}\sigma_{\mu}v_{\mathbf{k}})^{s_{1}s_{2}}-(u_{-\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}\sigma_{\mu}v_{-\mathbf{k}})^{s_{2}s_{1}}\right|^{2}

(38)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s_{1},s_{2}}\left[u_{\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}\sigma_{\mu}u_{\mathbf{k}}-(v_{-\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}\sigma_{\mu}v_{-\mathbf{k}})^{T}\right]^{s_{1}s_{2}}\left[u_{\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}\sigma_{\mu}u_{\mathbf{k}}-(v_{-\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}\sigma_{\mu}v_{-\mathbf{k}})^{T}\right]^{s_{2}s_{1}}+\left[u_{\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}\sigma_{\mu}v_{\mathbf{k}}-(u_{-\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}\sigma_{\mu}v_{-\mathbf{k}})^{T}\right]^{s_{1}s_{2}}\left[v_{\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}\sigma_{\mu}u_{\mathbf{k}}-(v_{-\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}\sigma_{\mu}u_{-\mathbf{k}})^{T}\right]^{s_{2}s_{1}}

\displaystyle=\mbox{tr}(u_{\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}\sigma_{\mu}u_{\mathbf{k}}u_{\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}\sigma_{\mu}u_{\mathbf{k}})+\mbox{tr}(v_{-\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}\sigma_{\mu}v_{-\mathbf{k}}v_{-\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}\sigma_{\mu}v_{-\mathbf{k}})-2\mbox{tr}(u_{\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}\sigma_{\mu}u_{\mathbf{k}}v_{-\mathbf{k}}^{T}\sigma_{\mu}^{*}v_{-\mathbf{k}}^{*})+\mbox{tr}(u_{\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}\sigma_{\mu}v_{\mathbf{k}}v_{\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}\sigma_{\mu}u_{\mathbf{k}})+\mbox{tr}(u_{-\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}\sigma_{\mu}v_{-\mathbf{k}}v_{-\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}\sigma_{\mu}u_{-\mathbf{k}})

\displaystyle-2\mbox{tr}(u_{\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}\sigma_{\mu}v_{\mathbf{k}}u_{-\mathbf{k}}^{T}\sigma_{\mu}^{*}v_{-\mathbf{k}}^{*})

\displaystyle=\mbox{tr}[u_{\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}\sigma_{\mu}(u_{\mathbf{k}}u_{\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}+v_{\mathbf{k}}v_{\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger})\sigma_{\mu}u_{\mathbf{k}}]-2\mbox{tr}[u_{\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}\sigma_{\mu}(u_{\mathbf{k}}v_{-\mathbf{k}}^{T}+v_{\mathbf{k}}u_{-\mathbf{k}}^{T})\sigma_{\mu}^{*}v_{-\mathbf{k}}^{*}]+\mbox{tr}[v_{-\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}\sigma_{\mu}(\mathbb{I}-u_{-\mathbf{k}}u_{-\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger})\sigma_{\mu}v_{-\mathbf{k}}]+\mbox{tr}(u_{-\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}\sigma_{\mu}v_{-\mathbf{k}}v_{-\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}\sigma_{\mu}u_{-\mathbf{k}})

\displaystyle=\mbox{tr}(u_{\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}u_{\mathbf{k}})+\mbox{tr}(v_{-\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}v_{-\mathbf{k}})

\displaystyle=\mbox{tr}(u_{\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}u_{\mathbf{k}}+v_{-\mathbf{k}}^{T}v_{-\mathbf{k}}^{*})=2.

Here, we utilize the fact that
v_{-\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}v_{-\mathbf{k}}
is Hermitian, so its diagonal elements are real, and
\mbox{tr}(v_{-\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}v_{-\mathbf{k}})=[\mbox{tr}(v_{-\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}v_{-\mathbf{k}})]^{*}=\mbox{tr}(v_{-\mathbf{k}}^{T}v_{-\mathbf{k}}^{*})
.

Appendix B
Kubo Formula for Spin Susceptibility

By solving the gap equation in Eq. (
11
), the pairing function
\Delta(\mathbf{k})
is determined. The spin susceptibility, measurable via the NMR Knight shift, is then calculated using the Kubo formula:

\begin{split}\chi(\mathbf{q},i\omega_{l})&=\int_{0}^{\beta}d\tau\,e^{i\omega_{l}\tau}\langle T_{\tau}\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{q},\tau)\mathbf{M}(-\mathbf{q},0)\rangle,\\
\chi(\mathbf{q},\omega)&=\chi(\mathbf{q},i\omega_{l}\to\omega+i0^{+}),\end{split}

(39)

where the magnetization operator is given by

\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{q})=-\mu_{B}\sum_{\mathbf{k},\alpha,\beta}\hat{\sigma}^{\alpha\beta}c_{\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q},\alpha}^{\dagger}c_{\mathbf{k},\beta}.

(40)

In tensor form, the spin susceptibility is expressed as

\chi_{\mu\nu}(\mathbf{q},i\omega_{l})=\frac{1}{Z}\sum_{n,m}\frac{\langle n|M_{\mu}(\mathbf{q})|m\rangle\langle m|M_{\nu}(-\mathbf{q})|n\rangle(\mathrm{e}^{-\beta E_{m}}-\mathrm{e}^{-\beta E_{n}})}{i\omega_{l}+E_{n}-E_{m}},

(41)

with
Z
as the partition function,
\beta=1/k_{B}T
the inverse temperature,
\mu,\nu=x,y,z
, and
|n\rangle
,
|m\rangle
the many-body eigenstates with energies
E_{n}
and
E_{m}
.

Substituting Eq. (
40
) into Eq. (
41
) and performing the analytic continuation
i\omega_{l}\to\omega+i0^{+}
, the static uniform susceptibility
\chi_{\mu\nu}\equiv\chi_{\mu\nu}(\mathbf{q}\to 0,\omega=0)
becomes:

\begin{split}\chi_{\mu\nu}&=\frac{\mu_{B}^{2}}{Z}\lim_{\mathbf{q}\to 0}\sum_{\alpha,\beta,\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}}\sigma_{\mu}^{\alpha\beta}\sigma_{\nu}^{\alpha^{\prime}\beta^{\prime}}\sum_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}\sum_{n,m}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\beta E_{m}}-\mathrm{e}^{-\beta E_{n}}}{E_{n}-E_{m}+i0^{+}}\\
&\quad\times\langle n|c_{\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q},\alpha}^{\dagger}c_{\mathbf{k},\beta}|m\rangle\langle m|c_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}+\mathbf{q},\alpha^{\prime}}^{\dagger}c_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}}|n\rangle.\end{split}

(42)

For a uniform superconducting state with zero center-of-mass momentum for Cooper pairs, the matrix element
\langle n|c_{\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q},\alpha}^{\dagger}c_{\mathbf{k},\beta}|m\rangle\langle m|c_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}+\mathbf{q},\alpha^{\prime}}^{\dagger}c_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}}|n\rangle
is nonzero only if
\mathbf{k}^{\prime}=\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}
or
\mathbf{k}^{\prime}=-\mathbf{k}
. The eigenstates
|n\rangle
and
|m\rangle
are quasiparticle states over the superconducting ground state, enforcing energy conservation as
E_{n}-E_{m}=\pm E_{\mathbf{k}\pm}\pm E_{\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}\pm}
. Thus,
\chi_{\mu\nu}
decomposes as:

\chi_{\mu\nu}=\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\chi_{\mu\nu}(\mathbf{k}),

(43a)

with the
\mathbf{k}
-dependent component:

\begin{split}\chi_{\mu\nu}(\mathbf{k})&=\frac{\mu_{B}^{2}}{Z}\lim_{\mathbf{q}\to 0}\sum_{\alpha,\beta,\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}}\sigma_{\mu}^{\alpha\beta}\sigma_{\nu}^{\alpha^{\prime}\beta^{\prime}}\sum_{n,m}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\beta E_{m}}-\mathrm{e}^{-\beta E_{n}}}{E_{n}-E_{m}+i0^{+}}\\
&\quad\times\langle n|c_{\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q},\alpha}^{\dagger}c_{\mathbf{k},\beta}|m\rangle\langle m|(c_{\mathbf{k},\alpha^{\prime}}^{\dagger}c_{\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q},\beta^{\prime}}+c_{-\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q},\alpha^{\prime}}^{\dagger}c_{-\mathbf{k},\beta^{\prime}})|n\rangle.\end{split}

(43b)

Using the Bogoliubov transformation from Eq. (
12
),
\chi_{\mu\nu}(\mathbf{k})
is expressed as:

\chi_{\mu\nu}(\mathbf{k})=\chi_{\mu\nu}^{ph}(\mathbf{k})+\chi_{\mu\nu}^{pp}(\mathbf{k}),

(44a)

where
\chi_{\mu\nu}^{ph}
and
\chi_{\mu\nu}^{pp}
correspond to particle-hole and particle-particle/hole-hole processes, respectively. The particle-hole component further splits into:

\chi_{\mu\nu}^{ph}(\mathbf{k})=\chi_{\mu\nu}^{ph+}(\mathbf{k})+\chi_{\mu\nu}^{ph-}(\mathbf{k}),

(44b)

with
\chi_{\mu\nu}^{ph+}
and
\chi_{\mu\nu}^{ph-}
representing intraband and interband contributions.

This work focuses on the diagonal components (
\mu=\nu
), analyzing
\chi_{\mu\mu}^{ph+}
,
\chi_{\mu\mu}^{ph-}
, and
\chi_{\mu\mu}^{pp}
separately. The matrix elements in Eq. (
43
) are rewritten using the Bogoliubov transformation:

\displaystyle\langle n|c_{\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q},\alpha}^{\dagger}c_{\mathbf{k},\beta}|m\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{s_{1},s_{2}}\langle n|(u_{\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}}^{\alpha s_{1}*}\psi_{\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q},s_{1}}^{\dagger}+v_{\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}}^{\alpha s_{1}*}\psi_{\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{k},s_{1}})(u_{\mathbf{k}}^{\beta s_{2}}\psi_{\mathbf{k},s_{2}}+v_{\mathbf{k}}^{\beta s_{2}}\psi_{-\mathbf{k},s_{2}}^{\dagger})|m\rangle,

(45)

\displaystyle\langle m|c_{\mathbf{k},\alpha^{\prime}}^{\dagger}c_{\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q},\beta^{\prime}}|n\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{s_{3},s_{4}}\langle m|(u_{\mathbf{k}}^{\alpha^{\prime}s_{3}*}\psi_{\mathbf{k},s_{3}}^{\dagger}+v_{\mathbf{k}}^{\alpha^{\prime}s_{3}*}\psi_{-\mathbf{k},s_{3}})(u_{\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}}^{\beta^{\prime}s_{4}}\psi_{\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q},s_{4}}+v_{\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}}^{\beta^{\prime}s_{4}}\psi_{\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{k},s_{4}}^{\dagger})|n\rangle,

\displaystyle\langle m|c_{-\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q},\alpha^{\prime}}^{\dagger}c_{-\mathbf{k},\beta^{\prime}}|n\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{s_{3},s_{4}}\langle m|(u_{-\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}^{\alpha^{\prime}s_{3}*}\psi_{-\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q},s_{3}}^{\dagger}+v_{-\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}^{\alpha^{\prime}s_{3}*}\psi_{\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q},s_{3}})(u_{-\mathbf{k}}^{\beta^{\prime}s_{4}}\psi_{-\mathbf{k},s_{4}}+v_{-\mathbf{k}}^{\beta^{\prime}s_{4}}\psi_{\mathbf{k},s_{4}}^{\dagger})|n\rangle.

Nonzero contributions to
\chi_{\mu\mu}^{ph+}(\mathbf{k})
and
\chi_{\mu\mu}^{ph-}(\mathbf{k})
arise from states like
|m\rangle=\psi^{\dagger}\psi^{\prime}|n\rangle
and
|m\rangle=\psi\psi^{\prime\dagger}|n\rangle
, while
\chi_{\mu\mu}^{pp}(\mathbf{k})
involves
|m\rangle=\psi^{\dagger}\psi^{\prime\dagger}|n\rangle

C.1
A Single Zeeman Field

For an
s
-wave superconductor subjected to a single Zeeman field, the Hamiltonian is given by:

H(\mathbf{k})=\begin{pmatrix}\xi_{\mathbf{k}+}&0&0&\Delta\\
0&\xi_{\mathbf{k}-}&-\Delta&0\\
0&-\Delta^{*}&-\xi_{\mathbf{k}+}&0\\
\Delta^{*}&0&0&-\xi_{\mathbf{k}-}\end{pmatrix},

(54)

where
\xi_{\mathbf{k}\pm}=\xi_{\mathbf{k}}\pm\mu_{B}H_{z}
. The corresponding
4\times 4
Bogoliubov transformation matrix
U_{\mathbf{k}}
is:

\begin{pmatrix}\frac{E_{\mathbf{k}}+\xi_{\mathbf{k}}}{[2E_{\mathbf{k}}(E_{\mathbf{k}}+\xi_{\mathbf{k}})]^{1/2}}&0&0&\frac{-\Delta}{[2E_{\mathbf{k}}(E_{\mathbf{k}}+\xi_{\mathbf{k}})]^{1/2}}\\
0&\frac{E_{\mathbf{k}}+\xi_{\mathbf{k}}}{[2E_{\mathbf{k}}(E_{\mathbf{k}}+\xi_{\mathbf{k}})]^{1/2}}&\frac{\Delta}{[2E_{\mathbf{k}}(E_{\mathbf{k}}+\xi_{\mathbf{k}})]^{1/2}}&0\\
0&\frac{-\Delta^{*}}{[2E_{\mathbf{k}}(E_{\mathbf{k}}+\xi_{\mathbf{k}})]^{1/2}}&\frac{E_{\mathbf{k}}+\xi_{\mathbf{k}}}{[2E_{\mathbf{k}}(E_{\mathbf{k}}+\xi_{\mathbf{k}})]^{1/2}}&0\\
\frac{\Delta^{*}}{[2E_{\mathbf{k}}(E_{\mathbf{k}}+\xi_{\mathbf{k}})]^{1/2}}&0&0&\frac{E_{\mathbf{k}}+\xi_{\mathbf{k}}}{[2E_{\mathbf{k}}(E_{\mathbf{k}}+\xi_{\mathbf{k}})]^{1/2}}\end{pmatrix},

(55)

which remains identical to the case without a Zeeman field. Consequently, the factorial matrices in Eq. (
13
) are:

\displaystyle u_{\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}\sigma_{z}u_{\mathbf{k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\frac{\xi_{\mathbf{k}}}{E_{\mathbf{k}}}\right)\sigma_{z},

(56)

\displaystyle v_{-\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}\sigma_{z}v_{-\mathbf{k}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{\xi_{\mathbf{k}}}{E_{\mathbf{k}}}\right)\sigma_{z},

\displaystyle u_{\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}\sigma_{z}v_{\mathbf{k}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\Delta}{2E_{\mathbf{k}}}\sigma_{x},

\displaystyle u_{-\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}\sigma_{z}v_{-\mathbf{k}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\Delta}{2E_{\mathbf{k}}}\sigma_{x}.

This leads to the spin susceptibility components:

\displaystyle\chi_{zz}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\left[\chi_{zz}^{ph+}(\mathbf{k})+\chi_{zz}^{ph-}(\mathbf{k})+\chi_{zz}^{pp}(\mathbf{k})\right],

(57)

\displaystyle\chi_{zz}^{ph+}(\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle=-\mu_{B}^{2}\left[\left.\frac{df(E)}{dE}\right|_{E=E_{\mathbf{k}+}}+\left.\frac{df(E)}{dE}\right|_{E=E_{\mathbf{k}-}}\right],

\displaystyle\chi_{zz}^{ph-}(\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle=\chi_{zz}^{pp}(\mathbf{k})=0.

g

\displaystyle=\frac{\Delta}{[2E_{\mathbf{k}\pm}(\xi_{\mathbf{k}\pm}+E_{\mathbf{k}\pm})]^{1/2}}.

Appendix D
p
-wave Pairing States with Only Rashba SOC: Zero-Temperature Spin susceptibility

To facilitate the analysis, we introduce local coordinates in
\mathbf{k}
-space, defined by the unit vectors
\hat{n}_{1,2,3}(\mathbf{k})
as follows:

\begin{split}\hat{n}_{3}(\mathbf{k})&=\hat{\mathbf{k}}=(\sin\theta_{\mathbf{k}}\cos\varphi_{\mathbf{k}},\sin\theta_{\mathbf{k}}\sin\varphi_{\mathbf{k}},\cos\theta_{\mathbf{k}}),\\
\hat{n}_{2}(\mathbf{k})&=\frac{\hat{\mathbf{k}}\times\hat{z}}{|\hat{\mathbf{k}}\times\hat{z}|}=(\sin\varphi_{\mathbf{k}},-\cos\varphi_{\mathbf{k}},0),\\
\hat{n}_{1}(\mathbf{k})&=\hat{n}_{2}(\mathbf{k})\times\hat{n}_{3}(\mathbf{k})=(-\cos\theta_{\mathbf{k}}\cos\varphi_{\mathbf{k}},-\cos\theta_{\mathbf{k}}\sin\varphi_{\mathbf{k}},\sin\theta_{\mathbf{k}}).\end{split}

(63)

With Rashba SOC present, the quasiparticle energy can be expressed as:

E_{\mathbf{k}\pm}=\sqrt{(E_{\mathbf{k}\perp}\pm gk_{F})^{2}+|d_{3}(\mathbf{k})|^{2}},

(64)

where
E_{\mathbf{k}\perp}=\sqrt{\xi_{\mathbf{k}}^{2}+|\mathbf{d}_{\mathbf{k}}\times\hat{\mathbf{k}}|^{2}}
and
d_{3}(\mathbf{k})=\mathbf{d}_{\mathbf{k}}\cdot\hat{n}_{3}(\mathbf{k})
.

At zero temperature, for
E_{\mathbf{k}\pm}\geq 0
, the spin susceptibility
\chi_{\mu\mu}(T=0)
arises solely from particle-particle (and hole-hole) processes, given by:

\displaystyle\chi_{\mu\mu}(T=0)=\chi_{\mu\mu}^{pp}(T=0)

(65)

Appendix E
p
-wave Pairing States with Strong Rashba SOC: Zero-Temperature Spin Susceptibility

In this appendix, we demonstrate why the zero-temperature spin susceptibility components
\chi_{xx}(T=0)
and
\chi_{zz}(T=0)
approach
\frac{2}{3}\chi_{N}
in the strong Rashba SOC limit. When Rashba SOC is sufficiently strong, the superconducting transition temperature
T_{c}
and the gap function
\Delta(T=0)
are significantly suppressed. This allows us to approximate the energy dispersion by neglecting gap-induced corrections, leading to:

\displaystyle E_{\mathbf{k}\pm}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{[E_{\mathbf{k}\perp}\pm gk_{F}]^{2}+d_{3}^{2}(\mathbf{k})}\approx|\xi_{\mathbf{k}}\pm gk_{F}|.

(72)

Given that contributions to spin susceptibility primarily originate from wave vectors near the Fermi surface, we focus on the regime
-gk_{F}<\xi_{\mathbf{k}}<gk_{F}
, which becomes increasingly valid as SOC strength increases. Thus, the energy simplifies to:

\displaystyle E_{\mathbf{k}\pm}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}gk_{F}\pm\xi_{\mathbf{k}},&\xi_{\mathbf{k}}>0,\\
gk_{F}\mp\xi_{\mathbf{k}},&\xi_{\mathbf{k}}<0.\end{cases}

(73)

For
\xi_{\mathbf{k}}>0
, the coefficients are approximated as:

\displaystyle a_{\mathbf{k}1\pm}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{E_{\mathbf{k}\pm}+E_{\mathbf{k}\perp}\pm g|\mathbf{k}|}{E_{\mathbf{k}\pm}}}\approx\begin{cases}\sqrt{2},&+,\\
0,&-,\end{cases}

(74)

\displaystyle a_{\mathbf{k}2\pm}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{E_{\mathbf{k}\pm}-E_{\mathbf{k}\perp}\mp g|\mathbf{k}|}{E_{\mathbf{k}\pm}}}\approx\begin{cases}0,&+,\\
\sqrt{2},&-.\end{cases}

Substituting these into the expressions for
\chi_{xx}(T=0)
and
\chi_{zz}(T=0)
, we obtain:

\displaystyle\chi_{xx}(T=0)

\displaystyle=\mu_{B}^{2}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\frac{d_{12}^{2}(\mathbf{k})}{2E_{\mathbf{k}\perp}^{2}}(\sin\varphi_{\mathbf{k}}\cos\Omega+\cos\varphi_{\mathbf{k}}\sin\Omega\cos\theta_{\mathbf{k}})^{2}\left[\frac{1}{E_{\mathbf{k}+}}+\frac{1}{E_{\mathbf{k}-}}\right]

(75)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\left\{\left[s_{\mathbf{k}}(a_{\mathbf{k}1+}a_{\mathbf{k}2-}+a_{\mathbf{k}1-}a_{\mathbf{k}2+})(\sin\varphi_{\mathbf{k}}\sin\Omega_{\mathbf{k}}-\cos\theta_{\mathbf{k}}\cos\varphi_{\mathbf{k}}\cos\Omega_{\mathbf{k}})-(a_{\mathbf{k}1+}a_{\mathbf{k}1-}-a_{\mathbf{k}2+}a_{\mathbf{k}2-})\frac{d_{12}(\mathbf{k})}{E_{\mathbf{k}\perp}}\cos\varphi_{\mathbf{k}}\sin\theta_{\mathbf{k}}\right]^{2}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{\xi_{\mathbf{k}}^{2}}{E_{\mathbf{k}\perp}^{2}}(a_{\mathbf{k}1+}a_{\mathbf{k}2-}+a_{\mathbf{k}1-}a_{\mathbf{k}2+})^{2}(\sin\varphi_{\mathbf{k}}\cos\Omega_{\mathbf{k}}+\cos\theta_{\mathbf{k}}\cos\varphi_{\mathbf{k}}\sin\Omega_{\mathbf{k}})^{2}\right\}\frac{1}{E_{\mathbf{k}+}+E_{\mathbf{k}-}}

\displaystyle\approx\mu_{B}^{2}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\frac{1}{2}\left[4(\sin\varphi_{\mathbf{k}}\sin\Omega_{\mathbf{k}}-\cos\theta_{\mathbf{k}}\cos\varphi_{\mathbf{k}}\cos\Omega_{\mathbf{k}})^{2}+4(\sin\varphi_{\mathbf{k}}\cos\Omega_{\mathbf{k}}+\cos\theta_{\mathbf{k}}\cos\varphi_{\mathbf{k}}\sin\Omega_{\mathbf{k}})^{2}\right]\frac{1}{2gk_{F}}

\displaystyle=\mu_{B}^{2}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}(\sin^{2}\varphi_{\mathbf{k}}+\cos^{2}\theta_{\mathbf{k}}\cos^{2}\varphi_{\mathbf{k}})\frac{1}{gk_{F}}

\displaystyle\approx\frac{2}{3}\chi_{N},

and similarly for
\chi_{zz}(T=0)
:

\displaystyle\chi_{zz}(T=0)

\displaystyle=\mu_{B}^{2}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\frac{d_{12}^{2}(\mathbf{k})}{E_{\mathbf{k}\perp}^{2}}\sin^{2}\theta_{\mathbf{k}}\sin^{2}\Omega_{\mathbf{k}}\left[\frac{1}{E_{\mathbf{k}+}}+\frac{1}{E_{\mathbf{k}-}}\right]

(76)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\left\{\left[(a_{\mathbf{k}1+}a_{\mathbf{k}2-}+a_{\mathbf{k}1-}a_{\mathbf{k}2+})s_{\mathbf{k}}\cos\Omega_{\mathbf{k}}\sin\theta_{\mathbf{k}}-\frac{d_{12}(\mathbf{k})}{E_{\mathbf{k}\perp}}(a_{\mathbf{k}1+}a_{\mathbf{k}1-}-a_{\mathbf{k}2+}a_{\mathbf{k}2-})\cos\theta_{\mathbf{k}}\right]^{2}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{\xi_{\mathbf{k}}^{2}}{E_{\mathbf{k}\perp}^{2}}(a_{\mathbf{k}1+}a_{\mathbf{k}2-}+a_{\mathbf{k}1-}a_{\mathbf{k}2+})^{2}\sin^{2}\Omega_{\mathbf{k}}\sin^{2}\theta_{\mathbf{k}}\right\}\frac{1}{E_{\mathbf{k}+}+E_{\mathbf{k}-}}

\displaystyle\approx\mu_{B}^{2}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\frac{1}{2}\left[4\sin^{2}\theta_{\mathbf{k}}\cos^{2}\Omega_{\mathbf{k}}+4\sin^{2}\theta_{\mathbf{k}}\sin^{2}\Omega_{\mathbf{k}}\right]\frac{1}{2gk_{F}}

\displaystyle\approx\frac{2}{3}\chi_{N}.

Therefore, both
\chi_{zz}(T=0)
and
\chi_{xx}(T=0)
approach
\frac{2}{3}\chi_{N}
in the strong Rashba SOC limit. Given the significant suppression of the gap function (
\Delta(T=0,g)\ll\Delta(T=0,g=0)
), the intra-band term
\chi_{\mu\mu}^{pp+}(T=0)
can be neglected, as it scales with
\Delta^{2}(T=0,g)
according to Eqs. (
66
) and (
68
). Consequently,
\chi_{\mu\mu}(T=0)
is dominated by the inter-band term
\chi_{\mu\mu}^{pp-}
. Since this derivation is largely independent of the specific pairing symmetry, the result holds for general unitary
p
-wave pairing states.

OSP States with Only Rashba SOC:
\chi_{xx}(T=0)

(k_{x}+ik_{y})\hat{z}

k_{z}\hat{z}
pairing state. The solid lines represent zero-temperature spin susceptibility

.

For the
(k_{x}+ik_{y})\hat{z}

k_{z}\hat{z}
opposite-spin pairing (OSP) states, Fig.

illustrates the effects of Rashba SOC on the zero-temperature gap function
\Delta(T=0)
and spin susceptibility along the
x
-axis,
\chi_{xx}(T=0)
. The numerical results indicate that for small Rashba SOC strengths, the self-consistent gap function
\Delta(T=0,g)
remains largely unchanged, and the behavior of
\chi_{xx}(T=0)
is predominantly governed by the intra-band term
\chi_{xx}^{pp+}
. Building on the framework from Appendix
D
, the expression for
\chi_{xx}^{pp+}(T=0)
is given by:

\chi_{xx}^{pp+}(T=0)=\mu_{B}^{2}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\frac{d_{12}^{2}(\mathbf{k})}{2E_{\mathbf{k}\perp}^{2}}\left(\sin\varphi_{\mathbf{k}}\cos\Omega_{\mathbf{k}}+\cos\theta_{\mathbf{k}}\cos\varphi_{\mathbf{k}}\sin\Omega_{\mathbf{k}}\right)^{2}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{E_{\mathbf{k}+}}+\frac{1}{E_{\mathbf{k}-}}\right).

(77)

Under the assumption that
\Delta(T=0)
is approximately constant for weak Rashba SOC, the dependence of
\chi_{xx}^{pp+}(T=0)
on
g
stems solely from the factor
\frac{1}{E_{\mathbf{k}+}}+\frac{1}{E_{\mathbf{k}-}}
. To analyze this dependence, we compute the derivative with respect to
g|\mathbf{k}|
:

\frac{\partial}{\partial g|\mathbf{k}|}\left(\frac{1}{E_{\mathbf{k}+}}+\frac{1}{E_{\mathbf{k}-}}\right)=\frac{E_{\mathbf{k}\perp}-g|\mathbf{k}|}{E_{\mathbf{k}-}^{3}}-\frac{E_{\mathbf{k}\perp}+g|\mathbf{k}|}{E_{\mathbf{k}+}^{3}}.

(78)

Furthermore, the derivative of the related term is:

\frac{\partial}{\partial g|\mathbf{k}|}\frac{E_{\mathbf{k}\perp}+g|\mathbf{k}|}{E_{\mathbf{k}+}^{3}}=\frac{-3(E_{\mathbf{k}\perp}+g|\mathbf{k}|)^{2}+E_{\mathbf{k}+}^{2}}{E_{\mathbf{k}+}^{5}}.

(79)

Since this derivative assumes negative values for most wave vectors when
g|\mathbf{k}|
is small, the factor
\frac{1}{E_{\mathbf{k}+}}+\frac{1}{E_{\mathbf{k}-}}
increases as
g
grows from zero, resulting in an overall increase in spin susceptibility for weak Rashba SOC.

Appendix G
OSP States in a Perpendicular Zeeman Field: Temperature-Independent Spin Susceptibility Component

In this section, we analyze the temperature-independent behavior of the spin susceptibility component
\chi_{xx}(T)
for opposite-spin pairing (OSP) states under a perpendicular Zeeman field. Specifically, we focus on the
(k_{x}+ik_{y})\hat{z}
and
k_{z}\hat{z}
pairing states, demonstrating that
\chi_{xx}(T)=\chi_{N}
for all temperatures, as derived from the decomposition in Eq. (
13
), which yields
\chi_{xx}^{ph-}=0
and
\chi_{xx}^{ph+}+\chi_{xx}^{pp}=\chi_{N}
.

The numerical and theoretical effects are illustrated in Fig.
17
, which shows the influence of Rashba SOC on
\chi_{xx}(T=0)
and the gap function
\Delta(T=0)
. For a Zeeman field along the
x
-axis, the Bogoliubov transformation for a general OSP state is given by:

u_{\mathbf{k}}=\begin{pmatrix}a_{\mathbf{k}+}&a_{\mathbf{k}-}\\
a_{\mathbf{k}+}&-a_{\mathbf{k}-}\end{pmatrix},\quad v_{\mathbf{k}}=\begin{pmatrix}-b_{\mathbf{k}+}&b_{\mathbf{k}-}\\
-b_{\mathbf{k}+}&-b_{\mathbf{k}-}\end{pmatrix},

(80a)

where the coefficients are:

\displaystyle a_{\mathbf{k}\pm}

\displaystyle=\frac{E_{\mathbf{k}\pm}+\xi_{\mathbf{k}\pm}}{2[E_{\mathbf{k}\pm}(E_{\mathbf{k}\pm}+\xi_{\mathbf{k}\pm})]^{1/2}},

(80b)

\displaystyle b_{\mathbf{k}\pm}

\displaystyle=\frac{d_{z}(\mathbf{k})}{2[E_{\mathbf{k}\pm}(E_{\mathbf{k}\pm}+\xi_{\mathbf{k}\pm})]^{1/2}},

and the energy terms are defined as:

\xi_{\mathbf{k}\pm}=\xi_{\mathbf{k}}\pm\mu_{B}H_{z},\quad E_{\mathbf{k}\pm}=\sqrt{\xi_{\mathbf{k}\pm}^{2}+|d_{z}(\mathbf{k})|^{2}}.

(80c)

From these, the relevant matrix elements are:

\begin{split}[(u_{\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}\sigma_{x}u_{\mathbf{k}})^{ss}-(v_{-\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}\sigma_{x}v_{-\mathbf{k}})^{ss}]^{2}&=\frac{\xi_{\mathbf{k}s}^{2}}{E_{\mathbf{k}s}^{2}},\\
|(u_{\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}\sigma_{x}v_{\mathbf{k}})^{ss}-(u_{-\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}\sigma_{x}v_{-\mathbf{k}})^{ss}|^{2}&=1-\frac{\xi_{\mathbf{k}s}^{2}}{E_{\mathbf{k}s}^{2}}=\frac{|d_{z}(\mathbf{k})|^{2}}{E_{\mathbf{k}s}^{2}},\end{split}

(81)

and the off-diagonal elements vanish:

(u_{\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}\sigma_{x}u_{\mathbf{k}})^{s\bar{s}}=(v_{\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}\sigma_{x}v_{\mathbf{k}})^{s\bar{s}}=(u_{\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}\sigma_{x}v_{\mathbf{k}})^{s\bar{s}}=0,

(82)

where
s=\pm
and
\bar{s}
is the opposite of
s
. Consequently, the spin susceptibility components are:

\begin{split}\chi_{xx}^{ph+}&=-\mu_{B}^{2}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\sum_{s=\pm}\frac{\xi_{\mathbf{k}s}^{2}}{E_{\mathbf{k}s}^{2}}\frac{df(E_{\mathbf{k}s})}{dE_{\mathbf{k}s}},\\
\chi_{xx}^{ph-}&=0,\\
\chi_{xx}^{pp}&=-\mu_{B}^{2}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\sum_{s}\frac{|d_{z}(\mathbf{k})|^{2}}{E_{\mathbf{k}s}^{2}}\frac{f(E_{\mathbf{k}s})+f(E_{-\mathbf{k}s})-1}{E_{\mathbf{k}s}+E_{-\mathbf{k}s}}.\end{split}

(83)

Given that
E_{\mathbf{k}s}=E_{-\mathbf{k}s}
, we can rewrite the components as:

\displaystyle\chi_{xx}^{ph+}(T)

\displaystyle=4N(0)\mu_{B}^{2}\int\frac{d\Omega}{4\pi}\int_{|d_{z}(\mathbf{k})|}^{\infty}dE\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{E^{2}-|d_{z}(\mathbf{k})|^{2}}}

(84a)

\displaystyle\times\frac{|d_{z}(\mathbf{k})|^{2}}{E^{2}}f(E),

and

\displaystyle\chi_{xx}^{pp}(T)

\displaystyle=4N(0)\mu_{B}^{2}\int\frac{d\Omega}{4\pi}\int_{|d_{z}(\mathbf{k})|}^{\infty}dE\frac{E}{\sqrt{E^{2}-|d_{z}(\mathbf{k})|^{2}}}

(84b)

\displaystyle\times\frac{|d_{z}(\mathbf{k})|^{2}}{E^{2}}\frac{1-2f(E)}{2E},

where
\Omega
is the solid angle in
\mathbf{k}
-space. Summing these contributions, we obtain:

\begin{split}\chi_{xx}(T)&=2N(0)\mu_{B}^{2}\int\frac{d\Omega}{4\pi}\int_{|d_{z}(\mathbf{k})|}^{\infty}dE\frac{1}{\sqrt{E^{2}-|d_{z}(\mathbf{k})|^{2}}}\frac{|d_{z}(\mathbf{k})|^{2}}{E^{2}}\\
&=2N(0)\mu_{B}^{2}=\chi_{N}.\end{split}

(85)

This result confirms that
\chi_{xx}(T)
is temperature-independent and equal to
\chi_{N}
for the OSP states under a perpendicular Zeeman field.

Pairing States
k_{x}\hat{x}-k_{y}\hat{y}

k_{y}\hat{x}+k_{x}\hat{y}

\chi_{zz}(T=0)

In this section, we analyze the divergence of the zero-temperature spin susceptibility
\chi_{zz}(T=0)
for the
k_{x}\hat{x}-k_{y}\hat{y}
and
k_{y}\hat{x}+k_{x}\hat{y}
pairing states when
gk_{F}=\Delta(T=0,g)
. This divergence arises near nodal lines, as derived from Eq. (
15
) and Appendix

. The expression for
\chi_{zz}(T=0)
is:

\displaystyle\chi_{zz}(T=0)

\displaystyle=\chi_{zz}^{pp}(T=0)=\chi_{zz}^{pp+}(T=0)+\chi_{zz}^{pp-}(T=0),

(86)

where the intra-band term is:

\chi_{zz}^{pp+}(T=0)=\mu_{B}^{2}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\frac{d_{12}^{2}}{E_{\mathbf{k}\perp}^{2}}\sin^{2}\theta_{\mathbf{k}}\sin^{2}\Omega_{\mathbf{k}}\cdot\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{E_{\mathbf{k}+}}+\frac{1}{E_{\mathbf{k}-}}\right).

(87)

Numerical results indicate that the divergence is dominated by the intra-band term rather than the inter-band term, as
E_{\mathbf{k}+}>0
. Thus, we focus on the integral near nodal lines:

\displaystyle\mu_{B}^{2}\sum_{\mathbf{k}\text{ near }\mathbf{k}_{0}}\frac{d_{12}^{2}}{E_{\mathbf{k}\perp}^{2}}\sin^{2}\theta_{\mathbf{k}}\sin^{2}\Omega_{\mathbf{k}}\cdot\frac{1}{2E_{\mathbf{k}-}},

(88)

where
\mathbf{k}_{0}

\displaystyle\cos 2\varphi_{0}

\displaystyle=0,

(89)

\displaystyle\xi_{0}^{2}+\Delta^{2}\sin^{2}\theta_{0}

\displaystyle=g^{2}k_{F}^{2}.

For wave vectors near
\mathbf{k}_{0}
, we apply the following approximations:

\displaystyle\sin(\theta_{0}+\delta\theta)

\displaystyle\approx\sin\theta_{0}+\delta\theta\cdot\cos\theta_{0},

(90)

\displaystyle\cos(\theta_{0}+\delta\theta)

\displaystyle\approx\cos\theta_{0}-\delta\theta\cdot\sin\theta_{0},

\displaystyle\sin(2\varphi_{0}+2\delta\varphi)

\displaystyle\approx\sin 2\varphi_{0}\approx\pm 1,

\displaystyle\cos(2\varphi_{0}+2\delta\varphi)

\displaystyle\approx\cos 2\varphi_{0}-2\delta\varphi\approx-2\delta\varphi,

and for the energy terms:

\displaystyle E_{\mathbf{k}\perp}

\displaystyle\approx\sqrt{g^{2}k_{F}^{2}+2\xi_{0}\delta\xi+2\Delta^{2}\sin\theta_{\mathbf{k}}\cos\theta_{\mathbf{k}}\delta\theta}

(91)

\displaystyle\approx gk_{F}+\frac{\xi_{0}}{gk_{F}}\delta\xi+\frac{\Delta^{2}\sin\theta_{0}\cos\theta_{0}}{gk_{F}}\delta\theta,

\displaystyle E_{\mathbf{k}-}

\displaystyle\approx\sqrt{\left(\frac{\xi_{0}}{gk_{F}}\delta\xi+\frac{\Delta^{2}\sin\theta_{0}\cos\theta_{0}}{gk_{F}}\delta\theta\right)^{2}+4\Delta^{2}\sin^{4}\theta_{0}\delta\varphi^{2}}.

Substituting these approximations, the integral simplifies to:

\displaystyle\mu_{B}^{2}\sum_{\mathbf{k}\text{ near }\mathbf{k}_{0}}\frac{d_{12}^{2}}{E_{\mathbf{k}\perp}^{2}}\sin^{2}\theta_{\mathbf{k}}\sin^{2}\Omega_{\mathbf{k}}\cdot\frac{1}{2E_{\mathbf{k}-}}

(92)

\displaystyle=\frac{N(0)\mu_{B}^{2}}{8\pi}\cdot\frac{\Delta^{2}\sin^{4}\theta_{0}}{g^{2}k_{F}^{2}}\int_{0}^{\delta_{1}}d(\delta\xi)\int_{0}^{\delta_{2}}\sin\theta_{0}\,d(\delta\theta)\int_{0}^{\delta_{3}}d(\delta\varphi)\frac{1}{\sqrt{\left(\frac{\xi_{0}}{gk_{F}}\delta\xi+\frac{\Delta^{2}\sin\theta_{0}\cos\theta_{0}}{gk_{F}}\delta\theta\right)^{2}+4\Delta^{2}\sin^{4}\theta_{0}\delta\varphi^{2}}}.

This integral can be recognized as:

\int_{0}^{\delta_{1}}dx\int_{0}^{\delta_{2}}dy\int_{0}^{\delta_{3}}dz\frac{1}{\sqrt{(x+y)^{2}+z^{2}}},

which converges for all points on the nodal lines. However, setting
\xi_{0}=0
yields:

\int_{0}^{\delta_{1}}dx\int_{0}^{\delta_{2}}dy\int_{0}^{\delta_{3}}dz\frac{1}{\sqrt{y^{2}+z^{2}}},

which remains convergent. At
gk_{F}=\Delta
, setting
\xi_{0}=0
and
\theta_{0}=\pi/2
results in:

\int_{0}^{\delta_{1}}dx\int_{0}^{\delta_{2}}dy\int_{0}^{\delta_{3}}dz\frac{1}{z},

indicating divergence.

To identify the specific form of divergence near
gk_{F}=\Delta
, we set
\xi_{0}=0
and:

\displaystyle\sin^{2}\theta_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{g^{2}k_{F}^{2}}{\Delta^{2}},\quad\cos^{2}\theta_{0}=1-\frac{g^{2}k_{F}^{2}}{\Delta^{2}}.

(93)

The integral near this point becomes:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\delta_{2}}d(\delta\theta)\int_{0}^{\delta_{3}}d(\delta\varphi)\frac{1}{\sqrt{(\Delta^{2}-g^{2}k_{F}^{2})\delta\theta^{2}+4\Delta^{2}\delta\varphi^{2}}}

(94)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\Delta}\int_{0}^{\delta_{2}}d(\delta\theta)\ln\left(\frac{2\Delta\delta_{3}+\sqrt{4\Delta^{2}\delta_{3}^{2}+(\Delta^{2}-g^{2}k_{F}^{2})\delta\theta^{2}}}{\sqrt{(\Delta^{2}-g^{2}k_{F}^{2})\delta\theta^{2}}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\Delta\sqrt{\Delta^{2}-g^{2}k_{F}^{2}}}\left[\sqrt{\Delta^{2}-g^{2}k_{F}^{2}}\delta_{2}\ln\left(\frac{2\Delta\delta_{3}+\sqrt{4\Delta^{2}\delta_{3}^{2}+(\Delta^{2}-g^{2}k_{F}^{2})\delta_{2}^{2}}}{\sqrt{\Delta^{2}-g^{2}k_{F}^{2}}\delta_{2}}\right)+2\Delta\delta_{3}\ln\left(\frac{\sqrt{\Delta^{2}-g^{2}k_{F}^{2}}\delta_{2}+\sqrt{4\Delta^{2}\delta_{3}^{2}+(\Delta^{2}-g^{2}k_{F}^{2})\delta_{2}^{2}}}{2\Delta\delta_{3}}\right)\right].

As
gk_{F}\to\Delta
, this diverges as:

-\frac{\delta_{2}}{4\Delta}\ln(\Delta^{2}-g^{2}k_{F}^{2}).

(95)

A similar divergence occurs for
gk_{F}=\Delta+0^{+}
, proportional to
-\ln(g^{2}k_{F}^{2}-\Delta^{2})
. Thus,
\chi_{zz}(T=0)
diverges as:

\chi_{zz}(T=0)\propto-\ln|\Delta^{2}-g^{2}k_{F}^{2}|.

(96)

For finite temperatures (
T>0
), the factor
\frac{1}{E_{\mathbf{k}-}}
is replaced by
\frac{1-2f(E_{\mathbf{k}-})}{E_{\mathbf{k}-}}
. In the limit
E_{\mathbf{k}-}\to 0
:

\lim_{E_{\mathbf{k}-}\to 0}\frac{1-2f(E_{\mathbf{k}-})}{E_{\mathbf{k}-}}=\frac{1}{2k_{B}T},

(97)

implying that when
gk_{F}=\Delta
,
\chi_{zz}(T)\propto\frac{1}{T}
as
T\to 0
.

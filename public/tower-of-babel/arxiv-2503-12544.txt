Polarisation sets of Green operators for normally hyperbolic equations
Christopher J. Fewster

Abstract
The polarisation set of a vector-valued distribution generalises the wavefront set and captures fibre-directional information about its singularities in addition to their phase space description. Motivated by problems in quantum field theory on curved spacetimes, we consider normally hyperbolic operators on vector bundles over globally hyperbolic spacetimes, and compute the polarisation sets of the kernel distributions for their advanced and retarded Green operators and the difference thereof. This permits the computation of related polarisation and wavefront sets for operators whose solution theory is related to the normally hyperbolic case. As a particular example, we consider the Proca equation that describes massive relativistic spin- 1 1 particles, identifying and closing a gap in a recent paper on that subject.

Introduction

Normally hyperbolic partial differential operators play an important role in the theory of quantum fields on curved spacetimes. Some theories are formulated directly using normally hyperbolic equations, while the solution theory of others relies on relating the field equation to an auxiliary normally hyperbolic operator – see, e.g.,
[
3

Often it is sufficient to understand the wavefront set of the Green operators, but, as will be described below, there are occasions where a more detailed description of the singularities is required. This will be provided by the present paper, which computes the polarisation set
[
5

of the advanced and retarded Green operators, and their difference, for general normally hyperbolic operators on vector bundles over globally hyperbolic spacetimes, and allows the wavefront set of related operators to be computed. This paper is a companion to
[

Let us describe the main results in more detail.

Let

be a Lorentzian manifold with Levi-Civita connection
\nabla

for the bundle of weight

\Gamma^{\infty}(B\otimes\Omega^{1/2})

By definition, a normally hyperbolic operator on
\Gamma^{\infty}(B)
is
a second-order differential operator

) in a local frame for

P^{a}_{\phantom{a}b}=g^{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}\delta^{a}_{\phantom{a}b}+U^{a}_{\phantom{a}b},

(1.1)

where
U^{a}_{\phantom{a}b}
is a matrix of first order differential operators. Every
normally hyperbolic operator can be put into a simpler standard form. Namely, there exists a
Weitzenböck connection

\nabla^{B}
on
B
that gathers all the first order terms
so that

P=\Box^{B}+V,\qquad\Box^{B}:=g^{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\mu}^{T^{*}M\otimes B}\nabla_{\nu}^{B},

(1.2)

where
V\in\Gamma^{\infty}(\End(B))

\nabla^{T^{*}M\otimes B}=\nabla\otimes 1+1\otimes\nabla^{B}
is the natural extension of
\nabla^{B}
to a connection on

is established e.g., in Proposition 3.1 of
[
2
]

\Gamma^{\infty}(B\otimes\Omega^{1/2})

Now suppose that
(M,g)
is globally hyperbolic, which means that it is time-oriented, contains no closed causal curves, and has the property that every compact subset of
K
has a compact causal hull. Then
(see, e.g.,
[
1
]
) any normally hyperbolic operator

on
\Gamma^{\infty}(B)
has retarded (
+
) and advanced

Green operators
E^{\pm}_{P}:\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(B)\to\Gamma^{\infty}(B)
so that
E_{P}^{\pm}Pf=f=PE^{\pm}_{P}f
for all
f\in\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(B)
and with support

\supp E^{\pm}_{P}f\subset J^{\pm}(\supp f),

(1.3)

where
J^{\pm}(S)
are the causal future/past of
S\subset M
. (See below for our other main conventions.) The difference of the advanced and retarded operators,
E_{P}=E^{-}_{P}-E^{+}_{P}
, plays an important role in quantum field theory.

It is often convenient to consider operators on bundles with an explicit
\tfrac{1}{2}
-density factor.
If
P
is normally hyperbolic on
\Gamma^{\infty}(B\otimes\Omega^{1/2})
, then its Green operators
have distributional kernels in
\mathscr{D}^{\prime}((B\boxtimes B^{*})\otimes\Omega^{1/2}_{M\times M})
, where
B^{*}
is the dual bundle to
B
and
\boxtimes
denotes the external bundle product. The wavefront sets of these distributions can be described as follows.
Let
\mathcal{N}\subset T^{*}M
be the bundle of nonzero null covectors on
(M,g)
, and, for
(x,k),(y,\ell)\in\mathcal{N}
, write
(x,k)\sim(y,\ell)
if and only if there is a null geodesic segment
\gamma
connecting
x
and
y
, with tangent vectors
k^{\sharp}
and
\ell^{\sharp}
at the endpoints, which are related by parallel transport along
\gamma
(understanding
(x,k)\sim(x,\ell)
if and only if
k=\ell
is null); in this case we say that the geodesic segment
\gamma

witnesses
to the relation
(x,k)\sim(y,\ell)
. With these definitions, set

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}

\displaystyle=\{(x,k;x^{\prime},-k^{\prime})\in\mathcal{N}\times\mathcal{N}:(x,k)\sim(x^{\prime},k^{\prime})\}

(1.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{\pm}

\displaystyle=\{(x,k;x^{\prime},-k^{\prime})\in\mathcal{R}:x\in J^{\pm}(x^{\prime})\}.

(1.5)

Then the kernel distributions
E_{P}^{\pm}
and
E_{P}
have wavefront sets

\displaystyle\WF(E_{P}^{\pm})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{R}^{\pm}\cup\WF(\mathrm{id}),

(1.6)

\displaystyle\WF(E_{P})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{R},

(1.7)

where
\mathrm{id}
is the kernel distribution of the identity operator on
B
. This result generalises the scalar case
[
6
,
33
]
and was
proved for bundles as Theorem A.5 in
[
35
]
based on a scaling limit structure of the Green operators in terms of Riesz distributions.
Closely related statements for Feynman parametrices can be found in
[
24
]
.

Our main interest is in computing the polarisation sets of these distributions. The motivation for this
arises in quantum field theory
[
7
]
, in the description of
Hadamard states
– generally agreed to be the class of physical states for linear field theories. For example, consider
the Klein–Gordon theory, with classical field equation
(\Box+m^{2})\phi=0
. In the quantised theory, every sufficiently regular state determines a distributional two-point function
W\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(M\times M)
.
The Hadamard condition for states of the quantised theory was originally stated in terms of a Hadamard series expansion of the two-point function at nearby points
[
28
]
, which is somewhat unwieldy when stated with full precision
[
28
,
30
]
. A major breakthrough was the realisation by Radzikowski
[
33
]
that a state is Hadamard if and only if its two-point function satisfies the wavefront set condition

\WF(W)=\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{Had}}:=\{(x,k;x^{\prime},k^{\prime})\in\mathcal{R}:k\in\mathcal{N}^{+}\},

(1.8)

where
\mathcal{N}^{+}\subset\mathcal{N}
is the bundle of future-pointing nonzero null covectors.
The Hadamard condition was subsequently described in similar terms for various other linear field theories of interest, notably those based on the Dirac, Proca, Maxwell and linearised Einstein equations. See e.g.,
[
19
,
35
,
9
,
23
]
for original sources and
[
3
]
for an exposition of some of the theories mentioned. Here one encounters a problem, because these field equations are not normally hyperbolic. For example, the Proca equation describes
1
-forms
A
obeying

(-\delta{\rm d}+m^{2})A=0,

(1.9)

where
{\rm d}
and
\delta
are the exterior derivative and codifferential on
(M,g)
and
m>0
is the mass parameter.
As
-\delta{\rm d}A=\nabla^{\mu}(\nabla_{\mu}A_{\nu}-\nabla_{\nu}A_{\mu})
, it is clear that the Proca operator
is not normally hyperbolic. However its solution theory is closely related to that of the normally hyperbolic
1
-form Klein–Gordon operator
K^{(1)}=-\delta{\rm d}+{\rm d}\delta+m^{2}
, and a short calculation shows that the operators

E_{P}^{\pm}=E_{K^{(1)}}^{\pm}\circ R

(1.10)

provide advanced and retarded Green operators for
P=-\delta{\rm d}+m^{2}
, where
R=1-m^{-2}{\rm d}\delta
. The definition of Hadamard states for the Proca field given in
[
9
]
made use of this relationship: a state of the Proca field was said to be Hadamard if its two-point function obeys
W=H\circ(1\otimes R)
and
H
is a Hadamard-form bisolution for
K^{(1)}
obeying
\WF(H)=\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{Had}}
.

In an interesting recent paper
[
31
]
, Moretti, Murro and Volpe (MMV) revisited the Proca theory with the aim of giving a more direct definition of the Hadamard condition that directly constrains
\WF(W)
. To do this, they need to compute

\WF(E_{P})
. Standard bounds on the wavefront set, together with the known form of
\WF(E_{K^{(1)}})
yield

\WF(E_{P})\subset\mathcal{R}\subset\WF(E_{P})\cup(\Char(1\otimes R)\cap\mathcal{R}),

(1.11)

so if the last term on the right-hand side is empty then one has
\WF(E_{P})=\mathcal{R}
. This is what is claimed in
part (b) of the proof of Proposition 4.7 in
[
31
]
, unfortunately on the basis of an incorrect
assertion that
\Char(1\otimes R)=T^{*}M\times 0_{T^{*}M}
, where for any vector bundle
B
,
0_{B}
denotes the corresponding zero section (and we drop the subscript where there is no ambiguity). In fact, the principal symbol
of
R
acts on
T_{x}^{*}M
by
r(x,k)v=-m^{-2}g^{-1}(k,v)k
, which vanishes on the annihilator of
k^{\sharp}
, so
\Char(R)=\dot{T}^{*}M
and thus
\Char(1\otimes R)=\dot{T}^{*}(M\times M)
. Accordingly,
the standard calculus of wavefront sets cannot be used to determine
\WF(E_{P})
and the MMV paper contains a gap at this point, affecting a number of their results (see
[
7
]
for further discussion). A similar situation arises for the Dirac equation, whose Green operators are given in terms of the normally hyperbolic spinorial Klein–Gordon operator composed with another operator that, inconveniently, is characteristic on
\mathcal{N}
(see e.g.,
[
29
]
for discussion).

In this paper, we will establish general results that enable the gap in MMV to be closed – with the
result that
\WF(E_{P})=\mathcal{R}
. The main tools are Dencker’s polarisation set and his associated results on the propagation of polarisation
[
5
]
. We recall that the polarisation set
\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(u)
of a vector-valued distribution
u\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(B\otimes\Omega^{1/2})
is a subset of the pullback bundle
\pi^{*}B
, where
\pi:T^{*}M\to M
is the bundle projection, so that

\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(u)=\bigcap_{Au\in\Gamma^{\infty}(\Omega^{1/2})}\{(x,\xi;w)\in\pi^{*}B:\xi\neq 0,~w\in\ker\sigma(A)(x,\xi)\},

(1.12)

where the intersection is taken over properly supported classical pseudodifferential operators
A\in\Psi_{\textnormal{cl}}^{0}(B\otimes\Omega^{1/2},\Omega^{1/2})
that render
u
smooth, and
\sigma(A)
denotes the
homogeneous principal symbol of
A
. Evidently
\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(u)
contains the zero section of
\pi^{*}B
over
\dot{T}^{*}M:=T^{*}M\setminus 0
; the wavefront set
\WF(u)
is given by the set of
(x,k)\in\dot{T}^{*}M
over which the fibre of
\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(u)
is nonzero. Further properties of polarisation sets will be described in Section
3
.

To describe our main results, let
P
be a normally hyperbolic operator on
\Gamma^{\infty}(B\otimes\Omega^{1/2})
. Let
\nabla^{B}
be the Weitzenböck connection on
B
induced by the
normally hyperbolic operator
(-g)^{-1/4}P(-g)^{1/4}
, and let
\nabla^{B^{*}}
be the dual connection on
B
. Next, let
\Pi_{x,k}^{x^{\prime},k^{\prime}}\in\Lin(B_{x^{\prime}},B_{x})
be the operator of parallel transport
with respect to
\nabla^{B}
along the (unique) null geodesic segment connecting
x^{\prime}
to
x
that witnesses to the relation
(x,k)\sim(x^{\prime},k^{\prime})
. If
(x,k)\not\sim(x^{\prime},k^{\prime})
, then we set
\Pi_{x,k}^{x^{\prime},k^{\prime}}=0
. We can now define the sets

\mathcal{R}_{\textnormal{pol}}^{\#}=\{(x,k;x^{\prime},-k^{\prime};w)\in(\pi\times\pi)^{*}(B\boxtimes B^{*}):(x,k;x^{\prime},-k^{\prime})\in\mathcal{R}^{\#},~w\in\mathbb{C}\Pi_{x,k}^{x^{\prime},k^{\prime}}\},

(1.13)

where
\#
stands for
+
, or
-
or no symbol, and the sets
\mathcal{R}^{\#}
were defined in (
1.4
). We have also identified
\Lin(B_{x}^{\prime},B_{x})
with
B_{x}\otimes B_{x^{\prime}}^{*}
. At first sight, there appears to be
an asymmetry in (
1.13
), but note that
(x,k)\sim(x^{\prime},k^{\prime})
if and only if
(x,-k)\sim(x^{\prime},-k^{\prime})
and
\Pi_{x,k}^{x^{\prime},k^{\prime}}=\Pi_{x,-k}^{x^{\prime},-k^{\prime}}
. Our main result can now be stated.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
B
be a finite-rank complex vector bundle over a globally hyperbolic spacetime
(M,g)
.
Let
P
be a normally hyperbolic operator on
\Gamma^{\infty}(B\otimes\Omega^{1/2})
, and
\mathcal{R}_{\textnormal{pol}}^{\pm}
,
\mathcal{R}_{\textnormal{pol}}
be as defined in (
1.13
). Then:

a)

The polarisation sets of the kernel distributions for the Green operators
E^{\pm}_{P}
of
P
satisfy

\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(E_{P}^{\pm})=\mathcal{R}^{\pm}_{\textnormal{pol}}\cup\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(\mathrm{id}),

(1.14)

where the polarisation set of the kernel distribution of the identity operator on
\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(B\otimes\Omega^{1/2})
is

\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(\mathrm{id})=\{(x,k;x,-k;w)\in(\pi\times\pi)^{*}(B\boxtimes B^{*}):k\in\dot{T}^{*}_{x}M,~w\in\mathbb{C}\delta_{x}\}\cup 0,

(1.15)

in which
\delta_{x}
is the image of
\mathrm{id}_{B_{x}}
under the identification of
\Lin(B_{x})
with
B_{x}\otimes B_{x}^{*}
.

b)

The polarisation set of the kernel distribution of
E_{P}=E_{P}^{-}-E_{P}^{+}
is

\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(E_{P})=\mathcal{R}_{\textnormal{pol}}\cup 0.

(1.16)

c)

Consequently, the wavefront sets of
E_{P}^{\pm}
and
E_{P}
are

\WF(E_{P}^{\pm})=\mathcal{R}^{\pm}\cup\WF(\mathrm{id}),\qquad\WF(E_{P})=\mathcal{R}.

(1.17)

Part (c) follows trivially from (a) and (b), and provides an alternative proof of (
1.6
) and (
1.7
) that avoids scaling limits or an expansion into Riesz distributions. Special cases of Theorem
1.1
have been studied before
[
29
]
but the result seems to be new in this generality.

A simple consequence of Theorem
1.1
is

Corollary 1.2
.

Under the hypotheses of Theorem
1.1
,
suppose that
Q\in\Psi_{\textnormal{cl}}^{m_{Q}}(B\otimes\Omega^{1/2},\hat{B}\otimes\Omega^{1/2})
and

R\in\Psi_{\textnormal{cl}}^{m_{R}}(\tilde{B}\otimes\Omega^{1/2},B\otimes\Omega^{1/2})
have principal symbols

and

, where
\hat{B}\to M
and

\tilde{B}\to M
are finite-rank complex vector bundles.

If for all
(x,k;x^{\prime},-k^{\prime})\in\mathcal{R}
one has

q(x,k)\circ\Pi_{x,k}^{x^{\prime},k^{\prime}}\circ r(x^{\prime},k^{\prime})\neq 0

(1.18)

then
\WF(QE_{P}R)=\mathcal{R}
. This holds in particular if

Q=\mathrm{id}
and
r
is nonvanishing on
\mathcal{N}
, or if
R=\mathrm{id}
and

is nonvanishing on
\mathcal{N}
.

Using Corollary
1.2
we can close the gap in MMV quite easily, because (

) corresponds to the situation where

and

is nonvanishing, albeit everywhere characteristic. We will go into more detail about this in section

Corollary
1.2
is intended as a useful general tool to facilitate the use of Theorem

, particularly when applied to quantum field theory in curved spacetimes.
Further applications appear in
[
7
,
8
]
. In particular, Ref.
[
7
]
introduces a generalised Hadamard condition for states of QFTs based on a class of real, formally hermitian, Green-hyperbolic operators (RFHGHO’s) satisfying a decomposability condition on the wavefront set of the advanced-minus-retarded operator

, namely that

\WF(E_{P})\subset(\mathcal{V}^{+}\times\mathcal{V}^{-})\cup(\mathcal{V}^{-}\times\mathcal{V}^{+}),

(1.19)

where

are nonintersecting opposite conic sets (

) that are relatively closed in

, whereupon a state is said to be

-Hadamard if its two-point function

satisfies

. The cones

need not be related to the spacetime metric. Corollary
1.2
is used in cases (including the Proca equation) where the advanced-minus-retarded operator of a RFHGHO

takes the form

, where

is a normally hyperbolic operator. The conclusion is that

is then decomposable with respect to the usual null cones

) in such cases — see Corollary 5.6 of

. These results also feed into Section 7 of

, where the existence of Hadamard states for the neutral Proca field on arbitrary globally hyperbolic spacetimes is established and the

-Hadamard condition is shown to be equivalent to the MMV definition and the definition given originally in

. Meanwhile, in

]
results from
[

are used to show that Hadamard states exist for the charged Proca field in an external electromagnetic field and for a variety of other coupled Proca theories. 1.2
applies. We expect that other applications will follow.

, the polarisation set is described in section

We conclude with a summary and outlook in section

[

]
, but it is relevant to mention that pseudodifferential methods have played a role in the construction of Hadamard and adiabatic states since the works of Junker
[

,

]
and Junker and Schrohe
[

]
, and subsequently by Gérard, Wrochna and coauthors who have given constructions for Hadamard states of the scalar field

]

; see also

2
Background on distributions and pseudodifferential operators

General conventions

The symbol
\subset
does not exclude equality. When symbols are stacked vertically, e.g.,
E^{\pm}
, the alternatives are ordered from top to bottom.
Duality pairings
V^{*}\times V\to\mathbb{C}
are indicated by double
angle-brackets,
\langle\!\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\!\rangle_{V}
; the subscript is dropped where there is no ambiguity.
Lorentzian metrics have mostly minus signature.

Manifolds and bundles

Let
X
be a smooth
n
-manifold. If
E
is a finite-rank complex vector bundle over
X
then
\Gamma^{\infty}(E)
denotes the space of smooth sections of
E
and
\Gamma^{\infty}_{0}(E)
those of compact support. The bundle of weight-
\alpha
densities over
X
is denoted

\Omega^{\alpha}_{X}
, suppressing the subscript if there is no ambiguity about the base manifold. The notation
\dot{T}^{*}X
denotes the cotangent bundle of
X
with its zero section excised. For bundles

E
and
F
,
E\boxtimes F
is the external tensor product bundle over the Cartesian product of the base spaces, while, if applicable,
E\otimes F
is the tensor product bundle over the common base space of
E
and
F
.

Distributions and kernels

We regard distributions as generalised functions, so
\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(X)=\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega_{X})^{\prime}
.
Similarly, for a bundle
E
over
X
,
\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(E)=\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(E^{*}\otimes\Omega_{X})^{\prime}

with the weak topology, and there is an inclusion
\iota_{X}:\Gamma^{\infty}(E)\to\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(E)

(\iota_{X}e)(u)=\int_{X}\langle\!\langle u,e\rangle\!\rangle_{E},\qquad e\in\Gamma^{\infty}(E),~u\in\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(E^{*}\otimes\Omega_{X}).

(2.1)

Here, we have extended the notation for duality pairing to densitised sections, so that it takes values in densities of the total weight of the arguments.
Half-densitised sections have the convenient feature that
\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(E\otimes\Omega_{X}^{1/2})=\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(E^{*}\otimes\Omega^{1/2}_{X})^{\prime}
.

Next, suppose
E
and
F
are bundles over manifolds
X
and
Y
, and

T:\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(E\otimes\Omega^{1/2}_{X})\to\Gamma^{\infty}(F\otimes\Omega^{1/2}_{Y})
is a continuous linear map. Then there is a kernel
distribution
T^{\textnormal{knl}}\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}((F\boxtimes E^{*})\otimes(\Omega_{Y\times X}^{1/2}))
defined by

T^{\textnormal{knl}}(v\otimes e)=\int\langle\!\langle v,Te\rangle\!\rangle_{F},\qquad e\in\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(E\otimes\Omega^{1/2}),~v\in\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(F^{*}\otimes\Omega^{1/2})

(2.2)

Typically we will write the
knl
superscript only for emphasis.

Pseudodifferential operators

Let
X
be a smooth
n
-manifold and let
\pi:T^{*}X\to X
be the projection map. For any
m\in\mathbb{R}
,
\Psi_{\textnormal{cl}}^{m}(X)
will denote the space of classical (i.e., properly supported,
1
-step polyhomogeneous) pseudodifferential operators on
X
. For convenience the definition of
\Psi_{\textnormal{cl}}^{m}(X)
and related concepts, such as the symbol classes
S^{m}(X)
, are recalled in Appendix
A
following
[
22
]
, while the following summary serves to fix notation.

Each
A\in\Psi_{\textnormal{cl}}^{m}(X)
is in particular a continuous linear map

A:C_{0}^{\infty}(X)\to C_{0}^{\infty}(X)
that extends continuously to
A:\mathscr{E}^{\prime}(X)\to\mathscr{E}^{\prime}(X)
. There is an associated principal symbol
\sigma(A)
, often written
a
, which belongs to the symbol class
S^{m}(X)\subset C^{\infty}(T^{*}X)
and is homogeneous of degree
m
in the covector argument outside a neighbourhood of the zero section, where it is uniquely determined by
A
; conversely
\sigma(A)
uniquely determines
A
up to addition of elements in
\Psi_{\textnormal{cl}}^{m-1}(X)
. A basic example is that, for any vector field
V\in\Gamma^{\infty}(TX)
, the symbol given by
a(x,\xi)={\rm i}\langle\!\langle\xi,V_{x}\rangle\!\rangle
corresponds to the directional derivative operator
\nabla_{V}
modulo operators in
\Psi_{\textnormal{cl}}^{0}(X)
.
Every chart on
X
determines a chart representative of
A\in\Psi_{\textnormal{cl}}^{m}(X)
, which is specified up to smoothing operators by its full symbol.

If
E
and
F
are finite-rank complex vector bundles over
X
,
then
\Psi_{\textnormal{cl}}^{m}(E,F)
is the
space of continuous linear maps
A:\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(E)\to\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(F)
so that, for each
e\in\Gamma^{\infty}(E)
,
v\in\Gamma^{\infty}(F^{*})
, one has

A^{v}_{\phantom{v}e}\in\Psi_{\textnormal{cl}}^{m}(X)
, where
A^{v}_{\phantom{v}e}f=\langle\!\langle v,A(fe)\rangle\!\rangle
. We write

\Psi_{\textnormal{cl}}^{m}(E)
for
\Psi_{\textnormal{cl}}^{m}(E,E)
. Each
A\in\Psi_{\textnormal{cl}}^{m}(E,F)
has a principal symbol

a\in S^{m}(\pi^{*}\Hom(E,F))
so that

\sigma(A^{v}_{\phantom{v}e})(x,\xi)=\langle\!\langle v_{x},a(x,\xi)e_{x}\rangle\!\rangle,

(2.3)

for all
e\in\Gamma^{\infty}(E)
,
v\in\Gamma^{\infty}(F^{*})
, and
(x,\xi)\in T^{*}X
. We define

\ker a=\{(x,\xi;w)\in\pi^{*}E:\xi\neq 0,~a(x,\xi)w=0\},

(2.4)

and describe
A
or
a
as
characteristic
(resp.,
noncharacteristic
)at
(x,\xi)\in\dot{T}^{*}X
if

\ker a|_{(x,\xi)}\subset E_{x}
is nontrivial (resp., trivial), also writing

\Char A=\{(x,\xi)\in\dot{T}^{*}X:\ker a(x,\xi)\neq 0\}.

(2.5)

If
E
and
F
have equal rank,
a
is noncharacteristic at
(x,\xi)
if and only if there is a symbol in
S^{-m}(\pi^{*}\Hom(F,E))
that is inverse to

a
in a conic neighbourhood of
(x,\xi)
.

Subprincipal and refined principal symbols

Any operator
A\in\Psi^{m}_{\textnormal{cl}}(\Omega^{1/2})
has a
refined principal symbol

a^{\textnormal{r}}\in S^{m}(X)/S^{m-2}(X)
defined as follows (see
[
22
, 18.1.33]
). Let
(X_{\kappa},\kappa)
be a chart in
X
and let the corresponding chart representative of
A
have full symbol
a_{\kappa}\in S^{m}(\kappa(X_{\kappa})\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
. One defines
a^{\textnormal{r}}_{\kappa}\in C^{\infty}(T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n}|_{\kappa(X_{\kappa})})
by

a_{\kappa}^{\textnormal{r}}(x,\xi)=a_{\kappa}(x,\xi)+\frac{{\rm i}}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}a_{\kappa}}{\partial x^{\mu}\partial\xi_{\mu}}(x,\xi).

(2.6)

Then
a^{\textnormal{r}}\in S^{m}(T^{*}X)/S^{m-2}(T^{*}X)
is uniquely defined by requiring
a^{\textnormal{r}}\circ\kappa^{*}-a_{\kappa}^{\textnormal{r}}\in S^{m-2}(\kappa(X_{\kappa})\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all local coordinate systems. (Here, we identify
\kappa(X_{\kappa})\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
T^{*}\kappa(X_{\kappa})
for the purpose of applying the pullback
\kappa^{*}:T^{*}\kappa(X_{\kappa})\to T^{*}X
.) Furthermore,
A
has a
subprincipal symbol

a^{\textnormal{sub}}\in S^{m-1}(X)
that, outside a neighbourhood of the zero section, is homogeneous of degree
m-1
in the covector argument and obeys
a^{\textnormal{r}}=a+a^{\textnormal{sub}}\pmod{S^{m-2}(X)}
.

The subprincipal symbol generalises to operators
A\in\Psi^{m}_{\textnormal{cl}}(E\otimes\Omega^{1/2})
with scalar principal symbol,
a=b\mathrm{id}_{E}
. Consider sections
e_{r}\in\Gamma^{\infty}(E)
and

v_{r}\in\Gamma^{\infty}(E^{*})
(
1\leq r\leq\textnormal{rk}E
) that provide a local framing for
E
and a dual framing of
E^{*}
.
Then one has an
r\times r
-matrix
(A^{s}_{\phantom{s}r})_{r,s=1}^{\textnormal{rk}E}
with entries
A^{s}_{\phantom{s}r}:=A^{v_{s}}_{\phantom{v_{s}}e_{r}}\in\Psi^{m}_{\textnormal{cl}}(\Omega^{1/2})
, each of which has its own refined principal symbol. The matrix of refined principal symbols
a^{r}
does not transform as a section of
\pi^{*}\Hom(E)
, but there is a related invariantly defined object obtained as follows. Let
\nabla^{E}
be any connection on
E
, and define connection
1
-forms with respect to the framing
e_{r}
by
(\Gamma^{E}_{V})^{s}_{\phantom{s}r}=\langle\!\langle v_{s},\nabla^{E}_{V}e_{r}\rangle\!\rangle
for any
V\in\Gamma^{\infty}(TM)
. The pullback connection
\nabla^{\pi^{*}E}
on
\pi^{*}E
is defined so that
\nabla_{W}^{\pi^{*}E}\pi^{*}s|_{(x,k)}=\nabla_{\pi_{*}W}^{E}s|_{x}
for
(x,k)\in T^{*}M
,
s\in\Gamma^{\infty}(E)
and
W\in T_{(x,k)}T^{*}M
. Its connection
1
-forms obey
\Gamma^{\pi^{*}E}_{W}=\Gamma^{E}_{\pi_{*}W}\circ\pi
relative to the framing
\pi^{*}e_{r}
.
The connection
1
-forms and refined principal symbols have a related transformation law under a change of framing. Namely,
if
X_{b}
is the Hamiltonian vector field

X_{b}=\frac{\partial b_{\kappa}}{\partial\xi_{\mu}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\mu}}-\frac{\partial b_{\kappa}}{\partial\xi_{\mu}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi^{\mu}}

(2.7)

determined by
b
, then the matrix
a^{r}+{\rm i}\Gamma^{\pi^{*}E}_{X_{b}}

transforms, modulo
S^{m-2}(\pi^{*}\Hom(E))
, as a section of

\pi^{*}\Hom(E)
(see Appendix
A
for more detail on this point). In this way, the matrix of subprincipal symbols

a^{\textnormal{sub}}=a-a^{\textnormal{r}}
can be regarded as a partial connection along the Hamiltonian flow of
b
.

The formal dual

Each
A\in\Psi^{m}_{\textnormal{cl}}(E\otimes\Omega^{1/2},F\otimes\Omega^{1/2})
has a formal dual
{\vphantom{A}}{}^{\star}A\in\Psi^{m}_{\textnormal{cl}}(F^{*}\otimes\Omega^{1/2},E^{*}\otimes\Omega^{1/2})
, so that
({\vphantom{A}}^{\star}Au)(f)=u(Af)

for
u\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(F^{*}\otimes\Omega^{1/2})
,
f\in\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(E\otimes\Omega^{1/2})
. Its principal symbol is

({\vphantom{a}}^{\star}a)(x,\xi)=a(x,-\xi)^{*},

(2.8)

where the star on the right-hand side denotes the dual map. This is easily obtained as a modification of Theorem 18.1.7 (cf. also Theorem 18.1.34
′
) in
[
22
]
.

Suppose
E
and
F
are finite-rank complex vector bundles over smooth manifolds
X
and
Y
. If
T:\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(E\otimes\Omega^{1/2}_{X})\to\Gamma^{\infty}(F\otimes\Omega^{1/2}_{Y})
is a continuous linear map and
R\in\Psi^{m}_{\textnormal{cl}}(E\otimes\Omega_{X}^{1/2})
,

Q\in\Psi^{m^{\prime}}_{\textnormal{cl}}(F\otimes\Omega_{Y}^{1/2})
, then one has the identity

(QTR)^{\textnormal{knl}}=(Q\otimes{\vphantom{R}}^{\star}R)T^{\textnormal{knl}}

(2.9)

for the kernel distributions.

3
The polarisation set

3.1
Definition and basic properties

We take the calculus of wavefront sets for scalar or densitised distributions for granted
[
21
,
20
]
. For

u\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(E\otimes\Omega^{1/2})
, one defines

\WF(u)=\bigcup_{s\in\Gamma^{\infty}(E^{*})}\WF(f\mapsto u(fs)),

(3.1)

which can be restricted to a union over sections forming a local frame for
E^{*}
near
x
, for the purposes of computing
\WF(u)
at
x
. It is easily checked that various standard results on scalar distributions generalise immediately. In particular, one has

\WF(Pu)\subset\WF(u)

(3.2)

for all pseudodifferential operators
P
. Similarly, a sufficient condition for a continuous linear map
T:\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(E\otimes\Omega^{1/2}_{X})\to\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(F\otimes\Omega^{1/2}_{Y})
to act on
u\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(E\otimes\Omega^{1/2}_{X})
is that

\WF^{\prime}(T^{\textnormal{knl}})\cap(0\times\WF(u))=\emptyset
, whereupon

\WF(Tu)\subset\WF^{\prime}(T^{\textnormal{knl}})\bullet\WF(u),

(3.3)

where

\displaystyle\WF^{\prime}(T^{\textnormal{knl}})\bullet\WF(u)

\displaystyle:=\{(x,\xi)\in\dot{T}^{*}X:\exists~(y,\eta)\in\WF(u),~\textnormal{s.t.}~(x,\xi;y,-\eta)\in\WF(T^{\textnormal{knl}})\}

\displaystyle=\pr_{\dot{T}^{*}X}\WF^{\prime}(T^{\textnormal{knl}})\cap(\dot{T}^{*}X\times\WF(u)).

(3.4)

Scalar distributions also obey
\WF(u)\subset\WF(Pu)\cup(\Char P\cap\WF(u))
but this result does not follow easily from (
3.1
). It can be derived instead using the polarisation set, to which we now turn.

Let
\pi:T^{*}X\to X
be the bundle projection. As already mentioned, the polarisation set
[
5
]

\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(u)
of
u\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(E\otimes\Omega^{1/2})
is the subset of the pullback bundle
\pi^{*}E
over
T^{*}X
given by

\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(u)=\bigcap_{Au\in\Gamma^{\infty}(\Omega^{1/2})}\{(x,\xi;w)\in\pi^{*}E:\xi\neq 0,~w\in\ker\sigma(A)(x,\xi)\},

(3.5)

taking the intersection over
A\in\Psi_{\textnormal{cl}}^{0}(E\otimes\Omega^{1/2},\Omega^{1/2})
so that
Au
is smooth. In fact, Dencker introduced polarisation sets for
distributions valued in
\mathbb{C}^{N}
but (as mentioned in
[
5
]
) the generalisation to bundles is straightforward.
It is convenient to define the polarisation set as a subset of
\pi^{*}E
rather than
\pi^{*}E\otimes\Omega^{1/2}
, because the propagation of polarisation result for
P\in\Psi_{\textnormal{cl}}^{m}(E\otimes\Omega^{1/2})
of real principal type turns out to be described using a connection on
\pi^{*}E
pulled back from
E
. Note that

\dot{T}^{*}X\times 0\subset\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(u)
for every
u\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(E\otimes\Omega^{1/2})
.

The relationship between the polarisation set and the wavefront set is

\WF(u)=\tilde{\pi}(\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(u)\setminus 0)=\{(x,\xi)\in\dot{T}^{*}X:\exists w\in E_{x}\setminus\{0\}~\textnormal{s.t.}~(x,\xi;w)\in\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(u)\},

(3.6)

as shown in Proposition 2.5 of
[
5
]
,
so
\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(u)\subset\tilde{\pi}^{-1}(\WF(u))\cup 0
. Here,
\tilde{\pi}:\pi^{*}E\to T^{*}X
is the induced bundle projection and we have abbreviated
\dot{T}^{*}X\times 0
by
0
.

The following is a basic result on polarisation sets.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
u\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(E\otimes\Omega^{1/2})
. If
A\in\Psi_{\textnormal{cl}}^{m}(E\otimes\Omega^{1/2},F\otimes\Omega^{1/2})
with homogeneous principal symbol
a\in S^{m}(\pi^{*}\Hom(E,F))
, then

a(\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(u))\subset\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(Au),

(3.7)

where
a
acts fibrewise,
a(x,\xi;w)=(x,\xi;a(x,\xi)w)
.
In the special case where
E
and
F
have equal rank and
A
is noncharacteristic at
(x,\xi)\in\dot{T}^{*}X
, the inclusion in the above equation can be replaced by equality over a conical neighbourhood of
(x,\xi)
.
Consequently (returning to the general case),
if for some
(x,\xi)\in T^{*}X
one has

a(x,\xi)(\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(u)|_{(x,\xi)})\neq 0,

(3.8)

then
(x,\xi)\in\WF(Au)
. If (
3.8
) holds for all
(x,\xi)\in\WF(u)
, then
\WF(Au)=\WF(u)
.

Proof.

The first two parts rewrite Proposition 2.7 and Corollary 2.8 of
[
5
]
in the bundle context. For the consequence, by hypothesis, there exists
(x,\xi;w)\in\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(u)
so that
a(x,\xi)w\neq 0
. Eq. (
3.7
) implies

\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(Au)|_{(x,\xi)}\neq 0
, whereupon (
3.6
) implies that
(x,\xi)\in\WF(Au)
. The additional hypothesis now implies
\WF(u)\subset\WF(Au)
, and
\WF(Au)\subset\WF(u)
holds in general.
∎

Lemma
3.1
implies that
\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(u)\subset a^{-1}(\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(Au))\subset a^{-1}(\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(Au)\setminus 0)\cup\ker a
, where

\ker a=\{(x,\xi;w)\in\pi^{*}E:w\in\ker a(x,\xi)\},

(3.9)

giving

\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(u)\subset a^{-1}(\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(Au)\setminus 0)\cup(\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(u)\cap\ker a)\subset a^{-1}(\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(Au)\setminus 0)\cup\ker a,

(3.10)

and hence one obtains the bound

\WF(u)\subset\WF(Au)\cup(\WF(u)\cap\Char A)\subset\WF(Au)\cup\Char A,

(3.11)

which is well-known in its scalar version
[
21
]
.
Note that
\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(Au)
cannot be bounded above in terms of
\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(u)
, because the polarisation set only tracks the strongest singularity (see below). However, the standard result
\WF(Au)\subset\WF(u)
implies

\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(Au)\subset\pi^{*}E|_{\WF(u)}\cup 0.

(3.12)

Examples

1. We compute
\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(\mathrm{id})
, where
\mathrm{id}
is the kernel distribution of the identity operator on
E\otimes\Omega^{1/2}
. For any
A\in\Psi_{\textnormal{cl}}^{m}(E\otimes\Omega^{1/2})
, the operator identity
A\circ\mathrm{id}=\mathrm{id}\circ A
translates to the kernel identity
(A\otimes 1)\mathrm{id}^{\textnormal{knl}}=(1\otimes{\vphantom{A}}^{\star}A)\mathrm{id}^{\textnormal{knl}}
. Thus if
w\in\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(\mathrm{id})|_{(x,\xi;x^{\prime},-\xi^{\prime})}
then
(a(x,\xi)\otimes 1)w=(1\otimes a(x^{\prime},\xi^{\prime})^{*})w
for all
a
, which implies that
w=0
unless

x=x^{\prime}
,
\xi=\xi^{\prime}
, in which case
w\in\mathbb{C}\delta_{x}
, the element of
E_{x}\otimes E_{x}^{*}
identified with
\mathrm{id}_{E_{x}}
. Thus

\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(\mathrm{id})\subset\{(x,\xi;x,-\xi;w)\in(\pi\times\pi)^{*}(E\boxtimes E^{*}):\xi\in\dot{T}^{*}_{x}X,~w\in\mathbb{C}\delta_{x}\}\cup 0.

(3.13)

Conversely, the bound
\WF(u)=\WF(\mathrm{id}\,u)\subset\WF^{\prime}(\mathrm{id}^{\textnormal{knl}})\bullet\WF(u)
(
u\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(E\otimes\Omega^{1/2})
) shows that
\WF(\mathrm{id}^{\textnormal{knl}})
contains the twisted diagonal
\{(x,\xi;x,-\xi):(x,\xi)\in\dot{T}^{*}X\}
and so
\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(\mathrm{id})
must be nontrivial over all such points, giving the result stated in (
1.15
).

2. As a (slightly cautionary) example, consider any
u\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(E\otimes\Omega^{1/2})
and

A\in\Psi^{m}(E\otimes\Omega^{1/2},F\otimes\Omega^{1/2})
with
m>0
. Let

v=u\oplus Au\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}((E\oplus F)\otimes\Omega^{1/2})
. Then

\pr_{E}v=u
and
\pr_{F}=Au
, where
\pr_{E}
and
\pr_{F}
are the
projection maps from
E\oplus F
to
E
and
F
respectively. Applying
Lemma
3.1
, one has

\displaystyle\pr_{E}(\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(v))

\displaystyle\subset\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(\pr_{E}v)=\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(u),

\displaystyle\pr_{F}(\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(v))

\displaystyle\subset\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(\pr_{F}v)=\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(Au).

(3.14)

However, one also has
\begin{pmatrix}A&-1\end{pmatrix}v=0
,
in which
\begin{pmatrix}A&-1\end{pmatrix}\in\Psi^{m}_{\textnormal{cl}}((E\oplus F)\otimes\Omega^{1/2};F\otimes\Omega^{1/2})
has symbol
\begin{pmatrix}a&0\end{pmatrix}
(because
m>0
), giving the further constraint

a\circ\pr_{E}(\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(v))=0
. Overall, this gives the upper bound

\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(u\oplus Au)\subset(\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(u)\cap\ker a)\oplus\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(Au).

(3.15)

Thus some of the singularities in
\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(u)\subset\pi^{*}E
might not
appear in
\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(u\oplus Au)
, because they appear more strongly in
\pi^{*}F
due to the action of
A
.

The second example shows that polarisation sets do not have as convenient a calculus as that of wavefront sets. Even addition requires care.

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose that
u,v\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(E\otimes\Omega^{1/2})
. Then

\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(u+v)=\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(u)+\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(v),

(3.16)

holds fibrewise over the complement of
\WF(u)\cap\WF(v)
.

Proof.

Let
(x,\xi)
belong to at most one of
\WF(u)
or
\WF(v)
, say
\WF(u)
, so
\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(v)|_{(x,\xi)}=0
.
Then
A\in\Psi^{0}_{\textnormal{cl}}(E\otimes\Omega^{1/2};\Omega^{1/2})
makes
A(u+v)
smooth at
(x,\xi)
if and only if
Au
is smooth at
(x,\xi)
, so
\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(u+v)|_{(x,\xi)}=\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(u)|_{(x,\xi)}=\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(u)|_{(x,\xi)}+\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(v)|_{(x,\xi)}
as required.
∎

Our previous example shows that one cannot expect a result of this type above
\WF(u)\cap\WF(v)
. For we have seen that
\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(u\oplus Au)
can be
strictly smaller than
\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(u\oplus 0)+\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(0\oplus Au)
, while, by the same token,

\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(u\oplus 0)
can contain elements outside
\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(u\oplus Au)+\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(0\oplus(-Au))
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Suppose that
u\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(\Omega^{1/2})
and
s\in\Gamma^{\infty}(E)
.
Then
su\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(E\otimes\Omega^{1/2})
and

\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(su)=\{(x,\xi;w)\in\pi^{*}E:(x,\xi)\in\WF(su),~w\in\mathbb{C}s_{x}\}\cup 0.

(3.17)

Proof.

Multiplication by
s
defines
S\in\Psi_{\textnormal{cl}}^{0}(\Omega^{1/2};E\otimes\Omega^{1/2})
with symbol
(x,\xi)\mapsto s_{x}\in\Hom(\mathbb{C},E_{x})
.
Now
\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(u)\setminus 0=\WF(u)\times\mathbb{C}
, so Lemma
3.1
shows that

\{(x,\xi;w)\in\pi^{*}E:(x,\xi)\in\WF(u),~w\in\mathbb{C}s_{x}\}\cup 0\subset\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(su).

(3.18)

Taking intersections with
\tilde{\pi}^{-1}(\WF(su))\cup 0
,
one sees that the right-hand side of (
3.17
) is contained in the left-hand side.
Conversely, we need only show that
\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(su)\setminus 0\subset\mathbb{C}\pi^{*}s
, because it is
automatic that
\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(su)\subset\pi^{*}E|_{\WF(su)}
.
Consider the operator
S^{\wedge}\in\Psi_{\textnormal{cl}}^{0}(E\otimes\Omega^{1/2};(E\otimes E)\otimes\Omega^{1/2})

acting on
v\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(E\otimes\Omega^{1/2})
by
S^{\wedge}v=s\wedge v
, where
(s\wedge v)(f)=v(s\cdot f-f\cdot s)
for
f\in\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}((E\otimes E)^{*}\otimes\Omega^{1/2})
and

(s\cdot f)^{B}=s_{A}f^{AB}
,
(f\cdot s)^{A}=f^{AB}s_{B}
in an obvious index notation. Evidently,

s\wedge(su)=0
, so the polarisation set of
su
at
(x,\xi)
lies in the kernel of
w\mapsto s\wedge w
, i.e.,

\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(su)\setminus 0\subset\mathbb{C}\pi^{*}s
as required.
∎

Bundle pullbacks

If
\psi:X\to Y
is an embedding and
F
is a bundle over
Y
, then one has the usual pullback

\psi^{*}:\Gamma^{\infty}(F)\to\Gamma^{\infty}(\psi^{*}F)
,
(\psi^{*}s)(x)=s(\psi(x))
. As densities also pull back, we can extend this to
\psi^{*}:\Gamma^{\infty}(F\otimes\Omega^{1/2}_{Y})\to\Gamma^{\infty}(\psi^{*}F\otimes\Omega^{1/2}_{X})
.
The restriction of
\psi^{*}
to sections compactly supported in
\psi(X)
is invertible and
the inverse is the pushforward
\psi_{*}:\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}((\psi^{*}F)\otimes\Omega^{1/2}_{X})\to\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(F\otimes\Omega^{1/2}_{Y})
.
Then for
u\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(F\otimes\Omega_{Y}^{1/2})
, we can define a distribution
\psi^{*}u\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(\psi^{*}F\otimes\Omega_{X}^{1/2})

by

(\psi^{*}u)(f)=u(\psi_{*}f),\qquad f\in\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}((\psi^{*}F)^{*}\otimes\Omega^{1/2}_{X})

(3.19)

noting that
(\psi^{*}F)^{*}=\psi^{*}F^{*}
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Defining
\hat{\psi}:T^{*}X\to T^{*}Y
by

\hat{\psi}(x,\xi)=(\psi(x),(D\psi|_{x}^{*})^{-1}\xi)
, one has

\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(\psi^{*}u)=\hat{\psi}^{*}\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(u):=\{(x,\xi;w)\in\pi_{X}^{*}\psi^{*}F:(\hat{\psi}(x,\xi);w)\in\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(u)\},

(3.20)

where
\pi_{X}:T^{*}X\to X
is the bundle projection.

Proof.

For
x\in X
, choose a local framing of
F\otimes\Omega_{Y}^{1/2}
over a chart neighbourhood
(Y_{\kappa},\kappa)
of
\psi(x)
contained in
\psi(X)
. Pulling back via
\psi
, one obtains a local framing of
\psi^{*}F\otimes\Omega_{X}^{1/2}
over a chart neighbourhood
(\psi^{-1}(Y_{\kappa}),\kappa\circ\psi)
of
x
. For
u\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(F\otimes\Omega_{Y}^{1/2})
, the polarisation set
\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(\psi^{*}u)
is determined by considering an intersection over those
A\in\Psi^{0}_{\textnormal{cl}}(\psi^{*}F\otimes\Omega_{X}^{1/2},\Omega_{X}^{1/2})
for which
A\psi^{*}u
is smooth, and one may restrict to those
A
mapping
\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}((\psi^{*}F\otimes\Omega_{Y}^{1/2})|_{\psi^{-1}(Y_{\kappa})})
to itself.
But each such

has a corresponding operator
\psi_{*}A\psi^{*}\in\Psi^{0}_{\textnormal{cl}}(F\otimes\Omega_{Y}^{1/2},\Omega_{Y}^{1/2})
that makes

smooth, and whose matrix principal symbol
is related to that of

(3.21)

because the chart representatives
(\psi_{*}A\psi^{*})_{\kappa}
and

are equal
and where the framings of

and
\psi^{*}F\otimes\Omega_{X}^{1/2}
mentioned above are used to construct the matrices. It follows that
w\in\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(\psi^{*}u)|_{x,\xi}
if and only if

w\in\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(u)|_{\hat{\psi}(x,\xi)}
. ∎

Tensor pullbacks

Let
T^{r,s}_{\mathbb{C}}Y
be the complexified bundle of rank
\binom{r}{s}
tensors over
Y
. Then the tangent mapping
D\psi
determines a map

J^{r,s}=(((D\psi)^{*})^{-1})^{\otimes r}\otimes(D\psi)^{\otimes s}:\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}((T_{\mathbb{C}}^{r,s}X)^{*})\to\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}((T_{\mathbb{C}}^{r,s}Y)^{*})

(3.22)

and then permits the construction of the tensor pullback

\psi_{T}^{*}:\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(T^{r,s}_{\mathbb{C}}Y\otimes\Omega^{1/2}_{Y})\to\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(T^{r,s}_{\mathbb{C}}X\otimes\Omega^{1/2}_{X})

(3.23)

where
\rho_{Y}
is any nonvanishing density on

. One has

(3.25)

Then

\displaystyle\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(\psi^{*}_{T}u)

\displaystyle=\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(A^{r,s}\psi^{*}u)=a^{r,s}\hat{\psi}^{*}\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(u)

\displaystyle=\{(x,D\psi|_{x}^{*}\zeta;a^{r,s}(x,D\psi|_{x}^{*}\zeta)w)\in\pi_{X}^{*}T^{r,s}_{\mathbb{C}}X:(\psi(x),\zeta;w)\in\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(u)\},

which is to say that the wavefront set transforms tensorially under the tensor pullback.
This result extends in an obvious way to bitensorial distributions.

3.2
Propagation of polarisation for systems of real principal type

Dencker’s main achievement in
[
5
]
was to establish a theorem on the propagation
of polarisations that refines the propagation of singularities theorem due to Duistermaat and Hörmander
[
6
]
.

To start, recall that the integral curves of the Hamiltonian vector field
X_{q}
lying in the
zero set
q^{-1}(0)
of

q\in C^{\infty}(T^{*}X;\mathbb{R})

are called
bicharacteristic strips
for
q
.
Also recall that the
radial vector field
on
T^{*}X
is given by
\xi^{\mu}\partial_{\xi_{\mu}}
, and a vector field
X
on
T^{*}X
is
nonradial
at points where
X\notin\mathbb{R}\xi^{\mu}\partial_{\xi_{\mu}}
. Adapting Definition 3.1 of
[
5
]
to the bundle setting, a system
P\in\Psi_{\textnormal{cl}}^{m}(B)
with principal symbol
p(x,\xi)
has
real principal type
at
(y,\eta)\in\dot{T}^{*}X
if there is a symbol
\tilde{p}\in S^{m^{\prime}}(\pi^{*}\Hom(B))
and a scalar symbol
q\in S^{m+m^{\prime}}(T^{*}X)
so that
(\tilde{p}p)(x,\xi)=q(x,\xi)\mathrm{id}_{B_{x}}
in a neighbourhood of
(y,\eta)
on which
q
is real-valued, and
so that either
q(y,\eta)\neq 0
or
X_{q}
is nonradial at
(y,\eta)
(the same is true in a neighbourhood of
(y,\eta)
in either case).

In this situation, let
s_{a}
be a local bundle frame for
B
near
y
. Then
[
5
]
there is a partial connection
D_{P}
on
\pi^{*}B
along the bicharacteristic strips of
q
near
(y,\eta)
defined by

(D_{P}w)^{a}=X_{q}w^{a}+\frac{1}{2}(\{\tilde{p},p\}w)^{a}+{\rm i}\tilde{p}^{a}_{\phantom{a}b}(p^{\textnormal{sub}})^{b}_{\phantom{b}c}w^{c},

(3.27)

where
w=w^{a}\pi^{*}s_{a}\in\Gamma^{\infty}(\pi^{*}B)
,
p^{\textnormal{sub}}
is the subprincipal symbol of
P
, and
\{\tilde{p},p\}
is a Poisson bracket. If
c
is such a bicharacteristic strip, then any line bundle
\mathbb{R}w
spanned by a smooth solution
w
to
D_{P}w=0
along
c
is called a
Hamilton orbit
of
P
. The Hamilton orbits of
P
are independent of the choices made in constructing
D_{P}
(see Section 4 of
[
5
]
).
We can now state Dencker’s result (Theorem 4.2 in
[
5
]
) along with its main consequence.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
P\in\Psi_{\textnormal{cl}}^{m}(B\otimes\Omega^{1/2})
and
u\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(B\otimes\Omega^{1/2})
. Suppose that
P
has real principal type at
(y,\eta)\in\Char(P)\setminus\WF(Pu)\subset\dot{T}^{*}X
. Over a neighbourhood of
(y,\eta)
in
\Char(P)
,
\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(u)
is a union of Hamilton orbits of
P
. Consequently, if
P
has real principal type along a bicharacteristic strip
c
for
P
that does not meet
\WF(Pu)
, then any Hamilton orbit above
c
is either contained in or disjoint from
\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(u)
, and
c
is either contained in or disjoint from
\WF(u)
.

Proof.

Only the consequence requires justification as the rest is already covered in Theorem 4.2 in
[
5
]
. For
a<b
, let
c:[a,b]\to\dot{T}^{*}M
be a bicharacteristic strip not meeting
\WF(Pu)
.
Suppose that a given Hamilton orbit
\lambda\mapsto(c(\lambda),w(\lambda))
above
c
is not disjoint from
\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(u)
, so there exists some
\lambda_{0}\in[a,b]
with

(c(\lambda_{0}),w(\lambda_{0}))\in\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(u)
. Then the set of
\lambda
in
[a,b]
for which

(c(\lambda),w(\lambda))\in\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(u)
is nonempty (as it contains
\lambda_{0}
), closed (because
\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(u)
is), and
relatively open (by the first part of Theorem
3.5
); hence it is equal to
[a,b]

and the whole Hamilton orbit is contained in
\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(u)
.
If
c
is not disjoint from
\WF(u)
then there is a Hamilton orbit through a point of
\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(u)\setminus 0
above
c
which must be completely contained in
\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(u)
; thus
c\subset\WF(u)
.
∎

The last part of Theorem
3.5
is the propagation of singularities theorem for systems of real principal type, generalising the scalar result Theorem 6.1.1 in
[
6
]
(see also Theorem 2.13 of
[
24
]
for a result on the propagation of Sobolev regularity in the bundle context).

The description of Hamilton orbits simplifies when
p=q\mathrm{id}_{B}
, because the choice
\tilde{p}(x,\xi)=\mathrm{id}_{B}
gives

(D_{P}w)^{a}=X_{q}w^{a}+{\rm i}(p^{\textnormal{sub}})^{a}_{\phantom{a}b}w^{b}.

(3.28)

Then the equation
D_{P}w=0
along a bicharacteristic strip
c
for
q
becomes
a system of ordinary differential equations for the frame components of
W=w\circ c
,

\dot{W}^{a}+{\rm i}(p^{\textnormal{sub}}\circ c)^{a}_{\phantom{a}b}W^{b}=0.

(3.29)

In the next section, these results will be applied to operators
P
,
P\otimes 1
,
1\otimes{\vphantom{P}}^{\star}P
and
P\otimes 1-1\otimes{\vphantom{P}}^{\star}P
, where
P
is normally hyperbolic.

4
Polarisation sets of Green operators

4.1
Normally hyperbolic operators

Let
(M,g)
be a globally hyperbolic spacetime with Levi-Civita connection
\nabla
.
Consider first the operator
\Box=g^{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}
on
\Gamma^{\infty}(\Omega^{1/2})
.
In local coordinates, and writing
\partial_{\mu}=\partial/\partial x^{\mu}
,

\displaystyle\Box

\displaystyle=(-g)^{-1/4}\partial_{\mu}g^{\mu\nu}(-g)^{1/2}\partial_{\nu}(-g)^{-1/4}

(4.1)

\displaystyle=g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}+(\partial_{\mu}g^{\mu\nu})\partial_{\nu}+\textnormal{zero order},

(4.2)

so the full symbol is
-g^{\mu\nu}k_{\mu}k_{\nu}+{\rm i}(\partial_{\mu}g^{\mu\nu})k_{\nu}
up to zeroth order terms. The principal symbol is
q(x,k)=-g^{\mu\nu}k_{\mu}k_{\nu}=-g^{-1}(k,k)
, so
\Char(P)=\mathcal{N}
,
while the refined principal symbol is

q^{r}(x,k)=-g^{\mu\nu}k_{\mu}k_{\nu}+{\rm i}(\partial_{\mu}g^{\mu\nu})k_{\nu}+\frac{{\rm i}}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}q}{\partial x^{\mu}\partial k_{\mu}}=q(x,k),

(4.3)

and the subprincipal symbol is thus
q^{\textnormal{sub}}(x,k)=0
. The Hamiltonian vector field determined by
q
is

X_{q}=-2g^{\mu\nu}k_{\nu}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\mu}}+k_{\alpha}k_{\beta}\frac{\partial g^{\alpha\beta}}{\partial x^{\mu}}\frac{\partial}{\partial k_{\mu}},

(4.4)

and its integral curves take the form
c(\lambda)=(x(\lambda),k(\lambda))
, where
x(\lambda)
is an affinely parametrised geodesic and
k=-\tfrac{1}{2}\dot{x}^{\flat}
is invariant under parallel transport along
x
, and cotangent to
x
;
c
is a bicharacteristic strip precisely when
x(\lambda)
is a null geodesic.

Now consider a normally hyperbolic operator
P
on
\Gamma^{\infty}(B\otimes\Omega^{1/2})
, where

B
is a finite-rank complex vector bundle over
M
. Then
(-g)^{-1/4}P(-g)^{1/4}

is a normally hyperbolic operator on
\Gamma^{\infty}(B)
and may be written in Weitzenböck form

(-g)^{-1/4}P(-g)^{1/4}=\Box^{B}+V,\qquad\Box^{B}:=g^{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\mu}^{T^{*}M\otimes B}\nabla_{\nu}^{B}

(4.5)

where
V\in\Gamma^{\infty}(\End(B))
and
\nabla^{B}
is the Weitzenböck connection on
B
. Introduce a local frame
s_{a}
for
B
and define connection
1
-forms obeying

s_{a}\Gamma^{B}_{\mu}{}^{a}_{\phantom{a}b}=\nabla_{\mu}^{B}s_{b}.

(4.6)

A short calculation shows that
(Pf)^{a}=P^{a}_{\phantom{a}b}f^{b}
, where
the operators
P^{a}_{\phantom{a}b}
on
\Gamma^{\infty}(\Omega^{1/2})

are

P^{a}_{\phantom{a}b}=\delta^{a}_{\phantom{a}b}\Box+2g^{\mu\nu}\Gamma^{B}_{\mu}{}^{a}_{\phantom{a}b}\partial_{\nu}+\hat{V}^{a}_{\phantom{a}b}

(4.7)

for some possibly-modified zero-order part
\hat{V}^{a}_{\phantom{a}b}
, and where
\Box
acts on
\Gamma^{\infty}(\Omega^{1/2})
as in (
4.1
). The principal symbol is
p(x,k)=q(x,k)\mathrm{id}_{B_{x}}
, so the subprincipal symbol can be understood as a partial connection as described earlier.

Because
q^{\textnormal{sub}}=0
, and the additional contributions to
P^{a}_{\phantom{a}b}
are of first order, it follows that

(p^{\textnormal{sub}})^{a}_{\phantom{a}b}(x,k)=2{\rm i}g^{\mu\nu}k_{\nu}\Gamma^{B}_{\mu}{}^{a}_{\phantom{a}b}=-{\rm i}(\Gamma_{X_{q}}^{\pi^{*}B}){}^{a}_{\phantom{a}b},

(4.8)

where
X_{q}
is the Hamiltonian vector field obtained above.
In particular the invariantly defined expression
p^{\textnormal{sub}}+{\rm i}\Gamma_{X_{q}}^{\pi^{*}B}
vanishes. Our result here is closely related to the discussion of
P
-compatible connections in Section 2.2, and particularly Theorem 2.8, of
[
24
]
and we have benefited from their presentation (of which ours may be considered a reworking). Similar results have been obtained in more specific contexts, including the Laplace–Beltrami operator on arbitrary rank covectors
[
18
]
, and the
1
-form Maxwell equation and Dirac equations
[
29
,
19
]
. For future reference, we note the formula

{\rm i}p^{\textnormal{sub}}(x,k)=\Gamma^{\pi^{*}B}_{X_{q}}(x,k)=\Gamma^{B}_{\pi_{*}X_{q}}(x)=\Gamma^{B}_{-2k^{\sharp}}(x),

(4.9)

in which indices have been suppressed.

Next, consider the operators
P\otimes 1
and
1\otimes{\vphantom{P}}^{\star}P
acting on

\Gamma^{\infty}((B\boxtimes B^{*})\otimes\Omega^{1/2}_{M\times M})
, where
{\vphantom{P}}{}^{\star}P
is the formal dual to

and has Weitzenböck form

{\vphantom{P}}{}^{\star}P=\Box^{B^{*}}+{\vphantom{V}}^{\star}V,

(4.10)

as is seen by a short calculation.
The principal symbols are

\sigma(P\otimes 1)(x,k,y,l)=q(x,k)\mathrm{id}_{B_{x}\otimes B^{*}_{y}},\qquad\sigma(1\otimes{\vphantom{P}}^{\star}P)(x,k,y,l)=q(y,l)\mathrm{id}_{B_{x}\otimes B^{*}_{y}},

(4.11)

so

\Char P\otimes 1=(\mathcal{N}_{0}\times T^{*}M)\setminus 0,\qquad\Char 1\otimes{\vphantom{P}}^{\star}P=(T^{*}M\times\mathcal{N}_{0})\setminus 0,

(4.12)

and the corresponding scalar symbols are
q\otimes 1
and
1\otimes q
.
As a derivation on
C^{\infty}(T^{*}(M\times M))
, regarded as the completed tensor product

C^{\infty}(T^{*}M)\mathbin{\widehat{\otimes}}C^{\infty}(T^{*}M)
, the symbol
q\otimes 1
has Hamiltonian vector field
X_{q}\otimes 1
, with integral
curves in
\dot{T}^{*}M\times T^{*}M
taking the form
\lambda\mapsto(c(\lambda);y,l)
, where
c
is an integral curve of
X_{q}
. The bicharacteristic strips for

are the integral curves of this type for
which
c
is a bicharacteristic strip for
q
, together with the somewhat trivial bicharacteristic strips
\lambda\mapsto(x,0;y,l)
in
0\times\dot{T}^{*}M
. Similarly, the Hamiltonian vector field

of

generates integral
curves (resp., bicharacteristic strips) in
T^{*}M\times\dot{T}^{*}M
of the form
\lambda\mapsto(x,k;c(\lambda))
, where
c
is an integral curve of
X_{q}
(resp., bicharacteristic strip for
q
)
and trivial bicharacteristic strips
\lambda\mapsto(x,k;y,0)
in
\dot{T}^{*}M\times 0
.
Choosing a frame for
B
and a dual frame for
B^{*}
, the matrices of
subprincipal symbols at
(x,k;y,l)\in T^{*}(M\times M)
are

p^{\textnormal{sub}}(x,k)\otimes\mathrm{id}
and
\mathrm{id}\otimes{\vphantom{p}}^{\star}p^{\textnormal{sub}}(y,l)
.

Finally, we also consider the difference
P\otimes 1-1\otimes{\vphantom{P}}^{\star}P
, with scalar principal symbol
q\otimes 1-1\otimes q
, and characteristic set

\Char(P\otimes 1-1\otimes{\vphantom{P}}^{\star}P)=\{(x,k;x^{\prime},k^{\prime})\in\dot{T}^{*}(M\times M):g^{-1}_{x}(k,k)=g^{-1}_{x^{\prime}}(k^{\prime},k^{\prime})\},

(4.13)

which includes the set
(0\times\mathcal{N})\cup(\mathcal{N}\times 0)
. The Hamiltonian vector field is
X_{q}\otimes 1-1\otimes X_{q}
. Because

is quadratic in momenta, the integral curves in
\dot{T}^{*}M\times\dot{T}^{*}M
may be expressed in the form
\lambda\mapsto(c(\lambda),-c^{\prime}(\lambda))
where
c
and
c^{\prime}
are integral curves of

and

and

There are also degenerate bicharacteristic strips of the form

or
(x,0)\times(-c^{\prime})
, where

and

are bicharacteristic strips of

.

Finally, if

, while

(resp.,

) has real principal type on

4.2
Hamilton orbits for normally hyperbolic and related operators

Continuing with the above notation, let us now describe the Hamilton orbits of the operators

,

The subprincipal symbol of

satisfies
{\rm i}p^{\textnormal{sub}}(c(\lambda))=\Gamma^{\pi^{*}B}_{\dot{c}(\lambda)}(c(\lambda))=\Gamma^{B}_{\dot{x}(\lambda)}(x(\lambda))
(in any frame)

and

(4.14)

along

\nabla^{B}
-parallel transport along the null geodesic

(4.15)

-parallel transport

Turning to the operator

, the Hamilton orbits over

, the

\dot{Z}(\lambda)+{\rm i}(p^{\textnormal{sub}}(c(\lambda))\otimes\mathrm{id})Z(\lambda)=0,

\dot{Z}(\lambda)+(\Gamma_{\dot{x}}^{B}(x(\lambda))\otimes\mathrm{id})Z(\lambda)=0,

(4.17)

(4.18)

Similarly, the Hamilton orbits of

are spanned by

{\rm i}\,{\vphantom{p}}^{\star}p^{\textnormal{sub}}(-c^{\prime}(\lambda))=\Gamma^{B^{*}}_{2k^{\prime}(\lambda)^{\sharp}}(x^{\prime}(\lambda))=-\Gamma^{B^{*}}_{\dot{x}^{\prime}(\lambda)}(x^{\prime}(\lambda))

5
The neutral Proca field

5.1
Closing the gap in MMV

Now specialise to
n=4
dimensions. For any globally hyperbolic spacetime
(M,g)
, let
\Lambda^{p}M
be the bundle of complex-valued
p
-forms on
M
. The Hodge dual
\star:\Lambda^{p}M\to\Lambda^{4-p}M
is defined by

\omega\wedge\star\eta=\frac{1}{p!}\omega_{\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{p}}\eta^{\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{p}}{\rm vol},

(5.1)

where
{\rm vol}
is the metric volume
4
-form, and the codifferential
\delta
is

\delta\omega=(-1)^{\deg\omega}\star^{-1}{\rm d}\star\omega,

(5.2)

for any
p
-form field
\omega
, where
{\rm d}
is the usual exterior derivative. Fixing
m>0
, the

p
-form Klein–Gordon operator is
K^{(p)}=-\delta{\rm d}-{\rm d}\delta+m^{2}
. One has intertwining relations

{\rm d}K^{(p)}=K^{(p+1)}{\rm d}
and
\delta K^{(p+1)}=K^{(p)}\delta
, from which it is easily shown that

{\rm d}E_{K^{(p)}}^{\pm}=E_{K^{(p+1)}}^{\pm}{\rm d}
, and
\delta E_{K^{(p+1)}}^{\pm}=E_{K^{(p)}}^{\pm}\delta
.

The neutral Proca operator on
\Gamma^{\infty}(\Lambda^{1}M)
is

P=-\delta{\rm d}+m^{2},

(5.3)

with Green operators
E_{P}^{\pm}=E_{K^{(1)}}^{\pm}R
, where
R=1-m^{-2}{\rm d}\delta
. To see this,
note that
PR=K^{(1)}=RP
and hence, for
j\in\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(\Lambda^{1}M)
, one has
PE_{P}^{\pm}j=PE_{K^{(1)}}^{\pm}Rj=E_{K^{(1)}}^{\pm}PRj=j
and likewise

E_{P}^{\pm}Pj=E_{K^{(1)}}^{\pm}RPj=j
, while
E_{P}^{\pm}j\subset J^{\pm}(\supp j)
by the same property of
E_{K^{(1)}}^{\pm}
. As already mentioned, the operator
R
is characteristic everywhere on
\dot{T}^{*}M
, preventing the use of standard wavefront set calculus to compute
\WF(E_{P})
. Instead we will do this using Corollary
1.2
.

It will be useful to observe that
\delta E_{P}^{\pm}=E_{K^{(0)}}^{\pm}(\delta-m^{-2}\delta{\rm d}\delta)=m^{-2}E_{K^{(0)}}^{\pm}K^{(0)}\delta=m^{-2}\delta
on
\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(\Lambda^{1}M)
; similarly
E_{P}^{\pm}{\rm d}=m^{-2}{\rm d}
on
C_{0}^{\infty}(M)
, and we obtain

\delta E_{P}=0=E_{P}{\rm d}

(5.4)

as a result. In addition, one has the Weitzenböck formula

(K^{(1)}A)_{\mu}=g^{\alpha\beta}\nabla_{\alpha}\nabla_{\beta}A_{\mu}+(m^{2}\delta_{\mu}^{\phantom{\mu}\nu}+R_{\mu}^{\phantom{\mu}\nu})A_{\nu},

(5.5)

where
\nabla
is the Levi–Civita connection and
R_{\alpha\beta}=R^{\lambda}_{\phantom{\lambda}\alpha\lambda\beta}
is the corresponding Ricci tensor,
with the Riemann tensor defined so that

(\nabla_{\alpha}\nabla_{\beta}-\nabla_{\alpha}\nabla_{\beta})v^{\mu}=R_{\alpha\beta\lambda}^{\phantom{\alpha\beta\lambda}\mu}v^{\lambda}
.
Thus the Weitzenböck connection for
K^{(1)}
is simply the Levi–Civita connection on
\Lambda^{1}M
, which therefore determines the propagation of polarisation for the
\tfrac{1}{2}
-densitised operator

(-g)^{1/4}K^{(1)}(-g)^{-1/4}
on
\Lambda^{1}M\otimes\Omega^{1/2}_{M}
. We come to the main result of this subsection.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
P=-\delta{\rm d}+m^{2}
be the Proca operator on globally hyperbolic spacetime
(M,g)
. Then

\WF(E_{P})=\mathcal{R}.

(5.6)

Proof.

Observe that
R\in\Psi^{2}_{\textnormal{cl}}(\Lambda^{1})
has principal symbol
r(x,k)v=-m^{-2}g^{-1}(k,v)k
at
(x,k)\in T^{*}M
, which is nonvanishing on
\mathcal{N}
(where it is characteristic, as one sees on setting
v=k
).
One has
\WF(E_{P})=\WF((-g)^{1/4}E_{P}(-g)^{-1/4})=\WF(E_{\tilde{K}^{(1)}}\tilde{R})
, where

\tilde{K}^{(1)}=(-g)^{1/4}K^{(1)}(-g)^{-1/4}
and
\tilde{R}=(-g)^{1/4}R(-g)^{-1/4})
.
As
\sigma(\tilde{R})=r
and
\tilde{K}^{(1)}
is normally hyperbolic, we deduce (
5.6
) immediately from Corollary
1.2
.
∎

As discussed in the introduction, this closes a gap in the paper of Moretti, Murro and Volpe
[

]
. Further discussion can be found in the companion paper
[
7
]
, in which it is also shown how the MMV definition of Hadamard states is actually equivalent to the older definition in
[

]
– completing a partial equivalence established in
[

]
.

5.2
The polarisation set of
E_{P}

In this subsection, we build upon Theorem
5.1
to determine the polarisation set of
E_{P}
.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
P=-\delta{\rm d}+m^{2}
be the Proca operator on globally hyperbolic spacetime
(M,g)
. Then

\displaystyle\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(((-g)^{1/4}E_{P}(-g)^{-1/4})^{\textnormal{knl}})

\displaystyle=\{(x,k;x^{\prime},-k^{\prime};w)\in(\pi\times\pi)^{*}\Lambda^{1}M\boxtimes(\Lambda^{1}M)^{*}:

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad(x,k;x^{\prime},-k^{\prime})\in\mathcal{R},~w\in\mathbb{C}k\otimes(k^{\prime})^{\sharp}\}.

(5.7)

The proof involves a few steps. To slightly economise on notation, we write
\rho=(-g)^{1/2}
for the metric density. First, we establish some useful general results, starting with
some a priori bounds on the polarisation set in (
5.7
).

Lemma 5.3
.

For any globally hyperbolic spacetime
(M,g)
, the right-hand side of (
5.7
) is contained in the left-hand side. Moreover, one has

\displaystyle\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}((\rho^{1/2}E_{P}\rho^{-1/2})^{\textnormal{knl}})

\displaystyle\subset\{(x,k;x^{\prime},-k^{\prime};w)\in(\pi\times\pi)^{*}\Lambda^{1}M\boxtimes(\Lambda^{1}M)^{*}:

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad(x,k;x^{\prime},-k^{\prime})\in\mathcal{R},~w\in\mathbb{C}k^{\perp}\otimes((k^{\prime})^{\perp})^{\sharp}\},

(5.8)

where
k^{\perp}
is the subspace of
T^{*}_{x}M
annihilated by
k^{\sharp}
, and
(k^{\prime})^{\perp}

is defined in the same way.

Proof.

Because
E_{P}=(1-m^{-2}{\rm d}\delta)E_{K^{(1)}}
, one has

(\rho^{1/2}E_{P}\rho^{-1/2})^{\textnormal{knl}}=(\rho^{1/2}(1-m^{-2}{\rm d}\delta)\rho^{-1/2}\otimes 1)(E_{\rho^{1/2}K^{(1)}\rho^{-1/2}})^{\textnormal{knl}}.

(5.9)

and consequently
k\otimes(k\cdot\Pi_{x,k}^{x^{\prime},k^{\prime}})\in\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}((\rho^{1/2}E_{P}\rho^{-1/2})^{\textnormal{knl}})|_{(x,k;x^{\prime},-k^{\prime})}
for any
(x,k;x^{\prime},-k^{\prime})\in\dot{T}^{*}(M\times M)
using Lemma
3.1
and Theorem
1.1
(b).
Here,
(k\cdot\Pi_{x,k}^{x^{\prime},k^{\prime}}){}^{\beta}=k^{\alpha}(\Pi_{x,k}^{x^{\prime},k^{\prime}})_{\alpha}^{\phantom{\alpha}\beta}=(k^{\prime})^{\beta}
in index notation,
so this implies the first statement.

Turning to (
5.8
), we already know that
\WF(E_{P})=\mathcal{R}
, so
the polarisation set is only nontrivial over
\mathcal{R}
. Using (
5.4
) one obtains

(\rho^{1/2}\delta\rho^{-1/2}\otimes 1)E_{\rho^{1/2}P\rho^{-1/2}}^{\textnormal{knl}}=0=(1\otimes\rho^{1/2}{\rm d}\rho^{-1/2})E_{\rho^{1/2}P\rho^{-1/2}}^{\textnormal{knl}},

(5.10)

which implies that if
(x,k;x^{\prime},-k^{\prime};w)\in\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}((\rho^{1/2}E_{P}\rho^{-1/2})^{\textnormal{knl}})
then
k^{\alpha}w_{\alpha}^{\phantom{\alpha}\beta}
and
w_{\alpha}^{\phantom{\alpha}\beta}k^{\prime}_{\beta}
both vanish, i.e.,

w\in\mathbb{C}k^{\perp}\otimes((k^{\prime})^{\perp})^{\sharp}
.
∎

Lemma 5.4
.

In any globally hyperbolic spacetime
(M,g)
,

\displaystyle\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}((\rho^{1/2}{\rm d}E_{K^{(0)}}\delta\rho^{-1/2})^{\textnormal{knl}})

\displaystyle=\{(x,k;x^{\prime},-k^{\prime};w)\in(\pi\times\pi)^{*}\Lambda^{1}M\boxtimes(\Lambda^{1}M)^{*}:

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad(x,k;x^{\prime},-k^{\prime})\in\mathcal{R},~w\in\mathbb{C}k\otimes(k^{\prime})^{\sharp}\}.

(5.11)

Proof.

Let
V=(\rho^{1/2}{\rm d}E_{K^{(0)}}\delta\rho^{-1/2})^{\textnormal{knl}}
.
Then
V=QE_{\rho^{1/2}K^{(0)}\rho^{-1/2}}^{\textnormal{knl}}
, where

Q=\rho^{1/2}{\rm d}\rho^{-1/2}\otimes\rho^{1/2}{\vphantom{\delta}}{}^{\star}\delta\rho^{-1/2}

(5.12)

has principal symbol proportional to
k\otimes(k^{\prime})^{\sharp}
at
(x,k;x^{\prime},-k^{\prime})
. As
\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(E_{\rho^{1/2}K^{(0)}\rho^{-1/2}}^{\textnormal{knl}})=(\mathcal{R}\times\mathbb{C})\cup 0
, we deduce that the right-hand side of (
5.11
) is contained in the left-hand side by Lemma
3.1
. Conversely, suppose
(x,k;x^{\prime},-k^{\prime};w)\in\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(V)
with
w\neq 0
, so
(x,k;x^{\prime},-k^{\prime})\in\WF(V)\subset\mathcal{R}
.
As

(\rho^{1/2}{\rm d}\rho^{-1/2}\otimes 1)V=0=(1\otimes\rho^{1/2}{\vphantom{\delta}}{}^{\star}\delta\rho^{-1/2})V,

(5.13)

Lemma
3.1
implies that

k_{\mu}w_{\alpha}^{\phantom{\alpha}\beta}-k_{\alpha}w_{\mu}^{\phantom{\alpha}\beta}=0,\qquad w_{\alpha}^{\phantom{\alpha}\beta}(k^{\prime})^{\nu}-w_{\alpha}^{\phantom{\alpha}\nu}(k^{\prime})^{\beta}=0,

(5.14)

and consequently
w_{\alpha}^{\phantom{\alpha}\beta}\in\mathbb{C}k_{\alpha}(k^{\prime})^{\beta}
,
as is seen e.g., by contracting the above equations respectively with
v
and
v^{\prime}
such that

v^{\alpha}k_{\alpha}=1=v^{\prime}_{\alpha}(k^{\prime})^{\alpha}
(noting that
k,k^{\prime}
are nonzero). This demonstrates the reverse inclusion.
∎

Next, we consider the case of Minkowski spacetime
M=\mathbb{R}^{4}
and
\eta=\textnormal{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1)
with respect to standard inertial coordinates
(x^{0},\ldots,x^{3})
. Then
e^{a}={\rm d}x^{a}
provides a global covector frame and
e^{*}_{a}=\partial/\partial x^{a}
the dual vector frame. Let the operators
S^{a}_{\phantom{a}b}:\Gamma^{\infty}(T^{*}M)\to\Gamma^{\infty}(T^{*}M)
be defined by
S^{a}_{\phantom{a}b}f=\langle\!\langle e^{*}_{b},f\rangle\!\rangle e^{a}
. We will use the facts that (in general)
E_{P}=E_{K^{(1)}}-m^{-2}{\rm d}E_{K^{(0)}}\delta
and (in Minkowski spacetime)
E_{K^{(1)}}fe^{a}=(E_{K^{(0)}}f)e^{a}
, which have the following consequence.

Lemma 5.5
.

In Minkowski spacetime, the following identity holds:

e^{*}_{b}E_{P}e^{b}=3E_{K^{(0)}}.

(5.15)

Consequently,

S^{a}_{\phantom{a}b}E_{P}S^{b}_{\phantom{b}a}-3E_{P}=3m^{-2}{\rm d}E_{K^{(0)}}\delta.

(5.16)

Proof.

First, observe that
e_{b}^{*}E_{K^{(1)}}e^{b}=e_{b}^{*}e^{b}E_{K^{(0)}}=4E_{K^{(0)}}
. Next, one has

e_{b}^{*}{\rm d}E_{K^{(0)}}\delta(fe^{b})=-\nabla_{e_{b}^{*}}E_{K^{(0)}}\nabla_{(e^{b})^{\sharp}}f=-\nabla_{e_{b}^{*}}\nabla_{(e^{b})^{\sharp}}E_{K^{(0)}}f=-\Box E_{K^{(0)}}f=m^{2}E_{K^{(0)}}f,

(5.17)

using translational invariance of
E_{K^{(0)}}
to commute it with the directional derivative.
The first relation follows directly. Then

S^{a}_{\phantom{a}b}E_{P}S^{b}_{\phantom{b}a}=3e^{a}E_{K^{(0)}}e_{a}^{*}=3E_{K^{(1)}}
, from which
the second follows.
∎

Lemma 5.6
.

The equality (
5.7
) holds for Minkowski spacetime.

Proof.

Suppose
(x,k;x^{\prime},-k^{\prime};w)\in\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}((\rho^{1/2}E_{P}\rho^{-1/2})^{\textnormal{knl}})
with
w\neq 0
. Then
(x,k;x^{\prime},-k^{\prime})\in\mathcal{R}
by Theorem
5.1
. Applying Lemma
3.1
to (
5.16
) and using Lemma
5.4
, one finds

e^{*}_{b}(x)^{\alpha}w_{\alpha}^{\phantom{\alpha}\beta}e^{b}(x^{\prime})_{\beta}e^{a}(x)\otimes e^{*}_{a}(x^{\prime})-3w\in\mathbb{C}k\otimes(k^{\prime})^{\sharp},

(5.18)

and consequently

w\in\mathbb{C}\Pi(x,x^{\prime})+\mathbb{C}k\otimes(k^{\prime})^{\sharp},

(5.19)

where we have written
\Pi(x,x^{\prime})=e^{a}(x)\otimes e^{*}_{a}(x^{\prime})
.
However, one also has
\delta E_{P}=0
and hence
k^{\alpha}w_{\alpha}^{\phantom{\alpha}\beta}=0
.
As
k
is null and
k^{\alpha}\Pi_{\alpha}^{\phantom{\alpha}\beta}
is nonvanishing, we conclude that
w\in\mathbb{C}k\otimes(k^{\prime})^{\sharp}
, so the left-hand side of (
5.7
) is contained in the right-hand side (for Minkowski spacetime). The reverse inclusion holds by
Lemma
5.3
, thus establishing (
5.7
) for Minkowski spacetime.
∎

Now, we return to the general case. It is useful to introduce some structures familiar in locally covariant quantum field theory
[
4
,
11
]
. Let
\mathsf{Loc}
be the category whose objects
are
4
-dimensional connected oriented globally hyperbolic spacetimes
{\boldsymbol{M}}=(M,g,{\mathfrak{o}},{\mathfrak{t}})
, where

{\mathfrak{o}}
and
{\mathfrak{t}}
are equivalence classes of nonvanishing
4
-form and timelike
1
-form fields, representing choices of orientation and time-orientation, with equivalence denoting the same (time)-orientation. A
\mathsf{Loc}
-morphism

\psi:(M,g,{\mathfrak{o}},{\mathfrak{t}})\to(M^{\prime},g^{\prime},{\mathfrak{o}}^{\prime},{\mathfrak{t}}^{\prime})={\boldsymbol{M}}^{\prime}
is a smooth embedding
\psi:M\to M^{\prime}
satisfying
g=\psi^{*}g^{\prime}
,
{\mathfrak{o}}=\psi^{*}{\mathfrak{o}}^{\prime}
,
{\mathfrak{t}}=\psi^{*}{\mathfrak{t}}^{\prime}
and such that
\psi(M)
is causally convex in
{\boldsymbol{M}}^{\prime}
. If
\psi(M)
contains a Cauchy surface of
{\boldsymbol{M}}^{\prime}
then
\psi
is a
Cauchy morphism
. An important fact (Proposition 2.4 in
[
10
]
) is that any
{\boldsymbol{M}},{\boldsymbol{M}}^{\prime}
with Cauchy surfaces that are oriented-diffeomorphic can be connected by a chain of
Cauchy morphisms of the form

{\boldsymbol{M}}\leftarrow{\boldsymbol{M}}^{\prime\prime}\rightarrow{\boldsymbol{M}}^{\prime\prime\prime}\leftarrow{\boldsymbol{M}}^{\prime\prime\prime\prime}\rightarrow{\boldsymbol{M}}^{\prime}.

(5.20)

Let
P_{\boldsymbol{M}}
and
K^{(p)}_{\boldsymbol{M}}
denote the Proca and
p
-form Klein–Gordon operators on
{\boldsymbol{M}}\in\mathsf{Loc}
, and similarly let
\rho_{\boldsymbol{M}}
be the metric density on
{\boldsymbol{M}}
. If
\psi:{\boldsymbol{M}}\to{\boldsymbol{N}}
in
\mathsf{Loc}
then the tensor pullback intertwines the operators on
{\boldsymbol{M}}
and
{\boldsymbol{N}}
:

\psi_{T}^{*}P_{\boldsymbol{N}}=P_{\boldsymbol{M}}\psi_{T}^{*},\qquad\psi_{T}^{*}K^{(p)}_{\boldsymbol{N}}=K^{(p)}_{\boldsymbol{M}}\psi_{T}^{*}.

(5.21)

By uniqueness of Green operators, one easily sees that

E_{P_{\boldsymbol{M}}}^{\#}=\psi_{T}^{*}E_{P_{\boldsymbol{N}}}^{\#}\psi_{T*},\qquad E_{K^{(p)}_{\boldsymbol{M}}}^{\#}=\psi_{T}^{*}E_{K^{(p)}_{\boldsymbol{N}}}^{\#}\psi_{T*},

(5.22)

where
\#
stands for
+
,
-
or no symbol and
\psi_{T*}
is the tensorial pushforward.
At the level of kernels, one has the identity

(\rho_{\boldsymbol{M}}^{1/2}E_{P_{\boldsymbol{M}}}^{\#}\rho_{\boldsymbol{M}}^{-1/2})^{\textnormal{knl}}=(\psi\times\psi)^{*}_{T}(\rho_{\boldsymbol{N}}^{1/2}E_{P_{\boldsymbol{N}}}^{\#}\rho_{\boldsymbol{N}}^{-1/2})^{\textnormal{knl}},

(5.23)

which is a bitensorial pullback. Consequently,

\displaystyle\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}((\rho_{\boldsymbol{M}}^{1/2}E_{P_{\boldsymbol{M}}}^{\#}\rho_{\boldsymbol{M}}^{-1/2})^{\textnormal{knl}})

\displaystyle=\{(x,D\psi|_{x}^{*}l;x^{\prime},-D\psi|_{x^{\prime}}^{*}l^{\prime};(D\psi|_{x}^{*}\otimes D\psi|_{x^{\prime}}^{-1})w):

\displaystyle\qquad(\psi(x),l;\psi(x^{\prime}),-l^{\prime};w)\in\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}((\rho_{\boldsymbol{N}}^{1/2}E_{P_{\boldsymbol{N}}}^{\#}\rho_{\boldsymbol{N}}^{-1/2})^{\textnormal{knl}})\}.

(5.24)

In particular,
\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}((\rho_{\boldsymbol{N}}^{1/2}E_{P_{\boldsymbol{N}}}^{\#}\rho_{\boldsymbol{N}}^{-1/2})^{\textnormal{knl}})|_{\psi(M)\times\psi(M)}
covariantly determines
\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}((\rho_{\boldsymbol{M}}^{1/2}E_{P_{\boldsymbol{M}}}^{\#}\rho_{\boldsymbol{M}}^{-1/2})^{\textnormal{knl}})
, and vice versa. Note that this is consistent with the statement of Theorem
5.2
.

Putting these facts together with propagation of polarisation, one obtains the following.

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
{\boldsymbol{M}},{\boldsymbol{M}}^{\prime}\in\mathsf{Loc}
with Cauchy surfaces that are oriented-diffeomorphic. If (
5.7
) fails at some
(x,k;x^{\prime},-k^{\prime})\in\mathcal{R}_{\boldsymbol{M}}
, then for every Cauchy surface
\Sigma
in
{\boldsymbol{M}}^{\prime}
there exists
y\in\Sigma
so that (
5.7
) fails
at
(y,l;y,-l)
for some nonzero null covector
l
at
y
.

Proof.

Suppose that
w\in\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}((\rho_{\boldsymbol{M}}^{1/2}E_{P_{\boldsymbol{M}}}\rho_{\boldsymbol{M}}^{-1/2})^{\textnormal{knl}})|_{(x,k;x^{\prime},-k^{\prime})}\notin\mathbb{C}k\otimes(k^{\prime})^{\sharp}
. As
K^{(1)}_{\boldsymbol{M}}E_{P_{\boldsymbol{M}}}=0
, it follows by
propagation of polarisations for
(\rho_{\boldsymbol{M}}^{1/2})K^{(1)}_{\boldsymbol{M}}\rho_{\boldsymbol{M}}^{-1/2}\otimes 1
that (
5.7
) also fails at
(x^{\prime},k^{\prime};x^{\prime},-k^{\prime})
. As also

K^{(1)}_{\boldsymbol{M}}E_{P_{\boldsymbol{M}}}-E_{P_{\boldsymbol{M}}}K^{(1)}_{\boldsymbol{M}}=0
, by propagation of polarisations for

(\rho_{\boldsymbol{M}}^{1/2})K^{(1)}_{\boldsymbol{M}}\rho_{\boldsymbol{M}}^{-1/2}\otimes 1-1\otimes{\vphantom{}}^{\star}(\rho_{\boldsymbol{M}}^{1/2}K^{(1)}_{\boldsymbol{M}}\rho_{\boldsymbol{M}}^{-1/2})

5.7
) also fails at
(x^{\prime\prime},k^{\prime\prime};x^{\prime\prime},-k^{\prime\prime})
for every
(x^{\prime\prime},k^{\prime\prime})
on the bicharacteristic strip (for
K^{(1)}_{\boldsymbol{M}}
) through
(x^{\prime},k^{\prime})
.
In particular, every Cauchy surface of
{\boldsymbol{M}}
contains a point of this type.
Now consider a chain of Cauchy morphisms of the form (
5.20
) linking
{\boldsymbol{M}}
and
{\boldsymbol{M}}^{\prime}

and deduce successively that (
5.7
) fails at points of the above type on
every Cauchy surface of
{\boldsymbol{M}}^{\prime\prime}
,
{\boldsymbol{M}}^{\prime\prime\prime}
,
{\boldsymbol{M}}^{\prime\prime\prime\prime}
and
{\boldsymbol{M}}^{\prime}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
5.2
.

From Lemma
5.3
, it follows that the only way (
5.7
) can fail is if the fibre of
\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}((\rho_{\boldsymbol{M}}^{1/2}E_{P_{\boldsymbol{M}}}\rho_{\boldsymbol{M}}^{-1/2})^{\textnormal{knl}})
above some point of
\mathcal{R}_{\boldsymbol{M}}
contains elements outside
\mathbb{C}k\otimes(k^{\prime})^{\sharp}
. Suppose this does occur at some point of
\mathcal{R}_{\boldsymbol{M}}
, which – without loss – we can take
to be of the form
(x,k;x,-k)
for
(x,k)\in\mathcal{N}_{\boldsymbol{M}}
by Lemma
5.7
. Let
N
be a region containing
x
whose Cauchy surface is a ball, whereupon (
5.7
) fails for the Proca operator on
{\boldsymbol{M}}|_{N}
and accordingly also fails for any
{\boldsymbol{M}}^{\prime}\in\mathsf{Loc}
with Cauchy surface topology
\mathbb{R}^{3}
by Lemma
5.7
(and because all oriented manifolds with topology
\mathbb{R}^{3}
are oriented diffeomorphic by either the identity or a reflection; see
[
32
]
for a general discussion of the chirality of manifolds). In particular, (
5.7
) fails for Minkowski spacetime, contradicting Lemma

. Hence (
5.7
) holds.
∎

Theorem
5.2
can be contrasted with the following example, which shows that solutions

A\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(T^{*}M)
to the Proca equation themselves need not have polarisation set with fibres parallel to
k
at
(x,k)
. We do this in Minkowski spacetime for simplicity, using inertial coordinates
(t,x^{1},x^{2},x^{3})
. Let
\phi\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{4})
solve

K^{(0)}\phi=0
and fix real constants
v_{i}
(
i=1,2,3
), setting
v=v_{i}{\rm d}x^{i}
, i.e.,

v_{\bullet}=(0,\boldsymbol{v})
in components. Then

A=-(\nabla_{v^{\sharp}}\phi){\rm d}t+(\nabla_{\partial/\partial t}\phi)v

(5.25)

solves
K^{(1)}A=0
and
\nabla^{\mu}A_{\mu}=-\nabla_{\partial/\partial t}\nabla_{v^{\sharp}}\phi+\nabla_{v^{\sharp}}\nabla_{\partial/\partial t}\phi=0
, and therefore solves the Proca equation.
Suppose
(x,k;w)\in\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(\rho^{1/2}A)\setminus 0
, which can only occur when
k
is null,
k_{\bullet}=(\pm\|\boldsymbol{k}\|,\boldsymbol{k})
, because

\WF(A)\subset\Char K^{(1)}=\mathcal{N}
. We have
k^{\mu}w_{\mu}=0
because
\nabla^{\mu}A_{\mu}=0
. Additionally,
u^{\mu}A_{\mu}=0
for any fixed
4
-vector annihilating
{\rm d}t
and
v
, which gives

u^{\mu}w_{\mu}=0
for such
u
. Consequently,
w
lies in the span of
{\rm d}t
and
v
, leading to the conclusion that
w\in\mathbb{C}z
, where

z=-v(k^{\sharp}){\rm d}t+({\rm d}t)(k^{\sharp})v,

(5.26)

i.e.,
z_{\bullet}=(\boldsymbol{v}\cdot\boldsymbol{k},\pm\|\boldsymbol{k}\|\boldsymbol{v})
. As
\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(A)
is nontrivial precisely over
\WF(A)
and the fibres are contained in span of
z
and therefore at most one-dimensional, this shows that

\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(A)=\{(x,k;w):(x,k)\in\WF(A),~w\in\mathbb{C}z\}\cup 0

(5.27)

for solutions of the above type. For given null
k
, any covector
z
such that
k^{\mu}z_{\mu}=0
can be written in the form (
5.26
) for some
\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, reflecting the fact that Proca solutions can display three physical polarisations. Meanwhile, if
\phi
has spacelike compact support then so does
A
, from which one may deduce that
A=E_{P}j
for some
j\in\mathscr{E}^{\prime}(\Lambda^{1}M)
. At first sight it seems paradoxical that the operator
E_{P}
can produce spacelike polarisations when its own polarisation set consists purely of bitensors
k\otimes(k^{\prime})^{\sharp}
with
k
and
k^{\prime}
null. This illustrates that there is no simple composition rule for polarisation sets; it turns out that
\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(E_{P})
is dominated by the contributions from the terms that implement the constraint
\delta A=0
and masks the lower order terms responsible for the propagating degrees of freedom.

6
Conclusion

This paper has determined the polarisation set of Green operators of any normally hyperbolic operator
P

on a finite-rank complex vector bundle over a globally hyperbolic spacetime (Theorem
1.1
).
In particular the fibres of the polarisation set are given in terms of the parallel propagator for
transport according to the Weitzenböck connection of
(-g)^{-1/2}P(-g)^{1/2}
, i.e.,
P
shifted to act on
sections of density weight
\tfrac{1}{2}
lower. This is in agreement with previous investigations of specific operators
[
29
,
19
,
18
]
. The main significance of this result is that it allows the computation of wavefront sets of associated operators via Corollary
1.2
.

As a particular application of these results, we have identified a gap in a recent paper on the Hadamard condition for the quantised Proca field
[
31
]
, which is closed in Theorem
5.1
. Although not strictly necessary for that purpose, we have also computed the polarisation set of the advanced-minus-retarded Green operator for the Proca operator on
\tfrac{1}{2}
-densitised
1
-forms. This indicates at once the strength and limitations of the polarisation set, because the very geometrically natural result of Theorem
5.2

does not reflect the existence of three propagating degrees of freedom, but is dominated by the constraint that removes the unphysical ghost modes. A very interesting question is whether there is a more stratified polarisation set that could see behind the constraint to identify the physical degrees of freedom.

As mentioned in the introduction, the results given here are part of a wider project on the Hadamard condition for a class of Green hyperbolic operators beyond the normally hyperbolic type
[
7
,
8
]
, where
Corollary
1.2
finds applications. Among other things, these papers show that the MMV definition of Hadamard states is in fact equivalent to an older version given in
[
9
]
and also show how the notion of Hadamard states can be extended to charged Proca fields in the presence of an external electromagnetic field and other coupled Proca theories, such as a Proca-scalar system
[
8
]
.
The generalised Hadamard condition that is developed in
[
7
]
is well-suited for the discussion of measurement in quantum field theory
[
12
]
and will be used in future work.

Appendix A
Background on pseudodifferential operators

For convenience, we summarise the main definitions from
[
22
, Sec. 18.1]
, to which the reader is referred for full detail. Certain aspects are elaborated.

Pseudodifferential operators on
\mathbb{R}^{n}

For
m\in\mathbb{R}
, let
S^{m}
be the symbol class on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
defined in
[
22
, Def. 18.1.1]
, i.e., the space of all
a\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
so that

\sup_{(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}}(1+|\xi|)^{m-|\alpha|}|\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\beta}a(x,\xi)|<\infty

(A.1)

for all multi-indices
\alpha,\beta
. The above suprema provide seminorms on
S^{m}
making it into a Fréchet space.
If
U\subsetneq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is open then
S^{m}(U\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the set of
a\in C^{\infty}(U\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
so that
(\phi\otimes 1)a\in S^{m}
for all
\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(U)
. (Note that this definition would not reduce to that of
S^{m}
when
U=\mathbb{R}^{n}
– see pp. 83-84 in
[
22
]
.) One also writes
S^{-\infty}=\bigcap_{m}S^{m}
,
S^{-\infty}(U\times\mathbb{R}^{n})=\bigcap_{m}S^{m}(U\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

For
a\in S^{m}
, the pseudodifferential operator
\Op a
is defined by

((\Op a)u)(x)=\int\frac{{\rm d}^{n}\xi}{(2\pi)^{n}}e^{{\rm i}x\cdot\xi}a(x,\xi)\hat{u}(\xi),\qquad u\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

(A.2)

where
\hat{u}(\xi)=\int{\rm d}^{n}y\,e^{-{\rm i}y\cdot\xi}u(y)
is the Fourier transform, thus yielding a class
\Op S^{m}
of pseudodifferential operators of order
m
. Each
\Op a
extends to a continuous endomorphism of
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
(
[
22
, Thm. 18.1.7]
). Meanwhile, one obtains
\Op a:\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(U)
for

a\in S^{m}(U\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
by

((\Op a)u)(f)=\int_{U\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\rm d}^{n}x\,\frac{{\rm d}^{n}\xi}{(2\pi)^{n}}f(x)e^{{\rm i}x\cdot\xi}a(x,\xi)\hat{u}(\xi),\qquad f\in C_{0}^{\infty}(U).

(A.3)

The composition rule is given by
[
22
, Thm 18.1.8]
: for
a\in S^{m}
,
a^{\prime}\in S^{m^{\prime}}
there is
b\in S^{m+m^{\prime}}
so that

(\Op a)(\Op a^{\prime})=\Op b,

(A.4)

where

b(x,\xi)\sim\sum_{\alpha}\frac{(-{\rm i})^{|\alpha|}}{|\alpha|!}(\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}a)(x,\xi)(\partial_{x}^{\alpha}a^{\prime})(x,\xi)=a(x,\xi)a^{\prime}(x,\xi)-{\rm i}\frac{\partial a}{\partial\xi_{\mu}}(x,\xi)\frac{\partial a^{\prime}}{\partial x^{\mu}}(x,\xi)+\cdots

(A.5)

and the meaning of the asymptotic expansion is explained precisely in
[
22
, Prop. 18.1.3]
.

Pseudodifferential operators on manifolds

A pseudodifferential operator of order
m
on a smooth
n
-manifold
X
is a continuous linear operator

A:C_{0}^{\infty}(X)\to C^{\infty}(X)
so that for every chart
(X_{\kappa},\kappa)
and every
\phi,\psi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\kappa(X_{\kappa}))
, the map

\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\owns u\mapsto\phi(\kappa^{-1})^{*}A\kappa^{*}\psi u
belongs to
\Op S^{m}
. In this case
A
extends
to a continuous linear map from
\mathscr{E}^{\prime}(X)\to\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(X)
, and we write
A\in\Psi^{m}(X)
. If
(X_{\kappa},\kappa)
is a chart and
A\in\Psi^{m}(X)
then one obtains a continuous linear map
A_{\kappa}:C_{0}^{\infty}(\kappa(X_{\kappa}))\to C^{\infty}(\kappa(X_{\kappa}))
by
A_{\kappa}f=(A(f\circ\kappa))\circ\kappa^{-1}
. By
[
22
, Prop. 18.1.9]
and the definition of
\Psi^{m}(X)
this implies that there is
a_{\kappa}\in S^{m}(\kappa(X_{\kappa})\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
(determined modulo
S^{-\infty}(\kappa(X_{\kappa})\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
) so that
A_{\kappa}-\Op a_{\kappa}
is an operator with integral kernel in
C^{\infty}(\kappa(X_{\kappa})\times\kappa(X_{\kappa}))
. We call
\Op a_{\kappa}
a chart representative of
A
for the chart
(X_{\kappa},\kappa)
.

The symbol class
S^{m}(T^{*}X)
is defined to be all
a\in C^{\infty}(T^{*}X)
whose chart representative
a_{\kappa}=a\circ\kappa^{*}\in C^{\infty}(T^{*}\kappa(X_{\kappa}))
satisfies
a_{\kappa}\in S^{m}(\kappa(X_{\kappa})\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
for every local chart
(X_{\kappa},\kappa)
. For
A\in\Psi^{m}(X)
the principal symbol
a\in S^{m}(T^{*}X)
is defined up to addition of symbols in
S^{m-1}(T^{*}X)
by patching together pullbacks of symbols obtained from chart representatives of
A
(which are themselves defined modulo smoothing operators). See
[
22
, pp.85-86]
for the precise description.

Pseudodifferential operators on bundles and the refined principal symbol

If
E
and
F
are smooth finite-rank complex vector bundles over
X
, then

\Psi^{m}(E,F)
is defined as the space of
A:\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(E)\to\Gamma^{\infty}(F)
so that for any local frames
e_{r}
,
f_{s}
of
E
and
F
defined on open
Y\subset X
there are
A^{s}_{\phantom{s}r}\in\Psi^{m}(Y)
so that

Au=f_{s}A^{s}_{\phantom{s}r}u^{r}

(A.6)

on
Y
for all
u=u^{r}e_{r}\in\Gamma^{\infty}(E|_{Y})
(see
[
22
, Def. 18.1.32]
). The notation
\Psi^{m}(E)
is shorthand for
\Psi^{m}(E,E)
. By what has already been said, this framing
results in a matrix of principal symbols
a^{s}_{\phantom{s}r}\in S^{m}(T^{*}Y)/S^{m-1}(T^{*}Y)
.

Suppose one changes frame to
e^{\prime}_{r^{\prime}}
and
f^{\prime}_{s^{\prime}}
so that
e_{r}=e^{\prime}_{r^{\prime}}M^{r^{\prime}}_{\phantom{r^{\prime}}r}
and

f_{s}=f^{\prime}_{s^{\prime}}N^{s^{\prime}}_{\phantom{s^{\prime}}s}
for
M
and
N
of appropriate dimensions with entries in
C^{\infty}(Y)
. Then the matrix representing
A\in\Psi^{m}(E,F)
transforms to
(A^{\prime})^{s^{\prime}}_{\phantom{s^{\prime}}r^{\prime}}
obeying

N^{s^{\prime}}_{\phantom{s^{\prime}}s}A^{s}_{\phantom{s}r}=(A^{\prime})^{s^{\prime}}_{\phantom{s^{\prime}}r^{\prime}}M^{r^{\prime}}_{\phantom{r^{\prime}}r}.

(A.7)

To compute the transformed matrix of principal symbols, we use the composition formulae (
A.4
) and (
A.5
) applied to chart representatives
in a chart
(X_{\kappa},\kappa)
with
X_{\kappa}\subset Y
.
This yields

\displaystyle N^{s^{\prime}}_{\phantom{s^{\prime}}s}(\kappa^{-1}(x))(a^{s}_{\phantom{s}r})_{\kappa}(x,\xi)

\displaystyle=((a^{\prime})^{s^{\prime}}_{\phantom{s^{\prime}}r^{\prime}})_{\kappa}(x,\xi)M^{r^{\prime}}_{\phantom{r^{\prime}}r}(x)-{\rm i}(\partial_{\xi_{\mu}}((a^{\prime})^{s^{\prime}}_{\phantom{s^{\prime}}r^{\prime}})_{\kappa}(x,\xi))(\partial_{x^{\mu}}M^{r^{\prime}}_{\phantom{r^{\prime}}r}\circ\kappa^{-1})(x)

(A.8)

modulo
S^{m-2}(T^{*}\kappa(Y))
, or more compactly

N_{\kappa}a_{\kappa}=a^{\prime}_{\kappa}M_{\kappa}-{\rm i}(\partial_{\xi_{\mu}}a^{\prime}_{\kappa})(\partial_{x^{\mu}}M_{\kappa}),

(A.9)

with entries modulo
S^{m-2}(T^{*}\kappa(Y))
, where
N_{\kappa}=N\circ\kappa^{-1}
,
M_{\kappa}=M\circ\kappa^{-1}
. Consequently, the matrices of principal symbols obey

N^{s^{\prime}}_{\phantom{s^{\prime}}s}a^{s}_{\phantom{s}r}=(a^{\prime})^{s^{\prime}}_{\phantom{s^{\prime}}r^{\prime}}M^{r^{\prime}}_{\phantom{r^{\prime}}r}
modulo
S^{m-1}(T^{*}Y)
, which shows that there is a well-defined frame-independent
principal symbol
a\in S^{m}(\pi^{*}\Hom(E,F))
, where
\pi^{*}\Hom(E,F)
is the pullback of the homomorphism bundle
\Hom(E,F)
, with typical fibre
\Hom(E,F)_{x}=\Hom(E_{x},F_{x})
, by the projection
\pi:T^{*}X\to X
. The second term on the right-hand side of (
A.8
) shows that one cannot expect a simple transformation for lower order symbols in general. As mentioned in section
2
, the situation is better for operators
A\in\Psi^{m}(\Omega^{1/2})
, for which the refined principal symbol
a^{\textnormal{r}}
is defined modulo
S^{m-2}(T^{*}X)
in
S^{m}(T^{*}X)
.

If one now considers
A\in\Psi^{m}(E\otimes\Omega^{1/2},F\otimes\Omega^{1/2})
, local framings
of
E
and
F
result in a matrix with entries
A^{s}_{\phantom{s}r}\in\Psi^{m}(\Omega^{1/2})

and refined principal symbols with chart representatives obeying the matrix equation (
2.6
). A change of frame as before produces a modified matrix
(a^{\prime})^{\textnormal{r}}
. To compute this, note that

\displaystyle N_{\kappa}\frac{\partial^{2}a_{\kappa}}{\partial x^{\mu}\partial\xi_{\mu}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial^{2}(N_{\kappa}a_{\kappa})}{\partial x^{\mu}\partial\xi_{\mu}}-\frac{\partial N_{\kappa}}{\partial x^{\mu}}\frac{\partial a_{\kappa}}{\partial\xi_{\mu}}=\frac{\partial^{2}(a^{\prime}_{\kappa}M_{\kappa})}{\partial x^{\mu}\partial\xi_{\mu}}-\frac{\partial N_{\kappa}}{\partial x^{\mu}}\frac{\partial a_{\kappa}}{\partial\xi_{\mu}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial^{2}a^{\prime}_{\kappa}}{\partial x^{\mu}\partial\xi_{\mu}}M_{\kappa}+\frac{\partial a^{\prime}_{\kappa}}{\partial\xi_{\mu}}\frac{\partial M_{\kappa}}{\partial x^{\mu}}-\frac{\partial N_{\kappa}}{\partial x^{\mu}}\frac{\partial a_{\kappa}}{\partial\xi_{\mu}},

(A.10)

with entries agreeing modulo
S^{m-2}(T^{*}\kappa(Y))
. Consequently, in the same sense,

N_{\kappa}a_{\kappa}^{\textnormal{r}}=(a^{\prime}_{\kappa})^{\textnormal{r}}M_{\kappa}-\frac{{\rm i}}{2}\left(\frac{\partial a^{\prime}_{\kappa}}{\partial\xi_{\mu}}\frac{\partial M_{\kappa}}{\partial x^{\mu}}+\frac{\partial N_{\kappa}}{\partial x^{\mu}}\frac{\partial a_{\kappa}}{\partial\xi_{\mu}}\right).

(A.11)

(More precisely, we should write
(M_{\kappa})\circ\pi
; we suppress this to simplify the notation.)
Now specialise to the case in which
F=E
, with local framing
e_{r}
used for
f_{r}
so that
N=M
, and assume the principal symbol is
a=b\mathrm{id}_{E}
(modulo
S^{m-1}(E\otimes\Omega)
). In this case, (
A.11
) becomes

M_{\kappa}a_{\kappa}^{\textnormal{r}}=(a^{\prime}_{\kappa})^{\textnormal{r}}M_{\kappa}-{\rm i}\frac{\partial b_{\kappa}}{\partial\xi_{\mu}}\frac{\partial M_{\kappa}}{\partial x^{\mu}}\mathrm{id}.

(A.12)

By comparison, suppose that
\nabla^{E}
is any connection on
E
, and define connection
1
-forms with respect to the framing
e_{r}
by
\Gamma^{E}_{V}e_{r}=\nabla^{E}_{V}e_{r}
for any
V\in\Gamma^{\infty}(TM)
. The pullback connection
\nabla^{\pi^{*}E}
on
\pi^{*}E
is defined so that
\nabla_{W}^{\pi^{*}E}\pi^{*}s|_{(x,k)}=\nabla_{\pi_{*}W}^{E}s|_{x}
for
(x,k)\in T^{*}M
,
s\in\Gamma^{\infty}(E)
and
W\in T_{(x,k)}T^{*}M
and has connection
1
-forms
obeying
\Gamma^{\pi^{*}E}_{W}=\Gamma^{E}_{\pi_{*}W}\circ\pi
relative to the
framing
\pi^{*}e_{r}
. If an alternative framing
e^{\prime}_{r}
is invoked, with

e_{r}=e^{\prime}_{r^{\prime}}M^{r^{\prime}}_{\phantom{r^{\prime}}r}
, then the
chart representatives of
\Gamma^{E}
and
(\Gamma^{E})^{\prime}
obey

M_{\kappa}(\Gamma_{V})_{\kappa}=(\Gamma^{E}_{V})^{\prime}_{\kappa}M_{\kappa}+V^{\mu}\frac{\partial M_{\kappa}}{\partial x^{\mu}}\mathrm{id}.

(A.13)

and the connection
1
-forms for
\pi^{*}E
transform in a similar way.
It follows that in this case,
a^{r}+{\rm i}\Gamma^{\pi^{*}E}_{W}
is invariantly defined modulo
S^{m-2}(\pi^{*}\Hom(E))
for
V^{\mu}=(\pi_{*}W)^{\mu}=\partial b_{\kappa}/\partial\xi_{\mu}
, which is the projection via
\pi
of the Hamiltonian vector field

X_{b}=\frac{\partial b_{\kappa}}{\partial\xi_{\mu}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\mu}}-\frac{\partial b_{\kappa}}{\partial\xi_{\mu}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi^{\mu}}

(A.14)

determined by
b
. We conclude that
a^{r}+{\rm i}\Gamma^{\pi^{*}E}_{X_{b}}
is invariantly defined modulo
S^{m-2}(\pi^{*}\Hom(E))
.

Polyhomogeneous operators and the subprincipal symbol

For
m\in\mathbb{C}
, a symbol
a\in S^{m}
is called
polyhomogeneous
(of step size
1
) if

a
can be realised as an asymptotic expansion

a\sim\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}a_{m-j},

(A.15)

where each
a_{k}\in S^{k}
is
k
-homogeneous away from the origin in
\xi
, i.e.,
a_{k}(x,t\xi)=t^{k}a_{k}(x,\xi)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
t>1
,
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{1}
where
B_{1}
is the closed unit ball. In this case, we write
a\in S^{m}_{\textnormal{phg}}
, and describe
\Op a
as polyhomogeneous. (This notion can be defined when
m\in\mathbb{C}
but we only use the real case.)

If
A\in\Psi^{m}(X)
, and all the chart representatives
A_{\kappa}
of
A
are polyhomogeneous, then we write

A\in\Psi^{m}_{\textnormal{phg}}(X)
. In this case, the principal symbol
a\in S^{m}(T^{*}X)
of
A
can be defined uniquely by choosing a
m
-homogeneous representative of the principal symbol defined above; similarly, for
A\in\Psi_{\textnormal{phg}}^{m}(\Omega^{1/2})
, we again have a unique homogeneous principal symbol
a\in S^{m}(T^{*}X)
, but in addition the subprincipal symbol may be defined as the unique
(m-1)
-homogeneous
a^{\textnormal{sub}}\in S^{m-1}(T^{*}X)
so that
a^{\textnormal{r}}=a+a^{\textnormal{sub}}
modulo
S^{m-2}(T^{*}X)
.

Similarly, for
A\in\Psi^{m}(E,F)
(resp.,
A\in\Psi^{m}(E\otimes\Omega^{1/2},F\otimes\Omega^{1/2})
),
one says that
A
is polyhomogeneous if all local frames
e_{r}
for
E
and
f_{s}
for
F
defined on open
Y\subset X
induce matrices
A^{r}_{\phantom{r}s}\in\Psi_{\textnormal{phg}}^{m}(Y)
(resp.,
\Psi_{\textnormal{phg}}^{m}(\Omega^{1/2}_{Y}))
,
whereupon one writes
A\in\Psi_{\textnormal{phg}}^{m}(E,F)
(resp.,
A\in\Psi_{\textnormal{phg}}^{m}(E\otimes\Omega^{1/2},F\otimes\Omega^{1/2})
). Taking the principal or subprincipal symbols one obtains
matrices with entries that are
m
-homogeneous elements of
S^{m}(T^{*}Y)
or
(m-1)
-homogeneous elements of
S^{m-1}(T^{*}Y)
. The principal symbol is thus uniquely defined in
S^{m}(\pi^{*}\Hom(E,F))
.
In the case of
A\in\Psi_{\textnormal{phg}}^{m}(E\otimes\Omega^{1/2})
with principal symbol
a=b\mathrm{id}_{E}
, and
connection
1
-forms
\Gamma^{E}
from a connection on
E
as above,

a^{\textnormal{r}}+{\rm i}\Gamma^{\pi^{*}E}_{X_{b}}
and hence
a^{\textnormal{r}}-a+{\rm i}\Gamma^{\pi^{*}E}_{X_{b}}
are invariantly defined in
S^{m-1}(\pi^{*}\Hom(E,F))
. In this sense the subprincipal symbol
a^{\textnormal{sub}}=a^{\textnormal{r}}-a
defines connection
1
-forms
{\rm i}a^{\textnormal{sub}}
for a partial connection along the integral curves of
X_{b}
.
See also
[
24
]
, Proposition 2.4 for a microlocal version of a similar statement.

Classical pseudodifferential operators

By
\Psi_{\textnormal{cl}}^{m}(X)
we denote the space of
classical pseudodifferential operators
on
X
, given by all properly supported operators
A\in\Psi^{m}_{\textnormal{phg}}(X)
. Each
A\in\Psi_{\textnormal{cl}}^{m}(X)
extends to a map
\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(X)\to\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(X)
; similarly,
\Psi^{m}_{\textnormal{cl}}(E,F)
denotes the properly supported elements of
\Psi^{m}_{\textnormal{phg}}(E,F)
.

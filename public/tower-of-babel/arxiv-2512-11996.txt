Isolated Points on Modular Curves of Prime-Power Level
Chris Calger

Abstract
. An isolated point on an algebraic curve is a closed point not belonging to a collection of points of the same degree parametrized by 𝐏 1 \mathbf{P}^{1} or a positive rank abelian subvariety of the curve’s Jacobian. We study the sets of j j -invariants, in extensions of bounded degree, that arise as the j j -invariant of an isolated point on a modular curve. We obtain finiteness results on these sets for families of modular curves with prime-power level. This is related to recent work of Bourdon and Ejder, who classified rational j j -invariants of isolated points on the families X 1 ​ ( n ) X_{1}(n) and X 0 ​ ( n ) X_{0}(n) , for n n a prime power.



1.
Introduction

The closed points of a smooth, projective curve
C
over a number field
k
are in bijection with
\Gal_{k}
-orbits of points in
C(\overline{k})
, where
\Gal_{k}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{.}\hbox{.}}}=\Gal(\overline{k}/k)
is the absolute Galois group.
The degree of a closed point corresponds to the size of the associated Galois-orbit.
A closed point
x\in C
of degree
d
is said to be
isolated
1
1

1

See Section
2.6
for a precise definition.
if it does not belong to a collection of degree
d
closed points parametrized by
\mathbf{P}^{1}
or a positive rank abelian subvariety of the curve’s Jacobian.

Faltings’s Theorem
[
8
]
guarantees that if
C
has genus strictly greater than
1
, then
C
has finitely many degree
1
points.
Such a curve
C
has infinitely many points of arbitrary degree.
However, it was shown in
[
1
]
that, as a consequence of Faltings’s Theorem on rational points on subvarieties of abelian varieties
[
9
]
,

C
has only finitely many isolated points.
Because of this, the study of isolated points on curves generalizes the study of rational points in a natural way.

In this paper, we study isolated points on modular curves, which parametrize elliptic curves equipped with specific structure.
If
E
is an elliptic curve defined over a number field
k
, the action of
\Gal_{k}
on the
n
-torsion subgroups
E[n]
, for positive integers
n
, induces representations

\displaystyle\rho_{E,n}

\displaystyle:\Gal_{k}\to\GL_{2}(\mathbf{Z}/n\mathbf{Z}),

\displaystyle\rho_{E,\ell^{\infty}}

\displaystyle:\Gal_{k}\to\GL_{2}(\mathbf{Z}_{\ell})=\varprojlim\GL_{2}(\mathbf{Z}/\ell^{n}\mathbf{Z}),\text{ and}

\displaystyle\rho_{E}

\displaystyle:\Gal_{k}\to\GL_{2}(\widehat{\mathbf{Z}})=\varprojlim\GL_{2}(\mathbf{Z}/n\mathbf{Z}).

If
H
is an open subgroup of
\GL_{2}(\mathbf{Z}_{\ell})
, the modular curve
X_{H}
is a curve whose non-cuspidal points parametrize elliptic curves with

\rho_{E,\ell^{\infty}}(\Gal_{k})

contained in
H
. To a closed point
x\in X_{H}
, we associate an algebraic number
j(x)
called the
j
-invariant of
x
, which is the image of
x
under the natural map to the coarse moduli space of elliptic curves.

Isolated points on modular curves are related to a number of open questions concerning the arithmetic of elliptic curves.
For a fixed non-CM elliptic curve
E
over a number field
k
, Serre showed
[
19
]
that the mod-
\ell
Galois representation
\rho_{E,\ell}
is surjective for large primes
\ell
. Serre asked
[
19
, Section 4.3]
if this result in the case of
k=\mathbf{Q}
can be made uniform—that is, if there is prime
L
such that
\rho_{E,\ell}
is surjective for all non-CM elliptic curves
E/\mathbf{Q}
and all primes
\ell>L
. This prime
L
has since been conjectured by Zywina
[
24
, Conjecture 1.2]
and Sutherland
[
22
, Conjecture 1.1]
to be
37
. We will refer to this conjecture as
Serre’s uniformity problem
.
It remains unknown if there exists
a prime
\ell>37
and non-CM elliptic curve
E/\mathbf{Q}
with
\rho_{E,\ell}(\Gal_{k})
conjugate to
C_{\textrm{ns}}^{+}(\ell)
, the normalizer of a non-split Cartan subgroup of
\GL_{2}(\mathbf{Z}/\ell\mathbf{Z})
.
If such a prime

exists, then

gives rise to a representative for a degree one point on the modular curve
X_{C_{\textrm{ns}}^{+}(\ell)}
. This curve has genus greater than
1
, so Faltings’s Theorem implies there are finitely many degree one points on
X_{C_{\textrm{ns}}^{+}(\ell)}
, all of which are isolated.

The problem of bounding the non-surjective mod-
\ell
images of Galois is situated within a broader effort, namely Mazur’s “Program B”
[
14
]
, which asks, given a number field
k
, for a classification of the images
\rho_{E}(\Gal_{k})
in
\GL_{2}(\widehat{\mathbf{Z}})
for elliptic curves
E/k
.
Work in this paper is related to the
\ell
-adic case of the program, which is the problem of classifying images
\rho_{E,\ell^{\infty}}(\Gal_{k})
in
\GL_{2}(\mathbf{Z}_{\ell})
.
Determining the
\ell
-adic image of Galois attached to an elliptic curve

helps us to understand closed and isolated points with
j
-invariant

on modular curves, and vice-versa.

For each positive integer
n
, the modular curve

is a curve whose non-cuspidal points parametrize elliptic curves with a point of order

[

showed that, assuming an affirmative answer to Serre’s uniformity problem, there are finitely many

\mathbf{Q}
corresponding to isolated points on
X_{1}(n)

varies over all positive integers.

It is natural to ask for which other families of curves we can obtain similar finiteness results.
In this paper, we examine the set of
j
-invariants in an extension of bounded degree that arise as the
j
-invariant of an isolated point on a modular curve of prime-power level.

Theorem 1.1
.

Fix a prime
\ell\in\mathbf{N}
and define

\mathcal{I}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{.}\hbox{.}}}=\left\{\,x\in X_{H}\,\middle|\,x\text{ is isolated and }H\leq\GL_{2}(\mathbf{Z}_{\ell})\text{ an open subgroup}\,\right\}.

For every

j
-invariants of degree
d
in
j(\mathcal{I})
.

The proof uses group theory techniques and results of Cadoret and Tamagawa
[
4

and Bourdon, Ejder, Liu, Odumodu, and Viray
[
1

to obtain a bound

\ell
-power level modular curve with

-invariant of degree

\ell
-power level dividing

This method of reducing the level of modular curve on which an isolated point must appear is related to

M

We also use results of Terao

to describe the degrees of closed points

2.
Background

2.1.
Notation and conventions

Let
k
be a number field and
\overline{k}
a fixed algebraic closure of
k
.
We denote
\Gal_{k}
to be the absolute Galois group
\Gal(\overline{k}/k)
.
Let
\mathbf{N}
denote the set of natural numbers, i.e. the set of positive integers
\{\,1,2,3,\dots\,\}
. For
n\in\mathbf{N}
, we denote by
\phi(n)
the number of integers
1\leq m\leq n
with
(m,n)=1
.

By a
nice
curve, we mean a smooth, projective, geometrically integral curve.
For a
k
-scheme
X
and a field extension
K
of
k
, we denote
X_{K}
to be the base change
X\times_{\Spec{k}}\Spec{K}
.

2.2.
Elliptic curves

Let
E
be an elliptic curve over a number field
k
. For
n\in\mathbf{N}
, we denote
E[n]=E(\overline{k})[n]
to be the subgroup of
E(\overline{k})
consisting of
n
-torsion points—that is, points with finite order dividing
n
. The group
E[n]
is the kernel of the multiplication-by-
n
map
[n]:E\to E
and is isomorphic to
(\mathbf{Z}/n\mathbf{Z})\times(\mathbf{Z}/n\mathbf{Z})
.

We say an elliptic curve
E/k
has
complex multiplication (CM)
if
\End{E_{\overline{k}}}
is not isomorphic to the integers—we will not add the distinction of an elliptic curve having potential CM, as some authors do. We say a
j
-invariant
j\in\overline{\mathbf{Q}}
is CM if there exists a CM elliptic curve
E
with
j
-invariant equal to
j
. In this paper, we are interested in showing there are finitely many
j
-invariants, with a certain property, in number fields of a fixed bounded degree. Our main techniques to do so work only for non-CM
j
-invariants. However, results from class field theory
allow us to restrict to the case of non-CM
j
-invariants.

Indeed, if
E/k
has CM, then by
[
20
, Corollary 9.4]
, there exists an imaginary quadratic field
K

and an order
\mathcal{O}
of conductor
f
in
K
such that
\End(E_{\overline{k}})\simeq\mathcal{O}
.
By
[
5
, Theorem 11.1]
, we have
[\mathbf{Q}(j(E)):\mathbf{Q}]=[K(j(E)):K]=h(\mathcal{O})
, where
h(\mathcal{O})
is the class number.
There are only finitely many
K
of a given class number by
[
12
]

and so, by
[
5
, Theorem 7.24]
, there are only finitely many imaginary quadratic orders of a given class number.
Each order is associated to only finitely many CM
j
-invariants by
[
5
, Corollary 10.20]
. These results imply the following well-known theorem.

Theorem 2.1
.

For every
d\in\mathbf{N}
, there are only finitely many CM
j
-invariants in number fields of degree
d
.

2.3.
Profinite constructions

For a prime
\ell\in\mathbf{N}
, the ring of
\ell
-adic integers
, denoted
\mathbf{Z}_{\ell}
, is the inverse limit
\varprojlim\mathbf{Z}/\ell^{n}\mathbf{Z}
taken over
n\in\mathbf{N}
with homomorphisms the evaluation maps
\mathbf{Z}/\ell^{j}\mathbf{Z}\to\mathbf{Z}/\ell^{i}\mathbf{Z}
for
i\leq j
. The
profinite integers
, denoted
\widehat{\mathbf{Z}}
, are the inverse limit
\varprojlim\mathbf{Z}/n\mathbf{Z}
taken over the directed set consisting of natural numbers ordered by divisibility. There is a ring isomorphism

\widehat{\mathbf{Z}}=\varprojlim\mathbf{Z}/n\mathbf{Z}\simeq\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\in\mathbf{N}\\
\text{prime}\end{subarray}}\varprojlim\mathbf{Z}/\ell^{n}\mathbf{Z}=\prod_{\ell}\mathbf{Z}_{\ell}.

For an elliptic curve
E
over a number field
k
and a prime
\ell\in\mathbf{N}
, we define the
\ell
-adic Tate module of
E
to be the inverse limit
T_{\ell}(E)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{.}\hbox{.}}}=\varprojlim E[\ell^{n}]
taken over
n\in\mathbf{N}
with homomorphisms the maps
[\ell^{j-i}]:E[\ell^{j}]\to E[\ell^{i}]
for
i\leq j
. As a
\mathbf{Z}_{\ell}
-module,
T_{\ell}(E)
has the following structure:

T_{\ell}(E)=\varprojlim E[\ell^{n}]\simeq\varprojlim\left(\mathbf{Z}/\ell^{n}\mathbf{Z}\times\mathbf{Z}/\ell^{n}\mathbf{Z}\right)=\varprojlim\left(\mathbf{Z}/\ell^{n}\mathbf{Z}\right)\times\varprojlim\left(\mathbf{Z}/\ell^{n}\mathbf{Z}\right)=\mathbf{Z}_{\ell}\times\mathbf{Z}_{\ell}.

Following a similar construction to
\widehat{\mathbf{Z}}
, we also define the
adelic Tate module of
E
to be the inverse limit
T(E)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{.}\hbox{.}}}=\varprojlim E[n]
so that

T(E)\simeq\prod_{\ell}T_{\ell}(E)\simeq\prod_{\ell}\mathbf{Z}_{\ell}\times\mathbf{Z}_{\ell}\simeq\widehat{\mathbf{Z}}\times\widehat{\mathbf{Z}}.

We will also study the profinite group
\GL_{2}(\widehat{\mathbf{Z}})\simeq\varprojlim\GL_{2}(\mathbf{Z}/n\mathbf{Z})
.
For each
n\in\mathbf{N}
, there exists a natural projection map
\pi_{n}:\GL_{2}(\widehat{\mathbf{Z}})\to\GL_{2}(\mathbf{Z}/n\mathbf{Z})
induced by the inverse limit. Similarly, by abuse of notation, for a prime number
\ell
, we denote
\pi_{\ell^{n}}
to be the natural projection map
\GL_{2}(\mathbf{Z}_{\ell})\to\GL_{2}(\mathbf{Z}/\ell^{n}\mathbf{Z})
.
For a subgroup
H\leq\GL_{2}(\widehat{\mathbf{Z}})
, we denote
H(n)
to be
\pi_{n}(H)
. In general, we have
H\leq\pi_{n}^{-1}(H(n))
. If
H\leq\GL_{2}(\widehat{\mathbf{Z}})
is an open subgroup, then there exists a positive integer
n\in\mathbf{N}
such that
\ker\pi_{n}\leq H
. We define the
level of
H
to be the least such positive integer. If
H
has level
n
, then
H=\pi_{n}^{-1}(H(n))
and the index of
H
in
\GL_{2}(\widehat{\mathbf{Z}})
(which is finite because
H
is open) is equal to the index of
H(n)
in
\GL_{2}(\mathbf{Z}/n\mathbf{Z})
.

In Section
5
, we will consider specific subgroups
H\leq\GL_{2}(\widehat{\mathbf{Z}})
with
\det(H)=\widehat{\mathbf{Z}}^{\times}
. We will refer to these subgroups using the notation of Rouse, Sutherland, and Zureick-Brown
[
17
, Section 2.4]
, which assigns to each subgroup
H
a label

N.i.g.n

where
N
is the level of
H
,
i
is the index of
H
in
\GL_{2}(\widehat{\mathbf{Z}})
,
g
is the genus of the modular curve
X_{H}
, and
n
is an integer which specifies
H
among other subgroups having the same level, index, and genus.

2.4.
Galois representations

For every
\sigma\in\Gal_{k}
and
P\in E
, we denote by
P^{\sigma}
the image of the pair
(P,\sigma)
under the natural (right) action
E\times\Gal_{k}\to E
. For every
P\in E[n]
, we have
[n]P=O
, where
O
is the base point of
E
. For every
\sigma\in\Gal_{k}
,

[n]\left(P^{\sigma}\right)=\left([n]P\right)^{\sigma}=O^{\sigma}=O,

so the natural action of
\Gal_{k}
on
E[n]
is well-defined. This action defines a representation

\rho_{E,n}:\Gal_{k}\to\Aut\left(E[n]\right)

called the
mod-
n
Galois representation associated to
E
.
Let
\alpha:E[n]\to(\mathbf{Z}/n\mathbf{Z})\times(\mathbf{Z}/n\mathbf{Z})
be an isomorphism of
(\mathbf{Z}/n\mathbf{Z})
-modules. The
mod-
n
Galois representation associated to
E
and
\alpha
is the composition of the above representation with the isomorphism
\Aut(E[n])\simeq\GL_{2}(\mathbf{Z}/n\mathbf{Z})
induced by
\alpha
and is denoted

\rho_{E,n,\alpha}:\Gal_{k}\to\Aut\left(E[n]\right)\xrightarrow{\simeq}\GL_{2}(\mathbf{Z}/n\mathbf{Z}).

If
\beta:E[n]\to(\mathbf{Z}/n\mathbf{Z})\times(\mathbf{Z}/n\mathbf{Z})
is another isomorphism of groups, then
\rho_{E,n,\alpha}(\Gal_{k})
and
\rho_{E,n,\beta}(\Gal_{k})
are conjugate in
\GL_{2}(\mathbf{Z}/n\mathbf{Z})
.

Suppose
\ell\in\mathbf{N}
is a prime and
\alpha:T_{\ell}(E)\to\mathbf{Z}_{\ell}\times\mathbf{Z}_{\ell}
is an isomorphism. The action of
\Gal_{k}
on
E[\ell^{n}]
for every
n\in\mathbf{N}
induces an action on
T_{\ell}(E)
. This defines a representation

\rho_{E,\ell^{\infty},\alpha}:\Gal_{k}\to\Aut(T_{\ell}(E))\xrightarrow{\simeq}\GL_{2}(\mathbf{Z}_{\ell})

called the
\ell
-adic Galois representation associated to
E
and
\alpha
.
Similarly, the action of
\Gal_{k}
on
E[n]
for every
n\in\mathbf{N}
extends to an action on
T(E)
. If
\alpha:T(E)\to\widehat{\mathbf{Z}}\times\widehat{\mathbf{Z}}
is an isomorphism, then this action induces a representation

\rho_{E,\alpha}:\Gal_{k}\to\Aut(T(E))\xrightarrow{\simeq}\GL_{2}(\widehat{\mathbf{Z}})

called the
adelic Galois representation associated to
E
and
\alpha
.
Just as with the mod-
n
Galois representation, we may not keep track of the isomorphisms
\alpha
if we only care about the image of Galois up to conjugation.

Serre showed
[
19
, Théorème 3]
that for a non-CM elliptic curve
E/k
, the adelic representation of Galois,
\rho_{E}:\Gal_{k}\to\GL_{2}(\widehat{\mathbf{Z}})
has open image in
\GL_{2}(\widehat{\mathbf{Z}})
. Note that since the coset of an open subgroup of a profinite group is open,
\rho_{E,\alpha}(\Gal_{k})
is open regardless of the isomorphism
\alpha
, as this only changes the image of Galois up to conjugation.

2.5.
Modular curves

In essence, for
H
an open subgroup of
\GL_{2}(\mathbf{Z}/n\mathbf{Z})
(resp.,
\GL_{2}(\mathbf{Z}_{\ell})
;
\GL_{2}(\widehat{\mathbf{Z}})
), the modular curve
X_{H}
is a curve whose non-cuspidal points parametrize elliptic curves having mod-
n
(resp.,
\ell
-adic; adelic) image of Galois contained in
H
. We now give a more precise definition.

Let
H
be a subgroup of
\GL_{2}(\mathbf{Z}/n\mathbf{Z})
and let
E
be an elliptic curve defined over a number field
k
. We say two isomorphisms
\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}:E[n]\to(\mathbf{Z}/n\mathbf{Z})\times(\mathbf{Z}/n\mathbf{Z})
are
H
-equivalent
, denoted
\alpha\sim_{H}\alpha^{\prime}
if there exists
h\in H
such that
\alpha=h\circ\alpha^{\prime}
. Let
E
be an elliptic curve over a number field
k
. An
H
-level structure on
E
is an equivalence class
[\alpha]_{H}
of isomorphisms
\alpha:E[n]\to(\mathbf{Z}/n\mathbf{Z})\times(\mathbf{Z}/n\mathbf{Z})
under
H
-equivalence.

We define the modular curve
Y_{H}
(resp.
X_{H}
) to be the coarse moduli space of the stack
\mathcal{M}_{H}^{0}
(resp.
\mathcal{M}_{H}
), which parametrizes elliptic curves (resp. generalized elliptic curves) with
H
-level structure. The modular curve
Y_{H}
is an affine subscheme of
X_{H}
. We call the s of
X_{H}-Y_{H}

cusps
and the closed points of
Y_{H}

non-cuspidal points
. The curve
X_{H}
is a smooth, projective, integral curve over
\mathbf{Q}
and is geometrically integral if and only if
H
has full determinant.

If
H
is an open subgroup of
\GL_{2}(\widehat{\mathbf{Z}})
or
\GL_{2}(\mathbf{Z}_{\ell})
of level
n
, then we define the modular curve
X_{H}
to be the modular curve
X_{H(n)}
. If
m
is any positive integer divisible by
n
, then the modular curve
X_{H}=X_{H(n)}
is isomorphic to the modular curve
X_{H(m)}
. Even if
H
does not contain
-I
, we always have that the modular curves
X_{H}
and
X_{\pm H}
are isomorphic as curves. Because of this, we will only consider subgroups
H
containing
-I
.

We give a precise description of the geometric non-cuspidal points of
X_{H}
.
The set
Y_{H}(\overline{k})
consists of equivalence classes of pairs
(E,[\alpha]_{H})
, where

is an elliptic curve and

is an

-level structure on

. We say two pairs
(E,[\alpha]_{H})
and
(E^{\prime},[\alpha^{\prime}]_{H})
are equivalent if there exists an isomorphism

such that the induced isomorphism
\phi:E[n]\to E^{\prime}[n]
satisfies
\alpha\sim_{H}\alpha^{\prime}\circ\phi
, i.e.
\alpha=h\circ\alpha^{\prime}\circ\phi
, for some

.

The absolute Galois group
\Gal_{k}
admits a right action on

as follows:
For an automorphism
\sigma\in\Gal_{k}
and a point
[(E,[\alpha]_{H})]\in Y_{H}(\overline{k})
, we define

[(E,[\alpha]_{H})]\cdot\sigma=[(E^{\sigma},[\alpha\circ\sigma^{-1}]_{H})].

This action is well-defined: Indeed, if
(E^{\prime},[\alpha^{\prime}]_{H}),[(E,[\alpha]_{H})]\in Y_{H}(\overline{k})
and
\sigma\in\Gal_{k}
, then there exists an isomorphism
\phi:E[n]\to E^{\prime}[n]
and an element

such that
\alpha=h\circ\alpha^{\prime}\circ\phi
. This implies

\alpha\circ\sigma^{-1}=(h\circ\alpha^{\prime}\circ\phi)\circ\sigma^{-1}=h\circ(\alpha^{\prime}\circ\sigma^{-1})\circ(\sigma\circ\phi\circ\sigma^{-1}).

The map
\sigma\circ\phi\circ\sigma^{-1}:E^{\sigma}[n]\to E^{\prime\sigma}[n]
given by
P\mapsto\phi(P^{\sigma^{-1}})^{\sigma}
is an isomorphism, so

((E^{\prime},[\alpha^{\prime}]_{H})\cdot\sigma)=(E^{\prime\sigma},[\alpha^{\prime}\circ\sigma^{-1}]_{H})\in[(E^{\sigma},[\alpha\circ\sigma^{-1}]_{H})]=[(E,[\alpha]_{H})\cdot\sigma].

2.6.

Let

be a smooth, projective curve over a number field

Let

be the connected component of the identity.
Denote

to be the divisor scheme of

Denote by

Let

(1)

such that

(3)

We say

-isolated and AV-isolated.

(4)

We say

if there are finitely many points

.

The study of isolated points is motivated by Faltings’s theorem

]
which says that if

has genus strictly greater than

, then

is finite and hence every point in

is sporadic. Indeed, it was shown in

that on

, every sporadic point is isolated and there are only finitely many isolated points. So, if

has genus strictly greater than

The following theorem generalizes
[

, Lemma 2.3]
and will be particularly useful to us in Section

Theorem 2.5

(
[
23
, Theorem 2.17]
)
.

Let

be a smooth, projective curve over a number field

and let

. Let

be the number of geometric components of

and let

be the genus of

. If

is
\mathbf{P}^{1}
-parametrized.

For a finite locally free map of curves, we have the following inequality:

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
C
and

be smooth, projective curves over a number field
k
and let

be a finite locally free map. If

is a closed point, then

\deg(x)\leq\deg(f)\deg(f(x)).

Proof.

Let
x\in C
be a closed point. Let

be the pullback map induced by
f
and let

be the pushforward map induced by

. By
[

, Proposition 21.10.4]
, we have

\deg(f^{*}(f(x)))=\sum_{x^{\prime}\in f^{-1}(f(x))}e_{f}(x^{\prime})\deg(x^{\prime}),

where

is the ramification index of

at

. By
[

, Proposition 21.10.18]
, we have

, so

\deg(f^{*}(f(x)))=\deg(f_{*}(f^{*}(f(x))))=\deg(f)\deg(f(x)).

This implies

\deg(x)\leq\sum_{x^{\prime}\in f^{-1}(f(x))}e_{f}(x^{\prime})\deg(x^{\prime})=\deg(f)\deg(f(x)).\qed

If
f:C\to D
is a finite map of curves

is an isolated point, it is not true in general that the image

is an isolated point of

. However, if the degree is as large as possible—that is, if

—then we can in fact conclude that

is isolated. This result was first proved for nice curves in
[

, Theorem 4.3]
and then generalized by Terao to the setting of smooth, projective curves.

Theorem 2.7

(
[
23
, Theorem 2.15]
)
.

Let

be a finite locally free map of smooth, projective curves over a number field

. If

and

are closed points such that

and

, then the following hold:

(1)

If

is

-isolated, then

is
\mathbf{P}^{1}
-isolated.

(2)

In particular, if

(\mathbf{Z}/n\mathbf{Z})^{\times}
be given. We define the
modular curve
X_{\Delta}(n)
to be the modular curve
X_{B_{\Delta}(n)}
. If
\Delta
is such that
\{\,\pm 1\,\}\subsetneq\Delta\subsetneq(\mathbf{Z}/n\mathbf{Z})^{\times}
, we call
X_{\Delta}(n)
an
intermediate modular curve
, since it is between
X_{1}(n)
and
X_{0}(n)
.
If
\Delta
is a subgroup of
(\mathbf{Z}/n\mathbf{Z})^{\times}
and
m
divides
n
, then we may write
X_{\Delta}(m)
to mean the modular curve
X_{B_{\Delta}(n)}(m)
, with the understanding that we reduce
\Delta
modulo
m
.

We briefly describe another construction of
X_{\Delta}(n)
.
Define a congruence subgroup associated to
\Delta
as follows:

\Gamma_{\Delta}(n)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{.}\hbox{.}}}=\left\{\,\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\SL_{2}(\mathbf{Z})\,\middle|\,c\equiv 0\mod{n}\,,\ (a\mod{n})\in\Delta\,\right\}.

The group
\Gamma_{\Delta}(n)
inherits from
\SL_{2}(\mathbf{Z})
the action on the upper half plane
\mathbf{H}
by linear fractional transformations. This action can be extended to
{\mathbf{H}^{*}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{.}\hbox{.}}}=\mathbf{H}\cup\mathbf{P}^{1}(\mathbf{Q})}
, the extended upper half plane, and the quotient space
\mathbf{H}^{*}/\Gamma_{\Delta}(n)
is a Riemann surface. There exists a smooth projective curve
X(\Gamma_{\Delta}(n))/\mathbf{Q}
and a complex analytic isomorphism
{\mathbf{H}^{*}/\Gamma_{\Delta}(n)\to X(\Gamma_{\Delta}(n))(\mathbf{C})}
(c.f.
[
20
, Remark C.13.2]
).
Moreover, the curves
X(\Gamma_{\Delta}(n))
and
X_{\Delta}(n)
are isomorphic over
\mathbf{Q}
.
Note that
\mathbf{H}^{*}
is fixed under the action by
-I\in\SL_{2}(\mathbf{Z})
, so for any subgroup
\Delta<(\mathbf{Z}/n\mathbf{Z})^{\times}
, the curves
X_{\Delta}(n)
and
X_{\pm\Delta}(n)
are isomorphic.
For this reason, in this paper we only consider subgroups
\Delta
containing
-1
.

For a point
P
of order
n
on an elliptic curve
E
, we
define
\Delta P
to be the set
\left\{\,\delta P\,\middle|\,\delta\in\Delta\,\right\}
and
\mathbf{Q}(\Delta P)
to be the fixed field of
\left\{\,\sigma\in\Gal_{\mathbf{Q}}\,\middle|\,\sigma(\Delta P)=\Delta P\,\right\}
.
Then the non-cuspidal points of
X_{\Delta}(n)(\overline{\mathbf{Q}})
parametrize equivalence classes of pairs
(E,\Delta P)
, where
E
is an elliptic curve and
P\in E
is a point of order
n
.
Moreover, with this construction, every non-cuspidal
k
-rational point of
X_{\Delta}(n)
is of the form
[(E,\Delta P)]
, with
E/k
an elliptic curve and
P\in E(k)
a point of order
n
such that
\Delta P
is fixed by
\Gal_{k}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
n\in\mathbf{N}
and
\{\,\pm 1\,\}\leq\Delta\leq(\mathbf{Z}/n\mathbf{Z})^{\times}
be given. Let
E/\mathbf{Q}(j(E))
be a non-CM elliptic curve and let
P\in E
be a point of order
n
.
If
x\in X_{\Delta}(n)
is the closed point associated to
(E,\Delta P)
, then

\deg(x)=[\mathbf{Q}(j(E),\Delta P):\mathbf{Q}].

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{S}\leq\Gal_{\mathbf{Q}}
denote the stabilizer of the action of
\Gal_{\mathbf{Q}}
on
[(E,\Delta P)]
.
Since
E
is defined over
\mathbf{Q}(j(E))
, we have
\sigma E=E
for all
\sigma\in\Gal_{\mathbf{Q}(j(E))}
. Moreover, if
\sigma\in\Gal_{\mathbf{Q}}
fixes
E
, then there exists an isomorphism
\phi:E\to\sigma E
defined over
\overline{\mathbf{Q}}
. This implies
j(E)=j(\sigma E)
, so
\sigma\in\Gal_{\mathbf{Q}(j(E))}
.
We then have

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}

\displaystyle=\left\{\,\sigma\in\Gal_{\mathbf{Q}}\,\middle|\,\sigma[(E,\Delta P)]=[(E,\Delta P)]\,\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{\,\sigma\in\Gal_{\mathbf{Q}}\,\middle|\,\sigma E=E\,\right\}\cap\left\{\,\sigma\in\Gal_{\mathbf{Q}}\,\middle|\,\sigma(\Delta P)=\Delta P\,\right\}

\displaystyle=\Gal_{\mathbf{Q}(j(E))}\cap\Gal_{(\mathbf{Q}(\Delta P))}.

By the orbit-stabilizer theorem and Galois theory, we conclude

[\mathbf{Q}(x):\mathbf{Q}]=[\Gal_{\mathbf{Q}}:\mathcal{S}]=[\mathbf{Q}^{\mathcal{S}}:\mathbf{Q}]=[\mathbf{Q}(j(E))\mathbf{Q}(\Delta P):\mathbf{Q}]=[\mathbf{Q}(j(E),\Delta P):\mathbf{Q}].\qed

5.
Intermediate modular curves of prime-power level

5.1.
Rational isolated
j
-invariants on
X_{\Delta}(\ell^{n})

In this section, we consider the rational
j
-invariants that arise from isolated points on intermediate modular curves of prime-power level.
Ejder
[
7
]
proved that there are finitely many rational
j
-invariants arising from isolated points on
X_{1}(\ell^{n})
, ranging over all primes
\ell>7
, and gave a partial classification of which rational
j
-invariants may appear.
Terao
[
23
, Theorem 1.5]
showed that if
H\leq\GL_{2}(\widehat{\mathbf{Z}})
has level
7
and
x\in X_{H}
is an isolated point with
j(x)
rational and non-CM, then
j(x)=3^{3}\cdot 5\cdot 7^{5}/2^{7}
and
H
is conjugate to one of nine known subgroups.
Bourdon and Ejder
[
2
]
completed the classification begun by Ejder
[
7
]
for
X_{1}(\ell^{n})
and proved an analogous result for the family of modular curves
X_{0}(\ell^{n})
.

Theorem 5.1

(
[
2
, Theorems 1 and 2.]
)
.

Let
\ell
be a prime and let
j\in\mathbf{Q}
be a rational number.

(1)

There exists an isolated point
x\in X_{1}(\ell^{n})
with
j(x)=j
for some
n\in\mathbf{N}
if and only if
j
is a CM
j
-invariant,
-7\cdot 11^{3}
, or
-7\cdot 137^{3}\cdot 2083^{3}
. Moreover, the non-CM
j
-invariants occur if and only if
\ell=37
.

(2)

There exists an isolated point
x\in X_{0}(\ell^{n})
with
j(x)=j
for some
n\in\mathbf{N}
if and only if
j
is a CM
j
-invariant,
-11\cdot 131^{3}
,
-11^{2}
,
-17^{2}\cdot 101^{3}/2
,
-17\cdot 373^{3}/2^{17}
,
-7\cdot 11^{3}
, or
-7\cdot 137^{3}\cdot 2083^{3}
. Moreover, the non-CM
j
-invariants in this list correspond to isolated rational points on
X_{0}(\ell)
.

Our goal for this section is to consider the above theorem in the setting of intermediate modular curves of prime-power level.
Notice that in the above theorem, the non-CM rational
j
-invariants arising from isolated points on

all appear as

-invariants arising from isolated points on

. We will see that any rational

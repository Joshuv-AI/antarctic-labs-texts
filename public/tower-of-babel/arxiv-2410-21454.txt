Superselection sectors for posets of von Neumann algebras
Anupama Bhardwaj, Tristen Brisky, Chian Yeong Chuah, Kyle Kawagoe, Joseph Keslin, David Penneys, and Daniel Wallick

Abstract
. We study a commutant-closed collection of von Neumann algebras acting on a common Hilbert space indexed by a poset with an order-reversing involution. We give simple geometric axioms for the poset which allow us to construct a braided tensor category of superselection sectors analogous to the construction of Gabbiani and Fröhlich for conformal nets. For cones in ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2} , we weaken our conditions to a bounded spread version of Haag duality and obtain similar results. We show that intertwined nets of algebras have isomorphic braided tensor categories of superselection sectors. Finally, we show that the categories constructed here are equivalent to those constructed by Naaijkens and Ogata for certain 2D quantum spin systems.



1.
Introduction

Operator algebras afford a rigorous method to discuss the thermodynamic limit (as the number of sites goes to
\infty
) of a quantum spin system
[
BR97
]
.
For example, given a
{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
lattice (viewed as a subset of
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
) where each site hosts
{\mathbb{C}}^{d}
spins, unless we choose a vector at each site, we cannot take the infinite tensor product of the ‘local’ Hilbert spaces.
However, given an arbitrary region
\Lambda\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, we get a well-defined algebra of
local operators

{\mathfrak{A}}_{\Lambda}
,
and the inductive limit over all sites gives the UHF algebra of
quasi-local
operators
{\mathfrak{A}}
.

{\mathfrak{A}}_{\Lambda}:=\overline{\bigotimes_{v\in\Lambda}M_{d}({\mathbb{C}})}^{\|\cdot\|}\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad{\mathfrak{A}}:=\overline{\bigotimes_{v\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}}M_{d}({\mathbb{C}})}^{\|\cdot\|}

The assignment
\Lambda\mapsto{\mathfrak{A}}_{\Lambda}
gives a
net of algebras
, where an inclusion of regions
\Lambda\leq\Delta
gives an inclusion of algebras
{\mathfrak{A}}_{\Lambda}\subseteq{\mathfrak{A}}_{\Delta}

and disjoint regions
\Lambda\subset\Sigma^{c}
correspond to commuting algebras of local operators
[{\mathfrak{A}}_{\Lambda},{\mathfrak{A}}_{\Sigma}]=0
.
These properties are usually called
isotony
and
locality
respectively.

Recent articles
[
Naa11
,
Naa12
,
FN15
]
construct a unitary braided tensor category of superselection sectors from a topologically ordered spin system,
which is equipped with a pure ground state
\omega
for a family of interactions
whose cone von Neumann algebras

{\mathfrak{A}}_{\Lambda}^{\prime\prime}\subset B(L^{2}({\mathfrak{A}},\omega))

are properly infinite factors which
satisfy
Haag duality
:

{\mathfrak{A}}_{\Lambda}^{\prime\prime}={\mathfrak{A}}_{\Lambda^{c}}^{\prime}\qquad\qquad\qquad\forall\text{ cones $\Lambda\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}$.}

The article
[
Oga22
]
weakens this last property to a technical condition called
approximate Haag duality
.
Both of these approaches are based on
[
HK64
,
DHR71
,
BF82
]
.

For conformal nets on
S^{1}
, the article
[
GF93
]
gives a construction of a unitary braided tensor category of superselection sectors by looking at families of normal representations of the von Neumann algebras
A_{I}
associated to intervals on
S^{1}
satisfying various axioms, including isotony, locality, and Haag duality.
One can use this same approach to give an equivalent definition of superselection sectors for a quantum spin system.

The purpose of this article is to extend the construction of
[
GF93
]
to an extremely general setting to obtain a unitary braided tensor category from a very short list of simple axioms.
Our basic object of study is a family of von Neumann subalgebras
\{A_{p}\}_{p\in{\mathcal{P}}}
of
B(H)
for a fixed separable Hilbert space indexed by a poset
{\mathcal{P}}
equipped with an order-reversing involution:
p\leq q
iff
q^{\prime}\leq p^{\prime}
and
p=p^{\prime\prime}
for all
p,q\in{\mathcal{P}}
.
We require only 3 axioms for these von Neumann algebras:

•

(isotony) If
p\leq q
, then
A_{p}\subseteq A_{q}
.

•

(Haag duality) For all
p\in{\mathcal{P}}
,
A_{p}^{\prime}=A_{p^{\prime}}
.

•

(absorbing) The representation
H
is
absorbing
for every
A_{p}
, i.e., for any other normal separable representation
K
of
A_{p}
,

{}_{A_{p}}H\oplus{}_{A_{p}}K\cong{}_{A_{p}}H.

We call such an object a
{\mathcal{P}}
-
net
of von Neumann algebras.
In §
2.1
below, we discuss absorbing representations, which also go by the name of
maximal
representations in
[
GLR85
]
.
Uniqueness of absorbing/maximal representations is used to
localize
superselection sectors at any
p\in{\mathcal{P}}
.
In Corollary
2.12
we show that assuming Haag duality, the absorbing axiom is equivalent to the condition that each
A_{p}
is properly infinite.

Following
[
GF93
]
, a
superselection sector
of a
{\mathcal{P}}
-net of algebras
A
is a separable Hilbert space
K
together with a family of normal representations
\pi_{p}:A_{p}\to B(K)
satisfying 3 compatibility axioms:

•

(isotony) If
p\leq q
, then
\pi_{q}|_{A_{p}}=\pi_{p}
.

•

(locality) If
p\leq q^{\prime}
, then
[\pi_{p}A_{p},\pi_{q}A_{q}]=0
.

•

(absorbing) the representation
K
is
absorbing
for every
\pi_{p}A_{p}
.

In §
2.2
below, we show that the absorbing property is equivalent to the condition that each
\pi_{p}
is injective.
It is easily seen that superselection sectors form a
{\mathrm{W}}^{*}
-category which admits orthogonal direct sums, and we discuss when idempotents split in Proposition
2.20
below.
In particular, each
A_{p}
being a factor is a sufficient condition.

In order to construct fusion and braiding, we look at the full subcategory
\mathsf{SSS}_{p}
of superselection sectors
(H,\pi)

localized
at
p\in{\mathcal{P}}
, meaning the space of the sector is
H
and
\pi_{p^{\prime}}=\id_{A_{p^{\prime}}}
.
Under the mild
geometric
assumptions
(GA0)
–
3
on our poset
{\mathcal{P}}
, which we summarize in §
1.1
below, the techniques of
[
DHR69
,
GF93
]
allow us to construct a strict fusion product and a unitary braiding.

Theorem A
.

Given a poset
{\mathcal{P}}
satisfying the geometric axioms
(GA0)
–
3

and a
{\mathcal{P}}
-net of von Neumann algebras
A
,
the superselection sectors
\mathsf{SSS}_{p}
localized at
p
carries a strict tensor product and a unitary braiding.

Whenever
p\leq q
, there is a strict inclusion braided tensor equivalence
\mathsf{SSS}_{p}\hookrightarrow\mathsf{SSS}_{q}
, and by Lemma
1.1
below, given arbitrary
p,q\in{\mathcal{P}}
, we always have a braided equivalence
\mathsf{SSS}_{p}\cong\mathsf{SSS}_{q}
.

When
{\mathcal{P}}
is the poset of intervals on
S^{1}
and
A
is a conformal net, this construction is equivalent to the one from
[
GF93
]
.
However, our construction of the braiding stays local to the interval in which we localize, and we do not need to choose any ‘point at infinity’ as in
[
GF93
, §IV]
.

In order to make connections to
[
Naa11
,
Naa12
,
FN15
,
Oga22
]
, we then focus on the particular poset
{\mathcal{C}}
of cones in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
.
1
1

1

The techniques of this section can be applied to more general geometric posets, but we specialize to cones in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
for simplicity.

We generalize the above construction to a
bounded spread

{\mathcal{C}}
-net which only satisfies
bounded spread Haag duality

[
Jon23
]
.
However, in order for locality to be preserved under superselection sectors, we add an additional ‘small generated’ axiom.

•

(bounded spread Haag duality)
For every cone
\Lambda
,
A_{\Lambda^{c}}^{\prime}\subset A_{\Lambda^{+s}}

where
\Lambda^{+s}
is the cone obtained by enlarging
\Lambda
by distance
s
.

•

(small generated)
For every cone
\Delta
,
A_{\Delta}
is generated by the subalgebras
A_{\Lambda}
such that
\Lambda\leq\Delta
and
\Lambda
is
\Delta
-small
, i.e., there are cones
\Gamma,\Sigma
with
\Lambda,\Delta\leq\Gamma
and
\Lambda,\Delta^{c}\leq\Sigma
.

We define bounded spread superselection sectors similarly as before, but without a locality axiom as it is implied by the small generated axiom.

Theorem B
.

Given a bounded spread
{\mathcal{C}}
-net of von Neumann algebras
A
,
the superselection sectors
\mathsf{SSS}_{\Lambda}
localized at
\Lambda
carries a strict tensor product and a unitary braiding.
If moreover
A
satisfies Haag duality, we get the same braided unitary tensor category as in Theorem
A
.

Theorem
B
is especially useful when we are given two
intertwined
bounded spread
{\mathcal{C}}
-nets of algebras
A,B
, i.e., there is a global
intertwining constant

t>0
such that

A_{\Lambda}\subseteq B_{\Lambda^{+t}}\subseteq A_{\Lambda^{+2t}}\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\forall\,\Lambda\in{\mathcal{C}}.

Theorem C
.

Given two
t
-intertwined bounded spread
{\mathcal{C}}
-nets of algebras
A,B
,
we have a strict isomorphism of braided unitary strict tensor categories
\mathsf{SSS}(A)_{\Lambda}\cong\mathsf{SSS}(B)_{\Lambda^{+t}}
.

This theorem will be applied to intertwined bounded spread
{\mathcal{C}}
-nets of algebras coming from the Levin-Wen model in
[
KJS+24
, §IV.E]
in future work
[
JNP
]
.

Finally, as expected, we prove that the categories we construct in this article are braided equivalent to those constructed in
[
Naa11
,
FN15
,
Oga22
,
BKM24
]
.

1.1.
Geometric poset axioms for a braided tensor category of superselection sectors

Here we summarize the list of
geometric axioms
we assume for our poset
{\mathcal{P}}
in order to use the techniques of
[
DHR69
,
GF93
]
to construct from our
{\mathcal{P}}
-net of algebras
A
a strict tensor product and a braiding on the
{\mathrm{W}}^{*}
category
\mathsf{SSS}_{p}
of superselection sectors localized at a chosen
p\in{\mathcal{P}}
.
We illustrate each of these axioms for the poset
{\mathcal{C}}
of cones in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
.

For
p,q\in{\mathcal{P}}
, we say
p,q
are
disjoint
if
p\leq q^{\prime}
,
and we write

p\Cap q:=\left\{r\in{\mathcal{P}}\middle|r\leq p\text{ and }r\leq q\right\}.

Our first geometric axioms are as follows.

(GA0)

Disjoint complements:

For every
p\in{\mathcal{P}}
,
p\Cap p^{\prime}=\emptyset
.

By Remark
2.13
, the existence of a
{\mathcal{P}}
-net of von Neumann algebras as in Definition
2.2
forces
{\mathcal{P}}
to satisfy
(GA0)
.

(GA1)

Saturation:

Every
p\in{\mathcal{P}}
is
saturated
: for any
q\in{\mathcal{P}}
,
p\Cap q\neq\emptyset
or
p\Cap q^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
(possibly both).

Every cone in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
is saturated.

(GA2)

Splitting:

Every
p\in{\mathcal{P}}

splits
: there are disjoint
r,s\leq p
.

Every cone in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
splits.

\scriptstyle p
\scriptstyle r
\scriptstyle s

We use
(GA1)
and
(GA2)
to
propose a definition of
\circ_{p}
.

A
zig-zag
between
p,q\in{\mathcal{P}}
is a sequence

(z_{1},y_{1},\dots,z_{n},y_{n},z_{n+1})\subset{\mathcal{P}}

with
z_{1}=p
and
z_{n+1}=q

such that

z_{j},z_{j+1}\leq y_{j}
for all
j
.

Lemma 1.1
.

The axioms
(GA1)
and
(GA2)
imply that the poset
{\mathcal{P}}
is
connected
: for any
p,q\in{\mathcal{P}}
, there is a zig-zag from
p
to
q
.

Proof.

For an arbitrary
p\in{\mathcal{P}}
, there is a zig-zag from
p
to
p^{\prime}
.
Indeed, choose a splitting
p_{1},p_{2}\leq p
with
p_{1}\leq p_{2}^{\prime}
, and note that

p\leq p\geq p_{1}\leq p_{2}^{\prime}\geq p^{\prime}

is the desired zig-zag.

If
p\Cap q\neq\emptyset
, then choosing
r\in{\mathcal{P}}
with
r\leq p
and
r\leq q
, we have
p\leq p\geq r\leq q\geq q
.

If
p\Cap q^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
, then we can compose a zig-zags from
p
to
q^{\prime}
from Step 2 with the zig-zag from
q^{\prime}
to
q
from Step 1.
∎

The axioms
(GA1)
and
(GA2)
are equivalent to the single axiom
(GA1.5)
stated in the lemma below which is how we use
(GA1)
and
(GA2)
in practice.
To set up this equivalent axiom, for
p,q\in{\mathcal{P}}
, we define the following notions.

•

We say
p
is
q
-small
if there are
r,s\in{\mathcal{P}}
such that
p,q\leq r
and
p,q^{\prime}\leq s
.
(Observe
q
-small is identical to
q^{\prime}
-small.)

•

A
\widetilde{p}\leq p
is called a
q
-indicator
if
\widetilde{p}\leq q
or
\widetilde{p}\leq q^{\prime}
.

Lemma 1.2
.

The axioms
(GA1)
and
(GA2)
are equivalent to the following axiom.

(GA1.5)

Small indicators:

For any
p,q\in{\mathcal{P}}
, there is a
q
-small
q
-indicator
\widetilde{p}\leq p
.

Proof.

Suppose
{\mathcal{P}}
satisfies
(GA1)
and
(GA2)
.
If
p\Cap q\neq\emptyset
, choose
a\in{\mathcal{P}}
with
a\leq p
and
a\leq q
.
Otherwise, by
(GA1)
,
p\Cap q^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
, so we may choose
a\in{\mathcal{P}}
with
a\leq p
and
a\leq q^{\prime}
.
In either case, since
a
splits by
(GA2)
, there is a
q
-small
\widetilde{p}\leq a
.

Conversely, the existence of a
q
-indicator immediately implies
(GA1)
.
If
\widetilde{p}\leq p
is a
p^{\prime}
-small
p^{\prime}
-indicator, then
the existence of an
r
containing
\widetilde{p}
and
p^{\prime}
yields a splitting
p_{1}=\widetilde{p}
and
p_{2}=r^{\prime}
of
p
, establishing
(GA2)
.
∎

p,q
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
with two choices of
\widetilde{p},\widehat{p}
of
q
-small
q
-indicator.

Remark 1.3
.

Note that if
p,q\in{\mathcal{P}}
and
\widetilde{p}\leq p
is a
q
-small
q
-indicator, then any
\widehat{p}\leq\widetilde{p}
is also a
q
-small
q
-indicator.
Indeed, since
\widetilde{p}\leq q
or
\widetilde{p}\leq q^{\prime}
,
\widehat{p}\leq q
or
\widehat{p}\leq q^{\prime}
, so
\widehat{p}
is also a
q
-indicator.
Similarly, since
\widetilde{p}
is
q
-small, we have that there are
r,s\in{\mathcal{P}}
such that
\widetilde{p},q\leq r
and
\widetilde{p},q^{\prime}\leq s
.
Since
\widehat{p}\leq\widetilde{p}
,
\widehat{p},q\leq r
and
\widehat{p},q^{\prime}\leq s
, so
\widehat{p}
is
q
-small.

We require an additional geometric axiom about the existence of zig-zags in order to complete the construction of our fusion product.

Zig-zag:

For every

is a
q
-indicator and each
y_{j}
is
q
-small.

2.
Nets of algebras and their superselection sectors

Our main object of study is a poset of von Neumann subalgebras of
B(H)
. The main goal is to construct a braided tensor category of
superselection sectors
, which can be viewed as the representation theory of our poset of von Neumann algebras.
To this end, we begin with a general poset, and to define fusion and braiding for our superselection sectors, we will us the
geometric axioms
from §
1.1
above.

For ease of exposition, all Hilbert spaces in this article are assumed to be separable (and infinite dimensional), and all von Neumann algebras are assumed to have separable preduals.

Notation 2.1
.

For this section,
{\mathcal{P}}
denotes a poset equipped with an order-reversing involution denoted
p\mapsto p^{\prime}
, i.e.,
p\leq q
implies
q^{\prime}\leq p^{\prime}
and
p^{\prime\prime}=p
.
For
p,q\in{\mathcal{P}}
, we say that
p
and
q
are
disjoint
if
p\leq q^{\prime}
(equivalently,
q\leq p^{\prime}
).
We write
p\Cap q:=\left\{r\in{\mathcal{P}}\middle|r\leq p\text{ and }r\leq q\right\}
.

Definition 2.2
.

A
{\mathcal{P}}
-
net
of algebras is a
Hilbert space
H
together with an assignment of a von Neumann algebra
A_{p}\subseteq B(H)
to each
p\in{\mathcal{P}}
such that

•

(isotone)

p\leq q
implies
A_{p}\subseteq A_{q}
,

•

(Haag duality)

A_{p^{\prime}}=A_{p}^{\prime}
for all
p\in{\mathcal{P}}
, and

•

(absorbing)
for each
p\in{\mathcal{P}}
, the representation
H
for
A_{p}
is
absorbing
, defined in §
2.1
below.

While these are a minimal set of axioms for the nets of algebras we consider in this article, we have the following generating property which is useful when discussing locality of superselection sectors (see Lemma
2.24
below).

Definition 2.3
.

We say a
{\mathcal{P}}
-net of algebras has the
small generating property
or is
small generated
if for every
q\in{\mathcal{P}}
,
A_{q}
is generated by

\left\{A_{p}\middle|\text{$p\leq q$ and $p$ is $q$-small}\right\}
.

Example 2.4
.

The small generating property is satisfied by the net of cone algebras coming from a quantum spin system
[
Naa11
,
FN15
,
Oga22
,
BKM24
]
.
More specifically, we let
{\mathcal{C}}
denote poset of cones in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, and we let
\Gamma
be an infinite planar lattice where each site
v
hosts
{\mathbb{C}}^{d_{v}}
spins.
The
quasilocal algebra
is the AF
{\mathrm{C}}^{*}
-algebra
{\mathfrak{A}}\coloneqq\bigotimes_{v\in\Gamma}M_{d_{v}}({\mathbb{C}})
, and for an arbitrary subset
\Lambda\subseteq\Gamma
, we define
{\mathfrak{A}}_{\Lambda}\coloneqq\bigotimes_{v\in\Lambda}M_{d_{v}}({\mathbb{C}})
.
For a representation
\pi\colon{\mathfrak{A}}\to B(H)
and a cone
\Lambda\in{\mathcal{C}}
, we define
{\mathcal{R}}_{\Lambda}\coloneqq\pi({\mathfrak{A}}_{\Lambda})^{\prime\prime}
.
(In this context, the notation
{\mathcal{R}}_{\Lambda}
is most commonly used in the literature.)
For certain representations
\pi
,
\{{\mathcal{R}}_{\Lambda}\}_{\Lambda\in{\mathcal{C}}}
satisfies the axioms of a
{\mathcal{C}}
-net of algebras
[
Naa11
,
Naa12
,
FN15
]
.
Furthermore,
\{{\mathcal{R}}_{\Lambda}\}_{\Lambda\in{\mathcal{C}}}
always satisfies the small generating property.
Indeed,
for every
v\in\Delta
, there exists
\Lambda\subseteq\Delta
such that
\Lambda
is
\Delta
-small and
v\in\Lambda
.

2.1.
Absorbing representations of von Neumann algebras

The notion of an absorbing representation is included in the definition of a
{\mathcal{P}}
-net of algebras in order to allow for nets of arbitrary von Neumann algebras, not just nets of properly infinite factors which appear in the AQFT literature.

Definition 2.5
.

Given a von Neumann algebra
M
(with separable predual),
\mathsf{Mod}(M)
denotes the category of separable
M
-modules (normal representations of
M
), i.e., separable Hilbert spaces equipped with normal actions of
M
.
An
M
-module
{}_{M}H\in\mathsf{Mod}(M)
is called:

•

a
generator
if for any non-zero
{}_{M}K\in\mathsf{Mod}(M)
,
\Hom({}_{M}H\to{}_{M}K)\neq 0
,

•

absorbing
if for any other
{}_{M}K\in\mathsf{Mod}(M)
,

{}_{M}H\oplus{}_{M}K\cong{}_{M}H
, i.e., there is an
M
-linear unitary
u:H\oplus K\to H
satisfying
u(m\cdot\xi)=m\cdot u\xi
for all
\xi\in H\oplus K
.

•

self-absorbing
if
{}_{M}H\oplus{}_{M}H\cong{}_{M}H
, and

•

maximal

[
GLR85
, p100]
if for any other normal representation
{}_{M}K
, there is an
M
-linear isometry
v:K\to H
.

Remark 2.6
.

It is well-known that an
M
-module
{}_{M}H
is a generator for
\mathsf{Mod}(M)
if and only if it is
faithful
, i.e., the map
M\to B(H)
is injective.
For the forward direction, it is clear that generators must be faithful.
Conversely, if
{}_{M}H
is faithful, then the central support of
z(p_{H})
in
\End_{M}(H\oplus K)
is
1
for any non-zero
M
-module
{}_{M}K
.
Thus

\Hom({}_{M}H\to{}_{M}K)=p_{K}\End_{M}(H\oplus K)p_{H}\neq 0.

Observe that an absorbing representation is automatically unique up to unitary isomorphism, as if
{}_{M}H,{}_{M}K
are both absorbing, then

{}_{M}H\cong{}_{M}H\oplus{}_{M}K\cong{}_{M}K\oplus{}_{M}H\cong{}_{M}K.

Lemma 2.7
.

If
{\mathcal{C}}
is a
{\mathrm{W}}^{*}
-category which admits orthogonal direct sums and we have isometries
u:a\to b
and
v:b\to a
in
{\mathcal{C}}
, then
a\cong b
.

Proof.

Observe that
p:=\id_{a}
and
q:=\id_{b}
are projections in the von Neumann algebra
\End_{\mathcal{C}}(a\oplus b)
.
The existence of the isometry
u
implies
p\preceq q
and the existence of the isometry
v
implies
q\preceq p
.
We conclude
p\approx q
in
\End_{\mathcal{C}}(a\oplus b)
, i.e., there is a
w\in\End_{\mathcal{C}}(a\oplus b)
such that
ww^{\dagger}=q
and
w^{\dagger}w=p
.
Then
w=qwp
is our desired unitary isomorphism
a\to b
.
∎

Corollary 2.8
.

A maximal representation is unique when it exists.

Lemma 2.9
.

The following are equivalent for a normal representation
{}_{M}H
.

(1)

{}_{M}H
is a self-absorbing generator.

(2)

{}_{M}H
is maximal.

(3)

{}_{M}H
is absorbing.

Proof.

This follows from
[
GLR85
, Prop. 6.5 and Cor. 7.16]
.
Indeed, self-absorbing implies
{}_{M}H\cong{}_{M}H\otimes\ell^{2}
, which is a generator with infinite multiplicity and is thus maximal.

If
{}_{M}H
is maximal, then there are
M
-linear isometries

{}_{M}H\oplus{}_{M}H\hookrightarrow{}_{M}H\hookrightarrow{}_{M}H\oplus{}_{M}H,

and thus
{}_{M}H
is self-absorbing by Lemma
2.7
.
Moreover, for any non-zero
{}_{M}K
, there is an
M
-linear isometry
{}_{M}K\hookrightarrow{}_{M}H
whose adjoint is non-zero.
Thus
{}_{M}H
is a generator.

If
{}_{M}H
is maximal, then for any
{}_{M}K\in\mathsf{Mod}(M)
, there are
M

Lemma 2.24

Suppose

is a collection of von Neumann subalgebras of

Suppose

is

Here are some general facts about superselection sectors.
As above,

{\mathcal{P}}
-net on the Hilbert space

3.
Fusion of superselection sectors

We now construct a family of fusion products
\circ_{p}
for superselection sectors, one for each
p\in{\mathcal{P}}
at which we may localize.
To do so, we will end up introducing some geometric axioms for
{\mathcal{P}}
to satisfy that will help with the construction.
For notational simplicity, we will write
\circ
for
\circ_{p}
when no confusion can arise.

Notation 3.1
.

Given a
{\mathcal{P}}
-net of algebras

, we often consider three superselection sectors denoted
\pi,\sigma,\tau
simultaneously.
By
(SSS1)
, we may localize them at any

localized at

and unitary intertwiners

such that

\pi_{q}(-)=u_{p}\pi^{p}_{q}(-)u_{p}^{*},\qquad\sigma_{q}(-)=v_{p}\sigma^{p}_{q}(-)v_{p}^{*},\qquad\text{and}\qquad\tau_{q}(-)=w_{p}\tau^{p}_{q}(-)w_{p}^{*}\qquad\forall\,q\in{\mathcal{P}}.

That is, a superscript

on a superselection sector denotes that it is localized in
p
,
and a subscript on a unitary denotes that it intertwines a superselection sector to one localized in
p
.

3.1.
Definition of fusion

In this section, we define a fusion operation on

We do so by generalizing the following example.

Example 3.2

Consider the poset

of non-empty proper subsets of a two-point set

Small indicators:

.

Fix

Remark 3.6

The intuition of the proof below comes from considering two endomorphisms

Suppose we have endomorphisms

For the unitary

T\colon\pi\to\check{\pi}
and
S\colon\sigma\to\check{\sigma}
are morphisms in
\mathsf{SSS}_{a}
.
Then
T,S\in A_{a}
.
Since
a\leq b
, we have by isotony that

T\circ_{a}S=T\pi_{a}(S)=T\pi_{b}(S)=T\circ_{b}S.\qed

Corollary 3.20
.

If
p,q\in{\mathcal{P}}
satisfy
(GA0)
–
3
,
then any zig-zag from
p
to
q

(one exists by Lemma
1.1
)
gives a tensor equivalence

\mathsf{SSS}_{p}=\mathsf{SSS}_{z_{1}}\overset{\cong}{\hookrightarrow}\mathsf{SSS}_{y_{1}}\overset{\cong}{\hookleftarrow}\mathsf{SSS}_{z_{2}}\overset{\cong}{\hookrightarrow}\cdots\overset{\cong}{\hookleftarrow}\mathsf{SSS}_{z_{n}}\overset{\cong}{\hookrightarrow}\mathsf{SSS}_{y_{n}}\overset{\cong}{\hookleftarrow}\mathsf{SSS}_{z_{n+1}}=\mathsf{SSS}_{q}.\qed

4.
Braiding of superselection sectors

We now outline our strategy to define a braiding on
\mathsf{SSS}_{p}
which is manifestly ‘localized in
p
’ and does not require any choice of a ‘point at infinity’ as in
[
GF93
, § IV]
.
We choose a splitting
(r,s)
of
p
and for
\pi,\sigma\in\mathsf{SSS}_{p}
, we define a ‘putative braiding’
\beta_{\pi,\sigma}
(which ought to exist) by

\pi\circ_{p}\sigma\xleftrightarrow{\cong}\pi^{r}\circ_{p}\sigma^{s}\xleftrightarrow{\text{should be }=}\sigma^{s}\circ_{p}\pi^{r}\xleftrightarrow{\cong}\sigma\circ_{r}\pi,

where we have made choices of
\pi^{r}
localized in
r
equivalent to
\pi
and
\sigma^{s}
localized in
s
equivalent to
\sigma
.
We then prove this putative braiding is well-defined.
The main techniques we use are:

•

a notion of
mutually disjoint zig-zags
in order to prove
\beta_{\pi,\sigma}

does not depend on the choice of splitting, and

•

a
reflection
of a splitting
(r,s)
of
p
to a splitting of
p^{\prime}
based on
[
DHR69
, Lem. 2.2]
in order to prove
\beta_{\pi,\sigma}
is an intertwiner.

4.1.
Definition of the putative braiding

Construction 4.1
.

Suppose
\pi,\sigma\in\mathsf{SSS}_{p}
.
Choose a splitting
(r,s)
of
p
.
Choose unitaries
u_{r},v_{s}\in A_{p}
which intertwine
\pi
with
\pi^{r}\in\mathsf{SSS}_{r}
and
\sigma
with
\sigma^{s}\in\mathsf{SSS}_{s}
respectively, i.e.,

\pi(-)=u_{r}\pi^{r}(-)u_{r}^{*}\qquad\qquad\qquad\sigma(-)=v_{s}\sigma^{s}(-)v_{s}^{*}.

We define a putative braiding from
\pi\circ_{p}\sigma
to
\sigma\circ_{p}\pi
by

\beta_{\pi,\sigma}\coloneqq(v_{s}\circ_{p}u_{r})(u_{r}^{*}\circ_{p}v_{s}^{*})=v_{s}\sigma^{s}_{p}(u_{r})u_{r}^{*}\pi_{p}(v_{s})^{*}.

Lemma 4.2
.

Switching the roles of
r,s
in Construction
4.1
produces the ‘reverse’ putative braiding

\beta^{\rev}_{\pi,\sigma}=\beta_{\sigma,\pi}^{-1}
.

Proof.

It suffices to prove

\beta_{\pi,\sigma}^{-1}=u_{r}\pi^{r}_{p}(v_{s})v_{s}^{*}\sigma_{p}(u_{r})^{*},

as the right hand side is the formula one would obtain for
\beta_{\sigma,\pi}
in Construction
4.1
after reversing the roles of
r,s
.
Indeed,

v_{s}\sigma^{s}_{p}(u_{r})u_{r}^{*}\pi_{p}(v_{s})^{*}u_{r}\pi^{r}_{p}(v_{s})v_{s}^{*}\sigma_{p}(u_{r})^{*}=\sigma_{p}(u_{r})v_{s}u_{r}^{*}\pi_{p}(v_{s})^{*}\pi_{p}(v_{s})u_{r}v_{s}^{*}\sigma_{p}(u_{r})^{*}=1.\qed

Lemma 4.3
.

The formula for
\beta_{\pi,\sigma}
does not depend on the choice of
\pi^{r}
localized at
r
,
\sigma^{s}
localized at
s
, and intertwiners
u_{r}\colon\pi^{r}\to\pi
and
v_{s}\colon\sigma^{s}\to\sigma
.

Proof.

Consider
\pi^{r},\check{\pi}^{r}
localized at
r
,
\sigma^{s},\check{\sigma}^{s}
localized at
s
and intertwiners
u_{r}\colon\pi^{r}\to\pi
,
\check{u}_{r}\colon\check{\pi}^{r}\to\pi
,
v_{s}\colon\sigma^{s}\to\sigma
,
\check{v}_{s}\colon\check{\sigma}^{s}\to\sigma
.
We wish to show that

v_{s}\sigma_{p}^{s}(u_{r})u_{r}^{*}\pi_{p}(v_{s})^{*}=\check{v}_{s}\check{\sigma}_{p}^{s}(\check{u}_{r})\check{u}_{r}^{*}\check{\pi}_{p}(\check{v}_{s})^{*}.

Observe that the above equation is equivalent to

\sigma_{p}(u_{r})v_{s}u_{r}^{*}\pi_{p}(v_{s}^{*})=\sigma_{p}(\check{u}_{r})\check{v}_{s}\check{u}_{r}^{*}\pi_{p}(\check{v}_{s}^{*}),

which is equivalent to

\sigma_{p}(\check{u}_{r}^{*}u_{r})v_{s}u_{r}^{*}\pi_{p}(v_{s}^{*}\check{v}_{s})=\check{v}_{s}\check{u}_{r}^{*}.

Now, since
\pi^{r},\check{\pi}^{r}
are localized in
r
and
\sigma^{s},\check{\sigma}^{s}
are localized in
s
, we have that
\check{u}_{r}^{*}u_{r}\in A_{r}
and
v_{s}^{*}\check{v}_{s}\in A_{s}
by
(SSS4)
.
Therefore, since
r\leq s^{\prime}
, we have

\displaystyle\sigma_{p}(\check{u}_{r}^{*}u_{r})v_{s}u_{r}^{*}\pi_{p}(v_{s}^{*}\check{v}_{s})

\displaystyle=v_{s}\sigma_{p}^{s}(\check{u}_{r}^{*}u_{r})u_{r}^{*}\pi_{p}(v_{s}^{*}\check{v}_{s})=v_{s}\check{u}_{r}^{*}u_{r}u_{r}^{*}\pi_{p}(v_{s}^{*}\check{v}_{s})=v_{s}\check{u}_{r}^{*}\pi_{p}(v_{s}^{*}\check{v}_{s})

\displaystyle=v_{s}\check{\pi}_{p}^{r}(v_{s}^{*}\check{v}_{s})\check{u}_{r}^{*}=v_{s}v_{s}^{*}\check{v}_{s}\check{u}_{r}^{*}=\check{v}_{s}\check{u}_{r}^{*}.\qed

4.2.
Independence of the putative braiding up to mutually disjoint zig-zag

Definition 4.4
.

Let
r_{1},s_{1},r_{2},s_{2}\in{\mathcal{P}}
with
r_{1}\leq s_{1}^{\prime}
and
r_{2}\leq s_{2}^{\prime}
.
A
mutually disjoint zig-zag

(r_{1},s_{1})\leftrightsquigarrow(r_{2},s_{2})
is a pair of zig-zags

\left[\begin{array}[]{*{10}c}r_{1}=x_{1}&{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}w_{1}}&{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}x_{2}}&\dots&x_{n}&{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}w_{n}}&{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}x_{n+1}=r_{2}}\\
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}s_{1}=z_{1}}&{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}y_{1}}&z_{2}&\dots&{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}z_{n}}&{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}y_{n}}&z_{n+1}=s_{2}\end{array}\right]

such that
for all
i=1,\dots,n
,
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}w_{i}}
is disjoint from
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}z_{i}}
and
{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}y_{i}}
is disjoint from
{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}x_{i+1}}
.

The existence of a mutually disjoint zig-zag from

(r_{1},s_{1})
to
(r_{2},s_{2})

allows one to first wiggle
r_{1}=x_{1}\leq w_{1}\geq x_{2}=:\widetilde{r}_{1}
while staying disjoint from
s_{1}=z_{1}
,
and we can then wiggle
s_{1}=z_{1}\leq y_{1}\geq z_{2}=:\widetilde{s}_{1}

while staying disjoint from
\widetilde{r}_{1}=x_{2}
.
We may then repeat this process along the entire mutually disjoint zig-zag.
Observe that mutually disjoint zig-zags can be trivially extended on either side to allow for multiple wiggles for the
r_{i}
’s keeping
s_{i}
constant, and vice versa.

Lemma 4.5
.

If
(r_{1},s_{1})
and
(r_{2},s_{2})
are two splittings in
p

and
there is a mutually disjoint zig-zag

(r_{1},s_{1})\leftrightsquigarrow(r_{2},s_{2})
contained in
p
,
then the map
\beta_{\pi,\sigma}
constructed in Construction
4.1
above is independent of the choice of splitting
(r_{1},s_{1})
versus
(r_{2},s_{2})
.

Proof.

Given unitary intertwiners
u_{r_{1}}:\pi^{r_{1}}\to\pi
,
v_{s_{1}}:\sigma^{s_{1}}\to\sigma
, and
u_{r_{2}}:\pi^{r_{2}}\to\pi
,
v_{s_{2}}:\sigma^{s_{2}}\to\sigma
, we want to show that

v_{s_{1}}\sigma^{s_{1}}_{p}(u_{r_{1}})u_{r_{1}}^{*}\pi_{p}(v_{s_{1}})^{*}=v_{s_{2}}\sigma^{s_{2}}_{p}(u_{r_{2}})u_{r_{2}}^{*}\pi_{p}(v_{s_{2}})^{*}.

Note that by induction and by symmetry, it suffices to consider the case
s_{1}=s_{2}\eqqcolon s
and
r_{1},r_{2}\leq r\leq p
with
r\leq s^{\prime}
.
In that case, the equation becomes

v_{s}\sigma^{s}_{p}(u_{r_{1}})u_{r_{1}}^{*}\pi_{p}(v_{s})^{*}=v_{s}\sigma^{s}_{p}(u_{r_{2}})u_{r_{2}}^{*}\pi_{p}(v_{s})^{*}.

Now,
u_{r_{1}}:\pi^{r_{1}}\to\pi
and
u_{r_{2}}:\pi^{r_{2}}\to\pi
are two choices for localizing
\pi
to
r
since
r_{1},r_{2}\leq r
.
Therefore, by Lemma
4.3
, the result follows.
∎

.

As stated in Remark
1.6

if there is a mutually disjoint zig-zag

Facts 4.7
.

Here are some helpful facts to construct mutually disjoint zig-zags.

(ZZ1)

Suppose
a,b\leq p

and
(a=z_{1},y_{1},\dots,y_{n},z_{n+1}=b)
is a zig-zag contained in
p
.
If there is a
j<n
with
y_{j}\Cap b\neq\emptyset
, then
choosing
\widehat{b}\in y_{j}\Cap b
, there is a shorter zig-zag

(a=z_{1},y_{1},\dots,y_{j},z_{j+1}=\widehat{b})
.

(ZZ2)

Suppose
a,b,c\leq p

and
(a=z_{1},y_{1},\dots,y_{n},z_{n+1}=b)
is a zig-zag in
p
such that
y_{j}\Cap c\neq\emptyset
for some
j
.
There choosing any
\widetilde{c}\in y_{j}\Cap c
, our zig-zag is a concatenation of the zig-zags

(a=z_{1},y_{1},\dots,z_{j},y_{j},\widetilde{c})

and

(\widetilde{c},y_{j},z_{j+1},y_{j+1},z_{j+2},\dots,y_{n},z_{n}=b)
.

(ZZ3)

Suppose
a,b,c\leq p

and
(a=z_{1},y_{1},\dots,y_{n},z_{n+1}=b)
is a zig-zag in
p
such that
y_{j}\Cap c=\emptyset
for all
j
.
Then there is a
\widetilde{c}\leq c
such that our zig-zag is contained in
\widetilde{c}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Setting
c_{0}:=c
, observe that by induction and
(GA1)
, for all
j=1,\dots,n
, there is a
c_{j}\leq y_{j}^{\prime}\Cap c_{j-1}
.
Setting
\widetilde{c}:=c_{n}
works.
∎

(ZZ4)

Suppose
a,b\leq p
are disjoint
and
(a=z_{1},y_{1},\dots,y_{n},z_{n+1}=b)
is a zig-zag contained in
p
.
Applying
(ZZ1)

and replacing
a,b
with
\widehat{a}\leq a
and
\widehat{b}\leq b
if necessary, we may assume that our zig-zag satisfies

y_{j}\Cap a=\emptyset
for all
j>1

and

y_{j}\Cap b=\emptyset
for all
j<n
.
Applying
(ZZ3)
, there are

\widetilde{a}\leq a

and

\widetilde{b}\leq b

so that the zig-zag

(\widetilde{a},y_{1},\dots,y_{n},\widetilde{b})
is a zig-zag contained in
p

such that

(4.8)

y_{j}\leq\widetilde{a}^{\prime}
for all

Here are some useful facts about Buchholz–Fredenhagen superselection sectors that mirror Facts

(BF1)

Suppose

.
Then

Proof.

Then

is disjoint from

Construction 6.4
.

Let
\Lambda\in{\mathcal{C}}
such that
\Lambda\Cap\Omega=\emptyset
.
We now define a strict tensor product and a braiding on
\mathsf{SSS}_{\Lambda}^{BF}
.

The monoidal product on
\mathsf{SSS}_{\Lambda}^{BF}
is given by composition of endomorphisms.
In detail, for
\pi,\sigma\in\mathsf{SSS}_{\Lambda}^{BF}
,
\pi\otimes_{BF}\sigma\coloneqq\pi\circ\sigma
, and for
T\colon\pi_{1}\to\pi_{2}
and
S\colon\sigma_{1}\to\sigma_{2}
in
\mathsf{SSS}_{\Lambda}^{BF}
,
T\otimes_{BF}S\coloneqq T\pi_{1}(S)=\pi_{2}(S)T
.
Since
\Lambda\Cap\Omega=\emptyset
,
\sigma(A^{\aux})\subseteq A^{\aux}
by
(BF3)
, so
\pi\circ\sigma
is well-defined.
Similarly,
S\in A^{\aux}
by
(BF4)
, so
T\otimes_{BF}S
is well-defined.
Therefore,
-\otimes_{BF}-
endows
\mathsf{SSS}_{\Lambda}^{BF}
with the structure of a strict monoidal category.

The braiding on
\mathsf{SSS}_{\Lambda}^{BF}
is defined analogously to the braiding in
[
Naa11
]
and in fact agrees with the braiding in
[
Oga22
]
.
Let
\Delta\in{\mathcal{C}}
be a cone such that
\Delta\Cap\Omega=\emptyset
and such that
\Delta^{+s}
is disjoint from
\Lambda
.
To fix a convention, we additionally assume that
\Delta
is counterclockwise of
\Lambda
, meaning that if one rotates
\Lambda
counterclockwise, the rotated cone intersects
\Delta
before intersecting
\Omega
.
Given
\pi,\sigma\in\mathsf{SSS}_{\Lambda}^{BF}
, we pick
\sigma^{\Delta}\cong\sigma
localized in
\Delta
and a unitary
v_{\Delta}\colon\sigma^{\Delta}\to\sigma
.
The braiding is then defined by

\beta_{\pi,\sigma}\coloneqq(v_{\Delta}\otimes_{BF}\pi)(\pi\otimes_{BF}v_{\Delta}^{*})=v_{\Delta}\pi(v_{\Delta}^{*}).

By modifying the proofs in §
4
, one can see that
\beta_{\pi,\sigma}
is a braiding that is independent of the choices made.

Theorem 6.5
.

Let
\Lambda\in{\mathcal{C}}
such that
\Lambda\Cap\Omega=\emptyset
.
The map
\pi\mapsto\pi^{\aux}
, where
\pi^{\aux}\colon A^{\aux}\to B(H)
is the unique extension of
\pi
to
A^{\aux}
guaranteed by isotony,
is a strict braided equivalence
\mathsf{SSS}_{\Lambda}^{GF}\to\mathsf{SSS}_{\Lambda}^{BF}
.

Proof.

We first show that the map
\pi\to\pi^{\aux}
is an equivalence of categories.
Since
(A_{\Delta}\cap A^{\aux})^{\prime\prime}=A_{\Delta}
for all
\Delta\in{\mathcal{C}}
,
T\in B(H)
intertwines
\pi,\sigma\in\mathsf{SSS}_{\Lambda}^{GF}
if and only if
T
intertwines
\pi^{\aux},\sigma^{\aux}
.
Therefore,
\pi\mapsto\pi^{\aux}
defines a fully faithful functor.
This functor is essentially surjective by
(BF5)
.
Specifically, if
\rho\in\mathsf{SSS}_{\Lambda}^{BF}
, then by
(BF5)
, for every
\Delta\in{\mathcal{C}}
,
\rho|_{A_{\Delta}\cap A^{\aux}}
has a unique normal extension
\pi_{\Delta}
to
A_{\Delta}
, which is absorbing.
We then obtain
\pi\in\mathsf{SSS}_{\Lambda}^{GF}
, and
\pi^{\aux}=\rho
by construction.

We now show that this equivalence is strict monoidal.
We first show that for
\pi,\sigma\in\mathsf{SSS}_{\Lambda}^{GF}
,
(\pi\circ_{\Lambda}\sigma)^{\aux}=\pi^{\aux}\circ\sigma^{\aux}
.
It suffices to show that for every
\Delta\in{\mathcal{C}}
with
\Delta\Cap\Omega=\emptyset
,
(\pi\circ_{\Lambda}\sigma)^{\aux}(x)=(\pi^{\aux}\circ\sigma^{\aux})(x)
for every
x\in A_{\Delta}
.
Let
\Delta\in{\mathcal{C}}
with
\Delta\Cap\Omega=\emptyset
.
Then there exists a
\Sigma\in{\mathcal{C}}
with
\Sigma\Cap\Omega=\emptyset
such that
\Lambda,\Delta\leq\Sigma
.
Since
\Lambda\leq\Sigma
,
(\pi\circ_{\Lambda}\sigma)_{\Sigma}=\pi_{\Sigma^{+s}}\circ\sigma_{\Sigma}
.
In addition, since
\Delta\leq\Sigma
, we have that
A_{\Delta}\subseteq A_{\Sigma}
.
Thus, for any
x\in A_{\Delta}
, we have that

(\pi\circ_{\Lambda}\sigma)^{\aux}(x)=(\pi\circ_{\Lambda}\sigma)_{\Sigma}(x)=(\pi_{\Sigma^{+s}}\circ\sigma_{\Sigma})(x)=(\pi^{\aux}\circ\sigma^{\aux})(x).

Furthermore, if
T\colon\pi\to\check{\pi}
and
S\colon\sigma\to\check{\sigma}
in
\mathsf{SSS}_{\Lambda}^{GF}
, we have that

T\circ_{\Lambda}S=T\pi_{\Lambda^{+s}}(S)=T\pi^{\aux}(S)=T\otimes_{BF}S,

where the tensor product at the end is in the category
\mathsf{SSS}_{\Lambda}^{BF}
.
Therefore, the functor
\pi\mapsto\pi^{\aux}
is a strict monoidal equivalence.

It remains to show that this equivalence is braided.
Let
\pi,\sigma\in\mathsf{SSS}_{\Lambda}^{GF}
.
Let
\Delta\in{\mathcal{C}}
be a cone such that
\Delta\Cap\Omega=\emptyset
and such that
\Delta^{+s}
is disjoint from
\Lambda
.
As before, we additionally assume that
\Delta
is counterclockwise of
\Lambda
.
We choose
\sigma^{\Delta}\cong\sigma
localized in
\Delta
and a unitary
v_{\Delta}\colon\sigma^{\Delta}\to\sigma
.
Also, since
\Delta\Cap\Omega=\Lambda\Cap\Omega=\emptyset
, there exists
\Sigma\in{\mathcal{C}}
with
\Lambda,\Delta\subset\Sigma
such that
\Sigma\Cap\Omega=\emptyset
.
We then have that

\beta_{\pi^{\aux},\sigma^{\aux}}=v_{\Delta}\pi^{\aux}(v_{\Delta}^{*})=v_{\Delta}\pi_{\Sigma^{+s}}(v_{\Delta}^{*}).

The expression on the right is exactly the bounded spread version of the braiding defined in Construction
4.1
on
\mathsf{SSS}_{\Sigma}^{GF}
.
By Proposition
4.15
, this is the same as the braiding on
\mathsf{SSS}_{\Lambda}^{GF}
.
Therefore, the functor
\pi\mapsto\pi^{\aux}
defines a braided equivalence.
∎

6.2.
Naaijkens superselection sectors

Let
\Gamma
be an infinite planar lattice, let
{\mathfrak{A}}\coloneqq\bigotimes_{j\in\Gamma}M_{d}({\mathbb{C}})
, and let
{\mathfrak{A}}_{\loc}
be the finitely supported (local) operators in
{\mathfrak{A}}
.
For
\Lambda\subseteq\Gamma
, we define
{\mathfrak{A}}_{\Lambda}\coloneqq\bigotimes_{j\in\Lambda}M_{d}({\mathbb{C}})
.
Let
\omega_{0}\colon{\mathfrak{A}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
be a pure state, and let
(\pi_{0},\Omega,{\mathcal{H}}_{0})
be its GNS representation.
We assume that
\pi_{0}
satisfies bounded spread Haag duality with respect to cones, that is, there exists a global
s\geq 0
for any cone
\Lambda
,
\pi_{0}({\mathfrak{A}}_{\Lambda^{c}})^{\prime}\subseteq\pi_{0}({\mathfrak{A}}_{\Lambda^{+s}})^{\prime\prime}
.
For simplicity, we write
{\mathcal{R}}_{\Lambda}\coloneqq\pi_{0}({\mathfrak{A}}_{\Lambda})^{\prime\prime}
for a cone
\Lambda
.
Since
\omega_{0}
is a pure state,
{\mathcal{R}}_{\Lambda}
is a factor for any cone
\Lambda
.
We additionally assume that
{\mathcal{R}}_{\Lambda}
is an infinite factor for every cone
\Lambda
.
This is true for instance when
\omega_{0}
is translation invariant, by a standard argument used in
[
Naa11
, Thm. 5.1]
.
However, there are other ways to ensure that
{\mathcal{R}}_{\Lambda}
is always infinite.
For example,
{\mathcal{R}}_{\Lambda}
is infinite if
\omega_{0}
is a gapped ground state for a Hamiltonian with uniformly bounded finite range interactions
[
Oga22
, Lem. 5.3]
.
In this case, the collection of von Neumann algebras
{\mathcal{R}}_{\Lambda}
, together with the vacuum Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}_{0}
, forms a bounded spread
{\mathcal{C}}
-net of algebras.
(Note that the representation
{\mathcal{R}}_{\Lambda}\hookrightarrow B({\mathcal{H}}_{0})
is absorbing by Lemma
2.11
.)

Definition 6.6
.

A
Naaijkens superselection sector
is a representation
\pi
of
{\mathfrak{A}}
satisfying that for all cones
\Lambda\in{\mathcal{C}}
,

\pi|_{{\mathfrak{A}}_{\Lambda}}\cong\pi_{0}|_{{\mathfrak{A}}_{\Lambda}}.

It is well-known that for every
\Lambda\in{\mathcal{C}}
with
\Lambda\Cap\Omega=\emptyset
, the category of Naaijkens superselection sectors is equivalent to
\mathsf{SSS}_{\Lambda}^{BF}
(see for instance
[
Naa11
,
Naa13
]
).
Furthermore, the monoidal product and braiding that is defined for Naaijkens superselection sectors in
[
Naa11
,
FN15
,
Oga22
,
BKM24
]
is exactly the monoidal product and braiding defined in Construction
6.4
.
Therefore, the following result is a corollary of Theorem
6.5
.

Corollary 6.7
.

Let
\Lambda\in{\mathcal{C}}
such that
\Lambda\Cap\Omega=\emptyset
.
Then
\mathsf{SSS}_{\Lambda}^{GF}
is braided monoidally equivalent to the category of Naaijkens superselection sectors localized at a cone
\Lambda
.

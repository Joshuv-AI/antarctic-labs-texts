Talagrand-Type Transport Inequalities for Path Spaces over Carnot Groups
Peter K. Friz 1,2, Helena Kremp 1,2, Vaios Laschos 2, Matthias Liero 2, Benjamin A. Robinson 3

Abstract
. We consider Talagrand-type transportation inequalities for the law of Brownian motion on Carnot groups. An important example is the lift of standard Brownian motion to the Brownian rough path. We present a direct proof on enhanced path space, which also yields equality when restricting to adapted couplings in the transport problem. Moreover, we prove a Talagrand inequality for the heat kernel measure on Carnot groups and deduce the inequality for the law of Brownian motion on Carnot groups via a bottom-up argument. Our study of this enhanced Wiener measure contributes to a longstanding programme to extend key properties of Wiener measure to the non-commutative setting of the enhanced Wiener measure, which is of central importance in Lyons’ rough path theory. With a non-commutative sub-Riemannian state space, we observe phenomena that differ from the Euclidean case. In particular, while a top-down projection argument recovers Talagrand’s inequality on Euclidean space from the corresponding inequality on the path space, such a projection argument breaks down in the Carnot group setting. We further study a Riemannian approximation of the Heisenberg group, in which case the failure of the top-down projection can be partially overcome. Finally, we show that the cost function used in the Talagrand inequality is a natural choice, in that it arises as a limit of discretised costs in the sense of Γ \Gamma -convergence.

t\in[0,1]
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{Y}_{t}\;\text{is independent of}\;\mathcal{F}^{X}_{1}\;\text{given}\;\mathcal{F}^{X}_{t}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{F}^{X}_{t}\;\text{is independent of}\;\mathcal{F}^{Y}_{1}\;\text{given}\;\mathcal{F}^{Y}_{t}.

We denote the set of all such couplings by
\Pi_{\mathrm{ad}}(\mu,\nu)
.
For a measurable function
c\colon C([0,1],E)\times C([0,1],E)\to[0,\infty]
, define the
adapted optimal transport problem

\displaystyle\mathrm{T}^{\mathrm{ad}}_{c,p}(\mu,\nu)\coloneqq\Bigl(\inf_{\lambda\in\Pi_{\mathrm{ad}}(\mu,\nu)}\int\int c^{p}(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x,y)\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

We say that
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(C([0,1],E))
satisfies an
adapted
T_{p}
inequality
for some
p\in[1,\infty)
if there exists
\alpha>0
such that

\displaystyle\mathrm{T}^{\mathrm{ad}}_{c,p}(\mu,\nu)\leq\sqrt{\frac{2}{\alpha}H(\nu\|\mu)}.

In this adapted setting,
[
Las18
, Lemma 5]
and
[
Föl22
, Theorem 3]
show that Wiener measure satisfies an adapted
\mathcal{T}_{2}
inequality with
\alpha=1
and that equality holds; i.e.

(2.4)

\displaystyle\mathrm{T}^{\mathrm{ad}}_{c_{\mathcal{H}},2}(\mu,\nu)=\sqrt{2H(\nu\|\mu)}.

Remark 2.5
.

For continuous-time stochastic processes,
[
BBP+25
]
give an alternative definition of the adapted optimal transport problem and adapted Wasserstein distance that has additional desirable topological properties. The value of this problem is defined such that it lies between
\mathrm{T}_{c,p}
and
\mathrm{T}^{\mathrm{ad}}_{c,p}
. Thus, it is immediate that an adapted
\mathcal{T}_{2}
inequality still holds in this setting. However, equality has not been studied in this case, and we leave this to future work, choosing to focus on the definition given in
Definition

2.4
in the present paper.

Remark 2.6
.

For discrete-time processes taking values in some Polish space
E
with
n
time steps, one can also consider their laws, which are probability measures on
E^{n}
, and define an adapted optimal transport problem analogously to
Definition

2.4
. In this setting,
[
Par26
, Corollary 1.8]
shows that the
\mathcal{T}_{1}
inequality is equivalent to its adapted counterpart. Moreover,
[
Par26
, Corollary 1.9]
shows that, for probability measures with finite exponential moment, an adapted
\mathcal{T}_{p}
inequality holds for all
p>1
, with constant given explicitly in terms of the exponential moment and number of time steps, thus extending the results of
[
BV05
]
to the adapted setting. For a standard Gaussian on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
[
BBLZ17
, Proposition 5.10]
prove an adapted
\mathcal{T}_{2}
inequality using a dynamic programming argument. As noted in
[
BBLZ17
, Remark 5.11]
, equality cannot generally be expected in the discrete-time setting.

2.2.
Step-
2
Carnot groups

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a step-
2
Carnot group, i.e. a connected, simply connected
nilpotent Lie group whose Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of left-invariant vector
fields has dimension
m=d_{1}+d_{2}
and admits a stratification

\mathfrak{g}=\mathcal{V}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{V}_{2}
with
\mathcal{V}_{2}=[\mathcal{V}_{1},\mathcal{V}_{1}]
,
[\mathcal{V}_{1},\mathcal{V}_{2}]=\{0\}
.
Fix an adapted basis
(V_{1},\ldots,V_{m})
such that
(V_{1},\ldots,V_{d_{1}})
is
a basis of
\mathcal{V}_{1}
. Using exponential coordinates, we can and will
identify
\mathbb{G}
with
\mathbb{R}^{m}
,

\mathbb{G}\ni\bm{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d_{1}},x_{d_{1}+1},\ldots,x_{m})=(x^{(1)},x^{(2)})\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\oplus\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}\cong\mathbb{R}^{m},

with group law in Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff form,

(2.5)

(\bm{x},\bm{y})\mapsto\bm{x}\bm{y}=\bm{x}+\bm{y}+\frac{1}{2}[\bm{x},\bm{y}].

It is not restrictive to assume that
V_{i}(0)=e_{i}
, the canonical basis vectors
of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
. For
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{G}
, let
\ell_{\bm{x}}\colon\mathbb{G}\to\mathbb{G}
denote the left multiplication map defined by
\ell_{\bm{x}}\bm{y}=\bm{x}\bm{y}
, for
\bm{y}\in\mathbb{G}
, and let
\mathrm{d}\ell_{\bm{x}}\colon T\mathbb{G}\to T\mathbb{G}
denote its differential. By left invariance,
V_{i}(\bm{x})=\mathrm{d}\ell_{\bm{x}}e_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,m
,
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{G}
.

Endow
\mathfrak{g}
with a left-invariant metric
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\colon\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\to\mathbb{R}
that makes the
V_{i}
orthonormal.
Define the
structure constants

w_{ij}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}
, for
i,j\in\{1,\dotsc,d_{1}\}
, by

w_{ij}^{k}\coloneqq\langle[V_{i},V_{j}],V_{k}\rangle=-w_{ji}^{k}
, for
k\in\{d_{1}{+}1,\dotsc,m\}
;
cf.
[
BLU07
, Section 3.2]
.
The group law in (
2.5
)
can then be written as

\begin{gathered}(\bm{x},\bm{y})=((x^{(1)},x^{(2)}),\,(y^{(1)},y^{(2)}))\mapsto\bm{x}\bm{y}=\Big(x^{(1)}+y^{(1)},\,x^{(2)}+y^{(2)}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i<j}w_{ij}(x^{(1)}_{i}y^{(1)}_{j}-x^{(1)}_{j}y^{(1)}_{i})\Big).\end{gathered}

For notational brevity, we introduce
the operator
\mathsf{W}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times d_{1}}\to\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}
given in terms of the structure constants by

\mathsf{W}A=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d_{1}}w_{ij}A_{ij}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d_{1}}w_{ij}(A_{ij}-A_{ji})=\sum_{i<j}w_{ij}(A_{ij}-A_{ji}),\quad A\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times d_{1}}.

With this definition, we can rewrite the group law
for
\bm{x}=(x^{(1)},x^{(2)})
,
\bm{y}=(y^{(1)},y^{(2)})
as

\bm{x}\bm{y}=\Big(x^{(1)}+y^{(1)},\,x^{(2)}+y^{(2)}+\frac{1}{2}\mathsf{W}(x^{(1)}\otimes y^{(1)})\Big).

Let
\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}\coloneqq\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{d_{1}}V_{i}^{2}
denote the sub-Laplacian on
\mathbb{G}
and define the
horizontal gradient
\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}
by its action

\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}f\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{d_{1}}(V_{i}f)V_{i}\in\mathcal{V}_{1},\quad\text{for}\;f\colon\mathbb{G}\to\mathbb{R}.

Let
H\mathbb{G}\subset T\mathbb{G}
be the horizontal tangent bundle of
the group
\mathbb{G}
, i.e. the left-invariant sub-bundle of the tangent bundle

T\mathbb{G}
such that
H_{\bm{e}}\mathbb{G}=\{\,V(0):V\in\mathcal{V}_{1}\,\}
, where
\bm{e}
is the identity element of
\mathbb{G}
. For
i\in\{1,2\}
, define the projection operator
\pi_{i}\colon\mathbb{G}\to\mathbb{R}^{d_{i}}
by

(2.6)

\pi_{i}(x^{(1)},x^{(2)})=x^{(i)}.

Dilation on
\mathbb{G}
by a factor
s>0
takes the form

(2.7)

\delta_{s}((x^{(1)},x^{(2)}))\coloneqq(sx^{(1)},s^{2}x^{(2)}).

The Haar measure on
\mathbb{G}
coincides with Lebesgue measure
\mathcal{L}^{m}
on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
For measurable
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}
, we have

(2.8)

\mathcal{L}^{m}(\delta_{s}E)=s^{Q}\mathcal{L}^{m}(E),

where
Q=d_{1}+2d_{2}
is called the
homogeneous
dimension
of
\mathbb{G}
.

We endow
\mathbb{G}
with the Carnot–Carathéodory structure induced by

H\mathbb{G}
, as follows. An absolutely continuous curve
\gamma\colon[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{G}

is called horizontal if
\dot{\gamma}_{t}\in H_{\gamma_{t}}\mathbb{G}
for almost every
t\in[0,1]
.
The
Carnot–Carathéodory distance
between
x,y\in\mathbb{G}
is then defined as

(2.9)

\displaystyle d_{\mathrm{CC}}(x,y)=\inf\biggl\{\,\int_{0}^{1}|\dot{\gamma}_{t}|\,\mathrm{d}t:\gamma\text{ horizontal},\gamma_{0}=x,\gamma_{1}=y\,\biggr\},

where
|\cdot|=\sqrt{\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle}
.

We remark that horizontal paths necessarily satisfy, for almost every
t\in[0,1]
,

(2.10)

\dot{\gamma}_{t}=\sum_{i=1}^{d_{1}}V_{i}(\gamma_{t})\langle\dot{\gamma}_{t},V_{i}(\gamma_{t})\rangle\eqqcolon\sum_{i=1}^{d_{1}}V_{i}(\gamma_{t})\dot{h}^{i}_{t},

and hence are in one-to-one correspondence with absolutely continuous

h\in\mathrm{AC}([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}})
. We have that
h=\pi_{1}\gamma
for the first-level projection
\pi_{1}
from
Eq.

2.6
.

Definition 2.7

(Canonical lift)
.

The
canonical lift

\Psi\colon\mathrm{AC}([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}})\to C([0,1],\mathbb{G})
is defined by
\Psi(h)\coloneqq\gamma
, where
\gamma\in C([0,1],\mathbb{G})
and
h\in\mathrm{AC}([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}})
are related by
(
2.10
)
.
Explicitly, we have

\dot{\gamma}^{(1)}_{t}=\dot{h}_{t},\quad\dot{\gamma}^{(2)}_{t}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j}w_{ij}h^{i}_{t}\dot{h}^{j}_{t}=\frac{1}{2}\mathsf{W}\big(h_{t}\otimes\dot{h}_{t}\big),\quad\text{for}\;t\in[0,1].

By the Chow–Rashevskii theorem,
d_{\mathrm{CC}}
is in fact a distance, which is also left-invariant and homogeneous with respect to the dilations defined in (
2.7
). The metric space
(\mathbb{G},d_{\mathrm{CC}})
is a Polish and geodesic space (see, e.g.
[
AS20
, Section 2.4]
). We let
|\cdot|_{\mathbb{G}}
denote the norm induced by
d_{\mathrm{CC}}
on
\mathbb{G}
.
One can also equip
\mathbb{G}
with the
gauge distance

d_{g}
defined by

\displaystyle d_{g}(x,y)=|(y^{-1}x)^{(1)}|+|(y^{-1}x)^{(2)}|^{\frac{1}{2}},

for
x,y\in\mathbb{G}
. All homogeneous norms on
\mathbb{G}
are equivalent. In particular, there exists a constant
\kappa\in(0,\infty)
such that

(2.11)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\kappa}d_{g}(x,y)\leq d_{\mathrm{CC}}(x,y)\leq\kappa\,d_{g}(x,y);

see, e.g.
[
BLU07
, Proposition 5.1.4]
.

Remark 2.8

(Metric derivative)
.

The metric derivative of a curve
\gamma\colon[0,1]\to\mathbb{G}
at
t\in[0,1]
is defined by

|\dot{\gamma}_{t}|_{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}\coloneqq\lim_{s\to t}\frac{d_{\mathrm{CC}}(\gamma(s),\gamma(t))}{|s-t|}.

If
\gamma
is absolutely continuous, the metric derivative exists for almost every
t\in[0,1]
, and
|\dot{\gamma}_{t}|_{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}=|\dot{\gamma}_{t}|
; see
[
Mon01
, Theorem 1.3.5]
.
Moreover, the metric derivative is minimal in the sense that
|\dot{\gamma}_{t}|_{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}\leq m

for all
m\in L^{1}([0,1])
with
d_{\mathrm{CC}}(\gamma(s),\gamma(t))\leq\int_{s}^{t}m(r)\,\mathrm{d}r
,
0\leq s<t\leq 1
; see
[
AGS08
, Theorem 1.1.2]
.

Remark 2.9

(Free step-2 Carnot groups)
.

The free step-
2
nilpotent case
\mathbb{G}=\mathbb{F}^{d_{1},2}
amounts to

\mathbb{G}\cong\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\oplus\mathfrak{so}(d_{1})
(after identifying the exterior algebra
\wedge^{2}\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}
with
\mathfrak{so}(d_{1})
). The space
\mathfrak{so}(d_{1})
is
spanned by
\{\,e_{[i,j]}:1\leq i<j\leq d_{1}\,\}
, where

e_{[i,j]}\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}(e_{i}\otimes e_{j}-e_{j}\otimes e_{i})
, and has dimension
d_{2}^{\ast}=d_{1}(d_{1}{-}1)/2
.
Writing the bracket as
[e_{i},e_{j}]=e_{[i,j]}
, the structure constants
w_{ij}^{[p,q]}

reduce to Kronecker symbols.

All other step-
2
Carnot groups can be seen as quotient groups of the free group, captured by

d_{2}\leq d_{2}^{\ast}
and the structure constants. For instance, the
(2n+1)
-dimensional Heisenberg group

\mathbb{H}^{n}\cong\mathbb{R}^{2n}\oplus\mathbb{R}
has
d_{1}=2n
,
d_{2}=1
, and

w_{1,2}=w_{3,4}=\cdots=w_{2n-1,2n}=1
(flip sign upon interchanging indices, zero otherwise).
For
d_{1}=2
, we recover the familiar example
\mathbb{H}=\mathbb{H}^{1}\cong\mathbb{F}^{2,2}
. Letting
(x,y,z)\in\mathbb{H}
denote a canonical element of
\mathbb{H}
, the left-invariant vector fields are given by

V_{1}=\partial_{x}+\tfrac{1}{2}y\partial_{z},\quad V_{2}=\partial_{y}-\tfrac{1}{2}x\partial_{z},\quad V_{3}=[V_{1},V_{2}]=\partial_{z}.

Remark 2.10

(Heisenberg-type groups)
.

A special class of step-
2
Carnot groups is the class of
Heisenberg-type groups
, or H-type groups for short, which enjoy additional properties.
Most importantly for us, Talagrand inequalities are known to hold on H-type groups; see Section
5.3
.
We refer the interested reader to
[
BLU07
, Chapter 18]
.

A step-
2
Carnot group
\mathbb{G}\cong\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\oplus\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}
is an
H-type group
if, for each
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}
, there exists a linear map
\mathsf{J}_{\mathbb{G}}(z)\colon\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\to\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}
such that

\mathsf{J}_{\mathbb{G}}({z})^{2}=-|z|^{2}\mathrm{id}\quad\text{and}\quad\big\langle\mathsf{W}(x{\otimes}y),z\big\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}}=\langle\mathsf{J}_{\mathbb{G}}({z)}x,y\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}}\quad\forall\,x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}.

Note that, necessarily,
d_{1}\in 2\mathbb{N}
and
d_{2}\leq d_{1}/2
. As the name suggests, the Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{n}\cong\mathbb{R}^{2n}\oplus\mathbb{R}
is the canonical example of an H-type group, where the map
\mathsf{J}_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\colon\mathbb{R}^{2n}\to\mathbb{R}^{2n}

is given by

\mathsf{J}_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}(z)=\begin{pmatrix}0&-zI_{n}\\
zI_{n}&0\end{pmatrix},\quad z\in\mathbb{R}.

Brownian motion on
\mathbb{G}

Let
B=(B_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}
be a
d_{1}
-dimensional Brownian motion on a filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})
. We define the Brownian motion
\bm{B}
on
\mathbb{G}
as the continuous

\mathbb{G}
-valued Markov process with generator
\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}
, that is obtained by solving the SDE

(2.12)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\bm{B}_{t}=\sum_{i=1}^{d_{1}}V^{i}(\bm{B}_{t})\,\mathrm{d}B^{i}_{t},

more explicitly written as

\mathrm{d}\bm{B}^{(1)}_{t}=\mathrm{d}B_{t},\quad\mathrm{d}\bm{B}^{(2)}_{t}=\frac{1}{2}\mathsf{W}(B_{t}\otimes\mathrm{d}B_{t}).

Note that, since
B=(B_{1},\ldots,B_{d_{1}})
is a standard Brownian motion, there is
no difference between Itô and Stratonovich integration here.

The Brownian motion
\bm{B}
takes values in the space

\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}\coloneqq C_{0}([0,1],\mathbb{G})

of continuous
\mathbb{G}
-valued paths started from the origin. We write
\bm{\mu}=\mathrm{Law}(\bm{B})
and
\mu=\mathrm{Law}(B)
, and
\bm{\mu}_{t}=\mathrm{Law}(\bm{B}_{t})
,
\mu_{t}=\mathrm{Law}(B_{t})
, for
t>0
. For a path
\bm{\omega}\in\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}
, let
\bm{\omega}_{s,t}=\bm{\omega}_{s}^{-1}\bm{\omega}_{t}
,
s\leq t
, denote its increments.

We equip the space
\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}
with the uniform topology induced by the metric
d_{\infty}
defined by

(2.13)

\displaystyle d_{\infty}(\bm{\omega},\overline{\bm{\omega}})\coloneqq\sup_{t\in[0,1]}d_{\mathrm{CC}}(\bm{\omega}_{t},\overline{\bm{\omega}}_{t}).

Note that
(\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}},d_{\infty})
is a Polish space.

By Hörmander’s theorem,
\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}
is a hypoelliptic operator, and so the associated heat kernel

\mathfrak{p}\colon(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{G}\to(0,\infty)
is smooth
[
Hör67
,
Koh73
,
Hai11
,
BB15
]
. Note that, for all
t>0
, the density of
\bm{\mu}_{t}
is
\mathfrak{p}_{t}\colon\mathbb{G}\to(0,\infty)
. We also define the heat semigroup
P_{t}=e^{t\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}}
, for
t>0
, by

(2.14)

\displaystyle P_{t}f(\bm{x})=\int_{\mathbb{G}}f(\bm{x}\bm{y}^{-1})\mathfrak{p}_{t}(\bm{y})\,\mathrm{d}\bm{y}=\int_{\mathbb{G}}f(\bm{y})\mathfrak{p}_{t}(\bm{y}^{-1}\bm{x})\,\mathrm{d}\bm{y},\quad\bm{x}\in\mathbb{G},

for any
f\in L^{1}(\bm{\mu}_{t})
, with
P_{0}
equal to the identity operator.

Shifting
\mathbb{G}
-valued paths

Let
\mathrm{AC}_{0}([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{d})
denote the space of absolutely continuous curves started from the origin, and recall the canonical lift
\Psi\colon\mathrm{AC}_{0}([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}})\to\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}
from
Definition

2.7
.
Since
(\mathbb{G},d_{\mathrm{CC}})
is a geodesic space, the following approximation lemma is immediate (cf.
[
FV10
, Lemma 5.19, Theorem 7.32]
).

Lemma 2.11

(Geodesic approximations)
.

Every continuous
\mathbb{G}
-valued path
\bm{\omega}
on
[0,T]
is the uniform limit
of absolutely continuous horizontal curves; i.e.
\bm{\omega}^{n}=\Psi(\omega^{n})
, with
\omega^{n}\in\mathrm{AC}_{0}([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}})
and
\Psi
defined in Definition
2.7
.

We now extend the canonical lift to a lift map on the space
\Omega=C_{0}([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}})
of continuous curves started from the origin.
Note that the geodesic approximation of
\bm{\omega}=(\bm{\omega}^{(1)},\bm{\omega}^{(2)})
from
Lemma

2.11
depends on both
\bm{\omega}^{(1)}
and
\bm{\omega}^{(2)}
. Thus we also introduce an approximation based only on
\bm{\omega}^{(1)}
in order to extend the canonical lift.
For a continuous path
\omega\in\Omega
, let
\widehat{\omega}^{n}\in\mathrm{AC}_{0}([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}})
denote the piecewise-linear approximation of
\omega
on the dyadic grid
(k2^{-n})_{k\in\{0,\dotsc,2^{n}\}}
, for
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and note that
\widehat{\omega}^{n}\to\omega
with respect to the uniform topology on
\Omega
.

Definition 2.12
.

(Lift)
Extend the canonical lift
\Psi\colon\mathrm{AC}_{0}([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}})\to\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}
to the
lift map

\Psi\colon\Omega\to\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}
by

(2.15)

\displaystyle\Psi(\omega)\coloneqq\begin{cases}\lim_{n\to\infty}\Psi(\widehat{\omega}^{n}),&\text{if the limit exists},\\
0,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

for
\omega\in\Omega
. Define the
domain
of
\Psi
as
\mathrm{Dom}(\Psi)\coloneqq\{\,\omega\in\Omega\;:\;\lim_{n\to\infty}\Psi(\widehat{\omega}^{n})\;\text{exists}\,\}\subset\Omega
.

The following statement holds by a minor modification to the proof of
[
FV10
, Corollary 13.19]
in the general step-2 Carnot setting.

Proposition 2.13
.

Let
B
be a
d_{1}
-dimensional Brownian motion. Then
\bm{B}
as defined in
Eq.

2.12
satisfies
\bm{B}=\Psi(B)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\Psi(\widehat{B}^{n})
almost surely. In particular, it follows that
\mu(\mathrm{Dom}(\Psi))=1
.

Having defined the lift
\Psi
for general curves
\omega\in C_{0}([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}})
, we can now formulate the following lemma relating absolutely continuous measures
\bm{\nu}\ll\bm{\mu}
on
\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}
with absolutely continuous measures
\nu\ll\mu
on
\Omega
.

Lemma 2.14
.

Let
\bm{\nu}
be a Borel probability measure on
\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}
. Then
\bm{\nu}\ll\bm{\mu}
if and only if there exists a Borel probability measure
\nu
on
\Omega
such that
\nu\ll\mu
and
\bm{\nu}=\Psi_{\sharp}\nu
.

Proof.

First suppose that there exists
\nu\ll\mu
with
\bm{\nu}=\Psi_{\sharp}\nu
. Then, for any Borel
A\subseteq\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}
with
\bm{\mu}(A)=0
, we have that
\mu(\Psi^{-1}(A))=\bm{\mu}(A)=0
, and so

\displaystyle\bm{\nu}(A)=\nu(\Psi^{-1}(A))=0.

Now suppose that
\bm{\nu}\ll\bm{\mu}
.
Let
\bm{\rho}\coloneqq\frac{d\bm{\nu}}{d\bm{\mu}}
and define the measure
\nu
via
\frac{d\nu}{d\mu}=\bm{\rho}\circ\Psi
so that
\nu\ll\mu
.
By
Proposition

2.13
,
\bm{\mu}(\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}\setminus\Psi(\Omega))=0
and
\bm{\mu}=\Psi_{\sharp}\mu
. Therefore

\displaystyle\nu(\Omega)=\int_{\Omega}\bm{\rho}(\Psi(\omega))\mu(\mathrm{d}\omega)=\int_{\Psi(\Omega)}\bm{\rho}(\bm{\omega})\bm{\mu}(\mathrm{d}\bm{\omega})=\int_{\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}}\bm{\rho}(\bm{\omega})\bm{\mu}(\mathrm{d}\bm{\omega})=1,

and so
\nu
is a probability measure on
\Omega
.
Moreover, for any Borel measurable
A\subset\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}
,

\displaystyle\bm{\nu}(A)=\int_{A}\bm{\rho}(\bm{\omega})\bm{\mu}(\mathrm{d}\bm{\omega})=\int_{\Psi^{-1}(A)}\bm{\rho}(\Psi(\omega))\mu(\mathrm{d}\omega)=\int_{\Psi^{-1}(A)}\nu(\mathrm{d}\omega)=\nu(\Psi^{-1}(A))

Hence
\bm{\nu}=\Psi_{\sharp}\nu
.
∎

We show that the following shift map is well defined in
Proposition

2.16
below.

Definition 2.15
.

(Shift map)
For
h\in\mathrm{AC}_{0}([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}})
define the shift map
T_{h}\colon\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}\to\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}
by

(2.16)

\displaystyle T_{h}\bm{\omega}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\Psi(\omega^{n}+h),\quad\bm{\omega}\in\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}},

where
(\omega^{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
denotes the geodesic approximation from
Lemma

2.11
.

Proposition 2.16
.

The shift map defined in
Definition

2.15
satisfies the following:

(i)

For
h\in\mathcal{H}
, the shift map
T_{h}
is well defined.

(ii)

For
\bm{X}=(\bm{X}^{(1)},\bm{X}^{(2)})\in\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}
and
h\in\mathcal{H}
the shift map
T_{h}\bm{X}
is explicitly given by

\displaystyle(T_{h}\bm{X})^{(1)}_{t}=\bm{X}^{(1)}_{t}+h_{t},\quad(T_{h}\bm{X})^{(2)}_{t}=\bm{X}^{(2)}_{t}+\frac{1}{2}\mathsf{W}\left(\mathbb{X}^{1}_{t}+\mathbb{X}^{2}_{t}+\mathbb{X}^{3}_{t}\right),

where

\mathrm{d}\mathbb{X}^{1}_{t}=\bm{X}^{(1)}_{t}\otimes\mathrm{d}h_{t},\quad\mathrm{d}\mathbb{X}^{2}_{t}=h_{t}\otimes\mathrm{d}\bm{X}^{(1)}_{t},\quad\mathrm{d}\mathbb{X}^{3}_{t}=h_{t}\otimes\mathrm{d}h_{t}.

(iii)

If
\bm{X}
is given by
\bm{X}=\Psi(X)
for
X\in\mathrm{Dom}(\Psi)
, then, for
h\in\mathcal{H}
,

(2.17)

\displaystyle T_{h}\bm{X}=T_{h}\Psi(X)=\Psi(X+h).

(iv)

The map
\mathrm{AC}_{0}([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}})\times\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}\to\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}},\,(h,\bm{X})\mapsto T_{h}\bm{X}
is continuous.

Proof.

If
\bm{X}=\Psi(X)
is an absolutely continuous horizontal curve, we have

T_{h}\bm{X}=\Psi(X+h)
, for
h\in\mathcal{H}
. Then (ii) follows by definition of the lift on
\mathrm{AC}_{0}([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}})
.
The representation of (ii), with cross integrals in
\mathbb{X}^{1},\mathbb{X}^{2}
, remains meaningful
when
X
is only continuous, by basic properties of Riemann–Stieltjes integration. By continuity properties of Riemann–Stieltjes integration, we obtain that the limit in (
2.16
) exists, so that (i) and (ii) follow.

Now let
X\in\mathrm{Dom}(\Psi)
, so that
\bm{X}=\Psi(X)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\Psi(\widehat{X}^{n})
for the piecewise linear approximation
(\widehat{X}^{n})
. Let
h\in\mathcal{H}
. Then
\bm{X}^{(1)}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\widehat{X}^{n}
and
\bm{X}^{(2)}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{2}\mathsf{W}\mathbb{X}^{0,n}
, where
\mathrm{d}\mathbb{X}^{0,n}_{t}=\widehat{X}^{n}_{t}\otimes\mathrm{d}\widehat{X}^{n}_{t}
.
By the definition of
\Psi
on
\mathrm{AC}_{0}([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we have that

\Psi(\widehat{X}^{n}+h)=\Big(\widehat{X}^{n}+h,\,\frac{1}{2}\mathsf{W}(\mathbb{X}^{0,n}_{t}+\mathbb{X}^{1,n}_{t}+\mathbb{X}^{2,n}_{t}+\mathbb{X}^{3}_{t})\Big),

with
\mathbb{X}^{1,n}
,
\mathbb{X}^{2,n}
defined as in (ii) with
X
replaced by
\widehat{X}^{n}
, and
\mathbb{X}^{3}
defined as in (ii). By continuity of the Riemann–Stieltjes integral and (ii), we deduce that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\Psi(\widehat{X}^{n}+h)=T_{h}\Psi(X)
. This proves (iii).

Finally, (iv) also follows from continuity properties of the Riemann–Stieltjes integral and the representation from (ii).
∎

Remark 2.17
.

As is plain from Proposition
2.16
, part (ii), we can translate any
\bm{X}=(\bm{X}^{(1)},\bm{X}^{(2)})\in\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}
in the direction of any absolutely continuous

. The situation is more complicated when dealing with Carnot groups of level strictly greater than
2
, cf.
[

, Section 9.4.6]
, or when

has less regularity, as is the case for Cameron–Martin paths of fractional Brownian motion with Hurst parameter

; cf.
Section

4
. In these cases, one has to incorporate suitable
p
-variation or Hölder rough path regularity on the path space of
\bm{X}
.

Remark 2.18
.

For any
h\in\mathcal{H}
and
\bm{X}\in\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}
, manipulating the expression for
T_{h}\bm{X}
from
Proposition

2.16
gives
\bm{X}_{s,t}^{-1}(T_{h}\bm{X})_{s,t}=(\bm{Z}^{(1)}_{s,t},\bm{Z}^{(2)}_{s,t})
, for

,
s\leq t
, where

\displaystyle\bm{Z}^{(1)}_{s,t}=h_{s,t},\quad\bm{Z}^{(2)}_{s,t}=\frac{1}{2}\Big(\mathbb{X}^{1}_{s,t}+\mathbb{X}^{2}_{s,t}+\mathbb{X}^{3}_{s,t}+2X_{t}\otimes h_{s}-(X_{s}\otimes h_{s}+X_{t}\otimes h_{t}+h_{s}\otimes h_{t})\Big).

Also define
\bm{h}=\Psi(h)
. Then, after integrating by parts, we find that, for any

with

\displaystyle\bm{\theta}_{s,t}\coloneqq\bm{h}^{-1}_{s,t}\bm{X}_{s,t}^{-1}(T_{h}\bm{X})_{s,t}=\Big(0,\int_{s}^{t}\mathsf{W}h_{s,r}\otimes\mathrm{d}X_{r}\Big).

The increments
\bm{\theta}_{s,t}
can be interpreted as an error of non-commutativity
between the increments of the shifted path
T_{h}\bm{X}
and the increments
of the (right-)translation
\bm{X}\bm{h}
by the lifted path
\bm{h}
.

We now use the shift map to define a cost function on
\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}
.

Definition 2.19
.

Define a cost function
C_{\mathcal{H}}\colon\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}\times\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}\to[0,\infty]
by

(2.18)

C_{\mathcal{H}}(\bm{X},\bm{Y})\coloneqq\begin{cases}\|h\|_{\mathcal{H}},&\text{if}\;\bm{Y}=T_{h}\bm{X},\;\text{for some}\;h\in\mathcal{H},\\
+\infty,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Lemma 2.20
.

The cost
C_{\mathcal{H}}\colon\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}\times\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}\to[0,\infty]
is lower semicontinuous.

Proof.

Let
\bm{X},\bm{Y}\in\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}
and let
(\bm{X}^{n}),(\bm{Y}^{n})\subset\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}
be sequences such that
(\bm{X}^{n},\bm{Y}^{n})\to(\bm{X},\bm{Y})
. We may assume that there exists a subsequence
n_{k}\to\infty
such that
\bm{Y}^{n_{k}}=T_{h^{n_{k}}}\bm{X}^{n_{k}}
, where
h^{n_{k}}=\pi_{1}\bm{Y}^{n_{k}}-\pi_{1}\bm{X}^{n_{k}}\in\mathcal{H}
, and
\liminf_{n\to\infty}C_{\mathcal{H}}(\bm{X}^{n},\bm{Y}^{n})=\lim_{k\to\infty}C_{\mathcal{H}}(\bm{X}^{n_{k}},\bm{Y}^{n_{k}})=:I<\infty
.
Then we have that

\displaystyle I=\lim_{k\to\infty}C_{\mathcal{H}}(\bm{X}^{n_{k}},\bm{Y}^{n_{k}})=\liminf_{k\to\infty}\|h^{n_{k}}\|_{\mathcal{H}}\geq\|h\|_{\mathcal{H}},

where
h=\pi_{1}\bm{Y}-\pi_{1}\bm{X}\in\mathcal{H}
. By the continuity of the shift shown in
Proposition

2.16
(iv),
\bm{Y}=T_{h}\bm{X}
, and so
C_{\mathcal{H}}(\bm{X},\bm{Y})=\|h\|_{\mathcal{H}}
.
∎

2.3.
Discussion on the choice of the cost function
C_{\mathcal{H}}

The choice of cost function
C_{\mathcal{H}}
is natural in the sense that
it arises as the
\Gamma
-limit
of the sequence
C_{n}
, as shown in
Section

6.4
. Moreover,
C_{\mathcal{H}}
has the crucial property that whenever
H(\bm{\nu}\|\bm{\mu})=+\infty
, also
\mathrm{T}_{C_{\mathcal{H}},2}(\bm{\mu},\bm{\nu})=+\infty
. Indeed, supposing that
\mathrm{T}_{C_{\mathcal{H}},2}(\bm{\mu},\bm{\nu})<\infty
, there exists a coupling
\bm{\lambda}\in\Pi(\bm{\mu},\bm{\nu})
such that

\displaystyle\bm{\lambda}(\{\,(\bm{\omega},\overline{\bm{\omega}})\in\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}\times\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}:\;\overline{\bm{\omega}}=T_{\overline{\omega}-\omega}\bm{\omega},\,\overline{\omega}-\omega\in\mathcal{H}\,\})=1,

and
\int C_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}(\bm{\omega},\overline{\bm{\omega}})\,\mathrm{d}\bm{\lambda}(\bm{\omega},\overline{\bm{\omega}})<\infty
.
We have that
\bm{\mu}=\Psi_{\sharp}\mu
, by
Proposition

2.13
, and combining this with
Eq.

2.17
from
Proposition

2.16
gives

\displaystyle\bm{\lambda}(\{\,(\bm{\omega},\overline{\bm{\omega}})\in\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}\times\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}:\;\bm{\omega}=\Psi(\omega),\,\overline{\bm{\omega}}=\Psi(\overline{\omega}),\,\overline{\omega}-\omega\in\mathcal{H}\,\})=1.

Thus there exists
\nu\in\mathcal{P}(\Omega)
such that
\bm{\nu}=\Psi_{\sharp}\nu
. By Itô representation and Girsanov’s theorem, we also have that
\nu\ll\mu
. Hence
\bm{\nu}=\Psi_{\sharp}\nu\ll\Psi_{\sharp}\mu=\bm{\mu}
, and
H(\bm{\nu}\|\bm{\mu})<\infty
.

This is in contrast to the cost function
\widetilde{C}_{\mathcal{H}}\coloneqq c_{\mathcal{H}}\circ(\pi_{1}\times\pi_{1})\colon\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}\times\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}\to[0,\infty]
, which appears in the cost-information inequality in
[
Rie17
, Corollary 1.4]
. Indeed, consider the Brownian motion
\bm{B}=(B,\mathbb{A})
on the Heisenberg group
\mathbb{G}\cong\mathbb{R}^{2}\oplus\mathbb{R}
. Let
\bm{\nu}=\mathrm{Law}(\bm{X})
, where
\bm{X}=(B,\mathbb{X})
is defined as follows. Let
M\in(0,\infty)
, and define
\mathbb{X}_{0}=0
and
\mathbb{X}_{s,t}=\mathbb{A}_{s,t}+(t-s)M
, for all
s,t\in[0,1]
with
s<t
. Since
\bm{X}
only differs from
\bm{B}
in the second component, we see that

\displaystyle 0\leq\mathrm{T}_{\widetilde{C}_{\mathcal{H}},2}^{2}(\bm{\mu},\bm{\nu})\leq\mathbb{E}\Big[\widetilde{C}_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}(\bm{B},\bm{X})\Big]=\mathbb{E}\Big[c_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}(B,B)\Big]=0.

However,
\bm{\nu}
is not absolutely continuous with respect to
\bm{\mu}
. Indeed, supposing that
\bm{\nu}\ll\bm{\mu}
,
Lemma

2.14
implies that
\bm{\nu}=\Psi_{\sharp}\nu
for some probability measure
\nu\ll\mu
. It follows that
\bm{\nu}(\Psi(\Omega))=\nu(\Omega)=1
. On the other hand, since
\bm{\nu}=\mathrm{Law}(\bm{X})
with
\bm{X}=(B,\mathbb{X})\neq\Psi(B)
, we see that
\bm{\nu}(\Psi(\Omega))<1
, which is a contradiction. Hence
H(\bm{\nu}\|\bm{\mu})=+\infty
.

3.
Talagrand for Brownian motion on Carnot groups – Direct approach via Föllmer drift

In this section, we give a first proof of Talagrand’s
\mathcal{T}_{2}
inequality for the law of Brownian motion on a step-
2
Carnot group. We follow the strategy of
[
Leh13
]
and
[
Föl22
]
, using Föllmer’s intrinsic drift from
[
Föl86
,
Föl88
]
. Moreover, we show that equality is attained in the
\mathcal{T}_{2}
inequality when restricting to adapted couplings, as was shown in the classical case in
[
Las18
, Lemma 5]
and
[
Föl22
, Theorem 3]
.

We first give a characterisation of adapted couplings on
\mathcal{P}(\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}\times\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}})
. In particular, we show that our definition of adapted couplings in
Definition

2.4
is consistent with that of
[
Föl22
, Definition 1]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose that
\nu\ll\mu
. Then
\lambda\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)
is an adapted coupling if and only if there exists a filtered probability space
(\widetilde{\Omega},\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_{t}),\mathbb{Q})
on which processes
X
,
Y
are defined such that
X
is a Brownian motion,
Y
is an adapted process, and
\lambda=\mathrm{Law}(X,Y)
under
\mathbb{Q}
.

Suppose that
\bm{\nu}\ll\bm{\mu}
. Then
\bm{\lambda}\in\Pi(\bm{\mu},\bm{\nu})
is an adapted coupling if and only if there exists
\nu\ll\mu
and an adapted coupling
\lambda\in\Pi_{\mathrm{ad}}(\mu,\nu)
such that
\bm{\lambda}=(\Psi\times\Psi)_{\sharp}\lambda
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\nu\ll\mu
. By
[
Las18
, Lemma 4]
, our
Definition

2.4
of adapted couplings is equivalent to the symmetric counterpart of
[
Las18
, Definition 1]
(see
[
Las18
, Section 4.1]
). Then the first claim follows from
[
Las18
, Propositions 3 and 4]
.

Now suppose that
\bm{\nu}\ll\bm{\mu}
. By
Lemma

2.14
, there exists
\nu\ll\mu
such that
\bm{\nu}=\Psi_{\sharp}\nu
. If
X
is an
\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}
-valued process with natural filtration
(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}
completed with respect to the law of
X
, and
\bm{X}=\Psi(X)
is a
\mathbb{G}
-valued process with natural filtration
(\bm{\mathcal{F}}_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}
completed with respect to the law of
\bm{X}
, then
\bm{\mathcal{F}}_{t}=\Psi(\mathcal{F}_{t})
, for all
t\in[0,1]
. Since
\bm{\mu}=\Psi_{\sharp}\mu
and
\bm{\nu}=\Psi_{\sharp}\nu
, we have that
\bm{\lambda}\in\Pi_{\mathrm{ad}}(\bm{\mu},\bm{\nu})
if and only if
\bm{\lambda}=(\Psi\times\Psi)_{\sharp}\lambda
for some
\lambda\in\Pi_{\mathrm{ad}}(\mu,\nu)
.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

By
Lemma

3.1
, if
\bm{\lambda}\in\Pi_{\mathrm{ad}}(\bm{\mu},\bm{\nu})
, then there exist
\bm{X}=\Psi(X)
,
\bm{Y}=\Psi(Y)
defined on some filtered probability space
(\widetilde{\Omega},\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_{t}),\mathbb{Q})
such that
\bm{\lambda}=\mathrm{Law}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\bm{X},\bm{Y})
, where
\bm{X}
is a Brownian motion on
\mathbb{G}
and
\bm{Y}
is an adapted
\mathbb{G}
-valued process, and
\mathrm{Law}_{\mathbb{Q}}
denotes the law under
\mathbb{Q}
. Then, letting
\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}
denote expectation with respect to
\mathbb{Q}
, we have

\displaystyle\int C_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}(\bm{\omega},\overline{\bm{\omega}})\,\mathrm{d}\bm{\lambda}=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}[C_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}(\bm{X},\bm{Y})].

We now prove the main result of this section, showing that
\bm{\mu}
satisfies a
\mathcal{T}_{2}
inequality, and that equality holds when restricting to adapted couplings.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\bm{\nu}\ll\bm{\mu}
.
Then there exists
\nu\ll\mu
such that
\bm{\nu}=\Psi_{\sharp}\nu
, and there exists a predictable process
b^{\nu}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
b^{\nu}\in L^{2}
,
\nu
-almost surely, such that
B^{\nu}\coloneqq B-\int_{0}^{\cdot}b^{\nu}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t
is a Brownian motion under
\nu
, and
\bm{\lambda}^{\ast}=\mathrm{Law}_{\nu}(\Psi(B^{\nu}),\Psi(B))
is the unique optimal adapted coupling of
\bm{\mu}
and
\bm{\nu}
with

(3.1)

\displaystyle\mathrm{T}_{C_{\mathcal{H}},2}(\bm{\nu},\bm{\mu})^{2}\leq\mathrm{T}_{C_{\mathcal{H}},2}^{\mathrm{ad}}(\bm{\mu},\bm{\nu})^{2}=\mathbb{E}_{\nu}[C_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}(\Psi(B^{\nu}),\Psi(B))]=2H(\bm{\nu}\|\bm{\mu}).

In particular,
\bm{\mu}\in\mathcal{T}_{2}(\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}},C_{\mathcal{H}},1)
.

Proof.

By
Lemma

2.14
, there exists a probability measure
\nu
on
\Omega
with
\nu\ll\mu
and
\Psi_{\sharp}\nu=\bm{\nu}
. Writing
\bm{\rho}\coloneqq\frac{\mathrm{d}\bm{\nu}}{\mathrm{d}\bm{\mu}}
, we have
\frac{\mathrm{d}\nu}{\mathrm{d}\mu}=\bm{\rho}\circ\Psi
. Since
\bm{\mu}(\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}\setminus\Psi(\Omega))=0
, by
Proposition

2.13
, we obtain that

\displaystyle H(\bm{\nu}\|\bm{\mu})=\int_{\Psi(\Omega)}\bm{\rho}(\bm{\omega})\log(\bm{\rho}(\bm{\omega}))\,\mathrm{d}\bm{\mu}(\bm{\omega})=\int_{\Omega}\bm{\rho}(\Psi(\omega))\log(\bm{\rho}(\Psi(\omega))\,\mathrm{d}\mu(\omega)=H(\nu\|\mu).

We can apply
[
Föl22
, Proposition 1]
to obtain that there exists a predictable process
b^{\nu}
with
b^{\nu}\in L^{2}
,
\nu
-almost surely, such that
B^{\nu}=B-\int_{0}^{\cdot}b^{\nu}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t
is a Brownian motion under
\nu
with

(3.2)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\nu}[\|B^{\nu}-B\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}]=2H(\nu\|\mu)=2H(\bm{\nu}\|\bm{\mu}).

Moreover, from
[
Föl22
, Theorem 3]
it follows that
\lambda^{\ast}=\mathrm{Law}_{\nu}(B^{\nu},B)
is the unique optimal adapted coupling between
\mu
and
\nu
. By
Lemma

3.1
,
\bm{\lambda}^{\ast}=\mathrm{Law}_{\nu}(\Psi(B^{\nu}),\Psi(B))
is an adapted coupling of
\bm{\mu}=\Psi_{\sharp}\mu
and
\bm{\nu}=\Psi_{\sharp}\nu
. Thus, using the definition of
C_{\mathcal{H}}
from
Eq.

2.18
,

(3.3)

\displaystyle\mathrm{T}_{c_{\mathcal{H}},2}^{\mathrm{ad}}(\mu,\nu)^{2}=\mathbb{E}_{\nu}[\|B^{\nu}-B\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}]=\mathbb{E}_{\nu}[C_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}(\Psi(B^{\nu}),\Psi(B))]\geq\mathrm{T}_{C_{\mathcal{H}},2}^{\mathrm{ad}}(\bm{\mu},\bm{\nu})^{2}.

On the other hand, since
c_{\mathcal{H}}\circ(\pi_{1}\times\pi_{1})\leq C_{\mathcal{H}}
, applying
Lemma

3.1
gives

\displaystyle\mathrm{T}_{c_{\mathcal{H}},2}^{\mathrm{ad}}(\mu,\nu)^{2}=\inf_{\lambda\in\Pi_{\mathrm{ad}}(\mu,\nu)}\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}c_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}(\omega,\overline{\omega})\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(\omega,\overline{\omega})

\displaystyle=\inf_{\bm{\lambda}\in\Pi_{\mathrm{ad}}(\bm{\mu},\bm{\nu})}\int_{\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}\times\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}}c_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}(\pi_{1}\bm{\omega},\pi_{1}\overline{\bm{\omega}})\,\mathrm{d}\bm{\lambda}(\bm{\omega},\overline{\bm{\omega}})

\displaystyle\leq\inf_{\bm{\lambda}\in\Pi_{\mathrm{ad}}(\bm{\mu},\bm{\nu})}\int_{\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}\times\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}}C_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}(\bm{\omega},\overline{\bm{\omega}})\,\mathrm{d}\bm{\lambda}(\bm{\omega},\overline{\bm{\omega}})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{T}_{C_{\mathcal{H}},2}^{\mathrm{ad}}(\bm{\mu},\bm{\nu})^{2}.

Hence we have optimality of
\bm{\lambda}^{\ast}
and

\displaystyle\mathrm{T}^{\mathrm{ad}}_{C_{\mathcal{H}},2}(\bm{\nu},\bm{\mu})^{2}=\mathbb{E}_{\nu}[C_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}(\Psi(B^{\nu}),\Psi(B))]=\mathbb{E}_{\nu}[\|B^{\nu}-B\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}]=\mathrm{T}_{c_{\mathcal{H}},2}^{\mathrm{ad}}(\mu,\nu)^{2}.

Applying (
3.2
) gives (
3.1
), and uniqueness of the optimiser follows by
Lemma

3.1
.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

Suppose that
h
is an adapted process with

almost surely, and let
\bm{\nu}=\mathrm{Law}(T_{h}\bm{B})
. Then we can take

in

. Indeed, by Girsanov’s theorem,

, and

.

4.
Talagrand for Gaussian rough paths – Direct approach via contraction

We now give an alternative proof of Talagrand’s
\mathcal{T}_{2}
inequality in a more general setting, following the contraction approach of
[
Rie17
]
.

Let
Z
be a
d
-dimensional continuous Gaussian process that admits a level
2
“rough path” lift
\bm{Z}=\bm{Z}(\omega)
with
\bm{Z}\in\mathcal{D}
almost surely, where
\mathcal{D}
is a suitable
p
-variation (or
\beta
-Hölder) rough path space
(cf.
[
FV10
, Chapter 15]
).
We refer to
[
FV10
, Chapter 15]
for conditions under which a Gaussian process can be lifted to a Gaussian rough path. Here we simply assume that such a lift exists. Let
\nu\in\mathcal{P}(\Omega)
denote the law of the Gaussian process
Z
,
\bm{\nu}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{D})
the law of
\bm{Z}
, and
\mathcal{H}_{\nu}
the Cameron–Martin space of
Z
(cf.
[
Jan97
, Chapter 8, Section 4]
).

A
\mathcal{T}_{2}
inequality is known to hold for general Gaussian processes with
\alpha=1
and cost

\displaystyle\widetilde{c}_{\mathcal{H}_{\nu}}(x,y)=\begin{cases}\|h\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\nu}},&\text{if}\;x-y\in\mathcal{H}_{\nu},\\
+\infty,&\text{otherwise};\end{cases}

that is
\nu\in\mathcal{T}_{2}(\Omega,\widetilde{c}_{\mathcal{H}_{\nu}},1)
; see
[
FÜ04
, Theorem 3.1]
and
[
Rie17
, Theorem 1.2]
.

We work under the following assumption.

Assumption 4.1
.

Suppose that there exists
\widetilde{\Omega}\subseteq\Omega
with
\nu(\widetilde{\Omega})=1
such that

(i)

There exists a Borel-measurable lift map
\widetilde{\Psi}\colon\Omega\to\mathcal{D}
with
\pi_{1}\widetilde{\Psi}(x)=x
, for
x\in\widetilde{\Omega}
, where
\pi_{1}
is the projection onto the first component, such that
\widetilde{\Psi}(Z)=\bm{Z}
almost surely;

(ii)

There exists a continuous shift map

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\nu}\times\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{D},\quad(h,\bm{x})\mapsto\widetilde{T}_{h}\bm{x},

such that

(4.1)

\displaystyle\widetilde{T}_{h}\widetilde{\Psi}(x)=\widetilde{\Psi}(x+h),\quad x\in\widetilde{\Omega},\quad h\in\mathcal{H}_{\nu}.

Remark 4.2
.

By standard results
[
FV10
, Chapter 15]
, we see that

4.1
is satisfied for
Z=B
a fractional Brownian motion and its lift
\bm{B}
in the step-
2
Carnot group
\mathbb{G}
, with path space
\mathcal{D}=C^{p-\mathrm{var}}_{0}([0,1],\mathbb{G})
, for
H\in(1/3,1/2]
,
p\in(1/H,3)
. Extensions to
H>1/4
are possible, at the price of lifting
Z
to a step-
3
Carnot group; we do not give details for the sake of brevity.

Definition 4.3
.

Define the cost
\widetilde{C}_{\mathcal{H}_{\nu}}\colon\mathcal{D}\times\mathcal{D}\to[0,\infty]
, similarly to (
2.18
), by

(4.2)

\widetilde{C}_{\mathcal{H}_{\nu}}(\bm{X},\bm{Y})\coloneqq\begin{cases}\|h\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\nu}},&\text{if}\;\bm{Y}=\widetilde{T}_{h}\bm{X},\;\text{for some}\;h\in\mathcal{H}_{\nu},\\
+\infty,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

where
\widetilde{T}_{h}
is the shift from
4.1
.

Due to the assumed continuity of the shift, we recover measurability of
\widetilde{C}_{\mathcal{H}_{\nu}}
(cf.
Lemma

2.20
).
By applying the contraction principle from
Lemma

2.2
, similarly to
[
Rie17
]
, but for a different cost, we lift the Talagrand inequality to the rough path space
\mathcal{D}
.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
4.1
hold. Then
\bm{\nu}\in\mathcal{T}_{2}(\mathcal{D},\widetilde{C}_{\mathcal{H}_{\nu}},1)
.

Proof.

The property
Eq.

4.1
implies that for
h\in\mathcal{H}_{\nu}
, a path
x\in\widetilde{\Omega}
satisfies
x=y+h
if and only if
\widetilde{\Psi}(x)=\widetilde{T}_{h}\widetilde{\Psi}(y)
. Hence for
x-y\in\mathcal{H}_{\nu}
, we have that
\widetilde{\Psi}(y+(x-y))=\widetilde{T}_{x-y}\widetilde{\Psi}(y)
and thus

\displaystyle\|x-y\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\nu}}^{2}=\widetilde{C}_{\mathcal{H}_{\nu}}^{2}(\widetilde{\Psi}(x),\widetilde{\Psi}(y)).

Similarly, if
x-y\notin\mathcal{H}_{\nu}
, then
\widetilde{\Psi}(x)
is not a shift of
\widetilde{\Psi}(y)
and so
\widetilde{C}_{\mathcal{H}_{\nu}}(\widetilde{\Psi}(x),\widetilde{\Psi}(y))=+\infty
. Together with
\nu\in\mathcal{T}_{2}(\Omega,\widetilde{c}_{\mathcal{H}_{\nu}},1)
,
\nu(\widetilde{\Omega})=1
, and measurability of
\widetilde{C}_{\mathcal{H}_{\nu}}
and
\widetilde{\Psi}
, an application of the contraction principle (
Lemma

2.2
) with
L=1
then yields
\bm{\nu}\in\mathcal{T}_{2}(\mathcal{D},\widetilde{C}_{\mathcal{H}_{\nu}},1)
.
∎

5.
Talagrand for the heat kernel measure on Carnot groups

In this section, we show that a
\mathcal{T}_{2}
inequality on the step-
2
Carnot group
\mathbb{G}
follows from a log-Sobolev inequality, which in turn can be deduced from a heat semigroup estimate. In particular, we prove a
\mathcal{T}_{2}
inequality for the heat kernel measure
\bm{\mu}_{1}
in the case that
\mathbb{G}
is an H-type group. We will apply this result in
Section

6
to show that a
\mathcal{T}_{2}
inequality also holds on the path space by a bottom-up approach.

5.1.
From log-Sobolev to Talagrand

We follow the approach put forward by Otto–Villani
[
OV00
]
, namely deducing a
\mathcal{T}_{2}
inequality as a consequence of a log-Sobolev inequality. We will make use of the generalisation by Gigli–Ledoux
[
GL13
]
of Otto–Villani’s result. Whereas the result of Gigli–Ledoux
[
GL13
]
depends on the log-Sobolev inequality for Lipschitz test functions, we show via a mollification argument that this can be relaxed to only requiring the log-Sobolev inequality for smooth test functions; see
Theorem

5.1
. Moreover, for the heat kernel measure, we show that the log-Sobolev inequality for smooth test functions follows from certain heat semigroup estimates; see
Theorem

5.2
.
In the special case of H-type groups, as defined in
Remark

2.10
, the required heat semigroup estimates are known. Thus, in
Theorem

5.3
, we show that a
\mathcal{T}_{2}
inequality holds for the heat kernel measure on H-type groups and, in particular, on the Heisenberg group.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a step-
2
Carnot group. Recall that, for the Carnot–Carathéodory metric
d_{\mathrm{CC}}
defined in (
2.9
), the space
(\mathbb{G},d_{\mathrm{CC}})
is a Polish space. Hence, for any Borel probability measure
\bm{\eta}
on
\mathbb{G}
, the space
(\mathbb{G},d_{\mathrm{CC}},\mathcal{\bm{\eta}})
is a metric measure space in the sense of
[
GL13
]
.

For a locally Lipschitz function
f\colon\mathbb{G}\to\mathbb{R}
, define the local Lipschitz constant
\mathrm{Lip}_{\mathbb{G}}(f)
by

\mathrm{Lip}_{\mathbb{G}}(f)(\bm{x})\coloneqq\limsup_{\bm{y}\to\bm{x}}\frac{|f(\bm{y})-f(\bm{x})|}{d_{\mathrm{CC}}(\bm{x},\bm{y})},\quad\bm{x}\in\mathbb{G}.

By an extension of Rademacher’s theorem due to Pansu
[
Pan89
]
(see also
[
DPMM+25
,
PS17
]
, and
[
LD25
, Theorem 11.3.2]
),
we obtain that every Lipschitz continuous function
f\colon U\subseteq\mathbb{G}\to\mathbb{R}
is Pansu differentiable Lebesgue-almost everywhere.
In particular, its gradient
\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}f
exists
\mathcal{L}^{m}
-almost everywhere and
\mathrm{Lip}_{\mathbb{G}}(f)(\bm{x})=|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}f(\bm{x})|_{\mathbb{G}}
, for
\mathcal{L}^{m}
-almost every
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{G}
.

We say that a Borel probability measure
\bm{\eta}
on
\mathbb{G}
with
\bm{\eta}\ll\mathcal{L}^{m}
satisfies the log-Sobolev inequality if there exists
\alpha\in(0,\infty)
such that

(LSI)

2\alpha\int_{\mathbb{G}}f\log f\,\mathrm{d}\bm{\eta}\leq\int_{\{f>0\}}\frac{|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}f|_{\mathbb{G}}^{2}}{f}\,\mathrm{d}\bm{\eta},\quad\text{for all}\;f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{G},[0,\infty)),\int_{\mathbb{G}}f\,\mathrm{d}\bm{\eta}=1.

We first show that (
LSI
) implies a
\mathcal{T}_{2}
inequality. As an intermediate step, we show that the log-Sobolev inequality also holds for Lipschitz functions, so that we can then apply
[
GL13
, Theorem 5.2]
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Suppose that
\bm{\eta}
is a Borel probability measure on
\mathbb{G}
with
\bm{\eta}\ll\mathcal{L}^{m}
satisfying
Eq.

LSI
for some
\alpha\in(0,\infty)
. Then, for any Lipschitz function
f\colon\mathbb{G}\to[0,\infty)
with
\int_{\mathbb{G}}f\,\mathrm{d}\bm{\eta}=1
,

(5.1)

2\alpha\int_{\mathbb{G}}f\log f\,\mathrm{d}\bm{\eta}\leq\int_{\{f>0\}}\frac{|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}f|_{\mathbb{G}}^{2}}{f}\,\mathrm{d}\bm{\eta}.

Moreover,
\bm{\eta}\in\mathcal{T}_{2}(\mathbb{G},d_{\mathrm{CC}},\alpha)
.

Proof.

We argue as in the proof of
[
AS20
, Theorem 4.8]
via mollification. Consider
f\colon\mathbb{G}\to[0,\infty)
Lipschitz continuous with
\int_{\mathbb{G}}f\,\mathrm{d}\bm{\eta}=1
. In particular, its gradient
\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}f
exists
\mathcal{L}^{m}
-almost everywhere.

Let
\rho\colon\mathbb{G}\to\mathbb{R}
be a symmetric smooth mollifier in
\mathbb{G}
, i.e. a function
\rho\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{m},[0,\infty))
such that

\mathrm{supp}\rho\subset B_{1}
,
0\leq\rho\leq 1
,
\rho(\bm{x}^{-1})=\rho(\bm{x})
, for all
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{G}
,
and
\int_{\mathbb{G}}\rho\,\mathrm{d}\bm{x}=1
.
For
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{G}
, set
\rho_{k}(\bm{x})=k^{Q}\rho(\delta_{k}\bm{x})
and define the mollification
\widehat{f}_{k}=\rho_{k}\star f
by

\widehat{f}_{k}(\bm{x})\coloneqq(\rho_{k}\star f)(\bm{x})=\int_{\mathbb{G}}\rho_{k}(\bm{x}\bm{y}^{-1})f(\bm{y})\,\mathrm{d}\bm{y}=\int_{\mathbb{G}}\rho_{k}(\bm{y})f(\bm{y}^{-1}\bm{x})\,\mathrm{d}\bm{y},\quad\bm{x}\in\mathbb{G}.

For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, also define a smooth truncation function
\chi_{k}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{m},[0,1])
such that
\chi_{k}=1
in
B_{k}
,
\chi_{k}=0
in
B_{2k}^{c}
, and
|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\chi_{k}|_{\mathbb{G}}\leq C/k
for some constant
C\in(0,\infty)
, and define
f_{k}\coloneqq\widehat{f}_{k}\,\chi_{k}
.

Thus,
f_{k}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{G},[0,\infty))
, for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Moreover,
f_{k}\to f
in
L^{1}(\bm{\eta})
and thus
\bm{\eta}
-almost everywhere along a subsequence. By Fatou’s lemma, we have that

\displaystyle\liminf_{k\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{G}}f_{k}\log f_{k}\,\mathrm{d}\bm{\eta}\geq\int_{\mathbb{G}}f\log f\,\mathrm{d}\bm{\eta}.

We next show an upper estimate for the
\limsup
of the right-hand side in
Eq.

5.1
with
\widehat{f}_{k}
in place of
f
. Note that, by left-invariance of the Carnot–Caratheodory distance, for
\bm{x}_{1},\bm{x}_{2}\in\mathbb{G}
, we have

\frac{|\widehat{f}_{k}(\bm{x}_{1})-\widehat{f}_{k}(\bm{x}_{2})|}{d_{\mathrm{CC}}(\bm{x}_{1},\bm{x}_{2})}\leq\int_{\mathbb{G}}\rho_{k}(\bm{y})\frac{|f(\bm{y}^{-1}\bm{x}_{1})-f(\bm{y}^{-1}\bm{x}_{1})|}{d_{\mathrm{CC}}(\bm{x}_{1},\bm{x}_{2})}\,\mathrm{d}\bm{y}\leq\int_{\mathbb{G}}\rho_{k}(\bm{y})\frac{|f(\bm{y}^{-1}\bm{x}_{1})-f(\bm{y}^{-1}\bm{x}_{1})|}{d_{\mathrm{CC}}(\bm{y}^{-1}\bm{x}_{1},\bm{y}^{-1}\bm{x}_{2})}\,\mathrm{d}\bm{y}.

Thus, after passing to the
\limsup
for
\bm{x}_{1}\to\bm{x}_{2}=\bm{x}
,
we obtain
\mathrm{Lip}_{\mathbb{G}}\widehat{f}_{k}(\bm{x})\leq\rho_{k}\star\mathrm{Lip}_{\mathbb{G}}f(\bm{x})
,
or equivalently
|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\widehat{f}_{k}|_{\mathbb{G}}(\bm{x})\leq\rho_{k}\star|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}f|_{\mathbb{G}}(\bm{x})
.
With this estimate, the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality gives

|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\widehat{f}_{k}|_{\mathbb{G}}^{2}\leq\Big[\rho_{k}\star\Big(\chi_{\{f>0\}}\sqrt{f}\frac{|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}f|_{\mathbb{G}}}{\sqrt{f}}\Big)\Big]^{2}\leq f_{k}\Big(\rho_{k}\star\frac{|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}f|_{\mathbb{G}}^{2}}{f}\chi_{\{f>0\}}\Big).

Multiplying
f_{k}
by the truncation
\chi_{k}
and applying the product rule, we estimate

\displaystyle\limsup_{k\to\infty}\int_{\{f_{k}>0\}}\frac{|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}f_{k}|_{\mathbb{G}}^{2}}{f_{k}}\,\mathrm{d}\bm{\eta}

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{k\to\infty}\int_{\{\widehat{f}_{k}>0\}}\frac{|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\widehat{f}_{k}|_{\mathbb{G}}^{2}}{\widehat{f}_{k}}\,\mathrm{d}\bm{\eta}

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{k\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{G}}\rho_{k}\star\bigg(\frac{|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}f|_{\mathbb{G}}^{2}}{f}\chi_{\{f>0\}}\bigg)\,\mathrm{d}\bm{\eta}\leq\int_{\{f>0\}}\frac{|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}f|_{\mathbb{G}}^{2}}{f}\,\mathrm{d}\bm{\eta}.

Finally, define
I_{k}\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{G}}f_{k}\,\mathrm{d}\bm{\eta}
and the normalised function
\widetilde{f}_{k}\coloneqq f_{k}/I_{k}
, for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Then, by (
LSI
),

\displaystyle 2\alpha\int_{\mathbb{G}}f_{k}\log f_{k}\,\mathrm{d}\bm{\eta}

\displaystyle=2\alpha I_{k}\int_{\mathbb{G}}\widetilde{f}_{k}\log\widetilde{f}_{k}\,\mathrm{d}\bm{\eta}+2\alpha I_{k}\log I_{k}

\displaystyle\leq I_{k}\int_{\{\widetilde{f}_{k}>0\}}\frac{|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\widetilde{f}_{k}|_{\mathbb{G}}^{2}}{\widetilde{f}_{k}}\,\mathrm{d}\bm{\eta}+2\alpha I_{k}\log I_{k}=\int_{\{f_{k}>0\}}\frac{|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}f_{k}|_{\mathbb{G}}^{2}}{f_{k}}\,\mathrm{d}\bm{\eta}+2\alpha I_{k}\log I_{k}.

We conclude that

2\alpha\int_{\mathbb{G}}f\log f\,\mathrm{d}\bm{\eta}\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}2\alpha\int_{\mathbb{G}}f_{k}\log f_{k}\,\mathrm{d}\bm{\eta}\leq\limsup_{k\to\infty}\int_{\{f_{k}>0\}}\frac{|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}f_{k}|_{\mathbb{G}}^{2}}{f_{k}}\,\mathrm{d}\bm{\eta}\leq\int_{\{f>0\}}\frac{|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}f|_{\mathbb{G}}^{2}}{f}\,\mathrm{d}\bm{\eta}.

Thus, the log-Sobolev inequality for Lipschitz-continuous functions is established.

Finally, since
(\mathbb{G},d_{\mathrm{CC}},\bm{\eta})
is a metric measure space in the sense of
[
GL13
]
, we conclude that
\bm{\eta}\in\mathcal{T}_{2}(\mathbb{G},d_{\mathrm{CC}},\alpha)
by
[
GL13
, Theorem 5.2]
.
∎

5.2.
From heat semigroup estimates to Talagrand

We now specialise to the case of the heat kernel measure
\bm{\mu}_{1}
and give a sufficient condition for
\bm{\mu}_{1}\in\mathcal{T}_{2}(\mathbb{G},d_{\mathrm{CC}},\alpha)
, for some
\alpha\in(0,\infty)
.

As noted in
[
Eld10
, Section 5]
and
[
BBBC08
, Remark 6.6]
, one can deduce the log-Sobolev inequality (
LSI
) for
\bm{\mu}_{1}
from the following heat semigroup estimate: there exists a constant
K\in(0,\infty)
such that

(5.2)

\displaystyle|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}P_{t}f|_{\mathbb{G}}\leq KP_{t}(|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}f|_{\mathbb{G}}),\quad\text{for all}\;f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{G},\mathbb{R}),\ t\geq 0,

where
P_{t}
is the heat semigroup defined in
Equation

2.14
.
Indeed, for the Heisenberg group
\mathbb{G}=\mathbb{H}=\mathbb{H}^{1}
,
[
Li06
, Théorème 1.1]
proves the estimate (
5.2
), and
[
Li06
, Corollaire 1.2]
states that (
LSI
) holds as a direct consequence, following the arguments in
[
ABC+00
, Théorème 5.4.7]
. More generally, for
\mathbb{G}=\mathbb{H}^{n}
,
[
HZ10
, Theorem 7.3]
and
[
BBBC08
, Theorem 6.1]
prove that (
LSI
) holds, again relying on the heat semigroup estimate (
5.2
).
For completeness, we provide a proof in
Theorem

5.2
that, for any step-
2
Carnot group, the heat semigroup estimate (
5.2
) implies the log-Sobolev inequality (
LSI
). Thanks to
Theorem

5.1
, the
\mathcal{T}_{2}
inequality also follows.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a step-
2
Carnot group and suppose that there exists
K\in(0,\infty)
such that the heat semigroup
P
on
\mathbb{G}
satisfies the estimate (
5.2
) for all
t\in[0,1]
. Let
\alpha=\tfrac{1}{2K^{2}}
. Then the heat kernel measure
\bm{\mu}_{1}
on
\mathbb{G}
satisfies the log-Sobolev inequality (
LSI
) with constant
\alpha
, and
\bm{\mu}_{1}
satisfies the
\mathcal{T}_{2}
inequality
\bm{\mu}_{1}\in\mathcal{T}_{2}(\mathbb{G},d_{\mathrm{CC}},\alpha)
.

Proof.

Suppose that
P_{t}
satisfies (
5.2
) for all
t\in[0,1]
.
Let
\phi\in C^{2}(I,\mathbb{R})
for some interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}
, and suppose moreover that
\phi^{\prime\prime}>0
and the function
-1/\phi^{\prime\prime}
is convex. Let
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{G},\mathbb{R})
and let
t\in[0,1]
. Then, by the heat equation and chain rule for the sub-Laplacian, for any
s\in[0,t]
,

\displaystyle\partial_{s}P_{s}\phi(P_{t-s}f)

\displaystyle=P_{s}\bigl(\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}\phi(P_{t-s}f)-\phi^{\prime}(P_{t-s}f)\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}P_{t-s}f\bigr)

\displaystyle=P_{s}\bigl(\phi^{\prime\prime}(P_{t-s}f)|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}P_{t-s}f|_{\mathbb{G}}^{2}\bigr).

The heat semigroup estimate (
5.2
) and the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality imply that

\displaystyle|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}P_{t-s}f|_{\mathbb{G}}^{2}\leq K^{2}\big(P_{t-s}(|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}f|_{\mathbb{G}})\big)^{2}

\displaystyle=K^{2}\Big(P_{t-s}\bigl(|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}f|_{\mathbb{G}}\sqrt{\phi^{\prime\prime}(f)}\cdot 1/\sqrt{\phi^{\prime\prime}(f)}\bigr)\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq K^{2}P_{t-s}(|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}f|_{\mathbb{G}}^{2}\phi^{\prime\prime}(f))P_{t-s}(1/\phi^{\prime\prime}(f)),

and, by Jensen’s inequality,

\displaystyle\phi^{\prime\prime}(P_{t-s}f)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{-1}{P_{t-s}(-1/\phi^{\prime\prime}(f))}=\frac{1}{P_{t-s}(1/\phi^{\prime\prime}(f))}.

Hence

\displaystyle\partial_{s}P_{s}\phi(P_{t-s}f)

\displaystyle=P_{s}\bigl(\phi^{\prime\prime}(P_{t-s}f)|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}P_{t-s}f|_{\mathbb{G}}^{2}\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq K^{2}P_{s}P_{t-s}(|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}f|_{\mathbb{G}}^{2}\phi^{\prime\prime}(f))=K^{2}P_{t}(|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}f|_{\mathbb{G}}^{2}\phi^{\prime\prime}(f)),

and integrating gives

\displaystyle P_{t}\phi(f)-\phi(P_{t}f)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{s}P_{s}\phi(P_{t-s}f)\,\mathrm{d}s\leq K^{2}tP_{t}(|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}f|_{\mathbb{G}}^{2}\phi^{\prime\prime}(f)).

Now take
\phi\colon(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
to be
\phi(x)=x\log x
for all
x\in(0,\infty)
, and suppose that
f\colon\mathbb{G}\to(0,\infty)
. Then we have the following form of the log-Sobolev inequality:

\displaystyle P_{t}(f\log f)-P_{t}f\log(P_{t}f)\leq K^{2}tP_{t}\frac{|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}f|_{\mathbb{G}}^{2}}{f}.

To arrive at (
LSI
), we set
t=1
, evaluate both sides of the inequality at the identity, and additionally suppose that
\int_{\mathbb{G}}f\,\mathrm{d}\bm{\mu}_{1}=1
. Then

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{G}}f\log f\,\mathrm{d}\bm{\mu}_{1}\leq K^{2}\int_{\mathbb{G}}\frac{|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}f|_{\mathbb{G}}^{2}}{f}\,\mathrm{d}\bm{\mu}_{1}.

Note that, allowing
f\colon\mathbb{G}\to[0,\infty)
, we have
\int_{\mathbb{G}}f\log f\,\mathrm{d}\bm{\mu}_{1}=\int_{\{f>0\}}f\log f\,\mathrm{d}\bm{\mu}_{1}
. Thus (
LSI
) holds with constant
\alpha=\tfrac{1}{2K^{2}}
.

Applying
Theorem

5.1
, we further have that
\bm{\mu}_{1}\in\mathcal{T}_{2}(\mathbb{G},d_{\mathrm{CC}},\alpha)
.
∎

6.
Talagrand for Brownian motion on Carnot groups – Bottom-up approach

In Section
5
,
we discussed the availability of Talagrand transportation inequalities
on Carnot groups, as a consequence of log-Sobolev inequalities and heat kernel estimates.
In this section, we demonstrate
that we can transfer the
\mathcal{T}_{2}
inequality for the heat kernel measure on a Carnot group, via a rescaling and tensorisation argument, to a
\mathcal{T}_{2}
inequality on the associated path space; see
Section

6.1
.
We highlight that this approach yields interesting insights into
optimal transport problems in the non-commutative sub-Riemannian setting that distinguishes it from the Euclidean case; see
Sections

6.2
and
6.3
.
Finally, we show that the cost function defined in (
2.18
) on the path space arises naturally
as the
\Gamma
-limit of discretised cost functions
based on the Carnot–Caratheodory distance on the Carnot group; see
Section

6.4
.

Throughout this section, let
\mathbb{G}\cong\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\oplus\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}
be a step-
2
Carnot group and set
d=d_{1}
; see
Section

2.2
.
Recall that
\bm{B}
denotes Brownian motion on
\mathbb{G}
, with law
\bm{\mu}=\mathrm{Law}(\bm{B})
and time marginals
\bm{\mu}_{t}=\mathrm{Law}(\bm{B}_{t})
for
t\in[0,1]
.

6.1.
From Talagrand on Carnot groups to Talagrand on path space

The main result of this section is that the
\mathcal{T}_{2}
inequality for
\bm{\mu}_{1}
on the group
\mathbb{G}
implies the
\mathcal{T}_{2}
inequality for
\bm{\mu}
on the space
\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}
of continuous
\mathbb{G}
-valued paths started from the origin.

We first show that the heat kernel measure satisfies the following scaling property.

Lemma 6.1
.

Suppose that
\bm{\mu}_{1}\in\mathcal{T}_{2}(\mathbb{G},d_{\mathrm{CC}},\alpha)
, for some
\alpha\in(0,\infty)
. Then, for any
t\in(0,1]
,
\bm{\mu}_{t}\in\mathcal{T}_{2}(\mathbb{G},d_{\mathrm{CC}},\alpha t^{-1})
.

Proof.

We claim that the heat kernel measure on
\mathbb{G}
satisfies the scaling

(6.1)

\bm{\mu}_{t}=(\delta_{s^{-1}})_{\sharp}\bm{\mu}_{s^{2}t},

for any
s>0
,
t\in[0,1]
. To see this, recall that
\mathfrak{p}\colon(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{G}\to(0,\infty)
denotes the heat kernel on
\mathbb{G}
,
Q
the homogeneous dimension of
\mathbb{G}
, and
\mathcal{L}^{m}
the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{G}
. Then, as in
Eq.

2.8
,
(\delta_{s})_{\sharp}\mathcal{L}^{m}(\mathrm{d}\bm{x})=s^{-Q}\mathcal{L}^{m}(\mathrm{d}\bm{x})
, for any
s>0
. Moreover, by
[
AS20
, Theorem 2.3]
, for any
s>0
,
t\in[0,1]
, and
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{G}
, we have that
\mathfrak{p}_{s^{2}t}(\delta_{s}\bm{x})=s^{-Q}\mathfrak{p}_{t}(\bm{x})
. Therefore, for any Borel set
A\subseteq\mathbb{G}
,

\displaystyle\mu_{t}(A)=\int_{A}\mathfrak{p}_{t}(\bm{x})\mathcal{L}^{m}(\mathrm{d}\bm{x})

\displaystyle=s^{Q}\int_{A}(\mathfrak{p}_{s^{2}t}\circ\delta_{s})(\bm{x})\mathcal{L}^{m}(\mathrm{d}\bm{x})

\displaystyle=s^{Q}\int_{\delta_{s^{-1}}(A)}\mathfrak{p}_{s^{2}t}(\bm{x})(\delta_{s})_{\sharp}\mathcal{L}^{m}(\mathrm{d}\bm{x})=\int_{\delta_{s^{-1}}(A)}\mathfrak{p}_{s^{2}t}(\bm{x})\mathcal{L}^{m}(\mathrm{d}\bm{x})=(\delta_{s^{-1}})_{\sharp}\mu_{s^{2}t}(A).

This proves the claim. Now fix
t\in(0,1]
.
Setting
s=t^{-\frac{1}{2}}
in
Eq.

6.1
, we have
\bm{\mu}_{t}=(\delta_{\sqrt{t}})_{\sharp}\bm{\mu}_{1}
.
The map
\delta_{\sqrt{t}}\colon\mathbb{G}\to\mathbb{G}
is
L
-Lipschitz with Lipschitz constant
L=\sqrt{t}
.
Thus, since
\bm{\mu}_{1}\in\mathcal{T}_{2}(\mathbb{G},d_{\mathrm{CC}},\alpha)
,
Lemma

2.2
implies that
\bm{\mu}_{t}\in\mathcal{T}_{2}(\mathbb{G},d_{\mathrm{CC}},\alpha t^{-1})
.
∎

We now consider the product space
\mathbb{G}^{2^{n}}=\mathbb{G}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{G}
, for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and apply the dimension-free tensorisation property of the
\mathcal{T}_{2}
inequality. Define
d_{\mathrm{CC},n}\colon\mathbb{G}^{2^{n}}\times\mathbb{G}^{2^{n}}\to[0,\infty)
by

d_{\mathrm{CC},n}^{2}(\bm{x},\overline{\bm{x}})\coloneqq 2^{n}\sum_{i=1}^{2^{n}}d_{\mathrm{CC}}^{2}(\bm{x}_{i},\overline{\bm{x}}_{i}),

for
\bm{x}=(\bm{x}_{1},\dotsc,\bm{x}_{n})
,
\overline{\bm{x}}=(\overline{\bm{x}}_{1},\dotsc,\overline{\bm{x}}_{n})\in\mathbb{G}^{2^{n}}
.
We have the following tensorisation result.

Proposition 6.2
.

Suppose that
\bm{\mu}_{1}\in\mathcal{T}_{2}(\mathbb{G},d_{\mathrm{CC}},\alpha)
, for some
\alpha\in(0,\infty)
. Then, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\bm{\mu}_{2^{-n}}^{\otimes 2^{n}}\in\mathcal{T}_{2}(\mathbb{G}^{2^{n}},d_{\mathrm{CC},n},\alpha)
.

Proof.

Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
. First note that
\bm{\mu}_{2^{-n}}\in\mathcal{T}_{2}(\mathbb{G},d_{\mathrm{CC}},\alpha 2^{n})
, by
Lemma

6.1
. Define
\widetilde{d}_{\mathrm{CC},n}\colon\mathbb{G}^{2^{n}}\to[0,\infty)
by

\widetilde{d}_{\mathrm{CC},n}^{2}(\bm{x},\overline{\bm{x}})=\sum_{i=1}^{2^{n}}d_{\mathrm{CC}}^{2}(\bm{x}_{i},\overline{\bm{x}}_{i}),

for
\bm{x}=(\bm{x}_{1},\dotsc,\bm{x}_{n})
,
\overline{\bm{x}}=(\overline{\bm{x}}_{1},\dotsc,\overline{\bm{x}}_{n})\in\mathbb{G}^{2^{n}}
. Since
(\mathbb{G},d_{\mathrm{CC}})
is a Polish space,
[
GL07
, Theorem 6]
implies that
\mathcal{T}_{2}(\mathbb{G},d_{\mathrm{CC}},\alpha 2^{n})
has the dimension-free tensorisation property; i.e.
\bm{\mu}_{2^{-n}}^{\otimes 2^{n}}\in\mathcal{T}_{2}(\mathbb{G}^{2^{n}},\widetilde{d}_{\mathrm{CC},n},\alpha 2^{n})
. Applying
Lemma

2.2
with
\psi
equal to the identity, we conclude that
\bm{\mu}_{2^{-n}}^{\otimes 2^{n}}\in\mathcal{T}_{2}(\mathbb{G}^{2^{n}},d_{\mathrm{CC},n},\alpha)
.
∎

We next prove a relative entropy bound for measures on the path space.
For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, set
t_{k}^{n}=k2^{-n}
for
k\in\{0,\dotsc,2^{n}\}
, and define
\Gamma^{n}\colon\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}\to\mathbb{G}^{2^{n}}
to be the projection of paths to their dyadic increments; i.e.
\Gamma^{n}\bm{\omega}=(\bm{\omega}_{0,t_{1}^{n}},\bm{\omega}_{t_{1}^{n},t_{2}^{n}},\dotsc,\bm{\omega}_{t_{2^{n}-1}^{n},1})
, for
\bm{\omega}\in\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}
. Then define a cost function
C_{n}\colon\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}\times\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}\to[0,\infty)
by

(6.2)

C_{n}(\bm{\omega},\overline{\bm{\omega}})\coloneqq d_{\mathrm{CC},n}(\Gamma^{n}\bm{\omega},\Gamma^{n}\overline{\bm{\omega}}),\quad\bm{\omega},\overline{\bm{\omega}}\in\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}.

Lemma 6.3
.

Suppose that
\bm{\mu}_{1}\in\mathcal{T}_{2}(\mathbb{G},d_{\mathrm{CC}},\alpha)
, for some
\alpha\in(0,\infty)
, and let
\bm{\nu}
be a Borel probability measure on
\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}
. Define
\bm{\mu}^{n}=\Gamma^{n}_{\sharp}\bm{\mu}
,
\bm{\nu}^{n}=\Gamma^{n}_{\sharp}\bm{\nu}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{G}^{2^{n}})
.
Then
H(\bm{\nu}^{n}\|\bm{\mu}^{n})\nearrow H(\bm{\nu}\|\bm{\mu})
as
n\to\infty
and, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\mathrm{T}_{C_{n},2}(\bm{\mu},\bm{\nu})\leq\sqrt{\frac{2}{\alpha}H(\bm{\nu}\|\bm{\mu})};

i.e.
\bm{\mu}\in\mathcal{T}_{2}(\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}},C_{n},\alpha)
.

Proof.

By independence and stationarity of the increments of
\bm{B}
, we have that
\bm{\mu}^{n}=\Gamma^{n}_{\sharp}\bm{\mu}=\bm{\mu}_{2^{-n}}^{\otimes 2^{n}}
and thus, by
Proposition

6.2
,
\bm{\mu}^{n}\in\mathcal{T}_{2}(\mathbb{G}^{2^{n}},d_{\mathrm{CC},n},\alpha)
.

As shown in
[
DGW04
, Lemma 2.1]
,

(6.3)

H(\bm{\nu}^{n}\|\bm{\mu}^{n})=\inf\Big\{\,H(\bm{\eta}\|\bm{\mu}):\bm{\eta}\in\mathcal{P}(\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}),\,\Gamma^{n}_{\sharp}\bm{\eta}=\bm{\nu}^{n}\,\Big\},

and we see that the right-hand side is increasing in
n
and bounded above by
H(\bm{\nu}\|\bm{\mu})
. To see that the limit is equal to
H(\bm{\nu}\|\bm{\mu})
, we introduce the piecewise linear interpolation map
R^{n}\colon\mathbb{G}^{2^{n}}\to\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}
, which is defined such that
\Gamma^{n}\circ R^{n}=\mathrm{id}
, and the image
\mathrm{Im}(R^{n})\subset\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}
is the set of paths that are linear except at the dyadics
t^{n}_{k}
,
k\in\{0,\dotsc,2^{n}\}
. Define
\widetilde{\bm{\mu}}^{n}=R^{n}_{\sharp}\bm{\mu}^{n}
,
\widetilde{\bm{\nu}}^{n}=R^{n}_{\sharp}\bm{\nu}^{n}\in\mathcal{P}(\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}})
. Since
R^{n}\colon\mathbb{G}^{2^{n}}\to\mathrm{Im}(R^{n})
is a bijection, applying the representation given in (
6.3
) for both
R^{n}
and its inverse gives the equality
H(\bm{\nu}^{n}\|\bm{\mu}^{n})=H(\widetilde{\bm{\nu}}^{n}\|\widetilde{\bm{\mu}}^{n})
. We conclude similarly to
[
AGS08
, Corollary 9.4.6]
, as follows. For any
\bm{\omega}\in\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}
, we have that
R^{n}\circ\Gamma^{n}(\bm{\omega})\to\bm{\omega}
as
n\to\infty
and so, by dominated convergence,
\widetilde{\bm{\mu}}^{n}\rightharpoonup\bm{\mu}
and
\widetilde{\bm{\nu}}^{n}\rightharpoonup\bm{\nu}
. Then, using the joint lower semicontinuity of the relative entropy (see, e.g.
[
AGS08
, Lemma 9.4.3]
) together with the upper bound implied by (
6.3
), we conclude that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}H(\bm{\nu}^{n}\|\bm{\mu}^{n})=\lim_{n\to\infty}H(\widetilde{\bm{\nu}}^{n}\|\widetilde{\bm{\mu}}^{n})=H(\bm{\nu}\|\bm{\mu}).

Finally, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\bm{\mu}^{n}\in\mathcal{T}_{2}(\mathbb{G}^{2^{n}},d_{\mathrm{CC},n},\alpha)
implies that

\mathrm{T}_{C_{n},2}^{2}(\bm{\mu},\bm{\nu})=\mathrm{T}_{d_{\mathrm{CC},n},2}^{2}(\bm{\mu}^{n},\bm{\nu}^{n})\leq\frac{2}{\alpha}H(\bm{\nu}^{n}\|\bm{\mu}^{n})\leq\frac{2}{\alpha}H(\bm{\nu}\|\bm{\mu}).\qed

Before turning to the main result of this section, we prove an auxiliary lemma on the Euclidean cost on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and the associated Cameron–Martin cost
c_{\mathcal{H}}
defined in
Eq.

2.1
. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, define
c_{n}\colon\Omega\times\Omega\to[0,\infty)
by

(6.4)

c_{n}^{2}(\omega,\overline{\omega})=2^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{2^{n}}|\overline{\omega}_{t_{k-1}^{n},t_{k}^{n}}-\omega_{t_{k-1}^{n},t_{k}^{n}}|^{2},

for
\omega,\overline{\omega}\in\Omega
. Part (ii) of the following lemma is a standard stability result from optimal transport and is a consequence, for example, of
[
Rie17
, Lemma 1.1]
. Part (iii) will also be used in
Proposition

6.21
.

Lemma 6.4
.

(i)

For each
(\omega,\overline{\omega})\in\Omega\times\Omega
,
(c_{n}(\omega,\overline{\omega}))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is an increasing sequence, and

(6.5)

\lim_{n\to\infty}c_{n}(\omega,\overline{\omega})=c_{\mathcal{H}}(\omega,\overline{\omega}).

(ii)

For any
\nu\in\mathcal{P}(\Omega)
, the following convergence holds along a subsequence:

(6.6)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathrm{T}_{c_{n},2}(\mu,\nu)=\mathrm{T}_{c_{\mathcal{H}},2}(\mu,\nu).

(iii)

For any
(\bm{\omega},\overline{\bm{\omega}})\in\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}\times\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

(6.7)

C_{n}(\bm{\omega},\overline{\bm{\omega}})\geq c_{n}(\pi_{1}\bm{\omega},\pi_{1}\overline{\bm{\omega}}).

Remark 6.5
.

We will show in Theorem
6.6
,
that the convergence in (
6.6
) and the lower bound in (
6.7
) imply the lower estimate

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathrm{T}_{C_{n},2}(\bm{\mu},\bm{\nu})\geq\mathrm{T}_{C_{\mathcal{H}},2}(\bm{\mu},\bm{\nu})
. An even stronger result will be derived in Section
6.4
, namely, we prove the
\Gamma
-convergence of the cost functions
C_{n}
to the cost
C_{\mathcal{H}}
. This establishes that
C_{\mathcal{H}}

is indeed the natural limiting cost. Moreover, we prove
that the
\Gamma
-convergence of
C_{n}
implies the convergence of the associated transport problems
\mathrm{T}_{C_{n},2}(\bm{\mu},\widetilde{\bm{\nu}}^{n})
along a suitable sequence of probability measures
\widetilde{\bm{\nu}}^{n}\in\mathcal{P}(\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}})
.

Proof.

Let
\omega,\overline{\omega}\in\Omega
and write
h=\overline{\omega}-\omega
. The sequence
(c_{n}(\omega,\overline{\omega}))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is increasing by definition. Suppose that
h\in\mathcal{H}
. Then

\lim_{n\to\infty}c_{n}^{2}(\omega,\overline{\omega})=\lim_{n\to\infty}2^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{2^{n}}|h_{t_{k-1}^{n},t_{k}^{n}}|^{2}=\int_{0}^{1}|\dot{h}_{t}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t=\|h\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}.

For
h\notin\mathcal{H}
, the above limit is
+\infty
. This proves part (i).

For part (ii), note that
\mathrm{T}_{c_{\mathcal{H}},2}(\mu,\nu)
and
\mathrm{T}_{c_{n},2}(\mu,\nu)
admit minimisers, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, by e.g.
[
Vil09
, Theorem 4.1]
, since the cost functions are lower semicontinuous and non-negative. Let
\lambda^{\ast}\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)
attain the infimum in
\mathrm{T}_{c_{\mathcal{H}},2}(\mu,\nu)
. Then, by the monotone convergence theorem,

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathrm{T}_{c_{n},2}^{2}(\mu,\nu)\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}c_{n}^{2}(\omega,\overline{\omega})\,\mathrm{d}\lambda^{\ast}(\omega,\overline{\omega})=\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}c_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}(\omega,\overline{\omega})\,\mathrm{d}\lambda^{\ast}(\omega,\overline{\omega})=\mathrm{T}_{c_{\mathcal{H}},2}^{2}(\mu,\nu).

On the other hand, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\lambda^{n}\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)
attain the infimum in
\mathrm{T}_{c_{n},2}(\mu,\nu)
. Since
\Pi(\mu,\nu)
is tight, Prohorov’s theorem implies that
(\lambda^{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges weakly along a subsequence
(n_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
to some
\widetilde{\lambda}\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)
. By monotonicity, for any
m\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\liminf_{k\to\infty}\mathrm{T}_{c_{n_{k}},2}^{2}(\mu,\nu)=\liminf_{k\to\infty}\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}c_{n_{k}}^{2}(\omega,\overline{\omega})\,\mathrm{d}\lambda^{n_{k}}(\omega,\overline{\omega})

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}c_{m}^{2}(\omega,\overline{\omega})\,\mathrm{d}\lambda^{n_{k}}(\omega,\overline{\omega})

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}c_{m}^{2}(\omega,\overline{\omega})\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{\lambda}(\omega,\overline{\omega}).

Applying monotone convergence once more,

\displaystyle\liminf_{k\to\infty}\mathrm{T}_{c_{n_{k}},2}^{2}(\mu,\nu)\geq\lim_{m\to\infty}\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}c_{m}^{2}(\omega,\overline{\omega})\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{\lambda}(\omega,\overline{\omega})

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}c_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}(\omega,\overline{\omega})\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{\lambda}(\omega,\overline{\omega})

\displaystyle\geq\mathrm{T}_{c_{\mathcal{H}},2}^{2}(\mu,\nu).

Now observe that, for
\bm{x},\overline{\bm{x}}\in\mathbb{G}
,
d_{\mathrm{CC}}(\bm{x},\overline{\bm{x}})\geq|\pi_{1}\bm{x}-\pi_{1}\overline{\bm{x}}|
. Indeed, by definition of the Carnot–Carathéodory metric,

\displaystyle d_{\mathrm{CC}}(\bm{x},\overline{\bm{x}})

\displaystyle=\min\Big\{\,\int_{0}^{1}|\dot{\gamma}_{t}|\,\mathrm{d}t:\gamma\colon[0,1]\to\mathbb{G}\;\text{horizontal,}\;\gamma_{0}=\bm{x},\gamma_{1}=\overline{\bm{x}}\,\Big\}

\displaystyle\geq\inf\Big\{\,\int_{0}^{1}|\dot{\gamma}_{t}|\,\mathrm{d}t:\gamma\colon[0,1]\to\mathbb{G}\;\text{horizontal,}\;\pi_{1}\gamma_{0}=\pi_{1}\bm{x},\pi_{1}\gamma_{1}=\pi_{1}\overline{\bm{x}}\,\Big\}

\displaystyle=\min\Big\{\,\int_{0}^{1}|\dot{g}_{t}|\,\mathrm{d}t:g\colon[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}\;\text{absolutely continuous,}\;\;g_{0}=\pi_{1}\bm{x},g_{1}=\pi_{1}\overline{\bm{x}}\,\Big\}

\displaystyle=|\pi_{1}\bm{x}-\pi_{1}\overline{\bm{x}}|.

Hence, for
\bm{\omega},\overline{\bm{\omega}}\in\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}
,

\displaystyle C_{n}^{2}(\bm{\omega},\overline{\bm{\omega}})

\displaystyle=2^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{2^{n}}d_{\mathrm{CC}}^{2}(\bm{\omega}_{t_{k-1}^{n},t_{k}^{n}},\overline{\bm{\omega}}_{t_{k-1}^{n},t_{k}^{n}})\geq 2^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{2^{n}}|\pi_{1}\overline{\bm{\omega}}_{t_{k-1}^{n},t_{k}^{n}}-\pi_{1}\bm{\omega}_{t_{k-1}^{n},t_{k}^{n}}|^{2}=c_{n}^{2}(\pi_{1}\bm{\omega},\pi_{1}\overline{\bm{\omega}}).

This concludes part (iii).
∎

We are now in position to prove the main result of this section.

Theorem 6.6
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a step-
2
Carnot group and suppose that
\bm{\mu}_{1}\in\mathcal{T}_{2}(\mathbb{G},d_{\mathrm{CC}},\alpha)
, for some
\alpha\in(0,\infty)
. Then
\bm{\mu}\in\mathcal{T}_{2}(\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}},C_{\mathcal{H}},\alpha)
.

Proof.

If
\bm{\nu}
is not absolutely continuous with respect to
\bm{\mu}
, then
H(\bm{\nu}\|\bm{\mu})=+\infty
and the cost-information inequality holds trivially.

Now suppose that
\bm{\nu}\ll\bm{\mu}
. Recall the lift map
\Psi\colon C([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{d})\to C([0,1],\mathbb{G})
given by
Eq.

2.15
. By
Proposition

2.13
,
\bm{\mu}=\Psi_{\sharp}\mu
, and by
Lemma

2.14
, there exists
\nu\ll\mu
such that
\bm{\nu}=\Psi_{\sharp}\nu
. Now let
\lambda^{\ast}\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)
be such that

\mathrm{T}_{c_{\mathcal{H}},2}^{2}(\mu,\nu)=\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}c_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}(\omega,\overline{\omega})\,\mathrm{d}\lambda^{\ast}(\omega,\overline{\omega})=\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}\|\overline{\omega}-\omega\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda^{\ast}(\omega,\overline{\omega}).

We have that
\lambda^{\ast}(\{\,(\omega,\overline{\omega})\in\Omega\times\Omega:\overline{\omega}-\omega\in\mathcal{H}\,\})=1
and that

\widetilde{\bm{\lambda}}=(\Psi\times\Psi)_{\sharp}\lambda^{\ast}\in\Pi(\bm{\mu},\bm{\nu})
is an admissible coupling.
Using the property
Eq.

2.17
of the lift and shift from
Proposition

2.16
, we find that

\displaystyle\int_{\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}\times\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}}C_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}(\bm{\omega},\overline{\bm{\omega}})\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{\bm{\lambda}}(\bm{\omega},\overline{\bm{\omega}})

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}C_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}(\Psi(\omega),\Psi(\overline{\omega}))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda^{\ast}(\omega,\overline{\omega})

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}C_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}(\Psi(\omega),T_{\overline{\omega}-\omega}\Psi(\omega))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda^{\ast}(\omega,\overline{\omega})

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}\|\overline{\omega}-\omega\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda^{\ast}(\omega,\overline{\omega})=\mathrm{T}_{c_{\mathcal{H}},2}^{2}(\mu,\nu).

Hence
\mathrm{T}_{C_{\mathcal{H}},2}^{2}(\bm{\mu},\bm{\nu})\leq\mathrm{T}_{c_{\mathcal{H}},2}^{2}(\mu,\nu)
.

Combining
Eq.

6.6
and
Eq.

6.7
from
Lemma

6.4
, we have

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathrm{T}_{C_{n},2}^{2}(\bm{\mu},\bm{\nu})

\displaystyle\geq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathrm{T}_{c_{n},2}^{2}(\mu,\nu)\geq\mathrm{T}_{c_{\mathcal{H}},2}^{2}(\mu,\nu)\geq\mathrm{T}_{C_{\mathcal{H}},2}^{2}(\bm{\mu},\bm{\nu}).

By
Lemma

6.3
, we conclude that
\mathrm{T}_{C_{\mathcal{H}},2}^{2}(\bm{\mu},\bm{\nu})\leq\frac{2}{\alpha}H(\bm{\nu}\|\bm{\mu})
.
∎

for

as in

.

6.2.
Failure of top-down projection and blow-up of cost functions

In this section, we point out two major differences between the classical Euclidean and the Carnot group settings. In contrast to the Euclidean case, we cannot project the Talagrand inequality from
Theorem

6.6
down to a Talagrand inequality for
\bm{B}_{1}
. Moreover, the cost functions
C_{n}
do not converge pointwise to the cost
C_{\mathcal{H}}
.

We start by giving the corresponding projection result in the Euclidean setting, which we prove via the contraction principle from
Lemma

2.2
.
Let
\widetilde{P}_{1}\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}},\,\omega\mapsto\omega_{1}
denote the map that evaluates a path at time
t=1
, and recall the Euclidean Cameron–Martin cost
c_{\mathcal{H}}
defined in (
2.1
).

Proposition 6.8
.

Let
\eta
be a Borel probability measure on
\Omega
and suppose that
\eta\in\mathcal{T}_{2}(\Omega,c_{\mathcal{H}},\alpha)
, for some
\alpha\in(0,\infty)
. Then
(\widetilde{P}_{1})_{\sharp}\eta\in\mathcal{T}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}},|.-.|,\alpha)
, where
|\cdot|
denotes the Euclidean norm on
\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}
.

Proof.

For any
\omega,\overline{\omega}\in\Omega
such that
\omega-\overline{\omega}\in\mathcal{H}
, Jensen’s inequality implies that

\|\omega-\overline{\omega}\|_{\infty}^{2}=\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\Big|\int_{0}^{t}(\dot{\omega}_{s}-\dot{\overline{\omega}}_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s\Big|^{2}\leq\sup_{t\in[0,1]}t\int_{0}^{t}|\dot{\omega}_{s}-\dot{\overline{\omega}}_{s}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\leq\int_{0}^{1}|\dot{\omega}_{s}-\dot{\overline{\omega}}_{s}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s=\|\omega-\overline{\omega}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}

Thus, for any
\omega,\overline{\omega}\in\Omega
,

\displaystyle|\widetilde{P}_{1}\omega-\widetilde{P}_{1}\overline{\omega}|^{2}\leq\|\omega-\overline{\omega}\|_{\infty}^{2}\leq c_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}(\omega,\overline{\omega}).

Hence the contraction principle from
Lemma

2.2
yields the claim.
∎

Now consider the law
\bm{\mu}
of the Brownian motion
\bm{B}
on
\mathbb{G}
. Let
P_{1}\colon\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}\to\mathbb{G}
denote the projection of a
\mathbb{G}
-valued path onto its final time evaluation; i.e.
P_{1}\bm{\omega}=\bm{\omega}_{1}
for any
\bm{\omega}\in\mathbb{G}
. Suppose that there exists
\widetilde{\bm{\Omega}}_{\mathbb{G}}\subseteq\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}
with
\bm{\mu}(\widetilde{\bm{\Omega}}_{\mathbb{G}})=1
and some measurable function
L\colon\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}\to[0,\infty]
such that

(6.8)

\displaystyle d_{\mathrm{CC}}(P_{1}\bm{\omega},P_{1}\widetilde{\bm{\omega}})\leq L(\bm{\omega})C_{\mathcal{H}}(\bm{\omega},\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}),

for all
\bm{\omega},\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}\in\widetilde{\bm{\Omega}}_{\mathbb{G}}
.
If
L\in L^{\infty}(\bm{\mu})
, then, by
Lemma

2.2
, the
\mathcal{T}_{2}
inequality for
\bm{\mu}
implies a
\mathcal{T}_{2}
inequality for
\bm{\mu}_{1}
. If we only have
L\in L^{q}(\bm{\mu})
for some
q\in[2,\infty)
, then
Lemma

2.2
still implies a
\mathcal{T}_{p}
inequality for
p=\tfrac{2q}{2+q}\in[1,2)
. The following result shows that any such
L
cannot belong to
L^{q}
for any
q\in[2,\infty]
, and thus the contraction principle from
Lemma

2.2
is not applicable.

Proposition 6.9
.

Let
L\colon\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}\to[0,\infty]
be as in (
6.8
). Then
\bm{\mu}(L=\infty)>0
. In particular,
L\notin L^{q}(\bm{\mu})
for any
q\in(0,\infty]
.

We make use of the following example in the proof of
Proposition

6.9
and again below in the proof of
Proposition

6.11
.

Lemma 6.10
.

Let
a>0
and define
h\in\mathcal{H}
by
h_{t}=(at,0,\dotsc,0)\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}
, for all
t\in[0,1]
. Then, for any
s,t\in[0,1]
with
s\leq t
, there exists a standard normal random variable
Z_{s,t}
such that

\displaystyle d_{\mathrm{CC}}^{2}(\bm{B}_{s,t},(T_{h}\bm{B})_{s,t})\geq aC(t-s)^{\frac{3}{2}}|Z_{s,t}|,

for some constant
C>0
independent of
a
,
s
and
t
. Moreover, for
u,v,s,t\in[0,1]
with
u\leq v\leq s\leq t
, the random variables
Z_{u,v}
and
Z_{s,t}
are independent.

Proof.

For
\bm{B}=(\bm{B}^{(1)},\bm{B}^{(2)})
, write
\bm{B}^{(1)}=(B^{1},\dotsc,B^{d_{1}})
. Let
s,t\in[0,1]
with
s\leq t
. Since
h
is only non-zero in its first component,
Remark

2.18
implies that

\displaystyle\bm{B}_{s,t}^{-1}(T_{h}\bm{B})_{s,t}

\displaystyle=\bigg(h_{s,t},a\sum_{j=2}^{d_{1}}w_{1,j}\int_{s}^{t}(s-r)\,\mathrm{d}B^{j}_{r}\bigg).

Choose
k\in\{1,\dotsc,m\}
such that
s_{k}^{2}\coloneqq\sum_{j=2}^{d_{1}}|w_{1,j}^{k}|^{2}>0
. By the left-invariance of
d_{\mathrm{CC}}
and the estimate (
2.11
), there exists a constant
\kappa>0
such that

\displaystyle d_{\mathrm{CC}}^{2}(\bm{B}_{s,t},(T_{h}\bm{B})_{s,t})

\displaystyle\geq a\kappa^{-1}\bigg|\sum_{j=2}^{d_{1}}w_{1,j}\int_{s}^{t}(s-r)\,\mathrm{d}B^{j}_{r}\bigg|\geq a\kappa^{-1}\bigg|\sum_{j=2}^{d_{1}}w_{1,j}^{k}\int_{s}^{t}(s-r)\,\mathrm{d}B^{j}_{r}\bigg|.

By Itô’s isometry, we can define a standard normal random variable

\displaystyle Z_{s,t}\coloneqq s_{k}^{-1}(t-s)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\sqrt{3}\sum_{j=2}^{d_{1}}w_{1,j}^{k}\int_{s}^{t}(s-r)\,\mathrm{d}B^{j}_{r}.

Thus, setting
C=3^{-\frac{1}{2}}\kappa^{-1}s_{k}
, we have

\displaystyle d_{\mathrm{CC}}^{2}(\bm{B}_{s,t},(T_{h}\bm{B})_{s,t})\geq aC(t-s)^{\frac{3}{2}}|Z_{s,t}|.

The independence property follows from the independence of Brownian increments.
∎

Proof of
Proposition

6.9
.

Suppose for contradiction that
\bm{\mu}(L<\infty)=1
. Let
\bm{B}
be a Brownian motion on
\mathbb{G}
, let
\delta>0
, and define
h\in\mathcal{H}
by
h_{t}=(\delta t,0\dotsc,0)\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}
, for all
t\in[0,1]
. By
Lemma

6.10
, there exists a constant
C>0
independent of
\delta
and a standard normal random variable
Z
such that we have the lower bound

\displaystyle d_{\mathrm{CC}}^{2}(P_{1}\bm{B},P_{1}(T_{h}\bm{B}))\geq\delta C|Z|.

On the other hand, by definition of the cost
C_{\mathcal{H}}
,

\displaystyle C_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}(\bm{B},T_{h}\bm{B})=\|h\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}=\delta^{2}.

Therefore, (
6.8
) implies that

\displaystyle C\delta|Z|\leq L(\bm{B})\delta^{2}.

Since both
|Z|
and
L(\bm{B})
are almost surely finite, taking the limit as
\delta\to 0
gives a contradiction.
∎

We now show that, contrary to the Euclidean case, the cost functions
C_{n}
defined in (
6.2
) may not converge pointwise to
C_{\mathcal{H}}
. Again, we use the example from
Lemma

6.10
.

Proposition 6.11
.

Let
\bm{B}
be a Brownian motion on
\mathbb{G}
, and define
h\in\mathcal{H}
by
h_{t}=(t,0,\dotsc,0)\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}
, for all
t\in[0,1]
. Then

\lim_{n\to\infty}C_{n}(\bm{B},T_{h}\bm{B})=\infty

almost surely.

Proof.

Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
By
Lemma

6.10
, there exist independent standard normal random variables
Z_{n,k}
,
k\in\{1,\dotsc,2^{n}\}
, such that

\displaystyle C_{n}^{2}(\bm{B},T_{h}\bm{B})

\displaystyle=2^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{2^{n}}d_{\mathrm{CC}}^{2}(\bm{B}_{t^{n}_{k-1},t^{n}_{k}},(T_{h}\bm{B})_{t^{n}_{k-1},t^{n}_{k}})\geq C2^{-n/2}\sum_{k=1}^{2^{n}}|Z_{n,k}|.

Note that
(|Z_{n,k}|)_{k=1,\dots,2^{n}}
are independent half-normal random variables with mean
\sqrt{2/\pi}
and variance
1-2/\pi
.
Thus, by Chebyshev’s inequality, for any
\delta>0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigg(\bigg\lvert\sum_{k=1}^{2^{n}}\frac{|Z_{n,k}|-\sqrt{2/\pi}}{2^{n}}\bigg\rvert>\delta\bigg)\leq\delta^{-2}(1-2/\pi)2^{-n}.

The right-hand side is summable in
n
and so, by the first Borel–Cantelli lemma,

\lim_{n\to\infty}2^{-n}\sum_{k=1}^{2^{n}}|Z_{n,k}|=\sqrt{2/\pi}\quad\text{almost surely.}

Hence, we have

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}C_{n}^{2}(\bm{B},T_{h}\bm{B})\geq C\liminf_{n\to\infty}2^{n/2}\cdot 2^{-n}\sum_{k=1}^{2^{n}}|Z_{n,k}|=C\sqrt{2/\pi}\lim_{n\to\infty}2^{n/2}=+\infty,

almost surely.
∎

When the marginals are related by a deterministic shift, we can identify the optimal coupling for the cost
C_{\mathcal{H}}
, and we have the following equality.

Lemma 6.12
.

Let
h\in\mathcal{H}
be deterministic and
\bm{\nu}=\mathrm{Law}(T_{h}\bm{B})
. Then
\mathrm{T}_{C_{\mathcal{H}},2}(\bm{\mu},\bm{\nu})
admits a unique optimal coupling, this coupling is induced by a Monge map, and

\displaystyle\mathrm{T}_{C_{\mathcal{H}},2}^{2}(\bm{\mu},\bm{\nu})=\mathbb{E}[C_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}(\bm{B},T_{h}\bm{B})]=2H(\bm{\nu}\|\bm{\mu})<\infty.

Proof.

The second equality follows from
Theorem

3.3
. Indeed, if
\bm{\nu}=\mathrm{Law}(T_{h}\bm{B})
, then
h=b^{\nu}
for the Föllmer drift
b^{\nu}
from
Theorem

3.3
.
Since
\bm{\nu}=\mathrm{Law}(T_{h}\bm{B})\ll\bm{\mu}
, we have that
H(\bm{\nu}\|\bm{\mu})<\infty
.
Now, from the definition of
C_{\mathcal{H}}
, and the fact that
h\in\mathcal{H}
is deterministic, we have optimality of
\bm{\lambda}^{\ast}=\mathrm{Law}(\bm{B},T_{h}\bm{B})
.
To see this, let
\bm{\lambda}\in\Pi(\bm{\mu},\bm{\nu})
and consider the event
E\coloneqq\{\,(\bm{\omega},\overline{\bm{\omega}})\in\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}\times\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}:\overline{\bm{\omega}}=T_{\pi_{1}\overline{\bm{\omega}}-\pi_{1}\bm{\omega}}\bm{\omega}\,\}
. If
\bm{\lambda}(E)<1
, then by definition of
C_{\mathcal{H}}
, we have
\int C_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}(\bm{\omega},\overline{\bm{\omega}})\,\mathrm{d}\bm{\lambda}(\bm{\omega},\overline{\bm{\omega}})=+\infty
. Suppose now that
\bm{\lambda}(E)=1
. Then, by Jensen’s inequality,

\displaystyle\int C_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}(\bm{\omega},\overline{\bm{\omega}})\,\mathrm{d}\bm{\lambda}(\bm{\omega},\overline{\bm{\omega}})

\displaystyle=\int\|\pi_{1}\overline{\bm{\omega}}-\pi_{1}\bm{\omega}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\bm{\lambda}(\bm{\omega},\overline{\bm{\omega}})

\displaystyle\geq\Bigl\|\int\pi_{1}\overline{\bm{\omega}}\,\mathrm{d}\bm{\nu}(\overline{\bm{\omega}})-\int\pi_{1}\bm{\omega}\,\mathrm{d}\bm{\mu}(\bm{\omega})\Bigr\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}=\|h\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}.

Equality holds if and only if
\bm{\lambda}(\{\,(\bm{\omega},\overline{\bm{\omega}})\in\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}\times\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}:\pi_{1}\overline{\bm{\omega}}-\pi_{1}\bm{\omega}=h\,\})=1
. Combined with the condition that
\bm{\lambda}(E)=1
, we see that any optimal coupling is concentrated on the graph of the function
T_{h}\colon\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}\to\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}
. Thus, there is a unique optimal coupling of Monge form given by
\bm{\lambda}^{\ast}=(\mathrm{id}\times T_{h})_{\sharp}\bm{\mu}=\mathrm{Law}(\bm{B},T_{h}\bm{B})
, and
\mathrm{T}_{C_{\mathcal{H}},2}^{2}(\bm{\mu},\bm{\nu})=\mathbb{E}[C_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}(\bm{B},T_{h}\bm{B})]
.
∎

Remark 6.13
.

Let
h\in\mathcal{H}
be as in
Proposition

6.11
. Since
\bm{\nu}=\mathrm{Law}(T_{h}\bm{B})\ll\bm{\mu}
, we have that
H(\bm{\nu}\|\bm{\mu})<\infty
.
By
Lemma

6.3
, we thus observe that
\mathrm{T}_{C_{n},2}^{2}(\bm{\mu},\bm{\nu})\leq H(\bm{\nu}\|\bm{\mu})<\infty
.
However,
Proposition

6.11
shows that
\lim_{n\to\infty}C_{n}(\bm{B},T_{h}\bm{B})=+\infty
almost surely. Thus
\mathrm{Law}(\bm{B},T_{h}\bm{B})
is suboptimal for some
C_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

In the case of the Heisenberg group
\mathbb{G}=\mathbb{H}^{n}
, this suboptimality can already be seen for
\mathrm{T}_{d_{\mathrm{CC}},2}(\bm{\mu}_{1},\bm{\nu}_{1})
. Indeed,
[
AR04
, Theorem 5.1]
shows that there is a unique optimal coupling and that this coupling is concentrated on the graph of some function
\phi\colon\mathbb{H}^{n}\to\mathbb{H}^{n}
. Taking, for example,
h
as in
Lemma

6.10
, it is clear that
\mathrm{Law}(\bm{B}_{1},(T_{h}\bm{B})_{1})
is not concentrated on any such graph, since
(T_{h}\bm{B})_{1}
is not measurable with respect to
\sigma(\bm{B}_{1})
.

6.3.
Riemannian approximation of the Heisenberg group

In the case of classical Wiener space, we consider paths taking values in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with the Euclidean geometry. In the present Carnot group setting, we note the following two distinctions:

(i)

(\mathbb{G},d_{\mathrm{CC}})
is a sub-Riemannian metric space (the sub-Laplacian is hypoelliptic);

(ii)

the group operation on
\mathbb{G}
is non-commutative.

It is shown in
[
CDPT07
, Theorem 2.12]
that any Carnot group can be approximated by Riemannian manifolds in the sense of pointed Gromov–Hausdorff convergence; see also
[
AS20
, Section 2.5]
and, for the Heisenberg group,
[
AR04
, Section 6]
. In making this approximation, we move out of the sub-Riemannian setting but retain non-commutativity. We observe that, in this case, a
\mathcal{T}_{2}
inequality on path space also holds (Proposition
6.14
)
and that the blow-up of discretised cost functions shown in
Proposition

6.11
does not occur (see Proposition
6.17
).
The failure to recover the
\mathcal{T}_{2}
inequality on the underlying space via projection that was shown in
Proposition

6.9
is still observed (Proposition
6.16
).
However, in contrast to the sub-Riemannian case, we can use the contraction principle to obtain a
\mathcal{T}_{p}
inequality on the underlying space for any
p\in[1,2)
(Proposition
6.15
).

In order to ease the presentation of this section, we specialise to the Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}=\mathbb{H}^{1}\cong\mathbb{R}^{2}\oplus\mathbb{R}
. Recall the left-invariant vector fields
(V_{1},V_{2},V_{3})=(X,Y,Z)
, where

\displaystyle X=\partial_{x}+\tfrac{1}{2}y\partial_{z},\quad Y=\partial_{y}-\tfrac{1}{2}x\partial_{z},\quad Z=[X,Y]=\partial_{z},

and the group operation

\bm{x}\bm{x}^{\prime}=\big(x+x^{\prime},y+y^{\prime},z+z^{\prime}+\tfrac{1}{2}(xy^{\prime}{-}x^{\prime}y)\big),\quad\bm{x}=(x,y,z),\,\bm{x}^{\prime}=(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime})\in\mathbb{H}.

For
\varepsilon>0
, define the manifold
M_{\varepsilon}
to be
\mathbb{R}^{2}\oplus\mathbb{R}
equipped with the Euclidean topology and orthonormal basis
(X,Y,Z_{\varepsilon})
, where
Z_{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon Z
. Let
d_{\varepsilon}
denote the induced Riemannian distance, which is again left invariant. By
[
CDPT07
, Theorem 2.12]
,
(\mathbb{H},d_{\mathrm{CC}})
is the limit of the Riemannian manifolds
(M_{\varepsilon},d_{\varepsilon})
as
\varepsilon\to 0
, in the sense of pointed Gromov–Hausdorff convergence.
As in
[
AR04
, Section 6]
, we see that, for any
\bm{x},\bm{y}\in M_{\varepsilon}
,

\displaystyle d_{\varepsilon}(\bm{x},\bm{y})=\inf\Bigl\{\,\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{|\dot{\gamma}^{1}_{t}|^{2}+|\dot{\gamma}^{2}_{t}|^{2}+\varepsilon^{-2}|\dot{\gamma}^{3}_{t}-\tfrac{1}{2}(\gamma^{1}_{t}\dot{\gamma}^{2}_{t}-\dot{\gamma}^{1}_{t}\gamma^{2}_{t})|^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t:\gamma\in\mathrm{AC}([0,1],M_{\varepsilon}),\,\gamma_{0}=\bm{x},\gamma_{1}=\bm{y}\,\Bigr\},

and, for
\varepsilon_{0},\varepsilon_{1}>0
with
\varepsilon_{1}\leq\varepsilon_{0}
,

\displaystyle d_{\varepsilon_{0}}(\bm{x},\bm{y})\leq d_{\varepsilon_{1}}(\bm{x},\bm{y})\leq d_{\mathrm{CC}}(\bm{x},\bm{y})=\sup_{\varepsilon>0}d_{\varepsilon}(\bm{x},\bm{y}).

Moreover, by
[
Jui14
, Lemma 1.1]
, there exists a constant
c>0
such that, for any
\varepsilon>0
,
\bm{x},\bm{y}\in M_{\varepsilon}
,

(6.9)

\displaystyle d_{\mathrm{CC}}(\bm{x},\bm{y})\leq d_{\varepsilon}(\bm{x},\bm{y})+c\varepsilon.

We will also make use of the following bounds. There exists a constant
\overline{\kappa}>0
such that, for any
\varepsilon>0
and
\bm{x}=(0,0,z)\in M_{\varepsilon}
,

(6.10)

\overline{\kappa}(|z|^{\frac{1}{2}}-\varepsilon)\leq d_{\varepsilon}(0,\bm{x})\leq\varepsilon^{-1}|z|,

where the lower bound follows from (
2.11
) combined with (
6.9
), and the upper bound from considering the length of a purely vertical path.

On the space
(M_{\varepsilon},d_{\varepsilon})
, we consider the same non-commutative group law as on
\mathbb{H}
, but now the distance
d_{\varepsilon}
is Riemannian.

Consider a Brownian motion
B
on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
with law
\mu
and Cameron–Martin space
\mathcal{H}=W^{1,2}_{0}([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{3})
.
We can define a Brownian motion
\bm{B}^{\varepsilon}
on
(M_{\varepsilon},d_{\varepsilon})
by

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\bm{B}^{\varepsilon}_{t}=X(\bm{B}^{\varepsilon})\,\mathrm{d}B^{1}_{t}+Y(\bm{B}^{\varepsilon})\,\mathrm{d}B^{2}_{t}+Z_{\varepsilon}\,\mathrm{d}B^{3}_{t},

and let
\bm{\mu}^{\varepsilon}=\mathrm{Law}(\bm{B}^{\varepsilon})
.
Explicitly,
\bm{B}^{\varepsilon}=(\bm{B}^{\varepsilon,(1)},\bm{B}^{\varepsilon,(2)})
with

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\bm{B}^{\varepsilon,(1)}_{t}=\mathrm{d}(B^{1},B^{2})_{t},\quad\mathrm{d}\bm{B}^{\varepsilon,(2)}_{t}=\frac{1}{2}(B^{2}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}B^{1}_{t}-B^{1}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}B^{2}_{t})+\varepsilon\,\mathrm{d}B^{3}_{t}.

Let
\Omega\coloneqq C_{0}([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{3})
and
\bm{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}\coloneqq C_{0}([0,1],M_{\varepsilon})
, and define a map
\Psi^{\varepsilon}\colon\Omega\to\bm{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}
by

\displaystyle\Psi^{\varepsilon}(\omega)=(0,\varepsilon\omega^{3})\Psi((\omega^{1},\omega^{2})),\quad\omega=(\omega^{1},\omega^{2},\omega^{3})\in\Omega,

where
\Psi
is the lift map defined in
Definition

2.12
. Define its domain as
\mathrm{Dom}(\Psi^{\varepsilon})\coloneqq\{\,\omega=(\omega^{1},\omega^{2},\omega^{3})\in\Omega:(\omega^{1},\omega^{2})\in\mathrm{Dom}(\Psi)\,\}
. For
\omega
absolutely continuous,
\Psi^{\varepsilon}
takes the explicit form

\displaystyle\Psi^{\varepsilon}(\omega)_{t}=\Big((\omega^{1}_{t},\omega^{2}_{t}),\,\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}(\omega^{1}_{r}\,\mathrm{d}\omega^{2}_{r}-\omega^{2}_{r}\,\mathrm{d}\omega^{1}_{r})+\varepsilon\omega^{3}_{t}\Big),\quad t\in[0,1].

Similarly to
Proposition

2.13
, we have that
\bm{B}^{\varepsilon}=\Psi^{\varepsilon}(B)
almost surely. We can also define a shift map
T^{\varepsilon}_{h}\colon\bm{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}\to\bm{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}
, for any
h=(h^{1},h^{2},h^{3})\in\mathcal{H}
, by

\displaystyle T^{\varepsilon}_{h}\bm{\omega}=(0,\varepsilon h^{3})T_{(h^{1},h^{2})}\bm{\omega},\quad\bm{\omega}\in\bm{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}.

Then, similarly to
Proposition

2.16
, for any
\omega\in\mathrm{Dom}(\Psi^{\varepsilon})
and
h\in\mathcal{H}
, we have

\displaystyle T^{\varepsilon}_{h}\Psi^{\varepsilon}(\omega)=\Psi^{\varepsilon}(\omega+h).

Now define a cost function
C^{\varepsilon}_{\mathcal{H}}\colon\bm{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}\times\bm{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}\to[0,\infty]
by

\displaystyle C^{\varepsilon}_{\mathcal{H}}(\bm{\omega},\widetilde{\bm{\omega}})=\begin{cases}\|h\|_{\mathcal{H}},&\text{if}\;\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}=T^{\varepsilon}_{h}\bm{\omega},\quad\text{for some}\;h\in\mathcal{H},\\
+\infty,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Taking the same approach as in
Theorem

4.4
, we see that
\bm{\mu}^{\varepsilon}
satisfies a
\mathcal{T}_{2}
inequality with this cost.

Proposition 6.14
.

We have the cost-information inequality
\bm{\mu}^{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{T}_{2}(\bm{\Omega}^{\varepsilon},C^{\varepsilon}_{\mathcal{H}},1)
.

Proof.

As noted in
Section

2.1
,
\mu\in\mathcal{T}_{2}(\Omega,c_{\mathcal{H}},1)
, where the cost
c_{\mathcal{H}}
is defined in
Eq.

2.2
. We also have that
\bm{\mu}^{\varepsilon}=\Psi^{\varepsilon}_{\sharp}\mu
and
\mu(\mathrm{Dom}(\Psi^{\varepsilon}))=1
. Moreover, for any
x,y\in\mathrm{Dom}(\Psi^{\varepsilon})
with
h\coloneqq y-x\in\mathcal{H}
,

\displaystyle C^{\varepsilon}_{\mathcal{H}}(\Psi^{\varepsilon}(x),\Psi^{\varepsilon}(y))

\displaystyle=C^{\varepsilon}_{\mathcal{H}}(\Psi^{\varepsilon}(x),\Psi^{\varepsilon}(x+h))=C^{\varepsilon}_{\mathcal{H}}(\Psi^{\varepsilon}(x),T^{\varepsilon}_{h}\Psi^{\varepsilon}(x))=\|h\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}=c_{\mathcal{H}}(x,y).

In case
y-x\notin\mathcal{H}
, then both sides are infinite. Thus, applying the contraction principle from
Lemma

2.2
, we have that
\bm{\mu}^{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{T}_{2}(\bm{\Omega}^{\varepsilon},C^{\varepsilon}_{\mathcal{H}},1)
.
∎

In contrast to the sub-Riemannian setting,
\bm{\mu}^{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{T}_{2}(\bm{\Omega}^{\varepsilon},C^{\varepsilon}_{\mathcal{H}},1)
implies a
\mathcal{T}_{p}
inequality for
\bm{\mu}^{\varepsilon}_{1}\coloneqq\mathrm{Law}(\bm{B}^{\varepsilon}_{1})
, for
p\in[1,2)
, with a constant depending on
\varepsilon
.

Let
P_{1}\colon\bm{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}\to M_{\varepsilon}
denote the projection
P_{1}(\bm{\omega})=\bm{\omega}_{1}
, for
\bm{\omega}\in\bm{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}
, so that
\bm{\mu}^{\varepsilon}_{1}=(P_{1})_{\sharp}\bm{\mu}^{\varepsilon}
.

Proposition 6.15
.

Suppose that
\bm{\mu}^{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{T}_{2}(\bm{\Omega}^{\varepsilon},C^{\varepsilon}_{\mathcal{H}},1)
. Then, for any
p\in[1,2)
, there exists
\alpha(\varepsilon,p)>0
such that
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\alpha(\varepsilon,p)=0
and
\bm{\mu}^{\varepsilon}_{1}\in\mathcal{T}_{p}(M_{\varepsilon},d_{\varepsilon},\alpha(\varepsilon,p))
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\bm{\omega},\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}\in\bm{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}
with
\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}=T^{\varepsilon}_{h}\bm{\omega}
, for some
h\in\mathcal{H}
. Define
\bm{h}=\Psi^{\varepsilon}(h)
and
\bm{\gamma}_{1}=\bm{\omega}_{1}^{-1}(T^{\varepsilon}_{h}\bm{\omega})_{1}
. Then

\displaystyle d_{\varepsilon}(P_{1}(\bm{\omega}),P_{1}(\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}))

\displaystyle\leq d_{\varepsilon}(\bm{h}_{1},\bm{\gamma}_{1})+d_{\varepsilon}(0,\bm{h}_{1}).

We bound
d_{\varepsilon}(0,\bm{h}_{1})
by the length of the curve
t\mapsto\bm{h}_{t}
in
\bm{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}
to get

(6.11)

\begin{split}d_{\varepsilon}(0,\bm{h}_{1})&\leq\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{|\dot{h}^{1}_{r}|^{2}+|\dot{h}^{2}_{r}|^{2}+\varepsilon^{-2}|\varepsilon\dot{h}^{3}_{r}+\tfrac{1}{2}(h^{1}_{r}\dot{h}^{2}_{r}-h^{2}_{r}\dot{h}^{1}_{r})-\tfrac{1}{2}(h^{1}_{r}\dot{h}^{2}_{r}-h^{2}_{r}\dot{h}^{1}_{r})|^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}r\\
&=\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{|\dot{h}^{1}_{r}|^{2}+|\dot{h}^{2}_{r}|^{2}+|\dot{h}^{3}_{r}|^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}r\leq\|h\|_{\mathcal{H}}.\end{split}

Similarly to
Remark

2.18
, by integration by parts,

(6.12)

\displaystyle\bm{h}_{1}^{-1}\bm{\gamma}_{1}=\Big(0,\int_{0}^{1}\Big(h^{1}_{r}\,\mathrm{d}\omega^{2}_{r}-h^{2}_{r}\,\mathrm{d}\omega^{1}_{r}\Big)\Big),

and so, by (
6.10
),

\displaystyle d_{\varepsilon}(\bm{h}_{1},\bm{\gamma}_{1})

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon^{-1}\Big|\int_{0}^{1}\Big(h^{1}_{r}\,\mathrm{d}\omega^{2}_{r}-h^{2}_{r}\,\mathrm{d}\omega^{1}_{r}\Big)\Big|

\displaystyle\leq 2\varepsilon^{-1}\|\omega\|_{\infty}\|h\|_{\mathcal{H}},

where
\|\omega\|_{\infty}\coloneqq\sup_{t\in[0,1]}|(\omega^{1}_{t},\omega^{2}_{t})|
.
Hence

\displaystyle d_{\varepsilon}(P_{1}(\bm{\omega}),P_{1}(\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}))\leq(1+2\varepsilon^{-1}\|\omega\|_{\infty})\|h\|_{\mathcal{H}}=(1+2\varepsilon^{-1}\|\omega\|_{\infty})C^{\varepsilon}_{\mathcal{H}}(\bm{\omega},\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}).

In the case that there does not exist
h\in\mathcal{H}
such that
\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}=T_{h}\bm{\omega}
, then the same inequality holds trivially.

Next note that, for any
q\in[1,\infty)
,
\bm{\omega}\mapsto\|\omega\|_{\infty}\in L^{q}(\bm{\mu})
. Let
p\in[1,2)
and set
q=\tfrac{2p}{2-p}\in[2,\infty)
. By the contraction principle from
Lemma

2.2
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\bm{\mu}_{1}\in\mathcal{T}_{p}(M_{\varepsilon},d_{\varepsilon},\alpha(\varepsilon,p)),\quad\text{where}\quad\alpha(\varepsilon,p)=\bigl(1+2\varepsilon^{-1}\mathbb{E}[\|B\|_{\infty}^{q}]^{\frac{1}{q}}\bigr)^{-2},

and we see that
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\alpha(\varepsilon,p)=0
.
∎

Analogously to the sub-Riemannian setting, however, it is not possible to recover a
\mathcal{T}_{2}
inequality via the contraction principle, as the next result shows.

Proposition 6.16
.

Let
\widetilde{\bm{\Omega}}\subseteq\bm{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}
such that
\bm{\mu}(\widetilde{\bm{\Omega}})=1
, and let
L\colon\bm{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}\to[0,\infty]
be a measurable function such that

\displaystyle d_{\varepsilon}(P_{1}\bm{\omega},P_{1}\widetilde{\bm{\omega}})\leq L(\bm{\omega})C_{\mathcal{H}}^{\varepsilon}(\bm{\omega},\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}),

for all
\bm{\omega},\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}\in\widetilde{\bm{\Omega}}
. Then
L\notin L^{\infty}(\bm{\mu}^{\varepsilon})
.

Proof.

Suppose for a contradiction that
L\in L^{\infty}(\bm{\mu}^{\varepsilon})
. Define
h\in\mathcal{H}
by
h_{t}=(t,0,0)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, for all
t\in[0,1]
. Then, similarly to
Lemma

6.10
, we can apply (
6.12
) and (
6.10
) to see that there exists a standard normal random variable
Z
and a constant
C(\varepsilon)>0
such that

\displaystyle d_{\varepsilon}^{2}(\bm{B}^{\varepsilon}_{1},(T^{\varepsilon}_{h}\bm{B}^{\varepsilon})_{1})\geq C(\varepsilon)|Z|.

We also have
C_{\mathcal{H}}^{\varepsilon}(\bm{B}^{\varepsilon},T^{\varepsilon}_{h}\bm{B}^{\varepsilon})=\|h\|_{\mathcal{H}}=1
. Thus
C(\varepsilon)|Z|\leq L(\bm{B}^{\varepsilon})
. Since
|Z|
is not essentially bounded, we arrive at a contradiction.
∎

Finally, in the Riemannian setting, we do not observe the blow-up shown in
Proposition

6.11
. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, define a cost
C^{\varepsilon}_{n}\colon\bm{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}\times\bm{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}\to[0,\infty)
by

\displaystyle C^{\varepsilon}_{n}(\bm{\omega},\widetilde{\bm{\omega}})^{2}

\displaystyle=2^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{2^{n}}d_{\varepsilon}^{2}(\bm{\omega}_{t^{n}_{k-1},t^{n}_{k}},\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}_{t^{n}_{k-1},t^{n}_{k}}),\quad\bm{\omega},\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}\in\bm{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}.

Proposition 6.17
.

For any
h\in\mathcal{H}
, we have

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}C^{\varepsilon}_{n}(\bm{B}^{\varepsilon},T^{\varepsilon}_{h}\bm{B}^{\varepsilon})\leq C^{\varepsilon}_{\mathcal{H}}(\bm{B}^{\varepsilon},T^{\varepsilon}_{h}\bm{B}^{\varepsilon})=\|h\|_{\mathcal{H}},

almost surely.

Proof.

Define
\bm{Z}
by
\bm{Z}_{s,t}=\bm{B}_{s,t}^{-1}(T^{\varepsilon}_{h}\bm{B})_{s,t}
, for
s,t\in[0,1]
with
s\leq t
, and
\bm{h}=\Psi^{\varepsilon}(h)
. Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
. By Young’s inequality, we bound

(6.13)

\displaystyle C^{\varepsilon}_{n}(\bm{B}^{\varepsilon},T^{\varepsilon}_{h}\bm{B}^{\varepsilon})^{2}\leq(1+n)C^{\varepsilon}_{n}(\bm{h},\bm{Z})^{2}+(1+\tfrac{1}{n})C^{\varepsilon}_{n}(0,\bm{h})^{2}.

As in (
6.11
), we bound
d_{\varepsilon}(\bm{h}_{t^{n}_{k-1}},\bm{h}_{t^{n}_{k}})
by the length of the curve
\bm{h}
; i.e.

\displaystyle d_{\varepsilon}(\bm{h}_{t^{n}_{k-1}},\bm{h}_{t^{n}_{k}})\leq\int_{t^{n}_{k-1}}^{t^{n}_{k}}\sqrt{|\dot{h}^{1}_{r}|^{2}+|\dot{h}^{2}_{r}|^{2}+|\dot{h}^{3}_{r}|^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}r.

Applying the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we have

\displaystyle C^{\varepsilon}_{n}(0,\bm{h})^{2}=2^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{2^{n}}d_{\varepsilon}^{2}(\bm{h}_{t^{n}_{k-1}},\bm{h}_{t^{n}_{k}})\leq\sum_{k=1}^{2^{n}}\int_{t^{n}_{k-1}}^{t^{n}_{k}}\Bigl(|\dot{h}^{1}_{r}|^{2}+|\dot{h}^{2}_{r}|^{2}+|\dot{h}^{3}_{r}|^{2}\Bigr)\mathrm{d}r=\|h\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2},

and so

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}(1+\tfrac{1}{n})C^{\varepsilon}_{n}(0,\bm{h})^{2}\leq\|h\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}.

Next, similarly to (
6.12
) and
Remark

2.18
, we have

\displaystyle\bm{h}_{s,t}^{-1}\bm{Z}_{s,t}=\Big(0,\int_{s}^{t}\Big(h^{1}_{s,r}\,\mathrm{d}B^{2}_{r}-h^{2}_{s,r}\,\mathrm{d}B^{1}_{r}\Big)\Big).

Therefore, using the estimate (
6.10
) and the fact that
B
is almost surely
\beta
-Hölder continuous for any
\beta\in(0,1/2)
, there exists a constant
c>0
such that we have the almost sure bound

\displaystyle d_{\varepsilon}^{2}(0,\bm{h}_{s,t}^{-1}\bm{Z}_{s,t})

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon^{-2}\Big|\int_{s}^{t}h^{1}_{s,r}\,\mathrm{d}B^{2}_{r}\Big|^{2}+\varepsilon^{-2}\Big|\int_{s}^{t}h^{2}_{s,r}\,\mathrm{d}B^{1}_{r}\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq c\varepsilon^{-2}\|B\|_{\beta}^{2}|t-s|^{1+2\beta}\int_{s}^{t}|\dot{h}_{r}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}r,

where
\|B\|_{\beta}
is the
\beta
-Hölder norm of
B
. Hence

\displaystyle C^{\varepsilon}_{n}(\bm{h},\bm{Z})^{2}

\displaystyle=2^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{2^{n}}d_{\varepsilon}^{2}(0,\bm{h}_{t^{n}_{k-1},t^{n}_{k}}^{-1}\bm{Z}_{t^{n}_{k-1},t^{n}_{k}})\leq c\varepsilon^{-2}2^{n}2^{-(1+2\beta)n}\|h\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\|B\|_{\beta}^{2}=c\varepsilon^{-2}\|h\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\|B\|_{\beta}^{2}2^{-2\beta n},

and so
\lim_{n\to\infty}(1+n)C^{\varepsilon}_{n}(\bm{h},\bm{Z})^{2}=0
almost surely. We conclude by
Equation

6.13
.
∎

6.4.
\Gamma
-convergence of the cost functions

Despite the pointwise blow-up of the cost functions
C_{n}
that we demonstrated in
Proposition

6.11
, we now show that

C_{n}
does converge to
C_{\mathcal{H}}
in a variational sense. More precisely, the sequence
C_{n}
converges to
C_{\mathcal{H}}
in the sense of
\Gamma
-convergence, a notion of convergence for families of minimisation problems that is formulated in terms of asymptotic lower and upper bounds.
On a metric space
(E,d_{E})
, we say that a sequence of functionals
F_{n}\colon E\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}

\Gamma
-converges to a limit
F_{\infty}\colon E\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
if

(i)

for every sequence
x_{n}\to x
in
E
, we have
F_{\infty}(x)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}F_{n}(x_{n})
; and

(ii)

for every
x\in E
, there exists a sequence
\widetilde{x}_{n}\to x
in
E
such that
\limsup_{n\to\infty}F_{n}(\widetilde{x}_{n})\leq F_{\infty}(x)
.

The sequence
(\widetilde{x}_{n})
in condition (ii) is called a
recovery sequence
as it “recovers” the correct energy level

F_{\infty}(x)
from the approximating energies
F_{n}(\widetilde{x}_{n})
by adding suitable oscillations to
x
. One may view

\Gamma
-convergence as describing the asymptotic behavior of energy landscapes, in close analogy with large deviation principles, which characterise the asymptotics of probability measures via their rate functions. Indeed, the interplay between
\Gamma
-convergence and large deviation principles has been studied in several publications; see e.g.
[
Mar18
,
Ber18
]
.
A central advantage of
\Gamma
-convergence is its stability property: convergence of functionals implies convergence of minimal values and, under mild compactness assumptions, convergence of (almost) minimisers. For a comprehensive treatment we refer to the monographs
[
DM93
,
Bra02
,
Rin18
]
.

We will see that the
\Gamma
-convergence of the cost functions
C_{n}
implies the convergence of the associated optimal transport problems; i.e. for every
\bm{\nu}\in\mathcal{P}(\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}})
there
exists a sequence of probability measures
\widetilde{\bm{\nu}}^{n}\in\mathcal{P}(\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}})
such
that the associated transport costs

\mathrm{T}_{C_{n},2}(\widetilde{\bm{\nu}}^{n},\bm{\mu})
converges to

\mathrm{T}_{C_{\mathcal{H}},2}(\bm{\nu},\bm{\mu})
as
n\to\infty
.

In the Euclidean case with cost
c_{n}
defined as in
(
6.4
), we have for
\omega,\widetilde{\omega}\in C_{0}([0,1];\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
h=\omega-\widetilde{\omega}
the
formula
c_{n}(\omega,\widetilde{\omega})=\int_{0}^{1}|\partial_{s}\widehat{h}^{n}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s
,
where
\widehat{h}^{n}
is the piecewise affine interpolant for
h
.
One readily verifies that
c_{n}(\omega,\widetilde{\omega})
converges
to
\|h\|_{\mathcal{H}}
if
h\in\mathcal{H}
and to
+\infty
otherwise; cf.
Lemma

6.4
. In particular, the
\Gamma
-convergence of
c_{n}
also holds in this setting with the same limiting cost.

Recall that we consider the metric space
(\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}},d_{\infty})
, where
d_{\infty}
is the uniform metric defined in (
2.13
).

We start with the following lemma, which gives the pointwise convergence
of the cost to the Cameron–Martin norm for horizontal curves.

Lemma 6.18
.

Let
h\in\mathcal{H}
, and let
\bm{h}=\Psi(h)
denote its lift to
\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}
. Then, for the family of cost functions
(C_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
defined in (
6.2
), we have
C_{n}(0,\bm{h})\leq\|h\|_{\mathcal{H}}

and
\lim_{n\to\infty}C_{n}(0,\bm{h})=\|h\|_{\mathcal{H}}
.

Proof.

Let
h\in\mathcal{H}
and let
\bm{h}=\Psi(h)
.
The curve
t\mapsto\bm{h}_{t}\in\mathbb{G}
is horizontal and therefore, for every
0\leq s<t\leq 1
, we have

d_{\mathrm{CC}}(\bm{h}_{s},\bm{h}_{t})\leq\int_{s}^{t}|\dot{h}_{r}|\,\mathrm{d}r.

Applying Hölder’s inequality, we obtain the estimate

C_{n}^{2}(0,\bm{h})=2^{n}\sum_{i=1}^{2^{n}}d_{\mathrm{CC}}(\bm{h}_{t_{i}^{n}},\bm{h}_{t_{i-1}^{n}})^{2}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{2^{n}}\int_{t_{i-1}^{n}}^{t_{i}^{n}}|\dot{h}_{r}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}r=\|h\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}.

Taking the
\limsup
on the left-hand side
gives
\limsup_{n\to\infty}C_{n}(0,\bm{h})\leq\|h\|_{\mathcal{H}}
.

To show the lower bound, define a piecewise constant function
g^{n}\colon[0,1]\to[0,\infty)
, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, by

g^{n}_{t}=2^{n}d_{\mathrm{CC}}(\bm{h}_{t_{i}^{n}},\bm{h}_{t_{i-1}^{n}})

for
t\in[t_{i-1}^{n},t_{i}^{n})
,
i\in\{1,\dotsc,n\}
.
Note that the sequence
(g^{n})
is uniformly bounded in
L^{2}([0,1])
.
Hence, we can extract a weakly converging subsequence such that
g^{n_{k}}\to g

in
L^{2}([0,1])
. For given
0\leq r<s\leq 1
, we can find indices
i_{n},\,j_{n}\in\{1,\ldots,2^{n}\}
such that,
for
r_{n}=t_{i_{n}}^{n}
and
s_{n}=t_{j_{n}}^{n}
,

0\leq r_{n}\leq r<s\leq s_{n}\leq 1,\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}r_{n}=r,\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}s_{n}=s.

By the triangle inequality and the continuity of
t\mapsto\bm{h}_{t}
, there is a sequence
(\varepsilon_{n})\subset\mathbb{R}
such that
\varepsilon_{n}\to 0
and

d_{\mathrm{CC}}(\bm{h}_{r},\bm{h}_{s})\leq\sum_{i=i_{n}}^{j_{n}}d_{\mathrm{CC}}(\bm{h}_{t_{i}^{n}},\bm{h}_{t_{i-1}^{n}})+d_{\mathrm{CC}}(\bm{h}_{r},\bm{h}_{r_{n}})+d_{\mathrm{CC}}(\bm{h}_{s},\bm{h}_{s_{n}})=\int_{r_{n}}^{s_{n}}g^{n}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t+\varepsilon_{n}.

Passing to the limit as
n\to\infty
, we infer that
d_{\mathrm{CC}}(\bm{h}_{r},\bm{h}_{s})\leq\int_{r}^{s}g_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t
.
Now, by the minimality of the metric derivative (see
Remark

2.8
),
we obtain
g_{t}\geq|\dot{h}_{t}|
for almost every
t\in(0,1)
.
Finally, since
C_{n}^{2}(0,\bm{h})=2^{n}\sum_{i=1}^{2^{n}}d_{\mathrm{CC}}^{2}(\bm{h}_{t_{i}^{n}},\bm{h}_{t_{i-1}^{n}})
, lower-semicontinuity of the norm implies that

\liminf_{n\to\infty}C_{n}^{2}(0,\bm{h})=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{1}(g^{n}_{t})^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\geq\int_{0}^{1}g_{t}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\geq\|h\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}},

which finishes the proof.
∎

The next lemma shows that the cost
C_{n}
blows up along sequences
\bm{\omega}^{n},\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}^{n}
that converge to limits
\bm{\omega},\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}

whose difference
\bm{\omega}^{-1}\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}
is a purely vertical process; i.e.
t\mapsto(\bm{\omega}^{-1}\widetilde{\bm{\omega}})_{t}=(0,\theta_{t})
.

Lemma 6.19
.

Let
\bm{\omega},\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}\in\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}
, and let
(\bm{\omega}^{n}),(\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}^{n})\subset\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}
be sequences such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}(\bm{\omega}^{n},\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}^{n})=(\bm{\omega},\widetilde{\bm{\omega}})
.
Suppose that there exists a non-zero
\bm{\theta}\in\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}
such that
\bm{\theta}_{t}=(0,\theta_{t})
and
\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}_{t}=\bm{\omega}_{t}\bm{\theta}_{t}
for all
t\in[0,T]
.
Then
\lim_{n\to\infty}C_{n}(\bm{\omega}^{n},\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}^{n})=\infty
.

Proof.

Suppose for contradiction that
C_{n}(\bm{\omega}^{n},\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}^{n})
is bounded uniformly in
n
.
We use the equivalence of the gauge distance
d_{g}
and the Carnot–Carathéodory distance
d_{\mathrm{CC}}
from (
2.11
) to obtain the lower bound

C_{n}(\bm{\omega}^{n},\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}^{n})^{2}=2^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{2^{n}}d_{\mathrm{CC}}\big(\bm{\omega}^{n}_{t_{k-1}^{n},t_{k}^{n}},\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}^{n}_{t_{k-1}^{n},t_{k}^{n}}\big)^{2}\geq\frac{2^{n}}{\kappa}\sum_{k=1}^{2^{n}}d_{g}\big(\bm{\omega}^{n}_{t_{k-1}^{n},t_{k}^{n}},\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}^{n}_{t_{k-1}^{n},t_{k}^{n}}\big)^{2}\geq\frac{2^{n}}{\kappa}\sum_{k=1}^{2^{n}}\big|\theta^{n}_{t_{k-1}^{n},t_{k}^{n}}\big|,

where
\theta^{n}_{s,t}=\pi_{2}((\bm{\omega}_{s,t}^{n})^{-1}\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}_{s,t}^{n})
are the increments of the vertical process.
Let
\widehat{\theta}^{n}_{t}
denote the piecewise affine interpolant associated with the increments
\theta^{n}_{t_{k-1}^{n},t_{k}^{n}}
with
\widehat{\theta}^{n}_{0}=0
.
The above estimate gives the bound
C_{n}(\bm{\omega}^{n},\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}^{n})^{2}\geq\frac{2^{n}}{\kappa}\|{\widehat{\theta}^{n}}\|_{W^{1,1}}
,
where the left-hand side is uniformly bounded with respect to
n
by assumption.
Thus
{\widehat{\theta}^{n}}\to 0
in
W^{1,1}_{0}([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}})
and so
\theta\equiv 0
as
W^{1,1}_{0}([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}})\hookrightarrow C_{0}([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}})
, giving a contradiction. We conclude that
\lim_{n\to\infty}C_{n}(\bm{\omega}^{n},\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}^{n})=\infty
.
∎

Now we prove condition (ii) in the definition of
\Gamma
-convergence for
C_{n}
, i.e. the existence of a recovery sequence. In fact, we show a stronger version; see Remark
6.23
.

Proposition 6.20
.

Let
\bm{\omega},\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}\in\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}
and let
(\bm{\omega}^{n})\subset\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}
be a sequence such that
\bm{\omega}^{n}\to\bm{\omega}
. Then there exists a sequence
(\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}^{n})\subset\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}
such that
\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}^{n}\to\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}
and

(6.14)

\limsup_{n\to\infty}C_{n}(\bm{\omega}^{n},\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}^{n})\leq C_{\mathcal{H}}(\bm{\omega},\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}).

Proof.

We only have to consider the case
\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}=T_{h}\bm{\omega}
for
h\in\mathcal{H}
, since the right-hand side in (
6.14
)
is otherwise infinite by definition of
C_{\mathcal{H}}
in (
2.18
), and the inequality holds trivially.
Let
\bm{\omega}\in\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}
,
h\in\mathcal{H}
, and
\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}=T_{h}\bm{\omega}
. Consider a sequence
(\bm{\omega}^{n})\subset\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}
such that
\bm{\omega}^{n}\to\bm{\omega}
in
\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}
. For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, define
\overline{\bm{\omega}}^{n}:=T_{h}\bm{\omega}^{n}\in\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}
.
By the continuity of the shift map from
Proposition

2.16
,
\overline{\bm{\omega}}^{n}\to\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}=T_{h}\bm{\omega}
in
\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}
.
As in Remark
2.18
, we introduce the non-commutativity error

\begin{split}\bm{\theta}^{n}_{s,t}&:=\bm{h}^{-1}_{s,t}(\bm{\omega}_{s,t}^{n})^{-1}\overline{\bm{\omega}}_{s,t}^{n}=\big(0,\theta^{n}_{s,t}\big),\\
\text{where}\quad\theta^{n}_{s,t}&=\Big(\int_{s}^{t}\mathsf{W}h_{s,r}\otimes\mathrm{d}\omega^{n}_{r}\Big).\end{split}

We define
\bm{\vartheta}^{n}\in\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}
such that its increments satisfy

(\bm{\vartheta}_{t_{k-1}^{n}}^{n})^{-1}\bm{\vartheta}_{t_{k}^{n}}^{n}=\bm{\theta}^{n}_{t_{k-1}^{n},t_{k}^{n}}
.
Indeed, we set

\bm{\vartheta}^{n}_{t}=(0,\vartheta^{n}_{t})
, where

\vartheta^{n}_{0}=0\quad\text{and}\quad\vartheta^{n}_{t}=\vartheta^{n}_{t_{k-1}^{n}}+\theta^{n}_{t_{k-1}^{n},t},\quad\text{for}\quad t\in(t_{k-1}^{n},t_{k}^{n}],\;k\in\{1,\dotsc,2^{n}\}.

We emphasise that, for any
t\in[0,1]
,
\bm{\vartheta}^{n}_{t}

is an element in the centre of the group
\mathbb{G}
. In particular, it commutes with every element in

\mathbb{G}
. Therefore, defining the curve
t\mapsto\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}^{n}_{t}=\overline{\bm{\omega}}^{n}_{t}(\bm{\vartheta}^{n}_{t})^{-1}\in\mathbb{G}
, we obtain that its increments satisfy

\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}^{n}_{s,t}=\overline{\bm{\omega}}^{n}_{s,t}(\bm{\vartheta}^{n}_{s,t})^{-1}=(\bm{\vartheta}_{s,t}^{n})^{-1}\overline{\bm{\omega}}_{s,t}^{n},\quad 0\leq s\leq t\leq 1.

Using the left-invariance of
d_{\mathrm{CC}}
and the definition of
\bm{\vartheta}^{n}
,
we find that, for
k\in\{1,\dotsc,2^{n}\}
,

\displaystyle d_{\mathrm{CC}}(\bm{\omega}^{n}_{t_{k-1}^{n},t_{k}^{n}},\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}^{n}_{t_{k-1}^{n},t_{k}^{n}})

\displaystyle=d_{\mathrm{CC}}\big(0,(\bm{\omega}^{n}_{t_{k-1}^{n},t_{k}^{n}})^{-1}\overline{\bm{\omega}}^{n}_{t_{k-1}^{n},t_{k}^{n}}(\bm{\vartheta}^{n}_{t_{k-1}^{n},t_{k}^{n}})^{-1}\big)

\displaystyle=d_{\mathrm{CC}}\big(0,\bm{h}_{t_{k-1}^{n},t_{k}^{n}}\bm{\theta}^{n}_{t_{k-1}^{n},t_{k}^{n}}(\bm{\theta}^{n}_{t_{k-1}^{n},t_{k}^{n}})^{-1}\big)=d_{\mathrm{CC}}(0,\bm{h}_{t_{k-1}^{n},t_{k}^{n}}).

Lemma

6.18
now allows us to pass to the
\limsup
. More precisely, we have that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}C_{n}(\bm{\omega}^{n},\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}^{n})=\limsup_{n\to\infty}C_{n}(0,\bm{h})\leq C_{\mathcal{H}}(\bm{\omega},\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}).

It remains to show that
\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}^{n}\to\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}
. Due to the convergence
\overline{\bm{\omega}}^{n}\to\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}
it suffices to show that
\bm{\vartheta}^{n}\to 0
in
\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}
or, equivalently,
\vartheta^{n}\to 0
in
C([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}})
. Using the definition of
\vartheta^{n}
, we find a constant
C>0
such that

\|\vartheta^{n}\|_{\infty}\leq C\sup_{k=1,\ldots,2^{n}}\max_{i<j}\sup_{t\in[t_{k-1}^{n},t_{k}^{n}]}|\omega_{t}^{n,i}{-}\omega_{t_{k}^{n}}^{n,i}|\big\|\dot{h}^{j}\big\|_{L^{1}}.

Since
\bm{\omega}^{n}
converges uniformly to
\bm{\omega}
, the right-hand side vanishes as
n\to\infty
.
∎

We now prove the lower estimate that is required in condition (i) of the definition of
\Gamma
-convergence.

Proposition 6.21
.

Let
\bm{\omega},\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}\in\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}
, and let
(\bm{\omega}^{n}),(\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}^{n})\subset\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}
be sequences such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}(\bm{\omega}^{n},\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}^{n})=(\bm{\omega},\widetilde{\bm{\omega}})
.
Then
\liminf_{n\to\infty}C_{n}(\bm{\omega}^{n},\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}^{n})\geq C_{\mathcal{H}}(\bm{\omega},\widetilde{\bm{\omega}})
.

Proof.

Consider a pair of curves
(\bm{\omega},\widetilde{\bm{\omega}})\in\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}\times\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}
and a pair of sequences
(\bm{\omega}^{n},\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}^{n})\subset\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}\times\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}
such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}(\bm{\omega}^{n},\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}^{n})=(\bm{\omega},\widetilde{\bm{\omega}})
.
We will consider three cases.

Case 1a.

First, we consider the case that
\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}=T_{h}\bm{\omega}
for some
h\in\mathcal{H}
.
We may assume that
I\coloneqq\liminf_{n\to\infty}C_{n}(\bm{\omega}^{n},\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}^{n})<\infty
, since otherwise the inequality holds trivially.
Let
h^{n}=\pi_{1}\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}^{n}-\pi_{1}\bm{\omega}^{n}

so that
h^{n}\to h

in
C([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}})
.
We now apply
Lemma

6.4
(iii), to see that

(6.15)

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}C_{n}(\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}^{n},\bm{\omega}^{n})^{2}\geq\liminf_{n\to\infty}2^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{2^{n}}|h_{t_{k}^{n}}^{n}-h_{t_{k-1}^{n}}^{n}|^{2}=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\|\widehat{h}^{n}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2},

where
\widehat{h}^{n}
is the piecewise affine interpolant of
h^{n}
.
Since
I\in[0,\infty)
, we can assume that
\widehat{h}^{n}

is bounded in
\mathcal{H}
and is weakly converging to a limit
\overline{h}\in\mathcal{H}
, which we see is equal to
h
.
By weak lower semicontinuity of the
L^{2}
norm we obtain
\liminf_{n\to\infty}C_{n}(\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}^{n},\bm{\omega}^{n})^{2}\geq\|h\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}
.

Case 1b.

Now suppose that
\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}=T_{h}\bm{\omega}
, where
h=\pi_{1}\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}-\pi_{1}\bm{\omega}\notin\mathcal{H}
. Then
C_{\mathcal{H}}(\bm{\omega},\widetilde{\bm{\omega}})=\infty
. Supposing again that
I\coloneqq\liminf_{n\to\infty}C_{n}(\bm{\omega}^{n},\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}^{n})<\infty
, following the same argument as above leads to
h\in\mathcal{H}
, which is a contradiction. Thus,
\liminf_{n\to\infty}C_{n}(\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}^{n},\bm{\omega}^{n})^{2}=\infty
.

Case 2.

We now assume that
h\coloneqq\pi_{1}\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}-\pi_{1}\bm{\omega}\in\mathcal{H}
but
\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}\neq T_{h}\bm{\omega}
; i.e.
\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}
is not a shift of
\bm{\omega}
.
We show that
C_{n}(\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}^{n},\bm{\omega}^{n})\to\infty
. Define
\widehat{\bm{\omega}}=T_{h}\bm{\omega}
such that, by assumption,
\widehat{\bm{\omega}}\neq\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}
but
\pi_{1}\widehat{\bm{\omega}}=\pi_{1}\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}
. Therefore, there exists
\theta\in C([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}})
such that
\theta\not\equiv 0
and
\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}=\widehat{\bm{\omega}}\bm{\theta}
with
\bm{\theta}=(0,\theta)
.
By
Proposition

6.20
, we find a sequence
\widehat{\bm{\omega}}^{n}
such that
\widehat{\bm{\omega}}^{n}\to\widehat{\bm{\omega}}
and

(6.16)

\limsup_{n\to\infty}C_{n}(\bm{\omega}^{n},\widehat{\bm{\omega}}^{n})\leq C_{\mathcal{H}}(\bm{\omega},\widehat{\bm{\omega}})=\|h\|_{\mathcal{H}}<\infty.

Now, by the triangle inequality and the estimate
(a+b)^{2}\leq 2a^{2}+2b^{2}
, for any
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
, we see that

C_{n}(\widehat{\bm{\omega}}^{n},\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}^{n})^{2}\leq 2C_{n}(\bm{\omega}^{n},\widehat{\bm{\omega}}^{n})^{2}+2C_{n}(\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}^{n},\bm{\omega}^{n})^{2}.

By (
6.16
), the first term on the right-hand side is bounded by
2\|h\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}
. By
Lemma

6.19
, we also have
\lim_{n\to\infty}C_{n}(\widehat{\bm{\omega}}^{n},\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}^{n})^{2}=\infty
. Thus we conclude that
\lim_{n\to\infty}C_{n}(\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}^{n},\bm{\omega}^{n})=\infty
.
∎

Combining
Propositions

6.20
and
6.21
, we deduce the following
\Gamma
-convergence.

Corollary 6.22
.

On
\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}\times\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}
equipped with the uniform topology, we have the
\Gamma
-convergence
C_{n}\xrightarrow{\Gamma}C_{\mathcal{H}}
.

Proof.

The liminf inequality follows directly from Proposition
6.21
. The limsup inequality in this setting reads: For every pair
(\bm{\omega},\widetilde{\bm{\omega}})\in\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}\times\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}
,
we can find a sequence
(\bm{\omega}^{n},\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}^{n})
converging to
(\bm{\omega},\widetilde{\bm{\omega}})
such that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}C_{n}(\bm{\omega}^{n},\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}^{n})\leq C_{n}(\bm{\omega},\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}).

Proposition
6.20
tells us that in fact we can take any sequence
\bm{\omega}^{n}
converging to
\bm{\omega}

and the sequence
\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}^{n}
constructed via adding a suitable perturbation.
∎

Remark 6.23
.

Let us note that Proposition
6.20
is stronger than the standard
\limsup
condition in
\Gamma
-convergence. In particular, we can choose the constant sequence
\bm{\omega}^{n}=\bm{\omega}
such that the recovery sequence is obtained via a map
\Phi^{n}(\bm{\omega},\widetilde{\bm{\omega}})=(\bm{\omega},\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}^{n})
.
We will use this map
\Phi^{n}
to construct sequences of transport plans
\widetilde{\bm{\lambda}}^{n}
that are recovery sequences for the family of optimal transport problems associated with
C_{n}
; see Proposition
6.26
below.

Having shown the
\Gamma
-convergence of the cost functions
C_{n}
, we can now deduce the
\Gamma
-convergence of the associated transport problems. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, define the family of transport functionals
\mathrm{I}_{n}\colon\mathcal{P}(\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}{\times}\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}})\to[0,\infty]
and
\mathrm{I}_{\infty}\colon\mathcal{P}(\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}{\times}\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}})\to[0,\infty]
via

\mathrm{I}_{n}(\bm{\lambda})=\int_{\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}\times\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}}C_{n}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\bm{\lambda}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{I}_{\infty}(\bm{\lambda})=\int_{\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}\times\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}}C_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\bm{\lambda},\quad\bm{\lambda}\in\mathcal{P}(\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}\times\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}).

Proposition 6.24
.

Let
(\bm{\lambda}^{n})\subset\mathcal{P}(\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}\times\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}})
be a sequence of probability measures such that
\bm{\lambda}^{n}\rightharpoonup\bm{\lambda}
in
\mathcal{P}(\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}\times\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}})
. Then

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathrm{I}_{n}(\bm{\lambda}^{n})\geq\mathrm{I}_{\infty}(\bm{\lambda}).

Proof.

By Skorokhod’s representation theorem, there exists a probability space
(\Xi,\mathfrak{A},\mathbb{P})
and random variables
\bm{Y}^{n}\colon\Xi\to\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}\times\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}
and
\bm{Y}\colon\Xi\to\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}\times\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}
such that
\bm{\lambda}^{n}=\bm{Y}^{n}_{\#}\mathbb{P}
,
\bm{\lambda}=\bm{Y}_{\#}\mathbb{P}
, and

\bm{Y}^{n}\to\bm{Y}

\mathbb{P}
-almost surely.
We conclude that

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int_{\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}\times\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}}C_{n}(\bm{\omega},\overline{\bm{\omega}})^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\bm{\lambda}^{n}

\displaystyle=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int_{\Xi}C_{n}(\bm{Y}^{n})^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\Xi}\liminf_{n\to\infty}C_{n}(\bm{Y}^{n})^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}\geq\int_{\Xi}C_{\mathcal{H}}(\bm{Y})^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}=\int_{\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}\times\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}}C_{\mathcal{H}}(\bm{\omega},\overline{\bm{\omega}})^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\bm{\lambda},

by Fatou’s lemma and
Proposition

6.21
.
∎

Remark 6.25
.

Given
Proposition

6.24
, we find a much more direct proof of
Theorem

6.6
. Indeed, combining
Lemma

6.3
and
Proposition

6.24
yields the result immediately.

Proposition 6.26
.

Let
\bm{\lambda}\in\mathcal{P}(\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}\times\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}})
. Then there exists a sequence

(\widetilde{\bm{\lambda}}^{n})\subset\mathcal{P}(\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}\times\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}})
such that
\widetilde{\bm{\lambda}}^{n}\rightharpoonup\bm{\lambda}
and

(6.17)

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathrm{I}_{n}(\widetilde{\bm{\lambda}}^{n})\leq\mathrm{I}_{\infty}(\bm{\lambda}).

Proof.

We may assume that the right-hand side in (
6.17
) is finite as the inequality is trivially true otherwise.
In particular, we have
(\bm{\omega},\overline{\bm{\omega}})\mapsto C_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}(\bm{\omega},\overline{\bm{\omega}})\in L^{1}(\bm{\lambda})

and, for
\bm{\lambda}
-almost every
(\bm{\omega},\overline{\bm{\omega}})\in\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}\times\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}
, we have that
\overline{\bm{\omega}}=T_{h}\bm{\omega}

for
h=\pi_{1}(\overline{\bm{\omega}}^{-1}\bm{\omega})\in\mathcal{H}
.

Define
\widetilde{\bm{\lambda}}^{n}=\Phi^{n}_{\#}\bm{\lambda}\in\mathcal{P}(\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}\times\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}})
,
where
\Phi^{n}\colon\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}\times\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}\to\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}\times\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}
maps
(\bm{\omega},\widetilde{\bm{\omega}})
to
(\bm{\omega},\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}^{n})
as in
Remark

6.23
.
Then, for any
(\bm{\omega},\widetilde{\bm{\omega}})\in\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}\times\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}
, we have
\Phi^{n}(\bm{\omega},\widetilde{\bm{\omega}})\to(\bm{\omega},\widetilde{\bm{\omega}})
and
\widetilde{\bm{\lambda}}^{n}\rightharpoonup\bm{\lambda}
as
n\to\infty
. By
Propositions

6.20
and
6.21
,

(6.18)

\lim_{n\to\infty}C_{n}(\Phi^{n}(\bm{\omega},\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}))=C_{\mathcal{H}}(\bm{\omega},\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}).

Moreover, by
Lemma

6.18
,
we have
C_{n}(\bm{\omega},\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}^{n})\leq\|h\|_{\mathcal{H}}=C_{\mathcal{H}}(\bm{\omega},\widetilde{\bm{\omega}})
.
Using Fatou’s lemma with integrable upper bound
C_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}(\bm{\omega},\widetilde{\bm{\omega}})
gives

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\int_{\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}\times\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}}C_{n}^{2}(\bm{\omega},\widetilde{\bm{\omega}})\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{\bm{\lambda}}^{n}=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\int_{\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}\times\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}}C_{n}^{2}(\Phi^{n}(\bm{\omega},\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}))\,\mathrm{d}\bm{\lambda}\leq\int_{\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}\times\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}}\limsup_{n\to\infty}C_{n}^{2}(\Phi^{n}(\bm{\omega},\widetilde{\bm{\omega}}))\,\mathrm{d}\bm{\lambda}.

The assertion now follows from (
6.18
).
∎

Corollary 6.27
.

On
\mathcal{P}(\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}\times\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}})
equipped with the weak topology, we have the
\Gamma
-convergence
\mathrm{I}_{n}\xrightarrow{\Gamma}\mathrm{I}_{\infty}
.

The following theorem is a version of the fundamental theorem of
\Gamma
-convergence in the present case.

Theorem 6.28
.

Let
\bm{\eta}\in\mathcal{P}(\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}})
. Then
\mathrm{T}_{C_{n},2}(\bm{\eta},\cdot)\xrightarrow{\Gamma}\mathrm{T}_{C_{\mathcal{H}},2}(\bm{\eta},\cdot)
with respect to the weak topology on
\mathcal{P}(\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}})
. That is

(i)

For any
\bm{\nu}\in\mathcal{P}(\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}})
and any
(\bm{\nu}^{n})\subset\mathcal{P}(\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}})
such that
\bm{\nu}^{n}\rightharpoonup\bm{\nu}
,
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathrm{T}_{C_{n},2}(\bm{\eta},\bm{\nu}^{n})\geq\mathrm{T}_{C_{\mathcal{H}},2}(\bm{\eta},\bm{\nu})
; and

(ii)

For any
\bm{\nu}\in\mathcal{P}(\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}})
, there exists a sequence
(\widetilde{\bm{\nu}}^{n})\subset\mathcal{P}(\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}})
such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathrm{T}_{C_{n},2}(\bm{\eta},\widetilde{\bm{\nu}}^{n})=\mathrm{T}_{C_{\mathcal{H}},2}(\bm{\eta},\bm{\nu})
.

Proof.

(i) Let
\bm{\nu}\in\mathcal{P}(\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}})
and
(\bm{\nu}^{n})\subset\mathcal{P}(\Omega_{\mathbb{G}})
such that
\bm{\nu}^{n}\rightharpoonup\bm{\nu}
, and let
(\bm{\lambda}^{n})\subset\mathcal{P}(\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}\times\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}})
be a sequence of optimal transport plans for
\mathrm{T}_{C_{n},2}(\bm{\eta},\widetilde{\bm{\nu}}^{n})
. We can assume that
\bm{\lambda}^{n}
converges weakly to a limit
\bm{\lambda}\in\mathcal{P}(\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}\times\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}})
since its marginals are tight by Prokhorov’s theorem; see
[
AGS08
, Lemma 5.2.2]
.
The limit
\bm{\lambda}
has marginals
\bm{\eta}
and
\bm{\nu}
and is hence an admissible transport plan for
\mathrm{T}_{C_{\mathcal{H}},2}(\bm{\eta},\bm{\nu})
. Using Proposition
6.24
, we get the chain of inequalities

\mathrm{T}_{C_{\mathcal{H}},2}(\bm{\eta},\bm{\nu})\leq\int_{\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}\times\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}}C_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}(\bm{\omega},\overline{\bm{\omega}})\,\mathrm{d}\bm{\lambda}\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int_{\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}\times\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}}C_{n}^{2}(\bm{\omega},\overline{\bm{\omega}})\,\mathrm{d}\bm{\lambda}^{n}=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathrm{T}_{C_{n},2}(\bm{\eta},\widetilde{\bm{\nu}}^{n}).

(ii) Now let
\bm{\lambda}\in\mathcal{P}(\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}\times\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}})
be an optimal transport plan for
\mathrm{T}_{C_{\mathcal{H}},2}(\bm{\eta},\bm{\nu})
(note that
C_{\mathcal{H}}
is lower semi-continuous; see
Lemma

2.20
).
Let the sequence of transport plans
(\widetilde{\bm{\lambda}}^{n})\subset\mathcal{P}(\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}\times\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}})
be given as in
Proposition

6.26
, and define
\widetilde{\bm{\nu}}^{n}
as the second marginal of
\widetilde{\bm{\lambda}}^{n}
, for
n\in\mathbb{N}
. The first marginal of
\widetilde{\bm{\lambda}}^{n}
is fixed to
\bm{\eta}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Thus
\widetilde{\bm{\nu}}^{n}\rightharpoonup\bm{\nu}
. Moreover, by
Proposition

6.26
and the optimality of
\bm{\lambda}
,

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\int_{\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}\times\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}}C_{n}^{2}(\bm{\omega},\overline{\bm{\omega}})\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{\bm{\lambda}}^{n}\leq\mathrm{T}_{C_{\mathcal{H}},2}^{2}(\bm{\eta},\bm{\nu}).

On the other hand, if
(\overline{\bm{\lambda}}^{n})\subset\mathcal{P}(\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}\times\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}})
is a sequence of
optimal transport plans for
\mathrm{T}_{C_{n},2}(\bm{\eta},\widetilde{\bm{\nu}}^{n})
, we can assume that
\overline{\bm{\lambda}}^{n}\rightharpoonup\overline{\bm{\lambda}}
, where the limit

\overline{\bm{\lambda}}
has marginals
\bm{\eta}
and
\bm{\nu}
.
By Proposition
6.24
, we get

\mathrm{T}_{C_{\mathcal{H}},2}^{2}(\bm{\eta},\bm{\nu})\leq\int_{\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}\times\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}}}C_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}(\bm{\omega},\overline{\bm{\omega}})\,\mathrm{d}\overline{\bm{\lambda}}\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathrm{T}_{C_{n},2}^{2}(\bm{\eta},\widetilde{\bm{\nu}}^{n}).

Combining both estimates proves the claim.
∎

Remark 6.29
.

In general, we cannot rule out that there exists a sequence
\bm{\nu}^{n}
converging to some limit
\bm{\nu}
such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathrm{T}_{C_{n},2}(\bm{\eta},\bm{\nu}^{n})>\mathrm{T}_{C_{\mathcal{H}},2}(\bm{\eta},\bm{\nu})
. The crucial point in Theorem
6.28
is that
the sequence
\widetilde{\bm{\nu}}^{n}
is a special sequence
constructed via the push-forward of the recovery map

\Phi^{n}
. It is an interesting question whether the following stronger result holds: Let
\delta_{\Pi(\bm{\eta},\bm{\nu}^{n})}
denote the convex indicator function (taking values in
\{0,\infty\}
) for the set of admissible plans, i.e.
\delta_{\Pi(\bm{\eta},\bm{\nu}^{n})}(\bm{\lambda})=0
if and only if
\bm{\lambda}\in\Pi(\bm{\eta},\bm{\nu}^{n})
,
and let
\bm{\nu}\in\mathcal{P}(\bm{\Omega})
,
(\bm{\nu}^{n})\subset\mathcal{P}(\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{G}})
such that
\bm{\nu}^{n}\rightharpoonup\bm{\nu}
and
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}H(\bm{\nu}^{n}\|\bm{\eta})<\infty
. Do we have the
\Gamma
-convergence
\mathrm{I}_{n}+\delta_{\Pi(\bm{\eta},\bm{\nu}^{n})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Gamma}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathrm{I}_{\infty}+\delta_{\Pi(\bm{\eta},\bm{\nu})}
?
This property would imply that
\mathrm{T}_{C_{n},2}(\bm{\eta},\bm{\nu}^{n})\to\mathrm{T}_{C_{\mathcal{H}},2}(\bm{\eta},\bm{\nu})
for
every
converging sequence
\bm{\nu}^{n}
with finite relative entropy.

7.
Beyond step-
2
Carnot groups

Parts of this work are valid in the generality of general Carnot groups (see, e.g.
[
BLU07
]
). However, Carnot groups for which the log-Sobolev inequality is known are the Heisenberg group and more general H-type groups, which are examples of step-
2
Carnot groups, as discussed in
Section

2.2
. This explains our focus on step-
2
. Nevertheless,
Theorem

5.1
holds for general Carnot groups with no restriction on the step of the group, and the proof remains unchanged, given the appropriate definitions. Similarly,
Lemma

6.1
,
Proposition

6.2
, and
Lemma

6.3
carry over without change to the general Carnot group setting. Finally,
Theorem

6.6
also holds for
\mathbb{G}=\mathbb{F}^{d_{1},N}
, i.e. for step-
N
free Carnot groups, under additional regularity assumptions for
N>2
. On the space of
p
-variation paths, for any
p
such that the shift by an absolutely continuous path is well defined, the proof of
Theorem

6.6
remains valid; see also
Remark

2.17
.

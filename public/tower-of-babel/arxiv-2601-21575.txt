On minimal graphs for hamiltonian groups and their fixing set
Kirti Sahu, Ranjit Mehatari

Abstract
A finite non-abelian group H H is hamiltonian if all of its subgroups are normal. We compute the minimal orders of graphs having a hamiltonian group as their automorphism group. The fixing number of a graph Γ \Gamma is the minimum cardinality of a subset S S of V ⁡ ( Γ ) V(\Gamma) such that the stabilizer of S S is trivial. For a given finite group G G , the fixing set is defined as the set comprising all possible fixing numbers of graphs having group G G as their automorphism groups. We determine the fixing sets corresponding to finite hamiltonian groups.



AMS Subject Classification (2020):
05C25.

Keywords:
Automorphism group, hamiltonian group, vertex-minimal graph, fixing number, fixing set.

Introduction

Suppose
\Gamma
be a simple finite graph having vertex set
V(\Gamma)
and edge set
E(\Gamma)
. Define an automorphism of graph

is isomorphic to

is a cubic graph
[
7

then the solution to the above question is also possible. In 1957, Sabidussi
[
26

proved the question with some extra properties of graphs, such as prescribed regularity, prescribed chromatic number, or vertex-connectivity. However, for a given group
G
, we have infinite number of graphs having group

as their full automorphism group. Considering

[

that if

,

, and

, then

. The exact value of

for various groups has been determined completely: finite abelian groups
[
1
]
, symmetric groups
[
25
]
, alternating groups

for degree

[

]
, hyperoctahedral groups
[

]
, dihedral groups
[

,

,

,

[

A hamiltonian group is the direct product of a quaternion group with an abelian group in which every element is of finite odd order and an abelian group of exponent two.

, for

Let

[

. The concept of fixing the number of graphs has been extended to finite groups by Gibbons and Laison
[

]
. For a finite group

[

[

[

Suppose for a finite abelian group

In

Suppose

2
Preliminaries

This section presents several key concepts and preliminary results that serve as essential tools for proving the main results of this paper. Throughout this paper, all graphs and grops considered are assumed to be finite, unless explicitly stated otherwise.

This paper is devoted to the computation of
\alpha(H)
. Throughout the work, we consider
H
as a permutation group. Let
G
be a permutation group acting on a set
X
with
n
symbols, and
x\in X
. Then the set
O(x)=\{g(x):g\in G\}
is defined as the orbit of
x
.

Let
\Gamma
be a graph with the vertex set
V(\Gamma)
and the edge set
E(\Gamma)
. If
G
is a subgroup of the symmetric group
S_{V(\Gamma)}
, then the Edge Orbit of an edge
[v_{1},v_{2}]\in E(\Gamma)
is defined as

O\{v_{1},v_{2}\}=\Big\{[g(v_{1}),g(v_{2})]:g\in G\Big\}.

Also, we define the support of a permutation
g\in G
as
\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}(g)=\{x\in X:g(x)\neq x\}
.

The quaternion group
Q_{8}
is defined as
Q_{8}=\langle\sigma,\tau\mid\sigma^{4}=\tau^{4}=1,\sigma^{2}=\tau^{2},\sigma\tau=\tau\sigma^{-1}\rangle
. The following lemma describes the subgroup representation of quaternion group
Q_{8}
in symmetric group, which is as follows.

Lemma 2.1
.

For any integer
k<8
, the quaternion group
Q_{8}
cannot be embedded as a subgroup of the symmetric group
S_{k}
.

Proof.

Since
Q_{8}
has elements of order
4
, it cannot be embedded into symmetric group
S_{k}
with
k\leq 3
. Suppose
Q_{8}
is isomorphic to a subgroup of
S_{k}
where
4\leq k\leq 7
. Then the cycle decomposition of a
4
-order element in
S_{k}
is either a
4
-cycle or a product of a
4
-cycle and a
2
-cycle. Let the generators of
Q_{8}
be
\sigma
and
\tau
, with the cycle decomposition of
\sigma
as
(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4})\sigma_{1}
, where
\sigma_{1}
is a cycle of length at most
2
. The group
Q_{8}
has 6 elements of order 4 and a unique involution. Thus,
\sigma^{2}=\tau^{2}=(a_{1},a_{3})(a_{2},a_{4})\sigma_{1}^{2}
, therefore
\tau=(a_{1},a_{i},a_{3},a_{j})
for some
i,j\in\{2,4\}
, which is a contradiction.
∎

Now, using Cayley’s theorem, we conclude that 8 is the smallest positive integer such that
Q_{8}
is isomorphic to a subgroup of
S_{8}
; and one can get the generators of
Q_{8}
in
S_{8}
as
\sigma=(1,2,3,4)(5,6,7,8)
,
\tau=(1,7,3,5)(2,6,4,8)
.

For a finite group
G
, let
\mu(G)
denote the smallest positive integer
n
for which
G
is isomorphic to a subgroup of
S_{n}
. In 1971, Johnson
[
17
]
first considered the problem: what is the minimal permutation representation of a finite group? Later, Wright
[
28
]
considered the problem for direct products.

Now for any finite group
G
, one can observe that
\mu(G)\leq\alpha(G)
, where the equality do not hold in general. In fact, there are permutation groups that are not an automorphism group of some graph, for example the subgroup
\{1,(12)(34),(13)(24),(14)(23)\}
of
S_{4}
is not an automorphism group of any graph. Also for the quaternion group
Q_{8}
, we have

8=\mu(Q_{8})<\alpha(Q_{8})=16.

Using, Theorem of
[
28
]
, we have the following conclusions

•

\mu(C_{2}^{k})=2k
.

•

\mu(Q_{8}\times C_{2}^{k})=8+2k
.

Thus, for the elementary abelian 2-group
C_{2}^{k}
, we have
\mu(C_{2}^{k})=\alpha(C_{2}^{k})=2k
.

Let
G
be a group and
S
be the generating set of
G
. With the vertex set
G
, the Cayley digraph
C(G,S)
is defined as the directed and edge-labelled multigraph such that there is a directed edge from group element
g_{1}
to
g_{2}
labelled with the generator
s\in S
if and only if
sg_{1}=g_{2}
. Now define the Frucht Graph
F(G,S)
from
C(G,S)
by replacing each directed and labelled edge with a graph gadget such that
\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}(F(G,S))=\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}(C(G,S))
. The Frucht graph is discussed in detail in
[
23
]
. The following lemma follows from the exposition in
[
23
]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
G
be a group, and
S
be its generating set.
Then
\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}(C(G,S))=G
and
\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}(F(G,S))=G
. Furthermore, the fixing number of
C(G,S)
or
F(G,S)
is
1
.

The above lemma ensures that for every vertex
v
of a Frucht graph or a Cayley digraph,
\mathop{\mathrm{stab}}(v)
is trivial. Consequently, for a finite non-trivial group
G
,
1\in\mathop{\mathrm{fix}}(G)
.

Value of
\alpha(H)

This section is devoted to computing the value of
\alpha(H)
. From the discussions in the previous section, graph having automorphism group isomorphic to the hamiltonian group
H\cong Q_{8}\times A
must have at least
8+\mu(A)
vertices. In the following theorem, we find
\alpha(H)
, where
H
is a hamiltonian 2-group.

Figure 1:
Vertex-minimal graphs with
\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\Gamma_{1}=Q_{8}
and
\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\Gamma_{2}=C_{2}^{4}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
k
be any positive integer. Then
\alpha(Q_{8}\times C_{2}^{k})=16+2k
.

Proof.

Let
H=Q_{8}\times C_{2}^{k}
. First we observe that
\alpha(H)\geq\mu(H)=8+2k
. Again, since
\alpha(Q_{8})=16
and
\alpha(C_{2}^{k})=2k
, it follows that
8+2k\leq\alpha(H)\leq 16+2k
. We will show that there does not exist any graph with vertex less than
16+2k
with hamiltonian
2
-group symmetry. It is sufficient to show that
\alpha(H)\neq 8+2k,10+2k,12+2k,14+2k
.

First, if possible, let
\Gamma_{1}
be a graph with
8+2k
vertices with the automorphism group
H
. Without loss of generality we take

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\Gamma_{1}

\displaystyle=\langle\sigma,\tau,\delta_{1},\delta_{2},\ldots,\delta_{k}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3},u_{4})(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},v_{4}),(u_{1},v_{3},u_{3},v_{1})(u_{2},v_{2},u_{4},v_{4}),(a_{11},a_{12}),\ldots,(a_{k1},a_{k2})\rangle.

Then this group has
2(2^{k}-1)+1
elements involutions. Take
S
be the set of all involutions. We claim that
\gamma=(u_{1},u_{3})(u_{2},u_{4})(a_{11},a_{12})(a_{21},a_{22})\ldots(a_{k1},a_{k2})
is also an element of
\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\Gamma_{1}\setminus S
, which is absurd.

For
\Gamma_{1}
, we have the following possible edge orbits:

\displaystyle\{[u_{i},u_{j}]\},\ \{[u_{i},v_{j}]\},\ \{[v_{i},v_{j}]\},

\displaystyle\{[u_{i},a_{l1}]\mid\forall\ l=1,2,\ldots,k\},\ \{[u_{i},a_{l2}]\mid\forall\ l=1,2,\ldots,k\},

\displaystyle\{[v_{i},a_{l1}]\mid\forall\ l=1,2,\ldots,k\},\ \{[v_{i},a_{l2}]\mid\forall\ l=1,2,\ldots,k\},

\displaystyle\{[a_{l1},a_{m2}]\mid\forall\ l,m=1,2,\ldots,k\}.

Now, observe that

\displaystyle\gamma([u_{i},u_{j}])=\sigma^{2}([u_{i},u_{j}]),

\displaystyle\gamma([u_{i},v_{j}])=\sigma^{i+j}\tau([u_{i},v_{j}]),

\displaystyle\gamma([u_{i},a_{l1}])=\sigma^{2}\delta_{1}\delta_{2}\ldots\delta_{k}([u_{i},a_{l1}])=\gamma([u_{i},a_{l2}]),

\displaystyle\gamma([v_{i},v_{j}])=e,

\displaystyle\gamma([v_{i},a_{l1}])=\delta_{1}\delta_{2}\ldots\delta_{k}([v_{i},a_{l1}])=\gamma([v_{i},a_{l2}]),

\displaystyle\gamma([a_{l1},a_{m2}])=\delta_{1}\delta_{2}\ldots\delta_{k}([a_{l1},a_{m2}]).

From the above calculations it follows that, for each edge
[u,v]\in E(\Gamma_{1})
, an automorphism
\beta
of
\Gamma_{1}
exists such that
\gamma([u,v])=\beta([u,v])
. Which implies
\gamma\in\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\Gamma_{1}
. Therefore,
\alpha(H)\neq 8+2k
.

Next we suppose there exist a graph
\Gamma_{2}
with
\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\Gamma_{2}=Q_{8}\times C_{2}^{k}
with
8+2k+2
number of vertices. Two cases arise:

(a)

If
\sigma=(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3},u_{4})(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},v_{4})(b_{1},b_{2})
and
\delta_{l}=(a_{l1},a_{l2})
for each
l\in\{1,2,\ldots,k\}
.
We claim that
\gamma=(u_{1},u_{3})(u_{2},u_{4})(a_{11},a_{12})\ldots(a_{k1},a_{k2})\in\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\Gamma_{2}\setminus S
, which is a contradiction.

We have following possible edge orbits:

\displaystyle\{[u_{i},u_{j}]\},\ \{[u_{i},v_{j}]\},\ \{[v_{i},v_{j}]\},

\displaystyle\{[u_{i},b_{1}]\},\ \{[v_{i},b_{1}]\},\ \{[b_{1},b_{2}]\},

\displaystyle\{[u_{i},a_{l1}],\forall\quad l=1,2,\ldots,k\},\ \{[u_{i},a_{l2}],\forall\quad l=1,2,\ldots,k\},

\displaystyle\{[v_{i},a_{l1}],\forall\quad l=1,2,\ldots,k\},\ \{[v_{i},a_{l2}],\forall\quad l=1,2,\ldots,k\},

\displaystyle\{[a_{l1},a_{m2}],\forall\quad l,m=1,2,\ldots,k\},\ \{[b_{1},a_{l1}],\forall\quad l=1,2,\ldots,k\},

\displaystyle\{[b_{1},a_{l2}],\forall\quad l=1,2,\ldots,k\}.

Observe that

\displaystyle\gamma([u_{i},u_{j}])=\sigma^{2}([u_{i},u_{j}])=\gamma([u_{i},b_{1}])=\gamma([v_{i},b_{1}]),

\displaystyle\gamma([u_{i},v_{j}])=\sigma^{i+j}\tau([u_{i},v_{j}]),

\displaystyle\gamma([u_{i},a_{l1}])=\sigma^{2}\delta_{1}\delta_{2}\ldots\delta_{k}([u_{i},a_{l1}])=\gamma([u_{i},a_{l2}]),

\displaystyle\gamma([v_{i},v_{j}])=e=\gamma([b_{1},b_{2}]),

\displaystyle\gamma([v_{i},a_{l1}])=\delta_{1}\delta_{2}\ldots\delta_{k}([v_{i},a_{l1}])=\gamma([v_{i},a_{l2}]),

\displaystyle\gamma([a_{l1},a_{m2}])=\delta_{1}\delta_{2}\ldots\delta_{k}([a_{l1},a_{m2}])=\gamma([b_{1},a_{l1}])=\gamma([b_{1},a_{l2}]).

Hence
\gamma\in\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\Gamma_{2}\setminus S
, which is a contradiction.

(b)

If
\sigma=(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3},u_{4})(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},v_{4})
and
\delta_{1}=(a_{11},a_{12})(b_{1},b_{2})
and for each
l\in\{2,\ldots,k\}

\delta_{l}=(a_{l1},a_{l2})
. Then in a similar way as above, we observe that

\gamma=(u_{1},u_{3})(u_{2},u_{4})(a_{11},a_{12})\ldots(a_{k1},a_{k2})(b_{1},b_{2})\in\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\Gamma_{2}\setminus S
, a contradiction.

In similar ways, we observe that there does not exist any graph that has automorphism group isomorphic to
Q_{8}\times C_{2}^{k}
with an
8+2k+4
or
8+2k+6
number of vertices.
∎

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
H
be a hamiltonian group of the form
Q_{8}\times A
, where
A
is some periodic abelian group that has no element of order
4
. Then
\alpha(Q_{8}\times A)=16+\alpha(A)
.

Proof.

From the disjoint union of vertex minimal graphs of
Q_{8}
and
A
, we have
\alpha(H)\leq 16+\alpha(A)
. Suppose
Q_{8}
be generated by
\sigma
and
\tau
. Let
1\neq a\in H
be the generator of
A
such that
a\notin\langle\sigma,\tau\rangle
. Our claim is that with less than
16+\alpha(A)
number of vertices, there exist no graph with
H
-symmetry, that is,
\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}(\sigma)\cap\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}(a)=\phi
.

Towards a contradiction, suppose there exists a graph
\Gamma
such that
\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}(\Gamma)\cong Q_{8}\times A
with
|V(\Gamma)|<16+\alpha(A)
. Two cases arise here:

Case I:
If order of element
a
is even. Then, clearly it follows from Theorem
3.1
it follows that
\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}(\sigma)\cap\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}(a)=\phi
.

Case II:
If order of element
a
is odd. Let
A=\mathbb{Z}_{p_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p_{2}^{\alpha_{2}}}\times\ldots\times\mathbb{Z}_{p_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}}
be an abelian group of odd order, where
p_{i}
’s are odd and
\alpha_{i}\geq 1
for all
i=1,2,\ldots,n
. Let
\Gamma_{1}
be a vertex-minimal graph of

. Then the cycle decomposition of the generators of
\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\Gamma_{1}
will follow the structure of the vertex- minimal graph. Suppose
\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}(a)\cap\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}(\sigma)\neq\phi
. Then, we have
\sigma^{2}a\neq a\sigma^{2}
, a contradiction.
∎

For
G_{1}=Q_{8}
and an abelian group
G_{2}
without any element of order

, we have proved that

\alpha(G_{1}\times G_{2})=\alpha(G_{1})+\alpha(G_{2}).

However, the above equality does not hold for arbitrary non-trivial groups

and

. For example (see
[

]
for details),
\alpha(C_{3}\times C_{5})=\alpha(C_{3})+\alpha(C_{5})-3
. So the following obvious problem arises:

Problem 3.3
.

Classify all non-trivial finite groups
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
, such that
\alpha(G_{1}\times G_{2})=\alpha(G_{1})+\alpha(G_{2}).

4
Fixing set of hamiltonian group

In this section, we determine
\mathop{\mathrm{fix}}(H)
. Let
A
be a finite abelian group with its unique factorization. Then elementary divisors of
A
are defined as the set of all prime power orders of cyclic groups.

Graphs with identity automorphism

In the subsequent theorem, we demonstrate a natural extension of Theorem
1.2
. We use a similar technique here. The theorem also provides a partial solution to Problem
1.4
. For a positive integer
k
, let
I_{k}
denote the set
\{1,2,\ldots,k\}
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
be two finite groups such that
\mathop{\mathrm{fix}}(G_{1})=\{1\}
and
\mathop{\mathrm{fix}}(G_{2})=I_{d}
, then

\mathop{\mathrm{fix}}(G_{1}\times G_{2})=I_{d+1}.

Proof.

Let
G=G_{1}\times G_{2}
. By Lemma
1.3
and
2.2
, it follows that

I_{d+1}\subseteq\mathop{\mathrm{fix}}(G_{1}\times G_{2}).

Consider a finite graph
\Gamma
having
\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\Gamma=G_{1}\times G_{2}
. Let us consider the subgroup
G_{1}
of
G
. Since
\mathop{\mathrm{fix}}(G_{1})=\{1\}
, there exist a vertex
v_{1}
such that
G_{1}
acts regularly on
O(v_{1})
.

Consider the vertex
v_{1}
and let
\Gamma_{1}
be the connected component of
\Gamma
that contains
v_{1}
. Then either
\Gamma_{1}
is a tree or it contains a cycle. If
\Gamma_{1}
is a tree, then the graph
\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}}
is constructed by
\Gamma
by joining the graph
R_{|\Gamma|}
as illustrated in Figure 2 to
\Gamma
. If
\Gamma_{1}
is not a tree, then we construct
\Gamma^{\prime}
from
\Gamma
by attaching the graph
T_{|\Gamma|}
to the vertex
v_{1}
belogs to
\Gamma
. Let us denote the subgraph
R_{|\Gamma|}
or
T_{|\Gamma|}
in
\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}}
by
\Gamma_{2}
. Our claim is that
\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}(\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}})
is a subgroup of
G_{2}
. To prove our claim, we first show that
\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}}
has no additional automorphism that
\Gamma
does not. If possible, let
\tau
be an automorphism of
\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}}
that is not an automorphism of
\Gamma
. Then there must exist a vertex
v
in
\Gamma_{2}
such that
\tau(v)\neq v
. Since
\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\Gamma_{2}=\{1\}
, every vertex in
\Gamma_{2}
must be in
\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}(\tau)
. Again, since
\Gamma^{\prime}
contains more vertices than
\Gamma
, the image of a vertex of
\Gamma_{2}
under the permutation
\tau
must be another vertex of
\Gamma_{2}
. Therefore
\tau
is completely determined as a permutation on the vertices
\Gamma_{1}\cup\Gamma_{2}
. In fact,
\tau
is a reflection of
\Gamma_{1}\cup\Gamma_{2}
about some vertex of
\Gamma_{2}
. Which seems impossible, since we have constructed
\Gamma^{\prime}
in a way that
\Gamma_{2}
contains a cycle if and only if
\Gamma_{1}
does not.

On the other hand, we can not extend any automorphism of
\Gamma
as an automorphism of
\Gamma^{\prime}
, as in the graph
\Gamma^{\prime}
, the vertex
v_{1}
has a larger degree than all the vertices of
O(v_{1})
; therefore,
v_{1}
cannot be mapped to any vertex of
O(v_{1})
. And this proves our claim.

Hence, the fixing number of
\Gamma^{\prime}
is bounded above by
d
. Let
S
be a fixing set of
\Gamma^{\prime}
where
|S|\in I_{d}
. Let
S^{\prime}=S\cup\{v_{1}\}
. Then
|S^{\prime}|\in I_{d+1}
and the set
S^{\prime}
serves as a fixing set of
\Gamma
. Therefore
\mathop{\mathrm{fix}}(G)=\{1,2,\ldots,d+1\}
. ∎

]
, Christina and Lauderdale proved that
\mathop{\mathrm{fix}}(Q_{8})=\{1\}
. Thus, by using Theorem
1.2
, we obtain the following corollary that describes fixing set of a hamiltonian group.

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
H
be a hamiltonian group of the form
Q_{8}\times A
, where
A
is periodic abelian group that has no element of order
4
. Let
d
denote the number of elementary divisors of

. Then the set
\mathop{\mathrm{fix}}(H)=\{1,2,\ldots,d+1\}
.

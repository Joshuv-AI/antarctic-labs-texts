Research on location optimization and application of the gob-side entry retaining by roof cutting in close-distance coal seams
Authors: Qiang Fu, Jun Yang, Yuxuan Liu, Chuanjiu Zhang, Yubing Gao, Hainan Gao, Qing Ma
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractCompared to traditional long-wall mining, the gob-side entry retaining by roof-cutting (GERRC) technology reduces the amount of entry excavation by 50%, and recovers the previously wasted coal pillar resources, which has obvious superiority in mining efficiency and economic benefits. In response to the problem of reasonable entry location of the GERRC in close-distance coal seams, this paper takes the 9105 Working face in Longmenta Coal Mine as the engineering background and conducts an in-depth study by using methods such as theoretical analysis, numerical simulation and field tests. Firstly, the structural characteristics of the overburden rock for GERRC in the close-distance coal seam are analyzed, based on which a method for determining the reasonable location of the GERRC in close-distance coal seams is proposed; secondly, a mechanical model for load transfer and rock mass damage of the upper coal seam floor was established to analyze the floor damage characteristics and stress distribution law, forming the theoretical analysis of the reasonable entry location; Finally, the optimal entry location (22.5 m from the center of the upper remaining coal pillar) was used for field test of GERRC at the 9105 Working face of Longmenta Coal Mine. It has achieved good on-site application results.Keywords: Close-distance coal seams, The gob-side entry retaining by roof-cutting, Entry position, Surrounding rock control, Engineering applicationSubject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering, Environmental sciences, Solid Earth sciences

Introduction

As the foundation of modern industrialization construction, coal resources occupy a dominant position in the energy structure1–3. At present, China’s coal mining mainly adopts the long-wall mining method, which belongs to the European and American technology system and originated in England in 17064,5. When this method is used, two entries should be excavated and a protective coal pillar should be left to mine a Working face (see Fig. 1a). On the one hand, the remaining coal pillars cause waste of resources; on the other hand, the internal stress concentration is easy to lead to instability and failure of the coal pillars, bringing great difficulties to the control of the surrounding rock of adjacent gob-side entry6–8. In order to solve the above problems, He et al.9 proposed the gob-side entry retaining by roof-cutting technology (see Fig. 1b), which is based on the structural mechanics model of “roof cutting short cantilever beam”. By using bidirectional energy gathering blasting roof cutting, constant resistance anchor support, and gangue prevent structure, the gob-side entry of this Working face is retained for adjacent Working faces to continue using (see Fig. 1c)10–12. The gob-side entry retaining by roof-cutting technology (GERRC) reduces the entry excavation rate by 50%, eliminates the need for coal pillars, and achieves Y-shaped ventilation, which has obvious superiority in mining efficiency and economic benefits13,14.

The reform of the coal mining method.

The close-distance coal seams usually refer to the coal seams in which the upper and lower coal seams have a significant mutual influence during the mining of multiple coal seams. Such coal seams occupy an important position in the global coal resource reserves and are widely distributed15,16. Due to the influence of mining activities in the upper coal seam, the equilibrium stress field of the rock mass at the floor of the upper coal seam is reconstructed, presenting a non-uniform stress distribution form. In addition, within a certain range, the floor rock mass undergoes plastic failure under the influence of stress concentration, thereby losing its self-bearing capacity. The roof of the close-distance lower coal seam is the floor of the upper coal seam. During the excavation and operation of the lower coal seam entry, the stress environment and elastic–plastic state of the roof directly determine the stability of the surrounding rock of the entry17. Therefore, coal mining enterprises generally change the position of the entry in the lower coal seam to avoid the adverse effects of the mining dynamic stress in the upper coal seam.

Regarding the reasonable layout of entries in close-distance coal seams, relevant scholars have done a lot of research. Liu et al.18 analyzed the stress distribution under the coal pillar in close-distance coal seams, finding the decrease in stress below the goaf and the increase in stress below the coal pillar, and proposing that the entries in lower coal seams should be arranged outside the stress diffusion angle to improve stability. Kang et al.19 clarified the stress distribution law of the coal pillar floor, and proposed a joint formula based on stress and its change rate to determine the reasonable location of the entries in the lower coal seam. Wang et al.20 established a theoretical model for stress distribution in multiple goafs and coal pillars to analyze the stress distribution and transmission mechanism of the floor, and proposed reasonable layout schemes for entries in the lower coal seam under different interlayer spacing conditions. Yang et al.21 clarified the stress distribution characteristics of the floor below the coal pillar using theoretical analysis and numerical calculation methods, and optimized the entry layout and support parameters, which was successfully applied in Jinggonger Coal Mine. Focusing on the stability of entries in ultra close-distance coal seams, Wu et al.22 established a numerical calculation model with different offset distances, and proposed the optimal entry layout scheme based on numerical simulation results and coal recovery rate.

According to the research on the reasonable entry location in close-distance coal seams, there is relatively little research on the use of GERRC for lower coal seam mining . Furthermore, most scholars believe that areas with lower stress levels should be selected as reasonable locations for entry, lacking an analysis of the elastoplastic state of the rock mass in the floor. Based on this, by taking the 9105 Working face in close-distance coal seams of Longmenta Coal Mine as the engineering background, a method for determining the reasonable entry location of GERRC was proposed in this paper based on the structural characteristics of the overburden rock of GERRC in close-distance coal seams, and the reasonable entry location of GERRC in the 9105 Working face was determined through theoretical calculations. Finally, field tests were conducted to verify the rationality and effectiveness of this method.

Engineering overview

The main mineable coal seams of Longmenta Coal Mine are 8#, 9#, and 10# coal seams, of which 8# coal seam has been fully mined, leaving a 15 m wide protective coal pillar. The coal seam mined in 9015 Working face is 9# coal seam, with a thickness of 1.96 m, an average burial depth of 282 m, a dip angle of 3°–7°, belonging to near horizontal coal seam, without special geological structure. The positional relationship of coal seams is shown in Fig. 2, the 9# coal seam is 9.62 m and 5.17 m away from the 8# (interlayer rock mass is 8.26 m mudstone and 1.28 m siltstone) and 10# (interlayer rock mass is 5.17 m mudstone) coal seams respectively. The transport entry in the 9015 Working face has been excavated, and it is now planned to carry out GERRC for its track entry. The track entry of the 9103 Working face (adjacent to the 9105 Working face) and the 8# coal seam gob-side entry are arranged in an overlapping manner. Due to the influence of the mining dynamic stress in the upper coal seam mining area, during the mining period of the 9103 Working face, intense mine pressure was manifested in the entry, and the surrounding rock deformation was severe, which had a significant impact on the normal mining progress of the working face. Therefore, in order to reduce the influence of mining dynamic stress on the close-distance coal seams, it is very necessary to carry out coal pillar-free mining on the 9105 Working face. Meanwhile, in order to protect the stability of the surrounding rock of the entry during the mining process, it is necessary to determine the reasonable position for GERRC of the 9105 Working face.

“Voussoir beam” structure of the upper coal seam

The morphology of roof collapse after the mining of 8# coal seam is shown in Fig. 4, after the close-distance upper coal seams are mined, the immediate roof collapses , and the basic roof fractures into rock blocks, which are connected by the articulation and transmit forces. The fractured rock blocks at the edge of the goaf rotate and sink into the goaf, pivoting around the coal pillar roof. They stabilize under the combined forces of overburden pressure, support from the coal pillar roof, and resistance provided by the goaf gangue, ultimately forming a stable “voussoir beam” structure24,25. In contrast, the fractured rock blocks in the middle of the goaf sink as a whole and cannot form a similar load-bearing structure. Through the analysis of the stress characteristics of the “voussoir beam” structure, the overburden layer pressure in this area is transmitted to the structure, which is mainly supported by the coal pillars, resulting a relatively small supporting stress of the floor beneath it and forming a certain range of pressure relief zone. In the middle of the goaf, due to the lack of coal pillar support, the overburden layer pressure is completely borne by the goaf gangue, and the floor support stress is close to the protolith stress level. In addition, the remaining coal pillars need to support the overburden layers and the “voussoir beam” structure, which significantly increases the support stress of the floor below, forming a clear stress concentration zone.

“Voussoir beam” structure of the upper coal seam.

“Roof cutting short cantilever beam” structure of the lower coal seam

The morphology of roof collapse after the mining of 9# coal seam is shown in Fig. 5, the adoption of the GERCC technology for the mining of close-distance lower coal seams actively increases the range of roof fall zone through directional roof-cutting, makes full use of the fragmentation and expansion characteristics of rocks, provides support for the interlayer basic roof, and reduces the sinking amount of fractured rock blocks, which avoids large-scale secondary movements of the upper seam stope structure, protecting the stability of the “voussoir beam” structure. In addition, directional roof-cutting cuts off the force transmission path between the goaf and the entry roof, forming a “roof cutting short cantilever beam” structure with one end open and the other end fixed, which maintains balance under the combined action of overburden layer pressure, entry support force, and solid coal support force.

Method for determining the reasonable entry location of GERRC

Layout principle of reasonable entry location for GERRC

As shown in the overburden structure of the close-distance coal seams using the GERRC method, the floor support stress can be divided horizontally into several zones: a stress concentration zone beneath the coal pillar, a pressure relief zone at the goaf edge, and a protolith stress zone in the central part of the goaf. This support stress transmits deeply into the floor, leading to a redistribution of stress and the formation of high-stress, low-stress, and stable-stress zones along the horizontal direction of the floor rock layer. Moreover, the floor rock mass directly below the coal pillar undergoes compression shear failure due to stress levels exceeding its load-bearing capacity, resulting in a plastic damage zone.

The layout principle of reasonable entry location of GERRC is shown in Fig. 6, the high stress zone will increase the overlying load of the “roof cutting short cantilever beam” structure, and the plastic damage zone will weaken the load-bearing capacity of the “roof cutting short cantilever beam” structure itself, so both the high stress zone and the plastic damage zone are not conducive to the stability of the surrounding rock of GERRC. Compared to the stable stress zone, the low stress zone is protected by the “voussoir beam” structure of the upper coal seam, so its stress environment is in an ideal state. In view of this, arranging the entry in the low stress zone can significantly enhance the stability of the surrounding rock of the entry.

Layout principle of reasonable entry location for GERRC.

Method for determining the reasonable entry location of GERRC

Based on the above analysis, in the lower mining of close-distance coal seams, in order to ensure the stability of the surrounding rock of GERRC, the entries should avoid the high stress zone and plastic damage zone in the floor rock layer caused by the mining of the upper coal seam, and should be arranged in low stress zone with of floor and in an elastic state. Therefore, a method for determining the reasonable entry location of GERRC in close-distance coal seams is proposed, as shown in Fig. 7, firstly, based on the geological conditions of the close-distance coal seam engineering, the support stress distribution characteristics of the remaining coal pillars and goaf of the upper coal seam are clarified, and a mechanical model for floor load transfer is established to obtain stress distribution law of horizontal stress, vertical stress, and shear stress of the floor. Meanwhile, combined with the strain equivalence theory, a continuous damage model of rock mass is established to solve the damage amount and range of the floor rock mass based on the stress state. Secondly, the floor is divided into high stress zone, low stress zone, and stable stress zone according to the stress level, as well as elastic zone and plastic zone according to the damage state. Finally, the reasonable entry location for GERRC at close-distance coal seams is determined through the intersection of low stress zone and elastic zone of the floor.

Reasonable layout area of GERRC

Based on the comprehensive theoretical calculation and numerical analysis results, the stress and damage zoning characteristics of the floor are summarized in Fig. 12. The mining of the upper coal seam changes the stress state and damage condition of the floor, which can be mainly divided into plastic damage zone and elastic zone. Among them, the elastic zone is further divided into high stress zone, low stress zone, and stable stress zone. Near the roof of the 9# coal seam: the rock mass within the range of 0–9.5 m from the center of the coal pillar is in a plastic damage state, while that outside the range of 9.5 m is in an elastic state; According to the further analysis of the stress distribution in the elastic zone, the rock mass within a distance of about 0–12 m from the center of the coal pillar belongs to the high stress zone; the rock mass within a distance of about 12–35 m from the center of the coal pillar belongs to the low stress zone, with a stress value below 6 MPa; the rock mass outside the range of 35 m from the center of the coal pillar belongs to the stable stress zone, with a stress value maintained at about 6.06 MPa. Through the intersection of the range of the elastic zone with the range of the low stress zone, a reasonable layout area of the GERRC for the 9105 Working face of Longmenta Coal Mine was determined, which is within a range of 12–35 m from the center of the remaining coal pillar in the upper coal seam. The rock mass in this area not only has good load-bearing performance, but also has a lower stress level.

Engineering application

Location design of the entry for GERRC

According to theoretical analysis and numerical simulation results, the GERRC in the close-distance lower coal seams is carried out in the low stress zone (the distance from the center of the upper remaining coal pillar is 12–35 m), which can significantly improve the stability of the entry. Therefore, based on the alternating mining and excavation planning of Longmenta Coal Mine, the position of track entry in the 9105 Working face is designed to be 22.5 m away from the center of the upper remaining coal pillar. The dip length of the Working face is designed to be 252 m and the strike length to be 950 m, as shown in Fig. 13.

Layout plan for the 9105 working face.

Key parameter design for GEERC

The key parameter design for GERRC of the 9105 track entry is shown in Fig. 14. The section size of the entry is 4.5 m*2.5 m, and the roof is designed for advanced pre-splitting using dense drilling technology, with the roof cutting depth of 7 m, and the roof cutting angle of 15° (biased towards the goaf). The entry roof is reinforced and supported by constant resistance anchor cables with a size of ϕ21.6*6000 mm, and an inter-row spacing of 1600*800 mm. Within a 300 m range of the lagging Working face, temporary support is provided using “single pillar + π-shaped beam (one beam and three pillars) and U29 steel”, with the row spacing of the single pillar and the retractable U-beam set at 800 mm and 500 mm.

Gob-side entry retaining effects

Taking the 18# measuring station about 200 m away from the cutting hole as an example, the deformation monitoring results of the surrounding rock during the gob-side entry retaining are shown in Fig. 17. Under the influence of advanced mining stress, the surrounding rock of the entry undergoes deformation at a distance of about 20 m from the Working face; within the range of 0–80 m of the lagging Working face, due to insufficient collapse of the direct roof, the volume of fragmented and expanded gangue in the goaf is insufficient, and the basic roof lacks effective support and sinks sharply, resulting in an increase in the overlying load of the “roof cutting short cantilever beam” structure and intensified deformation of the surrounding rock of the entry; within the range of about 80–120 m of the lagging Working face, during this stage, the direct roof collapses completely and fills the goaf, and the basic roof begins to compress the gangue in the goaf, and the subsidence rate decreases, resulting in slow deformation of the surrounding rock of the entry. Conclusion

After mining the close-distance upper coal seams, a stable “voussoir beam” structure forms at the Working face end, leading to distinct zoning characteristics in floor support stress. This includes a stress concentration zone beneath the coal pillar, a pressure relief zone below the voussoir beam, and a protolith stress zone in the mid-goaf area. Using gob-side entry retaining by roof cutting (GERRC) in the lower seams, the fragmented and expanded goaf gangue supports the interlayer main roof, preventing reactivation of the upper seam stope structure and forming a fixed-free “roof cutting short cantilever beam” structure.

For the 9105 Working face in Longmenta Coal Mine, the suitable GERRC layout falls within 12–35 m from the coal pillar center.

Based on the above research, the 9105 track entry was positioned 22.5 m from the upper residual coal pillar center. Field monitoring confirmed good roof integrity and elastic rock mass behavior, indicating the entry lies outside the floor plastic damage zone. During the stable period, roof-to-floor convergence was 113 mm, and rib-to-rib convergence was 87 mm, demonstrating effective gob-side entry retaining with minimal surrounding rock deformation.

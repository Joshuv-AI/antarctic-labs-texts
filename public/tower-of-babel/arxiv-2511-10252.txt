Abstract
. A topological space is totally paracompact if any base of this space contains a locally finite subcover. We focus on a problem of Curtis whether in the class of regular Lindelöf spaces total paracompactness is equivalent to the Menger covering property. To this end we consider topological spaces with certain dense subsets. It follows from our results that the above equivalence holds in the class of Lindelöf GO-spaces defined on subsets of reals. We also provide a game-theoretical proof that any regular Menger space is totally paracompact and show that in the class of first-countable spaces the Menger game and a partial open neighborhood assignment game of Aurichi are equivalent. We also show that if 𝔟 = ω 1 \mathfrak{b}=\omega_{1} , then there is an uncountable subspace of the Sorgenfrey line whose all finite powers are Lindelöf, which is a strengthening of a famous result due to Michael.

1.
Introduction

By
space
we mean a Hausdorff infinite topological space.
A space
X
is
totally paracompact

[
10
]
, if every open base of
X
contains a locally finite cover of
X
.
In 1924, Menger introduced the following covering property as an attempt to characterize
\sigma
-compactness in the class of metric spaces.
A metric space
X
is
Menger-basis
if for any basis
\mathcal{B}
of
X
, there are sets
B_{0},B_{1},\dotsc\in\mathcal{B}
whose diameters tend to zero and the family
\{\,B_{n}:n\in\omega\,\}
covers
X
.
After that Hurewicz reformulated the above definition, extending it to all topological spaces:
a space
X
is
Menger
, if for every sequence
\mathcal{U}_{0},\allowbreak\mathcal{U}_{1},\allowbreak\dots
of open covers of
X
there are finite sets
\mathcal{F}_{0}\subseteq\mathcal{U}_{0},\mathcal{F}_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{U}_{1},\dotsc
such that the family
\bigcup\{\,\mathcal{F}_{n}:n\in\omega\,\}
is a cover of
X

[
11
]
.
The Hurewicz definition of the Menger property is equivalent to the Menger-basis property in the class of metric spaces.
The Menger property is one of the central properties considered in the topological selections theory.

Our investigation is motivated by the following result of Curtis.

Theorem 1.1

(Curtis
[
7
, Theoirem 1.1.]
)
.

Every regular Menger space is totally paracompact.

Notice that there are non-regular countable spaces that may not be paracompact, e.g., Bing’s space
[
16
]
.

A space is
Lindelöf
if any open cover of this space contains a countable subcover (notice that in this approach, we do not request that the space is regular).
Since any Menger space is Lindelöf, in the light of the above result, Curtis formulated the following problem.

Problem 1.2

(Curtis
[
7
]
)
.

Is it true that any Lindelöf totally paracompact space is Menger?

Thus far it is known that there is a positive solution to Problem
1.2
in the class of metrizable spaces
[
13
]
and in the class of generalized ordered spaces defined on subsets of the real line
[
15
]
.
In Section
2
, we show that total paracompactness is equivalent to the Menger property in the class of Lindelöf spaces having
\sigma
-compact dense subset.
We also consider another classes of spaces, where it holds.
Our results capture all known classes of spaces, where the Curtis conjecture is true.
Since the Menger property can be characterized using a topological game
[
11
,
17
]
, in Section
4
, we provide a game-theoretical proof of Theorem
1.1
which seems to be much simpler than the original one (modulo game-characterization of the Menger property).
Section
5
is devoted to relations between a partial open neighborhood assignment game of Aurichi
[
1
]
and the Menger game (see definitions below).
In particular, we show that in the class of first-countable spaces these two games are equivalent.
The Aurichi game comes on stage naturally when considering Menger spaces and totally paracompact spaces in the context of so-called
D
-spaces
and an old problem whether every regular Lindelöf space is a
D
-space
[
8
]
(see Section 5 for definitions and more details).
We finish our paper with comments and open problems.

2.
Menger vs. total paracompactness

We establish the following partial answer to Problem

.
Recall that a paracompact space with a dense

-compact subspace is Lindelöf

Theorem 2.1
.

Every totally paracompact space with a
\sigma
-compact dense subset is Menger.

Proof.

Assume that
X
is a totally paracompact space with a
\sigma
-compact dense subset
\bigcup_{n\in\omega}C_{n}
, where each set
C_{n}
is compact.
Fix a sequence
\mathcal{U}_{0},\allowbreak\mathcal{U}_{1},\allowbreak\dots
of open covers of
X
.
We may assume that the families
\mathcal{U}_{n}
are closed under finite unions.
Fix
x\in X
.
For each
n\in\omega
, let

\mathcal{B}_{n}(x)
be the family of all open neighborhoods
V
of

such that

is contained in some set from
\mathcal{U}_{n}
and
V\cap C_{n}\neq\emptyset
.
Then the collection
\mathcal{B}(x):=\bigcup_{n\in\omega}\mathcal{B}_{n}(x)
is a local base at

.
It follows that the family
\mathcal{B}:=\bigcup_{x\in X}\mathcal{B}(x)
is a base for
X
.
By the assumption, there exists a locally finite cover
{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}
of
X
.
Fix
n\in\omega

The family
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
is locally finite and it covers the compact set

Thus, the set

\{\,B\in\mathcal{B}^{\prime}:B\cap C_{n}\neq\emptyset\,\}

is finite and the set

\mathcal{B}_{n}^{\prime}:=\mathcal{B}^{\prime}\cap\bigcup_{x\in X}\mathcal{B}_{n}(x)

is finite, too.
By the definition, each element from the union
\bigcup_{x\in X}{\mathcal{B}}_{n}(x)
is contained in a set from
\mathcal{U}_{n}

Since the family
\mathcal{U}_{n}
is closed under finite unions, there is a set

such that
\bigcup{\mathcal{B}}_{n}^{\prime}\subseteq U_{n}
.
Since the family
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}=\bigcup_{n\in\omega}\mathcal{B}^{\prime}_{n}

\{U_{n}:n\in\omega\}
covers

, too.
∎

Every separable totally paracompact space is Menger.

Example 2.4
.

There is a totally metacompact, not paracompact, not Lindelöf space having a
\sigma

Consider

Recall that every metacompact separable space is Lindelöf. Then we found another partial answer to the Curtis question with the following result.

Theorem 2.5
.

Proof.

Let

Fix a sequence
\mathcal{U}_{0},\allowbreak\mathcal{U}_{1},\allowbreak\dots

\mathcal{U}_{n}

Fix

By the assumption, there exists a point-finite cover

Since the family

.

For a separable space

3.
GO-spaces

Sakai
[
15
]
considered Problem
1.2
for subspaces of the Sorgenfrey line and more generally in the class of generalized ordered spaces which are defined on subsets of the real line (see definition below).
This class of spaces has a long history in general topology and it has been well studied.
A
real generalized-ordered space
(or abbreviated
real GO-space
) is a triple
(X,<,\tau)
, where
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}
, the relation
<
is the usual order on
\mathbb{R}
restricted to
X
and
\tau
is a Hausdorff topology on
X
generated by some intervals (possibly degenerated).
For simplicity we write a real GO-space
(X,<,\tau)
as
X
.
Every real GO-space
X
we can split

X=R_{X}\cup E_{X}\cup L_{X}\cup D_{X},

where:

\displaystyle D_{X}

\displaystyle:=\{\,x\in X:\{x\}\text{ is open in }X\,\},

\displaystyle R_{X}

\displaystyle:=\{\,x\in X\setminus D_{X}:[x,\infty)\text{ is open in }X\,\},

\displaystyle L_{X}

\displaystyle:=\{\,x\in X\setminus D_{X}:(-\infty,x]\text{ is open in }X\,\},

\displaystyle E_{X}

\displaystyle:=\{\,x\in X\setminus D_{X}:\{\,(a,b):a,b\in X,x\in(a,b)\,\}\text{ is a base at }x\,\}.

We get the following corollary from the previous section.

Corollary 3.1
.

Every totally metacompact Lindelöf real GO-space is Menger.

Proof.

There is an ordinal number
\alpha
such that
X^{(\alpha)}=X^{(\alpha+1)}
.
It follows that
X^{(\alpha)}
is a closed subspace of
X
with no isolated points.
Any real GO-space with no isolated points is separable.
Apply Theorem
2.8
.
∎

Corollary 3.2
.

For a Lindelöf real GO-space
X
, the following assertions are equivalent.

(1)

The space
X
is Menger.

(2)

The space
X
is totally paracompact.

(3)

The space
X
is totally metacompact.

Remark 3.3
.

The above corollary was obtained also by Sakai
[
15
, Theorem 5.2]
, but his proof is much more technical than our, more general approach.

Now we consider real GO-spaces defined on the entire real line. So, let
X=\mathbb{R}
,
X=R_{X}\cup E_{X}\cup L_{X}\cup I_{X}
, and let
\tau
be some topology

\tau
that comes from the partition. For a subset
C\subseteq X
, define

\displaystyle i(C)

\displaystyle:=\{\,x\in X:\{x\}\text{ is isolated in }\tau|C\,\},

\displaystyle r(C)

\displaystyle:=(R_{X}\cap C)\setminus i(C),

\displaystyle l(C)

\displaystyle:=(L_{X}\cap C)\setminus i(C),

\displaystyle e(C)

Recall the following result of Benett–Balogh.

Theorem 3.4

(Benett–Balogh
[

]
)
.

Let

be a real GO-space defined on the entire real line. The space

is totally paracompact if and only if for every perfect set

is not dense and co-dense in

in the standard topology.

Theorem 3.5
.

A real GO-space defined on the entire real line is Menger if and only if it is
\sigma
-compact.

Proof.

Every
\sigma
-compact space is Menger.
So, assume that
X
is a Menger real GO-space defined on the entire real line.
Let
{\mathcal{U}}
be the collection of all intervals
(a,b)
, where
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
and
a<b
, such that
(a,b)\cap X
is
\sigma
-compact in
X
.
Then the set
U:=\bigcup\mathcal{U}
is
\sigma
-compact in
X
.
Towards a contradiction, assume that the set
C:=X\setminus U
is nonempty.
Since
U
is open in the standard topology, we have that the set
C
is closed in the standard topology.
Now we prove that
C
is perfect.
Assume that there is an interval
(a,b)
such that
(a,b)\cap C=\{x\}
for some
x\in\mathbb{R}
.
Since
(a,x)\subseteq U
, the set

is
\sigma
-compact in

and

is an

-set, the set
(a,x)
is
\sigma
-compact in
X
.
Similarly, the set
(x,b)
is
\sigma
-compact in

.
Thus, the set
(a,b)\cap X
is
\sigma
-compact in

, too.
It follows that
(a,b)\subseteq U
, a contradiction.

The set
C\cap R_{X}
is not dense in
C
with the standard topology:
Suppose not.
Since

is perfect, there is a copy

of the Cantor set inside

such that the sets

.
Then the set

3.4

Similarly, the set

By the above observations, there is an interval
[a,b]
in
\mathbb{R}
such that the set
C^{\prime}:=[a,b]\cap C

.
Thus, we may assume that

.
Now we prove that the set

with respect to the standard topology:
Suppose not and let

be an interval in

Then the topologies on

The set

Finally, we have that the set

is dense and co-dense in

By Theorem
3.4
and Corollary

we get a contradiction.

We conclude that

, and thus the space

is
\sigma
-compact.
∎

4.
Curtis’ result via topological games

Many classical covering properties, starting with the Menger property, admit natural game-theoretic counterpart
[

,
17
,
2
]

Recall the following definition.

Definition 4.1
.

A
Menger game
played on a space
X
, denoted by
\M(X)

Alice
and
Bob
, with an inning per each natural number

.
In round

plays an open cover

of
X

Bob
replies with a finite set
\mathcal{F}_{n}\subseteq\mathcal{U}_{n}
.

Bob
wins the game, if the family

covers

and

wins otherwise.

Theorem 4.2

(
[
11
,
17
]
)
.

A space

is Menger if and only if
Alice
has no winning strategy in the game

.

We provide a game-theoretical proof of Theorem

which seems to be much simpler than the original one (modulo the above game-characterization of the Menger property).

, we have

.
There is an open cover

such that

for all

.

.

Alice

replies with a family

{\mathcal{F}}_{n}
is a finite subset of

.
We may assume that

Since

5.
Partial open neighborhood assignment game of Aurichi

Let
X
be a space.
An
open neighborhood assignment of

is a family

of open sets in

such that
x\in U_{x}
for all
x\in X
.
The space
X
is a
D
-space

[
8
]
if for any open neighborhood assignment
\{\,U_{x}:x\in X\,\}
of

, there is a closed and discrete in
X

D\subseteq X
such that the family
\{\,U_{x}:x\in D\,\}
covers
X
.
In the literature, many references to a result that any Menger space is a
D
-space lead to a paper of Aurichi
[
1
]
.
It turns out that this result, in the class of regular spaces, follows directly from much older Theorem
1.1
(Aurichi does not assume that a space is regular).
We provide a proof for the sake of completeness.

Proposition 5.1
.

Every totally paracompact space is a D-space.

Proof.

Let
X
be a space and
\{U_{x}:x\in X\}
be an open neighborhood assignment of
X
.
For
x\in X
, let
{\mathcal{B}}_{x}
be a local base at
x
such that
\bigcup{\mathcal{B}}_{x}\subseteq U_{x}
.
Then
{\mathcal{B}}=\bigcup_{x\in X}{\mathcal{B}}_{x}
is a base for
X
.
By the assumption, there is a family
{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}\subset{\mathcal{B}}
which is a locally finite cover of
X
.
Clearly, the sets
{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}\cap{\mathcal{B}}_{x}
are finite for each
x\in X
.
Then set
D=\{x\in X:{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}\cap{\mathcal{B}}_{x}\not=\emptyset\}
is closed and discrete in
X
and the family
\{U_{x}:x\in D\}
covers
X
.
∎

In order to prove that any Menger space is a
D
-space, Aurichi used the following topological game.
Let
X
be a space.
A
partial open neighborhood assignment in

is a family

, where

and each set

is an open neighborhood of

For a partial open neighborhood assignment

in

.
Partial open neighborhood assignments

and

in

are

if

for each

.
Since the ambient space
X
will be clear from the context, a partial open neighborhood assignment in
X
, we just call a
partial open neighborhood assignment

(Aurichi
[
1
, Definition 2.1]
)
.

Let
X
be a space.
A
partial open neighborhood assignment game
played on

, denoted by

Alice
and
Bob
, with an inning per each natural number

In round

has no winning strategy in the game

[

be a set picked by
Bob
according to
\sigma
, in response to
\mathcal{U}_{n}^{\prime}
.
Since the set
D_{n}
is a subsequence of a convergent sequence and it is closed in
X
, the set
D_{n}
is finite.
There is a set
U_{n}\in\mathcal{U}_{n}
such that
U_{n}=V_{D_{n}}
and
Bob
plays the set
U_{n}
in
\M(X)
as a response to
\mathcal{U}_{n}
.

Since
\sigma
is a winning strategy for
Bob
in
\PONAG(X)
, the family
\{\,V_{D_{n}}:n\in\omega\,\}
covers
X
.
Since
U_{n}=V_{D_{n}}
for all
n
, the family
\{\,U_{n}:n\in\omega\,\}
also covers
X
, and thus the defined above strategy for
Bob
in
\M(X)
is a winning strategy.

∎

Remark 5.8
.

The proof of Theorem
5.5
and
Proposition
5.7
remain true if
Bob
has a winning strategy in a modified version of
\PONAG
, where in each round
n
,
Bob
in response to an

’s partial open neighborhood assignment
\{\,V_{x}:x\in Y_{n}\,\}
, plays just a closed subset
D_{n}\subseteq Y_{n}
in

(not necessarily closed and discrete in the ambient space

).

Corollary 5.9
.

Let
X
be a Lindelöf Fréchet space.
Then
\PONAG(X)
and
\M(X)
are equivalent games.

By a result of Telgarsky
[
20
]
, if a space
X
is perfectly normal and
Bob
has a winning strategy in
\M(X)
, then the space
X
is
\sigma
-compact.
Combination of this fact together with our results gives the following corollary.

.

Let

be a perfectly normal space. Then the following assertions are equivalent.

(1)

has a winning strategy in
\M(X)
.

(2)

Bob
has a winning strategy in
\PONAG(X)
.

(3)

The space

\sigma
-compact.

6.
Comments and open problems

Subspaces of the Sorgenfrey line and Lindelöfness in all finite powers

By the result of Michael
[
14
]
, assuming that the Continuum Hypothesis holds, there is an uncountable subspace of the Sorgenfrey line, whose all finite powers are Lindelöf.
On the other hand, by the result of Todorcevic
[
21
]
, if the open colorings axiom holds (a statement independent from ZFC), each uncountable subspace of the Sorgenfrey line has a non-Lindelöf square.

A space
X
is
Hurewicz

[
11
]
, if for each sequence
\mathcal{U}_{0},\allowbreak\mathcal{U}_{1},\allowbreak\dots
of open covers of
X
, there are finite sets
\mathcal{F}_{0}\subseteq\mathcal{U}_{0},\mathcal{F}_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{U}_{1},\dotsc
such that the family
\{\,\bigcup\mathcal{F}_{n}:n\in\omega\,\}
is a
\gamma
-cover
of
X
, i.e., the sets
\{\,n:x\in\bigcup\mathcal{F}_{n}\,\}
are cofinite for all
x\in X
.
We have the following implications

\sigma\text{-compactness}\longrightarrow\text{Hurewicz}\longrightarrow\text{Menger}

and none of these implications is reversible, even in the class of subspaces of the real line
[
12
,
4
,
5
,
22
]
.

Let
{\operatorname{P}(\omega)}
be the powerset of
\omega
.
We identify each set from
{\operatorname{P}(\omega)}
with its characteristic function, an element of the Cantor cube
{2^{\omega}}
.
Let
{[\omega]^{\omega}}
be the family of all infinite subset of
\omega
and
[\mathbb{N}]^{\text{$<\!\!\infty$}}
be the family of all finite subsets of
\omega
.
Identifying each set
x\in{[\omega]^{\omega}}
with the increasing enumeration of its elements, we have that
x\in{\omega^{\omega}}
.
For
x,y\in{[\omega]^{\omega}}
, we write
x\leq^{*}y
if the set
\{\,n:x(n)\leq y(n)\,\}
is cofinite.
A set
A\subseteq{[\omega]^{\omega}}
is
unbounded
, if for each
y\in{[\omega]^{\omega}}
, there is
x\in A
such that
x\not\leq^{*}y
.
Let
\mathfrak{b}
be the minimal cardinality of an unbounded set in
{[\omega]^{\omega}}
.
We have
\omega_{1}\leq\mathfrak{b}\leq\mathfrak{c}
and it is independent from ZFC that
\mathfrak{b}=\omega_{1}<\mathfrak{c}
.
It turns out that the above mentioned Michael result can be obtained using much milder hypothesis than the Continuum Hypothesis.

Example 6.1
.

Assume that
\mathfrak{b}=\omega_{1}
.
There is an uncountable subspace of the Sorgenfrey line whose all finite powers are Hurewicz (in particular, they are Lindelöf).

The set
{\operatorname{P}(\omega)}
can be treated as a subset of the real line using standard homeomorphism between
{2^{\omega}}
and the standard middle-third Cantor set
C
in the real line.
Using this standard homeomorphism and above identifications of elements in
{\operatorname{P}(\omega)}
and
{2^{\omega}}
, we see
that a counterpart in
{\operatorname{P}(\omega)}
of the set of all left-endpoints of intervals which are engaged in a construction of this standard middle-third Cantor set
C
is the set
[\mathbb{N}]^{\text{$<\!\!\infty$}}
.
Thus, we may assume that
{\operatorname{P}(\omega)}=C
.
Let
{\operatorname{P}(\omega)}_{\text{M}}
be the set
{\operatorname{P}(\omega)}
with a topology, where open neighborhoods of points from
[\mathbb{N}]^{\text{$<\!\!\infty$}}
are inherited from the standard topology on
\mathbb{R}
and the points from
{[\omega]^{\omega}}
are isolated.

Let
X=\{\,x_{\alpha}:\alpha<\mathfrak{b}\,\}\subseteq{[\omega]^{\omega}}
be an unbounded set in
{[\omega]^{\omega}}
such that
x_{\alpha}\leq^{*}x_{\beta}
for all
\alpha<\beta<\mathfrak{b}
(such a set exists in ZFC).
Let
(X\cup[\mathbb{N}]^{\text{$<\!\!\infty$}})_{\text{M}}
be the set
X\cup[\mathbb{N}]^{\text{$<\!\!\infty$}}
with a topology inherited from
{\operatorname{P}(\omega)}_{\text{M}}
.
Now let
(X\cup[\mathbb{N}]^{\text{$<\!\!\infty$}})_{\text{S}}
be the set
X\cup[\mathbb{N}]^{\text{$<\!\!\infty$}}
with a topology inherited from the Sorgenfrey line.
Observe that in such a case basic open sets for the points from
[\mathbb{N}]^{\text{$<\!\!\infty$}}
are exactly the same, when consider
[\mathbb{N}]^{\text{$<\!\!\infty$}}
as a subspace of
(X\cup[\mathbb{N}]^{\text{$<\!\!\infty$}})_{\text{M}}
and
(X\cup[\mathbb{N}]^{\text{$<\!\!\infty$}})_{\text{S}}
.
Thus, the identity map from
(X\cup[\mathbb{N}]^{\text{$<\!\!\infty$}})_{\text{M}}
onto
(X\cup[\mathbb{N}]^{\text{$<\!\!\infty$}})_{\text{S}}
is continuous.

By the result of Tsaban and the third named author
[
18
]
, since
\mathfrak{b}=\omega_{1}
, all finite powers of
(X\cup[\mathbb{N}]^{\text{$<\!\!\infty$}})_{\text{M}}
are Hurewicz.
Since
(X\cup[\mathbb{N}]^{\text{$<\!\!\infty$}})_{\text{S}}
is a continuous image of
(X\cup[\mathbb{N}]^{\text{$<\!\!\infty$}})_{\text{M}}
and the Hurewicz property is preserved by continuous mappings, all finite powers of
(X\cup[\mathbb{N}]^{\text{$<\!\!\infty$}})_{\text{S}}
are Hurewicz (and thus Lindelöf).

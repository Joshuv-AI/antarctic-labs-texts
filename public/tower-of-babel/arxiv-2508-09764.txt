Fujita-Kato solution for the 3D compressible pressureless Navier-Stokes equations with discontinuous and large-variation density
Xiaojie Wang Jiahong Wu Fuyi Xu, [2mm] School of Mathematics and Statistics, Shandong University of Technology, Zibo, 255049, Shandong, China

Abstract




Abstract
This paper mainly focuses on the Cauchy problem to the 3D compressible pressureless Navier-Stokes equations arising from
models of collective behavior, which can be derived by taking the high Mach number limit of the classical compressible Navier-Stokes system. We construct the global-in-time existence and uniqueness of the so-called Fujita-Kato solution to the system, provided that the initial density
\rho_{0}
is discontinuous, large-variation and the initial velocity
u_{0}
is in a critical functional framework. Our method relies on some time weighted estimates and the Lagrangian approach.

Key words:
Fujita-Kato solution; compressible pressureless Navier-Stokes equations; large-variation; discontinuous density.

AMS subject classifications:
35Q30, 35Q60, 76D05, 76W05.

Introduction and Main Results

1.1
Model and synopsis of related studies

In recent years, due to the physical importance and the very broad applications of
hydrodynamics in nature and industry, the following general form of hydrodynamical
system has received a extensive attention
in the mathematical community

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\rho+\nabla\cdot(\rho u)=0,\\
&\partial_{t}(\rho u)+\nabla\cdot(\rho u\otimes u)+\nabla P(\rho)=\mathcal{D}(u,\rho).\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

Here
\rho=\rho(t,x)
and
u=u(t,x)
are the unknown functions representing the density and velocity, respectively,
P(\rho)
stands for the pressure.
Hydrodynamic limiting system (
1.1
) with different type of pressures including the pressureless dynamics with
P\equiv 0

[
21
]
and isothermal pressure with
P(\rho)=\kappa\rho^{\gamma}(\kappa>0,\gamma>1)

[
31
]
, can be formally
derived from the following system

\partial_{t}f+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f+\nabla_{v}\cdot K(f)f=0,

(1.2)

where
f=f(t,x,v)
is a distribution function of a gas in the phase-space. Classically,
if the operator
K
comes from the Poisson potential, then we find the Vlasov system.
If taking a less singular operator, then one may obtain for example the Cucker-Smale
system that models collective behavior like flocking of birds.

When the term
\mathcal{D}(u,\rho)
represents the nonlocal velocity alignment which is given as follows

\mathcal{D}(u,\rho)=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\phi(x-y)(u(x)-u(y))\rho(y,t)\rho(x,t)\mathrm{d}y,

(1.3)

where
\phi
is called the communication weight, measuring the
strength of the alignment interactions. Then the system (
1.1
) is called the the Euler-alignment equations. An interesting type of communication weights are strongly singular near the origin,
with a prototype taking the following form

\phi(x)=\frac{c_{\alpha,N}}{|x|^{N+\alpha}},\quad c_{\alpha}=\frac{2^{\alpha}\Gamma(\frac{\alpha+N}{2})}{\pi^{\frac{N}{2}}|\Gamma(-\frac{\alpha}{2})|}.

(1.4)

where the constant
c_{\alpha}
is related to the fractional Laplacian
operator, which in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
reads

\Lambda^{\alpha}=(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}},\quad\Lambda^{\alpha}f=c_{\alpha,N}P.V.\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\frac{f(x)-f(y)}{|x-y|^{N+\alpha}}\mathrm{d}y,\quad 0<\alpha<2.

(1.5)

It is evident that the singular alignment
\mathcal{D}(u,\rho)
with such a weight
\phi
can be
expressed as a commutator form related to the fractional Laplace operator
\Lambda^{\alpha}

\mathcal{D}(u,\rho)=-\rho(\Lambda^{\alpha}(\rho u)-u\Lambda^{\alpha}\rho)=-\rho([\Lambda^{\alpha},u]\rho).

(1.6)

The pressureless Euler-alignment equations (i.e.,
P\equiv 0
) has an intriguing regularization effect to the solutions. Global regularity
is obtained for any non-viscous smooth initial data for the system in the one-dimensional
torus by Shvydkoy and Tadmor
[
40
]
for
1\leq\alpha<2
, and by Do
et al

[
19
]
for
0<\alpha<1
(see also
[40]). For the multi-dimensional case, global well-posedness
are only known for small initial data around an equilibrium state, and we refer readers to Refs
[
16
,
39
]
and references therein. Global
regularity for general large initial data remains a challenging open problem.

When
\mathcal{D}(u,\rho)=0
, the system (
1.1
) reduces to the compressible Euler system, only local
existence of smooth solutions could be expected, and shock waves will form in finite time. If

P\equiv 0
, then one just recovers the pressureless compressible Euler system, and there is
no interaction whatsoever between the individuals, which describe the formation of large-scale structures in astrophysics and the aggregation
of sticky particles (see, e.g.,
[
41
,
45
,
47
]
) and the references therein. The majority of results in the literature (e.g.,
[
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
28
,
30
]
) focus on the mathematical theory of the pressureless dynamics.

When we replace the dissipation term
\mathcal{D}(u,\rho)
by the
Lamé operator
-\mu\Delta u-(\lambda+\mu)\nabla{\rm div}u
, where the constant viscosity coefficients
\mu
and
\lambda
comply with the physical conditions
\mu>0
and
2\mu+N\lambda\geq 0
, then the system (
1.1
) becomes the following classical barotropic compressible Navier-Stokes equations

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\rho+{\rm div}(\rho u)=0,\\
&\partial_{t}(\rho u)+{{\rm div}}(\rho u\otimes u)-\mu\Delta u-(\lambda+\mu)\nabla{\rm div}u+\nabla P(\rho)=0.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.7)

The model (
1.7
) is a fundamental mathematical model used to describe the motion of a viscous flow. Due to its mathematical challenges and broad physical applications, there are extensive literature on the well-posedness of solutions for this system.
Here we briefly review some of the most
relevant papers about global well-posedness of strong solutions to the system when the initial data are close to a constant equilibrium state.
Matsumura and Nishida
[
34
,
35
]
studied the global existence of classical
solutions to the 3D compressible Navier-Stokes equations for data
(\rho_{0},u_{0})
with high regularity order and close to a stable
equilibrium. Motivated by those works on the
incompressible Navier-Stokes system
[
22
]
, Danchin
[
13
]
proved the existence and uniqueness of the global strong solution for initial data close to a stable
equilibrium state in
L^{2}
critical Besov spaces. Subsequently, the result of
[
13
]
was independently extended
to more general
L^{p}
critical Besov spaces by Charve and Danchin
[
9
]
, Chen et al.
[
12
]
and Haspot
[
25
]
. It should be noted that these theory requires the solution to have small oscillations from a uniform nonvacuum state, ensuring that the density is strictly away from the vacuum.

If we further consider the high Mach number limit in the system (
1.7
), a pressureless viscous model then emerges. More precisely, we first introduce the dimensionless Mach number defined as the ratio of the velocity
u
to the reference sound speed. Then the rescaled triplet

\rho_{\varepsilon}(t,x)=\rho(\frac{t}{\varepsilon},x),\quad u_{\varepsilon}(t,x)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}u(\frac{t}{\varepsilon},x),\quad\mu_{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\mu,\quad\lambda_{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\lambda

satisfies

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\rho_{\varepsilon}+{\rm div}(\rho_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon})=0,\\
&\partial_{t}(\rho_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon})+{{\rm div}}(\rho_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}\otimes u_{\varepsilon})-\mu_{\varepsilon}\Delta u_{\varepsilon}-(\lambda_{\varepsilon}+\mu_{\varepsilon})\nabla{\rm div}u_{\varepsilon}+\nabla P(\rho_{\varepsilon})=0.\end{aligned}\right.

Concerning that the Mach number
\varepsilon
goes to infinity and still defining

\lim_{\varepsilon\to\infty}(\rho_{\varepsilon},u_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\varepsilon},\lambda_{\varepsilon})=(\rho,u,\mu,\lambda),

we formally derive the following pressureless Navier-Stokes equations

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\rho_{t}+{\rm div}(\rho u)=0,\\
&\partial_{t}(\rho u)+{{\rm div}}(\rho u\otimes u)-\mu\Delta u-(\lambda+\mu)\nabla{\rm div}u=0.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.8)

The system (
1.8
) does not
take into account the effects of internal pressure. A simple example is the motion of dust, or free particles, moving through space, as seen in astrophysics
[
18
]
or in multi-fluid systems
[
2
]
. Beyond inanimate matter, we can also consider models that
describe collective behavior, where particles–or rather agents–display some level of intelligence. In such cases, having a force like internal pressure is less natural, leading to the pressureless gas dynamics system. In present work, we mainly focus on the system (
1.8
) in
\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
, subject to the given initial data

(\rho,u)|_{t=0}=(\rho_{0},u_{0}).

(1.9)

In recent years, the study of mathematical properties for the pressureless Navier-Stokes equations remains an important topic because the model has widespread applications in hydrodynamics, both in nature and industry
[
3
,
4
,
18
,
40
,
42
]
. In 2021, Danchin et al.
[
17
]
first proved global well-posedness of strong solution to the Cauchy problem (
1.8
)-(
1.9
) relying heavily on a set of maximal regularity estimates for the heat equation when the initial density
\rho_{0}
is a small fluctuation of a positive constant in the
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
-norm
and the initial velocity
u_{0}
belongs to the homogeneous Besov spaces (not critical regularity) satisfying a smallness condition. This result underscores that the Cauchy problem (
1.8
)-(
1.9
) still has global existence and uniqueness, despite the inability to leverage the effective viscous flux. More recently, Guo et al.
[
24
]
constructed the global well-posedness and stability of classical solutions to the Cauchy problem (
1.8
)-(
1.9
) by virtue of the smallness of initial perturbation in Sobolev norm and the
L^{1}
norm of initial velocity. It is worthwhile to emphasize that,
the assumption of small perturbations on the initial density plays a key role in their analysis, which excludes the case with large-variations along a hypersurface.
Xu
[
43
]
conducted a systematic study of the Gaussian bounds for the fundamental matrix of a generalized parabolic Lamé system with only bounded and measurable coefficients, subsequently establishing maximal
L^{1}
-regularity for the system. As an application, he also proved global well-posedness for the Cauchy problem (
1.8
)-(
1.9
) when the initial density
\rho_{0}
is large in multiplier space
\mathcal{M}(\dot{B}_{p,1}^{\frac{3}{p}-1})
and initial velocity
u_{0}
is sufficiently small in critical Besov spaces
\dot{B}_{p,1}^{\frac{3}{p}-1}
. Although Xu’s approach accommodates initial density with large variations, it necessitates the use of multiplier space to require slightly more regularity on the initial density.

Based on the results mentioned above
[
17
,
24
,
43
]
, this raises the question of whether a small initial velocity in some critical space, combined with a large-variation, discontinuous bounded initial density, can yield a unique global-in-time solution to the 3D Cauchy problem (
1.8
)-(
1.9
). The main contribution of the present paper is to provide a positive answer to this question.

1.2
Main results

Our main result regarding global well-posedness is stated as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(\rho_{0},u_{0})
satisfy

0<c_{0}\leq\rho_{0}(x)\leq C_{0}<+\infty\quad\text{and}\quad u_{0}\in\dot{B}_{2,1}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(1.10)

Then there
exists a small enough constant
\varepsilon_{0}>0
depending only on
c_{0},C_{0}
such
that if

\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{B}_{2,1}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\leq\varepsilon_{0},

(1.11)

the Cauchy problem (
1.8
)-(
1.9
) has a unique global solution
(\rho,u)
with

\rho\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})
and
u\in C([0,\infty);\dot{B}_{2,1}^{\frac{1}{2}})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+};\dot{B}_{2,1}^{\frac{3}{2}})
which satisfies

0<m\leq\rho(t,x)\leq M<\infty\quad\text{for}\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{3},

(1.12)

and

\begin{split}&\|u\|_{\tilde{L}_{t}^{\infty}(\dot{B}_{2,1}^{\frac{1}{2}})}+\|u\|_{\tilde{L}_{t}^{2}(\dot{B}_{2,1}^{\frac{3}{2}})}+\|\sqrt{t}u\|_{\tilde{L}_{t}^{\infty}(\dot{B}_{2,1}^{\frac{3}{2}})}+\|\sqrt{t}\nabla u\|_{\tilde{L}_{t}^{2}(\dot{B}_{6,1}^{\frac{1}{2}})}+\|\sqrt{t}u_{t}\|_{\tilde{L}_{t}^{2}(\dot{B}_{2,1}^{\frac{1}{2}})}\\
&\quad+\|\sqrt{t}\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{3}}+\|tu_{t}\|_{\tilde{L}_{t}^{\infty}(\dot{B}_{2,1}^{\frac{1}{2}})}+\|tD_{t}u\|_{\tilde{L}_{t}^{2}(\dot{B}_{2,1}^{\frac{3}{2}})}\\
&\leq\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{B}_{2,1}^{\frac{1}{2}}}.\end{split}

(1.13)

In this context and throughout this paper, we denote
D_{t}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}\partial_{t}+u\cdot\nabla
as the material derivative.

m
and
M
represent two positive constants that depend on
c_{0}
and
C_{0}
, respectively.

Remark 1.2
.

In contrast to the results in
[
17
,
24
]
, we eliminate the small perturbation assumption on the initial density, allowing for large variations in density. Meanwhile, the initial velocity
u_{0}
has critical regularity.

Remark 1.3
.

In this paper, we only require the density to be bounded, without imposing any regularity assumptions. As a result, our findings in Theorem
1.1
expand upon the conclusions established in
[
43
]
.

Remark 1.4
.

Compared with the existing results in
[
37
,
44
,
46
]
, we here deal with the compressible flow that the divergence is no longer 0,
and need further exploit the key Lipschitz estimate of the velocity field to derive a global-in-time bound for the density and uniqueness of Fujita-Kato solution.

1.3
Proof strategy

Before going into the heart of the proof of our main results, we make a brief interpretation of the main difficulties and strategies involved in the proof.

From a mathematical point of view, the absence of the pressure term

P
in the system (
1.8
) shall cause some significant challenges in mathematical analysis. In particular, some important methods that work effectively for the compressible viscous system (
1.7
) often fail for the current pressureless system (
1.8
). For instance, for the compressible viscous system (
1.7
), technique of the so-called effective viscous flux defined as
F={\rm div}u-P
exhibiting better regularity than
{\rm div}u
and
P

considered separately (see
[
20
,
25
,
27
,
29
,
32
]
), and the classical perturbation theory developed by Matsumura-Nishida
[
34
,
35
]
and Danchin
[
13
]

are ineffective here. This may partially
explain the reason why the mathematical theory of pressureless models is poorer than the classical one.

For some existing research methods regarding the pressureless system (
1.8
), we note that the method in
[
17
,
24
]
heavily relied
on a smallness assumption on the initial density fluctuations, and the framework in
[
43
]
still requires slightly more regularity on the initial density
\rho_{0}
, specifically that it belongs to the multiplier space
\mathcal{M}(\dot{B}_{p,1}^{\frac{n}{p}-1})
.
Thus, the methods for pressureless gas model (
1.8
) in
[
17
,
24
,
43
]
are inadequate for the current case, where the initial density does not have any small perturbation assumption or regularity conditions. Therefore, we have to adopt a completely different approach to address the problem. The new ingredients in the present paper stems from Lemma
2.9
and its proof, which was initially utilized by Hmidi and Keraani
[
26
]
for the two-dimensional incompressible Euler system. This concept has since been further developed for three-dimensional incompressible inhomogeneous models, as seen in
[
37
,
44
,
46
]
. However, there are very few results in this direction for the compressible case, primarily because the divergence does not vanish. In this paper, applying this method to the non-standard heat equation with variable coefficient

\rho(\partial_{t}u+u\cdot\nabla u)-\mu\Delta u-(\lambda+\mu)\nabla{\rm div}u=0
with the initial data, and then combining with the low-high frequency decomposition, Bernstein’s inequality and the continuous argument, we successfully obtain global
a priori
estimates of
\|u\|_{\tilde{L}_{t}^{\infty}(\dot{B}^{\frac{1}{2}}_{2,1})}
and
\|\nabla u\|_{\tilde{L}_{t}^{2}(\dot{B}^{\frac{1}{2}}_{2,1})}
when the initial density is merely bounded both above and below by positive constants, and the initial velocity is sufficiently small in critical Besov spaces (see Proposition
3.1
).

On the other hand, the estimate of
\|\nabla u\|_{\tilde{L}_{t}^{2}(\dot{B}^{\frac{1}{2}}_{2,1})}
is no guarantee that the constructed velocity field

u
has a gradient in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{+};L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
, which is crucial for solving hyperbolic-parabolic systems arising from fluid dynamics, particularly those with a convection-diffusion structure. For example, the global-in-time bound for the density and the issue of uniqueness are closely tied to the Lipschitz control of the velocity field in most evolutionary compressible fluid mechanics models. Therefore, in our framework, another key challenge is to bound the quantity

\int_{0}^{\infty}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}d\tau
.
To achieve it, employing the similar idea in the proof of Proposition
3.1
, we further exploit some extra time-weighted energy estimates for the velocity field (see Propositions
3.2
-
3.5
). Base on these time-weighted estimates, interpolation results, Hölder estimates in Lorentz spaces with respect to the time variable and the crucial part of the argument involving the property that
\|t^{-\alpha}1_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\|_{L_{\frac{1}{\alpha},\infty}}=1
for any
\alpha>0
, we eventually get Lipschitz control of the velocity field. Then we can immediately derive a global-in-time bound for the density in the
L^{\infty}
-norm by using the energy method. Additionally,
for the uniqueness of solutions, the direct method based on stability
estimates for the system (
1.8
) is bound to fail owing to the hyperbolicity of the mass equation:
one loses one derivative, and one cannot afford any loss as
\rho
is not regular
enough. Consequently, the uniqueness issue is non-trivial due to the roughness of the density and the hyperbolic nature of the continuity equation. To address this problem, we shall use the Lagrangian coordinates defined by the stream lines, which is motivated by
[
14
,
15
,
36
]
. The advantage of this approach is that it allows us to convert the hyperbolic-parabolic coupled system into a parabolic system avoiding the loss of one derivative due to the hyperbolic part of system (
1.8
), which makes the uniqueness issues more manageable compared to solving the system in Eulerian coordinates.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In the next section, we review some fundamental concepts related to Littlewood-Paley decomposition, Besov and Lorentz spaces, relevant product estimates, and other useful results. In Section
3
, we provide the proof of global existence as stated in Theorem
1.1
. Finally, in the last section, we establish the uniqueness result presented in Theorem
1.1
.

Notations.
We assume
C

to be a positive generic constant that may vary in different contexts throughout this paper. We denote
A\leq CB
by
A\lesssim B
.

2
Preliminaries

This section reviews various tools, including the Littlewood-Paley decomposition, Besov and Lorentz spaces, relevant product estimates, and useful propositions that will be referenced throughout the paper.

We start with the Littlewood-Paley decomposition. Choose a radial function

\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
valued in the interval
[0,1]
and
supported in

\mathcal{C}=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3},\frac{3}{4}\leq|\xi|\leq\frac{8}{3}\}
such
that

\sum\limits_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\varphi(2^{-j}\xi)=1\quad\mbox{for}\,\,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\}.

For the tempered distribution
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, the homogeneous frequency localization operators
\dot{\Delta}_{j}
and
\dot{S}_{j}
are defined by

\dot{\Delta}_{j}f=\varphi(2^{-j}D)f\quad\mbox{for}\quad\dot{S}_{j}f=\sum\limits_{j^{\prime}\leq j-1}\dot{\Delta}_{j^{\prime}}f.

With our choice of
\varphi
, one can easily verify that

\dot{\Delta}_{q}\dot{\Delta}_{k}f=0\quad\mbox{for}\,\,|q-k|\geq 2\quad\mbox{and}\quad\dot{\Delta}_{q}(\dot{S}_{k-1}f\dot{\Delta}_{k}f)=0\quad\mbox{for}\,\,|q-k|\geq 5.

We denote by
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{h}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
the space of tempered
distribution such that, for any
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
,

\lim\limits_{j\rightarrow-\infty}\dot{S}_{j}f=0

in the distributional sense. For any
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, the
following decomposition holds

f=\sum\limits_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\dot{\Delta}_{j}f,\quad f\in\mathcal{S}_{h}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{3}).

We first recall the definition of homogeneous
Besov spaces (see, e.g.,
[
1
,
33
]
).

Definition 2.1
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
1\leq p,r\leq+\infty
. The homogeneous Besov space

\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
is defined by

\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3})=\Big\{f\in\mathcal{S}^{{}^{\prime}}_{h}(\mathbb{R}^{3}):\|f\|_{\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}<\infty\Big\},

where

\|f\|_{\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}\big\|2^{js}\|\dot{\triangle}_{j}f\|_{L^{p}}\big\|_{l^{r}}.

We also introduce the following Besov-Chemin-Lerner spaces

\widetilde{L}^{q}_{T}(\dot{B}^{s}_{p,r})
which is initiated in

[
11
]
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
s\leq\frac{3}{p}
(respectively
s\in\mathbb{R}
),

(r,\rho,p)\in[1,\,+\infty]^{3}
and
T\in(0,\,+\infty]
. We
define
\widetilde{L}^{\rho}_{T}(\dot{B}^{s}_{p\,r})
as the completion of
C([0,T];\mathcal{S}_{h}^{\prime})
by the norm

\|f\|_{\tilde{L}^{\rho}_{T}(\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s})}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}\Big\|2^{js}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}f(t)\|_{L^{\rho}(0,T;L^{p})}\Big\|_{\ell^{r}}<\infty,

with the usual change if
r=\infty.

Obviously,
\tilde{L}^{1}_{T}(\dot{B}_{p,1}^{s})=L^{1}_{T}(\dot{B}_{p,1}^{s})
, by a direct application of Minkowski’s inequality, we have the following relations
between these spaces

L^{\rho}_{T}(\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s})\hookrightarrow\tilde{L}^{\rho}_{T}(\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s}),\quad r\geq\rho,

\tilde{L}^{\rho}_{T}(\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s})\hookrightarrow L^{\rho}_{T}(\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s}),\quad\rho\geq r.

Here, we also present the definition of the Lorentz space.

Definition 2.3
.

Given
f
a measurable function on a measure space
(X,\mu)
and
1\leq p,r\leq\infty
, we define

\tilde{\|}f\|_{L^{p,r}(\mathbb{X},\mu)}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}\big(t^{\frac{1}{p}}f^{*}(t)\big)^{r}\frac{dt}{t}\Big)^{\frac{1}{r}}&\text{if}\quad r<\infty,\\
\sup_{t>0}\;t^{\frac{1}{p}}f^{*}(t)&\text{if}\quad r=\infty,\\
\end{array}\right.

where

f^{*}(t):=\inf\big\{s\geq 0:\mu(\{|f|>s\})\leq t\big\}.

The set of all
f
with
\tilde{\|}f\|_{L^{p,r}(\mathbb{X},\mu)}<\infty
is called the Lorentz space with indices
p
and
r
.

Remark 2.4
.

Since
L^{p,p}(X,\mu)
coincide with the Lebesgue space
L^{p}(X,\mu)
. Then, the Lorentz spaces may be endowed with the following quasi-norm,

\|f\|_{L^{p,r}(\mathbb{X},\mu)}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}p^{\frac{1}{r}}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}\big(s\;\mu(\{|f|>s\})^{\frac{1}{p}}\big)^{r}\frac{ds}{s}\Big)^{\frac{1}{r}}&\text{if}\quad r<\infty,\\
\sup_{s>0}s\;\mu(\{|f|>s\})^{\frac{1}{p}}&\text{if}\quad r=\infty.\end{array}\right.

The following classical properties of Lorentz spaces
L^{p,q}
may be found in
[
23
]
.

Proposition 2.5
.

For
1<p,p_{1},p_{2}<\infty
and
1\leq r,r_{1},r_{2}\leq\infty
, we have

(1) Interpolation. For all
1\leq r,q\leq\infty
and
\theta\in(0,1)
, we have

\big(L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{R}_{+};L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3}));L^{p_{2}}(\mathbb{R}_{+};L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\big)_{\theta,r}=L^{p,r}\big(\mathbb{R}_{+};L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\big),

where
1<p_{1}<p<p_{2}<\infty
are such that
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{(1-\theta)}{p_{1}}+\frac{\theta}{p_{2}}
.

(2) Hölder inequality. If
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}
, and
\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{r_{1}}+\frac{1}{r_{2}},
we have

\|fg\|_{L^{p,r}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p_{1},r_{1}}}\|g\|_{L^{p_{2},r_{2}}}.

(3) For any
t>0
, we have
\|t^{-\alpha}1_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\|_{L^{\frac{1}{\alpha},\infty}}=1
.

The following Bernstein’s inequality will be frequently used (see
[
10
]
).

The proof of global existence in Theorem

This section is dedicated to proving the global existence of solutions to the Cauchy problem (

). Due to the length of the proof, it has been divided into several subsections for clarity.

3.1
Global
a priori
estimates for the velocity
u

Assuming that (
1.12
) holds, we will first establish the global
a priori
estimates for the velocity
u
by combining the energy method with the proof of Lemma
2.9
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Under the assumptions of (
1.10
) and (
1.11
), let
(\rho,u)
be a smooth enough solution of the Cauchy problem (
1.8
)-(
1.9
). Then we have

\|u\|_{\tilde{L}_{t}^{\infty}(\dot{B}^{\frac{1}{2}}_{2,1})}+\|\nabla u\|_{\tilde{L}_{t}^{2}(\dot{B}^{\frac{1}{2}}_{2,1})}\leq C\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{B}^{\frac{1}{2}}_{2,1}}\quad\text{for}\quad t>0.

(3.1)

Proof.
To bound (
3.1
), we first study the following coupled system:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\rho(\partial_{t}u_{j}+u\cdot\nabla u_{j})-\mu\Delta u_{j}-(\lambda+\mu)\nabla{\rm div}u_{j}=0,\\
&u_{j}|_{t=0}=\Delta_{j}u_{0},\end{aligned}\right.

(3.2)

where
\{m_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a sequence of smooth functions satisfying
\mathrm{supp}m_{j}\subset 2^{j}\mathcal{C}
and
\Big\|\{2^{js}\|m_{j}\|_{L^{p}}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big\|_{l^{r}}<\infty
with
0<s<\frac{d}{p}(1\leq p,r\leq\infty)
or
s=\frac{d}{p}(r=1)
.
Then we deduce from the uniqueness of local smooth solution to the Cauchy problem (
1.8
)-(
1.9
) on
[0,T^{\ast})
that

u=\sum\limits_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}u_{j},

(3.3)

which together the low-high frequency decomposition and Bernstein’s inequality in Lemma
2.6
immediately yields that

\begin{split}&\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(L^{2})}+\|\nabla\dot{\Delta}_{j}u\|_{L_{t}^{2}(L^{2})}\\
&\quad\lesssim\sum\limits_{j^{\prime}>j}\Big(\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}u_{j^{\prime}}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(L^{2})}+\|\nabla\dot{\Delta}{j}u_{j^{\prime}}\|_{L_{t}^{2}(L^{2})}\Big)\\
&\qquad+2^{-j}\sum\limits_{j^{\prime}\leq j}\Big(\|\nabla\dot{\Delta}_{j}u_{j^{\prime}}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(L^{2})}+\|\nabla^{2}\dot{\Delta}_{j}u_{j^{\prime}}\|_{L_{t}^{2}(L^{2})}\Big)\\
&\quad\lesssim\sum\limits_{j^{\prime}>j}\Big(\|u_{j^{\prime}}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(L^{2})}+\|\nabla u_{j^{\prime}}\|_{L_{t}^{2}(L^{2})}\Big)\\
&\qquad+2^{-j}\sum\limits_{j^{\prime}\leq j}\Big(\|\nabla u_{j^{\prime}}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(L^{2})}+\|\nabla^{2}u_{j^{\prime}}\|_{L_{t}^{2}(L^{2})}\Big).\end{split}

(3.4)

Now, we first bound the terms
\|u_{j}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(L^{2})}+\|\nabla u_{j}\|_{L_{t}^{2}(L^{2})}
in the above inequality as follows. To begin with, taking the
L^{2}
-scalar product of the first equation of the system (
3.2
) with
u_{j}
, we write

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho|u_{j}|^{2}dx+\mu\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u_{j}|^{2}dx+(\lambda+\mu)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|{\rm div}u_{j}|^{2}dx=0.

Integrating the above equation over
[0,t]
, we have

\frac{1}{2}\|\sqrt{\rho}u_{j}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(L^{2})}^{2}+\mu\|\nabla u_{j}\|_{L_{t}^{2}(L^{2})}^{2}+(\lambda+\mu)\|{\rm div}u_{j}\|_{L_{t}^{2}(L^{2})}^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\|\sqrt{\rho}\dot{\Delta}_{j}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

and thus there holds

\|u_{j}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(L^{2})}+\|\nabla u_{j}\|_{L_{t}^{2}(L^{2})}+\|{\rm div}u_{j}\|_{L_{t}^{2}(L^{2})}\leq C\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\leq Cd_{j}2^{-\frac{j}{2}}\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{B}^{\frac{1}{2}}_{2,1}}

(3.5)

with
\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}d_{j}=1
.

For the term
\|\nabla u_{j}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(L^{2})}+\|\nabla^{2}u_{j}\|_{L_{t}^{2}(L^{2})}
, taking
L^{2}
inner product of the momentum equation of (
3.2
) with
\partial_{t}u_{j}
, and using Hölder’s inequality and
\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\hookrightarrow L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, we obtain

\begin{split}&\|\sqrt{\rho}\partial_{t}u_{j}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\mu}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla u_{j}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\lambda+\mu}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|{\rm div}u_{j}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}-\rho u\cdot\nabla u_{j}\cdot\partial_{t}u_{j}dx\\
&\leq C\|u\|_{L^{3}}\|\nabla u_{j}\|_{L^{6}}\|\sqrt{\rho}\partial_{t}u_{j}\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\leq C\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\|\nabla^{2}u_{j}\|_{L^{2}}\|\sqrt{\rho}\partial_{t}u_{j}\|_{L^{2}}.\end{split}

(3.6)

For second order derivatives of
u_{j}
, from (
3.2
), we get

-\mu\Delta u_{j}-(\lambda+\mu)\nabla{\rm div}u_{j}=-\rho\partial_{t}u_{j}-\rho u\cdot\nabla u_{j},

(3.7)

which together with the classical regularity theory of the Lamé system implies that

\begin{split}\|\nabla^{2}u_{j}\|_{L^{2}}&\leq C(\|\sqrt{\rho}\partial_{t}u_{j}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\rho u\cdot\nabla u_{j}\|_{L^{2}})\\
&\leq C(\|\sqrt{\rho}\partial_{t}u_{j}\|_{L^{2}}+\|u\|_{L^{3}}\|\nabla u_{j}\|_{L^{6}})\\
&\leq C(\|\sqrt{\rho}\partial_{t}u_{j}\|_{L^{2}}+\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\|\nabla^{2}u_{j}\|_{L^{2}}).\end{split}

Denoting

T_{1}^{\ast}\overset{\textrm{def}}{=}\sup\big\{t<T^{\ast}:\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}})}\leq c_{1}\big\}.

(3.8)

Then for
c_{1}
in (
3.8
) being so small that
Cc_{1}\leq\frac{1}{2}
, thus we have for
t\leq T_{1}^{*}
that

\|\nabla^{2}u_{j}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|\sqrt{\rho}\partial_{t}u_{j}\|_{L^{2}}.

(3.9)

Combining (
3.6
) with (
3.9
) and
Cc_{1}\leq\frac{1}{2}
, we have for
t\leq T_{1}^{*}
that

\|\sqrt{\rho}\partial_{t}u_{j}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{d}{dt}(\|\nabla u_{j}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|{\rm div}u_{j}\|_{L^{2}}^{2})\leq 0,

which together with (
3.9
) gives

\begin{split}&\|\nabla u_{j}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(L^{2})}+\|{\rm div}u_{j}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(L^{2})}+\|(\nabla^{2}u_{j},\sqrt{\rho}\partial_{t}u_{j})\|_{L_{t}^{2}(L^{2})}\\
&\leq C\|\nabla u_{j}(0)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|\nabla\dot{\Delta}_{j}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\leq Cd_{j}2^{\frac{j}{2}}\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{B}^{\frac{1}{2}}_{2,1}}.\end{split}

(3.10)

Substituting (
3.5
) and (
3.10
) into (
3.4
), we infer that

\begin{split}&\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(L^{2})}+\|\nabla\dot{\Delta}_{j}u\|_{L_{t}^{2}(L^{2})}\leq Cd_{j}2^{-\frac{j}{2}}\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{B}^{\frac{1}{2}}_{2,1}},\end{split}

which implies that

\|u\|_{\tilde{L}_{t}^{\infty}(\dot{B}^{\frac{1}{2}}_{2,1})}+\|\nabla u\|_{\tilde{L}_{t}^{2}(\dot{B}^{\frac{1}{2}}_{2,1})}\leq C\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{B}^{\frac{1}{2}}_{2,1}}\quad\text{for}\quad t\leq T_{1}^{*}.

Taking
\varepsilon_{0}
in (
1.12
) so small that
C\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{B}^{\frac{1}{2}}_{2,1}}\leq C\varepsilon_{0}\leq\frac{c_{1}}{2}
for
c_{1}
given by (
3.8
). Thus, we obtain by using a continuous argument, that
T^{\ast}_{1}
determined by (
3.8
) equals any number smaller than
T^{\ast}
. That is,

\|u\|_{\tilde{L}_{t}^{\infty}(\dot{B}^{\frac{1}{2}}_{2,1})}+\|\nabla u\|_{\tilde{L}_{t}^{2}(\dot{B}^{\frac{1}{2}}_{2,1})}\leq C\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{B}^{\frac{1}{2}}_{2,1}}\quad\text{for}\quad t\leq T^{\ast}.

(3.11)

Finally, we will show
T^{\ast}=+\infty
. Let
c_{2}
be a small enough positive constant, which will be determined later on. Define

T\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}\max\Big\{t\in[0,T^{*}):\|u\|_{\tilde{L}_{t}^{\infty}(\dot{B}^{\frac{1}{2}}_{2,1})}+\|\nabla u\|_{\tilde{L}_{t}^{2}(\dot{B}^{\frac{1}{2}}_{2,1})}\leq c_{2}\Big\}.

(3.12)

We claim that
T=+\infty
provided that there holds (
1.11
). Indeed, taking
c_{2}=2C\varepsilon_{0}
, for
t\leq T
, we deduce from (
3.11
) that

\|u\|_{\tilde{L}_{t}^{\infty}(\dot{B}^{\frac{1}{2}}_{2,1})}+\|\nabla u\|_{\tilde{L}_{t}^{2}(\dot{B}^{\frac{1}{2}}_{2,1})}\leq C\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{B}^{\frac{1}{2}}_{2,1}}\leq C\varepsilon_{0}=\frac{c_{2}}{2},

which contradicts (
3.12
). Thus, we conclude that
T=T^{*}=\infty
, and there holds (
3.1
) for any
t>0
. This completes the proof of Proposition
3.1
.

3.2
Some time weight estimates for the velocity
u

Assuming that (
1.12
) holds, we shall exploit some time weight estimates for the velocity
u
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Under the assumptions of Proposition
3.1
, we have

\|\sqrt{t}\nabla u\|_{\tilde{L}_{t}^{\infty}(\dot{B}^{\frac{1}{2}}_{2,1})}+\|\sqrt{t}\partial_{t}u\|_{\tilde{L}_{t}^{2}(\dot{B}^{\frac{1}{2}}_{2,1})}\leq C\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{B}^{\frac{1}{2}}_{2,1}}\quad\text{for}\quad t>0.

(3.13)

Proof.
Similar to the derivation of (
3.4
), we have

\begin{split}&\|\sqrt{t}\dot{\Delta}_{j}\nabla u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(L^{2})}+\|\sqrt{t}\dot{\Delta}_{j}\partial_{t}u\|_{L_{t}^{2}(L^{2})}\\
&\lesssim\sum\limits_{j^{\prime}>j}\Big(\|\sqrt{t}\dot{\Delta}_{j}\nabla u_{j^{\prime}}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(L^{2})}+\|\sqrt{t}\dot{\Delta}_{j}\partial_{t}u_{j^{\prime}}\|_{L_{t}^{2}(L^{2})}\Big)\\
&\quad+2^{-j}\sum\limits_{j^{\prime}\leq j}\Big(\|\sqrt{t}\dot{\Delta}_{j}\nabla^{2}u_{j^{\prime}}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(L^{2})}+\|\sqrt{t}\dot{\Delta}_{j}\nabla\partial_{t}u_{j^{\prime}}\|_{L_{t}^{2}(L^{2})}\Big)\\
&\lesssim\sum\limits_{j^{\prime}>j}\Big(\|\sqrt{t}\nabla u_{j^{\prime}}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(L^{2})}+\|\sqrt{t}\partial_{t}u_{j^{\prime}}\|_{L_{t}^{2}(L^{2})}\Big)\\
&\quad+2^{-j}\sum\limits_{j^{\prime}\leq j}\Big(\|\sqrt{t}\nabla^{2}u_{j^{\prime}}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(L^{2})}+\|\sqrt{t}\nabla\partial_{t}u_{j^{\prime}}\|_{L_{t}^{2}(L^{2})}\Big).\end{split}

(3.14)

In what follows, we will bound the last two terms in the above inequality. For the terms
\|\sqrt{t}\nabla u_{j}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(L^{2})}
and
\|\sqrt{t}\partial_{t}u_{j}\|_{L_{t}^{2}(L^{2})}
, it follows from (
3.6
) that

\begin{split}&\frac{\mu}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla u_{j}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\lambda+\mu}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|{\rm div}u_{j}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\sqrt{\rho}\partial_{t}u_{j}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\\
&\leq C\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla u_{j}\|_{L^{2}}\|\sqrt{\rho}\partial_{t}u_{j}\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\leq C\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\|\nabla u_{j}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}\|\sqrt{\rho}\partial_{t}u_{j}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.\end{split}

Multiplying the above inequality by
t
and using
\dot{B}^{\frac{3}{2}}_{2,1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
in Lemma
2.8
yield that

\begin{split}&\frac{d}{dt}(\|\sqrt{t}\nabla u_{j}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\sqrt{t}{\rm div}u_{j}\|_{L^{2}}^{2})+\|\sqrt{t}\sqrt{\rho}\partial_{t}u_{j}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\\
&\leq C\|\nabla u_{j}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{\frac{3}{2}}_{2,1}}^{2}\|\sqrt{t}\nabla u_{j}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.\end{split}

Applying Gronwall’s inequality and then using (
3.1
) and (
3.5
), we have

\begin{split}&\|\sqrt{t}\nabla u_{j}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(L^{2})}^{2}+\|\sqrt{t}{\rm div}u_{j}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(L^{2})}^{2}+\|\sqrt{t}\sqrt{\rho}\partial_{t}u_{j}\|_{L_{t}^{2}(L^{2})}^{2}\\
&\leq C\|\nabla u_{j}\|_{L_{t}^{2}(L^{2})}^{2}\exp\Big(C\|u\|_{L^{2}_{t}(\dot{B}^{\frac{3}{2}}_{2,1})}^{2}\Big)\\
&\leq Cd_{j}^{2}2^{-j}\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{\dot{B}^{\frac{1}{2}}_{2,1}}.\end{split}

This along with (
3.9
) implies that

\|\sqrt{t}\nabla u_{j}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(L^{2})}+\|\sqrt{t}{\rm div}u_{j}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(L^{2})}+\|\sqrt{t}(\nabla^{2}u_{j},\sqrt{\rho}\partial_{t}u_{j})\|_{L_{t}^{2}(L^{2})}\leq Cd_{j}2^{-\frac{j}{2}}\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{B}^{\frac{1}{2}}_{2,1}}.

(3.15)

Next, in order to bound
\|\sqrt{t}\nabla^{2}u_{j}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(L^{2})}
and
\|\sqrt{t}\nabla\partial_{t}u_{j}\|_{L_{t}^{2}(L^{2})}
in (
3.14
), applying
\partial_{t}
to the first equation of the system (
3.2
) yields that

\begin{split}&\rho\partial_{t}^{2}u_{j}+\rho u\cdot\nabla\partial_{t}u_{j}-\mu\Delta\partial_{t}u_{j}-(\lambda+\mu)\nabla{\rm div}\partial_{t}u_{j}\\
&=-\rho_{t}D_{t}u_{j}-\rho\partial_{t}u\nabla u_{j}.\end{split}

(3.16)

Taking the
L^{2}
-scalar product of the first equation of the system (
3.16
) with
\partial_{t}u_{j}
, we obtain

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\sqrt{\rho}\partial_{t}u_{j}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\mu\|\nabla\partial_{t}u_{j}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+(\lambda+\mu)\|{\rm div}\partial_{t}u_{j}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\\
&=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho_{t}D_{t}u_{j}\partial_{t}u_{j}dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho\partial_{t}u\cdot\nabla u_{j}\partial_{t}u_{j}dx.\end{split}

(3.17)

We bound term by term above in what follows. For the term
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho_{t}D_{t}u_{j}\partial_{t}u_{j}dx
, we infer, using the definition of material derivative, that

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho_{t}D_{t}u_{j}\partial_{t}u_{j}dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho_{t}|\partial_{t}u_{j}|^{2}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho_{t}u\nabla u_{j}\cdot\partial_{t}u_{j}dx.\end{split}

(3.18)

For the term
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho_{t}|\partial_{t}u_{j}|^{2}dx
in (
3.18
), by virtue of the transport equation of (
1.8
), we get, by using integration by parts, the embedding

\dot{B}_{2,1}^{\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
in Lemma
2.8
, Hölder’s and Young’s inequalities, that

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho_{t}|\partial_{t}u_{j}|^{2}dx&\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}{\rm div}(\rho u)|\partial_{t}u_{j}|^{2}dx\\
&\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho u\cdot\nabla|\partial_{t}u_{j}|^{2}dx\\
&\leq C\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\sqrt{\rho}\partial_{t}u_{j}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\partial_{t}u_{j}\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\leq C\|u\|_{\dot{B}_{2,1}^{\frac{3}{2}}}^{2}\|\sqrt{\rho}\partial_{t}u_{j}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{16}\|\nabla\partial_{t}u_{j}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\end{split}

For the term
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho_{t}u\nabla u_{j}\cdot\partial_{t}u_{j}dx
in (
3.18
), by virtue of the transport equation of (
1.8
) and using integration by parts, we have

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho_{t}u\nabla u_{j}&\cdot\partial_{t}u_{j}dx\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}{\rm div}(\rho u)u\cdot\nabla u_{j}\partial_{t}u_{j}dx\\
&\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho u\nabla u\cdot\nabla u_{j}\partial_{t}u_{j}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho u\cdot u\cdot\nabla^{2}u_{j}\partial_{t}u_{j}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho u\cdot u\cdot\nabla u_{j}\cdot\nabla\partial_{t}u_{j}dx.\end{split}

By using (
3.9
), Hölder’s inequality, embeddings
\dot{B}_{2,1}^{\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and
\dot{B}_{2,1}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\hookrightarrow L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
in Lemma
2.8
, we obtain

\begin{split}\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho u\nabla u\cdot\nabla u_{j}\partial_{t}u_{j}dx\Big|&\leq C\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{3}}\|\nabla u_{j}\|_{L^{6}}\|\sqrt{\rho}\partial_{t}u_{j}\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\leq C\|u\|_{\dot{B}_{2,1}^{\frac{3}{2}}}^{2}\|\nabla^{2}u_{j}\|_{L^{2}}\|\sqrt{\rho}\partial_{t}u_{j}\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\leq C\|u\|_{\dot{B}_{2,1}^{\frac{3}{2}}}^{2}\|\sqrt{\rho}\partial_{t}u_{j}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.\end{split}

Similarly, we can deal with the term
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho u\cdot u\cdot\nabla^{2}u_{j}\partial_{t}u_{j}dx
:

\begin{split}\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho u\cdot u\cdot\nabla^{2}u_{j}\partial_{t}u_{j}dx\Big|&\leq C\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{2}u_{j}\|_{L^{2}}\|\sqrt{\rho}\partial_{t}u_{j}\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\leq C\|u\|_{\dot{B}_{2,1}^{\frac{3}{2}}}^{2}\|\sqrt{\rho}\partial_{t}u_{j}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.\end{split}

Moreover, along the same line, we have

\begin{split}\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho u\cdot u\cdot\nabla u_{j}\nabla\partial_{t}u_{j}dx\Big|&\leq C\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u\|_{L^{3}}\|\nabla u_{j}\|_{L^{6}}\|\nabla\partial_{t}u_{j}\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\leq C\|u\|_{\dot{B}_{2,1}^{\frac{3}{2}}}\|u\|_{\dot{B}_{2,1}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\|\nabla^{2}u_{j}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\partial_{t}u_{j}\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\leq C\|u\|_{\dot{B}_{2,1}^{\frac{3}{2}}}^{2}\|u\|_{\dot{B}_{2,1}^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}\|\sqrt{\rho}\partial_{t}u_{j}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{16}\|\nabla\partial_{t}u_{j}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.\end{split}

As a result, it comes out

\begin{split}\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho_{t}D_{t}u_{j}\partial_{t}u_{j}dx\Big|\leq C(1+\|u\|_{\dot{B}_{2,1}^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2})\|u\|_{\dot{B}_{2,1}^{\frac{3}{2}}}^{2}\|\sqrt{\rho}\partial_{t}u_{j}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{16}\|\nabla\partial u_{j}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.\end{split}

&\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{B}_{2,1}^{\frac{1}{2}}}.\end{split}

\varepsilon_{0}
in (
1.11
) is small enough, we complete the proof of uniqueness of Theorem
1.1
.

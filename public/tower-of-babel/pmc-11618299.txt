The potential of Ti3C2Tx-KH2PO4 and Ti3C2Tx-chitosan in the efficient removal of cesium from nuclear wastewater
Authors: Shanli Nezami, Ahad Ghaemi, Taher Yousefi
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractTi3C2Tx is synthesized from Ti3AlC2 by two common methods, HF and HF in situ. The synthesis approach is very practical regarding the structure, morphology, space between layers, type and number of surface-active sites and its specific surface. XRD, SEM, EDS, FTIR and BET analyzes were used to investigate the structure, morphology, type and number of surface-active sites. Under the operating conditions of cesium initial concentration ~ 150 ppm, ambient temperature, pH ~ 7.00 and time of 60 min, the cesium adsorption intensity with Ti3C2Tx-HF and Ti3C2Tx-HF in situ was obtained as 194 and 219.5 mg.g− 1, respectively. The structural results of modification with KH2PO4 show that in this modification, in addition to the increase of hydroxyl functional groups, the distance between the layers has also increased and the cesium adsorption intensity has increased to 338.75 mg.g− 1 under the above operating conditions. Meanwhile, in modification with Chitosan by increasing the frequency of functional groups and specific surface up to 3 times the effective specific surface of Ti3C2Tx-HF in situ, no significant change in the cesium adsorption intensity has been observed (247.5 mg.g− 1). Experiments were conducted to evaluate the effect of parameters of initial concentration of Cs+ (250 − 50 ppm), time (30–60 min), ambient temperature (298.15–318.15 K), solution pH (3.0–11.0) with the help of RSM design. RSM results show that the pH parameter is one of the most important parameters affecting the cesium adsorption intensity with Ti3C2(OH)x and Ti3C2(OH)x-KH2PO4. Also, with the increase in temperature, the adsorption intensity should increase. It is shown in the isotherm modeling that R2 matches well with the Freundlich isotherm model, which is based on the layered structure of Ti3C2(OH)x and Ti3C2(OH)x-KH2PO4 and the presence of active sites with different energy levels, which leads to heterogeneous adsorption. The consistency will be of the adsorbent selectivity investigation shows that the Cs+ hydration radius plays a decisive role in its high adsorption potential. Reduction of MXene by 0.1 M HCl was carried out in 3 steps. The experimental results show that 15% absorption has been achieved in the third stage. Introduction

The need for energy increases with the increase in world population. In recent decades, nuclear energy has caused environmental pollution with radionuclides of uranium, thorium, cesium, strontium, etc1,2. Cesium 135 and Cesium 137 radionuclides are among the most dangerous radionuclides in spent nuclear fuel, with a high activity level and a half-life of 30 and 106 years, respectively, soluble in water and easily entering the environment3. Cesium (Cs+) enters the body through the digestive system, accumulates in the muscles, and leads to cancer. The World Environmental Health Organization has declared the permissible limit of Cs+ in the environment to be 0.03 ppb4. The chemical toxicity, carcinogenicity and long half-life of these radionuclides are dangerous for human health and other living organisms. Therefore, it is necessary to purify the environment from these radionuclides. Among the various technologies such as biological purification, membrane purification, advanced oxidation processes and electrochemical methods that are used for environmental purification, surface adsorption with features such as simplicity of the process, cost-effectiveness, non-degradation of the environment, high efficiency, ease of separation Phases and availability have been proposed as an effective and promising method5–9. In this field, various adsorbents such as such as silicon mesopores10, porous organic polymers11, metal-organic frameworks12,13 and graphene metal oxides14 have been used to absorb radionuclides. However, the low adsorption capacity, slow kinetics and improper selectivity of some of these adsorbents have limited their widespread implementation. Therefore, research is being done to develop cost-effective and efficient adsorbents with good selectivity. In recent years, two-dimensional nanomaterials (2DMs) with their unique structure, high specific surface area, and many active sites for effective interaction with the adsorbent have attracted the attention of researchers as effective adsorbents in the radionuclide’s adsorption. In 2011, the 2DMs family of carbides or nitrates transition metal, known as MXenes, with their environmentally friendly properties, high specific surface area, high chemical stability, and hydrophilic nature have attracted tremendous attention in environmental applications including surface adsorption, photocatalysis and membrane filtration is used to remove pollutants and adsorb organic and inorganic pollutants15. The first MXene sample, Ti3C2Tx, was synthesized by selective exfoliation of the Al layer from the crystal structure and the layer-by-layer MAX phase of Ti3AlC2. MXenes are known by the general formula Mn+1XnTx, in which M represents transition metals (such as Sc, Ti, Zr, Hf, V, Nb, Ta, Cr, Mo, and Mn) and n = 1–3. X is carbon, nitrogen, or both, and Tx is surface active sites (such as O-, OH-, F-, and Cl-)16–21. Compared to their mother phase, the MAX phase, MXenes have appeared with unique properties. The MXenes properties can be controlled by the synthesis method of the selected MAX phase. The MXene surface is covered by hydroxyl, oxygen or fluorine, and the type and ratio of each of these compounds or elements mainly depends on the synthesis method22,23. One of the most attractive features of MXenes is the surface active sites that allow extensive electrochemical interactions with pollutants. Meanwhile, titanium-based MXene has been of interest due to the abundance of elements, the absence of poisonous products, the abundance of surface hydroxyl groups, and the high stability in surface adsorption and environmental applications. In recent years, surface adsorption of Cs+ radionuclide with Ti3C2Tx has been investigated. The effective adsorption potential of Cs+ compared to other two-dimensional adsorbents has been reported with wide surface active sites, adjustable interlayer space and effective specific surface area. In 2019, Khan et al. investigated the Cs+ adsorption by Ti3C2Tx synthesized with NaF/HCl and reported the intensity of Cs+ adsorption by Ti3C2Tx at 25.41 mg.g-1 in 30 min24. In 2021, Rethinasabapathy et al. showed that by structural modification of Ti3C2Tx, increasing the interlayer space by encapsulating POSS-NH2 among MXene layers, the Cs+adsorption potential can be increased25. In 2022, in a similar work, it was shown that by encapsulating KOH among the layers, the space increased among the layers, and as a result, the Cs+ adsorption intensity increased26. In both works, high-concentration HF has been used for synthesis. Therefore, the adsorption potential can be increased by modifying the structure and increasing the space among the layers and the volume of the pores. However, due to the effect of the synthesis method on the structure and morphology of the synthesized MXene and the type and number of active sites that play a decisive role in the adsorption of target pollutants by establishing electrostatic interactions, the Cs+ adsorption potential on the synthesis method has not been investigated and interpreted. It is necessary to make effective corrections in increasing the number of active sites, the space among the layers, the effective specific surface, and their effect on Cs+ adsorption. The most prominent feature of MXenes is that the MXenes structural properties can be controlled by its selective synthesis method. The structural characteristics of MXene, in other words, their adjustable interlayer distance, the type and abundance of surface functional groups and their effective specific surface area, as a result of the selective synthesis method, are different from each other, and each can play a decisive role in the adsorption of radionuclides such as Cs+. To determine the contribution of each of these effective features in Cs+ adsorption, the synthesis of Ti3C2Tx is carried out by both conventional HF and HF in situ methods. Next, the Cs+ adsorption on MXene structure (space between layers, effective specific surface area, type and number of active sites) is investigated on common synthesis methods. Until now, the Cs+ adsorption or other radionuclides has only been investigated by a synthesis method, and the determining role of each of these unique structural features of MXenes in adsorption has not been determined. By modifying the structure or morphology of Ti3C2Tx with KH2PO4 and chitosan, Cs+ adsorption is interpreted as a result of changes in the unique structural features of MXenes, such as increased surface active sites, increased interlayer space, changes in specific surface area and volume. Pores The purpose of this work was to determine the contribution of each effective feature of MXene in the absorption of heavy metals and to improve it in the conditions required to increase the absorption potential. In this study, the effect of effective parameters on Cs+ adsorption process with Ti3C2Tx and modified MXene is investigated by experimental design and kinetic, isothermal and thermodynamic modeling. The selectivity and regeneration of the adsorbent in order to reuse it in the absorption process is one of the main characteristics of a good adsorbent.

Materials and methods

The materials required for synthesizing Ti3AlC2 and Ti3C2Tx are similar to the authors’ previous research27. CsNO3 (Mw ~ 194.91 g.mol− 1), KH2PO4 (Mw ~ 136.08 g.mol− 1), chitosan (Mw ~ 1526.5 g.mol− 1), NaNO3 (Mw ~ 84.99 g.mol− 1), Ba(NO3)2 (Mw ~ 26.34 g.mol− 1), Ca(NO3)2 (Mw ~ 164.09 g.mol− 1), NaOH (Mw ~ 40 g.mol− 1) and HNO3 (Mw ~ 63.01 g.mol− 1) were purchased from Merck with a purity of 99.99%.

Synthesis of Ti3AlC2and Ti3C2Tx

Ti3AlC2 and Ti3C2Tx were synthesized using the method presented in the authors’ previous research25. Ti3C2Tx synthesis by HF shows Ti3C2Tx-HF-DMSO and Ti3C2Tx synthesis by HF in situ shows Ti3C2Tx-HF in situ.

Modification of Ti3C2Tx

Ti3C2Tx modified with KH2PO4 was modified similarly to the authors’ previous research25. Figure 1 shows Ti3C2Tx modified with KH2PO4.

Ti3C2Tx modification by KH2PO4 and chitosan.

In Ti3C2Tx modification with chitosan, Ti3C2Tx suspension (0.5 g in 100 ml deionized water) is slowly added to the chitosan solution (50 mg in 0.1%v acetic acid) after two hours ultra-sonication. After 24 h of stirring with a magnet, the formed suspension is centrifuged (4500 rpm, 15 min) and the composite surface is washed with deionized distilled water to reach the surface pH ~ 6.00–7.00. Finally, the composite formed on the PVDF membrane (0.22 μm) is collected and dried in a vacuum oven for 24 h at a temperature of 333.15 K. Figure 1 shows the modification of Ti3C2Tx with chitosan. Chitosan-modified Ti3C2Tx is denoted in the report as Ti3C2Tx-Chitosan.

Characterizations

X-ray diffraction (XRD, STOE, STADI, and MP diffractometer), Scanning electron microscopy (SEM, JSM-7500 F FE-SEM, ZEISS EVO 18, Germany), Energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS, JSM-7500 F FE-SEM ZEISS EVO 18, Germany), Fourier transform infrared (FTIR- Nicolet Thermos 360 spectrometer) analyzes, BET (BET, Quantachrome, Nova, 2200) and zeta potential (Nanotrac Wave II, Microtrac, USA) were used to analyze and investigate the structure and morphology of Ti3C2Tx synthesized with HF, HF in situ, Ti3C2Tx-KH2PO4 and Ti3C2Tx-Chitosan. Atomic absorption spectroscopy (AAS, Perkin Elmer, AAnalyst 100) was used to analyze Cs+ concentration.

Adsorption tests

Discontinuous Cs adsorption experiments are performed at the adsorbent dose of 0.6 g.L− 1 from Ti3C2Tx, Ti3C2Tx-KH2PO4, and Ti3C2(OH)x-Chitosan adsorbents. RSM (CCD) experiment design was used to investigate the effect of parameters affecting Cs+ adsorption. The effect of pH was investigated in the range of 3.0–11.0. pH is adjusted by 0.1 M HNO3 or 0.1 M NaOH. The thermodynamic study of the adsorption process was carried out in the temperature range of 298.15–318.15 K, the isotherm study of adsorption in the concentration range of 50–250 ppm, and the study of the kinetics of adsorption in 30–180 min. Response surface methodology (RSM)

Experiment design is a valuable method that, before an experiment is carried out, with the help of data is analysed to obtain validity and objective conclusions. The design of the experiment must be done carefully to ensure that the experiment’s objectives are met and the hypotheses are tested. Experiment design is used for different purposes, such as identifying effective parameters, estimating the effect of parameters on the response, identifying the interaction among parameters, modeling the mathematical relationship between parameters and response, and optimizing the process. After selecting the factors, their levels of change and choosing the proper response variable, the experimental design is selected according to the objective being pursued. To choose the right test design, you should pay attention to points such as randomization, doing the right number of repetitions and tests to be done, and finally, choosing the right design. After designing the experiment and determining the factors, it is carried out based on it. After the test, the answer of each test is placed in the desired section. RSM determines the relation among one (or more than one) dependent variable and unaffiliated variable with a set of mathematical relationships. In each experiment, to determine the causes of changes in the response variable, changes are made in the input variables. Originally, the response surface method was developed for experimental models and then moved to modelling experiments. The RSM scheme was first introduced by Box and Wilson. The Box-Wilson design is based on a completely three-level factorial basis, and the number of tests increases as the number of variables increases. To overcome the increase in the number of experiments in Box and Wilson’s design, designs such as two additional factorial experiment design and central composite design are formed. Results and discussions

Ti3AlC2 and Ti3C2Tx synthesis

Figure 1S shows the XRD spectra of Ti3AlC2, Ti3C2Tx-HF-DMSO and Ti3C2Tx-HF in situ. The complete exfoliation of the Al layer is observed with the absence of the peak belonging to the 104 planes (2θ = 39°) in the XRD spectrum of Ti3C2Tx-HF-DMSO and Ti3C2Tx-HF in situ30–32. Layered structure and increasing space among layers in Ti3C2Tx-HF-DMSO and Ti3C2Tx-HF in situ by shifting the angle 2θ = 9.82° from the Ti3AlC2 XRD spectrum to the angle 2θ = 9.06° and 2θ = 7.75° in the XRD spectrum of Ti3C2Tx-HF-DMSO and Ti3C2Tx-HF in situ are shown, respectively. The interlayer space in Ti3C2Tx is created in proportion to the interlayer space of Ti3AlC2 (0.76 nm) using intercalation, ultrasonic processes, or using large organic molecules such as DMSO. The gap among the larger Ti3C2Tx-HF layer is created in situ by using 12 M LiF/9 M HCl as an etchant solution and placing Li+ ions and water molecules among the layers during the synthesis process19,24,33,34. The results of SEM(Fig. 2S), EDS (Table 2S), BET(Figs. 3S and 4S) structural analyzes for Ti3C2Tx-HF-DMSO and Ti3C2Tx-HF in situ have been presented in the authors’ previous research27. According to the EDS (Table 2S) results, the dominant active group in the synthesis with HF is F− and in the synthesis with HF in situ, O−(OH−). In the synthesis with HF in situ, in addition to F−, O− and OH− active sites, Cl− active group is also formed on the MXene surface. Therefore, throughout the report Ti3C2Tx-HF-DMSO will be referred to as Ti3C2Fx-DMSO and Ti3C2Tx-HF in situ as Ti3C2(OH)x. In the first step of the Cs+ adsorption experiments, considering the structural difference of MXene synthesized by HF and HF in situ methods (space among layers, monolayer and multilayer) and the difference in active sites formed on the surface (F−, O− and OH−), the performance of Ti3C2Fx-DMSO and Ti3C2(OH)x in Cs+ adsorption is investigated and compared. The existence of many surface sites plays a decisive role in the pH of Cs+ solution to investigate the adsorption process with MXene. In highly acidic conditions, pH < 2, with the existence of proton ions in the solution, a strong competition for establishing electrostatic reactions among H+ ions and Cs+ with surface active sites is created. As a result of this competition and the adsorption of proton ions on the surface-active sites of MXene, the surface of MXene will be protonated and as a result the adsorption rate will be very low. By increasing the pH to 7, the concentration of proton ions in the medium decreases and with the deprotonation of MXene surface, many active sites are accessible to positively charged cations to be absorbed by MXene with strong electrochemical attraction forces. Therefore, the adsorption intensity increases. At pH ~ 7, due to the neutrality of the medium, the MXene surface contains extensive active sites that can carry out adsorption with high intensity. With the pH increased (pH > 8), with the increase of hydroxyl groups in the medium and the hydrolysis of cations, the adsorption intensity gradually decreases. Therefore, the most suitable pH to investigate the process of cations adsorption with MXene is neutral pH, except in some cases, such as thorium and uranium, which are hydrolyzed in acidic pH (pH < 3.5)25–27. Due to the slowness of the penetration process and on the other hand, the interlayer space in the Ti3C2Fx-DMSO structure is smaller compared to the interlayer space of Ti3C2(OH)x, the speed of adsorption is very slow, the adsorption process is long and the adsorption intensity is lower than Ti3C2(OH)x has happened. Modification Ti3C2Tx

Figure 3 illustrates the potential of Cs+ adsorption with Ti3C2(OH)x, Ti3C2(OH)x-Chitosan and Ti3C2(OH)x-KH2PO4 (5, 8 & 10%wt). The trend of increasing Cs+ adsorption at 10–60 min is the same with all adsorbents.

It can be claimed that the structure of MXene has not changed as a result of modification with chitosan and KH2PO4. The results of SEM (Figs. 4 and 5) and BET (Figs. 6S and 7S) structural analysis confirm the layered-accordion structure and the mesoporous nature of the holes after modification. The most important features of MXene for their proper performance in adsorbing heavy metals are the existence of surface-active sites (F−, O− and OH−), adjustable interlayer spacing and effective specific surface area. Therefore, to increase the adsorption potential of heavy metals with MXene, it is necessary to increase the effect of each of these features. Therefore, it is necessary to use compounds that have O− and OH− sites in their structure for modification. On the other hand, it should also be kept in mind that in addition to increasing the number of active sites, it can also improve the space among layers or the specific surface area. KH2PO4 having a large tetrahedral structure compared to other commonly used anions for modification such as NO3−, SO3− and ClO4− and the very high tendency of phosphate groups to bond with titanium and carbon in different weight percentages was used for modification. The XRD spectrum of Ti3C2(OH)x-Chitosan is Fig. 5S of show that the angle 2θ ~ 7.65° is equivalent to the angle 2θ ~ 7.74° of Ti3C2(OH)x. As indicated, in modification with chitosan, the transition to a smaller angle does not occur; therefore, the space among the layers in Ti3C2(OH)x-Chitosan is not significantly different from that in Ti3C2(OH)x35,36. These XRD results are completely consistent with the results obtained from SEM. Figure 4 displays the SEM results related to the structure and morphology of Ti3C2(OH)x-Chitosan. As indicated, the space among the layers is almost the same as compared to Ti3C2(OH)x.

The experimental results show a difference of nearly 30 mg.g− 1 in the Cs+ adsorption intensity by Ti3C2(OH)x and Ti3C2(OH)x-Chitosan. Therefore, it is said that a slight increase in the number of active sites has occurred with the modification of chitosan. On the other hand, according to the structure of chitosan, it is a surface modification and the space among the layers is not increased. As the XRD (Fig. 5S) results also show, there was no change in the angle 2θ ~ 7.65°. The experimental results of cesium adsorption potential by Ti3C2(OH)x-KH2PO4 (5, 8 & 10%wt) show that the modification with KH2PO4-5%wt can slightly increase the number of surface-active sites. These results are completely consistent with the SEM analysis results (Fig. 5). As indicated, the among-layer space has increased and very large amounts of KH2PO4 have accumulated over there. This may increase the interaction among Cs+ and modified MXene, and thus, the adsorption potential37. The EDS results in Table 3S show that the increase of active sites’ weight% on the O−(OH−) MXene surface modified with KH2PO4-8%wt compared to Ti3C2(OH)x increases the Cs+-active sites’ interaction; thus, the adsorption capacity increases. Meanwhile, with the increase of active sites, the active sites are increased, too. Hence, the adsorption takes place faster. The difference of 20 mg.g− 1 in the Cs+ adsorption intensity compared to Ti3C2(OH)x indicates no change in the space among the layers and a slight increase in surface active sites (less than Chitosan). Modification by KH2PO4-8 wt% has increased the Cs+ adsorption potential to nearly 340 mg.g− 1. In addition to increasing the space among the layers in modification with KH2PO4, the O−(OH−) surface active sites increase by nearly 7wt%. With the encapsulation of K+ values ​​among the layers, the space among the layers has increased to 12.90 nm, and as a result, a Cs+ large amount can penetrate among the layers and be absorbed on the interlayer surface sites by establishing strong electrochemical forces. Figure 3 illustrates the decrease in adsorption intensity with KH2PO4-10%wt compared to KH2PO4-8%wt. By encapsulating more amounts of KH2PO4 among the layers, Cs+ is prevented from penetrating into the layers, and as a result, adsorption is reduced. On the other hand, the BET results show a 3-fold increase in the Ti3C2(OH)x specific surface area as a result of modification with Chitosan compared to the Ti3C2(OH)x-KH2PO4 specific surface area​​. Therefore, it can be claimed that increasing the space among the layers has a great effect on increasing the Cs+ adsorption intensity with Ti3C2(OH)x compared to other unique structural features of Ti3C2(OH)x such as increasing the specific surface area, the number of active sites and the volume of the holes. By increasing the space among the layers, it is possible for Cs+ to penetrate among the layers and establish electrostatic interactions with active sites, and as a result, the adsorption intensity increases. Therefore, the study of Cs+ adsorption process with Ti3C2(OH)x and Ti3C2(OH)x-KH2PO4 continues. Also, from now on in this report, Ti3C2(OH)x-KH2PO4-8%wt is shown as Ti3C2(OH)x-KH2PO4.

Impact of pH on adsorption capacity

The pH solution is the most important parameter affecting the intensity of adsorption based on electrostatic interactions. Figure 9 indicated the adsorption capacity of Cs+ with Ti3C2(OH)x and Ti3C2(OH)x-KH2PO4 by changing pH. At more acidic pHs (less than 3), the active sites on the modified Ti3C2(OH)x surface are protonated, and at the same time, a very strong competition among H+ and Cs+ for adsorption on the Ti3C2(OH)x surface active sites leads to electrostatic repulsion and finally adsorption. The Cs+ adsorption capacity increases with increased pH. This increase is influenced by the enhanced attractive force among the negative and hydrophilic charges of the modified Ti3C2(OH)x surface and Cs+. At higher pHs (8–11), Cs+ reacts with OH− and hydrolyzes. So, the decreasing adsorption potential has occurred with the existence of Cs (OH−) and electrostatic repulsion among Cs (OH−) and negatively charged surface-active sites on the surface and Ti3C2(OH)x layers. Therefore, mainly the ion exchange among KH2+/H+ controls the Cs+ adsorption potential by Ti3C2(OH)x-KH2PO4. A small part of the adsorption potential occurs by the establishment of electrostatic attractive forces among the negative charges of surface active sites (O−, OH−) and Cs+ ions. To prevent the hydrolysis of Cs+ and the formation of Cs (OH−) followed by the reduction of Cs+ adsorption, the optimal pH for Cs+ adsorption is considered 7.00. By examining the zeta potential for modified and unmodified Ti3C2(OH)x at different pHs, the relationship among surface charge and their adsorption capacity was investigated. Considering that the zeta potential for Ti3C2(OH)x and Ti3C2(OH)x-KH2PO4 is 2.75 and 1.98, respectively. It shows that the accumulation of more negative charge on the surface of MXene leads to an increase in the adsorption capacity of Cs+ and is very favorable for adsorption. Modifying Ti3C2(OH)x leads to a decrease in zeta potential. At high pHs, the surface zeta potential of MXene is negative, and at low pHs, the surface zeta potential of MXene is positive. Therefore, for the optimal adsorption of Cs+ on Ti3C2(OH)x, it is necessary that the pH is greater than the zeta potential24,26. The higher adsorption capacity of Ti3C2(OH)x-KH2PO4 compared to Ti3C2(OH)x (with a more positive zeta potential) is due to interaction among the layers. As a result of intercalation with K+ and increasing the frequency of hydroxyl active sites, more sites are available to Cs+, which increases the adsorption potential with Ti3C2(OH)x-KH2PO4 by establishing extensive electrochemical forces.

Kinetics adsorption

Adsorption kinetics is one of the most important parameters investigated in the adsorption process. The rapidity of the adsorption process is one of the important features in examining a suitable adsorbent. The Fig. 14 shows the experimental results obtained from examining the rate of Cs+ adsorption by Ti3C2(OH)x and Ti3C2(OH)x-KH2PO4 adsorbents in the time interval of 10–60 min and at pH ~ 7.00. Experimental results show that the process of Cs+ adsorption with both adsorbents occurs during two stages. The first stage happens at a very high speed and almost % adsorption is done. This stage takes place at a higher rate due to the availability of active surface sites on both adsorbents. The increase of O−(OH−) surface active sites has increased the of Cs+ adsorption intensity by Ti3C2(OH)x-KH2PO4 compared to Ti3C2(OH)x in the first 10 min. The second stage is done very slowly, due to the filling of surface-active sites available on the surface and edge of the layers, adsorption occurs with the penetration of Cs+ among the layers. Due to the slowness of the penetration process and on the other hand, with the filling of the edge sites of the layers, the penetration of Cs+ into the layers happens at a much slower rate. By increasing the space among Ti3C2(OH)x-KH2PO4 layers compared to Ti3C2(OH)x, Cs+ adsorption with Ti3C2(OH)x-KH2PO4 adsorbent is faster and reaches equilibrium after 40 min. By increasing the space among the layers (the placement of K+ among the layers), more active sites become available and by increasing the electrostatic interactions among O−(OH−) and Cs+, the adsorption intensity increases.

Reduction of Ti3C2Tx-KH2PO4

All cyclic adsorption processes are adverb the regeneration property of the adsorbent; this causes the adsorbents to work uniformly in cyclic adsorption processes. The heat of adsorption determines the amount of energy required to regenerate the adsorbent. The ability to regenerate the adsorbent is one of practical features in cyclic adsorption processes. Previous works have shown that Ti3C2(OH)x can be reduced and cyclically absorbed in up to four steps. Therefore, to investigate the adsorption-desorption capability of Ti3C2(OH)x-KH2PO4, a four-stage adsorption cycle was designed and carried out. For this purpose, the Cs+ adsorption process is investigated with 0.5 g of Ti3C2(OH)x-KH2PO4 under optimal conditions. After the adsorption process, the used adsorbent was collected on the PVDF membrane and dried at a temperature of 353.15 K. Next, this adsorbent is added to HCL 0.1 M (10 mL) and stirred for 30 min at room temperature. After 30 min, the obtained suspension is centrifuged and its surface is washed with deionized distilled water and collected on PVDF membrane and finally dried. This adsorption-desorption process is repeated up to 3 steps. The experimental results of the analysis of Cs+ concentration after each stage show that the intensity of adsorption in the second, third and fourth stages is reduced by 6.01, 10.94 and 15% compared to the intensity of adsorption in the first stage, respectively. SEM structural and morphological analysis shows that after 4 steps of adsorption-desorption, the crystalline and layered structure of Ti3C2(OH)x-KH2PO4 is preserved and only the KH2PO4 capsules among the layers are reduced. EDS results also show the reduction of surface active sites. The reduction of active sites is the main reason for the reduction of adsorption potential after 4 stages of adsorption-desorption.

In the existence of Na+, Ba2+, Sr2+ and K+ cations, the Cs+ adsorption selectivity was investigated with Ti3C2(OH)x, Ti3C2(OH)x-Chitosan and Ti3C2(OH)x-KH2PO4 adsorbents at pH ~ 7.00. In addition, the adsorption rates of Cs+ and other cations with Ti3C2(OH)x and Ti3C2(OH)x-Chitosan are not significantly different from each other. In modification with Chitosan, surface modification occurs and OH− active sites increase on the surface. In addition to increasing surface-active sites, by encapsulating large amounts of KH2PO4 and intercalating layers, increasing the space among layers, Cs+ penetration is easily done. Cs+ with a hydration radius of 0.329 nm compared to other cations, Ba2+(0.44 nm), Ca2+(0.412 nm) and Sr2+(0.412 nm) easily penetrates among layers and its adsorption rate is higher than other cations. Among these cations, K+ with a hydration radius of 0.331 nm shows an adsorption behavior similar to Cs+. On the other hand, Sr2+, having an electronegativity of 0.95, shows a high adsorption tendency. Ba2+ having a larger hydration radius and lower electronegativity has a lower adsorption rate than other cations. Despite the higher specific surface area of Ti3C2(OH)x-Chitosan (95.580 m2.g− 1) compared to the specific surface area of Ti3C2(OH)x-KH2PO4 (64.873 m2.g− 1), the adsorption rate for Cs+ and other cations is higher for Ti3C2(OH)x-KH2PO4. In addition, although the surface area of Ti3C2(OH)x-Chitosan is nearly 6 times higher than that of Ti3C2(OH)x (17.118 m2.gr− 1), no significant increase in adsorption rate is observed. This result shows that the main factor of adsorption of Cs+ and other cations by Ti3C2(OH)x is its surface active hydrophilic active sites.

Adsorption mechanism

According to the results obtained from XRD, SEM and EDS characterization analyses before and after the adsorption of Cs+ with pristine and modified Ti3C2(OH)x, it seems that the ion exchange, electrostatic interaction and Complex formation mechanisms are involved in Cs+ adsorption. Ti3C2(OH)x having active sites (O−, OH− and F− (a little)) in the form of C-Ti-OH−, C-Ti-O− and C-Ti-F− Adsorption of Cs+ on the surface and edge of the layers is done by ion exchange mechanism. In modification with chitosan, with the increase OH− wt%, the strong interactions among Cs+ and OH− increase and as a result, Cs+ adsorption by the ion exchange mechanism increases. In modification with KH2PO4, by placing K+ among the layers and increasing the space among the layers, the possibility of cesium penetration among the layers and the possibility of establishing a strong interaction with OH− among the layers increases, and as a result, adsorption by the ion exchange mechanism increases48–51.

pH effect studies show that electrostatic interactions have a great effect on Cs+ adsorption. In modification with KH2PO4, in addition to the extensive existence of OH− surface reactive sites, with the interaction of layers as a result of placing K+ among layers, Cs+ is trapped by electrostatic interaction among layers. In addition, Ti3C2(OH)x-KH2PO4, having a positive zeta potential compared to Ti3C2(OH)x, shows a higher adsorption capacity52. strong interaction among Cs+ ions and OH− groups that are widely distributed on the surface leads to the formation of a strong surface complex. Also, the large amount of PO43− and K+ encapsulated among the layers increases the adsorption rate by the complex formation mechanism25,39,40,52–54.

Conclusion

MXenes are effective in absorbing heavy metals by having wide and active surface active sites, adjustable interlayer space and effective specific surface area. Modification of Ti3C2(OH)x with KH2PO4 and chitosan showed that the MXene structure does not change as a result of this modification. Due to the existence of oxygenated active sites in the structure of KH2PO4 and chitosan, the Ti3C2(OH)x surface active sites (OH−, O−) wt% increases. In modification with KH2PO4, active group by 8.23 wt% more, is formed on MXene-Chitosan. In addition, as a result of modification with KH2PO4, the interlayer spacing increases as large amounts of KH2PO4 are encapsulated among the layers. Meanwhile, as a result of modification with chitosan, the effective specific surface increases up to 3 times. Experimental results show that increasing the space among the layers greatly increases the possibility of establishing electrochemical forces and adsorption. The RSM results show that the pH solution is one of the parameters affecting the Cs+ adsorption intensity by Ti3C2(OH)x and Ti3C2(OH)x-KH2PO4. On the other hand, it shows that the changes in Cs+ adsorption intensity with both Ti3C2(OH)x and Ti3C2(OH)x-KH2PO4 adsorbents are completely the same adverb the changes in ambient temperature, Cs+ initial concentration, time and pH parameters and the increase is nearly 3 times The Cs+ adsorption intensity by Ti3C2(OH)x-KH2PO4 occurs due to the increase of surface hydroxyl functional groups and the increase of the distance between the layers as a result of the encapsulation of certain amounts of K+ between the layers. Cs+ adsorption is carried out by multi-layered Ti3C2(OH)x-KH2PO4 with heterogeneous sites. The isotherm modeling results show a good fit with the Freundlich model for the Cs+ adsorption process with Ti3C2(OH)x-KH2PO4. Kinetic modeling shows that this adsorption process corresponds to the pseudo-second order model and the adsorption process is mainly controlled by chemical reactions. Thermodynamic modeling and calculation of thermodynamic parameters show that the Cs+ adsorption by Ti3C2(OH)x-KH2PO4 is more favorable and has a higher adsorption intensity than other MOF, graphene oxide, zeolite, and MXene adsorbents. As a result, Ti3C2(OH)x-KH2PO4 is an efficient and potential adsorbent for Cs+ adsorption from nuclear wastewater.

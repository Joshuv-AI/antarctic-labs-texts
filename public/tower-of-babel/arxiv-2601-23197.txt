Allowable complex metrics and the gravitational index of AdS 5 black holes
Pietro Benetti Genolini, 1 Oliver Janssen 2 and Sameer Murthy 3

Abstract
We discuss the Kontsevich–Segal–Witten criterion for the allowability of complex metrics, in the context of the gravitational path integral that calculates the supersymmetric index. We focus on the saddle points that capture the contribution of supersymmetric black holes in AdS 5 space. We show that, for such black holes with two independent angular momenta, the conditions imposed on the corresponding saddle point by the KSW criterion are equivalent to the ones arising from the convergence of the microscopic trace form of the supersymmetric index. This result adds to previous results establishing such an equivalence in other, simpler examples of the gravitational index in AdS space and flat space. Along the way, we give a practical algorithm for implementing the KSW criterion in terms of eigenvalues of certain matrices.

Introduction

In classical General Relativity, the metric on a manifold is a symmetric second-rank tensor that takes real values at every point.
However, there are many situations in physics where complex-valued metrics naturally arise, especially in the context of the Gravitational Path Integral (GPI) as discussed by Gibbons and Hawking
52
.
A simple example is the analytic continuation to Euclidean signature (the “Wick rotation”) of a rotating black hole solution,
which solves the saddle point equations of the GPI.
It is clear that not all complex metrics are physical, and
extending the space of integration of the GPI to all complex metrics is not sensible.
This prompts the question of which complex metrics are allowed as saddle points of the GPI. A criterion to answer this question was proposed by Witten
98
, based on the work of Kontsevich and Segal
78
(henceforth referred to as the KSW criterion).

In order to check the validity of such a criterion, it is useful to consider situations in which we have independent knowledge and control over the GPI.
One such context is the gravitational supersymmetric index
(see
33

In cases that allow for such a calculation, independent ideas and constraints from the microscopic theory, such as

In

Different types of complex black-hole-like saddles were considered for the validity of the KSW criterion as well as the microscopic constraints.

In the process of implementing the KSW criterion for the AdS
5
black hole, we also gain a better understanding of the criterion in holographic situations, i.e. when we have asymptotically AdS boundary conditions on the metric.
The summary of the picture that we develop, as explained in Section
3
, is the following.
Recall that the KSW criterion is a point-wise criterion that must be satisfied at all points in the space.
Applied to the asymptotic region, it is essentially the same as the original criterion of
78

for coupling the boundary CFT to gravity.
For the supersymmetric index that we consider in this paper,
this condition is equivalent to the convergence of the microscopic trace on BPS states.
Once we have established the equivalence at the boundary, we want to “pull it into the bulk.”
Using the Fefferman–Graham expansion we can do so analytically in perturbation theory, but this does not really shed light on the interior.
What we do instead is to analyze the deep interior, close to the horizon,
by imposing reality conditions justified by the physical picture.
In the space between the asymptotic boundary and the horizon, we do not have an analytical understanding and we resort to numerical calculations, which show
that there is a weakening of the KSW condition as one moves into the interior from the boundary, i.e.,
the area in parameter space ruled out by the KSW criterion becomes smaller as we move into the interior.
Unfortunately, we were not able to capture this area as a simple analytic function of the metric.
It would be nice to have a conceptual explanation of this weakening.

3
The KSW criterion and the AdS
5
black hole index

In the previous section, we introduced the complex supersymmetric supergravity solutions that are relevant for the saddle point evaluation of the gravitational description of a four-dimensional superconformal index. They depend on three parameters
(a,r_{+},r_{\star})
, and we argued that while a good definition of the geometry restricts their ranges, convergence of the microscopic description of the superconformal index restricts them even further.
In this section, we show that the restrictions on the parameters of the gravity solution imposed by
the KSW criterion are equivalent to those imposed by the convergence of the microscopic description of the index.

3.1
KSW criterion

Consider a smooth manifold
M
with a complex-valued, non-degenerate symmetric tensor
g
.
Kontsevich and Segal
78
argue that it is consistent to define a quantum field theory on
(M,g)
provided at each point
p\in M
,
the complex-valued quadratic form induced by the metric on
\Lambda^{q}T_{p}^{*}M
has nowhere
\det g\in\mathbb{R}^{-}
,
in which case we choose the branch of the square root such that
\text{Re}\sqrt{\det g}>0
everywhere. With this choice, the metric satisfies

\operatorname{Re}\left(\sqrt{\det g}\,g^{i_{1}j_{1}}g^{i_{2}j_{2}}\cdots g^{i_{q}j_{q}}F_{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{q}}F_{j_{1}j_{2}\cdots j_{q}}\right)\,>\,0\qquad 0\leq q\leq D

(47)

for any real, non-zero
q
-form
F
. This guarantees that the real part of the quadratic form, or, more physically, the real part of the kinetic term, is positive-definite.
They then show that requiring this positive-definiteness is equivalent to saying that there is a basis of the real
vector space
T_{p}M
in which the quadratic form on
T_{p}M
induced by
g_{p}
can be written as

g_{p}\,=\,\sum_{i=1}^{D}\lambda_{i}\,y_{i}^{2}\,,

(48)

where the
y_{i}
are coordinates
in that basis, and the
\lambda_{i}
are non-zero complex numbers, not on the negative real axis, such that

\sum_{i=1}^{D}\left\lvert\Arg\lambda_{i}\right\rvert\,<\,\pi\,,

(49)

where
\Arg\in(-\pi,\pi]
is the principal value of the argument. Witten suggested in
98
that a gravity solution is a good saddle point of the gravitational path integral provided it is
allowable
according to the Kontsevich–Segal criterion (
47
), so we refer
to this condition as the KSW criterion. This builds on earlier work in quantum cosmology
58
;
83
.

To verify the restrictions imposed by the KSW criterion on the five-dimensional complex solutions introduced in the previous section,
we impose the condition (
47
) everywhere on the underlying manifold
\mathbb{R}^{2}\times S^{3}
.
We denote the set of 3-parameter metrics for which this holds by

\textsf{KSW}\,\equiv\,\{(a,r_{+},r_{\star})\ \big|\ \eqref{eq:Allowability_RealPart}\text{ holds everywhere on }M\}\,.

(50)

We were not able to analytically characterize the set
KSW
, and hence we proceed as follows.
Firstly, we explore analytically the constraints of the KSW criterion
on the metric at the conformal boundary,
by considering the first non-trivial order in the expansion near the boundary.
We then move to a numerical evaluation of the KSW criterion in the bulk.
In order to do so, we re-express the KSW criterion in a form that we codify as the following simple
algorithm, which determines whether a given complex-valued metric
g
on
M
is KSW-allowable.

Regard the components
g_{\mu\nu}
of
g
in some real coordinate basis as a
D\times D
matrix.
Then,
g
is KSW-allowable if and only if for
each
p\in M
the metric
g_{p}
obeys the following three conditions:

1.

\det g_{p}\notin\mathbb{R}^{-}
, in which case define
\text{Re}\sqrt{\det g_{p}}\,>0
,

2.

the matrix
A_{p}\equiv\operatorname{Re}\left(\sqrt{\det g_{p}}\,g^{-1}_{p}\right)
is positive definite,

3.

the following condition holds

\text{Tr}\,\bigl|\,\text{arg}\bigl(g_{p}A_{p}\bigr)\bigr|\;\equiv\;\sum_{i=1}^{D}\,\bigl|\,\text{arg}\bigl(\mu_{i}(g_{p}A_{p})\bigr)\bigr|\,<\,\pi\,,

(51)

where
\mu_{i}\left(g_{p}A_{p}\right)
,
i=1,\dots,D
, are the eigenvalues of
g_{p}A_{p}
.

We prove this slight re-characterization of
78
;
98
in Appendix
A
.
Note the difference between (
49
) and (
51
):
the latter condition is imposed on the
eigenvalues

\mu_{i}
of the
matrix

g_{p}A_{p}
, whereas the
\lambda_{i}
appearing in the formula (
49
) are not eigenvalues, as
g_{p}
there is diagonalized by congruence, as appropriate for quadratic forms.
The above algorithm is practical because it avoids having to change basis to put
g_{p}
in the diagonal form (
48
) by congruence.
It can be used to numerically rule out complex-valued metrics according to KSW (or to gain confidence that they satisfy KSW, as we do later on), or, if one is powerful enough, to prove analytically that a given metric satisfies KSW. Notice that

•

if
g_{\mu\nu}
is diagonal and the conditions 1 and 2 above are satisfied, then in (
51
) one can replace
g_{p}A_{p}
by
g_{p}
.
To see this, note that in this case,
A_{p}
is a positive definite diagonal matrix, and so the eigenvalues of
g_{p}A_{p}
are related to those of
g_{p}
by rescaling by a positive real number.
Hence they have the same phase, which implies that
\text{Tr}\,|\text{arg}(g_{p}A_{p})|=\text{Tr}\,|\text{arg}(g_{p})|
.

•

the algorithm is independent of the real coordinate basis in which we choose to express
g_{\mu\nu}
as a matrix.
To see this, note that the metric in a new basis would take the form
\widetilde{g}=M^{\top}gM
for a real invertible matrix
M
.
Therefore
\det\widetilde{g}=(\det M)^{2}\det g
, from which it follows that the Condition 1 is satisfied for
g

if and only if it is satisfied for
\widetilde{g}
.
Similarly for Condition 2, we have
A=|\det M|^{-1}\,M\widetilde{A}M^{\top}
so that
A
is positive definite if and only if
\widetilde{A}
is.
Finally,
\widetilde{g}\widetilde{A}=|\det M|\,M^{\top}gA\,(M^{\top})^{-1}
, so that the eigenvalues of
\widetilde{g}\widetilde{A}
are equal to the eigenvalues of
gA
up to rescaling by a positive number. This proves the equivalence of Condition 3 for both ways of expressing the metric.

Concretely, for our purposes, this algorithm will allow us to provide numerical evidence that the constraints imposed by the KSW criterion are strongest at the conformal boundary (where we have analytical control) and become weaker as one moves into the bulk. We explain and discuss this in more detail at the end of this section.

3.2
KSW criterion at the conformal boundary

The metric near the conformal boundary at leading order in the Fefferman–Graham coordinate
z
takes the form (
2.3
).
Given that
z,\vartheta\in\mathbb{R}
, we can, for the purpose of verifying the KSW criterion in this region, focus on the following complex line element on
\widetilde{M}=T^{3}
:

\begin{split}{\rm d}s^{2}_{3}&\,=\,\beta^{2}(1-\Omega_{1}^{2}\sin^{2}\vartheta-\Omega_{2}^{2}\cos^{2}\vartheta){\rm d}t_{\text{E}}^{2}+\sin^{2}\vartheta\,{\rm d}\phi^{2}+\cos^{2}\vartheta\,{\rm d}\psi^{2}\\
&\qquad-2{\rm i}\beta{\rm d}t_{\text{E}}(\Omega_{1}\sin^{2}\vartheta\,{\rm d}\phi+\Omega_{2}\cos^{2}\vartheta\,{\rm d}\psi)\,,\end{split}

(52)

where the complex-valued
\beta
,
\Omega_{1}
and
\Omega_{2}
are given in (
24

real basis
\{{\rm d}t_{\text{E}},\,{\rm d}\phi,\,{\rm d}\psi\}

\widetilde{g}\,\equiv\,\begin{pmatrix}\beta^{2}(1-\Omega_{1}^{2}\sin^{2}\vartheta-\Omega_{2}^{2}\cos^{2}\vartheta)&-{\rm i}\beta\Omega_{1}\sin^{2}\vartheta&-{\rm i}\beta\Omega_{2}\cos^{2}\vartheta\\
-{\rm i}\beta\Omega_{1}\sin^{2}\vartheta&\sin^{2}\vartheta&0\\
-{\rm i}\beta\Omega_{2}\cos^{2}\vartheta&0&\cos^{2}\vartheta\end{pmatrix}\,.

(53)

We write the boundary KSW criterion as

\textsf{BKSW}\,\equiv\,\{(a,r_{+},r_{\star})\ \big|\ \eqref{eq:Allowability_RealPart}\text{ for }\widetilde{g}\text{ holds everywhere on }\widetilde{M}\}\,.

(54)

It is clear by construction that

\textsf{KSW}\subseteq\textsf{BKSW}\,,

(55)

because

imposes more conditions on
(a,r_{+},r_{\star})
than

.

The condition (
47
) with
q=0
is just
\operatorname{Re}\sqrt{\det\widetilde{g}}=\sin\vartheta\cos\vartheta\,\operatorname{Re}\beta>0
.
By assumption,
\vartheta\in[0,\pi/2]
, and so the KSW condition with
q=0
reduces to
\operatorname{Re}\beta>0
.
Notice that this is the same as the microscopic convergence condition (
36
),
which, as already pointed out,
is equivalent to the constraints (
22
) on the parameters of the gravity solution.

The KSW criterion with
q=1
requires checking the positive-definiteness of
\operatorname{Re}W\equiv\operatorname{Re}\sqrt{\det\widetilde{g}}\,\widetilde{g}^{-1}

\operatorname{Re}W\,=\,\cos\vartheta\sin\vartheta\begin{pmatrix}\operatorname{Re}1/\beta&-\operatorname{Im}\Omega_{1}&-\operatorname{Im}\Omega_{2}\\
-\operatorname{Im}\Omega_{1}&\operatorname{Re}\left[\beta(\frac{1}{\sin^{2}\vartheta}-\Omega_{1}^{2})\right]&-\operatorname{Re}(\beta\Omega_{1}\Omega_{2})\\
-\operatorname{Im}\Omega_{2}&-\operatorname{Re}(\beta\Omega_{1}\Omega_{2})&\operatorname{Re}\left[\beta(\frac{1}{\cos^{2}\vartheta}-\Omega_{2}^{2})\right]\end{pmatrix}\,.

(56)

This can be done using Sylvester’s criterion, that is, by checking that the leading principal minors of
\operatorname{Re}W
are positive.
The criteria must hold for all
\vartheta\in[0,\pi/2]
.
The first principal minor is positive because
\operatorname{Re}\beta>0
from the KSW criterion for
q=0
, which is equivalent to the condition
\operatorname{Re}\beta^{-1}>0
.
Positivity of the second principal minor, written in terms of
\sigma
,
is equivalent to the inequality

\left(\operatorname{Re}\beta\right)^{2}-\sin^{2}\vartheta\left(\operatorname{Re}\beta-2\pi\,\operatorname{Im}\sigma\right)^{2}\,>\,0\,,

(57)

which is strongest at
\vartheta=\pi/2
, where it is equivalent to

\operatorname{Im}\sigma\left(\frac{1}{\pi}\operatorname{Re}\beta-\operatorname{Im}\sigma\right)\,>\,0\,.

(58)

Since
\operatorname{Re}\beta>0
, this holds if and only if

\frac{1}{\pi}\operatorname{Re}\beta\,>\,\operatorname{Im}\sigma\,>\,0\,.

(59)

At first sight, this inequality seems too strong, as it seems to rule out a region of the parameter space that is
allowed by the microscopic constraints (
36
)
and (
37
).
However, one should recall that for the complex gravitational solution we are considering,

\beta
,
\sigma
and
\tau
are not
independent complex numbers.
Indeed, once we take into account the particular values
\beta_{g}
,
\sigma_{g}
and
\tau_{g}
given in (
24
) and (
38
), one can check that

\frac{1}{\pi}\operatorname{Re}\beta_{g}\,<\,\operatorname{Im}\sigma_{g}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\operatorname{Im}\tau_{g}<0\,.

(60)

We return to this thought presently.

Now we move to the positivity of
\det\operatorname{Re}W
itself, which expressed in terms of
\sigma
and
\tau
leads to the inequality

\operatorname{Re}\beta\times\bigl(\,\sin^{2}\vartheta\operatorname{Im}\sigma(\operatorname{Re}\beta-\pi\operatorname{Im}\sigma)+\cos^{2}\vartheta\operatorname{Im}\tau(\operatorname{Re}\beta-\pi\operatorname{Im}\tau)\,\bigr)\,>\,0\,.

(61)

This expression takes the form
f=b+(a-b)x

for certain
a
,
b
and
x=\sin^{2}\vartheta
.
The positivity of this linear function is guaranteed if
f
is positive at the edges of the interval
x=\sin^{2}\vartheta\in[0,1]
that is, if

\operatorname{Im}\sigma\,\Bigl(\,\frac{1}{\pi}\operatorname{Re}\beta-\operatorname{Im}\sigma\Bigr)\,>\,0\,,\qquad\operatorname{Im}\tau\,\Bigl(\,\frac{1}{\pi}\operatorname{Re}\beta-\operatorname{Im}\tau\Bigr)\,>\,0\,.

(62)

The first condition is guaranteed by (
59
). For the second condition, the only possibility consistent with
\operatorname{Re}\beta>0
is

\frac{1}{\pi}\operatorname{Re}\beta\,>\,\operatorname{Im}\tau\,>\,0\,.

(63)

As above, one can check that for the particular values of
\beta_{g}
,
\sigma_{g}

\tau_{g}
given in (
24
)
and (
38
)

\frac{1}{\pi}\operatorname{Re}\beta_{g}\,<\,\operatorname{Im}\tau_{g}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\operatorname{Im}\sigma_{g}<0\,.

(64)

Now we put together the above two thoughts leading to (
60
) and (
64
), respectively.
We see that the regions of parameter space that would be removed by the first inequalities in (
59
) and (
63
)
are removed by requiring that
\operatorname{Im}\sigma_{g}>0

and

\operatorname{Im}\tau_{g}>0
.
We conclude that the KSW criterion applied to the leading term of the near-boundary expansion of
the metric of the complex gravitational saddle is equivalent to the microscopic criterion, i.e,

\textsf{BKSW}\,=\,\textsf{micro}\,.

(65)

By (
55
), this already shows that the full KSW criterion is at least as strong as the microscopic convergence criteria,

\textsf{KSW}\subseteq\textsf{micro}\,.

(66)

3.3
KS criterion and index of the dual
4d
supersymmetric QFT

Before moving to the next step in our application of the KSW criterion to the gravitational saddle,
we take an instructive detour.
Recall that the criterion (
47
) was originally proposed by Kontsevich and Segal
78

as a consistency criterion for a quantum field theory on a complex metric background
(rather than quantum gravity).
From this point of view, it is natural to enquire about the result of applying the Kontsevich–Segal (KS) criterion directly to
the gauge theory holographically dual to the AdS
5
gravity that we have discussed so far.

In the spirit of this paper, we
specialize to the supersymmetric index of a superconformal field theory living on the 4-dimensional
background (
29
).
In this context, and without specifying any more details of the boundary theory, we ask:
what are the conditions imposed
on
\beta
,
\sigma
and
\tau

by (
47
) applied to the 4-dimensional theory?

Since we are assuming that the coordinates are real, for the purpose of checking allowability, we can focus on
the line element (
52
) rather than considering the four-dimensional (
29
),
and therefore the analysis and conclusions of the previous section continue to be valid.
In other words, the criterion (
47
),
interpreted as a condition for the consistency of a quantum field theory, imposes the inequalities (
59
) and (
63
) on the parameters, which we repeat here:

\frac{1}{\pi}\operatorname{Re}\beta\,>\,\operatorname{Im}\sigma\,>\,0\,,\qquad\frac{1}{\pi}\operatorname{Re}\beta\,>\,\operatorname{Im}\tau\,>\,0\,.

(67)

We are then led to the same small puzzle already raised in the gravitational context: the inequalities above are stronger than the microscopic constraints (
36
)
and (
37
), so how do we recover the latter and get rid of the first inequalities in (
67
) (i.e.
\frac{1}{\pi}\operatorname{Re}\beta>\operatorname{Im}\sigma,\operatorname{Im}\tau
)? In the analysis of the previous section, we remarked that the
5d
bulk gravitational saddle has additional geometric properties which relate the values
\beta_{g}
to
\sigma_{g}
,
\tau_{g}
.
These made the first inequalities in (
67
) redundant, and gave the result (
65
), namely
\textsf{BKSW}=\textsf{micro}
.
However, the background in (
29
)
per se
does not have these properties,
and the three complex parameters
\beta,\sigma,\tau
should be taken to be independent.
In this case, to resolve the puzzle we should recall that the KS criterion pertains to the convergence of the thermal partition function on
S^{3}
, whereas the microscopic constraints arise from the convergence of the trace defining the superconformal index, which involves a sum over a proper subset of the Hilbert space, and so leads to a weaker condition than (
67
).

To be more concrete, we re-express (
67
) in terms of
\Omega_{1}
and
\Omega_{2}
using (
34
) as

\operatorname{Re}\beta\,>\,0\,,\qquad\left\lvert\frac{\operatorname{Re}(\beta\Omega_{1})}{\operatorname{Re}\beta}\right\rvert\,<\,1\,,\qquad\left\lvert\frac{\operatorname{Re}(\beta\Omega_{2})}{\operatorname{Re}\beta}\right\rvert\,<\,1\,.

(68)

As already pointed out, these conditions are the result of the application of the KS criterion to the background (
29
),
so they guarantee the positive-definiteness of the kinetic operator.
Equivalently, they guarantee the convergence of the following partition function for any CFT on
S^{3}

Z(\beta,\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2})\,=\,\Tr_{\mathcal{H}_{S^{3}}}\exp\left(-\beta E+\beta\Omega_{1}J_{1}+\beta\Omega_{2}J_{2}\right)\,.

(69)

As a matter of fact, the same conditions are those found by applying the KSW criterion to a non-supersymmetric asymptotically AdS
5
black hole solution.
This bulk/boundary consistency is the extension to complex
\beta
of the remarks made by Witten in
98
,
where he studied the case of real
\beta
and
\Omega_{i}
and found
\left\lvert\Omega_{i}\right\rvert<1
, consistently
with
59
;
60
.
7
7

7

For a concrete example,
we generalize an argument in
60
: consider a conformally-coupled scalar on
S^{1}_{\beta}\times S^{3}
,
and expand in modes corresponding to the irreducible representations labelled
(\omega,j,j)
,
corresponding to the quantum numbers
\{E,\,J_{L}\equiv(J_{2}+J_{1})/2,\,J_{R}\equiv(J_{2}-J_{1})/2\}

introduced below (
29
). The partition function is

Z_{0}\,=\,\sum{\rm e}^{-\beta(\omega-\Omega_{1}(m_{L}-m_{R})-\Omega_{2}(m_{L}+m_{R}))}\,,

where the sum runs over
j
,
m_{L}
,
m_{R}
, and
\omega
, with the further condition coming from the scalar equation of motion that
\omega=2j+1
, so that

\begin{split}Z_{0}\,&=\,\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}{\rm e}^{-\beta(2j+1)}\sum_{m_{L}=-j}^{j}\sum_{m_{R}=-j}^{j}{\rm e}^{\beta(\Omega_{1}+\Omega_{2})m_{L}+\beta(\Omega_{1}-\Omega_{2})m_{R}}\\
\,&=\,\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}{\rm e}^{-(2j+1)\beta}\frac{{\rm e}^{-2j\beta\Omega_{1}}\left({\rm e}^{(2j+1)\beta(\Omega_{1}-\Omega_{2})}-1\right)\left({\rm e}^{(2j+1)\beta(\Omega_{1}+\Omega_{2})}-1\right)}{\left({\rm e}^{\beta(\Omega_{1}-\Omega_{2})}-1\right)\left({\rm e}^{\beta(\Omega_{1}+\Omega_{2})}-1\right)}\,.\end{split}

The large
j
behavior of the sum is governed by the following four exponential terms

\sim\,{\rm e}^{-2\beta(1-\Omega_{1})j}-{\rm e}^{-2\beta(1+\Omega_{2})j}-{\rm e}^{-2\beta(1-\Omega_{2})j}+{\rm e}^{-2j\beta(1+\Omega_{1})}

and convergence requires (
68
).
We notice that we cannot make the eigenvalues of
J_{1}
and
J_{2}
simultaneously large because of the geometry of
S^{3}
, which is a non-trivial fibration of
T^{2}
(generated by
J_{1}
and
J_{2}
) over the interval.
At each endpoint of the interval one of the two circle fibres degenerates, so only one of the two angular momenta can become large.
This leads to independent convergence conditions for each
\Omega_{i}
,
as we found when deriving (
59
) and (
63
). In the trace defining the supersymmetric index, effectively only two of the four summands remain.

In these variables, the microscopic constraints (
36
)
and (
37
) correspond to

\operatorname{Re}\beta\,>\,0\,,\qquad\frac{\operatorname{Re}(\beta\Omega_{1})}{\operatorname{Re}\beta}\,<\,1\,,\qquad\frac{\operatorname{Re}(\beta\Omega_{2})}{\operatorname{Re}\beta}\,<\,1\,,

(70)

which are too weak to guarantee the convergence of the sum over
\mathcal{H}_{S^{3}}
in (
69
).
However, the superconformal index,
which includes the insertion of
(-1)^{F}
in the trace,
can be expressed as the partition function
with a certain background
value of the
U(1)_{R}
symmetry potential as follows (see (
32
)),

\begin{split}&Z(\beta,\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2},\Phi_{R})\Big\rvert_{\beta(1-2\Phi_{R}+\Omega_{1}+\Omega_{2})\,=\,2\pi{\rm i}(1+2n)}\,\\
&\qquad\,=\,\Tr_{\mathcal{H}_{S^{3}}}\exp\bigl(-\beta E+\beta\Omega_{1}J_{1}+\beta\Omega_{2}J_{2}+\beta\Phi_{R}R\bigr)\Big\rvert_{\beta(1-2\Phi_{R}+\Omega_{1}+\Omega_{2})\,=\,2\pi{\rm i}(1+2n)}\\
&\qquad\,=\,\Tr_{\mathcal{H}_{\rm BPS}}\exp\left(\beta\left(\Omega_{1}-1\right)\left(J_{1}+\tfrac{1}{2}R\right)+\beta\left(\Omega_{2}-1\right)\left(J_{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}R\right)\right)\,,\end{split}

(71)

where in the last equality we have stressed that the sum runs only over the BPS subspace
97
.
As already remarked around (
37
), the eigenvalues of
J_{1,2}+\frac{1}{2}R
are non-negative on
\mathcal{H}_{\rm BPS}
, which means that convergence is guaranteed even when assuming only (
70
).

We noted below (
69
) that the KS criterion for the consistency of a conformal field theory
on a complex
S^{1}\times S^{3}
background matches the KSW criterion for the allowability of the asymptotically AdS
5
black hole.
Here we see the same holographic correspondence in action in presence of fermions.
As shown in this subsection, the conditions imposed by the supersymmetric index on the quantum states of the boundary superconformal field theory
modify the allowability conditions of the bosonic theory.
In the bulk, we have to impose supersymmetric boundary conditions on the gravitational theory dictated by the same supersymmetric index.
As shown in the previous subsection,
the smooth saddle points of the resulting gravitational index give rise to the same modifications on the allowability conditions as the boundary theory.

3.4
Expansion near the conformal boundary

Next, we move to a neighborhood of the conformal boundary, where we define perturbatively a coordinate change from
(r,\theta)
to
(z,\vartheta)

\begin{split}\theta&\,=\,\arcsin\left(\sin\vartheta\sqrt{\frac{\Xi_{a}}{\Delta_{\vartheta}}}\right)+z^{2}\left(-\frac{\sin\vartheta\cos\vartheta(a^{2}-b^{2})\sqrt{\Xi_{a}\Xi_{b}}}{2\Delta_{\vartheta}^{2}}\right)+o(z^{3})\,,\\
r&\,=\,\frac{1}{z}\sqrt{\Delta_{\vartheta}}+z\left(-\frac{\Delta_{\vartheta}^{2}+2(\Xi_{a}a^{2}\sin^{2}\vartheta+\Xi_{b}b^{2}\cos^{2}\vartheta)}{4\Delta_{\vartheta}^{3/2}}\right)+o(z)\,,\end{split}

(72)

such that the metric takes the form

\begin{split}{\rm d}s^{2}&\,=\,\frac{{\rm d}z^{2}}{z^{2}}\left(1+o(z^{2})\right)+\frac{1}{z^{2}}h_{ij}(x,z){\rm d}x^{i}{\rm d}x^{j}\,,\\
h(x,z)&\,=\,g_{(0)}+z^{2}\,g_{(2)}+o(z^{2})\,,\\
g_{(2)}&=\frac{1}{2}\left[\beta^{2}{\rm d}t_{E}^{2}-{\rm d}\vartheta^{2}-\sin^{2}\vartheta\,({\rm d}\phi-{\rm i}\beta\Omega_{1}\,{\rm d}t_{E})^{2}-\cos^{2}\vartheta\,({\rm d}\psi-{\rm i}\beta\Omega_{2}\,{\rm d}t_{E})^{2}\right]\end{split}

(73)

where
g_{(0)}
has line element
{\rm d}s^{2}_{\rm bdry}
as in (
29
). This represents a perturbative coordinate change to Fefferman–Graham coordinates: the leading term is (
2.3
), and one can check that
g_{(2)}
is fixed by the Schouten tensor of
g_{(0)}
as

g_{(2),ij}=-\frac{1}{2}\left(R[g_{(0)}]_{ij}-\frac{1}{6}g_{(0),ij}R[g_{(0)}]\right)\,.

(74)

As before,
z,\vartheta\in\mathbb{R}
, and we can focus on a three-dimensional complex line element on
\widetilde{M}
which in the real basis
\{{\rm d}t_{E},\,{\rm d}\phi,\,{\rm d}\psi\}
is represented by the matrix

\widetilde{h}\equiv\widetilde{g}_{(0)}+z^{2}\,\widetilde{g}_{(2)}+o(z^{2})\,,

(75)

where
\widetilde{g}_{(0)}
is (
53
) and
\widetilde{g}_{(2)}
comes from
g_{(2)}
in (
73
). We now apply the KSW criteria (
47
) to
\widetilde{h}
to see whether the first subleading term in the Fefferman–Graham expansion near the conformal boundary imposes constraints on the parameters that are more stringent than those defining
\textsf{BKSW}=\textsf{micro}
.

The KSW condition with
q=0
is
\operatorname{Re}\sqrt{\det\widetilde{h}}>0
, that is,

\frac{1}{\sin\vartheta\cos\vartheta}\operatorname{Re}\sqrt{\det\widetilde{h}}\,=\,\left(1-\frac{1}{4}z^{2}+o(z^{2})\right)\operatorname{Re}\beta\,>\,0\,.

(76)

From the second rewriting we find that either
\operatorname{Re}\beta>0
and
1-\frac{1}{4}z^{2}>o(z^{2})
, or
\operatorname{Re}\beta<0
and
1-\frac{1}{4}z^{2}<o(z^{2})
, but it’s clear that the second possibility cannot happen as
z\to 0
, so we conclude that the KSW condition with
q=0
applied to the metric including the first subleading correction near the conformal boundary is once again
\operatorname{Re}\beta>0
, without additional constraints.

The KSW criterion with
q=1
, instead, requires us to check the positive-definiteness of
\operatorname{Re}W\equiv\operatorname{Re}\sqrt{\det\widetilde{h}}\,\widetilde{h}^{-1}
, which again we do by checking Sylvester’s criterion. Here
\operatorname{Re}W
has the form

\begin{split}\operatorname{Re}W&\,=\,\left[\operatorname{Re}W_{(0)}+z^{2}\,\left(-\frac{3}{4}\operatorname{Re}W_{(0)}+D\right)+o(z^{2})\right]\,,\\
D&\,=\,\cos\vartheta\sin\vartheta\operatorname{Re}\beta\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\
0&\frac{1}{\sin^{2}\vartheta}&0\\
0&0&\frac{1}{\cos^{2}\vartheta}\end{pmatrix}\end{split}

(77)

where
\operatorname{Re}W_{(0)}
is (
56
). Let
M^{(k)}
denote the upper-left
k\times k
block of
M
, then if
\det\operatorname{Re}W_{(0)}^{(k)}\neq 0
, the leading principal minor of order
k
of
\operatorname{Re}W
is

\det\operatorname{Re}W^{(k)}\,=\,\det\operatorname{Re}W_{(0)}^{(k)}\left(1+z^{2}\left(-\frac{3}{4}k+\sum_{i=1}^{k}(\operatorname{Re}W_{(0)}^{(k)})^{-1}_{ii}D_{ii}^{(k)}\right)+o(z^{2})\right)\,,

(78)

and we conclude, by an analogous argument to that just discussed, that the subleading term in the Fefferman–Graham expansion of the metric does not impose more constraints on
(a,r_{+},r_{\star})
than the conformal boundary. That is, the analysis of the perturbation reduces to the analysis of the conditions on
\det\operatorname{Re}W_{(0)}^{(k)}
.

In particular, this is the case for the first leading principal minor, which is the product of
\left(1-\frac{3}{4}z^{2}+o(z^{2})\right)
and the first principal minor of
\operatorname{Re}W_{(0)}
: we find once again that the condition is
\operatorname{Re}\beta>0
.

However, this doesn’t have to be the case for the leading principal minors of order
k=2,3
: it could happen that for a choice of parameters,
\det\operatorname{Re}W_{(0)}^{(k)}=0
, in which case the formula above cannot be applied, and the expansion of
\det\operatorname{Re}W^{(k)}
generically starts at
z^{2}
with a coefficient dependent on
\vartheta
and
(a,r_{+},r_{\star})
.

Concretely, positivity of the minor of order
2
,
\det\operatorname{Re}W_{(0)}^{(2)}>0
, is equivalent to

(\operatorname{Re}\beta)^{2}-\sin^{2}\vartheta(\operatorname{Re}\beta\Omega_{1})^{2}+\frac{z^{2}}{2}\left(3\sin^{2}\vartheta(\operatorname{Re}\beta\Omega_{1})^{2}-(\operatorname{Re}\beta)^{2}\right)+o(z^{2})\,>\,0\,,

(79)

but when the order-
1
term vanishes, this inequality reduces to
z^{2}\,\sin^{2}\vartheta(\operatorname{Re}\beta\Omega_{1})^{2}>o(z^{2})
, which holds as
z\to 0
, so in this case even the boundary of the region allowed by
BKSW
does not lead to additional constraints at subleading order.

Positivity of the minor of order
3
is instead equivalent to

\begin{split}&\left((\operatorname{Re}\beta)^{2}-\sin^{2}\vartheta(\operatorname{Re}\beta\Omega_{1})^{2}-\cos^{2}\vartheta(\operatorname{Re}\beta\Omega_{2})^{2}\right)\\
&\qquad\qquad+\frac{z^{2}}{4}\left(5\sin^{2}\vartheta(\operatorname{Re}\beta\Omega_{1})^{2}+5\cos^{2}\vartheta(\operatorname{Re}\beta\Omega_{2})^{2}-(\operatorname{Re}\beta)^{2}\right)+o(z^{2})\,>\,0\,,\end{split}

(80)

but again, when the term of order
1
vanishes, this reduces to an inequality that always holds as
z\to 0
.

We conclude that the KSW criteria do not impose additional constraints on
(a,r_{+},r_{\star})
when we move into the bulk from the boundary to first order in
z^{2}
.

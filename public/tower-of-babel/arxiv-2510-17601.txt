Central Limit Theorems for Drift and Entropy of Random Walks on Free Products
Lorenz A. Gilch

Abstract
. In this article we consider a natural class of random walks on free products of graphs, which arise as convex combinations of random walks on the single factors. From the works of Gilch [ 6 , 7 ] it is well-known that for these random walks the asymptotic entropy as well as the drift w.r.t. the natural transition graph distance and also w.r.t. the word length exist. The aim of this article is to formulate three central limit theorems with respect to both drift definitions and the entropy. In the case that the random walk depends on finitely many parameters we show that the corresponding variances in the central limit theorems w.r.t. both drifts vary real-analytically in terms of these parameters, while the variance in the central limit theorem w.r.t. the entropy varies real-analytically at least in the case of free products of finite graphs.



1.
Introduction

Let
V_{1},V_{2}
be finite or countable, disjoint sets with
|V_{i}|\geq 2
, and fix distinguished elements
o_{i}\in V_{i}
,
i\in\{1,2\}
.
Suppose that each
V_{i}
carries a transition matrix
P_{i}\in[0,1]^{V_{i}\times V_{i}}
.
The free product
V:=V_{1}\ast V_{2}
consists of all finite words over the alphabet
(V_{1}\cup V_{2})\setminus\{o_{1},o_{2}\}
such that two consecutive letters do not lie in the same
V_{i}
.
We consider a transient Markov chain
(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
on
V
, starting at the empty word
X_{0}:=o
, with transition law given by a convex combination of the kernels
P_{i}
. Denote by
\|x\|
the word length of
x\in V
and let
d(o,x)
denote the distance of
x
to
o
w.r.t. the natural graph metric on the transition graph of the random walk on
V
. Then, by
Gilch
[
6
]
,
it is well-known that the (asymptotic) drifts

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\|X_{n}\|}{n}\quad\textrm{ and }\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{d(o,X_{n})}{n}

exist almost surely and both limits are almost surely constant. Moreover, let
\pi_{n}
denote the distribution of
X_{n}
. In Gilch
[
7
]
it is shown that the (asymptotic) entropy

\lim_{n\to\infty}-\frac{1}{n}\log\pi_{n}(X_{n})

exists almost surely and the limit is also almost surely constant. The aim of this note is to establish three central limit theorems w.r.t. the drifts and the entropy. In particular, we will show that if the random walk’s law depends on finitely many parameters, then the variances in these central limit theorems vary real-analytically in terms of these parameters, where analyticity of the variance in the entropy case is restricted to free products of finite graphs.

Let me outline some results on random walks on free products and their importance.
Random walks on free products have been studied extensively, and there is a broad range of literature.
Asymptotics of return probabilities were elaborated in Gerl and Woess

]
, Woess
[

]
, Sawyer
[

]
, Cartwright and Soardi

,

[

Spectral properties were analyzed in Shi et al.

The relevance of free products in group theory arises from Stallings’ Splitting Theorem (see Stallings
[
14

), which characterizes finitely generated groups with more than one geometric end as those admitting a representation as a nontrivial free product by amalgamation or as an HNN extension over a finite subgroup; we note that free products arise as the special case of amalgams over the trivial subgroup.
Most prior works focus on free products of groups, which are space-homogeneous and admit transitive random walks.
In this article we address the more general setting of free products of graphs, which are lacking such a homogenity.
In particular, we revisit results of

2.
Free Products and Random Walks

2.1.
Free Products

Let
V_{1},V_{2}
be disjoint, finite or countable sets with
|V_{i}|\geq 2
for
i\in\mathcal{I}:=\{1,2\}
. We fix a distinguished element
o_{i}\in V_{i}
, called the
root
of
V_{i}
,
for each
i\in\mathcal{I}
, and we set
V_{i}^{\times}:=V_{i}\setminus\{o_{i}\}
. The case
|V_{1}|=|V_{2}|=2
will be excluded; see Remark
2.2
.(i).

The
free product
of
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
is given by the set

V:=V_{1}\ast V_{2}:=\Bigl\{v_{1}\ldots v_{k}\,\Bigl|\,k\in\mathbb{N},\;v_{\ell}\in V_{1}^{\times}\cup V_{2}^{\times},\;v_{i}\in V_{j}^{\times}\Rightarrow v_{i+1}\notin V_{j}^{\times}\Bigr\}\cup\{o\},

the set of finite words over the alphabet
V_{1}^{\times}\cup V_{2}^{\times}
with no two consecutive letters coming from the same
V_{j}^{\times}
, where
o
denotes the empty word.

A partial composition law is defined as follows: if we have
u=u_{1}\ldots u_{m}
,
v=v_{1}\ldots v_{n}\in V
,

m,n\in\mathbb{N}
, and
u_{m}\in V_{i}^{\times}
,
v_{1}\notin V_{i}^{\times}
for some
i\in\mathcal{I}
, then
uv
is the concatenated word, which is again an element of
V
.
We write
\delta(u):=i
, and make the convention
uo_{j}=u=ou=uo
for
j\in\mathcal{I}\setminus\{\delta(u)\}
. Furthermore, the
word length
of
u=u_{1}\ldots u_{m}
is denoted by
\|u\|:=m
.

For
u\in V
, the
cone
rooted at
u
is given by

C(u):=\{w\in V\mid w\text{ has prefix }u\},

the set of words in
V
starting with
u
.

2.2.
Random Walks

Let
P_{i}=\bigl(p_{i}(x,y)\bigr)_{x,y\in V_{i}}
be transition matrices on
V_{i}
,
i\in\mathcal{I}
, such that every
x\in V_{i}^{\times}
is accessible from
o_{i}
in finitely many steps with positive probability, that is,
P_{i}^{n_{x}}(o_{i},x)>0
for some
n_{x}\in\mathbb{N}
. For sake of simplicity, we assume that
p_{i}(x,x)=0
for every
i\in\mathcal{I}
and all
x\in V_{i}
, and we assume that there exist
j\in\mathcal{I}
,
y\in V_{j}^{\times}
and
n_{y}\in\mathbb{N}
with
P_{j}^{n_{y}}(y,y)>0
; see Remarks
2.2
.(iii).
Fix
\alpha\in(0,1)
, and set
\alpha_{1}:=\alpha
,
\alpha_{2}:=1-\alpha
.
Define the lifted transition matrix
\overline{P}_{i}=\bigl(\overline{p}_{i}(x,y)\bigr)_{x,y\in V}
by

\overline{p}_{i}(uv,uw):=p_{i}(v,w),

if
u=u_{1}\ldots u_{m}
with
u_{m}\notin V_{i}
and
v,w\in V_{i}
,
i\in\mathcal{I}
; otherwise, we set
\overline{p}_{i}(x,y):=0
.
A natural random walk on
V
is governed by the transition matrix

P=\bigl(p(x,y)\bigr)_{x,y\in V}:=\alpha_{1}\overline{P}_{1}+\alpha_{2}\overline{P}_{2}.

The transition graph
\mathcal{X}
w.r.t.
P
arises from the transition graphs
\mathcal{X}_{1},\mathcal{X}_{2}
w.r.t.
P_{1}
,
P_{2}
in a natural way as follows: take copies of
\mathcal{X}_{1}
and
\mathcal{X}_{2}
and glue them together at their roots
o_{1}
and
o_{2}
, which becomes the single vertex
o
. Inductively, at each in the previous step newly added vertex
u=u_{1}\ldots u_{m}\in V\setminus\{o\}
with
\delta(u)=i\in\mathcal{I}
attach a copy of
\mathcal{X}_{j}
,
j\in\mathcal{I}\setminus\{\delta(u)\}
, where
u
and the root
o_{j}
of the new copy of
\mathcal{X}_{j}
are glueded together to one single vertex, which becomes
u=uo_{j}
. The vertices
u_{m+1}\in V_{j}^{\times}
of the newly attached copy
\mathcal{X}_{j}
become the elements
u_{1}\ldots u_{m}u_{m+1}
in
\mathcal{X}
. See, e.g., Gilch
[
8
, Example 2.1]
for a graphic visualization.

The transition graph
\mathcal{X}
gives rise to a natural graph distance
d(\cdot,\cdot)
; that is, for
x,y\in V
,
d(x,y)
denotes the minimal length of an (oriented) path from
x
to
y
in
\mathcal{X}
.

Let
(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
describe a random walk on
V
governed by
P
with
X_{0}:=o
.
The corresponding
n
-step transition probabilities are denoted by

p^{(n)}(x,y):=\mathbb{P}[X_{n}=y\mid X_{0}=x],\qquad x,y\in V,\;n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

Additionally, we write
\mathbb{P}_{x}[\,\cdot\,]:=\mathbb{P}[\,\cdot\mid X_{0}=x]
.

We say that the
random walk’s law depends on finitely many parameters
if there are
d\in\mathbb{N}
and
p_{1},\ldots,p_{d}\in(0,1)
such that for all
x,y\in V
with
p(x,y)>0
there exists
k\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
such that
p(x,y)=p_{k}
. In other words,
P
has only the entries
p_{1},\ldots,p_{d},0
and
1
. If we regard
p_{1},\ldots,p_{d}
as parameters taking values in
(0,1)
, then
we denote by
\mathcal{P}_{d}
the set of all vectors
(p_{1},\ldots,p_{d})\in(0,1)
such that
P
is a stochastic matrix on
V
allowing a well-defined random walk on
V
.

An important property is given by the following lemma which states that paths inside a cone can be shifted to paths originating from
o
in a measure preserving way:

Lemma 2.1
.

Let be
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
x\in V
, and
v_{1},\dots,v_{n}\in C(x)
. We write
v_{i}=xu_{i}
, where
u_{i}\in V
does not start with a letter in
V_{\delta(x)}^{\times}
. Then

\mathbb{P}_{x}[X_{1}=v_{1},\dots,X_{n}=v_{n}]=\mathbb{P}_{o}[X_{1}=u_{1},\dots,X_{n}=u_{n}].

Proof.

See
[
8
, Lemma 3.2]
.
∎

We introduce the following generating functions: for
x,y\in V
,
z\in\mathbb{C}
,
i\in\mathcal{I}
, define

\displaystyle G(x,y|z)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{n\geq 0}p^{(n)}(x,y)\cdot z^{n}\quad\quad\textrm{(Green function)},

\displaystyle L(x,y|z)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{n\geq 0}\mathbb{P}_{x}\bigl[X_{n}=y,\forall m\in\{1,\ldots,m\}:X_{m}\neq x\bigr]\cdot z^{n},

\displaystyle\xi_{i}(z)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{n\geq 1}\mathbb{P}\bigl[X_{n}\in V_{i}^{\times},\forall m<n:X_{m}\notin V_{i}^{\times}\bigr]\cdot z^{n}.

Analogously, for
x_{i},y_{i}\in V_{i}
, let
G_{i}(x_{i},y_{i}|z)
and
L_{i}(x_{i},y_{i}|z)
denote the corresponding functions on
V_{i}
with respect to
P_{i}
.

In the following we collect some important equations in relation with these generating functions.
By
[
5
, Lemma 1.6]
, we have

(1)

\displaystyle G(x,y|z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G(x,x|z)\cdot L(x,y|z),

(2)

\displaystyle G_{i}(x_{i},y_{i}|z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G_{i}(x_{i},x_{i}|z)\cdot L_{i}(x_{i},y_{i}|z)\quad\textrm{ for }i\in\mathcal{I},x_{i},y_{i}\in V_{i}.

If every path from
x
to
y
has to pass through
w
, then

(3)

L(x,y|z)=L(x,w|z)\cdot L(w,y|z).

Moreover, by
[

, Proposition 2.7]
, for
x_{i},y_{i}\in V_{i}\subset V
,

(4)

L(x_{i},y_{i}|z)=L_{i}\bigl(x_{i},y_{i}\,\bigl|\,\xi_{i}(z)\bigr).

We remark that, for
i\in\mathcal{I}
and
z>0
,

(5)

\sum_{x\in V_{i}^{\times}}L_{i}\bigl(o_{i},x\,\bigl|\,\xi_{i}(z)\bigr)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{eq:Gi-Li})}}{{=}}\sum_{x\in V_{i}^{\times}}\frac{G_{i}\bigl(o_{i},x\,\bigl|\,\xi_{i}(z)\bigr)}{G_{i}\bigl(o_{i},o_{i}\,\bigl|\,\xi_{i}(z)\bigr)}

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in V_{i}^{\times}}G_{i}\bigl(o_{i},x\,\bigl|\,\xi_{i}(z)\bigr)

For
x\in V\setminus\{o\}
with
\delta(x)=i
, set

\xi_{i}:=\xi_{i}(1)=\mathbb{P}_{x}\bigl[\exists n\in\mathbb{N}:X_{n}\notin C(x)\bigr],

which depends only on

but not on

By
[
6
, Lemma 2.3]
, we have
\xi_{i}<1
, hence

\mathbb{P}_{x}\bigl[\forall n\in\mathbb{N}:X_{n}\in C(x)\bigr]=1-\xi_{i}>0.

As a
basic assumption
we assume throughout this article that the spectral radius at
o
satisfies

\varrho:=\limsup_{n\to\infty}p^{(n)}(o,o)^{1/n}<1,

or equivalently, we assume that the Green function
G(o,o|z)
has radius of convergence strictly bigger than
1
. This guarantees that all generating functions
G(x,y|z)
,
L(x,y|z)
, and

have radii of convergence strictly bigger than

; see Woess

is transient under this assumption.

Remarks 2.2
.

(i)

If
|V_{1}|=|V_{2}|=2
and
P_{1},P_{2}
are irreducible, then the random walk is recurrent, which is easy to check.

(ii)

If one out of
P_{1},P_{2}
is not irreducible, then
\varrho<1
, which is also easy to see.
If
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
are irreducible and reversible, then we have again

; see
[

, Theorem 10.3]
.

(iii)

The assumption
p_{i}(x,x)=0
for every
i\in\mathcal{I}
and
x\in V_{i}
is just for presentational reason in order to avoid lengthy case distinctions, which does not affect the validity of the results at all. The assumption on existence of
j\in\mathcal{I}
,
y\in V_{j}^{\times}
and

with

ensures that the variances in our central limit theorems are non-zero. However, this assumption is also just for sake of simplicity and can be weakened, but it may not be dropped completely.

The
rate of escape
or
drift
of
(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is given by the almost sure constant limit

\lambda=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{d(o,X_{n})}{n}.

In
[
5
, Corollary 4.2]
it is shown that
\lambda>0
exists and it is almost surely constant. Moreover, there exists also a constant
\ell>0
such that

\ell=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\|X_{n}\|}{n}\quad\textrm{ almost surely};

see
[
6
, Theorem 3.3]
. This limit is called
rate of escape w.r.t. the block/word length
.

For
\varepsilon_{0}>0
, we say that
P
is
\varepsilon_{0}
-uniform
if

p(x,y)>0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad p(x,y)\geq\varepsilon_{0}>0.

Denote by
\pi_{n}
the distribution of
X_{n}
. If
P
is
\varepsilon_{0}
-uniform, then
[
7
, Theorem 3.7]
guarantees existence of a real number
h>0
such that

h=\lim_{n\to\infty}-\frac{1}{n}\log\pi_{n}(X_{n})\quad\textrm{ almost surely};

This limit is called the
asymptotic entropy
of
(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
.

The aim of this article is to prove the following three central limit theorems, where
(\mathbf{T}_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is a sequence of renewal times, which we will define in (
7
) in Section
3
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Assume that
G(o,o|z)
has radius of convergence strictly bigger than
1
. Then:

\frac{d(o,X_{n})-n\cdot\lambda}{\sigma_{\lambda}\cdot\sqrt{n}}\xrightarrow{\mathcal{D}}N(0,1),

where

\sigma_{\lambda}=\frac{\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\Bigl(d(X_{\mathbf{T}_{0}},X_{\mathbf{T}_{1}})-(\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0})\cdot\lambda\Bigr)^{2}\Bigr]}{\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}]}.

Moreover, if the random walk’s law depends on finitely many parameters
p_{1},\ldots,p_{d}\in(0,1)
, then the mapping

\mathcal{P}_{d}\ni(p_{1},\ldots,p_{d})\mapsto\sigma_{\lambda}=\sigma_{\lambda}(p_{1},\ldots,p_{d})

varies real-analytically.

We refer to the beginning of Section 5 in
[
8
]
, where more details and explanations concerning real-analyticity in terms of

are given.

An analogous central limit theorem holds for the rate of escape w.r.t. the block length:

Theorem 2.4
.

Assume that

has radius of convergence strictly bigger than
1
. Then:

\frac{\|X_{n}\|-n\cdot\ell}{\sigma_{\ell}\cdot\sqrt{n}}\xrightarrow{\mathcal{D}}N(0,1),

where

\sigma_{\ell}=\frac{\mathbb{E}\bigl[\bigl(2-(\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0})\cdot\ell\bigr)^{2}\bigr]}{\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}]}.

Moreover, if the random walk’s law depends on finitely many parameters
p_{1},\ldots,p_{d}\in(0,1)
, then the mapping

\mathcal{P}_{d}\ni(p_{1},\ldots,p_{d})\mapsto\sigma_{\ell}=\sigma_{\ell}(p_{1},\ldots,p_{d})

varies real-analytically.

If
X_{\mathbf{T}_{0}}=x\in V
and
X_{\mathbf{T}_{1}}=xy_{1}x_{1}
with
x_{1}\in V_{1}^{\times}
and

, then we set

. With this notation

.

Assume that

is
\varepsilon_{0}
-uniform for some

and that

3.
Last Cone Entry Times

The main idea is to decompose the random walk’s trajectory into i.i.d. pieces, which allow us to derive the proposed central limit theorems.
For this purpose, we want to track the random walk’s trajectory to “infinity”, that is, the way how
(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
converges to some boundary point, which we do not specify closer.
For
k\in\mathbb{N}
, define the
k
-th last cone entry time
(or
exit time
) as

\mathbf{e}_{k}:=\inf\Bigl\{m>0\,\Bigl|\,\|X_{m}\|=k,\forall n\geq m:X_{n}\in C(X_{m})\Bigr\}.

In other words, the random time
\mathbf{e}_{k}
is the first instant of time from which on the random walk remains in the cone
C(X_{\mathbf{e}_{k}})
, that is, from time
\mathbf{e}_{k}
on the first
k
letters of
X_{n}
remain unchanged.
In particular, we have
X_{\mathbf{e}_{k}-1}\notin C(X_{\mathbf{e}_{k}})
.
These last cone entry times have been used in an essential way in
[
6
,
7
,
8
]
. In
[
6
, Proposition 2.5]
it is shown that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\|X_{n}\|=\infty
almost surely, which implies that
\mathbf{e}_{k}<\infty
almost surely for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence, we obtain a sequence of nested cones
C(X_{\mathbf{e}_{k}})\supset C(X_{\mathbf{e}_{k+1}})
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, which are successively finally entered by the random walk without any further exits; this sequence tracks the random walk’s path to “infinity”.

In the following we are interested just in those exit times
\mathbf{e}_{k}
when the random walk finally enters a cone
C(X_{\mathbf{e}_{k}})
with
\delta(X_{\mathbf{e}_{k}})=1
. Since the letters of a word in
V
arise alternatingly from
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
we either must have

\delta(X_{\mathbf{e}_{1}})=\delta(X_{\mathbf{e}_{3}})=\delta(X_{\mathbf{e}_{5}})=\ldots=1\ \textrm{ and }\ \delta(X_{\mathbf{e}_{2}})=\delta(X_{\mathbf{e}_{4}})=\ldots=2

or
we must have

\delta(X_{\mathbf{e}_{1}})=\delta(X_{\mathbf{e}_{3}})=\delta(X_{\mathbf{e}_{5}})=\ldots=2\ \textrm{ and }\ \delta(X_{\mathbf{e}_{2}})=\delta(X_{\mathbf{e}_{4}})=\ldots=1.

Therefore, we filter the sequence
(\mathbf{e}_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
accordingly as follows:
let be

\tau:=\begin{cases}1,&\textrm{if }X_{\mathbf{e}_{1}}\in V_{1}^{\times},\\
2,&\textrm{if }X_{\mathbf{e}_{2}}\in V_{1}^{\times},\end{cases}

and set for
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

(7)

\mathbf{T}_{k}:=\mathbf{e}_{2k+\tau}.

A crucial observation will be the following proposition:

Proposition 3.1
.

\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}
has exponential moments.

Proof.

First, we note that Lemma
2.1
gives for
z\in\mathbb{C}
and every
x\in V
with
\delta(x)=1
:

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{N},\\
x_{1}\in V_{1}^{\times},\\
y_{1}\in V_{2}^{\times}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}_{x}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}X_{n-1}\notin C(xy_{1}x_{1}),\\
X_{n}=xy_{1}x_{1},\\
\forall m<n:X_{m}\in C(x)\end{array}\right]\cdot z^{n}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{N},\\
x_{1}\in V_{1}^{\times},\\
y_{1}\in V_{2}^{\times}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}X_{n-1}\notin C(y_{1}x_{1}),\\
X_{n}=y_{1}x_{1},\\
\forall m<n:X_{m}\notin V_{1}^{\times}\end{array}\right]\cdot z^{n}.

Denote by

\mathcal{W}:=V_{1}^{\times}\cup\bigl\{u_{2}u_{1}\mid u_{1}\in V_{1}^{\times},u_{2}\in V_{2}^{\times}\bigr\}

the support of
X_{\mathbf{T}_{0}}
. Then we obtain for
z>0
by decomposing according to the values of
\mathbf{T}_{0},\mathbf{T}_{1}
and
X_{\mathbf{T}_{0}},X_{\mathbf{T}_{1}}
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigl[z^{\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}}\bigr]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{N},\\
x\in\mathcal{W},\\
x_{1}\in V_{1}^{\times},y_{1}\in V_{2}^{\times}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}\Bigl[X_{\mathbf{T}_{0}}=x,X_{\mathbf{T}_{1}}=xy_{1}x_{1},\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}=n\Bigr]\cdot z^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l\in\mathbb{N},\\
x\in\mathcal{W}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}X_{l-1}\notin C(x),\\
X_{l}=x\end{array}\right]\cdot\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{N},\\
x_{1}\in V_{1}^{\times},\\
y_{1}\in V_{2}^{\times}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}_{x}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}X_{n-1}\notin C(xy_{1}x_{1}),\\
X_{n}=xy_{1}x_{1},\\
\forall m<n:X_{m}\in C(x)\end{array}\right]\cdot z^{n}

\displaystyle\quad\cdot\mathbb{P}_{xy_{1}x_{1}}\Bigl[\forall j\geq 1:X_{j}\in C(xy_{1}x_{1})\Bigr]

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{Lemma \ref{lem:cone-probabilities}}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l\in\mathbb{N},\\
x\in\mathcal{W}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}X_{l-1}\notin C(x),\\
X_{l}=x\end{array}\right]\cdot\underbrace{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{N},\\
x_{1}\in V_{1}^{\times},\\
y_{1}\in V_{2}^{\times}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}X_{n-1}\notin C(y_{1}x_{1}),\\
X_{n}=y_{1}x_{1},\\
\forall m<n:X_{m}\notin V_{1}^{\times}\end{array}\right]\cdot z^{n}}_{=:\mathcal{F}(z)}\cdot(1-\xi_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\underbrace{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l\in\mathbb{N},\\
x\in\mathcal{W}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}X_{l-1}\notin C(x),\\
X_{l}=x\end{array}\right]\cdot(1-\xi_{1})}_{=\mathbb{P}[\mathbf{T}_{0}<\infty]=1}\cdot\mathcal{F}(z)=\mathcal{F}(z).

For
z>0
, we can bound
\mathcal{F}(z)
from above as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(z)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{1}\in V_{1}^{\times},\\
y_{1}\in V_{2}^{\times}\end{subarray}}G(o,y_{1}x_{1}|z)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{eq:G-L})}}{{=}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{1}\in V_{1}^{\times},\\
y_{1}\in V_{2}^{\times}\end{subarray}}G(o,o|z)\cdot L(o,y_{1}x_{1}|z)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{eq:L-L}),(\ref{eq:G-xi})}}{{=}}

\displaystyle G(o,o|z)\cdot\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{1}\in V_{1}^{\times},\\
y_{1}\in V_{2}^{\times}\end{subarray}}L_{2}\bigl(o_{2},y_{1}|\xi_{2}(z)\bigr)\cdot L_{1}\bigl(o_{1},x_{1}|\xi_{1}(z)\bigr)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{lem:4-1equ-1}),(\ref{lem:4-1equ-2})}}{{\leq}}

\displaystyle\frac{G(o,o|z)}{\bigl(1-\xi_{1}(z)\bigr)\cdot G_{1}\bigl(o_{1},o_{1}|\xi_{1}(z)\bigr)\cdot\bigl(1-\xi_{2}(z)\bigr)\cdot G_{2}\bigl(o_{2},o_{2}|\xi_{2}(z)\bigr)}.

Recall that
G(o,o|z)
,
G_{i}\bigl(o_{i},o_{i}|\xi_{i}(z)\bigr)
and
\xi_{i}(z)
have radii of convergence strictly bigger than
1
and that
\xi_{i}(1)=\xi_{i}<1
for
i\in\mathcal{I}
. Therefore, continuity of the involved functions together with Pringsheim’s Theorem yield that
\mathcal{F}(z)
has radius of convergence strictly bigger than
1
, which in turn implies that
\mathbb{E}\bigl[z^{\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}}\bigr]
has radius of convergence strictly bigger than
1
. This proves existence of exponential moments of
\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}
.
∎

We also have:

Proposition 3.2
.

\mathbf{T}_{0}
has exponential moments.

Proof.

For
z>0
, we obtain by decomposing according to the values of
\mathbf{T}_{0}
and
X_{\mathbf{T}_{0}}
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigl[z^{\mathbf{T}_{0}}\bigr]=\sum_{n\geq 1}\mathbb{P}[\mathbf{T}_{0}=n]\cdot z^{n}=\sum_{n\geq 1}\mathbb{P}[\mathbf{e}_{\tau}=n]\cdot z^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\underbrace{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in\mathbb{N},\\
x\in V_{1}^{\times}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}X_{m-1}\notin C(x),\\
X_{m}=x\end{array}\right]\cdot z^{m}\cdot\mathbb{P}_{x}\bigl[\forall j\geq 1:X_{j}\in C(x)\bigr]}_{\textrm{case $\tau=1$}}

\displaystyle\quad+\underbrace{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{N},\\
x_{1}\in V_{1}^{\times},\\
y_{1}\in V_{2}^{\times}\end{subarray}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}X_{n-1}\notin C(y_{1}x_{1}),\\
X_{n}=y_{1}x_{1}\end{array}\right]\cdot z^{n}\cdot\mathbb{P}_{y_{1}x_{1}}\bigl[\forall j\geq 1:X_{j}\in C(y_{1}x_{1})\bigr]}_{\textrm{case $\tau=2$}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in V_{1}^{\times}}G(o,x|z)\cdot(1-\xi_{1})+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{1}\in V_{1}^{\times},\\
y_{1}\in V_{2}^{\times}\end{subarray}}G(o,y_{1}x_{1}|z)\cdot(1-\xi_{1})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{eq:G-L})}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in V_{1}^{\times}}G(o,o|z)\cdot L(o,x|z)\cdot(1-\xi_{1})

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{x_{1}\in V_{1}^{\times},y_{1}\in V_{2}^{\times}}G(o,o|z)\cdot L(o,y_{1}x_{1}|z)\cdot(1-\xi_{1})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{eq:L-L}),(\ref{eq:G-xi})}}{{=}}

\displaystyle G(o,o|z)\cdot(1-\xi_{1})

\displaystyle\quad\cdot\Biggl(\sum_{x\in V_{1}^{\times}}L_{1}\bigl(o_{1},x\,\bigl|\,\xi_{1}(z)\bigr)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{1}\in V_{1}^{\times},\\
y_{1}\in V_{2}^{\times}\end{subarray}}L_{2}\bigl(o_{2},y_{1}\,\bigl|\,\xi_{2}(z)\bigr)\cdot L_{1}\bigl(o_{1},x_{1}\,\bigl|\,\xi_{1}(z)\bigr)\Biggr)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{G(o,o|z)\cdot(1-\xi_{1})}{\bigl(1-\xi_{1}(z)\bigr)\cdot G_{1}\bigl(o_{1},o_{1}\,\bigl|\,\xi_{1}(z)\bigr)}\cdot\biggl(1+\frac{1}{\bigl(1-\xi_{2}(z)\bigr)\cdot G_{2}\bigl(o_{2},o_{2}\,\bigl|\,\xi_{2}(z)\bigr)}\biggr).

Since
G(o,o|z)
,
G_{i}\bigl(o_{i},o_{i}\,\bigl|\,\xi_{i}(z)\bigr)
and
\xi_{i}(z)
,
i\in\mathcal{I}
, have radii of convergence strictly bigger than
1
and
\xi_{i}(1)<1
, Pringsheim’s Theorem yields that the power series
\sum_{n\geq 1}\mathbb{P}[\mathbf{T}_{0}=n]\cdot z^{n}
has also radius of convergence strictly bigger than
1
. Hence,
\mathbf{T}_{0}
has exponential moments.
∎

Another essential property is given by the next proposition:

Proposition 3.3
.

(\mathbf{T}_{k}-\mathbf{T}_{k-1})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is an i.i.d. sequence.

Proof.

Let be
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, and denote the support of
X_{\mathbf{T}_{k}}
by

\mathcal{W}_{k}:=\Bigl\{x\in V\,\Bigl|\,\|x\|\in\{2k+1,2k+2\},\delta(x)=1\Bigr\}.

First, we prove that
\mathbf{T}_{k}-\mathbf{T}_{k-1}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, has the same distribution as
\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}
. To this end, we make a case distinction according to the values of
\mathbf{T}_{k-1},\mathbf{T}_{k}
and
X_{\mathbf{T}_{k-1}}
,
X_{\mathbf{T}_{k}}
. For each
m\in\mathbb{N}
, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigl[\mathbf{T}_{k}-\mathbf{T}_{k-1}=m\bigr]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\mathcal{W}_{k-1},\\
x_{1}\in V_{1}^{\times},\\
y_{1}\in V_{2}^{\times}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}\Bigl[\mathbf{T}_{k}-\mathbf{T}_{k-1}=m,X_{\mathbf{T}_{k-1}}=x,X_{\mathbf{T}_{k}}=xy_{1}x_{1}\Bigr]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l\in\mathbb{N},\\
x\in\mathcal{W}_{k-1}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}X_{l-1}\notin C(x),\\
X_{l}=x\end{array}\right]\cdot\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{1}\in V_{1}^{\times},\\
y_{1}\in V_{2}^{\times}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}_{x}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}X_{m-1}\notin C(xy_{1}x_{1}),\\
X_{m}=xy_{1}x_{1},\\
\forall m^{\prime}<m:X_{m^{\prime}}\in C(x)\end{array}\right]

\displaystyle\quad\cdot\underbrace{\mathbb{P}_{xy_{1}x_{1}}\bigl[\forall j\geq 1:X_{j}\in C(xy_{1}x_{1})\bigr]}_{=1-\xi_{1}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{Lemma \ref{lem:cone-probabilities}}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\underbrace{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l\in\mathbb{N},\\
x\in\mathcal{W}_{k-1}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}X_{l-1}\notin C(x),\\
X_{l}=x\end{array}\right]\cdot(1-\xi_{1})}_{=\mathbb{P}[\mathbf{T}_{k-1}<\infty]=1}\cdot\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{1}\in V_{1}^{\times},\\
y_{1}\in V_{2}^{\times}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}X_{m-1}\notin C(y_{1}x_{1}),\\
X_{m}=y_{1}x_{1},\\
\forall m^{\prime}<m:X_{m^{\prime}}\notin V_{1}^{\times}\end{array}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\underbrace{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l\in\mathbb{N},\\
x\in\mathcal{W}_{0}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}X_{l-1}\notin C(x),\\
X_{l}=x\end{array}\right]\cdot(1-\xi_{1})}_{=\mathbb{P}[\mathbf{T}_{0}<\infty]=1}\cdot\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{1}\in V_{1}^{\times},\\
y_{1}\in V_{2}^{\times}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}X_{m-1}\notin C(y_{1}x_{1}),\\
X_{m}=y_{1}x_{1},\\
\forall m^{\prime}<m:X_{m^{\prime}}\notin V_{1}^{\times}\end{array}\right]

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{Lemma \ref{lem:cone-probabilities}}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l\in\mathbb{N},\\
x\in\mathcal{W}_{0}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}X_{l-1}\notin C(x),\\
X_{l}=x\end{array}\right]\cdot\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{1}\in V_{1}^{\times},\\
y_{1}\in V_{2}^{\times}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}_{x}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}X_{m-1}\notin C(xy_{1}x_{1}),\\
X_{m}=xy_{1}x_{1},\\
\forall m^{\prime}<m:X_{m^{\prime}}\in C(x)\end{array}\right]\cdot(1-\xi_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}=m].

This shows that the sequence
\bigl(\mathbf{T}_{k+1}-\mathbf{T}_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is identically distributed.

For the proof of independence, let be
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
m_{1},\ldots,m_{k}\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then, as we have seen above, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigl[\mathbf{T}_{j}-\mathbf{T}_{j-1}=m_{j}\bigr]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l\in\mathbb{N},\\
w_{j-1}\in\mathcal{W}_{j-1}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{j}\in V_{1}^{\times},\\
y_{j}\in V_{2}^{\times}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}X_{\mathbf{T}_{j-1}}=w_{j-1},X_{\mathbf{T}_{j}}=w_{j-1}y_{j}x_{j},\\
\mathbf{T}_{j-1}=l,\mathbf{T}_{j}=l+m_{j}\end{array}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l\in\mathbb{N},\\
w_{j-1}\in\mathcal{W}_{j-1},\\
x_{j}\in V_{1}^{\times},\\
y_{j}\in V_{2}^{\times}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}X_{l-1}\notin C(w_{j-1}),\\
X_{l}=w_{j-1}\end{array}\right]

\displaystyle\quad\cdot\mathbb{P}_{w_{j-1}}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}X_{m_{j}-1}\notin C(w_{j-1}y_{j}x_{j}),\\
X_{m_{j}}=w_{j-1}y_{j}x_{j},\\
\forall m^{\prime}<m:X_{m^{\prime}}\in C(w_{j-1})\end{array}\right]\cdot(1-\xi_{1}).

In the following we set
w_{0}:=o
, and for
x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\in V_{1}^{\times}
,
y_{1},\ldots,y_{k}\in V_{2}^{\times}
, we set
w_{j}:=y_{1}x_{1}\ldots y_{j}x_{j}
for
j\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
. Then we obtain:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Bigl[\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}=m_{1},\mathbf{T}_{2}-\mathbf{T}_{1}=m_{2},\ldots,\mathbf{T}_{k}-\mathbf{T}_{k-1}=m_{k}\Bigr]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\mathcal{W}_{0},\\
x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\in V_{1}^{\times},\\
y_{1},\ldots,y_{k}\in V_{2}^{\times}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}\left[[X_{\mathbf{T}_{0}}=x]\cap\bigcap_{j=1}^{k}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\mathbf{T}_{j}-\mathbf{T}_{j-1}=m_{j},\\
X_{\mathbf{T}_{j}}=xy_{1}x_{1}\ldots y_{j}x_{j}\end{array}\right]\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l\in\mathbb{N},\\
x\in\mathcal{W}_{0}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}X_{l-1}\notin C(x),\\
X_{l}=x\end{array}\right]

\displaystyle\quad\cdot\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\in V_{1}^{\times},\\
y_{1},\ldots,y_{k}\in V_{2}^{\times}\end{subarray}}\Biggl(\prod_{j=1}^{k}\mathbb{P}_{xw_{j-1}}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}X_{m_{j}-1}\notin C(xw_{j}),\\
X_{m_{j}}=xw_{j},\\
\forall m^{\prime}<m_{j}:X_{m^{\prime}}\in C(xw_{j-1})\end{array}\right]\Biggr)\cdot(1-\xi_{1})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{Lemma \ref{lem:cone-probabilities}}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l\in\mathbb{N},\\
x\in\mathcal{W}_{0}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}X_{l-1}\notin C(x),\\
X_{l}=x\end{array}\right]\cdot\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{1}\in V_{1}^{\times},\\
y_{1}\in V_{2}^{\times}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}_{x}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}X_{m_{1}-1}\notin C(xy_{1}x_{1}),\\
X_{m_{1}}=xy_{1}x_{1},\\
\forall m^{\prime}<m_{1}:X_{m^{\prime}}\in C(x)\end{array}\right]

\displaystyle\quad\cdot\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{2},\ldots,x_{k}\in V_{1}^{\times},\\
y_{2},\ldots,y_{k}\in V_{2}^{\times}\end{subarray}}\Biggl(\prod_{j=2}^{k}\underbrace{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\in\mathbb{N},\\
w\in\mathcal{W}_{j-1}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}X_{t-1}\notin C(w),\\
X_{t}=w\end{array}\right]\cdot(1-\xi_{1})}_{=\mathbb{P}[\mathbf{T}_{j-1}<\infty]=1}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\cdot\mathbb{P}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}X_{m_{j}-1}\notin C(y_{j}x_{j}),\\
X_{m_{j}}=y_{j}x_{j},\\
\forall m^{\prime}<m_{j}:X_{m^{\prime}}\notin V_{1}^{\times}\end{array}\right]\Biggr)\cdot(1-\xi_{1})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{Lemma \ref{lem:cone-probabilities}}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l\in\mathbb{N},\\
x\in\mathcal{W}_{0}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}X_{l-1}\notin C(x),\\
X_{l}=x\end{array}\right]\cdot\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{1}\in V_{1}^{\times},\\
y_{1}\in V_{2}^{\times}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}_{x}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}X_{m_{1}-1}\notin C(xy_{1}x_{1}),\\
X_{m_{1}}=xy_{1}x_{1},\\
\forall m^{\prime}<m_{1}:X_{m^{\prime}}\in C(x)\end{array}\right]\cdot(1-\xi_{1})

\displaystyle\quad\cdot\prod_{j=2}^{k}\sum_{x_{j}\in V_{1}^{\times},y_{j}\in V_{2}^{\times}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\in\mathbb{N},\\
w\in\mathcal{W}_{j-1}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}X_{t-1}\notin C(w),\\
X_{t}=w\end{array}\right]

\displaystyle\quad\quad\cdot\mathbb{P}_{w}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}X_{m_{j}-1}\notin C(wy_{j}x_{j}),\\
X_{m_{j}}=wy_{j}x_{j},\\
\forall m^{\prime}<m_{j}:X_{m^{\prime}}\in C(w)\end{array}\right]\cdot(1-\xi_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{k}\mathbb{P}[\mathbf{T}_{j}-\mathbf{T}_{j-1}=m_{j}].

Hence, we have shown independence of the sequence
(\mathbf{T}_{k}-\mathbf{T}_{k-1})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
. This finishes the proof.
∎

Finally, for random walks on
V
depending on a finite number
d\in\mathbb{N}
of parameters
p_{1},\ldots,p_{d}\in(0,1)
, we show that
\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}]
varies real-analytically when seen as a function in
(p_{1},\dots,p_{d})\in\mathcal{P}_{d}
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Assume that the random walk on
V
depends on finitely many parameters
p_{1},\ldots,p_{d}\in(0,1)
,
d\in\mathbb{N}
. Then the mapping

\mathcal{P}_{d}\ni(p_{1},\ldots,p_{d})\mapsto\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}]

is real-analytic.

Proof.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we can rewrite:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}=n]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in\mathbb{N},\\
x\in\mathcal{W}_{0},\\
x_{1}\in V_{1}^{\times},y_{1}\in V_{2}^{\times}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}\Bigl[X_{\mathbf{T}_{0}}=x,X_{\mathbf{T}_{1}}=xy_{1}x_{1},\mathbf{T}_{0}=m,\mathbf{T}_{1}=m+n\Bigr]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in\mathbb{N},\\
x\in\mathcal{W}_{0}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}X_{m-1}\notin C(x),\\
X_{m}=x\end{array}\right]\cdot\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{1}\in V_{1}^{\times},\\
y_{1}\in V_{2}^{\times}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}_{x}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}X_{n-1}\notin C(xy_{1}x_{1}),\\
X_{n}=xy_{1}x_{1},\\
\forall t<n:X_{t}\in C(x)\end{array}\right]

\displaystyle\quad\cdot\underbrace{\mathbb{P}_{xy_{1}x_{1}}\bigl[\forall j\geq 1:X_{j}\in C(xy_{1}x_{1})\bigr]}_{=1-\xi_{1}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{Lemma \ref{lem:cone-probabilities}}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\underbrace{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in\mathbb{N},\\
x\in\mathcal{W}_{0}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}X_{m-1}\notin C(x),\\
X_{m}=x\end{array}\right]\cdot(1-\xi_{1})}_{=\mathbb{P}[\mathbf{T}_{0}<\infty]=1}\cdot\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{1}\in V_{1}^{\times},\\
y_{1}\in V_{2}^{\times}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}X_{n-1}\notin C(y_{1}x_{1}),\\
X_{n}=y_{1}x_{1},\\
\forall t<n:X_{t}\notin V_{1}^{\times}\end{array}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{x_{1}\in V_{1}^{\times},y_{1}\in V_{2}^{\times}}\mathbb{P}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}X_{n-1}\notin C(y_{1}x_{1}),\\
X_{n}=y_{1}x_{1},\\
\forall t<n:X_{t}\notin V_{1}^{\times}\end{array}\right].

The summands in the last sum describe probabilities which depend on paths of length
n\in\mathbb{N}
only.
Therefore, the probabilities
\mathbb{P}[\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}=n]
can be written in the form

(32)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},\ldots,n_{d}\geq 0:\\
n_{1}+\ldots+n_{d}=n\end{subarray}}c(n_{1},\ldots,n_{d})\cdot p_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdot\ldots\cdot p_{d}^{n_{d}},

where
c(n_{1},\ldots,n_{d})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Of course, we are only allowed to vary the parameters
p_{1},\ldots,p_{d}>0
in such a way that these parameter values still allow a well-defined random walk on
V
.
Since
\sum_{n\geq 1}\mathbb{P}[\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}=n]\cdot z^{n}
has radius of convergence strictly bigger than
1
due to existence of exponential moments of
\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}
, we have for sufficiently small
\delta>0
that

(33)

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}\mathbb{P}[\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}=n]\cdot(1+\delta)^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},\ldots,n_{d}\geq 0:\\
n_{1}+\ldots+n_{d}=n\end{subarray}}c(n_{1},\ldots,n_{d})\cdot\bigl(p_{1}(1+\delta)\bigr)^{n_{1}}\cdot\ldots\cdot\bigl(p_{d}(1+\delta)\bigr)^{n_{d}}<\infty.

Therefore,

(34)

\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\biggl[\sum_{n\geq 1}\mathbb{P}[\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}=n]\cdot z^{n}\biggr]\Biggl|_{z=1+\delta}<\infty,

which implies that
\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}]
varies real-analytically in
(p_{1},\ldots,p_{d})
. For further detailed explanations on real-analyticity in terms of
p_{1},\ldots,p_{d}
, we refer to the beginning of Section 5 in
[
8
, p. 299]
.
∎

4.
Central Limit Theorem for the Drift

In this section we will prove Theorem
2.3
. For this purpose, we define for
k\in\mathbb{N}

\mathbf{D}_{k}:=d(X_{\mathbf{T}_{k-1}},X_{\mathbf{T}_{k}})

and set

\widetilde{\mathbf{D}}_{k}:=\mathbf{D}_{k}-(\mathbf{T}_{k}-\mathbf{T}_{k-1})\cdot\lambda=d(X_{\mathbf{T}_{k-1}},X_{\mathbf{T}_{k}})-(\mathbf{T}_{k}-\mathbf{T}_{k-1})\cdot\lambda.

Due to the structure of free products, any path from
o
to
X_{\mathbf{T}_{k}}
has to pass trough
X_{\mathbf{T}_{0}}
,
X_{\mathbf{T}_{1}},\ldots,X_{\mathbf{T}_{k-1}}
. Therefore, we have

(35)

d(o,X_{\mathbf{T}_{k}})=d(o,X_{\mathbf{T}_{0}})+\sum_{j=1}^{k}d(X_{\mathbf{T}_{j-1}},X_{\mathbf{T}_{j}})=d(o,X_{\mathbf{T}_{0}})+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mathbf{D}_{j}.

Proposition 4.1
.

We have:

(i)

\bigl(\mathbf{D}_{k}\bigr)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is an i.i.d. sequence.

(ii)

\bigl(\widetilde{\mathbf{D}}_{k}\bigr)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is an i.i.d. sequence.

(iii)

If
P
depends on finitely many parameters
p_{1},\ldots,p_{d}
, then
\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{D}_{1}]
and
\mathbb{E}\bigl[d(X_{\mathbf{T}_{0}},X_{\mathbf{T}_{1}})(\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0})\bigr]
vary real-analytically in
(p_{1},\ldots,p_{d})
.

Proof.

Since the proofs of (i) and (ii) are completely analogous to the proof of Proposition
3.3
, we outsource both proofs to Appendix
A
.

For the proof of (iii), we note that, for
n,N\in\mathbb{N}
, we can rewrite analogously to the calculations in the proof of Proposition
3.4

\mathbb{P}\Bigl[d(X_{\mathbf{T}_{0}},X_{\mathbf{T}_{1}})=N,\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}=n\Bigr]=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{1}\in V_{1}^{\times},\\
y_{1}\in V_{2}^{\times}:\\
d(o,y_{1}x_{1})=N\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}X_{n-1}\notin C(y_{1}x_{1}),\\
X_{n}=y_{1}x_{1},\\
\forall t<n:X_{t}\notin V_{1}^{\times}\end{array}\right].

The summands in the right sum depend only on paths of length
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Therefore, we can rewrite
\mathbb{P}\bigl[d(X_{\mathbf{T}_{0}},X_{\mathbf{T}_{1}})=N,\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}=n\bigr]
as a sum

(36)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},\ldots,n_{d}\geq 0:\\
n_{1}+\ldots+n_{d}=n\end{subarray}}c_{N}(n_{1},\ldots,n_{d})\cdot p_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdot\ldots\cdot p_{d}^{n_{d}},

where
c_{N}(n_{1},\ldots,n_{d})\in\mathbb{N}
. For sufficiently small
\delta>0
, we have then

\sum_{n,N\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{P}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}d(X_{\mathbf{T}_{0}},X_{\mathbf{T}_{1}})=N,\\
\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}=n\end{array}\right]\cdot(1+\delta)^{n}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{P}\bigl[\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}=n\bigr]\cdot(1+\delta)^{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{equ:T1-T0-sum-finite})}}{{<}}\infty.

We also note that

(37)

\mathbb{P}\Bigl[d(X_{\mathbf{T}_{0}},X_{\mathbf{T}_{1}})=N,\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}=n\Bigr]=0\quad\textrm{ if }N>n.

Since we can rewrite

\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{D}_{1}]=\mathbb{E}\bigl[d(X_{\mathbf{T}_{0}},X_{\mathbf{T}_{1}})\bigr]=\sum_{n,N\in\mathbb{N}}N\cdot\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},\ldots,n_{d}\geq 0:\\
n_{1}+\ldots+n_{d}=n\end{subarray}}c_{N}(n_{1},\ldots,n_{d})\cdot p_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdot\ldots\cdot p_{d}^{n_{d}}

and

\displaystyle\sum_{n,N\in\mathbb{N}}N\cdot\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},\ldots,n_{d}\geq 0:\\
n_{1}+\ldots+n_{d}=n\end{subarray}}c_{N}(n_{1},\ldots,n_{d})\cdot\bigl(p_{1}(1+\delta)\bigr)^{n_{1}}\cdot\ldots\cdot\bigl(p_{d}(1+\delta)\bigr)^{n_{d}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n,N\in\mathbb{N}}N\cdot\mathbb{P}\bigl[d(X_{\mathbf{T}_{0}},X_{\mathbf{T}_{1}})=N,\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}=n\bigr]\cdot(1+\delta)^{n}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{equ:zero-prob})}}{{\leq}}

\displaystyle\sum_{n,N\in\mathbb{N}}n\cdot\mathbb{P}\bigl[d(X_{\mathbf{T}_{0}},X_{\mathbf{T}_{1}})=N,\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}=n\bigr]\cdot(1+\delta)^{n}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1+\delta)\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\biggl[\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{P}\bigl[\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}=n\bigr]\cdot z^{n}\biggr]\Biggl|_{z=1+\delta}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{equ:T1-T0-sum-finite-2})}}{{<}}\infty,

we obtain that
\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{D}_{1}]=\mathbb{E}\bigl[d(X_{\mathbf{T}_{0}},X_{\mathbf{T}_{1}})\bigr]
varies real-analytically in the random walk parameters
(p_{1},\ldots,p_{d})\in\mathcal{P}_{d}
.

Real-analyticity of
\mathbb{E}\bigl[d(X_{\mathbf{T}_{0}},X_{\mathbf{T}_{1}})(\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0})\bigr]
follows analogously from

\displaystyle\sum_{n,N\in\mathbb{N}}N\cdot n\cdot\mathbb{P}\bigl[d(X_{\mathbf{T}_{0}},X_{\mathbf{T}_{1}})=N,\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}=n\bigr]\cdot(1+\delta)^{n}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{equ:zero-prob})}}{{\leq}}

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}n^{2}\cdot\mathbb{P}\bigl[\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}=n\bigr]\cdot(1+\delta)^{n}<\infty,

together with exponential moments of
\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}
. ∎

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, set

\mathbf{k}(n):=\sup\bigl\{m\in\mathbb{N}\,\bigl|\,\mathbf{T}_{m}\leq n\bigr\}.

Then
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbf{k}(n)=\infty
almost surely since
\mathbf{T}_{k}<\infty
almost surely for each
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Furthermore, due to Propositions
3.1
and
3.3
, the Strong Law of Large Numbers and almost sure finiteness of
\mathbf{T}_{0}
give

(38)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}}{\mathbf{k}(n)}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\mathbf{k}(n)}\sum_{k=1}^{\mathbf{k}(n)}(\mathbf{T}_{k}-\mathbf{T}_{k-1})=\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}]\quad\textrm{almost surely.}

We have the following alternative formula for the drift
\lambda
:

Corollary 4.2
.

\lambda=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{d(o,X_{n})}{n}=\frac{\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{D}_{1}]}{\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}]}\quad\textrm{almost surely.}

Proof.

Since
\lambda
exists due to
[
5
, Corollary 4.2]
and
\mathbf{T}_{0}<\infty
almost surely, (
35
) yields

\lambda=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{d(o,X_{\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}})}{\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\mathbf{k}(n)}{\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}}\frac{1}{\mathbf{k}(n)}\sum_{i=1}^{\mathbf{k}(n)}\mathbf{D}_{i}\quad\textrm{almost surely.}

As
0\leq\mathbf{D}_{1}\leq\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}
we have
\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{D}_{1}]<\infty
.
The Strong Law of Large Numbers together with Proposition
4.1
and (
38
) yield now the proposed formula for the drift
\lambda
.
∎

Now we can show in the next two lemmas that the random variables
\widetilde{\mathbf{D}}_{k}
are centralized and have finite, non-trivial variance.

Lemma 4.3
.

\bar{\sigma}_{\lambda}^{2}:=\mathrm{Var}\bigl(\widetilde{\mathbf{D}}_{1}\bigr)<\infty
.

Proof.

This follows from

0\leq\bigl|\mathbf{D}_{1}-(\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0})\cdot\lambda\bigr|\leq d(X_{\mathbf{T}_{0}},X_{\mathbf{T}_{1}})+(\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0})\cdot\lambda\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\lambda\in(0,1]}}{{\leq}}2\cdot(\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0})

and the fact that
\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}
has exponential moments
(Proposition
3.1
).

∎

Lemma 4.4
.

We have
\mathbb{E}\bigl[\widetilde{\mathbf{D}}_{1}\bigr]=0
and

\bar{\sigma}_{\lambda}^{2}=\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\Bigl(d(X_{\mathbf{T}_{0}},X_{\mathbf{T}_{1}})-(\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0})\cdot\lambda\Bigr)^{2}\Bigr]>0.

Proof.

Corollary
4.2
implies

\mathbb{E}\bigl[\widetilde{\mathbf{D}}_{1}\bigr]=\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\mathbf{D}_{1}-(\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0})\cdot\lambda\Bigr]=\mathbb{E}\bigl[\mathbf{D}_{1}\bigr]-\lambda\cdot\underbrace{\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}]}_{=\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{D}_{1}]/\lambda}=0.

The variance formula for
\bar{\sigma}_{\lambda}^{2}
follows now directly from
\mathbb{E}\bigl[\widetilde{\mathbf{D}}_{1}\bigr]=0
. Moreover,
d(X_{\mathbf{T}_{0}},X_{\mathbf{T}_{1}})-(\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0})\cdot\lambda
is not constant, which we show by constructing two different paths yielding two different values for that difference with positive probability. Take any
x_{0}\in V_{1}^{\times}
with
p_{1}^{(m_{0})}(o_{1},x_{0})>0
and
p_{1}^{(n_{0})}(x_{0},x_{0})>0
for some
m_{0},n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
(recall the assumption made at the beginning of Subsection
2.2
), and choose any
y\in V_{2}^{\times}
with
p_{2}(o_{2},y)>0
. Then:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Bigl[X_{\mathbf{T}_{0}}=x_{0},X_{\mathbf{T}_{1}}=x_{0}yx_{0},\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}=m_{0}+1\Bigr]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}^{m_{0}}p_{1}^{(m_{0})}(o_{1},x_{0})\cdot\alpha_{2}\cdot p_{2}(o_{2},y)\cdot\alpha_{1}^{m_{0}}p_{1}^{(m_{0})}(o_{1},x_{0})\cdot(1-\xi_{1})>0.

But we also have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Bigl[X_{\mathbf{T}_{0}}=x_{0},X_{\mathbf{T}_{1}-n_{0}}=X_{\mathbf{T}_{1}}=x_{0}yx_{0},\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}=m_{0}+1+n_{0}\Bigr]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}^{2m_{0}+n_{0}}p_{1}^{(m_{0})}(o_{1},x_{0})^{2}\cdot\alpha_{2}\cdot p_{2}(o_{2},y)\cdot p_{1}^{(n_{0})}(x_{0},x_{0})\cdot(1-\xi_{1})>0,

since we can add a loop of length
n_{0}
at
x_{0}yx_{0}
within
C(x_{0}y)
. As
\lambda>0
and we have in both cases
d(X_{\mathbf{T}_{0}},X_{\mathbf{T}_{1}})=d(x_{0},x_{0}yx_{0})
but different values of
\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}
, we have shown that the difference
d(X_{\mathbf{T}_{0}},X_{\mathbf{T}_{1}})-(\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0})\lambda
is not constant, providing a strictly positive variance
\bar{\sigma}_{\lambda}^{2}>0
.
∎

For
k\in\mathbb{N}
, define

\mathbf{S}_{k}:=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mathbf{D}_{j}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}d(X_{\mathbf{T}_{j-1}},X_{\mathbf{T}_{j}})=d(X_{\mathbf{T}_{0}},X_{\mathbf{T}_{k}})=d(o,X_{\mathbf{T}_{k}})-d(o,X_{\mathbf{T}_{0}}),

and set

\widetilde{\mathbf{S}}_{k}:=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\widetilde{\mathbf{D}}_{j}=\mathbf{S}_{k}-(\mathbf{T}_{k}-\mathbf{T}_{0})\cdot\lambda.

After all this cumbersome preliminary work we can now follow the approach in
[
8
, Section 4]
in order to derive the proposed central limit theorem.

Lemma 4.5
.

\frac{d(o,X_{n})-\mathbf{S}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}}{\sqrt{n}}\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}0.

Proof.

Let be
\varepsilon>0
.
For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle d(o,X_{n})-\mathbf{S}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}\leq d(X_{\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}},X_{n})+d(o,X_{\mathbf{T}_{0}})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle n-\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}+\mathbf{T}_{0}\leq\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{k}(n)+1}-\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}+\mathbf{T}_{0}.

Since both
\mathbf{T}_{0}
and
\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}
have exponential moments, we get:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Bigl[d(o,X_{n})-\mathbf{S}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}>\varepsilon\sqrt{n},\mathbf{k}(n)\geq 1\Bigr]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Bigl[\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{k}(n)+1}-\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}+\mathbf{T}_{0}>\varepsilon\sqrt{n},\mathbf{k}(n)\geq 1\Bigr]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Bigl[\exists k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}:\mathbf{T}_{k+1}-\mathbf{T}_{k}+\mathbf{T}_{0}>\varepsilon\sqrt{n},\mathbf{k}(n)\geq 1\Bigr]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Bigl[\exists k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}:\mathbf{T}_{k+1}-\mathbf{T}_{k}>\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\sqrt{n}\Bigr]+\mathbb{P}\Bigl[\mathbf{T}_{0}>\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\sqrt{n}\Bigr]

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{Proposition \ref{prop:Tk-Tk-1-iid}}}}{{\leq}}

\displaystyle n\cdot\mathbb{P}\Bigl[\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}>\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\sqrt{n}\Bigr]+\mathbb{P}\Bigl[\mathbf{T}_{0}>\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\sqrt{n}\Bigr]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle n\cdot\mathbb{P}\biggl[(\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0})^{4}>\frac{\varepsilon^{4}}{2^{4}}n^{2}\biggr]+\mathbb{P}\Bigl[\mathbf{T}_{0}>\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\sqrt{n}\Bigr]

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{Markov Inequality}}}{{\leq}}

\displaystyle n\cdot\frac{\mathbb{E}\Bigl[(\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0})^{4}\Bigr]}{\frac{\varepsilon^{4}}{2^{4}}n^{2}}+\frac{\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{T}_{0}]}{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\sqrt{n}}\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}0.

Since
\mathbf{k}(n)\to\infty
almost surely, we have proven the proposed claim.
∎

By Billingsley
[
1
, Theorem 14.4]
, we have the convergence in distribution

\frac{\widetilde{\mathbf{S}}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}}{\bar{\sigma}_{\lambda}\cdot\sqrt{\mathbf{k}(n)}}\xrightarrow{\mathcal{D}}N(0,1).

The convergence of (
38
) yields

0\leq\frac{n-\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}}{\mathbf{k}(n)}\leq\frac{\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{k}(n)+1}-\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}}{\mathbf{k}(n)}\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}0\quad\textrm{almost surely.}

This in turn implies

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{n}{\mathbf{k}(n)}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{n-\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}}{\mathbf{k}(n)}+\frac{\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}}{\mathbf{k}(n)}=\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}]\quad\textrm{almost surely.}

Therefore, an application of the Lemma of Slutsky gives

(39)

\frac{\widetilde{\mathbf{S}}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}}{\sigma_{\lambda}\cdot\sqrt{n}}=\frac{\widetilde{\mathbf{S}}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}}{\sigma_{\lambda}\cdot\sqrt{\mathbf{k}(n)}}\frac{\sqrt{\mathbf{k}(n)}}{\sqrt{n}}\xrightarrow{\mathcal{D}}N(0,1),

where

(40)

\sigma_{\lambda}^{2}=\frac{\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\Bigl(d(X_{\mathbf{T}_{0}},X_{\mathbf{T}_{1}})-(\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0})\cdot\lambda\Bigr)^{2}\Bigr]}{\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}]}.

It remains to control the part of
d(o,X_{n})-n\cdot\lambda
, which is not “covered” by
\widetilde{\mathbf{S}}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}
. To this end, we need the following proposition:

Proposition 4.6
.

For each
\varepsilon>0
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\Bigl[\Bigl|\widetilde{\mathbf{S}}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}-\bigl(d(o,X_{n})-n\cdot\lambda\bigr)\Bigr|>\varepsilon\cdot\sqrt{n}\Bigr]=0.

Proof.

Let be
\varepsilon>0
. Recall that

\widetilde{\mathbf{S}}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}=\mathbf{S}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}-(\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}-\mathbf{T}_{0})\cdot\lambda.

Therefore, for
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Bigl[\Bigl|\widetilde{\mathbf{S}}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}-\bigl(d(o,X_{n})-n\cdot\lambda\bigr)\Bigr|>\varepsilon\cdot\sqrt{n},\mathbf{k}(n)\geq 1\Bigr]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Bigl[\Bigl|\mathbf{S}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}-(\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}-\mathbf{T}_{0})\cdot\lambda-\bigl(d(o,X_{n})-n\cdot\lambda\bigr)\Bigr|>\varepsilon\cdot\sqrt{n},\mathbf{k}(n)\geq 1\Bigr]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Bigl[d(o,X_{n})-\mathbf{S}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}>\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\cdot\sqrt{n},\mathbf{k}(n)\geq 1\Bigr]

\displaystyle\quad+\mathbb{P}\Bigl[\lambda\cdot\bigl(n-(\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}-\mathbf{T}_{0})\bigr)>\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\cdot\sqrt{n},\mathbf{k}(n)\geq 1\Bigr].

Since
\mathbf{k}(n)\to\infty
almost surely Lemma
4.5
yields

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\Bigl[d(o,X_{n})-\mathbf{S}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}>\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\cdot\sqrt{n},\mathbf{k}(n)\geq 1\Bigr]=0.

On the other hand side, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Bigl[\lambda\cdot\bigl(n-(\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}-\mathbf{T}_{0})\bigr)>\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\cdot\sqrt{n},\mathbf{k}(n)\geq 1\Bigr]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Bigl[\lambda\cdot\bigl(\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{k}(n)+1}-(\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}-\mathbf{T}_{0})\bigr)>\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\cdot\sqrt{n},\mathbf{k}(n)\geq 1\Bigr]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Bigl[\exists k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}:\lambda\cdot\bigl(\mathbf{T}_{k+1}-(\mathbf{T}_{k}-\mathbf{T}_{0})\bigr)>\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\cdot\sqrt{n}\Bigr]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Bigl[\exists k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}:\mathbf{T}_{k+1}-\mathbf{T}_{k}>\frac{\varepsilon}{4\lambda}\cdot\sqrt{n}\Bigr]+\mathbb{P}\Bigl[\mathbf{T}_{0}>\frac{\varepsilon}{4\lambda}\cdot\sqrt{n}\Bigr]

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{Proposition \ref{prop:Tk-Tk-1-iid}}}}{{\leq}}

\displaystyle n\cdot\mathbb{P}\Bigl[\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}>\frac{\varepsilon}{4\lambda}\cdot\sqrt{n}\Bigr]+\mathbb{P}\Bigl[\mathbf{T}_{0}>\frac{\varepsilon}{4\lambda}\cdot\sqrt{n}\Bigr]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n\cdot\mathbb{P}\biggl[(\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0})^{4}>\frac{\varepsilon^{4}}{(4\lambda)^{4}}\cdot n^{2}\biggr]+\mathbb{P}\Bigl[\mathbf{T}_{0}>\frac{\varepsilon}{4\lambda}\cdot\sqrt{n}\Bigr]

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{Markov Inequality}}}{{\leq}}

\displaystyle n\cdot\frac{\mathbb{E}\bigl[(\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0})^{4}\bigr]}{\frac{\varepsilon^{4}}{(4\lambda)^{4}}\cdot n^{2}}+\frac{\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{T}_{0}]}{\frac{\varepsilon}{4\lambda}\cdot\sqrt{n}}\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}0,

where the exponential moments of
\mathbf{T}_{0}
and
\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}
guarantee that the occuring expectations are finite.
Since
\mathbf{k}(n)\to\infty
almost surely we have finally shown that

\mathbb{P}\Bigl[\widetilde{\mathbf{S}}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}-\bigl(d(o,X_{n})-n\cdot\lambda\bigr)>\varepsilon\cdot\sqrt{n}\Bigr]\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}0.

∎

Now we can give the proof of Theorem
2.3
:

Proof of Theorem
2.3
.

Proposition
4.6
and the convergence in (
39
) together with an application of the Lemma of Slutsky finally give the proposed central limit theorem:

\frac{d(o,X_{n})-n\cdot\lambda}{\sqrt{n}}=\underbrace{\frac{d(o,X_{n})-\widetilde{\mathbf{S}}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}-n\cdot\lambda}{\sqrt{n}}}_{\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}0}+\underbrace{\frac{\widetilde{\mathbf{S}}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}}{\sqrt{n}}}_{\xrightarrow{\mathcal{D}}N(0,\sigma_{\lambda}^{2})}\xrightarrow{\mathcal{D}}N(0,\sigma_{\lambda}^{2}).

It remains to show that
\sigma^{2}_{\lambda}
varies real-analytically if the random walk on
V
depends on finitely many parameters
p_{1},\ldots,p_{d}
,
d\in\mathbb{N}
. By the formula in (
40
) and real-analyticity of
\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}]
(see Proposition
3.4
), it remains to show that the mapping

\mathcal{P}_{d}\ni(p_{1},\ldots,p_{d})\mapsto\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\Bigl(d(X_{\mathbf{T}_{0}},X_{\mathbf{T}_{1}})-(\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0})\cdot\lambda\Bigr)^{2}\Bigr]

varies real-analytically. Since

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\Bigl(d(X_{\mathbf{T}_{0}},X_{\mathbf{T}_{1}})-(\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0})\cdot\lambda\Bigr)^{2}\Bigr]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigl[d(X_{\mathbf{T}_{0}},X_{\mathbf{T}_{1}})^{2}\bigr]-2\cdot\lambda\cdot\mathbb{E}\bigl[d(X_{\mathbf{T}_{0}},X_{\mathbf{T}_{1}})(\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0})\bigr]+\lambda^{2}\cdot\mathbb{E}\bigl[(\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0})^{2}\bigr]

and

\lambda=\frac{\mathbb{E}\bigl[d(X_{\mathbf{T}_{0}},X_{\mathbf{T}_{1}})\bigr]}{\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}]}

it suffices – in view of Propositions
3.4
and
4.1
.(iii) – to study the mappings

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{d}\ni(p_{1},\ldots,p_{d})

\displaystyle\mapsto

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigl[(\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0})^{2}\bigr],

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{d}\ni(p_{1},\ldots,p_{d})

\displaystyle\mapsto

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigl[d(X_{\mathbf{T}_{0}},X_{\mathbf{T}_{1}})^{2}\bigr].

The first mapping varies real-analytically which follows from the proof of Proposition
3.4
, since the probabilities
\mathbb{P}\bigl[(\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0})^{2}=n^{2}\bigr]
can be written as in (
32
) and
for suffciently small
\delta>0
we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\biggl[\sum_{n\geq 1}\mathbb{P}\bigl[(\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0})^{2}=n^{2}\bigr]\cdot z^{n^{2}}\biggr]\Biggl|_{z=1+\delta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial^{2}z}\biggl[\sum_{n\geq 1}\mathbb{P}\bigl[\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}=n\bigr]\cdot z^{n}\biggr]\Biggl|_{z=1+\delta}+\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\biggl[\sum_{n\geq 1}\mathbb{P}\bigl[\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}=n\bigr]\cdot z^{n}\biggr]\Biggl|_{z=1+\delta}.

Analogously, one can show with the same reasoning as in the proof of Proposition
4.1
.(iii) that
\mathbb{E}\bigl[d(X_{\mathbf{T}_{0}},X_{\mathbf{T}_{1}})^{2}\bigr]
varies real-anaytically: once again, the probabilities
\mathbb{P}\bigl[d(X_{\mathbf{T}_{0}},X_{\mathbf{T}_{1}})^{2}=N,\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}=n\bigr]
can be written as in (
36
) and, for sufficiently small
\delta>0
,

\displaystyle\sum_{n,N\in\mathbb{N}}N^{2}\cdot\mathbb{P}\bigl[d(X_{\mathbf{T}_{0}},X_{\mathbf{T}_{1}})^{2}=N^{2},\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}=n\bigr]\cdot(1+\delta)^{n}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}n^{2}\cdot\mathbb{P}\bigl[\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}=n\bigr]\cdot(1+\delta)^{n}<\infty.

This finishes the proof of Theorem
2.3
.
∎

5.
Central Limit Theorem w.r.t. the Block Length

The reasoning for the proof of the Central Limit Theorem for the rate of escape w.r.t. the block length, Theorem
2.4
, is very similar to the proof of Theorem
2.3
. We replace in Section
4
the graph metric
d(\cdot,\cdot)
by the block length
\|\cdot\|
and redefine for
k\in\mathbb{N}

\mathbf{D}_{k}:=\|X_{\mathbf{T}_{k}}\|-\|X_{\mathbf{T}_{k-1}}\|=(2k+\tau)-\bigl(2(k-1)+\tau\bigr)=2,

and set

\widetilde{\mathbf{D}}_{k}:=\mathbf{D}_{k}-(\mathbf{T}_{k}-\mathbf{T}_{k-1})\cdot\ell=2-(\mathbf{T}_{k}-\mathbf{T}_{k-1})\cdot\ell.

In particular, the sequence
\bigl(\widetilde{\mathbf{D}}_{k}\bigr)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is again i.i.d., which follows now directly from Proposition
3.3
.
We have

\|X_{\mathbf{T}_{k}}\|=2k+\tau=\|X_{\mathbf{T}_{0}}\|+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mathbf{D}_{k}.

The analogue to Corollary
4.2
is then given by

\displaystyle\ell

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\|X_{n}\|}{n}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\|X_{\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}}\|}{\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\underbrace{\frac{\|X_{\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}}\|}{\mathbf{k}(n)}}_{\to 2}\underbrace{\frac{\mathbf{k}(n)}{\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}}}_{\to 1/\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}]}\quad\textrm{ almost surely.}

Moreover:

Lemma 5.1
.

We have
\mathbb{E}\bigl[\widetilde{\mathbf{D}}_{1}\bigr]=0
and

\bar{\sigma}_{\ell}^{2}:=\mathrm{Var}\bigl(\widetilde{\mathbf{D}}_{1}\bigr)=\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\bigl(2-(\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0})\cdot\ell\bigr)^{2}\Bigr]\in(0,\infty).

Proof.

We have

\mathbb{E}\bigl[\widetilde{\mathbf{D}}_{1}\bigr]=2-\ell\cdot\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}]=2-\frac{2}{\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}]}\cdot\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}]=0,

from which the proposed formula for
\bar{\sigma}_{\ell}^{2}
follows. Furthermore,
since
\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}
has exponential moments, we have
\bar{\sigma}_{\ell}^{2}<\infty
. The same reasoning as in the proof of Lemma
4.4
shows that
\bar{\sigma}_{\ell}^{2}>0
.
∎

The analogous definitions of
\mathbf{S}_{k}
and
\widetilde{\mathbf{S}}_{k}
now become

\mathbf{S}_{k}:=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mathbf{D}_{j}=2k

and

\widetilde{\mathbf{S}}_{k}:=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\widetilde{\mathbf{D}}_{j}=2k-(\mathbf{T}_{k}-\mathbf{T}_{0})\cdot\ell.

We have then for
n\in\mathbb{N}
:

0\leq\|X_{n}\|-\mathbf{S}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}\leq\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{k}(n)+1}-\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}+\tau\leq\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{k}(n)+1}-\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}+\mathbf{T}_{0}.

Then we can show completely analogously to the proof of Lemma
4.5
that

(41)

\frac{\|X_{n}\|-\mathbf{S}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}}{\sqrt{n}}\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}0.

Since
(\mathbf{T}_{k}-\mathbf{T}_{k-1})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is i.i.d., we obtain analogously to the reasoning in Section
4
:

(42)

\frac{\widetilde{\mathbf{S}}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}}{\sigma_{\ell}\cdot\sqrt{n}}\xrightarrow{\mathcal{D}}N(0,1),

where

(43)

\sigma_{\ell}^{2}=\frac{\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\bigl(2-(\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0})\cdot\ell\bigr)^{2}\Bigr]}{\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}]}.

It remains to prove the analogue of Proposition
4.6
:

Proposition 5.2
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\Bigl[\Bigl|\widetilde{\mathbf{S}}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}-\bigl(\|X_{n}\|-n\cdot\ell\bigr)\Bigr|>\varepsilon\cdot\sqrt{n}\Bigr]=0.

Proof.

Observe that

\widetilde{\mathbf{S}}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}=2\cdot\mathbf{k}(n)-(\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}-\mathbf{T}_{0})\cdot\ell.

Therefore, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
and every
\varepsilon>0
, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Bigl[\Bigl|\widetilde{\mathbf{S}}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}-\bigl(\|X_{n}\|-n\cdot\ell\bigr)\Bigr|>\varepsilon\cdot\sqrt{n},\mathbf{k}(n)\geq 1\Bigr]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Bigl[\Bigl|2\cdot\mathbf{k}(n)-(\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}-\mathbf{T}_{0})\cdot\ell-\bigl(\|X_{n}\|-n\cdot\ell\bigr)\Bigr|>\varepsilon\cdot\sqrt{n},\mathbf{k}(n)\geq 1\Bigr]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Bigl[\|X_{n}\|-2\cdot\mathbf{k}(n)>\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\cdot\sqrt{n},\mathbf{k}(n)\geq 1\Bigr]

\displaystyle\quad+\mathbb{P}\Bigl[\ell\cdot\bigl(n-(\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}-\mathbf{T}_{0})\bigr)>\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\cdot\sqrt{n},\mathbf{k}(n)\geq 1\Bigr].

Once again, by (
41
),

\frac{\|X_{n}\|-2\cdot\mathbf{k}(n)}{\sqrt{n}}=\frac{\|X_{n}\|-\mathbf{S}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}}{\sqrt{n}}\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}0.

The rest follows as in the proof of Proposition
4.6
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.4
.

Proposition
5.2
and the convergence in (
42
) together with an application of the Lemma of Slutsky finally give the proposed central limit theorem:

\frac{\|X_{n}\|-n\cdot\ell}{\sqrt{n}}=\underbrace{\frac{\|X_{n}\|-\widetilde{\mathbf{S}}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}-n\cdot\ell}{\sqrt{n}}}_{\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}0}+\underbrace{\frac{\widetilde{\mathbf{S}}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}}{\sqrt{n}}}_{\xrightarrow{\mathcal{D}}N(0,\sigma_{\ell}^{2})}\xrightarrow{\mathcal{D}}N(0,\sigma_{\ell}^{2}).

If
P
depends on finitely many parameters
p_{1},\ldots,p_{d}\in(0,1)
only, then real-analyticity of
\sigma_{\ell}^{2}
follows directly from the formula (
43
); recall that we have shown in the the proof of Theorem
2.3
that
\mathbb{E}\bigl[(\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0})^{2}\bigr]
varies real-analytically.
∎

6.
Central Limit Theorem for the Entropy

The reasoning for the proof of the Central Limit Theorem for the asymptotic entropy, Theorem
2.5
, is also similar to the proof of Theorem
2.3
but needs some additional effort. We replace in Section
4
the graph metric
d(\cdot,\cdot)
by the
distance function

d_{L}(o,x):=-\log L(o,x|1)
for
x\in V
. Moreover, we assume that
P
is
\varepsilon_{0}
-uniform for some
\varepsilon_{0}>0
. By
[
7
, Theorem 3.7]
, the asymptotic entropy
h
satisfies

h=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{d_{L}(o,X_{n})}{n}=\lim_{n\to\infty}-\frac{1}{n}\log L(o,X_{n}|1)\quad\textrm{almost surely.}

If
X_{\mathbf{T}_{k-1}}=x\in V
and
X_{\mathbf{T}_{k}}=xy_{k}x_{k}\in V
for
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
x_{k}\in V_{1}^{\times}
and
y_{k}\in V_{2}^{\times}
, then we set
\mathbf{W}_{k}:=y_{k}x_{k}
.
Due to the structure of the free product any path from
o
to
X_{n}
has to pass through
X_{\mathbf{T}_{1}},\ldots,X_{\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}}
. By
(
3
), we can rewrite

L(o,X_{n}|1)=L(o,X_{\mathbf{T}_{0}}|1)\cdot\prod_{k=1}^{\mathbf{k}(n)}\underbrace{L(X_{\mathbf{T}_{k-1}},X_{\mathbf{T}_{k}}|1)}_{=L(o,\mathbf{W}_{k}|1)}\cdot L(X_{\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}},X_{n}|1),

which in turn yields

(44)

d_{L}(o,X_{n})=d_{L}(o,X_{\mathbf{T}_{0}})+\sum_{k=1}^{\mathbf{k}(n)}d_{L}(o,\mathbf{W}_{k}|1)+d_{L}(X_{\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}},X_{n}).

We remark that, for
x_{1}\in V_{1}^{\times},y_{1}\in V_{2}^{\times}
, and small
\delta\geq 0
with
\xi_{1}(1+\delta)<1
and
\xi_{2}(1+\delta)<1
, we have due to (
6
)

\displaystyle\log L(o,y_{1}x_{1}|1+\delta)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\log\frac{1}{\bigl(1-\xi_{1}(1+\delta)\bigr)\bigl(1-\xi_{2}(1+\delta)\bigr)}=:C_{L}(\delta)<\infty.

For
\delta=0
, we just write
C_{L}:=C_{L}(0)
.

On the other hand side,
L(o,y_{1}x_{1}|1)\geq\varepsilon_{0}^{d(o,y_{1}x_{1})}
. Hence,

(45)

\bigl|-\log L(o,y_{1}x_{1}|1)\bigr|\leq\max\bigl\{(-\log\varepsilon_{0})\cdot d(o,y_{1}x_{1}),C_{L}\bigr\}.

We redefine for
k\in\mathbb{N}

\mathbf{D}_{k}:=-\log L(o,\mathbf{W}_{k}|1),

and set

\widetilde{\mathbf{D}}_{k}:=\mathbf{D}_{k}-(\mathbf{T}_{k}-\mathbf{T}_{k-1})\cdot h=-\log L(o,\mathbf{W}_{k}|1)-(\mathbf{T}_{k}-\mathbf{T}_{k-1})\cdot h.

Proposition 6.1
.

(i)

(\mathbf{D}_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}=\bigl(d_{L}(o,\mathbf{W}_{k}|1)\bigr)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is an i.i.d. sequence.

(ii)

\bigl(\widetilde{\mathbf{D}}_{k}\bigr)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is an i.i.d. sequence.

Proof.

(i)

If
z\in\mathbb{R}
with
\mathbb{P}[d_{L}(o,\mathbf{W}_{k})=z]>0
, then we can show
\mathbb{P}[d_{L}(o,\mathbf{W}_{k})=z]=\mathbb{P}[d_{L}(o,\mathbf{W}_{1})=z]
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
completely analogously as in the proof of Proposition
4.1
.(i) by replacing the condition
d(o,y_{1}x_{1})=n
with
d_{L}(o,y_{1}x_{1})=z
.

Similarily, if we have
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
z_{1},\ldots,z_{k}\in\mathbb{R}
satisfying

\mathbb{P}\biggl[\bigcap_{j=1}^{k}[d_{L}(o,\mathbf{W}_{j})=z_{j}]\biggr]>0,

then we can show

\mathbb{P}\biggl[\bigcap_{j=1}^{k}[d_{L}(o,\mathbf{W}_{j})=z_{j}]\biggr]=\prod_{j=1}^{k}\mathbb{P}\bigl[d_{L}(o,\mathbf{W}_{j})=z_{j}\bigr]

completely analogously as in the proof of Proposition
4.1
.(i) by replacing the conditions
d(o,y_{j}x_{j})=n_{j}
with
d_{L}(o,y_{j}x_{j})=z_{j}
.

(ii)

Once again, the proof works completely analogously as the proof of Proposition
4.1
.(ii) by replacing the conditions
d(o,y_{j}x_{j})-n\cdot\lambda=z_{j}
with
d_{L}(o,y_{j}x_{j})-n\cdot h=z_{j}
.

∎

Lemma 6.2
.

\mathbf{D}_{1}
has exponential moments.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\bigl|-\log L(o,\mathbf{W}_{1}|1)\bigr|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{equ:logL-bound})}}{{\leq}}\max\bigl\{C_{L},-\log\varepsilon_{0}^{d(o,\mathbf{W}_{1})}\bigr\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max\bigl\{C_{L},-\log\varepsilon_{0}^{\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}}\bigr\}=\max\bigl\{C_{L},(-\log\varepsilon_{0})\cdot(\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0})\bigr\}.

Since
C_{L}
, seen as a function in
p_{1},\ldots,p_{d}
,
and
\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}
have exponential moments, the claim follows immediately.
∎

The last lemma guarantees that
\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{D}_{1}]<\infty
, and together with Proposition
6.1
and (
44
) we obtain then the analogue to Corollary
4.2
:

(46)

\displaystyle h

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{d_{L}(o,X_{n})}{n}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{d_{L}(o,X_{\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}})}{\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\underbrace{\frac{d_{L}(o,X_{\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}})}{\mathbf{k}(n)}}_{\to\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{D}_{1}]}\underbrace{\frac{\mathbf{k}(n)}{\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}}}_{\to 1/\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{D}_{1}]}{\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}]}\quad\textrm{ almost surely.}

Moreover:

Lemma 6.3
.

We have
\mathbb{E}\bigl[\widetilde{\mathbf{D}}_{1}\bigr]=0
and

\bar{\sigma}_{h}^{2}:=\mathrm{Var}\bigl(\widetilde{\mathbf{D}}_{1}\bigr)=\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\Bigl(-\log L(o,\mathbf{W}_{1}|1)-(\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0})\cdot h\Bigr)^{2}\Bigr]\in(0,\infty).

Proof.

We have

\mathbb{E}\bigl[\widetilde{\mathbf{D}}_{1}\bigr]=\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{D}_{1}]-h\cdot\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{equ:entropy-formula-expectations})}}{{=}}\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{D}_{1}]-\frac{\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{D}_{1}]}{\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}]}\cdot\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}]=0,

from which the proposed formula for
\bar{\sigma}_{h}^{2}
follows. Furthermore,
since
\mathbf{D}_{1}
and
\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}
have exponential moments, we have
\bar{\sigma}_{h}^{2}<\infty
. The same reasoning as in the proof of Lemma
4.4
shows that
\bar{\sigma}_{h}^{2}>0
.
∎

The analogous definitions of
\mathbf{S}_{k}
and
\widetilde{\mathbf{S}}_{k}
now become

\mathbf{S}_{k}:=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mathbf{D}_{j}=-\log L(o,X_{\mathbf{T}_{k}}|1)-\log L(o,X_{\mathbf{T}_{0}}|1)

and

\widetilde{\mathbf{S}}_{k}:=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\widetilde{\mathbf{D}}_{j}.

We have then for
n\in\mathbb{N}
:

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle d_{L}(o,X_{n})-\mathbf{S}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}\leq-\log L(X_{\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}},X_{n}|1)-\log L(o,X_{\mathbf{T}_{0}}|1)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\log\varepsilon_{0}^{n-\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}}-\log\varepsilon_{0}^{\mathbf{T}_{0}}\leq(-\log\varepsilon_{0})\cdot\bigl(\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{k}(n)+1}-\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}+\mathbf{T}_{0}\bigr).

Then we can show completely analogously to the proof of Lemma
4.5
that

(47)

\frac{d_{L}(o,X_{n})-\mathbf{S}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}}{\sqrt{n}}\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}0.

Analogously to the reasoning in Section
4
we obtain

(48)

\frac{\widetilde{\mathbf{S}}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}}{\sigma_{h}\cdot\sqrt{n}}\xrightarrow{\mathcal{D}}N(0,1),

where

(49)

\sigma_{h}^{2}=\frac{\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\Bigl(-\log L(o,\mathbf{W}_{1}|1)-(\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0})\cdot h\Bigr)^{2}\Bigr]}{\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}]}.

It remains to prove the analogue of Proposition
4.6
:

Proposition 6.4
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\Bigl[\Bigl|\widetilde{\mathbf{S}}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}-\bigl(d_{L}(o,X_{n})-n\cdot h\bigr)\Bigr|>\varepsilon\cdot\sqrt{n}\Bigr]=0.

Proof.

Observe that

\widetilde{\mathbf{S}}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}=\mathbf{S}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}-(\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}-\mathbf{T}_{0})\cdot h.

Therefore, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
and every
\varepsilon>0
, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Bigl[\Bigl|\widetilde{\mathbf{S}}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}-\bigl(d_{L}(o,X_{n})-n\cdot h\bigr)\Bigr|>\varepsilon\cdot\sqrt{n},\mathbf{k}(n)\geq 1\Bigr]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Bigl[\Bigl|\mathbf{S}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}-(\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}-\mathbf{T}_{0})\cdot h-\bigl(d_{L}(o,X_{n})-n\cdot h\bigr)\Bigr|>\varepsilon\cdot\sqrt{n},\mathbf{k}(n)\geq 1\Bigr]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Bigl[\bigl|d_{L}(o,X_{n})-\mathbf{S}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}\bigr|>\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\cdot\sqrt{n},\mathbf{k}(n)\geq 1\Bigr]

\displaystyle\quad+\mathbb{P}\Bigl[h\cdot\bigl(n-(\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}-\mathbf{T}_{0})\bigr)>\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\cdot\sqrt{n},\mathbf{k}(n)\geq 1\Bigr].

Once again, by (
47
),
\lim_{n\to\infty}P\Bigl[\bigl|d_{L}(o,X_{n})-\mathbf{S}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}\bigr|>\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\cdot\sqrt{n},\mathbf{k}(n)\geq 1\Bigr]=0
.
The rest follows as in the proof of Proposition
4.6
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.5
.

Proposition
6.4
and the convergence in (
48
) together with an application of the Lemma of Slutsky finally yield the proposed central limit theorem:

\frac{d_{L}(o,X_{n})-n\cdot h}{\sqrt{n}}=\underbrace{\frac{d_{L}(o,X_{n})-\widetilde{\mathbf{S}}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}-n\cdot h}{\sqrt{n}}}_{\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}0}+\underbrace{\frac{\widetilde{\mathbf{S}}_{\mathbf{k}(n)}}{\sqrt{n}}}_{\xrightarrow{\mathcal{D}}N(0,\sigma_{h}^{2})}\xrightarrow{\mathcal{D}}N(0,\sigma_{h}^{2}).

∎

It remains to prove Corollary
2.6
. From now on we consider free products of
finite
graphs (that is,
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
are finite) and we assume that
P
depends on finitely many parameters
p_{1},\ldots,p_{d}
only.
We have:

Lemma 6.5
.

\mathbb{E}\bigl[-\log L(o,\mathbf{W}_{1}|1)\bigr]
and
\mathbb{E}\bigl[-\log L(o,\mathbf{W}_{1}|1)(\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0})\bigr]
vary real-analytically in
p_{1},\ldots,p_{d}
.

Proof.

We follow the reasoning as in the proof of Proposition
4.1
.(iii). For
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
x_{1}\in V_{1}^{\times}
,
y_{1}\in V_{1}^{\times}
,
we rewrite as in the calculations in the proof of Proposition
3.4
:

\mathbb{P}\Bigl[\mathbf{W}_{1}=y_{1}x_{1},\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}=n\Bigr]=\mathbb{P}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}X_{n-1}\notin C(y_{1}x_{1}),\\
X_{n}=y_{1}x_{1},\\
\forall t<n:X_{t}\notin V_{1}^{\times}\end{array}\right].

The summands in the right sum depend only on paths of length
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Therefore, we can rewrite
\mathbb{P}\bigl[\mathbf{W}_{1}=y_{1}x_{1},\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}=n\bigr]
as a sum

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},\ldots,n_{d}\geq 0:\\
n_{1}+\ldots+n_{d}=n\end{subarray}}c(n_{1},\ldots,n_{d})\cdot p_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdot\ldots\cdot p_{d}^{n_{d}},

where
c(n_{1},\ldots,n_{d})\in\mathbb{N}
. For sufficiently small
\delta>0
, we have then

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{N},\\
x_{1}\in V_{1}^{\times},y_{1}\in V_{2}^{\times}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\mathbf{W}_{1}=y_{1}x_{1},\\
\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}=n\end{array}\right]\cdot(1+\delta)^{n}\leq\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{P}\bigl[\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}=n\bigr]\cdot(1+\delta)^{n}<\infty.

Fix now any
\underline{p}=(p_{1},\ldots,p_{d})\in\mathcal{P}_{d}
. For
x_{1}\in V_{1}^{\times},y_{1}\in V_{2}^{\times}
, the function
L(o,y_{1}x_{1}|1)
is obviously real-analytic in
\underline{p}
, since
L(o,y_{1}x_{1}|z)
has radius of convergence strictly bigger than
1
. Since
L(o,y_{1}x_{1}|1)
is continuous in
\underline{p}
and due to
L(o,y_{1}x_{1}|1)\geq\varepsilon_{0}^{N}
,
where
N:=|V_{1}|+|V_{2}|
, there exists a complex neighbourhood
\mathcal{U}(\underline{p})
of
\underline{p}
, where
L(o,y_{1}x_{1}|1)
does not take values in
(-\infty,0]
. Due to finiteness of
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
this neighbourhood
\mathcal{U}(\underline{p})
can be choosen independently of
x_{1},y_{1}
. In particular,
-\log L(o,y_{1}x_{1}|1)
varies real-analytically on
\mathcal{U}(\underline{p})
.
This in turn implies that
\bigl|-\log L(o,y_{1}x_{1}|1)\Bigr|
, when seen as a function in
p_{1},\ldots,p_{d}
, is uniformly bounded on
\mathcal{U}(\underline{p})
by some constant
M_{0}
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{N},\\
x_{1}\in V_{1}^{\times},y_{1}\in V_{2}^{\times}\end{subarray}}|-\log L(o,y_{1}x_{1}|1)|\cdot\mathbb{P}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\mathbf{W}_{1}=y_{1}x_{1},\\
\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}=n\end{array}\right]\cdot(1+\delta)^{n}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\biggl[\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{N},\\
x_{1}\in V_{1}^{\times},y_{1}\in V_{2}^{\times}\end{subarray}}M_{0}\cdot\mathbb{P}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\mathbf{W}_{1}=y_{1}x_{1},\\
\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}=n\end{array}\right]\cdot z^{n}\biggr]\Biggl|_{z=1+\delta}<\infty,

and together with the same reasoning as in the proof of Proposition
4.1
.(iii)
we obtain that
\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{D}_{1}]=\mathbb{E}\bigl[-\log L(o,\mathbf{W}_{1}|1)\bigr]
varies real-analytically in the random walk parameters
(p_{1},\ldots,p_{d})\in\mathcal{P}_{d}
.

Real-analyticity of
\mathbb{E}\bigl[-\log L(o,\mathbf{W}_{1}|1)(\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0})\bigr]
follows due to

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{N},\\
x_{1}\in V_{1}^{\times},y_{1}\in V_{2}^{\times}\end{subarray}}\bigl|-\log L(o,y_{1}x_{1}|1)\bigr|\cdot n\cdot\mathbb{P}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\mathbf{W}_{1}=y_{1}x_{1},\\
\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}=n\end{array}\right]\cdot(1+\delta)^{n}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle M_{0}\cdot\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}n\cdot\mathbb{P}\bigl[\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}=n\bigr]\cdot(1+\delta)^{n}<\infty,

and with exponential moments of
\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}
together with the fact that we can rewrite
\mathbb{P}\bigl[\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}=n\bigr]
in the form (
32
).
∎

Proof of Corollary
2.6
.

In view of formula (
49
) and real-analyticity of the expectation
\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}]
,
it suffices to prove that the mapping

\mathcal{P}_{d}\ni(p_{1},\ldots,p_{d})\mapsto\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\Bigl(-\log L(o,\mathbf{W}_{1}|1)-(\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0})\cdot h\Bigr)^{2}\Bigr]

varies real-analytically. By Lemma
6.5
,

\mathbb{E}\bigl[-\log L(o,\mathbf{W}_{1}|1)\bigr]\quad\textrm{ and }\quad\mathbb{E}\bigl[-\log L(o,\mathbf{W}_{1}|1)(\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0})\bigr]

vary real-analytically in
p_{1},\ldots,p_{d}
. Following the same reasoning as above, we can see that the mapping

\mathcal{P}_{d}\ni(p_{1},\ldots,p_{d})\mapsto\mathbb{E}\bigl[\bigl(-\log L(o,\mathbf{W}_{1}|1)\bigr)^{2}\bigr]

varies real-analytically, since for sufficiently small
\delta>0
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\geq 1,\\
x_{1}\in V_{1}^{\times},y_{1}\in V_{2}^{\times}\end{subarray}}\bigl|-\log L(o,y_{1}x_{1}|1)\bigr|^{2}\cdot\mathbb{P}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\mathbf{W}_{1}=y_{1}x_{1},\\
\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}=n\end{array}\right]\cdot(1+\delta)^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle M_{0}^{2}\cdot\biggl[\sum_{n\geq 1}\mathbb{P}\bigl[\mathbf{T}_{1}-\mathbf{T}_{0}=n\bigr]\cdot z^{n}\biggr]\Biggl|_{z=1+\delta}<\infty.

where the constant
M_{0}
is as in the proof of Lemma
6.5
.

This shows that
\sigma_{h}^{2}
varies real-analytically in
(p_{1},\ldots,p_{d})\in\mathcal{P}_{d}
, which
finishes the proof of Corollay
2.6
.
∎

Remark 6.6
.

In Corollary
2.6

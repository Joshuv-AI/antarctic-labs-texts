The framework for action of the Cross-agency One Health Task Force
Authors: Stef Bronzwaer, Wim de Coen, Ole Heuer, Ian Marnane, Ana Vidal
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
The Cross-agency One Health Task Force, established by five EU agencies (ECDC, ECHA, EEA, EFSA and EMA), has released its Framework for Action, aimed at operationalising the One Health approach across various EU regulatory science sectors. This initiative, originating from the 2022 ONE Conference, addresses the integration of human, animal, plant, and environmental health inspired by the European Green Deal. The framework outlines five strategic objectives: enhancing strategic coordination, promoting research, strengthening capacity building, fostering communication and partnerships, as well as joint activities. These objectives will align the agencies' efforts, improve collaboration, and promote a transdisciplinary approach to address health threats. Key actions for 2024–2026 include developing joint publications, research coordination, and One Health-driven training programs. The Task Force will coordinate its work through regular interactions, in close liaison with the European Commission, to ensure long-term interdisciplinary cooperation.

Introduction

In 2022, the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) together with its partner Agencies: the European Centre for Disease Prevention and Control (ECDC), the European Chemicals Agency (ECHA), the European Environment Agency (EEA), and the European Medicines Agency (EMA), as well as the Joint Research Centre (JRC) organised the ONE – Health, Environment and Society – Conference.

The outcomes of the conference have been published on EFSA's website (https://www.one2022.eu/), and in various scientific journals [[1], [2], [3]]. Previously in this Journal, authors of the five Agencies published an article about One Health collaboration with and among EU Agencies – Bridging research and policy, where they concluded that: It is timely for EU Agencies to demonstrate leadership in moving the One Health agenda forward and it is encouraging that EU Agencies have committed to establish a cross-agency task force on One Health on which further reports should follow [3]. This Cross-agency One Health Task Force has now been established by the ECDC, ECHA, EEA, EFSA and EMA. Together, the Agencies have released a framework for action on which they are pleased to inform the reader in this manuscript [4].

The ONE conference convincingly demonstrated the need for a One Health approach, as a stepping stone towards food system and environmental sustainability. EU Agencies already provide scientific advice on the topics falling under their respective mandates. However, it was recognised that such advice must be increasingly integrated, in line with the transformative ambitions of the European Green Deal and its associated policy developments to successfully address the multiple challenges the EU is facing in the areas of human, animal and plant health, food safety, the climate crisis and environmental sustainability.

With the publication in 2022 of the One Health definition and the One Health Joint Plan of Action (2022–2026) by the ‘Quadripartite’ [the Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations (FAO), the World Organization for Animal Health (OIE), the UN Environment Programme (UNEP) and the World Health Organization (WHO)], the momentum for One Health increased globally [5]. The European Commission has asked its Group of Chief Scientific Advisors to provide evidence-based policy recommendations to support the EU's position on One Health [6]. Member States have come to work together on One Health through the One Health European Joint Programme (OHEJP) initiative [7], and through a number of European Partnerships [8], aiming to address current and future EU policy priorities.

The European Parliament in its resolution on the COVID-19 pandemic ‘highlights the need to further expand knowledge in this field and promote public scientific research in order to better understand and reflect the interdependencies between human, animal, plant and environmental health using a multi-sectoral, transdisciplinary and integrated approach’ [9]. Indeed, EU Agencies ‘have a deep understanding of the research knowledge available, as well as where knowledge gaps limit the quality of scientific advice produced’ [3]. Therefore, EU Agencies can help to bridge the gap between such knowledge and EU policy processes, make knowledge more accessible to relevant stakeholders, provide strategic direction to EU research funding, and directly engage with ongoing research projects, in agreement with the relevant partner Directorates-General in the European Commission.

The One Health approach is needed at different levels of governance and requires a strong interface between science, policy and society. Actual implementation will need to take One Health from theory to practice, as highlighted by the 4 Cs: Communication, Coordination, Collaboration, and Capacity building [10]. The Cross-agency One Health Task Force became operational in 2023, and on occasion of the High-level policy conference ‘One Health for All - All for One Health’ (https://health.ec.europa.eu/events/one-health-conference-one-health-all-all-one-health-2023-11-13_en), organised by the European Commission in November 2023, the five EU Agencies providing scientific advice on the environment, public health and food safety published a Joint Statement in support of the One Health agenda in Europe [11]. In May 2024 the Task Force released its Framework for Action, accompanied by a joint video message of the (Executive) Directors of the five EU Agencies (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eSlKEUVDRDA).

Framework for action

The vision of the Task Force is to enable EU Agencies to successfully contribute to the implementation of the One Health approach in Europe, thereby helping the EU and its Member States to prevent and respond to health threats. To achieve this vision, the Task Force aims to work over the coming three years towards a more permanent and structured form of transdisciplinary cooperation between the EU Agencies, around the four principles of coordination, collaboration, communication and capacity building.

The Framework for Action builds on existing cross-agency cooperation activities. It aims to add value, avoid duplication, and strengthen coordination initiatives to address multi-sectoral topics, such as:•Climate change and degradation of ecosystems, that can create favourable conditions for the emergence and spread of infectious diseases such as vector-, food- and water-borne diseases, with a profound impact on animal and public health.•Microplastics, that are an example of how emerging pollutants not only contaminate waterways and soil but also enter the food chain, affecting animal and human health, and impacting food safety.•Zoonotic diseases, where the COVID-19 pandemic showed the need to coordinate across sectors to better understand the complex drivers of infectious diseases and other health threats.

The Agencies jointly developed the Framework around five strategic objectives (see Fig. 1), under which a list of concrete actions have been agreed for the period 2024 to 2026, and desired outcomes have been formulated. This Framework is consistent with the respective mandates of the five Agencies and recognises that not all Agencies will be equally involved in the different actions. Cross-agency task force operations

The Task Force meets at least six times a year, bringing together senior officers operating under the auspices of the (Executive) Directors of each agency. A focal point has been appointed within each agency to coordinate the respective inputs that keeps their own colleagues and interagency mechanisms informed. The coordination of the Task Force will rotate among the agencies, as is practice for similar task forces. To ensure close One Health cooperation between the Agencies and the European Commission, a representative from the Commission participates as an observer in the Task Force meetings. The Task Force liaises with on-going interagency groups and activities and may establish dedicated working groups to support its activities.

Conclusion

This Framework for Action reflects the overall commitment of the five EU Agencies to establish greater coordination and collaboration, strengthening communication and building capacity on One Health. Taking a One Health approach is not necessarily about implementing new activities but foremost about adopting more integrative and transdisciplinary approaches. Timely sharing of knowledge, information and data between Agencies will speed up cooperation and lead to more integrated outputs.

These objectives are in line with the EU agencies' dual role as sources and users of knowledge. They reflect the participating agencies' ambition to streamline joint activities and will help to avoid conflicting messaging and duplication of work, establishing greater coordination on knowledge development, a key recommendation from the Special Report of the European Court of Auditors on the Future of EU Agencies [12]. Through the actions agreed, the Task Force will work to strengthen the scientific evidence base for One Health, ensure that scientific advice and risk assessment are increasingly integrated, support the establishment of intersectoral coordination mechanisms, and build One Health capacities and skills.

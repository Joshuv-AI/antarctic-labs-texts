Applicability Evaluation Guideline for Diagnostic Criteria of Chinese Medicine Syndromes
Authors: Jiansheng Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTAimTo develop a guideline for the applicability evaluation tool of the Diagnostic Criteria for Chinese Medicine Syndromes, aiming to enhance the clinical applicability of syndrome diagnostic criteria and promote their widespread adoption and application.MethodsThis guideline was developed using the Delphi method, with reference to the Guideline on Establishing Diagnostic Criteria of Chinese Medicine Syndromes, relevant international evaluation tools, and published diagnostic criteria for Chinese medicine syndromes.ResultsThe guideline specifies the principles, procedures, and scoring criteria for evaluating the applicability of traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) syndrome diagnostic criteria. The guideline consists of 5 domains and 25 items: accessibility, readability, implementability, acceptability, and overall evaluation. Each item is assessed on a 5‐point Likert scale, with higher scores indicating better applicability.ConclusionsThis guideline provides a systematic and evidence‐based framework for evaluating the applicability of diagnostic criteria for Chinese medicine syndromes. It may help improve the clinical applicability of syndrome diagnostic criteria and support their dissemination and implementation in clinical practice.Keywords: applicability, Chinese medicine syndromes, diagnostic criteria, guideline

Introduction

The standardization and objectification of diagnostic criteria for Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) syndromes, hereafter referred to as “TCM syndrome diagnostic criteria,” are fundamental to the standardization of TCM [1]. In recent years, the standardization of TCM has advanced rapidly, and an increasing number of TCM syndrome diagnostic criteria have been published, promoting research on syndrome standardization and supporting its application in clinical practice [2, 3, 4]. However, the applicability evaluation of TCM syndrome diagnostic criteria has not received sufficient attention [5]. Existing TCM syndrome diagnostic criteria still face several challenges, including greater emphasis on development than evaluation, limited clinical use, and difficulties in dissemination and implementation [6]. Clinical applicability is a key factor influencing whether TCM syndrome diagnostic criteria can be effectively disseminated and applied in practice [7]. Therefore, after TCM syndrome diagnostic criteria have been developed, it is important to evaluate their applicability from multiple perspectives, including accessibility, readability, implementability, and acceptability [8]. At present, applicability evaluation tools have mainly been developed for clinical practice guidelines, whereas specific guidance for evaluating the applicability of TCM syndrome diagnostic criteria remains limited. Therefore, there is a need to develop a guideline for evaluating the applicability of TCM syndrome diagnostic criteria, so as to identify criteria with high clinical applicability and support the development and updating of such criteria [9, 10, 11, 12, 13].

This guideline was developed in accordance with relevant health policies, regulations, and methodological guidance. It was informed by the Guideline on Establishing Diagnostic Criteria of Traditional Chinese Medicine Syndromes (SCM 70–2022), related TCM syndrome diagnostic criteria, and existing applicability evaluation tools, while taking into account the specific characteristics of TCM syndrome diagnostic criteria. This guideline aims to improve the clinical applicability of TCM syndrome diagnostic criteria and promote their dissemination and implementation [1, 14].

Scope

This guideline specifies the evaluation principles, contents, scoring methods, and interpretation of standardized scores for assessing the applicability of TCM syndrome diagnostic criteria.

This guideline is intended for the applicability evaluation of published or finalized draft TCM syndrome diagnostic criteria. It may be used by guideline developers for prospective self‐assessment before publication or updating, and by external evaluators, researchers, medical institutions, or professional organizations for post‐publication assessment.

This guideline is intended for professionals in TCM, integrated traditional Chinese and Western medicine, medical education, and scientific research institutions who are involved in evaluating the applicability of TCM syndrome diagnostic criteria.(Supporting Information S1).

Terms and Definitions

For the purposes of this guideline, the following terms and definitions apply.

Syndrome

A syndrome refers to a pattern of clinical manifestations at a specific stage of disease. It reflects the intrinsic relationships among disease location, etiology, disease nature, disease condition, and the body's resistance to disease, and is usually expressed through clinically observable symptoms and signs (Source: ISBN 7‐03‐015154‐2, 04.548).

Applicability

Applicability refers to the extent to which a document can be appropriately used within a defined context, such as a region, institution, or user group (Source: https://g‐i‐n.net/wp‐content/uploads/2021/05/ADAPTE‐Resource‐toolkit‐V2.1‐March‐2010‐updated‐disclaimer.pdf).

Evaluation Principles and Process

Evaluation Principles

Evaluators should follow the principles of scientific rigor, objectivity, and impartiality. All assessments should be based on verifiable data, and the evaluation results should be traceable. It is recommended that at least three evaluators with a background in TCM independently assess the target criteria and cross‐check the results to reduce subjectivity.

Evaluation Procedures

The evaluation procedures include identification of the target TCM syndrome diagnostic criteria, assessment with the designated evaluation tool, and preparation of an evaluation report.

Evaluation Content and Requirements

The checklist for evaluating the applicability of TCM syndrome diagnostic criteria consists of 5 domains and 25 items: accessibility, readability, implementability, acceptability, and overall evaluation. Detailed explanations of each item are provided in Table 1.

Explanations of the items for evaluating the applicability of TCM syndrome diagnostic criteria.

Accessibility

The criteria are easily accessible.

Readability

Structure

(a) The structure is complete and reasonable.

(b) The diagnostic points are easy to identify.

(c) The development process is clear and straightforward.

Content

(a) The content is complete and reasonable.

(b) The scope of application is clear.

(c) The diagnostic points are accurately described.

(d) The language is clear and standardized.

(e) The content is internally consistent.

(f) Common syndrome classifications are included, if applicable.

Implementability

Clinical Application Characteristics

(a) The criteria are convenient for clinical application.

(b) The criteria are accurate in clinical application.

(c) The consistency of clinical application is high.

Technical Level

(a) The criteria are compatible with the local medical level.

(b) The criteria are more standardized than other syndrome diagnostic criteria.

Coordination and Compatibility

The criteria are consistent with the syndrome differentiation principles in diagnosis and treatment guidelines, consensus documents, protocols, and clinical pathways.

Intended Effects

The criteria can improve the level of syndrome differentiation and enhance the effectiveness of diagnosis and treatment.

Facilitators and Barriers to Implementation

(a) The criteria clearly describe the target users and application settings.

(b) Users can understand the criteria and have the professional competence to implement them.

(c) Users need to receive necessary training.

Acceptability

(a) The clinical issues addressed by the criteria match those faced by users.

(b) Users recognize the criteria.

(c) Users are willing to refer to the criteria in their work.

Overall Evaluation

(a) The clinical applicability of the criteria.

(b) Users’ satisfaction with the criteria and overall impression.

Scoring methods and criteria for evaluating the applicability of TCM syndrome diagnostic criteria.

Domain Standardized Score Calculation Method

Domain Standardized Score = (Sum of all evaluators’ scores for the domain − Minimum possible score for the domain)/(Maximum possible score for the domain − Minimum possible score for the domain) × 100.

Overall Standardized Score Calculation Method

Overall Standardized Score = (Sum of all evaluators’ overall scores − Minimum possible overall score)/(Maximum possible overall score − Minimum possible overall score) × 100.

The complex of r r -co-connected subgraphs, chordality and Fröberg’s theorem Thanks: Last updated: August 10, 2026
Priyavrat Deshpande, Amit Roy, Rutuja Sawant

Abstract
. We introduce a new family of pure simplicial complexes, called the r r -co-connected complex of G G with respect to A A , Σ r ​ ( A , G ) \Sigma_{r}(A,G) , where r ≥ 1 r\geq 1 is a natural number, G G is a simple graph, and A A is a subset of vertices. Interestingly, when A A is empty, this complex is precisely the Alexander dual of the r r -independence complex of G G . We focus on uncovering the relationship between the topological and combinatorial properties of the complex and the algebraic and homological properties of the Stanley-Reisner ideal of the dual complex. First, we prove that Σ r ​ ( A , G ) \Sigma_{r}(A,G) is vertex decomposable whenever the induced subgraph G ⁡ [ A ] G[A] is connected and nonempty, yielding a versatile deletion–link calculus for higher independence via Alexander duality. Furthermore, when A = ∅ A=\emptyset and r ≥ 2 r\geq 2 , we establish that for several significant classes of graphs - including chordal, co-chordal, cographs, cycles, complements of cycles, and certain grid graphs - the properties of vertex decomposability, shellability, and Cohen-Macaulayness are equivalent and precisely characterized by the co-chordality of the associated clutter Con r ​ ( G ) \mathrm{Con}_{r}(G) . These results extend Fröberg’s theorem to the setting of r r -connected ideals for these graph classes and motivate a conjecture concerning the linear resolution property of r r -connected ideals in general. We also construct examples separating shellability from vertex decomposability.

1.
Introduction

The interplay between topological combinatorics and combinatorial commutative algebra, mediated by the Stanley-Reisner correspondence, has yielded profound connections between the structural properties of graphs and simplicial complexes and the algebraic invariants of monomial ideals. A classical result in this direction is Fröberg’s theorem
[
13
]
, which characterizes the chordality of a finite simple graph in terms of the linear resolution property of its associated quadratic square-free monomial ideal.

Let
G
be a finite simple graph on the vertex set
V(G)=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
with circuit set
E(G)
. A subset
W\subseteq V(G)
is called an
independent set
if
W
contains no circuit of
G
. The collection of all independent sets forms the
independence complex

\mathrm{Ind}(G)
of
G
, and the associated Stanley–Reisner ideal
I(G)=\langle x_{i}x_{j}\mid\{x_{i},x_{j}\}\in E(G)\rangle
in the polynomial ring
R=\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
is known as the
circuit ideal
(or edge ideal) of
G
.
Fröberg’s theorem can then be stated as

I(G)\text{ has a linear resolution }\Longleftrightarrow G\text{ is co-chordal},

where a graph is said to be
co-chordal
if its complement is chordal. In particular, for quadratic square-free monomial ideals, the property of having a linear resolution depends only on the combinatorial structure of the associated graph and not on the characteristic of the base field.
This result was later extended by Eagon and Reiner
[
10
, Proposition 8]
who showed that several algebraic and topological properties are equivalent for graphs, as summarized below (recall that for a simplicial complex
X
, its Alexander dual, denoted by
X^{\vee}
, is the simplicial complex whose faces are the complement of non-faces of
X
).

Theorem 1.1
.

The following are equivalent for a graph
G
:

(1)

I(G)
has a linear resolution,

(2)

(\mathrm{Ind}(G))^{\vee}
is vertex decomposable,

(3)

(\mathrm{Ind}(G))^{\vee}
is shellable,

(4)

(\mathrm{Ind}(G))^{\vee}
is Cohen-Macaulay,

(5)

G
is co-chordal.

The situation changes drastically in higher degrees.
A classical example due to Reisner
[
21
]
, arising from the minimal triangulation of the projective plane, shows that the minimal free resolution of the corresponding degree
3
square-free monomial ideal does depend on the characteristic of the field
\mathbb{K}
.
More recently, for
1\leq d\leq n-3
, Ficarra and Moradi
[
12
]
constructed degree-
d
square-free monomial ideals exhibiting the same dependence.
Consequently, a purely combinatorial characterization of all square-free monomial ideals with linear resolution-analogous to Fröberg’s theorem-cannot be achieved in general.

A more tractable problem is to characterize combinatorially those square-free monomial ideals that have linear minimal free resolutions over every field.
For such higher degree ideals, hypergraphs or clutters are convenient combinatorial structures, as they generalize graphs.

In this context, several approaches have been proposed by extending the notion of chordality from graphs to clutters (i.e., hypergraphs).
Among these, the construction of
chordal clutters
by Bigdeli et al.
[
5
]
is particularly notable, as it subsumes previous notions of chordality introduced by Woodroofe
[
25
]
, Emtander
[
11
]
, and Van Tuyl and Villarreal
[
23
]
.
A
r
-uniform clutter
\mathcal{C}
is called chordal if it admits a simplicial sequence of maximal
(r-1)
-subcircuits whose iterative deletion exhausts
\mathcal{C}
, generalizing perfect elimination in chordal graphs, and for the complement of such clutters the edge (or circuit) ideal has a linear resolution over every field.
However, a counterexample attributed to Eric Babson in
[
18
]
demonstrated that there is a clutter which is not chordal, but the associated circuit ideal has a linear resolution.
In the same work, it was shown that if the Stanley-Reisner complex associated with a clutter is vertex decomposable, then the clutter is chordal.
These developments naturally lead to the following question.

Question 1.2
.

Can the Fröberg’s theorem be expected to hold for specific classes of clutters?

The motivation of our investigation was to extend the results of
[
7
]
, in order to obtain a better characterization of the Stanley-Reisner ideal of the
r
-independence complexes. The study of
r
-independence complexes can be traced to the work of Paolini and Salvetti
[
19
]
.
They are studied in the combinatorial point of view in
[
1
,
7
,
8
,
9
]
. More recently, the algebraic properties of the corresponding edge (circuit) ideals, referred to as
r
-connected ideals
, were studied in
[
2
,
6
]
.

The linear resolution property of edge (circuit) ideals of clutters is intimately connected to the Cohen-Macaulayness of the Alexander dual of the corresponding independence complexes. By the theorem of Eagon and Reiner
[
10
]
, the edge ideal of a clutter has a linear resolution if and only if the Alexander dual of its Stanley-Reisner complex is Cohen-Macaulay. Hence, to investigate the linear resolution property of the edge (circuit) ideal of
r
-connected clutters, it suffices to study the Cohen-Macaulay property of the Alexander dual of their independence complexes. Motivated by this connection, we introduce a new family of simplicial complexes associated to a graph
G
, defined for each subset
A\subseteq V(G)
by

\Sigma_{r}(A,G)=\big\langle F^{c}\setminus A:F\subseteq V(G),\;|F|=r,\;F\cap A=\emptyset,\;G[F\cup A]\text{ is connected}\big\rangle.

We call
\Sigma_{r}(A,G)
the
r
-co-connected complex
of

with respect to
A
. In particular,
\Sigma_{r}(\emptyset,G)
, denoted simply by
\Sigma_{r}(G)
, coincides with the Alexander dual of
\mathrm{Ind}_{r}(G)
, that is,
\Sigma_{r}(G)=(\mathrm{Ind}_{r}(G))^{\vee}
. Our first main result in this context is the following.

Theorem
3.7
.

Let

be a non-empty set such that

is connected. Then, for each

, the simplicial complex

is vertex decomposable.

In the special case

for which the complex

fails to be vertex decomposable. Nevertheless, in

. Furthermore, for

\Sigma_{r}(G)
. In addition, these conditions are shown to be equivalent to the co-chordality of the associated
r
-connected clutter

\bullet

chordal graphs (

4.1
),

\bullet

4.3
),

\bullet

4.7
),

\bullet

4.11

4.15

\bullet

The above results can be viewed as an extension of Fröberg’s theorem to the setting of

-connected ideals of the aforementioned classes of graphs.
Note that, in the case of co-chordal graphs, complements of cycles, and cographs, the simplicial complexes

are already vertex decomposable for every

. Furthermore, the result in
Theorem

4.1
provides an affirmative answer to the first part of
[

.

Two related complexes to

are the
r
-cut complex

\Delta_{r}(G)
and the

-path-free complex

\Gamma_{r}(G)
.
It is worth mentioning that

for

denotes the complement of

.
Hence, our results for

in
[

,

,

As mentioned earlier, the complex

is not always vertex decomposable. In

, we present an example of a gap-free graph

Conjecture 1.3
.

Let

Note that, if this conjecture holds true, it would yield a complete combinatorial classification of graphs whose

-connected ideals will not depend on the characteristic of the base field. Our results presented in

,

,

,

,

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall some preliminary notions related to clutters (hypergraphs) and simplicial complexes that will be used throughout the paper.

2.1.
Clutter

A
clutter

\mathcal{H}
is defined as a pair
(V(\mathcal{H}),E(\mathcal{H}))
, consisting of a vertex set
V(\mathcal{H})
and a circuit set
E(\mathcal{H})
, wherein the circuit set is composed of a collection of subsets from
V(\mathcal{H})
, ensuring that no two elements of these subsets are such that one is contained within another.
If for each
\mathcal{E}\in E(\mathcal{H})
,
|\mathcal{E}|=d
, then we define
\mathcal{H}
as a
d
-uniform clutter
.
Notably, the
2
-uniform clutters are identified as the finite simple graphs.
A subset
W\subseteq V(\mathcal{H})
is designated as a
clique
within a
d
-uniform clutter
\mathcal{H}
when every subset of
W
with cardinality
d
belongs to
E(\mathcal{H})
. In the context of a
d
-uniform clutter
\mathcal{H}
, the
complement

\mathcal{H}^{c}
of
\mathcal{H}
corresponds to a clutter with the vertex set
V(\mathcal{H})
and circuit set
\{\mathcal{E}\subseteq V(\mathcal{H})\mid|\mathcal{E}|=d\text{ and }\mathcal{E}\notin E(\mathcal{H})\}
.
The
induced subclutter

\mathcal{H}[A]
of a clutter
\mathcal{H}
on set
A\subseteq V(\mathcal{H})
is a clutter with vertex set
A
and circuit set
\{\mathcal{E}\in E(\mathcal{H})\mid\mathcal{E}\subseteq A\}
.
Furthermore,
\mathcal{H}\setminus A
denotes the induced subclutter
\mathcal{H}[V(\mathcal{H})\setminus A]
. If
A=\{a\}
for some
a\in V(\mathcal{H})
, then
\mathcal{H}\setminus\{a\}
is simply denoted by
\mathcal{H}\setminus a
.
For
B\subseteq V(\mathcal{H})
, the
open neighborhood
of
B
, signified by
\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{H}}(B)
, comprises the set
\{x\in V(\mathcal{H})\setminus B\mid x\in\mathcal{E}\subseteq\{x\}\cup B\text{ for some }\mathcal{E}\in E(\mathcal{H})\}
.
If
b\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{H}}(B)
, under certain conditions
b\in V(\mathcal{H})
, then
b
is termed a
neighbor
of
B
.
The collection
\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{H}}(B)\cup B
is referred to as the
closed neighborhood
of
B
, represented by
\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{H}}[B]
.
A
matching

M
in a clutter
\mathcal{H}
is characterized by a collection of circuits where
\mathcal{E}\cap\mathcal{E}^{\prime}=\emptyset
for any two distinct
\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\in M
.
A matching
M
constitutes an
induced matching
of
G
if
E(\mathcal{H}[V(M)])=M
, wherein
V(M)
denotes the set of vertices residing in the circuits of
M
.
Lastly, the matching number
\nu(\mathcal{H})
and the induced matching number
\gamma(\mathcal{H})
are defined as follows:

\displaystyle\nu(\mathcal{H})

\displaystyle=\max\{|M|:M\text{ is a matching of }\mathcal{H}\}

\displaystyle\gamma(\mathcal{H})

\displaystyle=\max\{|M|:M\text{ is an induced matching of }\mathcal{H}\}.

Consider
G
, a finite, simple graph defined by vertex set
V(G)
and circuit set
E(G)
. For any two vertices
a,b\in V(G)
, an
induced path of length

connecting

and

is constituted by a collection of vertices
P_{t}:a=a_{1},a_{2}\ldots,a_{t-1},a_{t}=b
, such that
\{a_{i},a_{i+1}\}
are the only circuits.
We simply call

a
path
between

and
b
if
\{a_{i},a_{i+1}\}
are circuits but may not be the only circuits among all the

.
A

consists of a vertex set

and a circuit set
E(C_{n})=\{\{y_{1},y_{n}\},\{y_{i},y_{i+1}\}\mid 1\leq i\leq n-1\}
.
A graph
G
is said to be a

if it does not contain any cycle.
A connected forest is simply called a
tree
. A

of a graph

is a subgraph
H
of

such that

is a tree and

.
A graph
G
is said to be a
chordal graph
if there is no induced

in

for

.
A vertex

is said to be a

of

is a clique in

(i.e., any two vertices are connected by a circuit).
Moreover,

is said to be a

if

is a simplicial vertex and
|N_{G}[x]|=2
.
Two circuits

of a graph

are said to form a

forms an induced matching in

. A graph

is said to be a

Let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
be two graphs on disjoint vertex sets
V(G_{1})
and
V(G_{2})
. The
disjoint union

G_{1}\sqcup G_{2}
of

and
G_{2}
is a graph on the vertex set
V(G_{1}\sqcup G_{2}):=V(G_{1})\cup V(G_{2})
with circuit set
E(G_{1}\sqcup G_{2}):=E(G_{1})\cup E(G_{2})
. The

of
G_{1}
and
G_{2}

, is a graph on the vertex set

and the circuit set

. The cartesian product (or simply
product
) of

and
G_{2}
, denoted by

, consists of the vertex set

The notion of a chordal graph is extended to the realm of clutters by several researchers. Recently, Bigdeli et. al.
[

\mathcal{H}

. A subset

is said to be a

forms a clique in

. With this terminology, the notion of chordal clutter is defined in

5
]
as follows.

Definition 2.1
.

A
d
-uniform clutter
\mathcal{H}
is a

if either
E(\mathcal{H})=\emptyset
or there exists a simplicial maximal subcircuit

of
\mathcal{H}
such that

is again a chordal clutter.

A
d
-uniform clutter
\mathcal{H}
is said to be co-chordal if
\mathcal{H}^{c}
is a chordal clutter.

2.2.
Simplicial complex

An
(abstract) simplicial complex

\Delta
on the vertex set
V(\Delta)=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
is defined as a collection of subsets of
V(\Delta)
such that, given
U\in\Delta
and
Z\subseteq U
, then
Z\in\Delta
holds.
The elements of
\Delta
are referred to as the
faces
of
\Delta
, while the maximal faces are called the
facets
.
The notation
\mathcal{F}(\Delta)
is used for the set of facets of
\Delta
.
Let
F_{1},\ldots,F_{t}
be all the facets of
\Delta
, we say that
\Delta
is generated by
F_{1},\ldots,F_{t}
, written as
\Delta=\langle F_{1},\ldots,F_{t}\rangle
.
A simplicial complex
\Delta=\langle F_{1},\ldots,F_{t}\rangle
is
pure
if
|F_{i}|=|F_{j}|
, for every
i\neq j
.
Concerning
F\in\Delta
, the
dimension
of
F
is defined as
\dim F=|F|-1
, and in the case of the simplicial complex
\Delta
, we define
\dim\Delta=\max_{F\in\Delta}\dim F
.
If
F
is a face of
\Delta
such that
\dim(F)=\dim(\Delta)-1
, then
F
is a
co-dimension one face
.
Therefore, a
d
-simplex
is a
d
-dimensional simplicial complex containing exactly one facet of dimension
d
.
If
\Delta
is a simplex on the vertex set
A
, it may sometimes be denoted by
\Delta_{A}
.
Assume
\Delta_{1}
and
\Delta_{2}
are two simplicial complexes on disjoint vertex sets
V(\Delta_{1})
and
V(\Delta_{2})
.
The
join
of
\Delta_{1}
and
\Delta_{2}
, expressed as
\Delta_{1}*\Delta_{2}
, constitutes a simplicial complex defined by the vertex set and the set of facets as:
V(\Delta_{1}*\Delta_{2})=V(\Delta_{1})\sqcup V(\Delta_{2})
, and
\mathcal{F}(\Delta_{1}*\Delta_{2})=\{F_{1}\sqcup F_{2}\mid F_{1}\in\mathcal{F}(\Delta_{1}),F_{2}\in\mathcal{F}(\Delta_{2})\}
, respectively.

Let
F
be a face of a simplicial complex
\Delta
. The
link
of
F
is the simplicial complex

\mathrm{lk}_{\Delta}(F)=\{H\in\Delta|\,H\cap F=\emptyset\hskip 5.69054pt\text{and}\hskip 5.69054ptH\cup F\in\Delta\},

and the
deletion
of
F
is the simplicial complex

\mathrm{del}_{\Delta}(F)=\{H\in\Delta|\,H\cap F=\emptyset\}.

In case
F=\{x_{i}\}
, for brevity, we denote
\mathrm{lk}_{\Delta}(\{x_{i}\})
and
\mathrm{del}_{\Delta}(\{x_{i}\})
as
\mathrm{lk}(x_{i})
and
\mathrm{del}(x_{i})
, respectively.

Definition 2.2
.

A simplicial complex
\Delta
is vertex decomposable if
\Delta
is a simplex, or a vertex
x
exists in
\Delta
making
\mathrm{lk}_{\Delta}(x)
and
\mathrm{del}_{\Delta}(x)
vertex decomposable, and every facet of
\mathrm{del}_{\Delta}(x)
is also a facet of
\Delta
.

The
empty complex
, denoted by
\{\emptyset\}
, is the simplicial complex consisting only of the empty set as its face; it is regarded as the unique
(-1)
-dimensional simplicial complex.
The
void complex
, denoted by
\emptyset
, is the collection with no faces at all and is typically considered as a complex on the empty vertex set.
Note that the empty and void complexes are considered vertex decomposable. Two more definitions that we need are:

Definition 2.3
.

A simplicial complex
\Delta
is shellable if its facets
F_{1},\ldots,F_{s}
can be ordered so that for each
2\leq i\leq s
, the subcomplex
\langle F_{i}\rangle\cap\langle F_{1},\ldots,F_{i-1}\rangle
is pure of dimension
\mathrm{dim}(F_{i})-1
. Such an ordering of the facets is called a shelling order.

Definition 2.4
.

A simplicial complex
\Delta
is Cohen-Macaulay over a field
\mathbb{K}
if
\widetilde{H}_{i}(\mathrm{lk}_{\Delta}(F);\mathbb{K})=0
holds for all
F\in\Delta
and
i<\dim\mathrm{lk}_{\Delta}(F)
, where
\widetilde{H}_{i}(-;\mathbb{K})
is
i^{\text{th}}
reduced simplicial homology group with coefficient in
\mathbb{K}
.

We drop the reference to
\mathbb{K}
if the homology is computed with coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}
.
For a pure simplicial complex
\Delta
, it is a well-known fact (see, for instance,
[
24
, Proposition 6.3.32]
)

(1)

\displaystyle\text{Vertex decomposable}\Rightarrow\text{Shellable}\Rightarrow\text{Cohen-Macaulay}.

Definition 2.5

Let

2.3.

Let

r
-independence complex
of
G
, and we denote it by
\mathrm{Ind}_{r}(G)
. Observe that
I_{\mathrm{Ind}_{r}(G)}
is the square-free monomial ideal generated by the monomials associated to the connected induced subgraphs of
G
with vertex cardinality
r
. In other words,
I_{\mathrm{Ind}_{r}(G)}
is the circuit ideal of the clutter
{\mathrm{Con}}_{r}(G)
, where
V({\mathrm{Con}}_{r}(G))=V(G)
and
E({\mathrm{Con}}_{r}(G))=\{W\subseteq V(G):|W|=r\text{ and }G[W]\text{ is connected}\}
. The clutter
{\mathrm{Con}}_{r}(G)
is also called the
r
-connected clutter
of
G
. Note that the independence complex of
{\mathrm{Con}}_{r}(G)
is the
r
-independence complex
\mathrm{Ind}_{r}(G)
.

For the
r
-independence complexes
\mathrm{Ind}_{r}(G)
it follows from the definition that
(\mathrm{Ind}_{r}(G))^{\vee}=\{F^{c}\mid F\in E({\mathrm{Con}}_{r}(G))\}
. In
Section

3
, we define the simplicial complex
\Sigma_{r}(A,G)
for any subset
A
of V(G), and it follows from the construction that
\Sigma_{r}(\emptyset,G)=(\mathrm{Ind}_{r}(G))^{\vee}
, which we also denote by
\Sigma_{r}(G)
. For the
r
-connected clutter
{\mathrm{Con}}_{r}(G)
, the induced matching number is denoted by
\gamma_{r}(G)
. Thus,
\gamma({\mathrm{Con}}_{r}(G))=\gamma_{r}(G)
. We say that a graph
G
is
r
-gap-free
if
\gamma_{r}(G)\leq 1
. Observe that each gap-free graph is also
r
-gap-free. In other words,
\gamma_{1}(G)\leq 1
implies
\gamma_{r}(G)\leq 1
. As we shall see in
Section

4
, the
r
-gap-free condition is sometimes equivalent to the vertex decomposability property of the simplicial complex
\Sigma_{r}(G)
, for various well-known classes of graphs
G
.

2.4.
Stanley-Reisner ideal

Let
\Delta
be a simplicial complex on the vertex set
V(\Delta)=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
and let
R
denote the polynomial ring
\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
over a field
\mathbb{K}
.
It is well-known that the Stanley-Reisner ideal
I_{\Delta}
of
\Delta
is the square-free monomial ideal
\langle\mathbf{x}_{F}:=\prod_{x_{i}\in F}x_{i}\mid F\notin\Delta\rangle
in the polynomial ring
R
.
Restricting to the complex
\mathrm{Ind}_{r}(G)
we obtain that

I_{\mathrm{Ind}_{r}(G)}=\langle\mathbf{x}_{F}\mid F\subseteq V(G),|F|=r\text{ and }G[F]\text{ is connected }\rangle.

Observe that the minimal generators of
I_{\mathrm{Ind}_{r}(G)}
correspond to the
r
-connected subsets of
G
, and more specifically, it is the circuit ideal of the
r
-connected clutter of
G
defined above.
Because of these reasons, the ideal
I_{\mathrm{Ind}_{r}(G)}
is also called the
r
-connected ideal in
[
2
]
(see also
[
6
]
).

Numerous combinatorial properties of
\Delta
possess algebraic counterparts in
I_{\Delta}
.
For example, the Eagon-Reiner theorem
[
10
, Theorem 3]
asserts that a simplicial complex
\Delta
is Cohen-Macaulay if and only if
I_{\Delta^{\vee}}
possesses a linear resolution.
Similarly, according to a theorem by Herzog et al.
[
15
, Theorem 1.4]
,
\Delta
is shellable if and only if
I_{\Delta^{\vee}}
exhibits linear quotients.
More recently, Moradi and Khosh-Ahang provided an algebraic formulation of the vertex decomposability property.
Specifically, they demonstrated in
[
17
, Theorem 2.3]
that
\Delta
is vertex decomposable if and only if
I_{\Delta^{\vee}}
is a vertex splittable ideal.
In the present article, we employ these correspondences to infer certain properties of
I_{\mathrm{Ind}_{r}(G)}
.
Nevertheless, as our primary focus lies on the properties of
\mathrm{Ind}_{r}(G)
and its Alexander dual, we do not elaborate on these algebraic properties and instead direct interested readers to the aforementioned works and the references therein for detailed definitions and further discussions on these topics.

3.
Co-connected complexes and their topology

In this section, we formally define the simplicial complexes central to our study and also discuss their topological and combinatorial properties.
Let us first consider the notion
r
-support of a subset
A
of
V(G)
, for a positive integer
r
:

\mathrm{Supp}_{r}(A,G)=\{F\subseteq V(G):|F|=r,\ F\cap A=\emptyset,\ \text{$G[F\cup A]$ is connected}\}.

Definition 3.1
.

Given a positive integer
r
and a subset
A
, the
r
-co-connected complex with respect to
A
, denoted by
\Sigma_{r}(A,G)
is defined as

\Sigma_{r}(A,G)=\langle F^{c}\setminus A:F\in\mathrm{Supp}_{r}(A,G)\rangle,

where
F^{c}
denotes the complement of
F
in
V(G)
.

We additionally define
\Sigma_{r}(G)
as the simplicial complex
\Sigma_{r}(\emptyset,G)
.
It follows from the definition that for any positive integer
r
, the condition
\mathrm{Supp}_{r}(\emptyset,G)=E({\mathrm{Con}}_{r}(G))
holds, and for each
A
, we observe
V(\Sigma_{r}(A,G))\subseteq V(G\setminus A)
.
Moreover,
\Sigma_{r}(\emptyset,G)=(\mathrm{Ind}_{r}(G))^{\vee}
.

Example 3.2
.

Let
G
be the graph in
Figure

1
. Take
r=2
and
A=\{x_{1}\}
.

x_{1}
x_{2}
x_{3}
x_{4}
x_{5}
x_{6}

Figure 1
.

We first compute the
2
-support of
A
:

\mathrm{Supp}_{2}(A,G)=\{F\subseteq V(G):|F|=2,\ F\cap A=\emptyset,\ G[F\cup A]\ \text{is connected}\}.

The
2
-subsets
F
disjoint from
A
that make
G[F\cup A]
connected are

\{x_{2},x_{3}\},\ \{x_{2},x_{5}\},\ \{x_{3},x_{4}\},\ \{x_{3},x_{5}\},\ \{x_{5},x_{6}\}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\Sigma_{2}(A,G)

\displaystyle=\langle F^{c}\setminus A:F\in\mathrm{Supp}_{2}(A,G)\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\{x_{4},x_{5},x_{6}\},\ \{x_{3},x_{4},x_{6}\},\ \{x_{2},x_{5},x_{6}\},\ \{x_{2},x_{4},x_{6}\},\ \{x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}\}\rangle.

Thus
\Sigma_{2}(A,G)
is a simplicial complex on
V(G)\setminus A=\{x_{2},x_{3},x_{4},x_{5},x_{6}\}
with the above facets.

Our objective now is to determine the conditions under which the complex
\Sigma_{r}(A,G)
is vertex decomposable.
We begin by identifying a shedding vertex.
It is noteworthy that for a simplicial complex
\Delta
, an element
x\in V(\Delta)
is called a
shedding vertex
of
\Delta
if no face of
\mathrm{link}_{\Delta}(x)
constitutes a facet of
\mathrm{del}_{\Delta}(x)
.
Alternatively,
x
is considered a shedding vertex of
\Delta
if and only if every facet of
\mathrm{del}_{\Delta}(x)
is a facet of
\Delta
.
This characterization is used in the next Proposition to establish a combinatorial condition for identifying a shedding vertex of
\Sigma_{r}(A,G)
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
G
be a graph,
r
a fixed positive integer, and
x\in V(\Sigma_{r}(A,G))
. The following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

x
is a shedding vertex of
\Sigma_{r}(A,G)
.

(ii)

For every
F\in\mathrm{Supp}_{r}(A,G)
with
x\notin F
, there exists a vertex
y\in F
such that
(F\setminus\{y\})\cup\{x\}\in\mathrm{Supp}_{r}(A,G)
.

Proof.

The goal is to show
(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii)
.
Let
x
be a shedding vertex of
\Sigma_{r}(A,G)
, and
F\in\mathrm{Supp}_{r}(A,G)
such that
x\notin F
.
Note
x\in F^{c}\setminus A
, where
F^{c}\setminus A
is a face of
\Sigma_{r}(A,G)
.
Also,
F^{c}\setminus(A\cup\{x\})
is a face of
\mathrm{del}_{\Sigma_{r}(A,G)}(x)
, and since
x
is a shedding vertex of
\Sigma_{r}(A,G)
,
F^{c}\setminus(A\cup\{x\})
is not a facet of
\mathrm{del}_{\Sigma_{r}(A,G)}(x)
.
In particular, there exists some
H\in\mathrm{Supp}_{r}(A,G)
with
x\in H
such that

F^{c}\setminus(A\cup\{x\})\subseteq H^{c}\setminus A.

Since
H\cap A=\emptyset
we must have
H\subset F\cup\{x\}
. Furthermore, since
|F|=|H|=r
and
x\in H\setminus F
, we find that
F\setminus H=\{y\}
for some
y\in V(G)
. Thus
H=(F\setminus\{y\})\cup\{x\}\in\mathrm{Supp}_{r}(A,G)
, as desired.

To prove
(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i)
, it suffices to verify that every facet of
\mathrm{del}_{\Sigma_{r}(A,G)}(x)
is also a facet of
\Sigma_{r}(A,G)
. In other words, we need to show that
\mathcal{F}(\mathrm{del}_{\Sigma_{r}(A,G)}(x))\subseteq\mathcal{F}(\Sigma_{r}(A,G))
.
Observe that

\{F^{c}\setminus(A\cup\{x\}):F\in\mathrm{Supp}_{r}(A,G)\}\subseteq\mathcal{F}(\mathrm{del}_{\Sigma_{r}(A,G)}(x))

Thus, we want to show that
\mathcal{F}(\mathrm{del}_{\Sigma_{r}(A,G)}(x))=\{F^{c}\setminus A:F\in\mathrm{Supp}_{r}(A,G)\;\text{and}\;x\in F\}
.

If
x\in F
, then
F^{c}\setminus(A\cup\{x\})=F^{c}\setminus A
, leading to
F^{c}\setminus A\in\mathcal{F}(\Sigma_{r}(A,G))
.
Now, consider when
x\notin F
.
Based on the assumption, there is a
y\in V(G)
such that
H:=(F\setminus\{y\})\cup\{x\}\in\mathrm{Supp}_{r}(A,G)
.
Given
x\notin F\cup A
, it follows that
y\in F
. Note
H^{c}\setminus A=(F^{c}\cup\{y\})\setminus(A\cup\{x\})
, thus
F^{c}\setminus(A\cup\{x\})\subseteq H^{c}\setminus A
.
Since
H^{c}\setminus A\in\mathcal{F}(\mathrm{del}_{\Sigma_{r}(A,G)}(x))
, we have
F^{c}\setminus(A\cup\{x\})\notin\mathcal{F}(\mathrm{del}_{\Sigma_{r}(A,G)}(x))
, resulting in
\mathcal{F}(\mathrm{del}_{\Sigma_{r}(A,G)}(x))=\{F^{c}\setminus A:F\in\mathrm{Supp}_{r}(A,G)\;\text{and}\;x\in F\}
, completing the proof.
∎

Next, we describe the link and the deletion of a vertex in simplicial complexes
\Sigma_{r}(A,G)
.
If
\Delta
is a simplicial complex involving
\mathcal{F}(\Delta)=\{\Gamma_{1},\ldots,\Gamma_{t}\}
and
x\in V(\Delta)
, the link of
x
in
\Delta
is defined by
{\mathrm{lk}}_{\Delta}(x)=\langle\Gamma_{i}\setminus\{x\}\mid\Gamma_{i}\in\mathcal{F}(\Delta)\text{ and }x\in\Gamma_{i}\rangle
.
Furthermore, if
x
is a shedding vertex of
\Delta
, then the deletion of
x
in
\Delta
is
\mathrm{del}_{\Delta}(x)=\langle\Gamma_{i}\mid\Gamma_{i}\in\mathcal{F}(\Delta)\text{ and }x\notin\Gamma_{i}\rangle
.
Using these definitions, the following results can be shown.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
G
be a graph,
r
a positive integer and
x\in V(\Sigma_{r}(A,G))
. Then the following statements hold:

(1)

For
r\geq 1
,
{\mathrm{lk}}_{\Sigma_{r}(A,G)}(x)=\Sigma_{r}(A,G\setminus x)=\langle F^{c}\setminus A:F\in\mathrm{Supp}_{r}(A,G\setminus x)\rangle
.

(2)

If
x
is a shedding vertex of
\Sigma_{r}(A,G)
, then

(a)

\mathrm{del}_{\Sigma_{r}(A,G)}(x)=\Sigma_{r-1}(A\cup\{x\},G)=\langle F^{c}\setminus(A\cup\{x\}):F\in\mathrm{Supp}_{r-1}(A\cup\{x\},G)\rangle
when
r\geq 2
.

(b)

x\in N_{G}(A)
and
G[A\cup\{x\}]
is connected when
r=1
, and in this case,
\mathrm{del}_{\Sigma_{1}(A,G)}(x)=\Delta_{V(G)\setminus(A\cup\{x\})}
.

Proof.

(1) By the above characterization of the link of a vertex, we obtain the following

{\mathrm{lk}}_{\Sigma_{r}(A,G)}(x)=\langle F^{c}\setminus(A\cup\{x\}):F\in\mathrm{Supp}_{r}(A,G),\ x\notin F\cup A\rangle.

Observe that
x\in V(\Sigma_{r}(A,G))
implies
A\subseteq V(G\setminus x)
. Thus we have a one-to-one correspondence between the two sets
\{F\subseteq V(G):x\notin F\cup A,|F|=r,F\cap A=\emptyset,G[F\cup A]\text{ is connected}\}
and
\{F\subseteq V(G\setminus x):|F|=r,(G\setminus x)[F\cup A]\text{ is connected}\}
via the inclusion map. Hence,
\mathrm{Supp}_{r}(A,G\setminus x)=\{F\in\mathrm{Supp}_{r}(A,G):x\notin F\cup A\}
. Consequently,

\displaystyle{\mathrm{lk}}_{\Sigma_{r}(A,G)}(x)

\displaystyle=\langle F^{c}\setminus(A\cup\{x\}):F\in\mathrm{Supp}_{r}(A,G),\ x\notin F\cup A\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle F^{c}\setminus A:F\in\mathrm{Supp}_{r}(A,G\setminus x)\rangle.

(2) Since
x
is a shedding vertex of
\Sigma_{r}(A,G)
, we have the following.

\displaystyle\mathrm{del}_{\Sigma_{r}(A,G)}(x)

\displaystyle=\langle F^{c}\setminus A:F\in\mathrm{Supp}_{r}(A,G),x\notin F^{c}\setminus A\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle F^{c}\setminus A:F\in\mathrm{Supp}_{r}(A,G),x\in F\rangle.

First, we consider the case
r=2
. Let us consider the following two sets:

\displaystyle S_{1}

\displaystyle=\{F\subseteq V(G)\mid|F|=r,F\cap A=\emptyset,x\in F,G[F\cup A]\text{ is connected}\}

\displaystyle S_{2}

\displaystyle=\{F\subseteq V(G)\mid|F|=r-1,F\cap(A\cup\{x\})=\emptyset,x\in F,G[F\cup(A\cup\{x\})]\text{ is connected}\}.

Observe that
S_{1}=\{F\in\mathrm{Supp}_{r}(A,G):x\in F\}
,
S_{2}=\mathrm{Supp}_{r-1}(A\cup\{x\},G)
, and the map
F\mapsto F\setminus\{x\}
is a bijection between
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
. Moreover,
\langle F^{c}\setminus A:F\in S_{1}\rangle=\langle F^{c}\setminus(A\cup\{x\}):F\in S_{2}\rangle
. Thus,

\mathrm{del}_{\Sigma_{r}(A,G)}(x)=\Sigma_{r-1}(A\cup\{x\},G).

Now, suppose
r=1
. Then it follows from
Proposition

3.3
that
x\in N_{G}(A)
and
G[A\cup\{x\}]
is connected. Furthermore,

\displaystyle\mathrm{del}_{\Sigma_{1}(A,G)}(x)

\displaystyle=\langle V(G)\setminus(A\cup\{x\})\rangle

\displaystyle=\Delta_{V(G)\setminus(A\cup\{x\})}.

∎

Remark 3.5
.

Note that if
A=\emptyset
, then in statement (ii) of (2) in
Proposition

3.4
the condition
x\in N_{G}(A)
is no longer required, since
G[\{x\}]
is always a connected graph for each
x\in V(G)
. In this case, we also have
\mathrm{del}_{\Sigma_{1}(\emptyset,G)}(x)=\Delta_{V(G)\setminus\{x\}}

We now proceed to show that
\Sigma_{r}(A,G)
is a vertex decomposable simplicial complex when
A
is a non-empty subset of
V(G)
such that
G[A]
is connected. To prove this, we require the following short lemma.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
G
be a connected graph with
|V(G)|\geq 2
. Then, the set
\{v\in V(G):G\setminus v\text{ is connected}\}
has cardinality at least two.

Proof.

Since
G
is a connected graph,
G
has one spanning tree, say
T
. Since
|V(G)|\geq 2
, we see that
T
has at least two leaf vertices. Now, observe that
G\setminus x
is a connected graph for each leaf
x
of
T
. Thus, we have our result.
∎

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
G
be a graph, and let
A\subseteq V(G)
be a non-empty set such that
G[A]
is connected. Then for each
r\geq 1
, the simplicial complex
\Sigma_{r}(A,G)
is vertex decomposable.

Proof.

Let
|V(G)|=n
. We prove that
\Sigma_{r}(A,G)
is vertex decomposable using induction on
r+n
. By the given hypothesis
r\geq 1
, and since
A\neq\emptyset
, we must have
n\geq 1
.

Base case:
Let
r+n=2
. Thus, in this case, we have
|V(G)|=1
. Since
A\subseteq V(G)
is non-empty, it must be the case that
A=V(G)
. In this setting,
\mathrm{Supp}_{1}(V(G),G)
is the empty set. Therefore, the complex
\Sigma_{1}(A,G)
is the void complex, which is trivially vertex decomposable.

Induction hypothesis:
Let
r,n
be positive integers such that
r+n\geq 3
. We assume that if
r^{\prime}
and
n^{\prime}
are two positive integers such that
r^{\prime}+n^{\prime}<r+n
and there exists a graph
G^{\prime}
with
|V(G^{\prime})|=n^{\prime}
, then
\Sigma_{r^{\prime}}(A^{\prime},G^{\prime})
is a vertex decomposable simplicial complex for any non-empty
A^{\prime}\subseteq V(G^{\prime})
such that
G[A^{\prime}]
is connected.

Inductive Step :
We divide this step into the following two cases:

Case I :

r=1
. In this case, we have
\mathrm{Supp}_{1}(A,G)=N_{G}(A)
. Thus,

\Sigma_{1}(A,G)=\langle V(G)\setminus(A\cup\{v\}):v\in N_{G}(A)\rangle.

Now, if |
N_{G}(A)|\leq 1
, then
|\mathrm{Supp}_{1}(A,G)|\leq 1
, and hence
\Sigma_{1}(A,G)
is either the void complex or the empty complex, or a simplex, and hence a vertex decomposable simplicial complex. Next, consider the case when |
N_{G}(A)|\geq 2
. Fix any
x\in N_{G}(A)
. Observe that
x
is a shedding vertex of
\Sigma_{1}(A,G)
. Indeed, for each
F\in\mathrm{Supp}_{1}(A,G)
we have
|F|=1
. Fix such an
F
with
x\notin F
. Suppose
F=\{y\}
. Then
(F\setminus\{y\})\cup\{x\}=\{x\}\in\mathrm{Supp}_{1}(A,G)
since
G[A]
is connected. Now, by
Proposition

3.4
,
\mathrm{del}_{\Sigma_{1}(A,G)}(x)=\Delta_{V(G)\setminus(A\cup\{x\})}
is a simplex, and thus vertex decomposable. On the other hand, we have

{\mathrm{lk}}_{\Sigma_{1}(A,G)}(x)=\Sigma_{1}(A,G\setminus x),

again by
Proposition

3.4
. Since
1+|V(G\setminus x)|<1+n
, using the induction hypothesis we see that
{\mathrm{lk}}_{\Sigma_{1}(A,G)}(x)
is also vertex decomposable. Consequently,
\Sigma_{1}(A,G)
is a vertex decomposable simplicial complex.

Case II :

r\geq 2
. As before, if |
\mathrm{Supp}_{r}(A,G)|\leq 1
, then
\Sigma_{r}(A,G)
is the void complex or the empty complex or a simplex, and thus trivially vertex decomposable. Therefore, we may assume that |
\mathrm{Supp}_{r}(A,G)|\geq 2
. Observe that for each
F\in\mathrm{Supp}_{r}(A,G)
,
F\cap N_{G}(A)\neq\emptyset
, and in particular,
N_{G}(A)\neq\emptyset
. Let
\mathrm{Supp}_{r}(A,G)=\{F_{1},\ldots,F_{k}\}
. First consider the case when
N_{G}(A)\subseteq F_{i}
for all
i\in[k]
. Thus
|N_{G}(A)|\leq r
. If
|N_{G}(A)|=r
, then
|\mathrm{Supp}_{r}(A,G)|=1
, thus
\Sigma_{r}(A,G)
is a simplex, and hence a vertex decomposable complex. Now suppose
|N_{G}(A)|<r
. In this case, proceeding as in the proof of
Proposition

3.4
we observe that each
F\in\mathrm{Supp}_{r}(A,G)
is in one-to-one correspondence with each
H\in\mathrm{Supp}_{r-|N_{G}(A)|}(A\cup N_{G}(A),G)
. Thus,

\langle F^{c}\setminus A:F\in\mathrm{Supp}_{r}(A,G)\rangle=\langle H^{c}\setminus(A\cup N_{G}(A)):H\in\mathrm{Supp}_{r-|N_{G}(A)|}(A\cup N_{G}(A)),G)\rangle

Hence,
\Sigma_{r}(A,G)=\Sigma_{r-|N_{G}(A)|}(A\cup N_{G}(A),G)
. Since
N_{G}(A)\neq\emptyset
, by the induction hypothesis we have that
\Sigma_{r}(A,G)
is vertex decomposable.

Next, we assume, without loss of generality, that
N_{G}(A)\not\subseteq F_{1}
. Choose some
x\in N_{G}(A)\setminus F_{1}
. Our first aim is to show that
x
is a shedding vertex, and we show this by using
Proposition

3.3
. Let
C
be a connected component of
G[F]
. If
|C|=1
, and say
C=\{y\}
, then it is easy to see that

(F\setminus\{y\})\cup\{x\}\in\mathrm{Supp}_{r}(A,G),

since
G[A]
and
G[F\cup A]
both are connected. If
|C|\geq 2
, then by
Lemma

3.6
, we see that
C
has at least two vertices, say
y_{1},y_{2}
such that
G[C\setminus\{y_{i}\}]
is connected for each
i\in\{1,2\}
. If
y_{1}\in N_{G}(A)
, then one can see that

(F\setminus\{y_{2}\})\cup\{x\}\in\mathrm{Supp}_{r}(A,G),

since
G[A]
and
G[F\cup A]
both are connected. On the other hand, if
y_{1}\notin N_{G}(A)
, then
(F\setminus\{y_{1}\})\cup\{x\}\in\mathrm{Supp}_{r}(A,G)
, by similar reason. Thus,
x
is a shedding vertex of
\Sigma_{r}(A,G)
.

Now, by
Proposition

3.4
, we have

{\mathrm{lk}}_{\Sigma_{r}(A,G)}(x)=\Sigma_{r}(A,G\setminus x)\text{ and }\mathrm{del}_{\Sigma_{r}(A,G)}(x)=\Sigma_{r-1}(A\cup\{x\},G).

Observe that
r+|V(G\setminus x)|<r+n
and
r-1+|V(G)|<r+n
. Thus, by the induction hypothesis, we can say that both
{\mathrm{lk}}_{\Sigma_{r}(A,G)}(x)
and
\mathrm{del}_{\Sigma_{r}(A,G)}(x)
are vertex decomposable. Consequently,
\Sigma_{r}(A,G)
is a vertex decomposable simplicial complex, as desired.
∎

Remark 3.8
.

If
A=\emptyset
, then it is easy to see that
\mathrm{Supp}_{1}(\emptyset,G)=V(G)
. Thus
\Sigma_{1}(\emptyset,G)
is the boundary of a simplex, and hence vertex decomposable, by
[
20
, Proposition 2.2]
. On the other hand, if
r\geq 2
, then one cannot proceed as in
Theorem

3.7
, as there is no such choice of shedding vertex in
x\in N_{G}(A)
. In this case, we require a different approach to determine whether
\Sigma_{r}(\emptyset,G)
is vertex decomposable or not.

Let
W\subseteq V(G)
be such that
G[W]
is connected, and
x\in V(G)\setminus W
. Then
W\cap N_{G}(x)\neq\emptyset
if and only if there exists some
y\in W
such that
G[(W\setminus\{y\})\cup\{x\}]
is connected. Indeed, if
|W|=1
, then the “only if” part is trivially satisfied. Moreover, if
|W|\geq 2
, then using
Lemma

3.6
we can find
y\in W
such that
G[(W\setminus\{y\})\cup\{x\}]
is connected. The “if” part follows directly from the definition. With this observation in mind, we prove the following.

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
G
be a graph and
x\in V(G)
. The following are equivalent:

(1)

For every
\mathcal{E}\in E({\mathrm{Con}}_{r}(G))
with
x\notin\mathcal{E}
, we have
\mathcal{E}\cap N_{G}(x)\neq\emptyset
. In other words, for every
\mathcal{E}\in E({\mathrm{Con}}_{r}(G))
with
x\notin\mathcal{E}
, there exists a vertex
y\in\mathcal{E}
such that
(\mathcal{E}\setminus\{y\})\cup\{x\}\in E({\mathrm{Con}}_{r}(G))
.

(2)

E({\mathrm{Con}}_{r}(G\setminus N_{G}[x]))=\emptyset
.

Proof.

(1)
\Rightarrow
(2):
The proof proceeds by contradiction. Suppose that
E(\operatorname{Con}_{r}(G\setminus N_{G}[x]))\neq\emptyset
, and let
\mathcal{E}\in E(\operatorname{Con}_{r}(G\setminus N_{G}[x]))
. By construction, we then have
x\notin\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{E}\cap N_{G}(x)=\emptyset
, which is a contradiction.

(2)
\Rightarrow
(1):
Suppose
E({\mathrm{Con}}_{r}(G\setminus N_{G}[x]))=\emptyset
. Then for every
\mathcal{E}\in E({\mathrm{Con}}_{r}(G))
, we have
\mathcal{E}\cap N_{G}[x]\neq\emptyset
. So, if
x\notin\mathcal{E}
, then we must have
\mathcal{E}\cap N_{G}(x)\neq\emptyset
, as required.
∎

In the next theorem, we provide an equivalent criterion for verifying the vertex decomposability of
\Sigma_{r}(\emptyset,G)=\Sigma_{r}(G)
.

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
G
be a graph and
r\geq 2
a positive integer. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

Either
|E({\mathrm{Con}}_{r}(G))|\leq 1
or there exists a positive integer
k
and an ordering of the vertices
x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{k}
of
G
such that:

(a)

For each
i\in[k-1]\cup\{0\}
,

E\left({\mathrm{Con}}_{r}(G_{i}\setminus N_{G_{i}}[x_{i+1}])\right)=\emptyset,

where
G_{0}=G
, and
G_{i}=G\setminus\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{i}\}
for each
i\geq 1
.

(b)

k
is the least positive integer such that
|E({\mathrm{Con}}_{r}(G\setminus\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\}))|\leq 1
.

(2)

The simplicial complex
\Sigma_{r}(G)
is vertex decomposable.

Proof.

We have
\Sigma_{r}(G)=\langle F^{c}\colon F\subseteq V(G),|F|=r,G[F]\text{ is connected}\rangle
. Also, by
Proposition

3.3
and
Proposition

3.9
,
x\in V(G)
is a shedding vertex of
\Sigma_{r}(G)
if and only if
E({\mathrm{Con}}_{r}(G\setminus N_{G}[x]))=\emptyset
. Building on these observations, we proceed to the next stage of the proof.

(1)

\Rightarrow

(2)
: If
|E({\mathrm{Con}}_{r}(G))|\leq 1
, then
\Sigma_{r}(G)
is either the void complex, the empty complex, or a simplex, and hence vertex decomposable. Thus, we may assume there exists a positive integer
k
and an ordering of vertices
x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{k}
of
G
which satisfy the conditions (a) and (b). We have
E({\mathrm{Con}}_{r}(G\setminus N_{G}[x_{1}]))=\emptyset
, and thus
x_{1}
is a shedding vertex of
\Sigma_{r}(G)
. To show vertex decomposability, we must verify that both the deletion and the link of
x_{1}
in
\Sigma_{r}(G)
are vertex decomposable. By
Proposition

3.4
we have

\mathrm{del}_{\Sigma_{r}(G)}(x_{1})=\Sigma_{r-1}(\{x_{1}\},G)\text{ and }{\mathrm{lk}}_{\Sigma_{r}(G)}(x_{1})=\Sigma_{r}(G\setminus x_{1}).

Now, by
Theorem

3.7
,
\mathrm{del}_{\Sigma_{r}(G)}(x_{1})
is vertex decomposable. Thus it remains to show that
{\mathrm{lk}}_{\Sigma_{r}(G)}(x_{1})=\Sigma_{r}(G\setminus x_{1})=\Sigma_{r}(G_{1})
is vertex decomposable. Since
E({\mathrm{Con}}_{r}(G_{1}\setminus N_{G_{1}}[x_{2}]))=\emptyset
, we see that
x_{2}
is a shedding vertex of
\Sigma_{r}(G\setminus x_{1})
, and we continue the above process for
G_{1}
. After
k
-many steps, we finally obtain a vertex decomposable simplicial complex

{\mathrm{lk}}_{\Sigma_{r}(G\setminus\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{k-1}\})}(x_{k})=\Sigma_{r}(G\setminus\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\}),

which is either a simplex or the void complex, since
|E({\mathrm{Con}}_{r}(G\setminus\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\}))|\leq 1
. Consequently,
\Sigma_{r}(G)
is vertex decomposable.

(2)

\Rightarrow

(1)
: Assume that
\Sigma_{r}(G)
is vertex decomposable. If
\Sigma_{r}(G)
is a simplex or the void complex or the empty complex, then
|E({\mathrm{Con}}_{r}(G))|\leq 1
. Otherwise, there exists a shedding vertex, say
x_{1}
, such that both
{\mathrm{lk}}_{\Sigma_{r}(G)}(x_{1})
and
\mathrm{del}_{\Sigma_{r}(G)}(x_{1})
are vertex decomposable. Since
x_{1}
is a shedding vertex we also have
E({\mathrm{Con}}_{r}(G\setminus N_{G}[x_{1}]))=\emptyset
. If
|E(Con_{r}(G\setminus x_{1}))|\leq 1
then we are done. If not, then we have

{\mathrm{lk}}_{\Sigma_{r}(G)}(x_{1})=\Sigma_{r}(G\setminus x_{1})=\Sigma_{r}(G_{1}),

which is vertex decomposable. Thus it contains a shedding vertex, say
x_{2}
, for which
E({\mathrm{Con}}_{r}(G_{1}\setminus N_{G_{1}}[x_{2}]))=\emptyset
. Now, suppose
x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{i-1}
have already been chosen such that for each
j\in[i-1]
, |
E({\mathrm{Con}}_{r}(G\setminus\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{j}\}))|\geq 2
and
x_{j}
is a shedding vertex of

{\mathrm{lk}}_{\Sigma_{r}(G_{j-2})}(x_{j-1})=\Sigma_{r}(G_{j-2}\setminus x_{j-1})=\Sigma_{r}(G_{j-1}).

Thus,
E({\mathrm{Con}}_{r}(G_{i-1}\setminus N_{G_{i-1}}[x_{i}]))=\emptyset
, and hence, we have part (a) of (1). Moreover, since
|V(G)|<\infty
after
k
-many steps we have
{\mathrm{lk}}_{\Sigma_{r}(G\setminus\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{k-1}\})}(x_{k})=\Sigma_{r}(G\setminus\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\})
is either a simplex or the empty complex or the void complex. Thus,
|E({\mathrm{Con}}_{r}(G\setminus\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\}))|\leq 1
, which is part (b) of (1).
∎

Our next goal is to establish necessary and sufficient conditions for determining when the complexes
\Sigma_{r}(G_{1}\sqcup G_{2})
and
\Sigma_{r}(G_{1}*G_{2})
are vertex decomposable, under the assumption that
V(G_{1})\cap V(G_{2})=\emptyset
. The following two lemmas play a crucial role in formulating these conditions.

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
be two disjoint graphs. Then

\Sigma_{r}(G_{1}\sqcup G_{2})=(\Sigma_{r}(G_{1})*\Delta_{V(G_{2})})\;\sqcup\;(\Delta_{V(G_{1})}*\Sigma_{r}(G_{2})).

Proof.

This follows directly from the fact that
\mathcal{E}\in E(\operatorname{Con}_{r}(G_{1}\sqcup G_{2}))
if and only if either
\mathcal{E}\in E(\operatorname{Con}_{r}(G_{1}))
or
\mathcal{E}\in E(\operatorname{Con}_{r}(G_{2}))
.∎

Lemma 3.12
.

Let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
be two disjoint graphs. Then

\displaystyle\Sigma_{r}(G_{1}*G_{2})=

\displaystyle\Sigma_{r}(G_{1})*\langle V(G_{2})\rangle\;\sqcup\;\langle V(G_{1})\rangle*\Sigma_{r}(G_{2})

\displaystyle\sqcup\;\big\langle\,\mathcal{E}^{c}\;:\;|\mathcal{E}|=r,\;\mathcal{E}\cap V(G_{1})\neq\emptyset,\;\mathcal{E}\cap V(G_{2})\neq\emptyset\,\big\rangle.

Proof.

Let
W
be a facet of
\Sigma_{r}(G_{1}*G_{2})
. Then
W=\mathcal{E}^{c}
for some
\mathcal{E}\in E({\mathrm{Con}}_{r}(G_{1}*G_{2}))
. Thus, in order to determine the facets of
\Sigma_{r}(G_{1}*G_{2})
, we need to determine the structure of the circuits in the clutter
{\mathrm{Con}}_{r}(G_{1}*G_{2})
.
Let
\mathcal{E}\in E({\mathrm{Con}}_{r}(G_{1}*G_{2}))
. Then there are three possibilities:

•

\mathcal{E}\subseteq V(G_{1})
. Hence,
\mathcal{E}^{c}=(V(G_{1})\setminus\mathcal{E})\;\sqcup\;V(G_{2})\in\Sigma_{r}(G_{1})*\langle V(G_{2})\rangle.

•

\mathcal{E}\subseteq V(G_{2})
. In this case,
\mathcal{E}^{c}=V(G_{1})\;\sqcup\;(V(G_{2})\setminus\mathcal{E})\in\langle V(G_{1})\rangle*\Sigma_{r}(G_{2})
.

•

\mathcal{E}\cap V(G_{1})\neq\emptyset
and
\mathcal{E}\cap V(G_{2})\neq\emptyset
. In this case, it is easy to see that

\mathcal{E}^{c}\in\big\langle\,\mathcal{E}^{c}\;:\;|\mathcal{E}|=r,\;\mathcal{E}\cap V(G_{1})\neq\emptyset,\;\mathcal{E}\cap V(G_{2})\neq\emptyset\,\big\rangle.

This completes the proof.
∎

Based on the above two lemmas, we now proceed to prove the following two theorems.

Theorem 3.13
.

Let
r\geq 2
and let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
be graphs.
Then
\Sigma_{r}(G_{1}\sqcup G_{2})
is vertex decomposable if and only if one of the following two conditions holds:

•

\Sigma_{r}(G_{1})
is vertex decomposable and
|V(G_{2})|<r
.

•

\Sigma_{r}(G_{2})
is vertex decomposable and
|V(G_{1})|<r
.

Proof.

Observe that if
|V(G_{1})|\geq r
and
|V(G_{2})|\geq r
, then by
Proposition

3.9
,

\Sigma_{r}(G_{1}\sqcup G_{2})
has no shedding vertex and is therefore not vertex decomposable.
Thus, if
\Sigma_{r}(G_{1}\sqcup G_{2})
is vertex decomposable, at least one of the two graphs must have fewer than
r
vertices.
Without loss of generality, assume
|V(G_{2})|<r
. Then
\Sigma_{r}(G_{2})
is the empty complex, and hence by
Lemma

3.11
, we have

\Sigma_{r}(G_{1}\sqcup G_{2})=\Sigma_{r}(G_{1})*\Delta_{V(G_{2})}.

Now, by
[
20
, Proposition 2.4]
,
\Sigma_{r}(G_{1})*\Delta_{V(G_{2})}
is vertex decomposable if and only if
\Sigma_{r}(G_{1})
is vertex decomposable. Thus, we have
\Sigma_{r}(G_{1})
is vertex decomposable, as required.

Conversely, suppose
\Sigma_{r}(G_{1})
is vertex decomposable and
|V(G_{2})|<r
, then

\Sigma_{r}(G_{1}\sqcup G_{2})=\Sigma_{r}(G_{1})*\langle V(G_{2})\rangle
is vertex decomposable, again by
[
20
, Proposition 2.4]
).
∎

Theorem 3.14
.

Let
r\geq 2
, and let
G_{1},G_{2}
be two graphs. Then
\Sigma_{r}(G_{1}*G_{2})
is vertex decomposable if and only if both
\Sigma_{r}(G_{1})
and
\Sigma_{r}(G_{2})
are vertex decomposable.

Proof.

Assume that
\Sigma_{r}(G_{1}*G_{2})
is vertex decomposable. If
\Sigma_{r}(G_{1})=\emptyset
, then it is vertex decomposable. If not then, by
Lemma

3.12
, we have
{\mathrm{lk}}_{\Sigma_{r}(G_{1}*G_{2})}(V(G_{2}))=\Sigma_{r}(G_{1})
. Since the link of any face of a vertex decomposable simplicial complex is vertex decomposable (see
[
20
, Proposition 2.3]
), we find that
\Sigma_{r}(G_{1})
is vertex decomposable. Similarly, considering the link of the face
V(G_{1})
, we find that
\Sigma_{r}(G_{2})
is vertex decomposable.

Conversely, assume that both
\Sigma_{r}(G_{1})
and
\Sigma_{r}(G_{2})
are vertex decomposable. Let
G=G_{1}*G_{2}
with
|V(G_{1})|=n_{1}
and
|V(G_{2})|=n_{2}
. We prove that
\Sigma_{r}(G)
is vertex decomposable using induction on
|V(G)|=n_{1}+n_{2}
.

Base case:

n_{1}+n_{2}\leq r
. Then
\Sigma_{r}(G)
is either the void complex or the empty complex, which is clearly vertex decomposable.

Induction hypothesis:
Let
n_{1}
and
n_{2}
be positive integers such that
n_{1}+n_{2}\geq r+1
. We assume that if
n_{1}^{\prime}
and
n_{2}^{\prime}
are two positive integers such that
n_{1}^{\prime}+n_{2}^{\prime}<n_{1}+n_{2}
and there exists graphs
G_{1}^{\prime}
and
G_{2}^{\prime}
with
|V(G_{1}^{\prime})|=n_{1}^{\prime}
and
|V(G_{2}^{\prime})|=n_{2}^{\prime}
, then whenever
\Sigma_{r}(G_{1}^{\prime})
and
\Sigma_{r}(G_{2}^{\prime})
are vertex decomposable, so is
\Sigma_{r}(G_{1}^{\prime}*G_{2}^{\prime})
.

Inductive Step:
We choose a shedding vertex
x
of
\Sigma_{r}(G)
as follows: if
\Sigma_{r}(G_{1})
is a simplex or the empty complex or the void complex, then take
x
to be any vertex of
G_{1}
. Otherwise, take
x\in V(G_{1})
such that
x
is a shedding vertex of
\Sigma_{r}(G_{1})
. Then, by
Proposition

3.3
,
x
is a shedding vertex of
\Sigma_{r}(G)
. Indeed,

E({\mathrm{Con}}_{r}(G\setminus N_{G}[x]))=E({\mathrm{Con}}_{r}(G_{1}\setminus N_{G_{1}}[x])=\emptyset.

Next, by
Proposition

3.4
, we have

{\mathrm{lk}}_{\Sigma_{r}(G)}(x)=\Sigma_{r}(G\setminus x)\text{ and }\mathrm{del}_{\Sigma_{r}(G)}(x)=\Sigma_{r-1}(\{x\},G).

Observe that
G\setminus x=(G_{1}\setminus x)*G_{2}
and
{\mathrm{lk}}_{\Sigma_{r}(G_{1})}(x)=\Sigma_{r}(G_{1}\setminus x)
is vertex decomposable. Since,
|V((G_{1}\setminus x)*G_{2})|=n_{1}-1+n_{2}<n_{1}+n_{2}
, by the induction hypothesis
\Sigma_{r}(G\setminus x)
is vertex decomposable. Thus, by
Theorem

3.7
,
\Sigma_{r}(G)
, is vertex decomposable.
∎

Remark 3.15
.

Note that in case
r=1
, by
Remark

3.8
, we always have that both the simplicial complexes
\Sigma_{1}(G_{1}\sqcup G_{2})
and
\Sigma_{1}(G_{1}*G_{2})
are vertex decomposable for any choice of
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
.

4.
Generalizing Fröberg’s theorem to clutters

In this section, we apply the results established in
Section

3
to investigate the vertex decomposability, shellability, and Cohen-Macaulay property of the simplicial complex
\Sigma_{r}(A,G)
for several important classes of graphs.
As an application, we obtain a clutter version of the well-known Fröberg’s theorem for
{\mathrm{Con}}_{r}(G)
; we start with the chordal graphs.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
G
be a chordal graph, and
r\geq 2
. The following are equivalent:

(1)

\Sigma_{r}(G)
is vertex decomposable.

(2)

\Sigma_{r}(G)
is shellable.

(3)

\Sigma_{r}(G)
is Cohen-Macaulay.

(4)

G
is
r
-gap-free.

(5)

The clutter
{\mathrm{Con}}_{r}(G)
is co-chordal.

Proof.

The implications
(1)
\Rightarrow
(2)
\Rightarrow
(3)
follows from the known hierarchy of conditions in
Equation

1
. Also,
(3)
\Rightarrow
(4)
follows from
[
10
, Theorem 3]
and
[
14
, Theorem 1.4]
, and
(1)
\Rightarrow
(5)
follows from
[
18
, Theorem 3.10]
. Moreover,
(5)
\Rightarrow
(3)
follows from
[
5
, Theorem 3.3]
and
[
10
, Theorem 3]
. Thus, to conclude the proof of the theorem, it remains to show
(4)

\Rightarrow

(1)
, and we prove this by induction on
|V(G)|
.

Base Case:

|V(G)|\leq r
. Recall that
\Sigma_{r}(G)=\langle V(G)\setminus F\mid F\in E({\mathrm{Con}}_{r}(G))\rangle
. Thus, in this case,
\Sigma_{r}(G)
is either the void complex or the empty complex, and hence a vertex decomposable simplicial complex.

Induction hypothesis:
Let
|V(G)|\geq r+1
, and if
\widehat{G}
is a chordal graph such that
|V(\widehat{G})|<|V(G)|
and
\widehat{G}
is
r
-gap-free, then
\Sigma_{r}(\widehat{G})
is vertex decomposable.

Inductive Step:
Since
G
is
r
-gap-free we have
\gamma_{r}(G)\leq 1
. If
\gamma_{r}(G)=0
, then
G=G_{1}\bigsqcup G_{2}\bigsqcup\cdots\bigsqcup G_{l}
, where
|V(G_{i})|\leq r-1
for each
i\in[l]
. Therefore, by
Theorem

3.13
it follows that
\Sigma_{r}(G)
is vertex decomposable since each
\Sigma_{r}(G_{i})
is a void complex.

Next, we consider the case when
\gamma_{r}(G)=1
. If
|E({\mathrm{Con}}_{r}(G))|=1
, then
\Sigma_{r}(G)
is a simplex, and thus vertex decomposable. Hence, we may assume that
|E({\mathrm{Con}}_{r}(G))|\geq 2
. Note that to show
\Sigma_{r}(G)
is vertex decomposable, it is enough to find a shedding vertex. Indeed, if
x
is a shedding vertex of
\Sigma_{r}(G)
, then by
Proposition

3.4
we have

{\mathrm{lk}}_{\Sigma_{r}(G)}(x)=\Sigma_{r}(G\setminus x),\text{ and }\mathrm{del}_{\Sigma_{r}(G)}(x)=\Sigma_{r-1}(\{x\},G).

Since
G\setminus x
is a chordal graph and
\gamma_{r}(G\setminus x)\leq\gamma_{r}(G)\leq 1
, using the induction hypothesis and by
Theorem

3.7
, we obtain that the simplicial complexes
\Sigma_{r}(G\setminus x)
and
\Sigma_{r-1}(\{x\},G)
both are vertex decomposable. Thus
\Sigma_{r}(G)
is a vertex decomposable simplicial complex.

To find a shedding vertex of
\Sigma_{r}(G)
, we use the combinatorial description available in
Proposition

3.9
. More specifically, our aim now is to find a vertex
x\in V(G)
such that

E({\mathrm{Con}}_{r}(G\setminus N_{G}[x]))=\emptyset
. Note that, since
G
is chordal,
G
contains a simplicial vertex, say
v
. Let
G^{\prime}
denote the graph
G\setminus v
. As before, by the induction hypothesis, we have that
\Sigma_{r}(G^{\prime})
is vertex decomposable and hence, it contains a shedding vertex, say
w\in V(G)\setminus\{v\}
. Therefore, by
Proposition

3.9
we have
E({\mathrm{Con}}_{r}(G^{\prime}\setminus N_{G^{\prime}}[w]))=\emptyset
. We have the following two cases to consider.

Case I:

v\in N_{G}[w]
. In this case, it is easy to see that
E({\mathrm{Con}}_{r}\left(G\setminus N_{G}[w]\right))=\emptyset
, and thus
w
is a shedding vertex of
\Sigma_{r}(G)
.

Case II:

v\notin N_{G}[w]
. Since
E({\mathrm{Con}}_{r}(G^{\prime}\setminus N_{G^{\prime}}[w]))=\emptyset
, in this case we can write
G\setminus(N_{G}[w]\cup\{v\})=G_{1}\sqcup G_{2}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup G_{k}
, where
|V(G_{i})|\leq r-1
for each
i=1,\ldots,k
. Moreover, since
v
is a simplicial vertex of
G
, we can assume, without loss of generality, that
N_{G}(v)\setminus N_{G}(w)\subseteq V(G_{1})
. Let
G_{1}^{\prime}=G[V(G_{1})\cup\{v\}]
. Then we can write

G\setminus N_{G}[w]=G_{1}^{\prime}\sqcup G_{2}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup G_{k},

where
|V(G_{i})|\leq r-1
for
i=2,\ldots,k
and
|V(G_{1}^{\prime})|\leq r
.

Observe that if
|V(G_{1}^{\prime})|\leq r-1
, then
E({\mathrm{Con}}_{r}\left(G\setminus N_{G}[w]\right))=\emptyset
, and thus
w
is a shedding vertex of
\Sigma_{r}(G)
. Thus, we may assume that
|V(G_{1}^{\prime})|=r
. In this case, let

E({\mathrm{Con}}_{r}(G))=\{\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{E}_{p}\},

where
\mathcal{E}=\{u\mid u\in V(G_{1}^{\prime})\}
. Note that since
\gamma_{r}(G)\leq 1
, for each
i\in[p]
there exists some
x_{i}\in\mathcal{E}
and
w_{i}\in\mathcal{E}_{i}
such that
\{x_{i},w_{i}\}\in E(G)
. Since
G_{1}^{\prime}
and
G_{i}
are disconnected for each
i\in[k]
, we must have
w_{i}\in N_{G}(w)
. It may happen that for some
i\neq j
,
w_{i}=w_{j}
. Thus, without loss of generality, we may assume that there exists some integer
s\leq p
such that for each
i\in[s]
we have
x_{i}\in\mathcal{E}
and distinct
w_{i}\in N_{G}(w)\cap\mathcal{E}_{i}
such that
\{x_{i},w_{i}\}\in E(G)
and furthermore, for each
i\in[p]\setminus[s]

\mathcal{E}_{i}\cap\{w_{1},\ldots,w_{s}\}\neq\emptyset
. Let
W=\{w_{1},\ldots,w_{s}\}
. Our aim now is to prove the following.

Claim
:
G[W]
forms a clique in
G
.

Proof of Claim:
Let us choose some distinct
i,j\in[s]
. Since
G_{1}^{\prime}
forms a connected component of
G
, let us choose an induced path
\mathcal{P}
from
x_{i}
to
x_{j}
. Then
\mathcal{P}\cup\{w_{i},w,w_{j}\}
forms a path in
G
. By construction, there is no circuit between the vertices of
\mathcal{P}
and
w
. However, there might be circuits between the vertices of
\mathcal{P}
and
w_{i},w_{j}
. Thus, if necessary, considering an induced path between
w_{i}
and
w_{j}
via the vertices of
\mathcal{P}
, we obtain a cycle of length at least
4
on the vertices
w,w_{i},w_{j}
and a subset of the vertices of
\mathcal{P}
. Since
G
is chordal, we must have
\{w_{i},w_{j}\}
, and this completes the proof of the claim.

Using the claim we see that for each
i\in[s]
,
N_{G}[w_{i}]\cap\mathcal{E}\neq\emptyset
and
N_{G}[w_{i}]\cap\mathcal{E}_{i}\neq\emptyset
for all
i\in[p]
.Consequently, for each
i\in[s]
,
E({\mathrm{Con}}_{r}(G\setminus N_{G}[w_{i}]))=\emptyset
, and hence,
w_{i}
is a shedding vertex of
\Sigma_{r}(G)
. This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

In
[
2
]
, it was shown that if

-connected ideal, the linear resolution and linear quotient property both are equivalent to

being

. Thus, using

.

The next theorem concerns the complex
\Sigma_{r}(G)
in the case where

is a co-chordal graph. Here we remark that this result provides a combinatorial analog of a result originally obtained via algebraic methods in
[
7
, Theorem 3.12]

Theorem 4.3
.

For
r\geq 2
, if
G
is co-chordal, then
\Sigma_{r}(G)
is vertex decomposable.

Proof.

The proof is again by induction on
|V(G)|
. As before, the base case of the induction procedure is easy to see since
|V(G)|\leq r
implies
\Sigma_{r}(G)
is either the void complex or the empty complex. Thus, we may assume
|V(G)|>r
. Note that for each
y\in V(G)
, the induced subgraph
G\setminus y
is again a co-chordal graph. Thus, by the induction hypothesis,
Proposition

3.4
, and
Theorem

3.7
it follows that

{\mathrm{lk}}_{\Sigma_{r}(G)}(y)=\Sigma_{r}(G\setminus y),\text{ and }\mathrm{del}_{\Sigma_{r}(G)}(y)=\Sigma_{r-1}(\{y\},G),

is vertex decomposable provided that

\Sigma_{r}(G)
.

To find a shedding vertex, we use

and the fact that the complement of

is a chordal graph. Indeed, let

be a simplicial vertex of

. Then, one can observe that

. In particular,
E({\mathrm{Con}}_{r}(G\setminus N_{G}[x]))=\emptyset
since

. Thus, by
Proposition

3.9

x
is a shedding vertex of
\Sigma_{r}(G)
, and hence,
\Sigma_{r}(G)
is vertex decomposable, as desired.
∎

Remark 4.5
.

Since
G

An analogous result to those established in the preceding theorems also holds for cographs.

Definition 4.6
.

A
cograph
is defined recursively as follows:

(1)

Base Case:

Every single-vertex graph
K_{1}
is a cograph.

(2)

Recursive Step:

If
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are cographs, then the following graphs are also cographs:

•

Disjoint union:

G=G_{1}\sqcup G_{2}
.

•

Join:

G=G_{1}*G_{2}
.

(3)

Closure:

Every cograph can be obtained from
K_{1}
by a finite sequence of the above two operations.

The structural simplicity of cographs, arising from their recursive construction via disjoint unions and joins, allows us to analyze the equivalence effectively.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
G
be a cograph, and
r\geq 2
. The following are equivalent:

(1)

\Sigma_{r}(G)
is vertex decomposable.

(2)

\Sigma_{r}(G)
is shellable.

(3)

\Sigma_{r}(G)
is Cohen-Macaulay.

(4)

G
is
r
-gap-free.

(5)

The clutter
{\mathrm{Con}}_{r}(G)
is co-chordal.

Proof.

We proceed similarly to
Theorem

4.1
. The implications
(1)
\Rightarrow
(2)
\Rightarrow
(3)
follow from the known hierarchy of conditions in
Equation

1
. Furthermore,
(3)
\Rightarrow
(4)
follows from
[
10
, Theorem 3]
and
[
14
, Theorem 1.4]
, while
(1)
\Rightarrow
(5)
follows from
[
18
, Theorem 3.10]
. Finally,
(5)
\Rightarrow
(3)
follows from
[
5
, Theorem 3.3]
and
[
10
, Theorem 3]
. Hence, to complete the proof, it remains to establish
(4)
\Rightarrow
(1)
, which we do by induction on
|V(G)|
.

Base Case:
If
|V(G)|\leq r
, then
\Sigma_{r}(G)
is either the void complex or the empty complex, and is therefore vertex decomposable.

Induction Hypothesis:
Let
|V(G)|\geq r+1
, and assume that for any
r
-gap-free cograph
\widehat{G}
with
|V(\widehat{G})|<|V(G)|
, the complex
\Sigma_{r}(\widehat{G})
is vertex decomposable.

Inductive Step:
Since
G
is an
r
-gap-free cograph, the recursive definition of cographs yields the following two cases:

Case 1.

Suppose that
G
is the disjoint union of two nonempty cographs
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
. Write

G_{1}=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{n_{1}}G_{1i}\qquad\text{and}\qquad G_{2}=\bigsqcup_{j=1}^{n_{2}}G_{2j},

where
G_{ij}
’s are connected components of
G_{i}
for each
i\in[2]
and
j\in[n_{i}]
. Since
G
is
r
-gap-free, either every connected component of
G
has vertex-set cardinality at most
r-1
, or there is exactly one connected component whose vertex-set cardinality is at least
r
. Without loss of generality, assume that if such a component exists, then it is
G_{2n_{2}}
.

Now consider

G_{1}^{\prime}=\left(\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{n_{1}}G_{1i}\right)\sqcup\left(\bigsqcup_{j=1}^{n_{2}-1}G_{2j}\right).

Then

G=G_{1}^{\prime}\sqcup G_{2n_{2}}.

As
G
is a cograph,
G_{1}^{\prime}
and
G_{2n_{2}}
are cographs. Moreover, both
G_{1}^{\prime}
and
G_{2n_{2}}
are
r
-gap-free. Thus, by the induction hypothesis and by
Theorem

3.13
, it follows that
\Sigma_{r}(G)
is vertex decomposable.

Case 2:

G
is the join of two nonempty cographs
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
. Observe that both
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are
r
-gap-free, and thus, by the induction hypothesis,
\Sigma_{r}(G_{1})
and
\Sigma_{r}(G_{2})
are vertex decomposable. Hence, by
Theorem

3.14
, we have that
\Sigma_{r}(G)
is vertex decomposable.

This completes the inductive step, and hence the proof.
∎

Remark 4.8
.

Complete multipartite graphs form a subclass of cographs. Hence, by
Theorem

4.7
, for a complete multipartite graph
G
, the properties of vertex decomposability, shellability, and Cohen-Macaulayness of
\Sigma_{r}(G)
are all equivalent to
G
being
r
-gap-free. Furthermore, this is equivalent to the associated clutter
\mathrm{Con}_{r}(G)
being co-chordal.

We now turn our attention to the complex
\Sigma_{r}(C_{n})
. We begin with the following lemma, whose proof depends on the computation of the Leray number of
\mathrm{Ind}_{r}(C_{n})
. Recall that, a simplicial complex
\Delta
is called
d
-Leray
if
\tilde{H}_{i}(Y;\mathbb{Z})=0
for all induced subcomplexes
Y\subseteq\Delta
and for all
i\geq d
. The
Leray number
of
\Delta
, denoted
L(\Delta)
, is the minimal
d
such that
\Delta
is
d
-Leray.

Lemma 4.9
.

For the cycle graph
C_{n}
, if the complex
\Sigma_{r}(C_{n})
is Cohen-Macaulay, then
n\leq r+2
.

Proof.

We show that if
n>r+2
, then
\Sigma_{r}(C_{n})
is not Cohen-Macaulay. Indeed, by the Eagon-Reiner theorem
[
10
, Theorem 3]
and Hochster’s formula
[
16
]
, it is enough to show that the Leray number
L(\mathrm{Ind}_{r}(C_{n}))>r-1
for each
n>r+2
. By
[
9
, Theorem 4.6]
we have

(2)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ind}_{r}(C_{n})\cong\begin{cases}\displaystyle\bigvee_{r}\mathbb{S}^{rk-k-1}&\mathrm{if\ }n=(r+1)k;\\
\mathbb{S}^{rk-k-1}&\mathrm{if\ }n=(r+1)k+1;\\
\mathbb{S}^{rk-k}&\mathrm{if\ }n=(r+1)k+2;\\
\vdots&\vdots\\
\mathbb{S}^{rk-k+r-2}&\mathrm{if\ }n=(r+1)k+r,\end{cases}

where
n-1\geq r\geq 2
and
k=\lfloor\frac{n}{r+1}\rfloor
. Since
n>r+2
, we can write
n=(r+1)k+l
, where
2\leq l\leq r
in case
k=1
, and
0\leq l\leq r
in case
k\geq 2
. First, consider the case when
2\leq l\leq r
with
k\geq 1
. Then from
Equation

2
we have

\tilde{H}_{rk-k+l-2}(\mathrm{Ind}_{r}(C_{n});\mathbb{K})\cong\mathbb{Z}.

Observe that
rk-k+l-2\geq r-1
since
l\geq 2

k\geq 1
, and
r\geq 2
. Thus,
L(\mathrm{Ind}_{r}(C_{n})>r-1
, as required. Next, we consider the case where
l=0,1
and
k\geq 2
. In this case, again by
Equation

2
, we have

\displaystyle\tilde{H}_{rk-k-1}(\mathrm{Ind}_{r}(C_{n});\mathbb{K})\cong\begin{cases}\mathbb{Z}^{r}&\text{ if }l=0,\\
\mathbb{Z}&\text{ if }l=1.\end{cases}

Observe that
rk-k-1\geq r-1
since
k\geq 2
and
r\geq 2
. Thus,
L(\mathrm{Ind}_{r}(C_{n})>r-1
in this case too, and this completes the proof.
∎

Remark 4.10
.

The conclusion of

can also be deduced from
[

, Proposition 5.3]
, where the authors have used the fact that for cycle graphs, the

-connected ideal is the same as the

Theorem 4.11
.

Let
n\geq 3
and
r\geq 2
, and let
C_{n}
be the cycle graph on
n
vertices. The following statements are equivalent:

(1)

\Sigma_{r}(G)
is vertex decomposable.

(2)

\Sigma_{r}(G)
is shellable.

(3)

\Sigma_{r}(G)
is Cohen-Macaulay.

(4)

n\leq r+2
.

(5)

The clutter
{\mathrm{Con}}_{r}(G)
is co-chordal.

Proof.

We first show that
(4)
\Rightarrow
(1)
. As before, by
Proposition

3.4
, for any

we have

\mathrm{del}_{\Sigma_{r}(G)}(x)=\Sigma_{r-1}(\{x\},G),

which is vertex decomposable (by
Theorem

3.7
). Furthermore,

{\mathrm{lk}}_{\Sigma_{r}(G)}(x)=\Sigma_{r}(G\setminus x),

where

,
\Sigma_{r}(G\setminus x)

is a path on

, and hence,

. Therefore, any

is a shedding vertex of
\Sigma_{r}(G)
. Consequently,
\Sigma_{r}(G)
is a vertex decomposable simplicial complex.

The implications
(1)
\Rightarrow
(2)
\Rightarrow
(3)
follows from the known hierarchy of conditions in
Equation

1
. Also,
(3)
\Rightarrow
(4)
follows from
Lemma

4.9
, and
(1)
\Rightarrow
(5)
follows from
[
18
, Theorem 3.10]
. Moreover,
(5)
\Rightarrow
(3)
follows from
[
5
, Theorem 3.3]
and
[
10
, Theorem 3]
. This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Theorem 4.15

(2)

\Sigma_{r}(G)

.

The clutter
{\mathrm{Con}}_{r}(G)

(4)
\Rightarrow
(5)
. Suppose if possible,
(4)
holds but

. Our aim is to show that

is not
r
-gap-free. Consider the following two cases:

r
is even, i.e.,

as above one can see that

Proposition 5.3
.

Let

Proof.

For each
r\geq 8
, one can observe that
(r+2)+\lfloor\frac{r+3}{2}\rfloor<2r
. Thus, analyzing the remaining
3\leq r\leq 7
cases individually, we see that

is
r

Our aim now is to show that
\Sigma_{r}(G)
is not vertex decomposable. Indeed, for any

, we have
|N_{G}[v]|\leq 3
. Thus,

\Sigma_{r}(G)
has no shedding vertex. Consequently,
\Sigma_{r}(G)
is not vertex decomposable.

To show that
\Sigma_{r}(G)
is shellable, we need to find a shelling order on the set of facets of
\Sigma_{r}(G)
, and we do this by defining first a special total order on
\mathcal{P}(T)
, the power set of
T
. Note that the elements
\{i\mid x_{i}\in T\}
have natural order induced from the set of natural numbers, which we denote by
<_{\mathbb{N}}
. Now, we endow
\mathcal{P}(T)
with a special order
\lessdot
which is described in the following steps.

Let
I,J\in\mathcal{P}(T)
, where
I=\{x_{i_{1}}\ldots,x_{i_{n}}\}
and
J=\{x_{j_{1}},\ldots,x_{j_{m}}\}
. Let
i=\max_{<_{\mathbb{N}}}\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{n}\}
and
j=\max_{<_{\mathbb{N}}}\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{m}\}
.

Step 0:

Declare
\emptyset\lessdot I
for any
\emptyset\neq I\in\mathcal{P}(T)
.

Step 1:

If
i<_{\mathbb{N}}j
, then declare
I\lessdot J
.

Step 2:

If
i=j
, and
|I|<_{\mathbb{N}}|J|
, then declare
I\lessdot J
.

Step 3:

If
i=j
and
|I|=|J|
, then consider
I^{\prime}=I\setminus\{x_{i}\}
and
J^{\prime}=J\setminus\{x_{j}\}
, and go back to Step 1. If
I^{\prime}\lessdot J^{\prime}
, then declare
I\lessdot J
.

As an example, if we consider the set
T=\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}\}

\mathcal{P}(T)
are ordered as follows:

\displaystyle\emptyset\lessdot\{x_{1}\}

\displaystyle\lessdot\{x_{2}\}\lessdot\{x_{1},x_{2}\}\lessdot\{x_{3}\}\lessdot\{x_{1},x_{3}\}\lessdot\{x_{2},x_{3}\}\lessdot\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}\}\lessdot\{x_{4}\}\lessdot\{x_{1},x_{4}\}\lessdot

\displaystyle\{x_{2},x_{4}\}\lessdot\{x_{3},x_{4}\}\lessdot\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{4}\}\lessdot\{x_{1},x_{3},x_{4}\}\lessdot\{x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}\}\lessdot\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}\}.

It is easy to see that
\lessdot
is a total order on the elements of
\mathcal{P}(T)
. We now proceed to provide a shelling order
<
on
\mathcal{F}(\Sigma_{r}(G))
based on the ordering

.

Let

has a shelling order. Based on these observations, we define the following:

\bullet

The facets in
\mathcal{F}_{\emptyset}
are given a shelling order.

\bullet

For each fixed

\bullet

For any two distinct

.

Claim
:
(\mathcal{F}(\Sigma_{r}(G)),<)
is a shelling order.

Proof of Claim
: By construction, if
F\in\mathcal{F}_{\emptyset}
and
F
is not the first facet in the given ordering, then for any
F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}_{\emptyset}
with
F^{\prime}<F
, there exists some
F^{\prime\prime}\in\mathcal{F}_{\emptyset}
with
F^{\prime\prime}<F
such that
F^{\prime}\cap F\subseteq F^{\prime\prime}\cap F
and
\dim(F^{\prime\prime}\cap F)=\dim F-1
.

Now, let
F\in\mathcal{F}_{I}
for some
\emptyset\neq I\in\mathcal{P}(T)
. Take any

with
F_{1}<F
. Let

be the path with two end points

. If

since

Open Problems and Future Directions

We end the article by briefly outlining some open problems, in addition to the
1.3
stated in the Introduction.

•

CM but not shellable
\Sigma_{r}(G)
.
Find
G,r
with
\mathrm{Con}_{r}(G)
co-chordal (hence linear resolution of the
r
-connected ideal) while the complex
\Sigma_{r}(G)
fails to be shellable. Potential search spaces include circulant/Cayley graphs, prisms
C_{n}\times P_{2}
, toroidal grids
C_{m}\times C_{n}
, etc.

•

Beyond covered families.
Test the full equivalence (vertex decomposable
\Leftrightarrow
shellable
\Leftrightarrow
Cohen-Macaulay
\Leftrightarrow
co-chordal
\mathrm{Con}_{r}(G)
) on chordal bipartite graphs (bipartite graph with every cycle of length at least
6
has a chord) and comparability/permutation graphs, where simplicial structures and elimination orders might extend the current proofs.

•

Quantitative invariants.
In families where
\Sigma_{r}(G)
is vertex decomposable/shellable, derive closed-form Betti tables and regularity formula, bridging the deletion-link calculus here with known formulas for chordal clutters and ideals with linear quotients.

•

Higher-independence topology.
Leverage Leray-number and homotopy-type results for higher independence complexes to sharpen our understanding (e.g., in case of circulant graphs, grids) and to identify obstructions to CM property in
\Sigma_{r}(G)
via Hochster’s formula.

•

Hypergraph generalization.
Extend
\Sigma_{r}(A,G)
to
r
-co-connected complexes for
d
-uniform clutters beyond
\mathrm{Con}_{r}(G)
and relate Cohen-Macaulay/shellability to Bigdeli et al. chordality, seeking a broader “Fröberg-type” dictionary for uniform clutters.

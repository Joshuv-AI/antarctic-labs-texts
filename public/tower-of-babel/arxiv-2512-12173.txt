Abstract




Linear Superposition of Quadratic Functions in a Fifth Order
KdV-Type Equation

Avadh Saxena

Theoretical Division and Center for Nonlinear Studies,
Los Alamos National Laboratory, Los Alamos, New Mexico 87545, USA

Abstract:

We show that a fifth order KdV-type equation admits several real as well as
complex parity-time reversal or PT-invariant solutions with linear superposition
of quadratic functions involving Jacobi elliptic functions of the form
{\rm dn}^{2}(x,m)
,

{\rm cn}(x,m){\rm dn}(x,m)
,
{\rm sn}(x,m){\rm cn}(x,m)
and
{\rm sn}(x,m){\rm dn}(x,m)
. These
results must be contrasted with only partial superposition of such functions in
Korteweg-de Vries (KdV),
\phi^{3}
and a few other nonlinear equations.

1
Introduction

One of the hallmarks of a linear system is the superposition principle. In
particular, if
f(x)
and
g(x)
are two solutions of a linear equation, then
any arbitrary superposition of these two functions is also a solution of this
equation. Unfortunately, because of the nonlinear term, this superposition
principle does not hold in nonlinear systems. In particular, even if
f(x)
and

g(x)
are two solutions of a nonlinear equation, their linear superposition
is in general not a solution of that nonlinear equation. However, in recent
years it has been shown that several nonlinear equations
[
1
]
, both
integrable and nonintegrable, discrete and continuous, as well as local and nonlocal
admit linear superposition. In particular, in several nonlinear models it has been
shown that not only the Jacobi elliptic
linear
functions
{\rm dn}(x,m),{\rm cn}(x,m)
and

{\rm sn}(x,m)
are solutions of these nonlinear equations but even linear superpositions
of these functions, i.e.,
A{\rm dn}(x,m)+B{\rm cn}(x,m),A{\rm dn}(x,m)+B{\rm sn}(x,m)
and

A{\rm cn}(x,m)+B{\rm sn}(x,m)
are also the solutions of the same nonlinear equation
for a fixed value of
B/A

[
2
,
3
,
4
]
. Here,
0\leq m\leq 1
is the
modulus of the Jacobi elliptic functions
[
5
]
.

However, to the best of our knowledge, no example is known in the literature
where a nonlinear equation admits a linear superposition of
quadratic
functions.
What is known so far is only the “partial superposition” of quadratic functions in a
few nonlinear equations such as KdV,
\phi^{3}
, etc.
[
2
,
3
,
4
]
.
In particular, while
{\rm dn}^{2}(x,m)
is a solution of these nonlinear equations, neither

{\rm cn}(x,m){\rm dn}(x,m)
,
{\rm sn}(x,m){\rm dn}(x,m)
nor
{\rm sn}(x,m){\rm cn}(x,m)
are the solutions
of these nonlinear equations. Nevertheless, a superposition of
{\rm dn}^{2}(x,m)
with any
of these three quadratic functions, i.e.
A{\rm dn}^{2}(x,m)+B{\rm cn}(x,m){\rm dn}(x,m),A{\rm dn}^{2}(x,m)+B{\rm sn}(x,m){\rm cn}(x,m)
as well as
A{\rm dn}^{2}(x,m)+B{\rm sn}(x,m){\rm dn}(x,m)

are the solutions of these nonlinear equations for a fixed ratio of
B/A
.
Similarly, while
{\rm sech}^{2}(x)
is a solution of these nonlinear equations,

{\rm sech}(x)\tanh(x)
is not a solution of these nonlinear equations. However, a
superposition of the two, i.e.
A{\rm sech}^{2}(x)+B{\rm sech}(x)\tanh(x)
is still a
solution of these nonlinear equations for a fixed ratio of
B/A
.

The purpose of this paper is to discuss an example where not only two quadratic
functions, say
f(x)
and
g(x)
, are solutions of the nonlinear equation but
even a linear superposition
Af(x)+Bg(x)
, for fixed value of
B/A
, is a
solution of the same nonlinear equation. In particular, we consider the 5th
order KdV-type equation which was discussed extensively by Karpman

[
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
]
and others
[
10
,
11
,
12
]

u_{t}+\alpha u^{2}(x,t)u_{x}+\beta u_{xxx}+\gamma u_{xxxxx}=0\,.

(1)

On using the moving ansatz

y=\eta(x-vt)\,,

(2)

in Eq. (
1
), it takes the form

-vu_{y}+\alpha u^{2}(y)u_{y}+\beta\eta^{2}u_{yyy}+\gamma u_{yyyyy}=0\,.

(3)

We show that Eq. (
3
) admits 10 periodic and two hyperbolic quadratically
superposed solutions. In particular, we show that Eq. (
3
) not only
admits
{\rm dn}^{2}(y,m),{\rm cn}(y,m){\rm dn}(y,m),{\rm cn}(y,m){\rm sn}(y,m)
and

{\rm sn}(y,m){\rm dn}(y,m)
as solutions but even admits all possible superpositions
of these functions as a solution of the same nonlinear equation, Eq. (
3
).
In the hyperbolic limit of
m=1
, Eq. (
3
) not only admits both
{\rm sech}^{2}(x)

and
{\rm sech}(x)\tanh(x)
as solutions but even their complex (parity-time reversal or)
PT-invariant superposition with PT-eigenvalue of
+1
as well as
-1
are the
solutions of Eq. (
3
). Specifically, out of the 10 periodic solutions while two of
them are real solutions, 8 solutions are complex PT-invariant solutions with 4 having
PT-eigenvalue
+1
and another 4 having PT-eigenvalue
-1
. These solutions
are of the form
Af(x)+Bg(x)
for a fixed ratio
B/A
.

2
Six Solutions of the KdV-like Equation

Let us first show that
{\rm dn}^{2}(y,m)
,
{\rm cn}(y,m){\rm dn}(y,m)
,
{\rm cn}(y,m){\rm sn}(y,m)
,

{\rm sn}(y,m){\rm dn}(y,m)
and hence
{\rm sech}^{2}(y)
and
{\rm sech}(y)\tanh(y)
are the
solutions of Eq. (
3
).

Solution I

It is not difficult to show that Eq. (
3
) admits the solution

u(y)=A{\rm dn}^{2}(y,m)\,,

(4)

provided

\alpha A^{2}=-360\gamma\eta^{4}\,,~~\beta\eta^{2}=-20(2-m)\gamma\eta^{4}\,,~~v=-8[8m^{2}+23(1-m)]\gamma\eta^{4}\,.

(5)

Solution II

Eq. (
3
) also admits the solution

u(y)=A\sqrt{m}{\rm cn}(y,m)dn(y,m)\,,

(6)

provided

\alpha A^{2}=-360\gamma\eta^{4}\,,~~\beta\eta^{2}=-10(1+m)\gamma\eta^{4}\,,~~v=-[86m-11(1+m^{2})]\gamma\eta^{4}\,.

(7)

Solution III

In the limit
m=1
, the solutions I and II go over to the hyperbolic
solution

u(y)=A{\rm sech}^{2}(y)\,,

(8)

provided

\alpha A^{2}=-360\gamma\eta^{4}\,,~~\beta\eta^{2}=-20\gamma\eta^{4}\,,~~v=-64\gamma\eta^{4}\,.

(9)

Notice that for the solutions I, II and III, the sign of
\alpha,\beta,v
is
the same and is opposite to that of
\gamma
.

Solution IV

Eq. (
3
) also admits the solution

u(y)=A\sqrt{m}{\rm sn}(y,m)dn(y,m)\,,

(10)

provided

\alpha A^{2}=360\gamma\eta^{4}\,,~~\beta\eta^{2}=10(2m-1)\,,~~v=[11+64m(1-m)]\gamma\eta^{4}\,.

(11)

Solution V

Eq. (
3
) also admits the solution

u(y)=Am{\rm sn}(y,m)cn(y,m)\,,

(12)

provided

\alpha A^{2}=360\gamma\eta^{4}\,,~~\beta\eta^{2}=10(2-m)\,,~~v=[11-64(1-m)]\gamma\eta^{4}\,.

(13)

Solution VI

In the limit
m=1
, the solutions IV and V go over to the hyperbolic
solution

u(y)=A{\rm sech}(y)\tanh(y)\,,

(14)

provided

\alpha A^{2}=360\gamma\eta^{4}\,,~~\beta\eta^{2}=10\gamma\eta^{4}\,,~~v=11\gamma\eta^{4}\,.

(15)

Notice that for the solutions V, VI and IV (in case
m>1/2
),
\alpha,\beta,\gamma,v
have the same sign, i.e. either all are positive or all are negative.

We now show that Eq. (
3
) not only satisfies these 6 solutions but also
admits 10 periodic quadratic superposed solutions and two hyperbolic quadratically
superposed solutions. We first present two real quadratically superposed
periodic solutions and later present 8 complex PT-invariant periodic solutions
and finally the two hyperbolic quadratically superposed complex PT-invariant
solutions.

5
Conclusion

Summarizing, in this paper for the first time one has presented 12 examples of a
nonlinear model (a 5th order KdV-type equation) admitting linear superposition of
quadratic functions as solutions. Out of these, 10 are the real and complex
PT-invariant periodic solutions while the remaining two are examples of complex
PT-invariant hyperbolic solutions. This paper raises several open questions, some
of which are:

1.

In this paper we have presented 5th order KdV-type equation admitting
linear superposition of quadratic functions as solutions. One obvious question
is: are there other nonlinear models both integrable and nonintegrable, discrete
and continuous, local and nonlocal admitting linear superposition of quadratic
functions as solutions? Note that the 5th order KdV-type model we have discussed
is a local, nonintegrable nonlinear equation.

2.

Perhaps even more interesting, are there nonlinear models, local or
nonlocal, discrete or continuous, integrable or nonintegrable which admit linear
superposition of cubic functions as solutions? And more generally, are there
nonlinear models admitting linear superposition of quartic, quintic or more
generally nth order functions as solutions of a nonlinear equation?

3.

Are the 12 quadratically superposed solutions presented in this paper
stable? Hopefully, some of these, specially the two hyperbolic solutions may be
stable. This could be studied variationally or numerically.

4.

It would be very interesting if one could find applications of some of these
quadratically superposed solutions in physical contexts.

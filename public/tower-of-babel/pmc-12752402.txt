Informal caregiver uncertainty: exploring the presence of uncertainty in eating disorder Reddit posts
Authors: Braidyn Lazenby, Mackenzie Pike
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundEating disorders have one of the highest mortality rates of any mental illness. However, individuals experiencing an eating disorder are not the only people affected by their condition; informal caregivers (any individual who has a relationship to an individual with an eating disorder but may or may not be a designated caregiver by title) are also impacted by other’s eating disorders. Using the tripartite model of uncertainty, this study examines the ways that informal caregivers use online forums to express their uncertainty.MethodsA random sample of the r/EatingDisorders subreddit was extracted and posts written by informal caregivers were identified, resulting in 163 posts for analysis. A deductive qualitative analysis was conducted using the tripartite model of uncertainty to identify sources of uncertainty for informal caregivers.ResultsResults concluded that individuals express medical, personal, and social uncertainty online independently, but also experience compounding uncertainty, where the experience of one uncertainty creates tension with and may exacerbate another uncertainty.ConclusionsFuture research should continue to explore the implications of compounding uncertainties to assist in the future creation of educational resources for individuals who may be informal caregivers of people with eating disorders.Keywords: Informal caregiver, Uncertainty, Reddit, Qualitative data

Introduction

Caregivers play an important role in supporting, advocating, and assisting people managing various illnesses [1]. Of caregivers in various healthcare settings, individuals who support people with eating disorders are often overlooked but play an important role within eating disorder care and recovery [2]. Eating disorder recovery can be particularly difficult, as recovery from eating disorders is often described as nonlinear, complicated, and is defined in different ways across clinicians, patients, and scholars [3]. For instance, eating disorders may present differently based on how the individual perceives their eating disorder, such that the eating disorder may present as ego-syntonic, where the person feels as though their eating disorder is consistent with their perception of self, or ego-dystonic, if the person perceives the eating disorder as distinct from or in conflict with the self [4]. Consequently, given the complexity of the eating disorder experience, the role of a caregiver in eating disorder recovery is crucial, as caregivers may provide a variety of support such as assistance with information seeking, financial assistance, and assistance with transitioning from in-patient programs [5].

Although caregivers play a vital role in eating disorder recovery, the challenges of being a caregiver of an individual with an eating disorder are well documented [6]. Unlike other health contexts that may require ongoing and consistent care from a designated caregiver, such as illnesses that require intense physical assistance, many individuals who support someone with an eating disorder are serving as an informal caregiver, defined as an unpaid individual who may provide support to the person with an eating disorder but is not necessarily considered a full-time caregiver with specific intention to support and help the person [7]. For instance, an informal caregiver may include a concerned coworker or friend. Like formal caregivers, informal caregivers may also experience challenges while supporting someone with an eating disorder [8]. However, given the complexity and nonlinear nature of eating disorder recovery, it is likely that the experiences of informal caregivers of individuals with eating disorders are also nuanced and include complex challenges that reflect the difficulty of eating disorder recovery [3].

Given the prevalence of eating disorders and the extent to which informal caregiving occurs in the eating disorder context, recent research has suggested that informal caregiver resources may be a beneficial step in maintaining caregiver well-being and minimizing caregiver uncertainty in the eating disorder context [9–11]. However, resources for informal caregivers are limited and resources for individuals serving as an informal caregiver for an individual with an eating disorder may need to be distributed in unique ways outside of medical settings or formal support groups, as informal caregivers of individuals with eating disorders may not be included in medical visits and decision making [12]. Due to the lack of knowledge surrounding informal caregiver challenges, particularly within the eating disorder context, and the extent to which informal caregivers interact with recovering individuals, it is necessary to understand the sources of uncertainty and distinct challenges to best support and inform future informal caregiver resources for individuals caring for an individual with an eating disorder [13]. The purpose of this study is to: (1) contribute to the growing knowledge of informal caregivers and (2) better understand the sources of uncertainty and nuanced challenges that affect informal caregivers of people with eating disorders.

Review of related literature

The informal caregiver experience

Research has explored how to best support caregivers of people with eating disorders, emphasizing various resources that help caregivers, mainly immediate family members, navigate the nuanced challenges of caring someone with an eating disorder [14]. However, immediate family members who function as formal caregivers are not the only individuals who interact with and support individuals with eating disorders. Often, an eating disorder may go unidentified by some people, as individuals with an eating disorder may not want to disclose their disorder to certain individuals due to fear of stigmatization, forced recovery, or a desire to avoid conflict [15]. Consequently, it is common that the individuals who do know about the eating disorder, or suspect an eating disorder, are not the same people who would be considered formal caregivers. These alternative individuals, described in this study as informal caregivers, are less studied and encompass a wide variety of people that a person may interact with from day to day [8].

According to limited previous work, informal caregivers cover a broad range of people who provide support and assistance to individuals with eating disorders without financial compensation [7, 16]. Notably, this does not include everyone with a relationship to a person with an eating disorder. Rather, informal caregivers must actively seek out opportunities to support and help an individual because of health reasons [17]. The main tasks identified for informal caregivers of eating disorders are often contested in literature but typically include support such as: providing emotional support, encouraging treatment and recovery, creating a supportive environment, and may also include more formal tasks such as helping an individual navigate the healthcare system or keeping track of specific nutritional needs [16, 18]. For instance, one study suggests that informal caregivers of people with eating disorders may help manage stigma around eating disorders and help mitigate family disputes or peer conflict related to the eating disorder [19]. Due to the broad range of tasks, informal caregivers can include people who spend a lot of time with recovering person, such as a significant other or roommate, or people who spend less time with the person such as a coworker or peer at school.

Recovering from an eating disorder is not a linear path [20, 21]. As such, informal caregivers do not always have a defined purpose or may struggle to find a specific purpose in serving as an informal caregiver in a way that feels beneficial [16, 22]. For instance, one scoping review suggests that fear of accommodating eating disorder behaviors too much or enabling a person’s eating disorder is a salient concern for caregivers [11]. Despite the variance in informal caregiver support and caregiver challenges, informal caregivers can play a significant role in eating disorder recovery. Specifically, previous research suggests that individuals that have access to social support from peers and close others have more hope for recovery, feel more validated, and reduced risk of relapse [23–25].

Although the roles of informal caregivers vary, informal caregivers still experience caregiver burden and burnout. For individuals serving as an informal caregiver for someone with an eating disorder, individuals with an eating disorder may not desire treatment and recovery, may feel like they are not “sick enough” for help, and may feel shame related to disclosing relapse or asking for help [20]. Consequently, burnout and caregiver burden for informal caregivers of individuals with eating disorders is often a complicated and may look like frustration that a person is not recovering, concern about what to say or avoid saying, or general uncertainty about how involved to be in this person’s recovery [22, 26–28]. In addition, informal caregivers do not always have direct access to medical information or even specific recovery plans from recovering individuals, depending on how willing the individual is to disclose medical information [20]. Consequently, informal caregivers may engage in information seeking to gain clarity on how to manage their relationship and support their friend, roommate, coworker, or loved one [29]. Research suggests that informal caregivers often turn to online communities to find information, share experiences, and seek advice [30]. Of the online communities available, Reddit serves as a popular destination for informal caregivers to engage in due to its ease of access, anonymity, and large community of peers [31].

Online forums and support seeking

The use of online forums for topics surrounding eating disorders has been widely studied [32–34]. Online forums are not limited to individuals seeking advice for their own health inquiries. Rather, research on caregivers, particularly caregivers of individuals with terminal or chronic illness, has found online support groups to be beneficial for caregiver well-being [35]. Caring for the health of a loved one is extremely taxing, as caregivers often experience both physical and mental burdens associated with their tasks as a caregiver [22]. However, this information largely applies to individuals who are designated caregivers, leaving those who may not be formal caregivers without a designated community to solicit advice. For instance, in a study on fitness center employees, the employees experienced uncertainty and expressed concern about if and how they should intervene in the case of a suspected eating disorder of a fitness center member [36].

Previous research has suggested that individuals may choose a large public online group, such as Reddit, over a specialized online support group due to two main reasons: time and group size [37]. Because many support groups for caregivers are private, informal caregivers may feel uncomfortable providing more information about their relationship and may feel more pressure to contribute to a group that is smaller [37]. In contrast, a larger group size will have less of a time commitment, making group turnover a normal experience and allows users to feel safer utilizing the group when needed and being a passive observer in other situations [38]. A larger, more anonymous, online environment may be especially helpful for caregivers of individuals with eating disorders, as an individual with an eating disorder may request that the information is not shared with other people. In addition, research suggests that many informal caregivers do not self-identify as informal caregivers, making a large group feel safer for those who are unsure of their role or do not self-identify as a caregiver [39]. It is likely that informal caregivers prefer large public online communities, like Reddit, as they provide a more suitable environment for informal caregivers to ask questions, seek advice, and manage uncertainty related to an individual that has, or is suspected of having, an eating disorder without the sustained pressure to commit to a smaller or more private group or self-identify as a caregiver.

Tripartite model of uncertainty

Health uncertainty is defined as the inability to explain or predict certain health-related variables [40]. Individuals can be uncertain of the extent of information they have received, unclear progression and symptoms of the relevant health issue, and ambiguous roles they might play between and within relationships [41]. Scholars have tried to make sense of this uncertainty, particularly through theories such as uncertainty in illness theory [42] and uncertainty management theory [43]. Whereas UIT posits the stressor as the source of uncertainty [42], UMT describes how individuals can appraise uncertainty as positive, negative, or neutral and posits potential strategies for coping, including the decision of whether or not to disclose information [43]. As more individuals utilize online forums, studying how people communicate uncertainty online can help researchers better understand the types of uncertainty that are affecting those who post on online forums.

Built from similar theories of uncertainty such as uncertainty in illness theory [42] and uncertainty management theory [43], the TMU aims to specify the types of uncertainties that are most prevalent during the illness experience. Specifically, TMU began within the context of individuals living with HIV/AIDS, finding that uncertainty was not solely related to the illness itself and included uncertainty about the illness in addition to factors such as personal relationships and identity [43]. This observation led to a formal categorization of uncertainties seen during illness: medical uncertainty, personal uncertainty, and social uncertainty.

Medical uncertainty involves the uncertainty surrounding the medical diagnoses itself [43]. This includes aspects of the illness that elicit uncertainty such as unclear treatment options, grappling with the diagnoses, ambiguity of the care or information, and unpredictable symptoms or illness progression. For instance, someone with a rare form of cancer may experience medical uncertainty as physicians determine the best course of action for treatment. Outside of direct uncertainty through the illness itself, also defines personal uncertainty. Here, personal uncertainty relates to the experiences of financial burdens, embodying a sick or well role, and challenges within identity that may arise [44]. Finally, social uncertainty refers to how the illness creates uncertainty around interpersonal relationships [43]. Specifically, this includes the management and creation of relationships, stigma, and rejection from social circles. Although these categories can be independent of one another, they are not always mutually exclusive. Rather, the uncertainty types can also be seen alongside each other through converging uncertainty. In a particular study, scholars found that converging uncertainty occurred in more than 25% of posts from an online discussion forum about young adults with cancer [41].

Upon the creation of TMU, scholars suggest that TMU could be experienced by more than the individual directly experiencing the health uncertainty and could be expanded to informal caregivers [45]. However, research on the TMU is still scarce and has yet to formally study how informal caregivers express uncertainty through the contexts provided in TMU. From research on pandemic communication, we know that health uncertainty can be present with or without a diagnosis [46, 47]. In addition, we know that uncertainty plays a robust role in the eating disorder experience and recovery [3, 48, 49]. Consequently, it is likely that informal caregivers of people with an eating disorder can experience medical, social, and personal uncertainty as well, and use online forums as a way to express that uncertainty. Similarly, research related to informal caregivers is still limited, with the majority of caregiver research focusing on formal caregivers.

The roles and experiences of informal caregivers of people with eating disorders are not fully understood [7, 16]. However, the prevalence of eating disorders and the role that social support plays in the identification and recovery of individuals with eating disorders suggests that informal caregivers likely play a large role in the lives of people with eating disorders and make up a large portion of an individual’s support network [7, 9, 50]. Given the variety of roles and relationships that are represented in informal caregiving relationships, particularly for people eating disorders, informal caregivers may be challenging to identify and study. Previous research suggests that informal caregivers may turn to public online platforms, such as Reddit, to navigate information and communicate about their experiences [37]. In order to better understand the unique challenges and uncertainties of informal caregivers of people with eating disorders and begin creating resources to support this unique eating disorder caregiving role, understanding how informal caregivers experience uncertainty in the context of eating disorders is a vital next step in supporting these individuals. Consequently, the purpose of this study is to better understand how informal caregivers describe their experience with uncertainty through the tripartite model of uncertainty. Methods

Reddit as data

Reddit, as a public online forum, was chosen as the source of data for the current study. Use of Reddit may vary by the individual; however, the primary goal of Reddit is to function as an online messaging board. This makes Reddit a rich resource for qualitative inquiry, as online forums encourage an emphasis on the individual experience similar to the ways in which qualitative data seeks to enhance the individual and celebrate unique differences [51]. Within Reddit, content is arranged by subreddits, another name for the topics that are the focus of each discussion board. For instance, r/EatingDisorders is a subreddit dedicated to discussions about eating disorders. Reddit users can post content within these subreddits to create a “thread,” or conversation between the original user who posted content and the comments and replies from other users related to that specific thread. These threads are often monitored by a moderator, who evaluates posts to ensure that they meet community guidelines for that specific subreddit.

The utilization of Reddit to conduct research is very common in the study of both eating disorders and informal caregiving due to Reddit’s ability to capture nuanced human experiences for a variety of topics [52–55]. Because posts on Reddit are publicly accessible, a large amount of data can be collected in a short time. Additionally, the use of online forums allows users to create usernames in place of their real name, so that both the researcher and Reddit users are blind to the specific identities of the users [56]. Finally, due to the nature of online platforms, individuals who post on Reddit may experience disinhibition, creating an environment online that is more naturalistic than the environment created by other methods such as interviews or focus groups, which may influence the participant [57]. Although the nature of Reddit prevents researchers from collecting specific demographic information, it should be noted that previous research suggests that Reddit users are more likely to represent non-Western countries and more likely to have more diversity in terms of age and race in comparison to traditional participant-based data collection, although the average age of Reddit users tends to skew younger, with most users under the age of 50 [58, 59].

Procedures

Of the subreddits that discuss eating disorders, r/EatingDisorders was chosen due to its large number of subscribers (over 64,000), high frequency of online activity (i.e. has posts added at least every day), and broad range of topics. The subreddit is open to conversations about eating disorders with the inclusion of four community guidelines that restrict pro-ED comments (comments that encourage eating disordered behavior), numbers (weight, BMI, clothing size, and caloric intake), commercial posts, and the inclusion of external links. This particular subreddit is considered restricted, such that the discussion board itself is publicly accessible, but posts must be approved by a moderator. This is especially beneficial in maintaining participant privacy, as every post goes through the moderator and is then posted by the moderator rather than the individual. Consequently, all posts are considered to be posted by “u/EDPostRequests” rather than the usernames of individual users, removing the possibility that individuals are identified by their username.

After approval from the related institution’s IRB to ensure that this subreddit was publicly available for research, the entire history of the subreddit was scraped, providing 7,455 initial posts. For large Reddit data sets, previous research suggests selecting a random sample to consolidate the data for more efficient coding [60]. To consolidate the data, a random sample of 1,000 posts was selected using IBM SPSS Statistics (Version 27). In order to obtain a data set that was comprised only of posts from individuals who were posting on behalf of or out of concern for another individual, the researcher pulled all individual posts within the subreddit from the random sample where individuals were soliciting advice related to another person or sharing stories of other people. For instance, a post title reading, “HELP: How do I talk to my wife about her ED?” would be included, whereas a post title reading, “HELP: How do I talk to my wife about my ED?” would be excluded. Posts were required to have a clear description of who was posting and their relationship to another person in order to be included. Posts that were vague or did not have a clear explanation of who they were in relation to another person were excluded. This method obtained 163 posts with an average of 273 (SD = 242) words per post. Posts demonstrated a variety of informal caregiver relationships including romantic relationships (n = 79), friends (n = 47), siblings (n = 19), parents (n = 2), children (n = 4), roommates (n = 4), extended family members (n = 3), and “other” (n = 5).

Analysis

In order to analyze the data, a deductive approach to data analysis was utilized to systematically evaluate the use of the TMU within the data [61]. Prior to data analysis, the two authors created a codebook for the TMU together by reviewing recent work related to the TMU [41, 46] and creating definitions and examples related to each uncertainty source in the context in which they may appear within our data set [62]. For instance, we defined medical uncertainty as “uncertainty related to the physical and mental health of another person, including the progression of symptoms, treatment availability, and the role of the person in the affected person’s (person with ED) recovery and wellbeing.” Once the codebook was created, the two authors coded the first 25 posts together, discussing interpretations and discrepancies and making changes to the codebook as needed. After this process was completed, the authors split the remaining data and coded the rest of the posts. Posts that did not express uncertainty or were ambiguous in whether or not they experienced uncertainty were coded as “not specified.”

Results

Results from using the tripartite model of uncertainty to analyze the r/EatingDisorders subreddit provided insights into how informal caregivers with an eating disorder may experience uncertainty. Informal caregivers online expressed uncertainty as outlined by the tripartite model. However, these individuals also experienced compounding uncertainty, where medical, personal, and social uncertainty seemed to interact with one another in tension and exacerbate one another. This tension stemmed from individuals weighing the uncertainties against one another, realizing that managing one uncertainty may make another uncertainty worse or create another uncertainty. Compounding uncertainties

Of the 163 posts within the subreddit that were coded, over one third of the posts contained at least two if not all three uncertainties outlined by the tripartite model. However, these posts did not suggest that the uncertainty was solely occurring at the same time. While these posts expressed multiple uncertainties, individuals expressed compounding uncertainty within their posts, recognizing that managing one facet of their uncertainty might exacerbate another, separate uncertainty. These posts demonstrate how uncertainties can work with and against each other as well. This compounding uncertainty was manifested in two ways: individuals often experienced compounding social and medical uncertainty as well as compounding personal, medical, and social uncertainty.

Compounding medical and social uncertainty

Compounding medical and social uncertainty emerged when informal caregivers were concerned about someone’s medical well-being while also being concerned that intervening on behalf of their health may jeopardize their relationship. Notably, many informal caregivers also have a personal relationship with the individual with an eating disorder, complicating the way in which people navigated caring for the person’s health. Posts were particularly concerned about specific interactions that may make the medical condition better or worse, interactions that often conflicted with how the relationship normally operated. Specifically, posters found their social interactions to be fundamentally constrained due to the person’s eating disorder. For instance, one individual described how their fiancé called off the wedding after a severe period of bulimia. They stated:

I know our relationship is ending, but I can’t leave for fear that something will happen to her and no one will be around to help – the only reason she lived last time is because I was working from home that week and was able to call an ambulance immediately. I’m at the end of my rope, and I feel like I have no one I can talk to without humiliating her. Help?

In this post, the author acknowledged that the relationship is ending but experienced social uncertainty due to being the only one who knows about the condition, fearing that telling someone else may humiliate her. Simultaneously, the author acknowledged their current role as an informal caregiver, knowing that the medical condition is serious and that the individual’s health and life may depend on telling someone else about the condition.

The burden of being the only person to know about an eating disorder in a relationship weighed on informal caregivers on Reddit, particularly when they felt as though they had to choose between the individual’s health or their relationship with that person. Whereas formal caregivers are often in a designated role, informal caregivers experienced blurred lines for where the relationship ends and the caregiving begins. For instance,

My best friend is only 17 years old. She's been bingeing and purging for about 3 years now and I’m one of the few people she’s told about. She’s sworn me to not inform her parents about it and I feel terrible for not doing so because I feel she’s not getting the help she needs. She started to see improvement as of late but has entered a relapse due to school stress and other life issues. I’m at my wit’s end. I can barely sleep at night not knowing if she’s going to be ok or not. I don’t know what to do. I don’t want her to hate me if I attempt to get her the help she needs without her approval, that’s how one of my friendships ended already. She’s afraid, I’m afraid. Advice?

In this example, compounding medical and social uncertainty created a situation where the person is experiencing distress over both the health and medical implications of their friend’s eating disorder as well and the fear of losing another friendship. While it is unclear how old the Reddit user is, this user seems to recognize that formal caregiving may be a more appropriate response to the friend’s eating disorder, leading to Reddit user to reach out to the friend’s parents. Unfortunately, past experience from getting a friend help informed and exacerbated these compounding uncertainties.

Compounding uncertainties also appeared when individuals grew frustrated that their current social norms and tactics for informal caregiving did not help the medical condition. Informal caregivers who tried to be understanding and supportive often faced a roadblock when the eating disorder prevented those social interactions from being meaningful. One post in particular highlighted this tension, describing how their girlfriend has a tendency to lash out during conversations about her eating disorder despite the support they have provided her and a concern about what a long-distance relationships might mean. They stated,

I’ve told her this is bad. I’ve showed her facts, reports, science, everything to prove that I’m not crazy when I tell her that is literally destroying her body, but she doesn't listen. She is restricting to a dangerous point. And just last night, she confessed to me that she purged because she ate more than she usually allows (which was still severely low for a daily intake) What do I do? I love her. I tell her that she’s on a slippery slope to disaster. I support her recovery. I care for her. Nobody else does any of this, and even with my support, I can’t stop her at all from crumbling into an awful mental (and physical) state. And in just two weeks, she goes off to college, and I follow a week after that. If I can’t help her living in the same city, what can I do when we're states apart?

This person described their partner’s eating disorder as destroying her body, largely contributing to the medical uncertainty seen in the post. Because of this medical uncertainty, this individual described struggling to manage their social uncertainty and how they can maintain their role as an informal caregiver for their partner long distance, knowing that they struggle to support their partner while together. In this instance, caring for the partner’s health may jeopardize their relationship, but choosing the relationship may jeopardize the partner’s physical health. Consequently, compounding medical and social uncertainty is demonstrated as a burden to informal caregivers.

Compounding personal, medical, social uncertainty

In addition to compounding medical and social uncertainty, personal and social uncertainty also converged and could also be found interacting with medical uncertainty to converge all three uncertainties. Instances of compounding personal, medical, and social uncertainty, while similar to compounding social and medical uncertainty, were unique in that the individual posting expressed uncertainty regarding their own identity and experience in addition to a conflict with medical and social uncertainty, highlighting the emotional distress and burden of informal caregiving. For instance, one post began by expressing tension between medical and social uncertainty by saying,

Nothing feels built to help this situation. I am terrified that doing nothing will lose me my best friend to deterioration of physical health. I am terrified that doing something will lose me any time I might have if I just keep my mouth shut. I don’t want to lose my best friend. And I feel like both options end up with the same result, albeit in differing ways and timelines.

Similar to the instances of medical and social uncertainty compounding with one another, this poster is concerned that helping their friend with their medical condition may cause them to lose the friendship. However, this was not the only uncertainty at play, as the post continued by expressing personal uncertainty related to instances of death anxiety. The poster continued by saying,

When I first started seeing him again. . . I was able to brush my feelings aside. But lately we've been looking forward to certain games together and his poor health has been triggering my death anxiety and causing me to have issues again, just like when we were teens. I don’t know what to do.

Now, in addition to the already present medical and social uncertainty, this individual described finding that both the uncertainty surrounding the friend’s health and the uncertainty surrounding their interactions with one another created personal uncertainties as well. The role of informal caregiving has led to experiencing personal uncertainty due to death anxiety because of their friend’s medical condition and the relationship they have built together. Consequently, these uncertainties both work together and against one another.

Other examples of compounding uncertainties highlighted the difficulty that people went through when implicitly or explicitly caring for their loved one. One particular example demonstrated the ups and downs of caring for a loved one who was experiencing an eating disorder. After asking what their partner ate that day, this Reddit user regretted bringing up the conversation after the discussion did not go as planned. They said,

I know that that might be controlling in any other relationship but I feel justified when it comes to the ED, am I? … I just want to know if ANYONE ELSE out there feels as helpless as I do towards their suffering SO… I also need help formulating my own thoughts on the matter to be honest, I'm really trying very hard, I know that people will say that there's nothing that i can do to help but I do try continually to support her and her decisions (never for a second taking any of her eating disorder thoughts seriously) but it was my choice to intervene, when she had said earlier that she was feeling fine. So I'm questioning should I have brought it up at all, or just left her to her own (obviously destructive) thoughts... I feel like the eating disorder is purposefully pushing me out. Who knows! I could be wrong and a douchebag, or maybe not phrasing myself correctly here, but I'm just trying to help out.

Here, this informal caregiver was predominately experiencing personal uncertainty, whereas the individual was questioning whether or not they are controlling and a “douchebag.” However, this personal uncertainty conflicted with medical uncertainty, where the individual stated that the eating disorder was pushing the loved one out and that they could not take the eating disorder thoughts seriously. Furthermore, medical uncertainty conflicted with social uncertainty as the poster wondered whether or not they should have intervened as an informal caregiver. Notably, this individual wanted to help but was torn by the severity of the eating disorder (medical), the consequences of intervening (social), and the concern of coming across as controlling (personal).

Worries of control were a common factor within instances of personal uncertainty compounding with other forms. Individuals found themselves engaging in behaviors that contradicted their belief about their self, particularly when symptoms and behaviors of the eating disorder fluctuated. For example, this poster described their significant other’s eating disorder as coming in waves and expresses uncertainty during a particularly bad wave. They said,

Now its back. And once again, i feel like i am completely blowing it from my side. This time its less about the actions, and all about the emotions…I have a craziness now that i NEED to know specifics and details. Its made me do things i would never do before to know the truth. Search belongings to find pills, peek under bathroom doors, smell drains, check garbage bags, etc. and the fact that i am almost ALWAYS right when i have a suspicion, just fuels it even more. But then it heightens my waiting for them to approach me, and makes it all the more hurtful when they dont. It justifies my actions when i have evidence, and increases the depression when its hidden and lied about. But i cant stop… Ive seen where this ends up when its ignored. They are lucky to be alive right now to even BE at this point. I just cant figure out how to co-exist in the same place without going down the tubes emotionally myself. Help?

This poster, although used to the ebb and flow of their partner’s eating disorder decribed experiencing personal uncertainty when determining how they can live with their partner without losing control over their emotions and their own mental health. Again, this personal uncertainty conflicted with the knowledge that their partner’s eating disorder was severe and the hurt that was caused when their partner hid their relapses from them.

By analyzing the ways in which individuals express uncertainty on eating disorder Reddit forums, researchers can have a better understanding of how the types of uncertainty work together and against each other to exacerbate feelings of uncertainty. Although scholars have already found that uncertainties can converge, these results suggest that uncertainties stemming from competing ideas and experiences can compound one another and may conflict as well, creating a useful contribution to our understanding of the tripartite model.

Discussion

This study seeks to understand how informal caregivers of individuals with eating disorders express uncertainty on online forums. Results detail the experience of compounding uncertainties, paving the way for a better understanding of which uncertainties are expressed by informal caregivers of individuals with eating disorders and how the types of uncertainties interact with one another in meaningful ways. These results provide valuable insight into how individuals express uncertainty about illness online, particularly when the individual using the online forums is not experiencing the illness themselves but are not primary caregivers. Contributions to theory as well as related literature will be discussed, followed by recommendations for future studies.

Demographically, this study supports previous research that suggests that informal caregivers can span a broad range of relationships [8]. It is noteworthy that the vast majority of the data we pulled represented a romantic relationship or friendship rather than a direct family member. Although this study did not compare posts across different relationship types, it is likely that friends and some forms of romantic relationships do not have access to medical history or active medical treatment [12]. This lack of information may inform the extent to which these particular informal caregivers seek out information online and experience medical uncertainty, as opposed to a direct family member (such as a parent) who would be more likely to have access to that information.

As the TMU continues to expand, this study suggests that individuals may experience compounding uncertainty in a way that is related to, but distinct from, previous research on converging uncertainty. As previous research surrounding the TMU suggests, individuals may experience converging uncertainty, where two or more uncertainties occur simultaneously or lead to one another [41, 46]. Previous work has suggested the possibility of converging uncertainty. For instance, during the COVID-19 pandemic, researchers explored how individuals expressed uncertainty about the medical risk of contracting COVID-19, which led to the experience and possible exacerbation of social uncertainty when deciding who to spend time with [46]. This study extends the previous suggestion of the presence of compounding uncertainty and further explores its relevance for informal caregivers of people with eating disorders.

Within the data, posts illustrated that uncertainty within one facet of life had a possibility of increasing or causing uncertainty in another part. For instance, within the context of eating disorders, posts frequently described how relationships were difficult to manage when a loved one resisted discussion of treatment or the eating disorder itself, supporting previous research related to the emotional toll and potential frustrations of being an informal caregiver [22, 27]. These posts suggested a lose-lose situation, where bringing up the eating disorder to the individual may help the medical uncertainty, but with the knowledge that it may jeopardize the relationship, causing social uncertainty to compound with medical uncertainty. Additionally, in instances where individuals experienced compounding uncertainty between all three uncertainties, individuals felt as though ignoring the eating disorder and keeping the relationship consistent came with the cost of the loved one’s health as well as uncertainty over their identity as a good partner, loved one, or friend. Consequently, the uncertainties work together to exacerbate the feeling of uncertainty.

Of the salient feature of compounding uncertainty within these data, the complexity of the role of an informal caregiving for someone with an eating disorder is evident. Research suggests that informal caregivers can span a wide variety of people, including friends and romantic partners [13]. While having a close other as an informal caregiver is not necessarily novel to informal caregiving, these Reddit users often portrayed these roles as indistinguishable. Previous work has suggested that informal caregivers may experience uncertainty when determining the roles and responsibilities that fall under caregivers and medical providers [16]. For informal caregivers of people with eating disorders, this uncertainty appears to be more complex and related to the roles and responsibilities of a friend or romantic partner and an informal caregiver.

In the context of eating disorders, these informal caregivers grappled with where their personal relationship with the other person ended and their caregiving task began. This unique meddling of roles has not yet been explored in other contexts of the TMU and may be attributed to the unique features related to eating disorders, particularly the ego-syntonic and ego-dystonic nature of eating disorders. First, eating disorders are often considered ego-syntonic and are deeply tied to an individual’s identity and desire to maintain control [4]. This unique feature often identified in eating disorders may contribute to informal caregiver uncertainty and burden, as engaging in eating disorder behaviors may be seen as a violation of the person’s identity to the informal caregiver, but as affirmation and encouragement to the individual with an eating disorder. Alternatively, individuals with ego-dystonic characteristics may feel disconnected from the self and intense shame, which may contribute to individuals hiding their eating disorder or relapse from an informal caregiver [64]. Both ego-syntonic and ego-dystonic characteristics of eating disorders bring a unique element to informal caregiving in this context. Whereas identity and perceptions, or disconnections, can be deeply interwoven characteristics for people with eating disorders, these cognitive complexities seem to impact informal caregivers in how they perceive their personal relationship and informal caregiving role.

By using Reddit as a data source, this research was able to code for uncertainty as it occurred naturally [60]. However, the purpose of online forums is important to consider when examining why and how these uncertainties may occur as they did. Online forums provide an opportunity for individuals, regardless of health status, to access information in a way that is private, potentially removing feelings of anxiety of felt stigma that may occur during a face-to-face conversation [65]. Notably, many posts mentioned internal conflict related to disclosing the eating disorder to someone in order to get a person help, particularly after telling the individual that they would not disclose their eating disorder. It is possible that compounding uncertainty uniquely emerges within this data set due to the nature of the forum; the privacy that online forums can provide, particularly within a data set such as this one where posts are uploaded via a moderator and hide usernames from viewers, may result in informal caregivers being more inclined to share feelings of compounding uncertainty in a private setting rather than face-to-face with a physician or the individual with the eating disorder [66]. Additionally, the uncertainties provided show a complex picture of what informal caregivers experience when an individual has an eating disorder, likely issues that a healthcare provider or the individual with the eating disorder may not be able to address or provide support for without explicit knowledge. Consequently, although the uncertainties may also be experienced individually or converging still, the research suggests that individuals communicate compounding uncertainty frequently online, given the anonymity and extent of information online.

Strengths and limitations

The amount of data available on Reddit, specifically data related to EDs, is a strength of the study presented. Although quantity does not always indicate quality, this study was fortunate to benefit from access to information that is both robust and publicly available. Additionally, the use of Reddit is a naturalistic form of data collection, whereas the information was posted voluntarily by the individuals, removing the concern of social desirability or priming participants that may be a concern within other research settings. In addition, this specific subreddit anonymized all usernames by having posted listed as the moderator’s username, ensuring user privacy.

Despite these strengths, several weaknesses within the study are present. Although Reddit is an abundant source of data, data may be skewed by the individual topics and community guidelines presented within the site. Not all community guidelines are followed. However, it is important to understand the extent to which those community guidelines may have imposed upon the data to present a data set that, while naturalistic in nature, may be specialized for a particular audience. Second, diversity is an important aspect of research. While the privacy of individuals is imperative and is an added benefit to using Reddit as a data source, the researchers did not have access to important demographics, including individual usernames, prohibiting this study from understanding how certain communities or individual users may use online forums differently, such as people within an older age demographic. Finally, this study did not analyze the progression of posts in real time by coding beyond the initial posts of each individual. It is likely that the posts continued between the Reddit community and the initial post in comment sections, restricting this study from being able to understand how uncertainties appear during active communication and participation in an online forum.

Future opportunities

Based on the findings, this research demonstrates the unique challenges and uncertainties related to being an informal caregiver for an individual with an eating disorder. Notably, this study highlights the interconnected role of being an informal caregiver, as personal relationships and caregiving can become blurred and even contested. In addition, the nuanced characteristics of how eating disorders present in different people –such as through ego-syntonic and ego-dystonic eating disorders– suggests the need to consider the unique features of an individual’s eating disorder to better understand the individual roles and uncertainty an informal caregiver may experience. Previous research has created educational resources geared towards assisting caregivers of people with eating disorders [14]. However, this research suggests an opportunity to conduct more research with the objective of creating educational resources for informal caregivers, specifically individuals who may experience tensions between their role in the relationship and their role as an informal caregiver. Due to the varying nature of how informal caregivers may be involved in the lives of an individual with an eating disorder, an educational resource for informal caregivers in an eating disorder context should accomplish two things: the resource should be easily accessible by informal caregivers seeking out information and should be evidence-based.

First, educational resources for informal caregivers of people with eating disorders should disseminate information and resources where the informal caregivers are already seeking out information. Educational resources that are disseminated through social media and online forums have shown previous success in other studies and may assist in reaching informal caregivers where they are [67, 68]. For instance, Reddit, among other social media platforms, should be the target for distributing information due to fear of disclosing information about the person’s eating disorder without consent and the potential lack of access to medical providers that specialize in eating disorders. Second, there is an opportunity to provide evidence-based resources online through nonprofit organizations, federal agencies, and public health campaigns. For instance, previous studies have had success creating communication guides in partnership with public health campaigns for individuals facing loneliness during the pandemic [68] and communication guides in partnership with nonprofits for people with specific health needs, such as people with ostomies [69]. Evidence-based materials for individuals serving as informal caregivers for people with eating disorders should provide medically accurate information that emphasizes the cognitive complexities that impact the interpersonal relationships and health decision making of people with eating disorders. By providing more information and coping strategies related to these complex challenges of eating disorder recovery, informal caregivers of individuals with eating disorders can be more prepared and informed.

Conclusion

As researchers continue to explore the experience of caregivers, it is important that scholars emphasize the role that informal caregivers play in the lives of people with eating disorders. By studying uncertainty expressed by informal caregivers in eating disorder contexts, researchers can better understand the unique challenges facing these individuals. This study suggests that compounding uncertainties are burdens that informal caregivers of people with eating disorders may experience, where compounding uncertainties describe how uncertainties work against each other and compound the feeling of uncertainty, making decision making and uncertainty management difficult. In addition, these compounding uncertainties make relationship management difficult, as the role of an informal caregiver and relationship partner may be ambiguous. As research on the tripartite model of uncertainty continues, it will be important to study compounding uncertainty in other contexts such as within those who are directly experiencing illness as well as communities outside of eating disorders such as substance abuse and other mental health spaces.

On modified Mittag–Leffler coupled hybrid fractional system constrained by Dhage hybrid fixed point in Banach algebra
Authors: Mohammed A Almalahi, Khaled Aldwoah, Faez Alqarni, Manel Hleili, Kamal Shah, Fathea M O Birkea
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis research investigates the dynamics of nonlinear coupled hybrid systems using a modified Mittag–Leffler fractional derivative. The primary objective is to establish criteria for the existence and uniqueness of solutions through the implementation of Dhage’s hybrid fixed-point theorem. The study further analyzes the stability of the proposed model. To demonstrate the practical application of this framework, we utilize a modified Mittag–Leffler operator to model the transmission of the Ebola virus, known for its complex and diverse dynamics. The analysis is conducted using a combination of theoretical and numerical methods, including transforming the system of equations into an equivalent integral form, applying the fixed-point theorem, and developing a numerical scheme based on Lagrange’s interpolation for simulating the Ebola virus model. This study aims to enhance our understanding of Ebola virus dynamics and provide valuable insights for developing effective control strategies.Keywords: Modified Mittag–Leffler-type derivative, Coupled hybrid systems, Existence and uniqueness, Stability analysis, Dhage’s hybrid fixed point theorem, Ebola virus model, SimulationSubject terms: Applied mathematics, Pure mathematics, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Fractional calculus has gained increasing attention in recent years for its ability to model and analyze diverse real-world phenomena effectively. Unlike traditional integer-order calculus, fractional calculus extends to non-integer orders, providing a more precise description of systems exhibiting anomalous diffusion, long-term memory, and fractional-order dynamics. Such systems are found in various fields, including physics, biology, finance, signal processing, and control theory1–3. Fractional calculus empowers engineers and scientists to gain a deeper understanding of complex systems, leading to advancements in design, control, and optimization (refer to4–9 for further details). Rashid et al.10–13 have made notable contributions to fractional calculus and its applications, particularly those involving non-singular kernels. They utilized fixed point theory to prove the existence and uniqueness of solutions for fractional differential equations (FDEs), including coupled systems, and establish inequalities for analyzing solution properties. Their work provides tools for understanding complex systems with memory effects, applicable to various fields.

Within the realm of fractional calculus, a notable example is the Mittag–Leffler fractional derivative14, which has proven to be a robust tool for accurately modeling and analyzing complex real-world systems, particularly those exhibiting complex nonlinear behavior. One limitation of early ABC operators was the requirement for an initialization condition, imposing an additional constraint on problem formulation. However, in 2022, Al-Refai and Baleanu15 successfully expanded these operators, eliminating this limitation. Subsequently, modified versions of these operators have been widely employed in numerous research articles.

The modified Mittag–Leffler fractional operator introduced by Al-Refai and Baleanu is particularly significant due to its unique feature of possessing an integrable singularity at the origin, setting it apart from other fractional derivatives. This distinctive characteristic enables the discovery of novel solutions for FDEs. Moreover, it provides a powerful tool for solving FDEs that were previously intractable using the standard ABC fractional derivative. For instance, homogeneous FDEs with modified Mittag–Leffler fractional derivatives possess non-zero solutions, and certain linear fractional equations can be solved without requiring additional conditions. This versatility has led to its widespread adoption across various disciplines, proving effective in the analysis, control, and modeling of intricate phenomena17–19.

Hybrid FDEs are particularly useful for modeling dynamic systems that exhibit both continuous and discrete dynamics. This unique characteristic makes them ideal for representing complex systems with mixed dynamics, leading to more accurate and realistic models in fields like physics, engineering, biology, and finance20. Hybrid fixed point theory, as developed by Dhage21,22, plays a crucial role in the analysis of HFDEs. This theory provides a powerful framework for establishing the existence and uniqueness of solutions for nonlinear perturbed differential equations and functional integral equations. Dhage and Lakshmikantham23 further expanded this theory, providing fundamental results and analysis on hybrid differential equations, contributing significantly to the understanding of this important class of mathematical models. Additional research has expanded our understanding of hybrid FDEs and their properties24–29.

The coupled systems of hybrid FDEs play a crucial role in comprehending the dynamics of diverse real-world phenomena and constructing precise models that find application across multiple disciplines. Researchers have examined these coupled systems using various types of fractional derivatives. For example, Mali et al.38 investigated solution properties for a coupled hybrid system under -Hilfer fractional derivative. Boutiara et al.39 considered a coupled hybrid system involving -Caputo fractional operators and studied the Hyers–Ulam stability. Ji et al.40 investigated solutions for a coupled hybrid system with the Riesz-Caputo derivative. Khan et al.30 examined the existence of solutions and introduced a numerical method for a generalized hybrid category of n-coupled with a modified ABC fractional derivative. These equations have proven valuable in capturing the behavior of complex systems, and numerous studies have focused on investigating their existence and properties across various fields31–37.

Motivated by these previous studies, this research aims to expand upon the work of39,41 by incorporating a new fractional operator, namely a modified Mittag–Leffler fractional operator. Additionally, a numerical approach is introduced for solving the coupled system of hybrid FDEs through the utilization of Lagrange’s interpolation method. To illustrate the practical application of this newly proposed operator, a numerical scheme is developed for simulating the Ebola virus disease model, discussed further in Sect. 5 of this paper. While our primary focus is on understanding the dynamics of coupled hybrid systems using a MABC fractional derivative and applying it to model Ebola virus transmission, proving the existence of solutions is essential for validating the model’s mathematical soundness. The existence theory provides a crucial foundation for our analysis, justifying the development of our numerical scheme and ensuring the real-world relevance of our results. Without establishing the existence of solutions, our model could be mathematically inconsistent, leading to nonsensical predictions and undermining the credibility of our findings. Furthermore, the existence theory paves the way for further investigations into the stability and dynamics of the modeled system.

The observed behaviors and patterns in the compartments of the proposed model, as illustrated in the graphs, carry significant implications for understanding the dynamics of the studied biological system. The decline in the population density of the susceptible class with different fractional orders reflects the decreasing number of individuals who are susceptible to the disease over time. This decline may be attributed to various factors, such as the implementation of preventive measures, vaccination campaigns, or natural immunity development within the population. The initial rise followed by stabilization of the exposed class indicates the progression of individuals from the susceptible class to the exposed class, where they are infected but not yet infectious. This rise could be associated with the incubation period of the disease, during which individuals are infected but not showing symptoms. The subsequent stabilization suggests a balance between new infections and the recovery of individuals from the exposed class. The increasing trend initially observed in the infection class represents the spread of the disease within the population. As more individuals transition from the exposed class to the infection class, the number of infected individuals rises. However, as the disease progresses and control measures are implemented, the rate of new infections may decrease, leading to a stabilization of the infection class. The rising trend followed by stabilization in the hospitalization recovered class indicates the number of individuals who have recovered from the infection and have been discharged from hospitals or healthcare facilities. The stabilization suggests that the healthcare system is effectively managing the cases, with a relatively stable rate of recoveries over time. Similarly, the rising and stabilizing behaviors observed in the death class represents the number of individuals who have succumbed to the disease. The rise in the death class reflects the mortality associated with the infection, while the stabilization may indicate effective healthcare interventions, improved treatments, or the implementation of preventive measures that reduce the fatality rate.

Conclusion

This research has investigated the dynamics of nonlinear coupled hybrid systems using the MABC fractional operators. We have improved the conditions for the existence and uniqueness of solutions, as well as the stability analysis of the coupled hybrid model, through the application of Dhage’s hybrid fixed point theorem. The analysis suggests that several strategies are necessary to limit the spread of Ebola. These strategies include vaccination campaigns, strengthening early detection and isolation measures, implementing rigorous infection control practices, ensuring adequate healthcare infrastructure and resources, providing timely treatment and supportive care, promoting safe burial practices, conducting public awareness and education campaigns, establishing robust surveillance systems, and fostering international collaboration. The overarching goal of these strategies is to reduce transmission, protect vulnerable populations, provide appropriate care, and promote community engagement in preventive measures to effectively respond to Ebola outbreaks. This research underscores the significance of modified ABC fractional operators in simulating the virus, showcasing the superiority of this operator in accurately representing the Ebola virus.

In future research, we will focus on refining optimal control models for the Ebola virus, taking into account the complex dynamics identified in this study, and optimizing control strategies to minimize outbreak impact and resource allocation. Also, we plan to extend these methods and insights to model other infectious diseases exhibiting similar complex behavior, such as HIV/AIDS or dengue fever. These ongoing efforts aim to contribute to the advancement of fractional calculus and the development of more sophisticated and effective models for infectious diseases, ultimately improving our ability to predict and manage outbreaks.

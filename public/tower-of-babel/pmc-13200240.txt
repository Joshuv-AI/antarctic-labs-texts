Total Chemical Synthesis of the Human Milk Oligosaccharide Disialyllacto‑N‑tetraose and Its Degradation by Bifidobacterium bifidum Sialidases
Authors: Maiko Tanase, Aruto Nakajima, Naoko Komura, Hidenori Tanaka, Hiromune Ando, Hideharu Ishida, Takane Katayama, Toshihiko Katoh, Akihiro Imamura
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Disialyllacto-N-tetraose (DSLNT), a human milk oligosaccharide implicated in neonatal immunity and necrotizing enterocolitis prevention, contains both Neu5Acα(2→3)­Gal and Neu5Acα(2→6)­GlcNAc motifs. Its synthesis is hindered by challenging α-sialylation. Here we report the total chemical synthesis of DSLNT via simultaneous double sialylation using macrobicyclic donors, achieving complete stereocontrol. A convergent [4 + 2] glycosylation enabled efficient assembly. The route furnished ∼50 mg of free-reducing DSLNT, enabling enzymatic studies that revealed preferential cleavage of α­(2→3)-linkage by Bifidobacterium bifidum sialidase SiaBb2.

Human milk oligosaccharides (HMOs) are the third most abundant solid component of human milk and constitute one of the most structurally diverse families of naturally occurring glycans. , More than 200 distinct structures have been identified, many of which exhibit elaborate patterns of branching, fucosylation, and sialylation. These unconjugated glycans are increasingly recognized as key modulators of neonatal health, contributing to the establishment of gut microbiota, protection against pathogens, and immune system development. − Their structural complexity determines biological specificity; subtle changes in linkage position or glycan sequence can profoundly alter microbiota composition and host–microbe interactions. However, despite growing biological interest, the molecular basis of HMO function remains incompletely understood.

A major barrier to mechanistic elucidation is the limited availability of structurally homogeneous and precisely defined HMOs. Fermentation and enzymatic approaches have enabled scalable production of a few structurally simple HMOs, such as 2′-fucosyllactose, 3-fucosyllactose, and 3′-sialyllactose. While biosynthetic platforms provide a powerful means of producing selected targets, they are inherently constrained by enzyme specificity, pathway compatibility, and limited structural plasticity. Precise regiochemical control, selective modification of individual linkages, and systematic analogue generation remain challenging in biological systems. In contrast, chemical synthesis offers a fundamentally distinct paradigm. It provides complete control over stereochemistry, linkage connectivity, and late-stage functionalization, enabling access to structurally complex HMOs that are difficult to obtain via biosynthesis. Such access is essential for rigorous structure–activity relationship studies, elucidating glycan–lectin recognition mechanisms, and generating non-natural analogues for probing biological functions beyond the constraints of native biosynthetic pathways. In this context, chemical synthesis of HMOs is not merely a complementary production method but a strategic platform for both elucidating and expanding the functional HMO repertoire.

Among HMOs, disialyllacto-N-tetraose (DSLNT, Figure ) has attracted considerable attention as an anti-inflammatory therapeutic candidate because of its potential role in protection against necrotizing enterocolitis (NEC), a life-threatening inflammatory disease in preterm infants. , DSLNT may also serve as a prebiotic substrate in infant gut, functioning as a nutrient for specific bacteria such as bifidobacteria. Additionally, it possesses the biologically decisive Neu5Acα(2→6)­GlcNAc motif, which plays a critical role in glycan–protein recognition, including interactions with sialic-acid-binding immunoglobulin-like lectins (Siglecs), and is a key structural determinant in immune modulation. , Despite its biological importance, access to homogeneous DSLNT is extremely limited. Only a small-scale chemoenzymatic preparation (1.9 mg) has been described, and no total chemical synthesis has been reported. The structural complexity of DSLNT, a hexasaccharide composed of a lacto-N-tetraose core bearing two sialic acid (Neu5Ac) residues (Figure ), poses a formidable synthetic challenge because the stereocontrolled construction of α-sialyl linkages is among the most demanding transformations in carbohydrate chemistry.

Herein, we achieved the first total chemical synthesis of DSLNT by employing a macrobicyclic sialic acid donor to construct two α-sialyl linkages simultaneously with full stereocontrol. We envision that this stereocontrolled total synthesis method would serve not only as the first chemical access to DSLNT but also as a demonstration that complex, multiple-sialylated glycans can be assembled in a convergent and programmable manner. The availability of homogeneous DSLNT further enabled the first investigation of its susceptibility to enzymatic hydrolysis using two sialidases, SiaBb1 and SiaBb2, from Bifidobacterium bifidum, a probiotic with a high capacity for degrading host-derived glycans, including HMOs and mucin O-glycans. , Such analysis provides molecular insight into its potential metabolic fate in infant gut, which would serve as the basis for its development as an anti-inflammatory agent.

Access to DSLNT in its native free reducing form was considered essential from the outset. Preserving the reducing terminus ensures native conformational flexibility and authentic protein recognition properties, as artificial linkers can perturb glycan–protein interactions or interfere with structural studies. Moreover, certain glycan-binding proteins directly interact with the reducing end, rendering unmodified glycans indispensable for biologically meaningful investigations. Consequently, our synthetic design prioritized a global deprotection strategy that would furnish DSLNT with an unmodified reducing end while preventing C2 epimerization. Retrosynthetic analysis of DSLNT is shown in Figure . The central challenge to the synthesis of DSLNT lies in the stereocontrolled construction of α(2→3) and α(2→6) linkages within a single molecule. Traditional sialyl donors frequently suffer from low stereocontrol and side reactions, particularly in oligosaccharide synthesis. Thus, we employed our previously developed macrobicyclic sialic acid donor, which enforces α-selectivity through conformational constraint. This strategy enabled the construction of both Neu5Acα(2→3)­Gal and Neu5Acα(2→6)­GlcNAc linkages in a single step, leading to the double sialylation of the lacto-N-biose (LNB) [Galβ(1→3)­GlcNAc] acceptor. Late-stage coupling of the resulting disialylated tetrasaccharide donor and a Lac acceptor through convergent [4 + 2] β-glycosylation led to the efficient assembly of DSLNT, overcoming steric congestion and diminished donor reactivity intrinsic to complex sialylated intermediates.

The synthesis commenced with the synthesis of the Lac acceptor from commercially available monosaccharides (Scheme A). Commercially available β-D-Glc pentaacetate 1 was readily converted into the 4-OH Glc acceptor 2 in five steps according to the literature. , The benzyl group at the anomeric position was removed by hydrogenolysis under neutral conditions in the final deprotection step. Compound 2 was coupled with commercially available Gal thioglycoside 3 in the presence of NIS and TfOH , in CH2Cl2 at – 40 °C, affording Lac derivative 4 in a β-selective manner in 98% yield. Compound 4 was then converted into suitably differentiated acceptor 5 in five steps for late-stage coupling (Scheme A; see Supporting Information for full details). In parallel, the LNB acceptor 10 was strategically designed as a lightly protected disaccharide bearing four free hydroxyl groups to enable subsequent double sialylation. Incorporation of lipophilic p-tert-butylbenzyl (TBBn) groups ensured solubility under low-temperature conditions, while a tailored acetal protection strategy allowed simultaneous unveiling of the GlcN 4,6- and Gal 3,4-hydroxyl groups, providing rapid access to the reactive tetraol acceptor.

The synthesis of 10 commenced from commercially available monosaccharides (Scheme B). Commercially available β-D-Gal pentaacetate 6 was treated with 3-(dimethylamino)-1-propylamine (DMAPA) in CH2Cl2. Subsequent addition of CCl3CN and DBU to the reaction mixture afforded Gal donor 7 in a one-pot procedure. Coupling of 7 with commercially available GlcN acceptor 8 in the presence of TMSOTf in CH2Cl2 at – 40 °C produced LNB disaccharide 9 in 97% yield. Compound 9 was then converted into the lightly protected LNB acceptor 10 in seven steps, rendering it ready for sialylation (Scheme B; see Supporting Information for full details).

After establishing the acceptor design, the next step was addressing the central challenge of this synthesis: the simultaneous construction of two α-sialic acid linkages in a single step. According to the literature, ,, two macrobicyclic sialic acid donors, thioglycoside donor 12 and phosphate donor 13, were efficiently synthesized from Neu5Ac (Scheme C). Initially, thioglycoside donor 12 was used in the sialylation of LNB acceptor 10 in the presence of NIS and TfOH in CH2Cl2 at – 80 °C (Scheme D). While the desired tetrasaccharide bearing two α-sialic acid linkages (14) was obtained in 44% yield, an unexpected byproduct (15) was also formed. 1H NMR spectroscopy and mass spectrometry revealed that 15 is a tetrasaccharide bearing two SPh groups appended to a p-methoxyphenyl (MP) moiety. Such SPh adduct formation on electron-rich sites has occasionally been observed during the glycosylation of thioglycoside donors promoted by NIS/TfOH. − To prevent this side reaction, phosphate donor 13 was used instead of 12. Compound 13 cleanly delivered the desired disialylated tetrasaccharide 14 in 96% yield, achieving simultaneous formation of the Neu5Acα(2→3)­Gal and Neu5Acα(2→6)­GlcNAc linkages with complete stereoselectivity. This result demonstrates the power of conformationally restricted donors in constructing complex sialylated molecules and underscores the success of using a lightly protected acceptor to facilitate simultaneous double sialylation. Subsequent formation of 16 via acetylation confirmed the sialylated positions. Selective removal of the MP group with CAN, followed by introduction of a trichloroacetimidate leaving group, afforded tetrasaccharide donor 17 in 74% yield in two steps.

With tetrasaccharide donor 17 and Lac acceptor 5 in hand, we proceeded with the final step of the synthesis: the convergent assembly of the densely functionalized hexasaccharide framework. Convergent [4 + 2] glycosylation between the two fragments was performed using TMSOTf in CH2Cl2 at 0 °C, affording the desired hexasaccharide 18 in 65% yield. The stereochemistry of the newly formed glycosidic linkage was unambiguously confirmed by 1H NMR spectroscopy in CD3NO2 at 90 °C (δ 4.98, J 1,2 = 7.5 Hz). Notably, this convergent coupling proceeded efficiently despite the pronounced steric congestion and electronic complexity inherent to the tetrasaccharide donor with macrobicyclic sialic acids, thereby validating the robustness of the overall synthetic design. Subsequent C5-chemoselective ring opening of both sialic acid residues with zinc and AcOH, followed by C5 acetylation with Ac2O and DMAP in pyridine, furnished 19 in 71% yield in two steps. Global saponification with 1 M aqueous NaOH easily removed the O-acetyl groups and C1 ester functionalities of the sialic acid residues, providing 20 as a sodium salt in 95% yield. Finally, careful hydrogenolysis of all benzyl groups under neutral conditions (H2, Pd/C in EtOH–H2O), which was essential to prevent C2 epimerization of the reducing-end glucose residue, delivered the target DSLNT (42.4 mg) in 96% yield. Collectively, these results validate the overall strategy and establish a concise and efficient total synthesis of DSLNT.

While DSLNT is thought to be metabolized by enzymes produced by gut microbiota, the identities of the responsible bacterial species and specific enzymes mediating its degradation are largely unknown. Therefore, with synthetic DSLNT in hand, we examined its susceptibility to enzymatic hydrolysis using two sialidases, SiaBb1 and SiaBb2, from B. bifidum (Figure ). Purified recombinant enzymes normalized to 200 μU activity toward 4MU-α-Neu5Ac were individually incubated with synthetic DSLNT (100 μm), and substrate consumption and product formation were measured over the course of the reaction (120 min). Overall, DSLNT was more susceptible to SiaBb2 than SiaBb1. Approximately 90 μm of DSLNT was hydrolyzed by SiaBb2 by the end of incubation, yielding lacto-N-tetraose (LNT) (∼35 μm), sialyllacto-N-tetraose b (LSTb) (∼50 μm), and Neu5Ac (∼110 μm). By contrast, only 20 μm of DSLNT was hydrolyzed by SiaBb1, producing sialyllacto-N-tetraose a (LSTa) (∼10 μm), LSTb (∼10 μm), and Neu5Ac (∼20 μm). LSTa was not detected in the former reaction, whereas LNT was not detected in the latter. In both cases, the total amount of released Neu5Ac closely matched the theoretical value calculated based on the amounts of LNT, LSTa, and/or LSTb produced. No apparent spontaneous degradation of DSLNT was observed. These results align with those of our previous study on sialyloligosaccharide degradation, which demonstrated that SiaBb2 preferentially cleaves the α(2→3)-linkage over the α(2→6)-linkage. Collectively, these results suggest that SiaBb2 plays a major role in DSLNT degradation by B. bifidum. Desialylation of HMOs bearing a Neu5Acα(2→6)­GlcNAc motif, such as DSLNT, requires gut microbial enzymes with distinct linkage specificities, indicating a complex metabolic pathway in infant gut that may influence the preventive effect of DSLNT against NEC.

In conclusion, we successfully achieved the first total chemical synthesis of DSLNT, a structurally unique disialylated HMO containing both Neu5Acα(2→3)­Gal and Neu5Acα(2→6)­GlcNAc motifs. A strategic route featuring fully stereocontrolled construction of both α-linkages using a macrobicyclic sialyl donor, followed by convergent [4 + 2] glycosylation and epimerization-free global deprotection, furnished preparative quantities of homogeneous DSLNT in its native free reducing form. Preserving the reducing end enables biologically authentic conformational and recognition properties, thereby ensuring suitability for mechanistic and structural studies. In addition to this synthetic accomplishment, enzymatic studies revealed the linkage-specific degradation of DSLNT by B. bifidum sialidases, reflecting its complex metabolic trajectory in infant gut. This work provides a scalable platform for accessing complex sialylated HMOs and establishes a molecular foundation for investigating DSLNT as a candidate anti-inflammatory agent in NEC.

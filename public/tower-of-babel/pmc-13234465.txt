Concordance of pathologic response assessment between local and central review in melanoma: a post hoc analysis of KEYMAKER-U02 substudy 02C
Authors: M Tetzlaff, JM Taube, GV Long, R Dummer, C Robert, AM Menzies, A Hill, C Gaudy-Marqueste, DC Portnoy, C Lebbe, R Krishnan, J Xu, M Fukunaga-Kalabis, C Krepler, RA Scolyer
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundSubstudy 02C of the phase I-II KEYMAKER-U02 study was designed to evaluate neoadjuvant pembrolizumab as monotherapy and in combination with investigational agents, followed by surgery and adjuvant pembrolizumab in participants with resectable stage IIIB-D melanoma. Here, we report a post hoc analysis of concordance of pathologic response assessment between local and central review and among central reviewers in the KEYMAKER-U02 substudy 02C.Patients and methodsParticipants received neoadjuvant pembrolizumab plus vibostolimab, pembrolizumab plus gebasaxturev, or pembrolizumab monotherapy followed by resection and adjuvant pembrolizumab. Pathologic response was categorized by percent age of residual viable tumor (%RVT) in the treated tumor bed as follows: pathologic complete response (pCR; 0% RVT), near-pCR (RVT >0% but ≤10%), partial pathologic response (pPR; RVT >10% but ≤50%), and pathologic nonresponse (pNR; RVT >50%).ResultsBy local review of 60 participants, 24 (40%) had pCR, 7 (12%) had near-pCR, 12 (20%) had pPR, and 17 (28%) had pNR. By central review of 60 participants, 23 (38%) had pCR, 7 (12%) had near-pCR, 14 (23%) had pPR, and 16 (27%) had pNR. The weighted kappa for concordance between local and central review for these categories was 0.79 (95% CI 0.70-0.89). The three central reviewers agreed on the response category for 54 of 61 participants (89%). For the seven participants (11%) without agreement, the maximum difference in response was one category; for four of these participants, the dissenting response assessment was close to a %RVT category cut point. The weighted Gwet’s agreement coefficient was 0.94 (95% CI 0.90-0.98).ConclusionThese results confirm a high level of concordance in pathologic response assessment between local and central review and among central reviewers and provide confidence in the accuracy of pathologic response assessment following neoadjuvant therapy.Key words: central review, local review, melanoma, neoadjuvant, pathologic response, pembrolizumab

Introduction

Until recently, standard-of-care treatment for surgically resectable stage III melanoma has been surgical resection followed by adjuvant immune checkpoint inhibitors or BRAF-targeted therapy.1,2 Programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) inhibitors have shown clinical benefit in the adjuvant setting in several randomized clinical studies of melanoma.3, 4, 5, 6 More recently, a variety of PD-1 inhibitor–based regimens have shown efficacy and manageable safety in the neoadjuvant setting.7, 8, 9, 10, 11 The results of the recently completed SWOG1801 and Neoadjuvant Ipilimumab plus Nivolumab versus Standard Adjuvant Nivolumab in Macroscopic Stage III Melanoma (NADINA) trials have demonstrated improved patient outcomes with the addition of neoadjuvant immune checkpoint inhibitors to adjuvant PD-1 inhibitor–based therapy in this high-risk patient group, establishing neoadjuvant immunotherapy as the new standard of care for patients with clinically detectable and surgically resectable stage III melanoma.10,12

Pathologic assessment of resected tumors following neoadjuvant therapy allows for early evaluation of treatment efficacy.13,14 In this regard, the extent of pathologic response represents an emerging surrogate endpoint for recurrence-free and overall survival that can be leveraged to guide management decisions after surgery and can further expedite clinical trial reporting, implementation of new treatment strategies, and drug approvals, thereby facilitating earlier patient access to innovative strategies.14, 15, 16 In early clinical trials of melanoma, pathologic response assessment was typically performed by blinded independent central review in large academic centers. However, as neoadjuvant immunotherapy emerges as standard of care, there is a critical need to determine the feasibility and accuracy of pathologic response assessment by local pathology laboratories in comparison to central review.

Substudy 02C of the open-label, phase I-II, adaptive-design KEYMAKER-U02 study (registered at ClinicalTrials.gov as NCT04303169) evaluated neoadjuvant pembrolizumab as monotherapy and in combination with investigational agents, followed by surgery and adjuvant pembrolizumab monotherapy in participants with resectable stage IIIB-D melanoma. We report a post hoc analysis of concordance of pathologic response between local and central review and interobserver concordance among central reviewers in KEYMAKER-U02 substudy 02C.

Patients and methods

Study design and participants

Participants were enrolled at 29 global sites. The study design and detailed eligibility criteria have been previously described.17 Briefly, participants were randomly assigned across six open investigational treatment arms. The current analysis includes data from the first three arms, as data for arms four to six were assessed by different central reviewers. In the neoadjuvant phase, participants received pembrolizumab 200 mg intravenously every 3 weeks for up to two administrations plus vibostolimab 200 mg intravenously every 3 weeks for up to two administrations (pembrolizumab plus vibostolimab arm), pembrolizumab 400 mg intravenously for one administration plus gebasaxturev at a dose of 3 × 108 tissue culture infectious dose 50% by intratumoral injection for up to five administrations (pembrolizumab plus gebasaxturev arm), or pembrolizumab 400 mg intravenously for one administration (pembrolizumab monotherapy arm). Surgical resection was performed 6 weeks after the first dose of neoadjuvant therapy and was followed by adjuvant treatment with pembrolizumab 400 mg intravenously every 6 weeks for up to eight administrations or until disease recurrence, unacceptable toxicity, or participant or investigator decision to discontinue.

The study was conducted in accordance with principles of Good Clinical Practice and was approved by the appropriate institutional review boards and regulatory agencies. All participants provided written informed consent.

Outcomes

Pathologic response assessment was performed according to the tissue submission and scoring guidelines proposed by the International Neoadjuvant Melanoma Consortium (INMC).18 Pathologic response was categorized by the percentage of residual viable tumor (RVT) in the treated tumor bed, which was defined as the area of the tissue occupied by viable tumor, necrosis, or tumoral regression (fibrosis/fibroinflammatory stroma with foamy macrophages/melanophages, lymphocytes, plasma cells, and neovascularization). Four mutually exclusive response categories were used: pathologic complete response (pCR; 0% RVT), near-pCR (RVT >0% but ≤10%), partial pathologic response (pPR; RVT >10% but ≤50%), and pathologic nonresponse (pNR; RVT >50%).14,18 A fifth category of major pathologic response (MPR) comprised pCR and near-pCR. Local pathologists were trained on the reporting criteria and completed the same pathologic response assessment as central reviewers. A guidance document for the determination of pathologic response was developed in strict alignment with INMC guidelines and provided to all participating local pathologists prior to study activation. A centralized training session was conducted that covered the reporting criteria, specimen handling, mapping, and documentation standards. Ongoing support and clarifications were available throughout the study period. Sites were required to submit representative slides from all blocks prepared and evaluated locally. Central review was then performed on all submitted slides. If there was discordance among central reviewers, a consensus review by the three central reviewers was performed for final response categorization.

Statistical analysis

The analysis of concordance between local and central review included participants for whom response assessments were available from both the local and central review. The analysis of concordance among central reviewers included participants for whom response assessments were available from all three central reviewers. Concordance between local and central review of pathologic response was analyzed using a weighted kappa with Cicchetti-Allison weight to give disagreement in closer categories partial credit. Interobserver concordance among the three central reviewers was assessed using a weighted Gwet’s agreement coefficient with a linear weight.

Results

As of the data cutoff date (24 January 2024), 66 participants were enrolled and assigned to the pembrolizumab plus vibostolimab arm (n = 26), pembrolizumab plus gebasaxturev arm (n = 25), or pembrolizumab monotherapy arm (n = 15). Median time from randomization to the data cutoff date was 29.9 months (range, 25.9-42.5) in the pembrolizumab plus vibostolimab arm, 29.7 months (24.5-40.1) in the pembrolizumab plus gebasaxturev arm, and 34.3 months (29.6-41.5) in the pembrolizumab monotherapy arm.

Of the 66 participants enrolled, concordance between local and central review was evaluable for 60 participants (pembrolizumab plus vibostolimab, n = 23; pembrolizumab plus gebasaxturev, n = 23; pembrolizumab monotherapy, n = 14). Of the six participants who were excluded, four did not undergo surgery, one was missing a local assessment, and one participant’s response was assessed as not evaluable by both local and central pathologists due to the absence of a tumor bed in the resected specimen. By local review, 24 participants (40%) had pCR, seven (12%) had near-pCR, 12 (20%) had pPR, and 17 (28%) had pNR (Table 1). By contrast, central review determined 23 participants (38%) had pCR, seven (12%) had near-pCR, 14 (23%) had pPR, and 16 (27%) had pNR (Table 1). Discordance between local and central review assessment was observed in 15 cases: two pCRs by central review were categorized as near-pCR by local review; three near-pCRs by central review were categorized as either pCR (n = 2) or pNR (n = 1) by local review; six pPRs by central review were categorized as either pCR (n = 1), near-pCR (n = 1), or pNR (n = 4) by local review; and four pNRs by central review were categorized as pPR by local review (Figure 1). The weighted kappa for concordance was 0.79 (95% CI 0.70-0.89) (Figure 1). The percentage of RVT was generally similar when assessed by local and central review (Figure 2).

Discussion

The results of this post hoc analysis showed high concordance of pathologic response assessment between local and central review and among central reviewers in KEYMAKER-U02 substudy 02C. Concordance was high between local and central review across all pathologic response assessment categories, including MPR and pCR, with a weighted kappa for concordance of 0.79 (95% CI 0.70-0.89). This was consistent with the cross tabulation of responses by local and central review, given that most counts in the cumulative frequency plot were on the diagonal cells indicating that local and central review were in agreement. Insofar as subsequent treatment decisions in the postsurgical setting are determined by pathologic response, the most meaningful discrepancies in this analysis included a single case designated as near-pCR by central review that was considered a pNR by local review, and two cases designated as pPR by central review that were classified as pCR and near-pCR by local review. Notably, one participant’s response was assessed as not evaluable by both the local and central pathologists due to the absence of a tumor bed in the resected specimen. Although this observation was only from one participant, it is an encouraging sign that the INMC scoring guidelines can differentiate between a normal lymph node and one with 100% tumor regression. Interobserver agreement was also high, with the three central observers agreeing on a response category for 89% of participants; the weighted Gwet’s agreement coefficient was 0.94 (95% CI 0.90-0.98). Discordant responses among the central reviewers were reported for only seven participants, varying by a maximum of one response category. Notably, for four of the seven participants, the dissenting response assessment was at an RVT percentage that stratified a response category cutoff (i.e. 50% or 10%). For one of the discordant cases, a cystic cavity in the treated tumor was not consistently factored into the RVT percentage by each reviewer. However, currently, there is no clear consensus on accounting for such cavities when assessing for pathologic response. The high level of concordance in pathologic response assessment between local and central review and among central reviewers provides confidence in applying pathologic response as a surrogate endpoint, in accordance with recommendations from the INMC.18,19 Although the focus of the current analysis is melanoma, it is anticipated that these findings could be generalizable to other tumor types.

As neoadjuvant therapy becomes a standard of care in melanoma, accurate reporting of pathologic response is vital for early evaluation of treatment efficacy and clinical decision making in the adjuvant setting.14 There are few other analyses available that have evaluated concordance between local and central review of pathologic response in patients with melanoma. In the phase III NADINA trial, which evaluated neoadjuvant ipilimumab plus nivolumab followed by surgery and response-driven adjuvant therapy compared with standard adjuvant nivolumab in patients with resectable stage III melanoma, discordances between local and central assessment of pathologic responses were infrequent.12 Fewer than 1% of patients were classified as having non-MPR instead of MPR by central review, and 5.4% were classified as having had an MPR instead of no MPR. This low rate of discordance was attributed to the upfront education of pathologists and adherence to INMC guidelines for pathologic assessment.12 Together with our findings, these collective results support the use of pathologic response as a surrogate endpoint and highlight the importance of ongoing training for pathologists in community settings.

The primary limitation of this analysis was that the number of participants in some of the pathologic response categories was low; however, the number of participants with pCR and MPR was sufficient to indicate differences between treatment arms. Additionally, local investigators in KEYMAKER-U02 substudy 02C were trained on the pathology reporting criteria and completed the same pathologic response assessment as central reviewers. The findings of this analysis may therefore not be applicable to environments in which equivalent training or consistent criteria are not available. Furthermore, KEYMAKER-U02 substudy 02C was conducted at academic institutions and clinical research centers, which may not reflect broader community practice. Some discrepancies between local and central review were still observed, underscoring the importance of adequate training using standardized methods. Publicly available training materials detailing tissue handling and histologic scoring have recently been made available to help achieve broad standardization of pathologic response assessment.16 This analysis also utilized the RVT cutoffs for pathologic response initially defined by the INMC in 2018, but other clinically meaningful thresholds may become apparent as survival data continue to mature. For example, a retrospective analysis of stage III melanoma after neoadjuvant immunotherapy reported that an RVT of ≥70% and <10% fibrosis subtype may identify those at the highest risk of recurrence.20

In conclusion, the results of this post hoc analysis showed that there was a high level of concordance in pathologic response assessment between local and central review in KEYMAKER-U02 substudy 02C, indicating that local investigator-reported pathologic response can be considered accurate, assuming adequate training has been provided. The high level of interobserver concordance among central reviewers also further supports a high level of confidence in using pathologic response as a primary endpoint.

Surface Precision in Orthodontics: A Comprehensive Analysis of Interproximal Reduction Methods Using Atomic Force Microscopy
Authors: Abhishek Sinha, Sneha Surendran, Rashme Rashme, Savadamoorthi Kamatchi Subramani
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:Interproximal reduction (IPR) is a common orthodontic procedure aimed at creating space between teeth for proper alignment.Objective:The objective of this study is to comprehensively evaluate and compare the impact of three distinct IPR methods on the surface roughness of mandibular teeth using atomic force microscopy (AFM).Methods:A total of 128 extracted human mandibular teeth were allocated evenly into three groups: Group A (manual IPR), Group B (rotary-disc IPR), and Group C (laser-assisted IPR). Each group underwent specific IPR procedures, and the surface roughness was evaluated using AFM. The study employed rigorous randomization and precise calibration of instruments.Results:The findings indicate that Group A (manual IPR) exhibited the smoothest surfaces, while Group C (laser-assisted IPR) demonstrated the highest surface roughness. Statistical analyses revealed significant differences among the groups (P < 0.05). Location-specific analysis highlighted variations across different areas of the interproximal surfaces.Conclusion:The choice of IPR method significantly influences the surface roughness of mandibular teeth. Manual IPR appears to yield the smoothest surfaces, while laser-assisted IPR may result in increased roughness. These findings have implications for clinical decision-making in orthodontic practices.

INTRODUCTION

Orthodontic treatment often involves various procedures aimed at achieving optimal alignment of teeth, and one such common intervention is interproximal reduction (IPR).[1] IPR, also known as enamel stripping or slenderizing, entails the controlled removal of a small amount of enamel from the interproximal surfaces of teeth, creating space for proper alignment and alleviating crowding issues.[2] While IPR is widely employed in orthodontics, the choice of method for IPR remains a subject of debate and investigation.

The surface characteristics of teeth post-IPR are of paramount importance as they can influence oral health, patient comfort, and treatment outcomes.[3] Surface roughness, in particular, plays a crucial role in plaque retention, gingival health, and the overall patient experience during orthodontic treatment.[4] Therefore, understanding the impact of different IPR methods on tooth surface roughness is essential for informed clinical decision-making.

Several methods are utilized for IPR, ranging from traditional manual techniques using abrasive strips to modern approaches involving rotary discs and laser-assisted technologies.[5678]

MATERIALS AND METHODS

This research employed a prospective experimental design to investigate the effects of IPR methods on the surface characteristics of mandibular teeth. A total of 128 extracted human mandibular teeth, devoid of any apparent defects, were selected and allocated evenly into three distinct groups representing specific IPR methods: manual strips (Group A), rotary discs (Group B), and laser-assisted IPR (Group C). Rigorous randomization procedures ensured balanced distribution across the groups. Manual IPR in Group A involved fine-grit abrasive strips for controlled reduction. Group B utilized a low-speed handpiece with fine-grit discs for systematic reduction, while Group C employed a Biolase Waterlase iPlus laser for precision reduction, with meticulous calibration of the laser parameters. Atomic force microscopy (AFM) was used for surface roughness analysis, involving detailed imaging and comprehensive parameter calculation (Ra, Rq, and Rz). Calibration and quality control measures ensured data accuracy and reproducibility.

Surface roughness was analyzed at three distinct locations on each tooth’s interproximal surface. High-resolution AFM imaging facilitated precise data extraction and roughness parameter computation. Data were collected by a blinded examiner to ensure unbiased results. Robust statistical analysis, including one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) and post-hoc Tukey’s test, was conducted to identify variations in surface roughness among the three IPR groups. The data were processed using SPSS version 22.0, with a significance level set at P < 0.05.

RESULTS

The descriptive statistics table [Table 1] reveals valuable insights into the average surface roughness parameters (Ra, Rq, and Rz) for each IPR group at three distinct locations. Group A, representing manual IPR, exhibits the lowest average surface roughness across all locations, with Ra values ranging from 0.10 to 0.15 μm. This suggests that the use of fine-grit abrasive strips with controlled pressure in Group A results in a smoother interproximal surface compared to the other methods. On the contrary, Group C that underwent laser-assisted IPR demonstrates the highest average surface roughness values, with Ra ranging from 0.23 to 0.28 μm. The consistent trend across locations implies that laser-assisted reduction may lead to increased surface roughness on mandibular teeth.

The one-way ANOVA results [Table 2] indicate statistically significant differences in all surface roughness parameters (Ra, Rq, and Rz) among the three IPR groups. The obtained F-values are substantial (Ra: F = 12.45, Rq: F = 10.20, Rz: F = 15.78), suggesting that the variations observed are highly unlikely to be due to random chance. Post-hoc tests, such as Tukey’s test, would be conducted to identify specific differences between the groups. The P values are below the significance threshold of 0.05 (Ra: P = 0.001, Rq: P = 0.003, Rz: P = 0.001), reinforcing the notion that the IPR methods significantly influence the surface roughness of mandibular teeth. The location-specific analysis [Table 3] reveals interesting insights into the effects of IPR methods at each selected location. For instance, at Location 1, Group C consistently shows the highest roughness values compared to Groups A and B, with Ra values differing by 0.13 μm and 0.08 μm, respectively. This indicates that laser-assisted IPR may have a more pronounced impact on surface roughness, especially in specific anatomical regions. Furthermore, the positive differences between Group A and Group B in the other locations suggest that laser-assisted IPR tends to result in consistently higher surface roughness across different areas of the interproximal surfaces.

DISCUSSION

Rough surfaces may lead to increased friction between teeth and orthodontic appliances, potentially causing discomfort and compromising treatment efficiency. Therefore, the choice of IPR method becomes crucial not only for achieving optimal tooth alignment but also for ensuring a positive patient experience.[2]

Group A, utilizing fine-grit abrasive strips with controlled pressure, demonstrated the smoothest surfaces among the three groups, highlighting the precision and control of manual IPR methods. By contrast, Group B, employing rotary discs, resulted in slightly higher surface roughness values, consistent with the understanding that rotary instruments may have limitations in achieving the precision offered by manual techniques. Group C, representing laser-assisted IPR, exhibited the highest surface roughness values, which could be attributed to the nature of laser ablation and its thermal effects on enamel microstructure. The findings suggest that while laser-assisted IPR offers precision in minimizing damage to adjacent tissues, it may lead to increased roughness.[3] Clinicians must carefully consider the choice of IPR method based on the desired outcome, patient characteristics, and the specific clinical scenario.[4] Although this study provides valuable insights, its in vitro nature necessitates further clinical research to validate these findings in real-world orthodontic scenarios. Future studies should explore the long-term effects of different IPR methods, considering factors such as enamel thickness, age, and oral hygiene practices, to offer a comprehensive understanding of the implications of IPR techniques.[5678]

CONCLUSION

In conclusion, this study sheds light on the potential impact of different IPR methods on the surface roughness of mandibular teeth. Manual IPR demonstrated the smoothest surfaces, emphasizing the precision and predictability offered by this traditional technique.

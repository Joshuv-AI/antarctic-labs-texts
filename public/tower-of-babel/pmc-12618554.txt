Novel Sigma-1 receptor agonist alleviates renal ischemic injury by targeting apoptotic and inflammatory pathways
Authors: Akos R. Toth, Tamas Lakat, Andras Budai, Hanga Kallay, Balint Szokol, Laszlo J. Wagner, Laszlo Orfi, Marcell Kreko, Attila J. Szabo, Andrea Fekete, Adam Hosszu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Renal ischemia-reperfusion injury (IRI) is a major cause of acute kidney injury, yet its mechanisms remain unclear, and effective treatments are lacking. We previously showed that the Sigma-1 receptor (S1R) agonist fluvoxamine protects against IRI and IRI-induced graft injury during transplantation. Here, we developed a novel compound, ‘VCC904125’, with potent S1R affinity and minimal blood-brain barrier penetration to mitigate renal IRI without psychoactive side effects. Mice were treated with VCC904125 before clamping the left renal pedicles, followed by contralateral nephrectomy. VCC904125 markedly alleviated BUN and serum creatinine levels, KIM-1 and NGAL expression, and structural damage at both 24 and 48 h after reperfusion. S1R activation by VCC904125 targets key pathways underlying IRI, including apoptosis and inflammation. VCC904125 treatment impeded the apoptotic p53-Bax pathway and influenced CaMKII-NF-κB signaling, resulting in diminished proinflammatory cytokine expression. In the ex vivo model, kidneys were perfused and stored in an HTK preservation solution supplemented with VCC904125 to simulate cold storage conditions before transplantation. VCC904125 ameliorated structural injury profoundly after cold ischemia. Taken together, S1R activation by VCC904125 decreases renal IRI via ameliorating apoptotic and inflammatory pathways. These results highlight the therapeutic promise of S1R activation in mitigating cold and warm ischemia and improving transplant outcomes.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-23459-0.

Introduction

Renal ischemia/reperfusion injury (IRI) is the leading cause of acute kidney injury (AKI) occurring in a variety of clinical settings, including kidney transplantation, where it contributes to delayed graft function and adverse long-term outcomes. Given the global shortage of donors, minimizing graft damage prior to transplantation is crucial1–3. Despite improvement in dynamic organ preservation and refinement of surgical techniques, specific pharmacological therapies that have proven effective in reducing IRI-related AKI are lacking.

The molecular mechanisms underlying IRI-AKI are complex and not yet fully elucidated4. ATP depletion caused by inadequate blood supply and oxygen delivery leads to tubular cell injury and the activation of cell death programs, including apoptosis and necrosis. Additionally, the restoration of perfusion provokes subsequent tissue injury and triggers a robust inflammatory response due to increased calcium influx and the production of reactive oxygen species (ROS)5.

Sigma 1 receptor (S1R) is an intracellular, ligand-regulated chaperone protein that is crucial in maintaining cellular function and survival6. S1R activation protects against cell death and inflammation in ischemic brain and cardiac damage through the regulation of nitric oxide synthase pathways6–9. In our study, S1R activation improved post-ischemic survival and ameliorated structural and functional damage by enhancing perfusion after renal IRI10. Additionally, we showed that S1R activation by its endogenous ligand 17β-estradiol may contribute to superior outcomes in females after renal IRI11. Furthermore, our most recent study demonstrated that S1R agonist fluvoxamine reduces cold and warm ischemic graft injury during transplantation due to its anti-inflammatory and anti-apoptotic properties, which results in better post-transplant graft function12.

Notably, fluvoxamine is a selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor (SSRI) commonly used in psychiatry to treat depression. Its ability to cross the blood-brain barrier (BBB) and its effects on the central nervous system may limit its use in peripheral organs. Therefore, here we developed and tested a novel compound named ‘VCC904125’, which has potent S1R affinity but minimal BBB penetration. The renoprotective effects of VCC904125 were tested in a murine model of renal IRI and in an ex vivo model of graft cold storage. We hypothesized that VCC904125 reduces IRI-induced renal damage and improves donor organ condition by mitigating apoptosis and inflammation through S1R activation. This study aims to address the current gaps in pharmacological interventions for IRI-AKI and explore a promising therapeutic avenue that could impact clinical practices in kidney transplantation.

Results

The VCC904125 compound: blood-brain barrier penetration, binding affinity, and cellular effects

When designing a novel S1R agonist compound, we aimed to reduce blood-brain barrier penetration relative to fluvoxamine while maintaining a low affinity for the hERG channel. In silico calculations were performed to explore BBB penetration (QPlogBB) and determine lipophilicity (QPlogPo/w). As expected, the predictions indicated that fluvoxamine has a strong capability to cross the blood-brain barrier. VCC846743 and VCC904125 exhibited the highest QPlogPo/w​ values, attributed to the presence of appropriate functional groups, indicating low BBB penetration. Importantly, polar surface area (PSA), a critical factor influencing blood-brain barrier permeability, was also assessed. A PSA below 90 Å2 is generally considered favorable for cell membrane permeability, while higher PSA values limit BBB penetration13. VCC904125 has the highest PSA value of 83.5 Å2 and is expected to possess the lowest ability to cross the BBB, making it a promising candidate (Fig. 1a,b).

The competitive radioligand binding assay demonstrated that VCC904125 induced a similar maximal displacement as fluvoxamine in 10 µM concentration, coupled with low hERG affinity (Fig. 1b).

Based on its predicted low BBB penetration, high S1R binding affinity, and low hERG affinity, VCC904125 was chosen as the best S1R agonist drug candidate.

MTT and LDH assays were performed using the HK-2 cell line to assess the effect of VCC904125 on renal cell proliferation and cytotoxicity. The MTT assay showed no significant differences in cell proliferation across tested concentrations, indicating that VCC904125 does not compromise cell viability after 24 h of treatment (Fig. 1c). The two highest concentrations (25 and 50 µM) induced significant LDH release (Fig. 1d); therefore, the 10 µM concentration was selected for subsequent experiments.

VCC904125 ameliorates renal ischemic injury

To test the renoprotective effect of the novel S1R agonist, mice were treated with VCC904125 compound 30 min prior to the ischemic insult. Twenty-four hours after IRI, elevated BUN, and serum creatinine levels demonstrated the development of AKI. Treatment with VCC904125 significantly reduced BUN levels, indicating more preserved renal function (Fig. 2a,b).

Highly specific and sensitive tubular injury markers, KIM-1 and NGAL, were measured to assess tubular damage. Here, a massive 600-fold increase in renal mRNA expression of both biomarkers was substantially alleviated in the VCC904125-treated group (Fig. 2c,d). Similarly, VCC904125 attenuated HIF-1α protein levels, indicating a reduced hypoxic response (Fig. 2e).

PAS-stained kidney sections revealed severe tubular damage caused by IRI, including hyaline accumulation, vacuolization, loss of brush border, and tubular necrosis. VCC904125 treatment significantly mitigated the extent of acute tubular necrosis (ATN) (Fig. 2f).

The renoprotective effect of VCC904125 was further tested 48 h after reperfusion. VCC904125 exerted a robust protective effect as both BUN and serum creatinine were significantly improved (BUN was improved by 70% at T48 vs. 20% at T24; serum creatinine was improved by 81% at T48 vs. 10% at T24) (Fig. 3a,b). Tubular injury markers KIM-1 and NGAL were alleviated (Fig. 3c,d), and histological evaluation revealed even more substantial attenuation of structural damage than 24 h post-reperfusion (Fig. 3e). These results demonstrate that the beneficial effects of VCC904125 are sustained beyond the immediate acute phase of injury.

S1R agonist VCC904125 alleviates cold ischemia-induced renal injury

To simulate cold storage conditions before transplantation, the kidneys were perfused and stored in an ice-cold preservation solution (Custodiol HTK) supplemented with S1R agonist VCC904125. Assessment of tubular dilatation was a sufficiently sensitive approach to quantify structural damage in the CI model. While kidneys stored in the preservation solution typically show extended proximal tubular lumen area—indicating loss of tubular cell mass and epithelial flattening−VCC904125 counteracted these processes (Fig. 4). Thus, mitigated cold ischemic renal injury may lead to better graft condition prior to implantation, as well as to better transplant outcomes.

VCC904125 mitigates IRI-induced apoptosis

The tumor protein P53 (p53)- Bcl2-associated X (Bax) pathway and caspase-3 are crucial in mediating apoptosis in IRI-induced AKI. mRNA expression of p53 and Bax was upregulated in the T24 IRI group, but this was not observed upon VCC904125-treatment (Fig. 5a,b). While cleaved caspase-3 protein levels increased following IRI, treatment with VCC904125 led to a significant reduction compared to vehicle-treated controls (Fig. 5c).

VCC904125 treatment reduces inflammation following IRI

The NF-κB pathway plays a central role in producing pro-inflammatory cytokines during IRI-induced AKI. To confirm the anti-inflammatory effect of S1R activation, phosphorylated NF-κB, and phosphorylated CaMKII protein levels were measured. Both proteins were significantly reduced by VCC904125 treatment (Fig. 5d,e). In parallel, inflammatory cytokines Il1β and Tnf-α were also diminished in the T24 IRI + VCC904125 group (Fig. 5f,g).

Discussion

S1R agonists have garnered significant interest recently due to their therapeutic potential across various medical conditions. Our recent data showed that S1R agonist fluvoxamine allowed prolonged storage time and improved post-transplant kidney graft function in rat models of renal transplantation12. Originally classified as an SSRI, fluvoxamine exhibits a strong binding affinity (Ki = 36 nM) for S1R. Additionally, its lipophilic properties allow it to readily cross the BBB, quickly achieving therapeutic concentration in the brain. While its central nervous system activity may offer additional benefits during renal transplantation – given the association between depression and poorer graft survival in kidney recipients—it also raises concerns about potential adverse psychoactive effects14. Consequently, we designed novel S1R agonists with limited BBB penetration to minimize CNS effects. In this study, we developed and tested VCC904125, a novel S1R agonist compound with limited BBB penetration. Using murine models of renal IRI and ex vivo cold ischemia, we investigated the potential of VCC904125 to mitigate acute tubular damage. We explored the underlying mechanisms, with a particular focus on apoptosis and inflammation. Our results have been submitted for patent approval (Hungarian Intellectual Property Office Ref. No. P2500008).

The VCC904125 compound was designed as a fluvoxamine analog with increased polarity to reduce BBB permeability. The radioligand binding assay confirmed that VCC904125 retains a similarly high affinity and minimally lower specificity to S1R than fluvoxamine, ensuring its efficacy as a targeted agonist. Safety, particularly cardiac safety, is a cornerstone of drug development since cardiotoxicity is one of the major causes of failure in clinical phase I studies. Measuring human Ether-à-go-go-Related Gene (hERG) activity is one of the earliest and most essential assays, as blocking the hERG channel increases the risk of prolonged QT and arrhythmias15. Notably, VCC904125 displayed low hERG activity, suggesting that this original compound would offer a favorable cardiac safety profile. Comprehensive binding studies, including full dose–response analyses and ADMET measurements, were out of the scope of the present study, but are planned for future work.

Minimizing warm IRI is crucial in organ transplantation as it is significantly associated with an increased rate of AKI, delayed graft function, and a decline in long-term allograft function and survival16,17. Numerous novel surgical techniques and pharmacological therapies have been proposed to reduce warm IRI-caused organ damage; however, none of them have achieved a significant breakthrough. The pharmacological interventions targeting modulators of inflammatory pathways or oxidative stress are promising, but none have yet received regulatory approval for renal transplantation18.

In the present study, we strengthen our previous discovery that S1R is a promising therapeutic target in tissue preservation during renal IRI and transplantation. We showed that a single injection of the novel S1R agonist compound VCC904125 prior to the ischemic insult mitigates renal functional impairment and ameliorates structural damage. KIM-1 and NGAL are superior markers of renal tubular damage and predict functional impairment earlier than BUN or creatinine. Both were significantly lower in the kidneys of VCC904125-treated animals, suggesting more preserved organ condition and tubular function. Importantly, these renoprotective effects were not transient; by 48 h post-reperfusion, both functional and structural outcomes were even more substantially improved compared to the 24-hour time point, underscoring the robust and sustained protective potential of VCC904125.

Ischemic injury of the corticomedullary region typically manifests as ATN, characterized by brush border loss in proximal tubules, cell swelling, and necrosis. ATN is one of the most common causes of AKI, which highly increases the risk of graft failure and chronic kidney disease19. In addition, there is growing evidence that tubular epithelium recovery primarily depends on surviving endogenous tubular cells20. In this study, VCC904125 treatment alleviated ATN by significantly reducing the number of tubules exhibiting irreversible damage.

It is generally accepted that apoptosis is closely linked to delayed graft function following kidney transplantation21. The tumor suppressor gene p53 is a pivotal regulator of the cell cycle, DNA repair, and apoptosis in response to cellular stressors such as oxidative stress and hypoxia. Several studies showed the protective effect of p53 inhibition against apoptosis after renal IRI, highlighting its key role in the pathomechanism22. Upon activation, p53 translocates from the cytosol to the mitochondria and upregulates pro-apoptotic Bax, facilitating the release of cytochrome c and activating caspase-323,24.

Here, VCC904125 treatment downregulated p53 gene expression and reduced Caspase-3 protein levels after IRI. These results strengthen the few literary data suggesting a direct association between S1R activation and suppression of apoptosis. Maurice et al. reported that S1R agonist OZP002 attenuated the increment of Bax protein levels in the mouse hippocampus following amyloid Aβ25–35 peptide injection25. S1R knockdown or antagonist treatment has been shown to decrease cell survival and induce p53-mediated apoptosis26. Additionally, an in vitro study by Shen et al. showed that S1R inhibition upregulates Bax, Caspase-3, and Caspase-9 in microglial cells27.

Besides their anti-apoptotic properties, S1R agonists have been shown to alleviate inflammation, particularly in the brain, by modulating microglial activation and decreasing proinflammatory cytokine release in diseases like Alzheimer’s or Parkinson’s. S1R has also been demonstrated to ameliorate ischemic brain injury-induced inflammation by minimizing the infiltration of inflammatory cells and by stabilizing calcium homeostasis in the ER28.

The peripheral anti-inflammatory effects of S1R are less studied, but some research suggests that its activation may help reduce systemic inflammation in sepsis, IBD, and rheumatoid arthritis29,30. Notably, our recent study underlined the anti-inflammatory effect of S1R activation in a kidney transplantation model.

NF-κB is a “rapid response” transcription factor, which translocates into the nucleus upon phosphorylation and activates pro-inflammatory cytokines, such as TNF-α and IL-1β during renal IRI31–33. TNF-α can further stimulate the NF-κB pathway in a positive feedback loop34. There is strong evidence that CaMKII directly modulates NF-κB pathways and Moriguchi et al.. showed that decreased phosphorylation of neuronal calmodulin-dependent kinase II (CaMKII) was restored in mice treated with S1R agonist fluvoxamine or SA450335. In line with this, a single dose of VCC904125 attenuated the inflammatory response following IRI by decreasing levels of phosphorylated CaMKII, NF-κB, IL-1β, and TNF-α.

The primary stage of IRI in transplantation occurs during the cold storage of the graft, which is also an ideal opportunity for optimization to preserve tissue condition. While static cold storage remains the most commonly used method for organ preservation, there is promising research and clinical data on novel machine perfusion technologies that help maintain near-physiological conditions prior to transplantation36. Optimizing the composition of preservation fluids that can be used with all of the various organ preservation technologies is essential.

In this study, we supplemented the commercially available Custodiol HTK preservation solution with VCC904125 and assessed ischemic injury after 24 h of static cold storage. At this point, there were no signs of severe pathological changes; however, even minor improvements during preservation may significantly enhance graft survival. VCC904125 effectively hindered tubular dilation, which was otherwise prominent in kidneys preserved in Custodiol alone.

This study has some limitations, and further research is warranted to validate these findings. We utilized an ex vivo model to mimic cold ischemia and unilateral clamping with contralateral nephrectomy to induce warm IRI. However, employing a complete transplant model would be beneficial for comprehensively assessing the entire transplantation process. Additionally, while we investigated some key elements of inflammatory and apoptotic pathways, the exact mechanism of S1R action remains to be elucidated. Future research aims to better understand the precise mechanisms of S1R action, to optimize drug delivery methods, and to confirm the efficacy and safety of VCC in larger clinical trials.

In conclusion, this study demonstrates the therapeutic potential of the novel S1R agonist VCC904125 by targeting the primary mechanisms driving IRI pathology: apoptosis and inflammation. However, longitudinal studies are needed to confirm whether VCC904125 improves long-term graft survival and mitigates the development of tissue fibrosis.

We hope that in the future, supplementing preservation fluids with an S1R agonist may alleviate donor shortage by improving the condition of organs retrieved from extended criteria donors and expanding the maximum duration of storage without additional injury. Future research should focus on clarifying the precise mechanisms of S1R activation, optimizing drug delivery methods, and evaluating the efficacy and safety of VCC904125 in larger-scale clinical trials.

The VCC904125 compound (MW = 346.35 Da) was synthesized in five chemical steps as follows (Fig. 6):

3.00 g (11.5 mmol) 5-methoxy-1-[4-(trifluoromethyl) phenyl] pentan-1-one was suspended in 25 ml 48% HBr and stirred at 120 °C for 3 h. The reaction mixture was allowed to cool to room temperature and poured into saturated NaCl solution and extracted with 2 × 20 ml EtOAc. The organic layer was separated and dried over magnesium-sulphate. The solvent was removed under reduced pressure, and an oily product was obtained. In silico calculations

All processes were carried out in Schrödinger (Release 2019-2, Schrödinger, LLC, New York, NY, USA). The 3D structures of the compounds were determined by LigPrep using OPLS3e force field. Octanol-water partition coefficient, blood-brain barrier permeability, and polar surface area of the compounds were calculated using QikProp. Unionized molecules without salt components were used as the input for the calculations. The molecular surface and electrostatic potential (ESP) map of VCC904125 was generated using electron density and ESP data calculated by Jaguar.

Radioligand binding assay

A binding assay was performed by Eurofins Panlabs Discovery Services Ltd. (New Taipei City, Taiwan) on human Jurkat cells. To measure binding to S1R, membranes were incubated with specific antagonist 8.0 nM [3 H] haloperidol in the presence of a competing concentration of 10 µM VCC904125. Additional experiments were performed to determine the binding of VCC904125 to human Ether-à-go-go-Related Gene (hERG).

Cell proliferation and lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) assay

HK-2 human proximal tubular epithelial cells (American Type Culture Collection, Manassas, VA) were treated with 0.001–50 µM VCC904125. A methyl-thiazole tetrazolium (MTT, M6494 Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA) assay was performed according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) assay (C20300, Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA) was performed using the supernatant of the cells according to the manufacturer’s protocol.

Animals

6-week-old male C57BL/6J mice were obtained from Animalab Hungary Kft (Budapest, Hungary). Mice were kept in standard conditions with a 12-h dark/light cycle. Animals were housed in laboratory cages with food and water available ad libitum. Animal procedures were approved by the Committee on the Care of Laboratory Animals at Semmelweis University, Budapest, Hungary (PEI/001/1731-9-2015).

Renal IRI

Mice were randomly divided into groups of 6–8 animals. Thirty minutes before the ischemic insult, mice were treated ip. as follows: (i) Sham-operated animals that underwent similar surgical procedures without clamping (Sham); (ii) isotonic saline as vehicle (IRI); or (iii) 20 mg × bw kg−1 VCC904125 (IRI + VCC). VCC904125 dosage was determined based on previous experiments with fluvoxamine10. Mice were anesthetized with 90 mg × bw kg−1 ketamine (Richter Gedeon Plc., Budapest, Hungary) and 10 mg × bw kg−1 xylazine (Medicus Partner, Biatorbagy, Hungary) mixture. During the surgical procedure, the mice were kept at a constant body temperature of 37 °C using a heating pad. Renal ischemia was performed by occluding the left renal pedicles for 25 min with an atraumatic vascular clamp. Before the end of the ischemic time, contralateral nephrectomy was accomplished, followed by the removal of the clamp and 5 min observation of the left kidney to confirm complete reperfusion. Twenty-four or forty-eight hours after reperfusion, animals were re-anesthetized and euthanized by exsanguination. Blood and kidney samples were collected. All kidney samples were immediately snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen or fixed in 4% buffered paraformaldehyde (pH = 7.4) for further investigation. Plasma chemistry was performed using the standard autoanalyzer methods in our hospital.

Cold ischemia (CI)

CI was performed by cannulating the renal artery of the mice and flushing their kidneys with 4 °C isotonic saline for 1 min. Following this, the kidneys were (i) collected (Control) or perfused either with (ii) 200 mL of 4 °C Custodiol (Franz Köhler Chemie Gmbh., Bensheim, Germany) (24 h CI) or (iii) with 200 mL of 4 °C Custodiol + 10 µM VCC904125 (24 h CI + VCC) at a perfusion pressure of 80–100 mmHg and then kept on ice in the same solution for 24 h.

Quantitative RT-PCR

Total RNA was extracted using the RNeasy RNA Isolation Kit (Qiagen, Germantown, MD) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Two µg total RNA samples were reverse transcribed into cDNA using Maxima First Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit for RT-qPCR (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA). Quantitative RT-PCR was performed on the LightCycler 96 system (Roche Diagnostics, Mannheim, Germany) using cDNA samples, SYBR Green I Master enzyme mix (Roche Diagnostics, Mannheim, Germany), and specific primers (IDT, Coralville, Iowa, USA) (Table 1). Results were analyzed by LightCycler 96 software version 1.1.0.1320 (Roche Diagnostics, Mannheim, Germany). All data were normalized using the housekeeping gene Rn18S.

Western blot

All reagents were purchased from Bio-Rad (Hercules, CA), unless stated otherwise. Protein concentration was measured by a detergent–compatible protein assay kit. Denatured protein samples were separated on 12% SDS polyacrylamide gels and transferred electrophoretically to nitrocellulose membranes. Membranes were stained with Ponceau-S, then washed and incubated in blocking buffer (5% BSA or 5% non-fat dry milk in TBS). Next, membranes were incubated overnight at 4 °C with specific primary antibodies for mice pNfkb (1:1000; ab86299 Abcam, Cambridge, UK), phosphorylated calmodulin-dependent kinase II (pCaMkII) (1:500; 12716; Cell Signaling Technology, Danvers, MA, USA), Hif1α (1:250; 14179; Cell Signaling Technology) and cCasp3 (1:500; 9661; Cell Signaling Technology, Danvers, MA, USA). After washing, membranes were incubated with the corresponding HRP–HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies. Chemiluminescence was detected using a LuminataTM Forte (Millipore Corporation, Billerica, MA, USA) substrate, and immunoreactive bands were quantified densitometrically by Image Lab (version 6.1; Bio-Rad Laboratories, Hercules, CA, USA) as integrated optical density (IOD) after background subtraction. IODs were factored for Ponceau-S staining to correct for any variations in total protein loading.

Statistical analysis

All data are presented as means ± SDs. Statistical analyses were performed using Prism software (version 9.5.1; GraphPad Software, La Jolla, CA). After testing the normality with the Shapiro–Wilk test, one-way ANOVA followed by the Holm–Sidak post hoc test was used for multiple comparisons and possible interactions. For non-parametric data, Kruskal–Wallis ANOVA on ranks was used. P values of < 0.05 were considered significant.

The p p -rank stratification of the moduli space of double covers of a fixed elliptic curve
Kevin Chang, Dušan Dragutinović, Steven R. Groen, Yuxin Lin

Abstract
In this paper we investigate the p p -rank stratification of the moduli space of curves of genus g g that admit a double cover to a fixed elliptic curve E E in characteristic p > 2 p>2 . We show that the closed p p -rank strata of this moduli space are equidimensional of the expected dimension. We also show the existence of a smooth double cover of E E of all the possible values of the p p -rank on this moduli space.



1
Introduction

Let
k
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p>2
throughout this paper. We study the
p
-rank stratification of the moduli space of double covers of a fixed elliptic curve over
k
. In characteristic
p
, abelian varieties carry several interesting discrete invariants, such as the
p
-rank or the Newton polygon. The corresponding invariants for curves are defined to be those of their Jacobians. These invariants induce stratifications on the moduli spaces of abelian varieties, and consequently on the moduli spaces of curves.

In this paper, we focus on the
p
-rank of curves. The
p
-rank of an abelian variety
A
over
k
is the integer
f_{A}:=\dim(\text{Hom}(\mu_{p},A))
such that
\#A[p](k)=p^{f_{A}}
; it satisfies
0\leq f_{A}\leq\dim(A)
. The
p
-rank of a curve is defined to be the
p
-rank of its Jacobian. By
[
Oda69
]
, the
p
-rank of a curve
C
over
k
also equals the semisimple rank of the Frobenius operator on
H^{1}(C,\mathcal{O}_{C})
. The
p
-rank is invariant under isogenies, and the Newton stratification refines the
p
-rank stratification.

The
p
-rank stratifications of various moduli spaces have been studied extensively. Given a moduli space
\mathcal{M}
of curves or abelian varieties, denote by
V_{f}(\mathcal{M})
the
closed
p
-rank
f
stratum
, consisting of objects whose
p
-rank is at most
f
. It is known (
[
Oor74
, Lemmas 1.4 and 1.6]
and
[
Kob75
, Theorem 7.(1)]
) that
V_{f}(\mathcal{A}_{g})
is pure of codimension
g-f
. It is a subtler question how the
p
-rank stratification intersects the Torelli locus. It is proved in
[
FvdG04
, (2.3)]
that closed
p
-rank strata of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
are pure and have the expected codimension
g-f
. As a consequence, the generic point of any component of
V_{f}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g})
corresponds to a smooth curve. Similar investigations for certain covers of the projective line followed. In
[
GP05
]
, it is shown that the same holds for the closed
p
-rank strata of
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{g}
, the moduli space of hyperelliptic curves of genus
g
. The
p
-rank stratification of the moduli space of
Artin-Schreier covers
, separable degree
p
covers of the projective line, is explored in
[
PZ12
]
. The
p
-rank strata of the moduli space of degree
l
cyclic covers of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, where
l\neq p
is prime, are studied in
[
OPW22
]
. In
[
FvdG04
, (6.1)]
,
p
-ranks of unramified double covers of genus
2
curves are studied. In
[
Bou01
]
, ramified covers of degree coprime to
p
of generic
r
-pointed curves are studied for sufficiently large
p
.

A natural next step is to investigate the
p
-ranks of potentially ramified double covers of a fixed elliptic curve
E=(E,O_{E})
over
k
. Note that the
p
-rank
f_{E}
of
E
is either
f_{E}=0
or
f_{E}=1
, depending on whether
E
is supersingular or ordinary.
Let
\mathcal{B}_{E,g}
denote the moduli space of smooth genus-
g
curves over
k
that admit a
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
-cover to
E
, and let
\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{E,g}
denote its Deligne-Mumford compactification. Furthermore, let
\mathcal{B}_{g}
denote the bielliptic locus and
\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{g}
its Deligne-Mumford compactification. Our main result is that the closed
p
-rank strata of
\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{E,g}
and
\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{g}
are pure and have the expected (co)dimension.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
g\geq 2
and
p>2
. For any
f_{E}\leq f\leq g-1+f_{E}
, the locus
V_{f}(\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{E,g})
is pure of dimension
g-2+f-f_{E}
. As a consequence,
V_{f}(\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{g})
is pure of dimension
g-2+f
, for any
0\leq f\leq g
.

Furthermore, our geometric insights lead us to the following corollary.

Corollary 1.2
.

The following statements hold for any
g\geq 2
and any prime number
p>2
.

1.

For any
0\leq f\leq g-2
, there is a component of
V_{f}(\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{g})
that consists entirely of singular curves.

2.

For any
0\leq f\leq g
, there is a smooth bielliptic curve
C
of genus
g
in characteristic
p
whose
p
-rank equals
f
, except for the case
(p,g,f)=(3,2,0)
.

The spaces
\mathcal{B}_{E,g}
and
\mathcal{B}_{g}
are of particular interest because they have interesting arithmetic applications.
Any smooth supersingular curve of genus
g
admits a map to a supersingular elliptic curve. By choosing
E
to be supersingular and considering the space
\mathcal{B}_{E,g}
,
we learn more about the case where such a map has degree
2
. Understanding this could also lead to a better understanding of whether there exists a smooth supersingular curve of genus
g
. In fact, the existence of a smooth supersingular curve
C
of genus
g=4
in characteristic
p>3
(and hence for all
p>0
) was established in
[
KHS20
]
, where
C
was constructed as the normalization of the fiber product of two (well-chosen) supersingular elliptic curves
E_{i}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
, for
i=1,2
, over
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Note that the induced maps
C\to E_{1}
and
C\to E_{2}
are double covers. These moduli spaces are also relevant for Oort’s conjecture, which states that the generic point of any component of the locus of supersingular abelian varieties has the minimal automorphism group, that is
\{\pm 1\}
. An analog of this statement for supersingular curves was proved for
g=4
and
p>2
in
[
Dra24
]
, which implies Oort’s conjecture for
g=4
and
p>2
. The proof uses geometric insights into the
p
-rank
0
stratum of
\mathcal{B}_{4}
.

1.1
Structure of this paper

The proof of our main theorem makes extensive use of the boundary of
\mathcal{B}_{E,g}
in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
, and a large part of the paper is devoted to understanding that boundary. The reason the boundary is so useful is that, while it can be extremely difficult to construct a smooth curve with a given
p
-rank, it is relatively easy to construct singular curves with a given
p
-rank. Namely, one can clutch together multiple well-understood curves of smaller genus at ordinary double points. This provides a point in
V_{f}(\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{E,g})
. If the boundary components of
\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{E,g}
are well-understood, then, in many cases, one can argue by dimension reasons that
V_{f}(\mathcal{B}_{E,g})
is dense inside
V_{f}(\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{E,g})
and, in particular, also non-empty. Moreover, the dimension of the boundary can yield the dimension of the smooth locus.

To this end, in Section
2
, we introduce the Deligne-Mumford stack
\overline{M}_{E,g;n}
, which parametrizes admissible double covers of a genus 1 curve with one component identified with
E
, along with
n
marked points on the source curve. The substack
M_{E,g;n}
consists of points where the source curve is smooth. This provides the appropriate setting for proving the results we need about
\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{E,g}
, which is the image of the map
\overline{M}_{E,g}\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
that extracts the source curve.
In Lemma
2.4
and Corollary
2.6
, we explicitly describe the boundary of
\overline{M}_{E,g}
and deduce that its smooth locus
M_{E,g}
is dense. Since the forgetful morphism
\overline{M}_{E,g}\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
is not quasi-finite, we consider a modified moduli space
\overline{M}_{E,g;n}^{{\rm red}}
, consisting of covers whose branch points on
E
sum to the unit element
O_{E}
. In Proposition
2.9
, we prove that the forgetful map
\overline{M}_{E,g}^{{\rm red}}\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
is quasi-finite on the smooth locus
\overline{M}^{{\rm red}}_{E,g}
and generically finite on every boundary component.

In Section
3
, these results are used to analyze the
p
-rank stratification of
\mathcal{B}_{E,g}
. It is shown that every
p
-rank stratum intersects non-trivially with the boundary
\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{E,g}-\mathcal{B}_{E,g}
. The purity theorem
[
DJO00
, Theorem 4.1]
then provides a lower bound on the dimension of
V_{f}(\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{E,g})
, which we prove to be sharp following the strategy of
[
FvdG04
, (2.3)]
, resulting in Theorem
1.1
. More sophisticated arguments are required to handle the case when
E
is a supersingular curve.
Finally, we prove Corollary
1.2
using geometric insights into the
p
-rank
f
strata and the inductive approach of
[
Pri19
, Theorem 6.4]
.

In Section
4
, applications of Theorem
1.1
to the existence of smooth supersingular bielliptic curves are discussed. Finally, in Section
5
we construct explicit smooth double covers of
E
of low
p
-rank. In Appendix
A
, necessary results about the geometry of relevant Deligne-Mumford stacks such as smoothness and properness are proved.

2.2
The reduced moduli spaces
M_{E,g;n}^{{\rm red}}\subset\overline{M}_{E,g;n}^{{\rm red}}

In this section, we introduce a “reduced” moduli space
\overline{M}_{E,g;n}^{{\rm red}}
as a closed substack of
\overline{M}_{E,g;n}
. To construct this space,
we consider the map
\pi:\overline{M}_{E,g;n}\to E
that sends a cover
D\to C
to the sum of the branch points of the cover. Since one component of
C
is identified with
E
and all other components are copies of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, this sum of points indeed lives in
{\rm Jac}(C)\cong{\rm Jac}(E)\cong E
.

Definition 2.7
.

We define
\overline{M}_{E,g;n}^{{\rm red}}
as the fiber of the map
\pi
over the identity
O_{E}\in E
.
We also denote the open substack of smooth covers
M_{E,g;n}^{{\rm red}}\coloneqq M_{E,g;n}\cap\overline{M}_{E,g;n}^{{\rm red}}
.

Proposition 2.8
.

\overline{M}_{E,g;n}^{{\rm red}}
is a proper Deligne-Mumford stack of dimension
2g-3+n
.

Proof.

The claim that
\overline{M}_{E,g;n}^{{\rm red}}
is proper and Deligne-Mumford follows from the fact that
\overline{M}_{E,g;n}
is proper and Deligne-Mumford. To compute its dimension, we
observe that all the fibers of
\pi:\overline{M}_{E,g;n}\to E
are isomorphic. To get an isomorphism between
\pi^{-1}(O_{E})=\overline{M}_{E,g;n}^{{\rm red}}
and
\pi^{-1}(x)
, take a point
y\in E
such that
(2g-2)\cdot y=x
and use translation by
y
to get an isomorphism between
\pi^{-1}(O_{E})
and
\pi^{-1}(x)
. Because
\overline{M}_{E,g;n}
has dimension
2g-2+n
(Theorem
A.11
) and all the fibers of
\pi
are isomorphic, it follows that
\pi^{-1}(O_{E})=\overline{M}_{E,g;n}^{{\rm red}}
has dimension
2g-3+n
, as desired.
∎

One intuitive reason to consider
\overline{M}_{E,g;n}^{{\rm red}}
is that we ideally would like to identify double covers of
E
if they are the same after composing with an automorphism of
E
. Because
{\rm Aut}(E)
is an extension of a finite constant group scheme by
E
(where
E
acts by translations), taking the quotient by the automorphism group of
E
will reduce the dimension of our moduli space by 1. While
\overline{M}_{E,g;n}^{{\rm red}}
is not quite a quotient of
\overline{M}_{E,g;n}
, it is a slice for the action of
{\rm Aut}(E)
.
Therefore,
\overline{M}_{E,g;n}^{{\rm red}}
maps finitely and surjectively to
[\overline{M}_{E,g;n}/{\rm Aut}(E)]
. The reason we use
\overline{M}_{E,g;n}^{{\rm red}}
rather than the quotient
[\overline{M}_{E,g;n}/{\rm Aut}(E)]
is that
\overline{M}_{E,g;n}^{{\rm red}}
is a substack of
\overline{M}_{E,g;n}
, and hence it inherits the structure of a proper Deligne-Mumford stack. Furthermore, it is more straightforward to define the morphism in Proposition
2.9
for
\overline{M}_{E,g;n}^{{\rm red}}
than for
[\overline{M}_{E,g;n}/{\rm Aut}(E)]
.

This can be proved using a careful case-by-case analysis, as is done in the appendix, Proposition

∎

3
The
p
-rank stratification of
\mathcal{B}_{E,g}

Let
E=(E,O_{E})
be a fixed (smooth) elliptic curve over an algebraically closed field
k
of characteristic
p>2
with
p
-rank
f_{E}
. Recall that there are two possibilities:
f_{E}=1
if
E
is ordinary, and
f_{E}=0
if
E
is supersingular.
Consider the locus
\mathcal{B}_{E,g}
of smooth curves of genus
g\geq 2
that are double covers of
E
. Our goal is to describe the
p
-rank stratification of
\mathcal{B}_{E,g}
. We achieve this by analyzing its closure in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
using techniques similar to those in
[
FvdG04
]
,
[
GP05
]
, and
[
AP11
]
. As demonstrated in the proof of Theorem
3.12
, the cases when
E
is ordinary and when
E
is supersingular differ significantly, with the latter case requiring particular attention.

A smooth curve
D
of genus
g\geq 2
is called bielliptic if there exists an elliptic curve
E^{\prime}
and a double cover
D\to E^{\prime}
. We denote by
\mathcal{B}_{g}
the locus of bielliptic curves in
\mathcal{M}_{g}
. Given an elliptic curve
E^{\prime}
,
\mathcal{B}_{E^{\prime},g}
can be viewed as a subset of
\mathcal{B}_{g}
.
Consequently, we can describe the
p
-rank stratification of the entire locus
\mathcal{B}_{g}
of bielliptic curves of genus
g
.

As we have seen in Proposition
2.9
, there is a morphism
\overline{M}_{E,g}^{{\rm red}}\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
that is quasi-finite on the smooth locus and generically quasi-finite on every boundary component.
Denote by
\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{E,g}
the image of this morphism and note that
\mathcal{B}_{E,g}
coincides with the image of
M_{E,g}^{{\rm red}}
. Since
\overline{M}_{E,g}^{{\rm red}}
is a proper Deligne-Mumford stack by Proposition
2.8
and
M_{E,g}^{{\rm red}}\subseteq\overline{M}_{E,g}^{{\rm red}}
is open and dense, it follows that
\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{E,g}\subseteq\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
is the closure of
\mathcal{B}_{E,g}\subset\mathcal{M}_{g}
. We summarize some properties of
\mathcal{B}_{E,g}
and
\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{E,g}
below.

Lemma 3.1
.

For any
g\geq 2
, the moduli space
\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{E,g}
is irreducible, proper, and has dimension
2g-3
.

Proof.

Note that
\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{E,g}
is irreducible for any
g\geq 2
, because the map
\overline{M}^{{\rm red}}_{E,g}\to\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{E,g}
factors through
[\overline{M}_{E,g}/{\rm Aut}(E)]
and it was shown in Lemma
A.13
that
\overline{M}_{E,g}
is irreducible. The remaining properties follow from Propositions
2.8
and
2.9
.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

For any
g\geq 2
, the moduli space
\mathcal{B}_{E,g}
is irreducible, has dimension
2g-3
, and does not contain any positive-dimensional complete subvarieties.

Proof.

Lemma
3.1
and Proposition
2.9
imply that
\mathcal{B}_{E,g}
is irreducible.
Moreover, note that the coarse moduli space of
\mathcal{B}_{E,g}
is quasi-affine, and consequently,
\mathcal{B}_{E,g}
cannot contain positive-dimensional complete subvarieties. Indeed, let
\widetilde{M}_{E,g}^{\circ}
denote the open locus of
\widetilde{M}_{E,g}
where the source curve is smooth. Then
\mathcal{B}_{E,g}
is the image of
[\widetilde{M}_{E,g}^{\circ}/E]
under a quasi-finite and proper morphism (hence it induces a finite morphism on the coarse moduli space). The morphism is quasi-finite according to Proposition
2.9
, and it is proper because it is the restriction of
[\widetilde{M}_{E,g}/E]\to\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{E,g}
, which are both proper. Hence, it suffices to show that the coarse moduli space of
[\widetilde{M}_{E,g}^{\circ}/E]
is quasi-affine. If we let
\mathrm{PConf}^{2g-2}(E)
be the ordered configuration space of
2g-2
points on
E
, then there is a proper étale map
\widetilde{M}^{\circ}_{E,g}\to\mathrm{PConf}^{2g-2}(E)
that records the branch points of a cover, inducing a finite map on coarse moduli spaces. Now

\mathrm{PConf}^{2g-2}(E)\cong E\times\mathrm{PConf}^{2g-3}(E-\{O_{E}\})\text{ via }(x_{1},\ldots,x_{2g-2})\leftrightarrow(x_{1},(x_{2}-x_{1},\ldots,x_{2g-2}-x_{1})),

which implies
\mathrm{PConf}^{2g-2}(E)/E\cong\mathrm{PConf}^{2g-3}(E~-~\{O_{E}\})
. Finally,

[\mathrm{Conf}^{2g-2}(E)/E]\cong[\mathrm{PConf}^{2g-2}(E)/(E\times S_{2g-3})]\cong\mathrm{PConf}^{2g-3}(E-\{O_{E}\})/S_{2g-3}.

The coarse space of
\mathrm{PConf}^{2g-3}(E-\{O_{E}\})/S_{2g-3}
is affine because
E-\{O_{E}\}
is affine and thus the coarse spaces of
[\mathrm{Conf}^{2g-2}(E)/E]
and
[\widetilde{M}^{\circ}_{E,g}/E]
are affine.
∎

Remark 3.7
.

Let

.
By
[

, Corollary 1]
, given a double cover

Corollary 3.8
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve in characteristic

with
p
-rank

Proof.

It is enough to consider the case

Lemma 3.10

For

In particular, the preceding two lemmas imply that

(2)

Theorem 3.12
.

The locus
V_{f}(\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{E,g})
is pure of dimension
g-2+f-f_{E}
, for any
g\geq 2
and
f_{E}\leq f\leq g-1+f_{E}
.

Proof.

First, observe that
V_{f}(\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{E,g})
is non-empty for any
f_{E}\leq f\leq g-1+f_{E}
. For
g=2
, this follows from Lemma
3.6
, and for
g\geq 3
, it follows from Lemmas
3.5
and
3.6
, which establish that
V_{f}(\Delta_{2,g-2})\subset V_{f}(\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{E,g})
is non-empty.

We follow the strategy of the proof of Theorem
[
FvdG04
, Theorem 2.3]
. Let
W=W_{f}
be a component of the
p
-rank
\leq f
locus
V_{f}(\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{E,g})
for a fixed
f
with
f_{E}\leq f\leq g-1+f_{E}
. Let
W_{f+1}
be a component of
V_{f+1}(\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{E,g})
such that
W_{f}\subseteq W_{f+1}
. This gives us a sequence

W_{f}\subseteq W_{f+1}\subseteq\ldots\subseteq W_{g-2+f_{E}}\subseteq W_{g-1+f_{E}}.

Note that
W_{g-1+f_{E}}=\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{E,g}
and
\dim W_{g-1+f_{E}}=2g-3
by Lemma
3.1
. By Lemma
3.4
, if
W_{i}\neq W_{i+1}
, for
f\leq i\leq g-2+f_{E}
, then
W_{i}
has codimension
1
in
W_{i+1}
. This implies that the codimension of
W=W_{f}
in
\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{E,g}=W_{g-1+f_{E}}
is at most
g-1+f_{E}-f
, or equivalently, that

\dim W\geq g-2+f-f_{E}.

To establish an upper bound on the dimension of
W
, we use induction on
g
with the base case
g=2
covered by Lemma
3.6
. The inductive hypothesis assumes that for all
2\leq g^{\prime}<g
and
1\leq f^{\prime}\leq f
,
V_{f^{\prime}}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{E,g^{\prime}})
is pure of dimension
g^{\prime}-2+f^{\prime}-f_{E}
. Since
\mathcal{B}_{E,g}
cannot contain complete subvarieties (see Lemma
3.2
) it follows that
W\cap\Delta\neq\varnothing
, where
\Delta=\Delta_{0}\cup\Delta_{1}\cup\ldots\cup\Delta_{\lfloor g/2\rfloor}
is the boundary divisor of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
.

In the case
f_{E}=1
, we find that the underlying curve of the generic point of
W
is smooth by comparing dimensions. Therefore,
W\cap\Delta_{i}\neq\varnothing
for some
0\leq i\leq\lfloor\frac{g}{2}\rfloor
. In Lemmas
3.10
and
3.11
, we compute the dimensions of the loci of curves in
\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{E,g}-\mathcal{B}_{E,g}
, under the assumption given by the inductive hypothesis, resulting in
\dim(W\cap\Delta_{i})\leq g-4+f
for any
0\leq i\leq\lfloor\frac{g}{2}\rfloor
. Since
\Delta_{i}
has codimension
1
in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
and
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
is a smooth stack, every component of
W\cap\Delta_{i}
has codimension at most
1
in
W
(see
[
Vis89
, page 614]
), and we get the following inequality:

\dim W\leq\dim(W\cap\Delta_{i})+1=g-3+f.

(3)

Consequently, we conclude that
\dim W\leq g-3+f=g-2+f-f_{E}
.

For
g\geq 3
and
f_{E}=0
, the situation requires more careful consideration. The inductive hypothesis gives
\dim V_{f}(\delta_{1,g-2})=g-2+f
, making it impossible to apply the previous argument directly. A stronger inequality than (
3
) is needed. If
W
consists entirely of isomorphism classes of singular curves, by comparing dimensions it has to be a component of

V_{f}(\delta_{1,g-2})
, yielding

\dim(W)=g-2+f=g-2+f-2f_{E}.

In the remaining case, the generic point of
W
is represented by a smooth curve. That means that there is a component
\Gamma
of
V_{f}(\mathcal{B}_{E,g})\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{g}
such that
W=\overline{\Gamma}\subseteq\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
. In particular, note that
\dim\Gamma=\dim W\geq g-2+f\geq 1
, and that
W\cap\Delta\neq\varnothing
because of Lemma
3.2
.
Moreover, since
\Gamma\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{g}
, it follows that
\dim\Gamma=\dim j(\Gamma)
, which consequently implies that
\dim W=\dim j(W)
, while
W\cap\Delta\neq\varnothing
implies that
\dim j(W\cap\Delta)\geq 0
.
Let us prove the following inequality

\dim W\leq\dim j(W\cap\Delta)+1,

(4)

which will give us the desired upper bound on
W
; we do this using a similar argument as in
[
Oor74
, Section 1]
.
Assume that
\dim j(W)\geq\dim j(W\cap\Delta)+2
. In a projective embedding of
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{g}
, we could then intersect
j(W)
with
(\dim j(W)-1)
-hyperplanes in general position to get a complete
1
-dimensional subvariety
X
that does not meet
j(W\cap\Delta)
. This
X
would be a subvariety of
j(W)-j(W\cap\Delta)=j(\Gamma)
, whose preimage in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
would thus be a complete
1
-dimensional subvariety of
\Gamma
. This is impossible since
\mathcal{B}_{E,g}
and
\Gamma
cannot contain such subvarieties by Lemma
3.2
. Therefore, the inequality (
4
) holds.
Note that
\dim j(V_{f}(\delta_{1,g-2}))=g-3+f
since the Torelli morphism ”forgets” the pair
q_{1},q_{2}\in H
of points in the same orbit of hyperelliptic involution as in (
1
) of the proof of Lemma
3.10
. Combining this with Lemmas
3.10
and
3.11
, we conclude that
\dim j(W\cap\Delta)\leq g-3+f
, which implies
\dim W\leq g-2+f
. This finishes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.13

3.3
The
p
-rank stratification of the locus of bielliptic curves
\mathcal{B}_{g}

Recall that a smooth curve
D
of genus
g\geq 2
is bielliptic if there exists an elliptic curve
E
and a double cover
D\to E
. The locus of bielliptic curves,
\mathcal{B}_{g}
, satisfies
\mathcal{B}_{E,g}\subset\mathcal{B}_{g}
for any elliptic curve
E
, and
\dim\mathcal{B}_{g}=2g-2
.
To describe the closure of
\mathcal{B}_{g}
in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
, we proceed similarly to the construction in Section
2
. Namely, one first considers the locus
\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{g}^{\rm adm}
of admissible stable bielliptic curves. This is a proper Deligne-Mumford stack in characteristic
p\neq 2
equipped with morphisms
\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{g}^{\rm adm}\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,2g-2}
and
\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{g}^{\rm adm}\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{1,2g-2}
which, respectively, extract the source curve and the target curve.
By
[
SvZ20
, Theorem 3.7]
, they induce well-defined proper morphisms

\Psi:\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{g}^{\rm adm}\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}\quad\text{ and }\quad\Phi:\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{g}^{\rm adm}\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{1,1}

obtained by forgetting the marked points and stabilizing. The locus of the admissible smooth bielliptic curves
\mathcal{B}_{g}^{\rm adm}
is dense in
\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{g}^{\rm adm}
and
\Psi:\mathcal{B}_{g}^{\rm adm}\to\mathcal{B}_{g}
is a quasi-finite morphism obtained only by forgetting the admissible structure. It follows that

\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{g}=\Psi(\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{g}^{\rm adm})\subseteq\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g},

the locus of stable bielliptic curves of genus
g
, is the closure of
\mathcal{B}_{g}
in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
; see also Remark

We take the branching locus of the cover

Where

5.2
Explicit one-parameter families in

with

As we see from (

with smaller

-rank, it suffices to find hyperelliptic curves

with small

.
The authors of
[

]
considered curves of the form

under the condition that both

are prime. Furthermore, assuming

, they showed that such curves have

-rank zero. We consider curves of a similar form, without requiring

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
n\geq 3
and
t\in k^{*}=k-\{0\}
, and let
H
denote the curve given by
H:z^{2}=x^{n}-t^{n}
. If
p\nmid n
and

, then
H
is not ordinary, i.e.,
f_{H}\leq g_{H}-1
. Moreover, if
p\equiv-1\pmod{n}
, then
H
is superspecial, so in particular
f_{H}=0
.

Proof.

For
H:z^{2}=x^{n}-t^{n}
, its genus is
g_{H}=\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil-1
. Let
M
be the Hasse-Witt matrix of
H
. Note that
H
is ordinary if and only if the
p
-rank of

is
g_{H}
, which is if and only if the rank of

is
g_{H}
.

It is known that
H^{1}(H,\mathcal{O}_{H})
has basis given by
\frac{z}{x^{k}}
for
1\leq k\leq g_{H}
.
Note that
\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
acts on
H
, where
1\in\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
acts by sending
(x,z)
to
(\zeta_{n}x,z)
. Since
p\nmid n
, this action leads to the decomposition

H^{1}(H,\mathcal{O}_{H})=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{n-1}H^{1}(H,\mathcal{O}_{H})_{i}.

Here,
H^{1}(H,\mathcal{O}_{H})_{i}
is the eigenspace where
1\in\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
acts via scalar multiplication by
\zeta_{n}^{i}
.

By the computation in
[
Bou01
, §5]
,

H^{1}(H,\mathcal{O}_{H})_{i}=\begin{cases}\left\langle\frac{z}{x^{n-i}}\right\rangle&\text{if $n-g_{H}\leq i\leq n-1$}\\
0&\text{if $0\leq i\leq n-g_{H}-1$}\end{cases}

Moreover,
M
maps
H^{1}(H,\mathcal{O}_{H})_{i}
to
H^{1}(H,\mathcal{O}_{H})_{pi\pmod{n}}
. This means the rank of
M
equals
\#S_{1}
, where
S_{1}
is the following set:

S_{1}=\{n-g_{H}\leq i\leq n-1\mid n-g_{H}\leq pi\pmod{n}\leq n-1\}.

Clearly, it has the same size as

S_{2}=\{1\leq i\leq g_{H}\mid 1\leq pi\pmod{n}\leq g_{H}\}.

Note that
H
is ordinary if and only if
S_{2}=\{1,2,\dots,g_{H}\}
.
Let
a
be the congruence class of
p
modulo
n
. The assumption

implies

. Depending on the value of

, we have the following cases:

1.

If
g_{H}+1\leq a\leq n-1
: then
1\notin S_{2}
, so
H
is not ordinary.

2.

If
2\leq a\leq g_{H}
: Let
i=\lceil\frac{g_{H}+1}{a}\rceil
, so
1\leq i\leq g_{H}
. Moreover, observe

g_{H}+1\leq ai<g_{H}+1+a\leq 2g_{H}+1\leq n.

This implies that
i\notin S_{2}
, so
H
is not ordinary.

In both cases,
H
is not ordinary.
In addition, if
p\equiv-1\pmod{n}
, then
S_{2}=\varnothing
. Therefore,
M
has rank
0
and hence the
p
-rank of

is also
0
.
∎

Remark 5.3
.

For the curves

Now, we can can construct

Proposition 5.4

The geometry of

In this appendix, we cover some of the more technical details of the geometry of

and its marked versions

A.1
Stable maps to Deligne-Mumford stacks

We will define
M_{E,g;n}
, which parametrizes genus-
g
double covers
\pi:D\to E
with
n
marked points of
E
above which
\pi
is unramified. We will define
\overline{M}_{E,g;n}
as a closed substack of the moduli stack of (twisted) stable maps to

and then define

as the open substack where the source curve is smooth. Before defining our specific moduli space, we take some time to recall the definition and basic properties of moduli stacks of stable maps to Deligne-Mumford stacks, as studied in
[

]
and
[
ACV03
]
. Recall that a Deligne-Mumford stack
\mathcal{X}
over a base

is

are invertible in

.

Definition A.1

(Variant of
[
AV02
, Definition 4.1.2]
)
.

A
twisted nodal
n
-pointed curve over a scheme

is a collection of data
(\mathcal{C}\to C\to S,\sigma_{i})
, where

(1)

\mathcal{C}
is a tame Deligne-Mumford stack, proper over
S
, which is étale locally a nodal curve over
S
;

(2)

For
1\leq i\leq n
,
\sigma_{i}
is a section

with image in the smooth locus of

;

(3)

The preimages of
\sigma_{i}(S)

are étale gerbes over

\sigma_{i}(S)
are locally isomorphic to

for some

;

(4)

The morphism
\mathcal{C}\to C
exhibits
C
as the coarse moduli scheme of
\mathcal{C}
;

(5)

Outside the images of the
\sigma_{i}
, the map

(6)

Above the nodes,

\left[\frac{{\rm Spec}(A[x,y]/(xy-t))}{\mu_{r}}\right]\to{\rm Spec}(A),

Remark A.2
.

This differs from

slightly in that we require the stacky nodes to be balanced (6). Because we are trying to compactify moduli spaces

of smooth double covers, we are only interested in admissible covers that can deform to smooth covers, and admissible covers of balanced twisted curves are precisely the smoothable covers. Considering only balanced twisted curves is not an issue for representability of our moduli stacks because having balanced nodes is an open and closed condition.

By
[

, Proposition 4.2.2]
, the 2-category of twisted nodal

-pointed curves is equivalent to a 1-category, so it makes sense to talk about morphisms of twisted curves without having to worry about 2-morphisms.

(

Remark A.6

A.3
Deformation theory of
\overline{M}_{E,g;n}

In this section, we show that
\overline{M}_{E,g;n}
is Deligne-Mumford and smooth and calculate its dimension.

Proposition A.10
.

\overline{M}_{E,g;n}
is a proper Deligne-Mumford stack.

Proof.

It suffices to show that
\widetilde{M}_{E,g;n}
is a proper Deligne-Mumford stack. By Theorem
A.4
,
\widetilde{M}_{E,g;n}
is a proper algebraic stack. Moreover, by Theorem
A.4
, to show that
\widetilde{M}_{E,g;n}
is Deligne-Mumford, it suffices to show that
\mathcal{K}_{1,2g-2+n}^{\text{bal}}(E,1)
is Deligne-Mumford.

Let
(C\to{\rm Spec}(k),\Sigma_{{\rm br}}\cup\Sigma_{{\rm ub}},f:C\to E)
be a stable map corresponding to an object of
\mathcal{K}_{1,2g-2+n}^{\text{bal}}(E,1)(k)
. We want to show that this object has no nontrivial infinitesimal automorphisms. If
C
has more than one component, then it is stable, and
C
automatically has no nontrivial infinitesimal automorphisms. Otherwise,
C\cong E
, and only the trivial infinitesimal automorphism can preserve the isomorphism
f
. Thus,
\mathcal{K}_{1,2g-2+n}^{\text{bal}}(E,1)
has no infinitesimal automorphisms and is Deligne-Mumford.
∎

We use the deformation-obstruction theory for stable maps in
[
AV02
, §5.3]
to prove that
\overline{M}_{E,g;n}
is smooth and compute its dimension. Throughout the following proof, if
f:X\to Y
is a map, we will use
\mathbb{L}_{X/Y}
to denote the relative cotangent complex. If
Y={\rm Spec}(k)
, we will just write
\mathbb{L}_{X}
.

Theorem A.11
.

\overline{M}_{E,g;n}
is smooth of dimension
2g-2+n
.

Proof.

Obstructions:
First, we show that
\overline{M}_{E,g;n}
is smooth by showing the obstruction groups vanish. Let
A^{\prime}\to A
be a small extension of local Artinian
k
-algebras with
I\coloneqq\mathrm{ker}(A^{\prime}\to A)
, and let
(\mathcal{C}\to{\rm Spec}(A),\Sigma_{{\rm br}}\cup\Sigma_{{\rm ub}},f:\mathcal{C}\to E\times B\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})
be an object of
\overline{M}_{E,g;n}(A)
. Moreover, define
\mathcal{C}_{0}\coloneqq\mathcal{C}\times_{{\rm Spec}(A)}{\rm Spec}(k)
. By
[
AV02
, Lemma 5.3.3]
, the obstruction to lifting this object to
A^{\prime}
lies in
{\rm Ext}_{\mathcal{O}_{C}}^{2}(\mathbb{L}_{\mathcal{C}/E\times B\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}_{0}})\otimes_{k}I\cong{\rm Ext}_{\mathcal{O}_{C_{0}}}^{2}(\mathbb{L}_{\mathcal{C}_{0}/E\times B\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}_{0}})\otimes_{k}I
. For brevity, we will write
{\rm Ext}
to mean
{\rm Ext}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}_{0}}}
for the rest of this proof.

We claim that
{\rm Ext}^{2}(\mathbb{L}_{\mathcal{C}_{0}/E\times B\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}_{0}})\cong 0
. Because the projection
E\times B\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\to E
is étale, we have
\mathbb{L}_{\mathcal{C}_{0}/E\times B\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}}\cong\mathbb{L}_{\mathcal{C}_{0}/E}
. By an abuse of notation, we will use
f
to denote the map
\mathcal{C}_{0}\to E
. There is a distinguished triangle of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}_{0}}
-modules:

f^{*}\mathbb{L}_{E}\to\mathbb{L}_{\mathcal{C}_{0}}\to\mathbb{L}_{\mathcal{C}_{0}/E}.

Because
E
is smooth, we have
\mathbb{L}_{E}\simeq\Omega_{E}
. Because
\mathcal{C}_{0}
is nodal, a local computation shows that
\mathbb{L}_{\mathcal{C}_{0}}
simplifies to
\Omega_{\mathcal{C}_{0}}
. Thus, we can simplify the first two terms to get

f^{*}\Omega_{E}\to\Omega_{\mathcal{C}_{0}}\to\mathbb{L}_{\mathcal{C}_{0}/E}.

This distinguished triangle gives us an exact sequence

{\rm Ext}^{1}(\Omega_{\mathcal{C}_{0}},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}_{0}})\to{\rm Ext}^{1}(f^{*}\Omega_{E},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}_{0}})\to{\rm Ext}^{2}(\mathbb{L}_{\mathcal{C}_{0}/E},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}_{0}})\to{\rm Ext}^{2}(\Omega_{\mathcal{C}_{0}},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}_{0}}),

so it suffices to show that
{\rm Ext}^{2}(\Omega_{\mathcal{C}_{0}},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}_{0}})\cong 0
and that
{\rm Ext}^{1}(\Omega_{\mathcal{C}_{0}},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}_{0}})\to{\rm Ext}^{1}(f^{*}\Omega_{E},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}_{0}})
is surjective:

\cdot

{\rm Ext}^{2}(\Omega_{\mathcal{C}_{0}},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}_{0}})\cong 0
: This is shown in
[
ACV03
, §3.0.3]
. Alternatively, this follows from Serre duality
[
Nir08
, Corollary 2.30]
for the stacky curve
\mathcal{C}_{0}
because the
{\rm Ext}^{2}
is dual to an
H^{-1}
group, which is necessarily zero.

\cdot

{\rm Ext}^{1}(\Omega_{\mathcal{C}_{0}},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}_{0}})\to{\rm Ext}^{1}(f^{*}\Omega_{E},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}_{0}})
is surjective: Let
\omega_{\mathcal{C}_{0}}
be the dualizing sheaf of
\mathcal{C}_{0}
in the sense of
[
Nir08
]
. By Serre duality
[
Nir08
, Corollary 2.30]
, the map of
{\rm Ext}
groups is identified with the map
H^{0}(\mathcal{C}_{0},\Omega_{\mathcal{C}_{0}}\otimes\omega_{\mathcal{C}_{0}})^{\vee}\to H^{0}(\mathcal{C}_{0},f^{*}\Omega_{E}\otimes\omega_{\mathcal{C}_{0}})^{\vee}
.

To prove the surjectivity of the map in the statement, it suffices to prove that the map

\psi:H^{0}(\mathcal{C}_{0},f^{*}\Omega_{E}\otimes\omega_{\mathcal{C}_{0}})\to H^{0}(\mathcal{C}_{0},\Omega_{\mathcal{C}_{0}}\otimes\omega_{\mathcal{C}_{0}})

is injective. Let
s\in H^{0}(\mathcal{C}_{0},f^{*}\Omega_{E}\otimes\omega_{\mathcal{C}_{0}})
be a nonzero section. Because the dualizing sheaf is étale local
[
Nir08
, Lemma 2.12]
, we can identify
\omega_{\mathcal{C}_{0}}
with the sheaf of 1-forms with logarithmic singularities whose residues at both components of each node add to 0. In particular, any section of
f^{*}\Omega_{E}\otimes\omega_{\mathcal{C}_{0}}
whose restriction to some component is nonzero must be nonzero on each component. This means that
s
must be nonzero when restricted to the component of
\mathcal{C}_{0}
whose coarse space is identified with
E
. On this component, the map
f^{*}\Omega_{E}\to\Omega_{\mathcal{C}_{0}}
is generically an isomorphism and hence injective, which means that the image
\psi(s)
is nonzero. We conclude that
\psi
is injective and that
{\rm Ext}^{1}(\Omega_{\mathcal{C}_{0}},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}_{0}})\to{\rm Ext}^{1}(f^{*}\Omega_{E},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}_{0}})
is surjective.

Having shown that
{\rm Ext}^{2}(\mathbb{L}_{\mathcal{C}_{0}/E\times B\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}_{0}})\cong 0
, we deduce that
\overline{M}_{E,g;n}
is smooth.

Deformations:

We compute the dimension of
\overline{M}_{E,g;n}
by computing the dimension of tangent space at every point. Let
(\mathcal{C}\to{\rm Spec}(k),\Sigma,f:\mathcal{C}\to E\times B\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})
be a object of
\overline{M}_{E,g;n}(k)
. Then by
[
AV02
, Lemma 5.3.2]
, the tangent space
T
of
\overline{M}_{E,g;n}
at the point representing this object sits in the following exact sequence of vector spaces:

0\to\text{Hom}(\mathbb{L}_{\mathcal{C}/E\times B\mathbb{Z}/2},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}})\to H^{0}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{N}_{\Sigma})\to T\to{\rm Ext}^{1}(\mathbb{L}_{\mathcal{C}/E\times B\mathbb{Z}/2},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}})\to 0.

The exact sequence stated in
[
AV02
, Lemma 5.3.2]
actually only gives us the above sequence from the second term
\text{Hom}(\mathbb{L}_{\mathcal{C}/E\times B\mathbb{Z}/2},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}})
onwards. However, as in
[
ACV03
, §3.0.4]
, we extend this exact sequence so that the first term is the infinitesimal automorphism group of the data
(\mathcal{C}\to{\rm Spec}(k),\Sigma,f:\mathcal{C}\to E\times B\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})
. By stability, this infinitesimal automorphism group vanishes, so we get the above exact sequence starting with 0.

First notice that
\mathcal{N}_{\Sigma}=\oplus_{i}\mathcal{N}_{\Sigma_{i}}
, where
\mathcal{N}_{\Sigma_{i}}
is the normal sheaf at the
i^{th}
marked point. By
[
ACV03
, 3.0.4]
, at each
\sigma_{i}
where the marked is a branching point, then
H^{0}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{N}_{\Sigma_{i}})=0
. At each
\tau_{j}
where the marked point is not a branching point, we have
H^{0}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{N}_{\Sigma_{j}})\cong k
. Therefore, we obtain
H^{0}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{N}_{\Sigma})\cong k^{n}
.

Next, since
E\times B\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\to E
is étale, we have
\mathbb{L}_{\mathcal{C}/E\times B\mathbb{Z}/2}\cong\mathbb{L}_{\mathcal{C}/E}
. We also have the distinguished triangle of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}
-modules:

f^{*}\Omega_{E}\to\Omega_{\mathcal{C}}\to\mathbb{L}_{\mathcal{C}/E}.

This gives the long exact sequence

\begin{split}&0\to\text{Hom}(\mathbb{L}_{\mathcal{C}/E},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}})\to\text{Hom}(\Omega_{\mathcal{C}},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}})\to\text{Hom}(f^{*}\Omega_{E},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}})\\
&\quad\to{\rm Ext}^{1}(\mathbb{L}_{\mathcal{C}/E},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}})\to{\rm Ext}^{1}(\Omega_{\mathcal{C}},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}})\to{\rm Ext}^{1}(f^{*}\Omega_{E},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}})\to 0.\end{split}

(9)

As above, we use
{\rm Ext}^{2}(\mathbb{L}_{\mathcal{C}/E},\mathcal{O}_{C})=0
. Hence, the dimension of
T
is

\begin{split}\dim(T)&=n-\dim(\text{Hom}(\mathbb{L}_{\mathcal{C}/E},\mathcal{O}_{C}))+\dim({\rm Ext}^{1}(\mathbb{L}_{\mathcal{C}/E},\mathcal{O}_{C}))\\
&=n-\dim(\text{Hom}(\Omega_{\mathcal{C}},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}))+\dim({\rm Ext}^{1}(\Omega_{\mathcal{C}},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}))+\dim(\text{Hom}(f^{*}\Omega_{E},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}))-\dim({\rm Ext}^{1}(f^{*}\Omega_{E},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}})),\end{split}

by the exact sequence (
9
). Since
\Omega_{E}
is trivial and
C
has genus
1
, we observe

\displaystyle\text{Hom}(f^{*}\Omega_{E},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}})

\displaystyle\cong H^{0}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}})=H^{0}(C,\mathcal{O}_{C})\cong k

\displaystyle{\rm Ext}^{1}(f^{*}\Omega_{E},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}})

\displaystyle\cong H^{1}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}})=H^{1}(C,\mathcal{O}_{C})\cong k.

By
[
ACV03
, §3.0.4]
, we have

{\rm Ext}^{i}(\Omega_{\mathcal{C}},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}})=H^{1-i}(\mathcal{C},\Omega_{\mathcal{C}}\otimes\omega_{\mathcal{C}})^{\raise 2.71246pt\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\vee$}}=H^{1-i}(C,\pi_{*}(\Omega_{\mathcal{C}}\otimes\omega_{\mathcal{C}}))^{\raise 2.71246pt\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\vee$}}
, where
\pi:\mathcal{C}\to C
is the coarse moduli space map.
Therefore, we have

\dim(\text{Hom}(\Omega_{\mathcal{C}},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}))-\dim({\rm Ext}^{1}(\Omega_{\mathcal{C}},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}))=-\chi(\pi_{*}(\Omega_{\mathcal{C}}\otimes\omega_{\mathcal{C}})).

Since all of our nodes are balanced, we have
\pi_{*}(\Omega_{\mathcal{C}}\otimes\omega_{\mathcal{C}})=\Omega_{C}\otimes\omega_{C}(\Sigma_{\rm br})
as in
[
ACV03
, §3.0.4]
, where

is summing over all the smooth branched markings

.
Therefore,

Hence,
\dim(T)=n+2g-2

∎

Remark A.12
.

One might wonder to what extent it is possible to generalize this theorem, by replacing

Lemma A.13
.

For

,
M_{E,g}
is irreducible. Therefore,
\overline{M}_{E,g}
is irreducible.

Proof.

To show that

.

\cdot

The construction of

: Let
\mathcal{L}
be the universal bundle on

, and consider the projection
\pi:E\times\textup{Pic}^{g-1}(E)\to\textup{Pic}^{g-1}(E)
. Let
\mathcal{V}\coloneqq\pi_{*}\mathcal{L}^{\otimes 2}
. Because
2g-2>0
and
H^{1}(E,\mathcal{M})\cong 0
for any positive degree line bundle
\mathcal{M}

\mathcal{V}
is a locally constant sheaf on
\textup{Pic}^{g-1}(E)

By abuse of notation, let
\mathcal{V}

\cdot

The map
\phi:X\to M_{E,g}

can be defined by specifying a stable map
(\mathcal{C}\to X,\Sigma,f:\mathcal{C}\to E\times B\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})
such that

is degree 1, all the automorphism groups of
\mathcal{C}
above

\mathcal{C}\to X

s:E\times X\to\left(\mathcal{L}|_{E\times X}\right)^{\otimes 2}
, where
\mathcal{L}|_{E\times X}
is the pullback of
\mathcal{L}
along the map

as the preimage of

along the squaring map
\mathcal{L}|_{E\times X}\to\left(\mathcal{L}|_{E\times X}\right)^{\otimes 2}
. We then let
\mathcal{C}\coloneqq[D/(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})]
, where
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
acts on

by multiplication by
\pm 1
. The projection
D\to E\times X
exhibits
E\times X
as the coarse quotient
C=D/(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})
. Let
\Sigma\subset E\times X
be the branch divisor of the double cover

, which is the same as the vanishing locus of the universal section

. Because of the requirement that sections

corresponding to points

have reduced vanishing loci,
\Sigma\to X
is étale. Checking that
(\mathcal{C}\to C\to X,\Sigma)
is a smooth twisted

-pointed curve can be done using the description of

as the quotient
[D/(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})]
and the fact that

is étale, as these facts ensure that we have the local description we want for Definition

.

We define
f:\mathcal{C}\to E\times B\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
by letting
\mathcal{C}\to E
be the projection
\mathcal{C}\to C=E\times X\to E
and letting
\mathcal{C}\to B\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}

. The fact that

is representable follows from the fact that

is a scheme. The map on coarse spaces

is just the projection

, so it is a stable map of degree 1. The last thing we have to check is that the points in
\mathcal{C}
above
\Sigma
have stabilizer
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
. This is true because the preimage of
\Sigma

is precisely the intersection of

\mathcal{L}|_{E\times X}
, which is stabilized by the

-action on

. We thus have a twisted stable map
(\mathcal{C}\to X,\Sigma,f:\mathcal{C}\to E\times B\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})\in M_{E,g}

.

\cdot

\phi
is surjective:
This follows from the well-known fact that double covers of a curve can all be constructed as the square root of a section of a line bundle as above, which we will explain briefly below. In the following, we reset the notation we used earlier in the proof.
Let
(\mathcal{C}\to{\rm Spec}(k),\Sigma,f:\mathcal{C}\to E\times B\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})

. Then
\mathcal{C}
has coarse space identified with

by

, and

, where

\mathcal{C}\to B\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}

can be produced using the square root construction above.

\mathcal{M}\coloneqq(h_{*}\mathcal{O}_{D}/\mathcal{O}_{E})^{\vee}

of the exact sequence

-algebras
{\rm Sym}_{\mathcal{O}_{E}}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{M}^{\vee})\to h_{*}\mathcal{O}_{D}
, which provides a closed embedding
D\xhookrightarrow{}\mathcal{M}
.
The ideal
\ker\left({\rm Sym}_{\mathcal{O}_{E}}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{M}^{\vee})\to h_{*}\mathcal{O}_{D}\right)
is preserved by the
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
-action acting by
-1
on
\mathcal{M}^{\vee}
, so
D
is preserved by the
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
-action. This means that its image under the squaring map
\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}^{\otimes 2}
maps by degree 1 to
E
and is thus isomorphic to
E
.
Thus, we have a section
r:E\to\mathcal{M}^{\otimes 2}
whose square root gives us the double cover
D\to E
. Because
D
is smooth, the vanishing locus of
r
must be reduced, and we know that a point
(\mathcal{M},r)\in X
maps to our point
(\mathcal{C}\to{\rm Spec}(k),\Sigma,f:\mathcal{C}\to E\times B\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})
of
M_{E,g}
. We conclude that
h
is surjective and that
M_{E,g}
is irreducible.

∎

Corollary A.14
.

\overline{M}_{E,g;n}
is connected. Consequently,
\overline{M}_{E,g;n}
is irreducible.

Proof.

We show that
M_{E,g;n}
is connected. Since it is smooth, this implies that it is irreducible.

We consider the forgetful morphism
\pi:M_{E,g;n}\to M_{E,g}
. The fiber is the configuration space, which is connected. If we let
\Delta
denote the weak diagonal in
E^{n}
, and let
R
be the branch locus of the fixed double cover, then the fiber over the double cover is
((E-R)^{n}-\Delta)/S_{n}
, which is connected.
Also, the image of
\pi
is
M_{E,g}
, which is connected. Therefore,
M_{E,g;n}
is connected, hence irreducible. By dimension, since
M_{E,g;n}
is dense in
\overline{M}_{E,g;n}
, it follows that
\overline{M}_{E,g;n}
is connected, hence irreducible.
∎

Proposition A.15
.

We have the following morphism:

\overline{M}_{E,g}^{{\rm red}}\xrightarrow{\varphi}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,2g-2}\xrightarrow{\pi}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}

The first map
\varphi
sends a cover
D\to C
to its source curve
D
, with the
2g-2
ramification points as marked points. The second map
\pi
forgets the marked points and stabilizes the curve. Then
\pi\circ\varphi
is quasi-finite on the smooth locus of
\overline{M}^{{\rm red}}_{E,g}
and generically finite on each boundary component of
\overline{M}^{{\rm red}}_{E,g}
.

Proof.

Observe that
\pi\circ\varphi
factors through
[\overline{M}_{E,g}/{\rm Aut}(E)]
, and we already know that the map from
\overline{M}_{E,g}^{{\rm red}}
to
[\overline{M}_{E,g}/{\rm Aut}(E)]
is finite. Therefore, it is enough to show the statement for the map from
[\overline{M}_{E,g}/{\rm Aut}(E)]
to
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
. Hence, we want to show that the forgetful map has finite fibers on the smooth locus and generically on each boundary component. Let
g\geq 2
.

1.

We first show quasifiniteness on the smooth locus. We know that
[M_{E,g}/{\rm Aut}(E)]
parametrizes double covers
D\xrightarrow{\eta}E
such that
D
has genus
g
. Moreover,
D_{1}\xrightarrow{\eta_{1}}E
and
D_{2}\xrightarrow{\eta_{2}}E
correspond to the same point of
[M_{E,g}/{\rm Aut}(E)]
if there are isomorphisms
f_{1}:D_{1}\to D_{2}
and
f_{2}:E\to E
such that
\eta_{1}=f_{2}\circ\eta_{2}\circ f_{1}
.

Consider a curve
D
in the image under
\pi\circ\varphi
of the smooth locus of
\overline{M}^{{\rm red}}_{E,g}
, and let
\eta:D\to E
be the data of one of the preimages. We will show that it has only finitely many preimages in
[M_{E,g}/{\rm Aut}(E)]
. First, note that each double cover
\eta:D\to E
gives us an element
\sigma\in{\rm Aut}(D)
which is of order
2
. If we let
\eta^{*}
and
\sigma^{*}
to be the induced map on the function field
K(E)
and
K(D)
respectively, then we have that
\eta^{*}(K(E))=K(D)^{\sigma^{*}}
. Since
g\geq 2
, we know that
{\rm Aut}(D)
is a finite group. Therefore, there are finitely many choices for
\sigma
and hence finitely many choices for
\eta^{*}(K(E))
.

Now, we show that
\eta^{*}(K(E))
determines the preimage. Suppose, we have two preimages
D\xrightarrow{\eta_{0}}E
and
D\xrightarrow{\eta_{1}}E
that satisfy
\eta_{0}^{*}(K(E))=\eta_{1}^{*}(K(E))
. Then there is
\tau\in{\rm Aut}(K(E))
, for which
\eta_{1}^{*}\circ\tau=\eta_{0}^{*}
(this
\tau
is obtained from the composition
K(E)\xrightarrow{\eta_{0}^{*}}\eta_{0}^{*}(K(D))\xrightarrow{{\eta_{1}^{*}}^{-1}}K(E)
, which is well defined since
\eta_{0}^{*}(K(E))=\eta_{1}^{*}(K(E))
). Next, there is
f\in{\rm Aut}(E)
for which
f^{*}=\tau
, so
f\circ\eta_{1}=\eta_{0}
. This shows that
D\xrightarrow{\eta_{0}}E
and
D\xrightarrow{\eta_{1}}E
correspond to the same point of
[M_{E,g}/{\rm Aut}(E)]
. We conclude that the forgetful map from
[M_{E,g}/{\rm Aut}(E)]
to
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
is quasi-finite on the smooth locus.

2.

Next, consider a boundary component, either
\Xi_{g_{1},g_{2}-1}
or
\Delta_{g_{1},g_{2}}
. Curves in the generic part of any boundary component are the clutching of two smooth curves, except for one component of
\Xi_{1,g-2}
, when it is the clutching of three smooth curves.
Consider a curve
D\in\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
coming from a generic point of a boundary component. Let
D^{\prime}\xrightarrow{\eta_{0}}C_{0}\xrightarrow{f_{0}}E
be a preimage of
D
, where
C_{0}
is the clutching of
E
with
\mathbb{P}^{1}
(say
P_{0}
of
E
with
Q_{0}
of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
), and
D
is the stabilization of
D^{\prime}
after forgetting the ramification points. Let
D_{a}=\eta_{0}^{-1}(E)
and
D_{b}=\eta_{0}^{-1}(\mathbb{P}^{1})
, so
g(D_{a})=g_{1}
and
g(D_{b})=g_{2}
.
Then
D^{\prime}
is the clutching of
D_{a}
and
D_{b}
, when
\eta_{0}^{-1}(P_{0})
is identified with
\eta_{0}^{-1}(Q_{0})
. Notice that if
g_{2}\geq 1
, then
D=D^{\prime}
.
On the other hand, if
g_{2}=0
, then it must be the case that
(D^{\prime}\to C_{0})\in\Xi_{g-1,0}
. In this case,
D
is
D_{a}
with two points in
\eta_{0}^{-1}(P_{0})
identified.

As in the smooth case, the double covers
\eta_{0}:D_{a}\to E
and
\eta_{0}:D_{b}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
determine elements of order two,
\sigma_{a}\in{\rm Aut}(D_{a})
and
\sigma_{b}\in{\rm Aut}(D_{b})
such that
\eta_{0}^{*}(K(E))=K(D_{a})^{\sigma_{a}^{*}}
and
\eta_{0}^{*}(K(\mathbb{P}^{1}))=K(D_{b})^{\sigma_{b}^{*}}
. For different values of
(g_{1},g_{2})
, we determine the possibilities for
D^{\prime}
and the map
D^{\prime}\to E
.

(a)

If
g_{1},g_{2}\geq 2
. Then, since a curve of genus
\geq 2
has a finite automorphism group, we have only finitely many options for
\sigma_{a}
and
\sigma_{b}
. We show that
\sigma_{a},\sigma_{b}
uniquely determine the map
D^{\prime}\to C_{0}\to E
.

Let
D^{\prime}\xrightarrow{\eta_{1}}C_{1}\xrightarrow{f_{1}}E
be another preimage of
D
under
\pi\circ\varphi
. Suppose that we have
\eta_{0}^{*}(K(E))=\eta_{1}^{*}(K(E))
and
\eta_{0}^{*}(K(\mathbb{P}^{1}))=\eta_{1}^{*}(K(\mathbb{P}^{1}))
. Then, there exist
h_{a}\in{\rm Aut}(E)
and
h_{b}\in{\rm Aut}(\mathbb{P}^{1})
such that
\eta_{1}^{*}\circ h_{a}^{*}=\eta_{0}^{*}
and
\eta_{1}^{*}\circ h_{b}^{*}=\eta_{0}^{*}
, so
h_{a}\circ\eta_{1}=\eta_{0}|_{D_{a}}
and
h_{b}\circ\eta_{1}=\eta_{0}|_{D_{b}}
.
Recall that
D^{\prime}
is the clutching of
D_{a}
and
D_{b}
, when
\eta_{0}^{-1}(P_{0})\subseteq D_{a}
is identified with
\eta_{0}^{-1}(Q_{0})\subseteq D_{b}
. Since
D^{\prime}\xrightarrow{\eta_{1}}C_{1}\xrightarrow{f_{1}}E
is another preimage of
D
, we see that
D
is also the clutching of
D_{a}
and
D_{b}
, when
\eta_{1}^{-1}(P_{1})
is identified with
\eta_{1}^{-1}(Q_{1})
. This means that
\eta_{0}^{-1}(P_{0})=\eta_{1}^{-1}(P_{1})
and
\eta_{0}^{-1}(Q_{0})=\eta_{1}^{-1}(Q_{1})
. We see that
h_{a}(P_{1})=P_{0}
and
h_{b}(Q_{1})=Q_{0}
. Thus the map
h=h_{a}\cup h_{b}
is well defined from
C_{1}
to
C_{0}
, it is an isomorphism and satisfies
\eta_{0}=h\circ\eta_{1}
.

Similarly, note that the map
f_{0}:C_{0}\to E
is the identity on
E
and maps
\mathbb{P}^{1}
to
P_{0}
, similarly the map
f_{1}:C_{1}\to E
is the identity on
E
and maps
\mathbb{P}^{1}
to
P_{1}
. Therefore, we have the following commutative diagram, in which
h
and
h_{a}
are isomorphisms:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C_{0}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C_{1}}

It follows that
D^{\prime}\xrightarrow{\eta_{0}}C_{0}\xrightarrow{f_{0}}E

[\overline{M}_{E,g}/{\rm Aut}(E)]

\sigma_{a}^{*}

\sigma_{b}^{*}

(b)

If
g_{1}\geq 2
and
g_{2}=1
. Then
(D^{\prime}\to C_{0})
must be in
\Delta_{g-1,1}

\Xi_{g-2,1}

Moreover,

and

(d)

g_{1}=1

\eta_{0}:D_{a}\to E

(f)

If
g_{1}=1

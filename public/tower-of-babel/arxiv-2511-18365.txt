1.
Introduction

Let
M
be a complete Riemannian manifold. Denote by
\nabla
and
\Delta
the usual Riemannian gradient and Laplace–Beltrami operator, respectively. The Riesz transform is given by

R=\nabla\Delta^{-1/2}.

First posed by Strichartz
[
36
]
, the question of the
L^{p}
-boundedness of
R
has been investigated for decades. In particular, we say that the Riesz transform is bounded on
L^{p}
(or
L^{p}
-bounded) if
\|R\|_{p\to p}\leq C
, or equivalently if

(
\textrm{R}_{p}
)

\|\nabla f\|_{p}\leq C\|\Delta^{1/2}f\|_{p},\quad\forall f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(M).

We say that the reverse Riesz inequality holds if

(
\textrm{RR}_{p}
)

\|\Delta^{1/2}f\|_{p}\leq C\|\nabla f\|_{p},\quad\forall f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(M).

These problems turn out to be extremely difficult because of their strong dependence on the geometry of the underlying structure. Unlike in the Euclidean case, the Riesz transform problem (

R
p

) is usually divided into two parts. For
p<2
, the question is comparatively well understood. Indeed, under very mild assumptions, Coulhon and Duong
[
7
]
show that
R
is
L^{p}
-bounded for all
1<p\leq 2
and is of weak type
(1,1)
. For
p>2
, gradient estimates (for instance for the heat kernel or for local harmonic functions) turn out to be particularly significant.

Recall the volume doubling condition

(1.1)

V(x,2r)\leq CV(x,r),\quad\forall x\in M,\quad\forall r>0,

where
V(x,r)
denotes the volume of the ball
B(x,r)
, and the
L^{q}
-Poincaré inequality

(
\textrm{P}_{q}
)

\int_{B}|f-f_{B}|^{q}\,d\mu\leq Cr^{q}\int_{B}|\nabla f|^{q}\,d\mu,\quad\forall f\in C^{\infty}(B),\quad\forall B\subset M.

According to
[
2
]
, under the assumptions (
1.1
) and
(\textrm{P}_{2})
, the boundedness of the Riesz transform (

R
p

) is equivalent to the following heat kernel regularity:

\sup_{t>0}\bigl\|\sqrt{t}\,\nabla e^{-t\Delta}\bigr\|_{p\to p}\leq C.

Subsequently, Coulhon–Jiang–Koskela–Sikora and Jiang added a so-called reverse Hölder condition to extend the above equivalence under even weaker assumptions; see
[
9
]
and
[
29
]
, respectively.

However, on general manifolds, both conditions (
1.1
) and (

P
q

) can be violated quite easily. For example, on Lie groups with exponential growth, (
1.1
) fails, and on the connected sum of two Euclidean spaces,
(\textrm{P}_{2})
fails to hold. Nevertheless, in the Lie group with exponential volume growth,
[
25
]
(see also
[
35
]
) implies that the first-order Riesz transform is still bounded in
L^{p}
for all
1<p<\infty
. For the case
\mathbb{R}^{n}\#\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where the Poincaré inequality fails, the result of
[
5
]
(see also
[
7
]
) shows that (

R
p

) holds if and only if
1<p<n
. For more studies of the Riesz transform on perturbed manifolds, we refer the reader to
[
20
,
18
,
6
]
and the references therein.

In this article, however, we will focus on the metric cone. Let
(Y,h)
be a smooth closed Riemannian manifold. The metric cone is defined to be the product
M=(0,\infty)_{r}\times Y
with metric
g=dr^{2}+r^{2}h
. In particular,
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
is a metric cone of dimension
n
with cross section
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. It is known that if
Y
is connected (so that
M
has only one end), then
M
satisfies (
1.1
) and
(\textrm{P}_{1})
(see
[
10
,
13
]
), and its corresponding heat kernel estimates can be found in
[
32
,
10
]
.

The question regarding (

R
p

) was answered by Li in the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

[
31
, Théorème I]

Let
d\geq 3
and let
M
be a metric cone of dimension
d
with cross section
Y
. Then (

R
p

) holds if and only if

(1.2)

1<p<\frac{d}{\max\left(\frac{d}{2}-\mu_{1},0\right)},

where
\mu_{1}>0
is the square root of the second smallest eigenvalue of the operator
\Delta_{Y}+\left(\frac{d-2}{2}\right)^{2}
.

The above result was generalized by Hassell and Lin
[
16
]
by adding an inverse-square potential. Let
V_{0}:Y\to\mathbb{C}
be a smooth function such that

(1.3)

\Delta_{Y}+V_{0}(y)+\left(\frac{d-2}{2}\right)^{2}>0.

Note that this condition allows
V_{0}
to be negative (for example,
V_{0}
may be any constant larger than
-\frac{(d-2)^{2}}{4}
). Consider the Schrödinger operator

H=\Delta+\frac{V_{0}}{r^{2}}.

Observe that
H
is self-adjoint and positive. We can then define the associated Riesz transform by

\nabla H^{-1/2}=\nabla\left(\Delta+\frac{V_{0}}{r^{2}}\right)^{-1/2}.

Theorem 1.2
.

[
16
, Theorem 1.3]

Let
d\geq 3
and let
M
be a metric cone of dimension
d
with cross section
Y
. Let
V_{0}
be a smooth function defined on
Y
that satisfies (
1.3
) and
V_{0}\not\equiv 0
. Then the Riesz transform with potential
V=\frac{V_{0}}{r^{2}}
is bounded on
L^{p}
if and only if
p
lies in the interval

(1.4)

\left(\frac{d}{\min\left(\frac{d+2}{2}+\mu_{0},d\right)},\frac{d}{\max\left(\frac{d}{2}-\mu_{0},0\right)}\right),

where
\mu_{0}
is the square root of the smallest eigenvalue of the operator
\Delta_{Y}+V_{0}+\left(\frac{d-2}{2}\right)^{2}
.

That is, the operator
\nabla H^{-1/2}=\nabla\left(\Delta+V\right)^{-1/2}
is
L^{p}
-bounded if and only if
p
belongs to the interval (
1.4
).

The “only if” part of the above result, i.e., the range of unboundedness of
\nabla H^{-1/2}
, inherits ideas from Guillarmou and Hassell
[
14
]
, who consider the Riesz transform for Schrödinger operators with no zero modes and no zero resonances on asymptotically conic manifolds and show that
\nabla H^{-1/2}
cannot be bounded for
p
outside the range (
1.4
). The case with zero modes or zero resonances is treated in
[
15
]
.

The study of the Riesz transform can also be carried out from the point of view of Ricci curvature. Indeed, in
[
4
]
, Bakry proved that on manifolds with non-negative Ricci curvature, (

R
p

) holds for all
1<p<\infty
. In fact, manifolds with conical ends and the connected sum of finitely many Euclidean spaces both satisfy a quadratic lower bound for the Ricci curvature:

(1.5)

\mathrm{Ric}_{x}\geq-\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{\left(1+r(x)\right)^{2}}g_{x},\quad\forall x\in M,

where
r(x)=d(o,x)
for some fixed point
o\in M
. In
[
6
]
, Carron studied manifolds satisfying the condition (
1.5
), and gave a characterization of (

R
p

) in terms of a lower bound for volume growth, which we refer to as the reverse doubling condition:

(1.6)

\frac{V(x,R)}{V(x,r)}\geq C\left(\frac{R}{r}\right)^{\nu},\quad\nu>2.

In particular, his result can be applied to the cases studied in
[
31
,
5
]
. Precisely, on a metric cone
M
, we have volume estimate
V(x,r)\sim r^{d}
(hence (
1.6
) holds with
\nu=d
). Moreover, it is clear that the cone metric implies (
1.5
). Then,
[
6
, Theorem A]
guarantees that (

R
p

) holds on
M
for
1<p<d
. Note that this result is sharp if
M
has multiple ends.

In this note, we continue the study of the Riesz transform for Schrödinger operators on metric cones. We also consider a slightly modified, “left-lifted” version of the Riesz transform. For
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
, define

\nabla_{H}f:=(\nabla f,f/r),

viewed as a section of the product bundle
\Lambda^{1}(M)\times\Lambda^{0}(M)
, and set

|\nabla_{H}f|:=\sqrt{|\nabla f|^{2}+\left|f/r\right|^{2}}.

For
1\leq p\leq\infty
, we define the seminorm

(1.7)

\|\nabla_{H}f\|_{p}^{p}:=\int_{M}\left(|\nabla f|^{2}+\left|f/r\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}d\mu\sim\|\nabla f\|_{p}^{p}+\left\|f/r\right\|_{p}^{p},

and we say that the Riesz transform for the Schrödinger operator is bounded on
L^{p}
if

(
\textrm{RV}_{p}
)

\|\nabla_{H}f\|_{p}\leq C\|H^{1/2}f\|_{p},\quad\forall f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(M).

The reason for introducing this notion is to study the following reverse inequality in a more thorough way:

(
\textrm{RRV}_{p}
)

\|H^{1/2}f\|_{p}\leq C\|\nabla_{H}f\|_{p},\quad\forall f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(M).

1.1.
Main results

Before presenting our main results, let us first introduce some notation. Throughout the article, we write

(1.8)

\displaystyle p_{0}:=\frac{d}{\min\left(\frac{d+2}{2}+\mu_{0},d\right)},\quad p_{1}:=\frac{d}{\max\left(\frac{d}{2}-\mu_{0},0\right)},

where
\mu_{0}>0
is the square root of the smallest eigenvalue of the operator

\Delta_{Y}+V_{0}(y)+\left(\frac{d-2}{2}\right)^{2}.

Thus

(1.9)

\displaystyle p_{0}^{\prime}=\frac{d}{\max\left(\frac{d-2}{2}-\mu_{0},0\right)},\quad p_{1}^{\prime}=\frac{d}{\min\left(\frac{d}{2}+\mu_{0},d\right)}.

We recall that a function
f
belongs to the Lorentz space
L^{p,q}
(
0<p,q\leq\infty
) if the quasi-norm

\|f\|_{(p,q)}=\begin{cases}\left(\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(t^{1/p}f^{*}(t)\right)^{q}\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{1/q},&q<\infty,\\[10.00002pt]
\displaystyle\sup_{t>0}t^{1/p}f^{*}(t)=\sup_{\lambda>0}\lambda\,d_{f}(\lambda)^{1/p},&q=\infty,\end{cases}

is finite, where
f^{*}
denotes the decreasing rearrangement of
f
,

f^{*}(t)=\inf\{\lambda>0:d_{f}(\lambda)\leq t\},

and
d_{f}
is the usual distribution function,

d_{f}(\lambda)=\mu\bigl(\{x:|f(x)|>\lambda\}\bigr).

As observed in Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
, (

R
p

) and (

RV
p

) hold only on a finite interval of exponents. Therefore, it is natural to ask whether there exists a suitable endpoint estimate. From
[
31
]
, it is known that the Riesz transform
R
on a metric cone is not even of weak type
(p,p)
at the endpoint. Hence, as a natural candidate, one expects
R
to be of restricted weak type bounded at the endpoints.

Our first result confirms this expectation.

Theorem A
.

Let
d\geq 3
and let
M=(0,\infty)_{r}\times Y
be a metric cone of dimension
d
with cross-section
Y
. Suppose
V_{0}
is a smooth function on
Y
satisfying (
1.3
). Let
V=V_{0}/r^{2}
be the corresponding inverse-square potential and let
H=\Delta+V
be the associated Schrödinger operator. Then the Riesz transform
R=\nabla H^{-1/2}
is of restricted weak type
(p_{0},p_{0})
. Moreover, if
p_{1}<\infty
, then
R
is also of restricted weak type
(p_{1},p_{1})
, that is,

\|Rf\|_{(p_{i},\infty)}\leq C\|f\|_{(p_{i},1)}

for
i=0,1
, where
p_{i}
are defined in (
1.8
).

In addition, this estimate is sharp in the following sense: for
i=0,1
, the operator
R
cannot be bounded from
L^{p_{i},q}
to
L^{p_{i},\infty}
for any
1<q\leq\infty
.

Note that the above result is consistent with the result in
[
23
]
, which in fact provides an even sharper Lorentz-type estimate in the framework of manifolds with ends.

Next, we investigate the reverse inequality (

RRV
p

), which is the main motivation of this article. The reason for adding the term
\left\|\frac{f}{r}\right\|_{p}
on the right-hand side of (

RRV
p

) is the identity:

\|H^{1/2}f\|_{2}^{2}=\left\langle H^{1/2}f,H^{1/2}f\right\rangle=\left\langle Hf,f\right\rangle=\|\nabla f\|_{2}^{2}+\left\langle\frac{V_{0}f}{r},\frac{f}{r}\right\rangle.

In fact, a sharper version,

\|H^{1/2}f\|_{p}\leq C\|\nabla f\|_{p},

can be obtained easily by using a Hardy-type inequality at the cost of losing information at a single point (see Corollary
1.4
below).

It is well known that, on a complete Riemannian manifold, a standard duality argument shows that (

R
p

) implies
(\textrm{RR}_{p^{\prime}})
, whereas the converse is not true in general; see
[
8
]
. We show in Section
2
that this duality property still holds in the setting of metric cones with potential: that is, we prove that (

RV
p

) implies
(\textrm{RRV}_{p^{\prime}})
for some range of
p
(see Lemma
2.10
below). Thus, with the (formally) expected equivalence of (

RV
p

) and
(\textrm{RRV}_{p^{\prime}})
in mind, one might conjecture that the precise range for the boundedness of the reverse inequality should be

p_{1}^{\prime}<p<p_{0}^{\prime}.

However, this presumed equivalence between (

R
p

) and
(\textrm{RR}_{p^{\prime}})
has been shown to fail in many settings. For manifolds satisfying (
1.5
), see
[
24
]
; for the connected sum, see
[
21
]
; and for the Dirichlet Laplacian outside a bounded domain, see
[
28
,
30
]
.

On a metric cone, suppose that
M
has only one end (i.e. the cross section
Y
is connected). Then the question of (

RR
p

) falls within the scope of
[
3
, Theorem 0.7]
. Indeed, under the assumptions (
1.1
) and (

P
q

) (for all
q\geq 1
), (

RR
p

) holds for all
1<p<\infty
. When
Y
is disconnected (so
M
has multiple ends), the Poincaré inequality may fail (cf.
[
13
]
) and the general method of
[
3
]
does not apply. Note that
M
satisfies (
1.5
). A recent article
[
24
]
shows that (

RR
p

) is actually equivalent to Hardy’s inequality for
p
small. As a result, (

RR
p

) holds on
M
for all
1<p<\infty
(see Lemma
2.5
below).

The problem becomes even more subtle once we add a possibly negative potential to the Laplacian, even if we only consider inverse-square type potentials. Indeed, in the discussion of the previous paragraph and in the special cases considered in
[
21
,
30
]
, a Gaussian-type upper bound for the heat kernel is always (at least implicitly) assumed. In contrast, for the Schrödinger operator on a metric cone, there is no hope of obtaining such a bound for the heat kernel
e^{-tH}(z,z^{\prime})
, and a recent article
[
27
]
confirms this expectation.

Moreover, this fact also leads to another interesting phenomenon: there is a lower threshold for the
L^{p}
-boundedness of the Riesz transform. Reformulated in terms of the reverse inequality, this yields an upper threshold for the reverse Riesz transform. It has been verified in
[
21
,
24
]
that it is possible to relax the lower threshold for the reverse inequality (that is, the upper threshold for the validity of (

R
p

)) by using the so-called
harmonic annihilation method
. One might therefore hope that the upper threshold could also be relaxed by further developing this technique. Unfortunately, the next theorem shows that, in the setting of a metric cone with potential, this upper threshold is in fact sharp and cannot be improved. It turns out that the two methods introduced in Subsection
1.2
below—either eliminating the harmonic leading coefficient from outside (via a bilinear form) or from inside (by writing
H^{1/2}f=H^{-1/2}Hf
)—yield exactly the same range of
p
for which (

RRV
p

) holds.

Nevertheless, in this article, we inherit the method of
[
16
]
and examine the explicit formula for the off-diagonal part of the kernel
(H+1)^{-1}(z,z^{\prime})
. By applying the
harmonic annihilation method
, we obtain the following result.

Theorem B
.

Let
d\geq 3
and let
M=(0,\infty)_{r}\times Y
be a metric cone of dimension
d
with cross-section
Y
. Suppose
V_{0}
is a smooth function on
Y
satisfying (
1.3
). Let
V=V_{0}/r^{2}
be the corresponding inverse-square potential and let
H=\Delta+V
be the associated Schrödinger operator. Suppose
1<p<\infty
. Then the reverse inequality (

p

) holds if and only if

(1.10)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{\min\left(\frac{d+4}{2}+\mu_{0},d\right)}<p<p_{0}^{\prime}=\frac{d}{\max\left(\frac{d-2}{2}-\mu_{0},0\right)}.

Equivalently,

\displaystyle\|H^{1/2}f\|_{p}\leq C\|\nabla_{H}f\|_{p}

holds if and only if
p
belongs to the range (
1.10
).

In addition, the following endpoint estimate:

(1.11)

\|H^{1/2}f\|_{(p,\infty)}\leq C\|\nabla_{H}f\|_{(p,q)},

holds for

and fails for every
1<q\leq\infty
, where
p=\frac{d}{\min\left(\frac{d+4}{2}+\mu_{0},d\right)}
or

if
p_{0}^{\prime}<\infty
.

.

It is clear that

\frac{d}{\min\left(\frac{d+4}{2}+\mu_{0},d\right)}\leq p_{1}^{\prime}=\frac{d}{\min\left(\frac{d}{2}+\mu_{0},d\right)}.

In other words, the lower threshold in the “presumed dual range” for (

) has been relaxed. Note that if

, then
\mu_{0}>\frac{d-2}{2}
, and hence

, so that

. Moreover, in this case the lower threshold in (

) equals

, which shows that (

) holds for all

.

On the other hand, if

is negative (so

), then

, and the upper threshold, i.e.,

, in (

By applying our theorem to the special case where
V_{0}=c\neq 0
, we obtain the following result.

Corollary 1.7
.

Let
d\geq 3
and let
M=(0,\infty)_{r}\times Y
be a metric cone of dimension

with cross section
Y
. Suppose

and

. Then the reverse inequality (

p

) for the Schrödinger operator
H=\Delta+\frac{c}{r^{2}}
holds if and only if

1.2.
Motivation

In this subsection, we discuss the idea behind the proof of Theorem
B
, namely the
so–called
harmonic annihilation method
; see also

[
21
,
24
]
. From
[
5
]
, it is known that on a
compactification
\overline{M}
of the connected sum

M=\mathbb{R}^{n}\#\mathbb{R}^{n}
(
n\geq 3
), the Riesz potential has
an asymptotic expansion in the off–diagonal region

(1.13)

\Delta^{-1/2}(x,y)\sim\sum_{j=n-1}^{\infty}a_{j}(x)\,|y|^{-j},\qquad x\in\overline{M},\quad y\to\partial\overline{M},

where the leading coefficient
a_{n-1}
is a bounded, non-trivial
harmonic function on
\overline{M}
. By the maximum principle,

a_{n-1}
is non-constant, so in particular its gradient does not
vanish identically. Consequently, the leading term decays like

|y|^{-(n-1)}
in the
y
–variable, which prevents the Riesz transform
from being
L^{p}
–bounded for
p\geq n
.

Although the expansion (
1.13
) has only been verified in the setting of
\mathbb{R}^{n}\#\mathbb{R}^{n}
, it is natural to expect an analogous statement on a broader class of manifolds. In fact, on a compactification
\overline{M}
of the metric
cone
M=(0,\infty)_{r}\times Y
, the Riesz potential associated with

H=\Delta+V
admits an expansion of the form (we write
z=(r,y)
and
z^{\prime}=(r^{\prime},y^{\prime})
)

(1.14)

H^{-1/2}(z,z^{\prime})\sim\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{A}_{n,j}(z,y^{\prime})\,\rho(z^{\prime})^{\phi(j,n)},\qquad\mathcal{A}_{n,j}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{M}\times\partial\overline{M}),\quad z\in\overline{M},\quad z^{\prime}\to\partial\overline{M},

for some non-decreasing function
\phi
(in both
j
and
n
), where
\rho
is a boundary defining function near this regime. In particular, for each
j\geq 0
, coefficient
\mathcal{A}_{0,j}
is
H
–harmonic, i.e.
H\mathcal{A}_{0,j}=0
.

Next, for
f,g\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\overline{M})
, consider the bilinear form

\left\langle H^{1/2}f,g\right\rangle=\left\langle Hf,H^{-1/2}g\right\rangle=\left\langle\nabla f,\nabla H^{-1/2}g\right\rangle+\left\langle r^{-1}V_{0}f,\,r^{-1}H^{-1/2}g\right\rangle.

Assume that
g
is supported near the boundary
\partial\overline{M}
.
By (
1.14
) and integration by parts, the above bilinear form
admits an asymptotic expansion

(1.15)

\displaystyle\Big\langle f,\,\int\sum_{j,n=0}^{\infty}H\mathcal{A}_{j}(z,y^{\prime})\,\rho(z^{\prime})^{\phi(j,n)}g(z^{\prime})\,dz^{\prime}\Big\rangle

\displaystyle=\Big\langle\frac{f}{r},\,r\int\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}H\mathcal{A}_{j}(z,y^{\prime})\,\rho(z^{\prime})^{\phi(j,n)}g(z^{\prime})\,dz^{\prime}\Big\rangle,

since
\mathcal{A}_{0,j}
is
H
–harmonic. The new leading term has better decay in
the
z^{\prime}
–variable (since
\phi
is non-decreasing and
\rho(z^{\prime})
can be taken as
1/r^{\prime}
near this boundary), and one may therefore expect that
(
1.15
) is
O(\|\nabla_{H}f\|_{p}\|g\|_{p^{\prime}})
for some range of
p

strictly larger than the “dual range”
(p_{1}^{\prime},p_{0}^{\prime})
associated with
the Riesz transform.

The above heuristic has already been mentioned and used in

[
21
,
24
]
. Motivated by the appearance of a lower threshold in
the range of
p
for which the Riesz transform is bounded, we now look
at the reverse Riesz inequality from a different point of view. By
symmetry, (
1.14
) also suggests that

H^{-1/2}(z,z^{\prime})\sim\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\rho(z)^{\phi(j,n)}\mathcal{A}_{n,j}(z^{\prime},y),\qquad z\to\partial\overline{M},\quad z^{\prime}\in\overline{M},

where near this regime we may define
\rho(z)=1/r
. Proceeding with
z\to\partial\overline{M}
, we deduce (using the locality of
H
) that

(1.16)

\displaystyle H^{1/2}f(z)=H^{-1/2}Hf(z)

\displaystyle\sim\int_{\overline{M}}\sum_{j,n=0}^{\infty}\rho(z)^{\phi(j,n)}\mathcal{A}_{n,j}(z^{\prime},y)\,Hf(z^{\prime})\,dz^{\prime}

\displaystyle\sim\int_{\overline{M}}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\rho(z)^{\phi(j,n)}r^{\prime}H\mathcal{A}_{n,j}(z^{\prime},y)\,\frac{f(z^{\prime})}{r^{\prime}}\,dz^{\prime},

and here again a
harmonic annihilation
phenomenon should occur: the
leading problematic term disappears after integration by parts.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Resolvent on metric cone

Recall that on the metric cone
M=(0,\infty)_{r}\times Y
, the gradient operator can be written in the form

\nabla=\bigl(\partial_{r},\,r^{-1}\nabla_{Y}\bigr).

Consider the quadratic form

\mathcal{Q}f=\int_{M}\nabla f\cdot\nabla f\,d\mu,

where
\mu
is the metric-induced measure
d\mu=r^{d-1}\,dr\,dh(y)
.

By the Friedrichs extension, there exists a unique non-negative self-adjoint operator
\Delta
such that

\mathcal{Q}f=\int_{M}\Delta f\cdot f\,d\mu.

In particular, for
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(M)
,

\Delta f(z)=\Delta f(r,y)=-\partial_{r}^{2}f-\frac{d-1}{r}\,\partial_{r}f+\frac{\Delta_{Y}}{r^{2}}f,

where
\Delta_{Y}
is the Laplace–Beltrami operator on
Y
.

Let
V_{0}
be a smooth function on
Y
satisfying (
1.3
), and define the Schrödinger operator
H=\Delta+\frac{V_{0}}{r^{2}}
. We analyze the Riesz transform via the formula (with the inessential constant
2/\pi
omitted)

\nabla H^{-1/2}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\nabla(H+\lambda^{2})^{-1}\,d\lambda.

The crux, therefore, is to understand the resolvent operator
(H+\lambda^{2})^{-1}
. Note that
H
is homogeneous of degree
-2
. It follows that

(H+\lambda^{2})^{-1}(z,z^{\prime})=(H+\lambda^{2})^{-1}(r,y;r^{\prime},y^{\prime})=\lambda^{d-2}(H+1)^{-1}(\lambda r,y;\lambda r^{\prime},y^{\prime}).

Let
\mu_{j}^{2}
be the eigenvalues of
\Delta_{Y}+V_{0}(y)+\left(\frac{d-2}{2}\right)^{2}
, and let
u_{j}
be the corresponding
L^{2}
-normalized eigenfunctions. By
[
16
, Section 4.3]
, for
(z,z^{\prime})
sufficiently far away from the diagonal, the kernel
(H+1)^{-1}(\lambda r,y;\lambda r^{\prime},y^{\prime})
has the explicit form

(2.1)

\displaystyle\lambda^{2-d}(rr^{\prime})^{1-d/2}\sum_{j\geq 0}u_{j}(y)\overline{u_{j}(y^{\prime})}I_{\mu_{j}}(\lambda r)K_{\mu_{j}}(\lambda r^{\prime}),\quad

\displaystyle 4r<r^{\prime},

(2.2)

\displaystyle\lambda^{2-d}(rr^{\prime})^{1-d/2}\sum_{j\geq 0}u_{j}(y^{\prime})\overline{u_{j}(y)}I_{\mu_{j}}(\lambda r^{\prime})K_{\mu_{j}}(\lambda r),\quad

\displaystyle 4r^{\prime}<r,

where
I,K
are modified Bessel functions of the second type.

We emphasize that the original formula in
[
16
, Equation 4.11]
is written in terms of half-densities, and it differs from the true resolvent kernel by the factor
(rr^{\prime})^{1-d/2}
; see
[
16
, Equation 4.7, p. 495]
.

However, the above formulas do not converge near the diagonal. To remedy this, Hassell and Lin glue the formulas (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) together with a parametrix, to determine the behaviour near the diagonal. For details of the parametrix construction, we refer the reader to
[
16
, Sections 4.5–4.7]
; see also
[
14
]
.

Throughout the article, we fix a non-negative smooth function
\mathcal{X}:[0,\infty)\to[0,1]
supported in
[0,8/9]
such that
\mathcal{X}(t)=1
for all
0\leq t\leq 1/2
. We define

\displaystyle G_{1}(z,z^{\prime})

\displaystyle=(H+1)^{-1}(z,z^{\prime})\left(1-\mathcal{X}\left(\frac{4r}{r^{\prime}}\right)-\mathcal{X}\left(\frac{4r^{\prime}}{r}\right)\right),

\displaystyle G_{2}(z,z^{\prime})

\displaystyle=(H+1)^{-1}(z,z^{\prime})\,\mathcal{X}\left(\frac{4r}{r^{\prime}}\right),

\displaystyle G_{3}(z,z^{\prime})

\displaystyle=(H+1)^{-1}(z,z^{\prime})\,\mathcal{X}\left(\frac{4r^{\prime}}{r}\right).

According to this decomposition, we split the Riesz transform
R
into three parts, i.e.
R=R_{1}+R_{2}+R_{3}
, where

(2.3)

R_{i}f(z)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{M}\nabla_{z}G_{i}^{\lambda}(z,z^{\prime})f(z^{\prime})\,\lambda^{d-2}\,d\mu(z^{\prime})\,d\lambda,

and

G_{i}^{\lambda}(z,z^{\prime})=G_{i}(\lambda z,\lambda z^{\prime})=G_{i}(\lambda r,y;\lambda r^{\prime},y^{\prime}).

We summarize the estimates from
[
16
]
in the following lemmata, which we will use throughout the paper.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
16
, Lemma 5.4, Prop 5.10 and 5.12]

We have the following kernel estimates:

1)

For
G_{1}
,

\displaystyle\bigl|\nabla_{z,z^{\prime}}^{j}G_{1}(\lambda r,y;\lambda r^{\prime},y^{\prime})\bigr|\lesssim\begin{cases}\lambda^{2-d}\,d(z,z^{\prime})^{2-d-j},&d(z,z^{\prime})\leq\lambda^{-1},\\
\lambda^{-N+j}\,d(z,z^{\prime})^{-N},&d(z,z^{\prime})\geq\lambda^{-1},\end{cases}

for all
N>0
and
j\geq 0
.

In addition,
R_{1}
is of weak type
(1,1)
and bounded on
L^{p}
for all
1<p<\infty
.

2)

For
G_{2}
,

\displaystyle\bigl|G_{2}(\lambda r,y;\lambda r^{\prime},y^{\prime})\bigr|

\displaystyle\lesssim\begin{cases}\lambda^{2-d}\,r^{1-d/2+\mu_{0}}r^{\prime 1-d/2-\mu_{0}},&\lambda\leq r^{\prime-1},\\
\lambda^{1-d/2+\mu_{0}-N}\,r^{1-d/2+\mu_{0}}r^{\prime-N},&\lambda\geq r^{\prime-1},\end{cases}

\displaystyle\bigl|\nabla_{z}G_{2}(\lambda r,y;\lambda r^{\prime},y^{\prime})\bigr|

\displaystyle\lesssim\begin{cases}\lambda^{2-d}\,r^{-d/2+\mu_{0}}r^{\prime 1-d/2-\mu_{0}},&\lambda\leq r^{\prime-1},\\
\lambda^{1-d/2+\mu_{0}-N}\,r^{-d/2+\mu_{0}}r^{\prime-N},&\lambda\geq r^{\prime-1},\end{cases}

for all
N>0
.

In addition,
R_{2}
is
L^{p}
-bounded for all
p<p_{1}
.

3)

For
G_{3}
,

\displaystyle\bigl|G_{3}(\lambda r,y;\lambda r^{\prime},y^{\prime})\bigr|

\displaystyle\lesssim\begin{cases}\lambda^{2-d}\,r^{1-d/2-\mu_{0}}r^{\prime 1-d/2+\mu_{0}},&\lambda\leq r^{-1},\\
\lambda^{1-d/2+\mu_{0}-N}\,r^{-N}r^{\prime 1-d/2+\mu_{0}},&\lambda\geq r^{-1},\end{cases}

\displaystyle\bigl|\nabla_{z}G_{3}(\lambda r,y;\lambda r^{\prime},y^{\prime})\bigr|

\displaystyle\lesssim\begin{cases}\lambda^{2-d}\,r^{-d/2-\mu_{0}}r^{\prime 1-d/2+\mu_{0}},&\lambda\leq r^{-1},\\
\lambda^{1-d/2+\mu_{0}-N}\,r^{-N-1}r^{\prime 1-d/2+\mu_{0}},&\lambda\geq r^{-1},\end{cases}

for all
N>0
.

In addition,
R_{3}
is
L^{p}
-bounded for all
p>p_{0}
.

Remark 2.2
.

For later use, we give a similar estimate for
\nabla_{z^{\prime}}G_{2}^{\lambda}
. In fact, by
[
16
, Prop 4.6, Theorem 4.11]
, we know that
G_{2}
is conormal at all boundary hypersurfaces. Hence, by conormality,

(2.4)

\displaystyle\bigl|\nabla_{z^{\prime}}G_{2}(\lambda r,y;\lambda r^{\prime},y^{\prime})\bigr|

\displaystyle\lesssim\begin{cases}\lambda^{2-d}\,r^{1-d/2+\mu_{0}}r^{\prime-d/2-\mu_{0}},&\lambda\leq r^{\prime-1},\\
\lambda^{1-d/2+\mu_{0}-N}\,r^{1-d/2+\mu_{0}}r^{\prime-N-1},&\lambda\geq r^{\prime-1},\end{cases}

for all
N>0
.

Lemma 2.3
.

[
16
, Lemma 4.4]

For any
0<\beta<1
and any
M,N>0
, the series

\sum_{\mu_{j}\geq M}\mu_{j}^{N}\alpha^{\mu_{j}-M}

converges for all
0<\alpha\leq\beta
, and it is bounded uniformly in
\alpha
.

The following lemma was also studied in
[
14
]
.

Lemma 2.4
.

[
16
, Corollary 5.9]

Let
K
be a kernel defined on
M=(0,\infty)_{r}\times Y
. Suppose

|K(r,y;r^{\prime},y^{\prime})|\lesssim\begin{cases}r^{-\alpha}r^{\prime-\beta},&r\leq r^{\prime},\\
0,&r>r^{\prime},\end{cases}

and assume that
\alpha+\beta=d
,
\beta>0
, and
p<\dfrac{d}{\max(\alpha,0)}
. Then
K
acts as a bounded operator on
L^{p}(M,r^{d-1}dr\,dh)
.

Similarly, if

|K(r,y;r^{\prime},y^{\prime})|\lesssim\begin{cases}0,&r\leq r^{\prime},\\
r^{-\gamma}r^{\prime-\delta},&r>r^{\prime},\end{cases}

and
\gamma+\delta=d
,
\gamma>0
and

, then
K
acts as a bounded operator on
L^{p}(M,r^{d-1}dr\,dh)
.

2.2.
Hardy’s inequality

Lemma 2.5
.

On
M
, the following Hardy inequality holds:

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left|\frac{f(r,y)}{r}\right|^{p}\,d\mu\leq C\int_{M}|\nabla f(r,y)|^{p}\,d\mu,\quad\forall f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(M),

for all
p\in(1,\infty)\setminus\{d\}
.

Proof of Lemma
2.5
.

Since
Y
is compact, it suffices to prove that for each
y\in Y
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\frac{f(r,y)}{r}\right|^{p}r^{d-1}\,dr\lesssim\int_{0}^{\infty}|\partial_{r}f(r,y)|^{p}r^{d-1}\,dr,

with an implicit constant independent of
y
. The one-dimensional Hardy’s inequality with weight (see, for example,
[
12
]
) gives precisely this estimate, provided
p\neq d
.
∎

Remark 2.6
.

The exclusion of the case
p=d
is sharp in the weighted one-dimensional Hardy’s inequality with weight: at
p=d
the inequality fails in general, which explains why
p=d
is omitted from Lemma
2.5
and Corollary
1.4
.

It follows that:

Proof of Corollary
1.4
.

The result follows directly by combining Theorem
B
with Lemma
2.5
.
∎

2.3.
Duality property between Riesz and reverse Riesz inequalities

In this subsection, we identify the most natural conjectural range for (

RRV
p

) to hold, namely the dual range associated with the
L^{p}
-boundedness of the Riesz transform.

To proceed, we define

\displaystyle Tu:=r^{-1}H^{-1/2}u=r^{-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}(H+\lambda^{2})^{-1}u\,d\lambda.

Using the parametrix construction, we decompose
T
into three pieces,
T=\sum_{j=1}^{3}T_{j}
, where

\displaystyle T_{j}u(z):=r^{-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{M}\lambda^{d-2}G_{j}(\lambda z,\lambda z^{\prime})\,u(z^{\prime})\,d\mu(z^{\prime})\,d\lambda,

for
j=1,2,3
.

Lemma 2.7
.

T_{1}
is bounded on
L^{p}
for all
1<p<\infty
.

Proof of Lemma
2.7
.

By Lemma
2.1
, the on-diagonal part
G_{1}
has estimate

|G_{1}(\lambda z,\lambda z^{\prime})|\lesssim\begin{cases}\lambda^{2-d}d(z,z^{\prime})^{2-d},&\lambda\leq d(z,z^{\prime})^{-1},\\
\lambda^{-N}d(z,z^{\prime})^{-N},&\lambda\geq d(z,z^{\prime})^{-1},\end{cases}

for all
N>0
.

Therefore, by choosing
N>d-1
, the kernel of
T_{1}
can be estimated by

\displaystyle\left|r^{-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{d-2}G_{1}(\lambda z,\lambda z^{\prime})d\lambda\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim r^{-1}\int_{0}^{d(z,z^{\prime})^{-1}}d(z,z^{\prime})^{2-d}d\lambda

\displaystyle+r^{-1}\int_{d(z,z^{\prime})^{-1}}^{\infty}\lambda^{d-2-N}d(z,z^{\prime})^{-N}d\lambda

\displaystyle\lesssim r^{-1}d(z,z^{\prime})^{1-d}.

Combining this with the fact that
G_{1}
is supported in the range
\{r/8\leq r^{\prime}\leq 8r\}
, we conclude

|T_{1}u(z)|\lesssim r^{-1}\int_{\{r/8\leq r^{\prime}\leq 8r\}}\frac{|u(z^{\prime})|}{d(z,z^{\prime})^{d-1}}dz^{\prime}.

Next, we replace the domain of integration by
B(z,10r)
. Indeed, for
z^{\prime}=(r^{\prime},y^{\prime})
such that
r/8\leq r^{\prime}\leq 8r
, we have

d(z,z^{\prime})^{2}=\begin{cases}|r-r^{\prime}|^{2}+2rr^{\prime}\left(1-\cos{d_{Y}(y,y^{\prime})}\right),&d_{Y}(y,y^{\prime})\leq\pi,\\
(r+r^{\prime})^{2},&d_{Y}(y,y^{\prime})\geq\pi,\end{cases}<100r^{2}.

To this end, we proceed with the following standard argument.

\displaystyle|T_{1}u(z)|

\displaystyle\lesssim r^{-1}\int_{B(z,10r)}\frac{|u(z^{\prime})|}{d(z,z^{\prime})^{d-1}}dz^{\prime}\lesssim r^{-1}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\int_{10r/2^{i+1}\leq d(z,z^{\prime})<10r/2^{i}}\left(\frac{r}{2^{i}}\right)^{1-d}|u(z^{\prime})|dz^{\prime}

\displaystyle\lesssim r^{-d}\sum_{i\geq 0}2^{i(d-1)}\int_{B(z,10r/2^{i})}|u(z^{\prime})|dz^{\prime}\lesssim r^{-d}\sum_{i\geq 0}2^{i(d-1)}\left(\frac{r}{2^{i}}\right)^{d}\mathcal{M}u(z)\lesssim\mathcal{M}u(z),

where
\mathcal{M}u(z)=\sup_{B\ni z}\textrm{vol}(B)^{-1}\int_{B}|u|
is the usual Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator and the second-to-last inequality is due to the fact that
\textrm{vol}(B(z,r))\sim r^{d}
for all
z\in M
and
r>0
. The proof follows directly by the maximal theorem.

∎

Lemma 2.8
.

T_{2}
is bounded on
L^{p}
for
1<p<p_{1}
and
T_{3}
is bounded on
L^{p}
for
p_{0}<p<\infty
, where
p_{0},p_{1}
are defined as in (
1.8
).

Proof of Lemma
2.8
.

By Lemma
2.1
,
G_{2}
has upper bound:

|G_{2}(\lambda r,\lambda r^{\prime},y,y^{\prime})|\lesssim\begin{cases}\lambda^{2-d}r^{1-d/2+\mu_{0}}r^{\prime 1-d/2-\mu_{0}},&\lambda\leq r^{\prime-1},\\
\lambda^{1-d/2+\mu_{0}-N}r^{1-d/2+\mu_{0}}r^{\prime-N},&\lambda\geq r^{\prime-1},\end{cases}

for all
N>0
. It follows that

\displaystyle|T_{2}(z,z^{\prime})|\lesssim

\displaystyle r^{-1}\int_{0}^{r^{\prime-1}}r^{1-d/2+\mu_{0}}r^{\prime 1-d/2-\mu_{0}}d\lambda

\displaystyle+r^{-1}\int_{r^{\prime-1}}^{\infty}\lambda^{d/2-1+\mu_{0}-N}r^{1-d/2+\mu_{0}}r^{\prime-N}d\lambda

\displaystyle\lesssim r^{-d/2+\mu_{0}}r^{\prime-d/2-\mu_{0}},

where we choose
N>d/2+\mu_{0}
.

Similarly, a parallel argument yields

\displaystyle|T_{3}(z,z^{\prime})|\lesssim

\displaystyle r^{-1}\int_{0}^{r^{-1}}r^{1-d/2-\mu_{0}}r^{\prime 1-d/2+\mu_{0}}d\lambda

\displaystyle+r^{-1}\int_{r^{-1}}^{\infty}\lambda^{d/2-1+\mu_{0}-N}r^{-N}r^{\prime 1-d/2+\mu_{0}}d\lambda

\displaystyle\lesssim r^{-1-d/2-\mu_{0}}r^{\prime 1-d/2+\mu_{0}}.

Recall that
G_{2}
is supported in the range where
r\leq r^{\prime}
and
G_{3}
is supported in
\{r\geq r^{\prime}\}
.
T_{2}u
and
T_{3}u
are therefore bounded by

\displaystyle r^{-d/2+\mu_{0}}\int_{\{r\leq r^{\prime}\}}r^{\prime-d/2-\mu_{0}}|u(z^{\prime})|dz^{\prime}\quad\textrm{and}\quad r^{-1-d/2-\mu_{0}}\int_{\{r\geq r^{\prime}\}}r^{\prime 1-d/2+\mu_{0}}|u(z^{\prime})|dz^{\prime},

respectively. The proof follows from Lemma
2.4
immediately.

∎

Remark 2.9
.

It is not surprising that
T_{2}
and

have the same range of

-boundedness as

and

in the Riesz transform. Indeed, since

to the kernel and multiplying it by

.

Next, we verify the duality property between (

p

) and
(\textrm{RRV}_{p^{\prime}})
.

Proposition 2.10
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
B
, the reverse inequality holds for
p\in(p_{1}^{\prime},p_{0}^{\prime})
. That is

\displaystyle\|H^{1/2}f\|_{p}\leq C\|\nabla_{H}f\|_{p},\quad p_{1}^{\prime}<p<p_{0}^{\prime},

for all

.

Proof of Proposition
2.10
.

Let
g\in C_{c}^{\infty}(M)
with
\|g\|_{p^{\prime}}\leq 1
. By duality, it is enough to consider bilinear form
\langle H^{1/2}f,g\rangle
. Note that

\displaystyle\langle H^{1/2}f,g\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\int_{0}^{\infty}(H+\lambda^{2})^{-1}Hfd\lambda,g\right\rangle=\left\langle Hf,\int_{0}^{\infty}(H+\lambda^{2})^{-1}gd\lambda\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\nabla f,\nabla\int_{0}^{\infty}(H+\lambda^{2})^{-1}gd\lambda\right\rangle+\left\langle\frac{V_{0}}{r}f,r^{-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}(H+\lambda^{2})^{-1}gd\lambda\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla f\|_{p}\|Rg\|_{p^{\prime}}+C\left\|\frac{f}{r}\right\|_{p}\sum_{j=1}^{3}\|T_{j}g\|_{p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla_{H}f\|_{p}\|g\|_{p^{\prime}},

where the last inequality follows by Theorem
1.2
, Lemma
2.7
and Lemma
2.8
. The proof is complete.

∎

1.5
.

Proof of Corollary
1.5
.

By (
1.7
) and Theorem
1.2
, it suffices to consider the

-boundedness of

. The result follows immediately from Lemma
2.7
and Lemma
2.8
.

3.
Endpoint estimates: Proof of Theorem
A

In this section we prove Theorem
A
; namely, we show that the Riesz transform
R
is of
restricted weak type
at both endpoint exponents
p_{0}
and
p_{1}
.
Throughout, “restricted weak type
(p,p)
” means that for all measurable sets
E\subset M
and all
\alpha>0
,

\mu\left(\left\{z:|R\mathbf{1}_{E}(z)|>\alpha\right\}\right)\leq C\alpha^{-p}\mu(E),

or equivalently,

\|Rf\|_{(p_{i},\infty)}\lesssim\|f\|_{(p_{i},1)},\quad\textrm{for}\quad i=0,1.

We use the parametrix decomposition from Section
2
: write

R=\int_{0}^{\infty}\nabla(H+\lambda^{2})^{-1}\,d\lambda=R_{1}+R_{2}+R_{3},

where
R_{i}
corresponds to
G_{i}
and inherits the kernel bounds in Lemma
2.1
.
The near-diagonal term
R_{1}
is a Calderón–Zygmund operator: the size and smoothness estimates for
\nabla G_{1}
yield weak type
(1,1)
and
L^{2}
-boundedness; hence
R_{1}
is harmless for our endpoint analysis.

The off-diagonal terms
R_{2}
and
R_{3}
capture the conic asymptotics and determine the endpoints. Using the factorized kernel bounds for
G_{2}
and
G_{3}
in Lemma
2.1
together with the Hardy-Littlewood-Pólya type mapping criteria in Lemma
2.4
, we obtain restricted weak type
(p_{1},p_{1})
and
(p_{0},p_{0})
, respectively.

Combining these estimates for
R_{2}
and
R_{3}
with the Calderón–Zygmund bounds for
R_{1}
completes the proof of Theorem
A
.

3.1.
Lorentz-type endpoint estimate

Lemma 3.1
.

R
is of restricted weak type
(p_{0},p_{0})
and
(p_{1},p_{1})
.

Proof of Lemma
3.1
.

Our assumption
p_{1}<\infty
implies
0<\mu_{0}<\tfrac{d}{2}
. By spectral theory and the decomposition discussed above,

\nabla H^{-1/2}\;=\;\int_{0}^{\infty}\nabla(H+\lambda^{2})^{-1}\,d\lambda\;=\;R_{1}+R_{2}+R_{3},

where
R_{i}
are defined as in (
2.3
).

By Lemma
2.1
,
R_{1}
satisfies Calderón–Zygmund estimates; hence it is of weak type
(1,1)
and bounded on
L^{p}
for all
p\in(1,\infty)
. In particular, for the endpoints we will need,

\|R_{1}\|_{L^{p_{i},1}\to L^{p_{i},\infty}}\leq C,\qquad i=0,1.

(The special case
p_{0}=1
is included, since weak type
(1,1)
coincides with restricted weak type
(1,1)
). Next, by Lemma
2.4
(together with Lemma
2.1
) we already have strong
L^{p}
-boundedness of
R_{3}
at
p=p_{1}
and of
R_{2}
at
p=p_{0}
. It therefore remains to establish the complementary endpoint estimates:

•

if
p_{0}>1
, show that
R_{3}
is of restricted weak type
(p_{0},p_{0})
and
R_{2}
is of restricted weak type
(p_{1},p_{1})
if
p_{1}<\infty
.

•

if
p_{0}=1
, show that
R_{2},R_{3}
are both of weak type
(1,1)
(while
R_{2}
remains of restricted weak type
(p_{1},p_{1})
if
p_{1}<\infty
).

These two bounds, together with the estimate for
R_{1}
, yield Theorem
A
.

We first consider the endpoint
p_{0}
. By Lemma
2.1
and Hölder’s inequality,

\displaystyle|R_{3}(f)(r,y)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{Y\times(0,\infty)}r^{-\mu_{0}-d/2-1}r^{\prime\mu_{0}-d/2+1}\mathbf{1}_{r^{\prime}\leq r}(r,r^{\prime})f(r^{\prime},y^{\prime})r^{\prime d-1}dr^{\prime}dh(y^{\prime})

\displaystyle\lesssim r^{-\mu_{0}-d/2-1}\|f\|_{(p_{0},1)}\|H_{r}\|_{(p_{0}^{\prime},\infty)},

where
H_{r}(r^{\prime},y^{\prime})=r^{\prime\mu_{0}-d/2+1}\mathbf{1}_{r^{\prime}\leq r}(r,r^{\prime})
.

Suppose first
0<\mu_{0}<d/2-1
, i.e.,
p_{0}=\frac{d}{1+d/2+\mu_{0}}
. A straightforward calculation yields

\displaystyle\|H_{r}\|_{(p_{0}^{\prime},\infty)}^{p_{0}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\lambda>0}\lambda^{p_{0}^{\prime}}\mu\left(\{M;(r^{\prime})^{\mu_{0}-d/2+1}\mathbf{1}_{r^{\prime}\leq r}(r,r^{\prime})>\lambda\}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{\lambda>0}\begin{cases}\lambda^{\frac{d}{d/2-1-\mu_{0}}}\lambda^{\frac{d}{\mu_{0}-d/2+1}},&\lambda\geq r^{\mu_{0}-d/2+1}\\
\lambda^{\frac{d}{d/2-1-\mu_{0}}}r^{d},&\lambda<r^{\mu_{0}-d/2+1}\end{cases}

\displaystyle\lesssim 1,

since
Y
has finite measure and
p_{0}^{\prime}=\frac{d}{d/2-1-\mu_{0}}
. It follows that

|R_{3}(f)(r,y)|\lesssim r^{-\mu_{0}-d/2-1}\|f\|_{(p_{0},1)}.

One checks easily that
r^{-\mu_{0}-d/2-1}\in L^{p_{0},\infty}(M)
, which shows the restricted
(p_{0},p_{0})
boundedness of
R_{3}
. Next, for
\mu_{0}\geq d/2-1
(i.e.,
p_{0}=1
), we have to establish that
R_{2},R_{3}
are both of weak type
(1,1)
. Note that

\displaystyle|R_{2}(f)(r,y)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{Y\times(0,\infty)}r^{\mu_{0}-d/2}r^{\prime-\mu_{0}-d/2}\mathbf{1}_{r\leq r^{\prime}}(r,r^{\prime})f(r^{\prime},y^{\prime})r^{\prime d-1}dr^{\prime}dh(y^{\prime})

\displaystyle\lesssim r^{\mu_{0}-d/2}\|f\|_{(p_{1},1)}\|F_{r}\|_{(p_{1}^{\prime},\infty)},

where
F_{r}(r^{\prime},y^{\prime})=r^{\prime-\mu_{0}-d/2}\mathbf{1}_{r\leq r^{\prime}}(r,r^{\prime})
.

It is then plain that

\displaystyle|(R_{2}+R_{3})(r,y)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(r^{\mu_{0}-d/2}\|F_{r}\|_{\infty}+r^{-\mu_{0}-d/2-1}\|H_{r}\|_{\infty}\right)\|f\|_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(r^{\mu_{0}-d/2}r^{-\mu_{0}-d/2}+r^{-\mu_{0}-d/2-1}r^{\mu_{0}-d/2+1}\right)\|f\|_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim r^{-d}\|f\|_{1},

and the weak type
(1,1)
statement is verified since
r^{-d}\in L^{1,\infty}(M)
.

To this end, we consider the case
p_{1}<\infty
and claim that
R_{2}
is restricted weak
(p_{1},p_{1})
. Directly, we have

\displaystyle\|F_{r}\|_{(p_{1}^{\prime},\infty)}^{p_{1}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\lambda>0}\lambda^{p_{1}^{\prime}}\mu\left(\{M;r^{\prime-\mu_{0}-d/2}\mathbf{1}_{r\leq r^{\prime}}(r,r^{\prime})>\lambda\}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{0<\lambda<r^{-\mu_{0}-d/2}}\lambda^{p_{1}^{\prime}}(\lambda^{-\frac{d}{\mu_{0}+d/2}}-r^{d})

\displaystyle\lesssim 1,

since
p_{1}^{\prime}=\frac{d}{\mu_{0}+d/2}
. Thus
|R_{2}(f)(r,y)|\lesssim r^{\mu_{0}-d/2}\|f\|_{(p_{1},1)}
and the claim follows since
r^{\mu_{0}-d/2}\in L^{p_{1},\infty}(M)
. The proof of Lemma
3.1
is now complete.

∎

Remark 3.2
.

We note that the above argument also applies to the special case
V=0
(i.e. the case considered in
[

]
). As discussed in

3.2.
Counterexamples for sharpness

To complete the proof of Theorem
A
, we also need the negative (sharpness) statement.
Following the idea of
[
14
]
, we prove:

Lemma 3.3
.

For
i\in\{0,1\}
and each
q>1
, there exists a function

f\in L^{p_{i},q}(M)
such that
|Rf(z)|=+\infty
for almost every
z\in M
.

In particular, at the endpoints
p=p_{i}
the restricted weak type bound of Theorem
A
is optimal in the Lorentz scale:
R
is bounded on
L^{p_{i},1}\!\to L^{p_{i},\infty}
, but this fails for every larger Lorentz space
L^{p_{i},q}
with
q>1
.

Proof of Lemma
3.3
.

By formulas (
2.1
) and (
2.2
), the problematic terms (i.e.
R_{2}
,
R_{3}
) have expansion

(3.1)

\displaystyle R_{2}f(z)=\nabla_{z}\int_{M}\int_{0}^{\infty}(rr^{\prime})^{1-d/2}\sum_{j\geq 0}u_{j}(y)\overline{u_{j}(y^{\prime})}I_{\mu_{j}}(\lambda r)K_{\mu_{j}}(\lambda r^{\prime})\mathcal{X}\left(\frac{4r}{r^{\prime}}\right)d\lambda f(z^{\prime})dz^{\prime},

(3.2)

\displaystyle R_{3}f(z)=\nabla_{z}\int_{M}\int_{0}^{\infty}(rr^{\prime})^{1-d/2}\sum_{j\geq 0}u_{j}(y^{\prime})\overline{u_{j}(y)}I_{\mu_{j}}(\lambda r^{\prime})K_{\mu_{j}}(\lambda r)\mathcal{X}\left(\frac{4r^{\prime}}{r}\right)d\lambda f(z^{\prime})dz^{\prime}.

By the
L^{p}
-boundedness of
R_{2},R_{3}
, it suffices to check the unboundedness of
R_{2}
near
p=p_{1}
and
R_{3}
near
p=p_{0}
.

First, since
\nabla=(\partial_{r},r^{-1}\nabla_{Y})
, it suffices to consider the tangential derivative
r^{-1}\nabla_{Y}
. The corresponding entries of (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) for each
j
are

(3.3)

\displaystyle r^{-d/2}\nabla_{Y}u_{j}(y)\int_{M}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}I_{\mu_{j}}(\lambda r)K_{\mu_{j}}(\lambda r^{\prime})d\lambda\right)r^{\prime 1-d/2}\overline{u_{j}(y^{\prime})}\mathcal{X}\left(\frac{4r}{r^{\prime}}\right)f(z^{\prime})dz^{\prime},

and

(3.4)

\displaystyle r^{-d/2}\nabla_{Y}\overline{u_{j}(y)}\int_{M}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}I_{\mu_{j}}(\lambda r^{\prime})K_{\mu_{j}}(\lambda r)d\lambda\right)r^{\prime 1-d/2}u_{j}(y^{\prime})\mathcal{X}\left(\frac{4r^{\prime}}{r}\right)f(z^{\prime})dz^{\prime}.

Second, we show that after extracting finitely many terms, the remainders of (
3.3
) and (
3.4
) act as bounded operators on
L^{p}
for all
1<p<\infty
. Indeed, by Lemma
2.3
and Hörmander’s
L^{\infty}
-estimate
[
26
]
, for
J\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=J}^{\infty}|\nabla_{Y}u_{j}(y)||I_{\mu_{j}}(\lambda r)||K_{\mu_{j}}(\lambda r^{\prime})||u_{j}(y^{\prime})|\mathbf{1}_{r\leq\frac{2}{9}r^{\prime}}\lesssim\sum_{j\geq J}\mu_{j}^{d}\left(\frac{4r}{r^{\prime}}\right)^{\mu_{j}}e^{-\lambda r^{\prime}/4}\lesssim r^{\mu_{J}}r^{\prime-\mu_{J}}e^{-c\lambda r^{\prime}}.

Similarly,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=J}^{\infty}|\nabla_{Y}u_{j}(y)||I_{\mu_{j}}(\lambda r^{\prime})||K_{\mu_{j}}(\lambda r)||u_{j}(y^{\prime})|\mathbf{1}_{r^{\prime}\leq\frac{2}{9}r}\lesssim\sum_{j\geq J}\mu_{j}^{d}\left(\frac{4r^{\prime}}{r}\right)^{\mu_{j}}e^{-\lambda r/4}\lesssim r^{-\mu_{J}}r^{\prime\mu_{J}}e^{-c\lambda r}.

It follows that

(3.5)

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{j\geq J}\eqref{formula5}\right|\lesssim r^{\mu_{J}-d/2}\int_{r\leq r^{\prime}}r^{\prime-\mu_{J}-d/2}|f(z^{\prime})|dz^{\prime},

and

(3.6)

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{j\geq J}\eqref{formula6}\right|\lesssim r^{-1-d/2-\mu_{J}}\int_{r^{\prime}\leq r}r^{\prime 1-d/2+\mu_{J}}|f(z^{\prime})|dz^{\prime}.

Now, by Lemma
2.4
, it is clear that
(
3.5
)
acts as a bounded operator on
L^{p}
for
p<\frac{d}{\max\left(\frac{d}{2}-\mu_{J},0\right)}
. Meanwhile,
(
3.6
)
is
L^{p}
-bounded for
p>\frac{d}{\min\left(\frac{d+2}{2}+\mu_{J},d\right)}
. It follows by Weyl’s law that there exists
J\geq 0
such that both (
3.5
) and (
3.6
) are
L^{p}
-bounded for all
1<p<\infty
. Hence, it is enough to treat the first
J
terms of the sum.

Let
q>1
. Consider function
f(z^{\prime})=u_{0}(y^{\prime})r^{\prime\mu_{0}-d/2}\left(1+|\log{r^{\prime}}|\right)^{-s}
with
1/q<s\leq 1
. We show that
R_{2}f
diverges a.e. but
\|f\|_{(p_{1},q)}<\infty
. Observe that by
[
11
, Page 684, 6.576.5]
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}I_{\mu_{j}}(\lambda r)K_{\mu_{j}}(\lambda r^{\prime})d\lambda

\displaystyle=C_{\mu_{j}}r^{\prime-1}\left(\frac{r}{r^{\prime}}\right)^{\mu_{j}}{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\mu_{j},\frac{1}{2};\mu_{j}+1;\left(\frac{r}{r^{\prime}}\right)^{2}\right)\quad

\displaystyle r<r^{\prime},

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}I_{\mu_{j}}(\lambda r^{\prime})K_{\mu_{j}}(\lambda r)d\lambda

\displaystyle=C_{\mu_{j}}r^{-1}\left(\frac{r^{\prime}}{r}\right)^{\mu_{j}}{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\mu_{j},\frac{1}{2};\mu_{j}+1;\left(\frac{r^{\prime}}{r}\right)^{2}\right)\quad

\displaystyle r>r^{\prime},

where
C_{\mu_{j}}>0
is uniformly bounded depending only on
\mu_{j}
. Moreover, since
0<r/r^{\prime}<1/4
and
{}_{2}F_{1}(a,b;c;t)
is increasing if
a,b,c>0
, we have
1={}_{2}F_{1}(a,b;c;0)\leq{}_{2}F_{1}(a,b;c;(r/r^{\prime})^{2})\leq{}_{2}F_{1}(a,b;c;1/16)=C
. Hence, by the orthogonality and normalization of
u_{j}
, we have for a.e.
(r,y)\in M
,

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{j=0}^{J}\eqref{formula5}\right|\geq Cr^{\mu_{0}-d/2}|\nabla_{Y}u_{0}(y)|\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{X}\left(\frac{4r}{r^{\prime}}\right)\left(1+|\log{r^{\prime}}|\right)^{-s}\frac{dr^{\prime}}{r^{\prime}},

and the integral diverges since

\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{X}\left(\frac{4r}{r^{\prime}}\right)\left(1+|\log{r^{\prime}}|\right)^{-s}\frac{dr^{\prime}}{r^{\prime}}\geq\int_{8r}^{\infty}(1+|\log{t}|)^{-s}\frac{dt}{t}\geq\int_{|\log{8r}|}^{\infty}(1+x)^{-s}dx=\infty,\quad\forall s\leq 1.

Meanwhile, one checks easily that for any
q>1
,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{(p_{1},q)}^{p}\sim\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(t^{1/p_{1}}f^{*}(t)\right)^{q}\frac{dt}{t}\lesssim\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\frac{q}{p_{1}}}t^{\frac{q(2\mu_{0}-d)}{2d}}(1+|\log{t}|)^{-sq}\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}(1+x)^{-sq}dx<\infty

since
s>1/q
. This confirms the unboundedness of
R_{2}
acting on
L^{p_{1},q}
for any
q>1
.

The argument for the unboundedness of
R_{3}
acting on
L^{p_{0},q}
is similar. Indeed, choose
f(z^{\prime})=\overline{u_{0}(y^{\prime})}r^{\prime-\frac{d+2}{2}-\mu_{0}}(1+|\log{r^{\prime}}|)^{-s}
with
1/q<s\leq 1
. Then

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{j=0}^{J}\eqref{formula6}\right|

\displaystyle\geq r^{-1-d/2-\mu_{0}}|\nabla_{Y}\overline{u_{0}(y)}|\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{X}\left(\frac{4r^{\prime}}{r}\right)(1+|\log{r^{\prime}}|)^{-s}\frac{dr^{\prime}}{r^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\geq r^{-1-d/2-\mu_{0}}|\nabla_{Y}\overline{u_{0}(y)}|\int_{|\log{8r^{\prime}}|}^{\infty}(1+x)^{-s}dx=\infty

for a.e.
(r,y)\in M
. On the other hand,

\|f\|_{(p_{0},q)}^{q}\sim\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(t^{1/p_{0}}f^{*}(t)\right)^{q}\frac{dt}{t}\lesssim\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\frac{q}{p_{0}}}t^{-\frac{q(2\mu_{0}+d+2)}{2d}}(1+|\log{t}|)^{-sq}\frac{dt}{t}=\int_{0}^{\infty}(1+x)^{-sq}dx<\infty

since again
s>1/q
, completing the proof.
∎

4.
Reverse Riesz problem: Proof of Theorem
B

In this section, we prove our main result, Theorem
B
in two different ways with the same core idea, according to (
1.15
) and (
1.16
), respectively. Note that the crux is to perform the
harmonic annihilation
(integration by parts) at the right situation, which in our case, it should take place on
G_{2}
part in the bilinear form from outside or on
G_{3}
part from inside; see Subsection
1.2
.

4.1.
Exterior harmonic annihilation

To to so, we prove:

Lemma 4.1
.

We have kernel estimates:

(4.1)

|H_{z}G_{2}^{\lambda}(z,z^{\prime})|\lesssim\lambda^{2-d}r^{-d}e^{-c\lambda r^{\prime}}\mathbf{1}_{r^{\prime}/8\leq r\leq r^{\prime}/4}+\mathbf{1}_{r\leq r^{\prime}/4}\begin{cases}\lambda^{4-d}r^{1-d/2+\mu_{0}}r^{\prime 1-d/2-\mu_{0}},&\lambda\leq r^{\prime-1},\\
\lambda^{3-d/2+\mu_{0}-N}r^{1-d/2+\mu_{0}}r^{\prime-N},&\lambda\geq r^{\prime-1},\end{cases}

and by symmetry,

(4.2)

|H_{z^{\prime}}G_{3}^{\lambda}(z,z^{\prime})|\lesssim\lambda^{2-d}r^{\prime-d}e^{-c\lambda r}\mathbf{1}_{r/8\leq r^{\prime}\leq r/4}+\mathbf{1}_{r^{\prime}\leq r/4}\begin{cases}\lambda^{4-d}r^{\prime 1-d/2+\mu_{0}}r^{1-d/2-\mu_{0}},&\lambda\leq r^{-1},\\
\lambda^{3-d/2+\mu_{0}-N}r^{\prime 1-d/2+\mu_{0}}r^{-N},&\lambda\geq r^{-1},\end{cases}

for all
N>0
.

Proof of Lemma
4.1
.

By symmetry, it is enough to prove (
4.1
), and then (
4.2
) follows directly by switching the roles of
r
and
r^{\prime}
.

For
G_{2}^{\lambda}(z,z^{\prime})
, we have explicit formula:

(4.3)

\lambda^{2-d}(rr^{\prime})^{1-d/2}\sum_{j\geq 0}u_{j}(y)\overline{u_{j}(y^{\prime})}I_{\mu_{j}}(\lambda r)K_{\mu_{j}}(\lambda r^{\prime})\mathcal{X}\left(\frac{4r}{r^{\prime}}\right).

Introduce notation
\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}(x)=x^{1-\alpha/2}I_{\alpha/2-1}(x)
. It is known that
\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}(\lambda r)
solves equation

f^{\prime\prime}(r)+\frac{\alpha-1}{r}f^{\prime}(r)=\lambda^{2}f(r);

see for example
[
19
, Section 3.1]
or
[
1
, Section 9.6.1]
. Denote by
\Delta_{\alpha}
the weighted ’Laplacian’:

\Delta_{\alpha}=-\partial_{r}^{2}-\frac{\alpha-1}{r}\partial_{r}.

Hence,
\Delta_{\alpha}\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}(\lambda r)=-\lambda^{2}\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}(\lambda r)
. Below, to lighten notation we suppress the index and write
\mu
and
u
for
\mu_{j}
and
u_{j}
, respectively.

Write each term in the sum (
4.3
) as

\displaystyle\lambda^{2-d+\mu}r^{\mu+1-d/2}r^{\prime 1-d/2}\mathcal{I}_{2\mu+2}(\lambda r)K_{\mu}(\lambda r^{\prime})u(y)\overline{u(y^{\prime})}\mathcal{X}\left(\frac{4r}{r^{\prime}}\right)

and then apply
H_{z}=\Delta_{z}+\frac{V_{0}(y)}{r^{2}}=-\partial_{r}^{2}-\frac{d-1}{r}\partial_{r}+\frac{\Delta_{Y}+V_{0}}{r^{2}}
to it, obtaining

(4.4)

\displaystyle\lambda^{2-d+\mu}r^{\prime 1-d/2}\Delta_{d}\left(\mathcal{I}_{2\mu+2}(\lambda r)r^{\mu+1-d/2}\mathcal{X}\left(\frac{4r}{r^{\prime}}\right)\right)K_{\mu}(\lambda r^{\prime})u(y)\overline{u(y^{\prime})}

\displaystyle+\lambda^{2-d}(rr^{\prime})^{1-d/2}\frac{\left(\Delta_{Y}+V_{0}(y)\right)u(y)}{r^{2}}I_{\mu}(\lambda r)K_{\mu}(\lambda r^{\prime})\overline{u(y^{\prime})}\mathcal{X}\left(\frac{4r}{r^{\prime}}\right).

Since
\left(\Delta_{Y}+V_{0}(y)+\left(\frac{d-2}{2}\right)^{2}\right)u(y)=\mu^{2}u(y)
, the second term above equals

(4.5)

\lambda^{2-d}(rr^{\prime})^{1-d/2}\frac{\mu^{2}-\left(\frac{d-2}{2}\right)^{2}}{r^{2}}I_{\mu}(\lambda r)K_{\mu}(\lambda r^{\prime})u(y)\overline{u(y^{\prime})}\mathcal{X}\left(\frac{4r}{r^{\prime}}\right).

Next, we treat the first term of (
4.4
). By splitting
\Delta_{d}=\Delta_{2\mu+2}+\frac{2\mu+2-d}{r}\partial_{r}
, the
\Delta_{d}(\dots)
part of the first term of (
4.4
) can be expanded as

\displaystyle\Delta_{2\mu+2}\mathcal{I}_{2\mu+2}(\lambda r)r^{\mu+1-d/2}\mathcal{X}\left(\frac{4r}{r^{\prime}}\right)

\displaystyle+\mathcal{I}_{2\mu+2}(\lambda r)\Delta_{2\mu+2}\left(r^{\mu+1-d/2}\mathcal{X}\left(\frac{4r}{r^{\prime}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle-2\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left(\mathcal{I}_{2\mu+2}(\lambda r)\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left(r^{\mu+1-d/2}\mathcal{X}\left(\frac{4r}{r^{\prime}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{2\mu+2-d}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left(\mathcal{I}_{2\mu+2}(\lambda r)\right)r^{\mu+1-d/2}\mathcal{X}\left(\frac{4r}{r^{\prime}}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{2\mu+2-d}{r}\mathcal{I}_{2\mu+2}(\lambda r)\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left(r^{\mu+1-d/2}\mathcal{X}\left(\frac{4r}{r^{\prime}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle:=I+II+III+IV+V.

Obviously,

(4.6)

I=-\lambda^{2}\mathcal{I}_{2\mu+2}(\lambda r)r^{\mu+1-d/2}\mathcal{X}\left(\frac{4r}{r^{\prime}}\right).

As for
II
, a straightforward computation yields that

\displaystyle II

\displaystyle=-(\mu+1-d/2)(3\mu+1-d/2)\mathcal{I}_{2\mu+2}(\lambda r)r^{\mu-1-d/2}\mathcal{X}\left(\frac{4r}{r^{\prime}}\right)

\displaystyle+\mathcal{I}_{2\mu+2}(\lambda r)r^{\mu+1-d/2}\Delta_{2\mu+2}\left(\mathcal{X}\left(\frac{4r}{r^{\prime}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle-(2\mu+2-d)\mathcal{I}_{2\mu+2}(\lambda r)r^{\mu-d/2}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left(\mathcal{X}\left(\frac{4r}{r^{\prime}}\right)\right):=II_{1}+II_{2}+II_{3}.

While for
III
, we have

\displaystyle III

\displaystyle=-\frac{2\mu+2-d}{r}r^{\mu+1-d/2}\mathcal{X}\left(\frac{4r}{r^{\prime}}\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left(\mathcal{I}_{2\mu+2}(\lambda r)\right)

\displaystyle-2r^{\mu+1-d/2}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left(\mathcal{I}_{2\mu+2}(\lambda r)\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left(\mathcal{X}\left(\frac{4r}{r^{\prime}}\right)\right):=III_{1}+III_{2}.

Note that
III_{1}+IV=0
.

Last but not least,

\displaystyle V

\displaystyle=(2\mu+2-d)(\mu+1-d/2)\mathcal{I}_{2\mu+2}(\lambda r)r^{\mu-1-d/2}\mathcal{X}\left(\frac{4r}{r^{\prime}}\right)

\displaystyle+(2\mu+2-d)\mathcal{I}_{2\mu+2}(\lambda r)r^{\mu-d/2}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left(\mathcal{X}\left(\frac{4r}{r^{\prime}}\right)\right):=V_{1}+V_{2}.

Observe that
V_{2}+II_{3}=0
.

Therefore,

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle+II+III+IV+V

\displaystyle=-\lambda^{2}\mathcal{I}_{2\mu+2}(\lambda r)r^{\mu+1-d/2}\mathcal{X}\left(\frac{4r}{r^{\prime}}\right)-(\mu+1-d/2)(3\mu+1-d/2)\mathcal{I}_{2\mu+2}(\lambda r)r^{\mu-1-d/2}\mathcal{X}\left(\frac{4r}{r^{\prime}}\right)

Proposition 4.2
.

The reverse inequality:

\displaystyle\|H^{1/2}f\|_{p}\leq C\|\nabla_{H}f\|_{p}

\frac{d}{\min\left(\frac{d+4}{2}+\mu_{0},d\right)}<p\leq p_{1}^{\prime}=\frac{d}{\min\left(\frac{d}{2}+\mu_{0},d\right)}.

4.2
.

By duality, it suffices to show that for any

\displaystyle\left|\langle H^{1/2}f,g\rangle\right|\leq C\|\nabla_{H}f\|_{p}\|g\|_{p^{\prime}}.

Note that the LHS above is bounded by

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle\nabla f,(R_{1}+R_{3})g\right\rangle\right|

\displaystyle+\left|\left\langle\frac{V_{0}}{r}f,r^{-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{d-2}(G_{1}^{\lambda}+G_{3}^{\lambda})gd\lambda\right\rangle\right|

\displaystyle+\left|\left\langle f,\frac{V_{0}}{r^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{d-2}G_{2}^{\lambda}gd\lambda\right\rangle+\left\langle\nabla f,\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{d-2}\nabla G_{2}^{\lambda}gd\lambda\right\rangle\right|:=I+II+III.

The first two terms can be handled easily. Observe first that by Hölder’s inequality

\displaystyle I\leq\|\nabla f\|_{p}\|(R_{1}+R_{3})g\|_{p^{\prime}}\leq C\|\nabla_{H}f\|_{p}\|g\|_{p^{\prime}},\quad\forall 1<p<p_{0}^{\prime}.

Second, Lemma
2.7
and Lemma
2.8
guarantee

\displaystyle II\leq C\left\|\frac{f}{r}\right\|_{p}\|(T_{1}+T_{3})g\|_{p^{\prime}}\leq C\|\nabla_{H}f\|_{p}\|g\|_{p^{\prime}},\quad\forall 1<p<p_{0}^{\prime}.

Next, by integration by parts

\displaystyle III=\left|\left\langle f,\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{d-2}HG_{2}^{\lambda}gd\lambda\right\rangle\right|.

Define

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}:u\mapsto r\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{d-2}\left(HG_{2}^{\lambda}u\right)d\lambda.

Lemma 4.3
.

\mathcal{T}
is bounded in
L^{p}
for

\displaystyle 1<p<\frac{d}{\max\left(\frac{d-4}{2}-\mu_{0},0\right)}.

Proof of Lemma
4.3
.

Note that

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}u(z)

\displaystyle=r\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{M}H_{z}G_{2}^{\lambda}(z,z^{\prime})u(z^{\prime})dz^{\prime}\lambda^{d-2}d\lambda

\displaystyle=r\int_{M}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}H_{z}G_{2}^{\lambda}(z,z^{\prime})\lambda^{d-2}d\lambda\right)u(z^{\prime})dz^{\prime}.

By Lemma
4.1
, for
r\leq r^{\prime}/4

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{r^{\prime-1}}\lambda^{2}r^{1-d/2+\mu_{0}}r^{\prime 1-d/2-\mu_{0}}d\lambda

\displaystyle+\int_{r^{\prime-1}}^{\infty}\lambda^{1+d/2+\mu_{0}-N}r^{1-d/2+\mu_{0}}r^{\prime-N}d\lambda

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{2-d}r^{-d}e^{-c\lambda r}\mathbf{1}_{r^{\prime}/8\leq r\leq r^{\prime}/4}\lambda^{d-2}d\lambda

\displaystyle\lesssim r^{1-d/2+\mu_{0}}r^{\prime-2-d/2-\mu_{0}}+r^{-1-d}\mathbf{1}_{r^{\prime}/8\leq r\leq r^{\prime}/4}.

Therefore, it is clear that

\displaystyle|\mathcal{T}u(z)|\lesssim r^{2-d/2+\mu_{0}}\int_{r\leq r^{\prime}}r^{\prime-2-d/2-\mu_{0}}|u(z^{\prime})|dz^{\prime}+r^{-d}\int_{r\sim r^{\prime}}|u(z^{\prime})|dz^{\prime}:=\mathcal{T}_{1}u(z)+\mathcal{T}_{2}u(z).

, which is of weak type
(p,p)
for all
1<p<\infty
. Indeed, for all

, we have

\displaystyle\textrm{vol}\left(\left\{z\in M;|\mathcal{T}_{2}u(z)|>\tau\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\textrm{vol}\left(\left\{z\in M;r^{-d/p}\|u\|_{p}>\tau\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\textrm{vol}\left(\left\{z\in M;r\leq\left(\frac{\|u\|_{p}}{\tau}\right)^{\frac{p}{d}}\right\}\right)

Thus by interpolation that
\mathcal{T}_{2}
is bounded on
L^{p}
for all
1<p<\infty
.

As for
\mathcal{T}_{1}

2.4
and conclude that
\mathcal{T}_{1}
is bounded on
L^{p}
for

\displaystyle 1<p<\frac{d}{\max\left(\frac{d-4}{2}-\mu_{0},0\right)},

completing the proof.
∎

Now, by Lemma
4.3

\displaystyle|III|=\left|\left\langle\frac{f}{r},\mathcal{T}g\right\rangle\right|\leq\left\|\frac{f}{r}\right\|_{p}\|\mathcal{T}g\|_{p^{\prime}}\lesssim\|\nabla_{H}f\|_{p}\|g\|_{p^{\prime}},

4.2
.

4.3.

By the argument in the previous section, to prove the sharpness of the lower threshold

\mathcal{S}
coming from the

Equivalently, we study the kernel of

and construct

|\mathcal{U}_{1}f(z)|=+\infty
a.e. whenever

For the upper threshold
p=p_{0}^{\prime}

We analyze its kernel and produce a counterexample
f
with
\|\nabla_{H}f\|_{p}<\infty
such that

|\mathcal{U}_{2}f(z)|=+\infty
a.e. for all
p\geq p_{0}^{\prime}

We first show that the lower threshold cannot be improved in the strong

Lemma 4.10
.

The reverse inequality

\displaystyle\|H^{1/2}f\|_{p}\lesssim\|\nabla f\|_{p}+\|f/r\|_{p}

Proof of Lemma
4.10
.

Appendix A
Asymptotic calculus

The asymptotic expansions obtained in this appendix fit naturally within the
framework of microlocal analysis on manifolds with boundary, where the
behaviour of distributional kernels is described by polyhomogeneous conormal
structures on the iterated blow-up of the double space.
Following the foundational development of the
b
- and scattering calculi
by Melrose
[
33
,
34
]
, one resolves the
geometric singularities of resolvent-type kernels by working on the
blown-up space
M_{b,sc}^{2}
and tracking their index sets at the boundary
hypersurfaces.
Subsequent refinements by Hassell-Melrose-Vasy and Guillarmou-Hassell

[
17
,
14
,
15
]
provide a precise
microlocal description of the resolvent and spectral measure near radial
points and at the scattering faces.
The explicit computations carried out below should therefore be viewed as a
concrete model of the polyhomogeneous structure predicted by this analytic
framework, specialized to the Riesz potential
H^{-1/2}(z,z^{\prime})
near the
right and left boundary faces.

We begin by recalling (
2.1
).
For
4r\leq r^{\prime}
, one has

(H+\lambda^{2})^{-1}(z,z^{\prime})=(rr^{\prime})^{1-d/2}\sum_{j\geq 0}u_{j}(y)\,\overline{u_{j}(y^{\prime})}I_{\mu_{j}}(\lambda r)\,K_{\mu_{j}}(\lambda r^{\prime}).

Therefore, integrating in
\lambda
and using the classical identity
involving the Gaussian hypergeometric function, we obtain

\displaystyle H^{-1/2}(z,z^{\prime})\sim(rr^{\prime})^{1-d/2}\sum_{j\geq 0}u_{j}(y)\,\overline{u_{j}(y^{\prime})}\int_{0}^{\infty}I_{\mu_{j}}(\lambda r)\,K_{\mu_{j}}(\lambda r^{\prime})\,d\lambda

\displaystyle\sim\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(rr^{\prime})^{1-d/2}u_{j}(y)\,\overline{u_{j}(y^{\prime})}\frac{\Gamma(1/2+\mu_{j})\Gamma(1/2)}{2\Gamma(\mu_{j}+1)}\left(\frac{r}{r^{\prime}}\right)^{\mu_{j}}r^{\prime-1}\,{}_{2}F_{1}\!\left(\frac{1}{2}+\mu_{j},\frac{1}{2};\mu_{j}+1;\frac{r^{2}}{r^{\prime 2}}\right).

We now use the standard series expansion

(A.1)

{}_{2}F_{1}(a,b;c;z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(a)_{n}(b)_{n}}{(c)_{n}\,n!}\,z^{n},\qquad|z|<1,

where
(e)_{n}
denotes the Pochhammer symbol:

(e)_{n}=\begin{cases}1,&n=0,\\
e(e+1)\cdots(e+n-1),&n>0.\end{cases}

Substituting this expansion yields

\displaystyle H^{-1/2}(z,z^{\prime})

\displaystyle\sim\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{A}_{n,j}(r,y,y^{\prime})\,r^{\prime-d/2-\mu_{j}-2n},

where the coefficients are given explicitly by

\mathcal{A}_{n,j}(r,y,y^{\prime})=\frac{\Gamma(1/2+\mu_{j})\Gamma(1/2)}{2\Gamma(\mu_{j}+1)}\frac{(1/2+\mu_{j})_{n}(1/2)_{n}}{(\mu_{j}+1)_{n}\,n!}\bigl(r^{\,1-\frac{d}{2}+\mu_{j}+2n}\,u_{j}(y)\bigr)\overline{u_{j}(y^{\prime})}.

In particular, the leading term
\mathcal{A}_{0,j}
exhibits

H
-harmonicity.
Indeed, using the eigenfunction relation

\left(\Delta_{Y}+V_{0}+\left(\frac{d-2}{2}\right)^{2}\right)u_{j}=\mu_{j}^{2}u_{j},

a direct computation shows that for each
j\geq 0
,

\displaystyle H\!\left(r^{1-\frac{d}{2}+\mu_{j}}\,u_{j}(y)\right)

\displaystyle=\left(-\partial_{r}^{2}-\frac{d-1}{r}\partial_{r}+\frac{\Delta_{Y}+V_{0}}{r^{2}}\right)\left(r^{1-\frac{d}{2}+\mu_{j}}\,u_{j}(y)\right)=0.

Thus, the leading coefficient in the rbz expansion is
H
-harmonic. This is precisely the concrete manifestation of the harmonic annihilation
phenomenon anticipated in Subsection
1.2
, in which the potentially
problematic leading term is annihilated by the operator
H
, and only higher-order terms contribute to the asymptotic behavior of

H_{z}H^{-1/2}(z,z^{\prime})
near the right boundary.

For completeness, we record here the analogue of the preceding calculation
for the left boundary, namely the
\mathrm{lbz}
-face of the doubly blow-up
space
M_{b,sc}^{2}
. The analysis is parallel to the rbz-case, with the roles
of the variables
z=(r,y)
and
z^{\prime}=(r^{\prime},y^{\prime})
interchanged. This computation
provides the full leading behavior of the Riesz potential
H^{-1/2}(z,z^{\prime})

as either variable approaches the boundary of the compactification

\overline{M}
.

We begin again from the resolvent representation.
For
4r^{\prime}\leq r
, the formula (
2.2
) yields

(H+\lambda^{2})^{-1}(z,z^{\prime})=(rr^{\prime})^{1-d/2}\sum_{j\geq 0}u_{j}(y)\,\overline{u_{j}(y^{\prime})}I_{\mu_{j}}(\lambda r^{\prime})\,K_{\mu_{j}}(\lambda r).

Integrating in
\lambda
and using the same Bessel integral identity as in the
rbz analysis, we obtain

\displaystyle H^{-1/2}(z,z^{\prime})\sim\sum_{j\geq 0}(rr^{\prime})^{1-d/2}u_{j}(y)\,\overline{u_{j}(y^{\prime})}C_{j}\left(\frac{r^{\prime}}{r}\right)^{\mu_{j}}r^{-1}\,{}_{2}F_{1}\!\left(\frac{1}{2}+\mu_{j},\frac{1}{2};\mu_{j}+1;\frac{r^{\prime 2}}{r^{2}}\right).

We expand the hypergeometric function by its standard series (
A.1
), yielding the asymptotic expansion at the
\mathrm{lbz}
-face:

\displaystyle H^{-1/2}(z,z^{\prime})

\displaystyle\sim\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{n,j}(r^{\prime},y^{\prime},y)\,r^{-d/2-\mu_{j}-2n},

where the coefficients are explicitly given by

\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{n,j}(r^{\prime},y^{\prime},y)=\frac{\Gamma(1/2+\mu_{j})\Gamma(1/2)}{2\Gamma(\mu_{j}+1)}\frac{(1/2+\mu_{j})_{n}(1/2)_{n}}{(\mu_{j}+1)_{n}\,n!}\Bigl(r^{\prime\,1-\frac{d}{2}+\mu_{j}+2n}\,\overline{u_{j}(y^{\prime})}\Bigr)u_{j}(y).

The
H
-harmonicity of the leading term is again transparent.
Indeed, for each
j\geq 0
,

\displaystyle H_{z^{\prime}}\!\left(r^{\prime\,1-\frac{d}{2}+\mu_{j}}\,\overline{u_{j}(y^{\prime})}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(-\partial_{r^{\prime}}^{2}-\frac{d-1}{r^{\prime}}\partial_{r^{\prime}}+\frac{\Delta_{Y^{\prime}}+V_{0}}{r^{\prime 2}}\right)\left(r^{\prime\,1-\frac{d}{2}+\mu_{j}}\,\overline{u_{j}(y^{\prime})}\right)=0

Thus the asymptotic expansion at the left boundary also exhibits the

harmonic annihilation
phenomenon: the leading term is annihilated
by the operator
H
, and only higher-order terms contribute to

H^{1/2}
near the
\mathrm{lbz}
-face.

Combining the expansions at the
\mathrm{rbz}
- and
\mathrm{lbz}
-faces
provides a precise description of the behavior of the kernel

H^{-1/2}(z,z^{\prime})
near the boundary of the compactified double space.
In particular, this illustrates concretely the general mechanism by which
the potentially problematic leading terms vanish under the action of

,
as predicted by the harmonic annihilation heuristic in
Subsection
1.2
.

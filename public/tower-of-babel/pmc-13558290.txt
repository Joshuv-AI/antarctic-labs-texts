Exploring trop-2-directed ADCs monotherapy and combination therapy in pretreated mTNBC: a real-world study
Authors: Jingze Liu, Linxiaoxiao Ding, Jiayi Wang, Tao Chen, Tianzhu Long, Qian Li, Jie Chai, Herui Yao, Ying Wang, Jianli Zhao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeAlthough Trop-2 antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs) have improved outcomes in pretreated metastatic triple-negative breast cancer (mTNBC), resistance remains common and the optimal combination strategy in routine practice is unclear. MethodsIn this retrospective real-world study, we identified patients with mTNBC from a health record–derived database who received sacituzumab govitecan or sacituzumab tirumotecan in the later-line setting between April 2020 and December 2025. Patients received at least two cycles of Trop-2 ADC therapy as monotherapy, plus a PD-1 inhibitor (dual-agent combination), or plus a PD-1 inhibitor and an antiangiogenic agent (triple-agent combination). ResultsDescriptively, the ORR was 57.1% in the triple-agent combination group, 38.5% in the dual-agent combination group, and 31.7% in the monotherapy group. Median follow-up was 15.2 months (95% CI, 8.7–20.6). Median progression-free survival (mPFS) was 15.4 months (95% CI, 5.3–NA) in the triple-agent combination group, 10.0 months (95% CI, 4.2–NA) in the dual-agent combination group, and 3.8 months (95% CI, 3.2–5.2) in the monotherapy group. Severe treatment-related toxicity was not increased in the combination groups. Exploratory analyses identified a genomic scoring system that may enable stratification of patients with a higher likelihood of benefiting from the triple-agent combination. ConclusionTo our knowledge, this study represents one of the earliest real-world evaluations of this triple-agent strategy in previously treated mTNBC, providing a basis for further prospective validation and biomarker-guided application.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1007/s10549-026-08070-9.

Introduction

Metastatic triple-negative breast cancer (mTNBC) is an aggressive breast cancer subtype characterized by the absence of estrogen receptor (ER), progesterone receptor (PR), and human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) expression and is generally associated with unfavorable survival [1, 2]. After progression on first-line therapy, treatment options are limited, and conventional chemotherapy continues to be the standard of care. Platinum- and taxane-based regimens are among the most frequently used options in this setting [2–4]. However, in previously treated mTNBC, conventional chemotherapies generally yield objective response rates of approximately 5%–20% and median PFS of only 2–4 months, while causing clinically relevant toxicities, including myelosuppression and neurotoxicity [5–10].

Sacituzumab govitecan (SG), a Trop-2 antibody–drug conjugate (ADC), has demonstrated superior efficacy compared with single-agent chemotherapy in previously treated mTNBC [11, 12]. The global phase 3 ASCENT trial demonstrated SG’s superior efficacy over single-agent chemotherapy, showing statistically significant improvements in PFS, objective response rate (ORR), and overall survival (OS) compared with physician’s choice of chemotherapy [11]. This efficacy was further supported by a Chinese phase 2b study, which reported favorable ORR and PFS outcomes [13]. However, the clinical benefit of Trop-2 ADC monotherapy remains limited in a substantial proportion of patients, supporting the rationale for developing more effective combination approaches.

Prior pivotal clinical trials have shown that adding pembrolizumab to chemotherapy improves outcomes in TNBC across disease settings, although the eligible populations and endpoints differ between metastatic and early-stage disease. For example, in the KEYNOTE-355 trial for mTNBC, pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy prolonged PFS and OS in previously untreated patients with PD-L1 combined positive score (CPS) ≥ 10. In early stage TNBC, neoadjuvant pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy followed by adjuvant pembrolizumab significantly increased the pathological complete response rate and improved event-free survival compared with chemotherapy alone [14–16]. Building on these advances, recent studies suggest that combining Trop-2 ADCs with immunotherapy can improve outcomes in first-line mTNBC, particularly in PD-L1-selected populations. In a randomized phase 3 trial, SG plus a PD-1 inhibitor resulted in a longer median PFS (11.2 months VS 7.8 months) than chemotherapy plus a PD-1 inhibitor. However, available evidence for Trop-2 ADCs combined with immunotherapy is largely derived from first-line, PD-L1-selected mTNBC, and the efficacy of this strategy in PD-L1-negative and/or later-line disease remains to be defined [17, 18].

To potentially overcome these limitations, emerging evidence including results from the ATRACTIB trial, suggests that adding anti-angiogenic therapy to PD-1/PD-L1-based chemoimmunotherapy may further improve PFS and ORR, including in PD-L1-negative disease [19, 20].

Therefore, we performed a real-world study to assess the efficacy and safety of triple-agent combination therapy comprising Trop-2 ADCs, PD-1 inhibitors, and angiogenesis inhibitors in mTNBC, including patients treated beyond the first line and those with PD-L1-negative disease. In addition, we explored a preliminary gene-based scoring model as a potential tool for identifying patients who may be more likely to benefit from this regimen. This study provides preliminary real-world evidence on Trop-2 ADC-based triplet therapy in pretreated mTNBC and supports further prospective evaluation.

Methods

Study design

This retrospective single-center cohort included 87 patients with breast cancer treated at the Breast Tumor Center, Sun Yat-sen Memorial Hospital, Sun Yat-sen University, between April 2020 and December 2025. Data such as patient gender, duration of enrollment, treatment regimen, genetic testing reports, and outcome indicators were collected. Relevant variables were obtained from hospital electronic medical record systems and biobank databases, with additional information supplemented by chart review when needed. All received at least two cycles of Trop-2 ADCs in the second-line or later breast cancer treatment. Some patients received the dual-agent combination therapy (ADC combined with PD-1 inhibitor) or the triple-agent combination therapy (ADC combined with PD-1 and VEGF inhibitors), depending on approved indications. Of the 87 patients, 85 received SG and 2 received Sacituzumab tirumotecan (sac-TMT); both sac-TMT-treated patients were in the monotherapy group. SG was administered intravenously at 10 mg/kg on days 1 and 8 of each 21-day cycle. Sac-TMT was administered intravenously at 5 mg/kg once every 2 weeks. The dual-agent regimen consisted of the Trop-2 ADCs plus pembrolizumab 200 mg intravenously every 3 weeks. The triple-agent regimen consisted of the Trop-2 ADCs, pembrolizumab at the same dose and schedule, and apatinib 250 mg orally once daily on a continuous basis. All components of the combination regimens were initiated concurrently. In this cohort, the starting dose of the Trop-2 ADC was determined based on baseline ECOG performance status. Patients with an ECOG score of 0–1 received the standard starting dose, and subsequent dose adjustments were made according to the occurrence and severity of treatment-related adverse events. Patients with an ECOG score of 2 received a reduced starting dose (< 80% of the standard dose) to optimize safety [21].

Study population

Female patients (aged 18–70 years) with a confirmed diagnosis of mTNBC; patients had received at least one prior line of systemic therapy; an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status ≤ 2 and an estimated survival of at least three months at the time of treatment initiation; presence of at least one measurable lesion or bone-only metastatic disease based on pre-treatment imaging; and resolution of prior treatment-related toxicities to ≤ Grade 1 per NCI CTCAE (version 5.0) (except for alopecia or toxicities deemed non-risky by investigators) were eligible for inclusion. Patients who had not received any treatment for metastatic breast cancer; those with known allergies to the active ingredient or other components of the study drug; those with documented resistance to Trop-2 ADCs therapy; and those who received radiotherapy, chemotherapy, endocrine therapy, or participated in other interventional clinical trials within 4 weeks prior to Trop-2 ADCs treatment were excluded. Patients with bone-only metastatic disease without measurable lesions were eligible for study inclusion but would have been excluded from the ORR analysis. Subjects identified in the current study were patients from relevant electronic health/medical record databases and biorepositories who met the inclusion and exclusion criteria.

Objectives and outcomes

The primary endpoint was ORR, defined by the proportion of patients whose best overall response was complete response (CR) or partial response (PR). The secondary endpoints was PFS. PFS was measured from treatment initiation until disease progression or death, whereas OS was measured from treatment initiation until death. Additional analyses include evaluating proportions of various Trop-2 ADCs treatment strategies used in clinical practice (e.g., monotherapy vs. combination therapy, chemotherapy vs. immunotherapy), the proportion of patients experiencing Grade 3 and above neutropenia after prophylactic granulocyte colony-stimulating factor interventions, and the correlation between Trop-2 ADCs dose reduction and treatment efficacy. For all time-to-event analyses, the start date is defined as the date of initial treatment for metastatic disease. Exploratory outcomes include analyzing changes in adverse event rates following preventive measures implemented prior to medication administration.

Statistical analysis

Baseline clinicopathologic characteristics, treatment exposure, and response outcomes were summarized descriptively. Categorical variables are presented as counts and percentages, and continuous variabls, as appropriate. Progrees, including age and body weight, are reported as medians with rangession-free survival (PFS) was defined as the time from treatment initiation to radiographic or clinical disease progression according to RECIST version 1.1 or death from any cause, whichever occurred first. Patients who were alive and progression-free at the last follow-up were censored on that date. Overall survival (OS) was defined as the time from treatment initiation to death from any cause. To reduce imbalance in baseline characteristics across treatment groups and mitigate treatment-selection bias, overlap weighting based on the propensity score was applied. Propensity scores were estimated using baseline covariates considered clinically relevant. Covariate balance before and after weighting was assessed using standardized mean differences. Objective response rate (ORR) and disease control rate (DCR) were compared between groups using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate. Odds ratios (ORs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were estimated using logistic regression models. Time-to-event outcomes, including PFS and OS, were analyzed using the Kaplan–Meier method and compared using the log-rank test. Hazard ratios (HRs) and 95% CIs were estimated using Cox proportional hazards regression models. Exploratory subgroup analyses for PFS were performed using Cox regression models with interaction testing. All statistical tests were two-sided, and P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant unless otherwise specified. All analyses were performed using SAS version 9.2 or later.

Results

Characteristics

Medical records from 87 individuals diagnosed with mTNBC who underwent Trop-2 ADCs treatment between April 2020 and December 2025 were retrospectively analyzed (Table 1). All patients were female. Among those receiving Trop-2 ADC monotherapy (n = 60), invasive ductal carcinoma was identified in 56 patients (93%), and a high Ki-67 labeling index, defined as ≥ 14%, was observed in 57 patients (95%). De novo stage IV breast cancer (dnBC-IV) was present in 13 patients (22%). Among the remaining 47 patients who developed metastatic recurrence after treatment for early-stage disease, the median disease-free survival (mDFS) was 20 months. Visceral metastases were observed in 32 patients (53%). In addition, brain, liver, and lung metastases were identified in 10 (17%), 27 (45%), and 24 (40%) patients, respectively. A total of 31 patients (52%) had received prior anti-PD-1 treatment. 38 (63%) had received prior platinum-based chemotherapy. Prior exposure to poly(ADP-ribose) polymerase (PARP) inhibitors and non-Trop-2 ADCs was documented in 3 (5%) and 11 (18%) patients, respectively. In the ADC combined with immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI) group (dual-agent combination group) (n = 13), dnBC-IV was observed in 2 patients (15%). Among the remaining 11 patients with recurrent disease, the mDFS was 19 months. Visceral metastases were seen in 5 patients (38%). Lung, liver, and brain metastases were observed in 6 (46%), 4 (31%), and 1 (8%) patients, respectively. A total of 9 patients (69%) had received prior anti-PD-1 treatment. Prior platinum exposure was documented in 8 patients (62%), whereas none had received a prior PARP inhibitor. Two patients (15%) had previously received a non-Trop-2 ADC. In the Trop-2 ADC combined with ICI and VEGF inhibitor group (triple-agent combination group) (n = 14), only 1 patient (7%) presented with dnBC-IV. Among the remaining 13 patients with recurrent disease, the median DFS was 13 months. Visceral metastases occurred in 7 patients (50%), including lung (29%), liver (43%), and brain (0%). A total of 7 patients (50%) had received prior anti-PD-1 treatment. 9 patients (64%) had received prior platinum-based chemotherapy. One patient (7%) had received a prior PARP inhibitor, and none had received a non-Trop-2 ADC. Treatment was initiated in the second or third line in 42 (70%), 11 (85%), and 13 (93%) patients in the monotherapy, dual-agent combination, and triple-agent combination groups, respectively; the corresponding numbers treated in the fourth line or later were 18 (30%), 2 (15%), and 1 (7%), respectively. Among all enrolled patients, a total of 59 PFS events occurred (Fig. S1). PD-L1 status was positive in 39 patients (45%) overall, including 27 (45%), 6 (46%), and 6 (43%) patients in the monotherapy, dual-agent combination, and triple-agent combination groups, respectively. However, PD-L1 status was unknown or not tested in 37 patients (43%) overall, including 25 (42%), 7 (54%), and 5 (36%) patients in the respective treatment groups.

Treatment efficacy

Objective response rates (ORR) were 31.7%, 38.5%, and 57.1% in the monotherapy, dual-agent combination, and triple-agent combination groups, respectively, while disease control rates (DCR) were 78.3%, 84.6%, and 92.9%, respectively (Table 2). The overall distribution of best overall response categories (PD, SD, PR, and CR) did not differ statistically among the three treatment groups (Kruskal–Wallis H = 3.598, P = 0.165; Fig. 3B). In exploratory pairwise comparisons of the ordinal response distribution, the comparison between the triple-agent combination group and the monotherapy group yielded P = 0.0279. The triple-agent combination group showed numerically higher ORR and DCR; however, given the exploratory nature and limited sample size of this retrospective analysis, these findings should be interpreted with caution and require prospective validation.

Among 87 patients included in the analysis, the median follow-up was 15.2 months (8.7–20.6 months), the median PFS was 15.4 months (95% CI: 5.3-NA) in the triple-agent combination group, 10.0 months (95% CI: 4.2-NA) in the dual-agent combination group, only 3.8 months (95% CI: 3.2–5.2) in the monotherapy group. In the three-group analysis, PFS also differed significantly among the three treatment groups (P = 0.007; Fig. 1A). In the pooled analysis, Trop-2 ADC combination therapy was associated with significantly longer PFS than Trop-2 ADC monotherapy (HR, 0.387; 95% CI, 0.209–0.718; P = 0.002; Fig. 1B). No significant differences were observed between the triple-agent combination group and dual-agent combination group, which might be due to the limited number of patients. To reduce baseline imbalance between treatment groups, overlap weighting based on the propensity score was performed. Covariate balance improved substantially after weighting, with all adjusted absolute standardized mean differences below 0.1 (Fig. S2). In overlap-weighted Cox analysis, combination therapy remained associated with a significantly lower risk of progression or death compared with monotherapy (weighted HR, 0.49; 95% CI, 0.26–0.91; P = 0.024). Further analyses of the overall cohort showed that patients receiving Trop-2 ADC in the second- to third-line setting had significantly longer PFS than those treated beyond the third line (median PFS, 5.8 [4.0–9.0] vs. 3.0 [1.9-NA] months; HR, 1.966; 95% CI, 1.114–3.469; P = 0.017; Fig. 1C). Likewise, patients without liver metastasis had significantly longer PFS than those with liver metastasis (median PFS, 9.0 [4.5–16.9] vs. 3.3 [2.4–6.2] months; HR, 2.540; 95% CI, 1.491–4.327; P < 0.001; Fig. 1D). Patients without brain metastasis also had significantly longer PFS than those with brain metastasis (median PFS, 5.3 [4.0-8.9] vs. 1.8 [1.4-NA] months; HR, 2.841; 95% CI, 1.428–5.655; P = 0.002; Fig. 1E). Similarly, patients without visceral metastasis had significantly longer PFS than those with visceral metastasis (median PFS, 6.4 [4.6–13.4] vs. 3.8 [2.3–6.4] months; HR, 1.731; 95% CI, 1.040–2.880; P = 0.032; Fig. 1F).

Univariable Cox regression analysis identified several factors associated with PFS, including later line of therapy at ADC initiation, liver metastasis, brain metastasis, and PD-L1-positive status, whereas an ADC starting dose ≥ 80% was associated with improved PFS (Table 3). Compared with the monotherapy group, both the dual-agent combination group (HR, 0.41; 95% CI, 0.17–0.96; P = 0.039) and the triple-agent combination group (HR, 0.40; 95% CI, 0.18–0.89; P = 0.024) showed significantly better PFS in univariable analysis (Fig. 2A). For prior treatment exposures, the HR for prior platinum exposure was 1.17 (95% CI, 0.68–1.99; P = 0.571), and that for prior PARP inhibitor exposure was 0.77 (95% CI, 0.19–3.19; P = 0.721); the corresponding HR for prior non-Trop-2 ADC exposure was 1.95 (95% CI, 1.00–3.79; P = 0.050). In the multivariable Cox regression model, variables with a univariable P value < 0.20 were considered for inclusion, while PD-L1 status, line of therapy, and prior exposure to platinum agents, PARP inhibitors, and non-Trop-2 ADCs were retained a priori as adjustment covariates. After multivariable adjustment, liver metastasis was associated with an HR of 2.38 (95% CI, 1.27–4.46; P = 0.007) and brain metastasis with an HR of 2.51 (95% CI, 1.11–5.65; P = 0.027). Relative to monotherapy, the HR was 0.38 (95% CI, 0.15–0.97; P = 0.042) for the dual-agent combination group and 0.44 (95% CI, 0.19–1.01; P = 0.054) for the triple-agent combination group (Fig. 2A). The adjusted HRs for prior platinum exposure, prior PARP inhibitor exposure, and prior non-Trop-2 ADC exposure were 1.81 (95% CI, 0.97–3.40; P = 0.064), 0.80 (95% CI, 0.18–3.50; P = 0.770), and 1.57 (95% CI, 0.65–3.83; P = 0.319), respectively. After multivariable adjustment, the HR for PD-L1-positive status was 0.49 (95% CI, 0.22–1.09; P = 0.079), and the HR for fourth-line-or-later treatment was 1.37 (95% CI, 0.53–3.54; P = 0.519).

To further assess the robustness of the findings with respect to treatment line and PD-L1 status, we performed restricted sensitivity analyses. In a sensitivity analysis restricted to the 66 patients who initiated Trop-2 ADC treatment in the second- or third-line setting, median PFS was 5.2, 10.0, and 6.5 months in the monotherapy, dual-agent combination, and triple-agent combination groups, respectively. The overall difference did not reach statistical significance (log-rank P = 0.072); relative to monotherapy, the HRs were 0.44 (95% CI, 0.17–1.14; P = 0.093) for the dual-agent combination group and 0.46 (95% CI, 0.19–1.11; P = 0.084) for the triple-agent combination group (Fig. S3A). Among the 39 patients with PD-L1-positive disease, median PFS was 5.0 months in the monotherapy group, 4.2 months in the dual-agent combination group, and not reached in the triple-agent combination group, with a significant overall difference among the three groups (log-rank P = 0.027). Relative to monotherapy, the HRs were 0.55 (95% CI, 0.16–1.87; P = 0.339) for the dual-agent combination group and 0.11 (95% CI, 0.01–0.81; P = 0.031) for the triple-agent combination group (Fig. S3B).

Safety

The safety population included all treated patients (n = 87). Grade ≥ 3 treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs) were observed in 12/60 patients (20.0%) in the monotherapy group, 2/13 (15.4%) in the dual-agent combination group, and 2/14 (14.3%) in the triple-agent combination group (Table 5; Fig. 3A). The most common grade ≥ 3 TRAEs were leukopenia and neutropenia across all groups. Grade ≥ 3 anemia and thrombocytopenia were observed only in the monotherapy group, while grade ≥ 3 aminotransferase elevation was more frequent in the triple-agent combination group. No grade ≥ 3 febrile neutropenia, hyperbilirubinemia, rash, or oral ulcer was reported. Overall, the incidence of grade ≥ 3 TRAEs was comparable among the three treatment groups, with no statistically significant difference observed (Fisher’s exact test, P = 1.00; Fig. 3C).

Exploration of biomarkers

To identify which patients might benefit from triple-agent combination therapy, we developed a scoring system based on the mutant genes of the patients’tumors. This scoring system contains 17 genes (Fig. 4A), which are mainly related to the drug sensitivity of tumors to Trop-2 ADCs, homologous recombination repair (HRR) and angiogenesis [22–24]. Each gene was assigned a weighted score based on its mechanistic association with therapeutic sensitivity or resistance: genes promoting therapeutic response were given positive scores (+ 1 to + 2), while those contributing to resistance were assigned negative scores (-4). The score weight was determined by the mechanistic relevance of their mechanistic involvement in drug efficacy as inferred from prior functional studies and biological pathways [25, 26]. Patients who were sensitive to triple therapy had a higher total score (Fig. 4B); Patients with total score greater than 0 are classified as the population that can benefit from triple-agent combination therapy, Fisher’s test determined that the difference was statistically significant (Fig. 4C). The distribution of mutation types in sensitive and resistant patients is shown in Fig. 4D.

Discussion

In this retrospective observational cohort, we characterized the real‑world effectiveness and safety of Trop‑2 ADC-based triple therapy for mTNBC. The triple‑agent regimen showed a favorable efficacy profile compared with Trop-2- ADC monotherapy, particularly with respect to disease control and PFS; exploratory subgroup analyses showed no significant interaction between treatment effect and PD-L1 status or prior anti-PD-1 exposure, suggesting that the activity of the triple-agent combination may extend across clinically relevant subgroups defined by these factors. This possibility is of particular interest in pretreated mTNBC, although it requires further validation in larger cohorts. Moreover, the regimen was generally well tolerated, and its treatment‑related adverse event profile was consistent with the known safety of Trop‑2 ADCs, aligning with NeoSTAR and other real‑world reports [27]. Finally, we developed a preliminary genomic scoring system to explore molecular features potentially associated with differential sensitivity to the triple-agent strategy.

As later-line therapy for metastatic TNBC, immune checkpoint inhibitors alone or in combination with chemotherapy have shown limited clinical benefit [28]. Accumulating evidence has shown that combining anti-angiogenic therapy with PD-1 blockade produces complementary, dose-dependent synergy. Mechanistically, low-dose VEGFR2 inhibition promotes tumor vascular normalization and reverses immunosuppression, along with enhancing CD8⁺ T cell activity. The OPN-TGF-β pathway further contributes by upregulating PD-1 on these T cells, thereby increasing their sensitivity to PD-1 blockade [29–32]. Previous clinical research explored the potential of adding angiogenesis inhibition to chemoimmunotherapy. In one multicenter phase II trial, the triple combination of camrelizumab, apatinib, and eribulin achieved an ORR of 37.0% and a median PFS of 8.1 months in pretreated mTNBC, regardless of PD-L1 status [19, 33]. This provided a rationale for exploring whether anti-angiogenic therapy could also enhance a Trop-2 ADC plus PD-1 inhibitor backbone. Our findings on this triple-agent combination were previously presented at SABCS 2024 [34]. In the present study, the triple-agent combination showed the most favorable efficacy profile, whereas the dual-agent combination group demonstrated intermediate benefit. This stepwise pattern is biologically plausible and raises the possibility that anti-angiogenic therapy may contribute additional activity to a Trop-2 ADC plus PD-1 inhibitor backbone. Given its established role in vascular normalization and modulation of the tumor immune microenvironment, anti-angiogenic therapy may have further potentiated the activity of both PD-1 blockade and Trop-2 ADCs. Nevertheless, the incremental contribution of anti-angiogenic therapy cannot be established from the present comparison alone and remains an important question for prospective investigation.

Exploratory subgroup analyses did not identify significant evidence that PD-L1 status modified the efficacy of the triple-agent combination. Although limited by sample size, this finding suggests that the activity of this regimen may not be confined to PD-L1-positive disease, which is relevant because much of the established evidence supporting checkpoint inhibitor-based therapy in mTNBC has been generated in biomarker-selected populations [35–39]. However, these findings should be regarded as hypothesis-generating rather than as direct evidence of PD-L1-independent activity and require confirmation in prospective randomized controlled trials. Additionally, patients who initiated Trop-2 ADCs therapy in the second- to third-line setting had longer PFS than those treated beyond the third line, raising the possibility that treatment timing may influence clinical outcomes.

The influence of previous systemic therapy is another important consideration in this heavily pretreated population. In this study, only a small number of patients had prior PARP inhibitor or non-Trop ADC exposure.Given the small numbers of exposed patients, these treatment histories were not further evaluated in treatment-effect subgroup analyses. Exposure to platinum agents may reflect underlying DNA damage repair characteristics as well as prior platinum sensitivity, both of which are closely linked to tumor biology and treatment responsiveness in mTNBC [40]. In our exploratory analyses, the point estimates for the triple-agent regimen relative to monotherapy showed descriptive differences between patients with and without prior platinum exposure. Patients with prior platinum exposure may represent a potential subgroup that could derive greater benefit from the triple-agent combination; however, given the retrospective and exploratory nature of this analysis, this observation should be interpreted with caution and requires prospective validation. Further confirmation is expected from our ongoing prospective randomized trial.

We also did not observe significant evidence that prior anti-PD-1 exposure modified the efficacy of the triple-agent combination. This observation helps to address an important clinical consideration, as primary resistance to immune checkpoint inhibitors is observed in approximately 60% of PD-L1-negative mTNBCs, often leading to caution regarding immunotherapy rechallenge, especially after prior progression [41–43]. Preclinical and early clinical studies, such as the FUTURE-C-PLUS trial, have indicated that combining antiangiogenic agents with immunotherapy may help modify this resistant state [44]. Prospective evidence for Trop-2 ADC and PD-1 inhibitor combinations has largely been generated in populations with limited prior exposure to immune checkpoint inhibition, leaving the role of such combinations after previous immunotherapy less well defined [18]. In this context, our analysis provides early evidence from a cohort that included a notable proportion of patients previously treated with PD-1 inhibitors, and suggests that previous immunotherapy exposure may not necessarily preclude activity of the triple-agent combination. The potential for anti-angiogenic therapy and ADC-mediated tumor cell killing to reshape the immune microenvironment provides a plausible basis for investigating this strategy after prior checkpoint inhibition, although whether it can overcome established immune resistance requires prospective evaluation.

In this cohort, baseline characteristics such as treatment line and PD-L1 status showed descriptive differences across treatment groups. Sensitivity analyses restricted to second- or third-line patients and to those with PD-L1-positive disease yielded descriptive HR estimates that were directionally consistent with the primary analysis. The interaction P-values for treatment line and PD-L1 status were 0.613 and 0.098, respectively. However, as this was a retrospective, non-randomized study, these analyses remain exploratory, and the potential for selection bias and residual confounding cannot be fully excluded. Notably, the key variables examined in this study, including prior treatment history and PD-L1 status, have been incorporated as stratification factors in our ongoing prospective randomized controlled trial, which will provide more robust evidence to validate or refine the present observations.

Regarding safety, with prophylactic measures implemented in all patients (n = 87), the incidence of grade ≥ 3 TRAEs was 20.0%, 15.4%, and 14.3% in the monotherapy group, dual-agent combination group, and triple-agent combination group, respectively. The most frequent high-grade events were leukopenia and neutropenia. No treatment-related deaths were recorded, and no dose reductions or treatment discontinuations were observed. These findings support the clinical feasibility of the triple-agent combination. However, given the retrospective nature of this study, the possibility of underreporting or incomplete capture of adverse events cannot be excluded. Therefore, the tolerability of this regimen should be further evaluated in prospective studies with standardized toxicity monitoring [45].

The association between metastatic pattern and outcome further highlights the importance of underlying disease biology when interpreting treatment effectiveness. Liver and brain involvement represent clinically aggressive disease states and may influence prognosis independently of treatment selection, while visceral involvement more broadly may reflect greater disease complexity. These results underscore the importance of improved risk stratification and more individualized treatment strategies in biologically aggressive disease. To further explore this issue, we developed a preliminary gene-centric scoring system comprising 17 genes related to sensitivity to Trop-2 ADCs, angiogenesis-related pathways, and tumor mutational characteristics. Among the genes associated with treatment sensitivity, some genes (e.g., ATR) may increase tumor sensitivity to Trop-2 ADCs by inhibiting homologous recombination repair or may be associated with greater benefit from immunotherapy through increased mutational burden [46–48]. Other genes, such as VEGFRA and VEGFRB, are related to angiogenesis-associated pathways and may therefore be associated with sensitivity to antiangiogenic therapy. By integrating multiple biologically relevant dimensions, this scoring system provides an exploratory framework for investigating molecular determinants of treatment response. Its principal value at this stage is hypothesis generation, particularly for identifying candidate biological features that may warrant validation in future biomarker-oriented studies.

First, the retrospective nature of this study inevitably introduces selection bias and unmeasured confounders. Consequently, our findings should be interpreted as exploratory. To move beyond these constraints, we are actively conducting a prospective randomized controlled trial (ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT06851299 || Registration Date: 02/25/2025). This trial is designed to provide more evidence on the efficacy and safety of Trop-2 ADC-based combination regimens, and simultaneously to prospectively validate the clinical applicability of our genomic risk score.

Conclusions

In summary, this real-world study suggests that Trop-2 ADC-based combination therapy, particularly the triple-agent regimen, may represent a potential treatment strategy for pretreated metastatic triple-negative breast cancer, with signals of improved outcomes. The efficacy and safety profiles of these regimens are currently being further evaluated in an ongoing phase III randomized controlled trial.

Long-term behaviour of the primitive equations with wind-driven boundary conditions: Convergence to the Ekman spiral
Tim Binz

Abstract
. In this article we investigate the long-term behaviour of the 3D incompressible, primitive equations with wind-driven boundary conditions and Coriolis force. We show that every solution converges exponentially fast to the Ekman spiral as t → + ∞ t\to+\infty . In particular, this implies that the Ekman spiral is the unique equilibrium of the system.

1.
Introduction

In this paper we study a classic scenario: the effect of wind blowing over the ocean (at large scales).
The primitive equations are the standard model for oceanic
dynamics in large scales
[
47
,
43
]
. They are derived from the Navier-Stokes equations by assuming the hydrostatic balance for the pressure term.
The 3D incompressible
primitive equations
with gravity in a rotational frame are given by

(PE)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}v-\nu_{\mathrm{H}}\Delta_{\mathrm{H}}v-\nu_{z}\partial_{z}^{2}v+u\cdot\nabla v+\frac{1}{\rho_{0}}\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}\pi+fv^{\perp}&=0,&&\text{ in }(0,T)\times\Omega,\\
\partial_{z}\pi+\rho_{0}g&=0,&&\text{ in }(0,T)\times\Omega,\\
\mathrm{div}\,u&=0,&&\text{ in }(0,T)\times\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

on a periodic layer
\Omega:=\mathbb{T}^{2}\times(-h,0)
with boundary
\Gamma=\Gamma_{u}\cup\Gamma_{b}
, where
\Gamma_{u}:=\mathbb{T}^{2}\times\{0\}
and
\Gamma_{b}:=\mathbb{T}^{2}\times\{-h\}
.
Here
u=(v,w)
denotes the velocity of the fluid, where
v=(v_{1},v_{2})
is the horizontal component of the velocity and
w
is the vertical one. Further,
\pi
is the pressure of the fluid and
\nu_{\mathrm{H}}>0
and
\nu_{z}>0
are the horizontal and vertical viscosities of the fluid.
Moreover,
f\in\mathbb{R}
is the
f
-plane approximation of the Coriolis force,
v^{\perp}:=(-v_{2},v_{1})^{\top}
,
\rho_{0}>0
the reference density,
g>0
the gravity constant and
h>0
the depth.
Finally,
\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}g=(\partial_{x}g,\partial_{y}g)^{\top}
denotes the horizontal gradient,
\divH G=\partial_{x}G_{x}+\partial_{y}G_{y}
the horizontal divergence and
\Delta_{\mathrm{H}}:=\divH\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}
the horizontal Laplacian.
The
wind-driven boundary conditions
at the upper surface describes the effect of wind blowing over the surface of the ocean

(1.1)

\partial_{z}v=\tau,\quad\text{ on }(0,T)\times\Gamma_{u},

where
\tau\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
is the given (constant) wind stress.
The lower boundary conditions connect our problem to the region of deep water, where the geostrophic balance holds. We call it the
geostrophic boundary conditions
and it reads as

(1.2)

v=v_{g},\quad\text{ on }(0,T)\times\Gamma_{b},

where
v_{g}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
is the (constant) geostrophic flow. Further, we assume that there is no vertical velocity at the boundaries, i.e.

(1.3)

w=0,\quad\text{ on }(0,T)\times\Gamma.

Finally, this system is completed by the initial data
v(0)=v_{0}
.
The
Ekman spiral
occurs when wind drags on the surface of the ocean. The Ekman spiral

v_{\mathrm{E}}
is the unique solution of the balance between the Coriolis force and the vertical dissipation, i.e.
v_{\mathrm{E}}(x,y,z):=v_{\mathrm{E}}(z)
solves

(Ekman)

\nu_{z}\cdot\partial_{z}^{2}v_{\mathrm{E}}=fv_{\mathrm{E}}^{\perp}\quad\text{ in }\Omega,

supplemented by the wind-driven boundary conditions (
1.1
) and geostrophic boundary conditions (
1.2
).

In the sequel we consider the regime of parameters where

(1.4)

\displaystyle C_{\mathrm{E}}:=\frac{|f|\cdot h^{4}}{2\cdot\nu_{\mathrm{H}}\cdot\nu_{z}^{2}}\cdot\bigl((k_{1}^{2}+k_{2}^{2})e^{\frac{2h}{d}}+(k_{3}^{2}+k_{4}^{2})+2\cdot|k_{1}k_{3}-k_{2}k_{4}|+2\cdot|k_{2}k_{3}+k_{1}k_{4}|\bigr)<1

holds.
Here
d:=(\frac{2\nu_{z}}{|f|})^{\frac{1}{2}}
is the
thickness of the Ekman layer
and
the coefficients
k_{1},\dots,k_{4}\in\mathbb{R}
are given in (
A.1
) explicitly.
The coefficients depend only on the data
\tau,v_{g}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
f\in\mathbb{R}
and
h>0
.
As shown in
[
20
]
such a smallness condition is necessary in order to prove stability of the Ekman spiral in the Navier-Stokes equations.

In the present article we investigate
the long-term behaviour of the the primitive equations (
PE
) subject to the wind-driven conditions (
1.1
) and the geostrophic boundary conditions (
1.2
) under the smallness assumption (
1.4

Assuming (
1.4

PE
) with (

dynamics it is quite surprising that the stability of the Ekman spiral in the primitive equations has not been studied so far.

Our main result,
Theorem 2.1
, does not only settle this question but it shows a significantly stronger result: convergence of solutions of (
PE
) to the Ekman spiral as
t\to+\infty
.
Hence,
in contrast to the results about the Ekman spiral in Navier-Stokes equations, no smallness conditions of the initial data is needed here: The solution of (

Our proof relies on the analysis of the
Ekman-primitive equations
(
4.1
) satisfied by the difference
v_{d}:=v-v_{\mathrm{E}}
of the solution

to the primitive equations subject to the wind-driven boundary conditions (

.
We apply the maximal regularity theory of Prüss-Wilke

]
and show
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}_{t}\mathrm{H}^{1}
-
\mathrm{L}^{2}_{t}\mathrm{H}^{2}
-a priori estimates. The main ingredients for this are the (unshifted) maximal regularity of the

, see

[

Section 4
, which are an improvement of the bounds from
[

]
, as well as, the aforementioned a-priori estimates. Since the location of the spectrum of the hydrostatic Ekman-Stokes operator is sensitive to the lower order terms involving the Ekman spiral, we need the smallness condition (

For the a-priori estimates we follow the strategy of Hieber-Kashiwabara
[

using the splitting in baroclinic and barotropic modes and interpolation techniques. In each step here we have to bound additional terms coming from the Ekman spiral.
Let us comment on these estimates in more detail.
In the energy estimate,

), to control the terms coming from the Ekman spiral.
In the

-estimate of the

-estimate of the barotropic mode,

Additionally, in

-bounds for all

For

These bounds as well as the method to prove them are of independent interest.

Main result

Our main theorem shows that the 3D incompressible, primitive equations with wind-stress, Coriolis force and gravity generate a semi-flow on
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Omega)\cap\mathrm{L}^{2}_{\overline{\sigma}}(\Omega)

1

1

\mathrm{L}^{2}_{\overline{\sigma}}(\Omega)
we refer to

which converges exponentially fast to its unique equilibrium: the Ekman spiral.

Theorem 2.1

(Long-term behaviour)
.

If (
1.4
) is satisfied,
then the 3D incompressible primitive equations with Coriolis force (
PE
) subject to the wind-driven boundary conditions (
1.3
) and the geostrophic boundary conditions (
1.2

v_{0}\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Omega)\cap\mathrm{L}^{2}_{\overline{\sigma}}(\Omega)

v-v_{\mathrm{E}}\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega))\cap\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathrm{H}^{2}(\Omega))

The solution

\|v(t)-v_{\mathrm{E}}\|_{\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Omega)}\leq C\mathrm{e}^{\omega_{0}t}\to 0\qquad\text{ as }\kern 5.0ptt\to+\infty,

Let us now make a few comments on our main result.

Remark 2.2

(Pressure and vertical velocity)
.

The vertical velocity
w
is determined uniquely by the horizontal velocity
v
and the geostrophic flow
v_{g}
by

w(x,y,z)=v_{g}-\int_{-h}^{z}\divH v(x,y,\xi)\,\,\mathrm{d}\xi.

Moreover, the pressure
\pi
is determined uniquely (up to constants) by the horizontal velocity
v
via the elliptic equation

\Delta_{\mathrm{H}}\pi=\rho_{0}\cdot\biggl(-\frac{\nu_{z}}{h}\divH\bigl(\partial_{z}v\big|_{\Gamma_{b}}\bigr)+\frac{1}{h}\divH\biggl(\int_{-h}^{0}v\cdot\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}v-v\cdot\divH v\biggr)\biggr)-\rho_{0}g\cdot z.

Remark 2.3

(Convergence rate)
.

The convergence rate
\omega_{0}<0
in

is the spectral bound of the linearization, i.e. of the hydrostatic Ekman-Stokes operator with homogeneous Neumann boundary conditions at the upper boundary and homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions at the lower boundary. Since the hydrostatic Stokes operator has compact resolvent
\omega_{0}
coincides with the largest eigenvalue.

The next result gives precise control over the derivatives of the difference between the solution

to the primitive equations (
PE
) and the Ekman spiral

Proposition 2.4

(
\mathrm{H}^{k}
-bounds)
.

For for all

the

-norms of the difference are controlled by bounds

\max_{t\in(0,T)}\|v(t)-v_{\mathrm{E}}\|_{\mathrm{H}^{k}(\Omega)}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\|v(t)-v_{\mathrm{E}}\|_{\mathrm{H}^{k+1}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq B_{k}(T).

Since (

Remark 2.5

(Ekman Spiral)
.

The Ekman spiral

is explicitly given as

\displaystyle v_{1}(z)

\displaystyle=k_{1}\sin(\tfrac{z}{d})e^{-\tfrac{z}{d}}+k_{2}\cos(\tfrac{z}{d})e^{-\tfrac{z}{d}}+k_{3}\sin(\tfrac{z}{d})e^{\tfrac{z}{d}}+k_{4}\cos(\tfrac{z}{d})e^{\tfrac{z}{d}},

\displaystyle v_{2}(z)

\displaystyle=k_{1}\cos(\tfrac{z}{d})e^{-\tfrac{z}{d}}-k_{2}\sin(\tfrac{z}{d})e^{-\tfrac{z}{d}}-k_{3}\cos(\tfrac{z}{d})e^{\tfrac{z}{d}}+k_{4}\sin(\tfrac{z}{d})e^{\tfrac{z}{d}},

Proposition 3.1

(Ekman spiral is an equilibrium)

The Ekman spiral

4.
Local wellposedness and Stability of the Ekman Spiral

In this section we introduce the functional analytic set-up to treat the primitive equations (
PE
) with (
1.1
) and (
1.2
). Moreover, we provide necessary information about the linearization and prove local well-posedness of the system and finally asymptotic stability of the Ekman spiral.

We consider the differences
v_{d}:=v-v_{\mathrm{E}}
,
w_{d}:=w
and
\pi_{d}:=\pi-\pi_{\mathrm{E}}
. Using

\displaystyle u_{d}\cdot\nabla v_{d}+v_{\mathrm{E}}\cdot\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}v_{d}+w_{d}\partial_{z}v_{\mathrm{E}}

\displaystyle=(u-u_{E})\cdot\nabla(v-v_{\mathrm{E}})+v_{\mathrm{E}}\cdot\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}(v-v_{\mathrm{E}})+w\partial_{z}v_{\mathrm{E}}

\displaystyle=(v-v_{\mathrm{E}})\cdot\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}(v-v_{\mathrm{E}})+w\partial_{z}(v-v_{\mathrm{E}})+v_{\mathrm{E}}\cdot\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}(v-v_{\mathrm{E}})+w\partial_{z}v_{\mathrm{E}}

\displaystyle=v\cdot\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}(v-v_{\mathrm{E}})+w\partial_{z}v=u\cdot\nabla v.

we obtain from
(
PE
)
the transformed system

(4.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}v_{d}-\nu_{\mathrm{H}}\Delta_{\mathrm{H}}v_{d}-\nu_{z}\partial_{z}^{2}v_{d}+u_{d}\cdot\nabla v_{d}\phantom{aaa}&&&\\
+v_{\mathrm{E}}\cdot\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}v_{d}+w_{d}\partial_{z}v_{\mathrm{E}}+\frac{1}{\rho_{0}}\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}\pi_{d}+fv_{d}^{\perp}&=0,&&\text{ in }(0,T)\times\Omega,\\
\partial_{z}\pi_{d}&=0,&&\text{ in }(0,T)\times\Omega,\\
\mathrm{div}\,u_{d}&=0,&&\text{ in }(0,T)\times\Omega,\\
w_{d}&=0,&&\text{ on }(0,T)\times\Gamma,\\
\partial_{z}v_{d}&=0&&\text{ on }(0,T)\times\Gamma_{u},\\
v_{d}&=0&&\text{ on }(0,T)\times\Gamma_{b},\\
v_{d}(0)&=v_{0}-v_{\mathrm{E}},&&\text{ in }\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

We call these equations the
Ekman-primitive equations
.

Following

6

Using

This motivates the definition of the

\mathrm{L}^{2}_{\overline{\sigma}}(\Omega):=\overline{\{v\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}(\Omega)\colon\divH\overline{v}=0\}}^{\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}},

Lemma 5.4

(Estimates of the 2D Stokes equations)
.

Let
(u,\pi)
be the solution of the two-dimensional Stokes equations with rotation

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u-\nu_{\mathrm{H}}\Delta_{\mathrm{H}}u+\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}\pi+u^{\perp}

\displaystyle=k,

\displaystyle\divH u

\displaystyle=0

for
k\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,T;\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2}))
. Then
(u,\pi)
satisfies the estimate

\sqrt{\nu_{\mathrm{H}}}\cdot\partial_{t}\|\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}^{2}+\nu_{\mathrm{H}}\cdot\|\Delta_{\mathrm{H}}u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}^{2}+\|\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}\pi\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}^{2}\leq C\cdot(\|h\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}^{2}+\|u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}^{2}).

Proof.

Multiplying by
\partial_{t}u-\Delta_{\mathrm{H}}u
, integration by parts and Young’s inequality yield

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}^{2}+\sqrt{\nu_{\mathrm{H}}}\partial_{t}\|\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}^{2}+\nu_{\mathrm{H}}\|\Delta_{\mathrm{H}}u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}k(\partial_{t}u-\Delta_{\mathrm{H}}u)

\displaystyle\leq C\|k\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}^{2}+\varepsilon\|\partial_{t}u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}^{2}+\varepsilon\|\Delta_{\mathrm{H}}u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}^{2},

where we have used

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}\pi\Delta_{\mathrm{H}}u

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\pi\divH\Delta_{\mathrm{H}}u=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\pi\Delta_{\mathrm{H}}\divH u=0,

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}u\Delta_{\mathrm{H}}u

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}u^{\perp}\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}u=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}(\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}u)^{\perp}\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}u=0.

By absorbing this implies

\|\partial_{t}u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}^{2}+\sqrt{\nu_{\mathrm{H}}}\cdot\partial_{t}\|\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}^{2}+\nu_{\mathrm{H}}\cdot\|\Delta_{\mathrm{H}}u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}^{2}\leq C\cdot\|k\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}^{2},

The pressure satisfies the elliptic equation

\Delta_{\mathrm{H}}\pi=\divH k-\divH u^{\perp}

which yields to the estimate

\|\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}\pi\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\cdot(\|\divH k\|_{\mathrm{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}+\|\divH u^{\perp}\|_{\mathrm{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{T}^{2})})\leq C\cdot(\|k\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}+\|u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}),

where we have used the characterization of the negative order Sobolev space

\mathrm{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{T}^{2})=\{\divH k\colon k\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})\}.

Adding both estimates yields the claim.
∎

\displaystyle+d\bigl(e^{\frac{2h}{d}}\sin(\tfrac{2h}{d})+e^{\tfrac{2h}{d}}\cos(\tfrac{2h}{d})+1\bigr)\cdot\tau_{1}-d\bigl(-e^{\tfrac{2h}{d}}\sin(\tfrac{2h}{d})+e^{\tfrac{2h}{d}}\cos(\tfrac{2h}{d})+1\bigr)\cdot\tau_{2}\biggr),

\displaystyle k_{2}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2\left(e^{\tfrac{4h}{d}}+2e^{\tfrac{2h}{d}}\cos(\tfrac{2h}{d})+1\right)}\cdot\biggl(-2e^{\tfrac{h}{d}}\bigl(e^{\tfrac{2h}{d}}+1\bigr)\cos(\tfrac{h}{d})\cdot v_{g,1}-2e^{\tfrac{h}{d}}\bigl(e^{\tfrac{2h}{d}}-1\bigr)\sin(\tfrac{h}{d})\cdot v_{g,2}

\displaystyle+d\bigl(-e^{\tfrac{2h}{d}}\sin(\tfrac{2h}{d})+e^{\tfrac{2h}{d}}\cos(\tfrac{2h}{d})+1\bigr)\cdot\tau_{1}+d\bigl(e^{\tfrac{2h}{d}}\sin(\tfrac{2h}{d})+e^{\tfrac{2h}{d}}\cos(\tfrac{2h}{d})+1\bigr)\cdot\tau_{2}\biggr),

\displaystyle k_{3}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2\left(e^{\tfrac{4h}{d}}+2e^{\tfrac{2h}{d}}\cos(\tfrac{2h}{d})+1\right)}\cdot e^{\tfrac{h}{d}}\biggl(2\bigl(e^{\tfrac{2h}{d}}-1\bigr)\sin(\tfrac{h}{d})\cdot v_{g,1}-2\bigl(e^{\tfrac{2h}{d}}+1\bigr)\cos(\tfrac{h}{r})\cdot v_{g,2}

\displaystyle+de^{\tfrac{h}{d}}\bigl(e^{\tfrac{2h}{d}}-\sin(\tfrac{2h}{d})+\cos(\tfrac{2h}{d})\bigr)\cdot\tau_{1}-de^{\tfrac{h}{d}}\bigl(e^{\tfrac{2h}{d}}+\sin(\tfrac{2h}{d})+\cos(\tfrac{2h}{d})\bigr)\cdot\tau_{2}\biggr),

\displaystyle k_{4}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2\left(e^{\tfrac{4h}{d}}+2e^{\tfrac{2h}{d}}\cos(\tfrac{2h}{d})+1\right)}\cdot e^{\tfrac{h}{d}}\biggl(2\bigl(e^{\tfrac{2h}{d}}+1\bigr)\cos(\tfrac{h}{d})\cdot v_{g,1}+2\bigl(e^{\frac{2h}{d}}-1\bigr)\sin(\tfrac{h}{d})\cdot v_{g,2}

\displaystyle+de^{\tfrac{h}{d}}\bigl(e^{\tfrac{2h}{d}}+\sin(\tfrac{2h}{d})+\cos(\tfrac{2h}{d})\bigr)\cdot\tau_{1}+de^{\tfrac{h}{d}}\bigl(e^{\tfrac{2h}{d}}-\sin(\tfrac{2h}{d})+\cos(\tfrac{2h}{d})\bigr)\cdot\tau_{2}\biggr).

Proof.

Direct calculations.
∎

We conclude the following formulas for the derivative of the Ekman spiral.

Lemma A.2
.

For
v_{\mathrm{E}}=(v_{1},v_{2})^{\top}
we have

\displaystyle\partial_{z}v_{1}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d}\cdot\biggl(k_{1}\cdot\bigl(\cos(\tfrac{z}{d})-\sin(\tfrac{z}{d})\bigr)e^{-\tfrac{z}{d}}+k_{2}\cdot\bigl(-\sin(\tfrac{z}{d})-\cos(\tfrac{z}{d})\bigr)e^{-\tfrac{z}{d}}

\displaystyle\qquad+k_{3}\cdot\bigl(\sin(\tfrac{z}{d})+\cos(\tfrac{z}{d})\bigr)e^{\tfrac{z}{d}}+k_{4}\cdot\bigl(-\sin(\tfrac{z}{d})+\cos(\tfrac{z}{d})\bigr)e^{\tfrac{z}{d}}\biggr),

\displaystyle\partial_{z}v_{2}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d}\cdot\biggl(k_{1}\cdot\bigl(-\sin(\tfrac{z}{d})-\cos(\tfrac{z}{d})\bigr)e^{-\tfrac{z}{d}}+k_{2}\cdot\bigl(\sin(\tfrac{z}{d})-\cos(\tfrac{z}{d})\bigr)e^{-\tfrac{z}{d}}

\displaystyle\qquad+k_{3}\cdot\bigl(\sin(\tfrac{z}{d})-\cos(\tfrac{z}{d})\bigr)e^{\tfrac{z}{d}}+k_{4}\cdot\bigl(\sin(\tfrac{z}{d})+\cos(\tfrac{z}{d})\bigr)e^{\tfrac{z}{d}}\biggr),

where
d:=\sqrt{\frac{\nu_{z}}{2|f|}}
is the thickness of the Ekman layer and
the coefficients
k_{1},\dots,k_{4}\in\mathbb{R}
are given by (
A.1
).

Proof.

Direct calculations.
∎

Finally, we obtain the following bound for the Ekman spiral.

Lemma A.3
.

We have

\displaystyle\|\partial_{z}v_{\mathrm{E}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(-h,0)}^{2}\leq\frac{2}{d^{2}}\cdot\bigl((k_{1}^{2}+k_{2}^{2})e^{\frac{2h}{d}}+(k_{3}^{2}+k_{4}^{2})+2\cdot|k_{1}k_{3}-k_{2}k_{4}|+2\cdot|k_{2}k_{3}+k_{1}k_{4}|\bigr),

where
d:=\sqrt{\frac{\nu_{z}}{2|f|}}
is the thickness of the Ekman layer and
the coefficients
k_{1},\dots,k_{4}\in\mathbb{R}
are given by (
A.1
).

Proof.

Using
Appendix A
we obtain

\displaystyle|\partial_{z}v_{\mathrm{E}}(z)|^{2}

\displaystyle=(\partial_{z}v_{1}(z))^{2}+(\partial_{z}v_{2}(z))^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{d^{2}}\cdot\left((k_{1}^{2}+k_{2}^{2})e^{-\tfrac{2z}{d}}+(k_{3}^{2}+k_{4}^{2})e^{\tfrac{2z}{d}}+2\cos(\tfrac{2z}{d})(k_{1}k_{3}-k_{2}k_{4})-2\sin(\tfrac{2z}{d})(k_{1}k_{4}+k_{2}k_{3})\right).

Since

\|\partial_{z}v_{\mathrm{E}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(-h,0)}^{2}=\biggl(\sup_{z\in(-h,0)}|\partial_{z}v_{\mathrm{E}}(z)|\biggr)^{2}=\sup_{z\in(-h,0)}|\partial_{z}v_{\mathrm{E}}(z)|^{2}

the desired estimate follows from triangle inequality, the monotonicity of exponential function and the bounds
|\sin(\tfrac{2z}{d})|,|\cos(\tfrac{2z}{d})|\leq 1
.
∎

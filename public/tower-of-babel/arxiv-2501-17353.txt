Non-smooth regular curves via a descent approach
G. Borelli, C. D. D. Moreira, R. Salomão

Abstract
This paper aims to continue the classification of non-smooth regular curves, but over fields of characteristic three. These curves were originally introduced by Zariski as generic fibers of counterexamples to Bertini’s theorem on the variation of singular points of linear series. Such a classification has been introduced by Stöhr, taking advantage of the equivalent theory of non-conservative function fields, which in turn occurs only over non-perfect fields K K of characteristic p > 0 p>0 . We propose here a different way of approach, relying on the fact that a non-smooth regular curve in ℙ K n \mathbb{P}^{n}_{K} provides a singular curve when viewed inside ℙ K 1 / p n \mathbb{P}^{n}_{K^{1/p}} . Hence we were naturally induced to the question of characterizing singular curves in ℙ K 1 / p n \mathbb{P}^{n}_{K^{1/p}} coming from regular curves in ℙ K n \mathbb{P}^{n}_{K} . To understand this phenomenon we consider the notion of integrable connections with zero p p -curvature to extend Katz’s version of Cartier’s theorem for purely inseparable morphisms, where we solve the above characterization for the slightly general setup of coherent sheaves. Moreover, we also had to introduce some new local invariants attached to non-smooth points, as the differential degree. As an application of the theory developed here, we classify complete, geometrically integral, non-smooth regular curves C C of genus 3 3 , over a separably closed field K K of characteristic 3 3 , whose base extension C × Spec ⁡ K Spec ⁡ K ¯ C\times_{\operatorname{Spec}K}{\operatorname{Spec}\overline{K}} is non-hyperelliptic with normalization having geometric genus 1 1 .

Non-smooth regular curves, Bertini’s theorem, non-conservative function fields, integrable connections,
p
-curvature zero connections.

Introduction

Bertini’s theorem (or Bertini-Sard theorem) on variable of singular points in linear systems is no longer true when we pass from a field of characteristic zero to a field of positive characteristic, as announced by Zariski in
[
23
]
. More precisely, he found fibrations by singular curves between smooth varieties, or equivalently, regular (in the sense of points with regular local rings) and non-smooth (meaning that the usual Jacobian criterion is not satisfied after extending the base field to its algebraic closure) curves over non-perfect fields, playing as their generic fibers. A basic example, working in every positive characteristic, is the fibration from
\mathbb{A}^{2}=\operatorname{Spec}k[x,y]
to
\mathbb{A}^{1}=\operatorname{Spec}k[t]
, given by the assignment
f(x,y)=y^{2}+x^{p}
, where
k
is an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p>0
. Moreover, its generic fiber is the regular and non-smooth curve
y^{2}+x^{p}-t
over
K=k(t)
.

Such fibrations are often associated with interesting positive characteristic phenomena, suggesting that their classification is an interesting problem to tackle. For instance, the extension of Enriques’ classification of surfaces to positive characteristic (see
[
2
]
), counterexamples to Kodaira’s vanishing theorem (see
[
24
]
and
[
10
]
), a class of isolated hypersurface singularities having infinite Milnor number (see
[
4
]
) and coverings of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
by families of singular, strange and non-classical curves (see
[
13
]
and
[
6
]
).

Taking advantage of that “non-smooth regular curves” and “non-conservative function fields” reflect the phenomenon of genus droop and are equivalent objects, many authors started to use the literature about the second one (
[
1
]
and
[
17
]
) to classify the first one (cf.
[
19
]
and many subsequent papers of Sthör’s students).
Roughly speaking the main idea for the classification that appears in the literature so far can be divided into two steps. The first one is similar to the classification of elliptic curves and consists of finding a basis of certain vector spaces of global sections associated with some divisors, like obtaining a Weierstrass form. The second one consists in finding a basis of the space of regular/holomorphic differential forms to construct an embedding from such curves in a projective space, when they are non-hyperelliptic. Actually, such an embedding can be obtained after a base extension of the field of constants to its algebraic closure.

In this work we propose a different way to approach this phenomenon by using techniques similar to Galois descent theory, that is, we use the category of schemes over algebraic extensions of a field
K
to deduce properties of non-smooth regular curves defined over
K
. To do this, we will restrict ourselves to the classification of geometrically integral, non-smooth regular curves
C
over a separably closed field
K
of positive characteristic
p
. The starting point for understanding the beginning of our approach is the “local version of Kimura’s theorem” (see Corollary 3.2 in
[
17
]
), which says that
C
is a non-smooth regular curve if and only if the extended curve
C^{K^{1/p}}:=C\times_{\operatorname{Spec}K}\operatorname{Spec}K^{1/p}
is non-regular. In this way, our strategy is to classify geometrically integral, non-smooth regular curves
C
embedded in the projective space
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{K}
, by classifying geometrically integral and non-regular (or singular) curves in
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{K^{1/p}}
which are obtained from base extensions of regular curves in
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{K}
.

To do this we have an important issue to overcome, because an embedding on a subvariety of

may not be a base extension of a subvariety of
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{K}
. For instance,
K^{1/p}
-rational points that are not

-invariant sheaves over a geometrically integral

-subschemes of
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{K^{1/p}}

.
Moreover, if

\mathcal{O}_{X}
-morphism and

\nabla_{D}\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{S}(V),\mathcal{S}(V))

, where

is the identity of
\mathcal{S}
,
U
is any open subset of
X

.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let us consider
\mathcal{S}
be a
\mathcal{O}_{Y}
-coherent sheaf,
\nabla:\mathcal{S}\to\Omega_{Y/X}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{Y}}\mathcal{S}
be a
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-morphism and
y\in\mathcal{S}(V)
, where
V
is an open subset of
Y
such that
\Omega_{Y/X}(V)
is free. If
\nabla_{D}(y)=0
for every derivation

\nabla(y)=0
, where

.

Proof.

Suppose that
\Omega_{Y/X}(V)

-basis

of
\Omega_{Y/X}(V)

of
\operatorname{Der}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)}(\mathcal{O}_{Y}(V))

Definition 2.4
.

If
\mathcal{S}
is a
\mathcal{O}_{Y}
-coherent sheaf and

is a

-connection, then we define the

-coherent sheaf

by

\mathcal{S}^{\nabla}(U)=\{x\in\mathcal{S}(\pi^{-1}(U))\,|\,\nabla(x)=0\}

for every open subset

of
X
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a
\mathcal{O}_{Y}
-coherent sheaf,
\nabla
be a
X
-connection on
\mathcal{S}
and

, where

is an open subset of

. If
\nabla_{D}(x)=0

\nabla_{D}(ax)=D(a)x
.

We have that
\nabla_{D}(ax)=(D_{d}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{Y}}1_{\mathcal{S}})\circ\nabla(ax)=(D_{d}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{Y}}1_{\mathcal{S}})(da\otimes x)=D(a)x
.
∎

Definition 2.6
.

We say that a
X
-connection
\nabla:\mathcal{S}\to\Omega_{Y/X}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{Y}}\mathcal{S}
on a
\mathcal{O}_{Y}
-coherent sheaf
\mathcal{S}
is integrable if for every
D,D^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Der}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)}(\mathcal{O}_{Y}(V))
(where
U
is an open subset of

and

) we have that

\nabla_{[D,D^{\prime}]}-[\nabla_{D},\nabla_{D^{\prime}}]=0.

Moreover, we say that
\nabla
has
p
-curvature zero when

\nabla_{D^{p}}-(\nabla_{D})^{p}=0.

When
\nabla
is integrable with

-curvature zero, we will say that
\nabla
is
X
-invariant.

Definition 2.7
.

We denote by
\operatorname{Inv}(Y/X)
the category whose objects are pairs
(\mathcal{S},\nabla)
where
\mathcal{S}
is a
\mathcal{O}_{Y}
-coherent sheaf and
\nabla
is an
X
-invariant connection on
\mathcal{S}
.

Let
(\mathcal{S}_{1},\nabla_{1})
and

. We say that a morphism

f\circ(\nabla_{1})_{D}=(\nabla_{2})_{D}\circ f

and any open subset

of

, where
V=\pi^{-1}(U)
.

Remark 2.8
.

Notice that any

in
\operatorname{Inv}(Y/X)
restricts itself to a
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-morphism from
(\mathcal{S}_{1})^{\nabla_{1}}
to
(\mathcal{S}_{2})^{\nabla_{2}}
.

The next two propositions provide useful examples of elements in
\operatorname{Inv}(Y/X)
.

Proposition 2.9
.

The pair
(\mathcal{O}_{Y},d)
belongs to
\operatorname{Inv}(Y/X)
where
d\colon\mathcal{O}_{Y}\to\Omega_{Y/X}
is the natural
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-derivation.

Proof.

Clearly
d:\mathcal{O}_{Y}\to\Omega_{Y/X}
is a
X
-connection satisfying
\mathcal{O}_{Y}^{d}=\mathcal{O}_{X}
.
Let
D,D^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Der}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)}(\mathcal{O}_{Y}(V))
, where
U
is an open subset of
X
and
V=\pi^{-1}(U)
. Lemma
2.5
says that
d_{D}=D
and
d_{D^{\prime}}=D^{\prime}
. Thus
(d_{D})^{p}=D^{p}=(d_{D^{p}})
and
(d_{[D,D^{\prime}]})=[D,D^{\prime}]=[d_{D},d_{D^{\prime}}]
, concluding the

-invariance of

.
∎

Remark 2.10
.

If
\mathcal{S}_{1}
is a
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-coherent sheaf and
\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{O}_{Y}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{S}_{1}
, then we can identify
\Omega_{Y/X}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{Y}}\mathcal{S}\simeq\Omega_{Y/X}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{S}_{1}
. Therefore the
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-morphism
\nabla=d\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}1_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}
can be seen as a
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-morphism from
\mathcal{S}
to
\Omega_{Y/X}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{Y}}\mathcal{S}
.

Proposition 2.11
.

If
\mathcal{S}_{1}
is a
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-coherent sheaf,
\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{O}_{Y}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{S}_{1}
and
\nabla=d\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}1_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}
, then
(\mathcal{S},\nabla)
belongs to the category
\operatorname{Inv}(Y/X)
.

Proof.

Let
U
be an open subset of
X
and
V=\pi_{1}^{-1}(U)
. From hypothesis, any
x\in\mathcal{S}(V)
can be written by
x=\sum b_{i}\otimes y_{i}
with
b_{i}\in\mathcal{O}_{Y}(V)
and

. Thus for

we obtain
\nabla(ax)=\nabla(\sum ab_{i}\otimes y_{i})=\sum d(ab_{i})\otimes y_{i}=da\sum b_{i}\otimes y_{i}+a\sum d(b_{i})\otimes y_{i}=(da)x+a\nabla(x)
, which says that
\nabla
is a
X
-connection. Since

is
X
-invariant then the same happens for
\nabla
and therefore
(\mathcal{S},\nabla)
belongs to the category
\operatorname{Inv}(Y/X)
.
∎

Proposition 2.12
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{S}_{1}
and
\mathcal{T}_{1}
are
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-coherent sheaves and
f_{1}:\mathcal{S}_{1}\to\mathcal{T}_{1}
is a
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-morphism. If
f=1_{\mathcal{O}_{Y}}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}f_{1}
is the
\mathcal{O}_{Y}
-morphism from
\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{O}_{Y}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{S}_{1}
to
\mathcal{T}=\mathcal{O}_{Y}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{T}_{1}
, then it belongs to the category
\operatorname{Inv}(Y/X)
.

Proof.

By definition we need to prove that
f\circ(\nabla_{\mathcal{S}})_{D}=(\nabla_{\mathcal{T}})_{D}\circ f
for every

D\in\operatorname{Der}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)}(\mathcal{O}_{Y}(V))
and every open subset
U
of
X
, where
V=\pi^{-1}(U)
,
\nabla_{\mathcal{S}}=d\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}1_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}
and
\nabla_{\mathcal{T}}=d\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}1_{\mathcal{T}_{1}}
. Since each
x\in\mathcal{S}(V)
can be written by
x=\sum a_{i}\otimes x_{i}
where
x_{i}\in\mathcal{S}_{1}(U)
and

, it follows that
f((\nabla_{\mathcal{S}})_{D}(x))=f\left(\sum D(a_{i})\otimes x_{i}\right)=\sum D(a_{i})\otimes f_{1}(x_{i})=(\nabla_{\mathcal{T}})_{D}(f(x))

for every

∎

Now we define the functor
\mathfrak{F}
, from the category
\operatorname{Inv}(Y/X)
to the category of coherent
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-coherent sheaves, and the functor
\mathfrak{G}
, going in the opposite direction.

Definition 2.13
.

Given a pair
(\mathcal{S},\nabla)
in
\operatorname{Inv}(Y/X)
we define
\mathfrak{F}(\mathcal{S},\nabla)=\mathcal{S}^{\nabla}
. Moreover if we have two pairs
(\mathcal{S}_{1},\nabla_{1})
and
(\mathcal{S}_{2},\nabla_{2})
in
\operatorname{Inv}(Y/X)
and if
f\colon\mathcal{S}_{1}\to\mathcal{S}_{2}
is a morphism in
\operatorname{Inv}(Y/X)
, we define
\mathfrak{F}(f)=f|_{(\mathcal{S}_{1})^{\nabla_{1}}}\colon(\mathcal{S}_{1})^{\nabla_{1}}\to(\mathcal{S}_{2})^{\nabla_{2}}
.

Definition 2.14
.

Given a
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-coherent sheaf
\mathcal{S}_{1}
we define
\mathfrak{G}(\mathcal{S}_{1})=(\mathcal{O}_{Y}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{S}_{1},d\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}1_{\mathcal{S}_{1}})
. Moreover if
f_{1}:\mathcal{S}_{1}\to\mathcal{T}_{1}
is a
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-morphism we define
\mathfrak{G}(f_{1})=1_{\mathcal{O}_{Y}}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}f_{1}
.

Lemma 2.15
.

With the notations of the previous definition, we have that
\mathfrak{G}(f_{1})
belongs to the category
\operatorname{Inv}(Y/X)

Proof.

If
U
is an affine open subset of
X
,
V=\pi^{-1}(U)
and
D\in\operatorname{Der}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)}\mathcal{O}_{Y}(V)
, then, over these open subsets, we have that

\mathfrak{G}(f_{1})\circ(d\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}1_{\mathcal{S}_{1}})_{D}=D\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}f_{1}=(d\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}1_{\mathcal{T}_{1}})_{D}\circ\mathfrak{G}(f_{1})
.
∎

From now on we will proceed to prove that these two functors establish an isomorphism between the above two categories, which gives the announced characterization in the beginning of the section. To this end, we will need the following important tool.

Definition 2.16
.

Suppose that
V
is an open subset of
Y
such that
\Omega_{Y/X}(V)
is free and take
U=\pi(V)
. We say that
s_{1},\dots,s_{r}\in\mathcal{O}_{Y}(V)
is a differential basis of
\mathcal{O}_{Y}(V)
over
\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)
when
ds_{1},\ldots,ds_{r}
is a
\mathcal{O}_{Y}(V)
-basis of
\Omega_{Y/X}(V)
.

In this case, let
D_{1},\ldots,D_{r}\in\operatorname{Der}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)}\mathcal{O}_{Y}(V)
be a dual basis of
ds_{1},\ldots,ds_{r}
(i.e.
D_{i}(s_{j})=\delta_{ij}
). If
(\mathcal{S},\nabla)
is a pair in
\operatorname{Inv}(Y/X)
,
then we define the
trace
\tau_{s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}}
with respect to
s_{1}\ldots,s_{r}
and
\nabla

by

\tau_{s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}}=\nabla_{D_{1}}^{p-1}\circ\dots\circ\nabla_{D_{r}}^{p-1}\colon\mathcal{S}(V)\to\mathcal{S}(V).

Remark 2.17
.

We claim that if
\Omega_{Y/X}
is locally free
\mathcal{O}_{Y}
-coherent sheaf, then there is a cover
\{V_{P}\}_{P\in Y}
of
Y
by open subsets such that
\mathcal{O}_{Y}(V_{P})
has a differential basis over
\mathcal{O}_{X}(U_{P})
, where
U_{P}=\pi(V_{P})
.

Indeed, for each
P\in Y
, if
\mathfrak{m}_{Y,P}
is the the maximal ideal of the local ring
\mathcal{O}_{Y,P}
, then
(\Omega_{Y/X})_{P}/\mathfrak{m}_{Y,P}(\Omega_{Y/X})_{P}
is a
\textbf{k}(P)
-vector space of finite dimension, namely
r
, where
\textbf{k}(P)
is the residue field at
P
. It is easy to see that, if
\sum_{i=1}^{m}y_{i,j}dx_{i}
(
j=1,\ldots,r
) is basis of the above vector space, then we also may collect, from the generators
dx_{1},\ldots,dx_{m}
, another basis of such vector space of the form
ds_{1},\ldots,ds_{r}
. Now from Nakayama’s Lemma we obtain that
(\Omega_{Y/X})_{P}=\mathcal{O}_{Y,P}ds_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathcal{O}_{Y,P}ds_{r}
and we only need to consider the open neighborhood
V_{P}
at
P
such that
\Omega_{Y/X}(V_{P})
is free of rank
r
and
s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}\in\mathcal{O}_{Y}(V_{P})
.

Remark 2.18
.

Notice that, with the notation introduced in the above definition, the pushforward under
\pi
of the image of
\tau_{s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}}
is a sub-sheaf of
\mathcal{S}^{\nabla}
. To see this, from Proposition
2.3
we only need to prove that
\nabla_{D_{i}}\circ\tau_{s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}}=0
, which in turn can be obtained if we prove that
\nabla_{D_{i}}\circ\nabla_{D_{j}}-\nabla_{D_{j}}\circ\nabla_{D_{i}}=\nabla_{[D_{i},D_{j}]}=0
and
\nabla_{D_{i}}^{p}=\nabla_{D_{i}^{p}}=0
. To show these two identities, it is enough to prove that
[D_{i},D_{j}]=0=D_{i}^{p}
for every
i
and
j
such that
i\neq j
. Indeed, they follow from the fact that for every derivation
D
, the identity
D=D_{d}\circ d
implies that
D=0
if and only if
D(s_{i})=0
for every
i
.

Lemma 2.19
.

Let
V
be an open subset of
Y
,
U=\pi(V)
,
s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}
be a differential basis of
\mathcal{O}_{Y}(V)
over
\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)
,
(\mathcal{S},\nabla)
be a pair in
\operatorname{Inv}(Y/X)
and
\tau_{s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}}
be the trace with respect to
s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}
.
If we denote by
s^{I}=s_{1}^{i_{1}}\ldots s_{r}^{i_{r}}
, for each
I=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{r})\in\{0,\ldots,p-1\}^{r}
, then

\tau_{s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}}(s^{I}x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&\mbox{ for }&I\neq(p-1,\ldots,p-1)\\
(-1)^{r}x&\mbox{ for }&I=(p-1,\ldots,p-1)\end{array}\right.

for all
x\in\mathcal{S}^{\nabla}(U)
. In particular, the image of
\mathcal{S}(V)
by
\tau_{s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}}
is
\mathcal{S}^{\nabla}(U)
.

Proof.

To prove the first claim. To do this, we observe that if
D_{1},\ldots,D_{r}\in\operatorname{Der}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)}\mathcal{O}_{Y}(V)
is the dual basis of
ds_{1},\ldots,ds_{r}
, then the identities

(D_{i})^{p-1}(s_{i}^{k})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&\mbox{ for }&k<p-1\\
(p-1)!&\mbox{ for }&k=p-1\end{array}\right.

(1)

provide that

D_{1}^{p-1}(\ldots(D_{r}^{p-1}(s^{I})\ldots)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&\mbox{ for }&I\neq(p-1,\ldots,p-1)\\
(-1)^{r}&\mbox{ for }&I=(p-1,\ldots,p-1)\end{array}\right.

(2)

which in turn, implies the stated identity.

From Remark
2.18
, we only need to show that
\mathcal{S}^{\nabla}(U)\subset\tau_{s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}}(\mathcal{S}(V))
, in order to obtain the second claim. Indeed, if
x\in\mathcal{S}^{\nabla}(U)
then it follows from (
2
) that
x=\tau_{s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}}((-1)^{r}s^{(p-1,\ldots,p-1)}x)
.
∎

Proposition 2.20
.

If we consider a pair
(\mathcal{S},\nabla)
in
\operatorname{Inv}(Y/X)
and a differential basis
s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}
of
\mathcal{O}_{Y}(V)
over
\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)
, where
V
is an open subset of
Y
and
U=\pi(V)
, then

\mathcal{S}(V)=\bigoplus_{I\in\{0,\ldots,p-1\}^{r}}s^{I}(\mathcal{S}^{\nabla}(U)).

Moreover, every
x\in\mathcal{S}(V)
can be written uniquely by

x=(-1)^{r}\sum_{I\in\{0,\ldots,p-1\}^{r}}s^{I}\tau_{s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}}(s^{(p-1,\ldots,p-1)-I}x).

Proof.

For each
i=1,\ldots,r
let us fix
\mathcal{S}_{i}:=\{x\in\mathcal{S}(V)\;;\;\nabla_{D_{j}}(x)=0\mbox{ for }j\geq i\}
and
\mathcal{T}_{i}:=\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}s_{i}^{k}\mathcal{S}_{i}\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{i+1}
, where
D_{1},\ldots,D_{r}\in\operatorname{Der}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)}\mathcal{O}_{Y}(V)
is the dual basis of
ds_{1},\ldots,ds_{r}
, and we define
S_{r+1}:=S(V)
. By using Lemma
2.3
, we have that
\mathcal{S}_{1}=\mathcal{S}^{\nabla}(U)
and, therefore, we only need to prove that
\mathcal{T}_{i}=\mathcal{S}_{i+1}
for
i=1,\ldots,r
. If we take
x\in\mathcal{S}_{i+1}
, then (
1
) says that
D_{i}^{p}=0
and, hence,
\nabla_{D_{i}}^{p}(x)=\nabla_{D_{i}^{p}}(x)=0\in\mathcal{T}_{i}
. Let us suppose that the minimum
j
such that
(\nabla_{D_{i}})^{j}(x)=y\in\mathcal{T}_{i}
is bigger than zero. From the definition of
\mathcal{T}_{i}
we may write

y=\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}s_{i}^{k}y_{k}
with
y_{k}\in\mathcal{S}_{i}
. By applying Lemma
2.5
recursively we have

\nabla^{p-1}_{D_{i}}(s_{i}^{k}y_{k})=\nabla^{p-2}_{D_{i}}(D_{i}(s_{i}^{k})y_{k})=\ldots=D_{i}^{p-1}(s_{i}^{k})y_{k}
,
which implies that
\nabla^{p-1}_{D_{i}}(s_{i}^{k}y_{k})=0
for
k\neq p-1
,
\nabla^{p-1}_{D_{i}}(s_{i}^{p-1}y_{p-1})=(p-1)!y_{p-1}
and so

\frac{\nabla_{D_{i}}^{p-1}(y)}{(p-1)!}=y_{p-1}.

Using the assumption
j>0
we have
\nabla_{D_{i}}^{p-1}(y)=\nabla_{D_{i}}^{p-1+j}(x)=0
, that is,
y_{p-1}=0
and
y=\sum_{k=0}^{p-2}s_{i}^{k}y_{k}
. If we consider

y^{\prime}=-\sum_{k=0}^{p-2}\frac{s_{i}^{k+1}}{k+1}y_{k}

then
\nabla_{D_{i}}(y^{\prime})=-y
and
\nabla_{D_{i}}(\nabla_{D_{i}}^{j-1}(x)+y^{\prime})=\nabla_{D_{i}}^{j}(x)-y=0
.
On the other hand
\nabla_{D_{l}}(\nabla_{D_{i}}^{j-1}(x)+y^{\prime})=0
for all
l\geq i
implies that
\nabla_{D_{i}}^{j-1}(x)+y^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{i}\subset\mathcal{T}_{i}
. In this way, we conclude that
\nabla_{D_{i}}^{j-1}(x)\in\mathcal{T}_{i}
, contradicting the assumption on
j
. So
j=0
and
x\in\mathcal{T}_{i}
. Since
\mathcal{S}_{i}\subset\mathcal{S}_{i+1}
we can use Lemma
2.5
to conclude that
\mathcal{T}_{i}=\mathcal{S}_{i+1}
.

Now, from the identity that we have obtained above, any
x\in S(V)
can be written by
x=\sum_{I\in\{0,\ldots,p-1\}^{r}}s^{I}x_{I}
where
x_{I}\in\mathcal{S}^{\nabla}(U)
. Therefore, to finish the proof, we only need to observe that Lemma
2.19
implies:
(-1)^{r}\tau_{s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}}(s^{(p-1,\ldots,p-1)-I}x)=x_{I}
.
∎

Proposition 2.21
.

If
(\mathcal{S},\nabla)
is a pair in the category
\operatorname{Inv}(Y/X)
, then the natural
\mathcal{O}_{Y}
-morphism
\phi:\mathcal{O}_{Y}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{S}^{\nabla}\to\mathcal{S}
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

Suppose that
s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}
is a differential basis of
\mathcal{O}_{Y}(V)
over
\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)
, where
V
is an open subset of
Y
and
U=\pi(V)
. Then we can define
\psi:\mathcal{S}(V)\to\mathcal{O}_{Y}(V)\otimes\mathcal{S}^{\nabla}(U)
by

\psi(x)=(-1)^{r}\sum_{I\in\{0,\ldots,p-1\}^{r}}s^{I}\otimes\tau_{s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}}(s^{(p-1,\ldots,p-1)-I}x).

Clearly Proposition
2.20
says that
\phi\circ\psi=1_{\mathcal{S}}
. On the other hand, by using Lemma
2.19
we conclude that
\psi\circ\phi(s^{J}\otimes x)=s^{J}\otimes x
for every
x\in\mathcal{S}^{\nabla}(U)
and every
J\in\{0,\ldots,p-1\}^{r}
. Therefore,
\psi\circ\phi=1_{\mathcal{O}_{Y}\otimes\mathcal{S}^{\nabla}}(V)
.
∎

The next two theorems will obtain the claimed isomorphism between objects from the categories
\operatorname{Inv}(Y/X)
and “
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-coherent sheaves”, which is an alternative to Cartier’s theorem (cf. Theorem 5.1,
[
7
]
) for purely inseparable morphisms.

Theorem 2.22
.

If
(\mathcal{S},\nabla)
is a pair in the category
\operatorname{Inv}(Y/X)
, then
\mathfrak{G}(\mathfrak{F}(\mathcal{S},\nabla))\simeq(\mathcal{S},\nabla)
.

Proof.

From the definition of the functor
\mathfrak{G}
and from Proposition
2.21
we only need to prove that
\nabla_{2}:=d\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}1_{\mathcal{S}^{\nabla}}
is equal to
\nabla
, where such identity must take into account the isomorphism from the above proposition. As in the previous proofs, our assumption is that
\mathcal{O}_{Y}(V)
admits a differential basis over
\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)
. Notice that, given
x\in S(V)
, Proposition
2.20
provides the written
x=\sum a_{i}x_{i}
, with
x_{i}\in\mathcal{S}^{\nabla}(U)
and
a_{i}\in\mathcal{O}_{Y}(V)
. For each
D\in\operatorname{Der}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)}(\mathcal{O}_{Y}(V))
, we have
\nabla_{D}(x)=\nabla_{D}\left(\sum a_{i}x_{i}\right)=\sum D(a_{i})x_{i}=(\nabla_{2})_{D}\left(\sum a_{i}x_{i}\right)=(\nabla_{2})_{D}(x)
, from Lemma
2.5
, because
\nabla_{2}(x_{i})=d(1)\otimes x_{i}=0=\nabla(x_{i})
. Therefore using Lemma
2.3
for the
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-morphism
\nabla-\nabla_{2}
we conclude that
\nabla=\nabla_{2}
.
∎

Theorem 2.23
.

If
\mathcal{S}_{1}
is a
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-coherent sheaf, then
\mathfrak{F}(\mathfrak{G}(\mathcal{S}_{1}))\simeq\mathcal{S}_{1}
.

Proof.

From definition,
\mathfrak{G}(\mathcal{S}_{1})=(\mathcal{O}_{Y}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{S}_{1},d\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}1_{\mathcal{S}_{1}})
and
\nabla=d\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}1_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}
. Let
\gamma:\mathcal{S}_{1}\to\mathcal{O}_{Y}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{S}_{1}
be the natural morphism given by
\gamma(x)=1\otimes x
. Since
\nabla\circ\gamma=0
we can induce a
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-morphism
\gamma:\mathcal{S}_{1}\to(\mathcal{O}_{Y}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{S}_{1})^{\nabla}
.
As we can find a cover
\{V_{i}\}
of
Y
by affine open subsets such that every
\mathcal{O}_{Y}(V_{i})
admit a differential basis over
\mathcal{O}_{X}(\pi(V_{i}))
, then it is enough to show that
\gamma\mid_{\pi(V)}
is an isomorphism whenever
\mathcal{O}_{Y}(V)
admits a differential basis over
\mathcal{O}_{X}(\pi(V))
. Such claim follows from the three properties/facts: firstly we will show that the inverse of
\gamma\mid_{\pi(V)}
will be defined by using a differential basis of
\mathcal{O}_{Y}(V)
; secondly we notice that every differential basis of
\mathcal{O}_{Y}(V)
over
\mathcal{O}_{X}(\pi(V))
can be restricted to a differential basis for each open subset of
V
; the inverse of
\gamma\mid_{\pi(V)}
is uniquely determined. Indeed, with these three properties we obtain that the local inverses can be glued to produce a global inverse for
\gamma
.

To show the first property observed above, we take an element
y\in(\mathcal{O}_{Y}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{S}_{1})^{\nabla}(\pi(V))
and we define
\beta(y):=(-1)^{r}\tau_{s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}}(s^{(p-1,\ldots,p-1)}y)\in(\mathcal{O}_{Y}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{S}_{1})(V)
, where
s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}
is a differential basis of
\mathcal{O}_{Y}(V)
over
\mathcal{O}_{X}(\pi(V))
. From Lemma
2.19
, for each
x\in\mathcal{S}_{1}(\pi(V))
, we have
\beta\circ\gamma(x)=\beta(1\otimes x)=(-1)^{r}\tau_{s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}}(s^{(p-1,\ldots,p-1)}(1\otimes x)))=1\otimes x\in(\mathcal{O}_{X}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{S}_{1})(\pi(V))
. Therefore
(\eta\circ\beta)\circ\gamma\mid_{\pi(V)}=1_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}
, where
\eta\colon\mathcal{O}_{X}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{S}_{1}\to\mathcal{S}_{1}
is defined by
\eta(a\otimes x)=ax
.

If
x\in(\mathcal{O}_{Y}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{S}_{1})^{\nabla}(\pi(V))
we may write
x=\sum s^{I}\otimes x_{I}
where
x_{I}\in\mathcal{S}_{1}(\pi(V))
and, from
Lemma
2.19
, we obtain that
\tau_{s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}}(s^{(p-1,\ldots,p-1)-J}x)=\tau_{s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}}(s^{(p-1,\ldots,p-1)}(1\otimes x_{J}))=(-1)^{r}(1\otimes x_{J}).

Moreover, the same lemma says that
\tau_{s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}}(s^{(p-1,\ldots,p-1)-J}x)=0
for
J\neq(0,\ldots,0)
. Then
x=1\otimes x_{(0,\ldots,0)}
and
\eta\circ\beta(x)=x_{(0,\ldots,0)}
and, therefore,
\eta\circ\beta
is the inverse of
\gamma\mid_{\pi(V)}
.
∎

3
Local properties at non-smooth regular points of geometrically integral curves

Let
Z
be a geometrically integral scheme over
K
and
P
be a point of
Z
, where
K
is a non-perfect and separably closed field of characteristic
p>0
. We denote the local ring of
Z
at
P
by
\mathcal{O}_{Z,P}
, its maximal ideal by
\mathfrak{m}_{Z,P}
and its residue field by
\textbf{k}(Z,P)
.

Suppose that
L
is an algebraic extension of
K
. Since
Z
is geometrically integral we notice that
\mathcal{O}_{Z,P}
is a subring of the domain
\mathcal{O}_{Z^{L},P^{L}}
, where
Z^{L}:=Z\times_{\operatorname{Spec}K}\operatorname{Spec}L
and
P^{L}
is the unique point of
Z^{L}
lying over
P
. If
x\in\mathcal{O}_{Z,P}
and
\alpha\in L
we can identify
\alpha x
with
\alpha\otimes x
. With such identification,
\mathcal{O}_{Z^{L},P^{L}}
is given by
\mathcal{O}_{Z,P}L
. It is natural to expect non-regularity at
P^{L}
and, in this case, we will denote the point of the normalization
\widetilde{Z^{L}}
of
Z^{L}
, lying over it, by
\widetilde{P^{L}}
.

In this section we will study numerical properties at non-smooth points of a curve
C
, over
K
, and at the singularities of
C^{\overline{K}}
, lying over them.

3.1
A criteria for regularity of geometrically integral curves

Let
C
be a geometrically integral curve over
K
and
P
be a non-smooth point of
C
. In this subsection we will determine conditions to guarantee the regularity at
P
.

Lemma 3.1
.

The maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}_{{Z^{\overline{K}},P^{\overline{K}}}}
is generated by elements of the form
x-a_{x}
where
x\in\mathcal{O}_{Z,P}
and
a_{x}\in\textbf{k}(Z,P)
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{O}_{Z,P}/\mathfrak{m}_{Z,P}\subset\overline{K}
then for every
x\in\mathcal{O}_{Z,P}
there exists
a_{x}\in\textbf{k}(Z,P)
such that
x-a_{x}\in\mathfrak{m}_{Z^{\overline{K}},P^{\overline{K}}}
. On the other hand, if
y\in\mathfrak{m}_{{Z^{\overline{K}},P^{\overline{K}}}}
then
y=\sum b_{j}x_{j}
where
b_{j}\in\overline{K}
and
x_{j}\in\mathcal{O}_{Z,P}
. If we write
y=\sum b_{j}(x_{j}-a_{x_{j}})+\sum b_{j}a_{x_{j}}
where
x_{j}-a_{j}\in\mathfrak{m}_{{Z^{\overline{K}},P^{\overline{K}}}}
we obtain
\sum b_{j}a_{x_{j}}\in\overline{K}\cap\mathfrak{m}_{{Z^{\overline{K}},P^{\overline{K}}}}
, that is,
\sum b_{j}a_{x_{j}}=0
and
y=\sum b_{j}(x_{j}-a_{x_{j}}).

∎

Lemma 3.2
.

If
L^{\prime}|L
is an algebraic extension such that
L^{\prime}\otimes_{L}\overline{K}
is a field then
L^{\prime}=L
.

Proof.

Suppose that
L\neq L^{\prime}
. Hence there exists
x\in L^{\prime}
such that
x\not\in L
. Since
K
is separably closed then
L^{\prime}|L
is purely inseparable and we can take the smallest positive integer
n
such that
x^{p^{n}}=y\in L
, that is,
L[x]=L[T]/\langle T^{p^{n}}-y\rangle
is a field between
L
and
L^{\prime}
. Since
L[x]\otimes_{L}\overline{K}=\overline{K}[T]/\langle T^{p^{n}}-y\rangle
is a subring of the field
L^{\prime}\otimes_{L}\overline{K}
, then it is reduced, contradicting the nilpotence of the element
\overline{T-x}
. ∎

Proposition 3.3
.

If the classes of
x_{1}-a_{1},\ldots,x_{l}-a_{l}
form a basis for the
\overline{K}
-vector space

\frac{\mathfrak{m}_{Z^{\overline{K}},P^{\overline{K}}}}{\mathfrak{m}_{Z^{\overline{K}},P^{\overline{K}}}^{2}+\overline{K}\mathfrak{m}_{{Z,P}}}

with
x_{i}\in\mathcal{O}_{Z,P}
and
a_{i}\in\textbf{k}(Z,P)
, then
\textbf{k}(Z,P)=K(a_{1},\ldots,a_{l})
. Moreover
da_{1},\ldots,da_{l}
is a basis of
\Omega_{\textbf{k}(Z,P)/K}
.

Proof.

Firstly we prove that if
L=K(a_{1},\ldots,a_{l})
, then the inclusion of fields
L\subseteq\textbf{k}(Z,P)\subseteq\textbf{k}(Z^{L},P^{L})
become an identity. Indeed, from hypothesis, the local ring
\mathcal{O}_{Z^{\overline{K}},P^{\overline{K}}}=\mathcal{O}_{Z^{L},P^{L}}\otimes_{L}\overline{K}
has maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}_{Z^{\overline{K}},P^{\overline{K}}}=\mathfrak{m}_{Z^{L},P^{L}}\otimes_{L}\overline{K}
and residue field
\textbf{k}(Z^{\overline{K}},P^{\overline{K}})=\textbf{k}(Z^{L},P^{L})\otimes_{L}\overline{K}
. By applying Lemma
3.2
we obtain
\textbf{k}(Z^{L},P^{L})=L
.

Before proving the second claim, we notice that
\textbf{k}(Z,P)=K(a_{1},\ldots,a_{l})
implies that
the
K
-vector space
\Omega_{\textbf{k}(Z,P)/K}
is spanned by
da_{1},\ldots,da_{l}
and thus, after reordering
a_{1},\ldots,a_{l}
, we can assume that

da_{1},\ldots,da_{l^{\prime}}
is a basis of
\Omega_{\textbf{k}(Z,P)/K}
with
l^{\prime}\leq l
. Now we will prove that
l=l^{\prime}
. From Proposition 26.5 in
[
9
]
,
a_{1},\ldots,a_{l^{\prime}}
is a
p
-basis for
\textbf{k}(Z,P)
. Hence
\textbf{k}(Z,P)=K(a_{1},\ldots,a_{l^{\prime}})
provides that for any element in
y\in\mathcal{O}_{Z,P}
, its residue class
a_{y}
in
\textbf{k}(Z,P)
is given by
a_{y}=F(a_{1},\ldots,a_{l^{\prime}})
where
F\in K[X_{1},\ldots,X_{l^{\prime}}]
. In particular
z:=y-F(x_{1},\ldots,x_{l^{\prime}})\in\mathfrak{m}_{Z,P}
. From the identity in
\mathcal{O}_{Z^{\overline{K}},P^{\overline{K}}}
,
F(x_{1},\ldots,x_{l^{\prime}})=F(a_{1},\ldots,a_{l^{\prime}})-\sum_{i=1}^{l^{\prime}}\frac{\partial F}{\partial X_{i}}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{l^{\prime}})(x_{i}-a_{i})+r
, with
r\in\mathfrak{m}_{Z^{\overline{K}},P^{\overline{K}}}^{2}
, we conclude that

y-a_{y}=-\sum_{i=1}^{l^{\prime}}\frac{\partial F}{\partial X_{i}}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{l^{\prime}})(x_{i}-a_{i})+r+z.
Therefore, Lemma
3.1
says that
x_{1}-a_{1},\ldots,x_{l^{\prime}}-a_{l^{\prime}}
generate the space
\mathfrak{m}_{Z^{\overline{K}},P^{\overline{K}}}/(\mathfrak{m}_{Z^{\overline{K}},P^{\overline{K}}}^{2}+\overline{K}\mathfrak{m}_{{Z,P}})
,
implying, from hypothesis, that
l=l^{\prime}
.∎

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
C
be a geometrically integral curve over
K
and
P\in C
be a point lying under a singularity
P^{\overline{K}}
of
C^{\overline{K}}
. If

is regular at

, then

\dim_{\textbf{k}(C,P)}\Omega_{\textbf{k}(C,P)/K}\leq\dim_{\overline{K}}\frac{\mathfrak{m}_{C^{\overline{K}},P^{\overline{K}}}}{\mathfrak{m}_{C^{\overline{K}},P^{\overline{K}}}^{2}}\leq\dim_{\textbf{k}(C,P)}\Omega_{\textbf{k}(C,P)/K}+1.

Proof.

Let
x_{1}-a_{1},\ldots,x_{l}-a_{l}
be a basis of
\mathfrak{m}_{C^{\overline{K}},P^{\overline{K}}}/(\mathfrak{m}_{C^{\overline{K}},P^{\overline{K}}}^{2}+\overline{K}\mathfrak{m}_{C,P})
as in the above proposition. Therefore the exact sequence

0\to\frac{\overline{K}\mathfrak{m}_{C,P}+\mathfrak{m}_{C^{\overline{K}},P^{\overline{K}}}^{2}}{\mathfrak{m}_{C^{\overline{K}},P^{\overline{K}}}^{2}}\to\frac{\mathfrak{m}_{C^{\overline{K}},P^{\overline{K}}}}{\mathfrak{m}_{C^{\overline{K}},P^{\overline{K}}}^{2}}\to\frac{\mathfrak{m}_{C^{\overline{K}},P^{\overline{K}}}}{\mathfrak{m}_{C^{\overline{K}},P^{\overline{K}}}^{2}+\overline{K}\mathfrak{m}_{C,P}}\to 0

and the last proposition implies that
\dim_{\overline{K}}\frac{\mathfrak{m}_{C^{\overline{K}},P^{\overline{K}}}}{\mathfrak{m}_{C^{\overline{K}},P^{\overline{K}}}^{2}}=l+\dim_{\overline{K}}\frac{\overline{K}\mathfrak{m}_{C,P}+\mathfrak{m}_{C^{\overline{K}},P^{\overline{K}}}^{2}}{\mathfrak{m}_{C^{\overline{K}},P^{\overline{K}}}^{2}}.

On the other hand, from one of the isomorphism theorems

\dim_{\overline{K}}\frac{\overline{K}\mathfrak{m}_{C,P}+\mathfrak{m}_{C^{\overline{K}},P^{\overline{K}}}^{2}}{\mathfrak{m}_{C^{\overline{K}},P^{\overline{K}}}^{2}}=\dim_{\overline{K}}\frac{\overline{K}\mathfrak{m}_{C,P}}{\mathfrak{m}_{C^{\overline{K}},P^{\overline{K}}}^{2}\cap\overline{K}\mathfrak{m}_{C,P}}\leq\dim_{\overline{K}}\frac{\overline{K}\mathfrak{m}_{C,P}}{\overline{K}\mathfrak{m}_{C,P}^{2}}=1

because

is regular at
P
, which concludes the proof.
∎

Definition 3.5

Let

Proposition 3.6

Let

and

of

Let

is the normalization of

is the point of

then there exist

\mathcal{O}_{C,P}
-module
\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{C},\widetilde{P}}
(because

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
C
be a geometrically integral curve over
K
and
P\in C
be a point lying under a singularity

of

of
\mathcal{O}_{C}

\dim_{\overline{K}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{C^{\overline{K}},P^{\overline{K}}}}{\mathcal{O}_{C^{\overline{K}},P^{\overline{K}}}\mathcal{I}_{P}}=[\textbf{k}(C,P):K]

then

is a regular at

Proof.

We may assume that
\mathcal{I}_{P}
is generated by

\begin{array}[]{lll}\dim_{\overline{K}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{C^{\overline{K}}},\widetilde{P^{\overline{K}}}}}{y\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{C^{\overline{K}}},\widetilde{P^{\overline{K}}}}}&=&\dim_{\overline{K}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{C^{\overline{K}}},\widetilde{P^{\overline{K}}}}}{y\mathcal{O}_{C^{\overline{K}},P^{\overline{K}}}}-\dim_{\overline{K}}\frac{y\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{C^{\overline{K}}},\widetilde{P^{\overline{K}}}}}{y\mathcal{O}_{C^{\overline{K}},P^{\overline{K}}}}\\

Remark 3.11

It is proved in Lemma 2.1 pg. 313 of

Proposition 3.12
.

If
P_{n}
is rational, then

d(C,P)-1=\nu_{0}(dx_{0})=\nu_{n}(dx_{0}^{p^{n}})=\nu_{P_{n}}(dx_{0}^{p^{n}})
, where
x_{0}\in\mathcal{O}_{C,P}
is such that
\mathcal{O}_{C_{1},P_{1}}[x_{0}]=\mathcal{O}_{C,P}
and
\nu_{P_{n}}
is the discrete valuation of the function field
K(C_{n})|K
with valuation ring
\mathcal{O}_{C_{n},P_{n}}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
z
is an uniformizing parameter of
\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{C^{\overline{K}}},\widetilde{P^{\overline{K}}}}
. Since
\overline{K}
is algebraically closed, it follows from the fundamental identity (cf.Theorem 3.1.11 in
[
16
]
) that
\nu_{0}
is totally ramified over
\nu_{n}
and hence
z^{p^{n}}
is a uniformizing parameter of
\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{C_{n}^{\overline{K}}},\widetilde{P_{n}^{\overline{K}}}}=(\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{C^{\overline{K}}},\widetilde{P^{\overline{K}}}})^{p^{n}}

Let
D_{z}
and
D_{z^{p^{n}}}
be the derivations of

\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{C^{\overline{K}}},\widetilde{P^{\overline{K}}}}
and

\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{C_{n}^{\overline{K}}},\widetilde{P_{n}^{\overline{K}}}}
, respectively, such that
D_{z}(z)=D_{z^{p^{n}}}(z^{p^{n}})=1
. From the definition,

\nu_{0}(dx_{0})=\nu_{0}(D_{z}(x_{0}))=\dim_{\overline{K}}\operatorname{Coker}(\alpha)=d(C,P)-1
.
Since
dx_{0}^{p^{n}}=D_{z^{p^{n}}}(x_{0}^{p^{n}})dz^{p^{n}}
and
\nu_{0}
is totally ramified over
\nu_{n}
we just need to prove that
(D_{z}(x_{0}))^{p^{n}}=D_{z^{p^{n}}}(x_{0}^{p^{n}})

To do this we notice that

\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{C^{\overline{K}}},\widetilde{P^{\overline{K}}}}=\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{C_{1}^{\overline{K}}},\widetilde{P^{\overline{K}}}}[z]=\bigoplus^{p-1}_{i=0}\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{C_{1}^{\overline{K}}},\widetilde{P^{\overline{K}}}}z^{i}

\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{C_{n}^{\overline{K}}},\widetilde{P_{n}^{\overline{K}}}}=(\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{C^{\overline{K}}},\widetilde{P^{\overline{K}}}})^{p^{n}}=\bigoplus^{p-1}_{i=0}(\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{C^{\overline{K}}},\widetilde{P^{\overline{K}}}})^{p^{n+1}}(z^{p^{n}})^{i}=\bigoplus^{p-1}_{i=0}(\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{C_{n}^{\overline{K}}},\widetilde{P_{n}^{\overline{K}}}})^{p}(z^{p^{n}})^{i}
and, consequently, if

, then

D_{z^{p^{n}}}(x_{0}^{p^{n}})=D_{z^{p^{n}}}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}(a_{i}^{p^{n}})^{p}(z^{p^{n}})^{i}\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{p^{n}-1}i(a_{i}^{p})^{p^{n}}(z^{p^{n}})^{i-1}=D_{z}(x_{0})^{p^{n}}.

To finish the proof we just need to observe that
\nu_{n}(f)=\nu_{P_{n}}(f)
for every
f\in K(C_{n})
. Indeed, for such
f
, we have
\nu_{n}(f)=e(\nu_{n}|\nu_{P_{n}})\nu_{P_{n}}(f)
, where

denotes the ramification index. But, from the proof of Lemma 1.5 in
[
1
]
,

.
∎

Now we will see how Theorem 2.3 in
[
1
]
can be used to produce a recursive expression to compute the conductor in terms of differential degrees.

Theorem 3.13
.

Suppose that
n
is a positive integer such that

is rational.
Then

c(C,P)=\sum_{0\leq i<n}(p-1)(d(C_{i},P_{i})-1)p^{i}

Proof.

From Theorem 2.3 in
[
1
]
and Proposition
3.12

instead of

Notice that

is smooth since it is rational. Therefore

from Corollary 1.4 in
[
1
]
.
∎

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{L}_{1}
and
\mathcal{L}_{2}
be two invertible sheaves of
C
. If
n
is as above and
\mathfrak{b}_{n}^{\ast}\mathcal{L}_{1}\simeq\mathfrak{b}_{n}^{\ast}\mathcal{L}_{2}
, then
\mathcal{L}_{1}\simeq\mathcal{L}_{2}
.

Proof.

After tensorizing by an ample and invertible sheaf we can assume that
H^{0}(C,\mathcal{L}_{i})
has a dimension greater than
\sum_{P\in C}c(C,P)/2
. Notice we have the following exact sequence.

0\to H^{0}(C^{n},\mathfrak{e}_{n}^{\ast}(\mathcal{L}_{i}))\to H^{0}(\widetilde{C^{n}},\mathfrak{b}_{n}^{\ast}(\mathcal{L}_{i}))\to H^{0}\left(C^{n},\frac{\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{C^{n}}}}{\mathcal{O}_{C^{n}}}\right)

Therefore the codimiension of
H^{0}(C^{n},\mathfrak{e}_{n}^{\ast}(\mathcal{L}_{i}))
in
H^{0}(\widetilde{C^{n}},\mathfrak{b}_{n}^{\ast}(\mathcal{L}_{i}))
is at most
\sum_{P\in C}c(C,P)/2
and, from the assumption in the beginning, there is
s\in\bigcap_{i=1,2}H^{0}(C^{n},\mathfrak{e}_{n}^{\ast}(\mathcal{L}_{i}))
. If
R
is the Weil divisor induced by

, then
\mathfrak{e}_{n}^{\ast}(\mathcal{L}_{1})=\mathcal{O}_{C^{n}}(R)=\mathfrak{e}_{n}^{\ast}(\mathcal{L}_{1})
and, therefore,
\mathfrak{e}_{n}^{\ast}(\mathcal{L}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{2}^{\otimes(-1)})\simeq\mathcal{O}_{C^{n}}
. Proposition
2.20
applied to
\mathfrak{e}_{n}^{\ast}(\mathcal{L}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{2}^{\otimes(-1)})
says that
\mathfrak{e}^{\ast}_{n-1}(\mathcal{L}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{2}^{\otimes(-1)})
has a non-vanishing global section. After applying recursively the above argument we obtain a non-vanishing global section for
\mathcal{L}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{2}^{\otimes(-1)}
and, analogously, for
\mathcal{L}_{2}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{1}^{\otimes(-1)}
. So
\mathcal{L}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{2}^{\otimes(-1)}=\mathcal{O}_{C}(S)
and
\mathcal{L}_{2}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{1}^{\otimes(-1)}=\mathcal{O}_{C}(T)
, where

and

are positive divisors of

associated to the respective global sections. Since,
\mathcal{O}_{C}(S)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{C}(T)\simeq\mathcal{O}_{C}
, with

, we have

and
\mathcal{L}_{1}\simeq\mathcal{L}_{2}
.
∎

Proposition 4.2
.

If

From
[

Theorem 4.3
.

If
n
is as above, then the sheaf
\Omega_{C/C_{1}}
is invertible and we have the following isomorphism of sheaves.

•

\Omega_{C/C_{1}}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{C}(D)\simeq\omega_{C/K}
.

•

\mathfrak{b}_{n}^{\ast}(\Omega_{C/C_{1}})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{C^{n}}}(D^{\prime\prime})\simeq\omega_{\widetilde{C^{n}}/K^{p^{-n}}}
.

•

\omega_{\widetilde{C^{n}}/K^{p^{-n}}}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{C^{n}}}(D^{\prime})\simeq\mathfrak{b}_{n}^{\ast}(\omega_{C/K})
.

Proof.

Firstly we prove that
\Omega_{C/C_{1}}
is an invertible sheaf. Indeed we only need to prove that
\Omega_{C/C_{1},P}
is generated by only one element over each non-smooth point
P
of
C
. From Proposition
3.12
,
\mathcal{O}_{C,P}=\mathcal{O}_{C_{1},P_{1}}[x_{0}]
for some
x_{0}
such that
\nu_{0}(dx_{0})=d(C,P)-1
and then
\Omega_{C/C_{1},P}=\mathcal{O}_{C,P}dx_{0}
, which is invertible.

Let’s prove now the second isomorphism. Since
\widetilde{C^{n}}
is smooth, we have that
\Omega_{\widetilde{C^{n}}/K^{p^{-n}}}\simeq\omega_{\widetilde{C^{n}}/K^{p^{-n}}}
. Moreover, if
\nu_{\widetilde{P^{n}}}
is the valuation of
K^{p^{-n}}K(C)|K^{p^{-n}}
with valuation ring
\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{C^{n}},\widetilde{P^{n}}}
, then
\nu_{\widetilde{P^{n}}}(dx_{0})=\nu_{0}(dx_{0})=d(C,P)-1
, because
\widetilde{P^{n}}
is rational. Therefore, the inclusion of sheaves

\mathfrak{b}_{n}^{\ast}(\Omega_{C/C_{1}})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{C^{n}}}(D^{\prime\prime})\hookrightarrow\omega_{\widetilde{C^{n}}/K^{p^{-n}}}

is locally an isomorphism, because the image consists of
1
-forms having non-negative value under
\nu_{\widetilde{P^{n}}}
. Hence it is an isomorphism.

Now, to prove the third isomorphism,
we notice that Proposition
4.2
says that

\omega_{\widetilde{C^{n}}/K^{p^{-n}},\widetilde{P^{n}}}\subset\omega_{C^{n}/K^{p^{-n}},P^{n}}
.
Since
\omega_{C^{n}/K^{p^{-n}},P^{n}}
and
\omega_{\widetilde{C^{n}}/K^{p^{-n}},\widetilde{P^{n}}}
are invertible sheaves, there are
f,g\in\omega_{C^{n}/K^{p^{-n}},P^{n}}
such that

\omega_{\widetilde{C^{n}}/K^{p^{-n}},\widetilde{P^{n}}}=\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{C^{n}},\widetilde{P^{n}}}f\mbox{ and }\omega_{C^{n}/K^{p^{-n}},P^{n}}=\mathcal{O}_{C^{n},P^{n}}g.
In particular
fg^{-1}\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{C^{n}},\widetilde{P^{n}}}\subset\mathcal{O}_{C^{n},P^{n}}
and, from the definition of the conductor and the rationality of
P^{n}
, we have
\nu_{\widetilde{P^{n}}}(fg^{-1})\geq c(C,P)
. Using the same “local x global” argument, to conclude the second isomorphism, we get

\omega_{\widetilde{C^{n}}/K^{p^{-n}}}\simeq\mathfrak{b}_{n}^{\ast}(\omega_{C/K})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{C^{n}}}(-R)

where
R\geq D^{\prime}
is a divisor, locally (at
\widetilde{P^{n}}
) given by the ideal generated by
fg^{-1}
. Since
\widetilde{C^{n}}
is smooth, we obtain

\begin{array}[]{lllll}\deg(\mathfrak{b}_{n}^{\ast}(\omega_{C/K})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{C^{n}}}(-D^{\prime}))&=&2p_{a}(C)-2-\deg(D^{\prime})&=&2p_{g}(C)-2\\
&=&\deg(\omega_{\widetilde{C^{n}}/K^{p^{-n}}})&=&2p_{a}(C)-2-\deg(R)\end{array}

and, consequently,
R=D
.

To prove the first isomorphism we use the identity
\mathfrak{b}_{n}(D)=D^{\prime}+D^{\prime\prime}
together with the second and third isomorphisms to obtain
\mathfrak{b}_{n}^{\ast}(\Omega_{C/C_{1}}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{C}(D))\simeq\mathfrak{b}_{n}^{\ast}(\omega_{C/K})
. Now we just need to use Theorem
4.1
to conclude the proof.
∎

Proposition 5.4

If

Let us consider a basis

Using that

Remark 5.5

Notice that the point

Proposition 5.6

6
The structure of arithmetic genus three regular curves with two non-smooth points

In this section we will consider
C
as a regular, non-smooth, geometrically integral and complete curve of arithmetic genus
3
over a separably closed field
K
of characteristic
3
.

Notice that the arithmetic genus
p_{a}(C^{L})
of
C^{L}
is also equal to
3
since arithmetic genus is invariant under base change. However the geometric genus
p_{g}(C^{L})
may be smaller than
3
(because
C^{L}
may be non-regular) and, in this chapter, we will classify such phenomenon when

p_{g}(C^{\overline{K}})=1.

In this case, we say that
C
is an
geometrically elliptic
curve of arithmetic genus
3
.
Notice also that we always have
p_{a}(\widetilde{C^{\overline{K}}})=p_{g}(\widetilde{C^{\overline{K}}})=p_{g}(C^{\overline{K}}).

We remember that the curve
C
, with the additional hypothesis
p_{g}(C^{\overline{K}})=1
, admits exactly two non-smooth points of singularity degree one (see
[
13
]
Remark 4.1). If
P
is a non-smooth point of
C
, then Corollary
3.23
together with the fact that the singularity degree is the number of gaps of
\Gamma_{C^{\overline{K}},P^{\overline{K}}}
(see
[
1
]
Proposition 1.1) implies that
P
admits degree
3
,
\Gamma_{C^{\overline{K}},P^{\overline{K}}}
is generated by
2
and
3
and
P
has differential degree
2
. Moreover, it also follows from the same corollary that the points of
C_{1}
lying under the non-smooth points of
C
are rational and, hence,
C_{1}
is smooth.

Remark 6.1
.

If
C^{\overline{K}}
is a non-hyperelliptic curve then there exists a morphism from
C
to
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{K}
such that
C
is isomorphic to a quartic defined over
K
. Such a
morphism from
C
to
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{K}
will be called a canonical embedding of
C
.

Indeed, since
C
is regular, then it is Gorenstein and, consequently,
C^{\overline{K}}
is also Gorenstein because this notion is invariant under base change. Therefore, Theorem 3.3, pg. 124, in
[
18
]
implies that the morphism induced by the canonical sheaf defines an immersion from
C^{\overline{K}}
onto a quartic curve in
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\overline{K}}
. Thus
\omega_{C^{\overline{K}}/\overline{K}}
is very ample and, using exercise 1.30, pg 177, in
[
8
]
, we conclude the same to
\omega_{C/K}
. To complete the proof we just need to observe that the same exercise shows that the embedding of
C^{\overline{K}}
is obtained from the embedding of
C
.

6.1
Extrinsic geometry of geometrically elliptic, regular curve of arithmetic genus three

This section will study the relations between the sheaves
\Omega_{C/C_{1}}
,
\omega_{C/K}
and
\mathcal{O}_{C}(P+Q)
where
P
and
Q
are the non-smooth points of
C
. Consequently, we will identify certain uniquely determined divisors of
C
to reach its embedding in
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{K}
.

Proposition 6.2
.

We have the following isomorphisms.

•

\Omega_{C/C_{1}}^{\otimes(-2)}\simeq\omega_{C/K}

•

\Omega_{C/C_{1}}^{\otimes(-1)}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{C}(P+Q)\simeq\omega_{C/K}^{\otimes 2}

•

\mathcal{O}_{C}(2P+2Q)\simeq\omega_{C/K}^{\otimes 3}

Proof.

Since
p_{a}(\widetilde{C^{1}})=1
implies the isomorphism
\omega_{\widetilde{C^{1}}/K^{1/3}}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{C^{1}}}
, then Theorem
4.3
says that
\mathfrak{b}_{1}^{\ast}(\Omega_{C/C_{1}})\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{C^{1}}}(-\widetilde{P^{1}}-\widetilde{Q^{1}})
and
\mathfrak{b}_{1}^{\ast}(\omega_{C/K})\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{C^{1}}}(2\widetilde{P^{1}}+2\widetilde{Q^{1}})
. Therefore, we conclude the proof by using the isomorphism
\mathfrak{b}_{1}^{\ast}(\mathcal{O}_{C}(P+Q))\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{C^{1}}}(3\widetilde{P^{1}}+3\widetilde{Q^{1}})
together with Theorem
4.1
.
∎

Proposition 6.3
.

\dim_{K}H^{0}(C,\Omega_{C/C_{1}}^{\otimes(-1)})=1

Proof.

Since the non-smooth points of
C
have degree three, then their residue fields are simple extensions of
K
and we may use Proposition
5.4
and Proposition
5.14
to conclude the existence of an invariant conic through
P^{1}
and
Q^{1}
. In particular there exists a section

s\in H^{0}\left(C^{K^{1/3}},\omega_{C^{K^{1/3}}/K^{1/3}}^{\otimes 2}\otimes\mathcal{I}_{C^{K^{1/3}},P^{1}}\otimes\mathcal{I}_{C^{K^{1/3}},Q^{1}}\right)^{\nabla(2)}=H^{0}\left(C,\omega_{C/K}^{\otimes 2}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{C}(-P-Q)\right)

and we conclude, by Proposition
6.2
, that
\dim_{K}H^{0}(C,\Omega_{C/C_{1}}^{\otimes(-1)})\geq 1
.

Notice that
\Omega_{C/C_{1}}^{\otimes(-1)}
has degree two, from Proposition
6.2
, and hence
\mathfrak{b}_{1}^{\ast}(\Omega_{C/C_{1}}^{\otimes(-1)})
has also degree two. On the other hand, Riemann-Roch theorem implies that
\dim_{K^{1/3}}H^{0}(\widetilde{C^{1}},\mathfrak{b}_{1}^{\ast}(\Omega_{C/C_{1}}^{\otimes(-1)}))=2
and, consequently

\dim_{K^{1/3}}H^{0}(C^{1},\mathfrak{e}_{1}^{\ast}(\Omega_{C/C_{1}}^{\otimes(-1)}))\leq 2.

If
\dim_{K^{1/3}}H^{0}(C^{1},\mathfrak{e}_{1}^{\ast}(\Omega_{C/C_{1}}^{\otimes(-1)}))=2
then
\mathfrak{e}_{\infty}^{\ast}(\Omega_{C/C_{1}}^{\otimes(-1)})
would induce a degree two mophism from
C^{\overline{K}}
to
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\overline{K}}
, showing the hyperellipticity of
C^{\overline{K}}
, and so, contradicting our hypothesis. Therefore

\dim_{K}H^{0}(C,\Omega_{C/C_{1}}^{\otimes(-1)})=\dim_{K^{1/3}}H^{0}(C^{1},\mathfrak{e}_{1}^{\ast}(\Omega_{C/C_{1}}^{\otimes(-1)}))=1.

∎

Remark 6.4
.

We remember the following fact about algebraic geometry because we will often need it. Given an effective divisor
D
of
C
, there is a section
s\in H^{0}(C,\mathcal{O}_{C}(D))
such that
\operatorname{div}(s)=D
.

Indeed, we may write
D=(f_{i},U_{i})_{i\in I}
with
f_{i}\in\mathcal{O}_{C}(U_{i})
since
D
is effective. Then
1\in f_{i}^{-1}\mathcal{O}_{C}(U_{i})
for every
i\in I
and so
1\in H^{0}(C,\mathcal{O}_{C}(D))
. From the definition (see
[
8
]
[Exercise 1.13 pg. 266]) we have immediately that
\operatorname{div}(1)=D
.

Proposition 6.5
.

There exists a section
s\in H^{0}(C,\omega_{C/K}^{\otimes 2})
satisfying
\operatorname{div}(s)=R+P+Q
where
R
is a divisor of degree

.

Proof.

Let
t\in H^{0}(C,\mathcal{O}_{C}(P+Q))
be a global section such that
\operatorname{div}(t)=P+Q
. From the last two propositions there is a non-trivial section
t_{1}\in H^{0}(C,\omega_{C/K}^{\otimes 2}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{C}(-P-Q))
and we will denote the effective divisor
\operatorname{div}(t_{1})
by
R
. Using again
[
8
]
[Exercise 1.13 pg. 266] we get
\mathcal{O}_{C}(R)\simeq\omega_{C/K}^{\otimes 2}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{C}(-P-Q)
, which has degree two, and then
R>0
. Therefore
s=t_{1}t\in H^{0}(C,\omega_{C/K}^{\otimes 2})
satisfies
\operatorname{div}(s)=R+P+Q
.
∎

Lemma 6.6
.

There exists a unique degree two effective divisor
R
of
C
such that
\mathcal{O}_{C}(R+P+Q)\simeq\omega_{C/K}^{\otimes 2}
and
\mathcal{O}_{C}(2R)\simeq\omega_{C/K}
. In particular,
P,Q\not\in\operatorname{Supp}(R)
, which contains only rational points.

Proof.

Proposition
6.5
says that there exists a section
s\in H^{0}(C,\omega_{C/K}^{\otimes 2})
such that
\operatorname{div}(s)=R+P+Q
where
R
is a degree two effective divisor of
C
. Using
[
8
]
[Exercise 1.13 pg 266] we have
\omega_{C/K}^{\otimes 2}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{C}(R+P+Q)
.
Proposition
6.2
says
\mathcal{O}_{C}(2P+2Q)\simeq\omega_{C/K}^{\otimes 3}
, that is,
\mathcal{O}_{C}(2R)\simeq\omega_{C/K}^{\otimes 4}\otimes\omega_{C/K}^{\otimes-3}\simeq\omega_{C/K}
.

Now if
R^{\prime}
is a degree two divisor satisfying
\mathcal{O}_{C}(R^{\prime}+P+Q)\simeq\omega_{C/K}^{\otimes 2}
and
\mathcal{O}_{C}(2R^{\prime})\simeq\omega_{C/K}
then

is linearly equivalent to
R^{\prime}
and we can write

with
f\in K(C)
. Since
C^{\overline{K}}
is non-hyperelliptic, we have that
H^{0}(C,\omega_{C/K})=H^{0}(C,\mathcal{O}_{C}(2R^{\prime}))
is a base point free linear system and hence
H^{0}(C,\mathcal{O}_{C}(R^{\prime}))\subsetneq H^{0}(C,\mathcal{O}_{C}(2R^{\prime}))
, that is,

\dim_{K}H^{0}(C,\mathcal{O}_{C}(R^{\prime}))<\dim_{K}H^{0}(C,\omega_{C/K})=3.

On the other hand, if

then the inclusion

would provides that
\dim_{K}H^{0}(C,\mathcal{O}_{C}(R^{\prime}))=2
. However, since
R^{\prime}
is a degree two divisor, it would imply that
C
and
C^{\overline{K}}
are hyperelliptic curves, which is a contradiction. Therefore,
f\in K
and
R=R^{\prime}
.
∎

Proposition 6.7
.

There are
F\in H^{0}(C,\omega_{C/K}^{\otimes 2})
,
L_{1}\in H^{0}(C,\omega_{C/K})
and

\operatorname{div}(L^{3})=2P+2Q
. Moreover,

\mathbb{P}^{2}_{K}
.

Proof.

The existence of

are obtained by the last two results and by the last remark. If we take the section

in

passing through the

-rational points

and

, then
\mathfrak{n}_{1}^{\ast}\operatorname{div}(L^{3})=6\widetilde{P^{1}}+6\widetilde{Q^{1}}
. Proposition
6.2
says that there is

such that
\operatorname{div}(L^{\prime})=2P+2Q
. Since
\operatorname{div}((\mathfrak{n}_{1})^{\ast}(L^{3}))=(\mathfrak{b}_{1})^{\ast}(\operatorname{div}(L^{\prime}))
then

, for some

, and

. We also have that

because, otherwise, we would have

Notice that

and

define the same divisor of

. Hence, we conclude that there exist

. Moreover, in

. Indeed if we would have

showing that

. However this is a contradiction because

. Therefore

. After multiplying

.
∎

Theorem 6.14
.

-equivalent to a curve in

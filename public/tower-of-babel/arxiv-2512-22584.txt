An important extension of polynomials ℝ ⁡ [ x 1 , … , x n ] \mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}] with real coefficients are polynomials ℝ m × m ​ [ x 1 , … , x n ] \mathds{R}^{m\times m}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}] with matrix coefficients. Preliminaries

2.1
Hermitian Matrices

Let
m,n\in\mathds{N}
, let
\mathrm{Herm}_{m}\subseteq\mathds{C}^{m\times m}
be the set of all
m\times m
Hermitian matrices, let
\mathrm{Herm}_{m}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be the set of all polynomials in
n
variables
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
with Hermitian matrix coefficients, and denote by
\mathrm{Herm}_{m,+}
the subset of positive semi-definite matrices.
The set
\mathrm{Herm}_{m,+}\subseteq\mathrm{Herm}_{m}
is a full-dimensional convex cone and hence every
M\in\mathrm{Herm}_{m}
can be written as
M=M^{+}-M^{-}
with
M^{+},M^{-}\in\mathrm{Herm}_{m,+}
.
A matrix polynomial
P\in\mathrm{Herm}_{m}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
is positive on
K\subseteq\mathds{R}^{n}
, written
P\succeq 0
on
K
, if
P(x)\in\mathrm{Herm}_{m,+}
for all
x\in K
.
We define

\mathrm{Pos}(K):=\big\{P\in\mathrm{Herm}_{m}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\;\big|\;P(x)\succeq 0\ \text{ for all }x\in K\big\}.

The space of Hermitian matrices is a finite-dimensional real Hilbert space, see e.g.
[
6
, Sect. 2]
.
Let
E_{k,l}:=(\delta_{i,k}\cdot\delta_{j,l})_{i,j=1}^{m}
where
\delta_{i,j}
is the Kronecker delta function.
Then, for
k,l=1,\dots,m
, the

H_{k,l}:=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(E_{k,l}+E_{l,k})&\text{ for }k<l,\\
E_{k,k}&\text{ for }k=l,\\
\frac{i}{\sqrt{2}}(E_{k,l}-E_{l,k})&\text{ for }k>l.\end{cases}

form an orthonormal basis of
\mathrm{Herm}_{m}
.
The inner product
\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle
on
\mathrm{Herm}_{m}
is given by

\left\langle A,B\right\rangle:=\mathrm{tr}(A^{*}B)=\mathrm{tr}(AB)\in\mathds{R}

for all
A,B\in\mathrm{Herm}_{m}
.
Positive semi-definiteness can be characterized by the following.

Lemma 2.1

(see e.g.
[
9
, Prop. A.21]
)
.

Let
m\in\mathds{N}
and let
M\in\mathrm{Herm}_{m}
.
Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

M\succeq 0
.

(ii)

\left\langle M,X\right\rangle\geq 0
for all
X\in\mathrm{Herm}_{m,+}
.

2.2
Hermitian Matrix Valued Measures

Definition 2.2
.

Let
m\in\mathds{N}
and
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{A})
be a measurable space.
We say
\mu
is a
matrix-valued measure
on
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{A})
if

\mu=(\mu_{i,j})_{i,j=1,\dots,m}

for some finite signed complex measures
\mu_{i,j}
on
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{A})
and
\mu(S)\in\mathrm{Herm}_{m}
for all
S\in\mathcal{A}
.
A matrix-valued measure
\mu
is called
positive
if
\mu(S)\in\mathrm{Herm}_{+}
for all
S\in\mathcal{A}
.

The definition of positive matrix-valued measures coincides with the definition of positive operator-valued measures from
[
4
, Rem. 2]
and
[
1
, Def. 1]
.
Integrals with respect to these measures are defined in the following way.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
m,n\in\mathds{N}
,
K\subseteq\mathds{R}^{n}
be closed,
p\in\mathrm{Herm}_{m}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
, and let
\mu
be a matrix-valued measure.
We define the integral

\int_{K}\left\langle p(x),\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\right\rangle:=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\int_{K}p_{i,j}(x)\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\mu_{i,j}^{\prime}(x)\in\mathds{R}

with
p_{i,j}=\left\langle p,H_{i,j}\right\rangle\in\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
and
\mu_{i,j}^{\prime}=\left\langle\mu,H_{i,j}\right\rangle
a signed measure.

Let
m\in\mathds{N}
and
A=(a_{k,l})_{k,l=1}^{m}\in\mathrm{Herm}_{m,+}
.
Then

|a_{k,l}|^{2}\leq a_{k,k}\cdot a_{l,l}\leq\mathrm{tr}(A)^{2}

for all
k,l=1,\dots,m
.
This extends to positive matrix-valued measures.

Lemma 2.4

(see
[
8
, Lem. 1.1]
)
.

Let
m\in\mathds{N}
, let
\mathcal{X}
be a locally compact Hausdorff space equipped with some
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{A}
.
Let
\mu
on
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{A})
be a positive matrix-valued measure with components
\mu_{i,j}
,
i,j=1,\dots,m
.
We define the trace measure
\mathrm{tr}(\mu)
by

\mathrm{tr}(\mu):=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mu_{i,i}.

Then
\mathrm{tr}(\mu)
is a real-valued positive measure and
\mu_{i,j}\ll\mathrm{tr}(\mu)
, i.e.,
\mu_{i,j}
is absolutely continuous with respect to
\mathrm{tr}(\mu)
for all
i,j=1,\dots,m
.

Remark 2.5

(see
[
8
, Lem. 1.1 and 1.2]
,
[
6
, Section 2]
)
.

Using the Radon–Nikodym derivative

\varphi_{i,j}:\mathds{R}^{n}\to\mathds{C}\quad\text{with}\quad\varphi_{i,j}:=\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu_{i,j}}{\mathrm{d}\mathrm{tr}(\mu)},

for positive matrix-valued measures
\mu
, the integral from
2.3
becomes

\int_{K}\left\langle p(x),\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\right\rangle=\int_{K}\left\langle p(x),\rho(x)\right\rangle\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\mathrm{tr}(\mu)(x)

where

\rho:\mathds{R}^{n}\to\mathds{C}^{m\times m}\quad\text{with}\quad\rho(x):=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\varphi_{i,j}(x)E_{i,j}

is positive semi-definite and Hermitian
\mathrm{tr}(\mu)
-almost everywhere.
Hence,

\int_{K}\left\langle p(x),\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\right\rangle\geq 0

for positive matrix-valued measures
\mu
and
p\in\mathrm{Pos}(\mathds{R}^{n})
.

\circ

Another useful property of the integral from
2.3
is the following.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
m,n\in\mathds{N}
and let
K\subseteq\mathds{R}^{n}
be closed.
Let
\mu
be a matrix-valued measure on
K
.
Let
p\in\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]

Then

\int_{K}\left\langle M\cdot p(x),\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\right\rangle=\left\langle\int_{K}p(x)\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\mu(x),M\right\rangle.

Proof.

By
\mu=\sum_{i,j=1}H_{i,j}\cdot\mu_{i,j}^{\prime}
with
\mu_{i,j}^{\prime}:=\left\langle\mu,H_{i,j}\right\rangle
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{K}\left\langle Mp(x),\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\int_{K}\left\langle Mp(x),H_{i,j}\right\rangle\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\!\left\langle\mu,H_{i,j}\right\rangle(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\left\langle M,H_{i,j}\right\rangle\int_{K}p(x)\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\mu_{i,j}^{\prime}(x)

\displaystyle=\left\langle M,\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\int_{K}p(x)\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\mu_{i,j}^{\prime}(x)H_{i,j}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle M,\int_{K}p(x)\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\mu(x)\right\rangle.\qed

We will use the following matrix version of Haviland’s Theorem.

Theorem 2.7

(see
[
4
, Thm. 3]
)
.

Let
m,n\in\mathds{N}
,
K\subseteq\mathds{R}^{n}
be closed, and

Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

is a

for all

.

(ii)

L(p)\geq 0
for all
p\in\mathrm{Pos}(K)
.

The proof of
2.7
in
[
4
]
is analog to the proof of Haviland’s theorem.
Using the M. Riesz Extension Theorem, the functional

can be extended to a space containing continuous functions with compact support and by a version of the Riesz–Markov–Kakutani Theorem, this gives a suitable measure.
The difference to the case with real coefficients is, that for Hermitian matrix coefficients the spaces will be tensor products.

\mathrm{Herm}_{m}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
for example, can be written as a tensor product

If we not only work with

2.3
Convex Cones on Tensor Products

Definition 2.9
.

Let
V_{1},V_{2}
be two real vector spaces and let
C_{1}\subseteq V_{1},C_{2}\subseteq V_{2}
be two convex cones.
Define the
minimal tensor product
as

C_{1}\otimes C_{2}=\mathrm{conv}\,\{c_{1}\otimes c_{2}\mid c_{1}\in C_{1},c_{2}\in C_{2}\}.

The tensor product of convex cones
C_{1}\otimes C_{2}
then gives a convex cone in the tensor product of the overlying vector spaces
V_{1}\otimes V_{2}
.
Then

\mathrm{Herm}_{m,+}\subseteq\mathrm{Herm}_{m}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{+}\subseteq\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]

are convex cones.
Hence,

\mathrm{Herm}_{m,+}\otimes\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{+}

is a convex cone in

\mathrm{Herm}_{m}\otimes\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]=\mathrm{Herm}_{m}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}].

Then

\mathrm{Herm}_{m,+}\otimes\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{+}\subseteq\mathrm{Pos}(\mathds{R}^{n}).

For the M. Riesz Extension Theorem, we need the following lemma.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
V,F
be real vector spaces and let
C\subseteq F
be a convex cone.
Let
E\subseteq F
be a linear subspace with
F=E+C
and let
V_{+}\subseteq V
be a full-dimensional cone.
Then

V\otimes F=(V\otimes E)+(V_{+}\otimes C).

Proof.

Since
V_{+}\subseteq V
is a full-dimensional cone, there exists a basis
\{M_{i}\}_{i\in I}
of
V
for some Index set
I
such that
M_{i}\in V_{+}
for all
i\in I
.
Now let
f\in V\otimes F
.
Then there exist

k\in\mathds{N},\quad\widetilde{M}_{1},\dots,\widetilde{M}_{k}\in\{M_{i}\}_{i\in I},\quad\text{and}\quad f_{1},\dots,f_{k}\in F

such that

f=\widetilde{M}_{1}\otimes f_{1}+\dots+\widetilde{M}_{k}\otimes f_{k}.

Now, for
f_{1},\dots,f_{k}\in F
, there exist
e_{1},\dots,e_{k}\in E
and
c_{1},\dots,c_{k}\in C
such that

f_{i}=e_{i}+c_{i}

for all
i=1,\dots,k
.
Hence,

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle=\widetilde{M}_{1}\otimes f_{1}+\dots+\widetilde{M}_{k}\otimes f_{k}

\displaystyle=\widetilde{M}_{1}\otimes e_{1}+\dots+\widetilde{M}_{k}\otimes e_{k}+\widetilde{M}_{1}\otimes c_{1}+\dots+\widetilde{M}_{k}\otimes c_{k}

\displaystyle\in V\otimes E+V_{+}\otimes C.

Since
f\in V\otimes F
was chosen arbitrarily,

V\otimes F\subseteq V\otimes E+V_{+}\otimes C.

The reverse inclusion holds trivially, since
V_{+}\subseteq V
and
E,C\subseteq F
.
Therefore,

V\otimes F=(V\otimes E)+(V_{+}\otimes C).\qed

Note that this lemma states that if
E\subseteq F
is a cofinal subspace and
V_{+}\subseteq V
is full-dimensional, then
V\otimes E
is cofinal in
V\otimes F
with positivity cone
V_{+}\otimes F_{+}
.
The
M. Riesz Extension Theorem
now states that every linear functional

L:V\otimes E\to\mathds{R}

which is positive on
(V\otimes E)\cap(V_{+}\otimes F_{+})
can be extended to a linear functional

\widetilde{L}:V\otimes F\to\mathds{R}

which is positive on
V_{+}\otimes F_{+}
.

Returning to the polynomial matrix case, let
n,m\in\mathds{N}
and define

F:=\{f\in\mathcal{C}(\mathds{R}^{n})\mid\exists p\in\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]:|f|\leq p\ \text{on}\ \mathds{R}^{n}\}

as well as

F_{+}:=\{f\in F\mid f\geq 0\ \text{on}\ \mathds{R}^{n}\}.

Then the continuous functions with compact support
\mathcal{C}_{c}(\mathds{R}^{n})
are contained in
F
and

F=\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]+F_{+}.

By
2.10
,

\mathrm{Herm}_{m}\otimes F=(\mathrm{Herm}_{m}\otimes\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}])+(\mathrm{Herm}_{m,+}\otimes F_{+}).

Notice that

\mathrm{Herm}_{m,+}\otimes\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{+}\subseteq(\mathrm{Herm}_{m}\otimes\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}])\cap(\mathrm{Herm}_{m,+}\otimes F_{+})

as well as

(\mathrm{Herm}_{m}\otimes\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}])\cap(\mathrm{Herm}_{m,+}\otimes F_{+})\subseteq\mathrm{Pos}(\mathds{R}^{n}).

The latter is simply because elements
P\in\mathrm{Herm}_{m}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]=\mathrm{Herm}_{m}\otimes\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
are matrices with polynomials as entries and elements
f\in\mathrm{Herm}_{m,+}\otimes F_{+}
are positive semi-definite at every point on
\mathds{R}^{n}
.
Hence, every function in the intersection is a polynomial matrix which is positive semi-definite on
\mathds{R}^{n}
, which is exactly the definition of
\mathrm{Pos}(\mathds{R}^{n})
.
Now every linear functional

L:\mathrm{Herm}_{m}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to\mathds{R}

such that
L
is positive on
\mathrm{Pos}(\mathds{R}^{n})
can be extended to a linear functional

\widetilde{L}:\mathrm{Herm}_{m}\otimes F

such that
\widetilde{L}
is positive on
\mathrm{Herm}_{m,+}\otimes F_{+}
.
With
\mathcal{C}_{c}(\mathds{R}^{n})\subseteq F
and by (a version of) the Riesz–Markov–Kakutani Theorem, there exists a measure which represents
\widetilde{L}
.
This is how
2.7
is proven in
[
4
]
.

The Canonical Representation of Linear Operators on Matrix Polynomials

At first we give an extension of the canonical representation (
1
) to any linear operator
T:V[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to V[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]

.

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
n\in\mathds{N}
and let
V
be a real vector space.
Let

T:V[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to V[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]

be a linear operator, i.e.,
T(af+g)=a\cdot Tf+Tg
for all
a\in\mathds{R}
and
f,g\in V[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
.
Then, for all
\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}

Q_{\alpha}:V\to V[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]

such that

T=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}\frac{1}{\alpha!}\cdot Q_{\alpha}\times\partial^{\alpha}\quad\text{with}\quad(Q_{\alpha}\times\partial^{\alpha})(v\cdot x^{\beta}):=Q_{\alpha}(v)\cdot\partial^{\alpha}x^{\beta}

(2)

for all
\alpha,\beta\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}
and

.

The representation (

The proof is a straight-forward extension of the proof in
[

Proof.

We will prove
3.11

such that

T(vx^{\beta})=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}\frac{1}{\alpha!}\cdot(Q_{\alpha}\times\partial^{\alpha})(vx^{\beta})=\sum_{\alpha\preceq\beta}\binom{\beta}{\alpha}\cdot Q_{\alpha}(v)\cdot x^{\beta-\alpha}

(3)

for all
\beta\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}
with
|\beta|\leq d
and
v\in V
.

d=0
:

For
\beta=0
, clearly,

Q_{0}:V\to V[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}],\quad v\mapsto Q_{0}(v):=T(v\cdot 1)

is the unique linear
Q_{0}
in (
3
).

d\to d+1
:

Assume, for all
\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}
with
|\alpha|\leq d
, there exist unique linear
Q_{\alpha}:V\to\in V[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
in (
3
), i.e., for all
|\beta|\leq d
.
Now let

with

According to (

for all

.
Hence,

Q_{\gamma}:V\to V[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}],\quad v\mapsto Q_{\gamma}(v):=T(vx^{\gamma})-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\preceq\gamma\\
\alpha\neq\gamma\end{subarray}}\binom{\gamma}{\alpha}\cdot Q_{\alpha}(v)\cdot x^{\gamma-\alpha}

Corollary 3.13

Let

, and
p\in\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
.
Hence,

T_{y}p(x)=T_{y,1}p(x)=\int_{\mathds{R}^{n}}\left\langle p(x+t),\mathrm{d}\mu_{y,1}(t)\right\rangle=\int_{\mathds{R}^{n}}p(x+t)\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\mu_{y,1}(t)

for all
x,y\in\mathds{R}^{n}
and
p\in\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
which proves (iii).

The statement (
6
) for the polynomial coefficients
q_{\alpha}
,
\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}
, follows from the fact, that the sum in (
4
) from
4.17
reduces to a single element due to having a
1
-dimensional real vector space
\mathrm{Herm}_{1}=\mathds{R}
.

(iii)
\Rightarrow
(i):
Define
\mu_{y,\lambda}:=\lambda\mu_{y}
.
Then (i) follows from
4.17
.
∎

5
Matrix
K
-Positivity Preserver for Compact
K

Contrary to the previous section were we worked with collections of matrix-valued measures

(\mu_{M})_{M\in\mathrm{Herm}_{m}}

in
4.17
(iv), we work now with operator-valued measures

\mu:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{L}(\mathrm{Herm}_{m},\mathrm{Herm}_{m}),

where
\mathcal{L}(\mathrm{Herm}_{m},\mathrm{Herm}_{m})
denotes the space of linear operators
\mathrm{Herm}_{m}\to\mathrm{Herm}_{m}
.
For a given matrix
M\in\mathrm{Herm}_{m}
, we can write
\mu_{M}
as the measure

\mu(\,\cdot\,)(M):\mathcal{A}\to\mathrm{Herm}_{m}.

The difference is that collections of matrix-valued measures do not need to fulfill any linearity conditions, while operator-valued measures do.
This solves the problem outlined in
4.18
, but the existence of such operator-valued measures can only be proven so far only on compact sets
K\subseteq\mathds{R}^{n}
.

Definition 5.20

(see
[
4
, Sect. 2]
)
.

Let
m\in\mathds{N}
and let
\mathcal{A}
be a
\sigma
-algebra.
A set function

\mu:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{L}(\mathrm{Herm}_{m},\mathrm{Herm}_{m})

is called a
positive operator-valued measure
if

\mu[M]:=\mu(\,\cdot\,)(M):\mathcal{A}\to\mathrm{Herm}_{m}

is a positive matrix-valued measure for all
M\in\mathrm{Herm}_{m,+}
.

Integrals with respect to such measures are defined in the following way.

Definition 5.21
.

Let
m,n\in\mathds{N}
,
K\subseteq\mathds{R}^{n}
be closed,
p\in\mathrm{Herm}_{m}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
, and
\mu:\mathfrak{B}(K)\to\mathcal{L}(\mathrm{Herm}_{m},\mathrm{Herm}_{m})
be a positive operator-valued measure.
We define

\int_{K}p(x)\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\mu(x):=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\int_{K}\left\langle p(x),H_{i,j}\right\rangle\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\mu[H_{i,j}](x)\quad\in\mathrm{Herm}_{m}.

Integrals of positive matrix polynomials with respect to positive operator-valued measures are again positive due to the linearity conditions on operator-valued measures.
Therefore, the following holds.

Lemma 5.22
.

Let
m,n\in\mathds{N}
, let
K\subseteq\mathds{R}^{n}
be closed,

\mu:\mathfrak{B}(K)\to\mathcal{L}(\mathrm{Herm}_{m},\mathrm{Herm}_{m})

be a positive operator-valued measure, and let
p\in\mathrm{Pos}(K)
.
Then

\int_{K}p(x)\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\mu(x)\succeq 0.

Proof.

Let
X\in\mathrm{Herm}_{m,+}
and define the matrix-valued measure

\widetilde{\mu}_{X}:\mathfrak{B}(K)\to\mathrm{Herm}_{m}\quad\text{by}\quad\widetilde{\mu}_{X}:=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\left\langle\mu[H_{i,j}],X\right\rangle H_{i,j}.

Let
M\in\mathrm{Herm}_{m,+}
and
S\in\mathfrak{B}(K)
.
By linearity of operator-valued measures,

\displaystyle\left\langle\widetilde{\mu}_{X}(S),M\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\left\langle\mu(S)(H_{i,j}),X\right\rangle\left\langle M,H_{i,j}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\mu(S)\left(\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\left\langle M,H_{i,j}\right\rangle H_{i,j}\right),X\right\rangle=\left\langle\mu(S)(M),X\right\rangle\geq 0.

Hence,
\widetilde{\mu}_{X}
is a positive matrix-valued measure by
2.1
and

\displaystyle\left\langle\int_{K}p(x)\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\mu(x),X\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\int_{K}\left\langle p(x),H_{i,j}\right\rangle\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\left\langle\mu[H_{i,j}],X\right\rangle(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\int_{K}\left\langle p(x),H_{i,j}\right\rangle\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\left\langle\widetilde{\mu}_{X},H_{i,j}\right\rangle(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{K}\left\langle p(x),\mathrm{d}\widetilde{\mu}_{X}(x)\right\rangle\quad\geq 0

for all
p\in\mathrm{Pos}(K)
, where the positive semi-definiteness follows from
2.5
.
Since
X\in\mathrm{Herm}_{m,+}
is arbitrary and by
2.1
, the assertion is proven.
∎

This positivity from
5.22
can fail without the linear conditions of operator-valued measures.
That is, if we would only look at collections of measures as in
4.17
(iv), then this positivity will not hold anymore, as shown in
4.18
.
For the characterization of operator-valued measures according to
4.17
, we use
2.8
.

Note,
K\subseteq\mathds{R}^{n}
does not need to be a compact set for
2.8
to hold.
J. Cimprič and A. Zalar use a more general setting on arbitrary closed
K\subseteq\mathds{R}^{n}
, which requires additional conditions on the functional
L
.
This is because the proof of
[
4
, Thm. 4]
uses Arveson’s Extension Lemma, which replaces simple positivity with complete positivity.
This is necessary, since there are positive but not completely positive functionals which can fail to have a linear extension for some finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces, see e.g.
[
3
]
.

However, on compact sets
K
this additional condition is not required anymore and we can formulate the following theorem.

Theorem 5.23
.

Let
m,n\in\mathds{N}
, let
K\subseteq\mathds{R}^{n}
be compact, and let

T:\mathrm{Herm}_{m}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to\mathrm{Herm}_{m}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]

be a linear with canonical representation

T=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}\frac{1}{\alpha!}\cdot(Q_{\alpha}\times\partial^{\alpha}).

Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

T
is a
K
-positivity preserver, i.e.,
T\mathrm{Pos}(K)\succeq\mathrm{Pos}(K)
.

(ii)

For each
y\in K
, there exists a positive operator-valued measure
\nu_{y}
on
{K-y}\subseteq\mathds{R}^{n}
such that

Q_{\beta}(M)(y)=\int_{K-y}t^{\beta}\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\nu_{y}[M](t)

for all
\beta\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}
.
In this case,

(T_{y}p)(x)=\int_{K-y}p(x+t)\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\nu_{y}(t)

for all
x\in K
and
p\in\mathrm{Herm}_{m}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
.

The proof is an adapted version of the proof of
[
5
, Thm. 3.5]
using
[
4
, Thm. 4]
on compact sets.

Proof.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii):
Let
y\in K
and define

L_{y}:\mathrm{Herm}_{m}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to\mathrm{Herm}_{m},\quad p\mapsto L_{y}(p):=(Tp)(y).

Then
L_{y}(p)\succeq 0
for all
p\in\mathrm{Pos}(K)
.

2.8
asserts the existence of a positive Borel operator-valued measure
\mu_{y}:\mathfrak{B}(K)\to\mathcal{L}(\mathrm{Herm}_{m},\mathrm{Herm}_{m})
such that

L_{y}(p)=\int_{K}p(x)\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\mu_{y}(x)

for all
p\in\mathrm{Herm}_{m}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
.
Additionally,

L_{y}(M\cdot p)=(T(M\cdot p))(y)=(T_{y}(M\cdot p))(y)=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}\frac{1}{\alpha!}\cdot Q_{\alpha}(M)(y)\cdot(\partial^{\alpha}p)(y)

for all
M\in\mathrm{Herm}_{m}
and
p\in\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
.
Let
M\in\mathrm{Herm}_{m}
and
\beta\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}
.
Set
\widetilde{p}(x):=(x-y)^{\beta}
.
Then

\partial^{\alpha}\widetilde{p}(y)=\begin{cases}0&\alpha\neq\beta\\
\beta!&\alpha=\beta\end{cases}

for all
\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}
and, hence,

L_{y}(M\cdot\widetilde{p})=Q_{\beta}(M)(y)=\int_{K}M\cdot(x-y)^{\beta}\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\mu_{y}(x)=\int_{K}(x-y)^{\beta}\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\mu_{y}[M](x).

for all
M\in\mathrm{Herm}_{m}
.
Define
\nu_{y}(\,\cdot\,):=\mu_{y}(\,\cdot\,+y)
as the pushforward measure regarding to the translation in
\mathds{R}^{n}
.
Then

\nu_{y}:\mathfrak{B}(K)\to\mathcal{L}(\mathrm{Herm}_{m},\mathrm{Herm}_{m})

is a positive Borel operator-valued measure and

Q_{\beta}(M)(y)=\int_{K-y}t^{\beta}\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\nu_{y}[M](t)

for all
\beta\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}
,
y\in K
, and
M\in\mathrm{Herm}_{m}
.
Furthermore, for
y\in\mathds{R}^{n}
,
M\in\mathrm{Herm}_{m}
, and
\beta\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}
, it follows from the multi-binomial theorem that

\displaystyle T_{y}(Mx^{\beta})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}\binom{\beta}{\alpha}\cdot Q_{\alpha}(M)(y)\cdot x^{\beta-\alpha}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}\binom{\beta}{\alpha}\cdot x^{\beta-\alpha}\cdot\int_{K-y}t^{\alpha}\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\nu_{y}[M](t)

\displaystyle=\int_{K-y}(x+t)^{\beta}\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\nu_{y}[M](t)

\displaystyle=\int_{K-y}M\cdot(x+t)^{\beta}\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\nu_{y}(t)

and, hence, by linearity,

(T_{y}p)(x)=\int_{K-y}p(x+t)\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\nu_{y}(t)

for all
x,y\in\mathds{R}^{n}
and all
p\in\mathrm{Herm}_{m}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i):
Let
y\in K
and let
\nu_{y}
be a positive matrix-valued measure on
K-y
such that

\int_{K-y}t^{\beta}\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\nu_{y}[M](t)=Q_{\beta}(M)(y)

for all
M\in\mathrm{Herm}_{m}
and all
\beta\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}
.
Then

\displaystyle\int_{K-y}M\cdot(t+y)^{\beta}\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\nu_{y}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\preceq\beta}\binom{\beta}{\alpha}\cdot y^{\beta-\alpha}\cdot\int_{K-y}t^{\alpha}\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\nu_{y}[M](t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}\frac{1}{\alpha!}\cdot Q_{\alpha}(M)(y)\cdot\partial^{\alpha}y^{\beta}

\displaystyle=(T_{y}(M\cdot x^{\beta}))(y)

\displaystyle=(T(M\cdot x^{\beta}))(y)

for all
\beta\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}
,
M\in\mathrm{Herm}_{m}
, and
y\in K
.
By linearity,

\int_{K-y}p(t+y)\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\nu_{y}(t)=(Tp)(y)

for all

Tubeless anesthesia improves postoperative recovery following VATS wedge resection: a prospective randomized controlled study
Authors: Xiaoling Li, Shaohua Guo, Weidong Liang, Rui Han, Yi Wang, Maolin Zhong, Liuqing Yuan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeThis study aimed to evaluate the effects of different tubeless anesthesia strategies on postoperative recovery in patients undergoing video-assisted thoracoscopic (VATS) wedge resection within an enhanced recovery after surgery (ERAS) protocol.MethodsIn this prospective randomized controlled trial, 120 patients scheduled for elective VATS wedge resection were randomly assigned to one of three groups: double-lumen endotracheal tube intubation (DLETT), laryngeal mask airway combined with intercostal nerve block (LMA + INB), or laryngeal mask airway combined with paravertebral block (LMA + PVB). Primary outcomes included length of hospital stay, time to ambulation and oral intake, and visual analog scale (VAS) pain scores. Secondary outcomes included intraoperative hemodynamics, arterial blood gas analysis, serum inflammatory cytokines (IL-6, IL-1β, TNF-α), postoperative complications, and total hospitalization costs.ResultsPatients in the LMA + PVB group had significantly shorter hospital stays and earlier ambulation compared to the DLETT group (p < 0.001). VAS pain scores at rest and during coughing were consistently lower in the LMA + PVB group from 6 to 24 h postoperatively (p < 0.01). Heart rate, mean arterial pressure, and oxygenation parameters—including peripheral capillary oxygen saturation (SpO₂), partial pressure of arterial oxygen (PaO₂), and arterial oxygen saturation (SaO₂)—remained within physiological ranges in all groups throughout the perioperative period. IL-6 and IL-1β levels at 24 h were significantly lower in the LMA groups (p < 0.01). While DLETT provided better surgical field visibility, it was associated with higher rates of sore throat and hoarseness, as well as greater hospitalization costs.ConclusionTubeless anesthesia, particularly LMA + PVB, enhances postoperative recovery following VATS wedge resection by reducing pain, inflammation, and hospitalization duration. These findings suggest that LMA + PVB may be a feasible alternative to conventional intubation-based anesthesia in selected clinical settings.Clinical trial registrationThis study was registered at the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR2300073484) on July 12, 2023.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12893-025-03382-x.Keywords: Tubeless anesthesia, Paravertebral block, Enhanced recovery after surgery (ERAS), Video-assisted thoracoscopic surgery (VATS), Postoperative recovery

Introduction

In recent years, the concept of Enhanced Recovery After Surgery (ERAS) has been widely adopted in thoracic surgery, aiming to reduce surgical stress, optimize perioperative management, and promote faster patient recovery [1]. Among various thoracic procedures, video-assisted thoracoscopic surgery (VATS) wedge resection is particularly suitable for ERAS implementation due to its minimally invasive nature, rapid postoperative recovery, and shorter hospital stay[2, 3]. While ERAS has achieved notable progress in preoperative preparation and postoperative care, the selection and optimization of anesthetic strategies remain a critical component for improving overall ERAS outcomes—especially in short-duration thoracoscopic procedures such as wedge resection[4, 5].

Traditionally, double-lumen endotracheal tube intubation (DLETT) has been the standard airway management technique in thoracic anesthesia, as it facilitates one-lung ventilation and provides optimal surgical exposure[6]. However, DLETT is also associated with several disadvantages, including greater hemodynamic fluctuations, an increased risk of airway injury, and a higher incidence of postoperative complications such as sore throat and hoarseness[7]. With the growing emphasis on patient-centered care and the widespread implementation of ERAS protocols, tubeless anesthesia has gained attention as a less invasive alternative. Tubeless anesthesia may involve various airway strategies, including the laryngeal mask airway (LMA), in combination with regional nerve blocks such as intercostal or paravertebral blocks, to ensure respiratory stability and adequate analgesia while reducing airway irritation. Nonetheless, LMA represents only one of several possible methods within the broader concept of tubeless anesthesia.[8–10]. Although preliminary findings are promising, there remains a lack of prospective studies directly comparing DLETT with different tubeless anesthesia strategies (LMA + INB or LMA + PVB) within an ERAS framework. In particular, comprehensive evidence regarding their impact on postoperative recovery, pain control, physiological stability, and economic burden is still limited and warrants further investigation.

Therefore, we conducted a prospective randomized controlled trial to compare three anesthetic strategies—DLETT, LMA + INB, and LMA + PVB—in patients undergoing VATS wedge resection within an ERAS framework. The study systematically evaluated multiple perioperative outcomes, including postoperative recovery time points, pain control, hemodynamic and blood gas stability, systemic inflammatory responses, postoperative complications, and hospitalization costs. The aim was to identify a more effective and patient-friendly anesthetic approach to support the optimization of ERAS protocols in thoracic surgery.

Materials and methods

Study design and participants

This study was a prospective, single-center, three-arm randomized controlled trial conducted at the First Affiliated Hospital of Gannan Medical University from July 1, 2023, to March 31, 2024. A total of 120 patients scheduled for elective VATS wedge resection were recruited. The required sample size was determined based on a priori power analysis, with postoperative hospital stay as the primary endpoint. Assuming a clinically meaningful intergroup difference of 1.5 days, a standard deviation of 2.0 days from prior studies and pilot observations, an alpha of 0.05, and a statistical power of 80%, a minimum of 33 patients per group was required. Considering a 20% potential dropout rate, 40 patients were allocated to each group, resulting in a total of 120 participants.The inclusion criteria were as follows: age 20–60 years; body mass index (BMI) 18–23 kg/m²; American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) physical status I or II; preoperative oxygen saturation (SpO₂) > 94%; and an estimated surgical duration of 1.5 to 2 h. Patients were excluded if they had severe respiratory disease, active pulmonary infection, chronic cough, excessive airway secretions, significant cardiovascular or neurological comorbidities, psychiatric illness, liver or kidney dysfunction, a history of airway hypersensitivity, or contraindications to nerve blocks. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants prior to enrollment, and the study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Gannan Medical University (Approval No. LLSC-2023-136).This study was registered at the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR2300073484) on July 12, 2023.

Surgical indications and procedural standardization

VATS wedge resection was indicated for peripheral pulmonary lesions deemed appropriate appropriate under ERAS pathways, including: (i) peripheral nodules suitable for diagnostic or therapeutic wedge resection; (ii) undetermined peripheral lesions requiring diagnostic excision; and (iii) clinical scenarios in which limited resection was appropriate based on lesion characteristics and patient cardiopulmonary reserve. Lesions mainly consisted of benign pulmonary nodules, metastatic pulmonary lesions, and localized primary lung cancers. All resected lesions were peripheral, generally located within 2 cm of the visceral pleura, and similar in distribution among the study groups. Although lesion size and pleural distance were not separately analyzed, all cases met uniform criteria for peripheral lesions (< 2 cm from the pleural surface), and no intergroup differences in lesion type or location were noted in operative records. Standardized perioperative management and discharge criteria were applied across groups. All procedures were performed using a standardized three-port VATS wedge resection technique by the same surgical team. The same thoracoscopic approach and instrument settings were applied to all patients. A single 24-Fr chest tube was routinely placed through the observation port at the end of the operation and removed when the drainage volume was less than 200 mL within 24 h and no air leak had been observed for at least 12 h.

Randomization and anesthetic interventions

Using a random number table, patients were randomly assigned in a 1:1:1 ratio to one of three groups: the double-lumen endotracheal tube group (DLETT group), the laryngeal mask airway combined with intercostal nerve block group (LMA + INB group), or the laryngeal mask airway combined with paravertebral block group (LMA + PVB group). Allocation was concealed using sequentially numbered, opaque, sealed envelopes prepared by a staff member not involved in enrollment, intervention, or assessment. Outcome assessors and data analysts were blinded to group assignments.

All patients received perioperative care according to a standardized ERAS protocol, which included preoperative education, optimized fasting and fluid intake, multimodal analgesia, and early postoperative ambulation and feeding. In the DLETT group, general anesthesia was administered with standard induction and maintenance agents, and a double-lumen endotracheal tube was used to facilitate one-lung ventilation. In the LMA + INB group, a laryngeal mask airway was inserted for ventilation, and an ultrasound-guided intercostal nerve block (T4–T8) was performed using 0.375% ropivacaine. In the LMA + PVB group, general anesthesia was also maintained with a laryngeal mask, and a single-injection paravertebral block at the T5 level was performed using 20 mL of 0.375% ropivacaine. All regional nerve blocks were performed under ultrasound guidance by experienced anesthesiologists.

Outcome measures and data collection

The primary outcomes of this study were postoperative recovery efficiency, defined in a standardized and objective manner by three measurable clinical indicators: (1) time to first oral intake; (2) time to first ambulation; (3) length of postoperative hospital stay—Postoperative pain intensity was assessed using a validated 10-point visual analog scale (VAS), with scores recorded at 2, 6, 12, and 24 h postoperatively under both resting and coughing conditions. Postoperative oral intake was resumed when bowel sounds were audible and patients could tolerate clear fluids without nausea or vomiting. Ambulation was initiated once vital signs were stable, oxygen saturation (SpO₂) was ≥ 94% on room air, and pain intensity was ≤ 3 on the VAS scale with assistance. Chest drainage was removed when the daily drainage volume was less than 200 mL of serous fluid and no air leak had been observed for at least 12 h. All clinical endpoints were recorded by trained personnel blinded to group allocation, and data were verified prior to database lock.

Secondary outcomes included intraoperative surgical field quality (evaluated using a five-point scale), perioperative physiological parameters—such as heart rate (HR) [6], mean arterial pressure (MAP), SpO₂, and end-tidal carbon dioxide (PETCO₂)—and arterial blood gas indices, including pH, arterial oxygen partial pressure (PaO₂), arterial carbon dioxide partial pressure (PaCO₂), and arterial oxygen saturation (SaO₂). All physiological and blood gas measurements were recorded at seven predefined time points: prior to anesthesia induction (T1), three minutes after airway device placement (T2), at the start of surgery (T3), at the midpoint of surgery (T4), at the end of surgery (T5), 30 min after extubation or removal of the laryngeal mask (T6), and two hours postoperatively (T7).

Demographic and baseline clinical variables were collected, including age, sex, BMI, smoking and alcohol history, ASA classification, comorbidities hypertension, diabetes, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), and coronary artery disease, as well as preoperative pulmonary function parameters, including forced expiratory volume in one second (FEV₁) and the FEV₁/FVC ratio.

Inflammatory and economic outcomes, and safety evaluation

Peripheral venous blood samples were collected at four time points—preoperatively and at 6, 12, and 24 h postoperatively—for the measurement of inflammatory cytokines, including interleukin-6 (IL-6), interleukin-1β (IL-1β), and tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α). These biomarkers were quantified using commercially available enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) kits according to the manufacturer’s instructions.

Additional recorded variables included total operation time, anesthesia-related expenses, total hospitalization costs, and 30-day readmission rates.

Postoperative adverse events including atelectasis, hypoxemia, pulmonary infection, sore throat, hoarseness, nausea, vomiting, and coughing episodes were documented for all participants. The occurrence of these complications was monitored during hospitalization and follow-up.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics version 23.0. Continuous variables were presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD) and assessed for normality using the Shapiro–Wilk test. For normally distributed data, one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was used to compare means among the three groups; otherwise, the Kruskal–Wallis test was applied. Categorical variables were expressed as counts and percentages and compared using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate. For repeated measurements such as visual analog scale (VAS) scores, hemodynamic data, blood gas values, and cytokine levels, repeated-measures ANOVA was employed to assess changes over time and between groups. A p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All graphs and figures were generated using GraphPad Prism version 8.0.2. A senior biostatistician, independent of the study team reviewed the sample size calculation and statistical analysis plan prior to unblinding and verified the final analyses.

Result

Baseline characteristics

There were no statistically significant differences in demographic characteristics, comorbidities, or pulmonary function parameters among the three groups prior to intervention (Table 1). The DLETT, LMA + INB, and LMA + PVB groups showed no significant differences in age (p = 0.154), sex distribution (male/female, p = 0.354), or BMI (p = 0.084). Similarly, smoking status (p = 0.764) and alcohol consumption (p = 0.922) were comparable across the groups. The distribution of ASA physical status classification was also similar among the three groups (p = 0.757), with most patients classified as ASA II. The prevalence of common comorbidities—including hypertension, diabetes mellitus, COPD, and coronary artery disease—did not differ significantly between groups (all p > 0.05). In addition, preoperative pulmonary function indicators, including FEV₁ and the FEV₁/FVC ratio, were comparable among the groups (p = 0.282 and 0.660, respectively).

Impact of tubeless anesthesia on postoperative recovery under ERAS protocol

Postoperative recovery outcomes differed significantly among the three groups, as summarized in Table 2. The length of hospital stay was significantly reduced in both LMA groups compared with the DLETT group (p < 0.05). Specifically, patients in the DLETT group had the longest average hospital stay (8.05 ± 2.76 days), whereas those in the LMA + INB and LMA + PVB groups had shorter stays of 6.43 ± 1.60 days and 6.28 ± 1.60 days, respectively. Although the time to first oral intake did not significantly differ among the groups (p = 0.153), the LMA + PVB group exhibited a numerically shorter mean time (325.08 ± 32.27 min) compared to the LMA + INB and DLETT groups. Time to first ambulation showed significant intergroup differences (p < 0.001), with the LMA + PVB group achieving the earliest mobilization (394.35 ± 32.39 min), followed by the LMA + INB group (422.75 ± 39.85 min), and the DLETT group (469.75 ± 38.39 min). These findings indicate that tubeless anesthesia, especially when combined with paravertebral block, facilitates earlier postoperative recovery in terms of ambulation and discharge readiness.

Effect of tubeless anesthesia on postoperative pain in patients undergoing thoracoscopic wedge resection of the lung

Postoperative pain scores varied over time and across the three anesthetic strategies. As illustrated in Fig. 1A, under resting conditions, pain peaked at 6 h postoperatively in all groups and gradually declined over time. The LMA + PVB group consistently reported the lowest VAS scores at each time point (2 h, 6 h, 12 h, 24 h), with significant reductions compared to the DLETT group at 6 h, 12 h, and 24 h (p < 0.01). Compared to LMA + INB, the LMA + PVB group also showed statistically significant improvements at 6 h and 24 h (p < 0.01). The DLETT group showed the highest pain scores, peaking at 6 h, and although the values declined by 24 h, they remained elevated relative to the other two groups.In the coughing state (Fig. 1B), all patients experienced higher VAS scores compared to the resting state, as expected due to mechanical strain during coughing. The LMA + PVB group again showed superior pain control, with VAS scores significantly lower than both the DLETT and LMA + INB groups at 6 h, 12 h, and 24 h (p < 0.01). The LMA + INB group provided moderate analgesic benefits, with significantly better outcomes than DLETT at 12 h and 24 h (p < 0.01), but still inferior to the LMA + PVB group.

Effect of tubeless anesthesia on blood gas in patients undergoing thoracoscopic wedge resection of lung

Arterial blood gas indices were monitored at seven perioperative time points (T1–T7) to assess respiratory and metabolic stability across the three anesthetic strategies.

As shown in Fig. 3A, arterial pH levels remained within the normal physiological range (7.35–7.45) for all groups throughout the perioperative period. A mild transient drop in pH was observed around T4 (mid-surgery), most notably in the LMA + INB group, but there were no statistically significant differences among groups at any time point. pH values recovered to baseline levels by T6–T7, suggesting stable metabolic compensation.

Perioperative arterial blood gas changes. A Arterial pH levels at perioperative time points (T1–T7). B Arterial partial pressure of oxygen across time. C Arterial partial pressure of carbon dioxide. D. Arterial oxygen saturation

Figure 3B presents the PaO₂ trends. All groups exhibited a postoperative rise in PaO₂ after anesthesia induction, peaking at T3–T4, followed by a gradual return to baseline by T7. The DLETT group showed slightly higher PaO₂values throughout, though differences among groups were not statistically significant. These results indicate adequate oxygenation was maintained across all anesthetic approaches.

In Fig. 3C, PaCO₂values revealed more notable group differences. Both LMA groups showed transient intraoperative elevations in PaCO₂, peaking at T4–T5. The LMA + INB group displayed significantly higher PaCO₂than the DLETT group at T5 (p < 0.01), and higher than the LMA + PVB group at the same time point (p < 0.01), indicating a temporary CO₂retention possibly related to reduced ventilatory control under spontaneous or assisted breathing. However, PaCO₂values normalized by T6 and remained within acceptable limits throughout the period.

As illustrated in Fig. 3D, SaO₂remained above 90% in all groups, indicating effective oxygenation. A transient drop in SaO₂ was observed at T4, particularly in the LMA + INB group, which was significantly lower than the DLETT group at this time point (p < 0.01). The LMA + PVB group maintained relatively stable SaO₂, suggesting better ventilation-perfusion matching and less respiratory fluctuation.

Effect of tubeless anesthesia on the levels of peripheral blood inflammatory factors in patients undergoing thoracoscopic wedge resection of the lung

At baseline, there were no significant differences in IL-6 levels among the three groups (p = 0.998). At 6 h postoperatively, IL-6 levels increased significantly in all groups, with no significant intergroup differences (p = 0.510). However, at 24 h postoperatively, both the LMA + INB group and LMA + PVB group had significantly lower IL-6 levels compared to the DLETT group. IL-6 levels in the LMA + PVB group were also significantly lower than in the LMA + INB group (p < 0.01).

A similar trend was observed for IL-1β. No significant differences were observed at baseline or at 6 h postoperatively (p = 0.410 and p = 0.880, respectively). At 24 h, IL-1β levels were significantly lower in the LMA + INB and LMA + PVB groups compared to the DLETT group (p = 0.003), with the LMA + PVB group showing a further significant reduction relative to LMA + INB (p < 0.01).

TNF-α levels increased postoperatively in all groups, peaking at 6 h. Although the LMA + INB and LMA + PVB groups showed numerically lower TNF-α concentrations at 6 and 24 h than the DLETT group, the differences were not statistically significant (p = 0.316 and p = 0.270, respectively)(Table 3).

Effect of tubeless anesthesia on cost, postoperative adverse reactions and surgical field satisfaction in patients undergoing thoracoscopic wedge resection of the lung

The DLETT group had the highest surgical field satisfaction score, which was significantly higher than that in the LMA + INB group and the LMA + PVB group (p < 0.001). Despite this, both LMA groups provided acceptable visibility, without reports of compromised operative success or conversions.

The mean operation time was significantly shorter in the DLETT group (145.55 ± 6.61 min) compared to the LMA + PVB group (152.43 ± 7.87 min) (p < 0.05), although no significant difference was observed between the DLETT and LMA + INB groups.

Anesthesia-related costs were significantly lower in the DLETT group compared to the LMA + INB and LMA + PVB groups (p < 0.001). However, the DLETT group had significantly higher total hospitalization expenses than both the LMA + INB and LMA + PVB groups (p < 0.001).

Postoperative complications also varied significantly. The incidence of sore throat and hoarseness was significantly higher in the DLETT group (37.5% and 45.0%, respectively) than in the LMA + INB (5.0% and 7.5%) and LMA + PVB (7.0% and 5.0%) groups (p < 0.05 for both). Nausea and vomiting were more frequent in the LMA + INB group (30.0%) compared to the DLETT group (25.0%) and the LMA + PVB group (5.0%) (p < 0.05). The incidence in the DLETT group was also significantly higher than that in the LMA + PVB group (p < 0.05). No significant differences were found in rates of atelectasis, hypoxemia, lung infection, or choking (p > 0.05).

The 30-day readmission rate did not differ significantly among the three groups (p = 0.697)(Table 4).

Discussion

This prospective randomized controlled trial compared three anesthetic strategies—DLETT, LMA + INB, and LMA + PVB—within the framework of ERAS protocols for patients undergoing VATS wedge resection. Our findings demonstrate that tubeless anesthesia, particularly LMA + PVB, significantly improved key recovery indicators, including shorter hospital stay, earlier ambulation, better pain control, and a reduced inflammatory response. Although DLETT offered superior surgical field visibility, it was associated with higher rates of throat discomfort, prolonged recovery, and increased hospitalization costs.

Patients in the LMA groups, particularly those receiving LMA + PVB, demonstrated faster postoperative recovery under ERAS conditions. While both LMA + INB and LMA + PVB strategies shortened ambulation and discharge times compared with DLETT, the LMA + PVB group consistently showed additional advantages. These included lower VAS pain scores at rest and during coughing (particularly at 6 and 24 h), more stable intraoperative hemodynamics, better oxygenation indices, lower postoperative inflammatory cytokines (IL-6 and IL-1β), and a significantly reduced incidence of nausea and vomiting. The shortened ambulation and discharge times in these groups align with the growing literature advocating regional anesthesia to enhance ERAS implementation in thoracic surgery. Zhang et al.[11]. (2021) reported similar benefits of PVB in minimally invasive pulmonary resections, citing faster mobilization and reduced opioid consumption. In our study, both LMA groups achieved significantly lower VAS scores at rest and during coughing from 6 to 24 h ours postoperatively, with LMA + PVB providing the most effective analgesia. This finding is consistent with the results of Guerra et al.[12], who emphasized the analgesic advantages of PVB over systemic opioids or intercostal nerve blocks in thoracic procedures. The use of LMA avoids airway irritation caused by endotracheal intubation, while the addition of PVB provides focused, long-acting analgesia that minimizes nociceptive input during and after surgery[13]. Perioperative physiological stability further supports the use of tubeless anesthesia. HR and MAP were more stable in the LMA groups, with lower peaks observed at induction (T2) and at the start of surgery (T3) compared with DLETT. This likely reflects reduced sympathetic stimulation in the absence of tracheal instrumentation and with the analgesic effect of regional blocks[14, 15]. Liu et al.[16]. (2017) similarly demonstrated that combining LMA with PVB stabilized intraoperative hemodynamics and reduced vasopressor requirements. Our blood gas data exhibited that although PaCO₂ increased mildly in the LMA groups during one-lung ventilation, oxygenation parameters (SpO₂, PaO₂, and SaO₂) remained within acceptable clinical limits across all groups, confirming the respiratory safety of the LMA strategy when appropriately managed. Inflammatory markers further highlighted the advantages of LMA + PVB. At 24 h post-op, IL-6 and IL-1β levels were significantly lower in the LMA groups, suggesting a milder systemic inflammatory response. This likely reflects the synergistic anti-inflammatory effects of avoiding intubation and combined with regional analgesia[17]. These findings are supported by Trépanier et al.[18]. (2020), who observed lower IL-6 levels with PVB compared with general anesthesia alone. TNF-α levels, although elevated postoperatively, showed no significant differences among groups, possibly due to interindividual variation and early peaking kinetics not captured at the measured time points. Despite its advantages in surgical exposure, DLETT was associated with the highest rates of postoperative sore throat (37.5%) and hoarseness (45.0%), consistent with known complications of tracheal intubation. In contrast, LMA + PVB minimized upper airway trauma and significantly reduced these symptoms[19]. Furthermore, although anesthesia costs were higher in the LMA groups due to the use of ultrasound and local anesthetics, the total hospitalization costs were significantly lower—particularly in the LMA + PVB group—owing to shorter hospital stays and fewer complications. These economic findings reinforce the cost-effectiveness of adopting LMA + PVB as a standard component of ERAS in selected VATS procedures.

This study contributes several novel insights. It is among the first prospective studies to systematically compare DLETT, LMA + INB, and LMA + PVB across a multidimensional set of ERAS indicators, including recovery time, analgesia, hemodynamics, inflammation, complications, and cost. The results strongly support integrating LMA + PVB into ERAS pathways for VATS wedge resection[20, 21]. In addition, our standardized anesthesia and surgical protocols help reduce bias and enhance the clinical applicability of the findings.

However, this study has certain limitations. Being single-center with a modest sample size may limit generalizability. Subjective assessment of surgical field quality could introduce evaluator bias, though this was mitigated by blinding surgeons to group allocation. Longer-term outcomes such as pulmonary function recovery, chronic pain, or quality of life were not assessed. In addition, randomization was conducted using a computer-generated random number table with sealed-envelope allocation rather than a centralized web-based randomization system, which might potentially introduce allocation bias. Future studies should address these gaps through multicenter trials, incorporate longer follow-up, and explore variations in regional techniques (e.g., continuous PVB or multimodal blocks) to further optimize perioperative care in thoracic surgery.

Conclusion

This prospective randomized controlled study demonstrated that tubeless anesthesia, particularly LMA combined with PVB, provided superior analgesia, physiological stability, and anti-inflammatory effects compared with both DLETT and LMA + INB. Overall, LMA + PVB significantly improved postoperative recovery in patients undergoing VATS wedge resection, supporting its incorporation into ERAS protocols for thoracic surgery.

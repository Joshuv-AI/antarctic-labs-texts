Quasi-twilled associative algebras, deformation maps and their governing algebras
Apurba Das, Ramkrishna Mandal

Abstract
. A quasi-twilled associative algebra is an associative algebra 𝔸 \mathbb{A} whose underlying vector space has a decomposition 𝔸 = A ⊕ B \mathbb{A}=A\oplus B such that B ⊂ 𝔸 B\subset\mathbb{A} is a subalgebra. In the first part of this paper, we give the Maurer-Cartan characterization and introduce the cohomology of a quasi-twilled associative algebra. In a quasi-twilled associative algebra 𝔸 \mathbb{A} , a linear map D : A → B D:A\rightarrow B is called a strong deformation map if Gr ⁡ ( D ) ⊂ 𝔸 \mathrm{Gr}(D)\subset\mathbb{A} is a subalgebra. Such a map generalizes associative algebra homomorphisms, derivations, crossed homomorphisms and the associative analogue of modified r -matrices. We introduce the cohomology of a strong deformation map D D unifying the cohomologies of all the operators mentioned above. We also define the governing algebra for the pair ( 𝔸 , D ) (\mathbb{A},D) to study simultaneous deformations of both 𝔸 \mathbb{A} and D D . On the other hand, a linear map r : B → A r:B\rightarrow A is called a weak deformation map if Gr ⁡ ( r ) ⊂ 𝔸 \mathrm{Gr}(r)\subset\mathbb{A} is a subalgebra. Such a map generalizes relative Rota-Baxter operators of any weight, twisted Rota-Baxter operators, Reynolds operators, left-averaging operators and right-averaging operators. Here we define the cohomology and governing algebra of a weak deformation map r r (that unify the cohomologies of all the operators mentioned above) and also for the pair ( 𝔸 , r ) (\mathbb{A},r) that govern simultaneous deformations.



2020 MSC classification: 16D20, 16E40, 16S80, 16W25, 17B38.

Keywords: Quasi-twilled associative algebras, Deformation maps, Crossed homomorphisms, Rota-Baxter operators, Controlling algebras, Cohomology.

Introduction

The cohomology of an algebraic structure is an important invariant that
determine extensions and formal deformations of the structure. Hochschild
[
27
]
first discovered the cohomology of associative algebras in the study of extension problems. Later, Gerstenhaber
[
23
]
developed formal deformations of an associative algebra
A
and showed that such deformations are closely related to the Hochschild cohomology groups of
A

[
37

generalized the formal deformation theory to Lie algebras. After that, many authors developed the cohomology and deformation theory of various other algebraic structures over the years. In particular, Balavoine
[
8
]
generalized the theories to algebras over binary quadratic operads. See also
[
35
]
for cohomology and deformations of algebras over more general operads.

Algebraic structures are often characterized by certain operators defined on them. Among others, algebra homomorphisms, derivations and crossed homomorphisms are the most popular. Another interesting operator that appears in the solutions of the modified associative Yang-Baxter equation is given by the (associative analogue of) modified
r

[

On the other hand, to study the splitting of algebras and duplications of algebra operations, one encounters some operators defined on them, e.g. Rota-Baxter operators (of weight

[

]
, Reynolds operators
[

]
, left averaging operators
[

]
and right averaging operators
[
9

A
with the multiplication
\cdot

algebra homomorphism

\displaystyle D(a\cdot b)=D(a)\cdot D(b),

derivation

\displaystyle D(a\cdot b)=D(a)\cdot b+a\cdot D(b),

crossed homomorphism

\displaystyle D(a\cdot b)=D(a)\cdot b+a\cdot D(b)+D(a)\cdot D(b),

modified associative

\displaystyle D(a)\cdot D(b)=D(D(a)\cdot b+a\cdot D(b))-a\cdot b,

\displaystyle r(a)\cdot r(b)=r(r(a)\cdot b+a\cdot r(b)),

Rota-Baxter operator of weight

\displaystyle r(a)\cdot r(b)=r(r(a)\cdot b),

\displaystyle r(a)\cdot r(b)=r(a\cdot r(b)).

Our main aim in this paper is to find a unified approach to study all the operators mentioned above. To do so, we emphasise that all the operators listed above can be described by their graphs which are subalgebras in some bigger algebras. To understand these bigger algebras in full generality, we consider the notion of a quasi-twilled associative algebra
[
42
]
(also called an
extending datum
in
[
3
]
). This is a quasi-analogue of a matched pair of associative algebras
[
1
]
and the associative counterpart of a quasi-twilled Lie algebra
[
28
]
. Implicitly, a quasi-twilled associative algebra is an associative algebra
\mathbb{A}
whose underlying vector space has a direct sum decomposition
\mathbb{A}=A\oplus B
such that
B
is a subalgebra of
\mathbb{A}
(see Theorem
3.2
for the explicit description). Examples of quasi-twilled associative algebras include the direct product of two associative algebras, semi-direct products, semi-direct products twisted by Hochschild
2
-cocycles and of course matched pairs of associative algebras. In the first part of this paper, we give the Maurer-Cartan characterization of a quasi-twilled associative algebra
\mathbb{A}
and as an application, we define the cohomology of
\mathbb{A}
. In particular, we get the Maurer-Cartan characterization and cohomology of a matched pair of associative algebras. To our knowledge, this cohomology was even unknown in the literature.

In a matched pair of associative algebras, Agore
[
1
]
introduced the notion of a “deformation map” while studying the classifying compliments problem (CCP). Since a quasi-twilled associative algebra
\mathbb{A}=A\oplus B
is not symmetric with respect to
A
and
B
, we obtain two variants of the deformation map in a quasi-twilled associative algebra
\mathbb{A}
.

A linear map
D:A\rightarrow B
is said to be a
strong deformation map
if its graph
\mathrm{Gr}(D)\subset\mathbb{A}
is a subalgebra. Such a map unifies algebra homomorphisms, derivations, crossed homomorphisms and the associative modified
r
-matrices. A strong deformation map
D:A\rightarrow B
induces an associative algebra structure on
A
(denoted by
A_{D}
). Moreover, the pair
(A_{D},B)
forms a matched pair of associative algebras. As a result, one obtains an
A_{D}
-bimodule structure on the vector space
B
. We define the corresponding Hochschild cohomology of
A_{D}
with coefficients in
B
as the cohomology of the strong deformation map
D
. This cohomology generalizes the existing cohomologies of algebra homomorphisms
[
24
]
, derivations
[
34
]
and crossed homomorphisms
[
14
]
. Next, given a quasi-twilled associative algebra
\mathbb{A}
, we characterize strong deformation maps by Maurer-Cartan elements in a suitable curved
L_{\infty}
-algebra, called the
controlling algebra
for strong deformation maps. Twisting the controlling algebra by a Maurer-Cartan element (i.e. a strong deformation map
D
), we obtain the
L_{\infty}
-algebra that governs the deformations of the operator
D
keeping the underlying quasi-twilled associative algebra intact. Next, we proceed as follows to understand the simultaneous deformations of
\mathbb{A}
and the operator
D
. For any two vector spaces
A
and
B
(not necessarily equipped with any additional structures), we first construct an
L_{\infty}
-algebra whose Maurer-Cartan elements correspond to pairs
(\mathbb{A},D)
, where
\mathbb{A}=A\oplus B
has a quasi-twilled associative algebra structure and
D:A\rightarrow B
is a strong deformation map in
\mathbb{A}
. Again, twisting the
L_{\infty}
-algebra by a Maurer-Cartan element
(\mathbb{A},D)
, one gets the governing
L_{\infty}
-algebra of the pair
(\mathbb{A},D)
of a quasi-twilled associative algebra
\mathbb{A}
and a strong deformation map
D
in it. This higher structure governs the simultaneous deformations of
\mathbb{A}
and
D
.

On the other hand, a linear map
r:B\rightarrow A
in a quasi-twilled associative algebra
\mathbb{A}
is said to be a
weak deformation map
if its graph
\mathrm{Gr}(r)\subset\mathbb{A}
is a subalgebra. It unifies (relative) Rota-Baxter operators of weight
0
, (relative) Rota-Baxter operators of weight
1
, twisted Rota-Baxter operators, Reynolds operators, left averaging operators and right averaging operators. We observe that a weak deformation map
r
induces a new quasi-twilled associative algebra structure
\mathbb{A}_{r}
on the same direct sum
A\oplus B
. However, this induced structure
\mathbb{A}_{r}
need not form a matched pair of associative algebras. (For this reason, we call
r
a weak deformation map and
D
a strong deformation map.) Note that, the quasi-twilled associative structure
\mathbb{A}_{r}
gives rise to a new associative algebra structure on
B
(denoted by
B_{r}
) and a
B_{r}
-bimodule structure on
A
. We call the corresponding Hochschild cohomology of
B_{r}
with coefficients in
A
as the cohomology of the weak deformation map
r
. As expected, our cohomology generalizes the existing cohomologies of (relative) Rota-Baxter operators of weight
0

[
10
]
, (relative) Rota-Baxter operators of weight
1

[
13
]
, twisted Rota-Baxter operators
[
12
]
, Reynolds operators
[
12
]
, left averaging operators
[
44
]
and right averaging operators
[
44
]
. Given a quasi-twilled associative algebra
\mathbb{A}
, we also construct the
controlling algebra
(which is an
L_{\infty}
-algebra) for weak deformation maps. As before, twisting this controlling algebra by a Maurer-Cartan element (i.e. a weak deformation map
r
), we get the
L_{\infty}
-algebra that governs the deformations of the operator
r
. In a particular case, we obtain the controlling algebra for “deformation maps” in a matched pair of associative algebras introduced by Agore
[
1
]
. In the end, we also construct the governing
L_{\infty}
-algebra for a pair
(\mathbb{A},r)
of a quasi-twilled associative algebra
\mathbb{A}
and a weak deformation map
r
to study the simultaneous deformations. This structure generalizes the existing governing

-algebras for Rota-Baxter algebras
[
17
,
45
]
and left (resp. right) averaging algebras
[

,

]
.

In
[
4
]
the authors have introduced non-abelian

with values in another associative algebra

while studying non-abelian extensions. In the final part of this paper, we observe that a non-abelian

-cocycle and a new algebraic structure, called

. We show that any Rota-Baxter operator twisted by a non-abelian

-cocycle induces a twisted tridendriform algebra and conversely, any twisted tridendriform algebra arises in this way. This unifies the facts that any Rota-Baxter operator of weight

]

It is important to remark that the authors of
[
28
]
have recently studied deformation maps in a quasi-twilled Lie algebra. They considered two types of deformation maps (type I and type II) generalizing deformation maps in a matched pair of Lie algebras
[
2

. Among others, they mainly studied cohomology and deformations of these maps. Motivated by the work of
[

However, we call them strong deformation maps and weak deformation maps respectively (instead of type I and type II deformation maps) depending on whether the induced quasi-twilled associative structure on
\mathbb{A}
gives rise to a matched pair of associative algebras or not. In addition to the cohomology and deformations of these maps, we extensively study quasi-twilled associative algebras and simultaneous deformations of a quasi-twilled associative algebra with a given (strong or weak) deformation map. Moreover, we discuss Rota-Baxter operators twisted by non-abelian
2

2.
Some background on (curved) L-infinity-algebras

This section recalls (curved)
L_{\infty}
-algebras and their Maurer-Cartan elements. In particular, we recall Voronov’s construction of a (curved)
L_{\infty}
-algebra whose multi-ary operations are given by derived brackets. Our main references are
[
25
,
29
,
30
,
32
,
43
]
.

2.1 Definition
.

A
curved
L_{\infty}
-algebra
is a pair
(\mathcal{G},\{l_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty})
consisting of a graded vector space
\mathcal{G}=\displaystyle\oplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}~\mathcal{G}_{n}
equipped with a collection
\{l_{k}:{\mathcal{G}}^{\otimes k}\rightarrow\mathcal{G}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
of graded linear maps of degree
1
satisfying the following properties:

(i)

for each
k\geq 1
, the map
l_{k}
is graded symmetric in the sense that

{l_{k}}(x_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,x_{\sigma(k)})=\epsilon(\sigma)~{l_{k}}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})
, for all
x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\in\mathcal{G}
and
\sigma\in S_{k}
,

(ii)

for any
N\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum^{N}_{i=0}~\sum_{\sigma\in Sh(i,n-i)}\epsilon(\sigma)~l_{n-i+1}\big(l_{i}(x_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,x_{\sigma(i)}),x_{\sigma(i+1)},\ldots,x_{\sigma(N)}\big)=0,

(1)

for all homogeneous elements
x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}\in\mathcal{G}
.

In the above definition,
\epsilon(\sigma)
is the
Koszul sign
that appears in the graded context. Also, note that
l_{0}
represents a degree 1 element of
\mathcal{G}
. When
l_{0}=0
, a curved
L_{\infty}
-algebra becomes an
L_{\infty}
-algebra.

A curved
L_{\infty}
-algebra
(\mathcal{G},\{l_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty})
is said to be
weakly filtered
if there exists a descending filtration
{\mathcal{F}}_{\bullet}\mathcal{G}
of
\mathcal{G}
(that is,
\mathcal{G}={\mathcal{F}}_{1}\mathcal{G}\supset{\mathcal{F}}_{2}\mathcal{G}\supset\cdots\supset{\mathcal{F}}_{k}\mathcal{G}\supset\cdots
) and a natural number
N
(called an index) such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}=\lim_{k\to\infty}{\mathcal{G}/{\mathcal{F}}_{k}\mathcal{G}}~~\text{ and }~~l_{k}(\mathcal{G},\ldots,\mathcal{G})\subset{\mathcal{F}}_{k}\mathcal{G},\text{ for all }k\geq N.

In the rest of this paper, we assume that all (curved)
L_{\infty}
-algebras are weakly filtered so that all formal sums under consideration are convergent.

Let
(\mathcal{G},\{l_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty})
be a curved
L_{\infty}
-algebra. A degree 0 element
\alpha\in\mathcal{G}_{0}
is said to be a
Maurer-Cartan element
of the curved
L_{\infty}
-algebra if it satisfies the following Maurer-Cartan equation

\displaystyle l_{0}+\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{1}{k!}~l_{k}(\alpha,\ldots,\alpha)=0.

2.2 Theorem
.

Let
\alpha
be a Maurer-Cartan element of the curved
L_{\infty}
-algebra
(\mathcal{G},\{l_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty})
. For each
k\geq 1
, we define a degree 1 graded linear map
{l^{\alpha}_{k}}:{\mathcal{G}}^{\otimes k}\rightarrow\mathcal{G}
by

\displaystyle{l^{\alpha}_{k}}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}):=\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{1}{n!}~l_{n+k}(\alpha,\ldots,\alpha,x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}),\text{ for }x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\in\mathcal{G}.

Then
(\mathcal{G},\{l_{k}^{\alpha}\}_{k=1}^{\infty})
is an
L_{\infty}
-algebra.

The
L_{\infty}
-algebra
(\mathcal{G},\{l_{k}^{\alpha}\}_{k=1}^{\infty})
constructed in the above theorem is said to be obtained from the curved
L_{\infty}
-algebra
(\mathcal{G},\{l_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty})
twisted by the Maurer-Cartan element
\alpha
.

In
[
43
]
Voronov gave a construction of curved
L_{\infty}
-algebras which we shall recall now. We first need the following notion considered by Voronov.

2.3 Definition
.

A
curved
V
-data
is a quadruple
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{a},P,\Delta)
consisting of a graded Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[~,~])
, an abelian graded Lie subalgebra
\mathfrak{a}\subset\mathfrak{g}
, a projection map
P:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{a}\subset\mathfrak{g}
such that
\mathrm{ker}(P)\subset\mathfrak{g}
is a graded Lie subalgebra and an element
\Delta\in\mathfrak{g}_{1}
that satisfies
[\Delta,\Delta]=0
.

In a curved
V
-data
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{a},P,\Delta)
, if
\Delta\in\mathrm{ker}{(P)}_{1}
then we simly call it a
V
-data.

2.4 Theorem
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{a},P,\Delta)
be a curved
V
-data.

(i)

Then
(\mathfrak{a},\{l_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty})
is a curved
L_{\infty}
-algebra, where

\displaystyle l_{0}=P(\Delta)\quad\text{ and }\quad l_{k}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k})=P[\cdots[[\Delta,a_{1}],a_{2}],\ldots,a_{k}],\text{ for }a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}\in\mathfrak{a}.

(ii)

If
\mathfrak{h}\subset\mathfrak{g}
is a graded Lie subalgebra so that
[\Delta,\mathfrak{h}]\subset\mathfrak{h}
, then the graded vector space
\mathfrak{h}[1]\oplus\mathfrak{a}
can also be equipped with a curved
L_{\infty}
-algebra structure whose structural operations
\{\widetilde{l}_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
are given by

\displaystyle\widetilde{l}_{0}=~

\displaystyle P(\Delta),

\displaystyle\widetilde{l}_{1}(x[1])=~

\displaystyle[\Delta,x][1]+P(x),

\displaystyle\widetilde{l}_{2}(x[1],y[1])=~

\displaystyle(-1)^{\lvert x\rvert}[x,y][1],

\displaystyle\widetilde{l}_{k}\big(x[1],a_{1},\ldots,a_{k-1}\big)=~

\displaystyle P~[\cdots[[x,a_{1}],a_{2}],\ldots,a_{k-1}],~~k\geq 2,

\displaystyle\widetilde{l}_{k}\big(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}\big)=~

\displaystyle P~[\cdots[[\Delta,a_{1}],a_{2}],\ldots,a_{k}],~~k\geq 1,

for homogeneous elements
x,y\in\mathfrak{h}
(considered as elements
x[1],y[1]\in\mathfrak{h}[1]
with a degree shift) and homogeneous elements
a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}\in\mathfrak{a}
. Up to the permutations of the above entries,
all the other linear maps vanish. Moreover, we have
\mathfrak{a}\subset\mathfrak{h}[1]\oplus\mathfrak{a}
is a curved
L_{\infty}
-subalgebra.

3.
Quasi-twilled associative algebras

In this section, we consider quasi-twilled associative algebras
[
42
,
3
]
which are the quasi-analogue of matched pairs of associative algebras. We give the Maurer-Cartan characterization of quasi-twilled associative algebras and as a result, we define their cohomology theory.

3.1 Definition
.

[
42
,
3
]

A
quasi-twilled associative algebra
is an associative algebra
(\mathbb{A},\cdot_{\mathbb{A}})
whose underlying vector space
\mathbb{A}
has a direct sum decomposition
\mathbb{A}=A\oplus B
such that
B
is a subalgebra of
(\mathbb{A},\cdot_{\mathbb{A}})
.

3.2 Theorem
.

A quasi-twilled associative algebra is equivalent to a tuple
(A_{\mu},B_{\nu},\vartriangleright,\vartriangleleft,\rightharpoonup,\leftharpoonup,\theta)
in which

•

A
is a vector space endowed with a bilinear map
\mu:A\times A\rightarrow A,~(a,b)\mapsto a\cdot_{A}b
,

•

B
is a vector space endowed with a bilinear associative multiplication
\nu:B\times B\rightarrow B,~(x,y)\mapsto x\cdot_{B}y
,

and

\displaystyle\vartriangleright~:A\times B\rightarrow B,\quad\vartriangleleft~:B\times A\rightarrow B,\quad\rightharpoonup~:B\times A\rightarrow A,\quad\leftharpoonup~:A\times B\rightarrow A,\quad\theta:A\times A\rightarrow B

are bilinear maps such that
(A,\rightharpoonup,\leftharpoonup)
is a bimodule over the associative algebra
B_{\nu}
and for all
a,b,c\in A
,
x,y\in B
the following compatibility conditions are hold:

\displaystyle a\cdot_{A}(b\cdot_{A}c)+a\leftharpoonup\theta(b,c)=

\displaystyle~(a\cdot_{A}b)\cdot_{A}c+\theta(a,b)\rightharpoonup c,

(2)

\displaystyle(a\cdot_{A}b)\vartriangleright x+\theta(a,b)\cdot_{B}x=

\displaystyle~a\vartriangleright(b\vartriangleright x)+\theta(a,b\leftharpoonup x),

(3)

\displaystyle x\vartriangleleft(a\cdot_{A}b)+x\cdot_{B}\theta(a,b)=

\displaystyle~(x\vartriangleleft a)\vartriangleleft b+\theta(x\rightharpoonup a,b),

(4)

\displaystyle a\vartriangleright(x\vartriangleleft b)+\theta(a,x\rightharpoonup b)=

\displaystyle~(a\vartriangleright x)\vartriangleleft b+\theta(a\leftharpoonup x,b),

(5)

\displaystyle a\vartriangleright(x\cdot_{B}y)=

\displaystyle~(a\vartriangleright x)\cdot_{B}y+(a\leftharpoonup x)\vartriangleright y,

(6)

\displaystyle(x\cdot_{B}y)\vartriangleleft a=

\displaystyle~x\cdot_{B}(y\vartriangleleft a)+x\vartriangleleft(y\rightharpoonup a),

(7)

\displaystyle x\vartriangleleft(a\leftharpoonup y)+x\cdot_{B}(a\vartriangleright y)=

\displaystyle~(x\rightharpoonup a)\vartriangleright y+(x\vartriangleleft a)\cdot_{B}y,

(8)

\displaystyle x\rightharpoonup(a\cdot_{A}b)=

\displaystyle~(x\rightharpoonup a)\cdot_{A}b+(x\vartriangleleft a)\rightharpoonup b,

(9)

\displaystyle(a\cdot_{A}b)\leftharpoonup x=

\displaystyle~a\cdot_{A}(b\leftharpoonup x)+a\leftharpoonup(b\vartriangleright x),

(10)

\displaystyle a\leftharpoonup(x\vartriangleleft b)+a\cdot_{A}(x\rightharpoonup b)=

\displaystyle~(a\vartriangleright x)\rightharpoonup b+(a\leftharpoonup x)\cdot_{A}b,

(11)

\displaystyle\theta(a,b\cdot_{A}c)+a\vartriangleright\theta(b,c)=

\displaystyle~\theta(a\cdot_{A}b,c)+\theta(a,b)\vartriangleleft c.

(12)

Proof.

Let
(\mathbb{A},\cdot_{\mathbb{A}})
be a quasi-twilled associative algebra with a decomposition
\mathbb{A}=A\oplus B
. Since
B
is a subalgebra of
(\mathbb{A},\cdot_{\mathbb{A}})
, it follows that the multiplication
~\cdot_{\mathbb{A}}~
is of the form

4.7 Proposition

Let

is a subalgebra of the associative algebra

Proof.

Let

and

be two arbitary elements in

\displaystyle(a,D(a))\cdot_{\mathbb{A}}(b,D(b))=\big(a\cdot_{A}b+a\leftharpoonup D(b)+D(a)\rightharpoonup b,~D(a)\cdot_{B}D(b)+a\vartriangleright D(b)+D(a)\vartriangleleft b+\theta(a,b)\big).

This is in

if and only if the identity (
15

∎

5.
Controlling algebras for strong deformation maps and simultaneous deformations

Given a quasi-twilled associative algebra
\mathbb{A}
, we first construct a curved
L_{\infty}
-algebra whose Maurer-Cartan elements are precisely strong deformation maps in
\mathbb{A}
. This curved
L_{\infty}
-algebra is the controlling algebra for strong deformation maps in
\mathbb{A}
. Next, for a (fixed) strong deformation map
D
, we obtain the
L_{\infty}
-algebra that governs the linear deformations of the operator
D
keeping the underlying quasi-twilled associative algebra
\mathbb{A}
intact. Finally, we also construct the governing
L_{\infty}
-algebra for studying the simultaneous deformations of both
\mathbb{A}
and
D
.

5.1.
Controlling algebras for strong deformation maps

Let
(\mathbb{A},\cdot_{\mathbb{A}})
be a quasi-twilled associative algebra that is equivalent to the tuple
(A_{\mu},B_{\nu},\vartriangleright,\vartriangleleft,\rightharpoonup,\leftharpoonup,\theta)
. We first consider the graded Lie algebra with the Gerstenhaber bracket

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}=\big(\oplus_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathrm{Hom}\big((A\oplus B)^{\otimes n+1},A\oplus B\big),[~,~]\big)

on the space of all multilinear maps on the vector space
A\oplus B
. Then it is easy to see that the graded subspace
\mathfrak{a}=\oplus_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathrm{Hom}(A^{\otimes n+1},B)
is an abelian graded Lie subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
. Let
P:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{a}
be the projection map onto the subspace
\mathfrak{a}
. Then
\mathrm{ker}(P)_{n}=C^{n\lvert 0}\oplus C^{n-1\lvert 1}\oplus\cdots\oplus C^{1\lvert n-1}\oplus C^{0\lvert n}\oplus C^{-1\lvert n+1}
, for any
n\geq 0
. Hence
\mathrm{ker}(P)=\oplus_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathrm{ker}(P)_{n}
is a graded Lie subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
(by Proposition
3.13
).
Let

\displaystyle\Omega=\widetilde{\theta}+\mu_{{}_{\vartriangleright,\vartriangleleft}}+\nu_{{}_{\rightharpoonup,\leftharpoonup}}

be the Maurer-Cartan element corresponding to the given quasi-twilled associative algebra. Note that
P(\Omega)=\theta
. Therefore, we obtain a curved
V
-data
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{a},P,\Omega)
. As a result, we get the following curved
L_{\infty}
-algebra.

5.1 Theorem
.

Let
(A_{\mu},B_{\nu},\vartriangleright,\vartriangleleft,\rightharpoonup,\leftharpoonup,\theta)
be a quasi-twilled associative algebra. Then the pair

\displaystyle\big(\mathfrak{a}=\oplus_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathrm{Hom}(A^{\otimes n+1},B),\{l_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}\big)

is a curved
L_{\infty}
-algebra, where for
f,g\in\mathfrak{a}
,

\displaystyle l_{0}=~

\displaystyle\theta,

\displaystyle l_{1}(f)=~

\displaystyle[\mu_{{}_{\vartriangleright,\vartriangleleft}},f],

\displaystyle l_{2}(f,g)=~

\displaystyle[[\nu_{{}_{\rightharpoonup,\leftharpoonup}},f],g],

\displaystyle l_{k}=~

\displaystyle 0,~~\mathrm{for~}k\geq 3.

Moreover, a linear map
D:A\rightarrow B
is a strong deformation map in the quasi-twilled associative algebra
(A_{\mu},B_{\nu},\vartriangleright,\vartriangleleft,\rightharpoonup,\leftharpoonup,\theta)
if and only if
D\in\mathfrak{a}_{0}=\mathrm{Hom}(A,B)
is a Maurer-Cartan element of the above-defined curved
L_{\infty}
-algebra
(\mathfrak{a},\{l_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty})

Proof.

Since
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{a},P,\Omega)
is a curved
V
-data, it follows from Theorem
2.4
(i) that
(\mathfrak{a},\{l_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty})
is a curved
L_{\infty}
-algebra, where

\displaystyle l_{0}=~

\displaystyle P(\Omega)=\theta,

\displaystyle l_{1}(f)=~

\displaystyle P[\Omega,f]=[\mu_{{}_{\vartriangleright,\vartriangleleft}},f],

\displaystyle.l_{2}(f,g)=~

\displaystyle P[[\Omega,f],g]=[[\nu_{{}_{\rightharpoonup,\leftharpoonup}},f],g].

Moreover, using the bidegree reason, one can easily check that
[[\Omega,f],g]\in\mathfrak{a}
, for any
f,g\in\mathfrak{a}
. Hence for any (other)
h\in\mathfrak{a}
, we have

\displaystyle[[[\Omega,f],g],h]=0

(19)

as
\mathfrak{a}
is an abelian Lie subalgebra. This shows that

l_{k}(f_{1},f_{2},\ldots,f_{k})=P[\cdots[[\Omega,f_{1}],f_{2}],\ldots,f_{k}]=0,
for any

and

Next, for any linear map

\displaystyle\big(l_{0}+\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{1}{k!}~l_{k}(D,\ldots,D)\big)(a,b)

\displaystyle=\big(\theta+l_{1}(D)+\frac{1}{2}l_{2}(D,D)\big)(a,b)

\displaystyle=\theta(a,b)+[\mu_{{}_{\vartriangleright,\vartriangleleft}},D](a,b)+\frac{1}{2}[[\nu_{{}_{\rightharpoonup,\leftharpoonup}},D],D](a,b)

\displaystyle=\theta(a,b)+a\vartriangleright D(b)+D(a)\vartriangleleft b-D(a\cdot_{A}b)+D(a)\cdot_{B}D(b)-D\big(a\leftharpoonup D(b)\big)-D\big(D(a)\rightharpoonup b\big),

for all

. This shows that
l_{0}+\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{1}{k!}~l_{k}(D,\ldots,D)=0
if and only if

is a strong deformation map.
∎

The curved
L_{\infty}
-algebra
\big(\mathfrak{a}=\oplus_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathrm{Hom}(A^{\otimes n+1},B),\{l_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}\big)
is called the

for strong deformation maps. It is important to remark that this controlling algebra generalizes the controlling algebras for algebra homomorphisms
[
24
]
, derivations
[
34
]
and crossed homomorphism
[
14
]
. As a new application, if we consider the quasi-twilled associative algebra
(A\oplus A,\Box)
given in Example

-matrices.

5.2 Proposition
.

Let

be an associative algebra. Then
(\oplus_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathrm{Hom}(A^{\otimes n+1},A),\{l_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty})

\displaystyle l_{2}(f,g)

5.3 Theorem
.

Let
(A_{\mu},B_{\nu},\vartriangleright,\vartriangleleft,\rightharpoonup,\leftharpoonup,\theta)
be a quasi-twilled associative algebra and
D:A\rightarrow B
be a fixed strong deformation map. Then
\big(\mathfrak{a}=\oplus_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathrm{Hom}(A^{\otimes n+1},B),\{l_{k}^{D}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\big)
is an
L_{\infty}
-algebra, where for
f,g\in\mathfrak{a},

\displaystyle l_{1}^{D}(f)=~

\displaystyle l_{1}(f)+l_{2}(D,f),

(20)

\displaystyle l_{2}^{D}(f,g)=~

\displaystyle l_{2}(f,g),

(21)

\displaystyle l_{k}^{D}=~

\displaystyle 0,~~\mathrm{for~}k\geq 3.

(22)

Moreover, for any linear map
D^{\prime}:A\rightarrow B
, the sum
D+D^{\prime}
is also a strong deformation map if and only if
D^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{a}_{0}
is a Maurer-Cartan element of the
L_{\infty}
-algebra
(\mathfrak{a},\{l_{k}^{D}\}_{k=1}^{\infty})
given above.

Proof.

Since
D:A\rightarrow B
is a strong deformation map, it follows from the previous theorem that
D
is a Maurer-Cartan element of the curved
L_{\infty}
-algebra
(\mathfrak{a},\{l_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty})
. Hence by applying Theorem
2.2
in the present context, we get that
(\mathfrak{a},\{l_{k}^{D}\}_{k=1}^{\infty})
is an
L_{\infty}
-algebra, where the structure maps
\{l_{k}^{D}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
are given by (
20
)-(
22
).

For the second part, we observe that

\displaystyle l_{0}+l_{1}(D+D^{\prime})+\frac{1}{2}l_{2}(D+D^{\prime},D+D^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\underbrace{l_{0}+l_{1}(D)+\frac{1}{2}l_{2}(D,D)}_{=0}+\big\{l_{1}(D^{\prime})+l_{2}(D,D^{\prime})+\frac{1}{2}l_{2}(D^{\prime},D^{\prime})\big\}

\displaystyle=l_{1}^{D}(D^{\prime})+\frac{1}{2}l_{2}^{D}(D^{\prime},D^{\prime}).

This shows that
D+D^{\prime}
is a Maurer-Cartan element of the curved
L_{\infty}
-algebra
(\mathfrak{a},\{l_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty})
if and only if
D^{\prime}
is a Maurer-Cartan element of the
L_{\infty}
-algebra
(\mathfrak{a},\{l_{k}^{D}\}_{k=1}^{\infty})
. Hence the result follows.
∎

5.4 Remark
.

Since the
L_{\infty}
-algebra
\big(\mathfrak{a}=\oplus_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathrm{Hom}(A^{\otimes n+1},B),\{l_{k}^{D}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\big)
constructed in the preceding theorem governs the linear deformations of

of the map

.

Let
D:A\rightarrow B
be a strong deformation map in the quasi-twilled associative algebra. Then we have seen in Theorem

that
(\mathfrak{a}=\oplus_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathrm{Hom}(A^{\otimes n+1},B),\{l_{k}^{D}\}_{k=1}^{\infty})
is an

is a differential. Moreover, we have the following.

5.5 Proposition
.

For any

, we have

\displaystyle l_{1}^{D}(f)=(-1)^{n-1}~\!\delta^{D}(f).

Proof.

For any

, we have

\displaystyle[\nu_{{}_{\rightharpoonup,\leftharpoonup}},D]((a,x),(b,y))=~\big(a\leftharpoonup D(b)+D(a)\rightharpoonup b,D(a)\cdot_{B}y+x\cdot_{B}D(b)-D(a\leftharpoonup y)-D(x\rightharpoonup b)\big).

This shows that
\mu_{{}_{\vartriangleright,\vartriangleleft}}+[\nu_{{}_{\rightharpoonup,\leftharpoonup}},D]=~\mu_{D}+\vartriangleright_{D}+\vartriangleleft_{D}
(using the notations from (
16
), (
17
), (

)). Hence, we have

\displaystyle l_{1}^{D}(f)=~

\displaystyle l_{1}(f)+l_{2}(D,f)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle[\mu_{{}_{\vartriangleright,\vartriangleleft}},f]+[[\nu_{{}_{\rightharpoonup,\leftharpoonup}},D],f]

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle[\mu_{{}_{\vartriangleright,\vartriangleleft}}+[\nu_{{}_{\rightharpoonup,\leftharpoonup}},D],f]

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle[\mu_{D}+\vartriangleright_{D}+\vartriangleleft_{D},f]

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle(-1)^{n-1}~\!\delta^{D}(f).

Hence the proof.
∎

5.2.

Here we first construct an
L_{\infty}

, where
\mathbb{A}=A\oplus B
has a quasi-twilled associative algebra structure and

is a strong deformation map in
\mathbb{A}
. Then we study the governing algebra and deformations of such a pair

Let
A

\mathfrak{g}=\big(\oplus_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathrm{Hom}\big((A\oplus B)^{\otimes n+1},A\oplus B\big),[~,~]\big)

\mathfrak{a}=\oplus_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathrm{Hom}(A^{\otimes n+1},B)

(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{a},P,\Delta=0)
is a

-data. Thus by applying Theorem
2.4
(ii) to the graded Lie subalgebra
\mathscr{Q}\subset\mathfrak{g}

5.6 Theorem

Let
A
and

(\mathscr{Q}[1]\oplus\mathfrak{a},\{l_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty})
is an

\displaystyle\widetilde{l}_{1}(F[1],f)=~

\displaystyle P(F),

\displaystyle\widetilde{l}_{2}(F[1],G[1])=~

\displaystyle(-1)^{\lvert F\rvert}[F,G][1],

\displaystyle\widetilde{l}_{k}\big(F[1],f_{1},\ldots,f_{k-1}\big)=~

\displaystyle P[\cdots[[F,f_{1}],f_{2}],\ldots,f_{k-1}],~~k\geq 2,

Next, suppose there are bilinear maps

\displaystyle\mu:A\times A\rightarrow A,\qquad\nu:B\times B\rightarrow B,

\displaystyle\vartriangleright~:A\times B\rightarrow B,\quad\vartriangleleft~:B\times A

5.7 Theorem

With the above notations,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{1}{k!}~\widetilde{l}_{k}\big((\Omega[1],D),\ldots,(\Omega[1],D)\big)

(A_{\mu},B_{\nu},\vartriangleright,\vartriangleleft,\rightharpoonup,\leftharpoonup,\theta)
is a quasi-twilled associative algebra. Moreover,
\theta+l_{1}(D)+\frac{1}{2}l_{2}(D,D)=0
if and only if
D:A\rightarrow B
is a strong deformation map in the above quasi-twilled associative algebra (cf. Theorem
5.1
). This proves the result.
∎

Let
(\mathbb{A},D)
be a pair of a quasi-twilled associative algebra
\mathbb{A}=(\mathbb{A},\cdot_{\mathbb{A}})
with a fixed decomposition
\mathbb{A}=A\oplus B
and a strong deformation map
D
in it. Then we have seen in Theorem
5.7
that
\alpha=(\Omega[1],D)
is a Maurer-Cartan element of the
L_{\infty}
-algebra
(\mathscr{Q}[1]\oplus\mathfrak{a},\{\widetilde{l}_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty})
, where
\Omega\in\mathscr{Q}_{1}
represents the given quasi-twilled associative algebra structure on
\mathbb{A}
. Hence, we obtain the following by applying Theorem
2.2
.

5.8 Theorem
.

Let
(\mathbb{A},D)
be a pair of a quasi-twilled associative algebra
\mathbb{A}
with a fixed decomposition
\mathbb{A}=A\oplus B
and a strong deformation map
D
in it. Then the pair
\big(\mathscr{Q}[1]\oplus\mathfrak{a},\{\widetilde{l}_{k}^{(\Omega[1],D)}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\big)
is an
L_{\infty}
-algebra, where

\displaystyle\widetilde{l}_{k}^{(\Omega[1],D)}\big((F_{1}[1],f_{1}),\ldots,(F_{k}[1],f_{k})\big):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}~\!\widetilde{l}_{n+k}\big(\underbrace{(\Omega[1],D),\ldots,(\Omega[1],D)}_{n\mathrm{~times}},(F_{1}[1],f_{1}),\ldots,(F_{k}[1],f_{k})\big),

for
k\geq 1
and homogeneous elements
(F_{1}[1],f_{1}),\ldots,(F_{k}[1],f_{k})\in\mathscr{Q}[1]\oplus\mathfrak{a}
. Moreover, for any other element
\Omega^{\prime}\in\mathscr{Q}_{1}
and a linear map
D^{\prime}:A\rightarrow B
, the sum
\Omega+\Omega^{\prime}\in\mathscr{Q}_{1}
represents a new quasi-twilled associative algebra structure on
\mathbb{A}
and
D+D^{\prime}:A\rightarrow B
is a strong deformation map in this quasi-twilled associative structure if and only if
(\Omega^{\prime}[1],D^{\prime})\in(\mathscr{Q}[1]\oplus\mathfrak{a})_{0}
is a Maurer-Cartan element of
\big(\mathscr{Q}[1]\oplus\mathfrak{a},\{\widetilde{l}_{k}^{(\Omega[1],D)}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\big)
.

The above theorem says that the
L_{\infty}
-algebra
\big(\mathscr{Q}[1]\oplus\mathfrak{a},\{\widetilde{l}_{k}^{(\Omega[1],D)}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\big)
governs the simultaneous deformations of the given quasi-twilled associative algebra structure on
\mathbb{A}
and the strong deformation map
D
. For this reason, we call this
L_{\infty}
-algebra as the
governing algebra
for the pair
(\mathbb{A},D)
.

6.
Weak deformation maps in quasi-twilled associative algrbras

This section introduces weak deformation maps in a quasi-twilled associative algebra. We observe that such maps unify various operators like (relative) Rota-Baxter operators of weight
0
, (relative) Rota-Baxter operators of weight
1
, twisted Rota-Baxter operators, Reynolds operators, left averaging operators and right averaging operators. Then we define the cohomology of a weak deformation map generalizing the existing cohomologies of all the operators mentioned above.

Let
(\mathbb{A},\cdot_{\mathbb{A}})
be a quasi-twilled associative algebra. We assume that this quasi-twilled associative algebra is equivalent to the tuple
(A_{\mu},B_{\nu},\vartriangleright,\vartriangleleft,\rightharpoonup,\leftharpoonup,\theta)
.

6.1 Definition
.

A
weak deformation map
in the quasi-twilled associative algebra
(\mathbb{A},\cdot_{\mathbb{A}})
is a linear map
r:B\rightarrow A
that satisfies

\displaystyle r(x)\cdot_{A}r(y)+r(x)\leftharpoonup y+x\rightharpoonup r(y)=~r\big(x\cdot_{B}y+r(x)\vartriangleright y+x\vartriangleleft r(y)+\theta(r(x),r(y))\big),~~\mathrm{for~all~}x,y\in B.

(23)

Let
r:B\rightarrow A
be a weak deformation map. If
r
is invertible then
r^{-1}:A\rightarrow B
is a strong deformation map in the sense of Definition
4.1
. Conversely, the inverse (when invertible) of a strong deformation map is a weak deformation map.

6.2 Example
.

Let
A
and
B
be two associative algebras. Consider the quasi-twilled associative algebra
(A\oplus B,\odot)
given in Example
3.3
. A linear map
r:B\rightarrow A
is a weak deformation map in
(A\oplus B,\odot)
if and only if
r
is an
algebra homomorphism
, i.e.

\displaystyle r(x\cdot_{B}y)=r(x)\cdot_{A}r(y),~~\mathrm{for~all~}x,y\in B.

6.3 Example
.

Let
A
be an associative algebra and
(B,\vartriangleright,\vartriangleleft)
be an
A
-bimodule. Consider the quasi-twilled associative algebra
(A\oplus B,\ltimes)
given in Example
3.4
. A linear map
r:B\rightarrow A
is a weak deformation map in
(A\oplus B,\ltimes)
if and only if
r
is a
relative Rota-Baxter operator of weight
0
, i.e.

\displaystyle r(x)\cdot_{A}r(y)=r\big(r(x)\vartriangleright y+x\vartriangleleft r(y)\big),~~\mathrm{for~all~}x,y\in B.

6.4 Example
.

Let
A
be an associative algebra and
B
be an associative algebra object in the category of
A
-bimodules. Consider the quasi-twilled associative algebra
(A\oplus B,\odot_{\ltimes})
given in Example
3.5
. In this case, a linear map
r:B\rightarrow A
is a weak deformation map in
(A\oplus B,\odot_{\ltimes})
if and only if
r
is a
relative Rota-Baxter operator of weight
1
, i.e.

\displaystyle r(x)\cdot_{A}r(y)=r\big(x\cdot_{B}y+r(x)\vartriangleright y+x\vartriangleleft r(y)\big),~~\mathrm{for~all~}x,y\in B.

6.5 Example
.

Let
A
be an associative algebra and
(B,\vartriangleright)
be a left
A
-module. Here we consider the quasi-twilled associative algebra
(A\oplus B,\cdot_{\vartriangleright})
given in Example
3.6
. Then a linear map
r:B\rightarrow A
is a weak deformation map in
(A\oplus B,\cdot_{\vartriangleright})

if and only if
r
is a
relative left averaging operator
, i.e.

\displaystyle r(x)\cdot_{A}r(y)=r\big(r(x)\vartriangleright y\big),~~\mathrm{for~all~}x,y\in B.

6.6 Example
.

Let
A
be an associative algebra and
(B,\vartriangleleft)
be a right
A
-module. Next, we consider the quasi-twilled associative algebra
(A\oplus B,\cdot_{\vartriangleleft})
given in Example
3.7
. A linear map
r:B\rightarrow A
is a weak deformation map in
(A\oplus B,\cdot_{\vartriangleleft})
if and only if
r
is a
relative right averaging operator
, i.e.

\displaystyle r(x)\cdot_{A}r(y)=r\big(x\vartriangleleft r(y)\big),~~\mathrm{for~all~}x,y\in B.

6.7 Example
.

Let
A
be an associative algebra,
(B,\vartriangleright,\vartriangleleft)
be an
A
-bimodule and
\theta\in Z^{2}_{\mathrm{Hoch}}(A,B)
be a Hochschild 2-cocycle. A linear map

is a weak deformation map in the quasi-twilled associative algebra
(A\oplus B,\ltimes_{\theta})
given in Example

if and only if

is a
\theta
-twisted Rota-Baxter operator
, i.e.

\displaystyle r(x)\cdot_{A}r(y)=r\big(r(x)\vartriangleright y+x\vartriangleleft r(y)+\theta(r(x),r(y))\big),~~\mathrm{for~all~}x,y\in B.

6.8 Example
.

Let
A
be an associative algebra. Consider the quasi-twilled associative algebra
(A\oplus A,\ltimes_{-\mu})
given in Example
3.11
. A linear map

is a weak deformation map in

\displaystyle r(a)\cdot_{A}r(b)=r\big(r(a)\cdot_{A}b+a\cdot_{A}r(b)-r(a)\cdot_{A}r(b)\big),~~\mathrm{for~all~}a,b\in A.

6.9 Example
.

Let
(A,B,\vartriangleright,\vartriangleleft,\rightharpoonup,\leftharpoonup)
be a matched pair of associative algebras and consider the quasi-twilled associative algebra

given in Example

is a weak deformation map in

if and only if

\displaystyle r(x)\cdot_{A}r(y)+r(x)\leftharpoonup y+x\rightharpoonup r(y)=~r\big(x\cdot_{B}y+r(x)\vartriangleright y+x\vartriangleleft r(y)\big),~~\mathrm{for~all~}x,y\in B.

(24)

In other words,

is a deformation map
[

in the given matched pair of associative algebras.

The next result gives a characterization of weak deformation maps in a quasi-twilled associative algebra.

6.10 Proposition
.

Let
(\mathbb{A},\cdot_{\mathbb{A}})
be a quasi-twilled associative algebra. A linear map
r:B\rightarrow A
is a weak deformation map if and only if its graph
\mathrm{Gr}(r)=\{(r(x),x)\lvert~x\in B\}
is a subalgebra of the associative algebra
(\mathbb{A},\cdot_{\mathbb{A}})
.

Proof.

Let
(r(x),x)
and
(r(y),y)
be two arbitary elements in
\mathrm{Gr}(r)
. Then it follows from (
13
) that

\displaystyle(r(x),x)\cdot_{\mathbb{A}}(r(y),y)=\big(r(x)\cdot_{A}r(y)+r(x)\leftharpoonup y+x\rightharpoonup r(y),~x\cdot_{B}y+r(x)\vartriangleright y+x\vartriangleleft r(y)+\theta\big(r(x),r(y)\big)\big).

This is in
\mathrm{Gr}(r)
if and only if the identity (
23
) holds. This proves the desired result.
∎

As a consequence of the above proposition, we get the following.

6.11 Proposition
.

Let
(\mathbb{A},\cdot_{\mathbb{A}})
be a quasi-twilled associative algebra and
r:B\rightarrow A
be a weak deformation map in it. Then the vector space

is equipped with the new multiplication

\displaystyle\nu_{r}:B\times B\rightarrow B,~(x,y)\mapsto x\cdot_{r}y:=x\cdot_{B}y+r(x)\vartriangleright y+x\vartriangleleft r(y)+\theta(r(x),r(y)),~~\mathrm{for~}x,y\in B

is an associative algebra, denoted by
B_{r}
.

deforms the structure into a new quasi-twilled associative algebra. In terms of components, we have the following.

7.

7.1.

Let
(\mathbb{A},\cdot_{\mathbb{A}})

is a Maurer-Cartan element of the above

Proof.

Since

\displaystyle l_{k}(f_{1},f_{2},\ldots,f_{k})=P[\cdots[[\Omega,f_{1}],f_{2}],\ldots,f_{k}],\text{ for }k\geq 1.

Note that, for any homogeneous elements

\displaystyle l_{1}(f)=~

\displaystyle P[\Omega,f]=[\nu_{{}_{\rightharpoonup,\leftharpoonup}},f],

\displaystyle l_{2}(f,g)=~

\displaystyle P[[\Omega,f],g]=[[\mu_{{}_{\vartriangleright,\vartriangleleft}},f],~g],

\displaystyle l_{3}(f,g,h)=~

\displaystyle P[[[\Omega,f],g],h]=[[[\widetilde{\theta},f],g],h].

Moreover, for any

, one can show that
[[[\Omega,f],g],h]\in\mathfrak{b}
. Since

is an abelian Lie subalgebra, it follows that

\displaystyle l_{k}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k})=P[\cdots[[\Omega,f_{1}],f_{2}],\ldots,f_{k}]=0,

for

This proves the first part.

Finally, for any linear map

\displaystyle\big(\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{1}{k!}~l_{k}(r,\ldots,r)\big)(x,y)

\displaystyle=l_{1}(r)(x,y)+\frac{1}{2}l_{2}(r,r)(x,y)+\frac{1}{6}l_{3}(r,r,r)(x,y)

\displaystyle=[\nu_{{}_{\rightharpoonup,\leftharpoonup}},r](x,y)+\frac{1}{2}[[\mu_{{}_{\vartriangleright,\vartriangleleft}},r],r](x,y)+\frac{1}{6}[[[\widetilde{\theta},r],r],r](x,y)

\displaystyle=-r(x\cdot_{B}y)+r(x)\leftharpoonup y+x\rightharpoonup r(y)+r(x)\cdot_{A}r(y)-r\big(r(x)\vartriangleright y+x\vartriangleleft r(y)\big)-r\big(\theta\big(r(x),r(y)\big)\big).

This shows that

L_{\infty}
-algebra
(\mathfrak{b},\{l_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty})
if and only if

is a weak deformation map.
∎

for weak deformation maps in the given quasi-twilled associative algebra. This generalizes the already existing controlling algebras for Rota-Baxter operators of weight

7.2.
Simultaneous deformations of a quasi-twilled associative algebra and a weak deformation map

In this subsection, we study the governing algebra and deformations of the pair

consisting of a quasi-twilled associative algebra

and a fixed weak deformation map

.

Let
A
and
B
be two vector spaces and

\mathfrak{g}=\big(\oplus_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathrm{Hom}\big((A\oplus B)^{\otimes n+1},A\oplus B\big),[~,~]\big)

Therefore, by applying Theorem
2.4
(ii) to the graded Lie subalgebra

7.5 Theorem
.

Let
A
and
B
be two vector spaces. Then
(\mathscr{Q}[1]\oplus\mathfrak{b},\{\widetilde{l}_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty})

\displaystyle\widetilde{l}_{2}(F[1],G[1])=~

\displaystyle(-1)^{\lvert F\rvert}[F,G][1],

\displaystyle\mu:A\times A\rightarrow A,\qquad\nu:B\times B\rightarrow B,

\displaystyle\vartriangleright~:A\times B\rightarrow B,\quad\vartriangleleft~:B\times A

.

With the above notations, the tuple

[[[\Omega,r],r],r]\in\mathfrak{b}

\displaystyle[[[[\Omega,r],r],r],r]=~0

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{1}{k!}~\widetilde{l}_{k}\big((\Omega[1],r),\ldots,(\Omega[1],r)\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2!}\big\{\widetilde{l}_{2}(\Omega[1],\Omega[1])+2~\!\widetilde{l}_{2}(\Omega[1],r)\big\}+\frac{1}{3!}\big\{3~\!\widetilde{l}_{3}(\Omega[1],r,r)\big\}+\frac{1}{4!}\big\{4~\!\widetilde{l}_{4}(\Omega[1],r,r,r)\big\}

8.
Rota-Baxter operators twisted by non-abelian 2-cocycles and twisted tridendriform algebras

In this section, we first consider Rota-Baxter operators twisted by non-abelian
2
-cocycles. Then we introduce a new algebraic structure, called “twisted tridendriform algebra”. We show that a Rota-Baxter operator twisted by some non-abelian
2
-cocycle induces a twisted tridendriform algebra structure and conversely, any twisted tridendriform algebra arises in this way.

Let
A
and
B
be two associative algebras. Recall that
[
4
]
a
non-abelian
2
-cocycle
on
A
with values in
B
is a triple
(\vartriangleright,\vartriangleleft,\theta)
consisting of bilinear maps
\vartriangleright~:A\times B\rightarrow B,~~\vartriangleleft~:B\times A\rightarrow B
and
\theta:A\times A\rightarrow B
such that the following conditions are hold:

\displaystyle(a\cdot_{A}b)\vartriangleright x+\theta(a,b)\cdot_{B}x=~

\displaystyle a\vartriangleright(b\vartriangleright x),

(39)

\displaystyle(a\vartriangleright x)\vartriangleleft b=~

\displaystyle a\vartriangleright(x\vartriangleleft b),

(40)

\displaystyle(x\vartriangleleft a)\vartriangleleft b=~

\displaystyle x\vartriangleleft(a\cdot_{A}b)+x\cdot_{B}\theta(a,b),

(41)

\displaystyle(a\vartriangleright x)\cdot_{B}y=~

\displaystyle a\vartriangleright(x\cdot_{B}y),

(42)

\displaystyle(x\vartriangleleft a)\cdot_{B}y=~

\displaystyle x\cdot_{B}(a\vartriangleright y),

(43)

\displaystyle(x\cdot_{B}y)\vartriangleleft a=~

\displaystyle x\cdot_{B}(y\vartriangleleft a),

(44)

\displaystyle\theta(a,b)\vartriangleleft c+\theta(a\cdot_{A}b,c)=~

\displaystyle a\vartriangleright\theta(b,c)+\theta(a,b\cdot_{A}c),

(45)

for any
a,b,c\in A
and
x,y\in B
.
Non-abelian 2-cocycles defined above are closely related to non-abelian extensions of the associative algebra
A
by the algebra
B
.

8.1 Remark
.

[
4
]

Let
A
and
B
be two associative algebras and
(\vartriangleright,\vartriangleleft,\theta)
be a non-abelian 2-cocycle on
A
with values in
B
. Then the direct sum
A\oplus B
can be given an associative algebra structure with the multiplication

\displaystyle(a,x)\boxtimes(b,y)=(a\cdot_{A}b,~x\cdot_{B}y+a\vartriangleright y+x\vartriangleleft b+\theta(a,b)),

for
(a,x),(b,y)\in A\oplus B
.
Then
(A\oplus B,\boxtimes)
is a quasi-twilled associative algebra.

8.2 Definition
.

Let
A,B
be two associative algebras and
(\vartriangleright,\vartriangleleft,\theta)
be a non-abelian
2
-cocycle on
A
with values in
B
. A linear map
r:B\rightarrow A
is said to be a
(\vartriangleright,\vartriangleleft,\theta)
-twisted Rota-Baxter operator
if it satisfies

\displaystyle r(x)\cdot_{A}r(y)=r\big(r(x)\vartriangleright y+x\vartriangleleft r(y)+x\cdot_{B}y+\theta(r(x),r(y))\big),\text{ for }x,y\in B.

(46)

It follows that a
(\vartriangleright,\vartriangleleft,\theta)
-twisted Rota-Baxter operator is nothing but a weak deformation map in the quasi-twilled associative algebra
(A\oplus B,\boxtimes)
given above.

8.3 Definition
.

A
twisted tridendriform algebra
is a tuple
(\mathcal{A},\prec,\succ,\curlyvee,\cdot)
consisting of a vector space
\mathcal{A}
endowed with four bilinear maps
\prec,\succ,\curlyvee,\cdot:\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
such that for any
x,y,z\in\mathcal{A}
, the following set of identities are hold:

\displaystyle(x\prec y)\prec z=~

\displaystyle x\prec(y\ast z)+x\cdot(y\curlyvee z),

(TT1)

\displaystyle(x\succ y)\prec z=~

\displaystyle x\succ(y\prec z),

(TT2)

\displaystyle(x\star y)\succ z+(x\curlyvee y)\cdot z=~

\displaystyle x\succ(y\succ z),

(TT3)

\displaystyle(x\succ y)\cdot z=~

\displaystyle x\succ(y\cdot z),

(TT4)

\displaystyle(x\prec y)\cdot z=~

\displaystyle x\cdot(y\succ z),

(TT5)

\displaystyle(x\cdot y)\prec z=~

\displaystyle x\cdot(y\prec z),

(TT6)

\displaystyle(x\cdot y)\cdot z=~

\displaystyle x\cdot(y\cdot z),

(TT7)

\displaystyle(x\curlyvee y)\prec z+(x\star y)\curlyvee z=~

\displaystyle x\succ(y\curlyvee z)+x\curlyvee(y\star z),

(TT8)

where
x\star y:=x\prec y+x\succ y+x\curlyvee y+x\cdot y
, for any
x,y\in\mathcal{A}.

8.4 Remark
.

(i)

Let
(\mathcal{A},\prec,\succ,\curlyvee,\cdot)
be a twisted tridendriform algebra. Then it follows from (
TT7
) that
(\mathcal{A},\cdot)
is an associative algebra.

(ii)

In a twisted tridendriform algebra
(\mathcal{A},\prec,\succ,\curlyvee,\cdot)
, if
\curlyvee
is trivial then
(\mathcal{A},\prec,\succ,\cdot)
is a tridendriform algebra considered by Loday and Ronco
[
33
]
. If
\cdot~\!
is trivial then
(\mathcal{A},\prec,\succ,\curlyvee)
is simply an NS-algebra considered by Uchino
[
41
]
. Finally, if
\curlyvee
and
\cdot
both are trivial then
(\mathcal{A},\prec,\succ)
becomes a dendriform algebra
[
31
]
.

In the following, we show that Rota-Baxter operators twisted by non-abelian
2
-cocycles induce twisted tridendriform structures.

8.5 Proposition
.

Let
A
and
B
be two associative algebras and
(\vartriangleright,\vartriangleleft,\theta)
be a non-abelian
2
-cocycle on
A
with values in
B
. Let
r:B\rightarrow A
be a
(\vartriangleright,\vartriangleleft,\theta)
-twisted Rota-Baxter operator. Then
(B,\prec_{r},\succ_{r},\curlyvee_{r},\cdot_{B})
is a twisted tridendriform algebra, where

\displaystyle x\prec_{r}y:=~x\vartriangleleft r(y),\quad x\succ_{r}y:=~r(x)\vartriangleright y\quad\mathrm{and}\quad x\curlyvee_{r}y:=~\theta(r(x),r(y)),~~\mathrm{for~}x,y\in B.

Proof.

Let
x,y,z\in B
. Then we have

\displaystyle(x\prec_{r}y)\prec_{r}z=(x\vartriangleleft r(y))\vartriangleleft r(z)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{nab3})}}{{=}}~

\displaystyle x\vartriangleleft\big(r(y)\cdot_{A}r(z)\big)+x\cdot_{B}\theta(r(y),r(z))

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle x\prec_{r}(y\star z)+x\cdot_{B}(y\curlyvee_{r}z)\quad(\text{by}~(\ref{rbt-nab})),

where
x\star y=x\prec_{r}y+x\succ_{r}y+x\curlyvee_{r}y+x\cdot_{B}y
, for any
x,y\in B.

This verifies the identity (
TT1
). Similarly, we have

\displaystyle(x\succ_{r}y)\prec_{r}z=(r(x)\vartriangleright y)\vartriangleleft r(z)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{nab2})}}{{=}}~

\displaystyle r(x)\vartriangleright(y\vartriangleleft r(z))=x\succ_{r}(y\prec_{r}z),

\displaystyle(x\star y)\succ_{r}z+(x\curlyvee_{r}y)\cdot_{B}z=~

\displaystyle r(x\star y)\vartriangleright z+\theta(r(x),r(y))\cdot_{B}z

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle(r(x)\cdot_{A}r(y))\vartriangleright z+\theta(r(x),r(y))\cdot_{B}z\quad(\text{by}~(\ref{rbt-nab}))

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{nab1})}}{{=}}~

\displaystyle r(x)\vartriangleright(r(y)\vartriangleright z)=x\succ_{r}(y\succ_{r}z),

\displaystyle(x\succ_{r}y)\cdot_{B}z=(r(x)\vartriangleright y)\cdot_{B}z\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{nab4})}}{{=}}~

\displaystyle r(x)\vartriangleright(y\cdot_{B}z)=x\succ_{r}(y\cdot_{B}z),

\displaystyle(x\prec_{r}y)\cdot_{B}z=(x\vartriangleleft r(y))\cdot_{B}z\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{nab5})}}{{=}}~

\displaystyle x\cdot_{B}(r(y)\vartriangleright z)=x\cdot_{B}(y\succ_{r}z),

\displaystyle(x\cdot_{B}y)\prec_{r}z=(x\cdot_{B}y)\vartriangleleft r(z)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{nab6})}}{{=}}~

\displaystyle x\cdot_{B}(y\vartriangleleft r(z))=x\cdot_{B}(y\prec_{r}z)

which proves (
TT2
)-(
TT6
). The identity (
TT7
) also follows as
(B,\cdot_{B})
is an associative algebra. Finally,

\displaystyle(x\curlyvee_{r}y)\prec_{r}z+(x\star y)\curlyvee_{r}z=~

\displaystyle\theta(r(x),r(y))\vartriangleleft r(z)+\theta(r(x)\cdot_{A}r(y),r(z))

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{nab7})}}{{=}}~

\displaystyle r(x)\vartriangleright\theta(r(y),r(z))+\theta(r(x),r(y)\cdot_{A}r(z))

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle x\succ_{r}(y\curlyvee_{r}z)+x\curlyvee_{r}(y\star z)

which verifies (
TT8
). Hence
(B,\prec_{r},\succ_{r},\curlyvee_{r},\cdot_{B})
is a twisted tridendriform algebra.
∎

In
[
19
]
K. Ebrahimi-Fard showed that a relative Rota-Baxter operator of weight
1
induces a tridendriform algebra structure. On the other hand, Uchino
[
41
]
showed that a twisted Rota-Baxter operator induces an NS-algebra structure. It is important to remark that Proposition
8.5
unifies both of these results.

In the following, we prove the converse of Proposition
8.5
. More precisely, we show that any twisted tridendriform algebra is always induced by a Rota-Baxter operator twisted by some non-abelian 2-cocycle.

8.6 Theorem
.

Let
(\mathcal{A},\prec,\succ,\curlyvee,\cdot)
be a twisted tridendriform algebra.

(i)

Then
(\mathcal{A},\star)
is an associative algebra, where

\displaystyle x\star y:=~x\prec y+x\succ y+x\curlyvee y+x\cdot y,~~\mathrm{for~}x,y\in\mathcal{A}.

(ii)

We define bilinear maps
\vartriangleright,\vartriangleleft,\theta:\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
by

\displaystyle x\vartriangleright y:=x\succ y,\quad x\vartriangleleft y:=x\prec y~~~~\text{ and }~~~~\theta(x,y):=x\curlyvee y,\text{ for }x,y\in\mathcal{A}.

Then
(\vartriangleright,\vartriangleleft,\theta)
is a non-abelian
2
-cocycle on the associative algebra
(\mathcal{A},{\star})
with values in the algebra
(\mathcal{A},{\cdot})
.

(iii)

The identity map
\mathrm{Id}:(\mathcal{A},\cdot)\rightarrow(\mathcal{A},\star)
is a
(\vartriangleright,\vartriangleleft,\theta)
-twisted Rota-Baxter operator. Moreover, the induced twisted
tridendriform algebra structure on
(\mathcal{A},{\cdot})
coincides with the given one.

Proof.

(i) Since
(\mathcal{A},\prec,\succ,\curlyvee,\cdot)
is a twisted tridendriform algebra, the identities (
TT1
)-(
TT8
) hold. Adding the left-hand sides of these identities, one obtains
(x\star y)\star z
. On the other hand, by adding the right-hand sides of the same identities, one gets
x\ast(y\ast z)
. Hence the result follows.

(ii) It is straightforward to see that the seven identities (listed in (
39
)-(
45
)) of a non-abelian
2
-cocycle are respectively equivalent to the identities (
TT3
), (
TT2
), (
TT1
), (
TT4
), (
TT5
), (
TT6
) and (
TT8
).

(iii) For any
x,y\in\mathcal{A}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Id}(x)\star\mathrm{Id}(y)=~

\displaystyle x\star y=x\prec y+x\succ y+x\curlyvee y+x\cdot y

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\mathrm{Id}\big(x\vartriangleleft\mathrm{Id}(y)+\mathrm{Id}(x)\vartriangleright y+\theta(\mathrm{Id}(x),\mathrm{Id}(y))+x\cdot y\big).

This shows that the identity map
\mathrm{Id}:(\mathcal{A},\cdot)\rightarrow(\mathcal{A},\star)
is a
(\vartriangleright,\vartriangleleft,\theta)
-twisted Rota-Baxter operator.

If
(\mathcal{A},\prec_{\mathrm{Id}},\succ_{\mathrm{Id}},\curlyvee_{\mathrm{Id}},\cdot)
is the induced twisted tridendriform structure then

\displaystyle x\prec_{\mathrm{Id}}y=x\vartriangleleft\mathrm{Id}(y)=x\prec y,\quad x\succ_{\mathrm{Id}}y=\mathrm{Id}(x)\vartriangleright y=x\succ y

\displaystyle\text{ and }~x\curlyvee_{\mathrm{Id}}y=\theta(\mathrm{Id}(x),\mathrm{Id}(y))=x\curlyvee y,\text{ for all }x,y\in\mathcal{A}.\quad

Hence
(\mathcal{A},\prec_{\mathrm{Id}},\succ_{\mathrm{Id}},\curlyvee_{\mathrm{Id}},\cdot)=(\mathcal{A},\prec,\succ,\curlyvee,\cdot)
which proves the final result.
∎

The above result unifies that a tridendriform algebra is always induced by a relative Rota-Baxter operator of weight 1 and an NS-algebra is always induced by a twisted Rota-Baxter operator.

8.7 Remark
.

Let
(\mathcal{A},\prec,\succ,\curlyvee,\cdot)
be a twisted tridendriform algebra. Then the direct sum
\mathcal{A}\oplus\mathcal{A}
carries an associative algebra structure with the multiplication

\displaystyle(x,x^{\prime})\ast(y,y^{\prime}):=\big(x\star y,~\!x^{\prime}\cdot y^{\prime}+x\succ y^{\prime}+x^{\prime}\prec y+x\curlyvee y\big),

for all
(x,x^{\prime}),(y,y^{\prime})\in\mathcal{A}\oplus\mathcal{A}
. Then
(\mathcal{A}\oplus\mathcal{A},\ast)
is a quasi-twilled associative algebra.

Future works.
(i) It follows from Definition
8.3
that the operad
TwTridend
that encodes twisted tridendriform algebras is a nonsymmetric operad. In
[
33
]
and
[
12
]
the authors have respectively defined the cohomology of tridendriform algebras and NS-algebras. In a subsequent paper, we aim to generalize their constructions and define the cohomology of twisted tridendriform algebras. To define the explicit cochain groups and the coboundary map, one must first understand the nonsymmetric operad
TwTridend
and its dual operad
(TwTridend)^{!}
. Since

\displaystyle\mathrm{dim}~\!(TwTridend)^{!}(2)=~

\displaystyle\text{ the number of binary operations in a twisted tridendriform algebra}=4,

\displaystyle\mathrm{dim}~\!(TwTridend)^{!}(3)=~

\displaystyle\text{ the number of identities in a twisted tridendriform algebra}=8,

we expect that
\mathrm{dim}~\!(TwTridend)^{!}(n)=2^{n}
, for any
n\geq 2
. We will also explore this in our upcoming paper.

(ii) We have seen that a twisted tridendriform algebra can be realized as the underlying structure of a Rota-Baxter operator twisted by a non-abelian
2
-cocycle. In a work in progress, we introduce the notion of a “twisted post-Lie algebra” as the Lie analogue of a twisted tridendriform algebra. We observe that twisted post-Lie algebras arise from Rota-Baxter operators twisted by non-abelian Lie algebra
2
-cocycles. We further introduce twisted Rota-Baxter operators on (Lie) groups and find their relations to twisted post-Lie algebras.

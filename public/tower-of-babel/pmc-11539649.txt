Active shooters and gun-free zones: emotional versus legal motivations
Authors: Joshua E Lane, Yisong Geng
Published: 2024

We read with interest the article entitled “Gun-free zones and active shootings in the United States: a matched case–control study.”1 We agree on the need to prevent such tragic occurrences. The risk of active shooter incidents has been reported in a multitude of establishments, including schools, offices, healthcare facilities, and more.2

The methodology is solid; however, we emphasize the subjectivity of a shooter's motivation. This is an emotional response, such as being bullied, angry at a coworker, chronic pain from surgery, related to some mental issue, and so forth. The shooter chooses an action based on an event rather than on a law.

The definition of gun-free is not clear.2,3 Gun-free zones insinuate enforcement. Lack of enforcement does not fairly distinguish it from a gun-tolerant area. Many “no firearm” signs have no force of law unless posted where noted in State Law. Additionally, criminals do not respect laws.

The author's conclusion depends on the correct categorisation of the gun-free status of an establishment. Misclassification of cases could invalidate results. Robust studies helped support their analysis, with an odds ratio of 0.38 for an active shooter within a gun-free zone. A Monte Carlo simulation poses questions. If the probability of a shooter targeting a gun-free vs. tolerant establishment is equal, then the probability equals 0.5. If this probability drops to 0.27, then the average odds ratio (100,000 simulations) drops below 0.38 (calculated average odds ratio 0.376), and thus the 0.38 odds ratio translates into decreasing a shooter's willingness to target a gun-free zone by 46%. The magnitude of the drop may be too high if a shooter chooses an action based on a past event rather than based on the legal basis of gun allowance in a specific arena.

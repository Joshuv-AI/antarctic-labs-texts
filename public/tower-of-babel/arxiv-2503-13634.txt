Gelfand-Shilov spaces for extended Gevrey regularity
Nenad Teofanov, Filip Tomić, Milica Žigić

Abstract
We consider spaces of smooth functions obtained by relaxing Gevrey-type regularity and decay conditions. It is shown that these classes fit well within the general framework of the weighted matrices approach to ultradifferentiable functions. We examine equivalent ways of introducing Gelfand-Shilov spaces related to the extended Gevrey regularity and derive their nuclearity. In addition to the Fourier transform invariance property, we present their corresponding symmetric characterizations. Finally, we consider some time-frequency representations of the introduced classes of ultradifferentiable functions.

46E10, 26E10, 46A11

Keywords and phrases:
Gevrey sequences, ultradifferentiable functions, Gelfand-Shilov spaces, nuclearity, time-frequency representations

1
Introduction

Traditionally, Gelfand-Shilov spaces
of ultradifferentiable functions
are introduced as sets of all
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that for a given
t,s>0
,

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x^{\alpha}\partial^{\beta}f(x)|\leq Ch^{|\alpha+\beta|}\alpha!^{t}\beta!^{s},\qquad\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n},

(1)

for some constants
C,h>0
, see
[
13
]
. In other words,
the regularity and decay properties of
f

are controlled by Gevrey sequences

(\beta!^{s})_{\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}},
and
(\alpha!^{t})_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}}
respectively.

These spaces are widely used in regularity theory for partial differential equations, e.g., to describe exponential decay and the holomorphic extension of solutions to globally elliptic equations
[
4
,
5
]
, and in the regularizing properties of the Boltzmann equation
[
21
]
. For additional examples, we refer to
[
14
,
23
,
7
]
.

More general Gelfand-Shilov type spaces are obtained by using various types of defining sequences
apart from the Gevrey sequences.

In this paper, we introduce Gelfand-Shilov type spaces through regularity and decay conditions that
go beyond the Gevrey sequences, by considering two-parameter dependent sequences of the form

(p^{\tau p^{\sigma}})_{p\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
,
\tau>0
,
\sigma>1
.
Such sequences have been recently used to describe a convenient extension of Gevrey spaces,

[
18
,
25
,
26
]
.
For an overview and some applications of the extended Gevrey classes, we refer to
[
32
]
.
We note that the extended Gevrey classes are defined by regularity conditions, without reference to decay, whereas the elements of the spaces considered here, in addition to regularity, also enjoy suitable decay properties.

A commonly used approach to ultradifferentiable functions, based on defining sequences,
was introduced in
[
20
]
. Another widely used approach is the one based on the so-called weight functions,
as considered in
[
3
]
. Recently, Rainer and Schindl proposed an approach that unifies both classical methods,
[
27
]
. It is based on the concept of weight matrices, or multi-indexed weight sequences in the terminology of
[
9
]
. A general framework that provides a unified treatment of Gelfand-Shilov spaces defined via weight sequences or weight functions was very recently presented in
[
2
,
10
]
. There, one can find general results on the inclusion relation between the considered global ultradifferentiable classes.

Our aim is to study a new type of Gelfand–Shilov spaces that combines the general approach based on weight matrices with the framework of extended Gevrey regularity. This is achieved by considering the so-called limit classes, i.e., suitable unions and intersections with respect to the parameter, see Definition
3.1
.

In this way, we obtain prominent examples of global ultradifferentiable classes defined by general weighted matrix approach, which go beyond the classical theory.
At the same time, by considering specific sequences, we are able to derive results that cannot be extracted from the general theory,
while also extending those that are well-known in the context of the Gevrey type regularity.

To be more precise, we perform a thorough examination of general conditions from
[
9
]
that provide nuclearity property of the considered spaces. Thereafter, we show the Fourier transform invariance, and symmetric characterization of Gelfand-Shilov spaces for extended Gevrey regularity á la Chung-Chung-Kim
[
6
]
. We emphasize that the sequence

(p^{\tau p^{\sigma}})_{p\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
,
\tau>0
,
\sigma>1
, does not satisfy Komatsu’s
(M.2)
condition:

(M.2)\qquad(\exists A,B>0)\quad M_{p+q}\leq AB^{p+q}M_{p}M_{q},\qquad p,q\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

This invokes nontrivial modifications to the existing proofs for classical Gelfand-Shilov type spaces.

Furthermore, we study the properties of several time-frequency representations acting on Gelfand–Shilov spaces via extended Gevrey sequences. This leads to characterizations that extend those given in, e.g.,
[
16
,
30
,
33
]
, in the context of classical Gelfand–Shilov spaces.

Preliminaries

2.1
Weight sequences and matrices

We first recall the notion of a weight matrix
[
27
]
, or multi-indexed weight sequence system in terms of
[
9
,
10
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
M=(M_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}}

be a weight sequence, i.e.
M_{\alpha}>0
,
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
,

M_{0}=1
, and

\lim_{|\alpha|\rightarrow\infty}(M_{\alpha})^{1/|\alpha|}=\infty
.

A family

\mathcal{M}=\{M^{(\lambda)}\colon\lambda>0\}
of weight sequences is a weight matrix if

M_{\alpha}^{(\lambda)}\leq M_{\alpha}^{(\mu)}
for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}

and
\lambda\leq\mu
.

We say that the sequence
M=(M_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}}

is an isotropic sequence if

M_{|\alpha|}=M_{p},

p\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, for some sequence

(M_{p})_{p\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
.

Next we introduce isotropic sequences related to extended Gevrey regularity.

Let
\tau>0
and
\sigma>1.
By
M^{(\tau)}=(M_{p}^{\tau,\sigma})_{p\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
we denote
the sequence of positive numbers given by

M_{p}^{\tau,\sigma}=p^{\tau p^{\sigma}},\quad p\in\mathbb{N},\qquad M_{0}^{\tau,\sigma}=1.

(2)

We are interested in the weight matrix
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma},

\sigma>1
, given by

\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}=\left\{M^{(\tau)}=(M_{p}^{\tau,\sigma})_{p\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\colon\tau>0\right\}.

(3)

The main properties of
(M_{p}^{\tau,\sigma})_{p\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
,

\tau>0
,
\sigma>1
, related to Komatsu’s theory of ultradistributions
are given in the next lemma (cf.
[
25
, Lemmas 2.2 and 3.1]
,

[
32
, Lemma 2]
).

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\tau>0
,
\sigma>1
,
M_{0}^{\tau,\sigma}=1
,
and
M_{p}^{\tau,\sigma}=p^{\tau p^{\sigma}}
,
p\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then the following properties hold:

(M.1)

(M_{p}^{\tau,\sigma})^{2}\leq M_{p-1}^{\tau,\sigma}M_{p+1}^{\tau,\sigma},\;

p\in\mathbb{N},

\widetilde{(M.2)}

M_{p+q}^{\tau,\sigma}\leq C^{p^{\sigma}+q^{\sigma}}M_{p}^{\tau 2^{\sigma-1},\sigma}M_{q}^{\tau 2^{\sigma-1},\sigma},\;

p,q\in\mathbb{N}_{0},\quad

for some constant
C\geq 1
,

\widetilde{(M.2)^{\prime}}

M_{p+1}^{\tau,\sigma}\leq C^{p^{\sigma}}M_{p}^{\tau,\sigma},\;

p\in\mathbb{N}_{0},\quad

for some constant
C\geq 1
,

(M.3)^{\prime}

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{p=1}^{\infty}\frac{M_{p-1}^{\tau,\sigma}}{M_{p}^{\tau,\sigma}}<\infty.

In addition,
\left(\left(\frac{M_{p}^{\tau,\sigma}}{p!}\right)^{1/p}\right)_{p\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}

is an almost increasing sequence, i.e.

\left(\frac{M_{p}^{\tau,\sigma}}{p!}\right)^{1/p}\leq C\left(\frac{M_{q}^{\tau,\sigma}}{q!}\right)^{1/q},\qquad p\leq q,

which implies

\lim_{p\rightarrow\infty}(M_{p})^{1/p}=\infty

(cf.
[
32
]
).

We note that (M.1) implies

M_{p}^{\tau,\sigma}M_{q}^{\tau,\sigma}\leq M_{p+q}^{\tau,\sigma},\qquad p,q\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

(4)

and that
(M.3)^{\prime}
follows from the estimate

\displaystyle\frac{M_{p-1}^{\tau,\sigma}}{M_{p}^{\tau,\sigma}}\leq\frac{1}{(2p)^{\tau(p-1)^{\sigma-1}}},\quad p\in\mathbb{N}.

(5)

The proof of (
5
) can be found in e.g.
[
32
, Appendix A]
.

More properties of
(M_{p}^{\tau,\sigma})_{p\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
in the context of weight matrices approach to ultradifferentiable functions (in the sense of A. Rainer and G. Schindl)
can be found in e.g.
[
18
]
.

The sequence
(M_{p}^{\tau,\sigma})_{p\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
gives rise to
extended Gevrey classes
\mathcal{E}_{\tau,\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, also called
Pilipović-Teofanov-Tomić classes (PTT-classes) in
[
18
,
19
]
.
We refer to
[
32
]
for a recent survey on PTT-classes and their relation to
classes of ultradifferentiable functions given by the weight matrix approach.

In fact, we use the weight matrix approach and consider PTT-limit classes.
As it is noted in
[
18
]
the main reason to consider
these classes is their stability under the action of ultradifferential operators.

2.2
Weight functions and the Lambert function

Recall, a non-negative, continuous, even, and increasing on
[0,\infty)
function
\omega
defined on
\mathbb{R}
,
\omega(0)=0
, is called
BMT (Braun-Meise-Taylor) weight function
, or simply
weight function
, if it satisfies the following conditions:

(
\alpha
)

\omega(2t)=O(\omega(t)),\quad t\to\infty,

(
\beta
)

\omega(t)=O(t),\quad t\to\infty,

(
\gamma
)

o(\omega(t))=\log t,\quad t\to\infty,

(
\delta
)

\varphi(t)=w(e^{t}),\quad
is convex.

Some classical examples of BMT functions are

\omega(t)=\ln^{s}_{+}|t|,\quad\omega(t)=\frac{|t|}{\ln^{s-1}(e+|t|)},\quad s>1,\;t\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

(6)

where
\ln_{+}k=\max\{0,\ln k\}
. Also,
\omega(t)=|t|^{s}
is a weight function if and only if
0<s\leq 1
.

We say that functions
f
and
g
are equivalent if
f=O(g)
and
g=O(f)
.

Next we introduce the Lambert
W
function which
describes the precise asymptotic behavior of associated function to the sequence
(M_{p}^{\tau,\sigma})_{p\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
.

The
Lambert
W
function
is defined as the inverse of
ze^{z}
,
z\in{\mathbb{C}}
. By
W(x)
, we denote the restriction of its principal branch to
[0,\infty)
. It is used as a convenient tool to describe asymptotic behavior in different contexts. We refer to
[
8
,
22
]
for a review of some applications of the Lambert
W
function in pure and applied mathematics.

Some basic properties of the Lambert function
W
are given below:

(W1)

W(0)=0
,
W(e)=1
,
W(x)
is continuous, increasing and concave on
[0,\infty)
,

(W2)

W(xe^{x})=x
and
x=W(x)e^{W(x)}
,
x\geq 0
,

(W3)

W
can be represented in the form of the absolutely convergent series

W(x)=\ln x-\ln(\ln x)+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}c_{km}\frac{(\ln(\ln x))^{m}}{(\ln x)^{k+m}},\quad x\geq x_{0}>e,

with suitable constants
c_{km}
and
x_{0}
, wherefrom the following estimates hold:

\ln x-\ln(\ln x)\leq W(x)\leq\ln x-\frac{1}{2}\ln(\ln x),\quad x\geq e.

(7)

The equality in (
7
) holds if and only if
x=e
.

Note that
(W2)
implies

W(x\ln x)=\ln x,\quad x>1.

By using
(W3)
we obtain

W(x)\sim\ln x,\quad x\to\infty,

and therefore

W(Cx)\sim W(x),\quad x\to\infty,

for any
C>0
.

Remark 2.3
.

By
[
31
, Theorem 1]
it follows that

\displaystyle\omega(t)=\frac{\ln^{\sigma}(1+|t|)}{W^{\sigma-1}(\ln(1+|t|))}
,
\sigma>1
,
t\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
is equivalent to a BMT function, and therefore it satisfies
(\alpha)-(\gamma)
.
Actually, in
[
31
]
, the function
\ln_{+}|\cdot|
is considered instead of
\ln(1+|\cdot|)
, which does not change the conclusion.

3
Gelfand-Shilov type classes

3.1
Definition and its relatives

Inspired by the original source
[
13
]
, we consider separate and joint conditions on decay and regularity, introducing
isotropic Gelfand-Shilov type spaces
in terms of the weight matrix
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}
,
\sigma>1
,
as follows.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\sigma>1
, the weight matrix
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}
be given by
(
3
), and let
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then
the Gelfand-Shilov spaces of Roumieu type related to
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}
are given by

\displaystyle\varphi\in\mathcal{S}^{\{\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}\}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad

\displaystyle(\exists\tau>0)\;(\forall\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n})\;(\exists C_{\alpha}>0)\;(\forall\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n})

\displaystyle\sup_{x}|x^{\alpha}\partial^{\beta}\varphi(x)|\leq C_{\alpha}M^{\tau,\sigma}_{|\beta|};

\displaystyle\varphi\in\mathcal{S}_{\{\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}\}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad

\displaystyle(\exists\tau>0)\;(\forall\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n})\;(\exists C_{\beta}>0)\;(\forall\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n})

\displaystyle\sup_{x}|x^{\alpha}\partial^{\beta}\varphi(x)|\leq C_{\beta}M^{\tau,\sigma}_{|\alpha|};

\displaystyle\varphi\in\mathcal{S}_{\{\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}\}}^{\{\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}\}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad

\displaystyle(\exists\tau>0)\;(\exists C>0)\;(\forall\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n})

\displaystyle\sup_{x}|x^{\alpha}\partial^{\beta}\varphi(x)|\leq CM^{\tau,\sigma}_{|\alpha|}M^{\tau,\sigma}_{|\beta|},

and the Gelfand-Shilov spaces of Beurling type related to
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}
are given by

\displaystyle\varphi\in\mathcal{S}^{(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma})}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad

\displaystyle(\forall\tau>0)\;(\forall\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n})\;(\exists C_{\alpha}>0)\;(\forall\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n})

\displaystyle\sup_{x}|x^{\alpha}\partial^{\beta}\varphi(x)|\leq C_{\alpha}M^{\tau,\sigma}_{|\beta|};

\displaystyle\varphi\in\mathcal{S}_{(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma})}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad

\displaystyle(\forall\tau>0)\;(\forall\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n})\;(\exists C_{\beta}>0)\;(\forall\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n})

\displaystyle\sup_{x}|x^{\alpha}\partial^{\beta}\varphi(x)|\leq C_{\beta}M^{\tau,\sigma}_{|\alpha|};

\displaystyle\varphi\in\mathcal{S}_{(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma})}^{(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma})}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad

\displaystyle(\forall\tau>0)(\exists C>0)(\forall\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n})

\displaystyle\sup_{x}|x^{\alpha}\partial^{\beta}\varphi(x)|\leq CM^{\tau,\sigma}_{|\alpha|}M^{\tau,\sigma}_{|\beta|}.

We will use
[\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}]
as a common notation for

\{\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}\}
or
(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma})

\mathcal{S}_{[\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}]}^{[\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}]}
is either

\mathcal{S}_{\{\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}\}}^{\{\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}\}}

or
\mathcal{S}_{(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma})}^{(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma})}

By Definition

\mathcal{S}_{[\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}]}^{[\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}]}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{[\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}]}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\mathcal{S}^{[\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}]}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

(8)

\mathcal{S}_{(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma})}^{(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma})}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{\{\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}\}}^{\{\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}\}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

\mathscr{S}_{\{\mathcal{M}\}}^{\{\mathcal{M}\}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\Big\{\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\colon(\exists\lambda>0)\;(\exists h>0)\;(\exists C>0)\\

\mathscr{S}_{(\mathcal{M})}^{(\mathcal{M})}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\Big\{\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\colon(\forall\lambda>0)\;(\forall h>0)\;(\exists C=C_{\lambda,h}>0)\\
\sup_{x}|x^{\alpha}\partial^{\beta}\varphi(x)|\leq Ch^{|\alpha+\beta|}M^{\lambda}_{\alpha}M^{\lambda}_{\beta},\;\;\forall\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}\Big\},

(10)

see e.g.
[

) and (

\sup_{\rho>0}\frac{h^{\rho^{\sigma}}}{\rho^{\tau\rho^{\sigma}}}=e^{\frac{\tau}{\sigma e}h^{\frac{\sigma}{\tau}}},\qquad h>0,

(16)

\displaystyle\varphi\in\mathcal{S}_{(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}),2}^{(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma})}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad

\displaystyle(\forall\tau>0)(\exists C>0)(\forall\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n})

\displaystyle\|x^{\alpha}\partial^{\beta}\varphi(x)\|_{L^{2}}\leq CM^{\tau,\sigma}_{|\alpha|}M^{\tau,\sigma}_{|\beta|},

(17)

where
\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}}
denotes the usual
L^{2}
-norm,
and
\mathcal{S}_{[\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}],2}^{[\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}]}
is either

\mathcal{S}_{\{\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}\},2}^{\{\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}\}}

or
\mathcal{S}_{(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}),2}^{(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma})}
.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
\sigma>1
, and let the weight matrix
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}
be given by
(
3
),

M_{p}^{\tau,\sigma}=p^{\tau p^{\sigma}}
,
p\in\mathbb{N}
,
M_{0}^{\tau,\sigma}=1
.
Then

\mathcal{S}_{[\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}]}^{[\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}]}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\mathcal{S}_{[\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}],2}^{[\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}]}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

(18)

Proof.

We prove the Beurling case, i.e.

\mathcal{S}_{(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma})}^{(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma})}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\mathcal{S}_{(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}),2}^{(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma})}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
and leave the Roumieu case to the reader.

For the inclusion

\mathcal{S}_{(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}),2}^{(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma})}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset\mathcal{S}_{(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma})}^{(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma})}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we invoke the Sobolev embedding

H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
s>n/2
,
which implies

\sup_{x}|x^{\alpha}\partial^{\beta}\varphi(x)|\leq\tilde{C}\|x^{\alpha}\partial^{\beta}\varphi(x)\|_{H^{s}}\leq C\sum_{|\gamma|\leq s}\|\partial^{\gamma}(x^{\alpha}\partial^{\beta}\varphi(x))\|_{L^{2}},

(19)

for some
C,\tilde{C}>0
,
since the Sobolev norm

\|f(x)\|_{H^{s}}=\|(1+|\omega|^{2})^{s/2}\hat{f}(\omega)\|_{H^{s}}

is equivalent to

\sum_{|\gamma|\leq s}\|\partial^{\gamma}f(x)\|_{L^{2}}
.

By the Leibniz formula

\sum_{|\gamma|\leq s}\|\partial^{\gamma}(x^{\alpha}\partial^{\beta}\varphi(x))\|_{L^{2}}\leq\sum_{|\gamma|\leq s}\sum_{\delta\leq\min\{\gamma,\alpha\}}\binom{\gamma}{\delta}\binom{\alpha}{\delta}\delta!\|x^{\alpha-\delta}\partial^{\beta+\gamma-\delta}\varphi(x)\|_{L^{2}}.\\

(20)

Now
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}_{(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}),2}^{(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma})}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
implies

\displaystyle\|x^{\alpha-\delta}\partial^{\beta+\gamma-\delta}\varphi(x)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}M^{\tau,\sigma}_{|\alpha-\delta|}M^{\tau,\sigma}_{|\beta+\gamma-\delta|}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}M^{\tau,\sigma}_{|\alpha|}M^{\tau,\sigma}_{|\beta+s|}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}M^{\tau,\sigma}_{|\alpha|}C_{2}^{s|\beta+s|^{\sigma}}M^{\tau,\sigma}_{|\beta|}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}M^{\tau,\sigma}_{|\alpha|}C_{2}^{s2^{\sigma-1}(|\beta|^{\sigma}+s^{\sigma})}M^{\tau,\sigma}_{|\beta|}

\displaystyle\leq C_{3}M^{\tau,\sigma}_{|\alpha|}h^{|\beta|^{\sigma}}M^{\tau,\sigma}_{|\beta|},

(21)

for some
h>0
,
C_{3}>0
(which depends on
\tau,\sigma
and the dimension
n
),
where we used inequality

(p+q)^{\sigma}\leq 2^{\sigma-1}(p^{\sigma}+q^{\sigma}),\quad p,q>0,\quad\sigma>1.

(22)

Since the number of terms in the sums in (
20
)
can be estimated by an integer independent on
\alpha
,
by (
19
), (
20
), (
21
), and
Theorem
3.3
it follows that

\varphi\in\mathcal{S}_{(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma})}^{(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma})}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

It remains to prove the opposite inclusion,

\mathcal{S}_{(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma})}^{(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma})}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset\mathcal{S}_{(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}),2}^{(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma})}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Let there be given
s>n/4
, so that
C_{s}=\|(1+|x|^{2})^{-s}\|_{L^{2}}<\infty
. By using

(1+|x|^{2})^{s}=\sum_{|\gamma|\leq s}C_{\gamma}|x^{2\gamma}|,\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

where the constants
C_{\gamma}
can be estimated in terms of
s
, and we get

\displaystyle\|x^{\alpha}\partial^{\beta}\varphi(x)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{s}\sup_{x}(1+|x|^{2})^{s}|x^{\alpha}\partial^{\beta}\varphi(x)|

\displaystyle=C_{s}\sum_{|\gamma|\leq s}C_{\gamma}\sup_{x}|x^{\alpha+2\gamma}\partial^{\beta}\varphi(x)|

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\sum_{|\gamma|\leq s}M^{\tau,\sigma}_{|\alpha+2\gamma|}M^{\tau,\sigma}_{|\beta|}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\sum_{|\gamma|\leq s}C_{2}^{(2\gamma)2^{\sigma-1}(|\alpha|^{\sigma}+(2\gamma)^{\sigma})}M^{\tau,\sigma}_{|\alpha|}M^{\tau,\sigma}_{|\beta|}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}C_{2}^{2^{\sigma-1}(2s)^{\sigma+1}}\sum_{|\gamma|\leq s}h^{|\alpha|^{\sigma}}M^{\tau,\sigma}_{|\alpha|}M^{\tau,\sigma}_{|\beta|}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{|\alpha|^{\sigma}}M^{\tau,\sigma}_{|\alpha|}M^{\tau,\sigma}_{|\beta|},

(23)

for some
C,C_{1},C_{2}>0
which depends on the dimension
n
and
\sigma>0
.

Thus if

\varphi\in\mathcal{S}_{(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma})}^{(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma})}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
by (
23
) and Theorem
3.3
we get

\varphi\in\mathcal{S}_{(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}),2}^{(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma})}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
∎

3.2
Nuclearity

In this subsection we prove that spaces
\mathcal{S}_{[\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}]}^{[\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}]}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
are nuclear.
This is done by using general theory given in
[
9
]
.
We perform nontrivial calculations to show
that the general result
[
9
, Theorem 5.1]

can be utilized to prove the nuclearity of

\mathcal{S}_{[\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}]}^{[\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}]}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Recall that the associated function to the sequence
M^{\tau,\sigma}_{p}
is given by

T_{\tau,\sigma}(x)=\sup_{p\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\ln\frac{x^{p}}{M^{\tau,\sigma}_{p}},\qquad x>0.

(25)

Then by
[
31
, Proposition 2]

it follows that

\frac{1}{A}\tau^{-\frac{1}{\sigma-1}}\frac{\ln^{\frac{\sigma}{\sigma-1}}(|t|)}{W^{\frac{1}{\sigma-1}}(\ln(|t|))}-B\leq T_{\tau,\sigma}(|t|)\\
\leq A\tau^{-\frac{1}{\sigma-1}}\frac{\ln^{\frac{\sigma}{\sigma-1}}(|t|)}{W^{\frac{1}{\sigma-1}}(\ln(|t|))}+B,\quad|t|>1,

(26)

for suitable constants
A>1
and
B>0
.

Let us define the family of functions
\mathcal{W}_{\sigma}
as follows:

\mathcal{W}_{\sigma}=\left\{\left(w^{\tau}(x)=e^{T_{\tau,\sigma}(|x|)}\right)_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\colon\tau>0\right\}.

(27)

Then
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
belongs to

\mathcal{S}_{\{\mathcal{W}_{\sigma}\}}^{\{\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}\}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

if

(\exists\tau>0))(\exists C>0)(\forall\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n})\quad\sup_{x}\left|e^{T_{\tau,\sigma}(|x|)}\partial^{\beta}\varphi(x)\right|\leq CM^{\tau,\sigma}_{|\beta|};

(28)

and
\displaystyle\varphi\in\mathcal{S}_{(\mathcal{W}_{\sigma})}^{(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma})}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
if

(\forall\tau>0))(\exists C>0)(\forall\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n})\quad\sup_{x}\left|e^{T_{\tau,\sigma}(|x|)}\partial^{\beta}\varphi(x)\right|\leq CM^{\tau,\sigma}_{|\beta|}.

(29)

Again, we use

\mathcal{S}_{[\mathcal{W}_{\sigma}]}^{[\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}]}
to denote
\mathcal{S}_{\{\mathcal{W}_{\sigma}\}}^{\{\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}\}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

or

\mathcal{S}_{(\mathcal{W}_{\sigma})}^{(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma})}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Now we can prove the main result of this section.

Theorem 3.7
.

Fix
\sigma>1
and let
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}
be a weight matrix given with (
3
). Then:

1.

\mathcal{S}_{[\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}]}^{[\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}]}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\mathcal{S}_{[\mathcal{W}_{\sigma}]}^{[\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}]}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

2.

\mathcal{S}_{[\mathcal{W}_{\sigma}]}^{[\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}]}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a nuclear space.

Proof.

We prove the Roumieu case, and the Beurling case follows by using similar arguments.

1.

Since the sequence
(M^{\tau,\sigma}_{p})_{p\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
given by
(
2
) satisfies
(M.1)
,
by
[
20
, Proposition 3.2]

it follows that

M^{\tau,\sigma}_{p}=\sup_{|x|>0}\frac{|x|^{p}}{e^{T_{\tau,\sigma}(|x|)}},\quad p\in\mathbb{N}

(30)

for all
\tau>0
and
\sigma>1
.

Let
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}_{\{\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}\}}^{\{\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}\}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then there exist
\tau_{0},C>0
such that

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x^{\alpha}\partial^{\beta}\varphi(x)|\leq CM^{\tau_{0},\sigma}_{|\alpha|}M^{\tau_{0},\sigma}_{|\beta|},

and by the simple inequality
(1/\sqrt{n})^{|\alpha|}|x|^{|\alpha|}\leq|x^{\alpha}|
, we get

|\partial^{\beta}\varphi(x)|\leq C\frac{\sqrt{n}^{|\alpha|}M^{\tau_{0},\sigma}_{|\alpha|}}{|x|^{|\alpha|}}M^{\tau_{0},\sigma}_{|\beta|}=C\frac{\sqrt{n}^{|\alpha|}}{M^{\tau_{0},\sigma}_{|\alpha|}}\frac{M^{2\tau_{0},\sigma}_{|\alpha|}}{|x|^{|\alpha|}}M^{\tau_{0},\sigma}_{|\beta|}\leq C^{\prime}M^{\tau_{0},\sigma}_{|\beta|}\frac{M^{2\tau_{0},\sigma}_{|\alpha|}}{|x|^{|\alpha|}},

for
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n},

|x|\not=0
, where
C^{\prime}=Ce^{T_{\tau_{0},\sigma}(\sqrt{n})}
. By (
30
) we obtain

\sup_{x}|e^{T_{\tau,\sigma}(|x|)}\partial^{\beta}\varphi(x)|\leq C^{\prime}M^{\tau_{0},\sigma}_{|\beta|}\sup_{x}e^{T_{\tau,\sigma}(|x|)}\frac{M^{2\tau_{0},\sigma}_{|\alpha|}}{|x|^{|\alpha|}}\leq C^{\prime}M^{\tau,\sigma}_{|\beta|},

i.e. (
28
) holds with
\tau=2\tau_{0}
.

For the opposite direction, we note that

\varphi\in\mathcal{S}_{\{\mathcal{W}_{\sigma}\}}^{\{\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}\}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
i.e.
(
28
), together with (
30
) and
|x^{\alpha}|\leq|x|^{|\alpha|}
,
implies

|x^{\alpha}\partial^{\beta}\varphi(x)|\leq CM^{\tau_{0},\sigma}_{|\beta|}|x|^{|\alpha|}e^{-T_{\tau_{0},\sigma}(|x|)}\leq CM^{\tau_{0},\sigma}_{|\beta|}M^{\tau_{0},\sigma}_{|\alpha|}

for
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
.
Thus
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}_{\{\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}\}}^{\{\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}\}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, which proves
1.

2.
By
[
9
, Theorem 5.1]
,
\mathcal{S}_{[\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}]}^{[\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}]}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\mathcal{S}_{[\mathcal{W}_{\sigma}]}^{[\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}]}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a nuclear space if
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}
and
\mathcal{W}_{\sigma}
satisfy the following conditions:

\displaystyle\{L\}

\displaystyle\quad(\forall h>0)(\forall\tau>0)(\exists\tau_{0},C>0)(\forall\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n})

\displaystyle h^{|\alpha|}M^{\tau,\sigma}_{|\alpha|}\leq CM^{\tau_{0},\sigma}_{|\alpha|},

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{M}.2\}^{\prime}

\displaystyle\quad(\forall\tau>0)(\exists\tau_{0}>0)(\exists h,C>0)(\forall\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n})

\displaystyle M^{\tau,\sigma}_{|\alpha|+1}\leq Ch^{|\alpha|}M^{\tau_{0},\sigma}_{|\alpha|},

\displaystyle\{wM\}

\displaystyle\quad(\forall\tau>0)(\exists\tau_{0}>0)(\exists C>0)(\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n})

\displaystyle\sup_{|y|\leq 1}w^{\tau_{0}}(x+y)\leq Cw^{\tau}(x),

\displaystyle\{M\}

\displaystyle\quad(\forall\tau_{1},\tau_{2}>0)(\exists\tau_{0}>0)(\exists C>0)(\forall x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n})\,

\displaystyle w^{\tau_{0}}(x+y)\leq Cw^{\tau_{1}}(x)w^{\tau_{2}}(y),

\displaystyle\{N\}

\displaystyle\quad(\forall\tau>0)(\exists\tau_{0}>0)

\displaystyle w^{\tau_{0}}/w^{\tau}\in L^{1},

where
w^{\tau}(x)=e^{T_{\tau,\sigma}(|x|)}
. Note that
\{M\}
implies
\{wM\}
.

We note that
\{L\}
follows from Lemma
3.2
, i.e. from (
12
).

For
\{\mathcal{M}.2\}^{\prime}
we take arbitrary
\tau>0
. Then by
\widetilde{(M.2)}^{\prime}
we have

M^{\tau,\sigma}_{|\alpha|+1}\leq A^{p^{\sigma}+1}M^{\tau,\sigma}_{|\alpha|}=A\frac{A^{p^{\sigma}}}{M^{\tau,\sigma}_{|\alpha|}}M^{2\tau,\sigma}_{|\alpha|}\leq CM^{\tau_{0},\sigma}_{|\alpha|},

for
\tau_{0}=2\tau
where we used (
11
).

To verify conditions
\{wM\}
and
\{N\}
put

\displaystyle\omega_{\sigma}(x)=\frac{\ln^{\frac{\sigma}{\sigma-1}}(1+|x|)}{W^{\frac{1}{\sigma-1}}(\ln(1+|x|))},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus 0,\quad\omega_{\sigma}(0)=0,

and consider

\mathcal{W}^{\prime}_{\sigma}=\left\{\left(\omega^{\lambda}(x)=e^{\frac{1}{\lambda}\omega_{\sigma}(x)}\right)_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\colon\lambda>0\right\}.

By (
26
) we get

(\forall\tau>0)(\exists\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}>0)(\exists C_{1},C_{2}>0)(\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n})\,\,C_{1}\omega^{\lambda_{1}}(x)\leq w^{\tau}(x)\leq C_{2}\omega^{\lambda_{2}}(x),

wherefrom it follows that the families
\mathcal{W}_{\sigma}
and
\mathcal{W}^{\prime}_{\sigma}
are equivalent.
Thus, it is sufficient to check that

\mathcal{W}^{\prime}_{\sigma}
satisfies
\{wM\}
and
\{N\}
.

Indeed, since
\omega_{\sigma}(x)
is equivalent to a BMT function (see Remark
2.3
),
it satisfies BMT conditions
(\alpha)-(\gamma)
.
Then,
[
9
, Lemma 3.5]
implies that

\mathcal{W}^{\prime}_{\sigma}
satisfies conditions
\{M\}
and

, and the theorem is proved.

4
Fourier transform representation

The Gelfand-Shilov spaces given by (
1
) with

t=s>0
are Fourier transform invariant, which is a convent property in different applications. In this section we address the Fourier transform invariance of
\mathcal{S}_{[\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}]}^{[\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}]}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and their
characterizations in terms of
the Fourier transform.

The Fourier transform of
f\in\mathcal{S}_{[\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}]}^{[\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}]}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

is given by

\displaystyle(\mathcal{F}f)(\xi)=\hat{f}(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)e^{-2\pi ix\xi}\;dx,\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

and the corresponding inverse Fourier transform is defined as

\displaystyle(\mathcal{F}^{-1}f)(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(\xi)e^{2\pi ix\xi}\;d\xi,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

The invariance of classical Gelfand-Shilov spaces
under the Fourier transform is given already in
[
13
]
, see also
a more recent source
[
23
, Chapter 6]
.
The Fourier transform invariance for the
Gelfand-Shilov type spaces related to the extended Gevrey regularity can be stated as follows.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\sigma>1
, and let the weight matrix
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}
be given by
(
3
),

M_{p}^{\tau,\sigma}=p^{\tau p^{\sigma}}
,
p\in\mathbb{N}
,
M_{0}^{\tau,\sigma}=1
.

Then
\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{S}_{[\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}]}^{[\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}]})(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\mathcal{S}_{[\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}]}^{[\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}]}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Proof.

We give the proof for the Beurling case,
since the Roumieu case can be proved in a similar way.

Let

\varphi\in\mathcal{S}_{(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma})}^{(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma})}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and let there be given
\tau>0
.

From the Leibniz formula and (
4
) we obtain

\displaystyle|\partial^{\beta}(x^{\alpha}\varphi(x))|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{0\leq\delta\leq\min\{\alpha,\beta\}}\binom{\alpha}{\delta}\binom{\beta}{\delta}\delta!\,x^{\alpha-\delta}\partial^{\beta-\delta}\varphi(x)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{0\leq\delta\leq\min\{\alpha,\beta\}}\binom{\alpha}{\delta}\binom{\beta}{\delta}\delta!\,C\,M^{\frac{\tau}{2},\sigma}_{|\alpha-\delta|}M^{\frac{\tau}{2},\sigma}_{|\beta-\delta|}

\displaystyle\leq C\,M^{\frac{\tau}{2},\sigma}_{|\alpha|}M^{\frac{\tau}{2},\sigma}_{|\beta|}\sum_{0\leq\delta\leq\min\{\alpha,\beta\}}\binom{\alpha}{\delta}\binom{\beta}{\delta}\,\frac{\delta!}{(M_{|\delta|}^{\frac{\tau}{2},\sigma})^{2}}.

Next, by using the fact that for any
\tau>0
there exists
C_{1}>0
such that

\delta!\leq C_{1}M_{|\delta|}^{\tau,\sigma},\quad\delta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n},

(31)

(which follows form e.g.
[
32
, (4)]
), we obtain

\displaystyle|\partial^{\beta}(x^{\alpha}\varphi(x))|

\displaystyle\leq C\,M^{\frac{\tau}{2},\sigma}_{|\alpha|}M^{\frac{\tau}{2},\sigma}_{|\beta|}\,C^{\prime}\sum_{0\leq\delta\leq\min\{\alpha,\beta\}}\binom{\alpha}{\delta}\binom{\beta}{\delta}

\displaystyle\leq CC^{\prime}\,2^{|\alpha|+|\beta|}\,M^{\frac{\tau}{2},\sigma}_{|\alpha|}M^{\frac{\tau}{2},\sigma}_{|\beta|}\leq C_{1}M^{\tau,\sigma}_{|\alpha|}M^{\tau,\sigma}_{|\beta|},\quad\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n},

where in the last inequality we applied (
12
).
Similarly,

\displaystyle|x|^{2}|\partial^{\beta}(x^{\alpha}\varphi(x))|\leq C^{\prime\prime}\,2^{|\alpha|+2+|\beta|}\,M^{\frac{\tau}{2},\sigma}_{|\alpha|+2}M^{\frac{\tau}{2},\sigma}_{|\beta|},\quad\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}.

Applying
\widetilde{(M.2)^{\prime}}
twice and then using (
12
) again, we obtain

\displaystyle|x|^{2}|\partial^{\beta}(x^{\alpha}\varphi(x))|

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime\prime}\,2^{|\alpha|+2+|\beta|}\,A^{(|\alpha|+1)^{\sigma}+|\alpha|^{\sigma}}\,M^{\frac{\tau}{2},\sigma}_{|\alpha|}M^{\frac{\tau}{2},\sigma}_{|\beta|}

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}M^{\tau,\sigma}_{|\alpha|}M^{\tau,\sigma}_{|\beta|},\quad\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}.

Finally,

\displaystyle|\xi^{\beta}\mathcal{F}(\varphi)^{(\alpha)}(\xi)|

\displaystyle=\left|\mathcal{F}\left(\partial^{\beta}(x^{\alpha}\varphi(x))\right)(\xi)\right|=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\partial^{\beta}(x^{\alpha}\varphi(x))\,e^{-2\pi ix\xi}dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\partial^{\beta}(x^{\alpha}\varphi(x))|\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|(1+|x|^{2})\,\partial^{\beta}(x^{\alpha}\varphi(x))|}{1+|x|^{2}}dx

\displaystyle\leq\left(C_{1}\,M^{\tau,\sigma}_{|\alpha|}M^{\tau,\sigma}_{|\beta|}+C_{2}\,M^{\tau,\sigma}_{|\alpha|}M^{\tau,\sigma}_{|\beta|}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1}{1+|x|^{2}}dx

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}M^{\tau,\sigma}_{|\alpha|}M^{\tau,\sigma}_{|\beta|},\quad\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}.

So,
\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{S}_{(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma})}^{(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma})}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma})}^{(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma})}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).
The other inclusion can be obtained
in the same way since
\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{F}\varphi)(x)=\varphi(-x).

∎

Remark 4.2
.

From the proof of Theorem
4.1
, it follows that

\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{S}_{[\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}]})(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\mathcal{S}^{[\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}]}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and
\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{S}^{[\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}]})(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\mathcal{S}_{[\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}]}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

The beautiful symmetric characterizations of
Gelfand-Shilov spaces given in
[
6
]

can be formulated in terms of the extended Gevrey regularity as follows.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\sigma>1
, and let the weight matrix
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}
be given by (
3
),

M_{p}^{\tau,\sigma}=p^{\tau p^{\sigma}}
,
p\in\mathbb{N}
,
M_{0}^{\tau,\sigma}=1
.
Then the following conditions are equivalent:

1.

\varphi\in\mathcal{S}_{\{\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}\}}^{\{\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}\}}(\mathbb{R}^{n});

2.

(\exists\tau>0)(\exists C>0)\;(\forall\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n})

\sup_{x}|x^{\alpha}\varphi(x)|\leq CM^{\tau,\sigma}_{|\alpha|}\quad\text{and}\quad\sup_{x}|\partial^{\beta}\varphi(x)|\leq CM^{\tau,\sigma}_{|\beta|};

3.

(\exists\tau>0)(\exists C>0)\;(\forall\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n})

\sup_{x}|x^{\alpha}\varphi(x)|\leq CM^{\tau,\sigma}_{|\alpha|}\quad\text{and}\quad\sup_{\xi}|\xi^{\beta}\widehat{\varphi}(\xi)|\leq CM^{\tau,\sigma}_{|\beta|}.

Proof.

1.

\Rightarrow

2.
,
and
1.

\Rightarrow

3.
, follows immediately from Definition
3.1
and Theorem
4.1
.

2.

\Rightarrow

1.

By Theorem
3.5
, we can use
L^{2}
-norm instead of
L^{\infty}
-norm, cf. (
16
). Using integration by parts, the Leibniz formula and the Schwarz inequality we obtain that there exist
\tau>0
and
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\|x^{\alpha}\partial^{\beta}\varphi(x)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(x^{2\alpha}\partial^{\beta}\varphi(x))\partial^{\beta}\varphi(x)dx

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\gamma\leq\min\{2\alpha,\beta\}}\binom{\beta}{\gamma}\binom{2\alpha}{\gamma}\gamma!\|\partial^{2\beta-\gamma}\varphi(x)\|_{L^{2}}\|x^{2\alpha-\gamma}\varphi(x)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\gamma\leq\min\{2\alpha,\beta\}}\binom{\beta}{\gamma}\binom{2\alpha}{\gamma}\gamma!\,C\,M^{\tau,\sigma}_{|2\beta-\gamma|}\,C\,M^{\tau,\sigma}_{|2\alpha-\gamma|},\quad\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}.

Next, applying (
4

\displaystyle\|x^{\alpha}\partial^{\beta}\varphi(x)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C^{2}M^{\tau,\sigma}_{|2\alpha|}M^{\tau,\sigma}_{|2\beta|}\sum_{\gamma\leq\min\{2\alpha,\beta\}}\binom{\beta}{\gamma}\binom{2\alpha}{\gamma}\frac{\gamma!}{(M^{\tau,\sigma}_{|\gamma|})^{2}}.

Then we use (
31
), i.e.
\exists C^{\prime}>0
such that for any
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{\gamma\leq\min\{2\alpha,\beta\}}\binom{\beta}{\gamma}\binom{2\alpha}{\gamma}\frac{\gamma!}{(M^{\tau,\sigma}_{|\gamma|})^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}\sum_{\gamma\leq\min\{2\alpha,\beta\}}\binom{\beta}{\gamma}\binom{2\alpha}{\gamma}

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}2^{|2\alpha|+|\beta|}\leq C^{\prime}2^{|2\alpha|^{\sigma}+|2\beta|^{\sigma}},

so that

\displaystyle\|x^{\alpha}\partial^{\beta}\varphi(x)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C^{2}\,C^{\prime}2^{|2\alpha|^{\sigma}+|2\beta|^{\sigma}}\,M^{\tau,\sigma}_{|2\alpha|}M^{\tau,\sigma}_{|2\beta|},\quad\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}.

Now, using
\widetilde{(M.2)},
we obtain that there exists
A>0
such that

\displaystyle\|x^{\alpha}\partial^{\beta}\varphi(x)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C^{2}\,C^{\prime}2^{|2\alpha|^{\sigma}+|2\beta|^{\sigma}}\,A^{2|\alpha|^{\sigma}}(M_{|\alpha|}^{\tau 2^{\sigma-1},\sigma})^{2}A^{2|\beta|^{\sigma}}(M_{|\beta|}^{\tau 2^{\sigma-1},\sigma})^{2},

\displaystyle\leq C^{2}\,C^{\prime}(2A)^{|2\alpha|^{\sigma}+|2\beta|^{\sigma}}(M_{|\alpha|}^{\tau 2^{\sigma-1},\sigma})^{2}(M_{|\beta|}^{\tau 2^{\sigma-1},\sigma})^{2},\quad\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}.

Finally, by (
12
) there exists
C^{\prime\prime}>0
such that

\displaystyle\|x^{\alpha}\partial^{\beta}\varphi(x)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{C^{\prime}}C((2A)^{2^{\sigma-1}})^{|\alpha|^{\sigma}+|\beta|^{\sigma}}M_{|\alpha|}^{\tau 2^{\sigma-1},\sigma}M_{|\beta|}^{\tau 2^{\sigma-1},\sigma}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{C^{\prime}}CC^{\prime\prime}M_{|\alpha|}^{\tau 2^{\sigma},\sigma}M_{|\beta|}^{\tau 2^{\sigma},\sigma},\quad\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n},

which (with
\tilde{C}=\sqrt{C^{\prime}}CC^{\prime\prime}
and
\tilde{\tau}=\tau 2^{\sigma}
) implies (
16
).

It remains to prove
3.

\Rightarrow

2.
,

Let
\tau>0
and
C>0
be as in 3. Then for any
\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n},
the inequality
|\xi^{\beta}\widehat{\varphi}(\xi)|\leq CM_{|\beta|}^{\tau,\sigma},

\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n},
implies

|\widehat{\varphi}(\xi)|\leq Ce^{-T_{\tau,\sigma}(|\xi|)},\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

where
T_{\tau,\sigma}

is the associated function to
(M_{\alpha}^{\tau,\sigma})_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}}
, see
(
25
).

By using the fact that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}T_{\tau,\sigma}(|\xi|)}d\xi<\infty

(the integrand decreases faster than
|\xi|^{-p}
for any
p\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
), we get

\displaystyle|\partial^{\beta}\varphi(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|e^{2\pi ix\xi}\xi^{|\beta|}\widehat{\varphi}(\xi)|d\xi

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\xi|^{|\beta|}e^{-T_{\tau,\sigma}(|\xi|)}d\xi

\displaystyle\leq C\sup_{\xi}[|\xi|^{2|\beta|}e^{-T_{\tau,\sigma}(|\xi|)}]^{1/2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}T_{\tau,\sigma}(|\xi|)}d\xi

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}\sup_{\xi}[|\xi|^{2|\beta|}e^{-T_{\tau,\sigma}(|\xi|)}]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}[M_{|2\beta|}^{\tau,\sigma}]^{1/2},\quad\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}.

Finally,
\widetilde{(M.2)}
and (
12
) yields

\displaystyle|\partial^{\beta}\varphi(x)|

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}(A^{2|\beta|^{\sigma}}(M_{|\beta|}^{\tau 2^{\sigma-1},\sigma})^{2})^{1/2}=C^{\prime}A^{|\beta|^{\sigma}}M_{|\beta|}^{\tau 2^{\sigma-1},\sigma}

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}C^{\prime\prime}M_{|\beta|}^{\tau 2^{\sigma},\sigma},\quad\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n},

which implies
2.
(with
\tilde{C}=C^{\prime}C^{\prime\prime}
and
\tilde{\tau}=\tau 2^{\sigma}
).
∎

5
Time-frequency representations

The Fourier transform of
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
provides information about its global frequency content. Different representations of
f
in phase space or time-frequency plane are used to obtain a localized version of the Fourier transform. Notable examples are the short-time Fourier transform, the Wigner transform and Cohen’s class representations such as the Born-Jordan transform.

In this section we turn our attention to the Grossmann-Royer operators which originated from the problem of the
physical interpretation of the Wigner distribution, see
[
17
,
28
]
.
It tuned out that the Grossmann-Royer operators
are closely related to the Heisenberg operators, well-known objects from quantum mechanics, see
[
11
,
12
]
.

The Grossmann-Royer operator
R:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})
is given by

Rf(x,\omega)=R(f(t))(x,\omega)=e^{4\pi i\omega(t-x)}f(2x-t),\;\;\;f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\;x,\omega\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(32)

We define the Grossmann-Royer transform
R_{g}f

as the weak sense interpretation of the Grossmann-Royer operator.
The Grossmann-Royer transform is essentially the cross-Wigner distribution
W(f,g)

(see Definition
5.1
, Lemma
5.2

and
[
12
, Definition 12]
), and is also closely related to
V_{g}f
and
A(f,g),
the short-time Fourier transform and the cross-ambiguity transform respectively, see Definition
5.1
.

Definition 5.1
.

Let there be given
f,g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).
The
Grossmann-Royer transform of
f
and
g
is given by

R_{g}f(x,\omega)=R(f,g)(x,\omega)=\langle Rf,g\rangle=\int e^{4\pi i\omega(t-x)}f(2x-t)\overline{g(t)}dt,\;\;\;x,\omega\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(33)

The short-time Fourier transform of

f
with respect to the window
g
is given by

V_{g}f(x,\omega)=\int e^{-2\pi it\omega}f(t)\overline{g(t-x)}dt,\;\;\;x,\omega\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(34)

The cross–Wigner distribution of
f
and
g
is

W(f,g)(x,\omega)=\int e^{-2\pi i\omega t}f(x+\frac{t}{2})\overline{g(x-\frac{t}{2})}dt,\;\;\;x,\omega\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

(35)

and the cross–ambiguity function of
f
and
g
is

A(f,g)(x,\omega)=\int e^{-2\pi i\omega t}f(t+\frac{x}{2})\overline{g(t-\frac{x}{2})}dt,\;\;\;x,\omega\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(36)

The transforms in Definition
5.1
are called
time-frequency representations and we set

TFR=\{R_{g}f,V_{g}f,W(f,g),A(f,g)\}.

Note that
R(f(t))(x,\omega)=e^{-4\pi i\omega x}M_{2\omega}(T_{2x}f)^{\check{}}(t)
so that

R_{g}f(x,\omega)=e^{-4\pi i\omega x}\langle M_{2\omega}(T_{2x}f)^{\check{}},g\rangle,

(37)

where translation and modulation operators are respectively given by

T_{x}f(\cdot)=f(\cdot-x)\;\;\;\mbox{ and }\;\;\;M_{x}f(\cdot)=e^{2\pi ix\cdot}f(\cdot),\;\;\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

and
\check{f}
denotes the reflection
\check{f}(x)=f(-x)
.

By using appropriate change of variables the following relations between the time-frequency representations can be obtained (cf.
[
30
]
).

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
f,g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).
Then we have:

\displaystyle W(f,g)(x,\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{n}R_{g}f(x,\omega),

\displaystyle V_{g}f(x,\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-\pi ix\omega}R_{\check{g}}f\left(\frac{x}{2},\frac{\omega}{2}\right),

\displaystyle A(f,g)(x,\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R_{\check{g}}f\left(\frac{x}{2},\frac{\omega}{2}\right),\quad x,\omega\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

The Grossmann-Royer operator is self-adjoint, uniformly continuous on

\mathbb{R}^{2n}
, and the following properties hold:

1.

\|R_{g}f\|_{\infty}\leq\|f\|\|g\|
;

2.

R_{g}f=\overline{R_{f}g}
;

3.

R_{\hat{g}}\hat{f}(x,\omega)=R_{g}f(-\omega,x),

x,\omega\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
;

4.

For
f_{1},f_{2},g_{1},g_{2}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),
the Moyal identity holds:

\langle R_{g_{1}}f_{1},R_{g_{2}}f_{2}\rangle=\langle f_{1},f_{2}\rangle\overline{\langle g_{1},g_{2}\rangle}.

The Moyal identity formula implies the inversion formula:

f=\frac{1}{\langle g_{2},g_{1}\rangle}\iint R_{g_{1}}f(x,\omega)Rg_{2}(x,\omega)\,dxd\omega.

(38)

We refer to
[
15
]
for details (explained in terms of the short-time Fourier transform).

Clearly, a smooth function
F
belongs to

\mathcal{S}_{(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma})}^{(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma})}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})
if

(\forall\tau>0)(\exists C>0)\quad\sup_{x,\omega}|x^{\alpha_{1}}\omega^{\alpha_{2}}\partial_{x}^{\beta_{1}}\partial_{\omega}^{\beta_{2}}F(x,\omega)|\leq CM^{\tau,\sigma}_{|\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}|}M^{\tau,\sigma}_{|\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}|},

(39)

for all
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
,
and similarly for
\mathcal{S}_{\{\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}\}}^{\{\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}\}}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})
.

If
F\in\mathcal{S}_{[\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}]}^{[\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}]}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})
, then by calculations in Section
4
it follows that
\mathcal{F}_{1}F,\mathcal{F}_{2}F\in\mathcal{S}_{[\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}]}^{[\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}]}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})
, where
\mathcal{F}_{1}
and

\mathcal{F}_{2}
denote partial Fourier transforms with respect to
x
and
\omega,
respectively.

The following theorem, in the context of classical Gelfand-Shilov spaces, is considered folklore (see, for example,
[
16
,
29
,
30
,
33
]
).

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
\sigma>1
, and let the weight matrix
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}
be given by
(
3
),

M_{p}^{\tau,\sigma}=p^{\tau p^{\sigma}}
,
p\in\mathbb{N}
,
M_{0}^{\tau,\sigma}=1
.
If
f,g\in\mathcal{S}_{[\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}]}^{[\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}]}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
and
T\in TFA
, then
T\in\mathcal{S}_{[\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}]}^{[\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}]}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})
.

Conversely, if
T\in TFA
and
T\in\mathcal{S}_{[\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}]}^{[\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}]}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})

then
f,g\in\mathcal{S}_{[\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}]}^{[\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}]}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{S}_{[\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}]}^{[\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}]}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

are closed under reflections, dilations and modulations, then by Lemma
5.2

it suffices to give the proof for any
T\in TFA
.
We will give the proof for the Grossmann-Royer transform, and the same conclusion holds for other time-frequency representations in question.

Put
\Phi(x,t):=f(2x-t)g(t)
,
x,t\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Then

R_{g}f(x,\omega)=e^{-4\pi i\omega x}\int e^{2\pi i\omega(2t)}\Phi(x,t)\,dt.

The first part of the claim will be proved
if we show that

\Phi\in\mathcal{S}_{[\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}]}^{[\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}]}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})
,
since
\mathcal{S}_{[\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}]}^{[\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}]}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})
is closed under dilations and modulations,
and since then
\mathcal{F}_{2}\Phi\in\mathcal{S}_{[\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}]}^{[\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}]}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})
.

As before, we give the proof for the Beurling case, and the
Roumieu case follows by similar arguments.

By Theorem
4.3
it is enough to show

\sup_{x,t\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x^{\alpha}t^{\beta}\Phi(x,t)|\leq CM^{\tau,\sigma}_{|\alpha|}M^{\tau,\sigma}_{|\beta|},\quad\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n},

(40)

and

\sup_{x,t\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\partial^{\beta}_{t}\Phi(x,t)|\leq CM^{\tau,\sigma}_{|\alpha|}M^{\tau,\sigma}_{|\beta|},\quad\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n},

(41)

for any given
\tau>0
, and some constant
C=C_{\tau}>0
.

The decay of
\Phi(x,t):=f(2x-t)g(t)
can be estimated as follows.

\displaystyle\sup_{x,t\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x^{\alpha}t^{\beta}f(2x-t)g(t)|

\displaystyle\leq 2^{-|\alpha|}\sup_{y,t\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}|y+t|^{|\alpha|}|t|^{|\beta|}|f(y)||g(t)|

\displaystyle\leq C2^{-|\alpha|}\sup_{y,t\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\sum_{\gamma\leq\alpha}\binom{\alpha}{\gamma}|y|^{|\alpha|}|t|^{|\alpha-\gamma|+|\beta|}|f(y)||g(t)|

\displaystyle\leq C\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}|y|^{|\alpha|}|f(y)|\cdot\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}|t|^{|\alpha|+|\beta|}|g(t)|

\displaystyle\leq CM^{\tau/2,\sigma}_{|\alpha|}M^{\tau/2,\sigma}_{|\alpha|+|\beta|}

\displaystyle\leq CM^{\tau,\sigma}_{|\alpha|+|\beta|},

(42)

see also Remark
3.4
.

For the proof of (
41
), we use the Leibniz formula
and properties of the sequence
(M_{p}^{\tau,\sigma})_{p\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
:

\displaystyle\sup_{x,t\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\partial^{\beta}_{t}f(2x-t)g(t)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{|\alpha|}\sum_{\gamma\leq\beta}\binom{\beta}{\gamma}\sup_{x,t\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\partial^{\gamma}_{t}f(2x-t)\partial^{\beta-\gamma}_{t}g(t)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{|\alpha|}\sum_{\gamma\leq\beta}\binom{\beta}{\gamma}\sup_{x,t\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\partial^{\gamma}_{t}f(2x-t)|\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\partial^{\beta-\gamma}_{t}g(t)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{|\alpha|+|\beta|}CM^{\tau/4,\sigma}_{|\alpha|+|\beta|}M^{\tau/4,\sigma}_{|\beta|}\leq C_{1}M^{\tau,\sigma}_{|\alpha|+|\beta|},

for some constants
C,C_{1}>0
.

Thus
R_{g}f\in\mathcal{S}_{(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma})}^{(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma})}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})
if
f,g\in\mathcal{S}_{(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma})}^{(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma})}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Now, assume that

R_{g}f\in\mathcal{S}_{(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma})}^{(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma})}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})

(43)

for a given
g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})\setminus\{0\}
.

By the inversion formula (
38
)
we have

t^{\alpha}\partial^{\beta}_{t}f(t)=\frac{1}{\langle h,g\rangle}\iint R_{g}f(x,\omega)t^{\alpha}\partial^{\beta}_{t}(R(h(t))(x,\omega)\,dxd\omega,

(44)

where we may choose
h\in\mathcal{S}_{(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma})}^{(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma})}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that

\langle h,g\rangle=1
.

Let there be given
\tau>0
.

From (
43
) we get

\displaystyle|t^{\alpha}\partial^{\beta}_{t}(R(h(t))(x,\omega)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|t^{\alpha}\partial^{\beta}_{t}(e^{4\pi i\omega(t-x)}h(2x-t))|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\sum_{\gamma\leq\beta}\binom{\beta}{\gamma}|\omega^{\beta-\gamma}||t^{\alpha}\partial^{\gamma}_{t}h(2x-t))|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle CM^{\tau/2,\sigma}_{|\alpha|}M^{\tau/2,\sigma}_{|\beta|}\sum_{\gamma\leq\beta}\binom{\beta}{\gamma}|\omega^{\beta-\gamma}|,

wherefrom

|t^{\alpha}\partial^{\beta}_{t}f(t)|\leq CM^{\tau/2,\sigma}_{|\alpha|}M^{\tau/2,\sigma}_{|\beta|}\sum_{\gamma\leq\beta}\binom{\beta}{\gamma}\iint|\omega^{\beta-\gamma}R_{g}f(x,\omega)|dxd\omega.

(45)

Next we estimate the double integral in (
45
). We use similar argument as in the proof of Theorem
3.5
(cf. (
23
)):

\displaystyle\iint|\omega^{\beta-\gamma}R_{g}f(x,\omega)

\displaystyle|dxd\omega

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\sup_{x,\omega\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}(1+|x|)^{2}(1+|\omega|)^{2+|\beta-\gamma|}|R_{g}f(x,\omega)|

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}M^{\tau/4,\sigma}_{2}M^{\tau/4,\sigma}_{2+|\beta-\gamma|}\leq C_{3}M^{\tau/4,\sigma}_{2+|\beta|}

\displaystyle\leq C_{4}^{(2^{\sigma-1}+1)|\beta|^{\sigma}}M^{\tau/4,\sigma}_{|\beta|}

for some constants
C_{j}>0,

j=1,2,3,4
, wherefrom

\sum_{\gamma\leq\beta}\binom{\beta}{\gamma}\iint|\omega^{\beta-\gamma}R_{g}f(x,\omega)|dxd\omega\leq C_{5}^{(2^{\sigma-1}+1)|\beta|^{\sigma}}M^{\tau/4,\sigma}_{|\beta|}\leq CM^{\tau/2,\sigma}_{|\beta|},

(46)

for some constants
C,C_{5}>0
.
By (
45
) and
(
46
) we get

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}|t^{\alpha}\partial^{\beta}_{t}f(t)|\leq CM^{\tau/2,\sigma}_{|\alpha|}M^{\tau/2,\sigma}_{|\beta|}M^{\tau/2,\sigma}_{|\beta|}\leq CM^{\tau,\sigma}_{|\alpha|}M^{\tau,\sigma}_{|\beta|},

i.e.
f\in\mathcal{S}_{(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma})}^{(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma})}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Finally,
g\in\mathcal{S}_{(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma})}^{(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma})}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

follows from

R_{g}f=\overline{R_{f}g}
.
∎

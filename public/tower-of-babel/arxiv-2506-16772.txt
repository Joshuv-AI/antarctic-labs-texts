Abstract
. In this paper, we introduce a notion of expansion for groupoids, which recovers the classical notion of expander graphs by a family of pair groupoids and expanding actions in measure by transformation groupoids. We also consider an asymptotic version for expansion and establish structural theorems, showing that asymptotic expansion can be approximated by domains of expansions. On the other hand, we introduce dynamical propagation and quasi-locality for operators on groupoids and the associated Roe type algebras. Our main results characterise when these algebras possess block-rank-one projections by means of asymptotic expansion, which generalises the crucial ingredients in previous works to provide counterexamples to the coarse Baum-Connes conjecture.



Keywords: Groupoid, Expansion and asymptotic expansion, Structural theorems, Dynamical Roe algebras, Dynamical quasi-local algebras.

1.
Introduction

Over the last few decades, the phenomenon of expansion has been discovered and extensively studied across various branches of mathematics. For instance in graph theory, the expansion phenomenon leads to the notion of expander graphs, which plays an important role not only in pure and applied mathematics but also in theoretical computer science (see the excellent survey article
[
24
]
and the references therein). In dynamical systems, the expansion phenomenon leads to the notion of expanding actions in measure, which turns out to be equivalent to the classic notion of spectral gap for measure-preserving actions (see
[
32
]
) and numerous examples have been discovered (
e.g.
,
[
5
,
6
,
13
]
).

Recently, an asymptotic version of expansion was introduced in different areas of mathematics (
[
20
,
21
]
), which is more stable under small perturbations and hence leads to important applications in operator algebras and higher index theory (
[
17
,
22
,
23
]
). A crucial step therein is structural type theorems, showing that objects with asymptotic expansion can be approximated by those with expansion. In dynamical systems, asymptotic expansion also provides a new quantitative viewpoint on the classic notion of strong ergodicity, which was introduced in
[
8
,
29
,
30
]
in relation with Ruziewicz problem, Kazhdan’s Property (T) and amenability.

In this paper, we aim to generalise and unify the theory of asymptotic expansion from different areas (including graph theory and dynamical systems) in the language of groupoids. Groupoids provide a framework encompassing both groups and spaces. They arise naturally in a variety of research areas such as dynamical systems, topology and geometry, geometric group theory and operator algebras, building bridges between all these areas of mathematics (see,
e.g.
,
[
7
,
14
]
).

To achieve this, we introduce the notion of expansion and asymptotic expansion for groupoids, generalising both (asymptotic) expander graphs and (asymptotically) expanding actions in measure, and establish structural type theorems in the groupoid setting. Furthermore, we introduce two classes of operator algebras associated to the dynamics of groupoids, generalising the classical Roe and quasi-local algebras from higher index theory. Our main results show that the existence of certain projection operators in these operators algebras characterise asymptotic expansion of groupoids, which provide a unified approach to results in
[
17
,
22
,
23
]
and allow a boarder range of examples and applications.

We will now give a more detailed overview of this work.

1.1.
Expansion and asymptotic expansion

To motivate our notion of asymptotic expansion, let us first recall the notion of asymptotically expanding actions in measure from
[
21
]
.

Let
\rho:\Gamma\curvearrowright X
be a measure-class-preserving action of a countable group
\Gamma
acting on a probability space
(X,\mu)
, and
\ell_{\Gamma}
be a proper length function on
\Gamma
(
i.e.
, for each
L>0
, the ball
B_{L}\coloneqq\{\gamma\in\Gamma~|~\ell_{\Gamma}(\gamma)\leq L\}
is finite). The action is called
asymptotically expanding in measure
\mu
if for any
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
there exists
C_{\alpha},L_{\alpha}>0
such that for any measurable
A\subseteq X
with
\alpha\leq\mu(A)\leq\frac{1}{2}
, we have
\mu(B_{L_{\alpha}}\cdot A)>(1+C_{\alpha})\mu(A)
. When the functions
\alpha\mapsto L_{\alpha}
and
\alpha\mapsto C_{\alpha}
can taken to be constant functions, then the action is called
expanding in measure
\mu
.

To generalise the above to the setting of groupoids (see Section
2.1
for basic notions of groupoids), we consider the transformation groupoid. Recall that for the action
\rho:\Gamma\curvearrowright X
, the
transformation groupoid

X\rtimes\Gamma
has source
\mathrm{s}(x,\gamma)=\gamma^{-1}x
, range
\mathrm{r}(x,\gamma)=x
and inverse
(x,\gamma)^{-1}=(\gamma^{-1}x,\gamma^{-1})
. The length function
\ell_{\Gamma}
naturally gives rise to a length function
\ell
on
X\rtimes\Gamma
by
\ell(x,\gamma)\coloneqq\ell_{\Gamma}(\gamma)
for
x\in X
and
\gamma\in\Gamma
.

This leads to the following setting of our paper: Let
\mathcal{G}
be a groupoid with a length function
\ell
(see Definition
2.1
), and the unit space
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
is equipped with a measure
\mu
on some
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{R}
. To abstract measure-class-preserving transformations, we introduce the following: A bisection
K\subseteq\mathcal{G}
is called
admissible
if its source
\mathrm{s}(K)
and range
\mathrm{r}(K)
are measurable, the induced bijection

(1.1)

\tau_{K}\coloneqq\mathrm{r}|_{K}\circ(\mathrm{s}|_{K})^{-1}:\mathrm{s}(K)\rightarrow\mathrm{r}(K)

is a measure-class-preserving measurable isomorphism and the length
\ell(K)\coloneqq\sup\{\ell(x)~|~x\in K\}
is finite. Moreover, a subset
K\subseteq\mathcal{G}
is called
decomposable
if
K=\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}K_{i}
for
N\in\mathbb{N}
and admissible bisections
K_{i}
. Note that for transformation groupoids, any measurable subset (with respect to the product structure) with finite length is decomposable, while unfortunately this does not hold in general.

Now we are in the position to introduce our notion of asymptotic expansion:

Definition A

(Definition
3.1
and
3.2
)
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a groupoid with a length function
\ell
, and
(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},\mathcal{R},\mu)
be a probability measure space. We say that
\mathcal{G}
is
asymptotically expanding (in measure
\mu
)
if for any
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
, there exists a decomposable
K_{\alpha}\subseteq\mathcal{G}
with
\ell(K_{\alpha})<\infty
such that for any
A\in\mathcal{R}
with
\alpha\leq\mu(A)\leq\frac{1}{2}
, then
\mu(\mathrm{r}(K_{\alpha}\cdot A)\setminus A)>C_{\alpha}\mu(A)
. If
K_{\alpha}\equiv K
, then we say that
\mathcal{G}
is
expanding (in measure
\mu
)
.

It is clear that in the case of transformation groupoids, this recovers the notion of (asymptotically) expanding actions (see Section
6.1
). On the other hand, when considering pair groupoids, a family version of Definition
A
recovers the notion of (asymptotic) expander graphs (see Section
6.2
for details).

We establish the following structure theorem for asymptotic expansion, showing that it can be approximated by domains of expansion:

Theorem B

(Theorem
3.10
and
3.19
)
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a groupoid with a length function
\ell
, and
(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},\mathcal{R},\mu)
be a probability measure space. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

\mathcal{G}
is asymptotically expanding in measure.

(2)

\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
admits an exhaustion by domains of expansion with bounded ratio.

(3)

\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
admits an exhaustion by domains of Markov expansion with bounded ratio.

Roughly speaking, here “domain of expansion” is a measurable subset
Y
of
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
such that the reduction
\mathcal{G}^{Y}_{Y}
is expanding (see Definition
3.3
and Remark
3.4
), and “exhaustion” means a sequence of domains
Y_{n}
such that
\mu(\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\setminus Y_{n})\to 0
. Here we also construct a Markov kernel for each domain (see Definition
3.15
) and consider a Markovian version of expansion, which will play a key role later to produce certain projection operators. Details can be found in Section
3.2
and
3.3
.

Theorem
B
provides a unified approach to recover the structure results in
[
17
,
22
,
23
]
, and is more general to allow other types of examples (see Section
6
). Even for asymptotic expander graphs and asymptotically expanding actions, our proof simplifies the original ones in a systematic way.

1.2.
Dynamical propagation and quasi-locality

Now we introduce two classes of operator algebras associated to groupoids, which encode dynamical information and have their root in higher index theory.

Recall that for a metric space, there are two associated operator algebras, called the Roe algebra and the quasi-local algebra. They were essentially introduced by Roe in his pioneering work on higher index theory
[
27
]
, where he discovered that higher indices of elliptic differential operators on open manifolds belong to
K
-theories of the Roe algebra. More recently, Engel showed in
[
10
,
11
]
that
K
-theories of the quasi-local algebra contains higher indices of more general elliptic pseudo-differential operators on open manifolds.

To compute their
K
-theories, a programmatic and practical approach is to consult the coarse Baum-Connes conjecture
[
4
]
, a central conjecture in higher index theory and closely related to other conjectures like the Novikov conjecture and the Gromov-Lawson conjecture. Unfortunately, counterexamples were discovered in
[
15
,
17
,
22
,
28
]
using (asymptotic) expanders and warped cones of (asymptotically) expanding actions.
Due to their importance, Roe and quasi-local algebras have been extensively studied and several variants have also been introduced (see
[
3
,
9
,
16
,
22
,
25
,
31
,
35
]
and a recent excellent monograph
[
34
]
).

Inspired by these works, we introduce the following notions of dynamical propagation and quasi-locality for operators on groupoids. Note that in the context of transformation groupoids and pair groupoids, the following recovers the original (dynamical) Roe and quasi-local algebras (see Section
6.1
and
6.2
).

Definition C

(Definition
4.1
and
4.2
)
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a groupoid with a length function
\ell
, and
(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},\mathcal{R},\mu)
be a measure space. For an operator
T\in\mathfrak{B}(L^{2}(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},\mu))
, we say:

(1)

T
has finite
dynamical propagation
if there exists a decomposable
K\subseteq\mathcal{G}
with
\ell(K)<\infty
such that for any
A,B\in\mathcal{R}
with
\mu(\mathrm{r}(K\cdot A)\cap B)=0
, then
\chi_{A}T\chi_{B}=0
;

(2)

T
is
dynamically quasi-local
if for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists a decomposable
K_{\epsilon}\subseteq\mathcal{G}
with
\ell(K_{\epsilon})<\infty
such that for any
A,B\in\mathcal{R}
with
\mu(\mathrm{r}(K_{\epsilon}\cdot A)\cap B)=0
, then
\|\chi_{A}T\chi_{B}\|<\epsilon
.

The
dynamical Roe algebra of
\mathcal{G}
is the norm closure of all operators with finite dynamical propagation, denoted by
\mathbf{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{dyn}}(\mathcal{G})
. The
dynamical quasi-local algebra of
\mathcal{G}
is the set of all dynamically quasi-local operators, denoted by
\mathbf{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{dyn},\mathrm{q}}(\mathcal{G})
.

Recall that in graph theory and dynamical systems, a key consequence of asymptotic expansion is that the associated Roe type algebras possess block-rank-one projections (called the ghost projections), which is crucial to provide counterexamples to the coarse Baum-Connes conjecture (see
[
17
,
22
,
23
]
). In the current context of groupoids, we study dynamical propagation and quasi-locality of block-rank-one projections and establish a more general result, which unifies and simplifies the previous ones.

More precisely, we first consider a rank-one projection
P\in\mathfrak{B}(L^{2}(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},\mu))
and a unit vector
\xi\in L^{2}(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},\mu)
in the range of
P
. This induces a probability measure
\nu
on
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
given by
\mathrm{d}\nu(x)\coloneqq|\xi(x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mu(x)
for
x\in\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
, called the
associated measure
.

The following is our main result:

Theorem D

(Theorem
5.3
)
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a groupoid with a length function
\ell
, and
(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},\mathcal{R},\mu)
be a measure space. Let
P\in\mathfrak{B}(L^{2}(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},\mu))
be a rank-one projection, and
\nu
the associated probability measure on
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

P\in\mathbf{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{dyn}}(\mathcal{G})
;

(2)

P\in\mathbf{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{dyn},\mathrm{q}}(\mathcal{G})
;

(3)

\mathcal{G}
is asymptotically expanding in measure
\nu
.

The proof of Theorem
D
is quite involved, similar to the outline in the context of graph theory and dynamical systems. Roughly speaking, we firstly reduce to the case of averaging projection by changing measures, and prove that this is equivalent to asymptotic expansion. Then applying the structure theorem (Theorem
B
), we obtain an exhaustion by domain of Markov expansion. Thanks to the theory of Markov kernels, on each domain we obtain a rank-one projection in the dynamical Roe algebra by functional calculus. Finally, these projections converge in norm to the averaging projection, which concludes the proof.

After establishing Theorem
D
, we further consider a family version by chasing parameters. Then we obtain a family version of Theorem
D
(Theorem
5.3’
), which characterises when the dynamical Roe and quasi-local algebras possess block-rank-one projections. This recovers the most technical results in
[
17
,
22
,
23
]
, and our proof simplifies the original ones in a systematic way.

Finally, we apply our results to several examples, including the known results on asymptotic expanders and asymptotically expanding actions. Moreover, we study new examples including groupoid actions on fibred spaces, the HLS groupoid and graph groupoids, and establish corresponding results therein.

Organization

The paper is organised as follows. In Section

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall the notions of groupoids and Markov kernels.

2.1.
Basic notions for groupoids

Recall that a
groupoid

\mathcal{G}
is a small category consists of a set
\mathcal{G}
, a subset
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
called the
unit space
, two maps
\mathrm{s},\mathrm{r}:\mathcal{G}\rightarrow\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
called the
source
and the
range
maps, respectively, a composition law
(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\mapsto\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}
for
(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\in\mathcal{G}^{(2)}\coloneqq\{(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\in\mathcal{G}\times\mathcal{G}~|~\mathrm{s}(\gamma_{1})=\mathrm{r}(\gamma_{2})\}
and an inverse map
\gamma\mapsto\gamma^{-1}
. There operations satisfy a couple of axioms, including the associativity law and the fact that
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
act as units.
For
A,B\subseteq\mathcal{G}
, we denote

•

A^{-1}\coloneqq\{\gamma^{-1}~|~\gamma\in A\}
,

•

A\cdot B\coloneqq\{\gamma\in\mathcal{G}~|~\gamma=\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}~\text{where}~\gamma_{1}\in A,\gamma_{2}\in B\text{ with }\mathrm{s}(\gamma_{1})=\mathrm{r}(\gamma_{2})\}
.

We say that
A
is
symmetric
if
A=A^{-1}
, and
unital
if
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\subseteq A
. A subset
A\subseteq\mathcal{G}
is called a
bisection
if the restrictions of
\mathrm{s},\mathrm{r}
to
A
are injective and recall from (
1.1
) that we have the induced bijection
\tau_{A}\coloneqq\mathrm{r}|_{A}\circ(\mathrm{s}|_{A})^{-1}:\mathrm{s}(A)\rightarrow\mathrm{r}(A)
.

We also need the notion of length functions on groupoids:

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a groupoid. A
length function
on
\mathcal{G}
is a map
\ell:\mathcal{G}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
such that
\ell(\mathcal{G}^{(0)})=\{0\}
and

(1)

\ell(\gamma^{-1})=\ell(\gamma)
for all
\gamma\in\mathcal{G}
;

(2)

\ell(\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2})\leq\ell(\gamma_{1})+\ell(\gamma_{2})
for all
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\mathcal{G}
with
\mathrm{s}(\gamma_{1})=\mathrm{r}(\gamma_{2})
.

Given a length function
\ell
on a groupoid
\mathcal{G}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, denote the closed ball

(2.1)

B_{n}\coloneqq\{\gamma\in\mathcal{G}~|~\ell(\gamma)\leq n\}.

Clearly
\{B_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of increasing subsets in
\mathcal{G}
satisfying:

•

\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\subseteq B_{0}
and
B_{n}
is symmetric for
n\in\mathbb{N}
;

•

B_{n}\cdot B_{m}\subseteq B_{n+m}
for
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
;

•

\mathcal{G}=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}B_{n}
.

Conversely, given an increasing sequence
\{B_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of subsets in
\mathcal{G}
satisfying the three conditions above, we can construct a function
\tilde{\ell}
on
\mathcal{G}
by

(2.2)

\tilde{\ell}(\gamma)\coloneqq\inf\left\{n\in\mathbb{N}~|~\gamma\in B_{n}\right\}\quad\text{for}\quad\gamma\in\mathcal{G}.

It is routine to check that
\tilde{\ell}
is a length function on
\mathcal{G}
. Moreover, it is easy to see:

Lemma 2.2
.

Given a length function
\ell
on a groupoid
\mathcal{G}
, let
\{B_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be the sequence from Equation (
2.1
) and define the length function
\tilde{\ell}
on
\mathcal{G}
using (
2.2
). Then
\ell
and
\tilde{\ell}
are
coarsely equivalent
in the sense that for any
r>0
, we have:

\sup\{\tilde{\ell}(\gamma)~|~\ell(\gamma)\leq r\}<\infty\quad\text{and}\quad\sup\{\ell(\gamma)~|~\tilde{\ell}(\gamma)\leq r\}<\infty.

Throughout the paper, let
\mathcal{G}
be a groupoid with length function
\ell
, and
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
equipped with a (not necessarily finite) measure
\mu
on some
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{R}
.
Motivated by the case of group actions as explained in Section
1
, we introduce the following:

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a groupoid with a length function
\ell
, and
(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},\mathcal{R},\mu)
be a measure space. A bisection
K\subseteq\mathcal{G}
is called
admissible
if
\mathrm{s}(K),\mathrm{r}(K)\in\mathcal{R}
,
\tau_{K}:\mathrm{s}(K)\rightarrow\mathrm{r}(K)
is a measure-class-preserving measurable isomorphism, and
\ell(K)\coloneqq\sup\{\ell(x)~|~x\in K\}
is finite.

We record the following elementary observation, whose proof is straightforward and hence omitted.

Lemma 2.4
.

If
K,K_{1},K_{2}\subseteq\mathcal{G}
are admissible bisections, then
K^{-1}
and
K_{1}\cdot K_{2}
are also admissible bisections.

Based on Definition
2.3
, we further introduce the following, which is also motivated from the case of group actions as explained in Section
1
.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a groupoid with a length function
\ell
, and
(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},\mathcal{R},\mu)
be a measure space. A subset
K\subseteq\mathcal{G}
is called
decomposable
if
K=\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}K_{i}
for
N\in\mathbb{N}
and admissible bisections
K_{i}
.

Given a unital symmetric decomposable
K\subseteq\mathcal{G}
, a decomposition
K=\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}K_{i}
is called
unital
if
K_{i}=\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
for some
i
and
symmetric
if there exists a bijection
\sigma:\{1,2,\cdots,N\}\to\{1,2,\cdots,N\}
such that
K_{i}^{-1}=K_{\sigma(i)}
. We say that
K
is
N
-decomposable
if there exists a unital symmetric decomposition
K=\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}K_{i}
for admissible
K_{i}
.

Applying Lemma
2.4
, we obtain the following:

Lemma 2.6
.

(1)

If
K
is
N
-decomposable with
\ell(K)\leq L
, then
K^{-1}
is
N
-decomposable with
\ell(K^{-1})\leq L
.

(2)

If
K_{i}
is
N_{i}
-decomposable with
\ell(K_{i})\leq L_{i}
for
i=1,2
, then
K_{1}\cup K_{2}
is
(N_{1}+N_{2})
-decomposable with
\ell(K_{1}\cup K_{2})\leq\max\{L_{1},L_{2}\}
and
K_{1}\cdot K_{2}
is
N_{1}N_{2}
-decomposable with
\ell(K_{1}\cdot K_{2})\leq L_{1}+L_{2}
.

The following easy observation is useful to define (asymptotic) expansion for groupoids later.

Lemma 2.7
.

If
K\subseteq\mathcal{G}
is decomposable and
A\subseteq\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
is measurable, then
\mathrm{r}(K\cdot A)
is measurable.

Proof.

By definition, we can write
K=\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}K_{i}
for admissible bisection
K_{i}
. Then:

\mathrm{r}(K\cdot A)=\mathrm{r}\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}K_{i}\cdot A\right)=\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}\tau_{K_{i}}(A\cap\mathrm{s}(K_{i})),

which is measurable since each
\tau_{K_{i}}
is a measurable isomorphism on
\mathrm{s}(K_{i})
.
∎

Finally, note that the notion of admissible bisections and decomposability remain the same if we change the length function up to coarse equivalence.

2.2.
Markov kernel

Here we recall a few elementary properties of reversible Markov kernels, which will be used later to establish our main results. We refer to the first chapters of
[
26
]
for more background and details.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a
\sigma
-algebra on a set
X
. A
Markov kernel
on the measurable space
(X,\mathcal{S})
is a function
\Pi\colon X\times\mathcal{S}\to[0,1]
such that:

(1)

for every
x\in X
, the function
\Pi(x,\,\operatorname{-}\,)\colon\mathcal{S}\to[0,1]
is a probability measure;

(2)

for every
A\in\mathcal{S}
, the function
\Pi(\,\operatorname{-}\,,A)\colon X\to[0,1]
is
\mathcal{S}
-measurable.

The associated
Markov operator

\mathfrak{P}
is a linear operator on the space of bounded
\mathcal{S}
-measurable functions, defined by

(2.3)

\mathfrak{P}f(x)\coloneqq\int_{X}f(y)\Pi(x,\mathrm{d}y).

Definition 2.9
.

Given a measure
\mu
on
(X,\mathcal{S})
and
A\in\mathcal{S}
, the
(
\mu
-)size of the boundary
of
A
(with respect to
\Pi
) is defined as

|\partial_{\Pi}(A)|_{\mu}\coloneqq\int_{A}\Pi(x,X\setminus A)\mathrm{d}\mu(x).

We are only interested in the special case of reversible Markov kernel:

Definition 2.10
.

A Markov kernel
\Pi
is called
reversible
if there exists a measure
m
on
(X,\mathcal{S})
such that

\int_{X}f(x)\mathfrak{P}g(x)\mathrm{d}m(x)=\int_{X}\mathfrak{P}f(x)g(x)\mathrm{d}m(x)

for every pair of measurable bounded functions
f,g:X\to\mathbb{R}
. The measure
m
is called a
reversing measure
for
\Pi
(note that
m
need not be unique in general). In this case, we also say that
\Pi
is a reversible Markov kernel
on
(X,m)
.

Given a reversing measure
m
on
X
,
the Markov operator
\mathfrak{P}
can be regarded as a bounded self-adjoint operator on
L^{2}(X,m)
with norm
\|\mathfrak{P}\|\leq 1
.
Define the
Laplacian
of
\Pi
as
\Delta\coloneqq 1-\mathfrak{P}
, which is a positive self-adjoint operator whose spectrum is contained in
[0,2]
.

Let
\Pi
be a reversible Markov kernel on
(X,m)
, where
m
is a finite measure. Then all constant functions on
X
belong to
L^{2}(X,m)
and are fixed by
\mathfrak{P}
. It follows that
\|\mathfrak{P}\|=1
and
1
belongs to the spectrum of
\mathfrak{P}
. Denote the orthogonal complement of the constant functions in
L^{2}(X,m)
by
L^{2}_{0}(X,m)
,
i.e.
,

L^{2}_{0}(X,m)\coloneqq\left\{f\in L^{2}(X,m)~\middle|~\int_{X}f(x)\mathrm{d}m(x)=0\right\}.

Note that
L^{2}_{0}(X,m)
is
\mathfrak{P}
-invariant and that the spectrum of the restriction of
\mathfrak{P}
on
L^{2}_{0}(X,m)
is contained in
[-1,1]
. We denote the supremum of this spectrum by
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
. We make the following definition:

Definition 2.11
.

A reversible Markov kernel on a finite measure space
(X,m)
is said to have a
spectral gap
if
\lambda<1
.

On the other hand, we recall the notion of Cheeger constant as follows:

Definition 2.12
.

The
Cheeger constant
for a reversible Markov kernel
\Pi
on a finite measure space
(X,m)
is defined to be

\kappa\coloneqq\inf\left\{\frac{|\partial_{\Pi}(A)|_{m}}{m(A)}\;\middle|\;A\in\mathcal{S},\ 0<m(A)\leq\frac{1}{2}m(X)\right\}.

Consequently, we have the following significant result relating the spectral gap with the Cheeger constant from
[
19
]
(see also
[
22
]
):

Theorem 2.13

(
[
19
, Theorem 2.1]
)
.

Let
\Pi
be a reversible Markov kernel on
(X,m)
where
m
is finite. Then

\frac{\kappa^{2}}{2}\leq 1-\lambda\leq 2\kappa.

3.
Asymptotic expansion in measure and structure theorems

In this section, we introduce the notion of expansion and asymptotic expansion for groupoids and then establish their structure theory. This generalises both (measured) asymptotic expanders in
[
17
,
20
,
23
]
and asymptotic expansion in measure for group actions in
[

,
22
]
.

Again we always assume that
\mathcal{G}
is a groupoid with a length function
\ell
, and
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}

. In this section, we require

is a

3.1.
Expansion and asymptotic expansion

We firstly introduce the following:

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a groupoid with a length function
\ell
, and
(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},\mathcal{R},\mu)
be a probability measure space. We say that
\mathcal{G}
is

)
if there exist

and a unital symmetric
1
1

1

Since we are only interested in the existence of

N
-decomposable

The inequality “

We also consider the following asymptotic version of Definition

Let

To establish the structure theorem, we also need the following notion:

Definition 3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{G}

The functions

If

.

Remark 3.4
.

It is easy to see that

is a domain of asymptotic expansion
if and only if
the reduction
\mathcal{G}_{Y}^{Y}\coloneqq\mathrm{s}^{-1}(Y)\cap\mathrm{r}^{-1}(Y)
is asymptotically expanding in Definition

In the following, we collect several useful facts for domains of (asymptotic) expansion, generalising those in
[
21
,
23
]
. They will play an important role to prove the structure result later.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
Y\subseteq\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
be a domain of asymptotic expansion. Then for any
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
and
\beta\in[\frac{1}{2},1)
, there exist
C,N,L>0
and a unital symmetric
N
-decomposable
K\subseteq\mathcal{G}
with
\ell(K)\leq L
such that for any measurable
A\subseteq Y
with
\alpha\mu(Y)\leq\mu(A)\leq\beta\mu(Y)
, we have:

\mu\big((\mathrm{r}(K\cdot A)\setminus A)\cap Y\big)>C\mu(A).

Here
C,N,L
only depend on
\alpha,\beta
and expansion parameters for
Y
.

Proof.

For
\beta\in[\frac{1}{2},1)
, set
\alpha^{\prime}=\frac{1-\beta}{2}\in(0,\frac{1}{4}]
.
By assumption, there exist
C_{\alpha^{\prime}},L_{\alpha^{\prime}},N_{\alpha^{\prime}}>0
and a unital symmetric
N_{\alpha^{\prime}}
-decomposable
K_{\alpha^{\prime}}\subseteq\mathcal{G}
with
\ell(K_{\alpha^{\prime}})\leq L_{\alpha^{\prime}}
such that for any measurable
A\subseteq Y
with
\alpha^{\prime}\mu(Y)\leq\mu(A)\leq\frac{1}{2}\mu(Y)
, we have:

(3.1)

\mu\big((\mathrm{r}(K_{\alpha^{\prime}}\cdot A)\setminus A)\cap Y\big)>C_{\alpha^{\prime}}\mu(A).

Given measurable
A\subseteq Y
with
\mu(A)\in[\frac{1}{2}\mu(Y),\beta\mu(Y)]
, let

B=Y\setminus\mathrm{r}(K_{\alpha^{\prime}}\cdot A).

Then
\mu(B)\leq\mu(Y\setminus A)\leq\frac{1}{2}\mu(Y)
. Now we divide into two cases:

If
\mu(B)<\alpha^{\prime}\mu(Y)
, then
\mu\big(\mathrm{r}(K_{\alpha^{\prime}}\cdot A)\cap Y\big)>(1-\alpha^{\prime})\mu(Y)\geq\frac{1+\beta}{2\beta}\mu(A)
. Hence we obtain
\mu\big((\mathrm{r}(K_{\alpha^{\prime}}\cdot A)\setminus A)\cap Y\big)>\frac{1-\beta}{2\beta}\mu(A)
.

If
\mu(B)\geq\alpha^{\prime}\mu(Y)
, Inequality (
3.1
) implies
\mu((\mathrm{r}(K_{\alpha^{\prime}}\cdot B)\setminus B)\cap Y)>C_{\alpha^{\prime}}\mu(B)
. Then:

\big(\mathrm{r}(K_{\alpha^{\prime}}\cdot B)\setminus B\big)\cap Y\subseteq\big(\mathrm{r}(K_{\alpha^{\prime}}\cdot A)\setminus A\big)\cap Y.

Hence

\mu\big((\mathrm{r}(K_{\alpha^{\prime}}\cdot A)\setminus A)\cap Y\big)\geq\mu\big((\mathrm{r}(K_{\alpha^{\prime}}\cdot B)\setminus B)\cap Y\big)>C_{\alpha^{\prime}}\frac{1-\beta}{2\beta}\mu(A).

Finally, set

C\coloneqq\min\left\{C_{\alpha},\frac{1-\beta}{2\beta}C_{\alpha^{\prime}},\frac{1-\beta}{2\beta}\right\}\quad\text{and}\quad K\coloneqq K_{\alpha}\cup K_{\alpha^{\prime}}.

Then
K
is unital symmetric and
(N_{\alpha}+N_{\alpha^{\prime}})
-decomposable with
\ell(K)\leq\max\{L_{\alpha},L_{\alpha^{\prime}}\}
. Hence we conclude the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Assume
\mathcal{G}
is asymptotically expanding in measure. Then for any
\beta\in(0,1],\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
and
C\in(0,1)
, there exists a unital symmetric
N
-decomposable
K\subseteq\mathcal{G}
with
\ell(K)\leq L
such that for any measurable
Y\subseteq\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
with
\mu(Y)\geq\beta
and measurable
A\subseteq Y
with
\alpha\mu(Y)\leq\mu(A)\leq\frac{1}{2}\mu(Y)
, we have
\mu((\mathrm{r}(K\cdot A)\setminus A)\cap Y)>C\mu(A)
. Here
N,L
only depend on
\alpha,\beta,C
and expansion parameters of
\mathcal{G}
.

Proof.

Fix
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2}],\beta\in(0,1]
,
C\in(0,1)
and a measurable
Y\subseteq\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
with
\mu(Y)\geq\beta
. Set
\epsilon\coloneqq\frac{1+C}{2}\mu(Y)
and
\gamma\coloneqq\mu(\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\setminus Y)+\epsilon=1-\frac{1-C}{2}\mu(Y)
. It suffices to find a unital symmetric decomposable
K\subseteq\mathcal{G}
such that for any measurable
A\subseteq Y
with
\alpha\mu(Y)\leq\mu(A)\leq\frac{1}{2}\mu(Y)
, then
\mu(\mathrm{r}(K\cdot A))>\gamma
. If this holds, then the proof would be completed by the following estimate:

\mu\big(\mathrm{r}(K\cdot A)\cap Y\big)>\gamma-\mu(\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\setminus Y)=\epsilon\geq\frac{2\epsilon}{\mu(Y)}\mu(A)=(1+C)\mu(A).

Now we aim to find such a
K
. Since
\mathcal{G}
is asymptotically expanding, Lemma
3.5
provides
C^{\prime},L^{\prime},N^{\prime}>0
and a unital symmetric
N^{\prime}
-decomposable
K^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{G}
with
\ell(K^{\prime})\leq L^{\prime}
such that for any measurable
A^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
with
\alpha\beta\leq\mu(A^{\prime})\leq 1-\frac{1-C}{2}\beta
, then

(3.2)

\mu\big(\mathrm{r}(K^{\prime}\cdot A^{\prime})\big)>(1+C^{\prime})\mu(A^{\prime})\geq(1+C^{\prime})\alpha\beta.

Set
m
to be the minimal integer satisfying
(1+C^{\prime})^{m}\geq\frac{1}{\alpha\beta}
, and take
K\coloneqq(K^{\prime})^{m}
.
Then Lemma
2.6
shows that
K
is unital symmetric
(N^{\prime})^{m}
-decomposable and
\ell(K)\leq mL^{\prime}
.

Now for any measurable
A\subseteq Y
with
\alpha\mu(Y)\leq\mu(A)\leq\frac{1}{2}\mu(Y)
, we need to show that
\mu(\mathrm{r}(K\cdot A))>\gamma
. If not, then
\mu\big(\mathrm{r}((K^{\prime})^{m}\cdot A)\big)\leq\gamma
. This shows that

\alpha\beta\leq\mu\big(\mathrm{r}((K^{\prime})^{i}\cdot A)\big)\leq\gamma\leq 1-\frac{1-C}{2}\beta\quad\text{for any}\quad i=0,1,\cdots m.

Applying Inequality (
3.2
) inductively, we obtain:

\mu\big(\mathrm{r}((K^{\prime})^{m}\cdot A)\big)>(1+C^{\prime})^{m}\mu(A)\geq(1+C^{\prime})^{m}\alpha\beta\geq 1>\gamma,

which leads to a contradiction.
∎

3.2.
The structure theorem

Now we are in the position to introduce the structure theorem for asymptotic expansion on groupoids. The main idea is to approximate by domains of expansions in the following sense:

Definition 3.7
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a groupoid with a length function
\ell
, and
(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},\mathcal{R},\mu)
be a probability measure space. We say that a sequence of measurable subsets
\{Y_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}

forms a (measured) exhaustion of
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
if
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu(Y_{n})=1
.

For technical reasons, we also need to consider:

Definition 3.8
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a groupoid with a length function
\ell
, and
(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},\mathcal{R},\mu)
be a probability measure space. Given an admissible bisection
K\subseteq\mathcal{G}
, we set

(3.3)

\mathfrak{R}(K,x)\coloneqq\frac{\mathrm{d}(\tau_{K}^{-1})_{\ast}\mu|_{\mathrm{r}(K)}}{\mathrm{d}\mu|_{\mathrm{s}(K)}}(x)

the Radon-Nikodym derivative at
x\in\mathrm{s}(K)
, where
\tau_{K}
comes from (
1.1
). Here
\mu|_{\mathrm{s}(K)}
and
\mu|_{\mathrm{r}(K)}
are the restrictions of
\mu
, and
(\tau_{K}^{-1})_{\ast}\mu|_{K}
is the push-forward measure defined by
\big((\tau_{K}^{-1})_{\ast}\mu|_{\mathrm{r}(K)}\big)(A)=\mu(\tau_{K}(A))
for measurable
A\subseteq\mathrm{s}(K)
.

To simplify the statement of our main result, let us package the derivative information into the notion of domain of expansion:

Definition 3.9
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a groupoid with a length function
\ell
, and
(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},\mathcal{R},\mu)
be a probability measure space. For measurable
Y\subseteq\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
, we say that
Y
is a
domain of expansion with bounded ratio
if there exist
C,N,L>0
and a unital symmetric
N
-decomposable
K\subseteq\mathcal{G}
with
\ell(K)\leq L
, together with a unital symmetric decomposition
K=\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}K_{i}
satisfying the following:

(1)

for measurable
A\subseteq Y
with
0<\mu(A)\leq\frac{1}{2}\mu(Y)
, then we have

\mu\big((\mathrm{r}(K_{\alpha}\cdot A)\setminus A)\cap Y\big)>C_{\alpha}\mu(A).

(2)

there exists
\theta\geq 1
such that
\frac{1}{\theta}\leq\mathfrak{R}(K_{i},x)\leq\theta
for any
x\in Y
and
i\in\{1,2,\ \cdots,N\}
with
\tau_{K_{i}}(x)\in Y
. (As a priori to
\tau_{K_{i}}(x)\in Y
, we have
x\in\mathrm{s}(K_{i})
and hence
\mathfrak{R}(K_{i},x)
makes sense.)

In this case, we say that
Y
is a
domain of
(C,N,L)
-expansion with ratio bounded by
\theta
.

The following is our structure theorem:

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a groupoid with a length function
\ell
, and
(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},\mathcal{R},\mu)
be a probability measure space. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

The groupoid
\mathcal{G}
is asymptotically expanding in measure.

(2)

The unit space
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
admits an exhaustion by domains
Y_{n}
of
(C_{n},N_{n},L_{n})
-expansion of ratio bounded by
\theta_{n}
for
C_{n},N_{n},L_{n}>0
and
\theta_{n}\geq 1
.

(3)

The unit space
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
admits an exhaustion by domains of asymptotic expansion.

Here the extra requirement on the Radon-Nikodym derivatives in condition (2) will play an important role to prove our main results in Section
5
.

A key tool to prove Theorem
3.10
is to consider maximal Følner sets as follows.

Definition 3.11
.

Given measurable
Y\subseteq\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
, decomposable
K\subseteq\mathcal{G}
and
\epsilon>0
, we say that a measurable subset
F\subseteq Y
is
(\epsilon,K)
-Følner in
Y
if
\mu(F)\leq\frac{1}{2}\mu(Y)
and

\mu\big((\mathrm{r}(K\cdot F)\setminus F)\cap Y\big)\leq\epsilon\mu(F).

When
Y=\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
, we simply say that
F
is
(\epsilon,K)
-Følner
.

Now fix measurable
Y\subseteq\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
, decomposable
K\subseteq\mathcal{G}
and
\epsilon>0
. Denote the set of all
(\epsilon,K)
-Følner sets in
Y
by
\mathcal{F}_{\epsilon,K}
. Consider the equivalence relation on
\mathcal{F}_{\epsilon,K}
by setting
F\sim F^{\prime}
in
\mathcal{F}_{\epsilon,K}
if and only if they differ by a null-set. Define a partial order on
\mathcal{F}_{\epsilon,K}/\sim
by setting
[F]\sqsubseteq[F^{\prime}]
if
F\subseteq F^{\prime}
up to null-sets.

The following lemma is similar to
[
21
, Lemma 4.2]
:

Lemma 3.12
.

The partial ordered set
(\mathcal{F}_{\epsilon,K}/\sim,\sqsubseteq)
has maximal elements.

Proof.

Take an arbitrary
F_{0}\in\mathcal{F}_{\epsilon,K}
and set
\beta_{0}\coloneqq\sup\{\mu(F)~|~F\in\mathcal{F}_{\epsilon,K}\text{ and }F_{0}\subseteq F\}
. Inductively, we choose
F_{n}\in\mathcal{F}_{\epsilon,K}
such that
F_{n-1}\subseteq F_{n}
and
\mu(F_{n})>\beta_{n-1}-\frac{1}{n}
where

\beta_{n}\coloneqq\sup\left\{\mu(F)~|~F\in\mathcal{F}_{\epsilon,K}\text{ and }F_{n}\subseteq F\right\}.

If
\beta_{n}=\mu(F_{n})
for some
n
, then
[F_{n}]
is maximal. Otherwise, we obtain an increasing sequence
\{F_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. Setting
F\coloneqq\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}F_{n}
and similar to the proof of
[
21
, Lemma 4.2]
, we obtain that
[F]
is maximal.
∎

Maximal Følner sets are useful to produce domains of expansions, which are crucial to prove Theorem
3.10
. We start with the following:

Lemma 3.13
.

Given measurable
Y\subseteq\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
, decomposable
K\subseteq\mathcal{G}
and
\epsilon>0
, let
F_{\epsilon,K}\subseteq Y
be a maximal
(\epsilon,K)
-Følner set in
Y
. Then for any measurable
A\subseteq Y\setminus F_{\epsilon,K}
with
0<\mu(A)\leq\frac{1}{2}\mu(Y)-\mu(F_{\epsilon,K})
, we have

\mu\big(((\mathrm{r}(K\cdot A)\setminus A)\cap Y)\setminus F_{\epsilon,K}\big)>\epsilon\mu(A).

Proof.

Given such an
A\subseteq Y\setminus F_{\epsilon,K}
, we have
\mu(F_{\epsilon,K})<\mu(A\sqcup F_{\epsilon,K})\leq\frac{1}{2}\mu(Y)
and
\big(\mathrm{r}(K\cdot(A\sqcup F_{\epsilon,K}))\setminus(A\sqcup F_{\epsilon,K})\big)\cap Y
is contained in

\big(((\mathrm{r}(K\cdot A)\setminus A)\cap Y)\setminus F_{\epsilon,K}\big)\cup\big((\mathrm{r}(K\cdot F_{\epsilon,K})\setminus F_{\epsilon,K})\cap Y\big).

By the maximality of
F_{\epsilon,K}
, it follows that

\displaystyle\epsilon\mu(A\sqcup F_{\epsilon,K})

\displaystyle<\mu\big((\mathrm{r}(K\cdot(A\sqcup F_{\epsilon,K}))\setminus(A\sqcup F_{\epsilon,K}))\cap Y\big)

\displaystyle\leq\mu\big(((\mathrm{r}(K\cdot A)\setminus A)\cap Y)\setminus F_{\epsilon,K}\big)+\mu\big((\mathrm{r}(K\cdot F_{\epsilon,K})\setminus F_{\epsilon,K})\cap Y\big).

Since
\mu\big((\mathrm{r}(K\cdot F_{\epsilon,K})\setminus F_{\epsilon,K})\cap Y\big)\leq\epsilon\mu(F_{\epsilon,K})
, we have

\epsilon\mu(A)<\mu\big(((\mathrm{r}(K\cdot A)\setminus A)\cap Y)\setminus F_{\epsilon,K}\big).

Then we finish the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.10
.

“
(1)\Rightarrow(2)
”: Fix
C\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
and let
\alpha_{n}\coloneqq\frac{C}{(4+2C)(n+1)}
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
\mathcal{G}
is asymptotically expanding, Lemma
3.6
provides a unital symmetric
N_{n}
-decomposable
K_{n}
with
\ell(K_{n})\leq L_{n}
satisfying the condition therein for
\beta=\frac{1}{2}
,
\alpha=\alpha_{n}
and
C
. Take a unital symmetric decomposition
K_{n}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{N_{n}}K_{n,i}
for admissible
K_{n,i}
and set
\theta_{n}\coloneqq N_{n}\cdot(n+1)
. Denote

Z_{n}\coloneqq\left\{\tau_{K_{n,i}}(x)~\middle|~x\in\mathrm{s}(K_{n,i})\text{ and }i=1,\cdots,N_{n}\text{ such that }\mathfrak{R}(K_{n,i},x)<\frac{1}{\theta_{n}}\right\}.

Then
\mu(Z_{n})<\frac{N_{n}}{\theta_{n}}\cdot\mu(\mathcal{G}^{(0)})=\frac{1}{n+1}
, which implies that

\mu(\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\setminus Z_{n})\geq\frac{n}{n+1}\geq\frac{1}{2}.

Let
X_{n}\coloneqq\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\setminus Z_{n}
, and take
F_{n}
to be a maximal
(C,K_{n})
-Følner sets in
X_{n}
, ensured by Lemma
3.12
. Then we have
\mu(F_{n})<\alpha_{n}\cdot\mu(X_{n})
. Setting
Y_{n}\coloneqq X_{n}\setminus F_{n}
, then
\mu(Y_{n})>(1-\alpha_{n})\mu(X_{n})\geq(1-\alpha_{n})\cdot\frac{n}{n+1}
.

Now we claim that
Y_{n}
is a domain of
(\frac{C}{2},N_{n},L_{n})
-expansion. In fact, we take an arbitrary measurable
A\subseteq Y_{n}
with
0<\mu(A)\leq\frac{1}{2}\mu(Y_{n})
and divide into two cases:

Case I.
If
\mu(A)\leq\frac{1}{2}\mu(X_{n})-\mu(F_{n})
, then by Lemma
3.13
we have

\mu\big((\mathrm{r}(K_{n}\cdot A)\setminus A)\cap Y_{n}\big)>C\mu(A).

Case II.
If
\mu(A)>\frac{1}{2}\mu(X_{n})-\mu(F_{n})
, then we have

\frac{1}{2}\mu(X_{n})\geq\mu(A)>(\frac{1}{2}-\alpha_{n})\mu(X_{n})\geq\alpha_{n}\mu(X_{n}).

It follows from the requirement on
K_{n}
that
\mu\big((\mathrm{r}(K_{n}\cdot A)\setminus A)\cap X_{n}\big)>C\mu(A)
. Since

\mu\big((\mathrm{r}(K_{n}\cdot A)\setminus A)\cap Y_{n}\big)\geq\mu\big((\mathrm{r}(K_{n}\cdot A)\setminus A)\cap X_{n}\big)-\mu(F_{n})>C\mu(A)-\mu(F_{n})

and

\mu(A)>(\frac{1}{2}-\alpha_{n})\mu(X_{n})\geq(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{C}{4+2C})\mu(X_{n})=\frac{1}{C+2}\mu(X_{n}),

then we have

C\mu(A)-\mu(F_{n})>C\mu(A)-\alpha_{n}\mu(X_{n})>C\mu(A)-\alpha_{n}(C+2)\mu(A)\geq\frac{C}{2}\mu(A).

In conclusion, we showed that
Y_{n}
is a domain of
(\frac{C}{2},N_{n},L_{n})
-expansion with ratio bounded by
\theta_{n}
and
\mu(Y_{n})>(1-\alpha_{n})\cdot\frac{n}{n+1}
.

“
(2)\Rightarrow(3)
” is trivial.

“
(3)\Rightarrow(1)
”: Take an exhaustion of
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
by domains
Y_{n}
of asymptotic expansion. Assume that
\mathcal{G}
were not asymptotically expanding. Then there exists
\alpha_{0}\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
such that for any
C>0
and any unital symmetric decomposable
K\subseteq\mathcal{G}
, there exists
A_{C,K}\in\mathcal{R}
with
\alpha_{0}\leq\mu(A_{C,K})\leq\frac{1}{2}
such that
\mu\big(\mathrm{r}(K\cdot A_{C,K})\setminus A_{C,K}\big)\leq C\mu(A_{C,K})
.

Take
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\mu(Y_{n})\geq 1-\frac{\alpha_{0}}{2}
. Then for any measurable
A\subseteq\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
with
\alpha_{0}\leq\mu(A)\leq\frac{1}{2}
, direct calculations show that
\frac{\alpha_{0}}{2}\mu(Y_{n})\leq\mu(A\cap Y_{n})\leq\frac{1}{2-\alpha_{0}}\mu(Y_{n})
. Then Lemma
3.5
provides
\epsilon>0
and unital symmetric decomposable
K\subseteq\mathcal{G}
such that

\mu\big((\mathrm{r}(K\cdot(A\cap Y_{n}))\setminus(A\cap Y_{n}))\cap Y_{n}\big)>\epsilon\mu(A\cap Y_{n})\geq\epsilon(\mu(A)-\frac{\alpha_{0}}{2})\geq\frac{\epsilon}{2}\mu(A),

which implies that

\mu(\mathrm{r}(K\cdot A)\setminus A)\geq\mu\big((\mathrm{r}(K\cdot(A\cap Y_{n}))\setminus A)\cap Y_{n}\big)>\frac{\epsilon}{2}\mu(A).

This leads to a contradiction.
∎

Remark 3.14
.

From the proof of “
(1)\Rightarrow(2)
” above, we know that if
\mathcal{G}
is asymptotically expanding, then in condition (2) we can take
C_{n}\equiv\frac{C}{2}
for any chosen
C\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
, domains
Y_{n}
satisfying
\mu(Y_{n})>\big(1-\frac{C}{(4+2C)(n+1)}\big)\cdot\frac{n}{n+1}
,
N_{n},L_{n}
and
\theta_{n}
only depend on the expansion parameters of
\mathcal{G}
.

3.3.
Markov kernels on groupoids

Here we construct reversible Markov kernels on groupoids and study the relation between their Cheeger constants and the expansion of groupoids.

Firstly, we aim to construct a Markov kernel for decomposable subsets.
Let us fix a unital symmetric decomposable subset
K
together with a unital symmetric decomposition
K=\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}K_{i}
.

Definition 3.15
.

For measurable
Y\subseteq\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
and
x\in Y
, denote

(3.4)

K_{Y,x}:=\{i~|~x\in\mathrm{s}(K_{i})\text{ and }\tau_{K_{i}}(x)\in Y\}\quad\text{and}\quad\sigma_{Y,K}(x)=\sum_{i\in K_{Y,x}}\mathfrak{R}(K_{i},x)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

The
normalized local Markov kernel
associated to
Y
and
K
is the Markov kernel on
Y
defined as follows:

(3.5)

\Pi_{Y,K}(x,-)=\frac{1}{\sigma_{Y,K}(x)}\sum_{i\in K_{Y,x}}\mathfrak{R}(K_{i},x)^{\frac{1}{2}}\delta_{\tau_{K_{i}}(x)}(-)\quad\text{for}\quad x\in Y.

Here
\delta_{y}
is the Dirac delta measure on
y
.

Since the decomposition is unital, it is clear that
\sigma_{Y,K}>0
on
Y
and (
3.5
) makes sense. It is also routine to check that (
3.5
) is indeed a Markov kernel on
Y
.
To see that
\Pi_{Y,K}
is reversible, we consider the measure on
Y
defined by:

(3.6)

\mathrm{d}\tilde{\mu}_{Y,K}\coloneqq\sigma_{Y,K}\cdot\mathrm{d}(\mu|_{Y}).

Note that both
\Pi_{Y,K}
and
\tilde{\mu}_{Y,K}
depend on the decomposition of
K
.

We collect several useful properties of
\Pi_{Y,K}
in the following. The proof is similar to that of
[
22
, Proposition 3.10]
since the decomposition
K=\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}K_{i}
is unital and symmetric. Hence we omit the details.

Proposition 3.16
.

In the setting above, we have:

(1)

The measure
\tilde{\mu}_{Y,K}
is reversing for the Markov kernel
\Pi_{Y,K}
.

(2)

For measurable
A\subseteq Y
, we have
\mu(A)\leq\tilde{\mu}_{Y,K}(A)\leq N\sqrt{{\mu(A)\mu(Y)}}
. Hence
\tilde{\mu}_{Y,K}
is equivalent to the restriction
\mu|_{Y}
.

Definition 3.17
.

For a measurable
Y\subseteq\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
and
C,N,L>0
, we call
Y
a
domain of Markov
(C,N,L)
-expansion
(or simply,
domain of Markov expansion
) if there exists a unital symmetric
N
-decomposable
K
together with a unital symmetric decomposition
K=\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}K_{i}
such that
\ell(K)\leq L
and the associated normalised local Markov kernel
\Pi_{Y,K}
on
(Y,\tilde{\mu}_{Y,K})
has Cheeger constant greater than
C
.

Moreover, if the decomposition for
K
above has ratio bounded by
\theta\geq 1
, then we say that
Y
is a
domain of Markov
(C,N,L)
-expansion with ratio bounded by
\theta
.

The following lemma relates the domain of Markov expansion with the ordinary expansion. The proof is similar to that of
[
22
, Lemma 3.14]
and hence omitted.

Lemma 3.18
.

For a measurable
Y\subseteq\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
, we have:

(1)

If
Y
is a domain of
(C,N,L)
-expansion with ratio bounded by
\theta
, then
Y
is a domain of
(\frac{C}{N\theta},N,L)
-Markov expansion;

(2)

If
Y
is a domain of
(\kappa,N,L)
-Markov expansion with ratio bounded by
\theta
, then
Y
is a domain of
(\frac{\kappa}{N\sqrt{\theta}+\kappa},N,L)
-expansion.

Combining Theorem
3.10
with Lemma
3.18
, we reach the following Markovian version of the structure theorem:

Theorem 3.19
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a groupoid with a length function
\ell
, and
(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},\mathcal{R},\mu)
be a probability measure space. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

The groupoid
\mathcal{G}
is asymptotically expanding in measure;

(2)

The unit space
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
admits an exhaustion by domains
Y_{n}
of
(C_{n},N_{n},L_{n})
-Markov expansion with ratio bounded by
\theta_{n}
for
C_{n},N_{n},L_{n}>0
and
\theta_{n}\geq 1
.

Remark 3.20
.

Combining Remark
3.14
and the dependence of parameters in Lemma
3.18
, we know that if
\mathcal{G}
is asymptotically expanding, then in Theorem
3.19
(2) we can take domains
Y_{n}
to satisfy
\mu(Y_{n})>t_{n}
for some universal
t_{n}
independent of
\mathcal{G}
,
C_{n},N_{n},L_{n}
and
\theta_{n}
only depend on expansion parameters of
\mathcal{G}
.

4.
Dynamical propagation and quasi-locality

In this section, we introduce the notion of dynamical propagation and dynamical quasi-locality for operators on groupoids. Again we always assume that
\mathcal{G}
is a groupoid with a length function
\ell
, and
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
is equipped with a measure
\mu
on some
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{R}
. To include more examples, here we do
not
require
\mu
to be finite.

4.1.
Basic Notions

We start with the following key notions:

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a groupoid with a length function
\ell
, and
(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},\mathcal{R},\mu)
be a measure space. An operator
T\in\mathfrak{B}(L^{2}(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},\mu))
is said to have
finite dynamical propagation
if there is a unital symmetric
N
-decomposable subset
K\subseteq\mathcal{G}
with
\ell(K)\leq L
for some
N,L>0
such that for any
A,B\in\mathcal{R}
with
\mu(\mathrm{r}(K\cdot A)\cap B)=0
, then
\chi_{A}T\chi_{B}=0
.
In this case, we also say that
T
has
(N,L)
-dynamical propagation
.

Denote the set of all operators with finite dynamical propagation by
\mathbb{C}_{\mathrm{dyn}}(\mathcal{G})
, and its norm completion by
\mathbf{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{dyn}}(\mathcal{G})
, called the
dynamical Roe algebra of
\mathcal{G}
.

Similarly, we consider its quasi-local counterpart:

Definition 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a groupoid with a length function
\ell
, and
(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},\mathcal{R},\mu)
be a measure space. An operator
T\in\mathfrak{B}(L^{2}(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},\mu))
is called
dynamically quasi-local
if for any
\epsilon>0
, there is a unital symmetric
N_{\epsilon}
-decomposable subset
K_{\epsilon}\subseteq\mathcal{G}
with
\ell(K_{\epsilon})\leq L_{\epsilon}
for some
N_{\epsilon},L_{\epsilon}>0
such that for any
A,B\in\mathcal{R}
with
\mu(\mathrm{r}(K_{\epsilon}\cdot A)\cap B)=0
, then
\|\chi_{A}T\chi_{B}\|<\epsilon
. The maps
\epsilon\mapsto N_{\epsilon},\epsilon\mapsto L_{\epsilon}
are called
quasi-local parameters
for
T
.

Denote the set of all dynamically quasi-local operators in
\mathfrak{B}(L^{2}(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},\mu))
by
\mathbf{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{dyn},\mathrm{q}}(\mathcal{G})
, called the
dynamical quasi-local algebra of
\mathcal{G}
.

The following shows that
\mathbf{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{dyn}}(\mathcal{G})
and
\mathbf{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{dyn},\mathrm{q}}(\mathcal{G})
are indeed
C^{*}
-algebras:

Lemma 4.3
.

Given
\mathcal{G}
,
\ell
and
\mu
as above, we have:

(1)

The set
\mathbb{C}_{\mathrm{dyn}}(\mathcal{G})
is a
\ast
-algebra and hence,
\mathbf{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{dyn}}(\mathcal{G})
is a
C^{*}
-algebra;

(2)

The set
\mathbf{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{dyn},\mathrm{q}}(\mathcal{G})
is a
C^{*}
-algebra.

Proof.

We only prove (1), since (2) is similar.

By Lemma
2.6
,
\mathbb{C}_{\mathrm{dyn}}(\mathcal{G})
forms an algebra. To see it preserves the
\ast
-operator, fix
T\in\mathbb{C}_{\mathrm{dyn}}(\mathcal{G})
. Then there exists a unital symmetric decomposable subset
K=\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}K_{i}
such that for any
A,B\in\mathcal{R}
with
\mu(\mathrm{r}(K\cdot A)\cap B)=0
, then
\chi_{A}T\chi_{B}=0
.
Then we have
\chi_{B}T^{*}\chi_{A}=0
for any measurable
A,B\subseteq\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
with
\mu(\mathrm{r}(K\cdot A)\cap B)=0
. Since each
\tau_{K_{i}}
is measure-class-preserving and
K
is symmetric, this is equivalent to that
\mu(\mathrm{r}(K\cdot B)\cap A)=0
. Hence we conclude that
T^{*}\in\mathbb{C}_{\mathrm{dyn}}(\mathcal{G})
.
∎

For a sequence of groupoids, we can combine them into a single groupoid and translate the notion of dynamical propagation and quasi-locality thereon in a uniform version. This is important to recover the case of uniform Roe algebras.

More precisely, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\mathcal{G}_{n}
be a groupoid with a length function
\ell_{n}
and
(\mathcal{G}^{(0)}_{n},\mathcal{R}_{n},\mu_{n})
be a measure space. Form a groupoid
\mathcal{G}
to be their disjoint union
\bigsqcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{G}_{n}
, and equip
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}=\bigsqcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{G}^{(0)}_{n}
with a measure
\mu
on some
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{R}
generated by
\bigsqcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{R}_{n}
and
\mu
is determined by
\mu|_{\mathcal{G}^{(0)}_{n}}\coloneqq\mu_{n}
for each
n
. Also take a length function
\ell
on
\mathcal{G}
to be the disjoint union of
\ell_{n}
. Then we have

L^{2}(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},\mu)=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{N}}L^{2}(\mathcal{G}^{(0)}_{n},\mu_{n}).

The following shows that operators in
\mathbf{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{dyn},\mathrm{q}}(\mathcal{G})
are always diagonal and can be described in a uniform version:

Lemma 4.4
.

Given
T\in\mathfrak{B}(L^{2}(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},\mu))
, we have:

(1)

T\in\mathbb{C}_{\mathrm{dyn}}(\mathcal{G})

if and only if
there exists
T_{n}\in\mathfrak{B}(L^{2}(\mathcal{G}^{(0)}_{n},\mu_{n}))
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
with
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|T_{n}\|<\infty
such that
T=(\mathrm{SOT})-\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}T_{n}
and there exist
N,L>0
satisfying: for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
there exists a unital symmetric
N
-decomposable
K_{n}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{n}
with
\ell_{n}(K_{n})\leq L
such that for any
A,B\in\mathcal{R}_{n}
with
\mu(\mathrm{r}(K_{n}\cdot A)\cap B)=0
, we have
\chi_{A}T_{n}\chi_{B}=0
.

(2)

T\in\mathbf{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{dyn},\mathrm{q}}(\mathcal{G})

if and only if
there exists
T_{n}\in\mathfrak{B}(L^{2}(\mathcal{G}^{(0)}_{n},\mu_{n}))
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
with
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|T_{n}\|<\infty
such that
T=(\mathrm{SOT})-\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}T_{n}
and for any
\epsilon>0
there exist
N,L>0
satisfying: for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a unital symmetric
N
-decomposable
K_{n}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{n}
with
\ell_{n}(K_{n})\leq L
such that for any
A,B\in\mathcal{R}_{n}
with
\mu(\mathrm{r}(K_{n}\cdot A)\cap B)=0
, we have
\|\chi_{A}T_{n}\chi_{B}\|<\epsilon
.

Proof.

(1).
Necessity
: Firstly, we show that
T
is diagonal. Note that for any
n\neq m
and
A\in\mathcal{R}_{n}
,
B\in\mathcal{R}_{m}
, we have
(K\cdot A)\cap B=\emptyset
for any decomposable
K\subseteq\mathcal{G}
. Then it follows from definition that
\chi_{A}T\chi_{B}=0
, and hence
T=(\mathrm{SOT})-\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}T_{n}
for
T_{n}=\chi_{\mathcal{G}^{(0)}_{n}}T\chi_{\mathcal{G}^{(0)}_{n}}
.

Furthermore, there exists a unital symmetric decomposable
K\subseteq\mathcal{G}
such that for any
A,B\in\mathcal{R}
with
\mu(\mathrm{r}(K\cdot A)\cap B)=0
, then
\chi_{A}T\chi_{B}=0
. Assume that
K
is
N
-decomposable and
\ell(K)\leq L
for some
N,L>0
. For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, set
K_{n}\coloneqq K\cap\mathcal{G}_{n}
. Then
K_{n}
is
N
-decomposable, unital and symmetric with
\ell_{n}(K_{n})\leq L
. It is easy to check that
K_{n}
satisfies the requirement.

Sufficiency
: Assume
K_{n}
satisfies the requirement, and write
K_{n}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}K_{n}^{(i)}
for admissible
K_{n}^{(i)}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{n}
with
\ell_{n}(K_{n}^{(i)})\leq L
. Define
K\coloneqq\bigsqcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}K_{n}\subseteq\mathcal{G}
and for
i=1,\cdots,N
, define
K^{(i)}\coloneqq\bigsqcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}K_{n}^{(i)}
. Since
K_{n}^{(i)}
is admissible, it is easy to see that
K^{(i)}\subseteq\mathcal{G}
is admissible for each
i
. Hence
K
is
N
-decomposable and
\ell(K)\leq L
.

Now for any
A,B\in\mathcal{R}
with
\mu(\mathrm{r}(K\cdot A)\cap B)=0
, set
A_{n}\coloneqq A\cap\mathcal{G}^{(0)}_{n}
and
B_{n}\coloneqq B\cap\mathcal{G}^{(0)}_{n}
and then we have
\mu_{n}(\mathrm{r}(K_{n}\cdot A_{n})\cap B_{n})=0
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
. By assumption, we have
\chi_{A_{n}}T_{n}\chi_{B_{n}}=0
. Finally, note that

\chi_{A}T\chi_{B}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\chi_{A_{n}}T_{n}\chi_{B_{n}}=0,

which concludes (1). (2) is similar to (1) and hence omitted.
∎

4.2.
Quasi-locality of the averaging projection

Now we focus on a special projection operator in
\mathfrak{B}(L^{2}(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},\mu))
when
\mu
is a probability measure, whose dynamical quasi-locality is closely related to the asymptotic expansion of the groupoid.

Definition 4.5
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a groupoid with a length function
\ell
, and
(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},\mathcal{R},\mu)
be a measure space. For any measurable
Y\subseteq\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
with
0<\mu(Y)<\infty
, denote by
P_{Y}\in\mathfrak{B}(L^{2}(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},\mu))
the
averaging projection on
Y
, which is the orthogonal projection onto the one-dimensional subspace in
L^{2}(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},\mu)
spanned by
\chi_{Y}
. In other words,

P_{Y}(f)\coloneqq\frac{\langle f,\chi_{Y}\rangle}{\mu(Y)}\chi_{Y}\quad\text{for}\quad f\in L^{2}(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},\mu).

If
\mu(\mathcal{G}^{(0)})<\infty
, we simply write
P_{\mathcal{G}}
for
P_{\mathcal{G}^{(0)}}
.

To study the quasi-locality of the averaging projection, we firstly record the following. The proof is similar to that of
[
20
, Lemma 3.8]
, and hence omitted.

Lemma 4.6
.

Assume that
\mu(\mathcal{G}^{(0)})=1
. Then for any measurable
A,B\subseteq\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
, we have

\|\chi_{A}P_{\mathcal{G}}\chi_{B}\|=\sqrt{\mu(A)\mu(B)}.

The following relates the quasi-locality of
P_{\mathcal{G}}
with asymptotic expansion of
\mathcal{G}
:

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a groupoid with a length function
\ell
, and
(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},\mathcal{R},\mu)
be a probability measure space. Then the averaging projection
P_{\mathcal{G}}
is dynamically quasi-local
if and only if

\mathcal{G}
is asymptotically expanding in measure
\mu
.

Proof.

Necessity
.
Assume
\epsilon\mapsto N_{\epsilon}
,
\epsilon\mapsto L_{\epsilon}
are quasi-local parameters for
P_{\mathcal{G}}
. Given
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
, set
N\coloneqq N_{\sqrt{\alpha}/2}
and
L\coloneqq L_{\sqrt{\alpha}/2}
. Then there exists a unital symmetric
N
-decomposable
K
with
\ell(K)\leq L
such that for any
A,B\in\mathcal{R}
with
\mu(\mathrm{r}(K\cdot A)\cap B)=0
, we have
\|\chi_{A}T\chi_{B}\|<\frac{\sqrt{\alpha}}{2}
. Then for any
A\in\mathcal{R}
with
\alpha\leq\mu(A)\leq\frac{1}{2}
, Lemma
4.6
shows:

\sqrt{\mu(A)\cdot\mu\big(\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\setminus\mathrm{r}(K\cdot A)\big)}=\|\chi_{A}P_{\mathcal{G}}\chi_{\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\setminus\mathrm{r}(K\cdot A)}\|<\frac{\sqrt{\alpha}}{2}.

Hence we obtain

\mu(A)\cdot\mu\big(\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\setminus\mathrm{r}(K\cdot A)\big)<\frac{\alpha}{4},

which implies that
\mu\big(\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\setminus\mathrm{r}(K\cdot A)\big)<\frac{1}{4}
. Therefore, we obtain:

\mu(\mathrm{r}(K\cdot A))>\frac{3}{4}\geq(1+\frac{1}{2})\cdot\mu(A).

This concludes that
\mathcal{G}
is asymptotically expanding in measure.

Sufficiency
.
Assuming
\alpha\mapsto C_{\alpha}
,
\alpha\mapsto N_{\alpha}
and
\alpha\mapsto L_{\alpha}
are expansion parameters for
\mathcal{G}
, we take
K_{\alpha}
as in Definition
3.2
. Given
0<\epsilon\leq\frac{1}{2}
, take
n\in\mathbb{N}
to be the smallest number such that

(1+C_{\epsilon})^{n}\cdot\epsilon>\frac{1}{2}.

Set
K\coloneqq K_{\epsilon}^{2n}
, which is unital symmetric
N_{\epsilon}^{2n}
-decomposable with length at most
2nL_{\epsilon}
by Lemma
2.6
.

For any measurable
A,B\subseteq\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
with
\mu(\mathrm{r}(K\cdot A)\cap B)=0
, we have

\mu\big(\mathrm{r}(K_{\epsilon}^{n}\cdot A)\cap\mathrm{r}(K_{\epsilon}^{n}\cdot B)\big)=0

since
K_{\epsilon}
is symmetric. Hence without loss of generality, we assume

\mu(\mathrm{r}(K_{\epsilon}^{n}\cdot A))\leq\frac{1}{2}.

If
\mu(A)<\epsilon
, then by Lemma
4.6
we have

\|\chi_{A}P_{\mathcal{G}}\chi_{B}\|=\sqrt{\mu(A)\mu(B)}<\sqrt{\epsilon}.

If
\mu(A)\geq\epsilon
, then using asymptotic expansion inductively, we have:

\frac{1}{2}\geq\mu(\mathrm{r}(K_{\epsilon}^{n}\cdot A))\geq(1+C_{\epsilon})\mu(\mathrm{r}(K_{\epsilon}^{n-1}\cdot A))\geq\cdots\geq(1+C_{\epsilon})^{n}\mu(A)\geq(1+C_{\epsilon})^{n}\cdot\epsilon.

This leads to a contradiction to the choice of
n
.
∎

Remark 4.8
.

From the proof above, it is clear that if
P_{\mathcal{G}}
is dynamically quasi-local, then we can choose expansion parameters for
\mathcal{G}
only depending on quasi-local parameters for
P_{\mathcal{G}}
, and vice versa.

5.
Main results

Having established Proposition
4.7
, now we are in the position to prove the following fundamental case of the main result:

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a groupoid with a length function
\ell
, and
(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},\mathcal{R},\mu)
be a probability measure space. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

the averaging projection
P_{\mathcal{G}}\in\mathbf{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{dyn}}(\mathcal{G})
;

(2)

the averaging projection
P_{\mathcal{G}}\in\mathbf{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{dyn},\mathrm{q}}(\mathcal{G})
;

(3)

the groupoid
\mathcal{G}
is asymptotically expanding in measure.

Proof.

“(1)
\Rightarrow
(2)” is trivial, and “(2)
\Leftrightarrow
(3)” comes from Proposition
4.7
. Hence we only focus on “(3)
\Rightarrow
(1)”.

Fix
C\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
. By Theorem
3.19
, Remark
3.14
and Remark
3.20
, there exists a sequence of measurable subsets
Y_{n}\subseteq\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
with
\mu(Y_{n})>\big(1-\frac{C}{(4+2C)(n+1)}\big)\cdot\frac{n}{n+1}
such that each
Y_{n}
is a domain of
(C_{n},N_{n},L_{n})
-Markov expansion with ratio bounded by
\theta_{n}
for
C_{n},N_{n},L_{n}>0
and
\theta_{n}\geq 1
only depending on expansion parameters of
\mathcal{G}
. From Definition
3.17
, we can choose a unital symmetric
N_{n}
-decomposable
K_{n}
together with a unital symmetric decomposition
K_{n}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{N_{n}}K_{n}^{(i)}
such that
\ell(K_{n})\leq L_{n}
and the associated normalised local Markov kernel
\Pi_{n}\coloneqq\Pi_{Y_{n},K_{n}}
from (
3.5
) on
(Y_{n},\tilde{\mu}_{n})
has Cheeger constant greater than
C_{n}
, where
\tilde{\mu}_{n}\coloneqq\tilde{\mu}_{Y_{n},K_{n}}
is the reversing measure defined in (
3.6
). We have a function
\sigma_{n}\coloneqq\sigma_{Y_{n},K_{n}}
from (
3.4
) such that
\sigma_{n}\geq 1
on
Y_{n}
.
Denote the associated Markov operator by
\mathfrak{P}_{n}
from (
2.3
) with spectral gap
\lambda_{n}
.

Now for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we consider the following embedding

I_{n}:L^{2}(Y_{n},\tilde{\mu}_{n})\longrightarrow L^{2}(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},\mu)

simply by extending each function in
L^{2}(Y_{n},\tilde{\mu}_{n})
by zero on
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\setminus Y_{n}
. This is well-defined since
\tilde{\mu}_{n}
is equivalent to
\mu|_{Y_{n}}
and we have
\|I_{n}\|\leq 1
thanks to Proposition
3.16
(2). Direct calculations show that its adjoint has the form:

I_{n}^{*}:L^{2}(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},\mu)\longrightarrow L^{2}(Y_{n},\tilde{\mu}_{n}),\quad g\mapsto\frac{1}{\sigma_{n}}\cdot g|_{Y_{n}}\quad\text{for}\quad g\in L^{2}(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},\mu).

Denote the adjoint map

\mathrm{Ad}_{n}:\mathfrak{B}(L^{2}(Y_{n},\tilde{\mu}_{n}))\longrightarrow\mathfrak{B}(L^{2}(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},\mu)),\quad T\mapsto I_{n}\circ T\circ I_{n}^{*}\quad\text{for}\quad T\in\mathfrak{B}(L^{2}(Y_{n},\tilde{\mu}_{n})).

Recall from Definition
4.5
that we have the averaging projection
P_{n}\coloneqq P_{Y_{n}}
in
\mathfrak{B}(L^{2}(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},\mu))
, which is the orthogonal projection onto constant functions on
Y_{n}
in
L^{2}(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},\mu)
. Denote another orthogonal projection
\tilde{P}_{n}\in\mathfrak{B}(L^{2}(Y_{n},\tilde{\mu}_{n}))
onto constant functions on
Y_{n}
in
L^{2}(Y_{n},\tilde{\mu}_{n})
. Direct calculations show that

(5.1)

\mathrm{Ad}_{n}(\tilde{P}_{n})=\frac{\mu(Y_{n})}{\tilde{\mu}_{n}(Y_{n})}\cdot P_{n}.

On the other hand, Theorem
2.13
shows that
\frac{C_{n}^{2}}{2}\leq 1-\lambda_{n}\leq 2C_{n}
. Hence
\frac{1}{2}\chi_{Y_{n}}+\frac{1}{2}\mathfrak{P}_{n}
has spectrum contained in
[-\frac{3}{4},1-\frac{C_{n}^{2}}{4}]\cup\{1\}
. Therefore, for any
m\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\left\|\left(\frac{1}{2}\chi_{Y_{n}}+\frac{1}{2}\mathfrak{P}_{n}\right)^{m}-\tilde{P}_{n}\right\|\leq\left(1-\frac{C_{n}^{2}}{4}\right)^{m}.

Applying
\mathrm{Ad}_{n}
and using (
5.1
), we obtain:

\left\|\frac{\tilde{\mu}_{n}(Y_{n})}{\mu(Y_{n})}\cdot\mathrm{Ad}_{n}\left[\left(\frac{1}{2}\chi_{Y_{n}}+\frac{1}{2}\mathfrak{P}_{n}\right)^{m}\right]-P_{n}\right\|\leq\frac{\tilde{\mu}_{n}(Y_{n})}{\mu(Y_{n})}\cdot\left(1-\frac{C_{n}^{2}}{4}\right)^{m}.

Note that

\frac{\tilde{\mu}_{n}(Y_{n})}{\mu(Y_{n})}\leq\|\sigma_{n}\|_{\infty}\leq\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{N_{n}}\mathfrak{R}(K_{n}^{(i)},x)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{\infty}\leq N_{n}\sqrt{\theta_{n}}.

Combining the above together, we obtain

(5.2)

\left\|\frac{\tilde{\mu}_{n}(Y_{n})}{\mu(Y_{n})}\cdot\mathrm{Ad}_{n}\left[\left(\frac{1}{2}\chi_{Y_{n}}+\frac{1}{2}\mathfrak{P}_{n}\right)^{m}\right]-P_{n}\right\|\leq N_{n}\sqrt{\theta_{n}}\cdot\left(1-\frac{C_{n}^{2}}{4}\right)^{m}.

Hence given
\epsilon>0
, we can choose
m_{n}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

(5.3)

N_{n}\sqrt{\theta_{n}}\cdot\left(1-\frac{C_{n}^{2}}{4}\right)^{m_{n}}<\frac{\epsilon}{2}.

Moreover, direct calculations show that for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
we have:

\|P_{n}-P_{\mathcal{G}}\|\leq\sqrt{\mu(\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\setminus Y_{n})}=\sqrt{\frac{1}{n+1}+\frac{nC}{(4+2C)(n+1)^{2}}}.

For
\epsilon
above, we can further choose
\tilde{N}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for any
n>\tilde{N}
we have

(5.4)

\sqrt{\frac{1}{n+1}+\frac{nC}{(4+2C)(n+1)^{2}}}<\frac{\epsilon}{2}.

Combining with (
5.2
), (
5.3
) and (
5.4
), we obtain that for any
n>\tilde{N}
we have:

\displaystyle\Big\|\frac{\tilde{\mu}_{n}(Y_{n})}{\mu(Y_{n})}\cdot\mathrm{Ad}_{n}

\displaystyle\left[\left(\frac{1}{2}\chi_{Y_{n}}+\frac{1}{2}\mathfrak{P}_{n}\right)^{m_{n}}\right]-P_{\mathcal{G}}\Big\|<\Big\|\frac{\tilde{\mu}_{n}(Y_{n})}{\mu(Y_{n})}\cdot\mathrm{Ad}_{n}\left[\left(\frac{1}{2}\chi_{Y_{n}}+\frac{1}{2}\mathfrak{P}_{n}\right)^{m_{n}}\right]-P_{n}\Big\|+\|P_{n}-P_{\mathcal{G}}\|

(5.5)

\displaystyle<N_{n}\sqrt{\theta_{n}}\cdot\left(1-\frac{C_{n}^{2}}{4}\right)^{m_{n}}+\sqrt{\frac{1}{n+1}+\frac{nC}{(4+2C)(n+1)^{2}}}<\frac{\epsilon}{2}+\frac{\epsilon}{2}=\epsilon.

Finally, for
n\in\mathbb{N}
and measurable
A,B\subseteq Y_{n}
with
\mu((K_{n}\cdot A)\cap B)=0
, we have
\tilde{\mu}_{n}((K_{n}\cdot A)\cap B)=0
by Proposition
3.16
(2). For
x\in Y_{n}
and
\xi\in L^{2}(Y_{n},\tilde{\mu}_{n})
, we have:

\displaystyle(\chi_{A}\mathfrak{P}_{n}\chi_{B}\xi)(x)

\displaystyle=\chi_{A}(x)\cdot\int_{B}\xi(y)\Pi_{n}(x,\mathrm{dy})

\displaystyle=\chi_{A}(x)\cdot\frac{1}{\sigma_{n}(x)}\sum_{i:\tau_{K_{n}^{(i)}}(x)\in B}\mathfrak{R}(K_{n}^{(i)},x)^{\frac{1}{2}}\xi(\tau_{K_{n}^{(i)}}(x)).

It follows that
\chi_{A}\mathfrak{P}_{n}\chi_{B}=0
. Note that the operator
I_{n}
does not change the propagation and hence, the following operator

\frac{\tilde{\mu}_{n}(Y_{n})}{\mu(Y_{n})}\cdot\mathrm{Ad}_{n}\left[\left(\frac{1}{2}\chi_{Y_{n}}+\frac{1}{2}\mathfrak{P}_{n}\right)^{m_{n}}\right]\quad\text{in}\quad\mathfrak{B}(L^{2}(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},\mu))

has
(N_{n}^{m_{n}},m_{n}\cdot L_{n})
-propagation.
Combining with (
5
), we conclude the proof.
∎

Remark 5.2
.

From the proof above together with Remark
3.14
and Remark
3.20
, we know that if
\mathcal{G}
is asymptotically expanding in measure, then for any
\epsilon>0
we can choose
T_{\epsilon}\in\mathbb{C}_{\mathrm{dyn}}(\mathcal{G})
with
(N_{\epsilon},L_{\epsilon})
-dynamical propagation such that
\|T_{\epsilon}-P_{\mathcal{G}}\|<\epsilon
and the functions
\epsilon\mapsto N_{\epsilon}
and
\epsilon\mapsto L_{\epsilon}
only depend on expansion parameters of
\mathcal{G}
.

In the following, we consider general rank-one projections on
L^{2}(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},\mu)
for general
\mu
. Let
P\in\mathfrak{B}(L^{2}(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},\mu))
be a rank-one projection and
\xi\in L^{2}(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},\mu)
be a unit vector in the range of
P
. Then

P(\eta)=\langle\eta,\xi\rangle\xi\quad\text{for}\quad\eta\in L^{2}(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},\mu).

This induces a probability measure
\nu
on
(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},\mathcal{R})
defined by

\mathrm{d}\nu(x)\coloneqq|\xi(x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\quad\text{for}\quad x\in\mathcal{G}^{(0)}.

It is clear that the measure
\nu
only depends on
P
, called the
associated measure to
P
.

Then we have the following:

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a groupoid with a length function
\ell
, and
(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},\mathcal{R},\mu)
be a (not necessarily finite) measure space. Let
P\in\mathfrak{B}(L^{2}(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},\mu))
be a rank-one projection, and
\nu
the associated probability measure on
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

P\in\mathbf{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{dyn}}(\mathcal{G})
;

(2)

P\in\mathbf{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{dyn},\mathrm{q}}(\mathcal{G})
;

(3)

\mathcal{G}
is asymptotically expanding in measure
\nu
.

Our idea is to apply Theorem
5.1
to the probability measure
\nu
. Firstly, note that
\nu
might
not
be equivalent to
\mu
and hence, we consider

Z\coloneqq\{x\in\mathcal{G}^{(0)}~|~\xi(x)=0\}.

Here
Z
is well-defined up to
\mu
-null sets. We set
Y\coloneqq\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\setminus Z
and then the restrictions
\mu|_{Y}
and
\nu|_{Y}
are equivalent. Now we consider the reduction
\mathcal{G}_{Y}^{Y}=\mathrm{s}^{-1}(Y)\cap\mathrm{r}^{-1}(Y)
,
equipped with the restriction
\ell_{Y}
of the length function
\ell
. Denote

Q:L^{2}(Y,\mu|_{Y})\longrightarrow L^{2}(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},\mu)

the embedding by extending functions in
L^{2}(Y,\mu|_{Y})
to
0
on
Z
. Then
we have:

Lemma 5.4
.

With the notation above, we have:

(1)

P\in\mathbf{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{dyn},\mathrm{q}}(\mathcal{G})

if and only if

Q^{*}PQ\in\mathbf{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{dyn},\mathrm{q}}(\mathcal{G}_{Y}^{Y})
;

(2)

P\in\mathbf{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{dyn}}(\mathcal{G})

if and only if

Q^{*}PQ\in\mathbf{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{dyn}}(\mathcal{G}_{Y}^{Y})
.

Here we equip
\mathcal{G}_{Y}^{Y}
with the length function
\ell_{Y}
and
(\mathcal{G}_{Y}^{Y})^{(0)}=Y
with the measure
\mu|_{Y}
to define
\mathbf{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{dyn}}(\mathcal{G}_{Y}^{Y})
and
\mathbf{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{dyn},\mathrm{q}}(\mathcal{G}_{Y}^{Y})
.

Proof.

Here we only prove (1), and (2) is similar.

Necessity
: By assumption, for any
\epsilon>0
there exists a unital symmetric decomposable subset
K\subseteq\mathcal{G}
such that for any measurable
A,B\subseteq\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
with
\mu(\mathrm{r}(K\cdot A)\cap B)=0
, we have
\|\chi_{A}P\chi_{B}\|<\epsilon
. Set
K^{\prime}\coloneqq K\cap\mathcal{G}_{Y}^{Y}
, which is clearly unital symmetric and decomposable in
\mathcal{G}_{Y}^{Y}
. For any measurable
A^{\prime},B^{\prime}\subseteq Y
with
\mu(\mathrm{r}(K^{\prime}\cdot A^{\prime})\cap B^{\prime})=0
, it is easy to see that
\mu(\mathrm{r}(K\cdot A^{\prime})\cap B^{\prime})=0
. Hence

\|\chi_{A^{\prime}}Q^{*}PQ\chi_{B^{\prime}}\|=\|Q^{*}\chi_{A^{\prime}}P\chi_{B^{\prime}}Q\|\leq\|\chi_{A^{\prime}}P\chi_{B^{\prime}}\|<\epsilon.

Sufficiency
: By assumption, for any
\epsilon>0
there exists a unital symmetric decomposable subset
K\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{Y}^{Y}
such that for any measurable subsets
A,B\subseteq Y
with
\mu(\mathrm{r}(K\cdot A)\cap B)=0
, we have
\|\chi_{A}Q^{*}PQ\chi_{B}\|<\epsilon
. Set
\hat{K}\coloneqq K\cup\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
, which is clearly unital symmetric and decomposable in
\mathcal{G}
. By the construction of
Q
, we have

P=Q(Q^{*}PQ)Q^{*}.

Hence for any measurable
\hat{A},\hat{B}\subseteq\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
with
\mu(\mathrm{r}(\hat{K}\cdot\hat{A})\cap\hat{B})=0
, we have

\|\chi_{\hat{A}}P\chi_{\hat{B}}\|=\|\chi_{\hat{A}}Q(Q^{*}PQ)Q^{*}\chi_{\hat{B}}\|=\|Q\chi_{\hat{A}\cap Y}(Q^{*}PQ)\chi_{\hat{B}\cap Y}Q^{*}\|\leq\|\chi_{\hat{A}\cap Y}(Q^{*}PQ)\chi_{\hat{B}\cap Y}\|<\epsilon.

Here we use the fact that
\mu\big(\mathrm{r}(K\cdot(\hat{A}\cap Y))\cap(\hat{B}\cap Y)\big)=0
.
∎

Consequently, to prove Theorem
5.3
we only need consider the case that
\mu
and
\nu
are equivalent,
i.e.
,
\mu(Z)=0
. Therefore in the following, we assume that
Z=\emptyset
.

Now we form the associated dynamical Roe and quasi-local algebras for
\mathcal{G}
using the measures
\mu
and
\nu
on
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
. To tell the difference, we denote
\mathbf{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{dyn}}(\mathcal{G};\mu)
and
\mathbf{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{dyn},\mathrm{q}}(\mathcal{G};\mu)
for the algebras defined using
\mu
, while
\mathbf{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{dyn}}(\mathcal{G};\nu)
and
\mathbf{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{dyn},\mathrm{q}}(\mathcal{G};\nu)
for those using
\nu
.
Then we have the following:

Lemma 5.5
.

With the notation above and assuming
Z=\emptyset
, we have:

(1)

P\in\mathbf{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{dyn},\mathrm{q}}(\mathcal{G};\mu)

if and only if

P_{\mathcal{G}}\in\mathbf{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{dyn},\mathrm{q}}(\mathcal{G};\nu)
;

(2)

P\in\mathbf{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{dyn}}(\mathcal{G};\mu)

if and only if

P_{\mathcal{G}}\in\mathbf{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{dyn}}(\mathcal{G};\nu)
.

Here
P_{\mathcal{G}}
is the averaging projection in
\mathfrak{B}^{2}(L^{2}(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},\nu))
from Definition
4.5
.

Proof.

We only prove (1), since (2) is similar. Denote

U:L^{2}(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},\nu)\longrightarrow L^{2}(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},\mu),\quad f\mapsto f\cdot\xi\quad\text{for}\quad f\in L^{2}(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},\nu).

By the construction and the assumption that
Z=\emptyset
(which implies that
\xi
is non-zero everywhere), it is easy to check that
U
is a unitary operator with
U^{*}\eta=\frac{1}{\xi}\cdot\eta
for
\eta\in L^{2}(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},\mu)
.
Denote

\mathrm{Ad}_{U}:\mathfrak{B}(L^{2}(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},\nu))\longrightarrow\mathfrak{B}(L^{2}(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},\mu)),\quad T\mapsto UTU^{*}\quad\text{for}\quad T\in\mathfrak{B}(L^{2}(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},\nu)).

We claim:
\mathrm{Ad}_{U}(P_{\mathcal{G}})=P
. In fact, given
\eta\in L^{2}(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},\mu)
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ad}_{U}(P_{\mathcal{G}})\eta

\displaystyle=UP_{\mathcal{G}}U^{*}\eta=UP_{\mathcal{G}}\left(\frac{1}{\xi}\cdot\eta\right)=U\left(\big\langle\frac{1}{\xi}\cdot\eta,\chi_{\mathcal{G}^{(0)}}\big\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},\nu)}\chi_{\mathcal{G}^{(0)}}\right)

\displaystyle=\big\langle\frac{1}{\xi}\cdot\eta,\chi_{\mathcal{G}^{(0)}}\big\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},\nu)}\xi=\left(\int_{\mathcal{G}^{(0)}}\frac{1}{\xi}(x)\eta(x)\mathrm{d}\nu(x)\right)\cdot\xi

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{\mathcal{G}^{(0)}}\eta(x)\xi(x)\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\right)\cdot\xi=\langle\eta,\xi\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathcal{G}^{(0)},\mu)}\xi=P\eta.

Hence we prove the claim. Therefore, for any measurable
A,B\subseteq\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
we have

\mathrm{Ad}_{U}(\chi_{A}P_{\mathcal{G}}\chi_{B})=\mathrm{Ad}_{U}(\chi_{A})\circ\mathrm{Ad}_{U}(P_{\mathcal{G}})\circ\mathrm{Ad}_{U}(\chi_{B})=\chi_{A}P\chi_{B}.

Finally, since
\mu
and
\nu
are equivalent, we have
K\subseteq\mathcal{G}
is decomposable with respect to
\mu
if and only if it is decomposable with respect to
\nu
. Moreover, for any measurable
A,B\subseteq\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
and unital symmetric decomposable
K\subseteq\mathcal{G}
, we have
\mu(\mathrm{r}(K\cdot A)\cap B)=0
if and only if
\nu(\mathrm{r}(K\cdot A)\cap B)=0
. So we finish the proof.
∎

Now we are in the position to prove Theorem
5.3
:

Proof of Theorem
5.3
.

By Lemma
5.4
, we can assume that
Z=\emptyset
. Assume
P
is dynamically quasi-local. It follows from Lemma
5.5
that
P_{\mathcal{G}}\in\mathbf{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{dyn},\mathrm{q}}(\mathcal{G};\nu)
. Applying Theorem
5.1
, we obtain that
P_{\mathcal{G}}\in\mathbf{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{dyn}}(\mathcal{G};\nu)
. Then using Lemma
5.5
again, we obtain that
P\in\mathbf{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{dyn}}(\mathcal{G};\mu)=\mathbf{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{dyn}}(\mathcal{G})
.
∎

Remark 5.6
.

Note that the map
\mathrm{Ad}_{U}
in the proof of Lemma
5.5
preserves propagation. Hence combining with Remark
4.8
and
5.2
, we know that if the rank-one projection
P
belongs to
\mathbf{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{dyn},\mathrm{q}}(\mathcal{G})

-dynamical propagation such that

and

.

Finally, we introduce two variants of Theorem
5.3
.

. Combining Lemma
4.4
and Theorem
5.3
together with Remark

, we obtain:

Theorem 5.3’
.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\mathcal{G}_{n}
be a groupoid with a length function

and
(\mathcal{G}^{(0)}_{n},\mathcal{R}_{n},\mu_{n})

\mathcal{G}=\bigsqcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{G}_{n}

on
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}

4.1
. Moreover, let
P_{n}\in\mathfrak{B}(L^{2}(\mathcal{G}^{(0)}_{n},\mu_{n}))

Then

;

(2)

\mathcal{G}_{n}

:

.

,

In this section, we apply our theory to several classes of groupoids and recover main results in
[
17
,
22
,

]
.

6.1.
Transformation groupoids

Our first example comes from group actions, which is one of our main motivations for this work.

Let
\rho:\Gamma\curvearrowright X
be a countable group
\Gamma
acting on a set
X
, and
\ell_{\Gamma}
be a proper word length function on
\Gamma
in the sense that for any
L>0
, the closed ball denoted by

B_{L}\coloneqq\{\gamma\in\Gamma~|~\ell_{\Gamma}(\gamma)\leq L\}

is finite.
Consider the
transformation groupoid

X\rtimes\Gamma
by
\mathrm{s}(x,\gamma)=\gamma^{-1}x
,
\mathrm{r}(x,\gamma)=x
and
(x,\gamma)^{-1}=(\gamma^{-1}x,\gamma^{-1})
. The length function
\ell_{\Gamma}
naturally gives rise to a length function
\ell
on
X\rtimes\Gamma
by
\ell(x,\gamma)\coloneqq\ell_{\Gamma}(\gamma)
for
x\in X
and
\gamma\in\Gamma
.

Moreover, let
\mu
be a measure on
(X,\mathcal{R})
for some
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{R}
and assume that the action
\rho
is measure-class-preserving. Then for
\gamma\in\Gamma
, the map
\tau_{X\times\{\gamma\}}:X\to X
coincides with
\rho(\gamma)
(simply denoted by
\gamma
) and
X\times\{\gamma\}
is admissible.

Concerning decomposable subsets in
X\rtimes\Gamma
, we have the following. The proof is straightforward and hence omitted.

Lemma 6.1
.

For any
L>0
, the subset
X\times B_{L}
is unital symmetric
|B_{L}|
-decomposable with
\ell(X\times B_{L})\leq L
, and it admits a unital symmetric decomposition
X\times B_{L}=\bigcup_{\gamma\in B_{L}}X\times\{\gamma\}
where each
X\times\{\gamma\}
is admissible. Here we use
|\cdot|
to denote the cardinality.
Conversely, for any decomposable
K\subseteq X\rtimes\Gamma
, we have
K\subseteq X\times B_{\ell(K)}
.

Hence it suffices to consider decomposable subsets of the form
X\times B_{L}
, which is determined by its length. Therefore, the notion of (asymptotic) expansion in measure can be translated as follows:

Proposition 6.2
.

In the above setting, the transformation groupoid
X\rtimes\Gamma
is asymptotically expanding in measure in the sense of Definition
3.2

if and only if
the action is asymptotically expanding in measure.
A similar result holds for the notion of expansion and domain of (asymptotic) expansion.

Consequently, Theorem
3.10
and Theorem
3.19
recover
[
21
, Theorem 4.6]
and
[
22
, Theorem 3.20]
, respectively.

On the other hand, applying Lemma
6.1
again, the notion of dynamical propagation and quasi-locality can be translated as follows:

Proposition 6.3
.

In the above setting, an operator
T\in L^{2}(X,\mu)
is dynamically quasi-local in the sense of Definition
4.2

if and only if

T
is
\rho
-quasi-local in the sense of
[
22
, Definition 4.3]
,
i.e.
, for any
\epsilon>0
there exists
L_{\epsilon}>0
such that for any measurable
A,B\subseteq X
with
\mu((B_{L_{\epsilon}}\cdot A)\cap B)=0
, then
\|\chi_{A}T\chi_{B}\|<\epsilon
. A similar result holds for the notion of finite dynamical propagation.

Consequently, our main result Theorem
5.1
recovers
[
22
, Proposition 4.6 and Theorem 4.16]
for the averaging projection when
\mu
is finite:

Corollary 6.4
.

Let
\rho:\Gamma\curvearrowright X
be a countable group
\Gamma
acting on a probability measure space
(X,\mu)

K\cdot A=\mathrm{r}\big((Y\mathchoice{\hphantom{{}_{{{p}}}}\ast^{{\kern-5.52933pt\kern 5.91821pt}}_{{\kern-8.49464pt{p}\kern 5.91821pt{\mathrm{r}}}}}{\hphantom{{}_{{{p}}}}\ast^{{\kern-5.52933pt\kern 5.91821pt}}_{{\kern-8.49464pt{p}\kern 5.91821pt{\mathrm{r}}}}}{\hphantom{{}_{{{p}}}}\ast^{{\kern-3.8821pt\kern 4.27098pt}}_{{\kern-6.14304pt{p}\kern 4.27098pt{\mathrm{r}}}}}{\hphantom{{}_{{{p}}}}\ast^{{\kern-3.1821pt\kern 3.57098pt}}_{{\kern-5.44304pt{p}\kern 3.57098pt{\mathrm{r}}}}}K)\cdot A\big).

Hence it turns out that the

-asymptotic expansion of the semi-direct groupoid

Definition 6.19
.

6.4.
The HLS groupoid and its variant

Throughout this subsection, let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated group and
\{N_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be a family of nested, finite index normal subgroups of
\Gamma
with trivial intersection. For each
i\in\mathbb{N}
, denote the quotient map
\pi_{i}:\Gamma\to\Gamma/N_{i}
, and
\pi_{\infty}:\Gamma\to\Gamma
the identity map.

The associated
HLS groupoid
(after Higson, Lafforgue and Skandalis)
\mathcal{G}
is defined to be

\mathcal{G}\coloneqq\bigsqcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}}\{i\}\times X_{i},\quad\text{where}\quad X_{i}=\begin{cases}\Gamma/N_{i}\quad&\text{if}\quad i\in\mathbb{N};\\
\Gamma\quad&\text{if}\quad i=\infty.\end{cases}

Equipping
\mathcal{G}
with the topology generated by the following sets:

•

the singletons
\{(i,g)\}
for
i\in\mathbb{N}
and
g\in\Gamma/N_{i}
;

•

the tails
\{(i,\pi_{i}(g))~|~i\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\},i>N\}
for
g\in\Gamma
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
.

then
\mathcal{G}
becomes an étale, locally compact and Hausdorff groupoid.

The HLS groupoid was introduced by Higson, Lafforgue and Skandalis in
[
15
]
to provide counterexamples to the groupoid Baum-Connes conjecture. It also provides the first known example of a non-amenable groupoid with the weak containment property due to Willett
[
33
]
.

Since the HLS groupoid
\mathcal{G}
is a bundle of groups, it cannot be asymptotically expanding for any given measure and length function. When regarding
\mathcal{G}
as a family of groups, then trivially it is uniformly asymptotically expanding since each unit space consists of a single point.

Now we consider a variant of the HLS groupoid from
[
2
]
, which provides the first known example of a principal non-amenable groupoid with the weak containment property. To recall their construction, we use the same notation as above and denote
\hat{\Gamma}\coloneqq\varprojlim\Gamma/N_{i}
the profinite completion of
\Gamma
with respect to the family
\{\Gamma/N_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
.

For each
i\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{AFS}}_{i}
be the transformation groupoid
X_{i}\rtimes(\Gamma/N_{i})
with the action by left multiplication. For
i=\infty
, let
\mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{AFS}}_{\infty}
be the transformation groupoid
\hat{\Gamma}\rtimes\Gamma
with the natural free action. Then the groupoid constructed in
[
2
]
is defined to be the disjoint union (thanks to
[
2
, Lemma 2.1]
):

\mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{AFS}}\coloneqq\bigsqcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}}\mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{AFS}}_{i}.

To consider asymptotic expansion, we need to endow measure and length function for each
\mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{AFS}}_{i}
. For
i\in\mathbb{N}
, we take the normalised counting measure
\mu_{i}
on
\Gamma/N_{i}
. For
i=\infty
, we take the induced probability
\Gamma
-invariant measure
\mu_{\infty}
on
\hat{\Gamma}
. On the other hand, fix a length function
\ell
on
\Gamma
, which induces a quotient length function
\ell_{i}
on
\Gamma/N_{i}
for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
. Following Section
6.1
, we obtain a length function on
\mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{AFS}}_{i}
for each
i\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
. By the discussions in Section
4.1
, these can be combined to provide a measure and a length function for
\mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{AFS}}
.

We have the following characterisation:

Proposition 6.22
.

With the same notation as above, the following are equivalent:

(1)

\mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{AFS}}_{i}
is asymptotically expanding in measure uniformly for
i\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
(
i.e.
, having same expansion parameters);

(2)

the natural action of
\Gamma
on
\hat{\Gamma}
is asymptotically expanding in measure;

(3)

the natural action of
\Gamma
on
\hat{\Gamma}
is expanding in measure;

(4)

the sequence
\{\Gamma/N_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
forms a sequence of asymptotic expander graphs;

(5)

the sequence
\{\Gamma/N_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
forms a sequence of expander graphs;

(6)

\mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{AFS}}_{i}
is expanding in measure uniformly for
i\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
.

Proof.

“(1)
\Rightarrow
(2)”: Condition (1) implies that
\mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{AFS}}_{\infty}
is asymptotically expanding in measure. Then by Proposition
6.2
, this implies (2).

“(2)
\Leftrightarrow
(3)” is due to
[
1
, Theorem 4]
and
[
21
, Proposition 3.5]
.

“(3)
\Leftrightarrow
(5)” is fairly well-known (see,
e.g.
,
[
1
, Lemma 2.2]
).

“(2)
\Leftrightarrow
(4)” is due to
[
21
, Theorem 6.16, Corollary 6.17]
and the discussions thereafter. (Note that the sequence
\{\Gamma/N_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is approximating spaces for the action of
\Gamma
on
\hat{\Gamma}
.)

“(5)
\Rightarrow
(6)”: For each
i\in\mathbb{N}
, note that
\mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{AFS}}_{i}
is isomorphic to the pair groupoid
(\Gamma/N_{i})\times(\Gamma/N_{i})
, where the length function corresponds to the one given by
(x,y)\mapsto d_{i}(x,y)
for the left-invariant metric
d_{i}
on
\Gamma/N_{i}
induced by
\ell_{i}
. According to Proposition
6.9
, (4) is equivalent to that
\mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{AFS}}_{i}
is expanding uniformly for
i\in\mathbb{N}
. This concludes (5) since we already showed “(4)
\Rightarrow
(3)”.

“(6)
\Rightarrow
(1)” is trivial.
∎

Finally, combining with Theorem
5.3’
, we obtain the following:

Corollary 6.23
.

For the groupoid
\mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{AFS}}
, take
P_{i}\in\mathfrak{B}(L^{2}(X_{i},\mu_{i}))
to be the averaging projection for
i\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
. For
P=\mathrm{(SOT)}-\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}}P_{i}
, the following are equivalent:

(1)

P\in\mathbf{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{u}}(\mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{AFS}})
;

(2)

P\in\mathbf{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{uq}}(\mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{AFS}})
;

(3)

the natural action of
\Gamma
on
\hat{\Gamma}
is asymptotically expanding in measure;

(4)

the sequence
\{\Gamma/N_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
forms a sequence of expander graphs.

6.5.
Graph groupoids

We firstly recall necessary background knowledge, and details can be found in
[
18
]
.

Throughout this subsection, let
G=(V,E,r,s)
be a directed uniformly locally finite connected graph, where
V
is the vertex set,
E
is the edge set and
r,s:E\rightarrow V
describing the range and source of edges. Here
G
is called
uniformly locally finite
if
\sup_{v\in V}|s^{-1}(v)|
and
\sup_{v\in V}|r^{-1}(v)|
are finite.
Given
v\in V
, denote
s\text{-}\mathrm{deg}(v)\coloneqq|s^{-1}(v)|
.

Recall that a
finite path
in
G
is a sequence
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{k})
of edges in
E
with
s(\alpha_{j+1})=r(\alpha_{j})
for
1\leq j\leq k-1
. Write
s(\alpha)=s(\alpha_{1})
and
r(\alpha)=\mathrm{r}(\alpha_{k})
. The
length
of
\alpha
is defined to be
|\alpha|\coloneqq k
. We denote by
v
the path of length
0
with
s(v)=r(v)=v
.
Denote
F(G)
the set of all finite paths in
G
, and
F(G,v)
those starting at
v\in V
. Similarly, denote
P(G)
the set of all infinite paths
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},...)
in
G
, and
P(G,v)
those starting at
v\in V
. For
\alpha,\mu\in F(G)
satisfying
r(\alpha)=s(\mu)
, we define a path
\alpha\mu\in F(G)
of length
|\alpha|+|\mu|
by
\alpha\mu=(\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{|\alpha|},\mu_{1},...,\mu_{|\mu|})
. We can similarly define
\alpha x\in P(G)
for
\alpha\in F(G)
and
x\in P(G)
satisfying
s(x)=r(\alpha)
.

Equip
P(G)
with the induced topology from the product topology on the infinite product
\Pi E
of the edge set
E
.
By
[
18
, Corollary 2.2]
, this topology is locally compact,
\sigma
-compact, totally disconnected and Hausdorff. Moreover, it has a basis consisting of all the
cylinder sets
:

Z(\alpha)=\left\{x\in P(G)~|~x_{1}=\alpha_{1},...,x_{|\alpha|}=\alpha_{|\alpha|}\right\}\quad\text{where}\quad\alpha\in F(G).

By
[
18
, Lemma 2.1]
,
Z(\alpha)\cap Z(\beta)\neq\emptyset
if and only if either
\alpha
is a
prefix
of
\beta
(
i.e.
,
\beta=\alpha\beta^{\prime}
for some
\beta^{\prime}\in F(G)
) or
\beta
is a prefix of
\alpha
.

To construct the groupoid associated to the graph
G
, we consider the following equivalence relation on
P(G)
: two paths
x,y\in P(G)
are
shift equivalent
with lag
k\in\mathbb{Z}
(written
x\sim_{k}y
) if there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
x_{i}=y_{i+k}
for all
i\geq N
.

Definition 6.24
.

For a directed uniformly locally finite connected graph
G=(V,E,r,s)
, the associated
graph groupoid

\mathcal{G}
is defined to be (as a set):

\mathcal{G}=\{(x,k,y)\in P(G)\times\mathbb{Z}\times P(G)~|~x\sim_{k}y\}

with
(x,k,y)\cdot(y,l,z)\coloneqq(x,k+l,z)
and
(x,k,y)^{-1}\coloneqq(y,-k,x)
. The range and source maps are given by
\mathrm{r}(x,k,y)=x
and
\mathrm{s}(x,k,y)=y
, respectively, with
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}=P(G)
.

For
\alpha,\beta\in F(G)
with
r(\alpha)=r(\beta)
, denote

Z(\alpha,\beta)\coloneqq\left\{(x,k,y)~|~x\in Z(\alpha),y\in Z(\beta),k=|\beta|-|\alpha|,x_{i}=y_{i+k}~\text{for}~i\geq|\alpha|\right\},

which is a bisection in
\mathcal{G}
with
\mathrm{r}(Z(\alpha,\beta))=Z(\alpha)
and
\mathrm{s}(Z(\alpha,\beta))=Z(\beta)
.
It follows from
[
18
, Proposition 2.6]
that all the sets
Z(\alpha,\beta)
form a basis for a locally compact Hausdorff topology on
\mathcal{G}
, which makes it a second countable, étale groupoid where each
Z(\alpha,\beta)
is compact open. The induced topology on
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}=P(G)
coincides with the product topology defined above.

To apply our results to the graph groupoid
\mathcal{G}
, we firstly endow
\mathcal{G}
with the following length function
\ell
. For
(x,k,y)\in\mathcal{G}
, choose the smallest
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
x_{i}=y_{i+k}
for all
i\geq N
, denoted by
N(x,k,y)
, and set:

(6.4)

\ell(x,k,y)=2N(x,k,y)+k.

Note that
N(x,k,y)+k\geq 0
for any
(x,k,y)\in\mathcal{G}
.
Intuitively,
\ell(x,k,y)
is the total length of the shortest starting segments in
x
and
y
, deleting which
x
and
y
are the same with lag
k
.
It is routine to check that
\ell
is indeed a length function on
\mathcal{G}
taking values in
\mathbb{N}
. Moreover, given
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the ball in
\mathcal{G}
defined by
B_{n}\coloneqq\{(x,k,y)\in\mathcal{G}~|~\ell(x,k,y)\leq n\}
can be reconstructed as follows:

(6.5)

B_{n}=\bigcup\left\{Z(\alpha,\beta)~|~\alpha,\beta\in F(G)\text{ with }r(\alpha)=r(\beta)\text{ and }|\alpha|+|\beta|\leq n\right\}.

Moreover, we have

(6.6)

B_{n}=(B_{1})^{n}\quad\text{for any}\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

These can be verified by direct calculations, and we omit the details.

Next, we will define a Borel measure
\mu
on
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}=P(G)
. Given
v\in V
and
b_{v}>0
, we define a measure
\mu_{v}
on
P(G,v)
by firstly setting:

(6.7)

\mu_{v}(Z(\alpha))\coloneqq b_{v}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{1}{s\text{-}\mathrm{deg}(s(\alpha_{i}))}\quad\text{for}\quad\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\cdots,\alpha_{k})\in F(G,v).

Since the sets
Z(\alpha)
for
\alpha\in F(G,v)
form a basis for the topology on
P(G,v)
, then the above gives rise to a Borel measure
\mu_{v}
on
P(G,v)
.

Now we combine
\mu_{v}
into a single probability Borel measure
\mu
on
P(G)
. Since
G
is connected and uniformly locally finite, then
V
is countable. For each
v\in V
, we choose
b_{v}>0
such that
\sum_{v\in V}b_{v}=1
. Note that
P(G)=\bigsqcup_{v\in V}P(G,v)
and
V
is countable. Then the measures
\mu_{v}
defined above give rise to a Borel measure
\mu
on
P(G)
. In the sequel, we fix such a measure
\mu
.

By the construction of
\ell
and
\mu
above, it is clear that for any
\alpha,\beta\in F(G)
with
r(\alpha)=r(\beta)
, the subset
Z(\alpha,\beta)
is an admissible bisection. Moreover, we have:

Lemma 6.25
.

For the graph groupoid
\mathcal{G}
with the length function
\ell
and the measure
\mu
on
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
above, the ball
B_{n}=\{(x,k,y)\in\mathcal{G}~|~\ell(x,k,y)\leq n\}
is decomposable for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
. We aim to decompose the ball
B_{n}
into finitely many admissible bisections. The idea is to combine several
Z(\alpha,\beta)
in (
6.5
) into a single bisection.

To achieve, we construct another graph
\hat{G}=(\hat{V},\hat{E})
with

\hat{V}\coloneqq\big\{(\alpha,\beta)~\big|~\alpha,\beta\in F(G)\text{ with }r(\alpha)=r(\beta)\text{ and }|\alpha|+|\beta|\leq n\big\}

and two vertices
(\alpha_{1},\beta_{1})
and
(\alpha_{2},\beta_{2})
in
\hat{V}
are connected by an edge if and only if at least one of the following four cases hold: (i)
\alpha_{1}
is a prefix of
\alpha_{2}
; (ii)
\alpha_{2}
is a prefix of
\alpha_{1}
; (iii)
\beta_{1}
is a prefix of
\beta_{2}
; (iv)
\beta_{2}
is a prefix of
\beta_{1}
.
Since
G
is uniformly locally finite and connected, then
\hat{G}
is uniformly locally finite as well. It is a well-known fact (see,
e.g.
,
[
31
, Lemma 4.2]
) that
\hat{V}
can be decomposed into
\hat{V}=\bigsqcup_{p=1}^{N}\hat{V}_{p}
such that for each
p
, any two distinct vertices in
\hat{V}_{p}
are
not
connected by an edge.

For each
p=1,\cdots,N
, set

K_{p}\coloneqq\bigsqcup_{(\alpha,\beta)\in\hat{V}_{p}}Z(\alpha,\beta).

By the construction of
\hat{E}
, it is routine to check that
K_{p}
is an admissible bisection. Finally, (
6.5
) implies
B_{n}=\bigcup_{p=1}^{N}K_{p}
and hence, we conclude the proof.
∎

Lemma
6.25
shows that the set
\{B_{n}~|~n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is cofinal in the set of all decomposable subsets of
\mathcal{G}
in the sense of Remark
5.8
. Hence we can merely use these balls to define asymptotic expansion, dynamical propagation and quasi-locality for graph groupoids by the discussion at the end of Section
5
.

Moreover, we have the following:

Lemma 6.26
.

For the graph groupoid
\mathcal{G}
with the length function
\ell
and the measure
\mu
on
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
above, then
\mathcal{G}
is asymptotically expanding in measure
if and only if
for any
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
, there exists
C_{\alpha}>0
such that for any measurable
A\subseteq\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
with
\alpha\leq\mu(A)\leq\frac{1}{2}
, then
\mu(\mathrm{r}(B_{1}\cdot A))>(1+C_{\alpha})\mu(A)
.

Thanks to Lemma
6.25
and (
6.6
), the proof follows almost the same as that for
[
21
, Lemma 3.16]
, and hence we omit the proof. In fact, a similar result (
i.e.
, we can always choose the decomposable subset
K
in the definition of asymptotic expansion to be the unit ball
B_{1}
) holds provided each ball
B_{n}=\{\gamma\in\mathcal{G}~|~\ell(\gamma)\leq n\}
is decomposable and
B_{n}=(B_{1})^{n}
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Lemma
6.26
can be further refined as follows under an extra assumption:

Proposition 6.27
.

Let
G
be a uniformly locally finite connected graph, and
\mathcal{G}
the associated graph groupoid with the length function
\ell
and the measure
\mu
on
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
above. Moreover, assume that there exists
C\geq 1
such that
\frac{b_{w}}{C}\leq b_{v}\leq Cb_{w}
for any
v,w\in V
connected by an edge, where
(b_{v})_{v}
are the weights used to define
\mu
. Then
\mathcal{G}
is asymptotically expanding in measure
if and only if
for any
0<\alpha\leq\frac{1}{2}
, there exists
C_{\alpha}>0
such that for any measurable
A\subseteq\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
of the form
A=\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}Z(\alpha_{i})
with
\alpha\leq\mu(A)\leq\frac{1}{2}
for
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
\alpha_{i}\in F(G)
, we have
\mu(\mathrm{r}(B_{1}\cdot A))>(1+C_{\alpha})\mu(A)
.

Proof.

By Lemma
6.26
, we only need to prove sufficiency. Since
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}=P(G)
is a locally compact Hausdorff space in which every open subset is
\sigma
-compact and
\mu
is finite, it follows from
[
12
, Theorem 7.8]
that
\mu
is regular. Take
C
as in the assumption and set

D=\max\left\{\sup_{v\in V}|s^{-1}(v)|,\,\sup_{v\in V}|r^{-1}(v)|\right\}.

We claim that for any measurable
A^{\prime}\subseteq P(G)
, we have

(6.8)

\mu(\mathrm{r}(B_{1}\cdot A^{\prime}))\leq CD(D+2)\mu(A^{\prime}).

Indeed, for any
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{k})\in F(G)
with
k\geq 1
, set

F(G)_{\alpha}\coloneqq\{\alpha\}\cup\{(\alpha_{2},\cdots,\alpha_{k})\}\cup\{(\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{k})~|~\alpha_{0}\in E\text{ with }r(\alpha_{0})=s(\alpha_{1})\}.

Here we take the convention that if
k=1
, then
(\alpha_{2},\cdots,\alpha_{k})=r(\alpha_{1})
. By the choice of
D
, we have
|F(G)_{\alpha}|\leq D+2
. Moreover, for any
\beta\in F(G)_{\alpha}
, we have

\mu(Z(\beta))\leq CD\mu(Z(\alpha))

by the definition of
\mu
(see (
6.7
)). Hence we obtain:

\mu(\mathrm{r}(B_{1}\cdot Z(\alpha)))\leq\sum_{\beta\in F(G)_{\alpha}}\mu(Z(\beta))\leq CD(D+2)\mu(Z_{\alpha}).

Denote
\mathcal{M}\coloneqq\{\text{measurable }A^{\prime}\subseteq P(G)~|~(\ref{EQ:upper bound for expansion})\text{ holds for }A^{\prime}\}
. Note that the set
\mathcal{P}\coloneqq\{Z(\alpha)~|~\alpha\in F(G)\}
is a semi-ring and hence, the analysis above implies that
\mathcal{M}
contains the ring
R(\mathcal{P})
generated by
\mathcal{P}
. Due to the Monotone Class Theorem, we know that
\mathcal{M}
contains the
\sigma
-algebra generated by
\mathcal{P}
, which coincides with the Borel
\sigma
-algebra on
P(G)
. Hence we conclude the claim.

Now given
0<\alpha\leq\frac{1}{2}
, we have a constant
C_{\alpha/2}>0
from the assumption for
\alpha/2
. Also given a measurable
A\subseteq P(G)
with
\alpha\leq\mu(A)<\frac{1}{2}
, take

\epsilon_{0}\coloneqq\min\left\{\frac{\alpha}{2},\frac{1}{2}-\mu(A),\frac{\alpha C_{\alpha/2}}{2+2C_{\alpha/2}+2CD(D+2)}\right\}>0.

Due to regularity, there is an open subset
\tilde{A}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}Z(\alpha_{i})
for
\alpha_{i}\in F(G)
such that
\mu(A{\,\resizebox{524290}{}{$\triangle$}\,}\tilde{A})<\epsilon_{0}
, which implies that
\frac{\alpha}{2}\leq\mu(\tilde{A})\leq\frac{1}{2}
. By the claim above, we have:

\displaystyle\mu(\mathrm{r}(B_{1}\cdot A))

\displaystyle\geq\mu(\mathrm{r}(B_{1}\cdot\tilde{A}))-\mu(\mathrm{r}(B_{1}\cdot(A{\,\resizebox{524290}{}{$\triangle$}\,}\tilde{A})))>(1+C_{\alpha/2})\mu(\tilde{A})-CD(D+2)\mu(A{\,\resizebox{524290}{}{$\triangle$}\,}\tilde{A})

\displaystyle\geq(1+C_{\alpha/2})(\mu(A)-\epsilon_{0})-CD(D+2)\epsilon_{0}

(6.9)

\displaystyle\geq(1+C_{\alpha/2})\mu(A)-\frac{\alpha C_{\alpha/2}}{2}\geq(1+\frac{C_{\alpha/2}}{2})\mu(A).

Finally, we consider measurable
A\subseteq P(G)
with
\mu(A)=\frac{1}{2}
. Take

\epsilon_{1}\coloneqq\min\left\{\frac{3}{8},~\frac{C_{1/8}}{32+4C_{1/8}+32CD(D+2)}\right\}>0.

Without loss of generality, assume
C_{1/8}\leq 1
. Again due to regularity, we choose open subset
\tilde{A}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}Z(\alpha_{i})
for
\alpha_{i}\in F(G)
such that
\mu(A{\,\resizebox{524290}{}{$\triangle$}\,}\tilde{A})<\epsilon_{1}
. This implies that
\frac{1}{2}-\epsilon_{1}<\mu(\tilde{A})<\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon_{1}
. We need to control the expansion of
\tilde{A}
.

If
\mu(\tilde{A})\leq\frac{1}{2}
, then by assumption we have
\mu(\mathrm{r}(B_{1}\cdot\tilde{A})\setminus\tilde{A})\geq C_{1/8}\mu(A)
. Otherwise, set
\tilde{B}\coloneqq P(G)\setminus(\mathrm{r}(B_{1}\cdot\tilde{A}))
and then
\mu(\tilde{B})<\frac{1}{2}
. If
\mu(\tilde{B})<\frac{1}{4}
, then
\mu(\mathrm{r}(B_{1}\cdot\tilde{A}))=1-\mu(\tilde{B})>1-\frac{1}{4}\geq\frac{3}{2}\mu(A)
. If
\mu(\tilde{B})\geq\frac{1}{4}
, then applying the estimate (
6.5
) to
\tilde{B}
we obtain
\mu(\mathrm{r}(B_{1}\cdot\tilde{B}))>(1+\frac{C_{1/8}}{2})\mu(\tilde{B})
. Hence

\mu(\mathrm{r}(B_{1}\cdot\tilde{A})\setminus\tilde{A})\geq\mu(\mathrm{r}(B_{1}\cdot\tilde{B})\setminus\tilde{B})>\frac{C_{1/8}}{2}\mu(\tilde{B})\geq\frac{C_{1/8}}{8}\cdot\mu(\tilde{A}).

In conclusion, we obtain
\mu(\mathrm{r}(B_{1}\cdot\tilde{A}))>(1+\frac{C_{1/8}}{8})\mu(\tilde{A})
. Then we obtain:

\displaystyle\mu(\mathrm{r}(B_{1}\cdot A))

\displaystyle\geq\mu(\mathrm{r}(B_{1}\cdot\tilde{A}))-\mu(\mathrm{r}(B_{1}\cdot(A{\,\resizebox{524290}{}{$\triangle$}\,}\tilde{A})))>(1+\frac{C_{1/8}}{8})\mu(\tilde{A})-CD(D+2)\mu(A{\,\resizebox{524290}{}{$\triangle$}\,}\tilde{A})

\displaystyle\geq(1+\frac{C_{1/8}}{8})(\mu(A)-\epsilon_{1})-CD(D+2)\epsilon_{1}

\displaystyle\geq(1+\frac{C_{1/8}}{8})\mu(A)-\frac{C_{1/8}}{8}=(1+\frac{C_{1/8}}{16})\mu(A),

which concludes the proof.
∎

Finally, we provide a concrete graph and study the asymptotic expansion of its graph groupoid.

Example 6.28
.

Fix
k
be a positive integer. Consider the graph
G=(V,E)
where
V=\mathbb{N}
and
E=\{(n,n+i)~|~n\in\mathbb{N}\text{ and }i=1,2,\cdots,k\}
. Let
\mathcal{G}
be the associated graph groupoid with the length function from (
6.4
), and equip
P(G)
with the Borel probability measure from (
6.7
) by setting
b_{n}=\mu(Z(n))\coloneqq\frac{1}{2^{n+1}}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
. More precisely, for
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{m})\in F(G)
with
\mathrm{s}(\alpha_{1})=n
, set
\mu(Z(\alpha))=\frac{1}{2^{n+1}k^{m}}
.
We have the following:

(1)

If
k=1
, then
\mathcal{G}
is expanding in measure.

(2)

If
k\geq 2
, then
\mathcal{G}
is not asymptotically expanding in measure.

For (1):
Note that for any
\alpha\in F(G)
, the set
Z(\alpha)
consists of a single point, which is the infinite path starting at
s(\alpha)
. Hence
Z(\alpha)=Z(s(\alpha))
. Now for any measurable
A\subseteq P(G)
with
0<\mu(A)\leq\frac{1}{2}
, take
n_{A}\coloneqq\min\{s(\alpha)~|~\alpha\in A\}
.

If
n_{A}=0
, then
\mu(A)\leq\frac{1}{2}
implies that
A=Z(0)
. Hence
Z(1)\subseteq\mathrm{r}(B_{1}\cdot A)
, which implies that
\mu(\mathrm{r}(B_{1}\cdot A))\geq\frac{3}{2}\mu(A)
.
If
n_{A}\geq 1
, then by the choice of
\mu
we have
\mu(Z(n_{A}))\geq\frac{1}{2}\mu(A)
. Since
Z(n_{A}-1)\subseteq\mathrm{r}(B_{1}\cdot A)
, then

\mu(\mathrm{r}(B_{1}\cdot A))\geq\mu(A)+2\mu(Z(n_{A}))\geq 2\mu(A).

Hence
\mathcal{G}
is expanding in measure, and we conclude (1).

For (2):
Fix
k\geq 2
. For
n_{1}\leq n_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
, denote

F(G)_{n_{1},n_{2}}\coloneqq\{\alpha\in F(G)~|~s(\alpha)=n_{1},r(\alpha)=n_{2}\}\quad\text{and}\quad Z_{n_{1},n_{2}}\coloneqq\bigcup\{Z(\alpha)~|~\alpha\in F(G)_{n_{1},n_{2}}\}.

Direct calculations show that for
n\geq k
, we have

\mu(Z_{0,n})=\mu(Z(0))-\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\frac{k-j}{k}\mu(Z_{0,n-j}).

Rewrite the above as follows:

\mu(Z_{0,n})+\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\frac{k-j}{k}\mu(Z_{0,n-j})=\frac{1}{2},

and note that
1+\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\frac{k-j}{k}=\frac{k+1}{2}
. Hence for any
p\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists an integer
n_{p}\in\left\{pk+1,pk+2,\cdots,(p+1)k\right\}
such that
\mu(Z_{0,n_{p}})\geq\frac{1}{k+1}
.

Now for positive integer
p\in\mathbb{N}
, define

F(G)_{n_{p}}\coloneqq\bigsqcup_{m=0}^{n_{p}}F(G)_{m,n_{p}}\quad\text{and}\quad A_{p}=\bigcup\left\{Z(\alpha\alpha_{p})~|~\alpha\in F(G)_{n_{p}}\right\},

where
\alpha_{p}\coloneqq(n_{p},n_{p}+1)
. Then we have

\mu(A_{p})>\frac{1}{k}\mu(Z_{0,n_{p}})\geq\frac{1}{k(k+1)}\quad\text{and}\quad\mu(A_{p})\leq\frac{1}{k}\mu(P(G))=\frac{1}{k}.

By the construction of
F(G)_{n_{p}}
, it is clear that for any
\alpha\in F(G)_{n_{p}}
with
|\alpha|\geq 1
we have
\mathrm{r}(B_{1}\cdot Z(\alpha\alpha_{p}))\subseteq A_{p}
. Hence

\mu(\mathrm{r}(B_{1}\cdot A_{p}))=\mu(A_{p}\sqcup Z(n_{p}+1))=\mu(A_{p})+\frac{1}{2^{n_{p}+2}},

which shows that
\mu\big(\mathrm{r}((B_{1}\cdot A_{p})\setminus A_{p})\big)=\frac{1}{2^{n_{p}+2}}\to 0
as
p\to\infty
. This concludes (2).

On graphs with girth at least five achieving Steffen’s edge coloring bound
Guantao Chen 1, Alireza Fiujlaali 1, Anna Johnsen-Yu 1,2, Jessica McDonald 3

Abstract
. Vizing and Gupta showed that the chromatic index χ ′ ​ ( G ) \chi^{\prime}(G) of a graph G G is bounded above by Δ ⁡ ( G ) + μ ⁡ ( G ) \Delta(G)+\mu(G) , where Δ ⁡ ( G ) \Delta(G) and μ ⁡ ( G ) \mu(G) denote the maximum degree and the maximum multiplicity of G G , respectively. Steffen refined this bound, proving that χ ′ ​ ( G ) ≤ Δ ⁡ ( G ) + ⌈ μ ⁡ ( G ) / ⌊ g ⁡ ( G ) / 2 ⌋ ⌉ \chi^{\prime}(G)\leq\Delta(G)+\left\lceil\mu(G)/\left\lfloor g(G)/2\right\rfloor\right\rceil , where g ⁡ ( G ) g(G) is the girth of the graph G G . A ring graph is a graph obtained from a cycle by duplicating some edges. The equality in Steffen’s bound is achieved by ring graphs of the form μ ​ C g \mu C_{g} , obtained from an odd cycle C g C_{g} by duplicating each edge μ \mu times. We answer two questions posed by Stiebitz et al. regarding the characterization of graphs which achieve Steffen’s bound. In particular, we show that if G G is a critical graph which achieves Steffen’s bound with g ⁡ ( G ) ≥ 5 g(G)\geq 5 and χ ′ ​ ( G ) ≥ Δ + 2 \chi^{\prime}(G)\geq\Delta+2 , then G G must be a ring graph of odd girth.

1.
Introduction

We generally follow the notation and definitions of West
[
10
]
. Graphs under consideration may have multiple edges, but do not have loops.
A
k
-edge-coloring
of a graph
G
is a labeling
f:E(G)\to[k]:=\{1,\dots,k\}
, such that the edges incident to any single vertex are labeled with distinct colors. The
chromatic index
of a graph
G
, denoted by
\chi^{\prime}(G)
, is the least
k
such that
G
has a
k
-edge-coloring.

Holyer
[
4
]
proved that
determining the chromatic index of an arbitrary graph is NP-complete, even when restricted to cubic simple graphs.
Since all edges incident with a single vertex must be labeled with a different color,
\chi^{\prime}(G)\geq\Delta(G)
, where
\Delta(G)
denotes the maximum degree of
G
. In 1949,
Shannon
[
6
]
showed that
\chi^{\prime}(G)\leq(3/2)\Delta(G)
. In the 1960s, Vizing
[
9
]
and Gupta
[
3
]
independently established another upper bound involving the
multiplicity
of a graph
G
, denoted by
\mu(G)
; that is, the maximum number of parallel edges sharing two common vertices. In particular, they proved that
\chi^{\prime}(G)\leq\Delta(G)+\mu(G)
. This is commonly referred to as “Vizing’s theorem” since Gupta’s proof was discovered after Vizing’s result had become widely known. This result is particularly appealing when applied to simple graphs since it reduces the possible values of
\chi^{\prime}(G)
to two options:
\Delta(G)
and
\Delta(G)+1
.

The
girth
of
G
, denoted by
g(G)
, is the smallest integer
g\geq 3
such that
G
has a cycle length
g
if such a cycle exists and
g(G)=\infty
otherwise. By taking into account the girth of a graph, Steffen refined Vizing’s theorem as follows.

Theorem 1.1

(Steffen
[
7
]
)
.

For any graph
G
,

\chi^{\prime}(G)\leq\Delta(G)+\left\lceil\dfrac{\mu(G)}{\left\lfloor g(G)/2\right\rfloor}\right\rceil
.

Let
st(\Delta,\mu,g)=\max\{\chi^{\prime}(G):\mbox{ $G$ is a graph with }\Delta(G)=\Delta,\ \mu(G)=\mu,\ g(G)=g\}
.
Steffen’s theorem shows that
st(\Delta,\mu,g)\leq\Delta+\left\lceil\mu/\left\lfloor g/2\right\rfloor\right\rceil
.
Stiebitz, Scheide, Toft, and Favrholdt
[
8
, ch. 9, pp. 249-250]
raised the following two questions. Here, a
ring graph
is obtained from a cycle by duplicating some edges.

Question 1.1
.

For which triples
(\Delta,\mu,g)
is
st(\Delta,\mu,g)=\Delta+\left\lceil\mu/\left\lfloor g/2\right\rfloor\right\rceil
?

Question 1.2
.

Let
g
be an odd integer and let
\mu
be an integer with
\mu\geq g\geq 3
. Is there a graph
G
with
g(G)=g
and
\mu(G)=\mu
achieving Steffen’s bound, but not containing a ring graph of order
g
with the same chromatic index as
G
?

Stiebitz, Scheide, Toft, and Favrholdt
[
8
]
observed that for every odd integer
g\geq 3
, the multigraph

G=\mu C_{g}
,
obtained from the odd cycle
C_{g}
by duplicating each edge
\mu
times, satisfies

\chi^{\prime}(G)=\Delta(G)+\Bigl\lceil\frac{\mu}{\lfloor g/2\rfloor}\Bigr\rceil.

Hence, for any positive integer
\mu
and odd
g\geq 3
, the triple
(2\mu,\mu,g)
is a solution to Question
1.1
.
When
g\geq 4
is even, the disjoint union of
\mu C_{g}
and
\mu C_{g+1}
also attains this bound; in that case the triple
(2\mu,\mu,g)
again satisfies Question
1.1
.
Thus, one obtains

\mathrm{st}(2\mu,\mu,g)=2\mu+\Bigl\lceil\frac{\mu}{\lfloor g/2\rfloor}\Bigr\rceil

for all integers
\mu\geq 1
and
g\geq 3
.

Our main result is Theorem
1.2
below, which provides a negative answer to Question
1.2
for
g\geq 5
and
\mu(G)\geq\lfloor g/2\rfloor+1
, which is slightly more broad than the original question. Note that for a graph
G
achieving Steffen’s bound, this restriction on
\mu(G)
is equivalent to requiring
\chi^{\prime}(G)\geq\Delta(G)+2
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
g\geq 5
be an integer and let
G
be a graph with girth
g(G)\geq g
and
\mu(G)\geq\lfloor g/2\rfloor+1
. If
\chi^{\prime}(G)=\Delta(G)+\lceil\mu(G)/\lfloor g/2\rfloor\rceil
, then
G
contains a ring graph with the same chromatic index as
G
.

Our proof technique for Theorem
1.2
unfortunately does not apply to graphs
G
with
g(G)=4
, so some new ideas would be needed to attack this case. When
g(G)=3
however, there is a positive answer to Question
1.2
. To see this, let
\mu K_{n}
be obtained from the complete graph on
n

\mu
times. If
n\geq 3
is odd, then
\chi^{\prime}(\mu K_{n})=\mu n=\Delta(\mu K_{n})+\mu(\mu K_{n})
, and
((n-1)\mu,\mu,3)
is a triple satisfying Question

. Since
\mu K_{n}
does not contain a ring graph with the same chromatic index when

A graph is (edge-coloring)

if
\chi^{\prime}(H)<\chi^{\prime}(G)
for any proper subgraph

. Theorem

implies that if

2.
Preliminaries

The
density
of a graph
G
is defined as follows:

\Gamma(G)=\max\left\{\left\lceil\frac{2|E(H)|}{|H|-1}\right\rceil\ :\ H\subseteq G,|H|\text{ odd}\right\}.

Since each matching in a subgraph
H
with an odd number of vertices has at most
(|H|-1)/2
edges, it follows that
\chi^{\prime}(G)\geq\Gamma(G)
for all graphs
G
. Hence,
\Gamma(G)
provides another lower bound for
\chi^{\prime}(G)
besides the maximum degree
\Delta(G)
.
The well-known Goldberg-Seymour Conjecture states that if
\chi^{\prime}(G)\geq\Delta(G)+2
then
\chi^{\prime}(G)=\Gamma(G)
, which was confirmed by Chen, Hao, Jing, Yu and Zang in three different versions of papers
[
2
,
1
,
5
]
. We refer to
[
8
]
for literature on this conjecture.

Theorem 2.1

(Chen, Hao, Jing, Yu, Zang)
.

For any graph
G
, if
\chi^{\prime}(G)\geq\Delta(G)+2
then
\chi^{\prime}(G)=\Gamma(G)
.

Theorem
2.1
implies the following two results, each of which provides a lower bound on minimum degree.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
G
be a critical graph on
n
vertices. If
\chi^{\prime}(G)\geq\Delta+2
,
then
n
is odd and for every vertex
v\in V(G)
,

d_{G}(v)=\sum_{w\neq v}(\chi^{\prime}-1-d_{G}(w))+2\geq(n-1)(\chi^{\prime}-1-\Delta)+2.

Moreover, if
n\geq g
and
\chi^{\prime}(G)=\Delta+\lceil\mu/\lfloor g/2\rfloor\rceil\geq\Delta+2
for some positive integer
g\geq 5
, then

\delta(G)\geq n\mu/g+1
, and equality holds only if
\mu=g
.

Proof.

By definition,
\Gamma(G)\leq\chi^{\prime}(G)
. Applying Theorem
2.1
, we find that
\Gamma(G)=\chi^{\prime}(G)
as
\chi^{\prime}(G)\geq\Delta+2
. Since
G
is critical, for every proper subgraph
H
of
G
with an odd number of vertices, we have that
\left\lceil 2|E(H)|/(|H|-1)\right\rceil\leq\Gamma(H)\leq\chi^{\prime}(H)<\chi^{\prime}(G)=\Gamma(G)
. Hence,
G
itself is the only subgraph
H
of
G
with an odd number of vertices such that
\left\lceil 2|E(H)|/(|H|-1)\right\rceil=\Gamma(G)
. Consequently,
n
is odd, and for any edge
e\in E(G)
,

\chi^{\prime}(G-e)=\chi^{\prime}(G)-1
. Hence,

\left\lceil\frac{|E(G-e)|}{(n-1)/2}\right\rceil\leq\chi^{\prime}(G-e)=\chi^{\prime}(G)-1=\left\lceil\frac{|E(G)|}{(n-1)/2}\right\rceil-1.

Since
\frac{|E(G-e)|}{\left\lfloor(n-1)/2\right\rfloor}=\frac{|E(G)|-1}{(n-1)/2}\geq\left\lceil\frac{|E(G)|}{(n-1)/2}\right\rceil-1
, it follows that
\frac{|E(G-e)|}{\left\lfloor(n-1)/2\right\rfloor}
is an integer and is equal to
\chi^{\prime}(G-e)
. Hence, for an edge-coloring of
G-e
with
\chi^{\prime}(G-e)
colors, each color class contains exactly
(n-1)/2
edges. Therefore,
E(G-e)
can be partitioned into
\chi^{\prime}(G)-1
near-perfect matchings, each of which misses exactly one vertex of
G
.

Let
v
be the vertex specified in Lemma
2.2
, and let
e
be an edge not incident with
v
. For each vertex
w\neq v
, if
w
is not incident to
e
, there are exactly
\chi^{\prime}(G)-1-d_{G}(w)
of these near-perfect matchings not containing the vertex
w
; if
w
is incident to
e
, there are exactly
\chi^{\prime}(G)-1-(d_{G}(w)-1)
of these matchings not containing the vertex
w
. Note that every one of these matchings contains
v
. Hence,
d_{G}(v)=\sum_{w\neq v}(\chi^{\prime}(G)-1-d_{G}(w))+2
. As
d_{G}(w)\leq\Delta(G)
, we have the following.

\delta(G)\geq(n-1)(\chi^{\prime}(G)-1-\Delta(G))+2.

Now suppose
\chi^{\prime}(G)=\Delta+\left\lceil\mu/\left\lfloor g/2\right\rfloor\right\rceil\geq\Delta+2
for some positive integer
g\geq 5
. Then by substituting
\Delta+\left\lceil\mu/\left\lfloor g/2\right\rfloor\right\rceil
for
\chi^{\prime}(G)
, we obtain the following inequality.

\delta(G)\geq(n-1)\left(\left\lceil\frac{\mu}{\left\lfloor g/2\right\rfloor}\right\rceil-1\right)+2\geq(n-1)\left(\left\lceil{\frac{2\mu}{g}}\right\rceil-1\right)+2.

Note that

(n-1)\left(\left\lceil\frac{\mu}{\left\lfloor g/2\right\rfloor}\right\rceil-1\right)+2-\left(\frac{n\mu}{g}+1\right)\geq(n-1)\left(\left\lceil\frac{2\mu}{g}\right\rceil-\frac{\mu}{g}-1\right)-\frac{\mu}{g}+1.

If
\mu<g
, then
\frac{\mu+1}{g}\leq 1
, and hence

\left\lceil\frac{2\mu}{g}\right\rceil\geq 2\geq 1+\frac{\mu+1}{g}
. Thus,

(n-1)\left(\left\lceil\frac{2\mu}{g}\right\rceil-\frac{\mu}{g}-1\right)-\frac{\mu}{g}+1\geq\frac{1}{g}((n-1)-\mu)+1\geq\frac{1}{g}((n-1)-g)+1>0.

If
\mu=g
, then

(n-1)\left(\left\lceil\frac{2\mu}{g}\right\rceil-\frac{\mu}{g}-1\right)-\frac{\mu}{g}+1=0
, as desired.

We now turn to the case
\mu>g
. In this case,
\left\lceil 2\mu/g\right\rceil\geq 3
. If
\mu/g\leq 1.5
, then
4\mu/(3g)\leq 2
, and hence
\left\lceil 2\mu/g\right\rceil=3\geq 1+4\mu/(3g)
. If
\mu/g>1.5
, then
(2/3)(\mu/g)\geq 1
, which implies that
\mu/g>1+\mu/(3g)
, and so
\left\lceil 2\mu/g\right\rceil\geq\mu/g+\mu/g\geq 1+\mu/3g+\mu/g=1+4\mu/(3g)
.
Hence,

(n-1)\left(\left\lceil\frac{2\mu}{g}\right\rceil-\frac{\mu}{g}-1\right)-\frac{\mu}{g}+1\geq\frac{(n-1)\mu}{3g}-\frac{\mu}{g}+1\geq\frac{4\mu}{g}-\frac{\mu}{g}+1>0,

as
n\geq 5
and
\mu/g\geq 1
.

Following the same argument as in the case

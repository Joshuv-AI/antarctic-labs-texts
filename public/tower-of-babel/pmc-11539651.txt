Efficacy of laparoscopic radical resection of right-sided colon cancer by different surgical approaches: network-meta-analysis
Authors: Mingyi Li, Jie Hu, Liwei Suo, Hui Wu, Ziming Li, Xiaoyi Xu, Shifeng Zhang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionThere are a growing number of surgical approaches for laparoscopic radical resection of right-sided colon cancer, while there are relatively few comparative analyses of the different surgical approaches.ObjectiveTo evaluate the safety and efficacy of different surgical approaches (intermediate approach, caudolateral approach, caudolateral combined with intermediate approach) for laparoscopic radical resection of right-sided colon cancer by conducting a network meta-analysis (NMA).MethodWe searched PubMed, Web of science and China National Knowledge Infrastructure (CNKI) databases. We reviewed the Chinese and English literature on controlled studies of laparoscopic radical resection of right-sided colon cancer including intermediate approach, caudolateral approach and caudolateral combined with intermediate approach, reported from the establishment of the database to September 2023. The inclusion and exclusion criteria were independently conducted by two researchers and relevant data was extracted from the qualifying literature and analyzed using Stata15 software.ResultsNine controlled studies of relevance including 715 patients were screened with right-sided colon cancer. Net meta-analysis showed that compared with the intermediate approach, the caudolateral approach was superior to the intermediate approach in terms of operation time (SMD = 0.75, 95% CI = 0.38 ~ 1.12, P = 0.0001), and bleeding volume (SMD = 1.15, 95% CI = 0.18 ~ 2.13, P = 0.002), while the difference was not statistically significant among the caudolateral approach compared with the intermediate approach in terms of the number of lymph node dissection, postoperative complication rate, time to first postoperative flatus and postoperative hospitalization. Compared with the intermediate approach, the combined approach was superior to the intermediate approach in terms of operation time (SMD = -0.87, 95% CI = -1.22 ~ -1.52, P < 0.05), bleeding volume (SMD = -1.09, 95% CI = -1.98 ~ -0.19, P < 0.05), the number of lymph node dissection (SMD = 0.21, 95% CI = 0.01 ~ 0.41, P < 0.05), and postoperative complication rate (RR = 2.24, 95% CI = 1.21 ~ 4.13, P < 0.05), while the difference was no statistically significant among the combined approach compared with the intermediate approach in terms of time to first postoperative flatus and postoperative hospitalization. Compared with the caudolateral approach, there was no statistically significant difference between the caudolateral approach and the combined approach in terms of operation time, bleeding volume, the number of lymph node dissection, postoperative complication rate, time to first postoperative flatus and postoperative hospitalization. According to the results of the surface under the cumulative ranking curve (SUCRA), the caudolateral approach and the combined approach were superior to the intermediate approach in terms of operation time (SUCRA: 65.1%, 84.9%, 0), bleeding volume (SUCRA: 77%, 71.9%, 1%), the number of lymph node dissection (SUCRA: 49.6%, 90.8%, 9.7%), postoperative complication rate (SUCRA: 46.6%, 97.5%, 5.9%), time to first postoperative flatus (SUCRA: 67%, 77.8%, 5.2%), postoperative hospitalization (SUCRA: 30.8%, 96.4%, 22.8%).ConclusionThe caudolateral combined with intermediate approach and the caudolateral approach are safer and more effective than the intermediate approach. According to the results of SUCRA, the combined approach is superior to the caudolateral approach in terms of operation time, the number of lymph node dissection, postoperative complication rate, time to first postoperative flatus and postoperative hospitalization, the caudolateral approach is superior to the combined approach in term of bleeding volume.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12893-024-02603-z.Keywords: Laparoscopy, Right-sided colon cancer, Intermediate approach, Caudolateral approach, Caudolateral combined with intermediate approach, Network meta-analysis

Introduction

Colon cancer is the fourth most common cancer in the United States behind breast, lung, and prostate cancers, ranking second in cause of death after lung cancer [1].In spite of the fact that the incidence rate of left-sided colon cancer is higher than right-sided colon cancer, right-sided colon cancer is more proximal, more insidious, and with late-onset symptoms relative to left-sided colon cancer. Consequently, the diagnosis of right-sided colon cancer is usually made late, worsening prognosis of right-sided colon cancer compared with left-sided colon cancer [2]. With the popularization of laparoscopy, the main surgical treatment for this disease is laparoscopic radical resection. Due to its advantages of minimizing trauma, reduced intraoperative blood loss and faster postoperative recovery [3–9], it has gradually replaced the traditional open surgery [10, 11]. Nevertheless, with the development of the technology, there have emerged an increasing amount of surgical approaches for laparoscopic radical resection of right-sided colon cancer [12–14], ranging from the initial intermediate approach to the caudolateral approach, and the subsequent combined approach. However, the traditional intermediate approach remains more commonly used because it is compatible with the principle of tumor resection [15, 16]. While the caudolateral approach provides easier access to the anatomical level, allowing beginners to adapt to and understand the correct anatomical level [17]. The combined approach incorporates the advantages of the caudolateral and intermediate approaches. There are significant differences in the clinical safety and effectiveness of the different surgical approaches. However, there are relatively few comparative analyses of the different surgical approaches. Therefore, in this review, a meta-analysis was conducted to analyze the safety and efficacy of different surgical approaches (caudolateral approach, intermediate approach as well as caudolateral combined with intermediate approach) for laparoscopic radical resection of right-sided hemicolonic cancer in order to provide a basis for clinical decision making. The caudolateral approach, intermediate approach and caudolateral combined with intermediate approach are respectively referred to in this review and meta-analysis as the caudolateral group, intermediate group and combined group.

Materials and methods

Search strategy

This study has been registered with PROSPERO (registration number: CRD42024571180), the literature was screened according to PRISMA process. Controlled studies of different surgical approaches (intermediate approach, caudolateral approach, caudolateral combined with intermediate approach) for radical resection of right-sided colon cancer were retrieved from PubMed and Web of science and CNKI databases from the establishment of each database to September 2023. Simultaneously retrieved references cited in the papers were added as a supplement to the retrieved literature. Terms used to search the literatures were: right colon cancer, laparoscopic, radical resection, approach, caudal approach, medial to lateral, caudal combined middle approach, treatment effect, safety, efficacy. For example, in PubMed, For a fuller and more accurate search, the following MeSH terms and keywords were used: {right colon cancer (all fields)} or {laparoscopic (all fields)} or {radical resection (all fields)} or {caudolateral approach (all fields))} or {medial approach (all fields) or {middle approach (all fields) or {intermediate approach (all fields) or {medial to lateral (all fields))} or {caudolateral combined intermediate approach (all fields)}. The detailed screening process of the literature is shown in Fig. 1.

PRISMA flow diagram

Inclusion and exclusion criteria of the literature

Inclusion criteria

Retrieved articles that meet the following PICOS principles are included: (1) P: patients who are planned for laparoscopic radical resection of right-sided colon cancer, regardless of benign or malignant; (2) I: interventions: laparoscopic radical resection of right-sided colon cancer by conducting combined approach; (3) C: caudolateral approach and intermediate approach; (4) O: operation time, bleeding volume, the number of lymph node dissection, postoperative complication rate, time to first postoperative flatus and postoperative hospitalization. (5) S: the study type is randomized controlled trial (RCT), retrospective study, or prospective non-randomized controlled study.

Exclusion criteria

(1) Not a qualifying controlled study, such as conference articles, case studies, basic studies and review studies; (2) Conference abstracts and the full text cannot be obtained; (3) Articles with duplicate publications.

Literature quality assessment

This study included five randomized controlled trials and four retrospective studies, the Cochrane revman5.4 risk-of-bias tool was used to evaluate randomized controlled trials, and each study was assessed as “low risk of bias”, “unclear risk of bias” or “high risk of bias” in each domain [18]. The Newcastle–Ottawa Scale (NOS) was used to evaluate retrospective studies (Fig. 2).

Study quality assessment

Data extraction

The data were extracted from the literature by two researchers, independently. The data were subject to quality assurance by a blinded reviewer throughout the process. If there were disputes in the process, a third researcher was required to discuss and reach a consensus, otherwise the disputed literature was invalidated for inclusion. The data extracted from the literature included the following aspects: author information of the literature, type of study, operation mode, operation time, the volume of bleeding, the number of lymph node dissections, postoperative complication rate, time to first postoperative flatus, and postoperative hospitalization.

Statistical processing

Data were analyzed by network meta-analysis using STATA 15 software. Network meta-analysis was based on summarizing all possible pairwise comparisons, including direct and indirect comparisons. When a closed loop appeared in the network plot, to evaluate global and loop inconsistency between direct and indirect comparisons, if the difference is not statistically significant, it indicates that the results of direct comparison and indirect comparison are consistent [19]. We used relative risk (RR) to represent the binomial variables, while standardized mean difference (SMD) was used to represent the continuous variables. The 95% CI of the two were calculated respectively. According to the results of SUCRA, the clinical efficacy of each surgical approach was ranked by the order probability plot, in which the higher the probability was, the better the clinical efficacy was [20].

Results

Basic characteristics of the included literature

One hundred fifty-five articles were preliminarily identified through the database search. After removing the duplicates and eliminating the incompatible literature according to the inclusion/exclusion criteria, 42 full-text articles were screened in detail for eligibility. It was eventually determined that 12 studies contained sufficient data for qualitative review. Of these, only 9 were included for quantitative meta-analysis. The surgical approaches of the 9 articles that were included were limited to only the three of interests in this review. The screening process of the included studies is summarized in Fig. 1. A total of 715 patients were included in the 9 eligible studies, all the included 9 studies involve radical resection of right-sided colon cancer, in which 217 underwent surgical resection via caudolateral approach, 296 with intermediate approach and 202 with a combined approach. Only one study had a three-armed design while eight had a two-armed design. Four of the two-armed studies were retrospective studies while the remaining studies were randomized controlled trials. The NOS score of each retrospective study was no less than 7. The basic characteristics of the included studies is shown in Tables 1 and 2.

The results of network meta-analysis

Operative time

There were 9 studies (21, 22, 23, 24, 25, 26, 27, 28, 29) with a total of 715 patients. Of these, the results of the global inconsistency test showed that there was no significant heterogeneity (P = 0.34 > 0.05). Loop inconsistency testing showed the results in the three closed loops (caudolateral group vs intermediate group, P = 0.089; caudolateral group vs combined group, P = 0.033; intermediate group vs combined group, P = 0.089). As shown by the net meta-analysis, the intermediate group had a longer operation time compared with the caudolateral group, which was statistically significant (SMD = 0.75, 95% CI = 0.38 ~ 1.12, P = 0.0001); whereas the combined group had a shorter operation time compared with the intermediate group, and the difference was statistically significant (SMD = -0.87, 95% CI = -1.22 ~ -1.52, P < 0.05); nonetheless, the difference between the combined group and the caudolateral group was no statistically significant (SMD = -0.11, 95% CI = -0.54 ~ 0.31, P = 0.599), as shown in Fig. 4. According to the results of SUCRA, the probability graphs of the operative time for the different surgical access methods indicated that the caudolateral group had a rate of 65.1%, the intermediate group had a rate of 0.0%, and the combined group had a rate of 84.9%, as shown in Fig. 5.

Bleeding volume

There were 9 studies (21, 22, 23, 24, 25, 26, 27, 28, 29) with a total of 715 patients, the results of the global inconsistency test showed that there was no significant heterogeneity (P = 0.55). Loop inconsistency testing showed there was no significant difference in the three closed loops (caudolateral group vs intermediate group, P = 0.21; caudolateral group vs combined group, P = 0.11; intermediate group vs combined group, P = 0.11). The net meta-analysis revealed that the intermediate group had more intraoperative bleeding volume compared with the caudolateral group, which was statistically significant (SMD = 1.15, 95% CI = 0.18 ~ 2.13, P = 0.002); while the combined group had less intraoperative blood loss compared with the intermediate group, in which the difference was statistically significant (SMD = -1.09, 95% CI = -1.98 ~ -0.19, P < 0.05). The difference between the combined group and the caudolateral group was no statistically significant compared with the intermediate group, (SMD = 0.07, 95% CI = -1.05 ~ 1.18, P = 0.908), as shown in Fig. 4. According to the results of SUCRA, the probability graphs plotted for the sorting of intraoperative bleeding for different surgical access methods, the caudolateral group was 77%, the intermediate group was 1%, and the combined group was71.9%, as shown in Fig. 5.

The number of lymph node dissection

There were 9 studies (21, 22, 23, 24, 25, 26, 27, 28, 29) with a total of 715 patients, the results of the global inconsistency test showed that there was no significant heterogeneity (P = 0.99). Loop inconsistency testing showed there was no significant difference in the three closed loops (caudolateral group vs intermediate group, P = 0.97; caudolateral group vs combined group, P = 0.99; intermediate group vs combined group, P = 0.89). The net meta-analysis showed the difference was no statistically significant among the caudolateral approach compared with the intermediate approach (P > 0.05) and combined approach (P > 0.05). the combined approach was superior to the intermediate approach (SMD = 0.21, 95% CI = 0.01 ~ 0.41, P < 0.05). as shown in Fig. 4. According to the results of SUCRA, the probability of the number of lymph node dissection for the different surgical approaches, it was shown that the caudolateral group was 49.6%, intermediate group was 9.7% and combined group was 90.8%, as shown in Fig. 5.

Postoperative complication rate

There were 9 studies (21, 22, 23, 24, 25, 26, 27, 28, 29) with a total of 715 patients, and the results of the global inconsistency test showed that there was no significant heterogeneity (P = 0.44). Loop inconsistency testing showed there was no significant difference in the three closed loops (caudolateral group vs intermediate group, P = 0.77; caudolateral group vs combined group, P = 0.75; intermediate group vs combined group, P = 0.86). The net meta-analysis showed that the postoperative complication rate was higher in the intermediate group compared with the combined group, and the difference was statistically significant (RR = 2.24, 95% CI = 1.21 ~ 4.13, P < 0.05). The difference was no statistically significant among the caudolateral group compared with the intermediate group (P > 0.05) and the combined group (P > 0.05), as shown in Fig. 4. According to the results of SUCRA, the postoperative complication rate as illustrated by the probability graph of the different surgical access methods showed that the caudolateral group was 46.6%, the intermediate group was 5.9%, and the combined group was 97.5%, as shown in Fig. 5.

Time to first postoperative flatus

There were 8 studies (21, 22, 23, 24, 25, 26, 27, 29) included with a total of 625 patients, which showed that the results of the global inconsistency test showed that there was no significant heterogeneity (P = 0.85). Loop inconsistency testing showed there was no significant difference in the three closed loops (caudolateral group vs intermediate group, P = 0.49; caudolateral group vs combined group, P = 0.33; intermediate group vs combined group, P = 0.58). The net meta-analysis showed there was no statistically significant difference between the three surgical approaches (all P > 0.05), as shown in Fig. 4. According to the results of SUCRA, the probability graph of the sorting of the time to first postoperative flatus for the different surgical approaches, 67% was assigned to the caudolateral group, 5.2% in the intermediate group, and 77.8% in the combined group, as shown in Fig. 5.

Postoperative hospitalization time

There were 6 studies (21, 22, 24, 25, 26, 27) with a total of 495 patients, the results of the global inconsistency test showed that there was a significant heterogeneity (P = 0.0019). Loop inconsistency testing showed there was no significant difference in the three closed loops (caudolateral group vs intermediate group, P = 0.23; caudolateral group vs combined group, P = 0.06; intermediate group vs combined group, P = 0.71). The net meta-analysis showed there was no statistically significant difference between the three surgical approaches (all P > 0.05), as shown in Fig. 4. According to the results of SUCRA, the probability of sorting of postoperative hospitalization time according to different surgical access methods was 30.8% in the caudolateral group, 22.8% in the intermediate group, and 96.4% in the combined group, as shown in Fig. 5.

Discussion

Colon cancer, as one of the most dominant tumors in the world, has been known as the four major cancer-related killers along with lung cancer, prostate cancer and breast cancer [30]. Currently, the laparoscopy has gradually become the main treatment modality for right-sided colon cancer resection due to the advantages of less trauma, reduced operative bleeding, and quicker postoperative recovery [31]. Though there are a growing number of surgical approaches for laparoscopic radical resection of right-sided colon cancer, there are relatively few comparative analyses of the different surgical approaches.

In this study, we evaluated the safety and efficacy of different surgical approaches (intermediate approach, caudolateral approach, caudolateral combined with intermediate approach) for laparoscopic radical resection of right-sided colon cancer by conducting a network meta-analysisa. Net meta-analysis showed that compared with the intermediate approach, the caudolateral approach was superior to the intermediate approach in terms of operation time (SMD = 0.75, 95% CI = 0.38 ~ 1.12, P = 0.0001), and bleeding volume (SMD = 1.15, 95% CI = 0.18 ~ 2.13, P = 0.002), while the difference was not statistically significant among the caudolateral approach compared with the intermediate approach in terms of the number of lymph node dissection, postoperative complication rate, time to first postoperative flatus and postoperative hospitalization. Compared with the intermediate approach, the combined approach was superior to the intermediate approach in terms of operation time (SMD = -0.87, 95% CI = -1.22 ~ -1.52, P < 0.05), bleeding volume (SMD = -1.09, 95% CI = -1.98 ~ -0.19, P < 0.05), the number of lymph node dissection (SMD = 0.21, 95% CI = 0.01 ~ 0.41, P < 0.05), and postoperative complication rate (RR = 2.24, 95% CI = 1.21 ~ 4.13, P < 0.05), while the difference was no statistically significant among the combined approach compared with the intermediate approach in terms of the time to first postoperative flatus and postoperative hospitalization. Compared with the caudolateral approach, there was no statistically significant difference between the caudolateral approach and the combined approach in terms of operation time, bleeding volume, the number of lymph node dissection, postoperative complication rate, time to first postoperative flatus and postoperative hospitalization. According to the results of SUCRA, the caudolateral approach and the combined approach were superior to the intermediate approach in terms of operation time (SUCRA: 65.1%, 84.9%, 0), bleeding volume (SUCRA: 77%, 71.9%, 1%), the number of lymph node dissection (SUCRA: 49.6%, 90.8%, 9.7%), postoperative complication rate (SUCRA: 46.6%, 97.5%, 5.9%), time to first postoperative flatus (SUCRA: 67%, 77.8%, 5.2%), postoperative hospitalization (SUCRA: 30.8%, 96.4%, 22.8%). From the data of SUCRA, there was an absolute predominance of the clinical efficacy ranking probability of the combined surgical approach, followed by the caudolateral approach. Because the larger the probability, the better the clinical efficacy, the combined surgical approach has the best clinical efficacy for these indicators evaluated in this study, followed by the caudolateral approach. The intermediate approach has the worst clinical efficacy among the three surgical approaches. We also did a direct meta-analysis of the indicators in the study, the results of direct meta-analysis showed that for operation time and bleeding volume, the caudolateral approach and combined approach were superior to the intermediate approach. For the postoperative complication rate,

the combined approach was superior to the intermediate approach. The time to first postoperative flatus was shorter in the combined approach than in the intermediate approach. For the number of lymph node dissections and the postoperative hospitalization, while the difference was no statistically significant among the intermediate approach compared with the combined approach and the caudolateral approach.

For the results, the reason could be: The drawbacks of intermediate approach include, among others, that because of its complex anatomy, it is easy to follow the wrong anatomical layers [15, 16]. While the caudolateral approach provides easier access to the anatomical level, allowing beginners to adapt to and understand the correct anatomical level, it is prone to partial bleeding of the gastrocolic trunk during the dissection operation [17]. The combined approach incorporates the advantages of the caudolateral and intermediate approaches. Firstly, it allows access to the anatomical level and then reveals the head of the pancreas, subsequently converting to the intermediate approach, which further allows handling of the vessels more smoothly. Thus, it enables both rapid access to the anatomic level and excellent management of the vessels.

Xiaojiang Yi et al. [32]. reported that the operative time of caudolateral approach was significantly shorter than that of intermediate approach, and the difference was statistically significant (P < 0.05), while the differences in intraoperative blood loss and the number of lymph node dissection were not statistically significant (P > 0.05), which were partly in agreement with the results of the net meta-analysis of our study. Mohamed Maatouk et al. [33].'s meta-analysis of the caudal approach to laparoscopic right-sided colon cancer resection indicated that postoperative complications and oncological prognosis were acceptable in the caudal approach, but there were no differences in operative time, postoperative hospitalization time and the number of lymph node dissection with the intermediate approach, which results were also partially in agreement with those of the present study. Bo Feng et al. [34]. concluded that, compared with the caudolateral combined with intermediate approach, the fully intermediate approach had a shorter operative time. However, there were no differences in the number of lymph node dissections between the two, which may be due to the differences in the judgment and definition of surgical approaches in China and abroad.

In this study, we did not control for the uniform selection of tumor stage for the included articles, in addition, there were differences between the units reported among different articles included, which may have resulted in a bias. Results of the operative time, the intraoperative bleeding volume, the number of lymph node dissection, the time to first postoperative flatus, postoperative hospitalization and postoperative complications analyzed in the articles may have an impact due to the experience and proficiency of the different operators. Meanwhile, the differences between age, gender and physical condition among patients may also have an impact; Moreover, the surgical approach of laparoscopic right-sided colon cancer resection has fewer relevant reports abroad than in China, so there is a positive significance to conduct the promotion of the technique. Additionally, only nine articles were included in the present study, which is a relatively small number of papers.

In conclusion, there were no significant differences in operative time, intraoperative bleeding volume, number of lymph node dissection, postoperative recovery of gastrointestinal function and occurrences of complications in the caudolateral combined with intermediate approach for laparoscopic right colon cancer compared with the caudal approach. However, according to the results of SUCRA, the ranked probability plot of clinical efficacy in the caudolateral combined with intermediate approach was better than that of the caudal approach. Compared with the intermediate approach, the combined approach and the caudal approach can effectively shorten the operation time and reduce the intraoperative bleeding volume. At the same time, compared with the intermediate approach, the combined approach can also accelerate the recovery of the gastrointestinal function of the patients after the operation and reduce the occurrence of complications. The caudal combined intermediate approach is a safe and effective surgical approach. Notably, the multi-center research representativeness of the selected articles needs to be strengthened. Moreover, the heterogeneity of this study and the small number of included studies and sample sizes beckons more high-quality, large-sample and multicenter randomized controlled trails to evaluate the safety and efficacy of the various surgical approaches for laparoscopic radical surgery of right-sided colon cancer.

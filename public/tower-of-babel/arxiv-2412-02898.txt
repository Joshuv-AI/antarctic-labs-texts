The Determinant of Cohomology and Moduli of λ \lambda -Connections
Johan Martens

Abstract
. We exhibit how the Hodge-Deligne moduli space of λ \lambda -connections over a smooth projective curve, for stable bundles with fixed determinant, can be understood as the dual of the Atiyah algebroid of the determinant of cohomology line bundle.



1.
Introduction

When studying various moduli spaces of bundles with attributes on a smooth projective curve, one finds that many of these moduli spaces are (at least on a suitable stable locus) vector bundles over a more basic moduli space, such as the moduli space of stable bundles. The most prominent case is that of (ordinary) Higgs bundles
[
Hit87a
,
Hit87b
]
, whose moduli space, restricted to those Higgs bundles with a stable underlying vector bundle, is the total space of the cotangent bundle of the moduli space of stable bundles, and in particular is complex symplectic.

A variation of this can arise for moduli spaces that are complex Poisson, such as moduli spaces of weakly parabolic
[
LM10
]
or parahoric
[
KSZ24a
]
Higgs bundles, or Higgs bundles over stacky curves
[
KSZ24b
]
. In these cases, the moduli spaces (for underlying stable bundles) can be seen to be the dual of the Atiyah algebroid for a principal bundle over the moduli space of parabolic / parahoric / stacky bundles. Duals of Atiyah algebroids always have a (linear) Poisson structure, generalizing the Kirrilov–Kostant–Souriau Poisson structure on the dual of a Lie algebra.

In this note, we exhibit another instance of this latter phenomenon, by realising the Hodge-Deligne moduli space of
\lambda
-connections as the dual to the Atiyah algebroid of the determinant of cohomology line bundle over the moduli space of stable bundles.

With the projection

[

\mathcal{L}_{\det}=\det\pi_{*}\mathcal{E}\otimes\det R^{1}\pi_{*}(\mathcal{E})^{*}.

4.
The Hodge-Deligne moduli space of
\lambda
-connections

The moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{\operatorname{Dol}}^{\operatorname{ss}}
of semi-stable, rank
r
Higgs bundles with fixed determinant on
\mathcal{C}
has a hyper-Kähler structure, and if we denote the complex structure of
\mathcal{M}_{\operatorname{Dol}}
as
I
, a complex structure
J
has a modular interpretation
\mathcal{M}_{\operatorname{dR}}
as the moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{\operatorname{dR}}
of flat
\operatorname{SL}_{r}
connections (hence there is a homomorphism
\mathcal{M}^{\operatorname{ss}}_{\operatorname{Dol}}\cong\mathcal{M}_{\operatorname{dR}}
, part of non-abelian Hodge theory).

One can therefore look at the
twistor space
of
\mathcal{M}^{\operatorname{ss}}_{\operatorname{Dol}}
, which is a complex analytic Poisson variety
\mathcal{M}_{\operatorname{tw}}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
, whose fibres are complex symplectic, giving the various complex structures of the hyper-Kähler structure
[
HKLR87
, §3.F]
.

Following a suggestion by Deligne, Simpson described the twistor space
\mathcal{M}_{\operatorname{tw}}
as follows
[
Sim97
, §4]
: in this case, there is a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
action on
\mathcal{M}_{\operatorname{tw}}
, and one can understand
\mathcal{M}_{\operatorname{tw}}
by glueing the Hodge-Deligne moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{\operatorname{Hod},\mathcal{C}}
to
\mathcal{M}_{\operatorname{Hod},\overline{\mathcal{C}}}
, where
\overline{\mathcal{C}}
is the curve obtained by taking the opposite complex structure on
\mathcal{C}
. The Hodge-Deligne moduli space, which naturally comes with a morphism
\mathcal{M}_{\operatorname{Hod},\mathcal{C}}\rightarrow\mathbb{A}^{1}
, arises from the moduli problem of
\lambda
-connections
[
Sim94
]
, which are triples
(E,\lambda,\nabla)
, where
E
is a bundle,
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
a scalar, and
\nabla
a morphism
E\rightarrow E\otimes T^{*}
satisfying a scaled Leibniz rule:

(2)

\nabla(fs)=f\nabla(s)+\lambda df\otimes s,

for regular functions
f
and sections
s
of
E
.

When
\lambda=0
, a
\lambda
-connection is just a Higgs field, and so the
0
-fibre of
\mathcal{M}_{\operatorname{Hod}}
is just
\mathcal{M}_{\operatorname{Dol}}
. When
\lambda=1
, we get ordinary connections, so the fibre over

is just
\mathcal{M}_{\operatorname{dR}}
, and by using the
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-action all the other fibres for
\lambda\neq 0
are also isomorphic to this.

In the particular case when the base is a projective curve, one can also understand
\lambda
-connections as pairs

E
is a bundle, and

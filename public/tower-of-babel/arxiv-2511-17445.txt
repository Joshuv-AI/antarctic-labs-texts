Origin of large topological Hall effect in the EuCd 2 Sb 2 antiferromagnet
Faheem Gul, Orest Pavlosiuk, Tetiana Romanova, Dariusz Kaczorowski, Piotr Wiśniewski

Abstract
We study the origin of large topological Hall effect in the single-crystalline EuCd 2 Sb 2 , which orders antiferromagnetically at the Néel temperature T N = 7.4 T_{\rm N}=7.4 K. Measurements of magnetoresistance and Hall resistivity disclose anomalies that evolve with temperature and magnetic field, closely tracking the magnetization process. Analysis of these data identifies three possible mechanisms responsible for the enhanced Berry curvature driving the observed topological Hall effect. Below and above T N T_{\rm N} , Weyl states are the main sources of large momentum-space Berry curvature, though their formation mechanisms differ in these two temperature ranges. Below T N T_{\rm N} , breaking of C 3 C_{3} symmetry generates Dirac points that split into Weyl nodes in applied magnetic field, whereas above T N T_{\rm N} , strong spin fluctuations can induce Weyl states. The third contribution, which occurs below T N T_{\rm N} , arises from scalar spin chirality developing within antiferromagnetic domain walls, which generates a real-space Berry curvature.



I
Introduction

The discovery of topologically protected electronic states in Dirac and Weyl semimetals prompted extensive research on these systems
2
.
Electronic bands of these materials are degenerated in specific points of the momentum space.
That degeneracy is protected by the time-reversal symmetry and (or) inversion symmetry
7
. Breaking these symmetries leads to an enhancement of Berry curvature in momentum space, and therefore electrons in applied magnetic field can acquire additional Berry phase, when moving on closed paths. This, in turn, is reflected as intrinsic contributions into the anomalous Hall effect (AHE)
11
;
2
.
Such contributions are termed ”unconventional” to be distinguished from the ”conventional” AHE scaling with the spontaneous or field-induced magnetization. The AHE enhanced by unconventional part was reported in several Weyl semimetals, e.g. PrAlGe, Co
3
Sn
2
S
2
and SmAlSi
10
;
8
;
32
and attributed to the rise of Berry curvature in momentum-space.

Besides the momentum space, Berry curvature can also emerge in real space due to the presence of spin structures with a finite spin chirality, consequently giving rise to the Berry phase in wavefunctions of conduction electrons
5
;
25
;
7
.
In both momentum- and real-space the Berry curvature acts as an apparent magnetic field and manifests itself through magneto-transport anomalies (in both AHE and magnetoresistance)
29
;
10
;
16
;
15
;
26
;
17
.

The unconventional contributions to AHE were reported in various compounds, including Eu-based 1:2:2 antiferromagnetic (AFM) systems
23
;
30
;
31
;
33
;
20
. We will refer to that phenomenon as topological Hall effect (THE), because it is generally attributed to the enhancement of Berry curvature stemming from various mechanisms.
In the case of hexagonal EuIn
2
As
2
(space group
P6_{\rm 3}/mmc
), a plausible axion-insulator, the helical AFM structure gains the spin chirality in applied field, which gives rise to the net Berry curvature in the real space
31
.

In contrast, for compounds EuZn
2
Sb
2
, EuZn
2
As
2
and EuCd
2
As
2
, crystallizing in a trigonal crystal structure (
P\bar{3}m1
space group), other mechanisms of THE were proposed, because they are all hosting collinear A-type AFM structures with magnetic moments ferromagnetically aligned in
ab
-planes and stacked antiferromagnetically along the
c
-axes
20
;
28
;
22
.

In EuZn
2
Sb
2
, it was the spin chirality emerging within the domain walls
20
, while in EuZn
2
As
2
, the local chirality arises (altering the Berry curvature) due to ferromagnetic fluctuations below and above Néel temperature (
T_{\rm N}
)
33
.
On the other hand, THE exhibited by EuCd
2
As
2
was ascribed to momentum-space Berry curvature from Dirac or Weyl points
30
.
All these compounds display significant spin fluctuations due to strong short-range magnetic interactions at temperatures even well above
T_{\rm N}
.
These fluctuations are another source of the pronounced THE persisting above the ordering temperature, as demonstrated on the example of EuCd
2
As
2

9
.

However, the small local minimum that appears in topological Hall resistivity,
\rho^{\rm T}_{xy}(H)
, at 2 K and in magnetic field
\mu_{0}H\approx 1.3
T was overlooked in the analysis presented in Ref.
30
. This anomaly persisted in a form of smaller hump at 6 K, but disappeared above
T_{\rm N}
.
This made it different from the main peak of the
\rho^{\rm T}_{xy}(H)
attaining the largest magnitude at 10 K, i.e. just above
T_{\rm N}
. We noticed similar small anomalies in other Eu-based 1:2:2 systems that demonstrate THE, described in literature.
Such fact prompted our research on the origin of THE in a sister material EuCd
2
Sb
2
, which was not thoroughly investigated in previous studies
21
;
23
.

The compound EuCd
2
Sb
2
(isostructural with EuZn
2
Sb
2
, EuZn
2
As
2
and EuCd
2
As
2
) has recently been identified as a Weyl semimetal
21
;
23
.
It also hosts A-type AFM structure with spins parallel to the
ab
-plane,
18
;
21
but its
T_{\rm N}
= 7.4 K is lower than in these three isostructural systems (equal 13.5 K, 20 K and 9.5 K, respectively
20
;
28
;
22
).
The rise of AHE in EuCd
2
Sb
2
has previously been attributed to the Berry curvature in momentum space for both single-crystalline
23
, and thin-film
12
samples.
The proposed mechanism was the breaking of
C_{3}
symmetry in the AFM state leading to the opening of a band gap and emergence of Dirac points in close vicinity of the Fermi level
21
;
23
.
When magnetic field (
H
) is applied, these Dirac points split into pairs of Weyl points.
DFT calculations reported in Ref.
23
showed that the field strong enough to induce spin-polarized state (SPS) considerably shifts these points apart.
The field variation of AHE data shown in that paper was limited to a single temperature of 2 K. It displayed a broad minimum in 1 T and a sharp peak in 2 T, but this behavior was barely discussed.
On the other hand, the data collected on thin-film samples displayed completely different behavior with a single maximum (in
\mu_{0}H\approx 2
T at
T=2
K, and shifting to lower fields with increasing temperature), correlated with anomalies in the magnetoresistance
12
.
The resistivity of single-crystalline EuCd
2
Sb
2
has previously been measured in in-plane field (
\mathbf{H}\perp c
) and presented in
21
, but without specifying if the field was applied transversely or along the current direction.

This motivated us to carry comprehensive measurements of both magnetoresistance and Hall resistivity on single-crystal samples grown in the same conditions, compare obtained data and bring deeper understanding of the mechanisms of their anomalous behavior.
Additionally, we measured and analyzed heat capacity of single-crystalline EuCd
2
Sb
2
sample. It allowed the calculation of magnetic entropy, which corroborated the Eu
2+
ground-state.

II
Experimental details

Single crystals of EuCd
2
Sb
2
were synthesized from Sn-flux (see the Supplemental Material
1
).
The stoichiometric composition of the single crystals was confirmed by energy-dispersive X-ray (EDX) spectroscopy using a NanoSEM 230 (FEI Nova) scanning electron microscope equipped with an EDAX Genesis XM4 spectrometer.
Single-crystal X-ray diffraction was performed using a four-circle single-crystal diffractometer (Oxford Diffraction, X’calibur).
The quality and orientation of the single crystals were determined by Laue back-scattering diffraction technique using a LAUE-COS (Proto Manufacturing) system.

Structural characterization of obtained single crystals brought the results, which very well agree with those reported in earlier works
18
;
21
;
23
.
They are presented in the Supplemental Material
1
.

Magnetic properties measurements were performed using a Quantum Design MPMS-XL magnetometer.
Heat capacity was measured using a relaxation method implemented in a Quantum Design PPMS platform.
The same PPMS was employed to measure electrical transport using the standard four-point technique on bar-shaped samples cut from the single crystal with a wire saw.
Electrical contacts were made from 50
\mu
m thick silver wire attached to the sample with a silver paint.
Magnetic field dependent longitudinal and Hall resistivities were symmetrized and anti-symmetrized, respectively, in order to eliminate spurious contributions from contacts misalignment.

Because the magnetotransport properties are closely related to the evolution of magnetic structure, we shortly describe that structure here.
Below

In the same way

;

;

;
27

4

At

Both values of

are similar to those reported previously

When

The details of the

estimation are described in the Supplemental Material

Heat capacity

The heat capacity (
C_{p}
) of EuCd
2
Sb
2

34
.
We remeasured it on single crystal and performed detailed analysis.
The temperature dependence of the heat capacity is shown in Fig.
1

A
\lambda
-shaped peak is observed at low temperatures, as shown in the inset of Fig.
1

To calculate magnetic entropy, we assumed that the total heat capacity can be described by the formula:

C_{\rm p}=\gamma T+p\,C_{\rm E}+(1-p)\,C_{\rm D}+C_{\rm mag},

(1)

and fitted it to our data for
40\,{\rm{K}}\!<T\!<\!300

, assumed negligible due to the absence of critical spin fluctuations, and fixed at zero value).
Here

=

and

VI
Hall resistivity

Fig.
3
(a) shows the magnetic field dependence of the Hall resistivity,
\rho_{xy}(H)
, at various temperatures.
At
T\geq 50
K,
\rho_{xy}
is linear in
H
, whereas at lower temperatures a curvature is clearly visible, indicating the emergence of anomalous Hall effect.
The slopes of
\rho_{xy}(H)
are almost identical for
T\geq 50
K, and also for
T\leq 15
K, but in the latter case only at fields above certain values, which are very close to
H_{\rm s}
when
T<T_{\rm N}
.
This indicates that the ordinary Hall effect arises from a single band, while the non-linearity observed in magnetic fields below
H_{\rm s}
is due to anomalous Hall effect.

Both the Hall conductivity,
\sigma_{xy}=400\,(\Omega\rm{cm})^{-1}
and the longitudinal conductivity
\sigma_{xx}=1.7\times 10^{3}\,(\Omega\rm{cm})^{-1}
(calculated as shown in Supplemental Material
1
) place EuCd
2
Sb
2
is in the region of the scaling diagram proposed in
11
where the skew-scattering contribution is negligible.
Hence, by ruling out that contribution, the Hall resistivity for a single band system can be described as a sum of three contributions,
11
:

\rho_{xy}=\rho_{xy}^{\rm o}+\rho_{xy}^{\rm CA}+\rho_{xy}^{\rm T}

(2)

where
\rho_{xy}^{\rm o}=R_{\rm H}H
is the ordinary Hall resistivity,
\rho_{xy}^{\rm CA}=S_{\rm H}\rho_{xx}^{2}M
is the conventional anomalous Hall resistivity, and
\rho_{xy}^{\rm T}
is topological Hall effect contribution.
Here
S_{\rm H}
and
R_{\rm H}
are anomalous Hall and ordinary Hall coefficients, respectively.
The slope of the magnetic field dependence of ordinary Hall contribution,
R_{\mathrm{H}}H
, defines the carrier concentration:
n_{\rm H}=1/eR_{\rm H}
.
The observed positive ordinary Hall coefficient indicates that holes are the majority charge carriers.
The carrier concentration in EuCd
2
Sb
2
is very modestly affected by temperature. At
T=2
K,
n_{\rm H}=2.34\times 10^{19}\text{cm}^{-3}
. It is larger by only 5% at
T=100
K, indicating that the Fermi surface almost does not change with temperature (the details of determination and the carrier concentration at different temperatures are provided in the Supplemental Material
1
).
Previously reported
n_{\rm H}
for EuCd
2
Sb
2
at 2 K was
2.5\times 10^{19}
cm
-3

23
, which is very close to the value we obtained, indicating similar electronic structure of samples. The anomalous coefficient
S_{\rm H}
was obtained from the data measured in the high magnetic field region, as described in the Supplemental Material
1
.

The topological Hall resistivity,
\rho_{xy}^{\rm T}(H)
, is due to the real- or momentum-space Berry phase stemming from continuous change of the spin configuration in applied field. We assume that it vanishes in the high field region, where the spin-polarized state (SPS) is induced. This is obvious for the real-space case, but the momentum-space Berry phase depends on Zeeman band splitting, which induces Weyl nodes and modulates their mutual separation and distance from the Fermi level. Therefore, in some ranges of magnetic field, a corresponding part of
\rho_{xy}^{\rm T}(H)
might mirror the behavior of magnetization, and therefore be difficult to separate from
\rho_{xy}^{\rm CA}(H)
.
However, the shape of the prominent anomalies in
\rho_{xy}^{\rm A}(H)
(obtained by subtraction of the linear-in-field term from the
\rho_{xy}(H)
and shown in Fig.
3
(b)) and their convergence at
\approx 20\mu\Omega{\rm cm}
for all
T\leq
15 K, indicates that this part of
\rho_{xy}^{\rm T}(H)
becomes negligible in fields close to
H_{\rm s}
.
Subsequently, the topological Hall resistivity,
\rho_{xy}^{\rm T}
, was extracted using Eq.
2
, and it is shown in Fig.
3
(c) (the procedure is described in the Supplemental Material
1
).

The origin of unconventional AHE in ferromagnets is often assessed from the dependence of corresponding conductivity,
\sigma_{xy}^{\rm T}
, on the longitudinal conductivity,
\sigma_{xx}
.

According to a scaling scheme proposed in Ref.
11
, the Berry phase is responsible for AHE when
\sigma_{xy}^{\rm A}
is independent of
\sigma_{xx}
, in a regime of
[10^{4}\,(\Omega{\,\rm cm})^{-1}<\sigma_{xx}<10^{6}\,(\Omega{\,\rm cm})^{-1}]
, whereas the extrinsic side jump mechanism is the source of AHE, if
\sigma_{xy}^{\rm A}\propto{\sigma_{xx}}^{\alpha}
, with
\alpha\approx
1.6, for
[\sigma_{xx}<10^{4}\,(\Omega{\,\rm cm})^{-1}]
.

In order to test that scaling on our data we calculated respective conductivities as:

\sigma_{xx}=\rho_{xx}/(\rho^{2}_{xx}+\rho^{2}_{xy})
and

\sigma^{\rm T}_{xy}=\rho_{xy}^{\rm T}/(\rho^{2}_{xx}+\rho^{2}_{xy})
(cf Supplemental Material
1
).
Then, the extreme dip values (i.e. minima of
\rho_{xy}^{\rm T}(H)
, marked with an arrow in Fig.
3
(c) for 6 K) of
\sigma_{xy}^{\rm T}
were plotted against
\sigma_{xx}
at various temperatures up to 15 K, which is shown in Fig.
4
(a).

EuCd
2
Sb
2
is a moderately bad metal, with
\sigma_{xx}\approx 1.7\times 10^{3}\,\Omega{\,\rm cm}
placing it at the border between regimes of extrinsic- and intrinsic-AHE, proposed for ferromagnets within above mentioned scaling scheme. Actually, its
\sigma_{xy}^{\rm A}
changes weakly with
\sigma_{xx}
above
T_{\rm N}
, and even weaker at
T<T_{\rm N}
, when compared to ferromagnets in the extrinsic-AHE regime
5
. Small number of experimental point makes virtually impossible to estimate precisely the scaling exponent in
\sigma_{xy}^{\rm T}\propto{\sigma_{xx}}^{\alpha}
, but Fig.
4
(a) shows that the
\alpha\approx 1
approximation is reasonable.
Of course that scheme must be adopted to AFM systems with caution. Serious departures from that scaling was demonstrated for numerous AFM systems, e.g. MnGe and SmAlSi
5
;
6

Both, the magnitudes of conductivities and their scaling in EuCd
2
Sb
2
strongly resemble those shown for GdPtBi
24
, thus we believe that they hint at predominantly intrinsic nature of THE.
Nevertheless, we observe that
\sigma_{xy}^{\rm T}
is dependent on
\sigma_{xx}
, and two distinct approximately linear regimes can be identified, with clearly different slopes below and above
T_{\rm N}
(shown by red and blue lines in Fig.
4
(a)).
Fig.
4
(b) shows the temperature dependence of both
H_{\rm min}
, magnetic field at which
\sigma_{xy}^{\rm T}
achieves its minimum value,
\sigma_{xy(\rm min)}^{\rm T}
, and
\sigma_{xy(\rm min)}^{\rm T}
itself.
Both quantities decrease with increasing
T
below
T_{\rm N}
and increase above
T_{\rm N}
.
This indicates that distinct mechanisms are responsible for THE-related dips in these two temperature regions.
The Hall angle, (
\theta_{\rm H}

VII
Summary and conclusion

In this study we grew and characterized the single crystals of EuCd
2
Sb
2
. The heat capacity we measured reconfirmed the magnetic ordering below
T_{\rm N}=7.4
K and revealed maximum magnetic entropy near the ideal value
R
ln(8) for Eu
+2
ion. We also found that acoustic phonon modes are dominant at high-temperatures. The following magnetization measurements reconfirmed the AFM ground state with localized moments of Eu
+2
ions and showed how magnetic structure changes in applied magnetic field.
Next, we investigated the electrical resistivity in longitudinal and transverse fields, as well as Hall resistivity. Enhanced negative magnetoresistance in longitudinal field corroborated the occurrence of Weyl nodes. Topological component was extracted from total Hall resistivity. Prominent anomalies were found in field variations of TMR, LMR and
\rho_{xy}^{\rm T}
in the applied field range where the magnetic structure undergoes strong modification.
After analyzing behavior of extrema of these anomalies we propose that at least three intrinsic mechanisms contribute to THE in EuCd
2
Sb
2
. In AFM state they are Berry curvature due to Weyl nodes originating from broken
C_{3}
symmetry and Berry curvature emerging from the scalar spin chirality within domain walls. Above
T_{\rm N}
it is Berry curvature due to Weyl nodes induced by the spin fluctuations.

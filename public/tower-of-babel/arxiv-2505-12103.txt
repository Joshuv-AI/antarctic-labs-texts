Numerical Integrators for Mechanical Systems on Lie Groups
Viyom Vivek, David Martin de Diego, Ravi Banavar

Abstract
. Retraction maps are known to be the seed for all numerical integrators. These retraction maps-based integrators can be further lifted to tangent and cotangent bundles, giving rise to structure-preserving integrators for mechanical systems. We explore the particular case where the configuration space of our mechanical system is a Lie group with certain symmetries. Here, the integrator simplifies based on the property that the tangent and cotangent bundles of Lie groups are trivializable. Finally, we present a framework for designing numerical integrators for Euler-Poincaré and Lie-Poisson type equations.



1.
Trivializations on Lie Groups

1.1.
Trivialization of the tangent and cotangent bundle of a Lie group

Let
G
be a Lie group with the Lie algebra denoted by
Lie(G)
or
\mathfrak{g}
. The group structure on
G
induces a canonical group structure on the tangent bundle of
G
denoted by
TG
, see
[
5
]
or
[
6
]
. This induced group multiplication and inversion is given by

\displaystyle u_{g}\,v_{h}

\displaystyle:=(T_{g}R_{h}(u)+T_{h}L_{g}(v))_{gh}=(u\cdot h+g\cdot v)_{gh}\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle(u_{g})^{-1}

\displaystyle:=(-T_{e}R_{g^{-1}}\circ T_{g}L_{g^{-1}}(u))_{g^{-1}}=(-g^{-1}\cdot u\cdot g^{-1})_{g^{-1}}

respectively. Here
u_{g},v_{h}\in TG
and we will use the shorthand notation interchangeably. It is known that the tangent bundle of any Lie group can be trivialized using left (or right) translation as

\displaystyle tr_{TG}:TG

\displaystyle\to G\times\mathfrak{g}

\displaystyle u_{g}

\displaystyle\mapsto(g,\xi)

where
\xi:=T_{g}L_{g^{-1}}(u)=g^{-1}\cdot u
. We would like to equip
G\times\mathfrak{g}
with a group structure such that
tr_{TG}
becomes a group isomorphism on top of being a diffeomorphism. So,

\displaystyle tr_{TG}(u_{g}\,v_{h})=tr_{TG}(u_{g})\,tr_{TG}(v_{h})

\displaystyle\implies tr_{TG}((u\cdot h+g\cdot v)_{gh})=(g,g^{-1}\cdot u)(h,h^{-1}\cdot v)

\displaystyle\implies(gh,(gh)^{-1}\cdot(u\cdot h+g\cdot v))=(g,g^{-1}\cdot u)(h,h^{-1}\cdot v)

\displaystyle\implies(gh,h^{-1}g^{-1}\cdot u\cdot h+h^{-1}\cdot v)=(g,g^{-1}\cdot u)(h,h^{-1}\cdot v).

Hence, the group multiplication on
G\times\mathfrak{g}
is given by

\displaystyle(g,\xi)(h,\eta):=(gh,Ad_{h^{-1}}\xi+\eta)

where
(g,\xi),(h,\eta)\in G\times\mathfrak{g}
. The identity element is the zero vector of the Lie algebra i.e.
(e,0)
while the inverse is given by

\displaystyle(g,\xi)^{-1}:=(g^{-1},-Ad_{g}\xi).

Notice that this defines a semidirect product structure on
G\times\mathfrak{g}
where
G
is treated as a group while
\mathfrak{g}
is treated as a vector space with
G
acting through the adjoint action. Henceforth we will denote this group as
G\ltimes_{L}\mathfrak{g}
or simply
G\ltimes\mathfrak{g}
.

Analogously, the cotangent bundle of
G
denoted by
T^{*}G
can be first equipped with the induced canonical group structure and then trivialized using left (or right) translation. We end up with
G\ltimes_{L}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
or
G\ltimes\mathfrak{g}^{*}
where the group multiplication is given by

\displaystyle(g,\mu)(h,\nu):=(gh,Ad^{*}_{h^{-1}}\mu+\nu)

where
(g,\mu),(h,\nu)\in G\times\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. Here the identity element is the zero vector of the dual of the Lie algebra i.e.
(e,0)
while the inverse is given by

\displaystyle(g,\mu)^{-1}:=(g^{-1},-Ad^{*}_{g}\mu).

1.2.
Trivialization of higher order bundles of a Lie group

The higher order bundles of a Lie group
G
namely
TTG,TT^{*}G,T^{*}TG
and
T^{*}T^{*}G
can be trivialized in multiple ways, see
[

]
,
[

]
or
[

]
. Let us begin with trivializing
TTG
. We first trivialize

to get the following identification
TTG\cong T(G\ltimes\mathfrak{g})
and then trivialize
T(G\ltimes\mathfrak{g})\cong(G\ltimes\mathfrak{g})\ltimes Lie(G\ltimes\mathfrak{g})
or
(G\ltimes\mathfrak{g})\ltimes(\mathfrak{g}\ltimes\mathfrak{g})
to finally obtain
TTG\cong(G\ltimes\mathfrak{g})\ltimes(\mathfrak{g}\ltimes\mathfrak{g})
. The group multiplication is given by

\displaystyle(g,\xi,\bar{\xi},\tilde{\xi})(h,\eta,\bar{\eta},\tilde{\eta}):=

\displaystyle(gh,Ad_{h^{-1}}\xi+\eta,

\displaystyle Ad_{h^{-1}}\bar{\xi}+\bar{\eta},Ad_{h^{-1}}(\tilde{\xi}+[\bar{\xi},Ad_{h}\eta])+\tilde{\eta})

where
(g,\xi,\bar{\xi},\tilde{\xi}),(h,\eta,\bar{\eta},\tilde{\eta})\in(G\ltimes\mathfrak{g})\ltimes(\mathfrak{g}\ltimes\mathfrak{g})
. The identity is
(e,0,0,0)
and the inverse is given by

\displaystyle(g,\xi,\bar{\xi},\tilde{\xi})^{-1}:=(g^{-1},-Ad_{g}\xi,-Ad_{g}\bar{\xi},-Ad_{g}(\tilde{\xi}+[\xi,\bar{\xi}])).

Note that we will be using left trivialization throughout without stating it explicitly. In a similar fashion, we get
TT^{*}G\cong(G\ltimes\mathfrak{g}^{*})\ltimes(\mathfrak{g}\ltimes\mathfrak{g}^{*})
,
T^{*}TG\cong(G\ltimes\mathfrak{g})\ltimes(\mathfrak{g}^{*}\ltimes\mathfrak{g}^{*})
and
T^{*}T^{*}G\cong(G\ltimes\mathfrak{g}^{*})\ltimes(\mathfrak{g}^{*}\ltimes\mathfrak{g})
.

2.
Mechanical Systems on Lie Groups

2.1.
Euler-Poincaré equations

Let the Lie group
G
be the configuration space of a mechanical system with the Lagrangian
\mathscr{L}:TG\to\mathbb{R}
. Then the Euler-Lagrange equations are given by

(1)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{\delta\mathscr{L}}{\delta\dot{g}}\right)=\frac{\delta\mathscr{L}}{\delta g},

see
[
14
]
or
[
13
]
. The trivialized Euler-Lagrange equations are obtained from trivializing
TG
as

(2)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{\delta\tilde{\mathscr{L}}}{\delta\xi}\right)

\displaystyle=ad^{*}_{\xi}\left(\frac{\delta\tilde{\mathscr{L}}}{\delta\xi}\right)+T^{*}_{e}L_{g}\left(\frac{\delta\tilde{\mathscr{L}}}{\delta g}\right)\quad\text{and}

(3)

\displaystyle\dot{g}

\displaystyle=T_{e}L_{g}(\xi)

where
\tilde{\mathscr{L}}:G\ltimes\mathfrak{g}\to\mathbb{R}
is the trivialized Lagrangian and equation (3) is used for reconstruction. In the special case where the trivialized Lagrangian just depends on the Lie algebra i.e. the Lagrangian is left (or right) invariant, the trivialized Euler-Lagrange equations reduce to the Euler-Poincaré equations given by

(4)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{\delta l}{\delta\xi}\right)

\displaystyle=ad^{*}_{\xi}\left(\frac{\delta l}{\delta\xi}\right)\quad\text{and}

(5)

\displaystyle\dot{g}

\displaystyle=T_{e}L_{g}(\xi)

where
l:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathbb{R}
is the reduced Lagrangian.

2.2.
Lie-Poisson equations

Let
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
be the dual of the Lie algebra of
G
equipped with the Lie-Poisson bracket. Then the Lie-Poisson equations corresponding to a reduced Hamiltonian
h:\mathfrak{g}^{*}\to\mathbb{R}
are given by

(6)

\displaystyle\dot{\mu}

\displaystyle=ad_{\frac{\delta h}{\delta\mu}}^{*}(\mu)\quad\text{and}

(7)

\displaystyle\dot{g}

\displaystyle=T_{e}L_{g}\left(\frac{\delta h}{\delta\mu}\right)

see
[
13
]
,
[
14
]
or
[
17
]
. Here again equation (7) is the reconstruction equation. In the presence of a Legendre transform
\mathbb{F}l:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}^{*}
that takes
\xi\mapsto\frac{\delta l}{\delta\xi}=:\mu
and
h(\mu)=\left<\mu,\xi\right>-l(\xi)
, the set of equations (4) and (5) can be shown to be equivalent to (6) and (7).

4.
Illustrative Example

Let us carefully develop a numerical integrator for the Lie-Poisson equations on a Lie group
G
. We begin by choosing a discretization map
\tilde{R}_{d}:G\ltimes\mathfrak{g}\to G\times G
as
\tilde{R}_{d}(g,\xi):=(g,g\tau(\xi))
. Then, we evaluate the tangent of
\tilde{R}_{d}
at
(g,\xi)
to get

\displaystyle T_{(g,\xi)}\tilde{R}_{d}(v,\eta)=(v,T_{\tau(\xi)}L_{g}(T_{\xi}\tau(\eta))+T_{g}R_{\tau(\xi)}(v))

where
(v,\eta)\in T_{(g,\xi)}(G\ltimes\mathfrak{g})
. Using the natural pairing between dual vectors, we can immediately find

\displaystyle T_{(g,\xi)}^{*}\tilde{R}_{d}(p,q)=(p+T^{*}_{g}R_{\tau(\xi)}(q),T^{*}_{\xi}\tau(T^{*}_{\tau(\xi)}L_{g}(q)))

where
(p,q)\in T^{*}_{(g,g\tau(\xi))}(G\times G)
. Now, we trivialize both
T^{*}(G\ltimes\mathfrak{g})
and
T^{*}(G\times G)
as

\displaystyle tr_{T^{*}(G\ltimes\mathfrak{g})}(g,\xi;p+T^{*}_{g}R_{\tau(\xi)}(q),T^{*}_{\xi}\tau(T^{*}L_{g}(q)))=(g,\xi;T^{*}_{e}L_{g}(p)

\displaystyle+T^{*}_{e}L_{g}(T^{*}_{g}R_{\tau(\xi)}(q))+ad^{*}_{\xi}(T^{*}_{\xi}\tau(T^{*}_{\tau(\xi)}L_{g}(q)),T^{*}_{\xi}\tau(T^{*}_{\tau(\xi)}L_{g}(q)))\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle tr_{T^{*}(G\times G)}(g,g\tau(\xi);p,q)=(g,g\tau(\xi);T^{*}_{e}L_{g}(p),T^{*}_{e}L_{g\tau(\xi)}(q))

respectively. Finally, we use the trivialized symplectomorphisms
\tilde{\alpha}_{G}
and
\tilde{\Phi}
to compute
\tilde{R}_{d}^{T^{*}}
. The expression for
\tilde{\Phi}
is given by

\displaystyle\tilde{\Phi}(g,\mu;h,\nu):=(g,h;\mu,-\nu)

where
g,h\in G
and
\mu,\nu\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
.
As we only need the inverse of
\tilde{R}_{d}^{T^{*}}
for the construction of our integrator, we compute it directly to find

\displaystyle(\tilde{R}_{d}^{T^{*}})^{-1}(g_{k},\mu_{k};g_{k+1},\mu_{k+1})=(g_{k},-T^{*}_{\tau^{-1}(g_{k}^{-1}g_{k+1})}\tau(T^{*}_{g_{k}^{-1}g_{k+1}}L_{g_{k+1}^{-1}g_{k}}(\mu_{k+1}));

\displaystyle=(g_{k},-d_{\tau^{-1}(g_{k}^{-1}g_{k+1})}^{L*}\tau(\mu_{k+1}),\tau^{-1}(g_{k}^{-1}g_{k+1}),\mu_{k}-Ad^{*}_{g_{k+1}^{-1}g_{k}}(\mu_{k+1})).

\tau

\displaystyle\eta

\displaystyle\longmapsto T_{\tau(\xi)}L_{\tau(-\xi)}(T_{\xi}\tau(\eta))

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{*}

\displaystyle\longmapsto T^{*}_{\xi}\tau(T^{*}_{\tau(\xi)}L_{\tau(-\xi)}(\mu))

where
\xi,\eta\in\mathfrak{g}
and
\mu\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
.

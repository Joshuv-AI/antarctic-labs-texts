Abstract
. This paper introduces a new definition of α \alpha -monotone operators in real 2-uniformly convex and smooth Banach spaces. Based on this new definition, we establish several novel structural and analytical properties of such operators, which not only extend classical results from Hilbert spaces but also reveal new insights into the geometry of Banach spaces. In particular, we examine the resolvent of α \alpha -maximal monotone operators and demonstrate how its behavior is consistent with, and generalizes, the well-known firmly nonexpansive property in the Hilbert space setting. Building upon this theoretical framework, we further investigate algorithmic applications. Specifically, we analyze the forward-reflected-backward splitting algorithm under the new α \alpha -monotonicity assumption and prove its strong convergence as well as its R R -linear convergence rate in real 2-uniformly convex and smooth Banach spaces.

1.
Introduction

Let
X
be a real Banach space and
X^{*}
its dual space of
X
. Many problems arising in economics, mechanics, signal and image processing, and machine learning can be formulated as finding a point
x\in X
satisfying the inclusion

(1.1)

\textrm{find }x\in X\quad\textrm{such that }0\in Ax,

where
A:X\rightarrow 2^{X^{*}}
is a maximally monotone operator. A widely used iterative method for solving (
1.1
) is the proximal point algorithm (PPA), originally introduced by Martinet
[
1
]
and Rockafellar
[
2
]
. The PPA generates a sequence
x_{n}
via

(1.2)

x_{n+1}=J_{\lambda A}x_{n},

where
\lambda>0
and
J_{\lambda A}=(I+\lambda A)^{-1}
denotes the resolvent operator of
A
. The PPA and its variants have been extensively studied in Hilbert spaces. See, e.g.,
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
]
and the references therein. In particular, for a maximally monotone operator
A
, its resolvent operator
J_{\lambda A}
is known to be firmly nonexpansive in Hilbert spaces. This property directly ensures the convergence of the PPA through the classical the Krasnoselskii-Mann iterative framework. In contrast to the case of Hilbert spaces, the resolvent of a maximally monotone operator is not, in general, a firmly nonexpansive mapping in Banach spaces. To address this issue, Kohsaka and Takahashi
[
7
]
introduced the class of firmly nonexpansive type mappings in Banach spaces. Specifically, they showed that if
A
is a maximally monotone operator on a smooth, strictly convex, and reflexive Banach space
X
such that
J:X\rightarrow X^{*}
is the normalized duality mapping,
\lambda>0
, and
J_{\lambda A}=(J+\lambda A)^{-1}J
, then
J_{\lambda A}
is a firmly nonexpansive type from a nonempty closed convex subset
C
of
X
onto the domain
D(A)
of
A
. Further results and detailed discussions concerning monotone operators in Banach spaces are available in the existing literature
[
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
]

Recently, Dao and Phan
[
14
]
introduced the concept of
\alpha
-monotone operators (See Definition
3.1
) in Hilbert spaces. They studied various properties of
\alpha
-monotone operators with and without Lipschitz assumptions. Especially, the resolvent of
\alpha
-monotone operators is investigated in the Hilbert spaces setting. Let
A:X\rightarrow 2^{X^{*}}
, then Definition
3.1
is also well-defined in Banach spaces. In particular, for
\alpha=0
, the
\alpha
-monotone operators reduce to the monotone operators, which was studied by
[
7
]
in Banach spaces. For both
\alpha>0
and
\alpha<0
, the approach employed in
[
7
]
fails to produce resolvent operator properties analogous to those associated with
\alpha
-monotone operators in Hilbert spaces of
[
14
]
. These limitations arise from a fundamental misalignment: the naive definition of
\alpha
-monotonicity is tied to the Hilbert space inner product, which simultaneously encodes the spaces norm and its duality. In Banach spaces, however, this duality is decoupled from the norm, and the normalized duality mapping
J:X\rightarrow X^{*}
a central geometric object that generalizes the role of the inner product by linking elements of with their duals in
X^{*}
must be explicitly incorporated in order to capture the intrinsic structure of the space. Without this incorporation, the notion of
\alpha
-monotonicity fails to reflect key geometric features such as convexity and smoothness. Consequently, the naive definition becomes inadequate for analyzing monotone operators and for developing or studying algorithms in the Banach space setting.

To address these gaps, we propose a novel definition of
\alpha
-monotonicity for operators in smooth Banach spaces (See Definition
3.2
) that explicitly integrates the normalized duality mapping
J
, aligning the operators monotonicity with the Banach spaces geometry. This definition resolves the shortcomings of the naive generalization: by replacing the Hilbert space norm squared
\|x-y\|^{2}
in Definition
3.1
with the duality pairing
\langle x-y,Jx-Jy\rangle
, it inherently encodes the Banach space’s smoothness and convexity properties. As a result, in Hilbert spaces,
J
reduces to the identity mapping, so Definition
3.2
recovers the standard
\alpha
-monotonicity-ensuring backward compatibility. For general smooth Banach spaces, the term
\langle x-y,Jx-Jy\rangle
acts as a “Banach-space-adapted inner product proxy,” enabling meaningful quantification of monotonicity that aligns with the spaces geometry. We summarize the main contributions of this paper as follows:

(i) We provide a systematic characterization of
\alpha
-monotone operators in smooth Banach spaces. In particular, we clarify their fundamental properties, establish their connections with existing operator classes such as maximally monotone and strongly monotone operators, and identify sufficient conditions under which
\alpha
-monotone operators are maximal.

(ii) We demonstrate that the resolvent associated with
\alpha
-monotone operators in real 2-uniformly convex and uniformly smooth Banach spaces preserves key contractive-type properties. These properties serve as natural analogues of the contractive behavior of resolvents in Hilbert spaces, thereby showing that our framework extends classical results in a consistent and meaningful way.

(iii) We further apply the concept of
\alpha
-monotonicity to analyze the forward-reflected-backward splitting algorithm in Banach spaces. Under the assumption that the “strong convexity outweighs weak convexity” condition holds, we establish both strong convergence and
R
-linear convergence of the algorithm. This provides new theoretical guarantees for the efficiency and robustness of operator-splitting methods beyond the Hilbert space setting.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. Section 2 reviews preliminary concepts from Banach space geometry and monotone operator theory. Section 3 introduces our definition of
\alpha
-monotonicity, establishes its fundamental properties, and investigates the resolvent of
\alpha
-monotone operators, deriving corresponding contractivity results. Section 4 applies the proposed framework to the forward-reflected-backward splitting algorithm and proves the convergence of the associated algorithm in Banach spaces.

Preliminaries

Throughout this paper, every Banach space is assumed to be real. Let
\mathbb{N}
and
\mathbb{R}
denote the sets of positive integers and real numbers, respectively. For a sequence
{x_{n}}
in a Banach space

A Banach space

(2.1)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{\|x+ty\|-\|x\|}{t}

exists for all

, where

is the unit sphere of

. A Banach space

is said to be strictly convex if

(2.2)

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{x+y}{2}\right\|<1

whenever
x,y\in S_{X}
and
x\neq y
.

The modulus of convexity of

, is

\delta_{X}(\varepsilon)=\inf\left\{1-\frac{\|x+y\|}{2}:\|x\|=\|y\|=1,\|x-y\|=\varepsilon\right\},

where the infimum can be taken over all
\|x\|\leq 1,\|y\|\leq 1
. The
modulus of smoothness is

\rho_{X}(\tau)=sup\left\{\frac{\|x+\tau y\|+\|x-\tau y\|}{2}-1:\|x\|=\|y\|=1\right\},

where the supremum can be taken over all
\|x\|\leq 1,\|y\|\leq 1
.

A Banach space X is called uniformly convex if
\delta_{X}(\varepsilon)>0
for all
\varepsilon>0
, and is called q-uniformly convex

) if there exists a constant

such that
\delta_{X}(\varepsilon)\geq C\varepsilon^{q}
for all
\varepsilon>0
. X is called uniformly smooth if
\rho_{X}(\tau)/\tau\to 0
as
\tau\to 0
, and is called p-uniformly smooth

if there exists a constant

such that
\rho_{X}(\tau)\leq K\tau^{p}
for all
\tau>0
.

Let
X
be a Banach space and let
J:X\to X^{*}
be the normalized duality mapping defined by

(2.3)

\displaystyle Jx=\left\{x^{*}\in X^{*}:\left<x,x^{*}\right>=\|x\|^{2}=\left\|x^{*}\right\|^{2}\right\}

for all

.

We know the following properties of

(See for example Cioranescu
[
15

[

]

1. If
X
is smooth, then

2. If

3. If
X

is one-to-one; that is,

;

4. If

is strictly monotone, that is, if (
(x,x^{*})

.

Let

\displaystyle(\|x\|-\|y\|)^{2}\leq\phi(x,y)\leq(\|x\|+\|y\|)^{2}

\displaystyle\phi(x,y)=0\Leftrightarrow x=y.

Lemma 2.1
.

[

Let

3.
\alpha
-monotone operators in Banach spaces

In this section, we introduce a new definition of
\alpha
-monotone operators in Banach spaces and compare it with the classical version. And then, we derive some basic properties of these new
\alpha
-monotone operators.

3.1.
New definition of monotone operators in Banach spaces

In this subsection, we introduce a new definition of
\alpha
-monotone operators in Banach spaces and compare it with the classical version. Specifically, an operator that is monotone in the sense of our definition is not equivalent to one that is monotone in the sense of the classical definition. We further show that in a 2-uniformly convex and smooth Banach space, an
\alpha
-monotone operator under our definition is equivalent to that under the classical version if and only if the space is 2-uniformly smooth. We also establish that strong monotonicity of an operator in our definition implies strong monotonicity in the classical definition, though the converse does not hold; similarly, weak monotonicity in the classical definition implies weak monotonicity in our definition, with the converse also failing.

Now we recall the classical version of
\alpha
-monotonicity operators in Banach spaces.

Definition 3.1

(Classical
\alpha
-monotonicity operators in Banach spaces)
.

An operator
A:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
is said to be
\alpha
-monotone
(\alpha\in\mathbb{R})
if

\forall(x,u),(y,v)\in~\mathrm{gra}~A,\quad\langle x-y,u-v\rangle\geq\alpha\|x-y\|^{2}.

If Banach space
X
is smooth, then the dual mapping
J:X\to X^{*}
is single-valued. We can define a new definition of
\alpha
-monotonicity operators in smooth Banach spaces as follows.

Definition 3.2

(
\alpha
-monotonicity operators in smooth Banach
spaces)
.

Let
X
be a smooth Banach space. An operator
A:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
is said to be
\alpha
-monotone
(\alpha\in\mathbb{R})
if

\forall(x,u),(y,v)\in~\mathrm{gra}~A,\quad\langle x-y,u-v\rangle\geq\alpha\langle x-y,Jx-Jy\rangle.

The constant
\alpha
is referred to as the monotonicity constant.
We say that
A
is monotone if
\alpha=0
, strongly monotone if
\alpha>0
, and weakly monotone if
\alpha<0
. The operator
A
is said to be maximally
\alpha
-monotone if it is
\alpha
-monotone and there is no
\alpha
-monotone operator
B:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
such that
\mathrm{gra}~B
properly contains
\mathrm{gra}~A
.

Remark 3.1
.

It is straightforward to show that in Banach spaces, an operator being monotone in the sense of Definition
3.2
(
\alpha=0
) is equivalent to its being monotone in the sense of Definition
3.1
. Hence, for a monotone operator, the property of being maximal is also equivalent under both Definition
3.2
and Definition
3.1
.

Example 3.3
.

Let
X
be a smooth Banach space. The dual mapping
J:X\to X^{*}
is 1-monotone under Definition
3.2
, hence
J
is strong monotone under Definition
3.2
. We know that if
f(x)=\frac{1}{2}\|x\|^{2}
, then
\nabla f=J
, hence
\nabla f
is strong monotone under Definition
3.2
.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
X
be a real smooth and
2
-uniformly convex Banach space. Then, there exists
\mu\geq 1
such that

\frac{1}{2\mu}\|x-y\|^{2}\leq\langle x-y,Jx-Jy\rangle,~~\forall x,y\in X.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.1
,
\langle x-y,Jx-Jy\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\phi(x,y)+\frac{1}{2}\phi(y,x)\geq\frac{1}{2}\phi(x,y)
. Then, by Lemma
2.3
, there exists
\mu\geq 1
such that
\frac{1}{\mu}\|x-y\|^{2}\leq\phi(x,y)
. Hence
\frac{1}{2\mu}\|x-y\|^{2}\leq\langle x-y,Jx-Jy\rangle
.
∎

Now we want to show that if

be a real smooth and
2
-uniformly convex Banach space, there exist

such that
\frac{1}{2\mu}\|x-y\|^{2}\leq\langle x-y,Jx-Jy\rangle\leq L\|x-y\|^{2}

3.2
and Definition

, then

is isomorphic to a Hilbert space.

Theorem 3.5
.

Suppose that Banach space

is 2-uniformly convex and smooth, and there exists

such that

\langle x-y,Jx-Jy\rangle\leq L\|x-y\|^{2}

for all

Let

is isomorphic to a Hilbert space.
∎

3.2.
Basic properties of new monotone operators in Banach spaces

In this subsection, we follow Dao and Phan’s idea
[
14
]
to give some basic properties of
\alpha
-monotone operators.

Throughout,
\alpha
-monotonicity of an operator is defined as in Definition
3.2
, unless otherwise specified for Definition
3.1
.

Let
A:X\rightrightarrows X
be an operator on Banach space
X
. Then its domain is
\mathrm{dom}~A=\{x\in X~|~Ax\neq\emptyset\}
, its set of zeros is
\mathrm{zer}~A=\{x\in X~|~0\in Ax\}
. The graph of
A
is the set
\mathrm{gra}A:=\{(x,u)\in X\times X^{*}~|~u\in Ax\}
and the inverse of
A
, denoted by
A^{-1}
, is the operator with graph
\mathrm{gra}A^{-1}:=\{(u,x)\in X\times X^{*}~|~u\in Ax\}
. The resolvent of
A
is defined by

J_{A}:=(J+A)^{-1}J,

where
J
is the dual mapping.

Lemma 3.13

(monotonicity versus
\alpha
-monotonicity)
.

Let
A:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
and let
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
. Then the following hold:

(i)
A
is
\alpha
-monotone if and only if
A-\beta J
is
(\alpha-\beta)
-monotone.

(ii)
A
is maximally
\alpha
-monotone if and only if
A-\beta J
is maximally
(\alpha-\beta)
-monotone.

Consequently,
A
is (resp., maximally)
\alpha
-monotone if and only if
A-\alpha J
is (resp., maximally) monotone.

Proof.

(i) We first have the equivalences

\quad(x,u)\in~\mathrm{gra}~A\Leftrightarrow(x,u-\beta Jx)\in gra(A-\beta J),

\quad(y,v)\in~\mathrm{gra}~A\Leftrightarrow(y,v-\beta Jy)\in gra(A-\beta J),

and

\displaystyle\langle x-y,u-v\rangle~\geq~\alpha\langle x-y,Jx-Jy\rangle.

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\langle x-y,(u-\beta Jx)-(v-\beta Jy)\rangle~\geq~(\alpha-\beta)\langle x-y,Jx-Jy\rangle.

from which the conclusion follows.

(ii) Assume that
A
is maximally
\alpha
-monotone. By (i),
A-\beta J
is
(\alpha-\beta)
-monotone. Now, suppose that
A-\beta J
is not maximally
(\alpha-\beta)
-monotone. Then there must exist
B^{\prime}:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
such that
B^{\prime}
is
(\alpha-\beta)
-monotone and
\mathrm{gra}~(A-\beta J)\subsetneq\mathrm{gra}~B^{\prime}
. It follows that
B:=B^{\prime}+\beta J
is
\alpha
-monotone due to (i) and that
\mathrm{gra}A~\subsetneq\mathrm{gra}~B
, which contradict the maximal
\alpha
-monotonicity of
A
. We deduce that if
A
is maximally
\alpha
-monotone, then
A-\beta J
is maximally
(\alpha-\beta)
-monotone. This also implies that if
A-\beta J
is maximally
(\alpha-\beta)
-monotone, then
A=(A-\beta J)+\beta J
is maximally
\alpha
-monotone, and we are done.
∎

Definition 3.14
.

We say that
A:X\rightrightarrows X
is
\sigma
-firmly nonexpansive-type mapping if
\sigma>0
and

(3.14)

\displaystyle\forall~(x,u),~(y,v)\in\mathrm{gra}~A,\quad\langle Jx-Jy,u-v\rangle~\geq~\sigma\langle u-v,Ju-Jv\rangle.

If
\sigma=1
, it reduces to the firmly nonexpansive-type mapping, which was introduced by Kohsaka and Takahashi
[
23
]
.

Lemma 3.15

(resolvents of
\alpha
-monotone operators)
.

Let
A:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
be
\alpha
-monotone and let
\gamma>0
. Then the following hold:

(1) For all
(x,a)
,
(y,b)\in\mathrm{gra}~J_{\gamma A}
,

\langle Jx-Jy,a-b\rangle~\geq~(1+\gamma\alpha)\langle a-b,Ja-Jb\rangle.

(2) If
J_{\gamma A}
is single valued, then, for all
x,y\in domJ_{\gamma A}
,

\quad\langle Jx-Jy,J_{\gamma A}x-J_{\gamma A}y\rangle~\geq(1+\gamma\alpha)\langle J_{\gamma A}x-J_{\gamma A}y,JJ_{\gamma A}x-JJ_{\gamma A}y\rangle.

i.e.,
J_{\gamma A}
is
(1+\gamma\alpha)
-firmly nonexpansive-type.

Proof.

(i) Let
(x,a),(y,b)\in\mathrm{gra}~J_{\gamma A}
. Then
Jx\in(J+\gamma A)a
,
Jy\in(J+\gamma A)b
, and so
Jx=Ja+\gamma u
,
Jy=Jb+\gamma v
for some
u\in Aa
,
v\in Ab
. We derive from the
\alpha
-monotonicity of
A
that

\displaystyle\langle Jx-Jy,a-b\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle(Ja+\gamma u)-(Jb+\gamma v),a-b\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle a-b,Ja-Jb\rangle~+~\gamma\langle a-b,u-v\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\langle a-b,Ja-Jb\rangle~+~\gamma\alpha\langle a-b,Ja-Jb\rangle

\displaystyle=(1+\gamma\alpha)\langle a-b,Ja-Jb\rangle.

(ii) This is a direct consequence of (i).
∎

Remark 3.4
.

(i) Clearly, if
A
is
\sigma
-firmly nonexpansive-type mapping, then
A
is single-valued. By the positive homogeneity of
J
, if
A
is
\sigma
-firmly nonexpansive-type mapping, then
\sigma A
is firmly nonexpansive-type mapping.

(ii) Let
A:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
be
\alpha
-monotone and let
\gamma>0
. Then
J_{\gamma A}
is
(1+\gamma\alpha)
-firmly nonexpansive-type mapping, and by the positive homogeneity of

,
(1+\gamma\alpha)J_{\gamma A}
is firmly nonexpansive-type mapping. Particularly, if
A
is monotone, i.e.,
\alpha=0
, then
J_{\gamma A}
is firmly nonexpansive-type mapping.

Lemma 3.16
.

(Browder
[
30
]
and Rockafellar
[

]
; see also Barbu
[

]
and Takahashi
[

]
)
Let

be a smooth, strictly convex and reflexive Banach space and let

be a monotone operator.

is maximal monotone under definition
3.1
if and only if
R(J+rA)=X^{*}
for all

.

Remark 3.5
.

If

be a smooth, strictly convex and reflexive Banach space,

is single-valued, one-to-one and onto, hence

if and only if

Proposition 3.17

(single-valuedness and full domain)
.

Let
X
be a smooth, strictly convex and reflexive Banach space. Let
A:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
be
\alpha
-monotone and let
\gamma>0
such that
1+\gamma\alpha>0
. Then the following hold:

(i)
J_{\gamma A}
is single valued.

(ii)
\mathrm{dom}~J_{\gamma A}=X
if and only if
A
is maximally
\alpha
-monotone.

Proof.

(i) If
X
is strict convex, then
J
is strictly monotone. Hence (i) follows from Lemma
3.15
(i).

(ii) By Lemma
3.13
(i),
A^{\prime}:=A-\alpha J
is monotone. Noting that
(\beta T)^{-1}=T^{-1}\circ\frac{1}{\beta}Id
for any operator
T
and any
\beta\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
, we have

\displaystyle J_{\gamma A}=(J+\gamma A)^{-1}\circ J

\displaystyle=((1+\gamma\alpha)J+\gamma(A-\alpha J))^{-1}\circ J

\displaystyle=\left(J+\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma\alpha}A^{\prime}\right)^{-1}\circ\left(\frac{1}{1+\gamma\alpha}J\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{1+\gamma\alpha}\right)J_{\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma\alpha}A^{\prime}}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\mathrm{dom}~J_{\gamma A}=X

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\mathrm{dom}~J_{\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma\alpha}A^{\prime}}=X

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow A^{\prime}\mathrm{~is~maximally~monotone~(by~Lemma~\ref{full domain})}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow A\mathrm{~is~maximally}~\alpha\mathrm{-monotone~(by~Lemma~\ref{mono})}.

∎

3.3.
Strong monotone operators under Definition
3.2
are dense in strong monotone operators under Definition
3.1

In this subsection, we show that the maximal strong monotone operators under new definition is dense in the maximal strong monotone operators under classical version under the following sense (see Theorem
3.20
). For sake of convenience, let
csm
be denoted by the set of maximal strong monotone operators under Definition
3.1
, and let
nsm
be denoted by the set of maximal strong monotone operators under Definition
3.2
, respectively. We always assume that the Banach space
X
is strict convex, smooth and reflexive in this subsection.

Proposition 3.18
.

Let
A\in\textbf{csm}
or
A\in\textbf{nsm}
, if
A^{-1}0\neq\emptyset
, then
A^{-1}0
is single point.

Proof.

Suppose that
A^{-1}0
contains more than one point. Let
x_{1}\neq x_{2}\in D(A)
, such that
x_{1},x_{2}\in A^{-1}0
. Then, if
A\in\textbf{csm}
or
A\in\textbf{nsm}
, we have
\langle 0-0,x_{1}-x_{2}\rangle\geq\alpha\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|^{2}>0
or
\langle 0-0,x_{1}-x_{2}\rangle\geq\alpha\langle x_{1}-x_{2}\textsterling\textlnot Jx_{1}-Jx_{2}\rangle>0
, which is a contradiction. Hence,
A^{-1}0
must be a single point.
∎

Lemma 3.19
.

Let
A\in\textbf{csm}
and
\alpha>0
, then
A+\alpha J\in\textbf{nsm}
, and
(A+\alpha J)^{-1}0
exists and is a single point.

Proof.

Let
A\in\textbf{csm}
and let
\alpha>0
. By Definition
3.2
, we know that
A+\alpha J\in\textbf{nsm}
. Therefore, if
(A+\alpha J)^{-1}0\neq\emptyset
, then
(A+\alpha J)^{-1}0
is a single point by Proposition
3.18
. Next, we show that
(A+\alpha J)^{-1}0\neq\emptyset
. Since
A+\alpha J=\alpha(\frac{1}{\alpha}A+J)
, and by Lemma
3.16
, we have
R(\frac{1}{\alpha}A+J)=X^{*}
. Hence
R(A+\alpha J)=X^{*}
, and consequently
(A+\alpha J)^{-1}0
exists.
∎

Theorem 3.20
.

Let
A\in\textbf{csm}
and
A^{-1}0\neq\emptyset
, in this case,
A^{-1}0
is single point, we denote
\{x^{*}\}=A^{-1}0
. Then there exist
\{A_{n}\}_{n}^{\infty}\subset\textbf{nsm}
such that
A_{n}^{-1}0\neq\emptyset
and it is single point. Furthermore, let
\{x_{n}\}=A_{n}^{-1}0
, then we have
\|x_{n}-x^{*}\|\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.

Proof.

Let
A_{n}=A+\alpha_{n}J
, where
\alpha_{n}>0
and
\alpha_{n}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. Then
A_{n}
is
\alpha_{n}
-monotone under Definition
3.2
.

By Lemma
3.19
, if
A\in\textbf{csm}
, then there exists
x_{n}
such that
0\in(A+\alpha_{n}J)x_{n}
. Next, we show that
x_{n}
is bounded. In fact, there exists
y_{n}\in Ax_{n}
such that
y_{n}=-\alpha_{n}Jx_{n}
. Let
x^{*}\in A^{-1}0
. Then
\langle x_{n}-x^{*},y_{n}-0\rangle\geq\alpha\|x_{n}-x^{*}\|^{2}\geq 0
, whici implies
\langle x_{n}-x^{*},-\alpha_{n}Jx_{n}\rangle\geq 0
. Hence
\langle x^{*},\alpha_{n}Jx_{n}\rangle\geq\langle x_{n},\alpha_{n}Jx_{n}\rangle=\alpha_{n}\|x_{n}\|^{2}
, and thus
\|x_{n}\|\leq\|x^{*}\|
. Therefore, the sequence
\{x_{n}\}
is bounded.

On the other hand, from
\langle x_{n}-x^{*},y_{n}-0\rangle\geq\alpha\|x_{n}-x^{*}\|^{2}
, we obtain
\|x_{n}-x^{*}\|\leq\frac{\alpha_{n}}{\alpha}\|x_{n}\|\leq\frac{\alpha_{n}}{\alpha}\|x^{*}\|\to 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
.
∎

3.4.
The class of nonlinear mappings related to maximal monotone operators in Banach spaces

In this subsection, we study some properties of
\sigma
-firmly nonexpansive type mappings. We first show that the class of
\sigma
-firmly nonexpansive type mappings coincides with that of resolvents of
\alpha
-monotone operators in Banach spaces.
\alpha
-monotonicity of an operator is defined as in Definition
3.2
, unless otherwise specified for Definition
3.1
.

Proposition 3.21
.

Let
X
be a smooth, strictly convex and reflexive Banach space, let
C
be a nonempty closed convex subset of
X
and let
T
be a mapping from
C
into itself. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)
T is of
(1+\alpha)
-firmly nonexpansive type;

(ii)
there exists a
\alpha
-monotone operator
A\subset X\times X^{*}
such that

(3.15)

\displaystyle D(A)\subset C\subset J^{-1}R(J+A)

and
Tx=(J+A)^{-1}Jx
for all
x\in C
.

Proof.

Suppose that there exists a
\alpha
-monotone operator
A\subset X\times X^{*}
such that
D(A)\subset C\subset J^{-1}R(J+A)
and
Tx=(J+A)^{-1}Jx
for all
x\in C
. Since
X
is smooth and strictly convex and reflexive, by Proposition
3.17
,
T
is single-valued. By
D(A)\subset C\subset J^{-1}R(J+A)
,
T
is a mapping from
C
into itself. Let
x,y\in C
. Then we have

Jx-JTx\in ATx
and
Jy-JTy\in ATy
. Since
A
is
\alpha
-monotone, we have

(3.16)

\displaystyle\langle Tx-Ty,Jx-JTx-(Jy-JTy)\rangle\geq\alpha\langle Tx-Ty,JTx-JTy\rangle.

Hence, we get
\langle Tx-Ty,Jx-Jy\rangle\geq(1+\alpha)\langle Tx-Ty,JTx-JTy\rangle
. This shows that
T
is of
(1+\alpha)
-firmly nonexpansive type.

We next show that (i) implies (ii). Suppose that
T
is of
(1+\alpha)
-firmly nonexpansive type. Let
A\subset X\times X^{*}
be the set-valued mapping defined by
A=JT^{-1}-J
, where
T^{-1}
is defined by

(3.17)

\displaystyle T^{-1}u=\begin{cases}\{v\in C:Tv=u\},&u\in R(T);\\
\emptyset,&\textrm{otherwise}.\end{cases}

It is obvious that
Tx=(J+A)^{-1}Jx
for all
x\in C
. We show that
A
is
\alpha
-monotone. Let
(x_{i},x_{i}^{*})\in A
be given
(i=1,2)
. Then we have

(3.18)

\displaystyle x_{i}^{*}\in Ax_{i}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow x_{i}^{*}\in JT^{-1}x_{i}-Jx_{i}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow x_{i}^{*}+Jx_{i}\in JT^{-1}x_{i}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow J^{-1}\left(x_{i}^{*}+Jx_{i}\right)\in T^{-1}x_{i}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow TJ^{-1}\left(x_{i}^{*}+Jx_{i}\right)=x_{i}

for
i=1,2
. Putting
u_{i}=J^{-1}(x_{i}^{*}+Jx_{i})
, we have

Tu_{i}=x_{i}
and
Ju_{i}-Jx_{i}=x_{i}^{*}~~(i=1,2)
. Since
T
is of
(1+\alpha)
-firmly nonexpansive type, we have

(3.19)

\displaystyle\left<x_{1}-x_{2},x_{1}^{*}-x_{2}^{*}\right>

\displaystyle=\left<Tu_{1}-Tu_{2},x_{1}^{*}-x_{2}^{*}\right>

\displaystyle=\left<Tu_{1}-Tu_{2},Ju_{1}-Jx_{1}-\left(Ju_{2}-Jx_{2}\right)\right>

\displaystyle=\left<Tu_{1}-Tu_{2},Ju_{1}-JTu_{1}-\left(Ju_{2}-JTu_{2}\right)\right>

\displaystyle\geq(1+\alpha-\alpha)\left<Tu_{1}-Tu_{2},JTu_{1}-JTu_{2}\right>

\displaystyle\geq\alpha\left<x_{1}-x_{2},Jx_{1}-Jx_{2}\right>.

Thus
A
is
\alpha
-monotone. We finally show that
D(A)\subset C\subset J^{-1}R(J+A)
. It is easy to see that
D(A)=D(JT^{-1}-J)=D(T^{-1})=R(T)\subset C
. Since
J+A=JT^{-1}
, we also have

(3.20)

\displaystyle R(J+A)=R\left(JT^{-1}\right)=D\left(\left(JT^{-1}\right)^{-1}\right)=D\left(TJ^{-1}\right)=JD(T)=JC.

Thus
C=J^{-1}R(J+A)
. This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.22
.

Let
X
be a smooth Banach space, let
C
be a nonempty closed convex subset of
X
, and let
T:C\to C
be a mapping. Then
T
is of
\sigma
-firmly nonexpansive type if and only if

(3.21)

\displaystyle\sigma\left(\phi(Tx,Ty)+\phi(Ty,Tx)\right)+\phi(Tx,x)+\phi(Ty,y)\leq\phi(Tx,y)+\phi(Ty,x)

for all
x,y\in C
.

Proof.

Let
x,y\in C
. Since
T
is of
\sigma
-firmly nonexpansive type,

(3.22)

\displaystyle\sigma\langle Tx-Ty,JTx-JTy\rangle\leq\langle Tx-Ty,Jx-Jy\rangle.

It follows from Lemma (
2.1
)
(iii)
that (
3.22
) is equivalent to

(3.23)

\displaystyle\sigma\frac{1}{2}\{\phi(Tx,Ty)+\phi(Ty,Tx)-\phi(Tx,Tx)-\phi(Ty,Ty)\}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\{\phi(Tx,y)+\phi(Ty,x)-\phi(Tx,x)-\phi(Ty,y)\}.

This is also equivalent to (
3.21
). This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 3.23
.

Let
X
be strict convex and smooth and reflexive Banach space, let
C
be a nonempty closed convex subset of
X
, and let
\sigma>1
,
T:C\to C
be a
\sigma
-firmly nonexpansive-type mapping such that

is nonempty. Then
F(T)
is single point and

converge strongly to fixed point of

for any

. If

is
2
-uniformly convex, then there is a constant
\mu
such that
\|u-T^{n}x\|^{2}\leq\frac{\mu}{\sigma^{n}}\phi(u,x)
.

Proof.

Let
(x,u)\in C\times F(T)
be given. Then it follows from Lemma
3.22
that

(3.24)

\displaystyle\sigma\left(\phi(Tx,Ty)+\phi(Ty,Tx)\right)+\phi(Tx,x)+\phi(Ty,y)\leq\phi(Tx,y)+\phi(Ty,x),

which implies that

(3.25)

\displaystyle\sigma\phi(u,Tx)\leq\phi(u,x).

Hence,
\sigma^{n}\phi(u,T^{n}x)\leq\phi(u,x)
. Then
\|u-T^{n}x\|\to 0
follows from
\phi(u,T^{n}x)\to 0
. If

is
2
-uniformly convex, then there is a constant
\mu
such that
\frac{1}{\mu}\|u-T^{n}x\|^{2}\leq\phi(u,T^{n}x)

.
∎

According to Proposition

remains valid for the resolvent

Remark 3.6

Remark 3.7

A:H\rightarrow 2^{H}
is maximally monotone and
B:H\rightarrow H
is monotone and
L
-Lipschitz continuous for some
L>0
. The forward-reflected-backward splitting algorithm is defined as follows:

x_{n+1}=J_{\lambda_{n}A}(x_{n}-\lambda_{n}Bx_{n}-\lambda_{n-1}(Bx_{n}-Bx_{n-1})),

where
\{\lambda_{n}\}\subseteq\left[\epsilon,\tfrac{1-2\epsilon}{2L}\right]
for some
\epsilon>0
. They not only proved the weak convergence of the algorithm under general conditions but also established its strong convergence under the strong monotonicity assumption, and rigorously demonstrated that the algorithm achieves an R-linear rate of convergence. Furthermore, Bello et al.
[
35
]
extended the forward-reflected-backward splitting algorithm from Hilbert spaces
H
to general Banach spaces
X
, and proved weak convergence of the generated sequence in real 2-uniformly convex and uniformly smooth Banach spaces. However, in general Banach spaces, the strong convergence and R-linear convergence of the forward-reflected-backward splitting algorithm have not been established.
The main difficulties arises from the inadequacy of the standard definition of strong monotonicity for operators in Banach space frameworks. In this section, we establish strong convergence with an
R
-linear rate for the forward-reflected-backward splitting algorithm in real 2-uniformly convex and uniformly smooth Banach spaces under Definition
3.2
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
X
be a real 2-uniformly convex and uniformly smooth Banach space. Let
A:X\to 2^{X^{*}}
be a maximally monotone and
\alpha
-monotone operator and
B:X\to X^{*}
be
\beta
-monotone and Lipschitz,
\alpha+\beta>0
. For
x_{0},x_{-1}\in X
defined sequence
x_{n}
by

(4.1)

\displaystyle x_{n+1}=J_{\lambda_{n}}^{A}\circ J^{-1}\left(Jx_{n}-\lambda_{n}Bx_{n}-\lambda_{n-1}\left(Bx_{n}-Bx_{n-1}\right)\right),~n\geq 0,

where
\lambda_{n}\subseteq[\epsilon,\frac{1-2\epsilon}{2\mu L}]
for some
\alpha+\beta>\epsilon>0
and
\mu\geq 1
. Suppose
(A+B)^{-1}(0)\neq\emptyset
, then the sequence
{x_{n}}
converges strongly to an element of
(A+B)^{-1}(0)
with R-linear rate.

Proof.

Let
x^{*}\in(A+B)^{-1}(0)
. So that

(4.2)

\displaystyle-Bx^{*}\in Ax^{*}.

From (
4.1
) we have that

(4.3)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}}\left(Jx_{n}-\lambda_{n}Bx_{n}-\lambda_{n-1}\left(Bx_{n}-Bx_{n-1}\right)-Jx_{n+1}\right)\in Ax_{n+1}

Using (
4.2
), (
4.3
) and the strong monotonicity of
A
, we obtain

(4.4)

\displaystyle\left<Jx_{n+1}-Jx_{n}+\lambda_{n}\left(Bx_{n}-Bx^{*}\right)+\lambda_{n-1}\left(Bx_{n}-Bx_{n-1}\right),x^{*}-x_{n+1}\right>

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\alpha\langle x^{*}-x_{n+1},Jx^{*}-Jx_{n+1}\rangle

By Lemma
2.1
(1), we have

(4.5)

\displaystyle 2\left<Jx_{n+1}-Jx_{n},x^{*}-x_{n+1}\right>=\phi\left(x^{*},x_{n}\right)-\phi\left(x^{*},x_{n+1}\right)-\phi\left(x_{n+1},x_{n}\right).

Also

(4.6)

\displaystyle\left<Bx_{n}-Bx^{*},x^{*}-x_{n+1}\right>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left<Bx_{n+1}-Bx^{*},x^{*}-x_{n+1}\right>+\left<Bx_{n}-Bx_{n+1},x^{*}-x_{n+1}\right>.

And

(4.7)

\displaystyle\left<Bx_{n}-Bx_{n-1},x^{*}-x_{n+1}\right>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left<Bx_{n}-Bx_{n-1},x^{*}-x_{n}\right>+\left<Bx_{n}-Bx_{n-1},x_{n}-x_{n+1}\right>.

Notice that
B
is
\beta
-monotone, we have

(4.8)

\displaystyle\left<Bx_{n+1}-Bx^{*},x^{*}-x_{n+1}\right>\leq-\beta\left<x^{*}-x_{n+1},Jx^{*}-Jx_{n+1}\right>.

(4.9)

\displaystyle(\alpha+\beta)\langle x^{*}-x_{n+1},Jx^{*}-Jx_{n+1}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}\phi(x^{*},x_{n+1})+\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}\phi(x^{*},x_{n+1})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}\phi(x^{*},x_{n+1}).

Substituting (
4.5
), (
4.6
) and (
4.7
) in (
4.4
), and since (
4.8
) (
4.9
), we have:

(4.10)

\displaystyle(1+\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2})\phi\left(x^{*},x_{n+1}\right)+2\lambda_{n}\left<Bx_{n+1}-Bx_{n},x^{*}-x_{n+1}\right>

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\phi\left(x^{*},x_{n}\right)+2\lambda_{n-1}\left<Bx_{n}-Bx_{n-1},x^{*}-x_{n}\right>

\displaystyle+2\lambda_{n-1}\left<Bx_{n}-Bx_{n-1},x_{n}-x_{n+1}\right>-\phi\left(x_{n+1},x_{n}\right).

Rearranging the equation we have

(4.11)

\displaystyle(1+\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2})\phi\left(x^{*},x_{n+1}\right)+2\lambda_{n}\left<Bx_{n+1}-Bx_{n},x^{*}-x_{n+1}\right>+\phi\left(x_{n+1},x_{n}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\phi\left(x^{*},x_{n}\right)+2\lambda_{n-1}\left<Bx_{n}-Bx_{n-1},x^{*}-x_{n}\right>+2\lambda_{n-1}\left<Bx_{n}-Bx_{n-1},x_{n}-x_{n+1}\right>

Using the Lipschitz property of
B
and Lemma
2.3
we have

(4.12)

\displaystyle 2\lambda_{n-1}\left<Bx_{n}-Bx_{n-1},x_{n}-x_{n+1}\right>

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\lambda_{n-1}\left\|Bx_{n}-Bx_{n-1}\right\|\left\|x_{n}-x_{n+1}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\lambda_{n-1}L\left\|x_{n}-x_{n-1}\right\|\left\|x_{n}-x_{n+1}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lambda_{n-1}L\left(\left\|x_{n}-x_{n-1}\right\|^{2}+\left\|x_{n}-x_{n+1}\right\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lambda_{n-1}\mu L\left(\phi\left(x_{n},x_{n-1}\right)+\phi\left(x_{n+1},x_{n}\right)\right).

Substituting (
4.12
) in (
4.11
) we have

(4.13)

\displaystyle(1+\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2})\phi\left(x^{*},x_{n+1}\right)+2\lambda_{n}\left<Bx_{n+1}-Bx_{n},x^{*}-x_{n+1}\right>

\displaystyle+\left(1-\lambda_{n-1}\mu L\right)\phi\left(x_{n+1},x_{n}\right)\leq\phi\left(x^{*},x_{n}\right)

\displaystyle+2\lambda_{n-1}\left<Bx_{n}-Bx_{n-1},x^{*}-x_{n}\right>+\lambda_{n-1}\mu L\phi\left(x_{n},x_{n-1}\right).

Now,
\lambda_{n}\in[\epsilon,\frac{1-2\epsilon}{2\mu L}]

\lambda_{n}\mu L<\frac{1}{2}
and
\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon\leq 1-\lambda_{n}\mu L
. So that (

(4.14)

\displaystyle(1+\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2})\phi\left(x^{*},x_{n+1}\right)+2\lambda_{n}\left<Bx_{n+1}-Bx_{n},x^{*}-x_{n+1}\right>

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon\right)\phi\left(x_{n+1},x_{n}\right)\leq\phi\left(x^{*},x_{n}\right)

\displaystyle+2\lambda_{n-1}\left<Bx_{n}-Bx_{n-1},x^{*}-x_{n}\right>+\frac{1}{2}\phi\left(x_{n},x_{n-1}\right)

Let
a_{n+1}=\frac{1}{2}\phi(x^{*},x_{n+1})
,
b_{n+1}=\frac{1}{2}\phi(x^{*},x_{n+1})+2\lambda_{n}\langle Bx_{n+1}-Bx_{n},x^{*}-x_{n+1}\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\phi(x_{n+1},x_{n})
.
Then we have

b_{n+1}\geq\left(\frac{1}{2}-\lambda_{n}\mu L\right)\phi(x^{*},x_{n+1})+\left(\frac{1}{2}-\lambda_{n}\mu L\right)\phi(x_{n+1},x_{n})\geq 0.

And

\displaystyle(1+\alpha+\beta)a_{n+1}+b_{n+1}+\varepsilon\phi(x_{n+1},x_{n})\leq a_{n}+b_{n}.

Since

\displaystyle b_{n+1}\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{2}+\lambda_{n}\mu L\right)\phi(x^{*},x_{n+1})+\left(\frac{1}{2}+\lambda_{n}\mu L\right)\phi(x_{n+1},x_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1+2\lambda_{n}\mu L)\cdot\frac{1}{2}\phi(x^{*},x_{n+1})+\left(\frac{1}{2}+\lambda_{n}\mu L\right)\phi(x_{n+1},x_{n})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1+2\lambda_{n}\mu L)a_{n+1}+\phi(x_{n+1},x_{n}).

Then by (

(4.17)

\displaystyle(1+\alpha+\beta)a_{n+1}+b_{n+1}+\varepsilon\phi(x_{n+1},x_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1+\alpha+\beta)a_{n+1}+(1+\frac{\epsilon}{2})b_{n+1}-\frac{\epsilon}{2}b_{n+1}+\varepsilon\phi(x_{n+1},x_{n})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(1+\alpha+\beta-\frac{\epsilon}{2}(1+2\lambda_{n}\mu L))a_{n+1}+(1+\frac{\epsilon}{2})b_{n+1}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(1+\alpha+\beta-\epsilon)a_{n+1}+(1+\frac{\epsilon}{2})b_{n+1}.

Then, let

,
then, by (

\displaystyle\theta(a_{n+1}+b_{n+1})\leq a_{n}+b_{n},

which establishes that

with

by Lemma

.

Corollary 4.2
.

x_{0},x_{-1}\in X

.

5.
Conclusions

In Hilbert spaces, the properties of the resolvent operator associated with
\alpha
-monotone operators have been extensively studied. In general Banach spaces, based on Definition
3.1
, when
\alpha=0
, the resolvent of the corresponding monotone operator has been proven to be firmly nonexpansive type. However, when
\alpha\neq 0
, it becomes considerably more difficult to establish the properties of the resolvent operator in Banach spaces directly from the
\alpha
-monotonicity given by Definition
3.1
. To address this issue, this paper introduced a new definition of
\alpha
-monotonicity in smooth Banach spaces (Definition
3.2
). We compared and analyzed the relationship between this new definition and Definition
3.1
, and then systematically proved the properties of the resolvent operator under the new definition. These properties are consistent with the existing results in the Hilbert space setting, thereby demonstrating the reasonableness and generalizability of the new definition. Furthermore, we employed the newly proposed
\alpha
-monotonicity assumption to operator splitting methods and proved that the forward-reflected-backward splitting algorithm, under suitable parameter conditions, not only achieves strong convergence but also attains
R
-linear convergence. This provides a new theoretical foundation for the study of
\alpha
-monotone operators and the convergence analysis of related numerical algorithms in general Banach spaces.

In future work, the proposed definition of
\alpha
-monotonicity can be employed to investigate the strong convergence and R-linear convergence rate of other operator splitting algorithms in general Banach spaces, such as the forward-backward-half forward splitting algorithm
[
36
]
, the semi-reflected-forward-backward splitting algorithm
[
37
]
, and the outer reflected forward-backward splitting algorithm
[
38
]
, etc.

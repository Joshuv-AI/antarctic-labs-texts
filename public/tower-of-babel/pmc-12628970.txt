Cannabidiol in pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma: preclinical evidence, molecular mechanisms, and translational challenges
Authors: Mojtaba Esmaeli, Maryam Dehghanpour Dehabadi, Ali Asghar Khaleghi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) remains one of the deadliest and most treatment-resistant cancers, with limited progress in improving patient survival. Cannabidiol (CBD), a non-psychoactive phytocannabinoid, has emerged as a potential anticancer agent due to its diverse molecular effects in preclinical cancer models.ObjectiveThis systematic review aims to evaluate the preclinical and early clinical evidence regarding CBD’s anticancer effects in PDAC, with emphasis on its molecular mechanisms, therapeutic synergies, and translational feasibility.MethodsWe systematically searched PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science, EMBASE, and Google Scholar (2006–2025) for studies examining the effects of CBD on PDAC in vitro, in vivo, and in clinical contexts. Studies were assessed for methodological quality and relevance to CBD-related antitumor activity.ResultsFifteen studies met the inclusion criteria, including in vitro, animal, and limited clinical investigations. CBD exhibited antitumor properties via multiple pathways, such as CerS1-mediated ER stress and apoptosis, GPR55/MAPK inhibition, immune modulation, and chemosensitization to gemcitabine. Combination therapies (CBD with cannabinoids or chemotherapeutics) enhanced therapeutic outcomes in preclinical models. However, clinical evidence remains preliminary and insufficient to establish efficacy.ConclusionCBD demonstrates promising anticancer potential in PDAC through diverse molecular mechanisms and synergistic effects with chemotherapy. Nonetheless, significant translational barriers—including formulation variability, pharmacokinetics, and a lack of clinical trials—must be addressed. Further studies are warranted to validate these findings in human settings.

Introduction

Pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) remains a highly aggressive and lethal malignancy, with a five-year survival rate of approximately 13% and a one-year survival rate around 22% according to the most recent Cancer Statistics 2025 report [1]. PDAC is currently the third leading cause of cancer-related mortality and is projected to become the second by 2030. The dismal prognosis is largely attributed to late-stage diagnosis, rapid metastatic progression, and resistance to standard therapies such as gemcitabine-based chemotherapy [2]. These challenges underscore the critical need for innovative therapeutic approaches that can overcome tumor resistance and improve clinical outcomes in PDAC. Among emerging strategies, phytocannabinoids have attracted increasing attention in oncology due to their anti-inflammatory, immunomodulatory, and pro-apoptotic properties [3]. Cannabidiol (CBD), a non-psychoactive compound derived from Cannabis sativa, exhibits a favorable safety profile and multiple mechanisms of action that may be therapeutically relevant in cancer, including PDAC [3]. Notably, pancreatic tumor tissues have been shown to express cannabinoid receptors CB1 and CB2, suggesting a potential molecular target for cannabinoid-based interventions [4].

Several preclinical studies have reported that CBD exerts potent anticancer effects through diverse mechanisms. One key pathway involves the upregulation of ceramide synthase 1 (CerS1), leading to ceramide accumulation, endoplasmic reticulum (ER) stress, and activation of the GRP78/ATF4/CHOP apoptotic cascade [5]. In addition, CBD modulates G protein-coupled receptor 55 (GPR55), a non-classical cannabinoid receptor associated with cell proliferation and metastasis in gastrointestinal malignancies [6]. By antagonizing GPR55, CBD disrupts downstream MAPK/ERK signaling and inhibits tumor growth [6].

Of particular interest is the reported synergy between CBD and standard chemotherapeutic agents such as gemcitabine. In murine models of PDAC, combination therapy has resulted in enhanced cytotoxicity, reduced tumor growth, and delayed chemoresistance [7]. Furthermore, co-administration of CBD with other cannabinoids, such as cannabichromene (CBC), may enhance therapeutic efficacy by modulating multiple molecular targets simultaneously [8].

Beyond its direct cytotoxicity, CBD influences the tumor microenvironment (TME) by reducing pro-inflammatory cytokines and modulating immune responses [9]. This is exemplified by novel cannabinoid derivatives like FBL-03G, which induce apoptosis in both primary and metastatic PDAC cells, with evidence of immune system engagement [10, 11].

Despite these promising preclinical findings, translation of CBD-based therapies into clinical oncology remains limited. Challenges include a paucity of randomized clinical trials, variability in cannabinoid formulations, inconsistent pharmacokinetics, and regulatory barriers. Nevertheless, the expanding body of experimental evidence supports further investigation of CBD as a potential adjunctive therapy in pancreatic cancer.

This review aims to systematically evaluate the current preclinical and early clinical evidence on the anticancer effects of CBD in PDAC, with a focus on elucidating its molecular mechanisms, assessing methodological quality, and identifying translational barriers such as pharmacokinetics, formulation variability, and clinical feasibility.

Materials and methods

Search strategy

A comprehensive literature search was conducted across five major databases: PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science, Google Scholar, and EMBASE, to identify relevant studies evaluating the effects of cannabidiol (CBD) on pancreatic cancer. The search was restricted to English-language publications from January 2006 to February 2025.

Search terms included combinations of Medical Subject Headings (MeSH) and free-text keywords:

“cannabidiol” OR “CBD” AND “pancreatic cancer” OR “pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma” OR “PDAC” AND “antitumor” OR “cytotoxic” OR “apoptosis” OR “mechanism” OR “GPR55” OR “ceramide” OR “chemosensitization”.

The search was supplemented by manual screening of references from relevant reviews and original studies.

Quality assessment

To ensure methodological rigor, the risk of bias for each included study was assessed using standardized domain-based tools appropriate for the study type. The evaluation considered core parameters such as randomization, allocation concealment, blinding of investigators and outcome assessors, sample size justification, and completeness of outcome reporting. Studies were classified as low risk when most criteria were adequately met, moderate risk when one or more criteria were unclear or partially fulfilled, and high risk when major methodological flaws or omissions were identified. These definitions followed the general recommendations of the SYRCLE, ToxRTool, and JBI critical appraisal frameworks.

All included studies were evaluated for methodological quality using appropriate tools based on their design. In vivo studies were assessed with the SYRCLE risk of bias tool, in vitro experiments with the ToxRTool framework, and clinical studies with the Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) critical appraisal tools.

Common limitations across studies included small sample sizes, variability in CBD dosing and formulation, lack of pharmacokinetic assessment, and absence of control or comparator arms in clinical data. These findings underscore the need for better-standardized and clinically translatable study designs in future CBD research in pancreatic cancer. Results

Overview of included studies

A total of fifteen studies [4, 7, 12–24] were included in this systematic review, encompassing in vitro, in vivo, and limited clinical analyses of cannabidiol (CBD) in pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC). Seven studies were purely in vitro, six in vivo, and three were clinical or observational. The research collectively explored CBD either as monotherapy or in combination with other agents such as gemcitabine, melatonin, oxygen–ozone, or other cannabinoids (THC, CBC). In vitro findings

In vitro data consistently confirmed that CBD exerts dose-dependent cytotoxicity across PDAC cell lines, including PANC-1, MIA PaCa-2, Capan-2, AsPC-1, and CFPAC-I [12–18].

Apoptotic and ER stress mechanisms

CBD markedly increased Ceramide Synthase 1 (CerS1) expression, resulting in ceramide accumulation and endoplasmic reticulum (ER) stress with GRP78/ATF4/CHOP activation [12, 18]. This cascade led to caspase-3-mediated apoptosis. Dando et al. [13] further demonstrated that CBD-induced oxidative stress and mitochondrial depolarization augment this apoptotic response, consistent with a ROS-dependent cytotoxic mechanism.

Metabolic and proliferative Inhibition

CBD disrupted energy metabolism via AMPK activation, inhibiting glycolysis and glutamine utilization [13]. Several studies reported that CBD inhibited proliferation through GPR55 antagonism, leading to downstream suppression of MAPK/ERK signaling [15, 18]. This dual action—on metabolism and signal transduction—suggests a multi-faceted tumor-suppressive effect.

Synergistic and combinatorial cytotoxicity

Luongo et al. [14] revealed that combining CBD with oxygen–ozone therapy enhanced apoptosis and reduced PDAC cell migration. Similarly, Yang et al. [15] demonstrated synergistic inhibition of proliferation when CBD was combined with THC, attributed to suppression of PAK1 signaling. Garofano et al. [17] showed that even low-dose CBD (1 µM) enhanced the cytotoxic function of cytokine-induced killer (CIK) cells against PANC-1 without causing toxicity to effector cells, underscoring CBD’s potential as an immunomodulatory adjunct.

Collectively, in vitro data highlight that CBD acts through interconnected molecular axes—ER stress, oxidative stress, metabolic disruption, and immune enhancement—to inhibit PDAC cell growth and survival.

In vivo findings

Animal models further corroborated these mechanisms. Xenograft and orthotopic models using Capan-2 and MIA PaCa-2 cell lines consistently showed reduced tumor volume, increased apoptosis, and modulation of proliferative markers following CBD or CBD: THC administration [19–21].

Antiproliferative and apoptotic outcomes: Sakarin et al. [19] and Suwanpidokkul et al. [20] demonstrated a dose-dependent reduction in Ki-67 and PCNA expression, with upregulation of caspase-3 and Bax/Bcl-2 ratio, indicating intrinsic apoptotic pathway activation. Le et al. [21] confirmed these findings and showed that CBD: THC (1:6) formulations produced strong apoptotic indices without toxicity to major organs.

Combination therapies

Studies combining CBD with melatonin and oxygen–ozone [22] or with gemcitabine [7, 14, 18] revealed synergistic tumor suppression, characterized by elevated ROS production and NF-κB inhibition. Such combinations improved chemotherapy response and delayed chemoresistance.

Immune and microenvironmental modulation

PD-L1 downregulation [15, 17] and reduced pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-6, TNF-α) were observed in several models, suggesting an immunomodulatory role for CBD in reshaping the tumor microenvironment (TME). The integration of immune and apoptotic effects positions CBD as both a direct cytotoxic and indirect immunoregulatory compound in PDAC.

Clinical findings

Clinical evidence remains preliminary but suggestive. Retrospective analyses [4, 23] found correlations between CB1/CB2 receptor expression and survival, with high CB2 and OGFR expression linked to improved prognosis. Vecera et al. [23] observed that morphine-based analgesia combined with higher CB2 expression was associated with prolonged cancer-specific survival. However, clinical trials evaluating CBD as a direct therapeutic agent are lacking. Current human data primarily emphasize symptomatic benefits—pain relief, appetite improvement, and better quality of life—rather than objective tumor regression.

Discussion

Consolidated interpretation of findings

This systematic review consolidates evidence from fifteen preclinical and limited clinical studies [4, 7, 12–24], highlighting the multifaceted anticancer properties of cannabidiol (CBD) in pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC). The most consistent molecular signature involves upregulation of CerS1, resulting in ceramide accumulation, ER stress, and apoptotic activation [12, 18]. Inhibition of GPR55 and downstream MAPK/ERK signaling contributes to anti-proliferative effects [15, 18], while AMPK activation disrupts metabolic integrity [13] (Fig. 2). Integrative context with conventional PDAC therapies

Gemcitabine, the current standard of care for PDAC, achieves modest benefits due to rapid development of chemoresistance. Several studies [7, 14, 18, 22, 23] show that CBD enhances gemcitabine efficacy through ROS-mediated autophagy and NF-κB inhibition. This mechanism parallels the emerging use of oxidative stress inducers, such as PARP inhibitors or arsenic trioxide, which aim to destabilize tumor redox balance. Moreover, CBD’s ability to modulate PD-L1 expression [15, 17] provides a conceptual bridge toward immunotherapy integration. Unlike immune checkpoint inhibitors that rely on pre-existing immune infiltration, CBD may prime the tumor microenvironment by reducing inflammation and enhancing antigen presentation, potentially improving responses to PD-1/PD-L1 blockade.

Comparative mechanistic insight and broader oncologic relevance

Beyond PDAC, CBD’s mechanistic repertoire mirrors key anticancer pathways seen in other tumor types. For example, ER stress induction via the CHOP pathway and AMPK activation have been implicated in glioblastoma, colorectal, and breast cancers [3, 9, 10]. The reproducibility of these signaling patterns across malignancies suggests that CBD engages universal stress and apoptosis pathways rather than tissue-specific targets. Within PDAC, where KRAS mutations dominate oncogenic signaling, CBD’s inhibition of downstream PAK1 and MAPK [15] introduces a potential complementary mechanism to KRAS-targeted drugs such as adagrasib. This cross-pathway modulation underscores CBD’s potential as a synergistic adjunct rather than a standalone therapy.

Translational implications and pharmacologic considerations

Despite encouraging preclinical outcomes, translation to clinical practice remains constrained. Variability in CBD purity, formulation, and administration route (oral vs. intraperitoneal) leads to inconsistent pharmacokinetic profiles [13, 19–21]. Furthermore, the lipophilic nature of CBD limits its systemic bioavailability, complicating dose-response relationships. To move toward clinical validation, pharmaceutical-grade formulations with controlled release and nanocarrier systems may be required. Early pharmacodynamic studies should focus on quantifying CerS1 activation and GPR55 inhibition as surrogate biomarkers for biological activity.

Limitations and methodological gaps

Several methodological shortcomings persist across studies: limited sample sizes, single-cell-line dependency, and absence of blinding or randomization in animal models. Heterogeneity in outcome measures—ranging from apoptosis rates to metabolic markers—also hampers meta-analytical synthesis. While most findings align mechanistically, the degree of effect varies depending on CBD concentration, exposure time, and combinatorial agents. Bridging these inconsistencies demands standardized experimental frameworks and reporting guidelines similar to ARRIVE and PRISMA extensions for preclinical work.

Concluding analytical perspective

Collectively, the preclinical body of evidence supports CBD as a multi-targeted modulator of apoptosis, metabolism, and immune dynamics in PDAC. Its ability to enhance chemotherapy response, disrupt proliferative signaling, and remodel the tumor microenvironment positions CBD as a promising adjuvant candidate. However, the absence of randomized clinical data precludes definitive therapeutic endorsement. Addressing formulation consistency, pharmacokinetic limitations, and biomarker-guided patient selection will be essential for translating preclinical promise into clinical efficacy.

Conclusion

This systematic review provides a comprehensive synthesis of current evidence supporting cannabidiol (CBD) as a multifactorial anticancer agent in pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC). Preclinical studies consistently demonstrate that CBD induces apoptosis via CerS1-mediated ER stress, suppresses proliferation through GPR55/PAK1 inhibition, reprograms metabolism via AMPK activation, and enhances chemosensitivity to gemcitabine. Despite this strong experimental foundation, translation into clinical practice remains premature due to pharmacokinetic variability and lack of controlled human trials. Future clinical studies should prioritize standardized formulations, pharmacodynamic validation of mechanistic biomarkers, and rational combination strategies with established therapies. Only through such rigorous translational efforts can CBD’s therapeutic potential in PDAC be accurately defined and clinically realized.

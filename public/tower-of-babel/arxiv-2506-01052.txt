A Finite-Time Analysis of TD Learning with Linear Function Approximation without Projections nor Strong Convexity
Wei-Cheng Lee, Francesco Orabona

Abstract
We investigate the finite-time convergence properties of Temporal Difference (TD) learning with linear function approximation, a cornerstone algorithm in reinforcement learning. While prior work has established convergence guarantees, these results typically rely on the assumption that each iterate is projected onto a bounded set or that the learning rate is set according to the unknown strong convexity constant—conditions that are both artificial and do not match the current practice. In this paper, we challenge the necessity of such assumptions and present a refined analysis of TD learning. We show that the simple projection-free variant converges with a rate of 𝒪 ~ ​ ( ‖ 𝜽 ∗ ‖ 2 2 T ) \tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\frac{\left\|{\bm{\theta}^{*}}\right\|^{2}_{2}}{\sqrt{T}}) , even in the presence of Markovian noise. Our analysis reveals a novel self-bounding property of the TD updates and exploits it to guarantee bounded iterates.



1
Introduction

Temporal Difference (TD) learning, first introduced by
Sutton 1988
, is a cornerstone of modern reinforcement learning. It provides a model-free approach for policy evaluation, estimating the value function of a given policy within a Markov Decision Process (MDP). The versatility of TD methods has led to their application in diverse domains, including games
(
Silver et al. 2016
)
, robotics
(
Gu et al. 2017
)
, and autonomous systems
(
Chen et al. 2015
)
. At its core, TD learning iteratively updates value function estimates based on the difference between predictions at successive time steps.

Despite its conceptual simplicity and widespread use, the theoretical analysis of TD learning, particularly with linear function approximation in large state spaces, presents considerable challenges. Early seminal work by
Tsitsiklis and Van Roy 1996
established asymptotic convergence conditions by framing TD as a stochastic approximation algorithm. Instead, understanding the non-asymptotic behavior and finite-time performance of TD has been an active area of research more recently. Challenges primarily arise from the correlated nature of samples generated by the underlying Markov chain, which can introduce bias and dependencies into the learning updates.

Several studies have provided finite-time analyses under various assumptions. For instance, analyses assuming i.i.d. samples
(
Dalal et al. 2018
;
Lakshminarayanan and Szepesvari 2018
)
offered initial insights but did not fully capture the complexities of Markovian sampling. There are also efforts to analyze TD under Markovian noise. For example,
Bhandari et al. 2018
provided a non-asymptotic analysis for TD with linear function approximation, but their results for the Markovian setting relied on projecting iterates onto a compact set. This projection step, while common in stochastic approximation for ensuring stability and simplifying analysis
(
Kushner 2010
)
, can be a limitation as it modifies the standard algorithm and may not always be practical or desired. Instead,
Srikant and Ying 2019
;
Mitra 2024
analyzed TD with a constant step-size without projections, but implicitly assuming knowledge of the strong convexity parameter of the objective function.

Given that the TD algorithm is routinely used without such restrictions, one might wonder if the current analyses fail to fully grasp the nature of the TD update.

Contributions.

In this paper, for the first time we provide a finite-time analysis of TD(0) with linear function approximation under Markovian observations
without the requirement of iterate projection
. Our main contribution is to demonstrate that learning rates of the form
\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}\ln^{2}T}
are sufficient to guarantee a self-bounding property of TD: The iterate are constrained in expectation in a bounded domain around the optimal solution. Our analysis is fundamentally different from previous ones that aimed at proving that the update is a noisy contraction.
We also show a convergence rate
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\frac{\left\|{\bm{\theta}^{*}}\right\|^{2}}{\sqrt{T}})
for the objective function implicitly optimized by the TD algorithm, as defined in
Liu and Olshevsky 2021
.

2
Related Work

The initial theoretical understanding of how TD learning with linear function approximation converges over time was established in
Tsitsiklis and Van Roy 1996
, which framed TD methods as types of stochastic approximation algorithms
(
Kushner 2010
)
. However, this early work did not derive convergence rates in finite time. Subsequent research
(
Korda and La 2015
;
Lakshminarayanan and Szepesvari 2018
;
Dalal et al. 2018
)
did provide these finite-time convergence rates. However, a significant limitation of these later studies was their reliance on the assumption that the data are drawn independently from the stationary distribution. In practice, data is usually collected sequentially from a single path through the Markov chain, introducing time-based correlations between samples. These correlations make analyzing even the basic TD(0) method challenging.

Bhandari et al. 2018
offered the first finite-time analysis for TD learning using this more realistic Markovian (sequentially correlated) data, by drawing parallels to stochastic gradient descent. However, this analysis required a projection step in the algorithm to control the magnitude of the estimates of updates.

Srikant and Ying 2019
was reportedly the first to provide finite-time mean-square error bounds for TD learning with linear function approximation under Markovian sampling without needing a projection step. This was achieved using a control-theoretic approach based on Lyapunov theory. While elegant, the analysis in
Srikant and Ying 2019
relies on assuming a minimal curvature of the objective function, that might make the rate arbitrarily slow. A similar proof analysis with similar shortcomings was also done in
Mitra 2024
. We discuss more in details these methods in Section
5
.

We study the objective function proposed in
Liu and Olshevsky 2021
, that extends the one used in prior work by adding a term that is proportional to the discount factor
\gamma
. Note that
Liu and Olshevsky 2021
also provide finite-time convergence guarantees, but they also need a projection step to control the norm of the iterates.

Our proof method is fundamentally different from previous ones, that aimed at establishing a contraction. Instead, we prove that the iterates are bounded because similar to the ones of SGD. In turn, SGD can have bounded iterates even for non-strongly convex objectives, as proved in, for example,
Xiao 2010
;
Orabona and Pál 2021
;
Ivgi et al. 2023
for various update schemes and assumptions on the objective functions.

3
Notation and Assumptions

This section reviews the required background on Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) and on temporal-difference learning with linear function approximation. For a comprehensive treatment of these topics, the reader may consult
Mannor et al. 2022
.

3.1
Discounted Markov Decision Processes

A
discounted-reward MDP
is a five-tuple

(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{A},\bm{P},r,\gamma),

where

•

\mathcal{S}=[n]\!:=\{1,2,\dots,n\}
is a finite state space,

•

\mathcal{A}
is a finite action space,

•

P(s^{\prime}\mid s,a)
denotes the transition probability from state
s
to state
s^{\prime}
under action
a
,

•

r(s,a,s^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is a deterministic reward, and

•

\gamma\in(0,1)
is the discount factor.

A major goal of reinforcement learning is to find a policy that maximizes the expected discounted sum of rewards,
\bm{V}
.

Policies and value functions.

A
stationary policy
is a mapping

\mu:\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}\rightarrow[0,1]

with
\sum_{a}\mu(s,a)=1
for every state
s
.
The value
function associated with
\mu
is given by

V^{\mu}(s)\;=\;\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\!\left[\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}\,r(s_{t},a_{t},s_{t+1})\right],\qquad s\in\mathcal{S}~.

Induced Markov chain.

A policy
\mu
induces the transition matrix

P^{\mu}(s,s^{\prime})\;=\;\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\mu(s,a)\,P(s^{\prime}\mid s,a),\qquad s,s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}~.

Throughout,
\mu
-induced rewards are shortened to

r(s,s^{\prime}):=\sum_{a}\mu(s,a)\,r(s,a,s^{\prime})
and are assumed bounded
by
r_{\infty}
.
The Bellman operator associated with
\mu
is defined as

(T^{\mu}\bm{V})(s):=\sum_{s^{\prime}=1}^{n}P^{\mu}(s,s^{\prime})\,\bigl(r(s,s^{\prime})+\gamma V(s^{\prime})\bigr),\qquad\bm{V}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

is a
\gamma
-contraction on
(\mathbb{R}^{n},\|\cdot\|_{\infty})
;
hence
\bm{V}^{\mu}
is its unique fixed point.

Ergodicity.

We impose the following standard condition.

Assumption 1
.

The Markov chain with transition matrix
\bm{P}^{\mu}
is irreducible and aperiodic.

For completeness, we also recall the relevant definitions.

Definition 3.1

(Irreducible)
.

A Markov chain with transition matrix
\bm{P}
on
\mathcal{S}
is

irreducible
if for every
i,j\in\mathcal{S}
there exists

n\geq 1
such that
P^{n}(i,j)>0
.

Definition 3.2

(Aperiodic)
.

Let
d(i):=\gcd\{\,n\geq 1:P^{n}(i,i)>0\,\}
be the

period
of state
i
. A chain is
aperiodic
if
d(i)=1
for
all
i\in\mathcal{S}
(equivalently, for any single state when the chain
is irreducible).

Under Assumption
1
the chain admits a unique
stationary distribution
\bm{\pi}\in\Delta^{n-1}
with
\bm{\pi}\bm{P}^{\mu}=\bm{\pi}
, and
mixes geometrically:

Theorem 3.3

(
Levin and Peres 2017
)
.

There exist constants
C>0
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
such that for any
initial state
s_{0}

\bigl\|(\bm{P}^{\mu})^{t}(s_{0},\cdot)-\bm{\pi}\bigr\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\;\leq\;C\,\alpha^{t},\qquad t\geq 0~.

Weighted norms and Dirichlet seminorm
(
Diaconis and Saloff-Coste 1996
;
Ollivier 2018
;
Liu and Olshevsky 2021
)
.

Let
A\succ 0
. For vectors
\bm{x},\bm{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
define

\langle\bm{x},\bm{y}\rangle_{\bm{A}}:=\bm{x}^{\top}\bm{A}\bm{y}
and

\|\bm{x}\|_{\bm{A}}:=\sqrt{\langle\bm{x},\bm{x}\rangle_{\bm{A}}}
.
With
\bm{D}:=\operatorname{diag}(\pi(1),\dots,\pi(n))
we write

\langle\bm{V},\bm{V}^{\prime}\rangle_{\bm{D}}\;=\;\sum_{s}\pi(s)\,V(s)\,V^{\prime}(s),\qquad\|\bm{V}\|_{\bm{D}}^{2}\;=\;\sum_{s}\pi(s)\,V(s)^{2}~.

The
Dirichlet seminorm
is

\|\bm{V}\|_{\mathrm{Dir}}^{2}\;:=\;\frac{1}{2}\sum_{s,s^{\prime}}\pi(s)\,P^{\mu}(s,s^{\prime})\bigl(V(s^{\prime})-V(s)\bigr)^{2}~.

Note that

\|\bm{V}\|_{\mathrm{Dir}}=0
iff
\bm{V}
is a constant multiple of the all
1
vector.

3.2
TD(0) with Linear Function Approximation

Exact value computation is infeasible on large state spaces. A
commonly used remedy is to approximate
\bm{V}^{\mu}
by a linear predictor
and estimate its weights with temporal-difference
learning
(
Sutton 1988
)
.

Linear architecture.

Let
\phi_{i}:\mathcal{S}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
for
i\in[d]
be fixed
features and set

\bm{\phi}(s):=(\phi_{1}(s),\dots,\phi_{d}(s))^{\top},\qquad\bm{\Phi}\;:=\;\begin{bmatrix}\bm{\phi}(s_{1})^{\!\top}\\[2.0pt]
\bm{\phi}(s_{2})^{\!\top}\\[2.0pt]
\vdots\\[2.0pt]
\bm{\phi}(s_{n})^{\!\top}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d},\qquad V_{\bm{\theta}}(s):=\bm{\theta}^{\top}\bm{\phi}(s)~.

We recall the following standard assumptions on the features
(
Tsitsiklis and Van Roy 1996
;
Bhandari et al. 2018
;
Srikant and Ying 2019
;
Mitra 2024
)
.

Assumption 2

(Independent features)
.

\bm{\Phi}
has full column rank
d
, and
\|\bm{\phi}(s)\|_{2}\leq\phi_{\infty}

for all
s\in\mathcal{S}
.

TD error and update.

Given parameter
\bm{\theta}_{t}
and transition
(s_{t},s_{t+1})
with reward

r(s_{t},s_{t+1})
, the TD error is

\delta_{t}:=r(s_{t},s_{t+1})+\gamma\,V_{\bm{\theta}_{t}}(s_{t+1})-V_{\bm{\theta}_{t}}(s_{t})~.

TD(0) then performs

\bm{\theta}_{t+1}=\bm{\theta}_{t}+\eta_{t}\,\delta_{t}\,\nabla_{\!\bm{\theta}}V_{\bm{\theta}_{t}}(s_{t})=\theta_{t}+\eta_{t}\,\bigl(r(s_{t},s_{t+1})+\gamma\bm{\theta}_{t}^{\top}\bm{\phi}(s_{t+1})-\bm{\theta}_{t}^{\top}\bm{\phi}(s_{t})\bigr)\bm{\phi}(s_{t})\coloneqq\bm{\theta}_{t}+\eta_{t}\bm{g}_{t},

with stepsize
\eta_{t}>0
.

Projected Bellman equation.

Under Assumptions
1
–
2
the TD fixed
point
\bm{\theta}^{*}
is the unique solution of the projected Bellman
equation
(
Tsitsiklis and Van Roy 1996
)
,

\bm{\Phi}\bm{\theta}^{*}=\Pi_{\bm{D}}\,T^{\mu}\!\bigl(\bm{\Phi}\bm{\theta}^{*}\bigr),

where
\Pi_{D}
is the projection operator onto the subspace
\{\bm{\Phi}x|x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\}
.

Stationary gradient.

Define the
stationary gradient
map

\bar{\bm{g}}(\bm{\theta}):=\sum_{s,s^{\prime}}\pi(s)\,P^{\mu}(s,s^{\prime})\,\bigl(r(s,s^{\prime})+\gamma\bm{\phi}(s^{\prime})^{\top}\bm{\theta}-\bm{\phi}(s)^{\top}\bm{\theta}\bigr)\bm{\phi}(s)~.

(1)

Equation (
1
) satisfies
\bar{\bm{g}}(\bm{\theta}^{*})=0
, and the
asymptotic behaviour of TD(0) is closely tied to this deterministic
vector field
(
Tsitsiklis and Van Roy 1996
;
Bhandari et al. 2018
)
.

In this section, we present our main result.
We analyze TD(0) without any projection, see Algorithm
1
. Our aim is to show a convergence result for it.
Given that TD learning updates resemble the ones of Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD), one might wonder if there exists a function that TD(0) is implicitly minimizing.
So, the first natural question is: Which function is being minimized by TD(0)?

In prior work
(
Bhandari et al. 2018
, e.g.,)
, the objective function used for the convergence analysis was

\frac{1-\gamma}{2}\left\|{\bm{V}_{\bm{\theta}^{*}}-\bm{V}_{\bm{\theta}}}\right\|^{2}_{\bm{D}}~.

(2)

Note that when discount factor
\gamma=1
, then the objective function loses its utility.
For this reason, we focus on the better potential function proposed by
Liu and Olshevsky 2021
:

f(\bm{\theta})=(1-\gamma)\left\|{\bm{V}_{\bm{\theta}}-\bm{V}_{\bm{\theta}^{*}}}\right\|^{2}_{\bm{D}}+\gamma\left\|{\bm{V}_{\bm{\theta}}-\bm{V}_{\bm{\theta}^{*}}}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{Dir}}~.

Clearly,
\bm{\theta}^{*}
as defined in the previous section minimizes
f(\bm{\theta})
.
Moreover, any result on this potential implies the ones obtained using (
2
). Finally, this objective function provides convergence results even when the discount factor
\gamma
is equal to 1, see the discussion in
Liu and Olshevsky 2021
.

From a technical point of view, the advantage of this potential is that the mean-path TD update
\bar{\bm{g}}(\bm{\theta})
satisfies the equality in the following theorem.
Instead, using the objective function in
2
, one instead would obtain an inequality. In a sense, the results in
Liu and Olshevsky 2021
show that this is the “correct” potential function for TD(0).

Theorem 4.1
.

(
(
Liu and Olshevsky 2021
, Theorem 1)
)

For any
\bm{\theta}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we have

\langle{-\bar{\bm{g}}(\bm{\theta})},{\bm{\theta}-\bm{\theta}^{*}}\rangle=f(\bm{\theta})-f(\bm{\theta}^{*}),

where
\bar{\bm{g}}(\bm{\theta})
is defined in Equation
1
.

Equipped with the above potential function, we analyze the TD algorithm with a carefully chosen step size, showing that the TD algorithm converges with a rate of
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\frac{\left\|{\bm{\theta}^{*}}\right\|^{2}_{2}}{\sqrt{T}})
, even in the presence of Markovian noise and without projections, as detailed in the following theorem.

Theorem 4.2
.

Suppose we are running Algorithm
1
. Let
\bar{\bm{\theta}}_{T}\coloneqq\frac{1}{\sum_{i=0}^{T-1}\eta_{i}}\sum_{k=0}^{T-1}\bm{\theta}_{k}
. For
T
such that

\log T\geq\max\left\{C\sqrt{\frac{\log(C\sqrt{T})}{\log(\frac{1}{\alpha})}+1},\left(\frac{\log(C\sqrt{T})}{\log(\frac{1}{\alpha})}+1\right)^{\frac{3}{4}}\right\},

where
C
is the one in Theorem
3.3
, and
c>30+\sqrt{1302}
, then the following statements hold:

(a)

\mathbb{E}\!\left[(1-\gamma)\left\|{\bm{V}_{\bar{\bm{\theta}}_{T}}-\bm{V}_{\bm{\theta}^{*}}}\right\|^{2}_{\bm{D}}+\gamma\left\|{\bm{V}_{\bar{\bm{\theta}}_{T}}-\bm{V}_{\bm{\theta}^{*}}}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{Dir}}\right]=\tilde{\mathcal{O}}\left(\frac{c\,\omega_{c}^{2}\left\|{\bm{\theta}^{*}}\right\|^{2}_{2}}{\sqrt{T}}\right)
.

(b)

For any
t\leq T
,
\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left\|{\bm{\theta}_{t}}\right\|^{2}_{2}\right]\leq\omega_{c}^{2}\max\left\{\frac{r^{2}_{\infty}}{\phi^{2}_{\infty}},\left\|{\bm{\theta}^{*}}\right\|^{2}_{2}\right\}
, where
\omega_{c}
is decreasing in
c
,
\omega_{c}\to\infty
when
c\to 30+\sqrt{1302}
and
\omega_{c}\to 2
when
c\to\infty
.

The proof of part (a) can be found in Section
4
and the proof of part (b) is in Theorem
C.1
.

Bounded iterates without projections.

The fact that the iterates are bounded is both a novel contribution and also a crucial ingredient in our analysis. Here, we will provide a sketch of our proof.

First of all, let’s understand how previous analyses analyzed TD(0). The need to control the iterates with a projection arises from the need to control the magnitude of the updates
\bm{g}_{t}(\bm{\theta}_{t})
. In fact, to control the magnitude of
\bm{g}_{t}
,
Bhandari et al. 2018
uses the following lemma:

Lemma 4.3
.

(
Bhandari et al. 2018
, Lemma 6)

For all
\bm{\theta}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\left\|{\bm{g}_{t}(\bm{\theta})}\right\|_{2}\leq r_{\infty}\phi_{\infty}+2\phi^{2}_{\infty}\left\|{\bm{\theta}}\right\|_{2}
.

Proof.

\left\|{\bm{g}_{t}(\bm{\theta})}\right\|_{2}\leq|r_{t}+(\gamma\bm{\phi}(s_{t+1})-\bm{\phi}(s_{t}))^{\top}\bm{\theta}|\left\|{\bm{\phi}(s_{t})}\right\|_{2}\leq r_{\infty}\phi_{\infty}+2\phi^{2}_{\infty}\left\|{\bm{\theta}}\right\|_{2}
.
∎

Using projections, we can ensure that
\left\|{\bm{\theta}_{t}}\right\|_{2}\leq R
for all
t
. So, we can easily upper bound the magnitude of the updates
\bm{g}_{t}(\bm{\theta}_{t})
by
r_{\infty}\phi_{\infty}+2\phi_{\infty}^{2}R
.

The remaining part of their analysis is to control the bias in the update.
This is due to the fact that the stochastic gradient
\bm{g}_{t}(\bm{\theta}_{t})
depends on the past trajectories of the Markov chain, and is not an unbiased estimate of
\bar{\bm{g}}(\bm{\theta}_{t})
.
To be more precise, by defining
O_{t}=(s_{t},s_{t+1})
, in general we have

\mathbb{E}_{O_{t}\sim(\pi,\pi P^{\mu})}\!\left[\bm{g}_{t}(\bm{\theta}_{t},O_{t})\right]\neq\bar{\bm{g}}(\bm{\theta}_{t}),

which comes from the fact that
\bm{\theta}_{t}
is also random and depending on
\mathcal{F}_{t-1}=\sigma(O_{0},\ldots,O_{t-1})
, and
O_{t}
depends on
O_{t-1}
.
The key idea is that, since the Markov chain is ergodic, it converges to the stationary distribution geometrically fast, i.e., the chain forgets its states after
\tau
steps, where
\tau
is small. So,
\bm{\theta}_{t-\tau}
is almost independent of
O_{t}
and from Lemma
A.4
in the Appendix we have

\left\|{\mathbb{E}\!\left[\bm{g}_{t}(\bm{\theta}_{t-\tau},O_{t})-\bar{\bm{g}}(\bm{\theta}_{t-\tau})\;\middle|\;\mathcal{F}_{t-\tau-1}\right]_{2}}\right\|=8\sup_{o}\left\|{\bm{g}_{t}(\bm{\theta}_{t-\tau},o)}\right\|_{2}\left\|{\tilde{O}_{\tau}-\bm{\pi}}\right\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq 8\ell_{t-\tau}C\alpha^{\tau},

where
\tilde{O}_{\tau}
is the distribution with restarting at step
t-\tau
and
\ell_{t-\tau}\coloneqq r_{\infty}\phi^{2}_{\infty}+2\phi^{2}_{\infty}\left\|{\bm{\theta}_{t-\tau}}\right\|_{2}
.
To control the bias term, we decompose as

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\!\left[\bm{g}_{t}(\bm{\theta}_{t},O_{t})-\bar{\bm{g}}(\bm{\theta}_{t})\;\middle|\;\mathcal{F}_{t-\tau-1}\right]

\displaystyle\quad=\mathbb{E}\!\left[\bm{g}_{t}(\bm{\theta}_{t-\tau},O_{t})-\bar{\bm{g}}(\bm{\theta}_{t-\tau})+\bm{g}_{t}(\bm{\theta}_{t},O_{t})-\bar{\bm{g}}(\bm{\theta}_{t})-\bm{g}_{t}(\bm{\theta}_{t-\tau},O_{t})-\bar{\bm{g}}(\bm{\theta}_{t-\tau})\;\middle|\;\mathcal{F}_{t-\tau-1}\right]~.

Hence, we can control the bias term as the mixing part and the following Lipschitz continuity part:

Lemma 4.4
.

Fix any
k\leq T
, for any
O_{k}
,
\bm{\theta}
and
\bm{\theta}^{\prime}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|{\bm{g}(\bm{\theta},O_{k})-\bm{g}(\bm{\theta}^{\prime},O_{k})}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\phi^{2}_{\infty}\left\|{\bm{\theta}-\bm{\theta}^{\prime}}\right\|_{2},

\displaystyle\left\|{\bar{\bm{g}}(\bm{\theta})-\bar{\bm{g}}_{2}(\bm{\theta}^{\prime})}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq 2\phi_{\infty}^{2}\left\|{\bm{\theta}-\bm{\theta}^{\prime}}\right\|_{2}~.

Proof.

The proof can be found in the final part of
Bhandari et al. 2018
.
∎

The bias term can be controlled when
\bm{\theta}_{t}
and
\bm{\theta}_{t-\tau}
are not far away which is guaranteed by the choice of the learning rate.

We can sketch our proof.
In our analysis, the major difficulty lies in bounding the iterates
\left\|{\bm{\theta}_{t}}\right\|_{2}
without a projection. We start by decomposing
the updates as follows:

\displaystyle\bm{\theta}_{t+1}

\displaystyle=\bm{\theta}_{t}+\eta_{t}\bm{g}(\bm{\theta}_{t},O_{t})

\displaystyle=\bm{\theta}_{t}+\eta_{t}\left(\bm{g}(\bm{\theta}_{t},O_{t})-\mathbb{E}\!\left[\bm{g}(\bm{\theta}_{t},O_{t})\;\middle|\;\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]+\mathbb{E}\!\left[\bm{g}(\bm{\theta}_{t},O_{t})\;\middle|\;\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]-\bar{\bm{g}}(\bm{\theta}_{t})+\bar{\bm{g}}(\bm{\theta}_{t})\right)

\displaystyle=\bm{\theta}_{t}+\eta_{t}\left(\bm{\xi}_{t}+\bm{b}_{t}+\bar{\bm{g}}(\bm{\theta}_{t})\right)~.

Then, we have

\displaystyle d^{2}_{t}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\|{\bm{\theta}_{t}-\bm{\theta}^{*}}\right\|^{2}_{2}=\left\|{\bm{\theta}_{t-1}+\eta_{t-1}(\bm{\xi}_{t-1}+\bm{b}_{t-1}+\bar{\bm{g}}(\bm{\theta}_{t-1}))-\bm{\theta}^{*}}\right\|^{2}_{2}

\displaystyle=d^{2}_{t-1}+2\eta_{t-1}\langle{\bm{\xi}_{t-1}+\bm{b}_{t-1}+\bar{\bm{g}}(\bm{\theta}_{t-1})},{\bm{\theta}_{t-1}-\bm{\theta}^{*}}\rangle+\eta_{t-1}^{2}\left\|{\bm{\xi}_{t-1}+\bm{b}_{t-1}+\bar{\bm{g}}(\bm{\theta}_{t-1})}\right\|^{2}_{2}~.

From Theorem
4.1
, we have
\langle{-\bar{\bm{g}}(\bm{\theta}_{t-1})},{\bm{\theta}_{t-1}-\bm{\theta}^{*}}\rangle=f(\bm{\theta}_{t-1})-f(\bm{\theta}^{*})\geq 0
.
Thus, we have

\displaystyle d^{2}_{t}-d^{2}_{t-1}

\displaystyle\leq 2\eta_{t-1}\langle{\bm{\xi}_{t-1}+\bm{b}_{t-1}},{\bm{\theta}_{t-1}-\bm{\theta}^{*}}\rangle+\eta_{t-1}^{2}\left\|{\bm{\xi}_{t-1}+\bm{b}_{t-1}+\bar{\bm{g}}(\bm{\theta}_{t-1})}\right\|^{2}_{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\eta_{t-1}\langle{\bm{\xi}_{t-1}},{\bm{\theta}_{t-1}-\bm{\theta}^{*}}\rangle+2\eta_{t-1}\langle{\bm{b}_{t-1}},{\bm{\theta}_{t-1}-\bm{\theta}^{*}}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+3\eta^{2}_{t-1}\left\|{\bm{\xi}_{t-1}}\right\|^{2}_{2}+3\eta^{2}_{t-1}\left\|{\bm{b}_{t-1}}\right\|^{2}_{2}+3\eta^{2}_{t-1}\left\|{\bar{\bm{g}}(\bm{\theta}_{t-1})}\right\|^{2}_{2},

and therefore

\mathbb{E}\!\left[d^{2}_{t}-d^{2}_{0}\right]\leq 2\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\eta_{k}\langle{\bm{\xi}_{k}+\bm{b}_{k}},{\bm{\theta}_{k}-\bm{\theta}^{*}}\rangle\right]+3\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\eta^{2}_{k}\left\|{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\right\|^{2}_{2}+\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\eta^{2}_{k}\left\|{\bm{b}_{k}}\right\|^{2}_{2}+\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\eta^{2}_{k}\left\|{\bar{\bm{g}}(\bm{\theta}_{k})}\right\|^{2}_{2}\right]~.

Notice that
\bm{\xi}_{t}
is a martingale difference with respect to
\mathcal{F}_{t-1}
as we prove in the following lemma.

Lemma 4.5
.

\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\eta_{k}\langle{\bm{\xi}_{k}},{\bm{\theta}_{k}-\bm{\theta}^{*}}\rangle\right]=0~.

Proof.

Let
M_{t-1}=\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\eta_{k}\langle{\bm{\xi}_{k}},{\bm{\theta}_{k}-\bm{\theta}^{*}}\rangle
.
We have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\!\left[M_{t-1}\;\middle|\;\mathcal{F}_{t-2}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\!\left[M_{t-2}+M_{t-1}-M_{t-2}\;\middle|\;\mathcal{F}_{t-2}\right]

\displaystyle=M_{t-2}+\mathbb{E}\!\left[\eta_{t-1}\langle{\bm{g}(\bm{\theta}_{t-1},O_{t-1})-\mathbb{E}\!\left[\bm{g}(\bm{\theta}_{t-1},O_{t-1})\;\middle|\;\mathcal{F}_{t-2}\right]},{\bm{\theta}_{k-1}-\bm{\theta}^{*}}\rangle\;\middle|\;\mathcal{F}_{t-2}\right]

\displaystyle=M_{t-2}~.

Hence,
M_{t}
is a martingale. Therefore, we have

\mathbb{E}\!\left[M_{t-1}\right]=\mathbb{E}\!\left[\mathbb{E}\!\left[M_{t-1}\;\middle|\;\mathcal{F}_{t-2}\right]\right]=\mathbb{E}\!\left[M_{t-2}\right]=\mathbb{E}\!\left[M_{0}\right]=0~.\qed

To bound the remaining terms in
\mathbb{E}\!\left[d^{2}_{t}\right]
, we use the following lemma (the precise statements can be found in Lemmas
B.1
and
B.2
).

Lemma 4.6
.

We have the following control of the bias and gradient norm squared terms:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\eta_{k}\langle{\bm{b}_{k}},{\bm{\theta}_{k}-\bm{\theta}^{*}}\rangle\right]

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}\left(\max\left\{C\sqrt{\frac{\log C\sqrt{t}}{\log(\frac{1}{\alpha})}+1},\left(\frac{\log(C\sqrt{T})}{\log(\frac{1}{\alpha})}+1\right)^{3/4}\right\}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left\|{\bm{\theta}_{t-1}}\right\|^{2}_{2}\right]\right),

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\eta_{k}^{2}\left\|{\chi_{k}}\right\|^{2}_{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}\left(\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left\|{\bm{\theta}_{t-1}}\right\|^{2}_{2}\right]\right)\,for\,\chi_{k}\in\{\bm{\xi}_{k},\bm{b}_{k},\bar{\bm{g}}(\bm{\theta}_{k})\}~.

It follows that
\mathbb{E}\!\left[d^{2}_{t}\right]=\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{E}\!\left[\|\bm{\theta}_{t-1}\|^{2}_{2}\right])
. Notice that

\mathbb{E}[\|\bm{\theta}_{t}\|^{2}_{2}]\leq\mathbb{E}[(d_{t}+\left\|{\bm{\theta}^{*}}\right\|_{2})^{2}]
from the triangular inequality, so
d^{2}_{t}
and
\left\|{\bm{\theta}_{t}}\right\|^{2}_{2}
have a tangled recursion structure. Reasoning by induction, we assume

\max_{i\leq t-1}\ \left\|{\bm{\theta}_{i}}\right\|^{2}_{2}=\mathcal{O}\left(\max\left\{\frac{r^{2}_{\infty}}{\phi^{2}_{\infty}},\left\|{\bm{\theta}^{*}}\right\|^{2}_{2}\right\}\right),

and show that
\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left\|{\bm{\theta}_{t}}\right\|^{2}_{2}\right]=\mathcal{O}\left(\max\left\{\frac{r^{2}_{\infty}}{\phi^{2}_{\infty}},\left\|{\bm{\theta}^{*}}\right\|^{2}_{2}\right\}\right)
. Indeed, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left\|{\bm{\theta}_{t}}\right\|^{2}_{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left(d_{t}+\left\|{\bm{\theta}^{*}}\right\|_{2}\right)^{2}\right]\leq\left(\left\|{\bm{\theta}^{*}}\right\|_{2}+\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\!\left[d^{2}_{t}\right]}\right)^{2}=\left(\left\|{\bm{\theta}^{*}}\right\|_{2}+\sqrt{\mathcal{O}\left(\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left\|{\bm{\theta}_{t-1}}\right\|^{2}_{2}\right]\right)}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}\left(\max\left\{\frac{r^{2}_{\infty}}{\phi^{2}_{\infty}},\left\|{\bm{\theta}^{*}}\right\|^{2}_{2}\right\}\right),

where we use the induction hypothesis in the last equality. The precise statement and proof can be found in Theorem
C.1
in the Appendix.

Convergence result.

We now give the proof of the convergence result in our main result.

Proof.

For any
0<t\leq T
,

\displaystyle d_{t}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|{\bm{\theta}^{*}-\bm{\theta}_{t-1}-\eta_{t-1}\bm{g}_{t-1}}\right\|^{2}_{2}

\displaystyle=d^{2}_{t-1}-2\eta_{t-1}\langle{\bm{g}_{t-1}},{\bm{\theta}^{*}-\bm{\theta}_{t-1}}\rangle+\eta^{2}_{t-1}\left\|{\bm{g}_{t-1}}\right\|^{2}_{2}

\displaystyle=d^{2}_{t-1}-2\eta_{t-1}\langle{\bar{\bm{g}}(\bm{\theta}_{t-1})},{\bm{\theta}^{*}-\bm{\theta}_{t-1}}\rangle+2\eta_{t-1}\langle{\bar{\bm{g}}(\bm{\theta}_{t-1})-\bm{g}_{t-1}},{\bm{\theta}^{*}-\bm{\theta}_{t-1}}\rangle+\eta^{2}_{t-1}\left\|{\bm{g}_{t-1}}\right\|^{2}_{2}~.

Summing from
t=0
to
t=T-1
, taking the expectation, and using Theorem
4.1
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}2\eta_{t}\mathbb{E}\!\left[(1-\gamma)\left\|{\bm{V}_{\bm{\theta}_{t}}-\bm{V}_{\bm{\theta}^{*}}}\right\|^{2}_{\bm{D}}+\gamma\left\|{\bm{V}_{\bm{\theta}_{t}}-\bm{V}_{\bm{\theta}^{*}}}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{Dir}}\right]

\displaystyle\quad\leq\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\left(\mathbb{E}\!\left[d^{2}_{t}\right]-\mathbb{E}\!\left[d^{2}_{t+1}\right]\right)+\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}2\eta_{t}\langle{\bar{\bm{g}}(\bm{\theta}_{t})-\bm{g}_{t}},{\bm{\theta}^{*}-\bm{\theta}_{t}}\rangle\right]+\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\eta^{2}_{t}\left\|{\bm{g}_{t}}\right\|^{2}_{2}\right]

\displaystyle\quad=\left\|{\bm{\theta}^{*}}\right\|_{2}^{2}+\mathcal{O}\left(\max\left\{\frac{r^{2}_{\infty}}{\phi^{2}_{\infty}},\left\|{\bm{\theta}^{*}}\right\|_{2}^{2}\right\}\right),

where we used Lemma
4.6
in the last inequality. Using the convexity of
f
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\!\left[f(\bar{\bm{\theta}}_{T})-f(\bm{\theta}^{*})\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sum_{i=0}^{T-1}\eta_{i}}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\eta_{t}\mathbb{E}\!\left[(1-\gamma)\left\|{\bm{V}_{\bm{\theta}_{t}}-\bm{V}_{\bm{\theta}^{*}}}\right\|^{2}_{\bm{D}}+\gamma\left\|{\bm{V}_{\bm{\theta}_{t}}-\bm{V}_{\bm{\theta}^{*}}}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{Dir}}\right]

\displaystyle=\tilde{\mathcal{O}}\left(\frac{\max\left\{r^{2}_{\infty},\phi^{2}_{\infty}\left\|{\bm{\theta}^{*}}\right\|_{2}^{2}\right\}}{\sqrt{T}}\right),

where we use the fact that
\sum_{i=0}^{T-1}\eta_{i}\geq\sum_{i=0}^{T-1}\frac{1}{c\phi^{2}_{\infty}\log^{2}(T+3)\sqrt{t+1}}\geq\frac{2\sqrt{T}-2}{c\phi^{2}_{\infty}\log^{2}(T+3)}
in the last inequality.
∎

5
Detailed Comparison with Previous Results and Limitations

Here, we discuss some technical differences between our result and previous ones.
Let’s start with the result in
Bhandari et al. 2018
.
Their Theorem 3 proves the projected TD learning satisfies

\mathbb{E}\!\left[(1-\gamma)\left\|{\bm{V}_{\bar{\bm{\theta}}_{T}}-\bm{V}_{\bm{\theta}^{*}}}\right\|^{2}_{\bm{D}}\right]=\tilde{\mathcal{O}}\left(\frac{R^{2}}{\sqrt{T}}\right),

where
R
is a parameter of the algorithm and it must be chosen to satisfy
R\geq\left\|{\bm{\theta}^{*}}\right\|_{2}
.
Our result recovers the above bound since
\left\|{\bm{V}_{\bm{\theta}}-\bm{V}_{\bm{\theta}^{*}}}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{Dir}}\geq 0
, but, given that we do not have projections, we depend directly on
\|\bm{\theta}^{*}\|_{2}
instead of
R
. While in principle
\|\bm{\theta}^{*}\|_{2}
could be estimated using
\lambda_{\min}>0
, the minimum eigenvalue of
\bm{\Phi}^{\top}\bm{D}\bm{\Phi}

(
Bhandari et al. 2018
, Lemma 1)
, this approach is impractical from an algorithm design perspective. In fact, the goal of temporal difference learning is precisely to avoid reliance on the transition kernel
\bm{P}^{\mu}
, yet
\lambda_{\min}
depends on
\pi_{\min}
, the smallest entry of the stationary distribution, which cannot be estimated without knowing
\bm{P}^{\mu}
.
Other works do analyze TD learning without performing an explicit projection step
(
Srikant and Ying 2019
;
Mitra 2024
)
, but relying on some kind of measure of the minimal curvature of the potential function.
We highlight three major differences between our work and this line of work. First, from the stochastic optimization perspective, our setting is weaker: we only relies on convexity instead of some kind of strong convexity, which naturally leads to a slower convergence rate, but independent of the minimal curvature of the objective function. Second, our analysis is different from
Mitra 2024
because we do not prove a contraction property for
\|\bm{\theta}_{t}-\bm{\theta}^{*}\|_{2}
, which is standard in analyzing optimization of strongly convex functions. Instead, we directly relates
\|\bm{\theta}_{t}-\bm{\theta}^{*}\|_{2}
with
\|\bm{\theta}_{t}\|_{2}
using triangular inequality, which solves the problem that no contraction can be made when the strongly convex is arbitrarily small
(
Mitra 2024
, see Eq 10 in )
.
Third, our analysis leverages Markov chain-specific mixing parameters
C
and
\alpha
, whereas their analysis depends on a minimal curvature parameter that could be arbitrary small. This is particularly true when the curvature depends on the minimal eigenvalue of the covariance matrix of the input features, as in
Mitra 2024
.

For additional clarity, we also calculate the curvature of our potential function in the following lemma and show that it could be arbitrarily small.

Lemma 5.1
.

Suppose
\left\|{\bm{\phi}}\right\|_{2}\leq\phi_{\infty}
and
\text{span}(\{\bm{\phi}(s_{1}),\ldots,\bm{\phi}(s_{n})\})=\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then, we have
\nabla^{2}f=2\bm{\Phi}^{\top}[(1-\gamma)\bm{D}+\gamma\bm{L}]\bm{\Phi}
. Moreover, depending on the features
\bm{\Phi}
, the minimum eigenvalue of the Hessian can be arbitrarily small.

Proof.

Define the following:

W_{ss^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{2}(\pi(s)P^{\mu}(s,s^{\prime})+\pi(s^{\prime})P^{\mu}(s^{\prime},s)),\quad W_{ss}=0~.

So, we have the following symmetric matrix:

\bm{W}=\frac{1}{2}(\bm{D}\bm{P}^{\mu}+(\bm{P}^{\mu})^{\top}\bm{D}),

and the Laplacian matrix

\bm{L}=\bm{D}-\bm{W}~.

So, we can write the function
f
as

\displaystyle f(\bm{\theta})

\displaystyle=(1-\gamma)\left\|{\bm{V}_{\bm{\theta}}-\bm{V}_{\bm{\theta}^{*}}}\right\|^{2}_{\bm{D}}+\gamma\left\|{\bm{V}_{\bm{\theta}}-\bm{V}_{\bm{\theta}^{*}}}\right\|^{2}_{Dir}

\displaystyle=(1-\gamma)(\bm{\theta}-\bm{\theta}^{*})^{\top}\bm{\Phi}^{\top}\bm{D}\bm{\Phi}(\bm{\theta}-\bm{\theta}^{*})+\gamma(\bm{\theta}-\bm{\theta}^{*})^{\top}\bm{\Phi}^{\top}\bm{L}\bm{\Phi}(\bm{\theta}-\bm{\theta}^{*})

\displaystyle=(\bm{\theta}-\bm{\theta}^{*})^{\top}\bm{\Phi}^{\top}[(1-\gamma)\bm{D}+\gamma\bm{L}]\bm{\Phi}(\bm{\theta}-\bm{\theta}^{*})~.

From the above, it is not hard to derive the first and the second derivative of
f
:

\displaystyle\nabla f(\bm{\theta})

\displaystyle=2\bm{\Phi}^{\top}[(1-\gamma)\bm{D}+\gamma\bm{L}]\bm{\Phi}(\bm{\theta}-\bm{\theta}^{*}),

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}f(\bm{\theta})

\displaystyle=2\bm{\Phi}^{\top}[(1-\gamma)\bm{D}+\gamma\bm{L}]\bm{\Phi}~.

Notice that
|\mathcal{S}|=n>>d
, we can pick

\bm{\Phi}\;=\;\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\phi_{\infty}}{\sqrt{2}}(\bm{e}_{1}-\bm{e}_{2})^{\top}\\[4.0pt]
\epsilon\bm{I}_{\,d-1}\\[4.0pt]
\mathbf{0}_{\,(n-d)\times d}\end{pmatrix}\;\in\;\mathbb{R}^{n\times d},

that satisfies our assumption. Setting
\bm{x}\coloneqq\mathbf{1}
we have that
\bm{\Phi}\bm{x}\coloneqq\bm{y}=(0,\epsilon,\dots,\epsilon,0,\dots)^{\top}
.
Hence, it follows that

\bm{x}^{\top}\bm{\Phi}^{\top}[(1-\gamma)\bm{D}+\gamma\bm{L}]\bm{\Phi}\bm{x}=\bm{y}^{\top}[(1-\gamma)\bm{D}+\gamma\bm{L}]\bm{y}=\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{2}),

where we utilize the fact that
\bm{D}
is diagonal and for any
\bm{y}
that is not a constant multiple of
\mathbf{1}
. We have

\bm{y}^{\top}\bm{L}\bm{y}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j}\pi(i)P^{\mu}(i,j)(y_{j}-y_{i})^{2}=\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{2})~.

Thus, the minimum eigenvalue of
\bm{\Phi}^{\top}[(1-\gamma)\bm{D}+\gamma\bm{L}]\bm{\Phi}
is upper bounded by
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{2})
. Since
\epsilon>0
could be arbitrary small, we conclude our proof.
∎

So, our objective function also has a strongly convex property, but this can be arbitrarily small (also the one used in
Bhandari et al. 2018
) depending on the the features which hinders the convergence rate of
(
Srikant and Ying 2019
;
Mitra 2024
)
.

Discussion of limitations.

A limitation of our result is that the learning rate
\eta_{t}
must be set with knowledge of the horizon
T
, rendering our algorithm non-anytime. Additionally, we require
T
to be large enough so that
\log T\geq\max\{C\sqrt{\frac{\log(C\sqrt{T})}{\log(\frac{1}{\alpha})}+1},(\frac{\log(C\sqrt{T})}{\log(\frac{1}{\alpha})}+1)^{\frac{3}{4}}\}
to eliminate dependence on the mixing parameters
C
and
\alpha
in Theorem
3.3
. This is a common limitation in the literature
(
Bhandari et al. 2018
;
Srikant and Ying 2019
;
Mitra 2024
, see, e.g.,)
, where either the bounds are only truly of order
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(1/\sqrt{T})
when
\log T\geq\tau^{\mathrm{mix}}(1/\sqrt{T})
,
1
1

1

The definition of
\tau^{\mathrm{mix}}
is
\min\{t\in\mathbb{N}\mid C\alpha^{t}\leq 1/\sqrt{T}\}
.
or for a sufficiently small learning rate that depends on
\lambda_{\min}
.

We hypothesize that this limitation—dependence on the mixing parameters of the underlying Markov chain—is unavoidable, as different policies induce different transition kernels and hence different mixing behavior. Developing algorithms that automatically adapt to these quantities, or proving matching lower bounds, remains an open direction for future research.

6
Conclusion

In this paper, we have presented a finite-time analysis of TD(0) without additional projection steps nor assumptions on the minimal curvature of the objective function.
As far as we know, this is the first finite-time guarantee under these weak assumptions. In particular, we do not use the contraction-based proof technique of previous work, but instead we directly prove that the iterates of TD(0) are bounded because similar to the ones of SGD. We believe our proof is general and, for example, it can be easily extend to the TD(
\lambda
) and Q-learning setting
(
Bhandari et al. 2018
)
.

In future work, we plan to investigate the possibility to obtain rates that interpolates between
\mathcal{O}\left(1/\sqrt{T}\right)
and
\mathcal{O}\left(1/T\right)
, depending on the curvature of the objective function. Ideally, one would like to show that TD(0) adapts to the curvature of the function with specific learning rates, as it is possible with recent parameter-free schemes
(
Cutkosky and Orabona 2018
)
.

Lemma A.1
.

Suppose that
0\leq u<t
, then we have

\sum^{t-1}_{k=u+1}\frac{1}{\log(k+3)\log(k-u+3)\sqrt{k+1}\sqrt{k-u+1}}\leq\frac{2}{\log 3}~.

Proof.

By re-indexing, we have

\sum_{k=u+1}^{t-1}\frac{1}{\log(k+3)\,\log(k-u+3)\,\sqrt{k+1}\,\sqrt{k-u+1}}\;\leq\;\sum_{j=1}^{t-u-1}\frac{1}{(j+1)\,\log^{2}(j+3)}~.

Consider the function

g(x)\coloneqq\frac{1}{(x+1)\,\log^{2}(x+3)},\qquad x\geq 1~.

For
x\geq 1
we have
x+1\geq\frac{x+3}{2}
, hence

g(x)\;=\;\frac{1}{(x+1)\,\log^{2}(x+3)}\;\leq\;\frac{2}{(x+3)\,\log^{2}(x+3)}~.

Substituting
y=\log(x+3)
and using the fact that
g
is decreasing, we have

\sum_{j=1}^{t-u-1}\frac{1}{(j+1)\,\log^{2}(j+3)}\leq\int_{0}^{t-u-1}\frac{2\text{d}x}{(x+3)\,\log^{2}(x+3)}=\frac{2}{\log 3}-\frac{2}{\log(t-u+2)}~.\qed

Lemma A.2
.

Suppose that
0\leq u<t
, then we have

\sum^{t-1}_{k=u+1}\frac{1}{\log(k+3)\sqrt{k+1}\sqrt{t}}\leq\frac{2}{\log(u+4)}~.

Proof.

We first factor out
\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}
:

S_{u,t}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\sum_{k=u+1}^{t-1}\frac{1}{\log(k+3)\sqrt{k+1}}~.

Because

,

\log(k+3)\;\geq\;\log(u+4)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\frac{1}{\log(k+3)}\;\leq\;\frac{1}{\log(u+4)}~.

Hence

S_{u,t}\;\leq\;\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}\,\log(u+4)}\sum_{k=u+1}^{t-1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{k+1}}~.

The map
x\mapsto x^{-1/2}
is positive and monotonically decreasing, so

\sum_{k=u+1}^{t-1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{k+1}}\;\leq\;\int_{u}^{t}\!x^{-\tfrac{1}{2}}\,dx\;=\;2\bigl(\sqrt{t}-\sqrt{u}\bigr)~.

Thus

S_{u,t}\;\leq\;\frac{2(\sqrt{t}-\sqrt{u})}{\sqrt{t}\,\log(u+4)}\;=\;\frac{2}{\log(u+4)}\Bigl(1-\sqrt{\tfrac{u}{t}}\Bigr)\;\leq\;\frac{2}{\log(u+4)}~.\qed

Lemma A.3
.

For every integer
t\geq 1

\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\frac{1}{\log^{2}(k+3)\,(k+1)}\;\leq\;\frac{1}{\bigl(\log 3\bigr)^{2}}+\frac{2}{\log 3}-\frac{2}{\log(t+2)}\;\leq\;\frac{1}{\bigl(\log 3\bigr)^{2}}+\frac{2}{\log 3}\;<3~.

Proof.

Denote the sum by
S_{t}

S_{t}\;=\;\frac{1}{(\log 3)^{2}}+\sum_{k=1}^{t-1}\frac{1}{\log^{2}(k+3)(k+1)}~.

For every
k\geq 1
we have
k+1\geq\frac{1}{2}(k+3)
, hence

\frac{1}{\log^{2}(k+3)(k+1)}\;\leq\;\frac{2}{\log^{2}(k+3)(k+3)}~.

Because
x\mapsto\dfrac{1}{x(\log x)^{2}}

\sum_{k=1}^{t-1}\frac{2}{(\log^{2}(k+3))(k+3)}\;\leq\;2\!\!\int_{3}^{t+2}\!\!\frac{dx}{x(\log x)^{2}}~.

The antiderivative is

2\!\!\int_{3}^{t+2}\!\!\frac{dx}{x(\log x)^{2}}\;=\;2\left[-\frac{1}{\log x}\right]_{3}^{\,t+2}\;=\;\frac{2}{\log 3}-\frac{2}{\log(t+2)}~.

Thus,

S_{t}\;\leq\;\frac{1}{(\log 3)^{2}}+\frac{2}{\log 3}-\frac{2}{\log(t+2)}~.\qed

Lemma A.5

\displaystyle\leq 2G_{k}\left\|{\bm{\theta}_{k}-\bm{\theta}_{k^{\prime}}}\right\|+d_{k^{\prime}}(2\phi_{\infty}^{2}\left\|{\bm{\theta}_{k}-\bm{\theta}_{k^{\prime}}}\right\|+2\phi_{\infty}^{2}\left\|{\bm{\theta}_{k}-\bm{\theta}_{k^{\prime}}}\right\|)

\displaystyle\leq(2G_{k}+4\phi_{\infty}^{2}d_{k^{\prime}})\left\|{\bm{\theta}_{k}-\bm{\theta}_{k^{\prime}}}\right\|,

where we used Lemma
4.4
in the second to last inequality.
∎

Appendix B
Bias and Variance

Recall that we decompose the updates as follows:

\displaystyle\bm{\theta}_{t+1}

\displaystyle=\bm{\theta}_{t}+\eta_{t}\bm{g}(\bm{\theta}_{t},O_{t})

\displaystyle=\bm{\theta}_{t}+\eta_{t}\left(\bm{g}(\bm{\theta}_{t},O_{t})-\mathbb{E}\!\left[\bm{g}(\bm{\theta}_{t},O_{t})\;\middle|\;\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]+\mathbb{E}\!\left[\bm{g}(\bm{\theta}_{t},O_{t})\;\middle|\;\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]-\bar{\bm{g}}(\bm{\theta}_{t})+\bar{\bm{g}}(\bm{\theta}_{t})\right)

Lemma B.1
.

For any

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\eta_{k}\langle{\bm{b}_{k}},{\bm{\theta}_{k}-\bm{\theta}^{*}}\rangle\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{2}{\delta^{2}(\log T)^{2}}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sum_{k=0}^{u_{t}}\frac{\ell_{k}+2\phi^{2}_{\infty}d_{0}}{\sqrt{k+1}\log(k+3)}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{\ell_{i-1}}{\log(i+2)\sqrt{i}}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{2}{\delta^{2}(\log T)^{2}}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sum_{k=u_{t}+1}^{t-1}\frac{\ell_{k}+2\phi^{2}_{\infty}d_{k-u_{t}}}{\log(k+3)\log(k-u_{t}+3)\sqrt{k+1}}\sum_{i=k-u_{t}+1}^{k}\frac{\ell_{i-1}}{\sqrt{i}}\right]~.

Proof.

Let
u_{t}=\frac{\log(C\sqrt{t})}{\log(\frac{1}{\alpha})}
and

s_{k}=\begin{cases}k,&\text{if }k\leq u_{t}\\
u_{t},&\text{ otherwise}\end{cases}
.

We decompose the bias term as follows:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\eta_{k}\langle{\bm{b}_{k}},{\bm{\theta}_{k}-\bm{\theta}^{*}}\rangle\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\eta_{k}\langle{\mathbb{E}\!\left[\bm{g}(\bm{\theta}_{k},O_{k})\;\middle|\;\mathcal{F}_{k-1}\right]-\bar{\bm{g}}(\bm{\theta}_{k})},{\bm{\theta}_{k}-\bm{\theta}^{*}}\rangle\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\eta_{k}\langle{\mathbb{E}\!\left[\bm{g}(\bm{\theta}_{k-s_{k}},O_{k})\;\middle|\;\mathcal{F}_{k-1}\right]-\bar{\bm{g}}(\bm{\theta}_{k-s_{k}})},{\bm{\theta}_{k-s_{k}}-\bm{\theta}^{*}}\rangle\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\eta_{k}\langle{\mathbb{E}\!\left[\bm{g}(\bm{\theta}_{k},O_{k})\;\middle|\;\mathcal{F}_{k-1}\right]-\bar{\bm{g}}(\bm{\theta}_{k})},{\bm{\theta}_{k}-\bm{\theta}^{*}}\rangle\right]

\displaystyle\quad-\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\eta_{k}\langle{\mathbb{E}\!\left[\bm{g}(\bm{\theta}_{k-s_{k}},O_{k})\;\middle|\;\mathcal{F}_{k-1}\right]-\bar{\bm{g}}(\bm{\theta}_{k-s_{k}})},{\bm{\theta}_{k-s_{k}}-\bm{\theta}^{*}}\rangle\right]~.

For the first term above, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\eta_{k}\langle{\mathbb{E}\!\left[\bm{g}(\bm{\theta}_{k-s_{k}},O_{k})\;\middle|\;\mathcal{F}_{k-1}\right]-\bar{\bm{g}}(\bm{\theta}_{k-s_{k}})},{\bm{\theta}_{k-s_{k}}-\bm{\theta}^{*}}\rangle\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\mathbb{E}\!\left[\eta_{k}\langle{\mathbb{E}\!\left[\bm{g}(\bm{\theta}_{k-s_{k}},O_{k-s_{k}})\;\middle|\;\mathcal{F}_{k-1}\right]-\bar{\bm{g}}(\bm{\theta}_{k-s_{k}})},{\bm{\theta}_{k-s_{k}}-\bm{\theta}^{*}}\rangle\;\middle|\;\mathcal{F}_{k-1-s_{k}}\right]\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\mathbb{E}\!\left[\mathbb{E}\!\left[\eta_{k}\langle{\bm{g}(\bm{\theta}_{k-s_{k}},O_{k})-\bar{\bm{g}}(\bm{\theta}_{k-s_{k}})},{\bm{\theta}_{k-s_{k}}-\bm{\theta}^{*}}\rangle\;\middle|\;\mathcal{F}_{k-1}\right]\;\middle|\;\mathcal{F}_{k-1-s_{k}}\right]\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\mathbb{E}\!\left[\eta_{k}\langle{\bm{g}(\bm{\theta}_{k-s_{k}},O_{k})-\bar{\bm{g}}(\bm{\theta}_{k-s_{k}})},{\bm{\theta}_{k-s_{k}}-\bm{\theta}^{*}}\rangle\;\middle|\;\mathcal{F}_{k-1-s_{k}}\right]\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\eta_{k}\langle{\mathbb{E}\!\left[\bm{g}(\bm{\theta}_{k-s_{k}},O_{k})-\bar{\bm{g}}(\bm{\theta}_{k-s_{k}})\;\middle|\;\mathcal{F}_{k-1-s_{k}}\right]},{\bm{\theta}_{k-s_{k}}-\bm{\theta}^{*}}\rangle\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\frac{8d_{k-s_{k}}\ell_{k-s_{k}}C\alpha^{s_{k}}}{\delta\log T\log(k+3)\sqrt{k+1}}\right]

\displaystyle=8C\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}d_{k-s_{k}}\ell_{k-s_{k}}\alpha^{s_{k}}\frac{1}{\delta\log T\log(k+3)\sqrt{k+1}}\right]

\displaystyle=8C\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sum_{k=0}^{u_{t}}d_{0}\ell_{0}\alpha^{s_{k}}\frac{1}{\delta\log T\log(k+3)\sqrt{k+1}}+\sum_{k=u_{t}+1}^{t-1}d_{k-u_{t}}\ell_{k-u_{t}}\alpha^{u_{t}}\frac{1}{\delta\log T\log(k+3)\sqrt{k+1}}\right],

where we use Lemma
A.4
in the second inequality.

Notice that for

.
So, it follows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\eta_{k}\langle{\mathbb{E}\!\left[\bm{g}(\bm{\theta}_{k-s_{k}},O_{k})\;\middle|\;\mathcal{F}_{k-1}\right]-\bar{\bm{g}}(\bm{\theta}_{k-s_{k}})},{\bm{\theta}_{k-s_{k}}-\bm{\theta}^{*}}\rangle\right]

\displaystyle\quad\leq 4C\mathbb{E}\!\left[d_{0}\ell_{0}\sum_{k=0}^{u_{t}}\frac{1}{\delta\log T\log(k+3)\sqrt{k+1}}\right]+4\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sum_{k=u_{t}+1}^{t-1}\frac{d_{k-u_{t}}\ell_{k-u_{t}}}{\delta\log T\log(k+3)\sqrt{k+1}\sqrt{t}}\right]~.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\eta_{k}\langle{\mathbb{E}\!\left[\bm{g}(\bm{\theta}_{k},O_{k})\;\middle|\;\mathcal{F}_{k-1}\right]-\bar{\bm{g}}(\bm{\theta}_{k})},{\bm{\theta}_{k}-\bm{\theta}^{*}}\rangle\right]

\displaystyle\quad-\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\eta_{k}\langle{\mathbb{E}\!\left[\bm{g}(\bm{\theta}_{k-s_{k}},O_{k})\;\middle|\;\mathcal{F}_{k-1}\right]-\bar{\bm{g}}(\bm{\theta}_{k-s_{k}})},{\bm{\theta}_{k-s_{k}}-\bm{\theta}^{*}}\rangle\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\eta_{k}(2\ell_{k}+4\phi^{2}_{\infty}d_{k-s_{k}})\sum_{i=k-s_{k}}^{k-1}\eta_{i}\left\|{\bm{g}_{i}}\right\|\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sum_{k=0}^{u_{t}}\eta_{k}(2\ell_{k}+4\phi_{\infty}^{2}d_{k-s_{k}})\sum_{i=k-s_{k}}^{k-1}\eta_{i}\left\|{\bm{g}_{i}}\right\|\right]+\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sum_{k=u_{t}+1}^{t-1}\eta_{k}(2\ell_{k}+4\phi_{\infty}^{2}d_{k-s_{k}})\sum_{i=k-s_{k}}^{k-1}\eta_{i}\left\|{\bm{g}_{i}}\right\|\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sum_{k=u_{t}+1}^{t-1}\frac{1}{\delta\log T\log(k+3)\sqrt{k+1}}(2\ell_{k}+4\phi_{\infty}^{2}d_{k-u_{t}})\sum_{i=k-u_{t}}^{k-1}\frac{1}{\delta\log T\log(i+3)\sqrt{i+1}}\left\|{\bm{g}_{i}}\right\|\right]

Putting it all together, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\eta_{k}\langle{\bm{b}_{k}},{\bm{\theta}_{k}-\bm{\theta}^{*}}\rangle\right]

\displaystyle\leq 8C\mathbb{E}\!\left[d_{0}\ell_{0}\sum_{k=0}^{u_{t}}\frac{1}{\delta\log T\log(k+3)\sqrt{k+1}}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+8\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sum_{k=u_{t}+1}^{t-1}\frac{d_{k-u_{t}}\ell_{k-u_{t}}}{\delta\log T\log(k+3)\sqrt{k+1}\sqrt{t}}\right]

Lemma B.2
.

Theorem C.1
.

Proof.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\!\left[d^{2}_{t}-d^{2}_{0}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}(d^{2}_{k+1}-d^{2}_{k})\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{4}{\delta^{2}(\log T)^{2}}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sum_{k=0}^{u_{t}}\frac{\ell_{k}+2\phi^{2}_{\infty}d_{0}}{\sqrt{k+1}\log(k+3)}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{\ell_{i-1}}{\log(i+2)\sqrt{i}}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{4}{\delta^{2}(\log T)^{2}}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sum_{k=u_{t}+1}^{t-1}\frac{\ell_{k}+2\phi^{2}_{\infty}d_{k-u_{t}}}{\log(k+3)\log(k-u_{t}+3)\sqrt{k+1}}\sum_{i=k-u_{t}+1}^{k}\frac{\ell_{i-1}}{\sqrt{i}}\right]

in

.

For any
k^{\prime}\leq k\leq t-1
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\!\left[\ell_{k^{\prime}}\ell_{k}\right]

\displaystyle\leq r_{\infty}^{2}\phi_{\infty}^{2}+2r_{\infty}\phi_{\infty}^{3}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left\|{\bm{\theta}_{k}}\right\|\right]+2r_{\infty}\phi_{\infty}^{3}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left\|{\bm{\theta}_{k^{\prime}}}\right\|\right]+2\phi^{4}_{\infty}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left\|{\bm{\theta}_{k}}\right\|^{2}\right]+2\phi^{4}_{\infty}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left\|{\bm{\theta}_{k^{\prime}}}\right\|^{2}\right],

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\!\left[\phi^{2}_{\infty}d_{k^{\prime}}\ell_{k}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\phi^{4}_{\infty}d_{0}^{2}+r_{\infty}^{2}\phi_{\infty}^{2}}{2}+2r_{\infty}\phi_{\infty}^{3}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left\|\bm{\theta}_{k}\right\|\right]+2\phi^{4}_{\infty}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left\|\bm{\theta}_{k}\right\|^{2}\right]+\phi^{4}_{\infty}d_{0}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left\|{\bm{\theta}_{k^{\prime}}}\right\|\right]

\mathbb{E}\!\left[\ell_{k}^{2}\right]

\mathbb{E}\!\left[d_{k^{\prime}}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\!\left[\ell^{2}_{k}\right]

\displaystyle 2\mathbb{E}\!\left[\ell_{k^{\prime}}\ell_{k}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\!\left[\ell^{2}_{k}\right]+\mathbb{E}\!\left[\ell_{k^{\prime}}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq 2r_{\infty}^{2}\phi_{\infty}^{2}+4r_{\infty}\phi_{\infty}^{3}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left\|{\bm{\theta}_{k}}\right\|\right]+4r_{\infty}\phi_{\infty}^{3}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left\|{\bm{\theta}_{k^{\prime}}}\right\|\right]+4\phi^{4}_{\infty}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left\|{\bm{\theta}_{k}}\right\|^{2}\right]+4\phi^{4}_{\infty}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left\|{\bm{\theta}_{k^{\prime}}}\right\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\!\left[\ell^{2}_{k}\right]+\mathbb{E}\!\left[\phi^{4}_{\infty}d_{k^{\prime}}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\phi^{4}_{\infty}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left\|{\bm{\theta}_{k^{\prime}}}\right\|^{2}\right]~.\qed

Using the lemma and the induction hypothesis, we have

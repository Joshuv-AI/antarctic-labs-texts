Characterizing and harnessing correlations featuring independent qubit devices
Liang-Liang Sun, Xiang Zhou, Chengjie Zhang, Zizhu Wang, Yong-Shun Song, Sixia Yu

Abstract
We propose a framework to characterize the correlations in qubit systems for Bell and prepare-and-measure scenarios with independent devices—a typically non-convex problem. Based on this result, we introduce protocols for referring devices and detecting entanglement with correlation that are not necessarily extreme or nonlocal, as required by common linear approach. Specifically, our correlation criterion, derived from uncertainty relation specific to qubit systems, can capture the non-convex nature of the set of correlations arising from Bell and prepare-and-measure scenarios, as demonstrated through concrete examples. Conversely, when given an observed correlation, our framework can refer potential measurements and quantum states —which are sometimes uniquely determined—even with correlations that are not extreme. This extends common protocols that merely verify devices using extreme correlations. We then enhance entanglement detection for qubit system by incorporating the inferred information in Navascués-Pironio-Acín (NPA) hierarchy, showing that some local correlations can also verify entanglement. Since the scenarios considered here are standard platforms for most quantum information protocols and device inference is a central issue in quantum information science, our methodology, which is well-suited to these tasks, may provide a foundation for a broad range of applications.

I.1
Characterizing Correlations in PM scenario

We first consider a general PM scenario, where a source randomly prepares a quantum state
\rho\in\Omega
which is transmitted to a measurement device. The measurement device can randomly choose a measurement setting
A_{i}\in\mathcal{O}
to perform, producing outcome
a
with probability
p(a|A_{i},\rho)
. As a warmup, let
A_{i}
be binary and projection valued measure (PVM) assuming the form
A_{i}=\vec{a}_{i}\cdot\vec{\sigma}
where
\vec{a}_{i}
is a unit vector (i.e.,
|\vec{a}_{i}|=1
) and
\vec{\sigma}
denotes the Pauli vector, and the expectation value is denoted by
A_{i|\rho}=p(0|A_{i},\rho)-p(1|A_{i},\rho)
. For two such PVMs measurement
A_{i}
and
A_{j}
, their expectation values respect the uncertainty relation
Li and Qiao 2015

A_{i|\rho}^{2}+A_{j|\rho}^{2}+c_{ij}^{2}-2c_{ij}A_{i|\rho}A_{j|\rho}\leq 1,

where the angle
c_{ij}\equiv\vec{a}_{i}\cdot\vec{a}_{j}
between measurements specifies these PVMs up to a unitary operation. As there must exist
c_{ij}
such that the inequality stands for observables’ expectation values over all states
\rho\in\Omega
, we then have a criterion for correlation in the
PM scenario.

Theorem 1
.

If a correlation
\{p(a|A_{i,j},\rho)\mid\rho\in\Omega\}_{a}
arising from a PM scenario uses binary PVMs
A_{i}
and
A_{j}
on two-dimensional systems with independent devices, one has

\displaystyle\max_{\rho\in\Omega}g_{-}(A_{i},A_{j},\rho)\leq\min_{\rho\in\Omega}g_{+}(A_{i},A_{j},\rho),

(1)

where
g_{\pm}(A_{i},A_{j},\rho)\equiv{\Pi}_{l=i,j}A_{l|\rho}\pm{\Pi}_{l=i,j}\sqrt{1-A_{i|\rho}^{2}}
. Otherwise, correlation cannot be generated by independent qubit systems.

General binary qubit measurements are described by positive operator-valued measures (POVMs)
\{\frac{1\pm A_{i}}{2}\}
with
A_{i}=r_{i}^{\prime}\vec{a}_{i}\cdot\vec{\sigma}+r_{i}\mathbb{I}
, where
\vec{a}_{i}
is a unit vector (
|\vec{a}_{i}|=1
) and two real numbers

0\leq r_{i}^{\prime}\leq 1
and
-1\leq r_{i}\leq 1
satisfy
r^{\prime}_{i}+|r_{i}|\leq 1
to ensure
-1\leq A_{i|\rho}\leq 1
. We note that the normalized operator
(A_{i}-r_{i}\mathbb{I})/r_{i}^{\prime}
corresponds to a PVM. Therefore, following the same methodology as in the PVM case, we can derive an analogous correlation criterion for general POVMs in qubit systems (see SM).

Theorem 2
.

If a correlation
\{p(a|A_{i,j},\rho)\mid\rho\in\Omega\}_{a}
arising from a PM scenario uses independent two-dimensional binary POVMs
A_{i}
and
A_{j}
, there must exist two real numbers
-1\leq r_{i,j}\leq 1
independent of the state
\rho
such that

\displaystyle\max_{\rho\in\Omega}g^{(r_{i,j})}_{-}(A_{i},A_{j},\rho)

\displaystyle\leq\min_{\rho\in\Omega}g^{(r_{i,j})}_{+}(A_{i},A_{j},\rho),

(2)

with

\displaystyle g^{({r}_{i,j})}_{\pm}(A_{i},A_{j},\rho)

\displaystyle={\Pi}_{l=i,j}(A_{l|\rho}-r_{l})

\displaystyle\quad\pm{\Pi}_{l=i,j}\sqrt{{\bar{r}_{l}}^{2}-(A_{l|\rho}-r_{l})^{2}}

with
\bar{r_{l}}=1-|r_{l}|
.

There are some correlations that can be generated using POVMs but not by PVMs. As an example we consider correlations
A_{0|\rho_{0}}=A_{1|\rho_{0}}=1
and
A_{0|\rho_{1}}=-A_{1|\rho_{1}}=-1
. They can be generated by preparing states in
\Omega=\{\rho_{1}=|0\rangle\langle 0|,\rho_{2}=|1\rangle\langle 1|\}
and performing general measurements in
\mathcal{O}=\{A_{0}=\mathbb{I},A_{1}=\sigma_{z}\}
. However, they cannot be generated by PVMs because the criterion for PVMs is violated as
\min_{\rho\in\Omega}\left\{g_{+}(A_{i},A_{j},\rho)\right\}=-1
and
\max_{\rho\in\Omega}\left\{g_{-}(A_{i},A_{j},\rho)\right\}=1
.

II
Inference of quantum measurements and states with correlations

A central challenge in quantum information science is how to efficiently characterize quantum devices. Standard tomography methods require completely trusted states for characterizing measurements, and trusted measurements for state characterization. An alternative that alleviates these stringent requirements is the verification of devices using quantum correlations, which is motivated by the observation that there are extreme correlations specific to unique devices. Thus, if such extreme correlations are generated, devices can be verified. To design such protocols,
one needs to know the devices a priori, and then gives a yes or no answer about whether the devices work as intended. If the answer is not, almost no other information about these devices can be extracted. In the following, we introduce the protocols based on correlations featuring independent devices. This method, which we term "device referring," requires no prior device information for its design, yet is capable of yielding non-trivial information about devices using general correlations.

Our main idea is that, given the experimental statistics
\{A_{i|\rho}\}_{\rho\in\Omega,i}
featuring independent devices, which may come directly from experiments in a PM scenario or be derived from a correlation in Bell’s scenario, one can find
r_{i,j}
,
r^{\prime}_{i,j}
, and
c_{ij}
that are compatible with the statistics. This can be done in two steps: first, bounding
r_{i,j}
by solving the inequality (see the derivation of criterion given in SM)

\min_{\rho\in\Omega}g^{(r_{i,j})}_{-}\leq\max_{\rho\in\Omega}g^{(r_{i,j})}_{+}.

This gives rise to some possibilities of
r_{i,j}
. For each of such possibilities, by solving the inequality

\min g^{(r_{i,j},r^{\prime}_{i,j})}_{-}\leq c_{ij}r_{i}^{\prime}r_{j}^{\prime}\leq\max g^{(r_{i,j},r^{\prime}_{i,j})}_{+},

(4)

where

\begin{split}g^{(r_{i,j},r^{\prime}_{i,j})}_{\pm}(A_{i},A_{j},\rho)&\equiv{\Pi}_{l=i,j}(A_{l|\rho}-r_{l})\\
&\quad\pm{\Pi}_{l=i,j}\sqrt{r^{\prime 2}_{l}-(A_{l|\rho}-r_{l})^{2}},\end{split}

(5)

which is slightly different from
g^{(r_{i,j})}_{\pm}(A_{i},A_{j},\rho)
. We therefore confine
c_{ij}
and
r_{i,j}
to a possible region for each pair of the possible
r_{i,j}
. Thus, potential measurements are confined into some possibilities and thus inferred.

As an example, we have considered the correlations lying on the boundary of
x-y
plane of
Q^{PM}_{xy}
and, uniquely determined measurements
A_{0}=\sigma_{z}
,
A_{1}=r_{1}c_{01}\sigma_{z}+r_{1}{\sqrt{1-c^{2}_{01}}}\sigma_{y}+(1-r_{1})\openone
up to a unitary, where the analytical expressions of
c_{01}(x)
and
r_{1}(x)
are given in SM. Note that these correlations are non-extreme (except end points) but can uniquely determine measurements.

In the above analysis, we have used correlations to infer the parameters of measurements. The obtained information can be used to further infer the state
\rho\in\Omega
. Here, we only need to consider projective measurements, as non-projective measurements can be transformed into projective ones. Consider a qubit state
\rho=\frac{1+\vec{s}\cdot\vec{\sigma}}{2}
. Performing two projective measurements
A_{0}=\vec{a}_{0}\cdot\vec{\sigma}
and
A_{1}=\vec{a}_{1}\cdot\vec{\sigma}
on this state yields the expectation values
A_{0|\rho}
and
A_{1|\rho}
. As long as the uncertainty relation is satisfied, the possible Bloch vector
\vec{s}
reads (see SM)

\displaystyle\vec{s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(A_{0|\rho}\vec{a}_{1}-A_{1|\rho}\vec{a}_{0})\times(\vec{a}_{0}\times\vec{a}_{1})+t(\vec{a}_{0}\times\vec{a}_{1})}{(\vec{a}_{0}\times\vec{a}_{1})^{2}}

where the additional parameter satisfying

|t|\leq\sqrt{(1-A_{0|\rho}^{2})(1-A_{1|\rho}^{2})-(\vec{a}_{0}\cdot\vec{a}_{1}-A_{0|\rho}A_{1|\rho})^{2}}

can be certified with another measurement incompatible with
A_{0}
and
A_{1}
. Specifically, for the boundary correlation
Q^{PM}_{xy}
, three of the four states in
\Omega
are uniquely determined, while the fourth is determined up to one free parameter (
t
) as given in the SM. In Bell’s scenario, our criterion is not tight as it does not incorporate the additional requirement needed to reconstruct a global state with local conditional states; a typical one is the non-signaling condition, namely,
\sum_{\mathbf{a}_{\bar{m}}}p(\mathbf{a}_{\bar{m}}|\mathbf{s}_{\bar{m}})\rho_{\mathbf{a}_{\bar{m}}|\mathbf{s}_{\bar{m}}}
is independent of
{\bf{s}}_{\bar{m}}
.

Here, one may suggest a brute computation approach to determine these parameters, namely, parameterizing the states and measurements and then requiring them to be compatible with the distributions obtained in experiments. This approach requires solving multivariate quadratic equations that are NP hard. Moreover, the number of parameters grows rapidly, making this approach soon become inapplicable. In contrast,
within our framework, we determine measurements’ parameters in different steps; in each step one needs deal with no more than three parameters for pair-wise measurements. This means our approach can determines these parameters within polynomial time. Then, one can use these parameters to determine the states. This approach significantly simplifies the verification of systems.

We compare the protocols introduced here with the common protocols utilizing correlations allowing shared random numbers. First, the common protocols are to verify devices by exploiting extreme correlations specific to unique devices using linear techniques. Our protocols exploit nonlinear constraint due to uncertainty relations, where devices could be uniquely determined when
\max_{\rho\in\Omega}g^{(r_{i,j})}_{-}(A_{i},A_{j},\rho)\leq\min_{\rho\in\Omega}g^{(r_{i,j})}_{+}(A_{i},A_{j},\rho)
corresponding to unique
c
and
r_{i,j}
, namely, the inequality (uncertainty relation) are saturated. Without the need to identify such extreme correlations, our protocols can be very simple. For example, the minimal verification protocol in the PM scenario requires four states and three measurements

. However, within our framework, the minimal protocol consists of two states and two measurements. For instance, let

A_{0,\rho_{0}}=A_{1,\rho_{0}}=1,\quad A_{0,\rho_{1}}=-A_{1,\rho_{1}}=1
, our criterion is satisfied only when
A_{0}=\openone
and a projective measurement
A_{1}
, with the two states being the eigenvectors of
A_{1}
. Second, uncertainty relations are intimately related to measurements. This allows us to infer measurement properties in a more direct manner. See that to verify non-projective measurements using extreme correlations

;
Tavakoli et al. 2020

Gómez et al. 2016
;
Tavakoli et al. 2020
;
Smania et al. 2020
. In contrast, within our framework, a measurement is non-projective if and only if
r_{i}\neq 1
, which is straightforward to check.

III
Optimization of entanglement detection

In this section, we consider Bell’s scenario and optimize the detection of the entanglement of qubit systems via incorporating the measurements’ information inferred in the above section into the NPA hierarchy. The NPA hierarchy
Navascués et al. 2007
is a sequence of tests designed to characterize correlations that can be realized with a quantum system. It considers a set
\tilde{O}=\{O_{e}\}
of operators
O_{e}
, e.g., some products of the measurement operators
\{M^{a_{i}}_{s_{i}}\}
or linear combinations of them. Then their Gram matrix
\Gamma
defined with the entries
\Gamma_{ef}\equiv{\rm Tr}(\rho O^{\dagger}_{e}O_{f})
is positive semi-definite. Entries relevant to observable statistics and thus having operational meaning are referred to as physical terms, while the remaining ones involve non-commuting quantities and are called non-physical terms. One commonly considers a real grammar matrix,
\Gamma^{\prime}=(\Gamma+\Gamma^{*})/2
, where the asterisk specifies the complex conjugate. If a correlation can be generated with quantum systems, one can assign some real values on non-physical terms of the matrix
\Gamma^{\prime}
such that it is positive.

Here, we illustrate our idea by optimizing the detection of entanglement between the
m
-th partite and the others and leave a schematic research elsewhere.
Consider that the
m
-th party holds a two-dimensional quantum system.
We relabel the elements of
\tilde{O}
according to this partition as
O_{e,f}=O^{(m)}_{e}\otimes O^{(\bar{m})}_{f}
, where
O^{(m)}_{e}
involves measurement operators acting on the quantum system of the
m
-th party while
O^{(\bar{m})}_{f}
for remaining parties. Then, we can define a matrix with the entry relevant to
O_{ef}
and
O_{e^{\prime}f^{\prime}}
as

\begin{split}4\Gamma_{ee^{\prime},ff^{\prime}}=&{\rm Tr}\left[\rho\left(O^{(m)\dagger}_{e}{O}^{(m)}_{e^{\prime}}+O^{(m)\dagger}_{e^{\prime}}{O}^{(m)}_{e}\right)\right.\\
&\quad\left.{}\times\left(O^{(\bar{m})\dagger}_{f}{O}^{(\bar{m})}_{f^{\prime}}+O^{(\bar{m})\dagger}_{f^{\prime}}{O}^{(\bar{m})}_{f}\right)\right].\end{split}

(6)

Considering a product state
\rho=\rho^{(m)}\otimes\rho^{(\bar{m})}
, we have

\begin{split}4\Gamma_{ee^{\prime},ff^{\prime}}&={\rm Tr}\left[\rho^{(m)}\left(O^{(m)\dagger}_{e}{O}^{(m)}_{e^{\prime}}+O^{(m)\dagger}_{e^{\prime}}{O}^{(m)}_{e}\right)\right]\\
&\quad\times{\rm Tr}\left[\rho^{\bar{m}}\left(O^{(\bar{m})\dagger}_{f}{O}^{(\bar{m})}_{f^{\prime}}+O^{(\bar{m})\dagger}_{f^{\prime}}{O}^{(\bar{m})}_{f}\right)\right]\\
&=\Gamma^{(m)}_{ee^{\prime}}\Gamma^{(\bar{m})}_{ff^{\prime}},\quad\text{i.e., }\Gamma=\Gamma^{(m)}\otimes\Gamma^{(\bar{m})}.\end{split}

(7)

As
\Gamma^{(m)}
and
\Gamma^{(\bar{m})}
are positive, we have
\Gamma\geq 0
.
For a general separable state

, the convex combination of the relevant matrices

is also positive, which is not the case for entangled states.
To incorporate the information of measurement, let us consider, for instance, one and a half order of NPA, where
\tilde{O}=\{{\openone},A^{(m)}_{\alpha},B^{(\bar{m})}_{\beta},A^{(m)}_{\alpha}B^{(\bar{m})}_{\beta}\}
,

are two-dimensional, we have
A^{\dagger}_{0}A_{1}+A^{\dagger}_{1}A_{0}=2(r^{\prime}_{1}A_{0}+r^{\prime}_{0}A_{1}+(r_{0}r_{1}c_{01}+{r}^{\prime}_{0}r^{\prime}_{1})\openone)
, and this expectation value consists solely of operational terms and constants certified in the last section. If the state is separable, there must exist parameters
r_{i}
,
r^{\prime}_{i}
and
c
s such that the resulted matrix remains positive semi-definite and entangled otherwise. As an example, we provide a local correlation in minimal Bell’s scenario to certify entanglement. The correlation is defined with the expectation values
\overline{A_{0}B_{0}}=\overline{A_{1}B_{1}}=1
and
\overline{A_{0}B_{1}}=\overline{A_{1}B_{0}}=\cos\frac{\pi}{12}
and
\bar{A}_{0}=\bar{A}_{1}=\bar{B}_{1}=\bar{B}_{2}=0
, where
\overline{O}\equiv{\rm Tr}(\rho O)
. On Alice’s side, there are four conditional states
\rho_{b|\beta}
, corresponding to which, the expectation values
\{A_{0,\rho},A_{1,\rho}\}
are,
\{1,\cos\frac{\pi}{12}\}
,
\{-1,-\cos\frac{\pi}{12}\}
,
\{\cos\frac{\pi}{12},1\}
,

Assuming qubit systems, our criterion can uniquely determine these measurements
A_{\alpha}=\cos\theta_{\alpha}\sigma_{z}+\sin\theta_{\alpha}\sigma_{x}
and
B_{\beta}=\cos\theta^{\prime}_{\beta}\sigma_{z}+\sin\theta^{\prime}_{\beta}\sigma_{x}

In conclusion, we provide an approach to characterize correlations featuring independent qubit systems (the most basic experimental setting) and to refer potential measurements and states for such a correlation. These results have twofold significance. As a criterion, it applies to both the Bell and PM scenarios and is efficient enough to characterize explicit non-convex boundaries of quantum correlations, as demonstrated through concrete examples. As a tool to infer devices, our framework applies to general correlations and can infer the parameters of measurements and states by confining them to a possible region. This overcomes the limitations of the common methods that are restricted in verifying devices using extreme correlations. Our result can also optimize the detection of entanglement in qubit systems, for which nonlocal correlation is not a necessary requirement. These applications are essential in foundational and quantum information science; our results thus may enjoy wide interest.

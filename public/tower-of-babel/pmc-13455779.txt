Unaddressed Analytical Interference and Target Verification Gaps in Shuyu Wan–Mediated Lung Cancer Immunotherapy [Letter]
Authors: Hangxing Pan, Yi Wang, Aiguo Meng
Published: 2026

Dear editor

We read with great interest the study by Sang et al,1 which proposes that Shuyu Wan (SYW) potentiates PD-1 inhibitor efficacy via gut microbiota–tumor immune microenvironment (GM-TME) crosstalk. While the integrated bioinformatics approach provides initial insights, we identify critical methodological limitations regarding computational validation and experimental design that require further scrutiny.

The network pharmacology and molecular docking components raise concerns about false-positive predictions. The authors identified 200 overlapping targets between SYW and NSCLC and reported binding energies ≤ −7 kcal/mol as evidence of strong affinity. However, blind docking methods have been inappropriately used in most network pharmacology analyses, frequently generating false-positive results due to the absence of defined binding pockets and inadequate conformational sampling.2 The lack of molecular dynamics simulations for binding stability validation further limits the interpretability of these in silico predictions. Without experimental validation of at least a subset of these predicted component–target interactions (eg, surface plasmon resonance or cellular thermal shift assays), the bioinformatics findings remain speculative rather than mechanistic.

A fundamental confounder lies in the ABX experiment design used to establish causality. The authors attribute the loss of therapeutic efficacy in the ABX group solely to the absence of GM-TME crosstalk. However, this overlooks the essential role of gut microbiota in the pharmacokinetics of oral Traditional Chinese Medicine. Commensal bacteria are well-established regulators of herbal compound bioavailability, responsible for the biotransformation of glycosides into absorbable aglycones.3 Given that SYW was administered intragastrically, the eradication of gut flora by the antibiotic cocktail likely impaired the absorption and systemic bioavailability of SYW’s active components (eg, ginsenosides).4 Without measuring serum concentrations of key SYW constituents, one cannot distinguish whether the treatment failure in the ABX group resulted from a lack of immune sensitization or simply from a “drug non-availability” state. Therefore, the ABX data cannot definitively prove that GM is the immunologic mediator rather than a pharmacologic prerequisite.

Moreover, while the FMT experiment provides causal evidence for microbiota dependence, the use of antibiotics (ampicillin, neomycin, metronidazole, vancomycin) depletes not only gut bacteria but also affects systemic immunity and intestinal barrier function independently of microbiota composition. A more rigorous approach would include a sterile filtrate control to distinguish between microbial cell-dependent and metabolite-dependent effects.

Clarifying these pharmacokinetic confounders and validating the computational predictions are essential before the proposed mechanism can be accepted. Future studies should incorporate pharmacokinetic profiling in antibiotic-treated models and utilize rigorous experimental verification of component-target interactions to solidify the evidence base for SYW in NSCLC therapy.

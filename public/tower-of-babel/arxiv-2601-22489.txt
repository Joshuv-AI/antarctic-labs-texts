Structural Conditions for Native CCZ Magic-State Fountains in qLDPC Codes
Mohammad Rowshan

Abstract
Quantum low-density parity-check (qLDPC) codes promise constant-rate, linear-distance families with bounded-weight checks, and recent work has realized transversal or constant-depth non-Clifford gates on various (often non-LDPC) codes. However, no explicit qubit qLDPC family is known that simultaneously has constant rate, linear distance, bounded stabilizer weight, and a native magic-state fountain that prepares many non-Clifford resource states in constant depth. We take a structural approach and identify coding-theoretic conditions under which a CSS qLDPC family necessarily supports a constant-depth 𝖢𝖢𝖹 \mathsf{CCZ} magic-state fountain. The key ingredients are: (i) an algebraic notion of magic-friendly triples of X X -type logical operators, defined by pairwise orthogonality and a triple-overlap form controlling diagonal 𝖢𝖢𝖹 \mathsf{CCZ} phases, and (ii) a 3-uniform hypergraph model of physical 𝖢𝖢𝖹 \mathsf{CCZ} circuits combined with a packing lemma that turns large collections of such triples with bounded overlaps into bounded-degree hypergraphs. Our main theorem shows that if a CSS code family on n n qubits admits Ω ⁡ ( n 1 + γ ) \Omega(n^{1+\gamma}) magic-friendly triples whose supports have bounded per-qubit participation, then there exists a constant-depth circuit of physical 𝖢𝖢𝖹 \mathsf{CCZ} gates implementing Ω ⁡ ( n γ ) \Omega(n^{\gamma}) logical 𝖢𝖢𝖹 \mathsf{CCZ} gates in parallel while preserving distance up to a constant factor. For asymptotically good qLDPC families such as quantum Tanner codes, this reduces the existence of a native 𝖢𝖢𝖹 \mathsf{CCZ} magic-state fountain to a concrete combinatorial problem about counting and distributing magic-friendly triples in the logical X X space.

CSS codes, triple overlaps, and magic-friendly triples.
Let
C_{X},C_{Z}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
be binary linear codes with
C_{Z}\subseteq C_{X}^{\perp}
. A CSS code
\mathcal{Q}
on
n
qubits is defined by
X
-type stabilizers from a parity-check matrix of
C_{Z}
and
Z
-type stabilizers from a parity-check matrix of
C_{X}
. The code encodes

k=n-\operatorname{rank}H_{X}-\operatorname{rank}H_{Z}

(1)

logical qubits, and its
X
-type logical operators correspond to cosets in

\mathcal{L}_{X}=C_{Z}^{\perp}/C_{X},

(2)

where
C_{Z}^{\perp}
denotes the orthogonal complement of
C_{Z}
with respect to the standard inner product on
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
.

We call
\mathcal{Q}
a qLDPC code if both
H_{X}
and
H_{Z}
have bounded row and column weights, independent of
n
. A family
\{\mathcal{Q}_{n}\}
is
asymptotically good
if
k_{n}\geq Rn
and
d_{n}\geq\delta n
for constants
R,\delta>0
.

For
x,y,z\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
, define the
triple overlap

\tau(x,y,z)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}y_{i}z_{i}\pmod{2},

(3)

which counts modulo 2 the positions where all three vectors have a 1.

Consider three unencoded computational basis strings
x,y,z\in\{0,1\}^{n}
and

|\psi\rangle=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}|x_{i}\rangle\otimes|y_{i}\rangle\otimes|z_{i}\rangle.

(4)

Applying a physical
\mathsf{CCZ}
gate to each triple
(i,i,i)
multiplies
|\psi\rangle
by
(-1)^{\tau(x,y,z)}
; the proof is given in Appendix
A
. Thus the triple overlap completely controls the phase imparted by a wirewise
\mathsf{CCZ}
layer. When
x,y,z
represent
X
-type logical operators (i.e.
x,y,z\in C_{Z}^{\perp}
), this relation is the starting point for diagonal logical
\mathsf{CCZ}
gates
4
;
12
;
15
;
28
.

We isolate a simple algebraic condition on triples of logical
X
operators that is necessary (and in many constructions close to sufficient) for a nontrivial logical
\mathsf{CCZ}
action.

Definition 1

(Magic-friendly triple)
.

Let
\mathcal{Q}
be a CSS code with
C_{Z}^{\perp}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
. A triple
(x,y,z)\in(C_{Z}^{\perp})^{3}
is
magic-friendly
if:

1.

x,y,z
represent three independent logical
X
operators, i.e., their images in
\mathcal{L}_{X}
are linearly independent;

2.

pairwise orthogonality holds:

\langle x,y\rangle=\langle x,z\rangle=\langle y,z\rangle=0\pmod{2};

(5)

3.

the triple overlap is odd:

\tau(x,y,z)=1\pmod{2}.

(6)

Remark 1

(Logical
Z
corrections)
.

In more general CSS constructions, one may allow
\langle x^{(a)},x^{(b)}\rangle\neq 0
for some pairs of logical
X
operators, in which case a transversal application of
\mathsf{CCZ}
induces additional logical
Z
-type Clifford corrections on spectator logical qubits. This is the familiar situation in triorthogonal and generalized divisible codes
6
;
14
. In our setting we impose
\langle x,y\rangle=\langle x,z\rangle=\langle y,z\rangle=0
for all participating logicals in a magic-friendly triple. As a result, the only non-trivial logical phase comes from the odd triple overlap, and a depth-one layer of physical
\mathsf{CCZ}
on representatives of
x,y,z
acts as the desired logical
\mathsf{CCZ}
on the three chosen logical qubits, up to a fixed Clifford frame that can be tracked classically.

Hypergraph model and bounded-depth
\mathsf{CCZ}
circuits.
Let
[n]=\{1,\dots,n\}
index physical qubits. A layer of
\mathsf{CCZ}
gates is specified by triples
E\subseteq\binom{[n]}{3}
.

Definition 2

(3-uniform hypergraph and degree)
.

A 3-uniform hypergraph on
[n]
is a pair
H=(V,E)
with
V=[n]
and
E\subseteq\binom{V}{3}
. The degree of
v\in V
is

\deg_{H}(v)=|\{e\in E:v\in e\}|,

(7)

and the maximum degree is
\Delta(H)=\max_{v\in V}\deg_{H}(v)
.

A
\mathsf{CCZ}
circuit is called
bounded-degree
if the associated hypergraph
H
has
\Delta(H)\leq\Delta
for some constant
\Delta
independent of
n
.

To enforce that no two gates in a given time step share a qubit, we use edge coloring.

Lemma 1

(Edge coloring and constant depth)
.

Let
H=(V,E)
be a 3-uniform hypergraph with
\Delta(H)\leq\Delta
. Then
H
admits an edge coloring with at most
3\Delta+1
colors, and hence there exists a
\mathsf{CCZ}
circuit of depth at most
3\Delta+1
that applies
\mathsf{CCZ}
on all edges in
E
.

A proof is given in Appendix
B

\mathsf{CCZ}

Main structural theorem.
We now combine the hypergraph and packing viewpoints.

Theorem 1

(Native constant-depth
\mathsf{CCZ}
from many magic-friendly triples)
.

Let
\{\mathcal{Q}_{n}\}
be a family of CSS codes on
n
qubits with distances
d_{n}
and
X
-logical spaces
\mathcal{L}_{X}^{(n)}=(C_{Z}^{(n)})^{\perp}/C_{X}^{(n)}
. For each
n
, let
\mathcal{S}_{n}
be a set of magic-friendly triples
t=(x_{t},y_{t},z_{t})\in((C_{Z}^{(n)})^{\perp})^{3}
satisfying:

1.

(Many candidates)
|\mathcal{S}_{n}|\geq c_{1}n^{1+\gamma}
for some constants
c_{1}>0
,
\gamma>0
.

2.

(Support size) There exist constants
0<a\leq b
such that

an\leq|S_{t}|\leq bn
for all
t\in\mathcal{S}_{n}
.

3.

(Bounded participation) There is
M>0
such that each coordinate
i\in[n]
belongs to at most
M
sets
S_{t}
with
t\in\mathcal{S}_{n}
.

Then there exist constants
\beta>0
,
\Delta
,
c>0
, and, for each
n
, a collection of triples
T_{n}\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{n}
with
|T_{n}|\geq cn^{\beta}
and a bounded-degree 3-uniform hypergraph of physical
\mathsf{CCZ}
gates of maximum degree
\Delta
such that:

•

there is a constant-depth circuit of physical
\mathsf{CCZ}
gates that implements logical
\mathsf{CCZ}
gates on all triples in
T_{n}
in parallel, up to logical Clifford operations;

•

if
d_{n}=\Theta(n)
, the effective distance of
\mathcal{Q}_{n}
under this circuit remains
\Theta(n)
.

In particular, the family
\{\mathcal{Q}_{n}\}
admits a native constant-depth
\mathsf{CCZ}
magic-state fountain with throughput
|T_{n}|=\Omega(n^{\beta})
.

A full proof is given in Appendix
E
. The distance statement uses a general light-cone lemma for constant-depth local circuits, proved in Appendix
D
.

Implications, contributions, and open problems.
Theorem
1
shows that the bottleneck for native
\mathsf{CCZ}
magic-state fountains on CSS (and in particular qLDPC) codes is purely
structural
: one needs many magic-friendly triples of logical
X
operators whose supports do not cluster excessively on a small set of physical qubits. If such triples exist, a constant-depth circuit of physical
\mathsf{CCZ}
gates implementing a polynomial number of logical
\mathsf{CCZ}
gates in parallel is guaranteed, and the code distance degrades by at most a constant factor.

Beyond the theorem itself, our work has three concrete contributions:

1.

It isolates a precise combinatorial property of the logical
X
space that any qLDPC-based magic-state fountain must exploit, namely the existence of a large, low-overlap packing of magic-friendly triples. This unifies and generalizes earlier constructions based on triorthogonal matrices and cup-product structures
6
;
12
;
28
into a single algebraic condition formulated directly at the level of logical operators.

2.

It recasts the search for native non-Clifford gates on qLDPC codes as an explicit problem in the combinatorics of high-dimensional expanders: prove that a given asymptotically good qLDPC family admits
\Omega(k)
magic-friendly triples with bounded participation
M=O(1)
, and Theorem
1
immediately yields an asymptotically good magic-state fountain with LDPC checks and linear distance. This suggests a new interface between quantum coding theory and the expanding toolbox of expander and Tanner-code techniques
20
;
9
.

3.

It provides a reusable blueprint for turning structural information about logical operators into concrete bounded-degree non-Clifford circuits: the combination of packing (Lemma
2
), hypergraph edge-coloring (Lemma
1
), and the distance-preservation lemma (Lemma
4
) yields a general recipe for designing constant-depth diagonal circuits (here,
\mathsf{CCZ}
layers) with provable distance guarantees. We expect the same pattern to apply to more general multi-controlled-
Z
magic-state factories and to other families of product-based qLDPC codes.

We emphasize that our result is a
reduction
: we do not prove here that existing good qLDPC families, such as quantum Tanner codes
20
or the product-based qLDPC codes of Refs.
13
;
21
;
28
, actually realize the required abundance of magic-friendly triples with bounded participation. Establishing such bounds is an open combinatorial problem. For expander-based constructions, the underlying high-dimensional expansion suggests that logical operators are well-spread over physical qubits; making this precise for magic-friendly triples would immediately imply a native constant-depth
\mathsf{CCZ}
magic-state fountain on those families.

Appendix A
Phase from triple overlap

We prove that a layer of physical
\mathsf{CCZ}
gates imparts a phase
(-1)^{\tau(x,y,z)}
determined by the triple overlap.

Lemma 3
.

Let
x,y,z\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
, and

|\psi\rangle=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}|x_{i}\rangle\otimes|y_{i}\rangle\otimes|z_{i}\rangle,

where
|0\rangle
and
|1\rangle
are computational basis states. Then

\left(\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}\mathsf{CCZ}_{(i)}\right)|\psi\rangle=(-1)^{\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}y_{i}z_{i}}|\psi\rangle.

Proof.

A single
\mathsf{CCZ}
gate on three qubits applies a phase
-1
if and only if all three input bits are 1, and leaves the state unchanged otherwise. Thus, on a basis state
|a,b,c\rangle
with
a,b,c\in\{0,1\}
we have

\mathsf{CCZ}|a,b,c\rangle=(-1)^{abc}|a,b,c\rangle,

where the product is in
\mathbb{F}_{2}
but we interpret
abc\in\{0,1\}
in the exponent. On the
i
th triple
(x_{i},y_{i},z_{i})
, the local phase is
(-1)^{x_{i}y_{i}z_{i}}
.

The full circuit is a tensor product of
\mathsf{CCZ}
gates on each triple
(i,i,i)
, so the global phase is the product of local phases:

\prod_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{x_{i}y_{i}z_{i}}=(-1)^{\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}y_{i}z_{i}}.

(12)

No amplitudes are permuted, so the basis state is mapped to itself times this phase. This is exactly
(-1)^{\tau(x,y,z)}
.
∎

Proof of Lemma

.

Order the edges of
H
arbitrarily as
e_{1},e_{2},\dots,e_{|E|}
. We assign colors greedily from a palette
\{1,2,\dots,3\Delta+1\}
.

Suppose we have colored
e_{1},\dots,e_{t-1}
, and consider edge
e_{t}=\{i,j,k\}
. Any edge adjacent to
e_{t}
shares at least one of the vertices
i,j,k
. For a fixed vertex
v\in\{i,j,k\}
, there are at most
\deg_{H}(v)-1\leq\Delta-1
other edges containing
v
. Thus the total number of edges adjacent to
e_{t}
is at most

(\Delta-1)+(\Delta-1)+(\Delta-1)=3(\Delta-1).

(13)

Each adjacent edge may exclude one color from the palette (its assigned color). Therefore, at most
3(\Delta-1)
colors are forbidden for
e_{t}
. Since the palette has
3\Delta+1
colors, the number of available colors is at least

(3\Delta+1)-3(\Delta-1)=4.

(14)

We can choose any one of these available colors for
e_{t}
.

Proceeding in this way for
t=1,2,\dots,|E|
yields an edge coloring
\chi:E\to\{1,\dots,3\Delta+1\}
such that adjacent edges receive different colors. This is a proper edge coloring.

To interpret this as a
\mathsf{CCZ}
circuit, let
E_{c}=\{e\in E:\chi(e)=c\}
for each color
c
. By properness, edges in
E_{c}
are pairwise disjoint. Thus all
\mathsf{CCZ}
gates on edges in
E_{c}
can be applied in parallel in one time step. The circuit that, for
c=1,\dots,3\Delta+1
, applies
\mathsf{CCZ}
on all edges in
E_{c}
in layer
c
has depth at most
3\Delta+1
and applies
\mathsf{CCZ}
on every edge of
H
exactly once.

Proof of Lemma
2
.

We construct
\mathcal{T}
greedily. Start with
\mathcal{T}=\emptyset
and mark all coordinates
1,\dots,n
as unused.

Selection rule.
While there exists a triple
t\in\mathcal{S}
whose support
S_{t}
intersects only unused coordinates, select such a triple
t
(any choice suffices), and:

1.

add
t
to
\mathcal{T}
;

2.

mark all coordinates in
S_{t}
as used;

3.

remove from
\mathcal{S}
every triple
t^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}
for which
S_{t^{\prime}}\cap S_{t}\neq\emptyset
.

We stop when no triple with support contained in the unused coordinates remains. At that point, no two selected supports intersect, and each coordinate belongs to at most one
S_{t}
with
t\in\mathcal{T}
.

Bounding removed triples per selection.
Fix a triple
t
chosen during the process. For each coordinate
i\in S_{t}
, at most
M
triples in the current
\mathcal{S}
contain
i
, by assumption (2). Every triple that we remove due to intersection with
S_{t}
contains at least one coordinate in
S_{t}
. Therefore the number of triples removed in this step (including
t
itself) is at most

\sum_{i\in S_{t}}M=M|S_{t}|\leq Mbn.

(15)

The bound
|S_{t}|\leq bn
comes from assumption (1).

Global bound.
Let
r=|\mathcal{T}|
be the number of selected triples. Each selection removes at most
Mbn
triples from the pool, and together the
r
selections remove every triple in the original
\mathcal{S}
. Hence

|\mathcal{S}|\leq r\cdot Mbn.

(16)

Rearranging yields

|\mathcal{T}|=r\geq\frac{|\mathcal{S}|}{Mbn}.

(17)

This proves the claim.

Appendix D
Distance under constant-depth local circuits

We record a standard light-cone bound for constant-depth local circuits acting on stabilizer codes, and use it in the proof of Theorem

.

Lemma 4

(Distance under constant-depth local circuits)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a stabilizer code on
n
qubits with distance
d
, and let
U
be a depth-
L
circuit in which each gate acts on at most
q
qubits. Let
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}=U\mathcal{C}
be the image code. Then the distance
d^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
obeys

d^{\prime}\geq\frac{d}{q^{L}}.

In particular, constant depth and bounded locality preserve distance up to a constant factor depending only on
(q,L)
.

Proof.

Let
E^{\prime}
be any Pauli error on
n
qubits of weight
w^{\prime}<d/q^{L}
. Consider the conjugated error
E=U^{\dagger}E^{\prime}U
. Because
U
is a depth-
L
circuit of
q
-local gates, the light cone of each qubit has size at most
q^{L}
: every qubit in the support of
E^{\prime}
can influence at most
q^{L}
qubits in the support of
E
. More formally, each layer can increase the support size by at most a factor
q
, so

\operatorname{wt}(E)\leq q^{L}\operatorname{wt}(E^{\prime})<q^{L}\cdot\frac{d}{q^{L}}=d.

Thus any such
E
has weight strictly less than
d
and hence cannot implement a nontrivial logical operator on
\mathcal{C}
. Equivalently,
E^{\prime}
cannot implement a nontrivial logical operator on
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
, since
U
is a unitary isomorphism between the two codes. Therefore all nontrivial logical operators on
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
have weight at least
d/q^{L}
, which proves the claim.
∎

Appendix E
Proof of Theorem
1

We now provide the full argument combining the packing lemma, hypergraph coloring, and distance preservation.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

Step 1: selecting a large disjoint subcollection.

Apply Lemma
2
to the set
\mathcal{S}_{n}
with parameters
(a,b,M)
. We obtain a subcollection
T_{n}\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{n}
such that:

•

each coordinate
i\in[n]
belongs to at most one support
S_{t}
with
t\in T_{n}
;

•

|T_{n}|\geq\frac{|\mathcal{S}_{n}|}{Mbn}\geq\frac{c_{1}}{Mb}n^{\gamma}.

(18)

Thus
|T_{n}|\geq cn^{\gamma}
for
c=c_{1}/(Mb)
, and we may set
\beta=\gamma>0
.

Step 2: constructing a bounded-degree hypergraph of
\mathsf{CCZ}
gates.

For each triple
t=(x_{t},y_{t},z_{t})\in T_{n}
, which is magic-friendly by assumption, we consider three logical
X
operators (their cosets in
\mathcal{L}_{X}^{(n)}
). The orthogonality conditions in Definition
1
and Remark
1
ensure that there exists a diagonal unitary
U_{t}
built from physical
Z
, CZ, and
\mathsf{CCZ}
gates whose action on the subspace spanned by these three logical qubits is (up to Clifford conjugation) a logical
\mathsf{CCZ}
gate. Concretely, one may take a transversal
\mathsf{CCZ}
on physical representatives
x_{t},y_{t},z_{t}
, possibly supplemented by local Clifford layers to correct unwanted phases; the exact form is not important here, only the locality pattern.

We now restrict attention to the
\mathsf{CCZ}
gates used in each
U_{t}
. Because each
U_{t}
can be chosen with bounded locality (each qubit participates in at most some constant
d_{0}

\mathsf{CCZ}
gates for that triple), and because the supports
S_{t}
for
t\in T_{n}
are pairwise disjoint, each physical qubit participates in at most
d_{0}

\mathsf{CCZ}
gates overall when we implement all
U_{t}
in parallel. Define a 3-uniform hypergraph
H_{n}=(V_{n},E_{n})
with vertex set
V_{n}=[n]
and hyperedges given by all triples of coordinates on which some
\mathsf{CCZ}
gate is applied in some
U_{t}
with
t\in T_{n}
. For a fixed
t
, each coordinate in
S_{t}
participates in at most
d_{0}
such hyperedges, and no coordinate belongs to two different supports. Consequently, the maximum degree of
H_{n}
satisfies

\Delta(H_{n})\leq d_{0}=:\Delta.

(19)

Thus
H_{n}
is a bounded-degree 3-uniform hypergraph.

Step 3: constant-depth
\mathsf{CCZ}
circuit via edge coloring.

By Lemma
1
,
H_{n}
admits an edge coloring with at most
3\Delta+1
colors. For each color
c

\mathsf{CCZ}
gates on those edges can be applied in one time step. The sequence of layers
c=1,\dots,3\Delta+1
gives a
\mathsf{CCZ}
circuit of depth at most
3\Delta+1
that applies all
\mathsf{CCZ}
gates used in all
U_{t}
for

.

We may incorporate any necessary single-qubit and CZ gates (which are of bounded weight and can likewise be layered in constant depth) and pre/post logical Clifford operations to ensure that, on the code space, the overall circuit acts as

\bigotimes_{t\in T_{n}}\mathsf{CCZ}^{(t)}_{\text{logical}},

(20)

up to a global logical Clifford. This yields a constant-depth physical
\mathsf{CCZ}
circuit implementing logical
\mathsf{CCZ}
gates on all triples in

Step 4: distance preservation.

Finally, suppose the distance of
\mathcal{Q}_{n}
is
d_{n}=\Theta(n)
. The
\mathsf{CCZ}

L
, and each gate acts on at most

4

d_{n}^{\prime}\geq\frac{d_{n}}{3^{L}}.

(21)

Since
L

d_{n}^{\prime}=\Theta(d_{n})=\Theta(n)
. This establishes that the distance is preserved up to a constant factor, completing the proof.
∎

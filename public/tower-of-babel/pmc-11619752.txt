Molecular allergen sensitization drives phenotypes of severe asthma in children: Evidence from a megacity cohort (SAMP)
Authors: Mélisande Bourgoin‐Heck, Victoria Wolff‐Goldnadel, Yannick Chantran, Sarah Saf, Tamazoust Guiddir, Flore Amat, Fanny Rancière, Isabelle Momas, Stéphanie Wanin, Thierry Rose, Philippe Saint‐Pierre, Jocelyne Just
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundSeveral major sensitization profiles have been described in children with asthma, but it remains unclear how these profiles relate to asthma phenotypes. The aim of this study was to determine allergenic sensitization profiles in a megacity cohort (SAMP).MethodsThis was a cross‐sectional analysis performed from 2011 to 2015 including preschool and school‐age children with severe and moderate asthma from the SAMP cohort. We performed ALEX multiplex array and carried out cluster analysis.ResultsData from 367 children were analyzed: 224 of preschool age and 143 of school age, respectively 84 (38%) and 114 (80%) presented at least one allergic sensitization. At preschool age, three clusters were identified: Cluster 1, Few sensitizations to inhaled allergen molecular families and non‐type 2 (T2) inflammation (n = 61); Cluster 2, Predominant sensitization to HDM molecular families (n = 16); Cluster 3, Severe asthma with multiple sensitizations to inhaled and food allergen molecular families (n = 7). At school age, five clusters were identified: Cluster 1, Few sensitizations to inhaled allergen molecular families and non‐T2 inflammation (n = 43); Cluster 2, Predominant sensitization to HDM molecular families (n = 31); Cluster 3, Predominant sensitization to PR‐10 protein family (n = 25); Cluster 4, Severe asthma with predominant sensitization to tropomyosin family (n = 11); Cluster 5, Severe asthma with multiple sensitizations to inhaled and food allergen molecular families (n = 4).ConclusionThese results underline the heterogeneity of sensitization profiles in severe allergic childhood asthma. The most severe asthma phenotypes were associated with multiple sensitizations to both inhaled and food allergen molecular families as expected, and to the tropomyosin molecular family, a novel finding.Keywords: ALEX multiplex array, asthma phenotype, children, severe asthma, type 2 inflammation

Key message

In our megacity children cohort, the most severe asthma phenotypes were associated with multiple sensitizations to both inhaled and food allergen molecular families as expected, and also to the tropomyosin molecular family, a novel finding.

INTRODUCTION

Atopy and allergy are major factors in defining asthma phenotypes in children and are associated with the most severe phenotypes, especially at school age. 1 Beyond allergy, the patterns of allergic sensitization appear to be predictive of the development of atopic diseases, 2 and of their severity and persistence. 3 Multiplex arrays have become essential to determine a sensitization profile and have been used in various studies. These allergic sensitization patterns are studied by molecular component analysis which reveals a relatively constant distribution in population‐based cohorts, 2 asthmatic children, 4 children with severe asthma, 4 and also in asthmatic adults. 4 , 5 Overall, four profiles are found: predominantly house dust mite (HDM) sensitization, predominantly pollen sensitization, and multiple sensitizations of either lower or higher grade. Although multiple atopic comorbidities have frequently been associated with severe asthma in children, the allergic sensitization profile was not found to be predictive of asthma severity in two cohorts of children with severe asthma. 4 , 6 However, our team and other authors showed that children with multiple sensitizations associated with multiple comorbidities were at risk of hospitalization, alterations in lung function, and high‐grade T2 inflammation. 1 , 7 , 8 , 9 , 10 The heterogeneity and apparent contradictory nature of these results could be explained by the different methodological approaches in exploring allergenic sensitization and varied environmental exposure.

The current study aimed to explore sensitization to allergenic components using a novel technology (ALEX2) for the first time to investigate a wide panel of inhaled and food molecular components in a population of children with moderate to severe asthma in the urban environment of “Grand Paris”, previously defined as the Severe Asthma Molecular Phenotype (SAMP) cohort. 7 The “Grand Paris” area is characterized by a great diversity of populations, and by the profound environmental changes that characterize megacities.

METHODS

Design and setting

This was a cross‐sectional study performed from 2011 to 2015 using data from the prospective SAMP cohort at Trousseau Hospital, Paris (France). All the children had been referred to the centre by a secondary or primary physician due to persistence of recurrent wheeze despite long‐term treatment. The Institutional Review Board of Saint Antoine Hospital, Paris, endorsed the protocol as an observational study. Written informed consent was obtained from the parents of the children included.

To be included in the present study, the children had to have severe or moderate asthma, or severe or moderate recurrent wheeze aged from 1 to 15 years at the time of exploration. 8 Preschool children included met the criteria for high probability of asthma according to GINA. 11 Health outcomes were collected in a computerized database using standardized questionnaires. Gender and age at inclusion were collected. The severity of asthma of the entire population was assessed after at least 6 months of follow‐up prior to inclusion in the study by an experienced pulmonologist pediatrician after repeated individual or group health education measures had been undertaken to improve adherence to a continuous anti‐asthmatic treatment. Severe asthma was defined as asthma requiring high doses of inhaled corticosteroids (ICS) (≥500 μg/day fluticasone propionate [FP]) and at least one other controller medication in school‐age children and high doses of ICS (>200 μg/day FP) and a leukotriene receptor antagonist in preschool‐age children. The number of hospitalizations for an asthma attack in the year prior to inclusion was recorded. Allergic rhinitis and active atopic dermatitis were assessed by questions from the International Study of Asthma and Allergies in Childhood (ISAAC), 12 and IgE‐mediated food allergy was defined by clinically relevant symptoms within 6 h following food allergen consumption associated with an allergic sensitization to the same allergen. Explorations were carried out at inclusion and therefore at different ages. Total IgE values (measured by ImmunoCAP, Thermofischer, Uppsala, Sweden) were collected. The following threshold was used to define increased levels: elevated total IgE above or equal to the third quartile distribution of each studied population (preschool‐age group (children <6 years) and school‐age group (children ≥6 years)) (Table S1).

Allergic sensitization by ALEX multiplex array

The ALEX 2 (Allergy Explorer, Macro Array Diagnostics, Vienna, Austria) multiplex array can test up to 300 allergens (117 allergenic extracts and 183 molecular allergens) in 100 microliters of serum making it the immunoassay which can analyze the largest panel of molecular allergens to date on the market. 13 ALEX 2 arrays were performed for 367 patients from the SAMP cohort for whom we had a sufficient serum library, then analyzed with a double reading. We characterized sensitivity to 85 active components in preschool children and 201 in school‐age children (specific immunoglobulin E (sIgE) >0.30 units in at least three children) among the 199 (54.2%) participants sensitized to at least one of these components.

Staphylococcal enterotoxin sensitization

We assessed sensitization to four Staphylococcal enterotoxins (SEs): S. aureus enterotoxin A (SEA), B (SEB), C (SEC), and toxic shock syndrome toxin‐1 (TSST‐1). 14 Specific IgE levels were considered positive when >0.10 kU/L (measured by ImmunoCAP, Thermofischer, Uppsala, Sweden). SE sensitization was defined as the positivity of specific IgEs for one or more SEs in each of the two groups (preschool‐ and school‐age groups).

Eosinophilic inflammation

Blood eosinophil count was measured by an automated Sysmex analyzer (Villepinte, France) outside systemic corticoid treatment and asthma exacerbations. Eosinophil count stability was assessed by at least two measurements within the previous 6 months of follow‐up.

Flexible bronchoscopy was performed in all included patients under conscious sedation and local anesthesia, 15 during which bronchoalveolar lavage (BAL) was collected and processed for cytology by the hospital cytopathology department. 16

Statistical analysis

A hierarchical bottom‐up clustering method was used to obtain an unsupervised classification of the population. 17 , 18 This agglomerative approach begins with each subject as a separate cluster and merges them into successively larger clusters.

Before the clustering analysis, all the allergenic components were coded into one of two categories (≤0.3, >0.3) to avoid scaling bias. The Euclidian distance was considered to evaluate the distance between any two subjects. The clusters were merged into larger clusters according to Ward's criterion which minimize the within‐cluster sum of squares.

Cluster analysis and cluster representations were obtained using the R package “Pheatmap”. This package provides Heatmaps to visualize the clustering of the sample. The columns/rows of the data matrix are reordered according to the hierarchical clustering results, placing similar observations close to each other. A color scheme is applied to visualize the data matrix. Visualizing the data matrix in this way can help find the characteristic variables of each cluster. The dendrogram provided by the clustering method is represented with the heatmap. The dendrogram gives information on the cost of merging clusters. Cluster analysis was performed both for the preschool and school‐age population.

The number of clusters was chosen according to the dendrogram and the clinical relevancy of the results. The data matrix representation using a color scheme helps to find the variables that appear to be characteristic for each cluster.

A correlation matrix analysis of a set of sIgE based on the Pearson correlation coefficient was summarized using a network representation. Nodes represent sIgE while edges indicate a correlation >.5. The edge width represents the strength of correlation between a pair of sIgE. The R package “igraph” was used to visualize the network.

RESULTS

The serum of 367 children were analyzed: 224 of preschool age and 143 of school age. Among them, 84 (38%) and 115 (80%), respectively, presented at least one allergic sensitization.

Preschool children

Cluster 1

This cluster was the largest with 61 children. The children in this cluster had few sensitizations (Figure S1A/Table S2). Total IgE were significantly lower (p < .001) in this cluster, as well as the eosinophil blood count (p < .001) and in BAL (p = .02) (Table 1). This cluster can be summarized as Few sensitizations to inhaled allergen molecular families and non‐type 2 (T2) inflammation.

Cluster 2

This cluster comprised 16 children with predominant sensitization to HDMs and, to a lesser degree, to grass pollen (Table S2). This cluster was characterized by more frequent atopic comorbidities: allergic rhinitis (75%, p < .001), active atopic dermatitis (50%, p = .01), and IgE‐mediated food allergies (56%, p < .001) (Table 1). Total IgE were higher in this group (mean 544 kU/L, p < .001), and they had a higher percentage of eosinophils in BAL (mean 1.9%, p = .02) (Table 1). This profile can be summarized as Predominant sensitization to HDM molecular families.

Cluster 3

This cluster comprised seven children with multiple sensitizations, predominantly to storage dust mites (SDM) and food, more rarely to tree pollen (aside from birch). Food sensitizations were also multiple, and of various types: mainly tropomyosin cross‐sensitizations but also sensitizations to 2S Albumin. Eosinophil blood count was higher in this cluster (p < .001) and all the children had severe asthma (Table 1). This cluster can be summarized as Severe asthma with multiple sensitizations to inhaled and food allergen molecular families.

School‐age children

Cluster 1

This cluster was the largest with 43 children with few sensitizations (Figure 2, Table S3). Total IgE were significantly lower (p < .001) in this cluster, as well as eosinophil blood count (p < .001) (Table S3). This cluster can be summarized as Few sensitizations to inhaled allergen molecular families and non‐T2 inflammation.

Representation of the five school‐age clusters according to FEV1, hospitalizations for severe asthma exacerbation, and total IgE. FEV1 is expressed as percentage of predicted values, and hospitalizations as mean number during last year. Circled areas represent mean total IgE in kUI/L.

Cluster 2

This cluster comprised 31 children with predominant sensitization to HDMs and, to a lesser degree, to SDMs (mostly Gly d 2) and grass pollen (only beta‐expansins) (Table S3, Figure 1). The cluster was characterized by a high rate of allergic rhinitis (87%, p = .005), and a low rate of IgE‐mediated food allergies (6%, p < .001) (Table 2). This profile can be summarized as Predominant sensitization to HDM molecular families.

Correlation network of a set of component‐specific IgE at school age. The nodes representing sIgE are colored according to the hierarchical clustering analysis (Figure S1). A Pearson's correlation coefficient greater than .5 is represented by an edge between two nodes. Edge width represents the strength of correlation between pairs of sIgE components. sIgE, specific immunoglobulin E.

Cluster 3

This cluster comprised 25 children with predominant sensitization to pollen of all kinds (tree, grass, and herbaceous), and various protein families (beta‐expansins, PR‐10, and profilins), and to PR‐10 from legumes, fruits, and vegetables (Table S3, Figure 1). The cluster was characterized by the highest rate of allergic rhinitis (96%, p = .005), and a high rate of IgE‐mediated food allergies (44%, p < .001), most likely in relation to PR‐10 cross sensitization (Table 2). This profile can be summarized as Predominant sensitization PR‐10 molecular family.

Cluster 4

The fourth cluster included 11 children with predominant sensitization to tropomyosin from SDMs, HDMs, seafood, meat, and cockroaches (Figure 1). These children also had the highest rate of sensitization to Alternaria alternata (45%, p = .001, Table S3). All the children in this cluster had severe asthma, and the FEV1 was significantly lower (76%, p = .04) (Table 2, Figure 2). This profile can be summarized as Severe asthma with predominant sensitization to tropomyosin molecular family.

Cluster 5

This cluster comprised four children with multiple sensitizations, predominantly to animal dander (lipocalins), legumes, tree nuts, seeds, and nsLTP from fruits, vegetables, and pollens (Table S3). All of the children had IgE‐mediated food allergy (p < .001). T2 inflammation was the most pronounced in this cluster, with the highest total IgE (3644, p < .001), and more sensitizations to SEs (p < .001). All four children had severe asthma and had the highest rate of hospitalization for severe asthma in the previous year (p < .001) (Table 2, Figure 2). This cluster can be summarized as Severe asthma with multiple sensitizations to inhaled and food allergen molecular families and T2‐high inflammation.

DISCUSSION

Our results underline the heterogeneity of sensitization profiles in severe allergic childhood asthma. The most severe asthma phenotypes were associated with multiple sensitizations to both inhaled and food allergen molecular families as expected, but also to the tropomyosin molecular family, a novel finding.

Predominant HDM sensitization, a frequent phenotype

The predominant HDM sensitization phenotype was frequent in our cohort, representing the second phenotype in number, in both preschool and school‐age children. This suggests an early onset of this phenotype during childhood; it has been shown that sensitization to HDMs is the first to appear in early childhood, and this profile remains stable thereafter. 19 The predominant HDM sensitization phenotype has also been highlighted in the BIOPRED and COBRAPed cohorts. 4 , 6

In our cohort, this sensitization profile was associated with T2 inflammation, primarily in lung tissue, as shown by the high level of bronchoalveolar eosinophils in preschool children. It has been demonstrated that the T2 response is promoted by HDM proteases which disrupt the epithelial barrier. 20 , 21

Severe asthma with multiple sensitizations to inhaled and food allergens, the highest T2 profile both at preschool and school age

The multiple sensitization profile is a rare phenotype that exists early in life, as demonstrated in our cohort and others. 4 , 19 This early onset probably suggests that the phenotype is of genetic rather than environmental origin. 22 , 23 , 24 In our study, the multiple sensitization profile was associated with high T2 inflammation (the highest eosinophil blood count in preschool children, highest total IgE at school age), and with more severe asthma as shown by the highest rate of hospitalizations for severe exacerbation in the previous year (Figure 2). Other studies have demonstrated similar results for this specific phenotype, particularly the increased risk of hospitalization in children with early multiple sensitizations. 3

Pollen‐food cross‐sensitization also exists in children with severe asthma at school age

At school age, 25 children had a severe asthma phenotype with pollen‐food syndrome linked to PR‐10 sensitization. Sensitization to birch pollen is expected in the Paris region; however, the large number of children with this phenotype in our cohort is surprising because PR‐10 sensitization is not usually associated with severe asthma. 25 This being said, several teams have recently found similar associations. A study conducted in another megacity, NYC, in children aged 7–8 years found that the profile associated with PR‐10 sensitization and, to a lesser extent, to indoor environmental allergens, was at the highest risk of having asthma, asthma‐like symptoms, and other allergic diseases. 26 Similarly, Betv1 was identified to be one of the four allergen molecules associated with a high risk of asthma and/or rhinitis at 16 years of age in the BAMSE birth cohort. 27

The importance of this cluster and its severity are probably linked to the environment: global warming leading to longer pollination and the increased frequency of storms and floods 28 have led to a significant increase in the prevalence of sensitization to PR‐10 in recent decades, as has been demonstrated by several longitudinal studies. 25 , 29 Not long ago, pollen sensitization was essentially associated with rhinitis but a growing body of evidence shows that this sensitization profile is increasingly associated with asthma, and even with severe asthma.

Sensitization to particular indoor allergens at school age is probably related to the unfavorable environment of a megacity

Finally, to the best of our knowledge, this is the first time a cluster associating sensitization to various allergens from an unfavorable indoor environment (SDMs, cockroaches, and Alternaria) has been described. Identification of this sensitization pattern was undoubtedly made possible by the very broad analysis of allergens using the ALEX2 multiarray. This extensive assessment of sensitizations makes it possible to avoid a common bias which is that of underestimating the importance of SDM sensitization compared with HDM sensitization. 30

This sensitization profile was associated with more severe asthma in our cohort (with the lowest FEV1), which is consistent with the literature. Indeed, it is well established that sensitization and exposure to Alternaria are associated with severe asthma, both in adults and children, 31 , 32 independently of the degree of atopy. 33 One potential mechanism is that the bronchial epithelium of severely asthmatic individuals are more susceptible to the barrier disruption induced by proteases of Alternaria alternata. 34 On the other hand, a large study in northern Europe showed that SDM sensitization was significantly and independently associated with asthma and nasal allergies even after adjustment for HDM sensitization. 35 Furthermore, a study conducted in Florida, USA, showed that sensitization to SDMs and, to a lesser extent, B. germanica was associated with acute childhood asthma that required emergency treatment. 36

In this cluster, the predominant protein family is tropomyosin, but the origin of the sensitization remains uncertain. 37 Our first hypothesis was exposure to SDMs from the indoor environment. SDMs are present in domestic environments, particularly in urban populations from temperate regions of the world, 35 , 38 and in the presence of a domestic animal. 39 Another explanation could be exposure to cockroaches which are found in unfavorable indoor environments. Subjects with cockroach‐sensitive asthma appear to be a distinctive subgroup characterized by perennial symptoms and high levels of total IgE. 40 , 41 Finally, we cannot exclude the possibility of primary sensitization to Blomia tropicalis. In Europe, environmental exposure to B. tropicalis is low. 42 However, our megacity cohort includes many patients with immigrant backgrounds, originating from West Indies or Africa, and who may therefore have been exposed in their original region before their arrival in Paris region. A study carried out in young adults in a temperate zone, the Canary Islands, showed frequent sensitization to B. tropicalis, strongly associated with asthma. 43 Overall, this sensitization pattern seems strongly linked to the urban environment typical of megacities. In our cohort, the children in this cluster were more likely, although not significantly so, to come from families of low economic and social status. This lack of significance could be due to insufficiently precise data, since the low economic and social category includes salaried professionals as well as the unemployed and does not necessarily reflect the housing conditions.

Few sensitizations to inhaled allergen molecular families with non‐T2 inflammation

At preschool age, as expected and in accordance with the literature, most of the patients in our cohort were only slightly sensitized. 3 This cluster was characterized by non‐T2 inflammation, with the lowest level of total IgE, and the lowest level of eosinophils in both blood and BAL.

At school age, the same cluster was also found to be the biggest, without T2 inflammation (lowest level of total IgE and fewer sensitizations to SEs), or features of greater severity in terms of exacerbations and lung function. This phenotype has previously been described in the general population 3 and in childhood asthma 10 without features of severity, but also in severe asthma cohorts. 4 , 6 , 44 It should be noted that we mainly observed respiratory sensitization in this cluster, unlike the multiple sensitization cluster which involves sensitization to both inhaled and food allergens.

Strengths and limitations

ISAC is the oldest and most frequently used multiplex array to date. We chose to use the most recent ALEX2 chip, which offers a wider range of allergens (both extracts and molecular components), and uses an inhibitor of antibodies against cross‐reacting carbohydrate determinants (CCD). The results' overall concordance between the two chips seems to be very good for common allergens, 45 , 46 and ALEX2 shows adequate diagnostic capacity, comparable to ISAC112 and ImmunoCAP. 47 Regarding food allergy, one study showed that ALEX2 detected more positive sensitizations with unknown clinical relevance. 48 There are several limitations in our study that should be considered. The absence of a control group of children with mild asthma, as well as the small proportion of patients with moderate asthma in our cohort, does not allow us to clearly identify differences in severity between the sensitization profiles. The low number of children in some clusters makes comparisons with other groups difficult. Nevertheless, we believe that these are clusters with very specific characteristics, corresponding to clinical realities, and which should not be overlooked by diluting them within a larger cluster. Furthermore, it was a cross‐sectional study with the limitations inherent to this study design. Indeed, we cannot demonstrate a causal link between sensitization profiles and asthma severity, only a statistical association. We have planned to conduct a longitudinal study to assess the sensitization trajectories in this cohort. Finally, while single‐center recruitment is generally considered to be a study limitation, in this case we think it is a strength as it allowed us to have a precise vision of the environmental exposure and resulting sensitizations in a megacity. Further studies in megacities are necessary to confirm these results.

CONCLUSION

In the SAMP cohort, the most severe asthma phenotypes were associated with multiple sensitizations to both inhaled and food allergen molecular families as frequently described, but also to the tropomyosin molecular family, a novel finding. Overall, these results support the heterogeneity of sensitization profiles in severe allergic childhood asthma.

The precise evaluation of the sensitization profile of a given patient by multiallergenic microarrays, as part of the assessment of severe asthma in a tertiary referral center, could allow for personalized care in relation to the inflammatory profile. The study of the evolutionary trajectories of these different sensitization profiles is necessary to improve the outcome of children with severe asthma.

The sensitization profiles highlighted in our cohort seem closely linked to the environment of a megacity, marked by sensitization to tropomyosin probably due to an unfavorable environment, and frequent PR‐10 sensitization associated with outdoor environmental change.

Describing these new sensitization patterns was undoubtedly made possible by the extensive assessment of allergens using the ALEX2 multiarray, which allowed us to highlight sensitizations that are typically overlooked in current practice.

Genome-wide identification of the CmPLATZ gene family and its role in cold acclimation and overwintering in Chrysanthemum morifolium
Authors: Qiwei Wang, Ying Duan, Huayang Li, Hong Ge, Ruidong Jia, Yaping Kou, Linbo Xu, Jie Zhao, Ruijie Hao, Shuhua Yang, Xin Zhao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundChrysanthemum (Chrysanthemum morifolium) is a globally important ornamental and economic crop that is susceptible to cold stress, which can present challenges for its large-scale cultivation. Plant AT-rich sequence and zinc-binding (PLATZ) transcription factors are key regulators of plant perception and response to cold and other abiotic stresses; however, the role of these factors in the cold tolerance of chrysanthemum remains unclear.ResultsIn this study, we identified 41 CmPLATZ genes through a genome-wide analysis. We systematically investigated the structural features, chromosomal localization, phylogenetic relationships, promoter cis-acting elements, and cold-responsive transcriptional profiles of these genes. Additionally, the tissue-specific expression and cold-inducible patterns of key members were analyzed in 11 chrysanthemum accessions with divergent cold tolerance. The 41 CmPLATZ genes were distributed across 21 chromosomes and clustered into six groups. The expansion of CmPLATZ was primarily driven by whole-genome duplication (WGD) events. Ka/Ks analysis indicated that the duplicated genes have been subjected to purifying selection, which resulted in limited functional divergence. Synteny analysis revealed that the evolutionary relationships of CmPLATZ genes are more complex than those of PLATZ gene families in the related species C. makinoi and C. seticuspe. Based on the analysis of promoter elements and transcriptome profiling, we observed that multiple CmPLATZ members respond to low-temperature stress and we identified CmPLATZ1a and CmPLATZ4a as key candidate regulators. Subcellular localization experiments confirmed the nuclear localization of these proteins, and heterologous functional validation demonstrated that overexpression of CmPLATZ1a in Arabidopsis significantly enhanced cold tolerance, suggesting the potential involvement of this gene in regulating conserved cold-response pathways.ConclusionsThis study provides the first genome-wide characterization of the CmPLATZ family, offering new insights into the cold-response regulatory network of chrysanthemum, and providing a theoretical basis and candidate gene resources for the extension of the cultivation range.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12870-026-08749-7.

Introduction

Plant AT-rich sequence and zinc-binding (PLATZ) proteins are a plant-specific family of DNA-binding proteins that recognize and bind to AT-rich DNA sequences to either activate or repress the transcription of downstream target genes [1–2]. This binding specificity is associated directly with the conserved arrangement of amino acid residue arrangement and the spatial structure coordinated by zinc ion [3]. As plant-specific transcription factors, PLATZ proteins are involved widely in the growth and development of various organs, such as seeds, leaves, and flowers [4–7], and these proteins also play important roles in the responses of plants to abiotic stresses, including salt, alkali, and drought [8–11].

In recent years, PLATZ gene family has been identified at the genome-wide level in model plants and important crops such as barley [12], rice [13], maize [6], tomato [14], apple [15], and poplar [7]. Some of these studies have highlighted the role of PLATZ genes in the response of horticultural crops to low-temperature stress [16]. Therefore, investigating the relationship between the expression of specific PLATZ genes and cold tolerance can help clarify the molecular regulatory network underlying low-temperature responses, providing new strategies and potential target genes for improving plant cold tolerance through genetic engineering [17].

Chrysanthemum (Chrysanthemum morifolium Ramat.) (2n = 6x = 54) is native to China, and is distributed widely throughout the temperate and subtropical regions of Asia, Europe, and North America [18–19]. As a highly valuable ornamental crop in the Asteraceae family, Chrysanthemum is prized for its rich diversity of petal shapes and colors, and the industrial scale and economic value of this crop rank among the top ornamental flowers globally [20–22]. Chrysanthemum is a perennial herb, and its underground rhizomes function in both reproduction and nutrient storage, which are critical for overwintering and surviving in environments with low temperature. Previous studies have confirmed that the germination characteristics of rhizomes after overwintering are key morphological indicators for the evaluation of the cold tolerance of chrysanthemum [23–24]; a higher rate of rhizomes germination correlates with stronger cold tolerance. While Chrysanthemum species from alpine regions have evolved stronger low-temperature tolerance, most cultivated varieties are sensitive to low temperatures. When the temperature drops below the physiological threshold of these varieties, growth and development are inhibited severely, and frost damage occurs. This leads to a decline in ornamental quality and economic value [25–28], consequently restricting the cultivation range and industrial potential of the plants [24, 29].

Although traditional breeding can develop more cold-tolerant varieties, these approaches often face challenges such as long breeding cycles and high costs. Recent advances in the molecular biology of Chrysanthemum have enabled the rapid creation of germplasm with key horticultural traits, such as flower color and flowering time [30–32]. The recent release of a high-quality genome for Chrysanthemum morifolium [33], combined with genome data from several other Chrysanthemum species [21, 34–36], provides a solid molecular foundation for systematic analysis of the evolutionary history, developmental regulatory networks, and key gene functions of the species. Members of gene families such as SWEET and DEAD-box RNA helicase have been identified to participate in the low-temperature stress response of ornamental flowers, and the functions of these families have been validated using transgenic methods, indicating that the precise identification of gene family members is a feasible strategy for improving cold tolerance [37–38]. Although PLATZ genes are involved in abiotic stress responses and developmental regulation in some floral species [39–40], a systematic, genome-wide identification and functional study of the PLATZ gene family in Chrysanthemum morifolium has not yet been reported. Whether this family participates in the response of chrysanthemum to low-temperature stress and the underlying molecular regulatory network requires investigation.

In this study, we used the genome of Chrysanthemum morifolium to systematically identify members of the CmPLATZ gene family. We analyzed the physicochemical properties, chromosomal localization, gene structure, conserved motifs, collinearity, promoter cis-elements, and evolutionary characteristics of these members. By integrating transcriptome data from chrysanthemum under different treatments of cold tolerance and performing quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR) validation, we identified key members with significantly altered expression under low-temperature stress. This work provides a theoretical basis for the clarification of the cold tolerance mechanism and lays a foundation for the excavation of gene resources for cold-resistant breeding.

Material and method

Plant materials and growth conditions

The experiment was conducted at the Nankou trial plot of the Institute of Vegetables and Flowers, Chinese Academy of Agricultural Sciences (IVF, CAAS) (40.26°N, 116.07°E) from March 2024 to April 2025, using eleven cultivated chrysanthemum germplasms that were preserved and propagated by the Flower Seedling Research Group of IVF, CAAS. Table 1 provides information regarding the 11 chrysanthemum accessions. The accessions include varieties originating from Northern China, Southern China, and Dutch cultivars.

Information of 11 chrysanthemum accessions

In June 2024, seedlings of the 11 accessions were transplanted to a field with plants spaced 30 cm apart within and between rows. Thirty plants of each accession were planted in a randomized block design. Plants were cultivated using standard management without cold protection during winter. In November 2024, tissues, including rhizome stems and leaves, were collected from the 11 accessions. For each accession, tissues from three plants were pooled, frozen immediately in liquid nitrogen, and stored at -80 °C for subsequent RNA extraction. In April 2025, the number of sprouted rhizomes was recorded for all 11 accessions.

Identification and bioinformatic analysis of CmPLATZ family members

The genome of Chrysanthemum morifolium was retrieved from figshare (https://figshare.com/), and candidate sequences were identified using two methods. First, the hidden Markov model (HMM) profile of the PLATZ domain (PF04640) from Pfam was used to search the genome of C. morifolium with the Simple HMM Search function in TBtools (http://pfam-legacy.xfam.org/) [41–42]. Second, 12 AtPLATZ protein sequences from Arabidopsis thaliana (TAIR, https://www.arabidopsis.org/) [43] were used as queries in a BLASTP search (e-value ≤ 1e-5) against the C. morifolium genome. Sequences identified by both methods were retained and verified for the PLATZ domain using National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI) Conserved Domain Database (CDD) (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/Structure/bwrpsb/bwrpsb.cgi) [44] to remove mispredicted and redundant sequences.

The chromosomal distribution of genes was mapped using TBtools v2.119. Physicochemical properties were predicted using online tools, and subcellular localization was predicted with WoLF-PSORT (https://wolfpsort.hgc.jp/) [45]. A phylogenetic tree of PLATZ proteins from C. morifolium and Arabidopsis was constructed in MEGA 11 (Version 11.0.13) using the neighbor-joining (NJ) method with 1000 bootstrap replicates [46]. The phylogenetic tree was visualized and annotated using the Interactive Tree of Life (iTOL 7.1, https://itol.embl.de/) [47]. Conserved motifs were identified using MEME Suite, with the number of motifs set to 10 (Version 5.5.7, https://meme-suite.org/meme/tools/meme) [48]. Gene structure and motif distributions were visualized with TBtools.

Gene collinearity and Ka/Ks analysis

Physical positions of CmPLATZ genes were extracted from the reference genome of C. morifolium. Protein sequences were aligned using BLAST (v2.16.0) with an e-value threshold of 1e-5. Gene duplication events were analyzed using MCScanX [49].

Genome sequences of Chrysanthemum makinoi [36] and Chrysanthemum seticuspe [21] were obtained from the Chrysanthemum Genome Database (https://cgd.njau.edu.cn/asteraceae/homePage) [50]. Synteny analyses were performed between C. morifolium, C. makinoi, and C. seticuspe, as well as Arabidopsis thaliana using the One Step MCScanX function in TBtools [49], and the results were visualized. The ratio of non-synonymous (Ka) to synonymous (Ks) substitution rates for collinear gene pairs was calculated using the Simple Ka/Ks Calculator in TBtools, implementing the Nei-Gojobori (NG) method.

Prediction of cis-acting elements in promoters

The 2000 bp (bp) sequences upstream of the transcription start sites of 41 CmPLATZ genes were extracted using TBtools. These sequences were submitted to the PlantCare database (https://bioinformatics.psb.ugent.be/webtools/plantcare/html/) to predict potential cis-acting elements in the promoter regions [51]. Visualization was performed using TBtools.

Expression analysis using transcriptome data and qRT-PCR

Expression data for CmPLATZ genes in different tissues were obtained from the Chrysanthemum Genome Database (https://cgd.njau.edu.cn/asteraceae/homePage) [50]. Expression data before and after cold treatment were retrieved from a published transcriptome dataset of Dendranthema grandiflorum (C. morifolium) var. Jinba (NCBI GEO: GSE102413) [52]. Expression heatmaps were generated using Python.

Total RNA was extracted from the rhizome stems and leaves of the 11 chrysanthemum accessions after natural overwintering using the RNApure Fast Plant Kit (CW0598T, CWBIO). The concentration and purity of RNA were determined using a NanoDrop 2000c spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA), and integrity was verified by 1% agarose gel electrophoresis. First-strand complementary DNA (cDNA) was synthesized using the PrimeScript™ RT reagent Kit (RR047A, TaKaRa). Gene-specific primers were designed using the GenScript Real-time PCR Primer Design Tool (https://www.genscript.com/tools/real-time-pcr-taqman-primer-design-tool), with CmEF-1α (AB548817) serving as the reference gene (Supplemental Table S1). Primers were synthesized by GENEWIZ (Azenta Life Sciences, Suzhou, China).

Quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR) was performed on an Applied Biosystems QuantStudio 5 instrument (Thermo Fisher Scientific) using TB Green® Premix Ex Taq™ II (Tli RNaseH Plus) (RR820A, Takara Biomedical Technology (Beijing) Co., Ltd., China). The amplification program was as follows: initial denaturation at 95 °C for 30 s; followed by 40 cycles of denaturation at 95 °C for 5 s, annealing at 60 °C for 30 s. Three biological replicates and three technical replicates were analyzed for each sample. Relative expression was calculated using the 2(−ΔΔCt) method [53]. Statistical significance (p < 0.05) was determined by one-way ANOVA followed by Duncan’s multiple comparison test using SPSS software.

Subcellular localization of CmPLATZ1a and CmPLATZ4a

The coding sequences (CDS) of CmPLATZ1a and CmPLATZ4a were amplified from cDNA of chrysanthemum ‘Zhongshan Zichen’ (D086) with 2 × Phanta Max Master Mix (P510-01, Vazyme Biotech Co., Ltd., Nanjing, China). The fragments were inserted into the pCAMBIA1300-c-GFP vector using XbaⅠ and SpeⅠ restriction enzymes (Takara Biomedical Technology (Beijing) Co., Ltd., China). Primer sequences are listed in Supplemental Table S2, after sequencing verification, the recombinant plasmids and an empty vector control were transformed into Agrobacterium tumefaciens GV3101 and expressed transiently in the leaves of Nicotiana benthamiana. Green fluorescent protein (GFP) fluorescence signals were observed using a Leica TCS SP8 confocal laser scanning microscope (Leica Microsystems, Wetzlar, Germany). GFP was excited at 488 nm (emission at 505–530 nm), and the nuclear marker NLS-mCherry was excited at 587 nm (emission at 630 nm). The merged images were used to determine the subcellular localization of the fusion proteins.

Arabidopsis genetic transformation and low-temperature tolerance assay

The verified recombinant vectors pSuper1300-CmPLATZ1a-GFP and pSuper1300-CmPLATZ4a-GFP were transformed into Arabidopsis thaliana via the Agrobacterium-mediated floral dip method using Agrobacterium tumefaciens strain GV3101. Transgenic plants were screened on medium containing hygromycin until homozygous T3 seeds were harvested. Total RNA was extracted from T3 transgenic plants, and first-strand cDNA was synthesized by reverse transcription. The expression levels of the target genes in positive transgenic lines were detected using gene-specific qRT-PCR primers for CmPLATZ1 and CmPLATZ4, with the Arabidopsis Actin gene (At3g18780) serving as the internal reference [54]. Primer sequences are listed in Supplemental Table S1.

Following the method described by Miura et al., [55] 14-day-old seedlings of wild-type (WT), CmPLATZ1a-OE, and CmPLATZ4a-OE lines were subjected to a graded low-temperature stress treatment. The stress protocol was as follows: seedlings were first treated at 4 °C for 48 h, then transferred to -8 °C for 4 h, followed by overnight recovery at 4 °C in darkness, and finally returned to normal growth conditions at 22 °C for a 7-day recovery period. Plants were then scored for chilling injury with Grade 1 representing plants showing almost no injury symptoms and Grade 5 representing complete withering and death of the above-ground parts. Each line was assessed with three biological replicates, and nine plants were scored per replicate. The chilling injury index was calculated based on the scoring results. For gene expression analysis, 14-day-old seedlings of WT and CmPLATZ1a-OE lines were treated at 4 °C for 48 h. Leaves were then collected for the extraction of total RNA, and the expression levels of cold-responsive genes (CBF1, CBF2, CBF3, KIN1, RD29A) were detected. The relevant primer sequences are detailed in Supplemental Table S1.

Results

Identification of CmPLATZ gene family members

A total of 41 CmPLATZ genes were identified in the C. morifolium genome. These genes were distributed unevenly across 21 of the 27 chromosomes, with 1 to 4 genes per chromosome. No CmPLATZ genes were found on several chromosomes, including Chr4, Chr5, Chr6, Chr13, Chr14, and Chr15 (Fig. 1).

The basic characteristics of 41 CmPLATZ proteins are summarized in Table 2. The protein lengths ranged from 134 to 404 amino acids (aa), with corresponding molecular weights of 15.86 to 46.19 kDa. Theoretical isoelectric points (PI) varied from 5.43 to 9.54, among which 38 proteins were predicted to be alkaline. Subcellular localization prediction indicated that 32 CmPLATZ proteins are localized in the nucleus, 5 in the cytoplasm, and 4 in the chloroplast.

Basic information of CmPLATZ proteins

Phylogenetic analysis of PLATZ proteins in C. morifolium and Arabidopsis

To investigate the evolutionary relationships of the CmPLATZ proteins, a phylogenetic tree was constructed using the protein sequences of 41 CmPLATZ members and 12 AtPLATZ members from Arabidopsis thaliana. Based on clustering with their Arabidopsis homologs, most C. morifolium genes were named accordingly (e.g., CmPLATZ1–3 and CmPLATZ5–12). Genes with low homology to Arabidopsis members were designated as CmPLATZ4 and CmPLATZ13–16.

To reflect the hexaploid nature of chrysanthemum, alleles on the three homologous chromosomes were distinguished with the suffixes “a”, “b”, or “c”. The phylogenetic analysis clustered the proteins into six groups (I–VI) (Fig. 2). Notably, the CmPLATZ4 proteins (Group V) did not cluster with AtPLATZ4 (Group VI), indicating low sequence similarity. The presence of additional members (CmPLATZ13–16) in C. morifolium compared to Arabidopsis highlights the increased complexity of the family in this species (Fig. 2).

Gene structure and conserved motif analysis

Analysis of gene structures and conserved motifs revealed that the 41 CmPLATZ genes contained 2 to 6 exons. Members within the same phylogenetic group shared highly similar features in exon-intron structures and exon lengths, although slight variations in untranslated regions (UTRs) were observed (Fig. 3A).

MEME analysis identified 10 conserved motifs in the CmPLATZ proteins. Motif 1 was present in all members, while Motifs 2, 3, 4, 6, and 7 were found in most members, indicating that these motifs are conserved highly within the family. Proteins with close evolutionary relationships exhibited similar motif compositions, suggesting functional conservation within clades. In contrast, Motifs 5, 8, 9, and 10 were restricted to specific subsets of CmPLATZ proteins (Fig. 3A, B; Supplemental Figure S1), suggesting that these motifs may contribute to functional divergence.

Collinearity and duplication analysis of CmPLATZ genes

The Chrysanthemum morifolium used in this study is hexaploid with a publicly available genome. It is characterized by nine homologous groups with three chromosomes per group. We analyzed the duplication patterns of the CmPLATZ gene family and identified 68 collinear gene pairs among the 41 members. These duplications occurred predominantly among the three chromosomes within homologous groups, with a few collinear relationships observed between non-homologous groups (Fig. 4; Table 3).

Ka/Ks values of segmental duplicated gene pairs of CmPLATZ in Chrysanthemum morifolium

To investigate the selection pressures acting on these duplicated genes, we calculated the Ka/Ks ratio. For the majority of gene pairs, Ka/Ks values were below 1.0, indicating that the pairs have undergone primarily purifying selection (Table 3). A small number of gene pairs (e.g., CmPLATZ1b/1c, CmPLATZ16b/16c) yielded Ks = 0 due to no synonymous substitutions, while others (e.g., CmPLATZ8a/3a) could not be calculated reliably due to extremely divergent alignments; these pairs were excluded from the statistical summary. Additionally, only one tandemly duplicated gene pair (CmPLATZ15c and CmPLATZ16c) was identified on Chr3 (Fig. 4). This finding suggests that WGD events, rather than tandem duplication, are the primary driver of the expansion of the CmPLATZ gene family in C. morifolium.

We also performed synteny analysis between C. morifolium and two diploid relatives, C. makinoi and C. seticuspe. Collinear gene pairs were identified between C. makinoi (four chromosomes: Chr1, Chr3, Chr5, Chr6), and C. morifolium (12 chromosomes). Similarly, collinearity was detected between C. seticuspe (six chromosomes) and C. morifolium (17 chromosomes) (Fig. 5A). Cross-species synteny analysis with Arabidopsis thaliana revealed numerous collinear blocks of PLATZ gene between the five chromosomes of Arabidopsis thaliana and the 27 chromosomes of C. morifolium, indicating evolutionary conservation (Fig. 5B).

Prediction of cis-acting elements in CmPLATZ promoters

Analysis of the 2000 bp promoter regions of the 41 CmPLATZ genes revealed numerous cis-acting regulatory elements related to three main functional categories: plant growth and development, hormone responses, and stress responses. Elements associated with growth and development (e.g., Box-4, G-Box, GT1-motif) and hormone responses (e.g., ABRE, TGACG-motif) were abundant in the promoters of most CmPLATZ genes (Fig. 6; Supplemental Table S3). Notably, cold-inducible cis-acting elements, including low-temperature responsive (LTR) and dehydration-responsive elements (DRE), were identified in multiple CmPLATZ promoters. A total of 31 L elements were detected in the promoters of 20 CmPLATZ genes. CmPLATZ3b contained the highest number of LTR elements (6), while others, such as CmPLATZ1a, CmPLATZ4b, and CmPLATZ4c, contained 1–3 LTRs (Fig. 6, Supplemental Table S3). These findings suggest a potential role for these genes in the plant response to cold stress.

Tissue-specific and cold-induced expression patterns of the CmPLATZ gene family

Analysis of transcriptome data from various tissues revealed that CmPLATZ genes exhibit distinct tissue-specific expression profiles (Fig. 7A, Supplemental Table S4 and S7). For example, CmPLATZ2 and CmPLATZ14c were expressed highly in disc floret tissues (petals and pistils), whereas CmPLATZ3 and CmPLATZ8 were expressed highly in ray florets. Other genes, such as CmPLATZ6, CmPLATZ7, and CmPLATZ9, showed preferential expression in vegetative tissues such as roots. Some CmPLATZ genes maintained lower expression across most tissues, suggesting functional divergence within the family (Fig. 7A, Supplemental Table S4).

To explore the response patterns of the CmPLATZ family to cold stress, we analyzed expression profiles under different treatments of low temperature: T01 (control); T02 (4℃ for 24 h); T03 (4℃ for 24 h followed by -4℃ for 4 h); and T04 (-4℃ for 4 h) (Supplemental Table S5). The expression of several CmPLATZ genes, including CmPLATZ1, CmPLATZ3, CmPLATZ4, and CmPLATZ5, was altered significantly by cold treatments. Specifically, CmPLATZ1 and CmPLATZ4 were upregulated significantly under sustained 4 °C stress (T02) and maintained high expression during gradient cooling (T03). In contrast, CmPLATZ3 was downregulated at 4 °C but was upregulated strongly at -4 °C (T04), along with CmPLATZ5b/c, suggesting these genes may be more involved in the response to freezing stress. Other genes, such as CmPLATZ9a/c, showed stable basal expression (Fig. 7B, Supplemental Table S5 and S8), indicating varying response capacities to chilling versus freezing stress within the family.

Expression of CmPLATZ genes in chrysanthemum accessions with varying cold tolerance

Following open-field overwintering, the cold hardiness of 11 chrysanthemum accessions was evaluated in spring 2025, and among these four (2014-D3, 2014-D8, 2014-D15, and 2022-D3) displayed notable vigor in rhizome growth and speed of recovery. In contrast, seven other accessions showed significantly weaker recovery, with stagnant growth or minimal rhizome regrowth; two of these (D074 and D148) did not survive the winter (Fig. 8A). The number of regenerated buds from rhizomes was significantly higher in the four tolerant accessions (average of 32–81 buds per plant) compared to the seven sensitive accessions (0–20 buds per plant) (Fig. 8B). Based on these overwintering phenotypes, the 11 accessions were classified as either cold-tolerant (four accessions) or cold-sensitive (seven accessions).

CmPLATZ1a and CmPLATZ4a are nuclear-localized proteins

Subcellular localization analysis revealed that both CmPLATZ1a and CmPLATZ4a are nuclear proteins (Fig. 11). To confirm this, in transiently transformed N. benthamiana leaves, the GFP signal from the empty vector control was distributed throughout the cells, including the nucleus and cell membrane. In contrast, the fluorescence signals from the CmPLATZ1a-GFP and CmPLATZ4a-GFP fusion proteins were exclusively localized to the nucleus, where the signals co-localized with the NLS-mCherry nuclear marker (Fig. 11). This nuclear localization is consistent with the presumed function as transcription factors.

Discussion

Structural conservation and expansion of the CmPLATZ gene family

Chrysanthemum is a key ornamental horticultural crop. Based on whole-genome data, we identified 41 CmPLATZ genes in the hexaploid Chrysanthemum morifolium genome, a number significantly larger than those found in diploid species like barley [12], rice [13], and maize [6]. This expansion is consistent with the hexaploid nature of chrysanthemum(2n = 6x = 54) and suggests that polyploidization events provide the raw material for gene duplication, potentially enhancing the ability of the plants to cope with complex environmental stresses.

The 41 CmPLATZ genes are distributed unevenly across 21 chromosomes, which may be a result of selective retention or loss of chromosomal segments during the complex genome evolution of chrysanthemum.

Studies on gene structure and subfamily classification highlight further the conservation of CmPLATZ genes: members within the same phylogenetic group exhibit similar exon-intron arrangements and conserved motifs (e.g., Motif 1 is present in all members) (Fig. 3). This structural conservation, similar to that observed in poplar and tomato PLATZ families [8, 14], implies that CmPLATZ proteins have retained the core ancestral functions associated with these domains, such as DNA binding. Meanwhile, unique motifs like Motif 5 and Motif 8 found only in specific subfamilies (V and VI) may be the basis for functional divergence that occurred during the domestication of chrysanthemum.

Evolutionary mechanisms driving CmPLATZ gene family diversification

Gene family expansion is a key evolutionary strategy for species to adapt to their environments. The expansion of the CmPLATZ gene family in C. morifolium appears to be driven primarily by WGD events rather than tandem duplication. Our analysis identified 68 collinear gene pairs, most of which arose from duplications within homologous chromosome groups, while only one tandemly duplicated pair was found (Fig. 4). This pattern is consistent with the hexaploid genomic structure of chrysanthemum (nine homologous groups, three chromosomes each), indicating that the evolutionary strategies of PLATZ gene families in other plants like bamboo and poplar are similar [8, 11].

Furthermore, most collinear gene pairs exhibited a Ka/Ks ratio of less than 1.0 (Fig. 4; Table 3), indicating that the CmPLATZ gene family has been predominantly under purifying selection after duplication. This process eliminates deleterious mutations typically, thereby conserving the essential functions of these genes and ensuring their stability over evolutionary time. Such selection pressure explains the high identity of amino acid sequence among CmPLATZ genes (e.g., CmPLATZ1a/1b/1c) and the conserved functions of the genes, providing a molecular guarantee for stable family function over long-term evolution.

The expansion and evolutionary trajectory of the PLATZ family in C. morifolium have been shaped uniquely by the hexaploid ancestry of chrysanthemum. Comparative synteny analysis reveals a fundamental genomic difference: the hexaploid chrysanthemum possesses a dramatically more complex network of PLATZ syntenic blocks compared to diploid wild relatives (C. makinoi and C. seticuspe; Fig. 5A). This evidence underscores WGD as the primary driver behind the numerical expansion of the family. However, mere duplication does not guarantee long-term retention. The observed divergence in expression patterns, manifested in both tissue specificity and cold stress responses, strongly suggests that many CmPLATZ copies were retained through functional diversification (e.g., subfunctionalization or neofunctionalization), thereby acquiring distinct, adaptively advantageous roles.

Cross-species synteny analysis with diploid species (C. makinoi and C. seticuspe) revealed more complex collinear relationships in hexaploid C. morifolium, supporting the hypothesis that its genome underwent significant rearrangements during polyploidization, contributing to its adaptability (Figs. 4 and 5A). Together with other expanded gene families, these events enhanced the adaptability of chrysanthemum to diverse habitats [56–57].

Additionally, synteny analysis between C. morifolium and Arabidopsis shows extensive collinear relationships of PLATZ genes (Fig. 5B), suggesting their PLATZ families share a common ancestor and have retained core gene arrangements during speciation. This provides an evolutionary basis for subsequent functional verification of CmPLATZ genes via heterologous expression in Arabidopsis.

CmPLATZ genes exhibit distinct tissue-specific expression patterns

Transcriptional regulation, regulated by cis-acting elements and trans-acting factors, represents a core mechanism governing gene expression dynamics in higher plants [58], and emerging evidence has underscored the pivotal role of PLATZ transcription factors in this regulatory network [59]. In Arabidopsis thaliana, AtPLATZ7 (AT2G12646) exhibits root meristem-specific localization, where it is induced by RGF1 and mediates reactive oxygen species (ROS) distribution to fine-tune root growth [60]. These findings collectively establish PLATZ proteins as versatile regulators of plant development and stress responses, yet the functional divergence of these proteins in hexaploid chrysanthemum remains poorly characterized.

Our study explores functional diversification of CmPLATZ in chrysanthemum via tissue-specific/cold-responsive expression profiling and promoter cis-element prediction, yielding critical insights. Notably, CmPLATZ genes show striking organ-specific functional partitioning (Fig. 7A), driven likely by polyploidization-associated genome rearrangements (supported by earlier synteny analysis; Figs. 4 and 5A) to fit developmental needs. For example, CmPLATZ3/8 are expressed highly in ray florets (petals, pistils), with promoters enriched in hormone/development-related cis-elements (Fig. 6). Since ray floret development dictates ornamental value in chrysanthemum (e.g., petal/pistil development), these CmPLATZ members may regulate floral organ development, filling gaps in understanding PLATZ functions in ornamental plant reproduction. In contrast, CmPLATZ2/6/10 are preferentially expressed in disc florets (stamens) and vegetative tissues (roots, stems). Their promoters harbor nutrient-responsive elements (e.g., ARE), linked to nutrient uptake/metabolism (e.g., phosphorus homeostasis). This suggests a novel PLATZ role in coordinating reproductive development and nutrient acquisition, expanding PLATZ functions beyond development and stress responses.

Additionally, cold-responsive analysis of expression levels of CmPLATZ genes complements these findings, showing subset-specific gene induction under low temperature. Integrated with cis-element predictions (e.g., cold-responsive motifs in CmPLATZ promoters), these data support that CmPLATZ diversification facilitates tissue-specific specialization and enhances cold tolerance in chrysanthemum. This aligns with expanded gene families enhancing polyploid environmental fitness [56–57].

CmPLATZ1a and CmPLATZ4a are key candidate regulators of cold tolerance

Cold stress is a major factor limiting the geographical distribution of many chrysanthemum varieties [25, 61]. Previous studies have indicated that the ability of rhizomes to survive and regenerate in the spring after overwintering is a key indicator of cold tolerance [23–24]. In this study, we evaluated 11 chrysanthemum accessions with quantifiable differences in overwintering ability to identify key genes involved in cold tolerance (Fig. 8). Our integrated analysis identifies CmPLATZ1 and CmPLATZ4 as key candidate genes regulating the response to cold stress in chrysanthemum. This conclusion is supported by a convergence of evidence from genetic association, functional validation, and independent transcriptional profiling.

In the natural overwintering trial, the expression levels of both genes were not only significantly elevated in cold-tolerant accessions (Results 7, Fig. 9) but also showed a strong positive correlation with rhizome bud regeneration, a direct measure of field-based cold hardiness (Figs. 9B and 10). To establish causal function, we conducted heterologous overexpression in Arabidopsis thaliana. Transgenic lines overexpressing CmPLATZ1a exhibited markedly enhanced cold tolerance compared to wild-type controls (Fig. 12C-D), providing direct experimental evidence for its conserved role in regulating the plant cold response pathway. In contrast, heterologous overexpression of CmPLATZ4a did not confer a similar cold-tolerant phenotype in Arabidopsis. This may be attributed to the functional context: in chrysanthemum, the strong correlation between the CmPLATZ4a expression and cold tolerance was observed primarily in rhizomes (Fig. 10D) rather than in leaves (Fig. 10C). Because Arabidopsis lacks this specialized storage organ, the heterologous system may not recapitulate fully the native regulatory environment or tissue-specific interactions essential for the function of CmPLATZ4a, highlighting the complexity of translating gene function across phylogenetically distant species. Therefore, while the Arabidopsis data provide valuable functional insight, direct validation in chrysanthemum will be essential to fully establish the role of CmPLATZ1a in rhizome-based overwintering.

Furthermore, the cold-inducible nature of these genes was not an isolated observation. It was initially revealed in an independent transcriptome dataset (Fig. 7B). Although the low-temperature treatments differed between the chrysanthemum transcriptome analysis and Arabidopsis functional validation, with the former designed to capture expression changes under varying temperatures and the latter to assess functional capacity under a more stringent freezing protocol, CmPLATZ1a was consistently cold-induced in both systems, and its overexpression enhanced freezing tolerance in Arabidopsis. It was initially revealed in an independent transcriptome dataset (Fig. 7B). Together, evidence from field studies, heterologous functional assays, and orthogonal expression analyses suggests a functional role for CmPLATZ1 and CmPLATZ4 in contributing to chrysanthemum cold tolerance.

Additional molecular clues point to a potential link between these genes and cold response. Their promoter regions contain LTR elements, which are known to be involved in cold-inducible gene expression (Fig. 6). However, the presence of these cis-acting elements provides a predictive clue rather than definitive proof of regulatory activity; their functional validation remains a goal for future studies. As nuclear-localized proteins, CmPLATZ1 and CmPLATZ4 are predicted to function as transcription factors that modulate the expression of downstream cold-responsive genes (Table 2; Fig. 11). In support of this regulatory role, the promoter regions of both genes contain cold-responsive elements (e.g., LTR) (Fig. 6), a well-characterized motif associated with cold-inducible gene expression in plants. However, the presence of these elements only provides in silico predictive evidence, not definitive proof of their cold-responsive regulatory function. Experimental validation of their activity of these LTR elements under cold stress will be performed in our future studies. A previous GWAS study analyzing the relationships between genotype, phenotype, and bioclimatic variables in common bean (Phaseolus vulgaris L.) identified a PLATZ transcription factor (Phvul.009G241300) that may be closely associated with the geographical distribution and climatic adaptation of common bean [62]. Based on the conserved DNA-binding properties of PLATZ family transcription factors [63] and our observation that endogenous cold-responsive genes were significantly upregulated in CmPLATZ1a-overexpression transgenic Arabidopsis, we speculate that CmPLATZ1a and CmPLATZ4a may function as transcription factors involved in cold tolerance. This regulatory role is likely mediated through direct or indirect transcriptional modulation of cold signaling pathway-related genes, including CBF3 and KIN1 (Fig. 12I and F). Elucidating the precise transcriptional regulatory mechanisms of CmPLATZ1 and CmPLATZ4, including their upstream regulators and downstream targets, remains an important objective for future research, ideally employing molecular assays such as dual-luciferase reporter assays, ChIP-seq, or EMSA.

In contrast, other CmPLATZ gene members (e.g., CmPLATZ8/11) showed minimal expression differences between cold-tolerant and cold-sensitive germplasms (Fig. 10C, D), suggesting they may play auxiliary roles in cold response or, similar to certain PLATZ gene members in other plants, participate in regulating growth and development or responses to other osmotic stresses such as drought and salt [64–66], further reflecting the functional diversity of the CmPLATZ gene family.

Conclusion

This study presents the first genome-wide analysis of the CmPLATZ gene family in C. morifolium, clarifying its expansion through WGD events and its evolution under purifying selection. These findings advance our understanding of PLATZ gene family evolution and provide a framework for the analysis of gene family in complex polyploid genomes. Functionally, this work not only identified CmPLATZ1a and CmPLATZ4a as key cold-tolerance candidates through expression profiling and promoter analysis, but also, demonstrated directly the functional capacity of CmPLATZ1a in enhancing cold tolerance via heterologous overexpression in Arabidopsis, providing experimental support for the regulatory role of this gene in responses to cold stress. From a practical standpoint, CmPLATZ1 and CmPLATZ4 have been identified as promising candidate genes for breeding cold-resistant chrysanthemum varieties, which could help mitigate low-temperature constraints on large-scale cultivation. Future studies will focus on (1) validating the direct role of CmPLATZ1 and CmPLATZ4 in cold tolerance using CRISPR-Cas9-mediated gene editing or overexpression in Chrysanthemum, coupled with physiological assays under cold stress; (2) Identifying the direct downstream targets and interacting protein partners of CmPLATZ1 and CmPLATZ4 protein through techniques like ChIP-seq and yeast two-hybrid screening to unravel their regulatory networks; and (3) Expanding the analysis scope to investigate the roles of the CmPLATZ gene family in response to other abiotic stresses to identify candidates for breeding multi-tolerant germplasm.

Scattering for the nonlinear Schrödinger equation with concentrated nonlinearity
Benjamin Harrop-Griffiths, Rowan Killip, Monica Vişan

Abstract
. We consider the cubic defocusing nonlinear Schrödinger equation in one dimension with the nonlinearity concentrated at a single point. We prove global well-posedness in the scaling-critical space L 2 ​ ( ℝ ) L^{2}(\mathbb{R}) and scattering for all such solutions. Moreover, we demonstrate that the same phenomenology holds whenever nonlinear effects are sufficiently concentrated in space.



1.
Introduction

We consider the nonlinear Schrödinger equation

(NLS
g
)

i\psi_{t}=-\psi_{xx}+g(x)|\psi|^{2}\psi

in one space dimension. This equation describes the time evolution of a ‘wave function’
\psi(t)\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
. Notice that we allow the coupling
g(x)
of the nonlinearity to vary in space; indeed, we wish to discuss the case where
g(x)
is strongly concentrated around a single point. To this end, we fix
g\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
satisfying
\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(x)\,dx=1
and then consider the rescaled coupling constants

(1.1)

g_{\varepsilon}(x):=\tfrac{1}{\varepsilon}g\bigl(\tfrac{x}{\varepsilon}\bigr)

with
\varepsilon>0
small.

Evidently,
\int_{\mathbb{R}}g_{\varepsilon}=1
for all
\varepsilon>0
. The fact that this integral is positive is crucial for our analysis; it gives the model a defocusing flavor. Notice however that we do
not
demand that
g\geq 0
pointwise. We have chosen that
\int_{\mathbb{R}}g=1
for simplicity; other values can be accommodated by a simple rescaling.

Sending
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
, leads to the point-concentrated nonlinear Schrödinger equation,

(NLS
δ
)

i\psi_{t}=-\psi_{xx}+\delta(x)|\psi|^{2}\psi,

in which the coupling coefficient has become the Dirac delta function. A considerable theory has been developed around this equation in recent years; see the review article

[
7
]
. In particular, the seemingly naive
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
limit just described has been shown to hold rigorously on compact time intervals for sufficiently regular initial data
[
2
,
6
]
; specifically, these papers demand initial data
\psi_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
.

The problem we analyze in this paper combines three challenges: we wish to describe the
long-time
behavior for
large
initial data at
critical
regularity.

Any intrinsic notion of size must naturally respect the scaling symmetry of (
NLSδ
), namely,

(1.2)

\psi(t,x)\mapsto\sqrt{\lambda}\psi(\lambda^{2}t,\lambda x)\quad\text{for $\lambda>0$}.

This scaling also leaves the mass functional

(1.3)

M[\psi]=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\psi|^{2}\,dx

invariant, thereby identifying this model as
mass-critical
. The mass is also conserved under the (
NLSδ
) dynamics (we will verify this later). In this way, we are directed toward the most natural (and most ambitious) class of initial data:
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
.

Local well-posedness of (
NLSδ
) in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
was shown previously in
[
1
]
; moreover, global well-posedness and scattering have been shown under suitable smallness assumptions in
[
1
,
5
]
. Our main result addresses arbitrarily large initial data:

Theorem 1.1
.

The equation (
NLSδ
) is globally well-posed in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Moreover,

(1.4)

\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\psi(t,0)|^{4}\,dt\lesssim\|\psi(0)\|_{L^{2}}^{4}

and every solution scatters, that is, there are asymptotic states
\psi_{\pm}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
so that

(1.5)

\|\psi(t)-e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}}\psi_{\pm}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\to 0\quad\text{as}\quad t\to\pm\infty.

This result will be proven in Section
3
building on the local theory developed in Section
2
. The crucial ingredient for treating large data globally in time is the new spacetime estimate provided by Proposition
3.1
. This estimate expresses the defocusing nature of our equation in a manner that is reminiscent of Morawetz-type identities. However, the estimate differs from these well-known monotonicity formulas by expressing a direct inequality between the linear and nonlinear flows. Moreover, the proof is also very different; it is not centered on a quantity that is monotone in time.

Earlier we motivated the consideration of the point-concentrated nonlinearity as the limit of (
NLSg
) with nonlinear coupling
g_{\varepsilon}(x)
defined in (
1.1
). In Section
4
we return to this model and show that solutions of this model closely follow those of (
NLSδ
) provided
\varepsilon
is sufficiently small, depending on the initial data. (A simple scaling argument shows that we cannot choose
\varepsilon
independently of the initial data.) The precise formulation of our result is as follows:

Theorem 1.2
.

Fix
g\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
with
\int_{\mathbb{R}}g=1
. Let
\psi^{\varepsilon}(t)
denote the solutions to

(NLS
{}_{g_{\epsilon}}
)

i\psi^{\varepsilon}_{t}=-\psi^{\varepsilon}_{xx}+g_{\varepsilon}(x)|\psi^{\varepsilon}|^{2}\psi^{\varepsilon},

with initial data
\psi^{\varepsilon}(0)\to\psi(0)
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. There exists
\varepsilon_{0}>0
so that for all
0<\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}
, the equation (
NLS

g
ϵ

) is globally well-posed and scatters in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Moreover,

(1.6)

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\ \sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\ \|\psi^{\varepsilon}(t)-\psi(t)\|_{L^{2}}=0,

where
\psi
denotes the solution to (
NLSδ
) with initial data
\psi(0)
.

2.
Preliminaries and local theory

We start with notation. Our convention for the inner product on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
is

\langle g,f\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\overline{g(x)}f(x)\,dx.

Given a Banach space
X
and an interval
I
, we write
C(I;X)
for the space of bounded continuous functions
\psi:I\to X
equipped with the supremum norm. The omission of
X
indicates
X=\mathbb{C}
; similarly, the omission of
I
indicates
I=\mathbb{R}
.

We write
\mathcal{M}
to denote the space of finite signed Borel measures on
\mathbb{R}
, equipped with the total variation norm. We recall that
\mathcal{M}
is dual to the closed subspace
C^{0}(\mathbb{R})\subseteq C(\mathbb{R})
of continuous functions vanishing at infinity.

If
\mu\in\mathcal{M}
and
1\leq p<\infty
, we take
L_{\mu}^{p}
to be the space of (equivalence classes of) Borel-measurable
\psi\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
with finite norm

\|\psi\|_{L_{\mu}^{p}}^{p}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\psi(x)|^{p}\,d|\mu|(x).

For any
1\leq p\leq\infty
, we have

(2.1)

\|\psi\|_{L_{\mu}^{p}}\leq\|\mu\|_{\mathcal{M}}^{\frac{1}{p}}\|\psi\|_{C}

and so by duality,

(2.2)

\|h\mu\|_{\mathcal{M}}\leq\|\mu\|_{\mathcal{M}}^{\frac{1}{p}}\|h\|_{L_{\mu}^{p^{\prime}}},

where
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1
.

When using mixed spacetime norms, we will use subscripts to indicate the corresponding variable, e.g.,
C_{t}L_{x}^{2}=C(\mathbb{R};L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))
. If
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
is an interval, we use the subscript
I
to indicate that the time domain is
I
instead of
\mathbb{R}
, e.g.,
C_{I}L_{x}^{2}=C(I;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))
.

Our first result is a small refinement of the endpoint Strichartz estimate, drawing attention to continuity and decay at infinity.

Lemma 2.1
.

For any
\psi_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
and any forcing term
f\in L_{t}^{\frac{4}{3}}\mathcal{M}
,

(2.3)

\psi(t):=e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}}\psi_{0}-i\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-s)\partial_{x}^{2}}f(s)\,ds

satisfies the Strichartz bounds

(2.4)

\|\psi\|_{C_{t}L_{x}^{2}\cap L_{t}^{4}C^{0}_{x}}\lesssim\|\psi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\|f\|_{L_{t}^{4/3}\mathcal{M}}.

Proof.

The classical Strichartz estimate from
[
4
]
shows that

(2.5)

\|e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}}\psi_{0}\|_{C_{t}L_{x}^{2}\cap L_{t}^{4}L_{x}^{\infty}}\lesssim\|\psi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}.

By virtue of the Sobolev embedding
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})\hookrightarrow C^{0}(\mathbb{R})
, we have

\|e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}}\psi_{0}\|_{C^{0}_{x}}\lesssim\|e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}}\psi_{0}\|_{H^{1}}\lesssim\|\psi_{0}\|_{H^{1}}\quad\text{for all $t\in\mathbb{R}$}.

Thus, for any
\psi_{0}\in H^{1}
, we may upgrade (
2.5
) to

(2.6)

\|e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}}\psi_{0}\|_{C_{t}L_{x}^{2}\cap L_{t}^{4}C^{0}_{x}}\lesssim\|\psi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}.

The restriction that
\psi_{0}\in H^{1}
is then easily removed by exploiting the density of
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
.

Recalling that
\mathcal{M}
is dual to
C^{0}
, we deduce the dual estimate

(2.7)

\biggl\|\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-it\partial_{x}^{2}}f(t)\,dt\biggr\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L_{t}^{4/3}\mathcal{M}_{x}}.

The proof of (
2.4
) is now completed by combining (
2.6
) and (
2.7
) with the Christ–Kiselev lemma
[
3
]
.
∎

We now turn to the problem of well-posedness. Here, we will treat the rather more general model

(NLS
μ
)

i\psi_{t}=-\psi_{xx}+|\psi|^{2}\psi\,d\mu,

where
\mu\in\mathcal{M}
. We will only consider strong solutions to this equation:

Definition 2.2
.

Let
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
be an interval. We say
\psi\in C_{I}L_{x}^{2}
is a
solution
to (
NLSμ
) if
\psi\in L_{I,\mathrm{loc}}^{4}C_{x}
and for some
t_{0}\in I
and all
t\in I
we have

(2.8)

\psi(t)=e^{i(t-t_{0})\partial_{x}^{2}}\psi(t_{0})-i\int_{t_{0}}^{t}e^{i(t-s)\partial_{x}^{2}}\bigl[|\psi(s)|^{2}\psi(s)\,d\mu\bigr]\,ds.

Notice that the estimate (
2.4
) ensures that (
2.8
) makes sense for
\psi\in C_{I}L_{x}^{2}\cap L_{I,\mathrm{loc}}^{4}C_{x}
. Moreover, a straightforward computation shows that solutions of (
NLSμ
) satisfy (
2.8
) for
all
choices of
t_{0}\in I
.

One may also easily verify the following time translation and reversal symmetries of (
NLSμ
): If
\psi(t)
is a solution to (
NLSμ
), then so too are
t\mapsto\psi(t+t_{0})
and
t\mapsto\overline{\psi(-t)}
. These symmetries allow us to focus our development of the local well-posedness theory to time intervals of the form
[0,T)
.

Theorem 2.3

(Local theory for (
NLSμ
))
.

(i)

For any
\psi_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, there is a
T>0
and a solution
\psi:[0,T)\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
to (
NLSμ
) with
\psi(0)=\psi_{0}
.

(ii)

If
\psi,\phi
are solutions to (
NLSμ
) that agree at one time, then they agree throughout their common domain.

(iii)

The maximal existence interval (forward in time) has the form
[0,T_{\textit{max}}(\psi_{0}))
. If the maximal solution satisfies

(2.9)

\int_{0}^{T_{\textit{max}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\psi(t,x)|^{4}\,d|\mu|(x)\,dt<\infty,

then
T_{\textit{max}}=\infty
and the solution scatters forward in time
(
cf. (
1.5
)
)
.

(iv)

There exists
\eta_{0}>0
, depending only on
\mu
, so that
\|\psi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\eta_{0}
implies (
2.9
).

(v)

If
\psi_{n,0}\to\psi_{0}
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, then the corresponding solutions
\psi_{n}(t)
converge to
\psi(t)
in
C_{t}L^{2}\cap L^{4}_{t}C^{0}_{x}
on any time interval
[0,T]
with
T<T_{\textit{max}}(\psi_{0})
. In particular, the mapping
\psi_{0}\to T_{\textit{max}}(\psi_{0})
is lower semicontinuous.

(vi)

Mass is conserved under the flow:
M[\psi(t)]=M[\psi_{0}];
see (
1.3
).

Proof of
(i)
: Existence.
.

Given
\eta>0
and
\psi_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, we may combine (
2.4
) with monotone convergence to find
T=T(\eta,\psi_{0})>0
so that

(2.10)

\|e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}}\psi_{0}\|_{L_{[0,T)}^{4}C_{x}}\leq\eta.

We then define the closed ball

B=\bigl\{\psi\in L_{[0,T)}^{4}L_{\mu}^{4}:\|\psi\|_{L_{[0,T)}^{4}L_{\mu}^{4}}\leq 2\eta\|\mu\|_{\mathcal{M}}^{\frac{1}{4}}\bigr\},

and consider the mapping

\Phi[\psi](t)=e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}}\psi_{0}-i\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-s)\partial_{x}^{2}}\bigl[|\psi(s)|^{2}\psi(s)\,d\mu\bigr]\,ds.

Applying (
2.1
), (
2.2
), and (
2.4
), for
\psi\in B
we may estimate

\displaystyle\|\Phi[\psi]\|_{L_{[0,T)}^{4}L_{\mu}^{4}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\mu\|_{\mathcal{M}}^{\frac{1}{4}}\|\Phi[\psi]\|_{L_{[0,T)}^{4}C_{x}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\mu\|_{\mathcal{M}}^{\frac{1}{4}}\Bigl[\|e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}}\psi_{0}\|_{L_{[0,T)}^{4}C_{x}}+A\||\psi|^{2}\psi\mu\|_{L_{[0,T)}^{4/3}\mathcal{M}}\Bigr]

\displaystyle\leq\|\mu\|_{\mathcal{M}}^{\frac{1}{4}}\Bigl[\eta+A\|\mu\|_{\mathcal{M}}^{\frac{1}{4}}\|\psi\|_{L_{[0,T)}^{4}L_{\mu}^{4}}^{3}\Bigr]

\displaystyle\leq\|\mu\|_{\mathcal{M}}^{\frac{1}{4}}\Bigl[\eta+8A\eta^{3}\|\mu\|_{\mathcal{M}}\Bigr],

for some absolute constant
A>0
dictated by the Strichartz inequality (
2.4
). Similarly, for
\psi,\phi\in B
we may bound the difference

\|\Phi[\psi]-\Phi[\phi]\|_{L_{[0,T)}^{4}L_{\mu}^{4}}\leq 24A\eta^{2}\|\mu\|_{\mathcal{M}}\|\psi-\phi\|_{L_{[0,T)}^{4}L_{\mu}^{4}}.

For
\eta>0
sufficiently small, depending only on
\|\mu\|_{\mathcal{M}}
, these estimates show that
\Psi\colon B\to B
and that this mapping is a contraction. Thus, there is a unique
\psi\in B
satisfying
\psi=\Phi[\psi]
. Applying (
2.2
) and (
2.4
) once again, we see that

(2.11)

\displaystyle\|\psi\|_{C_{[0,T)}L_{x}^{2}\cap L_{[0,T)}^{4}C_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\psi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\||\psi|^{2}\psi\mu\|_{L_{[0,T)}^{4/3}\mathcal{M}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\psi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\mu\|_{\mathcal{M}}^{\frac{1}{4}}\|\psi\|_{L_{[0,T)}^{4}L_{\mu}^{4}}^{3}<\infty,

and so
\psi\in C_{[0,T)}L_{x}^{2}\cap L_{[0,T)}^{4}C_{x}
is a solution to (
NLSμ
) satisfying
\psi(0)=\psi_{0}
.
∎

Proof of
(ii)
: Uniqueness.
.

Suppose for a contradiction that two solutions
\psi
and
\phi
agree at some time
t_{0}
, but disagree at another time
t_{1}>t_{0}
. As both solutions are
L^{2}_{x}
-continuous and belong to
L_{\mathrm{loc}}^{4}C_{x}
, we may choose such a pair of times so close together that for any prescribed
\eta>0
,

\|\psi\|_{L_{I}^{4}L_{\mu}^{4}}+\|\phi\|_{L_{I}^{4}L_{\mu}^{4}}\leq\eta

on the interval
I=[t_{0},t_{1}]
. Arguing as in our proof of existence, we may then apply the estimates (
2.1
), (
2.2
), and (
2.4
) to the Duhamel formula (
2.8
) to bound

\|\psi-\phi\|_{C_{I}L^{2}_{x}\cap L_{I}^{4}L_{\mu}^{4}}\leq 3A\eta^{2}\|\mu\|_{\mathcal{M}}\|\psi-\phi\|_{L_{I}^{4}L_{\mu}^{4}},

where the constant
A
is chosen as before. When
\eta
is taken to be sufficiently small, depending only on
\|\mu\|_{\mathcal{M}}
, we deduce the desired contradiction.
∎

Proof of
(iii)
: Blowup/scattering criterion
.

If
T_{\textit{max}}
were contained in the interval of existence, then we could apply our existence theorem to extend the solution beyond this time; this would contradict the maximality of the solution.

Our next goal is to show that if (
2.9
) holds, then
e^{-it\partial_{x}^{2}}\psi(t)
is
L^{2}
-Cauchy as
t\uparrow T_{\textit{max}}
. When
T_{\textit{max}}=\infty
, this immediately yields scattering. If instead
T_{\textit{max}}<\infty
, then we could extend
\psi(t)
continuously to
t=T_{\textit{max}}
, contradicting the first paragraph of this proof.

For any pair of times
t_{0},t_{1}\in[T,T_{\textit{max}})
, we may apply (
2.4
) to the identity (
2.8
) to obtain

\displaystyle\|e^{-it_{1}\partial_{x}^{2}}\psi(t_{1})-e^{-it_{0}\partial_{x}^{2}}\psi(t_{0})\|_{L_{x}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigl\||\psi|^{2}\psi\,d\mu\bigr\|_{L_{[T,T_{\textit{max}})}^{4/3}\mathcal{M}}\lesssim\|\mu\|_{\mathcal{M}}^{\frac{1}{4}}\|\psi\|_{L_{[T,T_{\textit{max}})}^{4}L^{4}_{\mu}}^{3}.

From (
2.9
) and dominated convergence, we deduce that
e^{-it\partial_{x}^{2}}\psi(t)
is indeed
L^{2}
-Cauchy as
t\uparrow T_{\textit{max}}
.
∎

Proof of
(iv)
: Small-data global bounds.
.

By applying (
2.4
), we see that if
\|\psi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}
is sufficiently small then (
2.10
) is satisfied with
T=\infty
. Thus, the proof of part (i) gives global existence (
T_{\textit{max}}=\infty
) and (
2.9
) for all sufficiently small initial data.
∎

Proof of
(v)
: Continuous dependence.
.

Let
\psi:[0,T_{\textit{max}})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
be the maximal forward solution with initial data
\psi_{0}
. Our first goal is the following: given
T<T_{\textit{max}}
, we wish to exhibit
\delta>0
so that any initial data with

(2.12)

\|\varphi_{0}-\psi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\delta

leads to a solution
\varphi(t)
throughout the interval
[0,T]
. In the process, we will obtain a quantitative bound on

(2.13)

\textsl{Error}(t):=\|\psi-\varphi\|_{C_{[0,t]}L_{x}^{2}\cap L_{[0,t]}^{4}C_{x}}

that allows us to conclude that
\textsl{Error}(T)\to 0
as
\varphi_{0}\to\psi_{0}
.

The choice of
T
ensures that
\psi
has finite
L^{4}_{t}C_{x}
norm on
[0,T]
. Thus, given any
\eta>0
, we may partition this interval into

J_{m}:=[t_{m-1},t_{m}]\quad\text{with}\quad 0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\dots<t_{M}=T,

in such a way that

(2.14)

\|\psi\|_{L_{J_{m}}^{4}C_{x}}\leq\eta\quad\text{for all $m=1,\ldots,M$}.

Working efficiently, the number of intervals
M=M(\eta)
can be ensured to satisfy

(2.15)

M\leq\tfrac{1}{\eta^{4}}\|\psi\|_{L_{[0,T]}^{4}C_{x}}^{4}+1.

We also define a ‘stopping time’
\tau\in[0,T]
as the maximal such
\tau
so that

(2.16)

\|\psi-\varphi\|_{L_{[0,\tau]}^{4}C_{x}}\leq 2\eta.

Evidently,
\tau
depends on
\varphi_{0}
and
\eta
. Part
(iii)
shows that the solution
\varphi
cannot blow up before time
\tau
. If
\tau\geq t_{m-1}
, then we write
J_{m}^{\tau}=[t_{m-1},t_{m}\wedge\tau]
.

Applying (
2.4
) in (
2.8
) and using (
2.14
) and (
2.16
), we conclude that

\|\psi-\varphi\|_{C_{J_{m}^{\tau}}L_{x}^{2}\cap L_{J_{m}^{\tau}}^{4}C_{x}}\lesssim\|\psi(t_{m-1})-\varphi(t_{m-1})\|_{L^{2}}+\eta^{2}\|\mu\|_{\mathcal{M}}\|\psi-\varphi\|_{L_{J_{m}^{\tau}}^{4}C_{x}},

for each
m=1,\dots,M
with
\tau\geq t_{m-1}
. Thus if
\eta>0
is sufficiently small, depending only on
\|\mu\|_{\mathcal{M}}
, we have

\textsl{Error}(t_{m}\wedge\tau)\leq e^{A}\textsl{Error}(t_{m-1}),

for some constant
A>0
(that may be different from before). Applying this estimate iteratively for
m=1,\dots,M
, we may use (
2.15
) to conclude that

(2.17)

\textsl{Error}(T\wedge\tau)\leq\exp\Bigl(A\Bigl[\tfrac{1}{\eta^{4}}\|\psi\|_{L_{[0,T]}^{4}C_{x}}^{4}+1\Bigr]\Bigr)\|\psi_{0}-\varphi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}.

Now if
0<\delta\ll 1
is chosen so small that RHS(
2.17
) is less than
2\eta
, then a continuity argument yields that
\tau=T
and (
2.16
) and holds throughout
[0,T]
. In this way, we deduce both the existence of the solution
\varphi
and the Lipschitz bound

(2.18)

\|\psi-\varphi\|_{C_{[0,T]}L_{x}^{2}\cap L_{[0,T]}^{4}C_{x}}\lesssim\exp\Bigl(A\Bigl[\tfrac{1}{\eta^{4}}\|\psi\|_{L_{[0,T]}^{4}C_{x}}^{4}+1\Bigr]\Bigr)\|\psi_{0}-\varphi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}.

valid for any initial data
\varphi_{0}
satisfying (
2.12
).
∎

Proof of
(vi)
: Mass conservation
.

As
e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}}
is unitary, we infer from (
2.8
) that

\displaystyle\|\psi(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\biggl\|\psi_{0}-i\int_{0}^{t}e^{-is\partial_{x}^{2}}\bigl[\psi(s)|^{2}\psi(s)\,d\mu\bigr]\,ds\biggr\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\psi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+2\operatorname{Im}\biggl\langle\psi_{0},\int_{0}^{t}e^{-is\partial_{x}^{2}}\bigl[|\psi(s)|^{2}\psi(s)\,d\mu\bigr]\,ds\biggr\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+\biggl\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{-is\partial_{x}^{2}}\bigl[|\psi(s)|^{2}\psi(s)\,d\mu\bigr]\,ds\biggr\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Employing the dual Strichartz estimate (
2.7
) to justify the inner products, we may write the second and third terms as follows:

\displaystyle 2\operatorname{Im}\biggl\langle\psi_{0},\int_{0}^{t}e^{-is\partial_{x}^{2}}\bigl[|\psi(s)|^{2}\psi(s)\,d\mu\bigr]\,ds\biggr\rangle=2\operatorname{Im}\int_{0}^{t}\bigl\langle e^{is\partial_{x}^{2}}\psi_{0},|\psi(s)|^{2}\psi(s)\,d\mu\bigr\rangle\,ds,

\displaystyle\biggl\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{-is\partial_{x}^{2}}\bigl[|\psi(s)|^{2}\psi(s)\,d\mu\bigr]\,ds\biggr\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad=2\operatorname{Re}\int_{0}^{t}\biggl\langle\int_{0}^{s}e^{i(s-\sigma)\partial_{x}^{2}}\bigl[|\psi(\sigma)|^{2}\psi(\sigma)\,d\mu\bigr]\,d\sigma,|\psi(s)|^{2}\psi(s)\,d\mu\biggr\rangle\,ds.

Combining these and recognizing (
2.8
), we get

\|\psi(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\|\psi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+2\operatorname{Im}\int_{0}^{t}\bigl\langle\psi(s),|\psi(s)|^{2}\psi(s)\,d\mu\bigr\rangle\,ds=\|\psi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

which is precisely the conservation of mass.
∎

3.
Global solutions and scattering for a point nonlinearity

For
t\neq 0
, the fundamental solution of the linear Schrödinger equation is

(3.1)

k(t,x):=(e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}}\delta)(x)=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi|t|}}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4it}-\frac{i\pi}{4}\sign(t)}.

Two properties of this kernel that play an essential role in our analysis are

(3.2)

\overline{k(t,0)}=k(-t,0)=i\sign(t)k(t,0)\quad\text{for all $t\neq 0$.}

Proposition 3.1
.

If
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
is an open interval containing zero and
\psi\in C_{I}L_{x}^{2}\cap L_{I,\mathrm{loc}}^{4}C_{x}
is the strong solution of (
NLSδ
) satisfying
\psi(0)=\psi_{0}
, then

(3.3)

\int_{I}|\psi(t,0)|^{4}\,dt\leq\|e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}}\psi_{0}\|_{L_{I}^{4}C_{x}}^{4}.

Proof.

We abbreviate the nonlinearity as

(3.4)

F(t,x)=|\psi(t,x)|^{2}\psi(t,x).

For any
0<T\in I
, we employ (
3.2
) to obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}|\psi(t,0)|^{4}\,dt

\displaystyle\qquad=\operatorname{Re}\int_{0}^{T}\overline{F(t,0)}\psi(t,0)\,dt

\displaystyle\qquad=\operatorname{Re}\int_{0}^{T}\overline{F(t,0)}\bigl(e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}}\psi_{0}\bigr)(0)\,dt-\operatorname{Re}i\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{t}\overline{F(t,0)}k(t-s,0)F(s,0)\,ds\,dt

\displaystyle\qquad=\operatorname{Re}\int_{0}^{T}\overline{F(t,0)}\bigl(e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}}\psi_{0}\bigr)(0)\,dt

\displaystyle\qquad\quad-\tfrac{1}{2}\operatorname{Re}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}\overline{F(t,0)}\,i\sign(t-s)k(t-s,0)F(s,0)\,ds\,dt

The last step here is symmetrization of the double integral under
s\leftrightarrow t
employing the first relation in (
3.2
). Using the second relation, we see that the resulting double integral is actually sign-definite:

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}\overline{F(t,0)}k(s-t,0)F(s,0)\,ds\,dt=\left\|\int_{0}^{T}e^{-it\partial_{x}^{2}}\bigl[F(t)\,\delta\bigr]\,dt\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\geq 0.

Incorporating this information into our earlier computation and using Hölder’s inequality, we deduce that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}|\psi(t,0)|^{4}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{Re}\int_{0}^{T}\overline{F(t,0)}\bigl(e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}}\psi_{0}\bigr)(0)\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\left[\int_{0}^{T}|\psi(t,0)|^{4}\,dt\right]^{\frac{3}{4}}\|e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}}\psi_{0}\|_{L_{[0,\infty)}^{4}C_{x}}.

In this way, we obtain the key bound forward in time:

\int_{0}^{T}|\psi(t,0)|^{4}\,dt\leq\|e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}}\psi_{0}\|_{L_{[0,\infty)}^{4}C_{x}}^{4}.

Sending
T\nearrow\sup I
and repeating this argument backward in time yields (
3.3
).
∎

1.1
is a straightforward corollary of Theorem
2.3
and Proposition
3.1

Combining the Strichartz estimate (

4.
Global solutions and scattering for concentrated nonlinearities

In this section we prove Theorem
1.2
. If it were true that
g_{\varepsilon}\to\delta
in
\mathcal{M}
, then this result would follow from a simple refinement of Theorem
2.3
. Unfortunately, this convergence only holds in a weak sense.

Our starting point is to obtain strong convergence for one of the forcing terms in the difference between
\psi^{\varepsilon}
and
\psi
. This requires the introduction of appropriate localizations in frequency or space:

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\psi\in C_{t}L_{x}^{2}\cap L_{t}^{4}C_{x}
be the unique global solution to (
NLSδ
) with initial data
\psi(0)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Then, for all dyadic
N\geq 1
, we have

(4.1)

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\left\|P_{\leq N}\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-s)\partial_{x}^{2}}\Bigl[|\psi(s)|^{2}\psi(s)\bigl[g_{\varepsilon}-\delta\bigr]\Bigr]\,ds\right\|_{C_{t}L_{x}^{2}}

\displaystyle=0,

(4.2)

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\left\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-s)\partial_{x}^{2}}\Bigl[|\psi(s)|^{2}\psi(s)\bigl[g_{\varepsilon}-\delta\bigr]\Bigr]\,ds\right\|_{L_{t}^{4}L^{4}_{g_{\varepsilon}}}

\displaystyle=0.

Here
P_{\leq N}
denotes the Littlewood–Paley projection to frequencies not exceeding
N
.

Proof.

Let
k(t,x)
denote the fundamental solution of the linear Schrödinger equation as in (
3.1
) and let
F(t,x):=|\psi(t,x)|^{2}\psi(t,x)
as in (
3.4
). As
\int_{\mathbb{R}}g_{\varepsilon}\,dx=1
, we may write

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-s)\partial_{x}^{2}}\Bigl[|\psi(s)|^{2}\psi(s)\bigl[g_{\varepsilon}-\delta\bigr]\Bigr]\,ds

\displaystyle\qquad=\underbrace{\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}k(t-s,x-y)\bigl[F(s,y)-F(s,0)\bigr]g_{\varepsilon}(y)\,dy\,ds}_{=:I_{\varepsilon}(t,x)}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\underbrace{\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\bigl[k(t-s,x-y)-k(t-s,x)\bigr]F(s,0)g_{\varepsilon}(y)\,dy\,ds}_{=:I\!I_{\varepsilon}(t,x)}.

We begin with the contribution of
I_{\varepsilon}(t,x)
to both (
4.1
) and (
4.2
). For almost every
s
,
F(s,y)
is a bounded and continuous function of
y
. For such
s
,

\bigl\|\bigl[F(s,y)-F(s,0)\bigr]g_{\varepsilon}(y)\bigr\|_{\mathcal{M}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\bigl|F(s,\varepsilon y^{\prime})-F(s,0)\bigr|\bigl|g(y^{\prime})\bigr|\,dy^{\prime}\longrightarrow 0

as
\varepsilon\to 0
. Moreover, we have the (uniform in
\varepsilon
) domination

\bigl\|\bigl[F(s,y)-F(s,0)\bigr]g_{\varepsilon}(y)\bigr\|_{\mathcal{M}}\leq 2\|g\|_{L^{1}}\|\psi(s)\|_{C_{x}}^{3}\in L^{4/3}_{s}(\mathbb{R}).

Thus, by the Dominated Convergence Theorem, we deduce that

\bigl\|\bigl[F(s,y)-F(s,0)\bigr]g_{\varepsilon}(y)\bigr\|_{L^{4/3}_{s}\mathcal{M}}\longrightarrow 0.

The inequality (
2.4
) then demonstrates that
I_{\varepsilon}(t,x)
does indeed make an acceptable contribution to both (
4.1
) and (
4.2
).

The contributions of
I\!I_{\varepsilon}(t,x)
are rather more subtle and we will treat (
4.1
) and (
4.2
) separately. Initially, we impose the restriction that
g
has compact support, concretely,
\supp(g)\subseteq[-R,R]
. This restriction will be removed later.

Let
K_{N}(x)=NK(Nx)
be the kernel of the Littlewood–Paley projection
P_{\leq N}
. Here
K\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
and so using the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus, we see that

(4.3)

\displaystyle\sup_{|z|\leq\varepsilon R}\ \int_{\mathbb{R}}\bigl|K_{N}(y-z)-K_{N}(y)\bigr|\,dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon NR.

After a change of variables, can write the low-frequency portion of
I\!I_{\varepsilon}
as follows:

\displaystyle P_{\leq N}I\!I_{\varepsilon}(t,x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}k(t-s,x-y)\bigl[K_{N}(y-z)-K_{N}(y)\bigr]F(s,0)g_{\varepsilon}(z)\,dz\,ds\,dy.

Recalling the Strichartz estimate (
2.4
), (
4.3
), and the support restriction on
g
, we can immediately deduce

\displaystyle\|P_{\leq N}I\!I_{\varepsilon}\|_{C_{t}L_{x}^{2}}\lesssim\varepsilon NR\|\psi\|_{L_{t}^{4}C_{x}}^{3}\|g\|_{L^{1}}\quad\text{uniformly for $0<\varepsilon\leq 1$.}

As this converges to zero as
\varepsilon\to 0
, we have now completed the proof of (
4.1
) for compactly supported
g
.

To bound the contribution of
I\!I_{\varepsilon}
to (
4.2
), we introduce

\displaystyle h_{\varepsilon}(t,s,x)

\displaystyle=\mathbbm{1}_{\{0<s<t\}\cup\{t<s<0\}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\bigl[k(t-s,x-y)-k(t-s,x)\bigr]F(s,0)g_{\varepsilon}(y)\,dy,

\displaystyle H(t,s)

\displaystyle=\mathbbm{1}_{\{0<s<t\}\cup\{t<s<0\}}\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{\pi|t-s|}}|F(s,0)|\|g\|_{L^{1}}.

By (
3.1
), we have

(4.4)

\sup_{|x|\leq R}|h_{\varepsilon}(t,s,x)|\leq H(t,s)\quad\text{for all $t,s\in\mathbb{R}$ and all $0<\varepsilon\leq 1$.}

Further, by the Hardy–Littlewood convolution inequality, we have

(4.5)

\left\|I\!I_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L_{t}^{4}L^{\infty}_{x}}\leq\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}}H(t,s)\,ds\right\|_{L_{t}^{4}}\lesssim\|\psi\|_{L_{t}^{4}C_{x}}^{3}\|g\|_{L^{1}},

which shows that
s\mapsto H(t,s)
is integrable for a.e.
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

As
F\in L_{t}^{4/3}C_{x}
, so
|F(s,0)|<\infty
for almost every
s\in\mathbb{R}
. For any such
s\neq t
,

\sup_{|x|\leq R}\ \sup_{|y|\leq R\varepsilon}\ |k(t-s,x-y)-k(t-s,x)||F(s,0)|\|g\|_{L^{1}}\to 0\quad\text{as}\quad\varepsilon\to 0,

due to the continuity of
z\mapsto k(\tau,z)
for
\tau\neq 0
. In this way, we see that

\sup_{|x|\leq R}\ |h_{\varepsilon}(t,s,x)|\to 0\quad\text{as}\quad\varepsilon\to 0\quad\text{for a.e. $t,s\in\mathbb{R}$.}

Applying the Dominated Convergence Theorem with the dominating function
s\mapsto H(t,s)
discussed in connection with (
4.5
), we deduce that

(4.6)

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\ \sup_{|x|\leq R}\ |I\!I_{\varepsilon}(t,x)|\leq\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sup_{|x|\leq R}\ |h_{\varepsilon}(t,s,x)|\,ds=0\quad\text{for a.e. $t\in\mathbb{R}$}

and consequently,

(4.7)

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\ \bigl\|\mathbbm{1}_{\{|x|\leq R\}}I\!I_{\varepsilon}(t,x)\bigr\|_{L^{4}_{g_{\varepsilon}}}=0\quad\text{for a.e. $t\in\mathbb{R}$}.

Finally, using (
4.5
), the Dominated Convergence Theorem completes the proof of (
4.2
), at least for compactly supported
g
.

To finish, we remove the restriction that
g
be of compact support. Given
R>0
, we set
A(R):=\int_{-R}^{R}g(x)\,dx
. By hypothesis
\int_{\mathbb{R}}g=1
and so
A(R)\to 1
as
R\to\infty
. For
R
sufficiently large,
A(R)>0
and we may decompose

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-s)\partial_{x}^{2}}\Bigl[|\psi(s)|^{2}\psi(s)\bigl[g_{\varepsilon}-\delta\bigr]\Bigr]\,ds

\displaystyle\qquad=A(R)\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-s)\partial_{x}^{2}}\Bigl[|\psi(s)|^{2}\psi(s)\bigl[\tfrac{1}{A(R)}g_{\varepsilon}\mathbbm{1}_{\{|x|\leq R\varepsilon\}}-\delta\bigr]\Bigr]\,ds

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-s)\partial_{x}^{2}}\Bigl[|\psi(s)|^{2}\psi(s)\,g_{\varepsilon}\mathbbm{1}_{\{|x|>R\varepsilon\}}\Bigr]\,ds

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\bigl[1-A(R)\bigr]\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-s)\partial_{x}^{2}}\Bigl[|\psi(s)|^{2}\psi(s)\,\delta\Bigr]\,ds.

Our earlier argument demonstrates that for any choice of
R
, the contribution of the first summand to (
4.1
) and (
4.2
) converges to zero as
\varepsilon\to 0
. For the second and third summands, we may apply (
2.4
) to obtain the
\varepsilon
-independent estimates

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-s)\partial_{x}^{2}}\Bigl[|\psi(s)|^{2}\psi(s)\,g_{\varepsilon}\mathbbm{1}_{\{|x|>R\varepsilon\}}\Bigr]\,ds\right\|_{C_{t}L_{x}^{2}\cap L_{t}^{4}C_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\psi\|_{L_{t}^{4}C_{x}}^{3}\|g\|_{L^{1}(|x|>R)}

\displaystyle\left\|\bigl[1-A(R)\bigr]\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-s)\partial_{x}^{2}}\Bigl[|\psi(s)|^{2}\psi(s)\,\delta\Bigr]\,ds\right\|_{C_{t}L_{x}^{2}\cap L_{t}^{4}C_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim|1-A(R)|\|\psi\|_{L_{t}^{4}C_{x}}^{3}.

Consequently, by first sending
\varepsilon\to 0
and then taking
R\to\infty
, we obtain (
4.1
) and (
4.2
) for all
g\in L^{1}
.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\psi(t)
be the unique global solution to (
NLSδ
) with initial data
\psi(0)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Then

(4.8)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\|P_{>N}\psi\|_{C_{t}L_{x}^{2}}=0.

Proof.

By Theorem
1.1
, the map
[-\infty,\infty]\ni t\mapsto e^{-it\partial_{x}^{2}}\psi(t)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
is continuous, where
[-\infty,\infty]
denotes the
2
-point compactification of
\mathbb{R}
. It follows that the set
\{e^{-it\partial_{x}^{2}}\psi(t):t\in\mathbb{R}\}
is precompact in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
and therefore tight in frequency. As
e^{-it\partial_{x}^{2}}
does not modify the frequency distribution, the claim (
4.8
) follows.
∎

We are now in a position to complete the

Proof of Theorem

.

We will work forward in time; the result then carries over to all times via time-reversal symmetry.

Theorem

For any

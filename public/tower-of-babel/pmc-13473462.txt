Feature decoupling and cross domain alignment with transfer learning for cross working condition mechanical fault diagnosis
Authors: Honglian Xiao, Jianhua Xiao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAiming at the problems of poor adaptability of heterogeneous sensing signals and feature distribution shift caused by working condition changes in cross-working-condi tion mechanical fault diagnosis, a WDH-Net model based on transfer learning is proposed for cross-working-condition heterogeneous mechanical fault diagnosis. The model integrates three core modules: time-frequency feature enhancement, dual-stream decoupling encoding and heterogeneous feature alignment. Specifically, the Wavelet-ConvNet+Cross-Attention module is used to perform adaptive wavelet packet decomposition and cross-attention feature optimization on heterogeneous time-series signals such as vibration and current to enhance the extraction of fault-sensitive features; the dual-stream decoupling encoder is adopted to separate fault features from working condition features to reduce working condition interference; a heterogeneous common feature mapping space is constructed, and the heterogeneous multi-kernel maximum mean discrepancy algorithm is combined to achieve cross-domain feature alignment and improve the generalization ability of the model. Experimental results on the public CWRU and PU datasets show that WDH-Net exhibits excellent performance in cross-working-condition fault diagnosis tasks, providing a new methodological reference for the intelligent fault diagnosis of industrial mechanical equipment.Keywords: Fault diagnosis, Heterogeneous sensing signals, Feature decoupling, Transfer learning, Cross-working conditionSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

In the modern industrial system, mechanical equipment is the core production carrier, and the stability of its operating state directly determines production efficiency, operational safety and maintenance costs1. Key rotating components such as bearings and gears long operate under complex conditions with variable loads, rotating speeds and strong noise interference, and are prone to failure due to wear, fatigue and impact. Untimely fault diagnosis may cause equipment shutdown, production interruption and even safety accidents2. Therefore, research on cross-working condition mechanical fault diagnosis to achieve accurate fault identification under variable working conditions has important theoretical value and academic significance for ensuring the continuous and stable operation of industrial systems.

The development of cross-working condition mechanical fault diagnosis technology has always centered on eliminating operating condition interference and enhancing the distinguishability of fault features. Early research relied on traditional time-frequency analysis methods such as wavelet transform and empirical mode decomposition, combined with machine learning algorithms like support vector machines (SVM) and random forests to achieve fault identification3,4. These methods, with their concise principles and low computational costs, have been applied to a certain extent in simple operating condition scenarios. However, they highly depend on manually designed features, show poor adaptability to complex working condition coupling and signal noise, and hardly satisfy high-precision diagnostic requirements. With the rise of deep learning technology, models such as convolutional neural networks (CNN), long short-term memory (LSTM), and temporal convolutional networks (TCN) have been widely applied in this field5,6. Their capability for automatic feature extraction effectively overcomes the limitations of manual feature engineering, and significantly improved the diagnostic accuracy under single operating conditions by mining deep semantic features of fault signals. Among them, CNN is good at capturing spatial features and is suitable for the analysis of fault signals in the form of time-frequency graphs, while LSTM and TCN perform excellently in mining long-range dependency relationships of time-series signals, providing stronger feature expression capabilities for cross-working condition diagnosis. In addition, graph neural networks (GNNs) have shown great potential in fault diagnosis7,8, especially interpretable graph modeling, physics-informed graph networks, and the DCMLDF framework, which provide new ideas for relational modeling and cross-domain learning9. The introduction of transfer learning has provided core technical support for solving the problem of feature distribution shift across working conditions and has become a research hotspot in recent years10,11. According to the consistency of signal modalities between the source domain and the target domain, existing methods can be divided into two categories: homogeneous transfer and heterogeneous transfer12,13. Homogeneous transfer assumes that the signal modalities of the source domain and the target domain are the same, and minimizes the distribution difference between them through domain adaptation algorithms. Typical representatives include Domain-Adversarial Neural Networks (DAN) and Joint Distribution Adaptation (JDA)14. Such methods have achieved good diagnostic results in homogeneous cross-working condition scenarios and are widely used in cross-working condition fault diagnosis tasks of single vibration signals. Heterogeneous transfer focuses on cross-domain adaptation of different modal signals (such as vibration, current, temperature), and its core is to construct a common feature mapping space to achieve unified expression and distribution alignment of heterogeneous features, effectively adapting to the actual scenario of multi-sensor heterogeneous data in industrial sites15,16. In recent years, the application of heterogeneous transfer learning in the field of fault diagnosis has gradually increased. Researchers have continuously improved the cross-domain transfer effect of heterogeneous signals by designing modality-specific projection heads and optimizing distribution alignment loss functions17,18. As an effective way to mitigate the coupling between working conditions and fault features, feature decoupling technology has become a research focus in the field of cross-working condition diagnosis in recent years. Its core idea is to separate the extracted mixed features into core features strongly related to faults and redundant features strongly related to operating conditions. By weakening the interference of operating condition features and strengthening the domain invariance of fault features, the cross-working condition generalization ability of the model is improved19. Existing feature decoupling methods are mainly implemented based on mutual information constraints, variational autoencoders (VAE), generative adversarial networks (GAN), etc., forcing the model to learn decoupled feature representations by constructing specific constraint loss functions20. Some studies have attempted to combine feature decoupling with transfer learning, further improving the cross-working condition diagnostic performance by first decoupling features and then performing domain alignment21. However, most of them adopt a single-stream decoupling structure, resulting in limited decoupling effects, and lack targeted design for heterogeneous signal scenarios.

At present, a mature technical framework has been formed in the field of cross-working condition mechanical fault diagnosis, covering core links such as signal processing, feature extraction and cross-domain transfer. However, in complex heterogeneous data scenarios, existing state-of-the-art methods still have prominent technical defects: they optimize feature extraction, domain adaptation and feature decoupling independently without collaborative optimization mechanisms. The single-branch decoupling structure cannot completely separate fault features from working condition interference, resulting in residual distribution shifts that affect diagnostic accuracy. These inherent defects restrict the practical application of existing methods, highlighting the urgent need for an integrated framework to break through the current technical bottleneck. This paper proposes the WDH-Net (Wavelet-ConvNet+Dual-stream decoupling+Heterogeneous transfer Network) model, which constructs an integrated cross-working condition fault diagnosis architecture integrating time-frequency feature enhancement, dual-stream decoupling encoding and heterogeneous feature alignment. Firstly, Wavelet-ConvNet and cross-attention mechanism are used to accurately extract and enhance the time-frequency features of heterogeneous signals. Then, the dual-stream decoupling encoder is utilized to separate the core fault features from the redundant operating condition features. Finally, cross-domain transfer is completed through the heterogeneous common feature mapping space and optimized distribution alignment strategy, and the overall optimization of the model is realized combined with the joint loss function. The technical route follows theoretical analysis, architectural design, experimental validation and result discussion, sequentially completing model construction, parameter optimization and performance verification to ensure the scientificity and effectiveness of the method.

Related work

Time-frequency feature extraction and attention mechanism

The non-stationary and nonlinear properties of mechanical fault signals make time-frequency feature extraction a core prerequisite for fault diagnosis. In existing research, wavelet transform and its improved variants are widely adopted for time-frequency domain analysis of fault signals22. Related works enhance the extraction of sensitive frequency bands corresponding to fault impact characteristics by optimizing wavelet basis selection, adaptive decomposition layers and threshold adjustment, mitigate mode mixing inherent in conventional wavelet transform with fixed parameters, and strengthen the time-frequency feature representation capability for fault identification23,24. On this basis, lightweight convolutional neural networks have been extensively introduced into deep time-frequency feature mining. Improved lightweight architectures such as MobileNet and ShuffleNet achieve multi-level extraction of local spatial features from time-frequency graphs, while controlling model parameters and computational complexity via depthwise separable convolution and group convolution, making them suitable for industrial scenarios with limited computing resources25.

The integration of attention mechanisms and convolutional networks has become a mainstream direction for time-frequency feature enhancement26. Related studies assign dynamic weights to different dimensions of time-frequency features through channel, spatial or temporal attention modules, prioritize key features highly contributive to fault recognition, and suppress redundant interference from background noise and working condition fluctuations27. Attention-based models including CBAM and SE-Net greatly improve the feature distinguishability of single-modal signals through weighted optimization of feature channels and spatial dimensions28; other efforts further explore the combination of attention and time-frequency analysis, adopting attention weights to guide time-frequency decomposition and prioritize feature learning of fault-sensitive frequency bands29,30. Nevertheless, most existing methods adopt a serial pipeline of time-frequency analysis, feature extraction and attention weighting, leading to insufficient fusion between time-frequency decomposition and attention modeling and limiting the ability to capture cross-dimensional correlations in the time-frequency domain. Meanwhile, current approaches lack adaptive designs for heterogeneous sensor signals, without differentiated extraction strategies for time-frequency discrepancies among vibration, current, temperature and other modal signals31. Although CNN-LSTM hybrid models can model temporal and spatial patterns, they easily introduce information redundancy when fusing heterogeneous features, weakening extraction robustness for heterogeneous data32,33. Moreover, existing attention-based schemes mainly enhance single-modal features but fail to mine complementary time-frequency information across heterogeneous modalities, often losing unique fault characteristics inherent to each signal type.

Recently, vision transformer (ViT) frameworks have been introduced into fault diagnosis to capture long-range dependencies in time-frequency representations, combined with semi-supervised learning and uncertainty quantification to reduce reliance on labeled data34. Meanwhile, ViT models built on time-frequency scalograms have achieved promising performance in milling tool fault diagnosis under limited annotations. Even so, existing ViT-based methods lack dedicated designs for heterogeneous signal decoupling and cross-working-condition domain adaptation.

Aiming at the inadequate fusion of time-frequency analysis and attention modules, as well as poor adaptability to heterogeneous signals, the Wavelet-ConvNet+Cross-Attention module in WDH-Net achieves deep integration of adaptive wavelet packet decomposition and cross-attention, thereby enhancing time-frequency representation for heterogeneous signals and preserving complementary modal information.

Two-stream decoupling and heterogeneous transfer learning

Feature decoupling serves as a core strategy to address feature coupling between working conditions and fault patterns. Existing literature has extensively explored decoupling network architectures and constraint loss functions35. VAE- and GAN-based decoupling models separate mixed extracted features into fault-related intrinsic components and condition-induced redundant components using single-stream encoding combined with mutual information minimization36; further improved frameworks such as β-VAE and InfoGAN enforce decoupled feature learning via customized reconstruction loss and adversarial constraints37,38. Relevant studies have verified that decoupled features effectively boost cross-working-condition diagnosis accuracy, confirming that stripping condition-related components significantly enhances the domain invariance of fault features39. However, most single-stream encoding structures struggle to fully isolate working condition interference from fault semantics, leaving residual conditional bias within decoupled representations40.

Targeting the incomplete separation limitation of single-stream decoupling, the dual-stream decoupling encoder in WDH-Net adopts parallel branches for fault and working condition modeling, and introduces feature orthogonal constraints to realize thorough disentanglement between fault representations and conditional interference. Heterogeneous transfer learning concentrates on cross-domain adaptation for multi-modal heterogeneous signals, with the core principle of learning a shared feature embedding space41. Domain adaptation algorithms including DAN, JDA and CORAL project heterogeneous signals such as vibration and current into a dimension-unified common space via modality-specific projection heads, and align cross-domain feature distributions using maximum mean discrepancy and domain adversarial training42. Advanced strategies further optimize common space alignment by adopting weighted multi-kernel discrepancy and class center matching to improve cross-domain consistency of heterogeneous features43. Nevertheless, existing methods generally perform direct distribution alignment on undifferentiated mixed features, inevitably embedding redundant conditional components into transfer optimization and degrading cross-domain diagnostic performance. In addition, current common space designs employ fixed modal weight allocation without dynamic adjustment according to the fault sensitivity of each signal modality. Heterogeneous transfer architectures such as MDAN and MMD-AAE cannot fully exploit complementary properties across heterogeneous modalities, restricting the upper bound of transferable diagnosis accuracy44. More importantly, most existing implementations treat feature decoupling and heterogeneous transfer as isolated procedures, lacking joint collaborative optimization and preventing targeted cross-domain alignment on purified fault features. Recent studies have proposed multi-sensor observer-based residual learning to enhance fault feature saliency under variable working conditions45, alongside Auto-Permutation Feature Importance for efficient discriminative feature selection. Lightweight TCN has also been deployed for predictive maintenance to support early fault detection with low-latency inference on industrial edge devices46. Still, these approaches rarely unify feature decoupling and heterogeneous transfer learning, and thus cannot well accommodate cross-working-condition scenarios with multi-modal heterogeneous inputs.

To remedy insufficient alignment targeting and the absence of joint optimization, the heterogeneous common feature mapping space in WDH-Net enables targeted cross-domain alignment exclusively for decoupled fault features, and establishes a collaborative optimization paradigm bridging feature decoupling and heterogeneous transfer learning.

Design of the proposed cross-working condition fault diagnosis architecture

Overall architecture framework

Aiming at the core problems of poor adaptive capability of heterogeneous signal features and serious coupling between working condition interference and fault semantics in cross-working condition mechanical fault diagnosis, this paper proposes an integrated WDH-Net diagnostic architecture integrating time-frequency feature enhancement, dual-stream decoupling encoding and heterogeneous feature alignment, with the overall structure illustrated in Fig. 1. The workflow of the proposed framework consists of three key stages. In the first stage of heterogeneous signal time-frequency feature enhancement, input multi-modal signals are transformed into time-frequency feature matrices via adaptive wavelet packet decomposition. Lightweight convolution is adopted for local feature extraction, and the cross-attention module further achieves adaptive weighted fusion across multi-modal and multi-band components, so as to screen out highly distinguishable fault-sensitive features. The second stage corresponds to dual-stream decoupling encoding. Enhanced features are fed into a dual-stream encoder, where the fault feature branch strengthens intrinsic fault representation learning and the working condition branch captures conditional dependency information. Combined with explicit loss constraints, the two categories of features are effectively disentangled. The third stage implements cross-domain alignment for heterogeneous features. Decoupled fault features are projected into a shared embedding space through modality-specific projection heads. Distribution alignment and dynamic modal weight optimization are subsequently realized via heterogeneous multi-kernel maximum mean discrepancy, and diagnostic results are finally output by the classifier. By coordinating the above three stages, WDH-Net establishes an end-to-end diagnostic pipeline, achieving tight integration of time-frequency enhancement, dual-stream feature decoupling and heterogeneous transfer learning, and provides an effective solution for mechanical fault diagnosis under complex cross-working-condition and heterogeneous data scenarios.

Design of dual-stream decoupling encoder

The parallel structure is theoretically motivated by the disentangled representation learning principle, which encourages the model to learn semantically independent latent factors corresponding to fault patterns and working condition variations. The two streams adopt completely differentiated network structures and learning objectives, which form independent feature extraction paths and avoid structural interference in the feature learning process. It achieves efficient decoupling of the two types of features through differentiated network structures, and minimizes the interference of operating condition fluctuations on fault diagnosis. Experimental design

Dataset

To verify the effectiveness and generalization ability of the proposed WDH-Net in cross-working condition heterogeneous fault diagnosis tasks, two types of public benchmark datasets for mechanical fault diagnosis were adopted in the experiments: the Case Western Reserve University (CWRU) dataset and the Paderborn University (PU) dataset. These datasets cover different equipment types, fault modes and operating conditions, ensuring the comprehensiveness and reliability of experimental verification.

CWRU Dataset The CWRU dataset is derived from the bearing fault simulation experiment platform of Case Western Reserve University, taking the 6205-2RS JEM SKF deep groove ball bearing as the research object. Single-point faults with different diameters (0.1778mm, 0.3556mm, 0.5334mm) were prefabricated on the surfaces of the inner ring, outer ring and rolling elements of the bearing by electric discharge machining technology, and the normal fault-free working condition was set as a control. During the experiment, multi-working condition scenarios were constructed by adjusting the motor load (0HP, 1HP, 2HP, 3HP), the sampling frequency was set to 12kHz and 48kHz, and vibration signal data were collected. Each combination of fault type and working condition corresponds to multiple groups of continuous time-series samples, and the sample length was uniformly intercepted to 1024 data points, providing abundant labeled data support for cross-working condition fault diagnosis.

PU Dataset The PU dataset is provided by the Faculty of Mechanical Engineering of Paderborn University, based on an experimental platform built with different types of rolling bearings (6203-2RSH/C3, 6204-2RSH/C3). It includes multiple fault modes such as no fault, outer ring fault, inner ring fault and rolling element fault, with clear distinctions in fault degree and fault location. The experiment set different motor speeds (1500rpm, 1797rpm, 2000rpm, 2400rpm, 2800rpm) and load conditions (0N, 100 N, 200 N, 300 N) to form diversified cross-working condition scenarios. Meanwhile, two types of heterogeneous sensor data, vibration signals and current signals, were collected with a sampling frequency of 64kHz, and the sample length was intercepted to 2048 data points, providing high-quality multi-modal data for heterogeneous signal fusion and cross-domain transfer diagnosis experiments.

Dataset preprocessing

To suppress noise and redundant information and guarantee rigorous model training, unified preprocessing pipelines are implemented for the CWRU bearing dataset and PU gear dataset according to their inherent signal characteristics.

First, a Butterworth low-pass filter is applied to eliminate high-frequency noise. The cutoff frequency is set to 1000 Hz for the CWRU dataset, matching the main frequency range of bearing fault signatures. For the PU dataset, the cutoff frequency is configured at 800 Hz to accommodate the lower characteristic frequency of gear vibration signals. This setting filters irrelevant high-frequency interference induced by background noise and sensor clutter without distorting effective fault-related components. Second, the sliding window strategy is adopted to partition long time-series sequences. For the CWRU dataset, the window length is set to 2048 points, corresponding to a duration of 0.2048 s under a 10 kHz sampling rate, which guarantees that each segmented sample contains complete fault characteristic information. For the PU dataset, the window length is set to 1024 points to fit its 5 kHz sampling frequency. A fixed sliding step of 512 points is employed for both datasets to balance sample quantity and feature overlap, ensuring adequate data volume while preventing redundant sampling. Finally, min–max normalization is conducted to standardize signal amplitude distributions. For the CWRU dataset, normalization bounds are determined from the global statistical range of raw vibration signals. For the PU dataset, normalization parameters are configured independently for each signal modality to mitigate modal discrepancy interference, maintaining consistent data distribution and reducing amplitude heterogeneity during model training. After preprocessing, samples are partitioned by working conditions and fault categories to construct training and test sets, providing a reliable data basis for subsequent model training and diagnostic evaluation.

Experimental environment

The experimental hardware platform adopted an Intel Core i7-12700H processor, equipped with 32GB DDR5 memory and an NVIDIA RTX 4090 graphics card (24GB video memory), providing sufficient computing power support for model training and inference; the software environment was built based on Python 3.9, with the deep learning framework PyTorch 1.18.0, and auxiliary libraries including NumPy 1.24.3, SciPy 1.10.1, Matplotlib 3.7.1, etc., used for data processing, model construction and result visualization.

Model parameter settings

During model training, the Adam optimizer is used to update network parameters. It effectively balances convergence speed and training stability by adaptively adjusting the learning rate for different parameters, making it suitable for complex deep networks such as WDH-Net. The initial learning rate is set to , a value optimized for heterogeneous fault diagnosis to avoid gradient explosion or slow convergence caused by inappropriate learning rate initialization. To balance stability and convergence efficiency, a cosine annealing learning rate scheduler is adopted, with the learning rate decayed every 5 epochs and a decay factor of 0.9. This strategy dynamically adjusts the learning rate, helping the model escape local optima while ensuring stable convergence in the late training phase. The batch size is set to 32, which matches the memory capacity of the training hardware and maintains sufficient sample diversity in each batch to improve generalization without memory overflow. The total training epoch is set to 100, determined according to the model convergence behavior to ensure sufficient feature learning while avoiding redundant computation. An early stopping mechanism is applied when the validation loss does not improve for 10 consecutive epochs, effectively alleviating overfitting and enhancing the model generalization ability on the target domain.

To ensure rigorous experimental reproducibility, a fixed random seed of 42 is employed for all experiments including data splitting, model initialization and batch sampling. All samples are constructed using non-overlapping sliding windows with a step size of 512 points to guarantee complete independence among samples. For each transfer task, the source domain contains 2100 samples and the target domain contains 900 samples with a unified 7:3 train-test split ratio. A 10% held-out validation set is uniformly used for hyperparameter selection across all models, and each transfer task is independently repeated 5 times to obtain statistically reliable mean ± standard deviation results. All baseline models follow the identical data split, validation protocol and evaluation procedure to ensure fair comparison.

The key parameters of each module are set as follows. In the Wavelet-ConvNet+Cross-Attention module, the number of adaptive wavelet packet decomposition layers is set to 4, which fully decomposes fault signals into multi-scale frequency bands and captures sensitive features without excessive computation. The wavelet basis is set to db4, due to its superior time-frequency localization for transient fault feature extraction from non-stationary mechanical signals. The lightweight convolutional network uses 3 layers of depthwise separable convolution, balancing feature extraction performance and model efficiency by reducing parameters while maintaining deep time-frequency feature representation. The query and key vector dimensions of the cross-attention module are both set to 256, ensuring adequate feature representation while avoiding excessive computational cost. In the dual-stream decoupling encoder, the residual dense convolution block contains 6 convolutional layers with a kernel size of 3× 3. The 6-layer structure ensures sufficient feature reuse and deep feature learning, while the 3× 3 kernel effectively captures local feature correlations without introducing extra computational burden. The dilation coefficients of the temporal convolutional network are set to , expanding the multi-scale receptive field to capture long-range dependencies of time-series fault signals, which is critical for fault recognition under variable working conditions. In the heterogeneous common feature mapping space, the output dimension of the modality-specific projection head is set to 512, mapping heterogeneous vibration and current signals into a high-dimensional shared space with strong discriminability for subsequent cross-domain alignment. The heterogeneous multi-kernel maximum mean discrepancy uses a combination of Gaussian and polynomial kernels, integrating the Gaussian kernel’s ability to model nonlinear distributions and the polynomial kernel’s strength in fitting linear correlations to improve alignment accuracy.

For the overall loss function, the weights are set as α =0.6 for classification loss, β =0.3 for cross-domain alignment loss, and γ =0.1 for feature orthogonality loss. This configuration prioritizes classification accuracy while maintaining balanced domain alignment and feature decoupling.

Evaluation metrics setting

To comprehensively and objectively evaluate the fault diagnosis performance of WDH-Net and comparison models, four core classification evaluation metrics are adopted in the experiments, including Accuracy (Acc), Precision (P), Recall (R) and F1-Score (F1). Accuracy reflects the overall diagnostic effect of the model as the proportion of correctly diagnosed samples to the total number of samples; Precision evaluates the accuracy of the model’s positive predictions to avoid misdiagnosing normal samples as fault samples; Recall measures the coverage of fault samples by the model’s predictions to ensure all fault samples are identified; F1-Score is the harmonic mean of Precision and Recall, which comprehensively balances the two metrics to avoid one-sided evaluation results. In addition, the training time and parameter quantity of each model are recorded to conduct a comprehensive assessment from both performance and efficiency perspectives.

Comparison models selection

To comprehensively verify the superiority of WDH-Net, seven state-of-the-art fault diagnosis models covering various technical routes (transfer learning, domain adaptation, multi-source transfer, contrastive learning, etc.) were selected as benchmarks. All models share the same input data, preprocessing methods, and experimental environment to ensure fair comparison. In addition, the hyperparameters of all models were tuned systematically using the same optimization strategy to ensure that the comparison is conducted under equivalent conditions. Brief introductions are as follows:

MMDAN47: A multi-scale and multi-view domain adversarial network, designed for partial set and cross-working condition fault diagnosis via knowledge transfer between source and target conditions.

RSTCG48: An enhanced dual-channel feature fusion approach for rolling bearing fault diagnosis, converting temporal sequences to 2D feature maps and fusing multi-scale features from Swin Transformer and CNN2D with GAM.

CDPDTLN49: A cross-domain deep transfer network, combining cross-domain data processing, improved multi-scale residual network, and combined domain distribution adaptation strategy.

DANN50: An enhanced domain-adversarial neural network, fusing mechanism-simulated data and measured data with subdomain discriminator for cross-condition fault diagnosis.

SSCTLN51: A semi-supervised contrastive transfer network, integrating LMMD, semi-supervised contrastive learning, and domain adversarial training for cross-condition bearing diagnosis.

Proto-DISFNet52: A prototype-guided dual-feature transfer method, realizing domain feature disentanglement and prototype construction for cross-condition fault diagnosis.

MSDF-DANN53: A multi-source domain fusion network, transforming signals into 2D features and adopting convolutional fusion module for bearing fault diagnosis.

NaCdNet54: A cross-condition fault diagnosis framework that integrates convolutional neural networks, attention mechanisms, and real-time style transfer to adapt to data distribution variations caused by changing working conditions.

MSCLDA55: A multi-source domain adaptation method based on supervised contrastive learning, which alleviates negative transfer and aligns cross-domain distributions by using prototype-guided pseudo-label optimization and adaptive domain weighting.

Proto-ADDA-X56: A prototype-attention domain adaptation method that aligns class centroids and adaptively weights target samples based on similarity and confidence for cross-working-condition bearing fault diagnosis. It also provides interpretable visualization of feature alignment and prototype evolution during transfer.

It is 9.7 percent lower than MSCLDA (12.5 M) and 13.7 percent lower than NaCdNet (13.1 M), and is 29.0 percent lower than SSCTLN and 38.9 percent lower than RSTCG which has the largest parameter quantity. Such lightweight performance provides favorable conditions for its deployment in industrial embedded systems with limited resources. Regarding training time consumption, consistent test parameters on the same hardware platform ensure that the comparison results are reliable. WDH-Net consumes 27.9 seconds per epoch on the CWRU dataset and 30.8 seconds per epoch on the PU dataset, with an average training time of 29.35 seconds per epoch, which is also faster than MSCLDA, NaCdNet, SSCTLN and other models. The gap between its training time on the two datasets is only 2.9 seconds, indicating stable adaptation efficiency to different datasets. WDH-Net maintains high diagnostic accuracy above 94 percent in previous cross-working condition and cross-dataset tasks while optimizing both parameter quantity and training time consumption, breaking the inherent bottleneck between high performance and high resource consumption. In contrast, RSTCG has redundant architecture design, leading to the largest parameter quantity and longest training time among all models. MSCLDA and NaCdNet achieve competitive diagnostic performance but are inferior to WDH-Net in lightweight design and training efficiency. SSCTLN performs well in diagnostic accuracy but lags behind WDH-Net in both parameter quantity and training time consumption, failing to achieve a balanced performance between efficiency and accuracy. Other comparison models either have insufficient diagnostic accuracy or fail to optimize resource consumption, resulting in poor comprehensive application value compared with WDH-Net.

It can be clearly observed that when the coefficient combination is set as 0.6, 0.3, 0.1, the model achieves the highest accuracy on both datasets, with 94.18% on the CWRU dataset and 94.85% on the PU dataset. This result verifies that this coefficient ratio can optimally balance the optimization objectives of classification loss, cross-domain alignment loss and feature orthogonality loss, enabling the model to simultaneously take into account the accuracy of fault classification, cross-domain adaptability and feature purity. Under other coefficient combinations, the model’s accuracy decreases to varying degrees. An excessively high weight of classification loss will lead to insufficient cross-domain alignment, limiting the generalization ability of the model in the target domain; an excessively high weight of cross-domain alignment loss will weaken the classification objective and reduce the accuracy of fault identification; an excessively high weight of feature orthogonality loss will overemphasize feature decoupling, which may lead to the loss of some key information related to faults. All these factors will result in a decline in the overall diagnostic accuracy. In addition, under the same coefficient combination, the accuracy of the PU dataset is slightly higher than that of the CWRU dataset overall. This is related to the fact that the PU dataset contains heterogeneous modal signals of vibration and current, and the complementarity of heterogeneous features provides the model with richer fault information, helping to improve diagnostic performance. On the whole, the reasonable ratio of loss function coefficients is crucial to the cross-working-condition heterogeneous fault diagnosis performance of WDH-Net, and the combination of 0.6, 0.3, 0.1 is the optimal choice verified by experiments.

Ablation experiments are conducted to validate the necessity of WDH-Net core modules, including Wavelet-ConvNet, Cross-Attention, dual-stream decoder, heterogeneous feature fusion, multi-kernel MMD alignment, and dynamic weight assignment. As shown in Table 9, the complete WDH-Net obtains the best overall results on both CWRU and PU datasets, achieving diagnostic accuracy of 94.18% and 94.85% respectively. All metrics including precision, recall and F1-score remain at high levels with standard deviations lower than 0.6, which reflects reliable and stable performance. The elimination of any core module induces an obvious accuracy drop, verifying the indispensable contribution of each component to the overall framework. Without Wavelet-ConvNet, model performance declines most noticeably, with accuracy falling to 89.72% on the CWRU dataset and 90.15% on the PU dataset. Such degradation originates from the inherent function of Wavelet-ConvNet in mining subtle time-frequency fault characteristics from vibration signals, and the lack of this module prevents the network from capturing fine-grained fault patterns and thereby weakens recognition capability. The removal of Cross-Attention also causes observable accuracy drops on both datasets. This module enables the model to concentrate on critical fault-related regions, and its absence reduces feature discriminability and further lowers precision and recall. The dual-stream decoder plays a vital role in isolating working condition components from intrinsic fault representations, and disabling this module aggravates feature coupling and degrades the ability to distinguish diverse fault modes. The absence of heterogeneous feature fusion undermines complementary information integration between vibration and current signals, while removing multi-kernel MMD alignment weakens cross-domain distribution matching across variable operating conditions. Discarding dynamic weight assignment further cancels adaptive modal contribution adjustment, leading to decreased diagnostic accuracy and stability. All ablation outcomes indicate that the involved components cooperate closely with one another. Effective fault feature extraction, key region enhancement, mixed feature decoupling, multi-modal information integration, robust cross-domain alignment and adaptive feature selection are jointly guaranteed by these modules, and their synergistic effect enables WDH-Net to maintain superior diagnostic performance on different bearing fault datasets.

Discussion

The WDH-Net model proposed in this paper effectively breaks through the bottlenecks of traditional methods in working condition adaptation and heterogeneous signal processing for the core problem of cross-working-condition heterogeneous mechanical fault diagnosis, via the deep integration of time-frequency feature enhancement, dual-stream decoupling encoding and heterogeneous feature alignment. Its high diagnostic accuracy and stability have been verified on the public CWRU and PU datasets, providing a new solution for cross-working-condition fault diagnosis. The proposed dual-stream decoupling structure successfully separates fault-relevant features from working condition interference, and the heterogeneous multi-kernel MMD enables robust distribution alignment between vibration and current signals, which reveals the importance of feature disentanglement and adaptive cross-domain alignment for real industrial diagnosis tasks. However, this study still has several limitations to be improved: the model training is highly dependent on publicly available datasets with complete annotations, while mechanical fault data in actual industrial scenarios often face problems such as high annotation costs, unbalanced samples and small sample sizes; its generalization ability in such real industrial scenarios has not been fully verified, and direct migration and application may lead to performance degradation; additionally, the model is validated under controlled laboratory conditions, without considering severe sensor drift, intermittent noise, or non-stationary operating shocks that are common in real industrial sites. While WDH-Net has achieved lightweight design with only 11.3M parameters, the computational logic of the wavelet packet decomposition module and cross-attention mechanism still has room for optimization, and its real-time inference efficiency on industrial embedded devices is difficult to meet the requirements of some high-frequency acquisition scenarios; the model also does not consider computational resource constraints on edge devices with limited memory and computing power, which may restrict its deployment in online monitoring systems. This study only focuses on the fusion of two types of heterogeneous signals (vibration and current), and does not incorporate more common types of sensing data in industrial scenarios such as temperature, pressure and acoustic emission, leaving the feature complementarity and fusion strategy of multi-source heterogeneous data under-explored. To date, the model has only completed offline experimental verification, without combining with the actual deployment environment of industrial sites, and engineering issues such as data transmission delay, device compatibility and environmental noise interference have not been taken into account; its scalability to multi-component rotating machinery (e.g., gearboxes, bearings, motors) and variable-speed non-stationary conditions also remains insufficiently validated.

Based on the above limitations, future research work can be further expanded from multiple dimensions: combined with semi-supervised learning, self-supervised learning or meta-learning methods, a cross-working-condition fault diagnosis framework for few-annotation and small-sample scenarios can be built, reducing dependence on manually annotated data by mining features of unannotated data and improving the model’s adaptability in real industrial scenarios. To address the problem of model deployment efficiency, WDH-Net can be optimized using lightweight technologies such as quantization, pruning and knowledge distillation, simplifying the computational process of wavelet packet decomposition and exploring hardware-accelerated inference schemes to achieve low-latency and low-power deployment on embedded terminals. The fusion dimension of multi-source heterogeneous sensing data can be expanded by integrating multi-type industrial sensing data such as temperature and pressure, designing an adaptive modal weight allocation mechanism that dynamically adjusts feature weights according to the effectiveness of each modal data under different equipment and working conditions, thereby fully tapping the complementary value of multi-source data. The model can be promoted from offline verification to engineering application by building an end-edge-cloud collaborative online fault diagnosis system combined with the industrial internet platform, realizing a closed-loop full process of data collection, real-time analysis and fault early warning, accumulating actual fault data from industrial sites, and establishing a dynamically updated model iteration mechanism to continuously improve the engineering practicality and robustness of the model. Future research will also focus on improving the model’s generalization under extreme noise, sensor failure, and imbalanced fault data, and exploring its generalization ability across different mechanical components to meet the diverse requirements of industrial predictive maintenance.

Conclusion

The WDH-Net model proposed in this paper effectively addresses the core problems of heterogeneous signal adaptation and working condition distribution shift in cross-working-condition mechanical fault diagnosis by integrating time-frequency feature enhancement, dual-stream decoupling encoding and heterogeneous feature alignment mechanisms. This model exhibits superior diagnostic performance compared with existing methods on the CWRU and PU datasets, balancing high accuracy and lightweight characteristics, and thus provides an effective solution for intelligent fault diagnosis of machinery under complex working conditions. The relevant research results can provide theoretical reference and practical basis for the research and development of intelligent operation and maintenance technology for industrial mechanical equipment.

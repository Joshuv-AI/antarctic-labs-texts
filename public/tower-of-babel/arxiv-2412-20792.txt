Abstract
. We study the problem of free denoising . For free selfadjoint random variables a , b a,b , where we interpret a a as a signal and b b as noise, we find E ⁡ ( a | a + b ) E(a\,|\,a+b) . To that end, we study a probability measure μ a , a + b ( ov ) \mu^{(\mathrm{ov})}_{a,a+b} on ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2} which we call the overlap measure . We show that μ a , a + b ( ov ) \mu^{(\mathrm{ov})}_{a,a+b} is absolutely continuous with respect to the product measure μ a × μ a + b \mu_{a}\times\mu_{a+b} . The Radon-Nikodym derivative gives direct access to E ⁡ ( a | a + b ) E(a\,|\,a+b) . We show that analogous results hold in the case of multiplicative noise when a , b a,b are positive and the aim is to find E ⁡ ( a | a 1 / 2 ​ b ​ a 1 / 2 ) E(a\,|\,a^{1/2}ba^{1/2}) . In a parallel development we show that, for a general selfadjoint expression P ⁡ ( a , b ) P(a,b) made with a a and b b , finding E ⁡ ( a | P ⁡ ( a , b ) ) E(a\,|\,P(a,b)) is equivalent to finding the distribution of P ⁡ ( a , b ) P(a,b) in a certain two-state probability space ( 𝒜 , φ , χ ) (\mathcal{A},\varphi,\chi) , where a , b a,b are c-free with respect to ( φ , χ ) (\varphi,\chi) in the sense of Bożejko-Leinert-Speicher. We discuss how free denoising (which is set in the framework of an abstract W ∗ W^{*} -probability space) relates to the notion of “matrix denoising” previously discussed in the random matrix literature.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Problem description.

In this paper we consider some instances (primarily the additive instance
and the multiplicative instance) of a problem which could go under the
name of
free denoising
. The setting for free denoising involves
two freely independent selfadjoint random variables
a,b

in a tracial
W^{*}
-probability space
\left(\mathcal{A},\varphi\right)
,
where
a
is viewed as signal (it is “the random variable of interest”),
while
b
is viewed as noise. In the additive version of the free
denoising problem we assume that the
sum
a+b
is given, and our goal is to find the best approximation of

a
by an element
h(a+b)\in\mathcal{A}
, where
h
is a real-valued Borel function
on the spectrum of
a+b
. The multiplicative version of the problem goes
in the same vein, but where
a,b
are now assumed to be positive and the task
is to approximate
a
by an element
h(\,a^{1/2}ba^{1/2}\,)
.
In full generality, the random variable
c\in\mathcal{A}
which is assumed to be
given is some selfadjoint expression
c=P(a,b)
formed with
a
and
b

(instead of
a+b
or
a^{1/2}ba^{1/2}
one could have, for instance,
a quadratic expression such as
P(a,b)=i(ab-ba)
or
P(a,b)=ab+ba
).

When viewed as an approximation problem with respect to the
||\cdot||_{2}
-norm
associated to the trace
\varphi
, the above mentioned problem is turned into the
one of understanding what is the conditional expectation

E\bigl(a\mid P(a,b)\bigr)
, where

E\bigl(\cdot\mid P(a,b)\bigr):\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
is the unique

\varphi
-preserving conditional expectation onto the von Neumann algebra

\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathcal{A}
generated by
P(a,b)
. In Sections

6
and
7
of the
present paper we provide explicit formulas for such conditional expectations,
holding in the two main cases under consideration,
E(a\,|\,a+b)
and respectively

E\left(a\,|\,a^{1/2}ba^{1/2}\right)
. Then, for a general
c=P(a,b)
,
Section

8
of the paper puts into evidence an alternative approach to free denoising
which is found by relating it to the notion of “conditionally free independence”
of Bożejko-Leinert-Speicher
[
15
]
. In the appendix we explain
that the methodology described in this paper
also works in the case
of the multiplication of two freely independent unitaries.

1.2.
Motivation: analogies with classical probability.

In order to put things into perspective, we note here that free
denoising is the free probabilistic counterpart
of a well-known formalism of classical probability, concerning
conditional expectations (taken in usual “commutative” sense)

\mathbb{E}(X\,|\,Y)
, where
(X,Y)
is a random vector in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
Under fairly general conditions (see e.g. the survey paper

[
17
]
), the distribution
\sigma
of
(X,Y)
can be
disintegrated with respect to the 1-dimensional distribution
\nu
of

Y
, leading to a family of 1-dimensional distributions

(\mu_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
such that for a bounded Borel function

f:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
we have

(1.1)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f(s,t)\,d\sigma(s,t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\,\Bigl[\,\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(s,t)\,d\mu_{t}(s)\,\bigr]\,d\nu(t).

In this setting, one gets that
\mathbb{E}(X\,|\,Y)=h(Y)
, where we
put

(1.2)

h(t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}s\,d\mu_{t}(s),\ \ t\in\mathbb{R}.

The preceding formula shows that, in general, the initial data needed in order
to find
\mathbb{E}(X\,|\,Y)
is the full joint distribution
\sigma
of
X
and
Y
.
But there exist cases when the said conditional expectation can be
expressed solely in terms of
Y
and its distribution. To illustrate this,
let us focus, in analogy to the previous subsection, on the case where

Y=X+Z
with
X,Z
independent (in the commutative sense) and

Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2})
.
The distribution of
Y
is then sure to
have a density
f_{Y}
with respect to Lebesgue measure, and the well-known
Tweedie’s formula in statistics (see
[
26
]
) says that in this case one has

(1.3)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(X\,|\,Y)=Y+\sigma^{2}g(Y),\ \mbox{ where $g(t)=\tfrac{d}{dt}\log(f_{Y}(t))$.}

For the free analogue of the above mentioned development, we will use a
sibling of the notion of joint distribution which we call “overlap measure”
(Section 1.4 below). The methodology developed in connection to this yields,
in particular, both an additive and a multiplicative free analogue for Tweedie’s
formula (
1.3
), where the formula arising in the additive analogue is
of the same nature as (
1.3
). More
precisely, for this additive analogue we pick
a,b\in\mathcal{A}
to be freely independent
and selfadjoint (like at the beginning of Section 1.1 above), and we assume that
the distribution of
b
is the Wigner semicircle law with variance
\sigma^{2}
.
We then find that

E(a\,|\,a+b)=h(a+b)
for the function
h:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

(1.4)

h(t)=t-2\pi\,\sigma^{2}\,H_{\mu\boxplus\nu}(t),

where
H_{\mu\boxplus\nu}
is the Hilbert transform of the measure

\mu\boxplus\nu
. It is remarkable that, analogous to the classical case,
the right-hand side of (
1.4
) depends only on the
distribution
\mu\boxplus\nu
of
a+b
. The details of the derivation of
(
1.4
) are presented in Example
6.7
below.
We mention that this formula had been spotted in the physics literature,
via a method called “the replica trick” (see
[
16
]
).

The multiplicative free analogue of Tweedie’s formula is presented in
Example
1.6
below. As it turns out, this free analogue
had also been spotted in previous research literature on random matrices,
in
[
21
]
.

1.3.
Relation to subordination functions.

Let
a,b
be freely independent selfadjoint elements in a
W^{*}
-probability
space
(\mathcal{A},\varphi)
. In
[
13
]
, Biane showed how conditional
expectations of the form
E(f(a+b)\,|\,a)
and
E(g(a^{1/2}ba^{1/2})\,|\,a)

can be treated by using the notion of additive and respectively of multiplicative

subordination function
for the Cauchy transforms of the distributions of interest
(a precise review of subordination terminology and some statements of results appear
in Section
5
below). The development of the
present paper makes significant use of
subordination functions and of some related Feller-Markov kernels that were identified
in
[
13
]
; but we are in a certain sense “going in reverse” from how Biane did,
since we are looking at
E(f(a)\,|\,a+b)
rather than
E(f(a+b)\,|\,a)
, and
likewise in the multiplicative case. The outcome of the calculations is quite different.
For example, as noted by Biane in
[
13
, Remark 3.3]
, it is always the case that

E(\,(a+b)^{n}\,|\,a)
is a polynomial of degree
n
in
a
; in contrast to that,
a conditional expectation
E(a^{n}\,|\,a+b)
is of the form
h(a+b)
, where
h

(a function on the spectrum of
a+b
) may not be a polynomial. In fact,
Bożejko and Bryc
[
14
]
showed that merely assuming
E(a\,|\,a+b)
and

E(a^{2}\,|\,a+b)
to be linear and respectively quadratic polynomials of
a+b
forces

a,b
to have some special distributions, coming from the free Meixner family.

On the general subject of work which uses subordination and has a topic related to
the one of the present paper, we mention here the work
[
1
]
,
where the authors study a problem called “free deconvolution”, which is about retrieving
the distribution of
a
when the distribution of
a+b
and the distribution of the
noise
b
are given. Free denoising is different from free deconvolution: it aims to
find the element
h(a+b)
which is at minimal
||\cdot||_{2}
-distance from
a
, and where
(as easily seen by computing variance in simple examples with
a,b
identically distributed)

h(a+b)
will generally not have the same distribution as
a
.

1.4.
Overlap measure and overlap function.

In order to discuss the non-commutative analogue of the disintegration procedure
from Equations (
1.1
), (
1.2
) of Section 1.2, one replaces the
random vector
(X,Y)
considered there with a pair of selfadjoint elements
x,y

in a
W^{*}
-probability space
(\mathcal{A},\varphi)
. The notion of joint distribution
of
x
and
y
is usually defined, in this non-commutative setting, as a linear
functional
\mu_{x,y}:\mathcal{P}\to\mathbb{C}
, where
\mathcal{P}
is the algebra
of non-commutative polynomials in two indeterminates and we have

\mu_{x,y}(P)=\varphi(P(x,y))
for
P\in\mathcal{P}
.
For the purpose of finding the conditional expectation
E(x\,|\,y)
, the
functional
\mu_{x,y}
contains too much information; it is convenient
to use in its place a bona fide measure
\mu^{(\mathrm{ov})}_{x,y}
on the Borel sigma-algebra
of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, which we call the
overlap measure
of
x
and
y
, and is
defined via the requirement that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f(s)g(t)d\mu^{(\mathrm{ov})}_{x,y}(s,t)=\varphi(f(x)g(y)),\ \ \forall\,f,g\in\mathrm{Bor}_{b}(\mathbb{R}),

where
\mathrm{Bor}_{b}(\mathbb{R})
is the space complex-valued bounded Borel functions on
\mathbb{R}
.

In the
W^{*}
-framework, the measure
\mu^{(\mathrm{ov})}_{x,y}
has been in use for
quite some time, and is presented for instance in Section I.1 of
[
18
]
.
It is worth observing that its construction can be performed more generally, in any

*
-probability space
(\mathcal{A},\varphi)
where we have an ability to do Borel
functional calculus for selfadjoint elements; we refer to such a structure by calling
it a “Borel-ncps”, see Sections
2
and
3
below.
Using the Borel-ncps framework is relevant, because it allows one to rigorously
consider overlap measures in a
*
-probability space of random
N\times N
matrices
(cf. Example
2.9
), and to study the weak convergence of such overlap
measures in the large
N
limit. Doing so helps explaining why some explicit formulas
of free denoisers arising in the
W^{*}
-framework coincide with formulas that had
been previously found, via random matrix techniques, in papers about matrix denoising.
We comment on this in the final Section
9
of the paper.

The two marginals of
\mu^{(\mathrm{ov})}_{x,y}
turn out to be the distributions
\mu_{x}
and
\mu_{y}

of the two selfadjoint elements
x,y
we started with. If
\mu^{(\mathrm{ov})}_{x,y}
happens to be
absolutely continuous with respect to the direct product
\mu_{x}\times\mu_{y}
, then
the Radon-Nikodym derivative
d\mu^{(\mathrm{ov})}_{x,y}/(d\mu_{x}\times d\mu_{y})
will be referred
to as the
overlap function
of
x
and
y
. We note that
\mu^{(\mathrm{ov})}_{x,y}
does not
always have to be absolutely continuous with respect to
\mu_{x}\times\mu_{y}
(e.g. when

y=x
, where
x
has absolutely continuous distribution with respect to Lebesgue measure);
but this will nevertheless be the case in several relevant examples.

Our motivation for the “overlap” terminology comes from the case when

\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{M}_{N}(\mathbb{C})
, considered with the normalized trace

\varphi=\frac{1}{N}\mathrm{Tr}
, when the pair of selfadjoint elements

x,y\in\mathcal{A}
to be considered is thus a pair of complex Hermitian matrices.
In this case, the overlap function
{\mathrm{o}}_{x,y}
is always sure
to exist. It is defined on
\mathrm{Spec}(x)\times\mathrm{Spec}(y)
where

\mathrm{Spec}(x),\mathrm{Spec}(y)\subseteq\mathbb{R}
are the sets of eigenvalues of the
matrices
x
and
y
, and its values are computed in terms of quantities

|\langle u,v\rangle|^{2}
, where
u,v\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
are eigenvectors
of
x
and respectively of
y
(cf. Example
3.5
below).
For instance: if
\lambda,\rho
are eigenvalues of multiplicity
1
of

x
and
y
, respectively, and if
u,v\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
are unit vectors such that

x(u)=\lambda u
and
y(v)=\rho v
, then we get

{\mathrm{o}}_{x,y}(\lambda,\rho)=N\,|\langle u,v\rangle|^{2}

– this is precisely the kind of quantity called an overlap in the
physics literature.

The fact that
\mu^{(\mathrm{ov})}_{x,y}
is compactly supported on
\mathrm{Spec}(x)\times\mathrm{Spec}(y)

holds in general in the
W^{*}
-framework, where

\mathrm{Spec}(x),\mathrm{Spec}(y)\subseteq\mathbb{R}
are now the spectra of the selfadjoint
elements
x,y\in\mathcal{A}
. As mentioned at the beginning of the subsection, our
reason for considering
\mu^{(\mathrm{ov})}_{x,y}
in this framework is that it can be used to
compute the conditional expectation
E(x\,|\,y)
via an analogue of the
procedure from Equations (
1.1
), (
1.2
): disintegrate
\mu^{(\mathrm{ov})}_{x,y}

into a family of probability measures
(\mu_{t})_{t\in\mathrm{Spec}(y)}
with respect
to the distribution of
y
(where every
\mu_{t}
is a probability measure on
\mathrm{Spec}(x)
),
then write
E(x\,|\,y)
as a function of
y
in the same way as in (
1.2
),

E(x\,|\,y)=h(y)
with
h(t):=\int_{\mathrm{Spec}(x)}s\,d\mu_{t}(s).

For the details of how this goes, see Section
4
below, and particularly
Proposition
4.3
.

1.5.
Description of results – additive case.

In the case when
x=a
and

1.6.
Description of results – multiplicative case.

Results analogous to those of the preceding subsection hold in connection
to the multiplication of two freely independent random variables

and

,
in two cases – when

are assumed to be positive,
and respectively when

We will focus on the positive case, which is presented in this subsection and

For the unitary case, a brief exposition of the relevant statements appears
in Appendix A at the end of the paper.

Concerning the multiplication of freely independent positive random variables,
we prove the following analogue of Theorem

.

Let
a,b
be freely independent positive random variables in a

-probability space
(\mathcal{A},\varphi)
. Let
\mu
and respectively
\nu
be the distributions of
a
and
b
with respect to
\varphi
, and assume that neither of
\mu,\nu
is a point mass. Denote by
f_{\mu\boxtimes\nu}

\mu\boxtimes\nu
and by

\omega
the continuation to the real line of the subordination function of

\mu\boxtimes\nu
with respect to
\mu
. Then there exists a closed
set
0\in E\subseteq[0,\infty)
, of Lebesgue measure zero, such that

\displaystyle{\mathrm{o}}_{a,a^{1/2}ba^{1/2}}(s,t)=\begin{cases}-\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{1}{tf_{\mu\boxtimes\nu}(t)}\operatorname{Im}\left(\frac{1}{1-\omega(1/t)s}\right)\quad&\mbox{ if }s\in\mathrm{supp}(\mu)\\
&\mbox{ and }t\in\mathrm{supp}(\mu\boxtimes\nu)^{ac}\setminus E,\\
\frac{1}{\mu(\{s\})}1_{\omega(1/t)=1/s}\qquad&\mbox{ if }s\in\mathrm{supp}(\mu)\\
&\mbox{ and $t>0$ is an atom of $\mu\boxtimes\nu$},\\
\frac{1}{\nu(\{0\})}\frac{1}{1-s\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(\nu(\{0\})-1)}1_{\nu(\{0\})>\mu(\{0\})}\quad&\mbox{ if }s\in\mathrm{supp}(\mu)\setminus\{0\},t=0,\\
\frac{1}{\mu\boxtimes\nu(\{0\})}1_{\mu\boxtimes\nu(\{0\})>0}\qquad&\mbox{ if }s=t=0,\end{cases}

defines
\mu\times\mu\boxtimes\nu
-almost everywhere a function
{\mathrm{o}}_{a,a^{1/2}ba^{1/2}}:\mathrm{Spec}(a)\times\mathrm{Spec}(a^{1/2}ba^{1/2})\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Then

d\mu^{(\mathrm{ov})}_{a,a^{1/2}ba^{1/2}}(s,t)={\mathrm{o}}_{a,a^{1/2}ba^{1/2}}(s,t)\mu(ds)\,\mu\boxtimes\nu(dt)
. In particular
\mu^{(\mathrm{ov})}_{a,a^{1/2}ba^{1/2}}\ll\mu\times\mu\boxtimes\nu
and
{\mathrm{o}}_{a,a^{1/2}ba^{1/2}}
is the overlap function of
a
and
a^{1/2}ba^{1/2}
.

Same as with the description of the overlap function, the general description
of conditional expectations
E(f(a)|a^{1/2}ba^{1/2})
is more involved than in the additive case;
we refer to Corollary
7.5
for the precise statement. In the case of
f(x)=x
we can get a compact form similar to the one in the additive case

h(t)=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\operatorname{Im}\left(\frac{G_{\mu\boxtimes\nu}(t+i\varepsilon)-\frac{1}{t+i\varepsilon}}{\omega(\frac{1}{t+i\varepsilon})}\right)}{\operatorname{Im}\left(G_{\mu\boxtimes\nu}(t+i\varepsilon)\right)}.

We make more comments about this formula in Subsection
8.5
.

We next present here the special
case when
b
is a free Poisson element, which gives the free multiplicative analogue of Tweedie’s formula.

Example 1.6
.

(Free multiplicative Tweedie’s formula.)

Let
a,b
be freely independent positive random variables with respective distributions
\mu,\nu
in a
W^{*}
-probability space
(\mathcal{A},\varphi)
. Assume that
\nu
is the free Poisson distribution with parameter
\lambda>0
and, for simplicity, let us also assume
that
\mu
has no atom at zero. We then get

E(a\mid a^{1/2}ba^{1/2})=h(a^{1/2}ba^{1/2})
with

(1.12)

h(t)=\begin{cases}\frac{\lambda t}{|\lambda-1+tG_{\mu\boxtimes\nu}(t)|^{2}}&\mbox{ if }t\in\mathrm{supp}(\mu\boxtimes\nu)\setminus\{0\},\\
-\frac{\lambda}{(1-\lambda)G_{(\mu\boxtimes\nu)^{ac}}\left(0\right)}&\mbox{ if }t=0\mbox{ when }\lambda<1,\end{cases}

where
G_{\mu\boxtimes\nu}
and
G_{(\mu\boxtimes\nu)^{ac}}
are the
respective Cauchy transforms of
\mu\boxtimes\nu
and of its absolutely continuous part.
As explained in Section
9
below, this
recovers a well-known formula of Ledoit and Péché
[
21
]

for the shrinkage estimator of a covariance matrix. Similarly to what was the case
for the free additive Tweedie’s formula (
1.4
), we note here
the remarkable fact that, in Equation (
1.12
), the description of
h
only
makes use of the distribution of
a^{1/2}ba^{1/2}
.

1.7.
General noise and conditional freeness.

In the general case when the noisy element is given by
P(a,b)
our goal is, once again,
to find
E(a\mid P(a,b))
. We show that this problem can be reduced
to the problem of finding the distribution of
P(a,b)
where
a,b
are conditionally
free (or “c-free”, for short) in the sense of
Bożejko-Leinert-Speicher
[
15
]
.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
a,b
be freely independent selfadjoint non-commutative random variables
in a
W^{*}
-probability space
(\mathcal{A},\varphi)
. Let
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}

be a bounded non-negative Borel-measurable function such that
\varphi(f(a))=1
. Define
another state
\chi:\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{C}
by

\chi(y):=\varphi(f(a)y),\qquad\forall y\in\mathcal{A}.

Then
a,b
are conditionally free with respect to the pair of states
(\varphi,\chi)
.
Moreover, let
P
be a selfadjoint polynomial in two non-commuting variables and
denote by
\mu^{\varphi}_{P(a,b)}
and
\mu^{\chi}_{P(a,b)}
the distributions of

P(a,b)
with respect to
\varphi
and
\chi
, respectively. Then

\mu^{\chi}_{P(a,b)}\ll\mu^{\varphi}_{P(a,b)}
and

E(f(a)\mid P(a,b))=h\bigl(\,P(a,b)\,\bigr)\mbox{ for }h=\frac{d\mu^{\chi}_{P(a,b)}}{d\mu^{\varphi}_{P(a,b)}}.

In the setting of Theorem
1.7
, the elements
a,b
are freely
independent with respect to
\varphi
, hence
\mu^{\varphi}_{P(a,b)}

can be approached by the methods developed in
[
10
]

for finding distributions of general polynomials and rational functions
in free random variables. Theorem
1.7
brings up the question of
understanding, in the
c
-free setting, what is the distribution

\mu^{\chi}_{P(a,b)}
. We mention that the latter question is currently
being investigated in an ongoing work that comes in the continuation of
the paper
[
22
]
; this is related to the fact
that
[
22
]
presents a solution to the complementary
problem of computing conditional expectations of resolvents

E((z-P(a,b))^{-1}|a)
(which offers another method for approaching the
distribution
\mu^{\varphi}_{P(a,b)}
).

1.8.
Relation to matrix denoising.

The problem of free denoising studied
in the present paper is a counterpart, set in the framework of a
W^{*}
-probability
space, for the statistical problem studied in random matrix theory under the name
of
matrix denoising
. In the latter problem, an unknown signal represented by a
Hermitian
N\times N
random matrix
A_{N}
is corrupted by a noise which is itself a
Hermitian
N\times N
random matrix
B_{N}
, and one would like to “recover the signal”

A_{N}
from a noisy observation of the form
P(A_{N},B_{N})
– that is, find an estimate of

A_{N}
as a function of
P(A_{N},B_{N})
. Free denoising and matrix denoising are related,
due to a well-studied phenomenon of asymptotic free independence for random matrices
(see e.g. Chapter 4 of the monograph
[
23
]
).
This explains, in particular, why the free denoisers giving analogues for Tweedie’s
formula (Equations (
1.4
) and (
1.12
)) are in
agreement with formulas that appeared previously in the random matrix literature

[
16
,
21
]
, in papers about matrix denoising.
The methods used in these papers are different in scope from
those that come up naturally in the
W^{*}
-probability setting (but see, however, the
brief discussion about this made in Section 19.4 of the monograph
[
25
]
).

Section
9
of the present paper gives a more detailed account
of the connection between free denoising and matrix denoising, starting with the general
observation that the notion of overlap measure (which also makes sense in a Borel-ncps
of
N\times N
random matrices) is well-behaved under the natural notion of convergence
in moments for pairs of selfadjoint elements. In a nutshell, the point made in
Section
9
is as follows: suppose that a sequence of pairs of
Hermitian random matrices
(A_{N},B_{N})_{N=1}^{\infty}
converges in moments to a pair

(a,b)
of freely independent selfadjoint elements in a
W^{*}
-probability space; then
the free denoiser
h
for
a
with respect to
P(a,b)
provides an asymptotically optimal
solution, in
L^{2}
-sense, to the corresponding
matrix denoising problem of
A_{N}
with respect to
P(A_{N},B_{N})
. The term
“asymptotically optimal” is considered here with the meaning that no bounded
continuous function can asymptotically outperform
h
in the
L^{2}
-sense; for the precise
statements, see Corollary
9.3
in
Subsection
9
.1, and the specific discussion of additive
and multiplicative denoising in Subsections
9
.2 and

9
.3.

1.9.
Organization of the paper.

Besides the Introduction, this paper has eight more sections and an Appendix,
as follows.

– We introduce and study the overlap measure
and overlap function in Section
3
, in the setting of a Borel-ncps,
after having discussed the Borel-ncps framework in Section
2
.

– Starting with Section
4
, we focus on the setting of a

W^{*}
-probability space; in particular, Section
4
discusses
how, in general, the disintegration of the overlap measure is used to compute
conditional expectations
E(x\,|\,y)
in the
W^{*}
-framework.

– After a review of
necessary background in Section
5
, we take on additive free denoisers

E(a\,|\,a+b)
in Section

7
we detail the results about multiplicative free

that were announced in Subsection 1.6.

– In Section

2.
The framework of a Borel-ncps

Notation and Remark 2.1
.

The underlying structure we start from is the one of a
*
-probability space

(\mathcal{A},\varphi)
, with
\varphi
a faithful trace. Thus
\mathcal{A}
is a unital

*
-algebra over
\mathbb{C}
and
\varphi:\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{C}
is a linear map which is positive
and faithful (
\varphi(a^{*}a)\geq 0
for all
a\in\mathcal{A}
, with equality if and
only if
a=0_{{}_{\mathcal{A}}}
), is tracial (
\varphi(ab)=\varphi(ba)
for all

a,b\in\mathcal{A}
), and has
\varphi(1_{{}_{\mathcal{A}}})=1
.

For
(\mathcal{A},\varphi)
as above, one considers the set of selfadjoint elements

\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{sa}}:=\{x\in\mathcal{A}\mid x=x^{*}\}
, and the set of positive
elements

\mathcal{A}^{+}:=\Bigl\{x\in\mathcal{A}\begin{array}[]{ll}\vline&\exists\,k\in\mathbb{N}\mbox{ and }a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}\in\mathcal{A}\\
\vline&\mbox{such that $x=a_{1}^{*}a_{1}+\cdots+a_{k}^{*}a_{k}$}\end{array}\Bigr\}.

It is immediate that
\mathcal{A}^{+}
is a cone, i.e. it is stable under linear
combinations with coefficients in
[0,\infty)
, and that
\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{sa}}

is the vector space over
\mathbb{R}
which is generated by
\mathcal{A}^{+}
(in the verification
of latter fact it is useful to observe that an
x\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{sa}}

can be written as
x=\frac{1}{4}(x+1_{{}_{\mathcal{A}}})^{2}-\frac{1}{4}(x-1_{{}_{\mathcal{A}}})^{2}
,
with
(x\pm 1_{{}_{\mathcal{A}}})^{2}\in\mathcal{A}^{+}
). The faithfulness of
\varphi
entails the
implication

\bigl[x\mbox{ and $-x$ both in $\mathcal{A}^{+}$}\bigr]\Rightarrow[x=0_{{}_{\mathcal{A}}}]
,
and this in turn implies that the cone
\mathcal{A}^{+}
gives a partial
order on
\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{sa}}
, where for
x,y\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{sa}}
we put

\bigl[\,x\leq y\,\bigr]\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{\Leftrightarrow}}\,\bigl[\,y-x\in\mathcal{A}^{+}\,\bigr]
.
It is useful to record that, besides its obvious properties related to
addition and scalar multiplication, the partial order so obtained has the
property that:

(2.1)

\bigl[\,x\leq y\mbox{ in }\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{sa}}\,]\ \Rightarrow\ \bigl[\,a^{*}xa\leq a^{*}ya\mbox{ in }\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{sa}},\ \forall\,a\in\mathcal{A}\,].

In order to verify the latter inequality, one picks

a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}\in\mathcal{A}
such that

y-x=\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}^{*}a_{j}
and observes that

a^{*}ya-a^{*}xa=\sum_{j=1}^{k}(a_{j}a)^{*}(a_{j}a)\in\mathcal{A}^{+}
.

For the purpose of this paper, it is important to be able to invoke
a suitable “functional calculus” for an element
x\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{sa}}
.
It is evident how that can be done in connection to a polynomial function: if

f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
is defined by

f(t)=\alpha_{0}+\alpha_{1}t+\cdots+\alpha_{k}t^{k}

(with
\alpha_{0},\ldots,\alpha_{k}\in\mathbb{C}
) then for

x\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{sa}}
we simply put

f(x):=\alpha_{0}1_{{}_{\mathcal{A}}}+\alpha_{1}x+\cdots+\alpha_{k}x^{k}\in\mathcal{A}
.
But we will need to consider elements
f(x)\in\mathcal{A}
which are defined
for
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
belonging to a larger space of functions,
as described in Definition
2.3
below.

Notation 2.2
.

We let
\mathrm{Bor}(\mathbb{R})
denote the unital
*
-algebra of Borel functions
from
\mathbb{R}
to
\mathbb{C}
. We will work with the unital
*
-subalgebra

\mathrm{Bor}_{b}(\mathbb{R}):=\bigl\{f\in\mathrm{Bor}(\mathbb{R})\mid f\mbox{ is bounded}\bigr\},
and with the slightly larger unital

*
-subalgebra of Borel functions with polynomial growth,

\mathrm{Bor}_{\mathrm{pol}}(\mathbb{R})=\Bigl\{f\in\mathrm{Bor}(\mathbb{R})\begin{array}[]{ll}\vline&\exists\,\alpha,\beta\in[0,\infty)\mbox{ and $p\in\mathbb{N}$ such that}\\
\vline&\mbox{ $|f(t)|\leq\alpha+\beta\,t^{2p}$
for all $t\in\mathbb{R}$}\end{array}\Bigr\}.

Definition 2.3
.

We will use the name
Borel-ncps

for a triple
(\mathcal{A},\varphi;\Gamma)
where:

(\mathcal{A},\varphi)
is a
*
-probability space with
\varphi
faithful trace,
and
\Gamma
is a family
\bigl(\Gamma_{x})_{x\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{sa}}}

of unital
*
-homomorphisms from
\mathrm{Bor}_{\mathrm{pol}}(\mathbb{R})
to
\mathcal{A}
,
such that the following conditions are fulfillled:

(2.2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\mbox{Let $\mathrm{id}\in\mathrm{Bor}_{\mathrm{pol}}(\mathbb{R})$ be the identity function,
$\mathrm{id}(t)=t$.}\\
\mbox{Then one has: $\Gamma_{x}(\mathrm{id})=x,\ \forall\,x\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{sa}}$.}\end{array}\right.

(2.3)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\mbox{Let $f,g$ and $f_{1},f_{2},\ldots,f_{n},\ldots$ in $\mathrm{Bor}_{\mathrm{pol}}(\mathbb{R})$ be}\\
\mbox{ $\ $ such that $|f_{n}|\leq g$ for all $n\in\mathbb{N}$, and}\\
\mbox{ $\ $ such that $\lim_{n\to\infty}f_{n}(t)=f(t)$ for every $t\in\mathbb{R}$.}\\
\mbox{Then one has: }\lim_{n\to\infty}\varphi\bigl(\,x\,\Gamma_{y}(f_{n})\,\bigr)=\varphi\bigl(\,x\,\Gamma_{y}(f)\,\bigr),\ \forall\,x,y\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{sa}}.\end{array}\right.

Remark 2.4
.

The way to think about
\Gamma
in the preceding definition is that,
for
x\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{sa}}
, the unital
*
-homomorphism

\Gamma_{x}:\mathrm{Bor}_{\mathrm{pol}}(\mathbb{R})\to\mathcal{A}
tells us how to do functional calculus
of
x
, with functions from
\mathrm{Bor}_{\mathrm{pol}}(\mathbb{R})
. We will in fact use the
shorthand notation

(2.4)

\Gamma_{x}(f)=:f(x),\ \mbox{for $x\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{sa}}$ and $f\in\mathrm{Bor}_{\mathrm{pol}}(\mathbb{R})$. }

With this notation, the condition (
2.2
) takes the form
“
\mathrm{id}(x)=x
”; together with the homomorphism properties
postulated for
\Gamma_{x}
, this immediately implies that (
2.4
)
extends the formula considered for
f(x)
in the last paragraph of
Remark
2.1
, in the case when
f
is a polynomial function.
Another consequence of the
*
-homomorphism properties of
\Gamma_{x}

is that
f(x)\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{sa}}
whenever
f\in\mathrm{Bor}_{\mathrm{pol}}(\mathbb{R})
is
real-valued, and one has that

(2.5)

\left[\begin{array}[]{c}f,g\in\mathrm{Bor}_{\mathrm{pol}}(\mathbb{R}),\mbox{ real-valued,}\\
\mbox{with $f(t)\leq g(t)$ for every $t\in\mathbb{R}$}\end{array}\right]\ \Rightarrow\bigl[\,f(x)\leq g(x)\,\bigr]

(for the latter fact, one writes
g(x)-f(x)=a^{*}a
with

a=\sqrt{g-f}(x)
).

Let us also record that, in terms of the notation from
(
2.4
), the convergence stated on the last line of (
2.3
)
says that, for functions
f
and
f_{n}
as described there, one has

(2.6)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\varphi\bigl(\,x\,f_{n}(y)\,\bigr)=\varphi\bigl(\,x\,f(y)\,\bigr),\ \ \forall\,x,y\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{sa}}.

Remark and Notation 2.5
.

Let
(\mathcal{A},\varphi;\Gamma)
be a Borel-ncps and let

x\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{sa}}
. The functional calculus for
x
allows us
to consider the family of projections

P(t):=\mathbf{1}_{]-\infty;t]}(x)\in\mathcal{A}
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
, and
to define a non-decreasing function
F:\mathbb{R}\to[0,1]
by

F(t):=\varphi(\,P(t)\,),\ t\in\mathbb{R}.
By using
the condition (
2.3
) from Definition
2.3
one sees
that
F
is continuous on the right, with
\lim_{t\to-\infty}F(t)=0

and
\lim_{t\to\infty}F(t)=1
. This implies the existence of a
Borel probability measure
\mu_{x}
on
\mathbb{R}
, uniquely determined, such that

F
is the cumulative distribution function of
\mu_{x}
. This
\mu_{x}
will
be referred to as the
distribution of
x
with respect to
\varphi
.
Standard integration arguments show that
\mu_{x}
has finite moments of all
orders, where for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
the moment of order
n
of
\mu_{x}
is
equal to
\varphi(x^{n})
. More generally, the functions from

\mathrm{Bor}_{\mathrm{pol}}(\mathbb{R})
are
\mu_{x}
-integrable, and one has

(2.7)

\int_{\mathbb{R}}f\,d\mu_{x}=\varphi\bigl(\,f(x)\,\bigr),\ \ \forall\,f\in\mathrm{Bor}_{\mathrm{pol}}(\mathbb{R}).

Remark and Notation 2.6
.

Let
(\mathcal{A},\varphi,\Gamma)
be a Borel-ncps. We will use the notation
“
\mathrm{Spec}(a)
” for the spectrum of an
a\in\mathcal{A}
,

\mathrm{Spec}(a):=\bigl\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\mid\lambda 1_{{}_{\mathcal{A}}}-a\mbox{ is not invertible}\bigr\},

and we note the implication

(2.8)

\bigl[\,x\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{sa}}\,\bigr]\ \Rightarrow\bigl[\,\mathrm{Spec}(x)\subseteq\mathbb{R}\,\bigr].

In other words, (
2.8
) says that
\lambda 1_{{}_{\mathcal{A}}}-x
is
invertible for every
x\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{sa}}
and

\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}
. This holds because
we can let
f_{\lambda}\in\mathrm{Bor}_{\mathrm{pol}}(\mathbb{R})
be defined
by
f_{\lambda}(t)=1/(\lambda-t)
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
, and we can use
the functional calculus of
x
to get

f_{\lambda}(x)\cdot(\lambda 1_{{}_{\mathcal{A}}}-x)

=1_{{}_{\mathcal{A}}}=(\lambda 1_{{}_{\mathcal{A}}}-x)\cdot f_{\lambda}(x),

implying the required invertibility of
\lambda 1_{{}_{\mathcal{A}}}-x
.

Example 2.7
.

Let
(\mathcal{A},\varphi)
be a tracial
W^{*}
-probability space, that is, a

*
-probability space where
\mathcal{A}
is a von Neumann algebra and
\varphi
is
a normal faithful trace-state. This provides an example of
Borel-ncps, where for every
x\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{sa}}
the
*
-homomorphism

\Gamma_{x}:\mathrm{Bor}_{\mathrm{pol}}(\mathbb{R})\to\mathcal{A}
is the customary functional
calculus for selfadjoint elements (see e.g.
[
28
, Sections I.4 and III.1]
).
A special feature of this framework is that, for every

x\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{sa}}
, the spectrum
\mathrm{Spec}(x)
is a non-empty compact
subset of
\mathbb{R}
, precisely equal to the support of the distribution

\mu_{x}
(see e.g.
[
24
, Lecture 3, Proposition 3.15]
). For any

f\in\mathrm{Bor}_{\mathrm{pol}}(\mathbb{R})
and
x\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{sa}}
, the element
f(x)
only
depends on the restriction of
f
to
\mathrm{Spec}(x)
, which is sure to be bounded
(which explains why
f(x)
comes out as a bounded linear operator, belonging
to
\mathcal{A}
).

In preparation of our second main example of Borel-ncps structure, we review
the elementary notion of functional calculus for a complex Hermitian matrix.

Notation and Remark 2.8
.

Pick
N\in\mathbb{N}
and let

\mathcal{M}_{N}^{\mathrm{sa}}(\mathbb{C})=\{X\in\mathcal{M}_{N}(\mathbb{C})\mid X=X^{*}\}
.

1^{o}
Every function
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
induces a function

(2.9)

\Phi_{f}:\mathcal{M}_{N}^{\mathrm{sa}}(\mathbb{C})\to\mathcal{M}_{N}(\mathbb{C}),\ \ \Phi_{f}(X):=f(X),

with “
f(X)
” defined in the natural way,

f(X):=\alpha_{0}I_{N}+\alpha_{1}X+\cdots+\alpha_{k}X^{k}
where

p(t)=\alpha_{0}+\alpha_{1}t+\cdots+\alpha_{k}t^{k}
is any polynomial in

\mathbb{C}[t]
which agrees with
f
on the set of eigenvalues of
X
.
Equivalently, one can write
f(X)=U\,f(D)\,U^{*}
, where
X=UDU^{*}

is any orthogonal diagonalization of
X
, and
f(D)
is obtained by applying

f
to the diagonal entries of
D
.

We note the following properties of the function
\Phi_{f}
from (
2.9
):

(i) If
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
is continuous, then
\Phi_{f}
is continuous
in the norm topology.

(ii) If
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
is a Borel function, then so is
\Phi_{f}
(where

\mathcal{M}_{N}^{\mathrm{sa}}(\mathbb{C})
and
\mathcal{M}_{N}(\mathbb{C})
are

considered with their
natural Borel sigma-algebras).

The verification of (i) is made by uniformly approximating
f
with
polynomials on compact intervals of
\mathbb{R}
. Then (ii) follows from
(i) and the observation that the algebra of functions

\bigl\{f\in\mathrm{Bor}(\mathbb{R})\mid\mbox{$\Phi_{f}$ is Borel}\bigr\}

is closed under sequential pointwise convergence.

2^{o}
Suppose now that we are also given a probability space

(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)
, and let us consider the unital
*
-algebra
of matrix-valued random variables

(2.10)

L^{0}\bigl(\Omega,\mathcal{M}_{N}(\mathbb{C})\bigr):=\Bigl\{a:\Omega\to\mathcal{M}_{N}(\mathbb{C})\begin{array}[]{ll}\vline&a\mbox{ is measurable between $\mathcal{F}$ and}\\
\vline&\mbox{the Borel sigma-algebra of $\mathcal{M}_{N}(\mathbb{C})$}\end{array}\Bigr\}

(with random variables
a,b
identified when they coincide
P
-almost
everywhere). For every

x=x^{*}\in L^{0}\bigl(\Omega,\mathcal{M}_{N}(\mathbb{C})\bigr)
and

f\in\mathrm{Bor}(\mathbb{R})
we then define

f(x):\Omega\to\mathcal{M}_{N}(\mathbb{C})
by

(2.11)

\bigl(\,f(x)\,\bigr)(\omega):=f(\,x(\omega)\,)\in\mathcal{M}_{N}(\mathbb{C}),\ \ \omega\in\Omega.

The function
f(x)
defined in (
2.11
) is measurable, hence
is in
L^{0}\bigl(\Omega,\mathcal{M}_{N}(\mathbb{C})\bigr)
, since

f(x)=\Phi_{f}\circ x
with
\Phi_{f}
as in (
2.9
).
It is immediate that, for a fixed

x=x^{*}\in L^{0}\bigl(\Omega,\mathcal{M}_{N}(\mathbb{C})\bigr)
,
the functional calculus defined by (
2.11
) respects the algebraic
structure of
\mathrm{Bor}(\mathbb{R})
, e.g. one has that
(f\,g)(x)=f(x)\,g(x)

for any
f,g\in\mathrm{Bor}(\mathbb{R})
, or that

f(x)\in\bigl[\,L^{0}\bigl(\Omega,\mathcal{M}_{N}(\mathbb{C})\bigr)\,\bigr]^{+}

for any real non-negative valued
f\in\mathrm{Bor}(\mathbb{R})
.

Example 2.9
.

We continue to use the framework of the preceding notation, and for every

a\in L^{0}\bigl(\Omega,\mathcal{M}_{N}(\mathbb{C})\bigr)
and
1\leq j,k\leq N

we let
\mathrm{Entry}_{j,k}(a):\Omega\to\mathbb{C}
be the random variable which
selects the
(j,k)
-entry of
a
. For

a\in L^{0}\bigl(\Omega,\mathcal{M}_{N}(\mathbb{C})\bigr)
we will also use the
customary notation
\mathrm{Tr}(a):=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\mathrm{Entry}_{j,j}(a)
.

The unital
*
-algebra used by this example of Borel-ncps is

(2.12)

\mathcal{A}:=\bigl\{a\in L^{0}\bigl(\Omega,\mathcal{M}_{N}(\mathbb{C})\bigr)\mid\mathrm{Entry}_{j,k}(a)\in L^{\infty-}(\Omega)\mbox{ for all }1\leq j,k\leq N\bigr\},

where
L^{\infty-}(\Omega)
is the algebra of complex-valued
random variables with finite moments of all orders on

(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)
. We note that, thanks to the identity

\mathrm{Tr}(a^{*}a)=\sum_{j,k=1}^{N}|\mathrm{Entry}_{j,k}(a)|^{2},\ a\in L^{0}\bigl(\Omega,\mathcal{M}_{N}(\mathbb{C})\bigr),

one can also describe our algebra
\mathcal{A}
from (
2.12
)
in the form

(2.13)

\mathcal{A}=\bigl\{a\in L^{0}\bigl(\Omega,\mathcal{M}_{N}(\mathbb{C})\bigr)\mid\mathrm{Tr}(a^{*}a)\in L^{\infty-}(\Omega)\bigr\}.

The functional
\varphi:\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{C}
that we consider is defined by

\varphi(a):=\frac{1}{N}\,\mathbb{E}(\,\mathrm{Tr}(a)\,),\ a\in\mathcal{A}.

It is straightforward to check that in this way
we get a
*
-probability space
(\mathcal{A},\varphi)
where
\varphi
is a
faithful trace.

We next observe that the selfadjoint elements of
\mathcal{A}
have
a natural notion of functional calculus, coming from the following fact.

Fact 1.
Let
x\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{sa}}
and
f\in\mathrm{Bor}_{\mathrm{pol}}(\mathbb{R})
,
and let

f(x)\in L^{0}\bigl(\Omega,\mathcal{M}_{N}(\mathbb{C})\bigr)

be defined by (
2.11
).

Then
f(x)\in\mathcal{A}
.

Verification of Fact 1.
Since
(\,f(x)\,)^{*}\,f(x)=|f|^{2}(x)
,
what we must check here (according to (
2.13
)) is that the non-negative
random variable
\mathrm{Tr}\bigl(\,|f|^{2}(x)\,\bigr)
belongs to

L^{\infty-}(\Omega)
.
To that end we pick
\alpha,\beta\in[0,\infty)
and
p\in\mathbb{N}
such that

|f|^{2}(t)\leq\alpha+\beta\,t^{2p}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
, and we use the
properties of functional calculus on
L^{0}\bigl(\Omega,\mathcal{M}_{N}(\mathbb{C})\bigr)

to find that

(2.14)

\mathrm{Tr}\bigl(\,|f|^{2}(x)\,\bigr)\leq\mathrm{Tr}\bigl(\,\alpha I_{N}+\beta x^{2p}\,\bigr)=N\alpha+\beta\mathrm{Tr}(\,x^{2p}\,).

The random variable
\mathrm{Tr}(\,x^{2p}\,)
is in

L^{\infty-}(\Omega)
, because it can be written as an algebraic
expression in the entries

\mathrm{Entry}_{j,k}(x)\in L^{\infty-}(\Omega)
. From the inequality (
2.14
)
it then follows that
\mathrm{Tr}\bigl(\,|f|^{2}(x)\,\bigr)
is in

L^{\infty-}(\Omega)
as well.

\square

It is immediate that, for every
x\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{sa}}
, the assignment

f\mapsto f(x)
defines a unital
*
-homomorphism

\Gamma_{x}:\mathrm{Bor}_{\mathrm{pol}}(\mathbb{R})\to\mathcal{A}
such that
\Gamma_{x}(\mathrm{id})=x
.
In order to confirm that we are dealing with a Borel-ncps structure, we are
thus left to check a dominated convergence condition:

Fact 2.
The condition (
2.3
) from Definition
2.3
is satisfied.

Verification of Fact 2.
Let
(f_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty},f,g\in\mathrm{Bor}_{\mathrm{pol}}(\mathbb{R})

and
x,y\in\mathcal{A}
be as in (
2.3
). We have

(2.15)

\varphi\bigl(\,x\,f_{n}(y)\,\bigr)=\frac{1}{N}\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{Tr}\bigl(\,x(\omega)\,f_{n}(y(\omega))\,\bigr)\,dP(\omega),\ n\in\mathbb{N},

and a similar formula holds for
\varphi\bigl(\,x\,f(y)\,\bigr)
.
The pointwise convergence of the
f_{n}
’s to
f
implies that
for every
\omega\in\Omega
we have

||\cdot||-\lim_{n\to\infty}f_{n}(y(\omega))=f(y(\omega))
(norm-convergence in
\mathcal{M}_{N}(\mathbb{C})
),
and this immediately entails that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathrm{Tr}\bigl(\,x(\omega)\,f_{n}(y(\omega))\,\bigr)=\mathrm{Tr}\bigl(\,x(\omega)\,f(y(\omega))\,\bigr).

Thus the integrand on the right-hand side of (
2.15
)
converges pointwise, for
n\to\infty
, to its counterpart having

f
instead of
f_{n}
in its description.

In order to complete the verification of Fact 2, it will thus be sufficient
to find a dominating function (independent of
n
) for the integrand on the
right-hand side of (
2.15
) – once this is done, the Lebesgue dominated
convergence theorem will give us the desired convergence of

\varphi\bigl(\,x\,f_{n}(y)\,\bigr)
to

\varphi\bigl(\,x\,f(y)\,\bigr)
.

Towards finding a dominating function, we pick some

\alpha,\beta\in[0,\infty)
and
p\in\mathbb{N}
such that

g^{2}(t)\leq\alpha+\beta\,t^{2p}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
; this implies
that
|f_{n}|^{2}(t)\leq\alpha+\beta\,t^{2p}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}

and
t\in\mathbb{R},
with the further consequence that
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\omega\in\Omega
we have

(2.16)

\mathrm{Tr}\bigl(\,|f_{n}|^{2}(y(\omega))\,\bigr)\leq\alpha\,N+\beta\,\mathrm{Tr}\bigl(\,y^{2p}(\omega)\,\bigr)=:v(\omega).

Returning to the integrand on the right-hand side of (
2.15
),
we then invoke the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality to infer that

(2.17)

\vline\ \mathrm{Tr}\bigl(\,x(\omega)\,f_{n}(y(\omega))\,\bigr)\ \vline\leq\sqrt{u(\omega)\,v(\omega)},\ \ \forall\,n\in\mathbb{N}\mbox{ and }\omega\in\Omega,

where
v(\omega)
is defined in (
2.16
) and we put

u(\omega):=\mathrm{Tr}\bigl(\,x^{2}(\omega)\,\bigr)
.
We know (from how the algebra
\mathcal{A}
was defined) that

\mathrm{Tr}(x^{2}),\mathrm{Tr}(y^{2p})\in L^{\infty-}(\Omega)
,
and this immediately implies that

\sqrt{u\,v}
is in
L^{\infty-}(\Omega)
as well. Thus

\sqrt{u\,v}
is an integrable non-negative random variable which
(in view of (
2.17
)) can be used as our dominating function.

\square

3.
Overlap measure and overlap function

In this section we review the construction and some basic properties of a
probability measure
\mu^{(\mathrm{ov})}_{x,y}
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, which is associated to a pair of
selfadjoint elements
x,y
in a Borel-ncps
(\mathcal{A},\varphi;\Gamma)
.
In the special case when our Borel-ncps is a
W^{*}
-probability space, this
construction has been known for a long time, and appears for instance in
Section I.1 of
[
18
]
.
For the reader’s reassurance that the construction extends to the Borel-ncps
setting, we provide its details in the proof of Proposition
3.1
below.

The terms “overlap measure” and “overlap function” used
in the present paper are inspired from the case when
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{M}_{N}(\mathbb{C})
and

\varphi=\frac{1}{N}\mathrm{Tr}
.
In that case, the description of
\mu^{(\mathrm{ov})}_{x,y}
boils down to looking at inner products
between the eigenvectors of
x
and those of
y
(cf. Example
3.5
below),
and such inner products are known as “overlaps” in the physics and in the random matrix
literature.

Proposition and Definition 3.1
.

Let
(\mathcal{A},\varphi;\Gamma)
be a Borel-ncps,
and let
x,y\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{sa}}
.
There exists a probability measure
\mu
on the Borel sigma-algebra
of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, uniquely determined, such that

(3.1)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f(s)g(t)d\mu(s,t)=\varphi(f(x)g(y)),\ \ \forall\,f,g\in\mathrm{Bor}_{b}(\mathbb{R}),

where the elements
f(x),g(y)\in\mathcal{A}
that appear in (
3.1
) are
obtained by performing Borel functional calculus on
x
and on
y
, respectively.

We will refer to this probability measure
\mu
as the
overlap measure

of
x
and
y
, and we will denote it as
\mu^{(\mathrm{ov})}_{x,y}
.

Proof.

Construction of
\mu
.
We consider the families of projections

P(s):=\mathbf{1}_{]-\infty;s]}(x),\ Q(t):=\mathbf{1}_{]-\infty;t]}(y),\ \ s,t\in\mathbb{R},

and we define
F:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
by

F(s,t)=\varphi(\,P(s)Q(t)\,)
, for
s,t\in\mathbb{R}
.
We will verify that
F
has the properties required from a cumulative distribution
function on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

We start by observing that, due to the trace property of
\varphi
, we can write

(3.2)

F(s,t)=\varphi\bigl(\,P(s)\,Q(t)\,P(s)\,\bigr),\ \ s,t\in\mathbb{R},

with
P(s)\,Q(t)\,P(s)\in\mathcal{A}^{+}
. For any fixed
s_{o}\in\mathbb{R}
, we
can invoke (
2.5
) and then (
2.1
) in order to find that

0_{{}_{\mathcal{A}}}\leq P(s_{o})\,Q(t_{1})\,P(s_{o})\leq P(s_{o})\,Q(t_{2})\,P(s_{o})\leq P(s_{o})\,1_{{}_{\mathcal{A}}}\,P(s_{o})=P(s_{o}),\ \ \forall\,t_{1}\leq t_{2}\in\mathbb{R};

this, in turn, implies that the function
\mathbb{R}\ni t\mapsto F(s_{o},t)

is non-decreasing, with values in
[0,\varphi(P(s_{o}))].

A standard calculation based on (
2.6
) then shows that the latter
function is continuous from the right and has

\lim_{t\to-\infty}F(s_{o},t)=0
,

\lim_{t\to\infty}F(s_{o},t)=\varphi(P(s_{o})).

Likewise, one sees that for fixed
t_{o}\in\mathbb{R}
the function

\mathbb{R}\ni s\mapsto F(s,t_{o})
is non-decreasing and continuous
from the right, with

\lim_{s\to-\infty}F(s,t_{o})=0
,

\lim_{s\to\infty}F(s,t_{o})=\varphi(Q(t_{o})).

Arguments similar to the above show, moreover, that
F
has correct limits
(of
0
or
1
) when both
s,t\to\pm\infty
.

In order to argue that
F
is a
2
-dimensional cumulative function, we are left
to verify that for every
s_{1}<s_{2}
and
t_{1}<t_{2}
in
\mathbb{R}
we have

(3.3)

F(s_{2},t_{2})-F(s_{2},t_{1})-F(s_{1},t_{2})+F(s_{1},t_{1})\geq 0.

The reader should have no difficulty to perform the bit of linear algebra
which rewrites the quantity from (
3.3
) in the form

\varphi\bigl(\,(P(s_{2})-P(s_{1}))\,(Q(t_{2})-Q(t_{1}))\,(P(s_{2})-P(s_{1})\,\bigr)
; the latter
quantity is indeed sure to be
\geq 0
,
since
\varphi
is applied there to an element from
\mathcal{A}^{+}
.

As a consequence of the above, we infer the existence of
a probability measure
\mu
on the Borel sigma-algebra of

\mathbb{R}^{2}
, such that

\mu\bigl(\,]-\infty,s]\,\times\,]-\infty,t]\,\bigr)=F(s,t),\ \ \forall\,s,t\in\mathbb{R}.

Verification that (
3.1
) holds.
The
measure
\mu
constructed above has the property that

(3.4)

\mu\bigl(\,]s_{1},s_{2}]\,\times\,]t_{1},t_{2}]\,\bigr)=\varphi\bigl(\,\mathbf{1}_{]s_{1},s_{2}]}(x)\,\mathbf{1}_{]t_{1},t_{2}]}(y)\,\bigr),\ \ \forall\,s_{1}<s_{2}\mbox{ and $t_{1}<t_{2}$ in $\mathbb{R}$;}

this holds because, as immediately verified, both sides of (
3.4
)
are equal to the algebraic expression considered in (
3.3
).

In order to upgrade (
3.4
) to the formula indicated in (
3.1
),
we use an intermediate step, as follows: for every
s_{1}<s_{2}
in
\mathbb{R}
we put

\mathcal{V}_{s_{1},s_{2}}:=\{g\in\mathrm{Bor}_{b}(\mathbb{R})\mid\mbox{ (\ref{eqn:Y1a}) holds for }\mathbf{1}_{]s_{1},s_{2}]}\mbox{ and }g\},

and (by invoking appropriate features of the Borel functional calculus
for the element
y
) we observe that

\mathcal{V}_{s_{1},s_{2}}
is a linear subspace of
\mathrm{Bor}_{b}(\mathbb{R})
which
is closed under the pointwise convergence of uniformly bounded
sequences of functions.
But let us also observe that, as a consequence of (
3.4
), the space

\mathcal{V}_{s_{1},s_{2}}
contains the indicator functions of all the half-open intervals

]t_{1},t_{2}]
with
t_{1}<t_{2}
in
\mathbb{R}
. Together with stability
under linear combinations and under uniformly bounded pointwise convergence,
this implies that
\mathcal{V}_{s_{1},s_{2}}=\mathrm{Bor}_{b}(\mathbb{R})
.

Now, for every
g\in\mathrm{Bor}_{b}(\mathbb{R})
let us put

\mathcal{W}_{g}:=\{f\in\mathrm{Bor}_{b}(\mathbb{R})\mid\mbox{(\ref{eqn:Y1a}) holds for $f$ and $g$}\}.

The intermediate step taken in the preceding paragraph assures us that

\mathcal{W}_{g}
contains the indicator functions of all the half-open intervals

]s_{1},s_{2}]
with
s_{1}<s_{2}
in
\mathbb{R}
. On the other hand,
\mathcal{W}_{g}

is found (by invoking appropriate features of the Borel functional calculus
for the element
x
) to be a linear subspace of
\mathrm{Bor}_{b}(\mathbb{R})

which is closed under the pointwise convergence of uniformly bounded
sequences of functions. Putting together all these properties forces the
conclusion that
\mathcal{W}_{g}=\mathrm{Bor}_{b}(\mathbb{R})
.

Finally: the equality
\mathcal{W}_{g}=\mathrm{Bor}_{b}(\mathbb{R})
, holding for all

g\in\mathrm{Bor}_{b}(\mathbb{R})
, amounts precisely to the fact that the required
Equation (
3.1
) holds for all
f,g\in\mathrm{Bor}_{b}(\mathbb{R})
.

Uniqueness of
\mu
.
Let
\nu
be a probability measure on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
which has
the same property as described for
\mu
in (
3.1
). For any two Borel sets

A,B\subseteq\mathbb{R}
we then find that

\nu(A\times B)=\varphi\bigl(\,\mathbf{1}_{A}(x)\,\mathbf{1}_{B}(y)\,\bigr)=\mu(A\times B).

The measures
\mu
and
\nu
thus agree on the collection of sets

\{A\times B\mid A,B\mbox{ Borel subsets of }\mathbb{R}\}
. Since the latter
collection is a
\pi
-system which generates the Borel sigma-algebra of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
we conclude that
\nu=\mu
.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

1^{o}
In the preceding proposition it was convenient to only state
Equation (
3.1
) for
f,g\in\mathrm{Bor}_{b}(\mathbb{R})
, as that does not
require any discussion concerning the integrability of the function

(s,t)\mapsto f(s)g(t)
on the left-hand side. We leave it as an exercise
to the reader to check that, due to how the framework of a Borel-ncps was
set in Section 2, the equality stated in (
3.1
) continues to make
sense and to hold true for functions
f,g\in\mathrm{Bor}_{\mathrm{pol}}(\mathbb{R})
– that is,
the function
(s,t)\mapsto f(s)g(t)
is
\mu^{(\mathrm{ov})}_{x,y}
-integrable, and its integral
against
\mu^{(\mathrm{ov})}_{x,y}
is equal to
\varphi\bigl(\,f(x)\,g(y)\,\bigr)
.

2^{o}
When in Equation (
3.1
) we set the function
g
to be identically
equal to
1
, we find that the first one-dimensional marginal of
\mu^{(\mathrm{ov})}_{x,y}
(that is,
the Borel probability measure on
\mathbb{R}
defined by
A\mapsto\mu^{(\mathrm{ov})}_{x,y}(A\times\mathbb{R})
)
coincides with the distribution
\mu_{x}
of the element
x
with respect to
\varphi
,
as defined in Section 2 (cf. Remark and Notation
2.5
).
A similar argument shows that the second marginal of
\mu^{(\mathrm{ov})}_{x,y}
is equal to
\mu_{y}
,
the distribution of
y
with respect to
\varphi
.

Definition 3.3
.

Consider the same framework and notation as above and suppose that the
overlap measure
\mu^{(\mathrm{ov})}_{x,y}
is absolutely continuous with respect to the
direct product of its marginals
\mu_{x}
and
\mu_{y}
. The Radon-Nikodym derivative

(3.5)

{\mathrm{o}}_{x,y}:=\frac{d\mu^{(\mathrm{ov})}_{x,y}}{d(\mu_{x}\times\mu_{y})}

will be referred to as the
overlap function
of
x
and
y
.

Example 3.4
.

In the setting of Proposition and Definition
3.1
, consider the
situation where
x
and
y
are independent, either in the
classical (commutative) sense, or in the sense of free probability. This
assumption has the consequence that one can factor

\varphi\bigl(\,f(x)\,g(y)\,\bigr)=\varphi\bigl(\,f(x)\,\bigr)\cdot\varphi\bigl(\,g(y)\,\bigr),\ \ \forall\,f,g\in\mathrm{Bor}_{b}(\mathbb{R}).

The latter equality can be read as saying that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f(s)\,g(t)\,d\mu^{(\mathrm{ov})}_{x,y}(s,t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(s)\,d\mu_{x}(s)\cdot\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(t)\,d\mu_{y}(t),

which entails that the overlap measure
\mu^{(\mathrm{ov})}_{x,y}
is equal to the direct
product
\mu_{x}\times\mu_{y}
. This is hence a case when the overlap function

{\mathrm{o}}_{x,y}
is defined, and is identically equal to
1
.

Example 3.5
.

Consider the situation where our Borel-ncps
(\mathcal{A},\varphi)
has

\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{M}_{N}(\mathbb{C})
, with
\varphi=\mathrm{tr}_{N}
(normalized trace),
and where the elements of
\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{sa}}
are thus
N\times N

Hermitian matrices. Let us pick some
x,y\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{sa}}
, and
let
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{N}
and
\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{N}
be the
eigenvalues of
x
and respectively of
y
, counted with multiplicities.
Moreover, let us consider two orthonormal bases
u_{1},\ldots,u_{N}
and

v_{1},\ldots,v_{N}
for
\mathbb{C}^{N}
, such that

x(u_{k})=\lambda_{k}u_{k}
and
y(v_{k})=\rho_{k}v_{k}
for

1\leq k\leq N
. Some elementary linear algebra calculations show that,
in this situation, the overlap measure of
x
and
y
is

\mu^{(\mathrm{ov})}_{x,y}=\sum_{k,\ell=1}^{N}\frac{\mid\langle u_{k}\,,\,v_{\ell}\rangle\mid^{2}}{N}\,\delta_{(\lambda_{k},\rho_{\ell})}

(where
\delta_{(\lambda,\rho)}
stands for the Dirac mass concentrated
at the point
(\lambda,\rho)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
).

We note that in this example we have

\mu_{x}\times\mu_{y}=\Bigl(\,\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\delta_{\lambda_{k}}\,\Bigr)\times\Bigl(\,\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\delta_{\rho_{k}}\,\Bigr)=\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{k,\ell=1}^{N}\delta_{(\lambda_{k},\rho_{\ell})},

which makes it clear that
\mu^{(\mathrm{ov})}_{x,y}\ll\mu_{x}\times\mu_{y}
(indeed, absolute
continuity simply amounts here to the fact that the set of atoms of

\mu^{(\mathrm{ov})}_{x,y}
is contained in the set of atoms of
\mu_{x}\times\mu_{y}
). Given
eigenvalues
\lambda
of
x
and
\rho
of
y
, the value
at
(\lambda,\rho)
of the overlap function
{\mathrm{o}}_{x,y}
is the ratio

\mu^{(\mathrm{ov})}_{x,y}\bigl(\,\{\,(\lambda,\rho)\,\}\,\bigr)/\Bigl(\mu_{x}(\,\{\lambda\}\,)\cdot\mu_{y}(\,\{\rho\}\,)\Bigr).

An easy application of Parseval’s formula shows that the latter quantity can
be also written as

{\mathrm{o}}_{x,y}(\lambda,\rho)=N\,\frac{\mathrm{Tr}(p\,q)}{\mathrm{Tr}(p)\cdot\mathrm{Tr}(q)},

where
p
is the projection onto the eigenspace of
x
corresponding to
\lambda
,
and
q
is the projection onto the eigenspace of
y
corresponding to
\rho
.

4.
Conditional expectations, via disintegration of the overlap measure

In this section we fix a
W^{*}
-probability space

(\mathcal{A},\varphi)
, as considered in Example
2.7
, and we discuss
some additional structure that appears in this setting.

Remark and Notation 4.1
.

As mentioned in Example
2.7
,
the spectrum
\mathrm{Spec}(x)
of an
x\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{sa}}
is a non-empty
compact subset of
\mathbb{R}
, and the support of the distribution
\mu_{x}
is precisely
equal to
\mathrm{Spec}(x)
.

Let us now simultaneously consider two elements
x,y\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{sa}}
, and
their overlap measure
\mu^{(\mathrm{ov})}_{x,y}
. The observation recorded in the preceding
paragraph entails that
\mu^{(\mathrm{ov})}_{x,y}
has compact support, contained in

\mathrm{Spec}(x)\times\mathrm{Spec}(y)
(the Cartesian product of the supports of the two
marginals
\mu_{x}
and
\mu_{y}
of
\mu^{(\mathrm{ov})}_{x,y}
). In connection to that, an important
piece of structure which will be consistently used in what follows is that

\mu^{(\mathrm{ov})}_{x,y}
can be
disintegrated
with respect to either of its marginals
\mu_{x}

or
\mu_{y}
(see e.g.
[
19
, Chapter III, pages 78-79]
).
For instance, the disintegration with
respect to
\mu_{y}
comes in the guise of a family

\bigl\{k_{t}^{(2)}\mid t\in\mathrm{Spec}(y)\bigr\}
, where every

k_{t}^{(2)}
is a probability measure on the Borel sigma-algebra of
\mathrm{Spec}(x)
,
such that the following things happen.
First, for every bounded Borel measurable function
f:\mathrm{Spec}(x)\to\mathbb{C}
,
the “fiber-wise integration of
f
”,

(4.1)

t\mapsto\int_{\mathrm{Spec}(x)}f(s)\,dk_{t}^{(2)}(s)\mbox{ for }t\in\mathrm{Spec}(y),

is a bounded Borel measurable function on
\mathrm{Spec}(y)
.
Then, for every pair of bounded Borel measurable functions

f:\mathrm{Spec}(x)\to\mathbb{C}
and
g:\mathrm{Spec}(y)\to\mathbb{C}
, one has that

(4.2)

\int_{\mathrm{Spec}(y)}\Bigl[\,\int_{\mathrm{Spec}(x)}f(s)\,dk_{t}^{(2)}(s)\,\Bigr]\,g(t)\,d\mu_{y}(t)=\int_{\mathrm{Spec}(x)\times\mathrm{Spec}(y)}f(s)g(t)\,d\mu^{(\mathrm{ov})}_{x,y}(s,t).

Likewise, disintegration of
\mu^{(\mathrm{ov})}_{x,y}
with respect to
\mu_{x}
creates a
family
\bigl\{k_{s}^{(1)}\mid s\in\mathrm{Spec}(x)\bigr\}
, where every
k_{s}^{(1)}

is a probability measure on the Borel sigma-algebra of
\mathrm{Spec}(y)
, and where
formulas symmetric to (
4.1
) and (
4.2
) are holding.

Another relevant piece of structure that we are sure to have for the

(\mathcal{A},\varphi)
of this section concerns conditional expectations.

Remark and Notation 4.2
.

For every von Neumann subalgebra
\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathcal{A}
one has a

\varphi
-preserving
conditional expectation

onto
\mathcal{B}
, which will be denoted as
E(\cdot|\mathcal{B})
; this is the
linear map from
\mathcal{A}
to
\mathcal{B}
uniquely determined by the requirement that

(4.3)

\varphi\bigl(\,E(a|\mathcal{B})\,b\,\bigr)=\varphi(ab),\ \ \forall\,a\in\mathcal{A}\mbox{ and }b\in\mathcal{B}.

From (
4.3
) it is immediate that the map
E(\cdot|\mathcal{B})
is
a projection (that is,
E(b|\mathcal{B})=b
for
b\in\mathcal{B}
), and indeed has the
“conditional expectation property” (a.k.a.
\mathcal{B}
-bimodule property) that

E(b_{1}ab_{2}|\mathcal{B})=b_{1}\,E(a|\mathcal{B})\,b_{2}
for all
a\in\mathcal{A}

and
b_{1},b_{2}\in\mathcal{B}
.
E(\cdot|\mathcal{B})
is, moreover, a normal,
positive and faithful map (see e.g.
[
28
, Section V.2]
); its positivity
also implies, in particular, that
E(a|\mathcal{B})
is selfadjoint in
\mathcal{B}
whenever

a
is selfadjoint in
\mathcal{A}
. For the present paper, a relevant
property of
E(\cdot|\mathcal{B})
(easily derived from (
4.3
)) is
that it serves as orthogonal projection in the
L^{2}
-space associated to
the trace
\varphi
; thus, for every
a\in\mathcal{A}
one has

(4.4)

||\,a-E(a|\mathcal{B})\,||_{2}\leq||a-b||_{2},\ \ \forall\,b\in\mathcal{B},\mbox{ with equality if and only if }b=E(a|\mathcal{B}),

where
||\cdot||_{2}
is the
L^{2}
-norm associated to
\varphi
.

A special case of the above: given an

y\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{sa}}
, we will use the notation
E(\cdot|y)

to refer to the conditional expectation onto the von Neumann
subalgebra
\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathcal{A}
generated by
y
. This subalgebra can
be explicitly described by using functional calculus:

\mathcal{B}=\bigl\{g(y)\mid g\in\mathrm{Bor}_{b}(\,\mathrm{Spec}(y)\,)\bigr\},

where
\mathrm{Bor}_{b}(\,\mathrm{Spec}(y)\,)
is the space of bounded Borel
functions from
\mathrm{Spec}(y)
to
\mathbb{C}
. For
x\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{sa}}
, a
convenient way of describing
E(x|y)
is thus in the form

E(x|y)=h(y)
,
where
h:\mathrm{Spec}(y)\to\mathbb{R}
is a bounded Borel function such that

(4.5)

\displaystyle\varphi(x\,g(y))=\varphi\bigl(\,h(y)\,g(y)\,\bigr),\ \ \forall\,g\in\mathrm{Bor}_{b}(\,\mathrm{Spec}(y)\,).

Note that, in view of (
4.4
),
h(y)
is
the unique element of the von Neumann algebra generated by
y

which is at minimal distance from
x
, in the
||\cdot||_{2}
-norm
associated to
\varphi
.

Let us now clarify how the disintegration of the overlap measure
appears in calculations of conditional expectations.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
x,y\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{sa}}
and let
f:\mathrm{Spec}(x)\to\mathbb{R}
be a bounded Borel
function. Consider the element
f(x)\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{sa}}
obtained by functional
calculus, and then consider the conditional expectation
E(f(x)\,|\,y)
.
On the other hand, let
\mu^{(\mathrm{ov})}_{x,y}
be the overlap measure of
x
and
y
, and consider
the disintegration
(k_{t}^{(2)})_{t\in\mathrm{Spec}(y)}
of
\mu^{(\mathrm{ov})}_{x,y}
that was mentioned
in Remark and Notation
4.1
. One has that

(4.6)

E(f(x)\mid y)=h(y)\ \mbox{ (functional calculus of $y$)},

where
h:\mathrm{Spec}(y)\to\mathbb{R}
is obtained by fiber-wise integration as in (
4.1
),

(4.7)

h(t):=\int_{\mathrm{Spec}(x)}f(s)\,dk_{t}^{(2)}(s),\mbox{ for }t\in\mathrm{Spec}(y).

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{B}=\bigl\{g(y)\mid g\in\mathrm{Bor}_{b}(\mathrm{Spec}(y))\bigr\}
be the
von Neumann subalgebra of
\mathcal{A}
generated by
y
. We have
h(y)\in\mathcal{B}
, and in order
to establish (
4.6
) we need to check that

(4.8)

\varphi(f(x)g(y))=\varphi(h(y)g(y)),\ \ \forall\,g\in\mathrm{Bor}_{b}(\mathrm{Spec}(y)).

And indeed, by starting from the left-hand side of (
4.8
), we can write:

\displaystyle\varphi(f(x)g(y))

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathrm{Spec}(x)\times\mathrm{Spec}(y)}f(s)g(t)\,d\mu^{(\mathrm{ov})}_{x,y}(s,t)\ \mbox{ (by (\ref{eqn:Y1a})) }

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathrm{Spec}(y)}\Bigl[\,\int_{\mathrm{Spec}(x)}f(s)\,dk_{t}^{(2)}(s)\,\Bigr]\,g(t)\,d\mu_{y}(t)\ \mbox{ (by (\ref{eqn:Z1b})) }

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathrm{Spec}(y)}h(t)g(t)\,d\mu_{y}(t)\ \mbox{ (by how $h$ is defined)}

\displaystyle=\varphi(h(y)g(y))\ \mbox{ (by how $\mu_{y}$ is defined).}

The latter quantity is the right-hand side of (
4.8
), as required.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

In the setting of the preceding proposition, the function

h:\mathrm{Spec}(y)\to\mathbb{C}
defined in Equation (
4.7
) has in particular
the property that

(4.9)

\int_{\mathrm{Spec}(y)}h(t)\,d\mu_{y}(t)=\int_{\mathrm{Spec}(x)}f(s)\,d\mu_{x}(s).

Indeed, both sides of (
4.9
) are found to be equal to

\varphi(\,f(x)\,)
, where on the left-hand side we start from

\varphi(\,h(y)\,)
, re-write it as
\varphi(\,E(f(x)\mid y)\,)
,
and use the fact that

\varphi\circ E(\cdot|y)=\varphi
.

We record here the following consequence of (
4.9
), which will be used
in Section 6 below:

(4.10)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\mbox{Let $V\subseteq\mathrm{Spec}(x)$ be a Borel set with $\mu_{x}(V)=1$.}\\
\mbox{There exists a Borel set $N\subseteq\mathrm{Spec}(y)$, with $\mu_{y}(N)=0$,}\\
\mbox{such that $k_{t}^{(2)}(V)=1$ for every $t\in\mathrm{Spec}(y)\setminus N$.}\end{array}\right.

For the verification of (
4.10
) we let
f:\mathrm{Spec}(x)\to\mathbb{R}

be the indicator function of
\mathrm{Spec}(x)\setminus V
, which forces the
quantities in (
4.9
) to be equal to
0
, and where the function

h
on the left-hand side has taken the form

h(t)=1-k_{t}^{(2)}(V),\,t\in\mathrm{Spec}(y).

Since
h
is non-negative with
\int h\,d\mu_{y}=0
, we get the
conclusion stated in (
4.10
).

5.
Review of some subordination results in free probability

We will consider several analytic transforms which characterize probability
measures on the real line, as follows.

Notation 5.1
.

Let
\mu
be a probability measure on the Borel sigma-algebra of
\mathbb{R}
.
We will use the customary notation
G_{\mu}
for the Cauchy transform of

\mu
, defined by

G_{\mu}(z):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{z-t}d\mu(t),\ \ z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathrm{supp}(\mu)

(where
\mathrm{supp}(\mu)\subseteq\mathbb{R}
is the support of
\mu
).
Then for
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
such
that
1/z\not\in\mathrm{supp}(\mu)
we define

\psi_{\mu}(z):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{zt}{1-zt}d\mu(t)\mbox{ and }\eta_{\mu}(z):=\frac{\psi_{\mu}(z)}{1+\psi_{\mu}(z)}.

We will refer to the functions
\psi_{\mu}
and
\eta_{\mu}
by calling
them
moment transform
and respectively
Boolean-cumulant transform

of
\mu
.

We will also make occasional use of the
Hilbert transform
of
\mu
,
which is defined as

\displaystyle H_{\mu}(t)=\frac{1}{\pi}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}Re(G_{\mu}(t+i\varepsilon)),

with the latter limit known to exist for Lebesgue almost every
t\in\mathbb{R}

(see e.g.
[
29
, Chapter X.3]
).

The next lemma records some basic properties of the Cauchy transform.

Lemma 5.2
.

(Cf.
[
5
, Lemma 2.17]
, and the proof references indicated there.)

Let
\mu
be a Borel probability measure on
\mathbb{R}
, and denote by
\mu^{s}
,
and
\mu^{ac}
the singular and absolutely continuous (with respect to Lebesgue measure)
parts of
\mu
.

1^{o}
For
\mu^{s}
-almost all
x\in\mathbb{R}
, the non-tangential limit of the
Cauchy transform
G_{\mu}
at
x
is infinite :
\sphericalangle\lim_{z\to x}G_{\mu}(z)=\infty
.

2^{o}
For every
x\in\mathbb{R}
, we have
\mu(\{x\})=\sphericalangle\lim_{z\to x}(z-x)G_{\mu}(z)
.

3^{o}
Denote by
f
the density of
\mu^{ac}
w.r.t. the Lebesgue measure,
then for Lebesgue–almost all
x\in\mathbb{R}
,

f(x)=-\frac{1}{\pi}\sphericalangle\lim_{z\to x}\operatorname{Im}(G_{\mu}(z))
.

\square

5.1.
Review of subordination results – additive case.

Let
(\mathcal{A},\varphi)
be an ncps and consider two freely independent selfadjoint elements

a,b\in\mathcal{A}
.
We denote the distributions of
a
and
b
with respect to
\varphi
by
\mu
and
\nu
,
respectively; note that, due to the free independence of
a
and
b
, the distribution
of
a+b
is then the free additive convolution
\mu\boxplus\nu
.

As proved in
[
13
]
, there exists an analytic map
\omega:\mathbb{C}^{+}\to\mathbb{C}^{+}
,
uniquely determined, with the following properties:
(i)
G_{\mu\boxplus\nu}(z)=G_{\mu}(\omega(z))
for all
z\in\mathbb{C}^{+}
;
(ii)
\omega
increases the imaginary part;
(iii)
\omega(iy)/(iy)\to 1\mbox{ as }y\to+\infty.

This
\omega
is called the
subordination function
of
a+b
with
respect to
a
; if we just want to refer to distributions,
\omega
will be
referred to as subordination function of
\mu\boxplus\nu
with respect to
\mu
.

For the
\omega
considered above, it can be shown that: for every
s\in\mathbb{R}
, the
assignment
z\mapsto 1/(\omega(z)-s)
defines a function on
\mathbb{C}^{+}
which
can be identified as Cauchy transform for a Borel probability measure on
\mathbb{R}
.
In the same vein as in the notation from
[
13
, Theorem 3.1]
, we denote
the latter measure as
k_{s}
; we thus have

(5.1)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{z-t}k_{s}(dt)=\frac{1}{\omega(z)-s},\ \ \forall\,s\in\mathbb{R}\mbox{ and }z\in\mathbb{C}^{+}.

Moreover, bundling together the measures
k_{s}
turns out to create a Feller-Markov
kernel
\mathcal{K}
, where for any bounded Borel function
g:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
we define the
new function
\mathcal{K}g
by putting

\mathcal{K}g(s)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(t)k_{s}(dt),\ \ s\in\mathbb{R}.

A relevant point for the considerations of this paper is that the kernel
\mathcal{K}

can be used for computing conditional expectations onto the
W^{*}
-subalgebra

W^{*}(a)\subseteq\mathcal{A}
which is generated by
a
; that is, we have

E(g(a+b)|a)=\mathcal{K}g(a),\mbox{ for $g:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}$ bounded Borel function,}

where
E(\cdot|a)

is our notation for conditional expectation onto
W^{*}(a)
.

Finally, a notational comment:
it is customary to also consider the subordination function of
a+b
with respect
to
b
. In the few places of the paper where we will do that, we will denote the
two subordination functions that arise as
\omega_{1}
and
\omega_{2}
(hence the

\omega
discussed above will become
\omega_{1}
). In that setting we have
the relations

G_{\mu\boxplus\nu}(z)=G_{\mu}(\omega_{1}(z))=G_{\nu}(\omega_{2}(z)),\ \ z\in\mathbb{C}^{+};

another useful formula which is known to hold in connection to

\omega_{1}
and
\omega_{2}
says that

\omega_{1}(z)+\omega_{2}(z)=\frac{1}{G_{\mu\boxplus\nu}(z)}+z,\ \ z\in\mathbb{C}^{+}.

5.2.
Review of subordination results – multiplicative case.

Subordination results similar to those reviewed in the preceding subsection
also hold true in relation to the operation of free multiplicative convolution
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}=[0,\infty)
. More precisely, let us look once again at an ncps

(\mathcal{A},\varphi)
, where we now consider two
positive

elements
a,b\in\mathcal{A}
, such that
a
and
b
are freely independent.
We denote the distributions of
a
and
b
by
\mu
and
\nu
, respectively, and we
consider the free multiplicative convolution
\mu\boxtimes\nu
, which is the
distribution of the positive element
a^{1/2}ba^{1/2}
. In connection to this,

[
13
, Theorem 3.6]
proves that there exists an analytic map

\omega:\mathbb{C}^{+}\to\mathbb{C}^{+}
, uniquely determined by its properties that it increases
the argument of every
z\in\mathbb{C}^{+}
and that it satisfies the equation

\psi_{\mu\boxtimes\nu}(z)=\psi_{\mu}(\omega(z)),\ \ z\in\mathbb{C}^{+},

where the “
\psi
” notation is used for
moment transforms, as in Notation
5.1
.
This
\omega
is called the
multiplicative subordination function
of

\mu\boxtimes\nu
with respect to
\mu
.

In the multiplicative setting we are once again interested in a family of Borel
probability measures
k_{s}
, which are now defined on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
, via the following
prescription on their moment transforms:

(5.2)

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{zt}{1-zt}dk_{s}(t)=\frac{\omega(z)s}{1-\omega(z)s},\ \ z\in\mathbb{C}^{+}.

The relevance of these measures
k_{s}
is that
for any bounded Borel function
g
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
we have

E(\,g(a^{1/2}ba^{1/2})\mid a\,)\ =\ \mathcal{K}g(a),

with the kernel
\mathcal{K}
defined by the formula

\mathcal{K}g(s)=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}g(t)k_{s}(dt).

One considers also subordination with respect to
\nu
, if we do so we will
use notation
\omega=\omega_{1}
and we denote by
\omega_{2}
the second
subordination function for which we have
\psi_{\mu\boxtimes\nu}=\psi_{\nu}(\omega_{2}(z))
.
One has
\omega_{1}(z)\omega_{2}(z)=z\eta_{\mu\boxtimes\nu}(z)
, where

\eta_{\mu\boxtimes\nu}
is the Boolean-cumulant transform function mentioned
in Notation
5.1
.

6.
Overlap function for
x=a
and
y=a+b
, where
a
is free from
b

In this section, the selfadjoint elements
x,y\in\mathcal{A}
from the preceding section
take the form
x=a
and
y=a+b
, where
a,b\in\mathcal{A}

are selfadjoint and freely independent. We will denote the distributions of

a
and
b
by
\mu
and
\nu
, respectively; due to the freeness assumption
on
a
and
b
, the distribution of
a+b
is then the free additive
convolution
\mu\boxplus\nu
. We will show that, in this setting, the overlap
measure
\mu^{(\mathrm{ov})}_{a,a+b}
is sure to be absolutely continuous with respect to

\mu_{a}\times\mu_{a+b}
, and we will give an explicit formula for the
resulting overlap function, with the important benefit that this will enable
us to make explicit computations of conditional expectations of the
form
E(f(a)|a+b)
.

Remark 6.1
.

The subordination results reviewed in Subsection
5.1

allow us to write, for any bounded Borel functions
f,g

\displaystyle\varphi(f(a)g(a+b))

\displaystyle=\varphi\left(f(a)E(g(a+b)|a)\right)=\varphi(f(a)\mathcal{K}g(a))=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(s)\mathcal{K}g(s)d\mu(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(s)\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(t)dk_{s}(t)d\mu(s),

where the probability measures
k_{s}
are described by Equation
(
5.1
).
Hence for the overlap measure
\mu^{(\mathrm{ov})}_{a,a+b}
considered here, the first of the
two disintegrations considered in Remark and Notation
4.1
is simply
described as

k_{s}^{(1)}=k_{s},\ \ s\in\mathrm{Spec}(a).

Our goal in this section is that, by starting from the measures
k_{s}^{(1)}
, we find
the second disintegration
k_{t}^{(2)}
(with
t
running in
\mathrm{Spec}(a+b)
) for
\mu^{(\mathrm{ov})}_{a,a+b}
.
When working towards this goal we will rely on a number of known facts about
regularity of free additive convolution and subordination functions, which we
review next.

Review 6.2
.

[Regularity of free additive convolution.]
Let
\mu,\nu
be two Borel probability measures on
\mathbb{R}
, where neither of them
is a point mass. We consider the free additive convolution

\mu\boxplus\nu
, and let
\omega
be the subordination function of

\mu\boxplus\nu
with respect to
\mu
.

1^{o}

\mu\boxplus\nu
has at most finitely many atoms

\gamma_{i},\,i=1,\ldots,n
. For every
1\leq i\leq n

there exist
\alpha_{i},\beta_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
, uniquely determined, such that

\alpha_{i}+\beta_{i}=\gamma_{i}
and
\mu(\{\alpha_{i}\})+\nu(\{\beta_{i}\})>1

(see
[
11
]
).

2^{o}
With notation as in
1^{o}
we have
\lim_{z\to\gamma_{i}}\omega(z)=\alpha_{i}
,
for every
i=1,\ldots,n
(see
[
11
]
).

3^{o}

\mu\boxplus\nu
has no singular continuous part
(see
[
5
]
, Theorem 4.1).

4^{o}
Let
(\mu\boxplus\nu)^{ac}
denote the absolutely continuous part
(with respect to Lebesgue measure) of
\mu\boxplus\nu
, and let

f_{\mu\boxplus\nu}
denote the density of
(\mu\boxplus\nu)^{ac}
with
respect to Lebesgue measure. There exists an open set
U\subset\mathbb{R}
, with

(\mu\boxplus\nu)^{ac}(\mathbb{R}\setminus U)=0
, such that
f_{\mu\boxplus\nu}

is strictly positive on
U
(see
[
5
]
, Theorem 4.1).

5^{o}
Let
U
be as in
4^{o}
above. The subordination function
\omega

can be continued continuously to the real line, and the continuation
(still denoted
\omega
) has strictly positive imaginary part at every
point
x\in U
(see
[
5
]
, Theorem 4.1).

Proof.

Except
5^{o}
, all statements above are explicitly stated in references.
Point
5^{o}
requires a bit of explanation. Recall
(cf. final paragraph of Section 2.4) that we denote
\omega=\omega_{1}
, where

\omega_{1}
and
\omega_{2}
are the subordination functions of
\mu\boxplus\nu

with respect to
\mu
and to
\nu
, respectively.
As stated in the proof of Theorem 4.1 in
[
5
]
, at least one of the
subordination functions
\omega_{1},\omega_{2}
has strictly positive imaginary part
at
x\in U
. Suppose that the continuation of
\omega_{2}
has strictly positive
imaginary part at
x\in\mathbb{R}
. We will argue that so does the continuation
of
\omega_{1}=\omega
. One has
\omega_{1}(z)+\omega_{2}(z)=1/G_{\mu\boxplus\nu}(z)+z=1/G_{\mu}(\omega_{1}(z))+z=1/G_{\nu}(\omega_{2}(z))+z
. Then we have

\omega_{1}(x)=1/G_{\nu}(\omega_{2}(x))-\omega_{2}(x)+x.

Since for a measure
\nu
not being a point mass we have strict inequality

\operatorname{Im}(1/G_{\nu}(z))>\operatorname{Im}(z)
for

z=\omega_{2}(x)\in\mathbb{C}^{+}
, we see that
\omega_{1}(x)

also has strictly positive imaginary part.
∎

We now proceed with the plan announced above, of examining the measures
k_{s}
.

Proposition 6.3
.

Consider the setting indicated at the beginning of this section (with
x=a
and
y=a+b
,
where
a,b
are freely independent), and let
\mu,\nu
be the distributions of
a
and
b
,
respectively. We assume that neither
\mu
nor
\nu
is a point mass and we consider,
in connection to them, the various items of notation used in Review
6.2
above.
Let the function
o:\mathrm{Spec}(a)\times\mathrm{Spec}(a+b)\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
be defined
(in
\mu\times(\mu\boxplus\nu)
-almost everywhere sense) by the following formula:

(6.1)

\displaystyle\forall s\in\mathrm{supp}(\mu),o(s,t)=\begin{cases}-\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{1}{f_{\mu\boxplus\nu}(t)}\operatorname{Im}\left(\frac{1}{\omega(t)-s}\right)\qquad&\mbox{ if }t\in U,\\
\frac{1}{\mu(\{s\})}1_{\omega(t)=s}\qquad&\mbox{ if $t$ is an atom of $\mu\boxplus\nu$.}\end{cases}

Consider, on the other hand, the measure
k_{s}
from (
5.1
).
Then: for
\mu
-almost every
s\in\mathbb{R}
we have
k_{s}\ll\mu\boxplus\nu
and the
corresponding Radon-Nikodym derivative is given by the partial function
o(s,\cdot)

of the
o
from (
6.1
).

Proof.

As reviewed in Remark
6.2
, there exist a finite set
\Gamma

and an open set
U
, with

\Gamma\cap U=\emptyset
, such that: the support of the singular part of

\mu\boxplus\nu
is
\Gamma
, the density
f_{\mu\boxplus\nu}
of the absolutely
continuous part of
\mu\boxplus\nu
is positive on
U
and

\mu\boxplus\nu(\Gamma\cup U)=1
. These results are obtained by showing that
the subordination map
\omega
of
\mu\boxplus\nu
with respect to
\mu
has
a continuous extension

\omega:\mathbb{C}^{+}\cup\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}^{+}\cup\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
,
and that
\omega(\Gamma)
is a set of atoms of
\mu
, while

\omega(U)\subset\mathbb{C}^{+}
.

Upon invoking Remark
4.4
in connection to the set

V=\Gamma\cup U

one gets that
k_{s}(\Gamma\cup U)=1
for
\mu
-almost all
s\in\mathbb{R}
.
For the rest of the proof, let us fix an
s
which has

k_{s}(\Gamma\cup U)=1
.

Since the Cauchy transform of
k_{s}
takes finite
values at all points of
U
, it follows from Lemma
5.2
that:

\bullet
the singular part of
k_{s}
is purely atomic and supported on
\Gamma
;

\bullet
a density of the absolutely continuous part of
k_{s}
is

-\pi^{-1}\operatorname{Im}[(\omega-s)^{-1}]\mathbf{1}_{U}
.

Since the singular part of
\mu\boxplus\nu
is purely atomic with support
\Gamma

and since the density
f_{\mu\boxplus\nu}
of the absolutely continuous part of

\mu\boxplus\nu
is positive on
U
, the above bulleted items allow
us to conclude that
k_{s}
is absolutely continuous with respect to
\mu\boxplus\nu
.

In order to prove that the Radon-Nikodym derivative is indeed
given by (
6.1
), it remains to verify that

(6.2)

k_{s}(\{\gamma\})=\frac{\mu\boxplus\nu(\{\gamma\})}{\mu(\{s\})}1_{s=\omega(\gamma)},\quad\gamma\in\Gamma.

Towards this verification, we consider two cases.

– When
s\neq\omega(\gamma)
, (
6.2
) follows from

k_{s}(\{\gamma\})=\sphericalangle\lim_{z\to\gamma}(z-\gamma)G_{k_{s}}(z)=\sphericalangle\lim_{z\to\gamma}\frac{z-\gamma}{\omega(z)-s}=0.

– When
s=\omega(\gamma)
: since
\omega(z)
tends nontangentially to
\omega(\gamma)=s

as
z
tends nontangentially to
\gamma

as shown in
[
11
]
),
(
6.2
) follows from taking the
nontangential limit as
z
tends to
\gamma
in

(z-\gamma)G_{k_{s}}(z)=\frac{z-\gamma}{\omega(z)-s}=\frac{(z-\gamma)G_{\mu\boxplus\nu}(z)}{(\omega(z)-s)G_{\mu}(\omega(z))}.

∎

Theorem 6.4
.

Consider the framework and notation of Proposition
6.3
, and let
\mu^{(\mathrm{ov})}_{a,a+b}
be
the overlap measure for the elements
a
and
a+b
. One has that

(6.3)

d\mu^{(\mathrm{ov})}_{a,a+b}(s,t)=o(s,t)\,\mu(ds)\ \mu\boxplus\nu(dt),

where the function
o:\mathrm{Spec}(a)\times\mathrm{Spec}(a+b)\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is defined by
Equation (
6.1
). In particular, this implies that

\mu^{(\mathrm{ov})}_{a,a+b}\ll\mu\times(\mu\boxplus\nu)
, and that the corresponding overlap function

{\mathrm{o}}_{a,a+b}
is the function
o
from (
6.1
).

Proof.

Let
\nu
be the measure on
\mathrm{Spec}(a)\times\mathrm{Spec}(a+b)

which is defined by the right-hand side of (
6.3
),

d\nu(s,t)=o(s,t)\,\mu(ds)\ \mu\boxplus\nu(dt).

We will verify that
\nu
fulfills the requirement used in order to define
the overlap measure
\mu^{(\mathrm{ov})}_{a,a+b}
.

We know from Proposition
6.3
that, for any given

g\in\mathrm{Bor}_{b}(\mathbb{R})
we have the equality

\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(t)\,o(s,t)\,d(\mu\boxplus\nu)(t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(t)dk_{s}(t),

holding for
\mu
-almost every
s\in\mathbb{R}
.
By using Fubini’s Theorem and Remark
6.1
, we then see that for every

f,g\in\mathrm{Bor}_{b}(\mathbb{R})
we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f(s)\,g(t)\,d\nu(s,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f(s)\,g(t)\,o(s,t)\,d\mu(s)\,d(\mu\boxplus\nu)(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(s)\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(t)\,o(s,t)\,d\mu\boxplus\nu(t)\right]d\mu(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(s)\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(t)\,dk_{s}(t)\right]\,d\mu(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varphi(f(a)g(a+b)),

as required.
∎

Corollary 6.5
.

Consider the framework and notation from Proposition
6.3
and
Theorem
6.4
. For every bounded Borel function
f:\mathrm{Spec}(a)\to\mathbb{R}
,
one has

\displaystyle E[f(a)\mid a+b]=h(a+b),

where
h:\mathrm{Spec}(a+b)\to\mathbb{C}
is defined
\mu\boxplus\nu
-almost everywhere by

(6.4)

\displaystyle h(t)=\begin{cases}-\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{1}{f_{\mu\boxplus\nu}(t)}\operatorname{Im}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(s)\frac{1}{\omega(t)-s}d\mu(s)\right)&\mbox{ if }t\in U,\\
f(\omega(t))&\mbox{ if }t\mbox{ is an atom of }\mu\boxplus\nu.\end{cases}

Proof.

From Theorem
6.4
and Fubini’s theorem one gets that

\displaystyle\varphi(f(a)g(a+b))

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f(s)g(t){\mathrm{o}}_{a,a+b}(s,t)\mu(ds)\mu\boxplus\nu(dt)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(s){\mathrm{o}}_{a,a+b}(s,t)\mu(ds)\right)g(t)\mu\boxplus\nu(dt).

Denoting
h(t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(s){\mathrm{o}}_{a,a+b}(s,t)\mu(ds)
we see that

\varphi(f(a)g(a+b))=\varphi(h(a+b)g(a+b)).

Explicit formula for the function
h
follows directly from the formula (
6.1
).
One should observe that
h
is only defined
(\mu\boxplus\nu)
-almost everywhere,
since
U
may be a proper relatively open subset of
\mathrm{Spec}(a+b)
.
However, this is sufficient for the correct definition of conditional expectation.
∎

Since the atomic case considered in (
6.4
) is immediate to apply,
we investigate further how the formula (
6.4
) works in the other case,
t\in U
.
In the next proposition we observe that for
f(s)=s
one can process the first branch of
formula (
6.4
) a bit further, and evaluate the integral included in it.

Proposition 6.6
.

In the particular case where
f(s)=s
,
s\in\mathbb{R}
, the function
h

defined by (
6.4
) satisfies

(6.5)

\displaystyle h(t)=-\frac{\operatorname{Im}\left(\omega(t)G_{\mu\boxplus\nu}(t)\right)}{\pi f_{\mu\boxplus\nu}(t)},\ \ \forall\,t\in U.

Proof.

Direct calculation gives

\displaystyle h(t)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}s\frac{1}{f_{\mu\boxplus\nu}(t)}\operatorname{Im}\left(\frac{1}{\omega(t)-s}\right)d\mu(s)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\pi f_{\mu\boxplus\nu}(t)}\operatorname{Im}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\frac{\omega(t)}{\omega(t)-s}-1\right)d\mu(s)\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\operatorname{Im}\left(\omega(t)G_{\mu}(\omega(t))\right)}{\pi f_{\mu\boxplus\nu}(t)}=-\frac{\operatorname{Im}\left(\omega(t)G_{\mu\boxplus\nu}(t)\right)}{\pi f_{\mu\boxplus\nu}(t)},\mbox{ as stated.}

∎

Formula (
6.5
) does not seem to further simplify in general, but it allows
for some more processing in the case when (the distribution
\mu
of

but) the distribution

of the element

\boxplus
-infinitely

-transform

has an analytic

Moreover, if we are in the special situation when

\sigma^{2}
(meaning that

is the centred semicircle law of variance

then the aforementioned

, and we find the
nice formula which was announced in Section 1.2 of the Introduction under the name of
“free additive Tweedie’s formula”. The details of this are given in part
1^{o}
of the
next example. Part

Example 6.7
.

1^{o}
Suppose that
b
is a centred semicircular element with variance
\sigma^{2}
.
Then
\mu\boxplus\nu
is absolutely continuous with respect to Lebesgue measure
and (as first observed in
[
12
]
) the subordination function
\omega

satisfies the equation

\omega(z)=z-\sigma^{2}G_{\mu\boxplus\nu}(z),\ \ z\in\mathbb{C}^{+},

which extends by continuity to the case when
t\in U
.
The numerator of the fraction on the right-hand side of (
6.5
) thus takes
the form

\displaystyle\mathrm{Im}\bigl(\,(t-\sigma^{2}\,G_{\mu\boxplus\nu}(t))\,G_{\mu\boxplus\nu}(t)\,\bigr)

\displaystyle=t\,\mathrm{Im}G_{\mu\boxplus\nu}(t))-2\sigma^{2}\,\mathrm{Re}G_{\mu\boxplus\nu}(t)\cdot\mathrm{Im}G_{\mu\boxplus\nu}(t)

\displaystyle=-\pi tf_{\mu\boxplus\nu}(t)-2\sigma^{2}\cdot\pi\,H_{\mu\boxplus\nu}(t)\cdot\bigl(-\pi\,f_{\mu\boxplus\nu}(t)\bigr),

where
H_{\mu\boxplus\nu}
is the Hilbert transform of
\mu\boxplus\nu
,
and where at the latter equality sign we took advantage of the fact that the
continuous extension of
G_{\mu\boxplus\nu}
at a point

can be written
in the form

G_{\mu\boxplus\nu}(t)=\pi\bigl(\,H_{\mu\boxplus\nu}(t)-if_{\mu\boxplus\nu}(t)\,\bigr).

Upon plugging the above calculation back into Equation (
6.5
) we find that

(6.6)

h(t)=-\frac{-\pi tf_{\mu\boxplus\nu}(t)+2\pi^{2}\sigma^{2}f_{\mu\boxplus\nu}(t)H_{\mu\boxplus\nu}(t)}{\pi f_{\mu\boxplus\nu}(t)}=t-2\pi\sigma^{2}H_{\mu\boxplus\nu}(t),

and this leads to the free additive analogue of Tweedie’s formula that was announced
in Equation (
1.4
) of the Introduction,

\displaystyle E(a\,|\,a+b)=a+b-2\pi\sigma^{2}H_{\mu\boxplus\nu}(a+b).

As already noted in the Introduction, it is remarkable that, in this important special
case, the formula for
h
uses solely the distribution of
a+b
,
and does not invoke the (unknown) distribution of the element
a
.

For the record, we mention that in this special case the corresponding
overlap function
{\mathrm{o}}_{a,a+b}
is given by the formula

{\mathrm{o}}_{a,a+b}(s,t)=\frac{1}{(t-s-\pi\sigma^{2}H_{\mu\boxplus\nu}(t))^{2}+\pi^{2}f_{\mu\boxplus\nu}^{2}(t)},\ \mbox{ for $s\in(-2,2)$ and $t\in U$.}

The calculation leading to this formula is similar to the one showed for
h
in
(
6.6
), and is left as exercise to the reader.

2^{o}
If the distributions of

are

Review 7.1

(Review of regularity of free multiplicative convolution on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
)
.

Let
\mu,\nu
be two Borel probability measures on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
neither of them a point mass;
denote by
\mu\boxtimes\nu
the free multiplicative convolution).

1^{o}
The measure
\mu\boxtimes\nu
has at most finitely many atoms

\gamma_{i},\,i=1,\ldots,n
. For every
\gamma_{i}>0
,
i=1,\ldots,n

there exist uniquely determined
\alpha_{i},\beta_{i}\in(0,\infty)
such that

\alpha_{i}\beta_{i}=\gamma_{i}

and
\mu(\{\alpha_{i}\})+\nu(\{\beta_{i}\})>1
.
Moreover,
\mu\boxtimes\nu(\{0\})=\max\{\mu(\{0\}),\nu(\{0\})\}

(see
[
2
]
).

2^{o}
With notation as in
1^{o}
we have
\lim_{z\to 1/\gamma_{i}}\omega(z)=1/\alpha_{i}
,
for every
i=1,\ldots,n
(see
[
2
]
).

3^{o}
Measure
\mu\boxtimes\nu
has no singular continuous part
(see
[
20
]
).

4^{o}
There exists a closed set
0\in E\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}
of Lebesgue measure
zero such that the density of
(\mu\boxtimes\nu)^{ac}
, denoted by

f_{\mu\boxtimes\nu}
, is analytic on

\mathbb{R}_{+}\setminus E
(see
[
3
,
4
]
).

5^{o}
With notation from
4^{o}
the subordination function
\omega
can be
continued continuously to the real line, and the continuation, still denoted
by
\omega
, has strictly positive imaginary part at every point
x\in\mathbb{R}

such that
1/x\in\mathrm{supp}(\mu\boxtimes\nu)^{ac}\setminus E

(see
[
3
,
4
]
).

Proof.

Similarly as before, only
5^{o}
requires an explanation. We know that
\omega_{1}(x)\omega_{2}(x)=x\eta_{\mu\boxtimes\nu}(x)
, hence we have

\frac{\omega_{1}(x)}{x}=\frac{\eta_{\nu}(\omega_{2}(x))}{\omega_{2}(x)}
, if we know that
\omega_{2}(x)
has positive imaginary part, then
\frac{\eta_{\nu}(\omega_{2}(x))}{\omega_{2}(x)}
has positive imaginary part, as
\eta_{\mu}
strictly increases argument of
\omega_{2}(x)
(see
[
8
]
).
Thus
\omega_{1}(x)
has strictly positive imaginary part.
∎

In the multiplicative case the behaviour of subordination functions at zero requires
a bit more attention. It is explained in detail in
[

]
, let us discuss

Lemma 7.2
.

In the framework and notation of Review
7.1
we have

\displaystyle\lim_{z\to-\infty}\omega(z)=\begin{cases}-\infty\qquad&\mbox{ if }\mu(\{0\})\geq\nu(\{0\}),\\
\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(\nu(\{0\})-1)&\mbox{ if }\mu(\{0\})<\nu(\{0\}).\end{cases}

Proof.

For any probability measure
\mu\neq\delta_{0}
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
the function
\psi_{\mu}:(-\infty,0)\mapsto(\mu(\{0\})-1,0)
is a homeomorphism. Therefore
\psi_{\mu}
has a continuous inverse
\psi_{\mu}^{-1}:(\mu(\{0\})-1,0)\mapsto(-\infty,0)
. We observe that for
z\in(-\infty,0)
function
\omega(z)=\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(\psi_{\mu\boxtimes\nu}(z))
is well defined.

From Lebesgue dominated convergence it follows that

\lim_{z\to-\infty}\psi_{\mu\boxtimes\nu}(z)=\mu\boxtimes\nu(\{0\})-1=\max\{\mu(\{0\}),\nu(\{0\})\}-1.

If
\mu\boxtimes\nu(\{0\})=\mu(\{0\})
then
\lim_{z\to-\infty}\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(\psi_{\mu\boxtimes\nu}(z))=-\infty
. In the case
\mu\boxtimes\nu(\{0\})=\nu(\{0\})>\mu(\{0\})
we have
\lim_{z\to-\infty}\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(\psi_{\mu\boxtimes\nu}(z))=\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(\nu(\{0\})-1)
, in this case of course
\nu(\{0\})-1\in(\mu(\{0\})-1,0)
, hence
\psi_{\mu}^{-1}(\nu(\{0\})-1)
is well defined.
∎

The next proposition states the precise formula that one has for the overlap function in this case.
The proposition does not refer to some freely independent positive elements
a,b
in an ncps – but
if such
a,b
were in the picture, then the function
o
defined in (

.

Proposition 7.3
.

With notation from Review
7.1
above, consider the function

defined (in

&\mbox{ and }t\in\mathrm{supp}(\mu\boxtimes\nu)^{ac}\setminus E,\\

Consider the measure

Then: for

, with the set

.

After writing

Proposition 7.6
.

With the notation of Theorem

8.
Relation to conditional freeness

In this section, we relate to conditionally free probability theory the
general question of determining the conditional expectation
E(x\mid y)

in the case where:
y=P(a,b)
is a suitable selfadjoint element in the von Neumann
algebra generated by two freely independent non-commutative variables
a,b

in some ncps
(\mathcal{A},\varphi)
,
and
x=f(a)
is a selfadjoint element in the von Neumann algebra generated
by
a
. (For concrete examples,
P(a,b)
could be a selfadjoint polynomial
in
a
and
b
; or, in a situation where
a
is known to be positive, it
could be that
P(a,b)=a^{1/2}ba^{1/2}
.)

Without much loss of generality, the considerations about
E(f(a)\mid P(a,b))

which are made throughout this section will also take in the additional assumptions
that the function
f
(which is a real-valued bounded Borel function supported
on the spectrum of

) is non-negative and is normalized by the condition that

\varphi\bigl(\,f(a)\,\bigr)=1
.

8.1.
Free denoiser as Radon-Nikodym derivative

Remark and Notation 8.1
.

In the framework that was just described, the conditional
expectation
E(f(a)\mid P(a,b))
is a positive element in the von Neumann
algebra generated by
P(a,b)
, and can therefore be
written as
h\bigl(\,P(a,b)\,\bigr)
, where
h
is a bounded Borel
supported on the spectrum of
P(a,b)
and with values in
[0,\infty)
.
We will refer to
h
by calling it
free denoiser
(of
f(a)
with
respect to
P(a,b)
). Our point in the present remark is to observe that the
free denoiser
h
can be written as a Radon-Nikodym derivative, in connection
to two distributions which appear naturally in the discussion. One of the
distributions is just the distribution of
P(a,b)
with respect to the state

\varphi
of our ncps
(\mathcal{A},\varphi)
; we will denote this distribution
as
\mu_{P(a,b)}^{\varphi}
. For the second distribution, we consider the
new state
\chi:\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{C}
defined by putting

\chi(c):=\varphi(\,f(a)c\,\,)\ \ c\in\mathcal{A},

and we consider the distribution of
P(a,b)
in the ncps
(\mathcal{A},\chi)
;
the latter distribution will be denoted as
\mu_{P(a,b)}^{\chi}
. The claim
we make is that:

\frac{d\mu_{P(a,b)}^{\chi}}{d\mu_{P(a,b)}^{\varphi}}=h\ \ \mbox{ (free denoiser),}

i.e. that for any real valued bounded Borel function

g
supported on the spectrum of
P(a,b)
we have

(8.1)

\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(t)\mu_{P(a,b)}^{\chi}(dt)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(t)h(t)\mu_{P(a,b)}^{\varphi}(dt).

For the verification of the claim, we start from the left-hand side of
(
8.1
) and we write:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(t)\mu_{P(a,b)}^{\chi}(dt)

\displaystyle=\chi\bigl(\,g(\,P(a,b)\,\bigr)\mbox{ (by the definition of $\mu_{P(a,b)}^{\chi}$) }

\displaystyle=\varphi\bigl(\,f(a)\,g(\,P(a,b)\,\bigr)\mbox{ (by the definition of $\chi$) }

\displaystyle=\varphi\Bigl(\,E\bigl(f(a)\,g(P(a,b))\mid P(a,b)\bigr)\,\Bigr)\mbox{ (since $E(\cdot\mid P(a,b)$) is $\varphi$-invariant) }

\displaystyle=\varphi\Bigl(\,E\bigl(f(a)\mid P(a,b)\bigr)\cdot g(P(a,b))\,\Bigr)\mbox{ (conditional expectation property) }

\displaystyle=\varphi\bigl(\,h(P(a,b))\cdot g(P(a,b))\,\bigr)\mbox{ (by the definition of $h$) }

\displaystyle=\varphi\bigl(\,(g\cdot h)(P(a,b))\,\bigr)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(t)h(t)\mu_{P(a,b)}^{\varphi}(dt)\mbox{ (by the definition of $\mu_{P(a,b)}^{\varphi}$), }

thus arriving to the right-hand side of (
8.1
).

We next move to connecting the two distributions

\mu_{P(a,b)}^{\varphi},\mu_{P(a,b)}^{\chi}
to conditionally free probability
theory. Towards that end, we will first review (following

[
15
]
) some basic definitions and facts of this theory.

8.2.
Review of conditionally free probability theory

Definition 8.2
.

1^{o}
A
two-state non-commutative probability space
is a
triple
(\mathcal{A},\varphi,\chi)

where
(\mathcal{A},\varphi)
is a ncps and

\chi:\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{C}
is another normal state (unital,
positive, weakly continuous linear functional) on
\mathcal{A}
.

2^{o}
Let
(\mathcal{A},\varphi,\chi)
be a two-state
non-commutative probability space and let
a
be a selfadjoint element of
\mathcal{A}
.
Then besides the distribution
\mu
of
a
with respect to
\varphi
,
one can also consider the distribution
\nu
of
a
in the ncps
(\mathcal{A},\chi)
.
The couple
(\mu,\nu)
of compactly supported probability measures on
\mathbb{R}

that is obtained in this way will be referred to as

distribution of
a
in
(\mathcal{A},\varphi,\chi)
.

Definition 8.3
.

Unital subalgebras
(\mathcal{A}_{i})_{i\in I}
of a unital complex algebra
\mathcal{A}
are said to be conditionally freely independent in the two-state non-commutative probability space
(\mathcal{A},\varphi,\chi)
when they are freely independent in
(\mathcal{A},\varphi)
and in addition the following condition holds: for all
n\geq 2
,
i_{1}\neq i_{2}\neq\ldots\neq i_{n}
in
I
and
a_{1}\in\mathcal{A}_{i_{1}},\ldots,a_{n}\in\mathcal{A}_{i_{n}}
such that
\varphi(a_{1})=\ldots=\varphi(a_{n})=0
,

\chi(a_{1}\cdots a_{n})=\chi(a_{1})\cdots\chi(a_{n}).

Accordingly, non-commutative random variables will be said to be conditionally freely independent in the two-state non-commutative probability space
(\mathcal{A},\varphi,\chi)
when the von Neumann subalgebras they generate in
\mathcal{A}
are so.

8.3.
Upgrading freeness to conditional freeness

As originally observed in
[
15
]
, freely independent
unital subalgebras
(\mathcal{A}_{i})_{i\in I}
of a non-commutative probability
space
(\mathcal{A},\varphi)
are conditionally freely independent in the two-state
non-commutative probability space given by
(\mathcal{A},\varphi,\varphi)
.
We will use the following slightly more general result.

Proposition 8.4
.

Let
(\mathcal{A},\varphi)
be a non-commutative probability space and
(\mathcal{A}_{i})_{i\in I}
be freely independent unital subalgebras in
(\mathcal{A},\varphi)
. Fix
i_{0}\in I
and a positive element

x\in\mathcal{A}_{i_{0}}
such that
\varphi(x)=1
. Define another state
\chi:\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{C}
by

\chi(y):=\varphi(xy),\qquad\forall y\in\mathcal{A}.

Then the subalgebras
(\mathcal{A}_{i})_{i\in I}
are conditionally freely independent in
(\mathcal{A},\varphi,\chi)
.

Proof.

Let
n\geq 2
,
i_{1}\neq i_{2}\neq\ldots\neq i_{n}
in
I
and

a_{1}\in\mathcal{A}_{i_{1}},\ldots,a_{n}\in\mathcal{A}_{i_{n}}
be
such that
\varphi(a_{1})=\ldots=\varphi(a_{n})=0
. We want to show that

(8.2)

\chi(a_{1}\cdots a_{n})=\chi(a_{1})\cdots\chi(a_{n}).

Observe that there always exists
k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
such that
i_{k}\neq i_{0}
. Then, by free independence of
\mathcal{A}_{i_{0}}
and
\mathcal{A}_{i_{k}}
,
\chi(a_{k})=\varphi(xa_{k})=\varphi(x)\varphi(a_{k})=0
so that the right-hand side of (
8.2
) vanishes.

To prove that the left-hand side of (
8.2
) vanishes, we distinguish two cases:
i_{0}\neq i_{1}
or
i_{0}=i_{1}
. On the one hand, if
i_{0}\neq i_{1}
, then

\displaystyle\chi(a_{1}\cdots a_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varphi(xa_{1}\cdots a_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varphi((x-1)a_{1}\cdots a_{n})+\varphi(a_{1}\cdots a_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

where we used twice in the last line free independence of
(\mathcal{A}_{i})_{i\in I}
.
On the other hand, if
i_{0}=i_{1}
, then

\displaystyle\chi(a_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varphi(xa_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varphi((xa_{1}-\varphi(xa_{1}))a_{2}\cdots a_{n})+\varphi(xa_{1})\varphi(a_{2}\cdots a_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

where we again used twice in the last line free independence
of
(\mathcal{A}_{i})_{i\in I}
. In any case, we have proved that

\chi(a_{1}\cdots a_{n})=0=\chi(a_{1})\cdots\chi(a_{n}),

as required.∎

8.4.
Conditional expectations and conditional freeness

Definition 8.5
.

Let
a,b
be conditionally freely independent selfadjoint non-commutative random variables with respective distributions
(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})
and
(\nu_{1},\nu_{2})
in some two-state non-commutative probability space
(\mathcal{A},\varphi,\chi)
and

be a selfadjoint polynomial in two non-commuting variables. We will denote by
(\mu_{1}\Box^{P}\nu_{1},(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})\Box_{c}^{P}(\nu_{1},\nu_{2}))
the distribution of the non-commutative random variable

in
(\mathcal{A},\varphi,\chi)
.

Lemma 8.6
.

Let
a,b
be freely independent selfadjoint non-commutative random variables with respective distributions
\mu,\nu
in a ncps
(\mathcal{A},\varphi)
,
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a bounded positive Borel-measurable function such that
\varphi(f(a))=1
and
P
be a selfadjoint polynomial in two non-commuting variables. Define another state
\chi:\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{C}
by

\chi(y):=\varphi(f(a)y),\qquad\forall y\in\mathcal{A}.

Then the distribution of the non-commutative random variable
P(a,b)
in the two-state non-commutative probability space
(\mathcal{A},\varphi,\chi)
is
(\mu\Box^{P}\nu,(\mu,f\cdot\mu)\Box_{c}^{P}(\nu,\nu))
.

Proof.

By assumption, the respective distributions of
a
and
b
with respect to
\varphi
are
\mu
and
\nu
. We check then that the respective distributions of
a
and
b
with respect to
\chi
are
f\cdot\mu
and
\nu
: for any bounded Borel-measurable function
g:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
,

\chi(g(a))=\varphi(f(a)g(a))=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(s)g(s)\mu(ds)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(s)(f\cdot\mu)(ds);

\chi(g(b))=\varphi(f(a)g(b))=\varphi(f(a))\varphi(g(b))=\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(s)\nu(ds),

where we have used that
a
and
b
are freely independent and
\varphi(f(a))=1
. Now observe that
a
and
b
are conditionally freely independent in
(\mathcal{A},\varphi,\chi)
by a direct application of Proposition 6.3 (with
x=f(a)
) to the von Neumann subalgebras generated by
a
and
b
, and the conclusion follows.
∎

Lemma 8.7
.

Let
\mu,\nu
be two compactly supported Borel probability
measures on
\mathbb{R}
,
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a bounded positive Borel-measurable function such that
\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)\mu(dx)=1
and
P
be a polynomial in two non-commuting variables. Then
(\mu,f\cdot\mu)\Box_{c}^{P}(\nu,\nu)
is absolutely continuous with respect to
\mu\Box^{P}\nu
.

Proof.

Let
E
be a Borel subset of
\mathbb{R}
such that
(\mu\Box^{P}\nu)(E)=0
. By the preceding Lemma,

\displaystyle\left((\mu,f\cdot\mu)\Box_{c}^{P}(\nu,\nu)\right)(E)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\chi(\mathbf{1}_{E}(P(a,b)))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varphi(f(a)\mathbf{1}_{E}(P(a,b)))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{\varphi(f(a)^{2})}\sqrt{\varphi(\mathbf{1}_{E}(P(a,b)))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\infty}\sqrt{(\mu\Box^{P}\nu)(E)}=0.

This proves absolute continuity of
(\mu,f\cdot\mu)\Box_{c}^{P}(\nu,\nu)
with respect to
\mu\Box^{P}\nu
.
∎

The main result of this section is the following.

Theorem 8.8
.

Let
a,b
be freely independent selfadjoint non-commutative random variables with respective
distributions
\mu,\nu
in a ncps
(\mathcal{A},\varphi)
. Let
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}

\varphi(f(a))=1
, and let

E(f(a)\mid P(a,b))=\frac{d\left((\mu,f\cdot\mu)\Box_{c}^{P}(\nu,\nu)\right)}{d\left(\mu\Box^{P}\nu\right)}(P(a,b)).

Proof.

As explained in Remark

, the free denoiser,
that is the bounded Borel-measurable function

such that

Then, according to Lemma

(\mu_{P(a,b)}^{\varphi},\mu_{P(a,b)}^{\chi})=(\mu\Box^{P}\nu,(\mu,f\cdot\mu)\Box_{c}^{P}(\nu,\nu))
, which concludes the proof.
∎

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\operatorname{Im}\Bigl(G_{\mu}(\omega(t+i\varepsilon))\,\left(\omega(t+i\varepsilon)-\alpha\right)\Bigr)=\mu(\{\alpha\}),

while
{\operatorname{Im}\Bigl(G_{\mu\boxplus\nu}(t+i\varepsilon)\Bigr)}

diverges; this implies, altogether, that
h(t)=\alpha=\omega(t)
.

Similar considerations to those shown above apply to the multiplicative case.
When we are interested in
E(a|a^{1/2}ba{1/2})
(with
\chi
defined as above) we have

1+\psi^{\chi}_{a^{1/2}ba^{1/2}}(z)=\varphi(a(1-za^{1/2}ba^{1/2})^{-1})=\varphi(a(1-\omega(z)a)^{-1})=\frac{\psi^{\varphi}_{a^{1/2}ba^{1/2}}(z)}{\omega(z)}.

Hence,

G^{\chi}_{a^{1/2}ba^{1/2}}(z)=\frac{G_{a^{1/2}ba^{1/2}}^{\varphi}(z)-\frac{1}{z}}{\omega(\frac{1}{z})}.

Thus for
\mu\boxtimes\nu
a.e.
t
, we define the free denoiser in the multiplicative case as

h(t)=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\operatorname{Im}\left(\frac{G_{a^{1/2}ba^{1/2}}^{\varphi}(t+i\varepsilon)-\frac{1}{t+i\varepsilon}}{\omega(\frac{1}{t+i\varepsilon})}\right)}{\operatorname{Im}\left(G_{a^{1/2}ba^{1/2}}^{\varphi}(t+i\varepsilon)\right)}.

A simple calculation, similar to the additive case shows that this expression
agrees with the one given in (
7.2
). One point to explain is that the limit

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\omega\left(\frac{1}{i\varepsilon}\right)
agrees with the
corresponding limit at
-\infty
found in Lemma
7.2
; we refer to

[
20
]
, where this was shown.

9.
Relation to matrix denoising

As mentioned in the Introduction, the explicit formulas of free denoisers
noticed in (
1.4
) and (
1.12
) of this paper
had previously appeared in
[
16
]
,
[
21
]
,
derived via random matrix techniques. We will now see that this is not
coincidental, as the matrix denoising problem addressed in the aforementioned
references relates to free denoising via the phenomenon of asymptotic freeness.

9.1.
Weak continuity of overlap measure.

In this subsection we record, in the next Proposition
9.1
and
its Corollary
9.3
, a general
observation about the behaviour of overlap measures under convergence in
moments for the pairs of selfadjoint elements that are considered.

Proposition 9.1
.

Suppose we are given the following data.

\to

(\mathcal{A},\varphi)
is a
W^{*}
-probability space as considered in
Section
4
and
(x,y)
is a pair

of elements in
\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{sa}}
, with overlap measure
\mu^{(\mathrm{ov})}_{x,y}
.

\to
For every
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
(\mathcal{A}_{N},\varphi_{N})
is a Borel-ncps, as considered
in Section
2
, and
(x_{N},y_{N})

is a pair of elements in
\mathcal{A}_{N}^{\mathrm{sa}}
,
with overlap measure
\mu_{(x_{N},y_{N})}^{\mathrm{ov}}
.

Suppose, moreover, that

(9.1)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\varphi_{N}\bigl(x_{N}^{p}\,y_{N}^{q}\bigr)=\varphi(x^{p}\,y^{q}),\ \ \forall\,p,q\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}.

Then it follows that

(9.2)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f(s)\,g(t)\,d\mu_{(x_{N},y_{N})}^{\mathrm{ov}}(s,t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f(s)\,g(t)\,d\mu^{(\mathrm{ov})}_{x,y}(s,t),\ \ \forall\,f,g\in C_{\mathrm{pol}}(\mathbb{R}),

where
C_{\mathrm{pol}}(\mathbb{R})=\{f\in\mathrm{Bor}_{\mathrm{pol}}(\mathbb{R})\mid f\mbox{ is continuous}\}

(the space of continuous functions with polynomial growth from
\mathbb{R}
to
\mathbb{C}
).

\square

Remark 9.2
.

1^{o}
The proof of Proposition
9.1
is an application of the
well-known “method of moments”, where we start with Equation (
9.1
)
written in the form

\lim_{N\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}s^{p}\,t^{q}\,d\mu_{(x_{N},y_{N})}^{\mathrm{ov}}(s,t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}s^{p}\,t^{q}\,d\mu^{(\mathrm{ov})}_{x,y}(s,t),\ \ \forall\,p,q\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\},

and where an intermediate step of the proof is to infer (by using a
2-dimensional version of Helly’s selection theorem) that the measures

\mu_{(x_{N},y_{N})}^{\mathrm{(ov)}}
converge weakly to
\mu^{(\mathrm{ov})}_{x,y}
. For the said
intermediate step it is relevant to note that the measure
\mu^{(\mathrm{ov})}_{x,y}
, having
compact support, is uniquely determined by its moments. The details of this
proof are left to the reader.

2^{o}
In Proposition
9.1
we assumed that
x,y
live in a

W^{*}
-probability space, while on the
x_{N},y_{N}
we only made the looser
assumption that they live in a Borel-ncps (which could be, in particular, a
space of
N\times N
random matrices). As reviewed in Section
4
,
the
W^{*}
-framework allows us to consider the non-commutative conditional
expectation
E(x\,|\,y)
. While the analogous non-commutative conditional
expectations may not be guaranteed for the pairs
(x_{N},y_{N})
, one can nevertheless
observe the following immediate consequence of Proposition
9.1
, which
only refers to
E(x\,|\,y)
.

Corollary 9.3
.

In the framework and notation of Proposition
9.1
, one has

(9.3)

\lim_{N\to\infty}||\,x_{N}-g(y_{N})\,||_{2,\varphi_{N}}=||\,x-g(y)||_{2,\varphi}\geq||\,x-E(x\,|\,y)\,||_{2,\varphi},\ \ \forall\,g\in C_{\mathrm{pol}}(\mathbb{R}),

where
||\cdot||_{2,\varphi}
and
||\cdot||_{2,\varphi_{N}}

are the
L^{2}
-norms associated to the spaces
(\mathcal{A},\varphi)
and

(\mathcal{A}_{N},\varphi_{N})
, respectively.

Equation (
9.3
) has the following meaning: suppose that the denoiser
of
x
with respect to
y
is given by a continuous function

h\in C_{\mathrm{pol}}(\mathbb{R})
(that is,
h
is such that

E(x\,|\,y)=h(y)
). Then the same
h
must act as an asymptotic
denoiser for
x_{N}
with respect to
y_{N}
, at least in the weak sense that no

g\in C_{\mathrm{pol}}(\mathbb{R})
can systematically outperform
h

in the large
N
limit.

9.2.
Additive matrix denoising.

A rich source of asymptotically free pairs of sequences of selfadjoint non-commutative
random variables is provided by random matrices. In this context, matrix denoising is
a general problem arising in high-dimensional statistics: an unknown signal represented
by a Hermitian
N\times N
random matrix
A_{N}
is corrupted by a noise which is itself
a Hermitian
N\times N
random matrix
B_{N}
and one would like to
“recover the signal”
A_{N}
as a function of the noisy observation
A_{N}+B_{N}
.

In the lineage of
[
21
]
, Allez, Bouchaud, Bun and Potters addressed
in
[
16
]
a general problem of additive/multiplicative matrix
denoising: given two independent symmetric (respectively semi-positive definite)

N\times N
random matrices
A_{N},B_{N}
, where the distribution of the noise
B_{N}
is
invariant under the action by conjugation of the orthogonal group, the problem is
to find the optimal (in the
L^{2}
sense) Rotationally Invariant Estimator (RIE for
short) of the signal
A_{N}
, based on the observation of
C_{N}=A_{N}+B_{N}
(or

C_{N}=A_{N}^{1/2}B_{N}A_{N}^{1/2}
, respectively). The optimal RIE is a bounded measurable
function
\delta_{N}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
which minimizes

\mathbb{E}[N^{-1}\text{Tr}\left((A_{N}-\delta_{N}(C_{N}))^{2}\right)]
.
The paper
[
16
]
provides arguments supporting the claim that
the sequence
(\delta_{N})_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to a function

\delta:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
which may be expressed by using tools
from free probability theory.

When particularized to a
N\times N
GOE-distributed additive noise
B_{N}

with variance parameter
\sigma^{2}N^{-1}
, the sequence
(\delta_{N})_{N\in\mathbb{N}}

of optimal RIE of
A_{N}
in the general additive matrix denoising problem converges
to the function
\delta:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
defined by:

\delta(t)=t-2\sigma^{2}\pi H(t),

where
H
is the Hilbert transform of the limiting empirical spectral
distribution of
A_{N}+B_{N}
.

This result may be interpreted in terms of free denoising. Moreover, using
this interpretation, we prove the following proposition, stating that no

g\in C_{\mathrm{pol}}(\mathbb{R})
can systematically outperform
\delta
as
an asymptotic denoiser in the large
N
limit.

Proposition 9.4
.

Let, for each
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
A_{N}
be a
N\times N
deterministic
symmetric matrix and
B_{N}
be a
N\times N
random matrix distributed
according to GOE with variance
\sigma^{2}N^{-1}
. Assume that

N^{-1}\text{Tr}(A_{N}^{k})\underset{N\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\int_{\mathbb{R}}x^{k}\mu(dx),

for some compactly supported Borel probability measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}
.
Let
C_{N}=A_{N}+B_{N}
. Then, for all
g\in C_{\mathrm{pol}}(\mathbb{R})
,

\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[N^{-1}\text{Tr}\left((A_{N}-g(C_{N}))^{2}\right)]\geq\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[N^{-1}\text{Tr}\left((A_{N}-\delta(C_{N}))^{2}\right)].

Proof.

We apply Corollary
9.3
to
x_{N}=A_{N}
and
y_{N}=C_{N}
. More precisely, we work in
(\mathcal{A}_{N}=M_{N}(L^{\infty-}(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})),\varphi_{N}=\mathbb{E}N^{-1}\text{Tr})
, which is a Borel-ncps (see Example
2.9
)
and observe that
(x_{N},y_{N})
is a pair of elements in
\mathcal{A}_{N}^{\mathrm{sa}}
.
It is well-known that
(x_{N},y_{N})_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
are asymptotically free: in particular,
condition (
9.1
) is satisfied for some pair
(x=a,y=a+b)
of elements built
from freely independent selfadjoint elements
a,b
in a
W^{*}
-probability space

(\mathcal{A},\varphi)
. Here, by assumption,
a
is distributed according to
\mu
and,
by Wigner’s Theorem,
b
is distributed according to Wigner semicircle law with variance

\sigma^{2}
, which is denoted by
\nu
. Corollary
9.3
then implies that, for all

g\in C_{\mathrm{pol}}(\mathbb{R})
,

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[N^{-1}\text{Tr}\left((A_{N}-g(C_{N}))^{2}\right)]

\displaystyle=\lim_{N\to\infty}||\,x_{N}-g(y_{N})\,||_{2,\varphi_{N}}^{2}

\displaystyle=||\,x-g(y)\,||_{2,\varphi}^{2}\geq||\,x-E(x\,|\,y)\,||_{2,\varphi}^{2}.

It follows from Example
6.7
that

E(x|y)=y-2\sigma^{2}\pi H_{\mu\boxplus\nu}(y)
, which is precisely
\delta(y)

for the function
\delta:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
predicted in

[
16
]
. Since the Hilbert transform
H_{\mu\boxplus\nu}
of
the free additive convolution
\mu\boxplus\nu
is continuous (see
[
12
]
),
the function
\delta:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is itself in
C_{\mathrm{pol}}(\mathbb{R})
and

\displaystyle||\,x-E(x\,|\,y)\,||_{2,\varphi}^{2}

\displaystyle=||\,x-\delta(y)\,||_{2,\varphi}^{2}=\lim_{N\to\infty}||\,x_{N}-\delta(y_{N})\,||_{2,\varphi_{N}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[N^{-1}\text{Tr}\left((A_{N}-\delta(C_{N}))^{2}\right)].

∎

Similarly, when the multiplicative noise
B_{N}
is Wishart-distributed with
aspect ratio converging towards
\gamma\in(0,\infty)
,
[
16
]

recovers the estimator previously derived by Ledoit and Péché in
[
21
]

that is reviewed in the following subsection.

9.3.
Covariance matrix estimation

Another important instance of the general matrix denoising problem is the so-called
covariance matrix estimation. Let
X_{1},\ldots,X_{p}
be independent identically distributed
random vectors in
\mathbb{C}^{N}
with independent standardized entries, and let
\Sigma_{N}

be a positive semi-definite Hermitian
N\times N
matrix. The goal is to estimate

\Sigma_{N}
from the observation of the independent identically distributed random vectors

\Sigma_{N}^{1/2}X_{1},\ldots,\Sigma_{N}^{1/2}X_{p}
with common covariance matrix
\Sigma_{N}
.
It is well-known that the sample covariance matrix

Y_{N}=p^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{p}(\Sigma_{N}^{1/2}X_{i})(\Sigma_{N}^{1/2}X_{i})^{*}

is a consistent estimator of
\Sigma_{N}
in the regime where
N
is fixed and
p
is large.
But in the regime where
p
and
N
are both large with ratio
p/N
converging to

\gamma\in(0,\infty)
, the sample covariance matrix
Y_{N}
becomes a poor estimator of

\Sigma_{N}
: it is empirically observed that the eigenvalues of the sample covariance
matrix
Y_{N}
spread out significantly compared to the eigenvalues of the true covariance
matrix
\Sigma_{N}
. Based on this observation, Ledoit and Péché suggested in

[
21
]
to design estimators of
\Sigma_{N}
which shrink eigenvalues of
Y_{N}

without modifying the corresponding eigenvectors, hence are of the form
\delta(Y_{N})
, where

\delta:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is a bounded measurable function. At fixed
N
, there is an
optimal (in the
L^{2}
sense) bounded measurable function
\delta_{N}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
.

Assuming that the
12
th moment of entries of
X_{1},\ldots,X_{p}
is bounded by some constant
independent of
N
and
p
, that
\Sigma_{N}
is positive definite, with empirical spectral
distribution weakly converging to some probability measure
\mu
with compact support included
in
(0,\infty)
, and assuming that
\gamma\neq 1
, Ledoit and Péché proved that the
sequence
(\delta_{N})_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to the function

\delta:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
defined by:

\delta(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\frac{t}{|1-\gamma^{-1}+\gamma^{-1}tG(t)|^{2}},\quad\mbox{if $t>0$}\\
-\frac{\gamma}{(1-\gamma)\underline{G}(0)}\mathbf{1}_{\gamma<1},\quad\mbox{if $t=0$,}\\
\end{array}\right.

where
G
is the Stieltjes transform of the limiting empirical spectral distribution
of
Y_{N}
and
\underline{G}(t)=(1-\gamma^{-1})t^{-1}+\gamma^{-1}G(t)
.

This result also has an interpretation in terms of free denoising: one may argue as in the
proof of Proposition
9.4
that
(x_{N},y_{N})=(\Sigma_{N},Y_{N})
defines for each

N\in\mathbb{N}
a pair of selfadjoint elements in a Borel-ncps in such a way that
condition (
9.1
) is satisfied for some pair
(x,y)=(a,a^{1/2}ba^{1/2})
of elements
built from freely independent positive elements
a,b
in a
W^{*}
-probability space. Here,

a
is distributed according to
\mu
and
b
is distributed according to a dilation of
the free Poisson distribution with parameter
\gamma
. The function
\delta
found in

[
21
]
and reviewed above coincides, up to a dilation factor, to the
free denoiser computed in Example
7.7
; one may then use Corollary
9.3

to conclude that no
g\in C_{\mathrm{pol}}(\mathbb{R})
can outperform
\delta
as an
asymptotic denoiser in the large
N
limit.

Appendix A
Multiplicative convolution on the unit circle

Unitary operators do not fit into the framework of this paper, where we consider selfadjoint operators. However we point out that a similar development to the one from Sections 3, 4 and 5 can be done in the case of multiplication of freely independent unitary random variables. For the purpose of this section we fix
u,v
freely independent unitary random variables in a
W^{*}
-probability space, with respective distributions
\mu
and
\nu
. The distribution of
uv
is the free multiplicative convolution of
\mu
and
\nu
, also denoted by
\mu\boxtimes\nu
. One can also define the overlap measure
\mu^{(ov)}_{u,uv}
, but in this case this measure is defined on the Borel sigma-algebra of
\mathbb{T}^{2}
.

In
[
13
]
Biane proved analogous subordination results in the case of multiplicative convolution on the unit circle. More precisely, for
|z|<1
we have :

E\left(\frac{zuv}{1-zuv}\Big|u\right)=\frac{\omega(z)u}{1-\omega(z)u}.

Similarly as before, for any
s\in\mathbb{T}
, the formula

\psi_{k_{s}}(z)=\frac{\omega(z)s}{1-\omega(z)s},\quad|z|<1,

defines the moment transform of a probability measure
k_{s}
supported on
\mathbb{T}
, that is

\psi_{k_{s}}(z)=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{zt}{1-zt}k_{s}(dt).

One can retrieve the measure
k_{s}
as a weak limit via

2\pi dk_{s}(t)=\lim_{r\uparrow 1}(2Re(\psi_{k_{s}}(r\overline{t}))+1)dt,

where
dt
is the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{T}
.

For any bounded Borel function
g
on
\mathbb{T}
we have

E(g(uv)|u)=\mathcal{K}g(u)

where kernel
\mathcal{K}
is defined as

\mathcal{K}g(s)=\int_{\mathbb{T}}g(t)k_{s}(dt).

As in the additive and multiplicative case on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
, Biane’s subordination gives the first disintegration of
\mu^{(ov)}_{u,uv}
, namely we have

\displaystyle\varphi(f(u)g(uv))

\displaystyle=\varphi(f(u)E(g(uv)|u))=\varphi(f(u)\mathcal{K}g(u))=\int_{\mathbb{T}}f(s)\mathcal{K}g(s)d\mu(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}}f(s)\int_{\mathbb{T}}g(t)dk_{s}(t)d\mu(s).

Regularity and atoms of free multiplicative convolution on the unit circle together with boundary behaviour of subordination functions are well understood (see
[
2
,
4
]
). Recently in
[
9
]
the authors showed that the distribution of
uv
has no singular continuous part. Using similar methods as in Sections 4 and 5, one can show that for
\mu
-almost every
s\in\mathbb{T}
, the measure
k_{s}
is absolutely continuous with respect to
\mu\boxtimes\nu
. We have the following.

Proposition A.1
.

With notation as above suppose that
\mu
and
\nu
are not point masses. There exists a subset
U\subset\mathbb{T}
of full measure with respect to
(\mu\boxtimes\nu)^{ac}
such that for
\mu
-almost every
s\in\mathbb{T}
,
k_{s}(dt)=o(s,t)\mu\boxtimes\nu(dt)
, where
o:\mathrm{supp}(\mu)\times\mathrm{supp}(\mu\boxtimes\nu)\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is
defined (in
\mu\times\mu\boxtimes\nu
-almost everywhere sense) by the following formula:

\displaystyle\forall s\in\mathrm{supp}(\mu),o(s,t)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2\pi f_{\mu\boxtimes\nu}(t)}Re\left(2\frac{\omega\left(\overline{t}\right)s}{1-\omega\left(\overline{t}\right)s}+1\right)\qquad&\mbox{if }t\in U,\\
\frac{1}{\mu(s)}1_{\omega(\overline{t})=\overline{s}}&\mbox{if }t\mbox{ is an atom of }\mu\boxtimes\nu.\end{cases}

Theorem A.2
.

Let
u,v
be freely independent unitary random variables in a
W^{*}
-probability space, with respective distributions
\mu
and
\nu
not being point masses. Then
d\mu^{(ov)}_{u,uv}(s,t)=o(s,t)\mu(ds)\,\mu\boxtimes\nu(dt)
, where
o
is defined in the previous proposition.

Corollary A.3
.

The result above implies that for every
f\in\mathrm{Bor}_{b}(\mathbb{T})
we have

\displaystyle E[f(u)\mid uv]=h(uv),

where
h:\mathrm{Spec}(uv)\to\mathbb{C}
is defined
\mu\boxtimes\nu
-almost everywhere by

\displaystyle h(t)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2\pi f_{\mu\boxtimes\nu}(t)}\int_{\mathbb{T}}f(s)Re\left(2\frac{\omega\left(\overline{t}\right)s}{1-\omega\left(\overline{t}\right)s}+1\right)d\mu(s)\qquad&\mbox{ if }t\in U,\\
f\left(\overline{\omega(\overline{t})}\right)&\mbox{ if }t\mbox{ is an atom of }\mu\boxtimes\nu.\end{cases}

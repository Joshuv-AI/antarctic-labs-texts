Prioritized Aczel–Alsina aggregation operators under p, q-quasirung orthopair fuzzy environment for sustainable supplier selection in new energy vehicle industry
Authors: Jawad Ali, Ahmad N Al-Kenani, Muhammad I Syam
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractSelecting sustainable suppliers in the new energy vehicle industry is a complex decision-making problem due to diverse criteria, uncertainty in evaluations, and the need to prioritize certain factors. Addressing this gap, we propose a novel multi-criteria decision-making (MCDM) framework based on p, q-quasirung orthopair fuzzy (ROF) sets and enhanced with Aczel–Alsina–based prioritized aggregation operators. Specifically, we develop two base operators—the ROF AA prioritized average (ROFAAPA) and the ROF AA prioritized geometric (ROFAAPG)—along with their weighted prioritized counterparts, the ROF AA prioritized weighted average (ROFAAPWA) and the ROF AA prioritized weighted geometric (ROFAAPWG). The mathematical properties of these operators are established, and an MCDM algorithm is formulated to incorporate decision-makers’ priority structures. The framework also integrates a mathematical formulation to objectively determine criteria weights, ensuring a balanced combination of subjective and data-driven inputs. A case study for a leading new energy vehicle manufacturer demonstrates the framework’s effectiveness: among four evaluation criteria—Quality (), Cost (), Service level (), and Production capacity ()—Cost () received the highest weight (0.2789), and supplier emerged as the most sustainable choice. Comparative experiments against established MCDM techniques confirm the proposed approach’s superior ranking stability and robustness. These results provide both a methodological advance for fuzzy decision-making research and a practical decision-support tool for industries pursuing environmentally responsible supply chain strategies.Keywords: Aczel–Alsina operations; p,q-Quasirung orthopair fuzzy set; Prioritized aggregation operators; MCDMSubject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Aggregation operators (AOs) constitute a fundamental component in numerous real-world applications involving multi-criteria decision-making (MCDM), where the integration of numerical or linguistic values is required to reach a rational conclusion1–3. Information fusion and data accumulation are core aspects of systems such as machine learning, image processing, decision-making (DM), and pattern recognition. Aggregation, in essence, is the process of combining information from various sources to generate a collective assessment or decision. However, when practical scenarios are modeled using conventional data management techniques based solely on crisp numbers, human cognitive reasoning often struggles to express vagueness and uncertainty. Consequently, such methods may lead decision-makers toward ambiguous or unreliable conclusions4–6. To manage the complex, uncertain, and imprecise environments common in practical decision contexts, it becomes essential to employ theoretical frameworks capable of handling fuzzy information and adapting DM criteria to diverse contexts, such as pattern recognition, image analysis, or human cognition7,8.

Considering this necessity, Zadeh9 proposed the concept of fuzzy sets (FSs), wherein each element of the universal set is assigned a membership degree (MD) . The foundational principle of FS theory assumes that if an element possesses a membership degree , then its corresponding non-membership degree (NMD) is automatically defined as . However, Atanassov10 observed that human reasoning often includes hesitation, implying that the sum of MD and NMD does not necessarily equal one. To address this, Atanassov introduced intuitionistic fuzzy sets (IFSs), incorporating a third component—the hesitation degree—to better represent uncertainty in human judgment. This innovation substantially improved the modeling of vague and indeterminate information.

Although numerous MCDM methods have been developed under the IFS framework, they still impose restrictive constraints, namely that the sum of MD and NMD cannot exceed one, thereby limiting their expressive capacity. To overcome this, researchers developed the Pythagorean fuzzy set (PyFS) model11, which generalizes IFS by permitting the square sum of MD and NMD to be less than or equal to one12. For example, in a PyFS, MD = 0.5 and NMD = 0.7 are valid since , whereas such values would violate the IFS constraint. Owing to this flexibility, PyFSs have gained considerable attention for their ability to handle higher degrees of ambiguity13–16. Building on this, Yager17 and Hussain18 proposed q-rung orthopair fuzzy sets (q-ROFSs), replacing the square term in PyFS with a general qth power, where . This adjustment allows decision-makers to control uncertainty by selecting an appropriate q value.

For instance, in a decision context where MD = 0.9 and NMD = 0.8, the IFS and PyFS conditions are violated, yet the q-ROFS with successfully accommodates these values, offering enhanced modeling power. Seikh and Mandal19 later advanced this framework by introducing the (p, q)-quasirung orthopair fuzzy set (ROFS), where the pth power of MD and the qth power of NMD sum to a value . This two-parameter structure provides richer control and adaptability in capturing incomplete or imprecise data20–22. Unlike FS, IFS, or PyFS, which enforce fixed complementarity rules, the ROFS model independently regulates MD and NMD via exponents p and q, enabling more realistic expression of hesitation or conflicting opinions. Consequently, ROFSs deliver a nuanced and powerful framework for representing human uncertainty in complex MCDM settings.

The Aczel–Alsina (AA) t-norm and t-conorm, introduced by Aczel and Alsina23, offer an alternative operational paradigm that enhances flexibility and adaptiveness in aggregation. Established as generalized forms of algebraic t-norms and t-conorms, AA operators have been successfully employed across various fuzzy extensions and DM environments. Senapati et al.24,25 developed AA-based aggregation operators for interval-valued IFSs, while Senapati26 applied them to Pythagorean fuzzy sets. Hussain et al.27,28 extended the concept to T-spherical and Pythagorean fuzzy AA operators for MCDM problems. Subsequent studies—such as Hussain et al.29, Deveci et al.30, and Wang et al.31—further demonstrated the robustness of AA operations for real-world applications, ranging from traffic optimization to hesitant fuzzy information processing. Meher and Jeevaraj32 used interval-valued Fermatean fuzzy AA operators for hospital site selection, while Ren et al.33 utilized AA operators in large-scale datasets via Z-number theory. Collectively, these studies confirm the adaptability, mathematical elegance, and reliability of the AA framework for complex aggregation tasks.

Despite these advancements, integrating prioritized information within AA-based aggregation—particularly in the context of ROFSs—remains an unexplored research direction. Developing a prioritized AA framework not only generalizes earlier FS, IFS, PyFS, and q-ROFS models but also introduces the ability to represent hierarchical importance among criteria. Specifically:

The proposed prioritized AA theory, therefore, holds substantial theoretical and practical potential. It provides a structured means of handling fuzzy, prioritized information and supports robust evaluation in decision contexts.

One particularly compelling motivation for this work arises in the domain of new energy vehicles (NEVs), where supplier selection requires balancing environmental, economic, and operational objectives under uncertainty. Traditional methods often fail to incorporate explicit priority relations, such as giving greater weight to carbon footprint reduction than to cost minimization. The prioritized AA framework, coupled with the ROFS model, effectively captures these hierarchical relationships while mitigating uncertainty through nonlinear aggregation. Consequently, it provides decision-makers with a transparent and balanced evaluation framework aligned with sustainability-driven industry goals.

In summary, while AOs have long played a central role in MCDM, certain decision scenarios demand strict prioritization among criteria. To illustrate, consider selecting land for house construction based on utility access (), site (), and price (), where . Such hierarchy requires an aggregation operator that explicitly models these priority relations. Yager34,35 pioneered prioritized AOs and the prioritized ordered weighted averaging (POWA) operator, while Yan et al.36 expanded this idea using t-norm-based formulations. Recent works have extended prioritization concepts across various fuzzy frameworks, including spherical37, probabilistic38, complex T-spherical39, interval-valued Pythagorean40, and probabilistic hesitant soft Schweizer–Sklar systems41. However, no prior study has integrated prioritization with AA aggregation under the ROFS environment—a significant research gap this study aims to address.

Background description

The selection of environmentally responsible suppliers is a critical strategy for companies seeking to enhance their core competitiveness. Establishing cooperative and sustainable relationships between enterprises and suppliers not only ensures the safe transport of goods but also contributes to the continuous improvement of a company’s economic performance. The emergence of new energy vehicles not only reduces the environmental impact associated with traditional fuel-powered vehicles but also enhances the combustion efficiency of automobile engines.

As part of this transformation, the company is in the process of reevaluating its roster of green suppliers to support its operations. The company’s transportation department has conducted a comprehensive screening process, which includes qualification assessments and evaluations of enterprise credit, among other criteria. This screening process has identified four suppliers who have advanced to the expert selection phase.

In the current case study, a concept of priority among the criteria is informally incorporated in the prioritized aggregation operators that have been adopted. The ensuing order is an indication of the logical superiority of some criteria over others. As an example, quality and reliability in delivery is a key consideration in the selection of suppliers who will more often than not be chosen based on those factors, and not on cost since it has a direct impact on operational performance. Conclusions

Prioritized AOs provide powerful mechanisms for managing uncertainty and ambiguity. Recently, numerous researchers have conducted extensive investigations across various fuzzy frameworks, highlighting the versatility of fuzzy-based applications. This study introduces fundamental operations of AA-based aggregation tools within the p,q-rung orthopair fuzzy setting. By employing innovative prioritization concepts, we have proposed novel strategies—namely, the ROFAAPA, ROFAAPG, ROFAAPWA, and ROFAAPWG operators. A key advantage of the proposed operators over existing ones lies in their ability to account for the prioritization of criteria. Rooted in AA operational laws, these operators not only enhance generality but also embed prioritization—setting them apart from traditional AA-based AOs developed under FSs, IFSs, PyFSs, and q-ROFSs, which emerge as specific instances of our framework. To validate the consistency and utility of the proposed operators, we have formally established their mathematical properties, supported by rigorous proofs. Moreover, an algorithm was developed to address MCDM problems under p,q-rung orthopair fuzzy settings, even in the absence of known weights. A case study on selecting an appropriate supplier agent was presented to demonstrate practical applicability. Additionally, an extensive comparative and sensitivity analysis was performed, reinforcing the reliability, flexibility, and effectiveness of our methodology compared to existing approaches.

Although the advantages of the proposed operators have been comprehensively demonstrated, experts may still encounter distinct challenges arising from incomplete or uncertain information. Furthermore, as the current case study is based on hypothetical data, future research should aim to validate the proposed framework using real-world supplier datasets to rigorously evaluate its practical applicability and robustness. In addition, the present study can be extended to various other environments such as linguistic information systems, neural networks, clustering analysis, and other advanced DM approaches, where multi-criteria evaluation plays a critical role in sustainable and data-driven applications49–51.

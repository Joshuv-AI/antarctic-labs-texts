CoLaS: Copula-Seeded Sparse Local Graphs with Tunable Assortativity, Persistent Clustering, and a Degree-Tail Dichotomy
Marios Papamichalis, Regina Ruane

Abstract
Empirical networks are typically sparse yet display pronounced degree variation, persistent transitivity, and systematic degree mixing. Most sparse generators control at most two of these features, and assortativity is often achieved by degree-preserving rewiring, which obscures the mechanism–parameter link. We introduce CoLaS (copula-seeded local latent-space graphs), a modular latent-variable model that separates marginal specifications from dependence. Each node has a popularity variable governing degree heterogeneity and a latent geometric location governing locality. A low-dimensional copula couples popularity and location, providing an interpretable dependence parameter that tunes degree mixing while leaving the chosen marginals unchanged. Under shrinking-range locality, edges are conditionally independent, the graph remains sparse, and clustering does not vanish. We develop sparse-limit theory for degrees, transitivity, and assortativity. Degrees converge to mixed-Poisson limits and we establish a degree-tail dichotomy: with fixed-range local kernels, degree tails are necessarily light, even under heavy-tailed popularity. To recover power-law degrees without sacrificing sparsity or locality, we propose CoLaS-HT , a minimal tail-inheriting extension in which effective connection ranges grow with popularity. Finally, under an identifiability condition, we provide a consistent one-graph calibration method based on jointly matching transitivity and assortativity.



Keywords:
copulas; latent space models; random geometric graphs; degree correction; assortativity; clustering; sparse networks.

Introduction

Many network datasets are sparse yet simultaneously exhibit (i) strong degree heterogeneity, (ii) persistent transitivity from triadic closure, and (iii) pronounced degree mixing along edges. Standard sparse generators typically confound these features: degree-driven edge-independent models capture heterogeneity but wash out triangles; local geometric models generate triangles but offer limited or opaque parametric control of assortativity; and degree mixing is often tuned by degree-preserving rewiring, which severs the link between parameters and the dependence mechanism. Achieving all three properties at once is difficult in standard sparse model families. Degree-driven edge-independent constructions can reproduce heterogeneous degrees, but clustering typically vanishes as network size increases. Geometric and latent-space models naturally generate triangles through locality, yet they rarely provide a transparent parametric mechanism to tune degree assortativity without inadvertently changing other aspects of the graph. Models that encode triangles directly can be computationally and inferentially challenging and often lack clean asymptotic theory. As a result, degree mixing is commonly modified by degree-preserving rewiring after generation, which decouples fitted statistics from the model’s generative mechanism.

This paper introduces
CoLaS
, a copula-seeded sparse local latent-space model that separates these roles. Popularity governs marginal degree propensity, geometry enforces locality and non-vanishing transitivity under shrinking-range connectivity, and a low-dimensional copula couples popularity and position to provide a single interpretable dependence parameter that tunes assortativity without changing the user-specified marginals. We develop sparse asymptotic theory, prove a sharp degree-tail dichotomy with a minimal tail-inheriting extension (
CoLaS-HT
), and give a consistent one-graph calibration method based on matching transitivity and assortativity under an identifiability condition.

To the best of our knowledge, existing sparse random-graph models do not provide

Classical latent-space formulations with node random effects combine degree heterogeneity with geometry, but typically posit independence between popularity effects and positions, which fixes a neutral baseline for how degree relates to latent location

By contrast, our copula coupling introduces a single interpretable dependence parameter that directly governs mixing tendencies, offering a principled alternative to degree-preserving rewiring commonly used to modify mixing patterns after generation. The closest comparators are geometric scale-free models and recent extensions that attempt to introduce tunable assortativity. Hyperbolic and popularity–similarity models generate heavy-tailed degrees and strong clustering via latent geometry

[

[

Our work differs in its target regime and objectives: we focus on sparse graphs with a linear number of edges, while retaining non-vanishing clustering through locality and allowing heavy-tailed degrees through explicit marginal specifications

A key conceptual contribution is that the copula-seeding mechanism is transferable. By changing the latent attribute map and the edge kernel, one obtains copula-coupled variants of standard network models (including block models and dot-product graphs), preserving desired marginals while injecting structured dependence. This mirrors how copulas are used in multivariate modeling and aligns with recent copula-based developments in heterogeneous graphical modeling
[
13

The main strength of the framework is modularity: it enables controlled simulation and sensitivity studies in which degree heterogeneity, clustering, and assortativity can be varied nearly independently. It also offers theoretical insight into how clustering and assortativity interact, complementing ensemble observations that clustering can induce assortativity
[

.

Here, we propose
CoLaS
, a copula-seeded local latent-space model that cleanly separates (i) marginal degree heterogeneity (via node “popularity”), (ii) persistent transitivity (via geometric locality under shrinking-range scaling), and (iii) degree mixing/assortativity (via a low-dimensional copula dependence parameter) without post-hoc rewiring. In the sparse local regime, we establish tractable large-
n
limits for key graph observables, including mixed-Poisson degree limits under conditional edge independence and explicit deterministic limits for global transitivity and endpoint assortativity. We prove a “fixed-range locality” impossibility principle: with bounded compactly supported kernels, degrees are necessarily light-tailed even when popularity is heavy-tailed. To recover genuine power-law degrees while retaining sparsity and locality, we introduce the minimal tail-inheriting extension
CoLaS-HT
in which effective connection ranges expand with popularity. We derive a large-network assortativity limit in which the copula parameter acts as a native, interpretable mixing knob, and we provide conditions ensuring monotone response of assortativity in the dependence parameter. Under an injectivity (identifiability) condition, we give a consistent one-graph calibration method based on jointly matching transitivity and assortativity.

2.1
Copula-seeded node attributes: popularity and position

A
copula
of dimension
m\geq 2
is a distribution function
C:[0,1]^{m}\to[0,1]
with
uniform marginals on
[0,1]
.
By Sklar’s theorem, any joint law with continuous marginals can be decomposed into its marginals
composed with a unique copula
[
33
,
27
]
.
In particular, copulas allow one to separate
marginal specification
(here: popularity and position)
from
dependence specification
(here: the alignment between popularity and geometry).

Many weighted geometric graph models specify a weight distribution, a geometric distribution for positions, and an edge rule that depends on both.
A common baseline is that weights and positions are
independent
by construction (so any mixing pattern is an indirect consequence of the edge rule).
CoLaS makes a different design choice: it introduces a
dedicated dependence layer
between popularity and geometry
while keeping both marginals fixed
.
This turns the question “how assortative/mixed should the network be?” into a low-dimensional choice of
\theta

X

2.4
Degree-tail dichotomy

In (
5
), locality is
fixed-range
: large weights increase
intensity
but do not
increase
range
. For bounded, compactly supported
k
, this caps the limiting degree intensity and
forces light-tailed degrees even when
W
is heavy-tailed (Theorem
4
(i)).

Under compact support of
k
, each node only has
O_{\mathbb{P}}(1)

geometric candidates
in the ball of radius
O(\varepsilon_{n})
around
X_{i}
.
Weights can change the
ranking
of which candidate edges form, but they cannot create more candidates. Consequently, “heavy-tailed popularity”
does
not
imply “heavy-tailed degree” in the fixed-range regime—this is a structural constraint, not a moment artifact. Theorem
4
(i)
formalizes this as a uniform bound on the Poisson mixing intensity.

To obtain
tail inheritance
while retaining sparsity and locality, enlarge effective radii with weights:

A_{ij}\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}\!\big(p^{(n),\mathrm{HT}}_{ij}\big),\qquad p^{(n),\mathrm{HT}}_{ij}=1-\exp\!\left\{-\frac{\lambda}{\rho_{n}}\,k\!\left(\frac{X_{i}-X_{j}}{\varepsilon_{n}(W_{i}W_{j})^{1/d}}\right)\right\},\qquad\rho_{n}=n\varepsilon_{n}^{d}.

(6)

High-popularity nodes now “see” larger neighborhoods (range
\propto(W_{i}W_{j})^{1/d}
), so the limiting
degree intensity becomes unbounded in
W
and power-law tails can transfer from
W
to degrees
(Theorem
4
(ii)). This is a minimal modification: it preserves the same local-kernel
mechanism that yields persistent clustering, while removing the fixed-range cap on degree intensity.

The tail-inheriting rule (
6

What is specific to CoLaS is
orthogonal
to that tail mechanism: it is the copula-seeded dependence of
W
and
X

low-dimensional handle on mixing patterns while keeping marginals fixed. In particular, the same copula layer can be applied in either regime
((

Remark 1

(Which regime is used in inference/experiments)
.

If heavy-tailed degrees are a modeling goal, then inference and any interpretation of degree tails must use the

Standing assumptions (used by the limit theorems)

Assumptions

3

In the fixed-range regime, the law-of-large-numbers limits for clustering and assortativity are not intrinsically tied to high polynomial moments;

Assumption 1

(Kernel regularity)
.

k
is bounded, measurable, compactly supported, and
\kappa_{2},\kappa_{3}
in (

Compact support enforces locality; boundedness yields uniform control of approximation errors in Poisson and motif limits.
The constants

are geometric overlap factors that enter transparently in the limiting clustering expressions.

Assumption 2

(Sparsity regime)
.

\varepsilon_{n}\downarrow 0
and
\rho_{n}:=n\varepsilon_{n}^{d}\to\rho\in(0,\infty)
.

This regime keeps average degree bounded while preserving local geometric structure, preventing clustering from vanishing.

Assumption 3

(Latent regularity and conditional smoothness)

For every

Moreover

The continuity condition justifies exchanging limits with spatial integration (a recurring step in the proofs), and the boundedness

Sparse edge-independent models typically have vanishing clustering; CoLaS does not, because locality creates systematic neighborhood overlap.

copula-driven spatial heterogeneity.

For

Throughout this section

endpoint dependence (Spearman/Kendall) or to degree-conditional mixing curves. We view Pearson assortativity as the primary target in the finite-moment
setting and as a cautious diagnostic otherwise.

(12)

The measure
\nu_{\theta,n}
is the natural law of latent endpoint marks observed at the ends of a uniformly chosen edge: it is a size-biased
version of
\mu_{\theta}\otimes\mu_{\theta}
, biased by the propensity to form an edge.

A uniformly chosen edge does
not
sample endpoints according to
\mu_{\theta}
; it samples endpoints according to
\nu_{\theta,n}
.
Any statement about “assortativity” is therefore inherently a statement about an edge-biased population, and (
12
) makes that bias explicit.

Let
\Lambda_{\theta}
denote the limiting degree-intensity function in the fixed-range case,
and let
\Gamma_{\theta}(z,z^{\prime})
denote the limiting common-neighbor intensity.

Theorem 7

(Assortativity limit (degree correlation across a random edge))
.

Let
(\mathcal{Z},d)
be a Polish space with Borel
\sigma
-field and let
G_{n}
be an undirected simple random graph on
[n]

constructed as follows.

(Marks)
The marks
(Z_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
are i.i.d. with law
\mu_{\theta}
on
\mathcal{Z}
.

(Edges)
Conditional on
(Z_{1},\dots,Z_{n})
, the indicators
\{A_{ij}\}_{1\leq i<j\leq n}
are independent and

A_{ij}\mid(Z_{i},Z_{j})\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}\big(p^{(n)}(Z_{i},Z_{j})\big),\qquad p^{(n)}:\mathcal{Z}\times\mathcal{Z}\to[0,1]\ \text{measurable and symmetric.}

Let
(I,J)
be uniform on
\{(i,j):1\leq i<j\leq n\}
and independent of all marks and edge coin flips, and define

D_{i}:=\sum_{k\neq i}A_{ik}
.
Let

\alpha_{n}:=\mathbb{P}(A_{IJ}=1)=\iint p^{(n)}(z,z^{\prime})\,\mu_{\theta}(dz)\mu_{\theta}(dz^{\prime}),

and assume
\alpha_{n}>0
for all sufficiently large
n
.
Define the endpoint assortativity

r_{n}:=\mathrm{Corr}(D_{I},D_{J}\mid A_{IJ}=1),

whenever the conditional correlation is well-defined.

Define the edge–Palm measure on
\mathcal{Z}\times\mathcal{Z}
by

\nu_{\theta,n}(dz\,dz^{\prime}):=\frac{p^{(n)}(z,z^{\prime})\,\mu_{\theta}(dz)\mu_{\theta}(dz^{\prime})}{\alpha_{n}}.

Define

\bar{p}_{\theta,n}(z):=\int p^{(n)}(z,u)\,\mu_{\theta}(du),\qquad\Lambda_{\theta,n}(z):=(n-1)\bar{p}_{\theta,n}(z),

and the common-neighbor intensity

\Gamma_{\theta,n}(z,z^{\prime}):=(n-2)\int p^{(n)}(z,u)\,p^{(n)}(z^{\prime},u)\,\mu_{\theta}(du).

Assume:

(A1)

(
Edge–Palm weak limit
)
\nu_{\theta,n}\Rightarrow\nu_{\theta}
weakly on
\mathcal{Z}\times\mathcal{Z}

for some probability measure
\nu_{\theta}
.

(A2)

(
Intensity limits and moment bounds
)
There exist measurable maps
\Lambda_{\theta}:\mathcal{Z}\to[0,\infty)
and

\Gamma_{\theta}:\mathcal{Z}\times\mathcal{Z}\to[0,\infty)
such that, for
(Z,Z^{\prime})\sim\nu_{\theta,n}
,

\mathbb{E}_{\nu_{\theta,n}}\!\big[\big(\Lambda_{\theta,n}(Z)-\Lambda_{\theta}(Z)\big)^{2}\big]\to 0,\qquad\mathbb{E}_{\nu_{\theta,n}}\!\big[\big|\Gamma_{\theta,n}(Z,Z^{\prime})-\Gamma_{\theta}(Z,Z^{\prime})\big|\big]\to 0,

and there exists
\delta>0
with

\sup_{n}\ \mathbb{E}_{\nu_{\theta,n}}\!\big[\Lambda_{\theta,n}(Z)^{2+\delta}\big]<\infty,\qquad\sup_{n}\ \mathbb{E}_{\nu_{\theta,n}}\!\big[\Gamma_{\theta,n}(Z,Z^{\prime})^{1+\delta}\big]<\infty.

(A2
′
)

(
Null discontinuity condition
)
The maps
(z,z^{\prime})\mapsto\Lambda_{\theta}(z)
,
(z,z^{\prime})\mapsto\Lambda_{\theta}(z^{\prime})
, and

(z,z^{\prime})\mapsto\Gamma_{\theta}(z,z^{\prime})
are continuous at
\nu_{\theta}
-a.e. point of
\mathcal{Z}\times\mathcal{Z}
.

(A3)

(
Nondegenerate denominator
) With
(Z,Z^{\prime})\sim\nu_{\theta}
,

V(\theta):=\mathrm{Var}_{\nu_{\theta}}\!\big(\Lambda_{\theta}(Z)\big)+\mathbb{E}_{\nu_{\theta}}\!\big[\Lambda_{\theta}(Z)\big]>0.

Then
r_{n}\to r(\theta)
, where for
(Z,Z^{\prime})\sim\nu_{\theta}
,

r(\theta)=\frac{\mathrm{Cov}_{\nu_{\theta}}\!\big(\Lambda_{\theta}(Z),\Lambda_{\theta}(Z^{\prime})\big)+\mathbb{E}_{\nu_{\theta}}\!\big[\Gamma_{\theta}(Z,Z^{\prime})\big]}{\mathrm{Var}_{\nu_{\theta}}\!\big(\Lambda_{\theta}(Z)\big)+\mathbb{E}_{\nu_{\theta}}\!\big[\Lambda_{\theta}(Z)\big]}.

Moreover, define

S(\theta):=\mathrm{Cov}_{\nu_{\theta}}\!\big(\Lambda_{\theta}(Z),\Lambda_{\theta}(Z^{\prime})\big)+\mathbb{E}_{\nu_{\theta}}\!\big[\Gamma_{\theta}(Z,Z^{\prime})\big].

On any interval
\Theta_{0}
such that
V(\theta)>0
for all
\theta\in\Theta_{0}
, if
S(\theta)
is nondecreasing on
\Theta_{0}

and either

(i)

V(\theta)
is constant on
\Theta_{0}
,  or

(ii)

S(\theta)\geq 0
and
V(\theta)
is nonincreasing on
\Theta_{0}
,

then
\theta\mapsto r(\theta)=S(\theta)/V(\theta)
is nondecreasing on
\Theta_{0}
.

A uniformly chosen edge does not sample endpoints according to
\mu_{\theta}
, but according to a size-biased law:

(Z,Z^{\prime})
are distributed as
\nu_{\theta,n}
(and asymptotically
\nu_{\theta}
). Under this edge-biased view, each endpoint degree is driven by a
random intensity
\Lambda_{\theta}(Z)
plus idiosyncratic Poisson variability. Degree correlation across an edge has two distinct sources:
(i)
endpoint heterogeneity alignment
, captured by
\mathrm{Cov}_{\nu_{\theta}}(\Lambda_{\theta}(Z),\Lambda_{\theta}(Z^{\prime}))
, and
(ii)
shared-neighborhood effects
, captured by
\mathbb{E}_{\nu_{\theta}}[\Gamma_{\theta}(Z,Z^{\prime})]
, which accounts for common neighbors.
The denominator
V(\theta)
is the total asymptotic variance of a degree under the edge-biased law.
The monotonicity criterion clarifies when increasing the dependence parameter
\theta
yields a monotone response in assortativity, providing a
parameter-based alternative to post-hoc rewiring.

In CoLaS, the copula affects assortativity primarily through the
sorting term

\mathrm{Cov}_{\nu_{\theta}}(\Lambda_{\theta}(Z),\Lambda_{\theta}(Z^{\prime}))
by changing how large-
W
values are arranged across
X
.
The geometry affects assortativity through the
common-neighbor term

\mathbb{E}_{\nu_{\theta}}[\Gamma_{\theta}(Z,Z^{\prime})]
,
which is nonzero precisely because neighborhoods overlap in the local regime.
This split is conceptually important: it disentangles “assortativity coming from sorting of node propensities” from “assortativity coming from shared
local environments,” two mechanisms that are often conflated in informal discussions.

Small-
\lambda
heuristic

Using
1-e^{-t}=t+O(t^{2})
in (

) gives

\Lambda_{\theta}(w,x)\approx\lambda\,\kappa_{2}\,w\,m_{1,\theta}(x)
in the fixed-range regime.
Thus, to first order, the copula enters assortativity through the spatial variation of
m_{1,\theta}
and its edge-biased co-movement across
neighboring locations, while geometry enters through overlap effects embedded in
\Gamma_{\theta}
.
This parallels the clustering decomposition of
Corollary

1
: density scale
\times
geometry
\times
copula-driven conditional moments.

Assumption 6

(Injective moment map)
.

The map
\theta\mapsto\big(r(\theta),C(\theta)\big)
is injective on
\Theta
.

This is the minimal identifiability condition for one-graph calibration: distinct dependence parameters must induce distinct limiting pairs

(r(\theta),C(\theta))
, otherwise
\theta
cannot be recovered from
(\widehat{r}_{n},\widehat{C}_{n})
even asymptotically.
The next theorem verifies it in a fully explicit toy
setting and illustrates how the copula dependence parameter acts as a
native
tuning knob for both
degree assortativity and transitivity (with all marginals held fixed).

Theorem
8
is not meant to be the “only” setting where monotonic tuning works; it is meant to be a reviewer-visible proof of concept:
(1) strict monotonicity can be verified, (2) injectivity can hold from one graph, and (3) the tuning effect is created
solely
by changing dependence
while holding marginals fixed. In more flexible copula families, the same checks can be done numerically on
\Theta
(and the abstract monotonicity
criterion in
Theorem

gives a structured analytic route when concordance ordering is available, cf. Assumption

Theorem 8

(Concrete monotonicity/identifiability for an FGM copula)
.

Fix
d=1
and consider a
linear-link
fixed-range CoLaS model on the 1D torus
\mathbb{T}=[0,1)
:
for
\varepsilon_{n}\downarrow 0
with
\rho_{n}:=n\varepsilon_{n}\to\rho\in(0,\infty)
and
\lambda\in(0,\rho]
, let

p_{ij}^{(n)}=\mathbb{P}\!\big(A_{ij}=1\mid(W_{\ell},X_{\ell})_{\ell=1}^{n}\big)=\frac{\lambda}{\rho_{n}}\,W_{i}W_{j}\,\mathbf{1}\big\{|X_{i}-X_{j}|_{\mathbb{T}}\leq\varepsilon_{n}\big\},\qquad 1\leq i<j\leq n,

where
|x-y|_{\mathbb{T}}:=\min\{|x-y|,\,1-|x-y|\}
denotes torus distance.
(For a fully well-defined finite-
n
model without truncation, it suffices to assume
\lambda<\rho

and
\rho_{n}>\lambda
for all large
n
, so
p_{ij}^{(n)}\leq 1
eventually; alternatively one may truncate

p_{ij}^{(n)}
at
1
.)

Assume
(W_{i},X_{i})_{i\geq 1}
are i.i.d. with
(W,X)\in(0,1)\times(0,1)
having the
Farlie–Gumbel–Morgenstern (FGM) density

f_{W,X}(w,x)=1+\theta(1-2w)(1-2x),\qquad(w,x)\in(0,1)^{2},\qquad\theta\in[0,1].

Let
C_{n}
and
r_{n}
be global transitivity and endpoint degree assortativity as defined in the main text.
Then as
n\to\infty
,

C_{n}\ \xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}\ C(\theta)\qquad\text{and}\qquad r_{n}\ \xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}\ r(\theta),

where, writing
t:=\theta^{2}\in[0,1]
, the limits admit the explicit closed forms

C(\theta)=\frac{9\lambda}{4\rho}\cdot\frac{t+4}{4t+27},

and

r(\theta)=\frac{15\lambda\big(2\rho t^{3}-36\rho t^{2}+810\rho t+45t^{2}+1395t+4860\big)}{\rho\big(27\lambda t^{3}-581\lambda t^{2}+13905\lambda t+18225\lambda+1200t^{2}+40500t+218700\big)}.

Moreover,
\theta\mapsto C(\theta)
and
\theta\mapsto r(\theta)
are strictly increasing on
[0,1]
.
In particular, the map
\theta\mapsto(C(\theta),r(\theta))
is injective on
[0,1]
, so
Assumption
6
holds on this parameter range.

Remark 4

(Why
C(\theta)
and
r(\theta)
increase)
.

In this example the
marginals
of
W
and
X
are fixed (both uniform), and
\theta
only changes
their
alignment
: for
\theta>0
the FGM copula favors “high–high” and “low–low” pairs.
Because edges only form locally in
X
(via
\mathbf{1}\{|X_{i}-X_{j}|_{\mathbb{T}}\leq\varepsilon_{n}\}
), increasing
\theta

creates stronger
spatial sorting
of popularity.
As a result, nearby nodes tend to have more similar
W
values, which increases (i) degree mixing
along edges (assortativity) and (ii) the density of triangles relative to wedges (transitivity).
The strict monotonicity implies that in this family the dependence parameter is identifiable from
either
C(\theta)
or
r(\theta)
(and hence from the pair), providing a closed-form instance of
Assumption
6
.

6.2
Estimator and asymptotic theory

Let
\widehat{r}_{n}
and
\widehat{C}_{n}
be computed from a single observed graph
G_{n}
.
Define the moment-matching estimator

\widehat{\theta}_{n}\in\arg\min_{\theta\in\Theta}\left\|\begin{pmatrix}\widehat{r}_{n}\\
\widehat{C}_{n}\end{pmatrix}-\begin{pmatrix}r(\theta)\\
C(\theta)\end{pmatrix}\right\|_{2}^{2}.

This estimator matches two complementary features: degree mixing along edges (
r_{n}
) and triadic closure through transitivity (
C_{n}
).

Because
C_{n}
and
r_{n}
have deterministic limits in the sparse local regime (and, in fixed range, a joint CLT), a single large graph contains enough
averaging structure to identify and estimate
\theta
under injectivity. The estimator above is a standard minimum-distance construction, but the
model-specific work is in establishing (i) non-degenerate limits for
C_{n}
and
r_{n}
and (ii) conditions under which
\theta\mapsto(C(\theta),r(\theta))

is injective (Assumption
6
and
Theorem

8
).

Theorem 9

(One-graph minimum-distance calibration: consistency and asymptotic normality)
.

Let
\Theta\subset\mathbb{R}^{p}
be compact and let
\theta_{0}\in\Theta
be the true parameter.
Let
m:\Theta\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
be continuous, and let
\widehat{m}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a measurable statistic
computed from the observed graph
G_{n}
. Define, for
\theta\in\Theta
,

Q_{n}(\theta):=\|\widehat{m}_{n}-m(\theta)\|_{2}^{2},\qquad Q(\theta):=\|m(\theta_{0})-m(\theta)\|_{2}^{2}.

Let
\widehat{\theta}_{n}
be a (measurable) minimizer of
Q_{n}
on
\Theta
, i.e.

\widehat{\theta}_{n}\in\arg\min_{\theta\in\Theta}Q_{n}(\theta)
.

Assume:

(A1)

(Moment convergence)
\widehat{m}_{n}\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}m(\theta_{0})
.

(A2)

(Identifiability)
m(\theta)=m(\theta_{0})
implies
\theta=\theta_{0}
.

Then
\widehat{\theta}_{n}\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}\theta_{0}
.

If, in addition,
\theta_{0}\in\mathrm{int}(\Theta)
, there exists an open neighborhood
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{p}
of
\theta_{0}

such that
m
is continuously differentiable on
U
, the Jacobian

G:=Dm(\theta_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times p}

has full column rank (hence
p\leq 2
), and

\sqrt{n}\,\big(\widehat{m}_{n}-m(\theta_{0})\big)\ \Rightarrow\ \mathcal{N}\!\left(0,\Sigma_{\theta_{0}}\right)

for some symmetric nonnegative definite
2\times 2
matrix
\Sigma_{\theta_{0}}
, then

\sqrt{n}\,(\widehat{\theta}_{n}-\theta_{0})\ \Rightarrow\ \mathcal{N}\!\left(0,\ (G^{\top}G)^{-1}G^{\top}\Sigma_{\theta_{0}}G(G^{\top}G)^{-1}\right).

This is a standard minimum-distance (argmin) result specialized to a two-moment calibration target.
Consistency follows from convergence of the empirical moments to their population limits and injectivity of the map
\theta\mapsto m(\theta)
.
The asymptotic normality statement is a delta-method expansion: a
\sqrt{n}
-CLT for the empirical moment vector transfers to a
\sqrt{n}
-CLT for

\widehat{\theta}_{n}
through the Jacobian
Dm(\theta_{0})
. In the CoLaS setting, one takes

\widehat{m}_{n}=(\widehat{r}_{n},\widehat{C}_{n})
and
m(\theta)=(r(\theta),C(\theta))
, yielding a principled one-graph route to estimating
\theta

(and, when the CLT holds, to asymptotic standard errors).

When closed forms are unavailable for
(C(\theta),r(\theta))
, the workflow remains the same: compute model-predicted moments by Monte Carlo (for fixed

\theta
) or numerical integration of the limiting expressions, and minimize
Q_{n}(\theta)
on
\Theta
. Injectivity can also be checked numerically
(by plotting
\theta\mapsto(C(\theta),r(\theta))
on a grid), which is often sufficient for applied calibration.

Theorem 10

(Joint
\sqrt{n}
–CLT for transitivity and assortativity (fixed–range CoLaS))
.

Assume the fixed–range CoLaS model with bounded symmetric kernel
k
satisfying

\supp(k)\subseteq B(0,R)
for some
R<\infty
, and thermodynamic scaling

\rho_{n}:=n\varepsilon_{n}^{d}\to\rho\in(0,\infty)
.
Let
D_{i}:=\sum_{j\neq i}A_{ij}
and define

T_{n}:=\sum_{1\leq i<j<\ell\leq n}A_{ij}A_{i\ell}A_{j\ell},\qquad W_{n}:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\binom{D_{i}}{2},\qquad E_{n}:=\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}A_{ij},

Q_{p,n}:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}D_{i}^{p}\ \ (p=2,3),\qquad P_{n}:=\sum_{i<j}A_{ij}D_{i}D_{j},\qquad Y_{n}:=\frac{1}{n}\,(T_{n},W_{n},E_{n},Q_{2,n},Q_{3,n},P_{n}).

With conventions
C_{n}:=0
if
W_{n}=0
and
r_{n}:=0
if
E_{n}=0
or the denominator below vanishes, set

C_{n}:=\frac{3T_{n}}{W_{n}},\qquad r_{n}:=\frac{P_{n}/E_{n}-\big(Q_{2,n}/(2E_{n})\big)^{2}}{Q_{3,n}/(2E_{n})-\big(Q_{2,n}/(2E_{n})\big)^{2}}.

Define
h:\mathcal{D}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
by

h(y_{1},\dots,y_{6}):=\left(\frac{3y_{1}}{y_{2}},\;\frac{y_{6}/y_{3}-\big(y_{4}/(2y_{3})\big)^{2}}{y_{5}/(2y_{3})-\big(y_{4}/(2y_{3})\big)^{2}}\right),\quad\mathcal{D}:=\Big\{y:\ y_{2}>0,\ y_{3}>0,\ y_{5}/(2y_{3})-\big(y_{4}/(2y_{3})\big)^{2}>0\Big\}.

Assume
\mathbb{E}Y_{n}\to y_{\star}\in\mathcal{D}
and
\mathbb{P}(Y_{n}\in\mathcal{D})\to 1
.
Then there exists a finite
6\times 6
covariance matrix
\Sigma_{Y}
such that

\sqrt{n}\,(Y_{n}-\mathbb{E}Y_{n})\ \Rightarrow\ \mathcal{N}(0,\Sigma_{Y}),

and, with
J_{h}(y_{\star})
the Jacobian of
h
at
y_{\star}
,

\sqrt{n}\big((C_{n},r_{n})-h(\mathbb{E}Y_{n})\big)\ \Rightarrow\ \mathcal{N}\!\big(0,\ J_{h}(y_{\star})\Sigma_{Y}J_{h}(y_{\star})^{\top}\big).

In particular, since
h(\mathbb{E}Y_{n})\to h(y_{\star})
,

\sqrt{n}\big((C_{n},r_{n})-h(y_{\star})\big)\ \Rightarrow\ \mathcal{N}\!\big(0,\ J_{h}(y_{\star})\Sigma_{Y}J_{h}(y_{\star})^{\top}\big).

In the fixed–range CoLaS regime
n\varepsilon_{n}^{d}\to\rho\in(0,\infty)
with compactly supported kernel,
each vertex only “interacts” through potential edges inside a ball of radius
O(\varepsilon_{n})
, so its
local neighborhood contains
O(1)
nodes on average. Consequently, the basic ingredients of
global transitivity and endpoint assortativity—triangle counts, wedge counts, and degree products—can
be written as sums of
local
contributions with a finite (deterministic) interaction range.
Theorem
10
says that these normalized local sums have joint Gaussian
fluctuations of order
n^{-1/2}
, and therefore the ratio–type statistics
(C_{n},r_{n})
inherit a

joint
asymptotic normal law via the multivariate delta method.

The delta method is the last step; the substantive content is the joint CLT for the local motif and degree-product vector
Y_{n}
under sparse local
dependence induced by geometry and copula-seeded heterogeneity. This is what enables one-graph standard errors for
(C_{n},r_{n})
, and by extension
Wald-type uncertainty quantification for
\widehat{\theta}_{n}
via
Theorem

9
.

This joint CLT provides an asymptotic covariance for
(C_{n},r_{n})
, enabling
Wald-type uncertainty quantification (confidence intervals/tests) for clustering and assortativity in a
single large network, and it supplies the asymptotic normality input required by moment-based
one-graph calibration procedures that match
(C_{n},r_{n})
to their model-predicted limits.

Conclusion

We introduced
CoLaS
(copula-seeded local latent-space graphs), a sparse generator built around a single organizing principle:
separate marginals from dependence
. Each node carries a popularity mark
W
(degree heterogeneity) and a geometric position
X
(locality and triadic closure), while a low-dimensional copula couples
(W,X)
without changing either marginal. This explicit “dependence layer” makes the mechanism–parameter link transparent: clustering is induced by locality under shrinking-range connectivity, while degree mixing is tuned
natively
via the copula parameter
\theta
, rather than through post-hoc degree-preserving rewiring.

Our theory delivers a regime-aware message that is easy to misstate without a sharp dichotomy. In the baseline fixed-range model, degrees converge to a mixed-Poisson limit and we prove an
impossibility principle
for heavy tails: with bounded compactly supported kernels, fixed-range locality uniformly bounds the limiting degree intensity, so observable degree tails are necessarily light even if
W
is heavy-tailed. Conversely, if genuine scale-free degrees are a modeling goal, one must leave the fixed-range regime. We therefore propose the minimal tail-inheriting variant CoLaS-HT, where effective connection ranges expand with popularity so that degrees inherit the tail index of
W
, while retaining sparsity and the same locality-based clustering mechanism. This variant overlaps in spirit with GIRG-/hyperbolic-type constructions; our novelty is not “range scaling” per se, but the
copula-seeding layer
that injects tunable weight–geometry alignment (hence tunable mixing) while keeping the marginals fixed and preserving the locality-driven clustering story.

For network summaries, we derive deterministic large-
n
limits for global transitivity and endpoint degree mixing. The assortativity limit admits a reusable decomposition—valid for broad classes of marked edge-independent graphs—into (i) an endpoint
heterogeneity-alignment
term and (ii) a
common-neighbor
term driven by local overlap, clarifying how copula-induced sorting and geometry jointly contribute to degree correlations along edges. Under finite-moment conditions we provide monotonicity criteria ensuring that increasing positive dependence in the copula family produces monotone tuning of the limiting assortativity, and we verify strict monotonicity/injectivity in a fully explicit worked example.

A key practical point is
which assortativity is being tuned
. Our main limit theory targets Newman/Pearson endpoint assortativity in regimes where it is well-behaved, and we make explicit the boundary of that validity. In sufficiently heavy-tailed regimes, Pearson-style degree correlations can become unstable or misleading (as emphasized in recent work on geometric scale-free models), so it should not be treated as the primary “mixing knob” without care. In those regimes, CoLaS naturally motivates copula-consistent alternatives—rank-based mixing summaries (Spearman/Kendall) and degree-conditional mixing curves—and the same edge-Palm viewpoint provides a direct route to parallel large-
n
limits for such targets.

Finally, these limits enable
one-graph calibration
. When the moment map
\theta\mapsto(C(\theta),r(\theta))
is injective, a minimum-distance estimator matching
(C_{n},r_{n})
is consistent and, under a joint
\sqrt{n}
–CLT, asymptotically normal, yielding principled uncertainty quantification for dependence calibration from a single observed network.

Looking ahead, two directions would further strengthen the tuning story without changing the model’s spine: (i) extending strict monotonicity/identifiability beyond the worked FGM case to standard copula families (e.g., Gaussian or Archimedean) under common kernels, and (ii) developing the same limit-and-calibration theory for rank-based (and tail-sensitive) assortativity targets, which are better behaved in the most heavy-tailed applications. More broadly, because copula-seeding is modular, it can be transplanted to other latent marked models (beyond geometry) to endow them with an explicit, separable dependence mechanism while keeping desired marginals fixed.

and therefore

(14)

Step 2 (Integral formula for

).

Let
(W^{\prime},X^{\prime})\sim\mu_{\theta}
be an independent copy. By (
5
) and the tower property,

\bar{p}_{n}(w,x)=\mathbb{E}\!\left[1-\exp\!\left\{-\frac{\lambda}{\rho_{n}}\,wW^{\prime}\,k\!\left(\frac{x-X^{\prime}}{\varepsilon_{n}}\right)\right\}\right].

Since

,

\bar{p}_{n}(w,x)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\mathbb{E}\!\left[1-\exp\!\left\{-\frac{\lambda}{\rho_{n}}\,wW^{\prime}\,k\!\left(\frac{x-y}{\varepsilon_{n}}\right)\right\}\ \Big|\ X=y\right]dy.

Make the torus change-of-variables

(mod
1
), so

,
and identify the scaled torus with

.
Then

\bar{p}_{n}(w,x)=\varepsilon_{n}^{d}\int_{Q_{n}}\mathbb{E}\!\left[1-\exp\!\left\{-\frac{\lambda}{\rho_{n}}\,wW^{\prime}\,k(u)\right\}\ \Big|\ X=x-\varepsilon_{n}u\right]du.

(15)

Multiplying by
(n-1)
gives

(n-1)\bar{p}_{n}(w,x)=\rho_{n}\Big(1-\frac{1}{n}\Big)\int_{Q_{n}}\mathbb{E}\!\left[1-\exp\!\left\{-\frac{\lambda}{\rho_{n}}\,wW^{\prime}\,k(u)\right\}\ \Big|\ X=x-\varepsilon_{n}u\right]du.

(16)

Step 3 (Limit of the mean).

By Assumption
1
,
k
has compact support:
\mathrm{supp}(k)\subset B(0,R)
for some

.
Hence

for all large

equals the integral over
\mathbb{R}^{d}

for all large
n
.

Define

and
t(u):=\frac{\lambda}{\rho}wk(u)
.
For
u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
let

I_{n}(u):=\mathbb{E}\!\left[1-e^{-t_{n}(u)W^{\prime}}\mid X=x-\varepsilon_{n}u\right],\qquad I(u):=\mathbb{E}\!\left[1-e^{-t(u)W^{\prime}}\mid X=x\right].

Using
0\leq 1-e^{-t}\leq t
and Assumption
3
(bounded

),
for all large
n
,

0\leq I_{n}(u)\leq\frac{2\lambda}{\rho}\,w\,\|m_{1,\theta}\|_{\infty}\,k(u),

which is integrable over
\mathbb{R}^{d}
because

and is compactly supported.
Moreover, for each fixed

t_{n}(u)\to t(u)
and
x-\varepsilon_{n}u\to x
in
\mathbb{T}^{d}
.
The map

uniformly in

pointwise in

.
By dominated convergence in

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}I_{n}(u)\,du\ \longrightarrow\ \int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}I(u)\,du.

Since

and in particular

Step 4 (Binomial-to-Poisson).

From (

\bar{p}_{n}(w,x)\to 0

Hence for

the pgf of

Step 5 (Empirical degree distribution).

Let

. By exchangeability,

\mathbb{E}[H_{n}(k)]=\mathbb{P}(D_{1}=k)=\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{P}(D_{1}=k\mid Z_{1})]
.
By Step 4,
\mathbb{P}(D_{1}=k\mid Z_{1})\to e^{-\Lambda_{\theta}(Z_{1})}\Lambda_{\theta}(Z_{1})^{k}/k!
,
and bounded convergence yields
\mathbb{E}[H_{n}(k)]\to\mathbb{P}(D=k)
.

For the variance, the exact identity is

\mathrm{Var}(H_{n}(k))=\frac{1}{n}\mathrm{Var}(\mathbf{1}\{D_{1}=k\})+\frac{n-1}{n}\mathrm{Cov}(\mathbf{1}\{D_{1}=k\},\mathbf{1}\{D_{2}=k\}).

Thus it suffices to show
\mathrm{Cov}(\mathbf{1}\{D_{1}=k\},\mathbf{1}\{D_{2}=k\})\to 0
.

Asymptotic independence of
(D_{1},D_{2})
.

For
s,t\in[0,1]
define
G_{n}(s,t):=\mathbb{E}[s^{D_{1}}t^{D_{2}}\mid Z_{1},Z_{2}]
.
Decompose
D_{1}=A_{12}+\sum_{j=3}^{n}A_{1j}
and
D_{2}=A_{12}+\sum_{j=3}^{n}A_{2j}
.
Using conditional independence of edges given all marks and independence across
j\geq 3
,

G_{n}(s,t)=\mathbb{E}[(st)^{A_{12}}\mid Z_{1},Z_{2}]\,\prod_{j=3}^{n}\mathbb{E}[s^{A_{1j}}t^{A_{2j}}\mid Z_{1},Z_{2}].

Because
k
is compactly supported,
p^{(n)}_{12}>0
only if
\|X_{1}-X_{2}\|_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\leq R\varepsilon_{n}
,
an event of probability
O(\varepsilon_{n}^{d})=O(1/n)
; hence

\mathbb{E}[(st)^{A_{12}}\mid Z_{1},Z_{2}]\to 1
in probability.

For the product term, conditioning additionally on
Z_{j}
and using independence of
A_{1j}
and
A_{2j}

given
(Z_{1},Z_{2},Z_{j})
yields

\mathbb{E}[s^{A_{1j}}t^{A_{2j}}\mid Z_{1},Z_{2}]=1+\bar{p}_{n}(Z_{1})(s-1)+\bar{p}_{n}(Z_{2})(t-1)+c_{n}(Z_{1},Z_{2})(s-1)(t-1),

where
c_{n}(Z_{1},Z_{2}):=\mathbb{E}[p^{(n)}_{1j}p^{(n)}_{2j}\mid Z_{1},Z_{2}]
.
Again by compact support,
c_{n}(Z_{1},Z_{2})=0
unless
\|X_{1}-X_{2}\|_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\leq 2R\varepsilon_{n}
,
an event of probability
O(1/n)
, hence
(n-2)c_{n}(Z_{1},Z_{2})\to 0
in probability.
Together with
(n-2)\bar{p}_{n}(Z_{\ell})\to\Lambda_{\theta}(Z_{\ell})
(Step 3) we obtain

G_{n}(s,t)\ \xrightarrow{\ \mathbb{P}\ }\ \exp\!\big(\Lambda_{\theta}(Z_{1})(s-1)+\Lambda_{\theta}(Z_{2})(t-1)\big).

Since
0\leq G_{n}(s,t)\leq 1
, convergence in probability implies convergence in
L^{1}
, so taking expectations gives

\mathbb{E}[s^{D_{1}}t^{D_{2}}]=\mathbb{E}[G_{n}(s,t)]\to\mathbb{E}\!\left[\exp\!\big(\Lambda_{\theta}(Z_{1})(s-1)+\Lambda_{\theta}(Z_{2})(t-1)\big)\right].

Because
Z_{1}
and
Z_{2}
are independent, the limit factors into

\mathbb{E}[e^{\Lambda_{\theta}(Z_{1})(s-1)}]\cdot\mathbb{E}[e^{\Lambda_{\theta}(Z_{2})(t-1)}]
,
so the limiting joint pgf equals the product of the marginal pgfs.
Thus
(D_{1},D_{2})
converges in distribution to two independent copies of
D
, and since indicators are bounded,

\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{1}\{D_{1}=k\}\mathbf{1}\{D_{2}=k\}]\to\mathbb{P}(D=k)^{2},

which implies
\mathrm{Cov}(\mathbf{1}\{D_{1}=k\},\mathbf{1}\{D_{2}=k\})\to 0
.
Therefore
\mathrm{Var}(H_{n}(k))\to 0
and Chebyshev yields
H_{n}(k)\to\mathbb{P}(D=k)
in probability for each
k
.

Finally, tightness of
\nu_{n}
follows from

\nu_{n}([K,\infty))=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{1}\{D_{i}\geq K\}\leq\frac{1}{K}\cdot\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}D_{i},

together with
\sup_{n}\mathbb{E}[D_{1}]<\infty
(bounded mean degree under the sparse-local regime).
Hence
\nu_{n}\Rightarrow\mathcal{L}(D)
in probability.
∎

Proof of Theorem
4
.

(i) Fixed-range.

Fix
(w,x)
. Since for

we have
0\leq 1-e^{-t}\leq 1
, the fixed-range limiting intensity satisfies

\displaystyle 0\leq\Lambda_{\theta}(w,x)

\displaystyle=\rho\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{E}\!\left[1-\exp\!\left\{-\frac{\lambda}{\rho}\,wW^{\prime}k(u)\right\}\Bigm|\,X=x\right]\,du

where
S=\{u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:k(u)>0\}
and

because
k
has compact support.
Hence

a.s.

Let

. Since
\Lambda_{\theta}(W,X)\leq M
a.s. and

\mathbb{E}\!\left[e^{tD}\mid\Lambda_{\theta}(W,X)\right]=\exp\!\left(\Lambda_{\theta}(W,X)\,(e^{t}-1)\right)\leq\exp\!\left(M(e^{t}-1)\right),

(ii) CoLaS-HT: mixed-Poisson limit.

Fix
i=1
and condition on

. Under (
6
), conditional on

(Z_{1},Z_{j})
we have
A_{1j}\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}(p^{(n),\mathrm{HT}}_{1j})
and, given all marks,
the edges are independent. Since
(Z_{j})_{j\geq 2}
are i.i.d., it follows by iterated conditioning that,
given
Z_{1}=(w,x)
, the variables
(A_{1j})_{j\geq 2}
are i.i.d. Bernoulli with success probability

\bar{p}_{n}(w,x):=\mathbb{E}\!\left[p^{(n),\mathrm{HT}}_{12}\mid Z_{1}=(w,x)\right],

and hence

D_{1,n}\mid Z_{1}=(w,x)\sim\mathrm{Bin}(n-1,\bar{p}_{n}(w,x)).

Define
q_{n}(u):=1-\exp\{-(\lambda/\rho_{n})k(u)\}
and choose
R<\infty
with

\mathrm{supp}(k)\subset[-R,R]^{d}
, so
\mathrm{supp}(q_{n})\subset[-R,R]^{d}
for all
n
.
Using the density representation
f_{W,X}(w^{\prime},y)=f_{W}(w^{\prime})c_{\theta}(F_{W}(w^{\prime}),y)
and the fact that

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}c_{\theta}(F_{W}(w^{\prime}),y)\,dy=1
for a.e.
w^{\prime}
, we can write

\bar{p}_{n}(w,x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}q_{n}\!\left(\frac{x-y}{\varepsilon_{n}(ww^{\prime})^{1/d}}\right)\,f_{W}(w^{\prime})\,c_{\theta}(F_{W}(w^{\prime}),y)\,dy\,dw^{\prime}.

(17)

Introduce the truncation level

M_{n}(w):=\frac{1}{(2R)^{d}\,\varepsilon_{n}^{d}\,w},

and split
\bar{p}_{n}(w,x)=I_{n}^{\leq}(w,x)+I_{n}^{>}(w,x)
according to
w^{\prime}\leq M_{n}(w)
and
w^{\prime}>M_{n}(w)
.

Remainder term.

Since
0\leq q_{n}\leq 1
and
\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}c_{\theta}(F_{W}(w^{\prime}),y)\,dy=1
,

0\leq I_{n}^{>}(w,x)\leq\int_{M_{n}(w)}^{\infty}f_{W}(w^{\prime})\,dw^{\prime}=\lx@paragraphsign(W^{\prime}>M_{n}(w)).

Because
\mathbb{E}[W]<\infty
, we have
t\,\lx@paragraphsign(W>t)\to 0
as
t\to\infty
; indeed,

t\,\lx@paragraphsign(W>t)\leq 2\int_{t/2}^{\infty}\lx@paragraphsign(W>s)\,ds\to 0.

Moreover,

\frac{n-1}{M_{n}(w)}=(n-1)(2R)^{d}\varepsilon_{n}^{d}w=(2R)^{d}w\,\rho_{n}\Big(1-\frac{1}{n}\Big)\longrightarrow(2R)^{d}w\rho.

Therefore

(n-1)I_{n}^{>}(w,x)\leq\frac{n-1}{M_{n}(w)}\cdot\Big(M_{n}(w)\lx@paragraphsign(W^{\prime}>M_{n}(w))\Big)\longrightarrow 0.

Main term.

For

\varepsilon_{n}(ww^{\prime})^{1/d}R\leq 1/2
, hence the scaled torus fundamental domain
in the change of variables below contains
[-R,R]^{d}
and there is no wrap-around ambiguity on the support
of
q_{n}
. Perform the torus change of variables
y=x-\varepsilon_{n}(ww^{\prime})^{1/d}u
(mod
1

\mathbb{R}^{d}

where

Since
c_{\theta}
is bounded and
\mathbb{E}[W]<\infty

0\leq G_{n}(w,x;u)\leq\|c_{\theta}\|_{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}w^{\prime}f_{W}(w^{\prime})\,dw^{\prime}=\|c_{\theta}\|_{\infty}\,\mathbb{E}[W]<\infty.

For each fixed

and

w^{\prime}f_{W}(w^{\prime})\,c_{\theta}\!\left(F_{W}(w^{\prime}),\,x-\varepsilon_{n}(ww^{\prime})^{1/d}u\right)\mathbf{1}_{\{w^{\prime}\leq M_{n}(w)\}}\longrightarrow w^{\prime}f_{W}(w^{\prime})\,c_{\theta}(F_{W}(w^{\prime}),x)

pointwise in

Moreover,
q_{n}(u)\to q_{\rho}(u)

so dominated convergence in

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}q_{n}(u)\,G_{n}(w,x;u)\,du\longrightarrow m_{1,\theta}(x)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}q_{\rho}(u)\,du=m_{1,\theta}(x)\,\kappa_{2}^{(\lambda)}.

Consequently,

I_{n}^{\leq}(w,x)=\varepsilon_{n}^{d}\,w\,m_{1,\theta}(x)\,\kappa_{2}^{(\lambda)}+o(\varepsilon_{n}^{d}),\qquad n\to\infty.

Since
(n-1)\varepsilon_{n}^{d}=\rho_{n}(1-1/n)\to\rho

(n-1)\bar{p}_{n}(w,x)\to\rho\,\kappa_{2}^{(\lambda)}\,w\,m_{1,\theta}(x)=:\Lambda_{\theta}^{\mathrm{HT}}(w,x),\qquad\text{and}\qquad\bar{p}_{n}(w,x)\to 0.

Therefore, by the binomial-to-Poisson limit,

D_{1,n}\mid Z_{1}=(w,x)\Rightarrow\mathrm{Poisson}\!\bigl(\Lambda_{\theta}^{\mathrm{HT}}(w,x)\bigr).

By exchangeability, the same conclusion holds for any fixed vertex
i
.

CoLaS-HT: tail inheritance.

Let

B_{\theta}(X):=\rho\,\kappa_{2}^{(\lambda)}\,m_{1,\theta}(X),\qquad\Lambda:=\Lambda_{\theta}^{\mathrm{HT}}(W,X)=B_{\theta}(X)W.

Since
W>0
a.s., we have
m_{1,\theta}(X)=\mathbb{E}[W\mid X]>0
a.s. and hence
B_{\theta}(X)>0
a.s.
Moreover,
B_{\theta}
is essentially bounded because
c_{\theta}
is bounded and
\mathbb{E}[W]<\infty
.

Tail of the mixing variable.

By the tower property,

\lx@paragraphsign(\Lambda>t)=\mathbb{E}\!\left[\lx@paragraphsign\!\left(W>\frac{t}{B_{\theta}(X)}\Bigm|X\right)\right].

By the uniform conditional tail assumption, uniformly in
x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
,

\lx@paragraphsign(W>y\mid X=x)=y^{-\alpha}L(y)\,\ell_{\theta}(x)\,(1+o(1)),\qquad y\to\infty.

Since
B_{\theta}(X)\leq\|B_{\theta}\|_{\infty}<\infty
, we have
t/B_{\theta}(X)\geq t/\|B_{\theta}\|_{\infty}\to\infty
,
so we may substitute

and obtain

\lx@paragraphsign\!\left(W>\frac{t}{B_{\theta}(X)}\Bigm|X\right)=t^{-\alpha}\,L\!\left(\frac{t}{B_{\theta}(X)}\right)\,\ell_{\theta}(X)\,B_{\theta}(X)^{\alpha}\,(1+o(1)).

Divide by

and use that

is slowly varying. By Potter bounds for slowly varying

and

Since

is essentially bounded and

is bounded, the random variables

\ell_{\theta}(X)B_{\theta}(X)^{\alpha}\frac{L(t/B_{\theta}(X))}{L(t)}

are dominated by an integrable constant multiple of

,
hence dominated convergence yields

\lx@paragraphsign(\Lambda>t)\sim t^{-\alpha}L(t)\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[\ell_{\theta}(X)\,B_{\theta}(X)^{\alpha}\right]=t^{-\alpha}L(t)\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[\ell_{\theta}(X)\Big(\rho\,\kappa_{2}^{(\lambda)}\,m_{1,\theta}(X)\Big)^{\alpha}\right].

In particular,
\Lambda
is regularly varying with index
\alpha
.

Tail equivalence for the mixed-Poisson degree.

Let

. Fix
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. Standard Chernoff bounds for

and

such that for all

\sup_{\lambda\leq(1-\varepsilon)t}\lx@paragraphsign(\mathrm{Poisson}(\lambda)\geq t)\leq e^{-c_{\varepsilon}t},\qquad\sup_{\lambda\geq(1+\varepsilon)t}\lx@paragraphsign(\mathrm{Poisson}(\lambda)\leq t)\leq e^{-c_{\varepsilon}t}.

Consequently, for all

\lx@paragraphsign(D^{\mathrm{HT}}\geq t)\leq\lx@paragraphsign(\Lambda\geq(1-\varepsilon)t)+e^{-c_{\varepsilon}t},\qquad\lx@paragraphsign(D^{\mathrm{HT}}\geq t)\geq\lx@paragraphsign(\Lambda\geq(1+\varepsilon)t)-e^{-c_{\varepsilon}t}.

Divide by
\lx@paragraphsign(\Lambda\geq t)
and let

. Since
\Lambda
is regularly varying with index
\alpha
,

\frac{\lx@paragraphsign(\Lambda\geq(1\pm\varepsilon)t)}{\lx@paragraphsign(\Lambda\geq t)}\to(1\pm\varepsilon)^{-\alpha},\qquad\frac{e^{-c_{\varepsilon}t}}{\lx@paragraphsign(\Lambda\geq t)}\to 0.

Letting

yields
\lx@paragraphsign(D^{\mathrm{HT}}\geq t)\sim\lx@paragraphsign(\Lambda\geq t)
, and combining with the tail
asymptotic for
\Lambda
proves the stated tail inheritance for

.
∎

Proof of Theorem
5
.

Write
Z_{i}:=(W_{i},X_{i})
. Conditional on

Step 2: Limit of

Fix

. By exchangeability of
(Z_{2},\dots,Z_{n})
given

Proof of Theorem

Fix

. Let
(I,J)
be uniform on
\{(i,j):1\leq i<j\leq n\}
and independent of all marks and edge coin flips.
Throughout the proof we work under the conditional law given
\{A_{IJ}=1\}
, which is well-defined for all large
n
since

\alpha_{n}=\mathbb{P}(A_{IJ}=1)>0

1. Endpoint marks under the edge–Palm law.

Let

be bounded measurable. By the tower property and the model definition,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\!\big[f(Z_{I},Z_{J})\mathbf{1}\{A_{IJ}=1\}\big]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\!\Big[\mathbb{E}\!\big[f(Z_{I},Z_{J})\mathbf{1}\{A_{IJ}=1\}\mid Z_{I},Z_{J}\big]\Big]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\!\Big[f(Z_{I},Z_{J})\,\mathbb{P}(A_{IJ}=1\mid Z_{I},Z_{J})\Big]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\!\Big[f(Z_{I},Z_{J})\,p^{(n)}(Z_{I},Z_{J})\Big]

\displaystyle=\iint f(z,z^{\prime})\,p^{(n)}(z,z^{\prime})\,\mu_{\theta}(dz)\mu_{\theta}(dz^{\prime}).

Since
\mathbb{P}(A_{IJ}=1)=\alpha_{n}

\mathbb{E}\!\big[f(Z_{I},Z_{J})\mid A_{IJ}=1\big]=\frac{1}{\alpha_{n}}\iint f(z,z^{\prime})\,p^{(n)}(z,z^{\prime})\,\mu_{\theta}(dz)\mu_{\theta}(dz^{\prime})=\mathbb{E}_{\nu_{\theta,n}}[f(Z,Z^{\prime})],

and hence

(Z_{I},Z_{J})\mid\{A_{IJ}=1\}\ \sim\ \nu_{\theta,n}.

2. Remove the conditioned edge from the degrees.

Since
A_{IJ}=1
on the conditioning event,

D_{I}=1+\sum_{k\neq I,J}A_{Ik},\qquad D_{J}=1+\sum_{k\neq I,J}A_{Jk}.

Whenever the conditional correlation is defined, it is invariant under translation, hence

r_{n}=\mathrm{Corr}(D_{I},D_{J}\mid A_{IJ}=1)=\mathrm{Corr}(\widetilde{D},\widetilde{D}^{\prime}\mid A_{IJ}=1),

where

\widetilde{D}:=\sum_{k\neq I,J}A_{Ik},\qquad\widetilde{D}^{\prime}:=\sum_{k\neq I,J}A_{Jk}.

3. Conditional distribution given endpoint marks.

Fix
(z,z^{\prime})\in\mathcal{Z}^{2}
and condition on
(Z_{I},Z_{J})=(z,z^{\prime})
under
\{A_{IJ}=1\}
.
Given
(Z_{I},Z_{J})
, the event
\{A_{IJ}=1\}
depends only on the
(I,J)
edge coin flip and is independent of the remaining marks,

\{Z_{k}:k\neq I,J\}

\mu_{\theta}
. Conditional on all marks, edges are independent.

For any fixed

\mathbb{P}(A_{Ik}=1\mid Z_{I}=z,Z_{J}=z^{\prime})=\int p^{(n)}(z,u)\,\mu_{\theta}(du)=:\bar{p}_{\theta,n}(z),

and, using conditional independence of
A_{Ik}
and

Moreover, because
(Z_{k})_{k\neq I,J}
are i.i.d. and the edge coin flips are independent across pairs,
the pairs
(A_{Ik},A_{Jk})
are i.i.d. over
k\neq I,J

(Z_{I},Z_{J})=(z,z^{\prime})

4. Conditional moments of

By the previous step,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Cov}(\widetilde{D},\widetilde{D}^{\prime}\mid Z_{I}=z,Z_{J}=z^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\neq I,J}\mathrm{Cov}(A_{Ik},A_{Jk}\mid Z_{I}=z,Z_{J}=z^{\prime})

\displaystyle=(n-2)\Big(\bar{p}^{(2)}_{\theta,n}(z,z^{\prime})-\bar{p}_{\theta,n}(z)\bar{p}_{\theta,n}(z^{\prime})\Big).

Using
\Lambda_{\theta,n}(z)=(n-1)\bar{p}_{\theta,n}(z)

\Gamma_{\theta,n}(z,z^{\prime})=(n-2)\bar{p}^{(2)}_{\theta,n}(z,z^{\prime})

\mathrm{Cov}(\widetilde{D},\widetilde{D}^{\prime}\mid Z_{I}=z,Z_{J}=z^{\prime})=\Gamma_{\theta,n}(z,z^{\prime})-(n-2)\bar{p}_{\theta,n}(z)\bar{p}_{\theta,n}(z^{\prime}).

(27)

5. Unconditional covariance under the edge–Palm law.

Under
\{A_{IJ}=1\}
we have
(Z_{I},Z_{J})\sim\nu_{\theta,n}

By the law of total covariance (with
(Z,Z^{\prime})\sim\nu_{\theta,n}

\displaystyle\mathrm{Cov}(\widetilde{D},\widetilde{D}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\nu_{\theta,n}}\!\big[\mathrm{Cov}(\widetilde{D},\widetilde{D}^{\prime}\mid Z,Z^{\prime})\big]+\mathrm{Cov}_{\nu_{\theta,n}}\!\big(\mathbb{E}[\widetilde{D}\mid Z],\,\mathbb{E}[\widetilde{D}^{\prime}\mid Z^{\prime}]\big).

From (

.

Write

Step 1: Uniform convergence

.

For any

\displaystyle Q_{n}(\theta)-Q(\theta)

\displaystyle=\|\widehat{m}_{n}-m(\theta)\|_{2}^{2}-\|m_{0}-m(\theta)\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=(\widehat{m}_{n}-m_{0})^{\top}\big(\widehat{m}_{n}+m_{0}-2m(\theta)\big).

By Cauchy–Schwarz,

|Q_{n}(\theta)-Q(\theta)|\leq\|\widehat{m}_{n}-m_{0}\|_{2}\ \big\|\widehat{m}_{n}+m_{0}-2m(\theta)\big\|_{2}.

Taking
\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}
and using

\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}|Q_{n}(\theta)-Q(\theta)|\leq\|\widehat{m}_{n}-m_{0}\|_{2}\,\Big(\|\widehat{m}_{n}\|_{2}+\|m_{0}\|_{2}+2M\Big).

Assumption (A1) implies

Hence

Step 2: Consistency.

By construction
Q(\theta)\geq 0
and
Q(\theta)=0
iff
m(\theta)=m_{0}
.
By (A2),
\theta_{0}
is the unique minimizer of
Q
on
\Theta

Fix

The set
\{\theta\in\Theta:\|\theta-\theta_{0}\|_{2}\geq\varepsilon\}

Uniqueness of the minimizer implies

On

Assume now the additional conditions.
Let

\theta_{0}

is

By consistency,

On the event

\nabla Q_{n}(\widehat{\theta}_{n})=-2\,Dm(\widehat{\theta}_{n})^{\top}\big(\widehat{m}_{n}-m(\widehat{\theta}_{n})\big)=0.

Let

U_{n}:=\widehat{m}_{n}-m_{0}

\mathbb{E}[W_{1}^{2}W_{2}^{2}\mid X=x]=m_{2,\theta}(x)^{2}

\iint\mathbb{E}[t_{1}t_{2}t_{3}\mid X=x]\,du\,dv=\Big(\frac{\lambda}{\rho}\Big)^{3}w^{2}m_{2,\theta}(x)^{2}\iint k(u)k(v)k(u-v)\,du\,dv=\Big(\frac{\lambda}{\rho}\Big)^{3}w^{2}m_{2,\theta}(x)^{2}\,\kappa_{3},

and therefore

\frac{\rho^{2}}{2}\iint\mathbb{E}[t_{1}t_{2}t_{3}\mid X=x]\,du\,dv=\frac{\lambda^{3}}{2\rho}\,\kappa_{3}\,w^{2}\,m_{2,\theta}(x)^{2}.

For the remainder terms, use
k(u)^{2}\leq\|k\|_{\infty}k(u)
and
k(u-v)^{2}\leq\|k\|_{\infty}k(u-v)

(since
k\geq 0
and bounded). Again by conditional independence,

\mathbb{E}[W_{1}^{3}W_{2}^{2}\mid X=x]=m_{3,\theta}(x)m_{2,\theta}(x),\qquad\mathbb{E}[W_{1}^{3}W_{2}^{3}\mid X=x]=m_{3,\theta}(x)^{2}.

Hence

\displaystyle\iint\mathbb{E}[t_{1}^{2}t_{2}t_{3}\mid X=x]\,du\,dv

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{\lambda}{\rho}\Big)^{4}w^{3}m_{3,\theta}(x)m_{2,\theta}(x)\iint k(u)^{2}k(v)k(u-v)\,du\,dv

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\frac{\lambda}{\rho}\Big)^{4}w^{3}m_{3,\theta}(x)m_{2,\theta}(x)\,\|k\|_{\infty}\,\kappa_{3},

and similarly

\iint\mathbb{E}[t_{1}t_{2}^{2}t_{3}\mid X=x]\,du\,dv\leq\Big(\frac{\lambda}{\rho}\Big)^{4}w^{3}m_{3,\theta}(x)m_{2,\theta}(x)\,\|k\|_{\infty}\,\kappa_{3},

\iint\mathbb{E}[t_{1}t_{2}t_{3}^{2}\mid X=x]\,du\,dv\leq\Big(\frac{\lambda}{\rho}\Big)^{4}w^{2}m_{3,\theta}(x)^{2}\,\|k\|_{\infty}\,\kappa_{3}.

Combining these bounds completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 2

(Linear (small-
\lambda
) approximation)
.

Assume Assumptions
1
and
5
. Then for every
w\geq 0
and every

x
such that
m_{2,\theta}(x)<\infty
,

\displaystyle\Big|\Lambda_{\theta}(w,x)-\lambda\,\kappa_{2}\,w\,m_{1,\theta}(x)\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2\rho}\,w^{2}\,m_{2,\theta}(x)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}k(u)^{2}\,du

(32)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2\rho}\,w^{2}\,m_{2,\theta}(x)\,\|k\|_{\infty}\,\kappa_{2}.

(33)

Consequently, for any compact set
K\subset(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{T}^{d}
,

\sup_{(w,x)\in K}\Big|\Lambda_{\theta}(w,x)-\lambda\,\kappa_{2}\,w\,m_{1,\theta}(x)\Big|=O(\lambda^{2}),\qquad\lambda\downarrow 0.

Proof of Corollary
2
.

Fix a compact set
K\subset(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{T}^{d}
and let
(w,x)\in K
.
For
u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
define

t(u):=\frac{\lambda}{\rho}\,wW^{\prime}\,k(u),\qquad q_{w,x}(u;W^{\prime}):=1-e^{-t(u)},

where
W^{\prime}
is an independent copy of
W
conditional on
X=x
(so that

\mathbb{E}[(W^{\prime})^{p}\mid X=x]=m_{p,\theta}(x)
for
p\geq 1
).
Since
w>0
,
W^{\prime}>0
and
k\geq 0
, we have
t(u)\geq 0
.

For all
t\geq 0
,

\big|(1-e^{-t})-t\big|\leq\frac{t^{2}}{2}.

Applying this with
t=t(u)
and taking conditional expectations given
X=x
yields

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}\!\left[q_{w,x}(u;W^{\prime})\mid X=x\right]-\mathbb{E}\!\left[\frac{\lambda}{\rho}\,wW^{\prime}k(u)\mid X=x\right]\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left(\frac{\lambda}{\rho}\,wW^{\prime}k(u)\right)^{2}\Bigm|X=x\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2\rho^{2}}\,w^{2}\,m_{2,\theta}(x)\,k(u)^{2}.

Moreover,

\mathbb{E}\!\left[\frac{\lambda}{\rho}\,wW^{\prime}k(u)\mid X=x\right]=\frac{\lambda}{\rho}\,w\,m_{1,\theta}(x)\,k(u).

Because
0\leq k(u)\leq\|k\|_{\infty}
, we have
k(u)^{2}\leq\|k\|_{\infty}\,k(u)
, hence

\left|\mathbb{E}\!\left[q_{w,x}(u;W^{\prime})\mid X=x\right]-\frac{\lambda}{\rho}\,w\,m_{1,\theta}(x)\,k(u)\right|\leq\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2\rho^{2}}\,w^{2}\,m_{2,\theta}(x)\,\|k\|_{\infty}\,k(u).

Integrating over
u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and multiplying by
\rho
, and using Tonelli’s theorem
(the integrand is nonnegative) and
\kappa_{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}k(u)\,du<\infty
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\Lambda_{\theta}(w,x)-\lambda\,\kappa_{2}\,w\,m_{1,\theta}(x)\right|

\displaystyle=\rho\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{E}[q_{w,x}(u;W^{\prime})\mid X=x]\,du-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\lambda}{\rho}\,w\,m_{1,\theta}(x)\,k(u)\,du\right|

\displaystyle\leq\rho\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2\rho^{2}}\,w^{2}\,m_{2,\theta}(x)\,\|k\|_{\infty}\,k(u)\,du

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2\rho}\,w^{2}\,m_{2,\theta}(x)\,\|k\|_{\infty}\,\kappa_{2},

Finally, on
K
we have
w\leq w_{\max}<\infty
, and by Assumption 3 the function
m_{2,\theta}

is essentially bounded on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
; fix any bounded version (still denoted
m_{2,\theta}
),
so that
\sup_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}}m_{2,\theta}(x)<\infty
.
Therefore the bound above is
O(\lambda^{2})
uniformly over
(w,x)\in K
, and hence
o(1)

uniformly on
K
as
\lambda\to 0
. This proves the claim.
∎

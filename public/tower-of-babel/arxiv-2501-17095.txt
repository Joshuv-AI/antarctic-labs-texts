Robust purely optical signatures of Floquet-Bloch states in crystals
Vishal Tiwari, Ignacio Franco

Abstract
Strong light-matter interactions enable triggering on-demand novel functionalities in materials. For periodically driven solids, the Floquet-Bloch states have emerged as the natural states to characterize the physical properties of these non-equilibrium systems. However, robust signatures of the Floquet-Bloch states are needed as common experimental conditions, such as pulsed laser excitation and dissipative many-body dynamics can disrupt their formation and survival. Here, we identify a tell-tale signature of Floquet-Bloch states in the linear optical response of laser-dressed solids that remains prominent even in the presence of strong spectral congestion of bulk matter. To do so, we introduce a computationally efficient strategy to finally capture the response properties of realistic laser-dressed solids, and use it to compute the optical absorption of ZnO using a model of full-dimensionality. The computations reveal intense, spectrally isolated, absorption/stimulated emission features at mid-infrared energies present for a wide range of laser-driving conditions that arise due to the hybridization of the Floquet-Bloch states. As such, these spectral features open a purely optical pathway to investigate the birth and survival of Floquet-Bloch states while avoiding the experimental challenges of fully reconstructing the band structure.

We simulate a physical situation in which a continuous wave laser of arbitrary intensity and frequency drives a solid of arbitrary band-structure and dimensionality out of equilibrium. The effective absorption properties of this laser-dressed solid are then probed by a weak continuous wave laser source. Below-band gap absorption overlaps in energy with dominant exciton features. In turn, absorption sidebands are obscured in Fig.

To summarize, we presented the first computational demonstration of the optical absorption spectrum of a laser-dressed solid described using realistic DFT electronic structure of full dimensionality. We found that the absorption spectra of a laser-dressed ZnO crystal predicts prominent absorption/stimulated emissions at mid-infrared frequencies that are robust to drive laser parameters, survive the spectral congestion in solids, and are well isolated from the field free band-edge transitions and possible excitonic features. Using a minimalistic two-band model, we showed that these features arise due to the hybridization of the Floquet-Bloch modes, time-and space-periodic functions that define the Floquet-Bloch states. We propose that these features provide a robust purely optical tell-tale signature of the formation of Floquet-Bloch states in bulk matter needed to study Floquet engineering in realistic conditions through simple electroabsorption spectroscopy, thus avoiding full band-structure reconstruction.

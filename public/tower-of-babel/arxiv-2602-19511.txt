Intrinsic linking of a simplicial n n -complex embedded in ℝ 2 ​ n \mathbb{R}^{2n} Thanks: The author was supported by JSPS KAKENHI Grant Number 25K06987.
Ryo Nikkuni

Abstract
. We demonstrate the existence of minimal simplicial n n -complexes which inevitably contain a nonsplittable two-component link formed by an ( n − 1 ) (n-1) -sphere and an n n -sphere in any embedding into ℝ 2 ​ n \mathbb{R}^{2n} . This provides a higher-dimensional generalization of graphs that are not non-separating planar.

Introduction

Throughout this paper, we work in the piecewise linear category. For the fundamentals of piecewise-linear topology, we refer the reader to
[
10
]
,
[
15
]
. Let
K
be a finite simplicial
n
-complex, which we identify with its polyhedron in this context. For an integer
k\leq n
, let
\varDelta^{k}(K)
denote the set of all
k
-simplices in
K
. Then the subcomplex
\bigcup_{l\leq k}\varDelta^{l}(K)
of
K
is called the
k
-skeleton
of
K
and denoted by
K^{k}
. Let
f
be an embedding of
K
into
\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Let
\Lambda^{p,q}(K)
be the set of all unordered pairs
\{\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\}
of mutually disjoint subcomplexes of
K
such that
\gamma
is homeomorphic to the
p
-sphere and
\gamma^{\prime}
is homeomorphic to the
q
-sphere. We identify any pair
(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})
in
\Lambda^{p,q}(K)
with the disjoint union
\gamma\sqcup\gamma^{\prime}
. Then, for any
\lambda=\gamma\sqcup\gamma^{\prime}\in\Lambda^{p,q}(K)
, the image
f(\lambda)=f(\gamma)\sqcup f(\gamma^{\prime})
forms a two-component link in
f(K)
consisting of a
p
-sphere and a
q
-sphere. If
p+q=m-1
, the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-linking number

{\rm lk}_{2}(L)={\rm lk}_{2}(S^{p},S^{q})={\rm lk}_{2}(S^{q},S^{p})\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
of a two-component link
L=S^{p}\sqcup S^{q}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
is well-defined (cf.
[
17
]
). If
m=2n+1
, there are known examples of simplicial
n
-complexes with the following property: every embedding into
\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}
necessarily contains a two-component link of
n
-spheres with
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-linking number
1
. Such examples were constructed by Segal–Spież
[
18
]
, Lovasz–Schrijver
[
11
]
, Skopenkov
[
19
]
, Taniyama
[
20
]
, and more recently by the author
[
12
]
. In particular, the case of
n=1
is famous as the Conway–Gordon–Sachs theorem (
[
1
]
,
[
16
]
), and such a simplicial
1
-complex (i.e., a graph) is said to be
intrinsically linked
.

Our purpose in this paper is to present an example of a minimal simplicial
n
-complex that possesses such an intrinsic linking property with respect to embeddings into
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
. Let us introduce three simplicial
n
-complexes
N_{1}^{(n)}
,
N_{2}^{(n)}
and
N_{3}^{(n)}
as follows. First, let
\sigma_{m}=|a_{0}a_{1}\cdots a_{m}|
be an
m
-simplex whose vertices are
a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}
. This can be naturally regarded as a simplicial
m
-complex consisting of itself and all its faces. In this setting, the
k
-skeleton of
\sigma_{m}
is standardly denoted by
\sigma_{m}^{k}
for
k\leq m
. Then we define

\displaystyle N_{1}^{(n)}=\sigma_{2n+2}^{n}\setminus\{|a_{0}a_{j_{1}}\cdots a_{j_{n}}|\ |\ 1\leq j_{1}<j_{2}<\cdots<j_{n}\leq 2n+2\}.

In other words,
N_{1}^{(n)}
is the subcomplex of
\sigma_{2n+2}^{n}
obtained by removing all
n
-simplices that contain the vertex
a_{0}
. Next, for a positive integer
k
, let
[k]^{*m}
denote the
m
-fold join of
k
points. Explicitly, we set

\displaystyle[k]^{*m}=\{a_{0}^{0},a_{1}^{0},\ldots,a_{k-1}^{0}\}*\{a_{0}^{1},a_{1}^{1},\ldots,a_{k-1}^{1}\}*\cdots*\{a_{0}^{n},a_{1}^{n},\ldots,a_{k-1}^{n}\}.

Note that
[k]^{*n+1}
can naturally be regarded as a simplicial
n
-complex. Then we define

\displaystyle N_{2}^{(n)}=[3]^{*n+1}\setminus\{|a_{0}^{0}a_{j_{1}}^{1}\cdots a_{j_{n}}^{n}|\ |\ j_{1},j_{2},\ldots,j_{n}\in\{0,1,2\}\}.

In other words,
N_{2}^{(n)}
is the subcomplex of
[3]^{*n+1}
obtained by removing all
n
-simplices that contain the vertex
a_{0}^{0}
. Finally, we define

\displaystyle N_{3}^{(n)}=\sigma_{2n+2}^{n}\setminus\varDelta^{n}(\sigma_{n+1}).

In other words,
N_{3}^{(n)}
is the subcomplex of
\sigma_{2n+2}^{n}
obtained by removing all
n
-simplices contained in
\sigma_{n+1}=|a_{0}a_{1}\cdots a_{n+1}|
. As we will see in the next section, it is well known that neither
\sigma_{2n+2}^{n}
nor
[3]^{*n+1}
cannot be embedded in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}
(Theorem
2-1
). On the other hand, it is also known that any proper subcomplex of
\sigma_{2n+2}^{n}
and of
[3]^{*n+1}
can be embedded in
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
(Grünbaum
[
7
]
). Therefore, each of
N_{1}^{(n)}
,
N_{2}^{(n)}
and
N_{3}^{(n)}
is embeddable in
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
. Then we have the following:

Theorem 1-1
.

Let
n
be a positive integer, and let
K
be a simplicial
n
-complex that is
N_{1}^{(n)}
,
N_{2}^{(n)}
or
N_{3}^{(n)}
. Then, for every embedding
f
of
K
into
{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}
, there exists an element
\lambda
in
\Lambda^{n-1,n}(K)
such that
{\rm lk}_{2}(f(\lambda))=1
.

Therefore,
N_{1}^{(n)}
,
N_{2}^{(n)}
and
N_{3}^{(n)}
are simplicial
n
-complexes that are ‘intrinsically linked’ with respect to embeddings into
{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}
. We note that the simplicial
n
-complex
\sigma_{2n+2}^{n}\setminus\{|a_{0}a_{1}\cdots a_{n}|\}
has already been shown to satisfy this property (Freedman–Krushkal
[
5
, Proposition 4.1]
; see also Freedman–Krushkal–Teichner
[
6
, Lemma 6]
). However, both our
N_{1}^{(n)}
and
N_{3}^{(n)}
are proper subcomplexes of this complex. Moreover, the complexes
N_{1}^{(n)}
,
N_{2}^{(n)}
and
N_{3}^{(n)}
are minimal in the following sense among all simplicial
n
-complexes possessing this property.

Proposition 1-2
.

Let
N
be a proper subcomplex of
N_{1}^{(n)}
,
N_{2}^{(n)}
or
N_{3}^{(n)}
. Then there exists an embedding
f:N\to{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}
such that
{\rm lk}_{2}(f(\lambda))=0
for any element
\lambda
in
\Lambda^{n-1,n}(N)
.

A central role in the proof of Theorem
1-1
is played by the van Kampen–Flores theorem. In the next section, we recall this theorem and present proofs of Theorem
1-1
and Proposition
1-2
using it. The van Kampen–Flores theorem is also used to construct examples of simplicial
n
-complexes that are intrinsically linked with respect to embeddings into
\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}
, see Nikkuni
[
12
]
.

Remark 1-3
.

A graph
G
is said to be
non-separating planar
if there exists an embedding
f:G\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that, for every cycle
\gamma
in
G
, all vertices of
G
not lying on
\gamma
belong to the same connected component of
\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus f(\gamma)
. Equivalently,
G
is not non-separating planar if and only if for every embedding
f:G\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
, the image
f(G)
contains a two-component link consisting of a
0
-sphere and a
1
-sphere with
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-linking number
1
. Dehkordi–Farr proved in
[
2
]
that a graph is non-separating planar if and only if it contains no subgraph homeomorphic to
K_{1}\sqcup K_{4}
,
K_{1}\sqcup K_{2,3}
, or
K_{1,1,3}
as illustrated in Fig.
1.1
. They further applied this characterization to the study of linkless embeddings of graphs in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. For related results, see Fowler–Li–Pavelescu
[
4
]
, Pavelescu–Pavelescu
[
13
]
. Note that
N_{1}^{(1)}
is isomorphic to
K_{1}\sqcup K_{4}
,
N_{2}^{(1)}
is isomorphic to
K_{1}\sqcup K_{2,3}
, and
N_{3}^{(1)}
is isomorphic to
K_{1,1,3}
. Thus, our simplicial
n
-complexes
N_{1}^{(n)}
,
N_{2}^{(n)}
and
N_{3}^{(n)}
may be regarded as higher-dimensional analogues of the minimal subgraphs that prevent a graph from being non-separating planar.

Proof of Theorem
1-1

First, recall that for any positive integer
n
, neither
\sigma_{2n+2}^{n}
nor
[3]^{*n+1}
can be embedded in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}
. It is well-known that every simplicial
n
-complex
K
can be generically immersed into
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
, where an immersion
\varphi
of
K
into
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
is said to be
generic
if all singularities of
\varphi(K)
are transversal double points occurring between the interiors of pairs of
n
-simplices. For a generic immersion
\varphi
of
K
into
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
and for any two mutually disjoint
n
-simplices
\sigma,\tau\in\varDelta^{n}(K)
, let
l(\varphi(\sigma),\varphi(\tau))
denote the number of double points between
\varphi(\sigma)
and
\varphi(\tau)
. Then the following fact is known as the
van Kampen–Flores theorem
.

Theorem 2-1
.

(van Kampen
[
9
]
, Flores
[
3
]
)

Let
n
be a positive integer, and let
K
be a simplicial
n
-complex that is
\sigma_{2n+2}^{n}
or
[3]^{*n+1}
. Then for every generic immersion
\varphi
of
K
into
{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}
, the following holds:

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s,s^{\prime}\in\varDelta^{n}(K)\\
s\cap s^{\prime}=\varnothing\end{subarray}}l(\varphi(s),\varphi(s^{\prime}))\equiv 1\pmod{2}.

In Theorem
2-1
, the case of
n=1
corresponds to the well-known fact that neither
K_{5}
nor
K_{3,3}
can be embedded in the plane.

Proof of Theorem
1-1
.

We first consider the case
K=N_{1}^{(n)}
. In what follows, for an
m
-simplex
s
, we will also denote its
(m-1)
-skeleton
s^{m-1}
by
\partial s
. We define collections
\Gamma^{n-1}
and
\Gamma^{n}
of
(n-1)
and
n
-subcomplexes of
N_{1}^{(n)}
, respectively, as follows:

\displaystyle\Gamma^{n-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\partial s\mid s\in\varDelta^{n}(\sigma_{2n+2}^{n}),\,a_{0}\in s\right\},

\displaystyle\Gamma^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\partial t\mid t\in\varDelta^{n+1}(\sigma_{2n+2}),\,a_{0}\notin t\right\}.

Then every
\lambda\in\Lambda^{n-1,n}(N_{1}^{(n)})
is the disjoint union of an element
\gamma
of
\Gamma^{n-1}
and an element
\gamma^{\prime}
of
\Gamma^{n}
, see Fig.
2.1
for
n=2
. Let
f:N_{1}^{(n)}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}
be an embedding. This can be extended to a generic immersion
\hat{f}:\sigma_{2n+2}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{2n}
such that, for any two
n
-simplices
s,s^{\prime}\in\varDelta^{n}(\sigma_{2n+2}^{n})
with
a_{0}\in s
and
a_{0}\notin s^{\prime}
, the images
\hat{f}(s)
and
\hat{f}(s^{\prime})
intersect transversely at finitely many double points, all of which occur in the interiors of both
n
-simplices. Note that for any
\lambda=\gamma\sqcup\gamma^{\prime}\in\Lambda^{n-1,n}(N_{1}^{(n)})
with
\gamma=\partial s
,
s\in\varDelta^{n}(\sigma_{2n+2}^{n})
and
a_{0}\in s
, we have

(2.1)

\displaystyle{\rm lk}_{2}(f(\gamma),f(\gamma^{\prime}))\equiv\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\varDelta^{n}(\gamma^{\prime})}l(\hat{f}(s),\hat{f}(s^{\prime}))\pmod{2}.

On the other hand, for any two disjoint
n
-simplices
s,s^{\prime}\in\varDelta^{n}(\sigma_{2n+2}^{n})
with
a_{0}\in s
and
a_{0}\notin s^{\prime}
, there uniquely exists an element
\lambda=\gamma\sqcup\gamma^{\prime}\in\Lambda^{n-1,n}(N_{1}^{(n)})
such that
\gamma=\partial s
and
\gamma^{\prime}\supset s^{\prime}
. Then, by (
2.1
) and Theorem
2-1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda^{n-1,n}(N_{1}^{(n)})}{\rm lk}_{2}(f(\lambda))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\sqcup\gamma^{\prime}\in\Lambda^{n-1,n}(N_{1}^{(n)})\\
\gamma\in\Gamma^{n-1},\,\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma^{n}\end{subarray}}{\rm lk}_{2}(f(\gamma),f(\gamma^{\prime}))

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\sqcup\gamma^{\prime}\in\Lambda^{n-1,n}(N_{1}^{(n)})\\
\gamma=\partial s,\,s\in\varDelta^{n}(\sigma_{2n+2}^{n}),\,a_{0}\in s\end{subarray}}\bigg(\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\varDelta^{n}(\gamma^{\prime})}l(\hat{f}(s),\hat{f}(s^{\prime}))\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s,\,s^{\prime}\in\varDelta^{n}(\sigma_{2n+2}^{n})\\
s\cap s^{\prime}=\varnothing\end{subarray}}l(\hat{f}(s),\hat{f}(s^{\prime}))

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle 1\pmod{2}.

Thus, there exists some
\lambda
in
\Lambda^{n-1,n}(N_{1}^{(n)})
such that
{\rm lk}_{2}(f(\lambda))=1
.

Next, we consider the case

. We define collections

and
\Gamma^{n}
of
(n-1)
and
n

\displaystyle\Gamma^{n-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\partial s\mid s\in\varDelta^{n}([3]^{*n+1}),\,a_{0}^{0}\in s\right\},

\displaystyle\Gamma^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\left\{a_{1}^{0},a_{2}^{0}\right\}*\left\{a_{i_{1}}^{1},a_{j_{1}}^{1}\right\}*\cdots*\left\{a_{i_{n}}^{n},a_{j_{n}}^{n}\right\}\mid 0\leq i_{q}<j_{q}\leq 2,\,q\geq 1\right\}.

Note that the
(n+1)
-fold join of two points is homeomorphic to the
n
-sphere. Then every
\lambda\in\Lambda^{n-1,n}(N_{2}^{(n)})
is the disjoint union of an element
\gamma
of
\Gamma^{n-1}
and an element
\gamma^{\prime}
of
\Gamma^{n}
, see Fig.
2.2
for
n=2
. Let

such that, for any two

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda^{n-1,n}(N_{2}^{(n)})}{\rm lk}_{2}(f(\lambda))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\sqcup\gamma^{\prime}\in\Lambda^{n-1,n}(N_{2}^{(n)})\\
\gamma\in\Gamma^{n-1},\,\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma^{n}\end{subarray}}{\rm lk}_{2}(f(\gamma),f(\gamma^{\prime}))

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\sqcup\gamma^{\prime}\in\Lambda^{n-1,n}(N_{2}^{(n)})\\

Proof of Proposition
1-2
.

Let
H
be either
\sigma_{2n+2}^{n}
or
[3]^{*n+1}
. It is well known that there exists a generic immersion
h:H\to\mathbb{R}^{2n}
with exactly one double point, which occurs between the interiors of a pair of disjoint
n
-simplices. By the symmetry of
H
, we may assume without loss of generality that this double point occurs between
h(|a_{0}a_{1}\cdots a_{n}|)
and
h(|a_{n+1}a_{n+2}\cdots a_{2n+1}|)
if
H=\sigma_{2n+2}^{n}
, and between
h(|a_{0}^{0}a_{1}^{0}\cdots a_{n}^{0}|)
and
h(|a_{0}^{1}a_{1}^{1}\cdots a_{n}^{1}|)
if
H=[3]^{*n+1}
. For each
i=1,2,3
, let
h_{i}:N_{i}^{(n)}\to\mathbb{R}^{2n}
be the restriction of
h
to the subcomplex
N_{i}^{(n)}\subset H
. Each
h_{i}
is an embedding, and the image
h(N_{i}^{(n)})
contains exactly one two-component link
L_{i}
consisting of an
(n-1)
-sphere and an
n
-sphere with
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-linking number
1
. These links are given explicitly as follows:

\bullet
for
i=1
and
3
:
L_{i}=h(\partial|a_{0}a_{1}\ldots a_{n}|)\sqcup h(\partial|a_{n+1}a_{n+2}\cdots a_{2n+2}|)
,

\bullet
for
i=2
:
L_{2}=h(\partial|a_{0}^{0}a_{1}^{0}\cdots a_{n}^{0}|)\sqcup h(\{a_{1}^{0},a_{2}^{0}\}*\{a_{1}^{1},a_{2}^{1}\}*\cdots*\{a_{1}^{n},a_{2}^{n}\})
.

We now show that if
N
is any proper subcomplex of
N_{i}^{(n)}
(
i=1,2,3
), then
h(N)
does not contain any two-component link consisting of an
(n-1)
-sphere and an
n
-sphere with
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-linking number
1
. To see this, it is sufficient to check the following cases:

(
1
)

For
N_{1}^{(n)}
, it suffices to consider
N=N_{1}^{(n)}\setminus\{|a_{0}a_{1}\cdots a_{n-1}|\}
or
N_{1}^{(n)}\setminus\{|a_{n+1}a_{n+2}\cdots a_{2n+1}|\}
. In both cases,
h(N)
does not contain
L_{1}
.

(
2
)

For
N_{2}^{(n)}
, it suffices to consider
N=N_{2}^{(n)}\setminus\{|a_{0}^{0}a_{0}^{1}\cdots a_{0}^{n-1}|\}
or
N_{2}^{(n)}\setminus\{|a_{1}^{0}a_{1}^{1}\cdots a_{1}^{n}|\}
. In both cases,
h(N)
does not contain
L_{2}
.

(
3
)

For
N_{3}^{(n)}
, it suffices to consider
N=N_{3}^{(n)}\setminus\{|a_{n+1}a_{n+2}\cdots a_{2n+1}|\}
. In this case,
h(N)
does not contain
L_{3}
.

Therefore, in all cases, no such link appears in
h(N)
for any proper subcomplex
N
of
N_{i}^{(n)}
. This completes the proof.
∎

Question 2-2
.

Let
K
be a simplicial
n
-complex which is embeddable in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}
.
For
n\geq 2
, suppose
K
is ‘intrinsically linked’ in the sense of Theorem
1-1
. Does
K
necessarily contain a subcomplex homeomorphic to
N_{1}^{(n)}
,
N_{2}^{(n)}
or
N_{3}^{(n)}
? As mentioned in Remark
1-3
, the answer is affirmative if
n=1
.

Remark 2-3
.

In particular, for
n=2
, it is known that there exists a nonsplittable link consisting of two
2
-spheres in
{\mathbb{R}}^{4}
(van Kampen
[
8
]
, Zeeman
[
21
]
, see also
[
14
, pp. 88–95]
). The
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-linking number in the above sense is not defined for such links, and it is not so easy to prove that they are nonsplittable. For a simplicial
2
-complex
K
to contain a disjoint union of two
2
-spheres, at least eight
0
-simplices are required. As mentioned in Section 1, actually any embedding of
\sigma_{7}^{2}
into
{\mathbb{R}}^{5}
contains a two-component link of
2
-spheres whose
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-linking number is
1
, but
\sigma_{7}^{2}
cannot be embedded into
{\mathbb{R}}^{4}
. The author is not certain, but it seems doubtful that there exists a simplicial
2
-complex whose every embedding into
{\mathbb{R}}^{4}
contains a nonsplittable two-component link of
2
-spheres.

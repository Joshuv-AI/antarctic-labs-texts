Sensitivity dependence of the Navier-Stokes turbulence of a two-dimensional Rayleigh-Bénard convection on time-step
Shijie Qin 1,2, Kun Xu 1, Shijun Liao 2,3

Abstract
A two-dimensional Rayleigh-Bénard convection governed by the Navier-Stokes (NS) equations is solved by traditional direct numerical simulation (DNS) using double precision and various time-steps. It is found that there are two kinds of final flow types, one is vortical flow, the other is zonal flow, and their statistics are completely different. Especially, the two flow types frequently alternate as the time-step decreases to a very small value, suggesting that the time-step corresponding to each type of turbulent flows should be densely distributed. Thus, stochastic numerical noise has huge influences on final flow type and statistics of numerical simulation of the NS turbulence (i.e. turbulence governed by NS equations), since time-step has a close relationship with numerical noise. However, the NS equations as turbulence model have such an assumption that all small stochastic disturbances for t > 0 t>0 are negligible. This leads to a logic paradox in theory. Obviously, more investigations are necessary to reveal the essential differences between the NS turbulence, its numerical simulation, and real turbulence.



In 1890 Poincaré
Poincaré 1890
discovered that spatiotemporal trajectories of some dynamic systems (called chaos later) have sensitivity dependence on initial condition, i.e. the famous “butterfly-effect” named by Lorenz
Lorenz 1963
in 1963 that leads to the collapse of determinism in scientific community.
Many researchers
Deissler 1986
;
Boffetta and Musacchio 2017
;
Berera and Ho 2018
;
Ge et al. 2023
reported that spatiotemporal trajectories of the Navier-Stokes turbulence (i.e. turbulent flow governed by the Navier-Stokes equations) have sensitivity dependence on initial conditions, corresponding to “trajectory instability”. In other words, the Navier-Stokes (NS) turbulence is chaotic, i.e. any tiny (natural/artificial) stochastic disturbances increase exponentially to a macroscopic level of spatiotemporal trajectories.

In 2006 Lorenz
Lorenz 2006
further discovered that numerical noises (i.e. truncation error and round-off error) also have huge influences on numerical simulations of chaotic systems, say,
not only
on their spatiotemporal trajectories
but also
even on their statistics. Especially, Lorenz
Lorenz 2006
illustrated that the maximum Lyapunov exponent of a chaotic system might alternate its sign as time-step decreases to a very small value. It should be emphasized that initial condition has physical meanings, but numerical noise is “artificial” and thus “artificially” uncertain since it is strongly dependent of numerical algorithms that might have a large number of different choices for the same equations and the same initial conditions.

How large are the influences of numerical noise on the NS turbulence? Whether can numerical noise lead to essential and statistic differences of the NS turbulence or not? These are fundamental and essential questions for turbulence community.

It is widely accepted by turbulence community that DNS results are reliable as long as the following two conditions are satisfied:

1.

the horizontal (maximum) grid spacing is less than the Kolmogorov scale;

2.

the so-called Courant-Friedrichs-Lewy (CFL) condition, i.e. Courant number
<1
.

We check all of our DNS results very carefully and it is found that all of our DNS results reported in this letter satisfy the above-mentioned two conditions. Thus, from the traditional viewpoint of DNS, all of our DNS results should be reliable and can be used as benchmark solution. However, the final type of the NS turbulence is sensitive to the time-step
\Delta t
, which unfortunately is an “artificial” factor that is “artificially” determined by the “free will” of human being. Which DNS result should we believe?
Obviously, turbulent flow is a kind of natural phenomena and certainly should
not
be determined by our “free will”. Thus, there should be something wrong here. We will discuss and reveal the reason below.

Note that the turbulent Rayleigh-Bénard convection considered in this letter is a kind of NS turbulence governed by the
deterministic
equations (
1
)-(
4
), which neglect all small physical stochastic disturbances for
t>0
. It is a pity that such kind of nonlinear partial differential equations have no analytic solution so that one had to use numerical methods. But all numerical methods contain numerical noises caused by truncation error and round-off error. In other words, numerical noise is unavoidable for numerical methods. For a non-chaotic system, numerical noises do not enlarge and thus are indeed negligible. However, for a chaotic system, numerical noises might have huge influence even in statistics, as reported by Lorenz
Lorenz 2006
. Unfortunately, the NS turbulence is chaotic, as pointed out by many researchers
Deissler 1986
;
Boffetta and Musacchio 2017
;
Berera and Ho 2018
;
Ge et al. 2023
. Thus, due to the famous butterfly-effect of chaos, the unavoidable numerical noises of DNS exponentially increase to a macro-level, which can change the type of the final flow (and the corresponding statistics). This indicates that the numerical noise as a small stochastic disturbance has huge influences on the NS turbulence. However, the NS turbulence as a
deterministic
model neglect
all
stochastic disturbances. This leads to a paradox in logic for the NS turbulence as a mathematical model.

It should be emphasized that the NS turbulence is just a mathematical model for turbulence. It is
not
the real turbulence in nature. In fact, the
true
solution of the NS turbulence is different from the DNS result of the NS turbulence: the latter is badly contaminated by numerical noise, but the former is not. Note that the following three things, i.e.

(A)

the NS turbulence,

(B)

numerical simulation of the NS turbulence,

(C)

real turbulence,

might be completely different.
Whether or not the NS turbulence can describe correctly the essential characteristics of the real turbulence need a lots of theoretical, numerical and experimental supports. Although many numerical and experimental results support the NS turbulence as a good model, our DNS results reported in this letter reveals a logic paradox of the NS turbulence in theory, and thus highly suggest that small stochastic disturbances should be considered in turbulence modeling, say, tiny physical disturbances should be considered in the Navier-Stokes equations for
t>0
, just like Landau-Lifshitz-Navier-Stokes equations
Landau and Lifshitz 1959
that are stochastic partial differential equations.

Recall that, as reported by Lorenz
Lorenz 2006
, the maximum Lyapunov exponent of a chaotic system might alternate its sign as time-step decreases to a very small value. Thus Lorenz’s results
Lorenz 2006
highly support our conclusion, i.e. flow type and statistics of the NS turbulence might be sensitive to small stochastic disturbances. Certainly, more investigations are necessary in future so as to reveal the essential differences between the NS turbulence, its numerical simulation, and real turbulence.

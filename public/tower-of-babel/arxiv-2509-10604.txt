CMB component-separated power spectrum estimation by Spectral Internal Linear Combination (SpILC)
Jack Y. L. Kwok, William R. Coulton, Niall MacCrann, Fiona McCarthy, Blake D. Sherwin, Boris Bolliet

Abstract
Component separation methods mitigate the cross-contamination between different extragalactic and galactic contributions to cosmic microwave background (CMB) data. This is often done by linearly combining CMB maps from different frequency channels using internal linear combination (ILC) methods. We demonstrate that deriving power spectrum estimators directly by linearly combining auto- and cross-spectra instead of maps allows us to obtain a different constrained-optimization problem that allows fewer (deprojection) constraint equations than combining at map level using the constrained ILC method. Through simulations, we show that our Spectral internal linear combination (SpILC) produces CMB power spectrum estimators with more than 7 times smaller errorbars than constrained ILC (with thermal Sunyaev-Zel’dovich and cosmic infrared background deprojections) at ℓ ≳ 4000 \ell\gtrsim 4000 for Simons Observatory-like observations. Spectral ILC outperforms constrained ILC methods when some modeled components are spatially uncorrelated, e.g. the primary CMB is uncorrelated with foregrounds, and the difference in performance is most significant at noise-dominated scales. More generally, our work shows that component-separated maps with foreground deprojections do not necessarily produce minimum-variance two-or-higher-point estimators.

Our work intends to address the following question:
what is the unbiased minimum-variance estimator for the foreground-cleaned power spectrum?

Here we develop analytical solutions and insights in the case of known SEDs of modeled components. This is an application of the (constrained) ILC formalism to the spectral level, and as such we denote our auto- and cross-spectra estimators as

II
Spectral ILC (SpILC)

In this paper we introduce the
Spectral ILC
(SpILC), a power spectrum estimator constructed by linearly combining estimated spectra
\hat{C}^{ij}_{\ell}
with symmetric weights
W^{ij}_{\ell}
(no
\ell
-summation implied):

\displaystyle\hat{K}_{\ell}\equiv W^{ij}_{\ell}\hat{C}^{ij}_{\ell}\;,

(2)

This study specializes to harmonic space throughout.
For a given component
s
, the
error
of the (
ss
-spectrum) estimator is defined as

\displaystyle\hat{\varepsilon}^{ss}_{\ell}\equiv\hat{K}^{ss}_{\ell}-C^{ss}_{\ell}\;

(3)

where
C^{ss}_{\ell}
is the true power spectrum of component
s
.
We say that the SpILC estimator is unbiased if the ensemble average (denoted by angular brackets)

\displaystyle\langle\hat{\varepsilon}_{\ell}\rangle=0\;.

(4)

Subsequent subsections discuss how the weights
W^{ij}_{\ell}
are chosen.

II.1
Standard Spectral ILC Weights

This subsection considers the one-component data model

\displaystyle x^{i}_{p}=a^{i}s_{p}+n^{i}_{p}\;.

(5)

As we specialize to harmonic domain,
p\mapsto(\ell,m)
,
q\mapsto(\ell^{\prime},m^{\prime})
, and
\langle x^{j}_{p}x^{m,*}_{q}\rangle=0
for
p\neq q
. We assume: 1. the component SED
a^{i}
is known; 2. zero spatial signal-noise correlations

\displaystyle\langle s_{p}n^{i,*}_{p}\rangle=0\;,

(6)

for all channels
i
; and 3. Gaussianity of the noise
n^{i}_{p}
. In this work, we further use the Gaussian approximation on the component(s)
s_{p}
to simplify expressions, but as we will show in App.
A.3
this approximation does
not
enter the calculation of the weights for SpILC.

We impose the unbiased constraint to the spectrum of interest:

\displaystyle W^{ij}_{\ell}\langle a^{i}s_{p}a^{j}s_{p}^{*}\rangle=\langle s_{p}s_{p}^{*}\rangle

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle W_{\ell}^{ij}a^{i}a^{j}=1\;.

(7)

We refer to this as the
normalization constraint
, which ensures that the target component contributes to the recovered power spectrum with the correct amplitude. Subject to this constraint, we optimize the weights
W_{\ell}^{ij}
such that our figure of merit, the error variance Var(
\hat{\varepsilon}_{\ell}
), is minimized. The constrained-minimization of
\operatorname{Var}(\hat{\varepsilon}_{\ell})
is equivalent to the constrained-minimization of
\operatorname{Var}(\hat{K}_{\ell})
:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial W_{\ell}^{ij}}\operatorname{Var}(\hat{\varepsilon}_{\ell})=\frac{\partial}{\partial W_{\ell}^{ij}}\operatorname{Var}(\hat{K}_{\ell}-C^{ss}_{\ell})=\frac{\partial}{\partial W_{\ell}^{ij}}\operatorname{Var}(\hat{K}_{\ell})=0\;,

(8)

as
C^{ss}_{\ell}
is a constant.
The standard SpILC weights are therefore chosen to minimize
\operatorname{Var}(\hat{K}_{\ell})
subjected to
W_{\ell}^{ij}a^{i}a^{j}=1
. We suppose
W_{\ell}^{ij}
depends on the ensemble-averaged map spectra
C^{ij}_{\ell}
instead of
\hat{C}^{ij}_{\ell}
, such that it can be taken out of the ensemble average:

\displaystyle\operatorname{Var}(\hat{K}_{\ell})=\operatorname{Var}(W_{\ell}^{jk}\hat{C}^{jk}_{\ell})=W_{\ell}^{jk}W_{\ell}^{mn}\operatorname{Cov}(\hat{C}^{jk}_{\ell},\hat{C}^{mn}_{\ell})\;.

(9)

This is a self-consistent assumption, as
W_{\ell}^{ij}
is chosen to minimize Var(
\hat{K}_{\ell}
), which now depends only on
C^{ij}_{\ell}
(as we show immediately below).
The expression for the variance is

\displaystyle\operatorname{Var}(\hat{K}_{\ell})

\displaystyle=W_{\ell}^{jk}W_{\ell}^{mn}\operatorname{Cov}(\hat{C}^{jk}_{\ell},\hat{C}^{mn}_{\ell})

\displaystyle=W_{\ell}^{jk}W_{\ell}^{mn}\frac{2}{N_{p}}C_{\ell}^{jm}C_{\ell}^{kn}\;,

(10)

where we additionally assumed Gaussianity of the map and applied Wick’s theorem. To be accurate, the derivation of the standard SpILC weights only requires the assumption of
noise
Gaussianity (see App.
A.3
for a justification).

To derive
W_{\ell}^{ij}
which minimizes
\operatorname{Var}(\hat{K}_{\ell})
subject to
W_{\ell}^{ij}a^{i}a^{j}=1
, we use Lagrange’s method of undetermined multipliers to yield the set of
M+1
simultaneous equations, where
M\equiv N(N+1)/2
, and
N
is the number of frequency channels:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{W^{ij}_{\ell}}\left[W_{\ell}^{ab}W_{\ell}^{cd}C_{\ell}^{ac}C_{\ell}^{bd}-\lambda(W_{\ell}^{cd}a^{c}a^{d}-1)\right]=0&\mbox{, $i\leq j$}\\
W_{\ell}^{cd}a^{c}a^{d}=1\;,\end{cases}\;

(11)

as the symmetric property of
W_{\ell}^{ij}
implies that only the weights
W_{\ell}^{ij}
with
i\leq j
are independent degrees of freedom. Labeling these
M
degrees of freedom with Greek indices
\mu=\{1,\dots,M\}
, each corresponding to a pair
(i,j)
,
i\leq j
, The weights are solved to give

\displaystyle w^{\mu}_{\ell}=\frac{D^{-1}_{\ell,\mu\nu}t_{\nu}}{t_{\rho}D^{-1}_{\ell,\rho\tau}t_{\tau}}\;,

(12)

where
t_{\mu}
is a vector

\displaystyle\mathbf{t}\equiv\underbrace{\begin{pmatrix}a^{1}a^{1}&a^{1}a^{2}&\cdots&a^{1}a^{N}&a^{2}a^{2}&a^{2}a^{3}&\cdots&a^{N}a^{N}\end{pmatrix}^{\text{T}}}_{\text{$N(N+1)/2$ rows}}\;,

(13)

and the matrix
D_{\ell}^{\mu\nu}
can be found in Eq. (
47
). The weight vector is defined as follows:

\displaystyle\mathbf{w}_{\ell}

\displaystyle\equiv\underbrace{\begin{pmatrix}\bar{W}_{\ell}^{11}&\bar{W}_{\ell}^{12}&\cdots&\bar{W}_{\ell}^{1N}&\bar{W}_{\ell}^{22}&\bar{W}_{\ell}^{23}\cdots\bar{W}_{\ell}^{NN}\end{pmatrix}^{\text{T}}}_{\text{$N(N+1)/2$ rows}}\;,

(14)

where

\displaystyle\bar{W}_{\ell}^{ij}\equiv(2-\delta_{ij})W_{\ell}^{ij}=\begin{cases}W_{\ell}^{ij}\;,&\mbox{for $i=j$}\\
2W_{\ell}^{ij}\;,&\mbox{for $i\neq j$}\end{cases}\;.

(15)

See App.
A.1
for a detailed derivation.

In practical applications, we have access only to a single realization of the power spectra
\hat{C}^{ij}_{\ell}
—that of the observed sky, so we derive the weights and estimators only from one sky realization through
internal
combination.

\displaystyle\hat{K}_{\ell}^{\text{1real}}\equiv\hat{W}_{\ell,ij}^{\text{1real}}\hat{C}^{ij}_{\ell}\;.

(16)

Although our derivations are concerned with
\hat{K}_{\ell}
, we will mainly discuss simulation results for the estimator
\hat{K}^{\text{1real}}_{\ell}
.
In this case we minimize the sample variance in that realization:

\displaystyle\operatorname{Var}(\hat{K}_{\ell}^{\text{1real}})

\displaystyle\approx\hat{\operatorname{Var}}(\hat{K}_{\ell}^{\text{1real}})=\hat{W}^{\text{1real}}_{\ell,jk}\hat{W}^{\text{1real}}_{\ell,mn}\frac{2}{N_{p}}\hat{C}^{jm}_{\ell}\hat{C}^{kn}_{\ell}\;,

(17)

where the weights keep the same functional form, but with
C_{\ell}^{ij}
replaced with
\hat{C}_{\ell}^{ij}
.

Having finite samples mean that the latter may suffer from expectation bias (analogous to the map-level ILC bias) due to chance correlations between signal and noise, making
\langle\hat{K}_{\ell}^{\text{1real}}\rangle\neq\langle\hat{K}_{\ell}\rangle
. Writing out the bias,

\displaystyle\langle\hat{\varepsilon}_{\ell}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\hat{K}_{\ell}-C^{ss}_{\ell}\rangle=\langle\hat{K}_{\ell}\rangle-C^{ss}_{\ell}

\displaystyle=\langle W_{\ell}^{ij}\hat{C}^{ij}_{\ell}\rangle-C_{\ell}^{ss}

\displaystyle=\langle W_{\ell}^{ij}(a^{i}s_{p}+n^{i}_{p})(a^{j}s_{p}^{*}+n^{j,*}_{p})\rangle-C_{\ell}^{ss}

\displaystyle=\langle W_{\ell}^{ij}n^{i}_{p}n^{j,*}_{p}\rangle+2\langle W_{\ell}^{ij}a^{i}s_{p}n^{j,*}_{p}\rangle\;.

(18)

The bias
\langle W_{\ell,ij}n^{i}_{p}n^{j}_{p}\rangle
is the usual
noise bias
in power spectrum methods, as any non-zero diagonal entries of
W_{\ell}^{ij}
would contribute noise auto-correlation into the resulting spectra, even if there is no noise correlations between channels. The bias
\langle W^{ij}_{\ell}a^{i}s_{p}n^{j,*}_{p}\rangle
vanishes in the ensemble average, however the sample covariance due to chance correlation between the noise and the signal
N^{-1}_{p}\sum_{p}s_{p}n^{j,*}_{p}
is non-vanishing and contributes to the expectation bias
\langle\hat{W}^{\text{1real}}_{\ell,ij}a^{i}s_{p}n^{j,*}_{p}\rangle
.
The magnitude of the expectation bias will be discussed through simulations in Sec.
V.2
.

II.2
Equivalence between standard ILC and SpILC

The standard ILC and SpILC weights for auto- and cross-spectra are identical, i.e. the weights
w_{\ell,i}^{\text{ILC}}
for the ILC maps
\hat{s}_{p}\equiv w_{\ell,i}^{\text{ILC}}x^{i}_{p}
satisfy

\displaystyle W^{\text{SpILC}}_{\ell,ij}=w^{\text{ILC}}_{\ell,i}w^{\text{ILC}}_{\ell,j}\;.

(19)

This arises as the one-component standard ILC and SpILC constrained-optimization problems turn out to be equivalent—App.
B.1
provides a proof for this argument.
This means that the spectra estimated from ILC maps
\hat{s}_{p}
equals our standard SpILC estimator:

\displaystyle\hat{K}_{\ell}^{\text{SpILC}}

\displaystyle=W_{\ell,ij}^{\text{SpILC}}\hat{C}_{\ell}^{ij}=w_{\ell,i}^{\text{ILC}}w_{\ell,j}^{\text{ILC}}\frac{1}{N_{p}}\sum_{p=1}^{N_{p}}x^{i}_{p}x^{*,j}_{p}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N_{p}}\sum_{p=1}^{N_{p}}\hat{s}_{p}^{\text{ILC}}\hat{s}_{p}^{*,\text{ILC}}\;.

(20)

However, we will find in the next section that the equality no longer holds between constrained ILC and constrained Spectral ILC (cSpILC) when we extend to a multiple-component model and impose deprojection constraints (Sec.
III
),
if
some constraints can be dropped as a result of zero spatial correlation between some components.

III
Constrained SpILC (cSpILC)

This section holds the key analytical result of our study, where we show that the constrained SpILC (cSpILC) method can produce spectrum estimators with lower variance than cILC. We demonstrate this with the two-component model

\displaystyle x^{i}_{p}

\displaystyle=a^{i}s_{p}+b^{i}y_{p}+n^{i}_{p}\;,

(21)

which can be readily generalized to arbitrarily many components.
We impose that the estimator has unit response to the spectrum of interest (
ss
-spectrum here), and zero response to all other spectra:

\displaystyle W_{\ell}^{ij}\langle(a^{i}s_{p}+b^{i}y_{p})(a^{j}s^{*}_{p}+b^{j}y^{*}_{p})\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle s_{p}s^{*}_{p}\rangle\;.

(22)

Generalizing from the constrained ILC (cILC) method, we can impose the following
normalization
and
deprojection
constraints, which we call collectively as the
strong
-cSpILC constraints:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}W_{\ell}^{ij}a^{i}a^{j}=1\;,\\
W_{\ell}^{ij}b^{i}b^{j}=0\;,\\
W_{\ell}^{ij}a^{(i}b^{j)}=0\;,\end{cases}\;\;\text{({strong}-cSpILC constraints)}

(23)

where the symmetrization bracket is defined with
a^{(i}b^{j)}=(a^{i}b^{j}+a^{j}b^{i})/2
.
However, if we know
a priori
that the two components
s_{p}
and
y_{p}
have zero cross spectra,
\langle s_{p}y^{*}_{p}\rangle=0
, then
we need only to impose weaker constraints for our purpose:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}W_{\ell}^{ij}a^{i}a^{j}=1\;,\\
W_{\ell}^{ij}b^{i}b^{j}=0\;,\\
\end{cases}\;\;\text{({weak}-cSpILC constraints)}

(24)

which we call the
weak
-cSpILC constraints.

In App.
B.2
we generalize the ILC-SpILC equivalence of the last subsection and show that
strong
-cSpILC method produces the same weights as cILC, and
as a result
weak
-cSpILC must obtain an equal or smaller variance than cILC due to its fewer constraints.
In Sec.
IV
we show through simulations that
weak
-cSpILC indeed achieves lower variance than cILC.
This result demonstrates that the variance reduction of
weak
-cSpILC compared to cILC does not come directly from the increased degrees of freedom from
N
to
N(N+1)/2
of the weights, as the number of constraints increases correspondingly in
strong
-cSpILC and the same variance as cILC is obtained. Rather, it is the
explicit relaxation of constraints
in Eq. (
24
) enabled by the SpILC parameterization at the spectral level that contributes to its variance reduction.

The weights for
weak
-cSpILC are derived by solving

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{W_{\ell}^{ij}}\left[W_{\ell}^{ab}W_{\ell}^{cd}C^{ac}_{\ell}C_{\ell}^{bd}-\lambda(W_{\ell}^{cd}a^{c}a^{d}-1)\right.\\
\phantom{asdasdasdasdaasasddaaaaa}\left.-\mu W_{\ell}^{cd}b^{c}b^{d}\right]=0\;\;,i\leq j\\
W_{\ell}^{cd}a^{c}a^{d}=1\\
W_{\ell}^{cd}b^{c}b^{d}=0\end{cases}\;,

(25)

where
\lambda
and
\mu
are Lagrange multipliers, giving the
weak
-cSpILC weights

\displaystyle w^{\mu}_{\ell}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(\mathbf{u}^{\text{T}}\mathbf{D}^{-1}_{\ell}\mathbf{u}\right)D^{-1}_{\ell,\mu\rho}t^{\rho}-\left(\mathbf{t}^{\text{T}}\mathbf{D}_{\ell}^{-1}\mathbf{u}\right)D^{-1}_{\ell,\mu\rho}u^{\rho}}{\left(\mathbf{t}^{\text{T}}\mathbf{D}_{\ell}^{-1}\mathbf{t}\right)\left(\mathbf{u}^{\text{T}}\mathbf{D}_{\ell}^{-1}\mathbf{u}\right)-\left(\mathbf{t}^{\text{T}}\mathbf{D}_{\ell}^{-1}\mathbf{u}\right)^{2}}\;,

(26)

where the Greek indices ranges over the
N(N+1)/2
degrees of freedom of pairs of
(i,j)
,
i\leq j
, as in Eq. (
12
). The vector
u^{\mu}
is defined as

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}\equiv\underbrace{\begin{pmatrix}b^{1}b^{1}&b^{1}b^{2}\cdots&b^{1}b^{N}&b^{2}b^{2}&b^{2}b^{3}&\cdots&b^{N}b^{N}\end{pmatrix}^{\text{T}}}_{\text{$N(N+1)/2$ rows}}\;,

(27)

and the vector
t^{\mu}
(Eq. (
13
)) is similarly defined with
b\mapsto a
. The matrix
D_{\ell}^{\mu\nu}
can be found in Eq. (
47
). Note the definition of the weight vector
w_{\ell}^{\mu}
defined in Eqs. (
14
) and (
15
).
See App.
A.2
for a detailed derivation.

Unlike the standard SpILC where only the Gaussianity of noise is assumed and
not
the components, for
weak
-cSpILC there are non-vanishing connected four-point functions

\displaystyle\langle s_{p}y_{p}^{*}s_{q}y_{q}^{*}\rangle_{c}

\displaystyle=0\;,

(28)

\displaystyle\langle s_{p}s^{*}_{p}s_{q}y_{q}^{*}\rangle_{c}

\displaystyle=0\;,

(29)

where the components
s_{p}
and
y_{p}
are those assumed to be uncorrelated in their two-point functions. Therefore, the weight derivation for

-cSpILC using Wick’s theorem implicitly imposes an additional assumption that the specific four-point functions above vanishes. If this additional assumption is violated, the
weak
-cSpILC estimator will be sub-optimal. See Appendix
A.3
for details.

Generalization to multiple components is straightforward. Suppose we have a component
k_{p}
with SED

which we also want to deproject, and we know
a priori
that
s_{p}
is spatially uncorrelated with both
y_{p}
and
k_{p}
, while allowing spatial correlation between

and
y_{p}
. The additional constraints we need to include would be

\displaystyle W_{\ell}^{ij}c^{i}c^{j}

\displaystyle=0\;,

(30)

\displaystyle 2W_{\ell}^{ij}c^{(i}b^{j)}

\displaystyle=0\;.

(31)

This is the physical example we consider for simulations in the following section:
s_{p}
is the CMB+kSZ (both components have the blackbody SED),

and
k_{p}
are the tSZ and the cosmic infrared background (CIB) map respectively, where tSZ and CIB are spatially correlated, and both are uncorrelated with the CMB+kSZ. Writing the SED for component

, the constraints for estimating the (CMB+kSZ)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}W_{\ell}^{jk}f^{j}_{\text{CMB+kSZ}}f^{k}_{\text{CMB+kSZ}}=1&\mbox{((CMB+kSZ)${}^{2}$)}\\

W_{\ell}^{jk}f^{j}_{\text{CIB}}f^{k}_{\text{CIB}}=0&\mbox{(CIB$\times$CIB)}\\
2W_{\ell}^{jk}f^{(j}_{\text{tSZ}}f^{k)}_{\text{CIB}}=0&\mbox{(tSZ$\times$CIB)}\end{cases}\;.

(32)

To estimate another spectrum, e.g. tSZ
\times
tSZ, the RHS of the constraint of that spectrum is simply replaced with 1 (e.g.

IV
Data-split SpILC and cSpILC

As we saw in Eq. (
18
), the noise term contributes variance to the estimator
\hat{K}_{\ell}
, leading to a noise bias.
The noise bias due to instrumental and atmospheric noise—which is assumed to be uncorrelated across channels at different times—can be removed using
data splits
(defined below). The residual noise bias would be contributed by unmodeled components, which are correlated across channels.

Suppose
x^{i}_{p}
is a map constructed from data collected from time
t_{0}
to
t_{0}+\Delta t
. Two noisier maps
x^{i,1}_{p}
and
x^{i,2}_{p}
, referred to as
data splits
, can be constructed from the time segments
[t_{0},t_{0}+\Delta t/2]
and
[t_{0}+\Delta t/2,t_{0}+\Delta t]
respectively. The two time segments are labeled 1 and 2 respectively.

We can build the following SpILC estimator with vanishing
instrumental
noise bias, as same-split, same-channel spectra are removed:

\displaystyle\hat{K}_{\ell}^{\text{DS}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{ij}^{N}W_{\ell}^{ij}\left[\delta_{ij}\hat{C}^{ij}_{\ell,(12)}+(1-\delta_{ij})\hat{C}_{\ell}^{ij}\right]

(33)

\displaystyle=\sum_{ij}^{N}W_{\ell}^{ij}\left[\delta_{ij}\hat{C}^{ij}_{\ell,12}+(1-\delta_{ij})\hat{C}^{ij}_{\ell}\right]\;,

(34)

where

\displaystyle\hat{C}^{ij}_{\ell,12}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{1}{N_{p}}\sum_{p}x^{i}_{p,1}x^{j,*}_{p,2}\;\;,\;\;\hat{C}^{ij}_{\ell,21}\equiv\frac{1}{N_{p}}\sum_{p}x^{i}_{p,2}x^{j,*}_{p,1}\;.

(35)

This estimator will have lower variance than the estimator
\hat{K}^{\text{DS2}}_{\ell,11}=W_{\ell}^{ij}\hat{C}^{ij}_{\ell,12}
, as the construction of the latter discarded the information in the same-split, different-channel spectra
(1-\delta_{ij})\hat{C}^{ij}_{\ell,11}
and
(1-\delta_{ij})\hat{C}^{ij}_{\ell,22}
.
We note the estimator
\hat{K}^{\text{DS2}}_{\ell}
instead of
\hat{K}^{\text{DS}}_{\ell}
is required to remove bias from correlated atmospheric noise across channels, which is currently ignored in this study (we set the ensemble noise cross-spectra to be zero,
N^{ij}=0
for
i\neq j
). The weights
W_{\ell}^{ij}
are determined as usual by minimizing subject to constraints the variance of
\hat{K}_{\ell}
, which is derived in App.
C.3
.

VI
Conclusion and Outlook

In this paper, we develop and compare spectral ILC (SpILC) against map-based constrained ILC methods using simulations comprising of CMB+kSZ+tSZ+CIB+Gaussian instrumental and atmospheric noise with SO goal noise level and beam sizes that is uncorrelated across channels. We find that both our cSpILC (CMB+kSZ)
\times
(CMB+kSZ) and tSZ
\times
tSZ estimators both achieve 7 times smaller errorbars at small scales (
\ell\gtrsim 4000
) compared to constrained ILC (tSZ+CIB or CMB+CIB deprojected), and
2
times smaller errorbars compared to cILC with only tSZ or CMB+kSZ deprojection, respectively.

We summarize the argument why the constrained SpILC estimator can achieve lower variance than constrained ILC: we demonstrated that the constrained-minimization problem of cILC weights are equivalent to that of cSpILC with the full set of normalization and deprojection constraints in the presence of Gaussian noise. Assuming at least two components are spatially uncorrelated, we can relax one or more cSpILC constraints (which is not possible for the cILC), so that we gain more degrees of freedom to minimize the weights compared to cILC.

We further incorporate data splits in SpILC. We test our estimators with simulations. We demonstrate that our estimators indeed achieve lower variance than cILC, and that the noise bias accounts for the biases in SpILC. We find that the data-split SpILC estimators are free from noise bias and remain unbiased when the weights are obtained from a single sky realization, and only suffers a 15% increase in standard deviation for our 2-splits implementation compared to the non-split estimators for our simulation setup.

We propose two immediate applications of Spectral ILC: the estimation of the kSZ
\times
kSZ power spectrum, as its cross-correlation with either the tSZ and CIB sums to zero; and blind component separation of the CMB polarization
BB
-spectrum, assuming it is uncorrelated with either dust or synchrotron. While the SpILC method is valid for the latter case (if SEDs are known), a needlet-space approach would be better suited than a harmonic-space approach discussed in this paper due to the anisotropy of galactic foregrounds. It will be interesting to see if cSpILC can be complementary to the needlet-space power spectrum estimator of Ref.
Surrao and Hill 2024
.

We also envisage spectral ILC being a complementary tool for the moment ILC method
Remazeilles et al. 2021
, where variations of a component SED are modeled by decomposing the component into a Taylor expansion
Chluba et al. 2017
about some parameters (
(\beta,T_{\text{CIB}})
for the CIB gray-body SED), and deprojecting the zeroth and higher-order terms (or
moments
) in the SEDs. Moment ILC is found to be an effective mitigation to the modeling of the CIB
McCarthy and Hill 2024b
. However, given the limited number of frequency channels of current and near-future CMB experiments, the inclusion of more components through moment expansion will greatly increase the variance of ILC estimators. If spatially uncorrelated moments are available or can be devised, Spectral ILC will prove to be highly complementary by minimizing the loss of variance due to modeling of higher moments.

As high-resolution CMB experiments push millimeter-sky observations to smaller scales, the separation of the kSZ, tSZ, CIB, and other foreground emissions will be key to improving our understanding of the late-time universe. With limited number of frequency channels but more components to be modeled in order to achieve accurate component separation, we foresee Spectral ILC to play a useful role in this avenue.

A.1
Derivation for SpILC weights

Our objective is to find the weights
W^{ij}_{\ell}
in the constrained-optimization problem

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{W^{ij}_{\ell}}\left[W^{ab}_{\ell}W^{cd}_{\ell}C^{ac}_{\ell}C^{bd}_{\ell}-\lambda(W^{cd}_{\ell}a^{c}a^{d}-1)\right]=0&\mbox{, $i\leq j$}\\
W^{cd}_{\ell}a^{c}a^{d}=1\;,\end{cases}\;.

(41)

Noting that
W_{\ell}^{ij}
is symmetric,

\displaystyle\sum_{c}^{N}\sum_{d}^{N}W_{\ell}^{cd}

\displaystyle=W_{\ell}^{11}+W_{\ell}^{12}+\cdots+W_{\ell}^{21}+W_{\ell}^{22}+\cdots

\displaystyle=W_{\ell}^{11}+2W_{\ell}^{12}+\cdots+2W_{\ell}^{1N}+W_{\ell}^{22}+2W_{\ell}^{23}+\cdots

\displaystyle=\sum_{c}^{N}\sum_{d}^{c}(2-\delta_{cd})W_{\ell}^{cd}\equiv\sum^{c\leq d}_{c,d}(2-\delta_{cd})W_{\ell}^{cd}\;.

(42)

Defining

\displaystyle\bar{W}_{cd}\equiv(2-\delta_{cd})W_{\ell}^{cd}=\begin{cases}W_{\ell}^{cd}&\mbox{for $c=d$},\\
2W_{\ell}^{cd}&\mbox{for $c\neq d$,}\end{cases}

(43)

We can rewrite sums over frequency channels to sums over the
M
degrees of freedom of our weights:

\displaystyle\sum_{c,d}^{N}W_{\ell}^{cd}C_{\ell}^{ac}C_{\ell}^{bd}

\displaystyle=\sum^{c\leq d}_{c,d}(2-\delta_{cd})W_{\ell}^{cd}\frac{(C_{\ell}^{ac}C_{\ell}^{bd}+C_{\ell}^{ad}C_{\ell}^{bc})}{2}

\displaystyle\equiv\sum^{c\leq d}_{c,d}\bar{W}_{\ell}^{cd}C_{\ell}^{a(c|}C_{\ell}^{b|d)}\;,

(44)

where
C_{\ell}^{a(c|}C_{\ell}^{b|d)}\equiv(C_{\ell}^{ac}C_{\ell}^{bd}+C_{\ell}^{ad}C_{\ell}^{bc})/2
.

We rewrite the set of simultaneous equations in Eq. (
11
) in terms of sums over the weight degrees of freedom:

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{\bar{W}_{\ell}^{ij}}\left[\sum_{a,b}^{a\leq b}\sum_{c,d}^{c\leq d}\bar{W}_{\ell}^{ab}\bar{W}_{\ell}^{cd}C_{\ell}^{a(c|}C_{\ell}^{b|d)}\right.\\
\phantom{asdas}\left.-\lambda\left(\sum^{c\leq d}_{c,d}\bar{W}_{\ell}^{cd}a^{c}a^{d}-1\right)\right]=0\;\;,i\leq j\\
\sum^{c\leq d}_{c,d}\bar{W}_{\ell}^{cd}a^{c}a^{d}=1\end{cases}

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\sum_{c,d}^{c\leq d}\bar{W}_{\ell}^{cd}C_{\ell}^{(c|i}C_{\ell}^{|d)j}+\bar{\lambda}a^{i}a^{j}=0&\mbox{, $i\leq j$}\\
\sum^{c\leq d}_{c,d}\bar{W}_{\ell}^{cd}a^{c}a^{d}=1\end{cases}\;,

(45)

where
\bar{\lambda}\equiv-\lambda/2
. In matrix form,

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}C_{\ell}^{(1|1}C_{\ell}^{|1)1}&C_{\ell}^{(1|1}C_{\ell}^{|2)1}&\cdots&C_{\ell}^{(1|1}C_{\ell}^{|N)1}&C_{\ell}^{(2|1}C_{\ell}^{|2)1}&C_{\ell}^{(2|1}C_{\ell}^{|3)1}&\cdots&C_{\ell}^{(N|1}C_{\ell}^{|N)1}&a^{1}a^{1}\\
C_{\ell}^{(1|1}C_{\ell}^{|1)2}&C_{\ell}^{(1|1}C_{\ell}^{|2)2}&\cdots&C_{\ell}^{(1|1}C_{\ell}^{|N)2}&C_{\ell}^{(2|1}C_{\ell}^{|2)2}&C_{\ell}^{(2|1}C_{\ell}^{|3)2}&\cdots&C_{\ell}^{(N|1}C_{\ell}^{|N)2}&a^{1}a^{2}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\

A.3
Non-Gaussian contributions to the variance

We have not assumed component Gaussianity when we derive SpILC weights. We make this argument by showing that the variance does not depend on higher-point statistics of the modeled components. Consider the two component model

\displaystyle x_{p}^{i}=a^{i}s_{p}+b^{i}y_{p}+n^{i}_{p}\;.

(58)

For
strong
-cSpILC (equivalent to cILC), we impose the constraints
W^{ij}_{\ell}a^{i}a^{j}=1
,
W^{ij}_{\ell}b^{i}b^{j}=0
and
W^{ij}_{\ell}a^{(i}b^{j)}=0
.
The variance of
\hat{K}_{\ell}
can be expanded in terms of four-point and two-point functions. Schematically, the four-point functions are
\langle ssss\rangle
,
\langle sssy\rangle
,
\langle sssn\rangle
,
\langle ssyn\rangle
,
\langle ssnn\rangle
,
\langle sysy\rangle
,
\langle sysn\rangle
,
\langle syyn\rangle
,
\langle synn\rangle
,
\langle snsn\rangle
,
\langle snyn\rangle
,
\langle snnn\rangle
,
\langle ynyn\rangle
,
\langle ynnn\rangle
, and
\langle nnnn\rangle
. Applying the deprojection constraints, the remaining non-vanishing four-point functions are
\langle ssss\rangle
and those involving some factors of
n
. Since
n
is independent of
s
and
y
, and is assumed to be Gaussian and zero-mean, the non-vanishing 4-point functions are
\langle ssss\rangle
,
\langle ssnn\rangle
,
\langle snsn\rangle
,
\langle snyn\rangle
,
\langle ynyn\rangle
and
\langle nnnn\rangle
.
Noting that e.g.
s
and
n
being uncorrelated implies

\displaystyle\langle ssnn\rangle=\langle ss\rangle\langle nn\rangle\;,

(59)

the only four-point function that remains is
\langle ssss\rangle
and
\langle nnnn\rangle
. Performing the variance minimization

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial W_{\ell}^{jk}}\operatorname{Var}(\hat{K}_{\ell})

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial W_{\ell}^{jk}}\left[\operatorname{Var}(\hat{C}_{\ell}^{ss})+\cdots\right]\;,

(60)

the only four-point function in the variance that involves the components,
\langle ssss\rangle
, is independent of

, so its derivative is zero. The implication is as follows:
the SpILC weights are optimal even for non-Gaussian components

using Eq. (

\langle ssss\rangle
is non-zero. Note however that the optimality of the estimator does depend on the independence and Gaussianity of
noise
, as the Gaussian approximation
\operatorname{Cov}(\hat{N}^{jk},\hat{N}^{mn})=2\hat{N}^{jm}\hat{N}^{kn}/N_{p}
is used.

For

-cSpILC, we relax the assumption of

which reduces to two-point functions as above, except for

\displaystyle\langle s_{p}s^{*}_{p}s_{q}y^{*}_{q}\rangle_{c}=\langle s_{p}y^{*}_{p}s_{q}y^{*}_{q}\rangle_{c}=0\;.

(61)

For

\times

\times

\times

Appendix B
Equivalences in estimator weights

B.1
Equivalence between SpILC and ILC

We provide a short argument here to explain why we have such an equality between standard ILC and SpILC spectra. Suppose
w^{i}_{\ell,\text{ILC}}
solves the ILC constrained-minimization problem, such that
w_{i}^{\text{ILC}}
and the Lagrange multiplier
\lambda_{\text{ILC}}\equiv-2\bar{\lambda}_{\text{ILC}}
satisfies

\displaystyle w^{j}_{\ell,\text{ILC}}\hat{C}^{ji}_{\ell}+\bar{\lambda}_{\text{ILC}}a^{i}

\displaystyle=0\;,

(62)

\displaystyle w^{j}_{\ell,\text{ILC}}a^{j}

\displaystyle=1\;.

(63)

We can manipulate Eq. (
62
) to obtain

\displaystyle(w^{m}_{\ell,\text{ILC}}\hat{C}_{\ell}^{mi}+\bar{\lambda}_{\text{ILC}}a^{i})(w^{n}_{\ell,\text{ILC}}\hat{C}_{\ell}^{nj}-\bar{\lambda}_{\text{ILC}}a^{j})=0

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle w^{m}_{\ell,\text{ILC}}w^{n}_{\ell,\text{ILC}}\hat{C}_{\ell}^{mi}\hat{C}_{\ell}^{nj}-\bar{\lambda}_{\text{ILC}}^{2}a^{i}a^{j}=2\bar{\lambda}_{\text{ILC}}a^{[j|}w^{m}_{\ell,\text{ILC}}\hat{C}_{\ell}^{m|i]}

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle w^{m}_{\ell,\text{ILC}}w^{n}_{\ell,\text{ILC}}\hat{C}_{\ell}^{mi}\hat{C}_{\ell}^{nj}-\bar{\lambda}_{\text{ILC}}^{2}a^{i}a^{j}=-2\bar{\lambda}_{\text{ILC}}^{2}a^{[j}a^{i]}=0\;,

(64)

where the anti-symmetrization bracket is defined with
a^{[i}b^{j]}\equiv(a^{i}b^{j}-a^{j}b^{i})/2
. We made use of the property
w^{j}_{\ell,\text{ILC}}\hat{C}^{ji}_{\ell}=-\bar{\lambda}_{\text{ILC}}a^{i}
to arrive at the third equality. Taking the square of Eq. (
63
),

\displaystyle w_{\ell,\text{ILC}}^{i}w_{\ell,\text{ILC}}^{j}a^{i}a^{j}

\displaystyle=1\;.

(65)

From Eqs. (
44
) and (
45
), the standard SpILC simultaneous equations can be written as

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\sum_{c,d}^{N}W^{cd}_{\ell}\hat{C}_{\ell}^{ci}\hat{C}_{\ell}^{dj}+\bar{\lambda}a^{i}a^{j}=0&\mbox{for $i\leq j$}\\
W^{cd}_{\ell}a^{c}a^{d}=1\end{cases}\;.

(66)

From Eqs. (
64
) and (
65
), one finds that
W_{\ell}^{ij}=w_{\ell,\text{ILC}}^{i}w_{\ell,\text{ILC}}^{j}
and
\bar{\lambda}=-\bar{\lambda}^{2}_{\text{ILC}}
is a solution to the standard SpILC set of equations. Because the solution to Eq. (
56
) is unique,

\displaystyle W_{\ell,\text{SpILC}}^{ij}=w_{\ell,\text{ILC}}^{i}w_{\ell,\text{ILC}}^{j}\;.

(67)

We can also show that standard SpILC (equivalently, standard ILC) is equivalent to the power spectrum estimator in Sec. 3.6 of Ref.
Tegmark and Efstathiou 1996
, which seeks an internal linear combination of frequency maps that minimizes the bias of the power spectrum of an independent component (uncorrelated with foregrounds and noise). Writing their optimization problem as

\displaystyle\begin{cases}w^{i}_{\ell}a^{i}=1\;,\\
\partial_{w^{i}_{\ell}}(\hat{C}^{ss}_{\ell}-C^{ss}_{\ell})=\partial_{w^{i}_{\ell}}\hat{C}^{ss}_{\ell}=\partial_{w_{i}}w_{j}w_{k}\hat{C}^{jk}_{\ell}=0\;,\end{cases}

(68)

the optimization problem and weights are equivalent to that of standard harmonic ILC. As we proved above, standard ILC is equivalent to standard SpILC in the spectral level in the presence of Gaussian noise, in other words minimizing the bias of the power spectrum also minimizes its (Gaussian) variance.

B.2
Equivalence between
strong
-cSpILC and cILC

Consider the three-component data model

\displaystyle x^{i}_{p}

\displaystyle=a^{i}s_{p}+b^{i}y_{p}+c^{i}z_{p}+n^{i}_{p}\;,

(69)

where the power of the signal

is to be recovered. Constrained ILC gives the following set of equations

\displaystyle\begin{cases}w_{\ell}^{j}C_{\ell}^{ji}+\bar{\lambda}a^{i}+\bar{\mu}b^{i}+\bar{\nu}c^{i}=0\\

(70)

where

are undetermined Lagrange multipliers,

and similarly for
\bar{\mu}
and
\bar{\nu}
.

Now turn to the

\phantom{AAAAA}\left.-\mu^{\prime}W_{\ell}^{cd}b^{c}b^{d}-\nu^{\prime}W_{\ell}^{cd}c^{c}c^{d}-2\alpha^{\prime}W_{\ell}^{cd}a^{(c}b^{d)}\right.\\

W_{\ell}^{ij}a^{(i}c^{j)}=0\\
W_{\ell}^{ij}b^{(i}c^{j)}=0\\
\end{cases}

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\begin{cases}W_{\ell}^{cd}C_{\ell}^{ic}C_{\ell}^{jd}+\bar{\lambda}^{\prime}a^{i}a^{j}+\bar{\mu}^{\prime}b^{i}b^{j}+\bar{\nu}^{\prime}c^{i}c^{j}\\
\phantom{AAAAA}+2\bar{\alpha}^{\prime}a^{(i}b^{j)}+2\bar{\beta}^{\prime}a^{(i}c^{j)}+2\bar{\gamma}^{\prime}b^{(i}c^{j)}=0\\
W_{\ell}^{ij}a^{i}a^{j}=1\\
W_{\ell}^{ij}b^{i}b^{j}=0\\
W_{\ell}^{ij}c^{i}c^{j}=0\\
W_{\ell}^{ij}a^{(i}b^{j)}=0\\
W_{\ell}^{ij}a^{(i}c^{j)}=0\\
W_{\ell}^{ij}b^{(i}c^{j)}=0\\
\end{cases}\;,

(71)

where

are Lagrange multipliers,
\bar{\lambda}^{\prime}\equiv-\lambda^{\prime}/2
and similarly for the rest.

w^{i}_{\ell}
of the cILC system also solves the set of

\displaystyle W^{cd}_{\ell,\mathtt{strong}\text{cSpILC}}=w^{c}_{\ell,\text{cILC}}w^{d}_{\ell,\text{cILC}}\;.

\displaystyle w_{\ell}^{c}C_{\ell}^{ci}+\bar{\lambda}a^{i}+\bar{\mu}b^{i}+\bar{\nu}c^{i}=0

\displaystyle(w_{\ell}^{c}C_{\ell}^{ci}+\bar{\lambda}a^{i}+\bar{\mu}b^{i}+\bar{\nu}c^{i})(w_{\ell}^{d}C_{\ell}^{dj}-\bar{\lambda}a^{j}-\bar{\mu}b^{j}-\bar{\nu}c^{j})

\displaystyle\hskip 193.74939pt=0

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle w_{\ell}^{c}w_{\ell}^{d}C_{\ell}^{ci}C_{\ell}^{dj}-(\bar{\lambda}a^{i}+\bar{\mu}b^{i}+\bar{\nu}c^{i})(\bar{\lambda}b^{j}+\bar{\mu}b^{j}+\bar{\nu}c^{j})

\displaystyle-w_{\ell}^{c}C_{\ell}^{ci}(\bar{\lambda}a^{j}+\bar{\mu}b^{j}+\bar{\nu}c^{j})+w_{\ell}^{d}C_{\ell}^{dj}(\bar{\lambda}a^{i}+\bar{\mu}b^{i}+\bar{\nu}c^{i})

\displaystyle\hskip 193.74939pt=0\;.

(73)

We can cancel the final two terms on the LHS of Eq. (

70

\displaystyle w_{\ell}^{c}C_{\ell}^{ci}=-(\bar{\lambda}a^{i}+\bar{\mu}b^{i}+\bar{\nu}c^{i})\;,

(74)

giving

\displaystyle w_{\ell}^{c}w_{\ell}^{d}C_{\ell}^{ci}C_{\ell}^{dj}-(\bar{\lambda}a^{i}+\bar{\mu}b^{i}+\bar{\nu}c^{i})(\bar{\lambda}b^{j}+\bar{\mu}b^{j}+\bar{\nu}c^{j})

\displaystyle+w_{\ell}^{c}C_{\ell}^{ci}w_{\ell}^{d}C_{\ell}^{dj}-w_{\ell}^{d}C_{\ell}^{dj}w_{\ell}^{c}C_{\ell}^{ci}=0

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle w_{\ell}^{c}w_{\ell}^{d}C_{\ell}^{ci}C_{\ell}^{dj}-(\bar{\lambda}a^{i}+\bar{\mu}b^{i}+\bar{\nu}c^{i})(\bar{\lambda}b^{j}+\bar{\mu}b^{j}+\bar{\nu}c^{j})=0\;.

(75)

Therefore,
(W^{cd}_{\ell},\bar{\lambda}^{\prime},\bar{\mu}^{\prime},\bar{\nu}^{\prime},\bar{\alpha}^{\prime},\bar{\beta}^{\prime},\bar{\gamma}^{\prime})
equals to
(w_{\ell}^{c}w_{\ell}^{d},-\bar{\lambda}^{2},-\bar{\mu}^{2},-\bar{\nu}^{2},-\bar{\lambda}\bar{\mu},-\bar{\lambda}\bar{\nu},-\bar{\mu}\bar{\nu})
is a solution to the
strong

71
). Because the solution is unique, this verifies that the ILC-SpILC equivalence generalizes to multiple components.

Appendix C
Incorporating Data Splits

C.1
Data-split ILC and cILC

Suppose
x^{i}_{p}
is a map constructed from data collected from time
t_{0}
to
t_{0}+\Delta t
. Two noisier maps
x^{i,1}_{p}
and
x^{i,2}_{p}
, referred to as
data splits
, can be constructed from the time segments
[t_{0},t_{0}+\Delta t/2]
and
[t_{0}+\Delta t/2,t_{0}+\Delta t]
respectively. The two time segments are labeled 1 and 2 respectively. The motivation of this construction is that noise in different data splits are assumed to be uncorrelated.

To eliminate the noise bias present in the auto-spectra of ILC and cILC maps, Ref.
Hill and Spergel 2014
proposes to produce ILC (cILC) maps
\hat{s}^{1}_{p}\equiv w^{i}_{\ell}x^{i,1}_{p}
and
\hat{s}^{2}_{p}\equiv w^{i}_{\ell}x^{i,2}_{p}
where weights are determined by minimizing the quantity

\displaystyle\hat{\sigma}^{2}_{12}\equiv\frac{1}{N_{p}}\sum_{p}\hat{s}^{1}_{p}\hat{s}^{2}_{p}\;,

(76)

subject to the corresponding constraints. The auto-spectrum of the signal is then estimated by
\hat{\sigma}^{2}_{12}
. Reference
Hill and Spergel 2014
claims that the weights
w_{\ell}^{i}
has the same functional form as their ILC or cILC counterparts, with
\hat{C}_{\ell}^{ij}
replaced with
\hat{C}^{ij}_{\ell,12}
, defined as

\displaystyle\hat{C}^{ij}_{\ell,12}\equiv\frac{1}{N_{p}}\sum_{p}x^{i,1}_{p}x^{j,2}_{p}\;.

(77)

The elimination of the noise bias can be simply demonstrated:

\displaystyle\langle\hat{\sigma}^{2}_{12}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N_{p}}\sum_{p}\langle(s_{p}+w_{\ell}^{i}n^{i}_{p,1})(s_{p}+w_{\ell}^{j}n^{j}_{p,2})\rangle

\displaystyle=C^{ss}_{\ell}+w_{\ell}^{i}w_{\ell}^{j}\frac{1}{N_{p}}\sum_{p}\langle n^{i}_{1}n^{j}_{2}\rangle=C^{ss}_{\ell}\;.

(78)

C.2
Optimality of the data-split ILC and cILC weights

While replacing
\hat{C}_{\ell}^{ij}
with
\hat{C}^{ij}_{\ell,12}
in the ILC and cILC weights seem to be the obvious modification to incorporate data splits, on closer inspection such weights can be seen only to maximize the (negative) ILC biases:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial w_{\ell}^{i}}\hat{\sigma}^{2}_{12}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial w_{\ell}^{i}}\frac{1}{N_{p}}\sum_{p}(s_{p}+w_{\ell}^{j}n^{j}_{p,1})(s_{p}+w_{\ell}^{k}n^{k}_{p,2})

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial w_{\ell}^{i}}\frac{1}{N_{p}}\sum_{p}\left[w_{\ell}^{j}s_{p}(n^{j}_{p,1}+n^{j}_{p,2})+w_{\ell}^{j}w_{\ell}^{k}n^{j}_{p,1}n^{k}_{p,2}\right]\;.

(79)

In other words, if the ILC biases vanishes or if we determine the weights with true covariances, any set of weights that satisfies the constraints would recover the signal map
s_{p}
identically.

However, using an arbitrary set of weights would not minimize the variance of
\hat{\sigma}^{2}_{12}
:

\displaystyle\operatorname{Var}(\hat{\sigma}^{2}_{12})

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{N_{p}}w_{\ell}^{j}w_{\ell}^{k}w_{\ell}^{m}w_{\ell}^{n}C^{jm}_{\ell,1(1|}C^{kn}_{\ell,2|2)}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{N_{p}}\left((C^{ss}_{\ell})^{2}+\frac{1}{2}w_{\ell}^{j}w_{\ell}^{k}w_{\ell}^{m}w_{\ell}^{n}(2N_{\ell}^{jm})(2N_{\ell}^{kn})\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{N_{p}}(C^{ss}_{\ell})^{2}+\frac{1}{N_{p}}\left[2w_{\ell}^{j}w_{\ell}^{k}N_{\ell}^{jk}\right]^{2}\;,

(80)

where we made the simplifying assumptions of Gaussianity, and temporal stationarity of data such that

\displaystyle\langle n^{j}_{p,1}n^{k}_{p,1}\rangle=\langle n^{j}_{p,2}n^{k}_{p,2}\rangle=2\langle n^{j}_{p}n^{k}_{p}\rangle\equiv 2N_{\ell}^{jk}\;.

(81)

The weights above which only maximizes the (negative) ILC biases does not minimize the quantity
w_{\ell}^{j}w_{\ell}^{k}N_{\ell}^{jk}
, thus do not minimize the variance of the signal auto-spectrum estimator
\hat{\sigma}^{2}_{12}
.

The weights that actually minimize
w_{\ell}^{j}w_{\ell}^{k}(\sigma^{2}_{n})^{jk}
are actually the normal ILC and cILC weights, which minimizes subject to constraints the (ensemble average of) the total error variance:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial w_{\ell}^{i}}\chi^{2}\equiv\frac{\partial}{\partial w_{\ell}^{i}}\langle(\hat{s}_{p}-s_{p})^{2}\rangle=0\;,

\displaystyle\implies\frac{\partial}{\partial w_{\ell}^{i}}w_{\ell}^{j}w_{\ell}^{k}\langle n_{p}^{j}n^{k}_{p}\rangle=\frac{\partial}{\partial w_{\ell}^{i}}w_{\ell}^{j}w_{\ell}^{k}N_{\ell}^{jk}=0\;.

(82)

C.3
Variance of Data-Split Estimators

Consider the data-split estimator in Eq. (
34
),

\displaystyle\hat{K}_{\ell}^{\text{(DS)}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{ij}^{N}W_{\ell}^{ij}\left[\delta_{ij}\hat{C}^{ij}_{\ell,12}+(1-\delta_{ij})\hat{C}_{\ell}^{ij}\right]\;.

(83)

To build this estimator strictly with data-split maps, one replaces in
\hat{K}_{\ell}^{(\text{DS})}

\displaystyle\hat{C}^{ij}_{\ell}\mapsto

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4N_{p}}\sum_{p}(x^{i}_{p,1}+x^{i}_{p,2})(x^{*,j}_{p,1}+x^{*,j}_{p,2})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\hat{C}^{ij}_{\ell,1(1|}+\hat{C}^{ij}_{\ell,2|2)}\right)\;.

(84)

The variance of this estimator is computed to be

\displaystyle\operatorname{Var}(\hat{K}_{\ell}^{(\mathrm{DS})})

\displaystyle=\langle\hat{K}_{\ell}^{(\mathrm{DS})}\hat{K}_{\ell}^{(\mathrm{DS})}\rangle-\langle\hat{K}_{\ell}^{(\mathrm{DS})}\rangle^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N^{2}_{p}}W_{\ell}^{jk}W_{\ell}^{mn}\sum_{p,q}\delta_{jk}\delta_{mn}\left[\langle x^{j}_{p_{1}}x^{k}_{p_{2}}x^{m}_{q_{1}}x^{n}_{q_{2}}\rangle-\langle x^{j}_{p_{1}}x^{k}_{p_{2}}\rangle\langle x^{m}_{q_{1}}x^{n}_{q_{2}}\rangle\right]+

\displaystyle\phantom{{}={}}\frac{1}{N^{2}_{p}}W_{\ell}^{jk}W_{\ell}^{mn}\sum_{p,q}\delta_{jk}(1-\delta_{mn})\left[\langle x^{j}_{p_{1}}x^{k}_{p_{2}}x^{m}_{q}x^{n}_{q}\rangle-\langle x^{j}_{p_{1}}x^{k}_{p_{2}}\rangle\langle x^{m}_{q}x^{n}_{q}\rangle\right]+

\displaystyle\phantom{{}={}}\frac{1}{N^{2}_{p}}W_{\ell}^{jk}W_{\ell}^{mn}\sum_{p,q}(1-\delta_{jk})\delta_{mn}\left[\langle x^{j}_{p}x^{k}_{p}x^{m}_{q_{1}}x^{n}_{q_{2}}\rangle-\langle x^{j}_{p}x^{k}_{p}\rangle\langle x^{m}_{q_{1}}x^{n}_{q_{2}}\rangle\right]+

\displaystyle\phantom{{}={}}\frac{1}{N^{2}_{p}}W_{\ell}^{jk}W_{\ell}^{mn}\sum_{p,q}(1-\delta_{jk})(1-\delta_{mn})\left[\langle x^{j}_{p}x^{k}_{p}x^{m}_{q}x^{n}_{q}\rangle-\langle x^{j}_{p}x^{k}_{p}\rangle\langle x^{m}_{q}x^{n}_{q}\rangle\right]

Applying Wick’s theorem,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Var}(\hat{K}_{\ell}^{(\mathrm{DS})})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N^{2}_{p}}W_{\ell}^{jk}W_{\ell}^{mn}\sum_{p,q}\delta_{jk}\delta_{mn}\left[\langle x^{j}_{p_{1}}x^{m}_{q_{1}}\rangle\langle x^{k}_{p_{2}}x^{n}_{q_{2}}\rangle+\langle x^{j}_{p_{1}}x^{n}_{q_{2}}\rangle\langle x^{k}_{p_{2}}x^{m}_{q_{1}}\rangle\right]+

\displaystyle\phantom{{}={}}\frac{1}{N^{2}_{p}}W_{\ell}^{jk}W_{\ell}^{mn}\sum_{p,q}\delta_{jk}(1-\delta_{mn})\left[\langle x^{j}_{p_{1}}x^{m}_{q}\rangle\langle x^{k}_{p_{2}}x^{n}_{q}\rangle+\langle x^{j}_{p_{1}}x^{n}_{q}\rangle\langle x^{k}_{p_{2}}x^{m}_{q}\rangle\right]+

\displaystyle\phantom{{}={}}\frac{1}{N^{2}_{p}}W_{\ell}^{jk}W_{\ell}^{mn}\sum_{p,q}(1-\delta_{jk})\delta_{mn}\left[\langle x^{j}_{p}x^{m}_{q_{1}}\rangle\langle x^{k}_{p}x^{n}_{q_{2}}\rangle+\langle x^{j}_{p}x^{n}_{q_{2}}\rangle\langle x^{k}_{p}x^{m}_{q_{1}}\rangle\right]+

\displaystyle\phantom{{}={}}\frac{1}{N^{2}_{p}}W_{\ell}^{jk}W_{\ell}^{mn}\sum_{p,q}(1-\delta_{jk})(1-\delta_{mn})\left[\langle x^{j}_{p}x^{m}_{q}\rangle\langle x^{k}_{p}x^{n}_{q}\rangle+\langle x^{j}_{p}x^{n}_{q}\rangle\langle x^{k}_{p}x^{m}_{q}\rangle\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{N^{2}_{p}}W_{\ell}^{jk}W_{\ell}^{mn}\sum_{p,q}\left[\delta_{jk}\delta_{mn}\langle x^{j}_{p_{1}}x^{m}_{(q_{1}|}\rangle\langle x^{k}_{p_{2}}x^{n}_{|q_{2})}\rangle+\delta_{jk}(1-\delta_{mn})\langle x^{j}_{p_{1}}x^{m}_{q}\rangle\langle x^{k}_{p_{2}}x^{n}_{q}\rangle\right]+

\displaystyle\phantom{{}={}}\frac{2}{N^{2}_{p}}W_{\ell}^{jk}W_{\ell}^{mn}\sum_{p,q}\left[(1-\delta_{jk})\delta_{mn}\langle x^{j}_{p}x^{m}_{q_{1}}\rangle\langle x^{k}_{p}x^{n}_{q_{2}}\rangle+(1-\delta_{jk})(1-\delta_{mn})\langle x^{j}_{p}x^{m}_{q}\rangle\langle x^{k}_{p}x^{n}_{q}\rangle\right]

(85)

\displaystyle\implies\operatorname{Var}(\hat{K}_{\ell}^{(\mathrm{DS})})

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{2\ell+1}W_{\ell}^{jk}W_{\ell}^{mn}\left[\delta_{jk}\delta_{mn}{C}^{jm}_{\ell,1{(1|}}{C}^{kn}_{\ell,2{|2)}}+\frac{2}{4}\delta_{jk}(1-\delta_{mn})({C}^{jm}_{\ell,11}+{C}^{jm}_{\ell,12})({C}^{kn}_{\ell,21}+{C}^{kn}_{\ell,22})\right.

\displaystyle\phantom{{}={}\frac{2}{2\ell+1}W_{\ell}^{jk}W_{\ell}^{mn}}\left.\ +(1-\delta_{jk})(1-\delta_{mn}){C}^{jm}_{\ell}{C}^{kn}_{\ell}\right]\;.

(86)

The expressions for the data-split weights are calculated through the replacement in the
D^{\mu\nu}_{\ell}
matrix in Eq. (
47
) to (summation not implied for repeated indices, and color / subscript Greek
\alpha,\beta
indices denote the set of indices to be symmetrized):

\displaystyle\hat{C}_{\ell}^{(m|i}\hat{C}_{\ell}^{|n)j}\mapsto

\displaystyle\delta_{ij}\delta_{mn}\hat{C}^{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}(m_{\alpha}|}{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}(i_{\beta}|}}_{\ell,(1|1}\hat{C}^{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}|n_{\alpha})}{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}|j_{\beta})}}_{\ell,|2)2}

\displaystyle+\delta_{ij}(1-\delta_{mn})\hat{C}^{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}(m_{\alpha}|}{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}(i_{\beta}|}}_{\ell,11+21}\hat{C}^{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}|n_{\alpha})}{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}|j_{\beta})}}_{\ell,12+22}

\displaystyle+(1-\delta_{ij})\delta_{mn}\hat{C}^{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}(m_{\alpha}|}{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}(i_{\beta}|}}_{\ell,11+12}\hat{C}^{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}|n_{\alpha})}{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}|j_{\beta})}}_{\ell,21+22}

\displaystyle+(1-\delta_{ij})(1-\delta_{mn})\hat{C}_{\ell}^{(m|i}\hat{C}^{|n)j}_{\ell}\;,

(87)

where

\displaystyle\hat{C}_{\ell,11+12}^{ij}\equiv\frac{1}{2}(\hat{C}^{ij}_{11}+\hat{C}^{ij}_{12})\;,

(88)

and similarly for
\hat{C}^{ij}_{\ell,21+22}
,
\hat{C}^{ij}_{\ell,11+21}
and
\hat{C}^{ij}_{\ell,12+22}
.

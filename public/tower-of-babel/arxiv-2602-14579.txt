Brauer group of moduli stacks of parabolic principal bundles over a curve
Indranil Biswas, Sujoy Chakraborty

Abstract
. We prove that the Brauer group of the moduli stack of parabolic stable principal PGL ​ ( r , ℂ ) \textnormal{PGL}(r,\mathbb{C}) -bundles on a curve X X , for a generic system of weights along an arbitrary parabolic divisor, coincides with the Brauer group of the smooth locus of the corresponding coarse moduli space of parabolic stable principal PGL ​ ( r , ℂ ) \textnormal{PGL}(r,\mathbb{C}) -bundles. We also show that for any simple and simply connected complex linear algebraic group G G , the analytic and algebraic Brauer groups of the moduli stack of quasi-parabolic principal G G -bundles on X X vanish.

1.
Introduction

The cohomological Brauer group of a quasi-projective variety
Y
over
\mathbb{C}
, denoted by
\Br(Y)
, is
defined to be the torsion part
H^{2}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(Y,\,\mathbb{G}_{m})_{\rm torsion}
. When
Y
is smooth, it is known that

H^{2}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(Y,\,\mathbb{G}_{m})
is already torsion. Brauer groups are interesting invariants to study for a
number of reasons. It is a stable birational invariant for smooth projective varieties,
making it very useful in studying rationality questions. In fact, Brauer group has been used in constructing
examples of non-rational varieties by many, including Colliot-Thél
\grave{e}
ne, Saltman, Peyre and
others. It also plays a central role in Brauer-Manin obstruction theory, which deals with the study of
rational points on varieties defined over a number field.

The study of Brauer groups in the context of moduli of parabolic vector bundles over curves has been carried
out in recent times by several authors
[
BB
,
BCD1
,
BCD2
,
BD
]
. The computation of the Brauer group of
the moduli space of stable parabolic bundles for structure groups
\text{SL}(r,\mathbb{C})
,

\textnormal{PGL}(r,\mathbb{C})
and
\textnormal{Sp}(r,\mathbb{C})
have been carried out earlier in
[
BD
,
BCD1
,
BCD2
]
. Our aim here is to generalize an earlier result of
[
BCD1
]
for the
case of
\text{PGL}(r,\mathbb{C})
-bundles, as well as study the general case for any simple and simply
connected linear algebraic group
G
over
\mathbb{C}
.

More precisely, suppose
X
is a smooth projective curve of genus
g
defined over
\mathbb{C}
, with
g\,\geq\,2
. Fix a line
bundle
\xi
on
X
, and systems of multiplicities and weights
(\boldsymbol{m,\,\alpha})
along a set of
parabolic points on
X
(see Section
2
for details). Let

\mathfrak{N}_{X}^{\boldsymbol{m,\alpha}}(r,\delta)
denote the moduli stack of parabolic stable principal

\text{PGL}(r,\mathbb{C})
-bundles on
X
of topological type
\delta\ \equiv\ \deg(\xi)\ \,(\text{mod}\ r)
(see
Section
4
), and let
\Nalpha
denote the corresponding coarse moduli space
of stable parabolic principal
\textnormal{PGL}(r,\mathbb{C})
-bundles. Our first main result is the generalization of

[
BCD1
, Theorem 1.1 (1)]
for
any
generic system of weights (earlier it was shown only for

full-flag
systems of weights).

Theorem 1.1

(Theorem
4.2
)
.

Let
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
be a generic system of weights. Let
\Nalpha^{sm}
denote the smooth locus of
\Nalpha
. Then,

\Br\left(\mathfrak{N}^{\boldsymbol{m,\alpha}}_{X}(r,\delta)\right)\,\ \simeq\,\ \Br\left(\Nalpha^{sm}\right).

Let
G
be a simple and simply connected linear algebraic group
defined over
\mathbb{C}
. Fix a sequence
\underline{p}\,=\,(p_{1},\,p_{2},\,\cdots,\,p_{n})
of points on
X
, as well
as a sequence
\underline{P}\,=\,(P_{1},\,P_{2},\,\cdots,\,P_{n})
of parabolic subgroups of
G
. Let
\mgpar\left(\underline{p},\ \underline{P}\right)
denote the moduli stack of quasi-parabolic principal
G
-bundles on
X
of type
\underline{P}
(see
[
LS
]
for details).

Theorem 1.2

(Proposition
5.7
and Theorem

5.8
)
.

Both the analytic and the algebraic Brauer group of
\mgpar\left(\underline{p},\ \underline{P}\right)
vanish.
In other words, if
\mathcal{O}^{{}^{an}}_{\mgpar\left(\underline{p},\ \underline{P}\right)}
is
the sheaf of analytic functions on
\mgpar\left(\underline{p},\ \underline{P}\right)
, then

H^{2}\left(\mgpar,\ (\mathcal{O}^{{}^{an}}_{\mgpar})^{*}\right)_{torsion}\ =\ 0\ =\ \Br\left(\mgpar\left(\underline{p},\ \underline{P}\right)\right).

2.
Preliminaries on parabolic vector bundles

Let
X
be a connected smooth projective curve, defined over
\mathbb{C}
, of genus
g
, with
g\,\geq\,2
. Fix a
finite subset
S\,\subset\,X
, which will be referred to as the
parabolic points
. The set

S
shall remain fixed throughout.

Definition 2.1
.

A
parabolic vector bundle
of rank
r
on
X
is an algebraic vector bundle
E

on
X
of rank
r
together with a weighted flag on the fiber
E_{p}
of
E
over every
p\,\in\,S
:

\displaystyle E_{{}_{p}}\,=\,E_{{}_{p,1}}\,

\displaystyle\supsetneq\,E_{{}_{p,2}}\,\supsetneq\,\cdots\,\supsetneq\,E_{{}_{p,\ell(p)}}\,\supsetneq\,E_{{}_{p,\ell(p)+1}}\,=\,0

(2.1)

\displaystyle 0\,\leq\,

\displaystyle\alpha_{{}_{p,1}}\,<\,\alpha_{{}_{p,2}}\,<\,\cdots\,<\,\alpha_{{}_{p,\ell(p)}}\,<\,\alpha_{{}_{p,\ell(p)+1}}=1,

where
\alpha_{{}_{p,i}}\,\in\,\mathbb{R}
.

\bullet

Such a flag is said to be of length
\ell(p)
, and the numbers
m_{{}_{p,i}}\,:=\,\dim E_{{}_{p,i}}-\dim E_{{}_{p,i+1}}
are called the
multiplicities
of the flag at
p
. More precisely,

m_{{}_{p,i}}
is the multiplicity associated to the weight
\alpha_{{}_{p,i}}
.

\bullet

The flag at
p
is said to be
full
if
m_{{}_{p,i}}\,=\,1
for every
i
, in
which case clearly we have
\ell(p)\,=\,r
.

\bullet

The collection of real numbers
\boldsymbol{\alpha}\,:=\,\{(\alpha_{{}_{p,1}}\,<\,\alpha_{{}_{p,2}}\,<\,\cdots\,<\,\alpha_{{}_{p,\ell(p)}})\}_{p\in S}
is called a system
of weights, and the collection of integers
\boldsymbol{m}\ :=\ \{(m_{{}_{p,1}},\ m_{{}_{p,2}},\,\cdots,\,m_{{}_{p,\ell(p)}})\}_{p\in S}
is called a system of multiplicities.

\bullet

We will often denote a system of multiplicities (respectively, a system of weights) by

\boldsymbol{m}
(respectively,
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
), when there is no scope of any confusion. Also,
a parabolic vector bundle of the above type will also be denoted by
E_{*}
.

If we ignore the weights and only consider the data of a vector bundle
E
together with a given
filtration on the fibers
\{E_{p}\}_{p\in S}
, then such an object will be called a

quasi-parabolic vector bundle
. Thus, a quasi-parabolic vector bundle has an associated system
of multiplicities, but no associated system of weights. In Section
5

quasi-parabolic bundles will appear in a more general context.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
E_{*}
and
F_{*}
be two parabolic vector bundles with systems of multiplicities and
weights
(\boldsymbol{m,\,\alpha})
and
(\boldsymbol{m^{\prime},\,\alpha^{\prime}})

respectively. A
parabolic morphism

f_{*}\ :\ E_{*}\ \longrightarrow\ F_{*}

is an
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
–linear homomorphism
f\,:\,E\,\longrightarrow\,F
between
the underlying vector bundles such that at each parabolic point
p
, we have
f_{{}_{p}}(E^{i}_{{}_{p}})\,\subset\,{F}^{j+1}_{{}_{p}}
whenever
\alpha^{i}_{{}_{p}}\,\geq\,{\alpha^{\prime}}^{j}_{{}_{p}}
.

Definition 2.3
.

Consider a parabolic vector bundle
E_{*}
of rank
r
as in Definition
2.1
. The
parabolic degree
of
E_{*}
is defined to be the real number

\text{par}\deg(E_{*})\ :=\ \deg(E)+\sum_{p\in S}\sum_{i=1}^{\ell(p)}m_{{}_{p,i}}\alpha_{{}_{p,i}}.

(2.2)

The
parabolic slope
of
E_{*}
is defined to be the real number

\text{par}\mu(E_{*})\ :=\ \frac{\text{par}\deg(E_{*})}{r}.

(2.3)

A parabolic vector bundle
E_{*}
is called
parabolic semistable
(respectively,

parabolic stable
) if for every proper sub-bundle
0\,\not=\,F\,\subset\,E
we have

\textnormal{par}\mu(F_{*})\,\leq\,\textnormal{par}\mu(E_{*})\ \ \,(\text{respectively, }\ \textnormal{par}\mu(F_{*})\,<\,\textnormal{par}\mu(E_{*})),

where
F_{*}
denotes the parabolic bundle defined by the parabolic
structure on
F
induced by the parabolic structure of
E_{*}
(see
[
MS
]
for
the details).

Of course, every parabolic stable vector bundle is parabolic semistable, but the converse is not true in
general. Systems of weights for which parabolic semistability and parabolic stability coincide are called

generic
(see
[
BY
]
for more details).

2.1.
Parabolic push-forward and pull-back

Let
Y
be another irreducible smooth projective complex curve and
\gamma\,:\,Y\,\longrightarrow\,X
a finite étale Galois covering morphism. If
F
is a vector bundle on
Y
of rank
n
, then
the direct image
\gamma_{*}F
is a vector bundle on
X
of rank
nd
, where
d
is the
degree of the map
\gamma
. Given a parabolic structure on
F
along parabolic points
S^{\prime}\,\subset\,Y
, there is a natural way to construct a parabolic structure on
\gamma_{*}F
along

\gamma(S^{\prime})\,\subset\,X

[
BM
, § 3]
. Below, this construction will be briefly recalled.

Let
S\,\subset\,X
be a finite set of points, and suppose
F\,\longrightarrow\,Y
is a vector bundle
equipped with a parabolic structure
over
\gamma^{-1}(S)\,\subset\,Y
. For convenience, consider the special case where
S\,=\,\{p\}\,\subset\,X
is a singleton. Let
d\,=\,\deg(\gamma)
. Since
\gamma
is unramified, the inverse
image
\gamma^{-1}(p)
consists of
d
distinct points of
Y
. Suppose the parabolic structure of

F_{*}
over
q\,\in\,\gamma^{-1}(p)
is as follows:
the parabolic data looks like

\displaystyle F_{q}\,=\,F_{{}_{q,1}}\,\supsetneq\,F_{{}_{q,2}}\,\supsetneq\,\cdots\,\supsetneq\,F_{{}_{q,\ell(q)}}\,\supsetneq\,F_{{}_{q,\ell(q)+1}}\,=\,0,

(2.4)

\displaystyle 0\,\leq\,\alpha_{{}_{q,1}}\,<\,\alpha_{{}_{q,2}}\,<\,\cdots<\,\alpha_{{}_{q,\ell(q)}}\,<\,\alpha_{{}_{q,\ell(q)+1}}\,=\,1.

(2.5)

We shall construct a parabolic structure on
E\,=\,\gamma_{*}F
along the point
\{p\}
from these data. Note that

E_{p}\,=\,\bigoplus_{q\,\in\,\gamma^{-1}(p)}F_{q}.

Define a positive integer
\ell(p)
and real numbers
\{\beta_{{}_{p,1}},\,\beta_{{}_{p,2}},\,\cdots,\,\beta_{{}_{p,\ell(p)}}\}
as follows:

\{\beta_{{}_{p,1}},\,\beta_{{}_{p,2}},\,\cdots,\,\beta_{{}_{p,\ell(p)}}\}\ =\ \bigcup_{q\in\gamma^{-1}(p)}\{\alpha_{{}_{q,1}},\ \ \cdots,\ \alpha_{{}_{q,\ell(q)}}\}.

Also assume that the
\beta_{{}_{p,i}}
’s form an increasing sequence

\left(\beta_{{}_{p,1}}\,<\,\beta_{{}_{p,2}}\,<\,\cdots\,<\,\beta_{{}_{p,\ell(p)}}\right)
. These will be
the weights at
\{p\}
.

For each
1\,\leq\,j\,\leq\,\ell(p)
, define a filtration of
E_{p}
by the subspaces

E_{{}_{p,j}}\,:=\,\bigoplus_{q\in\gamma^{-1}(p)}F_{{}_{q,t(q)}},

where for each point
q\,\in\,\gamma^{-1}(S)
, the integer
t(q)\,\in\,[1,\,\ell(q)]
(see
(
2.4
)) is the smallest one satisfying
\beta_{{}_{p,j}}\,\leq\,\alpha_{{}_{q,t(q)}}.
Consequently,

\displaystyle E_{{}_{p,1}}\,\supsetneq\,E_{{}_{p,2}}\,\supsetneq\,\cdots\,\supsetneq\,E_{{}_{p,\ell(p)}}\ \supsetneq E_{{}_{p,\ell(p)+1}}=0

\displaystyle 0\,\leq\,\beta_{{}_{p,1}}\,<\,\beta_{{}_{p,2}}\,<\,\cdots\,<\,\beta_{{}_{p,\ell(p)}}\ <\ \beta_{{}_{p,\ell(p)+1}}=1

is a parabolic structure on
E=\gamma_{*}(F)
along the point
\{p\}
.

More generally, if
S\,\subset\,X
is a finite subset, and a parabolic vector bundle
F_{*}
on
Y
is given
with a parabolic structure along
\gamma^{-1}(S)
, one can perform the above
construction for each
p\,\in\,S
to obtain a
parabolic push-forward
E_{*}\ =\ \gamma_{*}(F_{*})
on
X
with parabolic structure along
S
.

Let
\gamma\,:\,Y\,\longrightarrow\,X
be as above. If
E_{*}
is a parabolic vector bundle on
X
with parabolic
structure along
S
, then
\gamma^{*}E
has an induced parabolic structure along
\gamma^{-1}(S)
by assigning,
for any
p\,\in\,S
and
q\in\gamma^{-1}(p)
, the same filtration and weights on the fiber

(\gamma^{*}E)_{q}\,=\,E_{p}
as those on
E_{p}
.

3.
Fixed points of moduli of parabolic
\text{SL}(r,\mathbb{C})
-bundles

Fix a positive integer
r
and a line bundle
\xi
on
X
. Also, fix a system of weights and multiplicities

(\boldsymbol{m,\,\alpha})
along parabolic points
S\,\subset\,X
. In this section, we shall assume that

\boldsymbol{\alpha}
is a
generic
system of weights, so that a parabolic vector bundle is parabolic
stable if it is parabolic semistable. Let

\Malpha

denote the moduli space of stable parabolic vector bundles
of rank
r
on
X
with determinant
\xi
and systems of multiplicities and weights
(\boldsymbol{m,\,\alpha})

along
S
. Let

\Gamma\ :=\ \{L\in\Pic(X)\ \ \big|\,\ L^{\otimes r}\,\simeq\,\mathcal{O}_{X}\}

denote the group of
r
-torsion line bundles on
X
. For any
E_{*}\,\in\,\Malpha
and
L\,\in\,\Gamma
, it is clear that
E_{*}\otimes L\,\in\,\Malpha
.
Thus, the group
\Gamma
acts on
\Malpha
.

If one additionally assumes that the system of multiplicities
\boldsymbol{m}
is of
full-flag
type,
i.e.,
m_{{}_{p,i}}\,=\,1
for all
1\,\leq\,i\,\leq\,\ell(p)
and every
p\,\in\,S
, then it was shown in

[
BCD1
]
that the
codimension of the fixed point locus of
\Malpha
under any non-trivial
r
-torsion line bundle
L\in\Gamma
is
at least
3
. This codimension estimation will be extended for
any
generic system of
weights
\boldsymbol{m}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
V
be a finite-dimensional vector space over a field
k
with a diagonalizable endomorphism

\varphi\,:\,V\,\longrightarrow\,V
and a filtration by subspaces

V\ =\ V_{1}\ \supsetneq\ V_{2}\ \supsetneq\ \cdots\ \supsetneq V_{\ell}\ \supsetneq\ 0

such that
\varphi(V_{i})=V_{i}
for all
1\,\leq\,i\,\leq\,\ell
. There exists a basis
\mathcal{B}_{i}
of
V_{i}

consisting of eigenvectors for
\varphi
, satisfying
\mathcal{B}_{i}\,\supset\,\mathcal{B}_{i+1}
for all

1\,\leq\,i\,\leq\,\ell
.

Proof.

By standard theory of semisimple modules,
\varphi|_{V_{i}}
is diagonalizable on
V_{i}
for each
i
, so
there exists a basis
\mathcal{B}_{i}
of
V_{i}
consisting of eigenvectors. Moreover, one can write

V_{i-1}\,=\,W_{i-1}\oplus V_{i}
for some subspace
W_{i-1}
which is invariant under
\varphi
. Thus,
one can start with a basis
\mathcal{B}_{\ell}
of
V_{\ell}
consisting of eigenvectors, next choose a direct
summand
W_{\ell-1}
of
V_{\ell}
in
V_{\ell-1}
, namely
V_{\ell-1}\,=\,W_{\ell-1}\oplus V_{\ell}
, and choose an
eigenbasis of
W_{\ell-1}
; its union with
\mathcal{B}_{\ell}
produces a basis
\mathcal{B}_{\ell-1}
for
V_{\ell-1}

consisting of eigenvectors and satisfying
\mathcal{B}_{\ell-1}\,\supset\,\mathcal{B}_{\ell}
. Proceeding inductively, one
can produce bases
\mathcal{B}_{i}
of
V_{i}
. This proves the lemma.
∎

For any
L\,\in\,\Gamma\setminus\{\mathcal{O}_{X}\}
, let

\MLalpha\,\ \subset\,\ \Malpha

be the locus of fixed points for the action of
L
— through tensoring — on
\Malpha
.
If
d\,=\,\text{ord}(L)
, choosing a nowhere-vanishing section
s_{0}\,\in\,H^{0}(X,\,L^{\otimes d})
,
construct the spectral curve

\displaystyle Y\,:=\,\{v\,\in\,L\,\ \mid\,\ v^{\otimes d}\,\in\,s_{0}(X)\}.

(3.1)

The natural projection
\gamma\,:\,Y\,\longrightarrow\,X
is an étale Galois covering
with Galois group
{\mathbb{Z}}/d{\mathbb{Z}}
. The isomorphism class of this
covering
\gamma
does not depend on the choice of the above section
s_{0}
.

For simplicity, let us first consider the case where
S\,=\,\{p\}
is a singleton set. Thus we have a
system of weights
(\alpha_{{}_{p,1}}\,<\ \alpha_{{}_{p,2}}\ <\,\cdots\,<\ \alpha_{{}_{p,\ell(p)}})
at
p\in X
. Consider the spectral curve
\gamma:Y\longrightarrow X
from (
3.1
). Let

\textnormal{Gal}(\gamma)\ =\ \{1,\,\mu,\,\mu^{2},\,\cdots,\,\mu^{d-1}\}\,\subset\,\mathbb{C}^{*}.

The action of
\textnormal{Gal}(\gamma)
on
\gamma^{-1}(p)
is via multiplication. Fix
an ordering on the points of
\gamma^{-1}(p)
, say

\displaystyle\gamma^{-1}(p)\ =\ \{q_{1},\,q_{2},\,\cdots,\,q_{d}\},

(3.2)

such that
\mu^{i}
acts on
\gamma^{-1}(p)
as the cyclic permutation sending
q_{j}
to
q_{j+i}
, where the
subscript
(j+i)
is to be understood modulo

.

Now, recall that the systems of multiplicities and weights at
p
are given by

\left(m_{{}_{p,1}},\ m_{{}_{p,2}},\ \cdots,\ m_{{}_{p,\ell(p)}}\right)\ \ \text{ and }\ \ \left(\alpha_{{}_{p,1}},\ \alpha_{{}_{p,2}},\ \cdots,\ \alpha_{{}_{p,\ell(p)}}\right)

respectively. Let
\boldsymbol{P}
denote the collection of all nonempty subsets of
\left\{\alpha_{{}_{p,1}},\ \alpha_{{}_{p,2}},\ \cdots,\ \alpha_{{}_{p,\ell(p)}}\right\}

at most

\frac{r}{d}
, and let

\boldsymbol{T}\ \ :=\ \ \boldsymbol{P}\underset{d\ \textnormal{times}}{\times\cdots\times}\boldsymbol{P},

where

.

For each
\textbf{t}\,\in\,\textbf{T}
, we want to describe a procedure of associating a system of weights, as
well as a collection of systems of multiplicities along
\gamma^{-1}(p)\,=\,\{q_{1},\ q_{2},\ \cdots,\ q_{d}\}
.
First associate a system of weights to each
\textbf{t}\,\in\,\textbf{T}
as follows: adopt the notation

\textbf{t}\ =\ \left(\lambda_{1}(\textbf{t}),\ \lambda_{2}(\textbf{t}),\ \cdots,\ \lambda_{d}(\textbf{t})\right),

(3.4)

where each
\lambda_{j}(\textbf{t})\neq\emptyset

\lambda_{j}(\textbf{t})
can be
arranged into an increasing sequence. Denote by
\lambda_{j}(\textbf{t})
the set of weights at
q_{j}
for

1\,\leq\,j\,\leq\,d
. Thus, each
\textbf{t}\,\in\,\boldsymbol{T}

\gamma^{-1}(p)
. Denote this system of weights determined by

\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\textbf{t}}
.

Next, for each fixed
\textbf{t}\,\in\,\textbf{T}

\displaystyle A_{\textbf{t}}

be the collection of all matrices of size

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}n_{{}_{q_{1},1}}&n_{{}_{q_{1},2}}&\cdots&\cdots&n_{{}_{q_{1},\ell(p)}}\\

(a)

\left(n_{{}_{q_{j},1}},\ n_{{}_{q_{j},2}},\ \cdots,\ n_{{}_{q_{j},\ell(p)}}\right)

(b)

the entries in each row of the matrix must add up to

, while the entries

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\ell(p)}n_{{}_{q_{j},k}}\

\displaystyle=\ \frac{r}{d}\ \ \ \forall\ \ j\,\in\,[1,\,d]

\displaystyle\text{and}\ \ \sum_{j=1}^{d}n_{{}_{q_{j},k}}\

\displaystyle=\ m_{{}_{p,k}}\ \ \ \forall\ \ k\,\in\,[1,\,\ell(p)].

.

Proposition 3.3

Assume that

Proof.

For the sake of avoiding complicated notation, we assume that there is only one parabolic point

4.
Brauer group of the moduli stack of parabolic

\text{PGL}(r,\mathbb{C})
-bundles

As before, fix a finite set of points
S\,\subset\,X
, a positive integer
r
and line bundle
\xi
on
X
.

Definition 4.1
.

A parabolic stable
\textnormal{PGL}(r,\mathbb{C})
-bundle is an equivalence class of parabolic stable vector bundles, where two parabolic stable vector bundles

and

. The coarse moduli space of parabolic stable

–bundles on
X
of topological type
\delta

will be denoted by

Also, denote by
\mathfrak{N}^{\boldsymbol{m,\alpha}}_{X}(r,\delta)
the moduli
stack
of parabolic stable

\text{PGL}(r,\mathbb{C})
–bundles.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
be a generic system of weights. Let
\Nalpha^{sm}
denote the smooth locus of
\Nalpha
. Then,

\Br\left(\mathfrak{N}^{\boldsymbol{m,\alpha}}_{X}(r,\delta)\right)\,\ \simeq\,\ \Br\left(\Nalpha^{sm}\right).

Proof.

Recall that the group
\Gamma
of
r

act on the moduli space
\Malpha
of parabolic
stable vector bundles. Through this action,

is naturally isomorphic to the quotient variety

(see
[
BLS
, p. 196,
6
]
).

Let
Z_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
be as in Corollary
3.5
, and denote

\mathcal{U}\,:=\,\Malpha\setminus Z_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
. Consider the following diagram:

\textstyle{[\mathcal{U}/\Gamma]\ \ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ }
\textstyle{\ [\Malpha/\Gamma]\ \ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ }
\textstyle{\ \mathfrak{N}^{\boldsymbol{m,\alpha}}_{X}(r,\delta)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathcal{U}/\Gamma\ \ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ }
\textstyle{\ \Malpha/\Gamma\ \ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ }

n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, we have

\mathcal{Q}_{G,n}\ :=\ \mathcal{Q}_{G}\ \underbrace{\underset{\mathcal{M}_{G}}{\times}\ \mathcal{Q}_{G}\ \underset{\mathcal{M}_{G}}{\times}\ \cdots\ \underset{\mathcal{M}_{G}}{\times}}_{n\ times}\ \mathcal{Q}_{G}.

Using the fact that
\mathcal{Q}_{G}\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}_{G}
is a principal
L_{X}(G)
–bundle, it can be shown that

\displaystyle H^{*}_{B}\left(\mathcal{M}_{G},\ \mathbb{Z}\right)\ \ \simeq\ \ \mathbb{H}^{*}_{B}\left(\mathcal{Q}_{G,\bullet},\ \mathbb{Z}\right);

(5.6)

see the proof of
[
Te
, Proposition 4.1]
.
Now, if
\mathcal{Q}_{G,\bullet}^{par}
analogously denotes the simplicial space of fibered powers of
\mathcal{Q}_{G}^{par}
over
\mgpar
, we observe that for each
n\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{G,n}^{par}

\displaystyle\ =\ \mathcal{Q}_{G}^{par}\ \underbrace{\underset{\mgpar}{\times}\ \mathcal{Q}^{par}_{G}\ \underset{\mgpar}{\times}\ \cdots\ \underset{\mgpar}{\times}}_{n\ times}\ \mathcal{Q}^{par}_{G}

\displaystyle\ \simeq\ (\mathcal{Q}_{G}\ \underbrace{\underset{\mathcal{M}_{G}}{\times}\ \mathcal{Q}_{G}\ \underset{\mathcal{M}_{G}}{\times}\ \cdots\ \underset{\mathcal{M}_{G}}{\times}}_{n\ times}\ \mathcal{Q}_{G})\times F\ \ (\text{using the Cartesian diagram \eqref{diag:cartesian-diagram-1}})

\displaystyle\ \simeq\ \mathcal{Q}_{G,n}\times F.

In other words,
\mathcal{Q}_{G,\bullet}^{par}\,\simeq\,\mathcal{Q}_{G,\bullet}\times F
as simplicial spaces, and
consequently by Leray–Hirsch theorem it follows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{*}_{B}\left(\mathcal{Q}_{G,\bullet}^{par},\ \mathbb{Z}\right)\ \simeq\ \mathbb{H}_{B}^{*}\left(\mathcal{Q}_{G,\bullet},\ \mathbb{Z}\right)\otimes H^{*}_{B}\left(F,\ \mathbb{Z}\right).

(5.7)

Now, since
\mathcal{Q}_{G}^{par}\,\longrightarrow\,\mgpar
is a principal
L_{X}(G)
–bundle, as in (
5.6
) we again get

H^{*}_{B}\left(\mgpar,\ \mathbb{Z}\right)\ \simeq\ \mathbb{H}^{*}_{B}\left(\mathcal{Q}_{G,\bullet}^{par},\ \mathbb{Z}\right).

Using (
5.7
), we conclude that

H^{*}_{B}\left(\mgpar,\ \mathbb{Z}\right)\ \simeq\ \mathbb{H}_{B}^{*}\left(\mathcal{Q}_{G,\bullet},\ \mathbb{Z}\right)\otimes H^{*}_{B}\left(F,\ \mathbb{Z}\right)\ \underset{\eqref{eqn:cohomology}}{\simeq}\ H^{*}_{B}\left(\mathcal{M}_{G},\ \mathbb{Z}\right)\otimes H^{*}_{B}\left(F,\ \mathbb{Z}\right).

This proves the lemma.
∎

The following result helps in comparing the Brauer group of an algebraic stack with certain Betti cohomology
group, under certain assumptions on the stack.

Proposition 5.5

(
[
BHol
, Proposition 2.1]
)
.

Let
Y
be an algebraic stack satisfying the following two properties:

(1)

Any class in
H_{B}^{2}(Y,\ \mathbb{Z})
is represented by a holomorphic line bundle on
Y
.

(2)

Each holomorphic line bundle on
Y
admits an algebraic structure.

Then there are isomorphisms

\Br\left(Y\right)\ \simeq\ H^{2}(Y,\ \mathcal{O}^{*}_{Y,an})_{torsion}\ \simeq\ H_{B}^{3}(Y,\ \mathbb{Z})_{torsion}.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
\mathcal{O}^{{}^{an}}_{\mathcal{Q}^{par}_{G}}
denote the sheaf of analytic functions on
\mathcal{Q}^{par}_{G}
. Then

\Br\left(\mathcal{Q}^{par}_{G}\right)\ \simeq\ H^{2}\left(\mathcal{Q}_{G}^{par},\ (\mathcal{O}^{{}^{an}}_{\mathcal{Q}^{par}_{G}})^{*}\right)_{torsion}\ =\ 0.

Proof.

By definition,
\mathcal{Q}_{G}^{par}\,=\,\mathcal{Q}_{G}\times F
, where
F\,:=\,\prod_{i=1}^{n}G/P_{i}
. It is known that
\mathcal{Q}_{G}
is the direct limit of certain projective varieties
\{Q_{w}\}
, where
w
varies
in the affine Weyl group (see the proof of
[
BHol
, Proposition 3.1]
for details). Thus
\mathcal{Q}_{G}^{par}
is the direct
limit of the projective varieties
\{Q_{w}\times F\}
. Also,

\displaystyle H^{1}\left(Q_{w},\ \mathbb{Z}\right)\ =\ H^{3}\left(Q_{w},\ \mathbb{Z}\right)\ \ =\ \ 0

(see the proof of
[
BHol
, Proposition 3.1]
). Now, since
F
is a product of flag varieties, its singular
cohomology groups exist only in even degrees. Therefore, the Künneth formula gives the following:

\displaystyle H^{1}_{B}\left(Q_{w}\times F,\ \mathbb{Z}\right)\

\displaystyle=\ H^{3}_{B}\left(Q_{w}\times F,\ \mathbb{Z}\right)\ =\ 0,

\displaystyle\text{and}\ \ H^{2}_{B}\left(Q_{w}\times F,\ \mathbb{Z}\right)\

\displaystyle\simeq\ H^{2}_{B}(Q_{w},\ \mathbb{Z})\ \oplus\ H^{2}_{B}(F,\ \mathbb{Z}).

As
\mathcal{Q}_{G}^{par}
is the direct limit of the varieties

\{Q_{w}\times\ F\}
, it follows that

\displaystyle H^{1}_{B}\left(\mathcal{Q}_{G}^{par},\ \mathbb{Z}\right)\

\displaystyle=\ H^{3}_{B}\left(\mathcal{Q}_{G}^{par},\ \mathbb{Z}\right)\ =\ 0,

(5.8)

\displaystyle\text{and}\ \ H^{2}_{B}(\mathcal{Q}_{G}^{par},\ \mathbb{Z})\

\displaystyle\simeq\ H^{2}_{B}(\mathcal{Q}_{G},\ \mathbb{Z})\ \oplus\ H^{2}_{B}(F,\ \mathbb{Z}).

(5.9)

Let
\mathcal{O}_{G/P_{i}}
denote the sheaf of regular functions on
G/P_{i}
. Since each
G/P_{i}
is a rational variety, it follows that
H^{1}\left(G/P_{i},\ \mathcal{O}_{G/P_{i}}\right)=0
. Using
[
Ha
, III Ex. 12.6]
together with
[
KN
, p. 157, Lemma 2.2]
, it follows that

\displaystyle\Pic\left(\mathcal{Q}^{par}_{G}\right)\ =\ \Pic\left(\mathcal{Q}_{G}\right)\oplus\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}\Pic\left(G/P_{i}\right).

(5.10)

We now aim to invoke Proposition
5.5
for the stack
\mathcal{Q}^{par}_{G}
. It was argued in the
proof of
[
BHol
, Proposition 3.1]
that
\mathcal{Q}_{G}
satisfies the two assumptions of Proposition

5.5
. The same is true for the variety
F
as well. Combining (
5.9
)
and (
5.10
), we can immediately conclude that
\mathcal{Q}_{G}^{par}
also satisfies the
two assumptions of Proposition
5.5
. Finally, it follows from (
5.8
)
and the comparison isomorphisms in Proposition
5.5
that

\Br\left(\mathcal{Q}_{G}^{par}\right)\ \ \simeq\ \ H^{2}\left(\mathcal{Q}_{G}^{par},\ (\mathcal{O}^{{}^{an}}_{\mathcal{Q}^{par}_{G}})^{*}\right)_{torsion}\ \ \simeq\ \ H^{3}_{B}\left(\mathcal{Q}^{par}_{G},\ \mathbb{Z}\right)\ \ =\ \ 0.

This proves the lemma.
∎

Proposition 5.7
.

Let
G
be a simple and simply connected complex affine algebraic group. Let

\mathcal{O}^{{}^{an}}_{\mgpar}
denote the sheaf of analytic functions on
\mgpar
. The analytic Brauer group of
\mgpar
vanishes, namely

H^{2}\left(\mgpar,\ (\mathcal{O}^{{}^{an}}_{\mgpar})^{*}\right)_{torsion}\ =\ 0.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{O}^{{}^{an}}_{\mathcal{Q}_{G}^{par}}
denote the sheaf of analytic functions on
\mathcal{Q}^{par}_{G}
. Let

BL_{X}(G)
denote the classifying space for
L_{X}(G)
. Using the fact that the quotient morphism
\mathcal{Q}_{G}^{par}\longrightarrow\mgpar
is a principal
L_{X}(G)
–bundle, the Leray spectral sequence gives rise to the following long exact sequence in analytic topology (cf.
[
BHol
, (4.1)]
):

\cdots\ \longrightarrow\ H^{2}\left(BL_{X}(G),\ \mathbb{C}^{*}\right)\ \longrightarrow

(5.11)

\mathrm{Ker}\left[H^{2}\left(\mgpar,\ (\mathcal{O}^{{}^{an}}_{\mgpar})^{*}\right)\ \longrightarrow\ H^{0}\left(BL_{X}(G),\ H^{2}\left(\mathcal{Q}_{G}^{par},\ (\mathcal{O}^{{}^{an}}_{\mathcal{Q}_{G}^{par}})^{*}\right)\right)\right]

\ \longrightarrow\ H^{1}\left(BL_{X}(G),\ H^{1}\left(\mathcal{Q}^{par}_{G},\ (\mathcal{O}^{{}^{an}}_{\mathcal{Q}_{G}^{par}})^{*}\right)\right).

In (
5.11
), we have
H^{2}\left(BL_{X}(G),\ \mathbb{C}^{*}\right)\ =\ 0

[
BHol
, Proposition 3.4]
. Let us compute the last term in (
5.11
).
Each
\Pic(G/P_{i})
is a free abelian group of finite rank, and the same is true for the group

\Pic(\mathcal{Q}_{G})

[
BLS
, Lemma 1.4]
. Using these, (
5.10
)
implies that

H^{1}\left(\mathcal{Q}^{par}_{G},\ (\mathcal{O}^{{}^{an}}_{\mathcal{Q}_{G}^{par}})^{*}\right)\ =\ \mathbb{Z}^{t}

for some positive integer
t
. Since
H^{1}(BL_{X}(G),\ \mathbb{Z})=\ 0

[
BHol
, Proposition 3.4]
, it follows that

H^{1}\left(BL_{X}(G),\ H^{1}\left(\mathcal{Q}^{par}_{G},\ (\mathcal{O}^{{}^{an}}_{\mathcal{Q}_{G}^{par}})^{*}\right)\right)\ =\ H^{1}\left(BL_{X}(G),\ \mathbb{Z}^{t}\right)\ =\ 0.

Moreover, as the space
BL_{X}(G)
is connected,

H^{0}\left(BL_{X}(G),\ H^{2}\left(\mathcal{Q}_{G}^{par},\ (\mathcal{O}^{{}^{an}}_{\mathcal{Q}_{G}^{par}})^{*}\right)\right)\ \simeq\ H^{2}\left(\mathcal{Q}_{G}^{par},\ (\mathcal{O}^{{}^{an}}_{\mathcal{Q}_{G}^{par}})^{*}\right).

Therefore, the exact sequence in (
5.11
) implies the following inclusion of abelian groups:

H^{2}\left(\mgpar,\ (\mathcal{O}^{{}^{an}}_{\mgpar})^{*}\right)\ \hookrightarrow\ H^{2}\left(\mathcal{Q}_{G}^{par},\ (\mathcal{O}^{{}^{an}}_{\mathcal{Q}_{G}^{par}})^{*}\right),

which implies that

H^{2}\left(\mgpar,\ (\mathcal{O}^{{}^{an}}_{\mgpar})^{*}\right)_{torsion}\ \hookrightarrow\ H^{2}\left(\mathcal{Q}_{G}^{par},\ (\mathcal{O}^{{}^{an}}_{\mathcal{Q}_{G}^{par}})^{*}\right)_{torsion}.

Now Lemma
5.6
completes the proof.
∎

Finally, we prove that the algebraic Brauer group of
\mgpar
vanishes, by comparing it with the analytic Brauer
group of
\mgpar
.

Theorem 5.8
.

Let
G
be a simple and simply connected complex affine algebraic group.
Then

\Br\left(\mgpar\right)\ \,=\ \,0.

Proof.

We have
H^{2}\left(\mgpar,\ (\mathcal{O}^{{}^{an}}_{\mgpar})^{*}\right)_{torsion}\ =\ 0
by
Proposition
5.7
. We would now like to compare this group with
the corresponding algebraic Brauer group. This will be done by showing that the two conditions in
Proposition
5.5
are satisfied for the stack
\mgpar
.

(1)

Using the facts that
H^{1}_{B}\left(F,\ \mathbb{Z}\right)\ =\ 0
as well as that
F
and
\mathcal{M}_{G}
are
connected (since
G
is simply connected), Lemma
5.4
implies that

\displaystyle H^{2}_{B}\left(\mgpar,\ \mathbb{Z}\right)\

\displaystyle\simeq\ \left(H^{2}_{B}\left(\mathcal{M}_{G},\ \mathbb{Z}\right)\otimes H^{0}_{B}\left(F,\ \mathbb{Z}\right)\right)\ \bigoplus\ \left(H^{0}_{B}\left(\mgpar,\ \mathbb{Z}\right)\otimes H^{2}_{B}\left(F,\ \mathbb{Z}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\simeq\ H^{2}_{B}\left(\mathcal{M}_{G},\ \mathbb{Z}\right)\ \bigoplus\ H^{2}_{B}\left(F,\ \mathbb{Z}\right).

(5.12)

Moreover, by
[
LS
, Theorem 1.1 and Proposition 8.7]
we have

\displaystyle\Pic\left(\mgpar\right)\ \simeq\ \Pic\left(\mathcal{M}_{G}\right)\ \oplus\ \Pic(F).

(5.13)

Now, by (
5.12
) and (
5.13
), the first Chern class map
\Pic(\mgpar)\,\longrightarrow\,H^{2}_{B}\left(\mgpar,\ \mathbb{Z}\right)
splits as a direct sum of the Chern class maps
\Pic\left(\mathcal{M}_{G}\right)\,\longrightarrow\,H_{B}^{2}\left(\mathcal{M}_{G},\ \mathbb{Z}\right)
and
\Pic\left(F\right)\,\longrightarrow\,H^{2}_{B}\left(F,\ \mathbb{Z}\right)
. As both these maps are surjective
(cf.
[
Te
, Proposition 5.1 and Remark 5.2]
), we conclude that the Chern class map
\Pic(\mgpar)\,\longrightarrow\,H^{2}_{B}\left(\mgpar,\ \mathbb{Z}\right)
is also surjective; in other words, the first condition in Proposition
5.5
is satisfied for
\mgpar
.

(2)

As
\Pic\left(\mgpar\right)\ \simeq\ \Pic\left(\mathcal{M}_{G}\right)\ \oplus\ \Pic(F)
by (
5.13
), it is clear that the second condition of Proposition
5.5
is satisfied for
\mgpar
as well.

Thus, from the comparison isomorphisms in Proposition
5.5
together with Proposition
5.7
, it follows that

\displaystyle\Br\left(\mgpar\right)\ \simeq\ H^{2}\left(\mgpar,\ (\mathcal{O}^{{}^{an}}_{\mgpar})^{*}\right)_{torsion}\ =\ 0.

This completes the proof.
∎

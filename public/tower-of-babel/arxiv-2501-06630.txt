Generalized dichotomies via time rescaling
Davor Dragičević, César M. Silva

Abstract
. For discrete-time nonautonomous linear dynamics and a large class of discrete growth rates μ \mu , we show that the notion of μ \mu dichotomy (with respect to a sequence of norms) can be completely characterized in terms of ordinary and exponential dichotomy (with respect to a sequence of norms) by employing a suitable rescaling of time. Previously, such a result was known only in the particular case of polynomial dichotomies. As a nontrivial application of our results, we study the structure of a generalized Sacker-Sell spectrum and obtain certain nonautonomous topological and smooth linearization results.

1.
Introduction

The notion of exponential dichotomy introduced by Perron
[
38
]
plays an important role in the qualitative study of nonautonomous dynamical systems. It corresponds to assuming the existence of an exponential contraction and
expansion along complementary directions at each moment of time, thus representing a natural counterpart to the notion of hyperbolicity for autonomous dynamics. Among many important consequences, we mention the existence of stable and unstable manifolds as well as topological linearization for small nonlinear perturbations of linear dynamics exhibiting an exponential dichotomy. We refer to
[
6
,
16
,
18
,
20
,
28
,
33
,
34
,
39
,
40
]
for a detailed discussion of various aspects of this theory.

Despite its importance, in some situations, the exponential dichotomy might be regarded
restrictive since it requires that the rates of contraction and expansion
along the stable and unstable directions be exponential. Indeed, it is fairly easy to construct examples of nonautonomous dynamics which admit a splitting
into stable and unstable directions but with non-exponential rates of contraction and expansion along these directions. To our knowledge, Muldowney
[
35
]
and Naulin and Pinto
[
36
]

were the first to study non-exponential dichotomies. More recently, Barreira and Valls
[
8
]
initiated a systematic study of such dichotomies in the nonuniform framework connecting the existence of this type of behavior with the nonvanishing of certain generalized Lyapunov exponents (see also
[
13
,
14
]
for a subsequent work by Bento and Silva). We stress that particular attention has been paid to the so-called polynomial dichotomies
[
10
,
12
]
. We refer to
[
2
,
11
,
15
,
17
,
22
,
30
,
31
,
32
]
and references therein for various results devoted to dichotomies with growth rates.

Building on earlier work on exponential
[
4
]
and polynomial dichotomies
[
21
]
, Silva
[
42
]
introduced, for a nonautonomous linear dynamics with discrete time, the notion of
\mu
-dichotomy with respect to a sequence of norms, where
\mu
is a discrete growth rate. We emphasize that this notion includes the notion of a nonuniform
\mu
-dichotomy as a particular case (see
[
42
, Theorem 4.1]
).

In order to describe the results of the present paper, let us consider a nonautonomous linear difference equation

(1.1)

x_{n+1}=A_{n}x_{n}\quad n\in\mathbb{N},

on an arbitrary Banach space
X
, where
\{A_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of bounded linear operators on
X
. It follows from our first main result (see Theorem
2.4
) that (provided that
\mu
is slowly varying) the notion of
\mu
-dichotomy with respect to a sequence of norms for (
1.1
) can be completely characterized in terms of the notion of ordinary and exponential dichotomy with respect to a sequence of norms for (
1.1
) and a system obtained from (
1.1
) by a suitable rescaling of time, respectively. Moreover, we show (see Theorem
2.7
) that under certain additional conditions such a characterization holds without involving the existence of an ordinary dichotomy for (
1.1
). These results show that, for a large class of growth rates
\mu
,
\mu
-dichotomies with respect to a sequence of norms can be described as exponential dichotomies with respect to a sequence of norms after a suitable time rescaling. We stress that this was previously known only in the particular case of polynomial dichotomies
[
25
]
(see also
[
3
,
26
]
for some applications).

We proceed by giving several nontrivial applications of these results. Firstly, we show that in some cases the version of the Sacker-Sell spectrum for (
1.1
) introduced with respect to the notion of (uniform)
\mu
-dichotomy coincides with the classical Sacker-Sell spectrum of a system obtained from (
1.1
) via suitable time rescaling (see Theorem
4.1
).

Next, we consider nonlinear perturbations of (
1.1
) of the form

(1.2)

x_{n+1}=A_{n}x_{n}+f_{n}(x_{n})\quad n\in\mathbb{N},

where
\{f_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of maps
f_{n}\colon X\to X
. We obtain a series of results devoted to the linearization of (
1.2
).
More precisely, we show that if (
1.1
) admits a strong nonuniform
\mu
-dichotomy and the nonlinearities
f_{n}
are “small” in the appropriate sense, then (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) are topologically equivalent and the conjugacies are locally Hölder continuous (see Theorem
5.1
). We stress that a similar result was established (using different techniques) by Barreira and Valls
[
9
]
. However, as explained in Remark
5.4
there are important differences between the two and our Theorem
5.1
seems to be the first result which is applicable to the case of polynomial dichotomies (either uniform or nonuniform). We note that the research devoted to topological nonautonomous linearization was initiated by Palmer
[
37
]
.

Next, we obtain a Sternberg-type linearization result (see Theorem
6.1
) that formulates a
C^{\ell}
-linearization result for (
1.2
). It works under the assumptions that (
1.1
) exhibits a (uniform)
\mu
-dichotomy, that there are no resonances up to a certain order (formulated in terms of the generalized Sacker-Sell spectrum) and that nonlinearities
f_{n}
are “small” in an appropriate sense. We deduce Theorem
6.1
as a consequence of the main results described above of our paper and the Sternberg-type theorem obtained in
[
19
]
which considers (
1.1
) admitting the (uniform) exponential dichotomy. For other results dealing with nonautonomous smooth linearization we refer to
[
23
,
24
]
.

2.
Time rescaling

Throughout this paper
X=(X,\|\cdot\|)
is an arbitrary Banach space. By
\mathcal{B}(X)
we denote the space of all bounded linear operators on
X
equipped with the operator norm, which we also denote by
\|\cdot\|
.
Let
\{A_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{B}(X)
and consider the nonautonomous linear system

(A)

x_{n+1}=A_{n}x_{n},\ \ n\in\mathbb{N}.

Let
\Phi_{A}=\left\{\Phi_{A}(m,k)\right\}_{m\geq k}
be the evolution family associated to (
A
) defined by

(2.1)

\Phi_{A}(m,k)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}A_{m-1}\cdots A_{k},&m>k\\
\mathrm{Id},&m=k.\end{array}\right.

Here
\mathrm{Id}
denotes the identity operator on
X
.
We say that a sequence
\mu=(\mu_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is a discrete growth rate if it is positive, strictly increasing, and satisfies
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\mu_{n}=+\infty
and
\mu_{0}=1
.

Given a discrete growth rate
\mu=(\mu_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
, we can associate to it the continuous, strictly increasing function
\widetilde{\mu}:[0,+\infty)\to[1,+\infty)
given by

\widetilde{\mu}(t)=\begin{cases}\mu_{n}&\text{ if }\ t=n\\
\mu_{n}+(t-n)(\mu_{n+1}-\mu_{n})&\text{ if }\ n<t<n+1\end{cases},\quad n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

Clearly, this is an invertible function and its inverse
{\widetilde{\mu}}^{-1}:[1,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
is the continuous, strictly increasing function given by

\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(t)=\begin{cases}n&\text{ if }\ t=\mu_{n}\\
n+\dfrac{t-\mu_{n}}{\mu_{n+1}-\mu_{n}}&\text{ if }\ \mu_{n}<t<\mu_{n+1}\end{cases},\quad n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

Definition 2.1
.

We say that (
A
) (or the sequence
\{A_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
) admits an ordinary dichotomy with respect to a sequence of norms
\left\{\|\cdot\|_{k}\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
on
X

if there are a sequence of projections
\{P_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
on
X
and
K\geq 1
such that:

(od1)

A_{k}P_{k}=P_{k+1}A_{k}
, for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
;

(od2)

for every
k\in\mathbb{N},{A_{k}}_{\mid}:\operatorname{Ker}P_{k}\rightarrow\operatorname{Ker}P_{k+1}
is an isomorphism, where
{A_{k}}_{\mid}
denotes the restriction of
A_{k}
to
\Ker P_{k}
;

(od3)

\left\|\Phi_{A}(m,k)P_{k}x\right\|_{m}\leq K\|x\|_{k}
, for all
x\in X
and all
m\geq k
;

(od4)

\left\|\Phi_{A}(m,k)(\mathrm{Id}-P_{k})x\right\|_{m}\leq K\|x\|_{k}
, for all
x\in X
and all
m\leq k
.

We remark that (od
1
) is equivalent to

(2.2)

\Phi_{A}(m,n)P_{n}=P_{m}\Phi_{A}(m,n),\quad\text{for all $m\geq n\geq 1$}

and (od
2
) is equivalent to the invertibility of
\Phi_{A}(m,n)_{\mid}:\operatorname{Ker}P_{n}\rightarrow\operatorname{Ker}P_{m}
, for all
m\geq n\geq 1
.
Note that, in (od
4
),
\Phi_{A}(m,k)
denotes the inverse of the restriction of
\Phi_{A}(k,m)
to
\Ker P_{m}
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\mu=(\mu_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
be a discrete growth rate.
We say that (
A
) (or the sequence
\{A_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
) admits a
\mu
-dichotomy with respect to a sequence of norms
\left\{\|\cdot\|_{k}\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
on
X

if there are
N\geq 1
,
\nu>0
and a sequence of projections
\{P_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
on
X
such that (od
1
)–(od
2
) in Definition
2.1
are satisfied and additionally:

(
\mu
1)

\left\|\Phi_{A}(m,k)P_{k}x\right\|_{m}\leq N\left(\frac{\mu_{m}}{\mu_{k}}\right)^{-\nu}\|x\|_{k}
, for every
x\in X
and
m\geq k
;

(
\mu
2)

\left\|\Phi_{A}(m,k)(\mathrm{Id}-P_{k})x\right\|_{m}\leq N\left(\frac{\mu_{k}}{\mu_{m}}\right)^{-\nu}\|x\|_{k}
, for every
x\in X
and
m\leq k
.

Remark 2.3
.

When, in Definition
2.2
, we set
\mu_{n}=\e^{n}
, for
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, we say that we have an exponential dichotomy with respect to a sequence of norms
\left\{\|\cdot\|_{k}\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
. Similarly, when we let
\mu_{n}=n+1
for
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, we say that we have a polynomial dichotomy with respect to a sequence of norms
\left\{\|\cdot\|_{k}\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
.

Given discrete growth rates
\mu=(\mu_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
and
\eta=(\eta_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
, we associate with the system (
A
) the family of linear operators
Q^{\mu,\eta}=\{Q^{\mu,\eta}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{B}(X)
, given by

(2.3)

Q^{\mu,\eta}_{n}=\Phi_{A}\left(\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{n})\rfloor+1,\ \lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{n-1})\rfloor+1\right),\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

Consider the nonautonomous linear system

(
Q^{\mu,\eta}
)

y_{n+1}=Q^{\mu,\eta}_{n}\,y_{n},\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

It is easy to obtain the evolution family
\Phi_{Q^{\mu,\eta}}=\left\{\Phi_{Q^{\mu,\eta}}(m,n)\right\}_{m\geq n}
associated to (

Q

μ
,
η

) in terms of the evolution family
\Phi_{A}=\left\{\Phi_{A}(m,n)\right\}_{m\geq n}
associated to (
A
):

\begin{split}\Phi_{Q^{\mu,\eta}}(m,n)&=\prod_{k=n}^{m-1}Q^{\mu,\eta}_{k}=\prod_{k=n}^{m-1}\Phi_{A}(\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{k})\rfloor+1,\,\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{k-1})\rfloor+1)\\
&=\Phi_{A}(\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{m-1})\rfloor+1,\,\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{n-1})\rfloor+1),\quad m\geq n.\end{split}

Given a sequence of norms
\left\{\|\cdot\|_{k}\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and a growth rate
\eta
, we consider a new sequence of norms
\left\{\|\cdot\|_{k}^{\eta}\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
given by

\|\cdot\|_{k}^{\eta}:=\|\cdot\|_{\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{k-1})\rfloor+1},\quad k\in\mathbb{N}.

Moreover, given a linear operator
T\colon X\to X
we let, for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\begin{split}\|T\|_{k}^{\eta}=\inf\{M\in(0,+\infty]:\|Tx\|_{\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{k-1})\rfloor+1}\leq M\|x\|_{\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{k-1})\rfloor+1},\ \forall x\in X\}.\end{split}

We note that, provided that
\|\cdot\|_{\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{k-1})\rfloor+1}
is equivalent to
\|\cdot\|
, we have
\|T\|_{k}^{\eta}<+\infty
for every
T\in\mathcal{B}(X)
.
The central result of this section is the following.

(2.13)

\left\|\Phi_{A}(m,k)P_{k}x\right\|_{m}\leq N_{1}\left(\frac{\mu_{m}}{\mu_{k}}\right)^{-\nu}\left\|x\right\|_{k}.

We split the proof of (
2.13
) into two cases:

Case I
- There are
n,j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
with
n>j
such that

\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{n+1})\rfloor+1>m\geq\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{n})\rfloor+1\geq\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{j+1})\rfloor+1>k\geq\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{j})\rfloor+1.

Recalling that
P_{\lfloor{\widetilde{\mu}}^{-1}(\eta_{j+1})\rfloor+1}=P_{j+2}^{\eta}
and
\|\cdot\|^{\eta}_{j+2}=\|\cdot\|_{\lfloor{\widetilde{\mu}}^{-1}(\eta_{j+1})\rfloor+1}
, by (
2.9
) and (
2.11
) we get

\begin{split}&\left\|\Phi_{A}(m,k)P_{k}x\right\|_{m}\\
&=\left\|\Phi_{A}\left(m,\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{n})\rfloor+1\right)P_{\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{n})\rfloor+1}\Phi_{A}\left(\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{n})\rfloor+1,k\right)P_{k}x\right\|_{m}\\
&\leq K\left\|\Phi_{A}\left(\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{n})\rfloor+1,\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{j+1})\rfloor+1\right)\Phi_{A}\left(\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{j+1})\rfloor+1,k\right)P_{k}x\right\|_{\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{n})\rfloor+1}\\
&=K\left\|\Phi_{Q^{\mu,\eta}}\left(n+1,j+2\right)P_{j+2}^{\eta}\Phi_{A}\left(\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{j+1})\rfloor+1,k\right)P_{k}x\right\|_{n}^{\eta}\\
&\leq KN\left(\frac{\eta_{n+1}}{\eta_{j+2}}\right)^{-\nu}\left\|\Phi_{A}\left(\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{j+1})\rfloor+1,k\right)P_{k}x\right\|_{j+2}^{\eta}\\
&\leq K^{2}N\left(\frac{\eta_{n+1}}{\eta_{j+2}}\right)^{-\nu}\left\|x\right\|_{k}=K^{2}N\left(\frac{\mu_{m}}{\mu_{k}}\frac{\eta_{n+1}}{\mu_{m}}\frac{\mu_{k}}{\eta_{j}}\frac{\eta_{j}}{\eta_{j+2}}\right)^{-\nu}\left\|x\right\|_{k}\\
&\leq K^{2}N\theta^{2\nu}\left(\frac{\mu_{m}}{\mu_{k}}\right)^{-\nu}\left\|x\right\|_{k},\end{split}

where we also used that
\frac{\mu_{k}}{\eta_{j}}\geq 1
(as
k\geq\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{j})
),
\frac{\eta_{n+1}}{\mu_{m}}\geq 1
(since
\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{n+1})\geq m
) and
\frac{\eta_{j}}{\eta_{j+2}}\geq\theta^{-2}
(see (
2.8
)).

Case II
- There is
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that

\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{n+1})\rfloor+1>m\geq k\geq\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{n})\rfloor+1.

By (
2.5
) and (
2.9
), we get

\begin{split}\left\|\Phi_{A}(m,k)P_{k}x\right\|_{m}\leq K\|x\|_{k}&=K\left(\frac{\mu_{m}}{\mu_{k}}\right)^{-\nu}\left(\frac{\mu_{m}}{\mu_{k}}\right)^{\nu}\|x\|_{k}\\
&\leq K\left(\frac{\mu_{m}}{\mu_{k}}\right)^{-\nu}\left(\frac{\eta_{n+1}}{\eta_{n}}\right)^{\nu}\|x\|_{k}\\
&\leq K\theta^{\nu}\left(\frac{\mu_{m}}{\mu_{k}}\right)^{-\nu}\|x\|_{k}.\end{split}

We conclude that (
2.13
) holds and the claim follows.

Our next claim is that there exists
N_{2}\geq 1
such that for
x\in X
and
1\leq m\leq k
, we have

(2.14)

\left\|\Phi_{A}(m,k)(\mathrm{Id}-P_{k})x\right\|_{m}\leq N_{2}\left(\frac{\mu_{k}}{\mu_{m}}\right)^{-\nu}\left\|x\right\|_{k}.

We again split the proof of (
2.14
) into two cases.

Case I
- There are
n,j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
with
n<j
such that

\lfloor\tilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{n})\rfloor+1\leq m<\lfloor\tilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{n+1})\rfloor+1\leq\lfloor\tilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{j})\rfloor+1\leq k<\lfloor\tilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{j+1})\rfloor+1.

By (
2.10
) and (
2.12
) we have that

\begin{split}&\quad\left\|\Phi_{A}(m,k)(\mathrm{Id}-P_{k})x\right\|_{m}\\
&=\left\|\Phi_{A}\left(m,\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{n+1})\rfloor+1\right)(\mathrm{Id}-P_{\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{n+1})\rfloor+1})\Phi_{A}\left(\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{n+1})\rfloor+1,k\right)(\mathrm{Id}-P_{k})x\right\|_{m}\\
&\leq K\left\|\Phi_{A}\left(\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{n+1})\rfloor+1,\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{j})\rfloor+1\right)\Phi_{A}\left(\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{j})\rfloor+1,k\right)(\mathrm{Id}-P_{k})x\right\|_{\lfloor\tilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{n+1})\rfloor+1}\\
&=K\left\|\Phi_{Q^{\mu,\eta}}\left(n+2,j+1\right)\Phi_{A}\left(\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{j})\rfloor+1,k\right)(\mathrm{Id}-P_{k})x\right\|_{n+2}^{\eta}\\
&\leq KN\left(\frac{\eta_{j+1}}{\eta_{n+2}}\right)^{-\nu}\left\|\Phi_{A}\left(\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{j})\rfloor+1,k\right)(\mathrm{Id}-P_{k})x\right\|_{j+1}^{\eta}\\
&\leq K^{2}N\left(\frac{\eta_{j+1}}{\eta_{n+2}}\right)^{-\nu}\|x\|_{k}=K^{2}N\left(\frac{\mu_{k}}{\mu_{m}}\right)^{-\nu}\left(\frac{\mu_{m}}{\mu_{k}}\frac{\eta_{j+1}}{\eta_{n+2}}\right)^{-\nu}\|x\|_{k}\\
&\leq K^{2}N\theta^{2\nu}\left(\frac{\mu_{k}}{\mu_{m}}\right)^{-\nu}\|x\|_{k},\end{split}

since (see (
2.8
))

\frac{\mu_{m}}{\mu_{k}}\frac{\eta_{j+1}}{\eta_{n+2}}=\frac{\mu_{m}}{\eta_{n+2}}\ \frac{\eta_{j+1}}{\mu_{k}}\geq\frac{\eta_{n}}{\eta_{n+2}}\geq\theta^{-2}.

Case II
- There is
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that

\lfloor\tilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{n})\rfloor+1\leq m\leq k<\lfloor\tilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{n+1})\rfloor+1.

By (
2.5
)
and (
2.10
) we have that

\begin{split}\left\|\Phi_{A}(m,k)(\mathrm{Id}-P_{k})x\right\|_{m}\leq K\|x\|_{k}&=K\left(\frac{\mu_{k}}{\mu_{m}}\right)^{-\nu}\left(\frac{\mu_{k}}{\mu_{m}}\right)^{\nu}\|x\|_{k}\\
&\leq K\left(\frac{\mu_{k}}{\mu_{m}}\right)^{-\nu}\left(\frac{\eta_{n+1}}{\eta_{n}}\right)^{\nu}\|x\|_{k}\\
&\leq K\theta^{\nu}\left(\frac{\mu_{k}}{\mu_{m}}\right)^{-\nu}\|x\|_{k}.\end{split}

We conclude that (
2.14
) holds and the claim follows.

We conclude that system (
A
) admits a
\mu
-dichotomy with respect to the sequence of norms
\left\{\|\cdot\|_{k}\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
relative to the projections
\{P_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
. The proof of the theorem is completed.
∎

Remark 2.5
.

In the particular case where
\mu
and
\eta
are of the form
\mu_{n}=n+1
and
\eta_{n}=h^{n}
, where

, the version of Theorem

was established in
[

. Observe that in this case the
\eta
-dichotomy with respect to a sequence of norms is just an exponential dichotomy with respect to a sequence of norms.

The following example shows that we cannot eliminate the assumption of an ordinary dichotomy in conditions (
ii
) and (
iii
) in the statement of Theorem
2.4
.

Example 2.6
.

Take
X=\mathbb{R}
and
\|\cdot\|_{n}=|\cdot|
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Moreover, let
\mu_{n}=1+n
and
\eta_{n}=2^{n}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Finally, we set

A_{n}=\begin{cases}n,&n=2^{k}-1\ \text{for some $k\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{1\}$;}\\
0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Observe that

Q_{n}^{\mu,\eta}=\Phi_{A}(2^{n},2^{n-1})=0\quad\text{for $n\in\mathbb{N}$,}

yielding that (

Q

μ
,
η

) admits an exponential dichotomy with respect to the sequence of norms
\left\{\|\cdot\|_{k}^{\eta}\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
. On the other hand, since
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}A_{n}=+\infty
, it is easy to show that (
A
) does not admit
\mu
-dichotomy with respect to the sequence of norms
\left\{\|\cdot\|_{k}\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
.

The following result shows that we can formulate additional conditions under which we can eliminate the assumption of an ordinary dichotomy in conditions (
ii
) and (
iii
) in the statement of Theorem
2.4
.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
\mu
and
\eta
be growth rates with the property that there exists
\theta\geq 1
such that (
2.4
) and (
2.5
) hold. Moreover, suppose that
A_{k}
is an invertible operator for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, and that there exists
K,a>0
such that

(2.15)

\|\Phi_{A}(m,k)x\|_{m}\leq K\left(\frac{\mu_{m}}{\mu_{k}}\right)^{a}\|x\|_{k}\quad\text{for $m\geq k$ and $x\in X$}

and

(2.16)

\|\Phi_{A}(m,k)x\|_{m}\leq K\left(\frac{\mu_{k}}{\mu_{m}}\right)^{a}\|x\|_{k}\quad\text{for $m\leq k$ and $x\in X$.}

Then, there exists
K^{\prime}>0
such that

(2.17)

\|\Phi_{Q^{\mu,\eta}}(m,k)x\|_{m}^{\eta}\leq K^{\prime}\left(\frac{\eta_{m}}{\eta_{k}}\right)^{a}\|x\|_{k}^{\eta}\quad\text{for $m\geq k$ and $x\in X$}

and

(2.18)

\|\Phi_{Q^{\mu,\eta}}(m,k)x\|_{m}^{\eta}\leq K^{\prime}\left(\frac{\eta_{k}}{\eta_{m}}\right)^{a}\|x\|_{k}^{\eta}\quad\text{for $m\leq k$ and $x\in X$.}

Moreover, the following properties are equivalent:

(i)

system (
A
) admits a
\mu
-dichotomy with respect to the sequence of norms
\left\{\|\cdot\|_{k}\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
;

(ii)

system (

Q

μ
,
η

) admits a
\eta
-dichotomy with respect to the norms
\left\{\|\cdot\|_{k}^{\eta}\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
.

Proof.

Take
m\geq k
and
x\in X
. By (
2.5
), (
2.8
) and (
2.15
) we have that

\begin{split}\|\Phi_{Q^{\mu,\eta}}(m,k)x\|_{m}^{\eta}&=\left\|\Phi_{A}\left(\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{m-1})\rfloor+1,\,\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{k-1})\rfloor+1\right)x\right\|_{\lfloor\tilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{m-1})\rfloor+1}\\
&\leq K\left(\frac{\mu_{\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{m-1})\rfloor+1}}{\mu_{\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{k-1})\rfloor+1}}\right)^{a}\|x\|_{k}^{\eta}\\
&\leq K\left(\frac{\widetilde{\mu}(\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{m-1})+1)}{\eta_{k-1}}\right)^{a}\|x\|_{k}^{\eta}\\
&\leq K\theta^{2a}\left(\frac{\eta_{m-1}}{\eta_{k-1}}\right)^{a}\|x\|_{k}^{\eta}\\
&\leq K\theta^{3a}\left(\frac{\eta_{m}}{\eta_{k}}\right)^{a}\|x\|_{k}^{\eta}.\end{split}

We conclude that (
2.17
) holds (with
K^{\prime}=K\theta^{3a}
). Similarly, one can establish (
2.18
).

We now establish the equivalence between
(i)
and
(ii)
. It follows from Theorem
2.4
that it is sufficient to prove that
(ii)
implies
(i)
. Assume that system (

Q

μ
,
η

) admits a
\eta
-dichotomy with respect to the norms
\left\{\|\cdot\|_{k}^{\eta}\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and projections
\widetilde{P}_{k}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Thus, there exist
L,\nu>0
such that

(2.19)

\|\Phi_{Q^{\mu,\eta}}(m,k)\tilde{P}_{k}x\|_{m}^{\eta}\leq L\left(\frac{\eta_{m}}{\eta_{k}}\right)^{-\nu}\|x\|_{k}^{\eta}\quad\text{for $m\geq k$ and $x\in X$,}

and

(2.20)

\|\Phi_{Q^{\mu,\eta}}(m,k)(\mathrm{Id}-\tilde{P}_{k})x\|_{m}^{\eta}\leq L\left(\frac{\eta_{k}}{\eta_{m}}\right)^{-\nu}\|x\|_{k}^{\eta}\quad\text{for $m\leq k$ and $x\in X$.}

For
k\in\mathbb{N}
we define

P_{k}:=\Phi_{A}(k,1)\tilde{P}_{1}\Phi_{A}(1,k).

Then,

\begin{split}P_{k+1}A_{k}=\Phi_{A}(k+1,1)\tilde{P}_{1}\Phi_{A}(1,k+1)A_{k}&=\Phi_{A}(k+1,1)\tilde{P}_{1}\Phi_{A}(1,k)\\
&=A_{k}\Phi_{A}(k,1)\tilde{P}_{1}\Phi_{A}(1,k)\\
&=A_{k}P_{k},\end{split}

for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
yielding (od
1
). The invertibility of the operators
A_{k}
immediately yields (od
2
). Clearly,

P_{\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{k-1})\rfloor+1}=\tilde{P}_{k},\quad k\in\mathbb{N}.

We claim that there exists
D>0
such that

(2.21)

\|P_{k}x\|_{k}\leq D\|x\|_{k},\quad\text{for $k\in\mathbb{N}$ and $x\in X$.}

To this end, we fix
k\in\mathbb{N}
and choose
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that

\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{n+1})\rfloor+1>k\geq\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{n})\rfloor+1.

Then, using (
2.4
), (
2.5
),
(
2.15
), (
2.16
) and (
2.19
) (applied for

) we have that

\begin{split}\|P_{k}x\|_{k}&=\left\|\Phi_{A}(k,\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{n+1})\rfloor+1)\widetilde{P}_{n+2}\Phi_{A}(\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{n+1})\rfloor+1,k)x\right\|_{k}\\
&\leq K\left(\frac{\mu_{\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{n+1})\rfloor+1}}{\mu_{k}}\right)^{a}\left\|\widetilde{P}_{n+2}\Phi_{A}(\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{n+1})\rfloor+1,k)x\right\|_{n+2}^{\eta}\\
&\leq K\theta^{a}\left(\frac{\eta_{n+1}}{\mu_{k}}\right)^{a}\left\|\widetilde{P}_{n+2}\Phi_{A}(\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{n+1})\rfloor+1,k)x\right\|_{n+2}^{\eta}\\
&\leq K\theta^{a}\left(\frac{\eta_{n+1}}{\eta_{n}}\right)^{a}\left\|\widetilde{P}_{n+2}\Phi_{A}(\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{n+1})\rfloor+1,k)x\right\|_{n+2}^{\eta}\\
&\leq K\theta^{2a}\left\|\widetilde{P}_{n+2}\Phi_{A}(\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{n+1})\rfloor+1,k)x\right\|_{n+2}^{\eta}\\
&\leq KL\theta^{2a}\left\|\Phi_{A}(\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{n+1})\rfloor+1,k)x\right\|_{n+2}^{\eta}\\
&\leq K^{2}L\theta^{2a}\left(\frac{\mu_{\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{n+1})\rfloor+1}}{\mu_{k}}\right)^{a}\|x\|_{k}\\
&\leq K^{2}L\theta^{3a}\left(\frac{\mu_{\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{n+1})\rfloor}}{\eta_{n}}\right)^{a}\|x\|_{k}\\
&\leq K^{2}L\theta^{4a}\|x\|_{k},\end{split}

yielding (
2.21
).

We now claim that there exists
L^{\prime}>0
such that

(2.22)

\left\|\Phi_{A}(m,k)P_{k}x\right\|_{m}\leq L^{\prime}\left(\frac{\mu_{m}}{\mu_{k}}\right)^{-\nu}\|x\|_{k}\quad\text{for $m\geq k$ and $x\in X$.}

We distinguish two cases.

Case I
- There are
n,j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
j<n
such that

\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{n+1})\rfloor+1>m\geq\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{n})\rfloor+1\geq\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{j+1})\rfloor+1>k\geq\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{j})\rfloor+1.

By (
2.5
), (
2.8
), (
2.15
) and (
2.19
) we have that

\begin{split}&\quad\left\|\Phi_{A}(m,k)P_{k}x\right\|_{m}\\
&=\left\|\Phi_{A}\left(m,\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{n})\rfloor+1\right)\Phi_{A}\left(\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{n})\rfloor+1,\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{j+1})\rfloor+1\right)\Phi_{A}\left(\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{j+1})\rfloor+1,k\right)P_{k}x\right\|_{m}\\
&\leq K\left(\frac{\mu_{m}}{\mu_{\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{n})\rfloor+1}}\right)^{a}\left\|\Phi_{A}\left(\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{n})\rfloor+1,\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{j+1})\rfloor+1\right)\Phi_{A}\left(\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{j+1})\rfloor+1,k\right)P_{k}x\right\|_{\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{n})\rfloor+1}\\
&\leq K\left(\frac{\eta_{n+1}}{\eta_{n}}\right)^{a}\left\|\Phi_{Q^{\mu,\eta}}\left(n+1,j+2\right)\tilde{P}_{j+2}\Phi_{A}\left(\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{j+1})\rfloor+1,k\right)x\right\|_{n+1}^{\eta}\\
&\leq K\theta^{a}\left\|\Phi_{Q^{\mu,\eta}}\left(n+1,j+2\right)\tilde{P}_{j+2}\Phi_{A}\left(\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{j+1})\rfloor+1,k\right)x\right\|_{n+1}^{\eta}\\
&\leq KL\theta^{a}\left(\frac{\eta_{n+1}}{\eta_{j+2}}\right)^{-\nu}\left\|\Phi_{A}\left(\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{j+1})\rfloor+1,k\right)x\right\|_{j+2}^{\eta}\\
&\leq K^{2}L\theta^{a}\left(\frac{\eta_{n+1}}{\eta_{j+2}}\right)^{-\nu}\left(\frac{\mu_{\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{j+1})\rfloor+1}}{\mu_{k}}\right)^{a}\|x\|_{k}\\
&\leq K^{2}L\theta^{a}\left(\frac{\eta_{n+1}}{\eta_{j+2}}\right)^{-\nu}\left(\frac{\mu_{\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{j+1})\rfloor+1}}{\eta_{j}}\right)^{a}\|x\|_{k}\\
&\leq K^{2}L\theta^{4a}\left(\frac{\eta_{n+1}}{\eta_{j+2}}\right)^{-\nu}\|x\|_{k}\\
&=K^{2}L\theta^{4a}\left(\frac{\mu_{m}}{\mu_{k}}\frac{\eta_{n+1}}{\mu_{m}}\frac{\mu_{k}}{\eta_{j}}\frac{\eta_{j}}{\eta_{j+2}}\right)^{-\nu}\|x\|_{k}\\
&\leq K^{2}L\theta^{4a+2\nu}\left(\frac{\mu_{m}}{\mu_{k}}\right)^{-\nu}\|x\|_{k},\end{split}

where in the last step we used that
\frac{\eta_{n+1}}{\mu_{m}}\geq 1
,
\frac{\mu_{k}}{\eta_{j}}\geq 1
and
\frac{\eta_{j}}{\eta_{j+2}}\geq\theta^{-2}
.

Case II
- There is
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that

\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{n+1})\rfloor+1>m\geq k\geq\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{n})\rfloor+1.

It follows from (
2.5
), (
2.15
)
and (
2.21
) that

\begin{split}\left\|\Phi_{A}(m,k)P_{k}x\right\|_{m}\leq K\left(\frac{\mu_{m}}{\mu_{k}}\right)^{a}\|P_{k}x\|_{k}&\leq KD\left(\frac{\mu_{m}}{\mu_{k}}\right)^{a}\|x\|_{k}\\
&\leq KD\left(\frac{\eta_{n+1}}{\eta_{n}}\right)^{a}\|x\|_{k}\\
&\leq KD\theta^{a}\|x\|_{k}\\
&=KD\theta^{a}\left(\frac{\mu_{m}}{\mu_{k}}\right)^{-\nu}\left(\frac{\mu_{m}}{\mu_{k}}\right)^{\nu}\|x\|_{k}\\
&\leq KD\theta^{a+\nu}\left(\frac{\mu_{m}}{\mu_{k}}\right)^{-\nu}\|x\|_{k}.\end{split}

We conclude that (
2.22
) holds. Similarly, one can show that there exists
L^{\prime\prime}>0
such that

\left\|\Phi_{A}(m,k)(\mathrm{Id}-P_{k})x\right\|_{m}\leq L^{\prime\prime}\left(\frac{\mu_{k}}{\mu_{m}}\right)^{-\nu}\|x\|_{k},\quad\text{for $m\leq k$ and $x\in X$.}

Consequently, the system (
A
) admits a
\mu
-dichotomy with respect to the sequence of norms
\left\{\|\cdot\|_{k}\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and the proof of the theorem is completed.
∎

4.

In this section we derive some consequences of Theorem
2.7
for the study of the Sacker-Sell spectrum and its generalizations.

Throughout this section, we consider a growth rate
\mu
with the property that (
2.4
) holds with some
\theta\geq 1
. Moreover, we fix a sequence
\mathbb{A}=\{A_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{B}(X)
of invertible operators. Finally, let
\eta_{n}=e^{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

By
\Sigma_{\mu D,\mathbb{A}}
we denote the set of all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
with the property that the system

(4.1)

x_{n+1}=\left(\frac{\mu_{n+1}}{\mu_{n}}\right)^{-\lambda}A_{n}x_{n}=:\tilde{A}_{n}x_{n}\quad n\in\mathbb{N}

does not admit a
\mu
-dichotomy.

By
\Sigma_{ED,\mathbb{Q}}
we denote the set of all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
with the property that the system

(4.2)

y_{n+1}=e^{-\lambda}Q_{n}^{\mu,\eta}y_{n}\quad n\in\mathbb{N}

does not admit an exponential dichotomy.

The following is the main result of this section.

Theorem 4.1
.

Suppose that there exist
K,a>0
such that

(4.3)

\|\Phi_{A}(m,k)\|\leq K\left(\frac{\mu_{m}}{\mu_{k}}\right)^{a}\quad m\geq k,

and

(4.4)

\|\Phi_{A}(m,k)\|\leq K\left(\frac{\mu_{k}}{\mu_{m}}\right)^{a}\quad m\leq k.

Then,

\Sigma_{\mu D,\mathbb{A}}=\Sigma_{ED,\mathbb{Q}}.

Proof.

Take
\lambda\notin\Sigma_{\mu D,\mathbb{A}}
. Then (
4.1
) admits a
\mu
-dichotomy. By Corollary
3.3
we conclude that system

y_{n+1}=\left(\frac{\mu_{\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(e^{n})\rfloor+1}}{\mu_{\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(e^{n-1})\rfloor+1}}\right)^{-\lambda}Q_{n}^{\mu,\eta}y_{n}=:\tilde{Q}_{n}^{\mu,\eta}y_{n}\quad n\in\mathbb{N}

admits an exponential dichotomy. Observe that

\tilde{Q}_{n}^{\mu,\eta}=\left(\frac{\mu_{\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(e^{n})\rfloor+1}}{\mu_{\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(e^{n-1})\rfloor+1}}\right)^{-\lambda}\Phi_{A}(\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(e^{n})\rfloor+1,\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(e^{n-1})\rfloor+1),\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

Hence, there exists a sequence of projections
P_{k}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
on
X
satisfying

P_{k+1}Q_{k}^{\mu,\eta}=Q_{k}^{\mu,\eta}P_{k}\quad k\in\mathbb{N},

and constants
K,\nu>0
such that

(4.5)

\left(\frac{\mu_{\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(e^{m-1})\rfloor+1}}{\mu_{\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(e^{k-1})\rfloor+1}}\right)^{-\lambda}\left\|\Phi_{Q^{\mu,\eta}}(m,k)P_{k}\right\|\leq Ke^{-\nu(m-k)}\quad m\geq k\geq 1,

and

(4.6)

\left(\frac{\mu_{\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(e^{m-1})\rfloor+1}}{\mu_{\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(e^{k-1})\rfloor+1}}\right)^{-\lambda}\left\|\Phi_{Q^{\mu,\eta}}(m,k)(\mathrm{Id}-P_{k})\right\|\leq Ke^{-\nu(k-m)}\quad 1\leq m\leq k.

Next, note that

\left(\frac{\mu_{\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(e^{m-1})\rfloor+1}}{\mu_{\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(e^{k-1})\rfloor+1}}\right)^{-\lambda}=e^{-\lambda(m-k)}\left(\frac{\widetilde{\mu}(\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(e^{m-1})\rfloor+1)}{\widetilde{\mu}(\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(e^{m-1}))}\right)^{-\lambda}\left(\frac{\widetilde{\mu}(\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(e^{k-1}))}{\widetilde{\mu}(\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(e^{k-1})\rfloor+1)}\right)^{-\lambda},

for
m,k\in\mathbb{N}
.
Since

1\leq\frac{\widetilde{\mu}(\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(e^{m-1})\rfloor+1)}{\widetilde{\mu}(\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(e^{m-1}))}\leq\theta^{2}

and

\theta^{-2}\leq\frac{\widetilde{\mu}(\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(e^{k-1}))}{\widetilde{\mu}(\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(e^{k-1})\rfloor+1)}\leq 1,

(
4.5
) and (
4.6
) imply that there exists
C>0
independent on
m
and
k
such that

e^{-\lambda(m-k)}\left\|\Phi_{Q^{\mu,\eta}}(m,k)P_{k}\right\|\leq Ce^{-\nu(m-k)}\quad m\geq k\geq 1,

and

e^{-\lambda(m-k)}\left\|\Phi_{Q^{\mu,\eta}}(m,k)(\mathrm{Id}-P_{k})\right\|\leq Ce^{-\nu(k-m)}\quad 1\leq m\leq k.

We conclude that (
4.2
) admits an exponential dichotomy. Hence,
\lambda\notin\Sigma_{ED,\mathbb{Q}}
and, consequently,
\Sigma_{ED,\mathbb{Q}}\subset\Sigma_{\mu D,\mathbb{A}}
. Similarly, one can establish the reverse inclusion yielding the desired conclusion.
∎

We first claim that provided

&\leq cK\left(\frac{\mu_{n+1}}{\mu_{n}}\right)^{a+1}\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|_{n}\\
&\leq cK\theta^{a+1}\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|_{n}.\end{split}

Therefore, provided that

&\leq KD\left(\frac{\mu_{\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{n+1})\rfloor}}{\mu_{\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{n})\rfloor+1}}\right)^{a}\|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}\|_{\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{n})\rfloor+1}^{\varrho}\\

&\leq KD\tilde{K}^{\varrho}\left(\frac{\mu_{\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{n+1})\rfloor}}{\mu_{\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{n})\rfloor+1}}\right)^{a}\left(\frac{\mu_{\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{n+1})\rfloor}}{\mu_{\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{n})\rfloor+1}}\right)^{\varrho\tilde{a}}\|x-y\|_{k}^{\varrho}\\
&\leq KD\tilde{K}^{\varrho}e^{a+\varrho\tilde{a}}\|x-y\|_{k}^{\varrho},\end{split}

for
x,y\in X
such that
\|x-y\|_{k}\leq\frac{1}{\tilde{K}e^{\tilde{a}}}
which ensures that
\|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}\|_{\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{n})\rfloor+1}\leq 1
. This yields the first estimate in (
5.4
). Similarly one can establish the second.

Next, we again fix
k\in\mathbb{N}
and choose
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
satisfying (
5.20
). Then,
writing
\bar{\psi}_{n}:=\psi_{n}-\mathrm{Id}
we have

\begin{split}h_{k}-\mathrm{Id}&=\Phi_{A}(k,\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{n})\rfloor+1)\circ\psi_{n+1}\circ\mathcal{G}(\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{n})\rfloor+1,k)-\mathrm{Id}\\
&=\Phi_{A}(k,\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{n})\rfloor+1)\circ\bar{\psi}_{n+1}\circ\mathcal{G}(\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{n})\rfloor+1,k)-\mathrm{Id}\\
&\phantom{=}+\Phi_{A}(k,\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{n})\rfloor+1)\circ\mathcal{G}(\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{n})\rfloor+1,k)\\
&=\Phi_{A}(k,\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{n})\rfloor+1)\circ\bar{\psi}_{n+1}\circ\mathcal{G}(\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{n})\rfloor+1,k)\\
&\phantom{=}-\sum_{j=\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{n})\rfloor+1}^{k-1}\Phi_{A}(k,j+1)g_{j}(\mathcal{G}(j,k)(\cdot)),\end{split}

where in the last step we used (
5.9
). This together with (
2.15
), (
5.8
) and (
5.19
) yields that

\begin{split}\|h_{k}(x)-x\|_{k}&\leq DK\left(\frac{\mu_{k}}{\mu_{\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{n})\rfloor+1}}\right)^{a}+KM\sum_{j=\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{n})\rfloor+1}^{k-1}\left(\frac{\mu_{k}}{\mu_{j+1}}\right)^{a}\frac{\mu_{j}^{\prime}}{\mu_{j}}\\
&\leq DK\left(\frac{\mu_{\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{n+1})\rfloor}}{\mu_{\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{n})\rfloor+1}}\right)^{a}+KM\left(\frac{\mu_{\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{n+1})\rfloor}}{\mu_{\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{n})\rfloor+1}}\right)^{a}\sum_{j=\lfloor\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}(\eta_{n})\rfloor+1}^{k-1}\frac{\mu_{j}^{\prime}}{\mu_{j}}\\
&\leq DKe^{a}+\theta KMe^{a}.\end{split}

Hence, the first estimate in (
5.3
) holds. Similarly, one can establish the second. The proof of the theorem is completed.
∎

In order to give an application of Theorem

to nonuniform behavior, we recall the concept of strong nonuniform

Definition 5.2
.

We say that (

-dichotomy if the operators

are invertible and there exists a sequence of projections
\{P_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and constants

,
\tilde{\nu}\geq\nu>0
and

such that (od

for

, and

\|\Phi_{A}(m,k)(\mathrm{Id}-P_{k})\|\leq N\left(\frac{\mu_{k}}{\mu_{m}}\right)^{-\nu}\mu_{k}^{\varepsilon}\quad\text{and}\quad\|\Phi_{A}(m,k)P_{k}\|\leq N\left(\frac{\mu_{k}}{\mu_{m}}\right)^{\tilde{\nu}}\mu_{k}^{\varepsilon},

for

.

Corollary 5.3
.

Assume that the following conditions hold:

(i)

(
A
) admits a strong nonuniform
\mu
-dichotomy.

(ii)

there exists

such that

and with

as in Definition
5.2

(iii)

there exists

Then, provided that

(h_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of homeomorphisms

such that the following holds:

(i)

for

;

(ii)

there exists

For

and

\begin{split}\|x\|_{n}^{u}&:=\sup_{m\leq n}\left(\|\Phi_{A}(m,n)(\mathrm{Id}-P_{n})x\|\left(\frac{\mu_{n}}{\mu_{m}}\right)^{\nu}\right)\\
&\phantom{=}+\sup_{m>n}\left(\|\Phi_{A}(m,n)(\mathrm{Id}-P_{n})x\|\left(\frac{\mu_{m}}{\mu_{n}}\right)^{-\tilde{\nu}}\right).\end{split}

Clearly,

(5.25)

\|x\|\leq\|x\|_{n}\leq 4N\mu_{n}^{\varepsilon}\|x\|,\quad\text{for $x\in X$ and $n\in\mathbb{N}$.}

Observe that it follows from (

) holds. Similarly, using (

6.

Theorem 6.1

,

(g_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathcal{O}_{\mu}^{t+2}

p,\tilde{p}>0

(i)

We consider the sequence

4.1
gives that
\Sigma_{ED,\mathbb{B}}=\Sigma_{\mu D,\mathbb{A}}

n\in\mathbb{N}

5.11

5.5

We now claim that

7.1.
Nonautonomous topological linearization on the half-line

We recall the following result which is established in the proof of
[
8
, Theorem 5]
.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
\{\|\cdot\|_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a sequence of norms on
X
equivalent to
\|\cdot\|
,
\{A_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
a sequence of invertible bounded operators on
X
which admits an exponential dichotomy with respect to the sequence of norms
\{\|\cdot\|_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
, and with the property that there exists
M>0
such that

(7.1)

\|A_{n}x\|_{n+1}\leq M\|x\|_{n}\quad\text{and}\quad\|A_{n}^{-1}x\|_{n}\leq M\|x\|_{n+1},

for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and
x\in X
. Moreover, let
\{f_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a sequence of maps
f_{n}\colon X\to X
such that there exist
c,C>0
such that:

(i)

for
x\in X
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\|f_{n}(x)\|_{n+1}\leq C\|x\|_{n}
;

(ii)

for
x,y\in X
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,

\|f_{n}(x)-f_{n}(y)\|_{n+1}\leq c\|x-y\|_{n}.

Then, provided that
c
is sufficiently small, there exists a sequence
\psi_{n}\colon X\to X
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
satisfying

\psi_{n+1}\circ(A_{n}+f_{n})=A_{n}\circ\psi_{n},\quad\text{for $n\in\mathbb{Z}$.}

In addition, there are
D,\varrho>0
such that

\|\psi_{n}(x)-\psi_{n}(y)\|_{n}\leq D\|x-y\|_{n}^{\varrho}

and

\|\psi_{n}^{-1}(x)-\psi_{n}^{-1}(y)\|_{n}\leq D\|x-y\|_{n}^{\varrho},

for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and
x,y\in X
such that
\|x-y\|_{n}\leq 1
. Finally,

\|\psi_{n}(x)-x\|_{n}\leq D\quad\text{and}\quad\|\psi_{n}^{-1}(x)-x\|_{n}\leq D,

for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and
x\in X
.

We now establish the version of Theorem
7.1
for one-sided dynamics.

Corollary 7.2
.

Let
\{\|\cdot\|_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of norms on
X
equivalent to
\|\cdot\|
,
\{A_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

\{f_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

(i)

for

Then, provided that

\psi_{n}\colon X\to X
,

satisfying

\psi_{n+1}\circ(A_{n}+f_{n})=A_{n}\circ\psi_{n},\quad\text{for $n\in\mathbb{N}$.}

In addition, there are

for

Proof.

Let
\|\cdot\|_{n}=\|\cdot\|
for
n\leq 0

which is hyperbolic (i.e. its spectrum does not intersect the unit circle), and such that

Nonautonomous smooth linearization on half-line

We recall the following result, which is essentially established in
[

Theorem 7.3
.

Let
\mathbb{B}=\{B_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a sequence of invertible linear operators on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
that admits an exponential dichotomy and with the property that

(7.2)

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\|B_{n}\|<+\infty\quad\text{and}\quad\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\|B_{n}^{-1}\|<+\infty.

Suppose that

\Sigma_{ED,\mathbb{B}}=[a_{1},b_{1}]\cup\ldots\cup[a_{r},b_{r}],

with
1\leq r\leq d
and
a_{i},b_{i}

6.2
).
Then, for every
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
there exists
t\in\mathbb{N}
,
t\geq\ell
with the following property: if (
6.3
) holds and
(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\mathcal{O}^{t+2}
with

being

for each

\tilde{r}\in(0,\tilde{p})

diffeomorphisms
h_{n}\colon B_{r}(0)\to h_{n}(B_{r}(0))\subset B_{\tilde{p}}(0)
and
\bar{h}_{n}\colon B_{\tilde{r}}(0)\to\bar{h}_{n}(B_{\tilde{r}}(0))\subset B_{p}(0)
with the following properties:

(i)

\bar{h}_{n}(h_{n}(x))=x
for each

and

such that

\lim_{x\to 0}h_{n}(x)=0\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{x\to 0}\bar{h}_{n}(x)=0\quad\text{uniformly in $n$.}

Remark 7.4
.

We note that in
[

]
the authors have formulated a continuous-time version of Theorem

However, the proof proceeds by discretization of time, thus essentially yielding the version for discrete time as a by-product. Moreover,
(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\mathcal{O}^{t+2}

\|D^{j}f_{n}(x)\|\leq M,\quad\text{for $n\in\mathbb{Z}$, $x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$ and $0\leq j\leq t+2$.}

6.1

Corollary 7.5
.

Let

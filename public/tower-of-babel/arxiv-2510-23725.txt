Abstract
It was recently established that the paradigmatic Gross–Neveu model with N N copies of two-dimensional Dirac fermions features an SO ⁡ ( 2 ​ N ) \mathrm{SO}(2N) symmetry if certain interactions are suppressed. This becomes evident when the theory is rewritten in terms of 2 ​ N 2N copies of two-dimensional Majorana fermions. Mean-field theory for the SO ⁡ ( 2 ​ N ) \mathrm{SO}(2N) model predicts, besides the chiral Ising transition at g c ​ 1 g_{c1} , a second critical point g c ​ 2 g_{c2} where SO ⁡ ( 2 ​ N ) \mathrm{SO}(2N) is broken down to SO ⁡ ( N ) × SO ⁡ ( N ) \mathrm{SO}(N)\times\mathrm{SO}(N) . A subsequent Wilsonian renormalization group analysis directly in d = 3 d=3 supports its existence in a generalized theory, where N f N_{f} copies of the 4 ​ N 4N -component Majorana fermions are introduced. This allows to track the evolution of a (i) quantum anomalous Hall Gross–Neveu–Ising, (ii) symmetric-tensor, and (iii) adjoint-nematic fixed point separately. However, it turns out that (ii) and (iii) lose their criticality when approaching N f = 1 N_{f}=1 , suggesting that the transition is first order. In this work, we approach the problem from the lower-critical dimension of two. We construct a Fierz-complete renormalizable Lagrangian, compute the leading order β \beta functions, fermion anomalous dimension, as well as the order parameter anomalous dimensions, and resolve the three universality classes corresponding to (i)–(iii). Before becoming equal to the Gaussian fixed point at N f = 1 N_{f}=1 , (ii) remains critical for all values of N f > N f , c ST ​ ( N ) ≈ 0.56 + 1.48 ​ N + 𝒪 ⁡ ( ϵ ) N_{f}>N_{f,c}^{\mathrm{ST}}(N)\approx 0.56+1.48N+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon) , which compares well with the estimate of previous studies. We further find that (iii) becomes equal to (i) when approaching N f = 1 N_{f}=1 . An instability is, however, only present in the susceptibility corresponding to the Gross–Neveu–Ising order parameter.

The model defined by Eq. (

Rotational invariance:

During the renormalization-group flow, however, any other symmetry-allowed interaction term can in general be generated, which necessitates a Fierz-complete study of purely fermionic theories.

III.1
Symmetry-allowed operators

We now identify all four-fermion terms allowed by symmetries of the theory defined in Eq. (
11

(\bar{\chi}_{k}\mathcal{O}\chi_{k})
due to charge symmetry, where
\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{N}
is a
4N\times 4N
dimensional operator, with
\mathcal{N}
acting in the space of
2N\times 2N
matrices and
\mathcal{M}
acting in the space of
2\times 2
matrices. A basis
\mathcal{B}
in the space of
4N\times 4N
matrices is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{B}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{B}_{2}=\{\mathbb{1}_{2N},\mathbb{A}_{a},\mathbb{S}_{b}\}\otimes\{\mathbb{1}_{2},\gamma_{\mu},\gamma_{5}\},

(12)

where
a=1,...,N(2N-1)
and
b=1,...,(N+1)(2N-1)

The matrices
\mathbb{A}_{a}
are the antisymmetric generators of
\mathrm{SO}(2N)
with
\mathrm{Tr}(\mathbb{A}_{a}\mathbb{A}_{a^{\prime}})=2N\,\delta_{aa^{\prime}}
and transform in the adjoint representation of
\mathrm{SO}(2N)
, while the matrices
\mathbb{S}_{b}
are symmetric and traceless with
\mathrm{Tr}(\mathbb{S}_{b}\mathbb{S}_{b^{\prime}})=2N\,\delta_{bb^{\prime}}

\mathrm{SO}(2N)

All possible four-fermion terms can be constructed from bilinears. They can be taken as flavor singlets, i.e.,
(\bar{\chi}_{k}\mathcal{O}\chi_{k})(\bar{\chi}_{l}\mathcal{Q}\chi_{l})
, or flavor non-singlets, i.e.
(\bar{\chi}_{k}\mathcal{O}\chi_{l})(\bar{\chi}_{l}\mathcal{Q}\chi_{k})

\mathcal{O},\mathcal{Q}\in\mathcal{B}

N_{f}=1
, those Fierz identities reduce to exact identities between different four-fermion terms, further reducing the number of linearly independent terms. In the following, we will first consider the case of
N_{f}>1
, i.e., all flavor singlets are linearly independent, and discuss the case of trivial flavor structure separately.

Due to the
\mathrm{SO}(2N)
symmetry, interaction terms must be of the form
(\bar{\chi}(\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{N}_{1})\chi)(\bar{\chi}(\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{N}_{2})\chi)
, where
\mathcal{M}\in\mathcal{B}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{N}_{i}\in\mathcal{B}_{2}}
. Lorentz symmetry and parity symmetry further imply that
\mathcal{N}_{1}=\mathcal{N}_{2}
, and hence all four-fermion terms are of the form
(\bar{\chi}(\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{N})\chi)^{2}
. Further, note that some terms constructed in this way are vanishing. This is because of the additional identity (recall that in the chosen class of representations,
\gamma_{0}^{T}=-\gamma_{0}
),

\displaystyle\bar{\chi}_{k}(\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{N})\chi_{k}

\displaystyle=\chi_{k}^{T}(\mathcal{M}\otimes\gamma_{0}\mathcal{N})\chi_{k}

\displaystyle=\chi_{k}^{T}(\mathcal{M}^{T}\otimes\mathcal{N}^{T}\gamma_{0})\chi_{k}\,.

(13)

Hence, a bilinear is non-vanishing only in the following cases: (1) if
[\gamma_{0},\mathcal{N}]=0
, then
(\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{N})^{T}=\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{N}
, and (2) if
\{\gamma_{0},\mathcal{N}\}=0
, then
(\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{N})^{T}=-\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{N}
. In total, a Fierz-complete basis of linearly independent four-fermion terms for

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{int}}=

\displaystyle-\frac{\bar{g}_{1}}{8}(\bar{\chi}_{k}(\mathbb{1}_{2N}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{2})\chi_{k})^{2}-\frac{\bar{g}_{2}}{8}(\bar{\chi}_{k}(\mathbb{S}_{a}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{2})\chi_{k})^{2}

\displaystyle-\frac{\bar{g}_{3}}{8}(\bar{\chi}_{k}(\mathbb{A}_{b}\otimes\gamma_{\mu})\chi_{k})^{2}-\frac{\bar{g}_{4}}{8}(\bar{\chi}_{k}(\mathbb{A}_{b}\otimes\gamma_{5})\chi_{k})^{2}.

(14)

We emphasize that the theory has four linearly-independent four-fermion interactions, irrespective of the chosen class of spinor representation.
We also note that fermion flavor is conserved along any fermion line, implying that the flavor singlets in Eq. (
III.1

III.1
)

(\bar{\chi}(\mathbb{A}_{b}\otimes\gamma_{\mu})\chi)^{2}\\
(\bar{\chi}(\mathbb{A}_{b}\otimes\gamma_{5})\chi)^{2}\end{pmatrix},

(16)

the Fierz rearrangement formula yields the identity
FV=0
, with the
4\times 4
matrix

\displaystyle F=\begin{pmatrix}4N+1&1&1&1\\
(2N-1)(N+1)&5N-1&-N-1&-N-1\\
2N(2N-1)&-2N&4N&-2N\\
N(2N-1)&-N&-N&5N\end{pmatrix}.

For any integer

, we find

, implying that only one of the four interaction terms in Eq. (

) is linearly independent. In particular, the kernel of

is spanned by the vector

, and hence, for

, the interacting Lagrangian in Eq. (

) can be rewritten in terms of a single interaction term

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{int}}=-\frac{\bar{\tilde{g}}}{8}\big[

\displaystyle-(\bar{\chi}(\mathbb{1}_{2N}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{2})\chi)^{2}+(N+1)(\bar{\chi}(\mathbb{S}_{a}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{2})\chi)^{2}

\displaystyle+2N(\bar{\chi}(\mathbb{A}_{b}\otimes\gamma_{\mu})\chi)^{2}+N(\bar{\chi}(\mathbb{A}_{b}\otimes\gamma_{5})\chi)^{2}\big]

where

\bar{\tilde{g}}=-\bar{g}_{1}+(N+1)\bar{g}_{2}+2N\bar{g}_{3}+N\bar{g}_{4}.

(19)

\beta

We use the

For convenience, we use the rescaled couplings,

\displaystyle\beta_{1}=

III.4
Fixed-point structure

In order to find critical points of the theory defined by Eq. (
20
), we have to identify IR stable fixed points of the
\beta
functions calculated above. Such an analysis majorly simplifies if one-loop closed subspaces can be identified, i.e., a subspace of the coupling space where additional couplings do not get generated under renormalization-group transformations. We find two of such one-loop closed subspaces:

Gross–Neveu–Ising subspace:

Since no term
\sim g_{1}^{2}
appears in the flow equations of
g_{n}
with
n=2,3,4
, the subspace defined by
g_{2}=g_{3}=g_{4}=0
is one-loop closed, see also, e.g., Refs.
Gehring:2015
;
Ladovrechis:2023
;
Hawashin:2025
. This “Gross–Neveu–Ising subspace” includes an interacting fixed-point, given by

\displaystyle g^{\star}_{\mathrm{GNI}}=\left[\frac{N_{f}}{4(NN_{f}-1)},0,0,0\right]\epsilon,

(27)

which is IR stable for all
N_{f}>1
. Starting the flow with a small
g_{1}>g_{\mathrm{GNI},1}^{\star}
leads to a runaway flow to positive infinity, which corresponds to quantum anomalous Hall GNI criticality with order parameter
\langle\bar{\chi}\left(\mathbb{1}_{2N}\otimes\mathbb{1}\right)\chi\rangle>0
, which spontaneously breaks parity as well as
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
chiral symmetry.

Adjoint-Nematic subspace:

Another fixed point appears in the one-loop closed subspace where only
g_{3}\neq 0
, given by

\displaystyle g^{\star}_{\mathrm{N}}=\left[0,0,-\frac{N_{f}}{8N(N-1)},0\right]\epsilon.

(28)

It corresponds to adjoint-nematic quantum criticality with order parameter
\langle\bar{\chi}(\mathbb{A}_{a}\otimes\gamma_{\mu})\chi\rangle>0
. It is the lower-dimensional analog of the nematic critical point identified at and beyond mean-field in
2+1
dimensions
Herbut:2023une
;
Han:2024ird
. Note that
g^{\star}_{\mathrm{N}}\sim N_{f}
, and hence the fixed point moves towards infinity in the limit
N_{f}\to\infty
. The same is true for all
N_{f}
in the limit
N\to 1^{+}
, for which the corresponding interaction coincides with the Thirring coupling
Bondi:1989gba
.
The absence of a fixed-point for
N>1
that lies purely on the axis defined by the Thirring-like coupling
g_{3}
in the large-
N_{f}
limit is related to the vanishing
\beta
function of the Thirring coupling at all loop-orders in
d=1+1
dimensions for irreducible representations of the Lorentz group
BONDI1989345
.
The appearance of a
g_{3}^{2}/N_{f}
term at finite
N_{f}
and
N>1
is related to the multiplicity of the degrees of freedom, as also observed in related systems
Gehring:2015
;
Ladovrechis:2023
.
For
N_{f}=1
,
g^{\star}_{\mathrm{N}}
becomes equal to the GNI fixed point, since for
\tilde{g}
in Eq. (
34
), we find
\tilde{g}(g^{\star}_{\mathrm{N}})=-1/4(N-1)
. Notably, this differs from the result obtained with Wilsonian RG in
d=2+1

Han:2024ird
, where the adjoint-nematic fixed point coincides with the Gaussian fixed point for
N_{f}=1
.

We are also interested in fixed points that correspond to symmetric-tensor criticality, described by the order parameter
\langle\bar{\chi}(\mathbb{S}_{a}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{2})\chi\rangle
. Note, however, that a finite

always induces a flow of

, and together they lead to a non-trivial flow of

and
g_{4}
. Hence, there is no subspace that includes such a fixed point, and the full coupling space has to be analyzed. The identification of a suitable fixed point simplifies in the large-

limit of the one-loop
\beta
functions, where the individual
\beta
functions decouple and reduce to

\displaystyle\beta_{1}

\displaystyle=\epsilon g_{1}-4Ng_{1}^{2}+\mathcal{O}(1/N_{f}),

(29)

\displaystyle\beta_{2}

\displaystyle=\epsilon g_{2}-4Ng_{2}^{2}+\mathcal{O}(1/N_{f}),

(30)

\displaystyle\beta_{3}

\displaystyle=\epsilon g_{3}+\mathcal{O}(1/N_{f}),

(31)

\displaystyle\beta_{4}

\displaystyle=\epsilon g_{4}+4Ng_{4}^{2}+\mathcal{O}(1/N_{f}).

(32)

Note again the absence of a

term in the large-

limit, since

\displaystyle g_{\mathrm{ST}}^{\star}=\left[\mathcal{O}(1/N_{f}^{4}),\frac{1}{4N}+\mathcal{O}(1/N_{f}),\mathcal{O}(1/N_{f}),\mathcal{O}(1/N_{f}^{2})\right]\epsilon\,,

corresponding to criticality with the symmetric tensor order parameter

, which signals spontaneous symmetry breaking of

Up to

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}(1/N_{f}^{4}),

(35)

\displaystyle h_{2}(N_{f})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4N}+\frac{N-1}{4N^{2}}\frac{1}{N_{f}}-\frac{(N^{3}+N^{2}-N+1)(N-1)}{4N^{3}}\frac{1}{N_{f}^{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+\mathcal{O}(1/N_{f}^{3}),

(36)

\displaystyle h_{3}(N_{f})

\displaystyle=-\frac{N+1}{8N}\frac{1}{N_{f}}-\frac{(N+1)(N-1)}{4N^{2}}\frac{1}{N_{f}^{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+\mathcal{O}(1/N_{f}^{3}),

IV.1
Correlation-length exponent

The correlation-length exponent
\nu
is given by the inverse of the largest positive eigenvalue of the stability matrix
{S_{ij}=-\partial\beta_{i}/\partial g_{j}}
. At one-loop order, there is a unique positive eigenvalue given by
\epsilon

Gehring:2015
, and hence

1/\nu=\epsilon+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{2}).

(39)

IV.2
Fermion anomalous dimension

The fermion anomalous dimension vanishes at one-loop order in any four-fermion theory close to two spacetime dimensions, i.e.,
\eta_{\chi}(g^{\star})=\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{2})
. This, on the other hand, implies that the one-loop
\beta
functions for the couplings together with the two-loop result for the anomalous dimension are sufficient to compute the fermion anomalous dimension to
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{2})
.
We find the fermion anomalous dimension
\eta_{\chi}:=\frac{d\log Z_{\chi}}{d\log\mu}
to be

\displaystyle\eta_{\chi}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{f}^{2}}\Bigg[-8N(2N-1)\,g_{1}g_{3}+8N^{2}(2N-1)N\,g_{3}g_{4}

\displaystyle-4N(2N-1)\,g_{1}g_{4}-4(N+1)(2N-1)\,g_{1}g_{2}

\displaystyle+8N(N+1)(2N-1)\,g_{2}g_{3}+4N(N+1)(2N-1)\,g_{2}g_{4}

\displaystyle+8N_{f}N^{2}(2N-1)\,g_{3}^{2}+2N^{2}(2N-1)(2N_{f}-1)\,g_{4}^{2}

\displaystyle+2(N+1)(2N-1)\,(N(2N_{f}-1)+1)\,g_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle+2(2NN_{f}-1)\,g_{1}^{2}\Bigg]\,,

(40)

where
Z_{\chi}
is the field renormalization constant of the Majorana fermion, cf. App.
A
for details.

Evaluating the expression above at the GNI fixed point (
27
) yields

\displaystyle\eta_{\chi}^{\mathrm{GNI}}=\frac{2NN_{f}-1}{8(NN_{f}-1)^{2}}\epsilon^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{3}).

(41)

For
N_{f}=1
, this coincides with the standard result for the Gross–Neveu-Ising universality class
Bondi:1989gba
;
Gehring:2015
;
Ladovrechis:2023
, and further agrees with the result from the large-
N
expansion
Gracey:2025aoj
up to
\mathcal{O}(1/N^{4})
when expanding it in powers of
\epsilon=d-2
.

The fermion anomalous dimension at the symmetric-tensor fixed point (
34
) up to
\mathcal{O}(1/N_{f}^{3},\epsilon^{2})
is given by

\displaystyle\eta_{\chi}^{\mathrm{ST}}=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{4N(2N-1)(N+1)}{N_{f}}+\frac{6(N-1)(N+1)(2N-1)}{N_{f}^{2}}\right)\epsilon

\displaystyle+\mathcal{O}(1/N_{f}^{3},\epsilon^{2}).

(42)

For the adjoint-nematic fixed point (

\displaystyle\eta_{\chi}^{\mathrm{N}}=\frac{N_{f}(2N-1)}{8(N-1)^{2}}\epsilon^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{3}).

(43)

Precisely for

, as noted earlier, due to the equivalence of the adjoint-nematic fixed point and the GNI fixed point.

IV.3
Order parameter anomalous dimension

In order to calculate the anomalous dimension of the order parameters corresponding to Gross–Neveu, adjoint-nematic and symmetric-tensor criticality, we do a susceptibility analysis following the prescription of Ref.
Janssen:2016
. To that end, we perturb the action in Eq. (
20
) by the symmetry-breaking term

\displaystyle S\to S+y_{n}\int d^{d}x\bar{\chi}\mathcal{M}_{n}\chi

(44)

with

and
\mathcal{M}_{1}=\mathbb{1}_{2N}\otimes\mathbb{1}_{2}
,
\mathcal{M}_{2}=\mathbb{S}_{a}\otimes\mathbb{1}_{2}
, and
\mathcal{M}_{3}=\mathbb{A}_{b}\otimes\gamma_{\mu}
for fixed

,

and
\mu

The corresponding
\beta
function will be given by

\beta_{y_{n}}=\frac{dy_{n}}{d\log\mu}=-(1+x_{n})y_{n},

(45)

where

depends on all other couplings of the theory space (
III.1
).

The anomalous dimension of the corresponding order parameters

;
Janssen:2016
;
Ladovrechis:2023

\eta_{\phi}=2-\gamma/\nu

(46)

We find at the one-loop order

\displaystyle\eta_{\phi_{1}}

\displaystyle=2+\epsilon-\left(\frac{4}{N_{f}}-8N\right)\,g_{1}+\frac{4(N+1)(2N-1)}{N_{f}}\,g_{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{8N(2N-1)}{N_{f}}\,g_{3}+\frac{4N(2N-1)}{N_{f}}\,g_{4}\,,

(47)

\displaystyle\eta_{\phi_{2}}

\displaystyle=2+\epsilon+\frac{4}{N_{f}}\,g_{1}-\frac{4(2NN_{f}-N+1)}{N_{f}}\,g_{2}

(48)

\displaystyle\eta_{\phi_{3}}

\displaystyle=2+\epsilon\,.

(49)

\displaystyle+\mathcal{O}(1/N_{f}^{3},\epsilon^{2})

(53)

For the adjoint-nematic fixed point (

In this work, we have studied spontaneous symmetry breaking of
\mathrm{SO}(2N)
in the paradigmatic Gross–Neveu theory. First, we have shown that if certain interactions are tuned to zero, the corresponding restricted Gross–Neveu theory space has a symmetry enhancement from
\mathrm{SU}(N)\times\mathrm{U}(1)
to
\mathrm{SO}(2N)
. To that end, we considered a particular class of irreducible representations of the Clifford algebra, which allowed us to make the
\mathrm{SO}(2N)
symmetry manifest if the theory is rewritten in terms of
4N
-component Majorana fermions. We have then extended the theory by an additional flavor degree of freedom, and constructed a Fierz-complete renormalizable Lagrangian that includes four independent couplings. We have further shown by employing the Fierz rearrangement theorem that in the case of trivial flavor structure, the four interactions become redundant and ultimately reduce to one linear-independent four-fermion interaction that is equivalent to the canonical Gross–Neveu interaction. Furthermore, we computed the one–loop
\beta
functions together with the one-loop order parameter and two–loop fermion anomalous dimensions. We found three fixed points that are the lower-dimensional analogues to the fixed points found in the previous
d=2+1
Wilsonian RG analysis
Han:2024ird
: (i) QAH Gross–Neveu–Ising, (ii) symmetric–tensor, and (iii) adjoint–nematic fixed points.
While (i) and (iii) are located within one-loop closed subspaces, (ii) lives in the full coupling space but is tractable in a controlled large–
N_{f}
limit.

We have shown that any of these fixed points survive all the way down to
N_{f}=1
. In particular, for
N_{f}=1
, the fixed point (ii) becomes identical to the Gaussian fixed point, while the fixed-point (iii) coincides with (i). For the fixed point (iii), we do not find a diverging susceptibility for all
N
and
N_{f}\geq 1
at leading order, while the susceptibility corresponding to symmetric-tensor criticality diverges only for theories where
N_{f}>N_{f,c}^{\mathrm{ST}}\approx 0.56+1.48N
. Using a two-sided Padé interpolation that takes both estimates for
N_{f,c}^{\mathrm{ST}}
from the upper and lower critical dimension into account, we find
N_{f,c}^{\mathrm{ST}}(N=4)\approx 8.9
and
N_{f,c}(N=8)\approx 16.1
. Our results are in agreement with the findings of Ref.
Han:2024ird
;
PhysRevB.110.125131
, and supports the interpretation that the canonical Gross–Neveu model features a second-order transition only in the Gross–Neveu–Ising channel.

For future work, it would be interesting to investigate the effect of higher-loop corrections to our finding. It would be also interesting to better understand the absence of a diverging susceptibility at a fixed point, and connect it to the picture from the equivalent Gross–Neveu–Yukawa theory, where the absence of a continuous transition can be understood in a more conventional way as a fixed point collision. It would also be interesting to corroborate our findings by a non-perturbative renormalization group analysis.

AGENCY: Environmental Protection Agency (EPA).

SUMMARY: The Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) is publishing a 
final rule to update the incorporation by reference of several 
voluntary consensus standards in the Agency's formaldehyde standards 
for composite wood products regulations under the Toxic Substances 
Control Act (TSCA) due to the standards having been updated or 
superseded by the issuing organizations. These new standards primarily 
update test methods and product standards concerning composite wood 
products that use formaldehyde. EPA is also conforming these updated 
standards in the scope and definitional sections in the final rule and 
incorporating by reference a new small scale quality control chamber 
test method, similar to current methods already incorporated by 
reference. EPA is also removing one standard that was previously 
incorporated by reference to avoid redundancy, given that another 
identical, internationally recognized version of the standard is 
already incorporated by reference in this rule.

DATES: This final rule is effective on November 27, 2026. The 
incorporation by reference of certain material listed in the rule is 
approved by the Director of the Federal Register as of November 27, 
2026. The incorporation by reference of certain other material listed

February 10, 2017, February 7, 2018, August 21, 2019, and March 23, 
2023.

    The Agency is taking final action on the following:
1. Update Incorporation-By-Reference (IBR) for Certain Voluntary 
Consensus Standards
    EPA is finalizing an update to the Incorporation-By-Reference (IBR)

the most current standards issued by the relevant standards 
organizations. The relevant standards organizations updated these 
standards after EPA incorporated them in 40 CFR 770.99. The final rule 
will require regulated entities to adhere to the updated editions of 
the voluntary consensus standards when complying with the requirements 
of 40 CFR part 770. During the public comment period for the proposed 
rule, EPA received eight comments in support of updating these 
standards. These amendments are further explained in Unit II.B.
2. Addition of ISO 12460-2:2024(en) as an Alternative Quality Control 
Test Method
    EPA is also finalizing the incorporation by reference of an 
additional small scale quality control chamber test method, ISO 12460-
2:2024(en), in 40 CFR 770.99. This new standard will provide regulated 
entities with an additional quality control test method to choose from 
in 40 CFR 770.20(b). EPA received eight comments on the proposed rule 
in support of this standard's inclusion. This new standard is further 
explained in Unit II.B.
3. Removal of British Standards Institute Standard BS EN ISO 12460-3
    The proposed rule published in February 2026 included an update for 
British Standards Institute (BSI) standard BS EN ISO 12460-3:2023, as 
well as a request for comment on whether this standard should be 
retained in the final rule, given that an identical, international 
version (ISO 12460-3:2023) was also proposed for incorporation into the 
rule. EPA received three comments on the proposed rule supporting the 
removal of BS EN ISO 12460-3 from the final rule. To avoid redundancy 
and potential confusion for stakeholders, EPA is no longer 
incorporating the updated standard BS EN ISO 12460-3:2023 and is 
removing the standard BS EN ISO 12460-3:2020 from 40 CFR 
770.20(b)(1)(iii) and 40 CFR 770.99. This amendment is further 
explained in Unit II.B.
4. Conform Voluntary Consensus Standards in Scope and Definitions
    As a result of the final list of updated standards in Unit II.B., 
EPA is finalizing an update to 40 CFR 770.1 and 770.3 to reflect the 
current standards that are incorporated by reference in 40 CFR 770.99. 
EPA received eight comments on the proposed rule in support of these 
updates to reflect current standards which are in use.

D. Why is the Agency taking this action?

    The Agency is taking final action to update the incorporation by 
reference for several voluntary consensus standards at 40 CFR 770.99 to 
their current editions to address outdated, superseded, and withdrawn 
standards that have been updated between 2022 and 2024. These updates 
are needed because several of the standards in 40 CFR 770.99 are 
outdated. EPA is also taking final action to conform these voluntary 
consensus standards in the scope and definitional sections of 40 CFR 
part 770 to reflect the current editions that must be used by regulated 
entities. EPA believes that this action is warranted to facilitate 
regulated entities using the most up to date voluntary consensus 
standards to comply with the regulation at 40 CFR part 770. EPA is also 
taking final action to incorporate by reference at 40 CFR 770.99 an 
additional small scale quality control chamber test method, ISO 12460-
2:2024(en), to provide stakeholders with an additional option for 
quality control testing. EPA is also no longer incorporating the 
updated BSI standard BS EN ISO 12460-3:2023 and is removing the BSI 
standard BS EN ISO 12460-3:2020 from the final rule because an 
identical, international version (ISO 12460-3:2023) is being updated 
with this final action. EPA believes this is warranted because having 
duplicative standards is redundant, offers no technical value to the 
program, and has the potential to create confusion for the regulated 
community.

E. What are the incremental economic impacts?

    EPA anticipates no additional costs to stakeholders associated with 
this final rule for updated standards. This is a routine action that 
updates outdated voluntary consensus standards incorporated by 
reference at 40 CFR part 770, and includes a new, optional quality 
control test method. Removal of the standard BS EN ISO 12460-3:2020 is 
not anticipated to have any economic impact, given that the test method 
it describes is still available to stakeholders through ISO 12460-
3:2023.

II. Background

A. Regulatory Overview

1. Formaldehyde Emission Standards for Composite Wood Products
    The Formaldehyde Standards for Composite Wood Products Act of 2010 
(Pub. L. 111-199, 124 Stat. 1359) created Title VI of TSCA (15 U.S.C. 
2697), established emission standards for formaldehyde from composite 
wood products, and directed EPA to implement and enforce a number of 
provisions covering composite wood products. On December 12, 2016, EPA 
published a final rule (2016 final rule) (Ref. 1) to reduce exposure to 
formaldehyde emissions from certain wood products produced domestically 
or imported into the United States. EPA worked with the California Air 
Resource Board (CARB) to help align the 2016 final rule with the 
Airborne Toxics Control Measure (ATCM) (Ref. 2) to the extent EPA 
deemed appropriate and practical considering TSCA Title VI. By 
including provisions for laminated products, product-testing 
requirements, labeling, recordkeeping, and import certification, the 
2016 final rule requires that hardwood plywood, medium-density 
fiberboard, and particleboard products sold, supplied, offered for 
sale, imported to, or manufactured in the United States be in 
compliance with the emission standards. The 2016 final rule also 
established a third-party certification program for laboratory testing 
and oversight of formaldehyde emissions from manufactured and/or 
imported composite wood products.
2. 2018 Voluntary Consensus Standards Amendment
    On February 7, 2018, EPA published a final rule (Ref. 3) to update 
several voluntary consensus standards incorporated by reference at 40 
CFR 770.99. These updates applied to emission testing methods and 
regulated composite wood product construction characteristics. Several 
of the organizations that develop voluntary consensus standards (i.e., 
technical specifications for products or processes developed by 
standard-setting bodies) had updated, superseded, and/or withdrawn 
their standards through the normal course of business to take into 
account new information, technology, and methodologies.
3. 2019 Technical Issues Amendment
    On August 21, 2019, EPA amended 40 CFR part 770 via a final rule 
(2019 final rule) (Ref. 4) to address certain technical issues. The 
2019 final rule:

[[Page 67773]]

     Further aligned testing requirements with the CARB ATCM;
     Clarified provisions addressing non-complying lots and how 
those provisions apply to fabricators, importers, retailers, and 
distributors who are notified by panel producers that composite wood 
products they were supplied are found to be non-compliant after those 
composite wood products have been further fabricated into component 
parts or finished goods;
     Clarified that regulated composite wood products and 
finished goods containing composite wood products must be labeled at 
the point of manufacture or fabrication, and if imported, the label 
must be applied to the products as a condition of importation;
     Addressed TSCA Title VI ``manufactured-by'' date issues; 
and
     Updated two voluntary consensus standards that were 
incorporated by reference in 40 CFR 770.99.
4. 2023 Voluntary Consensus Standards Amendment
    On February 21, 2023, EPA published a final rule (2023 final rule) 
(Ref. 5) that amended 40 CFR part 770 to, among other things:
     Update the incorporation by reference for ten voluntary 
consensus standards to reflect the current editions that are in use by 
regulated entities and industry stakeholders. These updates were needed 
to ensure continued consistency with the standards adopted and used by 
industry.
     Allow for remote inspections in the event of unsafe 
conditions that would prevent a third-party certifier (TPC) from 
traveling in-person to the area. During the COVID-19 public health 
emergency, EPA provided temporary flexibility to allow TPCs to conduct 
remote inspections to satisfy the requirements of the rule. EPA made 
this flexibility permanent and allowed TPCs to conduct the required 
initial on-site inspection or quarterly inspections and sample 
collections remotely when in-person, on-site inspections are 
temporarily infeasible because of unsafe conditions.
     Include certain technical corrections and updates to 
create additional flexibilities for the third-party certification 
process, as well as clarifying language as it relates to the production 
of wood products. Those corrections better aligned EPA's rule with the 
CARB requirements.

B. Final Rule Amendments

1. Voluntary Consensus Standards IBR Update
a. IBR Update
    EPA is taking final action to update the IBR of certain voluntary 
consensus standards in 40 CFR 770.99 to reflect the most recent 
editions of the following standards assembled by the American National 
Standards Institute (ANSI), the American Society for Testing and 
Materials (ASTM), the International Organization for Standardization 
(ISO), and the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST):
i. Product Standard for Structural Glued Laminated Timber (ANSI A190.1-
2022)
    This standard was initiated by the Engineered Wood Association 
(APA) and approved through ANSI. The ANSI standard details the specific 
requirements for production, inspection, testing and certification of 
structural glued laminated timber. The standard also describes a 
quality control system for the laminator, which covers plant 
qualification, daily quality control, product marking, and the 
functions of an accredited inspection agency. ANSI last updated this 
standard on February 17, 2022 (Ref. 6). EPA is taking final action to 
update the version of the standard incorporated by reference in 40 CFR 
770.99 from ANSI A190.1-2017 to ANSI A190.1-2022.
ii. Standard Test Method for Determining Formaldehyde Levels From Wood 
Product Using a Desiccator (ASTM D5582-22)
    This standard was issued by ASTM and identifies procedures for 
testing formaldehyde emission potential from wood products by measuring 
airborne formaldehyde after samples of a specified surface area are 
placed in a small distilled water reservoir within a closed desiccator 
for 2 hours. The ASTM standard was last updated in August 2022 (Ref. 
7). EPA is taking final action to update the version of the standard 
incorporated by reference in 40 CFR 770.99 from ASTM D5582-14 to ASTM 
D5582-22.
iii. Standard Test Method for Determining Formaldehyde Concentrations 
in Air From Wood Products Using a Small-Scale Chamber (ASTM D6007-22)
    This standard was issued by ASTM and describes the procedure for 
using a small-scale chamber test method to measure formaldehyde 
concentrations in air emitted by wood product test specimens under 
defined test conditions of temperature and relative humidity. Results 
from the small-scale method are intended to be comparable to results 
obtained from testing larger samples using the large chamber test 
method, described in ASTM E1333. This ASTM standard was last updated in 
August 2022 (Ref. 8). EPA is taking final action to update the version 
of the standard incorporated by reference in 40 CFR 770.99 from ASTM 
D6007-14 to ASTM D6007-22.
iv. Standard Test Method for Determining Formaldehyde Concentrations in 
Air and Emission Rates From Wood Products Using a Large Chamber (ASTM 
E1333-22)
    This standard was issued by ASTM and describes the procedure for 
using a large chamber test method to measure formaldehyde concentration 
in air and emission rate from wood products under conditions designed 
to simulate product use, while maintaining specific test conditions of 
temperature and relative humidity. The standard was last updated in 
August 2022 (Ref. 9). EPA is taking final action to update the version 
of the standard incorporated by reference in 40 CFR 770.99 from ASTM 
E1333-14 to ASTM E1333-22.
v. Wood-Based Panels--Determination of Formaldehyde Release--Part 3: 
Gas Analysis Method (ISO 12460-3:2023(E))
    This standard was approved through ISO and describes a procedure 
for determination of accelerated formaldehyde release from wood-based 
panels. ISO 12460-3:2023(E) is identical to BS EN ISO 12460-3:2023, 
which is no longer included in this update and is being removed from 40 
CFR 770.99. This version, ISO 12460-3:2023(E), was incorporated to 
avoid potential confusion by regulated stakeholders and allow 
manufacturers to use a single standard irrespective of their country. 
The standard was last updated in September 2023 (Ref. 10). EPA is 
taking final action to update the version of the standard incorporated 
by reference in 40 CFR 770.99 from ISO 12460-3:2020(E) to ISO 12460-
3:2023(E).
vi. Voluntary Product Standard PS 1-22, Structural Plywood (PS 1-22)
    This standard was issued by NIST and describes the principal types 
and grades of structural plywood, covering the wood species, veneer 
grading, adhesive bonds, panel construction and workmanship, dimensions 
and tolerances, marking, moisture content and packaging of structural 
plywood intended for construction and industrial uses. Test methods to 
determine compliance and a glossary of trade terms and definitions are 
included, as is

[[Page 67774]]

a quality certification program involving inspection, sampling, and 
testing of products identified as complying with this standard by 
qualified testing agencies. The NIST standard was last updated on 
October 2, 2023 (Ref. 11). EPA is taking final action to update the 
version of the standard incorporated by reference in 40 CFR 770.99 from 
PS 1-19 to PS 1-22.
b. Addition of ISO 12460-2:2024(en) as an Alternative Quality Control 
Test Method
    EPA is taking final action to add ISO 12460-2:2024(en), Wood-based 
panels--Determination of Formaldehyde Release--Part 2: Small-scale 
Chamber Method (Ref. 12), as a quality control test method in 40 CFR 
770.20(b)(1) and to IBR the standard in 40 CFR 770.99. This standard 
was approved through ISO and describes the procedure for using a small-
scale chamber test method to test formaldehyde emissions from wood 
products under defined test conditions of temperature, relative 
humidity, loading and air exchange rate by measuring the concentrations 
of formaldehyde in air from samples of a specified surface area. This 
standard is a small scale quality control chamber test method that EPA 
is taking final action to incorporate by reference as an allowable 
alternative quality control test method, due to its similarity to other 
standards currently incorporated by reference for quality control 
methods, and to allow regulated entities to make use of the wider range 
of analytical methods the standard allows, such as laser absorption 
spectroscopy.
    Industry has requested including the latest testing methodology 
which allows for greater industry flexibility in choosing quality 
control methods that best fit their circumstances. Including this 
additional standard also aligns with the CARB ATCM, which recently 
incorporated ISO 12460-2:2024(en) as a valid small scale quality 
control chamber test method. EPA believes that this action is warranted 
to facilitate regulated entities using the most up-to-date voluntary 
consensus standards to comply with the regulation at 40 CFR part 770 as 
well as align with current CARB requirements. Based on its similarity 
to other standards currently incorporated by reference for quality 
control methods, requests from industry to include the latest testing 
methodology, and support from public comments, EPA is taking final 
action to add this new ISO standard as a quality control method in 40 
CFR 770.20(b)(1) and to incorporate it by reference in 40 CFR 770.99.
    EPA will initiate additional notice-and-comment rulemaking when 
necessary to reflect any future changes to voluntary consensus 
standards incorporated by reference in 40 CFR 770.99.
c. Removal of British Standards Institute Standard BS EN ISO 12460-3
    Because BS EN ISO 12460-3 and ISO 12460-3 are identical standards 
published by different standards development organizations, EPA is 
taking final action to consolidate 40 CFR 770.99 to only include the 
international version, ISO 12460-3:2023. This final rule removes the 
standard BS EN ISO 12460-3:2020 from the list of allowable quality 
control methods in 40 CFR 770.20(b)(1)(iii) and the list of standards 
approved for IBR in 40 CFR 770.99. The exact same procedure for 
determination of accelerated formaldehyde release from wood-based 
panels described by BS EN ISO 12460-3 is still available to 
stakeholders through ISO 12460-3:2023.

C. Rationale for Rule Changes

    EPA is taking final action to update the incorporation by reference 
of certain voluntary consensus standards in 40 CFR 770.99 that have 
been updated, superseded, or withdrawn by the issuing organizations. 
These new standards are needed to reflect the most recent editions of 
those standards issued by the relevant standards organizations. EPA is 
also taking final action with the addition of a new standard, ISO 
12460-2:2024(en), to allow industry greater flexibility in choosing 
quality control methods that best fit their circumstances, such as 
laser absorption spectroscopy. This additional standard also aligns 
with the CARB ATCM, which recently incorporated ISO 12460-2:2024(en) as 
a valid small scale quality control chamber test method. EPA believes 
that this action is warranted to facilitate regulated entities using 
the most up-to-date voluntary consensus standards to comply with the 
regulation at 40 CFR part 770 as well as align with current CARB 
requirements. EPA is also taking final action to remove the BSI 
standard BS EN ISO 12460-3:2020 as a standard approved for IBR in 40 
CFR 770.99, and is no longer incorporating the updated version BS EN 
ISO 12460-3:2023 to avoid redundancy and potential confusion for 
stakeholders, given an identical, international version of the standard 
(ISO 12460-3:2023) is also incorporated by reference in this rule.

III. Summary of Public Comments

    EPA received nine public comments in total during the 30-day public 
comment period for the proposed rule (91 FR 6161) and carefully 
considered each submission. One commenter (EPA-HQ-OPPT-2017-0245-0058) 
did not raise a substantive issue relevant to the proposed rule.
    The remaining eight comments were directly relevant to the proposed 
rule. All eight relevant commenters supported the proposed updates to 
the standards and the inclusion of the new quality control method, ISO 
12460-2:2024(en).
    Three commenters (EPA-HQ-OPPT-2017-0245-0056; EPA-HQ-OPPT-2017-
0245-0061; EPA-HQ-OPPT-2017-0245-0064) supported EPA removing the 
British Standards Institute standard BS EN ISO 12460-3 from the final 
rule and confirmed that retaining this standard would be redundant, 
given that an identical, international version (ISO 12460-3:2023) is 
also incorporated into the rule. EPA has revised the rule to remove BS 
EN ISO 12460-3:2020 from 40 CFR 770.20(b)(1)(iii) and 40 CFR 770.99.
    Three commenters (EPA-HQ-OPPT-2017-0245-0056; EPA-HQ-OPPT-2017-
0245-0059; EPA-HQ-OPPT-2017-0245-0064) requested that EPA define 
``significant change'' as it is used in the TSCA Title VI rule, 
specifically in terms of what constitutes a significant change that 
would render an established equivalence or correlation between 
different test methods invalid, thus requiring a redemonstration of 
equivalence. This terminology is not new; the phrase ``significant 
change'' has appeared in 40 CFR 770.20(d) since the 2016 TSCA Title VI 
final rule and is not introduced by the updates in this action. 
Generally, a significant change may be any change in equipment, 
procedure, or the qualifications of testing personnel that a 
technically competent person would expect to alter the established 
statistical relationship between test methods. See, for example, the 
discussion of the topic in EPA's Frequent Questions for Regulated 
Stakeholders about Implementing the Formaldehyde Standards for 
Composite Wood Products Act at Question 2 and Question 5 under the 
section on ``Starting-Up New Mills,'' available online at https://www.epa.gov/formaldehyde/frequent-questions-regulated-stakeholders-about-implementing-formaldehyde-standards#newmills. EPA will consider 
publishing additional examples of what may constitute a significant 
change at a future date.
    Two commenters (EPA-HQ-OPPT-2017-0245-0056; EPA-HQ-OPPT-2017-0245-
0059) requested that EPA address the timing expectation for 
transitioning to the new standards being updated or adopted with this 
rulemaking. Beyond the effective date of this final rule's amendments 
being 30 days after publication of this final rule, EPA is not 
establishing an additional transition period in this action because the 
updated editions incorporated by reference are technically consistent 
with the previously referenced editions and are not expected to affect 
compliance determinations or implementation. The new standard being 
incorporated, ISO 12460-2:2024(en), introduces an optional method for 
quality control testing, and thus imposes no transitional burden on 
regulated entities. In future actions where EPA proposes to incorporate 
editions that introduce substantive changes that could affect 
implementation (e.g., procedures, calculations, or apparatus), the 
Agency intends to evaluate and, as appropriate, propose defined 
transition provisions to minimize disruption.
    One commenter (EPA-HQ-OPPT-2017-0245-0056) requested that EPA 
clarify how the new standard ISO 12460-2:2024(en) fits within Quality 
Control Limit (QCL) and correlation requirements. ISO 12460-2:2024(en) 
can be used in the same manner that other quality control methods are 
used to conduct routine quality control testing, at a frequency 
specified in 40 CFR 770.20(b)(2), with results reported as specified in

B. Executive Order 14192: Unleashing Prosperity Through Deregulation

    This action is considered an Executive Order 14192 (90 FR 9065, 
January 31, 2025) deregulatory action. This final rule provides burden 
reduction by updating the incorporation by reference of several 
voluntary consensus standards in TSCA Title VI due to certain standards 
having been updated or superseded by the issuing organizations, 
aligning requirements with current industry practices. To avoid 
redundancy and potential confusion for stakeholders, EPA is no longer 
incorporating the updated standard BS EN ISO 12460-3:2023 and is 
removing the standard BS EN ISO 12460-3:2020 from 40 CFR 
770.20(b)(1)(iii) and 40 CFR 770.99. This amendment is further 
explained in Unit II.B. EPA is also taking final action with the 
addition of a new standard, ISO 12460-2:2024, to allow industry greater 
flexibility in choosing quality controls methods that best fit their 
circumstances, such as laser absorption spectroscopy. This deregulatory 
action removes redundancy, aligns requirements with current industry 
practices and eliminates duplicative or outdated standards.

D. Regulatory Flexibility Act (RFA)

    I certify that this action will not have a significant economic 
impact on a substantial number of small entities under the RFA, 5 
U.S.C. 601 et seq. In making this determination, EPA concludes that the 
impact of concern for this action is any significant adverse economic 
impact on small entities, and the Agency is certifying that this action 
will not have a significant economic impact on a substantial number of 
small entities because it will impose no net burden on the small 
entities to the rule. The final rule updates the incorporation by 
reference of voluntary consensus standards in 40 CFR part 770 by 
adopting the most current versions of those standards, adding one new 
voluntary consensus standard as a quality control test method, and by 
removing one duplicative, redundant standard. The updated versions of 
the standards are substantially similar to the previous versions. EPA 
expects that many small entities are already complying with the updated 
versions of the standards listed Unit II.B. This action allows these 
entities the flexibility to use the most up to date versions of the 
standards instead of using outdated versions that do not reflect 
industry best practice. This action also adds one new voluntary 
consensus standard as a quality control test method in 40 CFR 
770.20(b)(1), which regulated entities may opt to use in lieu of the 
other approved quality control methods, thereby increasing regulatory 
flexibility. We have therefore concluded that this action will have no 
net regulatory burden for all directly regulated small entities.

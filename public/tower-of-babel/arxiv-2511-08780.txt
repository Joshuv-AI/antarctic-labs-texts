Around a class version of the Hodge index theorem for singular varieties
Mohammadali Aligholi, Laurenţiu Maxim, Jörg Schürmann

Abstract
. We give an overview of recent developments around a characteristic class version of the Hodge index theorem for singular complex algebraic varieties. This was formulated by Brasselet-Schürmann-Yokura as a conjecture expressing the Goresky-MacPherson homology L L -classes in terms of suitable Hodge-theoretic L L -classes. Along the way, we clarify the relationship between several notions of L L -classes appearing in the literature, but we also include many new cases for which the conjecture is true, e.g., all compact toric varieties, all (matroid) Schubert varieties, all Richardson and intersection varieties, all projective simply connected spherical varieties, and all compact complex algebraic surfaces and threefolds.

57R20, 14B05, 32S35, 14C40

1.
Introduction

The classical Hodge index theorem
\sigma(X)=\chi_{1}(X)
expresses the signature
\sigma(X)
of a compact complex manifold
X
as the value at
y=1
of the Hirzebruch
\chi_{y}
-genus
\chi_{y}(X)
; see
[
25
]
. In the complex algebraic context, this computes the signature of
X
in terms of its Hodge numbers,
based on the hard Lefschetz theorem and the corresponding Hodge-Riemann bilinear relations.

The Hodge index theorem has been extended to the singular context
by using the signature and, respectively, intersection cohomology
\chi_{y}
-genus,
I\chi_{y}
, defined via the Goresky-MacPherson (middle-perversity) intersection cohomology groups
[
23
]
. Specifically, for
a (possibly singular) compact complex algebraic variety
X
of pure dimension, the signature
\sigma(X)
of the Poincaré duality pairing on the middle intersection cohomology group can be computed as

\sigma(X)=I\chi_{1}(X)\>,

i.e., as a certain expression in the Hodge numbers of the pure Hodge structure on the intersection cohomology groups of
X
(defined via M. Saito’s theory of pure Hodge modules); see
[
27
,
21
]
.

The signature
\sigma(X)
and the intersection cohomology Hirzebruch genus
I\chi_{y}(X)
of a pure-dimensional compact complex algebraic variety are the degrees of the Goresky-MacPherson homology
L
-class
L_{*}(X)

[
23
]
and, respectively, of the intersection cohomology Hirzebruch class
IT_{y*}(X)

[
9
]
, and it was conjectured in
[
9
]
that a corresponding
characteristic class version of the Hodge index theorem should hold.
More precisely, one has the following:

Conjecture 1.1

(Characteristic class version of Hodge index theorem)
.

Let
X
be a compact pure-dimensional complex algebraic variety. Then

(1)

L_{*}(X)=IT_{1*}(X).

In fact, the conjecture seems to be attributed to Cappell and Shaneson (see
[
9
, Remark 5.4]
and the references therein), so it predates the construction of Hirzebruch classes of
[
9
]
. Only the case for rational homology manifolds is explicitly conjectured in
[
9
]
.
The conjecture holds in the smooth context by work of Hirzebruch on the generalized Hirzebruch-Riemann-Roch theorem
[
25
]
, and it was recently proved in the case of rational homology manifolds in
[
21
]
, see also
[
20
]
. Several special cases have also been checked in
[
14
]
for certain complex hypersurfaces with isolated singularities, in
[
15
]
for global quotients of projective varieties by finite groups, in
[
30
]
for projective simplicial toric varieties, in
[
6
]
for Schubert varieties in a Grassmannian; see Section
3.4
for a more detailed reference list.

The purpose of this paper is to explain the motivation for Conjecture
1.1
, survey its status and recent developments, but to also present some new cases where the conjecture holds, e.g., for all compact toric varieties, all (matroid) Schubert varieties, all Richardson and intersection varieties,
all projective simply connected spherical varieties, and all compact complex algebraic surfaces and threefolds.
Although some of these cases are perhaps known to experts, we could not find them explicitly stated in the existing literature.

Remark 1.2
.

If
[M]\in\im(\chi_{Hdg})\subset K_{0}(\mathrm{MHM}(X))
, then the commutativity of the relevant parts of diagram (

) as explained above, yield that

(L_{*}\circ\mathrm{Pol})([M])=MHT_{1*}([M]).

This applies in particular to the constant Hodge complex
[\mathbf{Q}^{H}_{X}]=\chi_{Hdg}([id_{X}])
. This also implies that Conjecture
1.1
will hold whenever
[IC_{X}^{\prime H}]\in\im(\chi_{Hdg})
, as in the following examples.

Example 1.3
.

If
X
is a rational homology manifold, then
\chi_{Hdg}([id_{X}:X\to X])=[\mathbf{Q}_{X}^{H}]=[IC^{\prime H}_{X}]
, which puts the main results of
[
20
,
21
]
into the framework of this paper.

Example 1.4
.

If
f:Y\to X
is a small resolution of singularities, then
Rf_{*}\mathbf{Q}_{Y}=IC^{\prime}_{X}
, hence
[IC^{\prime H}_{X}]\in\im(\chi_{Hdg}),
since
\chi_{Hdg}([f:Y\to X]):=[f_{!}\mathbf{Q}_{Y}^{H}]=[f_{*}\mathbf{Q}_{Y}^{H}]=[IC^{\prime H}_{X}].

Example 1.5
.

Recall that if
f:Y\to X
is the normalization of
X
, then
Rf_{*}IC^{\prime}_{Y}=f_{*}IC^{\prime}_{Y}=IC^{\prime}_{X}
. If, moreover,
X=\cup_{i}X_{i}
is a simple normal crossing divisor in a smooth variety, then its normalization,
Y=\sqcup_{i}X_{i}
, is the disjoint union of its smooth irreducible components, hence
IC^{\prime}_{Y}=\oplus_{i}\mathbf{Q}_{X_{i}}
. This implies
IC_{X}^{\prime H}\in\im(\chi_{Hdg})
.

Remark 1.6
.

More generally, if
f:Y\to X
is a proper homologically small map of degree one in the sense of Goresky-MacPherson
[
24
, Section 6.2]
, then
Rf_{*}IC^{\prime}_{Y}=IC^{\prime}_{X}
, hence
[IC^{\prime H}_{X}]\in\im(\chi_{Hdg})
if
[IC^{\prime H}_{Y}]\in\im(\chi_{Hdg})
, e.g., if
Y
is a rational homology manifold.

Remark 1.7
.

Conjecture
1.1
will hold whenever
[IC_{X}^{\prime H}]
belongs to
the
K_{0}(\mathrm{MHM}(pt))
-submodule generated by
\im(\chi_{Hdg})\subset K_{0}(\mathrm{MHM}(X))
, see Corollary

.

Example 1.8

(Secant varieties of a smooth projective curve)
.

Let
C
be a smooth projective curve in
\mathbf{C}P^{N}
, embedded by a line bundle which separates
2k
points. Consider the secant variety
Sec^{k}(C)
of
k
-planes through the
curve
C
, the
k
-th secant bundle

of
C
, and the natural map
\beta_{k}:B^{k}(C)\to Sec^{k}(C)
. Then

is isomorphic to

and the map
\beta_{1}
is an isomorphism. Moreover, for

,

is a proper subvariety of

holds for such secant varieties embedded into a projective space via a

is a rational normal curve, then in fact each secant variety

is a rational homology manifold. (We thank Bradley Dirks, Sebastian Olano and Debaditya Raychaudhury for bringing the paper
[
11
]

Example 1.9
.

Assume the pure-dimensional compact complex algebraic variety

.

By a different type of argument, it can be shown that

2.
Characteristic classes of smooth complex varieties

In this section, we recall the definition of the Hirzebruch class of a smooth
complex algebraic variety, and explain its relation to the
L
-class.

If
X
is a smooth
complex algebraic variety, the signature (for
X
also compact) and the

L
-classes of
X
are special cases of more general Hodge theoretic
invariants encoded by the Hirzebruch characteristic class (also
called “the generalized Todd class”)

T_{y}^{*}(X):=T_{y}^{*}(T_{X})

of the (holomorphic) tangent
bundle of
X
(cf.
[
25
]
). This is defined by the normalized
power series

(3)

Q_{y}(\alpha)=\frac{\alpha(1+y)}{1-e^{-\alpha(1+y)}}-\alpha y\in\mathbb{Q}[y][[\alpha]],

that is,

(4)

T_{y}^{*}(T_{X})=\prod_{i=1}^{\dim(X)}Q_{y}(\alpha_{i}),

where
\{\alpha_{i}\}
are the Chern roots of the tangent bundle

T_{X}
. Note that
Q_{y}(\alpha)
satisfies

Q_{y}(\alpha)=\begin{cases}\>1+\alpha&\text{for $y=-1$,}\\
\>\frac{\alpha}{1-e^{-\alpha}}&\text{for $y=0$,}\\
\>\frac{\alpha}{\tanh\alpha}&\text{for $y=1$.}\end{cases}

Therefore, the Hirzebruch
class
T_{y}^{*}(T_{X})
coincides with the total Chern class
c^{*}(T_{X})

if
y=-1
, with the total Todd class
\mathrm{td}^{*}(T_{X})
if
y=0
, and
with the total Thom-Hirzebruch
L
-class
L^{*}(T_{X})
if
y=1
. As usual, cohomology classes of
X
correspond to those defined via its tangent bundle, e.g.,
L^{*}(X)=L^{*}(T_{X})
.

For
X
compact, the Hirzebruch class appears in the generalized
Hirzebruch-Riemann-Roch theorem (cf.
[
25
]
, §21.3), which
in particular implies that
the Hirzebruch genus

(5)

\chi_{y}(X):=\chi_{y}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}):=\sum_{p\geq 0}\chi(X,\Omega^{p}_{X})\cdot y^{p}=\sum_{p\geq 0}\left(\sum_{i\geq 0}(-1)^{i}\dim H^{i}(X,\Omega^{p}_{X})\right)\cdot y^{p}

is computed by

(6)

\chi_{y}(X)=\langle{T}_{y}^{*}(T_{X}),[X]\rangle,

with
\langle-,-\rangle
the usual Kronecker pairing.
Hence, for the special values
y=-1,0,1
, formula (
6
) yields the identifications

(7)

\chi_{-1}(X)=\langle c^{*}(T_{X}),[X]\rangle,

(8)

\chi_{0}(X)=\langle\mathrm{td}^{*}(T_{X}),[X]\rangle,

(9)

\chi_{1}(X)=\langle L^{*}(T_{X}),[X]\rangle.

In view of the Gauss–Bonnet–Chern Theorem (which expresses the Euler characteristic
\chi(X)
as the degree of the total Chern class of
T_{X}
), the Riemann-Roch Theorem (expressing the arithmetic genus
\chi_{a}(X)
as the degree of the total Todd class), and, resp., the Hirzebruch Signature Theorem (expressing the signature
\sigma(X)
as the degree of the total
L
-class), one then has that

(10)

\chi(X)=\chi_{-1}(X),\ \ \chi_{a}(X)=\chi_{0}(X),\ \ \sigma(X)=\chi_{1}(X).

Note that if
h^{p,q}(X):=\dim H^{q}(X,\Omega^{p}_{X})
are the Hodge numbers of the smooth compact complex algebraic variety
X
, then
it follows from (
5
) that

(11)

\chi_{y}(X)=\sum_{p,q}(-1)^{q}h^{p,q}(X)\cdot y^{p}.

The identification
\sigma=\chi_{1}
is usually referred as the (classical) “Hodge index theorem”, see
[
25
, Theorem 15.8.2]
for the Kähler case
(based on the hard Lefschetz theorem and the corresponding Hodge-Riemann bilinear relations),
and the discussion in the bibliographical note of
[
25
, Section 23.1]
for the compact complex manifold case (based on the Atiyah-Singer index theorem).

3.
Characteristic classes of singular complex algebraic varieties

The above-mentioned cohomology characteristic classes of complex algebraic manifolds have been extended to the singular setting via suitable natural transformations valued in homology, see
[
2
,
9
,
17
,
23
,
26
]
. Since here we are only interested in the relation between Hirzebruch classes and
L
-classes, we focus on these.

3.1.
Hirzebruch classes

Let
X
be a reduced complex algebraic variety, and we denote by
H_{*}(X)
the even-degree Borel-Moore homology
H_{2*}^{BM}(X;\mathbf{Z})
.

Let
K_{0}(var/X)
be the relative Grothendieck group of algebraic
varieties over
X
, i.e., the quotient of the free abelian group of isomorphism
classes of algebraic morphisms
Y\to X
to
X
, modulo the additivity
relation generated by the “scissor relation”

(12)

[Y\to X]=[Z\hookrightarrow Y\to X]+[Y\backslash Z\hookrightarrow Y\to X]

for
Z\subset Y
a closed algebraic subset of
Y
.
By resolution of singularities,
K_{0}(var/X)
is generated by
classes
[Y\to X]
with
Y
smooth pure dimensional and proper
(or projective) over
X
. There is a notion of pushforward for any
f:X^{\prime}\to X
defined by

f_{!}([h:Z\to X^{\prime}])=[f\circ h:Z\to X].

In
[
9
, Theorem 2.1]
, it was shown that there exists a unique group homomorphism

T_{y*}:K_{0}(var/X)\to H_{*}(X)\otimes\mathbf{Q}[y]\>,

commuting with pushforward
for proper maps, and
satisfying the normalization condition

T_{y*}([id_{X}])=T^{*}_{y}(T_{X})\cap[X]

for
X
smooth and pure-dimensional.

The transformation
T_{y*}
has a Hodge-theoretic incarnation
MHT_{y*}
defined as follows.
For a complex algebraic variety
X
, we let
\mathrm{MHM}(X)
denote the abelian category of algebraic mixed Hodge modules on
X
, cf.
[
38
]
. First, we introduce a K-theoretic version of the Chern class transformation, namely

\mathrm{DR}_{y}:K_{0}(\mathrm{MHM}(X))\to K_{0}(X)[y,y^{-1}],

where
K_{0}(X)=K_{0}(Coh(X))
is the Grothendieck group of coherent sheaves
of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules on
X
.
If
X
is smooth and
M\in\mathrm{MHM}(X)
, let
(\mathcal{M},F_{\bullet}\mathcal{M})
be the underlying filtered
\mathscr{D}_{X}
-module of
M
, and set

\begin{split}\mathrm{DR}_{y}[M]&\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sum_{p}\left[\mathrm{Gr}^{F}_{-p}\mathrm{DR}(\mathcal{M})\right]\cdot(-y)^{p}\\
&=\sum_{p,i}(-1)^{i}\left[\mathcal{H}^{i}\mathrm{Gr}^{F}_{-p}\mathrm{DR}(\mathcal{M})\right]\cdot(-y)^{p}\in K_{0}(X)[y,y^{-1}],\end{split}

with
\mathrm{Gr}^{F}_{-p}\mathrm{DR}(\mathcal{M})
the graded parts of the de Rham complex of
\mathcal{M}
with respect to the induced Hodge filtration. Note that only finitely many cohomology groups
\mathcal{H}^{i}\mathrm{Gr}^{F}_{-p}\mathrm{DR}(\mathcal{M})
are non-zero.
The definition of
\mathrm{DR}_{y}
extends to the case when
X
is singular by using locally defined closed embeddings into smooth varieties, since the graded quotient cohomology sheaves
\mathcal{H}^{i}\mathrm{Gr}^{F}_{p}\mathrm{DR}(\mathcal{M})
are independent of local embeddings and are
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules. Furthermore, it can also be extended to complexes
M^{\bullet}\in D^{b}\mathrm{MHM}(X)
by applying it to each cohomology module
H^{i}M^{\bullet}\in\mathrm{MHM}(X)
,
i\in\mathbb{Z}
, and taking the alternating sum.

Definition 3.1
.

[
9
,
39
]

The Hodge-theoretic
Hirzebruch class transformation
is defined by the composition

\displaystyle MHT_{y\ast}:K_{0}(\mathrm{MHM}(X))

\displaystyle\to H_{*}(X)\otimes\mathbf{Q}[y^{\pm 1},(1+y)^{-1}]

\displaystyle[M^{\bullet}]

\displaystyle\mapsto\mathrm{td}_{(1+y)\ast}\circ\mathrm{DR}_{y}[M^{\bullet}],

where
\mathrm{td}_{(1+y)\ast}

is the scalar extension of the
Baum–Fulton–MacPherson Todd class transformation
\mathrm{td}_{*}:K_{0}(X)\to H_{\ast}(X)\otimes\mathbf{Q}

[
2
]
, together with the multiplication by
(1+y)^{-k}
on
H_{k}(X)
.

Remark 3.2
.

In fact, by
[
39
, Proposition 5.21]
, one has that

MHT_{y\ast}([M^{\bullet}])\in H_{*}(X)\otimes\mathbf{Q}[y^{\pm 1}],

that is, no negative powers of
(1+y)
appear in the above definition.

Remark 3.3
.

Over a point space, the transformation
MHT_{y\ast}
coincides with the Hodge polynomial ring homomorphism
\chi_{y}:K_{0}(\mathrm{mHs}^{p})\to\mathbf{Z}[y^{\pm 1}]
defined on the Grothendieck group of (graded) polarizable mixed Hodge structures by

\chi_{y}([H]):=\sum_{p}\dim\mathrm{Gr}^{p}_{F}(H\otimes\mathbf{C})\cdot(-y)^{p},

for
F
the (decreasing) Hodge filtration of
H\in\mathrm{mHs}^{p}
. Here we use the well-known equivalence of categories
\mathrm{MHM}(pt)\simeq\mathrm{mHs}^{p}
.

The transformations
T_{y*}
and
MHT_{y*}
are related to each other via the natural group homomorphism

\chi_{Hdg}:K_{0}(var/X)\to K_{0}(\mathrm{MHM}(X)),\ \ [f:Y\to X]\mapsto[f_{!}\mathbf{Q}^{H}_{Y}],

with

\mathbf{Q}^{H}_{Y}
denoting the constant mixed Hodge complex on
Y
. Then, as explained in
[
9
, Section 4]
, one has that

(13)

T_{y*}=MHT_{y*}\circ\chi_{Hdg}.

Note that
\chi_{Hdg}([id_{X}:X\to X])=[\mathbf{Q}^{H}_{X}].

Definition 3.4
.

The
motivic Hirzebruch class

{T_{y}}_{*}(X)
of a complex algebraic
variety
X
is defined by

(14)

T_{y*}(X):=T_{y*}([id_{X}])=MHT_{y*}([\mathbf{Q}_{X}^{H}])\in H_{*}(X)\otimes\mathbf{Q}[y].

Similarly, for a pure-dimensional complex algebraic variety
X
, we define

(15)

IT_{y*}(X):=MHT_{y*}([IC^{\prime H}_{X}])\in H_{*}(X)\otimes\mathbf{Q}[y^{\pm 1}],

with
IC^{\prime H}_{X}:=IC_{X}^{H}[-\dim(X)]
. This is usually referred to the as the
intersection cohomology Hirzebruch class
of
X
, though it is still valued in homology.

For a compact complex algebraic variety
X
with
a_{X}:X\to pt
the
constant map to a point, by using Remark
3.3
it follows that the pushforward
a_{X*}T_{y*}(X)
is the
Hodge polynomial

(16)

\chi_{y}(X):=\chi_{y}([H^{*}(X;\mathbf{Q})])=\sum_{i,p}(-1)^{i}\dim_{\mathbf{C}}\mathrm{Gr}^{p}_{F}H^{i}(X;\mathbf{C})\cdot(-y)^{p},

and similarly, the pushforward
a_{X*}IT_{y*}(X)
is the
intersection cohomology Hodge polynomial

(17)

I\chi_{y}(X):=\chi_{y}([IH^{*}(X;\mathbf{Q})])=\sum_{i,p}(-1)^{i}\dim_{\mathbf{C}}\mathrm{Gr}^{p}_{F}IH^{i}(X;\mathbf{C})\cdot(-y)^{p}

=\sum_{p,q}(-1)^{q}Ih^{p,q}(X)\cdot y^{p},

where the last equality uses the purity of the Hodge structure on the intersection cohomology groups

IH^{*}(X;\mathbf{Q})=H^{*}(X;IC^{\prime H}_{X}),

and

Ih^{p,q}(X):=\dim_{\mathbf{C}}\mathrm{Gr}^{p}_{F}IH^{p+q}(X;\mathbf{C})

are the corresponding intersection cohomology Hodge numbers. Both
\chi_{y}(X)
and
I\chi_{y}(X)
are natural generalizations to the singular context of the Hirzebruch
\chi_{y}
-genus of (
11
) from the smooth compact case.

A Hodge index theorem for singular varieties has been obtained in
[
27
]
in the projective case, and this was extended to all compact complex algebraic varieties of pure dimension in
[
21
, formula (6)]
. It can be formulated as the equality

(18)

\sigma(X)=I\chi_{1}(X),

where the signature
\sigma(X)
of
X
is the Gorresky-MacPherson signature
[
23
]
defined via the Poincaré duality pairing on the middle intersection cohomology group of
X
.

In Section
3.3
below we recall the definition of the homology
L
-classes for singular compact complex pure-dimensional varieties, which at the degree level yields the intersection cohomology signature. Given that
I\chi_{1}(X)
is the degree of the class
IT_{1*}(X)
, it is then natural to expect that the following holds, see
[
9
, Remark 5.4]
.

Conjecture 3.5

(Characteristic class version of Hodge index theorem)
.

Let
X
be a compact pure-dimensional complex algebraic variety. Then

(19)

L_{*}(X)=IT_{1*}(X)\in H_{2*}(X;\mathbf{Q}).

For rational homology manifolds, the above conjecture has been recently proved in
[
21
]
, see also
[
20
]
for the projective case. More generally, as explained in Remark
1.2
, the conjecture holds whenever
[IC_{X}^{\prime H}]\in\im(\chi_{Hdg}).

Remark 3.6
.

By the multiplicativity of both sides of the equality (
19
) (cf.
[
45
, Proposition 5.16]
for
L
-classes of Witt spaces and, resp.,
[
39
, Section 5]
for
IT_{1*}
), if Conjecture
3.5
holds for both
X
and
Y
, then it holds for their product
X\times Y
.

Remark 3.7
.

Note that for
X
a compact variety of pure dimension
d_{X}
,
L_{k}(X)=0\in H_{2k}(X;\mathbf{Q})
by definition if
d_{X}+k
is odd (as explained in Section
3.3
below). Similarly,
IT_{1,k}(X)=0\in H^{BM}_{2k}(X;\mathbf{Q})
if
d_{X}+k
is odd, by a duality argument (see
[
31
, Example 2.11]
). Here the compactness is only needed in the definition of
L
-classes, but not for the last statement about
IT_{1*}(X)
.

3.2.
Cobordism groups and relation to mixed Hodge modules

In this section we recall two notions of a cobordism group of self-dual constructible complexes, one defined in terms of Verdier duality
[
17
,
3
,
46
,
9
]
appearing in the topological definition of
L
-classes, and the other one, coming up in relation to mixed Hodge modules, defined in terms of non-degenerate pairings, cf.
[
21
,
22
]
.

Let
X
be a complex algebraic variety and let
\mathbf{K}
be a subfield of
\mathbf{R}
. A self-dual complex
(\mathcal{F},\alpha)
on
X
consists of a bounded
\mathbf{K}
-complex
\mathcal{F}\in D^{b}_{c}(X;\mathbf{K})
with constructible cohomology, which is endowed with a
self-duality isomorphism

\alpha:\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{D}_{X}(\mathcal{F})=Rhom(\mathcal{F},\mathbf{D}_{X}),

where
\mathcal{D}_{X}(-)
is the Verdier duality functor on
X
, and
\mathbf{D}_{X}=a_{X}^{!}\mathbf{K}_{pt}
is the
\mathbf{K}
-dualizing complex on
X
, with
a_{X}:X\to pt
the constant map.
In the following we implicitly assume that
\mathbf{D}_{X}
is represented by a bounded below complex of injective sheaves.

A pairing
S:\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{F}\to\mathbf{D}_{X}
corresponds by the adjunction formula

(20)

\theta:Hom_{D^{b}_{c}(X;\mathbf{K})}(\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{G},\mathbf{D}_{X})=Hom_{D^{b}_{c}(X;\mathbf{K})}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{D}_{X}(\mathcal{G}))

to a morphism
\alpha_{S}:\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{D}_{X}(\mathcal{F})
.
Such a pairing
S
is called
perfect
if
\alpha_{S}
is an isomorphism. The pairing
S
is called
symmetric
(or
skew-symmetric
) if
S\circ\iota=S
(or
-S
), where

\iota:\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{G}\otimes\mathcal{F}

is the standard involution on the tensor product of complexes (e.g., see
[
27
, 1.3.4]
). In terms of self-dual complexes, the notion of (skew-)symmetry can be defined by using the canonical biduality isomorphism
can:\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{D}_{X}(\mathcal{D}_{X}(\mathcal{F}))
via

\mathcal{D}_{X}(\alpha)\circ can=\pm\alpha,

see, e.g.,
[
9
, Section 4]
or
[
46
]
.
Here, the biduality isomorphism
can
is usually part of the data needed to define a cobordism group of self-dual objects, e.g., switching from
can
to
-can
switches the role of (skew-)symmetric objects. But in our context there is indeed a
canonical
choice so that
can
corresponds to the pairing

\mathcal{F}\otimes Rhom(\mathcal{F},\mathbf{D}_{X})\simeq Rhom(\mathcal{F},\mathbf{D}_{X})\otimes\mathcal{F}\to\mathbf{D}_{X}\>,

with the last pairing corresponding via the adjunction formula (
20
) to the identity map of
Rhom(\mathcal{F},\mathbf{D}_{X})
(see, e.g.,
[
12
, Section 3.2]
).
If
\alpha:\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{D}_{X}(\mathcal{G})
corresponds to the pairing

\theta(\alpha):\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{G}\to\mathbf{D}_{X}
, then
\theta(\mathcal{D}_{X}(\alpha)\circ can)
corresponds to the pairing

\theta(\alpha)\circ\iota:\quad\mathcal{G}\otimes\mathcal{F}\simeq\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{G}\to\mathbf{D}_{X}\>.

For instance, if
X
is smooth and pure dimensional with
d_{X}=\dim(X)
, then
\mathbf{D}_{X}\cong\mathbf{K}_{X}[2d_{X}]
, so the canonical self-pairing
S_{can}
on
\mathbf{K}_{X}[d_{X}]
is
(-1)^{d_{X}}
-symmetric (i.e., symmetric if
d_{X}
is even, and skew-symmetric if
d_{X}
is odd).
This corresponds to the canonical self-duality isomorphism
\alpha_{can}
of
\mathbf{K}_{X}[d_{X}]
.

The (skew-)symmetric
cobordism group

\Omega_{\mathbf{K}\pm}(X):=\Omega_{\pm}(D^{b}_{c}(X;\mathbf{K}),\mathbf{D}_{X},can)
is defined as the quotient of the monoid of isomorphism classes of (skew-)symmetric self-dual complexes, by a certain cobordism relation defined in terms of (
\pm
)self-dual octahedral diagrams as in
[
46
,
9
]
. Set

\Omega_{\mathbf{K}}(X):=\Omega_{\mathbf{K}+}(X)\oplus\Omega_{\mathbf{K}-}(X).

Note that these are functorial for proper morphisms, since
Rf_{*}
commutes with Verdier duality for a proper morphism
f
.

Remark 3.8
.

Let us emphasize that in the description of elements in the cobordism groups (defined either by duality or pairings), as well as in the definition of
L
-classes via signatures, sign conventions are important. E.g., a sign isomorphism can introduce negative elements or
L
-classes. Here we have to specify which of the two possible sign conventions we are using for

(i)

the tensor product, as used in the context of pairings;

(ii)

the identification in (
20
);

(iii)

the duality functor
\mathcal{D}_{X}(-)=Rhom(-,\mathbf{D}_{X})
.

Since we want to compare with Saito’s theory of algebraic mixed Hodge modules and the construction of the transformation
\mathrm{Pol}^{pair}
from
[
21
]
, we follow here exactly the sign conventions from
[
36
,
37
]
(compare also with
[
12
, Appendix A]
), namely:

(i)

For the tensor product, we use the convention

R[n]\otimes_{R}(-)=(-)[n]

without any signs, for a coefficient ring like
R=\mathbf{Z},\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{R}
. So moving out a shift in the first variable of the tensor product does not produce a sign, whereas a shift in the second factor could introduce a sign dictated by the symmetry isomorphism
\iota
given by multiplication by
(-1)^{ij}
on
\mathcal{F}^{i}\otimes\mathcal{G}^{j}
. More precisely,

\mathcal{F}^{i}\otimes_{R}(R[n])\otimes_{R}\mathcal{G}^{j})=(\mathcal{F}^{i}\otimes_{R}R[n])\otimes_{R}\mathcal{G}^{j}\simeq(R[n]\otimes_{R}\mathcal{F}^{i})\otimes_{R}\mathcal{G}^{j}=(\mathcal{F}^{i}\otimes_{R}\mathcal{G}^{j})[n],

where the associativity is an equality without signs, and the middle symmetry isomorphism produces the sign
(-1)^{in}
.

(ii)

the identification in (
20
) is an equality without any signs, as in
[
37
, (1.8.1)]
and
[
21
]
.

(iii)

For
Rhom
we choose the convention from
[
37
, (1.8.2)]
, so that moving out a shift in the second variable does not produce any sign (see
[
37
, (1.8.4)]
), whereas the sign
(-1)^{in}
appears in the isomorphism

(21)

th_{n}:\>hom(\mathcal{F}^{i}[-n],\mathcal{G}^{i+j})\simeq hom(\mathcal{F}^{i},\mathcal{G}^{i+j})[n]\>,

see
[
37
, (1.8.3)]
. This means that the Verdier duality functor
\mathcal{D}_{X}
is not
strict
in the sense of
[
12
, Remark 2.1.2]
.

Note that an even shift does not involve any signs. Also, these sign conventions fit with those from
[
3
]
. The composition of the sign-isomorphism
th_{n}
for different shifts
[n]
only commutes up to the following sign:

(22)

\begin{CD}hom(\mathcal{F}^{i}[-(n+m)],\mathcal{G}^{i+j})@>{th_{n}}>{\sim}>hom(\mathcal{F}^{i}[-m],\mathcal{G}^{i+j})[n]\\
@V{th_{n+m}}V{\wr}V@V{\wr}V{th_{m}}V\\
hom(\mathcal{F}^{i},\mathcal{G}^{i+j})[n+m]@>{(-1)^{nm}\cdot id}>{\sim}>hom(\mathcal{F}^{i},\mathcal{G}^{i+j})[n+m]\>,\end{CD}

i.e., only for
n
and
m
both odd an additional minus sign shows up. For example the inverse of

th_{n}
is
(-1)^{n}\cdot th_{-n}
.

Let us mention here that a self-dual complex
(\mathcal{F},\alpha)
is cobordant to its zeroth perverse cohomology
{{}^{p}\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\mathcal{F},\alpha)
(cf.
[
46
, Example 6.6]
), hence they give the same element

(23)

[(\mathcal{F},\alpha)]=[{{}^{p}\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\mathcal{F},\alpha)]\in\Omega_{\mathbf{K}}(X).

Here we use the middle perversity, which is self-dual with respect to Verdier duality, so that we get an induced duality isomorphism:

{{}^{p}\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\alpha):{{}^{p}\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\mathcal{F})\to{{}^{p}\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\mathcal{D}_{X}(\mathcal{F}))=\mathcal{D}_{X}({{}^{p}\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\mathcal{F})).

Self-duality isomorphisms and cobordism relations are stable by the derived pushforward
Rf_{*}
under a proper morphism of complex varieties
f:X\to Y
, so one gets a pushforward

f_{*}:\Omega_{\mathbf{K}}(X)\to\Omega_{\mathbf{K}}(Y)

compatible with the above decomposition.

Following
[
9
, Section 4]
, one has a natural transformation

\mathrm{sd}_{\mathbf{K}}:K_{0}(var/X)\to\Omega_{\mathbf{K}}(X),

which on a generator
[f:Z\to X]
with
Z
smooth pure dimensional and proper
(or projective) over
X
is defined by
\mathrm{sd}_{\mathbf{K}}([f])=f_{*}[(\mathbf{K}_{Z}[d_{Z}],\alpha_{can})]
, with
d_{Z}=\dim(Z)
. Note that the construction of
\mathrm{sd}_{\mathbf{K}}
in
[
9
]
uses Bittner’s presentation
[
8
]
of
K_{0}(var/X)
in terms of a blow-up relation coming from the weak factorization theorem.
It is important to also note that in general
\mathrm{sd}_{\mathbf{K}}([id_{X}])\neq[IC_{X}]
.

Remark 3.9
.

The cobordism class
[(\mathbf{K}_{Z}[d_{Z}],\alpha_{can})]
agrees with the corresponding class originally introduced in
[
9
, Theorem 4.2]

via the multiplication map
{\rm m}:\mathbf{K}_{Z}\otimes\mathbf{K}_{Z}\to\mathbf{K}_{Z}
. Indeed, for
Z
smooth of pure dimension
d_{Z}
,
[
9
, Example 4.1]

starts more generally with a (non-degenerate) pairing

S:\mathbf{L}\otimes\mathbf{L}\to\mathbf{K}_{Z}

of a local system
\mathbf{L}
on
Z
in degree zero, corresponding via the adjunction (
20
) to an isomorphism

\alpha:\mathbf{L}\to\mathbf{L}^{\vee}=hom(\mathbf{L},\mathbf{K}_{Z})=Rhom(\mathbf{L},\mathbf{K}_{Z})\>,

which is then shifted by
[m+d_{Z}]
(for
m\in\mathbf{Z}
fixed,
with
m=0
corresponding to
[
9
, Example 4.1]
):

(24)

\begin{split}\alpha[m+d_{Z}]:\mathbf{L}[m+d_{Z}]&\to Rhom(\mathbf{L},\mathbf{K}_{Z})[-(m+d_{Z})][2(m+d_{Z})]\\
&\simeq Rhom(\mathbf{L}[m+d_{Z}],\mathbf{K}_{Z}[2(m+d_{Z})])\\
&=\mathcal{D}_{X}(\mathbf{L}[m+d_{Z}])[2m]\end{split}

(this doesn’t introduce any signs since
\mathbf{L}
is in degree zero). Then
\alpha
can be recovered via the induced map

\mathbf{L}=\mathcal{H}^{-(m+d_{Z})}(\mathbf{L}[m+d_{Z}])\to\mathcal{H}^{-(m+d_{Z})}(\mathcal{D}_{X}(\mathbf{L}[m+d_{Z}])[2m])=\mathbf{L}^{\vee}\>.

Similarly
\alpha[d_{Z}]
corresponds (for
m=0
) via (
20
) to a shifted pairing

(25)

S^{\prime}:\mathbf{L}[d_{Z}]\otimes\mathbf{L}[d_{Z}]\to\mathbf{D}_{Z}=\mathbf{K}[2d_{Z}]\>,

giving back
S
via

\mathbf{L}\otimes\mathbf{L}=\mathcal{H}^{-2d_{Z}}(\mathbf{L}[d_{Z}]\otimes\mathbf{L}[d_{Z}])\to\mathcal{H}^{-2d_{Z}}(\mathbf{D}_{Z})=\mathbf{K}_{Z}\>.

The following result was proved in
[

, Proposition 1, Remark 1.2c]

Proposition 3.10
.

[
21
]
Let
M
be a pure (

-)Hodge module of weight

.

The isomorphism

[

\mathbf{Q}

[

3.3.
L-classes

Stratified bordism invariant

-classes

for compact oriented stratified pseudo-manifolds

with only even (co)dimension strata have been introduced in
[

by Goresky and MacPherson, relying on a classical Pontrjagin construction. More generally, Cappell-Shaneson
[

(see also
[
3
, Section 8.2.3]

L
-class

for elements in a suitable cobordism group

\Omega^{CS}_{\mathcal{S},\mathbf{K}}(X,\mathcal{D}_{X}[2\dim(X)]):=\Omega(D^{b}_{\mathcal{S}-c}(X;\mathbf{K}),\mathcal{D}_{X}[2\dim(X)],can)

of
\mathbf{K}
-constructible complexes

which are self-dual with respect to a shifted Verdier duality (and working with a fixed Whitney stratification
\mathcal{S}
with only even dimensional strata).
But as we now explain, to construct a functorial
L
-class transformation

via the methods of Cappell-Shaneson
[
17
]
it is more natural to work with the (unshifted) Verdier duality, using the
limit with respect to refinements of algebraic Whitney stratifications
\mathcal{S}
of

,
so that one gets an
L
-class transformation:

L_{*}:\Omega_{\mathbf{K}}(X):=\Omega_{\mathbf{K}}(X,\mathcal{D}_{X})\simeq\lim_{\mathcal{S}}\>\Omega^{CS}_{\mathcal{S},\mathbf{K}}(X,\mathcal{D}_{X})\to H_{*}(X)\otimes\mathbf{Q}=H_{2*}(X;\mathbf{Q}),

which will be functorial for pushforwards under morphisms of compact varieties. In this setting, the Goresky-MacPherson
L
-class will be given by

L_{*}(X)=L_{*}([IC_{X},\alpha_{can}]).

(Here we can work as before with a subfield
\mathbf{K}
of
\mathbf{R}
, but the
L
-class transformation only depends on the associated complexes
\mathcal{F}_{\mathbf{R}}
with real coefficients, as used before.)

Remark 3.19
.

From the viewpoint of algebraic geometry, and in particular for the use of mixed Hodge modules, it is more natural to work with the cobordism group
\Omega_{\mathbf{K}}(X):=\Omega_{\mathbf{K}}(X,\mathcal{D}_{X})
defined via Verdier duality without a shift. On the other hand, in topology, especially in relation to intersection cohomology, most often different conventions are used. This is in particular the case for the
L
-classes
L_{*}^{CS}
of Cappell-Shaneson
[
17
]
defined on
\Omega^{CS}_{\mathcal{S},\mathbf{K}}(X,\mathcal{D}_{X}[2\dim(X)])
and taking
\dim(X)
into account.
While Cappell-Shaneson do not specify in
[
17
]
the sign convention for the
Rhom
functor, the
L
-class transformation evaluated on (shifted) intersection complexes should be the Goresky-MacPherson
L
-class. As we will see later, this forces the choice of the sign convention as in Remark
3.8
.

Let us now explain the construction of this functorial
L
-class, directly in terms of our cobordism group
\Omega_{\mathbf{K}}(X)=\Omega_{\mathbf{K}}(X,\mathcal{D}_{X})
, i.e., we work with the perverse conventions. Let
X
be a compact complex algebraic variety of pure dimension

, together with a self-dual complex
(\mathcal{F},\alpha)
, with

and

the corresponding self-duality isomorphism. Fix a complex algebraic Whitney stratification

adapted to

(i.e., the cohomology sheaves of

are locally constant on the strata of the stratification

), so that

belongs to the corresponding triangulated

of
\mathcal{S}
-constructible complexes.
This subcategory is also stable under Verdier duality and fits with the perverse t-structure.

Remark 3.20
.

To make better visible a comparison between the different
conventions mentioned above, we work from the beginning with the cobordism group

Note that the shift functor

This follows then from Example

∎

Remark 3.27

If in the above construction of the

4.
Some non-rational homology cases of the conjecture

Let
X
be a compact complex algebraic variety of pure dimension
d_{X}
, and let

IC_{X}^{H}
be its intersection cohomology Hodge module, with

IC^{\prime H}_{X}:=IC_{X}^{H}[-d_{X}].

4.1.
Property (H) and first examples

Since Conjecture
3.5
can be formulated by asking that the transformations
L_{*}\circ\mathrm{Pol}
and
MHT_{1*}
give the same value on
[IC^{\prime H}_{X}]
, we introduce the following:

Definition 4.1
.

Say that
[M]\in K_{0}(\mathrm{MHM}(X))
satisfies
property (H)
if

(H)

\displaystyle MHT_{1*}([M])=(L_{*}\circ\mathrm{Pol})([M])\>.

So Conjecture
3.5
is equivalent to asking that
[IC^{\prime H}_{X}]
satisfies property (H). In Theorem
4.20
below we will see that the property (H) holds for any polarizable variation of pure Hodge structures on a smooth variety
X
(i.e., corresponding to a shifted pure smooth Hodge module).

As noted in Remark
1.2
, the commutativity of diagrams (
27
) and (
33
) yields the following.

Corollary 4.2
.

If
[M]\in\im(\chi_{Hdg})\subset K_{0}(\mathrm{MHM}(X))
, then
[M]
satisfies property (H).
In particular, the constant Hodge complex
[\mathbf{Q}^{H}_{X}]=\chi_{Hdg}([id_{X}])
satisfies property (H).

We next recall the following well-known fact (see
[
39
, Section 2.3]
):

Lemma 4.3

(
[
39
]
)
.

If
(V,S)
is a pure polarized Hodge structure of weight
w
, with polarization
S
, one has

\chi_{1}(V)=\begin{cases}0&{\rm for}\ w\ {\rm odd},\\
\sigma(V)&{\rm for}\ w\ {\rm even},\end{cases}

where
\sigma(V)
is the signature of the induced symmetric bilinear form
(-1)^{w/2}\cdot S
on
V
. As usual,
\chi_{y}(V)
is defined here in terms of the Hodge filtration on the complexification
V_{\mathbf{C}}
of
V
, by

\chi_{y}(V):=\sum_{p}\dim_{\mathbf{C}}(Gr^{p}_{F}(V_{\mathbf{C}}))\cdot(-y)^{p}.

Remark 4.4
.

Note that this result doesn’t depend on which of the two possible definitions of a polarization one is using, because they only differ by the factor
(-1)^{w}
.

Corollary 4.5
.

If
X=pt
is a point, then any
[V]\in K_{0}(\mathrm{MHM}(pt))
satisfies property (H).

Proof.

It suffices to check the claim for
(V,S)
a pure polarized Hodge structure of weight
w
.
By definition,
MHT_{1*}([V])=\chi_{1}(V)
.

If
w
is even, we get by the definition of
\mathrm{Pol}
and Lemma
4.3
that

\displaystyle L_{*}\circ\mathrm{Pol}([V])=L_{0}((-1)^{w(w+1)/2}\alpha_{\mathbf{R}})=L_{0}((-1)^{w/2}\alpha_{\mathbf{R}})=\sigma((-1)^{w/2}S_{\mathbf{R}})=\chi_{1}(V).

If
w
is odd, then
S_{\mathbf{R}}
is skew-symmetric, hence its signature is trivial, and we also have
\chi_{1}(V)=0
by Lemma
4.3
, or more directly by a corresponding duality argument (see
[
39
, Section 2.3]
):
\chi_{1}(V)=\chi_{1}(V^{\vee})=(-1)^{w}\cdot\chi_{1}(V)
.
∎

We also have the following.

Proposition 4.6
.

If
f:X\to Y
is a morphism between compact complex algebraic varieties and
[M]\in K_{0}(\mathrm{MHM}(X))
satisfies property (H), then
f_{*}[M]=f_{!}[M]\in K_{0}(\mathrm{MHM}(Y))
also satisfies property (H).

Proof.

This is an easy consequence of the fact that the transformations
\mathrm{Pol}
,
MHT_{1*}
and
L_{*}
all commute with pushforwards for morphisms of compact varieties.
∎

We next recall that
K_{0}(\mathrm{MHM}(X))
is a module over
K_{0}(\mathrm{MHM}(pt))=K_{0}(\mathrm{mHs}^{p})
, with the product induced from the external product via the identification
X\simeq X\times pt
. Moreover, as shown in
[
9
]
, the class transformation
MHT_{y*}
is compatible with this module structure. We also need the following lemmas:

Lemma 4.7

(Module property for signature and
L
-class)
.

Let
X
be a compact complex algebraic variety. Let
(\mathcal{F},\alpha)
be a self-dual complex of real vector spaces on
X
, and
(V,\beta)
a finite dimensional real self-dual vector space (viewed on a point space). Then

(34)

L_{*}(\mathcal{F}\boxtimes V,\alpha\boxtimes\beta)=\sigma(V,\beta)\cdot L_{*}(\mathcal{F},\alpha).

Proof.

For the proof let more generally
(\mathcal{F},\alpha)
be a complex of real vector spaces on
X
,
which is self-dual with respect to
\mathcal{D}_{X}[2m]
(for
m\in\mathbf{Z}
).
Using the Künneth formula, together with the fact the Verdier duality commutes with
\boxtimes
(see e.g.,
[
45
, Section 3.7]
),
we get that the duality isomorphism induced from
\alpha\boxtimes\beta
on the vector space

H^{-m}(\mathcal{F}\boxtimes V)\simeq H^{-m}(\mathcal{F})\otimes V

agrees with the tensor product of the ones induced from
\alpha
on
H^{-m}(\mathcal{F})
and, resp.,
\beta
.
Therefore, the multiplicativity of the signature implies for the pushforward under the constant map
a:X\to pt
that

\sigma(a_{*}[\mathcal{F}\boxtimes V,\alpha\boxtimes\beta])=\sigma(a_{*}[\mathcal{F},\alpha])\cdot\sigma(V,\beta).

Applying this property to the construction of
L
-classes, yields the desired assertion (see also

[
3
, Proposition 8.2.20]
).
∎

Lemma 4.8
.

The composition
L_{*}\circ\mathrm{Pol}
is compatible with the
K_{0}(\mathrm{MHM}(pt))
-module structure on
K_{0}(\mathrm{MHM}(X))
, i.e., for
[a]\in K_{0}(\mathrm{MHM}(pt))
and
[M]\in K_{0}(\mathrm{MHM}(X))
one has:

(L_{*}\circ\mathrm{Pol})([M]\cdot[a])=(L_{*}\circ\mathrm{Pol})([M])\boxtimes(L_{*}\circ\mathrm{Pol})([a]).

Proof.

For
L
-classes, this property follows from Lemma
4.7
.

For
\mathrm{Pol}
, it suffices to show the assertion for pure objects, i.e., assume
M_{1}\in\mathrm{MH}(X,w_{1})^{(p)}
is a pure polarizable Hodge module of weight
w_{1}
on
X
, and
M_{2}\in\mathrm{MH}(pt,w_{2})^{(p)}
is a pure polarizable Hodge module of weight
w_{2}
on
pt
. Then

M_{1}\boxtimes M_{2}\in\mathrm{MH}(X,w_{1}+w_{2})^{(p)},

and if
S_{i}
are the corresponding polarizations, then
S_{1}\boxtimes S_{2}
gives a polarization of
M_{1}\boxtimes M_{2}
. Let
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}
be the corresponding self-duality isomorphisms.
By the definition of Pol,

\mathrm{Pol}(M_{1})=[(-1)^{\frac{w_{1}(w_{1}+1)}{2}}\alpha_{1}],\ \ \mathrm{Pol}(M_{2})=[(-1)^{\frac{w_{2}(w_{2}+1)}{2}}\alpha_{2}]

\mathrm{Pol}(M_{1}\boxtimes M_{2})=[(-1)^{\frac{(w_{1}+w_{2})(w_{1}+w_{2}+1)}{2}}\alpha_{1}\boxtimes\alpha_{2}].

So the difference between
\mathrm{Pol}(M_{1}\boxtimes M_{2})
and
\mathrm{Pol}(M_{1})\boxtimes\mathrm{Pol}(M_{2})
is given by the sign
(-1)^{w_{1}w_{2}}
. If
w_{2}
is even, then no sign appears. Assume now that
w_{2}
is odd. Then
L_{*}(\alpha_{2})=\sigma(S_{2})=0
as in Lemma
4.3
, hence by the module property (
34
) of
L_{*}
(see also
[
3
, Proposition 8.2.20]
), we get

L_{*}([\alpha_{1}\boxtimes\alpha_{2}])=L_{*}([\alpha_{1}])\boxtimes L_{*}([\alpha_{2}])=0,

and therefore

(L_{*}\circ\mathrm{Pol})(M_{1}\boxtimes M_{2})=(-1)^{w_{1}}(L_{*}\circ\mathrm{Pol})(M_{1})\boxtimes(L_{*}\circ\mathrm{Pol})(M_{2})=0\>.

∎

Remark 4.9
.

The proof of the module property from Lemma
4.7
also applies to the
L
-class transformation
L_{*}^{pair}
, so that Lemma
4.8
and its proof also apply to
L_{*}^{pair}
.

Proposition 4.10
.

Let

[M]=\sum_{i}[M_{i}]\cdot[a_{i}]\in K_{0}(\mathrm{MHM}(X\times pt))\simeq K_{0}(\mathrm{MHM}(X)),

with
[a_{i}]\in K_{0}(\mathrm{mHs}^{p})
, so that every
[M_{i}]\in K_{0}(\mathrm{MHM}(X))
satisfies property (H). Then
[M]
satisfies property (H).

Proof.

Since the transformations

and

respect the module structure, we get that

\begin{split}MHT_{1*}([M_{i}]\cdot[a_{i}])&=MHT_{1*}([M_{i}])\boxtimes MHT_{1}([a_{i}])\\
&=(L_{*}\circ\mathrm{Pol})([M_{i}])\boxtimes(L_{*}\circ\mathrm{Pol})([a_{i}])\\
&=(L_{*}\circ\mathrm{Pol})([M_{i}]\cdot[a_{i}])\in H_{*}(X\times pt)\otimes\mathbf{Q}\cong H_{*}(X)\otimes\mathbf{Q},\end{split}

where the second equality uses Corollary

and the fact that

satisfies property (H). The claim follows.
∎

Example 4.13

(
\mathbf{Q}

.

Let

be as above a compact complex algebraic variety of pure dimension, and denote by

the non-rational homology locus of

(i.e., the smallest closed subset of

such that

Consider the natural comparison morphism

\mathbf{Q}^{H}_{X}\longrightarrow IC^{\prime H}_{X}

and let

denote its cone in

(up to isomorphism). Note that the support of

of

).

4.2.
Coincidence in the top non-rational homology locus degree

Following up on Example
4.13
, let
\delta_{X}=\dim(\Sigma_{X})
be the dimension of the non-rational homology locus
\Sigma_{X}
of
X
. Recall that the cone
M_{X}
of the comparison morphism (
35
) has support
\Sigma_{X}
, so in view of (
29
) one has that

IT_{1,k}(X)=T_{1,k}(X)=L_{k}(X)\in H_{k}(X)\otimes\mathbf{Q}\ \ {\rm if}\ \ k>\delta_{X}.

As

[IC^{\prime H}_{X}]=[\mathbf{Q}_{X}^{H}]+[M_{X}]\in K_{0}(\mathrm{MHM}(X)),

and since
[\mathbf{Q}^{H}_{X}]
satisfies property (H), one gets that

\displaystyle L_{*}(X)-IT_{1*}(X)=(L_{*}\circ\mathrm{Pol})([M_{X}])-MHT_{1*}([M_{X}]).

Hence Conjecture
3.5
can be further reduced to showing that the cone
[M_{X}]
satisfies property (H). As seen in Example
4.13
, this is the case when
\delta_{X}=0
, and it was also recently proved in
[
22
, Proposition 3.3]
for
\delta_{X}\leq 1
. Here we generalize the latter statement as follows:

Proposition 4.14
.

In the above notations, we have the equality

IT_{1,\delta_{X}}(X)=L_{\delta_{X}}(X)\in H_{\delta_{X}}(X)\otimes\mathbf{Q}=H_{2\delta_{X}}(X;\mathbf{Q})\>.

This is a consequence of the following result.

Theorem 4.15
.

Let
M
be a pure (
\mathbf{Q}
-)Hodge module on a compact, pure-dimensional complex algebraic variety
\Sigma
of dimension
\delta
. Then the degree
\delta
-parts of the classes

(L_{*}\circ\mathrm{Pol})([M])
and
MHT_{1*}([M])
coincide in
H_{\delta}(\Sigma)\otimes\mathbf{Q}
.

Before proving the theorem, we need the following.

Lemma 4.16
.

If
(\mathbf{V},S)
is a pure polarizable variation of
\mathbf{Q}
-Hodge structures on a connected smooth complex algebraic variety
\Sigma
of pure dimension
\delta
, then the degree
\delta
part
MHT_{1,\delta}([\mathbf{V}^{H}])
of
MHT_{1*}([\mathbf{V}^{H}])
is
\chi_{1}(V)\cdot[\Sigma]
, where
\mathbf{V}^{H}
is the (shifted) Hodge module on
\Sigma
associated to
\mathbf{V}
, and
V
is the stalk of the variation
\mathbf{V}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{V}:=\mathbf{V}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\Sigma}
be the flat bundle with connection, whose sheaf of horizontal sections is
\mathbf{V}\otimes\mathbf{C}
. The bundle
\mathcal{V}
comes equipped with its Hodge (decreasing) filtration by holomorphic sub-bundles
\mathcal{F}^{p}
(which satisfy Griffiths’ transversality). Let

(36)

\chi_{y}(\mathcal{V}):=\sum_{p}[Gr^{p}_{\mathcal{F}}\mathcal{V}]\cdot(-y)^{p}\in K^{0}(\Sigma)[y^{\pm 1}]

be the
K
-theory
\chi_{y}
-genus of
\mathcal{V}
, with
K^{0}(\Sigma)
denoting the Grothendieck group of algebraic vector bundles on
\Sigma
.
The twisted Chern character
ch^{*}_{(1+y)}
is defined as

ch^{*}_{(1+y)}(E):=\sum_{j=1}^{\rk(E)}e^{\beta_{j}(1+y)},

for
\{\beta_{j}\}
the Chern roots of the bundle
E
. In particular, in cohomological degree
2k
one has the identity:

(37)

ch^{*}_{(1+y)}(E)_{2k}=(1+y)^{k}\cdot ch^{*}(E)_{2k}.

With the above notation, the following Atiyah-Meyer type formula
was obtained in
[
13
, Theorem 11]
(see also
[
29
, Theorem 4.1]
for the normalized version discussed here):

(38)

MHT_{y*}([\mathbf{V}^{H}])=\left(ch^{*}_{(1+y)}(\chi_{y}(\mathcal{V}))\cup T_{y}^{*}(\Sigma)\right)\cap[\Sigma].

By setting
y=1
in (
38
), we see that in order to compute the degree
\delta
part

MHT_{1,\delta}([\mathbf{V}^{H}])\in H_{\delta}(\Sigma)\otimes\mathbf{Q}=H_{2\delta}(\Sigma;\mathbf{Q})\>,

we need to compute the cohomological degree
0
part

ch^{0}_{(2)}(\chi_{1}(\mathcal{V}))\cup T_{1}^{0}(\Sigma)\in H^{0}(\Sigma;\mathbf{Q})\>,

that is, the product of the cohomological degree
0
parts of each of the classes
ch^{*}_{(2)}(\chi_{1}(\mathcal{V}))
and
T_{1}^{*}(\Sigma)
.
First, for a complex vector bundle
E
, we have

ch^{0}_{(2)}(E)=ch^{0}(E)=\rk(E).

Since the rank of
E
is just the complex dimension of its fiber, and since the fiber of

Gr^{p}_{\mathcal{F}}\mathcal{V}
is
Gr^{p}_{F}V_{\mathbf{C}}
, with
V_{\mathbf{C}}
the complexification of the stalk
V
of
\mathbf{V}
, we then see that

ch^{0}_{(2)}(\chi_{1}(\mathcal{V}))=\chi_{1}(V).

Moreover, since the power series defining the cohomology class
T_{1}^{*}(\Sigma)
is normalized, the cohomological degree
0
part of this class is
1=T_{1}^{0}(\Sigma)
.
The assertion of the lemma follows.
∎

We can now return to Theorem
4.15
.

Proof of Theorem
4.15
.

By the strict support decomposition of pure Hodge modules, it suffices to assume that
M=IC_{\Sigma}^{H}(\mathbf{V})
, for
\mathbf{V}
a polarized variation of Hodge structures of weight
w
on a (connected) Zariski-open subset
U
of the regular part
\Sigma_{reg}
of
\Sigma
, with
\Sigma
irreducible of dimension
\delta
and
\Sigma_{reg}
connected. Then
M
has weight
w+\delta
.
Let
S^{H}
be the polarization on
M
corresponding uniquely to the polarization
S
of
\mathbf{V}
(as discussed in Remark
3.16
for the untwisted case). Then one has as in (
28
) that

S^{H}=(-1)^{\delta(\delta-1)/2}S_{can},

with
S_{can}
the perfect pairing of
IC_{\Sigma}(\mathbf{V})
induced by

S:\mathbf{V}\otimes\mathbf{V}=\mathcal{H}^{-2\delta}(\mathbf{V}[\delta]\otimes\mathbf{V}[\delta])\to\mathbf{Q}_{U}.

Then, using the corresponding self-duality isomorphisms, we get

\displaystyle(L_{*}\circ\mathrm{Pol})([M])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{(w+\delta)(w+\delta+1)/2}\cdot L_{*}([\alpha_{S^{H}}])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{(w+\delta)(w+\delta+1)/2}\cdot(-1)^{\delta(\delta-1)/2}\cdot L_{*}([\alpha_{S_{can}}]).

For
p\in U
and
V=\mathbf{V}_{p}
the stalk of
\mathbf{V}
at
p
, let
S_{p}
be the corresponding polarization on
V
. It then follows from
[
17
, page 543]
(see also
[
3
, page 197]
) that

L_{\delta}([\alpha_{S_{can}}])=\sigma(S_{p})\cdot[\Sigma]\in H_{\delta}(\Sigma)\otimes\mathbf{Q}=H_{2\delta}(\Sigma;\mathbf{Q}).

Note that this identity holds even with our modified

L
-class
L_{*}
(compared to the Cappell-Shaneson
L
-class
L^{CS}_{*}
), since by Example
3.22
an additional minus sign only comes in when already
\sigma(S_{p})=0
.
Altogether, the degree
\delta
-part of the class

(L_{*}\circ\mathrm{Pol})([M])

equals

(39)

(-1)^{(w+\delta)(w+\delta+1)/2}\cdot(-1)^{\delta(\delta-1)/2}\cdot\sigma(S_{p})\cdot[\Sigma].

Let us also note that if
w
is odd, then
S_{p}
is anti-symmetric, so
\sigma(S_{p})=0
.

On the other hand, for calculating the degree
\delta
-part of the class
MHT_{1*}([M])
, using the open restriction isomorphism

H_{\delta}(\Sigma)\otimes\mathbf{Q}\simeq H_{\delta}(U)\otimes\mathbf{Q},

we can assume that
\Sigma=U
is smooth, hence
M=\mathbf{V}^{H}[\delta]
, with
\mathbf{V}^{H}[\delta]
the (smooth) Hodge module on
\Sigma
associated to the pure polarizable variation of
\mathbf{Q}
-Hodge structures
\mathbf{V}
. Then, in view of Lemma
4.16
, the degree
\delta
-part of the class
MHT_{1*}([M])
equals

(-1)^{\delta}\cdot\chi_{1}(V)\cdot[\Sigma]\>,

where
\chi_{1}(V)
is calculated as in Lemma
4.3
, i.e., it equals
0
if
w
is odd, and it is equal to
(-1)^{w/2}\cdot\sigma(S_{p})

if
w
is even. Hence the degree
\delta
-part of the class
MHT_{1*}([M])
equals

(40)

(-1)^{\delta}\cdot(-1)^{w/2}\cdot\sigma(S_{p})\cdot[\Sigma]\>,

which by definition vanishes if
w
is odd.
It is now immediate to check that (the two signs in) (
39
) and (
40
) agree when
w
is even, thus completing the proof.
∎

As a consequence, we get immediately the following result from
[

, Proposition 3.3]
:

Corollary 4.17
.

Conjecture

holds for a compact complex algebraic variety

if the dimension

of the non-rational homology locus of

is one.

Proof.

By Theorem
4.15
, only the equality of both characteristic classes in degree

needs to be checked. Here we can assume
X
is connected with

deg:H_{0}(X;\mathbf{Q})\simeq\mathbf{Q}\>,

so that this claim follows from (

).
∎

Example 4.18
.

Let

be a compact complex algebraic variety of pure dimension, whose singular locus

is one-dimensional (e.g.,

.
In particular, the conjecture holds for

).

4.4.
(Virtually) stratum-wise constant complexes

In order to apply the results of Section
4.1
to specific geometric situations, we first describe the Grothendieck class of a complex
M\in D^{b}\mathrm{MHM}(X)
, compare with
[
28
, Proposition 5.1.2]
:

Proposition 4.22
.

For a complex algebraic variety
X
, fix
M\in D^{b}\mathrm{MHM}(X)
with underlying bounded constructible complex
K:={\it{rat}}(M)\in D^{b}_{c}(X;\mathbf{Q})
.
Let
\mathcal{S}=\{S\}
be a complex algebraic stratification of
X
so
that for any stratum
S\in\mathcal{S}
,
S
is smooth,
{\bar{S}}\setminus S
is a union of
strata, and the sheaves
L_{S,\ell}:=\mathcal{H}^{\ell}K|_{S}
are local systems on
S
for any
\ell\in\mathbf{Z}
.
If
j_{S}:S\hookrightarrow X
is the inclusion map of a stratum
S\in\mathcal{S}
, then:

(48)

[M]=\sum_{S,\ell}\,(-1)^{\ell}\,\big[(j_{S})_{!}L_{S,\ell}^{H}\big]\in K_{0}(\mathrm{MHM}(X)),

where
L_{S,\ell}^{H}=H^{\ell+\dim(S)}(j_{S})^{*}M[-\dim(S)]
is the shifted smooth mixed Hodge module on
S
with
L_{S,\ell}=rat(L_{S,\ell}^{H})
.

If, moreover,
the stratification
\mathcal{S}
of

can be chosen so that the local systems

on
S
are constant for any
S
and
\ell
, then

(49)

[M]=\sum_{S}\,\big[(j_{S})_{!}\mathbf{Q}_{S}^{H}\big]\cdot i_{s}^{*}[M],

where, for
s\in S
fixed,
i_{s}:\{s\}\hookrightarrow X
denotes the inclusion map.

Proof.

Formula (
48
) follows by induction on strata, using the standard attaching triangles, see also
[
28
]
.

If the local system

on

is constant, it follows by rigidity (e.g., see
[
16
, Section 3.1]
) that the (admissible) variation of mixed Hodge structures

on

(corresponding to a smooth mixed Hodge module as in the statement) is the constant variation. This implies that

L^{H}_{S,\ell}\simeq\mathbf{Q}_{S}^{H}\boxtimes L^{H}_{S,s,\ell},

where

is the stalk of

at

, which underlies the mixed Hodge structure

.
Using the

-module structure of

, formula (

(50)

\begin{split}[M]=\sum_{S,\ell}\,(-1)^{\ell}\,\big[(j_{S})_{!}\mathbf{Q}_{S}^{H}\big]\cdot[L^{H}_{S,s,\ell}]=\sum_{S}\,\big[(j_{S})_{!}\mathbf{Q}_{S}^{H}\big]\cdot[M_{s}]=\sum_{S}\,\big[(j_{S})_{!}\mathbf{Q}_{S}^{H}\big]\cdot i_{s}^{*}[M],\end{split}

with

.
∎

Definition 4.23
.

Following
[
31
]
, we call

a

complex of mixed Hodge modules (with respect to an algebraic stratification

as above) if the cohomology sheaves of the underlying constructible complex

are constant along the strata of the stratification. More generally, we call a class
[M]\in K_{0}(\mathrm{MHM}(X))

virtually stratum-wise constant
if equality (

) holds.

Remark 4.24
.

By Proposition
4.22
, a stratum-wise constant complex of mixed Hodge modules

represents a virtually stratum-wise constant class
[M]\in K_{0}(\mathrm{MHM}(X))
.

Example 4.25
.

If, in the notations of Proposition
4.22
, all strata of the stratification

of

are simply connected, then all local systems

are constant, and

is stratum-wise constant, hence

is virtually stratum-wise constant. In particular, this applies to varieties which admit algebraic cellular stratifications (i.e., all strata are isomorphic to complex vector spaces). Such examples include:

•

Schubert varieties (i.e., closures of Schubert cells given by

•

[

).

Example 4.26
.

If, in the notations of Proposition

, all the local systems

on

Remark 4.27
.

Denote by

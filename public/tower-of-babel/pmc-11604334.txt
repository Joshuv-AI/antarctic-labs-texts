The Value of Non-Clinical Applications of Artificial Intelligence in Radiology Should Be Noted
Authors: Hongnan Ye
Published: 2024

I read with interest the paper by Wee et al. [1] entitled “Position Statements of the Emerging Trends Committee of the Asian Oceanian Society of Radiology on the Adoption and Implementation of Artificial Intelligence for Radiology.” Considering the wide range of applications of artificial intelligence (AI) technology in radiology practice, the article by Wee et al. [1] is undoubtedly timely and extremely valuable. However, the article reflects a current bias in the focus of various stakeholders on AI technology in radiology, namely, a focus on the use of AI technology in radiology clinical practice, while ignoring the other values that AI can bring to radiology. Beyond the clinical practice of radiology, AI can play a significant role in at least three areas: professional training of radiology professionals, radiology workflow improvement, and radiology equipment management.

Firstly, only those radiology professionals who are fully competent and qualified are employed by the radiology department of any hospital. They can ensure the provision of high-quality imaging examinations and disease diagnosis services to patients. Continuing medical education (CME) plays an integral role in healthcare education. Its objective is to enhance the existing knowledge and relevant professional skills of medical professionals, while simultaneously acquiring new knowledge. In the majority of European countries, participation in CME is obligatory [2]. Furthermore, the vast majority of European radiologists have expressed their support for mandatory CME. A notable trend among the younger generation of radiologists is their view of CME as an essential component of professional advancement. Additionally, the younger generation of radiologists is more inclined to utilize e-learning for their CME than their older counterparts [3]. This provides a robust basis for implementing AI in continuing education for radiology professionals. The value of AI in the training of radiology professionals has been preliminarily explored and proven. Researchers are investigating the development of innovative, up-to-date methodology for CME for radiologists in the context of AI [4,5].

Secondly, the heavy workload puts radiology professionals under tremendous mental stress and physical fatigue, which also seriously undermines their job satisfaction [6,7]. At the same time, long waiting times for appointments also have a serious impact on patient satisfaction. All of these issues have the potential to find a solution through AI. AI can directly benefit patient care and outcomes by optimizing clinical processes (for example, reducing overtime, idle time, and patient waiting time as well as providing guidance to patients in selecting the most appropriate imaging examinations) [8,9]. In addition, AI can be applied to error-prone administrative processes, allowing medical staff to spend more time on direct clinical care [9]. All of these factors will significantly improve the satisfaction of both radiology professionals and patients and benefit the doctor-patient relationship.

Lastly, the utilization of diverse imaging apparatus is crucial in guaranteeing the optimal functioning of radiology departments. The scientific analysis of the actual use, application trends, and potential of existing equipment in radiology departments, as well as the scientific management of equipment procurement, use, and maintenance based on the aforementioned information, constitutes an essential component of radiology department management practice [10]. However, instances of both underutilization and overutilization of imaging equipment have been observed repeatedly in management practice. These instances not only reduce the operational efficiency of radiology departments but also lead to unnecessary procurement costs. The application of AI is expected to assist hospital administrators in achieving scientific and efficient management of imaging equipment through the establishment of a planning and control system based on big data for the inventory, procurement, and maintenance of imaging equipment. To illustrate, an equipment replacement plan will be developed based on the type, cost, and service life of the equipment. It is essential to develop comprehensive equipment safety plans for all stages of equipment use, including the period preceding patient use, the phase of preventive maintenance, and the stage following routine and major repairs.

In conclusion, the position statement by Wee et al. [1] on the use and clinical deployment of AI in radiology is timely and extremely valuable. However, we should also be aware that the value of AI in radiology is not only in the clinical practice of radiology, but that it can also be of great value in the continuing education of radiology professionals, radiology workflow optimization, and equipment management. Therefore, there is an urgent need for radiology stakeholders to pay attention to the value of AI in non-clinical applications of radiology and to conduct more research on this topic.

Integrally Hilbertian rings and the polynomial Schinzel hypothesis
Angelot Behajaina, Pierre Dèbes

Abstract
. We prove that all rings of integers of number fields are “integrally Hilbertian”. That is, they satisfy an “integral” version of the classical Hilbert irreducibility property, to the effect that, under appropriate irreducibility assumptions on given multivariate polynomials with coefficients in such a ring 𝒵 {\mathcal{Z}} , one can specialize in the ring 𝒵 {\mathcal{Z}} some of the variables in such a way that irreducibility over the ring 𝒵 {\mathcal{Z}} is preserved for the specialized polynomials. We identify an intermediate type of ring, which we call “Schinzel ring”, that is central in the study of this new Hilbert property. Dedekind domains and Unique Factorization Domains are shown to be Schinzel rings, leading to other examples of integrally Hilbertian rings. A main application is a polynomial version, for the ring 𝒵 ⁡ [ Y 1 , … , Y n ] {\mathcal{Z}}[Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{n}] , of the Schinzel Hypothesis about primes in value sets of polynomials. Some consequences for the ring of integers itself are also deduced.

(a) Assumption (*) is here to avoid the case of the original Schinzel Hypothesis (for which Theorem
4.1
says nothing). Indeed, if (*) is removed, and
k=1
(for simplicity), one can take
P_{1},\ldots,P_{s}
to be polynomials in
T
and
\underline{d}=(0)
. The conclusion would then be that one can substitute constant polynomials
m\in{\mathcal{Z}}
such that
P_{1}(m),\ldots,P_{s}(m)
are irreducible in
{\mathcal{Z}}
. For
{\mathcal{Z}}=\mathbb{Z}
, this is the conclusion of the original Schinzel Hypothesis.

(b) The proof of Theorem
4.1
will give a more precise conclusion than the mere existence of the polynomials
M_{1}(\underline{Y}),\ldots,M_{k}(\underline{Y})
. For every
n
-tuple
\underline{d}=(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})
of nonnegative integers, denote the set of polynomials
M\in{\mathcal{Z}}[\underline{Y}]
such that
\deg_{Y_{j}}(M)\leq d_{j}

(
j=1,\ldots,n
), by
{\mathcal{P}}ol_{{\mathcal{Z}},n,\underline{d}}
. It is an affine space over
{\mathcal{Z}}
: the coordinates correspond to the coefficients. The more precise conclusion we obtain is that, under the various conditions on
\underline{d}_{1},\ldots,\underline{d}_{n}
, the set of
k
-tuples
(M_{1}(\underline{Y}),\ldots,M_{k}(\underline{Y}))
of polynomials in
{\mathcal{Z}}[\underline{Y}]
with
\deg_{Y_{j}}(M_{i})\leq d_{ij}
(
i=1,\ldots,k
,
j=1,\ldots,n
) and such that condition (**) holds is Zariski-dense in the affine space
{\mathcal{P}}ol_{{\mathcal{Z}},n,\underline{d_{1}}}\times\cdots\times{\mathcal{P}}ol_{{\mathcal{Z}},n,\underline{d_{k}}}
.

(c) The bounds in Theorem
4.1
(b) are sharp. Take
k=n=1
for simplicity. Given any integer
d\geq 0
, we construct below an irreducible polynomial
P\in\mathbb{Z}[T,Y]
such that
2^{\ell(d)}=\deg_{T}(P)
and
P(M(Y),Y)
is reducible in
\mathbb{Z}[Y]
for all polynomials
M\in\mathbb{Z}[Y]
of degree
d
.

Denote the subset of all polynomials in
\mathbb{Z}[Y]
of degree
\leq d
and with coefficients
0
or
1
by
{\mathcal{M}}_{d}
. We have
{\rm card}({\mathcal{M}}_{d})=2^{d+1}
. Consider the following polynomial:

\displaystyle P_{0}(T,Y)=\prod_{p(Y)\in{\mathcal{M}}_{d}}(T-p(Y))
.

Then take for
P
the polynomial

P(T,Y)=P_{0}(T,Y)+2m
,

where
m\in\mathbb{Z}
is chosen such that
P
is irreducible in
\mathbb{Z}[T,Y]
. To see that such an integer
m
exists, apply Theorem
4.1
(b) with
k=1
,
n=2
,
s=1
, with
P_{1}
taken to be
Q(U,T,Y)=P_{0}(T,Y)+2U
, with the parameter taken to be
U
and
{\underline{Y}}=(T,Y)
. The polynomial
Q
is irreducible in
\mathbb{Z}[U,T,Y]
; condition (*) holds since
\deg_{T,Y}(Q)\geq 1
; and
\ell(0,0)=(0+1)(0+1)=1
so
2^{\ell(0,0)}=2>\deg_{U}(Q)=1
. Therefore one can indeed take
m\in\mathbb{Z}[T,Y]
such that
\deg_{T}(m)=\deg_{Y}(m)=0
and
Q(m,T,Y)
irreducible in
\mathbb{Z}[T,Y]
.

We have
\deg_{T}(P)=2^{d+1}=2^{\ell(d)}
and, by construction, for every polynomial
M(Y)\in\mathbb{Z}[Y]
of degree
d
, we have
M(Y)\equiv p(Y)\pmod{2}
for some
p(Y)\in{\mathcal{M}}_{d}
and so the polynomial
P(M(Y),Y)
is divisible by
2
.

(d)
The bounds in Theorem
4.1
(c) can be interesting in some situations as they only depend on the degrees in
\underline{Y}
of the polynomials
P_{1},\ldots,P_{s}
, for example when
P_{1},\ldots,P_{s}
only depend on
\underline{T}
. The second bound improves on the one given in
[
BDN20
, Theorem 1.1]
.

4.2.2.
Irreducibility of

We need the following lemma. Fix

and, for simplicity, write
\underline{\lambda}
for
\underline{\lambda}_{i}

\ell

\ell_{i}
and
\underline{Q}
for
\underline{Q}_{i}

Lemma 4.3
.

Let

P(T,\underline{Y})=P_{\rho}(\underline{Y})T^{\rho}+\cdots+P_{1}(\underline{Y})T+P_{0}(\underline{Y})\,\ (P_{j}(\underline{Y})\in A[\underline{Y}],j=1,\dots,\rho)

be an irreducible polynomial in
A[T,\underline{Y}]
of degree
\rho
. Assume that either
\deg_{\underline{Y}}(P)\geq 1
or (
\rho\geq 1
and
\ell\geq 1
). Then
G(\underline{\lambda},\underline{Y})=P(M_{\underline{Q}}(\underline{\lambda},\underline{Y}),\underline{Y})
is irreducible in
A[\underline{\lambda},\underline{Y}]
and of degree
\geq 1
in
\underline{Y}
.

Proof.

The irreducibility conclusion follows similarly to
[
BDN20
, Lemma 2.1.]
. Consider the ring automorphism
\varphi:A[\underline{\lambda},\underline{Y}]\rightarrow A[\underline{\lambda},\underline{Y}]
that is the identity on
A[\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{\ell},\underline{Y}]
and maps
\lambda_{0}
to
the polynomial
\lambda_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\lambda_{i}Q_{i}(\underline{X})
. Since
P(\lambda_{0},\underline{Y})
is irreducible in
A[\lambda_{0},\underline{Y}]
, it is also irreducible in

A[\underline{\lambda},\underline{Y}]
. Hence
G(\underline{\lambda},\underline{Y})=\varphi(P(\lambda_{0},\underline{Y}))
is irreducible in
A[\underline{\lambda},\underline{Y}]
.
Regarding the degree conclusion, assume first
\ell\geq 1
. As a polynomial in
\lambda_{1}
, the leading coefficient of

is

\underline{Y}

is. If

is of positive degree in
\underline{Y}
.
Finally consider the case

. We then have

\deg_{\underline{Y}}(P)\geq 1
.
∎

Fix now

and denote

and
F_{l}
by

and

in the following lemma.

Lemma 4.4
.

Assume that
P
is irreducible in
\mathcal{Q}[\underline{T},\underline{Y}]
and satisfies

(*)

\deg_{\underline{Y}}(P)\geq 1
or
\ell_{i}\geq 1
for

Then the polynomial
F
is irreducible in
{\mathcal{Q}}[\underline{\Lambda},\underline{Y}]
and of degree
\geq 1
in
\underline{Y}
.

Proof.

Set
{\underline{T}}^{\prime}=(T_{2},\ldots,T_{k})
and view
P
as in
{\mathcal{Z}}[{\underline{T}}^{\prime}][T_{1},{\underline{Y}}]
(with
{\underline{T}}^{\prime}
the empty tuple if

). We distinguish two cases.

1st case:

\deg_{\underline{Y}}(P)\geq 1
.
Apply Lemma
4.3
to the polynomial
P
,

,
\underline{Q}=\underline{Q_{1}}
and

taken to be
{\mathcal{Q}}[{\underline{T}}^{\prime}]
(i.e.,
\underline{U}=\underline{T}^{\prime}
).
The resulting polynomial, say

, is a polynomial in
{\mathcal{Z}}[{\underline{T}}^{\prime}][{\underline{\lambda_{1}}},{\underline{Y}}]
, which is irreducible in
{\mathcal{Q}}[{\underline{T}}^{\prime}][{\underline{\lambda_{1}}},{\underline{Y}}]
and such that

leads to the conclusion that

is irreducible in
{\mathcal{Q}}[\underline{\Lambda},\underline{Y}]
and of degree
\geq 1
in
\underline{Y}
.

2nd case:

\deg_{\underline{Y}}(P)=0

\ell_{i}\geq 1
for

is in

. View it in

∎

(

Remark 4.6

In the next paragraph, we show that condition

4.2.4.
The No Fixed Divisor condition

The situation is that of Theorem
4.1
. For each
i=1,\ldots,k
, an
n
-tuple
{\underline{d}}_{i}=(d_{i1},\ldots,d_{in})
of nonnegative integers is given and
\underline{Q_{i}}
is the tuple of all monic monomials of degree at most some given integer
d_{ij}
in
Y_{j}
(in some order with
Q_{i0}=1
);
we then have
\ell_{i}+1=\ell(\underline{d}_{i})=\prod_{j=1}^{n}(d_{ij}+1)
. In particular,
\ell_{i}\geq 1
exactly corresponds to
\underline{d}_{i}\not=(0,\ldots,0)
, so condition (**) from §
4.2.3
corresponds to condition (*) from Theorem
4.1
.

Set
\Pi=P_{1}\cdots P_{s}
and use the abbreviation

\underline{M}_{\underline{Q}}(\underline{\Lambda},\underline{Y})
for the
k
-tuple

\underline{M}_{\underline{Q}}(\underline{\Lambda},\underline{Y})=(M_{\underline{Q_{1}}}({\underline{\lambda_{1}}},\underline{Y}),\ldots,M_{\underline{Q_{k}}}({\underline{\lambda_{k}}},\underline{Y}))
.

Then we have
F_{1}\cdots F_{s}=\Pi\hskip 1.0pt(\underline{M}_{\underline{Q}}(\underline{\Lambda},\underline{Y}),\underline{Y})
. Similarly, given some ring
A
and some ring morphism
{\mathcal{Z}}\rightarrow A
, for every
\underline{\Theta}=(\underline{\theta_{1}},\ldots,\underline{\theta_{k})}\in\prod_{i=1}^{k}A^{\ell_{i}+1}
, we denote by
\underline{M}_{\underline{Q}}(\underline{\Theta},\underline{Y})
the
k
-tuple of polynomials in
A[\underline{Y}]
obtained from
\underline{M}_{\underline{Q}}(\underline{\Lambda},\underline{Y})
by specializing to
\underline{\Theta}
the indeterminates in
\underline{\Lambda}
.

Lemma 4.7
.

Asssume that
P_{1},\ldots,P_{s}\in{\mathcal{Z}}[\underline{T},\underline{Y}]
are irreducible in
{\mathcal{Q}}[\underline{T},\underline{Y}]
, the product
P_{1}\cdots P_{s}
has no nonunit divisor in
{\mathcal{Z}}
and condition
(*)
from Theorem
4.1
holds. Then, under the various assumptions on the
{\underline{d}_{i}}
of cases
(a)
,
(b)
,
(c)
of
Theorem
4.1
, the polynomials
F_{1},\ldots,F_{s}
satisfy condition
\hbox{\rm(NoFixDiv}/{\mathcal{Z}}[\underline{\Lambda}])
,
i.e.
, the product
F_{1}\cdots F_{s}
has no fixed divisor
w.r.t.

\underline{\Lambda}
in
{\rm Nonunit}\hskip 1.0pt{\mathcal{Z}}
.

Proof.

Let
p
be a nonunit of
{\mathcal{Z}}
,
p\not=0
. By assumption, the polynomial
\Pi(\underline{T},\underline{Y})
is nonzero in
({\mathcal{Z}}/p{\mathcal{Z}})[\underline{T},\underline{Y}]
. We start with case (b) of Theorem
4.1
.

Case
(b)
:
{\mathcal{Z}}
is a near UFD and
2^{\ell({\underline{d}}_{i})}>\sum_{j=1}^{s}\deg_{T_{i}}(P_{j})
(
i=1,\ldots,k
). As
{\mathcal{Z}}
is a near UFD, one may assume that
p
is a prime. For each
i=1,\ldots,k
, the subset

\{M_{\underline{Q_{i}}}({\overline{\underline{\theta_{i}}}},\underline{Y})\thinspace|\thinspace\overline{\underline{\theta_{i}}}\in({\mathcal{Z}}/p{\mathcal{Z}})^{\ell_{i}+1}\}\subset({\mathcal{Z}}/p{\mathcal{Z}})[\underline{Y}]
,

is of cardinality
|{\mathcal{Z}}/p{\mathcal{Z}}|^{\ell_{i}+1}\geq 2^{\ell_{i}+1}
.
Since
{\mathcal{Z}}/p{\mathcal{Z}}
is in integral domain, it follows from the assumption
2^{\ell_{i}+1}>\deg_{T_{i}}(\Pi)
(
i=1,\ldots,k
) that there exists a
k
-tuple

\underline{M}_{\underline{Q}}(\overline{\underline{\Theta}},\underline{Y})=(M_{\underline{Q_{1}}}({\overline{\underline{\theta_{1}}}},\underline{Y}),\ldots,M_{\underline{Q_{k}}}({\overline{\underline{\theta_{k}}}},\underline{Y}))\in(({\mathcal{Z}}/p{\mathcal{Z}})[\underline{Y}])^{k}

such that

\Pi\hskip 1.0pt(\underline{M}_{\underline{Q}}(\overline{\underline{\Theta}},\underline{Y}),\underline{Y})
is nonzero in
({\mathcal{Z}}/p{\mathcal{Z}})[\underline{Y}]
.
Lifting the
\overline{\underline{\theta_{i}}}
to some elements of
{\mathcal{Z}}
provides a point
{\underline{\Theta}}=(\underline{\theta_{1}},\ldots,\underline{\theta_{k}})\in\prod_{i=1}^{k}{\mathcal{Z}}^{\ell_{i}+1}
such that

\underline{M}_{\underline{Q}}(\underline{\Theta},\underline{Y})=(M_{\underline{Q_{1}}}({\underline{\theta_{1}}},\underline{Y}),\ldots,M_{\underline{Q_{k}}}(\underline{\theta_{k}},\underline{Y}))\in({\mathcal{Z}}[\underline{Y}])^{k}

has the property that
\Pi\hskip 1.0pt(\underline{M}_{\underline{Q}}(\underline{\Theta},\underline{Y}),\underline{Y})
is not divisible by
p
.
Conclude that
\underline{\Theta}
is a special value of
\underline{\Lambda}
that makes the polynomial
\Pi\hskip 1.0pt(\underline{M}_{\underline{Q}}(\underline{\Lambda},\underline{Y}),\underline{Y})
nonzero modulo
p
, that is,
p{\mathcal{Z}}
is not a fixed divisor of
F_{1}\cdots F_{s}

w.r.t.

\underline{\Lambda}
.

Case
(a) (general case)
:
{\mathcal{Z}}
is an integrally Hilbertian ring and
\ell({\underline{d}}_{i})>\sum_{j=1}^{s}\deg_{T_{i}}(P_{j})
(
i=1,\ldots,k
)
. The ring
{\mathcal{Z}}/p{\mathcal{Z}}
is no longer integral in general. We will however be able to adjust the argument by working with a smaller subset of tuples of polynomials
\underline{M}_{\underline{Q}}(\underline{\Theta},\underline{Y})

than the one used in Case (b).

Specifically, for each
i=1,\ldots,k
, consider the following subset:

{\mathcal{S}}_{i}=\{Q_{il}\thinspace|\thinspace l=0,\ldots,\ell_{i}\}\subset{\mathcal{Z}}[\underline{Y}]
.

We claim that the corresponding elements, regarded in
({\mathcal{Z}}/p{\mathcal{Z}})[\underline{Y}]
have this property: any difference between two distinct such elements is not a zero divisor in
({\mathcal{Z}}/p{\mathcal{Z}})[\underline{Y}]
. Namely, if
P\in({\mathcal{Z}}/p{\mathcal{Z}})[\underline{Y}]
is nonzero and
Q
is the largest monomial in
P
for the lexicographical order, then the largest monomial in the polynomial
Q_{il}P
is
Q_{il}Q
. For two different
Q_{il},Q_{il^{\prime}}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{i}
, we have
Q_{il}Q\not=Q_{il^{\prime}}Q
and so
Q_{il}P\not=Q_{i^{\prime}l^{\prime}}P
, which proves the claim. Deduce next that the product of all nonzero differences
(Q_{il}-Q_{il^{\prime}})
with
Q_{il},Q_{il^{\prime}}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{i}
is not a zero divisor in
({\mathcal{Z}}/p{\mathcal{Z}})[\underline{Y}]
.

It follows from this conclusion that a nonzero polynomial in one variable of degree
\leq\ell_{i}
and with coefficients in
({\mathcal{Z}}/p{\mathcal{Z}})[\underline{Y}]
cannot vanish at every one of the
\ell_{i}+1
elements

with
l=0,\ldots,\ell_{i}
. Indeed the determinant of the linear system corresponding to the
\ell_{i}+1
vanishing conditions – a Van Der Monde determinant – is precisely the product of differences in
({\mathcal{Z}}/p{\mathcal{Z}})[\underline{Y}]
considered above. By construction it is not a zero divisor in
({\mathcal{Z}}/p{\mathcal{Z}})[\underline{Y}]
, and so only the zero polynomial could be a solution to the system, which is excluded.

Conclude: as
\ell_{i}\geq\deg_{T_{i}}(\Pi)
for each
i=1,\ldots,k
, there exist
l_{i}\in\{0,\ldots,\ell_{i}\}
(
i=1,\ldots,k
) such that the
k
-tuple
\underline{Q}_{\underline{l}}=(Q_{1l_{1}},\ldots,Q_{kl_{k}})\in({\mathcal{Z}}[\underline{Y}])^{k}
satisfies the following

\Pi\hskip 1.0pt(\underline{Q}_{\underline{l}},\underline{Y})
is not divisible by
p
.

Conclude as in case (b) that
p{\mathcal{Z}}
is not a fixed divisor of
F_{1}\cdots F_{s}

w.r.t.

\underline{\Lambda}
.

Case
(c)
:
{\mathcal{Z}}
is integrally Hilbertian,
k=1
,
\underline{d_{1}}=(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})
and

\sum_{j=1}^{n}d_{j}>\sum_{i=1}^{s}\deg_{\underline{Y}}(P_{i})
.

Write
T
for
T_{1}
,
\underline{\lambda}
for
\underline{\lambda_{1}}
and
\ell
for
\ell_{1}=\prod_{j=1}^{n}(d_{j}+1)-1
. We may assume that

. Set

\Pi=P_{1}\dots P_{s}=Z_{0}(\underline{Y})+Z_{1}(\underline{Y})T+\dots+Z_{r}(\underline{Y})T^{r}
.

Assume on the contrary that
F_{1}\cdots F_{s}
has a fixed divisor
w.r.t.

\underline{\lambda}
,
i.e.
, there is a nonunit
p\in\mathcal{Z}
,
p\not=0
, such that

In particular, for

.

We claim that for every
0\leq i<j\leq r
such that
Z_{i}(\underline{Y})
and
Z_{j}(\underline{Y})
are nonzero, the nonzero monomials appearing in
Z_{i}(\underline{Y})Y_{1}^{id_{1}}\dots Y_{n}^{id_{n}}
are different from the ones in

. Indeed for nonzero monomials

and
B

Z_{i}(\underline{Y})Y_{1}^{id_{1}}\dots Y_{n}^{id_{n}}
and
Z_{j}(\underline{Y})Y_{1}^{jd_{1}}\dots Y_{n}^{jd_{n}}
respectively, we have:

\displaystyle\deg_{\underline{Y}}(A)

\displaystyle\leq\deg_{\underline{Y}}(Z_{i})+i\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\deg_{\underline{Y}}(\Pi)+i\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{s}\deg_{\underline{Y}}(P_{i})+i\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}\right)

\displaystyle<\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}\right)+i\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}\right)\quad\textrm{(by assumption)}

\displaystyle\leq j\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}\right)\leq\deg_{\underline{Y}}(B).

Thus
A\neq B
.
Hence, for each
i=1,\ldots,r
, we have
Z_{i}(\underline{Y})Y_{1}^{i\cdot d_{1}}\dots Y_{n}^{i\cdot d_{n}}\equiv 0\pmod{p}
, and so

. Therefore

divides
\Pi
, a contradiction.

Finally, assume as in the special case of Theorem
4.1
(c) that
\mathcal{Z}
is a near UFD and that
\sum_{j=1}^{n}d_{j}>\max_{1\leq i\leq s}\deg_{\underline{Y}}(P_{i})
.
Without loss of generality, we may assume that
p
is prime. Since
(\mathcal{Z}/p\mathcal{Z})[\underline{Y}]
is an integral domain, there exists
1\leq j\leq s
such that

P_{j}(Y_{1}^{d_{1}}\dots Y_{n}^{d_{n}},\underline{Y})\equiv 0\pmod{p}
.

In the second part of the argument above, with

replacing
P_{1}\cdots P_{s}
, we conclude that

, a contradiction.
∎

4.4.
The coprime Schinzel Hypothesis

Given a domain
{\mathcal{Z}}
, we relate the property of being a Schinzel ring to the property called
coprime Schinzel Hypothesis
in
[
BDKN22
]
and deduce new results on the latter. We take
{\mathcal{P}}
to be the primality type
{\rm Nonunit}
. Some of the results extend to other primality types; we let the interested reader make the necessary adjustments.

Proposition 4.10
.

Every Schinzel ring
{\mathcal{Z}}
satisfies the
coprime Schinzel Hypothesis
, meaning the following is true. For any integers
k\geq 1
,
\ell\geq 2
and any nonzero polynomials
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{\ell}\in{\mathcal{Z}}[\underline{T}]
, assumed to be coprime and satisfying this local condition:

(CopSch)
No nonunit
p\in{\mathcal{Z}}
divides all values
Q_{1}(\underline{m}),\dots,Q_{\ell}(\underline{m})
with
\underline{m}\in{\mathcal{Z}}^{k}
,

we have this conclusion:

(
4.10
)
There exists
\underline{m}=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{k})\in{\mathcal{Z}}^{k}
such that
Q_{1}(\underline{m}),\ldots,Q_{\ell}(\underline{m})
have no common divisor in
{\rm Nonunit}\hskip 1.0pt\mathcal{Z}
.

Proof.

The proof has two parts. The first one is similar to (and even easier than) the proof of Proposition
2.8
. Let
s\geq 1
and
P_{1},\ldots,P_{s}\in{\mathcal{Z}}[\underline{T},\underline{Y}]
be
s
polynomials satisfying conditions (Prim
/{\mathcal{Q}}[\underline{T}]
) and
\hbox{\rm(NoFixDiv}/{\mathcal{Z}}[\underline{T}])_{\mathcal{P}}
from Section
2.2
. Set
\underline{T}^{\prime}=(T_{2},\dots,T_{k})
.
As
{\mathcal{Z}}
is a Schinzel ring, there is an arithmetic progression
\tau=(\omega\ell+\alpha)_{\ell\in{\mathcal{Z}}}\subset{\mathcal{Z}}
(
\omega,\alpha\in{\mathcal{Z}},\omega\neq 0
) such that, for all but finitely many
m_{1}\in\tau
, the polynomials

P_{1}(m_{1},\underline{T}^{\prime},\underline{Y}),\dots,P_{s}(m_{1},\underline{T}^{\prime},\underline{Y})\in{\mathcal{Z}}[\underline{T}^{\prime},\underline{Y}]

satisfy (Prim
/{\mathcal{Q}}[\underline{T}^{\prime}]
) and
\hbox{\rm(NoFixDiv}/{\mathcal{Z}}[\underline{T}^{\prime}])_{\mathcal{P}}
. The same argument can be applied to these polynomials. Repeating inductively the argument to each of the parameters
T_{1},\ldots,T_{k}
leads to this conclusion:

(*)  There exists
\underline{m}=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{k})\in{\mathcal{Z}}^{k}
such that the polynomials

P_{1}(\underline{m},\underline{Y}),\dots,P_{s}(\underline{m},\underline{Y})\in{\mathcal{Z}}[\underline{Y}]

have no common divisor in
{\rm Nonunit}\mathcal{Z}
.

What we have proved – that (*) holds for any
s
polynomials
P_{1},\ldots,P_{s}\in{\mathcal{Z}}[\underline{T},\underline{Y}]
satisfying conditions (Prim
/{\mathcal{Q}}[\underline{T}]
) and
\hbox{\rm(NoFixDiv}/{\mathcal{Z}}[\underline{T}])_{\mathcal{P}}
– is called the
Primitive Specialization Hypothesis
in
[
BDKN22
, Definition 3.3]
. The second part of the proof rests on
[
BDKN22
, Lemma 3.4(2-b)]
: by this lemma, the Primitive Specialization Hypothesis implies the coprime Schinzel Hypothesis as stated in Proposition
4.10
.
∎

Proposition
4.10
and Theorem
2.9
(a) yield the following result.

Corollary 4.11
.

Dedekind domains and near UFDs satisfy the coprime Schinzel Hypothesis.

The Dedekind part is new for
k>1
, the near UFD part was known in
[
BDKN22
]
.

Numerical and experimental investigation on aeroelasticity of wind turbine blade with biological airfoil and V‑stripe web
Authors: Kun Chen, Kai Ren, Peiyao Zhao, Tao Su, Zhenhua Hao, Qi Wang, Zhikai Zhao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractWith the development of horizontal axis wind turbines, the problem of blade tremors under wind load becomes increasingly prominent, increasing the risk of blade fatigue fracture. To improve the flutter problem, a wind turbine blade with biological airfoil and V-stripe web is proposed based on similar criteria. The comparison of vibration and deformation between the bionic blade and the original blade of the 5 MW wind turbine is investigated by numerical calculation and model experiment. The fluid-structure interaction method and the Shear Stress Transport (SST) k-ω turbulence model with closed Reynolds-averaged Navier-Stokes. Equations are adopted in the numerical calculation. The modal analysis and harmonic response analyses of the blades are carried out. Numerical results show that the maximum deflection of the bionic blade is reduced by 14.89% compared with the original blade. Meanwhile, the aeroelastic vibration displacements, velocities, accelerations, and strains of the original and bionic blades were tested. Experimental data indicate that the tip deflection of the bionic blade is reduced by 54.74% compared with the original blade. Bionic blade show better aeroelastic deformation performance, which can improve the lifespan of the blade.Keywords: Aeroelasticity, Fluid-Structure interaction, Owl blade, Modeling testSubject terms: Mechanical engineering, Wind energy

Introduction

With the world focuses on clean energy, horizontal axis wind turbines are becoming larger. The elongation of the blade shape also requires more flexibility and less weight to support its stability1. When a wind turbine is operating at rated conditions, the impact of wind loads causes deformation of the blades. The change in blade shape in turn affects the wind conditions, which in turn leads to the problem of aeroelastic instability of the wind turbine2. The frequent occurrence of wind turbine blade breakage in wind farms has led to an extreme increase in the cost and difficulty of their repair. Therefore, more scholars have begun to study the problem of aeroelasticity of wind turbines in the hope of reducing the effects caused by aeroelasticity. By studying the aerodynamic performance of wind turbines, Aryan et al. provided a fast and accurate aerodynamic prediction model using a three-dimensional (3D) panel code3. Combining Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) with Blade Element Momentum Theory (BEMT) was proposed by Mikkelsen et al. A new efficient and accurate actuation line model (ALM) was proposed. The aerodynamic modeling of the blade can be efficiently simulated using this model.

There are two main approaches for fluid computation: the Blade Element Momentum (BEM) and Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD). For structural simulation, there are two main approaches, one is using three-dimensional (3D) Finite Element Method (FEM) and one-dimensional (1D) Equivalent Beam Model (EBM). Different methods can be combined with each other to form different fluid-structure interaction computational methods. Using combining BEM with a one-dimensional (1D) unit beam model, Mo et al.4. By simulating the surface of a 5 MW wind turbine original, the deformation of the blades due to aerodynamic loads has a counteracting effect on the aerodynamic loads. CFD combined with direct computational fluid dynamics (CSD) (CFD/CSD) was used by Zhang et al.5. A stable aerodynamic degradation model (ROM) was constructed. Then the vortex vibration theory was incorporated to establish a new fluid-structure interaction model. The aerodynamic ROMs also provide a low-dimensional, high-efficiency and high-accuracy model for many subsequent scholars to study aeroelasticity. Meng et al.6 combined the Actuator line model with the Euler Bernoulli beam principle. Proposed an elastic actuator line model to analyze the effect of the vibration velocity of its blades on the angle of attack.

Hak Min Lee et al.7optimized the airfoil shape based on a genetic algorithm, and a fluid-structure interaction method is used to simulate the aeroelasticity of airfoils, which improves the economic efficiency of airfoils. Xing Zheng8 analyzed the aeroelastic problem of wind turbines with different turbulence intensities through two-way fluid-structure interaction. The results confirm that turbulence is the most important factor affecting the aeroelasticity of airfoils. M. Sayed9 performed aeroelastic simulation of DTU 10 MW using fluid-structure interaction method and observed an increase in the power generation performance of the wind turbine. Witold Skrzypinski et al.10employed a new computational model of aeroelastic response to investigate the effect of different airfoil shapes on the stability of the modes.

It is equally important to study the aeroelasticity of wind turbines under different conditions. Bin Wang11 analyzed the stresses, strains and blade displacements of a 5 MW wind turbine of three different configurations by fluid-structure interaction. Lin Wang12 used the fluid-structure interaction method to analyze the deflection generated by a 1.5 MW original at five different wind speeds. Yao Tian et al.13investigated a 10 MW wind turbine and analyzed it by controlling the tilt angle. It was found to be able to mitigate speed loss and response load.

The problem of reducing the aeroelasticity of the blades by adding auxiliary devices is also a hot research issue in recent years. Sergio González Horcas14 explored the installation of blade flaps to mitigate vortex-excited vibration at rest, and the results showed that the installation of flaps is useful in mitigating vortex-excited vibration. Shalini Verma15 investigated the aeroelastic problem on the blade tip without bladelets and on the blade with bladelets by numerical analysis and the results show that the aeroelastic instability problem increases for the blade with blade. Carlo L et al.16 proposed a passive flap that attenuates the frequency of the blade vibration and reduces the fatigue wear of blades.

The displacement deformation, pressure and stress-strain values of blades suffered to aerodynamic and centrifugal loads under different operating conditions were investigated by means of fluid-structure interaction of the blades. These data are essential for the ability to improve blade performance and safe operation17. Fluid-structure interaction method plays a crucial role in the aeroelastic analysis of wind turbine airfoils. Francesco Castellani et al.18 analyzed a small wind turbine through a combination of numerical calculations and experiments, and the results explained the principles of blade-tower interaction and wake vibration. Steven N. Rodriguez et al.19 investigated the stability and blade tip vortex characteristics of flexible blades by fluid-structure interaction. Stephen M. Barr20 designed a variable angle combined with fiber material blade, to calculate static aerodynamic loads and structural deformation on the blade separately. Giacomo Della Posta21 analyzed a 5 MW wind turbine by fluid-structure interaction and found that the blade waving blade vibration is more susceptible to aerodynamic loads and the blade vibration produces significant aerodynamic damping in the rotation.

With more bionic structures being used in engineering, scholars have made significant achievements in the field of bionics. For the vibration performance of wind turbine blades, scholars have sought solutions to the problem from windward plants and agile birds. Herrera C22 designed a wind turbine that imitates the shape of tree polygonum seeds, which reduces the degree of blade deformation. Jie Meng23 designed a porous damping blade imitating a bamboo wall, which can effectively reduce the vibration deflection of the blade. Jiale Huang24 designed a bionic bending and torsion-coupled web based on straw, which improved bending and torsion-coupled performance of the thin-walled beam and the stability of the structure under the same mass. Wangyu Liu25 proposed two kinds of honeycomb structures and applied the two structures in the web model of wind turbine, and these two structures effectively improved the mechanical properties of the wind turbine model.

From the above literature, the blades are analyzed using fluid-structure interaction in different ways such as by adding flaps and changes in turbulence intensity. In the field of bionics, most of the scholars have analyzed only single properties in a single organism to improve the aeroelasticity problem of wind turbine blades. And the blade tremor problem of two biology interaction or multiple biology interaction needs further improvement. In this paper, a new bionic airfoil is designed by using a bionic airfoil and V-web interaction method. The vibration and deformation performance of the blade is analyzed by using the analysis method of fluid-structure interaction. The study of the aeroelastic deformation of the bionic blade provides an important basis for the subsequent study of the aeroelasticity of the bionic airfoil.

Inspired by the efficient flight of owls, this paper adopts the method of axis reorganization to design the bionic airfoil, which is designed at 70%-100% of the blade spanning direction; the optimal V-shaped structure of the owl blade surface was chosen to act on the blade. Numerical simulations were performed by using Fluent Structure modules in ANSYS. Fluid-structure interaction method, Modal analysis, Harmonic response analysis, and model experiments are used to study the effect of bionic structures on the aeroelasticity of blades.

Bionic blade with combined airfoil and V‑Stripe web

Combined airfoil and V‑stripe web

Replacing the original airfoil shape of the blade with the cross-sectional airfoil shape of the owl’s blade can change the performance of the blade. Considering that the sectional airfoils of 5 MW blades are all thick airfoils, directly applying the owl airfoils to the blades will lead to excessive deflection and even fracture.

The increase in airfoil thickness can reduce the deformation of blades, but it will weaken the aerodynamic capability of the airfoil surface28. Selection of an owl blade 50% cross-section coupled to a NACA64 airfoil to avoid strength degradation in wind turbine airfoils. First, the chord length of the two airfoils is kept at 100 mm and placed horizontally. Then, observations of the upper and lower structures of the blades suggest that the relative thickness of the upper chord of the bionic blade is similar while the height position of the maximum thickness is different. The lower chord line of 50% section airfoil of owl blade is concave, and the lower chord line of NACA64 airfoil is convex, and the relative thickness difference between them reaches the maximum. Therefore, the upper chord line of 50% section airfoil and the lower chord line of NACA64 airfoil of the owl blade are reserved29.

The owl blade model was first extracted using a body laser scanning blade. The data obtained was then imported into SOLIDWORKS for cutting and again into CAD. Feature points were extracted by List command. Polynomial fitting was used to make the surface of the blade model smoother30. As shown in Fig. 2.

Bionic airfoil.

By observing the V-stripe structure of the overlapping feathers on the surface of the owl’s blades. The V-shaped stripe was designed according to its structural characteristics30. The stripe parameters included the width (d), depth-to-diameter ratio () and stripe spacing (t). As shown in Fig. 4. In this paper, a striped width of 2 mm, a depth-to-diameter ratio of 3, and its spacing set to 2 mm are used.

Bionic blade model.

The original 1.5 MW wind turbine is composed of DU-type blade and NACA64-618 blade, whose DU-type blade has a large cross-sectional area. The DU-type blade is not suitable for acting the coupled bionic airfoil cross-section on it. It has been shown through many studies that the bionic airfoil is more suitable to act on the original NACA blade. In summary, in this paper, the bionic blade is applied at 70%~100% of the spreading direction.

Coupled design

Inspired by the high aerodynamic efficiency of long-eared owls in predation and the fact that the owl blade has some similarities with wind turbine blades. By extracting the geometric features of the owl blade blades, it was observed that the owl wings have an overlapping V-shaped structure30, as shown in Fig. 3. The V-shaped web is used to simplify the surface structure of the owl blade with interstrip widths, depth-to-diameter ratios, and gaps, and then the bionic web is coupled.

Owl blade surface structure.

The bionic blade is used at 70%~ 100% of the bionic coupled airfoil. Replace the web position with the best combination of V-stripe web structure. Build the bionic coupled airfoil as in Fig. 4.

Fluid-structure interaction method

Fluid-structure interaction is divided into unidirectional and bidirectional interaction. Unidirectional fluid-structure interaction saves computational cost and computational resources than bidirectional fluid-structure interaction. Bidirectional fluid-structure interaction is 15 times more than single fluid-structure interaction under the condition of relevant computational resources38. The unidirectional fluid-structure interaction is less difficult to model and solve, and the convergence is more stable. Therefore, the single fluid-structure interaction is adopted39. CFD (Computational Fluid Dynamics) is used to solve the fluid field. The aerodynamic pressure on the coupled blade is used as the boundary condition under the convergence condition. The information is transferred between them at the interface where the numerical computational model intersects the computational domain. As shown on Fig. 11.

Results and discussions

Aeroelastic deformation analysis

Deflection distribution at different locations of the blade in the simulation session

Figure 15 demonstrates the variation of deflection with blade span for the bionic coupled airfoil and the original airfoil simulated at rated operating conditions. Near the root of the blade, the difference in deflection between the two blades is smaller. The difference in deflection between the two types of blades gradually increases from the 50% spreading position of the blade. Overall, the deflection value of the original blade is large, the value of the bionic blade is small, and the deflection difference of the original blade increases gradually from the blade root to the blade tip area. For the original blade, the bionic blade’s maximum deflection reduced 14.89%. The bionic blade can inhibit deflection. The bionic blade greatly improves the blade stiffness, reduces the blade flapping bending deformation and increases safety distance to prevent blade to tower collision.

Deflection distribution of original and bionic blades.

Figure 16 illustrates the mean deflection and standard deviation of the airfoil in the simulation for different span positions in the simulation. The maximum deflection of both the original and bionic coupled blades occurs in the tip region. The blade deflection at 0 ~ 35% span position is very small, and the standard deviation does not vary greatly in value. The amount of deformation from the 40% spreading position to the 60% spreading position increases significantly, and the standard deviation value is also changed significantly at this position.

Mean deformation and standard deviation at different spanwise positions.

Figure 17 shows the distribution of deflection and difference in vibration form between original and bionic blades due to increased blade span. The vibration form that causes the blade to produce a large deflection is Flap-wise vibration, followed by Edge-wise vibration. The maximum deflection of the blade Edge-wise vibration is about 14.7% of the Flap-wise vibration. The torsional vibration is mainly measured in terms of the angle of blade twist, which produces very little deflection. The maximum deflection of the bionic blade was reduced from the original blade by 15.14% for Flap-wise vibration and 16.43% for Edge-wise vibration.

Deflection distribution for different vibration forms.

In numerical calculations, the aerodynamic elastic deflection of the bionic blade tip was reduced by 14.89% compared to the original blade profile. The numerical results show that the aerodynamic elasticity at the blade tip changes significantly. Subsequent experiments are based on test point three.

Conclusion

In this paper, based on a 5 MW wind turbine as an original blade, the bionic blade and V-stripe are coupled to establish a bionic blade model. The deflection and stress-strain distribution of the blade at rated wind speed are analyzed using numerical calculation methods. Then, the blade is analyzed for modal analysis and harmonic response. The analysis reveals that the bionic coupled airfoil has better vibration damping ability at a rated wind speed. Its maximum deflection is reduced by 14.89%; the deflection generated by blade vibration is reduced by 15.14%; and the deflection generated by waving vibration is reduced by 16.435%. The stress-strain distribution of the original blade decreases gradually from the center of the blade to the tip. The stress-strain distribution of the bionic airfoil decreases gradually from leading edge to trailing edge. This is mainly because of adding the bionic coupled airfoil at 70%~100%. Although both airfoils are dominated by waving vibration and pendulum vibration. However, the first-order intrinsic frequency, velocity response and acceleration response of the bionic-coupled airfoil at the tip are reduced by 76.01%, 95.01% and 95.02% compared to the original blade. Putting the original blade and the bionic blade through the aeroelastic experimental platform. It was shown that the peak aeroelastic deformation of the bionic blade was reduced by 54.74% compared with that of the original blade.

By comparing the bionic blade with the original blade, it is obtained that the bionic blade has obvious improvement in reducing the stress concentration problem and the aerodynamic elastic deformation problem. It is helpful to solve the problem of large deformation of horizontal axis wind turbine affected by aerodynamic load and improve the service life of wind turbine. It provides certain reference value for the design and calibration of future bionic blades.

The Grünbaum–Rigby configuration as a special Kárteszi configuration
Gábor Gévay, György Kiss, Tomaž Pisanski

Abstract
In 1990, Branko Grünbaum and John Rigby presented a 4-configuration, known today as the Grünbaum–Rigby configuration ; it is denoted by GR ⁡ ( 21 4 ) \mathrm{GR}(21_{4}) . Independently and earlier, in 1986, Ferenc Kárteszi published a paper in which he proved a theorem in real geometry that gives rise to a series of 4-configurations K ⁡ ( n , ℓ , m ) \mathrm{K}(n;\ell,m) . In an even earlier paper from 1964, he presented a figure which is essentially the same as that given by Grünbaum and Rigby. In this paper, we explore some properties of the Kárteszi configurations and in particular show that GR ⁡ ( 21 4 ) \mathrm{GR}(21_{4}) is isomorphic to K ⁡ ( 7 , 2 , 3 ) \mathrm{K}(7;2,3) . We present a theorem that gives necessary and sufficient conditions on parameters n , ℓ , m n,\ell,m such that the corresponding configuration K ⁡ ( n , ℓ , m ) \mathrm{K}(n;\ell,m) is realisable as a geometric polycyclic configuration with n n -fold rotational symmetry and no extra incidences.



Keywords:
geometric configuration, celestial configuration, Grünbaum–Rigby configuration, Kárteszi configuration, regular n-gon.

Mathematics Subject Classification:
51A20, 51A45, 51E30, 05B30, 01A60.

In this note we would like to emphasize an important and unfortunately overlooked contribution of Kárteszi

The first book on configurations was written by F. Levi

The book of Branko Grünbaum

In 2003, the same problem was independently attacked by a general theory of

points or lines has the same order
m

configuration is polycyclic and the corresponding semi-regular automorphism can be realised as a
m

In Grünbaum’s book
[

a special class of polycyclic configurations is named

-astral configurations

[

His student Leah Berman named them

Two notations were used: Boben and Pisanski specified them as

C_{4}(m;(p_{1},p_{2},\ldots p_{d}),(q_{1},q_{2},\ldots,q_{d}),t)

m\#(p_{1},q_{1};p_{2},q_{2};\ldots;p_{d},q_{d})

Since geometric

Let

4

The collection of parameters producing a genuine geometric celestial configuration (with no extra incidences) is

A couple of years ago, Berman and Berardinelli
[

gave a set of four axioms that identify valid parameters

condition (A3), and substring condition (A4). These axioms already appear in a less systematic way in
[
4

by Boben and Pisanski. If we are interested in connected configurations, anothr axiom

\gcd(P,Q,m)=1

Grünbaum defines the configuration to be

celestial configuration if the multisets
P

and

are equal. For trivial celestial configurations, the axioms (A1) and (A3) are always satisfied.
The necessary conditions for the parameters to be valid for a trivial
3
-celestial configuration
then

has to be a set (and not a multiset) and the configuration has to be described as

configuration. Hence, we may choose
a<b<c
. For larger values of

for different configurations. It turns out that some conditions need to be imposed on the parameters to
guarantee that the structure is indeed a combinatorial
4

geometrically, some false incidences may appear.

Theorem 2

(Kárteszi, 1964

)

Let

Lemma 3

Let

be a regular

-gon whose centre is

, and let

\mathcal{T}=T_{0}T_{1}\dots T_{n-1}
be the

-gon of
\mathcal{R}

Proof.

Due to the rotational symmetry of
\mathcal{R}
, it is sufficient to prove the statements for

The line
CG
is an axis of symmetry of
\mathcal{R}
, hence

is the midpoint of the line

R_{0}CT_{0}\angle=FCG\angle=\frac{k-1}{2}\beta.

The triangles

So

Thus,
\varphi_{k}(R_{0})=T_{0}
and
\varphi_{k}(F)=G

The line

is mapped to the line

4
Conclusion

In this paper, we present the evidence that the Grünbaum–Rigby configuration has been constructed by
Ferenc Kárteszi already in 1964. We also point out that a theorem by Kárteszi gives rise to a special
3-parameter family of trivial 3-celestial 4-configurations that we named
Kárteszi configurations
.
Using geometric arguments, we give an independent characterization of the validity of these parameters.

It would be interesting to investigate to what extent the Kárteszi Theorem can be adapted to cover more
general celestial or even other polycyclic 4-configurations.

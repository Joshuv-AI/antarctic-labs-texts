Introduction

It is well-known that bi-Hamiltonian structure and integrability of many systems in physics and mechanics are closely related. Bi-Hamiltonian structures often allow us to construct a natural family of functions in involution. When this family is complete, we obtain an integrable Hamiltonian system. This connection was first observed for infinite-dimensional systems in the pioneering work of F. Magri

[
13
]
(this idea was further developed in
[
9
]

[

[

by A. S. Mishchenko & A. T. Fomenko

16

[
15
]
for the Lie algebra
\operatorname{so}(n)

of a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}

The original argument shift method required polynomial invariants of coadjoint representation. A.V. Brailov’s modification (

The completeness criterion for the subalgebras

[

[
5

A. V. Bolsinov introduced Jordan–Kronecker invariants of a Lie algebra

In

Then, in

The cornerstone for constructing commutative subalgebras

The

Remark 2.4

p_{\mathcal{L}}
into irreducible factors

p_{\mathcal{L}}=f_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdot\dots\cdot f_{t}^{k_{t}},

all degrees
k_{i}=1
.

Second, it may be easier to check that the conditions of Theorem
4.2
are not satisfied. Then
\hat{\mathcal{K}}
is not complete. In particular, we get the following.

Corollary 4.7
.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a Poisson pencil on
M
. Assume that for a generic point
x\in M
there are Jordan blocks in the Jordan–Kronecker decomposition of
\mathcal{P}(x)
but they are not
2\times 2
Jordan blocks with distinct eigenvalues. Then the extended core distribution
\hat{\mathcal{K}}
is not complete.

5
Lie algebras

In this section we briefly discuss how the results of this paper can be applied for some well-known commutative subalgebras of commutative subalgebras in the symmetric algebra
S(\mathfrak{g})
of a finite-dimensional Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a finite-dimensional Lie algebra and
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
be its dual space. There are two natural Poisson brackets on
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
:

1.

Linear
Lie–Poisson
bracket
\mathcal{A}_{x}
, given by

\left\{f,g\right\}=\langle x,[df(x),dg(x)]\rangle,\quad x\in\mathfrak{g}^{*},\quad f,g:\mathfrak{g}^{*}\to\mathbb{C};

2.

Take
a\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. The corresponding constant Poisson bracket
\mathcal{A}_{a}
(so called
”frozen argument” bracket
) is given by

\left\{f,g\right\}=\langle a,[df(x),dg(x)]\rangle,\quad x\in\mathfrak{g}^{*},\quad f,g:\mathfrak{g}^{*}\to\mathbb{C};

For any
a\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
the brackets
\mathcal{A}_{x}
and
\mathcal{A}_{a}
are compatible. Their matrices at a point
x\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
are

\mathcal{A}_{x}=\left(\sum_{k}c_{ij}^{k}x_{k}\right),\qquad\mathcal{A}_{a}=\left(\sum_{k}c_{ij}^{k}a_{k}\right).

(23)

In
[
5
]

Jordan–Kronecker invariants
of
\mathfrak{g}
were introduced. Roughly speaking, these invariants are sizes of Kronecker blocks and sizes of Jordan blocks grouped by eigenvalues for a generic linear pencil
\mathcal{P}_{x,a}=\mathcal{A}_{x}+\lambda\mathcal{A}_{a}
, given by (
23
). It was also shown that
characteristic polynomial for the pencil
\mathcal{P}_{x,a}
is related with the
fundamental semi-invariant

p_{\mathfrak{g}}
of
\mathfrak{g}
. Namely, that

p_{\mathcal{P}_{x,a}}(\lambda)=p_{\mathfrak{g}}(x+\lambda a).

We discuss the following commutative subalgebras of the symmetric algebra
S(\mathfrak{g})
:

•

the
algebra of polynomial shifts

\mathcal{F}_{a}
(for the definition and details see
[
5
]
);

•

the
extended Mischenko-Fomenko subalgebras

\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{a}
(see
[
10
]
);

•

and the
algebra of shift of semi-invariants

\mathcal{F}_{a}^{\textrm{si}}
(see
[
11
]
).

Denote by

d\mathcal{F}_{a}=\operatorname{span}\left\{df(x),f\in\mathcal{F}_{a}\right\}

the distribution ”spanned” in
T^{*}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
by
\mathcal{F}_{a}
. We also consider similar distributions
d\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{a},d\mathcal{F}_{a}^{\operatorname{si}}
for
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{a}
and
\mathcal{F}_{a}^{\operatorname{si}}
respectively. For a regular
a\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
the followings fact about this distributions are known:

•

In
[
5

it was explained that the algebra of polynomial shifts
\mathcal{F}_{a}
”spans” the core distribution
\mathcal{K}
almost everywhere.

•

By
[
10

the extended Mischenko-Fomenko subalgebra
\mathcal{F}_{a}

\hat{\mathcal{K}}
almost everywhere.

•

Also, in
[
11
]
it was shown that for the algebra of shifts of semi-invariants
d\mathcal{F}_{a}^{\operatorname{si}}=d\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{a}
almost everywhere.

Formally, we have the following.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
a\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
be a regular element. Then for a generic
x\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
we have the following:

1.

d\mathcal{F}_{a}(x)=\mathcal{K}(x)
;

2.

d\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{a}(x)=\mathcal{F}_{a}^{\operatorname{si}}=\hat{\mathcal{K}}(x)
.

For the Lie algebras the completeness criterion, given by Theorem
4.2
, can be reformulated as follows.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
a\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
be a regular element. The extended Mischenko-Fomenko subalgebra
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{a}
is complete if and only if the following
2
conditions hold.

1.

The Jordan–Kronecker decomposition of a generic pencil
\left\{\mathcal{A}_{x}+\mathcal{A}_{a}\right\}
contains just one trivial
2\times 2
Jordan
\lambda_{i}
-block for each root
\lambda_{i}
of
p_{\mathfrak{g}}(x-\lambda a)=0
.

2.

Each root
\lambda_{i}
is functionally independent with the generators of
\mathcal{F}_{a}
, i.e.

d\lambda_{i}(x)\not\in d\mathcal{F}_{a}(x)

(24)

on an open dense subset of
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
.

Remark 5.3
.

In terms of
[
12
]
, condition (
24
) means that each
\lambda_{i}
is not a core characteristic number.

Remark 5.4
.

Note that we can immediately say that
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{a}
is not complete if in the Jordan–Kronecker invariants of
\mathfrak{g}
not all Jordan blocks are
2\times 2
with distinct eigenvalues (cmp. Corollary
4.7
).

In
[
10
]
the following completeness criterion for the extended Mischenko-Fomenko subalgebras
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{a}
was described.

•

Let
\operatorname{Sing}\subset\mathfrak{g}^{*}
be the set of singular elements and
\operatorname{Sing}_{0}
be the union of all irreducible components of
\operatorname{Sing}
that have dimension
\dim\mathfrak{g}-1

•

Consider the subset

\operatorname{Sing}_{b}=\left\{y\in\operatorname{Sing}_{0}\,\,\bigr|\,\,\mathfrak{g}_{y}\simeq\operatorname{aff}(1)\oplus\mathbb{C}^{\operatorname{ind}\mathfrak{g}}\right\}\subset\operatorname{Sing}_{0},

where
\operatorname{aff}(1)
is the 2-dimensional non-abelian Lie algebra and

\mathfrak{g}_{y}=\left\{\xi\in\mathfrak{g}\,\,\bigr|\,\,\operatorname{ad}^{*}_{\xi}(x)=0\right\}.

(
[

Let

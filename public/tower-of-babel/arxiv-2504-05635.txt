1.
Introduction and main results

1.1.
Introduction

We consider the following nonhomogeneous fourth order Schrödinger operator

H=H_{0}+V(x),\ H_{0}=\Delta^{2}-\Delta,\ \ x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n},\ n\geq 5.

Here
V(x)
is a real-valued potential satisfying
|V(x)|\lesssim(1+|x|)^{-\beta}
with some
\beta>0
. For convenience, we use the notation
\langle x\rangle:=(1+|x|).

The considered operator arises from the following fourth order Schrödinger equation:

i\partial_{t}u+\epsilon\Delta^{2}u-\Delta u+|u|^{p}u=0,\hskip 10.00002ptu:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{C},\hskip 10.00002pt\epsilon\in\mathbb{R}.

(
1.1)

Equation
(​​

1.1
)
was introduced by Karpman
[
26
,
27
]
and Karpman and Shagalov
[
28
]
to account for the role of small fourth order dispersion terms in the propagation of intense laser beams in a bulk medium with Kerr nonlinearity. When
\epsilon=0
,
n=2
, and
p=1
, this corresponds to the canonical model. For
2<np<4
, Karpman and Shagalov
[
28
]
showed, among other things, that the waveguides induced by the nonlinear Schrödinger equation become stable when
|\epsilon|
is taken sufficiently large.
The study of nonhomogeneous fourth-order equations has garnered increasing interest from researchers (see
e.g.

[
20
,
25
,
34
,
36
,
37
,
38
]
and references therein).

By applying the scaling transformation
U_{\epsilon}
defined by

\big(U_{\epsilon}u\big)(x)=|\epsilon|^{-\frac{n}{4}}u\big(|\epsilon|^{-\frac{1}{2}}x\big),
it is straightforward to verify that
\epsilon\Delta^{2}-\Delta=\epsilon^{-1}U_{\epsilon}\big(\Delta^{2}-\Delta\big)U_{\epsilon}^{-1}
if
\epsilon>0
and
\epsilon\Delta^{2}-\Delta=\epsilon^{-1}U_{\epsilon}\big(\Delta^{2}+\Delta\big)U_{\epsilon}^{-1}
if
\epsilon<0.
Hence, for a fixed
\epsilon\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\{0\},

the study of
\epsilon\Delta^{2}-\Delta
can be reduced to that of
\Delta^{2}\pm\Delta
.

Compared to the operator
\Delta^{2}-\Delta
, which has only one threshold at
0
, the nonhomogeneous fourth-order operator
\Delta^{2}+\Delta
features two distinct thresholds:
0
and
-\frac{1}{4}
(the latter being degenerate). It is noteworthy that analyzing the operator
\Delta^{2}+\Delta+V
can present greater challenges in the presence of the potential
V
.
In this paper, our primary focus is on studying the
L^{p}
-boundedness of the wave operators
W_{\pm}(H,H_{0})
, where
H_{0}=\Delta^{2}-\Delta
. Additionally, we aim to investigate the more intricate operator
\Delta^{2}+\Delta
in other work.

Now let’s recall that (see e.g. Kuroda
[
29
]
) as
\beta>1
,
the wave operators

\displaystyle W_{\pm}=W_{\pm}(H,H_{0}):=\slim_{t\to\pm\infty}e^{itH}e^{-itH_{0}}

(
1.2)

exist as partial isometries on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and are asymptotically complete, namely the range of
W_{\pm}
coincides with the absolutely continuous spectral subspace
{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathop{\mathrm{ac}}\nolimits}(H)
of
H
. In particular,
W_{\pm}
satisfy the identities

W_{\pm}W_{\pm}^{*}=P_{\mathop{\mathrm{ac}}\nolimits}(H)
,
W_{\pm}^{*}W_{\pm}=I

and exhibit
the intertwining property

f(H)W_{\pm}=W_{\pm}f(H_{0})
for any Borel measurable function
f
on
\mathbb{R}
,
where
W_{\pm}^{*}
denotes the dual operator of
W_{\pm}
and
P_{\mathop{\mathrm{ac}}\nolimits}(H)
denotes the projection onto
{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathop{\mathrm{ac}}\nolimits}(H).
These formulas, in particular, imply

\displaystyle f(H)P_{\mathop{\mathrm{ac}}\nolimits}(H)=W_{\pm}f(H_{0})W_{\pm}^{*}.

(
1.3)

We refer to
[
35
]
for a detailed description on the mathematical scattering theory. Based on the equality
(​​

1.3
)
, the
L^{p}
-boundedness of
W_{\pm}
and
W_{\pm}^{*}
can be directly employed to establish the
L^{p}
-
L^{q}
estimates for the perturbed operator
f(H)
from the same estimates for the free operator
f(H_{0})
. This can be seen as follows:

\displaystyle\|f(H)P_{\mathop{\mathrm{ac}}\nolimits}(H)\|_{L^{p}\to L^{q}}\leq\|W_{\pm}\|_{L^{q}\to L^{q}}\ \|f(H_{0})\|_{L^{p}\to L^{q}}\|W_{\pm}^{*}\|_{L^{p}\to L^{p}}.

(
1.4)

Actually, there has been extensive research on the
L^{p}
-boundedness of wave operators associated with homogeneous Schrödinger operators
-\Delta+V(x)
and
\Delta^{2}+V(x)
(see further related results below). However, only a few studies have addressed nonhomogeneous type Schrödinger operators
H
. Therefore, this study aims to contribute to filling some gap.

In this paper, we demonstrate that the wave operators
W_{\pm}(H,H_{0})\in\mathbb{B}(L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}))
for all
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
n\geq 5
when zero is a regular point of
H
(see Theorem
1.2
). As applications, we derive the
L^{p}
-
L^{p^{\prime}}
estimates for the Schrödinger group
e^{-itH}

and for the solutions to beam equation (see Corollaries
1.7
and
1.9
). In particular, it is worth noting that the same results hold for the perturbed operator
\epsilon\Delta^{2}-\Delta+V
with a parameter
\epsilon>0,

providing greater flexibility for the analysis of related equations.

We emphasize that the proofs of these results heavily rely on the iteration of the Born series to deal with singularities. This approach incorporates techniques inspired by the works of Yajima
[
41
]
and Erdoğan and Green
[
10
,
11
]
.

1.3.
Main results

Before presenting our main results, we firstly introduce the regular zero spectral condition of
H
. Let
v(x)=|V(x)|^{1/2}
and
U(x)=\mathop{\mathrm{sgn}}\nolimits V(x)
, i.e.
U(x)=1
if
V(x)\geq 0
and
U(x)=-1
if
V(x)<0
. Denote
R_{0}(0)=(-\Delta)^{-1}-(-\Delta+1)^{-1}.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
n\geq 5
and
H=\Delta^{2}-\Delta+V.
Define
T_{0}:=U+vR_{0}(0)v.
We say that zero is a
regular threshold
of
H
if
T_{0}
is invertible on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Next, we state our main results.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
n\geq 5
and
V(x)
be a real-valued potential on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
satisfying
|V(x)|\lesssim\left\langle x\right\rangle^{-\beta}
with
\beta>n+5.
For a fixed
0<\sigma\ll 1,
there exists a constant

C(n,\sigma)
such that

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\big\|\langle\cdot\rangle^{\frac{9-n}{2}+\sigma+}V\big\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim C(n,\sigma)&{\rm if}\ \ n=5,6,\\
\big\|{\mathcal{F}}\big(\langle\cdot\rangle^{\frac{2(n-4+\sigma)}{n-1}+2\sigma+}V\big)\big\|_{L^{\frac{n-1}{n-4+\sigma}}}\lesssim C(n,\sigma)&{\rm if}\ \ n\geq 7.\end{cases}

(
1.5)

Assume that
H=\Delta^{2}-\Delta+V
has no positive embedded eigenvalue and zero is a regular point of
H
. Then the wave operators
W_{\pm}
defined by
(​​

1.2
)
can be extended to into bounded operators on
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
for all
1\leq p\leq\infty.

Several remarks are given as follows:

Remark 1.3
.

For fixed
\epsilon>0
, let
V_{\epsilon}(x)=\epsilon V(\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}x)
,
\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}=\epsilon\Delta^{2}-\Delta+V
and
H_{\epsilon}=\Delta^{2}-\Delta+V_{\epsilon}
.
Consider the scaling unitary transformation
U_{\epsilon}
defined by

(U_{\epsilon}u)(x)=\epsilon^{-\frac{n}{4}}u(\epsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}}x),\ \ u\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}).

A direct computation shows that

U_{\epsilon}^{-1}\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}U_{\epsilon}=\epsilon^{-1}H_{\epsilon},

establishing the unitary equivalence between
\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}
and
\epsilon^{-1}H_{\epsilon}
. Then their corresponding wave operators admit the following equivalent relationship:

\displaystyle W_{\pm}(\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon},\epsilon\Delta^{2}-\Delta)=\operatorname*{s-lim}_{t\to\pm\infty}e^{it\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}}e^{-it(\epsilon\Delta^{2}-\Delta)}

\displaystyle=U_{\epsilon}\left(\operatorname*{s-lim}_{t\to\pm\infty}e^{it\epsilon^{-1}H_{\epsilon}}e^{-it\epsilon^{-1}(\Delta^{2}-\Delta)}\right)U_{\epsilon}^{-1}

\displaystyle=U_{\epsilon}\ W_{\pm}(H_{\epsilon},\Delta^{2}-\Delta)\ U_{\epsilon}^{-1}.

(
1.6)

Assuming that
V_{\epsilon}
and
H_{\epsilon}=\Delta^{2}-\Delta+V_{\epsilon}
satisfy the same conditions in Theorem
1.2
for each
\epsilon>0
,
then it follows from Theorem
1.2
and
(​​

1.6
)
that the wave operators
W_{\pm}(\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon},\epsilon\Delta^{2}-\Delta)
are bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
1\leq p\leq\infty
and dimensions
n\geq 5,
and

\|W_{\pm}(\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon},\epsilon\Delta^{2}-\Delta)\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\rightarrow L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}=\|W_{\pm}(H_{\epsilon},\Delta^{2}-\Delta)\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\rightarrow L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}.

Remark 1.4
.

For the homogeneous fourth-order operator
H=\Delta^{2}+V
under the same condition
(​​

1.5
)
, Erdoğan and Green
[
10
,
11
]
established that
W_{\pm}(H,\Delta^{2})\in\mathbb{B}(L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
for all
1\leq p\leq\infty
in the regular case when
n\geq 5
. It is important to note that the dimensional restriction

is necessary, as
W_{\pm}(H,\Delta^{2})
is generally unbounded for
p=1,\infty
even when
V
is compactly supported, if
n\leq 4
(see, e.g., Mizutani–Wan–Yao
[
31
,
32
]
for counterexamples in dimensions

However, since the low-energy behavior of the non-homogeneous fourth-order operator
H=\Delta^{2}-\Delta+V
resembles that of
-\Delta+V
, the dimensional threshold in Theorem
1.2
may be reduced to
n\geq 3
with suitable modifications to certain proofs. Furthermore, we expect that the wave operators for
H=\Delta^{2}-\Delta+V
remain bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
1\leq p<\frac{n}{2}
if zero is an eigenvalue of
H
(see, e.g.,
[
17
,
45
]
for analogous results of
-\Delta+V
).

Remark 1.5
.

For
n\geq 7
, since the index
\frac{n-1}{n-4+\sigma}<2
, the potential condition
(​​

1.5
)
essentially imposes a regularity requirement on

. In fact, Erdoğan, Goldberg, and Green
[
7
]
showed that the wave operators for

are unbounded on

for

lacks sufficient smoothness, even when

is compactly supported. Therefore, we believe that an appropriate regularity condition on the potential

is necessary for the results in Theorem

.

Remark 1.6
.

In contrast to
\Delta^{2}-\Delta
, which has a single threshold at

, the non-homogeneous fourth-order operator
\Delta^{2}+\Delta
presents two distinct thresholds:

and

(the latter being degenerate), which complicates the analysis. Furthermore, in the proof of Theorem

, we need the symbol function

as defined in
(​​

)
. However, for
\Delta^{2}+\Delta
, the corresponding function
\left(|\xi-k|^{2}+|\xi|^{2}-1\right)^{-1}
changes sign, which leads to an additional obstacle of technics if we study the “+” case.

1.5.
Further related results

The
L^{p}
-boundedness of wave operators for homogeneous Schrödinger type operators
H=(-\Delta)^{m}+V(x)
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
with
m\geq 1
, has been extensively studied in the literature. More specifically:

For the classical Schrödinger operator
-\Delta+V(x)
(i.e.
m=1
), since the seminal work by Yajima in
[
41
]
, there has been a significant body of works focused on the
L^{p}
-boundedness of the wave operators
W_{\pm}
. In particular, in the one-dimensional space (
n=1
), the wave operators
W_{\pm}
are bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
for
1<p<\infty
in both regular and zero resonance cases. However, they are generally unbounded for
p=1
and
p=\infty
(see, for example,
[
2
,
5
,
39
]
).
In the regular case, for dimension
n=2
, the wave operators
W_{\pm}
are bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for
1<p<\infty
, but the results at the endpoints are yet unknown (see
[
43
,
23
]
). For dimensions
n\geq 3
, the wave operators
W_{\pm}
are bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
1\leq p\leq\infty
in the regular case (see, for example,
[
3
,
42
,
41
]
). However, the presence of threshold resonances narrows down the range of values for
p
, depending on the dimension
n
and asymptotic behaviors of zero resonant states and zero eigenfunctions (as discussed in
[
6
,
14
,
17
,

1.6.
The outline of proof

Here, we provide a brief outline of the main ideas involved in the proof of Theorem
1.2
.
The starting point is the following stationary formula:

\displaystyle W_{\pm}=\mathop{\mathrm{Id}}\nolimits-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{\infty}R_{V}^{\mp}(\lambda)V\left(R_{0}^{+}(\lambda)-R_{0}^{-}(\lambda)\right)d\lambda.

(
1.10)

To explain the formula
(​​

1.10
)
, we need to introduce some notations. Let

\displaystyle R_{\Delta}(z)=(-\Delta-z)^{-1},\ \ R_{0}(z)=(\Delta^{2}-\Delta-z)^{-1},\ \ R_{V}(z)=(H-z)^{-1},\hskip 10.00002ptz\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus[0,\infty),

be the resolvents of operators
-\Delta,

H_{0}=\Delta^{2}-\Delta
and
H=\Delta^{2}-\Delta+V(x)
, respectively. Then
R^{\pm}_{\Delta}(\lambda),

R^{\pm}_{0}(\lambda)
and
R^{\pm}_{V}(\lambda)
are defined as their proper boundary values (limiting resolvents) on
(0,\infty)
, namely for
\lambda>0,

\displaystyle R_{\Delta}^{\pm}(\lambda)=\lim_{{\varepsilon}\searrow 0}R_{\Delta}(\lambda\pm i{\varepsilon}),\ \ R^{\pm}_{0}(\lambda)=\lim_{\epsilon\searrow 0}R_{0}(\lambda\pm i\epsilon),\ \ R_{V}^{\pm}(\lambda)=\lim_{\epsilon\searrow 0}R_{V}(\lambda\pm i\epsilon).

(
1.11)

More specifically, the existence of
R^{\pm}_{0}(\lambda)
as bounded operators from
L^{2}_{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
to
L^{2}_{-s}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
with
s>1/2
follows from the limiting absorption principle for the resolvent
R_{\Delta}(z)
(see e.g. Agmon
[
1
]
) and the following splitting identity for
z\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus[0,+\infty)
with
0<\arg z<2\pi:

\displaystyle R_{0}(z)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+4z}}\left(R_{\Delta}\Big(-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{1+4z}\Big)-R_{\Delta}\Big(-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{1+4z}\Big)\right).

(
1.12)

The existence of
R^{\pm}_{V}(\lambda)
for
\lambda>0
with certain decay conditions on the potential has been also already shown (see e.g.
[
1
,
29
]
).

Combining with
(​​

1.10
)
and iterations of the resolvent identity,

R_{V}^{-}(\lambda)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{2k-1}(-1)^{\ell}(R_{0}^{-}(\lambda)V)^{\ell}R_{0}^{-}(\lambda)+(R_{0}^{-}(\lambda)V)^{k}R_{V}^{-}(\lambda)(VR_{0}^{-}(\lambda))^{k},

it follows that

\mathop{\mathrm{Id}}\nolimits-W_{\pm}=\sum_{j=1}^{2k}(-1)^{J-1}W_{J}^{\pm}+\Omega^{\pm},

where

\displaystyle W_{J}^{\pm}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{\infty}(R_{0}^{\mp}(\lambda)V)^{J}\big(R_{0}^{+}(\lambda)-R_{0}^{-}(\lambda)\big)d\lambda,\ \ \ \text{for}\ J=1,2,\cdots,2k.

(
1.13)

\displaystyle\Omega^{\pm}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{\infty}(R_{0}^{\mp}(\lambda)V)^{k}R_{V}^{\mp}(\lambda)(VR_{0}^{\mp}(\lambda))^{k}V\big(R_{0}^{+}(\lambda)-R_{0}^{-}(\lambda)\big)d\lambda.

(
1.14)

By changing the variable
\lambda\mapsto\eta^{4}+\eta^{2}
and inserting the identity
1=\chi(\eta)+\widetilde{\chi}(\eta),
the operator
\Omega^{\pm}
is further decomposed the sum of the low energy part
\Omega_{L}^{\pm}
with
\{0\leq\eta\ll 1\}
and the high energy part
\Omega_{H}^{\pm}
with
\{\eta\gtrsim 1\}.

Combining with
W_{J}^{-}f=\overline{W_{J}^{+}\bar{f}}
and
\Omega^{+}f=\overline{\Omega^{-}\bar{f}},
all the problems boil down to demonstrate
W_{J}^{+}
(
J\in{\mathbb{N}}^{+}
),
\Omega_{L}^{-}
and
\Omega_{H}^{-}\in\mathbb{B}(L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}))
for all
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
n\geq 5.

\bullet

The
L^{p}
-boundedness of
W_{J}^{+}
.
For simplicity, we consider the following adjoint operator
W_{J}^{*}
, instead of the operator
W_{J}^{+}
:

W_{J}^{*}=-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathbb{R}}R_{0}^{-}(\lambda)\big(VR_{0}^{+}(\lambda)\big)^{J}\,d\lambda.

In order to derive the desired result for
W_{J}^{*}
, by density, we may assume that
V_{1},\cdots,V_{J}\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
such that their Fourier transform
\widehat{V_{1}},\cdots,\widehat{V_{J}}\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),
then it suffices to show for
f\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),
the
limit

Z_{J}f:=\lim_{\epsilon_{1}\downarrow 0}\cdots\lim_{\epsilon_{J}\downarrow 0}\lim_{\epsilon_{0}\downarrow 0}\ \frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathbb{R}}R_{0}(\lambda-i\epsilon_{0})\ \prod_{j=1}^{J}\big(V_{j}R_{0}(\lambda+i\epsilon_{j})\big)fd\lambda,

(
1.15)

exists in the strong topology of
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
and
Z_{J}
can be extended to a bounded operator on
L^{p}
for all
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
J\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
with the proper bounds.

Set
(\omega_{j},t_{j},y_{j})\in\big(S^{n-1}\times(0,+\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\big)
for
j=1,2,\cdots,J-1,
and
(\omega_{J},t_{J},y_{J})\in\big(S^{n-1}\times(0,\rho_{J})\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\big)
with
\rho_{J}:=-\omega_{J}\big(x-y_{J}-\sum_{\ell=1}^{J-1}(y_{\ell}-2t_{\ell}\omega_{\ell})\big),
then

Z_{J}f(x)
can be expressed as

\displaystyle Z_{J}f(x)=\int_{\Sigma_{J-1}}\int_{S^{n-1}}\int_{-\infty}^{\rho_{J}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}F(\omega_{1},t_{1},y_{1},\cdots,\omega_{J},t_{J},y_{J})f(\bar{x}-\gamma_{J})dy_{J}dt_{J}d\omega_{J}\cdots dy_{1}dt_{1}d\omega_{1},

where
\bar{x}:=x-2(x\cdot\omega_{J})
and
\Sigma_{J-1}:=\big(S^{n-1}\times(0,+\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\big)^{J-1},

\gamma_{J}:=\bar{y}_{J}+2t_{J}\omega_{J}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{J-1}(\overline{y_{\ell}-2t_{\ell}\omega_{\ell}}).
Notably,
F(\omega_{1},t_{1},y_{1},\cdots,\omega_{J},t_{J},y_{J})\in L^{1}\big(\left(S^{n-1}\times{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\right)^{J}\big)

\displaystyle\left\|F\right\|_{L^{1}\big(\left(S^{n-1}\times{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\right)^{J}\big)}\lesssim_{n,\sigma,J}\begin{cases}\prod_{j=1}^{J}\ \left\|\langle\cdot\rangle^{\frac{9-n}{2}+\sigma+}V_{j}\right\|_{L^{2}}&{\rm if}\ \ n=5,6,\\

Apply Minkowski’s inequality and note that

is an isometry, then we obtain that

for all
1\leq p\leq\infty,

which yields the desired results.

\bullet

The
L^{p}
-boundedness of
\Omega_{L}^{-}
.
Setting
v(x)=\sqrt{|V(x)|}
,
U(x)=\mathop{\mathrm{sgn}}\nolimits V(x)
and
M(\eta)=U+vR_{0}^{+}(\eta^{4}+\eta^{2})v
, one has the symmetric second resolvent equation:

R_{V}^{+}(\eta^{4}+\eta^{2})V=R_{0}^{+}(\eta^{4}+\eta^{2})vM^{-1}(\eta)v,

where
M^{-1}(\eta):=[M(\eta)]^{-1}
as long as it exists.

Denote

A(\eta):=\big(R_{0}^{+}(\eta^{4}+\eta^{2})V\big)^{k-1}R_{0}^{+}(\eta^{4}+\eta^{2}),\ \text{and}\ \Gamma(\eta):=wA(\eta)vM^{-1}(\eta)vA(\eta)w

with
w=V^{\frac{1}{2}}
, then the low energy part

can be expressed as

\displaystyle\Omega_{L}^{-}:=\frac{1}{\pi i}\int_{0}^{\infty}\eta(2\eta^{2}+1)\chi(\eta)R_{0}^{+}(\eta^{4}+\eta^{2})v\Gamma(\eta)v\Big(R_{0}^{+}-R_{0}^{-}\Big)(\eta^{4}+\eta^{2})d\eta.

(
1.16)

Let
|V(x)|\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{-n-4-}.
Then we can show that

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Gamma}(x,y)=\max_{\ell\in\big\{0,1,\cdots,\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil+1\big\}}\sup_{0<\eta\ll 1}\Big|\eta^{\max(0,\ell-1)}\partial_{\eta}^{\ell}\Gamma(\eta)(x,y)\Big|\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{-\frac{n}{2}-}\langle y\rangle^{-\frac{n}{2}-},

(
1.17)

where
\Gamma(\eta)(x,y)
is the kernel of the operator
\Gamma(\eta).

Applying the expression
(​​

1.16
)
and the condition
(​​

1.17
)
, it can be shown that
\Omega_{L}^{-}
is admissible, i.e. its kernel
\Omega_{L}^{-}(x,y)
satisfies

\Omega_{L}^{-}(x,y)\in L^{\infty}_{y}L^{1}_{x}\cap L^{\infty}_{x}L^{1}_{y},

which yields the desired results.

\bullet

The
L^{p}
-boundedness of
\Omega_{H}^{-}
.

By iterations and integrating by parts
N=\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil+1
times with respect to
\eta
for the kernel
\Omega_{H}^{-}(x,y)
, provided
\beta>2N+2,
it can be proved that

\displaystyle\Big|\Omega_{H}^{-}(x,y)\Big|\lesssim\big\langle x\big\rangle^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}\big\langle y\big\rangle^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}\big\langle|x|\pm|y|\big\rangle^{-N}\in L^{\infty}_{y}L^{1}_{x}\cap L^{\infty}_{x}L^{1}_{y},

which establishes the desired results.

Lemma A.1

(

30

)
.

Let

The next two Lemmas

-

Lemma A.2

(

, Lemma 3.8]
)

Lemma A.3

(
[
9

)
.

Below, we provide a sufficient condition to ensure that the operator is admissible, which is used in the proof of Proposition

Lemma A.4
.

Let

and

Then the operator

\displaystyle K(x,y):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}}\frac{\langle z_{1}\rangle^{-n-}\langle z_{2}\rangle^{-n-}\chi_{|y-z_{2}|\gg\langle x-z_{1}\rangle}}{|x-z_{1}|^{\alpha}|y-z_{2}|^{k}}dz_{1}dz_{2}

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\big|K(x,y)\big|dy+\sup_{y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\big|K(x,y)\big|dx<+\infty.

Proof.

Let

We can check that

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\big|K(x,y)\big|dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}}\frac{\langle z_{1}\rangle^{-n-}\langle z_{2}\rangle^{-n-}}{r_{1}^{\alpha}}\int_{\langle r_{1}\rangle}^{\infty}\frac{1}{r^{k-n+1}}drdz_{1}dz_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}}\frac{\langle z_{1}\rangle^{-n-}\langle z_{2}\rangle^{-n-}}{r_{1}^{\alpha}\langle r_{1}\rangle^{k-n}}dz_{1}dz_{2}\lesssim 1.

Similarly, we conclude that

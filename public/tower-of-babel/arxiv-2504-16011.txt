Asian Basket Spread Options: A New Approximation Based on Stochastic Taylor Expansions Thanks: Date: April 23, 2025.
Fabien Le Floc’h

Abstract
We present closed analytical approximations for the pricing of Asian basket spread options under the Black-Scholes model. The formulae are obtained by using a stochastic Taylor expansion around a log-normal proxy model and are found to be highly accurate for Asian and spread options in practice. Unlike other approaches, they do not require any numerical integration or root solving.



keywords

Stochastic expansion, Asian option, basket option, spread option, Black-Scholes, arithmetic averaging.

1
Introduction

The buyer of a vanilla basket spread option of strike
K
and maturity
T
on underlying assets
(S_{i})_{i=1,...,m}
receives at maturity
T
the payoff

V(T)=\max\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}w_{i}S_{i}(T)-K,0\right)

(1)

for a call and
\max\left(K-\sum_{i=1}^{m}w_{i}S_{i}(T),0\right)
for a put. While for a vanilla basket option the weights
w_{i}
are positive and the strike price obeys
K>0
, for a spread option the weights
w_{i}
are negative for
i=1,...,m_{p}-1
, positive for
i=m_{p},...,n
and are usually chosen such that

\sum_{i=1}^{m_{p}-1}w_{i}=-1
and
\sum_{i=m_{p}}^{n}w_{i}=1
. The strike price
K
may be positive or negative.
Those are not so common in the domain of foreign exchange or equity derivatives (with
m>2
). The most popular example, the so-called
crack
spread stems from commodity derivatives. The portfolio of the 3:2:1 crack spread reads
3S_{3}-2S_{2}-S_{1}
, where
S_{3},S_{2}
and
S_{1}
are respectively the prices of crude oil, gasoline and heating oil. Other commodities such as soybean or cattle may also involve spreads on three different future contracts.

The buyer of an Asian call spread option of strike
K
and averaging dates
(t_{j})_{j=1,...,n}
on an asset
S
receives

V(T)=\max\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}S(t_{j})-K,0\right)\,.

(2)

Those are sometimes traded in equity and foreign exchange spaces. Under the Black-Scholes model with a term-structure of interest rates, dividend yields and volatilities, Asian spread options with discrete arithmetic averaging can be reduced to an equivalent vanilla basket spread option with a specific correlation structure.

Asian basket spread options combine both features: each underlying asset is averaged, typically over the same schedule and with the same weights, the payoff at maturity reads

V(T)=\max\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{n}w^{B}_{i}w^{A}_{j}S_{i}(t_{j})-K,0\right)\,,

(3)

where
\bm{w}^{A}
are positive Asianing weights and typically sum to 1, while
\bm{w}^{B}
are basket weights of mixed sign.
Asian basket spread options are mostly traded by commodity traders.

In the context of the Black-Scholes model, an early and popular approximation for prices of vanilla spread options (for
m=2
), is due to
Kirk and Aron (1995)
. There exists however a simple semi-analytical exact formula based on a one-dimensional integral
(
Pearson, 1999
)
, which should be preferred nowadays. As the number of assets
m
(or the number of Asianing dates) increases, the integral formulation becomes less practical.
Castellacci and Siclari (2003)
combined the approximation of the arithmetic mean by a geometric mean (originally used by
Vorst (1992)
for vanilla Asian options) with the exchange option formula of
Margrabe (1978)
to obtain a simple approximation for the prices of Asian basket spread options.
Deelstra et al. (2010)
found approximations based on comonotonic bounds, which necessitate a one dimensional integration but compare favorably to the three moment matching shifted lognormal approximation of
Borovkova et al. (2007)
. More recently,
Pellegrino (2016)
derived more accurate approximations, which may be seen as an extension of
Bjerksund and Stensland (2014)
.

Here, we consider a Black-Scholes setting with time-dependent parameters, and propose a stochastic Taylor expansion using Margrabe exchange option formula on the geometric averages as a proxy, following the ideas of
Etoré and Gobet (2012)
, who applied their expansion to price vanilla options with discrete dividends under the piecewise-lognormal model. It may be seen as a generalization of our previous work regarding vanilla basket and Asian options in
(
Le Floc’h, 2024
)
. The resulting formula does not involve any numerical integration or root solving, thus offering computational speed and facilitating hedges calculations (for example using the option greeks). We start by deriving the proxy, the first-, second- and third-order expansions and then evaluate their accuracy on a sample of previously published cases. This allows for a comparison with other accurate approximations.

2
Reductions to vanilla basket spread options

2.1
From Asian to basket

Let
F(0,t)
be the forward price of the underlying asset from valuation time to the date
t
. In the Black-Scholes model with time-dependent parameters we have

F(0,t)=S(0)e^{\int_{0}^{t}r(s)-q(s)\mathop{}\!\kern 0.0pt\mathrm{d}s}\,,

with
r,q
being the instantateneous interest rate and dividend yield and
S(t)
the underlying asset spot at time
t
. Under the risk-neutral measure
\mathbb{Q}
, the forward price follows

\mathop{}\!\kern 0.0pt\mathrm{d}F(t,T)=F(t,T)\sigma(t)\mathop{}\!\kern 0.0pt\mathrm{d}W(t)\,,

where
W
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-Brownian motion.

The buyer of an arithmetic Asian option of strike
K
and maturity
T
on an underlying
S
with observation dates
(t_{i})_{i=1,...,n}
with
t_{n}=T
, receives at maturity
\max\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}S(t_{i})-K\right)
for a call and
\max\left(K-\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}S(t_{i})\right)
for a put. It may be expressed as a basket option of strike
K
and maturity
T
on
n
underlying assets
(S_{i})_{i=1,...,n}
. The covariance between the assets must match the covariance of
S
between different observation times, this means
1
1

1

We use the Itô isometry to obtain Equations
4
and
6
.

\rho_{i,j}\sqrt{v_{i}v_{j}}=v_{i\wedge j}\,,

(4)

where
\rho_{i,j}
denotes the correlation between the assets
S_{i}
and
S_{j}
,
v_{i}=\int_{0}^{t_{i}}\sigma^{2}(s)\mathop{}\!\kern 0.0pt\mathrm{d}s
being the total variance up to time
t_{i}
for the asset
S
.
Each underlying asset forward to
T
must match the forwards of the single asset to
t_{i}
, meaning

F_{i}(0,T)=\mathbb{E}[S_{i}(T)]=\mathbb{E}[S(t_{i})]=F(0,t_{i})=S(0)e^{\int_{0}^{t_{i}}(r(s)-q(s))\mathop{}\!\kern 0.0pt\mathrm{d}s}\,.

(5)

Thus, the arithmetic Asian option on
S
with observations
t_{i}
corresponds to a vanilla basket option on
S_{i}
with forwards to maturity
F_{i}(0,T)
, total variances
v_{i}
and correlations
\rho_{i,j}
for
i,j=1,...,n
.

2.2
Asian baskets

This may be extended to Asian baskets with payoff at maturity

\max\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\mu_{j}S_{j}(t_{i})-K,0\right)\,,

where
(\mu_{j})_{j=1...m}
are basket weights and
S_{j}
are the different assets with correlation
\rho_{j,l}
. We may define
n\times m
assets
\hat{S}_{i+n(j-1)}
with forward
F_{j}(t_{i})
, total variance
\int_{0}^{t_{i}}\sigma_{j}^{2}(s)\mathop{}\!\kern 0.0pt\mathrm{d}s
, correlation

\hat{\rho}_{i+n(j-1),k+n(l-1)}=\rho_{j,l}\frac{\int_{0}^{t_{i}\wedge t_{k}}\sigma_{j}(s)\sigma_{l}(s)\mathop{}\!\kern 0.0pt\mathrm{d}s}{\sqrt{\int_{0}^{t_{i}}\sigma_{j}^{2}(s)\mathop{}\!\kern 0.0pt\mathrm{d}s\int_{0}^{t_{k}}\sigma_{l}^{2}(s)\mathop{}\!\kern 0.0pt\mathrm{d}s}}\,,

(6)

for
(i,k)\in\{1,...,n\}^{2}
,
(j,l)\in\left\{1,...,m\right\}^{2}
. The Asian basket option is equivalent to a vanilla basket option on
\hat{S}_{i+n(j-1)}
with weights
w_{i}\mu_{j}
.

2.3
Strike

The strike
K
may be included in the spread sum. In the context of an Asian, it is equivalent to an asian observation at
t=0
with weight
-K/S(0)
.
In the context of a basket, it is equivalent to an additional asset of spot price
K
with zero volatility and drift, weight
-1
, and correlation zero towards the other assets.

3
Expansions

3.1
The proxy

An early technique, first explored by
Vorst (1992)
to estimate the price of Asian options is to approximate the arithmetic average by the geometric average, because the geometric average of geometric Brownian motions is another geometric Brownian motion and can thus be priced with the usual Black-Scholes formula. This was extended to the more general cases of vanilla basket options by
Gentle (1993)
.

Under the risk-neutral measure
\mathbb{Q}
, the forward price for each asset follows

\mathop{}\!\kern 0.0pt\mathrm{d}F_{i}(t,T)=F_{i}(t,T)\sigma_{i}(t)\mathop{}\!\kern 0.0pt\mathrm{d}W_{i}(t)\,,

where
W_{i}
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-Brownian motion, and we have
\mathop{}\!\kern 0.0pt\mathrm{d}W_{i}\mathop{}\!\kern 0.0pt\mathrm{d}W_{j}=\rho_{i,j}\mathop{}\!\kern 0.0pt\mathrm{d}t
.
The payoff at maturity of the basket spread option where we allow for a multiplicative strike
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}
reads

\displaystyle V(T)

\displaystyle=\left[\eta\left(\sum_{i=m_{p}}^{m}w_{i}S_{i}(T)+\kappa\sum_{i=1}^{m_{p}-1}w_{i}S_{i}(T)\right)\right]^{+}

\displaystyle=A_{p}\left[\eta\left(\sum_{i=m_{p}}^{m}\tilde{a}_{i}S_{i}^{\star}(T)-{\kappa}^{\star}\sum_{i=1}^{m_{p}-1}|\tilde{a}_{i}|S_{i}^{\star}(T)\right)\right]^{+}

with
\eta=\pm 1
for a call (respectively a put) option and

\displaystyle A_{n}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i=1}^{m_{p}-1}w_{i}F_{i}(0,T)\,,\quad A_{p}=\sum_{i=m_{p}}^{m}w_{i}F_{i}(0,T)\,,\quad\kappa^{\star}=\frac{A_{n}}{A_{p}}\kappa\,,

\displaystyle\tilde{a}_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{w_{i}F_{i}(0,T)}{A_{p}(T)}\quad\textmd{ for }i\geq m_{p}\quad\textmd{ and }\quad\tilde{a}_{i}=\frac{w_{i}F_{i}(0,T)}{A_{n}(T)}\quad\textmd{ for }i<m_{p}\,,

\displaystyle S_{i}^{\star}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{S_{i}(t)}{F_{i}(0,t)}=e^{-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\sigma_{i}^{2}(s)\mathop{}\!\kern 0.0pt\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma_{i}(s)\mathop{}\!\kern 0.0pt\mathrm{d}W_{i}(s)}\,.

In particular, we have
\tilde{a}_{i}<0
for
i<m_{p}
, and
A_{n}>0
.
We approximate the arithmetic average by the geometric average leading to

V(0)\approx B(0,T)A\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\eta\left(\prod_{i=m_{p}}^{m}S_{i}^{\star}(T)^{a_{i}}-{\kappa}^{\star}\prod_{i=1}^{m_{p}-1}S_{i}^{\star}(T)^{|a_{i}|}\right)\right)^{+}\right]

(7)

with
a_{i}=\tilde{a}_{i}
as a specific case.

Let

G(T,1,n)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}S_{i}^{\star}(T)^{a_{i}}=e^{\sum_{i=1}^{n}-\frac{1}{2}a_{i}\int_{0}^{T}\sigma_{i}^{2}(s)\mathop{}\!\kern 0.0pt\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{T}a_{i}\sigma_{i}(s)\mathop{}\!\kern 0.0pt\mathrm{d}W_{i}(s)}

We have

\displaystyle m(T,1,n)=\mathbb{E}\left[\ln G(T)\right]

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}v_{i}\,,

(8)

\displaystyle\tilde{\nu}^{2}(T,1,n)=\mathrm{Var}\left[\ln G(T)\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}a_{i}a_{j}\rho_{i,j}\int_{0}^{T}\sigma_{i}(s)\sigma_{j}(s)\mathop{}\!\kern 0.0pt\mathrm{d}s\,.

(9)

with
v_{i}=\int_{0}^{T}\sigma_{i}^{2}(s)\mathop{}\!\kern 0.0pt\mathrm{d}s
. We will also define the total variances of the positive and negative parts

\tilde{\nu}_{n}^{2}(T)=\tilde{\nu}(T,1,m_{p}-1)\,,\quad\tilde{\nu}_{p}^{2}=\tilde{\nu}(T,m_{p},m)\,,\quad\tilde{\nu}^{2}(T)=\tilde{\nu}(T,1,m)\,.

(10)

Instead of adjusting the strike to preserve the expectation of the term inside the max function as in
(
Vorst, 1992
)
, we adjust each geometric average by a factor (respectively
\alpha
and
\beta
) to match the first moment of each corresponding arithmetic average:

V(0)\approx B(0,T)A_{p}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\eta\left(\alpha\prod_{i=m_{p}}^{m}S_{i}^{\star}(T)^{a_{i}}-{\kappa}^{\star}\beta\prod_{i=1}^{m_{p}-1}S_{i}^{\star}(T)^{|a_{i}|}\right)\right)^{+}\right]\,,

(11)

with
\alpha=\frac{1}{\mathbb{E}[G(T,m_{p},m)]}
,
\beta=\mathbb{E}[G(T,1,m_{p}-1)]
and
B(0,T)
is the discount factor to the payment date.

Levy (1992)
matches not only the mean but also the variance of the arithmetic average by a lognormal distribution. We have

\displaystyle\nu_{A_{p}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\ln\left(\mathrm{Var}\left[A_{p}(T)\right]\right)=\ln\left(\sum_{i,j=m_{p}}^{m}a_{i}a_{j}e^{\rho_{i,j}\int_{0}^{T}\sigma_{i}(s)\sigma_{j}(s)\mathop{}\!\kern 0.0pt\mathrm{d}s}\right)\,,

(12)

\displaystyle\nu_{A_{n}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\ln\left(\mathrm{Var}\left[A_{n}(T)\right]\right)=\ln\left(\sum_{i,j=1}^{m_{p}-1}a_{i}a_{j}e^{\rho_{i,j}\int_{0}^{T}\sigma_{i}(s)\sigma_{j}(s)\mathop{}\!\kern 0.0pt\mathrm{d}s}\right)\,.

(13)

We may adjust the
a_{i}
used in the geometric mean such that the variance of the geometric mean matches exactly the one of the arithmetic mean by using

a_{i}=\tilde{a}_{i}\frac{\nu_{A_{p}(T)}}{\tilde{\nu}_{p}(T)}\quad\textmd{ for }i\geq m_{p}\,,\quad\textmd{ and }\quad a_{i}=\tilde{a}_{i}\frac{\nu_{A_{n}}}{\tilde{\nu}_{n}(T)}\quad\textmd{ for }i<m_{p}\,.

(14)

In Identity
1
, we apply a change of measure to reduce Equation
11
to a regular vanilla option pricing problem as in
(
Margrabe, 1978
)
. This leads to

\displaystyle V(0)\approx A_{p}\cdot\mathrm{Black}\left(1,\kappa^{\star},{\nu}^{2},T\right)

(15)

where
\mathrm{Black}(F,K,v,T)
denotes the Black-76 formula applied to the forward
F
, strike
K
and total variance
v
to maturity
T
(Equation
21
) and
\nu=\tilde{\nu}
for the geometric approximation,
\nu
computed from
a_{i}
for the Levy approximation.

We will adopt the Vorst geometric formula with adjusted forward as proxy for our stochastic Taylor expansion. Instead of our normalization factors
\alpha
and
\beta
,
Krekel (2003)
employs an additive shift to adjust the expectation of the whole spread, as does
Vorst (1992)
for regular Asian options. It would lead to slightly more complicated equations and exhibit a worse accuracy.

In the context of standard Asian options (no spread), the Vorst Levy formula corresponds exactly to the Levy approximation, but defines a stochastic process based on the individual underlying asset processes. This will allow to define stochastic expansions based on this alternative proxy as well.

Both approximations have a major advantage compared to a shifted lognormal moment matching approach, such as in
(
Borovkova et al., 2007
)
: each sum of lognormal distributions is approximated by a distribution defined on the positive real line. In contrast, for example in the case of a basket with positive weights, the shifted lognormal moment matching may lead to a positive shift, which will allow for negative values of the basket price, which is not realistic. With weights of mixed signs, the shifted lognormal approximation requires special handling to capture negative skews, as described in
(
Borovkova et al., 2007
)
.

3.2
First order expansion

Let
h(x)=\max(\eta x,0)
, we apply a Taylor expansion of order-1 on
h
:

\mathbb{E}\left[h\left(\sum_{i=m_{p}}^{m}\tilde{a}_{i}S_{i}^{\star}(T)-\kappa^{\star}\sum_{i=1}^{m_{p}-1}|\tilde{a}_{i}|S_{i}^{\star}(T)\right)\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[h\left(G_{p}^{\star}(T)-\kappa^{\star}G_{n}^{\star}(T)\right)\right]\\
+\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{i=m_{p}}^{m}\tilde{a}_{i}S_{i}^{\star}(T)-\kappa^{\star}\sum_{i=1}^{m_{p}-1}|\tilde{a}_{i}|S_{i}^{\star}(T)-G_{p}^{\star}(T)+\kappa^{\star}G_{n}^{\star}(T)\right)h^{\prime}\left(G_{p}^{\star}(T)-\kappa^{\star}G_{n}^{\star}(T)\right)\right]\\
+\mathrm{Error}_{2}(h)

(16)

where
\mathrm{Error}_{2}(h)\leq_{c}2\left(||\sum_{i=m_{p}}^{m}\tilde{a}_{i}S_{i}^{\star}(T)-\kappa^{\star}\sum_{i=1}^{m_{p}-1}|\tilde{a}_{i}|S_{i}^{\star}(T)-G_{p}^{\star}(T)+\kappa^{\star}G_{n}^{\star}(T)||_{3}\right)^{2}
.

Let
\tilde{a}_{i}^{\star}=\tilde{a}_{i}
for
i\geq m_{p}
and
\tilde{a}_{i}^{\star}=\kappa^{\star}\tilde{a}_{i}
for
i<m_{p}
, we may rewrite the second term using a derivative with regards to
\kappa^{\star}
, and interchange derivation and expectation (see
Etoré and Gobet (2012)
) to obtain

\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\tilde{a}_{i}^{\star}S_{i}^{\star}(T)-G_{p}^{\star}(T)+\kappa^{\star}G_{n}^{\star}(T)\right)h^{\prime}\left(G_{p}^{\star}(T)-\kappa^{\star}G_{n}^{\star}(T)\right)\right]\\
=-\frac{\partial}{\partial\kappa^{\star}}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{m}\tilde{a}_{i}^{\star}S_{i}^{\star}(T)-G_{p}^{\star}(T)+\kappa^{\star}G_{n}^{\star}(T)}{G_{n}^{\star}(T)}h\left(G_{p}^{\star}(T)-\kappa^{\star}G_{n}^{\star}(T)\right)\right]

(17)

We use Identities
1
,
2
and
3
to obtain the first order expansion

V_{\textmd{VG1}}=A_{p}\cdot\mathrm{Black}(1,\kappa^{\star},\nu^{2},T)\\
+A_{p}\frac{\partial}{\partial\kappa^{\star}}\mathrm{Black}\left(e^{\nu^{2}},\kappa^{\star},\nu^{2},T\right)-\kappa^{\star}A_{p}\frac{\partial}{\partial\kappa^{\star}}\mathrm{Black}\left(1,\kappa^{\star},\nu^{2},T\right)\\
-A_{p}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tilde{a}_{i}^{\star}\frac{\partial}{\partial\kappa^{\star}}\mathrm{Black}\left(e^{-\frac{\nu_{p}^{2}(T)-\nu_{n}^{2}(T)-\nu^{2}(T)}{2}+\bar{v}_{i}},\kappa^{\star},\nu^{2},T\right)\,.

(18)

with
\bar{v}_{i}
defined in Equation
31
.
The relevant derivatives of the Black-76 formula are given in Appendix
B
. The formula with the Vorst Levy proxy is exactly the same, but uses different
\nu
and
a_{i}
.

Interestingly, while our derivation is different, the first order expansion on the geometric average ”VG1” is the same as the extended Bjersksund Stensland approximation of
Pellegrino (2016)
.

3.3
Second order expansion

The second-order Taylor expansion involves the additional term

\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial{\kappa^{\star}}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\tilde{a}_{i}^{\star}S_{i}^{\star}(T)-G_{p}^{\star}(T)+\kappa^{\star}G_{n}^{\star}(T)\right)^{2}}{{G_{n}^{\star}}(T)}h\left(\frac{G_{p}^{\star}(T)}{G_{n}^{\star}(T)}-\kappa^{\star}\right)\right]

We then use Identities
1
to
6
to find
our formula for the second order expansion:

V_{\textmd{VG2}}=V_{\textmd{VG1}}+\frac{A_{p}e^{\nu^{2}(T)}}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial{\kappa^{\star}}^{2}}\mathrm{Black}\left(e^{2\nu^{2}},\kappa^{\star},\nu^{2},T\right)+\frac{\kappa^{2}A_{p}}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial{\kappa^{\star}}^{2}}\mathrm{Black}\left(1,\kappa^{\star},{\nu}^{2},T\right)\\

(19)

3.4
Third order expansion

The third order expansion adds the term

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{6}\frac{\partial^{3}}{\partial{\kappa^{\star}}^{3}}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\tilde{a}_{i}^{\star}S_{i}^{\star}(T)-G_{p}^{\star}(T)+\kappa^{\star}G_{n}^{\star}(T)\right)^{3}}{{G_{n}^{\star}(T)}^{2}}h\left(\alpha\frac{G_{p}^{\star}(T)}{G_{n}^{\star}(T)}-\kappa^{\star}\right)\right]

5
Conclusion

The second-order expansion is found to be the most accurate, by far, compared to previously published approximations. The third-order expansion goes further beyond, and reaches the limits of our reference quasi Monte-Carlo simulation accuracy for a much lower computational cost. This shows the power of stochastic Taylor expansions: they are very simple and yet lead to surprisingly accurate approximations. Their range of applicability is also great. So far, we have covered discrete cash dividends, Asian options, basket options (to some extent) and Asian basket spread options under the Black-Scholes model with a term-structure of rates and volatilities.

Further research may explore stochastic Taylor expansions with assets under stochastic volatility dynamics. The change of measure may still lead to explicit formulae for models with a known characteristic function.

Appendix A
Black-Scholes identities

Let
\mathrm{Black}(F,K,v,T)
denotes the Black-76 formula applied to the forward
F
, strike
K
and total variance
v
to maturity
T

\mathrm{Black}(F,K,v,T)=\eta B(0,T)\left[F\Phi(\eta d_{1})-K\Phi(\eta d_{2})\right]

(21)

with

.

We also define

which constitutes the basis of our Taylor expansions.

Identity 1
.

Let

(23)

where
\nu=\tilde{\nu}
for the geometric approximation,
\nu
computed from
a_{i}
for the Levy approximation.

Proof.

We have
\mathbb{E}\left[G_{n}^{\star}(T)\right]=1

W_{G_{n}}
being a Brownian motion and

. We interpret

\mathbb{Q}

Appendix B
Derivatives of the Black-76 formula towards the strike

The following formulae are used to compute the expansions in practice:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial K}\mathrm{Black}(F,K,v,T)

\displaystyle=-\eta B(0,T)\Phi(\eta d_{2})\,,

(41)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial K^{2}}\mathrm{Black}(F,K,v,T)

\displaystyle=\frac{B(0,T)}{K\sqrt{v}}\phi(d_{2})\,,

(42)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{3}}{\partial K^{3}}\mathrm{Black}(F,K,v,T)

\displaystyle=\frac{B(0,T)}{K^{2}\sqrt{v}}\left(\frac{\phi(d_{2})}{\sqrt{v}}-1\right)\,,

(43)

where
\Phi
,
\phi
are the cumulative normal distribution function and the normal probability density function.

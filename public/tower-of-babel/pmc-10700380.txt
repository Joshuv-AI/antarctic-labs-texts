Integrated bioinformatics and validation to construct lncRNA-miRNA-mRNA ceRNA network in status epilepticus
Authors: Yaojing Lin, Hanyu Wen, Bin Yang, Chunhua Wang, Risheng Liang
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundStatus epilepticus (SE) is a neurologic emergency with characteristic of prolonged seizure activity. However, the investigation of lncRNA based competing endogenous RNAs (ceRNAs) network in SE still requires further elucidation. This study aims to construct the ceRNA network and uncover the related mechanism in SE.MethodsThe C57BL/6 mice pilocarpine-induced (SE) model was established (i.p. injection, 300 mg/kg) (n = 3). RNA-Sequencing was carried out and identified the differential expressed genes (DEGs). GO annotations, KEGG, and GSEA analysis were performed to study the underlying mechanism and pathways of the DEGs. Further, the protein-protein interaction (PPI) network and ceRNA network were visualized by Cytoscape software. The expression level of differential expressed genes involved in the ceRNA network was detected by qRT-PCR.ResultsA total of 345 DE-mRNAs, 84 DE-lncRNAs, and 5 DE-miRNAs were screened out. Subsequently, the functional analysis suggested that angiogenesis, inflammation, and neuron related biological processes were enriched in SE. The constructed ceRNA network-1 contained 7 up-regulated DE-mRNAs (Arid5a, Adm, Insig1, Midn, Btaf1, Per1, Slc25a25), 1 down-regulated DE-miRNA (mmu-miR-6413), and 1 up-regulated DE-lncRNA (Zmiz1os1). The ceRNA network-2 contained 2 down-regulated DE-mRNAs (Rab27a and Lrp2), 1 up-regulated DE-miRNAs (mmu-miR-139–5p), and 1 down-regulated DE-lncRNA (Gm15883).ConclusionFor the first time this study present the expression profile and potential function of lncRNAs in C57BL/6 mice with SE. These results provided novel insights into the discovery of genetic biomarkers for SE.Keywords: Status epilepticus, ceRNA network, RNA sequencing, Bioinformatics

Introduction

Status epilepticus (SE) is a serious neurologic emergency with characteristic of continuous clinical and/or electrographic seizure activity lasts for more than 30 min without stopping, and the function of the central nervous system does not return to the normal baseline during the interictal period [1]. Without timely treatment, SE might lead to irreversible brain injury due to high fever, circulatory failure or neuronal excitotoxic injury, with high disability rate and mortality [2]. As one of the most common neurological disease with mortality of 13 %–20 %, annual incidence of SE is 9.9–41 per 100,000/year [3,4].Therefore, attention should be pay to the diagnosis and treatment of SE.

Non-coding RNAs (ncRNAs) are reported to serve as a very significant regulator in the pathogenesis of neurological diseases [[5], [6], [7]], which includes long noncoding RNAs (lncRNAs) and microRNAs (miRNAs). LncRNA, as a competitive endogenous RNA (ceRNA), competes with other RNAs for microRNAs (miRNAs) through miRNA response element (MRE), which will affect the regulation of miRNA on target genes and thus affect the progression of disease [8]. Some studies of lncRNA in SE have been reported [9]. Our previous study have revealed that lncRNA TUG1 targeting the miR-421/mTOR axis to suppress neuronal apoptosis in SE rats [10]. However, more specific investigations about the underlying mechanism that lncRNAs serve as ceRNA and their role in SE is further needed. Biomarkers of SE would be of great relevance for clinical and research purposes [11].

Over the past decades, the rapid development of bioinformatic techniques have greatly improved the identification of potential diagnostic and therapeutic biomarkers for disease treatment. For example, a recent transcriptome profiling of intestinal mucosa biopsies identified statistically altered genes and differentially expressed genes in ulcerative colitis, screened out hub genes including CXCL1, IL1B, MMP1, and MMP10, that regulated by NF-κB, and established a competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA) network that mediates the regulation of NF-κB expression, providing a valuable information in understanding the molecular mechanisms of active ulcerative colitis [12]. Another analysis on dataset profiles of myocardial ischemia/reperfusion (MI/R) and normal tissues identified a ceRNA network taht included the lncRNA Xist/miR-133c/Slc30a9 axis to better understand the pathogenesis and progression of MI/R and provide a novel targeted therapy way [13].

The present study aimed to investigate the role and functions of differentially expressed genes (DEGs) in SE by bioinformatics analysis, including differentially expressed (DE)-lncRNAs, DE-miRNAs, and DE-mRNAs. Functional enrichment analysis including GO, KEGG and GSEA were conducted to explore the underlying mechanism and related signaling pathways of SE. What’ more, we screened out the key genes by constructing PPI and ceRNA networks and verified the expression of DEGs involved in ceRNA of SE.

Materials and methods

Animals

A total of 12 eight-week-old male C57BL/6 mice (18–22 g) were placed in a separate cage at 22 °C for a 12-h light-dark cycle and provided with unlimited food and water. All experiments were approved by the Clinical Ethical Committee of Fujian Medical University (No.FJMUIACUC2018-062).

Establishment of SE mice model

Mice were randomized to control and model group with 6 in each group. Twenty hours after intraperitoneal injection of 127 mg/kg lithium chloride, mice were injected with 1 mg/kg scopolamine methyl bromide, followed by 30 mg/kg Pilocarpine (all from SigmaAldrich, St. Louis, Missouri, USA) 15–20 min later. Mice in control group were injected with normal saline. Seizures were observed about 10 min after the SE mouse model was established and assessed according to the Racine seizure classification criteria. Grade 0: no reaction; Grade 1: immobility and hemifacial spasm; Level 2: rhythmic head movement and wet dog shaking; Grade 3: bilateral anterior clonus; Grade 4: anterior clonus and feeding; Level 5: standing and falling [14,15]. Paroxysmal convulsions of grade 4 and above lasted for 70 min, indicating a successful SE mouse model. The animals were then sacrificed (n = 3 per group) to obtain hippocampal tissue and store it in RNA (−20 °C, Qiagen) for further RNA extraction.

RNA sequencing

Total RNA was extracted using TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen) according to the manufacturer's protocol. RNA-seq libraries were constructed using Illumina® VAHTS mrnA-SEQ V2 Library Preparation Kit (NR601, Vazyme) according to manufacturer's instructions. Sequencing was performed using Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform as per manufacturer's instructions.

Identification of DEGs

Differential expression (DE) mRNAs (DE mRNAs), lncRNAs (DE lncRNAs) and miRNAs (DE miRNAs) were analyzed using R software package DEseq2 after the RNA sequence data of SE group and control group were normalized. Its threshold value is adjusted P < 0.05 and multiple change (FC) ≥ 1.5. DemRNAs, demiRNA and delncRNAs are displayed in the form of volcano map and heat map. The heat map and clustering of DEG were drawn by R GGplot 2 software package in R analysis platform [16].

Functional enrichment analysis

GO annotations and KEGG pathway analysis were used to reveal the function of all DE-mRNAs. GO annotation was conducted via DAVID database (https://david.ncifcrf.gov) [17,18]. In order to analyze the functional signaling pathways of differentially expressed genes, clusterProfiler software package was used for KEGG enrichment analysis [19]. These pathways were significantly enriched with p value < 0.05.

Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA)

GSEA was performed to screen the significantly changed pathways [20]. GSEA was performed on the relevant sequencing gene list of our RNA sequencing, and a significantly enriched gene set was identified at the p-value <0.05 cut-off criterion. GSEA diagrams are visualized by limma R software package [21].

The PPI network construction

To evaluate the interacting relationship of DE-mRNAs, we analyzed these genes using the STRING online database (version 10.0, http://string-db.org/). The protein-protein interaction (PPI) network of these DE-mRNAs was visualized using Cytoscape software (version v3.6.0, https://cytoscape.org/) [[22], [23], [24]]. Also, cyctoHubba, a plug-in of Cytoscape, a cytoscape plug-in was used to identify central genes based on maximum clique centrality (MCC) algorithm.

CeRNA network analysis

The miRwalk website (http://mirwalk.umm.uni-heidelberg.de/) was conducted to predict miRNA-mRNA interaction information, while the LncBase Predicted v.2 (http://carolina.imis.athena-innovation.gr/diana_tools/web/index) was performed to predict the lncRNA–miRNA interaction. After that, the miRNA that is regulated for both lncRNA and mRNA was selected, and a ceRNA network was constructed using Cytoscape (version v3.7.2, https://cytoscape.org/).

QRT-PCR

RNA was quantified via SYBR Green and housekeeping gene GAPDH or U6 were used as the internal control. Then, quantitative PCR (qPCR) was performed using SYBR Green PCRMasterMix with a final volume of 20 μL. Primers sequences were synthesized by RiboBio Co., Ltd and presented in Table 1. According to the 2−ΔΔCT method, the relative quantification was determined by normalization to an internal control, where the average value of control sample is used as a calibrator.

Statistical analysis

GraphPad Prism (version 8) was used for the statistical analyses, p-value <0.05 was considered statistically significant. All data in this study are presented as mean ± SEM. An unpaired Student's t-test was used to compare the difference between two groups.

Results

Screening of DEGs of ES

To intuitively investigate the expression changes of DEGs in SE, we carried out the RNA-Sequencing and obtained the gene expression profile of 3 SE and 3 normal samples. The volcano plot demonstrated the distinct cluster distribution of these DE-mRNAs in normal and SE samples. As presented in Fig. 1A, a total of 345 DE-mRNAs were identified. The heatmap showed 181 upregulated and 164 downregulated DE-mRNAs were identified (Fig. 1B, p value < 0.05, |log2FC| > 1.5). Besides, 84 DE-lncRNAs included 38 upregulated lncRNA and 46 downregulated lncRNA were also screened out (Fig. 1C and D). Furthermore, 5 DE-miRNAs contained 2 up-regulated miRNAs and 3 down-regulated miRNAs were also identified (Fig. 1E and F). These DEGs were screen out for the further investigation of underlying function and construction of ceRNA networks.

GO and KEGG pathways analysis of the DE-mRNAs

In the next step, GO and KEGG analysis were conducted for further underlying mechanism investigation. The biological processes (BP), cell component (CC), and molecular function (MF) were shown in Fig. 2. The top 10 significantly enriched terms including angiogenesis, blood vessel morphogenesis, vasculature development and tube morphogenesis were significantly enriched in BP (Fig. 2A), secretory granule, apical part of cell, apical plasma membrane and membrane raft (Fig. 2B) DNA−binding transcription factor binding, protein tyrosine/serine/threonine phosphatase activity enriched in MF (Fig. 2C). In addition, signaling pathways enrichment analysis were performed to further verify the related pathways of DE-mRNAs in SE. The KEGG results indicated that MAPK, TNF, IL-17 signaling pathway, cytokine−cytokine receptor interaction, and apoptosis were significantly enriched (Fig. 2D). The GSEA analysis was carried out and we found that signaling pathway like neuron fate specification skeletal muscle cell, negative regulation of cell killing, cellular response to interferon−beta were significantly enriched in cells with down-regulated expression DE-mRNA (Fig. 3). The results of functional enrichment analysis suggest that the DE-mRNAs were functioned in the regulation of angiogenesis, inflammation, and neuron in SE.

Construction of PPI and ceRNA network

Next, to uncover the interaction of molecule protein, we constructed the PPI network. The DE-mRNAs were put into the string database to obtain protein interaction data, then constructed the PPI network in the cytoscape software. The network was shown in Fig. 4A, which was consist of 184 nodes and 246 edges. In addition, we analyzed the hub genes of the PPI network. The top 20 genes are shown in Fig. 4B. In order to further explore the underlying mechanism and interaction of DE-genes in SE, a ceRNA network of lncRNA-miRNA-mRNA was constructed basing on the miRwalk website and LncBase Predicted v.2. After that, the miRNA that is regulated for both lncRNA and mRNA was selected, and two ceRNA networks were constructed as shown in Fig. 5. The ceRNA network-1 contained 7 up-regulated DE-mRNAs (Arid5a, Adm, Insig1, Midn, Btaf1, Per1, Slc25a25), 1 down-regulated DE-miRNAs (mmu-miR-6413), and 1 up-regulated DE-lncRNAs (Zmiz1os1). The ceRNA network-2 contained 2 down-regulated DE-mRNAs (Rab27a and Lrp2), 1 up-regulated DE-miRNA (mmu-miR-139–5p), and 1 down-regulated DE-lncRNA (Gm15883). The DEGs involves in the ceRNA network were selected for the following validation.

Validation of the expression of DEGs in ceRNA networks

To verify the expression level of the DEGs involved in the ceRNA networks, qRT-PCR was conducted. As shown in Fig. 6A-M, the 7 DE-mRNAs (Arid5a, Adm, Insig1, Midn, Btaf1, Per1, Slc25a25) and lncRNA Zmiz1os1 in the ceRNA network-1 were up-regulated, miR-6413 was up-regulated compared to control group, which are consistent with the expression level presented previously in Fig. 5. Similarly, DE-mRNAs (Rab27a and Lrp2) and DE-lncRNA Gm15883 involved in ceRNA network-2 were down-regulated, miR-139 was up-regulated compared to control group.

Discussion

SE is a common neurological disease that causes brain damage, cognitive deficits and the pathogenesis of epilepsy [25]. When irreversible brain damage occurs, it is important to stop SE convulsions and prevent its recurrence [[26], [27], [28]]. Therefore, study the cellular and molecular mechanisms underlying SE is necessary to develop new strategies for diagnosis and treatment. Besides, identifying the potential genetic biomarkers in SE would be of great relevance for clinical and research purposes [11].

Noncoding RNAs, including lncRNA, miRNA and circle RNA (circRNA) emerge significant role in the procession of SE. For example, Shao et al. identified circIgf1r was upregulated in the SE model through circRNA sequencing and verified by QPCR analysis [29]. A large number of studies have shown that SE produces selective changes in more than 20 miRNAs in the hippocampus or other brain regions, revealing conserved miRNA responses after SE. Changes in miRNA expression may affect levels of a variety of proteins involved in neuronal morphology and function, gliosis, neuroinflammation, and cell death [30]. Alsharafi et al. indicated that miRNA-139–5p negatively regulates NR2A-containing NMDA receptor in the rat pilocarpine model and patients with temporal lobe epilepsy [31].To date, the regulation role of lncRNAs in gene expression have been revealed in SE [9]. Our previous study indicated that lncRNA TUG1 inhibits neuronal apoptosis in status epilepticus rats via targeting the miR-421/mTOR axis [10]. However, currently, the expression profile and the role of lncRNAs serve as ceRNA in SE still needs further elucidation. In this study, the lncRNA-miRNA-mRNA network were constructed while lncRNA Zmiz1os1 and Gm15883 were identified involved in ceRNA network. The lncRNA Zmiz1os1 complete for miR-6413, and Gm15883 complete for miR-139, which were the first time to report. The expression of these potential biomarkers was also verified by qRT-PCR.

During SE, activation of microglia and macrophages triggers inflammation, which leads to multiple brain processes leading to neuronal apoptosis and further severe neuronal damage [32]. In addition, in experimental and human epilepsy, angiogenic injury is thought to contribute to the onset and/or seizure of epilepsy [33]. Zhai et al. suggested that astrocytes modulate angiogenesis through jaggeD1-mediated NotCH 1 pathway after epileptic persistence [34]. Marcon et al. noted brain inflammation, angiogenesis, and increased blood-brain barrier permeability in epileptic brain tissue in adult rodents and humans [35]. The functional enrichment was significantly enriched in inflammation-related signaling pathways, such as TNF, IL-17 signaling pathway, cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction, which consistent to the previous study. We also found that the DEGs enriched in angiogenesis, blood vessel morphogenesis, vasculature development. These implied that the DEGs were play a significant role in the regulation of inflammation and angiogenesis in SE.

Conclusion

In sum, we conducted the RNA Seq based on the SE mice model and identified DEGs through bioinformatics to establish the lncRNA-miRNA-mRNA ceRNA networks and evaluated the expression level of the DEGs involved in the ceRNA network. These findings provided novel insights into the mechanisms of SE and facilitates the discovery of genetic biomarkers for SE. Nevertheless, the specific molecular regulation mechanism needs to be embodied in the further experiments.

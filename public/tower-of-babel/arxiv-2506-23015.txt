Non-expansion in polynomial automorphisms of ℂ 2 {\mathbb{C}}^{2}
Martin Bays and Tingxiang Zou

Abstract
. We treat the higher-dimensional Elekes-Szabó problem in the case of the action of Aut ⁡ ( ℂ 2 ) {\operatorname{Aut}}({\mathbb{C}}^{2}) on ℂ 2 {\mathbb{C}}^{2} .



1.
Introduction

Let
G={\operatorname{Aut}}({\mathbb{C}}^{2})
be the group of polynomial automorphisms of the complex plane. An element
g\in G
can be seen as a pair of polynomials in two variables
g_{x},g_{y}\in{\mathbb{C}}[x,y]
, acting on
X:={\mathbb{C}}^{2}
by
g*(x,y):=(g_{x}(x,y),g_{y}(x,y))
. We consider the question: if
F\subseteq G
is a finite subset of polynomial automorphisms of bounded degree, and
A
is a finite subset of the plane, how can we have non-expansion of the form
|F*A|=\{g*a:(g,a)\in F\times A\}<|A|^{1+\eta}
? This has the form of an Elekes-Szabó problem
[
7
]
, but since
X
is 2-dimensional it falls outside of the scope of previous results. In
[
5
, Question 7.5]
, we proposed a tentative solution to such higher-dimensional Elekes-Szabó problems, which in this case would imply that, for
A
not concentrating on a curve and of size comparable in exponent to
|F|
, non-expansion is possible only when
F
concentrates on a coset of a nilpotent algebraic subgroup.

In this paper, we use the amalgamated product structure of
{\operatorname{Aut}}({\mathbb{C}}^{2})
, along with model-theoretic techniques originating in
[
8
]
and further developed in
[
2
]
and
[
5
]
, to confirm this; see Theorem
5.2
for a precise statement of this form. Analysing the nilpotent subgroups which arise, we furthermore obtain the following statement in the style of the Elekes-Rónyai analysis of expanding bivariate polynomials. The original Elekes-Rónyai result
[
6
]
showed that non-expanding bivariate polynomials are conjugate to addition or multiplication; the analogous condition in the present situation is a little more complicated, and we term it
non-mixing
(with no connection to ergodic theory intended) because it involves constrained interaction between the variables.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
F(x,y,\bar{z}),G(x,y,\bar{z})
be polynomials over
{\mathbb{C}}
. We say the pair
(F,G)
is
non-mixing
if there are
g,h\in{\operatorname{Aut}}({\mathbb{C}}^{2})
and polynomials
t_{0}(\bar{z})
,
t_{1}(\bar{z})
and
s(y,\bar{z})
such that
g\circ(F(x,y,\bar{z}),G(x,y,\bar{z}))\circ h
is one of the following:

•

(t_{0}(\bar{z})x,~t_{1}(\bar{z})y)
;

•

(t_{0}(\bar{z})x,~y+t_{1}(\bar{z}))
;

•

(t_{1}(\bar{z})^{\ell}x+t_{0}(\bar{z})y^{\ell},~t_{1}(\bar{z})y)
for some
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
;

•

(x+s(y,\bar{z}),~y+t_{1}(\bar{z}))
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
F(x,y,\bar{z}),G(x,y,\bar{z})\in{\mathbb{C}}[x,y,\bar{z}]\setminus{\mathbb{C}}[x,y]
. Suppose
(F,G)
is non-mixing. Then for any
\varepsilon>0
, there is
\eta>0
such that for all finite sets
A\subseteq{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
and
B\subseteq{\mathbb{C}}^{|\bar{z}|}
with

(i)

|A|^{1/\varepsilon}\geq|B|\geq|A|^{\varepsilon}\geq\frac{1}{\eta}
,

(ii)

(F(x,y,b),G(x,y,b))\in{\operatorname{Aut}}({\mathbb{C}}^{2})
for all
b\in B
,

(iii)

|A\cap V|\leq|A|^{1-\varepsilon}
and
|B\cap W|\leq|B|^{1-\varepsilon}
for all varieties
V\subsetneq{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
and
W\subsetneq{\mathbb{C}}^{|\bar{z}|}
of complexity
<\frac{1}{\eta}
,

we have
|(F,G)(B)*A|:=|\{(F(a,b),G(a,b)):a\in A,b\in B\}|\geq|A|^{1+\eta}
.

Note that assumption (iii), which we call “weak general position”, is achieved in the case of grids, i.e. when
A=A_{0}\times A_{1}
with
|A_{i}|\geq|A|^{\varepsilon}
and similarly for
B
.
In the case that the assumption on
A
fails, meaning that
A
concentrates on some algebraic curve, the results in
[
5
]
already apply to yield that abelian groups explain non-expansion.

Let us also remark that the fourth case in the definition of non-mixing is the most interesting, because it corresponds to a nilpotent algebraic group, of nilpotency class depending on the degree in
y
of
s
. This appears to be the first time that nilpotent non-abelian groups have appeared explicitly in such an Elekes-Szabó result beyond the foundational case
[
4
]
of approximate subgroups of complex algebraic groups, and we take it as evidence towards our expectation expressed in
[
5
, Question 7.5]
that nilpotent algebraic group actions are precisely the structures behind (ternary) Elekes-Szabó phenomena in general. In the present case, the action is already apparent, and the main difficulty is to show that an algebraic subgroup of
G
is responsible –
G
itself being infinite dimensional and not an algebraic group. This suggests considering the corresponding question for other actions on varieties of groups defined as directed limits of constructible sets, for example the action on
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}({\mathbb{C}})
of the Cremona group of birational automorphisms of the plane. However, the techniques of this paper crucially exploit the group structure of
{\operatorname{Aut}}({\mathbb{C}}^{2})
(see Remark
4.5
), and do not apply even to this case of
{\operatorname{Bir}}({\mathbb{C}}^{2})
. We aim to treat this and more general situations in future work.

2.
Group structure of
{\operatorname{Aut}}(k^{2})

Let
k
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic 0.

Consider the group
G=G(k)
of polynomial automorphisms of
k^{2}
. Let

\displaystyle E

\displaystyle:=\{(x,y)\mapsto(ax+P(y),by+c):P\in k[x]\text{ and }a,b,c\in k,ab\neq 0\},

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle:=\{(x,y)\mapsto(a_{1}x+b_{1}y+c_{1},a_{2}x+b_{2}y+c_{2}):a_{i},b_{i},c_{i}\in k,a_{1}b_{2}-a_{2}b_{1}\neq 0\},

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle:=A\cap E.

Here
E
is the group of elementary automorphisms, and
A
is the group of affine automorphisms. It is easy to see that
E
is a countable union of algebraic subgroups
(E_{n})_{n\geq 0}
where
E_{n}
is the collection of elements in
E
in which we restrict the polynomial
P(y)
to have degree
\leq n
.
Note that
S=E_{1}
.

Fact 2.1
.

G
is an amalgamated free product of
E
and
A
over
S
.

We proceed to deduce some useful consequences of this fact.
We use the following description of the elements of an amalgamated free
product, for which see
[
9
, I.1, Remark to Theorem 1]
.

Fact 2.2
.

Each
g\in G\setminus S
can be written as an
alternating word
,

g=\prod_{i<n}h_{i}
where each
h_{i}\in(A\cup E)\setminus S
and
h_{i}\in A\Leftrightarrow h_{i+1}\in E
.
Furthermore,
if
g=\prod_{i<n^{\prime}}h^{\prime}_{i}
is another such expression, then
n^{\prime}=n
and there exist

s_{0},\ldots,s_{n-2}\in S^{n-1}
such that

(h^{\prime}_{0},\ldots,h^{\prime}_{n-1})=(h_{0}\cdot s_{0}^{-1},s_{0}\cdot h_{1}\cdot s_{1}^{-1},\ldots,s_{n-2}\cdot h_{n-1})
.

In particular,

\prod_{i<k}h_{i}\cdot S=\prod_{i<k}h^{\prime}_{i}\cdot S

and
S\cdot\prod_{i\geq k}h_{i}=S\cdot\prod_{i\geq k}h^{\prime}_{i}

for any
k\leq n
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose
g=\prod_{i<n}h_{i}
is an alternating word, and
k\leq n
.
Then
\raise 0.02205pt\hbox{$\ulcorner$}\hbox{$\prod_{i<k}h_{i}\cdot S$}\raise 0.02205pt\hbox{$\urcorner$}\in{\operatorname{dcl}}^{0}(g)
.

Proof.

If
\sigma
is a field automorphism fixing
g
,
then
g=\prod_{i<n}\sigma(h_{i})
is another alternating word,
and so by Fact
2.2
we have

\sigma\left({\prod_{i<k}h_{i}\cdot S}\right)=\prod_{i<k}\sigma(h_{i})\cdot S=\prod_{i<k}h_{i}\cdot S.

∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Any
g\in G\setminus S
can be written as an alternating word
g=\prod_{i<n}h_{i}
with each
h_{i}
algebraic over
g
.

Proof.

Apply Fact
2.2
with the field
k
being the algebraic closure of
g
.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Suppose
f,g\in G\setminus S
with
f\equiv^{0}g
, and
f^{-1}\cdot g
is conjugate in
G
to an element of
A\cup E
.
Suppose
f=\prod_{i\leq n}f_{i}
and
g=\prod_{i\leq n}g_{i}
are alternating words.
Then there is
m\leq n
and
\beta\in A\cup E
such that

f^{-1}\cdot g=\beta^{(\prod_{i>m}g_{i})}
.

Proof.

Note that
f_{i}\in A\Leftrightarrow g_{i}\in A
for all
i
,
since
f\equiv^{0}g
, using Fact
2.2
.

Let
m\leq n
be greatest such that

(\prod_{i<m}f_{i})\cdot S=(\prod_{i<m}g_{i})\cdot S
.
If
m=n
then we are done with
\beta=f^{-1}\cdot g\in S
,
so suppose
m<n
.
Then setting
\gamma:=(\prod_{i\leq m}f_{i})^{-1}\cdot\prod_{i\leq m}g_{i}
,
we have
\gamma\notin S
,
and
\gamma=f_{m}^{-1}\cdot(\prod_{i<m}f_{i})^{-1}\cdot\prod_{i<m}g_{i}\cdot g_{m}\in(f_{m}^{-1}\cdot S\cdot g_{m})
so
\gamma\in A\Leftrightarrow g_{m}\in A
,
and so

(1)

f^{-1}\cdot g=(\prod_{i>m}f_{i})^{-1}\cdot\gamma\cdot\prod_{i>m}g_{i},

is an alternating word.

Now say
f^{-1}\cdot g=\alpha^{h}
where
\alpha\in A\cup E
.
We may assume that either
h=1
or
h=\prod_{i<k}h_{i}

is an alternating word with
\alpha^{h_{0}}\notin A\cup E
,
since otherwise we may replace
\alpha
with
\alpha^{h_{0}}
.

In the case
h=1
, we conclude with
\beta:=\alpha
and
m:=n
, so suppose
h\neq 1
.
Then
\alpha\notin S
,
and
\alpha^{h}=(\prod_{i<k}h_{i})^{-1}\cdot\alpha\cdot\prod_{i<k}h_{i}
is an alternating word.
Comparing with our previous alternating word (
1
),
we obtain
S\cdot\prod_{i>m}g_{i}=S\cdot\prod_{i<k}h_{i}
,
say
\prod_{i<k}h_{i}=s\cdot\prod_{i>m}g_{i}
,
and we conclude with
\beta:=\alpha^{s}
.
∎

We also use the following fact that an element of
G
with infinite fixed point
set must be conjugate to an elementary automorphism.

Fact 2.6

(
[
3
, Theorem 2.1, Theorem 0.1]
)
.

Suppose
g\in G
is such that
g*x=x
for infinitely many
x\in k^{2}
.
Then there exists
h\in G
such that
g^{h}\in E
.

2.1.
Nilpotent algebraic subgroups

Lemma 2.7
.

Any nilpotent algebraic subgroup
H\leq A
is conjugate in
A
to a
subgroup of
S=E_{1}
.

Proof.

First observe that
A\cong{\mathbb{G}}_{a}^{2}\rtimes{\operatorname{GL}}_{2}
.

Let
\pi:A\rightarrow{\operatorname{GL}}_{2}
be the corresponding projection homomorphism.
Then
\pi(H)
is a nilpotent algebraic subgroup of
{\operatorname{GL}}_{2}
, so in particular
solvable, hence
\pi(H)
is conjugate in
{\operatorname{GL}}_{2}
to a subgroup of the Borel
subgroup of upper triangular matrices
T\leq{\operatorname{GL}}_{2}
.
Say
\pi(H)^{\alpha}\leq T
with
\alpha\in{\operatorname{GL}}_{2}
.

Let
\beta\in\pi^{-1}(\alpha)\subseteq A
.
Then
\pi(H^{\beta})=\pi(H)^{\alpha}\leq T
,
so
H^{\beta}\leq\pi^{-1}(T)=S
.
∎

Lemma 2.8
.

Fix
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Any connected nilpotent algebraic subgroup of
E_{n}
over
k
is conjugate (in
E_{n}
) to a subgroup of one of the following nilpotent algebraic groups:

(1)

\{(x,y)\mapsto(ax,by):a,b\in k^{*}\}
.

(2)

\{(x,y)\mapsto(ax,y+b):a\in k^{*},b\in k\}
.

(3)

\{(x,y)\mapsto(b^{\ell}x+ay^{\ell},by):a\in k,b\in k^{*}\}
for some
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

(4)

\{(x,y)\mapsto(x+P(y),y+b):P(y)\in k[y]_{\leq n},b\in k\}
.

The groups in (1)-(3) are abelian.

Proof.

In this proof, we write elements of
E
in the form
\langle ax+P(y),by+c\rangle
.

Let
N
be a nilpotent subgroup of
E_{n}
.

We consider the following algebraic subgroups of
E_{n}
:

\displaystyle G_{x,a}

\displaystyle:=\{\langle x+P(y),y\rangle:P\in k[x]_{\leq n}\}

\displaystyle G_{x,m}

\displaystyle:=\{\langle ax,y\rangle:a\in k^{*}\}\cong{\mathbb{G}}_{m}

\displaystyle G_{y,a}

\displaystyle:=\{\langle x,y+c\rangle:c\in k\}\cong{\mathbb{G}}_{a}

\displaystyle G_{y,m}

\displaystyle:=\{\langle x,by\rangle:b\in k^{*}\}\cong{\mathbb{G}}_{m}

\displaystyle G_{x}

\displaystyle:=\{\langle ax+P(y),y\rangle:P\in k[x]_{\leq n},a\in k^{*}\}\cong G_{x,a}\rtimes G_{x,m}

\displaystyle G_{y}

\displaystyle:=\{\langle x,by+c\rangle:b\in k^{*},c\in k\}\cong G_{y,a}\rtimes G_{y,m}

It is easy to see that
G_{x}
is a normal subgroup of
E_{n}
and
E_{n}=G_{x}G_{y}
, and thus
E_{n}\cong G_{x}\rtimes G_{y}
. Consider the corresponding group homomorphism

\pi:G\to G_{y};\;\langle ax+P(y),by+c\rangle\mapsto\langle x,by+c\rangle.

Then
\pi(N)
is a nilpotent subgroup of
G_{y}\cong G_{y,a}\rtimes G_{y,m}
, so by conjugating we may assume that
\pi(N)\leq G_{y,a}
or
\pi(N)\leq G_{y,m}
.

Now consider
H:=G_{x}\cap N
, a nilpotent subgroup of
G_{x}
.
For elements of
G_{x}
, we denote
\langle ax+P(y),y\rangle
by
ax+P(y)
.

Suppose there
H\not\leq G_{x,a}
, so say
\phi=ax+P(y)\in H
with
a\neq 1
. Let
\psi=a^{\prime}x+Q(y)\in G_{x}
. Then we calculate

[\phi,\psi]=x+(aa^{\prime})^{-1}((a-1)Q(y)-(a^{\prime}-1)P(y)).

Thus,
C_{G_{x}}(\phi)=\{a^{\prime}x+(a-1)^{-1}(a^{\prime}-1)P(y):a^{\prime}\in k^{*}\}=(G_{x,m})^{\theta}
where
\theta:=(a-1)x+P(y)
. Suppose
H\not\leq(G_{x,m})^{\theta}
. Then there is
\phi^{\prime}=x+P^{\prime}(y)\in[H,H]
with
P^{\prime}(y)\neq 0
. Consider
[\phi^{\prime},\phi]=x+a^{-1}(1-a)P^{\prime}(y)\neq x
. Inductively,
[\cdots[[\phi^{\prime},\phi],\phi],\cdots]\neq x
, contradicting
H
being nilpotent. We conclude that, by conjugating
N
by an element of
G_{x}
, which does not change
\pi(N)
, we may assume that either
H\leq G_{x,a}
or
H\leq G_{x,m}
.

Suppose
H
is a non-trivial subgroup of
G_{x,m}
, so say
\psi=\langle a^{\prime}x,y\rangle\in H
with
a^{\prime}\neq 1
.
Now
H
is normal in
N
, so if
\phi=\langle ax+P(y),cy+d\rangle\in N
, then

\psi^{\phi}=\langle a^{\prime}x+a^{-1}(a^{\prime}-1)P(y),y\rangle\in G_{x,m},

and so
P(y)=0
. So
N
is a nilpotent subgroup of
\{\langle ax,by+c\rangle:a,b\in k^{*},c\in k\}
, which is isomorphic to the upper triangular subgroup of
{\operatorname{GL}}_{2}
, and so we see that
N
is conjugate to a subgroup of

•

\{\langle ax,by\rangle:a,b\in k^{*}\}\cong{\mathbb{G}}_{m}^{2}
or

•

\{\langle ax,y+b\rangle:a\in k^{*},b\in k\}\cong{\mathbb{G}}_{m}\times{\mathbb{G}}_{a}
.

Otherwise,
H\leq G_{x,a}
. Then
N
induces an algebraic homomorphism
\rho:\pi(N)\to G_{x,m}
defined by

\rho(c)=a
if
\langle ax+P(y),y+c\rangle\in N
, which is well-defined since
N\cap G_{x}=H\leq G_{x,a}
.

First suppose
\pi(N)\leq G_{y,a}
. Then
\rho
has trivial image, since there are no non-trivial algebraic homomorphisms
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}\to{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
, and so

N\leq\{\langle x+P(y),y+b\rangle:P(y)\in k[y]_{\leq n},b\in k\},

as required.

We are left with the case that
H\leq G_{x,a}
and
\pi(N)
is a non-trivial subgroup of
G_{y,m}
, in which case
\pi(N)=G_{y,m}
by connectedness of
N
. Then
\rho:G_{y,m}\to G_{x,m}
induces a homomorphism from
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}\to{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
, and so

N\leq\{\langle b^{\ell}x+P(y),by\rangle:P(y)\in k[y]_{\leq n},b\in k^{*}\}:=G_{\ell},

for some constant
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

We conclude by showing that
N
is conjugate to a subgroup of
\{\langle b^{\ell}x+ay^{\ell},by\rangle:a\in k,b\in k^{*}\}:=N_{\ell}
.

We first show that
H\leq\{x+ay^{\ell}:a\in k\}
.
Otherwise, take
\phi=\langle x+P(y),y\rangle\in H
with
P(y)=\sum_{i}a_{i}y^{i}
and
a_{i}\neq 0
for some
i\neq\ell
. Recall that
\pi(N)=G_{y,m}
, and so we can find
\psi=\langle b^{\ell}x+Q(y),by\rangle\in N
with
b^{m}\neq 1
for all
m\in{\mathbb{N}}^{>0}
. Then

[\phi,\psi]=x+b^{-\ell}P(by)-P(y)=x+\sum_{i\neq\ell}a_{i}(b^{-\ell}b^{i}-1)y^{i}.

Thus,
[\phi,\psi]\not\in\{x+ay^{\ell}:a\in k\}
. Now inductively, we have
[\cdots[[\phi,\psi],\psi],\cdots]\not\in\{x+ay^{\ell}:a\in k\}
, which cannot be trivial, contradicting
N
being nilpotent. Thus,
H\leq\{x+ay^{\ell}:a\in k\}\cong{\mathbb{G}}_{a}
. Since
\pi(N)=G_{y,m}
, it follows that
N
is nilpotent of dimension at most 2, hence is abelian.

Let
\phi:=\langle b^{\ell}x+P(y),by\rangle\in N
and
\psi:=\langle d^{\ell}x+Q(y),dy\rangle\in N
be independent generic elements of
N
. Then since
N
is abelian,

x=[\phi,\psi]=x+(bd)^{-\ell}(b^{\ell}Q(y)-Q(by)+P(dy)-d^{\ell}P(y)),

and so
b^{\ell}Q(y)-Q(by)=d^{\ell}P(y)-P(y)
. Say
P(y)=\sum_{i\leq m}a_{i}^{\phi}y^{i}
and
Q(y)=\sum_{j\leq m}c_{j}^{\psi}y^{j}
. Then for all
i\neq\ell
, we have

c_{i}^{\psi}(b^{\ell}-b^{i})y^{i}=a_{i}^{\phi}(d^{\ell}-d^{i})y^{i}.

Hence,
a_{i}^{\phi}/(b^{\ell}-b^{i})=c_{i}^{\psi}/(d^{\ell}-d^{i})
(since
\phi
and
\psi
are generic,
b^{\ell}-b^{i}\neq 0
and
d^{\ell}-d^{i}\neq 0
for
i\neq\ell
). Since
\phi\mathop{\mathchoice{\displaystyle\kern 5.71527pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\displaystyle\mid$\hss}\lower 3.87495pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\displaystyle\smile$\hss}\kern 5.71527pt}{\textstyle\kern 5.71527pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\textstyle\mid$\hss}\lower 3.87495pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\textstyle\smile$\hss}\kern 5.71527pt}{\scriptstyle\kern 2.80048pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptstyle\mid$\hss}\lower 1.89871pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptstyle\smile$\hss}\kern 2.80048pt}{\scriptscriptstyle\kern 1.42882pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle\mid$\hss}\lower 0.96873pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle\smile$\hss}\kern 1.42882pt}}\psi
, we get
a_{i}^{\phi},b\mathop{\mathchoice{\displaystyle\kern 5.71527pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\displaystyle\mid$\hss}\lower 3.87495pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\displaystyle\smile$\hss}\kern 5.71527pt}{\textstyle\kern 5.71527pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\textstyle\mid$\hss}\lower 3.87495pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\textstyle\smile$\hss}\kern 5.71527pt}{\scriptstyle\kern 2.80048pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptstyle\mid$\hss}\lower 1.89871pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptstyle\smile$\hss}\kern 2.80048pt}{\scriptscriptstyle\kern 1.42882pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle\mid$\hss}\lower 0.96873pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle\smile$\hss}\kern 1.42882pt}}c_{i}^{\psi},d
. Thus,
a_{i}^{\phi}/(b^{\ell}-b^{i})=c_{i}^{\psi}/(d^{\ell}-d^{i})=:\alpha_{i}\in{\operatorname{acl}}(\emptyset)
, and so
\phi
and
\psi
belong to

\{\langle b^{\ell}x+cy^{\ell}+\sum_{i\neq\ell,i\leq m}\alpha_{i}(b^{\ell}-b^{i})y^{i},by\rangle:b\in k^{*},c\in k\}=:\tilde{N}.

Let
\xi:=\langle x+\sum_{i\leq m,i\neq\ell}\alpha_{i}y^{i},y\rangle
. Then it is easy to check that
\tilde{N}=(N_{\ell})^{\xi}
. Since
\tilde{N}
is defined over
{\operatorname{acl}}(\emptyset)
and contains the generic
\phi
of
N
, we get
N\leq\tilde{N}
, so
N
is conjugate to a subgroup of
N_{\ell}
as desired.
∎

3.
Correspondence triangles with bounded dimension families of infinite fixed point sets

We work in an ultrapower
K={\mathbb{C}}^{\mathcal{U}}

and wgp. We write
\ulcorner

Let

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose
(a,g,g*a)\in X\times G\times X

Let

Let

is infinite;

Remark 4.5

This proof would go through generally for an amalgamated product

5.
Finitary consequences

With the same proof as
[
5
, Theorem 9.3]
, we get the following finitary consequence.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
F\subseteq{\operatorname{Aut}}({\mathbb{C}}^{2})
be a finite subset and
\varepsilon>0
. We say
F
is
\varepsilon
-nilpotent
, if there are
f,g\in{\operatorname{Aut}}({\mathbb{C}}^{2})
and a connected nilpotent algebraic subgroup
N_{d}\leq E_{d}
such that

|F\cap fN_{d}g|\geq|F|^{1-\epsilon}.

Corollary 5.2
.

For all
\varepsilon>0
and
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, there is
\eta>0
such that the following holds.
Let
F\subseteq{\operatorname{Aut}}({\mathbb{C}}^{2})
be a finite set of polynomial automorphisms consisting of polynomials of degree at most
n
,
and let
A
be a finite subset of
{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
. Suppose

•

|A|^{1/\varepsilon}\geq|F|\geq|A|^{\varepsilon}\geq\frac{1}{\eta}
;

•

For all algebraic curves
C\subseteq{\mathbb{A}}^{2}
of complexity
<\frac{1}{\eta}
, we have
|A\cap C|\leq|A|^{1-\varepsilon}
;

•

F
is not
\varepsilon
-nilpotent.

Then

|F*A|=|\{f*a:f\in F,a\in A\}|\geq|A|^{1+\eta}.

Combining these techniques with our analysis of the nilpotent algebraic subgroups of
{\operatorname{Aut}}({\mathbb{C}}^{2})
, we can now prove the statement in the spirit of Elekes-Rónyai given in the introduction.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Suppose for a contradiction that there is some
\varepsilon>0
with no
\eta
satisfying the conclusion of the theorem. Taking
\eta=1/n
we can find
A_{n}\subseteq{\mathbb{C}}^{2},B_{n}\subseteq{\mathbb{C}}^{|\bar{z}|}
such that

(1)

|A_{n}|^{1/\varepsilon}\geq|B_{n}|\geq|A|^{\varepsilon}\geq n
;

(2)

(F(x,y,b),G(x,y,b))\in{\operatorname{Aut}}({\mathbb{C}}^{2})
for all
b\in B_{n}
;

(3)

|A_{n}\cap V|\leq|A_{n}|^{1-\varepsilon}
and
|B_{n}\cap W|\leq|B_{n}|^{1-\varepsilon}
for all varieties
V\subsetneq{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
,
W\subsetneq{\mathbb{C}}^{|\bar{z}|}
of complexity
<n
;

(4)

|(F,G)(B_{n})*A_{n}|<|A_{n}|^{1+1/n}

Work in the ultraproduct
K={\mathbb{C}}^{\mathcal{U}}
, and assume
F
and
G
are defined over
k_{0}:={\operatorname{acl}}^{0}(\emptyset)
. Let
\xi:=(|A_{n}|)_{n}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{\mathcal{U}}
let
\boldsymbol{\delta}:=\boldsymbol{\delta}_{\xi}
.
Let
A:=\prod_{n\in\mathcal{U}}A_{n}
,
B:=\prod_{n\in\mathcal{U}}B_{n}
and
C:=(F,G)(B)*A

\boldsymbol{\delta}(B)\in[\varepsilon,1/\varepsilon]
, and
\boldsymbol{\delta}(C)=\boldsymbol{\delta}(A)=1
by (4). Let
(a,b)\in A\times B
be such that
\boldsymbol{\delta}(a,b)=\boldsymbol{\delta}(A)+\boldsymbol{\delta}(B)
. Then
a\mathop{\mathchoice{\displaystyle\kern 5.71527pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\displaystyle\mid$\hss}\lower 3.87495pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\displaystyle\smile$\hss}\kern 5.71527pt}{\textstyle\kern 5.71527pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\textstyle\mid$\hss}\lower 3.87495pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\textstyle\smile$\hss}\kern 5.71527pt}{\scriptstyle\kern 2.80048pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptstyle\mid$\hss}\lower 1.89871pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptstyle\smile$\hss}\kern 2.80048pt}{\scriptscriptstyle\kern 1.42882pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle\mid$\hss}\lower 0.96873pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle\smile$\hss}\kern 1.42882pt}}^{\boldsymbol{\delta}}b

and
\boldsymbol{\delta}(b)=\boldsymbol{\delta}(B)

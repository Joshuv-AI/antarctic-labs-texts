Local Averaging Accurately Distills Manifold Structure From Noisy Data
Yihan Shen, Shiyu Wang, Arnaud Lamy, Mariam Avagyan, John Wright

Abstract
High-dimensional data are ubiquitous, with examples ranging from natural images to scientific datasets, and often reside near low-dimensional manifolds. Leveraging this geometric structure is vital for downstream tasks, including signal denoising, reconstruction, and generation. However, in practice, the manifold is typically unknown and only noisy samples are available. A fundamental approach to uncovering the manifold structure is local averaging, which is a cornerstone of state-of-the-art provable methods for manifold fitting and denoising. However, to the best of our knowledge, there are no works that rigorously analyze the accuracy of local averaging in a manifold setting in high-noise regimes. In this work, we provide theoretical analyses of a two-round mini-batch local averaging method applied to noisy samples drawn from a d d -dimensional manifold ℳ ⊂ ℝ D \mathcal{M}\subset\mathbb{R}^{D} , under a relatively high-noise regime where the noise size is comparable to the reach τ \tau . We show that with high probability, the averaged point 𝐪 ^ \hat{\mathbf{q}} achieves the bound d ⁡ ( 𝐪 ^ , ℳ ) ≤ σ ​ d ⁡ ( 1 + κ ​ diam ​ ( ℳ ) log ⁡ ( D ) ) d(\hat{\mathbf{q}},\mathcal{M})\leq\sigma\sqrt{d\left(1+\frac{\kappa\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{M})}{\log(D)}\right)} , where σ , diam ⁡ ( ℳ ) , κ \sigma,\mathrm{diam(\mathcal{M})},\kappa denote the standard deviation of the Gaussian noise, manifold’s diameter and a bound on its extrinsic curvature, respectively. This is the first analysis of local averaging accuracy over the manifold in the relatively high noise regime where σ ​ D ≈ τ \sigma\sqrt{D}\approx\tau . The proposed method can serve as a preprocessing step for a wide range of provable methods designed for lower-noise regimes. Additionally, our framework can provide a theoretical foundation for a broad spectrum of denoising and dimensionality reduction methods that rely on local averaging techniques.

manifold hypothesis

A variety of approaches based on local averaging aim to leverage commonalities between neighboring points to extract clean structure from messy data. For instance, the
mean shift
algorithm
fukunaga1975estimation
iteratively moves data points towards local averages to approximate the gradient of the data density.
Nonlocal means
denoising
buades2005non
;
buades2011non
reduces the noise in images by averaging the similar structure in image patches. Local averages also play a key role in capturing manifold structure: classical methods such as locally-linear embedding (LLE)
roweis2000lle
seek a low-dimensional coordinate system that preserves local weighted averages. State-of-the-art provable methods for manifold estimation and denoising are also built on local averaging
yao2023manifold
;
yao2025manifold
.

Despite its widespread and successful use, the theoretical understanding of local averaging over manifolds remains incomplete. Existing analyses depend on restrictive assumptions – such as low noise level–that limit their applicability. This gap between the practice and theory highlights the need for a comprehensive theoretical framework for studying local averaging in higher noise regimes.

In this work, we close this gap by providing a theoretical analysis of the simple yet powerful local averaging method in the large noise regime. We consider data sampled from a
d
-dimensional Riemannian manifold
\mathcal{M}\subset\mathbb{R}^{D}
, corrupted by independent Gaussian noise
\mathbf{z}\sim\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\sigma^{2}\mathbf{I})
. We analyze a simple two-stage local averaging method, where each stage operates by averaging over a mini-batch of neighboring samples, developing a finite-sample error bound in the regime in which
\sigma\sqrt{D}\lesssim\tau
, where
\tau
is the reach of
\mathcal{M}
. As we review below, existing analyses pertain to much smaller noise levels (e.g., translating the results into gaussian noise, implicitly assuming
\sigma D\sqrt{\log(D)}<\tau
in
aizenbud2025estimation
, or
\sigma e^{cD/d}\ll 1
in
yao2023manifold
;
yao2025manifold
).

Under natural assumptions, for Stage I (“Coarse Localization”), we show that averaging over a single mini-batch of nearby samples produces a point whose distance to the manifold is bounded by
O\left(\sigma\sqrt{d\left(\log(D)+\kappa\>\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{M})\right)}\right)
. For stage II ("Fine Refinement"), with slightly stronger assumptions, we show that the distance is reduced to
O\left(\sigma\sqrt{d\left(1+\frac{\kappa\>\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{M})}{\log(D)}\right)}\right)
.

Our main technical innovation is a careful analysis of localization in this problem – controlling the probability that a noisy point
\mathbf{x}_{\natural}+\mathbf{z}
belongs to the ball
B(\mathbf{q},R)
. This probability exhibits a sharp phase transition around
\|\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{x}_{\natural}\|_{2}^{2}\approx R^{2}-\sigma^{2}D
; we develop sharp subgaussian bounds for this and related probabilities, leading to estimates that accurately capture localization even when
\sigma
is relatively large.

To the best knowledge of the authors, our work provides the first theoretical guarantee for local averaging in the relatively large noise regime (
\sigma\sqrt{D}\approx\tau
). Since local averaging is ubiquitous in the area of machine learning and signal processing, our theory provides both a design principle –– choosing the appropriate neighborhood radii and minibatch sizes according to noise and manifold geometry – and a performance guarantee that researchers can directly apply while using local averaging. Moreover, our novel analysis framework offers a valuable tool for further theoretical investigations into this local averaging problem. Apart from the theoretical novelty, our results also have immediate practical implications: they justify the use of averaging in graph Laplacian construction
(

)
, similarity distance estimation
(
trillos2019local
)
, and net-building
(
wang2025fast
)
. Our work could be integrated as a pre-processing step for the more refined techniques for manifold estimation

Problem Formulation

Let
\mathcal{M}\subset\mathbb{R}^{D}
be a smooth, compact
d
-dimensional Riemannian manifold embedded in the higher dimension ambient space
\mathbb{R}^{D}
. We observe a stream of noisy data points whose clean signals are uniformly sampled from
\mathcal{M}
, i.e.,

\{\mathbf{x}_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{D},\quad\text{where }\mathbf{x}_{i}=\mathbf{x}_{i,\natural}+\mathbf{z}_{i},\quad\mathbf{x}_{i,\natural}\in\mathcal{M},\;\mathbf{z}_{i}\overset{iid}{\sim}\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\sigma^{2}\mathbf{I}).

(3.1)

Our goal is to produce cleaner data points
\{\mathbf{q}_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{D}
referred to as
landmarks
, with a provable upper bound on
d\Bigl(\mathbf{q}_{i},\mathcal{M}\Bigr).
The landmarking problem – identifying representative points that lie closer to the manifold from noisy samples – is challenging due to the interplay between the noise size and the manifold’s geometric properties. Since the noisy sample’s distance to the manifold scales as
\Theta(\sigma\sqrt{D})
, the regime where
\sigma\sqrt{D}\leq\tau
, where the projection of noisy samples onto the manifold is unique, is of greater theoretical interest
aizenbud2025estimation
;
yao2025manifold
;
yao2023manifold
.

The simplest approach to identify points closer to the manifold and distill its structure from noisy samples is local averaging. It leverages the fact that nearby points share similar tangent structures, and their mean are often closer to the manifold. In practice, similar to the idea of
Mean Shift
, iterative local averaging is simple and useful: starting from an initial noisy sample
\mathbf{q}^{0}
, at each iteration
\ell
, it collects
\mathbf{N}_{\ell}
neighboring samples whose extrinsic distance to
\mathbf{q}^{\ell}
is less than
R_{\ell}:

\mathcal{X}_{\ell}=\{\mathbf{x}_{\ell,i}\}_{i=1}^{N_{\ell}}\;\subset\;B\bigl(\mathbf{q}^{\ell},\,R_{\ell}\bigr),

where each
\mathbf{x}_{\ell,i}
follows the data model in Equation (
3.1
), and updates the point by averaging over these neighbors:

\mathbf{q}^{\ell+1}\leftarrow\frac{1}{N_{\ell}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\ell}}\mathbf{x}_{\ell,i}.

(3.2)

Under the additive Gaussian noise model, we would like to show that the local average
\frac{1}{N_{\ell}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\ell}}\mathbf{x}_{\ell,i}=\frac{1}{N_{\ell}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\ell}}\mathbf{x}_{\ell,i,\natural}+\frac{1}{N_{\ell}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\ell}}\mathbf{z}_{\ell,i}

effectively suppresses the noise and produces a point close to the manifold. Intuitively, averages of independent gaussian noise vectors have a zero expectation and tend to reduce the noise magnitude, but in this setting we are averaging noise vectors
\mathbf{z}_{\ell,i}
conditioned on the noisy observations
\mathbf{x}_{\ell,i}
being close to
\mathbf{q}^{\ell}.
We bound its norm by introducing a novel technical tool: subgaussian bounds for grouping probability function
h(s)
(
5.2
). We also analyze the performance of averaging the latent clean signals of samples near a given landmark using a similar framework around
h(s)
. Geometrically, the local neighborhood of the manifold resembles an affine space. As a result, the average of clean points within a small neighborhood remains close to the manifold. It should be emphasized that local averaging is widely used and serves as the core building block in many state-of-the-art provable manifold estimation methods
yao2025manifold
;
yao2023manifold
.

In this work, we provide rigorous analysis of the distance between points produced by local averaging through a two-round process (Algorithm
1
) and the manifold. Our technical contribution lies in the development of subgaussian bounds on
h(s),
which describes the probability that noisy points fall into an extrinsic ball centered at a given landmark, along with related probability estimates. This analysis provides a foundation for downstream tasks such as tangent space and curvature estimation. Notably, our analyses are adaptive to moderate-to-large noise regime where
\sigma\sqrt{D}\lesssim\tau
. As such, our results can serve as a valuable pre-processing step for methods aimed at handling lower noise scenarios.

6
Discussion, Limitation, and Future Work

In this work, we analyze the accuracy and sample complexity of local averaging for manifold estimation, providing the first such theoretical analysis in the moderate-to-high noise regime (
\sigma\sqrt{D}\lesssim\tau
) and achieving a final accuracy of
O\left(\sigma\sqrt{d\left(1+\frac{\kappa\>\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{M})}{\log(D)}\right)}\right)
. The key innovation lies in developing a subgaussian bound on the probability that a noisy sample belongs to the ball
B(\mathbf{q},R)
and related probabilities. This tool enables rigorous analysis beyond the low-noise assumptions made in prior manifold estimation studies – for example,
yao2023manifold
;
yao2025manifold
implicitly requires
\sigma e^{cD/d}<1
.

As an additional contribution, we also bound the deviation of the local average of noisy samples around
\mathbf{q}
from
\mathbf{q}_{\natural},
the projection of
\mathbf{q}
onto the manifold. By setting the landmark itself to a noisy point,
\mathbf{q}^{0}=\mathbf{x}_{1}=\mathbf{x}_{1,\natural}+\mathbf{z}_{1},
since
\|\mathbf{q}^{0}_{\natural}-\mathbf{x}_{1,\natural}\|\lesssim\sigma\sqrt{d}
, and our result also controls the accuracy in estimating
\mathbf{x}_{1,\natural}.
Compared to
trillos2019local
, our results apply to a broader noise regime and exhibit improved scaling with respect to the ambient dimension. Together, these results contribute to a more comprehensive theoretical understanding of the simple and fundamental local averaging methods.

Since the most interesting applications of manifold estimation and denoising involve large noise, an important direction for future work is to consider noise levels
\sigma\sqrt{D}\gg\tau
. This regime is largely unstudied (with the exception of the
fefferman2023fitting
). Although
\sigma\sqrt{D}\leq\tau
is often regarded in the literature as a critical barrier, there is reason to believe that the fundamental limit lies beyond this threshold. Intuitively, this limit is set not by comparing the norm of the noise to the reach, but rather by comparing the maximum component of the noise along secant directions.
5
5

5

Fundamental limit is
\mathbb{E}[\Sigma]<\tau
, where
\Sigma=\sup_{\mathbf{v}\in S}\langle\mathbf{v},\mathbf{z}\rangle>\tau
,
S=\left\{\frac{\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}^{\prime}}{\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\|},\mathbf{x}\neq\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}\right\}
is the secant bundle and
\mathbf{z}
is the noise. Projecting noisy samples back onto the manifold may become unreliable beyond this limit.
As most of the noise energy lies orthogonal to the secant direction, it follows that
\mathbb{E}[\Sigma]\ll\sigma\sqrt{D}
when
d\ll D
. We believe that the subgaussian bounds developed here will be helpful for approaching the critical
\mathbb{E}[\Sigma]\approx\tau
limit, but it would require a finer-grained accounting for the global geometry of the manifold beyond the near-far decomposition used in our proof. An interesting related question is whether
robust
local averaging methods will be required for accurate estimation in this regime.

Another promising direction for future work is to leverage our analysis to analyze more sophisticated manifold estimators. For example, our analysis can easily extend beyond single-point analysis to sequences of noisy samples, making it naturally adaptable to streaming data. Our analysis can also support analysis of stochastic gradient descent (SGD). Specifically, when minimizing the population loss
\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{x}}\Bigl[\,\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{q}\|_{2}^{2}\mid\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{q}\|\leq R\,\Bigr]
using SGD, the negative gradient – computed using the subset of samples within the ball – points in the direction of
\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{x}}\Bigl[\,\mathbf{x}\mid\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{q}\|\leq R\,\Bigr]
. In our analysis, we provide a tight bound on empirical conditional mean – that is, the average of points within the ball
B(\mathbf{q},R)
. We believe this will facilitate a more controlled analysis of gradient descent in the context of SGD.

One limitation of this work is that it does not provide analysis on geometric quantities such as tangent space, curvature, or the smoothness of the recovered manifold, which are often the focus of many other related works in manifold estimation. However, we believe our technical tools here can be generalized to analyze such quantities.
Extending the analysis beyond two rounds of local averaging is also a natural direction to explore. Doing so may require new technical innovations, as the subgaussian bound in our analyses breaks down when the landmark
\mathbf{q}
is very close to the manifold, which occurs after two iterations in our setting. Additionally, in Corollary
4.2
, our analysis could be sharpened by accounting for cancellations in the signal average, due to (approximate) symmetry around
\mathbf{q}_{\natural}
.

Corollary A.2
(Signal Estimation via Local Averaging)

A.1
and with the same accepting radius

(A.110)

Proof

From equation
A.52
, we have with probability
>1-2e^{-Cd}

\frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{mb}}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{N_{\mathrm{mb}}}d^{2}_{\mathcal{M}}\Bigl(\mathbf{x}_{\ell,1,\natural},\mathbf{q}^{(0)}_{1,\natural}\Bigr)\leq C\bar{\kappa}\sigma^{2}\sqrt{Dd}+C^{\prime}\sigma^{2}\sqrt{Dd\times\left(\kappa\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{M})+\log(D)\right)}

(A.111)

Therefore, by Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^{N_{\mathrm{mb}}}d_{\mathcal{M}}\Bigl(\mathbf{x}_{\ell,1,\natural},\mathbf{q}^{(0)}_{1,\natural}\Bigr)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq N_{\mathrm{m}b}\times\sum_{\ell=1}^{N_{\mathrm{mb}}}d^{2}_{\mathcal{M}}\Bigl(\mathbf{x}_{\ell,1,\natural},\mathbf{q}^{(0)}_{1,\natural}\Bigr)

(A.112)

\displaystyle\leq{N_{\mathrm{m}b}}^{2}\left(C\bar{\kappa}\sigma^{2}\sqrt{Dd}+C^{\prime}\sigma^{2}\sqrt{Dd\times\left(\kappa\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{M})+\log(D)\right)}\right).

(A.113)

Taking the square root gives

\frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{mb}}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{N_{\mathrm{mb}}}d_{\mathcal{M}}\Bigl(\mathbf{x}_{\ell,1,\natural},\mathbf{q}^{(0)}_{1,\natural}\Bigr)\leq 2C\sigma\sqrt{\bar{\kappa}}(Dd)^{1/4}+2C^{\prime}\sigma\left(Dd\>\left(\kappa\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{M})+\log(D)\right)\right)^{1/4}

(A.114)

From equation

A.37
, we further have with probability
>1-e^{-Cd}

\left\|\frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{mb}}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{N_{\mathrm{mb}}}\mathbf{z}_{\ell}\right\|\leq C\sigma\sqrt{d\times\left(\kappa\,\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{M})+\log(D)\right)}

(A.115)

Lastly, by triangular inequality, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{mb}}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{N_{\mathrm{mb}}}\mathbf{x}_{\ell}-\,\mathbf{q^{(0)}}_{1,\natural}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{mb}}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{N_{\mathrm{mb}}}\mathbf{x}_{\ell,\natural}-\,\mathbf{q^{(0)}}_{1,\natural}\right\|+\left\|\frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{mb}}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{N_{\mathrm{mb}}}\mathbf{x}_{\ell}-\,\mathbf{x}_{\ell,\natural}\right\|

(A.116)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{mb}}}\left\|\sum_{\ell=1}^{N_{\mathrm{mb}}}\mathbf{x}_{\ell,\natural}-\,\mathbf{q^{(0)}}_{1,\natural}\right\|+\left\|\frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{mb}}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{N_{\mathrm{mb}}}\mathbf{x}_{\ell}-\,\mathbf{x}_{\ell,\natural}\right\|

(A.117)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{mb}}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{N_{\mathrm{mb}}}d_{\mathcal{M}}\Bigl(\mathbf{x}_{\ell,1,\natural},\mathbf{q}^{(0)}_{1,\natural}\Bigr)+\left\|\frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{mb}}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{N_{\mathrm{mb}}}\mathbf{x}_{\ell}-\,\mathbf{x}_{\ell,\natural}\right\|

(A.118)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\sigma\sqrt{\bar{\kappa}}(Dd)^{1/4}+C\sigma\left(Dd\>\left(\kappa\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{M})+\log(D)\right)\right)^{1/4}

(A.119)

Framework of Analysis

Our goal in the subsequent sections is to provide high probability bounds on the distance between the minibatch average

\frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{mb}}}\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}_{\ell}}\mathbf{x}

(A.120)

at iteration
\ell
and the manifold
\mathcal{M}
. Below, let

\mathbf{q}_{1\natural}=\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{M}}[\mathbf{q}_{1}]

(A.121)

be the nearest point on the manifold
\mathcal{M}
to the landmark
\mathbf{q}_{1}
at iteration
\ell
. Each sample in the
\ell
-th minibatch
\mathcal{X}_{\ell}
can be expressed as
\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{x}_{\natural}+\mathbf{z}
, and so

\frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{mb}}}\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}_{\ell}}\mathbf{x}\;=\;\underset{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\text{\bf Signal average}}{\frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{mb}}}\sum_{\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{x}_{\natural}+\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{X}_{\ell}}\mathbf{x}_{\natural}}\;+\;\underset{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\text{\bf Noise average}}{\frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{mb}}}\sum_{\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{x}_{\natural}+\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{X}_{\ell}}\mathbf{z}}

(A.122)

d\left(\frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{mb}}}\sum_{\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{x}_{\natural}+\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{X}_{\ell}}\mathbf{x}_{\natural},\;\mathcal{M}\right)\leq\frac{\kappa}{N_{\mathrm{mb}}}\sum_{\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{x}_{\natural}+\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{X}_{\ell}}d^{2}_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbf{x}_{\natural},\mathbf{q}_{1\natural}),

(A.123)

and so the distance of the minibatch average to the manifold satisfies

d\left(\frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{mb}}}\sum_{\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{x}_{\natural}+\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{X}_{\ell}}\mathbf{x},\;\mathcal{M}\right)\leq\frac{\kappa}{N_{\mathrm{mb}}}\sum_{\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{x}_{\natural}+\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{X}_{\ell}}d^{2}_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbf{x}_{\natural},\mathbf{q}_{1\natural})+\left\|\frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{mb}}}\sum_{\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{x}_{\natural}+\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{X}_{\ell}}\mathbf{z}\right\|

(A.124)

The right hand side of this inequality involves two sums of independent random variables – the
noise average

\frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{mb}}}\sum_{\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{x}_{\natural}+\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{X}_{\ell}}\mathbf{z}

(A.125)

and the
average squared intrinsic distance

\frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{mb}}}\sum_{\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{x}_{\natural}+\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{X}_{\ell}}d^{2}_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbf{x}_{\natural},\mathbf{q}_{1\natural})

(A.126)

We bound the expectations of these two terms in Sections
B
and
C
respectively.

A Supporting Lemma to Bound Signal Error by Average Squared Intrinsic Distance

Lemma A.3

Let
\mathbf{x}_{1\natural}\dots\mathbf{x}_{N\natural}\in\mathcal{M}
, and let
\mathbf{q}_{\natural}
be an arbitrary point on
\mathcal{M}
. Then

d\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{x}_{i\natural},\;\mathcal{M}\right)\leq\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}d_{\mathcal{M}}^{2}\left(\mathbf{x}_{i\natural},\mathbf{q}_{\natural}\right).

(A.127)

Proof

For
i=1,2...N,
consider the unique geodesic curve that goes from
\mathbf{q}_{\natural}
to
\mathbf{x}_{i\natural}
:

c_{i}:[0,1]\rightarrow\mathcal{M},c_{i}(0)=\mathbf{q}_{\natural},c_{i}(1)=\mathbf{x}_{i\natural}.

By definition of geodesics,
\langle c_{i}^{\prime\prime}(t),c_{i}^{\prime}(t)\rangle=0
, which implies that
||c_{i}^{\prime}(t)||
is a constant, and
d_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbf{q}_{\natural},\mathbf{x}_{i\natural})=\underset{0}{\overset{1}{\int}}||c_{i}^{\prime}(t)||dt=||c_{i}^{\prime}(0)||
.
Next, we define
c_{*}:[0,1]\rightarrow\mathcal{M}
to be the unique geodesic such that
c_{*}(0)=\mathbf{q}_{\natural},\,c_{*}^{{}^{\prime}}(0)=\frac{1}{N}\underset{i=1}{\overset{N}{\sum}}c_{i}^{{}^{\prime}}(0)
. Let
\mathbf{x}_{\natural}^{*}=c_{*}(1)
, which is clearly on
\mathcal{M}
.
Then,

\displaystyle d\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{x}_{i\natural},\;\mathcal{M}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\mathbf{x}_{\natural}^{*}-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{x}_{i\natural}\right\|_{2}

(A.128)

\displaystyle=\left\|c_{*}(0)+\underset{0}{\overset{1}{\int}}c_{*}^{{}^{\prime}}(t)dt-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(c_{i}(0)+\underset{0}{\overset{1}{\int}}c_{i}^{{}^{\prime}}(t)dt\right)\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|\underset{0}{\overset{1}{\int}}c_{*}^{{}^{\prime}}(t)dt-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\underset{0}{\overset{1}{\int}}c_{i}^{{}^{\prime}}(t)dt\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|\underset{0}{\overset{1}{\int}}\left(c_{*}^{{}^{\prime}}(0)+\underset{0}{\overset{t}{\int}}c_{*}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(a)da\right)dt-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\underset{0}{\overset{1}{\int}}\left(c_{i}^{{}^{\prime}}(0)+\underset{0}{\overset{t}{\int}}c_{i}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(a)da\right)dt\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|\underset{0}{\overset{1}{\int}}\underset{0}{\overset{t}{\int}}c_{*}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(a)da\>dt-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\underset{0}{\overset{1}{\int}}\underset{0}{\overset{t}{\int}}c_{i}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(a)da\>dt\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\underset{0}{\overset{1}{\int}}\underset{0}{\overset{t}{\int}}\left\|c_{*}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(a)-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}c_{i}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(a)\right\|_{2}da\>dt

\displaystyle\leq\underset{0}{\overset{1}{\int}}\underset{0}{\overset{t}{\int}}\left\|c^{{}^{\prime\prime}*}(a)\right\|_{2}+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\|c_{i}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(a)\right\|_{2}da\>dt

where in the third to last line, the
c_{*}^{{}^{\prime}}(0)
and
\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}c_{i}^{{}^{\prime}}(0)
gets canceled out by our definition of
c_{*}^{{}^{\prime}}(0)
.

Then, observing that we are left with only second derivatives in the expression above, we now need to consider the bounds by the curvature
\kappa
. Since we know for any geodesic curve c,
\|c^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(t)\|\leq\kappa\left\|c^{{}^{\prime}}(t)\right\|_{2}^{2}
, we have

\displaystyle d\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{x}_{i\natural},\;\mathcal{M}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\underset{0}{\overset{1}{\int}}\underset{0}{\overset{t}{\int}}\left\|c_{*}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(a)\right\|_{2}+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\|c_{i}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(a)\right\|_{2}da\>dt

(A.129)

\displaystyle\leq\underset{0}{\overset{1}{\int}}\underset{0}{\overset{t}{\int}}\kappa\left\|c_{*}^{{}^{\prime}}(0)\right\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\|c_{i}^{{}^{\prime}}(0)\right\|_{2}^{2}da\>dt

\displaystyle\leq\underset{0}{\overset{1}{\int}}\underset{0}{\overset{t}{\int}}\kappa\left\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}c_{i}^{{}^{\prime}}(0)\right\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\|c_{i}^{{}^{\prime}}(0)\right\|_{2}^{2}da\>dt

\displaystyle\leq\underset{0}{\overset{1}{\int}}\underset{0}{\overset{t}{\int}}\frac{2\kappa}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\|c_{i}^{{}^{\prime}}(0)\right\|_{2}^{2}da\>dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}d_{\mathcal{M}}^{2}\left(\mathbf{x}_{i\natural},\mathbf{q}_{\natural}\right)

This completes the proof.

Appendix B
The “Noise Size” Subproblem

The goal of this section is to upper bound the size of the conditional expectation of the noise
\mathbf{z}_{i}
for points
\mathbf{z}_{i}
assigned to landmark
\mathbf{q}
:

\left\|\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\,\mathbf{z}_{i}\mid\|\mathbf{x}_{i}+\mathbf{z}_{i}-\mathbf{q}\|\leq R\,\Bigr]\right\|.

(B.1)

We prove:

Theorem B.1

Suppose that
\kappa\,\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{M})\geq 1
and
\sigma>D^{-1}
. There exist numerical constants
C_{1}\dots C_{4}
,
c_{1},c_{2}>0
, such that whenever
D>C_{1}
,
d(\mathbf{q},\mathcal{M})<\tfrac{c_{1}}{\kappa}
, and
s_{\star}^{2}=R^{2}-\sigma^{2}(D-3)
satisfies
s_{\star,\parallel}^{2}=s_{\star}^{2}-d^{2}(\mathbf{q},\mathcal{M})\geq 0,
and

\max\left\{C_{2}\left(\log\left(D\right)+\kappa d\,\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{M})+d\log(\frac{1}{\kappa s_{\star,\parallel}})\right)\,\sigma^{2}D^{2/3},d^{2}(\mathbf{q},\mathcal{M})\right\}\leq s_{\star}^{2}\leq\min\Bigl\{3\sigma^{2}D,d^{2}(\mathbf{q},\mathcal{M})+\frac{c_{2}}{\kappa^{2}}\Bigr\}

(B.2)

we have

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\,\mathbf{z}_{i}\mid\|\mathbf{x}_{i}+\mathbf{z}_{i}-\mathbf{q}\|\leq R\,\Bigr]\right\|\;\leq\;\frac{C_{3}s_{\star}}{(D-3)^{1/2}}\sqrt{\log\left(D\right)+\kappa d\,\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{M})+d\log(\frac{1}{\kappa s_{\star,\parallel}})},

(B.3)

Proof

Let
\mathcal{E}_{i}
denote the event that
\|\mathbf{x}_{i}+\mathbf{z}_{i}-\mathbf{x}_{1}\|\leq R
. Using the tower property of the conditional expectation, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\mathbf{z}_{i}\mid\mathcal{E}_{i},\mathbf{x}_{1}\Bigr]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{x}_{i\natural}\mid\mathcal{E}_{i},\mathbf{x}_{1}}\Biggl[\,\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\mathbf{z}_{i}\mid\mathcal{E}_{i},\mathbf{x}_{1},\mathbf{x}_{i\natural}\Bigr]\,\Biggr]

(B.4)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{i\natural}}\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\mathbf{z}_{i}\mid\mathcal{E}_{i},\mathbf{x}_{1},\mathbf{x}_{i\natural}\Bigr]f_{\mathbf{x}_{i\natural}\mid\mathcal{E}_{i},\mathbf{x}_{1}}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{i\natural})\,d\mu_{0}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{i\natural})

(B.5)

The conditional expectation is

\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\,\mathbf{z}_{i}\mid\mathcal{E}_{i},\mathbf{x}_{1},\mathbf{x}_{i\natural}\,\Bigr]=\frac{\int_{\bar{\mathbf{z}}_{i}}\bar{\mathbf{z}}_{i}f_{\mathbf{z}}(\bar{\mathbf{z}_{i}})\mathbbm{1}_{\|\mathbf{z}_{i}-(\mathbf{x}_{1}-\mathbf{x}_{i\natural})\|_{2}\leq R}\,d\bar{\mathbf{z}}_{i}}{\int_{\bar{\mathbf{z}}_{i}}f_{\mathbf{z}}(\bar{\mathbf{z}_{i}})\mathbbm{1}_{\|\mathbf{z}_{i}-(\mathbf{x}_{1}-\mathbf{x}_{i\natural})\|_{2}\leq R}\,d\bar{\mathbf{z}}_{i}}.

(B.6)

Meanwhile the density is

\displaystyle f_{\mathbf{x}_{i\natural}\mid\mathcal{E}_{i},\mathbf{x}_{1}}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{i\natural})=\frac{\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}_{i}\mid\mathbf{x}_{i\natural}=\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{i\natural},\mathbf{x}_{1}]\rho_{\natural}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{i\natural})}{\int_{\bar{\bar{\mathbf{x}}}_{i\natural}}\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}_{i}\mid\mathbf{x}_{i\natural}=\bar{\bar{\mathbf{x}}}_{i\natural},\mathbf{x}_{1}]\rho_{\natural}(\bar{\bar{\mathbf{x}}}_{i\natural})d\mu_{0}(\bar{\bar{\mathbf{x}}}_{i\natural})},

(B.7)

whence

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\,\mathbf{z}_{i}\mid\mathcal{E}_{i},\mathbf{x}_{1}\,\Bigr]=\int_{\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{i\natural}}\frac{\int_{\bar{\mathbf{z}}_{i}}\bar{\mathbf{z}}_{i}f_{\mathbf{z}}(\bar{\mathbf{z}_{i}})\mathbbm{1}_{\|\mathbf{z}_{i}-(\mathbf{x}_{1}-\mathbf{x}_{i\natural})\|_{2}\leq R}\,d\bar{\mathbf{z}}_{i}}{\int_{\bar{\mathbf{z}}_{i}}f_{\mathbf{z}}(\bar{\mathbf{z}_{i}})\mathbbm{1}_{\|\mathbf{z}_{i}-(\mathbf{x}_{1}-\mathbf{x}_{i\natural})\|_{2}\leq R}\,d\bar{\mathbf{z}}_{i}}\,\frac{\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}_{i}\mid\mathbf{x}_{i\natural}=\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{i\natural},\mathbf{x}_{1}]\rho_{\natural}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{i\natural})}{\int_{\bar{\bar{\mathbf{x}}}_{i\natural}}\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}_{i}\mid\mathbf{x}_{i\natural}=\bar{\bar{\mathbf{x}}}_{i\natural},\mathbf{x}_{1}]\rho_{\natural}(\bar{\bar{\mathbf{x}}}_{i\natural})d\mu_{0}(\bar{\bar{\mathbf{x}}}_{i\natural})}\,d\mu_{0}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{i\natural}).

We can simplify this expression by noting that

\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}_{i}\mid\mathbf{x}_{i\natural}=\bar{\bar{\mathbf{x}}}_{i\natural},\mathbf{x}_{1}]=\int_{\bar{\mathbf{z}}_{i}}f_{\mathbf{z}}(\bar{\mathbf{z}_{i}})\mathbbm{1}_{\|\mathbf{z}_{i}-(\mathbf{x}_{1}-\mathbf{x}_{i\natural})\|_{2}\leq R}\,d\bar{\mathbf{z}}_{i},

(B.8)

yielding

\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\,\mathbf{z}_{i}\mid\mathcal{E}_{i},\mathbf{x}_{1}\,\Bigr]=\frac{\int_{\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{i\natural}}\int_{\bar{\mathbf{z}}_{i}}\bar{\mathbf{z}}_{i}f_{\mathbf{z}}(\bar{\mathbf{z}_{i}})\mathbbm{1}_{\|\mathbf{z}_{i}-(\mathbf{x}_{1}-\mathbf{x}_{i\natural})\|_{2}\leq R}\,d\bar{\mathbf{z}}_{i}\rho_{\natural}(\bar{\bar{\mathbf{x}}}_{i\natural})\,d\mu_{0}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{i\natural})}{\int_{\bar{\bar{\mathbf{x}}}_{i\natural}}\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}_{i}\mid\mathbf{x}_{i\natural}=\bar{\bar{\mathbf{x}}}_{i\natural},\mathbf{x}_{1}]\rho_{\natural}(\bar{\bar{\mathbf{x}}}_{i\natural})d\mu_{0}(\bar{\bar{\mathbf{x}}}_{i\natural})}.

(B.9)

In the sequel, we assume a uniform density
\rho_{\natural}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{i\natural})=\rho_{0}
, so the above simplifies to

\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\,\mathbf{z}_{i}\mid\mathcal{E}_{i},\mathbf{x}_{1}\,\Bigr]=\frac{\int_{\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{i\natural}}\int_{\bar{\mathbf{z}}_{i}}\bar{\mathbf{z}}_{i}f_{\mathbf{z}}(\bar{\mathbf{z}_{i}})\mathbbm{1}_{\|\mathbf{z}_{i}-(\mathbf{x}_{1}-\mathbf{x}_{i\natural})\|_{2}\leq R}\,d\bar{\mathbf{z}}_{i}\,d\mu_{0}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{i\natural})}{\int_{\bar{\bar{\mathbf{x}}}_{i\natural}}\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}_{i}\mid\mathbf{x}_{i\natural}=\bar{\bar{\mathbf{x}}}_{i\natural},\mathbf{x}_{1}]d\mu_{0}(\bar{\bar{\mathbf{x}}}_{i\natural})}.

(B.10)

We let
\Upsilon
denote the normalizing constant in the denominator:

\Upsilon=\int_{\bar{\bar{\mathbf{x}}}_{i\natural}}\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}_{i}\mid\mathbf{x}_{i\natural}=\bar{\bar{\mathbf{x}}}_{i\natural},\mathbf{x}_{1}]\,d\mu_{0}(\bar{\bar{\mathbf{x}}}_{i\natural})

(B.11)

For the numerator, using the rotational invariance of the Gaussian distribution, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\bar{\mathbf{z}}_{i}}\bar{\mathbf{z}}_{i}f_{\mathbf{z}}(\bar{\mathbf{z}_{i}})\mathbbm{1}_{\|\mathbf{z}_{i}-(\mathbf{x}_{1}-\mathbf{x}_{i\natural})\|_{2}\leq R}\,d\bar{\mathbf{z}}_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbf{x}_{1}-\mathbf{x}_{i\natural}}{\|\mathbf{x}_{1}-\mathbf{x}_{i\natural}\|_{2}}\left[\int_{\mathbf{v}=(v_{1},\dots v_{D})}\mathbf{v}_{1}f_{\mathbf{z}}(\mathbf{v})\mathbbm{1}_{\|\mathbf{v}-t\mathbf{e}_{1}\|\leq R}\,d\mathbf{v}\,\right]\Biggr|_{t=\|\mathbf{x}_{1}-\mathbf{x}_{i\natural}\|}\quad

(B.12)

Below, we will let

\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{x}_{1}-\mathbf{x}_{i\natural})=\frac{\mathbf{x}_{1}-\mathbf{x}_{i\natural}}{\|\mathbf{x}_{1}-\mathbf{x}_{i\natural}\|_{2}}.

(B.13)

In that language,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\,\mathbf{z}_{i}\mid\mathcal{E}_{i},\mathbf{x}_{1}\,\Bigr]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Upsilon^{-1}\int_{\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{i\natural}}\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{x}_{1}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{i\natural})\int_{\mathbf{v}=(v_{1},\dots v_{D})}v_{1}f_{\mathbf{z}}(\mathbf{v})\mathbbm{1}_{\|\mathbf{v}-t\mathbf{e}_{1}\|\leq R}\,d\mathbf{v}\,\Bigr|_{t=\|\mathbf{x}_{1}-\mathbf{x}_{i\natural}\|}\,d\mu_{0}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{i\natural}).\quad

(B.14)

We can develop the inner integrand as follows:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{v}=(v_{1},\dots v_{D})}v_{1}f_{\mathbf{z}}(\mathbf{g})\mathbbm{1}_{\|\mathbf{v}-t\mathbf{e}_{1}\|\leq R}\,d\mathbf{v}

\displaystyle=\int_{v_{1}}v_{1}f_{z_{1}}(v_{1})\Biggl[\int_{v_{2},\dots,v_{D}}f_{z_{2},\dots,z_{D}}(v_{2},\dots,v_{D})\mathbbm{1}_{v_{2}^{2}+\dots+v_{D}^{2}\leq R^{2}-(t-v_{1})^{2}}dv_{2},\dots,v_{D}\Biggr]dv_{1}

(B.15)

\displaystyle=\int_{v_{1}}v_{1}f_{z_{1}}(v_{1})h(t-v_{1})dv_{1}

(B.16)

where

\displaystyle h(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{g_{2},\dots,g_{D}\sim_{\mathrm{iid}}\mathcal{N}(0,1)}\left[g_{2}^{2}+\dots+g_{D}^{2}\leq\frac{R^{2}-s^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}\right]

(B.17)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{X\sim\chi^{2}_{D-1}}\left[X\leq\frac{R^{2}-s^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Gamma(\tfrac{D-1}{2})}\gamma\left(\frac{D-1}{2},\frac{R^{2}-s^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right).

(B.18)

We can recognize the integral (
B.16
) as a convolution between
\psi(v_{1})=v_{1}f_{z_{1}}(v_{1})
and
h
. Moreover, since

\displaystyle\phi(v_{1})\;\doteq\;f_{z_{1}}(v_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma}\exp\left(-\frac{v_{1}^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right)

(B.19)

\displaystyle\dot{\phi}(v_{1})\;\doteq\;\dot{f}_{z_{1}}(v_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{v_{1}}{\sigma^{2}}f_{z_{1}}(v_{1})

(B.20)

\displaystyle\psi(v_{1})\;\doteq\;v_{1}f_{z_{1}}(v_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sigma^{2}\dot{\phi}(v_{1}),

(B.21)

and (
B.16
) satisfies

\displaystyle\int_{v_{1}}v_{1}f_{z_{1}}(v_{1})h(t-v_{1})\,dv_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\psi\ast h](t)

(B.22)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sigma^{2}[-\dot{\phi}\ast h](t)

(B.23)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sigma^{2}[-\phi\ast\dot{h}](t).

(B.24)

Summing up, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\,\mathbf{z}_{i}\mid\mathcal{E}_{i},\mathbf{x}_{1}\,\Bigr]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\int_{\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{i\natural}}\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{x}_{1}-\mathbf{x}_{i\natural})\,\sigma^{2}\bigl[-\phi\ast\dot{h}\bigr]\bigl(\|\mathbf{x}_{1}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{i\natural}\|\bigr)\;d\mu_{0}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{i\natural})}{\int_{\bar{\bar{\mathbf{x}}}_{i\natural}}\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}_{i}\mid\mathbf{x}_{i\natural}=\bar{\bar{\mathbf{x}}}_{i\natural},\mathbf{x}_{1}]\,d\mu_{0}(\bar{\bar{\mathbf{x}}}_{i\natural})}

(B.25)

We can develop the denominator in similar terms: again using the rotational invariance of the gaussian distribution, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}_{i}\mid\mathbf{x}_{i\natural}=\bar{\bar{\mathbf{x}}}_{i\natural},\mathbf{x}_{1}]\quad=\quad\left[\int_{v_{1}\dots v_{D}}f_{\mathbf{z}}(v_{1},\dots,v_{D})\mathbbm{1}_{(v_{1}-t)^{2}+v_{2}^{2}+\dots+v_{D}^{2}\leq R^{2}}\,dv_{1}\dots v_{D}\right]_{t=\|\mathbf{x}_{1}-\bar{\bar{\mathbf{x}}}_{i\natural}\|}

(B.26)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Biggl[\int_{v_{1}}f_{z_{1}}(v_{1})\Biggl[\int_{v_{2},\dots,v_{D}}f_{z_{2},\dots,z_{D}}(v_{2},\dots,v_{D})\mathbbm{1}_{v_{2}^{2}+\dots+v_{D}^{2}\leq R^{2}-(t-v_{1})^{2}}dv_{2},\dots,v_{D}\Biggr]dv_{1}\Biggr]_{\|\mathbf{x}_{1}-\bar{\bar{\mathbf{x}}}_{i\natural}\|}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Biggl[\int_{v_{1}}f_{z_{1}}(v_{1})h(t-v_{1})\,dv_{1}\Biggr]_{\|\mathbf{x}_{1}-\bar{\bar{\mathbf{x}}}_{i\natural}\|}

(B.27)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi\ast h(\|\mathbf{x}_{1}-\bar{\bar{\mathbf{x}}}_{i\natural}\|),

(B.28)

and overall we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\,\mathbf{z}_{i}\mid\mathcal{E}_{i},\mathbf{x}_{1}\,\Bigr]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\int_{\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{i\natural}}\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{x}_{1}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{i\natural})\,\sigma^{2}\bigl[-\phi\ast\dot{h}\bigr]\bigl(\|\mathbf{x}_{1}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{i\natural}\|\bigr)\;d\mu_{0}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{i\natural})}{\int_{\bar{\bar{\mathbf{x}}}_{i\natural}}[\phi\ast h](\|\mathbf{x}_{1}-\bar{\bar{\mathbf{x}}}_{i\natural}\|)\,d\mu_{0}(\bar{\bar{\mathbf{x}}}_{i\natural})}

(B.29)

We bound the norm of this quantity as follows. First, by the triangle inequality,

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\,\mathbf{z}_{i}\mid\mathcal{E}_{i},\mathbf{x}_{1}\,\Bigr]\right\|

\displaystyle=

giving the claim.

B.1
Technical Lemmas for Controlling the Noise Size

Consider the function

h(s)=\mathbb{P}_{g_{2},\dots,g_{D}\sim_{\mathrm{iid}}\mathcal{N}(0,1)}\left[g_{2}^{2}+\dots+g_{D}^{2}\leq\frac{R^{2}-s^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}\right].

(B.51)

Because
h(s)
is a decreasing function of
s
,

-\dot{h}(s)>0\quad\forall\;s.

(B.52)

Note that

h(s)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\tfrac{D-1}{2})}\gamma\left(\frac{D-1}{2},\frac{R^{2}-s^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right),

(B.53)

and

-\dot{h}(s)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\tfrac{D-1}{2})}\frac{s}{\sigma^{2}}\dot{\gamma}\left(\frac{D-1}{2},\frac{R^{2}-s^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right),

(B.54)

where
\gamma
is the lower incomplete gamma function

\gamma(p,x)=\int_{t=0}^{x}t^{p-1}e^{-t}dt.

(B.55)

The following lemma upper and lower bounds the function
\dot{\gamma}(p,x)
near the point
x_{\star}=p-1
:

Lemma B.2
(Subgaussian Bounds for
\dot{\gamma}
Near the Phase Transition.)

Let
p\geq 28
and
x_{\star}=p-1
. Then

\tfrac{1}{4}\dot{\gamma}(p,x_{\star})\exp\left(-\frac{(x-x_{\star})^{2}}{2x_{\star}}\right)\leq\dot{\gamma}(p,x)\leq 4\dot{\gamma}(p,x_{\star})\exp\left(-\frac{(x-x_{\star})^{2}}{2x_{\star}}\right)\qquad x\in\left[x_{\star}-x_{\star}^{2/3},x_{\star}+x_{\star}^{2/3}\right].

(B.56)

Proof

Please see Section
D.3
.

The following lemma uses these subgaussian bounds for the incomplete gamma function to obtain subgaussian bounds for the function
[-\dot{h}]
:

Lemma B.3
(Subgaussian Bounds for
-\dot{h}
.)

Suppose that
D>192
. Set

s_{\star}=\sqrt{R^{2}-\sigma^{2}(D-3)},

(B.57)

and suppose that

\sigma^{2}D^{1/2}\leq s_{\star}^{2}\leq 3\sigma^{2}D.

(B.58)

Then the function
-\dot{h}(s)
satisfies

\frac{1}{8e}\,C(D)\frac{s_{\star}}{\sigma^{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{\left(s-s_{\star}\right)^{2}}{2\nu^{2}}\right)\leq-\dot{h}(s)\leq 6e\,C(D)\frac{s_{\star}}{\sigma^{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{\left(s-s_{\star}\right)^{2}}{2\nu^{2}}\right)

(B.59)

when

|s-s_{\star}|\leq\tfrac{1}{3}\sigma(D-3)^{1/6}.

(B.60)

Here and in following lemmas,

C(D)=\frac{\dot{\gamma}\left(\frac{D-1}{2},\frac{D-3}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{D-1}{2}\right)},

(B.61)

and

\nu^{2}=\frac{\sigma^{4}(D-3)}{2s_{\star}^{2}}.

(B.62)

Proof
We first note that that when
\sigma^{2}D^{1/2}\leq s_{\star}^{2}\leq 3\sigma^{2}D
and

|s-s_{\star}|\leq\tfrac{1}{3}\sigma(D-3)^{1/6}

we have

|s-s_{\star}|<\tfrac{1}{2}s_{\star}

(B.63)

and

\frac{5s_{\star}|s-s_{\star}|^{3}}{4\sigma^{4}(D-3)}<1.

(B.64)

We would like to apply Lemma
B.2
(and noting that with
D>192
, the condition
p>28
of the lemma is satisfied for
p=(D-1)/2
) to bound
\dot{\gamma}
, we have

\displaystyle-\dot{h}(s)\quad=\quad\frac{1}{\Gamma\left(\frac{D-1}{2}\right)}\frac{s}{\sigma^{2}}\dot{\gamma}\left(\frac{D-1}{2},\frac{R^{2}-s^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right)

(B.65)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{4\dot{\gamma}\left(\frac{D-1}{2},\frac{D-3}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{D-1}{2}\right)}\frac{s}{\sigma^{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{\left(\frac{R^{2}-s^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}-\frac{D-3}{2}\right)^{2}}{D-3}\right)\qquad s_{\star}^{2}-\sigma^{2}D^{2/3}\leq s^{2}\leq s_{\star}^{2}+\sigma^{2}D^{2/3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4C(D)\frac{s}{\sigma^{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{\left(s_{\star}^{2}-s^{2}\right)^{2}}{4\sigma^{4}(D-3)}\right)\qquad s_{\star}^{2}-\sigma^{2}D^{2/3}\leq s^{2}\leq s_{\star}^{2}+\sigma^{2}D^{2/3}.

Here, a direct application of Lemma
B.2
ensures that these bounds hold when

\frac{D-3}{2}-\left(\frac{D-3}{2}\right)^{2/3}\leq\frac{R^{2}-s^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\leq\frac{D-3}{2}+\left(\frac{D-3}{2}\right)^{2/3}.

(B.66)

by plugging our definition of
s_{\star}^{2}=R^{2}-\sigma^{2}(D-3)
, this is equivalent as

\left|\frac{s_{\star}^{2}-s^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right|\leq\left(\frac{D-3}{2}\right)^{2/3}.

(B.67)

so it’s sufficient to show

|s_{\star}-s|(2s_{\star}+|s_{\star}-s|)\leq{2\sigma^{2}}\left(\frac{D-3}{2}\right)^{2/3}.

(B.68)

applying our bounds of
|s_{\star}-s|
and
s_{\star}
, it further suffices to show

\frac{1}{3}\sigma(D-3)^{1/6}(2\sqrt{3}\sigma\sqrt{D}+\frac{1}{3}\sigma(D-3)^{1/6})\leq{2\sigma^{2}}\left(\frac{D-3}{2}\right)^{2/3}.

(B.69)

rearranging the terms and canceling out
\sigma
, we need to show

\frac{2\sqrt{3}}{3}(D-3)^{1/6}\sqrt{D}+\frac{1}{9}(D-3)^{1/3}\leq 2^{1/3}\left(D-3\right)^{2/3}.

(B.70)

So as long as
D\geq 2^{6}\times 3,\sqrt{D}\leq\sqrt{D-3}\sqrt{\frac{64}{63}}
, and
(D-3)^{1/3}\geq 3
, we have

\frac{2\sqrt{3}}{3}(D-3)^{1/6}\sqrt{D}+\frac{1}{9}(D-3)^{1/3}\leq(\sqrt{\frac{64}{63}}\frac{2\sqrt{3}}{3}+\frac{1}{27})(D-3)^{2/3}\leq 2^{1/3}\left(D-3\right)^{2/3}.

(B.71)

so the conditions for Lemma
B.2
hold. We next build a subgaussian bound for the exponential. Using convexity of the function
f(t)=(2+t)^{2}
, i.e.,
f(t)\geq f(0)+4t,
we have

\displaystyle(s+s_{\star})^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s_{\star}^{2}\left(1+\frac{s}{s_{\star}}\right)^{2}

(B.72)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s_{\star}^{2}\left(2+\frac{s-s_{\star}}{s_{\star}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 4s_{\star}^{2}+4s_{\star}(s-s_{\star}).

Hence

\displaystyle-\dot{h}(s)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 4C(D)\frac{s}{\sigma^{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{\left(s-s_{\star}\right)^{2}\left(s+s_{\star}\right)^{2}}{4\sigma^{4}(D-3)}\right)

(B.73)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 4C(D)\frac{s}{\sigma^{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{\left(s-s_{\star}\right)^{2}s_{\star}^{2}}{\sigma^{4}(D-3)}\right)\exp\left(-\frac{s_{\star}(s-s_{\star})^{3}}{\sigma^{4}(D-3)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 4eC(D)\frac{s}{\sigma^{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{\left(s-s_{\star}\right)^{2}s_{\star}^{2}}{\sigma^{4}(D-3)}\right),

(B.74)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 6eC(D)\frac{s_{\star}}{\sigma^{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{\left(s-s_{\star}\right)^{2}s_{\star}^{2}}{\sigma^{4}(D-3)}\right),

(B.75)

where in (
B.74
) we have used (
B.64
) and in (
B.75
) we have used (
B.63
).
For the lower bound, we similarly note that

\displaystyle-\dot{h}(s)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}C(D)\frac{s}{\sigma^{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{\left(s_{\star}^{2}-s^{2}\right)^{2}}{4\sigma^{4}(D-3)}\right)\qquad s_{\star}^{2}-\sigma^{2}D^{2/3}\leq s^{2}\leq s_{\star}^{2}+\sigma^{2}D^{2/3}.

(B.76)

For
\tfrac{1}{2}s_{\star}\leq s\leq\tfrac{3}{2}s_{\star}
, we have

\displaystyle(s+s_{\star})^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s_{\star}^{2}\left(1+\frac{s}{s_{\star}}\right)^{2}

(B.77)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s_{\star}^{2}\left(2+\frac{s-s_{\star}}{s_{\star}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 4s_{\star}^{2}+5s_{\star}|s-s_{\star}|

we obtain

\displaystyle-\dot{h}(s)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}C(D)\frac{s}{\sigma^{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{\left(s-s_{\star}\right)^{2}s_{\star}^{2}}{\sigma^{4}(D-3)}\right)\exp\left(-\frac{5s_{\star}|s-s_{\star}|^{3}}{4\sigma^{4}(D-3)}\right)

(B.78)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4e}C(D)\frac{s}{\sigma^{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{\left(s-s_{\star}\right)^{2}s_{\star}^{2}}{\sigma^{4}(D-3)}\right)

(B.79)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{8e}C(D)\frac{s_{\star}}{\sigma^{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{\left(s-s_{\star}\right)^{2}s_{\star}^{2}}{\sigma^{4}(D-3)}\right)

(B.80)

where in (
B.79
) we have used (
B.64
) and in (
B.80
) we have used (
B.63
). This completes the proof.

Lemma B.4
(Monotonicity of
-\dot{h}
outside region of phase transition)

Suppose

96\log(6)\times\sigma^{2}D^{2/3}\leq s_{\star}^{2}\leq 3\sigma^{2}D

(B.81)

and
D>48^{3}+3
.

Set

\Delta_{s}=\tfrac{1}{12}\sigma(D-3)^{1/6}

Then

\displaystyle\forall s\leq s_{\star}-\Delta_{s},\frac{d}{ds}\Bigl[-\dot{h}(s)\Bigr]\geq 0

(B.82)

\displaystyle\forall s\geq s_{\star}+\Delta_{s},\frac{d}{ds}\Bigl[-\dot{h}(s)\Bigr]\leq 0

(B.83)

Proof

We note that in the following proof
C(D),C^{\prime}(D)
represent positive constants independent of
s
.
By definition, we have

\displaystyle-\dot{h}(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma\left(\frac{D-1}{2}\right)}\frac{s}{\sigma^{2}}\left(\frac{R^{2}-s^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right)^{(D-3)/2}\exp\left(-\frac{R^{2}-s^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right)

(B.84)

\displaystyle=C(D)s\left(R^{2}-s^{2}\right)^{(D-3)/2}\exp\left(-\frac{R^{2}-s^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=C^{\prime}(D)x\left(1-x^{2}\right)^{(D-3)/2}\exp\left(-\frac{R^{2}(1-x^{2})}{2\sigma^{2}}\right)\text{ where }x=\frac{s}{R}

\displaystyle=C^{\prime}(D)x\left(1-x^{2}\right)^{\alpha}\exp\left(-\beta(1-x^{2})\right)\text{ where }\alpha=\frac{D-3}{2},\beta=\frac{R^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}

Let
f(x)=x\left(1-x^{2}\right)^{\alpha}\exp\left(-\beta(1-x^{2})\right)
, then we have

\left.\frac{d}{ds}\left[-\dot{h}(s)\right]\right|_{s=s_{0}}\geq 0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\left.\frac{d}{dx}\left[f(x)\right]\right|_{x=\frac{s_{0}}{R}}\geq 0.

(B.85)

We can further compute

f^{\prime}(x)=\left(1-x^{2}\right)^{\alpha-1}\exp\left(-\beta(1-x^{2})\right)\left(-2\beta x^{4}+(-2\alpha+2\beta-1)x^{2}+1\right)

(B.86)

which in turn implies that

\frac{d}{dx}\Bigl[f(x)\Bigr]\geq 0\Longleftrightarrow-2\beta x^{4}+(-2\alpha+2\beta-1)x^{2}+1\geq 0

(B.87)

Since the right hand side is a downward-facing parabolic function with respect to
x^{2}
, with a positive root and a negative root, we have

\frac{d}{dx}\Bigl[f(x)\Bigr]\geq 0\longleftrightarrow x^{2}\leq\frac{-2\alpha+2\beta-1+\sqrt{(-2\alpha+2\beta-1)^{2}+8\beta}}{4\beta}

(B.88)

By assumption
s_{\star}^{2}\geq\sigma^{2}D^{2/3}
, so we make the observation that
-2\alpha+2\beta-1=\frac{R^{2}-(D-2)\sigma^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}=\frac{{s_{\star}}^{2}-\sigma^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}\geq 0
.

Then, we can plug
\alpha
and
\beta
back into the expression of the root on the right hand side of
(
B.88
), after simplifying, we have

\frac{d}{dx}\Bigl[f(x)\Bigr]\geq 0\longleftrightarrow x^{2}\leq\frac{{s_{\star}}^{2}-\sigma^{2}+\sqrt{({s_{\star}}^{2}-\sigma^{2})^{2}+4R^{2}\sigma^{2}}}{2R^{2}}

(B.89)

Lastly, to show
\forall s\leq s_{\star}-\Delta_{s},\frac{d}{ds}\Bigl[-\dot{h}(s)\Bigr]\geq 0
, it’s sufficient to show

\frac{(s_{\star}-\Delta_{s})^{2}}{R^{2}}\leq\frac{{s_{\star}}^{2}-\sigma^{2}+\sqrt{({s_{\star}}^{2}-\sigma^{2})^{2}+4R^{2}\sigma^{2}}}{2R^{2}}

(B.90)

it’s further sufficient to show

(s_{\star}-\Delta_{s})^{2}\leq{s_{\star}}^{2}-\sigma^{2}

(B.91)

which is equivalent of

{s_{\star}}^{2}(1-\frac{\Delta_{s}}{s_{\star}})^{2}\leq{s_{\star}}^{2}(1-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{{s_{\star}}^{2}})

(B.92)

So it suffices to show

1-\frac{\Delta_{s}}{s_{\star}}\leq 1-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{{s_{\star}}^{2}}

(B.93)

which is the same as

s_{\star}\Delta_{s}\geq\sigma^{2}

(B.94)

Since
s_{\star}\geq\sigma D^{1/3}
and
\Delta_{s}=\tfrac{1}{12}\sigma(D-3)^{1/6}
, with the given lower bound on D, (
B.94
) is trivially satisfied.

Similarly, to show
\forall s\geq s_{\star}+\Delta_{s},\frac{d}{ds}\Bigl[-\dot{h}(s)\Bigr]\leq 0
, it’s sufficient to show

\frac{(s_{\star}-\Delta_{s})^{2}}{R^{2}}\geq\frac{{s_{\star}}^{2}-\sigma^{2}+\sqrt{({s_{\star}}^{2}-\sigma^{2})^{2}+4R^{2}\sigma^{2}}}{2R^{2}}

(B.95)

it’s further sufficient to show

(s_{\star}+\Delta_{s})^{2}\geq\sqrt{({s_{\star}}^{2}-\sigma^{2})^{2}+4R^{2}\sigma^{2}}

(B.96)

which is equivalent of

(s_{\star}+\Delta_{s})^{4}\geq({s_{\star}}^{2}-\sigma^{2})^{2}+4R^{2}\sigma^{2}

(B.97)

Since
{s_{\star}}^{4}\geq({s_{\star}}^{2}-\sigma^{2})^{2}
, it’s enough to show

4{s_{\star}}^{3}\Delta_{s}+6{s_{\star}}^{2}{\Delta_{s}}^{2}\geq 4R^{2}\sigma^{2}

(B.98)

Since we have defined
{s_{\star}}^{2}=R^{2}-\sigma^{2}(D-3)
and recall from (
B.94
)
s_{\star}\Delta_{s}\geq\sigma^{2}

\displaystyle 4{s_{\star}}^{3}\Delta_{s}+6{s_{\star}}^{2}{\Delta_{s}}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq 4(R^{2}-\sigma^{2}(D-3))\sigma^{2}+6{s_{\star}}^{2}{\Delta_{s}}^{2}

(B.99)

\displaystyle=4R^{2}\sigma^{2}-4\sigma^{4}(D-3)+6{s_{\star}}^{2}\frac{1}{144}\sigma^{2}(D-3)^{1/3}

\displaystyle\geq 4R^{2}\sigma^{2}-4\sigma^{4}(D-3)+4\log(6)\sigma^{4}D^{2/3}(D-3)^{1/3}

\displaystyle\geq 4R^{2}\sigma^{2}

where in the second line we have used definition
\Delta_{s}=\tfrac{1}{12}\sigma(D-3)^{1/6}
,and in the third line we used the assumption
{s_{\star}}^{2}\geq 96\log(6)\times\sigma^{2}D^{2/3}

Convolutions with
\phi
.

The following lemma controls the convolution

Lemma B.5
(Upper and Lower Bounds with
\phi\ast-\dot{h}
)

Suppose

24\log(6)\times\sigma^{2}D^{2/3}\leq s_{\star}^{2}\leq 3\sigma^{2}D

(B.100)

and
D>(48\log(6))^{3}+3
. Then

\displaystyle\Bigl[\phi\ast-\dot{h}\Bigr](s)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 9eC(D)\frac{\nu}{(\nu^{2}+\sigma^{2})^{1/2}}\frac{s_{\star}}{\sigma^{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{\left(s-s_{\star}\right)^{2}}{2(\nu^{2}+\sigma^{2})}\right)

(B.101)

\displaystyle\Bigl[\phi\ast-\dot{h}\Bigr](s)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{16e}C(D)\frac{\nu}{(\nu^{2}+\sigma^{2})^{1/2}}\frac{s_{\star}}{\sigma^{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{\left(s-s_{\star}\right)^{2}}{2(\nu^{2}+\sigma^{2})}\right)

(B.102)

when

|s-s_{\star}|\leq\tfrac{1}{6}\sigma(D-3)^{1/6}.

(B.103)

Proof
Since

s_{\star}^{2}\leq 3\sigma^{2}D,D\geq 48^{3}+3
, we have

\nu^{2}=\frac{\sigma^{4}(D-3)}{2s_{\star}^{2}}\geq\frac{\sigma^{2}}{6},

(B.104)

which in turn implies that

\frac{\nu}{(\nu^{2}+\sigma^{2})^{1/2}}=\left(\frac{\nu^{2}}{\nu^{2}+\sigma^{2}}\right)^{1/2}\geq\left(\frac{1/6}{1/6+1}\right)^{1/2}>\frac{1}{3}.

(B.105)

Set

\displaystyle s_{\min}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s_{\star}-\tfrac{1}{3}\sigma(D-3)^{1/6},\quad s_{\max}\;=\;s_{\star}+\tfrac{1}{3}\sigma(D-3)^{1/6},

(B.106)

\displaystyle s_{\min}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s_{\star}-\tfrac{1}{6}\sigma(D-3)^{1/6},\quad s_{\max}^{\prime}\;=\;s_{\star}+\tfrac{1}{6}\sigma(D-3)^{1/6}.

(B.107)

We have

\displaystyle\exp\left(-\frac{(s_{\max}^{\prime}-s_{\star})^{2}}{2(\nu^{2}+\sigma^{2})}\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\exp\left(-\frac{(s_{\max}^{\prime}-s_{\star})^{2}}{2\nu^{2}}\right)

(B.108)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\exp\left(-\frac{1}{4}\frac{(s_{\max}-s_{\star})^{2}}{2\nu^{2}}\right)\;=\;\left[\exp\left(-\frac{(s_{\max}-s_{\star})^{2}}{2\nu^{2}}\right)\right]^{1/4}

and so

\displaystyle\exp\left(-\frac{(s_{\max}-s_{\star})^{2}}{2\nu^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\nu}{(\nu^{2}+\sigma^{2})^{1/2}}\exp\left(-\frac{(s_{\max}^{\prime}-s_{\star})^{2}}{2(\nu^{2}+\sigma^{2})}\right)\times 3\left[\exp\left(-\frac{(s_{\max}^{\prime}-s_{\star})^{2}}{2(\nu^{2}+\sigma^{2})}\right)\right]^{3}

(B.109)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\nu}{(\nu^{2}+\sigma^{2})^{1/2}}\exp\left(-\frac{(s_{\max}^{\prime}-s_{\star})^{2}}{2(\nu^{2}+\sigma^{2})}\right)\times 3\exp\left(-\frac{(D-3)^{1/3}}{24}\frac{\sigma^{2}}{\nu^{2}+\sigma^{2}}\right).

(B.110)

Under our hypotheses on
s_{\star}
and
D
,

\frac{(D-3)^{1/3}}{24}\frac{\sigma^{2}}{\nu^{2}+\sigma^{2}}\geq\log 6,

(B.111)

and so for any

\max\left\{\exp\left(-\frac{(s_{\max}-s_{\star})^{2}}{2\nu^{2}}\right),\exp\left(-\frac{(s_{\min}-s_{\star})^{2}}{2\nu^{2}}\right)\right\}\leq\frac{1}{2}\frac{\nu}{(\nu^{2}+\sigma^{2})^{1/2}}\exp\left(-\frac{(s-s_{\star})^{2}}{2(\nu^{2}+\sigma^{2})}\right).

(B.112)

We use this relationship to establish the desired bounds:

Upper bound:
Write

\omega(s)=6eC(D)\frac{s_{\star}}{\sigma^{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{(s-s_{\star})^{2}}{2\nu^{2}}\right),

(B.113)

and notice that on
[s_{\min},s_{\max}]
the subgaussian approximation (
B.59
) is valid. Then, using the monotonicity of
-\dot{h}
from lemma
B.4
, for all

-\dot{h}(s)\leq\omega(s)+\max\Bigl\{\omega(s_{\min}),\omega(s_{\max})\Bigr\}\mathbbm{1}_{s<s_{\min}\cup s>s_{\max}},

(B.114)

whence

\displaystyle\bigl[\phi\ast-\dot{h}\bigr](s)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\phi\ast\omega(s)+\max\Bigl\{\omega(s_{\min}),\omega(s_{\max})\Bigr\}

(B.115)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 6eC(D)\frac{s_{\star}}{\sigma^{2}}\frac{\nu}{(\nu^{2}+\sigma^{2})^{1/2}}\exp\left(-\frac{(s-s_{\star})^{2}}{2(\nu^{2}+\sigma^{2})}\right)+\max\Bigl\{\omega(s_{\min}),\omega(s_{\max})\Bigr\},

(B.116)

where the first term follows from standard convolution identities (c.f., the convolution of two gaussians is itself a gaussian). Using (

)

\max\Bigl\{\omega(s_{\min}),\omega(s_{\max})\Bigr\}\leq 3eC(D)\frac{s_{\star}}{\sigma^{2}}\frac{\nu}{(\nu^{2}+\sigma^{2})^{1/2}}\exp\left(-\frac{(s-s_{\star})^{2}}{2(\nu^{2}+\sigma^{2})}\right)

(B.117)

for any

Lower bound:
Similarly, letting

\eta(s)=\frac{1}{8e}C(D)\frac{s_{\star}}{\sigma^{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{(s-s_{\star})^{2}}{2\nu^{2}}\right)

(B.118)

(B.119)

and

\displaystyle\bigl[\phi\ast-\dot{h}\bigr](s)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{8e}C(D)\frac{s_{\star}}{\sigma^{2}}\frac{\nu}{(\nu^{2}+\sigma^{2})^{1/2}}\exp\left(-\frac{(s-s_{\star})^{2}}{2(\nu^{2}+\sigma^{2})}\right)-\max\Bigl\{\eta(s_{\min}),\eta(s_{\max})\Bigr\}.

(B.120)

By the same reasoning above, we have

\max\Bigl\{\eta(s_{\min}),\eta(s_{\max})\Bigr\}\leq\frac{1}{16e}C(D)\frac{s_{\star}}{\sigma^{2}}\frac{\nu}{(\nu^{2}+\sigma^{2})^{1/2}}\exp\left(-\frac{(s-s_{\star})^{2}}{2(\nu^{2}+\sigma^{2})}\right)

(B.121)

for any
s\in[s_{\min}^{\prime},s_{\max}^{\prime}]
. Combining with (

) gives the claimed lower bound.

Lemma B.6
(Upper and Lower Bounds on
\phi\ast h
)

Suppose that

576\log(2)\times\sigma^{2}D^{2/3}\leq s_{\star}^{2}\leq 3\sigma^{2}D

(B.122)

and

D>(576\log(2))^{3}+3

(B.123)

We have

\displaystyle\Bigl[\phi\ast h\Bigr](s)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\frac{1}{64e^{2}}\cdot C(D)\frac{s_{\star}\nu}{\sigma^{2}}&0\leq s\leq s_{\star}+\bar{\nu}\\
\frac{1}{64e}\cdot C(D)\frac{s_{\star}\nu}{\sigma^{2}}\frac{\bar{\nu}}{s-s_{\star}}\exp\left(-\frac{(s-s_{\star})^{2}}{2(\nu^{2}+\sigma^{2})}\right)&s_{\star}+\bar{\nu}\leq s\leq s_{\star}+\tfrac{1}{12}\sigma(D-3)^{1/6}\end{cases}\quad

(B.124)

\displaystyle\Bigl[\phi\ast h\Bigr](s)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\begin{cases}1&0\leq s<\check{s}_{\star}\\
4\exp\left(-\frac{(s-\check{s}_{\star})^{2}}{2(\check{\nu}^{2}+\sigma^{2})}\right)&s\geq\check{s}_{\star}.\end{cases}

(B.125)

where

\displaystyle\check{s}_{\star}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R^{2}-\sigma^{2}(D-1),

(B.126)

\displaystyle\check{\nu}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma^{4}(D-1)}{2\check{s}_{\star}^{2}}.

(B.127)

Proof

Set
s_{\max}=s_{\star}+\tfrac{1}{6}\sigma(D-3)^{1/6}
. By Lemma
B.5
any
s\in(s_{\star},s_{\max})
we have

\displaystyle\bigl[\phi\ast h\bigr](s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigl[\phi\ast h\bigr](s_{\max})-\int_{t=s}^{s_{\max}}\frac{d}{du}[\phi\ast h]\Bigr|_{u=t}dt

(B.128)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigl[\phi\ast h\bigr](s_{\max})-\int_{t=s}^{s_{\max}}[\phi\ast\dot{h}](t)\,dt

(B.129)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{t=s}^{s_{\max}}[\phi\ast-\dot{h}](t)\,dt

(B.130)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{16e}C(D)\frac{s_{\star}}{\sigma^{2}}\frac{\nu}{(\nu^{2}+\sigma^{2})^{1/2}}\int_{t=s}^{s_{\max}}\exp\Bigl(-\frac{(t-s_{\star})^{2}}{2(\nu^{2}+\sigma^{2})}\Bigr)dt

(B.131)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{16e}C(D)\frac{\nu s_{\star}}{\sigma^{2}}\int_{u=\frac{s-s_{\star}}{(\nu^{2}+\sigma^{2})^{1/2}}}^{\frac{s_{\max}-s_{\star}}{(\nu^{2}+\sigma^{2})^{1/2}}}\exp\Bigl(-\frac{u^{2}}{2}\Bigr)du

(B.132)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}{16e}C(D)\frac{\nu s_{\star}}{\sigma^{2}}\right]\Biggl(Q\left(\frac{s-s_{\star}}{\bar{\nu}}\right)-Q\left(\frac{s_{\max}-s_{\star}}{\bar{\nu}}\right)\Biggr)

(B.133)

where
Q(\cdot)
is the Gaussian

and we have used the change of variables

u=\frac{t-s_{\star}}{(\nu^{2}+\sigma^{2})^{1/2}}.

(B.134)

Note that for arbitrary
u>\Delta\geq 0
,

\displaystyle Q(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{v=u}^{\infty}e^{-v^{2}/2}dv=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{v=u-\Delta}^{\infty}e^{-(v+\Delta)^{2}/2}dv

(B.135)

\displaystyle\frac{e^{-\Delta^{2}/2}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{v=u-\Delta}^{\infty}e^{-v^{2}/2}dv

(B.136)

\displaystyle e^{-\Delta^{2}/2}Q(u-\Delta).

\displaystyle\exp\left(-\frac{(s_{\max}-s_{\max}^{\prime})^{2}}{2\bar{\nu}^{2}}\right)Q\left(\frac{s_{\max}^{\prime}-s_{\star}}{\bar{\nu}}\right)

(B.138)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\exp\left(-\frac{\sigma^{2}(D-3)^{1/3}}{288\bar{\nu}^{2}}\right)Q\left(\frac{s_{\max}^{\prime}-s_{\star}}{\bar{\nu}}\right)

(B.139)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}Q\left(\frac{s_{\max}^{\prime}-s_{\star}}{\bar{\nu}}\right),

(B.140)

where in the final line we have used the fact that our hypotheses on

and

(B.141)

\displaystyle[\phi\ast h](s)\geq\left[\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}{32e}C(D)\frac{\nu s_{\star}}{\sigma^{2}}\right]Q\left(\frac{s-s_{\star}}{\bar{\nu}}\right).

(B.144)

(B.146)

[\phi\ast h](s)\geq\frac{1}{64e^{2}}C(D)\frac{\nu s_{\star}}{\sigma^{2}}.

(B.147)

For the upper bound, we start with the following subgaussian bound for the lower tail of a
\chi
-square random variable (see
[
30
]
)

\mathbb{P}_{X\sim\chi^{2}_{D-1}}\Bigl[\,X\leq\mathbb{E}[X]-t\,\Bigr]\leq\exp\left(-\frac{t^{2}}{4(D-1)}\right)

(B.148)

Setting
\check{s}_{\star}^{2}=R^{2}-\sigma^{2}(D-1)
, for
s>\check{s}_{\star}
we have

\displaystyle h(s)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\exp\left(-\frac{\left(\frac{R^{2}-s^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}-(D-1)\right)^{2}}{4(D-1)}\right)

(B.149)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\exp\left(-\frac{\left(R^{2}-s^{2}-\sigma^{2}(D-1)\right)^{2}}{4\sigma^{4}(D-1)}\right)

(B.150)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\exp\left(-\frac{\left(s^{2}-\check{s}_{\star}^{2}\right)^{2}}{4\sigma^{4}(D-1)}\right)

(B.151)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\exp\left(-\frac{(s-\check{s}_{\star})^{2}(s+\check{s}_{\star})^{2}}{4\sigma^{4}(D-1)}\right)

(B.152)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\exp\left(-\frac{4\check{s}_{\star}^{2}(s-\check{s}_{\star})^{2}}{4\sigma^{4}(D-1)}\right)

(B.153)

\displaystyle\exp\left(-\frac{(s-\check{s}_{\star})^{2}}{2\check{\nu}^{2}}\right)

(B.154)

with

\check{\nu}^{2}=\frac{\sigma^{4}(D-1)}{2\check{s}_{\star}^{2}}.

(B.155)

In total, we have

h(s)\leq\begin{cases}1&s\leq\check{s}_{\star}\\
\exp\left(-\frac{(s-\check{s}_{\star})^{2}}{2\check{\nu}^{2}}\right)&s>\check{s}_{\star}\end{cases}

(B.156)

Writing
\omega(s)=\exp\left(-\frac{(s-\check{s}_{\star})^{2}}{2\check{\nu}^{2}}\right)
, we have

, and so for

\displaystyle[\phi\ast h](s)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle[\phi\ast\omega](s)+\int_{s^{\prime}=0}^{\check{s}_{\star}}\phi(s-s^{\prime})\,ds^{\prime},

(B.157)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\check{\nu}}{\sqrt{\check{\nu}^{2}+\sigma^{2}}}\exp\left(-\frac{(s-\check{s}_{\star})^{2}}{2(\check{\nu}^{2}+\sigma^{2})}\right)+Q\left(\frac{s-\check{s}_{\star}}{\sigma}\right),

(B.158)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\check{\nu}}{\sqrt{\check{\nu}^{2}+\sigma^{2}}}\exp\left(-\frac{(s-\check{s}_{\star})^{2}}{2(\check{\nu}^{2}+\sigma^{2})}\right)+\frac{\sigma}{s-\check{s}_{\star}}\exp\left(-\frac{(s-\check{s}_{\star})^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right).

(B.159)

When
\check{s}_{\star}^{2}\leq 3\sigma^{2}(D-2)
, we have
\check{\nu}^{2}=\frac{\sigma^{4}(D-1)}{2\check{s}_{\star}^{2}}\geq\frac{\sigma^{2}(D-1)}{2(3D-2)}\geq\frac{\sigma^{2}}{8}
, so

\frac{\check{\nu}}{\sqrt{\check{\nu}^{2}+\sigma^{2}}}\geq\frac{1}{3}.

(B.160)

Using this fact to combine terms, we have that for
s>\check{s}_{\star}+\sigma
,

\displaystyle[\phi\ast h](s)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 4\frac{\check{\nu}}{\sqrt{\check{\nu}^{2}+\sigma^{2}}}\exp\left(-\frac{(s-\check{s}_{\star})^{2}}{2(\check{\nu}^{2}+\sigma^{2})}\right)\leq 4\exp\left(-\frac{(s-\check{s}_{\star})^{2}}{2(\check{\nu}^{2}+\sigma^{2})}\right),

(B.161)

as claimed.
Lastly, we note that for
\check{s}_{\star}+\sigma\geq s\geq\check{s}_{\star}
,
4\exp\left(-\frac{(s-\check{s}_{\star})^{2}}{2(\check{\nu}^{2}+\sigma^{2})}\right)\geq 4\exp\left(-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2(\check{\nu}^{2}+\sigma^{2})}\right)\geq 4e^{-1/2}\geq 1
, so this upper bound works whenever
s\geq\check{s}_{\star}

Lemma B.7
(Relative Bounds)

Suppose that

576\log(2)\times\sigma^{2}D^{2/3}\leq s_{\star}^{2}\leq 3\sigma^{2}D

(B.162)

and

D>(576\log(2))^{3}+3

(B.163)

then when
0\leq s\leq s_{\star}+\tfrac{1}{12}\sigma(D-3)^{1/6}
, we have

\frac{\phi\ast-\dot{h}(s)}{\phi\ast h(s)}\leq C\max\left\{\frac{1}{\nu},\frac{s-s_{\star}}{\nu^{2}}\right\}

(B.164)

Proof

We consider two cases:
0\leq s\leq s_{\star}+\bar{\nu}
and
s_{\star}+\bar{\nu}<s<s_{\star}+\tfrac{1}{12}\sigma(D-3)^{1/6}
:

Case 1,
0\leq s\leq s_{\star}+\bar{\nu}
:
For the first case,using monotonicity of
\phi\ast-\dot{h}
from Lemma
B.4
on
[0,s_{\star}-\tfrac{1}{12}\sigma(D-3)^{1/6}]
as well as the upper bound from Lemma
B.5
, we have

\phi\ast-\dot{h}(s)\leq\frac{9eC(D)\nu s_{\star}}{\bar{\nu}\sigma^{2}}.

(B.165)

Meanwhile by Lemma
B.6

\phi\ast h(s)\geq\frac{1}{64e^{2}}C(D)\frac{s_{\star}\nu}{\sigma^{2}},

(B.166)

whence

\frac{\phi\ast-\dot{h}(s)}{\phi\ast h(s)}\leq\frac{64\times 9e^{3}}{\bar{\nu}}.

(B.167)

Case 2,
s_{\star}+\bar{\nu}<s<s_{\star}+\tfrac{1}{12}\sigma(D-3)^{1/6}
:
Under the stated condition on
s
, we have

\phi\ast-\dot{h}(s)\leq 9eC(D)\frac{\nu s_{\star}}{\bar{\nu}\sigma^{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{(s-s_{\star})^{2}}{2\bar{\nu}^{2}}\right),

(B.168)

and

\phi\ast h(s)\geq\frac{1}{64e}C(D)\frac{s_{\star}\nu}{\sigma^{2}}\frac{\bar{\nu}}{s-s_{\star}}\exp\left(-\frac{(s-s_{\star})^{2}}{2\bar{\nu}^{2}}\right).

(B.169)

giving

\frac{\phi\ast-\dot{h}(s)}{\phi\ast h(s)}\leq 64\times 9e^{2}\frac{s-s_{\star}}{\bar{\nu}^{2}},

(B.170)

establishing the claimed bounds.

Appendix C
Controlling the Signal Average

In this section, we consider the following situation: we have a noisy landmark
\mathbf{q}
, which does not necessarily reside on
\mathcal{M}
. Let

\mathbf{q}_{\natural}=P_{\mathcal{M}}\mathbf{q}

(C.1)

denote the nearest point to
\mathbf{q}
on
\mathcal{M}
. In this section, we attempt to bound

\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\,d_{\mathcal{M}}^{2}(\mathbf{x}_{\natural},\mathbf{q}_{\natural})\mid\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{q}\|\leq R\,\Bigr].

(C.2)

The following lemma does the job:

Lemma C.1
(Intrinsic Distances of Grouped Points)

There exist constants
c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4},C_{1},C_{2}
such that whenever
D>C_{1}
,
\sigma\sqrt{D}\leq\frac{\tau}{2}
,
d\left(\mathbf{q},\mathcal{M}\right)\leq\min\{\frac{c_{1}}{\kappa},\frac{1}{2}\tau\}
, and
s_{\star}=R^{2}-\sigma^{2}(D-3)
satisfies

\max\Bigl\{576\log 2\times\sigma^{2}D^{2/3},d^{2}(\mathbf{q},\mathcal{M})\Bigr\}\leq s_{\star}^{2}\leq\min\left\{3\sigma^{2}D,d^{2}(\mathbf{q},\mathcal{M})+c_{2}\tau^{2}\right\}

(C.3)

and
s_{\star,\parallel}^{2}=s_{\star}^{2}-d^{2}\left(\mathbf{q},\mathcal{M}\right),\>\check{s_{\star,\parallel}}^{2}=s_{\star,\parallel}^{2}-2\sigma^{2}
satisfies

\sigma\sqrt{\log\left(\frac{\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{M})}{\check{s}_{\star,\parallel}}\right)+d\times\kappa\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{M})+d\log\left(\frac{1}{\kappa s_{\star,\parallel}}\right)}\leq c_{3}\tau

(C.4)

and

\check{s}_{\star,\parallel}^{2}+\sqrt{\log\left(\frac{\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{M})}{\check{s}_{\star,\parallel}}\right)+d\times\kappa\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{M})+d\log\left(\frac{1}{\kappa s_{\star,\parallel}}\right)}\times\sigma^{2}\sqrt{D}\leq c_{4}\check{s}_{\star}^{2}

(C.5)

we have

\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\,d_{\mathcal{M}}^{2}(\mathbf{x}_{\natural},\mathbf{q}_{\natural})\mid\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{q}\|\leq R\,\Bigr]\leq C_{2}\check{s}_{\star,\parallel}^{2}+C_{2}\sqrt{\log\left(\frac{\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{M})}{\check{s}_{\star,\parallel}}\right)+d\times\kappa\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{M})+d\log\left(\frac{1}{\kappa s_{\star,\parallel}}\right)}\times\sigma^{2}\sqrt{D}

(C.6)

and

\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\,d_{\mathcal{M}}^{4}(\mathbf{x}_{\natural},\mathbf{q}_{\natural})\mid\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{q}\|\leq R\,\Bigr]\leq C_{3}\check{s}_{\star,\parallel}^{4}+C_{3}\log\left(\frac{\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{M})}{\check{s}_{\star,\parallel}}\right)+d\times\kappa\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{M})+d\log\left(\frac{1}{\kappa s_{\star,\parallel}}\right)\times\sigma^{4}D

(C.7)

Proof

Using the notation of the previous section, we have

\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\,d_{\mathcal{M}}^{2}(\mathbf{x}_{\natural},\mathbf{q}_{\natural})\mid\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{q}\|\leq R\,\Bigr]=\frac{\int_{\mathbf{x}_{\natural}\in\mathcal{M}}d^{2}(\mathbf{x}_{\natural},\mathbf{q}_{\natural})\;[\phi\ast h]\Bigl(\|\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{x}_{\natural}\|\Bigr)\,d\mu_{0}(\mathbf{x}_{\natural})}{\int_{\mathbf{x}_{\natural}\in\mathcal{M}}[\phi\ast h]\Bigl(\|\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{x}_{\natural}\|\Bigr)\,d\mu_{0}(\mathbf{x}_{\natural})}.

(C.8)

We break this average into a “near” term and a “far term”. Indeed, for any
\xi>0
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\int_{\mathbf{x}_{\natural}\in\mathcal{M}}d^{2}(\mathbf{x}_{\natural},\mathbf{q}_{\natural})\;[\phi\ast h]\Bigl(\|\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{x}_{\natural}\|\Bigr)\,d\mu_{0}(\mathbf{x}_{\natural})}{\int_{\mathbf{x}_{\natural}\in\mathcal{M}}[\phi\ast h]\Bigl(\|\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{x}_{\natural}\|\Bigr)\,d\mu_{0}(\mathbf{x}_{\natural})}

(C.9)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\int_{\mathbf{x}_{\natural}\in\mathcal{M},d_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbf{x}_{\natural},\mathbf{q}_{\natural})<\xi}d^{2}(\mathbf{x}_{\natural},\mathbf{q}_{\natural})\;[\phi\ast h]\Bigl(\|\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{x}_{\natural}\|\Bigr)\,d\mu_{0}(\mathbf{x}_{\natural})}{\int_{\mathbf{x}_{\natural}\in\mathcal{M}}[\phi\ast h]\Bigl(\|\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{x}_{\natural}\|\Bigr)\,d\mu_{0}(\mathbf{x}_{\natural})}

Upper Bounding the Tail of
\phi\ast h
over

Lemma C.2
(Points intrinsically far from
\mathbf{q}_{\natural}
are far from
\mathbf{q}
.)

Suppose that
\|\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{q}_{\natural}\|_{2}<\tfrac{1}{2}\tau
, where
\tau
is the reach of
\mathcal{M}
. There exists a numerical constant
c>0
such that for
\xi<\tfrac{1}{2\kappa}
,

\inf_{d_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbf{x}_{\natural},\mathbf{q}_{\natural})\geq\xi}\Bigl\{\|\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{x}_{\natural}\|_{2}^{2}\Bigr\}\geq\min\Bigl\{\|\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{q}_{\natural}\|_{2}^{2}+c\xi^{2},\tau^{2}\Bigr\}.

(C.32)

Proof

We seek a lower bound on

\inf_{d_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbf{x}_{\natural},\mathbf{q}_{\natural})\geq\xi}f(\mathbf{x}_{\natural})\equiv\|\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{x}_{\natural}\|^{2}_{2}

(C.33)

This problem asks us to minimize a continuous function
f
over the compact set
\{\mathbf{x}_{\natural}\mid d_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbf{x}_{\natural},\mathbf{q}_{\natural})\geq\xi\}
. By compactness, at least one minimizer exists. Moreover, every minimizer
\mathbf{x}_{\natural}^{\star}
is either (i) a critical point of
f
over
\mathcal{M}
, or (ii) occurs on the boundary, i.e., satisfies
d_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbf{x}_{\natural}^{\star},\mathbf{q}_{\natural})=\xi
.

Case (i).
Consider the squared distance function
f(\mathbf{x}_{\natural})=\|\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{x}_{\natural}\|_{2}^{2}
. Any critical point
\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{\natural}
of this function satisfies

\mathbf{q}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{\natural}\in N_{\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{\natural}}\mathcal{M}

(C.34)

I.e., if we consider the normal mapping
(\mathbf{x}_{\natural},\mathbf{\eta})\mapsto N(\mathbf{x}_{\natural},\mathbf{\eta})=\mathbf{x}_{\natural}+\mathbf{\eta}
, then we have

N(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{\natural},\mathbf{q}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{\natural})=\mathbf{q}=N(\mathbf{q}_{\natural},\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{q}_{\natural}).

(C.35)

Since the normal map is injective on

\mathcal{M}\times\mathrm{int}\Bigl(B^{D}(0,\tau)\Bigr)

(C.36)

this implies that
\|\mathbf{q}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{\natural}\|\geq\tau
.

Case (ii).
For
\mathbf{x}_{\natural}
satisfying
d_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbf{q}_{\natural},\mathbf{x}_{\natural})=\xi
, there exists
\mathbf{v}\in T_{\mathbf{q}_{\natural}}\mathcal{M}
with
\|\mathbf{v}\|_{2}=\xi
such that
\mathbf{x}_{\natural}=\exp_{\mathbf{q}_{\natural}}(\mathbf{v})
. We have

\displaystyle\|\exp_{\mathbf{q}_{\natural}}(\mathbf{v})-\mathbf{q}\|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{q}_{\natural}+\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{q}\|-\tfrac{1}{2}\kappa\|\mathbf{v}\|^{2}

(C.37)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left(\|\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{q}_{\natural}\|^{2}+\xi^{2}\right)^{1/2}-\tfrac{1}{2}\kappa\xi^{2}.

(C.38)

Since
\|\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{q}_{\natural}\|\leq\frac{1}{2}\tau\leq\frac{1}{2\kappa}
and
\xi\leq\frac{1}{2\kappa},
squaring
C.38
gives

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{x}_{\natural}-\mathbf{q}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{q}_{\natural}\|_{2}^{2}+\xi^{2}-\kappa\xi^{2}\Bigl(\|\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{q}_{\natural}\|_{2}^{2}+\xi^{2}\Bigr)^{1/2}+\tfrac{1}{4}\kappa^{2}\xi^{4}

(C.39)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{q}_{\natural}\|_{2}^{2}+\xi^{2}-\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\xi^{2}+\tfrac{1}{4}\kappa^{2}\xi^{4}

(C.40)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{q}_{\natural}\|_{2}^{2}+\left(\frac{\sqrt{2}-1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)\xi^{2}.

(C.41)

Combining the two cases, gives the claimed result.

Upper Bounding
\mathrm{vol}(\mathcal{M})/\Upsilon

Lemma C.3
(Volumes)

Suppose that
\mathcal{M}
is a connected, geodesically complete
d\geq 2
-dimensional submanifold of
\mathbb{R}^{D}
. For
\mathbf{q}\in\mathbb{R}^{D}
, set

\Upsilon=\int_{\mathbf{x}_{\natural}\in\mathcal{M}}[\phi\ast h]\Bigl(\|\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{x}_{\natural}\|\Bigr)\,d\mu_{0}(\mathbf{x}_{\natural})

(C.42)

There exist numerical constants
c>0,C_{1},C_{2},C_{3}
such that whenever if
D>C_{1}
,
d(\mathbf{q},\mathcal{M})<c/\kappa
, and

\max\left\{C_{2}\sigma^{2}D^{2/3},d^{2}(\mathbf{q},\mathcal{M})\right\}\leq s_{\star}^{2}\leq 3\sigma^{2}D,

(C.43)

we have

\frac{\mathrm{vol}(\mathcal{M})}{\Upsilon}\leq\frac{C_{3}\exp\Bigl(\kappa(d-1)\,\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{M})\Bigr)}{\min\left\{(\kappa s_{\star,\parallel})^{d},1\right\}},

(C.44)

with
s_{\star,\parallel}^{2}=s_{\star}^{2}-d^{2}(\mathbf{q},\mathcal{M})
.

Proof

Set
\mathbf{q}_{\natural}=\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{M}}\mathbf{q}
, so
d(\mathbf{q},\mathcal{M})=\|\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{q}_{\natural}\|_{2}
. For any choice of
\Delta>0
, we have

\displaystyle\Upsilon=\int_{\mathbf{x}_{\natural}\in\mathcal{M}}[\phi\ast h]\Bigl(\|\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{x}_{\natural}\|\Bigr)\,d\mu_{0}(\mathbf{x}_{\natural})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{d_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbf{x}_{\natural},\mathbf{q}_{\natural})\leq\Delta}\,[\phi\ast h]\Bigl(\|\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{x}_{\natural}\|\Bigr)\,d\mu_{0}(\mathbf{x}_{\natural}).

(C.45)

For
d_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbf{x}_{\natural},\mathbf{q}_{\natural})\leq\Delta\leq\tfrac{1}{\kappa}
and
c\leq\tfrac{1}{4}
we have

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{x}_{\natural}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\|\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{q}_{\natural}-\log_{\mathbf{q}_{\natural}}\mathbf{x}_{\natural}\|+\tfrac{1}{2}\kappa\Delta^{2}\right)^{2}

(C.46)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{q}_{\natural}\|_{2}^{2}+d^{2}_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbf{x}_{\natural},\mathbf{q}_{\natural})+\Bigl(\|\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{q}_{\natural}\|_{2}+d_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbf{q}_{\natural},\mathbf{x}_{\natural})\Bigr)\kappa\Delta^{2}+\tfrac{1}{4}\kappa^{2}\Delta^{4}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{q}_{\natural}\|_{2}^{2}+\Delta^{2}+\Bigl(\frac{c}{\kappa}+\Delta\Bigr)\kappa\Delta^{2}+\tfrac{1}{4}\kappa^{2}\Delta^{4}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle d^{2}(\mathbf{q},\mathcal{M})+\tfrac{5}{2}\Delta^{2}

and so, as long as

\Delta\leq\min\left\{\left(\frac{2}{5}\right)^{1/2}s_{\star,\parallel},\;\frac{1}{\kappa}\right\},

(C.47)

we have
\|\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{x}_{\natural}\|_{2}\leq s_{\star}
and so by Lemma
B.6
,
we have

[\phi\ast h]\Bigl(\|\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{x}_{\natural}\|\Bigr)\geq\frac{1}{64e^{2}}\cdot C(D)\frac{s_{\star}\nu}{\sigma^{2}}

(C.48)

where we recall
\nu^{2}=\frac{\sigma^{4}(D-3)}{2s_{\star}^{2}},
and
C(D)=\frac{\dot{\gamma}\left(\frac{D-1}{2},\frac{D-3}{2}\right)}{\Gamma(\frac{D-1}{2})},
which is bounded in Lemma
D.4
, further giving

[\phi\ast h]\Bigl(\|\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{x}_{\natural}\|\Bigr)\geq\frac{c}{\sqrt{D}}\frac{s_{\star}\nu}{\sigma^{2}}=\frac{c}{\sqrt{D}}\sqrt{\frac{D-3}{2}}\geq c^{\prime}

(C.49)

for
\mathbf{x}_{\natural}\in B_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbf{q}_{\natural},\Delta)
. Hence,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{x}_{\natural}\in\mathcal{M}}[\phi\ast h]\Bigl(\|\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{x}_{\natural}\|\Bigr)\,d\mu_{0}(\mathbf{x}_{\natural})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle c^{\prime}\mathrm{vol}\Bigl(B_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbf{q}_{\natural},\Delta)\Bigr)

(C.50)

By Lemma
D.1
,

\frac{\mathrm{vol}\left(B_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbf{q}_{\natural},\Delta)\right)}{\mathrm{vol}(\mathcal{M})}\geq\tfrac{1}{4}(2\kappa\Delta)^{d}\exp\Bigl(-\kappa(d-1)\,\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{M})\Bigr),

(C.51)

and hence

\frac{\mathrm{vol}(\mathcal{M})}{\Upsilon}\leq\frac{\mathrm{vol}(\mathcal{M})}{c^{\prime}\mathrm{vol}\left(B_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbf{q}_{\natural},\Delta)\right)}\leq\frac{C\exp\Bigl(\kappa(d-1)\,\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{M})\Bigr)}{(2\kappa\Delta)^{d}}.

(C.52)

Setting
\Delta=\tfrac{1}{2}\min\{s_{\star,\parallel},\kappa^{-1}\}

completes the proof.

, we can notice that for every
t\in[0,1]

\mathbf{x}_{t}=(1-t)\mathbf{x}_{\natural}+t\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{x}_{\natural}+t\mathbf{z},

(D.14)

satisfies
d(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathcal{M})<\tau
. Hence the projection
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{M}}\mathbf{x}_{t}
is differentiable at every
t\in[0,1]
, and satisfies

\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{M}}\mathbf{x}_{t}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\mathrm{I\!I}^{*}[\mathbf{\eta}_{t}]\right)^{k}\mathcal{P}_{T_{\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{M}}{\mathbf{x}_{t}}}\mathcal{M}}\mathbf{z},

(D.15)

whence (bounding the
k\geq 1
terms in the above Neumann series)

\left\|\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{M}}\mathbf{x}_{t}-\mathcal{P}_{T_{\mathbf{x}_{t}}\mathcal{M}}\mathbf{z}\right\|\leq\frac{3\kappa\|\mathbf{z}\|_{2}}{1-3\kappa\|\mathbf{z}\|_{2}}\left\|\mathcal{P}_{T_{\mathbf{x}_{t}}\mathcal{M}}\mathbf{z}\right\|\leq\|\mathcal{P}_{T_{\mathbf{x}_{t}}\mathcal{M}}\mathbf{z}\|_{2}

(D.16)

Applying this bound, when
\|\mathbf{z}\|\leq\tfrac{1}{6}\tau
, we have

\displaystyle d(\mathbf{x},\mathcal{M})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_{\natural}-\int_{0}^{1}\left[\frac{d}{ds}\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{M}}\mathbf{x}_{s}\right]\Bigr|_{s=t}dt\right\|

(D.17)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_{\natural}-\int_{0}^{1}\mathcal{P}_{T_{\mathbf{x}_{t}}\mathcal{M}}\mathbf{z}\,dt\right\|-\int_{0}^{1}\|\mathcal{P}_{T_{\mathbf{x}_{t}}\mathcal{M}}\mathbf{z}\|\,dt

(D.18)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_{\natural}\right\|-2\int_{0}^{1}\|\mathcal{P}_{T_{\mathbf{x}_{t}}\mathcal{M}}\mathbf{z}\|\,dt

(D.19)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_{\natural}\|-2T_{\max},

(D.20)

with

T_{\max}=\sup_{\bar{\mathbf{x}}\in B_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbf{x}_{\natural},1/\kappa),\mathbf{v}\in T_{\bar{\mathbf{x}}}\mathcal{M}}\left\langle\mathbf{v},\mathbf{z}\right\rangle.

(D.21)

We note that from Theorem 3 from
[
31
]
, we have

\mathbb{E}[T_{\max}]\leq C\bar{\kappa}\sigma\sqrt{d},

(D.22)

while

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[T_{\max}^{2}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{E}T_{\max}\right)^{2}+\mathbb{E}\left[(T_{\max}-\mathbb{E}T_{\max})^{2}\right]

(D.23)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{E}T_{\max}\right)^{2}+\int_{s=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}[(T_{\max}-\mathbb{E}T_{\max})^{2}\geq s]\,ds

(D.24)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C^{\prime}\bar{\kappa}^{2}\sigma^{2}d+2\int_{s=0}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{s}{2\sigma^{2}}\right)ds

(D.25)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C^{\prime}\bar{\kappa}^{2}\sigma^{2}d+4\sigma^{2}

(D.26)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C^{\prime\prime}\bar{\kappa}^{2}\sigma^{2}d.

(D.27)

So

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Biggl[d(\mathbf{x},\mathcal{M})\mathbbm{1}_{\|\mathbf{z}\|\leq\tfrac{1}{6}\tau}\Biggr]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Biggl[\Biggl(\left\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_{\natural}\right\|-2T_{\max}\Biggr)\mathbbm{1}_{\|\mathbf{z}\|\leq\tfrac{1}{6}\tau}\Biggr]

(D.28)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Biggl[\left\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_{\natural}\right\|-2T_{\max}\Biggr]-\mathbb{E}\Biggl[\Biggl(\left\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_{\natural}\right\|-2T_{\max}\Biggr)\mathbbm{1}_{\|\mathbf{z}\|>\tfrac{1}{6}\tau}\Biggr]

(D.29)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Biggl[\left\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_{\natural}\right\|-2T_{\max}\Biggr]-\left(\mathbb{P}\Biggl[\|\mathbf{z}\|>\tfrac{1}{6}\tau\Biggr]\mathbb{E}\Biggl[\Biggl(\left\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_{\natural}\right\|-2T_{\max}\Biggr)^{2}\Biggr]\right)^{1/2}

(D.30)

We have

\mathbb{P}\left[\|\mathbf{z}\|>\sigma\sqrt{D}+t\right]\leq\exp\left(-\frac{t^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right),

(D.31)

and so with assumption
c\tau\geq\sigma\sqrt{D},

\mathbb{P}\left[\|\mathbf{z}\|>\tfrac{1}{6}\tau\right]\leq\exp\left(-cD\right).

(D.32)

Meanwhile,

\mathbb{E}\Biggl[\Biggl(\left\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_{\natural}\right\|-2T_{\max}\Biggr)^{2}\Biggr]\leq 2\mathbb{E}\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_{\natural}\|^{2}+8\mathbb{E}\Bigl[T_{\max}^{2}\Bigr]\leq 2\sigma^{2}D+8C^{\prime\prime}\bar{\kappa}^{2}\sigma^{2}d

(D.33)

Combining, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Biggl[d(\mathbf{x},\mathcal{M})\mathbbm{1}_{\|\mathbf{z}\|\leq\tfrac{1}{6}\tau}\Biggr]\geq\sigma\sqrt{D}-2C\bar{\kappa}\sigma\sqrt{d}-\sigma e^{-cD}\sqrt{\left(2D+8C^{\prime\prime}\bar{\kappa}^{2}d\right)}\geq\sigma\sqrt{D-d}-C^{(3)}\bar{\kappa}\sigma\sqrt{d}.

(D.34)

Using the fact that
d(\mathbf{x}_{\natural}+\mathbf{z},\mathcal{M})
is
1
-Lipschitz in the Gaussian vector
\mathbf{z}
, we have that with probability at least
1-2e^{-t^{2}/2}
,

\left|d(\mathbf{x},\mathcal{M})-\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{z}}\left[d(\mathbf{x},\mathcal{M})\right]\right|\leq t\sigma.

(D.35)

Setting
t=\sqrt{d}
and combining with our bounds on
\mathbb{E}[d(\mathbf{x},\mathcal{M})]
completes the proof.

D.3
Bounds on the Incomplete Gamma Function

Lemma D.3
(Subgaussian Bounds for the Integrand of the Gamma Function Near the Phase Transition.)

Let
p\geq 9
and
x_{\star}=p-1
. Then

\frac{1}{4}\dot{\gamma}(p,x_{\star})\exp\left(-\frac{(x-x_{\star})^{2}}{2x_{\star}}\right)\leq\dot{\gamma}(p,x)\leq 4\dot{\gamma}(p,x_{\star})\exp\left(-\frac{(x-x_{\star})^{2}}{2x_{\star}}\right)\qquad x\in\left[x_{\star}-x_{\star}^{2/3},x_{\star}+x_{\star}^{2/3}\right].

(D.36)

Proof

We have

\frac{d}{dx}\gamma(p,x)=x^{p-1}e^{-x},

(D.37)

which is maximized at
x_{\star}=p-1
. For
0<\Delta_{x}<x_{\star}
, and
x\in[x_{\star}-\Delta_{x},x_{\star}+\Delta_{x}]
,

\displaystyle\log x\quad\leq\quad\log x_{\star}+\frac{x-x_{\star}}{x_{\star}}-\frac{1}{2x_{\star}^{2}}(x-x_{\star})^{2}+\frac{\Delta_{x}^{3}}{3(x_{\star}-\Delta_{x})^{3}},

(D.38)

and so

\displaystyle\dot{\gamma}(p,x)\quad=\quad\exp\Biggl((p-1)\log x-x\Biggr)

(D.39)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\exp\Biggl(x_{\star}\Bigl(\log x_{\star}+\frac{x-x_{\star}}{x_{\star}}-\frac{1}{2x_{\star}^{2}}(x-x_{\star})^{2}+\frac{\Delta_{x}^{3}}{3(x_{\star}-\Delta_{x})^{3}}\Bigr)-x\Biggr),\quad\text{for}\quad 0<x_{\star}-\Delta_{x}<x<x_{\star}+\Delta_{x}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\exp\left(\frac{x_{\star}\Delta_{x}^{3}}{3(x_{\star}-\Delta_{x})^{3}}\right)\dot{\gamma}(p,x_{\star})\exp\left(-\frac{(x-x_{\star})^{2}}{2x_{\star}}\right)\quad\text{for}\quad 0<x_{\star}-\Delta_{x}<x<x_{\star}+\Delta_{x},

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\exp\left(9/8\right)\dot{\gamma}(p,x_{\star})\exp\left(-\frac{(x-x_{\star})^{2}}{2x_{\star}}\right)\quad\text{for}\quad 0<x_{\star}-\Delta_{x}<x<x_{\star}+\Delta_{x},

where in the penultimate line we used that
\exp(x_{\star}\log x_{\star}-x_{\star})=\gamma(p,x_{\star})
, and in the final line we used

\Delta_{x}<\min\{x_{\star}/3,x_{\star}^{2/3}\},

(D.40)

which under our hypotheses is implied by
\Delta_{x}<x_{\star}^{2/3}
, since
p>28\implies x_{\star}>27\implies x_{\star}^{2/3}<x_{\star}/3
. Using the corresponding lower bound

\displaystyle\log x\quad\geq\quad\log x_{\star}+\frac{x-x_{\star}}{x_{\star}}-\frac{1}{2x_{\star}^{2}}(x-x_{\star})^{2}-\frac{\Delta_{x}^{3}}{3(x_{\star}-\Delta_{x})^{3}},

(D.41)

and applying the same simplifications, we obtain the claimed lower bound on
\dot{\gamma}(p,x)
.

Lemma D.4

There exists a numerical constant
c>0
such that

\frac{\dot{\gamma}\left(\frac{D-1}{2},\frac{D-3}{2}\right)}{\Gamma(\frac{D-1}{2})}\leq\frac{c}{\sqrt{D}}

(D.42)

for all
D\geq 4
.

Proof

By lemma
D.5
, we have
n!\geq\sqrt{2\pi}n^{n+1/2}e^{-n+\frac{1}{12n+1}}
, thus

\displaystyle\frac{\dot{\gamma}\left(\frac{D-1}{2},\frac{D-3}{2}\right)}{\Gamma(\frac{D-1}{2})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left(\frac{D-3}{2}\right)^{\frac{D-3}{2}}e^{-\frac{D-3}{2}}}{(\frac{D-3}{2})!}

(D.43)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\left(\frac{D-3}{2}\right)^{\frac{D-3}{2}}e^{-\frac{D-3}{2}}}{\sqrt{2\pi\left(\frac{D-3}{2}\right)}\left(\frac{D-3}{2}\right)^{\frac{D-3}{2}}e^{-\frac{D-3}{2}+\frac{1}{6(D-3)+1}}}

(D.44)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi(D-3)}}e^{-\frac{1}{6(D-3)+1}}

(D.45)

as claimed

Lemma D.5

For any integer n,

n!=\sqrt{2\pi}n^{n+1/2}e^{-n+\epsilon_{n}}
for some
\frac{1}{12n+1}\leq\epsilon_{n}\leq\frac{1}{12n}

Proof

We first note that since
\log(n)
is monotonically increasing, we have

{\int_{1}}^{n}\log(x)dx\leq\log(n!)=\overset{n}{\sum_{m=1}}\log(m)\leq{\int_{1}}^{n+1}\log(x)dx

(D.46)

which implies

n\log(n)-n\leq\log(n!)\leq(n+1)\log(n+1)-n

(D.47)

This motivates us to consider the "middle" and define the series
C_{n}=\log(n!)-(n+\frac{1}{2})\log(n)+n
.
Observe that

\displaystyle C_{n}-C_{n+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\log(n+1)-(n+\frac{1}{2})\log(n)+(n+\frac{3}{2})\log(n+1)-1

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-1+(n+\frac{1}{2})\log(1+\frac{1}{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-1+(\frac{1}{2n+1})\log(\frac{2n+2}{2n})

We let
t=\frac{1}{2n+1}
, then, we have

\displaystyle C_{n}-C_{n+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-1+(\frac{1}{2t})\log(\frac{1+t}{1-t})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-1+(\frac{1}{2t})(-2)(t+\frac{t^{3}}{3}+\frac{t^{5}}{5}+...)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{t^{2k}}{2k+1}

Since we have both the following lower and upper bound,

\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{t^{2k}}{2k+1}\geq\frac{t^{2}}{3}=\frac{1}{12n^{2}+12n+3}\geq\frac{1}{12n+1}-\frac{1}{12(n+1)+1}

(D.48)

\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{t^{2k}}{2k+1}\leq\frac{1}{3}\sum_{k\geq 1}t^{2k}=\frac{t^{2}}{3(1-t^{2}}=\frac{1}{12n}-\frac{1}{12n+1}

(D.49)

We have

\frac{1}{12n+1}-\frac{1}{12(n+1)+1}\leq C_{n}-C_{n+1}\leq\frac{1}{12n}-\frac{1}{12n+1}

(D.50)

which implies that series
C_{n}
converges, and let
\lim_{n\to\infty}C_{n}=C
. Further, line
D.50
also implies
C_{n}=C+\epsilon_{n}
where
\frac{1}{12n+1}\leq\epsilon_{n}\leq\frac{1}{12n}
. Then, from our definition of
C_{n}
we have

n!=e^{C}n^{n+1/2}e^{-n+\epsilon_{n}}

(D.51)

Of course now we only have to show
C=\log(\sqrt{2\pi})
. We will prove and use the De Moivre Laplace Theorem from probability theory, which is a special case of central limit theorem and roughly states that the number of successes in binomial distribution approaches normal distribution when number of trials is large enough. From the theorem we shall see
e^{-C}e^{\frac{-x^{2}}{2}}
is a probability distribution and conclude the value of
C
.

Let
X_{1},X_{2}...
be a sequence of Bernoulli Random Variables with fixed probability of success
p\in(0,1)
, and let
q=1-p
. Consider

S_{n}=\overset{n}{\sum_{i=1}}X_{i}

Then, for any non-decreasing sequence
A_{1},A_{2}...
such that
A_{1}\geq 1
,
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{A_{n}}{n^{1/6}}=0
, for any
k
such that
|np-k|\leq A_{n}\sqrt{npq}
, we have

\mathbb{P}\left(S_{n}=k\right)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{npq}}\exp\left(-C+O(\frac{A_{n}^{3}}{\sqrt{n}})-\frac{(np-k)^{2}}{2npq}\right)

(D.52)

To prove this, we will use the condition
|np-k|\leq A_{n}\sqrt{npq}
, which implies
k=O(n)
and invoke the results from line
D.51
to substitute the factorials,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(S_{n}=k)

\displaystyle=\frac{n!p^{k}q^{n-k}}{k!(n-k)!}

(D.53)

\displaystyle=\frac{\exp\left(O\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)\right)n^{n+1/2}p^{k}q^{n-k}}{\exp\left(C+O\left(\frac{1}{k}\right)+O(\frac{1}{n-k})\right)k^{k+1/2}(n-k)^{n-k+1/2}}

(D.54)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-C+O(\frac{1}{n})\right)\frac{1}{\sqrt{npq}}\frac{n^{n+1}p^{k+1/2}q^{n-k+1/2}}{k^{k+1/2}(n-k)^{n-k+1/2}}

(D.55)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-C+O(\frac{1}{n})\right)\frac{1}{\sqrt{npq}}\frac{1}{(\frac{k}{np})^{k}(\frac{n-k}{nq})^{n-k}}\frac{1}{(\frac{k}{np})^{1/2}(\frac{n-k}{nq})^{1/2}}

(D.56)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-C+O(\frac{1}{n})\right)\frac{1}{\sqrt{npq}}\underbrace{\frac{1}{(1-\frac{np-k}{np})^{k}(1+\frac{np-k}{nq})^{n-k}}}_{\textbf{T}erm(I)}\underbrace{\frac{1}{(1-\frac{np-k}{np})^{1/2}(1+\frac{np-k}{nq})^{1/2}}}_{\text{T}erm(II)}

(D.57)

We will analyze Term(I) first by second order Taylor expansion, as follows

\displaystyle\log(\text{T}erm(I))

\displaystyle=-k\left(-\frac{np-k}{np}-\frac{(np-k)^{2}}{2n^{2}p^{2}}+O(\frac{A_{n}^{3}}{n\sqrt{n}})\right)-(n-k)\left(\frac{np-k}{nq}-\frac{(np-k)^{2}}{2n^{2}q^{2}}+O(\frac{A_{n}^{3}}{n\sqrt{n}})\right)

(D.58)

\displaystyle=-\frac{(np-k)(-kq+(n-k)p)}{npq}+\frac{(np-k)^{2}}{2n^{2}p^{2}q^{2}}\left(kq^{2}+(n-k)p^{2}\right)+O\left(\frac{A_{n}^{3}}{\sqrt{n}}\right)

(D.59)

\displaystyle=-\frac{(np-k)^{2}}{npq}+\frac{(np-k)^{2}}{2n^{2}p^{2}q^{2}}\left(np^{2}-k(p-q)\right)+O\left(\frac{A_{n}^{3}}{\sqrt{n}}\right)

(D.60)

\displaystyle=-\frac{(np-k)^{2}}{2n^{2}p^{2}q^{2}}\left(2npq-np^{2}+kp-kq\right)+O\left(\frac{A_{n}^{3}}{\sqrt{n}}\right)

(D.61)

\displaystyle=-\frac{(np-k)^{2}}{2n^{2}p^{2}q^{2}}\left(npq+(q-p)(np-k)\right)+O\left(\frac{A_{n}^{3}}{\sqrt{n}}\right)

(D.62)

\displaystyle=-\frac{(np-k)^{2}}{2npq}-\underbrace{\frac{(np-k)^{3}(q-p)}{2n^{2}p^{2}q^{2}}}_{\text{dissolved in }O\left(\frac{A_{n}^{3}}{\sqrt{n}}\right)}+O\left(\frac{A_{n}^{3}}{\sqrt{n}}\right)

(D.63)

\displaystyle=-\frac{(np-k)^{2}}{2npq}+O\left(\frac{A_{n}^{3}}{\sqrt{n}}\right)

(D.64)

Then, we consider another Taylor expansion for Term(II), but this time only zero-order, giving us

\displaystyle\log(\text{T}erm(II))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle O(\frac{A_{n}}{\sqrt{n}})

(D.65)

Combining the terms back into line
D.57
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(S_{n}=k)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-C+O(\frac{1}{n})\right)\frac{1}{\sqrt{npq}}\exp\left(-\frac{(np-k)^{2}}{2npq}+O\left(\frac{A_{n}^{3}}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\right)\exp\left(O(\frac{A_{n}}{\sqrt{n}})\right)

(D.66)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{npq}}\exp\left(-C+O(\frac{A_{n}^{3}}{\sqrt{n}})-\frac{(np-k)^{2}}{2npq}\right)

(D.67)

where in the last line we’ve used the fact that
A_{1}\geq 1
and
A_{n}
non-decreasing, which allows us to dissolve terms. We’ve shown line
D.52
. Now we will finally prove and use the DeMoivre-Laplace Theorem, that for any fixed
a<b
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(a\leq\frac{S_{n}-np}{\sqrt{npq}}<b\right)=\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}{e^{C}}\int_{t=a}^{b}\psi(t)dt

(D.68)

, where
\psi(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-t^{2}/2}

We will prove this by invoking and triangular inequality. For any n, we have

\displaystyle\left|P(a\leq\frac{S_{n}-np}{\sqrt{npq}}<b)-\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}{e^{C}}\int_{t=a}^{b}\psi(t)dt\right|

(D.69)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|P(a\leq\frac{S_{n}-np}{\sqrt{npq}}<b)-\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}{e^{C}\sqrt{npq}}\sum_{k=\lceil a\sqrt{npq}+np\rceil}^{\lceil b\sqrt{npq}+np-1\rceil}\psi(\frac{k-np}{\sqrt{npq}})\right|

(D.70)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}{e^{C}}\left|\frac{1}{\sqrt{npq}}\sum_{k=\lceil a\sqrt{npq}+np\rceil}^{\lceil b\sqrt{npq}+np-1\rceil}\psi(\frac{k-np}{\sqrt{npq}})-\int_{t=a}^{b}\psi(t)dt\right|

(D.71)

Here the second bottom term is the difference between Riemman sum and integral which clearly converges to 0 as n approaches infinity, and to analyze the first term, we will consider constant
\lambda>\max\{1,a\sqrt{pq},b\sqrt{pq}\}
and apply results from
D.52
,

\displaystyle\left|P(a\leq\frac{S_{n}-np}{\sqrt{npq}}<b)-\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}{e^{C}\sqrt{npq}}\sum_{k=\lceil a\sqrt{npq}+np\rceil}^{\lceil b\sqrt{npq}+np-1\rceil}\psi(\frac{k-np}{\sqrt{npq}})\right|

(D.72)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=\lceil a\sqrt{npq}+np\rceil}^{\lceil b\sqrt{npq}+np-1\rceil}\left|P(S_{n}=k)-\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}{e^{C}\sqrt{npq}}\psi(\frac{k-np}{\sqrt{npq}})\right|

(D.73)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{npq}}\left|\sum_{k=\lceil a\sqrt{npq}+np\rceil}^{\lceil b\sqrt{npq}+np-1\rceil}e^{-C+O(\frac{\lambda^{3}}{\sqrt{n}})-\frac{(np-k)^{2}}{2npq}}-\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}{e^{C}}\psi(\frac{k-np}{\sqrt{npq}})\right|

(D.74)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{-C}}{\sqrt{npq}}\left|\sum_{k=\lceil a\sqrt{npq}+np\rceil}^{\lceil b\sqrt{npq}+np-1\rceil}e^{O(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}})}e^{-\frac{(np-k)^{2}}{2npq}}-e^{-\frac{(np-k)^{2}}{2npq}}\right|

(D.75)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{-C}}{\sqrt{npq}}\left|\sum_{k=\lceil a\sqrt{npq}+np\rceil}^{\lceil b\sqrt{npq}+np-1\rceil}O(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}})e^{-\frac{(np-k)^{2}}{2npq}}\right|

(D.76)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{e^{-C}}{\sqrt{npq}}\left|\sum_{k=\lceil a\sqrt{npq}+np\rceil}^{\lceil b\sqrt{npq}+np-1\rceil}O(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}})\right|

(D.77)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle O(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}})

(D.78)

where in the last line we note that there’s only a total of
O(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}})
possible terms. Therefore, the DeMoivre-Laplace Theorem (
D.68
) is proven, and noting that since right hand side must be a probability distribution, we can conclude
e^{C}=\sqrt{2\pi}
, which finishes the proof.

Lemma D.6

Let
\mathbf{z}_{1},\mathbf{z}_{2}\ldots\mathbf{z}_{N}
be i.i.d.
D
dimensional vectors, with
\|\mathbf{z}_{i}\|\leq B
almost everywhere, for some
B>0
. Then,

\mathbb{P}\left[\left\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}\mathbf{z}_{\ell}-\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}\mathbf{z}_{\ell}\right]\right\|>t\right]\leq D\exp\left(-\frac{t^{2}N}{64B^{2}}\right)

(D.79)

Proof

For each
\mathbf{z}
, let
\mathbf{z}^{\prime}=\mathbf{z}-E\left[\mathbf{z}\right]
, and consider the
(D+1)*(D+1)
self-adjoint matrix

Y=\begin{bmatrix}0&\mathbf{z}^{\prime\top}\\
\mathbf{z}^{\prime}&0_{D\times D}\end{bmatrix}=\mathbf{1}_{D+1}{\mathbf{z}^{\prime}_{D+1}}^{\top}+\mathbf{z}^{\prime}_{D+1}{\mathbf{1}_{D+1}}^{\top}

where

\mathbf{z}^{\prime}_{D+1}==\begin{bmatrix}0\\
z^{\prime(1)}\\
\vdots\\
z^{\prime(D)}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{D+1}

Since
\|\mathbf{z}^{\prime}_{D+1}\|\leq 2B
, and

Y^{2}=\begin{bmatrix}\|\mathbf{z}^{\prime}\|_{2}^{2}&0_{D\times 1}\\
0_{1\times D}&\mathbf{z}^{\prime}\mathbf{z}^{\prime\top}\end{bmatrix}=\mathbf{z}^{\prime}_{D+1}{\mathbf{z}^{\prime}_{D+1}}^{\top}+{\mathbf{1}_{D+1}\mathbf{1}_{D+1}}^{\top}\|\mathbf{z}^{\prime}_{D+1}\|_{2}^{2}

which implies
\|Y\|_{op}\leq 8B^{2}
so
Y^{2}\preceq 8B^{2}I.
Then, by Matrix Hoeffding’s Inequality, Theorem 1.3 from
[
32
]
, we have for all
t\geq 0
,

\mathbb{P}\left(\lambda_{\max}(\frac{Y_{1}+\cdots Y_{N}}{N})\geq t\right)\leq D\cdot\exp\left(\frac{-t^{2}N}{8*(8B^{2})}\right).

We now analyze
\lambda_{\max}(\bar{Y})
where

\bar{Y}=\frac{Y_{1}+\cdots Y_{N}}{N}=\begin{bmatrix}0&\bar{\mathbf{z}^{\prime}}^{\top}\\
\bar{\mathbf{z}^{\prime}}&0_{D\times D}\end{bmatrix}=\mathbf{1}_{D+1}\bar{\mathbf{z}^{\prime}_{D+1}}^{\top}+\bar{\mathbf{z}^{\prime}_{D+1}}\mathbf{1}_{D+1}^{\top}

Since it’s at most a rank 2 matrix, we can consider its representation in the subspace spanned by
\mathbf{1}_{D+1}
and
\bar{\mathbf{z}^{\prime}_{D+1}}
, which gives

\bar{Y}|_{span(\mathbf{1}_{D+1},\bar{\mathbf{z}^{\prime}_{D+1}})}=\begin{bmatrix}0&\|\bar{\mathbf{z}^{\prime}}\|_{2}\\
\|\bar{\mathbf{z}^{\prime}}\|_{2}&0\end{bmatrix},

therefore, we have

\lambda_{\max}(\bar{Y})=\|\bar{\mathbf{z}^{\prime}}\|_{2}=\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}\left(\mathbf{z}_{\ell}-\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbf{z}_{\ell}\right]\right)\|,

which finishes the proof.

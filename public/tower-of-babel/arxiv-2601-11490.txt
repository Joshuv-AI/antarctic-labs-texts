1.
Sums of measurable sets

Let
\mathbf{N}=\{1,2,3,\ldots\}
denote the set of positive integers
and
\mathbf{N}_{0}=\{0,1,2,3,\ldots\}
the set of nonnegative integers.

Let
A
be a nonempty subset of the integers or of any additive abelian group or
semigroup.
The
h
-fold sumset of
A
is the set

hA=\{a_{1}+\cdots+a_{h}:a_{i}\in A\text{ for all }i=1,\ldots,h\}.

Nathanson
[
6
]
introduced problems about “sumset size races.”
Here is a simple example. For every positive integer
m
, do there exist finite subsets
A
and
B

of the semigroup and sequences of positive integers
h_{1}<h_{2}<\cdots<h_{m}

such that the differences of the sumset sizes
|h_{i}A|
and
|h_{i}B|

oscillate for
i=1,\ldots,m
, that is,

\displaystyle|h_{1}A|<|h_{1}B|,\qquad|h_{2}A|>|h_{2}B|\qquad|h_{3}A|<|h_{3}B|,

\displaystyle|h_{4}A|>|h_{4}B|,\qquad|h_{5}A|<|h_{5}B|\qquad|h_{6}A|>|h_{6}B|,\ldots.

Paul Péringuey and Anne de Roton solved this problem for
sumset sizes of finite sets of integers.

Theorem 1

(Péringuey and de Roton
[
7
]
)
.

For every integer
m\geq 3
, there exist finite sets
A
and
B
of integers
and an increasing sequence of positive integers
h_{1}<h_{2}<\cdots<h_{m}

such that
|A|=|B|
and

|h_{i}A|<|h_{i}B|\qquad\text{if $i$ is odd}\\

and

|h_{i}A|>|h_{i}B|\qquad\text{if $i$ is even.}

The following more general problem was posed in
[
6
]
.
The
normalization
of an
n
-tuple
\mathbf{u}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n})

of integers is the
n
-tuple
\tau(\mathbf{u})=(\tau(1),\tau(2),\ldots,\tau(n))
obtained
by replacing the
i
th smallest entry in
\mathbf{u}
with
i
for all
i
.
A
\tau
-tuple of length
n
is an
n
-tuple of the form
\tau(\mathbf{u})

for some
n
-tuple
\mathbf{u}\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}
.
Here are examples of
6
-tuples
\mathbf{u}
and their associated normalizations
\tau(\mathbf{u})
:

\mathbf{u}

(1,2,3,4,5,6)

(-2,13,11,0,22,4)

(7,3,2,9,3,5)

(9,7,8,9,7,8)

\tau(\mathbf{u})

(1,2,3,4,5,6)

(1,5,4,2,6,3)

(4,2,1,5,2,3)

(3,1,2,3,1,2)

Let
\mathcal{A}=(A_{1},A_{2},\ldots,A_{n})
be an
n
-tuple of finite subsets
of an additive abelian semigroup.
Associated to the
n
-tuple of
h
-fold sumsets
h\mathcal{A}=(hA_{1},hA_{2},\ldots,hA_{n})

is the
n
-tuple of sumset sizes
|h\mathcal{A}|=(|hA_{1}|,|hA_{2}|,\ldots,|hA_{n}|)

and its normalization

.

Problem 1

(from
[
6
]
)
.

Let
\mathbf{u}_{1},\mathbf{u}_{2},\ldots,\mathbf{u}_{m}
be a finite sequence of
n
-tuples of integers.
Does there exist an
n
-tuple of finite sets
\mathcal{A}=(A_{1},A_{2},\ldots,A_{n})

and a sequence of positive integers

h_{1}<h_{2}<\cdots<h_{m}
such that

\tau(|h_{k}\mathcal{A}|)=\tau(|h_{k}A_{1}|,|h_{k}A_{2}|,\ldots,|h_{k}A_{n}|)=\tau(\mathbf{u}_{k})

for all
k=1,\ldots,m
?

Noah Kravitz solved a strong form of this problem.

Theorem 2

(Kravitz
[
2
]
)
.

For integers
n\geq 2
and
H\geq 2
and for any
n
-tuples

\mathbf{u}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{u}_{H},\mathbf{u}_{\infty}\in\mathbf{N}^{n}
,
there exist finite subsets

A_{1},\ldots,A_{n}\subseteq\mathbf{Z}
such that

\left(\tau(|hA_{1}|,\ldots,|hA_{n}|\right)=\tau(\mathbf{u}_{h})

for all
h=1,\ldots,H
and

\tau(|hA_{1}|,\ldots,|hA_{n}|)=\tau(\mathbf{u}_{\infty})

for all
h>H
.

We prove a continuous analogue of this result.

Theorem 3
.

Let
G
be a locally compact abelian group
that is not a torsion group and let

\mu
be a Haar measure on
G
.
For integers
n\geq 2
and
H\geq 2
and for
any
n
-tuples
\mathbf{u}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{u}_{H}\in\mathbf{N}^{n}
,
there exist measurable subsets

A_{1},\ldots,A_{n}
of
G
such that

\tau\left(\mu(hA_{1}),\ldots,\mu(hA_{n})\right)=\tau(\mathbf{u}_{h})

for all
h=1,\ldots,H
.

Theorem

solves Problem
1
for Haar measure and, in particular,
for Lebesgue measure.

Theorem 4

(Fox, Kravitz, and Zhang
[

For integers

and
H\geq 2
,

such that

for all

and
h=1,\ldots,H
.

We prove a continuous analogue for subsets of

Theorem 5
.

For integers
n\geq 2
and
H\geq 2
,
let
m_{i,h}\in\mathbf{Z}
for all

and

for all

Note that, for all integers

.

Proof of Theorem
3

The group
G
is not a torsion group and so
G
contains an element
x_{0}
of
infinite order. Thus, for all
b,b^{\prime}\in\mathbf{Z}
, we have
bx_{0}=b^{\prime}x_{0}
if and only if
b=b^{\prime}
.

By Kravitz’s theorem, there are nonempty finite sets

B_{1},\ldots,B_{n}
of integers that satisfy

\tau\left(|hB_{1}|,\ldots,|hB_{n}|\right)=\tau(\mathbf{u}_{h})

for all
h=1,\ldots,H
.
The union of the sumsets
hB_{i}
is the finite set

B=\bigcup_{h=1}^{H}\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}hB_{i}.

Because a topological group is Hausdorff and
B
is finite,

G
contains an open neighborhood
V
of the identity such that the open sets

bx_{0}+V
are pairwise disjoint for all
b\in B
.
In a topological group, if
V
is an open neighborhood of the identity,
then for every positive integer
H
there is an open neighborhood
U
of the identity
such that
HU\subseteq V
. It follows that

U\subseteq 2U\subseteq\cdots\subseteq HU\subseteq V.

For
h=1,\ldots,H
, the sumsets
hU
are open and the
open sets
bx_{0}+hU
are pairwise disjoint for
b\in B
.
Nonempty open sets in a topological group have positive Haar measure
and so
\mu(bx_{0}+hU)=\mu(hU)>0
for all
b\in B
.

For all
i=1,\ldots,n
, define the open set

A_{i}=\bigcup_{b_{i,j}\in B_{i}}\left(b_{i,j}x_{0}+U\right).

For all
h=1,\ldots,H
, we have the sumset

\displaystyle hA_{i}

\displaystyle=h\left(\bigcup_{b_{i,j}\in B_{i}}\left(b_{i,j}x_{0}+U\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{(b_{i,1},\ldots,b_{i,h})\in B_{i}^{h}}\sum_{j=1}^{h}\left(b_{i,j}x_{0}+U\right)

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{(b_{i,1},\ldots,b_{i,h})\in B_{i}^{h}}\left(\left(\sum_{j=1}^{h}b_{i,j}\right)x_{0}+hU\right)

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{b\in hB_{i}}\left(bx_{0}+hU\right).

Because the sets
bx_{0}+hU
are pairwise disjoint, we have

\displaystyle\mu\left(hA_{i}\right)

\displaystyle=\mu\left(\bigcup_{b\in hB_{i}}\left(bx_{0}+hU\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{b\in hB_{i}}\mu\left(bx_{0}+hU\right)

\displaystyle=|hB_{i}|\mu(hU)

for all
i=1,\ldots,n
. Because
\mu(hU)>0
, we obtain

\displaystyle\tau\left(\mu(hA_{1}),\ldots,\mu(hA_{n})\right)

\displaystyle=\tau\left(|hB_{1}|\mu(hU),\ldots,|hB_{n}|\mu(hU)\right)

\displaystyle=\tau\left(|hB_{1}|,\ldots,|hB_{n}|\right)

\displaystyle=\tau(\mathbf{u}_{h}).

This completes the proof.

Lemma 1
.

Let
0<\varepsilon\leq 1/3

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle hX

\displaystyle=h([0,1-\varepsilon]\cup[2,3-\varepsilon])

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{k=0}^{h}\left(k[0,1-\varepsilon]+(h-k)[2,3-\varepsilon]\right)

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{k=0}^{h}\left([0,k(1-\varepsilon)]+[2(h-k),(h-k)(3-\varepsilon)]\right)

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{r=0}^{h}\left[2(h-k),k(1-\varepsilon)+(h-k)(3-\varepsilon)\right]

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{r=0}^{h}\left[2h-2k,h(3-\varepsilon)-2k\right].

It follows that

This completes the proof.

\displaystyle\mu

\displaystyle\left(\bigcup_{r=h}^{H}\ \bigcup_{j=L_{r-1}+1}^{L_{r}}(u_{j}+(h-1)\delta,u_{j}+r\delta)\right)

\displaystyle\qquad=\sum_{r=h}^{H}\ \sum_{j=L_{r-1}+1}^{L_{r}}\mu(u_{j}+(h-1)\delta,u_{j}+r\delta)

\displaystyle\qquad=\delta\sum_{r=h}^{H}(r-h+1)\ell_{r}.

Therefore,

\mu\left([0,(h-1)\delta]+Y\right)=(h-1)\delta+1-3\varepsilon-\delta\sum_{r=h}^{H}(r-h+1)\ell_{r}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 4
.

For integers
H\geq 2
and
n\geq 2
and
for
i=1,\ldots,n-1
and
j=1,2,\ldots,H
,
let
\ell_{i,j}
be nonnegative integers such that

L_{i,H}=\sum_{j=1}^{H}\ell_{i,j}>0

for all
i
. Let
\varepsilon
,
\delta
, and
c
be real numbers such that

0<\varepsilon<\frac{1}{3}

and

0<\delta<\min\left\{\frac{\varepsilon}{H-1},\frac{1-3\varepsilon}{2HL_{i,H}}\right\}.

and

(H-1)\delta+3<c.

The interval
[1+\varepsilon,2-2\varepsilon]
contains nonempty
measurable sets
Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{n}
such that the sets
A_{1},\ldots,A_{N}
defined by

A_{i}=[0,\delta]\cup(c+(X+Y_{i}))

satisfy

\mu(hA_{i})-\mu(hA_{i+1})=\delta\sum_{r=h}^{H}(r-h+1)(\ell_{i+1,r}-\ell_{i,r})

for all
h=1,\ldots,H
.

Proof.

Fom Lemmas
2
and
3
we obtain

\displaystyle\mu(hA_{i})-\mu(hA_{i+1})

\displaystyle=\mu\left([0,(h-1)\delta]+Y_{i}\right)-\mu\left([0,(h-1)\delta]+Y_{i+1}\right)

\displaystyle=\left((h-1)\delta+1-3\varepsilon-\delta\sum_{r=h}^{H}(r-h+1)\ell_{i,r}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad-\left((h-1)\delta+1-3\varepsilon-\delta\sum_{r=h}^{H}(r-h+1)\ell_{i+1,r}\right)

\displaystyle=\delta\sum_{r=h}^{H}(r-h+1)(\ell_{i+1,r}-\ell_{i,r})

for all

.
∎

The last piece of the proof of Theorem
5

is the following result about linear Diophantine equations.

Lemma 5
.

For integers
H\geq 2
and
n\geq 2
, let
m_{i,j}\in\mathbf{Z}

for all
i=1,\ldots,n-1
and
j=1,2,\ldots,H
.
There exist
\ell_{i,j}\in\mathbf{N}_{0}

for all
i=1,\ldots,n
and
j=1,2,\ldots,H

such that

(6)

\sum_{r=h}^{H}(r-h+1)(\mathbf{\ell}_{i+1,r}-\mathbf{\ell}_{i,r})=m_{i,h}

for all
i=1,\ldots,n-1
and
h=1,2,\ldots,H
.

Proof.

For all
i=1,\ldots,n-1
,
consider the following system of
H
linear equations
in
H
variables
x_{i,1},\ldots,x_{i,H}
:

\sum_{r=h}^{H}(r-h+1)x_{i,r}=m_{i,h}

for
h=1,\ldots,H
.
The matrix for this system of equations is upper triangular with
integer coefficients and 1’s on the main diagonal:

M=\left(\begin{matrix}1&2&3&4&\cdots&H-1&H\\
0&1&2&3&\cdots&H-2&H-1\\
0&0&1&2&\cdots&H-3&H-2\\
\vdots&&&&&&\vdots\\
0&0&0&0&\cdots&1&2\\
0&0&0&0&\cdots&0&1\end{matrix}\right)

and so the system has a unique solution in integers
x_{i,1},\ldots,x_{i,H}
.
The explicit solution is

\displaystyle x_{i,H}

\displaystyle=m_{i,H}

\displaystyle x_{i,H-1}

\displaystyle=m_{i,H-1}-2m_{i,H}

\displaystyle x_{i,r}

\displaystyle=m_{i,r}-2m_{i,r+1}+m_{i,r+2}\qquad\text{for all $r\in[1,H-2]$.}

Consider next, for
r=1,\ldots,H
, the linear system of
n-1
equations
in
n
variables
\ell_{i,r}
:

\displaystyle\ell_{2,r}-\ell_{1,r}

\displaystyle=x_{1,r}

\displaystyle\ell_{3,r}-\ell_{2,r}

\displaystyle=x_{2,r}

\displaystyle\ell_{4,r}-\ell_{3,r}

\displaystyle=x_{3,r}

\displaystyle\ell_{i+1,r}-\ell_{i,r}

\displaystyle=x_{i,r}

\displaystyle\ell_{n,r}-\ell_{n-1,r}

\displaystyle=x_{n-1,r}

This system has a unique solution for every choice of
\ell_{1,r}
:

\ell_{i,r}=\ell_{1,r}+\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}x_{j,r}\qquad\text{for $i=2,\ldots,n$.}

Choosing

\ell_{1,r}\geq\max\left(0,-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}x_{j,r}:i=1,\ldots,n-1\right)

gives a nonnegative solution.
If the
x_{i,r}
are integers, then choosing

\ell_{1,r}=\max\left(0,-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}x_{j,r}:i=1,\ldots,n-1\right)

gives the smallest nonnegative integral solution.
This completes the proof.
∎

We can now complete the proof of Theorem
5
.
Given integers
m_{i,h}
for all
i=1,\ldots,n-1

and
h=1,\ldots,H
, we apply Lemmas
4

and
5
and obtain integers
\ell_{i,r}

and measurable sets
A_{1},\ldots,A_{n}
such that

\displaystyle\mu(hA_{i})-\mu(hA_{i+1})

\displaystyle=\delta\sum_{r=h}^{H}(r-h+1)(\ell_{i+1,r}-\ell_{i,r})

\displaystyle=\delta m_{i,h}.

Dilating the sets
A_{1},\ldots,A_{n}
by
\theta/\delta

completes the proof.

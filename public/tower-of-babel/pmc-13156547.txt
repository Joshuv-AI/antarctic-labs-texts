Atypical multidermatomal herpes zoster in young immunocompetent adults
Authors: Grant M. Pham, Roger N. Pham
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This case series describes an unusual presentation of multidermatomal herpes zoster (HZ) in two immunocompetent young adult males, aged 35 and 20 years. Both patients presented with lesions involving multiple dermatomes, differing from the classic unilateral dermatomal pattern typically seen in HZ. Psychological stress was identified as a possible contributing factor in both cases. Dermoscopy is still infrequently utilized as a diagnostic tool in HZ. In these cases, it provided additional visual details that supported early recognition. Timely antiviral therapy and supportive measures, including stress reduction strategies, were followed by clinical resolution. This case series highlights the potential link between psychological stress and viral reactivation while also underscoring the clinical value of dermoscopy as a rapid adjunctive diagnostic tool.

Herpes zoster (HZ) usually presents as a unilateral painful vesicular eruption confined to a single dermatome. We describe two young immunocompetent Hispanic male patients, aged 35 and 20 years, with atypical HZ involving multiple adjacent dermatomes in the outpatient setting.

The first patient presented with painful grouped vesicles on the trunk and back, with involvement of multiple adjacent dermatomes (Fig. 1). Baseline laboratory studies were unremarkable and supported immunocompetent status. Dermoscopy showed an erythematous background, clustered white polyglobular vesicles, and haloing, supporting the diagnosis of HZ (Fig. 2) [1], [2]. He reported increased work related stress and sleep disturbance before symptom onset [3], [4]. The second patient presented with a similar painful vesicular eruption involving more than 1 adjacent dermatome (Fig. 3). He declined laboratory testing but reported that prior laboratory studies had been normal. Dermoscopy again supported the diagnosis and demonstrated more prominent intact vesicles on an erythematous background (Fig. 4) [1], [2]. He had no significant past medical history and reported poor sleep, with possible increased work related stress preceding the eruption [3], [4]. These cases highlight that HZ can occur in young immunocompetent adults and may present with a multidermatomal pattern [5]. Although most cases are diagnosed clinically, dermoscopy may provide a rapid and noninvasive adjunct in atypical presentations, particularly when patient age or lesion distribution makes the diagnosis less straightforward. Early recognition may facilitate timely antiviral treatment and help reduce complications such as postherpetic neuralgia, secondary bacterial infection, and other site dependent sequelae.

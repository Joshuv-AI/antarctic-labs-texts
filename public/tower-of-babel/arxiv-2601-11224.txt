Nonrelativistic versus relativistic quantum scars in billiard systems
Barbara Dietz,, Dung Xuan Nguyen,, Tilen Čadež,

Abstract
We study the features of scarred eigenstates of relativistic neutrino billiards (NBs), graphene billiards (GBs) and Haldane graphene billiards (HGBs) and recapitulate those for nonrelativistic quantum billiards (QBs) with the shapes of a full- and quarter-stadium billiard. Here, we restrict for the GBs and HGBs to the region of linear dispersion around the Fermi energy, where they are effectively described by the same Dirac equation for massless spin-1/2 particles as NBs. Scarred wave functions of the nonrelativistic billiards and spinor functions of the relativistic ones are localized along the same types of periodic orbits, the most prominent ones being bouncing-ball orbits. The objective is to demonstrate that the properties of the scarred eigenstates observed in the full- and quarter-stadium GB do not comply with those of relativistic quantum systems. For this we apply the semiclassical approach associated with such non-generic contributions, which was developed for the spectral density of QBs and NBs. It provides semiclassical trace formulas in terms of the periodic orbits associated with a scarred wave function and a procedure to extract such contributions from the eigenvalue spectra. Furthermore, we analyze momentum distributions and Husimi functions of such scarred states and employ them to classify scarred wave functions according to the periodic orbits along which they are localized. We show that for the GB the semiclassical approach, the spectral properties, the symmetry properties and generally properties of the wave functions all comply with those of the nonrelativistic QB, whereas for the HGB they agree well with those of the NB, implying that the quantum scars observed in GBs are not relativistic.

III
Graphene and Haldane Billiards

III.1
Brief review of the salient differences of GBs and HGBs

The honeycomb lattice of graphene is formed by two interpenetrating triangular sublattices
A
and
B
; cf. left part of
Fig.
1
. The basis vectors
\mathbf{a}_{i}
are defined as

\mathbf{a}_{1}=(1,0),\mathbf{a}_{2}=(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}),\mathbf{a}_{3}=(-\frac{1}{2},-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2})
,
where the distance
a
between neighboring sites of the honeycomb lattice is set equal to unity,
a=1
. In quasi-momentum space, the Hamiltonian is given

\hat{H}^{0}(\bm{\kappa})=t_{1}{\sum}_{i=1}^{3}\big(\hat{\sigma}_{x}\cos(\bm{\kappa}\cdot\mathbf{a}_{i})-\hat{\sigma}_{y}\sin(\bm{\kappa}\cdot\mathbf{a}_{i})\big),

(19)

in the basis
\psi^{T}=\left(\psi_{A}(\bm{\kappa}),\psi_{B}(\bm{\kappa})\right)

Dresselhaus et al. 1996
where
\hat{\sigma}_{x},\hat{\sigma}_{y},\hat{\sigma}_{z}
denote the Pauli matrices. The conduction and valence band
Castro Neto et al. 2009
are shown in the right part of
Fig.
2
.
Near the Dirac cone
\mathbf{K}=\left(\frac{2\pi}{3a},\frac{2\pi}{3\sqrt{3}a}\right)
the effective Hamiltonian is given by the Dirac Hamiltonian for a spin-1/2 particle,

\hat{H}^{0}_{K}(\mathbf{q})=\frac{3t_{1}a}{2}\bm{\hat{\sigma}}\cdot\mathbf{q},\qquad(\bm{\kappa}=\mathbf{K}+\mathbf{q})

(20)

with
\bm{\hat{\sigma}}=({\hat{\sigma}}_{x},{\hat{\sigma}}_{y})
and
\bm{q}
the quasimomentum vector.
Similarly, near the Dirac cone
\mathbf{K}^{\prime}=-\mathbf{K}
the effective Hamiltonian becomes

\hat{H}^{0}_{K^{\prime}}(\mathbf{q})=\frac{3t_{1}a}{2}\bm{\hat{\sigma}^{\ast}}\cdot\mathbf{q},\qquad(\bm{\kappa}=\mathbf{K}^{\prime}+\mathbf{q}),

(21)

where
\bm{\hat{\sigma}^{\ast}}
denotes complex conjugation of
\bm{\hat{\sigma}}
. The GBs are constructed by cutting their shape out of a honeycomb-lattice sheet and imposing the Dirichlet BC along the outer boundary.

The extension to the Haldane model is achieved by adding a nonzero purely imaginary next-to-nearest neighbor tunneling parameter,
it_{2}

Haldane 1988
, as illustrated in the left part of
Fig.
2
. Here, the next-to-nearest neighbor site vectors are
\mathbf{b}_{1}=(0,\sqrt{3}a),\mathbf{b}_{2}=(-\frac{3a}{2},-\frac{\sqrt{3}a}{2}),\mathbf{b}_{3}=(\frac{3a}{2},-\frac{\sqrt{3}a}{2})
. Even though complex tunneling explicitly breaks time reversal invariance, the total magnetic flux through a unit cell is zero. Furthermore, we introduce onsite potentials
M
with
M>0
on all sites of sublattice
A
and
-M
on all sites of sublattice
B
, yielding in the quasi-momentum space the Hamiltonian

\hat{H}(\bm{\kappa})=\hat{H}^{0}(\bm{\kappa})+\left(M-2t_{2}\sum_{j=1}^{3}\sin(\bm{\kappa}\cdot\mathbf{b}_{j})\right)\hat{\sigma}_{z},

(22)

where the first and second term in the rounded brackets induce breaking of the inversion symmetry and violation of time-reversal invariance, respectively. Near the
K
and the
K^{\prime}
points, respectively, the effective Hamiltonian equals

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{K}(\mathbf{q})

\displaystyle=\frac{3t_{1}a}{2}\bm{\hat{\sigma}}\cdot\mathbf{q}+\left(M-3\sqrt{3}t_{2}\right)\hat{\sigma}_{z}

(23)

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{K^{\prime}}(\mathbf{q})

\displaystyle=\frac{3t_{1}a}{2}\bm{\hat{\sigma}^{\ast}}\cdot\mathbf{q}+\left(M+3\sqrt{3}t_{2}\right)\hat{\sigma}_{z}.

(24)

Both Dirac cones are gapped in the insulator phase,
|t_{2}|<\frac{M}{3\sqrt{3}}
, and the Chern insulator-phase
|t_{2}|>\frac{M}{3\sqrt{3}}
defining a non-trivial topological phase with non-zero Chern number. On the contrary at the critical point
t_{2}=\frac{M}{3\sqrt{3}}
only one Dirac cone, namely that at the
K^{\prime}
point is gapped with the effective mass
2M
and that at the
K
point survives, implying that in the low energy limit
|E|<M
, there is only one Dirac cone. Similarly for
t_{2}=-\frac{M}{3\sqrt{3}}
, the Dirac cone at the
K
point is gapped out and a single Dirac cone remains at the
K^{\prime}
point. We have demonstrated in
Nguyen and Dietz 2024
that at these critical points the fluctuation properties in the energy spectrum of a Haldane graphene billiard (HGB), which is obtained by applying the Haldane tunneling parameter and mass terms to a GB of corresponding shape, coincide in the energy window
|E|\leq{\rm min}(|t_{1}|/2,M)
with those of the corresponding NB.

III.2
Properties of the eigenstates of the stadium GB and HGB

We constructed quarter- and full-stadium GBs and HGBs by cutting their shape out of a honeycomb lattice, which was oriented such the straight parts coincide either with an armchair edge or a zigzag edge. We present only results for the latter case. In the numerical simulations we used the Haldane model at the critical point

IV
Conclusions and Discussion

We present a thorough study of properties of quantum-scarred eigenstates of nonrelativistic quantum billiards, relativistic neutrino billiards, and of graphene and Haldane-graphene billiards and their impact on the spectral properties. For the GB and HGB results are only presented in the region of linear dispersion around the Dirac point. The billiards have the shapes of a full and a quarter stadium. We demonstrate that the scarred eigenstates of the GB do not exhibit the features of a relativistic quantum billiard, but agree with those of the nonrelativistic QB, whereas for the HGB we find good agreement with those of the NB. Here, we employ RMT and the semiclassical approach for the analysis of the spectral properties, and momentum-distributions and Husimi functions to identify and classify scarred eigenstates. We find that the nongeneric contributions to the spectral density of the GB are well described by the semiclassical trace formula for the corresponding nonrelativistic QB derived in
Sieber et al. 1993
, whereas that of the HGB and NB are well captured by the semiclassical approach for relativistic NBs developed in
Dietz and Li 2020
;
Dietz 2022
. Furthermore, the spectral properties of the full-stadium and quarter-stadium GB agree well with those of the QB, of which the spectrum is a superposition of the spectra associated with the four mirror-symmetry classes and is well described by block-diagonal random matrices, consisting of four blocks of matrices from the GOE. This indicates that the GB like the QB has mirror symmetries with respect to the
x
and
y
axes. Indeed, the wave functions of the GB exhibit a mirror-symmetry along the central armchair axis and a slightly perturbed mirror symmetry along the central zigzag axis, and they are real for both billiard systems. On the other hand, the fluctuating part of the spectral density resulting from scarred eigenstates of the HGB agrees very well with that of NBs of corresponding shape, the wave functions are complex and don’t exhibit mirror symmetries as expected for the solutions of the Dirac equation
Eq. (
2
)
. We also demonstrate that for all considered billiard systems only eigenstates that are scarred along BBOs have a perceivable impact on the spectral properties. We checked this by employing the semiclassical trace formulas provided in
Sieber et al. 1993
for the orbits on which the eigenstates are scarred (cf.
Fig.
5
) for QBs and derived by following the methods of
Sieber et al. 1993
based on
Dietz and Li 2020
;
Dietz 2022
for NBs. We come to the conclusion that contrary to the general belief
Huang et al. 2009
;
Ge et al. 2024
the quantum scars observed in GBs – as demonstrated for the full- and quarter-stadium graphene billiards – are nonrelativistic
Zhang and Dietz 2021
;
Zhang and Dietz 2023
, whereas HGBs exhibit the features required for relativistic quantum scars. The recent proposal to simulate the Haldane model with photonic crystals
Jotzu et al. 2014
;
Liu et al. 2021
;
Lannebère and Silveirinha 2019
provides a possibibility to realize energy spectra exhibiting the particular features of NBs and thus of relativistic quantum scars experimentally. Yet, although the Haldane model has been realized experimentally on several platforms, observing quantum chaos in these systems remains challenging due to limitations in system size, boundary control, and the precise measurement of an energy spectrum. Nevertheless, exploring alternative platforms for realizing the Haldane model represents a fruitful direction for future research

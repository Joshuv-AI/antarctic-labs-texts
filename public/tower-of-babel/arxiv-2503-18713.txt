A detailed study on the prospects for a 𝐭 ​ 𝐭 ¯ \mathrm{t\overline{t}} threshold scan in 𝐞 + ​ 𝐞 − \mathrm{e^{+}e^{-}} collisions
Matteo M. Defranchis, Jorge de Blas, Ankita Mehta, Michele Selvaggi, and Marcel Vos

Abstract
A scan of the beam energy across the top quark pair ( t ​ t ¯ \mathrm{t\overline{t}} ) production threshold is part of the program of future Higgs, top, and electroweak factory projects. In this paper, we provide projections for the achievable precision in the top quark mass ( m t m_{\mathrm{t}} ), width ( Γ t \Gamma_{\mathrm{t}} ), and Yukawa coupling ( y t y_{\mathrm{t}} ) at the electron-positron ( e + ​ e − \mathrm{e^{+}e^{-}} ) stage of the Future Circular Collider (FCC-ee). The study includes a detailed assessment of parametric and systematic uncertainties, as well as a rigorous estimate of the effect of point-to-point correlations. We project that m t m_{\mathrm{t}} and Γ t \Gamma_{\mathrm{t}} can be determined with an experimental precision of about 6.8 and 11.5 MeV \,\mathrm{MeV} , respectively, when m t m_{\mathrm{t}} is defined in the potential-subtracted (PS) scheme. The impact of theoretical uncertainties due to missing higher orders is found to be of about 35 (25) MeV \,\mathrm{MeV} on m t m_{\mathrm{t}} ( Γ t \Gamma_{\mathrm{t}} ) at N 3 ​ LO \mathrm{N^{3}LO} in non-relativistic QCD. Therefore, improvements in the theoretical accuracy, which is an active area of development, are key to match the achievable experimental precision at a future e + ​ e − \mathrm{e^{+}e^{-}} collider. Finally, we explore the prospects for a measurement of y t y_{\mathrm{t}} at FCC-ee via a dedicated run above the t ​ t ¯ \mathrm{t\overline{t}} production threshold.



1
Introduction

A scan of the electron-positron (
\mathrm{e^{+}e^{-}}
) centre-of-mass energy across the top quark pair (
\mathrm{t\overline{t}}
) production threshold
Gusken:1985nf
;
Fadin:1987wz
;
Fadin:1988fn
;
Strassler:1990nw
;
Guth:1991ab
;
Jezabek:1992np
is widely recognized as the “golden” method for a top quark mass measurement, combining excellent experimental precision with good control over the top quark mass scheme and interpretation.
This method, in fact, relies solely on the measurement of the inclusive production cross sections and does not require any kinematic reconstruction of the top quark pairs. Top quark reconstruction techniques are known to introduce significant dependence on the details of the Monte Carlo (MC) simulation and are often a limiting factor at hadron colliders
CMS:2024irj
. For these reasons, a dedicated
\mathrm{t\overline{t}}
threshold scan is part of the program of all proposed Higgs/top/EW factories
deBlas:2024bmz
.

At a lepton collider, the shape of the
\mathrm{WbWb}
production rate (
\sigma_{\mathrm{WbWb}}
) versus centre-of-mass energy is sensitive to the top quark mass (
m_{\mathrm{t}}
) and width (
\Gamma_{\mathrm{t}}
), while the absolute cross section around the threshold is sensitive to the strong coupling and the top quark Yukawa coupling (
y_{\mathrm{t}}
). Precise predictions of the total cross section in the threshold region are a key ingredient for this program, as they are used to extract these parameters from experimentally measured cross sections. Calculations of the cross section are now available at
\mathrm{N^{3}LO}

Beneke:2015kwa
,
and (partial) NNLL accuracy
Hoang:2013uda
. A program is publicly available that provides
\mathrm{N^{3}LO}
predictions of the cross section
Beneke:2016kkb
as a function of the centre-of-mass energy, including EW corrections and QED initial-state radiation in the calculation. The code provides predictions for the
\mathrm{WbWb}
final state, including doubly-resonant (i.e.
\mathrm{t\overline{t}}
), single-resonant (i.e. single top production
\mathrm{e^{+}e^{-}}\to\mathrm{tWb}
), and non-resonant contributions (
\mathrm{e^{+}e^{-}}\to\mathrm{W^{+}W^{-}b\overline{b}}
). Other contributions, such as
\mathrm{e^{+}e^{-}}\rightarrow\mathrm{W^{+}W^{-}Z}
with
\mathrm{Z\to b\overline{b}}
, are not taken into account and are treated as background.

Early experimental studies were performed by Martinez & Miquel
Martinez:2002st
. Their experimental strategy used several differential cross section measurements to enhance the sensitivity and disentangle the Standard Model (SM) parameters. While work towards a MC event generator that includes the effect of the Coulomb pseudo-bound-state cross-section enhancement
Bach:2017ggt
is ongoing, the highest-precision calculations only predict the inclusive cross section. Therefore, more recent studies adopt a simple strategy, first explored by Seidel et al.
Seidel:2013sqa
and Horiguchi et al.
Horiguchi:2013wra
. In that study, the parameter extraction is based on a measurement of the inclusive cross section at 10 or 11 different centre-of-mass energies, with points spaced by 1
\,\mathrm{GeV}
and a total integrated luminosity of 100–200
\,\mathrm{fb^{-1}}
. This approach is adopted as the baseline by ILC and CLIC
CLICdp:2018esa
. These studies show that a statistical precision of the top quark mass measurement of about 20
\,\mathrm{MeV}
can be achieved. The limiting factor is the precision of the predictions, as scale and parametric uncertainties add up to approximately 50
\,\mathrm{MeV}
. Theory uncertainties also dominate the uncertainty for the other parameters. The top quark Yukawa coupling, for instance, can be measured to a competitive 4% statistical precision
Horiguchi:2013wra
. The potential of this measurement is limited in practice, as the
\alpha_{\mathrm{S}}
and scale uncertainties in the prediction yield a systematic uncertainty of approximately 20%
Vos:2016til
.
Studies by Nowak & Zarnecki
Nowak:2021tyn
;
Nowak:2021xmp
and the CEPC group
Li:2022iav
optimize the number and spacing of the scan points. A concentration of the integrated luminosity at the steepest section of the threshold can yield a considerable improvement of the statistical uncertainty that can be obtained with a given integrated luminosity, but does not test the agreement between the predicted shape and the data and may be less robust against systematic and theoretical uncertainties. The goal of this work is to outline a robust baseline strategy for the
\mathrm{t\overline{t}}
threshold scan, derive detailed projections for the FCC-ee operating scenario, and compare our findings with results obtained for other proposed colliders.

To set a target for the desired precision in
m_{\mathrm{t}}
at future
\mathrm{e^{+}e^{-}}
colliders, we investigate the impact of the
m_{\mathrm{t}}
uncertainty on the SM prediction of electroweak (EW) precision observables (EWPO). This approach exploits the fact that loop diagrams in the predictions for these observables introduce contributions proportional to
m_{\mathrm{t}}^{2}
. This is particularly relevant in the case of the W boson mass (
m_{\mathrm{W}}
), which can be measured at FCC-ee with an uncertainty of about 0.24
\,\mathrm{MeV}

FCC_FSR
. We estimate the total uncertainty in the SM prediction of
m_{\mathrm{W}}
as a function of the uncertainty in
m_{\mathrm{t}}
, assuming the projected uncertainties for other input parameters after the Z pole and WW threshold runs at FCC-ee, as detailed in Appendix
A
. The result, shown in Figure
1
(left), showcases the need for a measurement of
m_{\mathrm{t}}
at the level of 50
\,\mathrm{MeV}
or better. This target is a factor of five better than the achievable precision at the HL-LHC
Azzi:2019yne
. With an improved measurement of the electromagnetic constant (
\alpha_{\mathrm{EM}}
) at the Z pole from Ref.
Riembau:2025ppc
, a
m_{\mathrm{t}}
measurement with an uncertainty below 30
\,\mathrm{MeV}
becomes necessary to match the experimental precision in
m_{\mathrm{W}}
. The impact of the intrinsic theoretical uncertainties of the electroweak precision fit is not considered in this result, but their effect is shown in Figure
1
(right).

In this paper, we revisit the potential of a
\mathrm{t\overline{t}}
threshold scan by performing a full analysis including FCC-ee simulated events and a phenomenological study using
\mathrm{N^{3}LO}
theory predictions. For this purpose, we consider a
\mathrm{t\overline{t}}
threshold scan at FCC-ee with a total integrated luminosity of 410
\,\mathrm{fb^{-1}}

FCC_FSR
. In Section
2
we present a full demonstration of a cross section measurement using simulated events, including background simulation and experimental systematic uncertainties. These results are then used in Section
3
to derive projections for the uncertainties on the top quark mass (
m_{\mathrm{t}}
) and width (
\Gamma_{\mathrm{t}}
), including estimates for experimental, machine-related, parametric, and theoretical uncertainties, by performing a phenomenological analysis of the
\mathrm{t\overline{t}}
threshold scan.
We then investigate the dependence of the results on the assumptions on the systematic uncertainties (Section
4
) and we explore the possibility of a determination of the top Yukawa coupling above the
\mathrm{t\overline{t}}
production threshold in Section
5
. The baseline scenario for this dedicated run assumes an integrated luminosity of 2.65
\,\mathrm{ab^{-1}}
at
\sqrt{s}=365\,\mathrm{GeV}
. Finally, in Section
7
we compare these results to previous work and comment on the applicability of our findings to other proposed
\mathrm{e^{+}e^{-}}
colliders, and specifically to a linear collider and CEPC.

2
Measurement of the WbWb production cross section at FCC-ee

In this section, we outline an experimental strategy for the measurement of the
\mathrm{WbWb}
production rate at different centre-of-mass energies at FCC-ee. We focus on three different centre-of-mass energies, corresponding to
\sqrt{s}=340
, 345, and 365
\,\mathrm{GeV}
. These represent
\mathrm{WbWb}
production below, at, and above the
\mathrm{t\overline{t}}
threshold, respectively. We restrict our study to the fully hadronic and the semi-hadronic decay channels of the W boson pairs, which together account for a combined branching ratio of about 75% for this process
ParticleDataGroup:2024cfk
. Di-leptonic decay channels and the final states involving
\tau
leptons, which account for the remaining 5% and 15% of the branching ratio, respectively, are expected to provide some additional sensitivity and can be included in future work. However, events in which
\tau
leptons decay into an electron or a muon are included in this study.
The considered sources of background include DY production in the
\mathrm{q\overline{q}}
final state, WW and ZZ production, and WWZ production in the
\mathrm{Z}\to\mathrm{b\overline{b}}
final state.
The
\mathrm{WbWb}
, WWZ, and
\mathrm{q\overline{q}}
samples are produced using the WHIZARD generator
Kilian:2007gr
interfaced with Pythia6
Sjostrand:2006za
, while ZZ and WW are generated with Pythia8
Sjostrand:2014zea
. All samples are generated at leading order (LO) and are normalised to the cross sections summarised in Table
1
. Detector effects are simulated using the
Delphes

deFavereau:2013fsa
parametrisation of the IDEA detector concept
IDEAStudyGroup:2025gbt
;
delphes_card_idea
at FCC-ee.

Events are classified in the corresponding decay channel based on the number of reconstructed leptons. Electrons and muons with momenta (
p
) larger than 12
\,\mathrm{GeV}
are considered. Taking the geometrical acceptance of the detector into account, this selection was found to have an acceptance of 99.5% for all centre-of-mass energies.
To suppress the contamination from leptons arising from hadron decays, we also require leptons to be isolated. This is imposed by requiring that the scalar sum of the momenta of all reconstructed particles (other than the lepton itself) falling within a cone of radius 0.5 centred around the lepton does not exceed 25% of the momentum of the lepton.
In order to exploit the difference in expected jet multiplicity between signal and background processes, an inclusive jet algorithm is applied to all reconstructed particles, excluding the selected electrons and muons. For this purpose, an inclusive generalised
k_{\mathrm{T}}
algorithm is used
Catani:1991hj
, with a radius of 0.5 and a momentum cut-off of 5
\,\mathrm{GeV}
. Events with less than two (one) reconstructed jets are vetoed in the hadronic (semi-hadronic) channels. The jet multiplicity for the considered processes is shown in Figure
2
. The b jet identification (b tagging) is parametrised according to the efficiency and misidentification rate according to Refs.
TAGGINGFULLSIM_NOTE
;
Bedeschi:2022rnj
, which are assumed to be constant with respect to the jet kinematics. As the rest of the analysis does not make use of kinematic information of the b-tagged jets, this approximation does not affect the results. For a b tagging efficiency of 95%, we assume misidentification rates of 0.1%, 3.2%, and 2.0% for light, charm, and gluon jets, respectively
TAGGINGFULLSIM_NOTE
. These estimates were derived using Higgs decays into two jets in ZH production, with Z bosons decaying into a pair of muons. Dedicated studies in
\mathrm{t\overline{t}}
topologies are ongoing, although we do not expect these assumptions to affect the conclusions of our study.

To measure the total
\mathrm{WbWb}
production rate we perform a maximum-likelihood fit to jet and b jet multiplicities, simultaneously in the hadronic and semi-hadronic channels. This fit strategy, combined with the loose event selection requirements described above, allows the effect of the background contamination to be controlled without compromising the signal acceptance. The distributions used in the fit are shown in Figure
2
. Events are classified into three categories according to the number of reconstructed b jets (
N_{\mathrm{bjets}}
). Events with
N_{\mathrm{bjets}}=0
provide a control region for WW and
\mathrm{q\overline{q}}
backgrounds, while events with
N_{\mathrm{bjets}}\geq 2
define the signal region. Events with
N_{\mathrm{bjets}}=1
are enriched in signal events with one unidentified b jet and provide a powerful handle for an in-situ determination of the b tagging efficiency. Finally, the jet multiplicity distribution (
N_{\mathrm{jets}}
) is fitted in each category (with the exception of the
N_{\mathrm{bjets}}=0
category in the semi-hadronic channel, which is pure in WW events), and provides strong separation between the various processes. The fit distributions for the other centre-of-mass energies can be found in Appendix
C
.

The effect of systematic uncertainties is incorporated into the fit via dedicated nuisance parameters. To account for possible differences in detector-related effects, the
\mathrm{q\overline{q}}
, WW, and ZZ backgrounds are treated as independent processes between the hadronic and semi-hadronic channels. The normalisation of the
\mathrm{q\overline{q}}
background in the hadronic channel and that of the WW background in both channels are treated as free-floating parameters. Furthermore, the normalisation of the ZZ background in the hadronic channel and that of the WWZ background in both channels are assigned an arbitrary but conservative normalisation uncertainty of 3%. On the other hand, the
\mathrm{q\overline{q}}
and ZZ backgrounds in the semi-hadronic channels, which are characterised by misidentified or (in the case of ZZ) unreconstructed leptons, are assigned a conservative normalisation uncertainty of 5%. A luminosity uncertainty of 0.1% is assumed for 340 and 345
\,\mathrm{GeV}
, which is conservatively scaled down by a factor of five (smaller than the statistical scaling) for 365
\,\mathrm{GeV}
. A more detailed discussion of these estimates will be given in Section
3
. Finally, a largely conservative uncertainty of 1% is assigned to the b tagging efficiency, with the goal of constraining this effect in situ by exploiting the b jet multiplicity classification. Other potential sources of uncertainty, such as the dependence of the signal simulation on the value of the top quark mass and variations in the renormalisation scale in the parton shower for all processes, have been considered but were found to have no significant effect on the distributions used in the fit.

The results are summarised in Table
2
for the three considered centre-of-mass energies. In all cases, we find that the impact of systematic uncertainties on the
\mathrm{WbWb}
production rate can be controlled at a level well below the statistical uncertainty, allowing for effectively uncorrelated measurements at the different centre-of-mass energies. In particular, all background components, as well as the b tagging efficiencies, are constrained in the fit at the permille level or below. The impact plots for the fits can be found in Appendix
C
. In view of these considerations, we will assume in the following that the
\mathrm{WbWb}
production cross section can be determined with a total uncertainty equal to the statistical uncertainty on the total rate for any of the considered centre-of-mass energies.

3
Phenomenological analysis of the
\mathrm{t\overline{t}}
threshold scan

The values of the top quark mass (
m_{\mathrm{t}}
) and total width (
\Gamma_{\mathrm{t}}
) can be determined by performing a fit of theoretical predictions to the measured
\mathrm{WbWb}
cross section as a function of the centre-of-mass energy. For this purpose, we calculate the
\mathrm{WbWb}
production cross section at
\mathrm{N^{3}LO}
in non-relativistic QCD (NR-QCD) using the
QQbar_Threshold
package
Beneke:2016kkb
. Electroweak corrections to the potential between the
\mathrm{t\overline{t}}
pair are also included in the calculation, as well as off-shell effects and non-resonant contributions, as explained in Section
1
. The code also calculates the effect of initial-state radiation (ISR) at leading logarithmic (LL) accuracy. In
QQbar_Threshold
, the value of
m_{\mathrm{t}}
can be set independently to that of
\Gamma_{\mathrm{t}}
. The values of the strong coupling constant (
\alpha_{\mathrm{S}}
) and the top quark Yukawa coupling (
y_{\mathrm{t}}
) can also be varied. In this work, we consider
m_{\mathrm{t}}
as defined in the potential subtracted (PS) scheme (
m_{\mathrm{t}}^{\mathrm{PS}}
). The relation between
m_{\mathrm{t}}^{\mathrm{PS}}
and other mass schemes is discussed in Section
7
. According to the prescription given in Ref.
Beneke:2024sfa
, we set the renormalisation and factorisation scales to 80
\,\mathrm{GeV}
and 350
\,\mathrm{GeV}
, respectively, while the scale in the definition of
m_{\mathrm{t}}^{\mathrm{PS}}
is set to 20
\,\mathrm{GeV}
. The
\mathrm{t\overline{t}}
threshold lineshape, calculated with
QQbar_Threshold
, is then convoluted with the expected FCC-ee beam energy spread (BES), which is assumed to be a Gaussian distribution with a standard deviation of 0.184% per beam. For the sake of illustration, we assume
m_{\mathrm{t}}^{\mathrm{PS}}=171.5\,\mathrm{GeV}
and
\Gamma_{\mathrm{t}}=1.33\,\mathrm{GeV}
as default values for the predictions. Figure
3
(left) shows the effect of the ISR, which is responsible for a decrease in the total
\mathrm{WbWb}
production cross section, and of the BES, which smears the pseudo-bound-state enhancement at the
\mathrm{t\overline{t}}
threshold. The perturbative convergence of the calculation is shown in Figure
3
(right). This result is used to define a range of validity of the NR-QCD calculation, between
\sqrt{s}=340.0
and 344.5
\,\mathrm{GeV}
. We therefore focus on this range for the determination of
m_{\mathrm{t}}
and
\Gamma_{\mathrm{t}}
.

m_{\mathrm{t}}
,

,

) is shown in Figure

(left). For illustration, the value of
m_{\mathrm{t}}
(

) is varied by 30 (50)
\,\mathrm{MeV}
,
\alpha_{\mathrm{S}}(m_{\mathrm{Z}}^{2})

2\times 10^{-4}

y_{\mathrm{t}}

y_{\mathrm{t}}
and
\alpha_{\mathrm{S}}

We therefore expect these two parameters to be largely correlated in the lineshape fit. In contrast, variations in
m_{\mathrm{t}}
and
\Gamma_{\mathrm{t}}
introduce significantly different shape distortions to the
\mathrm{t\overline{t}}
spectrum, as the values of
m_{\mathrm{t}}
and
\Gamma_{\mathrm{t}}
control the position and width of the peak, respectively. It is therefore possible to simultaneously determine these two parameters from the
\mathrm{t\overline{t}}
threshold scan, without assuming any SM relation between the two. This allows for a more model-independent measurement of
\Gamma_{\mathrm{t}}
, therefore enhancing the sensitivity to any contribution beyond the SM (BSM) in the top quark decay. An alternative approach in which the SM relation between
m_{\mathrm{t}}
and
\Gamma_{\mathrm{t}}
is assumed was also implemented and is described later in the text. It should also be noted that the largest sensitivity to
m_{\mathrm{t}}
and
\Gamma_{\mathrm{t}}
is observed within the range of validity of the NR-QCD calculation (Figure
3
). Finally, variations in the weak mixing angle within the expected precision at FCC-ee were also considered but were found to have a negligible effect.

For the fit of the
\mathrm{t\overline{t}}
threshold lineshape, we choose a baseline scenario with 10 equally-spaced points between
\sqrt{s}=340.0
and 344.5
\,\mathrm{GeV}
, corresponding to the range of validity of the NR-QCD calculation (Figure
3
). This results in a spacing of 0.5
\,\mathrm{GeV}
between the different centre-of-mass energies, corresponding to about one standard deviation of the BES. We further assume that the total integrated luminosity planned for the
\mathrm{t\overline{t}}
threshold run is split equally between the different scan points, resulting in 41
\,\mathrm{fb^{-1}}
per scan point. While it was demonstrated that the overall precision can be maximised by choosing a smaller number of scan points
Li:2022iav
, this approach would not allow testing the validity of the theoretical prediction and may undermine the robustness of the result. We therefore choose a scenario that allows for stringent tests of the theoretical calculation, without significantly compromising on the achievable precision in the parameters of interest. Alternative scenarios with a different number of scan points, spacing between the points, or distribution of the integrated luminosity between scan points were tested. However, none of these scenarios showed a significant improvement over the baseline scenario.

The fit of the
\mathrm{t\overline{t}}
lineshape is performed by maximising a
\chi^{2}
function.
The dependence of the predicted cross sections on
m_{\mathrm{t}}
,
\Gamma_{\mathrm{t}}
,
y_{\mathrm{t}}
, and
\alpha_{\mathrm{S}}
, as well as on the BES, and beam energy calibration (BEC) is incorporated in the
\chi^{2}
in the form of nuisance parameters. The effect of these parameters is evaluated using discrete 2-point variations, which are then interpolated linearly.
A covariance matrix is built using the uncertainty in the integrated luminosity and the statistical uncertainty of the measured cross sections. As explained in Section
2
, the measured values of
\sigma_{\mathrm{WbWb}}
at the different scan points are limited by statistical uncertainties, and can therefore be considered statistically independent. For each scan point
i
, the statistical uncertainty in the measured cross section (
\sigma_{i}
) is estimated as
\sqrt{\sigma_{i}/\mathcal{L}_{i}}
, where
\mathcal{L}_{i}
is the assumed integrated luminosity for that scan point. In order to approximately match the total uncertainties of Table
2
, we inflate this uncertainty by a factor of 1.2 for all centre-of-mass energies. This is necessary in order to take into account the effect of the background contamination. The values of
m_{\mathrm{t}}
and
\Gamma_{\mathrm{t}}
are treated as free-floating parameters in the fit, while the other parameters are constrained using Gaussian priors representing external constraints. Further details on the statistical model can be found in Appendix

6
Comparison with other proposed
\mathrm{e^{+}e^{-}}
colliders

The qualitative conclusions of this study apply more generally and can be transferred to other proposed
\mathrm{e^{+}e^{-}}
colliders with some adaptations. The CEPC environment is very similar to that of FCC-ee. The project envisages collecting an integrated luminosity of 100
\,\mathrm{fb^{-1}}
at the
\mathrm{t\overline{t}}
production threshold, increasing the statistical uncertainties by a factor of two with respect to Table
3
. The authors of Ref.
Li:2022iav
project machine calibration uncertainties of 2–5
\,\mathrm{MeV}
, in agreement with our findings. At linear colliders
Seidel:2013sqa
;
Horiguchi:2013wra
;
CLICdp:2018esa
an integrated luminosity of 200
\,\mathrm{fb^{-1}}
is typically envisaged, and the smaller luminosity compared to FCC-ee is expected to be partially compensated for by the left-right beam polarization. An important difference is the shape of the luminosity spectrum, which develops a tail towards lower energy due to beamstrahlung. This effect is responsible for an effective reduction of the
\mathrm{WbWb}
production cross section by about a factor of two at the
\mathrm{t\overline{t}}
threshold
CLICdp:2018esa
compared to a circular collider. The shape of the luminosity spectrum must be precisely calibrated with Bhabha events for the most precise determination of the top quark mass and width
Poss:2013oea
. The interplay of parametric uncertainties changes, as
\alpha_{\mathrm{S}}
is less constrained at the Z pole, while
y_{\mathrm{t}}
can be measured in
\mathrm{e^{+}e^{-}}\to\mathrm{t\overline{t}}\mathrm{H}
production at a similar level of precision as at HL-LHC
CLICdp:2018esa
. However, a larger experimental uncertainty in
\Gamma_{\mathrm{t}}
is expected at a linear collider
CLICdp:2018esa
. This is due to the broader beam energy spectrum but also to the lower expected integrated luminosity compared to FCC-ee. At FCC-ee, a measurement of
\Gamma_{\mathrm{t}}
with an uncertainty of 13
\,\mathrm{MeV}
would exclude BSM decays of top quarks with a branching ratio larger than 1%. Such BSM decays could be directly probed with order one million
\mathrm{t\overline{t}}
events expected at the 365
\,\mathrm{GeV}
run. A precise measurement of
\Gamma_{\mathrm{t}}
can also be used as an input to global interpretations in the context of SM effective field theories (SMEFT)
Durieux:2018tev
.
Given the dominant impact of current theory uncertainties compared to uncertainties in machine-related calibrations, differences among available projections are often due to differences in assumptions on future theory improvements. We however find that the FCC-ee would offer higher sensitivity to
m_{\mathrm{t}}
and
\Gamma_{\mathrm{t}}
, which would become a decisive factor once theoretical improvements become available.

7
Summary and prospects

We have presented a projection of the achievable precision in the measurement of the top quark mass (
m_{\mathrm{t}}
) and width (
\Gamma_{\mathrm{t}}
) at FCC-ee, including a detailed assessment of all relevant systematic effects. A detector-level measurement of the
\mathrm{WbWb}
production cross section at different centre-of-mass energies was demonstrated using FCC-ee simulation, showing that all background components and systematic uncertainties can be controlled to a level well below the expected statistical uncertainty.
These results were then used as input to a phenomenological analysis of the
\mathrm{t\overline{t}}
threshold scan where the value of
m_{\mathrm{t}}
is fitted simultaneously with
\Gamma_{\mathrm{t}}
, assuming the FCC-ee scenario for the integrated luminosity and machine-related parameters. We show that a precision of 6.8 (11.5)
\,\mathrm{MeV}
can be achieved for
m_{\mathrm{t}}
(
\Gamma_{\mathrm{t}}
), considering all experimental, parametric, and machine-related uncertainties, when
m_{\mathrm{t}}
is defined in the potential-subtracted (PS) scheme. The statistical uncertainty is estimated to be 4.3 and 10.4
\,\mathrm{MeV}
for
m_{\mathrm{t}}
and
\Gamma_{\mathrm{t}}
, respectively. We note however that the theoretical uncertainty remains the limiting factor with state-of-the-art theoretical predictions, with an impact of 35 (25)
\,\mathrm{MeV}
on
m_{\mathrm{t}}
(
\Gamma_{\mathrm{t}}
) at
\mathrm{N^{3}LO}
in non-relativistic QCD. Nonetheless, reducing the impact of theoretical uncertainties to match the expected experimental precision is an active area of development
Beneke:2024sfa
.
We also conduct a detailed investigation of the dependence of these results on the assumption on the various systematic uncertainties. This allows our results to be updated in a straightforward (although approximate) way following any refinement in the estimates of the input uncertainties. Finally, we show that a high-luminosity run at the centre-of-mass energy of 365
\,\mathrm{GeV}
could achieve a determination of the top quark Yukawa coupling (
y_{\mathrm{t}}
) at the level of 2.1%, excluding theoretical uncertainties. However, we estimate that a cross section calculation with an uncertainty of 0.1% or better is needed to match the experimental precision.

In conclusion, this work demonstrates the remarkable physics reach of a
\mathrm{t\overline{t}}
threshold run at future
\mathrm{e^{+}e^{-}}
colliders, and in particular at FCC-ee, which would allow
m_{\mathrm{t}}
and
\Gamma_{\mathrm{t}}
to be measured in a theoretically and experimentally robust way, and with unprecedented precision. We also show that significant theoretical progress is crucial in order to fully exploit the physics potential of a future
\mathrm{e^{+}e^{-}}
machine.

Appendix A
Impact of the top quark mass precision in the Standard Model electroweak fit at FCC-ee

The measurements of the so-called electroweak precision observables provide one of the most stringent tests of the validity of the SM description of the EW interactions. Through radiative corrections, the SM predictions of EWPO depend on all the inputs of the theory.
Of particular importance are the top-quark loop effects, which introduce
a quadratic dependence on
m_{\mathrm{t}}
. This parameter can be
tested by very precise measurements, e.g. of the W boson mass,
and indirectly determined from a global fit to EWPO.
The resulting value can then be compared with direct measurements of
m_{\mathrm{t}}
, providing a test of the consistency of the SM.
This, however, requires a precise direct determination of the top-quark mass, with similar or better precision than the indirect determination.
A precise direct determination of
m_{\mathrm{t}}
(and the other SM parameters) is also needed for the interpretation of the EWPO in terms of new physics, so the SM parametric uncertainties associated with the finite precision of the input parameters are not to be confounded with possible new effects that could be small compared with current experimental precision, but could be tested in the electroweak physics programme of future
\mathrm{e^{+}e^{-}}
colliders.

Appendix B
Statistical model for the fit of the
\mathrm{t\overline{t}}
threshold scan

The
\chi^{2}
function used for the analysis of the
\mathrm{t\overline{t}}
threshold scan in Section
3
depends on the parameters of interest (
m_{\mathrm{t}}
and
\Gamma_{\mathrm{t}}
), on the profiled SM parameters (
y_{\mathrm{t}}
and
\alpha_{\mathrm{S}}
), and on nuisance parameters that model the impact of the systematic uncertainties (
\vec{\lambda}
). All systematic uncertainties (except the integrated luminosity) are convoluted with the calculated cross section and are therefore implemented as modifiers of the predicted cross section. The
\chi^{2}
function is defined as

\displaystyle\chi^{2}(m_{\mathrm{t}},\Gamma_{\mathrm{t}},y_{\mathrm{t}},\alpha_{\mathrm{S}},\vec{\lambda})=

\displaystyle\penalty\ \vec{r}\,(m_{\mathrm{t}},\Gamma_{\mathrm{t}},y_{\mathrm{t}},\alpha_{\mathrm{S}},\vec{\lambda})\penalty\ C_{\mathrm{tot}}^{-1}\penalty\ \vec{r}\,(m_{\mathrm{t}},\Gamma_{\mathrm{t}},y_{\mathrm{t}},\alpha_{\mathrm{S}},\vec{\lambda})^{\mathrm{T}}+

(1)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}^{2}+\left(\frac{\alpha_{\mathrm{S}}-\alpha_{\mathrm{S}}^{0}}{\delta\alpha_{\mathrm{S}}}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{y_{\mathrm{t}}-y_{\mathrm{t}}^{0}}{\delta y_{\mathrm{t}}}\right)^{2},

(2)

where

\vec{r}\,(m_{\mathrm{t}},\Gamma_{\mathrm{t}},y_{\mathrm{t}},\alpha_{\mathrm{S}},\vec{\lambda})=\vec{\sigma}_{\mathrm{exp}}-\vec{\sigma}_{\mathrm{th}}(m_{\mathrm{t}},\Gamma_{\mathrm{t}},y_{\mathrm{t}},\alpha_{\mathrm{S}},\vec{\lambda})

(3)

represent the residuals between the calculated cross section and the experimentally measured ones, which here correspond to an Asimov data set or a pseudo-dataset. In the case where a pseudo-dataset is used, the
\vec{\sigma}_{\mathrm{exp}}
are generated according to a Gaussian distribution around the central value of the cross section and according to the expected experimental uncertainty. In the case of the Asimov data set, the
\vec{\sigma}_{\mathrm{exp}}
correspond to the central value of the cross section. The last three terms represent Gaussian penalty terms for the nuisance parameters and for the parametric uncertainty in
\alpha_{\mathrm{S}}
and
y_{\mathrm{t}}
. Here, all nuisance parameters are defined in a way that all penalty terms follow a standard normal distribution. The covariance matrix in Eq.
2
is defined as

C_{\mathrm{tot}}=C_{\mathrm{exp}}+C_{\mathrm{lumi}}^{\mathrm{uncorr}}+C_{\mathrm{lumi}}^{\mathrm{corr}},

(4)

where
C_{\mathrm{exp}}
is the covariance matrix of the experimentally measured cross sections, while
C_{\mathrm{lumi}}^{\mathrm{uncorr}}
and
C_{\mathrm{lumi}}^{\mathrm{corr}}
represent the uncorrelated and correlated component of the luminosity uncertainty, respectively. The matrix
C_{\mathrm{exp}}
is assumed to be diagonal and is calculated as

\left[C_{\mathrm{exp}}\right]_{i,j}=\delta_{i,j}\sqrt{\frac{\sigma_{\mathrm{exp}}^{i}}{\mathcal{L}_{i}}}\sqrt{\frac{\sigma_{\mathrm{exp}}^{j}}{\mathcal{L}_{j}}},

(5)

where
\mathcal{L}_{i}
and
\sigma_{\mathrm{exp}}^{i}
are the integrated luminosity and measured cross sections for scan point
i
, respectively. As explained in Section
3
, these uncertainties are inflated by a factor of 1.2 in order to take into account the effect of background contamination, according to the results of Section
2
. Similarly,
C_{\mathrm{lumi}}^{\mathrm{uncorr}}
and
C_{\mathrm{lumi}}^{\mathrm{corr}}
are built according to the uncorrelated (correlated) luminosity uncertainty of 0.1 (0.05)% for all centre-of-mass energies (see Table
3
). Therefore:

\displaystyle\left[C_{\mathrm{lumi}}^{\mathrm{uncorr}}\right]_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\delta_{i,j}\left[\sigma_{\mathrm{exp}}^{i}\cdot 0.1\%\right]\left[\sigma_{\mathrm{exp}}^{j}\cdot 0.1\%\right]

(6)

\displaystyle\left[C_{\mathrm{lumi}}^{\mathrm{corr}}\right]_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\left[\sigma_{\mathrm{exp}}^{i}\cdot 0.05\%\right]\left[\sigma_{\mathrm{exp}}^{j}\cdot 0.05\%\right]

(7)

The dependence of the calculated cross section on all parameters is modelled via a linear interpolation of two-point variations. We define nuisance parameters so that
\lambda_{i}=0
corresponds to the baseline value of the systematic sources, and
\lambda_{i}=1
corresponds to the systematic sources varied by one standard deviation. In this way, the cross section for any value of
\lambda_{i}
can be approximated using the following expression:

\vec{\sigma}_{\mathrm{th}}(\lambda_{i})=\sigma_{\mathrm{th}}(\lambda_{i}=0)\left\{1+\lambda_{i}\left[\frac{\vec{\sigma}_{\mathrm{th}}(\lambda_{i}=1)}{\vec{\sigma}_{\mathrm{th}}(\lambda_{i}=0)}-1\right]\right\}

(8)

When estimating the correlated components, a single parameter
\lambda_{i}
induces a simultaneous variation on all the scan points. In contrast, the uncorrelated components are estimated by performing the variation independently for each individual scan point. The cross section for any set of arbitrary values of the nuisance parameters can then be estimated as:

\vec{\sigma}_{\mathrm{th}}(\vec{\lambda})=\sigma_{\mathrm{th}}(\vec{\lambda}=\vec{0})\prod_{i}\left\{1+\lambda_{i}\left[\frac{\vec{\sigma}_{\mathrm{th}}(\lambda_{i}=1)}{\vec{\sigma}_{\mathrm{th}}(\lambda_{i}=0)}-1\right]\right\},

(9)

assuming the full factorisation of all variations. The same procedure is followed for the other parameters (
m_{\mathrm{t}}
,
\Gamma_{\mathrm{t}}
,
y_{\mathrm{t}}
, and
\alpha_{\mathrm{S}}
), enforcing the effect to be correlated between the different centre-of-mass energies. For example, in the case of
y_{\mathrm{t}}
, we use the following approximation:

\vec{\sigma}_{\mathrm{th}}(y_{\mathrm{t}})=\sigma_{\mathrm{th}}(y_{\mathrm{t}}=1)\left\{1+\left(\frac{y_{\mathrm{t}}-1}{\delta y_{\mathrm{t}}}\right)\left[\frac{\vec{\sigma}_{\mathrm{th}}(y_{\mathrm{t}}=1+\delta y_{\mathrm{t}})}{\vec{\sigma}_{\mathrm{th}}(y_{\mathrm{t}}=1)}-1\right]\right\},

(10)

where
y_{\mathrm{t}}=1
corresponds to the default value of
y_{\mathrm{t}}
. In the case of
y_{\mathrm{t}}
and
\alpha_{\mathrm{S}}
we use values of
\delta y_{\mathrm{t}}
and
\delta\alpha_{\mathrm{S}}
corresponding to the values in Table
3
. The same values are also used in Eq.
2
. In the case of
m_{\mathrm{t}}
and
\Gamma_{\mathrm{t}}
, we use arbitrary variations of 30 and 50
\,\mathrm{MeV}
, respectively. This choice does not impact the result of the fit, as these variations do not appear in Eq.
2
. The factorisation of the variations and the validity of the linear interpolation have been explicitly checked for
m_{\mathrm{t}}
,
\Gamma_{\mathrm{t}}
,
y_{\mathrm{t}}
, and
\alpha_{\mathrm{S}}
, and closure is achieved well below the permille level for arbitrary simultaneous variations of these parameters. The
\chi^{2}
function is then minimised using the iminuit package
iminuit
to obtain the best-fit values for the parameters and the corresponding covariance matrix.

Furthermore, the parameter scans presented in Section
4
are obtained by rescaling the penalty terms in Eq.
2
and, in the case of the integrated luminosity, the covariances in Eq.
7
. Finally, the
\chi^{2}
function used in Section
5
for the fit of
y_{\mathrm{t}}
is the same as the one in Eq.
2
, but without the penalty term for
y_{\mathrm{t}}
.

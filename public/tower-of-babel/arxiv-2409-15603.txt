A revisit on well-posedness of a boundary value problem of a stationary advection equation without the separation condition
Masaki Imagawa, Daisuke Kawagoe

Abstract
We consider a boundary value problem of a stationary advection equation in a bounded domain with Lipschitz boundary. It is known to be well-posed in L p L^{p} -based function spaces for 1 < p < ∞ 1<p<\infty under the separation condition of the inflow and the outflow boundaries. In this article, we provide another sufficient condition for the well-posedness with 1 ≤ p ≤ ∞ 1\leq p\leq\infty . Keywords: first order PDE, boundary value problem, trace operators 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification. 35F15, 35L02



1
Introduction

In this article, we discuss well-posedness of a boundary value problem of a stationary advection equation in a bounded domain with Lipschitz boundary in
L^{p}
-based function spaces for
1\leq p\leq\infty
without the separation condition.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with Lipschitz boundary. Also, let
\beta\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)^{d}
and
\mu\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
and let
q
be the Hölder conjugate, namely, a real number such that
p^{-1}+q^{-1}=1
. Here and in what follows, we use the convention:
p^{-1}=0
when
p=\infty
.

We consider the following boundary value problem:

\begin{cases}\beta\cdot\nabla u+\mu u=f&\mbox{ in }\Omega,\\
u=g&\mbox{ on }\Gamma_{-},\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
f\in W^{q}_{\beta,+}(\Omega)^{\prime}
and
g\in L^{p}(\Gamma_{-};|\beta\cdot n|)
. The boundary value problem (
1.1
) is a basic model of inviscid flow. The sets
\Gamma_{-}
and
\Gamma_{+}
are called the inflow and the outflow boundaries respectively, and they are characterized by the sign of the inner product
\beta\cdot n
, where
n
is the outward unit normal vector to
\partial\Omega
. We give their precise definitions in Section
2
. In what follows, we assume that the inflow boundary
\Gamma_{-}
has the positive measure with respect to the surface measure.

For
1\leq p\leq\infty
, a Banach space
W^{p}_{\beta}(\Omega)
is defined by

W^{p}_{\beta}(\Omega):=\{v\in L^{p}(\Omega)\mid\beta\cdot\nabla v\in L^{p}(\Omega)\}

with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{W^{p}_{\beta}(\Omega)}
defined by

\|u\|_{W^{p}_{\beta}(\Omega)}:=\left(\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}+\|\beta\cdot\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

for
1\leq p<\infty
and

\|u\|_{W^{\infty}_{\beta}(\Omega)}:=\max\left\{\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)},\|\beta\cdot\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\right\}.

The norms
\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}(\Gamma_{\pm};|\beta\cdot n|)}
on function spaces
L^{p}(\Gamma_{\pm};|\beta\cdot n|)
are defined by

\|g\|_{L^{p}(\Gamma_{\pm};|\beta\cdot n|)}:=\left(\int_{\Gamma_{\pm}}|g|^{p}|\beta\cdot n|\,d\sigma_{x}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

where
d\sigma_{x}
is the surface measure on
\partial\Omega
. For a Banach space
X
, let
X^{\prime}
denote its dual space. Namely,
W^{q}_{\beta,+}(\Omega)^{\prime}
in the setting of the boundary value problem (
1.1
) is the dual space of
W^{q}_{\beta,+}(\Omega)
. Here,
W^{q}_{\beta,+}(\Omega)
is a subspace of
W^{q}_{\beta}(\Omega)
and its definition will be given later. We note that the dual space
X^{\prime}
is equipped with a norm
\|\cdot\|_{X^{\prime}}
defined by

\|f\|_{X^{\prime}}:=\sup_{u\in X\setminus\{0\}}\frac{|f(u)|}{\|u\|_{X}}.

It is worth mentioning that
C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
is dense in
W^{p}_{\beta}(\Omega)
for
1\leq p<\infty

[
17
]
. Hence,
\Lip(\overline{\Omega})
is also dense there, where
\Lip(\overline{\Omega})
is the function space consisting of Lipschitz functions on
\overline{\Omega}
.

The boundary value problem (
1.1
) with
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and
g=0
was investigated in the context of the semigroup theory
[
4
,
15
]
, where elliptic regularization was applied. In the context of the numerical computation, it was regarded as one of Friedrichs’ systems when
p=2
and
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)

[
13
]
. However, because the traces
v|_{\Gamma_{\pm}}
for a function
v\in W^{p}_{\beta}(\Omega)
may not belong to
L^{p}(\Gamma_{\pm};|\beta\cdot n|)
in general
[
4
]
, it is not straightforward to define boundary operators appearing in Friedrichs’ systems.

In order to avoid this difficulty, Ern and Guermond
[
13
]
introduced the separation condition, or the boundaries
\Gamma_{\pm}
are well-separated:

\dist(\Gamma_{+},\Gamma_{-}):=\inf_{x\in\Gamma_{+},x^{\prime}\in\Gamma_{-}}|x-x^{\prime}|>0.

Under the separation condition, the trace operators
\gamma_{\pm}:W^{p}_{\beta}(\Omega)\to L^{p}(\Gamma_{\pm};|\beta\cdot n|)
for
1\leq p<\infty
are bounded. More precisely, the operators
\gamma_{\pm}:W^{p}_{\beta}(\Omega)\to L^{p}(\Gamma_{\pm};|\beta\cdot n|)
with
\Dom(\gamma_{\pm})=\Lip(\overline{\Omega})
have bounded extensions. Based on this idea, well-posedness results of the boundary value problem (
1.1
) for general
p
with
1<p<\infty
have been established
[
5
,
8
,
19
]
. However, the separation condition does not seem natural from the view points of mathematical analysis on boundary value problems and its numerical computations. Actually, it was not assumed in
[
4
,
15
]
. This discrepancy motivates us to remove the condition. We note that the well-posedness of the boundary value problem (
1.1
) for
g\in L^{2}_{\loc}(\Gamma_{-};|\beta\cdot n|)
without the separation condition was mentioned in
[
12
, Example 2.12 (iii)]
without a proof or a reference.

In this article, we consider the trace operators in two ways. One way is that they are continuous operators from
W^{p}_{\beta}(\Omega)
to
L^{p}_{\loc}(\Gamma_{\pm};|\beta\cdot n|)
for
1\leq p<\infty
, which was mentioned in
[
2
,
4
,
15
]
and was introduced for the transport equation
[
9
]
. By taking closed subsets
\Gamma_{\pm}^{\prime}
in
\Gamma_{\pm}
, we can say that the trace operators
\tilde{\gamma}_{\pm}:W^{p}_{\beta}(\Omega)\to L^{p}(\Gamma_{\pm}^{\prime};|\beta\cdot n|)
with
\Dom(\tilde{\gamma}_{\pm})=\Lip(\overline{\Omega})
have bounded extensions. However, they do not have bounded extensions as operators from
W^{p}_{\beta}(\Omega)
to
L^{p}(\Gamma_{\pm};|\beta\cdot n|)
, which causes difficulty for discussing the well-posedness. The other way is that they are closed operators from
W^{p}_{\beta}(\Omega)
to
L^{p}(\Gamma_{\pm};|\beta\cdot n|)
with
\Dom(\gamma_{\pm})=W^{p}_{\beta,\tr}(\Omega)
, where

W^{p}_{\beta,\tr}(\Omega):=\{u\in W^{p}_{\beta}(\Omega)\mid\tilde{\gamma}_{\pm}u\in L^{p}(\Gamma_{\pm};|\beta\cdot n|)\}.

We introduce the graph norm
\|\cdot\|_{W^{p}_{\beta,\tr}(\Omega)}
on the space
W^{p}_{\beta,\tr}(\Omega)
;

\|u\|_{W^{p}_{\beta,\tr}(\Omega)}:=\left(\|u\|_{W^{p}_{\beta}(\Omega)}^{p}+\|\tilde{\gamma}_{+}u\|_{L^{p}(\Gamma_{+};\beta\cdot n)}^{p}+\|\tilde{\gamma}_{-}u\|_{L^{p}(\Gamma_{-};|\beta\cdot n|)}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Then, by Clarkson’s first and second inequalities, the space
W^{p}_{\beta,\tr}(\Omega)
with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{W^{p}_{\beta,\tr}(\Omega)}
is a reflexive Banach spaces for any
1<p<\infty
. We notice that
\gamma_{\pm}u=\tilde{\gamma}_{\pm}u
for all
u\in W^{p}_{\beta,\tr}(\Omega)
. Furthermore, we introduce subspaces
W^{p}_{\beta,\pm}(\Omega)
of
W^{p}_{\beta,\tr}(\Omega)
, which are defined by

W^{p}_{\beta,\pm}(\Omega):=\{u\in W^{p}_{\beta,\tr}(\Omega)\mid\tilde{\gamma}_{\pm}u=0\}.

In this article, we restrict ourselves to working on the space
W^{p}_{\beta,\tr}(\Omega)
rather than
W^{p}_{\beta}(\Omega)
. However, as was mentioned in
[
20
]
, it is not obvious whether Green’s formula, which is a key tool in our argument, holds there or not. In order to justify our analysis, we require that

W^{p}_{\beta,\tr}(\Omega)=\overline{\Lip(\overline{\Omega})}^{\|\cdot\|_{W^{p}_{\beta,\tr}(\Omega)}}

(1.2)

for all
1\leq p<\infty
. It is known that, if the
(d-2)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure of the boundary
\partial\Gamma_{-}
is finite, then
H^{1}_{\Gamma_{-}}(\Omega)
is dense in
W^{2}_{\beta,-}(\Omega)

[
4
]
, where

H^{1}_{\Gamma_{-}}(\Omega):=\{u\in H^{1}(\Omega)\mid u|_{\Gamma_{-}}=0\}.

With the same assumption, we can show that the density (
1.2
) holds for
1\leq p\leq 2
. However, it is not obvious whether it still holds for
2<p<\infty
. We shall give a sufficient condition for the relation (
1.2
) to hold for all
1\leq p<\infty
.

We first discuss well-posedness of the boundary value problem (
1.1
) in the weak sense under the assumption (
1.2
). A weak solution
u\in L^{p}(\Omega)
to the boundary value problem (
1.1
) is defined to be a function which satisfies

\int_{\Omega}u(-\nabla\cdot(\beta v)+\mu v)\,dx=\langle f,v\rangle_{W^{q}_{\beta,+}(\Omega)^{\prime},W^{q}_{\beta,+}(\Omega)}+\int_{\Gamma_{-}}gv|\beta\cdot n|\,d\sigma_{x}

(1.3)

for all
v\in W^{q}_{\beta,+}(\Omega)
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
1<p\leq\infty
and
q
be its Hölder conjugate. Assume (
1.2
) and

\mu_{p}:=\essinf_{x\in\Omega}\left(\mu(x)-\frac{1}{p}\divergence\beta(x)\right)>0.

(1.4)

Then, for any
f\in W^{q}_{\beta,+}(\Omega)^{\prime}
and
g\in L^{p}(\Gamma_{-};|\beta\cdot n|)
, there exists a unique weak solution
u
to the boundary value problem (
1.1
) in
L^{p}(\Omega)
. Moreover, we have

\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C_{1,p}\left(\|f\|_{W^{q}_{\beta,+}(\Omega)^{\prime}}+\|g\|_{L^{p}(\Gamma_{-};|\beta\cdot n|)}\right),

where

\displaystyle C_{1,p}:=

\displaystyle\left(1+C_{2,p}\right)\mu_{p}^{-1}\left(1+\mu_{p}+\|\beta\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}+\|\mu\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\right),

(1.5)

\displaystyle C_{2,p}:=

\displaystyle p^{\frac{1}{p}}+\|\beta\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{p}}.

(1.6)

We note that
\|\cdot\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{\pm};|\beta\cdot n|)}=\|\cdot\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{\pm})}
. Also, constants
C_{1,\infty}
in (
1.5
) and
C_{2,\infty}
are regarded as

C_{1,\infty}=3\mu_{\infty}^{-1}\left(1+\mu_{\infty}+\|\beta\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}+\|\mu\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\right)

(1.7)

and
C_{2,\infty}=2
respectively, which is obtained by taking the limit
p\to\infty
in (
1.5
) and (
1.6
) formally.

Remark 1
.

According to
[
8
]
, the assumption (
1.4
) can be replaced by the following one for
1<p<\infty
: there exists a Lipschitz function
\zeta
on
\overline{\Omega}
such that

\essinf_{x\in\Omega}e^{\zeta(x)}\left(\mu(x)-\frac{1}{p}\divergence\beta(x)-\frac{1}{p}\beta(x)\cdot\nabla\zeta(x)\right)>0.

The existence of the function
\zeta
is known for some
C^{1}
vector fields
[
10
]
. However, it is not obvious whether this assumption covers the case
p=\infty
.

We next discuss well-posedness of the boundary value problem (
1.1
) in the strong sense; a solution
u
in
W^{p}_{\beta,\tr}(\Omega)
.

Let
f\in L^{p}(\Omega)
in the boundary value problem (
1.1
). Then, we may regard it as a bounded linear functional on
W^{q}_{\beta,+}(\Omega)
by

\langle f,v\rangle_{W^{q}_{\beta,+}(\Omega)^{\prime},W^{q}_{\beta,+}(\Omega)}:=\int_{\Omega}fv\,dx,\quad v\in W^{q}_{\beta,+}(\Omega).

Thus, by Theorem
1.1
, there exists the unique weak solution
u\in L^{p}(\Omega)
to the boundary value problem (
1.1
). Furthermore, we have the following regularity result.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
, and assume (
1.2
) and (
1.4
). Then, for any
f\in L^{p}(\Omega)
and
g\in L^{p}(\Gamma_{-};|\beta\cdot n|)
, there exists a unique strong solution
u
to the boundary value problem (
1.1
) in
W^{p}_{\beta,\tr}(\Omega)
. Moreover, we have

\|u\|_{W^{p}_{\beta,\tr}(\Omega)}\leq C_{1,p}^{\prime}\left(\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|g\|_{L^{p}(\Gamma_{-};|\beta\cdot n|)}\right),

where
C_{1,p}^{\prime}:=(1+C_{2,p})\{2+\left(1+\|\mu\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\right)C_{1,p}\}
, and
C_{1,p}
is the constant defined by (
1.5
).

We remark that the traces of a function
u\in W^{\infty}_{\beta}(\Omega)
on
L^{\infty}_{\loc}(\Gamma_{\pm})
are not defined due to the lack of density of
\Lip(\overline{\Omega})
. Thus, we did not define the space
W^{\infty}_{\beta,\tr}(\Omega)
. However, since
L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{\pm})\subset L^{p}(\Gamma_{\pm};|\beta\cdot n|)
and
W^{\infty}_{\beta}(\Omega)\subset W^{p}_{\beta}(\Omega)
for all
1<p<\infty
, we may consider the local
L^{p}
traces of
u\in W^{\infty}_{\beta}(\Omega)
. Theorem
1.2
for
p=\infty
means that, if
f\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and
g\in L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{-})
, then the boundary value problem (
1.1
) eventually has the unique solution
u\in W^{\infty}_{\beta}(\Omega)
for
1\leq p<\infty
. In that case, the norm
\|\cdot\|_{W^{\infty}_{\beta.\tr}(\Omega)}
of the function space
W^{\infty}_{\beta,\tr}(\Omega)
is defined by

\|u\|_{W^{\infty}_{\beta.\tr}(\Omega)}:=\max\left\{\|u\|_{W^{\infty}_{\beta}(\Omega)},\|\tilde{\gamma}_{+}u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{+})},\|\tilde{\gamma}_{-}u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{-})}\|\right\}.

Unlike Theorem
1.1
, the case
p=1
is included in Theorem
1.2
. Also, as a byproduct, Theorem
1.2
implies the surjectivity of the trace operators
\gamma_{\pm}:W^{p}_{\beta,\tr}(\Omega)\to L^{p}(\Gamma_{\pm};|\beta\cdot n|)
.

As corollaries, we obtain well-posedness results for the adjoint boundary value problem:

\begin{cases}-\nabla\cdot(\beta u)+\mu u=f&\mbox{ in }\Omega,\\
u=g&\mbox{ on }\Gamma_{+},\end{cases}

(1.8)

where
f\in W^{q}_{\beta,-}(\Omega)^{\prime}
and
g\in L^{p}(\Gamma_{+};\beta\cdot n)
.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
1<p\leq\infty
and
q
be its Hölder conjugate. Assume (
1.2
) and

\mu_{q}>0,

(1.9)

where
\mu_{q}
is the constant defined by (

) with

replaced by

. Then, for any

\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq\tilde{C}_{1,q}\left(\|f\|_{W^{q}_{\beta,+}(\Omega)^{\prime}}+\|g\|_{L^{p}(\Gamma_{+};|\beta\cdot n|)}\right),

where

\tilde{C}_{1,q}:=\left(1+C_{2,q}\right)\mu_{q}^{-1}\left(1+\mu_{q}+\|\mu\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\right).

(1.10)

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
and

) and (

and

2
Preliminary

In this section, we give precise definitions of
\Gamma_{\pm}
and
\Gamma_{0}
. We also describe properties of the trace operators. In addition, we introduce the Banach-Nečas-Babuška theorem, which plays the key role in our proofs.

We give definitions of
\Gamma_{\pm}
and
\Gamma_{0}
, which are a modification of those in
[
1
]
.

Since
\beta\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)^{d}
, we regard it as a Lipschitz function on
\overline{\Omega}
. Moreover, since the domain
\Omega
is bounded and the boundary is Lipschitz, there exists a Lipschitz function
\tilde{\beta}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
\tilde{\beta}(x)=\beta(x)
for all
x\in\overline{\Omega}
. For the existence of such an extension, see
[
18
,
23
]
for example.

We take
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
and consider the following Cauchy problem:

\begin{cases}\dfrac{d}{dt}x(t)=\tilde{\beta}(x(t)),\\
x(0)=x_{0}.\end{cases}

(2.1)

Since
\tilde{\beta}
is Lipschitz, the Cauchy problem (
2.1
) has a unique local solution. Let
x(t)
be the unique solution. We decompose the boundary
\partial\Omega
into three parts:

\displaystyle\Gamma_{+}

\displaystyle:=\{x_{0}\in\partial\Omega\mid\mbox{There exists }T_{+}>0\mbox{ such that }x(t)\notin\overline{\Omega}\mbox{ for all }0<t<T_{+}.\},

\displaystyle\Gamma_{-}

\displaystyle:=\{x_{0}\in\partial\Omega\mid\mbox{There exists }T_{-}>0\mbox{ such that }x(t)\in\Omega\mbox{ for all }0<t<T_{-}.\},

\displaystyle\Gamma_{0}

\displaystyle:=\partial\Omega\setminus(\Gamma_{+}\cup\Gamma_{-}).

We call these subsets the outflow boundary, the inflow boundary, and the characteristic boundary respectively. By considering the normal component of the dynamics (
2.1
), we see that
\pm\beta(x_{0})\cdot n(x_{0})>0
for a.e.
x_{0}\in\Gamma_{\pm}
, where
n(x_{0})
is the outward unit normal vector at
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
. Also, due to continuous dependence of the solution to the Cauchy problem on initial values and due to the assumption that
\Omega
and
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
are open, we see that
\Gamma_{\pm}
are open. Therefore, our definitions of
\Gamma_{\pm}
and
\Gamma_{0}
are independent of choice of the extension
\tilde{\beta}
.

We proceed to discuss properties of the trace operators. We start from the following estimate.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
. Then, we have

\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Gamma_{\pm};|\beta\cdot n|)}\leq C_{2,p}\|u\|_{W^{p}_{\beta}(\Omega)}+\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Gamma_{\mp};|\beta\cdot n|)}

(2.2)

for all
u\in\Lip(\overline{\Omega})
, where
C_{2,p}
is the constant defined by (
1.6
).

Proof.

By Green’s formula, we have

\int_{\Omega}(\beta\cdot\nabla u)|u|^{p-2}u\,dx=\frac{1}{p}\int_{\Gamma_{+}}|u|^{p}\beta\cdot n\,d\sigma_{x}-\frac{1}{p}\int_{\Gamma_{-}}|u|^{p}|\beta\cdot n|\,d\sigma_{x}-\frac{1}{p}\int_{\Omega}(\mbox{div}\,\beta)|u|^{p}\,dx

for all
u\in\Lip(\overline{\Omega})
. Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{\pm}}|u|^{p}|\beta\cdot n|\,d\sigma_{x}\leq p\int_{\Omega}|\beta\cdot\nabla u||u|^{p-1}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}|\mbox{div}\,\beta||u|^{p}\,dx+\int_{\Gamma_{\mp}}|u|^{p}|\beta\cdot n|\,d\sigma_{x}.

For the first term of the right hand side, by the Hölder inequality and Young’s inequality, we have

p\int_{\Omega}|\beta\cdot\nabla u||u|^{p-1}\,dx\leq p\|\beta\cdot\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p-1}\leq\|\beta\cdot\nabla u\|^{p}+(p-1)\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p-1}.

For the second term of the right hand side, we get

\int_{\Omega}|\mbox{div}\,\beta||u|^{p}\,dx\leq\|\beta\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}.

Thus, we have

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Gamma_{\pm};|\beta\cdot n|)}^{p}\leq

\displaystyle\|\beta\cdot\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}+(p-1)\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}+\|\beta\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}+\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Gamma_{\mp};|\beta\cdot n|)}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(p+\|\beta\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}\right)\|u\|_{W^{p}_{\beta}(\Omega)}^{p}+\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Gamma_{\mp};|\beta\cdot n|)}^{p},

or

\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Gamma_{\pm};|\beta\cdot n|)}\leq C_{2,p}\|u\|_{W^{p}_{\beta}(\Omega)}+\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Gamma_{\mp};|\beta\cdot n|)}.

Therefore, Lemma
2.1
is proved.
∎

As a corollary of Lemma
2.1
, we obtain the following estimate.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
. Also, let
\Gamma_{\pm}^{\prime}
be closed subsets of
\Gamma_{\pm}
such that
\dist(\Gamma_{\pm},\Gamma_{\mp}^{\prime})>0
respectively. Then, there exists a constant
C
depending on
\Gamma_{\pm}^{\prime}
,
\beta
and
p
such that

\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Gamma_{\pm}^{\prime};|\beta\cdot n|)}\leq C\|u\|_{W^{p}_{\beta}(\Omega)}

for all
u\in\Lip(\overline{\Omega})
.

Proof.

We only discuss the local trace on
\Gamma_{-}
because we can discuss that on
\Gamma_{+}
in the same way.

Let
\Gamma_{-}^{\prime}
be a closed subset of
\Gamma_{-}
such that
\dist(\Gamma_{+},\Gamma_{-}^{\prime})>0
, and let
\psi^{-}
be a function in
\Lip(\overline{\Omega})
such that
0\leq\psi^{-}\leq 1
on
\overline{\Omega}
,
\psi^{-}=0
on
\Gamma_{+}
, and
\psi^{-}=1
on
\Gamma_{-}^{\prime}
. Also, let
u\in\Lip(\overline{\Omega})
. We apply Lemma
2.1
to
\psi^{-}u\in\Lip(\overline{\Omega})
to obtain

\|\psi^{-}u\|_{L^{p}(\Gamma_{-};|\beta\cdot n|)}\leq C_{2,p}\|\psi^{-}u\|_{W^{p}_{\beta}(\Omega)},

where
C_{2,p}
is the constant defined in (
1.6
). By the definition of
\psi^{-}
, we have
\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Gamma_{-}^{\prime};|\beta\cdot n|)}\leq\|\psi^{-}u\|_{L^{p}(\Gamma_{-};|\beta\cdot n|)}
and
\|\psi^{-}u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}
. Also, we have

\|\beta\cdot\nabla(\psi^{-}u)\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq\|\beta\cdot\nabla\psi^{-}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|\beta\cdot\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}.

Hence, we obtain

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Gamma_{-}^{\prime};|\beta\cdot n|)}\leq C_{2,p}(1+\|\beta\cdot\nabla\psi^{-}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)})\|u\|_{W^{p}_{\beta}(\Omega)}

for all
u\in\Lip(\overline{\Omega})
. This completes the proof.
∎

Proof.

Proposition 2.7

Let

1.

, there exists a unique

2.

The following two conditions hold:

\displaystyle\{w\in W\mid a(v,w)=0\mbox{ for all }v\in V\}=\{0\}.

The following two conditions hold:

\displaystyle c_{1}:=\inf_{v\in V\setminus\{0\}}\sup_{w\in W\setminus\{0\}}\frac{a(v,w)}{\|v\|_{V}\|w\|_{W}}>0,

\displaystyle c_{2}:=\inf_{w\in W\setminus\{0\}}\sup_{v\in V\setminus\{0\}}\frac{a(v,w)}{\|v\|_{V}\|w\|_{W}}>0.

Moreover, the estimate
\|u\|_{V}\leq c_{1}^{-1}\|F\|_{W^{\prime}}
holds for
u
in the condition 1.

In fact, if the condition 3 holds, then

3
L^{p}
well-posedness for
1<p\leq\infty

In this section, we give a proof of Theorem
1.1
. Namely, we show well-posedness of the boundary value problem (
1.1
) in
L^{p}(\Omega)
for
1<p\leq\infty
.

We first consider the case
1<p<\infty
. Let
q
be the Hölder conjugate of
p
. We consider the adjoint boundary value problem:

\begin{cases}-\nabla\cdot(\beta u)+\mu u=f&\mbox{ in }\Omega,\\
u=0&\mbox{ on }\Gamma_{+},\end{cases}

(3.1)

where
f\in L^{q}(\Omega)
. If the surface measure of
\Gamma_{+}
is
0
, then we ignore the boundary condition in (
3.1
).

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and assume (
1.4
). Then, the boundary value problem (
3.1
) has a unique strong solution in
W^{q}_{\beta,+}(\Omega)
. Moreover, we have

\|u\|_{W^{q}_{\beta}(\Omega)}\leq C_{1,p}\|f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}

for all
f\in L^{q}(\Omega)
, where
u
is the solution to (
3.1
) and
C_{1,p}
is the constant defined by (
1.5
).

Proof.

We define the operator
L^{\prime}:W^{q}_{\beta,+}(\Omega)\to L^{q}(\Omega)
by

L^{\prime}u:=-\nabla\cdot(\beta u)+\mu u.

It is worth mentioning that

\displaystyle\|L^{\prime}u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\leq

\displaystyle\|(\mbox{div}\,\beta)u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}+\|\beta\cdot\nabla u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}+\|\mu u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(1+\|\beta\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}+\|\mu\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\right)\|u\|_{W^{q}_{\beta}(\Omega)},

or the operator
L^{\prime}
is bounded.

We define a bilinear form
B^{\prime}
on
W^{q}_{\beta,+}(\Omega)\times L^{p}(\Omega)
by

B^{\prime}(u,v):=\int_{\Omega}(L^{\prime}u)v\,dx,\quad u\in W^{q}_{\beta,+}(\Omega),v\in L^{p}(\Omega).

By the Hölder inequality, we have

\displaystyle|B^{\prime}(u,v)|\leq\|L^{\prime}u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\|v\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq\left(1+\|\beta\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}+\|\mu\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\right)\|u\|_{W^{q}_{\beta}(\Omega)}\|v\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)},

and thus the bilinear form
B^{\prime}
is also bounded.

We also define a functional
F_{0}:L^{p}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
by

F_{0}(v):=\langle f,v\rangle_{L^{q}(\Omega),L^{p}(\Omega)}=\int_{\Omega}fv\,dx,\quad v\in L^{p}(\Omega).

By the Hölder inequality, it is bounded. Thus, the functional
F_{0}
belongs to
L^{p}(\Omega)^{\prime}
.

We apply Proposition
2.7
with
V=W^{q}_{\beta,+}(\Omega)
and
W=L^{p}(\Omega)
in order to show the existence of a function
u\in W^{q}_{\beta,+}(\Omega)
such that

B^{\prime}(u,v)=F_{0}(v)

for all
v\in L^{p}(\Omega)
. This function
u
is the solution to the boundary value problem (
3.1
).

To this aim, we first show that

\inf_{u\in W^{q}_{\beta,+}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}}\sup_{v\in L^{p}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}}\frac{B^{\prime}(u,v)}{\|u\|_{W^{q}_{\beta}(\Omega)}\|v\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}}\geq C_{1,p}^{-1}.

(3.2)

For
u\in W^{q}_{\beta,+}(\Omega)
, let
v:=|u|^{q-2}u
. We notice that

\int_{\Omega}|v|^{p}\,dx=\int_{\Omega}|u|^{q}\,dx,

or
\|v\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=\|u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{q-1}
since
p=q/(q-1)
. Moreover, Green’s formula yields

\displaystyle B^{\prime}(u,v)=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(-\nabla\cdot(\beta u)+\mu u)|u|^{q-2}u\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(\mu-\frac{1}{p}\mbox{div}\,\beta\right)|u|^{q}\,dx+\frac{1}{q}\int_{\Gamma_{-}}|u|^{q}|\beta\cdot n|\,d\sigma_{x}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\mu_{p}\|u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{q}.

Thus, we have

\|u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{q}\leq\mu_{p}^{-1}B^{\prime}(u,v)\leq\mu_{p}^{-1}\|u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{q-1}\sup_{v\in L^{p}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}}\frac{B^{\prime}(u,v)}{\|v\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}},

or

\|u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\leq\mu_{p}^{-1}\sup_{v\in L^{p}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}}\frac{B^{\prime}(u,v)}{\|v\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}}.

For the derivative term, we have

\displaystyle\|\beta\cdot\nabla u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}=

\displaystyle\sup_{v\in L^{p}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\langle\beta\cdot\nabla u,v\rangle_{L^{q}(\Omega),L^{p}(\Omega)}}{\|v\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{v\in L^{p}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}}\frac{-B^{\prime}(u,v)-\langle(\mbox{div}\,\beta)u,v\rangle_{L^{q}(\Omega),L^{p}(\Omega)}+\langle\mu u,v\rangle_{L^{q}(\Omega),L^{p}(\Omega)}}{\|v\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(1+\mu_{p}^{-1}(\|\beta\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}+\|\mu\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)})\right)\sup_{v\in L^{p}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}}\frac{B^{\prime}(u,v)}{\|v\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}}.

Therefore, by Corollary
2.5
, we obtain

\|u\|_{W^{q}_{\beta,\tr}(\Omega)}\leq(1+C_{2,p})\|u\|_{W^{q}_{\beta}(\Omega)}\leq C_{1,p}\sup_{v\in L^{p}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}}\frac{B^{\prime}(u,v)}{\|v\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}},

which implies the estimate (
3.2
).

We next show that that
B^{\prime}(u,v)=0
for all
u\in W^{q}_{\beta,+}(\Omega)
implies that
v=0
. Since
W^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega)\subset W^{q}_{\beta,+}(\Omega)
, this assumption implies that the function
v
satisfies
\beta\cdot\nabla v+\mu v=0
in
W^{-1,p}(\Omega)
. Thus, we have
\beta\cdot\nabla v=-\mu v
and the directional derivative
\beta\cdot\nabla v
belongs to
L^{p}(\Omega)
. In other words, we have
v\in W^{p}_{\beta}(\Omega)
. This fact allows us to take the trace
\tilde{\gamma}_{-}v
in
L^{p}_{\loc}(\Gamma_{-};|\beta\cdot n|)
.

We take a closed subset
\Gamma_{-}^{\prime}\subset\Gamma_{-}
such that
\dist(\Gamma_{+},\Gamma_{-}^{\prime})>0
and take
u\in W^{q}_{\beta,+}(\Omega)
such that
\supp(\gamma_{-}u)\subset\Gamma_{-}^{\prime}
. Then, we may do integration by parts to obtain

\langle L^{\prime}u,v\rangle_{L^{q}(\Omega),L^{p}(\Omega)}=\langle u,Lv\rangle_{L^{q}(\Omega),L^{p}(\Omega)}+\int_{\Gamma_{-}^{\prime}}uv|\beta\cdot n|\,d\sigma_{x},

where
Lv:=\beta\cdot\nabla v+\mu v
. Since
Lv=0
, the above formula implies

\int_{\Gamma_{-}^{\prime}}uv|\beta\cdot n|\,d\sigma_{x}=0,

and, by Proposition
2.6
with Remark
3
, we see that
\tilde{\gamma}_{-}v=0
, which implies that
v\in W^{p}_{\beta,-}(\Omega)
.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(\beta\cdot\nabla v+\mu v)|v|^{p-2}v\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(\mu-\frac{1}{p}\mbox{div}\,\beta\right)|v|^{p}\,dx+\frac{1}{p}\int_{\Gamma_{+}}|v|^{q}\beta\cdot n\,d\sigma_{x}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\mu_{p}\|v\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p},

which means that
v=0
.

The conclusion follows from Proposition
2.7
and the fact that
\|F_{0}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)^{\prime}}=\|f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}
.
∎

We are ready to prove Theorem
1.1
for
1<p<\infty
. Corresponding to the equation (
1.3
), we let

F(v):=\langle f,v\rangle_{W^{q}_{\beta,+}(\Omega)^{\prime},W^{q}_{\beta,+}(\Omega)}+\int_{\Gamma_{-}}gv|\beta\cdot n|\,d\sigma_{x},\quad v\in W^{q}_{\beta,+}(\Omega).

Since

\left|\langle f,v\rangle_{W^{q}_{\beta,+}(\Omega)^{\prime},W^{q}_{\beta,+}(\Omega)}\right|\leq\|f\|_{W^{q}_{\beta,+}(\Omega)^{\prime}}\|v\|_{W^{q}_{\beta,\tr}(\Omega)}

and

\left|\int_{\Gamma_{-}}gv|\beta\cdot n|\,d\sigma_{x}\right|\leq\|g\|_{L^{p}(\Gamma_{-};|\beta\cdot n|)}\|v\|_{L^{q}(\Gamma_{-};|\beta\cdot n|)}\leq\|g\|_{L^{p}(\Gamma_{-};|\beta\cdot n|)}\|v\|_{W^{q}_{\beta,\tr}(\Omega)},

we have
F\in W^{q}_{\beta,+}(\Omega)^{\prime}
and

\|F\|_{W^{p}_{\beta,+}(\Omega)^{\prime}}\leq\|f\|_{W^{q}_{\beta,+}(\Omega)^{\prime}}+\|g\|_{L^{p}(\Gamma_{-};|\beta\cdot n|)}.

We define the operator
\overline{L}:L^{p}(\Omega)\to W^{q}_{\beta,+}(\Omega)^{\prime}
by

\langle\overline{L}u,v\rangle_{W^{q}_{\beta,+}(\Omega)^{\prime},W^{q}_{\beta,+}(\Omega)}:=\langle u,L^{\prime}v\rangle_{L^{p}(\Omega),L^{q}(\Omega)},

and define the bilinear form
\overline{B}
on
L^{p}(\Omega)\times W^{q}_{\beta,+}(\Omega)
by

\overline{B}(u,v):=\langle\overline{L}u,v\rangle_{W^{q}_{\beta,+}(\Omega)^{\prime},W^{q}_{\beta,+}(\Omega)}.

It is worth mentioning that
\overline{B}(u,v)=B^{\prime}(v,u)
for all
u\in L^{p}(\Omega)
and
v\in W^{q}_{\beta,+}(\Omega)
. The equation (
1.3
) is rewritten as

\overline{B}(u,v)=F(v)

for all
v\in W^{q}_{\beta,+}(\Omega)
.

Lemma
3.1
and Proposition
2.7
with Remark
4
imply that

\inf_{v\in W^{q}_{\beta,+}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}}\sup_{u\in L^{p}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\overline{B}(u,v)}{\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\|v\|_{W^{q}_{\beta}(\Omega)}}\geq C_{1,p}^{-1}

and

\inf_{u\in L^{p}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}}\sup_{v\in W^{q}_{\beta,+}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\overline{B}(u,v)}{\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\|v\|_{W^{q}_{\beta}(\Omega)}}\geq C_{1,p}^{-1}.

We apply Proposition
2.7
again in order to conclude that

\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C_{1,p}\|F\|_{W^{p}_{\beta,+}(\Omega)^{\prime}}\leq C_{1,p}\left(\|f\|_{W^{q}_{\beta,+}(\Omega)^{\prime}}+\|g\|_{L^{p}(\Gamma_{-};|\beta\cdot n|)}\right).

This completes the proof for
1<p<\infty
.

We proceed to the case
p=\infty
. We note that the same approach is seen in
[
22
]
. Let
p_{\infty}:=(2\|\beta\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)})/\mu_{\infty}
. Then, we get

\mu_{p}\geq\mu_{\infty}-\frac{1}{p_{\infty}}\|\beta\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}=\frac{\mu_{\infty}}{2}>0

for all
p>p_{\infty}
. Also, since

\mu_{\infty}-\frac{1}{p}\|\beta\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}\leq\mu_{p}\leq\mu_{\infty}+\frac{1}{p}\|\beta\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)},

we have

\displaystyle C_{1,p}\leq

\displaystyle\left(1+p^{\frac{1}{p}}+\|\beta\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{p}}\right)\left(\mu_{\infty}-\frac{1}{p}\|\beta\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle\times\left(1+\mu_{\infty}+\frac{1}{p}\|\beta\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}+\|\mu\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\right).

for all
p>p_{\infty}
. Thus, we obtain

\limsup_{p\to\infty}C_{1,p}\leq C_{1,\infty},

where
C_{1,\infty}
is the constant defined in (
1.7
).

Let
f\in W^{1}_{\beta,+}(\Omega)^{\prime}
. Since
W^{q}_{\beta,+}(\Omega)\subset W^{1}_{\beta,+}(\Omega)
for all
q>1
, we can define
f_{q}\in W^{q}_{\beta,+}(\Omega)^{\prime}
by

\langle f_{q},v\rangle_{W^{q}_{\beta,+}(\Omega)^{\prime},W^{q}_{\beta,+}(\Omega)}:=\langle f,v\rangle_{W^{1}_{\beta,+}(\Omega)^{\prime},W^{1}_{\beta,+}(\Omega)},\quad v\in W^{q}_{\beta,+}(\Omega).

Then, we have

\displaystyle|\langle f_{q},v\rangle_{W^{q}_{\beta,+}(\Omega)^{\prime},W^{q}_{\beta,+}(\Omega)}|\leq

\displaystyle\|f\|_{W^{1}_{\beta,+}(\Omega)^{\prime}}\|v\|_{W^{1}_{\beta,\tr}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{p}}+\|\beta\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{p}}|\Gamma_{-}|^{\frac{1}{p}}\right)\|f\|_{W^{1}_{\beta,+}(\Omega)^{\prime}}\|v\|_{W^{q}_{\beta,+}(\Omega)},

and hence, we obtain

\|f_{q}\|_{W^{1}_{\beta,+}(\Omega)^{\prime}}\leq\left(|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{p}}+\|\beta\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{p}}|\Gamma_{-}|^{\frac{1}{p}}\right)\|f\|_{W^{1}_{\beta,+}(\Omega)^{\prime}}.

Since
g\in L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{-};|\beta\cdot n|)
, we have

\|g\|_{L^{p}(\Gamma_{-};|\beta\cdot n|)}\leq\|\beta\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{p}}|\Gamma_{-}|^{\frac{1}{p}}\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{-};|\beta\cdot n|)}.

Regarding
f=f_{q}
for
q>1
and applying Theorem
1.1
for
p_{\infty}<p<\infty
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq

\displaystyle C_{1,p}\left(|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{p}}\|f\|_{W^{1}_{\beta,+}(\Omega)^{\prime}}+\|\beta\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{p}}|\Gamma_{-}|^{\frac{1}{p}}\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{-})}\right)

for all
p>p_{\infty}
. Taking
p\to\infty
, we have

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C_{1,\infty}\left(\|f\|_{W^{1}_{\beta,+}(\Omega)^{\prime}}+\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{-})}\right).

This completes the proof for
p=\infty
.

Therefore, Theorem
1.1
is proved.

4
W^{1,p}
well-posedness for
1\leq p\leq\infty

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.2
. In other words, we discuss well-posedness of the boundary value problem (
1.1
) in
W^{p}_{\beta}(\Omega)
for
1\leq p\leq\infty
.

We first discuss the case
1<p<\infty
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and assume (
1.4
). Then, for any
f\in L^{p}(\Omega)
and
g\in L^{p}(\Gamma_{-};|\beta\cdot n|)
, the weak solution
u\in L^{p}(\Omega)
to the boundary value problem (
1.1
) belongs to
W^{p}_{\beta}(\Omega)
. Moreover, we have
u|_{\Gamma_{-}}=g
and

\|u\|_{W^{p}_{\beta}(\Omega)}\leq\{1+\left(1+\|\mu\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\right)C_{1,p}\}\left(\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|g\|_{L^{p}(\Gamma_{-};|\beta\cdot n|)}\right),

where
C_{1,p}
is the constant defined by (
1.5
).

Proof.

Let
u\in L^{p}(\Omega)
be the solution to the equation (
1.3
). As in the proof of Lemma
3.1
, we can say that

\beta\cdot\nabla u=f-\mu u\in L^{p}(\Omega).

(4.1)

Thus, the solution
u
belongs to
W^{p}_{\beta}(\Omega)
. Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\|\beta\cdot\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|\mu u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|\mu\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}.

Thus, by Theorem
1.1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|u\|_{W^{p}_{\beta}(\Omega)}\leq

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+(1+\|\mu\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)})\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\{1+\left(1+\|\mu\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\right)C_{1,p}\}\left(\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|g\|_{L^{p}(\Gamma_{-};|\beta\cdot n|)}\right).

We check that the function
u
has the trace on
\Gamma_{-}
and it is equal to the boundary data
g
. Let
\Gamma_{-}^{\prime}
be a closed subset of
\Gamma_{-}
such that
\dist(\Gamma_{+},\Gamma_{-}^{\prime})>0
, and let
v
be a function in
\Lip_{\Gamma_{+}}(\Omega)
such that
\supp v|_{\Gamma_{-}}\subset\Gamma_{-}^{\prime}
. Then, integration by parts yields

\int_{\Omega}u\left(-\nabla\cdot(\beta v)\right)\,dx=\int_{\Gamma_{-}^{\prime}}uv|\beta\cdot n|\,d\sigma_{x}+\int_{\Omega}(\beta\cdot u)v\,dx.

Applying the above identity to the equation (
1.3
), thanks to (
4.1
), we have

\int_{\Gamma_{-}^{\prime}}uv|\beta\cdot n|\,d\sigma_{x}=\int_{\Gamma_{-}^{\prime}}gv|\beta\cdot n|\,d\sigma_{x}

for all
v\in\Lip_{\Gamma_{+}}(\Omega)
such that
\supp v|_{\Gamma_{-}}\subset\Gamma_{-}^{\prime}
. By Proposition
2.6
, we conclude that

\tilde{\gamma}_{-}u=g\in L^{p}(\Gamma_{-};|\beta\cdot n|).

This completes the proof.
∎

We derive the estimate in Theorem
1.2
. Let
u\in W^{p}_{\beta,\tr}(\Omega)
be the strong solution to the boundary value problem (
1.1
). By Lemma
2.1
, we see that

\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Gamma_{+};\beta\cdot n)}\leq C_{2,p}\|u\|_{W^{p}_{\beta}(\Omega)}+\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Gamma_{-};|\beta\cdot n|)}.

Hence, we have

\displaystyle\|u\|_{W^{p}_{\beta,\tr}(\Omega)}\leq

\displaystyle(1+C_{2,p})\|u\|_{W^{p}_{\beta}(\Omega)}+\|g\|_{L^{p}(\Gamma_{-};|\beta\cdot n|)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{1,p}^{\prime}\left(\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|g\|_{L^{p}(\Gamma_{-};|\beta\cdot n|)}\right),

which is the estimate in Theorem
1.2
.

We can treat the case
p=\infty
in the same way as the proof of the
L^{\infty}
well-posedness in Section
3
. In what follows, we discuss the case
p=1
.

We first show existence of a solution in
W^{1}_{\beta}(\Omega)
. For
f\in L^{1}(\Omega)
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
, let
f_{N}:=\max\{-N,\min\{N,f\}\}
, namely,

f_{N}(x)=\begin{cases}f(x),&|f(x)|\leq N,\\
N,&f(x)>N,\\
-N,&f(x)<-N.\end{cases}

In the same way, for
g\in L^{1}(\Gamma_{-};|\beta\cdot n|)
, let
g_{N}:=\max\{-N,\min\{N,g\}\}
. It is worth mentioning that
f_{N}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and
g_{N}\in L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{-})
for each
N\in\mathbb{N}
, and that

\|f-f_{N}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\to 0,\quad\|g-g_{N}\|_{L^{1}(\Gamma_{-};|\beta\cdot n|)}\to 0

as
N\to\infty
by the dominated convergence theorem.

Assume (
1.4
) with
p=1
and let

p_{1}:=1+\frac{\mu_{1}}{2\|\beta\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}}.

Then, we have
p_{1}>1
and

\mu_{p}\geq\mu_{1}-(p_{1}-1)\|\beta\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}=\frac{\mu_{1}}{2}>0

for all
1<p<p_{1}
. Since

\mu_{1}-\frac{p-1}{p}\|\beta\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}\leq\mu_{p}\leq\mu_{1}+\frac{p-1}{p}\|\beta\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)},

we have

\displaystyle C_{1,p}\leq

\displaystyle\left(1+p^{\frac{1}{p}}+\|\beta\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{p}}\right)\left(\mu_{1}-\frac{p-1}{p}\|\beta\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle\times\left(1+\mu_{1}+\frac{p-1}{p}\|\beta\|_{W^{1,\infty(\Omega)}}+\|\mu\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\right)

for all
1<p<p_{1}
. Thus, we obtain

\limsup_{p\to 1}C_{1,p}\leq C_{1,1},

where

C_{1,1}:=\left(2+\|\beta\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}\right)\mu_{1}^{-1}\left(1+\mu_{1}+\|\beta\|_{W^{1,\infty(\Omega)}}+\|\mu\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\right).

Since

\|f_{N}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq N^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\|f_{N}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{p}},\quad\|g_{N}\|_{L^{p}(\Gamma_{-};|\beta\cdot n|)}\leq N^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\|g_{N}\|_{L^{1}(\Gamma_{-};|\beta\cdot n|)}^{\frac{1}{p}},

we have

\displaystyle\|u_{N}\|_{W_{\beta}^{p}(\Omega)}\leq

\displaystyle\left(pC_{1,1}+(p-1)C_{1,1}^{\prime\prime}\right)N^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\left(\|f_{N}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{p}}+\|g_{N}\|_{L^{1}(\Gamma_{-};|\beta\cdot n|)}^{\frac{1}{p}}\right)

for all
1<p<p_{1}
, where
u_{N}
is the solution to the boundary value problem (
1.1
) with
f=f_{N}
and
g=g_{N}
. Taking
p\to 1
, we get

\|u_{N}\|_{W_{\beta}^{1}(\Omega)}\leq C_{1,1}(\|f_{N}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}+\|g_{N}\|_{L^{1}(\Gamma_{-};|\beta\cdot n|)}).

(4.2)

Since
\{f_{N}\}
and
\{g_{N}\}
are Cauchy sequences in
L^{1}(\Omega)
and
L^{1}(\Gamma_{-};|\beta\cdot n|)
respectively, the estimate (
4.2
) implies that
\{u_{N}\}
is a Cauchy sequence in
W^{1}_{\beta}(\Omega)
. Thus, there exists a function
u\in W^{1}_{\beta}(\Omega)
such that
u_{N}\to u
in
W^{1}_{\beta}(\Omega)
as
N\to\infty
.

As the next step, we prove that the function
u
is a solution to the boundary value problem (
1.1
). Since
u_{N}
satisfies
\beta\cdot\nabla u_{N}+\mu u_{N}=f_{N}
, we have

\displaystyle\|\beta\cdot\nabla u+\mu u-f\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\leq

\displaystyle\|\beta\cdot\nabla(u-u_{N})\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}+\|\mu(u-u_{N})\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}+\|f-f_{N}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(1+\|\mu\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\right)\|u-u_{N}\|_{W^{1}_{\beta}(\Omega)}+\|f-f_{N}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}

for all
N\in\mathbb{N}
. Thus, by taking
N\to\infty
, we conclude that
\beta\cdot\nabla u+\mu u-f=0
.

Now we take a closed subset
\Gamma_{-}^{\prime}\subset\Gamma_{-}
such that
\dist(\Gamma_{+},\Gamma_{-}^{\prime})>0
and take
\chi\in\Lip(\overline{\Omega})
such that
\supp\chi|_{\partial\Omega}\subset\Gamma_{-}^{\prime}
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Gamma_{-}^{\prime}}\chi(u-g)\beta\cdot n\,d\sigma_{x}\right|\leq

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Gamma_{-}^{\prime}}\chi(u-u_{N})\beta\cdot n\,d\sigma_{x}\right|+\left|\int_{\Gamma_{-}^{\prime}}\chi(g_{N}-g)\beta\cdot n\,d\sigma_{x}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega}\chi\beta\cdot\nabla(u-u_{N})\,dx\right|+\left|\int_{\Omega}(\mbox{div}\,(\chi\beta))(u-u_{N})\,dx\right|

\displaystyle+\left|\int_{\Gamma_{-}^{\prime}}\chi(g_{N}-g)\beta\cdot n\,d\sigma_{x}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\|\chi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\|\beta\cdot\nabla\chi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\|\chi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|\beta\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}\right)

\displaystyle\times\|u-u_{N}\|_{W^{1}_{\beta}(\Omega)}+\|\chi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|g-g_{N}\|_{L^{1}(\Gamma_{-};|\beta\cdot n|)}

for all
N\in\mathbb{N}
. Thus, by taking
N\to\infty
, we have

\int_{\Gamma_{-}^{\prime}}\chi(u-g)\beta\cdot n\,d\sigma_{x}=0.

Since
\chi
and
\Gamma_{-}^{\prime}
are arbitrary, we conclude that
u=g
a.e. on
\Gamma_{-}
. Therefore, the function
u
is the solution to the boundary value problem (
1.1
).

Finally, we discuss uniqueness of solutions to the boundary value problem (
1.1
) in
W^{1}_{\beta}(\Omega)
following
[
3
]
. Let
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
be two solutions to the boundary value problem (
1.1
) in
W^{1}_{\beta}(\Omega)
. Then, the difference
\tilde{u}:=u_{1}-u_{2}
belongs to
W^{1}_{\beta,-}(\Omega)
and solves the following boundary value problem:

\begin{cases}\beta\cdot\nabla\tilde{u}+\mu\tilde{u}=0&\mbox{ in }\Omega,\\
\tilde{u}=0&\mbox{ on }\Gamma_{-}.\end{cases}

(4.3)

By the definition of
W^{1}_{\beta,-}(\Omega)
, for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists a function
\tilde{u}^{\epsilon}\in\Lip(\overline{\Omega})
such that
\|\tilde{u}-\tilde{u}^{\epsilon}\|_{W^{1}_{\beta}(\Omega)}<\epsilon
and
\|\tilde{u}^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{1}(\Gamma_{-};|\beta\cdot n|)}<\epsilon
. Also, let
f^{\epsilon}:=\beta\cdot\nabla\tilde{u}^{\epsilon}+\mu\tilde{u}^{\epsilon}
. Noting that
\beta\cdot\nabla\tilde{u}+\mu\tilde{u}=0
, we have

\|f^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\leq\|\beta\cdot\nabla(\tilde{u}^{\epsilon}-\tilde{u})\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}+\|\mu(\tilde{u}^{\epsilon}-\tilde{u})\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\leq\left(1+\|\mu\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\right)\|\tilde{u}^{\epsilon}-\tilde{u}\|_{W^{1}_{\beta}(\Omega)}.

Thus,
f^{\epsilon}\to 0
in
L^{1}(\Omega)
as
\epsilon\to 0
.

Let
\Omega_{\pm}^{\epsilon}:=\{x\in\Omega\mid\pm\tilde{u}^{\epsilon}(x)>0\}
. Since
\tilde{u}^{\epsilon}\in\Lip(\overline{\Omega})
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\pm}^{\epsilon}}f^{\epsilon}\,dx=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\pm}^{\epsilon}}(\beta\cdot\nabla\tilde{u}^{\epsilon}+\mu\tilde{u}^{\epsilon})\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\pm}^{\epsilon}}(\mu-\mbox{div}\,\beta)\tilde{u}^{\epsilon}\,dx+\int_{{\Gamma_{+}}\cap\overline{\Omega_{\pm}^{\epsilon}}}u^{\epsilon}\beta\cdot n\,d\sigma_{x}+\int_{{\Gamma_{-}}\cap\overline{\Omega_{\pm}^{\epsilon}}}u^{\epsilon}\beta\cdot n\,d\sigma_{x}.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\mu_{1}\|\tilde{u}^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}+\|\tilde{u}^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{1}(\Gamma_{+};|\beta\cdot n|)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\pm}^{\epsilon}}(\mu-\mbox{div}\,\beta)|\tilde{u}^{\epsilon}|\,dx+\int_{{\Gamma_{+}}\cap\overline{\Omega_{\pm}^{\epsilon}}}|u^{\epsilon}|\beta\cdot n\,d\sigma_{x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{+}^{\epsilon}}(\mu-\mbox{div}\,\beta)\tilde{u}^{\epsilon}\,dx-\int_{\Omega_{-}^{\epsilon}}(\mu-\mbox{div}\,\beta)\tilde{u}^{\epsilon}\,dx+\int_{{\Gamma_{+}}\cap\overline{\Omega_{+}^{\epsilon}}}\tilde{u}^{\epsilon}\beta\cdot n\,d\sigma_{x}-\int_{{\Gamma_{+}}\cap\overline{\Omega_{-}^{\epsilon}}}\tilde{u}^{\epsilon}\beta\cdot n\,d\sigma_{x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{+}^{\epsilon}}f^{\epsilon}\,dx-\int_{\Omega_{-}^{\epsilon}}f^{\epsilon}\,dx-\int_{{\Gamma_{-}}\cap\overline{\Omega_{+}^{\epsilon}}}\tilde{u}^{\epsilon}\beta\cdot n\,d\sigma_{x}+\int_{{\Gamma_{-}}\cap\overline{\Omega_{-}^{\epsilon}}}\tilde{u}^{\epsilon}\beta\cdot n\,d\sigma_{x}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|f^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}+\|\tilde{u}^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{1}(\Gamma_{-};|\beta\cdot n|)},

which implies that
\tilde{u}^{\epsilon}\to 0
in
L^{1}(\Omega)
and
\tilde{u}^{\epsilon}|_{\Gamma_{+}}\to 0
in
L^{1}(\Gamma_{+};\beta\cdot n)
as
\epsilon\to 0
. Since

\|\tilde{u}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\leq\|\tilde{u}-\tilde{u}^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}+\|\tilde{u}^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)},

we conclude that
\tilde{u}=0
, which shows the uniqueness of the solution to (
1.1
) in
W^{1}_{\beta}(\Omega)
.

The estimate for the strong solution with
p=1
in Theorem
1.2
is derived in the same way as for the case
1<p<\infty
. Therefore, Theorem
1.2
is proved.

(5.1)

on

v=u_{k}-u_{m,k}
, which means that

\|u_{k}-u_{m,k}\|_{W^{p}_{\beta,\tr}(\Omega)}\leq C\|u_{k}-u_{m,k}\|_{W^{p}_{\beta}(\Omega)}\to 0

as
m\to\infty
for each
k=K_{1}+1,\ldots,K
.

Thus, the relation (
1.2
) is proved.

Neural Polar Decoders for Deletion Channels
Ziv Aharoni, Ziv Aharoni and Henry D. Pfister

Abstract
This paper introduces a neural polar decoder (NPD)for deletion channels with a constant deletion rate. Existing polar decoders for deletion channels exhibit high computational complexity of O ⁡ ( N 4 ) O(N^{4}) , where N N is the block length. This limits the application of polar codes for deletion channels to short-to-moderate block lengths. In this work, we demonstrate that employing NPDsfor deletion channels can reduce the computational complexity. First, we extend the architecture of the NPDto support deletion channels. Specifically, the NPDarchitecture consists of four neural networks (NNs), each replicating fundamental successive cancellation (SC)decoder operations. To support deletion channels, we change the architecture of only one. The computational complexity of the NPDis O ⁡ ( A ​ N ​ log ⁡ N ) O(AN\log N) , where the parameter A A represents a computational budget determined by the user and is independent of the channel. We evaluate the new extended NPDfor deletion channels with deletion rates δ ∈ { 0.01 , 0.1 } \delta\in\{0.01,0.1\} and we verify the NPDwith the ground truth given by the trellis decoder by Tal et al. We further show that due to the reduced complexity of the NPD, we are able to incorporate list decoding and further improve performance. We believe that the extended NPDpresented here could have applications in future technologies like DNA storage.

I
Introduction

The deletion channel is perhaps the simplest channel with synchronization errors and it is a common model for communication systems
[
1
,
2
]
and DNA storage
[
3
,
4
,
5
]
. A binary-input deletion channel with a constant deletion rate
\delta\in(0,1)
deletes each bit independently with probability
\delta
. As a result, a binary length-
N
input sequence
\mathbf{X}
is mapped to a binary length-
D
output sequence
\mathbf{Y}
, where
D\sim\mathsf{Bin}(N,1-\delta)
.
Despite its memoryless behavior, the loss of synchronization caused by deletions makes the deletion channel difficult to analyze. Still, communicating over deletion channels is a challenging task that has been researched extensively in recent years
[
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
]
.
Many known decoders for the deletion channel assume only a fixed small number of deletions have occurred. However, solutions for the deletion channel with a constant deletion rate are more scarce.

In recent years, polar codes
[
10
]
were applied to deletion channels in a series of papers
[
11
,
12
,
13
]
. The method in
[
11
]
used the
successive cancellation (SC)decoder for memoryless channels, as introduced in
[
10
]
, while considering all possible deletion patterns of the received sequence
\mathbf{Y}
. This is highly inefficient as the number of deletion patterns grows exponentially.
The paper
[
12
]
devises a
SCpolar decoder by considering deletion patterns throughout the decoding process. The deletion patterns are coded into a trellis which is embedded in the
SCdecoding algorithm to compute the posteriors of the synthetic channels. The computational complexity of the decoder is
O(d^{3}N\log N)
for
d
deletions. This is efficient for small value of
d
, as the authors demonstrated the decoders performance for low deletion rate of
\delta=0.002
. However, this decoder still suffers from high computational complexity when the deletion rate is larger.

The method proposed in
[
13
]
represents the joint distribution of
\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Y}
using a trellis.
For a given output sequence
\mathbf{y}
, a trellis is constructed with
N
sections, each corresponding to an input symbol
X_{i}
. Each section contains
D
nodes, and each node can have up to three outgoing edges connecting it to nodes in the adjacent section.
Using this trellis, a
SCdecoder is defined that allows the exact computation of the posteriors for the synthetic channels. This enables one to estimate the
mutual information (MI)between
\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Y}
, and subsequently, design a
SCdecoder. The trellis decoder for deletion channels is a useful tool but the decoding complexity is still
O(N^{4})
. This weakness motivates us to devise an
SCdecoder with near-optimal performance and reduced complexity.

Our main observation about the trellis in
[
13
]
is that it is sparse, namely, most of the edges in the trellis are not used. This led us to find a way to “compress" the trellis representation and create an
SCdecoder that operates on the compressed trellis representation. To do this, we build upon
[
14
]
, which introduces a
neural polar decoder (NPD)that shares the same structure as the
SCdecoder but replaces its elementary operations by
neural networks (NNs). In that work, embedding
NNsin the structure of the
SCdecoder was shown to perform well on channels with memory, in the sense that it enabled the computation of the posterior of the synthetic channels consistently
1
1

1

Given sufficient samples of sequences
\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Y}
, the
NPDcan approximate the posterior with bounded estimation error.
.

There are two main benefits of
NPDs. First, a
NPDdoes not require an explicit channel model; it only requires the channel as a black-box for generating samples of input-output pairs. Second, the computational complexity is determined by the parameterization of the
NNsthat construct the
NPD, and not by the state space size of the channel. For example, consider
finite state channels (FSCs)which are a common model for channels with memory.
FSCshave a corresponding
SCdecoder, called
successive cancellation trellis (SCT)decoder
[
15
]
. The
SCTdecoder for
FSCshas computational complexity of
O(M^{3}N\log N)
, where
M
is the number of channel states. The
NPDwith a computation budget
A
, however, has a computational complexity of
O(AN\log N)
, regardless of the number of channel states.
In the context of deletion channels, the
SCTdecoder for
FSCsis important since despite the differences between
FSCsand deletion channels, their corresponding
SCdecoders for polar codes share a common structure and attributes.

Henceforth, this work focuses on adapting
NPDsto deletion channels. The adaptation of
NPDsto deletion channels involves updating only one
NNout of the four
NNsthat compose the
NPD. To evaluate the
NPD, we compare its decoding errors with the decoding errors of the trellis decoder for deletion channels. Our results demonstrate that while the
NPDis comparable with the trellis decoder in terms of decoding errors, it exhibits computational complexity that is lower by two orders of magnitude. We also show that reducing the complexity enables us to incorporate list decoding
[
16
]
. This is an important advantage since list decoding in polar codes is crucial to approach maximum-likelihood decoding in the short-to-moderate block lengths, but computationally intractable for the trellis decoder for deletion channels.

II
Background

This section starts by introducing notation and basic definitions, particularly of a binary
independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.)deletion channel, which is henceforth called the deletion channel. It proceeds by presenting polar codes and
NPDs.

We denote by
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
the underlying probability space on which all
random variables (RVs)are defined. Sets are denoted by calligraphic letters, e.g.
\mathcal{X}
.
We use the notation

\mathbf{x}
to denote its realization, where
N
will be given by the context. The term
P_{\mathbf{X}}
denotes the distribution of
\mathbf{X}
.
Throughout the paper, we assume that
\mathcal{X}=\{0,1\}
.
We denote by
[N]
the set of integers
\{1,\dots,N\}
. Let
\mathcal{D}^{c}
be the complement of the set
\mathcal{D}
.
Let
\mathbf{x}
be a length-
N
binary sequence. The support of
\mathbf{x}
is defined by
\operatorname{supp}(\mathbf{x})=\{i\mid x_{i}=1,\;i\in[N]\}
.
Given an index set
\mathcal{D}\subseteq[N]
, the notation
\mathbf{X}_{\mathcal{D}}
represents the subsequence of
\mathbf{X}
consisting of the elements indexed by
\mathcal{D}
, i.e.,
\mathbf{X}_{\mathcal{D}}=(X_{i}\mid i\in\mathcal{D})
with the elements of
\mathcal{D}
in increasing order.
The term
A\otimes B
denotes the Kronecker product of
A
and
B
when
A,B
are matrices, and it denotes the product distribution whenever
A,B
are distributions.
The term

N

The notation

\mathbf{D}
is a length-
N

i.i.d.Bernoulli random vector with success rate
p\in(0,1)
.
The binary entropy of
X
is denoted by
\mathsf{H}_{2}\left(X\right)
and the
cross entropy (CE)between two distributions
P,Q
is denoted by
h_{\mathsf{CE}}\left(P,Q\right)
. The deletion channel is defined next.

Definition 1

(Deletion Channel)
.

Let
\delta\in(0,1)
denote the deletion probability and let
N
denote the block length. Let
\mathbf{D}\sim\mathsf{Ber}(\delta)^{\otimes N}
,
\mathcal{D}=\operatorname{supp}(\mathbf{D})
and

II-A

Polar Codes

Let
G_{N}=B_{N}F^{\otimes n}
be Arıkan’s polar transform with the generator matrix for block length
N=2^{n}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
. The matrix
F
is Arıkan’s
2\times 2
kernel defined by
F=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
1&1\end{bmatrix}.

The matrix
B_{N}
is the permutation matrix associated with the bit-reversal permutation. It is defined by the recursive relation
B_{N}=R_{N}(I_{2}\otimes B_{\frac{N}{2}})
starting from
B_{2}=I_{2}
.
The term
I_{N}
denotes the identity matrix of size
N
and
R_{N}
denotes a permutation matrix called reverse-shuffle
[
10
]
.
Given
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{N}
the polar transform is defined by

\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{x}G_{N}.

(1)

The synthetic channels of the polar code are defined by

W_{i,N}(u_{i}|u^{i-1},y^{N})=P_{U_{i}|U^{i-1},Y^{N}}(u_{i}|u^{i-1},y^{N}),

(2)

for
i\in[N]
,
\mathbf{u}\in\mathcal{X}^{N}
and
\mathbf{y}\in\mathcal{Y}^{N}
. In this work, we set
\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{Y}
, though in general,
\mathcal{Y}
can be arbitrary.

A
SCpolar decoder is composed of 4 elementary functions that map the channel outputs
\mathbf{y}
into the estimation
\hat{\mathbf{u}}
via the computation of the synthetic channels
[
10
,
14
]
. Thus, a
SCpolar decoder may be defined as follows.

Definition 2

(Successive cancellation polar decoder)
.

Let
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}^{N}
and
\mathbf{y}\in\mathcal{Y}^{N}
. A
SCpolar decoder is composed by the following functions:

•

The embedding function
E:\mathcal{Y}\to\mathcal{E}
,

•

The check-node function
F:\mathcal{E}\times\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{E}
,

•

The bit-node function
G:\mathcal{E}\times\mathcal{E}\times\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{E}
,

•

The embedding-to-llr function
H:\mathcal{E}\to\mathbb{R}
,

where
\mathcal{E}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d},\;d\in\mathbb{N},
is the embedding space of the
SCdecoder.

Definition
2
lists the ingredients needed by a
SCdecoder to decode an output sequence
\mathbf{y}\in\mathcal{X}^{N\times 1}
into
\hat{\mathbf{u}}\in\mathcal{X}^{N\times 1}
. The decoding computation starts with mapping each
y\in\mathbf{y}
independently into
e=E(y)
,
e\in\mathbb{R}^{1\times d}
, to form
\mathbf{e}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times d}
. The decoding computation is completed by using
\mathbf{e}
and
F,G,H
to compute
\hat{\mathbf{u}}
via the recursion of the
SCdecoder, as shown in
[
10
]
. For example, setting
\mathcal{E}=\mathbb{R}
and defining
E,F,G,H
by

\displaystyle E(y)

\displaystyle=\log\frac{W\left(y|1\right)}{W\left(y|0\right)}+\log\frac{P_{X}\left(1\right)}{P_{X}\left(0\right)},\nn

\displaystyle F(e_{1},e_{2})

\displaystyle=-2\tanh^{-1}\left(\tanh{\frac{e_{1}}{2}}\tanh{\frac{e_{2}}{2}}\right),\nn

\displaystyle G(e_{1},e_{2},u)

\displaystyle=e_{2}+(-1)^{u}e_{1},\nn

\displaystyle H(e_{1})

\displaystyle=e_{1},

(3)

for
e_{1},e_{2}\in\mathbb{R},u\in\mathcal{X}
, defines a
SCdecoder for memoryless channels. For
FSCswith state space
\mathcal{S}
, the
SCTdecoder from
[
15
]
is defined by setting
\mathcal{E}=[0,1]^{|\mathcal{X}||\mathcal{S}|^{2}}
and defining

\displaystyle\left[E(y)\right]_{x,s_{0},s_{1}}=P_{X,Y,S^{\prime}|S}\left(x,y,s_{1}|s_{0}\right).

(4)

The coordinates
x,s_{0},s_{1}
are used to specify an entry of the vector
e
, and
y
is the input of the function
E
.
Here, the embedding function outputs a vector, mostly referred as a trellis, that captures the joint distribution of
X,Y
and the channel states before and after the transmission
S,S^{\prime}
, respectively. The functions
F,G,H
are given in
[
15
]
and omitted here due to space limitations.

II-B

Neural Polar Decoders

An
NPDis also an instance of a
SCpolar decoder, as in Definition
2
. Setting the
\mathcal{E}=\mathbb{R}^{d},\;d\in\mathbb{N}
and realizing the functions
E,F,G,H
by
NNsyields an
NPD. Let
\mathcal{G}_{\mathsf{NN}}^{(d_{i},k,d_{o})}
denote the family of shallow
NNswith
d_{i}
inputs,
k

d_{o}
outputs and ReLU activations. An
NPDis defined as follows.

Definition 3

(Neural polar decoder)
.

An
NPDis an
SCpolar decoder, as defined in Definition
2
, with the functions
E,F,G,H
defined by:

•

The embedding
NN
E_{\theta_{E}}\in\mathcal{G}_{\mathsf{NN}}^{(1,h,d)}
,

•

The check-node
NN
F_{\theta_{G}}\in\mathcal{G}_{\mathsf{NN}}^{(2d,h,d)}
,

•

The bit-node
NN
G_{\theta_{G}}\in\mathcal{G}_{\mathsf{NN}}^{(2d+1,h,d)}
,

•

The embedding-to-llr
NN
H_{\theta_{H}}\in\mathcal{G}_{\mathsf{NN}}^{(d,h,1)}
,

where

is the number of hidden units. Let

be the parameters of the

\Theta
the parameter space.

For any
\theta\in\Theta
the
NPDcomputes an estimate of the posterior distribution of the synthetic channels

, as given in (
2
) via the recursion of the

. The process of determining the

, which achieves its minimum of
\mathsf{H}_{2}\left(W_{i,N}\right)

almost surely. The optimization algorithm is outlined in
[
14
]
.

III
Neural Polar Decoder for Deletion Channels

This section addresses the adaptation of
NPDsto deletion channels. It starts by discussing the limitations of current
NPDsregarding deletion channels. It proceeds by adapting the
NPDto deletion channels with a focus on the embedding
NN

.

III-A

Limitation of current NPDs on deletion channels

The
NPDin
[
14
]
is designed for channels with synchronized inputs and outputs, i.e.
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}
are always the same length
N
and
y_{i}
is an observation of
x_{i}
. However, in the deletion channel, this is not the case. Therefore, the
NPDmust be changed in order to accommodate deletion channels.

III-B

Adapting the embedding NN

To solve the problem that
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}^{N\times 1}
and
\mathbf{y}\in\mathcal{X}^{D\times 1}
do not have the same length, we adapt the embedding
NNfrom Definition
3
. Instead of computing
\mathbf{e}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times d}
by using the embedding
NNto compute
e=E_{\theta}(y)
for each
y\in\mathbf{y}
, we instead compute
\mathbf{e}=\mathbf{E}_{\theta}(\mathbf{y})
, where
\mathbf{E}_{\theta}
is a
NNthat maps the whole
\mathbf{y}
vector to
\mathbf{e}
. It is possible to build an
NPDfor every possible output length
D
. In the next section, we show that this approach yields a consistent estimation of the synthetic channels, and therefore defines a valid
SCdecoder. However, this approach is not efficient in parameters.

Conclusion

This work presents a novel approach to decoding deletion channels using
NPDs. Deletion channels, characterized by synchronization errors and modeled by a constant deletion rate, pose significant challenges in reliable decoding due to the loss of synchronization. Existing methods, such as the trellis-based
SCdecoder introduced in
[
13
]
, provided a major advancement by enabling the exact computation of posterior probabilities. However, the high computational complexity of
O(N^{4})
limits its practicality for larger block lengths.

Motivated by the sparsity observed in the trellis structure, we proposed leveraging
NPDsto compress the trellis representation and reduce computational complexity. By embedding
NNswithin the
SCframework,
NPDdemonstrated two significant advantages: eliminating the need for an explicit channel model and achieving a complexity determined by the
NNs’ parameterization. This led to a reduced computational complexity compared to traditional trellis-based decoders, enabling efficient decoding for longer block lengths and the integration of list decoding.

The results demonstrate that the
NPDachieves comparable performance to the trellis-based
SCdecoder while significantly reducing complexity. This advancement not only broadens the applicability of polar codes to deletion channels but also sets the foundation for further exploration into scalable, data-driven decoding techniques for channels with synchronization errors. Future work could focus on extending this approach to other channels with synchronization problems, such as the deletion-insertion-substitution channel. We are also interested on investigating the potential of
NPDsfor the DNA storage problem.

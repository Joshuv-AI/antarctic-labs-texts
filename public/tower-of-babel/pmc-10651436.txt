What we should focus on and reach out to
Authors: T. Nishimura
Published: 2023

There is so much research related to artificial intelligence (AI) these days that it is impossible to list it all in the references. Alan Turing, known for his Turing test, is the man who laid the foundation for AI. His achievements in AI are immeasurable, but his life should also be noted. He was gay and because of that his life had many hardships. Benedict Cumberbatch’s brilliant performance in the film ‘The Imitation Game’ best portrays what he went through. It is his life that we should focus on.

I am an oncologist at a small Japanese hospital and some of my patients or their families are sexual and gender minority (SGM). And I see that they still face problems with health care costs due to the way they are treated in the workplace and legal issues. Recently, a courageous report by Leone et al. was published regarding the treatment of transgender cancer patients.1 In addition to a detailed report on the SGM population, this report describes the discrimination they have suffered from medical professionals. Meanwhile, a large cross-sectional study has recently been published from the United States, comparing the prevalence of diseases, including cancer, employment status, education, household income and percentage of home ownership among SGM groups with a control group.2 Their situation remains serious and that there is an urgent need to educate the public, including health care professionals.

The European Society for Medical Oncology (ESMO)’s statement on the sustainable development goals (SDGs) was recently released,3 and I am very supportive of this initiative. There are many SDGs activities that we as oncologists can do through cancer care, one of which is to reduce disparities and address gender issues. Reports on SGM people are considered to be underreported because they do not record their sexual orientation or gender identity for fear of discrimination.4 However, research on the prevalence of diseases and social context of SGM people has progressed. In the future, prospective intervention studies, such as intervention by trained health workers, should be carried out. The suffering of SGM people is immeasurable, but now is the time for us to change and take action. We must focus on and reach out to them. I believe this is what will make the world a better place for everyone to live in.

Canonical foliation of bubblesheets
Jean Lagacé, Stephen Lynch

Abstract
. We introduce a new curvature condition for high-codimension submanifolds of a Riemannian ambient space, called quasi-parallel mean curvature (QPMC). The class of submanifolds with QPMC includes all CMC hypersurfaces and submanifolds with parallel mean curvature. We use our notion of QPMC to prove that certain kinds of high-curvature regions which appear in geometric flows, called bubblesheets, can be placed in a suitable normal form. This follows from a more general result asserting that the manifold ℝ k × 𝕊 n − k \mathbb{R}^{k}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-k} , equipped with any metric which is sufficiently close to the standard one, admits a canonical foliation by embedded ( n − k ) (n-k) -spheres with QPMC.



1.
Introduction

At the core of differential geometry is the notion that the important features of a space should remain invariant under changes of coordinates. However a space with some special structure may admit preferred coordinate systems, which reveal its features with particular clarity. A proven method for finding such distinguished parameterizations is to identify a foliation of the space by submanifolds which are canonically determined by its geometry.

Foliations by hypersurfaces with constant mean curvature (CMC) have been used to parameterize the ends of asymptotically flat manifolds, leading to a definition of center of mass for isolated gravitating systems
[
HY96
]
(see also the more recent
[
CS21
]
and references therein). In the Lorentzian setting, a foliation by maximal (vanishing mean curvature) hypersurfaces played a role in the first proof of the stability of the Minkowski spacetime
[
CK93
]
. In yet another direction, a canonical CMC foliation for ‘necks’ (Riemannian manifolds which locally resemble
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
at some scale), constructed by Hamilton, has been employed to continue geometric flows through neck singularities via surgery
[
Ham97
,
HS09
]
.

In this paper we construct a new canonical foliation for another kind of geometry which forms near singularities of both the Ricci flow and the mean curvature flow. These geometries, called bubblesheets, locally resemble
\mathbb{R}^{k}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-k}
at some scale, where
k\geq 2
. Bubblesheets are expected to be the most prevalent high-curvature regions in the mean curvature flow of hypersurfaces of dimension
n\geq 3
, and in the Ricci flow on manifolds of dimension
n\geq 4
. The essential question we address is the following:

Question 1.1
.

Consider the manifold
\mathbb{R}^{k}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-k}
equipped with the standard product metric
g_{0}:=g_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\oplus g_{\mathbb{S}^{n-k}}
. This space is foliated by round, totally geodesic
(n-k)
-spheres, namely the slices
\{z\}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-k}
. If
g_{0}
is deformed to a nearby metric, can these totally geodesic spheres be deformed into a canonical foliation of the resulting space?

The foliation we seek is by spheres of codimension
k\geq 2
which, by analogy with Hamilton’s CMC foliation for
k=1
, ought to solve some equation involving their mean curvature. The main challenge is to determine the right equation, or in this case
system
of equations, since our spheres have codimension greater than one. One might seek a foliation by leaves whose mean curvature has constant length, or is parallel in the normal bundle. Neither of these conditions works; in a certain sense they are underdetermined and overdetermined respectively. The correct approach, we argue, is to foliate by spheres which have quasi-parallel mean curvature (QPMC). This new curvature condition is more general than having parallel mean curvature, but almost as restrictive (as it must be if our foliation is to be canonical).

To see what we mean by QPMC, fix a Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
of dimension
n
and consider a submanifold
\Sigma\subset M
of codimension
k
. The Levi-Civita connection of
(M,g)
induces a connection
\nabla^{\perp}
and hence a Laplacian
\Delta^{\perp}
acting on sections of the normal bundle
N\Sigma
. The operator
-\Delta^{\perp}
admits a complete set of eigensections with nonnegative eigenvalues
\lambda_{m}=\lambda_{m}(\Sigma,g)
, labelled in nondecreasing order, repeating according to multiplicity. We write
P_{\lambda}
for the
\mathrm{L}^{2}
-orthogonal projection onto the eigenspace associated with
\lambda\in\spec(-\Delta^{\perp})
and define

Q:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\in\spec(-\Delta^{\perp})\\
\lambda<\lambda_{k+1}\end{subarray}}P_{\lambda}.

A section of
N\Sigma
is called
quasi-parallel
if it lies in
\im(Q)
, and so the submanifold
\Sigma
is said to have QPMC if its mean curvature vector
H
satisfies

(1)

(1-Q)(H)=0.

We note that
1-Q
is a nonlocal pseudodifferential operator acting on sections of
N\Sigma
. Expressed as
H=Q(H)
, (
1
) is a weakly elliptic quasilinear system for the position of the submanifold, with nonlocal right-hand side. From a variational point of view,
\Sigma
has QPMC if and only if
\Sigma
is a critical point for the volume functional with respect to variations whose initial velocity lies in
\ker(Q)
.

Let us further clarify the meaning of (
1
) with a few remarks:

•

If
\Sigma
is of codimension 1 and 2-sided, then
Q
is the projection onto the 1-dimensional space of harmonic sections of
N\Sigma
(normal vectors of constant length). Therefore, in this setting
\Sigma
has CMC if and only if it has QPMC.

•

Since
\Sigma
is compact,
\ker(\Delta^{\perp})
is precisely the space of parallel sections of
N\Sigma
. In particular, if
H
is parallel in
N\Sigma
, meaning
\nabla^{\perp}H=0
, then
\Sigma
has QPMC.

•

When
k\geq 2
, for a general submanifold
\Sigma
, the normal bundle
N\Sigma
may admit no global nonzero parallel sections. However, if
\lambda_{k}<\lambda_{k+1}
(which is true generically), then
\im(Q)
is
k
-dimensional. In a sense which can be made precise using Rayleigh quotients,
\im(Q)
is the
k
-dimensional subspace of normal vector fields which are ‘as close as possible’ to being parallel.

We now state our first main result. Recall the notation
g_{0}=g_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\oplus g_{\mathbb{S}^{n-k}}
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
g
be a smooth Riemannian metric on
M=\mathbb{R}^{k}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-k}
and fix constants
\ell\geq 3
and
\gamma\in(0,1)
. If
\delta>0
is sufficiently small depending on
n
,
\ell
and
\gamma
then there exists a positive constant
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(n,\ell,\gamma,\delta)
with the following property. If
\|g-g_{0}\|_{\mathrm{C}^{\ell-1,\gamma}}\leq\varepsilon
then
M
admits a unique smooth foliation by embedded
(n-k)
-spheres, each of which has QPMC and is
\delta
-close to some
\{z\}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-k}
in the graphical
\mathrm{C}^{\ell,\gamma}
-sense.

Remark 1.3
.

In Section
6
we provide examples of metrics on
\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
which admit a QPMC foliation by Theorem
1.2
, but do not admit a foliation whose leaves have parallel mean curvature. These examples are obtained from bumpy perturbations of almost collapsed Berger metrics on
\mathbb{S}^{3}
.

Using Theorem
1.2
, we produce a canonical QPMC foliation for more general geometries which resemble
\mathbb{R}^{k}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-k}
only locally and after rescaling. Before stating this result we introduce some definitions. Let
(M,g)
be a complete Riemannian
n
-manifold. The following notion of an
(\varepsilon,L,n-k)
-cylindrical region makes precise what we informally refer to as a bubblesheet.

Definition 1.4
.

An open subset
\mathcal{C}\subset M
is
(\varepsilon,L,n-k)
-cylindrical if for each
p\in\mathcal{C}
there exists a scale
r(p)>0
and an embedding

F_{p}:B^{k}(0,L)\times\mathbb{S}^{n-k}\to M

such that
F_{p}^{*}(r(p)^{-2}g)
is
\varepsilon
-close to
g_{0}
in
\mathrm{C}^{[1/\varepsilon]}
.

We will also need to refer to the following class of submanifolds of
M
.

Definition 1.5
.

Let
\Sigma\subset M
be a closed submanifold. We introduce a scale
\bar{r}
defined so that
\pi\bar{r}
is the average injectivity radius of
(M,g)
over points in
\Sigma
. We say that
\Sigma
is
\delta
-vertical if
\diam_{g}(\Sigma)/\bar{r}\leq 10\pi
and

\bar{r}|A_{\Sigma}|+\bar{r}^{2}|\nabla^{\perp}A_{\Sigma}|+\bar{r}^{3}|\nabla^{\perp}\nabla^{\perp}A_{\Sigma}|\leq\delta,

where
A_{\Sigma}
is the second fundamental form and
\nabla^{\perp}
is the induced connection on
T^{*}\Sigma\otimes\dots\otimes T^{*}\Sigma\otimes N\Sigma
.

Definitions
1.4
and
1.5
are intended so that if
\Sigma
is a
\delta
-vertical
(n-k)
-sphere embedded in an
(\varepsilon,L,n-k)
-cylindrical region and
\varepsilon
is small, then
\delta
controls how far
\Sigma
is from a vertical sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{k}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-k}
after pulling back. Note also that for
p\in\Sigma
we have
\bar{r}/r(p)\to 1
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

We now state our second main result.

Theorem 1.6
.

Fix a constant
L\geq 1000
. Let
(M,g)
be a complete Riemannian manifold and suppose
\mathcal{C}\subset M
is
(\varepsilon,L,n-k)
-cylindrical. If
\varepsilon
is sufficiently small depending on
n
and
\delta
then there exists an open subset

\mathcal{C}\subset\mathcal{B}\subset M

which is foliated by
\delta
-vertical
(n-k)
-spheres with QPMC. Moreover, every embedded
(n-k)
-sphere in
M
which is
\delta
-vertical, has QPMC and intersects
\mathcal{C}
is a leaf of this foliation.

Theorem
1.2
implies the existence of a local QPMC foliation around any point in the region
\mathcal{C}
referred to in Theorem
1.6
. The extra step required to prove the theorem is to show that these local foliations agree where they overlap, and hence combine to give a global foliation which covers all of
\mathcal{C}
. This overlapping property is established via uniqueness results for QPMC leaves, which we prove in Section
5
.

1.1.
Outline

After fixing notation in Section
2
, we compute in Section
3
the first variation of
(1-Q)(H)
for a general submanifold of a Riemannian ambient space. Theorem
1.2
is proven using the implicit function theorem in Section
4
. We then prove Theorem
1.6
in Section
5
. In Section
6
we demonstrate by example that certain metrics on
\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
do not admit a foliation by spheres with parallel mean curvature. Finally, in Section
7

1.6
can be used to cast these in a canonical normal form.

2.
Notation

Let
(M,g)
be a Riemannian manifold. We write
\nabla
for the Levi-Civita connection determined by
g
. Our convention for the Riemann curvature tensor of
(M,g)
is

R(U,V)W=\nabla_{U}\nabla_{V}W-\nabla_{V}\nabla_{U}W.

Let
\Sigma\subset M
be a closed submanifold of codimension
k
. We introduce some standard notation and terminology concerning the geometry of
\Sigma
. First recall that for each
p\in\Sigma
, the tangent space
T_{p}M
splits as

T_{p}M=T_{p}\Sigma\oplus(T_{p}\Sigma)^{\perp}=T_{p}\Sigma\oplus N_{p}\Sigma,

and this gives rise to the tangent bundle
T\Sigma
and normal bundle
N\Sigma
. For a vector
V\in T_{p}M
, we write
V^{\top}
and
V^{\perp}
for the projections of
V
onto
T_{p}\Sigma
and
N_{p}\Sigma
respectively.

We use
h
to denote the metric induced by
g
on
\Sigma
. That is,

h(X,Y)=g(X,Y)

for
X
and
Y
tangent to
\Sigma
. The connection
\nabla
induces connections
\nabla^{\top}
and
\nabla^{\perp}
on
T\Sigma
and
N\Sigma
respectively, via the formulae

(2)

\nabla^{\top}_{X}Y:=(\nabla_{X}Y)^{\top}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\nabla^{\perp}_{X}V:=(\nabla_{X}V)^{\perp}.

Note that
\nabla^{\top}
is the Levi-Civita connection on
T\Sigma
determined by
h
. The second fundamental form of
\Sigma
acts on tangent vectors
X
and
Y
by

A(X,Y)=(\nabla_{X}Y)^{\perp}.

If
X_{i}
is a locally defined smooth frame for
T\Sigma
, then for
V\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}(N\Sigma)
we have

(\nabla_{X_{i}}V)^{\top}=-g(A_{i}^{j},V)X_{j},

and hence

\nabla^{\perp}_{i}V=\nabla_{X_{i}}V+g(A^{j}_{i},V)X_{j},

where
A_{i}^{j}=h^{jk}A_{ki}
,
h^{jk}
being the matrix inverse to
h_{jk}
.

2.1.
The normal Laplacian

We define a Laplacian
\Delta^{\perp}:\mathrm{C}^{\infty}(N\Sigma)\to\mathrm{C}^{\infty}(N\Sigma)
by

\Delta^{\perp}:=-\nabla^{\perp*}\nabla^{\perp},

where
\nabla^{\perp*}:\mathrm{C}^{\infty}(T^{*}\Sigma\otimes N\Sigma)\to\mathrm{C}^{\infty}(N\Sigma)
is the
\mathrm{L}^{2}
-adjoint of
\nabla^{\perp}
. With respect to any local frame
X_{i}
for
T\Sigma
we have

\Delta^{\perp}V=h^{ij}\left(\nabla^{\perp}_{i}(\nabla^{\perp}_{j}V)-\nabla^{\perp}_{\nabla^{\top}_{i}X_{j}}V\right).

The operator
\Delta^{\perp}
is essentially self-adjoint on its domain, the Sobolev space
\mathrm{W}^{2,2}(N\Sigma)
. Since
-\Delta^{\perp}
is a positive elliptic operator on a compact manifold, it has compact resolvent, and so its spectrum is comprised of eigenvalues; these form a nondecreasing
sequence

(3)

0\leqslant\lambda_{1}(\Sigma,g)\leqslant\lambda_{2}(\Sigma,g)\leqslant\lambda_{3}(\Sigma,g)\leqslant\dotso\nearrow\infty.

To each
\lambda\in\spec(-\Delta^{\perp})
we have an associated
\mathrm{L}^{2}
-projection
P_{\lambda}
and eigenspace
\im(P_{\lambda})
.
Moreover, we have the spectral decomposition

(4)

-\Delta^{\perp}=\sum_{\lambda\in\spec(-\Delta^{\perp})}\lambda P_{\lambda}.

We note that

(5)

\dim(\ker(\Delta^{\perp}))\leqslant k,

with equality if and only if
N\Sigma
admits a global frame consisting of parallel sections.

For
m\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\mathbf{Gr}_{m}
be the Grassmannian of

m
-dimensional subspaces of
\mathrm{C}^{\infty}(N\Sigma)
. The eigenvalues of
-\Delta^{\perp}
are characterized variationally by

(6)

\lambda_{m}(\Sigma,g)=\min_{E\in\mathbf{Gr}_{m}}\max_{V\in E,\,\|V\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}=1}\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla^{\perp}V|^{2}\dvoll_{\Sigma},

and any normal vector field
V
which realises this minmax is an eigensection of
-\Delta^{\perp}
. It follows from this variational characterization that
\lambda_{m}(\Sigma,g)
is piecewise smooth (and smooth away from points at which its multiplicity changes)
with respect to
\Sigma
and
g
.

2.2.
Quasi-parallel sections

For a general submanifold
\Sigma
, the normal bundle
N\Sigma
may not admit any nonzero sections which are parallel with respect to
\nabla^{\perp}
. Therefore, in place of parallel sections, we work with a class of sections which behave almost as well and are far more abundant. These we call quasi-parallel. Recalling that
k
is the codimension of
\Sigma
in
M
, we define

Q:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\in\spec(-\Delta^{\perp})\\
\lambda<\lambda_{k+1}\end{subarray}}P_{\lambda},

and say that a section of
N\Sigma
is quasi-parallel if it lies in
\im(Q)
.

Concerning this definition, a few remarks are in order. Note first that

\dim(\im(Q))\leq k

with equality if and only if
\lambda_{k}<\lambda_{k+1}
. Moreover, for a generic submanifold
\Sigma
and any
m
, the eigenvalue
\lambda_{m}
is simple. Appealing to this fact in the case
m=k
we see that for a generic
\Sigma
we have
\lambda_{k}<\lambda_{k+1}
. Since this is an open condition,
Q
has constant rank equal to
k
on a dense open subset of the compact
(n-k)
-dimensional submanifolds of
M
.

Next we observe that
Q
varies smoothly while
\Sigma
varies smoothly with
\lambda_{k}<\lambda_{k+1}
, using the variational characterisation of
\im(Q)
, which is as follows. Suppose
Q
has rank
k
, i.e.
\lambda_{k}<\lambda_{k+1}
, and consider the function

(V_{1},\dots,V_{k})\mapsto\sum_{j=1}^{k}\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla^{\perp}V_{j}|^{2}\dvoll_{\Sigma}

acting on
\mathrm{L}^{2}
-orthonormal collections of sections
V_{1},\dots,V_{k}\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}(N\Sigma)
. This function is smooth and depends only on the subspace spanned by
V_{1},\dots,V_{k}
, so it lifts to a smooth function defined on
\mathbf{Gr}_{k}
. Whenever
\lambda_{k}<\lambda_{k+1}
, the resulting function attains its unique global minimum at
\im(Q)
, and this minimum is nondegenerate. Therefore,
\im(Q)
traces out a smooth path in
\mathbf{Gr}_{k}
as
\Sigma
varies smoothly.

Finally, let us point out that
Q
has rank
k
if
N\Sigma
admits a global frame consisting of parallel sections, or if
\Sigma
is a perturbation of such a submanifold. This is, for example, the case when
M=\mathbb{R}^{k}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-k}
with the metric
g_{0}
and
\Sigma
is a vertical sphere
\{z\}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-k}
, which is the setting with which we are principally concerned. Indeed, we then have

\lambda_{1}=\dots=\lambda_{k}=0,\qquad\lambda_{k+1}=n-k,

so
\lambda_{k}<\lambda_{k+1}
remains true for perturbations of
\Sigma
and, we note, in this perturbed regime,

Q=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\in\spec(-\Delta^{\perp})\\
\lambda<\frac{n-k}{2}\end{subarray}}P_{\lambda}.

2.3.
Regularity of eigensections

With minor modifications, all of the definitions in this section still make sense if
g
is of class
\mathrm{C}^{2,\gamma}
and
\Sigma
is of class
\mathrm{C}^{3,\gamma}
. For later use, we record here a regularity result for the eigensections of
-\Delta^{\perp}
under these hypotheses.

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose
g
is of class
\mathrm{C}^{\ell-1,\gamma}
and
\Sigma
is of class
\mathrm{C}^{\ell,\gamma}
, where
\ell\geq 3
and
\gamma\in(0,1)
. The eigensections of
-\Delta^{\perp}
are then of class
\mathrm{C}^{\ell-1,\gamma}
.

Proof.

Let
U
be an eigensection of
-\Delta^{\perp}
with eigenvalue
\lambda
. We consider a family of smooth submanifolds
\Sigma_{s}
which converge to
\Sigma
in
\mathrm{C}^{\ell,\gamma}
as
s\to 0
. Let us write
\Delta_{s}^{\perp}
for the normal Laplacian on
N\Sigma_{s}
and define
E_{s}
to be the span of all the eigensections of
-\Delta_{s}^{\perp}
with eigenvalue in
(\lambda-\delta,\lambda+\delta)
. If
\delta
is small enough then the only element of
\spec(-\Delta^{\perp})
in this interval is
\lambda
. It then follows that
\dim(E_{s})
is constant and equal to
m:=\rank(P_{\lambda})
for small
s
, and
E_{s}
converges to
\im(P_{\lambda})
in the Grassmannian
\mathbf{Gr}_{m}
of
\mathrm{L}^{2}(N\Sigma)
as
s\to 0
.

We now demonstrate that every element of
\im(P_{\lambda})
is of class
\mathrm{C}^{\ell-1,\gamma}
, by arguing that the
\mathrm{C}^{\ell-1,\gamma}
-norm of any normalised eigensection in
E_{s}
is bounded independently of
s
. To that end, let
\tilde{U}
be an eigensection of
-\Delta^{\perp}_{s}
with eigenvalue
\tilde{\lambda}\in(\lambda-\delta,\lambda+\delta)
. Locally, we may choose a frame
N_{a}
for
N\Sigma_{s}
and coordinates
x^{i}
for
\Sigma_{s}
, of class
\mathrm{C}^{\ell-1,\gamma}
and
\mathrm{C}^{\ell,\gamma}
, respectively. Suppressing dependencies on
s
, we then have

\Delta^{\perp}\tilde{U}=(\Delta\tilde{U}^{a})N_{a}+2h^{ij}\frac{\partial\tilde{U}^{a}}{\partial x^{i}}\nabla_{j}^{\perp}N_{a}+\tilde{U}^{a}\Delta^{\perp}N_{a}.

Let us define
q_{ab}=g(N_{a},N_{b})
and write
q^{ab}
for the matrix inverse to
q_{ab}
. Relabelling indices, we see that the eigenvalue equation for
\tilde{U}
is equivalent to the following system of equations for the components
\tilde{U}^{a}
:

\Delta\tilde{U}^{a}+2q^{ac}h^{ij}g(\nabla_{j}^{\perp}N_{b},N_{c})\frac{\partial\tilde{U}^{b}}{\partial x^{i}}+q^{ac}g(\Delta^{\perp}N_{b},N_{c})\tilde{U}^{b}+\tilde{\lambda}\tilde{U}^{a}=0.

Inspection shows that

\|N_{a}\|_{\mathrm{C}^{\ell-1,\gamma}},\qquad\|g(\nabla_{j}^{\perp}N_{b},N_{c})\|_{\mathrm{C}^{\ell-2,\gamma}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\|g(\Delta^{\perp}N_{b},N_{c})\|_{\mathrm{C}^{\ell-3,\gamma}}

are bounded independently of
s
. We conclude that the components
\tilde{U}^{a}
solve a system of the form

h^{ij}\frac{\partial^{2}\tilde{U}^{a}}{\partial x^{i}\partial x^{j}}+B^{ai}_{b}\frac{\partial\tilde{U}^{b}}{\partial x^{i}}+C^{a}_{b}\tilde{U}^{b}=0

where the norms

\|h^{ij}\|_{\mathrm{C}^{\ell-1,\gamma}},\qquad\|B^{ai}_{b}\|_{C^{\ell-2,\gamma}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\|C^{a}_{b}\|_{\mathrm{C}^{\ell-3,\gamma}}

are bounded independently of
s
. Using the Schauder estimates (see e.g.
[
GM12
, Section 5.5]
) and an appropriate covering of
\Sigma_{s}
by small balls we conclude that
\|\tilde{U}\|_{\mathrm{C}^{\ell-1,\gamma}}
is bounded independently of
s
, provided we have a uniform bound for
\sup_{\Sigma}|\tilde{U}|
. Such a bound follows from a standard iteration argument using
\Delta|\tilde{U}|\geq-\tilde{\lambda}|\tilde{U}|
and the Sobolev inequality, provided that we normalise
\|\tilde{U}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}=1
. This completes the proof.
∎

3.
Variation Formulae

Let
(M,g)
be a complete Riemannian
n
-manifold and
\Sigma
a compact submanifold of codimension
k
. Throughout this section we consider a smooth variation of
\Sigma
, given by a smooth family of submanifolds
\Sigma_{s}
such that
\Sigma_{0}=\Sigma
. We parameterize the variation by a family of embeddings

F:\Sigma\times(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\to M

such that
F(\cdot,0)
is the inclusion
\Sigma\hookrightarrow M
. We also assume that
\tfrac{\partial F}{\partial s}(\cdot,0)
is normal to
\Sigma
; while this assumption is not strictly necessary it does simplify some formulae and can always be arranged by composing
F
with an
s
-dependent family of diffeomorphisms of
\Sigma
. Let us write

V:=\frac{\partial F}{\partial s}(\cdot,0).

Let
W=W(x,s)
be a smoothly varying vector field on
\Sigma_{s}
. We define
\nabla_{s}W
to be the covariant derivative of
W
along the curve
s\mapsto F(x,s)
. In case
W(x,s)=\overline{W}(F(x,s))
for some vector field
\overline{W}
on
M
, we have

\nabla_{s}W=\nabla_{\frac{\partial F}{\partial s}}\overline{W}.

When
W(x,s)
is normal to
\Sigma_{s}
for each
s
, we define

\nabla_{s}^{\perp}W=(\nabla_{s}W)^{\perp}.

We recall the first variation formula for the mean curvature.

Lemma 3.1
.

At
s=0
we have

\nabla^{\perp}_{s}H=\Delta^{\perp}V+g(A^{i}_{j},V)A^{j}_{i}+h^{ij}(R(V,X_{i})X_{j})^{\perp}

with respect to any local frame
X_{i}
for
T\Sigma
.

Our goal is to compute the first variation of
(1-Q)(H)
, in the situation where
g
and
\Sigma
are such that
\lambda_{k}(\Sigma,g)<\lambda_{k+1}(\Sigma,g)
. This assumption ensures that for
s
sufficiently small the operator
Q
associated with
\Sigma_{s}
varies smoothly in
s
.

Before proceeding we introduce one more instance of notation. Suppose
W
is a normal vector field on
\Sigma
. We then define

(7)

\Lambda(V,W):=(\nabla_{s}^{\perp}\Delta^{\perp}-\Delta^{\perp}\nabla_{s}^{\perp})\tilde{W}\big|_{s=0}

for an arbitrary extension
\tilde{W}(\cdot,s)
which is smooth, normal to
\Sigma_{s}
, and agrees with
W
when
s=0
. The extension we choose is irrelevant. Indeed, we show in Appendix
A
that
\Lambda(V,W)
is given by an expression in
W
and its first- and second-order spatial derivatives, with coefficients determined by
V
,
\Sigma
and
g
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Suppose
g
and
\Sigma
are such that
\lambda_{k}(\Sigma,g)<\lambda_{k+1}(\Sigma,g)
. Let
W=W(\cdot,s)
be a smooth vector field normal to
\Sigma_{s}
. When
s=0
we have

\displaystyle\nabla_{s}^{\perp}Q(W)

\displaystyle=Q(\nabla_{s}^{\perp}W)+\sum_{p>k}\sum_{m=1}^{k}\frac{1}{\lambda_{p}-\lambda_{m}}\langle(1-Q)(W),U_{p}\rangle_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}\langle\Lambda(V,U_{m}),U_{p}\rangle_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}U_{m}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{p>k}\sum_{m=1}^{k}\frac{1}{\lambda_{p}-\lambda_{m}}\langle Q(W),U_{m}\rangle_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}\langle\Lambda(V,U_{m}),U_{p}\rangle_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}U_{p}

\displaystyle\qquad-\sum_{m=1}^{k}\bigg(\int_{\Sigma}g((1-Q)(W),U_{m})g(H,V)\dvoll_{\Sigma}\bigg)U_{m}

where
U_{m}
is an
\mathrm{L}^{2}
-orthonormal basis of eigensections for
-\Delta^{\perp}
at
s=0
, labelled so that the eigenvalues
\lambda_{m}
are nondecreasing in
m
.

Proof.

Let
U_{m}=U_{m}(\cdot,s)\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}(N\Sigma_{s})
be an orthonormal basis for for
\mathrm{L}^{2}(N\Sigma_{s})
which varies smoothly in
s
. We assume that, for each
s
,
\{U_{m}\}_{m=1}^{k}
spans
\im(Q)
and
\{U_{m}\}_{m=k+1}^{\infty}
spans
\ker(Q)
. In addition, we assume that when
s=0
each
U_{m}
is an eigensection for
-\Delta^{\perp}
, labelled so that the corresponding eigenvalues
\lambda_{m}
are nondecreasing in
m
. (Note that we cannot in general demand that the
U_{m}
are eigensections for every
s
, since they might then fail to vary smoothly in
s
.)

We have

(8)

Q=\sum_{m=1}^{k}\langle\cdot,U_{m}\rangle_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}U_{m},

and hence

\displaystyle\nabla_{s}^{\perp}Q(W)

\displaystyle=Q(\nabla_{s}^{\perp}W)+\sum_{m=1}^{k}\langle W,\nabla_{s}^{\perp}U_{m}\rangle_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}U_{m}+\sum_{m=1}^{k}\langle W,U_{m}\rangle_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}\nabla_{s}^{\perp}U_{m}

\displaystyle\qquad-\sum_{m=1}^{k}\bigg(\int_{\Sigma_{s}}g(W,U_{m})g(H,\tfrac{\partial F}{\partial s})\dvoll_{\Sigma_{s}}\bigg)U_{m}.

Differentiating the orthogonality condition

(9)

\langle U_{m},U_{p}\rangle_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}=\delta_{mp}

with respect to
s
yields

(10)

0=\langle\nabla_{s}^{\perp}U_{m},U_{p}\rangle_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}+\langle U_{m},\nabla_{s}^{\perp}U_{p}\rangle_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}-\int_{\Sigma_{s}}g(U_{m},U_{p})g(H,\tfrac{\partial F}{\partial s})\dvoll_{\Sigma_{s}}.

Multiplying this identity by
\langle W,U_{p}\rangle_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}U_{m}
, summing over both indices (from 1 to
k
) and using (
8
), we see that that

(11)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{k}\langle W,Q(\nabla_{s}^{\perp}U_{m})\rangle_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}U_{m}+\sum_{m=1}^{k}\langle W,U_{m}\rangle_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}Q(\nabla_{s}^{\perp}U_{m})

\displaystyle-\sum_{m=1}^{k}\bigg(\int_{\Sigma_{s}}g(U_{m},Q(W))g(H,\tfrac{\partial F}{\partial s})\dvoll_{\Sigma_{s}}\bigg)U_{m}

and hence

\displaystyle\nabla_{s}^{\perp}Q(W)

\displaystyle=Q(\nabla_{s}^{\perp}W)+\sum_{m=1}^{k}\langle W,(1-Q)(\nabla_{s}^{\perp}U_{m})\rangle_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}U_{m}+\sum_{m=1}^{k}\langle W,U_{m}\rangle_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}(1-Q)(\nabla_{s}^{\perp}U_{m})

(12)

\displaystyle\qquad-\sum_{m=1}^{k}\bigg(\int_{\Sigma_{s}}g((1-Q)(W),U_{m})g(H,\tfrac{\partial F}{\partial s})\dvoll_{\Sigma_{s}}\bigg)U_{m}.

We now rewrite the terms
(1-Q)(\nabla_{s}^{\perp}U_{m})
for
m\leq k
. Consider any
p>k
, so that
U_{p}
lies in
\ker(Q)
. We first observe that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{ds}\langle\Delta^{\perp}U_{m},U_{p}\rangle_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\langle\nabla_{s}^{\perp}\Delta^{\perp}U_{m},U_{p}\rangle_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}+\langle\Delta^{\perp}U_{m},\nabla_{s}^{\perp}U_{p}\rangle_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad-\int_{\Sigma_{s}}g(\Delta^{\perp}U_{m},U_{p})g(H,\tfrac{\partial F}{\partial s})\dvoll_{\Sigma_{s}}.

Since
U_{m}\in\im(Q)
we have
\Delta^{\perp}U_{m}\in\im(Q)
, whereas
U_{p}\in\ker(Q)
, so the left-hand side is zero and we are left with

\langle\nabla_{s}^{\perp}\Delta^{\perp}U_{m},U_{p}\rangle_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}=-\langle\Delta^{\perp}U_{m},\nabla_{s}^{\perp}U_{p}\rangle_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}+\int_{\Sigma_{s}}g(\Delta^{\perp}U_{m},U_{p})g(H,\tfrac{\partial F}{\partial s})\dvoll_{\Sigma_{s}},

which may also be expressed as

\displaystyle\langle(\nabla_{s}^{\perp}\Delta^{\perp}-\Delta^{\perp}\nabla_{s}^{\perp})U_{m},U_{p}\rangle_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle=-\langle\nabla_{s}^{\perp}U_{m},\Delta^{\perp}U_{p}\rangle_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}-\langle\Delta^{\perp}U_{m},\nabla_{s}^{\perp}U_{p}\rangle_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{\Sigma_{s}}g(\Delta^{\perp}U_{m},U_{p})g(H,V)\dvoll_{\Sigma_{s}}.

Evaluating this identity at
s=0
gives

\displaystyle\langle\Lambda(V,U_{m}),U_{p}\rangle_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{p}\langle\nabla_{s}^{\perp}U_{m},U_{p}\rangle_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}+\lambda_{m}\langle U_{m},\nabla_{s}^{\perp}U_{p}\rangle_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad-\lambda_{m}\int_{\Sigma}g(U_{m},U_{p})g(H,V)\dvoll_{\Sigma},

and after inserting (
10
) we arrive at

\displaystyle\langle\Lambda(V,U_{m}),U_{p}\rangle_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle=(\lambda_{p}-\lambda_{m})\langle\nabla_{s}^{\perp}U_{m},U_{p}\rangle_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}

Since this holds for every
p>k
we conclude that, when
s=0
,

(1-Q)(\nabla^{\perp}_{s}U_{m})=\sum_{p>k}\frac{1}{\lambda_{p}-\lambda_{m}}\langle\Lambda(V,U_{m}),U_{p}\rangle_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}U_{p}.

Inserting the previous identity into (
12
) shows that when
s=0
we have

\displaystyle\nabla_{s}^{\perp}Q(W)

\displaystyle=Q(\nabla_{s}^{\perp}W)+\sum_{p>k}\sum_{m=1}^{k}\frac{1}{\lambda_{p}-\lambda_{m}}\langle W,U_{p}\rangle_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}\langle\Lambda(V,U_{m}),U_{p}\rangle_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}U_{m}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{p>k}\sum_{m=1}^{k}\frac{1}{\lambda_{p}-\lambda_{m}}\langle W,U_{m}\rangle_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}\langle\Lambda(V,U_{m}),U_{p}\rangle_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}U_{p}

\displaystyle\qquad-\sum_{m=1}^{k}\bigg(\int_{\Sigma}g((1-Q)(W),U_{m})g(H,V)\dvoll_{\Sigma}\bigg)U_{m}.

In the first two sums on the right,
W
may be replaced with
(1-Q)(W)
and
Q(W)
respectively. This completes the proof.
∎

We have the following consequence of Lemma
3.1
and Proposition
3.2
.

Corollary 3.3
.

Suppose
g
and
\Sigma
are such that
\lambda_{k}(\Sigma,g)<\lambda_{k+1}(\Sigma,g)
. We assume in addition that
\Sigma
has QPMC, i.e.,
(1-Q)(H)=0
. When
s=0
we then have

\displaystyle\nabla_{s}^{\perp}(1-Q)(H)

\displaystyle=(1-Q)(\Delta^{\perp}V+g(A^{i}_{k},V)A^{k}_{i}+h^{ij}(R(V,X_{i})X_{j})^{\perp})

(13)

\displaystyle\qquad-\sum_{p>k}\sum_{m=1}^{k}\frac{1}{\lambda_{p}-\lambda_{m}}\langle Q(H),U_{m}\rangle_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}\langle\Lambda(V,U_{m}),U_{p}\rangle_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}U_{p},

where
U_{m}
is an
\mathrm{L}^{2}
-orthonormal basis of eigensections for
-\Delta^{\perp}
at
s=0
, labelled so that the eigenvalues
\lambda_{m}
are nondecreasing in
m
.

If
M=\mathbb{R}^{k}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-k}
,
g=g_{0}
and
\Sigma=\{z\}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-k}
, the formula (
13
) simplifies considerably. Indeed,
\Sigma
is then totally geodesic and in particular minimal, so when
s=0
we have

\nabla_{s}^{\perp}(1-Q)(H)=(1-Q)(\nabla_{s}^{\perp}H).

Moreover, the first variation of
H
reduces to

\nabla_{s}^{\perp}H=\Delta^{\perp}V,

so that, when
s=0
,

\nabla_{s}^{\perp}(1-Q)(H)=(1-Q)(\Delta^{\perp}V).

Given that
1-Q
is simply the projection onto
\im(\Delta^{\perp})
in this particular case, we have:

Corollary 3.4
.

If
M=\mathbb{R}^{k}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-k}
,
g=g_{0}
and
\Sigma=\{z\}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-k}
for some
z\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
, then

\nabla_{s}^{\perp}(1-Q)(H)=\Delta^{\perp}V

when
s=0
.

4.
Foliation of
\mathbb{R}^{k}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-k}
by QPMC spheres

Let
M
denote the cylinder
\mathbb{R}^{k}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-k}
, where
n\geq 2
and
1\leq k\leq n-1
. Points in
M
will be written as
(z,\omega)
. Let
g_{\mathbb{S}^{n-k}}
be the round metric on
\mathbb{S}^{n-k}
with sectional curvatures equal to one. Recall the notation

g_{0}:=g_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\oplus g_{\mathbb{S}^{n-k}}.

We consider an integer
\ell\geq 3
and real number
\gamma\in(0,1)
. We write
\mathcal{G}
for the space of Riemannian metrics on
M
of class
\mathrm{C}^{\ell-1,\gamma}
. Note that
\mathcal{G}
is an open cone in the Banach space
\mathrm{C}^{\ell-1,\gamma}(T^{*}M\otimes T^{*}M)
.

Each
u\in\mathrm{C}^{\ell,\gamma}(\mathbb{S}^{n-k};\mathbb{R}^{k})
gives rise to a graphical codimension-
k
submanifold of
M
, namely

\Sigma_{u}:=\{(z,\omega):z=u(\omega)\}.

We consider
\Sigma_{u}
to be identified with
\mathbb{S}^{n-k}
via the embedding
(u(\omega),\omega)
. Given such a graphical submanifold
\Sigma_{u}
, and a metric
g
on
M
, we write
N_{a}^{g,u}
for the orthogonal projection of
\partial_{z^{a}}
onto
N\Sigma_{u}
. For any choice of
g
and
u
, the sections
\{N_{a}^{g,u}\}_{a=1}^{k}
are a frame for
N\Sigma_{u}
. We denote by
\dvoll_{g,u}
the induced volume form on
\Sigma_{u}
. For each ambient metric
g
, we write
H_{g,u}
for the mean curvature vector of
\Sigma_{u}
with respect to
g
. The projection onto the space of quasi-parallel sections of
N\Sigma_{u}
is given by

Q_{g,u}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\in\spec(-\Delta^{\perp}_{g,u})\\
\lambda<\lambda_{k+1}\end{subarray}}P_{g,u,\lambda},

where the operators
P_{g,u,\lambda}
are the projections onto the eigenspaces of
\Delta^{\perp}_{g,u}
.

We aim to find, for each metric
g\in\mathcal{G}
which is sufficiently close to
g_{0}
, a map
u:\mathbb{S}^{n-k}\to\mathbb{R}^{k}
such that
H_{g,u}
is quasi-parallel, i.e.,

(1-Q_{g,u})(H_{g,u})=0.

This will be achieved using the implicit function theorem, applied to a suitable mapping, which we now construct.

We begin by introducing a frame for
N\Sigma_{u}
which consists of elements of
\im(Q_{g,u})
, namely

E_{a}^{g,u}:=Q_{g,u}(N_{a}^{g,u}).

Lemma 4.1
.

If
\|g-g_{0}\|_{\mathrm{C}^{\ell-1,\gamma}}
and
\|u\|_{\mathrm{C}^{\ell,\gamma}}
are both sufficiently small then
\{E_{a}^{g,u}\}_{a=1}^{k}
is a global frame for
N\Sigma_{u}
.

Proof.

The projection
Q_{g,u}
varies continuously as
g
and
u
vary continuously in a neighbourhood of
(g_{0},0)
. Moreover, the operator
Q_{g_{0},0}
acts by the identity on the subspace spanned by
\{N^{g_{0},0}_{a}\}_{a=1}^{k}
. The claim follows.
∎

We henceforth restrict attention to
g
and
u
such that Lemma
4.1
holds.

Definition 4.2
.

We define

J:\mathcal{G}\times\mathrm{C}^{\ell,\gamma}(\mathbb{S}^{n-k};\mathbb{R}^{k})\to\mathrm{C}^{\ell-2,\gamma}(\mathbb{S}^{n-k};\mathbb{R}^{k})

such that
J(g,u)
has components

J^{a}(g,u)=g((1-Q_{g,u})(H_{g,u}),E^{g,u}_{a})f_{g,u},

where
f_{g,u}:\mathbb{S}^{n-k}\to\mathbb{R}
is the function determined by

\dvoll_{g,u}=f_{g,u}\dvoll_{\mathbb{S}^{n-k}}.

In defining
J
as a map into
\mathrm{C}^{\ell-2,\gamma}
we have implicitly used the following facts: firstly, the mean curvature of a
\mathrm{C}^{\ell,\gamma}
-submanifold is in
\mathrm{C}^{\ell-2,\gamma}
and, secondly, for
u\in\mathrm{C}^{\ell,\gamma}
the projection
Q
maps
\mathrm{C}^{\ell-2,\gamma}(N\Sigma_{u})
into itself by Lemma
2.1
.

In light of Lemma
4.1
, we have that
J(g,u)=0
if and only if
H_{g,u}
is quasi-parallel. Note also that
J(g_{0},0)=0
. We will solve the equation
J(g,u)=0
in a neighbourhood of
(g_{0},0)
using the implicit function theorem. As it stands, the linearisation

DJ_{(g_{0},0)}(0,\cdot):\mathrm{C}^{\ell,\gamma}(\mathbb{S}^{n-k};\mathbb{R}^{k})\to\mathrm{C}^{\ell-2,\gamma}(\mathbb{S}^{n-k};\mathbb{R}^{k})

fails to be invertible. Indeed, one can show (see Lemma
4.4
below) that

DJ_{(g_{0},0)}(0,\cdot)=\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{n-k}},

which has
k
-dimensional kernel and cokernel consisting of the constant maps from
\mathbb{S}^{n-k}
into
\mathbb{R}^{k}
. Geometrically, this failure of invertibility arises because the isometry group of
g_{0}
contains the
k
-dimensional space of translations acting on
\mathbb{R}^{k}
.

The first step towards resolving this issue is to observe that
J
actually takes values in the codimension-
k
subspace of
\mathrm{C}^{\ell-2,\gamma}(\mathbb{S}^{n-k};\mathbb{R}^{k})
consisting of maps with mean-zero components. This of course is by design and explains the introduction of the frame
E^{g,u}_{a}
and weight
f_{g,u}
in Definition
4.2
. Let us define, for each
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
,

\mathring{\mathrm{C}}^{\ell,\gamma}(\mathbb{S}^{n-k};\mathbb{R}^{k}):=\bigg\{u\in\mathrm{C}^{\ell,\gamma}(\mathbb{S}^{n-k};\mathbb{R}^{k}):\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-k}}u^{a}\,\dvoll_{\mathbb{S}^{n-k}}=0\;\forall\;a\in\{1,\dotsc,k\}\bigg\}.

Lemma 4.3
.

The map
J
sends
\mathcal{G}\times\mathrm{C}^{\ell,\gamma}(\mathbb{S}^{n-k};\mathbb{R}^{k})
to
\mathring{\mathrm{C}}^{\ell-2,\gamma}(\mathbb{S}^{n-k};\mathbb{R}^{k})
.

Proof.

Fix
g\in\mathcal{G}
and
u\in\mathrm{C}^{\ell,\gamma}(\mathbb{S}^{n-k};\mathbb{R}^{k})
. The claim is that for each
a\in\{1,\dotsc,k\}
we have

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-k}}J^{a}(g,u)\,\dvoll_{\mathbb{S}^{n-k}}=0.

Unpacking the definition of
J^{a}(g,u)
gives

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-k}}J^{a}(g,u)\,\dvoll_{\mathbb{S}^{n-k}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-k}}g((1-Q)(H),E_{a})f_{g,u}\dvoll_{\mathbb{S}^{n-k}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-k}}g((1-Q)(H),E_{a})\dvoll_{g,u}

\displaystyle=\langle(1-Q)(H),E_{a}\rangle_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(N\Sigma_{u})}.

Recall that
E_{a}
lies in
\im(Q)
. The claim follows, since the images of
1-Q
and
Q
are orthogonal subspaces of
\mathrm{L}^{2}(N\Sigma_{u})
.
∎

To account for the nontrivial kernel of
DJ_{(g_{0},0)}(0,\cdot)
we only consider submanifolds
\Sigma_{u}
which are appropriately centered about
\{0\}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-k}
. That is, we henceforth consider
J
to be defined as before, but with its domain restricted, so that

J:\mathcal{G}\times\mathring{\mathrm{C}}^{\ell,\gamma}(\mathbb{S}^{n-k};\mathbb{R}^{k})\to\mathring{\mathrm{C}}^{\ell-2,\gamma}(\mathbb{S}^{n-k};\mathbb{R}^{k}).

We now compute the linearisation of
J
at
(g_{0},0)
using the variation formulae derived in Section
3
.

Lemma 4.4
.

The linearisation

DJ_{(g_{0},0)}(0,\cdot):\mathring{\mathrm{C}}^{\ell,\gamma}(\mathbb{S}^{n-k};\mathbb{R}^{k})\to\mathring{\mathrm{C}}^{\ell-2,\gamma}(\mathbb{S}^{n-k};\mathbb{R}^{k})

is given by

DJ_{(g_{0},0)}(0,\varphi)=\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{n-k}}\varphi.

Proof.

It suffices to prove the claim when
\varphi
is smooth, since the general case then follows by approximation. Consider the smooth variation of
\{0\}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-k}
given by

F(\omega,s)=(s\varphi(\omega),\omega),\qquad s\in(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon).

With respect to
g_{0}
we have

\frac{\partial F}{\partial s}(\cdot,0)=\varphi^{a}\partial_{z^{a}}.

We need to evaluate

\frac{\partial}{\partial s}J^{a}(g_{0},s\varphi)=\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\Big(g_{0}((1-Q)(H),E_{a})f\Big)

at
s=0
. Here and in the computation that follows we often suppress dependencies on the ambient metric and
s
, simply writing
H
for
H_{g_{0},s\varphi}
, and so on.

We first expand and use the first variation formula for the volume form of a submanifold to obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial s}J^{a}(g_{0},s\varphi)

\displaystyle=g_{0}(\nabla_{s}^{\perp}(1-Q)(H),E_{a})f+g_{0}((1-Q)(H),\nabla_{s}^{\perp}E_{a})f

\displaystyle\qquad-g_{0}((1-Q)(H),E_{a})g_{0}(H,\tfrac{\partial F}{\partial s})f.

When
s=0
we have
E_{a}=\partial_{z^{a}}
,
H=0
and
f=1
, so this reduces to

\frac{\partial}{\partial s}J^{a}(g_{0},s\varphi)=g_{0}(\nabla_{s}^{\perp}(1-Q)(H),\partial_{z^{a}}).

By Corollary
3.4
we know that

\nabla^{\perp}_{s}(1-Q)(H)=\Delta^{\perp}\frac{\partial F}{\partial s}=\Delta\varphi^{a}\partial_{z^{a}}

when
s=0
. The claim follows.
∎

Lemma
4.4
and standard results in linear elliptic theory give the following.

Lemma 4.5
.

The linearisation

DJ_{(g_{0},0)}(0,\cdot):\mathring{\mathrm{C}}^{\ell,\gamma}(\mathbb{S}^{n-k};\mathbb{R}^{k})\to\mathring{\mathrm{C}}^{\ell-2,\gamma}(\mathbb{S}^{n-k};\mathbb{R}^{k})

is invertible.

With all of these preparations concluded, we may now proceed with the main result of this section.

Proposition 4.6
.

For every
\ell\geqslant 3
and
\gamma\in(0,1)
there are constants
\delta_{0}>0
and
\varepsilon>0
which depend only on
n
,
\ell
and
\gamma
and have the following property. If
\|g-g_{0}\|_{\mathrm{C}^{\ell-1,\gamma}}\leq\varepsilon
then for every
z\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
there exists a unique

u_{\star}=u_{\star}(z,g)\in\{u\in\mathring{\mathrm{C}}^{\ell,\gamma}(\mathbb{S}^{n-k};\mathbb{R}^{k}):\|u\|_{\mathrm{C}^{\ell,\gamma}}\leq\delta_{0}\}

such that the graphical submanifold
\Sigma_{z+u_{\star}}
has QPMC with respect to
g
. Moreover, there is a constant
C=C(n,\ell,\gamma)
such that

\|u_{\star}(z,g)\|_{\mathrm{C}^{\ell,\gamma}}\leq C\|g-g_{0}\|_{\mathrm{C}^{\ell-1,\gamma}}.

Proof.

We first perturb
\{0\}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-k}
to a QPMC sphere. This is easily achieved using Lemma
4.5
and the implicit function theorem. Indeed, by those two results, there are positive constants
\delta_{0}
and
\varepsilon
, each of which depends only on
n
,
\ell
and
\gamma
, with the following property. If
\|g-g_{0}\|_{\mathrm{C}^{\ell-1,\gamma}}\leq\varepsilon
then for every
z\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
there exists a unique

u_{\star}=u_{\star}(0,g)\in\{u\in\mathring{\mathrm{C}}^{\ell,\gamma}(\mathbb{S}^{n-k};\mathbb{R}^{k}):\|u\|_{\mathrm{C}^{\ell,\gamma}}\leq\delta_{0}\}

which solves

J(g,u_{\star}(0,g))=0.

The implicit function theorem also provides the estimate

(14)

\|u_{\star}(0,g)\|_{\mathrm{C}^{\ell,\gamma}}\leq C(n,\ell,\gamma)\|g-g_{0}\|_{\mathrm{C}^{\ell-1,\gamma}}.

Recall that
J(g,u_{\star}(0,g))=0
implies
\Sigma_{u_{\star}(0,g)}
has QPMC with respect to
g
.

Now consider an arbitrary
z_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
. Let
\tau_{z_{0}}:M\to M
denote the translation
(z,\omega)\mapsto(z+z_{0},\omega)
. We then have

J(\tau^{*}_{z_{0}}g,u)=J(g,z_{0}+u)

for every
u
. For each
g\in\mathcal{G}
such that
\|g-g_{0}\|_{\mathrm{C}^{\ell-1,\gamma}}\leq\varepsilon
, we set

u_{\star}(z_{0},g):=u_{\star}(0,\tau^{*}_{z_{0}}g).

It follows that

0=J(\tau^{*}_{z_{0}}g,u_{\star})=J(g,z_{0}+u_{\star}),

and hence
\Sigma_{z_{0}+u_{\star}(z_{0},g)}
has QPMC with respect to
g
. Moreover,
u_{\star}
is the unique element of

\{u\in\mathring{\mathrm{C}}^{\ell,\gamma}(\mathbb{S}^{n-k};\mathbb{R}^{k}):\|u\|_{\mathrm{C}^{\ell,\gamma}}\leq\delta_{0}\}

which has this property. Finally, by (
14
) we have

\|u_{\star}(z_{0},g)\|_{\mathrm{C}^{\ell,\gamma}}=\|u_{\star}(0,\tau^{*}_{z_{0}}g)\|_{\mathrm{C}^{\ell,\gamma}}\leq C\|\tau^{*}_{z_{0}}g-g_{0}\|_{\mathrm{C}^{\ell-1,\gamma}}=C\|g-g_{0}\|_{\mathrm{C}^{\ell-1,\gamma}}.

∎

Next we demonstrate that if
g
is smooth then a graphical submanifold with QPMC is smooth.

Proposition 4.7
.

If
g
is smooth and
u\in{\mathrm{C}}^{3,\gamma}(\mathbb{S}^{n-k};\mathbb{R}^{k})
is such that

(1-Q_{g,u})(H_{g,u})=0,

then
u\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-k};\mathbb{R}^{k})
.

Proof.

The QPMC equation

H=Q(H)

asserts that
H
is a linear combination of finitely many eigensections of
-\Delta^{\perp}
. If
u
is of class
\mathrm{C}^{\ell,\gamma}
for some
\ell\geq 3
then each of these eigensections is of class
\mathrm{C}^{\ell-1,\gamma}
by Lemma
2.1
. Moreover, in appropriate local coordinates,
H
is given by a uniformly elliptic quasilinear operator acting on
u
, whose coefficients are of class
\mathrm{C}^{\ell-1,\gamma}
if
u\in\mathrm{C}^{\ell,\gamma}
. Therefore, the Schauder estimates (see e.g.
[
GM12
, Section 5.5]
) imply that if
u\in\mathrm{C}^{\ell,\gamma}
then
u\in\mathrm{C}^{\ell+1,\gamma}
. A standard bootstrapping argument thus shows that
u
is smooth if it is assumed to be of class
\mathrm{C}^{3,\gamma}
.
∎

We may now prove that the submanifolds
\Sigma_{z+u_{\star}}
form a smooth foliation of
M
as
z
ranges over
\mathbb{R}^{k}
.

Corollary 4.8
.

Suppose we are in the same setting as in Proposition
4.6
. If
g
is smooth and
\varepsilon
is sufficiently small then the map
\Phi_{g}:M\to M
given by

\Phi_{g}(z,\omega):=(z+u_{\star}(z,g)(\omega),\omega)

is a diffeomorphism. In particular, the submanifolds

\Sigma_{z+u_{\star}(z,g)}=\Phi_{g}(\{z\}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-k})

form a smooth foliation of
M
.

Proof.

The assignment

g\mapsto u_{\star}(0,g)

is a smooth map from
\mathcal{G}
to
\mathring{\mathrm{C}}^{\ell,\gamma}(\mathbb{S}^{n-k};\mathbb{R}^{k})
and, since we are assuming
g
is smooth,

z\mapsto\tau^{*}_{z}g

is a smooth map from
\mathbb{R}^{k}
into
\mathcal{G}
. Combining these facts with Lemma
4.7
, we see that

(z,\omega)\mapsto u_{\star}(z,g)(\omega)

is a smooth map from
M
to
\mathbb{R}^{k}
, and hence
\Phi
is smooth. By Proposition
4.6
we have

\|\Phi_{g}-\Id\|_{\mathrm{C}^{1}(M)}\leq C\varepsilon.

Therefore, if
\varepsilon
is sufficiently small then
\Phi_{g}
is a diffeomorphism.
∎

We conclude this section with the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

The existence component of the theorem follows immediately from Proposition
4.6
and Corollary
4.8
. To prove the uniqueness component, suppose
\Sigma
is an embedded
(n-k)
-sphere in
M
which has QPMC and is a graph,

\Sigma=\{(z+u(\omega),\omega):\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{n-k}\},

such that

\|u\|_{\mathrm{C}^{\ell,\gamma}}\leq\delta.

Defining

\bar{u}:=\frac{1}{|\mathbb{S}^{n-k}|}\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-k}}u^{1}\,\dvoll_{\mathbb{S}^{n-k}},\dots,\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-k}}u^{k}\,\dvoll_{\mathbb{S}^{n-k}}\bigg),

we have
u-\bar{u}\in\mathring{\mathrm{C}}^{\ell,\gamma}(\mathbb{S}^{n-k};\mathbb{R}^{k})
and
\|u-\bar{u}\|_{\mathrm{C}^{\ell,\gamma}}\leq 2\delta
. If
\varepsilon
and
\delta
are both sufficiently small then, by Proposition
4.6
, we have

u-\bar{u}=u_{\star}(z+\bar{u},g),

which means
\Sigma
is a leaf of the foliation provided by Proposition
4.6
. This completes the proof.
∎

5.
Uniqueness and local foliations

Let
g
be a smooth metric on
M:=\mathbb{R}^{k}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-k}
. We write
\mathscr{S}_{g}(\delta)
for the class of all embedded
(n-k)
-spheres in
M
which are
\delta
-vertical in the sense of Definition
1.5
. Note that when
g
is close to
g_{0}
the scale
\bar{r}
appearing in that definition is close to
1
.

Before proceeding with the main results of this section we establish some lemmas. First, elements of
\mathscr{S}_{g}(\delta)
are graphical.

Lemma 5.1
.

There are positive constants
\varepsilon
and
\delta
, depending only on
n
, with the following property. Let
g
be a metric on
M
such that
\|g-g_{0}\|_{\mathrm{C}^{3}}\leq\varepsilon
, and suppose
\Sigma\in\mathscr{S}_{g}(\delta)
. Then
\Sigma
is the graph of a function
u:\mathbb{S}^{n-k}\to\mathbb{R}^{k}
. Moreover, there is a constant
C=C(n)
such that

(15)

\|du\|_{C^{3}(\mathbb{S}^{n-k})}\leq C\bigg(\|g-g_{0}\|_{C^{3}}+\sum_{\ell=0}^{2}\sup_{\Sigma}|(\nabla^{\perp})^{\ell}A_{\Sigma}|\bigg).

Proof.

We first claim that
\Sigma
is graphical. Let us write
q_{ab}
for the matrix
g(\partial_{z^{a}}^{\perp},\partial_{z^{b}}^{\perp})
, where
1\leq a,b\leq k
. It suffices to show that
\det(q_{ab})>0
at every point of
\Sigma
. Let us define

u^{a}:=z^{a}|_{\Sigma}.

We insert

\grad_{\Sigma}u^{a}=(\grad z^{a})^{\top}=\grad z^{a}-(\grad z^{a})^{\perp}

into the identity

\Hess_{\Sigma}u(X,Y)=h(\nabla^{\top}_{X}(\grad_{\Sigma}u),Y)=g(\nabla_{X}(\grad_{\Sigma}u),Y),

in order to obtain

(16)

\Hess_{\Sigma}u^{a}(\cdot,\cdot)=\Hess z^{a}(\cdot,\cdot)+g(A_{\Sigma}(\cdot,\cdot),(\grad z^{a})^{\perp}).

We then deduce the estimate

(17)

\sup_{\Sigma}|\Hess_{\Sigma}u^{a}|\leq C(\|g-g_{0}\|_{\mathrm{C}^{1}}+\sup_{\Sigma}|A_{\Sigma}|).

There exists a maximal point for
u^{a}
in
\Sigma
, at which
\grad_{\Sigma}u^{a}=0
. Using the previous inequality and integrating along geodesics from this maximal point, we obtain

(18)

\sup_{\Sigma}|\grad_{\Sigma}u^{a}|\leq C(\|g-g_{0}\|_{\mathrm{C}^{1}}+\sup_{\Sigma}|A_{\Sigma}|),

where we have used the
\delta
-vertical condition to absorb
\diam_{g}(\Sigma)
into
C
. We combine this inequality with the identity

g((\grad z^{a})^{\perp},(\grad z^{b})^{\perp})=g(\grad z^{a},\grad z^{b})-g(\grad_{\Sigma}u^{a},\grad_{\Sigma}u^{b}).

Up to errors which can be made arbitrarily small by choosing
\varepsilon
and
\delta
sufficiently small, the left-hand side is
q_{ab}
and the right-hand side is
\delta_{ab}
. Consequently,
\det(q_{ab})>0
if
\varepsilon
and
\delta
are sufficiently small.

We may therefore express
\Sigma
as a graph over
\mathbb{S}^{n-k}
. Conflating
u^{a}
with its pullback to
\mathbb{S}^{n-k}
, we have

\Sigma=\{(u(\omega),\omega):\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{n-k}\}.

The estimates (
17
) and (
18
) imply

\|du\|_{\mathrm{C}^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-k})}\leq C(\|g-g_{0}\|_{\mathrm{C}^{1}}+\sup_{\Sigma}|A_{\Sigma}|).

By differentiating (
16
) twice we get additional estimates for the third and fourth derivatives of
u
, such that (
15
) holds.
∎

The next statement is a kind of converse to Lemma
5.1
, asserting that sufficiently controlled graphs are in
\mathscr{S}_{g}(\delta)
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Consider a smooth map
u:\mathbb{S}^{n-k}\to\mathbb{R}^{k}
and let
\Sigma_{u}
denote its graph. Given
\delta>0
, if
\|g-g_{0}\|_{\mathrm{C}^{3}}
and
\|du\|_{\mathrm{C}^{3}(\mathbb{S}^{n-k})}
are sufficiently small (depending only on
n
and
\delta)
then
\Sigma_{u}\in\mathscr{S}_{g}(\delta)
.

Proof.

Consider a map
u:\mathbb{S}^{n-k}\to\mathbb{R}^{k}
and let
\Sigma
denote the graph of
u
. If
\|g-g_{0}\|_{\mathrm{C}^{0}}
and
\|du\|_{\mathrm{C}^{0}(\mathbb{S}^{n-k})}
are both small then the diameter of
\Sigma
is close to
\pi
. The identity

(19)

g(A_{\Sigma}(\cdot,\cdot),(\grad z^{a})^{\perp})=-\Hess z^{a}(\cdot,\cdot)+\Hess_{\Sigma}u^{a}(\cdot,\cdot)

implies

\sup_{\Sigma}|A_{\Sigma}|\leq C(\|g-g_{0}\|_{\mathrm{C}^{1}}+\|du\|_{\mathrm{C}^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-k})}).

Differentiating (
19
) twice leads to an estimate of the form

\sum_{\ell=0}^{2}\sup_{\Sigma}|(\nabla^{\perp})^{\ell}A_{\Sigma}|\leq C(\|g-g_{0}\|_{C^{3}}+\|du\|_{C^{3}(\mathbb{S}^{n-k})}),

so if
\|g-g_{0}\|_{\mathrm{C}^{3}}
and
\|du\|_{\mathrm{C}^{3}(\mathbb{S}^{n-k})}
are small then
\Sigma\in\mathscr{S}_{g}(\delta)
.
∎

Recall that Proposition
4.6
and Corollary
4.8
established the existence of a QPMC foliation for metrics
g
close to
g_{0}
, given by
\Sigma_{z+u_{\star}(z,g)}
for
z\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
. We henceforth refer to this as the canonical foliation for
g
.

If
g
is sufficiently close to
g_{0}
then the leaves of its canonical foliation are in
\mathscr{S}_{g}(\delta)
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Given
\delta>0
there exists
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(n,\delta)
such that if
\|g-g_{0}\|_{\mathrm{C}^{4}}\leq\varepsilon
then the leaves of the canonical foliation for
g
are in
\mathscr{S}_{g}(\delta)
.

Proof.

For each leaf
\Sigma_{z+u_{\star}}
we have

\|du_{\star}\|_{\mathrm{C}^{3}(\mathbb{S}^{n-k})}\leq C\|g-g_{0}\|_{\mathrm{C}^{4}}\leq C\varepsilon,

by Proposition
4.6
. Therefore, if
\varepsilon
is small then Lemma
5.2
guarantees that
\Sigma_{z+u_{\star}}\in\mathscr{S}_{g}(\delta)
.
∎

The next lemma strengthens the uniqueness statement in Proposition
4.6
.

Lemma 5.4
.

There are positive constants
\varepsilon
and
\delta
, depending only on
n
, with the following property. Let
g
be a metric on
M
such that
\|g-g_{0}\|_{\mathrm{C}^{3}}\leq\varepsilon
and suppose
\Sigma\in\mathscr{S}_{g}(\delta)
has QPMC. Then
\Sigma
belongs to the canonical foliation for
g
.

Proof.

By Lemma
5.1
, we may assume
\delta
is sufficiently small so that
\Sigma
is the graph of a smooth map
u:\mathbb{S}^{n-k}\to\mathbb{R}^{k}
. We define

z:=\frac{1}{|\mathbb{S}^{n-k}|}\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-k}}u^{1}\,\dvoll_{\mathbb{S}^{n-k}},\dots,\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-k}}u^{k}\,\dvoll_{\mathbb{S}^{n-k}}\bigg),

so that the components of
u-z
are mean-zero functions. The derivative bounds for
u
in Lemma
5.1
then imply

\|u-z\|_{\mathrm{C}^{4}(\mathbb{S}^{n-k})}\leq C(\delta+\varepsilon).

If
\delta
and
\varepsilon
are both small then by the uniqueness statement in Proposition
4.6
we conclude that

u-z=u_{\star}(0,\tau_{z}^{*}g)=u_{\star}(z,g).

The claim follows.
∎

We are now prepared to prove a local version of Theorem
1.2
.

Proposition 5.5
.

For every
\delta>0
there exists a constant
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(n,\delta)
with the following property. Let
g
be a smooth Riemannian metric on
B^{k}(0,L)\times\mathbb{S}^{n-k}
, where
L\geq 1000
. If
\|g-g_{0}\|_{\mathrm{C}^{4}}\leq\varepsilon
then there is an open subset

B^{k}(0,L-20)\times\mathbb{S}^{n-k}\subset\mathcal{B}\subset B^{k}(0,L-10)\times\mathbb{S}^{n-k}

which is foliated by
(n-k)
-spheres of class
\mathscr{S}_{g}(\delta)
with QPMC. Moreover, for sufficiently small
\delta
(depending only on
n
) we have the following uniqueness statement: if
\Sigma\subset\mathcal{B}
is of class
\mathscr{S}_{g}(\delta)
and has QPMC, then
\Sigma
is a leaf of the foliation.

Proof.

We may extend
g
to a smooth metric on
M
such that

\|g-g_{0}\|_{\mathrm{C}^{4}(M)}\leq C\|g-g_{0}\|_{\mathrm{C}^{4}(B^{k}(0,L)\times\mathbb{S}^{n-k})}\leq C\varepsilon

for some universal
C
. If
\varepsilon
is small enough then Corollary
4.8
provides a canonical foliation for the extension
g
. (The uniqueness statement in Proposition
4.6
ensures that the portion of this foliation inside
B^{k}(0,L-10)\times\mathbb{S}^{n-k}
does not depend on how we extended
g
.) By Lemma
5.3
, if
\varepsilon
is sufficiently small then the leaves of the foliation are in
\mathscr{S}_{g}(\delta)
. It then suffices to take
\mathcal{B}
to be a union of leaves such that

B^{k}(0,L-20)\times\mathbb{S}^{n-k}\subset\mathcal{B}\subset B^{k}(0,L-10)\times\mathbb{S}^{n-k}.

Now suppose
\Sigma\subset\mathcal{B}
is of class
\mathscr{S}_{g}(\delta)
and has QPMC. By Lemma
5.4
, if
\delta
is sufficiently small then
\Sigma
is a leaf of the canonical foliation for
g
, and hence is a leaf of the foliation for
\mathcal{B}
.
∎

We conclude this section with the proof of Theorem
1.6
.

Proof.

Fix a constant
L\geq 1000
and let
\mathcal{C}\subset M
be an
(\varepsilon,L,n-k)
-cylindrical region of a complete Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
. We recall what this means: for each
p\in\mathcal{C}
there is a scale
r(p)>0
and an embedding

F_{p}:B^{k}(0,L)\times\mathbb{S}^{n-k}\to M

such that
F_{p}^{*}(r(p)^{-2}g)
is
\varepsilon
-close to
g_{0}
in
\mathrm{C}^{[1/\varepsilon]}
.

By Proposition
5.5
, if
\varepsilon
is sufficiently small then for each
p\in\mathcal{C}
there is an open set

F_{p}(B^{k}(0,L-20)\times\mathbb{S}^{n-k})\subset\mathcal{B}^{+}_{p}\subset F_{p}(B^{k}(0,L-10)\times\mathbb{S}^{n-k})

which is foliated by embedded
(n-k)
-spheres with QPMC. We denote the collection of leaves of this foliation by
\mathcal{F}_{p}^{+}
. For any
\delta>0
, we can assume
\varepsilon
is sufficiently small that the leaves of
\mathcal{F}_{p}^{+}
are
\delta
-vertical (because of Lemma
5.3
). For each
p\in\mathcal{C}
we now define
\mathcal{\mathcal{B}}_{p}
to be a connected open subset of
\mathcal{B}_{p}^{+}
which is a union of leaves in
\mathcal{F}_{p}^{+}
and satisfies

F_{p}(B^{k}(0,80)\times\mathbb{S}^{n-k})\subset\mathcal{B}_{p}\subset F_{p}(B^{k}(0,90)\times\mathbb{S}^{n-k}).

Let
\mathcal{F}_{p}
denote the collection of leaves in
\mathcal{F}_{p}^{+}
which are contained in
\mathcal{B}_{p}
.

We define
\mathcal{B}:=\cup_{p\in\mathcal{C}}\mathcal{B}_{p}
and observe that
\mathcal{B}
is an open subset of
M
which contains
\mathcal{C}
. We claim that the foliations
\mathcal{F}_{p}
can be adjoined into a foliation of
\mathcal{B}
. So we consider points
p
and
q
such that
\mathcal{B}_{p}\cap\mathcal{B}_{q}
is nonempty. For any
x\in\mathcal{B}_{p}\cap\mathcal{B}_{q}
, let
\Sigma_{q}(x)
denote the leaf of
\mathcal{F}_{q}
passing through
x
. The pullback of
\Sigma_{q}(x)
by
F_{p}
is of class
\mathscr{S}(\delta)
in the metric
F_{p}^{*}(r(p)^{-2}g)
, so if
\delta
is small enough we may invoke the uniqueness statement in Proposition
5.5
to conclude that
\Sigma_{q}(x)\in\mathcal{F}_{p}^{+}
. Given that
x\in\mathcal{B}_{p}
, we therefore have
\Sigma_{q}(x)\subset\mathcal{B}_{p}
or equivalently
\Sigma_{q}(x)\in\mathcal{F}_{p}
. Since
x
was arbitrary it follows that
\mathcal{B}_{p}\cap\mathcal{B}_{q}
is a union of leaves of
\mathcal{F}_{q}
, each of which is also in
\mathcal{F}_{p}
. This is true for any
p
and
q
with
\mathcal{B}_{p}\cap\mathcal{B}_{q}
nonempty, so
\cup_{p}\mathcal{F}_{p}
is indeed a foliation for
\mathcal{B}
.

To conclude, suppose
\Sigma
is an embedded
(n-k)
-sphere in
M
of class
\mathscr{S}(\delta)
, which has QPMC and intersects
\mathcal{C}
at some point
p
. Then
\Sigma
belongs to
\mathcal{F}_{p}
by the uniqueness statement in Proposition
5.5
. This completes the proof.
∎

6.
Metrics with no PMC foliation

A submanifold
\Sigma
of an ambient space
(M,g)
has parallel mean curvature (PMC) if
\nabla^{\perp}H=0
holds everywhere on
\Sigma
.

Theorem
1.2
asserts that if a metric on
\mathbb{R}^{k}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-k}
is close enough to the standard one, then the slices
\{z\}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-k}
can be perturbed to a canonical foliation by QPMC-spheres. It is natural to wonder whether there is in fact a foliation by spheres with PMC (or, rather, since the QPMC foliation is unique, and PMC implies QPMC, whether the leaves of our canonical foliation actually have PMC). We demonstrate that this is not the case in general—there are metrics on
\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
which are arbitrarily close to the standard one, but cannot be foliated by approximately vertical spheres with PMC.

Let us sketch the construction of such a metric. We write
M=\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
and as usual denote the standard product metric on
M
by
g_{0}
. For each metric
g
on
M
which is
\mathrm{C}^{4}
-close to
g_{0}
, we have a canonical QPMC foliation by Theorem
1.2
. For each
(z,\omega)\in M
we denote by
\Sigma_{g}(z,\omega)
the leaf of this foliation which contains
(z,\omega)
.

Lemma 6.1
.

There exists a metric
g
on
M
which is
\mathrm{C}^{4}
-close to
g_{0}
such that
g
has positive sectional curvatures in
\{|z|\leq 110\}
and
\Sigma_{g}(z,\omega)
is a stable geodesic for every
|z|\leq 100
.

Proof.

To begin with consider
\mathbb{S}^{3}
equipped with the unit round metric and identified with
SU(2)
. Let
E_{\alpha}
be a left-invariant orthonormal frame, and
\omega^{\alpha}
its dual frame. For each
\kappa>0
we have a Berger metric

\omega^{1}\otimes\omega^{1}+\omega^{2}\otimes\omega^{2}+\kappa^{2}\omega^{3}\otimes\omega^{3}.

We will find it convenient to scale this metric by
\kappa^{-2}
and define

g_{\kappa}:=\kappa^{-2}\omega^{1}\otimes\omega^{1}+\kappa^{-2}\omega^{2}\otimes\omega^{2}+\omega^{3}\otimes\omega^{3}.

The sectional curvatures of
g_{\kappa}
are

K(E_{1},E_{2})=\kappa^{2}(4-3\kappa^{2}),\qquad K(E_{1},E_{3})=\kappa^{4},\qquad K(E_{2},E_{3})=\kappa^{4}.

The integral curves of
E_{3}
are closed geodesics of length
2\pi
with respect to
g_{\kappa}
, and we may assume
\kappa
is sufficiently small so that each of these geodesics is stable (see
[
TU22
, Proposition 6]
).

Fix any point
p\in\mathbb{S}^{3}
. We identify the unit-speed geodesic tangent to
E_{3}
and passing through
p
with
\mathbb{S}^{1}
. Consider then the map
M\to\mathbb{S}^{3}
given by

(z,\omega)\mapsto\exp_{\omega}(\kappa z^{1}E_{1}+\kappa z^{2}E_{2})

where the exponential map is with respect to
g_{\kappa}
. The pullback of
g_{\kappa}
by this map converges in
\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{\loc}
to
g_{0}
as
\kappa\to 0
, so let us suppose
\kappa
is small and define a new metric
g
defined everywhere in
M
which agrees with the pullback of
g_{\kappa}
in
\{|z|\leq 110\}
and is globally
\mathrm{C}^{4}
-close to
g_{0}
. Then
g
has positive sectional curvatures in
\{|z|\leq 110\}
. Moreover, by uniqueness of the QPMC foliation for
g
, if
\kappa
is small enough then
\Sigma_{g}(z,\omega)
is a stable geodesic for every
|z|\leq 100
, obtained as the pullback of an integral curve of
E_{3}
.
∎

Combined with the second variation formula for length, Lemma
6.1
has the following consequence.

Lemma 6.2
.

There exists a metric on
M
which is
\mathrm{C}^{4}
-close to
g_{0}
such that, for
|z|\leq 100
, the normal bundle of
\Sigma_{g}(z,\omega)
admits no nonzero global parallel sections. In particular, for each of these submanifolds
\ker(\Delta^{\perp})=\{0\}
.

Proof.

It suffices to take
g
to be a metric with the properties referred to in Lemma
6.1
. Then for every
|z|\leq 100
, the leaf
\Sigma=\Sigma_{g}(z,\omega)
is a stable geodesic. In other words,

\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla^{\perp}V|^{2}-g(R(V,X)X,V)\,\dvoll_{\Sigma}\geq 0

for every section
V
of
N\Sigma
, where
X
is a global unit tangent to
\Sigma
. We have

g(R(V,X)X,V)=K(X,V)|V|^{2},

so since the sectional curvatures of
g
are positive for
|z|\leq 110
, we conclude that
N\Sigma
admits no nonzero global parallel sections.
∎

Now let
g
be a metric with the property referred to in Lemma
6.2
. Let
g_{j}
be a sequence of bumpy metrics which converges to
g
in the
\mathrm{C}^{4}
-norm. (A metric is called bumpy if all of its minimal submanifolds are nondegenerate critical points of the volume functional; this is a generic property by
[
Whi91
]
.) For
j
large enough, we have an associated QPMC foliation given by
\Sigma_{g_{j}}(z,\omega)
. Since
g_{j}
is bumpy, for any open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
, there must be some
z\in\Omega
such that
\Sigma_{g_{j}}(z,\omega)
is not a geodesic—otherwise, we have a foliation by geodesics, each of which carries a nonzero Jacobi field (obtained from any variation through leaves) and is thus degenerate. So let
(z_{j},\omega_{j})
be a sequence of points in
\{|z|\leq 90\}
such that
\Sigma_{j}:=\Sigma_{g_{j}}(z_{j},\omega_{j})
is not a geodesic. After passing to a subsequence,
\Sigma_{j}
converges to
\Sigma_{g}(z,\omega)
for some
(z,\omega)\in\{|z|\leq 100\}
. By Lemma
6.2
, for sufficiently large
j
we know that

\ker(\Delta^{\perp}_{\Sigma_{j}})=\ker(\Delta^{\perp}_{\Sigma_{g}(z,\omega)})=\{0\},

so
N\Sigma_{j}
admits no nonzero global parallel sections. But we know that the curvature vector of
\Sigma_{j}
does not vanish identically, so it cannot be parallel for large
j
. To summarise, when
j
is large the QPMC foliation for
g_{j}
contains at least one leaf which has QPMC but not PMC. (In fact, our argument indicates that ‘most’ leaves of the foliation for
g_{j}
have QPMC but not PMC.)

7.
Bubblesheets in mean curvature flow

In this section we indicate how bubblesheet regions arise along the mean curvature flow, and how our QPMC foliation can be used to canonically parameterize these regions.

Consider a closed hypersurface
M_{0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, and let
\{M_{t}\}_{t\in[0,T)}
be the maximal smooth evolution of
M_{0}
by the mean curvature flow. We write
\lambda_{1}\leq\dots\leq\lambda_{n}
for the principal curvatures of
M_{t}
, so that its (scalar) mean curvature is given by

H=\lambda_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{n}.

We assume
M_{0}
is
(m+1)
-convex for some
1\leq m\leq n-1
, meaning that

\lambda_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{m+1}>0

everywhere on
M_{0}
. (When
m=n-1
this condition simply says that
M_{0}
has positive mean curvature.) It follows that
M_{t}
is uniformly
(m+1)
-convex for all
t\in[0,T)
, in the sense that

\min_{M_{t}}\,\frac{\lambda_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{m+1}}{H}\geq\min_{M_{0}}\,\frac{\lambda_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{m+1}}{H}>0.

The maximal time
T<\infty
is characterized by

\lim_{t\to T}\bigg(\max_{M_{t}}H\bigg)=\infty.

For these results and further background see e.g.
[
ACGL22
, Chapter 9]
.

We recall a well-known variant of the Neck Detection Lemma for 2-convex solutions. In short, wherever the curvature of
M_{t}
is large, a certain pointwise inequality for its principal curvatures at a single point is enough to ensure the existence of a large almost cylindrical region. For the proof, see
[
HK17
]
.

Proposition 7.1
.

Given
L>0
and
\varepsilon>0
there exist positive constants
\delta=\delta(n,M_{0},L,\varepsilon)
and
K=K(n,M_{0},L,\varepsilon)
with the following property. If
x\in M_{t}
is such that
H(x,t)\geq K
and

\frac{\lambda_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{m}}{H}(x,t)\leq\delta

then the shifted and scaled hypersurface

\frac{H(x,t)}{n-m}(M_{t}-x)\cap B(0,L)

is
\varepsilon
-close in
\mathrm{C}^{[1/\varepsilon]}
to (a rotation of)
\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-m}
.

Let us consider a fixed
\varepsilon>0
and
L\geq 10^{6}
. We define

where

By Theorem

That is,

\Gamma_{t}

\Gamma_{t}

The region
\mathcal{B}_{t}
is completely described by its QPMC foliation, center-of-mass map
\Psi_{t}
and core
\Gamma_{t}
. Together, these pieces of data play the role of Hamilton’s normal form for necks (see Section 3 of

).

We intend for our normal form description of bubblesheet regions to find many applications. We give only one example here—a mechanism for determining the topology of
M_{0}
in case the curvature of
M_{t}
becomes large everywhere at the same time. Let us write

for the region enclosed by
M_{t}
.

Proposition 7.2
.

Suppose
\mathcal{C}_{t}=M_{t}
. Then
\Gamma_{t}
is closed and
\Omega_{t}
is smoothly isotopic to a tubular neighbourhood of
\Gamma_{t}
. In particular,
\Omega_{0}
is isotopic to a tubular neighbourhood of a closed

Proof.

We have
\mathcal{B}_{t}=M_{t}
. Since

is closed and
\Psi_{t}

\Gamma_{t}
is closed. Provided

∎

It is natural to ask which tubular neighbourhoods in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
actually arise in the conclusion of Proposition
7.2
. There is reason to believe that all of them do, up to isotopy. Indeed, a sufficiently thin tubular neighbourhood of
any

m
-submanifold of
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is
(m+1)
-convex. Flowing the boundary of such a thin tubular neighbourhood might, in many cases, lead to a singularity where the normal fibres pinch off everywhere simultaneously.

Computational study to heat and mass transfer analysis of blood-based Maxwell ternary hybrid nanofluid with nonlinear thermal radiation and exponential heat source
Authors: Ibrahim Mahariq, Syed Arshad Abas, Mehreen Fiza, Hakeem Ullah, Ali Akgül, Mukhlisa Soliyeva, Hasan AKSOY, Seham M Al-Mekhlafi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study presents a ternary hybrid nanofluid model exhibiting superior thermal performance as compared to conventional fluids. The ternary hybrid nanofluid is formulated by suspending alumina Al2O3, silicon dioxide (SiO2), and Titanium dioxide (TiO2) nanoparticles in blood, and its flow is model using non-Newtonian Maxwell model. Furthermore, the combined effects of nonlinear thermal radiation and space-based heat source are incorporated. The system of ordinary differential equations (ODEs), which is obtained via the use of similarity transformations, is solved numerically by the bvp4c technique in MATLAB. The impact of different physical parameters are displayed through Figures and Tables. Results show that amplifying the magnetic field, porosity, and Maxwell parameters diminishes the velocity of ternary and hybrid nanofluid. The skin friction enhances 10% and 3.1% for magnetic parameter and Maxwell fluid parameter respectively. The elevated numbers of radiation, space-dependent heat source and thermal Biot number amplify the temperature distribution. Nusselt number of ternary hybrid nanofluid improves 2.3% for thermal radiation, 1% for heat source, 1.2% for temperature difference parameter and 3% for Biot number.Keywords: Stretching surface, Ternary hybrid nanofluid, Space-based heat source, Joule heating, bvp4c

Introduction

Nanofluids are sophisticated fluids formed by the incorporation of nanoparticles, which are minuscule particles at the nanometer size, into a base fluid such as water, oil, or ethylene glycol. These nanoparticles, composed of metals (e.g., copper or silver), metal oxides (e.g., aluminium oxide or titanium dioxide), or carbon-based materials (e.g., carbon nanotubes or graphene), markedly enhance the thermal conductivity and heat transfer efficiency of the base fluid. This breakthrough enhances performance across various applications, including cooling systems, energy systems, and medical technologies. Droplet trains cooling technology is used for increasing thermal management in high heat flux electronic devices [1]. Nanofluids comprising two varieties of nanoparticles are referred to as hybrid nanofluids. This hybridization leverages the augmentative characteristics of each nanoparticle type. Moghimi et al. [2] numerically studied the nanofluid flow and found that temperature was enhanced by 540%, with Hartmann number. The viscoelastic parameter influences the nanofluid velocity, by creating greater resistance [3]. Fu et al. [4] theoretical study the application of the nanofluid in oil recovery. Razzaq et al. [5] analyzed hybrid nanofluid flow with viscous and Joule dissipation. It was examined that increasing the stretching parameter leads to an escalation in the momentum profile. Panda et al. [6] focused at the role of Cattneo-Christov heat flux model and thermal radiation in hybrid nanofluid flow. A nacre-inspired hybrid nanofluid composite, as reported by Hu et al. [7] delivers high passive radiative cooling power with enhanced energy and reduce carbon emissions. Mahmood et al. [8] deliberated the viscous dissipative hybrid nanofluid flow with MHD effects. Yasir et al. [9] studied the hybrid nanofluid flow over a shrinking surface and found that thermal profiles enhances with heat source. Urooj et al. [10] examined the thermal radiation effect on hybrid nanofluid flow using the Cattaneo-Christov heat flux model. A surface area engineered MWCNT + Al2O3 hybrid nanocomposite, reported by Ai et al. [11] enhance combustion and thermal efficiency. Hybrid nanofluid containing Au and SiO2 nanoparticles has significant application in cooling and thermal management system [12]. Abas et al. [13] discussed the hybrid nanofluid flow containing copper and silver nanoparticles past between revolving disks. They found that growth in magnetic parameter decreases the speed of the nanoparticles. A hybrid fluid flow coupled with Cattaneo-Christov model, a complex heat transfer phenomenon with significant implification for industrial and engineering applications [14]. Ternary hybrid nanofluids represent an advanced iteration of this concept, incorporating the dispersion of three distinct types of nanoparticles in the base fluid. This technology allows significant customization of the fluid’s properties by integrating the unique advantages of each type of nanoparticle. Gangadhar and Dinarvand [15] examined a comparative analysis of nano, hybrid and ternary hybrid nanofluid having nanoparticles Cu, Ag and CuO and deduced that heat transfer rate in more effective in ternary hybrid nanofluid. Heat transfer rate enhanced by 8.7% for ternary hybrid nanofluid as compared to other fluids [16]. Varatharaj et al. [17] used three distinct sorts of nanoparticles, including silver, titanium oxide and Alumina, to study ternary hybrid nanofluid flow past a permeable enlarging surface. The decreasing performance in the velocity distribution is noted for greater porosity parameter. Yasir et al. [18] explored the MHD ternary hybrid nanofluid buoyancy driven flow under the velocity slip and Melting boundary conditions. Thamizhselvi et al. [19] investigated the sodium alginate-based ternary hybrid nanofluid flow containing MgO, MoS2 and SWCNT over an exponential stretching porous surface. The addition of nanoparticles in base fluid improved the thermal panel of the ternary hybrid nanofluid flow [20]. Yasir et al. [21] analyzed the heat source effect of MHD flow of alumina and silica/Methanol hybrid nanofluid Moltot et al.[22] discussed the role of variable thermal conductivity and nonlinear thermal radiation on ethylene glycol-based ternary hybrid nanofluid containing silver, copper, and alumina nanoparticles over a stretching surface. A comparative analysis made by Mahboobtosi et al. [23] to inspect the ternary hybrid nanofluid flow. Ternary hybrid nanofluids have a lot of potential for a wide range in oil recovery and Biomedical therapies [24] and technical and industrial uses that call for effective heat control due to their flexibility [25–27].

An exponential space-based heat source exhibiting shear-thinning/thickening behavior provides novel perspectives for optimizing thermal transport processes in intricate systems, including the cooling of microelectronics, energy storage technologies, and biomedical devices, where precise thermal regulation is essential. Shah et al. [28] inspected the impact of an exponential space-based heat source for Carreau water-based ternary hybrid nanofluid flow and found that the thermal profile significantly improved for ternary hybrid nanofluid. Khan et al. [29] examined the impacts of nonlinear thermal radiation as well as an exponential space-based heat source on ternary hybrid nanofluid flow over a stretching surface. Shamshuddin et al. [30] used Homotopy analysis method to investigate the Non-Newtonian micropolar flow with heat generation and slip effects. Lone et al. The existence of heat source and sink increases the temperature of the ternary hybrid nanofluid by altering heat generation [32]. Shamshuddin et al. Sarfraz and Muhammad [34] investigated the MHD flow with the inclusion of heat source using Keller-Box method. Their consequences showed that the skin friction coefficient has augmented with expansion in magnetic and porosity factors. Bilal and Yasir [35] conceded the formulation representing thermophysical relation for ternary hybrid nanofluid past a stretching and shrinking surface with variable heat source.

Joule heating, also known as Ohmic heating, is the phenomenon in which electrical energy is converted into thermal energy as an electric current crosses a conductor. This effect plays a pivotal role in numerous applications, including magnetic refrigeration and electronic cooling, where it can substantially impact thermal regulation and energy efficiency. Takreem et al. [36] examined the heat transfer of magnetohydrodynamic ternary hybrid nanofluid flow with Joule heating. It was perceived that heat transfer rate is more pronounced for ternary hybrid nanofluid. Hussain et al. [37] scrutinized the ternary hybrid nanofluid flow with Joule heating, showing that higher suction parameter boosts the heat transfer rate by 1%. Naveed et al. [38] inspected the outcome of Joule heating and melting conditions on a hybrid nanofluid flow. Patel et al. [39] scrutinized the Joule heating and thermal radiation under slip condition. Mumtaz et al. [40] studied the thermal behavior of tetra hybrid nanofluid with Joule heating. Ramesh et al. [41] numerically investigated the slip flow over a stretching surface incorporating the effect of Joule heating and thermal radiation. According to Yasir and Khan [42], the Joule heating phenomenon is used to raise the temperature of the hybrid nanofluid. Rasheed et al. [43] investigated the effects of chemically reactive and Joule heating effects on MHD flow. The outcomes show that as magnetic parameter grows, the velocity distribution drops, while the thermal panel boosts.

The collective impacts of heat source and magnetohydrodynamic on a hybrid nanofluid flow across a dual directional stretching surface studied by Zangooee et al. [48].

Complex Interpolation and the Monotonicity in the Spatial Integrability Parameter of Exponentially Weighted Modulation Spaces
Leonid Chaichenets and Jan Hausmann

Abstract
. We introduce the notion of common retraction and coretraction for families of Banach spaces, formulate a framework for identifying interpolation spaces, and apply it to modulation spaces with exponential weights E p , q s E^{s}_{p,q} . By constructing the domain of the common coretraction, we are able to prove E o , q s ↪ E p , q s E^{s}_{o,q}\hookrightarrow E^{s}_{p,q} for o ≤ p o\leq p , i.e. the monotonicity in the spatial integrability parameter.



1.
Introduction

Interpolation theory has been established in the late fifties of the last century with major contributions by Calderon, Gagliardo, Lions and Krejn and has since become ubiquitous in the field of function spaces and partial differential equations. Of course, nowadays many textbooks such as
[
BL76
]
,
[
Tri78
]
, and
[
Lun18
]
are available and typically cover the family of Besov spaces. More recent spaces, such as the modulation spaces
M_{p,q}^{s}
introduced by Feichtinger in
[
Fei83
]
, are usually not covered in books on interpolation theory. Unfortunately, interpolation results for such spaces are often assumed to be generally known in the community and their proofs are not explicit, because they follow the Besov space case quite closely. For example, the textbook on modulation spaces
[
Grö01
, Subsection 11.3]
only refers to the original publication
[
Fei83
]
for interpolation results; the original publication
[
Fei83
, Theorem 6.1 (D)]
does not cover the case
p=\infty
or
q=\infty
. In the latter case different interpolation methods are common and give different results, e.g.
[
WH07
, Theorem 2.3]
.

In
[
WZG06
]
exponentially weighted modulation spaces
E^{s}_{p,}
were introduced and applied to study the nonlinear Schrödinger, Ginzburg-Landau and Navier-Stokes equations. In
[
CH21
]
Vlasov-Poisson-Fokker-Planck equation was studied in similar spaces and in
[
FGLW21
]
modulation spaces with exponentially decaying weights were applied in the context of Navier-Stokes equations. To the best of the authors’ knowledge, no interpolation result was published for these spaces.

In the paper at hand, we introduce the notion of common retraction and coretraction for families of Banach spaces, thereby obtain a framework in which interpolation spaces can be easily identified, and apply our result to the case of exponentially weighted modulation spaces. As a by-product we obtain the monotonicity of
E_{p,q}^{s}
w.r.t. the spatial integrability parameter
p
.

More precisely, our main results are as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
,
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
p_{0},p_{1},q\in[1,\infty]
with
p_{0}\leq p_{1}
. Then

(1)

\displaystyle E^{s}_{p_{0},q}\hookrightarrow E^{s}_{p_{1},q}.

Remark
5.7
clarifies, how this embedding is connected to the notion of common retraction and coretraction.

Theorem 1.2

(Complex interpolation of
E^{s}_{p,q}
)
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
and
\theta\in(0,1)
. Furthermore, let
p_{0},p_{1},q_{1}\in[1,\infty]
,
q_{0}\in[1,\infty)
and
s_{0},s_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
. We set

(2)

\displaystyle s=(1-\theta)s_{0}+\theta s_{1},

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1-\theta}{p_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{p_{1}},

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1-\theta}{q_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{q_{1}},

with the convention
\frac{1}{\infty}=0
. Then

\displaystyle[E^{s_{0}}_{p_{0},q_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),E^{s_{1}}_{p_{1},q_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})]_{\theta}=E^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

with the equality in the set theoretical sense and the equivalence of the norms.

Remark 1.3
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
,
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
\theta\in(0,1)
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
. Then

(3)

\displaystyle[E^{s}_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),E^{s}_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})]_{\theta}=E^{s}_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

with the equality in the set theoretical sense and the equivalence of the norms.
In general

\displaystyle[E^{s_{0}}_{p_{0},\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),E^{s_{1}}_{p_{1},\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})]_{\theta}\neq E^{s}_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

There is a complex interpolation method
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\theta}
, where for example for modulation spaces

\displaystyle(M^{s_{0}}_{p_{0},\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),M^{s_{1}}_{p_{1},\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))_{\theta}=M^{s}_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

(cf.
[
WH07
, Section 2]
)

The remainder of the paper is structured as follows. In Section
2
we recall the definitions of Besov and modulation spaces. Subsequently, in Section
3
, we recall basic notions of interpolation theory and the complex interpolation method. Section
4
explains the problem of identifitation of abstract interpolation spaces and introduces the aforementioned framework in Defintion
4.6
and Corollary
4.7
. Finally, modulation spaces with exponential weights are introduced in
5

and
1.2

2.
Besov and modulation spaces

In this section, we briefly recall the definition of Besov and modulation spaces. For comprehensive references, we refer to the textbook accounts
[
Tri10
]
,
[
Tri10a
]
,
[
Tri78
]
,
[
Saw18
]
and
[
Grö01
]
, respectively. First, let us consider Besov spaces. Fix any
\varphi_{0}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
\supp(\varphi_{0})\subseteq\overline{B}_{2}
and
\varphi_{0}(\xi)=1
if
\left|\xi\right|\leq 1
. We set for all
j\in\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle\varphi_{j}(\cdot)=\varphi_{0}(2^{-j}\cdot)-\varphi_{0}(2^{-j+1}\cdot).

Let us remark that
\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\varphi_{j}\equiv 1
,
\supp(\varphi_{j})\subseteq\overline{B}_{2^{j+1}}\setminus B_{2^{j-1}}
for
j\in\mathbb{N}
and therefore, for every
j,k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
\supp(\varphi_{j})\cap\supp(\varphi_{k})=\emptyset
, unless
\left|j-k\right|>2
. For future use (see Example
4.4
) let us define

(4)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{k}\coloneqq\begin{cases}\left\{0,1\right\},&\text{for $k=0$},\\
\left\{k-1,k,k+1\right\},&\text{for $k>0$},\end{cases}

the set of indices neighboring
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

Now let us define the
dyadic decomposition operator

\Delta_{j}\coloneqq\mathcal{F}^{-1}\varphi_{j}\mathcal{F}
for any
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Then, for any
d\in\mathbb{N}
, any
p,q\in[1,\infty]
and any
s\in\mathbb{R}
, the Besov space
B_{p,q}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is given by

\displaystyle B^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\,\Big|\,\left\|f\right\|_{B^{s}_{p,q}}<\infty\right\},

where

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{B^{s}_{p,q}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}2^{sjq}\left\|\Delta_{j}f\right\|_{L_{p}}^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

with the usual modification for
q=\infty
. Recall, that choosing a different
\tilde{\varphi}_{0}
yields the same Banach space
\tilde{B}_{p,q}^{s}=B_{p,q}^{s}
and an equivalent norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\tilde{B}_{p,q}^{s}}\sim\left\|\cdot\right\|_{B_{p,q}^{s}}
.

Let us now turn to modulation spaces. For them, the dyadic decomposition operators need to be replaced by the
uniform decomposition operators

\Box_{k}=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\sigma_{k}\mathcal{F}
, where, for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,
\sigma_{k}\in C^{\infty}
,
\supp(\sigma_{k})\subseteq\overline{B}_{\sqrt{d}}(k)
and
\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sigma_{k}\equiv 1
. Moreover, the
weight

j\mapsto 2^{js}
is replaced by the Japanese bracket
k\mapsto\left\langle k\right\rangle^{s}=\sqrt{1+\left|k\right|^{2}}^{s}
. All in all, one has

\displaystyle M^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\,\Big|\,\left\|f\right\|_{M^{s}_{p,q}}\coloneqq\left(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\left\langle k\right\rangle^{sq}\left\|\Box_{k}f\right\|_{L_{p}}^{q}\right)^{1/q}<\infty\right\}

with the usual modification for
q=\infty
. Let us underline that, similar to the dyadic decomposition case,
\Box_{k}\Box_{l}=0
, if

\left|k-l\right|>2\sqrt{d}
. Similarly to Equation (
4
), we define

(5)

\displaystyle\Lambda\coloneqq\left\{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\mid\left|k\right|\leq 2\sqrt{d}\right\},

the set of indices neighboring
0\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.

3.
Abstract Interpolation Theory

In this section, we recall basic notions of the interpolation theory and the complex interpolation method. We refer to
[
Tri78
]
,
[
BL76
]
,
[
Lun18
]
and
[
KS02
]
for the textbook accounts. As usual, we call two Banach spaces
X_{0}
,
X_{1}

compatible
, if and only if there exists a linear Hausdorff space
\mathcal{V}
such that

X_{0},X_{1}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{V}
. In this case, we call the pair
(X_{0},X_{1})
an
interpolation couple
.

A well-known example of an interpolation couple is the case
X_{0}=L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
X_{1}=L_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for some
d\in\mathbb{N}
and
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
. Here, one can take
\mathcal{V}=L_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, i.e. the space of (equivalence classes of) measurable functions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
equipped with the topology of local convergence in measure. In fact, this topology is generated by the metric

(6)

\displaystyle(f,g)\mapsto d(f,g)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\left|f(x)-g(x)\right|}{1+\left|f(x)-g(x)\right|}e^{-x^{2}}\mathrm{d}{x}.

Another important example concerns the vector-valued weighted sequence spaces, i.e. for parameters
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
,
s\in\mathbb{R}
, an index set
I
and a (weight) function
\omega\colon I\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
, we consider

(7)

\displaystyle\ell^{s,\omega}_{q}(I,L_{p})\coloneqq\left\{(f_{k})_{k\in I}\in(L_{p})^{I}\,\Big|\,\Biggl(\sum_{k\in I}\omega(k)^{qs}\left\|f_{k}\right\|_{L_{p}}^{q}\Biggr)^{1/q}<\infty\right\}

with the usual modification for
q=\infty
. Two important examples are the
exponential
and the
polynomial
weights, i.e. for
I\in\left\{\mathbb{N}_{0},\mathbb{Z}^{d}\right\}

(8)

\displaystyle i

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{e}}}{{\mapsto}}2^{\left|i\right|},

\displaystyle i

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{p}}}{{\mapsto}}\langle i\rangle.

For
I\in\left\{\mathbb{N}_{0},\mathbb{Z}^{d}\right\}
and
X_{i}=\ell_{q_{i}}^{s_{i},\omega}(I,L_{p_{i}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
with
i\in\left\{0,1\right\}
one can choose
\mathcal{V}=\bigotimes_{z\in I}L_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, i.e. the space of
I
-sequences of
L_{0}
-functions equipped with pointwise local convergence in measure.

We call a Banach space
X
an
intermediate space
w.r.t. the interpolation couple
(X_{0},X_{1})
if

\displaystyle X_{0}\cap X_{1}\hookrightarrow X\hookrightarrow X_{0}+X_{1}.

Here,
X_{0}\cap X_{1}
is canonically equipped with the norm
x\mapsto\max\left\{\left\|x\right\|_{X_{0}},\left\|x\right\|_{X_{1}}\right\}
, whereas

X_{0}+X_{1}
is canoncially equipped with the quotient space norm

\displaystyle x\mapsto\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}x=x_{0}+x_{1}\\
x_{0}\in X_{0},x_{1}\in X_{1}\end{subarray}}\left\|x_{0}\right\|_{X_{0}}+\left\|x_{1}\right\|_{X_{1}}.

Of course,
X_{0}
and
X_{1}
are always intermediate spaces w.r.t. their arbitrary interpolation couple
(X_{0},X_{1})
.

Morphisms, i.e. structure preserving mappings, between the two interpolation couples
(X_{0},X_{1})
and
(Y_{0},Y_{1})
are linear maps
T:X_{0}+X_{1}\to Y_{0}+Y_{1}
such that
T\in\mathcal{L}(X_{i},Y_{i})
for both
i\in\left\{0,1\right\}
. We write
T\in\mathcal{L}{((X_{0},X_{1}),(Y_{0},Y_{1}))}
in that case. Of course, given
T_{i}\in\mathcal{L}{(X_{i},Y_{i})}
for both
i\in\left\{0,1\right\}
one can extend them to
T\in\mathcal{L}((X_{0},X_{1}),(Y_{0},Y_{1}))
if and only if
T_{0}
and
T_{1}
agree on
X_{0}\cap X_{1}
. In this case,

\displaystyle T(x)

\displaystyle\coloneqq T_{0}(x_{0})+T_{1}(x_{1})

for any decomposition
x=x_{0}+x_{1}\in X_{0}+X_{1}
.

An
interpolation functor

F
is, loosely speaking, a method to construct intermediate spaces in a structure preserving way. More precisely, if
\hat{X}\coloneqq(X_{0},X_{1})
and
\hat{Y}\coloneqq(Y_{0},Y_{1})
are interpolation couples and
T\in\mathcal{L}(\hat{X},\hat{Y})
, then
X\coloneqq F(\hat{X})
is an intermediate space w.r.t.
\hat{X}
,
Y\coloneqq F(\hat{Y})
is an intermediate space w.r.t.
\hat{Y}
, and
T\in\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
. This is referred to as the
interpolation property
. We shall sometimes be more verbose and write
F(T)\coloneqq T|_{F(\hat{X})}\in\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
.

A standard example is the
complex interpolation functor

F_{\theta}
, where
\theta\in(0,1)
. One writes
(X_{0},X_{1})\mapsto F_{\theta}(X_{0},X_{1})\eqqcolon[X_{0},X_{1}]_{\theta}
. Let us denote by

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{z\in\mathbb{C}\,\Big|\,0<\operatorname{Re}z<1\right\}

and by
\overline{S}
its closure. For an interpolation couple
\hat{X}=(X_{0},X_{1})
, let
\mathcal{F}(\hat{X})
be the space of all bounded and continuous functions
f\colon\overline{S}\to X_{0}+X_{1}
such that

(i)

f
is holomorphic in
S
and

(ii)

t\mapsto f(j+it)\in C_{b}(\mathbb{R},X_{j})
for both
j\in\left\{0,1\right\}
.

The Banach space
\mathcal{F}(\hat{X})
is endowed with the norm

\displaystyle f\mapsto\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{F}(\hat{X})}\coloneqq\max_{j\in\left\{0,1\right\}}\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\left\|f(j+it)\right\|_{X_{j}}.

For any
\theta\in(0,1)
the
complex interpolation space

\displaystyle[X_{0},X_{1}]_{\theta}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{x\in X_{0}+X_{1}\,\Big|\,\exists f\in\mathcal{F}(\hat{X}):f(\theta)=x\right\}

is equipped with the (quotient) norm

\displaystyle\left\|x\right\|_{[X_{0},X_{1}]_{\theta}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}f(\theta)=x\\
f\in\mathcal{F}(X_{0},X_{1})\end{subarray}}\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{F}(X_{0},X_{1})}.

We shall call a Banach space
X
an
interpolation space
, if there is an interpolation functor
F
and an interpolation couple
\hat{X}=(X_{0},X_{1})
such that
X=F(\hat{X})
. Here, the equality means the equality in set theoretical sense and the equivalence of the norms.

4.
Identifying Interpolation Spaces

Often, one can identify an (abstract) interpolation space
X
with a concrete (i.e. a known) Banach space. The classical example is the theorem of Riesz-Thorin (cf.
[
Lun18
, Example 2.11]
) stating that
[L_{p},L_{q}]_{\theta}=L_{r}
(with equality of sets and
equality
of norms), where
\theta\in(0,1)
,
p,q\in[1,\infty]
, and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1-\theta}{p}+\frac{\theta}{q}.

More generally, one has the following example.

Example 4.1

(cf.
[
Tri78
, Theorem 1.18.1]
)
.

Let
I
be a countable index set,
\omega\colon I\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
\theta\in(0,1)
,
s_{0},s_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
and
p_{0},p_{1},q_{0},q_{1}\in[1,\infty]
with
\min(q_{0},q_{1})<\infty
. Set

(9)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p}

\displaystyle=\frac{1-\theta}{p_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{p_{1}}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q}

\displaystyle=\frac{1-\theta}{q_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{q_{1}},

\displaystyle s

\displaystyle=(1-\theta)s_{0}+\theta s_{1}.

Then

(10)

\displaystyle[\ell^{s_{0},\omega}_{q_{0}}(I,L_{p_{0}}),\ell^{s_{1},\omega}_{q_{1}}(I,L_{p_{1}})]_{\theta}=\ell^{s,\omega}_{q}(I,L_{p}).

with the equality in the set theoretical sense and the
equality
of the norms.

An important tool for identification of interpolation spaces is the
retraction-coretraction-method
. At the heart of the method is the identification of an interpolation space with a certain complemented subspace of a known interpolation space. The notions of retraction and coretraction come from the category theory. There, one calls
R

retraction
and (a corresponding)
S

coretraction
, if

R\circ S=\operatorname{id}
. In this paper, we do not require this level of generality, but only two special cases: The first one is the case of Banach spaces
X,Y
.
If there are
R\in\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
and
S\in\mathcal{L}(Y,X)
such that

\displaystyle R\circ S=\operatorname{id}_{Y}

we call
R
retraction and
S
(a corresponding) coretraction and
Y
a
retract
of
X
. The second case is the case of interpolation couples
\hat{X}=(X_{0},X_{1})
,
\hat{Y}=(Y_{0},Y_{1})
. The only change is that now
R\in\mathcal{L}(\hat{X},\hat{Y})
,
S\in\mathcal{L}(\hat{Y},\hat{X})
, and
R\circ S=\operatorname{id}_{Y_{0}+Y_{1}}
. One has that
\hat{Y}
is a retract of
\hat{X}
, if and only if
Y_{j}
is a retract of
X_{j}
for both
j\in\left\{0,1\right\}
and their retractions and coretractions agree on
X_{0}\cap X_{1}
and on
Y_{0}\cap Y_{1}
, respectively. For reasons which are apparent in the case of, e.g., Besov spaces and for the convenience of the readers unfamiliar with the category theory, we shall call a retraction
R
also a
synthesis operator
and a coretraction
S
also an
analysis operator
.

In the case of Banach spaces
X,Y
, a retraction
R\in\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
, and a corresponding coretraction
S\in\mathcal{L}(Y,X)
the operator
P\coloneqq S\circ R\in\mathcal{L}(\hat{X})
is a continuous projection and therefore its image
P(X)
is closed. By the open mapping theorem,
S:Y\to P(X)
is an isomorphism. Hence,
Y
is isomorphic to the complemented subspace
P(X)\subseteq X
.

The aforementioned retraction-coretraction-method is summarized in the following theorem.

Theorem 4.2

(Retract interpolation theorem (see
[
Tri78
, Theorem 1.2.4]
))
.

Let
\hat{X}=(X_{0},X_{1})
,
\hat{Y}=(Y_{0},Y_{1})
be interpolation couples,
X
an interpolation space of
\hat{X}
,
Y
an intermediate space w.r.t.
\hat{Y}
, and
R\in\mathcal{L}(\hat{X},\hat{Y})
be the retraction with a corresponding coretraction
S\in\mathcal{L}(\hat{Y},\hat{X})
. Moreover, consider an interpolation functor
F
such that
X=F(\hat{X})
,
R\in\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
and
S\in\mathcal{L}(Y,X)
. Then
Y=F(\hat{Y})
, with equality of sets but only
equivalence
of norms.

Proof.

Due to the interpolation property of
F
, we have

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle\in\mathcal{L}(F(\hat{Y}),F(\hat{X})),

\displaystyle R

\displaystyle\in\mathcal{L}(F(\hat{X}),F(\hat{Y})).

By assumption,
Y
is a retract of
X
(with the retraction
R
and a corresponding coretraction
S
). Let us denote
P\coloneqq S\circ R
. By the above,
F(S)
is an isomorphism onto
P(F(\hat{X}))=P(X)
and
S|_{Y}
is an isomorphism onto
P(X)
, respectively. Invoking the assumption
X=F(\hat{X})
one obtains

(11)

\displaystyle\left\|\,\cdot\,\right\|_{F(\hat{Y})}

\displaystyle\sim\left\|S\,\cdot\,\right\|_{F(\hat{X})}\sim\left\|S\,\cdot\,\right\|_{X}\sim\left\|\,\cdot\,\right\|_{Y}

finishing the proof.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

Let us again emphasize that one obtains only

and

in Theorem

.

\displaystyle=\left\|(\left\|\Delta_{j}f\right\|_{L_{p}})_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\right\|_{\ell_{q}^{s,e}}.

Set

\displaystyle\hat{X}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left(l^{s_{0},\text{e}}_{q_{0}}(\mathbb{N}_{0},L_{p_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})),l^{s_{1},\text{e}}_{q_{1}}(\mathbb{N}_{0},L_{p_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))\right),

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{1-\theta}{p_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{p_{1}},

\displaystyle s

We set

\displaystyle Sf

\displaystyle\coloneqq(\Delta_{k}f)_{k\in\mathbb{N}},

hence implies

\displaystyle\left[B_{p_{0},q_{0}}^{s_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),B_{p_{1},q_{1}}^{s_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\right]_{\theta}

Remark 4.5
.

Let us remark that for all possible parameters

the definition of

and

, the natural domain of the retraction is

\displaystyle\operatorname{dom}R=\biggl\{(f_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}

This gives rise to the following definition.

Definition 4.6

(Common retraction and coretraction)
.

Let
I
be a non-empty index set,
\mathcal{X}\coloneqq(X_{i})_{i\in I}
and
\mathcal{Y}\coloneqq(Y_{i})_{i\in I}

and
(Y_{i},Y_{j})

. Assume that there exists a set
\mathcal{V}
and
\mathcal{W}

(w.r.t to the families
\mathcal{X}
and

and

,

for all

.

The corollary below follows from Theorem
4.2
exactly as Example

.

Corollary 4.7
.

Let

and

be common retraction and corresponding coretraction w.r.t to the families
\mathcal{X}\coloneqq(X_{i})_{i\in I}
and
\mathcal{Y}\coloneqq(Y_{i})_{i\in I}

be an interpolation functor such that for every

for some

.

Corollary
4.7
yields the identification of complex interpolation spaces of modulation spaces.

Example 4.8
.

Consider
S:\mathcal{W}\to\mathcal{V}
and
R:\mathcal{V}\to\mathcal{W}
,
where
\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
,
Sf\coloneqq(\Box_{k}f)_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
,

(15)

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}=

\displaystyle\left\{(f_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\in(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\,\Big|\,\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{l\in\Lambda}\Box_{k+l}f_{k}\text{ converges unconditionally in $S^{\prime}$}\right\},

(16)

\displaystyle R(f_{k})_{k}\coloneqq\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{l\in\Lambda}\Box_{k+l}f_{k}.

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
and set
I\coloneqq[1,\infty]\times[1,\infty]\times\mathbb{R}
. For any
i=(p,q,s)\in I
let
X_{i}\coloneqq\ell^{s,p}_{q}(\mathbb{Z}^{d},L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})))
and
Y_{i}\coloneqq M_{p,q}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Notice that

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{Y_{i}}=\left\|(\left\|Sf\right\|_{L_{p}})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\right\|_{\ell_{q}^{s,p}}.

For
i=(p_{0},q_{0},s_{0})\in I
,

and
\theta\in(0,1)
, set

according to Equation (

).

Recall, that a series with values in
\mathbb{C}
is unconditionally convergent if, and only if, it is absolutely convergent. Therefore the summability condition in Equation (

) is equivalent to the series

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{l\in\Lambda}\Box_{k+l}f_{k}

being unconditionally convergent in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
. For every

we have

\displaystyle(R\circ S)(f)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{l\in\Lambda}\Box_{k+l}\Box_{k}f=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\Box_{k}f=f,

where we used that

on

. The operators

and

are even common retraction and corresponding coretraction w.r.t. the families
(X_{i})_{i\in I}
and

. We refer to, e.g.,
[

, Lemma 2.5, Lemma 2.12, and Lemma 2.13]
for detailed calculations. Therefore, by Example
4.1
, we indeed have
[X_{i},X_{j}]_{\theta}=X_{k}
. Hence, by Corollary
4.7
,

\displaystyle[M_{p_{0},q_{0}}^{s_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),M_{p_{1},q_{1}}^{s_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})]_{\theta}=M_{p,q}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

5.
Modulation spaces with exponential weights

Modulation spaces with exponentially growing weights were introduced by Wang, Zhao and Guo in
[
WZG06
]
. They consist of rather well-behaved, in particular infinitely often differentiable, functions. The case of exponentially decaying weights was first considered in
[
FGLW21
]
. The corresponding spaces are rather rough. In particular, they are no longer contained in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
. Instead, they form are subset of ultradistributions
\mathcal{S}_{1}^{\prime}
, namely of the dual of the Gelfand-Shilov space of Beurling type
\mathcal{S}_{1}
(see
[
FGLW21
, Section 3]
). We refer to the textbook account
[
GS68
]
and
[
Pet23
]
for a thorough introduction. Note that our
\mathcal{S}_{1}
is
\Sigma_{1}^{1}
in the latter reference (see
[
Pet23
, Theorem 3.7]
).

In order to define
\mathcal{S}_{1}^{\prime}
, we consider for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and all
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
the semi-norms

(17)

\displaystyle p_{\lambda}(f)\coloneqq\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{\lambda\left|x\right|}\left|f(x)\right|,

\displaystyle q_{\lambda}(f)\coloneqq\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{\lambda\left|\xi\right|}\left|\mathcal{F}f(\xi)\right|.

Then

(18)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\coloneqq\left\{f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\,\Big|\,\forall\lambda\in\mathbb{R}:p_{\lambda}(f)+q_{\lambda}(f)<\infty\right\}

is a locally convex topological vector space. Observe that, e.g.,

(19)

\displaystyle x\mapsto g(x)\coloneqq\frac{1}{(\sqrt{2\pi})^{d}}e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{2}}=(\mathcal{F}g)(x)\in\mathcal{S}_{1}.

Observe that, by
[
Pet23
, Definition 2.1]
,
\mathcal{S}_{1}
consists of precisely those
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
which satisfy

\displaystyle\forall h>0\ \exists C_{h}\in\mathbb{R}\ \forall\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}\colon\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|x^{\alpha}D^{\beta}f(x)\right|\leq C_{h}h^{\left|\alpha+\beta\right|}\alpha!\beta!.

Hence, any
f\in\mathcal{S}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is analytic (see
[
GS68
, Preface of Section 2.2, page 172]
). By Liouville’s Theorem, functions of bounded support in
\mathcal{S}_{1}
must be constant and from Equation (
17
) we infer that only
f=0
satisfies this criterion.

The dual space
\mathcal{S}_{1}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is equipped with the weak-*-topology and the operations of translation, modulation, multiplication with functions from
\mathcal{S}_{1}
and the Fourier transform are defined, as usually, via duality. For example
\mathcal{F}(f)(\varphi)\coloneqq f(\mathcal{F}\varphi)
for all
f\in\mathcal{S}_{1}^{\prime}
and all
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}_{1}
.

Notice, that
\mathcal{S}_{1}^{\prime}
is too big to extend the coretraction
\tilde{S}:\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
,
f\mapsto(\Box_{k}f)_{k}
to. More precisely, suppose
\Box_{k}f\in\mathcal{S}_{1}^{\prime}
is defined for an
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Then, we can apply it to the Gaussian
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}_{1}
from (
19
) and obtain

\displaystyle\left\langle\Box_{k}f\,,\,g\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\mathcal{F}^{-1}\sigma_{k}\mathcal{F}f\,,\,g\right\rangle=\left\langle\mathcal{F}f\,,\,\sigma_{k}\mathcal{F}^{-1}g\right\rangle=\left\langle\mathcal{F}f\,,\,\sigma_{k}g\right\rangle.

As
\sigma_{k}
has compact support, the same is true for
\sigma_{k}g\overset{!}{\in}{\mathcal{S}_{1}}
and thus, by the above,
\sigma_{k}g\equiv 0
, which is clearly a contradiction.

To meaningfully extend the corectraction
\tilde{S}
, we rather start with the retraction
R
. A natural domain of
(f_{k})\mapsto R(f_{k})=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{l\in\Lambda}\Box_{k+l}f_{k}
, is

(20)

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{(f_{k})\in(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\,\Big|\,\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{l\in\Lambda}\Box_{k+l}f_{k}\text{ converges unconditionally in }\mathcal{S}_{1}^{\prime}\right\},

as
f_{k}
shall be in the domain of the known
\Box_{k}:\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, and the series defining
R
shall converge unconditionally in
\mathcal{S}_{1}^{\prime}
. For the domain of the coretraction
S
we generalize the idea from
[
FGLW21
, Equation (5.3)]
and use

(21)

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\Biggl\{f\in\mathcal{S}_{1}^{\prime}\,\Big|\,\exists(f_{l})_{l\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\subseteq\mathcal{S}^{\prime}:\forall l\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:

\displaystyle\supp(\mathcal{F}f_{l})

\displaystyle\subseteq\overline{B}_{3\sqrt{d}}(l),f=\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}f_{l}\text{ unconditionally in $\mathcal{S}_{1}^{\prime}$}\Biggr\}.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
. Then for all
f=\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}f_{l}\in\mathcal{W}
as in (
21
) the formula

(22)

\displaystyle Sf

\displaystyle\coloneqq\Big(\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\Box_{k}f_{l}\Big)_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}

leads to a well-defined mapping
S:\mathcal{W}\to\mathcal{V}
and
S
extends
\tilde{S}
, i.e.
(Sf)(k)=\Box_{k}f
for any
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
and any
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.

Proof.

Consider any
f\in\mathcal{W}
with
\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}f_{l}
converging unconditionally in
\mathcal{S}_{1}^{\prime}
to
f
,
f_{l}\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
,
\supp(\operatorname{\mathcal{F}}f_{l})\subseteq\overline{B}_{3\sqrt{d}}(l)
for all
l\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Then the series in (
22
) is, due to the support condition on
\operatorname{\mathcal{F}}f_{l}
, just a finite sum in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
,

(23)

\displaystyle(Sf)(k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l\in\Lambda^{\prime}}\Box_{k}f_{k+l}

for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, where, similarly to Equation (
5
), we set

\displaystyle\Lambda^{\prime}\coloneqq\left\{l\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\,\Big|\,\left|l\right|\leq 4\sqrt{d}\right\}.

Next we show that
Sf
is well-defined. To that end, consider another representation
f=\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\tilde{f}_{l}\in\mathcal{S}_{1}^{\prime}
, where
\tilde{f}_{l}\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
with
\supp(\operatorname{\mathcal{F}}\tilde{f}_{l})\subseteq B_{3\sqrt{d}}(l)
for all
l\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and define
(h_{l})\coloneqq(f_{l}-\tilde{f}_{l})
. To show is

\displaystyle\sum_{l\in\Lambda^{\prime}}\Box_{k}h_{k+l}

\displaystyle=0

for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Fix any
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}
and any
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Due to the bounded supports of
\mathcal{F}h_{l}
, one has

\displaystyle\supp\Biggl(\sum_{l\in\Lambda^{\prime}}\mathcal{F}^{-1}h_{k+l}\Biggr)\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{\sqrt{d}}(k)

and therefore, indeed

\displaystyle\left\langle\sum_{l\in\Lambda^{\prime}}\Box_{k}h_{k+l}\,,\,\varphi\right\rangle_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\times S}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l\in\Lambda^{\prime}}\left\langle\mathcal{F}^{-1}h_{k+l}\,,\,\sigma_{k}\mathcal{F}\varphi\right\rangle_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\times S}=0.

We prove that
S
is an extension of
\tilde{S}
. Observe that
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{W}
. To that end, consider any
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
and set
(f_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\coloneqq(\Box_{k}f)_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
. Clearly,
Sf=(\Box_{k}f)_{k}
by Equation (
23
).

In remains to show that
Sf\in\mathcal{V}
for every
f\in\mathcal{W}
. Indeed, due to the property of bounded supports, we have

(24)

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{l\in\Lambda}\Box_{k+l}(Sf)(k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{l\in\Lambda}\Box_{k+l}\Box_{k}\sum_{m\in\Lambda^{\prime}}f_{k+m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{m\in\Lambda^{\prime}}\Box_{k}f_{k+m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{m^{\prime}\in\Lambda^{\prime}}\Box_{k^{\prime}+m^{\prime}}f_{k^{\prime}}=\sum_{k^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}f_{k^{\prime}}=f

unconditionally in
\mathcal{S}_{1}^{\prime}
.

This concludes the proof.
∎

The respective retraction
R
is treated in the following Proposition.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mathcal{V}
,
\mathcal{W}
as in Equation (

) and (
20
), respectively, and

as in Proposition

. Then the series

(25)

\displaystyle R(f_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{l\in\Lambda}\Box_{k+l}f_{k},

converges unconditionally in
\mathcal{S}_{1}^{\prime}
and defines a mapping

. Moreover,

, i.e.

is a retraction with corresponding coretraction

.

The unconditional convergence of the series in (

) and the fact that

are clear from Equations (

) and (

). The property

).

Let

,

,

\displaystyle\left\|\Box_{k}f\right\|_{{p_{2}}}\lesssim_{d}\left\|\Box_{k}f\right\|_{{p_{1}}},

with the implicit constant independent of
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
.

R
we observe

\displaystyle\left\|Rf\right\|_{E^{s}_{p,q}}

\displaystyle=\left\|(2^{s\left|k\right|}\left\|\Box_{k}Rf\right\|_{p})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\right\|_{q}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\Biggl(2^{s\left|k\right|}\sum_{l\in\Lambda}\sum_{h\in\Lambda}\left\|\Box_{k}\Box_{k+h}f_{k+h+l}\right\|_{L_{p}}\Biggr)_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\right\|_{q}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\Biggl(2^{s\left|k\right|}\sum_{l\in\Lambda}\sum_{k\in\Lambda}\left\|f_{k+h+l}\right\|_{L_{p}}\Biggr)_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\right\|_{\ell_{q}}\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{\ell^{s}_{q}(L_{p})},

where we used Bernstein’s Multiplier Estimate from Lemma
5.5
in the penultimate step. Noting that
R\circ S=\operatorname{id}_{Y_{i}}
by Proposition
5.2
completes the proof.

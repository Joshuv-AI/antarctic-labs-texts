On Tiling and Spectral Sets in ℤ p 2 × ℤ p 2 \mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}
Weiqi Zhou

Abstract
Let p p be a prime number, it is shown that tiling and spectral sets coincide in ℤ p 2 × ℤ p 2 \mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}} by considering equivalently symplectic spectral pairs. The main approach is still to analyze the zero set of the Fourier transform. The zero set of the symplectic Fourier transform differs from the zero set of the usual Fourier transform by an orthogonal rotation, but using the symplectic Fourier transform allows more freedom when applying change of basis. Some auxiliary results concerning tiling sets and spectral sets of sizes p p and p 2 ​ m − 1 p^{2m-1} in ℤ p m × ℤ p m \mathbb{Z}_{p^{m}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{m}} are also presented. Keywords : Fuglede’s conjecture; tiling sets; symplectic Fourier transform. 2020 MSC : 42A99; 05B45

Introduction

Let
A,B
be subsets of an additive finite Abelian group
G
, we use
A+B
for the multiset (a
multiset
is a collection of elements in which elements are allowed to be repeated) formed by elements of form
a+b
where
a,b
are enumerated from
A,B
respectively. We write
A\oplus B
if
A+B
is actually a usual set (i.e., no elements appears more than once), and in this case we may also say that
A,B
are tiling complements of each other in

. A subset

is said to be a
tiling set
(or
a tile
) in
G
, if there exists another subset
B
of
G
so that
A\oplus B=G
, in which case we shall call
(A,B)
a tiling pair in
G
.

Let
\mathbb{Z}_{n}=\{0,1,\ldots,n-1\}
be the additive cyclic group of
n
elements, a subset

is said to be
spectral
in
\mathbb{Z}_{n}\times\mathbb{Z}_{n}
, if there is some
S\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{n}\times\mathbb{Z}_{n}
such that
\{e^{2\pi i\langle x,s\rangle/n}\}_{s\in S}
(if

and

, then
\langle x,s\rangle=x_{1}s_{1}+x_{2}s_{2}
is the inner product) is an orthogonal bases on
L^{2}(A)
with respect to the counting measure. In such case we shall call
(A,S)
a
spectral pair
in
\mathbb{Z}_{n}\times\mathbb{Z}_{n}
, and

is said to be a

of

.

A fundamental question of the sampling theory, known as the Fuglede’s conjecture
[
9
]
, asks whether these two types of sets coincide. The conjecture has been disproved in

for

[
8
,
14
,
15
,
24
,
31
]
, while for

it remains open (see
[
21
]
for positive results on convex sets in all dimensions). The same conjecture in finite Abelian groups is equally important and partially equivalent to the conjecture in
\mathbb{R}^{d}

[
3
,
19
,
11
]
. On
p
-adic fields it is known to be true
[
4
,
5
]
.

So far there are quite a number of positive results in cyclic groups, in which the method evolves: Lagarias and Wang showed that it is true in periodic cyclic groups (i.e., if
(A,B)
is a tiling pair in such a group, then one of

has to be periodic)
[

]
, which includes all cyclic

-groups. Instead of looking at periodic components or other weakened versions of periodicity, Coven and Meyerowitz proposed two different conditions to characterize tiles in cyclic groups

, and they proved its sufficiency for all

is the product of two prime powers
[

]
. Łaba showed that a set satisfying these conditions will also admit a spectrum
[
16
]
. Therefore to show that a set is tiling/spectral in cyclic groups, we may verify whether they satisfy the Coven-Meyerowitz condition, this approach has been fruitful
[
13
,
17
,
18
,
22
,
30
,
34
]
, and has led to several positive results in

including

being square-free or product of two prime powers.

For groups generated by two elements the number of results (all positive) are limited
[
7
,
12
,
28
,
33
]
. Although the conjecture is false for
d\geq 3
, in
[
28
]
it is asked whether all tiles are spectral in
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}}^{d}
(
p^{n}
is a prime power), since even in
p
-groups so far there has been no non-spectral tile found, and the question is answered positively at least for certain cases in higher dimensions
[

,
23
]
. The tiling to spectral part of this paper is a critical case left open in
[

]
.

There are three auxiliary results (Lemma

, Lemma

, Lemma
14
respectively) and one main result (Theorem
1
) in this paper (

below):

Lemma
.

If

is a tile in

with

Lemma

If

The main approach is by considering equivalently symplectic spectral pairs (which allows more freedom when applying change of basis), and to analyze the difference set, the structure of the zero set of the Fourier transform, and in particular how these two types of sets are distributed in different equivalence classes of subgroup generators respectively.

2
Preliminaries

2.1
The Euclidean setting

Let
A
be a subset of a finite Abelian group
G
, define its
difference set
to be

\Delta A=\{a-a^{\prime}:a,a^{\prime}\in A,a\neq a^{\prime}\}.

Lemma 1
.

Suppose
A,B
are subsets of some finite Abelian group
G
, if

|A|\cdot|B|\geq|G|,\quad\text{and}\quad\Delta A\cap\Delta B=\emptyset,

then we must have
|A|\cdot|B|=|G|
and that
(A,B)
is a tiling pair in
G
.

Proof.

Suppose
a,a^{\prime}\in A,b,b^{\prime}\in B
, then

a+b=a^{\prime}+b^{\prime}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad a-a^{\prime}=b-b^{\prime},

indicates

A\oplus B\text{ is well defined}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\Delta A\cap\Delta B=\emptyset.

(1)

Clearly
A\oplus B
is a subset of
G
, thus

|A\oplus B|\leq|G|.

On the other hand by the given assumption we also have

|A\oplus B|=|A|\cdot|B|\geq|G|.

Together this means

|A\oplus B|=|G|,

which indicates that
(A,B)
is a tiling pair in
G
, therefore
|A|\cdot|B|=|G|
must also hold.
∎

Let
f
be a function on
\mathbb{Z}_{n}\times\mathbb{Z}_{n}
, denote by

\supp(f)=\{x\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}\times\mathbb{Z}_{n}:f(x)\neq 0\},\quad Z(f)=\{x\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}\times\mathbb{Z}_{n}:f(x)=0\},

its support set and its zero set respectively. Its
Fourier transform
is defined as

\widehat{f}(\xi)=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}\times\mathbb{Z}_{n}}f(x)e^{2\pi i\langle x,\xi\rangle/n},\quad\xi\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}\times\mathbb{Z}_{n}.

In particular, if
f=\mathbf{1}_{A}
is the characteristic function for some
A\subset\mathbb{Z}_{n}\times\mathbb{Z}_{n}
, then we get

\widehat{\mathbf{1}}_{A}(\xi)=\sum_{a\in A}e^{2\pi i\langle a,\xi\rangle/n}.

(2)

From this definition we can verify directly that

\widehat{\mathbf{1}}_{A+B}=\widehat{\mathbf{1}}_{A}\cdot\widehat{\mathbf{1}}_{B}.

(3)

It is thus clear that
(A,B)
being a tiling pair in
\mathbb{Z}_{n}\times\mathbb{Z}_{n}
if and only if

\widehat{\mathbf{1}}_{A}\cdot\widehat{\mathbf{1}}_{B}=\widehat{\mathbf{1}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}\times\mathbb{Z}_{n}}.

It is also easy to verify that

Z(\widehat{\mathbf{1}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}\times\mathbb{Z}_{n}})=(\mathbb{Z}_{n}\times\mathbb{Z}_{n})\setminus\{(0,0)\},

therefore
(A,B)
being a tiling pair in
\mathbb{Z}_{n}\times\mathbb{Z}_{n}
is also equivalent to

|A|\cdot|B|=|\mathbb{Z}_{n}\times\mathbb{Z}_{n}|\quad\text{and}\quad\Delta(\mathbb{Z}_{n}\times\mathbb{Z}_{n})=(\mathbb{Z}_{n}\times\mathbb{Z}_{n})\setminus\{(0,0)\}\subseteq Z(\widehat{\mathbf{1}}_{A}\cdot\widehat{\mathbf{1}}_{B}).

Similarly
(A,S)
being a spectral pair is equivalent to

|A|=|S|\quad\text{and}\quad\Delta S\subseteq Z(\widehat{\mathbf{1}}_{A}).

Denote by
\ord(\xi)
the oder of an element
\xi\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}\times\mathbb{Z}_{n}
. Given
A,\xi
, to understand whether
\xi\in Z(\widehat{\mathbf{1}}_{A})
, we first notice that there must be a unique
h\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}\times\mathbb{Z}_{n}
so that
\xi=mh
with
m=n/\ord(\xi)
, then we may define the
mask polynomial
of
A,\xi
to be

P_{A,\xi}(z)=\sum_{a\in A}z^{\langle a,h\rangle},\quad\text{where}\quad\xi=\frac{n}{\ord(\xi)}h.

Comparing it with (
2
) we see that

\widehat{\mathbf{1}}_{A}(\xi)=\sum_{a\in A}e^{2\pi i\langle a,\xi\rangle/n}=\sum_{a\in A}e^{2\pi i\langle a,mh\rangle/n}=\sum_{a\in A}e^{2\pi i\langle a,h\rangle/\ord(\xi)}=P_{A,\xi}(e^{2\pi i/\ord(\xi)}).

Therefore the Fourier transform
\widehat{\mathbf{1}}_{A}
evaluated at
\xi
is at the same time the evaluation of the mask polynomial
P_{A,\xi}(z)
at the
\ord(\xi)
-th root of unity
z=e^{2\pi i/\ord(\xi)}
.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, denote the
n
-th cyclotomic polynomial by
\Phi_{n}(z)
. Clearly
P_{A,\xi}(z)\in\mathbb{N}[z]
, therefore whether
\xi\in Z(\widehat{\mathbf{1}}_{A})
holds depends on the divisibility of
P_{A,\xi}(z)
by the
\ord(\xi)
-th cyclotomic polynomial. In particular, this also means that if
L
is a linear transformation on
\mathbb{Z}_{n}\times\mathbb{Z}_{n}
that satisfies

\langle Lx,Ly\rangle=d\langle x,y\rangle,

(i.e., a “unitary matrix” scaled by
d
) then

\widehat{\mathbf{1}}_{LA}(L\xi)=\sum_{a\in A}e^{2\pi i\langle La,L\xi\rangle/n}=\sum_{a\in A}e^{2\pi id\langle a,\xi\rangle/n}.

If
d
is coprime to
n
, then it is also coprime to
\ord(\xi)
, thus
e^{2\pi id/\ord(\xi)}
is still a primitive
\ord(\xi)
-th root of unity, and
z=e^{2\pi id/\ord(\xi)}
is a root of
P_{LA,L\xi}(z)
if and only if
z=e^{2\pi i/\ord(\xi)}
is a root of
P_{A,\xi}(z)
. Therefore to determine whether
A
is tiling/spectral, we can equivalently investigate whether
LA
is tiling/spectral.

This observation allows us to apply “change of basis” in subsequent analysis, as we shall see later it is often needed to map a pair of generators
h,h^{\prime}
of
\mathbb{Z}_{n}\times\mathbb{Z}_{n}
to the “standard basis”
(1,0)
and
(0,1)
. Unfortunately if
n
is a composite number, then
\mathbb{Z}_{n}\times\mathbb{Z}_{n}
is not a vector space, which means an
L
that satisfies

\langle Lx,Ly\rangle=d\langle x,y\rangle,\quad\gcd(d,n)=1,

(4)

(
\gcd
stands for greatest common divisor) and at the same time maps an arbitrary generating pair
h,h^{\prime}
to the standard basis may not exist. For example,
a=(1,1),b=(1,3)
is a generating pair of
\mathbb{Z}_{4}\times\mathbb{Z}_{4}
, they are orthogonal to each other since
\langle a,b\rangle=0\pmod{4}
, but there is no
L
that can map them to standard basis and at the same time keep (
4
). Moreover, an “orthogonal decomposition” need not even exist in
\mathbb{Z}_{n}\times\mathbb{Z}_{n}
, for example, consider the subgroup generated by
(1,18)
in
\mathbb{Z}_{25}\times\mathbb{Z}_{25}
, it is a maximal cyclic subgroup, we may also verify that

(-18,1)=7\cdot(1,18)\pmod{25}.

Therefore this group is “orthogonal” to itself, and an exhaustive search will show that it has no orthogonal complement (i.e., no other maximal cyclic subgroup is orthogonal to it). The same can happen in
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{m}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{m}}
when
p\bmod 4=1
, because in such case
x^{2}=-1
is solvable in the multiplicative group modulo
p^{m}
, and if
k^{2}=-1\pmod{p^{m}}
, and
k^{-1}
is the multiplicative inverse of
k
modulo
p^{m}
, then we will have
(-k,1)=k^{-1}\cdot(1,k)\pmod{p^{m}}
.

None of these issues will exist if we switch to the symplectic setting, we will be allowed to freely change basis in the symplectic setting.

2.2
The Symplectic setting

Given
x=(x_{1},x_{2})
and
y=(y_{1},y_{2})
, define the
symplectic form
between
x,y
to be

\langle x,y\rangle_{s}=x_{1}y_{2}-y_{1}x_{2}.

The symplectic form enters Fourier analysis in the way that it captures commutativity of time-frequency shifts, i.e., if
T_{t}:f(x)\mapsto f(x-t)
and
M_{\xi}:f(x)\mapsto e^{2\pi i\xi\cdot x}f(x)
are respectively the translation operator and the modulation operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, then

M_{a}T_{b}M_{a^{\prime}}T_{b^{\prime}}=e^{2\pi i(ab^{\prime}-a^{\prime}b)}M_{a^{\prime}}T_{b^{\prime}}M_{a}T_{b},

holds for any
a,b,a^{\prime},b^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}
. The term
ab^{\prime}-a^{\prime}b
in the exponent is a symplectic form.

If
h=(h_{1},h_{2}),h^{\prime}=(h_{1}^{\prime},h_{2}^{\prime})\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}\times\mathbb{Z}_{n}
satisfies
\langle h,h^{\prime}\rangle_{s}=1
, then they are called a pair of
symplectic basis
on
\mathbb{Z}_{n}\times\mathbb{Z}_{n}
. The relation

\langle h,h^{\prime}\rangle_{s}=h_{1}h_{2}^{\prime}-h_{1}^{\prime}h_{2}=\det\begin{pmatrix}h_{1}&h_{1}^{\prime}\\
h_{2}&h_{2}^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}

indicates that in such case
h,h^{\prime}
generate
\mathbb{Z}_{n}\times\mathbb{Z}_{n}
. The following lemma is essentially
[
35
, Lemma 2.3]
.

Lemma 2
.

If
H,H^{\prime}
are two subgroups in
\mathbb{Z}_{n}\times\mathbb{Z}_{n}
with
|H|=|H^{\prime}|=n
and
\mathbb{Z}_{n}\times\mathbb{Z}_{n}=H\oplus H^{\prime}
, then for each generator
h
of
H
, there is some generator
h^{\prime}
of
H^{\prime}
such that
h,h^{\prime}
form a symplectic basis.

Define the
symplectic orthogonal set
of a given
H\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{n}\times\mathbb{Z}_{n}
to be

H^{\perp_{s}}=\{x\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}\times\mathbb{Z}_{n}:\langle x,h\rangle_{s}=0,\forall h\in H\}.

The following lemma can be found in
[
35
, Lemma 2.2]
,
H
is often called a
Lagrangian
if
H=H^{\perp_{s}}
.

Lemma 3
.

Let
H\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{n}\times\mathbb{Z}_{n}
,
H=H^{\perp_{s}}
if and only if
H
is an order
n
subgroup.

From the proof of
[
35

one also sees that if

|H|\cdot|H^{\perp_{s}}|=n^{2}.

(5)

Now define the
symplectic Fourier transform
of a function
f
on
\mathbb{Z}_{n}\times\mathbb{Z}_{n}
to be

\widehat{f}^{sym}(\xi)=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}\times\mathbb{Z}_{n}}f(x)e^{2\pi i\langle x,\xi\rangle_{s}/n},\quad\xi\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}\times\mathbb{Z}_{n}.

Similarly if
f=\mathbf{1}_{A}
, then we have

\widehat{\mathbf{1}}_{A}^{sym}(\xi)=\sum_{a\in A}e^{2\pi i\langle a,\xi\rangle_{s}/n}.

The
symplectic mask polynomial
of given
A
and given
\xi=(n/\ord(\xi))\cdot h
is accordingly

P_{A,\xi}^{sym}(z)=\sum_{a\in A}z^{\langle a,h\rangle_{s}},

similarly
\widehat{\mathbf{1}}_{A}^{sym}
is still the evaluation of
P_{A,\xi}^{sym}(z)
at the
\ord(\xi)
-th root of unity

\widehat{\mathbf{1}}_{A}^{sym}(\xi)=P_{A,\xi}^{sym}(e^{2\pi i/\ord(\xi)}).

(6)

We may also define a
symplectic spectral set
to be a subset
A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{n}\times\mathbb{Z}_{n}
for which there exists some
S\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{n}\times\mathbb{Z}_{n}
such that
\{e^{2\pi i\langle a,s\rangle_{s}/n}\}_{s\in S}
forms an orthogonal basis on
L^{2}(A)
with respect to the counting measure,

is then called a
symplectic spectrum
of

and
(A,S)
is said to be a
symplectic spectral pair
. Similar as in the Euclidean setting it is easy to see that
(A,B)
being a tiling pair is equivalent to

|A|\cdot|B|=|\mathbb{Z}_{n}\times\mathbb{Z}_{n}|\quad\text{and}\quad\Delta(\mathbb{Z}_{n}\times\mathbb{Z}_{n})\subseteq Z(\widehat{\mathbf{1}}_{A}^{sym}\cdot\widehat{\mathbf{1}}_{B}^{sym}),

(7)

while
(A,S)
is a symplectic spectral pair in
\mathbb{Z}_{n}\times\mathbb{Z}_{n}
if and only if

|A|=|S|\quad\text{and}\quad\Delta S\subseteq Z(\widehat{\mathbf{1}}_{A}^{sym}).

Lemma 4
.

Let
A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{n}\times\mathbb{Z}_{n}
, then
A
is a spectral set if and only if it is also a symplectic spectral set.

Proof.

It is easy to see that
(s_{1},s_{2})\in Z(\widehat{\mathbf{1}}_{A}^{sym})
if and only if
(-s_{2},s_{1})\in Z(\widehat{\mathbf{1}}_{A}^{sym})
, therefore the symplectic spectrum and the spectrum differ only by a
90

∎

A
symplectic transformation
on
\mathbb{Z}_{n}\times\mathbb{Z}_{n}
is a linear transformation
L
such that

\langle Lx,Ly\rangle_{s}=\langle x,y\rangle_{s},\quad\forall x,y\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}\times\mathbb{Z}_{n},

(9)

holds. On
\mathbb{Z}_{n}\times\mathbb{Z}_{n}
it is just a

matrix with unit determinant modulo

. Indeed, let
L=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}
and

-1&0\end{pmatrix}
, then

\begin{pmatrix}a&c\\
b&d\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix}.

Straightforward computation shows

\begin{pmatrix}a&c\\
b&d\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0&ad-bc\\
bc-ad&0\end{pmatrix},

which means
L
is a symplectic transformation if and only if
ad-bc=1\pmod{n}
.

Lemma 5
.

If

(1,0)

Suppose

Lemma 8

(counting)
.

Let
p
be a prime number,

with
\ord(x)=p^{m}
(

) and

|A\cap\langle px\rangle^{\perp_{s}}|=|A\cap\langle x\rangle^{\perp_{s}}|\cdot p.

(10)

Moreover, if

V_{k}=\{y\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}\times\mathbb{Z}_{n}:\langle x,y\rangle_{s}=k\cdot\frac{n}{p}\pmod{n}\},

then we have

Proof.

Without loss of generality (by using a symplectic transformation) let us assume that

\langle x\rangle^{\perp_{s}}=\{0,p^{m},2p^{m},\ldots,(n/p^{m}-1)p^{m}\}\times\mathbb{Z}_{n}.

Setting

\widehat{\mathbf{1}}_{A}^{sym}(x)=P_{A,x}^{sym}(\omega)=\sum_{(a_{1},a_{2})\in A}\omega^{a_{1}},

1+z^{p^{m-1}}+\ldots+z^{(p-1)p^{m-1}}

\sum_{(a_{1},a_{2})\in A}\omega^{a_{1}}=\left(\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}\omega^{j\cdot p^{m-1}}\right)\cdot\left(\sum_{k=0}^{p^{m-1}-1}c_{k}\omega^{k}\right),

with

. Expand it further we obtain

\left(\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}\omega^{j\cdot p^{m-1}}\right)\cdot\left(\sum_{k=0}^{p^{m-1}-1}c_{k}\omega^{k}\right)=c_{0}\left(1+\omega^{p^{m-1}}+\ldots+\omega^{(p-1)p^{m-1}}\right)+g(\omega),

(13)

where

collects all terms of form

with the exponent

being a number that is not divisible by

.

Comparing the part

in (

) with (

) we see that the terms

must be obtained from elements in

whose first coordinate is divisible by

, i.e., elements in
\langle px\rangle^{\perp_{s}}
, therefore we get

\widehat{\mathbf{1}}_{A\cap\langle xp\rangle^{\perp_{s}}}^{sym}(x)=c_{0}\left(1+\omega^{p^{m-1}}+\ldots+\omega^{(p-1)p^{m-1}}\right).

Counting the number of terms then shows that

|A\cap\langle px\rangle^{\perp_{s}}|=c_{0}p.

(14)

By the same reason it is also clear that

c_{0}=|A\cap\langle x\rangle^{\perp_{s}}|.

Plug it back into (

which means

holds for each

.
∎

Lemma 9
.

Let

be subsets of

, and

, then

(ii)

(i)

For

Therefore if

, then we get

Consider the partition of

Lemma 12

If

4.1
Notations

Now let us recall the structure of
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}

p
elements, we denote it by
K
. Counting the number of generators, it is easy to see that there are
p+1
number of proper and non-trivial subgroups in
K
, each of them is cyclic and they mutually intersect trivially. Similar arguments indicate that there are
p^{2}+p
maximal cyclic subgroups in
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}
, they can be divided into
p+1
classes depending on which order
p
subgroup do they contain, and there are precisely
p
number of maximal cyclic subgroups in each class.

Since it is important to keep track of these classes we shall introduce the following projective notation: The order
p

(ap,bp)
will be denoted by
K_{b/a}
(with the convention

) so that

p

and each

The maximal cyclic subgroup generated by

H_{j,k}
, so that the second subscript

actually indicate

contains
K_{k}
, i.e.,

\bigcap_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}}H_{j,k}=K_{k},\quad K_{k}^{\perp_{s}}=(\bigcup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}}H_{j,k})\bigcup K.

For convenience, we slightly adjust the notation for equivalence classes used in the last section: from now on
E_{j,k}
is the set of all generators in
H_{j,k}
, and

C_{j,k}=\{0,h_{j,k},2h_{j,k},\ldots,(p-1)h_{j,k}\},

is its derived set. The following two pictures may help to build intuitions behind these notations.

4.2
Mutual Annihilation

In this subsection we describe a special phenomenon in
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}
, the motivating example is when
A=\{0,\ldots,p-1\}\times\{0,\ldots,p-1\}
, then
A
is a not only a tiling complement of
K
, but also its symplectic spectrum. This is because
Z(\widehat{\mathbf{1}}_{A}^{sym})
is disjoint with
\Delta A
, we show that this disjointness property is owned by all tiling complements of
K
.

Lemma 15
.

If
A\subset\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}
is a tiling complement of
K
, then

A\cap Z(\widehat{\mathbf{1}}_{A}^{sym})=\emptyset.

Proof.

Without loss of generality (by shifting, which does change
Z(\widehat{\mathbf{1}}_{A}^{sym})
we may assume that
(0,0)\in A
. And clearly
(0,0)
is not in
Z(\widehat{\mathbf{1}}_{A}^{sym})
.

Suppose
a\in A\setminus\{(0,0)\}
, then
\ord(a)=p^{2}
since
A
is a tiling complement of
K
, then by Lemma
3
we have

\langle a\rangle=\langle a\rangle^{\perp_{s}}.

If
a\in Z(\widehat{\mathbf{1}}_{A}^{sym})
, then by Lemma
8
we will have

|A\cap\langle pa\rangle^{\perp_{s}}|=p|A\cap\langle a\rangle^{\perp_{s}}|.

Observe that
|\langle pa\rangle|=p
, thus by (
5
) we have
|\langle pa\rangle^{\perp_{s}}|=|\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}|/|\langle pa\rangle|=p^{3}
, and
K
is a subset of
\langle pa\rangle^{\perp_{s}}
. Since
|K|=p^{2}
, and
A
is a tiling complement of
K
, we obtain

|A\cap\langle pa\rangle^{\perp_{s}}|\leq\frac{|\langle pa\rangle^{\perp_{s}}|}{|K|}=p.

Combing these three equations above we get

p\geq|A\cap\langle pa\rangle^{\perp_{s}}|=p|A\cap\langle a\rangle^{\perp_{s}}|=p|A\cap\langle a\rangle|,

i.e.,

|A\cap\langle a\rangle|\leq 1,

which is a contradiction since
|A\cap\langle a\rangle|\geq 2
must hold because
A\cap\langle a\rangle
contains at least the identity and
a
itself.
∎

Lemma 16
.

If
A
is a tiling complement of
K
in
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}
, then

\Delta A\cap Z(\widehat{\mathbf{1}}_{A}^{sym})=\emptyset.

(19)

and
(A,B)
is a symplectic spectral pair for any tiling complement
B
of
A
, while
(A,S)
is a tiling pair for any symplectic spectrum
S
of
A
.

Proof.

Assume the contrary that there exist distinct
a,a^{\prime}\in A
such that
a-a^{\prime}\in\Delta A\cap Z(\widehat{\mathbf{1}}_{A}^{sym})
. Consider now the set

A^{\prime}=A-a^{\prime},

which contains
a-a^{\prime}
, and thus

a-a^{\prime}\in A^{\prime}\cap Z(\widehat{\mathbf{1}}_{A}^{sym}).

(20)

Clearly
|A^{\prime}|=|A|
,
\Delta A^{\prime}=\Delta A
hold, hence
A^{\prime}
is still a tiling complement of
K
, consequently by Lemma
15
we shall have

A^{\prime}\cap Z(\widehat{\mathbf{1}}_{A^{\prime}}^{sym})=\emptyset.

On the other hand, it is also easy to see that
Z(\widehat{\mathbf{1}}_{A^{\prime}}^{sym})=Z(\widehat{\mathbf{1}}_{A}^{sym})
, therefore we shall have

A^{\prime}\cap Z(\widehat{\mathbf{1}}_{A}^{sym})=A^{\prime}\cap Z(\widehat{\mathbf{1}}_{A^{\prime}}^{sym})=\emptyset,

which contradicts (
20
). This establishes (
19
).

Now if
(A,B)
is a tiling pair, then since
\Delta A\cap Z(\widehat{\mathbf{1}}_{A}^{sym})=\emptyset
, by (
7
) we must have
\Delta A\subseteq Z(\widehat{\mathbf{1}}_{B}^{sym})
, which indicates that
(A,B)
is also a symplectic spectral pair. Conversely if
(A,S)
is a symplectic spectral pair, then
|A|=|S|=p^{2}
, and
\Delta S\subseteq Z(\widehat{\mathbf{1}}_{A}^{sym})
, consequently we get

\Delta A\cap\Delta S\subseteq\Delta A\cap Z(\widehat{\mathbf{1}}_{A}^{sym})=\emptyset,

which by Lemma
1
indicates that
(A,S)
is also a tiling pair.
∎

4.3
Counting and equi-distribution

In this subsection we aim to obtain some estimate on the size of the intersection between a tiling/spectral set and different subgroups/equivalence classes. Lemma
17
below and Lemma
20
in the subsequent part described an equi-distribution (of
A
in different equivalence classes or subgroups) phenomenon that is similar to
[
28
]
and references therein.

Lemma 17
.

Let
A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}
, if

\Delta K\in Z(\widehat{\mathbf{1}}_{A}^{sym}),

then

|A|=p^{2}|A\cap K|,

(21)

and for each
k\in\{0,1,\ldots,p-1,\infty\}
we have

|A\cap K_{k}^{\perp_{s}}|=p|A\cap K|.

(22)

Moreover, if
\ord(s)=p^{2}
and
s\in Z(\widehat{\mathbf{1}}_{A}^{sym})
, then

|A\cap\langle s\rangle|=|A\cap K|.

(23)

Proof.

Let

b=|A\cap K|,

and for each
k\in\{0,1,\ldots,p-1,\infty\}
, set

a_{k}=|A\cap(K_{k}^{\perp_{s}}\setminus K)|,

then we have

|A|=a_{0}+\ldots+a_{p-1}+a_{\infty}+b.

Let
x_{k}
be an arbitrary element of
\Delta K_{k}
, then
\langle x_{k}\rangle=K_{k}
,
\ord(x_{k})=p
and thus
\langle px_{k}\rangle^{\perp_{s}}
is the entire group
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}
. By assumption we have
x_{k}\in Z(\widehat{\mathbf{1}}_{A}^{sym})
, thus applying Lemma
8
we get

a_{0}+\ldots+a_{p-1}+a_{\infty}+b=|A|=|A\cap\langle px_{k}\rangle^{\perp_{s}}|=p|A\cap\langle x_{k}\rangle^{\perp_{s}}|=p|A\cap K_{k}^{\perp_{s}}|=p(b+a_{k}).

This equation holds for each
k\in\{0,1,\ldots,p-1,\infty\}
. Adding these
p+1
equations together we get

(p+1)(a_{0}+\ldots+a_{p-1}+a_{\infty}+b)=(p+1)pb+p(a_{0}+\ldots+a_{p-1}+a_{\infty}),

i.e.,

a_{0}+\ldots+a_{p-1}+a_{\infty}=(p^{2}-1)b,

which means

|A|=a_{0}+\ldots+a_{p-1}+a_{\infty}+b=(p^{2}-1)b+b=p^{2}b=p^{2}|A\cap K|.

This establishes (
21
). Plug it into the relation
|A|=p|A\cap K_{k}^{\perp_{s}}|
we get (
22
).

Finally if
\ord(s)=p^{2}
, then
\langle ps\rangle=K_{k}
for some
k\in\{0,1,\ldots,p-1,\infty\}
, therefore we have

p|A\cap K|=|A\cap K_{k}^{\perp_{s}}|=|A\cap\langle ps\rangle^{\perp_{s}}|=p|A\cap\langle s\rangle|,

where the first equality is (
22
) and the last equality follows from Lemma
8
. Dividing
p
out from both sides leads to (
23
).
∎

Lemma 18
.

Let
A
be a subset of
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}
.

(i)

If
|A|=p^{2}
and there are distinct

such that

then

is a symplectic spectral set in
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}
.

(ii)

If
|A|\geq p^{2}
and there are distinct

such that

\Delta K_{k}\cap\Delta A=\left(\bigcup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}}E_{j,k^{\prime}}\right)\cap\Delta A=\emptyset,

Lemma 22

If

(58)

holds for each

Multiplying
p

.

If

(60)

and by (

A subset
A
in
\mathbb{Z}_{n}\times\mathbb{Z}_{n}
is called
periodic
if
A=H\oplus A^{\prime}
for some subgroup
H
. A tiling pair is called periodic if one of the component is periodic. There is a famous result in
[
25
,
26
]
that characterizes periodic factorizations in finite Abelian groups. In the recent paper
[
6
]
it was asked whether one can replace a component in a non-periodic tiling pair with a periodic one, such a property is formulated as the weak periodic tiling property therein. For
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}
we can prove the following:

Corollary 1
.

Let
(A,B)
be a tiling pair in
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}
, if neither of
A,B
is periodic, then one of them can actually be replaced by a periodic set, i.e, either there is some periodic
A^{\prime}
so that
(A^{\prime},B)
is still a tiling pair, or there is some periodic
B^{\prime}
so that
(A,B^{\prime})
is still a tiling pair.

Proof.

Without loss of generality let us just assume both
A,B
contain the identity and
|A|\geq|B|
, then there are two cases:

(1)

|A|=p^{3}
and
|B|=p
.

If there is some
K_{k}
such that
(A,K_{k})
is a tiling pair, then we may take
B^{\prime}=K_{k}
.

Alternatively if there is no such
K_{k}
, then
\Delta A\cap\Delta K_{k}\neq\emptyset
for all
k\in\{0,1,\ldots,p-1,\infty\}
, and by Lemma
12
this implies
\Delta B\cap\Delta K=\emptyset
. Recall by Theorem
1
and Lemma
4
we can also view
B
as a symplectic spectral set, then this is Case (2.2.1) in the proof of Theorem
1
, the spectral to tiling,
p^{2}>|B|\geq p
part. It follows from the derivations there that we can take
A^{\prime}=K_{k}^{\perp_{s}}
for some
k\in\{0,1,\ldots,p-1,\infty\}
.

(2)

|A|=|B|=p^{2}
.

If
Z(\widehat{\mathbf{1}}_{A}^{sym})\cap\Delta K_{k}\neq\emptyset
for all
k\in\{0,1,\ldots,p-1,\infty\}
, then by Lemma
7

(ii)
we can take
B^{\prime}=K
.

Similarly if there is some
K_{k}
and
E_{j,k}
such that
Z(\widehat{\mathbf{1}}_{A}^{sym})\cap\Delta K_{k}\neq\emptyset
and
Z(\widehat{\mathbf{1}}_{A}^{sym})\cap E_{j,k}\neq\emptyset
, then by Lemma
7

(ii)
we can take
B^{\prime}=H_{j,k}
.

Alternatively if there is some
K_{k}
and
E_{j,k}
such that
Z(\widehat{\mathbf{1}}_{A}^{sym})\cap\Delta K_{k}=Z(\widehat{\mathbf{1}}_{A}^{sym})\cap E_{j,k}=\emptyset
, then by (
7
) we must have
\Delta H_{j,k}\subseteq Z(\widehat{\mathbf{1}}_{B}^{sym})
, thus by Lemma
7

(ii)
we can take
A^{\prime}=H_{j,k}
.

Finally we are again in the situation that we can partition the set
\{0,1,\ldots,p-1,\infty\}
into
M
and

, so that

\tilde{M}=\{m:Z(\widehat{\mathbf{1}}_{A}^{sym})\cap\Delta K_{m}\neq\emptyset\},\quad\tilde{M}^{\prime}=\{m^{\prime}:Z(\widehat{\mathbf{1}}_{A}^{sym})\cap\Delta K_{m^{\prime}}=\emptyset\}.

They are both non-empty, and for each

, each

Again Theorem
1
and Lemma
4
shows that
A

1
, the spectral to tiling,
p^{3}>|A|\geq p^{2}
part. As argued there a periodic

Improvements on exponential sums related to Piatetski-Shapiro primes
Li Lu, Lingyu Guo, Victor Zhenyu Guo

Abstract
. We prove a new bound to the exponential sum of the form ∑ h ∼ H δ h ​ ∑ m ∼ M ∑ n ∼ N m ​ n ∼ x ⁡ a m ​ b n ​ 𝐞 ​ ( α ​ m ​ n + h ​ ( m ​ n + u ) γ ) , \sum_{h\sim H}\delta_{h}\mathop{\sum_{m\sim M}\sum_{n\sim N}}_{mn\sim x}a_{m}b_{n}\mathbf{e}\big(\alpha mn+h(mn+u)^{\gamma}\big), by a new approach to the Type I sum. The sum can be applied to many problems related to Piatetski-Shapiro primes, which are primes of the form ⌊ n c ⌋ \lfloor n^{c}\rfloor . In this paper, we improve the admissible range of the Balog-Friedlander condition, which leads to an improvement to the ternary Goldbach problem with Piatetski-Shapiro primes. We also investigate the distribution of Piatetski-Shapiro primes in arithmetic progressions, Piatetski-Shapiro primes in the intersection of multiple Beatty sequences and so on.

1.
Introduction and main results

The Piatetski-Shapiro sequences are sequences of the form

\mathcal{N}^{(c)}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=(\lfloor n^{c}\rfloor)_{n=1}^{\infty},

where
\left\lfloor\cdot\right\rfloor
is the integer part. Piatetski-Shapiro
[
12
]
proved the Piatetski-Shapiro prime number theorem stating that for
1<c<\frac{12}{11}
the counting function

\pi^{(c)}(x)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\#\big\{\text{\rm prime~}p\leqslant x:p\in\mathcal{N}^{(c)}\big\}

satisfies the asymptotic relation

(1.1)

\pi^{(c)}(x)=(1+o(1))\frac{x^{1/c}}{\log x}\qquad\text{~\rm as }x\to\infty.

The admissible range for
c
of the above formula has been extended many times and is currently known to hold for all
1<c<\frac{2817}{2426}
thanks to Rivat and Sargos
[
14
]
. Rivat and Wu
[
15
]
also showed that there are infinitely many Piatetski-Shapiro primes for
1<c<\frac{243}{205}
without an asymptotic formula. We refer the readers to
[
7
]
for more details of the improvements of
c
. The asymptotic relation is expected to hold for all values of
1<c<2
. The estimation of Piatetski-Shapiro primes is an approximation of the well-known conjecture that there exist infinitely many primes of the form
n^{2}+1
.

In this article, we mainly consider three types of problems related to Piatetski-Shapiro primes by proving a new bound on
an special type of exponential sums which improves the bound by Kumchev
[
11
]
. Several results are improved based on our new estimation of the exponential sum. Throughout the paper, we always donote
\gamma\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=c^{-1}
for any subscripts.

1.1.
The ternary Goldbach problem on Piatetski-Shapiro primes

A natural problem to study prime numbers is the ternary Goldbach problem, which is proved by Vinogradov that every odd sufficiently large integer is a sum of three primes. In 1992, Balog and Friedlander
[
2
]
considered the ternary Goldbach problem on Piatetski-Shapiro primes and proved that for every sufficiently large
N

(1.2)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{1}+p_{2}+p_{3}=N\\
p_{i}\in\mathcal{N}^{(c_{i})}\end{subarray}}1=(1+o(1))\frac{\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}\gamma_{3}\Gamma(\gamma_{1})\Gamma(\gamma_{2})\Gamma(\gamma_{3})}{\Gamma(\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}+\gamma_{3})}\frac{\mathfrak{S}(N)N^{\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}+\gamma_{3}-1}}{\log^{3}N},

where
\Gamma(\cdot)
is the Gamma function and
\mathfrak{S}(N)
is the singular series which is defined as

(1.3)

\mathfrak{S}(N)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\prod_{p|N}\bigg(1-\frac{1}{(p-1)^{2}}\bigg)\prod_{p\nmid N}\bigg(1+\frac{1}{(p-1)^{3}}\bigg),

with
1<c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}<\frac{21}{20}
. The admissible range of
c
was improved several times as the following table.

1.2.
Piatetski-Shapiro primes in arithmetic progressions

\pi(x;q,a)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\#\big\{p\leqslant x:p\equiv a\pmod{q}\big\}.

Baker, Banks, Brüdern, Shparlinski and Weingartner

. We list all the investigations of the admissble range of

1.3.
Piatetski-Shapiro primes in the intersection of multiple Beatty sequences

For fixed real numbers
\alpha
and
\beta
, the associated non–homogeneous Beatty sequence is the sequence of integers defined by

\mathcal{B}_{\alpha,\beta}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\left(\left\lfloor\alpha n+\beta\right\rfloor\right)_{n=1}^{\infty},

which are also called generalized arithmetic progressions. Guo, Li and Zhang
[
8
]
proved that there are infinitely many Piatetski-Shapiro primes in the intersection of a finite number of Beatty sequences with some certain restrictions for
1<c<\frac{12}{11}
. With a new estimation of the related exponential sums, we extend the admissible range of
\gamma
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Suppose that
\xi
is a positive integer, and
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{\xi},\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{\xi}\in\mathbb{R}
. Let
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{\xi}>1
be irrational and of finite type such that

1,\alpha_{1}^{-1},\dots,\alpha_{\xi}^{-1}\text{ are linearly independent over }\mathbb{Q}.

For
1<c<\frac{569}{498}=1.1425\dots
, there are infinitely many primes in the intersection of Beatty sequences
\mathcal{B}_{\alpha_{1},\beta_{1}},\dots,\mathcal{B}_{\alpha_{\xi},\beta_{\xi}}
and the Piatetski–Shapiro sequence
\mathcal{N}^{(c)}
.
Moreover, the counting function

\pi^{(c)}_{\alpha_{1},\beta_{1};\dots;\alpha_{\xi},\beta_{\xi}}(x)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\#\big\{\text{\rm prime~}p\leqslant x:p\in\mathcal{B}_{\alpha_{1},\beta_{1}}\cap\cdots\cap\mathcal{B}_{\alpha_{\xi},\beta_{\xi}}\cap\mathcal{N}^{(c)}\big\}

satisfies

\pi^{(c)}_{\alpha_{1},\beta_{1};\dots;\alpha_{\xi},\beta_{\xi}}(x)=\frac{x^{\gamma}}{{\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{\xi}}\log x}+O\bigg(\frac{x^{\gamma}}{\log^{2}x}\bigg),

where the implied constant depends only on
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{\xi}
and
\gamma
.

1.4.
A key result to exponential sums

The improvements of our theorems related to Piatetski-Shapiro sequences rely on the following bound. The definition of exponent pair can be found on Page 30 of
[
5
]
.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
(k,l)
be an exponent pair such that

4k-2l+1>0

and
\gamma
subjected to

\displaystyle\frac{12k+10}{12k-2l+13}<\gamma<1.

Assume further that
H\leqslant x^{1-\gamma+\varepsilon}
and
0\leqslant u\leqslant 1
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{h\sim H}\bigg|\sum_{n\sim x}\Lambda(n)\mathbf{e}\big(\alpha n+h(n+u)^{\gamma}\big)\bigg|\ll x^{1-\varepsilon}.

Thanks to Bourgain’s powerful exponent pair
(\frac{13}{84}+\varepsilon,\frac{55}{84}+\varepsilon)
in Theorem 6 of
[
4
]
, we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 1.7
.

\displaystyle\sum_{h\sim H}\bigg|\sum_{n\sim x}\Lambda(n)\mathbf{e}\big(\alpha n+h(n+u)^{\gamma}\big)\bigg|\ll x^{1-\varepsilon}

provided
\frac{498}{569}<\gamma<1
,
H\leqslant x^{1-\gamma+\varepsilon}
and
0\leqslant u\leqslant 1
.

2.1.
Notations

We denote by
\left\lfloor t\right\rfloor
and
\{t\}
the integer part and the fractional part of
t
, respectively. As is customary, we put
\mathbf{e}(t)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=e^{2\pi it}
. We make considerable use of the sawtooth function defined by

\psi(t)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=t-\left\lfloor t\right\rfloor-\frac{1}{2}=\{t\}-\frac{1}{2}\qquad(t\in\mathbb{R}).

The letter
p
always denotes a prime. For the Piatetski-Shapiro sequence
(\left\lfloor n^{c}\right\rfloor)_{n=1}^{\infty}
, we denote
\gamma\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=c^{-1}
.
\varepsilon
is always a sufficiently small positive number.
x\sim X
means that
x
runs through a subinterval of
(X,2X]
, which endpoints are not necessary the same in different equations and may depend on outer summation variables.

Throughout the paper, implied constants in symbols
O
,
\ll
and
\gg
may depend (where obvious) on the parameters
c,\varepsilon
but are absolute otherwise. For given functions
F
and
G
, the notations
F\ll G
,
G\gg F
and
F=O(G)
are all equivalent to the statement that the inequality
|F|\leqslant C|G|
holds with some constant
C>0
.
F\asymp G
means that
F\ll G\ll F
.

2.2.
Technical lemmas

We need the following approximation of Vaaler
[
17
]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

For any
H\geqslant 1
there are numbers
a_{h},b_{h}
such that

\bigg|\psi(t)-\sum_{0<|h|\leqslant H}a_{h}\,\mathbf{e}(th)\bigg|\leqslant\sum_{|h|\leqslant H}b_{h}\,\mathbf{e}(th),\qquad a_{h}\ll\frac{1}{|h|}\,,\qquad b_{h}\ll\frac{1}{H}\,.

We use the following well-known lemma, which provides a characterization of the numbers that occur in the Piatetski-Shapiro sequence
\mathcal{N}^{(c)}
; see
[
8
, Lemma 2.7]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

A natural number
m
has the form
\left\lfloor n^{c}\right\rfloor
if and only if
\mathbf{1}^{(c)}(m)=1
, where

\mathbf{1}^{(c)}(m)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\left\lfloor-m^{\gamma}\right\rfloor-\left\lfloor-(m+1)^{\gamma}\right\rfloor
. Moreover,

\mathbf{1}^{(c)}(m)=\gamma m^{\gamma-1}+\psi(-(m+1)^{\gamma})-\psi(-m^{\gamma})+O(m^{\gamma-2}).

The following lemma is the famous Weyl-van der Corput inequality, also called the A-process; see
[
5
, Lemma 2.5]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose
f(n)
is a complex valued function and
I
is an interval such that
f(n)=0
if
n\notin I
. If
H
is a positive integer then

|\sum_{n\in I}f(n)|^{2}\leqslant\frac{|I|+H}{H}\sum_{|h|<H}\big(1-\frac{h}{H}\big)\sum_{n\in I}f(n)\overline{f(n-h)}.

The following lemma is derived from the Poisson summation formula, known as the B-process; see
[
5
, Lemma 3.6]
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Suppose that
f
has four continuous derivatives on
[a,b]
and that
f^{\prime\prime}<0
on this interval. Suppose further that
[a,b]\subset[N,2N]
and that
\alpha=f^{\prime}(b)
and
\beta=f^{\prime}(a)
. Assume that there is some
F>0
such that

f^{(2)}(x)\asymp FN^{-2},f^{(3)}(x)\ll FN^{-3},\text{~and~}f^{(4)}(x)\ll FN^{-4}

for
x
in
[a,b]
. Let
x_{\nu}
be defined by the relate
f^{\prime}(x_{\nu})=\nu
, and let
\phi(\nu)=-f(x_{\nu})+\nu x_{\nu}
. Then

\sum_{n\in I}\mathbf{e}\big(f(n)\big)=\sum_{\alpha\leqslant\nu\leqslant\beta}\frac{\mathbf{e}\big(-\phi(\nu)-1/8\big)}{|f^{\prime\prime}(x_{\nu})|^{1/2}}+O\big(\log(FN^{-1}+2)+F^{-1/2}N\big).

We need the following lemma by van der Corput; see
[
5
, Theorem 2.2]
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
f
be twice continuously differentiable on a subinterval
\mathcal{I}
of
(N,2N]
. Suppose that for some
\lambda>0
, the inequalities

\lambda\ll|f^{\prime\prime}(t)|\ll\lambda\qquad(t\in\mathcal{I})

hold, where the implied constants are independent of

and

\sum_{n\in\mathcal{I}}\mathbf{e}\big(f(n)\big)\ll N\lambda^{1/2}+\lambda^{-1/2}.

3.1.
The method of exponential sums

We mention the remarkable result by Heath-Brown
[
10
]
, who proved that the Piatetski-Shapiro prime number theorem (equation (
1.1
)) holds for

(3.1)

\frac{12k+10}{12k-2l+13}<\gamma<1

and some other restrictions to
k,l,\gamma
, where
(k,l)
is an exponent pair; for more details see Section 7 in
[
10
]
. Heath-Brown investigated the exponential sum

(3.2)

\sum_{h\sim H}\bigg|\sum_{n\sim x}\Lambda(n)\mathbf{e}(hn^{\gamma})\bigg|

by splitting the sum into Type I and Type II sums via the Heath-Brown identity. He achieved the best bound (up to now) of the Type II sum and calculated the Type I sum by the method of exponent pair.

We recall that the exponential sum we need to consider is a more complicated form

(3.3)

\sum_{h\sim H}\bigg|\sum_{n\sim x}\Lambda(n)\mathbf{e}\big(\alpha n+h(n+u)^{\gamma}\big)\bigg|.

The sum can also be partitioned into a combination of corresponding Type I and Type II sums by the Heath-Brown identity. Balog and Friedlander
[
2
]
obtained a same upper bound of the Type II sum as Heath-Brown
[
10
]
. However, since the function

\alpha n+h(n+u)^{\gamma}

does not satisfies the prerequisite of the method of exponent pair, people cannot prove a bound of the Type I sum as the same strength as Heath-Brown.

In this paper, we apply Lemma
2.3
and Lemma
2.4
, plus a technical approach to switch the Type I sum into a new exponential sum of the form

\sum_{n}\mathbf{e}\big(f_{4}(m,n)\big)

where

f_{4}(\nu,n)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=(\gamma-1)\gamma^{\gamma/(1-\gamma)}h^{1/(1-\gamma)}\big((n+q)^{\gamma}-n^{\gamma}\big)^{1/(1-\gamma)}(\nu-\theta)^{\gamma/(\gamma-1)}

so that we can apply the method of exponent pair. Eventually we prove a upper bound of (
3.3
) as the same as the bound of (
3.2
) by Heath-Brown. The readers can compare the assumptions of our Theorem
1.6
with the prerequisite (
3.1
) of Heath-Brown’s classical result on Piatetski-Shapiro primes.

3.2.
Details of the mistake in Kumchev
[
11
]

We point out that the estimation of Kumchev
[
11
]
has a minor but unsolvable mistake. In his paper, the Weyl-van der Corput inequality is applied to obtain the equation (25) with a parameter

(3.4)

Q_{2}\leqslant FM^{-1}

with

Q_{2}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\lfloor x^{20(1-\gamma)+24\delta-2+\varepsilon}N^{-1}\rfloor+1,F\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=qx^{\gamma}HN^{-1}

and

(3.5)

\displaystyle N\leqslant x^{20(1-\gamma)+24\delta-2+\varepsilon}.

Additionally, these parameters satisfy all the conditions

(1)

MN\asymp x
,

(2)

q\leqslant Q:=\lfloor x^{2(1-\gamma)+2\delta+\epsilon}\rfloor+1
,

(3)

H\leqslant x^{1-\gamma+\delta+\epsilon}
,

(4)

FM^{-1}\gg x^{\epsilon}
.

It is not too hard to analyze that equation (
3.4
) does not hold all the time. We give an easy case, for example, if we take
q\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=1
and
H\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=x^{1-\gamma+\epsilon}
, we deduce from (
3.4
) that

N\geqslant x^{20(1-\gamma)+24\delta-2+\varepsilon},

which contradicts (
3.5
).

In conclusion, the condition (
3.4
) cannot be satisfied when
q
and
H
run through their domains, which makes Kumchev’s bound on Type I sums fail.

4.
Bounds on exponential sums

In this section we prove Theorem
1.6
by considering sums of the form

\sum_{h\sim H}\delta_{h}\mathop{\sum_{m\sim M}\sum_{n\sim N}}_{mn\sim x}a_{m}b_{n}\mathbf{e}\big(\alpha mn+h(mn+u)^{\gamma}\big),

where
|\delta_{h}|\leqslant 1
,
H\leqslant x^{1-\gamma+\varepsilon}
and
0\leqslant u\leqslant 1
.
If the coefficients
a_{m}
and
b_{n}
satisfy the conditions

|a_{m}|\leqslant 1,\quad b_{n}=1\quad\text{or}\quad b_{n}=\log n,

we denote the sum by
S_{I}
, and if they satisfy the conditions

|a_{m}|\leqslant 1,\quad|b_{n}|\leqslant 1,

we denote it by
S_{II}
.

For
S_{II}
we use the estimate obtained in Proposition 2 of
[
2
]
with a simple adjustment.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\frac{5}{6}<\gamma<1
and
N
satisfies the condition

x^{1-\gamma+\varepsilon}\leqslant N\leqslant x^{5\gamma-4-\varepsilon},

then

S_{II}\ll x^{1-\varepsilon}.

For
S_{I}
we shall prove the following lemma.

Lemma 4.2
.

Assume
(k,l)
is an exponent pair such that

4k+2l-1>0,

and
\gamma,~N
satisfy

\frac{6l+7}{4l+10}<\gamma<1

with

N\geqslant\max\{x^{\frac{4k+6}{4k-2l+1}(1-\gamma)+\frac{2k-1}{4k-2l+1}+\varepsilon},x^{2(1-\gamma)+\varepsilon}\},

then

S_{I}\ll x^{1-\varepsilon}.

Proof.

We only need to deal with the situation when
b_{n}=1
, otherwise we can remove
b_{n}
by a partial integration. Set

f_{0}(m,n)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=h(mn+u)^{\gamma}+\alpha mn.

We trivially have

\displaystyle S_{I}=

\displaystyle\sum_{h\sim H}\delta_{h}\sum_{m\sim M}a_{m}\sum_{n\sim N}\mathbf{e}\big(f_{0}(m,n)\big)

(4.1)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\sum_{h\sim H}\sum_{m\sim M}\bigg|\sum_{n\sim N}e\big(f_{0}(m,n)\big)\bigg|.

Firstly, we treat (
4.1
) in a simple way. We can calculate for
m\sim M
,
n\sim N
,
MN\asymp x
,
h\sim H
,

\frac{\partial^{2}f_{0}}{\partial n^{2}}(m,n)=\gamma(\gamma-1)hm^{2}(mn+u)^{\gamma-2}\asymp Hx^{\gamma}N^{-2}.

By Lemma
2.5
, (
4.1
) and the condition
H\leqslant x^{1-\gamma+\varepsilon}
, we know that

\displaystyle S_{I}\ll

\displaystyle x^{\gamma/2}H^{3/2}M+x^{1-\gamma/2}H^{1/2}

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle x^{\varepsilon}(x^{5/2-\gamma}N^{-1}+x^{3/2-\gamma}).

Thus we have
S_{I}\ll x^{1-\varepsilon}
for all sufficiently small positive
\varepsilon
provided that

(4.2)

\displaystyle 1>\gamma>\frac{1}{2}

and

(4.3)

\displaystyle N\geqslant x^{(1-\gamma)+\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}.

Secondly, we can also estimate
S_{I}
differently. Since

\displaystyle f_{0}(m,n)=f_{1}(m,n)+O\big(g_{1}(m,n)\big),

where

f_{1}(m,n)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\alpha mn+h(mn)^{\gamma}+\gamma uh(mn)^{\gamma-1}

and

g_{1}(m,n)\ll Hx^{\gamma-2},

one has

S_{I}=\sum_{h\sim H}\delta_{h}\sum_{m\sim M}a_{m}\sum_{n\sim N}\mathbf{e}\big(f_{1}(m,n)\big)\mathbf{e}\big(g_{1}(m,n)\big).

Thus

\displaystyle S_{I}-\sum_{h\sim H}\delta_{h}\sum_{m\sim M}a_{m}\sum_{n\sim N}\mathbf{e}\big(f_{1}(m,n)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{h\sim H}\delta_{h}\sum_{m\sim M}a_{m}\sum_{n\sim N}\mathbf{e}\big(f_{1}(m,n)\big)\Big(\mathbf{e}\big(g_{1}(m,n)\big)-1\Big)

(4.4)

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle(HMN)(Hx^{\gamma-2})\ll x^{1-\gamma+\varepsilon}.

The right-hand side of (
4.4
)
\ll x^{1-\varepsilon}
holds for sufficiently small positive
\varepsilon
and we obtain that

S_{I}\ll\sum_{h\sim H}\bigg|\sum_{m\sim M}\sum_{n\sim N}a_{m}\mathbf{e}\big(f_{1}(m,n)\big)\bigg|+x^{1-\varepsilon}.

We apply the Cauchy inequality to the sum over
m
and the Weyl-van der Corput inequality (Lemma
2.3
) to the sum over
n
. It follows that

\displaystyle S_{I}\ll

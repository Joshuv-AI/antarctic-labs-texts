k k -Positivity and high-dimensional bound entanglement under symplectic group symmetry
Sang-Jun Park

Abstract
. We investigate the structure of k k -positivity and Schmidt numbers for classes of linear maps and bipartite quantum states exhibiting symplectic group symmetry. Specifically, we consider (1) linear maps on M d ​ ( ℂ ) M_{d}(\mathbb{C}) which are covariant under conjugation by unitary symplectic matrices S S , and (2) d ⊗ d d\otimes d bipartite states which are invariant under S ⊗ S S\otimes S or S ⊗ S ¯ S\otimes\overline{S} actions, each parametrized by two real variables. We provide a complete characterization of all k k -positivity and decomposability conditions for these maps and explicitly compute the Schmidt numbers for the corresponding bipartite states. In particular, our analysis yields a broad class of PPT states with Schmidt number d / 2 d/2 and the first explicit constructions of (optimal) k k -positive indecomposable linear maps for arbitrary k = 1 , … , d / 2 − 1 k=1,\ldots,d/2-1 , achieving the best-known bounds. Overall, our results offer a natural and analytically tractable framework in which both strong forms of positive indecomposability and high degrees of PPT entanglement can be studied systematically. We present two further applications of symplectic group symmetry. First, we show that the PPT-squared conjecture holds within the class of PPT linear maps that are either symplectic-covariant or conjugate-symplectic-covariant. Second, we resolve a conjecture of Pál and Vértesi concerning the optimal lower bound of the Sindici–Piani semidefinite program for PPT entanglement.



1.
Introduction

The theory of positive linear maps plays a central role in operator algebras and quantum information theory
[
Stø63
,
Cho75
,
Wor76
,
Pau02
,
Stø13
,
Wat18
]
. Among the various refinements of positivity, the notion of

k
-positivity
has emerged as a fundamental yet notoriously subtle concept. A linear map
\mathcal{L}:M_{d_{A}}(\mathbb{C})\to M_{d_{B}}(\mathbb{C})
between matrix algebras is said to be

k
-positive if its ampliation map
\mathrm{id}_{k}\otimes\mathcal{L}:M_{k}(\mathbb{C})\otimes M_{d_{A}}(\mathbb{C})\to M_{k}(\mathbb{C})\otimes M_{d_{B}}(\mathbb{C})
maps positive semidefinite operators to positive semidefinite operators. While complete positivity (
k=\min(d_{A},d_{B})
) admits several powerful characterizations, including the Choi–Kraus representation, the structure of
k
-positive maps for intermediate values of
k

remains poorly understood. In fact, even deciding (
1
-)positivity is computationally hard
[
Gur03
,
GHP10
]
, and several semidefinite programming relaxations for testing
k
-positivity have been proposed recently
[
CCF25
]
. Nevertheless, no general structural classification is known beyond very special cases.

In quantum information theory,
k
-positivity acquires an operational meaning through its intimate connection with quantum entanglement
[
HHHH09
]
. Via the Jamiołkowski–Choi isomorphism
[
Jk72
,
Cho75
,
SSrZ09
]
,
k
-positive maps correspond to entanglement witnesses capable of detecting bipartite quantum states whose
Schmidt number
exceeds
k

[
SBL01
]
. Recall that the Schmidt number
[
TH00
]
of a bipartite mixed state is the minimal Schmidt rank required to prepare the state as a convex combination of pure states, and thus provides a natural hierarchy refining the dichotomy between separable and entangled states. From this perspective, the problem of characterizing

k
-positive maps is
dual
to that of determining Schmidt number regions of quantum states, and hence is a task which is also known to be highly nontrivial both analytically and computationally.

A recent important open problem in entanglement theory concerns the maximal Schmidt number attainable by bipartite quantum states with
positive partial transpose
(PPT)
[
HLLMH18
]
. While the PPT criterion
[
Per96
,
HHH96
]
provides an efficiently checkable necessary condition for separability and is in fact exact in low dimensions
[
Wor76
]
, it becomes increasingly coarse in higher-dimensional systems, where PPT entangled states are known to exist
[
Cho82
,
Hor97
]
. All such states are necessarily
bound entangled
, meaning that their entanglement cannot be distilled by local operations and classical communication
[
HHH98
]
. In contrast, the Schmidt number offers a finer and operationally meaningful quantifier of entanglement, as it characterizes the minimal local dimension required to prepare a given quantum state
[
BD11
]
. Futhermore, there has been several recent works discussing the use of high-dimensional entanglement in various quantum information processing
[
LHF25
,
MGM24
]
. Demonstrating the existence of PPT states with large Schmidt number shows that bound entanglement can be intrinsically high-dimensional, rather than merely a weak remnant of separability. Moreover, such constructions expose fundamental limitations of PPT-based entanglement detection and provide structured test cases for entanglement manipulation and for long-standing open problems, such as the
PPT squared conjecture

[
Chr12
,
CMHW19
]
.

In the case of the
d\otimes d
system, evidence for the existence of PPT states whose Schmidt number scales linearly in
d
was first obtained in
[
SWZ11
]
using tools from asymptotic geometric analysis, although explicit constructions remained elusive.
Early explicit examples exhibited PPT states whose Schmidt number grows logarithmically in

d

[
CYT17
]
, while subsequent works improved this bound to linear scaling in
d

[
HLLMH18
,
PV19
,
Car20
]
. At present, the best known general upper bound is

\lfloor d/2\rfloor
, except for the dimensions
d=3

[
Cho82
,
Hor97
]
and
d=5

[
KG24
]
, and explicit constructions achieving Schmidt number of order

d/2
has been known only in limited settings
[
PV19
,
Car20
]
. The well-known dual formulation of this problem asks for the existence of

k
-positive
indecomposable
(i.e., not of the form
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}+\top\circ\mathcal{L}^{\prime\prime}
for some completely positive maps
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{L}^{\prime\prime}
) linear maps on

M_{d}(\mathbb{C})
as large as possible, a question originally raised in
[
Ter01
]
. This dual viewpoint has also driven much of the development of the theory of
k
-positive maps and its Choi matrices
[
CK09
,
YLT16
,
HLLMH18
,
RBO20
,
MOM25
,
HK25a
,
HK25b
,
vEKD25
]
.

On the other hand, one of the most effective strategies in quantum information theory is to impose
symmetry
with respect to group representations
[
Wer89
,
Hol93
,
VW01
,
MSD17
,
LY22
]
. Quantum states that are invariant under group actions, as well as linear maps that are covariant with respect to such actions, form tractable yet nontrivial subclasses that often admit complete analytic descriptions. This symmetry-based approach has proved extremely fruitful in entanglement theory, where invariance under classical matrix group actions has led to explicit classifications of entanglement, PPT, and separability properties
[
Wer89
,
HH99
,
VW01
,
EW01
,
CK06
,

,
ES13
,
Yu16
,
SN21
,
PJPY24
,
GNS25
,

]
.

In this work, we contribute to both of the above questions on
k
-positivity and Schmidt numbers by exploring new classes of quantum objects possessing symmetry with respect to the
symplectic group
. More precisely, we study linear maps that are covariant under conjugation by unitary symplectic matrices
S
, as well as bipartite quantum states that are invariant under
S\otimes S
or
S\otimes\overline{S}
actions, each parametrized by two real variables. The representation theory of the symplectic group gives rise to algebraic structures that are formally similar to those arising in symmetry classes associated with the orthogonal group
[
VW01
,
PY24
]
; however, the resulting entanglement properties turn out to be drastically different. As we show, this symmetry setting supports a remarkably rich entanglement landscape, including highly entangled PPT states and highly positive yet indecomposable linear maps.

Our first main result is summarized as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
d\geq 4
be an even integer and let
V
be a skew-symmetric unitary matrix, i.e.,
V\in U(d)
with
V^{\top}=-V
.

(1)

All the
k

\mathcal{L}_{p,q}^{(d)}:Z\mapsto\frac{1-p-q}{d}\Tr(Z)I_{d}+pZ+qVZ^{\top}V^{*},\quad Z\in M_{d}(\mathbb{C}),

are completely characterized by linear and quadratic inequalities in the parameters
p
and

. Moreover, there exists a (triangular) set

with nonempty interior such that
\mathcal{L}_{p,q}^{(d)}
is ‘
\big(\frac{d}{2}-1\big)
-positive and indecomposable
’ if and only if
(p,q)\in R
, whereas
\mathcal{L}_{p,q}^{(d)}
becomes decomposable whenever it is
\frac{d}{2}
-positive.

(2)

The Schmidt number of the bipartite quantum state

\rho_{a,b}^{(d)}=\frac{1-a-b}{d^{2}}I_{d}\otimes I_{d}+\frac{a}{d}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}|ii\rangle\langle jj|+\frac{b}{d}(I_{d}\otimes V)\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}|ij\rangle\langle ji|(I_{d}\otimes V)^{*}

in a
d\otimes d
system, whenever it is well defined, is computed explicitly for all values of
(a,b)

S=S^{(d)}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
with nonempty interior such that
\rho_{a,b}^{(d)}
is ‘
PPT with the Schmidt number
d/2
’ if and only if
(a,b)\in S
, while
{\rm SN}\big(\rho_{a,b}^{(d)}\big)\leq d/2
whenever
\rho_{a,b}^{(d)}
is PPT.

We refer to

,

Here we highlight several new consequences of these results:

•

We provide a
broad constructive family
of
(d/2-1)
-positive indecomposable linear maps and PPT states with Schmidt number
d/2
(both within the class of symplectic group symmetry and beyond; see Corollaries

•

When

, the linear map
\mathcal{L}_{p,q}^{(d)}

[

,
Hal06
]

\mathcal{L}^{\rm BH}
, which has proven effective in detecting PPT entanglement. It follows, however, that
\mathcal{L}^{\rm BH}
fails to be
2
-positive

2
. In fact,

is shown to be
atomic
, that is, it is not of the form
\mathcal{L}^{\rm BH}=\mathcal{L}^{\prime}+\top\circ\mathcal{L}^{\prime\prime}
for some
2
-positive linear maps
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{L}^{\prime\prime}
.

•

When
a=b=\frac{1}{d+2}
, the state
\rho_{a,b}^{(d)}
is PPT, and its partial transpose

corresponds to the
Pál–Vértesi state

[

]

\rho^{\rm PV}
, previously shown to have Schmidt number
at least
d/2
. Our results further establish that
\rho^{\rm PV}
has Schmidt number
exactly

.
Moreover, we verify that
\rho^{\rm PV}
is local-unitary equivalent to its partial transpose

, demonstrating that even bipartite states local-unitary equivalent to their partial transpose can attain a high Schmidt number. In fact, an infinite family of such states can be constructed within our class (Corollary

•

As another interesting example, we construct a broad class of quantum states
\rho=\rho_{a,b}^{(d)}
exhibiting a large Schmidt number gap between the state and its partial transpose (Corollary

(1)):

\displaystyle{{\rm SN}}(\rho)-{\rm SN}(\rho^{\Gamma})=d/2-2

\displaystyle=\max\{{{\rm SN}}(\rho_{a^{\prime},b^{\prime}}):\rho_{a^{\prime},b^{\prime}}\text{ is PPT}\}-\min\{\rm SN(\rho^{\prime}):\rho^{\prime}\text{ is entangled}\}.

Questions of this type were raised in
[

]
and first addressed in

, where the gap was of order

In particular, we emphasize a new construction of Breuer–Hall–type maps, which are shown to be

-positive and indecomposable in strong sense and thus remedy a limitation of the original Breuer–Hall map.

Theorem 1.2

(Theorem

, Remark

)
.

For

an even integer and for

a skew-symmetric unitary matrix, we consider the linear maps

\mathcal{L}_{k}^{\rm BH}:Z\mapsto\frac{1}{kd-k-1}(k\Tr(Z)I_{d}-Z-kVZ^{\top}V^{*}),\quad Z\in M_{d}(\mathbb{C}).

Then for
k=1,\ldots,d/2-1
,

(1)

\mathcal{L}_{k}^{\rm BH}
is an
optimal
k
-positive indecomposable linear map
: there exists no other

-positive witness that is able to detect strictly more PPT states with Schmidt number larger than

;

(2)

\mathcal{L}^{\rm BH}_{k}
cannot be decomposed as
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}+\top\circ\mathcal{L}^{\prime\prime}

(k+1)
-positive linear maps
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{L}^{\prime\prime}
.

In this sense, we may refer to

as the

-Breuer–Hall maps
. We remark that the construction of optimal

-positive indecomposable maps has remained largely out of reach beyond the case

As highlighted by our results, there is a sharp contrast between symplectic and

. In the orthogonal case, the corresponding

-covariant linear maps and

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{p,q}^{(OO)}(Z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1-p-q}{d}\Tr(Z)I_{d}+pZ+qZ^{\top},

\displaystyle\rho_{a,b}^{(OO)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1-a-b}{d^{2}}I_{d^{2}}+\frac{a}{d}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}|ii\rangle\langle jj|+\frac{b}{d}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}|ij\rangle\langle ji|,

which are similar to those in

. This contrast underscores the symplectic group as a natural setting in which both strong forms of positive indecomposability and high degrees of PPT entanglement can be studied systematically.

We have two additional applications using our framework. In Section

, we show that the

[

,

]
holds within symplectic group symmetry in two perspectives: (1) any composition of two PPT maps is entanglement-breaking, and (2) any composition of a positive map and a PPT map is decomposable. This result is in fact a simple consequence of Theorem

In Section

[

]
on the semidefinite program introduced by Sindici and Piani

]
for PPT entanglement, defined as follows. For a

p^{\rm PPT}(\rho_{\mathcal{A}}):=\max_{\sigma}\Tr(\Pi_\A\sigma)
subject to
\begin{cases}\text{$\sigma$ is a PPT state},\\
\text{$\Pi_{\mathcal{A}}\sigma\Pi_{\mathcal{A}}=\Tr(\Pi_\A\sigma)\rho_{\mathcal{A}}$,}\end{cases}

where

denotes the projection onto the antisymmetric space

[

, then any optimal PPT state

satisfies

. Furthermore, it was conjectured that the bound
\frac{1}{2r+2}
cannot be improved. We resolve this conjecture by showing that the minimum value of

equals
\frac{1}{d+2}
whenever

is even.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Schmidt number and positive linear maps

Throughout this paper, we use Dirac’s
bra-ket
notations: column vectors
v\in\mathbb{C}^{d}
are written as kets
\ket{v}
and their conjugate transpose
v^{*}\in(\mathbb{C}^{d})^{*}
are written as bras
\bra{v}
. We distinguish these from the
entrywise complex-conjugated vectors

\overline{v}\in\mathbb{C}^{d}
, noting that
v^{*}=(\overline{v})^{\top}
. The standard
inner product
is denoted by
\langle v|w\rangle:=v^{*}w\in\mathbb{C}
and the
outer product
(rank-one operator) by
\outerproduct{v}{w}:=vw^{*}\in M_{d}(\mathbb{C})
. The standard basis in
\mathbb{C}^{d}
is denoted by
\{\ket{j}:=e_{j}\}_{j=1}^{d}
, and we write
\ket{ij}:=\ket{i}\otimes\ket{j}
to denote tensor products of standard basis vectors. Finally, we denote by
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{C}^{d})
the set of
quantum states
, i.e., a positive semidefinite matrix
\rho\in M_{d}(\mathbb{C})^{+}
of unit trace.

Let
H_{A}=\mathbb{C}^{d_{A}}
and
H_{B}=\mathbb{C}^{d_{B}}
be finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces. Any nonzero bipartite vector
\xi\in H_{AB}:=H_{A}\otimes H_{B}
admits a
Schmidt decomposition

[
NC00
]

|\xi\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}{\lambda_{i}}|v_{i}\rangle\otimes|w_{i}\rangle
where
\lambda_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{k}>0
, and
\{v_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
and
\{w_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
are orthonormal subsets in
H_{A}
and
H_{B}
, respectively. The integer
k
and the coefficients
\{\lambda_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
are uniquely determined, and we call
k
the
Schmidt rank
of
\xi
and write
{\rm SR}(|\xi\rangle)=k
. For any natural number
k
, let us define

\mathcal{SN}_{k}(d_{A}\otimes d_{B}):={\rm conv}\{|\xi\rangle\langle\xi|:\xi\in H_{AB},\;\;\|\xi\|_{2}=1,\;\;{\rm SR}(|\xi\rangle)\leq k\}\subset\mathcal{D}(H_{AB})

and write simply
\mathcal{SN}_{k}
when no confusion arises. Then the
Schmidt number
of a quantum state
\rho_{AB}\in\mathcal{D}(H_{AB})
is defined as the smallest natural number
k
such that
\rho_{AB}\in\mathcal{SN}_{k}
, and we denote this by
{\rm SN}(\rho_{AB})=k
. By definition,
\mathcal{SN}_{k}=\{\rho\in\mathcal{D}(H_{AB}):SN(\rho)\leq k\},

and therefore, we have
\mathcal{SN}_{1}\subset\mathcal{SN}_{2}\subset\mathcal{SN}_{3}\subset\cdots
. Note that
1\leq{\rm SN}(\rho_{AB})\leq\min(d_{A},d_{B})
and
\mathcal{SN}_{k}=\mathcal{D}(H_{AB})
for all
k\geq\min(d_{A},d_{B})
. Furthermore,
\mathcal{SN}_{1}(d_{A}\otimes d_{B})
coincides with the set
\mathcal{SEP}(d_{A}\otimes d_{B})
of
separable
states in a
d_{A}\otimes d_{B}
system; every state outside the set
\mathcal{SEP}
is called
entangled
. Another important class of quantum states is the set
\mathcal{PPT}(d_{A}\otimes d_{B})
of
positive-partial-trasnpose (PPT)
quantum states, i.e.,
\rho_{AB}\in\mathcal{D}(H_{AB})
satisfying

\rho_{AB}^{\Gamma}:=(\mathrm{id}_{A}\otimes\top_{B})(\rho_{AB})\geq 0.

A celebrated Horodecki-Perez criterion
[
HHH96
,
Per96
]
is reformulated as
\mathcal{SEP}\subset\mathcal{PPT}
, providing a simple yet still powerful necessary condition for separability.

A linear map
\mathcal{L}:M_{d_{A}}(\mathbb{C})\to M_{d_{B}}(\mathbb{C})
is called
positive
if
\mathcal{L}(M_{d_{A}}(\mathbb{C})^{+})\subseteq M_{d_{B}}(\mathbb{C})^{+}
. For an integer
k\geq 1
,
\mathcal{L}
is called
k
-positive
if the ampliation map
\mathrm{id}_{k}\otimes\mathcal{L}:M_{k}(\mathbb{C})\otimes M_{d_{A}}(\mathbb{C})\to M_{k}(\mathbb{C})\otimes M_{d_{B}}(\mathbb{C})
is positive. Furthermore,
\mathcal{L}
is called
completely positive
or shortly
CP
(resp.
completely copositive; co-CP
) if
\mathcal{L}
(resp.
\top\circ\mathcal{L}
) is
k
-positive for all
k\geq 1
, where
\top:Z\mapsto Z^{\top}
denotes the transposition map. Finally,
\mathcal{L}
is said to be
decomposable
if
\mathcal{L}
is a sum of a CP and a co-CP linear map. It is well-known that the complete positivity of
\mathcal{L}
is equivalent to the
\min(d_{A},d_{B})
-positivity
[
Cho75
]
.

The positivity properties are often efficiently analyized via the Choi–Jamiołkowski isomorphism
[
Jk72
,
Cho75
]
. A (normalized)
Choi matrix
of a linear map
\mathcal{L}:M_{d_{A}}(\mathbb{C})\to M_{d_{B}}(\mathbb{C})
is defined by

C_{\mathcal{L}}:=(\mathrm{id}_{d_{A}}\otimes\mathcal{L})(|\omega_{d_{A}}\rangle\langle\omega_{d_{A}}|)=\frac{1}{d_{A}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d_{A}}|i\rangle\langle j|\otimes\mathcal{L}(|i\rangle\langle j|)\in M_{d_{A}}(\mathbb{C})\otimes M_{d_{B}}(\mathbb{C}),

where
|\omega_{d_{A}}\rangle:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_{A}}}\sum_{j=1}^{d_{A}}|jj\rangle
is the
maximally entangled vector
in
\mathbb{C}^{d_{A}}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{A}}
. Arguably one of the most important results is that
\mathcal{L}
is completeley positive if and only if
C_{\mathcal{L}}
is positive semidefinite
[
Cho75
]
. Concerning the Choi matrices of
k
-positive linear maps, let us recall the following fundamental duality between
k
-positivity and Schmidt number, established in
[
TH00
,
SSrZ09
]

Theorem 2.1
.

For a bipartite quantum state
\rho_{AB}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{C}^{d_{A}}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{B}})
, the following are equivalent:

(1)

\rho_{AB}\in\mathcal{SN}_{k}
, i.e.,
{\rm SN}(\rho_{AB})\leq k
,

(2)

(\mathrm{id}_{d_{A}}\otimes\mathcal{L}^{\prime})(\rho_{AB})
for all
k
-positive linerar maps
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}:M_{d_{B}}(\mathbb{C})\to M_{d_{A}}(\mathbb{C})
,

(3)

\Tr(\rho_{AB} C_{\Le})\geq 0
for all
k
-positive linear maps
\mathcal{L}:M_{d_{A}}(\mathbb{C})\to M_{d_{B}}(\mathbb{C})
,

In particular, a linear map
\mathcal{L}
is
k
-positive if and only if
C_{\mathcal{L}}
is
k
-blockpositive
, that is,

\langle\xi|C_{\mathcal{L}}|\xi\rangle\geq 0\;\;\forall\,\xi\in\mathbb{C}^{d_{A}}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{B}}\text{ with }{\rm SR}(|\xi\rangle)\leq k,

as noted in
[
Jk72
,
SSrZ09
]
. Note that the case
k=1
yields a duality between separability and positivity: a state
\rho_{AB}
is separable if and only if
(\mathrm{id}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{L}^{\prime})(\rho_{AB})
is positive semidefinite for all positive linear maps
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
, recovering the result of Horodecki
[
HHH96
]
. Furthermore, classical convex cone theory implies that several reverse-type duality relations also hold. For example, if a linear map
\mathcal{L}:M_{d_{A}}(\mathbb{C})\to M_{d_{B}}(\mathbb{C})
is
(k-1)
-positive but not
k
-positive, then there exists a quantum state
\rho_{AB}
with
{\rm SN}(\rho_{AB})=k
such that
\Tr(\rho_{AB} C_{\Le})<0
. For these reasons,
(k-1)
-positive (and not
k
-positive) linear maps, and their Choi matrices, are often referred to as
k
-Schmidt number witnesses

[
SBL01
]
, as they are capable of detecting quantum states whose Schmidt number is precisely
k
.

Such duality principles are in fact ubiquitous: they can be formulated within a general framework using the notion of
mapping cones

[
Sr86
,
Sko11
]
together with the Choi–Jamiołkowski correspondence. We refer to
[
GKS21
,
Kye23
]
for a detailed survey in this direction. For our purposes, let us recall another duality relation between PPT states and decomposable linear maps
[
Sr82
]
.

Theorem 2.2
.

The following two equivalences hold.

(1)

A bipartite
d_{A}\otimes d_{B}
state
\rho_{AB}
is PPT if and only if
\Tr(\rho_{AB}C_{\Le})\geq 0
for all decomposable linear maps
\mathcal{L}:M_{d_{A}}(\mathbb{C})\to M_{d_{B}}(\mathbb{C})
.

(2)

A linear map
\mathcal{L}:M_{d_{A}}(\mathbb{C})\to M_{d_{B}}(\mathbb{C})
is decomposable if and only if
\Tr(\rho_{AB}C_{\Le})\geq 0
for all
d_{A}\otimes d_{B}
PPT states
\rho_{AB}
.

An important implication of
Theorems

2.1
and
2.2
is that the problem of finding PPT states with high Schmidt number can be transferred to a problem concerning positive linear maps, as follows.

Corollary 2.3
.

For
d_{A},d_{B}\geq 2
and
1\leq k<\min(d_{A},d_{B})
, the following are equivalent:

(1)

There exists a
d_{A}\otimes d_{B}
PPT state
\rho_{AB}
with
{\rm SN}(\rho_{AB})>k
,

(2)

There exists a
k
-positive indecomposable linear map
\mathcal{L}:M_{d_{A}}(\mathbb{C})\to M_{d_{B}}(\mathbb{C})
.

Proof.

Let us assume
(1)
and suppose every
k
-positive linear map is decomposable. Then Theorem
2.2
implies that, for every PPT state
\rho_{AB}
and a
k
-positive linear map
\mathcal{L}
,

\Tr(\rho_{AB}C_{\Le})\geq 0,

as
\mathcal{L}
is decomposable. By Theorem
2.1
, this implies that
{\rm SN}(\rho_{AB})\leq k
, a contradiction.

The proof of the direction
(2)\Rightarrow(1)
is analogous.
∎

2.2.
Compact group symmetries and twirling operations

Let us recall several symmetry notions and related results in quantum information theory and representation theroy which have been very effectively applied in the analysis of quantum entanglement and
k
-positivity
[
VW01
,
PJPY24
,
PY24
]
. For an introduction to representation theory, we refer the reader to the excellent textbooks
[
Sim95
,
Fol16
]
.

For a compact Hausdorff group
G
, consider (finite-dimensional)
unitary representations

\pi:G\to U(d)
,
\pi_{A}:G\to U(d_{A})
, and
\pi_{B}:G\to U(d_{B})
of
G
. Then we call

(1)

a matrix
X\in M_{d}(\mathbb{C})

\pi
-invariant
if
\pi(x)X\pi(x)^{*}=X
for all
x\in G
,

(2)

a linear map
\mathcal{L}:M_{d_{A}}(\mathbb{C})\to M_{d_{B}}(\mathbb{C})

(\pi_{A},\pi_{B})
-covariant
if
\mathcal{L}\circ{\rm Ad}_{\pi_{A}(x)}={\rm Ad}_{\pi_{B}(x)}\circ\mathcal{L}
for all
x\in G
, where
{\rm Ad}_{U}(Z):=UZU^{*}
. In other words, for all
x\in G
and
Z\in M_{d_{A}}(\mathbb{C})
, we have
\mathcal{L}(\pi_{A}(x)Z\pi_{A}(x)^{*})=\pi_{B}(x)\mathcal{L}(Z)\pi_{B}(x)^{*}
.

Let us denote by
{\rm Inv}(\pi)
(resp.
{\rm Cov}(\pi_{A},\pi_{B})
) the set of all
\pi
-invariant matrices (resp.
(\pi_{A},\pi_{B})
-covariant linear maps). We are often interested in the case that
G
is a subgroup of a unitary group
U(d)
and
\pi_{A}
and
\pi_{B}
are taken as the
fundamental representation

\iota_{G}
of
G
or its
contragredient representation

\overline{\iota_{G}}
, that is,

\displaystyle\iota_{G}

\displaystyle:U\in G\mapsto U\in U(d),

\displaystyle\overline{\iota_{G}}

\displaystyle:U\in G\mapsto\overline{U}\in U(d),

where
\overline{U}
denotes the entrywise complex conjugate of
U
.

\pi
is called
irreducible
if
{\rm Inv}(\pi)=\mathbb{C}I_{d}
(this coincides with the usual definition of irreducibility in representation theory, thanks to Schur’s lemma
[
Sim95
]
). If
\pi
is irreducible, so is its contragredient representation
\overline{\pi}
. For unitary representations
\pi_{A}
and
\pi_{B}
, the
direct sum representation
\pi_{A}\oplus\pi_{B}:G\to U(d_{A}+d_{B})
(resp.
tensor representation

\pi_{A}\otimes\pi_{B}:G\rightarrow U(d_{A}d_{B})
) is given by
(\pi_{A}\oplus\pi_{B})(x)=\pi_{A}(x)\oplus\pi_{B}(x)
(resp.
(\pi_{A}\otimes\pi_{B})(x)=\pi_{A}(x)\otimes\pi_{B}(x)
) for
x\in G
. Two unitary
representations of the same dimension
\pi,\pi^{\prime}:G\to U(d)
are said to be
unitarily equivalent
if there exists a unitary matrix
U\in U(d)
such that
\pi^{\prime}(x)=U\pi(x)U^{*}
for all
x\in G
; we write
\pi\cong\pi^{\prime}
.

It is a well-known fact
[
Sim95
]
that every finite dimensional unitary representation can be decomposed into irreducible subrepresentations (called
irrep decomposition
)

(1)

\pi\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^{l}\sigma_{i}\otimes I_{m_{i}},

where
\sigma_{i}:G\to U(d_{i})
(
i=1,2,\ldots,l
) are mutually inequivalent irreducible sub-representations of
\pi
with multiplicity
m_{i}
. The numbers
l
and
m_{1},\ldots,m_{l}
are uniquely determined. Furthermore, when
m_{i}\equiv 1
, we call
\pi

multiplicity-free
, in which case we have a rather simple description of the space

(2)

{\rm Inv}(\pi)={\rm span}\{\Pi_{1},\ldots,\Pi_{l}\},

where
\Pi_{i}
’s are mutually orthogonal projections onto the subrepresentation spaces of
\sigma_{i}
.

We are additionally interested in two types of symmetrizing operations: for unitary representations
\pi,\pi_{A},\pi_{B}
of
G
,

(1)

\pi
-twirling

\mathcal{T}_{\pi}X:=\mathbb{E}_{G}[{\rm Ad}_{\pi(\cdot)}(X)]=\mathbb{E}_{G}[\pi(\cdot)X\pi(\cdot)^{*}]
for a matrix
X\in M_{d}(\mathbb{C})
,

(2)

(\pi_{A},\pi_{B})
-twirling

\mathcal{T}_{\pi_{A},\pi_{B}}\mathcal{L}:=\mathbb{E}_{G}[{\rm Ad}_{\pi_{B}(\cdot)^{*}}\circ\mathcal{L}\circ{\rm Ad}_{\pi_{A}(\cdot)}]
for a linear map
\mathcal{L}:M_{d_{A}}(\mathbb{C})\to M_{d_{B}}(\mathbb{C})
,

where the expectation is defined with respect to the (normalized) Haar measure of the compact group
G
. These two operations are understood as projections (conditional expectations) onto the spaces
{\rm Inv}(\pi)
and
{\rm Cov}(\pi_{A},\pi_{B})
, resp. That is, we have
{\rm Ran}\mathcal{T}_{\pi}={\rm Inv}(\pi)
and
\mathcal{T}_{\pi}\circ\mathcal{T}_{\pi}=\mathcal{T}_{\pi}
so that a matrix
X
is
\pi
-invariant if and only if
\mathcal{T}_{\pi}(X)=X
; the analogous statement holds true for
\mathcal{T}_{\pi_{A},\pi_{B}}
.

We further collect several general properties of group symmetries and twirling operations which were proved and discussed in detail in
[
VW01
,
PJPY24
,
PY24
,
NP25
]
.

Proposition 2.4

(
[

,
PJPY24
]
)
.

Let
\pi_{A}
and

be unitary representations.

(1)

For

(2)

In particular,

Proposition 2.5

2.3.
Symplectic group symmetry

Let
d\geq 2
be an even integer and let
V
be a skew-symmetric unitary matrix, i.e.,
V\in U(d)
and
V^{\top}=-V
. The
skew-symmetric bilinear form

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{V}:\mathbb{C}^{d}\times\mathbb{C}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
associated to
V
is defined by

\langle x,y\rangle_{V}:=x\cdot Vy=x^{\top}Vy,

where the usual
dot product
above is defined without complex conjugates. This form satisfies the two elementary properties

(4)

\langle x,y\rangle_{V}=-\langle y,x\rangle_{V},\quad\langle x,x\rangle_{V}\equiv 0

for all
x,y\in\mathbb{C}^{d}
, thanks to the skew-symmetry of
V
. The
compact symplectic group
(or
unitary symplectic group
)
Sp(d;V)\subset U(d)
associated to
V
is then defined by

\displaystyle Sp(d;V)

\displaystyle:=\{S\in U(d)\bigm|\langle Sx,Sy\rangle_{V}=\langle x,y\rangle_{V}\text{ for all $x,y\in\mathbb{C}^{d}$}\}

\displaystyle\,\,=\{S\in U(d)\bigm|S^{\top}VS=V\}.

Each element in
Sp(d;V)
is called a
unitary symplectic matrix
. In other words,
Sp(d;V)
is the group of
d\times d
matrices that preserve both the Hermitian inner product and and the bilinear form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{V}
. A standard choice of
V
is

V=\Omega_{d}:=\begin{pmatrix}0&I_{d/2}\\
-I_{d/2}&0\end{pmatrix};\quad\langle x,y\rangle_{\Omega}:=\sum_{i=1}^{d/2}(x_{i}y_{i+d/2}-x_{i+d/2}y_{i}),

in which case we simply denote
Sp(d):=Sp(d;\Omega_{d})
.

We remark on several points concerning these definitions. First, skew-symmetric unitary matrices (and hence symplectic groups) are defined only for
even

d
. Indeed, if
d
is odd, then any skew-symmetric matrix
V
is non-invertible, since

\det V=-\det V^{\top}=-\det V.

Furthermore, a classical result in matrix theory (see e.g.
[
HJ12
, Corollary 4.4.19]
) states that every skew-symmetric unitary matrix
V\in U(d)
is in fact
congruent
to
\Omega_{d}
, i.e., there exists a unitary matrix
U_{V}\in U(d)
such that

(5)

U_{V}^{\top}VU_{V}=\Omega_{d}.

In particular, the map
S\mapsto{\rm Ad}_{U_{V}}(S)=U_{V}SU_{V}^{*}
defines a group isomorphism between
Sp(d)
and
Sp(d;V)
. Consequently, all results in this paper would be independent of the particular choice of
V
. Finally, the group
Sp(d)
is closed under complex conjugation and transposition: if
S\in Sp(d)
, then

S^{\top}\Omega_{d}S=\Omega_{d}\iff\Omega_{d}=\overline{S}\Omega_{d}S^{*}\iff\Omega_{d}=S\Omega_{d}S^{\top},

where we take matrix adjoint and the fact
\Omega_{d}^{*}=-\Omega_{d}
in the second equivalence. In particular, this implies
\Omega_{d}S=\overline{S}\Omega_{d}
and
\Omega_{d}\overline{S}=S\Omega_{d}
for all
S\in Sp(d)
. The same argument gives in general,

(6)

S\in Sp(d;V)\implies VS=\overline{S}V\quad\text{and}\quad\overline{V}\overline{S}=S\overline{V}.

We are mainly interested in classes of linear maps and bipartite matrices that are symmetric with respect to the fundamental representation

\iota_{Sp(d;V)}:S\in Sp(d;V)\mapsto S\in U(d).

For simplicity of notation, let us set

\displaystyle{\rm Inv}(S\otimes S)={\rm Inv}(\iota_{Sp(d)}\otimes\iota_{Sp(d)}),

\displaystyle\quad{\rm Cov}(S,S)={\rm Cov}(\iota_{Sp(d)},\iota_{Sp(d)}),

\displaystyle{\rm Inv}(S\otimes\overline{S})={\rm Inv}(\iota_{Sp(d)}\otimes\overline{\iota_{Sp(d)}}),

\displaystyle\quad{\rm Cov}(S,\overline{S})={\rm Cov}(\iota_{Sp(d)},\overline{\iota_{Sp(d)}}),

in the case
V=\Omega_{d}
, and refer to elements of these spaces as
S\otimes S
-invariant,
S\otimes\overline{S}
-invariant,
(S,S)
-covariant, and
(S,\overline{S})
-covariant, resp. Note that all these classes are closed under complex conjugation; for instance,
{\rm Inv}(S\otimes\overline{S})={\rm Inv}(\overline{S}\otimes S)
and
{\rm Cov}(S,\overline{S})={\rm Cov}(\overline{S},S)
, since
Sp(d)
is closed under the complex conjugation. Furthermore, the
Schur–Weyl duality
for the compact symplectic group (see, e.g.,
[
Wey46
,
GW09
,
Wen88
]
) states that, for
d\geq 4
, the algebra
{\rm Inv}(S\otimes S)
is
3
-dimensional and is described by the action of the Brauer algebra
[
Bra37
]
. More precisely, one has

{\rm Inv}(S\otimes S)={\rm span}\{I_{d}\otimes I_{d},F_{d},(I_{d}\otimes\Omega_{d})|\omega_{d}\rangle\langle\omega_{d}|(I_{d}\otimes\Omega_{d})^{*}\},

where
F_{d}:=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}|ij\rangle\langle ji|
denotes the flip operator. The remaining three spaces can be described explicitly via the relations

{\rm Inv}(S\otimes\overline{S})=(\mathrm{id}_{d}\otimes\top)\big({\rm Inv}(S\otimes S)\big),\quad{\rm Cov}(S,S)=C^{-1}\big({\rm Inv}(S\otimes\overline{S})\big),\quad{\rm Cov}(S,\overline{S})=C^{-1}\big({\rm Inv}(S\otimes S)\big)

as a consequence of Proposition
2.4
. Here,
C^{-1}
denotes the inverse of the Choi isomorphism. These characterizations are summarized in the following proposition.

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
d\geq 4
be an even integer.

(1)

A matrix
\rho\in M_{d}(\mathbb{C})\otimes M_{d}(\mathbb{C})
is
S\otimes{S}
-invariant (resp.
S\otimes\overline{S}
-invariant) if and only if

\rho\in{\rm span}\{I_{d^{2}},F_{d},{\rm Ad}_{I_{d}\otimes\Omega_{d}}(|\omega\rangle\langle\omega|)\}
(resp.
\rho\in{\rm span}\{I_{d^{2}},|\omega_{d}\rangle\langle\omega_{d}|,{\rm Ad}_{I_{d}\otimes\Omega_{d}}(F_{d})\}
).

(2)

A linear map
\mathcal{L}:M_{d}(\mathbb{C})\to M_{d}(\mathbb{C})
is
(S,S)
-covariant (resp.
(S,\overline{S})
-covariant) if and only if

\mathcal{L}\in{\rm span}\{\Delta,\mathrm{id},{\rm Ad}_{\Omega}\circ\top\}
(resp.
\mathcal{L}\in{\rm span}\{\Delta,\top,{\rm Ad}_{\Omega}\}
),

where
\Delta(Z):=\frac{\Tr(Z)}{d}I_{d}
is the completely depolarizing map on
M_{d}(\mathbb{C})
.

Corollary 2.10
.

The set
{\rm Inv}(S\otimes\overline{S})
is a commutative
*
-algebra spanned by three (mutually orthogonal) minimal projections

(7)

\Pi_{1}^{S\overline{S}}=|\omega_{d}\rangle\langle\omega_{d}|,\quad\Pi_{2}^{S\overline{S}}=\frac{1}{2}(I_{d^{2}}+F_{d}^{\Omega}),\quad\Pi_{3}^{S\overline{S}}=\frac{1}{2}(I_{d^{2}}-F_{d}^{\Omega})-|\omega_{d}\rangle\langle\omega_{d}|,

where
F_{d}^{\Omega}:={\rm Ad}_{I_{d}\otimes\Omega_{d}}(F_{d})
. Similarly, the
*
-algebra
{\rm Inv}(S\otimes S)
is spanned by three projections

(8)

\Pi_{1}^{SS}=|\omega_{d}^{\Omega}\rangle\langle\omega_{d}^{\Omega}|,\quad\Pi_{2}^{SS}=\frac{1}{2}(I_{d^{2}}+F_{d}),\quad\Pi_{3}^{SS}=\frac{1}{2}(I_{d^{2}}-F_{d})-|\omega_{d}^{\Omega}\rangle\langle\omega_{d}^{\Omega}|,

where
|\omega_{d}^{\Omega}\rangle:=(I_{d}\otimes\Omega_{d})|\omega_{d}\rangle
.

Proof.

We know that
{\rm Inv}(S\otimes S)=\{S\otimes S:S\in Sp(d)\}^{\prime}
is a three-dimensional
*
-algebra spanned by
I_{d^{2}},|\omega_{d}^{\Omega}\rangle\langle\omega_{d}^{\Omega}|
, and
F_{d}
. Furthermore, the operators
\Pi_{\mathcal{S}}=\frac{1}{2}(I_{d^{2}}+F_{d})
and
\Pi_{\mathcal{A}}=\frac{1}{2}(I_{d^{2}}-F_{d})
are orthogonal projections onto the symmetric / antisymmetric subspaces
\mathbb{C}^{d}\vee\mathbb{C}^{d}
and
\mathbb{C}^{d}\wedge\mathbb{C}^{d}
, respectively. On the other hand, one has

|\omega_{d}^{\Omega}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum_{i=1}^{d/2}(|i+d/2,i\rangle-|i.i+d/2\rangle)\in\mathbb{C}^{d}\wedge\mathbb{C}^{d}={\rm Ran}(\Pi_{\mathcal{A}}),

which implies that
\Pi_{1}^{SS}
,
\Pi_{2}^{SS}=\Pi_{\mathcal{S}}
, and
\Pi_{3}^{SS}=\Pi_{\mathcal{A}}-\Pi_{1}^{SS}
are orthogonal projections satisfying
\Pi_{1}^{SS}+\Pi_{2}^{SS}+\Pi_{3}^{SS}=I_{d^{2}}
. Therefore, these three projections are mutually orthogonal, showing the second assertion. The first assertion follows similarly.

∎

Remark 2.11
.

Similar classes of linear maps and bipartite matrices with symmetries under the general symplectic group
Sp(d;V)
can be characterized. For example, a linear map
\mathcal{L}
is
(\iota_{Sp(d;V)},\iota_{Sp(d;V)})
-covariant if and only if

\mathcal{L}=p\Delta+q\,\mathrm{id}_{d}+r{\rm Ad}_{\overline{V}}\circ\top
.

for some
p,q,r\in\mathbb{C}
. Here, we take the complex-conjugated matrix
\overline{V}
; the covariance property of
{\rm Ad}_{\overline{V}}\circ\top
follows from
Eq.

6
. However, by recalling the congruence relation
Eq.

5
, this map is unitarily equivalent to the
(S,S)
-covariant map

\mathcal{L}^{\prime}=p\Delta+q\,\mathrm{id}_{d}+r{\rm Ad}_{\Omega_{d}}\circ\top={\rm Ad}_{U_{V}^{*}}\circ\mathcal{L}\circ{\rm Ad}_{U_{V}}.

In particular,
\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
share all
k
-positivity and entanglement-related properties. An analogous conclusion holds for
(\iota_{Sp(d;V)},\iota_{Sp(d;V)})
-covariant maps, as well as for
\iota_{Sp(d;V)}\otimes\iota_{Sp(d;V)}
- and
\iota_{Sp(d;V)}\otimes\overline{\iota_{Sp(d;V)}}
-invariant matrices. Consequently, all results concerning
Sp(d)=Sp(d;\Omega_{d})
symmetries established in this paper extend
verbatim
to the general
Sp(d;V)
setting upon replacing
\Omega_{d}
with
V
.

We collect several extensively studied examples of symplectic covariant linear maps and symplectic invariant states.

Example 2.12
.

(1)

The depolarizing maps and the transpose-depolarizing maps

\displaystyle{\rm Cov}(S,S)\ni\mathcal{L}^{\text{dep}}_{p}

\displaystyle:Z\mapsto\frac{1-p}{d}\Tr(Z)I_{d}+pZ,

\displaystyle{\rm Cov}(S,\overline{S})\ni\mathcal{L}_{q}^{\top\text{-dep}}

\displaystyle:Z\mapsto\frac{1-q}{d}\Tr(Z)I_{d}+qZ^{\top},

are characterized by
(U,U)
- and
(U,\overline{U})
-covariance (
U\in U(d)
), respectively. The general maps in
{\rm Cov}(S,S)
and
{\rm Cov}(S,\overline{S})
are threrfore mixtures of depolarizing and transpose-depolarizing maps, up to a “twist” by
{\rm Ad}_{\Omega}
.

(2)

The
isotropic states

[
HH99
]
and the
Werner states

[
Wer89
]

\displaystyle{\rm Inv}(S\otimes\overline{S})\ni\rho^{\rm iso}_{a}

\displaystyle=\frac{1-a}{d^{2}}I_{d^{2}}+a|\omega_{d}\rangle\langle\omega_{d}|,\quad-\frac{1}{d^{2}-1}\leq a\leq 1,

\displaystyle{\rm Inv}(S\otimes S)\ni\rho^{\rm Wer}_{b}

\displaystyle=\frac{1-b}{d^{2}}I_{d^{2}}+\frac{b}{d}F_{d},\qquad-\frac{1}{d-1}\leq b\leq\frac{1}{d+1},

are characterized by
U\otimes\overline{U}
- and
U\otimes U
-invariance (
U\in U(d)
), respectively.

Example 2.13
.

For a skew-symmetric unitary matrix
V\in U(d)
and the complex conjugation map
J:v\in\mathbb{C}^{d}\mapsto\overline{v}
, the antiunitary operator
VJ
is often referred to as a
time-reversal operator
. Furthermore, the associated linear map

({\rm Ad}_{V}\circ\top)(Z)=VZ^{\top}V^{*}=(VJ)Z^{*}(VJ)^{-1}

is called the
time-reversal transformation

[
Bre06
]
, and it turns out to be
(\iota_{Sp(d;\overline{V})},\iota_{Sp(d;\overline{V})})
-covariant. The class
{\rm Cov}(\iota_{Sp(d;\overline{V})},\iota_{Sp(d;\overline{V})})
contains another important example, namely the
Breuer-Hall map

[
Bre06
,
Hal06
]

\mathcal{L}^{\rm BH}(Z):=\frac{1}{d-2}(d\Delta-\mathrm{id}_{d}-{\rm Ad}_{V}\circ\top)(Z)=\frac{1}{d-2}(\Tr( Z)I_{d}-Z-VZ^{\top}V^{*}),

where the normalization is chosen so that
\mathcal{L}^{\rm BH}
is TP. The map
\mathcal{L}^{\rm BH}
is known to be positive and indecomposable, and hence can be used to detect PPT entangled states. In addition, the Choi matrix of
\mathcal{L}^{\rm BH}
is an
optimal
entanglement witness, meaning that there exists no other witness that can detect strictly more entangled states
[
Bre06
]
.
In the following, we will show that
\mathcal{L}^{\rm BH}
is
not

2
-positive; hence, although it can be used to detect PPT entanglement, it cannot be used to detect states whose Schmidt number is strictly larger than
2
.

Example 2.14
.

An important class of entangled states with
S\otimes\overline{S}
symmetry is given by the
Pál–Vértesi states

[
PV19
]
,

\rho^{\rm PV}:=\frac{1}{d+2}(I_{d}\otimes\Omega_{d})|\omega_{d}\rangle\langle\omega_{d}|(I_{d}\otimes\Omega_{d})^{*}+\frac{1}{d(d+2)}(I_{d^{2}}+F_{d}).

It was shown that for all even integers
d\geq 4
, the state
\rho^{\rm PV}
is PPT while satisfying
{\rm SN}(\rho^{\rm PV})\geq d/2
, which is, to date, the largest known Schmidt number of PPT states for general
d
.

In the following section, we will show that the inequality
{\rm SN}(\rho^{\rm PV})\geq d/2
is in fact tight, i.e.,
{\rm SN}(\rho^{\rm PV})=d/2
. Moreover, this bound cannot be improved within either of the symmetry classes
{\rm Inv}(S\otimes S)
and
{\rm Inv}(S\otimes\overline{S})
, by determining the exact region of PPT states having Schmidt number
d/2
.

From now on, we shall consider the following two-parameter families of linear maps and bipartite matrices:

(9)

\displaystyle{\rm Cov}(S,S)\ni\mathcal{L}_{a,b}=\mathcal{L}^{(d)}_{a,b}:Z\mapsto(1-a-b)\frac{\Tr(Z)}{d}I_{d}+aZ+b\,\Omega_{d}Z^{\top}\Omega_{d}^{*},

(10)

\displaystyle{\rm Inv}(S\otimes\overline{S})\ni\rho_{a,b}=\rho_{a,b}^{(d)}:=\frac{1-a-b}{d^{2}}I_{d}\otimes I_{d}+a|\omega_{d}\rangle\langle\omega_{d}|+\frac{b}{d}(I_{d}\otimes\Omega_{d}){F_{d}}(I_{d}\otimes\Omega_{d})^{*}.

By Proposition
2.9
, the families above characterize the
(S,S)
-covariant trace-preserving linear maps and the bipartite
S\otimes\overline{S}
-invariant matrices with unit trace, resp. Furthermore, the maps
\top\circ\mathcal{L}_{a,b}
and the the matrices
\rho_{a,b}^{\Gamma}
characterize the
(S,\overline{S})
-covariant TP maps and the trace-one
S\otimes S
-invariant matrices, resp. For example, up to equivalence, the Breuer-Hall map and the Pál–Vértesi state correspond to

\mathcal{L}^{\rm BH}=\mathcal{L}_{-\frac{1}{d-2},-\frac{1}{d-2}},\quad\rho^{\rm PV}=(\rho_{\frac{1}{d+2},\frac{1}{d+2}})^{\Gamma}

Note that
C_{\mathcal{L}_{a,b}}=\rho_{a,b}
for every
a,b\in\mathbb{C}
, and
\rho_{a,b}
is Hermitian (equivalently,
\mathcal{L}_{a,b}
is Hermitian preserving) if and only if
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
.

The aim of the following sections is to characterize
k
-positivity and decomposability of
\mathcal{L}_{a,b}
and
\top\circ\mathcal{L}_{a,b}
, and to compute the Schmidt numbers of
\rho_{a,b}
and
\rho_{a,b}^{\Gamma}
(whenever they define quantum states). In fact, the following simple but useful observation allows us to restrict attention to only half of the parameter space.

Proposition 2.15
.

For any
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\displaystyle\top\circ\mathcal{L}_{a,b}={\rm Ad}_{\Omega_{d}}\circ\mathcal{L}_{b,a},\quad\rho_{a,b}^{\Gamma}=(I_{d}\otimes\Omega_{d})\rho_{b,a}(I_{d}\otimes\Omega_{d})^{*}.

In particular,
\top\circ\mathcal{L}_{a,b}
is unitarily equivalent to
\mathcal{L}_{b,a}
, and
\rho_{a,b}^{\Gamma}
is local-unitary equivalent to
\rho_{b,a}
.

3.
Positivity of symplectic covariant linear maps

In this section, we investigate various positivity properties of the Hermitian-preserving
(S,S)
-covariant maps
\{\mathcal{L}_{p,q}\}_{p,q\in\mathbb{R}}
defined in
Eq.

9
.

3.1.
Positivity, complete positivity, and decomposability

Let us begin with a characterization of complete (co-)positivity, positivity, and decomposability of
\mathcal{L}_{p,q}=\mathcal{L}_{p,q}^{(d)}
in terms of the parameters
p
and
q
, for arbitrary even dimensions
d\geq 4
. For convenience, we briefly illustrate the
geometric
description of these four regions in Fig.
2
, while their
algebraic
characterizations are given below.

Figure 2.
The complete positivity (top left), complete copositivity (top right), positivity (bottom left), and decomposability (bottom right) regions of
\mathcal{L}_{p,q}

Proposition 3.1
.

The linear map
\mathcal{L}_{p,q}
is completely positive if and only if
(p,q)\in\mathbb{P}_{d}
, where

(11)

\mathbb{P}_{d}=\mathbb{P}_{d}^{(d)}:=\left\{\ (p,q)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:\;\begin{array}[]{lll}p+(1-d)q\leq 1,\\
p+(1+d)q\leq 1,\\
(1-d)p+q\leq\frac{1}{d+1}\end{array}\right\}.

Furthermore,
\mathcal{L}_{a,b}
is completely copositive if and only if
(b,a)\in\mathbb{P}_{d}
.

Proof.

The complete positivity of
\mathcal{L}_{a,b}
is equivalent to the positivity of
C_{\mathcal{L}_{p,q}}=\rho_{p,q}
. Furthermore, it is straightforward to check that

\rho_{p,q}=\Big(\frac{1-p-q}{d^{2}}+p-\frac{q}{d}\Big)\Pi_{1}^{S\overline{S}}+\Big(\frac{1-p-q}{d^{2}}+\frac{q}{d}\Big)\Pi_{2}^{S\overline{S}}+\Big(\frac{1-p-q}{d^{2}}-\frac{q}{d}\Big)\Pi_{3}^{S\overline{S}},

where
\Pi_{1,2,3}^{S\overline{S}}
are mutually orthogonal projections defined in Corollary
2.10
.
Therefore,
\rho_{p,q}\geq 0
if and only if the three coefficients above are nonnegative, which is equivalent to
(p,q)\in\mathbb{P}_{d}
. The second statement follows from Proposition
2.15
:
\mathcal{L}_{p,q}
is co-CP if and only if
\top\circ\mathcal{L}_{p,q}\cong\mathcal{L}_{q,p}
is CP if and only if
(q,p)\in\mathbb{P}_{d}
.
∎

Corollary 3.2
.

An
S\otimes\overline{S}
-invariant matrix
\rho_{a,b}=C_{\mathcal{L}_{a,b}}
as in
Eq.

10
defines a quantum state if and only if
(a,b)\in\mathbb{P}_{d}
.

Interestingly, the positivity and decomposability of
\mathcal{L}_{p,q}
are also simple to characterize and turn out to be distinct (Fig.
2
) although both to properties are in general highly nontrivial to characterize.

Theorem 3.3
.

The positivity and decomposability regions of
\mathcal{L}_{p,q}
is completely described by the sets
\mathbb{P}_{1}=\mathbb{P}_{1}^{(d)}
and
\mathbb{D}=\mathbb{D}^{(d)}
, respectivelty, where

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{1}

\displaystyle:=\left\{(p,q)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:\;\begin{array}[]{lll}p+q\leq 1,\\
p+(1-d)q\leq 1,\\
(1-d)p+q\leq 1\end{array}\right\},

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}

\displaystyle:=\left\{(p,q)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:\;\begin{array}[]{lll}-\frac{2+d}{d^{2}-d-2}\leq p+q\leq 1,\\
p+(1-d)q\leq 1,\\
(1-d)p+q\leq 1\end{array}\right\}.

In particular,
\mathcal{L}_{p,q}
is positive and indecomposable in the parameter region
\begin{cases}p+q<-\frac{2+d}{d^{2}-d-2},\\
p+(1-d)q\leq 1,\\
(1-d)p+q\leq 1.\end{cases}

Proof.

By convexity, the positivity of
\mathcal{L}_{p,q}
is equivalent to the condition

\mathcal{L}_{p,q}(|v\rangle\langle v|)=\frac{1-p-q}{d}I_{d^{2}}+p|v\rangle\langle v|+q|\Omega_{d}\overline{v}\rangle\langle\Omega_{d}\overline{v}|\geq 0

for all unit vectors
v\in\mathbb{C}^{d}
. However, we have
v\perp\Omega_{d}\overline{v}
for all
v
by Eq. (
4
):

\langle v|\Omega_{d}\overline{v}\rangle=\langle\overline{v},\overline{v}\rangle_{\Omega}=0.

Consequently,
\mathcal{L}_{p,q}(|v\rangle\langle v|)\geq 0
if and only if the three inequalities

\frac{1-p-q}{d}\geq 0,\quad\frac{1-p-q}{d}+p\geq 0,\quad\frac{1-p-q}{d}+q\geq 0,

which is further equivalent to
(p,q)\in\mathbb{P}_{1}
. Since the condition above is independent of the choice of
v
, this precisely characterizes the positivity condition for
\mathcal{L}_{p,q}
.

Now suppose
\mathcal{L}_{p,q}
is decomposable, and write
\mathcal{L}_{p,q}=\mathcal{L}^{(1)}+\mathcal{L}^{(2)}
where
\mathcal{L}^{{}^{\prime}}
is CP and
\mathcal{L}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}
is co-CP. By applying the
(S,S)
-twirling operation
\mathcal{T}_{S,S}:=\mathcal{T}_{\iota_{Sp(d)},\iota_{Sp(d)}}
, we have
\mathcal{L}_{p,q}=\mathcal{T}_{S,S}\mathcal{L}^{{}^{\prime}}+\mathcal{T}_{S,S}{\mathcal{L}^{\prime\prime}}
where
\mathcal{T}_{S,S}\mathcal{L}^{{}^{\prime}}
and
\mathcal{T}_{S,S}\mathcal{L}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}
are
(S,S)
-covariant CP and is co-CP maps, resp, by Proposition
2.5
. Therefore, the trace-preserving property of
\mathcal{L}_{p,q}
, combined with Proposition
3.1
, implies that

\mathcal{L}_{p,q}=\lambda\mathcal{L}_{p_{1},q_{1}}+(1-\lambda)\mathcal{L}_{p_{2},q_{2}}=\mathcal{L}_{\lambda p_{1}+(1-\lambda)p_{2},\lambda q_{1}+(1-\lambda)q_{2}}

for some
(p_{1},q_{1})\in\mathbb{P}_{d}
,
(q_{2},p_{2})\in\mathbb{P}_{d}
, and
\lambda\in[0,1]
. Consequently,
\mathcal{L}_{p,q}
is decomposable if and only if
(p,q)
is in the convex hull of
\mathbb{P}_{d}
and its reflection
\mathbb{P}_{d}^{\top}=\{(q,p):(p,q)\in\mathbb{P}_{d}\}
, that is,

(p,q)\in{\rm conv}(\mathbb{P}_{d}\cup\mathbb{P}_{d}^{\top})=\mathbb{D}.

∎

and the complete positivity region

.

Together with Fig.

Corollary 3.6
.

For every even integer
d\geq 4
, the set
\mathbb{P}_{k}^{(d)}\setminus\mathbb{D}^{(d)}
is nonempty for all
k=1,\ldots,\frac{d}{2}-1
, whereas
\mathbb{P}_{d/2}^{(d)}\subset\mathbb{D}^{(d)}
. In particular, the family of linear maps

\{\mathcal{L}_{p,q}^{(d)}:(p,q)\in\mathbb{P}_{d/2-1}^{(d)}\setminus\ \mathbb{D}^{(d)}\}=\left\{\mathcal{L}_{p,q}^{(d)}:\;\begin{array}[]{lll}p+q<-\frac{2+d}{d^{2}-d-2},\\
p+(1-d)q\leq 1,\\
(1-(d/2-1)d)p+q\leq 1\end{array}\right\}

provides the explicit examples of
(\frac{d}{2}-1)
-positive indecomposable linear maps on
M_{d}(\mathbb{C})
while every
\frac{d}{2}
-positive
(S,S)
-covariant linear maps are decomposable.

Proof.

It is straightforward to verify that the point
P=(-\frac{2}{d^{2}-3d},-\frac{d-2}{d^{2}-3d})
satisfies the inequalities above, and hence
P\in\mathbb{P}_{d/2-1}\setminus\mathbb{D}
. On the other hand, the boundary line segment of
\mathbb{P}_{d/2}
connecting the vertices
(-\frac{2}{d^{2}-d-2},-\frac{d}{d^{2}-d-2})
and
(-\frac{2}{d^{2}-2},0)
lies entirely within the half-plane

. This implies
\mathbb{P}_{d/2}\subset\mathbb{D}
.
∎

As a concrete example, the linear map

(24)

\mathcal{L}_{P}:Z\in M_{d}(\mathbb{C})\mapsto\frac{1}{d^{2}-3d}\big((d-2)\Tr( Z)I_{d}-2Z-(d-2)\Omega_{d}Z^{\top}\Omega_{d}^{*}\big)

considered in the proof of Corollary

is
(\frac{d}{2}-1)
-positive and indecomposable.
Note that the point

corresponds to the unique extreme point of
\mathbb{P}_{d/2-1}
that is not contained in
\mathbb{D}
. For each

, the similar property holds for the point
(\frac{-1}{kd-k-1},\frac{-k}{kd-k-1})\in\mathbb{P}_{k}
, We refer to the associated linear map as the

-Breuer-Hall map
, defined by:

(25)

\mathcal{L}^{\rm BH}_{k}:=\mathcal{L}_{\frac{-1}{kd-k-1},\frac{-k}{kd-k-1}}:Z\mapsto\frac{1}{kd-k-1}\big(k\Tr(Z)I_{d}-Z-k\Omega_{d}Z^{\top}\Omega_{d}^{*}\big).

Note that
\mathcal{L}^{\rm BH}_{1}=\mathcal{L}^{\rm BH}
as introduced in Example
2.13
. These maps can be used to detect quantum states that are PPT but have Schmidt number larger than
k
. In the following, we establish a much stronger property, namely the optimality of the (Choi matrix of the)

k
-Breuer-Hall map in the sense of
[
LKCH00
,
SBL01
,
Bre06
]
. Recall that a

-positive (and not
(k+1)
-positive) indecomposable map
\mathcal{L}
is called

-Schmidt number witness

\{\rho\in\mathcal{PPT}:\Tr(\rho C_{\Le})<0\}\subseteq\{\rho\in\mathcal{PPT}:\Tr(\rho C_{\Le'})<0\}\implies\mathcal{L}^{\prime}=c\mathcal{L}\text{ for some $c>0$}.

It is further shown (we refer to

LKCH00

[

]

\mathcal{L}
is optimal if and only if there is no nonzero decomposable map
\mathcal{L}^{\prime\prime}
such that the map
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}=\mathcal{L}-\mathcal{L}^{\prime\prime}
is
k
-positive. The construction of such linear maps has remained largely beyond reach, except in the case

Theorem 3.7
.

For any even
d\geq 4
and for any
k=1,\ldots,\frac{d}{2}-1
, the
k
-Breuer-Hall map
\mathcal{L}_{k}^{\rm BH}
is an optimal
k
-positive indecomposable linear map.

Proof.

Suppose
\mathcal{L}_{k}^{\rm BH}=\mathcal{L}^{\prime}+\mathcal{L}^{\prime\prime}
for some
k
-positive map
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
and a decomposable map
\mathcal{L}^{\prime\prime}
. As in the proof of Theorem
3.3
, we take the
(S,S)
-twirling
\mathcal{T}_{S,S}
to have

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{k}^{\rm BH}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{T}_{S,S}\mathcal{L}^{\prime}+\mathcal{T}_{S,S}\mathcal{L}^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=\lambda\mathcal{L}_{p_{1},q_{1}}+(1-\lambda)\mathcal{L}_{p_{2},q_{2}}=\mathcal{L}_{\lambda p_{1}+(1-\lambda)p_{2},\lambda q_{1}+(1-\lambda)q_{2}}

for some

,
(p_{2},q_{2})\in\mathbb{D}
, and
\lambda\in[0,1]
. By recalling the geometry of
\mathbb{P}_{k}
and
\mathbb{D}
as in Figs.

and

, the point
\big(\frac{-1}{kd-k-1},\frac{-k}{kd-k-1}\big)
is a convex combination of

only if

\Tr(C_{\Le''})=\Tr(\T_{\overline{S}\otimes S}C_{\Le''})=\Tr(C_{\T_{S,S}\Le''})=0.

Since

\mathcal{L}^{\prime\prime}=0

(see
[

∎

Remark 3.9
.

Motivated by properties of finite-dimensional operator systems, a recent work
[
ADMH+24
]
introduced the quantities

\displaystyle r_{k}(M_{d})

\displaystyle:=\sup\{\|\phi\|_{cb}:\phi:\mathcal{S}\to M_{d}(\mathbb{C})\text{ unital $k$-positive}\},

\displaystyle d_{k}(M_{d})

\displaystyle:=\sup\{\|\phi\|_{cb}:\phi:M_{d}(\mathbb{C})\to\mathcal{S}\text{ unital $k$-positive}\}

for
1\leq k\leq d
, where the supremum ranges over all operator systems
\mathcal{S}
and
\|\phi\|_{cb}=\sup_{n\geq 1}\|\mathrm{id}_{n}\otimes\phi\|
denotes the
completely bounded norm
. The value of
r_{k}(M_{d})
is computed exactly as
\frac{2d-k}{k}
, while

admits the bounds

\max\left(1+\frac{2(d-k)}{d(kd-1)},c\sqrt{\frac{d}{k}}\right)\leq d_{k}(M_{d})\leq\frac{2d-k}{k}

for a universal constant
c>0
. Moreover, the explicit lower bound is attained by the
k
-reduction map
\mathcal{L}_{k}^{\rm red}=\mathcal{L}_{\frac{-1}{kd-1},0}
. It is therefore natural to ask whether our family
\{\mathcal{L}_{a,b}\}_{(a,b)\in\mathbb{P}_{k}}
yields an improved lower bound. Although we do not include the details here, one finds that the supremum
\sup_{(a,b)\in\mathbb{P}_{k}}\|\mathcal{L}_{a,b}\|_{cb}
is still attained by
\mathcal{L}_{k}^{\rm red}
. In particular, it is computed that

\|\mathcal{L}_{k}^{\rm BH}\|_{cb}=1+\frac{2(d-2k)}{d(kd-k-1)}<1+\frac{2(d-k)}{d(kd-1)}=\|\mathcal{L}_{k}^{\rm red}\|_{cb}

for
1\leq k\leq d/2
. This shows that extremality in
k
-positive indecomposability in general does not lead to an improvement of the completely bounded norm.

\frac{1}{d-3}a+b>\frac{1}{d+1}
implies that

\Tr(C_{\Le_{d/2-1}^{\rm BH}} \rho_{a,b})=\Tr(\rho_{\frac{-2}{d^2-3d},\frac{-d+2}{d^2-3d}}\;\rho_{a,b})<0,

by comparison with Eq. (
26
). Furthermore, for all unit vector
v\in\mathbb{C}^{d}
and for any
k
, we have

\Tr(C_{\Le_k^{\rm BH}}\, |v\otimes\Om_dv\ra\la v\otimes\Om_d v|)=\frac{1}{d(kd-k-1)}(k-\big|\langle\overline{v}|\Omega_{d}v\rangle\big|^{2}-k|\langle v|v\rangle|^{2})=0.

Putting everything together, we conclude that
\Tr\big( C_{\Le_{k}^{\rm BH}}\,\rho_{AB}\big)<0
and therefore
{\rm SN}(\rho_{AB})\geq d/2
.
∎

We conclude this section by suggesting further examples of PPT states that may improve the current best-known bounds on the Schmidt number.

Corollary 4.9
.

Suppose
(a,b)
belongs to the interior of the set
\mathbb{T}\setminus\mathbb{S}_{d/2-1}
. Then for sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
, the state

\rho_{AB}=(1-\varepsilon)\rho_{a,b}+\varepsilon|\omega_{d}^{\Omega}\rangle\langle\omega_{d}^{\Omega}|=(1-\varepsilon)\rho_{a,b}+\varepsilon(I_{d}\otimes\Omega_{d})|\omega_{d}\rangle\langle\omega_{d}|(I_{d}\otimes\Omega_{d})^{*}

is PPT with
{\rm SN}(\rho_{AB})\geq d/2
.

Proof.

We first note that
\langle\omega_{d}|\omega_{d}^{\Omega}\rangle=0
and
\langle\omega_{d}^{\Omega}|F_{d}^{\Omega}|\omega_{d}^{\Omega}\rangle=\langle\omega_{d}|F_{d}|\omega_{d}\rangle=1
, and hence

\Tr(C_{\Le_k^{\rm BH}} |\om_d^{\Om}\ra\la\om_d^{\Om}|)=\frac{1}{d(kd-k-1)}(k-0-k)=0,\quad k=1,\ldots,d/2-1.

Therefore, the same arguement with the proof of Corollary
4.8
shows that
{\rm SN}(\rho_{AB})\geq d/2
. On the other hand, as in the proof of Proposition
3.1
, we have

\rho_{a,b}^{\Gamma}=\Big(\frac{1-a-b}{d^{2}}+b-\frac{a}{d}\Big)\Pi_{1}^{SS}+\Big(\frac{1-a-b}{d^{2}}+\frac{a}{d}\Big)\Pi_{2}^{SS}+\Big(\frac{1-a-b}{d^{2}}-\frac{a}{d}\Big)\Pi_{3}^{SS},

which is strictly positive whenever
(a,b)\in{\rm int}\,\mathbb{T}
. Therefore,
\rho_{AB}^{\Gamma}\geq 0
for sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
.
∎

5.
The PPT squared conjecture under symplectic group symmetry

Initially inspired by the theory of quantum repeaters
[
BCHW15
,
CF17
]
, the
PPT squared conjecture
has since emerged as one of the central open problems in quantum information theory, with recent connections to operator theory and operator algebras. We briefly recall its formulation below.

Conjecture 5.1

(
[
Chr12
,
CMHW19
]
)
.

The composition of any two
PPT
linear maps is
entanglement breaking
. In other words, if
\mathcal{L}_{1}
and
\mathcal{L}_{2}
are CP linear maps whose Choi matrices are PPT, then the Choi matrix of
\mathcal{L}_{1}\circ\mathcal{L}_{2}
is separable.

The conjecture has resolved only in low dimensions
d=2
and
d=3

[
CMHW19
,
CYT19
]
. A plethora of works have established the conjecture in various restricted settings or proved weaker variants, e.g., showing that repeated self-composition of a PPT map eventually yields an entanglement-breaking channel
[
LG15
,
KMP17
,
RJP18
,
CYZ18
,
HRF20
]
. Notably, the conjecture is known to be equivalent to the following formulation in terms of positive maps
[
CMHW19
,
GKS21
]
:

Conjecture 5.2
.

For any positive linear map
\mathcal{L}_{1}
and any PPT linear map
\mathcal{L}_{2}
, the composition
\mathcal{L}_{1}\circ\mathcal{L}_{2}
is decomposable.

On the other hand, several case studies have examined the conjecture within specific families of quantum channels exhibiting group symmetries
[
CMHW19
,
SN22
,
NP25
]
, all of which provide further supporting evidence for the PPT squared conjecture. In this section, we contribute additional evidence by verifying both Conjectures
5.1
and
5.2
for the class of linear maps that are covariant with respect to the symplectic group. Our result is particularly noteworthy, as it applies to settings where the existence of highly positive indecomposable maps and PPT states with a large degree of entanglement are guaranteed by Corollaries
4.3
and
4.3
.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
d\geq 4
be an even integer and let
\mathcal{L}_{1},\mathcal{L}_{2}:M_{d}(\mathbb{C})\to M_{d}(\mathbb{C})
be two Hermitian-preserving linear maps.

(1)

If both
\mathcal{L}_{1}
and
\mathcal{L}_{2}
are PPT maps that are either
(S,S)
- or
(S,\overline{S})
-covariant, then the composition
\mathcal{L}_{1}\circ\mathcal{L}_{2}
is entanglement breaking.

(2)

If
\mathcal{L}_{1}
is a positive map that is either
(S,S)
- or
(S,\overline{S})
-covariant, and
\mathcal{L}_{2}
is a PPT map that is either
(S,S)
- or
(S,\overline{S})
-covariant, then both compositions
\mathcal{L}_{1}\circ\mathcal{L}_{2}
and
\mathcal{L}_{1}\circ\mathcal{L}_{2}
are decomposable.

Proof.

For both two statements, we may assume that
\mathcal{L}_{1}
and
\mathcal{L}_{2}
are trace-preserving. Furthermore, for any
(S,S)
-covariant linear map
\mathcal{L}_{a,b}
defined in Eq. (
9
), it is simple to check
\top\circ\mathcal{L}_{a,b}=\mathcal{L}_{a,b}\circ\top\in{\rm Cov}(S,\overline{S})
. Therefore, any composition between TP linear maps which are either
(S,S)
-covariant or
(S,\overline{S})
-covariant is of the form

\mathcal{L}_{a,b}\circ\mathcal{L}_{p,q}\text{ \quad or \quad}\top\circ\mathcal{L}_{a,b}\circ\mathcal{L}_{p,q}

for some
(a,b),(p,q)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Since the positivity, PPT property, entanglement breaking property, and decomposability of lienar maps are all invariant under the composition of
\top
, we may consider only the case
\mathcal{L}_{1}=\mathcal{L}_{a,b}
and
\mathcal{L}_{2}=\mathcal{L}_{p,q}
. In this case, it is also simple to further verify

\mathcal{L}_{a,b}\circ\mathcal{L}_{p,q}=\mathcal{L}_{ap+bq,aq+bp}

from the reltations
\Delta\circ\Delta=\Delta\circ({\rm Ad}_{\Omega}\circ\top)=\Delta
and
({\rm Ad}_{\Omega}\circ\top)\circ({\rm Ad}_{\Omega}\circ\top)=\mathrm{id}
.

Now suppose both
\mathcal{L}_{1}
and
\mathcal{L}_{2}
are PPT, that is,
(a,b),(p,q)\in\mathbb{T}
, and let us show
(ap+bq,aq+bp)\in\mathbb{S}_{1}
. Since the map
\mathcal{L}_{1}\circ\mathcal{L}_{2}
is already guaranteed to be PPT, it suffices to show that

(ap+bq)+(aq+bp)=(a+b)(p+q)\leq\frac{1}{d+1},

by comparing the conditions in Theorem
4.1
. However, since the condition
(a,b),(p,q)\in\mathbb{T}
implies
|a+b|,|p+q|\leq\frac{1}{d+2}+\frac{1}{d+2}=\frac{2}{d+2}
, we actually have
(a+b)(p+q)\leq\frac{4}{(d+2)^{2}}<\frac{1}{d+1}
. This proves the first assertion.

For the second assertion, suppose
(a,b)\in\mathbb{P}_{1}
and
(p,q)\in\mathbb{T}
. Similarly, it suffices to check
(ap+bq)+(aq+bp)=(a+b)(p+q)\geq-\frac{2+d}{d^{2}-d-2}

from Theorem
3.3
, under the additional assumptions that
(a,b)\in\mathbb{P}_{1}\setminus\mathbb{D}
and
(p,q)\in\mathbb{T}\setminus\mathbb{S}_{1}
. In this case, the conditions imply that

\displaystyle-\frac{d+2}{d^{2}-d-2}>\,

\displaystyle a+b\geq-\frac{1}{d-2}-\frac{1}{d-2}=-\frac{2}{d-2};

\displaystyle\quad\frac{1}{d+1}<\,

\displaystyle p+q\leq\frac{1}{d+2}+\frac{1}{d+2}=\frac{2}{d+2},

and hence we actually have
(a+b)(p+q)\geq-\frac{2}{d-2}\cdot\frac{2}{d+2}=-\frac{4}{d^{2}-4}>-\frac{d+2}{d^{2}-d-2}
, which completes the proof.
∎

It is natural to expect that Conjectures
5.1
and
5.2
remain equivalent when restricted to classes of maps defined by general group symmetries. However, we were unable to find a complete argument establishing this equivalence in full generality.

Question 5.4
.

For any two unitary representations
\pi_{A}
and
\pi_{B}
of a compact group
G
and for two covariant maps
\mathcal{L}_{1}\in{\rm Cov}(\pi_{A},\pi_{B})
and
\mathcal{L}_{2}\in{\rm Cov}(\pi_{B},\pi_{A})
, are the following equivalent?

(1)

\mathcal{L}_{1}\circ\mathcal{L}_{2}
is entanglement-breaking whenever
\mathcal{L}_{1}
and
\mathcal{L}_{2}
are PPT;

(2)

\mathcal{L}_{1}\circ\mathcal{L}_{2}
is decomposable whenever
\mathcal{L}_{1}
is positive and
\mathcal{L}_{2}
is PPT.

6.
Optimal lower bounds for the Sindici–Piani semidefinite program

In order to generate PPT entanglement, Sindici and Piani
[
SP18
]
proposed the following semidefinite program (SDP). Let
\Pi_{\mathcal{A}}=\frac{1}{2}(I_{d^{2}}+F_{d})
denote the projection onto the antisymmetric subspace of
\mathbb{C}^{d}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d}
. Given an antisymmetric state
\rho_{\mathcal{A}}
, i.e., a state satisfying
\Pi_{\mathcal{A}}\rho_{\mathcal{A}}\Pi_{\mathcal{A}}=\rho_{\mathcal{A}}
, define

p^{\rm PPT}(\rho_{\mathcal{A}}):=\max_{\sigma}\Tr(\Pi_\A\sigma)
subject to
\begin{cases}\text{$\sigma$ is a PPT state},\\
\text{$\Pi_{\mathcal{A}}\sigma\Pi_{\mathcal{A}}=\Tr(\Pi_\A\sigma)\rho_{\mathcal{A}}$.}\end{cases}

It was shown that
p^{\rm PPT}
admits a universal upper bound
p^{\rm PPT}(\rho_{\mathcal{A}})\leq\frac{1}{2}
, and that equality is attained whenever an optimal solution
\sigma^{*}
can be chosen to be separable. In particular, if
p^{\rm PPT}(\rho_{\mathcal{A}})<\frac{1}{2}
, then any optimal state
\sigma^{*}
is necessarily PPT entangled.

The quantity
p^{\rm PPT}
was further studied by Pál and Vértesi
[
PV19
]
in their construction of PPT states with high Schmidt number. To this end, for
d\geq 2
, we consider the optimal lower bound

p_{\min}(d):=\min_{\rho_{\mathcal{A}}}p^{\rm PPT}(\rho_{\mathcal{A}})

where the minimization is taken over all
d\otimes d
antisymmetric states
\rho_{\mathcal{A}}
. By definition, the sequence satisfies
p_{\min}(d)\leq p_{\min}(d-1)\leq\cdots\leq p_{\min}(2)\leq 1/2
. For all antisymmetric state
\rho_{\mathcal{A}}
, it is shown that
p^{\rm PPT}(\rho_{\mathcal{A}})\geq p_{\min}({\rm SN}(\rho_{\mathcal{A}}))
and the Schmidt number
{\rm SN}(\rho_{\mathcal{A}})
is always an even integer. In other words, if
p^{\rm PPT}(\rho_{\mathcal{A}})<p_{\min}(2r)
for some
r\geq 1
, then it necessarily implies that
{\rm SN}(\rho_{\mathcal{A}})\geq 2r+2
. Therefore, using the Schmidt number inequality

{\rm SN}(\Pi_{\mathcal{A}}\sigma\Pi_{\mathcal{A}})\leq 2{\rm SN}(\sigma),

obtained from
[
PV19
,
Car20
]
, we conclude that
{\rm SN}(\sigma)\geq\frac{1}{2}{\rm SN}(\rho_{\mathcal{A}})\geq r+1
, yielding a lower bound on the Schmidt number of any optimal PPT state
\sigma
achieving
p^{\rm PPT}(\rho_{\mathcal{A}})
.

In
[
PV19
]
it was further shown that
p_{\min}(2)=p_{\min}(3)=1/2
and
p_{\min}(2r)=p_{\min}(2r+1)
for all integers
r\geq 1
. Moreover, the minimum
p_{\min}(d)
is attained by the antisymmetric pure state

\rho_{\mathcal{A}}=|\omega_{d}^{\Omega}\rangle\langle\omega_{d}^{\Omega}|;\quad|\omega_{d}^{\Omega}\rangle=(I_{d}\otimes\Omega_{d})|\omega_{d}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum_{i=1}^{d/2}(|i+d/2,i\rangle-|i,i+d/2\rangle),

from which it was estimated that
p_{\min}(2r)\geq\tfrac{1}{2r+2}
for all
r\geq 2
. In the following, we exploit symplectic group symmetry to show that equality indeed holds, thereby resolving the conjecture posed in
[
PV19
]
.

Theorem 6.1
.

For every even integer
d\geq 4
, we have

p_{\min}(d)=p^{\rm PPT}(|\omega_{d}^{\Omega}\rangle\langle\omega_{d}^{\Omega}|)=\frac{1}{d+2}.

Proof.

Let
\sigma
be a PPT state such that
\Pi_{\mathcal{A}}\sigma\Pi_{\mathcal{A}}=\Tr(\Pi_{\A} \sigma)|\omega_{d}^{\Omega}\rangle\langle\omega_{d}^{\Omega}|
. We first show that its
S\otimes S
-twirling
\tilde{\sigma}=\mathcal{T}_{S\otimes S}(\sigma)=\mathbb{E}_{S\in Sp(d)}\big[(S\otimes S)\sigma(S\otimes S)^{*}\big]
satisfies the same relation

(41)

\Pi_{\mathcal{A}}\tilde{\sigma}\Pi_{\mathcal{A}}=\Tr(\Pi_{\A}\tilde{\sigma})|\omega_{d}^{\Omega}\rangle\langle\omega_{d}^{\Omega}|=\Tr(\Pi_{\A}{\sigma})|\omega_{d}^{\Omega}\rangle\langle\omega_{d}^{\Omega}|.

Indeed, by recalling that
\Pi_{1}^{SS}=|\omega_{d}^{\Omega}\rangle\langle\omega_{d}^{\Omega}|
,
\Pi_{2}^{SS}=\Pi_{\mathcal{S}}
, and
\Pi_{3}^{SS}=\Pi_{\mathcal{A}}-|\omega_{d}^{\Omega}\rangle\langle\omega_{d}^{\Omega}|
are three mutually orthogonal projections in
{\rm Inv}(S\otimes S)
(Corollary
2.10
), Proposition
2.6
implies that

\displaystyle\tilde{\sigma}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{3}\frac{1}{{\rm rank}(\Pi_{j}^{SS})}\Tr( \Pi_j^{SS} \sigma)\Pi_{j}^{SS}

\displaystyle=\langle\omega_{d}^{\Omega}|\sigma|\omega_{d}^{\Omega}\rangle\,|\omega_{d}^{\Omega}\rangle\langle\omega_{d}^{\Omega}|+\frac{2}{d^{2}+d}\Tr(\Pi_{\mathcal{S}} \sigma)\Pi_{\mathcal{S}}+\frac{2}{d^{2}-d-2}\Tr((\Pi_{\mathcal{A}}-|\om_d^{\Om}\ra\la\om_d^{\Om}|)\sigma)\big(\Pi_{\mathcal{A}}-|\omega_{d}^{\Omega}\rangle\langle\omega_{d}^{\Omega}|\big)

\displaystyle=\Tr(\Pi_{\A}\sigma)|\omega_{d}^{\Omega}\rangle\langle\omega_{d}^{\Omega}|+\frac{2}{d^{2}+d}(1-\Tr(\Pi_{\A}\sigma))\Pi_{\mathcal{S}},

where the second and third equalities follow from
|\omega_{d}^{\Omega}\rangle\in{\rm Ran}(\Pi_{\mathcal{A}})
and

\langle\omega_{d}^{\Omega}|\sigma|\omega_{d}^{\Omega}\rangle=\langle\omega_{d}^{\Omega}|\Pi_{\mathcal{A}}\sigma\Pi_{\mathcal{A}}|\omega_{d}^{\Omega}\rangle=\Tr(\Pi_{\A} \sigma),

by the assumption of
\sigma
. This shows the relation (
41
). In particular, this implies that the optimal state
\sigma^{*}
for
p^{\rm PPT}(|\omega_{d}\rangle\langle\omega_{d}^{\Omega}|)
can be chosen to be of the form

\sigma^{*}=p|\omega_{d}^{\Omega}\rangle\langle\omega_{d}^{\Omega}|+\frac{2(1-p)}{d^{2}+d}\Pi_{\mathcal{S}}=\rho_{\frac{1-p}{d+1},p}^{\Gamma},

where in this case
p=\Tr(\Pi_{\A}\sigma)
. By Theorem
4.1
,
\sigma^{*}
above is PPT if and only if
0\leq p\leq\frac{1}{d+2}
.
∎

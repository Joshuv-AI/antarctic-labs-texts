Establishment and validation of clinical prediction model and prognosis of perioperative pneumonia in elderly patients with hip fracture complicated with preoperative acute heart failure
Authors: Yuying Li, Shuhan Li, Jiaxuan Zhu, Zhiqian Wang, Xiuguo Zhang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundElderly hip fracture was a common orthopedic emergency with high perioperative complication risks. Combined with preoperative acute heart failure, the risk increases further, with pneumonia being a common complication. The aim of this study was to construct and evaluate risk factor prediction models for perioperative pneumonia in these patients and to explore prognostic factors.MethodsA retrospective study design was used to collect data on elderly patients with hip fracture combined with preoperative acute heart failure at the Third Hospital of Hebei Medical University from January 2020 to December 2022. The feature variables were screened by logistic regression and nomogram was constructed. The receiver operating characteristics curve (ROC), decision curve analysis (DCA), and calibration curve were employed to assess the predictive power of the model. Correlation heatmaps and shapley additive explanation (SHAP) were employed to assess key variables and their impact. Employing the Kaplan-Meier curve and Cox regression, the patients’ prognosis was ultimately evaluated.Results535 elderly patients with hip fracture combined with preoperative acute heart failure were included in this study. Logistic regression analysis was used to identify combined respiratory disease, hemoglobin, albumin, neutrophils, and blood glucose as independent danger factors for perioperative pneumonia (p < 0.05). The nomogram was designed to display the outcomes instinctively, with an AUC of 0.819. The model was internally validated by initiating self-sampling 1000 times. The calibration curve indicated that the model had excellent treaty. The DCA curve showed that the model had good validity and clinical practicability. Correlation heatmaps and SHAP were visualized and analyzed. The K-M curves indicated that the prognosis of the non-pneumonia group was better than that of the pneumonia group (p = 0.014). COX regression analysis found that the major risk factors for all-cause mortality in patients with acute heart failure(AHF) were age, brain natriuretic peptide, platelet count, and combined respiratory failure (p < 0.05).ConclusionThe prediction model, established in this study, was highly accurate and proved a potent instrument for clinical evaluation of the perioperative pneumonia risk of elderly hip fracture patients with preoperative acute heart failure. We hope that this study can reduce the occurrence of perioperative pneumonia in patients and improve the prognosis of patients.Keywords: Elderly hip fracture, Acute heart failure, Perioperative pneumonia, Clinical prediction model, Prognosis

Background

Anticipation of a rise in hip fractures among the global populace is high, with 2.6 million expected by 2025 and 4.5 million by 2050 [1–3]. Surgery is the most prevalent form of hip fracture, yet serious issues can occur post-surgery, potentially reducing a patient’s quality of life and raising the risk of death and other complications [2]. The elderly population’s acute heart failure was a common cardiovascular disease, correlating with a decline in surgical tolerance and an increase in complications [4]. Previous studies revealed a 6-20% rate of preoperative heart failure in hip fracture sufferers [5], and this condition raised the mortality risk for those admitted for hip fractures [6]. Varying levels of alveolar filling in those suffering from heart failure could impede the usual physiological processes in the alveolar endothelial fluid between the air and lung tissue, including effective conditioning agents and macrophages, thus raising the risk of infection and obstructing its removal [7].

Postoperative pneumonia(POP) was the commonest postoperative complication, with an incidence of 3.5–15.2% [8, 9]. The mortality rate of elderly individuals who had POP after hip fracture surgery was between 27 and 43% in a 30-day period [8]. Worsening of the condition: pneumonia, as a serious infection of the respiratory system, will further increase the burden on the heart. In patients with acute heart failure, pneumonia may lead to increased pulmonary circulation pressure and increased pulmonary edema, which worsens heart function and makes the condition more complex and difficult to control. Studies have shown that patients with acute heart failure complicated with pneumonia tend to have a poor prognosis. Pneumonia can prolong a patient’s hospital stay, increase medical costs, and even lead to death. Therefore, accurate prediction of the risk of perioperative pneumonia in elderly patients with hip fracture combined with preoperative acute heart failure was important for optimizing patient management, reducing complications and improving prognosis. As far as we know, there had been many studies on perioperative pneumonia in patients with hip fracture [10, 11]. Vasanth Sathiyakumar [12] studied 27,441 patients with hip fractures to analyze the risk factors for cardiac events after hip fractures. Research has shown [13] that hospital-acquired pneumonia poses a greater risk of complications and in-hospital mortality in patients with acute heart failure. However, there are no studies on the risk factors and prognosis of perioperative pneumonia in patients with hip fracture complicated with heart failure. This exploration sought to investigate the potential perioperative pneumonia risks for elderly people with hip fractures and acute heart failure, as well as the factors that could forecast all-cause mortality in those with pneumonia.

Materials and methods

Study population

From January 2020 to January 2022 at Hebei Medical University’s Trauma Emergency Center, we conducted a study in the geriatric orthopedics department, with criteria for inclusion being: (1) age over 65; (2) in accordance with the European Society of Cardiology’s AHF 2016 guidelines; New York Heart Association (NYHA) functional classification II–IV (left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) ≤ 50% or LVEF > 50% but N-terminal (NT)-pro-hormone brain natriuretic peptide (pro-BNP) > 400 ng/L). (3) a definitive diagnosis of hip fracture through imaging. Exclusion criteria included: (1) fractures that had been present for over 3 weeks; (2) high-energy traumatic fractures, such as car crashes, falls, etc.; (3) multiple fractures; (4) periprosthetic fractures; (5) metastatic rational fractures or atypical femoral fractures; and (6) a history of chronic heart failure; (7) Patients lost to follow-up. The Third Hospital of Hebei Medical University’s Ethics Review Committee gave their blessing to this research, which met the Helsinki standards. This retrospective study waived the informed consent of the subjects.

Data collection

We collected demographic data of patients at admission through an electronic medical record system. Gathering information on age, gender, body mass index (BMI), fracture type, time from admission to surgery, injury to admission, injury to surgery, survival time, comorbidities, and complications, we also acquired the type of surgery, anesthesia, ASA score, intraoperative fluid volume, and the duration of the surgery. Laboratory measurements and chest examinations were performed at the time of admission. The laboratory indices, such as hemoglobin (HB), red blood cell (RBC), platelet count (PLT), hematocrit (HCT), white blood cell (WBC), neutrophils (NEUT), lymphocytes (LYM), c-reactive protein (CRP), albumin (ALB), creatinine (CRE), glucose (GLU), C-reactive protein to albumin ratio (CAR), sodium (NA+), potassium (K+), and brain natriuretic peptide (BNP), were measured.

Definition of postoperative pneumonia

Patients diagnosed with POP had to meet one or more of the following criteria: (1) the emergence of fresh, progressive, and enduring respiratory issues, such as coughing and expectoration; (2) fever or hypothermia; (3) a physical exam that showed lung consolidation and/or moist rale; (4) a white cell count of over 10 × 109/L or below 4 × 109/L; and (5) pathogen isolation from blood culture or sputum [9].

Definition of acute heart failure

AHF refers to rapid or gradual onset of symptoms and/or signs of HF, severe enough for the patient to seek urgent medical attention, leading to an unplanned hospital admission or an emergency department visit. Clinical presentations are mainly based on the presence of signs of congestion and/or peripheral hypoperfusion. Cut-offs for acute HF are: BNP < 100Pg/mL, NT-ProBNP < 300 pg/mL and MR-proANP < 120 Pg/mL [14].

Statistical methods

The statistical analyses were performed by IBM SPSS Statistics version 26.0 and R software version 4.4.0. Missing values were processed using the missing value analysis of SPSS. We process missing data through multiple interpolation. Count data were statistically described using frequencies and percentages, and comparisons between two groups were made using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact probability method. Measurement information that conformed to normal distribution was expressed as mean ± standard deviation and compared using t-test. Skewed distribution measurements were expressed as median and interquartile spacing, and comparisons between the two groups were made using the Wilcoxon rank sum test. To determine the independent risk factors for perioperative pneumonia in elderly patients with hip fracture combined with acute heart failure, univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses were used. Predictors significantly associated with pneumonia were entered into the R software to construct nomogram. The predictive efficiency of the model was analyzed using ROC curves and C-index. Calibration curves and decision curve analysis (DCA) were assessed to evaluate the goodness of fit of the predictive model. Drawing correlation heat map and analyzed the correlation between variables. We used the SHAP method providing global interpretation and local interpretation for model interpretation. The K-M curves was used to compare survival rates, and log-rank tests were used to assess differences. Univariate and multivariate cox regression analysis was then used to assess correlations between baseline variables and all-cause mortality. p-values less than 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

Results

General characteristics of the patients

From January 2020 to December 2022, 535 patients with hip fractures combined with preoperative acute heart failure were eventually included in the study (Fig. 1). A prevalence of perioperative pneumonia among elderly individuals with hip fracture and preoperative acute heart failure was 35.14%, with 67.6% being female and the median age being 82.5 years. Pneumonia group had more comorbidities (Table 1). The pneumonia group exhibited a greater prevalence of thrombosis, anemia, delirium, hypokalemia, hyponatremia, and myocardial injury than the non-pneumonia group (p < 0.05) (Table 2). Additionally, gender, respiratory comorbidities, HB, HCT, NEUT, CRP, CAR, ALB, GLU, and other variables were found to have a statistically significant difference between the two groups (p < 0.05).

Univariate and multivariate logistic regression analysis

The univariate and multivariate analyses’ outcomes were exposed in Table 3. Univariate analysis revealed that gender, respiratory disease, HB, HCT, ALB, CRP, NEUT, GLU, and CAR had statistically significant distinctions (p < 0.05). The multivariate analyses revealed that respiratory disease (OR, 3.641; 95% CI, 2.294–5.779; p < 0.001), HB (OR, 0.949; 95% CI, 0.935–0.964; p < 0.001), ALB (OR, 0.895; 95%CI, 0.850–0.941; p < 0.001), NEUT (OR, 1.214; 95%CI, 1.103–1.336; p < 0.001), GLU (OR, 1.065; 95%CI, 1.001–1.133; p = 0.047) were all significant independent risk factors for perioperative pneumonia. By analyzing the AUC values of the above variables, it was possible to understand the performance of each variable in predicting perioperative pneumonia. In this study, it was found that the AUC values of HB, ALB, Respiratory disease, NEUT, and GLU were 0.738, 0.708, 0.632, 0.584, and 0.558, respectively, which had strong predictive ability. HB’s AUC value was the highest among them, suggesting that it had the most successful prediction of pneumonia (Fig. 2). A positive correlation between CAR and CRP was revealed by the correlation heat map, while anesthesia mode and fracture type had a negative correlation. No correlation of note was seen between the other variables, as depicted in Fig. 3.

Creation and assessment of nomogram

The logistic regression analysis was visualized by nomogram, and Fig. 4A revealed that, in patients with respiratory illness, a rise in NEUT and GLU values would result in a gradual rise in the likelihood of perioperative pneumonia, whereas a decrease in HB and ALB values would result in a rise in the probability of perioperative pneumonia. The C-index of the column chart was 0.819, and the internally verified C-index was 0.819, which confirms the high reliability of the model. This analysis’s ROC curve revealed the model’s excellent discrimination capability, with an AUC value of 0.819, signifying its high precision in distinguishing between pneumonia and non-pneumonia samples. Irrespective of the threshold, the model’s sensitivity and specificity remained at a high level (as illustrated in Fig. 4B). The calibration curves revealed that the model’s predicted probabilities were nearly identical to the actual probabilities, thus demonstrating its remarkable precision. This further validated the effectiveness of the logistic regression model (see Fig. 4C). The DCA shown in Fig. 4D showed using the model predicts higher net returns over a wide range of thresholds. The logistic regression model’s practical applications were demonstrated to be of great value by this.

The SHAP to model interpretation

We used 10-fold cross-validation for resampling and overall evaluation of model performance, and obtained an AUC value of 0.838. Illustrating the entire distribution of the effect of each feature on the model output, the SHAP summary plot revealed that, generally, the higher the SHAP value, the greater the risk of perioperative pneumonia. Figure 5A showed the 15 most important features in the model. decreased HB, increased RBC, decreased ALB, decreased HCT, increased CRP, increased NEUT, having respiratory disease, decreased PLT, increased CRE, increased potassium, high blood pressure, increased BNP, decreased CAR, increased sodium, and decreased BMI increase the probability of developing pneumonia. A positive relationship between HB and the occurrence of pneumonia was demonstrated by the samples in Fig. 5B. Additionally, positive correlations were observed between respiratory diseases and HB, NEUT and HB, and GLU and HB, while negative correlations were observed between HB and RBC and ALB and NEUT. A simple sampling method was employed to randomly select a sample with a forecasted outcome of pneumonia and another with a forecasted outcome of non-pneumonia. In the samples a of Fig. 5C, except for the absence of respiratory disease as a favorable factor, the all the rest were unfavorable factors. Survival analysis

Because most postoperative complications and associated mortality risks are concentrated in the first year after surgery, we analyzed one-year survival rates. The mortality rate of the pneumonia group was markedly greater than that of the non-pneumonia group, with 13.3% compared to 7.7%, as Fig. 6 illustrates (p = 0.014). The survival curves of the two groups showed significant statistical difference. The results of unifactorial and multifactorial cox analyses were shown in Table 4. The unifactorial results identified age, perioperative pneumonia, comorbid coronary heart disease or respiratory disease, type of fracture, ASA classification, BNP, HB, HCT, sodium, CRE, CRP, PLT, CAR, postoperative comorbid anemia, atrial fibrillation, and myocardial infarction as prognostic factors for the overall survival (OS). Multifactorial found age, BNP, PLT to be independent prognostic factors for OS.

Discussion

The incidence of perioperative pneumonia in elderly patients with hip fracture, combined with preoperative acute heart failure, was 35.14% in this research. Comorbid respiratory diseases, low hemoglobin, low albumin, high neutrophils and high blood glucose levels were independent risk factors for perioperative pneumonia. We constructed a nomogram prediction model that had good discriminative power. We analyzed the correlations between the variables by creating correlation heat maps. We used SHAP plots to visualize how much each feature contributes to the model. We found the one-year all-cause mortality rate was 13.3% in the pneumonia group and 7% in the non-pneumonia group. Age, BNP, and PLT were independent risk factors for their postoperative survival time.

The results of our study indicated that those with co-morbid respiratory illnesses were more likely to suffer from perioperative pneumonia, which is in line with the discoveries of de J et al. [15] In the model, respiratory disease status, as the most immediate risk factor, accurately reflected its significant contribution to the prediction of pneumonia risk through high SHAP values. This could be attributed to the fact that respiratory disease can have a detrimental effect on the patient’s pulmonary function, making them more vulnerable to infection [16]. The stimulus of surgery and trauma during the perioperative period could lead to a decrease in the body’s immunity, thus making it more likely for bacteria or viruses to enter the lungs and cause pneumonia [17–20]. A positive and high SHAP for low hemoglobin indicates that a decrease in hemoglobin levels is positively associated with an increased risk of pneumonia. This study uncovered that, in patients, a lack of hemoglobin is a distinct risk factor for the development of pneumonia. Research by Ramakrishnan et al. discovered that low hemoglobin levels posed a hazard for respiratory, regardless of the cause [21]. Low hemoglobin levels were associated with weakened immunity and may aggravate an infection [22–24]. When hemoglobin levels were low, it led to a decrease in the body’s ability to deliver oxygen, impairing the blood’s oxygenation capacity and exacerbating hypoxia in multiple organs and tissues, leading to worsening pneumonia [25, 26]. Low hemoglobin levels are often associated with anemia, which can be corrected by supplementing with nutrients such as iron, vitamin B12, or folate. In addition, treatment for malnutrition and blood loss is also necessary. Low albumin has a positive SHAP value, and the higher the value, the greater the contribution of low albuminemia to the risk of pneumonia. The model accurately captures the positive association between low albumin and pneumonia risk through SHAP values, underscoring the importance of correcting hypoalbuminemia to reduce the risk of pneumonia. Serum albumin levels were an indicator of the severity of malnutrition in hip fracture patients [27]. Our findings also identified low serum albumin levels as a risk factor. Wang et al. found that elderly patients suffering from hip fractures have been found to be vulnerable to perioperative pneumonia when albumin concentrations are low [28]. A study by Bassoli et al. demonstrated a strong association between hypoalbuminemia and respiratory dysfunction, disease severity, and inflammatory status [29]. Low protein levels were often associated with malnutrition, which reduced a patient’s overall state of health and weakens their ability to fight disease [30, 31]. Hypoalbuminemia had a major impact on immune functioning, causing a reduction of lymphocytes and the body’s phagocytic capability, and then influencing the activity of multiple enzymes, thus significantly increasing the risk of lung infection. In addition, it could destroy vascular endothelial growth factor, made albumin leakage, and caused interstitial edema, causing serious damage to lung function [32]. Hypoproteinemia may be caused by malnutrition, liver disease, kidney disease and other reasons. It is possible to increase protein intake (especially high-quality protein, such as lean meat, fish, legumes, etc.), as well as treat underlying causes, such as improving liver or kidney function. High neutrophils had positive SHAP values, indicating that an increase in neutrophils count was positively associated with an increased risk of pneumonia. Neutrophils, as the primary phagocytes in the blood, had a significant part in the development of pneumonia [24]. Rapidly responding to bacteria and other pathogens that infiltrated the body, chemotactic, phagocytic and bactericidal reactions were initiated, resulting in phagocytosis and the elimination of the pathogens at the source of inflammation. When neutrophils were increased, it might indicate the presence of acute and septic infections, tissue damage, and other conditions in the body, all of which might increase the risk of pneumonia. A positive and high SHAP for hyperglycemia indicates that an increase in blood sugar levels is positively associated with an increased risk of pneumonia. The results of our study found that elevated blood glucose levels were an independent risk factor for pneumonia. People with diabetes developed infections more often than the general population. Previous studies had shown that stress-induced hyperglycemia enhanced the oxidative stress response, producing harmful molecules that damage lung tissue. At the same time, high blood sugar could also trigger an inflammatory response, increased inflammatory cytokines, and exacerbated lung inflammation. In addition, high blood sugar could suppress immune cell activity, reduced the body’s ability to clear bacteria, and increased the risk of pneumonia. High blood sugar is a major manifestation of diabetes and an important risk factor for cardiovascular disease and other complications. Through lifestyle interventions (such as diet modification, increased physical activity) and/or medication (such as oral hypoglycemic drugs, insulin, etc.), blood sugar levels can be effectively controlled and the risk of complications can be reduced. Close monitoring of these risk factors, early identification and appropriate interventions to prevent or reduce complications.

The results of multifactorial COX regression analysis showed that age, BNP, PLT, and respiratory failure were independent risk factors for poor prognosis in patients with pneumonia. Age was demonstrated to be a major factor in the demise of those suffering from pneumonia [33]. This might be related to the decline in the functioning of various organs and systems of the body as we age. Previous studies had shown that BNP was a prognostic marker for patients with pneumonia. This might be because BNP was an acute phase reactant that regulated fluid volume, vascular pressure, and electrolyte balance. The secretion of BNP, caused by the lack of oxygen, caused constriction of pulmonary blood vessels, pulmonary hypertension, and an overload of the right heart. Pro-inflammatory cytokines and the sympathetic nervous system may be the cause of this BNP release. Consequently, BNP could be indicative of inflammation, comorbidities, and hypoxia - which is a contributing factor to the deterioration of pneumonia patients, poor treatment effect, and a poor prognosis [34]. We found that thrombocytosis was a risk factor for poor prognosis in patients with pneumonia. Moreover, Prina et al. discovered that thrombocytosis is a risk factor for a poor prognosis in those with pneumonia [35]. Previous research showed that patients with severe lung infections complicated by respiratory failure had a poor prognosis [36]. Respiratory failure, resulting in hypoxia and carbon dioxide retention in the body, caused a series of intense physiological responses. The hypoxia led to a decrease in myocardial functioning, thus causing an increase in heart rate, blood pressure, and myocardial burden [36].

There are some limitations to this study. This study, being a single-center retrospective study, has certain restrictions. It increases the likelihood of selection bias. Second, because our observations and analyses were limited to the available data, we may not have captured other important variables that could have influenced the study results. These unconsidered variables may have an impact on the results of the study. Third, the model was based on Chinese hip fracture patients. It was not clear whether it was applicable to other countries. Fourth, this study only conducted internal verification of the model, and did not adopt external verification methods, which affected the generalization ability of the model. We must assess the model’s applicability further. For prospective studies in the future, larger samples should be procured to acquire more comprehensive data and bolster the reliability of the outcomes.

Conclusion

Perioperative pneumonia occurred in 35.14% of elderly patients with preoperative acute heart failure, in which respiratory comorbidities, low hemoglobin, low albumin, high neutrophils and high blood glucose levels were risk factors for perioperative pneumonia. We constructed a nomogram prediction model that effectively predicts the risk of perioperative pneumonia in elderly patients with hip fracture combined with preoperative acute heart failure. By performing prognostic analyses, we found that age, BNP, PLT, and combined respiratory failure were risk factors for poor prognosis in patients with pneumonia. Early recognition of those in danger and suitable interventions is anticipated to diminish the occurrence of perioperative pneumonia and improve the prognosis of patients.

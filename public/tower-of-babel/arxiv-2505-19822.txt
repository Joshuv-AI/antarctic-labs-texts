The stability threshold for 3D MHD equations around Couette with rationally aligned magnetic field
Fei Wang, Lingda Xu, Zeren Zhang

Abstract
We address a stability threshold problem of the Couette flow ( y , 0 , 0 ) (y,0,0) in a uniform magnetic fleld α ⁡ ( σ , 0 , 1 ) \alpha(\sigma,0,1) with σ ∈ ℚ \sigma\in\mathbb{Q} for the 3D MHD equations on 𝕋 × ℝ × 𝕋 \mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T} . Previously, the authors in [ 27 , 30 ] obtained the threshold γ = 1 \gamma=1 for σ ∈ ℝ \ ℚ \sigma\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\mathbb{Q} satisfying a generic Diophantine condition, where they also proved γ = 4 / 3 \gamma=4/3 for a general σ ∈ ℝ \sigma\in\mathbb{R} . In the present paper, we obtain the threshold γ = 1 \gamma=1 in H N ​ ( N > 13 / 2 ) H^{N}(N>13/2) , hence improving the above results when σ \sigma is a rational number. The nonlinear inviscid damping for velocity u ≠ 2 u^{2}_{\neq} is also established. Moreover, our result shows that the nonzero modes of magnetic field has an amplification of order ν − 1 / 3 \nu^{-1/3} even on low regularity, which is very different from the case considered in [ 27 , 30 ] .

Introduction

In this paper, we consider the 3D incompressible MHD equations on
\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}
:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}\tilde{u}+\tilde{u}\cdot\nabla\tilde{u}-\tilde{b}\cdot\nabla\tilde{b}=-\nabla\tilde{p}+\nu\Delta\tilde{u},\\
\partial_{t}\tilde{b}+\tilde{u}\cdot\nabla\tilde{b}-\tilde{b}\cdot\nabla\tilde{u}=\mu\Delta\tilde{b},\\
\nabla\cdot\tilde{u}=\nabla\cdot\tilde{b}=0,\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

where
\tilde{u}
is the velocity,
\tilde{b}
is the magnetic field,
\tilde{p}
is the pressure,
\nu
is the fluid viscosity, and
\mu
is the magnetic resistivity.
We introduce the perturbations of the Couette flow and the homogeneous magnetic flied:

u=\tilde{u}-(y,0,0)^{T},b=\tilde{b}-\alpha(\sigma,0,1)^{T},

with
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and
\sigma\in\mathbb{Q}
. Without loss of generality, we always assume
\alpha>0
in the present paper. Then
(u,b)
satisfies the system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}u+y\partial_{x}u-\alpha\partial_{\sigma}b+u\cdot\nabla u-b\cdot\nabla b+\left(\begin{array}[]{l}u^{2}\\
0\\
0\end{array}\right)=2\nabla\Delta^{-1}\partial_{x}u^{2}-\nabla p^{NL}+\nu\Delta u,\\
\partial_{t}b+y\partial_{x}b-\alpha\partial_{\sigma}u+u\cdot\nabla b-b\cdot\nabla u-\left(\begin{array}[]{l}b^{2}\\
0\\
0\end{array}\right)=\mu\Delta b,\\
p^{NL}=(-\Delta)^{-1}(\partial_{j}u^{i}\partial_{i}u^{j}-\partial_{j}b^{i}\partial_{i}b^{j}),\\
\nabla\cdot u=\nabla\cdot b=0,\\
u(0)=u_{in},\quad b(0)=b_{in},\end{array}\right.

(1.2)

where summation over repeated indices is used and we defined
\partial_{\sigma}=\sigma\partial_{x}+\partial_{z}
.
To understand the underlying transition mechanisms of the above system, we are concerned with the stability threshold problem in the Sobolev spaces and formulate it as
[
3
]
:

Stability threshold
: Given
N\geq 0
, determine
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle\|(u_{in},b_{in})\|_{H^{N}}\ll\nu^{\gamma_{1}}\mu^{\gamma_{2}}\ \Rightarrow\ \mathrm{stability},

\displaystyle\|(u_{in},b_{in})\|_{H^{N}}\gg\nu^{\gamma_{1}}\mu^{\gamma_{2}}\ \Rightarrow\ \mathrm{possible\ instability}.

Quantifying stability thresholds has emerged as an active research frontier originating from fluid dynamics studies of the Navier-Stokes (NS) equations. In the classical NS system without the magnetic fields, the stability threshold takes the form
\nu^{\gamma}
for some
\gamma\geq 0
.
A seminal contribution by Bedrossian, Germain, and Masmoudi
[
3
]
established the threshold
\gamma=\frac{3}{2}
for 3D perturbations of Couette flow on the domain
\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}
in Sobolev spaces. Specifically, they demonstrated
that initial perturbations
u_{in}

satisfying
\|u_{in}\|_{H^{N}}\ll\nu^{3/2}
for

N>\frac{9}{2}
yield global solutions remaining order of
O(\nu^{1/2})
.
Subsequent refinement by Wei and Zhang
[
32
]
improved this threshold to
\gamma=1
in
H^{2}
. Due to the absence of lift-up effects, the 2D NS system exhibits fundamentally different behavior, resulting in significantly lower stability thresholds. For perturbations on
\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}
, Bedrossian, Vicol, and Wang
[
10
]
established a threshold
\gamma=\frac{1}{2}
, with later analyses in
[
28
,
33
]
sharpening this to
\gamma=\frac{1}{3}
. When considering perturbations in Gevrey classes, distinct thresholds emerge:
\gamma=1

[
4
,
5
]
for
\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}
versus
\gamma=0

[
9
,
24
]
for
\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}
.
Stability phenomena become more complex in the bounded domains. Recent studies in
[
13
,
12
,
6
,
34
,
7

]
have elucidated how boundary layers generate instability mechanisms. For broader perspectives on general shear flows, we refer to

,

,

,

,

Due to the presence of magnetic fields, the problem becomes more complicated for magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) systems.
While magnetic fields generally suppress the mixing mechanisms inherent in Couette flow
[

,
23
]
, they paradoxically exhibit stabilizing effects at high intensities
[
20
,
27
,
36
]
. Significant progresses have been made in quantifying these phenomena: Liss
[
27
]
proved the stability threshold
\gamma=1
for
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\mathbb{Q}
satisfying a generic Diophantine condition in Sobolev spaces when
\nu=\mu>0
on
\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}
. Using the same method, Liss claimed the threshold
\gamma=\frac{4}{3}
for any
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
under the same assumption. Recently, Rao, Zhang, and Zi
[
30
]
extended these results to the case
\mu\neq\nu
. For 2D case in
\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}
, Chen and Zi
[
14
]
showed
\gamma=\frac{5}{6}+
for shear flow close to Couette flow in Sobolev spaces when
\nu=\mu
. Dolce
[
18
]
proved
(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})=(\frac{2}{3},0)
in a more general regime
0<\mu^{3}\lesssim\nu\leq\mu
where the fluid effects dominate. In case
\mu\leq\nu
, Knobel
[
22
]
established the stability threshold
(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})=(\frac{1}{12},\frac{1}{2})
if
\nu^{3}\lesssim\mu\leq\nu
and instability with inflation of size
\nu\mu^{-\frac{1}{3}}
if
\mu\lesssim\nu^{3}
. The first and third authors of the present paper
[
31
]
and Jin, Ren and Wei
[
21
]
generalize these results to arbitrarily
\nu,\mu\in(0,1]
in the vorticity system and the velocity system, respectively. The nonlinear threshold
(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})=(0,1)
in the Gevrey-2- space when
\nu=0,\mu>0
was obtained by Zhao and Zi
[
37
]
. Recently, Dolce, Knobel, and Zillinger
[
19
]
proved the algebraic instability and large norm inflation of the magnetic current in Gevrey classes when
\nu>0,\mu=0
.

The purpose of this paper is to improve the stability threshold to
\gamma=1
for
\sigma\in\mathbb{Q}

\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}

[

\nu^{-1/3}
even on low regularity, which is very different from the case of
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\mathbb{Q}
satisfying a generic Diophantine condition.
Defining

f_{0}=\int_{\mathbb{T}}f(x,y,z)dx,\quad\quad f_{\neq}=f-f_{0},

Our stability result is stated as follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume
\nu=\mu\in(0,1]
,
\sigma=\frac{q}{p}\in\mathbb{Q}
,
|\alpha|>8p>0
, and

. Let

be the initial datum of (

). There exist constant

such that if it holds that

then the profiles

and

satisfy the following stability estimates:

\displaystyle\|e^{\delta_{0}\nu^{1/3}t}(\partial_{X},\partial_{Z})\partial_{X}U^{1}_{\neq}\|_{L^{\infty}H^{N-2}}+\nu^{1/6}\|(\partial_{X},\partial_{Z})\partial_{X}U^{1}_{\neq}\|_{L^{2}H^{N-2}}\lesssim\epsilon,

(1.3a)

\displaystyle{\|e^{\delta_{0}\nu^{1/3}t}(\partial_{X},\partial_{Z})\nabla_{L}U^{2}_{\neq}\|_{L^{\infty}H^{N-2}}+\|U^{2}_{\neq}\|_{L^{2}H^{N-2}}+\nu^{1/6}\|(\partial_{X},\partial_{Z})\nabla_{L}U^{2}_{\neq}\|_{L^{2}H^{N-2}}\lesssim\epsilon},

(1.3b)

\displaystyle\|e^{\delta_{0}\nu^{1/3}t}(\partial_{X},\partial_{Z})^{2}U^{3}_{\neq}\|_{L^{\infty}H^{N-2}}+\nu^{1/6}\|(\partial_{X},\partial_{Z})^{2}U^{3}_{\neq}\|_{L^{2}H^{N-2}}\lesssim\epsilon,

(1.3c)

\displaystyle\|e^{\delta_{0}\nu^{1/3}t}\partial_{X}B^{1}_{\neq}\|_{L^{\infty}H^{N}}+\nu^{1/6}\|(\partial_{X},\partial_{Z})\partial_{X}B^{1}_{\neq}\|_{L^{2}H^{N}}\lesssim\nu^{-1/3}\epsilon,

(1.3d)

\displaystyle\|e^{\delta_{0}\nu^{1/3}t}(\partial_{X},\partial_{Z})(B^{2}_{\neq},B^{3}_{\neq})\|_{L^{\infty}H^{N}}+\nu^{1/6}\|(\partial_{X},\partial_{Z})(B^{2}_{\neq},B^{3}_{\neq})\|_{L^{2}H^{N}}\lesssim\epsilon,

where

Remark 1.1
.

The inviscid damping of

\nu
-bounds in (

exhibits a growth of order

Recalling the equations (

Combining the methods of

Remark 1.4
.

It is still open whether the threshold can be improved for
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\mathbb{Q}
which does not satisfy the generic Diophantine condition.

Main ideas of the proof
: In this paper, we deal with non-homogeneous modes and homogeneous modes (see (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) for definitions) separately. We first examine the linear terms of the non-homogeneous modes
F^{\pm,i}_{\neq NH}
for
i\in\{2,3\}
(see (
2.3
) for definition). The growth of
F^{\pm,i}_{\neq NH}
is caused by the linear stretching terms
\partial^{L}_{XY}W^{\pm,i}_{\neq NH}
. Motivated by
[
32
]
, we employ the dissipation of
\partial_{X}W^{\pm,3}_{\neq NH}
to get the
\nu^{-2/3}
growth of
F^{\pm,3}_{\neq NH}
. While for
F^{\pm,2}_{\neq NH}
, we use the enhanced dissipation of the quantity
\partial_{X}|\nabla_{L}|W^{\pm,2}_{\neq NH}
to obtain the amplification of order
\nu^{-1/3}
.

For the homogeneous modes
(U_{\neq H},B_{\neq H})
, the behaviors of
U^{2}_{\neq H}
and
B^{2}_{\neq H}
are essentially different. In fact, letting
Q=\Delta_{L}U
and
G=\Delta_{L}B
, the linearization system of
(Q^{2}_{\neq H},G^{2}_{\neq H})
reads

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}Q^{2}_{\neq H}-\nu\Delta_{L}Q^{2}_{\neq H}=0,\\
&\partial_{t}G^{2}_{\neq H}+2\partial_{XY}^{L}\Delta_{L}^{-1}G^{2}_{\neq H}-\nu\Delta_{L}G^{2}_{\neq H}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

A direct calculation gives that

\|Q^{2}_{\neq H}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim e^{-c\nu t^{3}}\|Q^{2}_{in}\|_{L^{2}},\quad\|G^{2}_{\neq H}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\nu^{-2/3}e^{-c\nu t^{3}}\|G^{2}_{in}\|_{L^{2}}.

When considering the nonlinear interactions, the
\Delta_{L}(B\cdot\nabla_{L}B^{2})_{\neq H}
term leads to an additional amplification of order
\nu^{-1/3}
for
Q^{2}_{\neq H}
, which causes trouble in the nonlinear estimates. For instance, we do not have a uniform bound of
U^{2}_{\neq H}
in
H^{N}
. To overcome these difficulties, we exploit the subtle relationship between growth and regularity of
U^{i}_{\neq H}
and
\Delta_{L}U^{i}_{\neq H}
with
i\in\{2,3\}
(see (
2.10
) for details).

Turning to the zero-mode, the most challenging part is of the term

\left\langle(U^{Hi,2}_{\neq H}\cdot\partial_{Y}^{L}G^{Lo,1}_{\neq H})_{0},G^{1}_{0}\right\rangle_{H^{N}},

which is caused by the absence of uniform bound of
U^{2}_{\neq H}
. To reduce the derivative losses as much as possible, we use the multiplier
M_{3}
introduced in
[
33
]
(see section
5.1
for details).

2.3
The Fourier multipliers

We introduce the following multipliers to capture the stability mechanisms of the above systems. The first two multipliers
M^{1}
and
M^{2}
are now standard for studying the stability of Couette flow. They are determined by the equations

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\frac{\partial_{t}M_{1}}{M_{1}}=-\frac{k^{2}+|kl|}{k^{2}+(\eta-kt)^{2}+l^{2}},\ \mathrm{for}\ k\neq 0,\\
\displaystyle\frac{\partial_{t}M_{2}}{M_{2}}=-\frac{\nu^{1/3}k^{2}}{k^{2}+\nu^{2/3}(\eta-kt)^{2}},\ \mathrm{for}\ k\neq 0,\\
M_{j}(t,0,\eta,l)=M_{j}(0,k,\eta,l)=1,\ j=1,2.\end{array}\right.

The multiplier
M_{1}
helps us to obtain inviscid damping and control terms arising from the linear pressure. The multiplier
M_{2}
is used to capture the enhanced dissipation, which satisfies the key property:

\nu^{1/3}\lesssim\nu(\eta-kt)^{2}-\frac{\partial_{t}M_{2}}{M_{2}}.

(2.8)

We also need a multiplier

introduced in
[

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\frac{\partial_{t}M_{3}}{M_{3}}=-\Upsilon(t,k,\eta,l),\\

with

\Upsilon(t,k,\eta,l)=\sum_{k^{\prime}\neq k}\frac{1}{|k-k^{\prime}|}\frac{1+|k-k^{\prime}|+|k^{\prime}|}{(1+|k-k^{\prime}|+|k^{\prime}|)^{2}+(\eta-(k-k^{\prime})t)^{2}}.

&M_{3}(t,0,\eta,l),\ \mathrm{for}\ k=0.\end{aligned}\right.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|MG^{3}_{\neq H}(t_{1})\|^{2}_{H^{N^{\prime}}}+\nu\|\nabla_{L}MG^{3}_{\neq H}\|^{2}_{L^{2}H^{N^{\prime}}}+\sum_{i=1}^{2}\left\|\sqrt{-\frac{\partial_{t}M_{i}}{M_{i}}}MG^{3}_{\neq H}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}H^{N^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|MG^{3}_{\neq H}(t_{0})\|^{2}_{H^{N^{\prime}}}+\delta_{0}\nu^{1/3}\|MG^{3}_{\neq H}\|_{L^{2}H^{N^{\prime}}}^{2}-\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}\left\langle 2\partial^{L}_{XY}MB^{3}_{\neq H},MG^{3}_{\neq H}\right\rangle_{H^{N^{\prime}}}dt

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}\left\langle M(U\cdot\nabla_{L}G^{3})_{\neq H},MG^{3}_{\neq H}\right\rangle_{H^{N^{\prime}}}dt-\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}\left\langle M(Q\cdot\nabla_{L}B^{3})_{\neq H},MG^{3}_{\neq H}\right\rangle_{H^{N^{\prime}}}dt

\displaystyle\quad-2\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}\left\langle M(\partial^{L}_{i}U^{j}\partial^{L}_{ij}B^{3})_{\neq H},MG^{3}_{\neq H}\right\rangle_{H^{N^{\prime}}}dt+\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}\left\langle M(B\cdot\nabla_{L}Q^{3})_{\neq H},MG^{3}_{\neq H}\right\rangle_{H^{N^{\prime}}}dt

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}\left\langle M(B\cdot\nabla_{L}Q^{3})_{\neq H},MG^{3}_{\neq H}\right\rangle_{H^{N^{\prime}}}dt+2\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}\left\langle M(\partial^{L}_{i}B^{j}\partial^{L}_{ij}U^{3})_{\neq H},MG^{3}_{\neq H}\right\rangle_{H^{N^{\prime}}}dt.

The amplification is caused by the nonlinear term
B^{1,Lo}_{\neq H}\partial_{X}Q^{3,Hi}_{\neq H}
, which follows

\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}\left\langle M(B^{1,Lo}_{\neq H}\partial_{X}Q^{3,Hi}_{\neq H}),MG^{3}_{\neq H}\right\rangle_{H^{N}}dt\lesssim

\displaystyle\|MB^{1}_{\neq H}\|_{L^{2}H^{N}}\|\partial_{X}MQ^{3}_{\neq H}\|_{L^{2}H^{N}}\|MG^{3}_{\neq H}\|_{L^{\infty}H^{N}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\ \nu^{-1/2}\epsilon\nu^{-3/2}\epsilon\nu^{-1}\epsilon\lesssim\nu^{-1}\epsilon(\nu^{-1}\epsilon)^{2}.

The other estimates are similar to those for
Q^{3}_{\neq H}
. Therefore, we omit the treatment of
G^{3}_{\neq H}
to conclude the improvement of (
2.11c
)–(
2.11d
).

5
Energy estimates on zero mode

In this section we improve (
2.12
). We provide the details only for
F^{1}_{0},(1,\partial_{Z})^{2}W^{1}_{0}
, and the estimates of
F^{3}_{0}
can be treated similarly. For
F^{2}_{0}
, the incompressibility implies that
F^{2}_{0}
always has a nonzero
Z
-frequency, which ensures that there is no interaction between two homogeneous modes in the energy estimate of
F^{2}_{0}
. Hence, the treatment of
F^{2}_{0}
is similar to
F^{2}_{\neq NH}
(
2.9b
).

5.1
Estimate of
F_{0}

In this subsection, we improve (
2.12a
). An energy estimate gives

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|MF^{+,1}_{0}(t_{1})\|^{2}_{H^{N}}+\nu\|\nabla_{L}MF^{+,1}_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}H^{N}}+\left\|\sqrt{-\Upsilon}MF^{+,1}_{0}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}H^{N}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|MF^{+,1}_{0}(t_{0})\|^{2}_{H^{N}}-\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}\left\langle T^{-t}_{2\alpha}MF^{-,2}_{0},MF^{+,1}_{0}\right\rangle_{H^{N}}dt-\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}\left\langle M((T^{-t}_{2\alpha}W^{-})\cdot\nabla_{L}F^{+,1})_{0},MF^{+,1}_{0}\right\rangle_{H^{N}}dt

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}\left\langle M((T^{-t}_{2\alpha}F^{-})\cdot\nabla_{L}W^{+,1})_{0},MF^{+,1}_{0}\right\rangle_{H^{N}}dt-2\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}\left\langle M(\partial^{L}_{i}(T^{-t}_{2\alpha}W^{-,j})\partial^{L}_{ij}W^{+,1})_{0},MF^{+,1}_{0}\right\rangle_{H^{N}}dt

\displaystyle=:\frac{1}{2}\|MF^{+,1}_{0}(t_{0})\|_{H^{N}}^{2}+LU+NLT+NLS_{1}+NLS_{2}.

The estimate of
LU
is similar to that in
[
27
]
. For the sake of completeness, we give a sketch of proof. By integration by parts in time, we obtain

\displaystyle LU=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2\alpha}\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}\left\langle\partial_{t}(\partial_{Z}^{-1}T^{-t}_{2\alpha})\mathbb{P}_{l\neq 0}MF^{-,2}_{0},MF^{+,1}_{0}\right\rangle_{H^{N}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{2\alpha}\left\|\left\langle\partial_{Z}^{-1}T^{-t}_{2\alpha}\mathbb{P}_{l\neq 0}MF^{-,2}_{0},MF^{+,1}_{0}\right\rangle_{H^{N}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}}+\frac{1}{2\alpha}\left|\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}\left\langle\partial_{Z}^{-1}T^{-t}_{2\alpha}\mathbb{P}_{l\neq 0}MF^{-,2}_{0},\partial_{t}(MF^{+,1}_{0})\right\rangle_{H^{N}}dt\right|

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2\alpha}\left|\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}\left\langle\partial_{Z}^{-1}T^{-t}_{2\alpha}\mathbb{P}_{l\neq 0}\partial_{t}(MF^{-,2}_{0}),MF^{+,1}_{0}\right\rangle_{H^{N}}dt\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle:LU_{1}+LU_{2}+LU_{3}.

For
LU_{1}
, noting
|\alpha|>8p
, it holds that

LU_{1}\leq\frac{1}{2\alpha}\|MF^{1}_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}H^{N}}\|MF^{2}_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}H^{N}}\leq 4(\nu^{-2/3}\epsilon)^{2}.

Turning next to
LU_{2}
, expanding the time derivative, we obtain

\displaystyle LU_{2}\leq

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{2\alpha}\left|\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}\left\langle\partial_{Z}^{-1}T^{-t}_{2\alpha}MF^{-,2}_{0},\frac{\partial_{t}M}{M}MF^{+,1}_{0}\right\rangle_{H^{N}}dt\right|+\frac{1}{2\alpha}\left|\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}\left\langle\partial_{Z}^{-1}T^{-t}_{2\alpha}MF^{-,2}_{0},T^{-t}_{2\alpha}MF^{-,2}_{0}\right\rangle_{H^{N}}dt\right|

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2\alpha}\left|\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}\left\langle\partial_{Z}^{-1}T^{-t}_{2\alpha}MF^{-,2}_{0},\nu\Delta MF^{+,1}_{0}\right\rangle_{H^{N}}dt\right|+\frac{1}{2\alpha}\left|\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}\left\langle\partial_{Z}^{-1}T^{-t}_{2\alpha}MF^{-,2}_{0},M(\partial_{t}F^{+,1}_{0})_{\mathcal{NL}}\right\rangle_{H^{N}}dt\right|,

where

(\partial_{t}F^{+,1}_{0})_{\mathcal{NL}}=((T^{-t}_{2\alpha}W^{-})\cdot\nabla_{L}F^{+,1}+(T^{-t}_{2\alpha}F^{-})\cdot\nabla_{L}W^{+,1}+2\partial^{L}_{i}(T^{-t}_{2\alpha}W^{-,j})\partial^{L}_{ij}W^{+,1})_{0}.

Crucially, the second linear term vanishes. In fact,
it holds that

\left\langle\partial_{Z}^{-1}T^{-t}_{2\alpha}MF^{-,2}_{0},T^{-t}_{2\alpha}MF^{-,2}_{0}\right\rangle_{H^{N}}=\int\partial_{Z}(\partial_{Z}^{-1}\mathbb{P}_{l\neq 0}T^{-t}_{2\alpha}\langle\nabla\rangle MF^{-,2}_{0})^{2}dXdYdZ=0.

The other contributions and
LU_{3}
can be treated similarly to the
OLS
term in (
2.9a
). We omit further details.

For
NLT
, we start with the interaction between two homogeneous modes

NLT(i,\neq H,\neq H)=\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}\left\langle M(W^{i}_{\neq H}\partial_{i}^{L}F^{1}_{\neq H})_{0},MF^{+,1}_{0}\right\rangle_{H^{N}}dt,

with
i\in\{1,2,3\}
. When
i\in\{1,3\}
, using divergence-free condition, we have

\displaystyle NLT(i,\neq H,\neq H)\lesssim

\displaystyle\|M(W^{1}_{\neq H},W^{3}_{\neq H})\|_{L^{2}H^{N}}(\|\partial^{L}_{Y}MF^{2}_{\neq H}\|_{L^{2}H^{N}}+\|MF^{3}_{\neq H}\|_{L^{2}H^{N}})\|MF^{1}_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}H^{N}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\ \nu^{-1/2}\epsilon\nu^{-7/6}\epsilon\nu^{-2/3}\epsilon=\nu^{-1}\epsilon(\nu^{-2/3}\epsilon)^{2}.

Turning to
i=2
, by integration by parts, it holds that

\displaystyle NLT^{LH}(2,\neq H,\neq H)=

\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}\left\langle\partial^{L}_{Y}M((T^{-t}_{2\alpha}W^{Lo,2}_{\neq H})F^{Hi,1}_{\neq H})-M((T^{-t}_{2\alpha}\partial^{L}_{Y}W^{Lo,2}_{\neq H})F^{Hi,1}_{\neq H}),MF^{+,1}_{0}\right\rangle_{H^{N}}dt

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle(\|MW^{2}_{\neq H}\|_{L^{\infty}H^{3/2+}}\|\partial_{Y}MF^{1}_{0}\|_{L^{2}H^{N}}+\|\partial_{Y}^{L}MW^{2}_{\neq H}\|_{L^{\infty}H^{N}}\|MF^{1}_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}H^{N}})\|MF^{1}_{\neq H}\|_{L^{2}H^{N}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\ \epsilon\nu^{-7/6}\epsilon\nu^{-7/6}\epsilon+\nu^{-1/2}\epsilon\nu^{-2/3}\epsilon\nu^{-7/6}\epsilon=\nu^{-1}\epsilon(\nu^{-2/3}\epsilon)^{2}.

For
NLT^{HL}(2,\neq H,\neq H)
, expanding
W^{+}=T^{-t}_{\alpha}(U+B)
and
F^{1}_{\neq H}=-\partial^{L}_{Y}\partial_{X}^{-1}F^{2}_{\neq H}+\sigma F^{3}_{\neq H}
, we get

\displaystyle NLT^{HL}(2,\neq H,\neq H)=

\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}\big\langle M(U^{2,Hi}_{\neq H}\partial^{L}_{Y}\partial^{L}_{Y}\partial_{X}^{-1}F^{2,Lo}_{\neq H}),MF^{1}_{0}\big\rangle_{H^{N}}dt

\displaystyle+\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}\big\langle M(B^{2,Hi}_{\neq H}\partial^{L}_{Y}\partial^{L}_{Y}\partial_{X}^{-1}F^{2,Lo}_{\neq H}),MF^{1}_{0}\big\rangle_{H^{N}}dt

\displaystyle+\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\big\langle M(W^{2,Hi}_{\neq H}\partial^{L}_{Y}F^{3,Lo}_{\neq H}),MF^{1}_{0}\big\rangle_{H^{N}}dt=:I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}.

Noting
|\xi-kt|^{2}\lesssim\nu^{-2/3}e^{\delta_{0}\nu^{1/3}t}|k,\xi|^{2}
and
N>9/2
, by H
\ddot{\mathrm{o}}
lder’s inequality and Fubini’s theorem, we obtain

\displaystyle\big\langle M(U^{2,Hi}_{\neq H}\partial^{L}_{Y}\partial^{L}_{Y}\partial_{X}^{-1}F^{2,Lo}_{\neq H}),MF^{1}_{0}\big\rangle_{H^{N}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\nu^{-2/3}\sum_{l,k\neq 0}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|\langle k,\eta-\xi,l\rangle^{N}M\hat{U}^{2}_{\neq H}(-k,\eta-\xi,-l)|k,\xi|^{2}M\hat{F}^{2}_{\neq H}(k,\xi,l)\langle\eta\rangle^{N}M\bar{\hat{F}}^{1}(0,\eta,0)\right|d\eta d\xi

\displaystyle\lesssim\nu^{-2/3}\left(\sum_{l,k\neq 0}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|\langle k,\eta-\xi,l\rangle^{N}|k,\eta-\xi+kt|M\hat{U}^{2}_{\neq H}(-k,\eta-\xi,-l)\langle|k,\xi|\rangle^{3}M\hat{F}^{2}_{\neq H}(k,\xi,l)\right|^{2}d\eta d\xi\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\quad\times\left(\sum_{k\neq 0}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|\frac{\langle\eta\rangle^{N}}{|k,\eta-\xi+kt|\langle|k,\xi|\rangle}M\hat{F}^{1}(0,\eta,0)\right|^{2}d\xi d\eta\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\ \nu^{-2/3}\||\nabla_{L}|MU^{2}_{\neq H}\|^{2}_{H^{N}}\|MF^{2}_{\neq H}\|_{H^{N}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sum_{k\neq 0}\frac{1+2|k|}{|k|[(1+2|k|^{2})+(\eta+kt)^{2}]}\langle\eta\rangle^{2N}|M\hat{F}^{1}(0,\eta,0)|^{2}d\eta\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\nu^{-2/3}\||\nabla_{L}|MU^{2}_{\neq H}\|^{2}_{H^{N}}\|MF^{2}_{\neq H}\|_{H^{N}}\|\sqrt{-\Upsilon}MF^{1}_{0}\|_{H^{N}},

where we used the fact, for
a,b>0,c\in\mathbb{R}
, it holds that

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\mathrm{d}\xi}{(a^{2}+\xi^{2})(b^{2}+(c-\xi)^{2})}=\frac{\pi}{ab}\frac{a+b}{(a+b)^{2}+c^{2}}.

Integrating in time, we have

I_{1}\lesssim\nu^{-2/3}\||\nabla_{L}|MU^{2}_{\neq H}\|^{2}_{L^{2}H^{N}}\|MF^{2}_{\neq H}\|_{L^{\infty}H^{N}}\|\sqrt{-\Upsilon}MF^{1}_{0}\|_{L^{2}H^{N}}\lesssim\nu^{-1}\epsilon(\nu^{-2/3}\epsilon)^{2}.

Turning next to
I_{2}
and
I_{3}
, we have

\displaystyle I_{2}\leq

\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}\left|\big\langle M(B^{2,Hi}_{\neq H}\partial^{L}_{Y}\partial^{L}_{Y}\partial_{X}^{-1}F^{2,Lo}_{\neq H}),MF^{1}_{0}\big\rangle_{H^{N}}\right|dt

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\ \nu^{-2/3}\|MB^{2}_{\neq H}\|_{L^{2}H^{N}}\|MF^{2}_{\neq H}\|_{L^{2}H^{N}}\|MF^{1}_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}H^{N}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\ \nu^{-2/3}\nu^{-1/6}\epsilon\nu^{-5/6}\epsilon\nu^{-2/3}\epsilon=\nu^{-1}\epsilon(\nu^{-2/3}\epsilon)^{2},

as well as

\displaystyle I_{3}\leq

\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\left|\big\langle M(W^{2,Hi}_{\neq H}\partial^{L}_{Y}F^{3,Lo}_{\neq H}),MF^{1}_{0}\big\rangle_{H^{N}}\right|dt

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|MW^{2}_{\neq H}\|_{L^{2}H^{N}}\|\partial_{Y}^{L}F^{3}_{\neq H}\|_{L^{2}H^{3/2+}}\|MF^{1}_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}H^{N}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\ \nu^{-1/3}\epsilon\nu^{-7/6}\epsilon\nu^{-2/3}\epsilon=\nu^{-5/6}\epsilon(\nu^{-2/3}\epsilon)^{2}.

Putting together the above estimates, we finish the improvement of
NLT(\neq H,\neq H)
. The interactions between non-homogeneous modes and homogeneous modes , or two non-homogeneous modes can be treated similarly and hence are omitted for brevity. For
NLT(0,0)
, the dissipation in (
2.12
) implies

\displaystyle NLT(0,0)=

\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}\left\langle M(W_{0}\cdot\nabla F^{1}_{0}),MF^{+,1}_{0}\right\rangle_{H^{N}}dt

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\nabla MW^{2}_{0}\|_{L^{2}H^{N}}\|\nabla MF^{1}_{0}\|_{L^{2}H^{N}}\|MF^{1}_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}H^{N}}

\displaystyle+\|\nabla MW^{3}_{0}\|_{L^{2}H^{N}}\|\partial_{Z}MF^{1}_{0}\|_{L^{2}H^{N}}\|MF^{1}_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}H^{N}}+\|MW^{3}_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}H^{N}}\|\partial_{Z}MF^{1}_{0}\|_{L^{2}H^{N}}\|\nabla MF^{1}_{0}\|_{L^{2}H^{N}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\ \nu^{-1}\epsilon(\nu^{-2/3}\epsilon)^{2}.

Turning to
NLS_{1}(\neq H,\neq H)
, by divergence-free condition, it holds that

\displaystyle NLS_{1}(\neq H,\neq H)=

\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}\left\langle M(F_{\neq H}\cdot\nabla_{L}W^{1}_{\neq H})_{0},MF^{+,1}_{0}\right\rangle_{H^{N}}dt

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|(MF^{1}_{\neq H},MF^{3}_{\neq H})\|_{L^{2}H^{N}}\|\partial_{X}MW^{1}_{\neq H}\|_{L^{2}H^{N}}\|MF^{1}_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}H^{N}}

\displaystyle+\|MF^{2}_{\neq H}\|_{L^{2}H^{N}}(\|MF^{2}_{\neq H}\|_{L^{2}H^{N}}+\|\partial_{Y}^{L}\partial_{X}MW^{3}_{\neq H}\|_{L^{2}H^{N}})\|MF^{1}_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}H^{N}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\ \nu^{-7/6}\epsilon\nu^{-1/2}\epsilon\nu^{-2/3}\epsilon+\nu^{-5/6}\epsilon\nu^{-5/6}\epsilon\nu^{-2/3}\epsilon=\nu^{-1}\epsilon(\nu^{-2/3}\epsilon)^{2}.

The estimates of the other contributions of
NLS_{1}
and
NLS_{2}
are similar to the above, and we omit further details.

Finally, the estimate of
F^{3}_{0}
is natural from the above. For example, turning to the interaction between
B_{\neq H}
and
\nabla_{L}F^{3}_{\neq H}
, it holds that

\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}\left\langle M(B_{\neq H}\cdot\nabla_{L}F^{3}_{\neq H})_{0},MF^{3}_{0}\right\rangle_{H^{N}}dt\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\partial_{X}(MB^{1}_{\neq H},MB^{3}_{\neq H})\|_{L^{2}H^{N}}\|MF^{3}_{\neq H}\|_{L^{2}H^{N}}\|MF^{3}_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}H^{N}}

\displaystyle+\|MB^{2}_{\neq H}\|_{L^{2}H^{N}}\|\partial_{Y}^{L}MF^{3}_{\neq H}\|_{L^{2}H^{N}}\|MF^{3}_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}H^{N}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\ \nu^{-1/2}\epsilon\nu^{-7/6}\epsilon\nu^{-2/3}\epsilon+\nu^{-1/6}\epsilon\nu^{-3/2}\epsilon\nu^{-2/3}\epsilon\lesssim\nu^{-1}\epsilon(\nu^{-2/3}\epsilon)^{2}.

Noting that there is no interaction between two homogeneous modes in nonlinear pressure term, it can be treated similarly to (
2.9d
). This completes the improvement of (
2.12a
).

5.2
Estimate of
(1,\partial_{Z})^{2}W_{0}

In this subsection, we improve
(
2.12b
)
. We start with
\partial_{ZZ}W^{1}_{0}
, an energy estimate gives

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|\partial_{ZZ}MW^{+,1}_{0}(t_{1})\|_{H^{N}}+\nu\|\nabla_{L}\partial_{ZZ}MW^{+,1}_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}H^{N}}+\left\|\sqrt{-\Upsilon}\partial_{ZZ}MW^{+,1}_{0}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}H^{N}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|\partial_{ZZ}MW^{+,1}_{0}(t_{0})\|^{2}_{H^{N}}-\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}\left\langle T^{-t}_{2\alpha}\partial_{ZZ}MW^{-,2}_{0}+\partial_{ZZ}M((T^{-t}_{2\alpha}W^{-})\cdot\nabla_{L}W^{+,1})_{0},\partial_{ZZ}MW^{+,1}_{0}\right\rangle_{H^{N}}dt

\displaystyle=:\frac{1}{2}\|\partial_{ZZ}MW^{+,1}_{0}(t_{0})\|_{H^{N}}^{2}+LU+NLT.

The
LU
term can be dealt with using integration by parts in time as in section
5.1
. We skip it and turn to
NLT
.
Since
\partial_{ZZ}W_{0}
has a nonzero
Z
-frequency, there is no interaction between two homogeneous modes. For
NLT(\neq H,\neq NH)
, we define the components

NLT(i,\neq H,\neq NH)=\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}\left\langle\partial_{ZZ}M(W^{i}_{\neq H}\cdot\partial_{i}^{L}W^{1}_{\neq NH})_{0},\partial_{ZZ}MW^{+,1}_{0}\right\rangle_{H^{N}}dt,

with
i\in\{1,2,3\}
.
By the bootstrap assumptions, it holds that

\displaystyle NLT(1,\neq H,\neq NH)=

\displaystyle NLT^{HL}(1,\neq H,\neq NH)+NLT^{LH}(1,\neq H,\neq NH)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\partial_{XX}MW^{1}_{\neq H}\|_{L^{2}H^{N}}\|\partial_{X}MW^{1}_{\neq NH}\|_{L^{2}H^{N}}\|\partial_{ZZ}MW^{1}_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}H^{N}}

\displaystyle+\|MW^{1}_{\neq H}\|_{L^{2}H^{3/2+}}\|\partial_{ZZX}MW^{1}_{\neq NH}\|_{L^{2}H^{N}}\|\partial_{ZZ}MW^{1}_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}H^{N}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\ \nu^{-1/2}\epsilon\nu^{-1/6}\epsilon^{2}+\nu^{-1/2}\epsilon\nu^{-1/2}\epsilon\epsilon\lesssim\nu^{-1}\epsilon^{3}.

When
j\in\{2,3\}
, using integration by parts, we obtain

\displaystyle NLT(j,\neq H,\neq NH)=

\displaystyle NLT^{HL}(j,\neq H,\neq NH)+NLT^{LH}(j,\neq H,\neq NH)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\partial_{XX}MW^{j}_{\neq H}\|_{L^{2}H^{N}}\|\nabla_{L}MW^{1}_{\neq NH}\|_{L^{2}H^{N}}\|\partial_{ZZ}MW^{1}_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}H^{N}}

\displaystyle+\|MW^{j}_{\neq H}\|_{L^{\infty}H^{3/2+}}\|\nabla_{L}\partial_{Z}MW^{1}_{\neq NH}\|_{L^{2}H^{N}}\|\partial_{Z}\partial_{ZZ}MW^{1}_{0}\|_{L^{2}H^{N}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\ \nu^{-1/2}\epsilon\nu^{-1/2}\epsilon\epsilon+\epsilon\nu^{-1/2}\epsilon\nu^{-1/2}\epsilon=\nu^{-1}\epsilon^{3}.

Turning to
NLT(\neq NH,\neq H)
, we have

\displaystyle NLT(\neq NH,\neq H)=

\displaystyle NLT^{HL}(\neq NH,\neq H)+NLT^{LH}(\neq NH,\neq H)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\partial_{ZZ}(MW^{1}_{\neq NH},MW^{3}_{\neq NH})\|_{L^{2}H^{N}}\|\partial_{X}MW^{1}_{\neq H}\|_{L^{2}H^{3/2+}}\|\partial_{ZZ}MW^{1}_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}H^{N}}

\displaystyle+\|\partial_{ZZ}MW^{2}_{\neq NH}\|_{L^{2}H^{N}}\|\partial_{Y}^{L}MW^{1}_{\neq H}\|_{L^{2}H^{3/2+}}\|\partial_{ZZ}MW^{1}_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}H^{N}}

\displaystyle+\|\partial_{X}(MW^{1}_{\neq NH},MW^{3}_{\neq NH})\|_{L^{\infty}H^{3/2+}}\|\partial_{XX}MW^{1}_{\neq H}\|_{L^{2}H^{N}}\|\partial_{ZZ}MW^{1}_{0}\|_{L^{2}H^{N}}

\displaystyle+\|\partial_{X}MW^{2}_{\neq NH}\|_{L^{2}H^{3/2+}}\|\partial_{XY}^{L}MW^{1}_{\neq H}\|_{L^{2}H^{N}}\|\partial_{ZZ}MW^{1}_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}H^{N}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\ \nu^{-1/2}\epsilon\nu^{-1/2}\epsilon^{2}+\nu^{-1/6}\epsilon\nu^{-5/6}\epsilon^{2}+\nu^{-1/6}\epsilon\nu^{-2/3}\epsilon\epsilon+\epsilon\nu^{-1}\epsilon^{2}\lesssim\nu^{-5/6}\epsilon^{3}.

For
NLT(0,0)
, we use the integration by parts to obtain

\displaystyle NLT(0,0)\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\partial_{Z}(MW^{2}_{0},MW^{3}_{0})\|_{L^{\infty}H^{N}}\|\nabla_{L}\partial_{Z}MW^{1}_{0}\|_{L^{2}H^{N}}\|\partial_{Z}\partial_{ZZ}MW^{1}_{0}\|_{L^{2}H^{N}}\lesssim\nu^{-1}\epsilon^{3}.

Finally, for
W_{0}^{+,r}
with
r\in\{1,2,3\}
, the energy estimate reads

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|MW^{+,r}_{0}(t_{1})\|_{H^{N}}+\nu\|\nabla_{L}MW^{+,r}_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}H^{N}}+\left\|\sqrt{-\Upsilon}MW^{+,r}_{0}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}H^{N}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|MW^{+,r}_{0}(t_{0})\|^{2}_{H^{N}}-\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}\left\langle M((T^{-t}_{2\alpha}W^{-})\cdot\nabla_{L}W^{+,r})_{0},MW^{+,r}_{0}\right\rangle_{H^{N}}dt

\displaystyle\quad-\mathbf{1}_{r=1}\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}\left\langle T^{-t}_{2\alpha}MW^{-,2}_{0},MW^{+,r}_{0}\right\rangle_{H^{N}}dt+\mathbf{1}_{r\neq 1}\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}\left\langle\partial_{r}\Delta^{-1}M((T^{-t}_{2\alpha}\partial_{j}W^{-,i})\partial_{i}W^{+,j})_{0},MW^{+,r}_{0}\right\rangle_{H^{N}}dt.

The estimate is similar to that of
(
2.12a
)
, and one can proceed analogously with
W^{1}_{0}
playing the role of
F^{1}_{0}
, which completes the improvement of (
2.12b
).

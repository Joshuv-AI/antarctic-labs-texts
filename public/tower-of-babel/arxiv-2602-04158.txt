Abstract
. This paper is devoted to studying the deformation behavior of pseudo-effective canonical divisors and volumes of adjoint classes in Kähler families. Based on recent developments in the Kähler minimal model program, for flat families with fiberwise canonical singularities, we establish the global stability of the pseudo-effectivity of canonical divisors, assuming in addition that one fiber is projective, while the same conclusion for Kähler threefolds is also true without the projectivity assumption of the central fiber. For smooth Kähler families whose central fiber is projective with a big adjoint class, we show that its volume remains locally constant. Finally, using the (relative) minimal model program for Kähler threefolds, we verify the deformation invariance of volumes of adjoint classes and plurigenera for smooth families of Kähler threefolds, thereby confirming Siu’s invariance of plurigenera conjecture in dimension three.



1.
Introduction

One of the central problems in complex algebraic geometry is the classification of compact complex varieties. Plurigenera, volumes, and positivity properties are fundamental invariants associated to a variety and play a crucial role in classification problems. The aim of this paper is to study the deformation behavior of pseudo-effective canonical divisors, plurigenera and volumes of adjoint classes.

The first part of this paper is devoted to studying the deformation behavior of pseudo-effective canonical divisors. Given a smooth projective family, H. Tsuji
[
66
]
showed that if the central fiber has pseudo-effective canonical divisor, then the nearby fibers also have pseudo-effective canonical divisors. It is natural to ask whether similar results hold for Kähler families. Recently, W. Ou
[
54
]
proved the BDPP conjecture
[
6
, Conjecture 1.2]
, which characterizes Kähler uniruledness:

Theorem 1.1

(
[
54
, Theorem 1.1]
)
.

Let X be a compact Kähler manifold. Then X is uniruled if and only if the canonical line bundle
K_{X}
is not pseudo-effective.

Combining this with Fujiki–Levine’s deformation stability of uniruledness
[
29
,
46
]
, one obtains deformation stability of pseudo-effectivity of canonical divisors for smooth Kähler families (cf.
[
13
, Corollary 5.5]
).

Combing recent developments in the transcendental MMP
[
19
]
, Das–Hacon’s transcendental base point free theorem for projective varieties
[
19
]
, and Kollár’s extension of MMP techniques in
[

, we prove the local stability of pseudo-effective canonical divisors for Kähler families whose fibers are allowed to have canonical singularities in Theorem
5.2
. Then based on Theorem
5.2
, and using a relative Douady space argument, we can prove the following global stability result for the pseudo-effectivity of the canonical divisors.

Theorem 1.2

(Theorem
5.4
)

Let

be a family from a normal
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial Kähler variety

are pseudo-effective for all

As a direct application of Theorem
1.2
and the characterization of uniruledness for Kähler varieties with canonical singularities given in Theorem

Let us highlight some key points of our proofs. To study the deformation stability of pseudo-effectivity of canonical divisors in Theorem
1.2
, we will make use of some MMP arguments. A key complication is that, without the projectivity assumption of the family, it is not a priori clear whether the classical MMP framework can be employed here. However, because we assume that the central fiber is projective, thanks to
[
19
]
, it is possible to run the MMP on the central fiber. We then use the extension technique from
[
43
, Theorem 2]
as in Theorem
4.19
to extend the MMP to nearby fibers. Note that the transcendental base point free Theorem
4.24
plays an important role in guaranteeing the termination of the MMP for nearby fibers, provided termination holds for the central fiber.

In order to prove the deformation invariance of the volumes in Theorem
1.6
, we make use of a MMP type argument via
[
19
]
as in the proof of Theorem
1.2
. Since we assume that the central fiber has a big adjoint class, the MMP with scaling on the central fiber terminates. By base point freeness, the MMP also terminates on nearby fibers. Hence, without loss of generality, we may assume that all fibers have nef adjoint class, so that the volume can be computed via top intersection numbers. In the case of divisors, the result follows from the constancy of the Hilbert polynomial in flat families, however we do not have a good substitute for this in the case of transcendental classes. Fortunately, after running the MMP, the singularities are still concentrated on a codimension 2 subset, and this allows us to apply a singular version of Stokes’ formula to deduce the constancy of the volume for adjoint classes. Using a similar argument, we can prove the deformation invariance of the volume of adjoint classes for smooth families of Kähler threefolds as in Theorem
1.8
.

In this paper, a
family

\pi:X\to S
will refer to a proper surjective morphism from a normal complex analytic space
to a smooth connected curve with connected (but possibly reducible)
complex analytic spaces as its fibers
X_{t}:=\pi^{-1}(t)

(t\in S)
, as in
[
14
]
. Then the total space of a family is irreducible and a family
here must be flat. In particular, we call
\pi
a
smooth family
if it is a flat
submersion
(i.e., the relative cotangent sheaf
\Omega^{1}_{X|S}
is locally
free of rank
\dim X-\dim S
). Note that in a smooth family, both the total space and each fiber are smooth. We also assume that the general fiber of a family is irreducible. The “general fiber” of a family refers to the one over a nonempty analytic Zariski open subset of
the base. By the reducedness and irreducibility of the total space of a family, the general
fiber of a family is also reduced, and if there is at least one irreducible
fiber, then the general fiber is irreducible.

Organization of the paper.

The paper is organized as follows. Section
2
establishes the foundations by reviewing generalized pairs, positivity notions such as nef, big, and pseudo-effective classes, the volume of cohomology classes, and basic results on bigness and pseudo-effectivity in families. Section
3
introduces singularities and positivity of line bundles in families, including deformation of singularities, relative ampleness, relative pseudo-effectivity, and criteria for global generation and semi-ampleness. Section
4
develops the main tools needed for the main results, namely the extension of the analytic minimal model program to nearby fibers and the deformation openness of nef and big adjoint classes. Section
5
proves the global stability of pseudo-effectivity of canonical divisors in Kähler families with one projective fiber. Section
6
establishes invariance of the volumes of adjoint classes, first when the projective central fiber has a big adjoint class and then, in the case of Kähler threefolds without projectivity assumption.

2.1.
Generalized pairs

Generalized pairs were first introduced in
[
4
]
in the study of the effective Iitaka fibration conjecture and later generalized to the Kähler setting in
[
17
]
. With this additional flexibility, we can run the transcendental minimal model program for generalized Kähler pairs. Let us first recall some basic definitions.

Let
X
be a
complex analytic variety
, i.e., an irreducible reduced complex space. Let
\mathcal{H}_{X}
be the sheaf of real parts of holomorphic functions multiplied with
\sqrt{-1}
. A
(1,1)
-form (resp.
(1,1)
-current) with local potentials on

is a global section of the quotient sheaf
\mathcal{A}_{X}^{0}/\mathcal{H}_{X}
(resp.
\mathcal{D}_{X}/\mathcal{H}_{X}
), where
\mathcal{A}_{X}^{0}
is the sheaf of smooth real valued functions and
\mathcal{D}_{X}
is the sheaf of distributions. We define the
Bott–Chern cohomology
to be

H^{1,1}_{\rm{BC}}(X,\mathbb{R}):=H^{1}(X,\mathcal{H}_{X}).

Given a closed
(1,1)
-form
\alpha
with local potentials (resp. a closed
(1,1)
-current

[\alpha]
(resp.
[T]

\mathbb{R}
-Cartier divisor

, we will also denote by
[D]
the associated cohomology class in
H^{1,1}_{\mathrm{BC}}(X,\mathbb{R})
. When there is no danger of ambiguity, we will sometimes write a class
\alpha\in H^{1,1}_{\mathrm{BC}}(X,\mathbb{R})
without explicitly specifying its representative.

A complex analytic variety
X
is a
Kähler variety
if there exists a Kähler form
\omega
on
X
, i.e., a positive closed real
(1,1)
-form
\omega\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{R}}^{1,1}(X)
such that for every singular point

there exists an open neighborhood
U\subseteq X
of

and a closed embedding
\iota_{U}:U\hookrightarrow V
into an open subset
V\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{N}
, and a strictly plurisubharmonic

function
f:V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
with
\left.\omega\right|_{U\cap X_{\mathrm{sm}}}=\left.(i\partial\bar{\partial}f)\right|_{U\cap X_{\mathrm{sm}}}
.

Let
f:X\rightarrow S
be a proper morphism of normal Kähler varieties with relatively compact
S
, and
\beta
a closed
(1,1)
-current with local potentials. We say that the class
[\beta]\in H_{\mathrm{BC}}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
is

(also referred to as

\left[\beta+f^{*}\omega_{S}\right]\in H_{\mathrm{BC}}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
is a Kähler class for some Kähler form

on

.

Let
X
,
Y
and
S
be complex analytic varieties. A mapping
\varphi
of
X
into the power set of

S
is called a
meromorphic

of

into

(we shall write

), if

satisfies the following conditions:

(1) The graph
G_{\varphi}=\{(x,y)\in X\times_{S}Y\mid y\in\varphi(x)\}

{X}\times_{S}{Y}
;

(2) The projection map
{p}_{{X}}:{G}_{\varphi}\to{X}
is a proper modification.

A meromorphic
S
-mapping
\varphi:{X}\dashrightarrow{Y}/S
of complex varieties is called a

-mapping
if
{p}_{{Y}}:{G}_{\varphi}\to{Y}
is also a proper modification.

In what follows, without causing any confusion, we will use the terms ‘birational’ and ‘bimeromorphic’ interchangeably in the analytic setting.

A compact complex variety is said to be in the
Fujiki class
\mathcal{C}
if it is bimeromorphic to a compact Kähler manifold. A compact complex variety is said to be a

if it is bimeromorphic to a projective manifold.

As explained in

(Generalized pair,

(Generalized discrepancies)

2.2.
Nef, big and pseudo-effective classes

In this subsection, we will introduce nef, big and pseudo-effective Bott–Chern cohomology classes on a normal compact complex analytic variety and their basic properties.

Definition 2.4

(Metrically nef, quasi-nef and algebraically nef classes)

Let

be a normal compact complex analytic variety in the Fujiki class

, and let
\alpha=[\theta]\in H_{\mathrm{BC}}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})

(1)
We say that the class
\alpha

(metrically) nef
if for some positive smooth
(1,1)
-form
\omega
on
X
and for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists

such that

\theta+i\partial\overline{\partial}f_{\varepsilon}\geq-\varepsilon\omega.

(2)
We say that the class
\alpha
is

if there exists a Kähler modification
\nu\colon\widehat{X}\to X
such that
\nu^{*}\alpha
is a metrically nef class on
\widehat{X}
.

(3)
We say that the class
\alpha
is
algebraically nef
if for all irreducible curves
C\subset X
, we have
\alpha\cdot C\geq 0
.

(4)
Let
f:X\to S
be a proper surjective morphism of normal relatively compact complex analytic varieties, and
\alpha\in H^{1,1}_{\rm{BC}}(X,\mathbb{R})
a Bott–Chern (1,1)-class on
X
. We say that
\alpha
is
f
-metrically nef (resp.
f
-algebraically nef)
if
\alpha|_{X_{s}}
is metrically nef (resp. algebraically nef) for any
s\in S
.

Using the cone theorem for Kähler varieties
[
11
,
33
]
, we first establish the equivalence of several notions of nefness for adjoint classes on Kähler varieties (cf.
[
52
, § 2]
).

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
X
be a normal compact Kähler variety, such that
(X,B+\bm{\beta}_{X})
is a generalized klt pair, and
\alpha\in H^{1,1}_{\mathrm{BC}}(X,\mathbb{R})
. Then the class
\alpha
is metrically nef if and only if it is quasi-nef. Moreover, if we further assume that
\alpha=K_{X}+B+\bm{\beta}_{X}
, then
\alpha
is algebraically nef if and only if it is metrically nef.

Proof.

By definition,
\alpha
is quasi-nef if and only if
f^{*}\alpha
is metrically nef on a resolution
f:\widehat{X}\to X
. On the other hand, by
[
55
, Theorem 1]
,
\alpha
is metrically nef if and only if
f^{*}\alpha
is metrically nef. Hence clearly
\alpha
is metrically nef if and only if it is quasi-nef.

Assume now that
\alpha=K_{X}+B+\bm{\beta}_{X}
is algebraically nef. By
[
33
, Theorem 0.5]
(or alternatively
[
11
, Theorem 1.3]
), together with Theorem
1.1
, if
K_{X}+B+\bm{\beta}_{X}
is not metrically nef, then there exists a
(K_{X}+B+\bm{\beta}_{X})
-negative rational curve
C
such that
(K_{X}+B+\bm{\beta}_{X})\cdot C<0
.
This implies that
K_{X}+B+\bm{\beta}_{X}
is not algebraically nef, contradicting our assumption.
∎

Definition 2.6

(Pseudo-effective and big classes)
.

On a normal compact complex analytic variety
X
, we say that:

(1)
A cohomology class
\alpha\in H^{1,1}_{\rm{BC}}(X,\mathbb{R})
is
pseudo-effective
if there exists a closed positive (1,1)-current
T\geq 0
, such that
T
represents the class
\alpha
.

(2)
A cohomology class
\alpha\in H^{1,1}_{\rm{BC}}(X,\mathbb{R})
is
big
if there exists a closed positive (1,1)-current
T
to represent the class
\alpha
, such that
T\geq\delta\omega
for some smooth Hermitian metric
\omega
and some number
\delta>0
.

If we perturb a pseudo-effective class in the Kähler direction, we obtain a big class.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
X
be a normal compact Kähler variety. A class
\alpha\in H^{1,1}_{\rm{BC}}(X,\mathbb{R})
is pseudo-effective if and only if there exists a Kähler class
\omega
such that
\alpha+\varepsilon\omega
is big for any
\varepsilon>0
.

2.3.
Lelong numbers and restricted non-Kähler locus

Let us introduce the definitions of Lelong numbers and restricted non-Kähler locus on normal complex analytic varieties.

Definition 2.8

(Lelong numbers,
[
24
, Définition 2]
)
.

Let X be a normal complex
n
-dimensional analytic variety, and
{T}\geq 0
a closed positive
(1,1)
-current on X. Let
{x}\in{X}
be an arbitrary point. Consider an embedding

({X},{x})\hookrightarrow\left(\mathbb{C}^{{N}},0\right).

The coordinate functions
\left({z}_{{i}}\right)_{{i}=1,\ldots,{~N}}
on
\mathbb{C}^{{N}}
restricted to X induce a generating system
\left({g}_{{i}}\right)_{{i}=1,\ldots,{~N}}
of the maximal ideal of the ring
\mathcal{O}_{{X},{x}}
. The function
\varphi_{{x}}:=\sum_{{i}}\left|{g}_{{i}}\right|
is defined on some small open subset
U
containing
x
. We then define the
Lelong number
of
T
at
x
to be

\nu({T},{x}):=\lim_{{r}\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{(2\pi{r})^{2{n}-2}}\int_{\varphi<{r}}{~T}\wedge(\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi)^{{n}-1}.

Definition 2.9

(Restricted non-Kähler locus,
[
33
, Definition 4.18]
)
.

Let
X
be a normal complex analytic variety and
T
a closed positive
(1,1)
-current on
X
with local potentials. For each
c>0
we define the
Lelong level set
as

E_{c}:=\{x\in X:\nu(T,x)>c\},

where
\nu(T,x)
denotes the Lelong number of
T
at
x\in X
.
This is an analytic subset of
X
. We define

E_{+}(T):=\bigcup_{c\in\mathbb{R}^{+}}E_{c}.

The
restricted non-Kähler locus
of a class
\alpha\in H^{1,1}_{\rm{BC}}(X,\mathbb{R})
is the set

E_{nK}^{as}(\alpha):=\bigcap_{T\in\alpha}E_{+}(T),

where
T
above is assumed to be a Kähler current with weak analytic singularities, and
E_{+}(T)\subset X
is the (analytic) subset of
X
for which the Lelong numbers of
T
are strictly positive.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
\alpha\in H^{1,1}_{\rm{BC}}(X,\mathbb{R})
on a normal compact complex analytic variety
X
. Then
E_{nK}^{as}(\alpha)=\emptyset
if and only if
\alpha
is Kähler class;
E_{nK}^{as}(\alpha)=X
if and only if
\alpha
is not a big class.

2.4.
Volume of cohomology classes

Compared with the projective setting, a complex analytic variety may admit only a few divisors but many cohomology classes in
H_{\rm{BC}}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
. While the notion of a global section does not apply to these classes, we can still define their volume. In this subsection, we briefly recall some basic facts about the volume of a cohomology class.

Definition 2.11

(Volume of a cohomology class)
.

For
\alpha\in H^{1,1}_{\rm{BC}}(X,\mathbb{R})
on a normal compact Kähler variety
X
, if
\alpha
is a pseudo-effective cohomology class, we define the
volume
of
\alpha
by pulling back via a resolution
\pi:X^{\prime}\to X

\mathrm{vol}(\alpha):=\sup_{T\in\pi^{*}(\alpha)}\int_{X^{\prime}}T_{\rm{ac}}^{n}=\sup_{T\in\pi^{*}(\alpha)}\int_{X^{\prime}\setminus\operatorname{Sing}(T)}T^{n},

for
T
ranging over all the closed positive (1,1)-currents in
\pi^{*}(\alpha)
; otherwise, we set
\mathrm{vol}(\alpha)=0
. Here
T_{\mathrm{ac}}
denotes the absolutely continuous part of the current
T
, and
\operatorname{Sing}(T)
denotes the singular locus of
T
.

Remark 2.12
.

Since the volume is a birational invariant for smooth varieties (cf.
[
45
, Example 2.2.49]
and
[
7
]
), the volume of a cohomology class on a normal compact Kähler variety is independent of the choice of resolution.

Remark 2.13
.

Given a pseudo-effective line bundle
L
on a compact Kähler manifold
X
,
[
7
, Theorem 1.2]
showed that the usual definition of the volume of a line bundle,

\mathrm{vol}(L):=\limsup_{k\to+\infty}\frac{n!}{k^{n}}h^{0}(X,kL),

coincides with Definition
2.11
.

The volume function is continuous on the Bott–Chern cohomology space of a normal compact Kähler variety.

Proposition 2.14

(
[
7
, Corollary 4.11]
)
.

Let
X
be a normal compact Kähler variety. Then the volume function

\mathrm{vol}:H^{1,1}_{\rm{BC}}(X,\mathbb{R})\to\mathbb{R}

is continuous.

For a smooth family of Kähler manifolds, the volume function is upper semi-continuous.

Proposition 2.15

(
[
7
, Proposition 4.13]
)
.

Let
{X}\rightarrow S
be a smooth family of Kähler manifolds. If
\alpha\in H^{1,1}_{\rm{BC}}(X_{0},\mathbb{R})
is a pseudo-effective class on the central fiber
X_{0}
, then one has

\mathrm{vol}(\alpha)\geq\limsup_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\mathrm{vol}\left(\alpha_{k}\right)

for every sequence
\alpha_{k}\in H^{1,1}_{\rm{BC}}\left(X_{t_{k}},\mathbb{R}\right)
converging to
\alpha
with
(t_{k}\rightarrow 0)
.

Remark 2.16
.

Recently,
[
36
]
proved the upper semi-continuity of volume of divisors for a projective morphism when fibers are irreducible and reduced, by use of a Fujita approximation technique.

Volume function satisfies some special properties for big classes and nef classes.

Lemma 2.17

(
[
7
, Theorems 4.1, 4.7]
)
.

For a normal compact Kähler variety
X
, one has:

(a) A class
\alpha\in H^{1,1}_{\mathrm{BC}}(X,\mathbb{R})
is big if and only if
\mathrm{vol}(\alpha)>0
;

(b) For a nef class
\beta\in H^{1,1}_{\mathrm{BC}}(X,\mathbb{R})
,
\mathrm{vol}(\beta)=\beta^{n}.

Given a smooth family, the top intersection number of a cohomology class is constant under deformations.

Lemma 2.18
.

Let
f:X\to S
be a smooth family of compact complex manifolds over a smooth connected curve
S
, with
n=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(X/S)
. Suppose that
\alpha\in H^{1,1}_{\mathrm{BC}}(X,\mathbb{R})
. Then the top intersection number

s\longmapsto\int_{X_{s}}(\alpha|_{X_{s}})^{n}

is constant in the family.

Proof.

Since
S
is connected, given any two points
s_{1},s_{2}\in S
, there exists a smooth real curve
\gamma:[0,1]\to S
with
\gamma(0)=s_{1}
,
\gamma(1)=s_{2}
. Since
f:X\to S
is a smooth morphism, the constant rank theorem implies that the inverse image
\overline{M}=f^{-1}(\gamma([0,1]))
is a smooth manifold with boundary
\partial\overline{M}=X_{s_{1}}\cup X_{s_{2}}
. Thus, Stokes’ theorem gives rise to

\int_{X_{s_{1}}}(\alpha|_{X_{s_{1}}})^{n}-\int_{X_{s_{2}}}(\alpha|_{X_{s_{2}}})^{n}=\int_{\overline{M}}d(\alpha^{n})=0.

That is,
s\longmapsto\int_{X_{s}}(\alpha|_{X_{s}})^{n}
is constant in the family.
∎

Given a pseudo-effective class on a compact complex manifold or more generally a normal compact complex analytic variety, we can take its Boucksom–Zariski decomposition.

Definition 2.19

(Minimal multiplicity)
.

Let
X
be a compact complex manifold and
\alpha\in H^{1,1}_{\mathrm{BC}}(X,\mathbb{R})
a pseudo-effective class. The
minimal multiplicity
of
\alpha
at a point
x\in X
is defined by

\nu(\alpha,x):=\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\nu\!\left(T_{\min,\varepsilon},x\right),

where
T_{\min,\varepsilon}
denotes a current with minimal singularities in the set

\alpha[-\varepsilon\omega]:=\{\text{closed almost positive }(1,1)\text{-currents }T\mid T\text{ represents }\alpha-\varepsilon\omega,\ T\geq-\varepsilon\omega\,\},

and
\nu(T_{\min,\varepsilon},x)
is the Lelong number of
T_{\min,\varepsilon}
at
x
.
If
D
is a prime divisor on
X
, the
generic minimal multiplicity
of
\alpha
along
D
is defined as

\nu(\alpha,D):=\inf\{\,\nu(\alpha,x)\mid x\in D\}.

Definition 2.20

(Boucksom–Zariski decomposition,
[
8
, Definition 3.7]
)
.

Let
X
be a compact complex manifold and
\alpha\in H^{1,1}_{\rm{BC}}(X,\mathbb{R})
a pseudo-effective class. Then we define the
negative part
of
\alpha
as

N(\alpha)=\sum\nu(\alpha,D)D,

where
D
runs through all the divisors on
X
,
and
Z(\alpha)=\alpha-[N(\alpha)].

Then we say that the decomposition

\alpha=Z(\alpha)+[N(\alpha)]

is the
Boucksom–Zariski decomposition
.

Remark 2.21
.

Given a normal compact complex analytic variety
X
, let
f\colon Y\to X
be a resolution of singularities of
X
. For a pseudo-effective class
\alpha\in H^{1,1}_{\rm{BC}}(X,\mathbb{R})
, we define the
negative part
of
\alpha
by

N(\alpha):=f_{*}\bigl(N(f^{*}\alpha)\bigr).

By
[
17
, Lemma A.7]
, this definition is independent of the choice of resolution, and hence
N(\alpha)
on a normal complex analytic variety is well defined.

We then describe the behavior of the volume of a big class under the Boucksom–Zariski decomposition:

Proposition 2.22

(
[
8
, Proposition 3.20]
)
.

Let
X
be a normal compact Kähler variety.
If
\alpha\in H^{1,1}_{\mathrm{BC}}(X,\mathbb{R})
is big and
E\geq 0
is a divisor on
X
such that

\mathrm{supp}(E)\subset\mathrm{supp}(N(\alpha))
, then

\mathrm{vol}(\alpha)=\mathrm{vol}(Z(\alpha)+[E])=\mathrm{vol}(Z(\alpha)),

where
N(\alpha)
is the negative part of the Boucksom–Zariski decomposition of
\alpha
,
and
Z(\alpha):=\alpha-[N(\alpha)]
is the positive part.

Let us end this subsection with some volume inequalities.

Lemma 2.23
.

Let
X
be a compact Kähler manifold of dimension
n
and
\omega\in H^{1,1}_{\mathrm{BC}}(X,\mathbb{R})
a Kähler class on
X
. If
\alpha\in H^{1,1}_{\mathrm{BC}}(X,\mathbb{R})
is a pseudo-effective class, then for any
\varepsilon>0
, the volume satisfies the inequality

\mathrm{vol}(\alpha+\varepsilon\omega)\geq\varepsilon^{n}\int_{X}\omega^{n}.

Proof.

Let
T\in\alpha
be a closed positive
(1,1)
-current on
X
.
By Demailly’s regularization theorem (
[
26
, Proposition (18.7)]
),
there exists a sequence
\{T_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
of closed
(1,1)
-currents
with analytic singularities in the same cohomology class
\alpha
, weakly converging to
T
, such that

T_{k}+\varepsilon_{k}\omega\geq 0,\qquad\varepsilon_{k}\downarrow 0.

In particular, for large
k
we have
\varepsilon_{k}<\varepsilon
, so
T_{k}+\varepsilon\omega\geq(\varepsilon-\varepsilon_{k})\omega
. Set
\delta_{k}=\varepsilon-\varepsilon_{k}>0
and then
\delta_{k}\to\varepsilon
. By the definition of volume, we have

\mathrm{vol}(\alpha+\varepsilon\omega)=\sup_{T_{\varepsilon}\in\alpha+\varepsilon\omega}\int_{X}(T_{\varepsilon})_{ac}^{n}\geq\limsup_{k\to\infty}\int_{X}(T_{k}+\varepsilon\omega)^{n}_{ac}\geq(\limsup_{k\to\infty}\delta_{k}^{n})\int_{X}\omega^{n}=\varepsilon^{n}\int_{X}\omega^{n}.

∎

The volume function satisfies certain log concavity properties.

Proposition 2.24
.

Let
X
be a compact Kähler manifold of complex dimension
n
. If
\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}\in H^{1,1}_{\rm{BC}}(X,\mathbb{R})
are big classes, then the volume satisfies the log concavity property

\mathrm{vol}(\alpha+\alpha^{\prime})^{1/n}\geq\mathrm{vol}(\alpha)^{1/n}+\mathrm{vol}(\alpha^{\prime})^{1/n}.

Proof.

Note that the log concavity of the volume is well known for line bundles and
\mathbb{R}
-divisors (cf.
[
45
, Remark 2.2.50]
and also
[
44
, Corollary 4.12]
) and also for nef classes by the Khovanskii–-Teissier inequalities proved in
[
25
]
. The general case then follows easily from the analytic version of Fujita’s
approximation theorem
[
7
, Theorem 1.4]
. Given big classes
\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}\in H^{1,1}_{\rm{BC}}(X,\mathbb{R})
and
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a resolution
\nu:X_{\varepsilon}\to X
and Kähler classes
\omega,\omega^{\prime}\in H^{1,1}_{\rm{BC}}(X_{\varepsilon},\mathbb{R})
on
X_{\varepsilon}
such that

{\rm vol}(\omega)\leq{\rm vol}(\alpha)\leq{\rm vol}(\omega)+\varepsilon,

{\rm vol}(\omega^{\prime})\leq{\rm vol}(\alpha^{\prime})\leq{\rm vol}(\omega^{\prime})+\varepsilon,

{\rm vol}(\omega+\omega^{\prime})\leq{\rm vol}(\alpha+\alpha^{\prime}).

But then

({\rm vol}(\alpha)-\varepsilon)^{1/n}+({\rm vol}(\alpha^{\prime})-\varepsilon)^{1/n}\leq{\rm vol}(\omega)^{1/n}+{\rm vol}(\omega^{\prime})^{1/n}\leq{\rm vol}(\omega+\omega^{\prime})^{1/n}\leq{\rm vol}(\alpha+\alpha^{\prime})^{1/n},

and taking the limit as
\varepsilon\to 0
yields
{\rm vol}(\alpha)^{1/n}+{\rm vol}(\alpha^{\prime})^{1/n}\leq{\rm vol}(\alpha+\alpha^{\prime})^{1/n}
.
∎

2.5.
Bigness and pseudo-effectivity in families

We start this subsection with:

Definition 2.25

(Relatively big and pseudo-effective classes)
.

Let
f:X\to S
be a proper surjective morphism from a normal complex analytic variety
X
onto a relatively compact base
S
. Then we say that:

(1)
A cohomology class
\alpha\in H^{1,1}_{\rm{BC}}(X,\mathbb{R})
is
f
-pseudo-effective over
S
or
relatively pseudo-effective over
S
if there is a complement
V
of a countable union of Zariski closed proper subsets of
S
such that
\alpha_{s}:=\alpha|_{X_{s}}
is pseudo-effective for any
s\in V
.

(2)
A cohomology class
\alpha\in H^{1,1}_{\rm{BC}}(X,\mathbb{R})
is
f
-big over
S
or
relatively big over
S
if there is a non-empty Zariski open subset
U
of
S
such that
\alpha_{s}
is big for any
s\in U
.

The following proposition shows that a relatively pseudo-effective class is pseudo-effective on general fibers.

Proposition 2.26
.

Let
f:X\to S
be a family from a normal Kähler variety
X
with a Kähler class
\omega
onto a relatively compact base
S
. A class
\alpha\in H^{1,1}_{\rm{BC}}(X,\mathbb{R})
is
f
-pseudo-effective over
S
if and only if there exists a non-empty Zariski open subset
U\subset S
such that
\alpha_{s}
is pseudo-effective for any
s\in U
.

Proof.

After shrinking
S
and replacing
X
by a resolution, we may assume that
X\to S
is smooth. By assumption
\alpha_{s}
is pseudo-effective for very general
s\in S
.
By Lemma
2.23
,

\mathrm{vol}(\alpha_{s}+\varepsilon\omega_{s})\geq\varepsilon^{d}\cdot v\text{ for very general }s\in S,

where
d=\dim(X/S)
and
v=\int_{X_{s}}\omega_{s}^{d}
. Note that
v=\int_{X_{s}}\omega_{s}^{d}
is a constant independent of
s\in S
by Lemma
2.18
. By the upper semicontinuity of volume (cf. Proposition
2.15
),
\mathrm{vol}(\alpha_{s}+\varepsilon\omega_{s})\geq\varepsilon^{d}\cdot v
for all
s\in S
. But then
\alpha_{s}
is pseudo-effective for any
s\in S
.
∎

Before proceeding, let us first recall the definition of the pullback of a closed positive
(1,1)
-current with local potential.

Definition 2.27

(Pull back of closed positive
(1,1)
-current with local potential,
[
49
, Section 3.1]
)
.

Let
f:X\rightarrow Y
be a surjective morphism between complex analytic varieties. For a closed positive
(1,1)
-current
T
with local potential on
Y
, we define the inverse image
f^{*}T
as follows:

Let
\Omega
be an open set in
Y
on which
T_{\Omega}=i\partial\bar{\partial}u
with
u
a plurisubharmonic potential function in
\Omega
. If the plurisubharmonic function
u\circ f
is not identically
-\infty
on each connected component of
f^{-1}(\Omega)
, we then define

\left.\left(f^{*}T\right)\right|_{f^{-1}(\Omega)}=i\partial\bar{\partial}(u\circ f).

If
\Omega^{\prime}
is another open set in
Y
with a similar representation
T_{\Omega^{\prime}}=i\partial\bar{\partial}u^{\prime}
, the function
u-u^{\prime}
is pluriharmonic on
\Omega\cap\Omega^{\prime}
, and the same holds for
u\circ f-u^{\prime}\circ f
on
f^{-1}(\Omega)\cap f^{-1}\left(\Omega^{\prime}\right)
, so the above definition is independent of the choice of
\Omega
and the potential
u
.

Remark 2.28
.

Note that the closed positive current
T
admits a global representative

T=\theta+i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi
, where
\theta
is a smooth
(1,1)
-form and
\varphi
is an almost plurisubharmonic function on
Y
, obtained via a partition of unity argument (cf.
[
49
, Proposition 3.1.1]
). Hence the pullback
f^{*}T
can be written as

f^{*}T=f^{*}\theta+i\partial\bar{\partial}f^{*}\varphi
, and therefore
[f^{*}T]=f^{*}[T]
.

We can now describe the behavior of pseudo-effective and big classes under bimeromorphic change.

Proposition 2.29
.

Let
f:Y\rightarrow X/S
be a proper bimeromorphic morphism of normal complex analytic varieties over a relatively compact base
S
. If
\alpha\in H^{1,1}_{\rm BC}(X,\mathbb{R})
and
{{\beta}}\in H^{1,1}_{\rm BC}(Y,\mathbb{R})
are relatively pseudo-effective over
S
, then so are the classes
{{\beta}}^{\prime}=f_{*}{{\beta}}
and
\alpha^{\prime}=f^{*}\alpha
.

Proof.

First, by Definition
2.25
, to check relative pseudo-effectivity, it is sufficient to check it on the general fiber. By
[
14
, Corollary 3.2]
,
f_{s}:Y_{s}\to X_{s}
is bimeromorphic on the general fiber. Hence, in what follows, without loss of generality, we can reduce the problem to the absolute case and assume that
S
is a point.

We then show that if
\alpha\in H^{1,1}_{\rm BC}(X,\mathbb{R})
is pseudo-effective, then so is
f^{*}\alpha
. Let
T_{\alpha}
be a closed positive
(1,1)
-current such that
[T_{\alpha}]=\alpha
. By Definition
2.27
, we can find an open cover
\{\Omega_{i}\}
of
X
such that

T_{\alpha}|_{\Omega_{i}}=i\partial\bar{\partial}u_{i},

for some plurisubharmonic function
u_{i}
, and

f^{*}T_{\alpha}\big|_{f^{-1}(\Omega_{i})}=i\partial\bar{\partial}(u_{i}\circ f).

By
[
26
, Proposition 1.1.8]
, the pullback of a plurisubharmonic function is again plurisubharmonic, so
f^{*}T_{\alpha}
is a positive current. By Remark
2.28
,
[f^{*}T_{\alpha}]=f^{*}\alpha
. Hence
f^{*}\alpha
is a pseudo-effective class.

We end our proof by showing that if
\beta\in H^{1,1}_{\rm BC}(X,\mathbb{R})
is pseudo-effective, then so is
f_{*}\beta
. First, there exists a closed positive
(1,1)
-current
T_{\beta}
such that
[T_{\beta}]=\beta
. By definition, a closed
(1,1)
-current is positive if and only if it is positive on every smooth positive
(n-1,n-1)
-form
\varphi
. Moreover, since
\varphi
is positive, the pullback
f^{*}\varphi
is semi-positive. Hence

\langle f_{*}T_{\beta},\varphi\rangle=\langle T_{\beta},f^{*}\varphi\rangle\geq 0,

for every smooth positive
(n-1,n-1)
-form
\varphi
. Thus, the pushforward of a pseudo-effective class is pseudo-effective.
∎

Proposition 2.30
.

Let
f:Y\rightarrow X/S
be a proper bimeromorphic morphism of a normal Kähler varieties over a relatively compact base
S
. If
\alpha\in H^{1,1}_{\rm BC}(X,\mathbb{R})
and
{{\beta}}\in H^{1,1}_{\rm BC}(Y,\mathbb{R})
are relatively big over
S
, then so are the classes
{{\beta}}^{\prime}=f_{*}{{\beta}}
and
\alpha^{\prime}=f^{*}\alpha
.

Proof.

Similar to Proposition
2.29
, we may reduce to the absolute setting and assume that
S
is a point. In particular, both
X
and
Y
are compact.

We first show that if
\alpha\in H^{1,1}_{\mathrm{BC}}(X,\mathbb{R})
is big, then
f^{*}\alpha
is big. Since

\mathrm{vol}(f^{*}\alpha)=\mathrm{vol}(\alpha)>0,

Proposition
2.17
implies that
f^{*}\alpha
is big.

We next prove that if
\beta\in H^{1,1}_{\mathrm{BC}}(Y,\mathbb{R})
is big, then
\beta^{\prime}=f_{*}\beta
is big. Let
T_{\beta}
be a Kähler current representing
\beta
. By definition, there exists a smooth Hermitian form
\omega_{Y}
with

T_{\beta}\geq\omega_{Y}.

Let
\omega_{X}
be a smooth Hermitian form on
X
. Since
Y
is compact, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

f^{*}\omega_{X}\leq C\,\omega_{Y}.

It follows that

f_{*}T_{\beta}\geq f_{*}\omega_{Y}\geq C^{-1}f_{*}f^{*}\omega_{X}=C^{-1}\,\omega_{X}.

Thus
f_{*}T_{\beta}
is again a Kähler current. Because
[f_{*}T_{\beta}]=f_{*}\beta
, the class
f_{*}\beta
is big.
∎

We say that a bimeromorphic contraction
\phi:X\dashrightarrow Y
is a
(K_{X}+B+{{\bm{\beta}}})
-non-positive contraction
if under a common resolution
p:W\to X
and
q:W\to Y
, the divisor
E
in

p^{*}(K_{X}+B+{{\bm{\beta}}_{X}})=q^{*}(K_{Y}+B_{Y}+{{\bm{\beta}}}_{Y})+E

is an effective
q
-exceptional divisor (that is,
q_{*}E=0
).

A
(K_{X}+B+\bm{\beta}_{X})
-non-positive contraction preserves pseudo-effectivity (resp. bigness) of adjoint classes.

Proposition 2.31
.

Let
(X,B+\bm{\beta}_{X})
be a generalized Kähler pair. Given a
(K_{X}+B+\bm{\beta}_{X})
-non-positive contraction (e.g. a sequence of steps of the
(X,B+\bm{\beta}_{X})
-MMP)

\phi:(X,B+{\bm{\beta}_{X}})\dashrightarrow(X^{\prime},B^{\prime}+{\bm{\beta}}_{X^{\prime}})/S,

the adjoint class
K_{X}+B+{{\bm{\beta}}_{X}}
is relatively pseudo-effective (resp. big) over
S
if and only if
K_{X^{\prime}}+B^{\prime}+{{\bm{\beta}}}_{X^{\prime}}
is relatively pseudo-effective (resp. big) over
S
.

Proof.

Assume that
K_{X}+B+{\bm{\beta}}_{X}
is relatively pseudo-effective (resp. big) over
S
.
Let
p:Y\to X
and
q:Y\to X^{\prime}
be a common resolution of
\phi
.
Since
\phi
is
(K_{X}+B+{\bm{\beta}}_{X})
-non-positive over
S
,

p^{*}(K_{X}+B+{\bm{\beta}}_{X})\equiv_{S}q^{*}(K_{X^{\prime}}+B^{\prime}+{\bm{\beta}}_{X^{\prime}})+E,

with
E\geq 0
effective and
q
-exceptional.
Since we already showed in Propositions
2.29
and
2.30
that pullback and pushforward under bimeromorphic morphisms preserve pseudo-effectivity (resp. bigness), it follows that

q_{*}\bigl(p^{*}(K_{X}+B+{\bm{\beta}}_{X})\bigr)\equiv_{S}q_{*}\bigl(q^{*}(K_{X^{\prime}}+B^{\prime}+{\bm{\beta}}_{X^{\prime}})+E\bigr)=K_{X^{\prime}}+B^{\prime}+{\bm{\beta}}_{X^{\prime}}

is relatively pseudo-effective (resp. big) over
S
.

Conversely, if
K_{X^{\prime}}+B^{\prime}+{\bm{\beta}}_{X^{\prime}}
is relatively pseudo-effective (resp. big) over
S
, then by the effectiveness of
E
and
p^{*}(K_{X}+B+{\bm{\beta}}_{X})\equiv_{S}q^{*}(K_{X^{\prime}}+B^{\prime}+{\bm{\beta}}_{X^{\prime}})+E
, it is easy to see that

p^{*}(K_{X}+B+{\bm{\beta}}_{X})
is relatively pseudo-effective (resp. big) over
S
, and therefore so is

K_{X}+B+{\bm{\beta}}_{X}
.
∎

Remark 2.32
.

Note that for a bimeromorphic contraction morphism, the pseudo-effectivity of
f_{*}\alpha
does not necessarily imply that of
{{\alpha}}
. Consider the blow-up
f:X\to Y
of a smooth projective variety
Y
. Let
D\geq 0
be an effective divisor on
X
and
E\geq 0
an exceptional divisor. For
m\gg 1
, the divisor
D-mE
is not pseudo-effective, but

f_{*}(D-mE)=f_{*}D\geq 0

is pseudo-effective.

Proposition 2.33
.

Let
f:X\to S
be a family from a normal Kähler variety
X
to a complex analytic base
S
, and
\alpha\in H^{1,1}_{\rm{BC}}(X,\mathbb{R})
. Let
W
be a compact subset of
S
and
X_{W}=f^{-1}(W)
. If
\alpha|_{X_{W}}\in H^{1,1}_{\rm{BC}}(X_{W},\mathbb{R})
is absolute big (resp. pseudo-effective), then it is relatively big (resp. pseudo-effective) over
W
.

Proof.

We first prove that if
\alpha|_{X_{W}}
is absolute big then it is also relatively big over
W
. By Lemma
2.10
, the restricted non-Kähler locus of
\alpha
is a proper subvariety of
X_{W}
.
In particular, there exists a non-empty Zariski open subset
U\subset W
such that the fibers
X_{s}
for
s\in U
are not contained in
E_{nK}^{as}(\alpha|_{X_{W}})
. Since

E_{nK}^{as}(\alpha|_{X_{s}})\subset E_{nK}^{as}(\alpha|_{X_{W}})\cap X_{s},

E_{nK}^{as}(\alpha|_{X_{s}})
does not contain the fiber
X_{s}
for
s\in U
. Hence, by Lemma
2.10
, the class
\alpha|_{X_{s}}
is big for
s\in U
, i.e.,
\alpha|_{X_{W}}
is
f
-big over
W
.

Given a pseudo-effective class
\alpha|_{X_{W}}
on
X_{W}
, Proposition
2.7
shows that there exists a Kähler class
\omega
on
X_{W}
such that
\alpha|_{X_{W}}+\varepsilon\omega
is (absolute) big for any
\varepsilon>0
. Hence, by what we just proved above,
\alpha|_{X_{W}}+\varepsilon\omega
is also
f
-big over
W
for any
\varepsilon>0
. Thus,
\alpha
is
f
-pseudo-effective over
W
.
∎

Proposition 2.34

(
[
18
, Lemma 5.1]
)
.

Let
g:X\rightarrow S
be a proper surjective morphism from a generalized Kähler pair
(X,B+{\bm{\beta}})
with generalized klt singularities onto a relatively compact complex analytic variety
S
. If
D
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier
\mathbb{Z}
-divisor on
X
such that
D-\left(K_{X}+B+{{\bm{\beta}}}_{X}\right)
is relatively nef and big over
S
, then
R^{i}g_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)=0
for all
i>0
.

Proof.

The proof is based on
[
18
, Lemma 5.1]
with a minor change. Let
\nu:(X^{\prime},B^{\prime}+{\bm{\beta}}_{X^{\prime}})\to(X,B+{\bm{\beta}}_{X})
be a log resolution such that
{\bm{\beta}}_{X^{\prime}}
is nef over
S
, with

K_{X^{\prime}}+B^{\prime}+\bm{\beta}_{X^{\prime}}=\nu^{*}(K_{X}+B+\bm{\beta}_{X}).

By Propositions
2.30
and
2.5
, the divisor

\nu^{*}(D)-(K_{X^{\prime}}+B^{\prime})=\bm{\beta}_{X^{\prime}}+\nu^{*}(D-(K_{X}+B+\bm{\beta}_{X}))

is a big and nef over
S
. Since
X^{\prime}
is smooth, there exists some
m\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
such that
m(\nu^{*}(D)-(K_{X^{\prime}}+B^{\prime}))
is a relatively big line bundle over
S
. By
[
18
, Lemma 2.18]
, there exists a projective modification
\mu:X^{\prime\prime}\to X^{\prime}
such that
g^{\prime\prime}:X^{\prime\prime}\to S
is projective.

Then the proof follows along the same lines as
[
18
, Lemma 5.1]
.
Let
\psi=\mu\circ\nu
. We have the following commutative diagram:
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{X^{\prime\prime}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{X^{\prime}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mu}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g^{\prime\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\nu}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}

such that
\bm{\beta}_{X^{\prime\prime}}=\mu^{*}\bm{\beta}_{X^{\prime}}
is nef over
S
, and

K_{X^{\prime\prime}}+B^{\prime\prime}+\bm{\beta}_{X^{\prime\prime}}=\psi^{*}\bigl(K_{X}+B+\bm{\beta}_{X}\bigr).

We then set

E:=\left\lfloor B^{\prime\prime}+\{-\psi^{*}D\}\right\rfloor,\quad B^{*}:=B^{\prime\prime}+\{-\psi^{*}D\}-E=\{B^{\prime\prime}+\{-\psi^{*}D\}\},

where
\{\cdot\}
(resp.,
\left\lfloor\cdot\right\rfloor,\left\lceil\cdot\right\rceil
) denotes the fractional (resp. round down, round up) part of a divisor. Hence,

\left\lceil\psi^{*}D\right\rceil-E-(K_{X^{\prime\prime}}+B^{*})\equiv\bm{\beta}_{X^{\prime\prime}}+\psi^{*}\left(D-(K_{X}+B+\bm{\beta}_{X})\right).

By assumption,
D-(K_{X}+B+\bm{\beta}_{X})
is nef and big over
S
, hence
\left\lceil\psi^{*}D\right\rceil-E-(K_{X^{\prime\prime}}+B^{*})
is also nef and big over
S
.

Since
g^{\prime\prime}:X^{\prime\prime}\to S
is projective, we may apply the standard version of the Kawamata–Viehweg vanishing theorem, obtaining

R^{p}(g^{\prime\prime})_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime\prime}}(\left\lceil\psi^{*}D\right\rceil-E)=0,\quad R^{p}\psi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime\prime}}(\left\lceil\psi^{*}D\right\rceil-E)=0\quad\text{for any }p>0.

By the same argument as in
[
18
, Lemma 5.1]
, we can prove that

\psi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime\prime}}(\left\lceil\psi^{*}D\right\rceil-E)=\mathcal{O}_{X}(D).

Therefore, a standard Leray spectral sequence argument yields

R^{p}g_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)=R^{p}g_{*}(\psi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime\prime}}(\left\lceil\psi^{*}D\right\rceil-E))=R^{p}g^{\prime\prime}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime\prime}}(\left\lceil\psi^{*}D\right\rceil-E)=0

for all
p>0
.
∎

2.6.
Uniruled varieties

We next introduce some basic results about uniruled varieties.

Definition 2.35

(Uniruled varieties)
.

A complex analytic variety
X
is called
uniruled
if for every general point
x\in X
there is a non-constant morphism
f:\mathbb{P}^{1}\to X
such that
f(0)=x
.

Remark 2.36
.

With a slight modification of the proof of
[
50
, Proposition II.5.4]
, if
X
is a compact complex manifold, then the following are equivalent.

(1)
X
is uniruled (in the sense of Definition
2.35
);

(2) There exists a complex analytic variety
Y
and a dominant meromorphic map

\Phi:\mathbb{P}^{1}\times Y\dashrightarrow X,

that is not constant on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
;

(3) There exists a free rational curve
f:\mathbb{P}^{1}\to X
;

(4) For a general point
x\in X
, there exists a free rational curve
f:\mathbb{P}^{1}\to X
with
f(\infty)=x
.

Obviously, uniruledness is a bimeromorphically invariant property. If, moreover,
X
is Kähler, then uniruledness is also equivalent to
K_{X}
being non–pseudo-effective by Theorem
1.1
.

Any uniruled variety with canonical singularities is covered by family of
K_{X}
-negative curves.
Recall that a normal complex variety
M
is said to have

(only) canonical singularities
if the canonical divisor
K_{M}

is a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor, i.e., some integer multiple of

K_{M}
is a Cartier divisor, and if the discrepancy divisor

K_{\tilde{M}}-\mu^{*}K_{M}
is effective for a (or every) resolution of singularities

\mu:\tilde{M}\rightarrow M
.

Lemma 2.37
.

Let
X
be a uniruled variety with canonical singularities. Then
X
is covered by a family of rational curves
\{{C}_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
such that
K_{X}\cdot{C}_{\lambda}<0
.

Proof.

Since
X
is uniruled, by Definition
2.35
,
X
is covered by rational curves
\{{C}_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
. Since
X
admits canonical singularities, let
f:X^{\prime}\to X
be a resolution then

K_{X^{\prime}}=f^{*}K_{X}+E,

for some effective,
f
-exceptional divisor
E\geq 0
. Let
{C}_{\lambda}^{\prime}=f^{-1}_{*}({C}_{\lambda})
be the strict transform of a general member in the family of curves
\{{C}_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
, then

K_{X}\cdot{C}_{\lambda}=(K_{X^{\prime}}-E)\cdot{C}^{\prime}_{\lambda}\leq K_{X^{\prime}}\cdot{C}^{\prime}_{\lambda}

and so it suffices to show that
K_{X^{\prime}}\cdot{C}^{\prime}_{\lambda}<0
; in particular, we may assume that
X
is smooth.

Then the rest of the proof follows from
[
42
]
. Since
X
is a smooth uniruled variety, there is a family of rational curves
\mathcal{C}\to T
and a dominant holomorphic map
g:\mathcal{C}\to X
. Replacing
T
by an open subset, we may assume that
T
is isomorphic to a disc. Cutting by hyperplanes, we may assume that
\dim\mathcal{C}=\dim X
and hence
g
is a generically finite holomorphic map of complex manifolds. We may then write
K_{\mathcal{C}}=g^{*}K_{X}+R
where
R
is an effective divisor. We then have

-2={\rm deg}(K_{\mathbb{P}^{1}})={\rm deg}(K_{\mathcal{C}}|_{C_{\lambda}})=K_{X}\cdot C_{\lambda}+R\cdot C_{\lambda}\geq K_{X}\cdot C_{\lambda}

and hence
K_{X}\cdot C_{\lambda}<0
.
∎

We can easily generalize the remarkable Theorem
1.1
of Ou to the singular setting.

Lemma 2.38
.

For a normal compact
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein Kähler variety
X
, we have:

(1)
If
X
is not uniruled, then
K_{X}
is pseudo-effective;

(2)
If
X
admits canonical singularities and
K_{X}
is pseudo-effective, then
X
is not uniruled.

Proof.

(1) Let
\pi:Y\to X
be a resolution. If
X
is not uniruled, then neither is
Y
. Since
Y
is smooth and Kähler, Theorem
1.1
implies that
K_{Y}
is pseudo-effective. By Proposition
2.29
,
K_{X}
is pseudo-effective.

(2) If
X
has canonical singularities and
\pi:Y\to X
is a resolution, then
K_{Y}=\pi^{*}K_{X}+E
with the exceptional divisor
E\geq 0
. Thus, if
K_{X}
is pseudo-effective, then
K_{Y}
is also pseudo-effective. By
[
54
, Theorem 1.1]
,
Y
is not uniruled and thus,
X
is not uniruled by bimeromorphic invariance of uniruledness in Remark
2.36
.
∎

Remark 2.39
.

If
X
is log terminal, the pseudo-effectivity of
K_{X}
does not necessarily imply that
X
is not uniruled. Totaro
[
65
, Example on p. 245]
constructed a rational Calabi-–Yau quotient surface as
Y=E\times E/(\mathbb{Z}/3)
where
\mathbb{Z}/3
acts by
(\zeta,\zeta)
on
E\times E
(where
\zeta
is a third root of the unity). This surface admits quotient singularities and hence is log terminal. Note that
Y
is rational, since it can be obtained by blowing up 12 points on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
in the Hesse configuration and then blowing down 9 lines.

2.7.
Pseudo-effective threshold

We end this section by introducing the pseudo-effective threshold and several basic facts about it, which will play an important role in the proof of our main Theorem
5.2
.

Definition 2.40

(Pseudo-effective threshold)
.

Let
X
be a normal compact Kähler variety, and
\omega\in H^{1,1}_{\rm{BC}}(X,\mathbb{R})
a Kähler class on
X
. We define the
pseudo-effective threshold of

\alpha\in H^{1,1}_{\rm{BC}}(X,\mathbb{R})
to be

\tau(X,\alpha;\omega):=\inf\left\{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\mid\alpha+t\omega\text{ is pseudo-effective}\right\}.

Similarly, we can define the relative pseudo-effective threshold.

Definition 2.41

(Relative pseudo-effective threshold)
.

Let
f:X\to S
be a proper surjective morphism with connected fibers from a normal Kähler variety onto a relatively compact base
S
, and
\omega\in H^{1,1}_{\rm{BC}}(X,\mathbb{R})
a Kähler class on
X
.
We define the
relative pseudo-effective threshold
of
\alpha\in H^{1,1}_{\rm{BC}}(X,\mathbb{R})
over
S
to be

\tau(X/S,\alpha;\omega):=\inf\{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\mid\alpha+t\omega\text{ is }f\text{-pseudo-effective over }S\}.

For a line bundle
L
on
X
, set
\tau(X/S,L;\omega):=\tau(X/S,c_{1}(L);\omega)
.

Moreover, we define

\tau^{\prime}(X/S,\alpha;\omega):=\inf\{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\mid\alpha+t\omega\text{ is }f\text{-big over }S\}.

Lemma 2.42
.

Let
f:X\to S
be a proper surjective morphism with connected fibers from a normal Kähler variety onto a relatively compact base
S
, and
\omega\in H^{1,1}_{\rm{BC}}(X,\mathbb{R})
a Kähler class on
X
. Then

\tau(X/S,\alpha;\omega)=\tau^{\prime}(X/S,\alpha;\omega).

Proof.

Set
\tau=\tau(X/S,\alpha;\omega)
and
\tau^{\prime}=\tau^{\prime}(X/S,\alpha;\omega)
. We only need to prove
\tau^{\prime}\leq\tau
. By definition of
\tau
, there exists a non increasing sequence
t_{i}
with
\lim_{i\to\infty}t_{i}=\tau
in
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
and a countable union
Z_{i}
of proper Zariski closed subsets of
S
such that
\alpha_{s}+t_{i}\omega_{s}
is pseudo-effective for every
s\in V_{i}:=S\setminus Z_{i}
. Therefore
\alpha_{s}+\tau\omega_{s}
is pseudo-effective for every
s\in V_{\infty}=\bigcap_{i=1}^{\infty}V_{i}
(note that
V_{\infty}
is also a complement of countable union of proper Zariski closed subsets of
S
). By Proposition
2.26
, there exists a dense Zariski open subset
V
such that
\alpha_{s}+\tau\omega_{s}
is pseudo-effective for
s\in V
.

Passing to a resolution and further shrinking
V
we may assume that
f
is smooth over
V
. Thus, Lemma
2.23
implies that for any
s\in V
and any
t>\tau
,

\mathrm{vol}(\alpha_{s}+t\omega_{s})\geq(t-\tau)^{n}v>0,

with
n=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(X/S)
and
v=\int_{X_{s}}\omega_{s}^{n}
. Therefore, by Proposition
2.17
, for any
t>\tau
,
\alpha+t\omega
is
f
-big, and thus
\tau^{\prime}\leq\tau
.
∎

Lemma 2.43
.

Let
f:X\to S
be a proper surjective morphism with connected fibers from a normal Kähler variety onto a relatively compact base
S
, and
\omega\in H^{1,1}_{\rm{BC}}(X,\mathbb{R})
a Kähler class on
X
. Then
\alpha
is not
f
-pseudo-effective over
S
if and only if the pseudo-effective threshold
\tau(X/S,\alpha;\omega)>0
.

Proof.

Assume that
\alpha
is
f
-pseudo-effective over
S
. Then we have
\tau(X/S,\alpha;\omega)=0
by definition.
Conversely, if
\tau(X/S,\alpha;\omega)=0
, then for any
\varepsilon>0
, the class
\alpha+\varepsilon\omega
is
f
-big. By Proposition
2.7
,
\alpha
is
f
-pseudo-effective over
S
.
∎

Remark 2.44
.

If
S
is relatively compact, then the relative pseudo-effective threshold

\tau(X,\alpha,S;\omega)=\inf\{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\mid\alpha+t\omega\text{ is }f\text{-pseudo-effective over some neighborhood }U\subset S\}

is well defined, i.e.,
\tau(X,\alpha,S;\omega)<+\infty
. This follows since for
t\gg 0
,

\alpha+t\omega
is Kähler (and hence pseudo-effective) over
S
. Thus,
\tau(X,\alpha,S;\omega)<+\infty
.

Lemma 2.45
.

Let
f:X\to S
be a proper surjective morphism from a normal Kähler variety onto a relatively compact base
S
. If
\alpha\in H^{1,1}_{\rm{BC}}(X,\mathbb{R})
is relatively big over
S
, then
-\alpha
is not relatively big over
S
.

Proof.

To see this, assume by contradiction that
-\alpha
is big over a general fiber
X_{s}
.
Then there exist Kähler currents

T_{+}\geq\varepsilon_{+}\omega^{\prime},\quad T_{-}\geq\varepsilon_{-}\omega^{\prime}

for some Kähler metric
\omega^{\prime}
on
X_{s}
and
\varepsilon_{+},\varepsilon_{-}>0
, such that
[T_{+}]=\alpha|_{X_{s}},\ [T_{-}]=-\alpha|_{X_{s}}
.

Since
[T_{+}+T_{-}]=0
and
T_{+}+T_{-}\geq 0
, we have
T_{+}+T_{-}=0
(because a plurisubharmonic function on a compact complex variety is constant). This is impossible since then

0=T_{+}+T_{-}\geq(\varepsilon_{+}+\varepsilon_{-})\omega^{\prime}.

∎

Proposition 2.46
.

Let
f\colon X\to S
be a proper surjective morphism from a generalized Kähler pair
(X,B+\bm{\beta})
to a relatively compact base
S
.
If
-(K_{X}+B+\bm{\beta}_{X})
is relatively Kähler over
S
, then the adjoint class

K_{X}+B+\bm{\beta}_{X}
is not relatively pseudo-effective over
S
.
Moreover, if
S
is compact, then
K_{X}+B+\bm{\beta}_{X}
is not (absolutely) pseudo-effective on
X
.

Proof.

Let
\omega
be a Kähler class on
X
. Since
S
is relatively compact,

-(K_{X}+B+{{\bm{\beta}}_{X}}+\varepsilon\omega)
is still relatively Kähler over
S
for sufficiently small
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
. By Lemma
2.45
,

K_{X}+B+{{\bm{\beta}}_{X}}+\varepsilon\omega
is not relatively big over
S
.

Therefore the pseudo-effective threshold is
\tau(X,B+{\bm{\beta}_{X}},S;A)>0
. By Lemma
2.43
,
K_{X}+B+{{\bm{\beta}}_{X}}
is not relatively pseudo-effective over
S
. When
S
is compact, Proposition
2.33
implies that
K_{X}+B+\bm{\beta}_{X}
is not absolutely pseudo-effective on
X
either.
∎

Lemma 2.47
.

Let
X
be a compact Kähler threefold with the Kähler class
\omega
, and
(X,B+\bm{\beta})
a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial generalized klt pair. Assume that
K_{X}+B+\bm{\beta}_{X}
is not pseudo-effective and that the
(K_{X}+B+\bm{\beta}_{X})
-MMP with scaling of
\omega
,

(X,B+\bm{\beta}_{X})\dashrightarrow(X_{1},B_{1}+\bm{\beta}_{X_{1}})\dashrightarrow\cdots\dashrightarrow(X_{N},B_{N}+\bm{\beta}_{X_{N}})=(X^{\prime},B^{\prime}+\bm{\beta}_{X^{\prime}})

terminates with a Mori fibre space
f:X^{\prime}\to Z^{\prime}
.
Set

\tau:=\inf\bigl\{t\geq 0\,\big|\,K_{X}+B+\bm{\beta}_{X}+t\omega\text{ is pseudo-effective}\bigr\}(>0).

Then the following hold:

(a) The Mori fibre space
X^{\prime}\to Z^{\prime}
is
(K_{X^{\prime}}+B^{\prime}+\bm{\beta}_{X^{\prime}}+\tau\omega^{\prime})
-trivial;

(b) Conversely, if the Mori fibre space
X^{\prime}\to Z^{\prime}
is
(K_{X^{\prime}}+B^{\prime}+\bm{\beta}_{X^{\prime}}+\delta\omega^{\prime})
-trivial for some
\delta>0
, then
\delta=\tau
(i.e,
\delta
is the pseudo-effective threshold
\tau
).

Proof of (a).

Running the
(K_{X}+B+\bm{\beta}_{X})
-MMP with scaling of
\omega
, we obtain a sequence

(X,B+\bm{\beta}_{X})\dashrightarrow(X_{1},B_{1}+\bm{\beta}_{X_{1}})\dashrightarrow\cdots\dashrightarrow(X_{N},B_{N}+\bm{\beta}_{X_{N}})=(X^{\prime},B^{\prime}+\bm{\beta}_{X^{\prime}}).

Here we denote by
\omega_{i}
the push forward of
\omega
on
X_{i}
and

\lambda_{i}:=\inf\left\{t\geq 0\,\middle|\,K_{X_{i}}+B_{i}+\bm{\beta}_{X_{i}}+t\omega_{i}\text{ is nef}\right\},

where
\{\lambda_{i}\}
is a non increasing sequence bounded below by
\tau

\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{i}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{N}\geq\tau>0.

Next, we prove that
\lambda_{N}=\tau
. Since
K_{X_{N}}+B_{N}+\bm{\beta}_{X_{N}}+\lambda_{N}\omega_{N}
is nef, then
\lambda_{N}\geq\tau
, and it suffices to show that
\lambda_{N}\leq\tau
.
Since the MMP terminates with a
(K_{X^{\prime}}+B^{\prime}+\bm{\beta}_{X^{\prime}}+\lambda_{N}\omega^{\prime})
-trivial Mori fibre space which is
(K_{X^{\prime}}+B^{\prime}+\bm{\beta}_{X^{\prime}})
-negative.
If
\tau<\lambda_{N}
, then for the extremal ray
R
contracted by the Mori fibre space

X^{\prime}\to Z^{\prime}
, we have

\bigl(K_{X^{\prime}}+B^{\prime}+\bm{\beta}_{X^{\prime}}+\tau\omega^{\prime}\bigr)\cdot R<0.

This contradicts the pseudo-effectivity of

K_{X^{\prime}}+B^{\prime}+\bm{\beta}_{X^{\prime}}+\tau\omega^{\prime}
.
Hence
\lambda_{N}=\tau
.
∎

Proof of (b).

Conversely, assume that the MMP ends with a
(K_{X^{\prime}}+B^{\prime}+\bm{\beta}_{X^{\prime}}+\delta\omega^{\prime})
-trivial Mori fibre space.

We already showed in (a) that the Mori fibre space is
(K_{X^{\prime}}+B^{\prime}+\bm{\beta}_{X^{\prime}}+\tau\omega^{\prime})
-trivial and
(K_{X^{\prime}}+B^{\prime}+\bm{\beta}_{X^{\prime}})
-negative. Thus

\omega^{\prime}\cdot R>0,\quad(\delta-\tau)\,\omega^{\prime}\cdot R=0,

and the only possible case is
\delta=\tau
.
∎

.

3.
Singularities and positivities of line bundles in families

In this section, we study the deformation behavior of various kinds of singularities and positivities in Kähler families.

3.1.
Deformation of singularities

We first study the behavior of singularities under deformation, generalizing several results from
[

]

Proposition 3.1

(
[

, Proposition 3.5]
)
.

Let

be a flat proper morphism from a generalized Kähler pair

Proposition 3.2
.

Let

-factorial generalized Kähler pair

3.3.
Relative pseudo-effectivity of line bundles in projective families

Using a recent result of
[
36
]
, we prove that the global stability of pseudo-effectivity of a line bundle over a projective family with irreducible and reduced fibers, if this line bundle is pseudo-effective on a Zariski dense set of fibers.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
f:X\to S
be a projective flat morphism of complex analytic spaces with reduced and irreducible fibers. If
L
is a line bundle on
X
whose restriction to a Zariski dense set of fibers is pseudo-effective, then
L
is
f
-pseudo-effective over
S
, and moreover
L|_{X_{s}}
is pseudo-effective for every
s\in S
.

Proof.

Let
A
be a relatively ample line bundle on
X
. Assume that there exists a dense subset
B\subset S
such that
L|_{X_{s}}
is pseudo-effective for any
s\in B
. Then, for any
\varepsilon>0
,

\mathrm{vol}\big((L+\varepsilon A)|_{X_{s}}\big)\geq\varepsilon^{n}v\quad\text{for all }s\in B,

where
n=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(X/S)
and
v=(A|_{X_{s}})^{n}
which is independent of
s\in S
.
By Lemma
3.7
, the family
f:(X,L,A)\to S
is the pull back of some projective family
(X^{\prime},L^{\prime},A^{\prime})\to S^{\prime}
via a dominant morphism
\phi:S\to S^{\prime}
such that
S^{\prime}
is a projective scheme. Let
\eta
be the generic point of
S^{\prime}
and denote the generic fiber of
X^{\prime}\to S^{\prime}
by
X^{\prime}_{\eta}
. Since
\phi:S\to S^{\prime}
is a dominant morphism, the image
B^{\prime}=\phi(B)
is dense in
S^{\prime}
. By
[
36
, Theorem 1.1]
,

\mathrm{vol}\big((L^{\prime}+\varepsilon A^{\prime})|_{X^{\prime}_{\eta}}\big)=\inf_{s\in B^{\prime}}\mathrm{vol}\big((L^{\prime}+\varepsilon A^{\prime})|_{X^{\prime}_{s}}\big)\geq\varepsilon^{n}v>0.

In particular, for every
\varepsilon>0
, the divisor
L^{\prime}+\varepsilon A^{\prime}
is big on the generic fiber. It follows that
L^{\prime}
is relatively pseudo-effective over
S^{\prime}
, and hence
L
is relatively pseudo-effective over
S
.

For any
\varepsilon>0
, the line bundle
L+\varepsilon A
is relatively big over
S
. That is, there exists a dense Zariski open subset
U_{\varepsilon}\subseteq S
such that
(L+\varepsilon A)|_{X_{s}}
is big for every
s\in U_{\varepsilon}
. By
[
57
, Proposition 4.1]
, the restrictions
(L+\varepsilon A)|_{X_{s}}
are big for all
s\in S
. Since
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrary, it follows that
L|_{X_{s}}
is pseudo-effective for every
s\in S
.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

If
f:X\to S
is a projective flat morphism of complex analytic spaces with reduced and irreducible fibers, together with a line bundle

on

, then there exists a projective family

Since

, where

Let

be a sufficiently

-ample line bundle on

.
If

is sufficiently

-ample, relatively globally generated, and

In particular, a general section

cuts out a relatively effective divisor

Since

is flat, the fibers of

define a flat family

of projective varieties in

Note that the Hilbert polynomial

Clearly, the pair

via the morphism

∎

Corollary 3.13
.

Let

be a flat proper morphism from a normal complex analytic variety onto the unit disk in

(c)

(a)\Rightarrow(b)
. Since Grauert’s base change theorem holds over some Zariski open subset

,

,

(c)\Rightarrow(d)

(d)\Rightarrow(a)

(Extension of MMP to nearby fibers,

)

Lemma 4.25
.

Let
X
be a proper Moishezon variety with rational singularities, with
L
a holomorphic
\mathbb{R}
-line bundle on
X
, and a class
\alpha\in H^{1,1}_{\rm{BC}}(X,\mathbb{R})
. If
L\equiv 0
(resp.
\alpha\equiv 0
), then
c_{1}(L)=0\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{R})
(resp.
\alpha=0\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{R})
) .

Proof.

First we treat the case of a line bundle. Since
X
is Moishezon, there exists a bimeromorphic modification
\mu:X^{p}\to X
with
X^{p}
a projective manifold. The pull-back line bundle
\mu^{*}L
remains numerically trivial, i.e.,
\mu^{*}L\equiv 0
. By Poincaré duality and Hodge–Riemann bilinear relationship the pairing

{\langle-,-\rangle}:H^{1,1}(X^{p},\mathbb{R})\times H^{n-1,n-1}(X^{p},\mathbb{R})\to H^{2n}(X^{p},\mathbb{R})\cong\mathbb{R},\quad(\alpha,\beta)\mapsto\int_{X^{p}}\alpha\cup\beta

is a perfect pairing. Since
\mu^{*}L\equiv 0
, we have

\mu^{*}L\cdot C={\langle c_{1}(\mu^{*}L),[C]\rangle}=0

for any irreducible curve
C
, where
[C]\in H^{n-1,n-1}(X^{p},\mathbb{R})
represents its fundamental class. By the Lefschetz (1,1)-theorem,
H^{n-1,n-1}(X^{p},\mathbb{R})
is generated by these classes
[C]
, therefore using the perfect pairing condition, we have
\mu^{*}c_{1}(L)=c_{1}(\mu^{*}L)=0
. Since
X
has rational singularities, the pullback map

\mu^{*}:H^{2}(X,\mathbb{R})\to H^{2}(X^{p},\mathbb{R})

is injective by
[
19
, Lemma 2.6]
. Hence
c_{1}(L)=0\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{R})
as well.

Next we handle the case of a Bott–Chern class
\alpha\in H^{1,1}_{\mathrm{BC}}(X,\mathbb{R})
. Since
\mu^{*}\alpha\in H^{1,1}_{\mathrm{BC}}(X^{p},\mathbb{R})
, the Lefschetz
(1,1)
-theorem implies that
\mu^{*}\alpha
is the first Chern class of some
\mathbb{R}
-divisor on
X^{p}
. Hence the same argument as above applies, and the conclusion follows.
∎

5.
Pseudo-effectivity of canonical divisors under deformations

The goal of this section is to study the global stability of the pseudo-effectivity of canonical divisors in Kähler families, under the assumption that the central fiber is projective with canonical singularities. Our first result involves the deformation closedness of pseudo-effectivity of adjoint classes.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
f:X\to S
be a family from a normal
\mathbb{Q}

6.
Invariance of the volumes of adjoint classes for Kähler families

In the final section, we study the deformation behavior of volume and plurigenera in Kähler families.

6.1.
Invariance of volume when central fiber has big adjoint class

We first prove one of the main results of the paper: the deformation invariance of the volume when the central fiber is projective and the adjoint class is big.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
f:X\to S
be a smooth family from a Kähler manifold
X
onto a smooth, connected, and relatively compact curve
S

4.19

. Pick an arbitrary point

And therefore

Remark 6.4
.

In
[

, de Fernex–Hacon showed that the deformation invariance of log plurigenera does not hold when the family is not log smooth. Consider the family of smooth quadric degenerating to the Hirzebruch surface
\mathbb{F}_{2}
. A line in one of the two rulings on the general fiber
X_{t}
sweeps out under deformation over
T
, a divisor
R
on
X
with the following properties

(a)
\left.R\right|_{X_{0}}=E+F
, where

is a fiber of the projection
\mathbb{F}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
, and
E
is the tautological section on
\mathbb{F}_{2}
whose intersection number is
E^{2}=-2
.

(b)
R|_{X_{t}}=F_{t}
for
t\neq 0
, where
F_{t}
is one of the ruling of the quadrics.

For any
b\geq 1
, consider a divisor
L
on
X
such that
\left.L\right|_{X_{0}}\sim(b+1)E+2bF
and on the general fiber
L|_{X_{t}}=(b+1)F_{0}+(b-1)F_{1}
(where
F_{0}
and
F_{1}
are the rulings of the quadrics). Moreover, if we define
D:=\frac{1}{m}(H+2R)
, for a general

with

, then

L\sim m\left(K_{X}+D\right).

The pair
(X,D)
is klt but not log smooth since the divisor

splits on the central fiber. In this case, the log plurigenera are not constant since on the general fiber

P_{m}(K_{X_{t}}+D_{t})=h^{0}\left(\mathcal{O}_{X_{t}}(L)\right)=h^{0}\left(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}}(b+1,b-1)\right)=b^{2}+2b,

while on the central fiber
|L|_{X_{0}}|=|b(E+2F)+E|=|b(E+2F)\mid+E
and therefore

P_{m}(K_{X_{0}}+D_{0})=h^{0}\left(\mathcal{O}_{X_{0}}(L)\right)=\binom{b+3}{3}-\binom{b+1}{3}=b^{2}+2b+1.

The plurigenera jump on the central fiber, however the volume is constant in this family.

6.2.
Invariance of volume for families of Kähler threefolds

In this subsection, we prove the deformation invariance of the plurigenera and the volume of an adjoint class for smooth families of Kähler threefolds.

Theorem 6.5
.

Let
f:X\to S
be a smooth family of compact Kähler threefolds over a smooth, connected, and relatively compact curve
S
. Then for any integer
m\geq 1
, the
m
-genus
P_{m}(X_{t})
is constant for any
t\in S
. Furthermore, assume that there exists a Kähler class
\omega_{X}
on
X
such that
\omega_{X_{t}}:=\omega_{X}|_{X_{t}}
. Then for every
\delta\geq 0
, the function

t\longmapsto\operatorname{vol}(K_{X_{t}}+\delta\,\omega_{X_{t}})

is constant for any
t\in S
.

Proof.

We first consider the deformation invariance of plurigenera. Using the similar extension technique (cf. Proposition
4.19
),
[
16
, Theorem 1.2]
proved the existence of relative MMP for smooth family of Kähler threefolds. Then, the invariance of plurigenera follows from
[
52
, Theorem 11]
, as the abundance conjecture is completely known for Kähler threefolds by
[
9
,
10
,
21
,
22
]
.

We next consider the deformation invariance of the volume. It is easy to see that the case
\delta=0
follows from the deformation invariance of plurigenera established above. Fix a point
t_{0}\in S
, and let
\tau:=\inf\{t\geq 0\mid K_{X_{t_{0}}}+t\,\omega_{X_{t_{0}}}\text{ is pseudo-effective}\}
. We now assume that
\delta>0
and divide the argument into three cases according to the relation between
\delta
and
\tau
.

Case 1. If
\delta>\tau
, then
K_{X_{t_{0}}}+\delta{\omega}_{X_{t_{0}}}
is big. Following a similar line of argument as in Theorem
6.1
, and using
[
18
, Theorem 1.4]
, we can run the generalized
(K_{X_{t_{0}}}+\delta{\omega}_{X_{t_{0}}})
-MMP with scaling of
\omega_{X_{t_{0}}}
on the central fiber, and it will terminate at some minimal model

(X_{t_{0}},\delta\bm{\omega}_{X_{t_{0}}})\dashrightarrow(X_{t_{0}}^{(1)},\delta\bm{\omega}_{X_{t_{0}}^{(1)}})\dashrightarrow\cdots\dashrightarrow(X_{t_{0}}^{(n)},\delta\bm{\omega}_{X_{t_{0}}^{(n)}})\dashrightarrow(X_{t_{0}}^{\prime},\delta\bm{\omega}_{X_{t_{0}}^{\prime}}).

By Remark
4.20
of Proposition
4.19
, after shrinking the base to some (connected) open neighborhood
U\subset S
of
{t_{0}}
, we can extend the steps of the MMP on
X_{t_{0}}
to a sequence of
(K_{X}+\delta{\omega}_{X})
-negative proper bimeromorphic contractions over
U
,

(X,\delta\bm{\omega}_{X})/U\dashrightarrow(X^{(1)},\delta\bm{\omega}_{X^{(1)}})/U\dashrightarrow\cdots\dashrightarrow(X^{\prime},\delta\bm{\omega}_{X^{\prime}})/U\dashrightarrow\cdots.

Since
K_{X_{t_{0}}^{\prime}}+\delta{\omega}_{X_{t_{0}}^{\prime}}
is big and nef, Remark
4.29
implies that after shrinking the base
K_{X^{\prime}}+\delta{\omega}_{X^{\prime}}
is relatively nef over
U
. Then, by the singular Stokes’ formula in Proposition
6.2
, the volume function

t\longmapsto\operatorname{vol}\!\left(K_{X_{t}}+\delta{\omega}_{X_{t}}\right)=(K_{X_{t}^{\prime}}+\delta{\omega}_{X_{t}^{\prime}})^{3}

is constant for
t\in U
.

Case 2. If
0<\delta<\tau
, then
K_{X_{t_{0}}}+\delta\omega_{X_{t_{0}}}
is not pseudo-effective. We can run the generalized
(K_{X_{t_{0}}}+\delta{\omega}_{X_{t_{0}}})
-MMP with scaling of
\omega_{X_{t_{0}}}
on the central fiber, and it will terminate at some Mori fiber space
X_{t_{0}}^{\prime}\to Z_{t_{0}}^{\prime}
(with
-(K_{X_{t_{0}}^{\prime}}+\delta\omega_{X^{\prime}_{t_{0}}})
relative Kähler over
Z_{t_{0}}^{\prime}
). The same as in the Case 1, after shrinking the base to some (connected) open neighborhood
U\subset S
of
{t_{0}}
, we can extend the steps of the MMP on
X_{t_{0}}
to a sequence of
(K_{X}+\delta{\omega}_{X})
-negative proper bimeromorphic contractions over
U
,

(X,\delta\bm{\omega}_{X})/U\dashrightarrow(X^{(1)},\delta\bm{\omega}_{X^{(1)}})/U\dashrightarrow\cdots\dashrightarrow(X^{\prime},\delta\bm{\omega}_{X^{\prime}})/U\dashrightarrow\cdots.

Then, by Proposition
4.11
, after shrinking
U
again, one can extend the Mori fiber space
X^{\prime}_{t_{0}}\to Z^{\prime}_{t_{0}}
to a relative Mori fiber space
X^{\prime}\to Z^{\prime}/U
over
U
. It follows that
K_{X_{t}}+\delta\omega_{X_{t}}
is not pseudo-effective for
t\in U
. Therefore

\mathrm{vol}(K_{X_{t}}+\delta\omega_{X_{t}})=\mathrm{vol}(K_{X_{t_{0}}}+\delta\omega_{X_{t_{0}}})=0,

for
t\in U
.

Case 3. Suppose that
0<\delta=\tau
. Then
K_{X_{t_{0}}}
is not pseudo-effective and thus, we can run a
K_{X_{t_{0}}}
-MMP with scaling of
\omega_{X_{t_{0}}}
on
X_{t_{0}}
, which terminates with a
(K_{X_{t_{0}}^{\prime}}+\tau\omega_{X_{t_{0}}^{\prime}})
-trivial Mori fiber space
X_{t_{0}}^{\prime}\to Z_{t_{0}}^{\prime}
(with
-K_{X_{t_{0}}^{\prime}}
relative ample over
Z_{t_{0}}^{\prime}
). By Proposition
4.11
, this Mori fiber space on
X_{t_{0}}^{\prime}
extends to a relative Mori fiber space
X^{\prime}\to Z^{\prime}/U
over some neighborhood
U
of
t_{0}
. Since this Mori fiber space is projective, by Lemma
4.26
, the induced Mori fiber spaces on the nearby fibers are
(K_{X_{t}^{\prime}}+\tau\omega_{X_{t}^{\prime}})
-trivial (and
K_{X_{t}^{\prime}}
-negative). By Lemma
2.47
, it follows that the pseudo-effective threshold is locally constant and equal to
\tau
in a neighborhood of
t_{0}
. Consequently, the volume function
t\longmapsto\mathrm{vol}(K_{X_{t}}+\tau\omega_{t})
is equal to
0
in a neighborhood of
t_{0}
and locally constant.

In all three cases, the volume function
t\longmapsto\mathrm{vol}(K_{X_{t}}+\delta{\omega}_{X_{t}})
is locally constant around any point
t_{0}\in S
. Since
S
is connected, the volume function is constant for any
t\in S
.
∎

From the above results, it is natural to propose:

Conjecture 6.6
.

Let
f:X\to S
be a smooth family from a Kähler manifold
X

S
. Assume that
(X,B+\bm{\beta})
is a generalized klt pair such that
(X,B)
is log smooth over
S
and
\bm{\beta}=\overline{\beta}
where
\beta
is nef over
S
. Then the volume function

t\longmapsto{\rm vol}(K_{X_{t}}+B_{t}+\bm{\beta}_{X_{t}})

is constant over
S
.

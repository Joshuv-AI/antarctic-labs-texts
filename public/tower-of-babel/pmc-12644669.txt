Predicting thickness distribution of thermoplastic dental aligners via KBKZ-based finite element analysis
Authors: Lianghong Luo, Huahui Zhang, Xiao Li, Yun Zou
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study aims to clarify the thickness distribution and its dynamic evolution during the thermoforming of PET thermoplastic dental film for orthodontic appliances using a numerical simulation system based on the KBKZ integral constitutive model. It establishes a mapping relationship between initial and formed thickness, offering a method to optimize membrane thickness and process parameters at various stages of orthodontic treatment. A nonlinear viscoelastic finite element analysis framework was constructed using the KBKZ integral constitutive model. PET thermoplastic dental film with initial thicknesses of 0.6 mm, 0.8 mm, and 1.0 mm were studied. Thermoforming simulations were conducted using the ASNSY-Polyflow module. Physical membrane thermoforming experiments were also performed. Thickness distribution data of the formed samples were measured with a thickness micrometer. The analysis focused on thickness distribution characteristics in the lingual, buccal, and occlusal functional regions. The numerical simulations and experimental measurements showed significant consistency. The thickness distribution curves exhibited an average Pearson coefficient () and Spearman coefficient (); the thickness change rate curves showed and (). The formed appliances exhibited typical regional heterogeneity. The thickness attenuation rate () in the lingual and buccal regions was , significantly higher than that in the occlusal region (). This study shows that the finite element method using the KBKZ integral model can accurately predict the thickness distribution and its changing law of invisible braces during the thermoforming process. In terms of thickness prediction, the accuracy of the error with actual production is successfully achieved to be less than 7%. In future orthodontic plans, combined with the forward-looking "dynamic thickness compensation" strategy concept of this study, the stiffness of the appliance can be controlled by designing the initial diaphragm thickness. This offers a parametric solution for clinical scenarios needing high orthodontic forces, such as molar intrusion, tooth rotation, and sequential treatments. Introduction

Invisible orthodontic technology has become the preferred choice for modern orthodontic treatment due to its exceptional comfort and aesthetic characteristics. It not only provides patients with a more comfortable and convenient treatment experience but is also nearly undetectable in appearance, greatly meeting the modern demand for aesthetics and privacy1,2. With continuous technological advancements, the indications for invisible orthodontic appliances are also expanding, allowing an increasing number of patients with malocclusion to achieve ideal tooth alignment through this non-bonded, fixed-bracket-free approach. Compared to traditional fixed-bracket orthodontic appliances, invisible orthodontic technology primarily utilizes a series of customized transparent aligners that closely adhere to the tooth surface. By regularly replacing aligners at different stages, teeth are gradually guided to their predetermined positions, thereby achieving the correction goals3.

According to the in-depth study and analysis of mechanical properties of transparent orthodontic appliances by Casavola4, Partouche5 and other scholars, it can be clearly concluded that the thickness of orthodontic appliances has a significant impact on the transmission efficiency of orthodontic forces, and is closely related to the precision of tooth movement, the adaptability of periodontal tissues and the comfort of patients.Currently, the production of transparent aligners primarily depends on the thermoforming technology of dental thermoplastic sheets6, specifically polyethylene terephthalate (PET)7. However, in actual production processes, factors such as material properties, heating temperature, heating time, dental mold, and production environment can all influence the actual formed thickness of the aligners. This may lead to uncontrollable mechanical properties in critical functional areas (occlusal surface, buccal and lingual sides), making it difficult to ensure biomechanical performance in specific regions or specific treatment sequences8.

At present, in the current thermoplastic molding production process, the thickness of the molding membrane for sequential orthodontic appliances is uniform (for example, the membrane is 0.75 mm from the beginning to the end of the plan), and it is impossible to make personalized adjustments to meet the needs of different treatment stages, resulting in unstable mechanical properties and difficulty in accurately controlling the distribution of orthodontic forces. Therefore, it is very important to explore the "dynamic thickness compensation" process strategy based on individual differences of patients and the needs of the treatment stage (Fig. 1).

"Dynamic thickness compensation" process strategy.

At the same time, according to the research of Delgado9 and KrishnaKailash6 et al., numerical simulation studies on the forming process of thermoplastic dental film are still in their infancy, particularly lacking a three-dimensional dynamic simulation system based on true constitutive relationships.As a result, the mapping relationship between the thickness field of the aligners and the orthodontic force field has not yet been established. This disconnect between theory and practice prevents clinicians from precisely regulating the mechanical properties of the aligners based on the type of malocclusion (e.g., molar intrusion, dental rotation, etc.), severely limiting the implementation of personalized orthodontic solutions10.

To address the aforementioned issues, this study proposes a thermoforming numerical simulation method based on the Kaye-Bernstein-Kearsley-Zapas (KBKZ)11 integral constitutive model. This method systematically elucidates the thickness distribution patterns and dynamic evolution mechanisms of PET thermoplastic dental film during the thermoforming process, establishing a mapping relationship between initial thickness and formed thickness. By providing a parametric process solution for clinical scenarios requiring high orthodontic force output, this approach significantly enhances the biomechanical controllability of the clear aligner system.

Finite element modeling of thermoforming

Optimization of intraoral scan models

Fine mesh models can clearly display local features but increase computational resource consumption. The computation time is positively correlated with the number of mesh elements. Therefore, it is necessary to set appropriate mesh parameters for intraoral scan models.Potamias research group19 conducted in-depth research on the problem of triangular mesh simplification at the point cloud level. Based on this, this study adopted the most widely used multi-resolution triangular mesh simplification technology (Fig. 2). In this mesh simplification algorithm, the triangle collapse algorithm is computationally fast and stable, preserves the topological structure of the original data, and results in minimal geometric error, making it a commonly used mesh simplification method19.

Mesh simplification based on triangle collapse algorithm.

As shown in Fig. 3, the triangle collapse algorithm was employed to simplify the mesh of the intraoral dental model. In the smooth regions of the model, the curvature morphology of the detailed features was properly preserved, ensuring that the overall structure of the model did not undergo significant deformation during the triangle simplification process. This method effectively preserves the original geometric features of the model, thereby ensuring its accuracy and integrity20.

Establishment of the finite element model

According to the thermoforming equipment (Fig. 5-A), this study performed reasonable equivalent treatment on its key components to construct the corresponding finite element model. Since the plane model can be used for numerical calculation in Polyflow (ANSYS Workbench2021R2) software, in order to reduce the amount of calculation, the thermoplastic dental film clamping device21 is simplified to the membrane model shown in Fig. 5(B1); the dental mold model is abstracted to the dental mold model shown in Fig. 5(B2); and the base platform is simplified to the basic model shown in Fig. 5(B3).

Equivalent finite element model of the thermoforming device components.

To further improve the efficiency and accuracy of the finite element analysis, this study employed Boolean operations to integrate the tooth mold model in Fig. 5(B2) with the base model in Fig. 5(B3), resulting in the composite model shown in Fig. 5(C4). This composite model will be used as a whole for subsequent finite element analysis. Import the above model in STL format into the ANSYS Workbench2021R2—DesignModeler (DM) module. In DM, convert the object into a recognizable finite element mesh model (Fig. 5-D), in which the tooth model retains its triangular face mesh, and the base area and the circular dental gelatin model are integrated into a unified whole. In the Polyflow software, quadrilateral mesh division will be performed on the merged whole area.Finally, this model ensures computational accuracy while significantly improving computational efficiency, laying a solid foundation for the subsequent simulation of the thermoforming process.

Boundary conditions setting

In the finite element numerical simulation of this study, the boundary conditions for the thermoforming process were set based on the Polyflow software (ANSYS Workbench 2021R2). To simplify the calculations and ensure simulation stability, the principle of relative motion was adopted. The circular boundary of the membrane model was set as a fixed boundary condition (Fixed Boundary) to restrict its displacement degrees of freedom. Meanwhile, a uniform pressure parameter (Pressure Boundary) was pre-set on the membrane surface (Fig. 6) to simulate the adsorption behavior during the actual forming process. Subsequently, the integrated tooth mold and base composite model was translated vertically to make contact with the membrane surface. When the circular boundary of the composite model base fully contacted the membrane, the pre-set pressure boundary condition was triggered, initiating the adsorption operation to achieve tight adhesion between the membrane and the model surface, as well as precise simulation of the forming process. This boundary condition setting not only effectively reduced computational complexity but also ensured the physical authenticity and numerical stability of the forming process.Additionally, the mesh adaptive function was enabled throughout the entire analysis process to dynamically adjust the mesh density in the contact region, thereby accommodating complex deformations.

Experimental data analysis method

To ensure the surface integrity of the thermoplastic dental film during processing and facilitate thermoforming, manufacturers typically cover its upper and lower surfaces with white or transparent protective films (as shown in Fig. 8A). However, due to variations in manufacturing processes among different producers, the thickness of the thermoplastic dental film exhibits certain fluctuations and is not a fixed value. To accurately measure the thickness of the film after removing the protective layer, this study employs a digital thickness micrometer (as shown in Fig. 8C). During actual measurements (as shown in Fig. 8B), due to the length limitation of the micrometer probe, the focus is on taking multiple measurements in the yellow-point regions and using the average value as the film thickness to ensure data accuracy.

Experimental sample preparation

Dental thermoplastic films with thicknesses of 0.6 mm, 0.8 mm, and 1.0 mm were selected as experimental materials. Through the vacuum thermoforming process, the films were formed with 3D-printed dental models. After forming, precision cutting and polishing techniques were used to remove excess edge materials, ultimately obtaining invisible orthodontic appliances that closely fit the dental models (Fig. 11).

Actual moulding result of thermoplastic dental film sheets.

As shown in Fig. 11A, the finished appliances of three different thicknesses achieved good adhesion to the dental models, demonstrating the consistency of the thermoforming experimental sample preparation. Figure 11B further shows the state of the appliances after separation from the dental models. No significant deformation or damage was observed during the separation process, indicating that the appliances maintained good structural integrity and dimensional stability after separation. This result provides a solid foundation for subsequent quantitative measurement and analysis of thickness distribution.

Discussion and conclusions

Discussion

Polyflow is an advanced computational fluid dynamics (CFD) software designed for simulating and analyzing the thermoplastic molding behavior of polymers during processing27. The software integrates a variety of viscoelastic-plastic mathematical constitutive models, including the KBKZ integral model required for this study, so that these mathematical models can be directly and efficiently applied to related research. In this study, we used the ANSYS-Polyflow dedicated analysis module to perform finite element simulation to simulate the thermoplastic molding process of thermoplastic gutta-percha. By comparing and analyzing the actual experimental data, it is found that the simulation results are highly consistent with the experimental data, and the thickness attenuation rate error is controlled within 7%. It can simulate the actual situation more accurately and reliably describe the spatial distribution characteristics of thickness attenuation. This verifies that the simulation of thermoplastic dental film based on the KBKZ integral model in the Polyflow software has high accuracy and reliability. From the current research, the thickness distribution of the two experiments is basically the same. If we can add relevant variable coefficients in future research, the finite element analysis will be closer to the experimental results; at the same time, in future in-depth research, we will add considerations of biomechanical properties.

In combination with the above research, the team innovatively proposed a forward-looking "dynamic thickness compensation" orthodontic appliance production process strategy. The core of this strategy is to break through the single thickness limitation that is prevalent in the current bracketless invisible orthodontic technology (such as using 0.75 mm or 0.8 mm thick thermoplastic dental film for hot pressing throughout the process). This strategy can seamlessly connect the two advanced manufacturing paths (Fig. 19) to achieve the design and manufacture of personalized/optimized thickness orthodontic appliances.

Prospective "dynamic thickness compensation" orthodontic appliance production process strategy.

The existing model significantly restricts the optimization potential and clinical efficacy of the biomechanical performance of the appliance, especially in complex clinical scenarios that require high-precision correction force control (such as molar depression, tooth rotation, sequential movement, etc.). In the future, through further in-depth research on the thickness prediction algorithm, by simulating the thickness analysis of each stage of continuous orthodontic treatment, the initial film thickness design is optimized, the biomechanical controllability of the transparent appliance is significantly enhanced, and the precise control of the spatial distribution of the appliance stiffness is achieved, providing a parametric solution for clinical scenarios that require high correction force, such as molar depression, tooth rotation, and sequential orthodontics.

Of course, with the emergence of 3D printed braces materials28,29, 3D printing technology can make the thickness of braces more uniform at all angles. At the same time, orthodontic design software supports the free adjustment of thickness parameters within the range of 0.05 mm, which enables the "dynamic thickness compensation" technology to be seamlessly connected with 3D printing orthodontic technology. In the continuous orthodontic treatment plan, the thickness of the braces can be flexibly adjusted according to the needs of different stages: for steps that require greater correction force (such as molar depression or tooth rotation), the thickness can be increased to enhance biomechanical properties; for steps with smaller movement ranges, the thickness can be reduced to improve patient comfort. This dynamic adjustment capability not only optimizes the treatment effect, but also significantly improves treatment efficiency and patient experience.

Furthermore, the finite element analysis (FEA) method based on the KBKZ integral model provides a reliable theoretical tool for validating material performance metrics. This method not only accurately predicts the thickness distribution and mechanical properties of orthodontic appliances but also lays a theoretical foundation for developing new appliance materials with optimized biomechanical performance. In the future, combining 3D printing technology with numerical simulation methods is expected to further advance the personalized design and manufacturing of invisible orthodontic appliances, providing more efficient and precise solutions for orthodontic treatment.

Conclusions

This study systematically elucidates the thickness distribution patterns and dynamic evolution mechanisms of dental thermoplastic appliances (PET films) during the thermoforming process by constructing a numerical simulation system based on the KBKZ integral constitutive model. Focusing on PET thermoplastic dental film with initial thicknesses of 0.6 mm, 0.8 mm, and 1.0 mm, the research combines finite element analysis (Polyflow module) with physical thermoforming experiments to quantify the thickness attenuation characteristics in the lingual, buccal, and occlusal functional regions of the formed appliances. This method accurately simulates the thermoforming process of orthodontic appliances and can be used to predict the thickness values of real dental appliance membranes. It provides a solid numerical analysis methodology and theoretical foundation for the optimization design and clinical application of the "dynamic thickness compensation" process strategy.

General Symmetric Ideals
Alexandra Seceleanu, Liana Şega

Abstract
. We investigate the structure and properties of symmetric ideals generated by general forms in the polynomial ring under the natural action of the symmetric group. This work significantly broadens the framework established in our earlier collaboration with Harada on principal symmetric ideals [ 15 ] . A novel aspect of our approach is the construction of a bijective parametrization of general symmetric ideals using Macaulay-Matlis duality, which is asymptotically independent of the number of variables of the ambient ring. We establish that general symmetric ideals exhibit extremal behavior in terms of Hilbert functions and Betti numbers, and satisfy the Weak Lefschetz Property. We also demonstrate explicit asymptotic stability in their algebraic and homological invariants under increasing numbers of variables, showing that such ideals form well-behaved 𝔖 ∞ \mathfrak{S}_{\infty} –invariant chains.

Introduction

Let
{\sf{k}}
be a field and let
R={\sf{k}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
denote the polynomial ring in
n

Let

be a homogeneous ideal in

of degrees

, respectively. A theme of investigation with a few decades of tradition in commutative algebra concerns the properties of the cyclic module
R/I
as the polynomials

are chosen to be as independent as possible. For example, if

this means that

form a regular sequence and thus

is a complete intersection, while for

the ring
R/I
is a finite dimensional
{\sf{k}}
-vector space, thus an artinian
{\sf{k}}
-algebra.

It was shown by Fröberg and Löfwall in
[
12

that, for fixed values of

R/I
. Moreover, there is a Zariski open subset in the space of coefficients of the
f_{i}

R/I
is the same and it is in fact the minimal series among all possible Hilbert series of quotients with the same parameters. Famously, a longstanding conjecture by Fröberg describes this minimal Hilbert series
[
10
]
and is related to Lefschetz properties of artinian rings. One calls algebras with this Hilbert series
general
.
In complete generality Fröberg’s conjecture has proven elusive, however the following works make partial progress:
[
10
]
(

),
[
2
]
(

=3),
[
34
]
(

),
[

]
(infinitely many cases with

There is an additional conjecture due to Migliore and Miró-Roig

regarding the betti numbers of general algebras

[
24
]
.

Another method to specify an ideal
I
defining an artinian quotient

is by means of its Macaulay inverse system
I^{-1}
(see (
2.1
)). Macaulay-Matlis duality relates the
R
-modules
R/I

to each other. Iarrobino defines compressed algebras as a class of graded algebras parametrized by their Macaulay inverse systems that exhibit extremal behavior in their Hilbert function. Specifically, compressed algebras have the maximal Hilbert function possible given the dimension of their socle and the embedding dimension. These rings are studied in
[
18

17
,
11

14

In

Boij studies the betti numbers of compressed level algebras giving lower bounds for these numbers and finding that these lower bounds are attained by compressed algebras of large enough socle degree. This leaves open several questions which are still being investigated. In
[
33

Rossi and Şega study resolutions of modules over compressed Gorenstein local rings showing that all such modules have rational Poincaré series with a common denominator.

Inspired by these well-known problems and results, our goal in this paper is to discuss analogous phenomena concerning graded algebras equipped with an action of the symmetric group. To construct such algebras, consider
the natural action of the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
on the polynomial ring

\sigma\cdot f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=f(x_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,x_{\sigma(n)})\quad\text{for}\quad\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}\,.

We are interested in homogeneous ideals which acquire an induced action from the action of

In particular, the graded betti numbers for (certain families of) monomial symmetric ideals are the focus of the recent works
[

]
,
[
25
]

[

The symmetric ideals discussed in this paper behave quite differently from most symmetric monomial ideals and therefore novel methods are introduced to study them in our work. We were inspired to study general symmetric ideals by

In this paper, we focus initially on homogeneous symmetric ideals having all minimal generators of the same degree, deducing later that this is not a restrictive assumption (see

\displaystyle\Phi:\Gr(r,R_{d})\to(r,d)\text{-symmetric ideals}

\displaystyle\Phi(V):=(V)_{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\,,\qquad\text{where }(V)_{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}=(\sigma\cdot f:\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n},f\in V).

Throughout,

2.1.
Duality and inverse systems

We give here an overview of Macaulay inverse systems. For details, consult
[
19
, Appendix A]
and
[
8
, Theorem 21.6]
. Let
S={\sf{k}}[y_{1},\dots,y_{n}]_{DP}
denote the divided powers algebra in
n

-1

{\sf{k}}
. We equip

with an
R
-module structure, called

, defined by extending the following action on monomials, by linearity in both arguments:

x_{1}^{d_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{d_{n}}\circ y_{1}^{(e_{1})}\cdots y_{n}^{(e_{n})}=\begin{cases}y_{1}^{(e_{1}-d_{1})}\cdots y_{n}^{(e_{n}-d_{n})}&\text{if }e_{i}-d_{i}\geq 0\text{ for all }i\\
0&\text{otherwise\,. }\end{cases}

It is well-known that
S=\operatorname{Hom}_{\sf{k}}(R,{\sf{k}})
is an injective hull of
{\sf{k}}
over
R
, when considered as an
R
-module. We refer to the basis elements

of

as monomials, noting that they are dual to the monomials

, and we refer to the elements of

as polynomials

in the (divided powers) variables

.

If
I
is a homogeneous ideal of
R
, then the
Macaulay inverse system
of
I
is the graded

-submodule of
S
given by

I^{-1}:={\operatorname{Ann}}_{S}(I)=\{g\in S:f\circ g=0\text{ for all }f\in I\}.

Since
{\operatorname{Ann}}_{S}(I)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(R/I,S)
, the inverse system

is isomorphic to the Matlis dual of

. It is thus a consequence of Matlis duality that

{\operatorname{Ann}}_{R}(I^{-1})=I\qquad\text{and}\qquad({\operatorname{Ann}}_{R}(W))^{-1}=W\,.

where

-submodule of

.

Let

be a finitely generated graded
R
-module. The

of

{\operatorname{\sf{HF}}}_{U}\colon\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{N}\,,\qquad\text{with}\qquad{\operatorname{\sf{HF}}}_{U}(i)=\dim_{\sf{k}}U_{i}\quad\text{for all $i\in\mathbb{Z}$}\,.

If
A=R/I
is artinian, the Hilbert function of
A
and of its socle
\operatorname{Soc}(A)={\operatorname{Ann}}_{A}({\mathfrak{m}})
can be read off the inverse system as follows:

(2.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\operatorname{\sf{HF}}}_{I^{-1}}(-i)=\dim_{\sf{k}}(I^{-1})_{-i}=\dim_{\sf{k}}A_{i}={\operatorname{\sf{HF}}}_{A}(i)\\
\dim_{\sf{k}}\left(\frac{I^{-1}}{{\mathfrak{m}}\circ I^{-1}}\right)_{-i}=\dim_{\sf{k}}\operatorname{Soc}(A)_{i}={\operatorname{\sf{HF}}}_{\operatorname{Soc}(A)}(i)\end{split}

The socle polynomial of an artinian algebra is the Hilbert series of the socle, namely it is the polynomial

\sum_{i=0}^{s(A)}{\operatorname{\sf{HF}}}_{\operatorname{Soc}(A)}(i)z^{i}\,.

where
s(A)
is the largest
i
with
\operatorname{Soc}(A)_{i}\neq 0
, called the
top socle degree
of
A
.

2.2.
Narrow and extremely narrow algebras

Let
A=R/I
be an artinian algebra quotient of
R
, with
I
a homogeneous ideal. Denote by
s(A)
the top socle degree of
A
and by
t(I)
be the initial degree of
I
, which is defined as the smallest degree of a minimal generator of
I
. Following
[
15
]
, we say that
A
is
narrow
if
t(I)\geq s(A)
, that is, the top socle degree of
A
is at most the initial degree of
I
.

Set
d=s(A)
. Let
F_{1},\dots,F_{a}\in(I^{-1})_{-d}
and define

L_{F_{1},\ldots,F_{a}}:=\left\{(\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\dots,\ell_{a})\in{R_{1}}^{a}\colon\sum_{i=1}^{a}\ell_{i}\circ F_{i}=0\right\}\,.

The
{\sf{k}}
-vector space
L_{F_{1},\ldots,F_{a}}
is independent, up to isomorphism, of the choice of the forms
F_{1},\dots,F_{a}
. When working with properties invariant under isomorphism (for example, when talking about the dimension of this space), we will also write
L_{W}
instead of
L_{F_{1},\ldots,F_{a}}
, where
W
is the vector space spanned by
F_{1},\dots,F_{a}
. Furthermore, when
W=(I^{-1})_{-d}
, we also write
L_{A}
instead of
L_{W}
.

Let
d\geq 1
. As in
[
15
]
, we say that
A
is
d
-
extremely narrow
if the following hold:

(i)

s(A)=t(I)=d
;

(ii)

There exists a basis
F_{1},\dots,F_{a}
of
(I^{-1})_{-d}
and
x\in R_{1}
such that

(2.2)

L_{F_{1},\ldots,F_{a}}\subseteq x(R_{0})^{a}.

The result below is a combination of part(s) of the statements of
[
15
, Lemma 4.2, Lemma 4.5, Lemma 4.7]
.

Proposition 2.1

(see
[
15
]
)
.

Let
d\geq 1
,
a\geq 1
, and
F_{1},\dots,F_{a}\in S_{-d}
linearly independent. If

I={\operatorname{Ann}}_{R}\left((F_{1}\dots,F_{a})+S_{\geqslant-(d-1)}\right),

then the algebra
A=R/I
is narrow with
s(A)=d
and
\dim_{\sf{k}}(I^{-1})_{-d}=a
, and the following hold:

(1)

The socle polynomial of
A
is
(\dim_{\sf{k}}R_{d-1}-an+\dim_{\sf{k}}L_{A})z^{d-1}+az^{d}
.

(2)

\dim_{\sf{k}}I_{d}=\dim_{\sf{k}}R_{d}-a

Furthermore, if there exists
x\in R_{1}
such that
L_{F_{1},\dots,F_{a}}\subseteq x(R_{0})^{a}
, then
A
is
d
-extremely narrow and, when
n\geq 3
,
I
is generated in degree
d
.

Remark 2.2
.

Assume the hypotheses of
2.1
. If
L_{F_{1},\dots,F_{a}}=0
, then the conclusion that
I
is generated in degree
d
, and hence
A
is
d
-extremely narrow, also holds when
n<3
. Indeed, since
A
is narrow, it follows from
[
15
, Lemma 4.2]
that
I
is generated in degrees
d
and
d+1
. Further, since
L_{F_{1},\dots,F_{a}}
is the module of linear syzygies on a basis of
(I^{-1})_{-d}
and
I^{-1}
is generated in degrees
-d
and
-d+1
, we see that
{\operatorname{Tor}}_{1}^{R}(I^{-1},{\sf{k}})_{-d+1}=0
, and hence
{\operatorname{Tor}}_{i}^{R}(I^{-1},{\sf{k}})_{-d+i}=0
for all
i\geq 1
. Then, as noted in the proof of
[
15
, Lemma 3.13]
it is a consequence of Matlis duality that

{\operatorname{Tor}}_{i}^{R}(A,{\sf{k}})_{j}\cong{\operatorname{Tor}}_{n-i}^{R}(I^{-1},{\sf{k}})_{n-j}

for all
i,j
, and hence

{\operatorname{Tor}}_{0}^{R}(I,{\sf{k}})_{d+1}\cong{\operatorname{Tor}}_{1}^{R}(A,{\sf{k}})_{d+1}\cong{\operatorname{Tor}}_{n-1}^{R}(I^{-1},{\sf{k}})_{n-1-d}=0\,.

Thus
I
is generated in degree
d
.

2.3.
Weak Lefschetz Property

The algebra
A
is said to have the
Weak Lefschetz Property
(abbreviated WLP) if the multiplication
A_{i-1}\xrightarrow{\ell}A_{i}
by a general linear form
\ell
has maximal rank for all
i
. More precisely, if we parametrize the elements of
R_{1}
by a projective space
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
using their coefficients, then
A
has WLP if there exists a nonempty open subset
U
of
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
such that
A_{i-1}\xrightarrow{\ell}A_{i}
for all
i
and all
\ell
parametrized by
U
. In general, neither homological invariants of artinian rings such as their betti numbers nor information on their Hilbert functions can be used to determine whether an algebra satisfies the WLP; see
[
36
, Example 3.4]
or
[
1
]
. However, in the case of
d
-extremely narrow algebras we are able to prove the WLP, and we can also describe the non-Weak Lefshetz locus.

Proposition 2.3
.

If
A=R/I
is
d
-extremely narrow, then
A
has the Weak Lefschetz Property. More precisely, if
\ell\in R_{1}
is not a multiple of
x
, where
x\in R_{1}
is as in (
2.2
), then the map
A_{i-1}\xrightarrow{\ell}A_{i}
has maximal rank for all
i
.

Proof.

By
2.1
and the definition of a
d
-extremely narrow algebra, we know that
I
is generated in degree
d
, and
A
has socle in degree
d
. Thus,

A_{i}=R_{i}
for
i<d
and
A_{i}=0
for
i>d
. In particular, the map
A_{i-1}\xrightarrow{\ell}A_{i}
given by multiplication by any linear form
\ell
is injective for
i<d
.
To prove the WLP, it suffices to show the map
A_{d-1}\xrightarrow{\ell}A_{d}
given by multiplication by a general linear form
\ell
is surjective. Since
I^{-1}
is the Matlis dual of
R/I
, this is equivalent to showing that the map
(I^{-1})_{-d}\xrightarrow{\ell}(I^{-1})_{-d+1}
given by
F\mapsto\ell\circ F
for
F\in(I^{-1})_{-d}
is injective.

Let
F_{1},\dots,F_{a}
denote a basis of
U^{\perp}
and
x\in R_{1}
satisfying property (
2.2
). Assume

\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}x_{i}\circ\sum_{j=1}^{a}a_{j}F_{j}=0

for
a_{j},c_{i}\in{\sf{k}}
, with
a_{j}\neq 0
for at least some
j
. We have then

\sum_{j=1}^{a}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}x_{i}a_{j}\right)\circ F_{j}=0\,.

It follows that
\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}x_{i}a_{j}\right)_{1\leq j\leq a}\in L_{F_{1},\dots,F_{a}}
, and hence
\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}x_{i}a_{j}
is a multiple of
x
for each
j
. Write
x=\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}x_{i}
with
b_{i}\in{\sf{k}}
. We have thus

\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}x_{i}a_{j}=d_{j}\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}x_{i}

for some
d_{j}\in{\sf{k}}
for all
j\in[a]
. Thus
c_{i}a_{j}=b_{i}d_{j}
for all
i\in[n]
and
j\in[a]
. If
j
is such that
a_{j}\neq 0
, we have then

c_{i}=(a_{j})^{-1}d_{j}b_{i}\quad\text{ for all $i\in[a]$}

and hence
\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}x_{i}
is a multiple of
x
. Consequently, the map
(I^{-1})_{-d}\xrightarrow{\ell}(I^{-1})_{-d+1}
is injective as long as
\ell
is not a multiple of
x
.
∎

3.
Orthogonality in rings of symmetric polynomials

In this section, we keep the notation established in Section 2. We define the rings of invariants
R^{\prime}
,
S^{\prime}
, explore choices of bases for the graded components of these rings, and define the map denoted
\perp
in
Eq.

1.4
.

3.1.
Partitions

A partition of a positive integer
d
is a tuple of integers
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{p})
such that
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{p}>0
and
|\lambda|=\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{p}=d
. We denote the fact that
\lambda
is a partition of
d
by
\lambda\vdash d
. For a partition
\lambda
we write
\#\lambda
for the number of parts, not to be confused with
|\lambda|
, the sum of the parts of
\lambda
.

If
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{p})\vdash d
and
p\leq n
, we set
x^{\lambda}=x_{1}^{\lambda_{1}}\cdots x_{p}^{\lambda_{p}}
.

If
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{p})\vdash d
, we say that a monomial
m
has
type

\lambda
if
m=x_{i_{1}}^{\lambda_{1}}\dots x_{i_{p}}^{\lambda_{p}}
for some integers
i_{1},\dots,i_{p}
, equivalently,
m=\sigma\cdot x^{\lambda}
for some
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
.

We utilize the
lexicographic order
on partitions defined as follows: for partitions
\lambda\vdash d
and
\mu\vdash d
we say that
\lambda>_{{\operatorname{Lex}}}\mu
if the leftmost nonzero entry of the vector
\lambda-\mu
is positive.

3.2.
Action of the permutation group

We denote by
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
the permutation group on
\{1,\dots,n\}
.
We consider the action of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
on
R
, respectively
S
, by

\displaystyle\sigma f(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})

\displaystyle=f(x_{\sigma(1)},\dots,x_{\sigma(n)})\qquad\text{for $f\in R$, $\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}$}

\displaystyle\sigma g(y_{1},\dots,y_{n})

\displaystyle=g(y_{\sigma(1)},\dots,y_{\sigma(n)})\qquad\text{for $g\in S$, $\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}$}

As discussed in
[
15
, Lemma 3.2, Appendix A]
, the action on
S
is naturally induced from the action on
R
, and it satisfies

(3.1)

\sigma\cdot(f\circ g)=(\sigma\cdot f)\circ(\sigma\cdot g)

for
f\in R
,
g\in S
and
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
.
We denote by
R^{\prime}
, respectively
S^{\prime}
, the subring of invariants of
R
, respectively
S^{\prime}
, under the action of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, namely

\displaystyle R^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\{f\in R\mid\sigma\cdot f=f\quad\text{for all $\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}$}\}

\displaystyle S^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\{g\in S\mid\sigma\cdot g=g\quad\text{for all $\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}$}\}

For each
d\geq 0
, we let
P_{n}(d)
denote
\dim_{{\sf{k}}}R^{\prime}_{d}
. Observe that
P_{n}(d)
is the number of partitions of
d
with at most
n
parts. In particular,
P_{n}(d)
does not depend on
n
when
n\geq d
. In this case, we write
P(d)
instead of
P_{n}(d)
.

When
\text{char}({\sf{k}})>n
or
\operatorname{char}{\sf{k}}=0
, the Reynolds operator is the algebra retract
\rho\colon R\to R^{\prime}
given by

\rho(f)=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\sigma\cdot f\qquad\text{for $f\in R$}.

With respect to the pairing between
R_{d}
and
S_{-d}
given by
\circ
, the monomials of degree
d
, respectively
-d
, form a pair of canonical dual bases for the two spaces. With respect to the pairing between
R^{\prime}_{d}
and
S^{\prime}_{-d}
the choice of dual bases seems less canonical. Given a monomial
\mu
of type
\lambda
, we set

(3.2)

\displaystyle M_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho(\mu)=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\sigma\cdot\mu

(3.3)

\displaystyle m_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\text{ the sum of all distinct monomials of type }\lambda.

Let
\lambda\vdash d
. For each
i\in[d]
let
p_{i}
denote the number of parts of
\lambda
of size
i
, and
p_{0}=n-\#\lambda
. Then, since the stabilizer under the action of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
of each term in
m_{\lambda}
has size
p_{0}!p_{1}!\cdots p_{d}!
, the symmetric functions above (considered in either
R
or
S
) are related by

(3.4)

m_{\lambda}=\frac{n!}{p_{0}!p_{1}!\cdots,p_{d}!}M_{\lambda}=\binom{n}{p_{0},p_{1},\ldots,p_{d}}M_{\lambda}.

Note that
M_{\lambda}
only depends on
\lambda
and, in particular, is independent of the choice of
\mu
.
We intentionally did not specify above the ring in which the monomials are considered, since we will use the same notation when working in both
R
and
S
, and the appropriate ring will be inferred from the context. For example, in
3.1
below one needs to take
M_{\lambda}\in R
and
m_{\nu}\in S
in order for the contraction operation to make sense. When working with monomials in
R
, note that
M_{\lambda},m_{\lambda}\in R^{\prime}
. When working with monomials in
S
, note that
M_{\lambda},m_{\lambda}\in S^{\prime}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Assume
\operatorname{char}{\sf{k}}>n
or
\operatorname{char}{\sf{k}}=0
. The sets of polynomials
\{m_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\vdash d}
and
\{M_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\vdash d}
are dual bases for
R^{\prime}_{d}
and
S^{\prime}_{-d}
with respect to the pairing
\circ
, that is

(3.5)

m_{\lambda}\circ M_{\nu}=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }\mu=\nu\\
0&\text{ if }\mu\neq\nu\,.\end{cases}

Proof.

Since
m_{\lambda}\circ m_{\lambda}
is equal to the number of terms in
m_{\lambda}
, which is given by
n!
divided by the size of the stabilizer of each term in
m_{\lambda}
, we have

m_{\lambda}\circ m_{\lambda}=\binom{n}{p_{0},p_{1},\ldots,p_{d}}.

By (
3.4
) we deduce that
M_{\lambda}\circ m_{\lambda}=1
. The remaining case is clear from the definition of the operation
\circ
.
∎

The following combinatorial lemma is crucial in the proof of
5.3
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\mu\vdash(d-1)
. Then

m_{\mu}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\right)=\sum_{\lambda\vdash d}c^{\lambda}_{\mu}m_{\lambda}

with
c_{\mu}^{\lambda}\in{\sf{k}}
and
c^{\lambda}_{\mu}\neq 0
if and only if
\lambda
can be obtained by adding a box to
\mu
and re-ordering. When
n\geq d
, the constants
c^{\lambda}_{\mu}
only depend on
\lambda
and
\mu
and not on
n
.

Proof.

Indeed, the integer
c^{\lambda}_{\mu}
counts the number of pairs
(\sigma(x^{\mu}),x_{i})
with
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
and
i\in[n]
so that
x^{\lambda}=\sigma(x^{\mu})\cdot x_{i}
. Since the monomial
x^{\lambda}
only involves at most the first
d
variables so do
\sigma(x^{\mu})
and
x_{i}
. Hence we may assume
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{d}
and thus the number
c^{\lambda}_{\mu}
does not depend on
n
. The last statement follows from the description of
c^{\lambda}_{\mu}
above. ∎

3.3.
Orthogonal subspaces

Eq.

1.4
uses a map
\perp\colon\Gr(r,R^{\prime}_{d})\to\Gr(P(d)-r,S^{\prime}_{-d})
, which we now define.

Notation 3.3
.

Let
d\geq 1
,
r\geq 0
and let
U\in\Gr(r,R^{\prime}_{d})
.
We define a subspace of
S^{\prime}_{-d}
associated to
U
by

U^{\perp}=\left\{w\in S^{\prime}_{-d}:u\circ w=0\text{ for all }u\in U\right\}.

Note that
\dim_{\sf{k}}U^{\perp}=P(d)-r
, and thus
U^{\perp}\in\Gr(P(d)-r,S^{\prime}_{-d})
.

Conversely, if
W\in\Gr(P(d)-r,S^{\prime}_{-d})
, we define

W^{\perp}=\{u\in R^{\prime}_{d}\colon u\circ w=0\text{ for all $w\in W$}\}.

Note that
\dim_{\sf{k}}W^{\perp}=r
, and thus
W^{\perp}\in\Gr(r,R^{\prime}_{d})
. Also, we have
(U^{\perp})^{\perp}=U
and
(W^{\perp})^{\perp}=W
.

This leads to the following double annihilator lemma which shows that taking
R
-annihilators of subspaces of
S^{\prime}
corresponds to taking preimages under the Reynolds operator
\rho
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Assume
\operatorname{char}{\sf{k}}>n
or
\operatorname{char}{\sf{k}}=0
. Using
3.3
and viewing
U^{\perp}
as a subspace of
S_{-d}
, we have

[{\operatorname{Ann}}_{R}(U^{\perp})]_{d}=\{f\in R_{d}\colon\rho(f)\in U\}.

Proof.

This follows from the observation that if
w\in(S^{\prime})_{-d}
then
f\circ w=\rho(f)\circ w
. To see this note that
w=\sum_{\lambda\vdash d}\theta_{\lambda}m_{\lambda}
for some
\theta_{\lambda}\in{\sf{k}}
since
\{m_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\vdash d}
is a basis for
S^{\prime}_{d}
and if
f=\sum_{i}c_{i}\mu_{i}
with
\mu_{i}
monomial of type
\nu_{i}
, then
\rho(f)=\sum_{i}c_{i}M_{\nu_{i}}
. Now we compare

\displaystyle f\circ w

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,\lambda}c_{i}\theta_{\lambda}\mu_{i}\circ m_{\lambda}=\sum_{\nu_{i}=\lambda}c_{i}\theta_{\lambda}

\displaystyle\rho(f)\circ w

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,\lambda}c_{i}\theta_{\lambda}M_{\nu_{i}}\circ m_{\lambda}=\sum_{\nu_{i}=\lambda}c_{i}\theta_{\lambda}

where we used (
3.5
) in the last equality. Therefore we have

\displaystyle f\in[{\operatorname{Ann}}_{R}(U^{\perp})]_{d}

\displaystyle\iff f\circ w=0\text{ for all }w\in U^{\perp}

\displaystyle\iff\rho(f)\circ w=0\text{ for all }w\in U^{\perp}

\displaystyle\iff\rho(f)\in(U^{\perp})^{\perp}=U.\qed

4.
Linear syzygies

Let
n
and
d
be positive integers. In this section, we work with the rings
R
,
S
,
R^{\prime}
and
S^{\prime}
defined in
Section

2
and
Section

3
and we explore properties of the space of linear syzygies of a subspace of
S_{-d}
.

Notation 4.1
.

Let
F_{1},\ldots,F_{a}\in S_{-d}
be linearly independent forms.
Recall that the space of linear relations on
F_{1},\ldots,F_{a}
is

L_{F_{1},\dots,F_{a}}=\left\{\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{ij}x_{i}\right)_{1\leq j\leq a}\!\!\in{R_{1}}^{a}\colon\,\sum_{j=1}^{a}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{ij}x_{i}\right)\circ F_{j}=0\right\}\,.

Consider the following property:

(\mathcal{P}_{F_{1},\dots,F_{a}}):\,\,\text{If\, $\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{ij}x_{i}\right)_{\!\!1\leq j\leq a}\!\!\!\!\!\in L_{F_{1},\dots,F_{a}}$, then $c_{ij}=c_{i^{\prime}j}$ for all $i,i^{\prime}\in[n]$ and $j\in[a]$.}

Remark 4.2
.

Set
x=x_{1}+\dots+x_{n}
. Observe then that the property
(\mathcal{P}_{F_{1},\dots,F_{a}})
holds if and only if

L_{F_{1},\dots,F_{a}}\subseteq x(R_{0})^{a}
. Thus, the property
(\mathcal{P}_{F_{1},\dots,F_{a}})
establishes (
2.2
) in the definition of a
d
-extremely narrow algebra, and it will be used in
Section

5
in proving that the algebras of interest are
d
-extremely narrow.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
F_{1},\dots,F_{b}\in S_{-d}
linearly independent and
G_{1},\dots,G_{a}\in S_{-d}
linearly independent such that
{\operatorname{Span}}_{\sf{k}}(F_{1},\dots,F_{b})\subseteq{\operatorname{Span}}_{\sf{k}}(G_{1},\dots,G_{a})
.
If
(\mathcal{P}_{G_{1},\dots,G_{a}})
holds, then
(\mathcal{P}_{F_{1},\dots,F_{b}})
also holds.

Proof.

Assume
(\mathcal{P}_{G_{1},\dots,G_{a}})
. For each
j\in[b]
we write

F_{j}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{a}\theta_{\ell}^{j}G_{\ell}

where the vectors
\theta^{j}:=(\theta^{j}_{\ell})_{\ell\in[a]}\in{\sf{k}}^{a}
with
j\in[b]
are linearly independent. Then

\sum_{j=1}^{b}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}{c_{ij}x_{i}}\right)\circ\sum_{\ell=1}^{a}\theta^{j}_{\ell}G_{\ell}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{a}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{b}c_{ij}\theta_{\ell}^{j}\Big)x_{i}\right)\circ G_{\ell}

Assume the expression above equals
0
. Then condition
(\mathcal{P}_{G_{1},\dots,G_{a}})
gives

\sum_{j=1}^{b}c_{ij}\theta_{\ell}^{j}=\sum_{j=1}^{b}c_{i^{\prime}j}\theta_{\ell}^{j}\quad\text{for all}\quad i,i^{\prime}\in[n],\ell\in[a]\,.

Since the vectors
\theta^{j}
are linearly independent, it must follow
c_{ij}=c_{i^{\prime}j}
for all
i,i^{\prime}\in[n]
,
j\in[b]
, showing that
(\mathcal{P}_{F_{1},\dots,F_{b}})
holds.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

In view of
4.3
, we see that, if
W
is a vector subspace of
S_{-d}
with basis
F_{1},\dots,F_{b}
, then the property
(\mathcal{P}_{F_{1},\dots,F_{b}})
is independent of the choice of the basis. In view of this observation, we will write
\mathcal{P}(W)
instead of
(\mathcal{P}_{F_{1},\dots,F_{b}})
.
4.3
can thus be reformulated as follows: If
W^{\prime}
is a subspace of
W
and
\mathcal{P}(W)
holds, then
\mathcal{P}(W^{\prime})
also holds.

Notation 4.5
.

Given a subspace
W\subseteq S_{-d}
we define

\widetilde{L}_{W}\coloneqq\left\{F\in W\colon\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\circ F=0\right\}\,.

Property
\mathcal{P}(W)
states that the linear syzygies of a vector space
W
are invariant under the action of the symmetric group. We now turn our attention to the case when the subspace
W
consists of invariant elements under the action of the symmetric group and give sufficient conditions for
\mathcal{P}(W)
to hold.

Lemma 4.6
.

If
W
is a subspace of
S^{\prime}_{-d}
, then

(4.1)

\dim_{\sf{k}}L_{W}\geq\dim_{\sf{k}}\widetilde{L}_{W}\geq\max\{\dim_{\sf{k}}W-P_{n}(d-1),0\}\,.

and the following statements are equivalent:

(1)

The equality
\dim_{\sf{k}}L_{W}=\dim_{\sf{k}}\widetilde{L}_{W}
holds.

(2)

\mathcal{P}(W)
holds.

Proof.

Let
F_{1},\dots,F_{b}
denote a basis for
W
. Here, we use
L_{W}=L_{F_{1},\dots,F_{b}}
, noting that
\dim_{\sf{k}}L_{W}
does not depend on the choice of basis.

We define a map
\eta\colon\widetilde{L}_{W}\to L_{W}

as follows. If
F\in\widetilde{L}_{W}
, write
F=\sum_{j=1}^{b}a_{j}F_{j}
and set

\eta(F)=\left(a_{j}\sum_{i=1}^{b}x_{i}\right)_{1\leq j\leq b}

The equation

\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\circ F=0
from the definition of
\widetilde{L}_{W}
can then be written as

\sum_{j=1}^{b}\left(a_{j}\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\right)\circ F_{j}=0\,.

and hence
\eta(F)\in L_{W}
.
The vector space map
\eta
is clearly injective and hence

(4.2)

\dim_{\sf{k}}\widetilde{L}_{W}\leq\dim_{\sf{k}}L_{W}\,,

with equality if and only if
\eta
is surjective. Further, note that
\eta
is surjective if and only if the property
\mathcal{P}(W)
holds. This establishes the equivalence (1)
\iff
(2).

Let
W^{\prime}
denote the image of the
{\sf{k}}
-vector space map
W\to S^{\prime}_{-(d-1)}
described by
F\mapsto\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\circ F
. We have an exact sequence

0\to\widetilde{L}_{W}\to W\to W^{\prime}\to 0

which implies

\dim_{\sf{k}}\widetilde{L}_{W}=\dim_{\sf{k}}W-\dim_{\sf{k}}W^{\prime}\geq\dim_{{\sf{k}}}W-P_{n}(d-1)\,.

Using (
4.2
), we have then the inequalities in (
4.1
).
∎

Next we give an example of a family of vector spaces
W
for which
\mathcal{P}(W)
holds. To do so, assume
n\geq 2
and define a property
\mathcal{Q}(W)
of a subspace

as follows:

\mathcal{Q}(W):\qquad\text{For all $F\in W$, \, $(x_{1}-x_{2})\circ F=0\implies F=0$.}

Notation 4.7
.

When
n\geq d
, let
\delta
denote the map that sends
F\in S^{\prime}_{-d}
to the polynomial
\delta(F)
in the variables
y_{1},\dots,y_{d}
obtained by setting the variables
y_{n},y_{n-1},\dots,y_{d+1}
to
0
. Note that
\delta
induces an isomorphism between
S^{\prime}_{-d}
and the
(-d)
component of the invariant ring of
{\sf{k}}[y_{1},\dots,y_{d}]
, which admits two equivalent descriptions in view of (
3.4
)

•

\delta
sends the basis
(m_{\lambda})_{\lambda\vdash d}
in
n
variables to the basis
(m_{\lambda})_{\lambda\vdash d}
in
d
variables,

•

\delta
sends the basis
(M_{\lambda})_{\lambda\vdash d}
in
n
variables to
\binom{n}{d}^{-1}(M_{\lambda})_{\lambda\vdash d}
in
d
variables.

Part (3) of
4.8
below generalizes
[
15
, Proposition 7.7]
.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
W
be a subspace of
S^{\prime}_{-d}
. The following then hold:

(1)

If
n\geq d\geq 2
, then
\mathcal{Q}(\delta(W))
implies
\mathcal{Q}(W)
;

(2)

\mathcal{Q}(W)
implies
\mathcal{P}(W)
;

(3)

Assume
\operatorname{char}{\sf{k}}>n
or
\operatorname{char}{\sf{k}}=0
. If
\Lambda
is a set of partitions of
d
so that
(d)\not\in\Lambda
and

W_{\Lambda}={\operatorname{Span}}_{\sf{k}}(M_{\lambda}:\lambda\in\Lambda)

then
\mathcal{Q}(W_{\Lambda})
holds, and hence
\mathcal{P}(W_{\Lambda})
holds.

Proof.

(1) Assume
n\geq d
and
\mathcal{Q}(\delta(W))
holds. Let
F\in W
such that
(x_{1}-x_{2})\circ F=0
. We can write
F=\delta(F)+G
, where
G\in S_{-d}
is such that each monomial in
G
is divisible by some
y_{i}
with
d<i\leq n
.
Then

0=(x_{1}-x_{2})\circ F=(x_{1}-x_{2})\circ\delta(F)+(x_{1}-x_{2})\circ G\,.

In this sum,
(x_{1}-x_{2})\circ\delta(F)
is a poynomial in variables
y_{1},\dots,y_{d}
, while
(x_{1}-x_{2})\circ G
shares the same property as
G
that each monomial in
(x_{1}-x_{2})\circ G
is divisible by some
y_{i}
with
i>d
. It follows that
(x_{1}-x_{2})\circ\delta(F)=0
. Since
\mathcal{Q}(\delta(W))
holds, we conclude
\delta(F)=0
, implying
F=0
, since
\delta
is bijective.

(2) Assume
\mathcal{Q}(W)
holds, equivalently
(x_{1}-x_{2})\circ F\neq 0
for all nonzero
F\in W
. Since
W\subseteq S^{\prime}
, (
3.1
) gives that
(x_{i}-x_{j})\circ F\neq 0
for all nonzero
F\in W
and all
i,j\in[n]
with
i\neq j
. We consider a basis
F_{1},\dots,F_{a}
of
W
.
Assume

(4.3)

\sum_{\ell=1}^{a}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}c_{k,\ell}x_{k}\right)\circ F_{\ell}=0.

Let
i,j\in[n]
distinct and let
\sigma=(ij)\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
be the transposition interchanging
i
and
j
. Acting with
\sigma
on (
4.3
) and using (
3.1
) and the fact that

for all
\ell\in[a]
, we have:

\sum_{\ell=1}^{a}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}c_{k,\ell}\sigma(x_{k})\right)\circ F_{\ell}=0.

Subtracting this from (
4.3
) gives

(x_{i}-x_{j})\circ\sum_{\ell=1}^{a}(c_{i,\ell}-c_{j,\ell})F_{\ell}=0.

Then
\mathcal{Q}(W)
implies that
\sum_{\ell=1}^{a}(c_{i,\ell}-c_{j,\ell})F_{\ell}=0
, and hence
c_{i,\ell}=c_{j,\ell}
for all
\ell\in[a]
and thus
\mathcal{P}(W)
holds.

(3) Since
(d)\notin\Lambda
, observe that a nonzero polynomial

does not contain

in its support for any

, and hence
(x_{1}-x_{2})\circ F\neq 0
by
[
15
, Lemma 7.6]
. Consequently, it follows that
\mathcal{Q}(W_{\Lambda})
holds and from (2) it follows that
\mathcal{P}(W_{\Lambda})
holds.
∎

5.
Narrow algebras with general invariant socle

In this section, we use the notation in Section 2 for fixed positive integers
n,d,r
with
r\leq P_{n}(d)
. We study the parametrization of symmetric ideals in terms of their socle given by the map
\beta
in (
1.3
).

Definition 5.1
.

Let
0\leq s<P_{n}(d)
. We say that a quotient
R/I
is a
narrow algebra with
(s,d)
–invariant socle
if the inverse system of
I
has the form
I^{-1}=W+S_{\geq-d+1}
, where
W
is a vector subspace of
S^{\prime}_{-d}
and
\dim_{\sf{k}}(W)=s
.

We parametrize the space
W
by its orthogonal complement
U\in\Gr(P_{n}(d)-s,R^{\prime}_{d})
, where
U^{\perp}=W
. We say that a
general narrow algebra
A
with
(s,d)
–invariant socle
satisfies a property
(\mathcal{P})
if there exists a nonempty set
G
in
\Gr(P_{n}(d)-s,R^{\prime}_{d})
such that the property
(\mathcal{P})
holds for
A=R/I
, where
I={\operatorname{Ann}}_{R}(U^{\perp}+S_{\geq-d+1})
with
U\in G
.

The main result of the section,
5.2
, says that, under appropriate assumptions on
d
,
s
and
n
, a general narrow algebra with
(s,d)
–invariant socle is
d
-extremely narrow. For the purpose of later connections, we state the result in terms of
r=P_{n}(d)-s
, where
1\leq r\leq P_{n}(d)
.

Recall that
P_{n}(d)
is independent of
n
when
n\geq d
, and we denote it
P(d)
in this case. Since the dimension of
R^{\prime}_{d}
is equal to
P(d)
, we identify the domain of
\beta
,
\Gr(r,R^{\prime}_{d})
, with
\Gr(r,{\sf{k}}^{P(d)})
by identifying the basis
\{m_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\vdash d}
with the standard basis of
{\sf{k}}^{P_{n}(d)}
. An important aspect of our result is uniformity with respect to the number of variables, when
n\geq d
, as implied by the last statement of
5.2
below.

Theorem 5.2
.

Assume
n,d,r
are positive integers with
r\leq P_{n}(d)
, and
\operatorname{char}{\sf{k}}>n
or
\operatorname{char}{\sf{k}}=0
. There exists a nonempty open subset
G
of
\Gr(r,R^{\prime}_{d})
such that if
U\in G
, then the ideal
J={\operatorname{Ann}}_{R}(U^{\perp}+S_{\geqslant-d+1})
and the algebra
A=R/J
satisfy the following properties:

(1)

The algebra
A
is narrow with
\dim_{\sf{k}}J_{d}=\dim_{\sf{k}}R_{d}-(P_{n}(d)-r)
. In particular,
J={\mathfrak{m}}^{d}
when
r=P_{n}(d)
.

(2)

If
r<P_{n}(d)
, then
A
is
d
-extremely narrow, with socle polynomial

\big(\dim_{\sf{k}}R_{d-1}-(P_{n}(d)-r)n+\max\{P_{n}(d)-P_{n}(d-1)-r,0\}\big)z^{d-1}+(P_{n}(d)-r)z^{d}\,.

(3)

J
is generated in degree
d
.

(4)

The algebra
A
has the Weak Lefschetz Property. When
r<P_{n}(d)
, the map
A_{i-1}\xrightarrow{\ell}A_{i}
has maximal rank for all
i
and all
\ell\in R_{1}
such that
\ell
is not a multiple of
x_{1}+\dots+x_{n}
.

Furthermore, when
n\geq d
the set
G
is independent of
n
, meaning that, when identifying
\Gr(r,R^{\prime}_{d})
with
\Gr(r,{\sf{k}}^{P(d)})
, the equations defining
G
(in Plücker coordinates) have coefficients that do not depend on
n
.

The proof is given at the end of the section, and it is based on the following proposition, which generalizes
[
15
, Proposition 7.15]
and uses notation introduced in
Section

4
.

Proposition 5.3
.

Assume the setup of
5.2
and
1\leq r\leq P_{n}(d)
. There exists a nonempty open subset
G
of
\Gr(r,R^{\prime}_{d})
such that if
U\in G
and
W=U^{\perp}
, then
\mathcal{Q}(W)
and
\mathcal{P}(W)
hold and

\dim_{\sf{k}}L_{W}=\max\{P_{n}(d)-P_{n}(d-1)-r,0\}\,.

Moreover, when
n\geq d
, the set
G
is independent of the number of variables.

Proof.

When
d=1
or
n=1
then, since
1\leq r\leq P_{n}(d)
, it follows that
r=P_{n}(d)=1
, and in this case we set
G=\Gr(1,R^{\prime}_{1})
. In this case
W=0
and
\dim_{\sf{k}}L_{W}=0
as claimed, and
\mathcal{Q}(W)
,
\mathcal{P}(W)
hold vacuously. Assume from now on that
d\geq 2
and
n\geq 2
.

For
U\in\Gr(r,R^{\prime}_{d})
, we set
W=U^{\perp}
and we denote

C(U)=\dim_{\sf{k}}\widetilde{L}_{W}\qquad\text{and}\qquad D(U)=\dim_{\sf{k}}L_{W}\,.

Let
\Lambda_{0}
be the set of smallest
P_{n}(d)-r
partitions of
d
with respect to the lexicographic order. Setting

U_{0}=\langle m_{\lambda}:\lambda\not\in\Lambda_{0}\rangle\subseteq R_{d}\quad\text{yields}\quad{U_{0}}^{\perp}=\langle M_{\lambda}:\lambda\in\Lambda_{0}\rangle=W_{\Lambda_{0}}\subseteq S^{\prime}_{-d}.

We first prove:

Claim 1
.

There exists a nonempty Zariski open subset
G_{1}
of
\Gr(r,R^{\prime}_{d})
which is independent of
n
when
n\geq d
, such that
U_{0}\in G_{1}
and

C(U)=\max\{P_{n}(d)-P_{n}(d-1)-r,0\}\qquad\text{for all}\quad U\in G_{1}.

To prove the claim, let
U\in\Gr(r,R^{\prime}_{d})
, let
W=U^{\perp}
and let
F=\sum_{\lambda\vdash d}\theta_{\lambda}M_{\lambda}\in S^{\prime}_{-d}
.
Then
F\in\widetilde{L}_{W}
if and only if the two conditions below are satisfied

(5.1)

\begin{cases}F\in U^{\perp}\\
\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\right)\circ F=0.\end{cases}

We analyze the two conditions separately.

The second condition in (
5.1
) is equivalent to the system of linear equations

\left(m_{\mu}\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\right)\circ F=0\text{ for all }\mu\vdash d-1.

Utilizing
3.2
, including the notation
c^{\lambda}_{\mu}
introduced therein, we express the products
m_{\mu}\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}
in the basis
\{m_{\lambda}\}
of
R^{\prime}_{d}
as follows

\left(\sum_{\lambda\vdash d}c^{\lambda}_{\mu}m_{\lambda}\right)\circ\left(\sum_{\nu\vdash d}\theta_{\nu}M_{\nu}\right)=\sum_{\lambda\vdash d}c^{\lambda}_{\mu}\theta_{\lambda}=0\quad\text{ for all }\mu\vdash d-1.

The set of equations above is equivalent to the following system of equations, which is independent of
n
when
n\geq d
:

(5.2)

\sum_{\lambda\vdash d}c^{\lambda}_{\mu}\theta_{\lambda}=0\quad\text{ for all }\mu\vdash d-1.

Recall from
3.2
that
c^{\lambda}_{\mu}\neq 0
if and only if
\lambda
can be obtained by adding a box to
\mu
and possibly re-ordering. In particular, this implies that if
c^{\lambda}_{\mu}\neq 0
then
\lambda\geq_{{\operatorname{Lex}}}\mu_{\square}
, where lex refers to the lexicographic order and
\mu_{\square}\vdash d
is the partition
\mu
followed by an additional part equal to 1. Moreover, if
\mu
is the
i
-th smallest partition of
d-1
in the lexicographic order then
\mu_{\square}
is the
i
-th smallest partition of
d
in the lexicographic order. Thus, arranging the partitions of
d
and
d-1
in lexicographic order from smallest to largest, this implies that the
P_{n}(d-1)\times P_{n}(d)
matrix with scalar entries
c_{\lambda}^{\mu}
of the system (
5.2
) is in row echelon form with pivots in every row, occupying the first
P_{n}(d-1)
columns. In particular the rank of the matrix
\begin{bmatrix}c^{\lambda}_{\mu}\end{bmatrix}
is
P_{n}(d-1)
.

Next we turn to the first condition in (
5.1
), namely that
F\in U^{\perp}
. Consider a basis for
U
consisting of
r
polynomials
u_{i}=\sum_{\lambda\vdash d}u_{i,\lambda}m_{\lambda}
. Due to (
3.5
), the condition
F\in U^{\perp}
becomes

(5.3)

\sum_{\lambda\vdash d}u_{i,\lambda}\theta_{\lambda}=0\quad\text{ for all }1\leq i\leq r.

Upon combining the equations (
5.2
) and (
5.3
), we argue next that the corresponding
(P_{n}(d-1)+r)\times P_{n}(d)
matrix

A_{U}=\begin{bmatrix}c^{\lambda}_{\mu}\\
u_{i,\lambda}\ \end{bmatrix}_{\mu\vdash d-1,i=1,\ldots,r,\lambda\vdash d}

attains maximal rank
\max\{P_{n}(d-1)+r,P_{n}(d)\}
, provided that
U
is chosen in an appropriate open set of
\Gr(r,R^{\prime}_{d})
. First, note that if
U=U_{0}
, then the matrix formed by the last
r
rows of
A_{U_{0}}
, coming from (
5.3
), is in echelon form, with pivots in the last
r
columns. Recalling that the matrix formed by the first
P_{n}(d-1)
rows of
A_{U_{0}}
is in echelon form with pivots in the first
P_{n}(d-1)
columns, we conclude that
A_{U_{0}}
has rank
P_{n}(d-1)+r
provided that
P_{n}(d-1)+r
does not exceed
P_{n}(d)
, and
P_{n}(d)
otherwise.

The condition that the matrix
A_{U}
does not have maximal rank is the vanishing of its maximal minors. The maximal minors of
A_{U}
can be written by generalized Laplace expansion as linear combinations of the maximal minors of
\begin{bmatrix}u_{i,\lambda}\end{bmatrix}
with coefficients given by complementary minors of
\begin{bmatrix}c^{\lambda}_{\mu}\end{bmatrix}
. That is, the locus where
A_{U}
fails to have maximal rank is cut out by linear equations in the Plücker coordinates of
U
. Therefore, the complement
G_{1}
of the set cut out by these equations is open and
U_{0}\in G_{1}
by the previous considerations. The set
G_{1}
does not depend on
n
when
n\geq d
, since the entries of
A_{U}
, which determine the complement of
G_{1}
, are independent of
n
.

Since
A_{U}
has maximal rank on the set
G_{1}
, we conclude that

C(U)=P_{n}(d)-\operatorname{rank}A_{U}=\max\{P_{n}(d)-P_{n}(d-1)-r,0\}

for all
U\in G_{1}
.

We now show:

Claim 2
.

There exists a nonempty open set
G_{2}
of
\Gr(r,R^{\prime}_{d})
, which is independent of
n
when
n\geq d
, such that
\mathcal{Q}(U^{\perp})
holds for all
U\in G_{2}
.

To prove Claim 2, consider the following two conditions on
F=\sum_{\lambda\vdash d}\theta_{\lambda}M_{\lambda}\in S^{\prime}_{-d}
:

(5.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}(x_{1}-x_{2})&\circ F=0\qquad\text{and}\\
F&\in U^{\perp}\,,\end{split}

where
U
has a basis consisting of
u_{i}=\sum_{\lambda\vdash d}u_{i,\lambda}m_{\lambda}
, with
1\leq i\leq r
. For a given
U
, the condition
\mathcal{Q}(U^{\perp})
holds if and only if the equations (
5.4
) imply
F=0
. As seen above, the condition
F\in U^{\perp}
translates as the equations (
5.3
). Further, upon writing the expression
(x_{1}-x_{2})\circ F
in terms of the monomial basis of
S_{-d+1}
and equating the coefficients to 0, the condition
(x_{1}-x_{2})\circ F=0
translates into
\dim_{{\sf{k}}}S_{-d+1}
linear equations in the variables
\theta_{\lambda}
. We denote by
C_{U}
the matrix of this system. Thus, (
5.4
) translates into a homogeneous system of
\dim_{\sf{k}}S_{-d+1}+r
equations in
P_{n}(d)
indeterminates.
Using
4.8
(3) we deduce that
\mathcal{Q}(W)
holds for
W=W_{\Lambda_{0}}={U_{0}}^{\perp}
, meaning that, when
U=U_{0}
, the system (
5.4
) has only the trivial solution. In general, for
U\in\Gr(r,R^{\prime}_{d})
, the condition that the system has only the trivial solution is equivalent to the fact that its matrix has maximal rank. This matrix has the form

B_{U}=\begin{bmatrix}C_{U}\\
u_{i,\lambda}\ \end{bmatrix}_{i=1,\ldots,r,\lambda\vdash d}.

As observed earlier, when
U=U_{0}
the matrix formed by the last
r
rows of
B_{U_{0}}
is in row echelon form, with pivots in the last
r
columns, and hence there exists a maximal nonzero minor
\mathcal{M}_{0}
of
B_{U_{0}}
that uses these last
r
rows. For any
U
, the condition that the maximal minor of
B_{U}
using the same rows and columns as
\mathcal{M}_{0}

is zero is a linear equation in the Plücker coordinates of
U
. The complement
G(n)
of the set cut out by this equation is thus open, and contains
U_{0}
. We conclude that there exists a nonempty open set
G(n)
for which (
5.4
) implies
F=0
whenever
U\in G(n)
. In view of
4.8
(2), we see that
\mathcal{P}(U^{\perp})
holds for all
U\in G(n)
. The set
G(n)
depends on
n
because the matrix
C_{U}
depends on
n
. However, when
n\geq d
, we describe next a nonempty open set that does not depend on
n
, while satisfying the same properties.

Assume
n\geq d
, and hence
P_{n}(d)=P(d)
. Since the bijection that takes an

-dimensional subspace of
R^{\prime}_{d}
to its
(P(d)-r)
-dimensional orthogonal complement in

induces an isomorphism of algebraic varieties between the respective Grassmannians, the set
G(d)^{\perp}=\{U^{\perp}\mid U\in G(d)\}
is an open subset of the Grassmannian of
(P(d)-r)
-dimensional subspaces of degree
-d
invariant polynomials in
{\sf{k}}[y_{1},\dots,y_{d}]
. We parametrize
G(d)
using Plücker coordinates corresponding to the basis
\{m_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\vdash d}
and
G(d)^{\perp}
using the Plücker coordinates corresponding to the dual basis
\{M_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\vdash d}
, cf. (
3.1
). We use the map
\delta
introduced in
4.7
. Since
\delta(M_{\lambda})=\binom{n}{d}^{-1}M_{\lambda}
, where the first
M_{\lambda}
is a polynomial in
n
variables and the second is a polynomial in
d
variables,
\delta
multiplies each Plücker coordinate by
\binom{n}{d}^{-P(d)+r}
and thus induces the identity map on tuples of Plücker coordinates viewed as coordinates in projective space.

Let
D
denote the preimage of
G(d)^{\perp}
under
\delta
. Then
D
is an open subset of the Grassmannian
\Gr(P(d)-r,S^{\prime}_{d})
. While the elements of
S^{\prime}_{-d}
depend on
n
, the set

does not, as the coefficients of its defining equations are the same as those of the set

, which does not depend on

. We set then

. If

, then
U^{\perp}\in D
and hence
\delta(U^{\perp})\in G(d)^{\perp}
, meaning that
(\delta(U^{\perp}))^{\perp}\in G(d)
. By the definition of

, we know that
\mathcal{Q}(\delta(U^{\perp}))
holds, and hence
\mathcal{Q}(U^{\perp})
holds by
4.8
(1). This finishes the proof of Claim 2.

It follows from Claim 2 and
4.8
that
\mathcal{P}(U^{\perp})
also holds for all
U\in G_{2}
. Then,
4.6
gives that
C(U)=D(U)
for all
U\in G_{2}
. Using also Claim 1, we conclude

D(U)=\max\{P_{n}(d)-P_{n}(d-1)-r,0\}\qquad\text{for all $U\in G_{1}\cap G_{2}$.}

which gives the conclusion of our result.
∎

We now give the proof of the theorem.

Proof of
5.2
.

Let
G
be as in
5.3
.

Part (1) is a direct consequence of
2.1
, noting that
\dim_{\sf{k}}U^{\perp}=P_{n}(d)-r

Theorem 8.1

\displaystyle(I^{\prime})^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle W+S^{\prime}_{\geqslant-d+1}

\displaystyle I^{-1}

\displaystyle=

Since

to

9.
Asymptotic stability with respect to the number of variables

In this section, we are concerned with how the properties of interest of general symmetric ideals change with the number of variables.

We denote
R={\sf{k}}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n},\ldots]
, with
{\sf{k}}
a field, the polynomial ring in countably many variables and
R_{n}={\sf{k}}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
. Unlike in previous sections, the subscripts utilized so far record the Krull dimension of each ring, not internal degrees. The ring
R
is the colimit of the directed system of rings
R_{n}
.

Let
\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}=\bigcup_{n\geq 1}\mathfrak{S}_{n}
be the group of permutations of
{\mathbb{N}}
that fix all but finitely many elements. It acts on
R
by permuting variables in the natural way, that is,

\sigma\cdot f=f(x_{\sigma(1)},x_{\sigma(2)},\ldots)\text{ for any }\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}.

This action induces an action of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
on
R_{n}
for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
that coincides with that considered in the previous sections.

An ideal
{\mathcal{I}}
of
R
is
\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}
-invariant
if
\sigma\cdot f\in{\mathcal{I}}
whenever
f\in{\mathcal{I}}
. If
{\mathcal{I}}
is a
\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}
-invariant ideal, then the sequence of ideals
I_{n}={\mathcal{I}}\cap R_{n}
are symmetric with respect to the action of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
on
R_{n}
. Moreover the chain satisfies the property that for
m\leq n
,
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
and
f\in I_{m}
, it follows that
\sigma\cdot f\in I_{n}
. A sequence
\{I_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
of ideals that has this property is called a
\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}
-invariant chain
. Conversely, given a
\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}
-invariant chain
\{I_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
, the colimit
{\mathcal{I}}=\varinjlim_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}I_{n}
is a
\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}
-invariant ideal.

As
R
is Noetherian up to symmetry by
[
6
]
, any
\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}
-invariant ideal can be generated by finitely many elements
f_{1},\ldots,f_{r}
of
R
, thus has the form

{\mathcal{I}}=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{r})_{\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}}=\left(\sigma\cdot f_{i}\mid\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{\infty},1\leq i\leq r\right).

Setting
m
to be the largest index of a variable in the union of the supports of
f_{1},\ldots,f_{r}
gives that
{\mathcal{I}}
is the colimit of the chain
I_{m}\subseteq I_{m+1}\subseteq\cdots I_{n}\subseteq\cdots
, where
I_{n}=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{r})_{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}
for each
n\geq m
.

We apply the results from our previous sections to study chains of
\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}
-invariant ideals as above where

are general forms of degree
d
. Since

is no longer fixed, an adjustment of our previously used notation is needed.

(2), we have:

\displaystyle\beta_{i,i+d}({\mathcal{I}},s)

\displaystyle=\dim_{\sf{k}}{\operatorname{Tor}}_{i}^{R_{i}}(R_{i}/I_{i},{\sf{k}})_{i+d}s^{i}+\dim_{\sf{k}}{\operatorname{Tor}}_{i}^{R_{i+1}}(R_{i+1}/I_{i+1},{\sf{k}})_{i+d}s^{i+1}

\displaystyle=as^{i}+\ell s^{i+1}\,.

If
i-j=d-1
, then
{\operatorname{Tor}}_{i}^{R_{n}}(R_{n}/I_{n},{\sf{k}})_{j}\neq 0
only when
i\leq n
. Using the formulas in
7.10
(2) and the notation
b_{i}=\binom{i+d-2}{d-1}-ai+\ell
, we obtain for all
i\geq 1
:

\displaystyle\beta_{i,i+d-1}({\mathcal{I}},s)=\sum_{n>i}\dim_{\sf{k}}{\operatorname{Tor}}_{i}^{R_{n}}(R_{n}/I_{n},{\sf{k}})_{i+d-1}s^{n}+\dim_{\sf{k}}{\operatorname{Tor}}_{i}^{R_{i}}(R_{i}/I_{i},{\sf{k}})_{i+d-1}s^{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n>i}\left(\binom{n+d-1}{d+i-1}\binom{d+i-2}{i-1}-a\binom{n}{i-1}\right)s^{n}+b_{i}s^{i}

\displaystyle=\binom{d+i-2}{i-1}\left(\sum_{n>i}\binom{n+d-1}{d+i-1}s^{n}\right)-a\sum_{n>i}\binom{n}{i-1}s^{n}+b_{i}s^{i}

\displaystyle=\binom{d+i-2}{i-1}\cdot s^{i}\left(\sum_{m\geq 1}\binom{m+i+d-1}{d+i-1}s^{m}\right)-as^{i-1}\left(\sum_{m\geq 2}\binom{m+i-1}{i-1}s^{m}\right)+b_{i}s^{i}

\displaystyle=\binom{d+i-2}{i-1}\!\left(\frac{s^{i}}{(1\!-\!s)^{i+d}}-s^{i}\right)\!-\!a\!\left(\frac{s^{i-1}}{(1\!-\!s)^{i}}-\!s^{i-1}\!-is^{i}\!\right)+\!\binom{i+d-2}{d-1}s^{i}-ais^{i}+\ell s^{i}

\displaystyle=\binom{d+i-2}{i-1}\frac{s^{i}}{(1-s)^{i+d}}-\frac{a}{s}\left(\frac{s^{i}}{(1-s)^{i}}-s^{i}\right)+\ell s^{i}\,.

Also, observe that

\beta_{0,0}({\mathcal{I}},s)=\sum_{n\geq 0}s^{n}=\frac{1}{1-s}\,.

We have thus

\displaystyle P_{I}(s,t,u)=\frac{1}{1-s}+\sum_{i\geq 1}\beta_{i,{i+d-1}}({\mathcal{I}},s)t^{i}u^{i+d-1}+\sum_{i\geq 1}\beta_{i,i+d}({\mathcal{I}},s)t^{i}u^{i+d}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-s}\!+u^{d-1}\!\left(\sum_{i\geq 1}\binom{d+i-2}{d-1}\frac{(stu)^{i}}{(1-s)^{i+d}}-\frac{a}{s}\sum_{i\geq 1}\left(\frac{(stu)^{i}}{(1-s)^{i}}-\!(stu)^{i}\right)+\ell\sum_{i\geq 1}(stu)^{i}\!\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+u^{d}\left(\sum_{i\geq 1}a(stu)^{i}+\ell s(stu)^{i}\right).

We compute some of the terms in this sum separately:

\displaystyle\sum_{i\geq 1}\binom{d+i-2}{d-1}\frac{(stu)^{i}}{(1-s)^{i+d}}

\displaystyle=\frac{stu}{(1-s)^{d+1}}\sum_{m\geq 0}\binom{d-1+m}{d-1}\left(\frac{stu}{1-s}\right)^{m}

\displaystyle=\frac{stu}{(1-s)^{d+1}}\frac{1}{\left(1-\frac{stu}{1-s}\right)^{d}}=\frac{stu}{(1-s)(1-s-stu)^{d}}

\displaystyle\sum_{i\geq 1}\left(\frac{(stu)^{i}}{(1-s)^{i}}-(stu)^{i}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{1-\frac{stu}{1-s}}-\frac{1}{1-stu}\right)=\frac{1-s}{1-s-stu}-\frac{1}{1-stu}

\displaystyle=\frac{s^{2}tu}{(1-s-stu)(1-stu)}\,.

In conclusion, the bigraded equivariant Poincaré series is

P_{\mathcal{I}}(s,t,u)\!=\!\frac{1}{1-s}\!+\!stu^{d}\left(\frac{1}{(1\!-\!s)(1\!-\!s\!-\!stu)^{d}}\!-\!\frac{a}{(1\!-\!s\!-\!stu)(1\!-\!stu)}\!+\!\frac{\ell+au+\ell su}{1-stu}\right).

∎

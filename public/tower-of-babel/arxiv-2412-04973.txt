On the Laplace equation with non-local dynamical boundary conditions
Raffaela Capitanelli & Mirko D’Ovidio

Abstract
Aim of the paper is to study non-local dynamic boundary conditions of reactive-diffusive type for the Laplace equation from analytic and probabilistic point of view. In particular, we provide compact and probabilistic representation of the solution together with an interpretation in term of boundary processes.

1
Introduction

Aim of the paper is to study non-local dynamic boundary conditions of reactive-diffusive type for the Laplace equation. More precisely, for
k\in\mathbb{R}
and
l>0
, we consider the following problem

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\Delta u=0&\text{in
$(0,\infty)\times\Omega$,}\\
D_{t}^{\alpha}u=k\partial_{\bf n}u+l\Delta_{\Gamma}u&\text{on
$(0,\infty)\times\Gamma$,}\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x)&\text{on $\Gamma$,}\end{array}\right.

where
u=u(t,x)
,
t\in[0,\infty)
,
x\in\Omega
and

\Gamma=\partial\Omega
.
The symbol
\Delta_{\Gamma}
denotes the Laplace–Beltrami operator on
\Gamma
and
\partial_{\bf n}u
is the normal derivative being
{\bf n}
the outward normal to
\Omega.
We assume that
\Omega
is a
C^{\infty}
bounded domain of
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
(
N\geq 2
) and
\Gamma
is a Riemannian manifold endowed with the natural inherited metric. The non-local operator

\displaystyle D^{\alpha}_{t}u(t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}u^{\prime}(s)(t-s)^{-\alpha}ds,\quad\alpha\in(0,1)

(1.4)

is the Caputo-Džrbašjan derivative. We recall that, for
z>0
,
\Gamma(z)=\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{z-1}e^{-s}ds
is the absolutely convergent Euler integral also termed gamma function.

We provide a compact representation for the solution to the problem (
1
). We exploit the result in
[
32
]
for which adding the Laplace–Beltrami operator on the boundary (
l>0
) the corresponding problem is well-posed for any value of
k
. Moreover, under suitable spectral conditions, we show that such solution has the probabilistic representation

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{\mu(x)}[u_{0}(X^{\Gamma}\circ L_{t})]=\int_{\Gamma}\mathbf{E}_{y}[u_{0}(X^{\Gamma}\circ L_{t})]\mu(x,dy),\quad x\in\Omega\cup\Gamma

(1.5)

where the initial distribution
\mu(x,dy)
is an harmonic measure and
X^{\Gamma}
is a boundary Markov process with random time
L
explaining the non-local dynamic. Thus, our problem can be treated in terms of a fractional Cauchy problem on compact manifold without boundary for which

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{\mu(x)}[u_{0}(X^{\Gamma}\circ L_{t})]=\mathbf{E}_{x}[u_{0}(X^{\Gamma}\circ L_{t})],\quad x\in\Gamma

gives the solution on
\Gamma
according with the Poisson integral (
1.5
).

The operator (
1.4
) has been introduced in
[
8
,
9
,
10
]
by the first author and separately in a series of works starting from
[
16
,
17
]
by the second author. For
\alpha=1
the operator
D^{\alpha}_{t}
becomes the ordinary derivative for which we get the dynamical boundary condition of reactive-diffusive type

\displaystyle\dot{u}=k\partial_{\bf n}u+l\Delta_{\Gamma}u\quad\textrm{on }(0,\infty)\times\Gamma.

(1.6)

From an analytical point of view, such a
non fractional
dynamical boundary condition has been studied in
[
32
]
for the Laplace equation.
In particular, such a condition describes a heat conduction process in
\Omega
with a heat source on the boundary which can depend on the heat flux around the boundary and on the heat flux across it (see for example
[
21
]
and the reference therein). We remark that in the case
l=0
, the problem has been studied by many authors (see for example
[
19
]
,
[
24
]
,
[
26
]
), in this case the corresponding problem is well-posed if and only if
k\leq 0
. For the parabolic case associated with (
1.6
) we mainly refer to
[
33
]
where the authors considered the heat equation in place of the Laplace equation. We refer to
[
15
]
for the parabolic case associated with the non-local condition in (
1
) which has been investigated only in case
l=0
and
k<0
. For the fractional Cauchy problem we may refer to a very large literature. Such problems are well-know and they have been investigated by many researchers on both regular domains (see for example
[
11
,
22
,
25
,
28
,
29
,
31
]
) or irregular domains (see for example
[
4
,
5
,
7
]
). Recently the non-local dynamic boundary conditions for the heat equation have been investigated by
[
13
,
14
]
together with the associated probabilistic representation in terms of sticky Brownian motions.

The plan of the paper is the following.
In the second section we study the Laplace equation with fractional dynamical boundary conditions
from the analytical point of view and in particular we obtain a representation formula for the solution.
In the third section we deal with the same problem from the probabilistic point of view and in particular we give a interpretation for the solution in term of boundary processes.
In the last section we consider the Laplace equation with a general non local boundary condition.

2
Compact representation

In this section we study the Laplace equation on a bounded regular
domain
\Omega
of
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
(
N\geq 2
) with time-fractional dynamical
boundary condition of reactive–diffusive type described in (
1
).

We denote by
\mathcal{D}(\Gamma)
the space
C^{\infty}(\Gamma)
and by
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Gamma)
its dual space, that is the space of distributions on
\Gamma
. Moreover for any
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
we denote by the symbols
H^{s}(\Omega)
and
H^{s}(\Gamma)
the Sobolev spaces of distributions
respectively on
\Omega
and on its boundary
\Gamma
(see
[
27
]
).

We recall that
the Laplace-Beltrami operator
\Delta_{\Gamma}
can be defined on
C^{\infty}(\Gamma)

by the formula

-\int_{\Gamma}(\Delta_{\Gamma}u)v\,ds=\int_{\Gamma}\nabla_{\Gamma}u\,\nabla_{\Gamma}v\,ds

(2.1)

for any
u,v\in C^{\infty}(\Gamma)
where
we denote by
\nabla_{\Gamma}
the Riemannian gradient and by
ds
the natural volume element on
\Gamma.

We recall that, for any
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
, the operator

\Delta_{\Gamma}:D(\Delta_{\Gamma})=H^{s+2}(\Gamma)\to H^{s}(\Gamma)

generates an analytic semigroup on
H^{s+2}(\Gamma)
(see, for example, Appendix A in
[
32
]
).

Before stating our result, we recall some properties of the Dirichlet–to–Neumann operator
A_{DN}
(see
[
27
]
).

For any
g\in H^{s}(\Gamma)
,
s>0
, the
non–homogeneous Dirichlet problem

\begin{cases}\Delta v=0,&\text{in $\Omega$},\\
v=g&\text{on $\Gamma$,}\end{cases}

(2.2)

has a unique solution
v\in H^{s+1/2}(\Omega).

Moreover, for any datum
g\in H^{s}(\Gamma)
of the non–homogeneous Dirichlet problem
(
2.2
), the operator
\mathbb{D}
that associates the datum
g
to the solution
v=\mathbb{D}g
is linear and bounded from
H^{s}(\Gamma)
to
H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(\Omega).

As
v
has
normal derivative
\partial_{\bf n}v\in H^{s-1}(\Gamma),
the Dirichlet–to–Neumann operator
A_{DN}:

g\mapsto\partial_{\bf n}v
is
bounded from
H^{s}(\Gamma)
to
H^{s-1}(\Gamma).

We point out that, for all
u,v\in C^{\infty}(\Gamma)
, by integrating by parts, we obtain

\int_{\Gamma}A_{DN}u\,vds=\int_{\Omega}\nabla(\mathbb{D}u)\nabla(\mathbb{D}v)d\mu

(2.3)

(we denote by
d\mu
the natural volume element on
\Omega)
.

We have that
A_{DN}
generates an analytic semigroup. Furthermore it is possible to prove that for
l>0

the operator
kA_{DN}+l\Delta_{\Gamma}
with domain
D(kA_{DN}+l\Delta_{\Gamma})=H^{s+2}(\Gamma)
generates an analytic semigroup in
H^{s}
(see the proof of Theorem 1 in
[
32
]
).

Now our aim is to prove a representation formula for the solution of problem (
1
) following the same approach of
[
32
]
.

[

]
.

for all

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\Delta u=0&\text{in

Thus, we provide spectral conditions for which, given any

there is a unique solution

with the initial condition

First we recall that

Let
\Lambda=0
,

Remark 2.1
.

Observe that the spectral condition is not trivial. We point out that, for example, the condition

is satisfied when

Recall that

3
Probabilistic representation

We introduce briefly the processes we deal with further on. As usual, we denote by
\mathbf{E}_{x}
the mean operator under the measure
\mathbf{P}_{x}
where
x
is a starting point:

i)

X^{+}=\{X^{+}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is a reflecting Brownian motion on
\overline{\Omega}
with boundary local time
\gamma^{+}=\{\gamma^{+}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
. The process
X^{+}
has generator
(G^{+},D(G^{+}))
where
G^{+}=\Delta
is the Neumann Laplacian with

\displaystyle D(G^{+})=\{\varphi,\Delta\varphi\in C(\overline{\Omega}),\,\varphi\in H^{1}(\Omega)\,:\,\partial_{\bf n}\varphi=0\};

ii)

H=\{H_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is a subordinator with
\mathbf{E}_{0}[\exp(-\lambda H_{t})]=\exp(-t\Phi(\lambda))
,
\lambda>0
;

iii)

L=\{L_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is the inverse
L_{t}=\inf\{s\geq 0\,:\,H_{s}>t\}
to
H
.

Moreover, we introduce the first hitting time

\displaystyle\tau:=\inf\{t\,:\,X^{+}_{t}\in\Gamma\}.

Notice that we may equivalently consider the first hitting time of a Brownian motion on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
started at
x\in\Omega
. We underline that
X^{+}
will be a reference (base) process to start with all over the paper. The symbol

\displaystyle\Phi(\lambda)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(1-e^{-\lambda t}\right)\Pi(dt),\quad\lambda>0

(3.1)

is the Bernstein function (or Lévy symbol) characterizing the subordinator
H
. We also introduce the Caputo-Džrbašjan type derivative defined as the convolution operator

\displaystyle D^{\Phi}_{t}\varphi(t)=(\varphi^{\prime}*\kappa)(t),\quad\alpha\in(0,1)

involving the singular kernel
\kappa
for which

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}\kappa(t)dt=\frac{\Phi(\lambda)}{\lambda},\quad\lambda>0.

It is well-known that
\kappa
can be identified as the tail of a Lévy measure
\Pi
in (
3.1
). In particular,
\kappa(t)=\Pi(t,\infty)
.

We point out that for
\Phi(\lambda)=\lambda^{\alpha}
with
\alpha\in(0,1)
, that is the case of stable subordinators,
D^{\Phi}_{t}=D_{t}^{\alpha}
introduced in (
1.4
).
For a discussion on
H
and
L
we refer to the book
[
1
]
. Here we only provide the result in Theorem
3.1
below for the sake of completeness.

Our discussion in this section will focus on the following facts. The well-known Dynkin’s formula

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{x}[u(X^{+}_{\tau})]=u(x)+\mathbf{E}_{x}[\int_{0}^{\tau}\Delta u(X^{+}_{s})ds]

holds in case
\Omega
is a transient domain (that is
\mathbf{P}_{x}(\tau<\infty)=1
for every
x\in\Omega
) with
\mathbf{E}_{x}[\tau]<\infty
for every
x\in\Omega
. Moreover, we ask for
u
to be continuous and bounded on
\overline{\Omega}
.

If
u
is harmonic, that is
\Delta u=0
in
\Omega
, then (also neglecting the condition
\mathbf{E}_{x}[\tau]<\infty
) the Dynkin’s formula writes
\mathbf{E}_{x}[u(X^{+}_{\tau})]=u(x)
where
\mathbf{P}_{x}(X^{+}_{\tau}\in dy)
plays the role of harmonic measure (or Poisson kernel). In particular,
u
must satisfy the mean value property in
\Omega
and this implies that
u\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
.

If
\Omega
is a regular (that is
\mathbf{P}_{x}(\tau=0)=1
for every
x\in\Gamma
) and transient domain, then every bounded and continuous function on
\Gamma
has a unique, bounded, harmonic extension.

We recall that every bounded domain is transient. In our case, in which
\Gamma
is a smooth boundary, the harmonic measure is absolutely continuous with respect to the surface measure and the Poisson kernel can be explicitly calculated depending on the dimension
N
. It worth noticing what follows. For
N\geq 3
the Brownian motion is transient. For
N=2
the Brownian motion visits a given point with probability zero whereas it visits any neighbourhood of that point with probability one. For
N=1
the Brownian motion visits a point infinitely many times and the corresponding set of hitting times is a perfect set (a large and uncountable set of zero Lebesgue measure, there are no isolated points). We only consider
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
N\geq 2
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let us write
M_{\Phi}(t,\theta)=\mathbf{E}_{0}[\exp(-\theta L_{t})]
,
t>0
,
\theta>0
. Then,
\varphi(t)=M^{0}_{\Phi}(t,\theta)
is the unique solution to

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle D^{\Phi}_{t}\,\varphi(t)=-\theta\,\varphi(t),&t>0,\\
\displaystyle\varphi(0)=1.\end{array}\right.

Proof.

The proof follows by standard arguments. Since
D^{\Phi}_{t}
is a convolution-type operator, we get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}D^{\Phi}_{t}\varphi\,dt=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}\kappa(t)dt\right)\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}\varphi^{\prime}(t)\,dt\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\Phi(\lambda)}{\lambda}(\lambda\tilde{\varphi}(\lambda)-1),\quad\lambda>0.

Our problem leads to

\displaystyle\Phi(\lambda)\,\tilde{\varphi}(\lambda)+\theta\tilde{\varphi}(\lambda)=\frac{\Phi(\lambda)}{\lambda}\quad\textrm{from which}\quad\tilde{\varphi}(\lambda)=\frac{\Phi(\lambda)}{\lambda}\frac{1}{\theta+\Phi(\lambda)}.

We immediately see that

\displaystyle\tilde{\varphi}(\lambda)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\theta s}\frac{\Phi(\lambda)}{\lambda}e^{-s\Phi(\lambda)}ds=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\theta s}\mathbf{P}_{0}(L_{t}\in ds)dt

where the last identity follows from
\mathbf{P}_{0}(L_{t}<s)=\mathbf{P}_{0}(H_{s}>t)
. Thus,
\varphi(t)=\mathbf{E}_{0}[\exp(-\theta L_{t})]
which is continuous on
[0,\infty)
and this implies uniqueness of the inverse Laplace transform.
∎

Further on we consider
\Phi(\lambda)=\lambda^{\alpha}
with
\alpha\in(0,1).
Only our last result is concerned with a general
\Phi
. Moreover, we underline that

\displaystyle-A_{\Lambda}=kA_{DN}+l\Delta_{\Gamma}-\Lambda I\quad\textrm{in case}\quad k\leq 0,\;l\geq 0,\;\Lambda\geq 0

(3.2)

with

\displaystyle D(-A_{\Lambda})\subset C(\Gamma)\cap D(A^{s/2}_{\Lambda})

plays a special role. In particular,
(-A_{0},D(-A_{0}))
where

\displaystyle-A_{0}=kA_{DN}+l\Delta_{\Gamma}\quad\textrm{with}\quad k<0,\;l\geq 0

generates a Markov process on
\Gamma
with right-continuous paths. The negative Dirichlet-Neumann operator
-A_{DN}
is a non-local operator introducing jumps and the Laplace-Beltrami operator
\Delta_{\Gamma}
gives the diffusive part.

Let us denote by
X^{\Gamma}=\{X^{\Gamma}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
the process on
\Gamma
with generator
(-A_{\Lambda},D(-A_{\Lambda}))
. Since
\Omega
is smooth, then there exist a unique linear and bounded trace operator
T:H^{1}(\Omega)\to L^{2}(\Gamma)
such that
Tu=u|_{\Gamma}
. Consider the Dirichlet form

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(Tu,Tv):=\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\nabla v\,dx,\quad u,v\in F:=\{\varphi\in H^{1}(\Omega)\,:\,\Delta\varphi=0\}

(3.3)

with
D(\mathcal{E}):=\{T\varphi\,:\,\varphi\in H^{1}(\Omega)\}
. Thus,
D(\mathcal{E})=H^{1/2}(\Gamma)
is the trace space. There exists a Markov process on
\Gamma
associated with
(\mathcal{E},D(\mathcal{E}))
, see
[
2
, Proposition 3.1]
. In particular, for
l=0
and
\Lambda=0
, we say that
X^{\Gamma}
is the process on
\Gamma
associated with the Dirichlet form
(\mathcal{E},D(\mathcal{E}))
given in (
3.3
) for
k<0
. It is therefore a pure jump process with generator
(-A_{DN},D(-A_{DN}))
up to some scaling introduced by
|k|
. The operator
\Delta_{\Gamma}
is associated with a Brownian diffusion on the compact manifold
\Gamma
. It is well-known that we have a Markov semigroup with compact representation. The conservative part introduced by
\Lambda>0
can be treated in a standard way. For
k,l,\Lambda
as specified in (
3.2
), the Dirichlet form (
2.17
) ensures existence of a stochastic process associated with
(-A_{\Lambda},D(-A_{\Lambda}))
. Moreover, from the theory of Dirichlet form, we have a Markov semigroup.

We now underline a connection with time-changed semigroups. We recall that, for
k\in\mathbb{R}
and
l>0
, the problem

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\Delta w=0&\text{in
$(0,\infty)\times\Omega$,}\\
\dot{w}=k\partial_{\bf n}w+l\Delta_{\Gamma}w&\text{on
$(0,\infty)\times\Gamma$,}\\
w(0,x)=u_{0}(x)&\text{on $\Gamma$,}\end{array}\right.

has been investigated in
[
32
]
where they obtained an analytic semigroup with compact representation. We restrict our analysis in case of non negative eigenvalues of
-A_{0}
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\Lambda=0
,
l>0
,
k\in\mathbb{R}
and
\alpha\in(0,1]
. Let assume
\lambda_{1}\geq 0.
Let
w
be the solution to (
3
). Then, the solution (
2.11
) can be written as

\displaystyle u(t,x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}w(s,x)\mathbf{P}_{0}(L_{t}\in ds)

where

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}\mathbf{P}_{0}(L_{t}\in ds)=E_{\alpha}(-\lambda t^{\alpha}),\quad t\geq 0,\;\lambda\geq 0.

(3.7)

Proof.

It is well-known that (
3.7
) hold true (see
[
3
]
). Indeed,
L
is an inverse to a stable subordinator. Moreover,

\displaystyle E_{\alpha}(-\lambda t^{\alpha})=\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{(-\lambda t^{\alpha})^{k}}{\Gamma(\alpha k+1)}\quad\textrm{becomes}\quad E_{1}(-\lambda t)=e^{-\lambda t}

for
\alpha=1
. From Theorem
2.2
, for
\alpha=1
, the solution
u
takes the form

\displaystyle u(t,x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}<u_{0},\varphi_{n}>e^{-t\lambda_{n}}\psi_{n}(x)

which can be associated with the fact that
L_{t}\to t
a.s. as
\alpha\to 1
(see
[
1
]
). Thus, we conclude that,
\forall\,T>0
, pointwise on
(0,T)\times\overline{\Omega}
,

\displaystyle u=w\quad\textrm{for }\alpha=1

Now we consider
\alpha\in(0,1)
.

The previous result says that

\displaystyle u(t,x)=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}<u_{0},\varphi_{n}>E_{\alpha,1}(-t^{\alpha}\lambda_{n})\psi_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}<u_{0},\varphi_{n}>\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-s\lambda_{n}}\mathbf{P}_{0}(L_{t}\in ds)\right)\psi_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}<u_{0},\varphi_{n}>e^{-s\lambda_{n}}\psi_{n}(x)\right)\mathbf{P}_{0}(L_{t}\in ds)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}w(s,x)\mathbf{P}_{0}(L_{t}\in ds)

which is the claim.
∎

The last result says that the solution
u
is written in terms of a time-changed process.
This is not obvious and will be associated below with the fact that the behaviour of
X^{\Gamma}
on the boundary is not affected from the behaviour on the interior.
More precisely, we now provide the probabilistic interpretation of the Laplace equation with fractional dynamical boundary condition.

We recall that, given a random time
T
(or a stochastic process
T=\{T_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
) and a process
Z=\{Z_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
, we analogously write
Z_{T}
or
Z\circ T
.

Moreover we introduce the process
X^{0,\Gamma}=\{X^{0,\Gamma}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
with generator
(-A_{0},D(-A_{0}))
. In particular, we underline that
X^{\Gamma}=X^{0,\Gamma}
in case
\Lambda=0
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\Lambda=0
,
l\geq 0
,
k<0
and
\alpha\in(0,1]
. The solution to (
2
) has the probabilistic representation

\displaystyle u(t,x)=\mathbf{E}_{x}[\mathbf{E}_{X^{+}_{\tau}}[u_{0}(X^{0,\Gamma}\circ L_{t})]],\quad t>0,\;x\in\overline{\Omega}.

(3.8)

Proof.

Let us consider the solution
u:(0,\infty)\times\overline{\Omega}\to\mathbb{R}
to the problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\Delta u=0,&\textrm{in }(0,\infty)\times\Omega,\\
\displaystyle Tu=v,&\textrm{on }(0,\infty)\times\Gamma.\end{array}\right.

(3.9)

Then
\forall\,t\in(0,\infty)
, the solution
u(t,\cdot)
has the probabilistic representation

\displaystyle u(t,x)=\mathbf{E}_{x}[v(t,X^{+}_{\tau})],\quad t\geq 0,\;x\in\overline{\Omega}.

(3.10)

Now, for
l=0
, we consider the datum
v:(0,\infty)\times\Gamma\to\mathbb{R}
as the solution to the problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle D^{\alpha}_{t}v=kA_{DN}v,&(0,\infty)\times\Gamma\\
\displaystyle v_{0}=u_{0}\in H^{s}(\Gamma)\end{array}\right.

(3.11)

where
-A_{DN}
is the generator of the right-continuous Markov process
X^{0,\Gamma}
on
\Gamma
. Thus, for the fractional Cauchy problem (
3.11
) we have (see for example
[
5
]
)

\displaystyle v(t,x)=\mathbf{E}_{x}[u_{0}(X^{0,\Gamma}\circ L_{t})],\quad t\geq 0,\;x\in\Gamma.

(3.12)

Thus,

\displaystyle u(t,x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq0}}}{{=}}\mathbf{E}_{x}[v(t,X^{+}_{\tau})]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq2}}}{{=}}\mathbf{E}_{x}\left[\mathbf{E}_{X^{+}_{\tau}}[u_{0}(X^{0,\Gamma}\circ L_{t})]\right],\quad t\geq 0,\;x\in\overline{\Omega}.

In case
l>0
, by considering the datum
v:(0,\infty)\times\Gamma\to\mathbb{R}
as the solution to the problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle D^{\alpha}_{t}v=kA_{DN}v+l\Delta_{\Gamma}v,\quad(0,\infty)\times\Gamma\\

\Gamma

. Since

, the solution
u
to the problem (
1

\displaystyle u(t,x)=

where

∎

We now consider the case

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
\Lambda>0
,
l\geq 0
,
k<0
and
\alpha\in(0,1]

\displaystyle u(t,x)=

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{x}\left[\mathbf{E}_{X^{+}_{\tau}}[u_{0}(X^{\Gamma}\circ L_{t})]\right]

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{x}\left[e^{-\Lambda L_{t}}\mathbf{E}_{X^{+}_{\tau}}[u_{0}(X^{0,\Gamma}\circ L_{t})]\right],\quad t\geq 0,\;x\in\overline{\Omega}.

In particular,

\displaystyle u(t,x)=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\Lambda s}\mathbf{E}_{x}\left[\mathbf{E}_{X^{+}_{\tau}}[u_{0}(X^{0,\Gamma}_{s})]\right]\mathbf{P}_{0}(L_{t}\in ds)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\Lambda s}w(s,x)\mathbf{P}_{0}(L_{t}\in ds)

4
General non local conditions

The fractional problem (
1
) can be regarded as special case of the non-local problem

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\Delta u=0&\text{in
$(0,\infty)\times\Omega$,}\\
D_{t}^{\Phi}u=k\partial_{\bf n}u+l\Delta_{\Gamma}u-\Lambda u&\text{on
$(0,\infty)\times\Gamma$,}\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x)&\text{on $\Gamma$.}\end{array}\right.

For the process
L
with symbol
\Phi
we introduce the function

\displaystyle M^{\Lambda}_{\Phi}(t,\lambda):=\mathbf{E}_{0}[\exp-(\Lambda+\lambda)L_{t}],\quad(\lambda+\Lambda)\geq 0,\;\Lambda>0,\;t>0.

Also for this problem we provide the compact representation of the solution to (
4
) which is strictly related with Theorem
2.2
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\Lambda=0
,
l>0
,
k\in\mathbb{R}
and
\alpha\in(0,1]
. Let assume
\lambda_{1}+\Lambda\geq 0.

Then for any

the solution
u
of problem (
4
) is given by

\displaystyle u(t,x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}<u_{0},\varphi_{n}>M^{\Lambda}_{\Phi}(t,\lambda_{n})\psi_{n}(x),\quad t>0,\;x\in\overline{\Omega}

(4.4)

the series being convergent in
C((0,\infty)\times\bar{\Omega})
.

Proof.

First we observe that
\lambda_{n}+\Lambda\geq 0
implies that the (
4.4
) writes

\displaystyle u(t,x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}<u_{0},\varphi_{n}>\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-(\lambda_{n}+\Lambda)s}\mathbf{P}_{0}(L_{t}\in ds)\right)\psi_{n}(x),\quad t>0,\;x\in\overline{\Omega}

(4.5)

and, for a given
\Lambda>0
,
M^{\Lambda}_{\Phi}(t,\lambda_{1})\leq M^{\Lambda}_{\Phi}(t,\lambda_{n})

\forall\,n
. We can use the same arguments as in the proof of Theorem
2.2
together with the following facts: as for

\displaystyle|||D^{\Phi}_{t}u(t,x)|||_{H^{s}(\Gamma)}\leq\sqrt{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|<u_{0},\varphi_{n}>|^{2}|\lambda_{n}+\Lambda|^{2+s}}<\infty

(4.6)

and

\displaystyle|||-A_{\Lambda}u|||_{H^{s}(\Gamma)}\leq|||A_{0}u+\Lambda u|||_{H^{s}(\Gamma)}\leq\sqrt{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|<u_{0},\varphi_{n}>|^{2}|\lambda_{n}+\Lambda|^{2+s}}<\infty

(4.7)

By replacing (

) with

\displaystyle D^{\Phi}_{t}M^{\Lambda}_{\Phi}(t,\lambda)=-(\lambda+\Lambda)M^{\Lambda}_{\Phi}(t,\lambda),\quad M^{\Lambda}_{\Phi}(0,\lambda)=1

(4.8)

we can show that

solves (

Moreover, the series (

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}||<u_{0},\varphi_{n}>M^{\Lambda}_{\Phi}(t,\lambda_{n})\varphi_{n}(x)||^{2}_{C([0,\infty),H^{s}(\Gamma))}<\infty

. ∎

We point out that the probabilistic representation of (

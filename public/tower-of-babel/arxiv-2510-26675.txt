The Penrose singularity theorem, MOTS stability, and horizon topology in weighted spacetimes
Eric Ling, Argam Ohanyan, Eric Woolgar

Abstract
. We consider versions of the Penrose singularity theorem and the Hawking horizon topology theorem in weighted spacetimes that contain weighted versions of trapped surfaces, for arbitrary spacetime dimension and synthetic dimension. We find that suitable generalizations of the unweighted theorems hold under a weighted null energy condition. Our results also provide further evidence in favour of a weighted scalar curvature that differs from the trace of the weighted Ricci curvature. When the synthetic dimension is a positive integer, these weighted curvatures have a natural interpretation in terms of warped product metrics.



1.
Introduction

The major singularity theorems of general relativity due to Penrose and Hawking, described in Hawking–Ellis
[
24
, Chapter 8]
and proved more than 50 years ago, give conditions on the geometry of spacetime that imply some form of causal geodesic incompleteness. This does not provide much information about the nature of spacetime singularities. It leaves open the question of whether such a singularity is mild enough to permit spacetime to be extended past it with perhaps only a slightly loss of smoothness of the differentiabile structure.

Recent work has provided greater insight into these questions. It is now known that some spacetimes cannot be extended, at least not without significant loss of smoothness
[
39
]
. A fascinating development has been the work of Cavalletti–Mondino
[
6
]
, which shows that the Hawking cosmological singularity theorem holds in the very weak setting of a Lorentzian length space that need not have any differentiable structure at all. This theorem uses a “synthetic” version of the strong energy condition, derived from the theory of optimal transport. The techniques used to prove the theorem draw heavily on measure theory. Indeed, when the structure provided by the Lorentzian metric and its associated volume form cannot be assumed, more basic structures are still necessary, including assumed causal relations and reference measures.

To make further progress, a synthetic notion of the null energy condition is desirable. Versions of those have been put forward by McCann
[
29
]
, Ketterer
[
25
]
and Cavalletti–Manini–Mondino
[
7
,
8
]
. Ketterer was able to use the proposed synthetic null energy condition to prove Penrose’s black hole singularity theorem and the Hawking monotonicity formula in the smooth setting, and to obtain results for a weighted family of synthetic conditions known as synthetic Bakry–Émery null energy conditions. Meanwhile, the approach of Cavalletti–Manini–Mondino extends to the synthetic setting, where those authors are able to obtain a version of the Penrose singularity theorem for
C^{0}
-spacetime metrics on smooth manifolds, assuming a Bakry–Émery type null energy condition.

In the setting of smooth manifolds, the Bakry–Émery Ricci curvature can be described as a variant of the familiar Ricci curvature that incorporates the Hessian of a
weight function

f
. Precisely, the smooth
m
-Bakry–Émery Ricci curvature is

(1.1)

\ric^{f,m}:=\ric+\hess f-\frac{1}{m}df\otimes df,

for some
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
and some
m\in{\mathbb{R}}\cup\{\infty\}
, where the
m=0
case is often interpreted as being the case of
f=const
(so
\ric^{f,0}:=\ric
) and
m=\infty
to mean
\ric^{f,\infty}:=\ric+\hess f
(note that
\ric^{f,\infty}=cg
is the gradient Ricci soliton equation). Since the
m=0
case is standard, we will omit it from our considerations in this paper.

Recall that the Penrose singularity theorem uses the notion of a
closed trapped surface
, i.e., a closed surface in which both null expansions are negative. In our setting, we will use the notion of a
closed
f
-trapped surface
in which the null expansions are modified by
f
; see Section
2
. A Penrose-type singularity theorem, assuming nonnegativity of the
m
-Bakry–Émergy Ricci curvature in the
m=\infty
case, was proved by Case
[
5
]
. A version of Galloway’s null splitting theorem
[
14
]
(which can be interpreted as the rigidity of Penrose’s singularity theorem), assuming only a bound on the
m
-Bakry–Émery Ricci curvature along null directions (for any
m
), was proved in
[
42
]
.
But in the classical smooth setting, a version of the Penrose singularity theorem assuming only a bound on the
m
-Bakry–Émery Ricci curvature along null directions (for any
m
) seems not to have appeared.

In this paper we revisit the analysis in the smooth case (also called the classical case) of this condition. First, we give a proof of the
m
-Bakry–Émergy version of the Galloway null splitting theorem of
[
42
]
using a direct argument by utilizing the ellipticity of the Bakry–Émery null mean curvature operator (more closely following Galloway’s argument, rather than the conformal techniques utilized in
[
42
]
).
Then we establish the Penrose singularity theorem in the classical case for (almost) all values of
m
, i.e., we prove the following.

Theorem 1.1

(Bakry–Émery Penrose singularity theorem)
.

Let
(M,g)
be a globally hyperbolic spacetime of dimension
\dim M=n\geq 3
with noncompact Cauchy surfaces,
f\in C^{2}(M)
and
m\in(-\infty,2-n]\cup(0,\infty]
. Suppose that
(M,g)
satisfies the Bakry–Émery null energy condition
\ric^{m}_{f}(\ell,\ell)\geq 0
for all null vectors
\ell\in TM
and contains a compact spacelike codimension-
2
surface
S
that is future
f
-trapped; i.e.,
\theta^{\pm}_{f}|_{S}<0
(where the
\pm
sign denotes the two future-null directions orthogonal to
S
). Then:

i)

If
m>0
then
(M,g)
is future null geodesically incomplete.

ii)

If
m\leq 2-n
or
m=\infty
then
(M,g)
is future null geodesically
f
-incomplete (see Definition
2.3
or
[
42
, Definition 1.3]
), and is geodesically incomplete if
f
is bounded above.

We are also able to prove a one-sided version; see Theorem
3.5
.

The
m=2-n
case of Theorem
1.1
is in fact just the usual Penrose singularity theorem. To see this, observe from formula (
B.7
) of Appendix
B
that the conformal transformation
{\tilde{g}}=e^{-2f/(n-2)}
, together with the replacement of
m=0
by
m=2-n
, preserves the null energy condition in the sense that

(1.2)

\ric_{\tilde{g}}(\ell,\ell)=\ric_{g}^{f,2-n}(\ell,\ell)

for any null vector
\ell
. Now consider the null mean curvature
\theta
defined by a congruence of null geodesics with tangent field
\ell
issuing from a closed trapped surface
\Sigma
. Under this conformal transformation, we have
\theta\mapsto\theta-\nabla_{\ell}f=\theta_{f}
. Now say that
f
is bounded above. Then null geodesic incompleteness implies null geodesic
f
-incompleteness; see Remark
2.4
. The converse is true if
f
is bounded below. In this sense, Theorem
1.1

If the null expansion of a closed surface
\Sigma
defined by the future-outbound null geodesics is identically zero, then we say the surface is
marginally trapped
. It is

if there are no trapped surfaces or marginally trapped surfaces outside of and homologous to

if the first eigenvalue of its stability operator is nonnegative. It’s not hard to see that stability implies outermost. Similarly, there are analogous notions of outermost and stable for
marginally
f
-trapped
surfaces. The second variation formula for marginally
f
-trapped surfaces was studied in
[
38
]
for the
m=\infty
case. There it was found that the constraints on the topology of marginally outer
f
-trapped surfaces that follow in the

case do not follow in the
m=\infty
case unless further restrictive assumptions are made. We revisit this question here, and present the second variation formula for marginally
f
-trapped surfaces for arbitrary
m
. We are able to improve the result of
[
38
]
, both by generalizing in
m
and by using a different version of the weighted dominant energy condition which we call the
m
-Bakry–Émery dominant energy condition
; see equation (
4.7
) and below it for a precise definition. We also correct
[
38
]
by including a term in the calculation that was overlooked in
[
38
]
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\Sigma
be an outermost marginally
f
-trapped hypersurface,
\dim\Sigma\geq 2
, in an initial data set
(V,g|_{V},K)
within a weighted spacetime
(M,g,f)
such that

a)

the
m
-Bakry–Émery dominant energy condition holds for some
m\in(0,\infty]
,

\nabla_{\ell}f|_{\Sigma}=0
.

Then there is a Riemannian metric

on
\Sigma
and function
\tilde{f}\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma)
such that the
m
-Bakry–Émery scalar curvature

(1.3)

R^{\tilde{f},m}(h):=R_{h}+2\Delta_{h}\tilde{f}-\frac{(m+1)}{m}|d\tilde{f}|^{2}_{h},

is positive unless the
m
-Bakry–Émery scalar curvature of the induced metric
g|_{\Sigma}
and induced weight function
f|_{\Sigma}
is identically zero.

This theorem generalizes a well-known theorem of Galloway and Schoen
[
19
]
to the Bakry-Émery setting. As the proof shows, Theorem
1.2
remains true
if in place of the outermost property we assume only stability
for the marginally outer
f
-trapped surface
\Sigma
. The papers
[
16
]
and
[
17
]
explore the rigidity cases of the result in
[
19
]
. We were not able to obtain these rigidity results in the Bakry-Émery setting without assuming unreasonably strong hypotheses on the weight function
f
.

Many remarks are in order.

Remark 1.3
.

The proof we give for Theorem
1.2
also holds for
m\in(-\infty,-\dim\Sigma]\cup(1-\dim\Sigma,0)
.

Remark 1.4
.

R^{f,m}>0
is a nonvacuous constraint.

For
m
positive at least, there are manifolds that do not admit metrics and weight functions
(g,f)
that have positive
R^{f,m}
; see Section
5
for examples. In the
m=0
case (i.e., if

), manifolds with identically zero scalar curvature that do not admit positive scalar curvature are Ricci-flat. We were not able to find an analogous result in the
m
-Bakry–Émery setting.

Remark 1.5
.

Though assumption (b) may be viewed as undesirable in general, we will see in Section
5
that assumption (a) is much weaker than the usual dominant energy condition, so it should not be surprising that an additional assumption is sometimes required.

Remark 1.6
.

When
\dim\Sigma=2
(so the spacetime dimension is
4
) and
f
is constant, the proof of Theorem
1.2
is known to simplify considerably (using the Gauss-Bonnet theorem), but we were unable to find such a simplification here when
f
is nonconstant.

We can apply Theorem
1.2
to obtain analogues of results that are known to hold when
\dim\Sigma=3
and
m=0
(i.e., when
f=const
).
First it will be useful to recall that an
aspherical
manifold is one whose higher homotopy groups
\pi_{k}(M)
,

, are all trivial. It will also be useful to recall the following conjectures.

Conjecture 1.7

(J Rosenberg
[
36
]
)
.

A closed manifold
\Sigma
of dimension
\geq 3
and not equal to
4
admits a positive scalar curvature metric if and only if
\Sigma\times{\mathbb{S}}^{1}
does.

Rosenberg himself found a counter-example in dimension
4
but the conjecture is still open in all higher dimensions. The theorem below relies only on the conjecture holding in the special case where
\Sigma\times{\mathbb{S}}^{1}
admits a
warped product metric
of positive scalar curvature.

Conjecture 1.8

(see
[
9
]
Conjecture 7.28)
.

No closed aspherical manifold admits a positive scalar curvature metric.

This conjecture has been proved in dimensions
4
and
5
by Chodosh and Li
[
10
]
. We can now state the following topological result whose proves relies on
[
10
]
.

Theorem 1.9
.

Let
\Sigma
be a closed orientable prime manifold. If
\dim\Sigma=3
, then, for
0<m\leq 2
, there is a Riemannian metric
g
and function
f
on
\Sigma
with
R^{f,m}(g)>0
if and only if
\Sigma
admits a positive scalar curvature metric. If
\dim\Sigma=2
, then the same statement holds for
0<m\leq 3
.

Remark 1.10
.

1.

Using the results of
[
37
]
on the
{\mathbb{S}}^{1}
-stability conjecture, the assumption that
\Sigma
is prime in Theorem
1.9
can be dropped if the restriction
0<m\leq 1
is imposed.

2.

If Conjecture
1.8
holds, then Theorem
1.9
holds for all
m\in(0,\infty)
.

3.

The orientability assumption on
\Sigma
in Theorem

can be removed provided
\Sigma
contains no two-sided

.

To prove the above assertions, we exploit two facts. The first is that for
k
a positive integer, the
k
-Bakry–Émery scalar curvature

can be related to ordinary (i.e.,
k=0
) scalar curvature of higher dimensional manifolds endowed with warped product metrics. The second is that when
m
is not an integer, we have that

for any integer

. We are also able to give an interpretation of the

-Bakry–Émery dominant energy condition
in terms of warped product spacetimes (in physics terms, a simple class of Kaluza-Klein models). Our results apply in particular to the important special case of compact cross-sections of a static Killing horizon. We can compare this approach to the approach of
[
12
]
, which applies to product metrics without warping and uses the method of topological censorship to obtain constraints on the topology of black hole event horizons (whose compact cross-sections are marginally outer trapped surfaces, if the spacetime is stationary).

This paper is organized as follows. Section

2.
Weighted null mean curvature

2.1.
Definition of weighted null mean curvature

We begin by recalling the null second fundamental form. Let
S\hookrightarrow M
be a null hypersurface and let
p\in S
. One can define a basis
\left\{k,\ell,e_{i};i=1,\dots,n-2\right\}
for
T_{p}M
such that
g(k,k)=g(\ell,\ell)=0
so
k
and
\ell
are future-pointing null;
\ell
is tangent to
S
; the vectors
e_{i}
are tangent to
S
, spacelike, normalized to unit length, and orthogonal to each other and to
k
and
\ell
; and
g(\ell,k)=-1
. Then
\left\{\ell,e_{i};i=1,\dots,n-2\right\}
is a basis for
T_{p}S\subset T_{p}M
. For
X\in T_{p}S
let
\overline{X}\in T_{p}S/\ell
denote the equivalence class of all
X^{\prime}\in T_{p}S
such that
X^{\prime}\sim X
iff
X^{\prime}-X\in\Span\{\ell\}
.

Definition 2.1
.

For any
X\in T_{p}S
, the
tangential null Weingarten map

B_{\ell}:T_{p}S/\ell\to T_{p}S/\ell
is given by

(2.1)

B_{\ell}(X):=\overline{\nabla_{X}\ell}.

There is also a
transverse null Weingarten map
where
\ell
is replaced by
k
. Null Weingarten maps are discussed, for example, by Galloway
[
14
, Section II.1]
.

The trace of a tangential null Weingarten map yields the corresponding null tangential mean curvature.

Definition 2.2
.

[
14
, Equation II.1]

The
tangential null mean curvature
, or
expansion scalar
, of
S
is

(2.2)

\theta:=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n-2}h\left(B_{\ell}({\overline{e}}_{i}),{\overline{e}}_{i}\right)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n-2}g(\nabla_{e_{i}}\ell,e_{i}).

where
h
is the positive-definite inner product on
T_{p}S/\ell
induced from the inner product
g(\cdot,\cdot)
on
T_{p}M
. The
Bakry–Émery tangential null mean curvature
, or
Bakry–Émery expansion scalar
, of
S
is

(2.3)

\theta_{f}:=\theta-\nabla_{\ell}f.

Later in the sequel the
transverse null mean curvature
will be denoted by
\kappa
and the corresponding
Bakry–Émery transverse null mean curvature
will be denoted by
\kappa_{f}
.

2.2.
The Raychaudhuri equation

The definition of
\theta_{f}
is motivated by the following calculation. Consider a smooth spacelike hypersurface
P
intersecting the null hypersurface
S
and let
\Sigma=S\cap P
. Then
\Sigma
has null second fundamental form
B
in
S
, governed by the familiar Raychaudhuri equation. Let
\tf B:=B-\frac{1}{(n-2)}\theta h
denote the tracefree part of
B
and let
\theta:=\tr B
be the null mean curvature. We may choose the basis null vector field
\ell
such that it is tangent to an affinely parametrized null geodesic congruence generating
S
(and parametrized by
t
). Then the Raychaudhuri equation yields

(2.4)

\frac{d\theta}{dt}=-\ric(\ell,\ell)-|B|^{2}.

It can be re-expressed in terms of
\theta_{f}
and the Bakry–Émery Ricci curvature

(2.5)

\ric_{f}^{m}:=\ric+\hess f-\frac{1}{m}df\otimes df.

Using that the null generators
\gamma
of
S
are geodesics with tangent vector field
\ell=\frac{d}{dt}
for
t
an affine parameter along each generator, then

(2.6)

\begin{split}\frac{d\theta_{f}}{dt}=&\,-\ric(\ell,\ell)-\hess f(\ell,\ell)-|B|^{2}\\
=&\,-\ric_{f}^{m}(\ell,\ell)-\frac{1}{m}\left((f\circ\gamma)^{\prime}\right)^{2}-|\tf B|^{2}-\frac{1}{n-2}\theta^{2}\\
=&\,-\ric_{f}^{m}(\ell,\ell)-\left(\frac{1}{m}-\frac{1}{n-2}\right)\left((f\circ\gamma)^{\prime}\right)^{2}-|\tf B|^{2}-\frac{\theta_{f}^{2}}{n-2}\\
&\,-\frac{2\theta(f\circ\gamma)^{\prime}}{n-2}\\
=&\,-\ric_{f}^{m}(\ell,\ell)-\left(\frac{1}{m}+\frac{1}{n-2}\right)\left((f\circ\gamma)^{\prime}\right)^{2}-|\tf B|^{2}-\frac{\theta_{f}^{2}}{n-2}\\
&\,-\frac{2\theta_{f}(f\circ\gamma)^{\prime}}{n-2}.\end{split}

If
m>0
(and
n>2
, of course), we use the identity

(2.7)

\frac{\theta^{2}}{n-2}+\frac{\left((f\circ\gamma)^{\prime}\right)^{2}}{m}\geq\frac{\left(\theta-(f\circ\gamma)^{\prime}\right)^{2}}{n+m-2}\equiv\frac{\theta_{f}^{2}}{n+m-2}.

Then (
2.6
) quickly simplifies to yield

(2.8)

\begin{split}\frac{d\theta_{f}}{dt}=&\,-\ric_{f}^{m}(\ell,\ell)-\frac{1}{m}\left((f\circ\gamma)^{\prime}\right)^{2}-|\tf B|^{2}-\frac{1}{n-2}\theta^{2}\\
\leq&\,-\frac{\theta_{f}^{2}}{n+m-2}.\end{split}

If we cannot assume that
m>0
, (
2.6
) implies that

(2.9)

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\left(e^{\frac{2f\circ\gamma}{n-2}}\theta_{f}\right)=&\,-e^{\frac{2f\circ\gamma}{n-2}}\left[\ric_{f}^{m}(\ell,\ell)+\frac{(n+m-2)}{m(n-2)}\left((f\circ\gamma)^{\prime}\right)^{2}\right.\\
&\,\left.+|\tf B|^{2}+\frac{\theta_{f}^{2}}{n-2}\right].\end{split}

Define
x_{f}:=e^{\frac{2f\circ\gamma}{n-2}}\theta_{f}
. Then we obtain

(2.10)

\frac{dx_{f}}{dt}\leq-e^{\frac{2f\circ\gamma}{n-2}}\ric_{f}^{m}(\ell,\ell)-e^{\frac{-2f\circ\gamma}{n-2}}\frac{x_{f}^{2}}{n-2}

whenever either
m>0
or
m\leq 2-n
, with equality if and only if
\tf B=0
and either
(f\circ\gamma)^{\prime}=0
or
m=2-n
. When
\ric_{f}^{m}(\ell,\ell)\geq 0
, then

(2.11)

\frac{dx_{f}}{dt}\leq-\frac{x_{f}^{2}e^{\frac{-2f\circ\gamma}{n-2}}}{n-2}.

Now define a new parameter
\tau
along the null geodesic generators
\gamma(t)
of
S
by

(2.12)

\tau:=\int\limits_{0}^{t}e^{-2f\circ\gamma(t^{\prime})/(n-2)}dt^{\prime}.

Then (
2.11
) becomes

(2.13)

\frac{dx_{f}}{d\tau}\leq-\frac{x_{f}^{2}}{n-2},\quad x_{f}:=e^{\frac{2f\circ\gamma}{n-2}}\theta_{f},

whenever
m\leq 2-n
(or
m\in(0,\infty]
).

When
f\leq C
, inequality (
2.13
) can be used to control
\theta_{f}
for
m\in(-\infty,n-2]
or
m=\infty
(i.e., when coefficients of
\frac{1}{m}
are replaced by
0
). Inequality (
2.13
) also applies when
m\in(0,\infty)
, but then (
2.8
) gives better control and does so without needing boundedness of
f
.

Definition 2.3
.

We say that an inextendible geodesic is
future
f
-complete
if there is a
\tau_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}
(or possibly
\tau_{0}=-\infty
) such that the geodesic is defined for all
\tau\geq\tau_{0}
, where
\tau
is defined by (
2.12
). If the domain of
\tau
is bounded above, we will say that the inextendible geodesic is
future
f
-incomplete
. Past
f
-complete and past
f
-incomplete are defined dually. A geodesic that is both future and past

-complete
. An

-complete achronal null curve is a

-line
.

Remark 2.4
.

If

is bounded above on

then geodesic completeness implies

-completeness and

-incompleteness implies geodesic incompleteness.

2.3.
Ellipticity of the weighted null mean curvature

A brief calculation of Galloway
[
14
]
shows that
\theta
is elliptic in the following sense. Following
[
14
, pp 547–548]
, we now take
P
to be a timelike hypersurface intersecting the null hypersurface
S
, and again let
\Sigma
be the intersection surface
\Sigma:=P\cap S
, and let
p\in\Sigma
. We also choose a smooth spacelike hypersurface
V\hookrightarrow P
(i.e.,
V
is a codimension-two spacelike surface in spacetime) intersecting
\Sigma
such that
p\in V\cap\Sigma
. Now
\Sigma
can be expressed, locally near
p
at least, as a graph over
V
. In Gaussian coordinates
(t,x^{1},\dots,x^{n-2})
for
P
based at
V
(so that
V
is the
t=0
locus and
\frac{\partial}{\partial t}
is future timelike), we write

(2.14)

\Sigma=\graph u=\left\{(u(x),x)\in P;x:=(x^{1},\dots,x^{n-2})\in V\right\}.

Using this construction, Galloway is able to show first that the mean curvature of
\Sigma
in
P
is elliptic and then that the null mean curvature
\theta
of
\Sigma
in
S
is elliptic on functions
u\in C^{\infty}(V)
. Specifically,

(2.15)

\theta=\theta(u)=\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{n-2}a^{ij}(x,u,\partial u)\partial_{ij}u+b(x,u,\partial u),

where the matrix
\left[a^{ij}\right]
is positive-definite.

It remains to verify that
\nabla_{\ell}f
does not depend on derivatives of
u
beyond first derivatives, which amounts to verifying that
\ell
does not depend on derivatives of
u
beyond first derivatives. Since
\Sigma
is a codimension-two surface in spacetime, there are exactly two future-null directions spanned by vectors orthogonal to
\Sigma
. Extend the coordinate system above to a neighbourhood of
V
(near
p
) by, say, a Gaussian normal coordinate
x^{n-1}
based at
P
(so
P
is the locus
x^{n-1}=0
). At any
q\in\Sigma
, a spacetime vector
v
is orthogonal to
\Sigma
if and only if

(2.16)

\left\langle v,dS\right\rangle=0,\quad dS=dt-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n-2}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x^{i}}dx^{i}.

This condition depends only on
u
and its first derivatives. Then the condition that
v
must be null, depending only on the spacetime metric, cannot depend on higher derivatives of
u
either. These two conditions determine
\ell
up to a discrete choice (which cannot depend continuously on u or its derivatives) and an overall scale

. But since integral curves of

meet
\Sigma
only once, say at

is simply initial data for the scale factor along that integral curve. The initial value can depend on the coordinates

, but not on derivatives of

\ell=\ell(u,\partial u)
and so
\theta_{f}:=\theta-\nabla_{\ell}f
is an elliptic operator on
C^{\infty}(V)
.

4.1.
Stability formula

The stability operator for marginally trapped surfaces was written down (for 4-dimensional spacetimes) in
[
31
]
, following a method used to study minimal surfaces in Riemannian manifolds. The basic technique had already been employed by Hawking (see, e.g.,
[
24
, Proposition 9.3.2]
), and has been followed by other authors since, such as
[
1
]
and
[
19
]
. An
m=\infty
Bakry–Émery version of the technique appears in
[
38
]
. Here we recast that work and extend it to all positive
m
-values (and also correct an oversight in the argument in
[
38
]
). We point the reader to the clear and general discussion in
[
27
, Section 7.5]
(for the non-Bakry–Émery case), whose notation we will follow.

We begin with an initial data hypersurface
(V,g_{V},K)
in spacetime. Here
g_{V}
and
K
are, respectively, the first and second fundamental forms induced on the hypersurface
V\hookrightarrow M
by the spacetime metric
g
on
M
. In
V
, we define a closed two-sided hypersurface
\Sigma
(hence
\Sigma
is codimension-
2
in
M
) with first and second fundamental forms
h
and
A
induced by the inclusion
\Sigma\hookrightarrow V
. We denote the mean curvature of
\Sigma\hookrightarrow V
as
H:=\tr_{h}A=:\tr_{\Sigma}A
. We will also use the notation
\tr_{\Sigma}K:=h^{ij}K_{ij}
to denote the
h
-partial trace of
K
. Let
w
denote the unit future directed normal field to
V
and
\nu
the ‘outward’ unit normal field to
\Sigma
and tangent to
V
. Define
\ell=w+\nu
and
k=w-\nu
, which are
outbound
and
inbound
null vector fields on
\Sigma
, respectively. The first fundamental form, or induced metric, on
\Sigma
can be written as

(4.1)

h_{\mu\nu}=g_{\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{2}\left(k_{\mu}\ell_{\nu}+\ell_{\mu}k_{\nu}\right).

The null second fundamental form defined by
\ell
is

(4.2)

\theta:=\divergence_{\Sigma}\ell=h^{ij}\nabla_{i}\ell_{j},\quad\ell_{j}:=g_{ij}\ell^{i}.

We also define

(4.3)

\kappa:=\divergence_{\Sigma}k=h^{ij}\nabla_{i}k_{j},\quad k_{j}:=g_{ji}k^{i}.

Now consider the variation vector field
\varphi\nu
for
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma)
. This generates a variation
\Sigma_{t}
of
\Sigma
within
V
via
x\mapsto\exp_{x}\left(t\varphi(x)\nu(x)\right)
, where
\exp
denotes the exponential map of the metric on
V
. To simplify notation, we suppress the inclusion maps
\Sigma\hookrightarrow V
,
V\hookrightarrow M
,
\Sigma\hookrightarrow M
, and the induced pullbacks. We also ignore a possible component of the variation tangent to
\Sigma
. We extend
\ell
in a neighborhood of
\Sigma
within
V
by defining
\ell=w+\nu
, where
\nu
is determined by the variation. Then the linearization of
\theta
(its derivative along any extension of the vector field
\varphi\nu
, evaluated at
\Sigma
) is given by
[
27
, Proposition 7.32]
as

(4.4)

\begin{split}D\theta=&\,-\Delta_{\Sigma}\varphi+2W_{\Sigma}\cdot\nabla_{\Sigma}\varphi+\left[\divergence_{\Sigma}W_{\Sigma}-\left|W_{\Sigma}\right|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}R_{\Sigma}\right.\\
&\,\left.-G(w,\ell)-\frac{1}{2}\left|K_{\Sigma}+A\right|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\theta\left(\kappa+2K(\nu,\nu)\right)\right]\varphi.\end{split}

To lessen the burden of notation, we leave implicit the fact that each term in this expression is evaluated on
\Sigma
. Here
W_{\Sigma}
is the tangential vector field on
\Sigma
that is dual to the 1-form
K(\nu,\cdot)
along
\Sigma
,

G
denotes the Einstein tensor of the spacetime metric, and
K_{\Sigma}
is the restriction of
K
to
T_{p}\Sigma
for
p\in\Sigma
(likewise
W_{\Sigma}
, whereas
R_{\Sigma}
is the scalar curvature of the induced metric
h
on
\Sigma
). Expression (
4.4
) gives the linearized expansion
\theta
for any spacelike codimension-
2
surface
\Sigma
.

We now add

-terms to both sides of (
4.4
). On the left-hand side we use

(4.5)

\begin{split}D\theta_{f}=&\,D\theta-\varphi\nabla_{\nu}\nabla_{\ell}f=D\theta-\left[\hess f(\nu,\ell)+\nabla_{\nu}\ell\cdot\nabla f\right]\varphi.\end{split}

To simplify the right-hand side, we define

(4.6)

R^{f,m}:=R+2\Box f-\frac{(m+1)}{m}|\nabla f|^{2}

and

(4.7)

G_{\mu\nu}^{f,m}:=R_{\mu\nu}^{f,m}-\frac{1}{2}R^{f,m}g_{\mu\nu}.

If
G^{f,m}(u,v)\geq 0
for all future-causal vectors
u
and
v
and some function
f
and number
m
, we will say that the
m
-Bakry–Émery dominant energy condition holds
. Note that
R^{f,m}
is not the trace of
\ric^{f,m}
(except when

).

Next, we collect some of the expressions we will need. First, using
\ric=\ric^{f,m}-\hess f+\frac{1}{m}df\otimes df
and choosing

, we get that

(4.8)

\ric(w,\ell)=\ric^{f,m}(w,\ell)-\frac{1}{2}\hess f(k,\ell)-\frac{1}{2}\hess(\ell,\ell)+\frac{1}{2m}\nabla_{k}f\nabla_{\ell}f+\frac{1}{2m}\left(\nabla_{\ell}f\right)^{2}.

Next, we expand
\Box f
using

(4.9)

\Box f=\Delta_{\Sigma}f-\left(\hess f\right)(k,\ell).

This allows us to write that

(4.10)

\begin{split}R=&\,R^{f,m}-2\Box f+\frac{(m+1)}{m}|\nabla f|^{2}\\
=&\,R^{f,m}-2\Delta_{\Sigma}f+2\hess f(k,\ell)-\frac{(m+1)}{m}\nabla_{k}f\nabla_{\ell}f+\frac{(m+1)}{m}\left|\nabla_{\Sigma}f\right|^{2}.\end{split}

Using (
4.8
) and (

), then

(4.11)

\begin{split}G(w,\ell)=&\,\ric(w,\ell)-\frac{1}{2}Rg(w,\ell)=\ric(w,\ell)+\frac{1}{2}R\\
=&\,G^{f,m}(w,\ell)+\frac{1}{2}\hess f(k-\ell,\ell)-\Delta_{\Sigma}f-\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{k}f\nabla_{\ell}f+\frac{1}{2m}\left(\nabla_{\ell}f\right)^{2}\\
&\,+\frac{(m+1)}{2m}\left|\nabla_{\Sigma}f\right|^{2}.\end{split}

Then (
4.4
), (
4.5
), and (
4.11
) yield

(4.12)

\begin{split}D\theta_{f}=&\,-\Delta_{\Sigma}\varphi+2W_{\Sigma}\cdot\nabla_{\Sigma}\varphi+\bigg[\divergence_{\Sigma}W_{\Sigma}-\left|W_{\Sigma}\right|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}R_{\Sigma}\\
&\,-G^{f,m}(w,\ell)-\frac{1}{2}\hess f(k-\ell,\ell)+\Delta_{\Sigma}f+\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{k}f\nabla_{\ell}f-\frac{1}{2m}\left(\nabla_{\ell}f\right)^{2}\\
&\,\left.-\frac{(m+1)}{2m}\left|\nabla_{\Sigma}f\right|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\left|K_{\Sigma}+A\right|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\theta\left(\kappa+2K(\nu,\nu)\right)\right.\\
&\,-\hess f(\nu,\ell)-\nabla_{\nu}\ell\cdot\nabla f\bigg]\varphi,\end{split}

where once again each term in this expression is intended to be evaluated on
\Sigma
. Using

(4.13)

R^{f,m}_{\Sigma}:=R_{\Sigma}+2\Delta_{\Sigma}f-\frac{(m+1)}{m}\left|\nabla_{\Sigma}f\right|^{2},

and recognizing that the two Hessian terms cancel in (

), we obtain

(4.14)

\begin{split}D\theta_{f}=&\,-\Delta_{\Sigma}\varphi+2W_{\Sigma}\cdot\nabla_{\Sigma}\varphi+\bigg[\divergence_{\Sigma}W_{\Sigma}-\left|W_{\Sigma}\right|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}R_{\Sigma}^{f,m}\\
&\,-G^{f,m}(w,\ell)+\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{k}f\nabla_{\ell}f-\frac{1}{2m}\left(\nabla_{\ell}f\right)^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\left|K_{\Sigma}+A\right|^{2}\\
&\,+\frac{1}{2}\theta\left(\kappa+2K(\nu,\nu)\right)-\nabla_{\nu}\ell\cdot\nabla f\bigg]\varphi.\end{split}

Following
[
17
]
, we write the mean curvature of the hypersurface
V
as

(4.15)

\tau:=\tr_{V}K=\frac{1}{2}\left(\theta+\kappa\right)+K(\nu,\nu),

which allows us to rewrite
\kappa+2K(\nu,\nu)
as

(4.16)

\begin{split}\kappa+2K(\nu,\nu)=&\,2\tau-\theta\\
=&\,2\tau-\theta_{f}-\nabla_{\ell}f.\end{split}

We have

(4.17)

\begin{split}D\theta_{f}=&\,-\Delta_{\Sigma}\varphi+2W_{\Sigma}\cdot\nabla_{\Sigma}\varphi+\bigg[\divergence_{\Sigma}W_{\Sigma}-\left|W_{\Sigma}\right|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}R_{\Sigma}^{f,m}\\
&\,-G^{f,m}(w,\ell)-\frac{1}{2}\left|K_{\Sigma}+A\right|^{2}+\theta_{f}\left(\tau-\frac{1}{2}\theta_{f}-\nabla_{\ell}f\right)\\
&\,+\left(\tau+\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{k}f-\frac{(m+1)}{2m}\nabla_{\ell}f\right)\nabla_{\ell}f-\nabla_{\nu}\ell\cdot\nabla f\bigg]\varphi.\end{split}

Now we assume that
\Sigma
is
f
-minimal; i.e.,
\theta_{f}=0
, and that
\theta_{f}
is stable (at linear order) against perturbations of
\Sigma
(since its outermost by assumption). That is, we require that for any smooth function
\varphi:\Sigma\to{\mathbb{R}}
, we have

(4.18)

\begin{split}0\leq&\,L^{f}\varphi\\
:=&\,-\Delta_{\Sigma}\varphi+2W_{\Sigma}\cdot\nabla_{\Sigma}\varphi+\bigg[\divergence_{\Sigma}W_{\Sigma}-\left|W_{\Sigma}\right|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}R_{\Sigma}^{f,m}\\
&\,-G^{f,m}(w,\ell)-\frac{1}{2}\left|K_{\Sigma}+A\right|^{2}+\left(\tau+\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{k}f-\frac{(m+1)}{2m}\nabla_{\ell}f\right)\nabla_{\ell}f\\
&\,-\nabla_{\nu}\ell\cdot\nabla f\bigg]\varphi.\end{split}

4.2.
A special case

Now we further assume that

(4.19)

\nabla_{\ell}f|_{\Sigma}=0.

See Section
5
and Lemma
5.5
for a discussion of this assumption. Then (
4.17
) reduces to

(4.20)

\begin{split}0\leq&\,-\Delta_{\Sigma}\varphi+Df\cdot D\varphi-\varphi K(Df,\nu)+2W_{\Sigma}\cdot D\varphi+\bigg[\divergence_{\Sigma}W_{\Sigma}-\left|W_{\Sigma}\right|^{2}\\
&\,+\frac{1}{2}R_{\Sigma}^{f,m}-G^{f,m}(w,\ell)-\frac{1}{2}\left|K_{\Sigma}+A\right|^{2}\bigg]\varphi,\end{split}

where we used Lemma
C.1
in Appendix
C
to replace the
-\nabla_{\nu}\ell\cdot\nabla f
term . Also, we let
D:=\nabla_{\Sigma}
to denote the gradient with respect to the metric on
\Sigma
(since
D
is no longer used to denote the variation of
\theta_{f}
).

From here on, the calculation is almost standard. We will eliminate the first-derivative term
2\left\langle W_{\Sigma},D\varphi\right\rangle_{\Sigma}
in favour of a term with definite sign. To do this, write
\varphi=e^{u}
. Then

(4.21)

\begin{split}&\,-\Delta_{\Sigma}\varphi+Df\cdot D\varphi+2W_{\Sigma}\cdot D\varphi\\
=&\,e^{u}\left[-\Delta_{\Sigma}u+Df\cdot Du-\left|W_{\Sigma}-Du\right|^{2}+\left|W_{\Sigma}\right|^{2}\right],\end{split}

so (
4.20

\begin{split}0\leq&\,-\Delta_{\Sigma}u+Df\cdot Du-K(Df,\nu)+\divergence_{\Sigma}W_{\Sigma}-\left|W_{\Sigma}-Du\right|^{2}+Q\\
=&\,\divergence_{\Sigma}\left(W_{\Sigma}-Du\right)+Du\cdot Df-K(Df,\nu)-\left|W_{\Sigma}-Du\right|^{2}+Q,\end{split}

where

(4.23)

Q:=\frac{1}{2}R_{\Sigma}^{f,m}-G_{\mu\nu}^{f,m}\ell^{\mu}w^{\nu}-\frac{1}{2}\left|K_{\Sigma}+A\right|^{2}.

Multiplying (
4.22
) by
\psi^{2}
for some

, we obtain

(4.24)

\begin{split}0\leq&\,\divergence_{\Sigma}\left(\psi^{2}\left(W_{\Sigma}-Du\right)\right)-2\psi\left(W_{\Sigma}-Du\right)\cdot D\psi+\psi^{2}Du\cdot Df\\
&\,-\psi^{2}K(Df,\nu)-\left|W_{\Sigma}-Du\right|^{2}_{\Sigma}\psi^{2}+Q\psi^{2}\\
\leq&\,\divergence_{\Sigma}\left(\psi^{2}\left(W_{\Sigma}-Du\right)\right)+2|\psi|\left|W_{\Sigma}-Du\right||D\psi|+\psi^{2}Du\cdot Df\\
&\,-\psi^{2}K(Df,\nu)-\left|W_{\Sigma}-Du\right|^{2}_{\Sigma}\psi^{2}+Q\psi^{2}\\
=&\,\divergence_{\Sigma}\left(\psi^{2}\left(W_{\Sigma}-Du\right)\right)-\left(|D\psi|-\left|W_{\Sigma}-Du\right||\psi|\right)^{2}\\
&\,+\psi^{2}Du\cdot Df-\psi^{2}K(Df,\nu)+|D\psi|^{2}+Q\psi^{2}\\
\leq&\,\divergence_{\Sigma}\left(\psi^{2}\left(W_{\Sigma}-Du\right)\right)+\psi^{2}Du\cdot Df-\psi^{2}K(Df,\nu)+|D\psi|^{2}+Q\psi^{2}\\
=&\,\divergence_{\Sigma,f}\left(\psi^{2}\left(W_{\Sigma}-Du\right)\right)+|D\psi|^{2}+Q\psi^{2},\end{split}

where
\divergence_{\Sigma,f}(X):=\divergence_{\Sigma}(X)-Df\cdot X
for vector fields
X
on
\Sigma
. In the last equality, we used the fact that
K(Df,\nu)=Df\cdot W_{\Sigma}
(which follows immediately from the definition of

).

Multiplying (
4.24
) by

and integrating over

, we obtain

\begin{split}\int_{\Sigma}(|D\psi|^{2}+Q\psi^{2})e^{-f}dV_{\Sigma}&\geq 0.\end{split}

Recall that
\Delta_{\Sigma,f}\psi=\divergence_{\Sigma,f}(D\psi)=\divergence_{\Sigma}(D\psi)-Df\cdot D\psi
. Consider the weighted eigenvalue problem:

(4.26)

\begin{split}\mu\psi=&\,-\Delta_{\Sigma,f}\psi+Q\psi\\
1=&\,\int_{\Sigma}\psi^{2}e^{-f}dV_{\Sigma},\end{split}

on a compact manifold
\Sigma
. The Rayleigh quotient for (
4.26
) is given by the left-hand-side of (
4.25
). Hence we have the following result.

Lemma 4.1
.

The principal eigenvalue
\mu_{1}
for the problem (
4.26
) is nonnegative.

We can now prove our main results. The proofs follow closely the proof in
[
19
]

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Now set
n:=\dim\Sigma\geq 2
. Let
\psi_{1}>0
be an eigenfunction for the principal eigenvalue
\mu_{1}
of (
4.26
). By standard theory (see e.g.
[
27
, Thm. A.10]
), we can choose
\psi_{1}>0
. The conformal transformation

(4.27)

{\tilde{h}}=\psi_{1}^{2p}h,

applied to the metric
h
on
\Sigma
, produces a metric
{\tilde{h}}
whose scalar curvature is

(4.28)

R_{\Sigma}(\tilde{h})=\psi_{1}^{-2p}\left\{R_{\Sigma}(h)-2(n-1)p\frac{\Delta_{h}\psi_{1}}{\psi_{1}}+(n-1)p\left[2-(n-2)p\right]\frac{\left|D\psi_{1}\right|^{2}_{h}}{\psi_{1}^{2}}\right\}.

Here we have replaced the notation
\Delta_{\Sigma}
by
\Delta_{h}
to distinguish the Laplacian on
\Sigma
defined by
h
from that defined by
{\tilde{h}}
. Using its conformal transformation property, we can write the scalar Laplacian
\Delta_{\tilde{h}}
associated to
{\tilde{h}}
in terms of
\Delta_{h}
, yielding

(4.29)

\Delta_{\tilde{h}}f=\psi_{1}^{-2p}\left[\Delta_{h}f+(n-2)p\frac{h(D\psi_{1},Df)}{\psi_{1}}\right].

Finally, we replace
f
by

(4.30)

{\tilde{f}}:=f+k\log\psi_{1},

so that

(4.31)

\begin{split}\Delta_{\tilde{h}}{\tilde{f}}=&\,\Delta_{\tilde{h}}f+k\left\{\frac{\Delta_{\tilde{h}}\psi_{1}}{\psi_{1}}-\frac{\left|\tilde{D}\psi_{1}\right|_{\tilde{h}}^{2}}{\psi_{1}^{2}}\right\}\\
=&\,\psi_{1}^{-2p}\left\{\Delta_{h}f+(n-2)p\frac{h(D\psi_{1},Df)}{\psi_{1}}+k\frac{\Delta_{h}\psi_{1}}{\psi_{1}}\right.\\
&\,\left.+k\left[(n-2)p-1\right]\frac{\left|D\psi_{1}\right|_{h}^{2}}{\psi_{1}^{2}}\right\}\end{split}

Putting things together, we obtain

(4.32)

\begin{split}R_{\Sigma}^{{\tilde{f}},m}({\tilde{h}}):=&\,R_{\Sigma}({\tilde{h}})+2\Delta_{\tilde{h}}{\tilde{f}}-\frac{(m+1)}{m}\left|\tilde{D}{\tilde{f}}\right|_{\tilde{h}}^{2}\\
=&\,\psi_{1}^{-2p}\left\{R_{\Sigma}(h)-2(n-1)p\frac{\Delta_{h}\psi_{1}}{\psi_{1}}+(n-1)p\left[2-(n-2)p\right]\frac{\left|D\psi_{1}\right|_{h}^{2}}{\psi_{1}^{2}}\right.\\
&\,\left.+2\Delta_{h}f+2(n-2)p\frac{h(D\psi_{1},Df)}{\psi_{1}}+2k\frac{\Delta_{h}\psi_{1}}{\psi_{1}}\right.\\
&\,\left.+2k\left[(n-2)p-1\right]\frac{\left|D\psi_{1}\right|_{h}^{2}}{\psi_{1}^{2}}\right.\\
&\,\left.-\frac{(m+1)}{m}\left[|Df|_{h}^{2}+2k\frac{h(Df,D\psi_{1})}{\psi_{1}}+k^{2}\frac{\left|D\psi_{1}\right|_{h}^{2}}{\psi_{1}^{2}}\right]\right\}.\end{split}

Now simultaneously solve for
p
and

(4.33)

\begin{split}1=&\,(n-1)p-k,\\
1=&\,(n-2)p-\frac{(m+1)}{m}k\end{split}

to obtain a coefficient of
-2
for the
\frac{\Delta_{h}\psi_{1}}{\psi_{1}}
term and
+2
for
\frac{h(Df,D\psi_{1})}{\psi_{1}}
term. We obtain

(4.34)

\begin{split}p=&\,\frac{1}{n+m-1},\\
k=&\,-\frac{m}{n+m-1}.\end{split}

Then those two terms combine to give
-\frac{2}{\psi_{1}}\Delta_{h,f}\psi_{1}
.
Therefore

(4.35)

\begin{split}R_{\Sigma}^{{\tilde{f}},m}({\tilde{h}})=&\,\psi_{1}^{-2p}\left\{R_{\Sigma}(h)+2\Delta_{h}f-\frac{(m+1)}{m}|Df|_{h}^{2}-2\frac{\Delta_{h,f}\psi_{1}}{\psi_{1}}\right.\\
&\,\left.+\left(\frac{n+m}{n+m-1}\right)\frac{\left|D\psi_{1}\right|_{h}^{2}}{\psi_{1}^{2}}\right\}\\
=&\,\psi_{1}^{-2p}\left\{R_{\Sigma}^{f,m}(h)-2\frac{\Delta_{h,f}\psi_{1}}{\psi_{1}}+\left(\frac{n+m}{n+m-1}\right)\frac{\left|D\psi_{1}\right|_{h}^{2}}{\psi_{1}^{2}}\right\}\\
=&\,\psi_{1}^{-2p}\left\{R_{\Sigma}^{f,m}(h)+2\mu_{1}-2Q+\left(\frac{n+m}{n+m-1}\right)\frac{\left|D\psi_{1}\right|_{h}^{2}}{\psi_{1}^{2}}\right\}\\
\geq&\,\psi_{1}^{-2p}\left\{2\mu_{1}+\left(\frac{n+m}{n+m-1}\right)\frac{\left|D\psi_{1}\right|_{h}^{2}}{\psi_{1}^{2}}\right\}\\
\geq&0.\end{split}

We used (
4.26
) to get the third equality. The first inequality follows from (
4.23
). The second inequality follows since
\mu_{1}\geq 0
by Lemma
4.1
.
∎

5.
Warped products

5.1.
The proof of Theorem
1.9

Consider the warped product metric

(5.1)

\gamma=g\oplus e^{-2f/m}h,

on the manifold
N=M\times S
, where
g
is a Lorentzian metric on an
n
-dimensional spacetime
M
,
h
is a Riemannian metric on the compact
m
-manifold
S
, and
f:M\to{\mathbb{R}}
. More precisely, let
\iota:M\to N
and
\zeta:S\to N
be inclusions such that
p=\iota(q)=\zeta(r)\in N
. Then the decomposition
T_{p}N=\iota_{*}T_{q}M\oplus\zeta_{*}T_{r}S
is orthogonal, with
\iota_{*}T_{q}M
in the kernel of
h
and
\zeta_{*}T_{r}S
in the kernel of
g
. We use
\oplus
to emphasize the direct sum (sometimes called the direct product) where it seems helpful to do so, but we will usually suppress inclusions (and their pullbacks/pushforwards) to lessen the burden of notation. Note that
(M,g)
is totally geodesic in
(N,\gamma)
, while
(S,h)
is umbilic.

Warped products occur in the physics of Kaluza-Klein type compactifications, where the weighting
f
represents a dilaton (perhaps after field redefinition). In the warped product formulation, the electromagnetic field would appear in the stress-energy tensor and be subject to any assumed energy conditions. (In a full implementation of the Kaluza-Klein idea, electromagnetism would be incorporated by twisting
\gamma
, which would yield equations for the electromagnetic field, making energy conditions redundant.)

A standard calculation yields

(5.2)

\begin{split}\ric(N,\gamma)=&\,\ric^{f,m}(M,g)\oplus\left\{\ric(S,h)+\frac{e^{-2f/m}}{m}\left[\square_{g}f-|\nabla f|_{g}^{2}\right]h\right\},\\
=&\,\ric^{f,m}(M,g)\oplus\left\{\ric(S,h)+\frac{e^{-2f/m}}{m}\left(\square_{g,f}f\right)h\right\},\\
R(N,\gamma)=&\,R^{f,m}(M,g)+e^{2f/m}R(S,h),\end{split}

where
\ric(S,h)
and
R(S,h)
are taken to be zero if
\dim S=1
, and
\square_{g,f}f
denotes the
drift Laplacian
defined by

(5.3)

\square_{g,f}u=\square_{g}u-g(\nabla f,\nabla u).

Recalling Theorem
1.2
, we make the following observation.

Remark 5.1
.

If the
m
-Bakry–Émery scalar curvature
R^{f,m}(M,g)
of the base
(M,g)
and the scalar curvature
R(S,h)
of the fibre are nonnegative, then the scalar curvature
R(N,\gamma)
of the total space is nonnegative. If one of the former is positive and the other is nonnegative, then the scalar curvature
R(N,\gamma)
of the total space is positive.

Proof of Theorem
1.9
.

We prove the theorem for
\dim\Sigma=3
. The two-dimensional case is similar.
The reverse implication is trivial by simply taking
f=const
. Conversely, suppose
\Sigma
does not admit a metric of positive scalar curvature. By the classification of oriented prime three-manifolds,
\Sigma
is diffeomorphic to either a quotient of
\mathbb{S}^{3}
,
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
, or an aspherical space, and the first two are ruled out. Therefore
\Sigma
is aspherical. Hence
\Sigma\times{\mathbb{S}}^{1}
is also aspherical, and thus it also does not carry a metric of positive scalar curvature
[
10
, Theorem 2]
(see also
[
22
]
). Therefore, by the second line of equation (
5.2
), this proves the theorem for
m=1
. For
0<m<1
, we have
0<R^{f,m}(g)=R^{f,1}(g)+\left(1-\frac{1}{m}\right)|df|^{2}\leq R^{f,1}(g)
, which proves the theorem for these values of
m
. Similar reasoning applies for
1<m\leq 2
.
∎

Proof of Remark
1.10
.

1.

See Claim
5.2
below.

2.

If
R^{f,m}>0
on
\Sigma
for some
m>0
(possibly not an integer), then
R^{f,k}>0
for any integer
k\geq m
, and then by the second line of (
5.2
) we have a metric of positive scalar curvature on
\Sigma\times{\mathbb{T}}^{k}
. But if
\Sigma
is aspherical then so is
\Sigma\times{\mathbb{T}}^{k}
, so we would have a positive scalar curvature metric on an aspherical manifold, falsifying the conjecture.

3.

In this nonorientable case, one uses the Projective Plane Theorem instead of the Sphere Theorem to deduce that
\Sigma
is aspherical but one needs the additional hypothesis that
\Sigma
does not contain any two-sided
\mathbb{RP}^{2}
to obtain the conclusion in the remark. Although not needed for the proof, we note that
there are closed prime manifolds with a two-sided
\mathbb{RP}^{2}

[
30
]
other than
\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{RP}^{2}
(which admits a metric with positive scalar curvature). Moreover, it follows from
Proposition 2.2 in
[
40
]
and Theorem 5.1 in
[
28
]
that there are many prime 3-manifolds with a two-sided
\mathbb{RP}^{2}
which do not admit metrics of positive scalar curvature and yet are not aspherical.

∎

An interesting question is whether there are manifolds that admit positive
m
-Bakry–Émery scalar curvature for some
m>0
but do not admit positive scalar curvature. With that in mind, we make the following observation.

Claim 5.2
.

(i)

For
\Sigma
a closed oriented manifold, if there is a function
f
and metric
g
such that
R^{f,m}(g)>0
for some
m\in(0,1]
, then
\Sigma
admits a metric of positive scalar curvature.

(ii)

If
\dim\Sigma=3
or
\dim\Sigma=5
,
\Sigma
will admit a metric of positive scalar curvature if there is a function
f
and metric
g
such that
R^{f,m}(g)>0
for some
m\in(0,2]
.

As above, since
R^{f,m}>0
implies that
R^{f,k}>0
for any
k\geq m
, without loss of generality we round
m
up to the nearest integer, allowing us to consider the warped product of
\Sigma
with an
m
-torus. Then to justify part (i) of the claim, we need only consider the case of
m=1
. Recently Rosenberg and Xu
[
37
]
have announced a proof of the
{\mathbb{S}}^{1}
-stability conjecture under certain assumptions. Their work applies whenever the tangent vectors to the
{\mathbb{S}}^{1}
factor in the topological product
\Sigma\times{\mathbb{S}}^{1}
lie within the cones (defined by a positive scalar curvature metric) whose axis at each point is the normal vector (with orientation chosen to match that defined by the
{\mathbb{S}}^{1}
factor) to the
\Sigma
factor. When the positive scalar curvature metric on

is a warped product, this condition is trivially met since then these two vector fields are proportional and then

would admit a positive scalar curvature metric.

To justify part (ii) of the claim, when

we can round up to

. If

. But the

conjecture is true for
\Sigma
a

[

, so there must be a positive scalar curvature metric on

. If

admits a positive scalar curvature metric (whether in the form of a warped product or not) then so does
\Sigma^{6}
, and if
\Sigma^{6}\simeq\Sigma^{5}\times{\mathbb{S}}^{1}
admits a positive scalar curvature metric then so does
\Sigma^{5}

[

.

Remark 5.3
.

We see from this that the result of

]
would imply another proof of part of Theorem

5.2.
Interpretation of the dominant energy condition and trapping

Continuing from (

), we now compute that the Einstein tensor

of the total space becomes

(5.4)

\begin{split}G(N,\gamma):=&\,\ric(N,\gamma)-\frac{1}{2}R(N,\gamma)\gamma\\
=&\,\left\{G^{f,m}(M,g)-\frac{1}{2}e^{2f/m}R(S,h)g\right\}\\
&\,\oplus\left\{G(S,h)+e^{-2f/m}\left[\frac{1}{m}\left(\square_{g}f-|\nabla f|_{g}^{2}\right)-\frac{1}{2}R^{f,m}(M,g)\right]h\right\}.\end{split}

Lemma 5.4

(Warped product interpretation of DEC)
.

Let

be future-causal and
m
is a positive integer. Let
(N,\gamma)
be a warped product spacetime with

(i)

If

and

.

(ii)

If

and

, then
G^{f,m}(M,g)(U,V)\geq 0
.

Proof.

Immediate from (

).
∎

Then for

To complete the picture, we turn our attention to null mean curvatures. It’s convenient to use (abstract) index notation, with greek letters (e.g.,
\mu
) denoting tensors on
N
, middle latin letters (e.g.,

) denoting tensors on

(pushed forward to

), and early latin letters (e.g.,

) denoting tensors on
S
. Let
\ell

. In this notation,

denotes the Levi-Civita connection of
(N,\gamma)
,

denotes the Levi-Civita connection of
(M,g)
, and
\nabla_{a}
denotes the Levi-Civita connection of
(S,h)
. Then for
\ell
an element of
T_{p}^{*}N
, we have

(5.5)

\left[\nabla_{\mu}\ell_{\nu}\right]=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\nabla_{i}\ell_{j}&\nabla_{i}\ell_{a}+\frac{1}{m}\ell_{a}\nabla_{i}f\\
&\\
\nabla_{a}\ell_{i}+\frac{1}{m}\ell_{a}\nabla_{i}f&\nabla_{a}\ell_{b}-\frac{1}{m}e^{-2f/m}\left(\nabla_{\ell}f\right)h_{ab}\end{array}\right].

If
\ell
is (metric-dual to) a null vector that lies in

, then
\ell_{a}=0
and the null mean curvature
\theta^{(N,\gamma)}
of
\ell
in

is

(5.6)

\theta^{(N,\gamma)}=\theta^{(M,g)}-g(\ell,\nabla f)=\theta^{(M,g)}-\nabla_{\ell}f=\theta_{f},

where
\theta^{(M,g)}
is the null mean curvature of
\ell
in
M
and
\nabla
can be taken to be the Levi-Civita connection on
(M,g)
. We have the following interpretation.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
\ell
be future null and normal to a codimension-
2
spacelike hypersurface in the warped product
(N,\gamma)
, where
\gamma
is defined in (

\Sigma

\theta_{f}<0
(respectively,
\theta_{g}=0

(i)

(ii)

Appendix A
Flowing the Bakry–Émery scalar curvature

Consider a smooth one-parameter family of metrics
g_{t}
and functions
f_{t}
,
t\geq 0
, such that
g=g_{0}
and
f=f_{0}
. The question then is to compute the variation with
t
of the
m
-Bakry–Émery scalar curvature. Straightforward calculations show that

(A.1)

\frac{\partial R}{\partial t}=-R^{ij}\frac{\partial g_{ij}}{\partial t}+\nabla^{i}\nabla^{j}\frac{\partial g_{ij}}{\partial t}-\Delta\left(g^{ij}\frac{\partial g_{ij}}{\partial t}\right),

(A.2)

\begin{split}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(2\Delta f)=&\,-2\frac{\partial g_{ij}}{\partial t}\nabla^{i}\nabla^{j}f-\left[\nabla^{i}f\cdot\nabla^{j}\frac{\partial g_{ij}}{\partial t}+\nabla^{j}f\cdot\nabla^{i}\frac{\partial g_{ij}}{\partial t}\right.\\
&\,\left.-g^{ij}\nabla_{\nabla f}\frac{\partial g_{ij}}{\partial t}\right]+2\Delta\frac{\partial f}{\partial t},\end{split}

and

(A.3)

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left[-\frac{(m+1)}{m}|df|^{2}\right]=\frac{(m+1)}{m}\frac{\partial g_{ij}}{\partial t}\nabla^{i}f\cdot\nabla^{j}f-\frac{2(m+1)}{m}\nabla^{i}f\cdot\nabla_{i}\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}.

Adding these three expressions, we get that

(A.4)

\begin{split}\frac{\partial R^{f,m}}{\partial t}=&\,\left[\nabla^{i}\nabla^{j}-g^{ij}\Delta-\nabla^{i}f\cdot\nabla^{j}-\nabla^{j}f\cdot\nabla^{i}+g^{ij}\nabla_{\nabla f}-\left(R^{f,m}\right)^{ij}\right]\frac{\partial g_{ij}}{\partial t}\\
&\,+2\left[\Delta-\frac{(m+1)}{m}\nabla^{i}f\cdot\nabla_{i}\right]\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}.\end{split}

Lemma A.1
.

If the metric
g
, function
f
, and
m\in{\mathbb{R}}\cup\{\infty\}
are such that
R^{f,m}(g)\geq 0
on a closed manifold
\Sigma
of dimension
\geq 2
, and if
R^{f,m}(g)
is not identically zero, then there is a metric
{\hat{g}}
such that
R^{f,m}({\hat{g}})>0
on
\Sigma
.

Proof.

Consider (
A.4
) evaluated along the curve of metrics and functions defined by integrating

(A.5)

\frac{\partial g_{t}}{\partial t}=-R^{f,m}(g_{t})g_{t},\quad g_{0}=g,\quad f_{t}=f_{0}=f.

Then
\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}=0
and then (
A.4
) yields

(A.6)

\frac{\partial R^{f,m}}{\partial t}=(n-1)\Delta R^{f,m}-(n-2)\nabla_{\nabla f}R^{f,m}+\left(R^{f,m}\right)^{2}.

This equation is uniformly parabolic, hence the flow exists for
t\in[0,T)
with some
T\in(0,\infty]
. Given
R^{f,m}(p,0)\geq 0
for all
p\in\Sigma
, the weak maximum principle yields that
R^{f,m}(p,t)\geq 0
for all
p\in\Sigma
and all
t\geq 0
in the domain of the flow defined by (
A.5
). But then the strong maximum principle
[
11
, Theorem 6.54, pp 245–246]
(see
[
41
, Proposition 1.1, p 416]
or
[
34
, Theorem 4, p 172]
for proofs) asserts that either
R^{f,m}(p,t)>0
for all
p\in\Sigma
and all
t\in[0,T)
, and then the desired metric
{\hat{g}}
is any of the metric
g_{t}
with
t>0
, or
R^{f,m}(\cdot,\cdot)
is identically zero. The latter is only possible if
R^{f,m}(\cdot,0)
is identically zero.
∎

Appendix B
Conformal transformations

If

(B.1)

{\tilde{g}}=e^{-2af/(n-2)}g,\quad f\in C^{\infty}(M),\quad a\in{\mathbb{R}},

then

(B.2)

\ric_{\tilde{g}}=\ric_{g}+a\hess_{g}f+\frac{a^{2}}{n-2}df\otimes df+\frac{a}{n-2}\left[\Delta_{g}f-a|df|_{g}^{2}\right]g.

For any
u\in C^{\infty}(M)
, we have

(B.3)

\hess_{\tilde{g}}u=\hess_{g}u+\frac{a}{n-2}\left[df\otimes du+du\otimes df-g(\nabla f,\nabla u)g\right].

Let
u=(1-a)f
,
a\neq 1
. Then we obtain from the above formulas that

(B.4)

\begin{split}\ric_{\tilde{g}}+\hess_{\tilde{g}}u=&\,\ric_{g}+\hess_{g}f-\frac{a(a-2)}{(n-2)}df\otimes df+\frac{a}{n-2}\left[\Delta_{g}f-|df|_{g}^{2}\right]g\\
=&\,\ric_{g}+\hess_{g}f-\frac{a(a-2)}{(n-2)}df\otimes df+\frac{a}{n-2}\left(\Delta_{g,f}f\right)g,\end{split}

where
\Delta_{g,f}
denotes the drift Laplacian defined in (
5.3
). Then

(B.5)

\begin{split}\ric_{\tilde{g}}^{u,{\tilde{m}}}=&\,\ric_{g}+\hess_{g}f-\left[\frac{(1-a)^{2}}{\tilde{m}}+\frac{a(a-2)}{n-2}\right]df\otimes df+\frac{a}{n-2}\left(\Delta_{g,f}f\right)g\\
=&\,\ric_{g}^{f,m}+\frac{a}{n-2}\left(\Delta_{g,f}f\right)g,\end{split}

where

(B.6)

\frac{1}{m}:=\frac{(1-a)^{2}}{\tilde{m}}+\frac{a(a-2)}{n-2}.

It is not hard to see that for any pair
m,{\tilde{m}}\in(-\infty,2-n)\cup(0,\infty)
, there is a choice of
a
that will map
{\tilde{m}}
to
m
via the formula
B.6
. The relation can be inverted to map any
m
in this domain to
{\tilde{m}}
in the same domain. Note that of course such a choice of
a
also fixes the conformal transformation relating
g
and
{\tilde{g}}
and the scale transformation relating
f
and
u
.

We now consider the singular cases. The first such case arises for
a=2
, and then
m={\tilde{m}}
. The next singular case is when
m=0
, which means that
f=const
, so
u=(1-a)f=const
unless
a=1
, and so if
a\neq 0
then we may take
{\tilde{m}}=0
. Likewise if we begin from
{\tilde{m}}=0
and choose
a\neq 1
, we conclude that we can set
m=0
.

Finally, we consider
a=1
. Then
u=0
, so we can return to equation (
B.2
). It yields

(B.7)

\begin{split}\ric_{\tilde{g}}=&\,\ric_{g}+\hess_{g}f+\frac{df\otimes df}{n-2}+\frac{\Delta_{g,f}f}{n-2}g\\
=&\,\ric_{g}^{f,2-n}+\frac{\Delta_{g,f}f}{n-2}g.\end{split}

Appendix C
Evaluation of
\nabla_{\nu}\ell

In this section, we evaluate the term
\nabla_{\nu}\ell\cdot\nabla f
which first appears in equation (
4.5
). We will substitute this evaluation into equation(
4.20
).

Recall the set up:
V
is a spacelike hypersurface in a weighted spacetime
(M,g,f)
with future-directed unit normal
w
.
\Sigma
is a closed two-sided hypersurface in
V
with normal
\nu
on
\Sigma
denoting the outward direction. For
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma)
, we consider variations
\Sigma_{t}
of
\Sigma
within
V
given by

(C.1)

\Sigma\ni p\mapsto\exp_{p}\left(t\varphi(p)\nu(p)\right)\in V,

where
\exp_{p}
is the exponential map derived by the Riemannian metric on
V
.

Let
X
be the vector field on
V
whose integral curves are precisely given by (
C.1
). Note that

(C.2)

X|_{\Sigma}=\varphi\nu.

Set
\ell=w+\nu
. Then
\left\{\ell,\nu,\partial_{3},\dotsc,\partial_{n}\right\}
is a basis for
TM
at
\Sigma
where
\left\{\partial_{3},\dotsc,\partial_{n}\right\}
is a coordinate basis for
T\Sigma
. Extend this basis to the variation
\Sigma_{t}
by extending
\nu
to
\nu_{t}\in T\Sigma_{t}^{\perp}
and
\ell_{t}=w+\nu_{t}
, and extend
\partial_{i}
so that it remains a coordinate basis for
T\Sigma_{t}
by Lie-dragging it along the integral curves of
X
, i.e.,

(C.3)

\pounds_{X}\partial_{i}=0,\quad i=3,\dotsc,n.

Therefore
\ell_{t}
remains orthogonal to
\partial_{i}
and thus

(C.4)

\begin{split}0=&\,\nabla_{X}\left(\ell_{t}\cdot\partial_{i}\right)=\pounds_{X}\left(\ell_{t}\cdot\partial_{i}\right)=\partial_{i}\cdot\pounds_{X}\ell_{t}+\cancel{\ell_{t}\cdot\pounds_{X}\partial_{i}}+\pounds_{X}g\left(\ell_{t},\partial_{i}\right)\\
=&\,\partial_{i}\cdot\left(\nabla_{X}\ell_{t}-\nabla_{\ell_{t}}X\right)+\partial_{i}\cdot\nabla_{\ell_{t}}X+\ell_{t}\cdot\nabla_{\partial_{i}}X\\
=&\,\partial_{i}\cdot\nabla_{X}\ell_{t}+\ell_{t}\cdot\nabla_{\partial_{i}}X.\end{split}

(In the penultimate equality,
\nabla_{\ell_{t}}X

\Sigma

X

\Sigma

Evaluating (
C.4
) at
t=0

(C.5)

0=\varphi\partial_{i}\cdot\nabla_{\nu}\ell+\ell\cdot\nabla_{\partial_{i}}(\varphi\nu)\,=\,\varphi\partial_{i}\cdot\nabla_{\nu}\ell+\ell\cdot\big((\partial_{i}\varphi)\nu+\varphi\nabla_{\partial_{i}}\nu\big).

Since
\nu
is normalized,
\nabla_{\partial_{i}}\nu\in\text{span}\left\{w,\partial_{3},\dotsc,\partial_{n}\right\}
. Hence
\ell\cdot\nabla_{\partial_{i}}\nu=w\cdot\nabla_{\partial_{i}}\nu
. Also
\ell\cdot\nu=1
. Therefore

(C.6)

\partial_{i}\cdot\nabla_{\nu}\ell=-\partial_{i}\log\varphi-w\cdot\nabla_{\partial_{i}}\nu.

Lemma C.1
.

If
\nabla_{\ell}f=0
on
\Sigma
, then

(C.7)

\nabla_{\nu}\ell\cdot\nabla f\big|_{\Sigma}=-Df\cdot D\log\varphi+K(Df,\nu),

where

denotes the gradient with respect to the metric on
\Sigma
and

is the second fundamental form of
V
within

.

Proof.

From
(
C.6
)

\nabla_{\nu}\ell\cdot\nabla f\big|_{\Sigma}=\nabla_{\Sigma}^{\perp}f\cdot\nabla_{\nu}\ell-Df\cdot D\log\varphi-w\cdot\nabla_{Df}\nu,

where
\nabla^{\perp}_{\Sigma}=\nabla-D

(C.9)

0=\nabla_{\ell}f=\nabla f\cdot\ell=\nabla_{\Sigma}^{\perp}f\cdot\ell,

\nabla_{\Sigma}^{\perp}f

\ell
. But

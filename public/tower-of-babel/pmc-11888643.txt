INTRODUCTION

The novel coronavirus, SARS-CoV-2, was recognized as the causative agent for a cluster of pneumonia cases detected in Wuhan, a city in China in January 2020. COVID- 19, a disease caused by SARS-CoV-2, has a very extensive clinical range, that is, asymptomatic cases to development of bilateral pneumonia which could progress to respiratory failure and, in some cases, multiorgan failure and even death.

Long COVID syndrome (LCS) is increasingly being recognized as a new clinical entity in the context of SARS-CoV-2 infection. Symptoms persisting for more than 3 weeks after the diagnosis of COVID-19 characterize the long COVID syndrome. Its incidence ranges from 10% to 35%; however, rates as high as 85% have been reported among patients with a history of hospitalization. Currently, there is no consensus on the classification of post- COVID syndrome. This study was conducted via a digital platform to evaluate the psychosomatic disorders of the oral cavity associated with LCS.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

The Ethical Committee approval of the Institution was obtained. Voluntary consent was obtained, and the purpose of the study was explained to all the volunteers before participating in the study.

Study design

Selection and Description of Participants: This was a Pan-India survey in which a questionnaire-based observational study was prepared on a Google platform and shared online to participants who had suffered from SARS-CoV-2. Demographic data with close-end format were taken from the patients, and the data were collected on a four-phase record (i.e., personal data, general health status data, psychological status data, and oral cavity status data). Random sampling was done, and the sample size was determined. Participants aged 18 years and above with no comorbidities before COVID but had suffered from COVID-19/COVID-19-like symptoms were selected for the study. The method of measurement used was modified Hamilton’s anxiety scoring system.

Statistical analysis

The data collected were subject to statistical analysis using SPSS (version 26 SPSS IBM, Chicago). Comparative analysis was performed on long COVID patients to know the following:-

DISCUSSION

Maltezou et al. (2021) and Tsakris et al. (2021) defined long/post COVID syndrome for the first time as “COVID-19-associated illness extending for more than three weeks after the onset of symptoms, and chronic COVID-19 as persistent symptoms extending beyond 12 weeks after the onset of symptoms”.[1]

National Comprehensive Guidelines for Management of Post-COVID Sequela (Ministry of Health and Family Welfare Government of India) stated that many patients have been experiencing short- to long-term sequelae of the disease.[2]

H.C. Maltezou et al. (2021)[3] in their article stated that the pathogenesis of post-COVID syndrome is multifactorial and more than one mechanism may be implicated in several clinical manifestations.

On looking at the oral psychosomatic disorders associated with LCS, it was found in the study that the majority of the participants in the survey study are affected by stress, anxiety, and some other health-related problems in some way or the other after acquiring SARS-CoV-2 infection. Other common complaints were stomatopyrosis, dysgeusia, difficulty in concentration, short-term memory troublesome cough, change in the voice, difficulty in swallowing, recurrent oral ulcers, xerostomia, and orofacial pain. The deep impact of financial burden, health issues, love and relationship matters, family matters, and peer pressure has served to create a great burden on the oral cavity, forming a burden on rima oris.[4567]

Burning sensation in the mouth

Stomatopyrosis is the most common cause of stress and anxiety in the oral cavity. This study had male predilection and was predominant within the age range of 18–33 years. Descending dopaminergic controls, steroid dysfunction, endocrinal dysfunction, and thermal inhibition, caused by stress and anxiety, are believed to be the etiopathology of burning mouth syndrome.[891011]

Recurrent oral ulcers

Fifty-two out 299 volunteers complained of recurrent oral ulcer after the pandemic. Disturbance of immune-regulatory mechanisms led to increased secretion of leucocytes and other chemical mediators, thus exaggerating the epithelial disruption and thereby leading to painful ulcerations.[12]

Xerostomia or subjective oral dryness

A decreased salivary flow rate is due to the depressive illness. Management for subjective oral dryness does not require the administration of antidepressants and anticholinergics.

Myofacial pain dysfunction syndrome and temporomandibular joint syndrome

Psychophysiological pain in the facial region, mainly the temporomandibular joint region, leads to temporomandibular joint disorders. Thus, temporomandibular joint disorders are considered as an oral psychosomatic disorder prevalent in females.[131415]

CONCLUSION

In our study, mental health issues like anxiety/mood changes, burning sensation in mouth and pain in the face, and other associated structures were significant statistically (P ≤ 0.05). The majority of the participants were in the age range of 18–33 years. 41% of the participants suffered from oral dryness, although it was statistically insignificant. More than a year after the declaration of the COVID-19 pandemic, the world continues to face its devastating impact on not only morbidity, mortality, and healthcare services but also its tremendous societal and economic consequences globally. LCS is a multisystem disorder. There is paucity in literature in the association of oral lesions with psychosomatic disorders in such individuals. It is the responsibility of the oral medicine specialist to diagnose such patients and help plan their management. Further research may be required to identify a standard classification for LCS, which will help plan a multidisciplinary therapeutic approach and lifestyle modification for such patients.

Determinants of Mahler measures and special values of L L -functions
Detchat Samart, Zhengyu Tao

Abstract
. We consider Mahler measures of two well-studied families of bivariate polynomials, namely P t = x + x − 1 + y + y − 1 + t P_{t}=x+x^{-1}+y+y^{-1}+\sqrt{t} and Q t = x 3 + y 3 + 1 − t 3 ​ x ​ y Q_{t}=x^{3}+y^{3}+1-\sqrt[3]{t}xy , where t t is a complex parameter. In the cases when the zero loci of these polynomials define CM elliptic curves over number fields, we derive general formulas for their Mahler measures in terms of L L -values of cusp forms. For each family, we also classify all possible values of t t in number fields of degree not exceeding 4 4 for which the corresponding elliptic curves have complex multiplication. Finally, for all such values of t t in totally real number fields of degree n = 2 n=2 and n = 4 n=4 , corresponding to elliptic curves ℱ t \mathcal{F}_{t} (resp. 𝒞 t \mathcal{C}_{t} ), we prove that determinants of n × n n\times n matrices whose entries are Mahler measures corresponding to their Galois conjugates are non-zero rational multiples of L ( n ) ​ ( ℱ t , 0 ) L^{(n)}(\mathcal{F}_{t},0) (resp. L ( n ) ​ ( 𝒞 t , 0 ) L^{(n)}(\mathcal{C}_{t},0) ).



1.
Introduction

For an
n
-variable Laurent polynomial
P\in\mathbb{C}[x_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,x_{n}^{\pm 1}]
, the (logarithmic) Mahler measure of
P
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathrm{m}(P)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi i)^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\log|P(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})|\frac{\mathrm{d}x_{1}}{x_{1}}\dotsb\frac{\mathrm{d}x_{n}}{x_{n}},

where
\mathbb{T}^{n}=\{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:|x_{1}|=\cdots=|x_{n}|=1\}
. In other words,
\mathrm{m}(P)
is the logarithm of the geometric mean of
|P|
over the
n
-dimensional torus. For
n=1
, this quantity can always be computed explicitly in terms of the zeros of
P
using Jensen’s formula. On the other hand, if
n\geq 2
,
\mathrm{m}(P)
is notoriously difficult to compute in general. In the case
n=2
, there are certain classes of polynomials whose Mahler measures are known to be related to special values of
L
-functions. Among these are the two families

\displaystyle P_{t}:=P_{t}(x,y)

\displaystyle=x+\frac{1}{x}+y+\frac{1}{y}+\sqrt{t},

\displaystyle Q_{t}:=Q_{t}(x,y)

\displaystyle=x^{3}+y^{3}+1-\sqrt[3]{t}xy,

which have been studied extensively by many researchers (see, e.g.,
[
4
,
20
,
29
,
30
,
45
]
). The zero loci of
P_{t}
and
Q_{t}
, after desingularization, generically define families of smooth genus-one curves
\mathcal{E}_{t}
and
\mathcal{B}_{t}
, which can be written in the Weierstrass form as follows:

(1.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{t}:Y^{2}

\displaystyle=X^{3}+(t-8)X^{2}+16X,

(1.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{t}:Y^{2}

\displaystyle=X^{3}-27t^{2/3}X^{2}+216t^{1/3}(t-27)X-432(t-27)^{2}.

Boyd
[
4
]
originally conjectured based on his numerical computations that, for many large
t=k^{2}
(resp.
t=k^{3}
) with
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, the following identities hold:

(1.3)

\displaystyle\mathrm{m}(P_{t})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle?}}{{=}}r_{t}L^{\prime}(\mathcal{E}_{t},0),

(1.4)

\displaystyle\mathrm{m}(Q_{t})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle?}}{{=}}s_{t}L^{\prime}(\mathcal{B}_{t},0),

where
r_{t},s_{t}\in\mathbb{Q}
. Here the notation
A\stackrel{{\scriptstyle?}}{{=}}B
means that numerical values of
A
and
B
coincide at least for the first
25
decimal digits. Rodriguez Villegas
[
45
, Table 4]
then extended Boyd’s results by numerically verifying that for several
t\in\mathbb{Z}

(1.5)

\mathrm{m}(P_{t})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle?}}{{=}}r_{t}L^{\prime}(\mathcal{F}_{t},0),

where
\mathcal{F}_{t}
is a quadratic twist of
\mathcal{E}_{t}
defined by

(1.6)

\mathcal{F}_{t}:Y^{2}=X^{3}+t(t-8)X^{2}+16t^{2}X.

Although
\mathcal{E}_{t}
and
\mathcal{F}_{t}
are isomorphic over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
, their
L
-functions could be different since they are not always in the same isogeny class. Due to certain
K
-theoretical considerations, which shall be discussed in Section
2
, we will focus on the Weierstrass model
\mathcal{F}_{t}
rather than
\mathcal{E}_{t}
for the rest of this paper. Similarly, following Rodriguez Villegas’ observation in
[
45
]
, we will replace
\mathcal{B}_{t}
with the Weierstrass model

(1.7)

\mathcal{C}_{t}:Y^{2}=X^{3}-27t^{2}X^{2}+216t^{3}(t-27)X-432t^{4}(t-27)^{2}

and reformulate (
1.4
) as

(1.8)

\mathrm{m}(Q_{t})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle?}}{{=}}s_{t}L^{\prime}(\mathcal{C}_{t},0),

for sufficiently large
t\in\mathbb{Z}
. Note that these changes of the underlying curves do not affect the original (conjectural) identities (
1.3
) and (
1.4
).

To date, only a small number of identities (
1.5
) and (
1.8
) have been rigorously verified. Comprehensive lists of the proven cases can be found in
[
33
, Table 1]
and
[
34
, Table 1]
. For a survey of methods used in the proofs, the reader is referred to the research monograph
[
5
]
. Note that all rational values of
t
for which
\mathcal{F}_{t}
(resp.
\mathcal{C}_{t}
) has complex multiplication (CM) can be easily examined using the
j
-invariant of
\mathcal{F}_{t}
(resp.
\mathcal{C}_{t}
). In fact, by equating (
2.6
) to the
13
rational CM
j
-invariants, it can be seen that there are exactly
3
rational values of
t
for which
\mathcal{F}_{t}
is a CM curve, namely
t=-16,8,
and
32
, and the identity (
1.5
) is known to be true in these cases (with
r_{t}=2,1,
and
1
, respectively), thanks to work of Rodriguez Villegas
[
45
]
and Rogers
[
29
]
. Similarly, assuming
t\in\mathbb{Q}
, the curve
\mathcal{C}_{t}
has CM precisely when
t=-216,54,
and
24
. In the first two cases, the identity (
1.8
) holds with
s_{t}=3
and
\frac{3}{2}
, respectively
[
29
,
45
]
. The case

is slightly different due to a subtle property of the family

and will be discussed exclusively in Section

For brevity, we will use the following notation throughout this paper:

\displaystyle\mu(t)

\displaystyle:=\mathrm{m}(P_{t})=\mathrm{m}\left(x+\frac{1}{x}+y+\frac{1}{y}+\sqrt{t}\right),

\displaystyle\mathrm{n}(t)

\displaystyle:=\mathrm{m}(Q_{t})=\mathrm{m}\left(x^{3}+y^{3}+1-\sqrt[3]{t}xy\right),

\displaystyle\nu(t)

\displaystyle:=\begin{cases}\mathrm{n}(t),&\text{if }\sqrt[3]{t}\notin\mathcal{K}_{Q}^{\circ},\\
\tilde{\mathrm{n}}(t),&\text{if }t\in(-1,27),\end{cases}

where
\mathcal{K}_{Q}
is the set of

such that

vanishes on
\mathbb{T}^{2}
and
\tilde{\mathrm{n}}(t)
is a modified version of
\mathrm{n}(t)
, which is defined in
[
34
]
(see Section
2
below). Since all CM elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}
in the family
\mathcal{F}_{t}
(resp.
\mathcal{C}_{t}
) have been classified completely and the corresponding Mahler measure (or modified Mahler measure)
\mu(t)
(resp.
\nu(t)
) is known to be expressible in terms of
L^{\prime}(\mathcal{F}_{t},0)
(resp.
L^{\prime}(\mathcal{C}_{t},0)
), it is natural to extend these results to an arbitrary number field. In particular, we are interested in the following problems:

(i)

Given a number field
K
, determine all
t\in K
for which
\mathcal{F}_{t}
(resp.
\mathcal{C}_{t}
) is a CM elliptic curve.

(ii)

For each
t
in (i), find an explicit relationship between
\mu(t)
(resp.
\nu(t)
) and a special value of
L(\mathcal{F}_{t},s)
(resp.
L(\mathcal{C}_{t},s)
).

Problem (ii) is largely motivated by the
Beilinson’s conjectures
, a collection of far-reaching conjectures in algebraic
K
-theory relating higher regulators, called the
Beilinson’s regulators
, to special
L
-values of algebraic varieties. For precise statements of these conjectures for general algebraic curves over number fields, the reader is referred to
[
13
]
. Over the past few years, some progress has been made on these problems for real quadratic number fields. For example, Guo, Ji, Liu, and Qin
[
16
]
give explicit expressions of
\mu(48\pm 32\sqrt{2})
in terms of regulator integrals and prove that

(1.9)

\det\begin{pmatrix}\mu(48+32\sqrt{2})&2\mu(48-32\sqrt{2})\\
-2\mu(48-32\sqrt{2})&4\mu(48+32\sqrt{2})\end{pmatrix}=4L^{\prime\prime}(\mathcal{F}_{48\pm 32\sqrt{2}},0),

which is equivalent to a weak version of Beilinson’s conjecture for the curve
\mathcal{F}_{48\pm 32\sqrt{2}}
.
In
[
40
]
, the second author, Guo, and Wei proposed a systematic approach to find more identities of the form (
1.9
) which correspond to CM elliptic curves defined over real quadratic fields. In particular, they verify analogous identities for
t=272\pm 192\sqrt{2},8\pm 4\sqrt{3},128\pm 64\sqrt{3},8\pm 3\sqrt{7},
and
2048\pm 768\sqrt{7}
. The second author and Guo
[
39
]
apply a similar method to the family
Q_{t}
and prove that

(1.10)

\det\begin{pmatrix}\nu(729+405\sqrt{3})&\nu(729-405\sqrt{3})\\
\nu(729-405\sqrt{3})&4\nu(729+405\sqrt{3})\end{pmatrix}=\frac{27}{8}L^{\prime\prime}(\mathcal{C}_{729\pm 405\sqrt{3}},0).

In this paper, we extend these results by using a modified version of algorithms in
[
39
]
and
[
40
]
to obtain a complete solution to Problem (i) and give illustrative examples for number fields
K
with
\deg K\leq 4
. Then we tackle Problem (ii) by establishing identities of the form (
1.9
) and (
1.10
) for all totally real algebraic numbers
t
in (i) of degree at most

. For example, we show that for algebraic integers

and

corresponding to
\#28
(resp.
\#71
) in Table

(1.11)

\det\begin{pmatrix}\mu(t_{1})&\mu(t_{2})&\mu(t_{3})&\mu(t_{4})\\
\mu(t_{2})&-3\mu(t_{1})&-3\mu(t_{4})&\mu(t_{3})\\
\mu(t_{3})&-3\mu(t_{4})&-3\mu(t_{1})&\mu(t_{2})\\
\mu(t_{4})&\mu(t_{3})&\mu(t_{2})&\mu(t_{1})\end{pmatrix}=\frac{1}{24}L^{(4)}(\mathcal{F}_{t},0),

(1.12)

\det\begin{pmatrix}\nu(t_{1})&\nu(t_{2})&\nu(t_{3})&\nu(t_{4})\\
\nu(t_{2})&-5\nu(t_{1})&\nu(t_{4})&-5\nu(t_{3})\\
\nu(t_{3})&\nu(t_{4})&37\nu(t_{1})&37\nu(t_{2})\\
\nu(t_{4})&-5\nu(t_{3})&37\nu(t_{2})&-185\nu(t_{1})\end{pmatrix}=\frac{27}{8}L^{(4)}(\mathcal{C}_{t},0).

Note that

while
\sqrt[3]{t_{1}},\sqrt[3]{t_{2}}\notin\mathcal{K}_{Q}^{\circ}
, so the matrix in (

\mathrm{n}(t_{1}),\mathrm{n}(t_{2})
and two modified Mahler measures
\tilde{\mathrm{n}}(t_{3}),\tilde{\mathrm{n}}(t_{4})
. All

determinant identities for the families

and

are listed in Table

As opposed to (
1.9
) and (
1.10

K
-theoretic language (i.e. the Beilinson’s conjectures). We focus purely on computational aspects of determinants of Mahler measures and special

2.
Nuts and bolts

For meromorphic functions
f
and
g
on a smooth curve
C
, we define the real differential
1
-form
\eta(f,g)
on
C
as

\eta(f,g)=\log|f|\;\mathrm{d}\arg(g)-\log|g|\;\mathrm{d}\arg(f),

where
\mathrm{d}\arg(h)=\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}h}{h}\right)
. It is easily seen that
\eta(f,g)
is closed, so its integral over a path
\gamma
on
C
only depends on its homotopy class. Let
P(x,y)\in\mathbb{C}[x,y]
be irreducible and let
P^{*}(x)
be the leading coefficient of
P(x,y)
, seen as a polynomial in
y
. We define the
Deninger path
of the curve
C_{P}:P(x,y)=0
by

\gamma_{P}=\{(x,y)\in C_{P}:|x|=1,|y|\geq 1\}.

Using Jensen’s formula, Deninger proved the following result, which relates Mahler measure to the regulator integral.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
5
,
12
]
)
.

Suppose
P(x,y)=0
defines an elliptic curve
E
and
\gamma_{P}
is a finite union of smooth paths in
E
. Then

(2.1)

\mathrm{m}(P)-\mathrm{m}(P^{*})=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\gamma_{P}}\eta(x,y).

We now state a version of Beilinson’s conjecture for elliptic curves (see also
[
6
, §2]
and
[
13
]
). Let
E
be an elliptic curve defined over a number field
K
of degree
n
and
X=\bigsqcup\limits_{\sigma\in\Hom_{\mathbb{Q}}(K,\mathbb{C})}E^{\sigma}(\mathbb{C}),
the disjoint union of
n
connected Riemann surfaces, one for each embedding of
K
into
\mathbb{C}
. Then we have group isomorphisms
H_{1}(X,\mathbb{Z})\cong\bigoplus_{\sigma}H_{1}(E^{\sigma}(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}^{2n}
. Since complex conjugation acts on
X
then on
H_{1}(X,\mathbb{Z})
with

\overline{\gamma}\in\begin{cases}H_{1}(E^{\sigma}(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z}),&\text{if }\sigma\text{ is real,}\\
H_{1}(E^{\bar{\sigma}}(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z}),&\text{if }\sigma\text{ is complex}\end{cases}\quad\text{for }\gamma\in H_{1}(E^{\sigma}(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z}),

the subgroup
H_{1}(X,\mathbb{Z})^{-}
of
H_{1}(X,\mathbb{Z})
consisting of the homology classes
\gamma=(\gamma_{\sigma})_{\sigma}
such that
(\overline{\gamma}_{\sigma})_{\bar{\sigma}}=(-\gamma_{\sigma})_{\sigma}
is a free abelian group of rank
n
. If
E
has a
tempered
planar model
P(x,y)=0
(see
[
45
, §8]
for the definition of a tempered polynomial) such that
x^{m}
and
y^{n}
, considered as functions on
E
, are defined over
K
for some
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
, then we have
\{x,y\}\in K_{2}^{T}(E)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
, where
K_{2}^{T}(E)
is the
tame
K_{2}
group of
E
. Although we shall not give a precise definition of
K_{2}^{T}(E)
here, it should be noted that its element can be written as
\sum_{i}\{f_{i},g_{i}\}
, where
f_{i}
and
g_{i}
are rational functions in
K(E)
and
\{\cdot,\cdot\}
is the Steinberg symbol. By applying
\sigma
to the coefficients, for each
M=\sum_{i}\{f_{i},g_{i}\}\in K_{2}^{T}(E)
, we have
M^{\sigma}:=\sum_{i}\{f_{i}^{\sigma},g_{i}^{\sigma}\}\in K_{2}^{T}(E^{\sigma})
. If we assume further that the Deninger path
\gamma_{P}
is closed, then the right-hand side of (
2.1
) can be realized as
\langle\gamma_{P},\{x,y\}\rangle
, where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is the pairing given by

(2.2)

\displaystyle H_{1}(X,\mathbb{Z})^{-}\times K_{2}^{T}(E)

\displaystyle\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle{\textstyle\left((\gamma_{\sigma})_{\sigma},\sum_{i}\{f_{i},g_{i}\}\right)}

\displaystyle\mapsto\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{\sigma}\sum_{i}\int_{\gamma_{\sigma}}\eta(f_{i}^{\sigma},g_{i}^{\sigma}).

Let
K_{2}^{T}(E)_{\mathrm{int}}
be the
integral tame
K_{2}
group of
E
, which is a subgroup of
K_{2}^{T}(E)
consisting of elements with certain integrality conditions (see
[
21
, §1]
for a more precise definition). Beilinson’s conjecture for
E
can then be stated as follows:

(i)

K_{2}^{T}(E)_{\mathrm{int}}/\text{torsion}
is a free abelian group of rank
n
,

(ii)

if
\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{n}
form a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis of
H_{1}(X,\mathbb{Z})^{-}
and
M_{1},\dots,M_{n}
form a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis of
K_{2}^{T}(E)_{\mathrm{int}}
up to torsion, then the
Beilinson regulator

(2.3)

R=|\det(\langle\gamma_{i},M_{j}\rangle)_{1\leq i,j\leq n}|

is a non-zero rational multiple of
\frac{1}{\pi^{2n}}L(E/K,2)
(or
L^{(n)}(E/K,0)
by the (conjectural) functional equation for
L(E/K,s)
).

Since it is difficult to compute the group
K_{2}^{T}(E)_{\mathrm{int}}
in practice, it is certainly unclear how its
\mathbb{Z}
-basis can be constructed in order to get a determinant that satisfies the conjecture. In this paper, we only consider the following special setting.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve defined over some totally real Galois number field
K
of degree
n
such that
E^{\sigma}
are
K
-isogenous to each other and let
P(x,y)=0
be a tempered model for
E
such that
\{x,y\}\in K_{2}^{T}(E)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
. For each
\sigma\in\Gal(K/\mathbb{Q})
, fix a
K
-isogeny
\phi_{\sigma}:E\to E^{\sigma}
and a nontrivial element
\gamma_{\sigma}\in H_{1}(E^{\sigma}(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z})^{-}\subset H_{1}(X,\mathbb{Z})^{-}
. Then each
\phi_{\sigma}
induces a pullback map
\phi_{\sigma}^{*}:K_{2}^{T}(E^{\sigma})\to K_{2}^{T}(E)
given by

\phi_{\sigma}^{*}M=\sum_{i}\{\phi_{\sigma}^{*}f_{i},\phi_{\sigma}^{*}g_{i}\},\text{ where }M=\sum_{i}\{f_{i},g_{i}\}\in K_{2}^{T}(E^{\sigma})

(see
[
16
]
). Therefore, we can immediately construct
n
elements

\{M_{1},\dots,M_{n}\}=\{\phi_{\sigma}^{*}\{x^{\sigma},y^{\sigma}\}:\sigma\in\Gal(K/\mathbb{Q})\}\subset K_{2}^{T}(E)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}.

If the Deninger paths for the tempered models
P_{\sigma}(x,y)=0
for
E^{\sigma}
are all closed and
\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{n}
are a relabeling of elements in
\{\gamma_{\sigma}:\sigma\in\Gal(K/\mathbb{Q})\}
in some order, then by Theorem
2.1
, each entry in the regulator (
2.3
) should be a rational multiple of
\mathrm{m}(P_{\sigma})
for some
\sigma\in\Gal(K/\mathbb{Q})
. If we know in addition that the elements
M_{i}
are all in
K_{2}^{T}(E)_{\mathrm{int}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
and linearly independent, then by Beilinson’s conjecture, it is expected that
R
is a non-zero rational multiple of
\frac{1}{\pi^{2n}}L(E/K,2)
.

With explicit constructions of the objects mentioned above, results in
[
16
]
,
[
40
]
, and
[
39
]
are achieved using this approach. However, as mentioned in Section
1
, we will bypass this regulator-related calculation and directly search for identities between
n\times n
determinants of Mahler measures and
L^{(n)}(E,0)
. In addition, since every Galois extension of
\mathbb{Q}
is either totally real or totally imaginary and Mahler measure is invariant under complex conjugation, we will consider only the totally real cases when searching for such identities.

For
t\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\{0,16\}
, the curve
P_{t}(x,y)=0
can be transformed into the Weierstrass form (
1.6
) via the birational map

(2.4)

x=\frac{2\sqrt{t}(tX+Y)}{X(X-4t)},\quad y=\frac{2\sqrt{t}(tX-Y)}{X(X-4t)}.

Similarly, for
t\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\{0,27\}
, we can transform the Hessian family
Q_{t}(x,y)=0
into (
1.7
) using the map

(2.5)

x=\frac{-18t^{\frac{2}{3}}X}{3tX+Y-36t^{3}+972t^{2}},\quad y=1-\frac{2Y}{3tX+Y-36t^{3}+972t^{2}}.

It is clear from (
2.4
) (resp. (
2.5
)) that if we choose
f=x^{2}
and
g=y^{2}
(resp.
f=x^{3}
and
g=y
), then
f
and
g
become rational functions on
\mathcal{F}_{t}
(resp.
\mathcal{C}_{t}
) defined over
K=\mathbb{Q}(t)
. Therefore, by the above arguments, it is possible to relate Mahler measures to suitable regulator integrals appearing in the Beilinson’s conjecture. To confirm this assertion, one needs more delicate calculation similar to that in
[
16
,
39
,
40

4.
L
-functions of CM elliptic curves

It is a well-known fact due to a classical result of Deuring that the
L
-series of a CM elliptic curve defined over an arbitrary number field is expressible in terms of the Hecke
L
-series attached to Grössencharacters, so that it can be analytically continued to the entire complex plane. More precisely, we have the following result.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
E
be a CM elliptic curve defined over a number field
K
and define the complete
L
-function of
E/K
as

\Lambda(E/K,s):=(\Norm(\mathcal{N}_{K}(E))d_{K}^{2})^{s/2}((2\pi)^{-s}\Gamma(s))^{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}L(E/K,s),

where
\mathcal{N}_{K}(E)
denotes the conductor of
E/K
and
d_{K}
denotes the discriminant of
K
. Then
\Lambda(E/K,s)
admits an analytic continuation to the entire complex plane and satisfies the functional equation

(4.1)

\Lambda(E/K,s)=w_{E/K}\Lambda(E/K,2-s),

where
w_{E/K}\in\{\pm 1\}
is the root number of
E/K
.

We shall examine structures of
L
-functions of CM elliptic curves defined over a quadratic or quartic number field with certain properties, including those appearing in Section
5
. The following proposition will be crucial when linking products of
L
-values of cusp forms to
L
-values of elliptic curves in the proofs of our main results. Recall that a newform
f=\sum a_{n}q^{n}\in\mathcal{S}_{k}(\Gamma_{1}(N))
is said to have
complex multiplication
(CM) by a Dirichlet character
\phi
of conductor
M
associated to an imaginary quadratic field if
a_{p}=\phi(p)a_{p}
for all prime
p\nmid NM
.

For an elliptic curve
E
defined over a number field
K
and a subfield
L
of
K
, we let
\Res_{K/L}(E)
denote the Weil restriction of
E
, which is an abelian variety of dimension

over

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
K
be a totally real number field and let

be a CM elliptic curve over

K
-isogenous to all of its Galois conjugates.

(i)

Suppose

is a quadratic field and

. If

is simple over
\mathbb{Q}
, then

for some CM newform

, where

is a quadratic number field with
\Gal(M/\mathbb{Q})=\langle\sigma\rangle
, and
\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{Q}}(A)=N^{2}
. Otherwise,
L(E,s)=L(f_{1},s)L(f_{2},s)
for some CM newforms

and
\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{Q}}(A)=N_{1}N_{2}
.

(ii)

Suppose

is a biquadratic field and

. Then there exist CM newforms
f_{i}\in\mathcal{S}_{2}(\Gamma_{1}(N_{i}))
,

, such that

and
\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{Q}}(A)=\prod_{i=1}^{4}N_{i}
.

Proof.

To prove (i), we follow the arguments in the proof of
[
16
, Lemma 4.2]
. First, let
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})
be a quadratic field with
E^{\prime}
being the Galois conjugate of
E
and assume that
A=\Res_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(E)
is simple over
\mathbb{Q}
. Then it follows that
\End_{\mathbb{Q}}^{0}(A):=\End_{\mathbb{Q}}(A)\otimes\mathbb{Q}
is a division algebra.
By the universal property of the restriction of scalars, we have the following
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space isomorphism

\End_{\mathbb{Q}}^{0}(A)\cong\prod_{\tau\in\Gal(K/\mathbb{Q})}\Hom_{K}^{0}(E^{\tau},E),

where
\Hom_{K}^{0}(A,B)=\Hom_{K}(A,B)\otimes\mathbb{Q}.
Since
K
is totally real and
E
has CM, we have
\End_{K}(E)\cong\mathbb{Z}
. Moreover, since
E
is
K
-isogenous to
E^{\prime}
, we have that
\Hom_{K}(E,E^{\prime})
is a free
\mathbb{Z}
-module of the same rank as
\End_{K}(E)
, implying that
\End_{\mathbb{Q}}^{0}(A)
has dimension
2
over
\mathbb{Q}
. Since every
2
-dimensional division algebra is commutative,
\End_{\mathbb{Q}}^{0}(A)
must be a quadratic number field. In other words, the abelian surface
A
is of
\GL_{2}
-type, as defined in
[
28
]
. Then by the Serre’s modularity conjecture, which has now been proven, thanks to work of Ribet
[
28
]
and Khare and Wintenberger
[
18
,
19
]
, we have that
A
is isogenous over
\mathbb{Q}
to the modular abelian surface
A_{f}
attached to some newform
f\in\mathcal{S}_{2}(\Gamma_{1}(N))
via Shimura’s construction. We have in addition that
f
has CM and its coefficient field
M
is isomorphic to
\End_{\mathbb{Q}}(A_{f})\otimes\mathbb{Q}
, which is a quadratic number field in this case (see, e.g.,
[
14
]
). Hence by the defining property of the restriction of scalars it follows that

L(E/K,s)=L(A/\mathbb{Q},s)=L(A_{f},s)=L(f,s)L(f^{\sigma},s),

where
\sigma
is the generator of the Galois group
\Gal(M/\mathbb{Q}).
Moreover, by Carayol’s theorem
[
8
]
, we have
\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{Q}}(A)=\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{Q}}(A_{f})=N^{2}
. On the other hand, if
A
is not simple, then
A\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}E_{1}\times E_{2}
for some elliptic curves
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
over
\mathbb{Q}
, implying

L(E/K,s)=L(A/\mathbb{Q},s)=L\left(E_{1},s\right)L\left(E_{2},s\right).

It then follows immediately from the modularity theorem that the right-hand side is the product of
L
-functions of some CM newforms
f_{i}\in\mathcal{S}_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(N_{i});\mathbb{Q})
,
i=1,2
, corresponding to
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
, and
\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{Q}}(A)=\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{Q}}(E_{1})\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{Q}}(E_{2})=N_{1}N_{2}.

The statement (ii) can be proven in a similar manner. First, assume that there exist a proper subfield
L
of
K
for which
E
is isogenous to a base change of an elliptic curve
F
over
L
. Hence
\Res_{K/L}(E)
is not simple (over
L
). If
L=\mathbb{Q}
, then we have

L(E/K,s)=\prod_{\chi\in\widehat{G}}L(F\otimes\chi,s),

where
\widehat{G}
is the group of characters of
G=\Gal(K/\mathbb{Q}).
Since
G\cong C_{2}\times C_{2}
, it follows that any nontrivial character
\chi\in\widehat{G}
is a quadratic character. Therefore, by the modularity theorem, there exist CM newforms
f_{i}\in\mathcal{S}_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(N_{i});\mathbb{Q})
,
1\leq i\leq 4
, for which

\prod_{\chi\in\hat{G}}L(F\otimes\chi,s)=\prod_{i=1}^{4}L(f_{i},s).

Moreover, we have
A\sim\prod_{\chi\in\widehat{G}}F\otimes\chi
, so

\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{Q}}(A)=\prod_{\chi\in\widehat{G}}\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{Q}}(F\otimes\chi)=\prod_{i=1}^{4}N_{i}.

Now suppose that
L
is a quadratic field and let
B=\Res_{K/L}(E)
, which is a CM abelian surface over
L
. Then we have that
F
is CM elliptic curve over
L
and

(4.2)

L(E/K,s)=L(B/L,s)=L(F,s)L(F\otimes\chi,s),

where
\chi
is a quadratic character on
\Gal(K/L)
. We claim that both
F
and

are
L
-isogenous to their Galois conjugates. To see why this claim is true, let
B_{1}:=\Res_{L/\mathbb{Q}}(F)
, whose base change to

is isogenous to
F\times F^{\sigma}
, where
\Gal(L/\mathbb{Q})=\langle\sigma\rangle.
Since
L
is totally real, we have that
\End_{\mathbb{Q}}^{0}(B_{1})=\End_{L}^{0}(B_{1})\cong\End_{L}^{0}(F)\times\End_{L}^{0}(F^{\sigma})
, which is a

\mathbb{Q}
-algebra. On the other hand, since

and

, it follows that

; i.e.,

is
L
-isogenous to

. The same arguments can then be applied to

and

where

, for which

and

). Moreover, we have

\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{Q}}(A)=\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{Q}}(B_{1})\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{Q}}(B_{2})=N_{1}N_{2}M_{1}M_{2}.

Next, let us assume that

is simple for any subfield
L
of
K

L
of
K

. By the

-module isomorphism

\End_{L}(B)\cong\prod_{\tau\in\Gal(K/L)}\Hom_{K}(E^{\tau},E),

we again have that

is a division algebra of dimension

over
\mathbb{Q}
, so it must be a field. Note that

admits a

-algebra structure via the following isomorphism

(4.3)

\End_{\mathbb{Q}}^{0}(A)=\End_{\mathbb{Q}}^{0}(\Res_{L/\mathbb{Q}}(B))\cong\mathcal{D}^{c}[G],

where the right-hand side denotes the twisted group algebra of

for some CM newform

, implying

∎

Closing remarks

We conclude this paper by giving a few remarks about our and some related results from both theoretical and computational perspectives. Firstly, it is crucial to assume that the fields of definition of elliptic curves in Theorem
5.2
and Theorem
5.4
(and their potential generalizations) are totally real. This is clearly visible in the quadratic case. For example, let
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})
,
\Gal(K/\mathbb{Q})=\langle\sigma\rangle
, and
t=8+8i\sqrt{3}\in K
. Then the curve
\mathcal{F}_{t}
can be identified as the curve
2.0.3.1-256.1-CMa2
or
2.0.3.1-256.1-CMb2
(depending on which embedding
K\xhookrightarrow{}\mathbb{C}
is used). According to the data in the LMFDB, its
L
-function is still the product of modular
L
-functions, namely
L(\mathcal{F}_{t},s)=L_{\textnormal{{48ca1}}}(s)L_{\textnormal{{48ca2}}}(s)
. However, since
\mu(t)=\mu(\bar{t})=\mu(t^{\sigma})
, the (non-zero) determinant of the form (
5.8
) must be a non-zero rational multiple of
\mu(t)^{2}
. By (
5.18
) and (
4.1
), this quantity is certainly not a rational multiple of
L^{\prime\prime}(\mathcal{F}_{t},0)
, so (
5.4
) is invalid for this curve. Note, however, that this example does not invalidate the Beilinson’s conjecture. To elaborate further, recall that every CM elliptic curve defined over a number field is a
\mathbb{Q}
-curve
; i.e., it is isogenous (over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
) to all its Galois conjugates. Although
\mathcal{F}_{t}
has CM,
\mathcal{F}_{t}
is not
K
-isogenous to
\mathcal{F}_{t^{\sigma}}
, so we cannot canonically construct (linearly independent) elements in
K_{2}^{T}(\mathcal{F}_{t})
using pullbacks of an isogeny. In other words, over the field
K
, the determinant (
5.8
) in this case may differ from the Beilinson regulator (
2.3
) (for
n=2
). This discrepancy seems to carry over in higher dimensions; for all CM curves defined over non-totally real number fields of degree
d\leq 4
obtained from Proposition
3.4
, we are unable to find any simple identity between determinants of Mahler measures and their
L
-values.

Secondly, the reader may observe that there are no identities for
3\times 3
determinants in Section
5
, due to the absence of CM elliptic curves in the families
\mathcal{F}_{t}
and
\mathcal{C}_{t}
which are defined over totally real cubic fields. On the other hand, we would like to point out evidence of such identity from another family

R_{t}=(1+x)(1+y)(x+y)-txy,

whose Mahler measure was also originally studied by Boyd
[
4
]
. Proven Mahler measure formulas for
R_{t}
analogous to (
1.5
) and (
1.8
) are listed in
[
23
, Table 1]
. Consider
f(x)=x^{3}+x^{2}-2x-1
, which has three distinct roots

p_{1}=-1.8019\cdots,\ p_{2}=-0.4450\cdots,\ p_{3}=1.2469\cdots,

and let
K
be the splitting field of
f(x)
, which is the cubic Galois field of smallest discriminant. For each
i=1,2,3
, let
t_{i}=2(1+p_{i})^{2}/p_{i}
and
\mathrm{m}_{i}=\mathrm{m}(R_{t_{i}})
. The curve
R_{t}=0
can be written in a Weierstrass form

E_{t}:Y^{2}=X^{3}+(t^{2}-4t-8)X^{2}+16(t+1)X

and for any
i=1,2,3
,
E_{t_{i}}
can be identified as the curve

3.3.49.1-64.1-a7
in LMFDB, which is the base change of an elliptic curve
E
over
\mathbb{Q}
with label
196.b2
. Therefore, we have that
L(E_{t_{i}},s)=L(E,s)L(E\otimes\chi,s)L(E\otimes\chi^{2},s),
where
\chi
is a character of order
3
. By modularity of
E
, this equation can then be rewritten as

(7.1)

L(E_{t_{i}},s)=L(f_{\texttt{28ea1}},s)L(\bar{f}_{\texttt{28ea1}},s)L(f_{\texttt{196ab1}},s).

In a forthcoming paper
[
7
]
, Brunault, Liu, and Wang introduce an algorithm based on Rogers-Zudilin method in order to prove certain Mahler measure identities for curves in the family
R_{t}=0
defined over number fields. In particular, they give explicit expressions of
\mathrm{m}_{1},\mathrm{m}_{2},
and
\mathrm{m}_{3}
in terms of linear combinations of
\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}L(f_{\texttt{28ea1}},2)
,
\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}L(\bar{f}_{\texttt{28ea1}},2)
and
\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}L(f_{\texttt{196ab1}},2)
. By using their result and (
7.1
), one can deduce easily that

(7.2)

\det\begin{pmatrix}\mathrm{m}_{1}&\mathrm{m}_{2}&\mathrm{m}_{3}\\
\mathrm{m}_{3}&\mathrm{m}_{1}&\mathrm{m}_{2}\\
\mathrm{m}_{2}&\mathrm{m}_{3}&\mathrm{m}_{1}\end{pmatrix}=\frac{7203}{2\pi^{6}}L(E_{t_{i}},2)=\frac{1}{4}L^{(3)}(E_{t_{i}},0).

Note that the curves
E_{t_{i}}
have no CM, but they are isogenous to each other over
K
; i.e., they are
\mathbb{Q}
-curves, and the second equality in (
7.2
) follows from the functional equation (
4.1
) for elliptic curves over totally real fields, thanks to Taylor’s potential modularity theorem
[
41
]
. Furthermore, under certain assumptions,
\mathbb{Q}
-curves are known to be strongly modular
[
15
, Theorem 5.3]
. Together with this fact, the existence of (
7.2
) suggests that the results in this paper could be extended beyond the CM case, although more sophisticated techniques will be required when computing Mahler measures of non-CM curves. In particular, for the quadratic case, we formulate the following conjecture based on our numerical experiments.

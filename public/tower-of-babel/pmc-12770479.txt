Composite material surface microscopic defect detection and classification combining diffusion models and zero-shot learning
Authors: Weijun Fan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis research aims to address the technical challenges of data scarcity and the identification of unseen defect types in the detection of microscopic defects on the surface of composite materials. An innovative detection framework integrating diffusion model and zero-shot learning has been constructed, adopting a dual-path collaborative architecture design. In this framework, the diffusion path learns the potential distribution characteristics of defects through the conditional diffusion process, and the zero-shot path realizes cross-modal knowledge transfer through the vision-semantic joint embedding space. The framework integrates an improved denoising diffusion probability model, a zero-shot classifier based on attribute description, and a multi-level feature fusion mechanism, achieving collaborative optimization among modules through a joint training strategy. Evaluations on multiple benchmark datasets verified the effectiveness of the method. The zero-shot learning mechanism achieved detection accuracy comparable to that of traditional supervised learning without the need for labeled samples, significantly reducing labeling costs. At the same time, it demonstrated excellent generalization ability across different types of composite materials. This framework provides an efficient technical solution for intelligent quality control in the composite materials manufacturing industry, promoting the development of industrial defect detection technology towards intelligence.Keywords: Composite material defect detection, Diffusion model, Zero-shot learning, Vision-semantic mapping, Intelligent manufacturingSubject terms: Engineering, Materials science, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Composite materials have emerged as critical components in aerospace, automotive, and renewable energy industries, with the global market projected to exceed 150 billion USD by 2025. The structural integrity of these materials directly impacts operational safety and performance reliability, making microscopic defect detection an essential quality control measure. The field of automated defect detection is undergoing significant technological transformation, as comprehensive reviews have documented the evolution from manual inspection to AI-driven systems1. Deep learning generative models have demonstrated potential for enhancing minor defect recognition2, while traditional ultrasonic testing methods continue to evolve for carbon fiber-reinforced polymers3. Acoustic emission data combined with deep learning approaches has enabled sophisticated damage source classification4, complemented by thermal imaging techniques using deep autoencoders for carbon fiber inspection5. Advanced manufacturing processes now incorporate real-time defect detection with closed-loop adjustment capabilities6, while radio frequency sensors provide additional non-destructive evaluation modalities7. The successful application of deep neural networks extends beyond detection to composite material design optimization8, with machine learning enabling quantitative damage monitoring throughout the product lifecycle9. These developments represent a comprehensive transformation in structural health monitoring for composite materials10.

Despite these technological advances, fundamental challenges prevent widespread industrial deployment of automated defect detection systems. Fast and accurate detection networks like SDDNet achieve impressive computational efficiency but remain critically dependent on training data quality and quantity11. Research specifically targeting composite materials reveals that data scarcity represents a universal bottleneck across different material types and defect categories12. Few-shot detection methods using patch matching techniques provide partial mitigation but suffer from limited generalization to unseen defect morphologies13. Machine vision approaches consistently struggle with the complex textures and variable surface characteristics inherent in composite materials14. Modern architectures based on deep convolutional neural networks have achieved incremental improvements but fail to address the fundamental few-shot learning problem15. Performance evaluations of improved algorithms like YOLOv5n demonstrate that architectural refinements alone cannot overcome data dependency16. Comparative studies across metal surface defect detection confirm that even state-of-the-art object detection and semantic segmentation models experience severe performance degradation when training samples are limited17. The current state of deep learning object detection in materials science reveals systematic limitations rather than isolated technical challenges18. Pre-trained deep convolutional networks show promise for invisible crack detection but lack adaptability to novel defect types19. Advances in computational intelligence for polymer composites highlight the persistent gap between theoretical capabilities and practical implementation requirements20.

This research addresses these fundamental barriers through a novel integration of diffusion models and zero-shot learning, establishing a knowledge-driven paradigm that eliminates traditional data dependencies. Zero-shot learning, which enables recognition of categories absent from training data through semantic knowledge transfer from seen to unseen classes, provides the theoretical foundation for handling novel defect types without retraining21. The paradigm has achieved significant success in general image recognition tasks through attribute-based and embedding-based approaches, with recent developments utilizing deep visual-semantic alignments for fine-grained classification and object detection scenarios. In the industrial inspection context, this paradigm leverages attribute-based reasoning to bridge the gap between known and unknown defect categories, addressing the dynamic nature of manufacturing environments where new defect patterns emerge continuously. The framework draws inspiration from cross-modal feature alignment strategies demonstrated in infrared-to-visible object mapping22, incorporating non-global fuzzy pre-enhancement principles to optimize feature extraction23. Synthetic data generation techniques validated through game engine simulations provide theoretical support for the generative approach24. The diffusion probabilistic model architecture builds upon established frameworks for surface defect detection while introducing conditional generation capabilities25. Zero-sample defect detection methodologies inform the unseen category identification mechanism21, enhanced through generalized zero-shot learning with multi-label networks for compound fault diagnosis26. Conditional prompt synthesis techniques guide the cross-modal feature fusion design27, while multimodal large language model architectures expand the reasoning capabilities for anomaly detection28. Manufacturing-specific few-shot learning research validates the knowledge transfer strategies employed29, with cross-modal zero-shot learning successfully demonstrated in ultrasonic inspection applications30. Diffusion model applications in steel surface defect generation confirm the high-fidelity synthesis capabilities31, while spatiotemporal detection networks trained on simulated data demonstrate the effectiveness of synthetic training approaches32. Comprehensive surveys of machine learning-driven material defect detection provide the theoretical foundation and contextual positioning for this integrated approach33.

The core contribution of this research lies in constructing a unified framework that fundamentally reimagines defect detection through the deep integration of diffusion models and zero-shot learning, bridging two previously disparate paradigms in computer vision—generative modeling and zero-shot recognition. This framework advances beyond existing approaches where diffusion models serve merely as data augmentation tools with fixed denoising architectures, or zero-shot learning operates in isolation without generative feature enhancement. Three key innovations establish the methodological originality. The conditional generation mechanism integrates category-specific constraints and spatial guidance into the diffusion process through cross-attention layers, enabling controlled synthesis of defect patterns with explicit morphological specifications rather than relying solely on unconstrained stochastic sampling. The feature space alignment strategy employs maximum mean discrepancy to enforce distributional consistency between the generative pathway and the discriminative pathway, ensuring stable convergence without adversarial training instabilities while preserving complementary information from both modalities. The attribute-driven reasoning framework decomposes visual defect characteristics into weighted semantic descriptors spanning shape, texture, boundary, and distribution properties, establishing compositional knowledge transfer for unseen categories through learnable embeddings rather than requiring exhaustive category-specific annotations. This integration creates a synergistic framework that transcends the limitations of each individual approach, addressing the fundamental tension between diffusion models’ ability to capture complex data distributions despite requiring substantial training samples, and zero-shot learning’s capability for recognizing unseen categories while suffering from the semantic gap between visual features and attribute descriptions. The proposed dual-path architecture leverages these complementary strengths to establish a knowledge-driven detection system that eliminates the traditional prerequisite of exhaustive labeled datasets, which has been identified as the primary bottleneck in deploying deep learning solutions for quality control in composite materials manufacturing. This methodological advance demonstrates particular significance for industrial environments where production runs are often limited and defect types continuously evolve with changing material formulations and processing parameters, providing an efficient, flexible and scalable technical solution that promotes the development of non-destructive testing technology towards intelligence and adaptability.

Materials and methods

Overall architecture design

The microscopic defect detection framework for composite material surfaces proposed in this study builds an end-to-end unified detection system by deeply integrating diffusion models with zero-shot learning mechanisms. The core design concept of this framework lies in leveraging the powerful generative ability of the diffusion model to enhance feature representation, while taking advantage of the generalization benefit of zero-shot learning to accurately identify unseen defect categories. The overall architecture adopts a dual-path collaborative design. The diffusion path is responsible for learning the potential distribution of defects and generating high-quality feature representations, while the zero-shot path achieves cross-modal knowledge transfer through visual-semantic mapping.

The system processes input images through a progressive pipeline. The input composite material image is standardized by the preprocessing module and then simultaneously input into the diffusion model encoder and the visual feature extractor. The diffusion encoder learns the hierarchical representation of the image through multi-scale noise modulation, while the visual feature extractor focuses on capturing local defect patterns and global context information. The features extracted from the two paths are adaptively weighted and combined in the middle fusion layer through the attention mechanism to ensure that the key defect information is fully retained and enhanced, as shown in Fig. 1.

The diffusion path implements a U-Net architecture beginning with a Conv 7 × 7 stride-2 preprocessing layer that transforms the 512 × 512 × 3 input into 256 × 256 × 64 features. The three encoder blocks progressively reduce spatial dimensions through strided convolutions, specifically from 128 × 128 × 128 in Encoder Block 1 to 64 × 64 × 256 in Block 2 and 32 × 32 × 512 in Block 3, incorporating group normalization and activation functions at each stage. Skip connections link corresponding encoder-decoder pairs to preserve fine-grained defect information, while conditional inputs and time embeddings modulate the generation process throughout the network. The zero-shot path utilizes Vision Transformer (ViT-B/16) with patch embedding and a 12-layer transformer block operating on 768-dimensional features, complemented by BERT-base semantic encoding for attribute text processing. Visual and semantic projections map both modalities into a shared embedding space. The entire system achieves parameter optimization through end-to-end training, ensuring a balance between detection accuracy and generalization ability. The architectural design enables simultaneous processing of defect information through complementary pathways: the diffusion-based pathway generates high-fidelity feature representations by modeling the statistical distribution of defect patterns, while the zero-shot pathway leverages semantic attributes to enable recognition of novel defect categories without prior training instances, addressing the fundamental challenge of limited labeled data in industrial quality control applications.

Results

In this study, we propose a method for detecting and classifying microscopic defects on the surface of composite materials that combines diffusion models and zero-shot learning. The core objective of this method is to effectively address the issues of data scarcity and incomplete defect categories in traditional detection methods by generating high-quality defect images and zero-shot learning. To verify the effectiveness of our method, we designed multiple experiments covering aspects such as the generation quality and efficiency of the diffusion model, the performance of zero-shot learning, and the comprehensive performance at the system level. The following will provide a detailed introduction to the design, purpose and setup of the experiment, and present the experimental results.

Experimental configuration

To comprehensively verify the effectiveness of our method, we selected multiple public datasets, including NEU-DET42, DAGM 200743, MVTec AD44, etc. These datasets contain various types of composite material defects, such as cracks, pores, inclusions, etc., and have complex backgrounds and diverse defect morphologies. The experimental environment was equipped with a workstation featuring an NVIDIA RTX 3090 graphics card and 64GB of memory, and experiments were conducted using the PyTorch deep learning framework. These configurations can ensure that we have sufficient computing resources when conducting large-scale data training and inference. All experiments were conducted in the same hardware environment to ensure the fairness of the experiments and the comparability of the results.

In terms of evaluation metrics, we adopted multiple indicators such as Accuracy, Precision, Recall, F1-Score, and Inference Time to comprehensively assess the performance of the model. Accuracy and F1 score can measure a model’s ability to detect different types of defects, while inference time reflects the model’s efficiency in practical industrial applications.

To ensure the reliability of the experiment, we conducted verification on different composite material datasets, including carbon fiber composites and glass fiber composites, etc. The focus of the experiment is to demonstrate the effectiveness of our method in cross-material defect detection and its performance in few-shot learning.

Core verification experiment

To verify the effectiveness of the method we proposed in practical applications, we designed multiple experiments to respectively evaluate the quality and efficiency of the images generated by the diffusion model, the detection ability of zero-shot learning, and the overall performance of the system. The design of each experiment is closely integrated with the actual industrial needs to ensure that the application potential of our method can be comprehensively verified.

The industrial value of zero-shot testing

To verify the practical effectiveness of the zero-shot learning framework in the detection of composite material defects, we designed comparative experiments to quantitatively evaluate its detection performance in both visible and unseen defect categories. The motivation for the experiment stems from the dynamic changes in defect types in industrial sites. New defects often lack labeled samples, making it difficult for traditional supervised learning to adapt quickly. The experimental configuration adopted the MVTec AD dataset, which was divided into visible categories (cracks, pores, inclusions) and invisible categories (delamination, fiber breakage, foreign matter) in a 7:3 ratio. The comparison includes Traditional CNN as a baseline supervised method, Few-shot Learning and YOLOv8 + FSL representing few-shot approaches, DRAEM and PatchCore as anomaly detection methods, and our proposed diffusion-zero-shot framework. Accuracy and F1-score metrics were evaluated with five-fold averaging for statistical reliability. The experimental protocol establishes rigorous controls to quantify both challenges independently through structured validation procedures. Data scarcity effects are isolated by systematically reducing labeled training samples from 1000 images through intermediate levels of 500 and 100 samples to complete absence, where traditional supervised methods undergo full retraining at each annotation level using identical architectures and hyperparameters to ensure performance variations reflect pure data availability differences rather than implementation artifacts, while the zero-shot framework maintains operation without any labeled training data across all evaluation stages to quantify annotation elimination capability. Incomplete category generalization is validated through strict dataset partitioning where three morphologically diverse unseen types (planar delamination, linear fiber breakage, irregular foreign matter) remain completely excluded from all training procedures including gradient computations and parameter updates, with baseline methods receiving exclusive training exposure on the three seen categories (cracks, pores, inclusions) to establish equivalent knowledge foundations before evaluation on withheld types, thereby measuring pure compositional reasoning ability rather than memorization-based recognition. As shown in Fig. 4, the methods exhibit distinct performance patterns across seen and unseen defect categories, with traditional supervised approaches showing severe degradation on unseen defects while our method maintains robust performance on both.

Discussion

The diffusion-zero-shot joint framework proposed in this paper has significant technical advantages in the defect detection of composite materials. The FID score obtained by this method is 18.2, which represents an essential improvement in generation quality compared with 42.8 of the traditional GAN method. Comprehensive research on machine learning-driven material defect detection points out that traditional deep learning methods are generally restricted by data scarcity and generalization ability limitations. However, this study effectively solves this technical bottleneck through conditional diffusion modeling33. The defect-free manufacturing paradigm emphasizes that detection methods need to adapt to dynamically changing defect types. The zero-shot learning framework of this study is precisely a direct response to this demand45. The review of non-destructive testing techniques for fiber-reinforced polymer composites emphasizes the importance of multimodal fusion. The dual-path collaborative design in this study is highly consistent with this view and further integrates the advantages of generative models and discriminative models46.

From the perspective of industrial application feasibility, the average reasoning time achieved in this study is 140.5ms, meeting the real-time requirements. Although the fatigue damage detection technology for composite material structures based on Lamb waves and deep autoencoders performs well in specific scenarios, its strong reliance on pre-training data limits its adaptability to material types. In this study, the mAP@50 scores of various materials such as carbon fibers and glass fibers all remain above 0.713. It demonstrated stronger generalization performance47. The integrated innovation of artificial intelligence and non-destructive testing technology provides a theoretical basis for this study. This study achieves a deeper feature learning ability by introducing the diffusion model48. Although the generative model training method based on fractal geometry synthesis has contributed to data augulation, it lacks systematic consideration of zero-shot scenarios. The attribute driven zero-shot detection framework proposed in this study fills this technical gap49.

Although this research has achieved remarkable results, it also has certain limitations. Research on defect detection methods of composite materials based on deep learning networks indicates that there is still a problem of detection errors in complex texture backgrounds. The robustness of this study in extremely complex backgrounds still has room for improvement. Studies on the detection of invisible cracks in ceramic materials by pre-trained deep convolutional neural networks show that the domain adaptability of the pre-trained model has a decisive influence on the detection performance. This study still needs to be expanded in terms of the coverage of larger material types. Research on the progress of computational intelligence in polymer composites reveals the development trend of the deep integration of materials science and artificial intelligence. This study provides a new technical path for this integration, but further exploration is still needed in terms of the depth of integration with physical modeling. While the current zero-shot framework eliminates the dependency on labeled training data, practical industrial deployments often encounter scenarios where limited annotations exist for frequently occurring defect types alongside completely unseen categories. This hybrid data availability presents an opportunity for semi-supervised extensions, where the framework could leverage available labels through a dual-objective loss function that combines supervised learning for known defects with attribute-based reasoning for novel patterns, thereby refining visual-semantic alignment while maintaining zero-shot generalization capability. Such extensions warrant systematic ablation studies to quantify the performance-cost tradeoffs across varying annotation budgets, representing a promising direction for maximizing detection accuracy when partial labels are available. Beyond the domain of composite material inspection, the proposed knowledge-driven paradigm demonstrates natural extensibility to other process monitoring applications characterized by dynamic operational regimes. Specifically, the zero-shot learning mechanism could address working condition identification in rotary kilns where combustion states evolve continuously with feedstock variations and process adjustments50, and enable soft sensing for index prediction in cement clinker burning processes where material compositions and thermal profiles change dynamically51. These applications share the fundamental requirement of adapting to previously unseen operational patterns without extensive retraining, validating the broader industrial relevance of the proposed approach.

Conclusions

A framework for detecting microscopic defects on the surface of composite materials based on the deep fusion diffusion model and zero-shot learning mechanism has achieved a technological breakthrough for traditional detection methods in scenarios of data scarcity and dynamic changes in defect types by constructing a dual-path collaborative architecture. This framework adopts a deep coupling design of conditional diffusion process and vision-semantic joint embedding space. On multiple benchmark datasets such as NEU-DET, DAGM 2007 and MVTec AD, it achieves a detection accuracy of mAP@50 0.777 and a recall rate of 0.745, and the reasoning time for a single image is controlled within 140.5ms. It meets the requirements of real-time industrial detection. The zero-shot learning mechanism achieved a detection performance of an F1 score of 0.692 without the need for labeled samples, which is equivalent to the effect of traditional supervised learning methods using 500 labeled samples, achieving a 100% cost savings in labeling.

This study verified the feasibility of integrating generative artificial intelligence and zero-shot learning in the field of industrial defect detection, demonstrating significant technical advantages and economic value in practical engineering applications. Through ablation experiments, it was verified that the collaborative optimization of LoRA adaptive adjustment, mask guidance mechanism and attribute prompt module increased the system’s F1 score to 0.738 and reduced the FID score to 22.4, fully demonstrating the effective synergy among various technical modules. This framework demonstrates excellent generalization ability on different matrix materials, providing a practical and feasible technical solution for intelligent quality control in the composite materials manufacturing industry, and laying a solid foundation for the in-depth application of industrial artificial intelligence in the manufacturing field.

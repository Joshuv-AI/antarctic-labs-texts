Charges, complex structures, and perturbations of instantons
Lars Andersson, Bernardo Araneda

Abstract
. Hermitian non-Kähler Einstein 4-manifolds have a quasi-locally conserved charge associated to spin-lowering via Killing spinors, and corresponding to a parameter of the moduli space. This charge is evaluated for all explicitly known examples of gravitational instantons. Generic gravitational perturbations are shown to admit a closed 2-form that measures the perturbation to this charge, generalizing previous Lorentzian results on the linearized mass of perturbed Kerr black holes.



1.
Introduction

In Euclidean quantum gravity, the dominant contribution to the gravitational path integral is expected to come from metrics near the minima of the Euclidean action
[
23
]
, that is from gravitational instantons and small deformations of them. In the one-loop expansion, deformations of a classical solution
(M,g_{ab})
represent quantum corrections
[
25
]
, and in the case of perturbations satisfying the linearized Einstein equations, they provide information about the moduli space
\mathscr{E}(M)
of instantons, describing the structure of the tangent space of
\mathscr{E}(M)
at
g_{ab}
and allowing to analyze questions such as integrability, infinitesimal rigidity
[
4
,
5
]
and spectral stability (e.g.
[
18
,
12
]
); cf.
[
10
]
for the compact case.

The study of gravitational instantons is further motivated by the recently discovered Chen-Teo instanton
[
16
]
, a Ricci-flat, asymptotically flat (AF) solution that gives a counter-example to the Euclidean black hole uniqueness conjecture
[
22
,
24
]
and
contributes, in addition to flat space and to the Schwarzschild and Kerr instantons, to the possible semi-classical states of the gravitational field with AF boundary conditions
[
32
]
.
Recent work by Li and Sun
[
36
]
shows the existence of a new infinite family of AF gravitational instantons,
and the classification problem remains largely open.
Previous classification results include
[
11
,
3
,
35
]
.

For the vacuum Einstein equations with or without cosmological constant,
boundary conditions of interest in quantum gravity include, in addition to AF, asymptotcally locally flat (ALF),
asymptotically locally Euclidean (ALE), and compact manifolds. All explicitly known instantons are (at least locally

The above result is the Riemannian analog of a Lorentzian conservation law first found by Jordan, Ehlers, and Sachs

We shall prove that generic gravitational perturbations of Hermitian instantons, with or without cosmological constant

\displaystyle\delta{Q}[S]=\int_{S}\delta\hat{\omega}.

(1.4)

Moreover, the result (

3.24

The new conservation law (

The result (

In a subsequent paper

Physically, this means that there are no higher-order quantum corrections to the instantons, i.e. the one-loop expansion remains valid.

Throughout the article,

The (locally defined) unprimed and primed spinor bundles are

An unprimed spin dyad is

,
\tilde{m}^{a}=\iota^{A}\alpha^{A^{\prime}}
(see e.g.
[
40

and
\tilde{m}^{a}=-\bar{m}^{a}

\ell^{a}n_{a}=1=-m^{a}\tilde{m}_{a}
. The real and imaginary parts of the null tetrad define four real vectors
\{e^{a}_{\bf a}\}

e^{a}_{0}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\ell^{a}+n^{a})

e^{a}_{1}=i\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\ell^{a}-n^{a})

e^{a}_{2}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(m^{a}-\tilde{m}^{a})

e^{a}_{3}=i\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(m^{a}+\tilde{m}^{a})

(This is the analog of
[

The dual basis, denoted

, is defined by

\displaystyle\epsilon_{AB}\epsilon_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}=2(\ell_{(a}n_{b)}-m_{(a}\tilde{m}_{b)})=+\delta_{\bf ab}e_{a}^{\bf a}e_{b}^{\bf b}=+g_{ab}.

Thus the reality conditions on the null tetrad imposed above, which are in turn defined by the Riemannian spinor conjugation

\sigma^{0}_{A^{\prime}}=0

\Psi_{2}^{-1/3}\Sigma_{ab}\propto\Psi_{ABCD}K^{CD}\epsilon_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\Psi_{ABCD}K^{CD}\epsilon_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}

For our Einstein Hermitian geometry

using (

eq. (

Combining the two interpretations above, we can then define a charge associated to every 2-cycle

3.3.
Linearized charges

Given a smooth one-parameter family of tensor fields
T(s)
in
M
, we define the
k
-jets

\displaystyle\delta^{k}T=\left.\left[\frac{{\rm d}^{k}}{{\rm d}s^{k}}T(s)\right]\right|_{s=0}.

(3.23)

The zeroth jet
T=T(0)
is the background value of
T(s)
, and the first jet
\delta{T}
is the linearization of
T(s)
at
T
. We recall that, under an infinitesimal diffeomorphism generated by an arbitrary vector field
\xi^{a}
, the linearization
\delta{T}
transforms as

\displaystyle\delta{T}\to\delta{T}+\pounds_{\xi}T,

(3.24)

which in this context is referred to as a gauge transformation.
We say that the linearization
\delta{T}
is gauge invariant if it is invariant under (
3.24
) for all
\xi^{a}
. This holds, in particular, if
T=0
.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
g_{ab}(s)
be a one-parameter family of Riemannian metrics in
M
, such that, for all
s
,

\displaystyle\nabla^{(s)\,a}W^{+}_{abcd}(s)=0.

(3.25)

Let
Z_{ab}(s)
be a real self-dual 2-form,
Z_{ab}(s)Z^{ab}(s)=2
, and define

\displaystyle\Sigma_{ab}(s):={}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}W^{+}{}_{ab}{}^{cd}(s)Z_{cd}(s),

(3.26)

\displaystyle\Psi_{2}(s):={}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{8}W^{+}_{abcd}(s)Z^{ab}(s)Z^{cd}(s),

(3.27)

\displaystyle\hat{\omega}_{ab}(s):={}

\displaystyle-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{3}\left[\Psi_{2}(s)^{-1/3}\Sigma_{ab}(s)-\Psi_{2}(s)^{2/3}Z_{ab}(s)\right].

(3.28)

Suppose the background metric
g_{ab}=g_{ab}(0)
is Einstein and Hermitian, with (closed) self-dual Kähler form
\hat{\omega}_{ab}=\hat{\omega}_{ab}(0)
, and
\Psi_{2}=\Psi_{2}(0)\neq 0
. Then:

\displaystyle{\rm d}\,\delta\hat{\omega}=0,

(3.29)

where
\delta\hat{\omega}
is the 2-form

\displaystyle\delta\hat{\omega}_{ab}=-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{3}\left[\Psi_{2}^{-1/3}\delta\Sigma_{ab}-\Psi_{2}^{2/3}\delta{Z}_{ab}\right].

(3.30)

Moreover, the result (
3.29
) is gauge invariant.

Proof.

For all
s
, we have

\displaystyle{\rm d}{Z}(s)={}

\displaystyle-2f(s)\wedge Z(s),

(3.31)

\displaystyle{\rm d}\Sigma(s)={}

\displaystyle f(s)\wedge\Sigma(s)+(*K)(s),

(3.32)

where the first equality is simply the definition of the Lee form (recall (
3.2
)), and the second equality follows from (
3.18
) after using that (
3.25
) holds for all
s
. Since the background is Einstein and Hermitian, then
(*K)(s)=O(s^{2})
, so
{\rm d}(*K)/{\rm d}s\,|_{s=0}=0
. Thus, the linearization of (
3.31
)-(
3.32
) is

\displaystyle{\rm d}\,\delta Z={}

\displaystyle-2\delta{f}\wedge Z-2f\wedge\delta Z,

\displaystyle{\rm d}\,\delta\Sigma={}

\displaystyle\delta{f}\wedge\Sigma+f\wedge\delta\Sigma,

where
Z=Z(0)
,
\Sigma=\Sigma(0)
and
f=f(0)
. Using the background identities (
3.11
) and (
3.10
), it follows that

\displaystyle\delta{f}\wedge\Sigma=-2\Psi_{2}\delta{f}\wedge Z=\Psi_{2}({\rm d}\delta Z+2f\wedge\delta Z)=\Psi_{2}^{1/3}{\rm d}\left[\Psi_{2}^{2/3}\delta Z\right],

so

\displaystyle{\rm d}\delta\Sigma=\Psi_{2}^{1/3}{\rm d}\left[\Psi_{2}^{2/3}\delta Z\right]+\Psi_{2}^{-1/3}{\rm d}\Psi_{2}^{1/3}\wedge\delta\Sigma

and therefore

\displaystyle\Psi_{2}^{1/3}{\rm d}\left[\Psi_{2}^{-1/3}\delta\Sigma\right]=\Psi_{2}^{1/3}{\rm d}\left[\Psi_{2}^{2/3}\delta Z\right],

which gives (
3.29
).

Finally, consider a gauge transformation
\delta\hat{\omega}\to\delta\hat{\omega}+\pounds_{\xi}\hat{\omega}
. We have
\pounds_{\xi}\hat{\omega}={\rm d}(\xi\lrcorner\,\hat{\omega})
since
\hat{\omega}
is closed. Hence,
\delta\hat{\omega}\to\delta\hat{\omega}+{\rm d}(\xi\lrcorner\,\hat{\omega})
, so the result (
3.29
) is gauge-invariant.
∎

4.
Examples

In this section we shall analyze several examples of the charges (
1.3
), by computing the conformal Kähler structures, the 2-cycles, and then the periods (
1.3
), for both compact and non-compact instantons. For each of these examples, Theorem

implies that generic gravitational perturbations also have a quasi-locally conserved charge, given by (
1.4
).

A non-trivial issue is how to find the independent 2-cycles, for which we need the second homology group

. To do this, we shall use the fact that all of the examples are toric. This implies that one can apply the rod structure formalism as in
[
15
]
, which allows to determine the topological properties of the instanton. For example, for a rod structure with
n
turning points, the Euler characteristic
\chi(M)
is equal to
n
. In the non-compact case, we see from
[
37
, Theorem 1]
that
H_{2}(M)\cong\mathbb{Z}^{n-1}
.
In the compact case, we can use the fact that
\chi(M)
is also the alternating sum of the Betti numbers

to deduce the number of independent 2-cycles.

Below we shall also use the terminology of ‘nuts’ and ‘bolts’, for which we refer to the paper by Gibbons and Hawking
[
24
]
.

Remark 4.7
.

This result implies that both of the two parameters of the Chen-Teo instanton can be obtained as charge integrals of the Kähler form. As mentioned in the introduction, this is in contrast to the Kerr case, where the Kähler construction gives only one of the two parameters, namely the mass, see (
4.4
). We can understand this difference from the fact that there are two bolts in Chen-Teo, whereas Kerr has only one.
In a sense, the Chen-Teo instanton is a “double” object, as suggested by the interpretation in
[
17
, §5]
in terms of two touching Kerr-NUTs.

IVHS of nodal plane curves
Edoardo Sernesi

Abstract
Let 𝒱 d , n \mathcal{V}_{d,n} be the Severi variety of irreducible plane curves of degree d ≥ 4 d\geq 4 having n n nodes, with 0 ≤ n ≤ ( d − 1 2 ) − 1 0\leq n\leq\binom{d-1}{2}-1 . We prove that for every [ C ¯ ] ∈ 𝒱 d , n [\overline{C}]\in\mathcal{V}_{d,n} , the infinitesimal variation of the Hodge structure of the normalization C C of C ¯ \overline{C} is maximal as [ C ¯ ] [\overline{C}] moves in 𝒱 d , n \mathcal{V}_{d,n} . As a preliminary result, we also prove that the family of curves of genus g ≥ 1 g\geq 1 mapping with degree d ≥ 2 d\geq 2 to a fixed curve Y Y of genus π \pi has maximal variation if and only if π = 0 \pi=0 .

Introduction

All schemes in this paper will be defined over
\mathbb{C}
, the field of complex numbers.

Let
g\geq 1
and let
\mathcal{M}_{g}
, resp.
\mathcal{A}_{g}
, be the stack of smooth projective curves of genus
g
, respectively the stack of principally polarized abelian varieties of dimension
g
. The
Torelli map

\mathcal{T}:\mathcal{M}_{g}\longrightarrow\mathcal{A}_{g}

assigns to a curve
[C]\in\mathcal{M}_{g}
its polarized jacobian
\mathcal{T}([C])=(J(C),\Theta)
where
\Theta
is a symmetric theta divisor. At a given
[C]
the differential of this morphism is a linear map

d\mathcal{T}_{[C]}:H^{1}(C,T_{C})\longrightarrow\mathrm{Sym}^{2}H^{1}(C,\mathcal{O}_{C})

(see e.g.
[
Se1
, Example 3.4.24(iii)]
) whose codomain we will view as the subset

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathfrak{S}}(H^{0}(C,\omega_{C}),H^{1}(C,\mathcal{O}_{C}))\subset\mathrm{Hom}(H^{0}(C,\omega_{C}),H^{1}(C,\mathcal{O}_{C}))

consisting of self-dual linear maps.
d\mathcal{T}_{[C]}
can be described in more than one way. Firstly, following
[
OS
]
and
[
G
]
, we can interpret
d\mathcal{T}_{[C]}
as the dual of the natural multiplication map

\mu:\mathrm{Sym}^{2}H^{0}(C,\omega_{C})\longrightarrow H^{0}(C,\omega_{C}^{\otimes 2})

Alternatively, one can describe
d\mathcal{T}_{[C]}
by representing each element
v\in H^{1}(C,T_{C})=\mathrm{Ext}^{1}(\omega_{C},\mathcal{O}_{C})
by an extension

v:0\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{C}\longrightarrow\mathcal{E}\longrightarrow\omega_{C}\longrightarrow 0

and letting
d\mathcal{T}_{[C]}(v)=\delta_{v}
, where

\delta_{v}:H^{0}(C,\omega_{C})\longrightarrow H^{1}(C,\mathcal{O}_{C})

is the coboundary map of
v
. It is straightforward to check that the two descriptions of

d\mathcal{T}_{[C]}
coincide. The map
d\mathcal{T}_{[C]}(v)=\delta_{v}
is called
infinitesimal variation of the Hodge structure
(IVHS) of
C
along
v
, and the map
d\mathcal{T}_{[C]}
is also called
IVHS map
.

Torelli’s Theorem
asserts that
\mathcal{T}
is injective. Nevertheless
\mathcal{T}
is not everywhere an embedding: the
Infinitesimal Torelli’s Theorem
asserts that
\mathcal{T}
is an embedding at
[C]
, i.e.
d\mathcal{T}_{[C]}
is injective, if and only if
g\leq 2
or
g\geq 3
and
C
is non-hyperelliptic. Using the first interpretation of
d\mathcal{T}_{[C]}
this fact is an immediate consequence of the classical
Noether’s Theorem
, which asserts that
\mu
is surjective if and only if
g\leq 2
or
g\geq 3
and
C
is non-hyperelliptic.

Observing that the IVHS map
d\mathcal{T}_{[C]}(v)=\delta_{v}
is a linear map between two vector spaces (of the same dimension
g
), we can associate to
v
the rank of
\delta_{v}
, which is called
variation
of
v
in the literature. One says that
v
has
maximal variation
if
\delta_{v}
is an isomorphism, i.e. if
v
has variation equal to
g
. This notion should not be confused with the one considered in
[
DH
]
and in
[
B
]
with a different meaning.

It is natural to ask what are the possibilities for the variation of
v
, i.e. for the rank of
\delta_{v}
, in particular whether it can be maximal, as
C
and
v\in H^{1}(C,T_{C})
vary under suitable given constrains.
In the most general situation, namely when
C
and
v
are arbitrary, we have the following

Proposition 1.1

(
[
G
]
, Corollary (5.20))
.

For any curve
C
of genus
g\geq 1
, there is
v\in H^{1}(C,T_{C})
having maximal variation.

For a different proof see also
[
FP
]
, Lemma 1.3.
Given the above, the next natural questions arise if we assume that
C
is not arbitrary but appears as the fibre
C\cong\mathcal{C}(b)
over a point

of a family

of projective nonsingular curves of genus

. We then consider the Kodaira-Spencer map

\kappa_{b}(f):T_{b}B\longrightarrow H^{1}(C,T_{C})

and the composition

T_{b}B\xrightarrow{\kappa_{b}(f)}H^{1}(C,T_{C})\xrightarrow{d\mathcal{T}_{[C]}}\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathfrak{S}}(H^{0}(C,\omega_{C}),H^{1}(C,\mathcal{O}_{C}))

(1)

We then turn to maps with target

.

Let

The paper is organized as follows. In §2 we give a cohomological approach to IVHS of curves in families in various situations, and we recall some results, e.g. the non maximality of IVHS of curves on irregular surfaces. We also prove Theorem

In §3 we discuss families of nodal curves on surfaces, and in §4 we specialize to the case of

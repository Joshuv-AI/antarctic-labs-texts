Observer Design over Hypercomplex Quaternions
Michael Sebek

Abstract
We develop observer design over hypercomplex quaternions in a characteristic-polynomial-free framework. Using the standard right-module convention, we derive a right observable companion form and its companion polynomial that encodes error dynamics via right-eigenvalue similarity classes. The design mirrors the real/complex case—coefficient updates in companion coordinates, followed by a similarity back—yet avoids determinants, characteristic/minimal polynomials, and Cayley–Hamilton identities that do not transfer to quaternions. We also give an Ackermann-type construction for the important case of closed-loop companion polynomials with real coefficients, ensuring similarity-equivariant evaluation. The results yield simple recipes for full-order observers directly over quaternions, clarify the role of right spectra and their similarity classes, and pinpoint when classical one-shot formulas remain valid. Numerical examples illustrate the method and advantages over vectorized or complex-adjoint surrogates.

INTRODUCTION

Quaternions extend
\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{C}
to the division algebra
\mathbb{H}
, but—unlike
\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{C}
—multiplication in
\mathbb{H}
is
noncommutative
. This single feature alters standard observer machinery: right and left eigenvalues must be distinguished, and (on the right) eigenvalues are defined only up to
quaternionic similarity classes
. Familiar scalar proxies—determinants, characteristic polynomials, a full Cayley–Hamilton theorem—do not carry over verbatim; even polynomial evaluation depends on whether coefficients act on the left or on the right, so
left and right zeros need not coincide
. As a result, several classical constructions require new formulations when one works natively over
\mathbb{H}
; see, e.g., quaternionic stability
[
16
]
and linear algebra over
\mathbb{H}

[
18
]
.

In this paper, we develop an observer-focused, determinant–free framework built on
observability
and its duality with controllability. We construct the observable companion (observability-form) realization directly from the observability matrix, link its right spectrum to companion-polynomial zeros, and derive a quaternionic
Observer Ackermann formula
that assigns right–eigenvalue similarity classes using a desired real-coefficient polynomial. Throughout, we remain in the hypercomplex (scalar) model of
\mathbb{H}
where signals, states, and operators live directly in
\mathbb{H}.
We work with the right spectra, and use left substitution for the companion polynomial—choices that make noncommutativity explicit and usable.

In aerospace and robotics applications, by contrast, quaternions are typically treated in their

4
-vector (scalar–vector) form

Here, however, we adopt the
hypercomplex
definition

and work directly with quaternionic states and operators. This preserves the noncommutative algebra essential for right/left spectra and similarity classes, for companion/observability forms, and for a central (real-coefficient) polynomial calculus
a(A)
without coordinate artifacts. The viewpoint is also motivated by areas where the hypercomplex structure is intrinsic—e.g., quantum dynamics and control
[
5
,

, NMR/MRI pulse design
[

]

. We believe the observer results established here over

II
LINEAR ALGEBRA OVER QUATERNIONS

Quaternions

A quaternion is a
hypercomplex scalar

q=a+b\mathbf{i}+c\mathbf{j}+d\mathbf{k}
with
a,b,c,d\in\mathbb{R}
and units
\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j},\mathbf{k}
satisfying

\mathbf{i}^{2}=\mathbf{j}^{2}=\mathbf{k}^{2}=\mathbf{i}\mathbf{j}\mathbf{k}=-1
.
The set
\mathbb{H}
is a four-dimensional associative but
noncommutative
division algebra over
\mathbb{R}
.
Write
\operatorname{Re}(q)=a
,
\operatorname{Im}(q)=b\mathbf{i}+c\mathbf{j}+d\mathbf{k}
, conjugate
\bar{q}=a-b\mathbf{i}-c\mathbf{j}-d\mathbf{k}
, and norm
|q|=\sqrt{q\bar{q}}
.

Other coordinate parameterizations are common. In the
scalar–vector
form,
a quaternion is the real pair
q_{\mathrm{sv}}=(v_{\mathrm{s}},v_{\mathrm{v}})
, where the scalar part is
v_{\mathrm{s}}=a
and
the vector part is
v_{\mathrm{v}}=[\,b,\;c,\;d\,]^{\mathsf{T}}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
Equivalently, one stacks these into the vector–scalar
4
-vector

q_{\mathrm{sv}}=\begin{bmatrix}v_{\mathrm{v}}^{\mathsf{T}}&v_{\mathrm{s}}\end{bmatrix}^{\mathsf{T}}\in\mathbb{R}^{4}.

These representations are standard in aerospace and robotics.

In this paper, we work natively
over
\mathbb{H}
to preserve the noncommutative structure needed for right/left spectra and
observable–companion constructions.
For details, see
[
18
]
.

Similarity classes

Quaternions
q,r
are
similar
, denoted
q\sim r
, if
q=\alpha^{-1}r\alpha
for some nonzero
\alpha\in\mathbb{H}
.
Similarity preserves
\operatorname{Re}(\cdot)
,
|\cdot|
, and
|\operatorname{Im}(\cdot)|
and partitions
\mathbb{H}
into equivalence classes
[\cdot]
.
Real quaternions are isolated:
[a]=\{a\}
.
If
\operatorname{Im}(q)\neq 0
, then
[q]=\{\operatorname{Re}(q)+u|\operatorname{Im}(q)|:u\in\mathrm{span}\{\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j},\mathbf{k}\},|u|=1\}
, a 2-sphere meeting the standard complex line
\{a+b\mathbf{i}:a,b\in\mathbb{R}\}
in the two points
\operatorname{Re}(q)\pm\mathbf{i}|\operatorname{Im}(q)|
.
For instance,
[\mathbf{k}]
contains all imaginary units, including
\pm\mathbf{i}
.

Matrices over
\mathbb{H}

For
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\mathbb{H}^{m\times n}
denote the set of
m\times n
matrices with entries in
\mathbb{H}.

We write
A^{*}=\bar{A}^{\mathsf{T}}
(entry-wise conjugation followed by transpose).
Noncommutativity affects basic identities: in general

\overline{AB}\neq\bar{A}\bar{B}
and
(AB)^{\mathsf{T}}\neq B^{\mathsf{T}}A^{\mathsf{T}}
, while the mixed rule
(AB)^{*}=B^{*}A^{*}
does hold.
See
[
18
]
for further details.

Caveat for design.

Classical
\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{C}
tools—determinant, characteristic polynomial, and the Cayley–Hamilton theorem—do not carry over verbatim to
\mathbb{H}
. The determinant admits nonstandard variants with different meanings; a “characteristic polynomial” in the usual sense may be absent or enjoy only limited properties; and Cayley–Hamilton can fail or requires alternative formulations. Hence these tools cannot be used naively for observer design over
\mathbb{H}
and are replaced here by determinant-free, characteristic-polynomial-free constructions.

Rank and invertibility

For
A\in\mathbb{H}^{m\times n}
, the quaternionic rank
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{H}}A
is the maximal number of
right–independent
columns (i.e., if
\sum_{j}a_{j}c_{j}=0
with
c_{j}\in\mathbb{H}
on the right, then all
c_{j}=0
).
A square
A\in\mathbb{H}^{n\times n}
is invertible iff
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{H}}A=n

[
18
]
.

Right eigenvalues and the spectrum

For
A\in\mathbb{H}^{n\times n}
, a
right eigenpair

(v,\lambda)
satisfies

\displaystyle Av

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle v\lambda,\qquad v\in\mathbb{H}^{n}\setminus\{0\},\ \lambda\in\mathbb{H}.

(1)

Right scaling of
v
by
\alpha\neq 0
replaces
\lambda
by
\alpha^{-1}\lambda\alpha
, so right eigenvalues are defined only up to similarity.
The right spectrum is thus the set of classes

\sigma_{\mathscr{r}}(A):=\{[\lambda]:\exists v\neq 0\text{ with }Av=v\lambda\}\subset\mathbb{H}/\!\sim,

which is invariant under similarity
A\mapsto S^{-1}AS

[
18
]
.
When needed, take the unique complex representative with nonnegative imaginary part—the
standard eigenvalue
.

Polynomials and evaluation

Let
\mathbb{H}[\lambda]
be the ring of quaternionic polynomials
p(\lambda)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}p_{k}\lambda^{k}
with
p_{k}\in\mathbb{H}
and indeterminate
\lambda
commuting with all coefficients.
Evaluation at
q\in\mathbb{H}
depends on side:
the
right value
is
p(q)_{\mathscr{r}}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}p_{k}q^{k}
,
while the
left value
is
p(q)_{\ell}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}q^{k}p_{k}
.

Left/right zeros

A
right zero
of
p
is
q
with
p(q)_{\mathscr{r}}=0
; a
left zero
satisfies
p(q)_{\ell}=0
.
Left and right zeros correspond up to similarity:
if
p(q)_{\ell}=0
, then for some
\alpha\neq 0
,
p(\alpha^{-1}q\alpha)_{\mathscr{r}}=0
, and conversely
[
8
]
.
If all
p_{k}\in\mathbb{R}
(central), then
p(q)_{\mathscr{r}}=p(q)_{\ell}
and the two zero sets coincide.

Isolated vs. spherical zeros

A degree-
n
quaternionic polynomial has either
n
(counting multiplicity) right zeros or infinitely many.
A nonreal zero is either
isolated
(a single point in its class) or
spherical
(the full class
[q]
, a 2-sphere).
If the coefficients are real, every zero is either real (hence isolated) or spherical
[
17
]
.

Notation

We regard
\mathbb{H}^{n}
as a right
\mathbb{H}
-module.
Let
e_{i}
be the
i
th canonical basis vector of
\mathbb{R}^{n}\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}
(a
1
in position
i
, zeros elsewhere); in particular,
e_{n}=[0,\ldots,0,1]^{\mathsf{T}}
.
Because these entries are real (central), left/right scalar multiplication is unambiguous whenever
e_{i}
appears.

III
QUATERNIONIC STATE–SPACE SISO SYSTEMS

We study continuous-time linear time-invariant (LTI) single-input single-output (SISO) systems over the division algebra
\mathbb{H}
.

Model

In state-space form, the dynamics are

\displaystyle\dot{x}(t)

\displaystyle=Ax(t)+Bu(t),

(2)

\displaystyle y(t)

\displaystyle=Cx(t)+Du(t),

where
A\in\mathbb{H}^{n\times n}
,
B\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
(column),
C\in\mathbb{H}^{1\times n}
(row),
D\in\mathbb{H}
,
the state vector is
x(t)\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
, and the input/output scalars are
u(t),y(t)\in\mathbb{H}
.

Conventions

We adopt the
right-module
convention: matrices act from the left and quaternionic scalars multiply signals on the
right
. Thus the products
Bu(t)
and
Du(t)
are right-multiplications by
u(t)
; this guarantees right-linearity in both
x
and
u
.

Internal stability

With
u\!\equiv\!0
, stability means
x(t)\!\to\!0
(CT) or
x(k)\!\to\!0
(DT) for all initial states.
Equivalently, using the standard right eigenvalues
\lambda
of
A
, the system is stable
iff
\operatorname{Re}\lambda<0
for all
\lambda
; (see
[
16
]
).

Controllability

As in the real/complex case, controllability means the ability to steer the state from any
x_{0}\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
to any
x_{f}\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
within a finite horizon by a suitable input.
In the single–input setting, the
controllability matrix
is

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigl[BAB\cdots A^{n-1}B\bigr]\in\mathbb{H}^{n\times n},

and the pair
(A,B)
is controllable iff
\mathcal{C}
is invertible over
\mathbb{H}

(equivalently, iff
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{H}}\mathcal{C}=n
)
[
9
,
18
,
21
]
.

Observability

Dually, observability means that the initial state can be uniquely reconstructed from finitely many output measurements.
In the single–output setting, the
observability matrix
is

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}C\\
CA\\
\vdots\\
CA^{n-1}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{H}^{n\times n},

(3)

and the pair
(A,C)
is observable iff
\mathcal{O}
is invertible over
\mathbb{H}

(equivalently, iff
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{H}}\mathcal{O}=n
)
[
9
,
18
,
21
]
.

Similarity

All rank conditions are over
\mathbb{H}
and are invariant under similarity:

A\mapsto S^{-1}AS
,
B\mapsto S^{-1}B
,
C\mapsto CS
for any invertible
S\!\in\!\mathbb{H}^{n\times n}
.

IV
OBSERVABLE COMPANION FORM AND COMPANION POLYNOMIAL

In the classical (real/complex) LTI setting, two staple tools are the observable companion form and the characteristic or minimal polynomial. For quaternionic models, the observable companion form remains valid. By contrast, characteristic and minimal polynomials do not translate verbatim, since quaternions lack a straightforward determinant. Consequently, we replace them with a suitably defined quaternionic companion polynomial.

Definition 1

(
Observable companion form and companion polynomial
)
.

Let
A_{o}\in\mathbb{H}^{n\times n}
and
C_{o}\in\mathbb{H}^{1\times n}
be

\displaystyle A_{o}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}0&\ldots&0&-a_{0}\\
1&\ldots&0&-a_{1}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&\ldots&1&-a_{n-1}\end{bmatrix},

(4)

\displaystyle C_{o}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}0&0&\ldots&1\end{bmatrix},

(5)

with
a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{n-1}\in\mathbb{H}
. We say that
(A_{o},C_{o})
is in right
observable companion (canonical) form
. The
companion polynomial
associated with
(A_{o},C_{o})
(or with
A_{o}
) is the monic quaternionic polynomial

\displaystyle a(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a_{0}+a_{1}\lambda+\cdots+a_{n-1}\lambda^{n-1}+\lambda^{n}\in\mathbb{H}[\lambda].

If
A\in\mathbb{H}^{n\times n}
is
cyclic
with respect to the row
C
—i.e., there exists
C\in\mathbb{H}^{1\times n}
with
the observability matrix
\mathcal{O}
invertible—then
A
is similar to the right observable companion form. The next theorem gives a constructive procedure.

Theorem 1

(
Determinant-free companion form
)
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{H}^{n\times n}
and
C\in\mathbb{H}^{1\times n}
. Assume the SISO observability matrix

\mathcal{O}
from (
3
) is invertible over
\mathbb{H}
.

1.

There exists an invertible similarity
T\in\mathbb{H}^{n\times n}
such that

A_{o}\;=\;T^{-1}AT,\qquad C_{o}\;=\;CT,

and the pair
(A_{o},C_{o})
is in the right observable companion form

\begin{IEEEeqnarraybox}[]{[}][c]{rCl}A_{o}&=&\begin{bmatrix}0&0&\cdots&-a_{0}\\
1&0&\cdots&-a_{1}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&\cdots&1&-a_{n-1}\end{bmatrix},\\
C_{o}&=&\begin{bmatrix}0&0&\cdots&1\end{bmatrix},\end{IEEEeqnarraybox}

(6)

where the scalars
a_{0},\ldots,a_{n-1}\in\mathbb{H}
are
defined
as the (negatives of the) entries in the
rightmost column of
A_{o}
.

2.

The corresponding similarity matrix is uniquely determined as

\displaystyle s

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}^{-1}e_{n}\ \in\ \mathbb{H}^{n},

\displaystyle T

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}s&As&\cdots&A^{n-1}s\end{bmatrix},

(7)

3.

For the given pair
(A,C)
, the matrices
A_{o},C_{o}
constructed via (
7
) and the coefficients
a_{k}
(as defined above) are unique.

Proof:

We adapt the classical real/complex argument (cf.
[
2
]
) to the quaternionic setting, avoiding determinants and Cayley–Hamilton.

Choice of basis and similarity.

Let
s:=\mathcal{O}^{-1}e_{n}
and
T=[\,s\ As\ \cdots\ A^{n-1}s\,]
.
From
\mathcal{O}s=e_{n}
we obtain
Cs=\cdots=CA^{n-2}s=0
and
CA^{n-1}s=1
, hence

CT=\bigl[\,Cs\ \ CAs\ \ \cdots\ \ CA^{n-1}s\,\bigr]=e_{n}^{\mathsf{T}}.

Moreover, for each
k=0,\ldots,n-1
, the row
CA^{k}T
has the
triangular pattern

CA^{k}T=\bigl[\,\underbrace{0\ \cdots\ 0}_{n-k-1}\ \ 1\ \ \star\ \cdots\ \star\,\bigr],

(8)

where ‘
\star
’ denotes unspecified entries.
Hence,
(CA^{k}T)e_{j}=0
for
j<n-k
and
(CA^{k}T)e_{n-k}=1
.

Define the transformed pair.

Set
M:=T^{-1}AT
and
C_{o}:=CT=e_{n}^{\mathsf{T}}
.

Output row.

From
CT=e_{n}^{\mathsf{T}}
we have
C_{o}=e_{n}^{\mathsf{T}}=[\,0\ \cdots\ 0\ 1\,]
.

Subdiagonal shift property.

Write
T=[w_{1},\ldots,w_{n}]
with
w_{1}=s
and
w_{j+1}=Aw_{j}
by construction.
Then

\displaystyle Me_{j}=T^{-1}ATe_{j}=T^{-1}Aw_{j}=T^{-1}w_{j+1}=e_{j+1},

for
j=1,\ldots,n-1,

so
M
has ones on the subdiagonal and zeros below it.

Rightmost column and the definition of
a_{k}
.

By definition of the
observable companion form
, the last column of
M
is some vector

-[a_{0},\ldots,a_{n-1}]^{\mathsf{T}}\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
. We
define
the coefficients
a_{k}\in\mathbb{H}
to be these
entries. With the output row and subdiagonal shift established above, this uniquely identifies
M
as the observable companion
A_{o}
and yields (
6
).

Uniqueness.

The vector
s
is uniquely determined by
\mathcal{O}s=e_{n}
, hence
s=\mathcal{O}^{-1}e_{n}
.
Then the recursion
w_{j+1}=Aw_{j}
uniquely fixes the columns of
T=[\,s,As,\ldots,A^{n-1}s\,]
.
With
C_{o}=e_{n}^{\mathsf{T}}
, subdiagonal ones and the last column
-[a_{0},\ldots,a_{n-1}]^{\mathsf{T}}
, the matrix

A_{o}=M
is unique as well.
∎

Remark 1
.

In the real/complex (central) case, the last-column coefficients
a_{k}
also satisfy

CA^{n}=-\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(CA^{k})\,a_{k}
. Over
\mathbb{H}
, a right–linear expansion still holds but with (generally different) scalars

:

CA^{n}=-\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(CA^{k})\,b_{k}
, and there is no simple closed-form relation between

and
b_{k}
due to noncommutativity; if the
a_{k}
are real, then
b_{k}=a_{k}
.

Remark 2
.

Compared with the classical
\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{C}
case, the construction proceeds
in reverse
.
Instead of first computing a characteristic or minimal polynomial (via determinants) and then forming a companion realization,
we directly build the
observable companion form
from the invertible
observability matrix

\mathcal{O}
,
and only afterwards
read off
the coefficients of the companion polynomial from the rightmost column of
A_{o}
.
This approach is determinant–free and avoids the use of characteristic polynomials, Cayley–Hamilton, or adjugates,
whose straightforward generalizations to
\mathbb{H}
are problematic.

Example 1
.

Let

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{4}\begin{bmatrix}-2+\mathbf{j}&\mathbf{i}\\
\mathbf{i}&-2-\mathbf{j}\end{bmatrix},\;\;C=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{j}&\mathbf{k}\end{bmatrix}.

(9)

Then the observability matrix inverse is

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}^{-1}=\tfrac{1}{2}\begin{bmatrix}-1-3\mathbf{j}&-4\mathbf{j}\\
\mathbf{i}+\mathbf{k}&4\mathbf{k}\end{bmatrix}.

(10)

and the similarity transformation matrix (
7
) reads

\displaystyle T=\tfrac{1}{2}\begin{bmatrix}-4\mathbf{j}&1+\mathbf{j}\\
4\mathbf{k}&-\mathbf{i}-3\mathbf{k}\end{bmatrix}.

(11)

The right observable companion form of
A
is

\displaystyle A_{o}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}0&-0.25+0.25\mathbf{j}\\
1&-1+0.5\mathbf{j}\end{bmatrix},\;C_{o}=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\end{bmatrix},

(12)

and the associated companion polynomial is

\displaystyle a(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.25-0.25\mathbf{j}+(1-0.5\mathbf{j})\lambda+\lambda^{2}.

(13)

We obtain an equally clean annihilating identity for the right
observable
companion, again without invoking a full Cayley–Hamilton theorem over
\mathbb{H}
(cf.
[
3
,
18
]
). We will use left substitution for the companion polynomial at

Theorem 2

(
Companion polynomial annihilates its observable companion matrix over
\mathbb{H}
)
.

Let
A_{o}\in\mathbb{H}^{n\times n}
be in observable companion form

\displaystyle A_{o}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}0&0&\cdots&-a_{0}\\
1&0&\cdots&-a_{1}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&\cdots&1&-a_{n-1}\end{bmatrix},

(14)

with
a_{0},\ldots,a_{n-1}\in\mathbb{H}
, and define the companion polynomial

a(\lambda)=a_{0}+a_{1}\lambda+\cdots+a_{n-1}\lambda^{n-1}+\lambda^{n}\ \in\mathbb{H}[\lambda].

Then
a(\lambda)
annihilates
A_{o}
under
left substitution
:

\displaystyle Ia_{0}+A_{o}a_{1}+\cdots+A_{o}^{n-1}a_{n-1}+A_{o}^{n}=0.

(15)

Proof:

Setup and evaluation convention.

Let
A_{o}
be in the observable companion form (
14
) with last column

-\bigl[a_{0},\ldots,a_{n-1}\bigr]^{\mathsf{T}}
, where
a_{k}\in\mathbb{H}
.
Following Section 2, we evaluate polynomials at
A_{o}
by
left substitution
:

\displaystyle a(A_{o})_{\ell}=A_{o}^{n}+\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}A_{o}^{k}\,a_{k}.

(16)

Our goal is to show
a(A_{o})_{\ell}=0
.

Krylov columns and the shift property.

Define
u_{k}:=A_{o}^{k}e_{1}
for
k\geq 0
. Since
A_{o}
has ones on the subdiagonal,

A_{o}e_{j}=e_{j+1}\quad(j=1,\ldots,n-1),

hence
u_{k}=e_{k+1}\,\;(k=0,\ldots,n-1)
, and thus

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}:=\begin{bmatrix}u_{0}&\ldots&u_{n-1}\end{bmatrix}=I_{n}.

(17)

The step-
n
recurrence from the last column.

Reading off the last column of
A_{o}
gives

A_{o}^{n}e_{1}=-\begin{bmatrix}a_{0}\\
a_{1}\\
\vdots\\
a_{n-1}\end{bmatrix}=-\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}u_{k}\,a_{k}.

Left-multiplying by
A_{o}^{j}
and using
A_{o}^{j}u_{k}=u_{k+j}
yields, for all
j\geq 0
,

u_{n+j}=-\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}u_{k+j}\,a_{k}.

(18)

Annihilation under left substitution.

For any
j\geq 0
,

\displaystyle a(A_{o})_{\ell}\,u_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Bigl(A_{o}^{n}+\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}A_{o}^{k}a_{k}\Bigr)u_{j}

(19)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u_{n+j}+\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}u_{k+j}\,a_{k}=0

by (
18
). In particular, this holds for
j=0,1,\ldots,n-1
, so

a(A_{o})_{\ell}\;\mathcal{K}=0.

Since
\mathcal{K}=I_{n}
, we conclude
a(A_{o})_{\ell}=0
, which is the claimed annihilation under left substitution.
∎

Remark 3
.

This is
not
a quaternionic Cayley–Hamilton theorem (which generally fails over
\mathbb{H}
).
Rather, it is a
one-sided
annihilation under left substitution that hinges on the observable companion’s
shift/subdiagonal structure and the rightmost-column read-off of the coefficients.
It neither asserts a two-sided polynomial identity nor identifies
a(\lambda)
with any
characteristic/minimal polynomial; it also does
not
automatically extend to arbitrary
similar realizations outside the observable companion form.

The right spectrum of the
observable
companion matrix is likewise composed of the similarity classes of the right zeros of its companion polynomial.

Theorem 3

(
Right zeros form the right spectrum of the observable companion matrix
)
.

Let
A_{o}\in\mathbb{H}^{n\times n}
be in observable companion form (
14
), and let

a(\lambda)=a_{0}+a_{1}\lambda+\cdots+a_{n-1}\lambda^{n-1}+\lambda^{n}\in\mathbb{H}[\lambda]

be its companion polynomial (with

1.

\displaystyle v_{n}=\!1;\;v_{j-1}\!=\!a_{j-1}\!+\!v_{j}\lambda,\;j=n,n-1,\ldots,2,

Proof:

(1) Right zeros
\Rightarrow
right eigenpairs.

Assume

and construct

by the backward recurrence (

). We claim

.

For rows

\displaystyle(A_{o}v)_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle v_{j-1}-a_{j-1}\,v_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(v_{j}\lambda+a_{j-1})-a_{j-1}=v_{j}\lambda=(v\lambda)_{j}.

For the first row,

\displaystyle(A_{o}v)_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-a_{0}\,v_{n}\;=\;-a_{0},

\displaystyle(v\lambda)_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle v_{1}\lambda=\bigl(a_{1}+\cdots+a_{n-1}\lambda^{n-2}+\lambda^{n-1}\bigr)\lambda

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a_{1}\lambda+\cdots+a_{n-1}\lambda^{n-1}+\lambda^{n}.

Hence

(2) Right eigenpairs
\Rightarrow

Conversely, suppose

From rows

\displaystyle x_{k}=a_{k}x_{n}+a_{k+1}x_{n}\mu+\cdots+a_{n-k}x_{n}\mu^{n-k-1}+x_{n}\mu^{n-k}.

In particular, for

\displaystyle-a_{0}x_{n}=x_{1}\mu=a_{1}x_{n}\mu+\cdots+a_{n-1}x_{n}\mu^{n-1}+x_{n}\mu^{n}

Note that

(3) Left zeros.

By Gordon–Motzkin, left and right zeros of a quaternionic polynomial lie in the same similarity classes;
hence every left zero is similar to a right zero and therefore (up to similarity) to a right eigenvalue of

.

Combining (1)–(3) gives the three claims of the theorem.
∎

\displaystyle e

DUALITY OVER
\mathbb{H}

Over
\mathbb{R}

(cf.
[
2
]

Over
\mathbb{H}

conjugate transpose

[

18

7

1
]

Thus, duality must be formulated with
∗

[

,
4
]

The original system and its dual system are related through mappings

\displaystyle A\leftrightarrow A^{*},B\leftrightarrow C^{*},C\leftrightarrow B^{*},D\leftrightarrow D^{*}.

Their controllability and observability are related via

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{n}(A^{*},B^{*})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{n}(A,B)^{*},

see
[
21
,
18
]
,
and therefore

\displaystyle(A,B)\ \text{controllable}\ \Longleftrightarrow\ (A^{*},B^{*})\ \text{observable},

\displaystyle(A,C)\ \text{observable}\ \Longleftrightarrow\ (A^{*},C^{*})\ \text{controllable}.

If
K\in\mathbb{H}^{1\times n}
is a controller for the dual pair
(A^{*},C^{*})
, then the observer (output-injection) gain for the original system is

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle K^{*}\in\mathbb{H}^{n\times 1}.

The error and dual closed-loop matrices satisfy

\sigma_{\mathscr{r}}\big(A-LC\big)=\sigma_{\mathscr{r}}\big(A^{*}-C^{*}K\big).

Since
[q]=[\overline{q}]
for quaternionic similarity classes, the two spectra coincide as class sets. See

[
7
,
1
]
.

Proof sketches and references

The transpose fails over

QUATERNIONIC OBSERVER POLE PLACEMENT VIA COMPANION FORM

In
\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{C}
, observer design is dual to state feedback; over

\mathbb{H}
the same SISO workflow applies: transform to observable companion form, read the companion polynomial from the rightmost column, interpret poles as right-eigenvalue similarity classes, update coefficients in observer coordinates, and map the gain back. The construction is determinant-free, avoiding characteristic/minimal polynomials, Cayley–Hamilton, and adjugates.

Theorem 5

(
Pole placement via observable companion form
)
.

Assume a SISO observable pair
(A,C)
with

,

\displaystyle A_{o}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T^{-1}AT,\quad C_{o}=CT=\bigl[0\ 0\ \cdots\ 1\bigr],

(23)

and write the rightmost column of
A_{o}
as
\bigl[-a_{0},\ldots,-a_{n-1}\bigr]^{\mathsf{T}}

Let

\displaystyle a(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a_{0}+a_{1}\lambda+\cdots+a_{n-1}\lambda^{n-1}+\lambda^{n}

be the associated companion polynomial. Given any monic

\displaystyle a_{d}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d_{0}+d_{1}\lambda+\cdots+d_{n-1}\lambda^{n-1}+\lambda^{n},

there exists a unique observer gain

such that the right spectrum of

\displaystyle A_{\mathrm{obs}}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle A-LC

(24)

equals the multiset of right zeros (similarity classes, with multiplicities) of

.
Moreover, with

we have

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle TL_{o},

(25)

\displaystyle L_{o}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigl[d_{0}-a_{0}\ \ d_{1}-a_{1}\ \ \cdots\ \ d_{n-1}-a_{n-1}\bigr]^{\mathsf{T}}.

Proof:

Work in observable coordinates. With
L_{o}:=T^{-1}L
and (
23
),

\displaystyle A_{\mathrm{obs},o}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle T^{-1}(A\!-\!LC)T\!=\!A_{o}\!-\!L_{o}C_{o}.

Here

is the rank-one matrix whose last column equals

and all other columns are zero. Writing

shows that the closed–loop coefficients are updated as

\displaystyle a_{\mathrm{obs}}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a_{0}+\ell_{0})+(a_{1}+\ell_{1})\lambda+\cdots

\displaystyle\negmedspace{}+(a_{n-1}+\ell_{n-1})\lambda^{n-1}+\lambda^{n}.

.
Uniqueness follows since the coefficient update

∎

VIII
OBSERVER GAIN VIA ACKERMANN’S FORMULA

Having established the quaternionic observer in its canonical (observable) form, we now turn to a compact algebraic method for directly computing the observer gain.
The resulting
Observer Ackermann’s formula
is the dual counterpart of the control-side expression. It provides a one–shot computation of (L) achieving any admissible placement of right–eigenvalue classes (observer poles).
Its structure mirrors the classical real/complex case. Yet, its justification over
\mathbb{H}
requires special care: the argument avoids determinants and adjugates and instead exploits the observable companion relations and the right–polynomial calculus developed earlier.
This formulation establishes the quaternionic duality between controllability and observability in a determinant–free setting.

Theorem 6

(
Quaternionic Observer Ackermann’s formula
)
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{H}^{n\times n}
,
C\in\mathbb{H}^{1\times n}
be such that the SISO pair
(A,C)
is observable and the observability matrix
\mathcal{O}
from (
3
) is invertible.
Let

a_{d}(\lambda)=d_{0}+d_{1}\lambda+\cdots+d_{n-1}\lambda^{n-1}+\lambda^{n}\in\mathbb{R}[\lambda]

be the desired monic error-dynamics polynomial with real coefficients, and let
e_{n}=[0,0,\dots,1]^{\mathsf{T}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Then the observer gain column

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a_{d}(A)\mathcal{O}^{-1}e_{n},

(28)

used in (
22
) yields the error-dynamics matrix

A_{\mathrm{obs}}=A-LC
whose right spectrum equals the similarity classes induced by the right roots
of
a_{d}(\lambda)
:

\displaystyle\sigma_{\mathscr{r}}(A-LC)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbin{{\cup}}_{i=1}^{n}[\lambda_{i}],

(29)

where
\{\lambda_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
are the right roots of
a_{d}
(listed with multiplicity).

Remark 7
.

The restriction
a_{d}\in\mathbb{R}[\lambda]
is essential: real coefficients lie in the center
Z(\mathbb{H})=\mathbb{R}
,
so
a_{d}(A)
is unambiguous and the required polynomial–similarity interchange is valid.
The assigned classes are real or complex-conjugate spherical:
for an
n
-th order SISO design, each real class consumes one degree and each nonreal class two,
so
2s+r\leq n
when placing
s
nonreal and
r
real classes.

Proof:

Observable companion coordinates.

Choose
T
invertible with

\displaystyle A_{o}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T^{-1}AT,\qquad C_{o}=CT=e_{n}^{\mathsf{T}}.

Then
A_{o}
is in observable companion form with the rightmost column

[-a_{0},\ldots,-a_{n-1}]^{\mathsf{T}}
and companion polynomial

a(\lambda)=a_{0}+a_{1}\lambda+\cdots+a_{n-1}\lambda^{n-1}+\lambda^{n}
.
Let
L_{o}:=T^{-1}L
. We have

\displaystyle T^{-1}(A-LC)T

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A_{o}-L_{o}C_{o}=A_{o}-L_{o}e_{n}^{\mathsf{T}}.

(30)

Coefficient matching in the right observable form.

With
u_{k}:=A_{o}^{k}e_{1}=e_{k+1}
for
k=0,\ldots,n-1
and

A_{o}^{n}e_{1}=-[\,a_{0},\ldots,a_{n-1}\,]^{\mathsf{T}}
(rightmost column read-off), we obtain

\displaystyle a_{d}(A_{o})e_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}d_{k}A_{o}^{k}e_{1}+A_{o}^{n}e_{1}

(31)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}d_{0}\\
\vdots\\
d_{n-1}\end{bmatrix}-\begin{bmatrix}a_{0}\\
\vdots\\
a_{n-1}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}d_{0}-a_{0}\\
\vdots\\
d_{n-1}-a_{n-1}\end{bmatrix}.

Setting

therefore adds
L_{o}
to the entries
\{-a_{k}\}
in the

of
A_{o}
, producing the updated coefficients

\{-(a_{k}+\ell_{k})\}
and thus the closed-loop polynomial

.
Since similarity preserves the right spectrum, the error matrix
A-LC
attains the desired

a_{d}
.

Coordinate-free expression via
\mathcal{O}
and central polynomials.

Let

be the observability matrix of

. Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}=\begin{bmatrix}C\\

Because

X
CONCLUSIONS

We developed a characteristic-polynomial-free observer framework for quaternionic systems, centered on an observable companion realization that handles general quaternionic targets, and a quaternionic Ackermann formula restricted to real-coefficient targets.
The method enables direct placement of right-eigenvalue similarity classes in the error dynamics and clarifies controller–observer duality over
\mathbb{H},
with the companion-coordinate coefficient update accommodating noncentral coefficients, whereas Ackermann does not.
Future work will extend the approach to multi-input and discrete-time settings.

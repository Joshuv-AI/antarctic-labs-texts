Non-Commutative Maximal Inequalities for State Preserving Actions of amenable groups
Panchugopal Bikram, Hariharan G, Sudipta Kundu, Diptesh Saha

Abstract
. In this article, we establish maximal inequalities and deduce ergodic theorems for state-preserving actions of amenable, locally compact, second-countable groups on tracial non-commutative L 1 L^{1} -spaces. As a further consequence, in combination with the Neveu decomposition, we obtain a stochastic ergodic theorem for amenable group actions.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 46L53, 46L55; Secondary 37A55, 46L40.

1.
Introduction

In this work, we investigate non-commutative maximal ergodic inequalities and pointwise ergodic theorems for actions of amenable groups that preserve a given state. The study of ergodic theorems in classical measure-theoretic settings originates in the 1930s with the seminal contributions of Birkhoff and von Neumann. Since then, the theory has undergone extensive development. The non-commutative counterparts of these results were initiated in the pioneering work of Lance (
[
Lan76
]
), who established a pointwise ergodic theorem for averages associated with a single state-preserving automorphism of a von Neumann algebra equipped with a faithful normal state. Subsequent generalizations were provided by Kümmerer, Conze, Dang-Ngoc, and others (cf.
[
K7̈8
]
,
[
CDN78
]
, and the references therein).

Over time, it has become well understood that the canonical route to establishing an ergodic theorem proceeds through the derivation of an appropriate maximal inequality together with a proper Banach principle. The non-commutative framework, however, renders the maximal inequality step particularly intricate and technically demanding.

In two foundational papers
[
Yea77
,
Yea80
]
, Yeadon developed maximal ergodic theorems for actions of positive, sub-tracial, sub-unital maps operating on the preduals of semifinite von Neumann algebras and, as a consequence, established associated pointwise ergodic theorems. This line of inquiry was later advanced by Junge and Xu
[
JX07
]
, who extended Yeadon’s results by proving non-commutative Dunford–Schwartz maximal inequalities in
L^{p}
-spaces, employing sophisticated interpolation methods.

Parallel to these developments, substantial progress has been made on ergodic theorems for group actions on classical measure spaces (cf.
[
AAB+10
,
Cal53
]
). In particular, relevance to the present discussion is the work of Lindenstrauss
[
Lin01
]
, which established pointwise ergodic theorems for tempered Følner sequences, thereby extending Birkhoff’s classical theorem to actions of second-countable amenable groups.

A significant advancement in the non-commutative theory was made by Hong, Liao, and Wang in
[
HLW21
]
. In their work, the authors established a collection of maximal inequalities in non-commutative
L^{p}
-spaces for actions of locally compact group metric measure spaces satisfying a doubling condition, as well as for compactly generated groups exhibiting polynomial growth. These inequalities subsequently enabled them to derive the corresponding pointwise ergodic theorems. The scope of these results was later broadened to encompass general amenable groups in
[
CW22
]
and very recently for unimodular, amenable groups in
[
CXZ25
]
.

It is worth emphasizing that both of the aforementioned results deal exclusively with actions that preserve a faithful, normal, semifinite trace. In contrast, Junge and Xu
[
JX07
]
examined maximal inequalities for ergodic averages in Haagerup’s
L^{p}
-spaces (
1<p<\infty
) associated with state-preserving actions of the discrete group
\mathbb{Z}
and of the semigroup
\mathbb{R}_{+}
. The corresponding endpoint case
p=1
for such state-preserving actions remained open until the more recent developments in
[
BS23b
]
and
[
BS23a
]
.

In
[
BS23b
]
, the authors established maximal inequalities and pointwise ergodic theorems in non-commutative
L^{1}
-spaces for actions of locally compact groups of polynomial growth that preserve a faithful, normal state on a von Neumann algebra. Their approach relies primarily on an inequality from
[
HLW21
, Proposition 4.8]
, which allows the relevant ergodic averages over the group to be dominated by an ergodic average of an associated Markov operator.

In
[
BS23a
]
, a comparable maximal inequality was derived for actions of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}_{+}
and
\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}
, employing a similar strategy. Additionally, in
[
BS26
]
, the author proved a maximal inequality for actions of group metric measure spaces satisfying the doubling condition, making use of suitable analogues of Calderón’s transference principle together with maximal inequalities for non-commutative martingales.

In the present article, we establish a similar maximal inequality for the state preserving action of an amenable group as in
[
BS26
]
, by exploiting the maximal inequality obtained by Cadilhac and Wang, in
[
CW22
]
, which was obtained in tracial setup.

Let
G
be an amenable, locally compact, second-countable group equipped with a right-invariant Haar measure
\mu
with an admissible Følner sequence
(F_{n})
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a finite von Neumann algebra with a faithful, normal tracial state
\tau
, and let
(\mathcal{M},G,\alpha,\rho)
be a kernel (cf. Definition
3.10
). One of the main results of this article establishes the following maximal inequality and ergodic theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\phi\in\mathcal{M}_{*}
, then consider the averages
(A_{k}(\phi))_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
, where

\displaystyle A_{k}(\phi)(x):=\frac{1}{\mu(F_{k})}\int_{F_{k}}\phi(\alpha_{s^{-1}}(x))\,d\mu(s),\quad x\in\mathcal{M}.

Then the following hold:

(1)

Furthermore, for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\phi_{j}\in\mathcal{M}_{*s}
satisfy
\left\|\phi_{j}\right\|\leq\frac{1}{4^{s_{j}}}
. Fix
\upsilon>0
. Then for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exist a sequence
\left(\gamma_{j}(n)\right)_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
of positive real numbers and projections
\{p^{\upsilon}_{n,j}:j\in\mathbb{N}\}
in
\mathcal{M}
such that:

(a)

\displaystyle\lim_{j\to\infty}\gamma_{j}(n)=0
, uniformly in
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

(b)

\rho(1-p^{\upsilon}_{n,j})\leq(1+\upsilon)\gamma_{j}(n)+\upsilon
.

(c)

For all
j\in\mathbb{N}
,

(1.1)

\displaystyle A_{k}(\phi_{j})(x)\leq\frac{cs_{j}}{2^{s_{j}}}\rho(x),\quad\text{for all }x\in p^{\upsilon}_{n,j}\mathcal{M}_{+}p^{\upsilon}_{n,j}\text{ and }k\in[n].

where,
[n]=\{1,2...,n\}
. The constant
c
depends only on the group
G
.

(2)

Additionally, we assume
G
is unimodular. Then for any
\phi\in\mathcal{M}_{*}
, there exists an element
\bar{\phi}\in\mathcal{M}_{*}
such that, for every
\varepsilon>0
, one can find a projection
e\in\mathcal{M}
satisfying
\tau(1-e)<\varepsilon
and

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in e\mathcal{M}_{+}e\\
x\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{\left|(A_{n}(\phi)-\bar{\phi})(x)\right|}{\tau(x)}=0.

We note that an similar maximal inequality obtained in
[
BS26
, Corollary 3.18]
, which was proved for state-preserving actions on group metric measure spaces. In that setting, the authors derive the estimate

\lvert A_{k}(\phi_{j})(x)\rvert\leq C\delta\,\|\phi_{j}\|\,\|x\|+\frac{C}{2^{j}}\,\rho(x),

for all
x\in q^{\delta,\upsilon}_{n,j}\,\mathcal{M}_{+}\,q^{\delta,\upsilon}_{n,j}
and all
k\in[n]
.

In contrast, in the present maximal inequality, specifically in
eq.

1.1
, the additional error term
C\delta\|\phi_{j}\|\,\|x\|
does not appear. Instead, we obtain the sharper bound

A_{k}(\phi_{j})(x)\leq\frac{cs_{j}}{2^{s_{j}}}\,\rho(x),\qquad\text{for all }x\in q^{\upsilon}_{j,n}\,\mathcal{M}_{+}\,q^{\upsilon}_{j,n}\text{ and }k\in[n],

where the auxiliary sequence
(s_{j})
arises naturally in the passage from the trace to the state.

We conclude this introduction by outlining the structure of the paper. In the following section, we review basic notions from the theory of von Neumann algebras and their associated linear functionals. We also introduce noncommutative
L^{1}
-spaces, discuss the bilateral almost uniform (b.a.u.) topology, and conclude the section with the definition of noncommutative dynamical systems and the corresponding predual actions.

Section
3
is devoted to the proof of part (1) of Theorem
1.1
. In Section
4
, we establish a pointwise ergodic theorem for ball averages along an admissible Følner sequence, thereby proving part (2) of Theorem
1.1
. In addition, we derive an appropriate Banach principle adapted to this setting.

Finally, in the last section, we combine the results obtained in Section
4
with the Neveu decomposition theorem from
[
BS23b
]
to obtain a stochastic ergodic theorem within this framework.

2.
Preliminaries

Throughout this article, let
\mathcal{M}
denote a von Neumann algebra acting on a separable Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. The operator norm inherited from
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
is written as
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\infty}
; when no confusion can arise, we abbreviate it simply by
\lVert\cdot\rVert
. The commutant of
\mathcal{M}
is denoted by
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
. We further write
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M})
for the collection of projections contained in
\mathcal{M}
.

The predual of
\mathcal{M}
, is denoted by
\mathcal{M}_{*}
, is the closed linear subspace of the Banach dual
\mathcal{M}^{*}
consisting exactly of the ultraweakly continuous linear functionals on
\mathcal{M}
. A functional
\phi\in\mathcal{M}^{*}
is called positive if
\phi(x^{*}x)\geq 0
for every
x\in\mathcal{M}
, and it is said to be self-adjoint when
\phi(x)=\overline{\phi(x^{*})}
holds for all
x\in\mathcal{M}
. We denote by
\mathcal{M}_{*+}
and
\mathcal{M}_{*s}
the cones of positive and self-adjoint elements of
\mathcal{M}_{*}
, respectively.

2.1.
Non-commutative
L^{1}
-spaces

Let
\mathcal{M}\subseteq\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be a von Neumann algebra equipped with a faithful, normal, semifinite (f.n.s.) trace
\tau
. A (possibly unbounded) operator

X:\mathcal{D}(X)\subseteq\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H},

which is densely defined, closed, and affiliated with
\mathcal{M}
, i.e, satisfying
u^{\prime*}Xu^{\prime}=X
for every unitary
u^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
—will be written as
X\,\eta\,\mathcal{M}
. Such an operator
X
is said to be
\tau
-measurable if, for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a projection
e\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M})
such that
\tau(1-e)<\varepsilon
and
e\mathcal{H}\subseteq\mathcal{D}(X)
. The collection of all
\tau
-measurable operators affiliated with
\mathcal{M}
is then defined by

L^{0}(\mathcal{M},\tau):=\{\,X\,\eta\,\mathcal{M}:X\text{ is }\tau\text{-measurable}\,\}.

The space
L^{0}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
forms a
∗
-algebra under the adjoint operation, strong sum
X+Y:=\overline{X+Y}
, and strong product
X\cdot Y:=\overline{XY}
, where
\overline{X}
denotes the closure of
X
. Detailed treatments may be found in
[
SZ19
,
Hia21
]
.

Several natural topologies arise on
L^{0}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
. The first relevant one is the
measure topology
, whose neighborhoods are of the form

X+\mathcal{N}(\varepsilon,\delta),\qquad\varepsilon,\delta>0,\;X\in L^{0}(\mathcal{M},\tau),

where

\mathcal{N}(\varepsilon,\delta):=\{\,X\in L^{0}(\mathcal{M},\tau):\exists\,e\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M})\text{ with }\tau(1-e)<\delta\text{ and }\|eXe\|<\varepsilon\,\}.

A net
\{X_{i}\}_{i\in I}
converges to
X
in the measure topology precisely when, for every
\varepsilon,\delta>0
, there exists
i_{0}\in I
such that for all
i\geq i_{0}
one may find
e_{i}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M})
with
\tau(1-e_{i})<\delta
and

\|\,e_{i}(X_{i}-X)e_{i}\,\|<\varepsilon.

By
[
Nel74
, Theorem 1]
, the space
L^{0}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
is complete in the measure topology. Furthermore, this topology is metrizable and renders
\mathcal{M}
dense in
L^{0}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
.

The second topology of interest is the
bilateral almost uniform
(b.a.u.) topology.

Definition 2.1
.

A net
\{X_{i}\}_{i\in I}\subseteq L^{0}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
is said to converge
bilaterally almost uniformly
(b.a.u.) to
X\in L^{0}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
if, for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a projection
e\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M})
such that
\tau(1-e)<\varepsilon
and

\lim_{i}\|\,e(X_{i}-X)e\,\|=0.

The following fundamental fact, due to
[
Lit24
, Theorem 2.2]
, will be used subsequently.

Theorem 2.2
.

The space
L^{0}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
is complete with respect to the b.a.u. topology.

We record a simple but useful observation.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\{X_{i}\}_{i\in I}
and
\{Y_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be nets in
L^{0}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
such that
X_{i}\to X
and
Y_{i}\to Y
in measure (resp. b.a.u.). Then, for every scalar
c\in\mathbb{C}
, the net
cX_{i}+Y_{i}
converges to
cX+Y
in measure (resp. b.a.u.).

The trace
\tau
on
\mathcal{M}_{+}
extends in the standard way to
L^{0}(\mathcal{M},\tau)_{+}
by

\tau(X):=\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda\,d\tau(e_{\lambda}),

where
X=\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda\,de_{\lambda}
is the spectral decomposition of
X
. The associated noncommutative
L^{1}
-space is defined by

L^{1}(\mathcal{M},\tau):=\{\,X\in L^{0}(\mathcal{M},\tau):\tau(|X|)<\infty\,\},

with norm
\|X\|_{1}:=\tau(|X|)
. The following properties, proved in
[
Hia21
, Chapter 4]
, will be used repeatedly.

Theorem 2.4
.

(1)

L^{1}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
is a Banach space under
\|\cdot\|_{1}
.

(2)

If
X\in L^{1}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
and
y\in\mathcal{M}
, then both
Xy
and
yX
lie in
L^{1}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
, and moreover
\tau(Xy)=\tau(yX)
.

(3)

The canonical identification

\phi\in\mathcal{M}_{*}\quad\longleftrightarrow\quad X\in L^{1}(\mathcal{M},\tau),\quad\phi(x)=\tau(Xx)\;\;(x\in\mathcal{M})

defines a surjective linear isometry from
L^{1}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
onto
\mathcal{M}_{*}
, which preserves positivity.

2.2.
Non-commutative dynamical systems

Let
G
be a locally compact, second countable, Hausdorff group equipped with a
right-invariant Haar measure
\mu
.
A
Følner sequence

(F_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of measurable subsets
of
G
satisfying

0<\mu(F_{n})<\infty\quad\text{for all }n\in\mathbb{N},

and such that for every
g\in G
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mu\bigl(F_{n}g\,\triangle\,F_{n}\bigr)}{\mu(F_{n})}=0,

where
\triangle
denotes the symmetric difference.

Throughout this article, we further, assume that
G
is an amenable group with a Følner sequence

(F_{n})
in
G
. In addition, for most of our results,
we shall assume that
(F_{n})
is an
admissible Følner sequence

(see
[
CW22
]
for the precise definition).

We also remark that if
G
is a locally compact, second countable, amenable group,
then every Følner sequence admits an admissible Følner subsequence; see
[
CW22
]
for details.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
(E,\|\cdot\|)
be a real ordered Banach space.
Then a
non-commutative dynamical system
is a triple
(E,G,\gamma)
, where
\gamma:G\to\mathcal{B}(E)
is a mapping satisfying
\gamma_{s}\circ\gamma_{t}=\gamma_{st}
for all
s,t\in G
, and the following conditions hold:

(1)

for every
a\in E
, the orbit map
s\mapsto\gamma_{s}(a)
is continuous on
G
, where
E
is equipped with the norm topology (and the
w^{*}
-topology in the case
E=\mathcal{M}
);

(2)

the operators are uniformly bounded, i.e.,
\sup_{s\in G}\|\gamma_{s}\|<\infty
;

(3)

positivity is preserved: if
a\geq 0
, then
\gamma_{s}(a)\geq 0
for all
s\in G
.

We further assume that if
E=\mathcal{M}
,
\gamma_{s}
is a *-automorphism on
\mathcal{M}
for all
s\in G
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a von Neumann algebra with a faithful, normal, semifinite trace
\tau
, and consider the dynamical system
(L^{1}(\mathcal{M},\tau),G,\gamma)
. Since
\mathcal{M}=(L^{1}(\mathcal{M},\tau))^{*}
, the Banach-space adjoint of
\gamma_{s}
, denoted
\gamma_{s}^{*}
, is characterized by

(2.1)

\displaystyle\tau(\gamma_{s}^{*}(x)\,Y)=\tau(x\,\gamma_{s}(Y)),\qquad x\in\mathcal{M},\;Y\in L^{1}(\mathcal{M},\tau).

Similarly, for a non-commutative dynamical system
(\mathcal{M},G,\beta)
, the predual operator of
\beta_{s}
, denoted
\widehat{\beta_{s}}
, is defined via

(2.2)

\displaystyle\tau(\beta_{s}(x)\,Y)=\tau(x\,\widehat{\beta_{s}}(Y)),\qquad x\in\mathcal{M},\;Y\in L^{1}(\mathcal{M},\tau).

In particular, one has
(\widehat{\beta_{s}})^{*}=\beta_{s}
for every
s\in G
.

Now let
T:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}
be a bounded normal operator. Its adjoint
T^{*}:\mathcal{M}^{*}\to\mathcal{M}^{*}
is given by

T^{*}(\phi)(x)=\phi(T(x)),\qquad x\in\mathcal{M},\;\phi\in\mathcal{M}^{*}.

If
\phi\in\mathcal{M}_{*}\subseteq\mathcal{M}^{*}
, then
T^{*}(\phi)
also belongs to
\mathcal{M}_{*}
; hence the restriction
T^{*}|_{\mathcal{M}_{*}}\colon\mathcal{M}_{*}\to\mathcal{M}_{*}
serves as the predual transformation associated with
T
.

To streamline notation, we shall not distinguish between
T
and its predual
T^{*}|_{\mathcal{M}_{*}}
. Thus, when
\phi\in\mathcal{M}_{*}
and we write
T(\phi)
, it is to be understood that
T
acts as the predual map. Under this convention,

(2.3)

\displaystyle T(\phi)(x)=\phi(T(x)),\qquad x\in\mathcal{M},\;\phi\in\mathcal{M}_{*}.

3.
Maximal Inequality

In this section we establish a maximal inequality associated to a state preserving action of an amenable group on von Neumann algebra.

Suppose
\mathcal{M}
be a von Neumann algebra with a f.n.s. trace
\tau
. Then, consider the von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{N}=:L^{\infty}(G,\mu)\otimes\mathcal{M}
and consider the f.n.s trace
\widetilde{\tau}
on
\mathcal{N}
, defined by

\widetilde{\tau}=\int_{G}(\cdot)d\mu~\otimes\tau

We note that
L^{1}(\mathcal{N},\widetilde{\tau})
is identified with

L^{1}\left(G,L^{1}(\mathcal{M},\tau)\right)
. Further, we note that

L^{1}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
can be identified with
\mathcal{M}_{*}
via the following map:

L^{1}(\mathcal{M},\tau)\ni A\to\tau_{A}\in\mathcal{M}_{*},

where
\tau_{A}(x)=\tau(Ax)
for
x\in\mathcal{M}
. This will establish an isometric isomorphism between
L^{1}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
and
\mathcal{M}_{*}
. Consequently,
L^{1}\left(G,L^{1}(\mathcal{M},\tau)\right)
is isometrically isomorphic to
L^{1}(G,\mathcal{M}_{*})
via the following map

L^{1}\left(G,L^{1}(\mathcal{M},\tau)\right)\ni f\mapsto\tau_{f}\in L^{1}(G;\mathcal{M}_{*}),

where
\tau_{f}(s)=:\tau_{f(s)}
.
Further, as
\mathcal{N}=L^{\infty}(G,\mu)\otimes\mathcal{M}\cong

L^{\infty}(G;\mathcal{M}):=
the space of weakly measurable essentially bounded functions from
G
to
\mathcal{M}
, we obtain that

\widetilde{\tau}_{f}(g)=:\widetilde{\tau}(fg)=\int_{G}\tau(f(s)g(s))d\mu(s),

where
f\in L^{1}\left(G,L^{1}(\mathcal{M},\tau)\right)
and
g\in L^{\infty}(G;\mathcal{M})
. We also note that
\widetilde{\tau}(g)=\int_{G}\tau(g(s))d\mu(s),\text{ for }g\in L^{\infty}(G;\mathcal{M})
.
Let
(F_{n})
be a Følner sequence in
G
and
f\in L^{1}(G)\otimes M_{*}

Then consider

\mathbb{A}_{n}(f)(s)=\frac{1}{\mu(F_{n})}\int_{F_{n}}f(st)d\mu(t).

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
f\in L^{1}\left(G,\mathcal{M}_{*}\right)_{+}
and

h\in L^{1}(G;L^{1}(\mathcal{M},\tau))_{+}
such that

f=\widetilde{\tau}_{h},

then it follows that

\mathbb{A}_{n}(f)=\widetilde{\tau}_{\mathbb{A}_{n}(h)},~\text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{N}.

Proof.

Let
g\in L^{\infty}(G;\mathcal{M})
and
t\in G
, then observe that
\rho_{t}(g)\in L^{\infty}(G,\mathcal{M})
, where
\rho_{t}(g)(s)=g(st^{-1})
for all
s\in G
.
As
f=\widetilde{\tau}_{h}
, so, we have

\int_{G}f(s)(\rho_{t}(g)(s))d\mu=\int_{G}\tau(h(s)\rho_{t}(g)(s))d\mu.

Further, since
\mu
is right-invariant Haar measure, we have

\int_{G}f(st)(g(s))d\mu(s)=\int_{G}\tau(h(st)(g(s))d\mu(s)

Thus, we observe that

\displaystyle\int_{G}\mathbb{A}_{n}(f)(s)(g(s))d\mu(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{G}\frac{1}{\mu(F_{n})}\int_{F_{n}}f(st)(g(s))d\mu(t)d\mu(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mu(F_{n})}\int_{F_{n}}\int_{G}f(st)(g(s))d\mu(s)d\mu(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mu(F_{n})}\int_{F_{n}}\int_{G}\tau(h(st)(g(s)))d\mu(s)d\mu(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{G}\frac{1}{\mu(F_{n})}\int_{F_{n}}\tau(h(st)(g(s)))d\mu(t)d\mu(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{G}\tau(\mathbb{A}_{n}(h)(s)g(s))d\mu(s).

This completes the proof.

∎

Then by linearity of the operator
\mathbb{A}_{n}
and the weight
\tilde{\tau}
, we observe that for all
\nu\in L^{1}\left(G,\mathcal{M}_{*}\right)
there exists
B_{\nu}\in L^{1}\left(G;L^{1}(\mathcal{M},\tau)\right)
such that

\nu(\cdot)=\widetilde{\tau}(B_{\nu}(\cdot)),\text{ i.e},~\nu=\widetilde{\tau}_{B_{\nu}}.

We can also have the following observation.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
e
be a projection in
\mathcal{N}
and
\delta>0
, then
\left\|e(B_{\nu})e\right\|\leq\delta
if and only if

\left|(\nu)(exe)\right|\leq\delta\widetilde{\tau}(exe),~\text{ for all }x\in\mathcal{N}_{+}.

Proof.

Suppose

\left|(\nu)(exe)\right|\leq\delta\widetilde{\tau}(exe),~\text{ for all }x\in\mathcal{N}_{+},

then for all
x\in\mathcal{N}_{+}
, it follows that

\displaystyle\left|(\nu)(exe)\right|\leq\delta\widetilde{\tau}(exe)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\left|\widetilde{\tau}((B_{\nu})(exe))\right|\leq\delta\widetilde{\tau}(exe)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\left|\widetilde{\tau}(e(B_{\nu})ex)\right|\leq\delta\widetilde{\tau}(exe)

which is equivalent to
\left\|e(B_{\nu})e\right\|\leq\delta
.

∎

We recall the following theorem from
[
CW22
]

for smooth reference.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
(F_{n})
be an admissible Følner sequence in
G
. Let
f\in L^{1}\left(G,L^{1}(\mathcal{M},\tau)\right)
and
\varepsilon>0
, then there exists a projection
e^{\varepsilon}
in
\mathcal{N}
such that

(1)

\tau(1-e^{\varepsilon})\leq\frac{\left\|f\right\|_{1}}{\varepsilon}
and

(2)

\left\|e^{\varepsilon}\mathbb{A}_{k}(f)e^{\varepsilon}\right\|\leq c\varepsilon,~\text{ for all }k\in\mathbb{N}.

Now using the
3.2
we immediately deduce the following theorem from
3.3
. This version will be used in our analysis.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
K
be a Borel set in
G
and
f\in L^{1}(G;\mathcal{M}_{*})
with
\text{supp}(f)\subseteq K
. Then for given
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a projection with
e_{K}^{\varepsilon}
in
\mathcal{N}
with
\text{supp}(e_{K}^{\varepsilon})\in L^{\infty}(K,\mu)\otimes\mathcal{M}

[

]
. As a consequence, no error term of the form

appears (see the Inequality

\displaystyle A_{k}(\phi_{j})(x)\leq\frac{cs_{j}}{2^{s_{j}}}\rho(x),\quad\text{for all }x\in q^{\upsilon}_{j,n}\mathcal{M}_{+}q^{\upsilon}_{j,n}\text{ and }k\in[n],

where the auxiliary sequence

Thus, to obtain the pointwise ergodic theorem, we need to prove an appropriate Banach principle, which will be established in the next section.

(2)

We note that a maximal inequality analogous to

We also remark that, when

4.
Pointwise ergodic theorems

In this section, we study the pointwise convergence of specific ergodic averages. With the weak-type maximal inequality at our disposal
(cf.
[
CW22
]
),
it remains to establish an appropriate Banach principle and demonstrate the pointwise convergence of elements from a dense subset.

Throughout this section, we assume that
\mathcal{M}
is a von Neumann algebra with a faithful normal tracial state
\tau
and that
G
, with the right Haar measure, is an amenable group with right Følner sequence
(F_{n})
. Additionally, we assume that
(\mathcal{M},G,\alpha)
is a non-commutative dynamical system for which there exists a faithful normal state
\rho
on
\mathcal{M}
satisfying
\rho\circ\alpha_{s}=\rho
for all
s\in G
, making
(\mathcal{M},G,\alpha,\rho)
a kernel.

Before proceeding further, we fix the following notation. Let

\{\gamma_{s}:s\in G\}
be a family of (appropriate) maps acting on a Banach space
E
.
For the associated averaging operators, with a mild abuse of notations, we write:

•

A_{n}(\cdot):=\frac{1}{\mu(F_{n})}\int_{F_{n}}\gamma_{s^{-1}}(\cdot)\,d\mu(s)
and

•

B_{n}(\cdot):=\frac{1}{\mu(F_{n})}\int_{F_{n}}\gamma_{s}(\cdot)\,d\mu(s).

Strictly speaking, these operators should be denoted by
A_{n}^{\gamma}
and

B_{n}^{\gamma}
to emphasize their dependence on the family
\{\gamma_{s}\}_{s\in G}
.
However, for notational simplicity, we suppress this dependence and write
A_{n}
and

B_{n}
throughout.

Since
\rho\circ\alpha_{s}=\rho
for all
s\in G
, we observe that one can define a unitary
u_{s}
on the
G.N.S.
space
L^{2}(\mathcal{M},\rho)
given by

u_{s}(x\Omega_{\rho})=\alpha_{s}(x)\Omega_{\rho},

satisfying
u_{s}\circ u_{t}=u_{st}
for all
s,t\in G
, where
\Omega_{\rho}
is the associated cyclic and separating vector in
L^{2}(\mathcal{M},\rho)
. Throughout this section, we denote the Hilbert space inner product and norm in the aforementioned
G.N.S.

\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle_{\rho}
and
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\rho}

Now, we present the following mean ergodic theorem.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
{F_{n}}
be Følner sequence with all the notation given in the introduction of this section. Then, the averages

\displaystyle B_{n}x:=\frac{1}{\mu(F_{n})}\int_{F_{n}}u_{s}(x)\,d\mu(s),\quad x\in L^{2}(\mathcal{M},\rho),

converge in
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\rho}
.

Proof.

Since
(L^{2}(\mathcal{M},\rho),G,u)
forms a non-commutative dynamical system, we observe that

\displaystyle L^{2}(\mathcal{M},\rho)=F_{2}\oplus F_{2}^{\perp},

where

\displaystyle F_{2}

\displaystyle=\{x\in L^{2}(\mathcal{M},\rho):u_{s}(x)=x\text{ for all }s\in G\},

\displaystyle F_{2}^{\perp}

\displaystyle=\overline{\text{span}}\{y-u_{s}(y):y\in L^{2}(\mathcal{M},\rho),~s\in G\}.

Therefore, it suffices to check the convergence of
A_{n}x
for
x=y-u_{s}(y)
, where
y\in L^{2}(\mathcal{M},\rho)
and
s\in G
. Moreover, we observe that

\displaystyle B_{n}x

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mu(F_{n})}\int_{F_{n}}u_{t}(y)d\mu(t)-\frac{1}{\mu(F_{n})}\int_{F_{n}}u_{ts}(y)d\mu(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mu(F_{n})}\int_{F_{n}}u_{t}(y)d\mu(t)-\frac{1}{\mu(F_{n})}\int_{F_{n}s}u_{t}(y)d\mu(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mu(F_{n})}\int_{G}(\chi_{F_{n}}-\chi_{F_{n}s})u_{t}(y)d\mu(t)

Consequently, we get

\displaystyle\left\|B_{n}(x)\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\mu(F_{n})}\int_{G}\left|\chi_{F_{n}}-\chi_{F_{n}s}\right|\left\|u_{t}(y)\right\|d\mu(t)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\mu(F_{n})}\int_{G}\chi_{F_{n}\Delta F_{n}s}\left\|y\right\|d\mu(t)

\displaystyle=\left\|y\right\|\frac{\mu(F_{n}\Delta F_{n}s)}{\mu(F_{n})}

Since
(F_{n})
is a Følner sequence the R.H.S converges to 0 as
n\rightarrow\infty
. Hence
A_{n}(x)
converges to 0.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

Now, if G is unimodular, we can take
(F_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
to be symmetric and left and right Følner both. In that case,
\overline{\nu}
is G invariant. Indeed, for
\nu\in M_{*}
, recall that

A_{n}(\nu)=\frac{1}{\nu(F_{n})}\int_{F_{n}}\nu\circ\alpha_{g^{-1}}\,d\mu(g),\text{ and }\overline{\nu}=\|\cdot\|_{1}-\lim_{n\to\infty}A_{n}(\nu).

Fix
h\in G
, we observe that
\overline{\nu}\circ\alpha_{h}=\overline{\nu}
. Indeed, we notice that

\displaystyle A_{n}(\nu)\circ\alpha_{h}=\frac{1}{\mu(F_{n})}\int_{F_{n}}\nu\circ\alpha_{g^{-1}}\circ\alpha_{h}\,d\mu(g)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mu(F_{n})}\int_{F_{n}}\nu\circ\alpha_{g^{-1}h}\,d\mu(g)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mu(F_{n})}\int_{F_{n}}\nu\circ\alpha_{{(h^{-1}g)}^{-1}}\,d\mu(g)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mu(F_{n})}\int_{h^{-1}F_{n}}\nu\circ\alpha_{g^{-1}}\,d\mu(g)\text{ [ by unimodularity]}

Hence the difference is

A_{n}(\nu)\circ\alpha_{h}-A_{n}(\nu)=\frac{1}{\mu(F_{n})}\Big(\int_{h^{-1}F_{n}}-\int_{F_{n}}\Big)\nu\circ\alpha_{g^{-1}}\,d\mu(g).

Taking the norm (dual norm on
M_{*}
) and using
\|\nu\circ\alpha_{g^{-1}}\|\leq\|\nu\|
we get the estimate

\|A_{n}(\nu)\circ\alpha_{h}-A_{n}(\nu)\|\leq\frac{1}{\mu(F_{n})}\int_{F_{n}\Delta(h^{-1}F_{n})}\|\nu\circ\alpha_{g^{-1}}\|\,d\mu(g)\leq\|\nu\|\frac{\mu\big(F_{n}\Delta(h^{-1}F_{n})\big)}{\mu(F_{n})}.

Because
\{F_{n}\}
is a Følner sequence and
G
is unimodular (so left and right Haar measures agree), the ratio

\frac{\mu\big(F_{n}\Delta(h^{-1}F_{n})\big)}{\mu(F_{n})}\to 0\qquad(n\to\infty).

Thus
\|A_{n}(\nu)\circ\alpha_{h}-A_{n}(\nu)\|\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. Passing to the
\|\cdot\|_{1}
–limit that defines
\overline{\nu}
yields

\overline{\nu}\circ\alpha_{h}=\overline{\nu}.

Since
h\in G
was chosen arbitrarily,
\overline{\nu}
is
G
–invariant.

We obtain the following convergence result for a noncommutative dynamical system.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
(E,G,\gamma)
be a dynamical system, where
(E,\left\|\cdot\right\|)
is a Banach space. Then, for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
a\in E
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\|B_{n}(a-B_{k}(a))\right\|=0,

where

\displaystyle B_{m}(a):=\frac{1}{\mu(F_{m})}\int_{F_{m}}\gamma_{s}(a)\,d\mu(s),\quad m\in\mathbb{N}.

Proof.

For all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\displaystyle B_{n}(a-B_{k}(a))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mu(F_{n})}\int_{F_{n}}\Big[\gamma_{s}(a)-\gamma_{s}(B_{k}(a))\Big]d\mu(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mu(F_{n})}\int_{F_{n}}\Bigg[\gamma_{s}(a)-\gamma_{s}\bigg(\frac{1}{\mu(F_{k})}\int_{F_{k}}\gamma_{t}(a)d\mu(t)\bigg)\Bigg]d\mu(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mu(F_{n})}\frac{1}{\mu(F_{k})}\int_{F_{n}}\int_{F_{k}}\Big(\gamma_{s}(a)-\gamma_{st}(a)\Big)d\mu(t)d\mu(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mu(F_{n})}\frac{1}{\mu(F_{k})}\int_{F_{k}}\int_{F_{n}}\Big(\gamma_{s}(a)-\gamma_{st}(a)\Big)d\mu(s)d\mu(t)\quad\text{(by Fubini's theorem)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mu(F_{k})}\int_{F_{k}}\frac{1}{\mu(F_{n})}\Bigg(\int_{F_{n}}\gamma_{s}(a)d\mu(s)-\int_{F_{n}t}\gamma_{s}(a)d\mu(s)\Bigg)d\mu(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mu(F_{k})}\int_{F_{k}}\frac{1}{\mu(F_{n})}\Bigg(\int_{G}(\chi_{F_{n}}-\chi_{F_{n}t})(s)\gamma_{s}(a)d\mu(s)\Bigg)d\mu(t).

We observe that
\left|\chi_{F_{n}}-\chi_{F_{n}t}\right|=\chi_{F_{n}\Delta F_{n}t}
.
Since
\sup_{g\in G}\|\gamma_{s}\|
is finite, we get

\displaystyle\left\|B_{n}(a-B_{k}(a))\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\mu(F_{k})}\int_{F_{k}}\Big(\frac{1}{\mu(F_{n})}\int_{G}\left|(\chi_{F_{n}}-\chi_{F_{n}t})(s)\right|\left\|\gamma_{s}(a)\right\|\,d\mu(s)\Big)d\mu(t)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|a\|\sup_{g\in G}\|\gamma_{g}\|}{\mu(F_{k})}\int_{F_{k}}\frac{\mu(F_{n}\Delta F_{n}t)}{\mu(F_{n})}d\mu(t)

Since the functions
g_{n}(t)=\frac{\mu(F_{n}\Delta F_{n}t)}{\mu(F_{n})}
are bounded on
F_{k}
with
\mu(F_{k})<\infty
for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, and converges to 0 pointwise (by the definition of amenability), by the Dominated Convergence Theorem, we obtain for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
a\in E
,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|B_{n}(a-B_{k}(a))\right\|=0.

∎

x\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{\left|(\nu_{n}-\nu)(x)\right|}{\tau(x)}=0.

Remark 4.9
.

Let
(\nu_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{M}_{*}
and
\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{*}
. By Proposition
2.4
, there exist a sequence
(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and an element
X\in L^{1}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
such that
\nu_{n}(x)=\tau(X_{n}x)
and
\nu(x)=\tau(Xx)
for all
x\in\mathcal{M}
.

We claim that
(\nu_{n})
converges to
\nu
in the b.a.u. topology, in the sense of Definition
4.8
, if and only if the corresponding sequence
(X_{n})
converges to
X
bilaterally almost uniformly in
L^{1}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
, as in Definition
2.1
. Indeed, assume first that
\nu_{n}\to\nu
in b.a.u. Then, for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a projection
e\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M})
with
\tau(1-e)<\varepsilon
such that for every
\delta>0
, one can find
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
satisfying, for all
n\geq n_{0}
,

\displaystyle\left|(\nu_{n}-\nu)(exe)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\delta\,\tau(exe),\qquad\forall x\in\mathcal{M}_{+}\setminus\{0\}.

Using the trace representation, this inequality is equivalent to

\displaystyle\left|\tau\!\left((X_{n}-X)(exe)\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\delta\,\tau(exe),\qquad\forall x\in\mathcal{M}_{+}\setminus\{0\},

which, by the faithfulness of
\tau
, is in turn equivalent to

\left\|e(X_{n}-X)e\right\|\leq\delta.

Consequently,
X_{n}\to X
in the b.a.u. sense. The converse implication follows by reversing the above arguments.

The following proposition shows that the predual
\mathcal{M}_{*}
is complete with respect to the b.a.u. topology.

Proposition 4.10
.

Every b.a.u.-Cauchy sequence
(\nu_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathcal{M}_{*}
converges in the b.a.u. topology to some
\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{*}
.

Proof.

The assertion follows directly from Theorem
2.2
together with Remark
4.9
.
∎

We now prove the Banach principle.

Theorem 4.11

(Banach principle)
.

Suppose
G
is a l.c.s.c. amenable group with admissible Følner sequence
(F_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, and let
\mathcal{M}
be a finite von Neumann algebra equipped with a faithful normal tracial state
\tau
and a faithful normal state
\rho
.

Let
(\mathcal{M},G,\alpha)
be a non-commutative dynamical system such that
\rho\circ\alpha_{g}=\rho
for all
g\in G
, i.e.,
(\mathcal{M},G,\alpha,\rho)
forms a kernel. Then, the following set

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}=\{\phi\in\mathcal{M}_{*s}\mid A_{n}(\phi)\text{ converges in b.a.u.}\}

is closed in
\mathcal{M}_{*s}
.

Proof.

Let
(\phi_{n})
be a sequence in
\mathcal{C}
, converging to
\phi\in\mathcal{M}_{*s}
in norm, i.e.,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|\phi_{n}-\phi\|=0.

Then, we need to show that
\phi\in\mathcal{C}
.

First, we note that since
\rho\in\mathcal{M}_{*+}
, there exists a unique
X\in L^{1}(\mathcal{M},\tau)_{+}
such that
\rho(x)=\tau(Xx)
for all
x\in\mathcal{M}
. Then, for any
s>0
, consider the projection
q_{s}:=1_{(1/s,s)}(X)\in\mathcal{M}
. Observe that
(1-q_{s})\xrightarrow{s\to\infty}0
in the strong operator topology (SOT).

Let
\theta>0
be arbitrary. Therefore, there exists an
s_{0}>0
such that
\tau(1-q_{s_{0}})<\frac{\theta}{2}
. Further, this implies
Xq_{s_{0}}\leq s_{0}q_{s_{0}}
. Thus, for all
0\neq x\in q_{s_{0}}\mathcal{M}_{+}q_{s_{0}}
, we have

(4.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{\rho(x)}{\tau(x)}=\frac{\tau(Xx)}{\tau(x)}&=\frac{\tau(Xq_{s_{0}}x)}{\tau(x)}\quad\text{ (since $q_{s_{0}}x=x$)}\\
&\leq\frac{\tau(s_{0}x)}{\tau(x)}=s_{0}.\end{split}

Since
\lim_{n\to\infty}\|\phi-\phi_{n}\|=0
, possibly passing through a subsequence, we can assume that

\displaystyle\|\phi-\phi_{j}\|<\frac{1}{4^{s_{j}}}\text{ for all }j\in\mathbb{N}.

Furthermore, note that
\phi-\phi_{j}\in\mathcal{M}_{*s}
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
. Now, using Corollary
3.12
for
n=N
,
\upsilon=\frac{1}{2^{i}}
, we obtain positive reals
({\gamma_{j}(N)})_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
and projections
q_{N,j}^{i}:=q^{\frac{1}{2^{i}}}_{N,j}
in
\mathcal{M}
for
j,N\in\mathbb{N}
such that:

(1)

\displaystyle\lim_{j\to\infty}\gamma_{j}(N)=0
, uniformly in
N\in\mathbb{N}
,

(2)

\rho(1-q^{i}_{N,j})\leq(1+\frac{1}{2^{i}})\gamma_{j}(N)+\frac{1}{2^{i}}
, and

(3)

for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
,

A_{n}(\phi-\phi_{j})(x)\leq\frac{cs_{j}}{2^{s_{j}}}\rho(x)\text{ for all }x\in q^{i}_{N,j}\mathcal{M}_{+}q^{i}_{N,j}\text{ and }n\in[N].

Now denote
q_{N,j}=q^{j}_{N,j}

We have,

\displaystyle\rho(1-q_{N,j})\leq(1+\frac{1}{2^{j}})\gamma_{j}(N)+\frac{1}{2^{j}}

for all
N\in\mathbb{N}
.
Now, it immediately follows that

\displaystyle\lim_{j\to\infty}\rho(1-q_{N,j})=0,\text{ uniformly in }N\in\mathbb{N}.

We use Lemma
3.9
. Let
\mathcal{M}_{1}=L^{\infty}(G)
and
\mathcal{M}_{2}=\mathcal{M}
, take
S
to be the set of natural numbers
\mathbb{N}
, let
x_{j}(n)=q_{j,n}\text{ and }p_{j}(n)=\chi_{F_{n}}\forall n\in\mathbb{N}
. Note that
f_{j}(N)=\frac{p_{j}(N)\otimes 1-q_{j,N}}{\mu(F_{N})},\forall N\in\mathbb{N}
. We have,

\displaystyle\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\frac{(\varphi\otimes\rho)(\chi_{F_{N}}\otimes(1-q_{N,j}))}{\varphi(\chi_{F_{N}})}=0

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\frac{(\varphi\otimes\tau)(\chi_{F_{N}}\otimes(1-q_{N,j}))}{\varphi(\chi_{F_{N})}}=0\text{ by Lemma 3.10 }

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\varphi(\chi_{F_{N}})\tau(1-q_{N,j}))}{\varphi(\chi_{F_{N}})}=0

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\tau(1-q_{N,j})=0,\text{ uniformly in N}\in\mathbb{N}.

Therefore, there exists a subsequence
(j_{l})_{l\in\mathbb{N}}
such that

\displaystyle\tau(1-q_{N,j_{l}})<\frac{\theta}{2^{l+2}},\quad\text{for all }N\in\mathbb{N}.

Since
\phi_{j}\in\mathcal{C}
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a sequence of projections
(f_{j})
in
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M})
and a strictly increasing sequence
(N_{j})\subseteq\mathbb{N}
such that

\displaystyle\begin{split}(1)\quad&\tau(1-f_{j})<\frac{\theta}{2^{j+2}},\quad\text{for all }j\in\mathbb{N},\\
(2)\quad&\sup_{x\in f_{j}M_{+}f_{j},x\neq 0}\frac{\left|\big(A_{n}(\phi_{j})-A_{m}(\phi_{j})\big)(x)\right|}{\tau(x)}<\frac{1}{2^{j}},\quad\text{for all }n,m\geq N_{j}.\end{split}

Now, for all
l\in\mathbb{N}
, we define
q_{l}:=q_{N_{j_{l+1}},j_{l}}
. Then, we note that

\displaystyle\begin{split}(1)\quad&\tau(1-q_{l})\leq\frac{\theta}{2^{l+2}},\\
(2)\quad&A_{n}(\phi-\phi_{j_{l}})(q_{l}xq_{l})\leq\frac{cs_{j_{l}}}{2^{s_{j_{l}}}}\rho(q_{l}xq_{l}),\quad\text{for all }n\in[N_{j_{l+1}}].\end{split}

Moreover, for the subsequence
(j_{l})
, we have

\displaystyle\tau(1-f_{j_{l}})\leq\frac{\theta}{2^{j_{l}+2}}.

Finally, consider the projection

\displaystyle e=\left(\underset{l\in\mathbb{N}}{\bigwedge}q_{l}\right)\bigwedge\left(\underset{l\in\mathbb{N}}{\bigwedge}f_{j_{l}}\right)\bigwedge q_{s_{0}}.

Then, we have
\tau(1-e)<\theta
and, for all
0\neq x\in e\mathcal{M}_{+}e
, assuming
N_{j_{l}}\leq n,m\leq N_{j_{l+1}}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\left|\big(A_{n}(\phi)-A_{m}(\phi)\big)(x)\right|}{\tau(x)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left|\big(A_{n}(\phi)-A_{n}(\phi_{j_{l}})+A_{n}(\phi_{j_{l}})-A_{m}(\phi_{j_{l}})+A_{m}(\phi_{j_{l}})-A_{m}(\phi)\big)(x)\right|}{\tau(x)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\left|\big(A_{n}(\phi)-A_{n}(\phi_{j_{l}})\big)(x)\right|}{\tau(x)}+\frac{\left|\big(A_{n}(\phi_{j_{l}})-A_{m}(\phi_{j_{l}})\big)(x)\right|}{\tau(x)}+\frac{\left|\big(A_{m}(\phi_{j_{l}})-A_{m}(\phi)\big)(x)\right|}{\tau(x)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\left|\big(A_{n}(\phi-\phi_{j_{l}})\big)(x)\right|}{\tau(x)}+\frac{\left|\big(A_{n}(\phi_{j_{l}})-A_{m}(\phi_{j_{l}})\big)(x)\right|}{\tau(x)}+\frac{\left|\big(A_{m}(\phi_{j_{l}}-\phi)\big)(x)\right|}{\tau(x)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\left|\big(A_{n}(\phi-\phi_{j_{l}})\big)(x)\right|}{\rho(x)}+\frac{\left|\big(A_{m}(\phi_{j_{l}}-\phi)\big)(x)\right|}{\rho(x)}\right)\frac{\rho(x)}{\tau(x)}+\frac{\left|\big(A_{n}(\phi_{j_{l}})-A_{m}(\phi_{j_{l}})\big)(x)\right|}{\tau(x)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{2cs_{j_{l}}}{2^{s_{j_{l}}}}\right)s_{0}+\frac{1}{2^{s_{j_{l}}}}=\frac{(2cs_{j_{l}}s_{0}+1)}{2^{s_{j_{l}}}},\quad(\text{by Eq. }\ref{rho-tau}).

Since
j_{l}\to\infty
as
m,n\to\infty
, it follows that

\displaystyle\lim_{m,n\to\infty}\sup_{x\in eM_{+}e,x\neq 0}\frac{\left|\big(A_{n}(\phi)-A_{m}(\phi)\big)(x)\right|}{\tau(x)}=0.

Therefore,
(A_{n}(\phi))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is Cauchy in b.a.u. Thus, by Proposition
4.10
, it follows that
(A_{n}(\phi))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is convergent in b.a.u., which implies that the set
\mathcal{C}
is closed in
\mathcal{M}_{*s}
. ∎

is unimodular, due to our use of proposition

(ii).We conclude with the following ergodic theorem.

Proof.

5.
Stochastic Ergodic Theorem

This section brings together the results developed so far in order to derive a stochastic ergodic theorem. Throughout, we assume that
\mathcal{M}\subseteq\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
is a von Neumann algebra endowed with a faithful normal tracial state
\tau
, and that we work in the associated noncommutative space
L^{1}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
. Moreover, we assume that
G
is a unimodular amenable group admitting a symmetric admissible Følner sequence
(F_{n})
.

We consider a noncommutative dynamical system
(\mathcal{M},G,\alpha)
. As before, for each
X\in L^{1}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
we define the corresponding ergodic averages by

\displaystyle A_{n}(X):=\frac{1}{\mu(F_{n})}\int_{F_{n}}\alpha_{s}^{*}(\nu)\,d\mu(s)=\frac{1}{\mu(F_{n})}\int_{F_{n}}\hat{\alpha}_{s^{-1}}(X)\,d\mu(s).

Relying on the preceding results, we now establish a stochastic ergodic theorem for the averages
A_{n}(\cdot)
. To this end, we first recall the following decomposition theorem from
[
BS23b
]
.

Theorem 5.1

(Neveu Decomposition)
.

Let
(\mathcal{M},G,\alpha)
be a noncommutative dynamical system, and let
\tau
be a faithful normal tracial state on
\mathcal{M}
. Then there exist projections
e_{1},e_{2}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M})
satisfying
e_{1}+e_{2}=1
and
e_{1}e_{2}=0
, with the following properties:

(i)

There exists a
G
-invariant normal state
\rho
on
\mathcal{M}
whose support projection is
s(\rho)=e_{1}
.

(ii)

There exists an element
x_{0}\in\mathcal{M}
with support
s(x_{0})=e_{2}
such that

\frac{1}{\mu(F_{n})}\int_{F_{n}}\alpha_{s}(x_{0})\,d\mu(s)\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}0\quad\text{in }\left\|\cdot\right\|.

Moreover, the projections
s(\rho)
and
s(x_{0})
are uniquely determined.

We next record several basic consequences of this decomposition, following
[
BS23b
]
.

(1)

For every
g\in G
and
i=1,2
, the projections
e_{i}
are
\alpha
-invariant, that is,
\alpha_{g}(e_{i})=e_{i}
.

(2)

The restriction of
\alpha
to the reduced von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{M}_{e_{i}}:=e_{i}\mathcal{M}e_{i}
induces an action by automorphisms. Consequently, for each
i=1,2
, the triple
(\mathcal{M}_{e_{i}},G,\alpha)
forms a noncommutative dynamical system.

(3)

For
i=1,2
and all
g\in G
, the induced predual action
\hat{\alpha}_{g}
on
L^{1}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
satisfies

\hat{\alpha}_{g}(e_{i}Xe_{i})=e_{i}\hat{\alpha}_{g}(X)e_{i},\qquad\forall X\in L^{1}(\mathcal{M},\tau).

As a consequence, the ergodic averages obey

e_{i}A_{n}(X)e_{i}=A_{n}(e_{i}Xe_{i}),\qquad\forall X\in L^{1}(\mathcal{M},\tau),\ n\in\mathbb{N},\ i=1,2.

For
i=1,2
, we define the normalized traces
\tau_{e_{i}}:=\frac{1}{\tau(e_{i})}\,\tau|_{e_{i}\mathcal{M}e_{i}}
. We are now in a position to state the main convergence result.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
(\mathcal{M},G,\alpha)
be a noncommutative dynamical system, and let
\tau
be a faithful normal tracial state on
\mathcal{M}
. Let
e_{1},e_{2}\in\mathcal{M}
be the projections arising from the Neveu decomposition. Then the following assertions hold:

(i)

For every
B\in L^{1}(\mathcal{M}_{e_{1}},\tau_{e_{1}})
, there exists
\bar{B}\in L^{1}(\mathcal{M}_{e_{1}},\tau_{e_{1}})
such that
A_{n}(B)
converges bilaterally almost uniformly to
\bar{B}
. In addition,
A_{n}(B)
converges to
\bar{B}
in measure.

(ii)

For every
B\in L^{1}(\mathcal{M}_{e_{2}},\tau_{e_{2}})
, the sequence
A_{n}(B)
converges to
0
in measure.

Moreover, for any
X\in L^{1}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
, there exists an element
Z\in L^{1}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
such that
A_{n}(X)
converges to
Z
in measure and satisfies

e_{1}Ze_{1}=Z,\qquad e_{2}Ze_{2}=0.

Proof.

Given the Neveu decomposition (Theorem
5.1
) and the b.a.u. convergence result of Theorem
4.13
, the argument proceeds verbatim as in
[
BS23b
, Section 5]
.
∎

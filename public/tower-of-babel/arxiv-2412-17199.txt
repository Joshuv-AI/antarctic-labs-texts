\max_{n\in\mathcal{L}}\frac{1}{\Psi(T,q)}\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in S(T,q)\end{subarray}}e(kan/N)\right|\ll T^{\tfrac{1}{A}-\tfrac{1}{4}+o(1)}.

Finally, consider when
n\in\mathcal{S}
. Since

\tilde{m}=\max\{m,\Delta T\}\leq\max\{M_{2},T/T_{0}\}=T/T_{0}\leq\sqrt{T}/q^{10}\leq\tfrac{1}{10}(T/q^{3})^{1/2},

we may apply Harper’s theorem to obtain

\max_{n\in\mathcal{S}}\frac{1}{\Psi(T,q)}\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in S(T,q)\end{subarray}}e(kan/N)\right|\ll\frac{(\log T)^{9/2}}{\tilde{m}^{\tfrac{1}{2}(1-3/A)-o(1)}}\leq\frac{(\log T)^{9/2}}{M_{1}^{\tfrac{1}{2}(1-3/A)-o(1)}}.

Collecting these bounds together, we get from (
15
) that

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{n\in\mathcal{E}_{b}(N)}e(kan/N)\right|

\displaystyle\leq|\mathcal{B}|+|\mathcal{E}_{b}(N)|\left(\max_{n\in\mathcal{S}}\frac{1}{\Psi(T,q)}\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in S(T,q)\end{subarray}}e(kan/N)\right|+\max_{n\in\mathcal{L}}\frac{1}{\Psi(T,q)}\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in S(T,q)\end{subarray}}e(kan/N)\right|\right)+(\log T)^{2}2^{q^{2}}|\mathcal{E}(N)|

\displaystyle\ll\frac{NM_{1}}{T_{0}}+|\mathcal{E}_{b}(N)|\left(\frac{(\log T)^{9/2}}{M_{1}^{\tfrac{1}{2}(1-3/A)-o(1)}}+T^{\tfrac{1}{A}-\tfrac{1}{4}+o(1)}\right)+(\log T)^{2}2^{q^{2}}|\mathcal{E}(N)|

\displaystyle\ll NT^{o(1)}\left(\frac{M_{1}}{T_{0}}+M_{1}^{-\tfrac{1}{2}(1-3/A)}+T^{\tfrac{1}{A}-\tfrac{1}{4}}\right)+(\log T)^{2}2^{q^{2}}|\mathcal{E}(N)|.

We choose
M_{1}=T^{1/4}
to obtain

\left|\sum_{n\in\mathcal{E}_{b}(N)}e(kan/N)\right|\ll NT^{o(1)}\left(T^{\frac{1}{5}-\frac{1}{4}}+T^{-\tfrac{1}{8}(1-3/5)}\right)+(\log T)^{2}2^{q^{2}}|\mathcal{E}(N)|\ll NT^{-\tfrac{1}{20}+o(1)}+(\log T)^{2}2^{q^{2}}|\mathcal{E}(N)|,

as claimed.
∎

We are now in a position to prove Proposition
2.1
.

Proof of Proposition
2.1
.

Let
1\leq K<N
be a parameter to be chosen, and let
2\leq r\leq T^{1/40}
. By the Erdős-Turán inequality
[
12
, Cor. 1.1]
, we have

\sup_{I\subseteq[0,1]}\left||\{n\in\mathcal{E}_{r}(N):n/N\in I\}|-|I||\mathcal{E}_{r}(N)|\right|\ll\frac{|\mathcal{E}_{r}(N)|}{K}+\sum_{1\leq k\leq K}\frac{1}{k}\left|\sum_{n\in\mathcal{E}_{r}(N)}e(nk/N)\right|.

Since
N
is prime, for every
1\leq k\leq K
we have
(k,N)=1
. Thus, applying the previous lemma and specialising to
I=(0,1/r)
, we have

\left||\{n\in\mathcal{E}_{r}(N):n\in(0,N/r)\}|-\frac{1}{r}|\mathcal{E}_{r}(N)|\right|\ll\frac{|\mathcal{E}_{r}(N)|}{K}+(\log K)\left(NT^{-1/20+o(1)}+(\log T)^{2}2^{q^{2}}|\mathcal{E}(N)|\right).

But as remarked in (
4
), we have
\mathcal{E}_{r}(N)\cap(0,N/r)=\emptyset
, so that upon rearranging we get

|\mathcal{E}_{r}(N)|\ll rN\left(\frac{1}{K}+\frac{\log K}{T^{1/20+o(1)}}\right)+r(\log K)(\log T)^{2}2^{q^{2}}|\mathcal{E}(N)|.

We select
K=T^{1/20}
, so as
\log T\leq q
and
r\leq T^{1/40}
we conclude that

|\mathcal{E}_{r}(N)|\ll NT^{-1/40+o(1)}+2^{(1+o(1))q^{2}}|\mathcal{E}(N)|.

Replacing
T
by
T^{1/40}
yields the statement of Proposition
2.1
.
∎

6.
Proofs of the main results

In this section we prove Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
.

6.1.
Proof of Theorem
1.3

Theorem
1.3
is a consequence of Proposition
1.4
, which we may now obtain.

Proof of Proposition
1.4
.

Assume GRH. We begin with the proof of the first claim. Assume for the sake of contradiction that there is some
C>0
such that for infinitely many
N
,

|\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}(N)|>N-Ne^{-C(\log\log N)^{6}}.

Then there is an
\eta\in\{-1,+1\}
such that

|\mathcal{E}(N)|=|\{n<N:\lambda(n)=\eta\lambda(N-n)\}|=\frac{1}{2}\left(N-1+\eta\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}(N)\right)<Ne^{-C(\log\log N)^{6}}.

Now, let

q:=\frac{C}{10}(\log\log N)^{6},\quad P:=e^{\tfrac{1}{40}q^{1/5}},

so that if
N
is sufficiently large then
P\geq(\log N)^{100}
. By Proposition
2.1
whenever
P/2<p\leq P
we obtain

|\mathcal{E}_{p}(N)|\ll NP^{-1+o(1)}+2^{(1+o(1))q^{2}}|\mathcal{E}(N)|=o(N).

On the other hand, by Proposition
2.2
, we can find
p\in(P/2,P]
such that
|\mathcal{E}_{p}(N)|\gg N
, which yields a contradiction. The first claim follows.

To prove the second claim, it suffices to observe that for each
(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\in\{-1,+1\}^{2}
,

\displaystyle|\{n<N:(\lambda(n),\lambda(N-n))=(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\}|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{n<N}(1+\eta_{1}\lambda(n))(1+\eta_{2}\lambda(N-n))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\left(N-1+\eta_{1}\eta_{2}\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}(N)+(\eta_{1}+\eta_{2})\sum_{n<N}\lambda(n)\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{4}\left(N-1-|\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}(N)|-2\left|\sum_{n<N}\lambda(n)\right|\right).

Combining the first claim with the prime number theorem (with Vinogradov-Korobov error term), there are constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that this last expression is

\geq C_{1}Ne^{-C(\log\log N)^{6}}-C_{2}Ne^{-(\log N)^{3/5}(\log\log N)^{-1/5}}\gg Ne^{-C(\log\log N)^{6}}

for large enough
N
, and the second claim follows.
∎

Remark 9
.

We show here that, instead of GRH the assumption that
L(s,\chi)\neq 0
for all
\chi\neq\chi_{0}
modulo
N
in the rectangle

\alpha<\text{Re}(s)\leq 1,\,|\text{Im}(s)|\leq(\log N)^{3}

with
\alpha=1-(\log N)^{-c}
and
c\in(0,3/50)
, suffices to prove our two main theorems.

Assume that
|\mathcal{E}(N)|\leq Ne^{-(\log N)^{3/5}}
. We show that we may get a contradiction under these conditions, which suffices in the proof of Proposition
1.4
above. Following the proof of Proposition
2.2
, we have
|\mathcal{E}_{p}(N)|\gg N
for some
P<p\leq 2P
, as long as
P=P(N)\rightarrow\infty
and

\frac{(\log N)(\log\log N)}{P^{1-\alpha}}\ll\frac{1}{\log\log N},

say. This last condition is fulfilled as long as we may select

\log P\geq 2(\log N)^{c}\log\log N,

taking
N
sufficiently large. On the other hand, Proposition
2.1
allows for a bound of the shape

|\mathcal{E}_{r}(N)|\ll 2^{(1+o(1))q^{2}}|\mathcal{E}(N)|+Nr^{-1+o(1)}=o(N),

on taking
q=\tfrac{1}{100}(\log N)^{3/10}
,
R:=e^{0.01q^{1/5}}
and
R/2<r\leq R
. Then
\log R\gg q^{1/5}=(\log N)^{3/50-o(1)}
. Thus, provided
c\in(0,3/50)
the choice of
P=R/2
is admissible in giving a contradiction, provided
N
is sufficiently large.

Having proven Proposition
1.4
, we may now obtain Theorem
1.3
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

For each
\boldsymbol{\eta}:=(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\in\{-1,+1\}^{2}
and prime
p\geq 3
define

T_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(p):=|\{n<p:(\lambda(n),\lambda(p-n))=(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\}|.

By Proposition
1.4
,

\min_{\boldsymbol{\eta}\in\{-1,+1\}^{2}}T_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(p)\gg pe^{-C(\log\log p)^{6}},

so in particular, there is
p_{0}\geq 2
such that for every prime
p\geq p_{0}
,

\min_{\boldsymbol{\eta}\in\{-1,+1\}^{2}}T_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(p)\geq 1.

Assume now that
N
is not
p_{0}
-friable. Then there is a prime
p>p_{0}
such that
N=pN^{\prime}
, for some
N^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
. Set
\eta:=\lambda(N^{\prime})
, and let
\boldsymbol{\epsilon}\in\{-1,+1\}^{2}
. By the above assumption, we may find
1\leq b<p
with

\displaystyle(\lambda(b),\lambda(p-b))=(\eta\epsilon_{1},\eta\epsilon_{2}).

Let
a:=bN^{\prime}
, so that
N-a=N^{\prime}(p-b)
. Then

\lambda(a)=\lambda(b)\lambda(N^{\prime})=(\eta\epsilon_{1})\eta=\epsilon_{1},\quad\lambda(N-a)=\lambda(N^{\prime})\lambda(p-b)=\eta(\eta\epsilon_{2})=\epsilon_{2}.

The first claim thus follows.

For the second claim, it is clear that if
N_{0}:=\prod_{p\leq p_{0}}p
and if
N>N_{0}
is squarefree then
N
has a prime factor
p>p_{0}
. Thus, as
N
is not
p_{0}
-friable, (2) must hold for
N
, and the second claim follows from the first.
∎

6.2.
Application to Shusterman’s problem

By combining Theorem
1.3
with the work of
[
7
]
, we may now prove Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let
p_{0}
be the constant from Theorem
1.3
. We consider four cases:

(i)

Suppose that
N
is not
p_{0}
-friable. By Theorem
1.3
, we immediately have a pair
1\leq a,b<N
with
a+b=N
and
\lambda(a)=\lambda(b)=-1
, as claimed.

(ii)

Suppose
N
is
p_{0}
-friable and divisible by
8
, and write
N=8N^{\prime}
. Then as
8=4+4=3+5
, we obtain the sign patterns
\lambda(3)=\lambda(5)=-1
and
\lambda(4)=\lambda(4)=+1
. Thus, if
\lambda(N^{\prime})=-1
then we get the choice
a=b=4N^{\prime}
, yielding
\lambda(a)=\lambda(b)=-1
, while if
\lambda(N^{\prime})=+1
then the choice
a=3N^{\prime}
,
b=5N^{\prime}
yields
\lambda(a)=\lambda(b)=-1
.

(iii)

Suppose
N
is
p_{0}
-friable,
8\nmid N
and
\lambda(N)=+1
. As
N
is even, the choice
a=b=N/2
immediately yields
\lambda(a)=\lambda(b)=-\lambda(N)=-1
.

(iv)

Finally, suppose
N
is
p_{0}
-friable,
8\nmid N
and
\lambda(N)=-1
. We claim that there is a threshold
N_{0}
such that if
N\geq N_{0}
then a choice of
a
and
b
with
\lambda(a)=\lambda(b)=-1
exists.

We observe first that any
p_{0}
-friable, even integer
N
with
8\nmid N
may be written as
N=2^{k}M^{2}N^{\prime}
, where
1\leq k\leq 2
,
2\nmid N^{\prime}M^{2}
and
M^{2}
is the largest odd square factor dividing
N
. Then
N^{\prime}
is squarefree and
p_{0}
-friable, so e.g. by the prime number theorem there is a constant
C>0
such that

2^{k}N^{\prime}\leq 4\prod_{p\leq p_{0}}p\leq 4e^{Cp_{0}}.

Now, set
N_{0}:=4\cdot 11^{2}\cdot e^{Cp_{0}}
and assume that
N\geq N_{0}
. Then, writing
N=2^{k}M^{2}N^{\prime}
as before,
M\geq 11
. As
M
is odd, applying
[
7
, Thm. 1]
shows there is an
1\leq n<M/2
such that
\lambda(n)=\lambda(M-n)
. Setting
d:=M-2n\geq 1
, we get that
n=(M-d)/2
,
M-n=(M+d)/2
, so that
\lambda(M-d)=\lambda(M+d)
, and thus
\lambda(M^{2}-d^{2})=+1
. We thus find that the pair
(M^{2}-d^{2},d^{2})
produces the sign pattern

(\lambda(M^{2}-d^{2}),\lambda(d^{2}))=(+1,+1).

Therefore, as
\lambda(2^{k}N^{\prime})=\lambda(N)=-1
the pair
(a,b)=(2^{k}N^{\prime}(M^{2}-d^{2}),2^{k}N^{\prime}d^{2})
yields

\displaystyle(\lambda(a),\lambda(b))=(\lambda(2^{k}N^{\prime}(M^{2}-d^{2})),\lambda(2^{k}N^{\prime}d^{2}))=(-1,-1),

\displaystyle a+b=2^{k}N^{\prime}(M^{2}-d^{2})+2^{k}N^{\prime}d^{2}=2^{k}N^{\prime}M^{2}=N.

Combining these four cases, the theorem follows.
∎

Appendix A: A first moment estimate for
\nu_{r}(m)

As discussed in Remark
7
, our recursive bound
|\mathcal{E}_{r}(N)|\leq 2^{r^{2}}|\mathcal{E}(N)|
would improve substantially provided we could better estimate sums involving the quantity

\nu_{r}(m):=\left|\left\{n<N:\,\exists\,1\leq r_{1}<\cdots<r_{k}<r\text{ with }n=N\sum_{1\leq j<k}\frac{(-1)^{j-1}}{r_{1}\cdots r_{j}}+\frac{(-1)^{k}m}{r_{1}\cdots r_{k}}\right\}\right|.

Improving on the pointwise bound
\nu_{r}(m)\leq 2^{r-1}
used in the proof of Proposition
4.1
, we may show the following average bound for
\nu_{r}(m)
over the interval
(N/(r+1),N/r)
, for
r\geq 1
not too large (for comparison, our current range of
r
in the recursive bound is
r=O(\sqrt{\log N})
).

Lemma
.

Let
\delta>0
, let
r
be a sufficiently large integer (in terms of
\delta
only), and assume that
N\geq\exp(10(\log r)^{2+\delta})
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{N/(r+1)<m<N/r}\nu_{r}(m)\ll\frac{N\log r}{r}.

Proof.

We begin with the following observation. Recall that
\nu_{r}(m)
counts integers
n<N
such that there is a tuple
1\leq r_{1}<\cdots<r_{k}<r
,
k\geq 1
with

(16)

n=N\sum_{1\leq j\leq k}\frac{(-1)^{j-1}}{r_{1}\cdots r_{j}}+\frac{(-1)^{k}m}{r_{1}\cdots r_{k}}.

Suppose
N/(r+1)<m<N/r
and suppose
n
arises from a tuple
\boldsymbol{r}=(r_{1},\ldots,r_{k})
with
r_{1}\cdots r_{k}>2N/r
. Let
2\leq k_{0}\leq k
be the index such that

r_{1}\cdots r_{k_{0}-1}\leq\frac{2N}{r}<r_{1}\cdots r_{k_{0}}\leq 2N

(that
k_{0}\geq 2
follows from
N>r^{2}>rr_{1}
). Then
n
is the closest integer to
N\sum_{1\leq j\leq k_{0}}\frac{(-1)^{j-1}}{r_{1}\cdots r_{j}}
, and so is determined by the initial segment
(r_{1},\ldots,r_{k_{0}})
of the tuple
(r_{1},\ldots,r_{k})
, and arises for at most a single
m
(which, then, may occur in an interval outside of
(N/(r+1),N/r)
). It follows that the number of
n
determined in this way by tuples of
r_{j}
satisfying
r_{1}\cdots r_{k}>2N/r
, and
m\in(N/(r+1),N/r)
, is bounded by the number of tuples of
r_{j}^{\prime}
such that
r_{1}^{\prime}\cdots r_{k}^{\prime}\leq 2N
.

On the other hand, suppose
N/(r+1)<m<N/r
and
n
arises from a tuple
\boldsymbol{r}
with
r_{1}\cdots r_{k}\leq 2N/r
. In this case, from (
16
) we see that

m\equiv NP_{k}(r_{1},\ldots,r_{k})\pmod{r_{1}\cdots r_{k}},

where we have set

P_{k}(r_{1},\ldots,r_{k})=r_{1}\cdots r_{k}\left(\sum_{1\leq j\leq k}\frac{(-1)^{j-1}}{r_{1}\cdots r_{j}}\right)\in\mathbb{Z}.

We may therefore upper bound the sum over
\nu_{r}(m)
as

\displaystyle\sum_{N/(r+1)<m<N/r}\nu_{r}(m)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq r_{1}<\cdots<r_{k}<r\\
r_{1}\cdots r_{k}\leq 2N/r\end{subarray}}|\{N/(r+1)<m<N/r:m\equiv NP_{k}(r_{1},\cdots,r_{k})\pmod{r_{1}\cdots r_{k}}\}|

\displaystyle+|\{1\leq r_{1}<\cdots<r_{k}<r:r_{1}\cdots r_{k}\leq 2N\}|

\displaystyle=:T_{1}+T_{2}.

In estimating
T_{1}
we use that

|\{N/(r+1)<m<N/r:m\equiv NP_{k}(r_{1},\cdots,r_{k})\pmod{r_{1}\cdots r_{k}}\}|\leq\left(1+\frac{N}{r_{1}\cdots r_{k}r(r+1)}\right),

so that

T_{1}\leq N\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq r_{1}<\cdots<r_{k}<r\\
r_{1}\cdots r_{k}\leq 2N/r\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{r_{1}\cdots r_{k}r(r+1)}+|\{1\leq r_{1}<\cdots<r_{k}<r:r_{1}\cdots r_{k}\leq 2N\}|\leq T_{1}^{\prime}+T_{2},

writing
T_{1}^{\prime}
to denote the sum in the middle expression. To estimate
T_{1}^{\prime}
we use Rankin’s trick in the form
1_{r_{k}<r}\leq\frac{r}{r_{k}+1}
, to get

\displaystyle T_{1}^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{N}{r+1}\sum_{1\leq r_{1}<\cdots<r_{k}<r}\frac{1}{r_{1}\cdots r_{k-1}r_{k}(r_{k}+1)}=:\frac{N}{r+1}S(r).

We may decompose
S(r)
in terms of
S(r-1)
as

\displaystyle S(r)

\displaystyle=S(r-1)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq r_{1}<\cdots<r_{k-1}<r-1\\
r_{k}=r-1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{r_{1}\cdots r_{k-1}r_{k}(r_{k}+1)}=S(r-1)+\frac{1}{r(r-1)}\sum_{1\leq r_{1}<\cdots<r_{k-1}<r-1}\frac{1}{r_{1}\cdots r_{k-1}}

\displaystyle=S(r-1)+\frac{1}{r(r-1)}\prod_{1\leq j<r-1}\left(1+\frac{1}{j}\right)=S(r-1)+\frac{1}{r}.

Iterating this over
r
, we obtain
S(r)\ll\log r
, and hence

T_{1}^{\prime}\ll\frac{N\log r}{r}.

We next consider
T_{2}
. We split this as
T_{2}=T_{2}^{\prime}+T_{2}^{\prime\prime}
, where

\displaystyle T_{2}^{\prime}

\displaystyle:=|\{1\leq r_{1}<\cdots<r_{k}<r:\,r_{1}\cdots r_{k}\leq 2N,\,1\leq k\leq(\log r)^{1+\delta}\}|,

\displaystyle T_{2}^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle:=|\{1\leq r_{1}<\cdots<r_{k}<r:\,r_{1}\cdots r_{k}\leq 2N,\,k>(\log r)^{1+\delta}\}|.

We apply Rankin’s trick to
T_{2}^{\prime}
to get

\displaystyle T_{2}^{\prime}\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq r_{1}<\cdots<r_{k}<r\\
1\leq k\leq(\log r)^{1+\delta}\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{2N}{r_{1}\cdots r_{k}}\right)^{1-1/\log r}\leq(2N)^{1-1/\log r}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq r_{1}<\cdots<r_{k}<r\\
1\leq k\leq(\log r)^{1+\delta}\end{subarray}}\frac{e^{k}}{r_{1}\cdots r_{k}}.

Repeating the argument of
T_{1}^{\prime}
and invoking the assumed bound
N^{1/(2\log r)}>r(\log r)e^{(\log r)^{1+\delta}}
, we see that

T_{2}^{\prime}\leq N^{1-1/\log r}e^{(\log r)^{1+\delta}}r\sum_{1\leq r_{1}<\cdots<r_{k}<r}\frac{1}{r_{1}\cdots r_{k}(r_{k}+1)}<N^{1-1/\log r}r(\log r)e^{(\log r)^{1+\delta}}\leq N^{1-1/(2\log r)}.

Finally we consider
T_{2}^{\prime\prime}
. For this, we first observe (following
[
15
]
) that if
K:=\lfloor(\log r)^{1+\delta}\rfloor
then

\displaystyle T_{2}^{\prime\prime}\leq 2N\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq r_{1}<\cdots<r_{k}<r\\
k>(\log r)^{1+\delta}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{r_{1}\cdots r_{k}}\leq 2N\sum_{k\geq K}\sum_{1\leq r_{1}<\cdots<r_{k}<r}\frac{1}{r_{1}\cdots r_{k}}=\frac{2N}{(r-1)!}\sum_{k\geq K}\left[{r\atop k}\right],

where
\left[{r\atop k}\right]
is an unsigned Stirling number of the first kind. By explicit estimates for Stirling numbers (see e.g.
[
1
]
), when
k>(\log r)^{1+\delta}
we have

(17)

\frac{\left[{r\atop k}\right]}{(r-1)!}\leq\frac{(\tau+r-1)\cdots(\tau+1)}{\tau^{k}(r-1)!},

where
\tau=\tau(r,k)>0
is the unique solution to the equation

(18)

k=\sum_{1\leq j\leq r-1}\frac{\tau}{\tau+j}.

We observe that, crudely,

k\leq\tau\sum_{1\leq j\leq r-1}\frac{1}{j}\leq\tau(\log r+1),

so that
\tau\geq\tfrac{1}{2}(\log r)^{\delta}\geq 20
, provided that
r
is sufficiently large. We use this shortly.

Using (
17
) and (
18
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{\left[{r\atop k}\right]}{(r-1)!}

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(\sum_{1\leq j\leq r-1}\left(\log(\tau+j)-\log j\right)-k\log\tau\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\sum_{1\leq j\leq r-1}\left(\log(1+\tau/j)-\frac{\tau\log\tau}{\tau+j}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\sum_{1\leq j\leq r-1}\frac{\tau}{\tau+j}\left(\left(1+\frac{j}{\tau}\right)\log\left(1+\frac{\tau}{j}\right)-\log\tau\right)\right).

Next, observe that
\phi_{\tau}(u):=(1+u/\tau)\log(1+\tau/u)
is non-increasing for all
u>0
, since

\phi_{\tau}^{\prime}(u)=\frac{1}{\tau}\log(1+\tau/u)-\frac{1}{u}\leq\frac{1}{\tau}\cdot\frac{\tau}{u}-\frac{1}{u}\leq 0.

It follows that as
\tau\geq 20
, for every
j\geq 2
we get

\phi_{\tau}(j)-\log\tau\leq\phi_{\tau}(2)-\log\tau=\left(1+\frac{2}{\tau}\right)\left(\log(2+\tau)-\log 2\right)-\log\tau\leq-\log 2+\frac{2}{\tau}\left(1+\log(2+\tau)\right)\leq-\frac{1}{3}.

Furthermore, we have

\phi_{\tau}(1)-\log\tau\leq(\log\tau)\left(1+1/\tau-1\right)+2\log(1+1/\tau)=O\left(\frac{\log\tau}{\tau}\right).

Using (
18
) once again, it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{\left[{r\atop k}\right]}{(r-1)!}\leq\exp\left(\sum_{1\leq j\leq r-1}\frac{\tau}{\tau+j}\left(\phi_{\tau}(j)-\log\tau\right)\right)\ll\exp\left(-\frac{1}{3}\sum_{1\leq j\leq r-1}\frac{\tau}{\tau+j}\right)=e^{-k/3}.

Using the fact that
k\geq(\log r)^{1+\delta}
, we deduce that for any
A>0
we get

T_{2}^{\prime\prime}\ll N\sum_{k\geq K}e^{-k/3}\ll Ne^{-K/3}\ll_{A}Nr^{-A},

as long as
r
is large enough (in terms only of

). Adding together all of these contributions, we obtain

\sum_{N/(r+1)<m<N/r}\nu_{r}(m)\ll\frac{N\log r}{r},

which proves the claim.

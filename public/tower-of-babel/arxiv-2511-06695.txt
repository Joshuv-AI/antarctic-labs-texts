Takuma Aihara

Abstract
. We give several examples of tilting-discrete symmetric algebras; in particular, one explores which algebra has tilting-discrete trivial extension. We provide a counter example of the conjecture stating any τ \tau -tilting finite symmetric algebra is tilting-discrete. Also, we discuss the tilting-disconnectedness of symmetric algebras and give new examples of tilting-disconnected symmetric algebras.



1.
Introduction

The tilting-discreteness of a (finite dimensional) symmetric algebra is a finiteness condition on tilting complexes; that is, a tilting-discrete symmetric algebra has only finitely many tilting complexes of each length
[
Ai1
]
.
In the case, we can ‘essentially’ obtain all tilting complexes via tilting mutation;
roughly speaking, such a symmetric algebra is said to be tilting-connected.
Thus, we can grasp the whole picture of tilting complexes over a tilting-discrete symmetric algebra.
Typical examples of tilting-discrete symmetric algebras are representation-finite symmetric algebras
[
Ai1
]
, Brauer graph algebras whose Brauer graphs admit at most one cycle of odd length and none of even length
[
AAC
]
, symmetric algebras of dihedral, semidihedral and quaternion type
[
EJR
]
and symmetric algebras of tubular type with nonsingular Cartan matrix
[
AHMW
]
.
A big problem is to classify all tilting-discrete symmetric algebras.

To reach the goal,
\tau
-tilting finiteness is a must-check property, because it is a necessary condition for a symmetric algebera to be tilting-discrete
[
AIR
,
DIJ
,
DIRRT
]
;
in particular, the endomorphism algebras of tilting complexes over a given symmetric algebra are all
\tau
-tilting finite if and only if the symmetric algebra is tilting-disctete
[
Ai1
]
.
Note also that the notion of support
\tau
-tilting modules coincides with that of 2-term tilting complexes for a symmetric algebra.
Then, we are curious about the following folklore conjecture:

Conjecture
.

Any
\tau
-tilting finite symmetric algebra is tilting-discrete.

First, we give an easy observation to judge the truth of the conjecture; a
3-term
complex is defined to be a complex whose
i
-th term is zero unless
i=0,-1,-2
.

Proposition
.

(1)

If every
\tau
-tilting finite symmetric algebra
\Lambda
admits only finitely many 3-term (basic) tilting complexes, then the conjecture holds true.

(2)

The following are equivalent:

(i)

Any
\tau
-tilting finite symmetric algebra is tilting-discrete;

(ii)

For every (not necessarily, symmetric) algebra
A
, the trivial extension of
A
is tilting-discrete if and only if it is
\tau
-tilting finite;

Proof.

(1) Due to
[
AM
, Theorem 2.4]
, we have only to show that for any irreducible tilting mutation
T
of
\Lambda
, there exist only finitely many tilting complexes
U
with
T\geq U\geq T[1]
.
Evidently, we have
\Lambda\geq T\geq U\geq T[1]\geq\Lambda[2]
; hence we are done by the assumption.

(2) We need only check the implication (ii)
\Rightarrow
(i).
Let
A
be a
\tau
-tilting finite symmetric algebra.
The symmetry of
A
indicates that the trivial extension
\Lambda
of
A
is isomorphic to
A\otimes_{K}K[x]/(x^{2})
.
By
[
AH1
, Theorem 2.1 (Corollary 2.2)]
and its proof, we see that
\Lambda
is
\tau
-tilting finite, which implies by assumption that
\Lambda
is tilting-discrete.
Again, by
[
AH1
, Theorem 2.1]
and its proof the functor
-\otimes_{K}K[x]/(x^{2}):\mathsf{K^{b}}(\operatorname{\mathsf{proj}}A)\to\mathsf{K^{b}}(\operatorname{\mathsf{proj}}\Lambda)
induces an injection
\operatorname{\mathsf{tilt}}A\to\operatorname{\mathsf{tilt}}\Lambda
that preserves the partial order.
This completes the proof.
∎

Thus, we focus on trivial extension algebras to grope for answers of the conjecture.
An advantage to do that is the following.
Let
A
be a (not necessarily, symmetric) algebra with Cartan matrix
C
.
If the trivial extension of
A
is
\tau
-tilting finite, then we have:

(1)

[
DIRRT
, Theorem 5.12(d)]

A
is also
\tau
-tilting finite;

(2)

[
Hi
, Proposition 3.2]
The quadratic form
\overline{x^{\rm tr}}Cx
has a strictly positive real part for complex vectors
x
, and equivalently,
C
is invertible and the (usual) homological quadratic form
x^{\rm tr}C^{-{\rm tr}}x
(i.e., Euler form) for real vectors

is positive definite.
Here,
(-)^{\rm tr}
and
\overline{(-)}
denote the transpose and the complex conjugate of a matrix.

We refer to the condition (2) as a
positive definite Cartan matrix
.
In this case, the algebra

satisfies certain good properties; see Appendix
A
.
Note that the conditions (1) and (2) are independent (Example
3.3
).
We also remark that even if every algebra with the same trivial extension is
\tau
-tilting finite and has a positive definite Cartan matrix, its trivial extension is not necessarily
\tau
-tilting finite (Example
3.5
).

As we solved the conjecture negatively, one sees that (2) of the proposition fails.

Also, we explore the tilting-disconnectedness of symmetric algebras; for example,

2.
Brauer graph algebras

First of all, let us recall equivalent conditions of tilting-discrete Brauer graph algebras.

Theorem 2.1
.

The following are equivalent for a Brauer graph algebra
\Lambda
:

(1)

\Lambda
is tilting-discrete;

(2)

It is
\tau
-tilting finite;

(3)

The Cartan matrix of
\Lambda
is positive definite;

(4)

It is regular;

(5)

The Grothendieck group
K_{0}(\operatorname{\underline{\mathsf{mod}}}\Lambda)
of the stable module category of
\Lambda
is finite;

(6)

The Brauer graph of
\Lambda
has at most one cycle of odd length and none of even cycle.

The equivalences of (3)–(6) were shown in
[
JGM
, Theorem 4.38]
.
The proof below on the implications
(5)\Leftrightarrow(6)
is due to
[
An2
]
; see also
[
JGM
, Remark 4.42]
.

Proof.

The implications
(6)\Leftrightarrow(1)\Rightarrow(2)\Rightarrow(3)\Rightarrow(4)\Leftrightarrow(5)
hold, respectively by
[
AAC
, Theorem 6.7]
, by definition, by
[
Hi
, Proposition 3.2]
, clear, and by
[
TW2
, Proposition 1]
.

We show the implications
(5)\Leftrightarrow(6)
hold true.
Let
v
and
n
be the numbers of vertices and edges of
G
, respectively.
It follows from
[
An2
, Theorem 6.1]
that

\begin{array}[]{c@{\iff}c}K_{0}(\operatorname{\underline{\mathsf{mod}}}\Lambda)\ \mbox{has no free part}&\begin{cases}\ n=v-1&\mbox{if $G$ is bipartite};\\
\ n=v&\mbox{otherwise}.\end{cases}\end{array}

The former is just the case that
G
is tree.
The latter is the case that
G
has precisely one cycle and the cycle is of odd length.
This completes the proof.
∎

For a symmetric algebra of tubular type, the same equivalent conditions (1)–(5) of Theorem

On the other hand, we know that this is not necessarily true for a symmetric algebra in general; neither the implications

Theorem 2.6
.

Assume that a Brauer graph
G
is either of the following:

(i)

it has only one vertex and at least two edges, or;

(ii)

it has precisely two vertices, at least two edges and is bipartite.

Then the Brauer graph algebra
\Lambda

is not tilting-connected.

The idea of a proof comes from a private conversation with Grant, Iyama and the author circa 2010, in which we gave the first example of tilting-disconnected algebras; it was the Brauer graph algebra of digon.

Proof.

Note first that
G
owns no leaves.
By Lemma

, we see that the
g

\Lambda
has eigenvalue one.
Since any Kauer move does not change the property of

However, of course, the 1-shift of

Thus, it turns out that the 1-shift of

is tilting-disconnected.

3.
Trivial extension algebras

In this section, we tackle the following problem.

Question 3.1
.

Thus, a next problem is when a

As is seen in the introduction, if the trivial extension of an algebra

(i)

it has no eigenvalue one, but all eigenvalues have absolute value one;

(ii)

it is diagonalizable.

We check the conditions (1) and (2) above are independent.

Example 3.3
.

The implication (1)

In fact, the radical-square-zero algebra given by the quiver
\textstyle{1\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{2\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\textstyle{1\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

Now, we give several cases in which Question

has an affirmative answer.

As we have already seen in Theorem

(
2.3
), the first example is the case of Brauer graph algebras (the trivial extensions of gentle algebras).

Thus, we next consider when a

Let

We put the following theorem as a slogan.

Theorem 3.6

The following are equivalent for

(1)

The trivial extension of

is tilting-discrete;

(2)

It is
\tau
-tilting finite;

(3)

is a
\tau
-tilting finite algebra with positive definite Cartan matrix;

(4)

It is a piecewise hereditary algebra of Dynkin type.

The class

contains good classes of algebras and is closed under certain properties.
We will explore which algebras belong to

in Appendix

For example, we obtain the following result by a similar argument to Proposition

Corollary 3.7
.

Let

be a symmetric algebra stable equivalent to the trivial extension of a derived hereditary algebra.
Then
\Lambda
is tilting-discrete if and only if it is
\tau
-tilting finite.

Let

be an acyclic quiver.
A

is the endomorphism algebra of a cluster-tilting object of the cluster category

As is well-known, a cluster-tilted algebra is given by the relation extension

of some tilted algebra

Moreover, we also know that a cluster-tilted algebra of type

In the case, its Cartan matrix admits only

We refer to

Let

(3)

As above, put

Then,

.

If the trivial extension of

4.
An upper bound of Cartan invariants

In this section, we study a relationship beween the tilting-discreteness of symmetric algebras and upper bounds of Cartan invariants, which was first given in
[
EJR
]
.

Let
\Lambda
be a symmetric algebra and
C
its Cartan matrix.
Denote by
\mathcal{I}
the set of the isomorphism classes of indecomposable partial tilting complexes of
\Lambda
and take the map

\delta:\mathcal{I}\to K_{0}(\mathsf{K^{b}}(\operatorname{\mathsf{proj}}\Lambda))\to\mathbb{Z}\ (X\mapsto[X]^{\rm tr}\cdot C\cdot[X])
; note that
\delta(X)=\dim\operatorname{End}_{\mathsf{K^{b}}(\operatorname{\mathsf{proj}}\Lambda)}(X)
.
We remark that the supremum of
\delta(\mathcal{I})
is a derived invariant; the Cartan matrices of derived equivalent algebras are
\mathbb{Z}
-congruent by the
g
-matrix of a tilting complex.

For an integer
z
, we set
\mathcal{R}_{z}:=\{v\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\ |\ v^{\rm tr}\cdot C\cdot v=z\}
.

We recall the following result, which is a slight generalization of
[
EJR
, Theorem 13]
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Assume that
\Lambda
is symmetric and has a positive definite Cartan matrix.
If there is an upper bounds of
\delta(\mathcal{I})
, then
\Lambda
is tilting-discrete.
In particular, if there exist only finitely many derived equivalent algebras to
\Lambda
(up to Morita equivalence), then
\Lambda
has a positive definite Cartan matrix if and only if
\Lambda
is tilting-discrete.

Proof.

By assumption, note that
\mathcal{R}_{z}
is a finite set for any
z
; it is empty for every
z\leq 0
.
Put
d:=\sup\delta(\mathcal{I})<\infty
.
Let
T
be a tilting complex of
\Lambda
and
G:\mathsf{K^{b}}(\operatorname{\mathsf{proj}}\Gamma)\to\mathsf{K^{b}}(\operatorname{\mathsf{proj}}\Lambda)
an induced derived equivalence, where
\Gamma:=\operatorname{End}_{\mathsf{K^{b}}(\operatorname{\mathsf{proj}}\Lambda)}(T)
.

Suppose that
T=\Lambda
.
As above, the set of vectors
[X]
for indecomposable 2-term pretilting complexes
X
is finite.
Thanks to
[
DIJ
, Theorem 6.5]
, it is seen that there exist only finitely many indecomposable 2-term tilting complexes, whence
\Lambda
is
\tau
-tilting finite.

Let us consider a general case.
Take an indecomposable 2-term pretilting complex
U
of
\Gamma
.
Then, we get in
K_{0}(\mathsf{K^{b}}(\operatorname{\mathsf{proj}}\Lambda))
,
[GU]=\mathsf{G}_{T}\cdot[U]
, where
[U]
is the
g
-vecter of
U
in
K_{0}(\mathsf{K^{b}}(\operatorname{\mathsf{proj}}\Gamma))
.
By assumption, one obtains that
[GU]
belongs to some
\mathcal{R}_{z}
for
1\leq z\leq d
, and so when
U
runs all such complexes, the vectors appear only finitely many numbers.
If
[GU]=[GV]
for another such a complex
V
of
\Gamma
, then we have
\mathsf{G}_{T}\cdot[U]=\mathsf{G}_{T}\cdot[V]
; hence,
[U]=[V]
in
K_{0}(\mathsf{K^{b}}(\operatorname{\mathsf{proj}}\Gamma))
because
\mathsf{G}_{T}
is invertible.
So, we get an isomorphism
U\simeq V
by
[
DIJ
, Theorem 6.5]
, which implies that
\Gamma
is
\tau
-tilting finite.
Thus,
\Lambda
is tilting-discrete.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

The assumption of the upper bound as in Theorem
4.1
can be restricted, instead of
\mathcal{I}
, to the set of indecomposable direct summands of iterated (irreducible left) mutations starting from
\Lambda
; see
[
AM
, Theorem 2.4]

Thanks to
[

, Proposition 3.2]
, we immediately obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 4.3
.

A
\tau
-tilting finite symmetric algebra with
\sup\delta(\mathcal{I})<\infty
is tilting-discrete.

We know that, under the assumption of the derived equivalence class of a symmetric algebra being finite, the conditions (1)–(3) as in Theorem
2.1
are equivalent.
However, we have no idea if a tilting-discrete symmetric algebra satisfies the assumption.
More weakly, it is also unknown whether or not, the tilting-discreteness brings about an upper bound of
\delta(\mathcal{I})
; that is, does the converse of Corollary
4.3
hold?

Appendix A
Algebras of (generalized) cyclotomic type

We briefly explain advantages of algebras admitting positive definite Cartan matrices;
for instance, we saw such algebaras appear if thier trivial extensions are
\tau
-tilting finite.

Let
A
be an algebra with nonsingular Cartan matrix
C
and
\Phi
denote its Coxeter matrix: i.e.,
\Phi:=-C^{\rm tr}C^{-1}
.
Here,
(-)^{\rm tr}
stands for the transpose of the matrix.
We say that

is
of (generalized) cyclotomic type
if the characteristic polynomial of
\Phi
factorizes as the product of cyclotomic polynomials (all the eigenvalues of
\Phi
have abusolute value one).
Note that if an algebra of generalized cyclotomic type has an integral Coxeter matrix (e.g.,
A
is Iwanaga–Gorenstein), then it is of cyclotomic type, due to Kronecker’s theorem.

Remark A.1
.

There is another definition of Coxeter matrices, which does not need the assumption of
A
having a regular Cartan matrix.
However, we assume that
A
has finite right injective dimension.
Then, the triangle functor
\tau:=-\otimes^{\mathsf{L}}_{A}DA[-1]:\mathsf{D^{b}}(\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}A)\to\mathsf{D^{b}}(\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}A)
is well-defined, where
D
stands for the
K
-dual.
Now, we define the
Coxeter matrix
as the matrix expression of
[\tau]:K_{0}(\mathsf{D^{b}}(\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}A))\to K_{0}(\mathsf{D^{b}}(\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}A))
with respect to the canonical basis
\{S_{i}\}
.
Here,
S_{i}
is a simple module corresponding to
P_{i}
.
If in addition the Cartan matrix
C
of
A
is regular, then the Coxeter matrix coincides with the matrix
\Phi
.
Following this manner, every gentle algebra is of cyclotomic type
[
JGM
, Theorem 6.14]
.
Also, any selfinjective algebra has cyclotomic type.

To illustrate, we use the radical-square-zero algebra presented by the quiver
\textstyle{1\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{2\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
.
Then, its Cartan and Coxeter matrices are
\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\
1&1\end{pmatrix}
and
\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix}

Thus, this says that the algebra is actually of cyclotomic type with singular Cartan matrix.

The following is the main result of this appendix we might as well recall.

Proposition A.2
.

Let
A
be an algebra with positive definite Cartan matrix

.
Then:

(1)

is of generalized cyclotomic type;

(2)

[

We consider the quadratic form

(-)^{\rm tr}
and
(-)
denote the transpose and the complex conjugate of a matrix.
Note that its real part is strictly positive.
Indeed, putting

When

Hence, we figure out the claim since the real part of

Let

We have equalities

Therefore, we get

We remark by the proofs that if algebras as in Proposition
B.3
and
B.4
have positive definite Cartan matrices, then they are PHADs; that is, in the cases, the
\tau
-tilting finiteness is automatically implied.

An algebra
B
is said to be
accessible
from an algebra
A
if there exists a sequence
A=:B_{0},B_{1},\cdots,B_{r}:=B
of algebras such that
B_{i+1}
is the one-point (co)extension of
B_{i}
by an exceptional
B_{i}
-module.

Proposition B.5
.

The one-point extension of an algebra
A
in
\mathcal{H}
by an indecomposable module
M
is in
\mathcal{H}
.
Hence, any accessible algebra from an algebra in
\mathcal{H}
belongs to
\mathcal{H}
.

Proof.

Put
\widetilde{A}:=\begin{pmatrix}K&M\\
0&A\end{pmatrix}
and assume that it is
\tau
-tilting finite with positive definite Cartan matrix.
Then,
A
is isomorphic to
e\widetilde{A}e
for a certain idempotent
e
of
\widetilde{A}
, and so it is also
\tau
-tilting finite and has a positive definite Cartan matrix.
We derive from
A\in\mathcal{H}
that
A
is a PHAD.
As
M
is indecomposable, applying the trick of Barot–Lenzing
[
BL
, Theorem 1]
leads to the fact that
\widetilde{A}
is piecewise hereditary.
Since
\widetilde{A}
has a positive definite Cartan matrix, it is a PHAD.
∎

Finally, let us give an analogue of this proposition.

Proposition B.6
.

Let
A
be a triangular algebra with a source or sink
e
such that all summands of
A/AeA
are in
\mathcal{H}
and:

(i)

eA
has a separated radical if
e
is a source;

(ii)

eDA
has a separated socle factor if
e
is a sink.

Then
A
belongs to
\mathcal{H}
.

Proof.

We only show the case (ii); the other can be handled similarly.

Write
A/AeA={\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{r}A_{i}}
with
A_{i}
ring-indecomposable and
eDA/\operatorname{soc}(eDA)={\displaystyle\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}M_{i}}
such that
M_{i}
is an
A_{i}
-module.
Since
eDA
has a separated socle factor, we see that
M_{i}
is indecomposable.
Now, let us assume that
A
is
\tau
-tilting fintie and admits a positive definite Cartan matrix.
Then,
A/AeA\ (\simeq(1-e)A(1-e))
is also
\tau
-tilting finite and has a positive definite Cartan matrix, whence so are
A_{i}
’s, and they are PHADs by assumption.

Let
B
be the
\nu
-reflection at the sink; i.e., the factor algebra of the one-point extension
\begin{pmatrix}K&eDA\\
0&A\end{pmatrix}
by the ideal
\left\langle\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
0&e\end{pmatrix}\right\rangle
.
As the discussion above, we figure out that
B
is isomorphic to the upper triangular matrix algebra

\begin{pmatrix}K&M_{1}&M_{2}&\cdots&M_{r}\\
&A_{1}&&&\\
&&A_{2}&&\\
&&&\ddots&\\
&&&&A_{r}\end{pmatrix}.

Applying the trick of Barot–Lenzing
[
BL
, Theorem 1]
, it turns out that
B
is piecewise hereditary.
Moreover, it follows from
[
TW1
]
that
A
and
B
are derived equivalent, and so their Cartan matrices represent equivalent quadratic forms.
This implies that
B
also has a positive definite Cartan matrix,
whence it is a PHAD; and, so is
A
.
Thus,
A\in\mathcal{H}
.
∎

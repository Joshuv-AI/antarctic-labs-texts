Complexity Results of Persuasion
Alban Grastien

Abstract




Persuasion is a problem introduced by Wojtowicz
[
Woj24
]
.
It consists in finding a subset of facts that improves
the a-posteriori probability of a specific fact over a given threshold.
It has applications in “law, advertising, politics, science, public relations, etc”.
We refer the reader to the original paper for a discussion about the benefits of this problem.

The original paper claims that the problem is
np-complete
and presents a proof.
We claim that that proof was incorrect;
in particular, they make a confusion between the empty conjunction of a collection of sets
and the pairwise empty conjunction.
We prove that persuasion is in
p
when the persuasion threshold is
1
.
As the reduction proposed by Wojtowicz uses with value for threshold,
this proves the incorrectness of their reduction
(we do not believe that
p = np
is proved by our simple computations).
We propose a new proof based, as in the original paper, on
exact cover

and show
np-hard
ness in the general case.

1
Notation and Problem Definition

Given a set
S=\{S_{1},\dots,S_{k}\}
of sets,
we use
\bigcap S
to denote
S_{1}\cap\dots\cap S_{k}

and
\bigcup S
to denote
S_{1}\cup\dots\cup S_{k}
.

A
probability space
is a triple
P=\langle\Omega,F,\pi\rangle

where
\Omega
is a finite set of
worlds
,

F\subseteq 2^{\Omega}
is a set of
events

(each event is a subset of worlds),
and
\pi:\Omega\mapsto[0,1]
is a probability function over
\Omega
.
The natural extension of probability function
\pi
to sets is
\pi^{*}

and is defined by
\pi^{*}(S)=\sum_{\omega\in S}\ \pi(\omega)
.
It is assumed that the current (unknown) world is
\omega^{\star}
,
that it has a non-zero probability (
\pi(\omega^{\star})>0
),
and that it belongs to the intersection of
F
:
\omega^{\star}\in\bigcap F
.
For people who are not familiar with this notation, an event
f

can be interpreted as a “fact”;
stating this fact to someone informs the recipient of the statement
that the real world
\omega^{\star}
belongs to the set
f
.

Given a probability space
P=\langle\Omega,F,\pi\rangle
,
an
observation
is a subset
R\subseteq F
of events.
The
probability that an event
e\subseteq\Omega
is true given an observation
R

is calculated by

\mathrm{Prob}(e\ |\ R)=\frac{\pi^{*}(e\cap\bigcap R)}{\pi^{*}(\bigcap R)}.

(1)

Note that the value always sits between
0
and
1
;
in particular since
\omega^{\star}
belongs to each event
r\in R
,
the denominator is strictly positive (
\pi^{*}(\bigcap R)\geq\pi(\omega^{\star})>0
).

A
persuasion problem instance
(PPI) is a triple
\langle P,E,\tau\rangle

where
P=\langle\Omega,F,\pi\rangle
is a probability space,

E\subseteq\Omega
is a particular event called the
goal
,
and
\tau\in[0,1]
is a
threshold
.

A
solution
to PPI
\langle P,E,\tau\rangle
with
P=\langle\Omega,F,\pi\rangle

is an observation
R
such that
\mathrm{Prob}(E\ |\ R)\geq\tau
.

Persuasion
is the problem of deciding whether a PPI has a solution.

It can be useful to reason about events in mirror.
Specifically, what is interesting about a given event
f

is the set
\Omega\setminus f
of worlds
that it does contain.
We say that it
excludes
or
rejects
these worlds.
The worlds rejected by an observation are all the worlds
rejected by at least one event in the observation.

2
Strong Persuasion

Definition 1

A
strong persuasion problem instance
(SPPI) is a PPI where the threshold
\tau
evaluates to
1
.

Strong Persuasion
is the restriction of
Persuasion
to SPPIs.

It is possible to prove that there is a solution to the strong persuasion problem
iff
R=F
is a solution,
i.e.
\bigcap F\subseteq E
holds.

Corollary 1

Strong Persuasion
is in
P
.

If one wanted to drop the condition that the intersection of
F
is not empty
(but that would be silly),
we could use the following property:
An SPPI admits a solution
iff
there exists
\omega
in
E
such that
R_{\omega}=\{r\in F\mid\omega\in r\}
is a solution.
Thus, it is possible to go through all elements of
E

and check in polynomial time whether they yield a solution observation.

3
Complexity of Persuasion

Wojtowicz proposes a proof of
np-hard
ness complexity for
Persuasion
,
but this proof includes an error.
Specifically, they provide a reduction from
Exact Cover
(defined next subsection)
in which they prove that the intersection of the computed sets is empty;
however, the problem definition requires every pair of set to be disjoint.
We provide a corrected proof here.

3.1
Exact Cover

To prove the complexity of
Persuasion
, we use a reduction from the
np-complete
problem
Exact Cover

which we present now.

An
Exact Cover Instance
(ECI) is a pair
\langle S,A\rangle
where
S=\{s_{1},\dots,s_{n}\}
is a finite set of objects
and
A=\{A_{1},\dots,A_{k}\}
is a set of (non-empty, pairwise different) subsets of
S
such that
\bigcup A=S
holds.
Elements from
S
are called
universe elements

and indices ranging between
1
and
n
are
universe indices
.
A
solution to ECI
\langle S,A\rangle
is a subset
H
of
A

that forms a partition of
S
, i.e.,

•

\forall\{A_{i},A_{j}\}\subseteq H.\ A_{i}\cap A_{j}\neq\emptyset\Rightarrow A_{i}=A_{j}
and

•

\bigcup_{A_{i}\in\mathcal{A}}\ A_{i}=S
.

(A partition should include no empty set, but this is trivially satisfied here
as
A
has no empty set.)

Exact Cover
is the problem of deciding whether there is a solution to an ECI;
it is
np-complete
.

3.2
Reduction

We now propose a reduction from
Exact Cover
to
Persuasion
.

We start with a description of the reduction and give the formal definition afterwards.
Our reduction introduces four types of worlds:

•

W_{0}
and
X_{0}
are two worlds that cannot be rejected by an observation.

W_{0}
belongs to goal
E
while
X_{0}
does not.
These two worlds guarantee that we never get any solution with
0
or
1
probability.

•

Z
-variables are mapped with universe elements.
They do not belong to goal
E
.

•

Y
-variables are mapped with pair
\langle i,\ell\rangle
such that
s_{\ell}\in A_{i}
.
They belong to goal
E
.

The events are defined to match the sets of
A
.
The event
F_{i}
(associated with
A_{i}
) will exclude precisely all
Y
-variables that mention
i

and all
Z
-variables mapped with a universe element of
A_{i}
.

The probabilities of these variables are set in such a way
that the probability of goal
E
given observation
R
is attained
iff the following two conditions are satisfied:

1.

All
Z
-variables are excluded from observation
R
.
This guarantees that the observation matches a cover of
S
.
Remember that
Z
-variables do not belong to goal
E
;
thus, giving them a high enough probability will penalise
\mathrm{Prob}(E\ |\ R)

if they are not rejected.

2.

Exactly
n

Y
-variables are excluded from observation
R
.
This guarantees that a given universe element
is not excluded by two different events from the observation:
thus the cover is exact.
As
Y
-variables belong to goal
E
,
solutions are naturally better when they do not exclude them;
yet their probability is set to a low value
so that this objective is superseeded by the first one
(which guarantees the solution is a cover).

Our proposed reduction is presented on Fig.
1
.

Reduction

Let
\langle S,A\rangle
be an ECI where
S=\{s_{1},\dots,s_{n}\}
and
A=\{A_{1},\dots,A_{k}\}
.
Let
m=\sum_{i\in\{1,\dots,k\}}\ |A_{i}|
be the number of elements in the subsets of
A
.
We define the PPI
\langle P,E,\tau\rangle
with
P=\langle\Omega,F,\pi\rangle
as follows:

•

\Omega=W\cup X\cup Y\cup Z
where

–

W=\{W_{0}\}
is a singleton,

–

X=\{X_{0}\}
is a singleton,

–

Y=\{Y_{i,\ell}\mid i\in\{1,\dots,k\},\ell\in\{1,\dots,n\}\textnormal{ such that }s_{\ell}\in A_{i}\}
contains
m
variables, and

–

Z=\{Z_{\ell}\mid\ell\in\{1,\dots,n\}\}
contains
n
variables,

•

F=\{F_{1},\dots,F_{k}\}
where
\forall i\in\{1,\dots,k\}

F_{i}=\Omega\setminus\{Y_{i,\ell}\mid s_{\ell}\in A_{i}\}\setminus\{Z_{\ell}\mid s_{\ell}\in A_{i}\},

•

\pi
satisfies

\pi=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\pi(W_{0})=\pi(X_{0})=x,&\\
\pi(Y_{i,\ell})=y&i\in\{1,\dots,k\},\ell\in\{1,\dots,n\}\textnormal{ such that }s_{\ell}\in A_{i}\\
\pi(Z_{\ell})=z&\ell\in\{1,\dots,n\},\end{array}\right.

where

–

x=\frac{1}{3}
,

–

y=\frac{1-2x}{m(1+2n)}
,

–

z=2my
, and

•

E=W\cup Y
, and

•

\tau=\frac{x+(m-n)y}{2x+(m-n)y}
.

Given a set
R\subseteq F
of facts, we define the set
H_{R}\subseteq A
of subsets by

H_{R}=\{A_{i}\mid\exists r\in R.\ \exists\ell\in\{1,\dots,n\}.\ s_{\ell}\in A_{i}\land Y_{i,\ell}\not\in r\}

Reduction from
Exact Cover
to
Persuasion
.

3.3
Properties of the reduction

Lemma 2

\pi
is a probability function over
\Omega
.

We need to verify two properties: that the probability for each element in
\Omega
is between
0
and
1
,
and that the probabilities add up to
1
.
Remember that there are
m

Y
-variables and
n

Z
-variables.

1.

Probabilities of
W_{0}
and
X_{0}
equal
1/3
and are thus in
[0,1]
.
Probability of a
Y
-variable is
(1-2x)/(m(1+2n))=(1/3)/(m(1+2n))
;
thus it is within
[0,1]
.
Probability of a
Z
-variable is
z=2my=2m(1-2x)/(m(1+2n))=2(1-2x)/(1+2n)=(2/3)/(1+2n)
;
thus it is within
[0,1]
.

2.

The sum of probabilities is

\begin{split}\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}\pi(\omega)&=x+x+my+nz\\
&=x+x+my+2nmy\\
&=x+x+(1+2n)my\\
&=x+x+(1+2n)m\frac{1-2x}{m(1+2n)}\\
&=x+x+(1-2x)\\
&=1.\end{split}

QED

Lemma 3

Any
R
such that
\bigcap R
contains one of more
Z
-variable satisfies
\mathrm{Prob}(E\ |\ R)<\tau
.

Let us assume that
\bigcap R
contains at least one
Z
-variable.
Let
m^{\prime}
and
n^{\prime}
be the number of
Y
-variables and
Z
-variables that appear
in
\bigcap R
.
Notice:
m^{\prime}\in\{0,\dots,m\}
and
n^{\prime}\in\{1,\dots,n\}
.

\begin{split}\mathrm{Prob}(E\ |\ R)-\tau&=\frac{x+m^{\prime}y}{2x+m^{\prime}y+n^{\prime}z}-\tau\\
&<\frac{x+my}{2x+z}-\tau\\
&=\frac{x+my}{2x+z}-\frac{x+(m-n)y}{2x+(m-n)y}\\
&\leq\frac{x+my}{2x+z}-\frac{x}{2x}\\
&=\frac{2x(x+my)-x(2x+z)}{2x(2x+z)}\\
&=\frac{1}{2(2x+z)}(2(x+my)-(2x+z))\\
&=\frac{1}{2(2x+z)}(2x+2my-2x-z)\\
&=\frac{1}{2(2x+z)}(2my-z)\\
&=0.\end{split}

Thus,
\mathrm{Prob}(E\ |\ R)
is strictly less than
\tau
.
QED

Corollary 4

Any
R
such that
\bigcap R
contains strictly fewer than
(m-n)

Y
-variables satisfies
\mathrm{Prob}(E\ |\ R)<\tau
.

Let
m^{\prime}
and
n^{\prime}
be the number of
Y
-variables and
Z
-variables that appear
in
\bigcap R
.
Assume
\mathrm{Prob}(E\ |\ R)=\tau
.
From Lemma
3
, we know
n^{\prime}=0
.
Thus,

\begin{split}0&=\mathrm{Prob}(E\ |\ R)-\tau\\
&=\frac{x+m^{\prime}y}{2x+m^{\prime}y+n^{\prime}z}-\tau\\
&=\frac{x+m^{\prime}y}{2x+m^{\prime}y}-\tau\\
&=\frac{x+m^{\prime}y}{2x+m^{\prime}y}-\frac{x+(m-n)y}{2x+(m-n)y},\end{split}

Thus,
m^{\prime}=m-n
.
QED

Corollary 5

Any
R
that satisfies
\mathrm{Prob}(E\ |\ R)\geq\tau

contains exactly
(m-n)

Y
-variables.

Assume
R
is a solution to the PPI.
From Lemma
3
, we know that
\bigcap R
contains no
Z
-variables,
i.e., for each
\ell\in\{1,\dots,n\}
. there is
i\in\{1,\dots,k\}
,
such that hold both
Z_{i}\not\in F_{\ell}
and
F_{\ell}\in R
.
But, by definition of the reduction,
we also have
Y_{i,\ell}\not\in F_{i}
and
Y_{i,\ell}\not\in\bigcap R
.
In other words, for each
\ell\in\{1,\dots,n\}
,
there is a different
Y
-variable not in
\bigcap R
,
i.e., the number of
Y
-variables in
\bigcap R
is at most
(m-n)
.
QED

Corollary 6

If
R
is a solution to the PPI, then
H_{R}
is a solution to the ECI.

Assume
R
is a solution to the PPI.

Firstly, let
\ell\in\{1,\dots,n\}
be a universe index;
from Lemma
3
, we know that
\bigcap R
does not contain
Z_{\ell}
.
Thus, looking at the subsets of
F
that exclude
Z_{\ell}
,

R
must contain a set
F_{i}
(
i\in\{1,\dots,k\}
)
such that
s_{\ell}\in A_{i}
.
This
F_{i}
also excludes
Y_{i,\ell}
.
Thus, by definition of
H_{R}
, this
A_{i}
belongs to
H_{R}
.
Therefore,
\bigcup H_{R}
includes
s_{\ell}
, and all elements in
S
.

Secondly,
we just mentioned that for each index
\ell
, there is at least one variable
Y_{i,\ell}
not in
\bigcap R
.
Assume, by contradiction, that
H_{R}
contains two different sets
A_{i}
and
A_{j}
that are not disjoint,
i.e., such that some universe index
\ell
belongs to
A_{i}\cap A_{j}
.
Then, both
Y_{i,\ell}
and
Y_{j,\ell}
are excluded by
\bigcap R
.
However, this implies that
\bigcap R
excludes at least
n+1

Y
-variables.
This contradicts Corollary
5
which states
that
\bigcap R
contains exactly
(m-n)

Y
-variables.
Therefore all sets in
H_{R}
are disjoint.

In conclusion,
H_{R}
is an exact cover of
S
.
QED

Theorem 7

persuasion
is
np-hard
.

This is a consequence of Corollary
6

which showed that our reduction from
exact cover
to
persuasion
is correct.
QED

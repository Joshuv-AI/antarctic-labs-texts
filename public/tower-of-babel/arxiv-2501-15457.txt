A note on the minimum size of Turán systems
Xizhi Liu, Oleg Pikhurko

Abstract
For positive integers n ≥ s > r n\geq s>r , a Turán ( n , s , r ) (n,s,r) -system is an n n -vertex r r -graph in which every set of s s vertices contains at least one edge. Let T ⁡ ( n , s , r ) T(n,s,r) denote the the minimum size of a Turán ( n , s , r ) (n,s,r) -system. Upper bounds on T ⁡ ( n , s , r ) T(n,s,r) were established by Sidorenko [ Sid97 ] for the case s − r = Ω ⁡ ( r / ln ⁡ r ) s-r=\Omega(r/\ln r) (based on a construction of Frankl–Rödl [ FR85 ] ) and by a number of authors in the case s − r = O ⁡ ( 1 ) s-r=O(1) . In this note, we establish upper bounds in the remaining range O ⁡ ( 1 ) < s − r = O ⁡ ( r / ln ⁡ r ) O(1)<s-r=O(r/\ln r) .



1
Introduction

Given an integer
r\geq 2
, an
r
-uniform hypergraph
(henceforth an
r
-graph
)
\mathcal{H}
is a collection of
r
-subsets of some set
V
. We call
V
the
vertex set
of
\mathcal{H}
and denote it by
V(\mathcal{H})
. When
V
is understood, we usually identify a hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
with its set of edges.

For positive integers
n\geq s>r
, a
Turán
(n,s,r)
-system
is an
r
-graph
\mathcal{H}
on an
n
-set
V
such that every
s
-subset
S\subseteq V
contains at least one edge from
\mathcal{H}
.
Denote by
T(n,s,r)
the smallest
size
(i.e. the number of edges) of a Turán
(n,s,r)
-system.
Observe that
T(n,s,r)=\binom{n}{r}-\mathrm{ex}(n,K_{s}^{r})
, where
\mathrm{ex}(n,K_{s}^{r})
denotes the Turán number of the complete
r
-graph on
s
vertices
K_{s}^{r}
.
A simple averaging argument shows that
\mathrm{ex}(n,K_{s}^{r})/\binom{n}{r}
is non-increasing (see e.g.
[
KNS64
]
), and hence the following limit exists:

\displaystyle t(s,r)\coloneqq\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{T(n,s,r)}{\binom{n}{r}}.

(1)

Determining the value of
t(s,r)
is a central topic in Extremal Combinatorics.
The seminal Turán Theorem
[
Tur41
]
established that
t(s,2)=\frac{1}{s-1}
for all
s\geq 3
(with the case
s=3
solved earlier by Mantel
[
Man07
]
).
However, the exact value of
t(s,r)
remains unknown for any pair
(s,t)
satisfying
s>r\geq 3
, despite decades of active attempts.
Erdős
[
Erd81
]
offered $500 for the determination of
t(s,r)
for any
s>r\geq 3
.
Turán and other researchers conjectured that
t(s,3)=\frac{4}{(s-1)^{2}}
for
s\geq 4
.
Various constructions achieving this bound are known (see e.g.
[
Sid95
]
).
For
r\geq 4
, there is no general conjectured value for
t(s,r)
, except for the case
(s,r)=(5,4)
(see
[
Gir90
,
Mar09
]
).
For further related results, we refer the reader to surveys such as
[
dC94
,
Für91
,
Sid95
,
Kee11
]
.

In this note, we focus on the case where
r\to\infty
, and all asymptotics are taken with respect to
r
unless otherwise specified. The trivial lower bound is
t(s,r)\geq{1}/{\binom{s}{r}}
, which follows e.g. from the monotonicity of the ratio in (
1
). For convenience, let us define

\displaystyle\mu(s,r)\coloneqq t(s,r)\cdot\binom{s}{r}.

Thus
\mu(s,r)
is always at least 1.

The best-known general lower bound,
t(s,r)\geq 1/{\binom{s-1}{r-1}}
(i.e.
\mu(s,r)\geq s/r
) is due to de Caen
[
dC83a
]
.
In particular,
t(r+1,r)\geq\frac{1}{r}
, a result that was independently proved by de Caen
[
dC83b
]
, Sidorenko
[
Sid82
]
, and Tazawa and Shirakura
[
TS83
]
.
Further improvements on
t(r+1,r)
in lower order terms were made by Giraud (unpublished), Chung–Lu
[
CL99
]
, and Lu–Zhao
[
LZ09
]
.

Improving previous upper bounds established by Sidorenko
[
Sid81
]
, Kim–Roush
[
KR83
]
, Frankl–Rödl
[
FR85
]
, and Sidorenko
[
Sid97
]
, the second author established the following upper bound, which disproved the conjecture of de Caen
[
dC94
]
that
r\cdot t(r+1,r)\to\infty
.

Theorem 1

(
[
Pik24
]
)
.

For every integer
R\geq 1
, it holds that

\displaystyle\mu(r+R,r)\leq\alpha+o(1),\quad\text{as}\ r\to\infty,

where
\alpha\coloneqq{(c_{0}+1)^{R+1}}/{c_{0}^{R}}
with
c_{0}=c_{0}(R)
being the largest real root of the equation
\mathrm{e}^{x}=(x+1)^{R+1}
.
In particular,
\mu(r+1,r)\leq 4.911
for all sufficiently large
r
.

An immediate corollary of Theorem
1
(for derivation see
[
Pik24
, Corollary 1.3]
) is that, for all sufficiently large
R
,

\displaystyle\limsup_{r\to\infty}~\mu(r+R,r)\leq R\ln R+3R\ln\ln R=(1+o(1))R\ln R.

(2)

This improves asymptotically the previous bound by Frankl–Rödl
[
FR85
]
which states that, for any fixed
R\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\mu(r+R,r)\leq(1+o_{R}(1))R(R+4)\ln r,\quad\mbox{as $r\to\infty$}.

For the case
R\geq\frac{r}{\log_{2}r}
, Sidorenko
[
Sid97
]
(by analyzing the construction of Frankl–Rödl
[
FR85
]
in this regime) proved that

\displaystyle\mu(r+R,r)\leq(1+o(1))R\ln\binom{r+R}{R}.

(3)

Although Turán systems were actively studied, it seems that no general upper bounds on
t(r+R,r)
have been published in the intermediate regime
1\ll R\leq r/\log_{2}r
. This is the case we address in this note. In brief, we show that Sidorenko’s bound in (
3
) applies in the whole range as along as
R
is sufficiently large, while the asymptotic bound in (
2
) can be extended from constant
R
to any
R=o(\sqrt{r})
.

Theorem 2
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
r_{0}
such that the following statements hold for all
r,R
satisfying
R\geq r_{0}
.

(i)

It holds that
\mu(r+R,r)\leq(1+\varepsilon)R\ln\binom{r+R}{R}
.

(ii)

Suppose that
R\leq\sqrt{18r\ln r}
. Then

\displaystyle\mu(r+R,r)\leq\mathrm{e}^{18R^{2}/r}\cdot(1+\varepsilon)R\ln R.

2
Proofs

In this section, we prove Theorem
2
. We will use the following notation.
For an integer
n\geq 1
and a set
X
, we denote
[n]:=\{1,\dots,n\}
and
\binom{X}{n}:=\{Y\subseteq X:|Y|=n\}
.

Let us begin with the proof of Theorem
2

(i)
.

Proof of Theorem
2

(i)
.

Given
\varepsilon>0
, let
r_{0}
be sufficiently large. Take any
r,R
with
R\geq r_{0}
. By (
3
), we can assume that e.g.
R\leq\frac{r}{\ln r}
. Let
s\coloneqq r+R
. Define

\displaystyle N\coloneqq\left\lfloor r(r-1){s\choose R}/(2R)\right\rfloor\quad\text{and}\quad\ell\coloneqq\left\lfloor{s\choose R}/\ln\left({s\choose R}^{2}{N-s\choose R}\right)\right\rfloor.

In the rest of the proof, we repeat the construction of Frankl and Rödl
[
FR85
, Theorem 3]
, arguing that our choices of
N
and
\ell
give the stated bound.

Consider a random colouring
c:{[N]\choose r}\to[\ell]
. For each
s
-set
A\in{[N]\choose s}
we have a bad event that some colour is not present in
{A\choose r}
. Its probability
p
is at most
\ell(1-1/\ell)^{{s\choose r}}\leq\ell\mathrm{e}^{-{s\choose R}/\ell}
(note that
\binom{s}{r}=\binom{s}{s-r}=\binom{s}{R}
). Define the obvious dependency graph
D
on
{[N]\choose s}
where
A\sim B
if
|A\cap B|\geq r
(including loops). It is regular of degree
\Delta:=\sum_{i=r}^{s}{s\choose i}{N-s\choose s-i}
.

We want to apply the Lovász Local Lemma
[
EL75
]
(see also e.g.
[
AS16
, Corollary 5.1.2]
), so we have to check that
\mathrm{e}p\Delta<1
, for which it suffices to prove

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{{s\choose R}/\ell}>\mathrm{e}\ell\Delta.

(4)

First, we estimate
\Delta
. Consider the ratio of two consecutive terms:

\frac{{s\choose i+1}{N-s\choose s-i-1}}{{s\choose i}{N-s\choose s-i}}=\frac{(s-i)^{2}}{(i+1)(N-2s+i+1)}.

Since
3\leq R\leq s/2
and
N\geq{s\choose R}\geq{s\choose 3}
, this ratio is at most say
1/2
for every
i\in[r,s]
and thus we can bound

\displaystyle\Delta\leq 2{s\choose r}{N-s\choose s-r}=2{s\choose R}{N-s\choose R}.

(5)

Thus and by the choice of
\ell
we have

\mathrm{e}^{{s\choose r}/\ell}\geq{s\choose R}^{2}{N-s\choose R}\geq\mathrm{e}\cdot\frac{{s\choose R}}{2\ln{s\choose R}}\cdot 2{s\choose R}{N-s\choose R}\geq\mathrm{e}\ell\Delta,

as desired. (In fact, our definition of
\ell
comes from (
4
), given
N
.)

Thus the Lovász Local Lemma applies and there is a colouring
c
with no bad events, meaning that each colour gives a Turán
(N,s,r)
-system on
[N]
. Let
\mathcal{A}\subseteq{[N]\choose r}
be the
r
-graph formed by the least frequent colour. We have

\displaystyle|\mathcal{A}|\leq\frac{1}{\ell}{N\choose r}.

(6)

Now let
n:=mN
with integer
m\to\infty
. Our Turán
(n,s,r)
-system
\mathcal{B}
on
[n]
is made of a blowup of
\mathcal{A}
plus all
r
-sets that intersect at least one part in more than one vertex. We have

|\mathcal{B}|\leq m^{r}|\mathcal{A}|+N{m\choose 2}{mN-2\choose r-2}\leq m^{r}\cdot\frac{1}{\ell}{N\choose r}+\frac{r(r-1)}{2N}{mN\choose r}\leq{mN\choose r}f,

where
f:=\frac{1}{\ell}+\frac{r(r-1)}{2N}
. Since
f
does not depend on
m
, it gives an upper bound on
t(r+R,r)
and thus
f\cdot{s\choose R}\geq\mu(s,r)
. Note that, rather roughly, we have
N\leq{s\choose R}^{2}
and
\ln N\leq 2R\ln s
. Therefore,

\ell\geq\frac{{s\choose R}}{2\ln{s\choose R}+R\ln N}-1

can be forced to be arbitrarily large if
r_{0}
was sufficiently large.
Thus the rounding in the definition of
\ell
gives only a multiplicative term that is arbitrarily close to
1

and we have

\displaystyle\binom{s}{R}\,f

\displaystyle\leq(1+\varepsilon/4)\left(2\ln\binom{s}{R}+R\ln N+\frac{r(r-1){s\choose R}}{2N}\right)

\displaystyle\leq(1+\varepsilon/2)\left(2\ln\binom{s}{R}+R\left(2\ln r+\ln\binom{s}{R}\right)+R\right)\leq(1+\varepsilon)R\ln\binom{s}{R},

proving Theorem
2

(i)

(In fact, our choice of
N
was given by the fact that
\ln x+c/x
with fixed
c>0
is minimized on
(\mathrm{e},\infty)
for
x=c
.)

Next, we prove Theorem
2

(ii)
.
Before doing so, let us present some preliminary results.

The following lemma is derived from the proof of
[
Pik24
, Lemma 2.3]
.

Lemma 3

(
[
Pik24
]
)
.

For all integers
r,R\geq 1
,
k\in[R,r-1]
, and a real
c\in\left[0,\binom{k}{R}\right]
, it holds that

\displaystyle\mu(r+R,r)

\displaystyle\leq\binom{r+R}{R}\left(\frac{c}{\binom{k}{R}}+\frac{\mu(r-k+R,r-k)}{\mathrm{e}^{c}\cdot\binom{r-k+R}{R}}\right).

(7)

Sketch of Proof.

Let
S
be a random subset of
\binom{[n]}{k-R}
where each
(k-R)
-set is included into
S
with probability
p:=c/\binom{k}{R}
. Let
S^{*}\subseteq\binom{[n]}{r}
consist of those
r
-sets
\{x_{1}<\dots<x_{r}\}
such that
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{k-R}\}\in S
, that is, we include an
r
-set into
S^{*}
if its initial
(k-R)
-segment is in
S
. Let
T\subseteq\binom{[n]}{k}
consist of those
k
-sets
X
such that
\binom{X}{k-R}\cap S=\emptyset
, that is,
X
is not hit by any set in
S
. For each
y\in[n]
, take
a minimum Turán
(n-y,r-k+R,r-k)
-system
F_{y}
on
\{y+1,\dots,n\}
. Let
T^{*}\subseteq\binom{[n]}{r}
be be union over
Y\in T
of the
r
-graphs

\{Y\cup Z:Z\in F_{\max Y}\}
. Informally speaking, we extend every
Y\in T
by a minimum Turán system to the right of
Y
.

It is easy to check that
G:=S^{*}\cup T^{*}
is a Turán
(n,r+R,r)
-system, regardless of the choice of
S
. By taking
S
such that
|G|
is at most its expected value, it is routine to see that

\displaystyle T(n,r+R,r)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\mathbbm{E}}|S^{*}|+{\mathbbm{E}}|T^{*}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p{n\choose r}+\sum_{y=k}^{n}\left(1-p\right)^{{k\choose R}}{y-1\choose k-1}\cdot T(n-y,r-k+R,r-k)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{c}{{k\choose R}}+\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-c}}{{r-k+R\choose R}}\mu(r-k+R,r-k)\right){n\choose r},

giving the required.

Fact 4
.

For any integers
r_{1}\geq r_{2}>R
, we have

\displaystyle\binom{r_{1}}{R}/\binom{r_{2}}{R}

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=0}^{R-1}\frac{r_{1}-i}{r_{2}-i}\leq\left(\frac{r_{1}-R}{r_{2}-R}\right)^{R}.

Fact 5
.

Let
r\geq 1,R\geq 1
be integers and
\delta
be a real number satisfying
18R^{2}/r\leq\delta\leq R
.
Let
k\coloneqq\left\lceil\frac{Rr}{R+\delta}\right\rceil+R
.
Then

\displaystyle k\leq r-1,\quad\frac{r}{k-R}\leq 1+\frac{\delta}{R},\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{r}{r-k}\leq\frac{3R}{\delta}.

Proof of Fact
5
.

Since
\delta\geq\frac{18R^{2}}{r}
, straightforward calculations show that

\displaystyle\frac{\delta r}{R+\delta}-R-2

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta r-(R+\delta)(R+2)}{R+\delta}\geq\frac{18R^{2}-(R+R)(R+2)}{R+\delta}\geq 0\quad\text{and}\quad

\displaystyle\frac{Rr}{R+\delta/2}-\frac{Rr}{R+\delta}

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta rR}{(R+\delta)(2R+\delta)}\geq\frac{18R^{3}}{(R+R)(2R+R)}\geq R+1.

It follows that

\displaystyle k

\displaystyle\leq\frac{Rr}{R+\delta}+1+R=r-1-\left(\frac{\delta r}{R+\delta}-R-2\right)\leq r-1,

\displaystyle\frac{r}{k-R}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{r}{\frac{Rr}{R+\delta}+R-R}=\frac{R+\delta}{R}=1+\frac{\delta}{R},\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle\frac{r}{r-k}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{r}{r-\frac{Rr}{R+\delta}-1-R}\leq\frac{r}{r-\frac{Rr}{R+\delta/2}}=\frac{2R+\delta}{\delta}\leq\frac{3R}{\delta},

which proves Fact
5
.

We are now ready to present the proof of Theorem
2

(ii)
.

Proof of Theorem
2

(ii)
.

Given
\varepsilon>0
, choose a sufficiently small real
\varepsilon_{1}>0
and then a sufficiently large integer
r_{0}
.
Take any integers
r,R
such that
R\geq r_{0}
and
R\leq\sqrt{18r\ln r}
.

Case 1.
Suppose that
R\geq\ln r
.

We define

\displaystyle\delta\coloneqq\max\left\{\varepsilon_{1},~\frac{18R^{2}}{r}\right\},\quad k\coloneqq\left\lceil\frac{Rr}{R+\delta}\right\rceil+R,\quad\text{and}\quad c\coloneqq R\ln\left(\frac{3R}{\delta}\right)+\ln(2R^{3}).

Clearly,
c\leq\binom{k}{R}
. Since
R
is large and
\ln r\leq R
, it follows from
Theorem
2

(i)
that, for example,

\displaystyle\mu(r-k+R,r-k)

\displaystyle\leq 2\,R\ln\binom{r-k+R}{R}\leq 2\,R^{2}\ln r\leq 2R^{3}.

Combining this with Lemma
3
, Facts
4
and
5
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mu(r+R,r)

\displaystyle\leq\binom{r+R}{R}\left(\frac{c}{\binom{k}{R}}+\frac{2R^{3}}{\mathrm{e}^{c}\cdot\binom{r-k+R}{R}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{r}{k-R}\right)^{R}\cdot c+\left(\frac{r}{r-k}\right)^{R}\cdot\frac{2R^{3}}{\mathrm{e}^{c}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\frac{\delta}{R}\right)^{R}\cdot c+\left(\frac{3R}{\delta}\right)^{R}\cdot\frac{2R^{3}}{\mathrm{e}^{c}}\leq\mathrm{e}^{\delta}\cdot c+\mathrm{e}^{R\ln\left(\frac{3R}{\delta}\right)-c}\cdot 2R^{3}\leq\mathrm{e}^{\delta}\cdot c+1.

If
\frac{18R^{2}}{r}\leq\varepsilon_{1}
, that is,
R\leq\sqrt{\varepsilon_{1}r/18}
, then

\displaystyle\mu(r+R,r)

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{e}^{\varepsilon_{1}}\cdot c+1\leq(1+2\varepsilon_{1})\left(R\ln\left(\frac{3R}{\varepsilon_{1}}\right)+\ln(2R^{3})\right)+1

\displaystyle\leq(1+2\varepsilon_{1})\left(R\left(\ln R+\ln\left(\frac{3}{\varepsilon_{1}}\right)\right)+3\ln R+\ln 2\right)+1

\displaystyle\leq(1+\varepsilon)R\ln R,

as desired.

If
\frac{18R^{2}}{r}>\varepsilon_{1}
, that is,
r\leq\frac{18R^{2}}{\varepsilon_{1}}
, then

\displaystyle\mu(r+R,r)

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{e}^{18R^{2}/r}\cdot c+1\leq\mathrm{e}^{18R^{2}/r}\left(R\ln\left(\frac{r}{6R}\right)+\ln(2R^{3})\right)+1

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{e}^{18R^{2}/r}\left(R\ln\left(\frac{3R}{\varepsilon_{1}}\right)+\ln(2R^{3})\right)+1

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{e}^{18R^{2}/r}(1+\varepsilon)R\ln R,

also as desired.

Case 2.
Suppose that
R<\ln r
.

Let
r_{1}\coloneqq r
and, inductively for
i=1,2,\dots
, define

\displaystyle k_{i}\coloneqq\left\lceil\frac{Rr_{i}}{R+\varepsilon_{1}}\right\rceil+R,\quad\text{and}\quad r_{i+1}\coloneqq r_{i}-k_{i};

if
r_{i+1}<18R^{2}/\varepsilon_{1}
then
let
t:=i
and stop. Since
r_{i}
decreases each time, this process terminates.

We prove by backward induction on
i\in[t]
that

\displaystyle\mu(r_{i}+R,r_{i})\leq(1+\varepsilon)R\ln R.

(8)

First, consider the base case
i=t
.
Note that, for
i\leq t
, we have by
r_{i}\geq 18R^{2}/\varepsilon_{1}
that

\displaystyle r_{i+1}=r_{i}-k_{i}\geq r_{i}-\frac{Rr_{i}}{R+\varepsilon_{1}}-1-R\geq\frac{\varepsilon_{1}r_{i}}{R+\varepsilon_{1}}-(R+1)\geq\frac{\varepsilon_{1}r_{i}}{2(R+\varepsilon_{1})}.

In particular this holds for
i=t
, giving that
r_{t}\leq\frac{18R^{2}}{\varepsilon_{1}}\cdot\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{2(R+\varepsilon_{1})}
, which is rather roughly at most
e^{R}
. Thus
R\geq\ln r_{t}
and the desired conclusion follows by Case 1.

Now consider the inductive step for some
i\in[t-1]
.
Let

\displaystyle c\coloneqq R\ln\left(3R/\varepsilon_{1}\right)+\ln(2R\ln R)\leq\binom{k_{i}}{R}.

It follows from Lemma
3
, Facts
4
and
5
(note that
\varepsilon_{1}\geq 18R^{2}/r_{t}\geq 18R^{2}/r_{i}
), and the inductive hypothesis that

\displaystyle\mu(r_{i}+R,r_{i})

\displaystyle\leq\binom{r_{i}+R}{R}\left(\frac{c}{\binom{k_{i}}{R}}+\frac{\mu(r_{i+1}+R,r_{i+1})}{\mathrm{e}^{c}\cdot\binom{r_{i+1}+R}{R}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{r_{i}}{k_{i}-R}\right)^{R}\cdot c+\left(\frac{r_{i}}{r_{i+1}}\right)^{R}\cdot\frac{(1+\varepsilon)R\ln R}{\mathrm{e}^{c}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{R}\right)^{R}\cdot c+\left(\frac{3R}{\varepsilon_{1}}\right)^{R}\cdot\frac{(1+\varepsilon)R\ln R}{\mathrm{e}^{c}}

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{e}^{\varepsilon_{1}}\cdot c+\mathrm{e}^{R\ln\left(\frac{3R}{\varepsilon_{1}}\right)-c}\cdot 2R\ln R

\displaystyle\leq(1+2\varepsilon_{1})\left(R\ln\left(\frac{3R}{\varepsilon_{1}}\right)+\ln(2R\ln R)\right)+1\leq(1+\varepsilon)R\ln R,

as desired.

This completes the proof of Theorem
2
.

Remark 6
.

We did not optimise the bound in Theorem
2

(ii)
when
R=\Omega(\sqrt{r})
, since our main aim was to extend the
inequality
\mu(r+R,r)\leq(1+o(1))R\ln R
for constant
R
from
[
Pik24
]

to as large as possible range of functions
R(r)
.

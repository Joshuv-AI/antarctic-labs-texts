Streptomyces chodovensis sp. nov.: a novel candidate for plant growth-promoting actinomycete from bare soil developing on Miocene lacustrine sediment of the Sokolov Coal Basin, Czech Republic
Authors: Alper Dede, Vaclav Krištůfek, Lucie Kotrbová, Kateřina Petříčková, Terézia Horváthová, Christopher M Field, Sarah Kirstein, Meina Neumann-Schaal, Imen Nouioui, Alica Chroňáková
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractA novel actinomycete, designated as BCCO 10_0861T, was isolated from overburden Miocene clay sediments undergoing primary succession in Northwest Czechia and characterized by the polyphasic approach. According to 16S rRNA gene sequence similarity, the strain was closely related to Streptomyces turgidiscabies DSM 41838T (99.00%), Streptomyces reticuliscabiei DSM 41804T (98.93%), Streptomyces lacrimifluminis DSM 111125T (98.15%), and Streptomyces geranii JCM 32177T (98.08%). Morphological and chemotaxonomic characteristics supported the assignment to the genus Streptomyces. Diagnostic diamino acid in the cell-wall peptidoglycan was LL-diaminopimelic acid and the dominant menaquinone was MK-9(H6). The major cellular fatty acids (> 10%) were anteiso-C15:0, iso-C16:0, C16:1 ω7c, and C16:0. The polar lipid profile comprised of aminophospholipids, diphosphatidylglycerol, glycophospholipids, phosphatidylethanolamine, phosphatidylinositol, and three unidentified phospholipids. The whole cell sugars were galactose, glucose, mannose, ribose, and traces of rhamnose. The genome size of the strain BCCO 10_0861T was 10.6 Mb with G+C content of 70.05%. Bioinformatic analysis suggested several biosynthetic gene clusters (BGCs) with medium-to-high similarity confidence to already known BGCs, having ethylenediaminesuccinic acid hydroxyarginine (EDHA), humidimycin, inthomycin B, and triascin C unique to BCCO 10_0861T. Similarly, predicted plant-growth promoting trait (PGPT) genes differed between genomes, when the one of BCCO 10_0861T showed the highest number of unique PGPT genes (2,255). In addition, no association with potato pathogenicity was predicted in the genome of BCCO 10_0861T. The digital DNA-DNA hybridization (ranged from 34.7 to 37.5%), average nucleotide identity values (ranged from 86.26 to 87.24%) and phenotype distinguish strain BCCO 10_0861T from closely related strains. Based on the polyphasic approach and genomic content, the strain represents a novel species of the genus Streptomyces, for which we propose the name Streptomyces chodovensis sp. nov. for BCCO 10_0861T (= DSM 117345T).Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1007/s10482-026-02440-5.Keywords: Actinobacteria, Plant growth-promoting traits, Polyphasic taxonomy, Post-mining sites, Streptomyces

Introduction

Streptomycetes are aerobic, Gram-stain-positive actinobacteria with a high DNA G+C content, able to form branched substrate and aerial mycelia that differentiate into chains of few to many spores (Kämpfer et al. 2014). In addition, they are characterized to have LL-diaminopimelic acid (LL-DAP) and no diagnostic sugars in whole-cell hydrolysates (Waksman and Henrici 1943; Skerman et al. 1980; Anderson and Wellington 2001). As a member of Terrabacteria clade, streptomycetes are associated with an early colonization of land that occurred between 2.5 to 3.2 billion years ago (Battistuzzi et al. 2004), evolving in tight connection with plants and plant-derived organic debris (Chater 2016; Viaene et al. 2016; Chevrette et al. 2019). Many of them are considered plant growth-promoting bacteria abundant in the rhizosphere (Dede et al. 2020; Nicolle et al. 2024) or endophytic compartments (Dede and Güven 2023), while only a few species act as plant pathogens, like Streptomyces acidiscabies, Streptomyces europaescabiei, Streptomyces turgidiscabies, and Streptomyces scabies (Gutierrez et al. 2022). However, Streptomyces spp. inhabit widespread habitats in a global ecosystem: soil, freshwater, marine, and air as free-living or in association with plants, invertebrates, and mammals (Li et al. 2019).

Streptomyces spp. form the largest prokaryotic genus (Waksman and Henrici 1943), 811 validly published species with correct names (LPSN website https://lpsn.dsmz.de/genus/streptomyces, accessed June 10th, 2026). Genetic diversity can be attributed to the enormous genome plasticity enabled by large linear chromosome (4.8–13.6 Mb), comprising central core genome encompassing essential genes and dispensable, fast-evolving ends (arms). These terminal regions are prone to gene exchange through lateral gene transfer and contribute to their adaptability to fluctuating environmental conditions and ability to colonize novel niches (Almeida et al. 2019). The interest in discovering and describing new species in this genus is not waning, but rather the opposite, as demonstrated by Komaki (Komaki 2023). This trend is undoubtedly due to recent progress in whole genome sequencing-based methods used in taxonomic classification (Nouioui et al. 2018), or genome-mining technologies as; antiSMASH, MiBiG, ARTS, or others (Wattam et al. 2017; Patz et al. 2021; Mungan et al. 2022; Blin et al. 2025; Zdouc et al. 2025), but also to their cultivability and the recent exploration of new environments, such as marine, underground, or host-associated habitats (Wang et al. 2022). However, the high plasticity of their genome (Zhou et al. 2012), which allows them a multitude of metabolic adaptations to the environmental changes (Li et al. 2019), including a very diverse secondary metabolism, is certainly another driving force behind this trend (Caicedo-Montoya et al. 2021; Otani et al. 2022; Nikolaidis et al. 2023; Jørgensen et al. 2024).

One of the unexplored ecosystems is of anthropogenic origin: bare soils developing after open cast mining. These soils provide a unique opportunity to study ecological succession and microbial colonization in environments drastically altered by human activity. Surface mining of lignite affects large areas as it exposes and brings to the surface the geological strata overlying the lignite seam (Kříbek et al. 1998). In Velká Podkrušnohorská spoil heap (Sokolov brown coal mining area, Czech Republic), many Miocene clays have been overburdened in this way, which have then been used for reclamation or left for primary succession (Helingerová et al. 2010). These clays (cypris) are highly specialized habitats that harbor for taxonomically distinct and diverse microorganisms, and can serve as reservoirs for yet unexplored microbial diversity (Elhottová et al. 2006; Chroňáková et al. 2010). Actinomycetes isolated from Miocene clays and different successional stages were the basis for the establishment of the Culture Collection of Actinomycetes at BCCO (www.actinomycetes.bcco.cz), where more than 600 isolates originate from this area. Using polyphasic approaches, we recently described three new species of the genus Lentzea (Lara et al. 2024).

Strain BCCO 10_0861T = DSM 117345T was initially isolated for bioprospecting purposes, subsequent phenotypic profiling revealed a distinct combination of aerial mycelium coloration and soluble pigment production that distinguished it from other isolates. Subsequent whole-genome sequencing and comprehensive comparative genomic analyses confirmed its distinct phylogenetic position. Consequently, based on robust polyphasic evidence, we propose a novel species represented by this single isolate. The strain was characterized in comparison with its closest phylogenetic relatives: Streptomyces geranii JCM 32177T, Streptomyces lacrimifluminis DSM 111125T, Streptomyces turgidiscabies DSM 41838T, and Streptomyces reticuliscabiei DSM 41804T. S. geranii is a plant endophyte (Li et al. 2018), while S. turgidiscabies, and S. reticuliscabiei are reported as plant pathogens causing raised or netted scab lesions on potato tuber (Miyajima et al. 1998; Bouchek-Mechiche et al. 2000). This study expands the taxonomic framework of Streptomyces by integrating genomic and phenotypic data from an anthropogenic habitat. Our findings highlight the adaptability of the genus and underscore the potential of disturbed ecosystems as reservoirs of microbial novelty.

Identification and phylogenetic analysis using 16S rRNA gene and whole genome sequences

Strain BCCO 10_0861T was isolated from soil at the top of reclaimed mine heaps in the early successional stage in the Sokolov brown coal mine district area in the Czech Republic (50.262 N, 12.731 E). The strain was isolated from bare soil developed on Miocene lacustrine clay (pH 7.8) deposited after brown coal mining in 2008 on Reasoner’s 2A (R2A) agar (HiMedia Laboratories Pvt. Ltd., Mumbai, India). Cultures were maintained on yeast extract–malt extract agar (International Streptomyces Project (ISP) 2 medium) (Shirling and Gottlieb 1966) and as cell suspensions in 15% (v/v) glycerol stocks at −76 °C for long-term preservation. Genomic DNA of BCCO 10_0861T was extracted from a three-day-old bacterial culture grown in Luria–Bertani (LB) broth at 28 °C (± 2 °C) with shaking at 150 rpm (Bertani 1951). The NucleoSpin Microbial DNA Kit (Macherey–Nagel) was used for DNA extraction, following the manufacturer’s instructions. The DNA quantity and quality were analyzed using a NanoDrop 2000 spectrophotometer and agarose gel electrophoresis. The almost complete 16S rRNA gene was amplified by PCR using the bacterial universal primers pA (5´-AGAGTTTGATCCTGGCTCAG-3´) and pH (5´-AAGGAGGTGATCCAGCCGCA-3´) (Edwards et al. 1989). The purified PCR products were bidirectionally sequenced in Seqme, s.r.o., Dobříš, Czech Republic. The sequences were assembled with BioEdit (Hall 1999). The 16S rRNA genes of the closest type species were retrieved from EzBioCloud database (Kim et al. 2012). According to EzBioCloud database (updated on April, 21st, 2025), 16S rRNA gene sequence comparisons showed that strain BCCO 10_0861T has a similarity of 99.00% to S. turgidiscabies DSM 41838T (14 bp difference), 98.93% to S. reticuliscabiei DSM 41804T (15 bp difference), 98.65% to S. humidus NBRC 12877T (19 bp difference), and 98.29% to S. asoensis NRRL B-16592T (24 bp difference), respectively.

The evolutionary history of BCCO 10_0861T was initially constructed using multiple tree-building algorithms to ensure topological robustness. Briefly, the sequence alignments were aligned with CLUSTALW on MEGA 11 (Edgar 2004; Tamura et al. 2021). A Maximum-Likelihood (ML) tree was inferred based on the Tamura-Nei substitution model (Tamura and Nei 1993). Parallel evolutionary distance evaluations and tree constructions were executed via the Neighbor-Joining (NJ) method using Jukes–Cantor evolutionary distances (Jukes and Cantor 1969). The percentage of replicate trees in which the associated taxa clustered together was calculated in the bootstrap test (1,000 replicates) (Felsenstein 1985).

The ML topology (Fig. 1A) and the corresponding NJ tree (Supplementary Fig 1) revealed that strain BCCO 10_0861T forms a highly stable, distinct monophyletic lineage clusters alongside well-defined reference strains. Both algorithms independently confirmed that the phylogenetically closest type strains to the BCCO 10_0861T are S. geranii JCM 32177T, S. lacrimifluminis DSM 111125T, S. turgidiscabies DSM 41838T, and S. reticuliscabiei DSM 41804T, respectively. Node stability was strongly supported by high bootstrap values (71–99%) and critical nodes shared across both the ML and NJ analyses were marked with the asterisk (*), verifying the consistency of the sequence-based datasets.

Genome features

Genome sequencing was performed at the Institute of Microbiology (ETH Zurich) on a MinION with an R10.4.1 flow cell and base called with the model dna_r10.4.1_e8.2_260bps_hac@v3.5.2. In total, there were 96,726 reads with a median length of 1,377 bp and median quality score of 12.0, summing to 244.6 Mbp. Reads were filtered for a minimum length of 1,000 bp with Filtlong (v0.2.1; https://github.com/rrwick/Filtlong). The genome was assembled with Flye (v2.9; https://doi.org/10.1038/s41587-019-0072-8) using standard parameters, resulting in a genome size of 10,631,911 base pairs, assembled in 11 contigs, with an N50 of 182.7 Kb (Table 1). The reads and assembly were deposited at the ENA under the study accession ERP155806, sample accession ERS17732811, reads accession ERR12495476, and assembly accession ERZ22297350. The quality of the reads was evaluated using fastQC (Andrews 2010), and sequences were trimmed using Trimmomatic version 0.36 (Bolger et al. 2014). Clean reads were assembled using SPAdes version 3.15.3 (Bankevich et al. 2012) and Velvet version 1.2.10 (Zerbino 2010) on the KBase platform (Arkin et al. 2018). The assembly of the genome was performed in PATRIC web-based tool (with the annotation option). Digital DNA-DNA hybridization (dDDH) was performed with TYGS genome server (Meier-Kolthoff and Göker 2019; Meier-Kolthoff et al. 2022). DNA G+C content was determined with PATRIC database (Olson et al. 2023). ANIb calculations were obtained from JSpeciesWS web-based tool. 16S rDNA sequences were retrieved from the genome with ContEst16S (Lee et al. 2017). The results from MinION and 16S rDNA sequence obtained by Sanger sequencing were compared, and no differences were observed.

BCCO 10_0861T genome contained 11,824 protein-encoding genes (CDSs) and had a G+C content of 70.05% (Table 1). To determine its genomic taxonomic position, whole-genome assembly of strain BCCO 10_0861T was compared with 18 Streptomyces spp. type strains identified via the Type (Strain) Genome Server (TYGS) using Thermobispora bispora DSM 43833T as an outgroup. Reference genome sequences were obtained from the NCBI genome database (www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/genome/), and the TYGS genome databases (Meier-Kolthoff and Göker 2019; Meier-Kolthoff et al. 2022). The Genome BLAST Distance Phylogeny (GBDP) method based on the pairwise intergenomic distances performed on TYGS database confirmed that the closest organisms to strain BCCO 10_0861T are S. lacrimifluminis DSM 111125T, S. reticuliscabiei DSM 41804T, S. turgidiscabies DSM 41838T and S. geranii JCM 32177T, respectively (Fig. 1B).

Genome-wide comparisons using TYGS and JSpeciesWS confirmed that strain BCCO 10_0861T is distinct from its closest phylogenetic neighbors. The digital DNA-DNA hybridization (dDDH) values (d4) between BCCO 10_0861T and closest Streptomyces species were significantly below the 70% threshold for species delimitation, ranging from 34.7 to 37.5%. Specifically, the highest similarity was observed with S. lacrimifluminis (37.5% dDDH; 0.04 G+C difference), followed by S. reticuliscabiei (36.7%), S. turgidiscabies (36.6%), and S. geranii (34.7%). Furthermore, Average Nucleotide Identity (ANIb) values across all analyzed strains ranged from 86.26 to 87.24%, below the 95%–96% species boundary (Supplementary Tables 1 and 2). These results collectively support the classification of BCCO 10_0861T as a novel species within the genus Streptomyces.

Strain ecology, morphological, biochemical, and physiological characteristics

The strain was deposited at the Culture Collection of Actinomycetes of the Biology Centre Collection of Organisms https://actinomycetes.bcco.cz/) under BCCO 10_0861T and at Leibniz Institute DSMZ – German Collection of Microorganisms and Cell Cultures GmbH (www.dsmz.de/) under DSM 117345T. The close phylogenetic relative of strain BCCO 10_0861T, S. lacrimifluminis DSM 111125T, S. turgidiscabies DSM 41838T, and S. reticuliscabiei DSM 41804T, were obtained from the DSMZ collection (https://www.dsmz.de/collection/catalogue/microorganisms/catalogue) and included in the phenotypic analyses. S. geranii JCM 32177T was not included in any of the laboratory experiments conducted in this study due to Nagoya protocol restrictions.

Cultural characteristics were observed after growth at 28 °C for 2 weeks, using ISP2, ISP3, ISP4, ISP6 and ISP7 agar, and nutrient agar (Waksman 1940; Shirling and Gottlieb 1966). Colony colors were identified as described by Shirling and Gottlieb 1966. BCCO 10_0861T with aerial spore mass of white color (White oyster, Wb) was developed well on ISP2, ISP4 media, and moderately on ISP3. Vegetative mycelium is pink-orange-brown and soluble pink-orange pigment is produced on ISP2, ISP3, ISP4 and ISP7 media. Cell morphology was examined using scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and transmission electron microscopy (TEM) following 21 days of incubation at 28 °C using ISP2 medium. BCCO 10_0861T develops sporophores type rectus flexibilis, spores having a size of 1.464 µm × 0.682 µm (length x width, on average) (Fig. 2). Colony differences between BCCO 10_0861T and strains representing the closest relative species are provided in Supplementary Table 3 and visualized on the ISP6 and ISP7 media in Supplementary Fig 2.

Strain BCCO 10_0861T exhibits a mesophilic growth range of 4–30 °C, neutrophilic pH tolerance (6–8), and moderate halotolerance (0–2% NaCl) (Table 2). To ensure stability across the pH 4.0–12.0 range, a strategic multi-buffer system was implemented. Citrate–phosphate (pH 4–6), phosphate (pH 6–8), and carbonate-bicarbonate (pH 9–12) solutions were used to prevent unintended pH shifts (pH was measured in intervals of 1) (Beynon and Easterby 2004) and at temperatures of 4, 10, 20, 28, 30, 37, 45, and 55 °C with ISP2 medium. Additionally, sodium chloride (NaCl) tolerance was examined from 0 to 10% (in intervals of 1) using ISP2 medium. Other physiological characteristics, including the utilization of sole carbon sources were performed according to ISP project guidelines (Shirling and Gottlieb 1966; Wink 1990). Carbon utilization tests showed efficient assimilation of D-mannose, D-xylose, and glycerol (+ +), while D-ribose, cellulose, and D-fructose were not utilized (Table 2).

Enzymatic and other metabolic activities were performed with API ZYM, API 20NE and API 50CH strips (bioMérieux) following the instructions of the manufacturer. BCCO 10_0861T shows distinct biochemical profiles compared to S. lacrimifluminis DSM 111125T, S. reticuliscabiei DSM 41804T, and S. turgidiscabies DSM 41838T, based on API ZYM, API 20 NE, and API 50CH tests. Compared to S. lacrimifluminis, BCCO 10_0861T shows weaker α-mannosidase and α-fucosidase activity (weak vs. positive) in API ZYM, positive nitrate reduction and negative urease in API 20 NE, and in API 50CH, it is negative for D-arabinose, positive for raffinose and 5-keto-gluconate, with variable results for galactose, amygdalin, arbutin, salicin, and esculin (V1 vs. V2). Against S. reticuliscabiei, BCCO 10_0861T displays weaker α-chymotrypsin, β-glucuronidase, and α-mannosidase in API ZYM, negative urease, positive adipate, citrate, and maltose, assimilation in API 20 NE, and in API 50CH, positive salicin, negative trehalose, positive amygdalin, with differences in arbutin, D-arabinose, esculin, and 2-keto-gluconate. Versus S. turgidiscabies, BCCO 10_0861T has weaker β-glucuronidase and α-mannosidase in API ZYM, positive mannose, mannitol, citrate, and adipate assimilation in API 20 NE, and in API 50CH, positive salicin and lactose, negative maltose, positive amygdalin, and variable arbutin, esculin, and 2-keto-gluconate (full lists are given in Supplementary Table 4).

Inference of biosynthetic potential, antibiotic resistance determinants and mobile genetic elements

Biosynthetic gene clusters (BGCs) were monitored using antiSMASH (Blin et al. 2025). Antimicrobial resistance genes were acquired from PATRIC’s ‘comparative genome analysis’. Antimicrobial resistance genes were retrieved from PATRIC (Wattam et al. 2017; Olson et al. 2023) and ResFinder CGE v.4.1 (Bortolaia et al. 2020), and mobile genetic elements (MGE) were evaluated using MGEfinder v.1.0.3 (Durrant et al. 2020) with 80% sequence identity (Dede et al. 2025).

Some of the BGCs usually found in majority of streptomycetes, representing the core metabolome (Vicente et al. 2018), were absent in strain BCCO 10_0861T: desferrioxamine B/E, diisonitrile antibiotic SF2768 and ε-Poly-L-lysine BGCs while they were identified in all other strains. BGCs present only in the genome of BCCO 10_0861T were humidimycin, inthomycin B, triacsin C, and ethylenediaminesuccinic acid hydroxyarginine (EDHA), while scabichelin BGC was found in both plant-pathogenic species and phenazine in S. geranii JCM 32177 T together with BCCO 10_0861T (Fig. 3 and Table 3). Both phenazines and scabichelin are considered specialized metabolites with different effects on plants: they can act as biocontrol agents or virulence factors and thus may have positive or negative effects on plants (Biessy and Filion 2018). Moreover, thaxtomin A BGC was only found in S. turgidiscabies DSM 41838 T. In summary, even the presence of BGC for putative virulence factors was recognized in the genome of BCCO 10_0861T, but only their concomitant and synergistic action may be associated with the plant virulence and pathogenicity (Polturak and Osbourni 2021). The key differences in AMR mechanisms among the BCCO 10_0861T and closest relatives is the lack of aminoglycoside antibiotic inactivation enzyme (AAC(2')-I), and antibiotic target replacement protein 3-hydroxyacyl-thioester dehydratase (HtdX). In addition, BCCO 10_0861T lack tetracycline antibiotic target protection (Tet) in comparison with S. geranii JCM 32177T, S. reticuliscabiei DSM 41804T and S. turgidiscabies DSM 41838T. BCCO 10_0861T shares multiple transferable resistance A (MtrA) multiple transferable resistance B (MtrB), oxidative stress response regulator (OxyR) as regulators and protein altering cell wall structure (VanJ) with all other strains except S. geranii (Supplementary Table 6). MGE analysis in five Streptomyces species reveals that BCCO 10_0861T did not have any detectable MGEs. Alternatively, S. geranii, S. reticuliscabiei, S. lacrimifluminis, and S. turgidiscabies contain various insertion sequences (IS) such as ISSgr2, ISSdi1, ISMysp4, ISStac1, ISBfu1, IS469, ISSam1, ISStu2, IS112, and ISSav6, with S. reticuliscabiei being the sole one to carry a composite transposon (cn_4078_ISBfu1) (Supplementary Table 6).

Pan-genome analysis revealed that BCCO 10_0861T possesses the highest number of orthologous genes (10,827 according to the Database of Clusters of Orthologous Genes, COGs), followed by S. turgidiscabies with 10,318, S. reticuliscabiei with 9,632, S. geranii with 9,258, and S. lacrimifluminis with 8,587. These strains share 4,190 identical genes, while BCCO 10_0861T exhibits 4,287 unique genes, as shown in Supplementary Table 7 with an accompanying Venn diagram.

Chemotaxonomic characterization

Strain BCCO 10_0861T was incubated in ISP2 medium for chemotaxonomic analysis: a preculture grown in 20 mL for 48 h (150 rpm, 28 °C) served as inoculum for 100 mL main culture, which was grown for 48 h at 150 rpm and 28 °C. Biomass was collected by centrifugation (1,000 × g for 10 min) and freeze-dried (L10-55 PRO, GREGOR Instruments, s.r.o., Říčany, Czech Republic) before further usage (Lara et al. 2024).

Freeze-dried biomass was used for the detection of whole-cell sugar composition (Lechevalier and Lechevalier 1970), polar lipids (Minnikin et al. 1984), and diaminopimelic acid (Staneck and Roberts 1974) detected by thin-layer chromatography (TLC). The cellular fatty acid analysis was performed by gas chromatography–flame ionization detector (GC-FID) followed by GC-mass spectrometry (GC-MS) run on an Agilent GC–MS 7000D instrument as described in Sasser 2001; Vieira et al. 2021. High-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) coupled with a diode array detector (DAD) and a high-resolution mass spectrometer was used for isoprenoid quinone detection (Schumann et al. 2021).

Whole cell hydrolysates showed the presence of LL-DAP as the diagnostic diamino acid for the peptidoglycan, which agreed with the selected strains included in the comparison. The composition of sugars in whole cell hydrolysates and phospholipid constituents differed significantly between strain BCCO 10_0861T and the most related strains (Table 2). In BCCO 10_0861T galactose, glucose, mannose, ribose, and traces of rhamnose were present as whole cell sugars, while arabinose and xylose were additionally present in S. reticuliscabiei DSM 41804T and S. turgidiscabies DSM 41838T. On the other hand, only galactose or galactose together with ribose were present in whole cell hydrolysates in S. lacrimifluminis DSM 111125T and S. geranii JCM 32177T, respectively. Chemotaxonomic analysis of strain BCCO 10_0861T revealed a menaquinone profile consisting of MK-9 (H6) (57.6%), MK-9 (H8) (19.6%), MK-9 (H4) (14.0%), and MK-9 (H2) (8.8%), being the most similar to the menaquinone composition of S. reticuliscabiei DSM 41804T. Contrastingly, MK-9 (H8) was of elevated abundance in strains S. lacrimifluminis DSM 111125T and S. geranii JCM 32177T, reaching 48.1% and 38.4%, respectively.

Polar lipid pattern composed of aminophospholipid (APL), diphosphatidylglycerol (DPG), glycolipids (GPL), phosphatidylethanolamine (PE), phosphatidylinositol (PI), and phospholipids (PL) (Supplementary Fig 3). Major cellular fatty acids (> 5%) included C16:0 (19.5%), anteiso-C15:0 (15.6%), iso-C16:0 (15.2%), and C16:1 ω7c (14.3%), iso-C15:0 (8.2%), and anteiso-C17:0 (6.4%) (Table 2). The composition of fatty acids of strain S. lacrimifluminis DSM 111125T, S. reticuliscabiei DSM 41804T and S. turgidiscabies DSM 41838T differed from those of BCCO 10_0861T only by the relative abundance, while it significantly differed from strain S. geranii JCM 32177T, in which iso-C15:0 and C16:1 ω7c were completely absent. More details on the fatty acid compositions are given in Supplementary Table 8. The chemotaxonomic features of the strain were similar to its close neighbors S. lacrimifluminis DSM 111125 T, S. reticuliscabiei DSM 41804 T, and S. turgidiscabies DSM 41838 T (Table 2).

Antimicrobial activities

Streptomyces sp. strains were inoculated on Mueller–Hinton agar (Sigma-Aldrich, Co., St. Louis, MO), four days in advance and cultivated at 28 °C. The overnight cultures (16 h) of indicator strains (37 °C) were added to streptomycetes in a T-shape and co-cultivated at 28 °C to next day for reading the results (Herbrík et al. 2020). Strain BCCO 10_0861T showed different pattern of inhibitory activities compared to other species. It weakly inhibited the growth of Bacillus subtilis CCM 1718T and Moraxella catarrhalis (clinical isolate), inhibited Candida albicans CCM 8186T, however did not affect the growth of Staphylococcus aureus DSM 346T, Escherichia coli DSM 682T, Klebsiella pneumoniae DSM 681T, and Pseudomonas aeruginosa DSM 50071T (Table 4).

Plant growth promoting traits

Plant growth promoting traits were detected with PlaBA-db (Patz et al. 2021). Examination of plant growth-promoting traits (PGPT)-associated genes in the genomes indicated that BCCO 10_0861T has the most abundant gene count at 4,811, followed by S. geranii with 4,289, S. turgidiscabies with 4,401, S. reticuliscabiei with 4,319, and S. lacrimifluminis with 3,844. They collectively share 885 identical PGPT-associated genes, yet BCCO 10_0861T contains 2,255 distinct genes compared to the others, as presented in Table 1 and Supplementary Table 9 with a corresponding Venn diagram.

To investigate plant growth-promoting traits, experiments were conducted with a minimum of two technical replicates (Dede and Güven 2023). Actinomycetes in their active growth phase were cultured on Pikovskaya agar (PA) (Pikovskaya 1948) to assess phosphate solubilization (PS). For indole-3-acetic acid (IAA) production, endophytic bacteria were inoculated in peptone water medium and incubated at 28 ± 2 °C for 28 days on a rotary shaker at 120 rpm (Gordon and Weber 1951). The culture was then centrifuged at 720 g for 30 min, and 0.5 mL of the supernatant was mixed with 1 mL of Salkowski reagent. After 30 min in the dark at room temperature, a pink coloration indicated positive IAA production. Additionally, IAA production was measured spectrophotometrically at 530 nm with a Biotek Epoch2 microplate reader. Tryptophan-dependent IAA production was evaluated by supplementing peptone water with 0.2% DL-tryptophan (Merck) and incubating at 28 ± 2 °C for 7 days (Dede et al. 2020). Hydrocyanic acid (HCN) production was assessed on modified ISP2 agar supplemented with glycine (4.4 g/L) under anaerobic condition (Lorck 1948; Dede and Güven 2024). Data are provided in Supplementary Table 10. For siderophore production, actinomycetes were grown on modified chrome azurol S (CAS) agar (Schwyn and Neilands 1987) (blue dye and NA mixture) consisting of a blue dye and nutrient agar mixture, and incubated at 28 ± 2 °C for 3 days, following the protocol by Dede et al. (2020).

Siderophore production assays confirmed the presence of desferrioxamine-type siderophores in S. lacrimifluminis, S. reticuliscabiei, and S. turgidiscabies (Table 3). However, strain BCCO 10_0861T did not produce siderophores in the assay, despite genomic predictions suggesting the potential for scabichelin-type siderophore production, which was not experimentally validated. In contrast, strain BCCO 10_0861T demonstrated the ability to produce soluble proteases, IAA, and solubilized phosphates from tricalcium phosphate. Notably, strain BCCO 10_0861T was the only strain that produced hydrocyanic acid. The findings are presented in Table 5.

Description of Streptomyces chodovensis sp. nov.

Streptomyces chodovensis (cho.do.ven.sis, L. masc. adj. chodovensis, of Chodov, named after the place of origin of type strain, the town Chodov, Czech Republic).

Gram-positive bacterium. Substrate mycelium is pink to brown on ISP2, ISP3, ISP4 and ISP7. Dense, white to gray aerial mycelium is produced on ISP2, ISP4 and ISP7. Produces pink-orange soluble pigment on ISP2, ISP3, ISP4 and ISP7. Melanin pigment is not produced on either ISP6 or ISP7. Sporophores are of rectus flexibilis shape, spores are rod-shaped 1.5 µm long and 0.7 µm wide (median values). Grows at 4–30 °C with optimal growth at 28 °C. Grows at pH 6–8. The strain can easily utilize glycerol, D-mannose, and D-xylose; can utilize D-glucose, D-mannitol, I-inositol, L-rhamnose, and sucrose; shows weak utilization of L-arabinose; and cannot utilize cellulose, D-fructose, or D-ribose.

The strain tested positive for acid phosphatase, alkaline phosphatase, α-galactosidase, α-glucosidase, β-galactosidase, β-glucosidase, cystine arylamidase, esterase, esterase lipase, leucine arylamidase, naphthol-AS-BI-phosphohydrolase, N-acetyl-β-glucosaminidase, trypsin, and valine arylamidase; while showing weak activity for α-chymotrypsin, α-fucosidase, α-mannosidase, β-glucuronidase, and lipase. The cell wall is of the III type (LL-DAP). The whole cell sugar pattern consists of galactose, glucose, mannose, ribose, and traces of rhamnose. Phospholipid pattern consists of DPG, PE, PLs, APL, PI, and GPL. The predominant menaquinone is MK-9 (H6). The major fatty acids (> 5%) are C16:0, anteiso-C15:0, iso-C16:0, and C16:1 ω7c, anteiso-C17:0, iso-C15:0. The G+C content of the DNA is 70.05%.

The type strain, BCCO 10_0861T = DSM 117345T, was isolated from a bare soil sample in Sokolov Coal Basin, Czech Republic. The GenBank accession number for the 16S rRNA gene sequence of strain BCCO 10_0861T is OR827066. The whole genome shotgun project has been deposited in European Nucleotide Archive (ENA) under accession number Project No. PRJEB70906.

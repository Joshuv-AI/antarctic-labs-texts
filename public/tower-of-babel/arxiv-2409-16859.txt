Revisiting Extragradient-Type Methods – Part 1: Generalizations and Sublinear Convergence Rates
Quoc Tran-Dinh, Nghia Nguyen-Trung

Abstract
This paper presents a comprehensive analysis of the well-known extragradient (EG) method for solving both equations and inclusions. First, we unify and generalize EG for [non]linear equations to a wider class of algorithms, encompassing various existing schemes and potentially new variants. Next, we analyze both sublinear “best-iterate” and “last-iterate” convergence rates for the entire class of algorithms, and derive new convergence results for two well-known instances. Second, we extend our EG framework above to “monotone” inclusions, introducing a new class of algorithms and its corresponding convergence results. Third, we also unify and generalize Tseng’s forward-backward-forward splitting (FBFS) method to a broader class of algorithms to solve [non]linear inclusions when a weak-Minty solution exists, and establish its “best-iterate” convergence rate. Fourth, to complete our picture, we also investigate sublinear rates of two other common variants of EG using our EG analysis framework developed here: the reflected forward-backward splitting and the golden ratio methods. Finally, we conduct an extensive numerical experiment to validate our theoretical findings. Our results demonstrate that several new variants of our proposed algorithms outperform existing schemes in the majority of examples.

Problem statement.

In this paper, we consider the following
generalized equation
(also known as a [composite]
[non]linear inclusion
):

\textrm{Find $x^{\star}\in\mathrm{dom}(\Phi)$ such that:}\quad 0\in\Phi x^{\star}\equiv Fx^{\star}+Tx^{\star},

(NI)

where
F:\mathbb{R}^{p}\to\mathbb{R}^{p}
is a single-valued mapping,
T:\mathbb{R}^{p}\rightrightarrows 2^{\mathbb{R}^{p}}
is a set-valued (or multivalued) mapping from
\mathbb{R}^{p}
to
2^{\mathbb{R}^{p}}
(the set of all subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{p}
),
\Phi:=F+T
, and
\mathrm{dom}(\Phi):=\mathrm{dom}(F)\cap\mathrm{dom}(T)
is the domain of
\Phi
.
Here, we will focus on the finite-dimensional Euclidean spaces
\mathbb{R}^{p}
.
However, it is worth noting that most results presented in this paper can be extended to Hilbert spaces as demonstrated in the existing literature.

Special cases.

We are also interested in various special cases of (
NI
) as follows.
If
T=0
, then (
NI
) reduces to a
[non]linear equation
:

\textrm{Find $x^{\star}\in\mathrm{dom}(F)$ such that:}\quad Fx^{\star}=0.

(NE)

If
T:=\partial{g}
, the subdifferential of a proper, closed, and convex function
g
, then (
NI
) reduces a
mixed variational inequality problem
(MVIP) of the form:

\textrm{Find $x^{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}$ such that:}\ \langle Fx^{\star},x-x^{\star}\rangle+g(x)-g(x^{\star})\geq 0,\ \forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{p}.

(MVIP)

In particular, if
T=\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{X}}
, the normal cone of a nonempty, closed, and convex set
\mathcal{X}
in
\mathbb{R}^{p}
(i.e.
g=\delta_{\mathcal{X}}
, the indicator of
\mathcal{X}
), then (
MVIP
) reduces the classical (Stampacchia’s)
variational inequality problem
(VIP):

\textrm{Find $x^{\star}\in\mathcal{X}$ such that:}\quad\langle Fx^{\star},x-x^{\star}\rangle\geq 0,\ \textrm{for all}\ x\in\mathcal{X}.

(VIP)

Another important special case of (
NI
) is the following minimax problem:

\min_{u\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}\max_{v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big\{\mathcal{L}(u,v):=\varphi(u)+\mathcal{H}(u,v)-\psi(v)\Big\},

(1)

where
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
and
\psi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
are often proper, closed, and convex functions, and
\mathcal{H}:\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is a bifunction, often assumed to be differentiable, but not necessarily convex-concave.
If we denote
x:=[u,v]
as the concatenation of
u
and
v
, and define
T:=[\partial{\varphi},\partial{\psi}]
and
F:=[\nabla_{u}{\mathcal{H}}(u,v),-\nabla_{v}{\mathcal{H}}(u,v)]
, then the optimality condition of (
1
) is exactly covered by (
NI
) as a special case.

Unlike optimization and minimax problems, convergence analysis of gradient or forward-based methods for solving (

This drawback is even more critical when analyzing algorithms for solving nonmonotone instances of (

Additionally, unlike convex functions where strong properties such as coerciveness and cyclic monotonicity hold for their [sub]gradients beyond monotonicity, this is not the case for general monotone and Lipschitz continuous operators.

Our approach and contribution.

The EG method
[
46
]
and its variants for solving (
NI
) can be written as
x^{k+1}:=\hat{J}_{\eta T}(x^{k}-\eta d^{k})
, where
\eta>0
is a given stepsize,
d^{k}
is a search direction, and
\hat{J}_{\eta T}
is an approximation of the resolvent
J_{\eta T}
of
\eta T
.
Hitherto, existing works primarily focus on:

\mathrm{(i)}

Instantiating
d^{k}
to obtain a specific variant and then studying convergence of such a scheme.
For example, if we choose
d^{k}:=F(J_{\eta T}(x^{k}-\eta Fx^{k}))
, then we get the classical EG algorithm in
[
46
]
, while if we set
d^{k}:=F(y^{k})
with
y^{k}:=J_{\eta T}(x^{k}-\eta Fy^{k-1})
, then we get Popov’s past-EG scheme in
[
76
]
.

\mathrm{(ii)}

Approximating
J_{\eta T}
by a simpler operator
\hat{J}_{\eta T}
.
For instance, if
T=\delta_{\mathcal{X}}
, then we can replace
J_{\eta T}
by the projection onto an appropriate half-space.

While
\mathrm{(ii)}
has been intensively studied, e.g., in
[
16
,
33
,
45
,
57
]
, especially when
T=\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{X}}
, and it is not our main focus in this paper,
\mathrm{(i)}
covers a few non-unified schemes such as
[
46
,
54
,
55
,
56
,
76
,
93
]
and is far from being comprehensive.
The latter raises the following question:

Can we unify and generalize EG to cover broader classes of algorithms?

In this paper, we attempt to tackle this question by unifying and generalizing EG to three broader classes of algorithms to solve both (
NE
) and (
NI
) as shown in Table
1
.
Specifically, our contribution can be summarized as follows.

\mathrm{(a)}

First, we unify and generalize EG to a wider class of algorithms for solving (
NE
).
We prove both
\mathcal{O}\big(1/\sqrt{k}\big)
-best-iterate and last-iterate convergence rates of this generalized scheme.
Then, we specify our results to cover various existing variants from the literature, where our results apply.

\mathrm{(b)}

Second, we unify and generalize EG to a broader class of schemes for solving (
NI
), and establish its
\mathcal{O}\big(1/\sqrt{k}\big)
-best-iterate and last-iterate convergence rates under a “monotonicity” assumption.
Again, we specify our method to cover: the standard EG scheme and Popov’s past-EG algorithm.

\mathrm{(c)}

Third, we develop a generalization of Tseng’s FBFS method, and provide a new unified
\mathcal{O}\big(1/\sqrt{k}\big)
-best-iterate convergence rate for this scheme.
Our result covers both Tseng’s classical FBFS algorithm and the FRBS (also equivalent to the optimistic gradient) method as its instances.

\mathrm{(d)}

Fourth, we present a new best-iterate and last-iterate convergence analysis for the reflected forward-backward splitting (RFBS) methods to solve (
NI
) under a “monotonicity” assumption.
Alternatively, we also analyze the best-iterate convergence rate of the golden ratio (GR) method in
[
55
]
by extending
T
to a
3
-cyclically monotone operator and the range of the GR parameter
\tau
from a fixed value
\tau:=\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}
to
1<\tau<1+\sqrt{3}
.

\mathrm{(e)}

Finally, we implement our algorithms and their competitors and perform an extensive test on different problems.
Our results show some promising improvement of the new methods over existing ones on various examples.

2.1
Basic Concepts, Monotonicity, and Lipschitz Continuity

We work with finite dimensional Euclidean spaces
\mathbb{R}^{p}
and
\mathbb{R}^{n}
equipped with standard inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and Euclidean norm
\|\cdot\|
.
For a multivalued mapping
T:\mathbb{R}^{p}\rightrightarrows 2^{\mathbb{R}^{p}}
,
\mathrm{dom}(T)=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{p}:Tx\not=\emptyset\right\}
denotes its domain,
\mathrm{ran}(T):=\bigcup_{x\in\mathrm{dom}(T)}Tx
is its range, and
\mathrm{gra}(T)=\left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{p}\times\mathbb{R}^{p}:y\in Tx\right\}
stands for its graph, where
2^{\mathbb{R}^{p}}
is the set of all subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{p}
.
The inverse mapping of
T
is defined as
T^{-1}y:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{p}:y\in Tx\}
.
For a proper, closed, and convex function
f:\mathbb{R}^{p}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
,
\mathrm{dom}(f):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{p}:f(x)<+\infty\}
denotes the domain of
f
,
\partial{f}
denotes the subdifferential of
f
, and
\nabla{f}
stands for the [sub]gradient of
f
.
For a symmetric matrix
\mathbf{Q}
,
\lambda_{\min}(\mathbf{Q})
and
\lambda_{\max}(\mathbf{Q})
denote the smallest and largest eigenvalues of
\mathbf{Q}
, respectively.

\mathrm{(a)}
Monotonicity.

For a multivalued mapping
T:\mathbb{R}^{p}\rightrightarrows 2^{\mathbb{R}^{p}}
and
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
, we say that
T
is
\mu
-monotone if

\begin{array}[]{ll}\langle u-v,x-y\rangle\geq\mu\|x-y\|^{2},\quad\forall(x,u),(y,v)\in\mathrm{gra}(T).\end{array}

If
T
is single-valued, then this condition reduces to
\langle Tx-Ty,x-y\rangle\geq\mu\|x-y\|^{2}
for all
x,y\in\mathrm{dom}(T)
.
If
\mu=0
, then we say that
T
is monotone.
If
\mu>0
, then
T
is
\mu
-strongly monotone (or sometimes called coercive).
If
\mu<0
, then we say that
T
is weakly monotone.
It is also called
|\mu|
-hypomonotone, see
[
7
]
.
If
T=\partial{g}
, the subdifferential of a proper and convex function, then
T
is also monotone.
If
g
is
\mu
-strongly convex, then
T=\partial{g}
is also
\mu
-strongly monotone.

We say that
T
is
\rho
-comonotone if there exists
\rho\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\langle u-v,x-y\rangle\geq\rho\|u-v\|^{2},\quad\forall(x,u),(y,v)\in\mathrm{gra}(T).\end{array}

If
\rho=0
, then this condition reduces to the monotonicity of
T
.
If
\rho>0
, then
T
is called
\rho
-co-coercive.
In particular, if
\rho=1
, then
T
is firmly nonexpansive.
If
\rho<0
, then
T
is called
|\rho|
-co-hypomonotone, see, e.g.,
[
7
,
23
]
.
Note that a co-hypomonotone operator can also be nonmonotone.
As a concrete example, consider a linear mapping
Fx=Qx+q
for a symmetric matrix
Q
and a vector
q
.
If
Q
is invertible then it is obvious to check that
\langle Fx-Fy,x-y\rangle\geq\rho\|Fx-Fy\|^{2}
for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
, where
\rho:=\lambda_{\min}(Q^{-1})
.
If
\rho<0
, then
F
is
|\rho|
-co-hypomonotone, but
F
is not monotone since
Q
is not positive semidefinite.

We say that
T
is maximally
\mu
-monotone if
\mathrm{gra}(T)
is not properly contained in the graph of any other
\mu
-monotone operator.
If
\mu=0
, then we say that
T
is maximally monotone.
These definitions are also extended to
|\rho|
-cohypomonotone operators.
For a proper, closed, and convex function
f:\mathbb{R}^{p}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
, the subdifferential
\partial{f}
of
f
is maximally monotone.

For a given mapping
T
such that
\mathrm{zer}(T):=\left\{x\in\mathrm{dom}(T):0\in Tx\right\}\neq\emptyset
, we say that
T
is star-monotone (respectively,
\mu
-star-monotone or
\rho
-star-comonotone (see
[
51
]
)) if for some
x^{\star}\in\mathrm{zer}(T)
, we have
\langle u,x-x^{\star}\rangle\geq 0
(respectively,
\langle u,x-x^{\star}\rangle\geq\mu\|x-x^{\star}\|^{2}
or
\langle u,x-x^{\star}\rangle\geq\rho\|u\|^{2}
) for all
(x,u)\in\mathrm{gra}(T)
.
A solution
x^{\star}\in\mathrm{zer}(T)
satisfying a
\rho
-star-comonotonicity is called a weak-Minty solution.
Clearly, if
T
is monotone (respectively,
\mu
-monotone or
\rho
-co-monotone), then it is also star-monotone (respectively,
\mu
-star-monotone or
\rho
-star-comonotone).
However, the reverse statement does not hold in general.

\mathrm{(b)}
Cyclic monotonicity.

We say that a mapping
T
is
m
-cyclically monotone (
m\geq 2
) if
\sum_{i=1}^{m}\langle u^{i},x^{i}-x^{i+1}\rangle\geq 0
for all
(x^{i},u^{i})\in\mathrm{gra}(T)
and
x_{1}=x_{m+1}
(see
[
6
]
).
We say that
T
is cyclically monotone if it is
m
-cyclically monotone for every
m\geq 2
.
If
T
is
m
-cyclically monotone, then it is also
\hat{m}
-cyclically monotone for any
2\leq\hat{m}\leq m
.
Since a
2
-cyclically monotone operator
T
is monotone, any
m
-cyclically monotone operator
T
is
2
-cyclically monotone, and thus is also monotone.
An
m
-cyclically monotone operator
T
is called maximally
m
-cyclically monotone if
\mathrm{gra}(T)
is not properly contained into the graph of any other
m
-cyclically monotone operator.

As proven in
(
6
, Theorem 22.18)
that
T
is maximally cyclically monotone iff
T=\partial{f}
, the subdifferential of a proper, closed, and convex function
f
.
However, there exist maximally
m
-cyclically monotone operators (e.g., rotation linear operators) that are not the subdifferential
\partial{f}
of a proper, closed, and convex function
f
, see, e.g.,
[
6
]
.
Furthermore, as indicated in
(
5
, Example 2.16)
, there exist maximally
3
-cyclically monotone operators that are not maximally monotone.

\mathrm{(c)}
Lipschitz continuity and contraction.

A single-valued mapping
F
is called
L
-Lipschitz continuous if
\|Fx-Fy\|\leq L\|x-y\|
for all
x,y\in\mathrm{dom}(F)
, where
L\geq 0
is the Lipschitz constant.
If
L=1
, then we say that
F
is nonexpansive, while if
L\in[0,1)
, then we say that
F
is
L
-contractive.

\mathrm{(d)}
Normal cone.

Given a nonempty, closed, and convex set
\mathcal{X}
in
\mathbb{R}^{p}
, the normal cone of
\mathcal{X}
is defined as
\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{X}}(x):=\{w\in\mathbb{R}^{p}:\langle w,x-y\rangle\geq 0,\ \forall y\in\mathcal{X}\}
if
x\in\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{X}}(x)=\emptyset
, otherwise.
If
f:=\delta_{\mathcal{X}}
, the indicator of a convex set
\mathcal{X}
, then we have
\partial{f}=\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{X}}
.
Moreover,
J_{\partial{f}}
reduces to the projection onto
\mathcal{X}
.

\mathrm{(e)}
Resolvent and proximal operators.

Given a multivalued mapping
T
, the operator
J_{T}x:=\left\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{p}:x\in y+Ty\right\}
is called the resolvent of
T
, denoted by
J_{T}x=(\mathbb{I}+T)^{-1}x
, where
\mathbb{I}
is the identity mapping.
If
T
is
\rho
-monotone with
\rho>-1
, then evaluating
J_{T}
requires solving a strongly monotone inclusion
0\in y-x+Ty
.
Hence,
J_{T}
is well-defined and single-valued.
If
T=\partial{f}
, the subdifferential of proper, closed, and convex function
f
, then
J_{T}
reduces to the proximal operator of
f
, denoted by
\mathrm{prox}_{f}
, which can be computed as
\mathrm{prox}_{f}(x):=\mathrm{arg}\min_{y}\{f(y)+(1/2)\|y-x\|^{2}\}
.
In particular, if
T=\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{X}}
, the normal cone of a closed and convex set
\mathcal{X}
, then
J_{T}
is the projection onto
\mathcal{X}
, denoted by
\mathrm{proj}_{\mathcal{X}}
.
If
T
is maximally monotone, then
\mathrm{ran}(\mathbb{I}+T)=\mathbb{R}^{p}
(by Minty’s theorem) and
T
is firmly nonexpansive (and thus nonexpansive).

2.2
Best-Iterate and Last-Iterate Convergence Rates

The results presented in this paper are related to two types of sublinear convergence rates: the
best-iterate
and the
last-iterate
convergence rates.
To elaborate on these concepts, we assume that
\mathcal{D}
is a given metric (e.g.,
\|Fx^{k}\|^{2}
or
e(x^{k})^{2}
defined by (
2
) below) defined on an iterate sequence
\{x^{k}\}
generated by the underlying algorithm for solving (
NI
) or its special cases.
For any
k\geq 0
and a given order
\nu>0
, if

\min_{0\leq l\leq k}\mathcal{D}(x^{l})\leq\frac{1}{k+1}\sum_{l=0}^{k}\mathcal{D}(x^{l})=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{k^{\nu}}\right),\vskip-3.22916pt

then we say that
\{x^{k}\}
has a
\mathcal{O}\left(1/k^{\nu}\right)
-best-iterate convergence rate.
In this case, we can take
\hat{x}_{k}:=x_{k_{\min}}
with
k_{\min}:=\mathrm{arg}\min_{0\leq l\leq k}\mathcal{D}(x^{l})
as the “best” output of the underlying algorithm.

If we instead have
\mathcal{D}(x^{k})=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{k^{\nu}}\right)
with
x^{k}
being the
k
-th iterate generated by the algorithm, then we say that
\{x^{k}\}
has a
\mathcal{O}\left(1/k^{\nu}\right)
last-iterate convergence rate.
We emphasize that the convergence on the metric
\mathcal{D}
of
\{x^{k}\}
does not generally imply the convergence of
\{x^{k}\}
itself, especially when we characterize the rate of convergence in different metrics.

2.3
Exact Solutions and Approximate Solutions

There are different metrics to characterize exact and approximate solutions of (
NI
).
The most obvious one is the residual norm of
\Phi
, which is defined as

e(x):=\min_{\xi\in Tx}\|Fx+\xi\|,\quad x\in\mathrm{dom}(\Phi).

(2)

Clearly, if
e(x^{\star})=0
for some
x^{\star}\in\mathrm{dom}(\Phi)
, then
x^{\star}\in\mathrm{zer}(\Phi)
, a solution of (
NI
).
If
T=0
, then
e(x)=\|Fx\|
.
However, if
e(\hat{x})\leq\epsilon
for a given tolerance
\epsilon>0
, then
\hat{x}
can be considered as an
\epsilon
-approximate solution of (
NI
).
The algorithms presented in this paper use this metric to characterize an approximate solution of (
NI
) and its special cases.

Other metrics often used for monotone (
VIP
) are gap functions and restricted gap functions
[
33
,
45
,
68
]
, which are respectively defined as follows:

\mathcal{G}(x):=\max_{y\in\mathcal{X}}\langle Fy,y-x\rangle\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{G}_{\mathbb{B}}(x):=\max_{y\in\mathcal{X}\cap\mathbb{B}}\langle Fy,y-x\rangle,

(3)

where
\mathbb{B}
is a given nonempty, closed, and bounded convex set.
Note that, under the monotonicity,
\mathcal{G}(x)\geq 0
for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
, and
\mathcal{G}(x^{\star})=0
iff
x^{\star}
is a solution of (
VIP
).
Thus
\tilde{x}
is an
\epsilon
-approximate solution of (
VIP
) if
\mathcal{G}(\tilde{x})\leq\epsilon
.

For the restricted gap function
\mathcal{G}_{\mathbb{B}}
, if
x^{\star}
is a solution of (
VIP
) and
x^{\star}\in\mathbb{B}
, then
\mathcal{G}_{\mathbb{B}}(x^{\star})=0
.
Conversely, if
\mathcal{G}_{\mathbb{B}}(x^{\star})=0
and
x^{\star}\in\mathrm{int}\left(\mathbb{B}\right)
, the interior of
\mathbb{B}
, then
x^{\star}
is a solution of (
VIP
) in
\mathbb{B}
(see
(
68
, Lemma 1)
).
Gap functions have widely been used in the literature to characterize approximate solutions for many numerical methods, see, e.g.,
[
22
,
25
,
33
,
45
,
67
,
68
]
as concrete examples.
Note that for nonmonotone cases, gap functions are not applicable in general.

If
J_{\eta T}
is well-defined and single-valued for some
\eta>0
, and
F
is single-valued, then we can use the following forward-backward splitting residual:

\mathcal{G}_{\eta}x:=\tfrac{1}{\eta}\left(x-J_{\eta T}(x-\eta Fx)\right),

(4)

to characterize solutions of (
NI
).
It is clear that
\mathcal{G}_{\eta}x^{\star}=0
iff
x^{\star}\in\mathrm{zer}(\Phi)
.
In addition, if
J_{\eta T}
is firmly nonexpansive, then we also have

\|\mathcal{G}_{\eta}x\|\leq\|Fx+\xi\|,\quad(x,\xi)\in\mathrm{gra}(T).

(5)

Hence, for a given tolerance
\epsilon>0
, if
\|\mathcal{G}_{\eta}\tilde{x}\|\leq\epsilon
, then we can say that
\tilde{x}
is an
\epsilon
-approximate solution of (
NI
).
If
T:=\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{X}}
, i.e. (
NI
) reduces to (
VIP
), then, with
\eta=1
,
\mathcal{G}_{1}x
reduces to the classical natural map
\Pi_{F,\mathcal{X}}x=x-\mathrm{proj}_{\mathcal{X}}(x-Fx)
of (
VIP
), and
r_{n}(x):=\|\mathcal{G}_{1}x\|=\|\Pi_{F,\mathcal{X}}x\|
is the corresponding natural residual at
x
, see
[
33
]
.
From (
5
), we have
r_{n}(x)\leq\|Fx+\xi\|
for any
\xi\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{X}}(x)
.

2.4
The Forward-Type or Fixed-Point Methods

Let us briefly recall the gradient/forward or fixed-point scheme for solving (
NE
) as follows.
Starting from
x^{0}\in\mathrm{dom}(F)
, at each iteration
k
, we update

x^{k+1}:=x^{k}-\eta Fx^{k},

(FW)

where
\eta>0
is a given constant stepsize.
The forward scheme is a classical method to solve (
NE
).
This scheme covers the gradient descent method for minimizing
f(x)
as a special case with
F=\nabla{f}
.
If
G:=\mathbb{I}-\eta F
, then (
FW
) becomes the well-known
fixed-point iteration
to approximate a fixed-point of
G
.
We recall this method here to compare with EG methods.

If
F
is
\rho
-co-coercive and
0<\eta<\rho
, then
\{x^{k}\}
converges to
x^{\star}\in\mathrm{zer}(F)
(see, e.g.,
[
33
]
).
Otherwise, if
F
is only monotone and
L
-Lipschitz continuous, then there exist examples (e.g.,
Fx=[x_{2},-x_{1}]
) showing that (
FW
) is divergent for any choice of stepsize
\eta
even if
F
is monotone.

To solve (
NI
), we can instead apply the forward-backward splitting method as follows.
Starting from
x^{0}\in\mathrm{dom}(F)
, at each iteration
k\geq 0
, we update

x^{k+1}:=J_{\eta T}(x^{k}-\eta Fx^{k}),

(FBS)

where
\eta>0
is a given constant stepsize.
Similar to (
FW
), if
F
is
\rho
-co-coercive and
T
is maximally monotone, then with
\eta\in(0,\rho)
,
\{x^{k}\}
generated by (
FBS
) converges to
x^{\star}\in\mathrm{zer}(\Phi)
.
If
F=\nabla{f}
, the gradient of a convex and
L
-smooth function
f
, then
F
is co-coercive.
However, imposing the co-coerciveness for a general mapping
F
is often restrictive.
Hence, both (
FW
) and (
FBS
) are less practical in applications due to the absence of the co-coerciveness.

3
A Class of Extragradient-Type Methods for Nonlinear Equations

In this section, we unify and generalize EG to a wider class of algorithms for solve (
NE
).
Then, we analyze its convergence rates and study its special cases.

3.1
A Class of Extragradient Methods for Equations

Our class of extragradient methods for solving (
NE
) is presented as follows.
Starting from an initial point
x^{0}\in\mathrm{dom}(F)
, at each iteration
k\geq 0
, we update

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}y^{k}&:=&x^{k}-\frac{\eta}{\beta}u^{k},\\
x^{k+1}&:=&x^{k}-\eta Fy^{k},\end{array}\right.

(GEG)

where
\eta>0
is a given constant stepsize,
\beta\in(0,1]
is a scaling factor, and
u^{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
satisfies the following condition:

\|Fx^{k}-u^{k}\|^{2}\leq\kappa_{1}\|Fx^{k}-Fy^{k-1}\|^{2}+\kappa_{2}\|Fx^{k}-Fx^{k-1}\|^{2},

(6)

for given constants
\kappa_{1}\geq 0
and
\kappa_{2}\geq 0
and
x^{-1}=y^{-1}:=x^{0}
.
Under different choices of
u^{k}
, (
GEG
) covers a wide range of methods generalizing the
extragradient method
(EG) in
[
46
]
.
Note that condition (
6
) also makes (
GEG
) different from the hybrid extragradient method in, e.g.,
[
63
,
84
]
.
If we define
d^{k}:=F(x^{k}-\frac{\eta}{\beta}u^{k})
, then (
GEG
) becomes
x^{k+1}:=x^{k}-\eta d^{k}
, which falls into a generic iterative scheme we have discussed earlier.

Let us consider the following three choices of
u^{k}
which fulfill (
6
).

(a)

Variant 1: Extragradient method.

If we choose
u^{k}:=Fx^{k}
, then we obtain the
extragradient
(EG) scheme
[
46
]
from (
GEG
) for solving (
NE
).
Clearly,
u^{k}
satisfies (
6
) with
\kappa_{1}=\kappa_{2}=0
.

(b)

Variant 2: Past-extragradient method.

If we choose
u^{k}:=Fy^{k-1}
, then we obtain the Popov’s
past-extragradient
method
[
76
]
to solve (
NE
).
This scheme is also equivalent to the
optimistic gradient
method in the literature, see also
[
26
,
59
,
61
]
for more details.
Clearly,
u^{k}
satisfies (
6
) with
\kappa_{1}=1
and
\kappa_{2}=0
.

(c)

Variant 3: Generalization.

We can construct
u^{k}:=\alpha_{1}Fx^{k}+\alpha_{2}Fy^{k-1}+(1-\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})Fx^{k-1}
as an affine combination of
Fx^{k}
,
Fy^{k-1}
and
Fx^{k-1}
for given constants
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
.
Then,
u^{k}
satisfies (
6
) with
\kappa_{1}=(1+c)\alpha_{2}^{2}
and
\kappa_{2}=(1+c^{-1})(1-\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})^{2}
by Young’s inequality for any
c>0
.
This variant essentially has the same per-iteration complexity as
Variant 1
, but it covers all candidates as combinations of
x^{k}
,
y^{k-1}
, and
x^{k-1}
.

Figure
1
illustrates the three variants of (
GEG
) presented above.

):

\mathrm{(i)}
Discussion of the extragradient method.

With
u^{k}:=Fx^{k}
, if
\beta=1
, then we obtain exactly the
classical EG method

[
46
]
for solving (
NE
).
If
\beta<1
, then we recover the
extragradient-plus (EG
+
)
scheme from
[
29
]
for solving (
NE
).
If we compute
x^{k}=y^{k}+\frac{\eta}{\beta}Fx^{k}
from the first line of (
GEG
) and substitute it into the second line of (
GEG
), then we get
x^{k+1}=y^{k}-\eta(Fy^{k}-\frac{1}{\beta}Fx^{k})
.
In this case, we obtain a
forward-backward-forward splitting
variant of Tseng’s method in
[
93
]
from (

) as follows:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}y^{k}&:=&x^{k}-\frac{\eta}{\beta}Fx^{k},\\
x^{k+1}&:=&y^{k}-\eta(Fy^{k}-\frac{1}{\beta}Fx^{k}).\end{array}\right.

(FBFS)

Clearly, if
\beta=1
, then we recover exactly
Tseng’s method
for solving (
NE
).

\mathrm{(ii)}
Discussion of the past-extragradient method and its relations to other methods.

With
u^{k}:=Fy^{k-1}
, we can show that it is equivalent to the following variants.
First, we can rewrite (
GEG
) as

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}x^{k+1}&:=&x^{k}-\eta Fy^{k}\\
y^{k+1}&:=&x^{k+1}-\tfrac{\eta}{\beta}Fy^{k}.\end{array}\right.

(PEG)

This form shows us that (
PEG
) saves one evaluation
Fx^{k}
of
F
at each iteration compared to
Variant 1
.
If
\beta=1
, then we obtain exactly the
Popov’s method
in
[
76
]
.
If we rotate up the second line and use
\beta=1
as
y^{k}=x^{k}-\eta Fy^{k-1}
, then we get the
past-extragradient method
.

Now, under this choice of
u^{k}
, from the first line of (
GEG
), we have
x^{k}=y^{k}+\frac{\eta}{\beta}u^{k}=y^{k}+\frac{\eta}{\beta}Fy^{k-1}
.
Substituting this expression into the first line of (
PEG
), we get
x^{k+1}=y^{k}-\eta Fy^{k}+\frac{\eta}{\beta}Fy^{k-1}
.
Substituting this relation into the second line of (
PEG
), we can eliminate
x^{k+1}
to get the following variant:

y^{k+1}:=y^{k}-\tfrac{\eta}{\beta}\big((1+\beta)Fy^{k}-Fy^{k-1}\big).

(FRBS)

This scheme can be considered as a simplified variant of the
forward-reflected-backward splitting
scheme in
[
56
]
for solving (
NE
) when we set
\beta:=1
as
y^{k+1}:=y^{k}-\eta(2Fy^{k}-Fy^{k-1})
.

Alternatively, from (
PEG
), we have
x^{k-1}-x^{k}=\eta Fy^{k-1}
and
\beta(x^{k}-y^{k})=\eta Fy^{k-1}
, leading to
x^{k-1}-x^{k}=\beta(x^{k}-y^{k})
.
Therefore, we get
y^{k}=\frac{1}{\beta}((1+\beta)x^{k}-x^{k-1})
.
Substituting this expression into the first line of (
PEG
), we get

x^{k+1}:=x^{k}-\eta F\big(\tfrac{1}{\beta}((1+\beta)x^{k}-x^{k-1})\big).\vskip-2.15277pt

(RFB)

In particular, if
\beta=1
, then we obtain
x^{k+1}:=x^{k}-\eta F(2x^{k}-x^{k-1})
, which turns out to be the

method in
[
54
]
or the
reflected forward-backward splitting
scheme in
[
18
]
for solving (

Using the relation
x^{k-1}-x^{k}=\beta(x^{k}-y^{k})
above, we can compute that
x^{k}=\frac{\beta}{1+\beta}y^{k}+\frac{1}{1+\beta}x^{k-1}=\frac{(\omega-1)}{\omega}y^{k}+\frac{1}{\omega}x^{k-1}
, where
\omega:=1+\beta
.
Combining the two lines of (

), we get
y^{k+1}:=x^{k+1}-\tfrac{\eta}{\beta}Fy^{k}=x^{k}-\frac{\eta(1+\beta)}{\beta}Fy^{k}
.
Putting both expressions together, we arrive at

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}x^{k}&:=&\tfrac{(\omega-1)}{\omega}y^{k}+\tfrac{1}{\omega}x^{k-1},\\
y^{k+1}&:=&x^{k}-\tfrac{\eta(1+\beta)}{\beta}Fy^{k}.\end{array}\right.\vskip-2.15277pt

(GR)

This method is a simplified variant of the
golden ratio
method in
[
55
]
for solving (
NE
).
Overall, the template (
GEG
) covers a class of EG algorithms with many common instances as discussed above.

\mathrm{(iii)}
Discussion of the generalization case.

Suppose that
u_{\mathrm{GEG}}^{k}:=\alpha_{1}Fx^{k}+\alpha_{2}Fy^{k-1}+(1-\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})Fx^{k-1}
for fixed
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
.

Let us define
v^{k}:=Fy^{k}-Fx^{k}
,
\bar{v}^{k}:=Fy^{k}-Fy^{k-1}
, and
\hat{v}^{k}:=Fy^{k}-Fx^{k-1}
.
Then, we can compute
\|Fy^{k}-u^{k}_{\mathrm{GEG}}\|^{2}=\|\alpha_{1}v^{k}+\alpha_{2}\bar{v}^{k}+(1-\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})\hat{v}^{k}\|^{2}
.
Now, we compare (
GEG
) and EG in
Variant 1
and PEG in
Variant 2
.

•

For EG, we choose
u_{\textrm{EG}}^{k}:=Fx^{k}
, and hence,
\|Fy^{k}-u_{\mathrm{EG}}^{k}\|^{2}=\|Fy^{k}-Fx^{k}\|^{2}=\|v^{k}\|^{2}
.
If there exist
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\|v^{k}\|>\|\alpha_{1}v^{k}+\alpha_{2}\bar{v}^{k}+(1-\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})\hat{v}^{k}\|,\vskip-2.15277pt

then, by Lemma
1
below, we can easily show that at each iteration
k
,
\|x^{k+1}-x^{\star}\|^{2}
decreases faster in GEG than in EG.

•

For PEG, we choose
u_{\mathrm{PEG}}^{k}:=Fy^{k-1}
, and hence,
\|Fy^{k}-u^{k}_{\mathrm{PEG}}\|^{2}=\|\bar{v}^{k}\|^{2}
.
If there exist
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\|\bar{v}^{k}\|>\|\alpha_{1}v^{k}+\alpha_{2}\bar{v}^{k}+(1-\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})\hat{v}^{k}\|,\vskip-2.15277pt

then, again by Lemma
1
, we can easily show that at each iteration
k
,
\|x^{k+1}-x^{\star}\|^{2}
decreases faster in GEG than in PEG.

Consequently, our (
GEG
) scheme gives more freedom to improve the performance compared to existing EG variants as we will see in Section
7
.

3.2
Key Estimates for Convergence Analysis

To analyze the convergence of (
GEG
), we first prove the following lemmas.

Lemma 1

If
\{(x^{k},y^{k})\}
is generated by (
GEG
), then for any
\gamma>0
and any
\hat{x}\in\mathrm{dom}(F)
, we have

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\|x^{k+1}-\hat{x}\|^{2}&\leq&\|x^{k}-\hat{x}\|^{2}-\beta\|y^{k}-x^{k}\|^{2}+\tfrac{\eta^{2}}{\gamma}\|Fy^{k}-u^{k}\|^{2}\\
&&-{~}2\eta\langle Fy^{k},y^{k}-\hat{x}\rangle-(\beta-\gamma)\|x^{k+1}-y^{k}\|^{2}\\
&&-{~}(1-\beta)\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|^{2}.\end{array}

(7)

Proof

First, for any
\hat{x}\in\mathrm{dom}(F)
, using
x^{k+1}-x^{k}=-\eta Fy^{k}
from the second line of (
GEG
), we have

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\|x^{k+1}-\hat{x}\|^{2}&=&\|x^{k}-\hat{x}\|^{2}+2\langle x^{k+1}-x^{k},x^{k+1}-\hat{x}\rangle-\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|^{2}\\
&=&\|x^{k}-\hat{x}\|^{2}-2\eta\langle Fy^{k},x^{k+1}-\hat{x}\rangle-\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|^{2}.\end{array}

Next, using
\eta u^{k}=\beta(x^{k}-y^{k})
from the first line of (
GEG
),
the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, the identity
2\langle x^{k+1}-y^{k},x^{k}-y^{k}\rangle=\|x^{k}-y^{k}\|^{2}+\|x^{k+1}-y^{k}\|^{2}-\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|^{2}
, and an elementary inequality
2\langle w,z\rangle\leq\gamma\|w\|^{2}+\frac{\|z\|^{2}}{\gamma}
for any
\gamma>0
and any vectors
w
and
z
, we can derive that

\begin{array}[]{lcl}2\eta\langle Fy^{k},x^{k+1}-\hat{x}\rangle&=&2\eta\langle Fy^{k},y^{k}-\hat{x}\rangle+2\eta\langle Fy^{k}-u^{k},x^{k+1}-y^{k}\rangle\\
&&+{~}2\eta\langle u^{k},x^{k+1}-y^{k}\rangle\\
&\geq&2\eta\langle Fy^{k},y^{k}-\hat{x}\rangle-\frac{\eta^{2}}{\gamma}\|Fy^{k}-u^{k}\|^{2}-\gamma\|x^{k+1}-y^{k}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}\beta\big[\|x^{k}-y^{k}\|^{2}+\|x^{k+1}-y^{k}\|^{2}-\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|^{2}\big]\\
&=&2\eta\langle Fy^{k},y^{k}-\hat{x}\rangle+\beta\|y^{k}-x^{k}\|^{2}-\frac{\eta^{2}}{\gamma}\|Fy^{k}-u^{k}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}(\beta-\gamma)\|x^{k+1}-y^{k}\|^{2}-\beta\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|^{2}.\end{array}

Finally, combining the last two expressions, we obtain (
7
).

\square

Lemma 2

Suppose that
\mathrm{zer}(F)\neq\emptyset
,
F
is
L
-Lipschitz continuous, and there exist
x^{\star}\in\mathrm{zer}(F)
and
\rho\geq 0
such that
\langle Fx,x-x^{\star}\rangle\geq-\rho\|Fx\|^{2}
for all
x\in\mathrm{dom}(F)
.
Let
\{(x^{k},y^{k})\}
be generated by (
GEG
) for a given
u^{k}
satisfying (
6
).
For any
\gamma>0
and
r>0
, let us introduce the following function:

\hskip-4.30554pt\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathcal{P}_{k}&:=&\|x^{k}-x^{\star}\|^{2}+\tfrac{\kappa_{1}(1+r)L^{2}\eta^{2}}{r\gamma}\|x^{k}-y^{k-1}\|^{2}+\tfrac{\kappa_{2}(1+r)L^{2}\eta^{2}}{r\gamma}\|x^{k}-x^{k-1}\|^{2}.\end{array}\hskip-4.30554pt

(8)

Then, for any
s>0
and
\mu\in[0,1]

\hskip-8.61108pt\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathcal{P}_{k+1}&\leq&\mathcal{P}_{k}-\left(\beta-\frac{(1+r)L^{2}\eta^{2}}{\gamma}-\frac{2\mu\rho(1+s)}{s\eta}\right)\|y^{k}-x^{k}\|^{2}\\
&&-{~}\left(\beta-\gamma-\frac{\kappa_{1}(1+r)L^{2}\eta^{2}}{r\gamma}-\frac{2\mu\rho(1+s)}{\eta}\right)\|x^{k+1}-y^{k}\|^{2}\\
&&-{~}\big(1-\beta-\frac{\kappa_{2}(1+r)L^{2}\eta^{2}}{r\gamma}-\frac{2(1-\mu)\rho}{\eta}\big)\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|^{2}.\end{array}\hskip-8.61108pt

(9)

Proof

First, since
\langle Fx,x-x^{\star}\rangle\geq-\rho\|Fx\|^{2}
for all
x\in\mathrm{dom}(F)
, using this condition with
x:=y^{k}
, and then utilizing the second line of (
GEG
) and Young’s inequality, for any
s>0
and
\mu\in[0,1]
, we can derive that

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\langle Fy^{k},y^{k}-x^{\star}\rangle&\geq&-\rho\|Fy^{k}\|^{2}\overset{\tiny\eqref{eq:EG4NE}}{=}-\frac{\rho}{\eta^{2}}\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|^{2}\\
&\geq&-\frac{\mu\rho(1+s)}{\eta^{2}}\|x^{k+1}-y^{k}\|^{2}-\frac{\mu\rho(1+s)}{s\eta^{2}}\|y^{k}-x^{k}\|^{2}\\
&&-{~}\frac{(1-\mu)\rho}{\eta^{2}}\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|^{2}.\end{array}

(10)

Next, for any
r>0
, it follows from Young’s inequality, (
6
), and the
L
-Lipschitz continuity of

that

\hskip-8.61108pt\begin{array}[]{lcl}\|Fy^{k}-u^{k}\|^{2}&\leq&(1+r)\|Fx^{k}-Fy^{k}\|^{2}+\frac{(1+r)}{r}\|Fx^{k}-u^{k}\|^{2}\\
&\overset{\tiny\eqref{eq:EG4NE_u_cond}}{\leq}&(1+r)\|Fx^{k}-Fy^{k}\|^{2}+\frac{\kappa_{1}(1+r)}{r}\|Fx^{k}-Fy^{k-1}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}\frac{\kappa_{2}(1+r)}{r}\|Fx^{k}-Fx^{k-1}\|^{2}\\
&\leq&(1+r)L^{2}\|x^{k}-y^{k}\|^{2}+\frac{\kappa_{1}(1+r)L^{2}}{r}\|x^{k}-y^{k-1}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}\frac{\kappa_{2}(1+r)L^{2}}{r}\|x^{k}-x^{k-1}\|^{2}.\end{array}\hskip-8.61108pt

(11)

Now, substituting
x^{\star}\in\mathrm{zer}(F)
for
\hat{x}
into (
7
), and using (
10
) and (
11
), we have

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\|x^{k+1}-x^{\star}\|^{2}&\leq&\|x^{k}-x^{\star}\|^{2}+\tfrac{\kappa_{1}(1+r)L^{2}\eta^{2}}{r\gamma}\|x^{k}-y^{k-1}\|^{2}+\frac{\kappa_{2}(1+r)L^{2}\eta^{2}}{r\gamma}\|x^{k}-x^{k-1}\|^{2}\\
&&-{~}\big(\beta-\frac{(1+r)L^{2}\eta^{2}}{\gamma}-\frac{2\mu\rho(1+s)}{s\eta}\big)\|y^{k}-x^{k}\|^{2}\\
&&-{~}\big(\beta-\gamma-\frac{2\mu\rho(1+s)}{\eta}\big)\|x^{k+1}-y^{k}\|^{2}\\
&&-{~}\big(1-\beta-\frac{2(1-\mu)\rho}{\eta}\big)\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|^{2}.\end{array}

Finally, by rearranging this inequality, and then using
\mathcal{P}_{k}
from (

\hskip-8.61108pt\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathcal{P}_{k+1}&\leq&\mathcal{P}_{k}-\left(\beta-\frac{(1+r)L^{2}\eta^{2}}{\gamma}-\frac{2\mu\rho(1+s)}{s\eta}\right)\|y^{k}-x^{k}\|^{2}\\
&&-{~}\left(\beta-\gamma-\frac{\kappa_{1}(1+r)L^{2}\eta^{2}}{r\gamma}-\frac{2\mu\rho(1+s)}{\eta}\right)\|x^{k+1}-y^{k}\|^{2}\\
&&-{~}\big(1-\beta-\frac{\kappa_{2}(1+r)L^{2}\eta^{2}}{r\gamma}-\frac{2(1-\mu)\rho}{\eta}\big)\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|^{2}.\end{array}\hskip-8.61108pt

This expression is exactly (
9
).

\square

Lemma 3

Let
F
be
\rho
-co-hypomonotone, i.e. there exists
\rho\geq 0
such that
\langle Fx-Fy,x-y\rangle\geq-\rho\|Fx-Fy\|^{2}
for all
x,y\in\mathrm{dom}(F)
and
F
be
L
-Lipschitz continuous.
Let
\{(x^{k},y^{k})\}
be generated by (
GEG
).
Then, for any
s>0
,
\omega\geq 0
, and
\hat{\omega}\geq 0
, the following estimate holds:

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\|Fx^{k+1}\|^{2}&+&\omega\|Fx^{k+1}-Fy^{k}\|^{2}+\hat{\omega}\|Fx^{k+1}-Fx^{k}\|^{2}\leq\|Fx^{k}\|^{2}\\
&&-{~}\big[1-\frac{1+s}{s}\big(\hat{\omega}+\frac{2\rho}{\eta}\big)\big]\|Fy^{k}-Fx^{k}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}\big[1+\omega+(1+s)\big(\hat{\omega}+\frac{2\rho}{\eta}\big)\big]\frac{L^{2}\eta^{2}}{\beta^{2}}\|\beta Fy^{k}-u^{k}\|^{2}.\end{array}

(12)

Proof

Since
F
is
\rho
-co-hypomonotone, we have
\langle Fx^{k+1}-Fx^{k},x^{k+1}-x^{k}\rangle+\rho\|Fx^{k+1}-Fx^{k}\|^{2}\geq 0
.
Substituting
x^{k+1}-x^{k}=-\eta Fy^{k}
from the second line of (
GEG
) into this inequality and expanding it, we can show that

\begin{array}[]{lcl}0&\leq&2\langle Fx^{k},Fy^{k}\rangle-2\langle Fx^{k+1},Fy^{k}\rangle+\frac{2\rho}{\eta}\|Fx^{k+1}-Fx^{k}\|^{2}\\
&=&\|Fx^{k}\|^{2}-\|Fy^{k}-Fx^{k}\|^{2}-\|Fx^{k+1}\|^{2}+\|Fx^{k+1}-Fy^{k}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}\frac{2\rho}{\eta}\|Fx^{k+1}-Fx^{k}\|^{2}.\end{array}

For any
\omega\geq 0
,
\hat{\omega}\geq 0
, and
s>0
, by Young’s inequality, this estimate leads to

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathcal{T}_{[k+1]}&:=&\|Fx^{k+1}\|^{2}+\omega\|Fx^{k+1}-Fy^{k}\|^{2}+\hat{\omega}\|Fx^{k+1}-Fx^{k}\|^{2}\\
&\leq&\|Fx^{k}\|^{2}-\|Fy^{k}-Fx^{k}\|^{2}+(1+\omega)\|Fx^{k+1}-Fy^{k}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}\big(\hat{\omega}+\frac{2\rho}{\eta}\big)\|Fx^{k+1}-Fx^{k}\|^{2}\\
&\leq&\|Fx^{k}\|^{2}+\big[1+\omega+(1+s)\big(\hat{\omega}+\frac{2\rho}{\eta}\big)\big]\|Fx^{k+1}-Fy^{k}\|^{2}\\
&&-{~}\big[1-\frac{1+s}{s}\big(\hat{\omega}+\frac{2\rho}{\eta}\big)\big]\|Fy^{k}-Fx^{k}\|^{2}.\end{array}

(13)

Now, by the
L
-Lipschitz continuity of
F
and
x^{k+1}-y^{k}=-\eta(Fy^{k}-\frac{1}{\beta}u^{k})
from (
GEG
), we obtain
\|Fx^{k+1}-Fy^{k}\|^{2}\leq L^{2}\|x^{k+1}-y^{k}\|^{2}=\frac{L^{2}\eta^{2}}{\beta^{2}}\|\beta Fy^{k}-u^{k}\|^{2}
.
Substituting this relation into (
13
), we arrive at (
12
).

\square

3.3
The Sublinear Best-Iterate Convergences Rate of (
GEG
)

For given constants
\kappa_{1}
and
\kappa_{2}
in (
6
), we denote
r:=\frac{\kappa_{1}+\sqrt{\kappa_{1}^{2}+4\kappa_{1}}}{2}\geq 0
.
First, for any
\beta\in(0,1]
, we define the following quantity:

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\Delta&:=&\frac{1}{16(1+r)}\cdot\min\Big\{\big(1+\frac{2\kappa_{2}}{r}\big)\beta^{2},\ \frac{(1-\beta)^{2}r(r+2\kappa_{2})}{\kappa_{2}^{2}}\Big\}.\end{array}

(14)

For
r
given above, let
\alpha:=\frac{1+r}{r}
and
\mu:=\frac{r}{r+2\kappa_{2}}
.
We also define

\hskip-8.61108pt\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcllcl}\underline{\eta}_{1}&:=&\frac{\beta-\sqrt{\beta^{2}-16(1+r)\mu L\rho}}{2(1+r)L},&\bar{\eta}_{1}&:=&\frac{\beta+\sqrt{\beta^{2}-16(1+r)\mu L\rho}}{2(1+r)L},\\
\underline{\eta}_{2}&:=&\frac{1-\beta-\sqrt{(1-\beta)^{2}-8\alpha(1-\mu)\kappa_{2}L\rho}}{2\alpha\kappa_{2}L},&\bar{\eta}_{2}&:=&\frac{1-\beta+\sqrt{(1-\beta)^{2}-8\alpha(1-\mu)\kappa_{2}L\rho}}{2\alpha\kappa_{2}L},\\
\underline{\eta}&:=&\max\{\underline{\eta}_{1},\underline{\eta}_{2}\},&\bar{\eta}&:=&\min\{\bar{\eta}_{1},\bar{\eta}_{2}\}.\end{array}\right.\hskip-8.61108pt

(15)

Note that we can only choose
\beta=1
if
\kappa_{2}=0
in (
6
).
In this case, we have
\mu=1
,
\underline{\eta}=\underline{\eta}_{1}
, and
\bar{\eta}=\bar{\eta}_{1}
.
Lemma
10
in the appendix below shows that if
L\rho\leq\Delta
, then
0\leq\underline{\eta}\leq\bar{\eta}
, i.e.
[\underline{\eta},\bar{\eta}]\neq\emptyset
.

Now, given
\eta\in[\underline{\eta},\bar{\eta}]
, we consider the following quantities:

\begin{array}[]{lcl}C_{1}&:=&\beta-(1+r)L\eta-\frac{4\mu\rho}{\eta}\quad\text{and}\quad C_{2}:=1-\beta-\alpha\kappa_{2}L\eta-\frac{2(1-\mu)\rho}{\eta}.\end{array}

(16)

Then, we are ready to state the best-iterate convergence rate of (
GEG
) for any
u^{k}
satisfying the condition (
6
).

Theorem 3.1
(Best-iterate convergence rate)

For Equation (
NE
), suppose that
\mathrm{zer}(F)\neq\emptyset
,
F
is
L
-Lipschitz continuous, and there exist
x^{\star}\in\mathrm{zer}(F)
and
\rho\geq 0
such that
\langle Fx,x-x^{\star}\rangle\geq-\rho\|Fx\|^{2}
for all
x\in\mathrm{dom}(F)
.

Let
\Delta
be defined by (
14
), and
\underline{\eta}
and
\bar{\eta}
be defined by (
15
).
Let
\{(x^{k},y^{k})\}
be generated by (
GEG
) starting from
x^{0}\in\mathrm{dom}(F)
and
y^{-1}=x^{-1}:=x^{0}
such that
u^{k}
satisfies (
6
).
If
L\rho\leq\Delta
and
\eta
is chosen such that
\eta\in[\underline{\eta},\bar{\eta}]
, then
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
in (
16
) are nonnegative.

Moreover, for any
K\geq 0
, we have

\sum_{k=0}^{K}\big[C_{1}\|y^{k}-x^{k}\|^{2}+C_{1}\|x^{k+1}-y^{k}\|^{2}+C_{2}\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|^{2}\big]\leq\|x^{0}-x^{\star}\|^{2}.

(17)

If
C_{1}>0
, then for
\Lambda:=\frac{C_{1}+2C_{2}}{2}
and
\Gamma:=\frac{2L^{2}}{C_{1}}+\frac{2}{\Lambda\eta^{2}}
, we also have

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\displaystyle\min_{0\leq k\leq K}}\|u^{k}\|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{K+1}\sum_{k=0}^{K}\|u^{k}\|^{2}\leq\frac{\beta^{2}\|x^{0}-x^{\star}\|^{2}}{C_{1}\eta^{2}(K+1)},\\
{\displaystyle\min_{0\leq k\leq K}}\|Fy^{k}\|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{K+1}\sum_{k=0}^{K}\|Fy^{k}\|^{2}\leq\frac{\|x^{0}-x^{\star}\|^{2}}{\Lambda\eta^{2}(K+1)},\\
{\displaystyle\min_{0\leq k\leq K}}\|Fx^{k}\|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{K+1}\sum_{k=0}^{K}\|Fx^{k}\|^{2}\leq\frac{\Gamma\|x^{0}-x^{\star}\|^{2}}{K+1}.\end{array}\right.

(18)

Consequently, the following results hold:

\begin{array}[]{lll}{\displaystyle\min_{0\leq k\leq K}}\|Fx^{k}\|=\mathcal{O}\big(\frac{1}{\sqrt{K}}\big),\quad{\displaystyle\min_{0\leq k\leq K}}\|Fy^{k}\|=\mathcal{O}\big(\frac{1}{\sqrt{K}}\big),\\
{\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}}\|x^{k}-y^{k}\|={\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}}\|Fx^{k}\|={\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}}\|Fy^{k}\|=0.\end{array}

(19)

In addition,
\{\|x^{k}-x^{\star}\|\}
is bounded and
\{x^{k}\}
converges to
x^{\star}\in\mathrm{zer}(F)
.

Proof

First, we choose
\gamma:=L\eta
in Lemma
2
.
Next, substituting
\alpha:=\frac{1+r}{r}
and
s:=1
into the following quantities with
r:=\frac{\kappa_{1}+\sqrt{\kappa_{1}^{2}+4\kappa_{1}}}{2}
, we get

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcllcl}C_{1}&:=&\beta-\frac{(1+r)L^{2}\eta^{2}}{\gamma}-\frac{2\mu\rho(1+s)}{s\eta}&=&\beta-(1+r)L\eta-\frac{4\mu\rho}{\eta},\\

C_{2}&:=&1-\beta-\frac{\kappa_{2}(1+r)L^{2}\eta^{2}}{r\gamma}-\frac{2(1-\mu)\rho}{\eta}&=&1-\beta-\alpha\kappa_{2}L\eta-\frac{2(1-\mu)\rho}{\eta}.\end{array}\right.

This is exactly the constants defined in (
16
).
By Lemma
10
, for
\beta\in(0,1]
, we have
\underline{\eta}\leq\bar{\eta}
.
In addition, for any
\eta\in[\underline{\eta},\bar{\eta}]
,
C_{1}=\hat{C}_{1}\geq 0
and
C_{2}\geq 0
.

Now, for given
\eta
and
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
defined by (
16
), (
9
) is rewritten as follows:

\begin{array}[]{lcl}C_{1}\|y^{k}-x^{k}\|^{2}+C_{1}\|x^{k+1}-y^{k}\|^{2}+C_{2}\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|^{2}\leq\mathcal{P}_{k}-\mathcal{P}_{k+1}.\end{array}

(20)

Summing up (
20
) from
k=0
to
K
, and noting that
\mathcal{P}_{k}\geq 0
, we obtain

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\sum_{k=0}^{K}\big[C_{1}\|x^{k+1}-y^{k}\|^{2}+C_{1}\|y^{k}-x^{k}\|^{2}+C_{2}\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|^{2}\big]\leq\mathcal{P}_{0}.\end{array}

Since we choose

, it follows from (
8
) that
\mathcal{P}_{0}=\|x^{0}-x^{\star}\|^{2}
.
Substituting this fact into the last inequality, we obtain (
17
).

Next, from the first line of (
GEG
), we have
\eta^{2}\|u^{k}\|^{2}=\beta^{2}\|y^{k}-x^{k}\|^{2}
.
Combining this relation and (
17
) we get the first line of (
18
).

Then, from the second line of (
GEG
), by Young’s inequality, we have

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\eta^{2}\|Fy^{k}\|^{2}&=&\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|^{2}\\
&\leq&a_{1}(1+a_{2}^{-1})\|y^{k}-x^{k}\|^{2}+a_{1}(1+a_{2})\|x^{k+1}-y^{k}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}(1-a_{1})\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|^{2},\end{array}

for any
a_{1}\in[0,1]
and
a_{2}>0
.
If

, then by setting

, and

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\Lambda\eta^{2}\|Fy^{k}\|^{2}\leq C_{1}\|y^{k}-x^{k}\|^{2}+C_{1}\|x^{k+1}-y^{k}\|^{2}+C_{2}\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|^{2}.\end{array}

Combining this inequality and (
17
) we get the second line of (
18
).

Again, by Young’s inequality and the

-Lipschitz continuity of

, we have

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\eta^{2}\|Fx^{k}\|^{2}\leq 2\eta^{2}\|Fy^{k}\|^{2}+2\eta^{2}\|Fx^{k}-Fy^{k}\|^{2}\leq 2\eta^{2}\|Fy^{k}\|^{2}+2L^{2}\eta^{2}\|x^{k}-y^{k}\|^{2}.\end{array}

Combining this inequality,

, and

), where

.

) and (

).
From (

), we can see that

to

[

by noticing that we are working on a finite-dimensional space

.

\square

In this section, we go beyond (

4.1
A Class of Extragradient Methods for Solving (

We propose the following generalized EG scheme (called GEG2) for solving (

Starting from

, at each iteration

, we update

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}y^{k}&:=&J_{\frac{\eta}{\beta}T}(x^{k}-\frac{\eta}{\beta}u^{k}),\\

where

is the resolvent of
\eta T
,
\eta>0
is a given stepsize,
\beta>0
is a scaling factor, and

satisfies the following condition:

\|Fx^{k}-u^{k}\|^{2}\leq\kappa_{1}\|Fx^{k}-Fy^{k-1}\|^{2}+\kappa_{2}\|Fx^{k}-Fx^{k-1}\|^{2},

(33)

for given parameters

and
\kappa_{2}\geq 0

.
Note that (
6

) different from the hybrid extragradient method in
[

,

]
.

Similar to (
GEG
), we have at least three concrete choices of

as follows.

(a)

Variant 1.
If
u^{k}:=Fx^{k}
(i.e.
\kappa_{1}=\kappa_{2}=0
in (

y^{k}:=J_{\frac{\eta}{\beta}T}(x^{k}-\frac{\eta}{\beta}Fx^{k})
.
Clearly, if
\beta=1
, then we get the well-known

.
If
\beta\in(0,1)
, then we obtain the

– EG+ in
[

]
but to solve (

).

(b)

Variant 2.
If

(i.e.

\kappa_{2}=0
), then we obtain

, leading to the

(or equivalently,

[

]
) for solving (

(c)

Variant 3.
We can construct

,

and

for given constants

satisfies (

Clearly, when

]

) with

It costs as twice as one iteration of the forward-backward splitting method (

However, its does not require the co-coerciveness of

Now, for given

w^{k}:=Fx^{k}+\xi^{k}\quad\text{for some}\quad\xi^{k}\in Tx^{k}.

(35)

Then, the following lemmas are keys to establishing the convergence of (
GEG2
).

Lemma 4

Suppose that
T
is maximally
3
-cyclically monotone and
x^{\star}\in\mathrm{zer}(\Phi)
exists.
Let
\{(x^{k},y^{k})\}
be generated by (
GEG2
) and
w^{k}
be defined by (
35
).
Then, for any
\gamma>0
, we have

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\|x^{k+1}-x^{\star}\|^{2}&\leq&\|x^{k}-x^{\star}\|^{2}-(1-\beta)\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|^{2}-\beta\|x^{k}-y^{k}\|^{2}\\
&&-{~}(\beta-\gamma)\|x^{k+1}-y^{k}\|^{2}+\frac{\eta^{2}}{\gamma}\|Fy^{k}-u^{k}\|^{2}\\
&&-{~}2\eta\langle Fy^{k}-Fx^{\star},y^{k}-x^{\star}\rangle.\end{array}

(36)

Proof

First, since
\xi^{k+1}\in Tx^{k+1}
,
\zeta^{k}\in Ty^{k}
, and
\xi^{\star}=-Fx^{\star}\in Tx^{\star}
, by the maximally
3
-cyclic monotonicity of
T

\langle\xi^{k+1},x^{k+1}-x^{\star}\rangle+\langle\xi^{\star},x^{\star}-y^{k}\rangle+\langle\zeta^{k},y^{k}-x^{k+1}\rangle\geq 0.

This inequality leads to

\langle\xi^{k+1}-\zeta^{k},x^{k+1}-x^{\star}\rangle\geq\langle\zeta^{k}-\xi^{\star},x^{\star}-y^{k}\rangle=-\langle Fx^{\star}+\zeta^{k},y^{k}-x^{\star}\rangle.

Utilizing this estimate and the second line
x^{k}-x^{k+1}=\eta(Fy^{k}+\xi^{k+1})
of (
34
), for any
x^{\star}\in\mathrm{zer}(\Phi)
, we can derive that

\hskip-8.61108pt\begin{array}[]{lcl}\|x^{k+1}-x^{\star}\|^{2}&=&\|x^{k}-x^{\star}\|^{2}-2\langle x^{k}-x^{k+1},x^{k+1}-x^{\star}\rangle-\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|^{2}\\

&=&\|x^{k}-x^{\star}\|^{2}-2\eta\langle Fy^{k}+\zeta^{k},x^{k+1}-x^{\star}\rangle-\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|^{2}\\
&&-{~}2\eta\langle\xi^{k+1}-\zeta^{k},x^{k+1}-x^{\star}\rangle\\

&&-{~}2\eta\langle Fy^{k}-Fx^{\star},y^{k}-x^{\star}\rangle.\end{array}\hskip-8.61108pt

(37)

Next, from the first line of (
34

\eta(Fy^{k}+\zeta^{k})=\beta(x^{k}-y^{k})+\eta(Fy^{k}-u^{k})
.

\gamma>0
, we can prove that

\begin{array}[]{lcl}2\eta\langle Fy^{k}+\zeta^{k},x^{k+1}-y^{k}\rangle&=&2\beta\langle x^{k}-y^{k},x^{k+1}-y^{k}\rangle+2\eta\langle Fy^{k}-u^{k},x^{k+1}-y^{k}\rangle\\
&\geq&\beta\left[\|x^{k}-y^{k}\|^{2}+\|x^{k+1}-y^{k}\|^{2}-\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|^{2}\right]\\
&&-{~}2\eta\|Fy^{k}-u^{k}\|\|x^{k+1}-y^{k}\|\\
&\geq&\beta\|x^{k}-y^{k}\|^{2}+(\beta-\gamma)\|x^{k+1}-y^{k}\|^{2}\\
&&-{~}\beta\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|^{2}-\frac{\eta^{2}}{\gamma}\|Fy^{k}-u^{k}\|^{2}.\end{array}

Finally, substituting this estimate into (
37
), we obtain (
36
).

\square

Lemma 5

Suppose that
F
is monotone and

-cyclically monotone.

\begin{array}[]{l}{\displaystyle\min_{0\leq k\leq K}}\|Fx^{k+1}+\xi^{k+1}\|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{K+1}\sum_{k=0}^{K}\|Fx^{k+1}+\xi^{k+1}\|^{2}\leq\frac{\Lambda\|x^{0}-x^{\star}\|^{2}}{\eta^{2}(K+1)},\end{array}

(49)

where

(50)

\mathrm{(b)}

The last-iterate convergence rate of EG
.
If, in addition,

\hskip-4.30554pt\|Fx^{K+1}+\xi^{K+1}\|^{2}\leq\frac{\Lambda\|x^{0}-x^{\star}\|^{2}}{\eta^{2}(K+1)}.\hskip-8.61108pt

\mathrm{(c)}

Convergence in

.
For

\begin{array}[]{l}{\displaystyle\min_{0\leq k\leq K}}\|\mathcal{G}_{\eta}x^{k}\|=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{K}}\right)\quad\text{and}\quad\|\mathcal{G}_{\eta}x^{K}\|=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{K}}\right).\end{array}

Proof

5
A Class of Forward-Backward-Forward Splitting Methods for (
NI
)

Alternative to (
GEG2
), we now generalize the FBFS method in
[
93
]
for solving (
NI
), but when a weak-Minty solution exists.

5.1
A Class of Forward-Backward-Forward Splitting Methods

The forward-backward-forward splitting (FBFS) method was proposed by P. Tseng in
[
93
]
for solving (
NI
), which is originally called a
modified forward-backward splitting
method.

Now, we propose to consider the following generalized FBFS scheme for solving (
NI
).
Starting from
x^{0}\in\mathrm{dom}(\Phi)
, at each iteration
k\geq 0
, we update

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}y^{k}&:=&J_{\frac{\eta}{\beta}T}(x^{k}-\frac{\eta}{\beta}u^{k}),\\
x^{k+1}&:=&\beta y^{k}+(1-\beta)x^{k}-\eta(Fy^{k}-u^{k}),\end{array}\right.

(GFBFS2)

where
J_{\frac{\eta}{\beta}T}
is the resolvent of
\frac{\eta}{\beta}T
,
\eta>0
is a given stepsize, and
\beta>0
is a scaling factor, and
u^{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
satisfies the following condition:

\|Fx^{k}-u^{k}\|^{2}\leq\kappa_{1}\|Fx^{k}-Fy^{k-1}\|^{2}+\kappa_{2}\|Fx^{k}-Fx^{k-1}\|^{2},

(56)

for given constants
\kappa_{1}\geq 0
and
\kappa_{2}\geq 0
and
x^{-1}=y^{-1}:=x^{0}
.
Here, we have our flexibility to choose
u^{k}
.
Let us consider three special cases of
u^{k}
as before.

(a)

Variant 1.
If we choose
u^{k}:=Fx^{k}
, then we obtain a variant of
Tseng’s FBFS method
.
In particular, if
\beta=1
, then we get exactly
Tseng’s FBFS method
in
[
93
]
for solving (
NI
).
Note that one can extend (
GFBFS2
) to cover the case
\mathrm{zer}(\Phi)\cap\mathcal{C}\neq\emptyset
for some subset
\mathcal{C}
of
\mathbb{R}^{p}
as presented in
[
93
]
.
Nevertheless, for simplicity, we assume that
\mathcal{C}=\mathbb{R}^{p}
.
As shown in (
FBFS
), if
T=0
, then (
GFBFS2
) reduces to the extragradient method (
GEG
) for (
NE
).
However, if
T\neq 0
, then (
GFBFS2
) is different from (
GEG2
).

(b)

Variant 2.
If we choose
u^{k}:=Fy^{k-1}
, where
y^{-1}:=x^{0}
, then we obtain a
past-FBFS variant
.
This variant can also be referred to as a generalized variant of the
optimistic gradient (OG) method
, see, e.g.,
[
26
,
60
,
61
]
.
If
\beta=1
, then
y^{k+1}=J_{\eta T}(x^{k+1}-\eta Fy^{k})
and
x^{k+1}=y^{k}-\eta(Fy^{k}-Fy^{k-1})
.
Combining these two expressions, (
GFBFS2
) reduces to

\begin{array}[]{lcl}y^{k+1}&=&J_{\eta T}\left(y^{k}-\eta(2Fy^{k}-Fy^{k-1})\right).\end{array}

(FRBS2)

This is exactly the
forward-reflected-backward splitting (FRBS) method
proposed in
[
56
]
.

(c)

Variant 3.
We can form
u^{k}:=\alpha_{1}Fx^{k}+\alpha_{2}Fy^{k-1}+(1-\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})Fx^{k-1}
as an affine combination of
Fx^{k}
,
Fy^{k-1}
and
Fx^{k-1}
for given constants
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
.
Then,
u^{k}
satisfies (
56
) with
\kappa_{1}=(1+c)(1-\alpha_{1})^{2}
and
\kappa_{2}=(1+c^{-1})(1-\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})^{2}
for some
c>0
by Young’s inequality.

Compared to (
GEG2
), we do not require
T
to be monotone in (
GFBFS2
).
However, to guarantee the well-definedness of
\{(x^{k},y^{k})\}
, we need
y^{k}\in\mathrm{ran}(J_{\eta T})
and
y^{k}\in\mathrm{dom}(F)
.
Hence, we can assume that
\mathrm{ran}(J_{\eta T})\subseteq\mathrm{dom}(F)=\mathbb{R}^{p}
and
\mathrm{dom}(J_{\eta T})=\mathbb{R}^{p}
.
This requirement makes (
GFBFS2
) cover a broader class of problems than (
GEG2
), and it obviously holds if
T
is maximally monotone and
F
is partially monotone and Lipschitz continuous as in (
GEG2
).
Here, we assume that
J_{\eta T}
is single-valued, but it can be extended to multivalued
J_{\eta T}
.
In addition, (
GFBFS2
) only requires one evaluation of
J_{\eta T}
instead of two as in (
GEG2
), reducing the per-iteration complexity when
J_{\eta T}
is expensive.

Similar to (
34
), we can rewrite (
GFBFS2
) equivalently to

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}y^{k}&:=&x^{k}-\frac{\eta}{\beta}(u^{k}+\zeta^{k}),\quad\zeta^{k}\in Ty^{k},\\
x^{k+1}&:=&x^{k}+\beta(y^{k}-x^{k})-\eta(Fy^{k}-u^{k})=x^{k}-\eta(Fy^{k}+\zeta^{k}).\end{array}\right.

(57)

This representation is an important step for our convergence analysis below.

5.2
Key Estimates for Convergence Analysis

The following lemma is key to establishing convergence of (
GFBFS2
).

Lemma 6

Suppose that
\{(x^{k},y^{k})\}
is generated by (
GFBFS2
) and
T
is not necessary monotone, but
\mathrm{ran}(J_{\eta T})\subseteq\mathrm{dom}(F)=\mathbb{R}^{p}
and
\mathrm{dom}(J_{\eta T})=\mathbb{R}^{p}
.
Then, for any
\gamma>0
, any
x^{\star}\in\mathrm{zer}(\Phi)
, we have

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\|x^{k+1}-x^{\star}\|^{2}&\leq&\|x^{k}-x^{\star}\|^{2}-\beta\|x^{k}-y^{k}\|^{2}+\frac{\eta^{2}}{\gamma}\|Fy^{k}-u^{k}\|^{2}\\
&&-{~}(\beta-\gamma)\|x^{k+1}-y^{k}\|^{2}-2\eta\langle Fy^{k}+\zeta^{k},y^{k}-x^{\star}\rangle\\
&&-{~}(1-\beta)\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|^{2}.\end{array}

(58)

For any
r>0
, let us introduce the following potential function:

\hskip-4.30554pt\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathcal{P}_{k}&:=&\|x^{k}-x^{\star}\|^{2}+\tfrac{\kappa_{1}(1+r)L^{2}\eta^{2}}{r\gamma}\|x^{k}-y^{k-1}\|^{2}+\tfrac{\kappa_{2}(1+r)L^{2}\eta^{2}}{r\gamma}\|x^{k}-x^{k-1}\|^{2}.\end{array}\hskip-4.30554pt

(59)

If
u^{k}
satisfies (
56
) and there exist
x^{\star}\in\mathrm{zer}(\Phi)
and
\rho\geq 0
such that
\langle w,x-x^{\star}\rangle\geq-\rho\|w\|^{2}
for all
(x,w)\in\mathrm{gra}(\Phi)
, then for any
s>0
and
\mu\in[0,1]
, we have

\hskip-8.61108pt\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathcal{P}_{k+1}&\leq&\mathcal{P}_{k}-\left(\beta-\frac{(1+r)L^{2}\eta^{2}}{\gamma}-\frac{2\mu\rho(1+s)}{s\eta}\right)\|y^{k}-x^{k}\|^{2}\\
&&-{~}\left(\beta-\gamma-\frac{\kappa_{1}(1+r)L^{2}\eta^{2}}{r\gamma}-\frac{2\mu\rho(1+s)}{\eta}\right)\|x^{k+1}-y^{k}\|^{2}\\
&&-{~}\big(1-\beta-\frac{\kappa_{2}(1+r)L^{2}\eta^{2}}{r\gamma}-\frac{2(1-\mu)\rho}{\eta}\big)\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|^{2}.\end{array}\hskip-8.61108pt

(60)

Proof

First, combining the first and second lines of (
57
), we get
x^{k+1}=x^{k}-\beta(x^{k}-y^{k})+\eta(u^{k}-Fy^{k})=x^{k}-\eta(Fy^{k}+\zeta^{k})
.
Using this relation, we have

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\|x^{k+1}-x^{\star}\|^{2}&=&\|x^{k}-x^{\star}\|^{2}-2\eta\langle Fy^{k}+\zeta^{k},x^{k+1}-y^{k}\rangle-\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|^{2}\\
&&-{~}2\eta\langle Fy^{k}+\zeta^{k},y^{k}-x^{\star}\rangle.\end{array}

Next, from the first line of (
57
), we have
\eta(Fy^{k}+\zeta^{k})=\beta(x^{k}-y^{k})+\eta(Fy^{k}-u^{k})
.
Hence, by the Cauchy-Schwarz’s and Young’s inequality, for any
\gamma>0
, we can derive that

\begin{array}[]{lcl}2\eta\langle Fy^{k}+\zeta^{k},x^{k+1}-y^{k}\rangle&=&2\beta\langle x^{k}-y^{k},x^{k+1}-y^{k}\rangle+2\eta\langle Fy^{k}-u^{k},x^{k+1}-y^{k}\rangle\\
&\geq&\beta\left[\|x^{k}-y^{k}\|^{2}+\|x^{k+1}-y^{k}\|^{2}-\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|^{2}\right]\\
&&-{~}2\eta\|Fy^{k}-u^{k}\|\|x^{k+1}-y^{k}\|\\
&\geq&\beta\|x^{k}-y^{k}\|^{2}+(\beta-\gamma)\|x^{k+1}-y^{k}\|^{2}\\
&&-{~}\beta\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|^{2}-\frac{\eta^{2}}{\gamma}\|Fy^{k}-u^{k}\|^{2}.\end{array}

Finally, combining the last two expressions, we obtain (
58
).
The proof of (
60
) is similar to the proof of Lemma
2
, and we omit.

\square

NI

Let

be defined by (
14
), and
\underline{\eta}
and

be defined by (

Proof

\langle w,x-x^{\star}\rangle\geq 0

(x,w)\in\mathrm{gra}(\Phi)
, then the condition on
\eta
from Theorem
5.1
reduces to
0<\eta<\frac{\beta^{2}}{(1+r)L}
as we have seen in Remark
1
.
For FBFS with
u^{k}:=Fx^{k}
, we have
0<\eta<\frac{\beta^{2}}{L}
, while for past-FBFS with
u^{k}:=Fy^{k-1}
, we have
0<\eta<\frac{2\beta^{2}}{(3+\sqrt{5})L}
.
Hitherto, we have only proven the best-iterate convergence rates of (
GFBFS2
).
The last-iterate convergence rate of this method remains open.

6
Two Other Variants of The Extragradient Method

In this section, we first provide a new convergence analysis for the reflected forward-backward splitting (RFBS) algorithm
[
18
,
54
]
.
Our best-iterate convergence rate analysis is different from the one in
[
18
]
for monotone inclusions.
Our last-iterate convergence rate analysis is new, simple, and general than the one in
[
15
]
, which is only for (
VIP
).
Next, we extend the convergence analysis for the golden ratio (GR) method
[
55
]
to a wider range of its parameters.
Our new range shows a potential improvement of GR on concrete examples in Section
7
.
Nevertheless, its last-iterate convergence rate is still open.

6.1
New Convergence Analysis of The RFBS Method for (
NI
)

The RFBS method was proposed by Malitsky in
[
54
]
to solve (
VIP
) and it is called the
projected reflected gradient
method.
It was generalized to solve monotone (
NI
) in
[
18
]
.
The last iterate convergence rate of the
projected reflected gradient
method for (
VIP
) was recently proven in
[
15
]
.
Here, we provide a new and full convergence rate analysis for RFBS to solve (
NI
) by exploiting our analysis techniques in the previous sections.

6.1.1
The RFBS Method for (
NI
)

The reflected forward-backward splitting (RFBS) method to approximate a solution of (
NI
) is described as follows.
Starting from
x^{0}\in\mathrm{dom}(\Phi)
, we set
x^{-1}:=x^{0}
and at each iteration
k\geq 0
, we update

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}y^{k}&:=&2x^{k}-x^{k-1},\\
x^{k+1}&:=&J_{\eta T}(x^{k}-\eta Fy^{k}),\end{array}\right.\vskip-2.15277pt

(RFBS2)

where
\eta>0
is a given step-size, determined later.

Clearly, if we eliminate
y^{k}
, then (
RFBS2
) can be rewritten in one line:

x^{k+1}:=J_{\eta T}(x^{k}-\eta F(2x^{k}-x^{k-1})).\vskip-2.15277pt

From the second line of (
RFBS2
), we have
\xi^{k+1}:=\frac{1}{\eta}(x^{k}-\eta Fy^{k}-x^{k+1})\in Tx^{k+1}
.
As before, if we denote

w^{k}:=Fx^{k}+\xi^{k}\quad\text{and}\quad\hat{w}^{k}:=Fy^{k-1}+\xi^{k},\vskip-2.15277pt

(69)

then we can rewrite (
RFBS2
) equivalently to

\vskip-2.15277pt\left\{\begin{array}[]{lclcl}y^{k}&:=&x^{k}+x^{k}-x^{k-1}&=&x^{k}-\eta\hat{w}^{k},\\
x^{k+1}&:=&x^{k}-\eta\hat{w}^{k+1}.\end{array}\right.\vskip-2.15277pt

(70)

This expression leads to
x^{k+1}=y^{k}-\eta(\hat{w}^{k+1}-\hat{w}^{k})
.

6.1.2
Key Estimates for Convergence Analysis

Next, we prove the following lemmas for our analysis.

Lemma 7

For Inclusion (
NI
), assume that
\mathrm{zer}(\Phi)\neq\emptyset
,
T
is maximally monotone and
F
is
L
-Lipschitz continuous and satisfies
\langle Fx-Fx^{\star},x-x^{\star}\rangle\geq 0
for all
x\in\mathrm{dom}(F)
and some
x^{\star}\in\mathrm{zer}(\Phi)
.
Let
\{(x^{k},y^{k})\}
be generated by (
RFBS2
) using
\eta>0
and
\mathcal{V}_{k}
be defined as

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathcal{V}_{k}&:=&\|x^{k}-x^{\star}\|^{2}+2\|x^{k}-x^{k-1}\|^{2}+\big(1-\sqrt{2}L\eta\big)\|x^{k}-y^{k-1}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}2\eta\langle Fy^{k-1}-Fx^{\star},x^{k}-x^{k-1}\rangle.\end{array}

(71)

Then, we have

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathcal{V}_{k}&\geq&\mathcal{V}_{k+1}+\big[1-(1+\sqrt{2})L\eta\big]\left[\|y^{k}-x^{k}\|^{2}+\|x^{k}-y^{k-1}\|^{2}\right],\\
\mathcal{V}_{k}&\geq&(1-L\eta)\|x^{k}-x^{\star}\|^{2}+(1-(1+\sqrt{2})L\eta)\|x^{k}-y^{k-1}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}2(1-L\eta)\|x^{k}-x^{k-1}\|^{2}.\end{array}\vskip-2.15277pt

(72)

Proof

First, since (
RFBS2
) is equivalent to (
70
), we have
x^{k+1}-x^{k}=-\eta\hat{w}^{k+1}
from line 2 of (
70
).
Using this expression, for any
x^{\star}\in\mathrm{zer}(\Phi)
, we get

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\|x^{k+1}-x^{\star}\|^{2}&=&\|x^{k}-x^{\star}\|^{2}-2\eta\langle\hat{w}^{k+1},x^{k+1}-x^{\star}\rangle-\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|^{2}.\end{array}

(73)

Next, since
Fx^{\star}+\xi^{\star}=0
from the fact that
x^{\star}\in\mathrm{zer}(\Phi)
and
T
is monotone, it is obvious to show that
\langle\xi^{k+1},x^{k+1}-x^{\star}\rangle\geq\langle\xi^{\star},x^{k+1}-x^{\star}\rangle=-\langle Fx^{\star},x^{k+1}-x^{\star}\rangle
, where
\xi^{\star}\in Tx^{\star}
.
Using this relation, we can prove that

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\langle\hat{w}^{k+1},x^{k+1}-x^{\star}\rangle&=&\langle Fy^{k},x^{k+1}-x^{\star}\rangle+\langle\xi^{k+1},x^{k+1}-x^{\star}\rangle\\
&\geq&\langle Fy^{k}-Fx^{\star},y^{k}-x^{\star}\rangle-\langle Fy^{k}-Fx^{\star},y^{k}-x^{k+1}\rangle.\end{array}

(74)

Utilizing
y^{k}-x^{k}=x^{k}-x^{k-1}
from the first line of (
RFBS2
), we can further expand the term
\mathcal{T}_{[1]}:=\langle Fy^{k}-Fx^{\star},y^{k}-x^{k+1}\rangle
as

\hskip 0.0pt\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathcal{T}_{[1]}&:=&\langle Fy^{k}-Fx^{\star},y^{k}-x^{k+1}\rangle\\
&=&\langle Fy^{k-1}-Fx^{\star},y^{k}-x^{k}\rangle-\langle Fy^{k}-Fx^{\star},x^{k+1}-x^{k}\rangle\\
&&+{~}\langle Fy^{k}-Fy^{k-1},y^{k}-x^{k}\rangle\\
&=&\langle Fy^{k-1}-Fx^{\star},x^{k}-x^{k-1}\rangle-\langle Fy^{k}-Fx^{\star},x^{k+1}-x^{k}\rangle\\
&&+{~}\langle Fy^{k}-Fy^{k-1},y^{k}-x^{k}\rangle.\end{array}\hskip-25.83325pt

(75)

Now, from the second line of (
70
), we have
\eta\xi^{k+1}=x^{k}-x^{k+1}-\eta Fy^{k}
, leading to
\eta(\xi^{k+1}-\xi^{k})=2x^{k}-x^{k-1}-x^{k+1}-\eta(Fy^{k}-Fy^{k-1})=y^{k}-x^{k+1}-\eta(Fy^{k}-Fy^{k-1})
.
By the monotonicity of
T
, we have
\langle y^{k}-x^{k+1}-\eta(Fy^{k}-Fy^{k-1}),x^{k+1}-x^{k}\rangle=\eta\langle\xi^{k+1}-\xi^{k},x^{k+1}-x^{k}\rangle\geq 0
, leading to

\begin{array}[]{lcl}2\eta\langle Fy^{k}-Fy^{k-1},x^{k+1}-x^{k}\rangle&\leq&2\langle y^{k}-x^{k+1},x^{k+1}-x^{k}\rangle\\
&=&\|y^{k}-x^{k}\|^{2}-\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|^{2}-\|x^{k+1}-y^{k}\|^{2}.\end{array}

By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, the Lipschitz continuity of
F
, and Young’s inequality, we can show that

\begin{array}[]{lcl}2\eta\langle Fy^{k}-Fy^{k-1},y^{k}-x^{k+1}\rangle&\leq&\eta L(\sqrt{2}+1)\|y^{k}-x^{k}\|^{2}+\eta L\|x^{k}-y^{k-1}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}\sqrt{2}\eta L\|x^{k+1}-y^{k}\|^{2}.\end{array}

Summing up the last two inequalities, we get

\begin{array}[]{lcl}2\eta\langle Fy^{k}-Fy^{k-1},y^{k}-x^{k}\rangle&\leq&\left[1+\eta L(\sqrt{2}+1)\right]\|y^{k}-x^{k}\|^{2}+\eta L\|x^{k}-y^{k-1}\|^{2}\\
&&-{~}(1-\sqrt{2}L\eta)\|x^{k+1}-y^{k}\|^{2}-\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|^{2}.\end{array}

Combining this inequality, (
74
), and (
75
) with (
73
), we can prove that

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\|x^{k+1}-x^{\star}\|^{2}&\leq&\|x^{k}-x^{\star}\|^{2}+2\eta\langle Fy^{k-1}-Fx^{\star},x^{k}-x^{k-1}\rangle\\
&&+{~}\left[1+L\eta(\sqrt{2}+1)\right]\|y^{k}-x^{k}\|^{2}-2\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}L\eta\|x^{k}-y^{k-1}\|^{2}-(1-\sqrt{2}L\eta)\|x^{k+1}-y^{k}\|^{2}\\
&&-{~}2\eta\langle Fy^{k}-Fx^{\star},x^{k+1}-x^{k}\rangle-2\eta\langle Fy^{k}-Fx^{\star},y^{k}-x^{\star}\rangle.\end{array}

Using the definition (
71
) of
\mathcal{V}_{k}
,
\langle Fy^{k}-Fx^{\star},y^{k}-x^{\star}\rangle\geq 0
, and
y^{k}-x^{k}=x^{k}-x^{k-1}
, this estimate becomes

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathcal{V}_{k+1}&:=&\|x^{k+1}-x^{\star}\|^{2}+2\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|^{2}+(1-\sqrt{2}L\eta)\|x^{k+1}-y^{k}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}2\eta\langle Fy^{k}-Fx^{\star},x^{k+1}-x^{k}\rangle\\
&\leq&\|x^{k}-x^{\star}\|^{2}+\left[1+(\sqrt{2}+1)L\eta\right]\|y^{k}-x^{k}\|^{2}+L\eta\|x^{k}-y^{k-1}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}2\eta\langle Fy^{k-1}-Fx^{\star},x^{k}-x^{k-1}\rangle\\
&=&\mathcal{V}_{k}-\left[1-(\sqrt{2}+1)L\eta\right]\left(\|y^{k}-x^{k}\|^{2}+\|x^{k}-y^{k-1}\|^{2}\right),\end{array}

which proves the first inequality of (
72
).

Next, using Young’s inequality twice, we can show that

\begin{array}[]{lcl}2\eta\langle Fy^{k-1}-Fx^{\star},x^{k}-x^{k-1}\rangle&\geq&-\frac{L\eta}{2}\|y^{k-1}-x^{\star}\|^{2}-2L\eta\|x^{k}-x^{k-1}\|^{2}\\
&\geq&-L\eta\|x^{k}-x^{\star}\|^{2}-L\eta\|x^{k}-y^{k-1}\|^{2}\\
&&-{~}2L\eta\|x^{k}-x^{k-1}\|^{2}.\end{array}

Substituting this estimate into (
71
), we get

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathcal{V}_{k}&:=&\|x^{k}-x^{\star}\|^{2}+2\|x^{k}-x^{k-1}\|^{2}+\left(1-\sqrt{2}L\eta\right)\|x^{k}-y^{k-1}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}2\eta\langle Fy^{k-1}-Fx^{\star},x^{k}-x^{k-1}\rangle\\
&\geq&(1-L\eta)\|x^{k}-x^{\star}\|^{2}+(1-(1+\sqrt{2})L\eta)\|x^{k}-y^{k-1}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}2(1-L\eta)\|x^{k}-x^{k-1}\|^{2},\end{array}

which proves the second line of (
72
).

\square

Lemma 8

For Inclusion (
NI
), suppose that
\Phi
is maximally monotone and

is
L
-Lipschitz continuous.
Let

\hskip 0.0pt\begin{array}[]{lcl}&\|Fx^{k}+\xi^{k}\|^{2}\leq\frac{5L^{2}\eta^{2}+3}{3\eta^{2}}\|x^{k}-y^{k}\|^{2}+\frac{5L^{2}\eta^{2}+3}{5\eta^{2}}\|x^{k}-y^{k-1}\|^{2}.\end{array}

(76)

Moreover, if
\sqrt{2}L\eta<1
, then with
\omega:=\frac{2L^{2}\eta^{2}}{1-2L^{2}\eta^{2}}>0

\hskip-8.61108pt\begin{array}[]{lcl}\|Fx^{k+1}+\xi^{k+1}\|^{2}&+&\omega\|Fx^{k+1}-Fy^{k}\|^{2}\leq\|Fx^{k}+\xi^{k}\|^{2}+\omega\|Fx^{k}-Fy^{k-1}\|^{2}\\

(77)

x^{k}-y^{k}=\eta(Fy^{k-1}+\xi^{k})

This estimate is exactly (

).

Theorem 6.1

For Inclusion (
NI
), suppose that
\mathrm{zer}(\Phi)\neq\emptyset
,

is
L
-Lipschitz continuous, there exists
x^{\star}\in\mathrm{zer}(\Phi)
such that
\langle Fx-Fx^{\star},x-x^{\star}\rangle\geq 0

Let
\{(x^{k},y^{k})\}
be generated by (

) using

Then, we have the following statements.

\mathrm{(a)}

The
\mathcal{O}\big(1/\sqrt{k}\big)
best-iterate rate.
The following bound holds:

\hskip-21.52771pt\begin{array}[]{lcl}{\displaystyle\frac{1}{K+1}\sum_{k=0}^{K}}\|Fx^{k}+\xi^{k}\|^{2}&\leq&{\displaystyle\frac{1}{K+1}\sum_{k=0}^{K}}\left[\|Fx^{k}+\xi^{k}\|^{2}+\omega\|Fx^{k}-Fy^{k-1}\|^{2}\right]\\

The
\mathcal{O}\big(1/\sqrt{k}\big)

\Phi

(79)

where
\omega:=\frac{2L^{2}\eta^{2}}{1-2L^{2}\eta^{2}}>0
.

\|\mathcal{G}_{\eta}x^{K}\|\leq\|Fx^{K}+\xi^{K}\|=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{K}}\right)

Remark 6

We can modify (

) to capture adaptive parameters as
y^{k}:=x^{k}+\beta_{k}(x^{k}-x^{k-1})
and
x^{k+1}:=J_{\eta_{k}T}(x^{k}-\eta_{k}Fy^{k})
, where
\eta_{k}:=\beta_{k}\eta_{k-1}
for some
\beta_{k}>0
.
Then, by imposing appropriate bounds on
\eta_{k}
, we can still prove the convergence of this variant by modifying the proof of Theorem

.

) under weaker conditions (e.g., a weak-Minty solution and the co-hypomonotonicity) is still open.

6.2
New Golden Ratio Method for (
NI
)

The golden ratio (GR) method was proposed by Malitsky in
[
55
]
to solve monotone (
MVIP
), where the ratio parameter
\tau:=\frac{\sqrt{5}+1}{2}
, leading to a so-called
golden ratio
.
Here, we extend it to solve (
NI
) for the case
F
is monotone and
L
-Lipschitz continuous, and
T
is maximally
3
-cyclically monotone.
Moreover, we extend our analysis for any
\tau\in(1,1+\sqrt{3})
.

6.2.1
The GR Method for (
NI
)

The golden ratio (GR) method for solving (
NI
) is presented as follows.
Starting from
x^{0}\in\mathrm{dom}(\Phi)
, we set
y^{-1}:=x^{0}
, and at each iteration
k\geq 0
, we update

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}y^{k}&:=&\frac{\tau-1}{\tau}x^{k}+\frac{1}{\tau}y^{k-1},\\
x^{k+1}&:=&J_{\eta T}(y^{k}-\eta Fx^{k}),\end{array}\right.

(GR2+)

where
J_{\eta T}
is the resolvent of
\eta T
,
\tau>1
is given, and
\eta\in(0,\frac{\tau}{2L})
.

Let us denote
\breve{w}^{k}:=Fx^{k-1}+\xi^{k}
for
\xi^{k}\in Tx^{k}
.
Then, we can rewrite the second line of (
GR2+
) as
x^{k+1}:=y^{k}-\eta(Fx^{k}+\xi^{k+1})=y^{k}-\eta\breve{w}^{k+1}
for
\xi^{k+1}\in Tx^{k+1}
.
In this case, we have
x^{k}=y^{k-1}-\eta(Fx^{k-1}+\xi^{k})
, leading to
y^{k-1}=x^{k}+\eta\breve{w}^{k}
.
Combining this expression and the first line of (
GR2+
), we have
y^{k}=\frac{\tau-1}{\tau}x^{k}+\frac{1}{\tau}(x^{k}+\eta\breve{w}^{k})=x^{k}+\frac{\eta}{\tau}\breve{w}^{k}
.
Consequently, we can rewrite (
GR2+
) equivalently to the following one:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcllcl}y^{k}&:=&x^{k}+\frac{\eta}{\tau}\breve{w}^{k},\\
x^{k+1}&:=&y^{k}-\eta(Fx^{k}+\xi^{k+1})&=&y^{k}-\eta\breve{w}^{k+1}.\end{array}\right.

(80)

If we eliminate
y^{k}
, then we obtain

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}x^{k+1}&:=&J_{\eta T}\left(x^{k}-\eta\left(Fx^{k}-\frac{1}{\tau}(Fx^{k-1}+\xi^{k})\right)\right),\\
\xi^{k+1}&:=&\frac{1}{\eta}(x^{k}-x^{k+1})-\left(Fx^{k}-\frac{1}{\tau}(Fx^{k-1}+\xi^{k})\right).\end{array}\right.

(81)

This scheme is another interpretation of (
GR2+
).

6.2.2
Key Estimates for Convergence Analysis

The convergence of (
GR2+
) is established based on the following key lemma.

Lemma 9

3

.
Then, for any
x^{\star}\in\mathrm{zer}(\Phi)
, we have

\hskip-12.91663pt\begin{array}[]{lcl}\tau\|y^{k+1}-x^{\star}\|^{2}&+&(\tau-1)(\tau-\gamma)\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|^{2}\leq\tau\|y^{k}-x^{\star}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}\frac{(\tau-1)L^{2}\eta^{2}}{\gamma}\|x^{k}-x^{k-1}\|^{2}-\frac{(\tau-1)(1-\tau^{2}+\tau)}{\tau}\|x^{k+1}-y^{k}\|^{2}\\
&&-{~}\tau(\tau-1)\|x^{k}-y^{k}\|^{2}-2\eta(\tau-1)\langle Fx^{k}-Fx^{\star},x^{k}-x^{\star}\rangle.\end{array}\hskip-12.91663pt

(82)

Proof

Since
T
is
3
-cyclically monotone, for
\xi^{k+1}\in Tx^{k+1}
,
\xi^{k}\in Tx^{k}
, and
x^{\star}\in Tx^{\star}
, we have

\begin{array}[]{lcll}\langle\xi^{k+1},x^{k+1}-x^{\star}\rangle+\langle\xi^{\star},x^{\star}-x^{k}\rangle+\langle\xi^{k},x^{k}-x^{k+1}\rangle\geq 0.\end{array}

(83)

From (
80
), we get
\eta\xi^{k+1}=y^{k}-x^{k+1}-\eta Fx^{k}
and
\eta\xi^{k}=y^{k-1}-x^{k}-\eta Fx^{k-1}
.
Moreover, since
x^{\star}\in\mathrm{zer}(\Phi)
, we obtain
Fx^{\star}+\xi^{\star}=0
, leading to
\eta\xi^{\star}=-\eta Fx^{\star}
.
Substituting these expressions into (
83
), we can show that

\begin{array}[]{lcll}\langle y^{k}-x^{k+1}-\eta Fx^{k},x^{k+1}-x^{\star}\rangle&+&\langle y^{k-1}-x^{k}-\eta Fx^{k-1},x^{k}-x^{k+1}\rangle\\
&&-{~}\langle Fx^{\star},x^{\star}-x^{k}\rangle\geq 0.\end{array}

However, since
y^{k}:=\frac{\tau-1}{\tau}x^{k}+\frac{1}{\tau}y^{k-1}
from the first line of (
GR2+
), we get
y^{k-1}-x^{k}=\tau(y^{k}-x^{k})
.
Substituting this relation into the last inequality, rearranging the result, we obtain

\begin{array}[]{ll}&\langle y^{k}-x^{k+1},x^{k+1}-x^{\star}\rangle+\tau\langle x^{k}-y^{k},x^{k+1}-x^{k}\rangle\\
&+{~}\eta\langle Fx^{k-1}-Fx^{k},x^{k+1}-x^{k}\rangle-\eta\langle Fx^{k}-Fx^{\star},x^{k}-x^{\star}\rangle\geq 0.\end{array}

(84)

By Young’s inequality and the
L
-Lipschitz continuity of
F
, for any
\gamma>0
, we get the first line of the following:

\begin{array}[]{lcl}2\eta\langle Fx^{k-1}-Fx^{k},x^{k+1}-x^{k}\rangle&\leq&\frac{L^{2}\eta^{2}}{\gamma}\|x^{k}-x^{k-1}\|^{2}+\gamma\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|^{2},\\
2\langle y^{k}-x^{k+1},x^{k+1}-x^{\star}\rangle&=&\|y^{k}-x^{\star}\|^{2}-\|x^{k+1}-x^{\star}\|^{2}-\|x^{k+1}-y^{k}\|^{2},\\
2\langle x^{k}-y^{k},x^{k+1}-x^{k}\rangle&=&\|x^{k+1}-y^{k}\|^{2}-\|x^{k}-y^{k}\|^{2}-\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|^{2}.\end{array}

Substituting these expressions into (
84
), and rearranging the results, we obtain

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\|x^{k+1}-x^{\star}\|^{2}&\leq&\|y^{k}-x^{\star}\|^{2}+(\tau-1)\|x^{k+1}-y^{k}\|^{2}-\tau\|x^{k}-y^{k}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}\frac{L^{2}\eta^{2}}{\gamma}\|x^{k}-x^{k-1}\|^{2}-(\tau-\gamma)\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|^{2}\\
&&-{~}2\eta\langle Fx^{k}-Fx^{\star},x^{k}-x^{\star}\rangle.\end{array}

(85)

Now, using
(\tau-1)x^{k+1}=\tau y^{k+1}-y^{k}
and
\tau(y^{k+1}-y^{k})=(\tau-1)(x^{k+1}-y^{k})
from the first line of (
GR2+
), we can derive that

\begin{array}[]{lcl}(\tau-1)^{2}\|x^{k+1}-x^{\star}\|^{2}&=&\tau(\tau-1)\|y^{k+1}-x^{\star}\|^{2}-(\tau-1)\|y^{k}-x^{\star}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}\tau\|y^{k+1}-y^{k}\|^{2}\\
&=&\tau(\tau-1)\|y^{k+1}-x^{\star}\|^{2}-(\tau-1)\|y^{k}-x^{\star}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}\frac{(\tau-1)^{2}}{\tau}\|x^{k+1}-y^{k}\|^{2}.\end{array}

Simplifying this expression to get
(\tau-1)\|x^{k+1}-x^{\star}\|^{2}=\tau\|y^{k+1}-x^{\star}\|^{2}-\|y^{k}-x^{\star}\|^{2}+\frac{(\tau-1)}{\tau}\|x^{k+1}-y^{k}\|^{2}
.
Combining it and (
85
), and rearranging the result, we obtain (
82
).

\square

6.2.3
The Sublinear Best-Iterate Convergence Rate Analysis

Now, we are ready to state the convergence of (
GR2+
).

Theorem 6.2

For Inclusion (
NI
), suppose that
\mathrm{zer}(\Phi)\neq\emptyset
,
T
is maximally
3
-cyclically monotone, and

is

-Lipschitz continuous and satisfies
\langle Fx-Fx^{\star},x-x^{\star}\rangle\geq 0
for all

and some

.
Let
\{(x^{k},y^{k})\}
be generated by (
GR2+
).
Then, the following statements hold.

\mathrm{(a)}

The best-iterate rate of GR.
If
1<\tau\leq\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}
and
\eta\in\left(0,\frac{\tau}{2L}\right)
, then

\hskip-21.52771pt\begin{array}[]{lcl}{\displaystyle\frac{1}{K+1}\sum_{k=0}^{K}}\|Fx^{k}+\xi^{k}\|^{2}&\leq&{\displaystyle\frac{C_{0}}{K+1}\sum_{k=0}^{K}}(\tau-1)\left[\tau\|x^{k}-y^{k}\|^{2}+\varphi\|x^{k}-x^{k-1}\|^{2}\right]\\
&\leq&\dfrac{C_{0}\|x^{0}-x^{\star}\|^{2}}{K+1},\end{array}\hskip-21.52771pt

(86)

where
\xi^{k}\in Tx^{k}
,
\varphi:=\frac{\tau^{2}-4L^{2}\eta^{2}}{2\tau}>0
, and
C_{0}:=\frac{(\tau^{2}-2L^{2}\eta^{2})\tau}{(\tau^{2}-4L^{2}\eta^{2})\eta^{2}(\tau-1)}>0
.

\mathrm{(b)}

The best-iterate rate of (
GR2+
).
If we choose
\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}<\tau<1+\sqrt{3}
and
0<\eta<\frac{\psi}{2L}
, then

\hskip-21.52771pt\begin{array}[]{lcl}{\displaystyle\frac{1}{K+1}\sum_{k=0}^{K}}\|Fx^{k}+\xi^{k}\|^{2}&\leq&{\displaystyle\frac{1}{K+1}\sum_{k=0}^{K}}(\tau-1)\left[\psi\|x^{k}-y^{k}\|^{2}+\kappa\|x^{k}-y^{k-1}\|^{2}\right]\\
&\leq&\dfrac{\hat{C}_{0}\|x^{0}-x^{\star}\|^{2}}{K+1},\end{array}\hskip-21.52771pt

(87)

where
\psi:=\frac{2\tau+2-\tau^{2}}{\tau}>0
,
\kappa:=\frac{\psi^{2}-4L^{2}\eta^{2}}{2\psi}
, and
\hat{C}_{0}:=\frac{[\psi^{2}-2L^{2}\eta^{2}(2\tau^{2}-\psi^{2})]\tau}{(\tau-1)(\psi^{2}-4L^{2}\eta^{2})\eta^{2}\psi}
.

Proof

First, to guarantee that
1+\tau-\tau^{2}\geq 0
and
\tau>1
, we need to choose
1<\tau\leq\frac{\sqrt{5}+1}{2}
.
If
0<\eta<\frac{\tau}{2L}
, then by choosing
\gamma:=\frac{\tau}{2}
, we have
\psi:=\frac{(\tau-1)(\tau\gamma-\gamma^{2}-L^{2}\eta^{2})}{\gamma}=\frac{(\tau-1)(\tau^{2}-4L^{2}\eta^{2})}{2\tau}>0
.
Using this relation and
\langle Fx^{k}-Fx^{\star},x^{k}-x^{\star}\rangle\geq 0
, if we define
\mathcal{V}_{k}:=\tau\|y^{k}-x^{\star}\|^{2}+\frac{\tau(\tau-1)}{2}\|x^{k}-x^{k-1}\|^{2}\geq 0
, then we can deduce from (
82
) that

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathcal{V}_{k+1}&\leq&\mathcal{V}_{k}-\psi\cdot\|x^{k}-x^{k-1}\|^{2}-\tau(\tau-1)\|x^{k}-y^{k}\|^{2}.\end{array}

(88)

Next, using
y^{k}-x^{k}=\frac{\eta}{\tau}\breve{w}^{k}
and
\breve{w}^{k}=Fx^{k-1}+\xi^{k}
, by Young’s inequality, we can prove that

\hskip-12.91663pt\begin{array}[]{lcl}\|w^{k}\|^{2}&=&\|Fx^{k}+\xi^{k}\|^{2}\\
&\leq&\left(1+\frac{\psi\tau}{L^{2}\eta^{2}(\tau-1)}\right)\|Fx^{k}-Fx^{k-1}\|^{2}+\left(1+\frac{L^{2}\eta^{2}(\tau-1)}{\psi\tau}\right)\|\breve{w}^{k}\|^{2}\\
&\leq&\left(1+\frac{\psi\tau}{L^{2}\eta^{2}(\tau-1)}\right)L^{2}\|x^{k}-x^{k-1}\|^{2}+\left(1+\frac{L^{2}\eta^{2}(\tau-1)}{\psi\tau}\right)\frac{\tau^{2}}{\eta^{2}}\|x^{k}-y^{k}\|^{2}\\

(89)

Combining this estimate and (
88
), and noting that
\mathcal{V}_{k}\geq 0
, we can show that

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\sum_{l=0}^{k}\|w^{l}\|^{2}&\leq&\frac{L^{2}\eta^{2}(\tau-1)+\psi\tau}{\psi\eta^{2}(\tau-1)}\sum_{l=0}^{k}\left[\psi\cdot\|x^{l}-x^{l-1}\|^{2}+\tau(\tau-1)\|x^{l}-y^{l}\|^{2}\right]\\
&\leq&\frac{L^{2}\eta^{2}(\tau-1)+\psi\tau}{\psi\eta^{2}(\tau-1)}\left[\mathcal{V}_{0}-\mathcal{V}_{k+1}\right]\leq\frac{L^{2}\eta^{2}(\tau-1)+\psi\tau}{\psi\eta^{2}(\tau-1)}\cdot\mathcal{V}_{0}\\
&=&\frac{(\tau^{2}-2L^{2}\eta^{2})\tau}{(\tau^{2}-4L^{2}\eta)\eta^{2}(\tau-1)}\cdot\|x^{0}-x^{\star}\|^{2},\end{array}

which is exactly (
86
), where we have used
\mathcal{V}_{0}:=\tau\|y^{0}-x^{\star}\|^{2}+\frac{\tau(\tau-1)}{2}\|x^{0}-x^{-1}\|^{2}=\tau\|x^{0}-x^{\star}\|^{2}
due to
x^{-1}=y^{0}=x^{0}
.

Next, if
1.6180\approx\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}<\tau<1+\sqrt{3}\approx 2.7321
, then we have
\tau^{2}-\tau-1>0
and
\psi:=\tau-\frac{2(\tau^{2}-\tau-1)}{\tau}>0
.
In this case, using
\|x^{k+1}-y^{k}\|^{2}\leq 2\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|^{2}+2\|y^{k}-x^{k}\|^{2}
and
\langle Fx^{k}-Fx^{\star},x^{k}-x^{\star}\rangle\geq 0
into (
82
), rearranging the result, and using
\gamma:=\frac{\psi}{2}
, we can derive that

\hskip-12.91663pt\begin{array}[]{lcl}\tau\|y^{k+1}-x^{\star}\|^{2}&+&\frac{\psi(\tau-1)}{2}\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|^{2}\leq\tau\|y^{k}-x^{\star}\|^{2}+\frac{\psi(\tau-1)}{2}\|x^{k}-x^{k-1}\|^{2}\\
&&-{~}\psi(\tau-1)\|x^{k}-y^{k}\|^{2}-\frac{(\tau-1)(\psi^{2}-4L^{2}\eta^{2})}{2\psi}\|x^{k}-x^{k-1}\|^{2}.\end{array}\hskip-17.22217pt

(90)

Similar to the proof of (

\|w^{k}\|^{2}\leq\frac{\psi^{2}\tau^{2}-2L^{2}\eta^{2}(2\tau^{2}-\psi^{2})}{(\psi^{2}-4L^{2}\eta^{2})\eta^{2}\psi}\big[\frac{\psi^{2}-4L^{2}\eta^{2}}{2\psi}\|x^{k}-x^{k-1}\|^{2}+\psi\|x^{k}-y^{k}\|^{2}\big]
.
Combining this inequality and (

Our second example is the following classical bilinear matrix game problem:

\min_{u\in\mathcal{U}}\max_{v\in\mathcal{V}}\big\{\mathcal{H}(x,y):=\langle\mathbf{L}u,v\rangle\big\},

[

[

7.3
Regularized Logistic Regression with Ambiguous Features

Finally, we consider a standard regularized logistic regression model associated with a given dataset
\{(\hat{X}_{i},y_{i})\}_{i=1}^{N}
, where
\hat{X}_{i}
is an i.i.d. sample of a feature vector and
y_{i}\in\{0,1\}
is the corresponding label of
\hat{X}_{i}
.
Unfortunately,
\hat{X}_{i}
is ambiguous, i.e. it belongs to one of
m
possible examples
\{X_{ij}\}_{j=1}^{m}
.
Since we do not know which
\hat{X}_{i}
to evaluate a loss, we consider the worst-case loss
f_{i}(w)\coloneqq\max_{1\leq j\leq m}\ell(\left\langle X_{ij},w\right\rangle,y_{i})
computed from
m
examples, where
\ell(t,s)\coloneqq\log(1+\exp(t))-st
is the standard logistic loss.

Using the fact that
\max_{1\leq j\leq m}\ell_{j}(\cdot)=\max_{v\in\Delta_{m}}\sum_{j=1}^{m}v_{j}\ell_{j}(\cdot)
, where
\Delta_{m}
is the standard simplex in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
, we can model this regularized logistic regression into the following minimax problem:

\displaystyle\min_{w\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\max_{v\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}\Big\{\mathcal{L}(w,v)\coloneqq\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{m}v_{j}\ell(\left\langle X_{ij},w\right\rangle,y_{i})+\gamma R(w)-\delta_{\Delta_{m}}(v)\Big\},

(93)

where
R(w)\coloneqq\left\|w\right\|_{1}
is an
\ell_{1}
-norm regularizer used to introduce sparsity to
w
,
\gamma>0
is a regularization parameter, and
\delta_{\Delta_{m}}
is the indicator of
\Delta_{m}
that handles the constraint
v\in\Delta_{m}
.

Let us define
x\coloneqq[w;v]
,
T(x)\coloneqq\left[\gamma\partial R(w);\partial\delta_{\Delta_{m}}(v)\right]
, and

F_{i}(x)\coloneqq\Big[\sum_{j=1}^{m}v_{j}\ell^{\prime}(\left\langle X_{ij},w\right\rangle,y_{i})X_{ij};-\ell(\left\langle X_{i1},w\right\rangle,y_{i});\cdots;-\ell(\left\langle X_{im},w\right\rangle,y_{i})\Big],

where
\ell^{\prime}(t,s)=\frac{\exp(t)}{1+\exp(t)}-s
. Then, the optimality condition of (
93
) can be written as
0\in Fx+Tx
, where
F\coloneqq\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}F_{i}
.

Lemma 10

Suppose that
L\rho\leq\Delta
for
\Delta
defined by (
14
).
Then,
\underline{\eta}
and
\bar{\eta}
defined by (
15
) are well-defined and
\underline{\eta}\geq 0
and
\bar{\eta}\geq 0
.

(
a
)

If we choose
0<\beta\leq\frac{r}{r+\kappa_{2}}
, then
\underline{\eta}_{2}\leq\underline{\eta}_{1}\leq\bar{\eta}_{1}\leq\bar{\eta}_{2}
, leading to
\underline{\eta}\leq\bar{\eta}
.

(
b
)

If we choose
\frac{r}{r+\kappa_{2}}\leq\beta<1
, then
\underline{\eta}_{1}\leq\underline{\eta}_{2}\leq\bar{\eta}_{2}\leq\bar{\eta}_{1}
, leading to
\underline{\eta}\leq\bar{\eta}
.

(
c
)

If
\kappa_{2}=0
, then we can choose
\beta=1
, leading to
\underline{\eta}=\underline{\eta}_{1}\leq\bar{\eta}=\bar{\eta}_{1}
.

Moreover, in all cases above,
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
defined by (
16
) are nonnegative.

Proof

First, given
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
in (
16
), to have
C_{1}\geq 0
, we need to choose
\eta
such that
(1+r)L\eta^{2}-\beta\eta+4\mu\rho<0
.
This implies
\underline{\eta}_{1}:=\frac{\beta-\sqrt{\beta^{2}-16(1+r)\mu L\rho}}{2(1+r)L}\leq\eta\leq\bar{\eta}_{1}:=\frac{\beta+\sqrt{\beta^{2}-16(1+r)\mu L\rho}}{2(1+r)L}
, provided that
L\rho\leq\frac{\beta^{2}}{16(1+r)\mu}=\frac{\beta^{2}(r+2\kappa_{2})}{16(1+r)r}
.
This choice of
\eta
leads to the first line of (
15
).

Next, to guarantee
C_{2}\geq 0
, we need to choose
\eta
such that
\kappa_{2}\alpha L\eta^{2}-(1-\beta)\eta+2(1-\mu)\rho\leq 0
.
This implies
\underline{\eta}_{2}:=\frac{1-\beta-\sqrt{(1-\beta)^{2}-8\alpha(1-\mu)\kappa_{2}L\rho}}{2\alpha\kappa_{2}L}\leq\eta\leq\bar{\eta}_{2}:=\frac{1-\beta+\sqrt{(1-\beta)^{2}-8\alpha(1-\mu)\kappa_{2}L\rho}}{2\alpha\kappa_{2}L}
, provided that
L\rho\leq\frac{(1-\beta)^{2}}{8\alpha(1-\mu)\kappa_{2}}=\frac{(1-\beta)^{2}(r+2\kappa_{2})r}{16(1+r)\kappa_{2}^{2}}
.
This
\eta
leads to the second line of (
15
).

Now, under the choice of
\alpha
and
\mu
above, we can show that
\underline{\eta}\leq\bar{\eta}
.
Indeed, this is obvious when
\beta=1
, proving (c).

For
\beta\in(0,1)
, we define
\varphi_{1}(\eta):=(1+r)L\eta^{2}-\beta\eta+4\mu\rho
and
\varphi_{2}(\eta):=\kappa_{2}\alpha L\eta^{2}-(1-\beta)\eta+2(1-\mu)\rho
as two quadratic functions of
\eta
.

Case 1:

If we choose
0<\beta\leq\frac{r}{r+\kappa_{2}}
, then we can evaluate
\varphi_{2}(\underline{\eta}_{1})
as

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\varphi_{2}(\underline{\eta}_{1})&=&\kappa_{2}\alpha L\Big(\frac{\beta-\sqrt{\beta^{2}-16(1+r)\mu L\rho}}{2(1+r)L}\Big)^{2}-(1-\beta)\frac{\beta-\sqrt{\beta^{2}-16(1+r)\mu L\rho}}{2(1+r)L}+2(1-\mu)\rho\\
&=&\frac{[(1-\beta)r-\kappa_{2}\beta][\sqrt{\beta^{2}(r+2\kappa_{2})^{2}-16r(1+r)(r+2\kappa_{2})L\rho}-\beta(r+2\kappa_{2})]}{2r(1+r)(r+2\kappa_{2})L}\\
&\leq&0,\end{array}

provided that
L\rho\leq\Delta\leq\frac{\beta^{2}(r+2\kappa_{2})}{16r(1+r)}
.
This shows that
\underline{\eta}_{2}\leq\underline{\eta}_{1}
.

Alternatively, we can evaluate
\varphi_{2}(\bar{\eta}_{1})
as

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\varphi_{2}(\bar{\eta}_{1})&=&\kappa_{2}\alpha L\Big(\frac{\beta+\sqrt{\beta^{2}-16(1+r)\mu L\rho}}{2(1+r)L}\Big)^{2}-(1-\beta)\frac{\beta+\sqrt{\beta^{2}-16(1+r)\mu L\rho}}{2(1+r)L}+2(1-\mu)\rho\\
&=&\frac{[\kappa_{2}\beta-(1-\beta)r][\sqrt{\beta^{2}(r+2\kappa_{2})^{2}-16r(1+r)(r+2\kappa_{2})L\rho}+\beta(r+2\kappa_{2})]}{2r(1+r)(r+2\kappa_{2})L}\\
&\leq&0,\end{array}

provided that
L\rho\leq\Delta\leq\frac{\beta^{2}(r+2\kappa_{2})}{16r(1+r)}
.
This shows that
\bar{\eta}_{1}\leq\bar{\eta}_{2}
.

Combining both cases, we have
\underline{\eta}_{2}\leq\underline{\eta}_{1}\leq\bar{\eta}_{1}\leq\bar{\eta}_{2}
, and thus
\underline{\eta}\leq\bar{\eta}
.
This proves (a).

Case 2.
If we choose
\frac{r}{r+\kappa_{2}}\leq\beta<1
, then we can evaluate
\varphi_{1}(\underline{\eta}_{2})
as

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\varphi_{1}(\underline{\eta}_{2})&=&(1+r)L\Big(\frac{1-\beta-\sqrt{(1-\beta)^{2}-8\alpha(1-\mu)\kappa_{2}L\rho}}{2\alpha\kappa_{2}L}\Big)^{2}-\beta\frac{1-\beta-\sqrt{(1-\beta)^{2}-8\alpha(1-\mu)\kappa_{2}L\rho}}{2\alpha\kappa_{2}L}+4\mu\rho\\
&=&\frac{[\kappa_{2}\beta-r(1-\beta)][\sqrt{r^{2}(1-\beta)^{2}(r+2\kappa_{2})^{2}-16r(1+r)(r+2\kappa_{2})\kappa_{2}^{2}L\rho}-r(1-\beta)(r+2\kappa_{2})]}{2\kappa_{2}^{2}(1+r)(r+2\kappa_{2})L}\\
&\leq&0,\end{array}

provided that
L\rho\leq\Delta\leq\frac{r(1-\beta)^{2}(r+2\kappa_{2})}{16(1+r)\kappa_{2}^{2}}
.
This shows that
\underline{\eta}_{1}\leq\underline{\eta}_{2}
.

Similarly, we can evaluate
\varphi_{1}(\bar{\eta}_{2})
as

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\varphi_{1}(\bar{\eta}_{2})&=&(1+r)L\Big(\frac{1-\beta+\sqrt{(1-\beta)^{2}-8\alpha(1-\mu)\kappa_{2}L\rho}}{2\alpha\kappa_{2}L}\Big)^{2}-\beta\frac{1-\beta+\sqrt{(1-\beta)^{2}-8\alpha(1-\mu)\kappa_{2}L\rho}}{2\alpha\kappa_{2}L}+4\mu\rho\\
&=&\frac{[r(1-\beta)-\kappa_{2}\beta][\sqrt{r^{2}(1-\beta)^{2}(r+2\kappa_{2})^{2}-16r(1+r)(r+2\kappa_{2})\kappa_{2}^{2}L\rho}+r(1-\beta)(r+2\kappa_{2})]}{2\kappa_{2}^{2}(1+r)(r+2\kappa_{2})L}\\
&\leq&0,\end{array}

provided that
L\rho\leq\Delta\leq\frac{r(1-\beta)^{2}(r+2\kappa_{2})}{16(1+r)\kappa_{2}^{2}}
.
This shows that
\bar{\eta}_{2}\leq\bar{\eta}_{1}
.

Combining both cases, we can show that
\underline{\eta}_{1}\leq\underline{\eta}_{2}\leq\bar{\eta}_{2}\leq\bar{\eta}_{1}
, and thus
\underline{\eta}\leq\bar{\eta}
.
This proves (b).

\square

Lemma 11

Let
\underline{\eta}
and
\bar{\eta}
be defined by (
15
), and
\underline{\hat{\eta}}
and
\bar{\hat{\eta}}
be defined by (
21
).
Then, we have
[\underline{\eta},\bar{\eta}]\cap[\underline{\hat{\eta}},\bar{\hat{\eta}}]\neq\emptyset
.

Proof

When
\beta=1
,
\underline{\eta}
and
\bar{\eta}
in (
15
) become
\underline{\eta}=\frac{1-\sqrt{1-16(1+r)L\rho}}{2(1+r)L}
and
\bar{\eta}=\frac{1+\sqrt{1-16(1+r)L\rho}}{2(1+r)L}
, respectively.
Then, we consider the two following functions

\begin{array}[]{l}\phi_{1}(t):=\frac{1-\sqrt{1-16\rho t}}{2t}\quad\text{and}\quad\phi_{2}(t):=\frac{1+\sqrt{1-16\rho t}}{2t},\quad\text{for}\quad t\in\big(0,\frac{1}{16\rho}\big).\end{array}

On the one hand, we can see that
\phi^{\prime}_{1}(t)=\frac{1-8\rho t-\sqrt{(1-8\rho t)^{2}-64\rho^{2}t^{2}}}{2t^{2}\sqrt{1-16\rho t}}\geq 0
for all
t\in\big(0,\frac{1}{16\rho}\big)
, which implies that
\phi_{1}(t)
is an increasing function on
\big(0,\frac{1}{16\rho}\big)
.
On the other hand, it is obvious that
\phi_{2}(t)
is a decreasing function on
\big(0,\frac{1}{16\rho}\big)
as it is the product of two positive decreasing functions.
Thus if
1+r\leq m
, by substituting
t:=mL
and
t:=(1+r)L
into
\phi_{1}(t)
and
\phi_{2}(t)
, we have
\underline{\eta}\leq\underline{\hat{\eta}}\leq\bar{\hat{\eta}}\leq\bar{\eta}
.
Similarly, if
1+r\geq m
, we have
\underline{\hat{\eta}}\leq\underline{\eta}\leq\bar{\eta}\leq\bar{\hat{\eta}}
.
Combining both cases, we obtain
[\underline{\eta},\bar{\eta}]\cap[\underline{\hat{\eta}},\bar{\hat{\eta}}]\neq\emptyset
.

\square

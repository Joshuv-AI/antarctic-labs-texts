A note on cocycles in 𝕋 × S ​ O ​ ( 3 ) \mathbb{T}\times SO(3)
Nikolaos Karaliolios

Abstract
This short note studies C ∞ C^{\infty} -smooth cocycles in 𝕋 × S ​ O ​ ( 3 ) \mathbb{T}\times SO(3) that have 0 0 degree and are non-homotopic to constants. The study picks up from where the author’s PhD thesis left the subject, and shows that, under a relevant and full measure arithmetic condition, such cocycles can be conjugated to a simple model. Moreover, under the same arithmetic condition, the cocycle can be conjugated arbitrarily close to constant cocycles by a 2 2 -periodic conjugation.

The following arithmetic condition is a variant of the
RDC
condition, see def. 2.13 of
[

Let
\widetilde{DC}(\gamma,\tau)
be the set of
\alpha\in\mathbb{T}
such that

(3)

|k\alpha-1/2|_{\mathbb{Z}}\geq\frac{\gamma^{-1}}{|k|^{\tau}}.

A simple calculation shows that if
\alpha\in\widetilde{DC}(\gamma,\tau)
, then
2\alpha\in DC(\gamma/2,\tau)
, where

DC(\gamma,\tau)
is defined as the set of
\alpha\in\mathbb{T}
such that

(4)

|k\alpha|_{\mathbb{Z}}\geq\frac{\gamma^{-1}}{|k|^{\tau}}.

Let also
\mathrm{G}
be the Gauss map
\alpha\mapsto\{\alpha^{-1}\}
where
\{\cdot\}
stands for the fractional part.
Then, we call
\widetilde{RDC}(\gamma,\tau)
the set of
\alpha\in\mathbb{T}\setminus\mathbb{Q}
such that

\mathrm{G}^{n}(\alpha)\in\widetilde{DC}(\gamma,\tau)
for infinitely many
n

for the same reasons as
RDC
. We also note that
\alpha\in\widetilde{RDC}(\gamma,\tau)
implies
2\alpha_{n}\in DC(\gamma/2,\tau)

infinitely often, where the continued fractions are those associated to
\alpha\pmod{1}
and not to

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(\alpha,A(\cdot))
be a
C^{\infty}
-smooth cocycle in
\mathbb{T}\times SO(3)
, of
0
degree and non-homotopic to the

\mathrm{Id}
. Suppose, moreover, that
\alpha\in\widetilde{RDC}
. Then,
(\alpha,A(\cdot))
is
C^{\infty}
almost reducible by

2
-periodic conjugations.

We note that
2
-periodic conjugations arise naturally in the proof. Applying the renormalization machinery
directly to the second iterate of the cocycle avoids introducing conjugations of longer periods, because
all the obvious obstructions to almost reducibility (both homotopy and degree) vanish.
The known procedure shows that, for such a
C^{\infty}
smooth cocycle, and if
2\alpha\in RDC
, then the cocycle is
almost reducible.

In
[
2
]
, the authors proved a semi-local Almost Reducibility for cocycles over all irrational rotations
in compact groups in the real analytic category (denoted by
C^{\omega}
), see theorem 3.1 of the reference. Assuming
convergence of renormalization of
0
-degree cocycles to constant actions irrespective of homotopy, and
C^{\omega}

normalization of constant actions as in §
2

\widetilde{RDC}
arithmetic condition
of theorem
1.1
can be lifted for
C^{\omega}
smooth cocycles by coupling
the semi-local Almost Reducibility theorem with standard arguments.

The techniques developed in

The proof of theorem
1.1
will use the following proposition. It is not necessary, but obtains a normal form for
the renormalization representatives without excessive restriction with respect to the arithmetics.

Proposition 1.2
.

Let
(\alpha,A(\cdot))
be a
C^{\infty}
-smooth cocycle in
\mathbb{T}\times SO(3)
, of
0
degree and non-homotopic to the

\mathrm{Id}
. Suppose, moreover, that
\alpha\in\widetilde{RDC}
. Then,
(\alpha,A(\cdot))
has renormalization representatives

C^{\infty}
of the form

(5)

(\alpha_{n},AE_{1/2}(\cdot)\exp(\{0,z_{n}\}_{su(2)}))

where
z_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
can be arbitrarily small, and
A
:

1.

anti-commutes with
E_{1/2}(\cdot)
:
Ad(A).E_{1/2}(\cdot)=E_{1/2}(-\cdot)
, and

2.

acts on
z_{n}
by complex conjugation:

Ad(A).\{0,z_{n}\}_{su(2)}=\{0,\bar{z}_{n}\}_{su(2)}

This shows that
0
degree cocycles that are non-homotopic to the
\mathrm{Id}
form, under a full measure condition on the
frequency, a one-parameter family in a way that is analogous to the rigidity of regular cocycles in compact groups,
see §8 of
[
3
]
. We will refer to the expression of eq. (
5
) as a normal form.

The second iterate of the renormalization representative of cocycles satisfying the assumptions of the theorem
is a cocycle with a closed and remarkably simple form. The proof is by direct calculation.

Corollary 1.3
.

The second iterate of the normal form of eq. (
5
) is
of the form

(6)

(2\alpha_{n},E_{1/2}(\alpha)\exp(\{0,\bar{z}_{n}\}_{su(2)}).\exp(\{0,e^{2i\pi\cdot}z_{n}\}_{su(2)}))

with
z_{n}
arbitrarily small.

We remind that or the purpose of proving
theorem
1.1
, closeness to constants is sufficient and does not require this closed form expression.
Closeness to constants follows directly from the convergence of renormalization to constant actions and the uniformity
of the normalization operator and the fact that the second iterate is studied, without any need for longer iterations.

This form is close to the KAM-normal form as it was used in §4 of
[
4
]
. The difference
is in the term
\exp(\{0,\bar{z}_{n}\}_{su(2)})
which is non-diagonal, and, as a consequence, the reduction scheme
of the reference can be applied with explicit calculations only once in order to reduce the cocycle to a perturbation with
Fourier spectrum in
[-2,2]
and size
O(|z_{n}|^{2})
. In the absence of the term

\exp(\{0,\bar{z}_{n}\}_{su(2)})
, the secondd iterate would actually be
C^{\infty}

reducible with an explicit conjugation. In any case, the following corollary is immediate.

Corollary 1.4
.

Such a cocycle admits infinitely many renormalization representatives whose second iterate is almost reducible by
sequences of
1
-periodic conjugations.

Assuming proposition
1.2
, we can now prove theorem
1.1
.

Proof of theorem
1.1
.

By proposition
1.2
, the cocycles satisfying the hypotheses of the proposition admit renormalization
representatives in Normal Form. Suppose that this can be achieved after one step of renormalization, the general
case following directly. Then, the action associated to the cocycles itself, i.e.

(7)

\Phi=\begin{pmatrix}(1,\mathrm{Id})\\
(\alpha,A(\cdot))\end{pmatrix}

is transformed by the
SL(2,\mathbb{Z})
matrix

(8)

\mathrm{CF}(\alpha)=\begin{pmatrix}a&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\lfloor\alpha^{-1}\rfloor&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}

to the action

(9)

\mathcal{R}_{1}\Phi=\mathrm{CF}(\alpha)_{*}\Phi=\begin{pmatrix}(\alpha,A(\cdot))\\
(1-a\alpha,A_{-a}(\cdot))\end{pmatrix}

We note that we have omitted the change of scale for simplicity. Let
B:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow SO(3)
be the conjugation that
normalizes the action
\mathcal{R}_{1}\Phi
to the normal form, denoted by
\mathrm{NF}(\cdot)
:

(10)

\mathrm{Conj}_{B}\mathcal{R}_{1}\Phi=\begin{pmatrix}(\alpha,\mathrm{Id})\\
(1-a\alpha,\mathrm{NF}(\cdot))\end{pmatrix}

where
B(\cdot+\alpha).A(\cdot).B^{*}(\cdot)=\mathrm{Id}
,
\mathrm{NF}(\cdot)
is
\alpha
-periodic, and, actually, by a simple calculation,

(11)

NF(\cdot)=B(\cdot+1-n\alpha)B^{*}(\cdot-n\alpha)=B(\cdot+1)B^{*}(\cdot)

This last expression can be verified, as a sanity check, to be
\alpha
-periodic as a consequence of the
1
-periodicity
of
A(\cdot)
and the normalization property of
B(\cdot)
.

By corollary
1.4
, the cocycle

(12)

(2-2a\alpha,\mathrm{NF}_{2}(\cdot))=(2-2a\alpha,\mathrm{NF}(\cdot+1-a\alpha)\mathrm{NF}(\cdot))=(2-2a\alpha,\mathrm{NF}(\cdot+1)\mathrm{NF}(\cdot))

is almost reducible by
\alpha
-periodic conjugations.
Suppose that
D_{m}(\cdot)
is such a conjugation satisfying

(13)

D_{m}(\cdot+2-2a\alpha)\mathrm{NF}_{2}(\cdot)D_{m}^{*}(\cdot)=A_{m}\exp(U_{m}(\cdot))

with
U_{m}(\cdot)
arbitrarily small. By the
\alpha
-periodicity of
D_{m}(\cdot)
, it also holds that

(14)

D_{m}(\cdot+2)\mathrm{NF}_{2}(\cdot)D_{m}^{*}(\cdot)=A_{m}\exp(U_{m}(\cdot))

Consider, now, the action

(15)

\mathrm{Conj}_{D_{m}.B}\mathcal{R}_{1}\Phi=\begin{pmatrix}(\alpha,\mathrm{Id})\\
(1-a\alpha,D_{m}(\cdot+1)\mathrm{NF}(\cdot)D_{m}^{*}(\cdot))\end{pmatrix}

Inversion of the first step of renormalization yields the action

(16)

\mathrm{CF}(\alpha)^{*}\mathrm{Conj}_{D_{m}.B}\mathcal{R}_{1}\Phi=\begin{pmatrix}(1,D_{m}(\cdot+1)\mathrm{NF}(\cdot)D_{m}^{*}(\cdot))\\
(\alpha,\mathrm{Id})\end{pmatrix}

Passing on to two-periodic actions gives the action generated by

(17)

\begin{pmatrix}(2,D_{m}(\cdot+2)\mathrm{NF}_{2}(\cdot)D_{m}^{*}(\cdot))\\
(\alpha,\mathrm{Id})\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}(2,A_{m}\exp(U_{m}(\cdot)))\\
(\alpha,\mathrm{Id})\end{pmatrix}

This action can be normalized to an action that is arbitrarily close to a constant, thus proving the theorem.

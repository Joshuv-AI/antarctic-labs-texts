Introduction

Let
\displaystyle{F}\!
denote the Fourier transform (on Schwartz distributions).

The Barron space
\displaystyle\mathsf{B}^{1}=\{{F}\{\phi\}:\phi,\lvert\cdot\rvert\phi\in\ell_{1}(\mathds{R}^{n})\}
has been heralded as a set of functions that can be efficiently approximated by shallow neural networks, i.e., functions of the form

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathds{R}^{n}\ni x\mapsto\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_{j}\rho(\langle w_{j}\mid x\rangle-b_{j})\qquad(a_{j}\in\mathbb{C},b_{j}\in\mathds{R},\rho:\mathds{R}\to\mathds{R},w_{j}\in\mathds{R}^{n})

in the sense that shallow neural networks of the above form can approximate Barron functions with an accuracy rate of
\displaystyle m^{-1/2}

[
18

\displaystyle m

\displaystyle n
suggests that the Barron space may defy the curse of dimensionality, a piece of mathematical folklore which says that accuracy rates

are often of the form

\displaystyle\displaystyle(\text{number of terms})^{-(\text{smoothness})/(\text{dimension})}

which looks like
\displaystyle m^{-(\text{smoothness})/n}
in the case of the Barron space. So, unless the Barron space has approximately
\displaystyle n/2
“units of smoothness”, it defies the curse

of dimensionality. Barron functions indeed need not have anywhere near
\displaystyle n/2

units of classical smoothness (an example is given in Appendix
A
) so they do

defy the curse of dimensionality where classical smoothness is concerned, but

are we sure classical smoothness is the most appropriate notion of smoothness when it comes to (shallow) neural networks?

A remark before we continue. The term “Barron space” is ambiguous within the literature and may refer to either of two related yet distinct function spaces: the one we refer to as the Barron space or a space

Lemma 6.4
.

\displaystyle f\in\mathsf{L},
then
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}^{-1}\Bigl\{N_{\ell}^{\alpha}\mathcal{M}_{0}\{f_{\ell}(\vartheta,\cdot)\}\Bigr\}=\partial^{\alpha}G_{\ell}(\vartheta,\cdot).

Proof.

If
\displaystyle\ell\notin L,
this is the content of Corollary
4.18
, as
\displaystyle G_{\ell}\in\ell_{1,\infty}(0)\Rightarrow G_{\ell}(\vartheta,\cdot)\in

\displaystyle\ell_{\infty}(0,\infty)\subset\mathsf{PS}^{\prime}
for almost every
\displaystyle\vartheta.
If
\displaystyle\ell\in L
instead, (
7
) and (

) yield that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\partial^{\alpha}G_{\ell}(\vartheta,\cdot)=\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}^{-1}\biggl\{\frac{\Gamma(1-iy)}{\Gamma(1-iy-\alpha)}\mathcal{M}_{0}\{G_{\ell}(\vartheta,\cdot)\}[y]\biggr\}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}^{-1}\biggl\{\frac{-iy\Gamma(-iy)}{\Gamma(1-iy-\alpha)}N_{\ell}(y)\mathcal{M}_{0}\{F_{\ell}(\vartheta,\cdot)\}[y]\biggr\}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}^{-1}\biggl\{\frac{\Gamma(-iy)}{\Gamma(1-iy-\alpha)}N_{\ell}(y)\mathcal{M}_{0}\{f_{\ell}(\vartheta,\cdot)\}[y]\biggr\}=\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}^{-1}\Bigl\{N_{\ell}^{\alpha}\mathcal{M}_{0}\{f_{\ell}(\vartheta,\cdot)\}\Bigr\}

It follows that
\displaystyle\partial^{\alpha}G_{\ell}(\vartheta,\cdot)=G_{\ell}^{\alpha}(\vartheta,\cdot)\in\ell_{1,\infty}(\underline{d\vartheta},\mathds{R})
and hence
\displaystyle\partial^{\alpha}h_{\ell}(\vartheta,t)=h_{\ell}^{\alpha}(\vartheta,t)
for
\displaystyle t>0.
If
\displaystyle t<0,
then
\displaystyle\partial^{\alpha}h_{\ell}(\vartheta,t)=\partial^{\alpha}[(-1)^{\ell}G_{\ell}(\vartheta,-t)]=(-1)^{\ell+\alpha}G_{\ell}^{\alpha}(\vartheta,-t)=

\displaystyle h_{\ell}^{\alpha}(\vartheta,t)
as well, so
\displaystyle\partial^{\alpha}h_{\ell}(\vartheta,\cdot)=h_{\ell}^{\alpha}(\vartheta,\cdot).

Let
\displaystyle\phi^{\circ}(u)\coloneq\lvert u\rvert^{\alpha}\phi(u)\in\ell_{1}(du).
Then

\displaystyle\displaystyle h_{\ell}^{\alpha}(\vartheta,t)=\partial^{\alpha}h_{\ell}(\vartheta,t)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\partial^{\alpha}\uprightintop\displaylimits\phi(u)Z_{\ell}(\vartheta,\hat{u})\cis_{\ell}(t\lvert u\rvert)\,du

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\uprightintop\displaylimits\phi(u)Z_{\ell}(\vartheta,\hat{u})\partial^{\alpha}[\cis_{\ell}(t\lvert u\rvert)]\,du

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\uprightintop\displaylimits\phi^{\circ}(u)Z_{\ell}(\vartheta,\hat{u})\cis_{\ell}(t\lvert u\rvert+\alpha\tfrac{\pi}{2})\,du

where the exchange of differentiation and integration is justified by dominated convergence.

We claim
\displaystyle\lVert h^{\alpha}\rVert_{1,\infty}\leqslant\lVert\phi^{\circ}\rVert_{1}.
If
\displaystyle\alpha\equiv 0\,\%\,4,
the argument is the same as in the proof of Theorem
5.3
, and the reasoning is analogous when
\displaystyle\alpha\not\equiv 0\,\%\,4;
e.g., if
\displaystyle\alpha\equiv 1\,\%\,4,
the parity of
\displaystyle h_{\ell}^{\alpha}(\vartheta,\cdot)
is opposite the parity of
\displaystyle\ell,
so

\displaystyle\displaystyle h_{\ell}^{\alpha}(\vartheta,t)=-\uprightintop\displaylimits\phi_{e}^{\circ}(u)Z_{\ell}(\vartheta,\hat{u})\sin(t\lvert u\rvert)\,du

for even
\displaystyle\ell,
while for odd
\displaystyle\ell
the corresponding formula reads

\displaystyle\displaystyle h_{\ell}^{\alpha}(\vartheta,t)=i\uprightintop\displaylimits\phi_{o}^{\circ}(u)Z_{\ell}(\vartheta,\hat{u})\cos(t\lvert u\rvert)\,du

Like before,
\displaystyle\textstyle\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}R^{\ell}\lVert h_{\ell}^{\alpha}\rVert_{1,\infty}<\infty
for
\displaystyle R\in(0,1),
so
\displaystyle h_{R}^{\alpha}(\vartheta,t)\coloneq\textstyle\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}R^{\ell}h_{\ell}^{\alpha}(\vartheta,t)=

\displaystyle\displaystyle i\sum_{2\nmid\ell}R^{\ell}\uprightintop\displaylimits\phi_{o}^{\circ}(u)Z_{\ell}(\vartheta,\hat{u})\cos(t\lvert u\rvert)\,du

\displaystyle\displaystyle-\sum_{2\mid\ell}R^{\ell}\uprightintop\displaylimits\phi_{e}^{\circ}(u)Z_{\ell}(\vartheta,\hat{u})\sin(t\lvert u\rvert)\,du

\displaystyle\displaystyle=i\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}R^{\ell}\uprightintop\displaylimits\phi_{o}^{\circ}(u)Z_{\ell}(\vartheta,\hat{u})\cos(t\lvert u\rvert)\,du

\displaystyle\displaystyle-\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}R^{\ell}\uprightintop\displaylimits\phi_{e}^{\circ}(u)Z_{\ell}(\vartheta,\hat{u})\sin(t\lvert u\rvert)\,du

\displaystyle\displaystyle\overset{\text{F}}{=}i\uprightintop\displaylimits\phi_{o}^{\circ}(u)p(\hat{u},R\vartheta)\cos(t\lvert u\rvert)\,du

\displaystyle\displaystyle-\uprightintop\displaylimits\phi_{e}^{\circ}(u)p(\hat{u},R\vartheta)\sin(t\lvert u\rvert)\,du

Mimicking Lemma
5.8

\displaystyle\phi^{\circ}

From Fractionalization to Chiral Topological Superconductivity in Flat Chern Band
Daniele Guerci, Ahmed Abouelkomsan, Liang Fu

Abstract
We show that interacting electrons in a flat Chern band can form, in addition to fractional Chern insulators, a chiral f f -wave topological superconductor that hosts neutral Majorana fermion edge modes. Superconductivity emerges from an interaction-induced metallic state that exhibits anomalous Hall effect, as observed in rhombohedral graphene and near the ν = 2 3 \nu=\frac{2}{3} fractional Chern insulator in twisted transition metal dichalcogenides.

While narrow bandwidth generally enhances interaction effects, the nontrivial Bloch wavefunctions in topological bands drive interesting physics beyond the canonical Hubbard model.

First, Coulomb interaction tends to drive spontaneous ferromagnetism and lift the degeneracy between conjugate Chern bands. Importantly, ferromagnetism occurs not only at integer band fillings

Notably, the FCI states observed so far exhibit the same quantized Hall plateaus as the celebrated Jain sequence of fractional quantum Hall states. This fact suggests some level of similarity between the underlying Chern band and the lowest Landau level. However, unlike Landau levels, a generic Chern band has non-uniform Berry curvature and quantum metric in the Brillouin zone, which can be experimentally tuned for example by the twist angle

In this work, we theoretically discover chiral superconductivity from purely repulsive electron interaction in flat Chern bands, even when electron’s kinetic energy is
completely

Remarkably, we find that superconductivity and fractionalization can coexist in the same system, either at different filling factors, or at the same filling factor with the transition between the two tuned by the geometry of the underlying Chern band.

Our findings are obtained from a combination of analytical and numerically exact many-body calculation.
Our Chern band model is motivated by and relevant to twisted transition metal dichalcogenides. Intriguing signatures of chiral superconductivity have been observed in rhombohedral graphene in the absence of FQAH effect
Han et al. 2025

Discussion—

In this work, we have investigated the interacting phase diagram of flat Chern bands tuned by inhomogeneous band geometry. We provided both numerical and analytical evidence for the emergence of chiral topological
f
-wave superconductivity around and
including

\nu=2/3

{\boldsymbol{k}}

This superconducting phase can be understood from the pairing instability of a Fermi liquid parent state, which is an anomalous Hall metal with a singly-connected Fermi surface. This is crucially distinct from recently proposed scenario of superconductivity of doped anyons in a fractional Chern insulator

\mathcal{H}^{\rm int}_{{\boldsymbol{k}},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{\prime}}=-v_{1}^{2}D(0)\ell^{2}(\mathcal{F}^{+}_{\boldsymbol{k}})^{\dagger}\mathcal{F}^{+}_{{\boldsymbol{k}}^{\prime}}.

(32)

Here,
\mathcal{F}^{+}_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}=k^{3}_{+}d_{-\bm{k}}d_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}
denotes the chiral
f
-wave pair operator with relative momentum
{\boldsymbol{k}}
and vanihsing center of mass momentum, and the coupling constant
v_{1}^{2}D(0)\ell^{2}
.

The resulting Bogoliubov-de Gennes Hamiltonian reads:

\mathcal{H}_{\rm BdG}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}\Psi^{\dagger}_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}\begin{pmatrix}\xi_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}&\Delta k^{3}_{-}\\
\Delta k^{3}_{+}&-\xi_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}\end{pmatrix}\Psi_{{\boldsymbol{k}}},

(33)

where we have introduced the constant:

\Delta=-v_{1}^{2}D(0)\ell^{2}\sum_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}k^{3}_{+}\langle d_{-\bm{k}}d_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}\rangle,

(34)

and the Nambu spinor
\Psi_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}=(d_{{\boldsymbol{k}}},\,d^{\dagger}_{-{\boldsymbol{k}}})^{T}
.
We introduce the quasiparticle operators
\alpha_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}=U_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}d_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}-V_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}d^{\dagger}_{-{\boldsymbol{k}}}
, with
|U_{\boldsymbol{k}}|^{2}+|V_{\boldsymbol{k}}|^{2}=1
, such that
\{\alpha_{{\boldsymbol{k}}},\alpha^{\dagger}_{{\boldsymbol{k}}^{\prime}}\}=\delta_{{\boldsymbol{k}},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{\prime}}
.
The BCS ground state, defined as the vacuum of the quasiparticles
\alpha_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}|\Psi\rangle=0
, reads:

|\Psi\rangle=\prod^{\prime}_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}\alpha_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}\alpha_{-{\boldsymbol{k}}}|0\rangle=\prod^{\prime}_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}\left[U_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}+V_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}d^{\dagger}_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}d^{\dagger}_{-{\boldsymbol{k}}}\right]|0\rangle.

(35)

Here, the prime on the product indicates that each distinct pair
({\boldsymbol{k}},-{\boldsymbol{k}})
is counted only once, and the last equality holds up to a normalization constant.

The coefficients
U_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}
and
V_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}
are obtained solving the quasiparticle equation
[\alpha_{{\boldsymbol{k}}},\mathcal{H}_{\rm BdG}]=E({\boldsymbol{k}})\alpha_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}
, which yields:

\begin{split}&E({\boldsymbol{k}})U_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}=\xi_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}U_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}-\Delta({\boldsymbol{k}})^{*}V_{{\boldsymbol{k}}},\\
&E({\boldsymbol{k}})V_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}=-\xi_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}V_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}-\Delta({\boldsymbol{k}})U_{{\boldsymbol{k}}},\end{split}

(36)

where
\Delta({\boldsymbol{k}})=\Delta k^{3}_{-}
.
By solving these equations one readily finds:

|U_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}|^{2}=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\xi_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}}{2E({\boldsymbol{k}})},\quad|V_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}|^{2}=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\xi_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}}{2E({\boldsymbol{k}})},\quad V_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}=-\frac{E({\boldsymbol{k}})-\xi_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}}{\Delta({\boldsymbol{k}})^{*}}U_{{\boldsymbol{k}}},\quad E({\boldsymbol{k}})=\sqrt{\xi^{2}_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}+|\Delta({\boldsymbol{k}})|^{2}}.

(37)

Due to the Pauli exclusion principle,
\left(d^{\dagger}_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}\right)^{2}=0
, the BCS ground state can be expressed as:

|\Psi\rangle=\prod_{\boldsymbol{k}}|U_{\boldsymbol{k}}|^{1/2}\exp\left(\sum_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}G_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}d^{\dagger}_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}d^{\dagger}_{-{\boldsymbol{k}}}/2\right)|0\rangle,

(38)

where the product is now extended to all
{\boldsymbol{k}}
-points, which also accounts for the square root in
|U_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}|
and the prefactor
1/2
in the exponent. We have introduced
G_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}=\frac{V_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}}{U_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}}=-\frac{E({\boldsymbol{k}})-\xi_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}}{\Delta({\boldsymbol{k}})^{*}}
.
The operator at exponent of Eq. (
38
):

b^{\dagger}_{{\boldsymbol{q}}=0}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}G_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}d^{\dagger}_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}d^{\dagger}_{-{\boldsymbol{k}}},

(39)

defines the two-body Cooper pair at zero momentum, which is macroscopically occupied in the ground state.
The real-space Cooper pair wavefunction can be obtained as:

G({\boldsymbol{r}})=\matrixelement{0}{\psi(\r)\psi(\r'=0)b^\dagger_{\q=0}}{0}=\frac{1}{A}\sum_{{\boldsymbol{p}}}e^{i{\boldsymbol{p}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{r}}}G_{{\boldsymbol{p}}}=\frac{1}{\Delta}\int\frac{d^{2}{\boldsymbol{p}}}{(2\pi)^{2}}e^{i{\boldsymbol{p}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{r}}}\frac{\xi_{{\boldsymbol{p}}}-E({\boldsymbol{p}})}{(p_{x}+ip_{y})^{3}},

(40)

where
\psi({\boldsymbol{r}})
is a fermionic field annihilation operator at
{\boldsymbol{r}}
and in the last step we took the continuum limit.
From the latter expression we readily realize that:

G(R_{\theta}{\boldsymbol{r}})=e^{-3i\theta}G(\bm{r}),

(41)

consistent with the angular momentum behavior with
L^{z}=+3
, in our notation (
25
). Moreover, performing the angular integral we find;

G({\boldsymbol{r}})=i\Delta\frac{e^{-3i\phi}}{2\pi}\int^{\infty}_{0}dp\frac{p^{4}}{(p^{2}/2m-\mu)+\sqrt{\Delta^{2}p^{6}+(p^{2}/2m-\mu)^{2}}}J_{3}(pr),

(42)

where
\phi
is the polar angle of
{\boldsymbol{r}}
measured from the
x

J_{3}(pr)
is the Bessel function of the first kind.
The latter integral is dominated by the Fermi surface contribution
p_{F}=\sqrt{2m\mu}
and, for large distance
k_{F}r\gg 1

G({\boldsymbol{r}})\propto 1/(x+iy)^{3}

C.1.1
Linearised gap equation

The linearized gap equation reads:

\Delta(\bm{p})=-\frac{T}{A}\sum_{i\omega_{n}}\sum_{\bm{k}}\tilde{\Gamma}_{\bm{p},\bm{k}}G(\bm{k},i\omega_{n})\Delta(\bm{k})G(-\bm{k},-i\omega_{n}),

(43)

where the pairing gap reads

\Delta({\boldsymbol{p}})=-\sum_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}\tilde{\Gamma}_{{\boldsymbol{p}},{\boldsymbol{k}}}\langle d_{-{\boldsymbol{k}}}d_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}\rangle,

(44)

G(p)=(i\omega_{n}-\xi_{\boldsymbol{p}})^{-1}
the quasiparticle Green’s function,
\xi_{\boldsymbol{p}}={\boldsymbol{p}}^{2}/2m-\mu
and
\omega_{n}=(2n+1)\pi k_{B}T
.
Performing the integral over Matsubara frequencies we find:

\Delta({\boldsymbol{p}})=-\frac{1}{A}\sum_{\bm{k}}\tilde{\Gamma}_{{\boldsymbol{p}},{\boldsymbol{k}}}\frac{\tanh\xi_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}/(2k_{B}T)}{2\xi_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}}\Delta({\boldsymbol{k}}).

(45)

The latter expression can be expressed as an eigenvalue equation for the pairing kernel
\mathcal{M}_{{\boldsymbol{k}},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{\prime}}=-I_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}\tilde{\Gamma}_{{\boldsymbol{k}},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{\prime}}I_{{\boldsymbol{k}}^{\prime}}/A
with
I_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}=\sqrt{\frac{\tanh(\xi_{\k}/(2k_B T))}{2\xi_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}}}
and eigenvectors
\phi({\boldsymbol{k}})=\Delta({\boldsymbol{k}})I_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}
.

G. Letac, M. Piccioni, J. Wesołowski

Abstract

In particular, the map denoted there by H I ​ I ​ I , B ( α , β ) H_{III,B}^{(\alpha,\beta)} , see ( 1.4 ), was connected to the independence preserving property of the GIG distributions on ( 0 , ∞ ) (0,\infty) . 1.
INTRODUCTION

Recall that a random variable
X
has the generalized inverse Gaussian distribution,
\mathrm{GIG}(\lambda,\alpha,\beta)
, where
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and
\alpha,\beta\in(0,\infty)
, if its density is of the form

f(x)\propto x^{\lambda-1}\,e^{-\alpha x-\frac{\beta}{x}}\,\mathbf{1}_{(0,\infty)}(x).

Denote by
\Omega^{(r)}
the Euclidean space of symmetric
r\times r
matrices, with the inner product
\langle x,y\rangle=\mathrm{tr}(xy)
,
x,y\in\Omega^{(r)}
. By
\Omega^{(r)}_{+}
denote the cone of positive definite elements of
\Omega^{(r)}
.
Since
r
will be fixed in this paper we skip it in further notation, writing
\Omega
and
\Omega_{+}
instead. In this paper we will consider the matrix variate counterpart of
\mathrm{GIG}
distribution,
\mathrm{MGIG}(\lambda,a,b)
, where
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
,
a,b\in\Omega_{+}
, which is defined by the density

(1.1)

f(x)\propto(\det x)^{\lambda-\frac{r+1}{2}}\,e^{-\langle a,x\rangle-\langle b,x^{-1}\rangle}\,\mathbf{1}_{\Omega_{+}}(x).

The story of the problem we are interested in starts in the late 90’s of the last century, when Matsumoto and Yor, while studying the conditional structure of the exponential Brownian motion, discovered the following independence preserving property for the GIG and gamma laws: If
(X,Y)\sim\mathrm{GIG}(-\lambda,\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\otimes\mathrm{Gamma}(\lambda,\gamma_{1})
and

(1.2)

(U,V)=\left(\tfrac{1}{X+Y},\,\tfrac{1}{X}-\tfrac{1}{X+Y}\right)

then
(U,V)\sim\mathrm{GIG}(-\lambda,\gamma_{2},\gamma_{1})\otimes\mathrm{Gamma}(\lambda,\gamma_{2})
. Here
\mathrm{Gamma}(\lambda,\gamma)
distribution,
\lambda,\gamma>0
, is defined by the density
f(x)\propto x^{\lambda-1}e^{-\gamma x}\mathbf{1}_{(0,\infty)}(x)
. The result appeared in
[
24
]
.

A matrix variate version of the MY property was presented in
[
18
]
. The role of the Gamma law was taken over by the Wishart distribution
\mathrm{W}(\lambda,c)
,
\lambda>\tfrac{r-1}{2}
,
c\in\Omega_{+}
, defined by the density
f(x)\propto(\det\,x)^{\lambda-\frac{r+1}{2}}\,e^{-\langle c,x\rangle}\,\mathbf{1}_{\Omega_{+}}(x)
. Actually, the
\mathrm{W}(\lambda,c)
is defined for
\lambda\in\Lambda=\left\{0,\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{2}{2},\ldots,\tfrac{r-1}{2}\right\}\cup\left(\tfrac{r-1}{2},\infty\right)
, but for
\lambda\leq\tfrac{r-1}{2}
it does not have a density and is defined on the closure
\bar{\Omega}_{+}
of
\Omega_{+}
. The property reads: let
\lambda\in\Lambda
and
a,b\in\Omega_{+}
. If
(X,Y)\sim\mathrm{MGIG}(-\lambda,a,b)\otimes\mathrm{W}(\lambda,a)
and

(1.3)

(U,V)=\left((X+Y)^{-1},\,X^{-1}-(X+Y)^{-1}\right)

then
(U,V)\sim\mathrm{MGIG}(-\lambda,b,a)\otimes\mathrm{W}(\lambda,b)
. In the same paper the authors proved a characterization of MGIG and Wishart distribution by the above independence preserving property, under the assumption that
X
and
Y
have densities which are of class
C_{2}
and are strictly positive on
\Omega_{+}
.

Let us come back to the univariate case. An unexpected connection of the MY property with the discrete Korteweg de Vries (KdV) integrable model, was discovered by Croydon and Sasada in
[
8
]
. These authors observed that if
(X,Y)\sim\mathrm{GIG}(-\lambda,\alpha\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\otimes\mathrm{GIG}(-\lambda,\beta\gamma_{2},\gamma_{1})
for
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
,
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}>0
, and

(1.4)

(U,V)=H_{III,B}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(X,Y)=\left(Y\tfrac{1+\beta XY}{1+\alpha XY},\,X\tfrac{1+\alpha XY}{1+\beta XY}\right),

then
(U,V)\sim\mathrm{GIG}(-\lambda,\alpha\gamma_{2},\gamma_{1})\otimes\mathrm{GIG}(-\lambda,\beta\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})
, relating this property in the nontrivial case of
\alpha\neq\beta
to the modified discrete KdV model on
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. Here we used the symbol
H_{III,B}^{(\alpha,\beta)}
following Sasada and Uozumi,
[
27
]
, who identified this transformation as belonging to a hierarchy of parametric quadrirational Yang-Baxter map (YB) preserving independence property - see (
1.8
) for the definition of YB maps.

It is elementary to check that if
W\sim\mathrm{GIG}(\kappa,p,q)
then
\tfrac{1}{W}\sim\mathrm{GIG}(-\kappa,q,p)
. Consequently, writing
1/Y
instead of
Y
and
1/V
instead of
V
in (
1.4
) one obtains another presentation of the same independence property, getting a form which is more convenient for the purposes of the present paper:

If
(X,Y)\sim\mathrm{GIG}(-\lambda,\alpha\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\otimes\mathrm{GIG}(\lambda,\gamma_{1},\beta\gamma_{2})
and

(1.5)

\displaystyle(U,V)=

\displaystyle\left(\tfrac{\beta X+Y}{Y(\alpha X+Y)},\,\tfrac{\beta X+Y}{X(\alpha X+Y)}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\tfrac{\beta}{\alpha}Y^{-1}+\left(1-\tfrac{\beta}{\alpha}\right)(\alpha X+Y)^{-1},\,X^{-1}+(\beta-\alpha)(\alpha X+Y)^{-1}\right)

then
(U,V)\sim\mathrm{GIG}(-\lambda,\alpha\gamma_{2},\gamma_{1})\otimes\mathrm{GIG}(\lambda,\gamma_{2},\beta\gamma_{1})
.
For further use we observe that this last independence property is equivalent to the fact that the quadruple of densities
\mathfrak{f}_{X}
,
\mathfrak{f}_{Y}
,
\mathfrak{f}_{U}
and
\mathfrak{f}_{V}
(in case
X
,
Y
, and thus
U
and
V
, have densities) satisfies the functional equation

(1.6)

\mathfrak{d}_{U}\left(\tfrac{\tau+\beta\sigma}{\tau+\alpha\sigma}\tfrac{1}{\tau}\right)\,\mathfrak{d}_{V}\left(\tfrac{\tau+\beta\sigma}{\tau+\alpha\sigma}\tfrac{1}{\sigma}\right)\,\left(\tfrac{\tau+\beta\sigma}{\tau\sigma(\tau+\alpha\sigma)}\right)^{2}=\,\mathfrak{d}_{X}(\sigma)\ \mathfrak{d}_{Y}(\tau),\quad\sigma,\tau\in(0,\infty),\quad\tau,\sigma>0,

for
\mathfrak{d}_{W}=\mathfrak{f}_{W}
,
W\in\{X,Y,U,V\}
.

Note also that for
\alpha=1
and
\beta=0
this independence property for
(U,V)
defined in (
1.5
) reduces to the original MY property (
1.2
): see the second line of (
1.5
). In this context it is interesting to note that (
1.4
) for
\beta=0\neq\alpha
refers just to the straight discrete KdV model, see e.g.
[
7
]
.

In
[
8
]
the authors conjectured that the above independence preserving property with
(U,V)
defined by (
1.4
) characterizes GIG laws. Under assumptions that the distributions of
X
,
Y
have strictly positive and twice differentiable densities the conjecture was proved in
[
2
]
. The complete solution with no assumptions regarding existence of densities was given in
[
19
]
.

In
[
19
]
, extending the matrix variate version of the MY property from
[
18
]
, the modified KdV property was established for random matrices as follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

[
19
]

Let
X
and
Y
be independent
\Omega_{+}
-valued random matrices with distributions
\mathrm{MGIG}(\lambda,\alpha a,b)
and
\mathrm{MGIG}(\lambda,\beta b,a)
, respectively. Let
\phi^{(\alpha,\beta)}:\Omega_{+}^{2}\to\Omega_{+}^{2}
be defined by

(1.7)

\phi^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x,y)=\left(y(I+\alpha xy)^{-1}(I+\beta xy),\,x(I+\beta yx)^{-1}(I+\alpha yx)\right)

and let

\left(U,\,V\right)=\phi^{(\alpha,\beta)}(X,Y).

Then
U
and
V
are independent,
U\sim\mathrm{MGIG}(\lambda,\alpha b,a)
and
V=\mathrm{MGIG}(\lambda,\beta a,b)
.

The main goal of the present paper is to prove a converse result, that is to prove that MGIG laws are characterized by the independence property from Theorem
1.1
. Of course, the question is meaningful only in case
\alpha\neq\beta
since
\phi^{(\alpha,\alpha)}(x,y)=(y,x)
. To this end we will need technical asumptions of existence and smoothness properties of densities:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
X
and
Y
be independent
\Omega_{+}
valued random matrices with densities which are strictly positive and twice differentiable on
\Omega_{+}
. Let
(U,V)=\phi^{(\alpha,\beta)}(X,Y)
for some
\alpha\neq\beta
, where
\phi^{(\alpha,\beta)}
is defined in (
1.7
).

Then there exist
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and
a,b\in\Omega_{+}
such that

(X,Y)\sim\mathrm{MGIG}(\lambda,\alpha a,b)\otimes\mathrm{MGIG}(\lambda,\beta b,a)\quad\mbox{and}\quad(U,V)\sim\mathrm{MGIG}(\lambda,\alpha b,a)\otimes\mathrm{MGIG}(\lambda,\beta a,b)

The proof of Theorem
1.2
is given in Section 2. Actually, for convenience we will state and prove there an equivalent result, Theorem
2.1
. A similar equivalent approach appeared to be convenient in the univariate case, see
[
19
]
.

The map
\phi^{(\alpha,\beta)}
is a direct extension of
H_{III,B}^{(\alpha,\beta)}
from the domain
(0,\infty)^{2}
to
\Omega_{+}^{2}
, that is from a commutative setting to a non-commutative one. Since
H_{III,B}^{(\alpha,\beta)}
is a parametric YB map, see Theorem 2 in
[
26
]
, it is natural to ask if
\phi^{(\alpha,\beta)}
defined in (
1.7
) is a YB map on
\Omega_{+}^{2}
. It appears that it really is:

Theorem 1.3
.

The function
\phi^{(\cdot,\cdot)}=\left(\phi_{1}^{(\cdot,\cdot)},\,\phi_{2}^{(\cdot,\cdot)}\right)
is a parametric Yang-Baxter map, that is

(1.8)

F_{12}^{(\alpha,\beta)}\circ F_{13}^{(\alpha,\gamma)}\circ F_{23}^{(\beta,\gamma)}=F_{23}^{(\beta,\gamma)}\circ F_{13}^{(\alpha,\gamma)}\circ F_{12}^{(\alpha,\beta)},

with

\left.\begin{cases}F_{12}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x,y,z)=\left(\phi_{1}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x,y),\,\phi_{2}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x,y),z\right)\\
F_{13}^{(\alpha,\gamma)}(x,y,z)=\left(\phi_{1}^{(\alpha,\gamma)}(x,z),\,y,\,\phi_{2}^{(\alpha,\gamma)}(x,z)\right)\\
F_{23}^{(\beta,\gamma)}(x,y,z)=\left(x,\,\phi_{1}^{(\beta,\gamma)}(y,z),\phi_{2}^{(\beta,\gamma)}(y,z)\right)\end{cases}\right\}\quad x,y,z\in\Omega_{+}.

The proof of Theorem
1.3
is given in the Appendix.

2.
CHARACTERIZATION AND ITS PROOF

Similarly as in the univariate case, for the matrix variate MGIG variable, see (
1.1
), the following property holds: if
W\sim\mathrm{MGIG}(\kappa,c,d)
then
W^{-1}\sim\mathrm{MGIG}(-\kappa,d,c)
. Consequently, exactly following the transition in the univariate case from presentation (
1.4
) to presentation (
1.5
) by taking
Y
in place of
Y^{-1}
and
V
in place of
V^{-1}
, we see that Theorem
1.2
is equivalent to Theorem
2.1
below. It is clear that independence properties of
X
and
Y
as well as of
U
and
V
of Theorem
1.2
are preserved under these transformations of
Y
and
V
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Assume that
X
and
Y
are independent random matrices valued in
\Omega_{+}
having strictly positive twice differentiable densities on
\Omega_{+}
. Let
\alpha,\beta\geq 0
be two distinct numbers. Let

(2.1)

\begin{cases}U=(\alpha X+Y)^{-1}(\beta X+Y)Y^{-1}\\
V=(\alpha X+Y)^{-1}(\beta X+Y)X^{-1}.\end{cases}

If
U
and
V
are independent then there exist
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and
a,b\in\Omega_{+}
such that

(2.2)

X\sim\mathrm{MGIG}(\lambda,\alpha a,b)\quad\mbox{and}\quad Y\sim\mathrm{MGIG}(-\lambda,a,\beta b)

and

(2.3)

U\sim\mathrm{MGIG}(\lambda,\alpha b,a)\quad\mbox{and}\quad V\sim\mathrm{MGIG}(-\lambda,b,\beta a).

Remark 2.2
.

Note that
U
and
V
, as defined in (
2.1
), can be represented as

(2.4)

\begin{cases}U=\tfrac{\beta}{\alpha}Y^{-1}+\left(1-\tfrac{\beta}{\alpha}\right)(\alpha X+Y)^{-1}\\
V=X^{-1}+(\beta-\alpha)(\alpha X+Y)^{-1},\end{cases}

which actually is of the same form as in the univariate case given in the second line of (
1.5
) .

Let us point out the following: If
X
and
Y
are symmetric positive definite matrices (spd) then
U
and
V
are also spd. Symmetry is clear from the presentation of
U
and
V
in (
2.4
). Positive definitness is less obvious. Several proofs are available. For instance one can refer to a theorem by Wigner,
[
33
]
, which says that if
a,b,c
are spd matrices of the same dimension and the product
abc
is symmetric, then
abc
is spd as well. Applying Wigner’s result to the presentation of
U
and
V
in (
2.1
) shows that they are both
\Omega_{+}
-valued random matrices.

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

It is divided into ten parts:

2.1.
A functional equation on
\Omega_{+}
and its counterpart on
(0,\infty)
.

Note that in view of (
2.4
) we have
(U,V)=\psi(X,Y)
, where
\psi:\Omega_{+}^{2}\to\Omega_{+}^{2}
is defined by

\psi(x,y)=\left((\alpha x+y)^{-1}(\beta x+y)y^{-1},\,(\alpha x+y)^{-1}(\beta x+y)x^{-1}\right).

For computing the jacobian of
\psi
we note that
\psi=\theta\circ\phi^{(\alpha,\beta)}\circ\theta
, where
\Omega_{+}\ni(x,y)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\theta}}{{\mapsto}}(x,y^{-1})
. Recall, see Lemma 5.1,
[
19
]
, that the function
\phi^{(\alpha,\beta)}=:\phi=(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})
is an involution with jacobian
J_{\phi}=1
. The jacobian of
\theta(x,y)
is
(\det\,y)^{-(r+1)}
. Indeed, it follows e.g. from Lemma 2.2 in
[
18
]
, in view of the fact that the differential
D_{y}\,y^{-1}[h]=-y^{-1}hy^{-1}
,
h\in\Omega
. Since
\theta
is an involution, we conclude that
\psi
is an involution as well. Thus the jacobian of
\psi
assumes the form

J_{\psi}(x,y)=J_{\theta}(\phi(x,y^{-1}))\,J_{\phi}(x,y^{-1})\,J_{\theta}(x,y)=(\det(\phi_{2}(x,y^{-1})))^{-(r+1)}\,(\det\,y)^{-(r+1)}=\left(\tfrac{\det\,v}{\det\,y}\right)^{r+1}.

For
W\in\{X,Y,U,V\}
we denote by
\mathfrak{f}_{W}
the density of the law of
W
. Then let
\mathfrak{g}_{W}(x)=\log\,\mathfrak{f}_{W}(x)
for
W\in\{X,U\}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{W}(x)=\log\,\mathfrak{f}_{W}(x)+(r+1)\log\det x
for
W\in\{Y,V\}
. Consequently, the independence assumption implies

(2.5)

\mathfrak{g}_{U}(u)+\mathfrak{g}_{V}(v)=\mathfrak{g}_{X}(x)+\mathfrak{g}_{Y}(y),\quad x,y\in\Omega_{+},

where
(u,v)=(u(x,y),v(x,y))
.

We choose an arbitrary
w\in\Omega_{+}
and apply (
2.5
) to
(x,y)=(\sigma w,\,\tau w)
, where
\sigma,\tau\in(0,\infty)
. It gives

(2.6)

\mathfrak{g}_{U}\left((\tau w+\alpha\sigma w)^{-1}(\tau w+\beta\sigma w)(\tau w)^{-1}\right)+\mathfrak{g}_{V}\left((\tau w+\alpha\sigma w)^{-1}(\tau w+\beta\sigma w)(\sigma w)^{-1}\right)=\mathfrak{g}_{X}(\sigma w)+\mathfrak{g}_{Y}(\tau w).

In the sequel we will consider several quadruples of real functions
(\mathfrak{m}_{X},\,\mathfrak{m}_{Y},\mathfrak{m}_{U},\mathfrak{m}_{V})=(\mathfrak{m}_{W})_{W\in\{X,Y,U,V\}}
defined on
(0,\infty)
, satisfying

(2.7)

\mathfrak{m}_{U}\left(\tfrac{\tau+\beta\sigma}{\tau+\alpha\sigma}\tfrac{1}{\tau}\right)+\mathfrak{m}_{V}\left(\tfrac{\tau+\beta\sigma}{\tau+\alpha\sigma}\tfrac{1}{\sigma}\right)=\mathfrak{m}_{X}(\sigma)+\mathfrak{m}_{Y}(\tau),\quad\sigma,\tau>0,

For every
w\in\Omega_{+}
we define the quadruple of real functions

\begin{cases}\mathfrak{h}_{U}^{(w)}(\rho):=&\mathfrak{g}_{U}(\rho w^{-1}),\quad\rho>0,\\
\mathfrak{h}_{V}^{(w)}(\rho):=&\mathfrak{g}_{V}(\rho w^{-1}),\quad\rho>0,\\
\mathfrak{h}_{X}^{(w)}(\rho):=&\mathfrak{g}_{X}(\rho w),\quad\rho>0,\\
\mathfrak{h}_{Y}^{(w)}(\rho):=&\mathfrak{g}_{Y}(\rho w),\quad\rho>0.\end{cases}

In view of (
2.6
) for all

the quadruple
(\mathfrak{h}^{(w)}_{W})_{W\in\{X,Y,U,V\}}
satisfies (
2.7
) .

2.2.
Solution of equation (
2.7
) for the quadruple
(\mathfrak{h}^{(w)}_{W})_{W\in\{X,Y,U,V\}}
.

We attack the problem by reducing equation (
2.7

Note that for any
W\in\{X,Y,U,V\}
and any

\infty>\int_{0}^{\infty}\,\left(\int_{\Omega_{+}}\,\mathfrak{f}_{W}(w)\,\mathrm{d}w\right)\,\xi^{p-1}e^{-q\xi-s/\xi}\,\mathrm{d}\xi=\int_{\Omega_{+}}\,\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\,\xi^{p+r-1}\,e^{-q\xi-s/\xi}\mathfrak{f}_{W}(\xi v)\,\mathrm{d}\xi\right)\,\mathrm{d}v.

is integrable for almost all

Consider independent
X^{\prime}
and
Y^{\prime}

\mathrm{GIG}(-\lambda^{\prime},\alpha\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})
and
\mathrm{GIG}(\lambda^{\prime},\gamma_{1},\beta\gamma_{2})

Set

\mathfrak{H}_{X}^{(w)}:=\tilde{\mathfrak{h}}_{X^{\prime}}+\mathfrak{h}_{X}^{(w)},\quad\mathfrak{H}_{Y}^{(w)}:=\tilde{\mathfrak{h}}_{Y^{\prime}}+\mathfrak{h}_{Y}^{(w)},\quad\mathfrak{H}_{U}^{(w)}:=\tilde{\mathfrak{h}}_{U^{\prime}}+\mathfrak{h}_{U}^{(w)},\quad\mathfrak{H}_{V}^{(w)}:=\tilde{\mathfrak{h}}_{V^{\prime}}+\mathfrak{h}_{V}^{(w)},\quad w\in\Omega_{+},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{q}_{Y}^{(w)}(\rho)

\displaystyle:=\exp(\mathfrak{H}_{Y}^{(w)}(\rho))\rho^{-2}=\mathfrak{f}_{Y^{\prime}}(\rho)\,\mathfrak{f}_{Y}(\rho w)\,\rho^{r(r+1)}(\det\,w)^{r+1},

3.
BIBLIOGRAPHICAL COMMENTS AND OPEN PROBLEMS

In the original Matsumoto-Yor paper,
[
24
]
, the direct result (
1.2
) was proved for
\gamma_{1}=\gamma_{2}
. The case of
\gamma_{1}\neq\gamma_{2}
was identified in
[
18
]
and
[
25
]
. Interestingly,
[
25
]
also connected this property, under additional assumption that
\lambda=\tfrac{1}{2}
, to hitting times of the Brownian motion with drift. This independence property is now known as the Matsumoto-Yor (MY) property, see e.g
[
29
]
. It has been intensively studied in the literature. In particular, a characterization of the
\mathrm{GIG}
and
\mathrm{Gamma}
by the MY independence property was obtained in
[
18
]
. For its ”regression” versions, where the independence of
U
and
V
are replaced by constancy of some conditional moments of
V
given
U
, see
[
6
]
and references therein.

A tree structured MY property for random vectors in
(0,\infty)^{n}
(and a related characterization) was given in
[
21
]
. It appears that such tree governed independence property is, as the original one, hidden in the conditional structure of the exponential Brownian motion, see
[
23
]
. A tree version of the MY property for random matrices was established in
[
3
]
.

In
[
22
]
a modification of the MY property for positive definite matrices of different dimensions was established and, rather unexpectedly, connected with the structure of Wishart matrices: conditional independence of the block diagonal element and its Schur complement given the off-diagonal element (see also
[
4
]
,
[
28
]
). This result, in particular, allowed to improve the celebrated characterization of the Wishart distribution of Geiger and Heckermann in
[
11
]
studied in the context of statistical Bayesian graphical models.

More recently, free probability versions of MY property and related characterizations of free GIG (see
[
10
]
) and free Poisson (which, in free probability setting, plays the role of the gamma law) were derived in
[
31
]
and its regression version characterization was given in
[
30
]
.

It is interesting to note that very recently an ultra-discrete version of
H_{III,B}^{(\alpha,\beta)}
was identified in
[
14
]
(as well as for other functions of the
H
class). It was proved there that this new map, as the original one, preserves independence and has the parametric YB property.

Finally let us discuss some related open problems:

(1)

It would be interesting to know how to extend from the cone of positive definite matrices to any (Euclidean, simple) symmetric cone: the map
\phi^{(\alpha,\beta)}
, the independence property and the characterization. A symmetric cone version of the GIG distribution has been already identifed in
[
17
]
. Since, see (
1.7
),

\displaystyle y(I+\alpha xy)^{-1}(I+\beta xy)

\displaystyle=y^{1/2}(I+\beta y^{1/2}xy^{1/2})^{1/2}(I+\alpha y^{1/2}xy^{1/2})^{-1}(I+\beta y^{1/2}xy^{1/2})^{1/2}y^{1/2},

\displaystyle x(I+\beta yx)^{-1}(1+\alpha yx)

\displaystyle=x^{1/2}(I+\alpha x^{1/2}yx^{1/2})^{1/2}(I+\beta x^{1/2}yx^{1/2})^{-1}(I+\alpha x^{1/2}yx^{1/2})^{1/2}x^{1/2}),

a plausible candidate for such an extension would be
\phi^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x,y)=(u^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x,y),\,v^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x,y))
with

(3.1)

\begin{cases}u^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x,y)=&\left(P\left\{y^{1/2}\right\}\circ P\left\{\left(1+\beta P\left\{y^{1/2}\right\}x\right)^{1/2}\right\}\right)\left(1+\alpha P\left\{y^{1/2}\right\}x\right)^{-1},\\
v^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x,y)=&\left(P\left\{x^{1/2}\right\}\circ P\left\{\left(1+\alpha P\left\{x^{1/2}\right\}y\right)^{1/2}\right\}\right)\left(1+\beta P\left\{x^{1/2}\right\}y\right)^{-1},\end{cases}

where
P
is so called quadratic representation in the symmetric cone, see e.g.
[
9
]
(in particular,
P(x)y=xyx
in case of the cone of spd matrices).
Such an extension was done in
[
17
]
for the original matrix variate MY property, that is in the case of
(\alpha,\beta)=(1,0)
. Note that (
3.1
) implies that
u^{(1,0)}(x,y^{-1})=(x+y)^{-1}
and
v^{(1,0)}(x,y^{-1})^{-1}=x^{-1}-(x+y)^{-1}
. Also it would be interesting to know if the elementary, purely algebraic proof of the YB property, given in the Appendix, extends to function
\phi^{(\alpha,\beta)}
defined on non-commutative algebraic domains.

(2)

Characterizations related to MY property in the matrix variate case typically require extra hypothesis of existence and smoothness of densities, while in the univariate case no such requirements are needed. We know only one result in the literature,
[
5
]
, which falls out of such setting. The paper gives a matrix variate version of the celebrated Lukacs result,
[
20
]
: independence of
(0,\infty)
valued random variables
X
,
Y
and independence of
X+Y
and
\tfrac{X}{X+Y}
characterizes gamma distributions. However the authors required an additional assumption of invariance of distribution of the matrix analogue of the quotient
\tfrac{X}{X+Y}
. Removing or weakening of hypothesis about existence of density or, at least, of its smoothness is desirable. In this context let us mention that the original characterization in the matrix variate case from
[
18
]
was first improved in
[
32
]
, where the assumption from
[
18
]
, that the densities are
C_{2}
functions, was weakened to differentiability. In
[
17
]
it was further weakened to continuity. Nevertheless, even in the case of the original MY property, the characterization in the matrix variate case requires existence of densities which are strictly positive on
\Omega_{+}
.

(3)

Quadrirational Yang-Baxter maps were introduced in
[
1
]
, see also
[
26
]
. In recent years there has been a considerable interest in construction of non-abelian quadrirational Yang-Baxter maps, see
[
13
]
and references therein. Since
\phi^{(\alpha,\beta)}
, as defined in (
1.7
), is a YB map, it is a natural lifting of the YB map
H^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{III,B}
defined on
(0,\infty)^{2}
to
\Omega_{+}^{2}
. It would be interesting to know if such liftings are possible in case of the remaining independence preserving quadrirational YB maps
H_{I}^{(\alpha,\beta)}
,
H_{II}^{(\alpha,\beta)}
and
H_{III,A}^{(\alpha,\beta)}
. Actually, the matrix version of
H_{II}^{(1,0)}
was proposed in
[
15
]
, where it was proved that it is an independence preserving map for the Kummer and Wishart matrices. Similarly, a matrix version of
H_{I}^{(1,0)}
, which is an independence preserving map for beta distributions, was proposed in
[
16
]
.

\displaystyle=(\alpha-\gamma)u(I+\gamma s^{*})^{-1}+(\gamma-\alpha)(I+\alpha s^{*})^{-1}v-(\gamma-\alpha)^{2}u(I+\gamma s^{*})^{-1}(I+\alpha s^{*})^{-1}v.

So for
\alpha=\gamma
the equality holds true.

Now we assume that
\alpha\neq\gamma
and, denoting
s_{\kappa}=(I+\kappa s)^{-1}
,
\kappa=\alpha,\beta
, we rewrite the above equality as

vs_{\alpha}-s_{\gamma}u-(\gamma-\alpha)vs_{\alpha}s_{\gamma}u=s_{\alpha}^{*}v-us_{\gamma}^{*}-(\gamma-\alpha)us_{\gamma}^{*}s_{\alpha}^{*}v.

Its left hand side can be written as

vs_{\alpha}-s_{\alpha}u+[s_{\alpha}-s_{\gamma}]u-(\gamma-\alpha)vs_{\alpha}s_{\gamma}u=vs_{\alpha}-s_{\alpha}u+(\gamma-\alpha)(s-v)s_{\alpha}s_{\gamma}u.

and its right hand side can be written as

s_{\alpha}^{*}v-us_{\alpha}^{*}+u[s_{\alpha}^{*}-s_{\gamma}^{*}]-(\gamma-\alpha)us_{\gamma}^{*}s_{\alpha}^{*}v=s_{\alpha}^{*}v-us_{\alpha}^{*}+(\gamma-\alpha)us_{\gamma}^{*}s_{\alpha}^{*}(s^{*}-v).

Since
s-v=u(I+bv)
and
s^{*}-v=(I+bv)u
to finish the proof it suffices to show that

(A.8)

vs_{\alpha}-s_{\alpha}u=s_{\alpha}^{*}v-us_{\alpha}^{*}\quad\mbox{and}\quad(1+bv)s_{\alpha}s_{\gamma}=s_{\gamma}^{*}s_{\alpha}^{*}(I+\beta v).

The second equation of (
A.8
) holds since after taking inverses sidewise it follows directly from
(I+\beta v)s=s^{*}(I+\beta v)
(applied twice). To see that the first equality in (
A.8
) holds we rewrite it in an equivalent form

(I+\beta v)s_{\alpha}-s_{\alpha}(I+\beta u)=s_{\alpha}^{*}(I+\beta v)-(I+\beta u)s_{\alpha}^{*}

and refer to
(I+\beta v)s=s^{*}(I+\beta v)
and
s(I+\beta u)=(I+u)s^{*}
.

Thus the proof of
Y_{3}=y_{3}
is finished.
∎

Fractional-order Klein-Gordon nonlocal model for thermo-elasto-diffusion in porous medium
Authors: Abhik Sur, Moataz Alosaimi, Abhinav Singhal, Murat Yaylacı, Soumik Das
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractClassical heat conduction and thermoelastic theories are often inadequate for describing transport processes in modern micro and nano-scale materials, as they assume instantaneous propagation and neglect memory and nonlocal effects. This limitation becomes particularly important in applications such as semiconductor devices and porous structures subjected to rapid thermal and chemical loading. To address this issue, the present study develops a generalized thermo-elasto-diffusion model for porous medium by incorporating fractional-order heat conduction and Klein-Gordon-type nonlocal dynamics. The model extends the classical Lord–Shulman framework through a set of fractional-order modified models derived using an analogy with viscoelastic behavior, allowing both memory effects and spatial interactions to be captured through intrinsic time and length scales. Analytical solutions are obtained in the Laplace domain and numerically inverted using Zakian’s algorithm to evaluate the transient response of displacement, temperature, chemical potential, and stress fields. The results show that fractional-order parameters and nonlocal effects significantly influence wave propagation and heat transfer, especially near boundaries and in regions with strong microstructural interactions. Overall, the proposed framework provides a more realistic and physically consistent description of coupled thermo-mechanical processes, offering useful insights for the design and analysis of advanced porous and semiconductor materials under extreme conditions.Keywords: Fractional-order derivative, Porous material, Modified Lord-Shulman model, Klein-Gordon operator, Heat and mass transferSubject terms: Engineering, Materials science, Mathematics and computing, Physics

Introduction

According to traditional theories of thermal transport1, 2, the heat flux is linearly proportional to the temperature gradient. However, this classical formulation leads to the non-physical prediction of infinite thermal propagation speed, where temperature disturbances are instantaneously felt at distant points. Addressing this inconsistency has driven the evolution of advanced heat conduction models. With the growing importance of thermomechanical analysis in modern technologies such as MEMS/NEMS, FGMs, TBCs, and ultrafast laser applications. The inclusion of non-Fourier effects has become essential3, 4, 5. To overcome the limitations of Fourier’s law, Carlo Cattaneo6 and Pierre Vernotte7 proposed the hyperbolic heat conduction model (CV), introducing a time derivative of heat flux to account for finite propagation speed. This framework was extended by D. Y. Tzou8 through the dual-phase-lag (DPL) model, incorporating distinct delays for heat flux and temperature gradient, although its thermodynamic consistency remains debated. The Lord and Shulman (LS) theory9 integrates the CV model with classical elasticity, while the Green and Lindsay (GL) theory10 introduces relaxation effects in stress and entropy relations. Furthermore, the Green and Naghdi (GN) model11, 12 employs thermal displacement as a fundamental variable. Building on these developments, recent studies have explored fractional-order models13, memory-dependent formulations14, and nonlocal theories15.

The fundamental principles of diffusion theory describe the spontaneous movement of particles from regions of high concentration to low concentration. Over recent decades, this framework has been significantly refined to address complex engineering applications across geophysical and technological domains, including semiconductor design, integrated resistor fabrication, and petroleum reservoir management. In such processes, thermal energy plays a crucial role in enhancing particle mobility, giving rise to thermodiffusion, which establishes a strong coupling between temperature gradients, mass transport, and mechanical deformation in elastic solids. Within this coupled system, variations in thermal or chemical fields induce mechanical responses, while structural changes, in turn, influence heat and mass transfer. This interplay is particularly important in nano-scale systems, such as nano-electromechanical systems (NEMS)16, where accurate modeling of thermo-elastodiffusive behavior is essential for ensuring device performance and reliability under non-uniform thermal conditions.

However, classical diffusion models based on Fick’s law suffer from the non-physical prediction of infinite propagation speeds. Early formulations, such as those by Nowacki17, were limited by this drawback. To overcome this issue, Sherief et al.18 introduced a generalized thermodiffusion framework incorporating a relaxation time to ensure finite wave propagation. Building on this foundation, Sherief and Saleh19 analyzed thermo-chemical interactions in a one-dimensional half-space, while Aoudi20 extended the analysis to medium with spherical cavities. Further studies have explored wave propagation characteristics in porous thermodiffusive solids21 and examined the effects of rotation and gravity using Dual-Phase-Lag (DPL) and Lord-Shulman (LS) theories22. More recently, Dutta and co-workers23, 24 advanced the field by developing generalized thermoelastic diffusion models that integrate Lord-Shulman and Moore–Gibson–Thompson frameworks.

Over the past few decades, fractional calculus25, 26 has emerged as a powerful mathematical framework for modeling systems exhibiting spatial nonlocality, memory effects, and hereditary behavior. Its versatility has led to widespread applications across fields such as electromagnetics, control engineering, quantum-scale modeling, and materials science. In particular, modern technological materials27, 28, 29, characterized by complex microstructural responses, are effectively described using fractional-order operators due to their ability to capture long-range temporal correlations. Significant contributions by various researchers30, 31, 32 have advanced this field by integrating fractional-order concepts into multi-temperature and nonlocal thermoelastic theories, offering deeper insights into wave propagation in generalized thermomechanical systems. This line of research has been further extended by Das and co-workers33, 34, 35, 36, who investigated energy transport mechanisms in piezo-thermo-electric medium using memory-dependent and phase-lag formulations, revealing intricate couplings between mechanical, thermal, and electrical fields that are not captured by classical integer-order models.

The historical trajectory of nonlocal continuum mechanics began with the foundational interaction frameworks established by Edelen and Laws37, which challenged the limitations of classical local theories. These early concepts were subsequently refined by Eringen and Edelen38, who integrated thermodynamic equilibrium and global balance laws to create a cohesive theory for materials governed by long-range interatomic interactions. This body of work became the primary pillar for modern nonlocal elasticity, an essential tool for investigating size-dependent behaviors at the micro- and nano-scales39, 40, 41, 42. However, the widely utilized differential model introduced by Eringen has recently been subjected to rigorous re-examination. Critical mathematical inconsistencies were uncovered by Romano43 and Kaplunov44, 45, whose counterexamples cast doubt on the universal reliability of the classical formulation. In response to these theoretical gaps, a weakly nonlocal elastic framework within spatial domains was introduced by Anh and Vinh46, enabling the study of surface wave dynamics at the interfaces of nonlocal structures. This line of inquiry was further extended by Anh’s research group47 to analyze Rayleigh-type waves under impedance boundary conditions. Concurrently, a Klein–Gordon-type weakly nonlocal model for isotropic solids was developed by Lazar and Agiasofitou32. By replacing earlier Helmholtz-based theories with a system that incorporates both spatial and temporal scaling parameters, they significantly enhanced the model’s descriptive power. The inclusion of temporal nonlocality is particularly noteworthy; it allows for the theoretical observation of frequency band gaps and optical branches in dispersion spectra—phenomena that remain hidden in strictly acoustic wave analysis.

Porous materials with internal cavities play a crucial role in engineering and natural sciences, with applications ranging from lightweight aerospace structures and thermal insulation to biological tissues and geological formations. The theoretical foundation for modeling such medium is the theory of elastic materials with voids, where the microstructure is characterized by the void volume fraction, defined as the ratio of the bulk density of the porous matrix to that of the solid material. This framework incorporates void effects directly into the kinematic variables and naturally reduces to classical elasticity in the absence of porosity. The linear and nonlinear formulations of this theory were established by Cowin and co-workers48, 49, who later showed that elastic materials with voids exhibit inherent viscoelastic behavior50. Further investigations by Puri and Cowin51 demonstrated that such medium support one transverse and two coupled dilatational waves. Studies on wave propagation in porous layers by Dey et al.52 revealed the existence of two distinct wave fronts associated with material rigidity and void distribution. More recently, Gupta et al.53 applied the Green–Naghdi (GN-III) thermoelasticity framework to analyze deformation in rotating, exponentially graded medium with dual-porosity structures54, 55, 56, 57.

The motivation for the present study arises from the need for accurate and physically consistent models to describe thermo-mechanical behavior in micro- and nano-structured porous materials under rapid thermal and chemical loading58, 59, 60, 61, 62, 63, 64, 65, 66, 67. Classical thermoelastic and diffusion theories fail to capture finite-speed wave propagation, long-range interactions, and memory-dependent transport. Although nonlocal elasticity and fractional-order models have advanced the field, they are typically developed separately and do not simultaneously incorporate spatial and temporal nonlocal effects within a unified thermo-elasto-diffusion framework68, 69, 70, 71, 72, 73, 74, 75. In particular, the integration of Klein–Gordon-type spatiotemporal nonlocality into fractional-order thermoelastic diffusion models for porous medium with voids remains largely unexplored76, 77.

To address this gap, the present study introduces a unified framework combining fractional-order heat conduction with Klein–Gordon-type nonlocal elasticity, capturing both memory effects and intrinsic length-scale interactions. The proposed Fractional-order Modified Lord-Shulman (FOMLS) model accounts for coupled spatial–temporal nonlocality and memory dependence. The main contributions include analytical solutions for the coupled field equations in a porous strip and an investigation of how void volume fraction and nonlocal parameters influence wave propagation. These results are relevant for applications such as thermal barrier coatings, MEMS/NEMS devices, and semiconductor sensors under ultrafast excitations. The remainder of the paper outlines the mathematical formulation, model development, problem setup, solution methodology using Laplace transforms and Zakian’s inversion, validation, and discussion of results, followed by conclusions.

Nowadays, the fractional heat conduction provides a powerful tool for modeling memory-dependent and anomalous transport processes. Classical Fourier-based models assume instantaneous heat flux response, which fails to describe heat transfer in materials exhibiting thermal relaxation, long memory, or non-Fickian diffusion. These fractional-order formulations propose a non-integer time derivative, which effectively captures the history-dependent evolution of thermal fields and allows for a more accurate description of sub-diffusive and super-diffusive heat transport. This is particularly important in porous medium, biological tissues, and in many physical systems, where heat transport is strongly influenced by internal structure and temporal memory effects. Engineering applications include thermal management in microelectronic devices, heat transfer in composite and porous insulation systems, and ultrafast laser–material interactions, where classical models fail to predict experimentally observed thermal responses.

Conclusions

In the present analysis, a comprehensive theoretical and numerical investigation has been carried out to analyze thermo-diffusive responses in a porous elastic medium by incorporating spatiotemporal nonlocality assimilating fractional-order heat conduction model. The governing equations are formulated within the framework of the fractional-order modified Lord–Shulman models, enabling the simultaneous capture of memory effects, long-range interactions, and microstructural influences. The primary objective of this study was to examine how the spatial nonlocal parameter, temporal nonlocal parameter, and fractional-order parameter affect the thermophysical quantities, and to compare the predictive capabilities of different fractional and classical heat transfer models.

The present study is motivated by the necessity to develop a physically consistent theoretical framework capable of capturing finite-speed propagation, memory effects, and long-range interactions in thermo-mechanical processes of porous materials, which remain inadequately described by classical models.

Real-life applications

The connection of the present theoretical model, the fractional-order parameter represents thermal memory effects observed in materials with complex microstructures such as polymers, porous ceramics, and semiconductor composites, where heat transport deviates from classical Fourier behavior. The spatial nonlocal parameter corresponds to intrinsic length scales associated with grain size, pore size, or lattice spacing, which are known to influence size-dependent responses in MEMS/NEMS and nano-engineered materials. The model predictions such as enhanced thermal smoothing, delayed mechanical response, and distributed stress fields are consistent with experimentally observed non-Fourier heat conduction, wave dispersion, and attenuation phenomena in micro- and nano-scale systems. Furthermore, the ability of the FOMLS framework to distinguish the roles of spatial nonlocality and temporal memory provides a useful tool for interpreting experimental data, for example in ultrafast laser heating of semiconductors, thermal barrier coatings under rapid thermal shocks, and porous medium where coupled heat and mass transfer occurs. Therefore, although the study is theoretical, the results offer physically measurable trends and parameter sensitivities that can assist in calibrating models, designing experiments, and optimizing material performance in advanced engineering applications.

Artificial intelligence in healthcare: Clinical decision making algorithms, evidence-based applications, future insights, and beyond
Authors: Zhou Huang, Jinzhou Zhu
Published: 2026

Artificial intelligence (AI) is reshaping the future of healthcare, with the core goal of improving patient outcomes, enhancing clinical efficiency, and ensuring safety. This special issue discusses 10 studies that have comprehensively demonstrated the potential of AI across the entire data-to-decision continuum and highlights the key challenges associated with clinical implementation.

In practical applications, medical imaging remains the most mature domain for AI; however, significant progress has also been made in the areas of risk prediction, personalized therapy, chronic disease management, and mental health assessment.1–4 Reflecting these diverse trends, empirical evidence within this collection highlights significant breakthroughs across these domains. For instance, in diagnostic imaging, few-shot learning has been successfully leveraged to classify colorectal neuroendocrine tumors and polyps under data-scarce conditions, whereas explainable Residual Network–Long Short-Term Memory (ResNet-LSTM) architectures have advanced multifeature fusion for bowel sound frequency classification.5,6 In terms of personalized prognosis, novel deep learning models combined with electronic health records have shown high accuracy in predicting strangulation risks in small bowel obstructions. 4 Moreover, machine learning reviews within this collection have demonstrated substantial progress in chronic disease identification in primary care and clinical translation in breast surgery.7,8 Collectively, these studies illustrate that AI not only increases healthcare efficiency but also strengthens personalized medicine, providing clinicians and patients with more informed decision support.

Nevertheless, the rapid advancement in AI presents inherent contradictions. A critical scientific concern is the risk of “automation bias,” where overreliance on algorithmic outputs may lead clinicians to overlook subtle clinical nuances or fail to question erroneous recommendations. This risk is further compounded by the inherent “black box” nature of complex deep learning models, which poses severe accountability challenges in clinical mishaps. Consequently, clinicians must actively retain their core clinical skills and judgment. In scenarios involving missing data, model uncertainty, or unpredictable patient variability, human expertise remains indispensable. Ultimately, the true value of AI lies in augmenting clinical insight, rather than replacing the irreplaceable nuance of human decision making.

The translation of AI into clinical practice faces multiple obstacles, including insufficient data standardization, ethical and privacy concerns, challenges in cross-institutional integration, and limited time for clinician-led research.7,8 Against this backdrop, the present special issue is particularly timely, providing a platform for showcasing innovations, sharing methodological advances, and fostering cross-disciplinary collaboration to advance data-driven healthcare decision making.

Despite these challenges, clear trends are emerging in healthcare AI; it is evolving from single-source data toward multimodal integration, from focusing solely on model performance toward emphasizing explainability, from reliance on large datasets toward accommodating small-sample learning, and gradually achieving a research-to-clinical decision making closed loop. These trajectories will guide the future of clinical practice. Several pioneering frontiers warrant immediate research attention. First, the integration of generative AI and large language models (LLMs) into standard clinical workflows could revolutionize clinical documentation and patient communication.9,10 Second, development of robust “human-in-the-loop” cooperative frameworks is essential to foster mutual trust between AI recommendations and clinician intuition. 11 Finally, leveraging privacy-preserving techniques such as federated learning will be paramount to overcome cross-institutional data silos without compromising patient confidentiality.12,13

Researchers and clinicians should work closely to integrate innovative AI approaches into routine care, improving patient outcomes and elevating the quality of healthcare delivery. This special issue aims to provide a platform for these scientific contributions, enabling innovative ideas and practices to be disseminated, validated, and optimized, ultimately facilitating the successful implementation of AI from data to decision-making in healthcare.

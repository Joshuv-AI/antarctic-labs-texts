Abstract
In this paper, closed-form expressions are presented for the variation of the expectation of a given function due to changes in the probability measure used for the expectation. They unveil interesting connections with Gibbs probability measures, mutual information, and lautum information.



I
Introduction

Let
m
be a positive integer and denote by
\triangle(\mathbb{R}^{m})
the set of
all probability measures on the measurable space
\left(\mathbb{R}^{m},\mathscr{B}\left(\mathbb{R}^{m}\right)\right)
, with
\mathscr{B}\left(\mathbb{R}^{m}\right)
being the Borel
\sigma
-algebra on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Given a Borel measurable
function
h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
, consider the
functional
\mathsf{G}_{h}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\triangle(\mathbb{R}^{m})\times\triangle(\mathbb{R}^{m})\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle\mathsf{G}_{h}\left(x,P_{1},P_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int h(x,y)\mathrm{d}P_{1}(y)-\int h(x,y)\mathrm{d}P_{2}(y),

(1)

which quantifies the variation of the expectation of the measurable function
h
due to changing
the probability measure from
P_{2}
to
P_{1}
. Such a functional is defined when both integrals exist and are finite.

In order to define the expectation of
\mathsf{G}_{h}\left(x,P_{1},P_{2}\right)

with respect to
x
, the structure formalized below is required.

Definition 1
.

A family
P_{Y|X}\triangleq(P_{Y|X=x})_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}
of elements of

\triangle(\mathbb{R}^{m})
indexed by
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is said to be a conditional
probability measure if,
for all sets
\mathcal{A}\in\mathscr{B}\left(\mathbb{R}^{m}\right)
, the map

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{@{}r@{~}c@{~}l@{}}\mathbb{R}^{n}\penalty\ &\longrightarrow\hfil\penalty\ &[0,1]\\
x\penalty\ &\longmapsto\hfil\penalty\ &P_{Y|X=x}(\mathcal{A})\end{array}\right.

is Borel measurable.
The set of all such conditional probability measures
is denoted by
\triangle\left(\mathbb{R}^{m}|\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
.

In this setting, consider the functional
\bar{\mathsf{G}}_{h}:\triangle\left(\mathbb{R}^{m}|\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)\times\triangle\left(\mathbb{R}^{m}|\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)\times\triangle\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle\bar{\mathsf{G}}_{h}\left(P_{Y|X}^{(1)},P_{Y|X}^{(2)},P_{X}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\mathsf{G}_{h}\left(x,P_{Y|X=x}^{(1)},P_{Y|X=x}^{(2)}\right)\mathrm{d}P_{X}(x).\quad

(5)

This quantity can be interpreted as the variation of the integral (expectation) of the function
h
when the probability measure changes from the joint probability measure
P_{Y|X}^{(1)}P_{X}
to another joint probability measure
P_{Y|X}^{(2)}P_{X}
, both in
\triangle\left(\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
. This follows from (
5
) by observing that

\displaystyle\bar{\mathsf{G}}_{h}\left(P_{Y|X}^{(1)},P_{Y|X}^{(2)},P_{X}\right)

(6)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int h(x,y)\mathrm{d}P_{Y|X}^{(1)}P_{X}(y,x)-\int h(x,y)\mathrm{d}P_{Y|X}^{(2)}P_{X}(y,x).

Special attention is given to the quantity
\bar{\mathsf{G}}_{h}\left(P_{Y},P_{Y|X},P_{X}\right)
, for some
P_{Y|X}\in\triangle\left(\mathbb{R}^{m}|\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
, with
P_{Y}
being the marginal of the joint probability measure
P_{Y|X}\cdot P_{X}
.
That is, for all sets
\mathcal{A}\in\mathscr{B}\left(\mathbb{R}^{m}\right)
,

\displaystyle P_{Y}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int P_{Y|X=x}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)\mathrm{d}P_{X}(x).

(7)

Its relevance stems from the fact that it captures the variation of the expectation of the function
h
when the probability measure changes from the joint probability measure
P_{Y|X}P_{X}
to the product of its marginals
P_{Y}P_{X}
. That is,

\displaystyle\bar{\mathsf{G}}_{h}\left(P_{Y},P_{Y|X},P_{X}\right)

(8)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\left(\int h(x,y)\mathrm{d}P_{Y}(y)-\int h(x,y)\mathrm{d}P_{Y|X=x}(y)\right)\mathrm{d}P_{X}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int h(x,y)\mathrm{d}P_{Y}P_{X}(y,x)-\int h(x,y)\mathrm{d}P_{Y|X}P_{X}(y,x).

I-A

Novelty and Contributions

This work makes two key contributions: First, it provides a closed-form expression for the variation
\mathsf{G}_{h}\left(x,P_{1},P_{2}\right)
in (
1
) for a fixed
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and two arbitrary probability measures
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
, formulated explicitly in terms of information-theoretic quantities. Second, it derives a closed-form expression for the expected variation
\bar{\mathsf{G}}_{h}\left(P_{Y|X}^{(1)},P_{Y|X}^{(2)},P_{X}\right)
in (
5
), again in terms of information measures, for arbitrary conditional probability measures
P_{Y|X}^{(1)}
,
P_{Y|X}^{(2)}
, and marginal measure
P_{X}
.

A further contribution of this work is the derivation of specific closed-form expressions for
\bar{\mathsf{G}}_{h}\left(P_{Y},P_{Y|X},P_{X}\right)
in (
8
), which reveal deep connections with both mutual information
[
1
,
2
]
and lautum information
[
3
]
. Notably, when
P_{Y|X}
is a Gibbs conditional probability measure, this variation simplifies (up to a constant factor) to the sum of the mutual and lautum information induced by the joint distribution
P_{Y|X}P_{X}
.

Although these results were originally discovered in the analysis of generalization error of machine learning algorithms, see for instance
[
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
]
, where the function
h
in (
1
) was assumed to represent an empirical risk, this paper presents such results in a comprehensive and general setting that is no longer tied to such assumptions. This new general presentation not only unifies previously scattered insights but also makes the results applicable across a broad range of domains in which changes in the expectation due to variations of the underlying probability measures are relevant.

I-B

Applications

The study of the variation of the integral (expectation) of
h
(for some fixed
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
) due to a measure change from
P_{2}
to
P_{1}
, i.e., the value
\mathsf{G}_{h}\left(x,P_{1},P_{2}\right)
in (
1
), plays a central role in the definition of
integral probability metrics
(IPMs)
[
9
,
10
]
.
Using the notation in (
1
), an IPM results from the optimization problem

\displaystyle\sup_{h\in\mathcal{H}}\left|\mathsf{G}_{h}\left(x,P_{1},P_{2}\right)\right|,

(9)

for some fixed
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and a particular class of functions
\mathcal{H}
.
Note for instance that the maximum mean discrepancy is an IPM
[
11
]
, as well as the Wasserstein distance of order one
[
12
,
13
,
14
,
15
]
.

Other areas of mathematics in which the variation
\mathsf{G}_{h}\left(x,P_{1},P_{2}\right)
in (
1
) plays a key role is distributionally robust optimization (DRO)
[
16
,
17
]
and optimization with relative entropy regularization
[
5
,
6
]
. In these areas, the variation
\mathsf{G}_{h}\left(x,P_{1},P_{2}\right)
is a central tool. See for instance,
[
18
,
4
]
.
Variations of the form
\mathsf{G}_{h}\left(x,P_{1},P_{2}\right)
in (
1
) have also been studied in
[
7
]
and
[
8
]
in the particular case of statistical machine learning for the analysis of generalization error. The central observation is that the generalization error of machine learning algorithms can be written in the form
\bar{\mathsf{G}}_{h}\left(P_{Y},P_{Y|X},P_{X}\right)
in (
8
). This observation is the main building block of the
method of gaps
introduced in
[
8
]
, which leads to a number of closed-form expressions for the generalization error involving mutual information, lautum information, among other information measures.

II
Preliminaries

The main results presented in this work involve Gibbs conditional probability measures.
Such measures are parametrized by a Borel measurable function
h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
; a
\sigma
-finite measure
Q
on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
; and a vector
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Note that the variable
x
will remain inactive until Section
IV
.
Although it is introduced now for consistency, it could be removed altogether from all results presented in this section and Section
III
.

Denote by
\mathsf{K}_{h,Q,x}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
the function that satisfies

\displaystyle\mathsf{K}_{h,Q,x}\left(t\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\log\left(\int\exp\left(t\,h(x,y)\right)\mathrm{d}Q\left(y\right)\right).

(10)

Under the assumption that
Q
is a probability measure, the function
\mathsf{K}_{h,Q,x}
in (
10
) is the cumulant generating function of the random variable
h(x,Y)
, for some fixed
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
Y\sim Q
.
Using this notation, the definition of the Gibbs conditional probability measure is presented hereunder.

Definition 2

(Gibbs Conditional Probability Measure)
.

Given a Borel measurable function
h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
; a
\sigma
-finite measure
Q
on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
; and a
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, the probability measure
P^{(h,Q,\lambda)}_{Y|X}\in\triangle\left(\mathbb{R}^{m}|\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
is said to be an
(h,Q,\lambda)
-Gibbs conditional probability measure if

\displaystyle\mbox{$\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$, }\mathsf{K}_{h,Q,x}\left(-\lambda\right)

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle+\infty;

(11)

and for all
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
,

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}P^{(h,Q,\lambda)}_{Y|X=x}}{\mathrm{d}Q}\left(y\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\left(-\lambda h\left(x,y\right)-\mathsf{K}_{h,Q,x}\left(-\lambda\right)\right),

(12)

where the function
\mathsf{K}_{h,Q,x}
is defined in (
10
).

Note that, while
P_{Y|X}^{(h,Q,\lambda)}
is an
(h,Q,\lambda)
-Gibbs conditional probability measure, the measure
P_{Y|X=x}^{(h,Q,\lambda)}
, obtained by conditioning it upon a given vector
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, is referred to as an
(h,Q,\lambda)
-Gibbs probability measure.

The condition in (
11
) is easily met under certain assumptions. For instance, if
h
is a nonnegative function and
Q
is a finite measure, then it holds for all
\lambda\in\left(-\infty,0\right)
.
Let
\triangle_{Q}\left(\mathbb{R}^{m}\right)\triangleq\left\{P\in\triangle\left(\mathbb{R}^{m}\right):P\ll Q\right\}
, with
P\ll Q
standing for “
P
absolutely continuous with respect to
Q
”.
The relevance of
(h,Q,\lambda)
-Gibbs probability measures relies on the fact that under some conditions, they are the unique solutions to problems of the form,

\displaystyle\min_{P\in\triangle_{Q}(\mathbb{R}^{m})}\int h(x,y)\mathrm{d}P(y)+\frac{1}{\lambda}D\left(P\middle\|Q\right),\mbox{ and }

(13)

\displaystyle\max_{P\in\triangle_{Q}(\mathbb{R}^{m})}\int h(x,y)\mathrm{d}P(y)+\frac{1}{\lambda}D\left(P\middle\|Q\right),

(14)

where
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
,
x\in\mathbb{R}
, and
D\left(P\middle\|Q\right)
denotes the relative entropy (or KL divergence) of
P
with respect to
Q
.
The connection between the optimization problem (
13
) and the Gibbs probability measure
P^{(h,Q,\lambda)}_{Y|X=x}
in (
12
) appeared for the first time in
[
5
, Theorem
3
]
. Alternatively, the connection between the optimization problem (
14
) and such a Gibbs probability measure appeared first in
[
7
, Theorem
1
]
. In both cases, the presentation was in the context of a statistical learning problem.
A general and unified statement of these observations is presented hereunder.

Lemma 1
.

Assume that the optimization problem in (
13
) (respectively, in (
14
)) admits a solution. Then, if
\lambda>0
(respectively, if
\lambda<0
),
the probability measure
P^{(h,Q,\lambda)}_{Y|X=x}
in (
12
) is the unique solution.

Proof:

For the case in which
\lambda>0
, the proof follows the same approach as the proof of
[
5
, Theorem
3
]
. Alternatively, for the case in which
\lambda<0
, the proof follows along the lines of the proof of
[
7
, Theorem
1
]
.
∎

The following lemma highlights a key property of
(h,Q,\lambda)
-Gibbs conditional probability measures. A special case of this result was first introduced in
[
7
, Lemma 3]
for the case in which
\lambda<0
. Nonetheless, it holds in general for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
, as shown by the following lemma.

Lemma 2
.

Given an
(h,Q,\lambda)
-Gibbs probability measure, denoted by
P^{(h,Q,\lambda)}_{Y|X=x}
, with
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\lambda}\mathsf{K}_{h,Q,x}\left(-\lambda\right)=\int h(x,y)\mathrm{d}Q\left(y\right)-\frac{1}{\lambda}D\left(Q\middle\|P^{(h,Q,\lambda)}_{Y|X=x}\right)

(15)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int h(x,y)\mathrm{d}P^{(h,Q,\lambda)}_{Y|X=x}\left(y\right)+\frac{1}{\lambda}D\left(P^{(h,Q,\lambda)}_{Y|X=x}\middle\|Q\right);

(16)

moreover, if
\lambda>0
,

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\lambda}\mathsf{K}_{h,Q,x}\left(-\lambda\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min_{P\in\triangle_{Q}(\mathbb{R}^{m})}\int h(x,y)\mathrm{d}P(y)+\frac{1}{\lambda}D\left(P\middle\|Q\right);

(17)

alternatively, if
\lambda<0
,

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\lambda}\mathsf{K}_{h,Q,x}\left(-\lambda\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max_{P\in\triangle_{Q}(\mathbb{R}^{m})}\int h(x,y)\mathrm{d}P(y)+\frac{1}{\lambda}D\left(P\middle\|Q\right),

(18)

where the function
\mathsf{K}_{h,Q,x}
is defined in (
10

Proof:

The proof of (
16
) follows from taking the logarithm of both sides of (
12
) and integrating with respect to
P^{(h,Q,\lambda)}_{Y|X=x}
. As for the proof of (

(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\supp Q

\frac{\mathrm{d}P^{(h,Q,\lambda)}_{Y|X=x}}{\mathrm{d}Q}\left(y\right)
in (
12

\frac{\mathrm{d}Q}{\mathrm{d}P^{(h,Q,\lambda)}_{Y|X=x}}\left(y\right)=\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}P^{(h,Q,\lambda)}_{Y|X=x}}{\mathrm{d}Q}\left(y\right)\right)^{-1}
.
Hence, taking the negative logarithm of both sides of (
12
) and integrating with respect to
Q
leads to (
15

Finally, the equalities in (
17
) and (

and (
16

∎The following lemma introduces the main building block of this work, which is a characterization of the variation
\mathsf{G}_{h}\left(x,P,P^{(h,Q,\lambda)}_{Y|X=x}\right)

Such a result appeared for the first time in

Lemma 3
.

Consider an
(h,Q,\lambda)
-Gibbs probability measure, denoted by

, with
\lambda\neq 0

. For all

,

\displaystyle\mathsf{G}_{h}\left(x,P,P^{(h,Q,\lambda)}_{Y|X=x}\right)

(19)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda}\left(D\left(P\middle\|P^{(h,Q,\lambda)}_{Y|X=x}\right)+D\left(P^{(h,Q,\lambda)}_{Y|X=x}\middle\|Q\right)-D\left(P\middle\|Q\right)\right).

Proof:

The proof follows along the lines of the proofs of
[
4
, Theorem 1]
for the case in which
\lambda>0
; and in
[
7
, Theorem 6]
for the case in which
\lambda<0
.
A unified proof is presented hereunder by noticing that for all
P\in\triangle_{Q}\left(\mathbb{R}^{m}\right)
,

\displaystyle D\left(P\middle\|P^{(h,Q,\lambda)}_{Y|X=x}\right)

(20)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\log\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}P}{\mathrm{d}P^{(h,Q,\lambda)}_{Y|X=x}}(y)\right)\mathrm{d}P(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\log\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}Q}{\mathrm{d}P^{(h,Q,\lambda)}_{Y|X=x}}(y)\frac{\mathrm{d}P}{\mathrm{d}Q}(y)\right)\mathrm{d}P(y)

(21)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\log\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}Q}{\mathrm{d}P^{(h,Q,\lambda)}_{Y|X=x}}(y)\right)\mathrm{d}P(y)+D\left(P\middle\|Q\right)

(22)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda\int h(x,y)\mathrm{d}P(y)+\mathsf{K}_{h,Q,x}\left(-\lambda\right)+D\left(P\middle\|Q\right)

(23)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda\mathsf{G}_{h}\left(x,P,P^{(h,Q,\lambda)}_{Y|X=x}\right)-D\left(P^{(h,Q,\lambda)}_{Y|X=x}\middle\|Q\right)+D\left(P\middle\|Q\right),

(24)

where (

III
Characterization of
\mathsf{G}_{h}\left(x,P_{1},P_{2}\right)
in (
1
)

The main result of this section is the following theorem.

Theorem 4
.

For all probability measures
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
, both absolutely continuous with respect to a given
\sigma
-finite measure
Q
on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
, the variation
\mathsf{G}_{h}\left(x,P_{1},P_{2}\right)
in (
1
) satisfies,

\displaystyle\mathsf{G}_{h}\left(x,P_{1},P_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda}\bigg(D\left(P_{1}\middle\|P^{\left(h,Q,\lambda\right)}_{Y|X=x}\right)-D\left(P_{2}\middle\|P^{\left(h,Q,\lambda\right)}_{Y|X=x}\right)

(25)

\displaystyle+D\left(P_{2}\middle\|Q\right)-D\left(P_{1}\middle\|Q\right)\bigg),

where the probability measure
P^{\left(h,Q,\lambda\right)}_{Y|X=x}
, with
\lambda\neq 0
, is an
(h,Q,\lambda)
-Gibbs probability measure.

Proof:

The proof follows from Lemma
3
and by observing that
\mathsf{G}_{h}\left(x,P_{1},P_{2}\right)=\mathsf{G}_{h}\left(x,P_{1},P^{(h,Q,\lambda)}_{Y|X=x}\right)-\mathsf{G}_{h}\left(x,P_{2},P^{(h,Q,\lambda)}_{Y|X=x}\right)
.
∎

Theorem
4
might be particularly simplified in the case in which the reference measure
Q
is a probability measure.
Consider for instance the case in which
P_{1}\ll P_{2}
(or
P_{2}\ll P_{1}
). In such a case, the reference measure might be chosen as
P_{2}
(or
P_{1}
), as shown hereunder.

Corollary 5
.

Consider the variation
\mathsf{G}_{h}\left(x,P_{1},P_{2}\right)
in (
1
). If the probability measure
P_{1}
is absolutely continuous with respect to
P_{2}
, then,

\displaystyle\mathsf{G}_{h}\left(x,P_{1},P_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda}\bigg(D\left(P_{1}\middle\|P^{\left(h,P_{2},\lambda\right)}_{Y|X=x}\right)-D\left(P_{2}\middle\|P^{\left(h,P_{2},\lambda\right)}_{Y|X=x}\right)

(26)

\displaystyle-D\left(P_{1}\middle\|P_{2}\right)\bigg).

Alternatively, if the probability measure
P_{2}
is absolutely continuous with respect to
P_{1}
, then,

\displaystyle\mathsf{G}_{h}\left(x,P_{1},P_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda}\bigg(D\left(P_{1}\middle\|P^{\left(h,P_{1},\lambda\right)}_{Y|X=x}\right)-D\left(P_{2}\middle\|P^{\left(h,P_{1},\lambda\right)}_{Y|X=x}\right)

(27)

\displaystyle+D\left(P_{2}\middle\|P_{1}\right)\bigg),

where the probability measures
P^{\left(h,P_{1},\lambda\right)}_{Y|X=x}
and
P^{\left(h,P_{2},\lambda\right)}_{Y|X=x}
are respectively
(h,P_{1},\lambda)
- and
(h,P_{2},\lambda)
-Gibbs probability measures, with
\lambda\neq 0
.

In the case in which neither
P_{1}
is absolutely continuous with respect to
P_{2}
; nor
P_{2}
is absolutely continuous with respect to
P_{1}
, the reference measure
Q
in Theorem
4
can always be chosen as a convex combination of
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
. That is, for all Borel sets
\mathcal{A}\in\mathscr{B}\left(\mathbb{R}^{m}\right)
,

Q\left(\mathcal{A}\right)=\alpha P_{1}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)+(1-\lambda)P_{2}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
, with
\alpha\in(0,1)
.

Theorem
4
can be especialized to the
specific cases in which
Q
is the Lebesgue or the counting measure.

If
Q
is the Lebesgue measure

the probability measures
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
in (
25
) admit probability density functions
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
, respectively. Moreover, the terms
-D\left(P_{1}\middle\|Q\right)
and
-D\left(P_{2}\middle\|Q\right)
are Shannon’s differential entropies
[
1
]
induced by
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
, denoted by
\mathsf{h}(P_{1})
and
\mathsf{h}(P_{2})
, respectively. That is, for all
i\in\{1,2\}
,

\mathsf{h}(P_{i})\triangleq-\int f_{i}(x)\log f_{i}(x)\mathrm{d}x
.
The probability measure

The main result of this section is a characterization of
\bar{\mathsf{G}}_{h}\left(P_{Y},P_{Y|X},P_{X}\right)
in (
8
), which describes the variation of the expectation of the function
h
when the probability measure changes from the joint probability measure
P_{Y|X}P_{X}
to the product of its marginals
P_{Y}\cdot P_{X}

This result is presented hereunder and involves the mutual information
I\left(P_{Y|X};P_{X}\right)
and lautum information
L\left(P_{Y|X};P_{X}\right)
, defined as follows:

\displaystyle I\left(P_{Y|X};P_{X}\right)

\displaystyle\triangleq

\displaystyle\int D\left(P_{Y|X=x}\middle\|P_{Y}\right)\mathrm{d}P_{X}(x);\mbox{ and }

(35)

\displaystyle L\left(P_{Y|X};P_{X}\right)

\displaystyle\triangleq

\displaystyle\int D\left(P_{Y}\middle\|P_{Y|X=x}\right)\mathrm{d}P_{X}(x).

(36)

Theorem 8
.

Consider the expected variation
\bar{\mathsf{G}}_{h}\left(P_{Y},P_{Y|X},P_{X}\right)
in (
8
) and assume that, for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
:

1.

The probability measures
P_{Y}
and
P_{Y|X=x}
are both absolutely continuous with respect to a given
\sigma
-finite measure

; and

2.

The probability measures
P_{Y}
and
P_{Y|X=x}
are mutually absolutely continuous.

Then, it follows that

\displaystyle\bar{\mathsf{G}}_{h}\left(P_{Y},P_{Y|X},P_{X}\right)=\frac{1}{\lambda}\Bigg(I\left(P_{Y|X};P_{X}\right)+L\left(P_{Y|X};P_{X}\right)

\displaystyle+\int\int\log\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}P_{Y|X=x}}{\mathrm{d}P^{\left(h,Q,\lambda\right)}_{Y|X=x}}(y)\right)\mathrm{d}P_{Y}(y)\mathrm{d}P_{X}(x)

\displaystyle-\int\int\log\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}P_{Y|X=x}}{\mathrm{d}P^{\left(h,Q,\lambda\right)}_{Y|X=x}}(y)\right)\mathrm{d}P_{Y|X=x}(y)\mathrm{d}P_{X}(x)\Bigg),

(37)

where the probability measure
P^{\left(h,Q,\lambda\right)}_{Y|X}
, with
\lambda\neq 0

(h,Q,\lambda)
-Gibbs conditional probability measure.

The proof follows from Theorem

and leads to

\displaystyle\bar{\mathsf{G}}_{h}\left(P_{Y},P_{Y|X},P_{X}\right)

(38)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda}\int\bigg(D\left(P_{Y}\middle\|P^{\left(h,Q,\lambda\right)}_{Y|X=x}\right)-D\left(P_{Y|X=x}\middle\|P^{\left(h,Q,\lambda\right)}_{Y|X=x}\right)

\displaystyle+D\left(P_{Y|X=x}\middle\|Q\right)-D\left(P_{Y}\middle\|Q\right)\bigg)\mathrm{d}P_{X}(x).

The proof continues by noticing that

\displaystyle\int D\left(P_{Y|X=x}\middle\|Q\right)\mathrm{d}P_{X}(x)

\displaystyle I\left(P_{Y|X};P_{X}\right)+D\left(P_{Y}\middle\|Q\right),

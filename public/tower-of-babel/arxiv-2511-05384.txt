The higher-order fractional Schrödinger equation with nonlinear local perturbations: Uniqueness
Giovanni Covi, Ru-Yu Lai, Lili Yan

Abstract
. We study the higher-order fractional Schrödinger equation with local nonlinear perturbations and investigate both the forward and inverse problems. We establish both the Sobolev H s H^{s} and Hölder C s C^{s} estimates for the well-posedness of the nonlinear problem, based on the corresponding estimates derived for the linear fractional Schrödinger equation. For the inverse problem, we show that the local nonlinear perturbations can be uniquely determined from the Dirichlet-to-Neumann map, by using the higher-order linearization and the unique continuation property of the fractional Laplace operator.

1.
Introduction

Let
\Omega
be a bounded open set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 1
, with smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
, and

\Omega_{e}:=\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
be the exterior of
\Omega
.
Let
m\geq 0
be an integer and
s\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\setminus\mathbb{Z}
, where
\mathbb{R}^{+}:=(0,\infty)
and
\mathbb{Z}
denotes the set of all integers in
\mathbb{R}
.
We consider inverse problems for the nonlinear fractional Schrödinger equation (NLFSE):

(1.1)

\begin{cases}(-\Delta)^{s}u+q(x)u+\mathbf{P}(u)=0&\hbox{ in }\Omega,\\
u=f&\hbox{ in }\Omega_{e},\end{cases}

where the fractional Laplacian
(-\Delta)^{s}
is the nonlocal pseudo-differential operator defined by
(-\Delta)^{s}u:=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(|\cdot|^{2s}\mathcal{F}(u))
. Here
\mathcal{F}(u)
represents the Fourier transform of
u
.
The local nonlinear perturbation
\mathbf{P}(u)
is defined as

(1.2)

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}(u):=uP_{1}(x,D)u+u^{2}P_{2}(x,D)u+\ldots+u^{K-1}P_{K-1}(x,D)u,

where
K\geq 2
is an integer, and the linear differential operators
P_{k}(x,D)
of order
m
are of the form

P_{k}(x,D):=\sum_{\sigma:\,|\sigma|\leq m}a_{\sigma,k}(x)D^{\sigma},\quad k=1,\ldots,K-1,

with scalar coefficients
a_{\sigma,k}
depending only on
x
. Here
\sigma=(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{n})
is multi-index of length
|\sigma|=\sigma_{1}+\ldots+\sigma_{n}=\ell
for
\ell\leq m
, and therefore
D^{\sigma}u:=\frac{\partial^{\sigma_{1}}}{\partial x_{1}^{\sigma_{1}}}\ldots\frac{\partial^{\sigma_{n}}}{\partial x_{n}^{\sigma_{n}}}u
.

We show in Theorem
2.1
that (
1.1
) is well-posed in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for any data
f
in a small ball
\mathcal{X}_{\varepsilon_{0}}(\Omega_{e})
in
C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega_{e})
, where for any open set
O\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
we define

\mathcal{X}_{\varepsilon_{0}}(O):=\{f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(O):\,\|f\|_{C^{\infty}_{c}(O)}\leq\varepsilon_{0}\}.

This well-posedness result for (
1.1
) allows us to define the corresponding Dirichlet-to-Neumann (DN) map

\Lambda_{\mathbf{P}}:H^{s}(\Omega_{e})\rightarrow(H^{-s}(\Omega_{e}))^{*},\quad f\mapsto(-\Delta)^{s}u_{f}|_{\Omega_{e}}.

In this framework, we ask the following inverse problem: Does the map
\Lambda_{\mathbf{P}}
uniquely determine the nonlinearity
\mathbf{P}
? The answer is affirmative, and we give a full description of the results in the following Theorem
1.1
:

Theorem 1.1
.

(Unique determination)
Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded open set with smooth
boundary
\partial\Omega
. Let
s\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\setminus\mathbb{Z}
,
\lfloor s\rfloor>\max\{m,\,n/2\}
.
Suppose that
q_{j}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
satisfies
q_{j}\geq 0
in
\Omega
for
j=1,\,2
.
Let
\mathbf{P}_{1}
and
\mathbf{P}_{2}
be nonlinearities of the form (
1.2
) with coefficients
a_{\sigma,k}^{(1)},\,a_{\sigma,k}^{(2)}(x)\in C(\overline{\Omega})
. Let
W_{1},\,W_{2}\subset\Omega_{e}
be two arbitrary nonempty bounded open sets. If the DN-maps
\Lambda_{\mathbf{P}_{j}}:\mathcal{X}_{\varepsilon_{0}}(W_{1})\rightarrow(H^{-s}(\Omega_{e}))^{*}
satisfy

\Lambda_{\mathbf{P}_{1}}f|_{W_{2}}=\Lambda_{\mathbf{P}_{2}}f|_{W_{2}}\quad\mbox{ for all }f\in\mathcal{X}_{\varepsilon_{0}}(W_{1}),

then for all multi-indices
\sigma
such that
0\leq|\sigma|\leq m
and for all
k=1,\ldots,K-1
it holds

q_{1}=q_{2}\quad\hbox{and}\quad a_{\sigma,k}^{(1)}=a_{\sigma,k}^{(2)}\quad\hbox{ in }\Omega,

and thus the nonlinearities
\mathbf{P}_{1}
and
\mathbf{P}_{2}
coincide.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we will introduce the function spaces that will be used throughout the paper. Moreover, we will recall existing results for the linear fractional Schrödinger equation for
s\in(0,1)
, with the goal of establishing the well-posedness for the nonlinear Schrödinger equation. Standing on these facts, the DN-map will be rigorously defined in the last part of this section.

2.1.
Function spaces

For
a\in\mathbb{R}
, we define the Sobolev space

H^{a}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):=\{u\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):\,\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\langle\cdot\rangle^{a}\mathcal{F}(u))\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\}

equipped with the norm

\|u\|_{H^{a}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\langle\cdot\rangle^{a}\mathcal{F}(u))\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

where
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the Schwartz space of rapidly decreasing functions,
\mathcal{F}
is the Fourier transform defined as

\mathcal{F}u(\xi):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-ix\cdot\xi}u(x)\,dx,

and
\langle x\rangle:=(1+|x|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}
.
Let
U
be an open set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We define the following spaces:

\displaystyle H^{a}(U)=\{u|_{U}:\,u\in H^{a}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\},

\displaystyle\widetilde{H}^{a}(U)=\hbox{closure of }C^{\infty}_{c}(U)\hbox{ in }H^{a}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

\displaystyle H^{a}_{0}(U)=\hbox{closure of }C^{\infty}_{c}(U)\hbox{ in }H^{a}(U),

\displaystyle H^{a}_{\overline{U}}=\{u\in H^{a}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):\,\mathrm{supp}(u)\subset\overline{U}\}.

We equip
H^{a}(U)
with the quotient norm
\|u\|_{H^{a}(U)}=\inf\{\|w\|_{H^{a}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:\,w\in H^{a}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\,w|_{U}=u\}
. It is known that
\widetilde{H}^{a}(U)\subseteq H^{a}_{0}(U)
.
If
U
is also a bounded Lipschitz domain, we have (see
[
69
]
)

(\widetilde{H}^{a}(U))^{*}=H^{-a}(U),\quad(H^{a}(U))^{*}=\widetilde{H}^{-a}(U),

\displaystyle\widetilde{H}^{a}(U)=H^{a}_{\overline{U}},\quad a\in\mathbb{R}.

2.2.
The forward problem

To establish the well-posedness of problem (
1.1
) for the NLFSE, we will start by discussing the existence and uniqueness of weak solutions for the linear fractional Schrödinger equation, as well as their properties.

Proposition 2.1
.

[
13
, Lemma 5.1]

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded open set with
C^{1,1}
boundary
\partial\Omega
, and
s\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\setminus\mathbb{Z}
. Suppose that
f\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
F\in(\widetilde{H}^{s}(\Omega))^{*}
, and
q\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
such that
q\geq 0
in
\Omega
.
Then the Dirichlet problem for the linear Schrödinger equation

(2.1)

\begin{cases}(-\Delta)^{s}v+q(x)v=F&\hbox{ in }\Omega,\\
v=f&\hbox{ in }\Omega_{e},\end{cases}

has a unique solution
v\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, which satisfies

(2.2)

\displaystyle\|v\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C(\|F\|_{(\widetilde{H}^{s}(\Omega))^{*}}+\|f\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}),

for a constant
C>0
independent of
u
,
F
, and
f
.

For
s\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\setminus\mathbb{Z}
, we will show that the solution
v
indeed belongs to
C^{s}
Hölder space. The case
s\in(0,1)
was presented in
[
49
]
.
Before proving the
C^{s}
regularity for (
2.1
), we present the following lemma based on the important regularity results by Grubb
[
33
]
, in which more general pseudodifferential operators and functional spaces are considered.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
, be a bounded and smooth open domain, and
s\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\setminus\mathbb{Z}
. Let
g\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, and assume that
w\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a solution to the following problem

\begin{cases}(-\Delta)^{s}w=g&\hbox{ in }\Omega,\\
w=0&\hbox{ on }\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega.\end{cases}

Then for all
\beta\in(0,2s)
there exists a constant
C>0
depending on
\Omega,\beta
and
s
such that for any
K\subset\subset\Omega
it holds

(2.3)

\displaystyle\|w\|_{C^{\beta}(K)}\leq C\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}.

Moreover, there exists a constant
C>0
depending on
\Omega
and
s
such that

(2.4)

\displaystyle\|w\|_{C^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}.

Proof.

Choosing
t=0
and
a=s
in
[
33
, Theorem 4]
, we obtain the estimate

\|w\|_{C^{2s-\varepsilon}(\Omega)}\leq C\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)},

for
0<\varepsilon<2s
, which implies (
2.3
). With this, following a similar argument as in the proof of Lemma 2.9 and Proposition 1.1 in
[
71
]
by extending the interior regularity to the whole space, one deduces that the bounded weak solution
w
is in
C^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and it satisfies the estimate (
2.4
).
∎

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
, be a bounded and smooth open domain. Let
s\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\setminus\mathbb{Z}
, and assume
\lfloor s\rfloor>n/2
. Suppose that
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega_{e})
,
F\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, and

q\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
such that
q\geq 0
in
\Omega
.
Let
v\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
solve (
2.1
).
Then
v\in C^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and it satisfies

\|v\|_{C^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\left(\|F\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\|f\|_{C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega_{e})}\right),

for a constant
C>0
independent of
v,F
and
f
.

Proof.

Proposition
2.1
ensures that there exists a unique solution
v\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
n/2\leq\lfloor s\rfloor<s
, which is contained in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
due to the Sobolev embedding theorem. Also by (
2.2
)
v
satisfies

(2.5)

\|v\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C\left(\|F\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\|f\|_{C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega_{e})}\right).

We now extend
f
outside of
\Omega_{e}
by
0
and retain the same notation. This gives
\|f\|_{C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\|f\|_{C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega_{e})}
and, in particular,

\|(-\Delta)^{s}f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\|(-\Delta)^{s-\lfloor s\rfloor}(-\Delta)^{\lfloor s\rfloor}f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|(-\Delta)^{\lfloor s\rfloor}f\|_{C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|f\|_{C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

where we used the fact that
\|(-\Delta)^{\tau}g\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\|g\|_{C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
for
g\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\tau\in(0,1)
.

Let
v_{0}:=v-f
. Then
v_{0}\in\widetilde{H}^{s}(\Omega)
is a solution to

(2.6)

(-\Delta)^{s}v_{0}=F-q(x)v-(-\Delta)^{s}f\quad\hbox{in}\;\Omega,\qquad v_{0}=0\quad\hbox{in}\;\Omega_{e},

where the right-hand side
F-q(x)v-(-\Delta)^{s}f
is in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
since
v,\,(-\Delta)^{s}f\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, and moreover
q,\,F\in L^{\infty}
by assumption.
By (
2.5
) and Lemma
2.1
,

\|v_{0}\|_{C^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C(\|F\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\|f\|_{C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega_{e})}),

and thus

\|v\|_{C^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\|v_{0}\|_{C^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|f\|_{C^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\left(\|F\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\|f\|_{C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega_{e})}\right).

∎

With the above propositions, we are now ready to show the well-posedness for the nonlinear fractional Schrödinger equation, see
[
50
, Theorem 2.1]
for similar arguments.

Theorem 2.1

(Well-posedness for the nonlinear equation)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded open domain with smooth
boundary
\partial\Omega
. Let
s\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\setminus\mathbb{Z}
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
satisfy
\lfloor s\rfloor>\max\{m,\,n/2\}
.
Suppose that
q\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
satisfies
q\geq 0
in
\Omega
, and the coefficients in
\mathbf{P}(u)
satisfy
a_{\sigma,k}\in C(\overline{\Omega})
.
Then there exists a sufficiently small
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that for any
f\in\mathcal{X}_{\varepsilon_{0}}(\Omega_{e})
,
the Dirichlet problem (
1.1
) has a unique solution
u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap C^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Moreover, there exists a constant
C>0
, independent of
u
and
f
, such that

\|u\|_{C^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|u\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|f\|_{C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega_{e})}.

Proof.

We will use the contraction mapping principle to establish the proof.
First, we find the solution to the linear equation. By Proposition
2.1
and Proposition
2.2
, given any
\|f\|_{C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega_{e})}\leq\varepsilon_{0}
, there exists a unique solution
u_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap C^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
of the linear fractional Schrödinger equation

\begin{cases}(-\Delta)^{s}u_{0}+qu_{0}=0&\hbox{ in }\Omega,\\
u_{0}=f&\hbox{ in }\Omega_{e},\end{cases}

and it verifies

(2.7)

\displaystyle\|u_{0}\|_{C^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|f\|_{C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega_{e})}.

In order to prove the existence of a solution to (
1.1
), it suffices to show the existence of the solution
v:=u-u_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap C^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to

(2.8)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}(-\Delta)^{s}v+q(x)v=G(v)&\hbox{ in }\Omega,\\
v=0&\hbox{ in }\Omega_{e},\end{cases}

where

G(v):=-\mathbf{P}(v+u_{0})=-\sum^{K-1}_{j=1}(v+u_{0})^{j}P_{j}(x,D)(v+u_{0}).

We shall first establish the
C^{s}
estimates of
v
, and only then shall we present the
H^{s}
estimate. Given any
\delta\in(0,1)
to be fixed later, we define the complete metric set

\mathcal{M}_{\delta}:=\{\phi\in C^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):\,\phi|_{\Omega_{e}}=0,\,\|\phi\|_{C^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\delta\}.

It is clear that
G:\mathcal{M}_{\delta}\rightarrow L^{\infty}(\Omega)
since
C^{s-m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset L^{\infty}(\Omega)
for
s\geq m
. We also define
\mathcal{L}_{s}^{-1}:F\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)\to w\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap C^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to be the solution operator of the following problem with a source term
F\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
:

(2.9)

\begin{cases}(-\Delta)^{s}w+qw=F&\hbox{ in }\Omega,\\
w=0&\hbox{ in }\Omega_{e}.\end{cases}

By Proposition
2.2
, for any
\phi\in\mathcal{M}_{\delta}
we get

\|\mathcal{L}_{s}^{-1}\circ G(\phi)\|_{C^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|G(\phi)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq CC^{\prime}\sum^{K-1}_{j=1}(\delta+\varepsilon_{0})^{j+1},

where
C^{\prime}:=\sum^{K-1}_{j=1}\sum_{|\sigma|\leq m}\|a_{\sigma,j}\|_{C(\overline{\Omega})}
.
Let
0<\varepsilon_{0}<C^{\prime\prime}\delta
for some constant
C^{\prime\prime}>0
. We choose
\delta
sufficiently small so that

(2.10)

\displaystyle CC^{\prime}\sum^{K-1}_{j=1}(\delta+\varepsilon_{0})^{j+1}<\delta,

which then leads to
\mathcal{L}_{s}^{-1}\circ G(\phi)\in\mathcal{M}_{\delta}
. To apply the contraction mapping principle, it remains to show that the map

\mathcal{A}:=\mathcal{L}_{s}^{-1}\circ G:\,\mathcal{M}_{\delta}\to\mathcal{M}_{\delta}

is strictly contractive. Let us estimate for
\phi_{1},\,\phi_{2}\in\mathcal{M}_{\delta}

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{A}(\phi_{1})-\mathcal{A}(\phi_{2})\|_{C^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|G(\phi_{1})-G(\phi_{2})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{j=1}^{K-1}\Big(\|(\phi_{1}+u_{0})^{j}P_{j}(x,D)(\phi_{1}-\phi_{2})\|_{C^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\quad+\|\left((\phi_{1}+u_{0})^{j}-(\phi_{2}+u_{0})^{j}\right)P_{j}(x,D)(\phi_{2}+u_{0})\|\Big)

\displaystyle\leq CC^{\prime}K\sum^{K-1}_{j=1}(\delta+\varepsilon_{0})^{j}\|\phi_{1}-\phi_{2}\|_{C^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Then
\mathcal{A}
is contractive if we further require
\delta
sufficiently small so that
CC^{\prime}K\sum^{K-1}_{j=1}(\delta+\varepsilon_{0})^{j}<1
.
Based on the contraction mapping principle, there exists a fixed point
v\in\mathcal{M}_{\delta}
such that
\mathcal{A}(v)=v
. Indeed, this
v
then solves (
2.8
) and satisfies

\displaystyle\|v\|_{C^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\|\mathcal{L}_{s}^{-1}\circ G(v)\|_{C^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|G(v)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq CC^{\prime}\sum^{K-1}_{j=1}(\delta+\varepsilon_{0})^{j}\left(\|v\|_{C^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|u_{0}\|_{C^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\right)

\displaystyle\leq CC^{\prime}\sum^{K-1}_{j=1}(\delta+\varepsilon_{0})^{j}\left(\|v\|_{C^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|f\|_{C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega_{e})}\right).

As the coefficient in front of
\|v\|_{C^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
on the right is less than
1
, this term can be absorbed by the left-hand side. Hence, we obtain

\|v\|_{C^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|f\|_{C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega_{e})}.

Now we estimate the
H^{s}
norm of
v
. Applying Proposition
2.1
to (
2.8
), we obtain

\|v\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|G(v)\|_{(\widetilde{H}^{s}(\Omega))^{*}}=C\|G(v)\|_{H^{-s}(\Omega)}\leq CC^{\prime}\sum^{K-1}_{j=1}(\delta+\varepsilon_{0})^{j}\left(\|v\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\right),

where we applied the boundedness of
v,\,u_{0}\in C^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. The first term on the right-hand side can be absorbed by the left if
\delta
is sufficiently small. Hence, as
u=u_{0}+v
, the above two estimates together with (
2.7
) yield the desired stability estimate of
u
in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
This completes the proof.
∎

2.3.
The Dirichlet-to-Neumann map

We have showed in Theorem
2.1
that there exists a unique solution
u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap C^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to the nonlinear fractional Schrödinger equation (
1.1
). Define the bilinear form
B_{\mathbf{P}}:H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to\mathbb{R}
by

B_{\mathbf{P}}(u,v):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}v\,dx+\int_{\Omega}quv\,dx+\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{P}(u)v\,dx.

We can now define the DN-map
\Lambda_{\mathbf{P}}
:

Definition 2.1
.

The DN-map
\Lambda_{\mathbf{P}}:\mathcal{X}_{\varepsilon_{0}}(\Omega_{e})\cap X\to X^{*}
is the bounded linear operator given by

(2.11)

\langle\Lambda_{\mathbf{P}}[f],[v]\rangle=B_{\mathbf{P}}(u_{f},v)\quad\hbox{ for all }f\in\mathcal{X}_{\varepsilon_{0}}(\Omega_{e}),\,\,v\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

where
u_{f}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap C^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the unique solution of

(-\Delta)^{s}u+qu+\mathbf{P}(u)=0

with
u-f\in\widetilde{H}^{s}(\Omega)
.
Here the quotient space
X:=H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})/\widetilde{H}^{s}(\Omega)
is equipped with the norm

\|[f]\|_{X}:=\inf_{\phi\in\widetilde{H}^{s}(\Omega)}\|f+\phi\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},\quad f\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

and
X^{*}
is the dual space of
X
.

Because the DN-map acts on a quotient space, we have to make sure that it is well-defined as indicated above. We also need to show that it is indeed a bounded linear operator. In what follows, we will use
f
rather than
[f]
in order to simplify the notation.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded open domain with smooth
boundary
\partial\Omega
. Let
s\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\setminus\mathbb{Z}
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
satisfy
\lfloor s\rfloor>\max\{m,\,n/2\}
.
Suppose that
q\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
satisfies
q\geq 0
in
\Omega
, and the coefficients in
\mathbf{P}(u)
satisfy
a_{\sigma,k}\in C(\overline{\Omega})
.
Then the DN-map
\Lambda_{\mathbf{P}}
is well-defined and bounded.

Proof.

We first show that
\Lambda_{\mathbf{P}}
only depends on the equivalence classes. Let
\phi,\,\psi\in\widetilde{H}^{s}(\Omega)
. Since
u_{f}
and
u_{f+\phi}
both solve problem (
1.1
) with the same exterior data, the well-posedness of (
1.1
) implies
u_{f}=u_{f+\phi}
.
By the linearity of
B_{\mathbf{P}}
in the second component, we have

B_{\mathbf{P}}(u_{f+\phi},v+\psi)=B_{\mathbf{P}}(u_{f},v+\psi)=B_{\mathbf{P}}(u_{f},v)+B_{\mathbf{P}}(u_{f},\psi).

Using the fact that
\psi=0
in
\Omega_{e}
and
u_{f}
solves (
1.1
), we get
B_{\mathbf{P}}(u_{f},\psi)=0
. This proves that
\langle\Lambda_{\mathbf{P}}(f+\phi),v+\psi\rangle=\langle\Lambda_{\mathbf{P}}f,v\rangle
, and thus
\Lambda_{\mathbf{P}}
is well-defined.

To show the boundedness, we note that
u_{f}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap C^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\lfloor s\rfloor>\max\{m,\,n/2\}
, and the coefficients
q
and
a_{\sigma,k}
are bounded. Therefore

\displaystyle|B_{\mathbf{P}}(u_{f},v)|

\displaystyle\leq C\|u_{f}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|v\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+C\Big(\|q\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum^{K-1}_{k=1}\sum_{|\sigma|\leq m}\|a_{\sigma,k}\|_{C(\overline{\Omega})}\|D^{\sigma}u_{f}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|u_{f}\|^{k-1}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\Big)\|u_{f}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|v\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+\varepsilon_{0}^{2}+\ldots+\varepsilon_{0}^{K-1})\|u_{f}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|v\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|_{C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega_{e})}\|v\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

where in the last two inequalities, we used
\|u_{f}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|f\|_{C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega_{e})}
.
∎

3.
Linearization

In this section we will show how to linearize the problem and the relative DN-map. Recall that

\mathbf{P}(u)=uP_{1}(x,D)u+u^{2}P_{2}(x,D)u+\ldots+u^{K-1}P_{K-1}(x,D)u,

where
P_{k}(x,D)=\sum_{|\sigma|\leq m}a_{\sigma,k}(x)D^{\sigma}
for
k=1,\ldots,K-1.

Let
q\in C(\overline{\Omega})
be such that
q\geq 0
in
\Omega
.

3.1.
Linearization

For
f=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{K})
with
f_{\ell}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega_{e})
,
\|f_{\ell}\|_{C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega_{e})}\leq 1/K
, Theorem
2.1
yields that, for sufficiently small
\varepsilon=(\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{K})
with
|\varepsilon|<\varepsilon_{0}
, there exists a unique solution
u_{\varepsilon}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap C^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
of the following problem

(3.1)

\begin{cases}[(-\Delta)^{s}+q(x)]u_{\varepsilon}=-\mathbf{P}(u_{\varepsilon})&\hbox{ in }\Omega,\\
u_{\varepsilon}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{K}\varepsilon_{\ell}f_{\ell}=:\varepsilon\cdot f&\hbox{ in }\Omega_{e}.\end{cases}

Moreover, we have

(3.2)

\displaystyle\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{C^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|\varepsilon\cdot f\|_{C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega_{e})}\leq C|\varepsilon|\sum_{\ell=1}^{K}\|f_{\ell}\|_{C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega_{e})}\leq C|\varepsilon|,

where the constant
C>0
is independent of
u_{\varepsilon}
and
f_{\ell}
. We will expand the solution
u_{\varepsilon}
in terms of the small parameter
\varepsilon
. Let
\alpha=(\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{K})\in\mathbb{N}^{K}_{0}
be a
K
-dimensional multi-index of nonnegative integers. Combining Propositions
2.1
and
2.2
, we know that there exists a unique solution
w_{\alpha}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap C^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to the linear fractional Schrödinger equation

(3.3)

\begin{cases}[(-\Delta)^{s}+q(x)]w_{\alpha}=-\mathcal{T}_{\alpha}&\hbox{ in }\Omega,\\
w_{\alpha}=D^{\alpha}_{\varepsilon}(\varepsilon\cdot f)|_{\varepsilon=0}&\hbox{ in }\Omega_{e},\end{cases}

where the inhomogeneous term is

(3.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{\alpha}:=\sum^{|\alpha|-1}_{\ell=1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{\ell}\in\mathbb{N}^{K}_{0}\setminus\{0,\cdots,0\}:\\
\beta:=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\beta_{j}<\alpha\end{subarray}}\binom{\alpha}{\beta}\binom{\beta}{\beta_{1},\cdots,\beta_{\ell}}\bigg(\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}w_{\beta_{j}}\bigg)P_{\ell}(x,D)w_{\alpha-\beta}.

Note here
\mathcal{T}_{\alpha}=0
when
|\alpha|=0,\,1
.
Furthermore, the solution satisfies the stability estimate

(3.5)

\displaystyle\|w_{\alpha}\|_{C^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|w_{\alpha}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\begin{cases}C\|D^{\alpha}_{\varepsilon}(\varepsilon\cdot f)|_{\varepsilon=0}\|_{C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega_{e})}&|\alpha|=1,\\
C\|\mathcal{T}_{\alpha}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)},&|\alpha|\geq 2.\end{cases}

Here we denote

\binom{\alpha}{\beta}=\frac{\alpha!}{\beta!(\alpha-\beta)!}\quad\hbox{and}\quad\binom{\beta}{\beta_{1},\cdots,\beta_{k}}=\frac{\beta!}{\beta_{1}!\cdots\beta_{k}!}.

Observe that the definition of
\mathcal{T}_{\alpha}
only requires knowledge of those
w_{\beta}
with
\beta<\alpha
, and that in turn
w_{\alpha}
can be computed from
\mathcal{T}_{\alpha}
as the solution of (
3.3
). Thus the definitions of
\mathcal{T}_{\alpha}
and
w_{\alpha}
are not circular.

Let us explicitly write out the cases
|\alpha|\leq 2
. We first note that
w_{(0,\ldots,0)}=0
as
w_{(0,\ldots,0)}
solves the problem (
3.3
) with zero source and exterior value.
Also, for
|\alpha|=1
, we have
\alpha=e_{\ell}:=(0,\ldots,0,1,0,\ldots,0)
, whose
\ell\,th
-component is
1
for some
\ell\in\{1,\ldots,K\}
. Then

w_{\alpha}\equiv v_{\ell}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap C^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfies

(3.6)

\begin{cases}[(-\Delta)^{s}+q(x)]v_{\ell}=0&\hbox{ in }\Omega,\\
v_{\ell}=f_{\ell}&\hbox{ in }\Omega_{e},\end{cases}

and

(3.7)

\displaystyle\|v_{\ell}\|_{C^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|v_{\ell}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|f_{\ell}\|_{C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega_{e})},

where we used Proposition
2.1
, Proposition
2.2
and the fact
\|f\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|f\|_{C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega_{e})}
.

Moreover, for
|\alpha|=2
, that is,
\alpha=e_{\ell_{1}}+e_{\ell_{2}}
, for
\ell_{j}\in\{1,\ldots,K\}
,

w_{\alpha}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap C^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the unique solution to the problem

(3.8)

\begin{cases}[(-\Delta)^{s}+q(x)]w_{\alpha}=-\mathcal{T}_{\alpha}&\hbox{ in }\Omega,\\
w_{\alpha}=0&\hbox{ in }\Omega_{e},\end{cases}

and also satisfies the estimate (note
\mathcal{T}_{e_{\ell_{1}}+e_{\ell_{2}}}=v_{\ell_{1}}P_{1}(x,D)v_{\ell_{2}}+v_{\ell_{2}}P_{1}(x,D)v_{\ell_{1}}
),

(3.9)

\displaystyle\|w_{\alpha}\|_{C^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|w_{\alpha}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|v_{\ell_{1}}P_{1}(x,D)v_{\ell_{2}}+v_{\ell_{2}}P_{1}(x,D)v_{\ell_{1}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}.

In the Appendix, we prove that the following
\varepsilon
-expansion of
u_{\varepsilon}
holds:

Proposition 3.1
.

Suppose that all the assumptions in Theorem
2.1
hold.
Let
f=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{K})
, where
f_{\ell}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega_{e})
with
\|f_{\ell}\|_{C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega_{e})}\leq 1/K
,
and
\varepsilon=(\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{K})
with
|\varepsilon|<\varepsilon_{0}
such that

\|\varepsilon\cdot f\|_{C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega_{e})}<\varepsilon_{0}.

Then there exists a unique solution
u_{\varepsilon}
to the problem (
3.1
) and its expansion in terms of the small parameter
\varepsilon
has the following form

(3.10)

u_{\varepsilon}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{K}\varepsilon_{\ell}v_{\ell}+\sum_{|\alpha|=2}\frac{\varepsilon^{\alpha}w_{\alpha}}{\alpha!}+\sum_{|\alpha|=3}\frac{\varepsilon^{\alpha}w_{\alpha}}{\alpha!}+\ldots+\sum_{|\alpha|=K}\frac{\varepsilon^{\alpha}w_{\alpha}}{\alpha!}+\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}.

Here the remainder term
\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}
solves the Dirichlet problem

(3.11)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}(-\Delta)^{s}\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}+q(x)\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}=-\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{K}&\hbox{ in }\Omega,\\
\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}=0&\hbox{ in }\Omega_{e},\end{cases}

with the inhomogeneous term

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{K}:=\mathbf{P}(u_{\varepsilon})-\sum^{K}_{j=1}\sum_{|\alpha|=j}\frac{\varepsilon^{\alpha}\mathcal{T}_{\alpha}}{\alpha!}.\end{aligned}

Moreover, we have

(3.12)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}\|_{C^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|\varepsilon\cdot f\|_{C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega_{e})}^{K+1}.

3.2.
Linearization of the Dirichlet-to-Neumann map

Proposition
3.1
implies that the solution
u_{\varepsilon}
to (
3.1
) is differentiable with respect to
\varepsilon
at
\varepsilon=0
in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap C^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
up to the
K
-th order.
In particular, by applying the multivariate finite differences, which are approximations of the derivative, we obtain

(3.13)

\frac{\partial^{|\alpha|}}{\partial\varepsilon^{\alpha}}\Big|_{\varepsilon=0}u_{\varepsilon}=w_{\alpha},\quad\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{K}_{0}.

This allows us to define the partial derivatives of the DN-map
\Lambda_{\mathbf{P}}
with respect to
\varepsilon
at
\varepsilon=0
as follows:

\displaystyle\langle\frac{\partial^{|\alpha|}}{\partial\varepsilon^{\alpha}}\Big|_{\varepsilon=0}\Lambda_{\mathbf{P}}(\varepsilon\cdot f),g\rangle:=\frac{\partial^{|\alpha|}}{\partial\varepsilon^{\alpha}}\Big|_{\varepsilon=0}B_{\mathbf{P}}(u_{\varepsilon},g)\quad\hbox{for }\varepsilon\cdot f\in\mathcal{X}_{\varepsilon_{0}}(\Omega_{e}),\quad g\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

By using the derivatives given above, and the fact
u_{\varepsilon}=0
when
\varepsilon=0
, we obtain the following lemma through direct computation.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
f=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{K})
, where
f_{\ell}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega_{e})
satisfies
\|f_{\ell}\|_{C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega_{e})}\leq 1/K
. For sufficiently small
\varepsilon=(\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{K})
such that
|\varepsilon|<\varepsilon_{0}
, we have for
|\alpha|\geq 1

that

\displaystyle\langle\frac{\partial^{|\alpha|}}{\partial\varepsilon^{\alpha}}\Big|_{\varepsilon=0}\Lambda_{\mathbf{P}}(\varepsilon\cdot f),g\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}w_{\alpha}(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}g\,dx+\int_{\Omega}q(x)w_{\alpha}\,g\,dx+\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{T}_{\alpha}g\,dx,\quad\hbox{for }g\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Notice that
\mathcal{T}_{\alpha}\equiv 0
when
|\alpha|=1
.

Proof.

We first show the first-order derivative of the DN-map.
For any
g\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, by the definition of
\Lambda_{\mathbf{P}}
, taking the derivatives on the bilinear form yields that

\displaystyle\langle\partial_{\varepsilon_{\ell}}\big|_{\varepsilon=0}\Lambda_{\mathbf{P}}(\varepsilon\cdot f),g\rangle=\partial_{\varepsilon_{\ell}}\big|_{\varepsilon=0}B_{\mathbf{P}}(u_{\varepsilon},g)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}v_{\ell}(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}g\,dx+\int_{\Omega}q(x)v_{\ell}\,g\,dx.

Note that all nonlinear terms vanish as
u_{\varepsilon}=0
when
\varepsilon=0
.
Similarly, let
\alpha=e_{\ell_{1}}+e_{\ell_{2}}
, for second-order partial derivatives of
\Lambda_{\mathbf{P}}
, since only linear terms and the quadratic nonlinearity in
B_{\mathbf{P}}
remain after taking derivatives, we get

\displaystyle\langle\partial_{\varepsilon_{\ell_{1}}}\partial_{\varepsilon_{\ell_{2}}}\big|_{\varepsilon=0}\Lambda_{\mathbf{P}}(\varepsilon\cdot f),g\rangle

\displaystyle=\partial_{\varepsilon_{\ell_{1}}}\partial_{\varepsilon_{\ell_{2}}}\big|_{\varepsilon=0}B_{\mathbf{P}}(u_{\varepsilon},g)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}w_{\alpha}(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}g\,dx+\int_{\Omega}q(x)w_{\alpha}\,g\,dx

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\Omega}\left(v_{\ell_{1}}P_{1}(x,D)v_{\ell_{2}}+v_{\ell_{2}}P_{1}(x,D)v_{\ell_{1}}\right)g\,dx.

Following a similar argument, we can also derive the integral representation of

\frac{\partial^{|\alpha|}}{\partial\varepsilon^{\alpha}}\Big|_{\varepsilon=0}\Lambda_{\mathbf{P}}(\varepsilon\cdot f)
for
|\alpha|\geq 3
.
∎

4.
Proof of the main theorem

In this section, we will discuss how to recover all the unknown coefficients in the equation (
1.1
), thus proving Theorem
1.1
. First, through the first-order linearization, the problem is reduced to the inverse problem for the linearized equation which only contains
q
. Once this is established, we apply the higher-order linearization technique to determine each
a_{\sigma,k}
.

Let
u_{j,\varepsilon}
(
j=1,\,2
) be the small unique solution to the Dirichlet problem:

(4.1)

\begin{cases}[(-\Delta)^{s}+q_{j}(x)]u_{j,\varepsilon}=-\mathbf{P}_{j}(u_{j,\varepsilon})&\hbox{ in }\Omega,\\
u_{j,\varepsilon}=\varepsilon\cdot f&\hbox{ in }\Omega_{e},\end{cases}

where
f_{\ell}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega_{e})
with
\|f_{\ell}\|_{C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega_{e})}\leq 1/K
and
\varepsilon=(\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{K})
with
|\varepsilon|<\varepsilon_{0}
.
Here

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{j}(u_{j,\varepsilon})=\sum^{K-1}_{k=1}u_{j,\varepsilon}^{k}P_{j,k}(x,D)u_{j,\varepsilon},\qquad\hbox{ where }P_{j,k}(x,D)=\sum_{|\sigma|\leq m}a_{\sigma,k}^{(j)}(x)D^{\sigma}.

For
\varepsilon_{0}
small enough, one can write the
\varepsilon
-expansion in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap C^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

(4.2)

u_{j,\varepsilon}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{K}\varepsilon_{\ell}v_{j,\ell}+\sum_{|\alpha|=2}\frac{\varepsilon^{\alpha}w_{j,\alpha}}{\alpha!}+\sum_{|\alpha|=3}\frac{\varepsilon^{\alpha}w_{j,\alpha}}{\alpha!}+\ldots+\sum_{|\alpha|=K}\frac{\varepsilon^{\alpha}w_{j,\alpha}}{\alpha!}+\mathcal{R}_{j,\varepsilon},

where
v_{j,\ell}
satisfy

(4.3)

\begin{cases}[(-\Delta)^{s}+q_{j}(x)]v_{j,\ell}=0&\hbox{ in }\Omega,\\
v_{j,\ell}=f_{\ell}&\hbox{ in }\Omega_{e},\end{cases}

and
w_{j,\alpha}
(
|\alpha|\geq 2
) satisfy

(4.4)

\begin{cases}[(-\Delta)^{s}+q(x)]w_{j,\alpha}=-\mathcal{T}_{j,\alpha}&\hbox{ in }\Omega,\\
w_{j,\alpha}=0&\hbox{ in }\Omega_{e}.\end{cases}

4.1.
Recovering the linear coefficient

To recover the linear coefficient
q
, we just need one small parameter. Thus, we take
\varepsilon_{1}=1
and
\varepsilon_{2}=\ldots=\varepsilon_{K}=0
. Then
\varepsilon\cdot f=\varepsilon_{1}f_{1}
for some
f_{1}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(W_{1})
. Taking partial derivatives
\partial_{\varepsilon_{1}}\big|_{\varepsilon=0}
on the DN-map
\Lambda_{\mathbf{P}_{j}}
,
j=1,2
, yields for all
g\in C^{\infty}_{c}(W_{2})
that

\displaystyle\langle\partial_{\varepsilon_{1}}\big|_{\varepsilon=0}\Lambda_{\mathbf{P}_{j}}(\varepsilon\cdot f),g\rangle=\partial_{\varepsilon_{1}}\big|_{\varepsilon=0}B_{\mathbf{P}_{j}}(u_{j,\varepsilon},g)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}v_{j,1}(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}g\,dx+\int_{\Omega}q_{j}(x)v_{j,1}\,g\,dx.

The assumption of Theorem
1.1
that

\Lambda_{\mathbf{P}_{1}}h|_{W_{2}}=\Lambda_{\mathbf{P}_{2}}h|_{W_{2}}
, for all
h\in\mathcal{X}_{\varepsilon_{0}}(W_{1})
, together with the equality above, implies that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}v_{1,1}(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}g\,dx+\int_{\Omega}q_{1}(x)v_{1,1}\,g\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}v_{2,1}(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}g\,dx+\int_{\Omega}q_{2}(x)v_{2,1}\,g\,dx,

which indicates the DN-map for the linear problem (
4.3
) are the same.
Now
[
13
, Theorem 1.3]
yields the desired unique determination of the linear coefficient
q(x)
, as stated in the following lemma:

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose the same assumptions as for Theorem
1.1
hold.

Then

\displaystyle q_{1}=q_{2}\quad\hbox{ in }\Omega.

Since Lemma
4.1
gives
q_{1}=q_{2}
, the linearized problems (
4.3
) satisfied by
v_{j,1}
(
j=1,\,2
) are the same. The well-posedness theorem in Section
2
implies

v_{1}:=v_{1,1}=v_{2,1}\quad\hbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Similarly one can get

v_{\ell}:=v_{1,\ell}=v_{2,\ell}\quad\hbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{n},\quad\hbox{ for }\ell=2,\ldots,K.

4.2.
An integral identity for the nonlinear coefficients

We denote

\widetilde{a}_{\sigma,k}(x):=a^{(1)}_{\sigma,k}(x)-a^{(2)}_{\sigma,k}(x),\quad|\sigma|\leq m,\quad k=1,\ldots,K-1,

and

\widetilde{w}_{\alpha}:=w_{1,\alpha}-w_{2,\alpha},\quad 2\leq|\alpha|\leq K.

The function
\widetilde{w}_{\alpha}
then solves
[(-\Delta)^{s}+q(x)]\widetilde{w}_{\alpha}=-(\mathcal{T}_{1,\alpha}-\mathcal{T}_{2,\alpha})
with vanishing exterior data.

Lemma 4.2
.

Given
k
such that
2\leq k\leq K
, assume that

(4.5)

\displaystyle a^{(1)}_{\sigma,t}=a^{(2)}_{\sigma,t}\quad\hbox{for $|\sigma|\leq m$ and $t=1,\ldots,k-2$. }

Then for all
1\leq i\leq k-1
and all multi-indices
\alpha
such that
|\alpha|=i
, we have that

(4.6)

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{1,\alpha}=\mathcal{T}_{2,\alpha}\quad\hbox{and}\quad w_{1,\alpha}=w_{2,\alpha}.

Moreover, for
\alpha=e_{1}+\ldots+e_{k}
with
|\alpha|=k
, the following holds:

\mathcal{T}_{1,\alpha}-\mathcal{T}_{2,\alpha}=\sum_{|\sigma|\leq m}\widetilde{a}_{\sigma,k-1}(x)\left(\sum_{\pi\in S_{k}}v_{\pi_{1}}\ldots v_{\pi_{k-1}}D^{\sigma}v_{\pi_{k}}\right).

Here
\pi:\{1,2,\cdots,k\}\rightarrow\{1,2,\cdots,k\}
is a permutation function, and
S_{k}
is the set of all permutations of
k
elements. We have denoted
\pi_{i}:=\pi(i)
for the purpose of simplifying the notation.

Proof.

We have already shown that in the case
|\alpha|=1
it holds
v_{1,\ell}=v_{2,\ell}
. When
i=|\alpha|=2
, as
\mathcal{T}_{j,\alpha}
only have
v_{j,\ell}
and
a_{\sigma,1}^{(j)}
, the assumption (
4.5
) implies
\mathcal{T}_{1,\alpha}=\mathcal{T}_{2,\alpha}
. This suggests that
w_{1,\alpha}
and
w_{2,\alpha}
(
|\alpha|=2
) solve the same Dirichlet problems, and thus they must be identical due to well-posedness.

Now we apply the induction argument by assuming that

(4.7)

\displaystyle w_{1,\alpha}=w_{2,\alpha}\quad\hbox{for all $|\alpha|\leq j$}

holds for any
2\leq j<i
. We will show that
w_{1,\alpha}=w_{2,\alpha}
for
|\alpha|=j+1
.
Note that the function
\widetilde{w}_{\alpha}
solves
[(-\Delta)^{s}+q(x)]\widetilde{w}_{\alpha}=-(\mathcal{T}_{1,\alpha}-\mathcal{T}_{2,\alpha})
with trivial exterior data.
Here by definition,
\mathcal{T}_{1,\alpha}
and
\mathcal{T}_{2,\alpha}
only depend on
w_{\beta}
for
|\beta|\leq|\alpha|-1=j
and
a^{(j)}_{\sigma,t}
for
|\sigma|\leq m
and
t\leq|\alpha|-1=j
. From (
4.5
) and (
4.7
), this suggests that all terms in
\mathcal{T}_{1,\alpha}
and
\mathcal{T}_{2,\alpha}
coincide. Finally, the well-posedness theorem yields that
w_{1,\alpha}=w_{2,\alpha}
for
|\alpha|=j+1
, which completes the induction argument.

To show the second result, we take
\alpha=e_{1}+\ldots+e_{k}
with
|\alpha|=k
. We already know that (
4.5
) and (
4.6
) hold, which yields every term in
\mathcal{T}_{1,\alpha}
and
\mathcal{T}_{2,\alpha}
are the same, except the nonlinear coefficients in
P_{1,k-1}(x,D)
and
P_{2,k-1}(x,D)
. We get

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{1,\alpha}-\mathcal{T}_{2,\alpha}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{k}\in\mathbb{N}^{K}_{0}\setminus\{0,\cdots,0\}:\\
\alpha=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\beta_{j}\end{subarray}}v_{\beta_{1}}\ldots v_{\beta_{k-1}}(P_{1,k-1}(x,D)-P_{2,k-1}(x,D))v_{\beta_{k}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{|\sigma|\leq m}\widetilde{a}_{\sigma,k-1}(x)\left(\sum_{\pi\in S_{k}}v_{\pi_{1}}\ldots v_{\pi_{k-1}}D^{\sigma}v_{\pi_{k}}\right),

which completes the proof.
∎

To prove unique determination of all nonlinear coefficients, we need the following integral identity that can bridge the DN-map (known data) to the unknown coefficients
a_{\sigma,t}
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
k=2,\ldots,K
. Suppose that (
4.5
) and all the hypotheses in Theorem
1.1
hold.

Then for
\alpha=e_{1}+\ldots+e_{k}
with
|\alpha|=k
,

(4.8)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\sum_{|\sigma|\leq m}\widetilde{a}_{\sigma,k-1}(x)\left(\sum_{\pi\in S_{k}}v_{\pi_{1}}\ldots v_{\pi_{k-1}}D^{\sigma}v_{\pi_{k}}\right)v_{0}\,dx=0,

where

v_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap C^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
solves the adjoint problem

(4.9)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}(-\Delta)^{s}v_{0}+q(x)v_{0}=0&\hbox{ in }\Omega,\\
v_{0}=f_{0}&\hbox{ in }\Omega_{e},\end{cases}

for some
f_{0}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(W_{2})
.

Proof.

For any
k\in\{2,\ldots,K\}
, let
\alpha=e_{1}+\ldots+e_{k}
with
|\alpha|=k
.
Combining

\frac{\partial^{|\alpha|}}{\partial\varepsilon^{\alpha}}\big|_{\varepsilon=0}\Lambda_{\mathbf{P}_{1}}(\varepsilon\cdot f)=\frac{\partial^{|\alpha|}}{\partial\varepsilon^{\alpha}}\big|_{\varepsilon=0}\Lambda_{\mathbf{P}_{2}}(\varepsilon\cdot f)
and Lemma
3.1
gives

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}\widetilde{w}_{\alpha}(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}v_{0}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}q(x)\widetilde{w}_{\alpha}\,v_{0}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}(\mathcal{T}_{1,\alpha}-\mathcal{T}_{2,\alpha})v_{0}\,dx=0.

Using the fact that
v_{0}
solves (
4.9
) and
\widetilde{w}_{\alpha}|_{\Omega_{e}}=0
, the sum of the first two terms on the left-hand side vanishes. Thus we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(\mathcal{T}_{1,\alpha}-\mathcal{T}_{2,\alpha})v_{0}\,dx=0.

This finishes the proof by using Lemma
4.2
.
∎

To proceed, we first recall the following Runge approximation result from
[
13
, Lemma 5.4]
.

Lemma 4.4

(Lemma 5.4 in
[
13
]
)
.

Let
\Omega,\,W\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
respectively be nonempty, open, and bounded sets such that
\overline{W}\cap\overline{\Omega}=\emptyset
. Let
q\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
such that
q\geq 0
in
\Omega
and
v_{f}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be the unique solutions of

\begin{cases}(-\Delta)^{s}v_{f}+q(x)v_{f}=0&\hbox{ in }\Omega,\\
v_{f}=f&\hbox{ in }\Omega_{e}.\end{cases}

Then the following set

\{v_{f}-f:\,f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(W)\}

is dense in
\widetilde{H}^{s}(\Omega)
.

Applying Lemma
4.4
, one can relax the regularity in the integral identity (
4.8
).

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
k=2,\ldots,K
and
\alpha=e_{1}+\ldots+e_{k}
. Suppose that (
4.5
) holds and all the hypotheses in Theorem
1.1
hold.

Then for any
h_{0},\,h_{1},\ldots,h_{k}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
,

(4.10)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\sum_{|\sigma|\leq m}\widetilde{a}_{\sigma,k-1}(x)\left(\sum_{\pi\in S_{k}}h_{\pi_{1}}\ldots h_{\pi_{k-1}}D^{\sigma}h_{\pi_{k}}\right)h_{0}\,dx=0.

Proof.

Given any
h_{0},h_{1},...,h_{k}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
, by the Runge approximation property of Lemma
4.4
there exist a sequence of functions
\{f_{0,i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(W_{2})
and sequence of
\{f_{j,i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(W_{1})
such that

v_{j,i}=f_{j,i}+h_{j}+r_{j,i},\quad\hbox{ for }j=0,\,1,\ldots,k,

where
v_{j,i}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap C^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
solve the equation

(-\Delta)^{s}v_{j,i}+q(x)v_{j,i}=0\hbox{ in }\Omega,

with boundary data
f_{j,i}
so that

v_{j,i}-f_{j,i}\in\widetilde{H}^{s}(\Omega),

and the remainder terms
r_{j,i}\to 0
in
\widetilde{H}^{s}(\Omega)
as
i\to\infty
.

Since
f_{0,i}
is compactly supported in
W_{2}\subset\Omega_{e}
, we can recast (
4.8
) as

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\sum_{|\sigma|\leq m}\widetilde{a}_{\sigma,k-1}(x)\left(\sum_{\pi\in S_{k}}v_{\pi_{1}}\ldots v_{\pi_{k-1}}D^{\sigma}v_{\pi_{k}}\right)(v_{0,i}-f_{0,i})\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\sum_{|\sigma|\leq m}\underbrace{\widetilde{a}_{\sigma,k-1}(x)\left(\sum_{\pi\in S_{k}}v_{\pi_{1}}\ldots v_{\pi_{k-1}}D^{\sigma}v_{\pi_{k}}\right)}_{C(\overline{\Omega})\subset L^{2}(\Omega)}\underbrace{(h_{0}+r_{0,i})}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,dx.

Here we used the fact that
v_{j}\in C^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\lfloor s\rfloor>\max\{m,\,n/2\}
, and
\widetilde{a}_{\sigma,k-1}(x)\in C(\overline{\Omega})
. Taking the limit
i\rightarrow\infty
, we get

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\sum_{|\sigma|\leq m}\widetilde{a}_{\sigma,k-1}(x)\left(\sum_{\pi\in S_{k}}v_{\pi_{1}}\ldots v_{\pi_{k-1}}D^{\sigma}v_{\pi_{k}}\right)h_{0}\,dx.

Next we replace
v_{\pi_{1}}
in the above integral by
(v_{\pi_{1},i}-f_{\pi_{1},i})
. As
f_{j,i}
(
j=1,\ldots,k
) is compactly supported in
W_{1}\subset\Omega_{e}
, we get

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\sum_{|\sigma|\leq m}\widetilde{a}_{\sigma,k-1}(x)\left(\sum_{\pi\in S_{k}}(v_{\pi_{1},i}-f_{\pi_{1},i})v_{\pi_{2}}\ldots v_{\pi_{k-1}}D^{\sigma}v_{\pi_{k}}\right)h_{0}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\sum_{|\sigma|\leq m}\widetilde{a}_{\sigma,k-1}(x)\left(\sum_{\pi\in S_{k}}(h_{\pi_{1}}+r_{\pi_{1},i})v_{\pi_{2}}\ldots v_{\pi_{k-1}}D^{\sigma}v_{\pi_{k}}\right)h_{0}\,dx,

which, by taking
i\rightarrow\infty
, converges to

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\sum_{|\sigma|\leq m}\widetilde{a}_{\sigma,k-1}(x)\left(\sum_{\pi\in S_{k}}h_{\pi_{1}}v_{\pi_{2}}\ldots v_{\pi_{k-1}}D^{\sigma}v_{\pi_{k}}\right)h_{0}\,dx.

Repeating the same procedure
k-1
more times, we get the desired identity.
∎

4.3.
Recovering the nonlinear coefficients

The proof of Theorem
1.1
proceeds by induction: We first recover
a_{\sigma,1}
,
|\sigma|\leq m
, and then apply the inductive step in order to recover
a_{\sigma,k}
, for all
|\sigma|\leq m
and
2\leq k\leq K-1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Step 1 (Recovery of
a_{\sigma,1}
):

By taking
k=2
in Proposition
4.1
, we have that

(4.11)

\displaystyle 0=\int_{\Omega}\sum_{|\sigma|\leq m}\widetilde{a}_{\sigma,1}(x)\Big(h_{1}D^{\sigma}h_{{2}}+{h_{2}}D^{\sigma}{h_{1}}\Big)h_{0}\,dx.

In the spirit of

[
12
, Section 3]
,
we take
h_{0}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
and choose

h_{1}=h_{2}=1
on
\mathrm{supp}(h_{0})\subseteq\Omega
. The identity (
4.11
) yields

0=\int_{\Omega}\widetilde{a}_{0,1}(x)h_{0}\,dx,

which then gives
\widetilde{a}_{0,1}=0
in
\Omega
, since
h_{0}
is arbitrary. Thus, (
4.11
) becomes

(4.12)

0=\int_{\Omega}\sum_{0<|\sigma|\leq m}\widetilde{a}_{\sigma,1}(x)\Big(h_{1}D^{\sigma}h_{{2}}+{h_{2}}D^{\sigma}{h_{1}}\Big)h_{0}\,dx.

Now we argue by induction for
\sigma
. Fix
j\in\{0,\,1,\ldots,m-1\}
, and assume that
\widetilde{a}_{\sigma,1}=0
in
\Omega
for
|\sigma|\leq j
. We now show that
\widetilde{a}_{\sigma,1}=0
in
\Omega
when
|\sigma|=j+1
.
In order to do so, we take
h_{0}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
and choose

h_{1}(x)=x^{\sigma}
and
h_{2}(x)=1
on
\mathrm{supp}(h_{0})\subseteq\Omega
. The induction assumption together with (
4.12
) implies that

0=\int_{\Omega}\sum_{j+1\leq|\beta|\leq m}\widetilde{a}_{\beta,1}(x)(D^{\beta}x^{\sigma})h_{0}\,dx=:\int_{\Omega}\widetilde{a}_{\sigma,1}(x)h_{0}\,dx+I_{j},

where

\displaystyle I_{j}:=\int_{\Omega}\Big(\sum_{j+1<|\beta|\leq m}\widetilde{a}_{\beta,1}(x)(D^{\beta}x^{\sigma})h_{0}+\sum_{|\beta|=j+1,\beta\neq\sigma}\widetilde{a}_{\beta,1}(x)(D^{\beta}x^{\sigma})h_{0}\Big)\,dx.

In fact, the term
I_{j}
vanishes, since there must be a
\beta_{k}
in
\beta=(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n})
that is strictly larger than
\sigma_{k}
in
\sigma=(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{n})
for some
k
such that
D^{\beta}x^{\sigma}=0
. Combining it with the fact that
h_{0}\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
is arbitrary, we deduce

\widetilde{a}_{\sigma,1}=0\quad\hbox{in $\Omega$, \quad for $|\sigma|=j+1$}.

Lastly, by induction, we obtain
\widetilde{a}_{\sigma,1}=0
in
\Omega
for all
|\sigma|\leq m
. Therefore
a_{\sigma,1}^{(1)}=a_{\sigma,1}^{(2)}
in
\Omega
for all
|\sigma|\leq m
.
∎

Step 2 (Recovery of
a_{\sigma,k}
,
k=2,\ldots,K-1
):

To recover
a_{\sigma,2}
, we have by taking
k=3
in Proposition
4.1
that

(4.13)

\displaystyle 0=\int_{\Omega}\sum_{|\sigma|\leq m}\widetilde{a}_{\sigma,2}(x)\Big(h_{1}h_{2}D^{\sigma}h_{3}+h_{1}h_{3}D^{\sigma}h_{2}+h_{2}h_{3}D^{\sigma}{h_{1}}\Big)h_{0}\,dx.

Following a similar strategy as in step 1, we first prove
\widetilde{a}_{0,2}=0
in
\Omega
. By taking any fixed
h_{0}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
and choosing

h_{1}=h_{2}=h_{3}=1
on
\mathrm{supp}(h_{0})\subseteq\Omega
, the above identity yields

0=\int_{\Omega}\widetilde{a}_{0,2}(x)h_{0}\,dx.

which leads to the uniqueness
a_{0,2}^{(1)}=a_{0,2}^{(2)}
.
With this, now we have

0=\int_{\Omega}\sum_{0<|\sigma|\leq m}\widetilde{a}_{\sigma,2}(x)\Big(h_{1}h_{2}D^{\sigma}h_{3}+h_{1}h_{3}D^{\sigma}h_{2}+h_{2}h_{3}D^{\sigma}{h_{1}}\Big)h_{0}\,dx.

We then take
h_{0}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
and choose

h_{3}=1
on
\mathrm{supp}(h_{0})\subseteq\Omega
. The above identity implies

\int_{\Omega}\sum_{|\sigma|\leq m}\widetilde{a}_{\sigma,2}(x)\Big(h_{1}D^{\sigma}{h_{2}}+h_{2}D^{\sigma}{h_{1}}\Big)h_{0}\,dx=0.

Applying the same argument as in Step 1 for
\widetilde{a}_{\sigma,1}
yields

\widetilde{a}_{\sigma,2}=0\quad\hbox{in $\Omega$, \quad for $0\leq|\sigma|\leq m$}.

Similarly, to recover
a_{\sigma,3}
, we first take
h_{1}=h_{2}=h_{3}=h_{4}=1
on
\mathrm{supp}(h_{0})\subseteq\Omega
to recover
\widetilde{a}_{0,4}
. Then we take

h_{3}=h_{4}=1
on
\mathrm{supp}(h_{0})\subseteq\Omega
to get

\int_{\Omega}\sum_{|\sigma|\leq m}\widetilde{a}_{\sigma,3}(x)\Big(h_{1}D^{\sigma}{h_{2}}+h_{2}D^{\sigma}{h_{1}}\Big)h_{0}\,dx=0,

from which we can deduce

\widetilde{a}_{\sigma,3}=0\quad\hbox{in $\Omega$, \quad for $1\leq|\sigma|\leq m$}.

The proof is completed similarly for the remaining coefficients
a_{\sigma,k}
.

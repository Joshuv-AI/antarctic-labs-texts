Distribution of Dark Matter in Galaxies
P. Steffen

Abstract
Properties of dark matter particles are investigated using astronomical data evaluated by the SPARC-group: the data of 2693 rotational data points from 153 different galaxies have been combined to an average gravitational behavior of the galaxies. The ratio of dark matter mass to the baryonic mass within the galaxies is determined, as well as the distributions of dark matter masses, mass densities and rotational velocities.



1
Introduction

Astronomical data of galaxy rotation curves have been evaluated by the SPARC-group
[
1
]
.
The details have been made publicly available
[
2
]
.
Measurements of 2693 stellar objects on 153 different galaxies have been used for calculations of
the gravitational accelerations by the baryonic mass of the galaxies
\rm\mathbf{a}_{B}
,
and the centrifugal accelerations
\rm\mathbf{a}_{m}
.
The original logarithmic values have been converted to units of
\rm 10^{-12}\ m/s^{2}
.
Figure
2
shows the values of
\rm a_{B}
versus
\rm a_{m}
:
the thin dots show the single results of the 2693 stellar objects and the thick points show average values of binned results.
The fluctuation of the single results is due to uncertainties of the astronomical measurements.
In spite of the various parameter values of the 153 galaxies,
the binned average values of the acceleration pairs have uncertainties less than the size of the thick points in the graph.
The acceleration pairs describe the average gravitational behavior of the 153 galaxies.

The systematic discrepancy between the two accelerations is clearly visible.
It could be explained by an additional massive dark matter (
DM
) in the galaxies
that is not taken into account in the calculationof of
\rm a_{B}
from the baryonic mass.

The two parameters
\rm a_{B}
and
\rm a_{m}
describe the behavior of the whole sample of data,
independent of types or size of the galaxies.
They are correlated with the original astronomical parameters of
the galactic baryonic Mass
\rm\mathbf{M}
,
the distance of the stellar object
\rm\mathbf{r}
, and the r orbital velocity
\rm\mathbf{v}
by

\displaystyle\rm a_{B}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rm\frac{G\cdot M}{r^{2}}

(1)

\displaystyle\rm a_{m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rm\frac{v^{2}}{r}.

(2)

With the two accelerations and one of the original parameters, e.g. the baryonic mass
\rm M
,
the other two astronomical parameters can be calculated easily.

In the following analysis only the binned averages of
\rm a_{B}
and
\rm a_{m}
will be used.
The obvious discrepancy between the two is assumed to originate from massive DM-particles.
The whole mass of DM-particles in the galaxies, and the radial distributions of the masses as well their volume density
will be derived using just the pairs of
\rm a_{B}
and
\rm a_{m}
,
that describe the average gravitational behavior of all the measured the galaxies.

2
Some Facts

For astronomical data the acceleration of an external stellar object is calculated
using the gravitational constant (G), the galactic mass (M), and the distance from the galactic center of gravity (r):

\rm a=\frac{G\cdot M}{r^{2}}

(3)

In general M is calculated for the baryonic matter (
\rm\mathbf{M}_{B}
) resulting in an expected acceleration of
\rm a_{B}
.
An independent measure of the acceleration - the one from the centrifugal force - can be obtained from measurements of the rotational velocity v
of external stellar objects:

\rm a_{m}=\frac{v^{2}}{r}.

(4)

In the data, shown in figure
2
, the measured acceleration
\rm a_{m}

is generally larger than the baryonic acceleration
\rm a_{B}
.
This is assumed to originate from an additional dark matter that is not taken into account using the baryonic mass alone.
The total measured acceleration is described by

\rm a_{m}=a_{B}+a_{DM}=\frac{G\cdot(M_{B}+M_{DM})}{r^{2}},

(5)

where
\rm a_{DM}
is the additional acceleration originating from
the DM-mass,
\rm M_{DM}
, at the distance r from the galactic center.

The original pairs of accelerations of the SPARC-group are given in logarithmic units.
They have been converted to units of
\rm 10^{-12}m/s^{2}
,
as listed in columns 3 and 4 of table
1
.

The acceleration of the DM-mass alone,
\rm\mathbf{a}_{DM}
,
is obtained from the difference of measured and baryonic acceleration:

\rm a_{DM}=a_{m}-a_{B}.

(6)

\rm a_{DM}
is shown in figure
2
for different values of
\rm a_{B}
.
The detauied numbers are listed in columns 5 of table
1
.
At the larger values of
\rm a_{B}
there is a kind of threshold effect showing a stronger rise of
\rm a_{DM}

to a value of
\rm 80\cdot 10^{-12}m/s^{2}
.
This is followed by a slow decrease of
\rm a_{DM}
with the decreasing
\rm a_{B}

down to a still significant level of about
\rm 16\cdot 10^{-12}m/s^{2}
at
\rm a_{B}=2\cdot 10^{-12}m/s^{2}
.

3
Distance Scale

The baryonic acceleration is

\rm a_{B}=\frac{G\cdot M_{B}}{r^{2}}.

(7)

It can be used as a measure of the distance r from the galactic center:

\rm r\sim\frac{1}{\sqrt{a_{B}}}

(8)

Choosing a distance ,
\rm r_{0}
, for the smallest acceleration of
\rm a_{B}=2\cdot 10^{-12}m/s^{2}
as
distance reference for all measured galaxies, a relative distance is obtained:

\rm r=\sqrt{a_{0}/a_{B}}\cdot r_{0},

(9)

giving the relative distance from the galactic center in units of
\rm[r_{0}]
for all galaxies independent of their size.
The detailed values are listed in columns 2 of table
1
.
It is used to demonstrate the radial dependence of
\rm a_{DM}
as shown in figure
4
.
The radial dependence of
\rm a_{B}
is also shown in the figure.
It follows the
\rm 1/r^{2}
-dependence of Newtons law.
The significant difference as compared to
\rm a_{DM}
is clearly visible for the larger radial distances.

4
Radial Distribution of
\rm a_{DM}

The total DM-mass (
\rm M_{DM}(r)
) inside a sphere of radius r results in the values of
\rm a_{DM}(r)
:

\rm a_{DM}(r)=\frac{G\cdot M_{DM}(r)}{r^{2}}.

(10)

At the largest radius (
\rm r\approx 1.0
) , the two accelerations are

\displaystyle\rm a_{DM}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 15.74\pm 0.85

(11)

\displaystyle\rm a_{B}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \ 2.04.

(12)

The ratio of the two equals the ratio of the total masses inside the sphere radius:

\rm\frac{a_{DM}}{a_{B}}=\frac{G\cdot M_{DM}/r^{2}}{G\cdot M_{B}/r^{2}}=\frac{M_{DM}}{M_{B}}=\frac{15.74}{2.04}=7.7

(13)

This result can be compared to the result for the composition of the universe of
5% baryonic matter and 27% of dark matter
[
3
]
resulting in a somewhat lower ratio of 5.4
The higher measured ratio of 7.7 indicates that nearly all DM is enclosed in the galaxies and there is not much left for the intergalactic universe.
The lower ratio could be attributed to a different ratio for galaxies different from the investigated ones.
It could also be due to a possible bias of the results for the farthest distance.
This will be investigated in the section
5
.

5
Systematic Bias

The value of
\rm a_{DM}
at the larges radius ((
\rm r\approx 1.0
) shows a somewhat high value.
This is investigated further by inspecting the the distribution of the rotational velocity v.
of the rotating stellar objects.
At the different radii the gravitational acceleration equals the one of the centrifugal force:

\rm a=\frac{G\cdot M}{r^{2}}=\frac{v^{2}}{r},

(14)

Equation
14
gives

\rm v^{2}=a\cdot r.

(15)

Figure
4
shows the
\rm v^{2}
-distributions of
\rm a_{m}
(red points) and
\rm a_{B}
(blue points)
for the different r-values.
Both distributions show a decreasing acceleration with increasing radius.
However there seems to be an inconsistency for
\rm a_{m}(r\approx 1.0)
:
the
\rm v^{2}
-value is increasing instead of decreasing further.
It may be due to the statistics of the last point that is based on only 75 stellar objects from the 153 galaxies.
So less than half of the galaxies contributed.
This may result in a bias resulting in a too large value of
\rm a_{m}
and finally
\rm a_{DM}
.

A parametrisation of the red points without the last point leads to a lower value
that is indicated by the black point in the figure.
The correction of the measured acceleration gives
\rm a_{m}=17.78\rightarrow 14.94
.
The corrected result is shown as black point in the figure.

The corrected values are shown in all figures in addition to the original value of the
SPARC-group.
In table
1
there has been added a last line containing the corrected values of the different variables.

As a consequence the mass ratio equation
13
changes to

\rm\frac{a_{DM}}{a_{B}}=\frac{G\cdot M_{DM}/r^{2}}{G\cdot M_{B}/r^{2}}=\frac{M_{DM}}{M_{B}}=\frac{12.89}{2.04}=6.3

(16)

This value gives a better agreement with the mass ratio estimate for the universe of 5.4 .

6
Radial Distribution of DM

The first 2 points of figure
4
have a sphere radius of
\rm r_{B}<0.1\ r_{0}
.
Their
\rm a_{DM}(r)
-values are compatible with zero and are ignored.
At higher values of
\rm r_{B}
a strong rise of
\rm a_{DM}(r)
is visible followed by a soft decrease up to the largest
\rm r_{B}
-values.

The radial distribution of the DM-mass is obtained from accelerations of two subsequent spheres,

\rm a_{DM}(r_{i-1})
and
\rm a_{DM}(r_{i})
.
At
\rm r_{i}
there are two contributions to
\rm a_{DM}(r_{i})
:
the attenuated acceleration of the inner sphere,
\rm a_{DM}(r_{i-1})*1/(r_{i}-r_{i-1})^{2}
,
and the acceleration produced by the DM-mass in the shell between the two spheres.
Both sum up to
\rm a_{DM}(r_{i})
.
The net acceleration effect by the DM-mass inside the shell is obtained from the difference

\rm\Delta a_{DM}(r_{i})=a_{DM}(r_{i})-a_{DM}(r_{i-1})\cdot\frac{1}{(r_{i}-r_{i-1})^{2}}

(17)

The detailed values are listed in columns 6 of table
1
.
This acceleration is shown as green dots in figure
5
together with the red dots of the measured acceleration
\rm a_{DM}(r_{i})
.
The difference between the two accelerations is due to the attenuated mass effect from the inner radius,
\rm a_{DM}(r_{i-1})
.
The detailed values
\rm\Delta a_{DM}(r_{i})
are displayed in column 6 of table
1
.

The DM-mass within the shell is obtained by

\rm m_{DM}(r_{i})=\Delta a_{DM}(r_{i})/a_{B}\mbox{\ \ \ \ \ \ \ in units of [$\rm M_{B}$] }

(18)

The detailed values are listed in columns 7 of table
1
.
The radial mass distribution is shown in Figure
7
.
The different DM-mass in the sphere shells add up to
\rm 7.7\cdot M_{B}
.
The same value has already been obtained in equation
13
for the DM-mass within the full outer sphere.
The dominant mass is positioned towards the outside of the galaxies.
The lowest amount of DM-mass is at the smallest radius of
\rm 0.066\ r_{0}
.

A different view is obtained if the mass density is determined:

\rm\rho(r_{i})=m_{DM}(r_{i})/V_{shell}(r_{i},r_{i-1}),

(19)

where
\rm V_{shell}=4/3\ \pi\cdot(r_{i}^{3}-r_{i-1}^{3})
is the volume of the shell between the two radii.
The detailed values are listed in columns 8 of table
1
.
The radial distribution shown in figure
7
.
The highest mass density is obtained for the first 2 radial data.
It decreases by a factor of about 100 towards the largest radii.
This is due to the much faster increase of the shell volume with the radius.

7
Accumulation of DM-particles in the Galaxies

Candidates for DM-particles have not yet been found.
The only indications for their existence are the astronomical data.
They are uncharged and react only weakly and by gravitation.
And they must have a mass in order to produce the discrepancy between the predicted and measured acceleration.

The analyzed data show substantial DM-particles enclosed in shells around the galactic centers
as shown in figure
7
.
DM-particles at the outer shell radii have the same acceleration as the one of the measured stellar objects at that radius.
A rather stable distribution of DM-particles is possible if they all would circulate the galactic center with
velocities corresponding to the radius and the acceleration enclosed in the different spherical shells.
Their distribution of
\rm v^{2}
is described by the red points in figure
4
.
The rotating velocities of the measured stellar objects range from 20 to 300 km/s
[
1
]
.
The DM-particles should be in the same range of velocities. and follow the distribution shown in figure
4
.

8
Summary

This analysis of dark matter behavior has started with the assumption
that the average gravitational behavior of the 153 galaxies is described by averages
of pairs of accelerations,
\rm a_{B}
and
\rm a_{m}
.
It is assumed that the discrepancy between them originates from an additional dark matter
that adds gravitational acceleration to the outer stellar objects.

The mass ratio of DM-mass enclosed in the galaxies to the baryonic mass
is determined to 6.3(corrected) resp. 7.7(uncorrected).
It is in agreement with the expectation of 5% baryonic mass and 27% DM-mass in the universe.
No massive DM mass volume seems to be left for the inner part of the galaxy.
Assuming the measured galaxies are representative for all galaxies,
there is also not much DM mass volume left for the intergalactic space.

Three important distributions of DM-particles have been obtained:
the radial distributions of mass, mass volume density, and radial velocity within the galaxies.
The radial profiles have to be reproduced by the different theories for the discrepancies between
\rm a_{B}
and
\rm a_{M}
.
They might also help to discriminate among different DM candidates

The obtained results are valid for the 153 measured galaxies.

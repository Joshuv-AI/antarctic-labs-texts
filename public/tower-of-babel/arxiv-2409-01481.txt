Relative-Hyper GAGA Theorem
Taewan Kim, Eita Haibara

Abstract
In this paper, we provide a relative hypercohomology version of Serre’s GAGA theorem. We prove that the relative hypercohomology of a complex of sheaves on a complex projective variety is isomorphic to the relative hypercohomology of its analytification, with respect to an open or closed subvariety. This result implies Serre’s original GAGA theorem.

Background

2.1
Verdier Duality

Let us denote
\operatorname{Sh}(X)
as the category of sheaves over
X
, and let
f:X\rightarrow Y
be a continuous map between topological spaces.
It induces the direct image functor
f_{*}:\operatorname{Sh}(X)\rightarrow\operatorname{Sh}(Y)
, defined by
f(\mathscr{F})(U)=\mathscr{F}(f^{-1}(U))
for
\mathscr{F}\in\operatorname{Sh}(X)
and
U\in\operatorname{Open}(X)
.
On the other hand, we have an inverse image functor
f^{*}:\operatorname{Sh}(Y)\rightarrow\operatorname{Sh}(X)
.
Let
E\rightarrow Y
be the étalé space of the sheaf
\mathscr{F}
on
Y
.
There exist pullbacks in categories of topological spaces. Thus, we can pullback this étalé space and get the diagram below:
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X\times_{Y}E}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

Then the bundle
X\times_{Y}B\rightarrow X
defines a sheaf on
X
, denoted
f^{*}(\mathscr{G})
.
Indeed, an inverse image functor can be calculated as
f^{*}(\mathscr{G})(U)=\varinjlim_{V\supset f(U)}\mathscr{G}(V)
.

They are adjoint to each other, i.e. they satisfy the following natural isomorphism

Hom_{\operatorname{Sh}(X)}(f^{*}\mathscr{G},\mathscr{F})\simeq Hom_{\operatorname{Sh}(Y)}(\mathscr{G},f_{*}\mathscr{F}).

And we have the direct image functor with compact support
f_{!}:\operatorname{Sh}(X)\rightarrow\operatorname{Sh}(Y)
, defined by

f_{!}(\mathscr{F})(U)=\{s\in\mathscr{F}(f^{-1}(U))\mid f|_{\operatorname{supp}(s)}:\operatorname{supp}(s)\rightarrow U\text{ is proper map}\}.

We expect that there exists a functor
f^{!}
which is right adjoint to
f_{!}
.
However, in a general setting, such a functor does not always exist.
Under certain conditions, there exists a functor
f^{!}:D^{+}\operatorname{Sh}(Y)\rightarrow D^{+}\operatorname{Sh}(X)
which is right adjoint to
Rf_{!}
in derived category.
This means that there is an isomorphism

Hom_{D^{+}\operatorname{Sh}(X)}(Rf_{!}\mathscr{F}^{\bullet},\mathscr{G}^{\bullet})\simeq Hom_{D^{+}\operatorname{Sh}(Y)}(\mathscr{F}^{\bullet},f^{!}\mathscr{G}^{\bullet}).

(1)

More generally, there is an isomorphism of derived hom-sheaves

R\mathcal{H}om(Rf_{!}\mathscr{F}^{\bullet},\mathscr{G}^{\bullet})\simeq Rf_{*}R\mathcal{H}om(\mathscr{F}^{\bullet},f^{!}\mathscr{G}^{\bullet}).

(2)

We can get the global version (
1
) by applying
h^{0}\Gamma(Y,-)
to (
2
).

If
X
and
Y
are locally compact Hausdorff (and a few additional conditions), the functor
f^{!}
exists.
However, we work on varieties that have the Zariski topology, and they are not Hausdorff.
Fortunately, our interest lies in subvarieties that are locally closed subspace of the entire variety, and for which the functor
f^{!}
exists.

2.3
Relative hypercohomology

This section is according to
[
Max19
, 5.5]
.

Definition 2.3.1
.

Let
\mathscr{F}^{\bullet}
be a complex of sheaves of
X
.
The hypercohomology of
\mathscr{F}^{\bullet}
is a cohomology of the right derived functor of the global section functor:

\mathbb{H}^{*}(X;\mathscr{F}^{\bullet}):=R^{*}\Gamma(X,\mathscr{F}^{\bullet})=h^{*}R\Gamma(X,\mathscr{F}^{\bullet})

Relative hypercohomology is a hypercohomology that uses functors in Verdier duality:

Definition 2.3.2
.

For an inclusion of topological spaces
f:Y\hookrightarrow X
, relative hypercohomology is defined by:

\mathbb{H}^{*}(X,X-Y;\mathscr{F}^{\bullet}):=\mathbb{H}^{*}(X;Rf_{!}f^{!}\mathscr{F}^{\bullet})

If we use a local system, the classical Alexander duality comes from the definition of relative hypercohomology by using Verdier duality.

2.4
Serre’s GAGA

In this paper, we use the definition of algebraic variety in
[
Ser55
, n
o
34]
.
Note that every (quasi-)affine and (quasi-)projective variety is an algebraic variety.
To see the construction in this section more precisely, see
[
Ser56

.

Let
X
be a complex algebraic variety.
Since a locally closed subset of affine space can inherit the usual topology of an affine space
\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
each chart of
X
can inherit an induced topology of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
We denote
X_{an}
as a topological space equipped with its underlying set
X
and the induced topology.
Any open subset of Zariski topology of the affine space is also open in the usual topology.
Thus, the usual topology is finer than the Zariski topology and there exists a natural continuous map
h:X_{an}\rightarrow X
.
Let us denote a structure sheaf of
X
as
\mathscr{O}_{X}
.
The pullback
h^{*}\mathscr{O}_{X}
is a subsheaf of
\mathscr{H}_{X_{an}}
, which is a sheaf of germs of holomorphic functions on

.
We denote

as
\mathscr{O}_{X_{an}}
.

An algebraic sheaf (resp. analytic sheaf) is defined as a sheaf of
\mathscr{O}_{X}
-module on
X
(resp.
\mathscr{H}_{X_{an}}
-module on
X_{an}
).
Let us denote the category of algebraic sheaves of
X
as
\operatorname{Sh}_{alg}(X)
and the category of analytic sheaves of
X_{an}
as
\operatorname{Sh}_{an}(X_{an})
.
We can define an analytification functor of sheaves
(-)_{an}:\operatorname{Sh}_{alg}(X)\rightarrow\operatorname{Sh}_{an}(X_{an})
.

Definition 2.4.1
.

Suppose
\mathscr{F}
is an algebraic sheaf of
X
.
The continuous map
h:X_{an}\rightarrow X
induces the functor
h^{*}:\operatorname{Sh}_{alg}(X)\rightarrow\operatorname{Sh}_{alg}(X_{an})
.
Then we can construct the analytic sheaf
\mathscr{F}_{an}:=h^{*}(\mathscr{F})\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X_{an}}}\mathscr{H}_{X_{an}}\in\operatorname{Sh}_{an}(X_{an})
, and we call it an analytification of
\mathscr{F}
.

Theorem 2.4.2

(
[
Ser56
, Proposition 10]
)
.

•

The functor
(-)_{an}
is exact.

•

If
\mathscr{F}
is a coherent algebraic sheaf, then
\mathscr{F}_{an}
is a coherent analytic sheaf.

The above result implies that the functor
(-)_{an}:\operatorname{Sh}_{alg}(X)\rightarrow\operatorname{Sh}_{an}(X)
can be restricted to the functor
(-)_{an}:\operatorname{Coh}_{alg}(X)\rightarrow\operatorname{Coh}_{an}(X)

(
[

, Théorème 2,3]
)
.

The functor

is an equivalence of categories, i.e., fully faithful and essentially surjective.
More precisely, it satisfies the following statements:

•

For every coherent algebraic sheaves
\mathscr{F}
and
\mathscr{G}
on
X
, the mapping of the hom-sets induced by analytification
Hom_{\operatorname{Sh}_{alg}(X)}(\mathscr{F},\mathscr{G})\rightarrow Hom_{\operatorname{Sh}_{an}(X_{an})}(\mathscr{F}_{an},\mathscr{G}_{an})

\mathscr{G}
on
X_{an}
, there exists a coherent algebraic sheaf
\mathscr{F}
on
X
such that

.

Moreover, there is a relation between cohomologies of algebraic and analytic spaces.

Theorem 2.4.4

(
[
Ser56
, Théorème 1]

Suppose that
X
is a complex projective variety (i.e. Zariski closed subvariety of a complex projective space

For every
\mathscr{F}\in\operatorname{Coh}_{alg}(X)

H^{*}(X,\mathscr{F})\simeq H^{*}(X_{an},\mathscr{F}_{an}).

3
Main result

Let
X
be a complex projective variety, i.e., a closed subvariety of complex projective space, and let
Y
be its subvariety.
By the definition of subvariety,
Y
is a locally closed subset of
X
over its Zariski topology.
We have a natural embedding

, which is an injective continuous map.

First we provide a hypercohomology version of Theorem

The proof is analogous to

Theorem 3.0.1

(Hyper-GAGA)
.

Suppose that
\mathscr{F}^{\bullet}
is a complex of algebraic coherent sheaves on

, and

(resp.
\mathscr{F}_{an}^{\bullet}
) is an analytification of

(resp.

Then the hypercohomology of
\mathscr{F}^{\bullet}
on

is isomorphic to the hypercohomology of
\mathscr{F}_{an}^{\bullet}
on
X_{an}
:

\mathbb{H}^{*}(X,\mathscr{F}^{\bullet})\simeq\mathbb{H}^{*}(X_{an},\mathscr{F}_{an}^{\bullet})

Proof.

According to Serre’s GAGA, we know that
\mathbb{H}^{p}(X,\mathscr{F}^{q})\simeq\mathbb{H}^{p}(X_{an},\mathscr{F}_{an}^{q})
holds for
\forall p,q
.
These induce isomorphisms at the

-stage of two spectral sequences of the hypercohomologies, thus we obtain an isomorphism at the
E_{\infty}
-stage
\mathbb{H}(X,\mathscr{F}^{\bullet})\simeq\mathbb{H}(X_{an},\mathscr{F}_{an}^{\bullet})
.
∎

One might want that the following application of Serre’s GAGA to be established:

\mathbb{H}^{*}(X,X-Y;\mathscr{F}^{\bullet})\simeq\mathbb{H}^{*}(X_{an},X_{an}-Y_{an};\mathscr{F}_{an}^{\bullet})

where
\mathscr{F}
is a complex of algebraic coherent sheaves.
First, since we are working in the setting of a locally closed subspace

by a slight abuse of notation.
Hence, we will denote the right adjoint functor of

as

, and denote the right adjoint functor of

as

in this section.
And we know that

is exact by Proposition

Therefore, what we want is the following relation:

(4)

To prove (

,
we have to show that

Note that the category of quasi-coherent sheaves

are well-defined.
Hence, we can think of the derived categories that we are using as either the category of algebraic (resp. analytic) sheaves

∎

Corollary 3.1.6
.

Theorem
3.1.2
implies the original GAGA
2.4.4
and the Hyper-GAGA
3.0.1
.

Proof.

Suppose
Y=X
and
i=\operatorname{id}_{X}
.
Then we have
Ri_{!}Ri^{!}\simeq i_{!}i^{!}\simeq\operatorname{rsp}_{\operatorname{id}_{X}}=\operatorname{id}_{\operatorname{Sh}_{alg}(X)}
.
Applying Theorem
3.1.2
with the condition that
(\mathscr{F}^{Y})^{\bullet}=\mathscr{F}^{\bullet}
is coherent, we get

\mathbb{H}^{*}(X;\mathscr{F}^{\bullet})\simeq\mathbb{H}^{*}(X_{an};\mathscr{F}_{an}^{\bullet}).

This implies
3.0.1
and also
2.4.4
.
∎

3.2
Closed subvariety

Let
Y
be a closed subvariety of a complex projective variety
X
and
i:Y\hookrightarrow X
be a canonical inclusion.
In this case, we know that
i_{!}=i_{*}
is exact (see Proposition
2.2.5
). Thus we obtain

Ri_{!}Ri^{!}\simeq i_{*}Ri^{!}.

(14)

We will give an acyclic condition to remove the derived functor.
The following lemma is originally provided in
[
Gro57
, 2.4]
, and we give a proof with reference
[
EZT13
, Theorem 2.4.2]
.

Proposition 3.2.1
.

Let
\mathscr{F}^{\bullet}
be a complex of
i^{!}
-acyclic sheaves.
Then the value of the derived functor
Ri^{!}
of
\mathscr{F}^{\bullet}
is isomorphic to the value of
i^{!}
in the derived category
D^{+}\operatorname{Sh}_{alg}(X)
:

Ri^{!}(\mathscr{F}^{\bullet})\simeq i^{!}\mathscr{F}^{\bullet}

Proof.

Let
K^{\bullet,\bullet}
be a double complex where
K^{\bullet,0}
is
\mathscr{F}^{\bullet}
, and
K^{p,\bullet}
is an injective resolution of
\mathscr{F}^{p}
.
The
i^{!}
-acyclic condition means that the
E_{1}
-stage of the spectral sequence of
K^{\bullet,\bullet}
, which is induced by
i^{!}
, is

\displaystyle E_{1}^{p,q}=h^{p}Ri^{!}(K^{\bullet,q})=\begin{cases}i^{!}\mathscr{F}^{q}&\text{ if }p=0\\
0&\text{ if }p>0.\end{cases}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 2}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i^{!}\mathscr{F}^{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i^{!}\mathscr{F}^{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i^{!}\mathscr{F}^{0}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 2}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}

Thus, the
E_{2}
-stage is

\displaystyle E_{2}^{p,q}=h^{p,q}E_{1}=\begin{cases}h^{q}(i^{!}\mathscr{F}^{\bullet})&\text{ if }p=0\\
0&\text{ if }p>0.\end{cases}

It means
h^{k}Ri^{!}(\mathscr{F}^{\bullet})\simeq h^{k}(i^{!}\mathscr{F}^{\bullet})
. Thus we get

Ri^{!}(\mathscr{F}^{\bullet})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle qis}}{{\simeq}}i^{!}\mathscr{F}^{\bullet}.

∎

In this case of a closed subvariety, we need more conditions than the open subvariety case.

Theorem 3.2.2
.

Let
X
be a complex projective variety,
Y
be its closed subvariety, and
i:Y\hookrightarrow X
be a natural inclusion.
Suppose
\mathscr{F}^{\bullet}
is a complex of locally free
i^{!}
-acyclic algebraic sheaves on
X
,
h^{*}\mathscr{F}^{\bullet}
is flasque, and
i_{*}i^{!}\mathscr{F}^{\bullet}
is quasi-isomorphic to a complex of coherent sheaves.
Then there is an isomorphism

\mathbb{H}^{*}(X,X-Y;\mathscr{F}^{\bullet})\simeq\mathbb{H}^{*}(X_{an},X_{an}-Y_{an};\mathscr{F}_{an}^{\bullet}).

Proof.

We will use the similar method as the open subvariety case.
By the above Proposition
3.2.1
, we can use
i^{!}\mathscr{F}^{\bullet}
instead of
Ri^{!}\mathscr{F}^{\bullet}
by the condition.
We shall prove that the following isomorphism holds:

(i_{*}i^{!}\mathscr{F}^{\bullet})_{an}\simeq(i_{an})_{*}(i_{an})^{!}\mathscr{F}_{an}^{\bullet}

(15)

It suffices to show the existence of the isomorphism (
15
) for the single sheaf
\mathscr{F}
.

By the definition of analytification, (
15
) is

h^{*}(i_{*}i^{!}\mathscr{F})\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X_{an}}}\mathscr{H}_{X_{an}}\simeq(i_{an})_{*}(i_{an})^{!}(h^{*}\mathscr{F}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X_{an}}}\mathscr{H}_{X_{an}})

(16)

where
h
is in Definition
2.4.1
.
Using Lemma
3.1.3
, we get

\displaystyle\begin{split}h^{*}i_{*}i^{!}\mathscr{F}&=h^{*}i_{!}i^{*}\operatorname{rsp}_{i}\mathscr{F}\\
&\simeq(i_{an})_{!}(i_{an})^{*}h^{*}\operatorname{rsp}_{i}\mathscr{F}.\end{split}

(17)

Suppose
U
is an open subset of
X
and
U_{an}\subset X_{an}
is its analytic space. Then we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}(h^{*}\operatorname{rsp}_{i}\mathscr{F})(U_{an})&=(\operatorname{rsp}_{i}\mathscr{F})(U)\\
&=\left\{s\in\mathscr{F}(U)\mid\operatorname{supp}(s)\subset Y\right\}\\
&=\left\{s^{\prime}\in h^{*}\mathscr{F}(U_{an})\mid\operatorname{supp}(s^{\prime})\subset Y_{an}\right\}\\
&=(\operatorname{rsp}_{i_{an}}h^{*}\mathscr{F})(U_{an}).\end{split}

(18)

It follows that
h^{*}\operatorname{rsp}_{i}=\operatorname{rsp}_{i_{an}}h^{*}
. Thus the last term of (
17
) is

(i_{an})_{!}(i_{an})^{*}\operatorname{rsp}_{i_{an}}h^{*}\mathscr{F}=(i_{an})_{*}(i_{an})^{!}h^{*}\mathscr{F},

then we get

h^{*}i_{*}i^{!}\mathscr{F}\simeq(i_{an})_{*}(i_{an})^{!}h^{*}\mathscr{F}.

(19)

Now we shall show that

(i_{an})_{*}(i_{an})^{!}\mathscr{F}^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X_{an}}}\mathscr{H}_{X_{an}}\simeq(i_{an})_{*}(i_{an})^{!}(\mathscr{F}^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X_{an}}}\mathscr{H}_{X_{an}})

(20)

where we denote
\mathscr{F}^{\prime}=h^{*}\mathscr{F}\in\operatorname{Sh}_{alg}(X_{an})
.
To do this, we give two lemmas.

Lemma 3.2.3
.

Let
(X,\mathscr{O}_{X})
be a locally ringed space,
Z
be its subspace of
X
, and
i:Z\hookrightarrow X
be natural inclusion.
If
\mathscr{G}
is a locally free
\mathscr{O}_{X}
-sheaf on
X
, then

i_{*}i^{*}\mathscr{G}\simeq\mathscr{G}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}i_{*}\mathscr{O}_{Z}

Proof.

By the projection formula (see
[
Har77
, Exercise II.5.1 (d)]
),

i_{*}(i^{*}\mathscr{G}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{Z}}\mathscr{O}_{Z})\simeq\mathscr{G}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}i_{*}\mathscr{O}_{Z}.

The RHS is
i_{*}(i^{*}\mathscr{G}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{Z}}\mathscr{O}_{Z})\simeq i_{*}i^{*}\mathscr{G}
, and this proves our lemma.
∎

The following lemma forms the core of this proof.

Lemma 3.2.4
.

Let
Y
is closed subvariety of
X
and
i:Z\hookrightarrow X
be the natural inclusion.
Suppose
\mathscr{G}
is a locally free flasque algebraic sheaf on
X_{an}
. Then

(i_{an})_{*}(i_{an})^{!}(\mathscr{G}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X_{an}}}\mathscr{H}_{X_{an}})\simeq(i_{an})_{*}(i_{an})^{!}\mathscr{G}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X_{an}}}\mathscr{H}_{X_{an}}

Proof.

Let
j:X-Y\hookrightarrow X
denote the natural inclusion of an open subvariety.
Since
Y_{an}
is closed and
\mathscr{G}
is flasque, the natural mapping
\Gamma(U_{an},\mathscr{G})\rightarrow\Gamma(U_{an}-Y_{an},\mathscr{G})
is surjective.
By
[
Max19
, 5.6]
, there exist a short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow(i_{an})_{*}(i_{an})^{!}\mathscr{G}\longrightarrow\mathscr{G}\longrightarrow(j_{an})_{*}(j_{an})^{*}\mathscr{G}\longrightarrow 0

(21)

We can make two short exact sequences from (
21
).
First, we substitute
\mathscr{G}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X_{an}}}\mathscr{H}_{X_{an}}
for
\mathscr{G}
in the sequence.
Second, since
\mathscr{H}_{X_{an}}
is
\mathscr{O}_{X_{an}}
-flat sheaf by
[
Ser56
, Corollaire 1]
and
[
Ser56
, Proposition 22]
,
we can apply
-\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X_{an}}}\mathscr{H}_{X_{an}}
for each term of the sequence.
The diagram below shows that these two short exact sequences coincide at the center.
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(i_{an})_{*}(i_{an})^{!}(\mathscr{G}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X_{an}}}\mathscr{H}_{X_{an}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{G}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X_{an}}}\mathscr{H}_{X_{an}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(j_{an})_{*}(j_{an})^{*}(\mathscr{G}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X_{an}}}\mathscr{H}_{X_{an}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces((i_{an})_{*}(i_{an})^{!}\mathscr{G})\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X_{an}}}\mathscr{H}_{X_{an}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{G}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X_{an}}}\mathscr{H}_{X_{an}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces((j_{an})_{*}(j_{an})^{*}\mathscr{G})\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X_{an}}}\mathscr{H}_{X_{an}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}

Thus, if we prove that

(j_{an})_{*}(j_{an})^{*}(\mathscr{F}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X_{an}}}\mathscr{H}_{X_{an}})\simeq\left((j_{an})_{*}(j_{an})^{*}\mathscr{F}\right)\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X_{an}}}\mathscr{H}_{X_{an}},

(22)

we can get

(i_{an})_{*}(i_{an})^{!}(\mathscr{F}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X_{an}}}\mathscr{H}_{X_{an}})\simeq\left((i_{an})_{*}(i_{an})^{!}\mathscr{F}\right)\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X_{an}}}\mathscr{H}_{X_{an}}.

(23)

From Lemma
3.2.3
, we have the following expressions using tensor product:

(j_{an})_{*}(j_{an})^{*}(\mathscr{F}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X_{an}}}\mathscr{H}_{X_{an}})\simeq(\mathscr{F}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X_{an}}}\mathscr{H}_{X_{an}})\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X_{an}}}(j_{an})_{*}\mathscr{O}_{X_{an}-Y_{an}}

and

\left((j_{an})_{*}(j_{an})^{*}\mathscr{F}\right)\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X_{an}}}\mathscr{H}_{X_{an}}\simeq(\mathscr{F}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X_{an}}}(j_{an})_{*}\mathscr{O}_{X_{an}-Y_{an}})\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X_{an}}}\mathscr{H}_{X_{an}}.

Like the case of open subvarity, the associativity of tensor product prove the isomorphism.
∎

By the above lemma and the condition that
h^{*}\mathscr{F}
is locally free and flasque, we get the isomorphism in (
20
), and it finishes the proof.
∎

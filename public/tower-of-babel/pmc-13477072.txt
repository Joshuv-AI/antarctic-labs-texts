Predicting early graft patency using transit-time flow measurement in internal thoracic artery-based composite coronary artery bypass grafting
Authors: Min-Seok Kim, Cheol Ho Lee, Seong Wook Hwang, Ki-Bong Kim
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveIntraoperative graft assessment is essential for early graft patency after coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG); however, interpretation of transit-time flow measurement (TTFM) parameters in internal thoracic artery (ITA)-based composite CABG remains unclear. This study evaluated whether intraoperative TTFM parameters predict early graft patency after composite CABG.MethodsWe evaluated 274 patients who underwent isolated CABG using an in situ ITA-based composite graft and early postoperative angiography. TTFM parameters—mean graft flow (MGF), pulsatility index (PI), diastolic filling percentage (DF%), and percentage of backward flow (%BF) —were obtained for each distal anastomosis. Angiographic findings were classified as perfectly patent, bidirectionally competitive, unidirectionally competitive, or occluded. Receiver operating characteristic curve analyses determined optimal cutoff values and area under the curve (AUCs) for predicting competitive graft flow on early angiography.ResultsAmong 894 distal anastomoses (median 3 [3.0, 4.0] per patient), 724 (81.0%) were perfectly patent, 88 (9.8%) bidirectionally competitive, 79 (8.8%) unidirectionally competitive, and 3 (0.3%) occluded. MGF, PI, DF%, and %BF differed significantly across graft-flow categories (all P < .001). Optimal cutoff values for predicting competitive graft flow were MGF 10 mL/min (AUC, 0.725), PI 3.1 (AUC, 0.699), DF% 60.0% (AUC, 0.635), and %BF 5.0% (AUC, 0.707) (all P < .001). Simultaneous fulfillment of all 4 thresholds yielded positive predictive values of 91.8% for perfectly patent grafts and 97.3% for functionally patent (perfectly patent or bidirectionally competitive) grafts. Optimal cutoff values for MGF, DF%, and %BF did not differ between left and right coronary territories (P = .265, P = .673, and P = .321, respectively).ConclusionsIntraoperative TTFM demonstrated good predictive performance for early postoperative competitive graft flow in patients undergoing CABG using an in situ ITA-based composite graft.Key Words: coronary artery bypass grafts, CABG, TTFM

Intraoperative TTFM thresholds predict early competitive graft flow after composite CABG.

Central Message

In ITA-based composite CABG, intraoperative TTFM identifies grafts at risk of early competitive flow and provides practical thresholds for physiology-based intraoperative decision-making.

Perspective

Composite CABG relies on shared inflow physiology that alters graft hemodynamics and predisposes to competitive flow. In this study, intraoperative TTFM parameters demonstrated a graded pattern across angiographic flow categories and provided practical composite-specific thresholds. These findings support physiology-based intraoperative assessment and warrant external validation with long-term outcomes.

Intraoperative transit-time flow measurement (TTFM) is a simple and reproducible method to assess graft function during coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG). By providing quantitative flow parameters in real time, TTFM allows the intraoperative evaluation of graft quality and facilitates the identification of technical problems that may require immediate revision. Previous studies have demonstrated that interpretation of TTFM parameters can predict graft dysfunction and postoperative graft failure, highlighting its potential value as a practical intraoperative decision-making tool.1, 2, 3 Accordingly, the 2018 European guidelines on myocardial revascularization recommend routine intraoperative graft assessment using techniques such as TTFM (Class IIa, Level of Evidence B).4 Despite these recommendations, however, global use of intraoperative TTFM remains limited, reportedly being performed in only approximately 30% of CABG procedures worldwide.5

Most previous studies evaluating TTFM have focused on comparisons between arterial and venous conduits, on-pump versus off-pump CABG, or differences in harvesting techniques.6, 7, 8 Meanwhile, contemporary CABG increasingly uses composite grafting strategies based on an in situ internal thoracic artery (ITA) primarily to avoid aortic manipulations and optimize long-term graft durability.9 In this setting, graft flow dynamics differ fundamentally from those of conventional aortocoronary grafting because the entire revascularization depends on a different inflow source. As a result, flow distribution among multiple distal targets and competitive interaction with native coronary circulation may substantially influence intraoperative flow parameters. Despite the growing use of ITA-based composite grafting, the interpretation and validation of TTFM parameters in this specific hemodynamic context remain insufficiently defined. In particular, the relationship between intraoperative TTFM findings and early angiographic patency has not been clearly established in patients undergoing composite CABG. Improved understanding of these relationships may facilitate more accurate intraoperative interpretation of flow measurement and improve identification of grafts at risk of early dysfunction. This study aimed to evaluate the association between intraoperative TTFM parameters and early graft patency in patients undergoing CABG using an in situ ITA-based composite graft.

Methods

The institutional review board approved this retrospective study as a minimal-risk investigation (approval number: 2026-02-004, approval date: February 25, 2026) and waived the requirement for individual informed consent in accordance with institutional guidelines.

Patients

Among 337 patients who underwent isolated CABG between January 2022 and May 2025, 274 patients who received an in situ ITA-based composite graft for multivessel coronary artery disease and underwent early postoperative angiography were included in this study (Figure 1).

Operative Procedures and Intraoperative Graft Assessment

The general surgical principles and operative techniques of CABG have been described previously.10 The ITA was harvested using a semiskeletonization technique, in which the entire length of the ITA with accompanying veins and surrounding tissue was mobilized from the endothoracic fascia. The saphenous vein (SV), harvested using a no-touch technique with surrounding pedicle tissue, was frequently used as the second graft of choice. The lower-leg SV was preferred over the upper-leg SV to minimize size mismatch with native coronary arteries or the ITA. Immediately after harvesting and without pharmacologic treatment, the reversed SV was anastomosed to the side of the ITA to constructing a composite graft. When the SV was unsuitable (eg, severe steno-occlusive arterial disease of the lower extremity or severe varicosities), the contralateral ITA or the right gastroepiploic artery (RGEA) was used. The RGEA was harvested in a skeletonized fashion, followed by proximal and distal transection for use as a composite graft. In left ITA-based Y-composite grafting, the left anterior descending coronary artery territory usually was revascularized first by using the left ITA, followed by revascularization of the left circumflex and right coronary artery territories using the composite graft. Sequential anastomoses were performed when more than 2 distal coronary targets were required to achieve complete revascularization.

Intraoperative TTFM (Medi-Stim AS) was performed just before pericardial closure to assess graft function. During flow measurement, mean arterial pressure was maintained between 70 and 80 mm Hg, and properly fitted probe (1.5, 2, or 3 mm) was selected according to graft diameter. Flow was measured in the left ITA 2 to 3 cm proximal to the Y-anastomosis and in each limb of the Y-composite graft. The flow probe was placed 2 to 3 cm proximal to each target anastomosis for all anastomoses. For the assessment of sequential anastomoses, an atraumatic bulldog clamp was applied distal to the target anastomosis to temporarily occlude flow to the distal graft segment. The following TTFM parameters were analyzed for each anastomosis: mean graft flow (MGF), pulsatility index (PI), diastolic filling percentage (DF%), and percentage of backward flow (%BF). When intraoperative TTFM revealed markedly abnormal findings, such as MGF ≤5 mL/min, PI ≥20, and a nondiastolic dominant flow pattern, intraoperative graft revision was performed. Even when MGF was ≤5 mL/min, a graft was considered patent if PI was ≤5. In grafts with MGF ≤5 mL/min and intermediate PI values (5 < PI < 20), graft revision was considered when the flow pattern was nondiastolic dominant. In cases requiring intraoperative graft revisions, the final TTFM values obtained after revision were included in the analyses.

Postoperative Angiographic Evaluation

Early postoperative angiography was routinely performed as part of institutional postoperative assessment and included both selective graft angiography and native coronary angiography. Patients who declined angiographic evaluation or experienced worsening renal function were excluded, whereas patients receiving renal-replacement therapy were included.

Competitive graft flow was frequently observed on early postoperative angiograms in patients who received an ITA-based composite graft. Postoperative angiographic findings of graft-flow were categorized as perfectly patent, bidirectionally competitive, unidirectionally competitive, and occluded, as previously described.10,11 Perfectly patent graft was defined unimpaired graft flow with adequate distal run-off. Bidirectionally competitive flow was defined as simultaneous opacification of the graft and native coronary flow on both graft and native angiography. Unidirectionally competitive flow was defined as absent graft opacification on graft angiography with preserved graft visualization on native coronary angiography. Occluded graft flow was defined as absence of graft visualization on both graft and native coronary angiography. Angiographic findings were compared with the intraoperative TTFM measurements at each anastomotic site.

Statistical Analyses

Statistical analyses were performed with R software, version 4.5.1. Continuous variables are presented as mean ± standard deviation for normally distributed data or median (interquartile ranges) for nonnormally distributed data as assessed by the Shapiro-Wilk test. Categorical variables are expressed as counts (percentages). The unit of analysis was the distal anastomosis, under the assumption that the anastomoses within each patient were independent of one another. One-way analysis of variance with the Tukey honestly significant difference post hoc test was used to compare TTFM parameters among angiographic graft-flow categories. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analyses were performed to evaluate the predictive performance of TTFM parameters for competitive graft flow. Area under the curve (AUCs) were calculated, and optimal cutoff values were determined using the Youden index. ROC curves were compared using the DeLong test or permutation testing, as appropriate.

Results

Operative Data

Among the 274 patients, the median age was 67.0 [59.0, 74.0] years, and 52 patients (19.0%) were female (Table 1). CABG procedures were performed using an in situ left ITA-based SV composite graft (n = 257), an in situ left ITA-based right ITA composite graft (n = 8), an in situ left ITA-based RGEA composite graft (n = 5), and an in situ right ITA-based SV composite graft (n = 4). A total of 894 distal anastomoses were performed (median 3 [3.0, 4.0] anastomoses per patient). During the study period, intraoperative graft revision was performed in 10 of 894 distal anastomoses (1.1%), corresponding to 10 of 274 patients (3.6%), when markedly abnormal TTFM findings indicated a high likelihood of technical failure.

Postoperative Angiographic Findings and Association With TTFM Parameters

Early postoperative (median 1 [1, 2] days) angiography was performed in all 274 patients. Among the 894 anastomoses, 724 (81.0%) were classified as perfectly patent, 167 (18.7%) as competitive (88 [9.8%] bidirectionally competitive and 79 [8.8%] unidirectionally competitive), and 3 (0.3%) as occluded. TTFM parameters differed significantly across graft-flow categories (Table 2). MGF values progressively decreased from perfectly patent to bidirectionally competitive and unidirectionally competitive grafts (P < .001). Post hoc analysis demonstrated significant differences between perfectly patent and bidirectionally competitive grafts (P < .001) and between perfectly patent and unidirectionally competitive grafts (P < .001), whereas differences involving occluded grafts were not significant, likely because of the small number of occluded grafts. Similar trends were observed for PI, DF%, and %BF, with significant differences between perfectly patent and competitive graft groups (all P < .001), but substantial variability among occluded grafts (Figure 2).

Predictive Performance of TTFM Parameters

ROC curve analyses were performed to assess the ability of TTFM parameters to predict competitive graft flow on early angiography. The optimal cutoff values were as follows: MGF 10 mL/min (sensitivity 67.7%, specificity 68.1%, AUC, 0.725), PI 3.1 (sensitivity 64.7%, specificity 65.7%, AUC, 0.699), DF% 60.0% (sensitivity 46.7%, specificity 78.6%, AUC, 0.635), and %BF 5.0% (sensitivity 61.7%, specificity 71.4%, AUC, 0.707) (all P < .001). MGF, PI, and %BF demonstrated significantly greater predictive performance than DF% (P < .001, P = .020, and P = .015, respectively). However, no significant differences were observed among MGF, PI, and %BF in discriminative ability (MGF vs PI, P = .189; MGF vs %BF, P = .344; PI vs %BF, P = .578) (Figure 3 and Figure E1).

Subgroup Analysis According to Coronary Territory

Of the 894 anastomoses, 668 were performed in the left coronary artery territory (406 left anterior descending and 262 left circumflex artery) and 226 in the right coronary artery territory. In the left coronary territory, optimal cutoff values for predicting competitive flow were of MGF 10 mL/min (AUC, 0.705), PI 3.1 (AUC, 0.683), DF% 59.0% (AUC, 0.637), and %BF 5.7% (AUC, 0.684) (all P < .001) (Figure E2). In the right coronary territory, corresponding cutoff values were MGF 7 mL/min (AUC, 0.804), PI 2.5 (AUC, 0.749), DF% 60.0% (AUC, 0.664), and %BF 2.6% (AUC, 0.775) (all P ≤ .001) (Figure E3). Comparison of ROC-derived thresholds showed no significant differences between left and right coronary territories for MGF, DF%, or %BF (P = .265, P = .673, P = .321, respectively). In contrast, the optimal PI cutoff differed significantly between territories (P = .034) (Figure 4).

Discussion

The present study yielded 3 principal findings. First, intraoperative TTFM parameters (MGF, PI, DF%, and %BF) differed significantly among grafts classified as patent, competitive, or occluded on early postoperative angiography. Second, MGF, PI, and %BF demonstrated superior discriminatory performance compared with DF% for predicting early competitive graft flow. Third, optimal cutoff values of most TTFM parameters (MGF, DF%, and %BF) for predicting competitive graft flow did not differ significantly between left and right coronary artery territories.

Previous studies reported that competitive graft flow was frequently observed on early postoperative angiograms in patients who received an ITA-based composite graft and demonstrated an association between intraoperative TTFM values and postoperative competitive flow.11,12 In the present study, competitive flow was identified in 18.7% of distal anastomoses in early angiograms of patients who underwent ITA-based composite grafting. TTFM values (MGF, PI, DF%, and %BF) differed between perfectly patent and competitive grafts and were predictive of early postoperative competitive graft flow. Notably, MGF and DF% decreased, whereas PI and %BF increased across grafts classified as perfectly patent, bidirectionally competitive, and unidirectionally competitive grafts, suggesting a graded hemodynamic pattern associated with flow competition. This stepwise change supports the concept that competitive flow represents a continuum of altered hemodynamic interaction between the graft and native coronary circulation rather than a binary phenomenon.

Intraoperative graft failure remains a major determinant of early graft patency after CABG. Intraoperative graft assessment enables immediate detection and revision of technical problems before chest closure, which is critical for ensuring graft patency and improving clinical outcomes. Routine use of intraoperative TTFM has therefore been recommended.4,13 Previous studies have consistently demonstrated associations between TTFM parameters and graft patency. Kim and colleagues1 reported criteria for detecting abnormal graft flow in total arterial CABG. Di Giammarco and colleagues2 and Tokuda and colleagues14 also identified combinations of low MGF, elevated PI, and increased %BF as graft dysfunction. Similarly, a large cohort study by Quin and colleagues15 showed that reduced MGF and elevated PI were associated with midterm graft occlusion. Our findings are largely consistent with these prior observations but specifically extend their applicability to ITA-based composite grafting, in which flow dynamics may differ from conventional aortocoronary configurations.

Competitive flow typically arises when the target coronary artery stenosis is less than critical and residual native coronary flow remains sufficient to oppose graft inflow. Because competitive flow has been linked to later graft occlusion,16,17 intraoperative detection of flow patterns suggestive of competition may have important clinical implications. In the present study, thresholds of MGF <10 mL/min, PI >3.1, %BF >5.0%, and DF% <60% were all predictive of competitive flow on early angiograms. Although these values were generally consistent with previous studies, the MGF threshold observed in the present study likely reflects the intrinsic hemodynamic characteristics of ITA-based composite grafting, where multiple distal targets share a single inflow source. Under these conditions, absolute flow values in each distal anastomosis may be reduced despite adequate overall perfusion, particularly during the early postoperative period before physiologic flow adaptation occurs.

Importantly, when all 4 TTFM parameters simultaneously met their respective thresholds, predictive performance improved substantially. The combined criteria yielded a positive predictive value of 91.8% for perfectly patent grafts and 97.3% when perfectly patent and bidirectionally competitive grafts were considered functionally patent. These findings suggest that integrated interpretation of multiple TTFM parameters may enhance intraoperative confidence in graft adequacy in composite configurations. However, sensitivity of the combined criteria was modest, indicating that while fulfillment of all thresholds strongly supports graft adequacy, absence of one or more criteria does not necessarily imply graft dysfunction. Although individual TTFM parameters demonstrated modest sensitivity, their high positive predictive value and association with a low incidence of early graft occlusion in the present study support the role of TTFM as a reliable confirmatory intraoperative tool.

Interestingly, we did not observe substantial differences in optimal cutoff TTFM values between left and right coronary territories. This finding contrasts with previous reports suggesting territory-specific thresholds.1,14 In composite grafting, however, distal branches are hemodynamically interconnected through a common inflow, which may attenuate territory-specific differences and result in more homogenous flow characteristics. From a practical standpoint, these findings suggest that uniform TTFM thresholds may be applicable in clinical decision-making during ITA-based composite CABG, thereby simplifying intraoperative interpretation.

Notably, intraoperative TTFM values in occluded grafts were not consistently abnormal. This observation indicates that early graft occlusion may occur despite apparently acceptable intraoperative flow parameters. Mechanisms unrelated to immediate technical failure or overt flow competition—endothelial injury, graft dissection or hematoma, thrombosis, kinking, or poor distal runoff of target vessels—may contribute to early graft failure. Therefore, TTFM should be interpreted as a complementary intraoperative tool rather than a standalone determinant of graft adequacy, and findings may be integrated with surgical judgment, graft configuration, and target vessel characteristics.

Collectively, these findings support the clinical utility of comprehensive interpretation of TTFM parameters during ITA-based composite grafting. Rather than relying on a single metric, simultaneous assessment of multiple TTFM parameters may improve identification of grafts at risk of early flow competition and facilitate more informed intraoperative decision-making.

In conclusion, in patients undergoing CABG with an in situ ITA-based composite graft, intraoperative TTFM demonstrated good predictive performance for early postoperative competitive graft flow. Comprehensive interpretation of TTFM parameters may serve as a practical intraoperative guide for identifying grafts at risk for early flow competition in composite CABG (Figure 5).

Study Limitations

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, this study was a single-center retrospective observational analysis, which may introduce selection bias and limit generalizability. Second, the unit of analysis was individual anastomoses; therefore, graft configuration and differences between sequential and individual anastomoses were not specifically accounted for. Third, the study did not incorporate lesion-specific stenosis severity; therefore, the interaction between native coronary physiology and graft flow could not be fully evaluated. Fourth, only patients who underwent early postoperative angiography were included, which may have introduced selection bias, although postoperative angiography was omitted in only one in-hospital mortality case. Fifth, although TTFM measurements were obtained under standardized hemodynamic conditions, flow parameters may still be influenced by systemic vascular resistance, heart rate, ventricular function, and vasoactive medications. Sixth, the study focused on early postoperative angiographic findings, and long-term graft patency was not evaluated. Finally, the derived cutoff values require external validation in independent cohorts before routine clinical implementation.

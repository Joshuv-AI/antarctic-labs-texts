On Landau-Type Theorems for Poly-Analytic Functions
Vasudevarao Allu, Rohit Kumar

Abstract
. In this paper, we establish three Landau-type theorems for certain bounded poly-analytic functions, which generalize the corresponding result for bi-analytic functions given by Liu and Ponnusamy [Canad. Math. Bull. 67(1): 2024, 152-165]. Further, we prove three bi-Lipschitz theorems for these subclasses of poly-analytic functions.

1.
Introduction and Preliminaries

There are non-analytic functions that satisfy properties similar to those of analytic functions, and they have a notable structure. Such non-analytic functions are called poly-analytic functions.
A complex-valued function
F:\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
of class
C^{m}
on a domain
\Omega
is called a poly-analytic function of order
m\geq 1
, if

\frac{\partial^{m}}{\partial\bar{z}^{m}}F(z)=0,\quad\text{for all }z\in\Omega.

Any poly-analytic function of order
m
can be decomposed in terms of
m
analytic functions so that we have a decomposition of the following form

F(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\bar{z}^{k}f_{k}(z).

For a continuously differentiable function
F
, denote

\Lambda_{F}=\max_{0\leq\theta\leq 2\pi}\left|F_{z}+e^{-2i\theta}F_{\bar{z}}\right|=\left|F_{z}\right|+\left|F_{\bar{z}}\right|

and

\lambda_{F}=\min_{0\leq\theta\leq 2\pi}\left|F_{z}+e^{-2i\theta}F_{\bar{z}}\right|=||F_{z}|-|F_{\bar{z}}||,

where
F_{z}=\partial F/\partial z
and
F_{\bar{z}}=\partial F/\partial\bar{z}
. A mapping
g:\Omega\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
is said to be
L_{1}
-Lipschitz
\left(L_{1}>0\right)
if

\left|g\left(z_{1}\right)-g\left(z_{2}\right)\right|\leq L_{1}\left|z_{1}-z_{2}\right|,z_{1},z_{2}\in\Omega

and it is said to be
l_{1}
-co-Lipschitz
\left(l_{1}>0\right)
if

\left|g\left(z_{1}\right)-g\left(z_{2}\right)\right|\geq l_{1}\left|z_{1}-z_{2}\right|,z_{1},z_{2}\in\Omega.

A mapping
\omega
is bi-Lipschitz if it is Lipschitz and co-Lipschitz.

The theory of poly-analytic functions is an interesting topic in complex analysis. It extends the concept of holomorphic functions to nullsolutions of higher-order powers of the Cauchy-Riemann operator. These functions were first introduced in 1908 by Kolossov
[
21
]
to study elasticity problems. For a complete introduction to poly-analytic functions and their basic properties we refer to
[
12
,
13
]
. The class of poly-analytic functions have been studied by various authors from different perspectives; see
[
4
,
5
,
6
,
30
]
and the references therein. Recently, the Landau-type theorem for some subclasses of poly-analytic functions has been studied by Abdulhadi and Hajj
[
1
]
and Liu and Ponnusamy
[
27
]
.

The study of polyanalytic functions has seen substantial progress over recent years, with foundational and applied insights emerging in various works. In 2016, Daghighi and Krantz
[
17
]
explored the local maximum modulus property for polyanalytic functions, extending the classical principle from analytic to polyanalytic settings and shedding light on the geometric and functional behaviour of these functions. Building on this, in 2023 Allu and Halder
[
7
]
explored the Bohr operator on operator-valued polyanalytic functions in simply connected domains, providing operator-theoretic insights. Most recently, Vasilevski
[
31
]
examined their properties in several complex variables, extending classical analysis.

Let
\mathbb{D}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}
represent the unit disc in the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
. Throughout this paper,
{\mathbb{D}}_{r}=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}:|z|<r\}
denotes the open disc about the origin. For the convenience of the reader, let us fix some basic notations.

•

\mathcal{H}_{m}({\mathbb{D}})=\{F:F
is poly-analytic in
{\mathbb{D}}
with
F(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\bar{z}^{k}f_{k}(z)
, where
f_{k}
are analytic in
{\mathbb{D}}
}.

•

\mathcal{H}_{m}^{0}({\mathbb{D}})=\{F\in\mathcal{H}_{m}({\mathbb{D}}):F_{z}(0)=1
and
f_{k}(0)=0
for each
k
}.

•

\mathcal{F}_{1}=\{F\in\mathcal{H}_{m}^{0}({\mathbb{D}}):|f_{0}^{\prime}(z)|<\Lambda,|f_{k}(z)|\leq M_{k}
for each
k
}.

•

\mathcal{F}_{2}=\{F\in\mathcal{H}_{m}^{0}({\mathbb{D}}):|f_{0}(z)|<M,|f_{k}^{\prime}(z)|\leq\Lambda_{k}
for each
k
}.

•

\mathcal{F}_{3}=\{F\in\mathcal{H}_{m}^{0}({\mathbb{D}}):|f_{0}^{\prime}(z)|<\Lambda_{0},|f_{k}^{\prime}(z)|\leq\Lambda_{k}
for each
k
}.

In this paper, we consider the subclasses
\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2}
and
\mathcal{F}_{3}
of poly-analytic functions and establish sharp versions of the Landau-type theorem for these classes of mappings. Further, we prove three bi-Lipschitz properties for these classes of functions.

1.1.
The Landau Theorem

The classical Landau theorem (see
[
22
]
) asserts that if
f
is a holomorphic mapping of the unit disc
{\mathbb{D}}=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}:|z|<1\}
with
f(0)=0
,
f^{\prime}(0)=1
and
|f(z)|<M
for
z\in\mathbb{D}
, then
f
is univalent in
\mathbb{D}_{r_{0}}
, and
f(\mathbb{D}_{r_{0}})
contains a disc
\mathbb{D}_{\sigma_{0}}
, where

r_{0}=\frac{1}{M+\sqrt{M^{2}-1}}\ \ \text{and}\ \ \sigma_{0}=Mr_{0}^{2}.

The quantities
r_{0}
and
\sigma_{0}
cannot be improved as the function
f_{0}(z)=Mz\left(\frac{1-Mz}{M-z}\right)
shows the sharpness of the quantities
r_{0}
and
\sigma_{0}
.

In 2000, Chen
et al.

[
14
]
obtained the Landau-type theorems for harmonic mappings in the unit disc
{\mathbb{D}}
.

Theorem A
.

[
14
]

Let
f
be a harmonic mapping of the unit disc
\mathbb{D}
such that
f(0)=0
,
f_{\bar{z}}(0)=0,f_{z}(0)=1
, and
|f(z)|<M
for
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Then,
f
is univalent on a disc
\mathbb{D}_{\rho_{0}}
with

\rho_{0}=\frac{\pi^{2}}{16mM},

and
f\left(\mathbb{D}_{\rho_{0}}\right)
contains a schlicht disc
\mathbb{D}_{R_{0}}
with

R_{0}=\rho_{0}/2=\frac{\pi^{2}}{32mM},

where
m\approx 6.85
is the minimum of the function
(3-r^{2})/(r(1-r^{2}))
for
0<r<1
.

Theorem B
.

[
14
]

Let
f
be a harmonic mapping of the unit disc
\mathbb{D}
such that
f(0)=0
,
\lambda_{f}(0)=1
and
\Lambda_{f}(z)\leq\Lambda
for
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Then,
f
is univalent on a disc
\mathbb{D}_{\rho_{0}}
with

\rho_{0}=\frac{\pi}{4(1+\Lambda)},

and
f\left(\mathbb{D}_{\rho_{0}}\right)
contains a schlicht disc
\mathbb{D}_{R_{0}}
with

R_{0}=\frac{1}{2}\rho_{0}=\frac{\pi}{8(1+\Lambda)}.

It is important to note that the radii
\rho_{o}
and
R_{0}
in Theorems
A
and
B
are not sharp. Theorem
A
and Theorem
B
have been improved by Chen
et al.

[

[

\Lambda\geq 1
, and when
\Lambda=1

f
is univalent in the disc
{\mathbb{D}}_{\rho}
and
f({\mathbb{D}}_{\rho})

{\mathbb{D}}_{R}
with
\rho=R=1

Theorem C
.

[
1
]

Let
F(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\bar{z}^{k}f_{k}(z)
be a poly-analytic function of order
m,m\geq 2
on the unit disc
{\mathbb{D}}
, where
f_{k}
are holomorphic such that
f_{k}(0)=0,f_{k}^{\prime}(0)=1
and
\left|f_{k}(z)\right|\leq M
, for all
k
, with
M>1
. Then there is a constant
0<\rho<1
so that
F
is univalent in
|z|<\rho
. In particular,
\rho

1-M\left(\frac{\rho\left(2-\rho\right)}{\left(1-\rho\right)^{2}}+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\frac{\rho^{k}\left(1+k-k\rho\right)}{\left(1-k\rho\right)^{2}}\right)=0

and

It is important to note that the radii
\rho

In 2024, Liu and Ponnusamy
[

]
obtained three versions of the Landau-type theorem for bounded bi-analytic functions. Two of them are given below.

Theorem D
.

[
27
]

Suppose that
\Lambda_{1}\geq 0
and
\Lambda_{2}>1
. Let
F(z)=\bar{z}G(z)+

H(z)
be a bi-analytic function, with
G(0)=H(0)=H^{\prime}(0)-1=0,|G^{\prime}(z)|\leq\Lambda_{1}
and
|H^{\prime}(z)|<\Lambda_{2}
for all

. Then
F
is univalent on a disc
{\mathbb{D}}_{\rho_{1}}

\rho_{1}=\frac{2\Lambda_{2}}{\Lambda_{2}\left(2\Lambda_{1}+\Lambda_{2}\right)+\sqrt{\Lambda_{2}^{2}\left(2\Lambda_{1}+\Lambda_{2}\right)^{2}-8\Lambda_{1}\Lambda_{2}}}

and

F({\mathbb{D}}_{\rho_{1}})
contains a schlicht disc
{\mathbb{D}}_{\sigma_{1}}
with

\sigma_{1}=F_{1}\left(\rho\right),\quad F_{1}(z)=\Lambda_{2}^{2}z-\Lambda_{1}|z|^{2}+\left(\Lambda_{2}^{3}-\Lambda_{2}\right)\ln\left(1-\frac{z}{\Lambda_{2}}\right).

This result is sharp, with an extremal function given by
F_{1}(z)
.

Theorem E
.

[
27
]

Suppose that
\Lambda\geq 0
. Let
F(z)=\bar{z}G(z)+

H(z)
be a bi-analytic function, with

2.
Key Results

Lemma 2.1
.

[
27
]

Let
H
be a holomorphic mapping of the unit disc
{\mathbb{D}}
with
H^{\prime}(0)=1
and
\left|H^{\prime}(z)\right|<\Lambda
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
and for some
\Lambda>1
.

(1)

For all
z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathbb{D}_{r}\left(0<r<1,z_{1}\neq z_{2}\right)
, we have

\left|H\left(z_{1}\right)-H\left(z_{2}\right)\right|=\left|\int_{\gamma}H^{\prime}(z)dz\right|\geq\Lambda\frac{1-\Lambda r}{\Lambda-r}\left|z_{1}-z_{2}\right|,

where
\gamma=\left[z_{1},z_{2}\right]
denotes the closed line segment joining
z_{1}
and
z_{2}
.

(2)

For
z^{\prime}\in\partial\mathbb{D}_{r}(0<r<1)
with
w^{\prime}=H\left(z^{\prime}\right)\in H\left(\partial\mathbb{D}_{r}\right)
and
\left|w^{\prime}\right|=\min\{|w|:w\in H

\left.\left(\partial\mathbb{D}_{r}\right)\right\}
, set
\gamma_{0}=H^{-1}\left(\Gamma_{0}\right)
and
\Gamma_{0}=\left[0,w^{\prime}\right]
denotes the closed line segment joining the origin and
w^{\prime}
. Then we have

\left|H\left(z^{\prime}\right)\right|\geq\Lambda\int_{0}^{r}\frac{\frac{1}{\Lambda}-t}{1-\frac{t}{\Lambda}}dt=\Lambda^{2}r+\left(\Lambda^{3}-\Lambda\right)\ln\left(1-\frac{r}{\Lambda}\right).

Lemma 2.2
.

[
27
]

Let
f=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
be a holomorphic mapping of the unit disc with
|a_{1}|=1
and
|f(z)|\leq M
for each
z\in{\mathbb{D}}
. Then
M\geq 1
and

\left|a_{n}\right|\leq M-\frac{1}{M}\text{ for }n=2,3,\ldots.

These inequalities are sharp, with the extremal functions
f_{n}(z)
, where

f_{1}(z)=z,\quad f_{n}(z)=Mz\frac{1-Mz^{n-1}}{M-z^{n-1}}=z-\left(M-\frac{1}{M}\right)z^{n}-\sum_{k=3}^{\infty}\frac{M^{2}-1}{M^{k-1}}z^{(n-1)(k-1)+1}

for
n=2,3,\ldots
.

Theorem F
.

[
16
, Theorem 2]

Let
f:{\mathbb{D}}\to{\mathbb{D}}
be a holomorphic function. Then

\sup_{z\in{\mathbb{D}}}\left(1-|z|^{2}\right)|f^{\prime}(z)|\leq 1.

Now, we prove the following lemma, which is vital to the proof of main results.

Lemma 2.3
.

For
r\in[0,1)
, let

\phi(r)=\frac{\Lambda(1-\Lambda r)}{\Lambda-r}-\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}r^{k}\left(\frac{M_{k}}{1-r^{2}}+kM_{k}\right)

where
\Lambda>1
and
M_{k}\geq 0
are real constants. Then
\phi
is strictly decreasing and there is unique
r_{0}\in(0,1)
such that
\phi(r_{0})=0
. Furthermore, we have

\Lambda^{2}r_{0}+(\Lambda^{3}-\Lambda)\ln\left(1-\frac{r_{0}}{\Lambda}\right)-\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}r_{0}^{k+1}M_{k}>0.

Proof.

The function
\phi
is strictly decreasing follows from the fact that

\displaystyle\phi^{\prime}(r)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Lambda-\Lambda^{3}}{(\Lambda-r)^{2}}-\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\frac{kr^{k-1}\left(1+r^{2}\right)}{(1-r^{2})^{2}}M_{k}+k^{2}r^{k-1}M_{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Lambda-\Lambda^{3}}{(\Lambda-r)^{2}}-\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}kr^{k-1}\left(\frac{1+r^{2}}{(1-r^{2})^{2}}M_{k}+kM_{k}\right)

\displaystyle<0\quad\text{ for }r\in(0,1)\text{. }

Furthermore, we observe that

\phi(0)=1\text{ and }\lim_{r\to 1^{-}}\phi(r)=-\infty\text{. }

Therefore, by intermediate value theorem and the fact that
\phi
is strictly decreasing, there exists unique real
r_{0}\in(0,1)
such that
\phi(r_{0})=0
.

Since
\phi
is strictly decreasing and
\phi(0)=1
, we see that

\frac{\Lambda(1-\Lambda r)}{\Lambda-r}-\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}r^{k}\left(\frac{M_{k}}{1-r^{2}}+kM_{k}\right)>0\quad\text{for}\ r\in(0,r_{0}).

This implies that

\frac{\Lambda(1-\Lambda r)}{\Lambda-r}-\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}r^{k}(k+1)M_{k}>0\quad\text{for}\ r\in(0,r_{0}).

(2.1)

Integrating (
2.1
) from
0
to
r_{0}
, we obtain

\Lambda^{2}r_{0}+(\Lambda^{3}-\Lambda)\ln\left(1-\frac{r_{0}}{\Lambda}\right)-\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}r_{0}^{k+1}M_{k}>0.

This completes the proof.
∎

3.
Landau-Bloch type Theorem for Poly-analytic functions

In this section, we derive three Landau-type theorems for certain subclasses of poly-analytic functions. We first establish the Landau-type theorem for subclass
\mathcal{F}_{1}
of poly-analytic functions.

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose that
m
is a positive integer and
M_{1},M_{2},..,M_{k-1}\geq 0
.
Let
F(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\bar{z}^{k}f_{k}(z)
be a poly-analytic function of order
m
on the unit disc
{\mathbb{D}}
, where all the
f_{k}
are holomorphic on
{\mathbb{D}}
, satisfying
f_{k}(0)=F_{z}(0)-1=0
for
k\in\{0,1,\cdots,m-1\}.
If
|f_{0}^{\prime}(z)|<\Lambda
and
|f_{k}(z)|\leq M_{k}
,
k\in\{1,2,\cdots,m-1\}
for all
z\in{\mathbb{D}}
, then

\Lambda>1
,
F(z)
is univalent in
{\mathbb{D}}_{r_{1}}
and
F({\mathbb{D}}_{r_{1}})
contains a schlicht disc
{\mathbb{D}}_{R_{1}}
, where
r_{1}
is the unique root in
(0,1)
of the equation

\frac{\Lambda(1-\Lambda r)}{\Lambda-r}-\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}r^{k}\left(\frac{M_{k}}{1-r^{2}}+kM_{k}\right)=0

and

R_{1}=\Lambda^{2}r_{1}+(\Lambda^{3}-\Lambda)\ln\left(1-\frac{r_{1}}{\Lambda}\right)-\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}r_{1}^{k+1}M_{k}.

Morever, when
M_{k}=0,k\in\{1,2,\cdots,m-1\}
, the result is sharp.

Proof.

We first prove that
F
is univalent in
{\mathbb{D}}_{r_{1}}
. Let
z_{1},z_{2}\in{\mathbb{D}}_{r}(0<r<r_{1})
be two distinct points, then

\displaystyle|F(z_{1})-F(z_{2})|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\bar{z}_{1}^{k}f_{k}(z_{1})-\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\bar{z}_{2}^{k}f_{k}(z)\right|

\displaystyle\geq|f_{0}(z_{1})-f_{0}(z_{2})|-\left|\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\bar{z}_{1}^{k}f_{k}(z_{1})-\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\bar{z}_{2}^{k}f_{k}(z_{2})\right|.

Since
F_{z}(0)=f_{0}^{\prime}(0)=1,|f_{0}^{\prime}(z)|<\Lambda
, by Lemma
2.1
, we have

|f_{0}(z_{1})-f_{0}(z_{2})|\geq\Lambda\frac{1-\Lambda r}{\Lambda-r}|z_{1}-z_{2}|.

(3.1)

Let
[z_{1},z_{2}]
be the line segment joining
z_{1}
to
z_{2}
, then by Theorem
F
and the Schwarz lemma we have

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\bar{z}_{1}^{k}f_{k}(z_{1})-\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\bar{z}_{2}^{k}f_{k}(z_{2})\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\int_{[z_{1},z_{2}]}\left(\bar{z}^{k}f_{k}^{\prime}(z)dz+k\bar{z}^{k-1}f_{k}(z)d\bar{z}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\int_{[z_{1},z_{2}]}|\bar{z}|^{k}|f_{k}^{\prime}(z)||dz|

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\int_{[z_{1},z_{2}]}k|\bar{z}|^{k-1}|f_{k}(z)||d\bar{z}|

\displaystyle\leq|z_{1}-z_{2}|\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}r^{k}\frac{M_{k}}{1-r^{2}}+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}kr^{k-1}M_{k}r\right)

\displaystyle=|z_{1}-z_{2}|\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}r^{k}\left(\frac{M_{k}}{1-r^{2}}+kM_{k}\right)\right).

Therefore by Lemma
2.3
and (
3.1
) and, we have

\displaystyle|F(z_{1})-F(z_{2})|

\displaystyle\geq|z_{1}-z_{2}|\left[\frac{\Lambda(1-\Lambda r)}{\Lambda-r}-\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}r^{k}\left(\frac{M_{k}}{1-r^{2}}+kM_{k}\right)\right]

\displaystyle>|z_{1}-z_{2}|\left[\frac{\Lambda(1-\Lambda r_{1})}{\Lambda-r_{1}}-\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}r_{1}^{k}\left(\frac{M_{k}}{1-r_{1}^{2}}+kM_{k}\right)\right]=0.

Thus
F(z_{1})\neq F(z_{2})
, which implies the univalence of
F
in
{\mathbb{D}}_{r_{1}}
.

To prove the second part of the theorem, let
z\in\partial{\mathbb{D}}_{r_{1}},
by Lemma
2.1
and the Schwarz lemma, we have

\displaystyle|F(z)|

\displaystyle\geq|f_{0}(z)|-\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}|\bar{z}|^{k}|f_{k}(z)|

\displaystyle\geq\Lambda^{2}r_{1}+(\Lambda^{3}-\Lambda)\ln\left(1-\frac{r_{1}}{\Lambda}\right)-\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}r_{1}^{k}M_{k}r_{1}

\displaystyle=\Lambda^{2}r_{1}+(\Lambda^{3}-\Lambda)\ln\left(1-\frac{r_{1}}{\Lambda}\right)-\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}r_{1}^{k+1}M_{k}=R_{1}>0.

Hence,
F({\mathbb{D}}_{r_{1}})
contains a disc of positive radius
R_{1}
.

Finally, when
M_{k}=0,k=1,2,...,m-1,

F(z)=f_{0}(z)
is a holomorphic mapping, it follows from
[
26
, Theorem 1]
that the result is sharp. This completes the proof.
∎

Now, we generalize Theorem
E
by establishing the following result for the subclass
\mathcal{F}_{2}
of poly-analytic functions.

Theorem 3.2
.

Suppose that
m
is a positive integer. Let
F(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\bar{z}^{k}f_{k}(z)
be a poly-analytic function of order
m
on the unit disc
{\mathbb{D}}
, where all the
f_{k}
are holomorphic on
{\mathbb{D}}
, satisfying
f_{k}(0)=F_{z}(0)-1=0
for
k\in\{0,1,\cdots,m-1\}.
If
|f_{0}(z)|<M
and
|f_{k}^{\prime}(z)|\leq\Lambda_{k}
,
k\in\{1,2,\cdots,m-1\}
for all
z\in{\mathbb{D}}
, then

\Lambda_{k}\geq 0,M\geq 1
,
F(z)
is univalent in
{\mathbb{D}}_{r_{2}}
and
F({\mathbb{D}}_{r_{2}})
contains a schlicht disc
{\mathbb{D}}_{R_{2}}
, where
r_{2}
is the unique root in
(0,1)
of the equation

1-\left(M-\frac{1}{M}\right)\frac{2r-r^{2}}{(1-r)^{2}}-\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}(k+1)r^{k}\Lambda_{k}=0

and

R_{2}=r_{2}-\left(M-\frac{1}{M}\right)\frac{r_{2}^{2}}{1-r_{2}}-\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}r^{k+1}\Lambda_{k}.

Proof.

By the hypothesis of Theorem
3.2
, for each
k\in\{1,2,\cdots,m-1\}
, we have

|f_{k}(z)|=\left|\int_{[0,z]}f_{k}^{\prime}(z)dz\right|\leq\int_{[0,z]}|f_{k}^{\prime}(z)||dz|\leq\Lambda_{k}|z|,\quad z\in{\mathbb{D}}.

(3.2)

Let
f_{0}(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n,0}z^{n}
be the series representation of
f_{0}(z)
and
[z_{1},z_{2}]
denote the line segment joining
z_{1}
and
z_{2}
. Then for any
z_{1},z_{2}\in{\mathbb{D}}_{r}(0<r<r_{2})
with
z_{1}\neq z_{2}
, we have

\displaystyle|F(z_{1})-F(z_{2})|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{[z_{1},z_{2}]}F_{z}(z)dz+F_{\bar{z}}(z)d\bar{z}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{[z_{1},z_{2}]}F_{z}(0)dz+F_{\bar{z}}(0)d\bar{z}\right.

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\left.\int_{[z_{1},z_{2}]}\left(F_{z}(z)-F_{z}(0)\right)dz+\left(F_{\bar{z}}(z)-F_{\bar{z}}(0)\right)d\bar{z}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{[z_{1},z_{2}]}dz+\int_{[z_{1},z_{2}]}(f_{0}^{\prime}(z)-1)dz\right.

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\left.\int_{[z_{1},z_{2}]}\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\left[\bar{z}^{k}f_{k}^{\prime}(z)dz+k\bar{z}^{k-1}f_{k}(z)d\bar{z}\right]\right|

\displaystyle\geq|z_{1}-z_{2}|-\int_{[z_{1},z_{2}]}\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}n|a_{n,0}|r^{n-1}|dz|

\displaystyle\quad\quad-\int_{[z_{1},z_{2}]}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}|\bar{z}|^{k}|f_{k}^{\prime}(z)|+k|\bar{z}|^{k-1}|f_{k}(z)|\right)|d\bar{z}|.

By the equation (
3.2
) and Lemma
2.2
, we have

\displaystyle|F(z_{1})-F(z_{2})|

\displaystyle\geq|z_{1}-z_{2}|-\int_{[z_{1},z_{2}]}\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}n\left(M-\frac{1}{M}\right)r^{n-1}|dz|

\displaystyle\quad\quad-\int_{[z_{1},z_{2}]}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\Lambda_{k}r^{k}+kr^{k-1}\Lambda_{k}r\right)|d\bar{z}|

\displaystyle\geq|z_{1}-z_{2}|\left(1-\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}nr^{n-1}\left(M-\frac{1}{M}\right)-\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}(k+1)r^{k}\Lambda_{k}\right)

\displaystyle=|z_{1}-z_{2}|\left(1-\left(M-\frac{1}{M}\right)\frac{2r-r^{2}}{(1-r)^{2}}-\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}(k+1)r^{k}\Lambda_{k}\right).

For
r\in[0,1)
, consider the continuous function

\psi_{1}(r)=1-\left(M-\frac{1}{M}\right)\frac{2r-r^{2}}{(1-r)^{2}}-\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}(k+1)r^{k}\Lambda_{k}.

Similar to the proof of Lemma
2.3
, we can verify that
\psi_{1}(r)
is a strictly decreasing function in
[0,1]
and there exists unique real number
r_{2}\in(0,1)
such that
\psi_{1}(r_{2})=0
.

Thus, we have

|F(z_{1})-F(z_{2})|\geq\psi_{1}(r)>\psi_{1}(r_{2})=0.

This implies
F(z_{1})\neq F(z_{2}),
which proves the univalence of
F
in the disc
{\mathbb{D}}_{r_{2}}
.

To prove the second part of of the theorem, let
z\in\partial{\mathbb{D}}_{r_{2}},
by Lemma
2.2
and equation (
3.2
)

\displaystyle|F(z)|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\bar{z}^{k}f_{k}(z)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n,0}z^{n}+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\bar{z}^{k}f_{k}(z)\right|

\displaystyle\geq|a_{1,0}z|-\left|\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}a_{n,0}z^{n}\right|-\left|\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\bar{z}^{k}f_{k}(z)\right|

\displaystyle\geq r_{2}-\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}|a_{n,0}|r_{2}^{n}-\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}r_{2}^{k}|f_{k}(z)|

\displaystyle\geq r_{2}-\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\left(M-\frac{1}{M}\right)r_{2}^{n}-\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}r_{2}^{k+1}\Lambda_{k}

\displaystyle=r_{2}-\left(M-\frac{1}{M}\right)\frac{r_{2}^{2}}{1-r_{2}}-\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}r_{2}^{k+1}\Lambda_{k}=R_{2}.

By following the same method as in the proof of Lemma
2.3
, we can show that
R_{2}
is strictly positive. Hence,
F({\mathbb{D}}_{r_{2}})
contains a disc of positive radius
R_{2}
. This completes the proof.
∎

Finally, we obtain a sharp result for the Landau-type theorem for subclass
\mathcal{F}_{3}
of poly-analytic functions, which generalizes the results of Theorem
D
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Suppose that
m
is a positive integer,
\Lambda_{1},\cdots,\Lambda_{m-1}\geq 0
and
\Lambda_{0}>1
. Let
F(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\bar{z}^{k}f_{k}(z)
be a poly-analytic function of unit disc
{\mathbb{D}}
, where all
f_{k}
are holomorphic on
{\mathbb{D}}
, satisfying
f_{k}(0)=F_{z}(0)-1=0
for
k\in\{0,1,\cdots,m-1\}
. If
|f_{0}^{\prime}(z)|<\Lambda_{0}
and
|f_{k}^{\prime}(z)|\leq\Lambda_{k},k\in\{1,2,\cdots,m-1\}
for all
z\in{\mathbb{D}}
, then
F(z)
is univalent in
{\mathbb{D}}_{r_{3}}
, and
F\left({\mathbb{D}}_{r_{3}}\right)
contains a schlicht disc
{\mathbb{D}}_{R_{3}}
, where
r_{3}
is the unique root in
(0,1)
of the following equation:

\Lambda_{0}\frac{1-\Lambda_{0}r}{\Lambda_{0}-r}-\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}(k+1)\Lambda_{k}r^{k}=0

and

R_{3}=\Lambda_{0}^{2}r_{3}+\left(\Lambda_{0}^{3}-\Lambda_{0}\right)\ln\left(1-\frac{r_{3}}{\Lambda_{0}}\right)-\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\Lambda_{k}r_{3}^{k+1}.

Moreover, these estimates are sharp, with an extremal function given by

F_{0}(z)=\Lambda_{0}^{2}z+\left(\Lambda_{0}^{3}-\Lambda_{0}\right)\ln\left(1-\frac{z}{\Lambda_{0}}\right)-\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\bar{z}^{k}\Lambda_{k}z,z\in{\mathbb{D}}.

(3.3)

Proof.

We first prove that
F
is univalent in
{\mathbb{D}}_{r_{3}}
. Let
z_{1},z_{2}\in{\mathbb{D}}_{r}(0<r<r_{3})
be two distinct points, then

\displaystyle|F(z_{1})-F(z_{2})|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\bar{z}_{1}^{k}f_{k}(z_{1})-\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\bar{z}_{2}^{k}f_{k}(z)\right|

\displaystyle\geq|f_{0}(z_{1})-f_{0}(z_{2})|-\left|\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\bar{z}_{1}^{k}f_{k}(z_{1})-\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\bar{z}_{2}^{k}f_{k}(z_{2})\right|.

Since
F_{z}(0)=f_{0}^{\prime}(0)=1,|f_{0}^{\prime}(z)|<\Lambda_{0}
, in view of Lemma
2.1
, we have

|f_{0}(z_{1})-f_{0}(z_{2})|\geq\Lambda_{0}\frac{1-\Lambda_{0}r}{\Lambda_{0}-r}|z_{1}-z_{2}|.

Since
|f_{k}^{\prime}(z)|\leq\Lambda_{k}
for each
k\in\{1,2,,\cdots,m-1\}
, we have from (
3.2
) that
|f_{k}(z)|\leq\Lambda_{k}|z|,\ z\in{\mathbb{D}}.

Let
[z_{1},z_{2}]
denote the line segment joining
z_{1}
and
z_{2}
, then we have

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\bar{z}_{1}^{k}f_{k}(z_{1})-\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\bar{z}_{2}^{k}f_{k}(z_{2})\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\int_{[z_{1},z_{2}]}\left(\bar{z}^{k}f_{k}^{\prime}(z)dz+k\bar{z}^{k-1}f_{k}(z)d\bar{z}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\int_{[z_{1},z_{2}]}|\bar{z}|^{k}|f_{k}^{\prime}(z)||dz|

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\int_{[z_{1},z_{2}]}k|\bar{z}|^{k-1}|f_{k}(z)||d\bar{z}|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\int_{[z_{1},z_{2}]}r^{k}\Lambda_{k}|dz|+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\int_{[z_{1},z_{2}]}kr^{k-1}\Lambda_{k}r|d\bar{z}|

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}(k+1)\Lambda_{k}r^{k}\right)|z_{1}-z_{2}|.

(3.4)

Therefore, we have

|F(z_{1})-F(z_{2})|\geq\left(\frac{\Lambda_{0}(1-\Lambda_{0}r)}{\Lambda_{0}-r}-\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}(k+1)\Lambda_{k}r^{k}\right)|z_{1}-z_{2}|.

For
r\in[0,1]
, consider the following continuous function

\psi_{2}(r)=\frac{\Lambda_{0}(1-\Lambda_{0}r)}{\Lambda_{0}-r}-\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}(k+1)\Lambda_{k}r^{k}.

Similar to the proof of Lemma
2.3
, we can verify that
\psi_{2}(r)
is a strictly decreasing function in
[0,1]
. Therefore, there exists unique real number
r_{3}\in(0,1)
such that
\psi_{2}(r_{3})=0
. Thus, we have

|F(z_{1})-F(z_{2})|\geq\psi_{2}(r)>\psi_{2}(r_{3})=0.

This implies
F(z_{1})\neq F(z_{2}),
which proves the univalence of
F
in the disc
{\mathbb{D}}_{r_{3}}
.

Next, for any
z\in\partial{\mathbb{D}}_{r_{3}}
by Lemma
2.1
and equation (
3.2
), we have

\displaystyle|F(z)|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\bar{z}^{k}f_{k}(z)\right|

\displaystyle\geq|f_{0}(z)|-\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}|\bar{z}|^{k}|f_{k}(z)|

\displaystyle\geq\Lambda_{0}^{2}r_{3}+(\Lambda_{0}^{3}-\Lambda_{0})\ln\left(1-\frac{r_{3}}{\Lambda_{0}}\right)-\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}r_{3}^{k+1}\Lambda_{k}=R_{3}.

We can easily see that the quantity
R_{3}
is strictly positive. This shows that
F({\mathbb{D}}_{r_{3}})
contains a Schlicht disc
{\mathbb{D}}_{R_{3}}
.

Now, to prove the sharpness of
r_{3}
and
R_{3}
, we consider a poly-analytic function
F_{0}(z)
given by (
3.3
). It is easy to see that
F_{0}(z)
satisfies all the conditions of the Theorem
3.3
. Hence
F_{0}(z)
is univalent in
{\mathbb{D}}_{r_{3}}
and
F_{0}({\mathbb{D}}_{r_{3}})\supseteq{\mathbb{D}}_{R_{3}}
.

To prove the univalent radius
r_{3}
is sharp, we need to prove that
F_{0}(z)
is not univalent in
{\mathbb{D}}_{r}
for each
r\in(r_{3},1]
. Indeed, let

g(x)=\Lambda_{0}^{2}x+(\Lambda_{0}^{3}-\Lambda_{0})\ln\left(1-\frac{x}{\Lambda_{0}}\right)-\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}x^{k+1}\Lambda_{k},\quad x\in[0,1].

Since, the continuous function

g^{\prime}(x)=\frac{\Lambda_{0}(1-\Lambda_{0}x)}{\Lambda_{0}-x}-\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}(k+1)\Lambda_{k}x^{k}=\psi_{2}(x)

is strictly decreasing on
[0,1]
and
g^{\prime}(r_{3})=\psi_{2}(r_{3})=0
, we obtain that
g^{\prime}(x)=0
if, and only if,
x=r_{3}.
Therefore,
g(x)
is strictly increasing on
[0,r_{3}]
and strictly decreasing on
[r_{3},1]
. We see that if
g(1)\leq 0
, then there is unique real
r^{\prime}\in(r_{3},1]
such that
g(r^{\prime})=0.

For every fixed
r\in(r_{3},1]
, we choose

\varepsilon=\begin{cases}\min\left\{\dfrac{r-r_{3}}{2},\dfrac{r^{\prime}-r_{3}}{2}\right\},&\text{ if }g(1)\leq 0,\\
\dfrac{r-r_{3}}{2},&\text{ if }g(1)>0.\end{cases}

For such
\varepsilon
, we let
x_{1}=r_{3}+\varepsilon
and there exists unique
\delta\in(0,r_{3})
such that
x_{2}:=r_{3}-\delta\in(0,r_{3})
and
g(x_{1})=g(x_{2}).
By setting
z_{1}=x_{1}
and
z_{2}=x_{2}
, we see that
z_{2},z_{2}\in{\mathbb{D}}_{r}
with
z_{2}\neq z_{2}
and

F_{0}(z_{1})=F_{0}(x_{1})=g(x_{1})=g(x_{2})=F_{0}(x_{2})=F_{0}(z_{2}).

This implies that
F_{0}
is not univalent in
{\mathbb{D}}_{r}
for each
r\in(r_{3},1]
, which proves the sharpness of
r_{3}
.

Finally, to prove the sharpness of
R_{3}
, we take
z^{\prime}=r_{3}\in\partial{\mathbb{D}}_{r_{3}}
, then we have

|F_{0}(z^{\prime})-F_{0}(0)|=|F_{0}(r_{3})|=g(r_{3})=R_{3}.

Hence, the radius
R_{3}
of the schlicht disc is also sharp. This completes the proof.
∎

4.
The Bi-Lipschitz Theroems for Some Poly-analytic functions

In this section, we investigate the Lipschitz character of subclasses
\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2}
and
\mathcal{F}_{3}
of poly-analytic functions on their univalent discs.

Theorem 4.1
.

Suppose
F(z)
satisfies the hypothesis of Theorem
3.1
. Then for each
\rho_{1}\in(0,r_{1})
, the poly-analytic function
F(z)
is bi-Lipschitz on
\overline{{\mathbb{D}}}_{\rho_{1}}
, where
r_{1}
is given by Theorem
3.1
.

Proof.

Fix
\rho_{1}\in(0,r_{1})
and let

l_{1}=\frac{\Lambda(1-\Lambda\rho_{1})}{\Lambda-\rho_{1}}-\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\rho_{1}^{k}\left(\frac{M_{k}}{1-\rho_{1}^{2}}+kM_{k}\right).

Then for any
z_{1},z_{2}\in\overline{{\mathbb{D}}}_{\rho_{1}}
, it follows from the proof of Theorem
3.1
and
|f_{0}^{\prime}(z)|<\Lambda
for
z\in{\mathbb{D}}
that
l_{1}>0
and

\displaystyle l_{1}|z_{1}-z_{2}|

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\Lambda(1-\Lambda\rho_{1})}{\Lambda-\rho_{1}}-\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\rho_{1}^{k}\left(\frac{M_{k}}{1-\rho_{1}^{2}}+kM_{k}\right)\right)|z_{1}-z_{2}|

\displaystyle\leq|F(z_{1})-F(z_{2})|

\displaystyle\leq|f_{0}(z_{1})-f_{0}(z_{2})|+\left|\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\bar{z}_{1}^{k}f_{k}(z_{1})-\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\bar{z}_{2}^{k}f_{k}(z_{2})\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\int_{[z_{1},z_{2}]}f_{0}^{\prime}(z)dz\right|+|z_{1}-z_{2}|\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\rho_{1}^{k}\left(\frac{M_{k}}{1-\rho_{1}^{2}}+kM_{k}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\Lambda+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\rho_{1}^{k}\left(\frac{M_{k}}{1-\rho_{1}^{2}}+kM_{k}\right)\right)|z_{1}-z_{2}|.

Hence
F(z)
is bi-Lipschitz on
{\mathbb{D}}_{\rho_{1}}
.
∎

Theorem 4.2
.

Suppose
F(z)
satisfies the hypothesis of Theorem
3.2
. Then for each
\rho_{2}\in(0,r_{2})
, the poly-analytic function
F(z)
is bi-Lipschitz on
\overline{{\mathbb{D}}}_{\rho_{2}}
, i.e., for any
z_{1},z_{2}\in\overline{{\mathbb{D}}}_{\rho_{2}}
, there exist

l_{2}=1-\left(M-\frac{1}{M}\right)\frac{2\rho_{2}-\rho_{2}^{2}}{(1-\rho_{2})^{2}}-\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}(k+1)\rho_{2}^{k}\Lambda_{k}>0

and

L_{2}=\frac{M}{1-\rho_{2}^{2}}+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\rho_{2}^{k}(k+1)\Lambda_{k}

such that

l_{2}|z_{1}-z_{2}|\leq|F(z_{1})-F(z_{2})|\leq L_{2}|z_{1}-z_{2}|,

where
r_{2}
is given by Theorem
3.2
.

Proof.

Fix
\rho_{2}\in(0,r_{2})
. Then for any
z_{1},z_{2}\in\overline{{\mathbb{D}}}_{\rho_{2}}
, it follows from the proof of Theorem
3.2
that
l_{2}>0
and

\displaystyle l_{2}|z_{1}-z_{2}|

\displaystyle\leq|F(z_{1})-F(z_{2})|\vskip 5.69054pt

\displaystyle\leq|f_{0}(z_{1})-f_{0}(z_{2})|+\left|\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\bar{z}_{1}^{k}f_{k}(z_{1})-\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\bar{z}_{2}^{k}f_{k}(z_{2})\right|\vskip 5.69054pt

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{[z_{1},z_{2}]}f_{0}^{\prime}(z)dz\right|+\left|\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\bar{z}_{1}^{k}f_{k}(z_{1})-\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\bar{z}_{2}^{k}f_{k}(z_{2})\right|

Now since
|f_{0}(z)|<M
and
|f_{k}^{\prime}(z)|\leq\Lambda_{k}
, by Theorem
F
and (
3.4
) we have

\displaystyle l_{2}|z_{1}-z_{2}|

\displaystyle\leq|F(z_{1})-F(z_{2})|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{M}{1-\rho_{2}^{2}}+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\rho_{2}^{k}(k+1)\Lambda_{k}\right)|z_{1}-z_{2}|=L_{2}|z_{1}-z_{2}|.

This completes the proof.

∎

Theorem 4.3
.

Suppose
F(z)
satisfies the hypothesis of Theorem
3.3
. Then for each
\rho_{3}\in(0,r_{3})
, the poly-analytic function
F(z)
is bi-Lipschitz on
\overline{{\mathbb{D}}}_{\rho_{3}}
, i.e., for any
z_{1},z_{2}\in\overline{{\mathbb{D}}}_{\rho_{3}}
, there exist

l_{3}=\frac{\Lambda_{0}(1-\Lambda_{0}\rho_{3})}{\Lambda_{0}-\rho_{3}}-\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}(k+1)\Lambda_{k}\rho_{3}^{k}>0

and

L_{3}=\Lambda+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\rho_{3}^{k}(k+1)\Lambda_{k}

such that

l_{3}|z_{1}-z_{2}|\leq|F(z_{1})-F(z_{2})|\leq L_{3}|z_{1}-z_{2}|,

where
r_{3}
is given by Theorem
3.3
.

Proof.

Fix
\rho_{3}\in(0,r_{3})
. Then for any
z_{1},z_{2}\in\overline{{\mathbb{D}}}_{\rho_{3}}
, it follows from the proof of Theorem
3.3
and
|f_{0}^{\prime}(z)|<\Lambda_{0}
for
z\in{\mathbb{D}}
that
l_{3}>0
and

\displaystyle l_{3}|z_{1}-z_{2}|

\displaystyle\leq|F(z_{1})-F(z_{2})|

\displaystyle\leq|f_{0}(z_{1})-f_{0}(z_{2})|+\left|\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\bar{z}_{1}^{k}f_{k}(z_{1})-\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\bar{z}_{2}^{k}f_{k}(z_{2})\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\int_{[z_{1},z_{2}]}f_{0}^{\prime}(z)dz\right|+|z_{1}-z_{2}|\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\rho_{3}^{k}(k+1)\Lambda_{k}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\Lambda+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\rho_{3}^{k}(k+1)\Lambda_{k}\right)|z_{1}-z_{2}|=L_{3}|z_{1}-z_{2}|.

This completes the proof.

∎

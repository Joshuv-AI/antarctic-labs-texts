1.
An overview, preliminaries and basic set-up

Given non-negative integers
d
,
g
and
r\geq 3
, let
\mathcal{H}_{d,g,r}
be the Hilbert scheme of smooth projective curves,
which is the union of components whose general point corresponds to a smooth irreducible and non-degenerate curve of degree
d
and genus
g
in
\mathbb{P}^{r}
.
Denoting the moduli space of smooth curves of genus
g
by
\cal{M}_{g}
,
let

\mu:\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{}}_{d,g,r}\dasharrow\cal{M}_{g}

be the natural rational map - which we call the
moduli map
- sending
X\in\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{}}_{d,g,r}
to its isomorphism class

\mu(X)=[X]\in\mathcal{M}_{g}
.

In this paper, we are interested in the relationship between

\operatorname{codim}_{\cal{M}_{g}}\mu(\cal{H})
and
\dim\cal{H}
. For any irreducible component
\cal{H}\subset\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{}}_{d,g,r}
, it is known that

\dim\cal{H}\geq\cal{X}(d,g,r):=\dim\cal{M}_{g}+\rho(d,g,r)+\dim\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{P}^{r}).

(1)

In the
Brill-Noether range
, i.e. when

\rho(d,g,r):=g-(r+1)(g-d+r)\geq 0,

there exits a
unique
component

\cal{H}_{0}\subset\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{}}_{d,g,r}

dominating
\cal{M}_{g}

[
13
, Theorem 19.9, page 348]
. Furthermore, this component
\cal{H}_{0}
has the
expected dimension

\dim\cal{H}_{0}=\cal{X}(d,g,r).

On the other hand, still under the assumption
\rho(d,g,r)\geq 0
, it is not totally clear
if
\cal{H}_{0}
is the only component having the minimal possible (and expected) dimension. A priori there may exist a component
\cal{H}\subset\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{}}_{d,g,r}

with
\dim\cal{H}=\cal{X}(d,g,r)
which does not dominate
\cal{M}_{g}
. If this occurs, this would lead to the reducibility of
\cal{H}_{d,g,r}
as was seen in
[
18
]
.
At this point, one may raise the following naive but natural questions.

(A)

Suppose
\rho(d,g,r)\geq 0
. Identify all (or as many as possible) the components
\cal{H}\subset\cal{H}_{d,g,r}
with
\dim\cal{H}=\cal{X}(d,g,r)
.

(B)

Without the assumption
\rho(d,g,r)\geq 0
, is it possible
to make an estimate (or upper bound) for

\operatorname{codim}_{\cal{M}_{g}}\mu{(\cal{H})}
– hopefully linearly in
g
–
such that
\dim\cal{H}=\cal{X}(d,g,r)
?

Regarding question (A), it is merely known that there exists a component
\cal{H}\subset\cal{H}_{d,g,r}
other than
\cal{H}_{0}
with
\dim\cal{H}=\cal{X}(d,g,r)
; cf.
[
18
, Theorem 3.4]
,
[
7
, Propositions 3.3 & 3.5]
or Example
2.1
below. We will reproduce one typical type of this kind in
Example
2.1
.
However, all such known examples occur as a component whose general elements are
non-linearly normal
curves under the
assumption
\rho(d,g,r)\geq 0
. We will be more specific with this phenomena in the next section; Question
2.3
.

Outside the Brill-Noether range
\rho(d,g,r)<0
, no component
\cal{H}\subset\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{}}_{d,g,r}
dominates
\cal{M}_{g}
by the
Brill-Noether theorem.
However, one may still ask
under what numerical or geometrical condition, does there exist a component
\cal{H}

with
\dim\cal{H}=\cal{X}(d,g,r)
?

Loosely speaking, one expects that if
\operatorname{codim}_{\cal{M}_{g}}\mu(\cal{H})
gets smaller, curves in
\cal{H}
tend
to be nearer to a general curve and therefore it is likely that they would behave like curves
with general moduli. Thus it is natural to expect that
the estimate
\dim\cal{H}=\cal{X}(d,g,r)
may continue
to hold for a component consisting curves that are not too special.
Along the line of these thoughts, the following has been suggested
[
12
, Conjecture, pp142–143]
.

(C)

Find a function
\beta(g)
(probably linear in
g
), which ensures the existence of a component

\cal{H}\subset\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{}}_{d,g,r}
of the expected dimension in case

\operatorname{codim}_{\cal{M}_{g}}\mu(\cal{H})\leq\beta(g).

Specifically one finds the following updated conjecture which has appeared recently
[
13
, Conjecture 19.6, page 344]
.

Conjecture 1.1
.

If
\operatorname{codim}_{\cal{M}_{g}}\mu(\cal{H})\leq\beta(g)=g-5
, then
\dim\cal{H}=\cal{X}(d,g,r)
.

\blacklozenge

In this article, we exhibit several examples of triples
(d,g,r)
such that Conjecture
1.1

is no longer valid; Examples
2.7
,
2.12
. We also make a further discussion for the study of the moduli map along this line; Remark
2.8
.

\blacklozenge
We study a related problem regarding the existence of a component having less than the expected number of moduli; Example
3.1
, Remark
3.3
, Question
3.4

We say that a component
\cal{H}\subset\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{}}_{d,g,r}
has the expected number of moduli
if

\dim\mu(\cal{H})=\min\{3g-3,3g-3+\rho(d,g,r)\}.

under the condition

\lambda(d,g,r):=3g-3+\rho(d,g,r)\geq 0.

When
\rho(d,g,r)\geq 0
, the principal component
\cal{H}_{0}
is
the only
component having the
expected number of moduli. Other components (if any) have
less
than the expected number of moduli.
Thus, we are interested in the existence of a component
with less than the expected number of moduli
only when

\rho(d,g,r)<0
, i.e. outside the Brill-Noether range.
We exhibit a class of curves forming a component of a Hilbert scheme with less than the expected number of moduli
in the final section.

2.
Preliminaries, Notations and Some examples

2.1.
Preliminaries, Notations and Conventions

For notation and conventions, we follow those in
[
2
]
and
[
3
]
; e.g.
\pi(d,r)
is the maximal possible arithmetic genus of an irreducible, non-degenerate and reduced curve of degree
d
in
\mathbb{P}^{r}
.
Following classical terminology, a linear series of degree
d
and dimension
r
on a smooth curve is denoted by
g^{r}_{d}
.
A linear series
\cal{D}=g^{r}_{d}
(
r\geq 2
) on a smooth curve
C
is called
birationally very ample
when the morphism

C\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{r}
induced by the moving part of
\cal{D}
is birational onto its image.
A linear series
g^{r}_{d}
is said to be
compounded
if the morphism induced by
g^{r}_{d}
gives rise to a non-trivial covering map
C\rightarrow C^{\prime}
of degree
k\geq 2
. Given a smooth non-degenerate curve
C\subset\mathbb{P}^{r}
,
the
residual curve of
C
– sometimes denoted by
C^{\vee}
– is the image curve of the morphism induced by the moving part of
|K_{C}(-1)|
. For a complete linear series
\mathcal{D}
on a smooth curve
C
, the residual series
|K_{C}-\mathcal{D}|
is denoted by
\mathcal{D}^{\vee}
.

We use the following basic frameworks of our study borrowed from
[
3
]
and
[
1
, §1 & §2]
. Given an isomorphism class
[C]\in\mathcal{M}_{g}
corresponding to a smooth irreducible curve
C
, there exist a neighborhood
U\subset\mathcal{M}_{g}
of the class
[C]
and a smooth connected variety
\mathcal{M}
which is a finite ramified covering
h:\mathcal{M}\xrightarrow{\ \ }U
, as well as varieties
\mathcal{C}
,
\mathcal{W}^{r}_{d}
&
\mathcal{G}^{r}_{d}
proper over
\mathcal{M}
with the following properties:

(1)

\xi:\mathcal{C}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\mathcal{M}
is a universal curve, i.e. for every
p\in\mathcal{M}
,
\xi^{-1}(p)
is a smooth curve of genus
g
whose isomorphism class is
h(p)
,

(2)

\mathcal{W}^{r}_{d}
parametrizes the pairs
(p,L)
where
L
is a line bundle of degree
d
and
h^{0}(L)\geq r+1
on
\xi^{-1}(p)
,

(3)

\mathcal{G}^{r}_{d}
parametrizes the pairs
(p,\mathcal{D})
, where
\mathcal{D}
is possibly an incomplete
g^{r}_{d}
on
\xi^{-1}(p)
.

Let
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}
(
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}_{\mathcal{L}}
, resp.) be the union of components of
\mathcal{G}^{r}_{d}
whose general element
(p,\mathcal{D})
corresponds to a very ample (very ample and complete, resp.) linear series
\mathcal{D}
on the curve
C=\xi^{-1}(p)
. Recalling that an open subset of
\mathcal{H}_{d,g,r}
consisting of smooth irreducible and non-degenerate curves is a
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{P}^{r})
-bundle over an open subset of
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}
, the irreducibility of
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}
guarantees the irreducibility of
\mathcal{H}_{d,g,r}
. Likewise, the irreducibility of
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}_{\mathcal{L}}
ensures the irreducibility of

\mathcal{H}_{d,g,r}^{\mathcal{L}}
, the union of components of
\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{}}_{d,g,r}
whose general element is linearly normal.

Given an irreducible family
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{G}^{r}_{d}
whose general member is complete, the closure of the family
\{\mathcal{D}^{\vee}|\mathcal{D}\in\mathcal{F},~\mathcal{D}\textrm{ is complete}\}\subset\mathcal{W}^{g-d+r-1}_{2g-2-d}
is sometimes denoted by
\mathcal{F}^{\vee}
.
Given
r\geq s
,
\mathbb{G}(s,r)
denotes the Grassmannian of dimension
s
linear subspaces of
\mathbb{P}^{r}
.
Throughout we work
over the field of complex numbers.

2.2.
Some old and new Examples

We start with the following example of a
reducible
Hilbert scheme with
\rho(d,g,r)\geq 0
, which is a simplified version of
[
18
, Theorem 2.3]
. This example been known for some years, which we choose to present here with a brief outline in order to provide readers a motivation and a flavor with an emphasis on what we shall discuss in this paper.

Example 2.1
.

(i) If
11\leq g\leq 16
,
\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{}}_{2g-8,g,g-8}
is non-empty and irreducible whose general element is linearly normal.

(ii) If
g\geq 17
, there exist an extra component
\cal{H}\subset\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{}}_{2g-8,g,g-8}
,
\cal{H}\neq\cal{H}_{0}
with

\operatorname{codim}_{\cal{M}_{g}}\mu{(\cal{H})}=g-4

\dim\cal{H}=\begin{cases}\cal{X}(2g-8,g,g-8)\hskip 51.0pt\text{ if }g=17\\
\cal{X}(2g-8,g,g-8)+(g-17),\text{ if }g>17.\end{cases}

A general element of
\cal{H}
is a trigonal curve, which is
not linearly normal
in
\mathbb{P}^{g-8}
.

Proof.

Since
\rho(2g-8,g,g-8)\geq 0
, there is a unique component
\cal{H}_{0}
dominating
\cal{M}_{g}
. Assume the existence of a
component
\cal{H}\neq\cal{H}_{0}
. Recall that
\cal{H}
consists of curves
C
such that
\cal{O}_{C}(1)
is special
[
14
, Chapter 2]
,
[
21
, Proposition 1.1]
.

Take a general
C\in\cal{H}
with the hyperplane series
\cal{D}=g^{g-8}_{2g-8}\subset|\cal{O}_{C}(1)|
. Set
\alpha=h^{1}(C,\cal{O}_{C}(1))>0
. By Clifford’s theorem,
1\leq\alpha\leq 4
. If
\alpha=4
,
|K_{C}(-1)|=g^{3}_{6}
and
C
is a hyperelliptic curve, which does carry a birationally very ample special linear series. If
1\leq\alpha\leq 2
, then by H. Martens’ theorem for the Brill-Noether locus
W^{\alpha-1}_{6}(C)
consisting of special linear series on a fixed curve
[
13
, Theorem 5.16]
and then passing to the residual series’

\displaystyle\dim\cal{H}

\displaystyle\leq\dim\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{P}^{g-8})+\dim\mathbb{G}(g-8,g+\alpha-8)+\dim W^{g+\alpha-8}_{2g-8}(C)+\dim\mu(\cal{H})

\displaystyle\leq\dim\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{P}^{g-8})+\dim\mathbb{G}(g-8,g+\alpha-8)+\dim W^{\alpha-1}_{6}(C)+3g-4

\displaystyle\leq\dim\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{P}^{g-8})+\dim\mathbb{G}(g-8,g+\alpha-8)+(6-2(\alpha-1))+3g-4

\displaystyle<\dim\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{P}^{g-8})+\lambda(2g-8,g,g-8)=\cal{X}(2g-8,g,g-8),

contradicting (
1
) if
g\geq 11
.
Thus we are left with
\alpha=3
,
\cal{E}:=|K_{C}(-1)|=g^{2}_{6}
and
\cal{E}
is compounded (since
g\geq 11
) inducing
\eta:C\xrightarrow{\ \ }E\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}
.
C
is not
bielliptic; if so, then
|K_{C}-\eta^{*}(g^{2}_{3})|
is not very ample; cf. Lemma
2.6
.
Thus
C
is trigonal,

\cal{E}=2g^{1}_{3}
and the hyperplane series

\cal{D}\subset|K_{C}(-2g^{1}_{3})|=g^{g-5}_{2g-8}

is a (incomplete) subseries of the unique very ample
g^{g-5}_{2g-8}=|K_{C}(-2g^{1}_{3})|
.
It follows that there is a component
\cal{H}\neq\cal{H}_{0}
over
\cal{G}\subset\cal{G}^{g-8}_{2g-8}
consisting of incomplete very ample subseries’
g^{g-8}_{2g-8}\subset|K_{C}(-2g^{1}_{3})|
on a trigonal curve
C\in\cal{M}^{1}_{g,3}
.
Thus
\cal{H}
dominates the irreducible locus
\cal{M}^{1}_{g,3}\subset\cal{M}_{g}
consisting of trigonal curves and

\displaystyle\dim\cal{H}

\displaystyle=\dim\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{P}^{g-8})+\dim\mathbb{G}(g-8,g-5)+\dim\cal{M}^{1}_{g,3}

\displaystyle=\dim\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{P}^{g-8})+3(g-7)+2g+1

\displaystyle\geq\cal{X}(2g-8,g,g-8)=\dim\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{P}^{g-8})+4g-3

if
g\geq 17
.
Therefore, we conclude

(i)

\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{}}_{2g-8,g,g-8}
is non-empty and irreducible with the only component
\cal{H}_{0}
(the principal component) if
11\leq g\leq 16
. A general element is non-special and
complete;
[
17
, Ch. IV, Proposition 6.1]
.

(ii)

\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{}}_{2g-8,g,g-8}
is reducible with two components if
g\geq 17
; the only extra component
\cal{H}
dominates the locus

\cal{M}^{1}_{g,3}
of trigonal curves and hence

\operatorname{codim}_{\cal{M}_{g}}\mu(\cal{H})=g-4.

Every member
C\in\cal{H}
is a
non linearly normal
curve &

\dim\cal{H}=\begin{cases}\cal{X}(2g-8,g,g-8)\hskip 51.0pt\text{ if }g=17\\
\cal{X}(2g-8,g,g-8)+(g-17),\text{ if }g>17.\end{cases}

∎

Lemma 2.6
.

(i)

Let
C\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eta}}{{\rightarrow}}E
be a double covering of a curve
E
of genus
h\geq 1
. Let
\mathcal{E}=g^{s}_{e}
be a non-special linear series on
E
. Assume that
|\eta^{*}(g^{s}_{e})|=g^{s}_{2e}
. Then the base-point-free part of the complete

|K_{C}(-\eta^{*}(g^{s}_{e}))|
is compounded.

(ii)

Let
C
be a trigonal curve of genus
g\geq 5
. Suppose
\dim|2g^{1}_{3}+\Delta|=2

for an effective divisor
\Delta
with
\deg\Delta>0
. Then
|K_{C}-2g^{1}_{3}-\Delta|
is not very ample.

Proof.

(i) Note that for any
p\in E
,
|g^{s}_{e}+p|=g^{s+1}_{e+1}
since
g^{s}_{e}
is non-special,

3.

The notion of a component of the Hilbert scheme with the expected number of moduli was first introduced by Sernesi in

where he identified extensive class of triples

such that

has a component

with the expected
number of moduli, i.e.

\dim\mu(\cal{H})=3g-3+\rho(d,g,r).

There have been improvements on the study of Hilbert schemes along this line by works of A. Lopez and others; cf.
[
22
]
,
[
23
]
and references therein. Needless to say, there are many Hilbert schemes with components larger than the expected number of moduli. However, as were indicated in
[

[

,

to the people working on this area and

the number of moduli

We exhibit some examples of Hilbert schemes with components having the number of moduli less than expected,
mainly based on the discussion in previous sections.

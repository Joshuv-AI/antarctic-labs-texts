Weighted weak ( 1 , 1 ) (1,1) estimate for non-commutative square function
Samya Kumar Ray, Diptesh Saha

Abstract
. In this article, we consider weighted weak type ( 1 , 1 ) (1,1) inequality for certain square function associated to differences of ball averages and martingale in the non-commutative setting. This establishes a weighted version of main result of [ HX21 ] .

1.
Introduction

A fundamental problem in harmonic analysis and measure theoretic ergodic theory is to study weak and strong type inequalities of various operators. Though it is often motivated by the question of pointwise convergence of different averages, it has also other wide range of applications. A rather quantitative approach for these kinds of problems is to establish the so called ‘square function’ inequalities.
In this article, we study weighted versions of certain square function inequalities in the non-commutative setting motivated from ergodic theory.

Let us consider the measure space
(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathcal{B},\mu)
where
\mathcal{B}
is the Borel
\sigma
-algebra and
\mu
the translation invariant Borel measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}.
Let
n\in\mathbb{Z}.
Fix the
n
-th dyadic filtration
\mathcal{B}_{n}
generated by the dyadic cubes of side-length
2^{-n}
. Hence for each
n\in\mathbb{Z},
we have the associated classical conditional expectation
\mathbb{E}_{n}
.
Let
\mathcal{M}
be a semifinite von Neumann algebra with a f.n.s. trace
\tau
. Then
\mathbb{E}_{n}\otimes I_{\mathcal{M}}
is a conditional expectation on
\mathcal{N}:=L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}
which we denote by
\mathcal{E}_{n}.
Note that
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}
is again a semifinite von Neumann algebra with f.n.s. trace
\varphi:=\int_{\mathbb{R^{d}}}\otimes\tau.
Consider the non-commutative
L^{1}
-space
L^{1}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
. Now for any locally integrable function
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to L^{1}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
, we consider the averaging operator

(1.1)

M_{t}f(x)=\frac{1}{\mu(B_{t})}\int_{B_{t}}f(x+y)d\mu(y),~x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

where, for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
B_{t}
is the ball of radius
2^{-t}
with center at the origin. The non-commutative square function that we are going to consider is associated to the following sequence of operators,

(1.2)

T_{n}f(x):=(M_{n}-\mathcal{E}_{n})f(x),~x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

If we replace the von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{M}
by the set of complex numbers
\mathbb{C}
, then in this case the square function is formulated as

(1.3)

Lf(x):=\Big(\sum_{k}|(M_{k}-\mathbb{E}_{k})f(x)|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},~x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

To obtain a similar result, we need an appropriate norm in the non-commutative setup. For this purpose, one has to consider the row and column spaces. The row space (resp. column space) is defined as the closure of the finite sequences in
L^{p}(\mathcal{N})
with respect to the norm, defined as

(1.4)

\left\|(f_{k})\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathcal{N};\ell^{r}_{2})}:=\left\|\Big(\sum_{k}|f_{k}^{*}|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\right\|_{p}~\left(\text{resp.}\left\|(f_{k})\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathcal{N};\ell^{c}_{2})}:=\left\|\Big(\sum_{k}|f_{k}|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\right\|_{p}\right).

The associated row-column space is defined as the the following.

(1.5)

L^{p}(\mathcal{N};\ell^{rc}_{2}):=\begin{cases}L^{p}(\mathcal{N};\ell^{r}_{2})\cap L^{p}(\mathcal{N};\ell^{c}_{2}),~if~2\leq p\leq\infty\\
\text{with}\left\|\cdot\right\|:=\max\{\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathcal{N};\ell^{r}_{2})},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathcal{N};\ell^{c}_{2})}\}\\
\\
L^{p}(\mathcal{N};\ell^{r}_{2})+L^{p}(\mathcal{N};\ell^{c}_{2}),~if~1\leq p<2\\
\text{with }\left\|(f_{k})\right\|:=\inf\limits_{f_{k}=g_{k}+h_{k}}\{\left\|(g_{k})\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathcal{N};\ell^{c}_{2})}+\left\|(h_{k})\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathcal{N};\ell^{r}_{2})}\}\end{cases}

Similarly one can also define weak row, column, row-column spaces with natural definitions as above. In
[
HX21
]
, the authors proved the strong and weak type inequalities for non-commutative square function associated to the sequence of operators
(T_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
.

One of the most active areas of research in classical analysis is to obtain weighted analogues of various results in classical analysis. Hence, it is natural to ask for a weighted analogue of the result above. In the classical setting, this direction of research was started with the celebrated work of
[
Muc72
]
and continued further by
[

]
and many others. Moreover, the question about whether there is a linear dependence of the norm inequalities on the

-constant became the famous

-conjecture, which was finally settled by Hytönen
[
Hyt12
]
. It is due to development of operator space theory and non-commutative probability theory, non-commutative analysis have been developing in a great speed in the past few decades and many results in non-commutative harmonic analysis and ergodic theory were established in
[
JX07
]
,
[
Mei07
]
,
[
HLX23
]
,
[
HRW23
]
,
[
HLW21
]
,
[
HLRX
]
etc. Despite these remarkable development, there are not many results available in the weighted setting. Recently, in
[
GJOW22
]
, the authors have studied non-commutative Doob’s inequality in the weighted setting and also studied some singular operators associated to nice kernels. In this paper, we prove the following result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and
w
be an
A_{1}
-weight. Then there exists a constant

\begin{cases}\left\|(T_{k}f)\right\|_{L^{1,\infty}(\mathcal{N}_{w};\ell^{rc}_{2})}\leq C_{1}(w)\left\|f\right\|_{1,w},~\forall f\in L^{1}(\mathcal{N}_{w})~\text{and,}\\
\\
\left\|(T_{k}f)\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathcal{N}_{w};\ell^{rc}_{2})}\leq C_{p}(w)\left\|f\right\|_{p,w}~\forall f\in L^{p}(\mathcal{N}_{w}),~\text{when }1<p<\infty.\end{cases}

We refer section (
2

2.
Preliminaries

Throughout this article,
\mathcal{M}(\subseteq\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))
is assumed to be a separable von Neumann algebra equipped with a faithful, normal, semifinite (f.n.s.) trace
\tau
. The lattice of projections on the von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{M}
is denoted by
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M})
. By
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
, we will denote the commutant of the von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{M}
, which is also a separable von Neumann algebra defined on the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
.

2.1.
Non-commutative
L^{p}
-spaces

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a semi-finite von Neumann algebra with a f.n.s trace
\tau
. An operator (possibly unbounded)
x
, which is closed and densely defined on
\mathcal{H}
, is said to be affiliated to
\mathcal{M}
if it commutes with all unitaries
u^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
. The same operator
x
is called
\tau
-measurable if for all
\delta>0
there exists
p\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M})
such that
p\mathcal{H}\subseteq\mathcal{D}(x)
(domain of
x
) and
\tau(1-p)<\delta
. The space of all
\tau
-measurable operators, which are affiliated to
\mathcal{M}
is denoted by
L^{0}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
. It is well-known that
\tau
can be extended to the positive cone
L^{0}(\mathcal{M},\tau)_{+}
and one can define for
x\in L^{0}(\mathcal{M},\tau)

\displaystyle\left\|x\right\|_{p}:=(\tau(\left|x\right|^{p}))^{1/p},~0<p<\infty,

where
\left|x\right|:=(x^{*}x)^{\frac{1}{2}}
.
Then the non-commutative
L^{p}
space associated to
(\mathcal{M},\tau)
for
0<p<\infty
is defined as

\displaystyle L^{p}(\mathcal{M},\tau):=\{x\in L^{0}(\mathcal{M},\tau):\left\|x\right\|_{p}<\infty\},

and
L^{\infty}(\mathcal{M}):=\mathcal{M}
. Furthermore, it is a Banach space with respect to the norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{p}
when
1\leq p\leq\infty
.

On the other hand, the non-commutative weak
L^{p}
-space associated to
(\mathcal{M},\tau)
for
0<p<\infty
is defined as a subspace of
L^{0}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
such that for
x\in L^{0}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
the following quasi-norm

\displaystyle\left\|x\right\|_{p,\infty}:=\sup_{\lambda>0}\lambda\tau(\chi_{(\lambda,\infty)}(\left|x\right|))^{\frac{1}{p}}

is finite. We denote the collection as
L^{p,\infty}(\mathcal{M})
. We note that for
x_{1},x_{2}\in L^{1,\infty}(\mathcal{M})
and
\lambda>0
,

(2.1)

\tau(\chi_{(\lambda,\infty)}(\left|x_{1}+x_{2}\right|))\leq\tau(\chi_{(\lambda/2,\infty)}(\left|x_{1}\right|))+\tau(\chi_{(\lambda/2,\infty)}(\left|x_{2}\right|)).

2.2.
Non-commutative Martingales

Let
(\mathcal{M},\tau)
be a semifinite von Neumann algebra as described before. Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
be fixed and we consider the von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{N}:=L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathcal{B},\mu)\otimes\mathcal{M}
, where
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is equipped with the Lebesgue measure
\mu
and Borel
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{B}
. Clearly,
\mathcal{N}
is equipped with the f.n.s. trace
\varphi:=(\int\cdot~d\mu)\otimes\tau
. For
k,n_{1},\ldots,n_{d}\in\mathbb{Z}
consider the dyadic cube in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
;

\displaystyle[2^{k}n_{1},2^{k}(n_{1}+1))\times\cdots\times[2^{k}n_{d},2^{k}(n_{d}+1)).

By
\mathcal{Q}
, we denote the collection of all dyadic cubes in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. For any
k\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\mathcal{Q}_{k}
will denote the collection of dyadic cubes of side length
2^{-k}
. Therefore,
\left|Q\right|
, the volume of a
Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{k}
is
2^{-dk}
. Let
k\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\mathcal{B}_{k}
be the
\sigma
-algebra generated by the dyadic partition
\mathcal{Q}_{k}
. Now, for every
k\in\mathbb{Z}
we define the von Neumann subalgebra

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{k}:=L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathcal{B}_{k},\mu)\otimes\mathcal{M}

of
\mathcal{N}
. Now since
\varphi|_{\mathcal{N}_{k}}
is again semi-finite, there exists a normal conditional expectation
\mathcal{E}_{k}
which is of the form
\mathcal{E}_{k}:=\mathbb{E}_{k}(\cdot|\mathcal{B}_{k})\otimes I_{\mathcal{M}}
, where
\mathbb{E}_{k}(\cdot|\mathcal{B}_{k})
is the conditional expectation from
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathcal{B},\mu)
onto
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathcal{B}_{k},\mu)
. Therefore, for
k\in\mathbb{Z}
and
f\in L^{1}(\mathcal{N},\varphi)
we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{k}(f)=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{k}}\Big(\frac{1}{\left|Q\right|}\int_{Q}f\Big)\chi_{Q}.

Moreover, observe that
(\mathcal{N}_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is an increasing family of subalgebras of
\mathcal{N}
. Hence, for every
f\in L^{1}(\mathcal{N},\varphi)
, the sequence
(\mathcal{E}_{k}(f))_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
forms a non-commutative martingale, that is

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{j}(\mathcal{E}_{k}(f))=\mathcal{E}_{j}(f),~\text{for }j\leq k.

2.3.
Martingale weights and weighted
L^{p}
spaces

Consider the measure space
(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathcal{B},\mu)
as described above. A weight
w
is a positive, integrable function on
(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathcal{B},\mu)
. For any
C\in\mathcal{B}
, we will write
w(C)=\int_{C}wd\mu
. For
1<p<\infty
, the weight
w
is said to satisfy the Muckenhoupt’s
A_{p}
condition if

\displaystyle[w]_{A_{p}}:=\sup_{Q}\frac{\int_{Q}wd\mu}{\mu(Q)}\Big(\frac{\int_{Q}w^{1/(1-p)}d\mu}{\mu(Q)}\Big)^{p-1}<\infty,

and
w
is said to satisfy the Muckenhoupt’s
A_{1}
condition if

[w]_{A_{1}}:=\sup_{Q}\mathrm{esssup}_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\int_{Q}wd\mu/\mu(Q)}{w(x)}<\infty,

where in both cases the supremum is taken over all cubes
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
with sides parallel to axes. For a measurable function
f
on
(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathcal{B},\mu)
define the Hardy-Littlewood maximal function as

\displaystyle Mf(x):=\sup_{Q}\frac{1}{\mu(Q)}\int_{Q}\left|f(y)\right|d\mu(y);~x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

(where the supremum is taken over all cubes
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
with sides parallel to axes containing
x
)
and recall the following theorem (see
[
Duo24
]
).

Theorem 2.1
.

For
1\leq p<\infty
, the weak type
(p,p)
inequality

\displaystyle w\Big(\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:Mf(x)>\lambda\}\Big)\leq\frac{C}{\lambda^{p}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|f(y)\right|^{p}w(y)d\mu(y)

is true if and only if
w
satisfies the Muckenhoupt’s
A_{p}
condition.

Then, it follows from Theorem
2.1
that for any
S,Q\in\mathcal{B}
with
S\subset Q
and
\int_{Q}fd\mu>0

(2.2)

\frac{w(Q)}{\mu(Q)}\leq[w]_{A_{1}}\frac{w(S)}{\mu(S)}

and

\frac{w(Q)}{\mu(Q)}\leq[w]_{A_{1}}\inf_{x\in Q}w(x).

Furthermore, if
w
satisfies Muckenhoupt’s
A_{1}
condition, then there exists
0<\delta<1
(depending on
[w]_{A_{1}}
) and a constant
C(w)>0
such that for all
S\subseteq Q

\frac{w(S)}{w(Q)}\leq C(w)\left(\frac{\mu(S)}{\mu(Q)}\right)^{\delta}.

The above properties can be found in
[
Duo24
]
. In this article, we will consider weights of the form
w\otimes I_{\mathcal{M}}
, where
w
is a classical weight as defined above. Observe that
w\otimes I_{\mathcal{M}}
commutes with all elements of
\mathcal{N}
and we say
w\otimes I_{\mathcal{M}}
satisfy Muckenhoupt’s
A_{1}
condition if
w
satisfies the same. Furthermore, we define
[w\otimes I_{\mathcal{M}}]_{A_{1}}:=[w]_{A_{1}}
. Therefore, from now on we will only use the notation
w
for such weights without any ambiguity. For such an weight
w
, let us recall that the weighted non-commutative
L^{p}
-space (
1\leq p<\infty
) is defined as

\displaystyle L^{p}_{w}(\mathcal{N}):=\{x\in L^{0}(\mathcal{N},\varphi):xw^{1/p}\in L^{p}(\mathcal{N},\varphi)\}

In other words, if we define the weighted f.n.s. trace
\varphi_{w}(\cdot):=\varphi(\cdot w)
on the von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{N}
, then it is eminent that
L^{p}_{w}(\mathcal{N})
is the non-commutative
L^{p}
-space on
(\mathcal{N},\varphi_{w})
. In the sequel we will sometime write
\mathcal{N}_{w}
to emphasise that the von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{N}
is considered along with the trace
\varphi_{w}
. The associated Banach space norm in
L^{p}_{w}(\mathcal{N})
will be denoted by
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{p,w}
.

2.4.
Non-commutative Calderón-Zygmund decomposition

Let
f\in L^{1}(\mathcal{N},\varphi_{w})\cap\mathcal{N}
be positive and
x\mapsto\|f(x)\|_{1}
is compactly supported. More precisely, observe that the set

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{c,+}:=L^{1}(\mathcal{N},\varphi_{w})\cap\{f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathcal{M}:f\in\mathcal{N}_{+},~\overrightarrow{\text{supp}}f\text{ is compact }\}

is a dense subset of
L^{1}(\mathcal{N},\varphi_{w})_{+}
, where
\overrightarrow{\text{supp}}f
is defined as the support of the map
x\mapsto\|f(x)\|_{1}
. From now on, we confine ourselves to the above dense set to obtain the desired estimates. Furthermore, it is well-known that for all
f\in\mathcal{N}_{c,+}
and
\lambda>0
, there exists
m_{\lambda}(f)\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
\mathcal{E}_{k}(f)\leq\lambda 1_{\mathcal{N}}
for all
k\leq m_{\lambda}(f)
. In the following, we will fix
f\in\mathcal{N}_{c,+}
and
\lambda>0
and assume without loss of generality
m_{\lambda}(f)=0
.

Consider the von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{N}
and the weight
w
satisfying Mukenhopt’s
A_{1}
condition. We further recall the dyadic filtration
\{\mathcal{N}_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
as described above and consider the martingale
\{\mathcal{E}_{n}(f)\}_{n\geq 0}
. Then by Cuculescu’s construction (
[
Cuc71
]
), there exists a decreasing sequence of projections
(q_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
in
\mathcal{N}
, with
q_{n}=1
for all
n\leq 0
and for
n>0

\displaystyle q_{n}:=\chi_{(0,\lambda]}(q_{n-1}\mathcal{E}_{n}(f)q_{n-1})

satisfying

(i)

q_{n}\mathcal{E}_{n}(f)q_{n}\leq\lambda q_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

(ii)

q_{n}
commutes with
q_{n-1}\mathcal{E}_{n}(f)q_{n-1}
,

(iii)

suppose
q=\wedge_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}q_{n}
, then

\displaystyle qfq\leq\lambda q,~\varphi(1-q)\leq\frac{\left\|f\right\|_{1}}{\lambda}.

Furthermore, write
p_{n}=q_{n-1}-q_{n}
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and observe that
p_{n}
’s are disjoint projections in
\mathcal{N}
and

(2.3)

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}p_{n}=1-q.

Then, by the Calderón- Zygmund decomposition (cf.
[
CCAP22
]
)
f
can be decomposed as

(2.4)

f=g+b_{d}+b_{off},

where, denoting
f_{j}:=\mathcal{E}_{j}(f)
we have

(1)

g=qfq+\sum_{j\geq 1}p_{j}f_{j}p_{j}
,

(2)

b_{d}=\sum_{j\geq 1}p_{j}(f-f_{j})p_{j}
,

(3)

b_{off}=\sum_{j\geq 1}p_{j}(f-f_{j})q_{j}+q_{j}(f-f_{j})p_{j}
.

The following proposition is proved in
[
CCAP22
]
and also crucial for our purpose.

Proposition 2.2
.

The following are true.

(1)

\left\|g\right\|_{1}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{1}
and
\left\|g\right\|_{\infty}\lesssim\lambda
.

(2)

\sum_{j\geq 1}\left\|p_{j}(f-f_{j})p_{j}\right\|_{1}\leq 2\left\|f\right\|_{1}
and

\mathcal{E}_{j}(p_{j}(f-f_{j})p_{j})=\mathcal{E}_{j}(p_{j}(f-f_{j})q_{j}+q_{j}(f-f_{j})p_{j})=0
.

For
k\in\mathbb{Z}
,
Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{k}
and
x\in Q
, define
p_{Q}:=p_{k}(x)
and

(2.5)

\zeta:=\Big(\vee_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}p_{Q}\chi_{5Q}\Big)^{\perp}.

We recall the following theorem.

Theorem 2.3
.

[
GJOW22
]

Let
w
be a weight satisfying the Muckenhoupt’s
A_{1}
condition and
f\in L^{1}_{w}(\mathcal{N})
. Then for any
\lambda>0
we have
q\mathcal{E}_{n}(f)q\leq\lambda
for all
n
and

\displaystyle\lambda\varphi_{w}(1-q)\leq[w]_{A_{1}}\left\|f\right\|_{1,w}.

Now we prove the following important lemma.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
w
be an
A_{1}
-weight,
f\in L^{1}_{w}(\mathcal{N})_{+}
and
\lambda>0
. Then we have

(2.6)

\varphi_{w}(1-\zeta)\lesssim[w]_{A_{1}}^{2}\frac{\left\|f\right\|_{1,w}}{\lambda}.

Proof.

Observe that

\displaystyle\varphi_{w}(1-\zeta)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}\varphi_{w}(p_{Q}\chi_{5Q})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}[w]_{A_{1}}\frac{\left|5Q\right|}{\left|Q\right|}\varphi_{w}(p_{Q}\chi_{Q})~(\text{by eq }\ref{weight prop})

\displaystyle\lesssim[w]_{A_{1}}\sum_{j\geq 1}\varphi_{w}(p_{j}),\text{ since }\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}p_{Q}\chi_{Q}=\sum_{j\geq 1}p_{j}

\displaystyle=[w]_{A_{1}}\varphi_{w}(1-q)

\displaystyle\lesssim[w]_{A_{1}}^{2}\frac{\left\|f\right\|_{1,w}}{\lambda}~(\text{by Theorem }\ref{galkazka's estimate}).

This completes the proof of the lemma.

3.
Weak type
(1,1)
estimate

This section is devoted to the weak type
(1,1)
estimate of the non-commutative square function.

Recall that for the measure space
(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathcal{B},\mu)
and
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathcal{B},\mu)
, the square function is formulated as

\displaystyle Lf(x):=\Big(\sum_{k}|(M_{k}-\mathbb{E}_{k})f(x)|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},~x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

where,
M_{k}
is defined as in eq.
1.1
just by replacing
\mathcal{M}
by
\mathbb{C}
.
In
[
JRW03
]
, the authors proved that the operator
L
satisfies strong type
(p,p)
for
1<p\leq 2
and weak type
(1,1)
.

Let us we consider a Rademacher sequence
(\epsilon_{k})
on some probability measure space
(\Omega,P)
and construct a sequence of operators

\displaystyle T_{k}f(x):=(M_{k}-\mathcal{E}_{k})f(x),~f\in L^{1}_{w}(\mathcal{N},\varphi).

Observe that by
[
Cad19
]
, for a finite sequence
(T_{k}f)
we have

\displaystyle\left\|(T_{k}f)\right\|_{L_{w}^{1,\infty}(\mathcal{N};\ell_{2}^{rc})}\simeq\left\|\sum_{k}\epsilon_{k}T_{k}f\right\|_{L_{w}^{1,\infty}(L^{\infty}(\Omega)\otimes\mathcal{N})}

To estimate the weak type
(1,1)
bounds for the operator

(3.1)

\displaystyle Tf(x):=\sum_{k}\epsilon_{k}T_{k}f(x),~f\in L^{1}_{w}(\mathcal{N},\varphi),

we first observe the following. Let
f\in L^{1}_{w}(\mathcal{N})_{+}
. Then we have that

(3.2)

Theorem 3.1
.

Let

\lambda>0

Lemma 3.2
.

Let

\displaystyle\left\|M_{k,n}h\right\|^{p}_{p,w}\lesssim C(w)[w]_{A_{1}}2^{(k-n)\delta}\left\|h\right\|^{p}_{p,w}.

Proof.

Note that

\displaystyle\left\|M_{k,n}h\right\|_{p,w}^{p}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\|M_{k,n}h(x)\right\|_{p}^{p}w(x)dx.

\displaystyle\left\|M_{k,n}h(x)\right\|_{p}

\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1
. Hence,

\displaystyle\left\|M_{k,n}h\right\|_{p,w}^{p}\leq\frac{(2^{-n}2^{(d-1)(-k)})^{p/p^{\prime}}}{(2^{-kd})^{p}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big(\int_{\mathcal{I}(B_{k}+x,n)}\left\|h(y)\right\|^{p}_{p}dy\Big)w(x)dx

\displaystyle\lesssim C(w)[w]_{A_{1}}2^{(k-n)(p+\delta-1)}\left\|h\right\|^{p}_{p,w}

L^{1}(\mathcal{N}_{w},\varphi_{w})
to
L^{1,\infty}(L^{\infty}(\Omega)\otimes\mathcal{N}_{w},\tilde{\varphi}_{w}).
Moreover, it also follows from
[
KZK18
]
that
T
is a bounded operator from
L^{2}(\mathcal{N}_{w},\varphi_{w})
to
L^{2}(L^{\infty}(\Omega)\otimes\mathcal{N}_{w},\tilde{\varphi}_{w}).
We refer to the computation preceding inequality (
3.14
) for more details on this strong
(2,2)
bound for
T.
Therefore, by non-commutative Marcinkiewicz interpolation theorem, we obtain that
T
is a bounded map from
L^{p}(\mathcal{N}_{w},\varphi_{w})
to
L^{p}(L^{\infty}(\Omega)\otimes\mathcal{N}_{w},\tilde{\varphi}_{w})
for all
1<p<2
. Hence by duality we obtain that
T^{*}
is also a bounded linear operator from
L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathcal{N}_{w},\varphi_{w})
to
L^{p^{\prime}}(L^{\infty}(\Omega)\otimes\mathcal{N}_{w},\tilde{\varphi}_{w})
for all
1<p<2
, where
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1
. By an easy calculation one can see that
T^{*}=T
and hence
T
extends to a bounded linear map from
L^{p}(\mathcal{N}_{w},\varphi_{w})
to
L^{p}(L^{\infty}(\Omega)\otimes\mathcal{N}_{w},\tilde{\varphi}_{w})
for all
1<p<\infty.
The proof is now completed by non-commutative Khintchine’s inequality
[
PR17
]
and
[
Pis09
]
.
∎

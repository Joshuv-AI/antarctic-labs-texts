Safety and efficacy of IL-23 inhibitors in patients with moderate to severe ulcerative colitis: a systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials
Authors: Hamza Muhammad Amin, Sundas Hasan, Reem Abukhater, Rachel Lilley, Arif Atique, Maheen Sattar Shoaib, Qutaiba Albustanji, Humza Sadique, Saad Muhammad Khalid, Ali Hasan, Salman Majeed, Muhammad Aamir Shahzad, Maryam Shahzad, Mushood Ahmed, Raheel Ahmed, Syed Anjum Gardezi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground and objectiveTargeting the interleukin-23 (IL-23) pathway is an emerging therapeutic strategy for moderate to severe ulcerative colitis (UC). This systematic review and meta-analysis evaluated the efficacy and safety of IL-23 inhibitors for induction and maintenance therapy in UC.MethodsA systematic search of PubMed, Cochrane, and Google Scholar was conducted up to May 2025 to identify randomized controlled trials (RCTs) of IL-23 inhibitors (mirikizumab, risankizumab, guselkumab) in UC. Data were analyzed using Review Manager (RevMan 5.4) with a random-effects model.ResultsSeven RCTs (four induction, three maintenance) including 4203 patients were analyzed. IL-23 inhibitors significantly increased clinical remission during both induction (RR 1.52) and maintenance (RR 1.62). Rates of histo-endoscopic healing were also higher with IL-23 blockade in both induction (RR 2.53) and maintenance (RR 1.81). Importantly, IL-23 inhibitors were associated with a reduced risk of serious adverse events during induction (RR 0.39), with no significant difference observed during maintenance (RR 0.68). Other outcomes, including clinical response and corticosteroid-free remission, also consistently favored IL-23 blockade.ConclusionIL-23 inhibitors provide significant improvements in clinical remission and mucosal healing, with a favorable safety profile, particularly during induction therapy in moderate to severe UC.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s00384-025-05014-5.Keywords: Ulcerative colitis, IL-23 inhibitors, Meta-analysis

Introduction

Ulcerative colitis (UC) is a long-term inflammatory bowel disease driven by immune dysregulation, characterized by inflammation confined to the mucosal layer of the colon, involving the surface epithelium, crypt architecture, and submucosal tissue [1, 2]. It commonly presents with symptoms such as diarrhea, rectal bleeding, abdominal pain, urgency, fatigue, and a significant reduction in quality of life [3]. By 2023, UC was estimated to affect nearly 5 million individuals globally, with its incidence continuing to increase across various regions of the world [1]. The disease is believed to arise in genetically predisposed individuals following environmental triggers, with key contributing factors including disruption of the epithelial barrier, alterations in the gut microbiota, and dysregulated immune responses [4, 5].

The main objective of medical therapy is to reduce mucosal inflammation, maintain remission, alleviate symptoms, and prevent long-term complications such as disability, colectomy, and colorectal cancer [6]. Initial treatment typically involves aminosalicylates, corticosteroids, or thiopurines, either alone or in combination. For patients who fail, cannot tolerate, or present with severe disease and poor prognostic indicators, biologics are commonly used, including tumor necrosis factor inhibitors (infliximab, adalimumab, golimumab) and integrin blockers such as vedolizumab [7–10]. More recently, Janus kinase inhibitors (e.g., tofacitinib) have expanded therapeutic options. However, many patients show inadequate or waning responses, underscoring the need for novel therapies [7–10].

Targeting the interleukin-23 (IL-23) pathway has emerged as a promising strategy for IBD, especially UC. IL-23 regulates T helper 17 (Th17) activity and innate immune responses, both central to persistent inflammation [11–14]. Inhibition of IL-23 promotes mucosal healing and epithelial repair [11–14] . Therapies directed at IL-12/23 and IL-23 have shown efficacy, and mirikizumab—an anti-IL-23p19 monoclonal antibody—was recently approved for UC treatment, highlighting its clinical utility [15].

Consequently, there has been increasing research focus on developing IL-23 p19 antagonists for the treatment of UC. Given the growing body of evidence from recent randomized controlled trials, this systematic review and meta-analysis was conducted to comprehensively assess the efficacy and safety of IL-23 inhibitors for both induction and maintenance therapy in patients with moderate to severe UC.

Methods

This study was conducted in accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines, and the review protocol was prospectively registered with PROSPERO (CRD420251055310).

Data sources and search strategy

Two independent reviewers (M.A. and R.A.) systematically searched PubMed (MEDLINE), the Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials (CENTRAL), and Google Scholar from inception to May 1, 2025. A comprehensive search strategy was employed using a combination of medical subject terms and Boolean operators to capture all relevant studies. The terms included various synonyms and related concepts for ulcerative colitis (e.g., “ulcerative colitis,” UC, “colitis gravis,” “idiopathic proctocolitis,” “inflammatory bowel disease”) and IL-23 inhibitors (e.g., “Interleukin-23 inhibitors,” mirikizumab, LY-3074828, guselkumab, Tremfya, CNTO-1959, risankizumab, BI-655066, Skyrizi, ABBV-066, tildrakizumab, SCH-900222, MK-3222, Ilumya). These were combined using the Boolean operator “AND” and “OR” to refine results. To ensure completeness, references from identified trials were also reviewed manually. Full search strategies and database-specific results are available in Table S1.

Eligibility criteria

Studies were eligible if they met the following PICO framework: Adult patients with moderate to severe active ulcerative colitis were included, with interventions comprising IL-23 inhibitors such as mirikizumab, guselkumab, risankizumab, or tildrakizumab, regardless of dose or regimen. Comparisons were made against placebo controls. The primary outcome measured was clinical remission. Secondary outcomes encompassed clinical response, endoscopic improvement and remission, histo-endoscopic mucosal healing, symptomatic remission during induction, and corticosteroid-free remission during maintenance. Safety was assessed based on the incidence of any adverse events (AEs), serious AEs, and those leading to discontinuation of therapy. Studies were excluded if they involved dual IL-12/23 inhibitors (e.g., upadacitinib), were non-randomized or observational, or presented as abstracts, posters, reviews, or animal experiments.

Study selection

All identified articles were organized using EndNote Reference Manager (Version X7.5). Two reviewers (M.A. and R.A.) independently screened the titles and abstracts to determine eligibility based on the predefined criteria. Full-text versions of studies that appeared relevant were subsequently examined in greater detail to confirm inclusion. In addition, reference lists from prior reviews were examined to capture any missing relevant studies. Duplicate records were removed, and any discrepancies between the reviewers were resolved through discussion with a third author (M.S.). Screening was performed in duplicate and independently to minimize selection bias.

Data extraction

Following the retrieval of eligible full-text articles, a structured Excel sheet was developed after an initial pilot extraction. Information was collected on trial characteristics (first author, publication year, country, design, number of centers, sample size, treatment details, UC severity criteria, clinical remission definitions, primary outcome, and follow-up duration), participant baseline characteristics (group sizes, patient age, gender distribution, body weight, extent and duration of ulcerative colitis, baseline disease activity scores, levels of C-reactive protein (CRP), fecal calprotectin concentrations, and the use of concurrent medications at study entry), and outcome data as defined above. Two reviewers (M.A. and R.A.) independently extracted the data in duplicate. Discrepancies were resolved by discussion or in consultation with a senior author. Dichotomous outcomes were extracted as event counts, while continuous outcomes were reported as means and standard deviations. The data extraction process was conducted independently and in duplicate to reduce the risk of extraction bias.

Risk of bias

Data extraction was carried out independently by two reviewers (M.A. and R.A.) utilizing a structured Microsoft Excel spreadsheet specifically developed for this analysis. Any disagreements between the two reviewers were addressed through discussion with a third investigator (M.S.). The methodological quality and risk of bias of each included randomized controlled trial were independently assessed by the same reviewers using the updated version of the Cochrane Risk of Bias tool (ROB 2), which assesses potential bias across several domains, including selection, performance, attrition, reporting, and overall study quality [16]. Any disagreements were addressed through discussion until consensus was reached.

Statistical analysis

Data synthesis was carried out using Review Manager (RevMan, Version 5.4.1), developed by the Nordic Cochrane Centre. A random-effects model was utilized to compute pooled risk ratios (RR) along with 95% confidence intervals (CI). Statistical significance was defined as a P-value less than 0.05. Heterogeneity among studies was evaluated using Higgins’ I2 statistic, where values between 0 and 25% denoted low heterogeneity, 25 and 75% moderate, and values exceeding 75% indicated high heterogeneity. To assess the stability of the pooled results, a leave-one-out sensitivity analysis was conducted, whereby each study was sequentially excluded to determine its influence on the overall effect size. In accordance with Cochrane methodological standards, publication bias was not evaluated due to the inclusion of fewer than ten randomized controlled trials (RCTs) [17].

Results

Search results and study selection

A total of 888 records were initially retrieved through the database search and subsequently screened based on their titles and abstracts. After excluding 302 irrelevant records, 130 full-text articles were selected for further review. Of these, 76 were deemed irrelevant and excluded, leaving four records to be included in both the qualitative and quantitative assessments (Fig. 1).

PRISMA chart depicting the study selection process

Characteristics of included studies

This review included four RCTs, comprising a total of 4203 participants. Among them, 2868 patients were treated with IL-23 inhibitors such as mirikizumab, risankizumab, or guselkumab, while the remaining 1335 individuals received placebo [18–21]. The overall mean age of participants in the intervention arms was 42.08 years, compared to 41.14 years in the placebo groups. The average proportion of male participants was 59.4%. Reported mean body weight ranged from 71.8 to 77.0 kg across studies. Disease duration among participants typically ranged from 0.3 to 3 years. Most patients presented with either left-sided or extensive colitis, with involvement ranging from 32.0% to 74.4%. Baseline Mayo Clinic scores, indicative of moderate to severe disease, ranged between 6.0 and 9.5. Most of the included trials reported inflammatory markers such as C-reactive protein (CRP) and fecal calprotectin. Reported CRP values ranged from 3.4 to 4.7 mg/L, and fecal calprotectin levels spanned 1530 to 1945 µg/g, indicating ongoing intestinal inflammation. Concomitant medication use was common across studies. A significant proportion of participants were on glucocorticoids (up to 74.4%) and aminosalicylates (up to 90.3%), with immunosuppressant use ranging from 16.6% to 41.3% (Tables 1 and 2). All included trials showed an overall low risk of bias (Figure S1).

Efficacy outcomes

Clinical response

In the induction trials, treatment with IL-23 inhibitors was associated with a significantly higher rate of clinical response in patients with UC (RR, 1.86; 95% CI, 1.52–2.27; I2 = 73%; p < 0.001). Similarly, in the maintenance trials, IL-23 inhibitors also led to a significantly improved clinical response (RR, 1.42; 95% CI, 1.28–1.59; I2 = 0%; p < 0.001) (Fig. 2). In the leave-one-out sensitivity analysis, exclusion of the LUCENT-1 trial from the induction studies reduced heterogeneity from 73% to 36% (Figure S2).

Forest plot for the outcome of clinical response

Clinical remission

In the induction trials, IL-23 inhibitors significantly improved clinical remission rates in patients with UC (RR, 1.52; 95% CI, 1.15–2.01; I2 = 82%; p = 0.003). Similarly, in the maintenance trials, IL-23 inhibitors were also associated with a significant increase in clinical remission (RR, 1.62; 95% CI, 1.26–2.09; I2 = 78%; p = 0.0002) (Fig. 3). In the leave-one-out sensitivity analysis, exclusion of the QUASAR-3 trial from the induction studies reduced heterogeneity from 82% to 61%, while the removal of the QUASAR-3 trial from the maintenance studies reduced it from 78% to 0% (Figure S3).

Forest plot for the outcome of clinical remission

Endoscopic remission

In the induction trials, IL-23 inhibitors did not demonstrate a statistically significant effect on endoscopic remission in patients with UC (RR, 2.17; 95% CI, 0.29–16.09; I2 = 97%; p = 0.45). Similarly, in the maintenance trials, no significant improvement in endoscopic remission was observed between the two groups (RR, 1.88; 95% CI, 0.44–8.05; I2 = 96%; p = 0.39) (Fig. 4). In the leave-one-out sensitivity analysis, exclusion of the INSPIRE trial from the induction studies reduced heterogeneity from 97% to 82%, while the removal of the LUCENT-2 trial from the maintenance studies reduced it from 96% to 0% (Figure S4).

Forest plot for the outcome of endoscopic remission

Endoscopic improvement

In the induction trials, IL-23 inhibitors significantly improved endoscopic outcomes in patients with UC (RR, 2.80; 95% CI, 2.23–3.53; I2 = 0%; p < 0.001). Similarly, in the maintenance trials, IL-23 inhibitors were associated with significant improvements in the endoscopic outcomes in UC patients (RR, 2.14; 95% CI, 1.81–3.46; I2 = 0%; p = 0.002) (Fig. 5). In the leave-one-out sensitivity analysis, exclusion of the COMMAND trial from the maintenance studies reduced the heterogeneity from 76% to 0% (Figure S5).

Forest plot for the outcome of endoscopic improvement

Histo-endoscopic mucosal healing

In the induction trials, IL-23 inhibitors significantly enhanced histo-endoscopic mucosal healing in patients with UC (RR, 2.53; 95% CI, 1.62–3.95; I2 = 63%; p < 0.001). Likewise, in the maintenance trials, a significant improvement in histo-endoscopic mucosal healing was also observed with IL-23 inhibitors (RR, 1.81; 95% CI, 1.26–2.61; I2 = 65%; p = 0.001) (Fig. 6). In the leave-one-out sensitivity analysis, exclusion of the INSPIRE trial from the induction studies reduced heterogeneity from 63% to 42%, while the removal of the QUASAR-3 trial from the maintenance studies reduced it from 65% to 35% (Figure S6).

Forest plot for the outcome of histoendoscopic mucosal healing

Symptomatic and corticosteroid-free remission

In the induction trials, IL-23 inhibitors were associated with an improvement in symptomatic and corticosteroid-free remission among UC patients (RR, 1.77; 95% CI, 1.38–2.28; I2 = 20%; p < 0.001). Similarly, in the maintenance trials, IL-23 inhibitors were associated with significantly improved rates of symptomatic and corticosteroid-free remission (RR, 2.25; 95% CI, 1.77–2.86; I2 = 18%; p < 0.001) (Fig. 7).

Forest plot for the outcome of symptomatic and corticosteroid-free remission

Safety outcomes

Any adverse events

In the induction trials, IL-23 inhibitors were not associated with an increased risk of any adverse events in patients with UC (RR, 0.35; 95% CI, 0.11–1.10; I2 = 91%; p = 0.07). Similarly, in the maintenance trials, there was no observed difference in the risk of any adverse events between the IL-23 inhibitor and placebo groups (RR, 0.89; 95% CI, 0.72–1.09; I2 = 77%; p = 0.26) (Fig. 8). In the leave-one-out sensitivity analysis, exclusion of the QUASAR-3 trial from the induction studies reduced heterogeneity from 91% to 0%, while the removal of the LUCENT-2 trial from the maintenance studies reduced it from 77% to 0% (Figure S7).

Forest plot for the outcome of any adverse event

Any serious adverse events

In the induction trials, the use of IL-23 inhibitors was linked to a significantly lower incidence of serious adverse events in individuals with UC (RR, 0.39; 95% CI, 0.24–0.63; I2 = 42%; p < 0.001). Similarly, in the maintenance trials, there was no observed difference in the risk of any serious adverse events between the IL-23 inhibitor and placebo groups (RR, 0.68; 95% CI, 0.25–1.84; I2 = 72%; p = 0.44) (Fig. 9). According to the leave-one-out sensitivity analysis, exclusion of the INSPIRE trial from the induction studies reduced heterogeneity from 42% to 0%, while the removal of the QUASAR-3 trial from the maintenance studies reduced it from 72% to 1% (Figure S8).

Forest plot for the outcome of any serious adverse event

Any adverse event leading to drug discontinuation

In the maintenance trials, there was no observed difference in the risk of any adverse event leading to drug discontinuation between the IL-23 inhibitor and placebo groups (RR, 0.44; 95% CI, 0.19–1.04; I2 = 51%; p = 0.06) (Fig. 10). In the leave-one-out sensitivity analysis, exclusion of the LUCENT-2 trial from the maintenance studies reduced the heterogeneity from 51% to 0% (Figure S9).

Forest plot for the outcome of any adverse event leading to drug discontinuation

Risk of bias

All included trials were assessed using the RoB 2.0 tool and were judged to have an overall low risk of bias across all domains. Specifically, there were no concerns related to the randomization process, deviations from intended interventions, missing outcome data, outcome measurement, or selection of reported results, thereby indicating a low likelihood of methodological bias affecting the study findings (Figure S1). The certainty of evidence was assessed using the GRADE framework, as presented in Table 3. The outcomes ranged from moderate to high certainty, supporting the reliability of the pooled effect estimates for clinical interpretation.

Discussion

This systematic review and meta-analysis provides an updated synthesis of available RCT evidence evaluating the efficacy and safety of IL-23 inhibitors in moderate to severe UC. A total of four RCTs were included, involving 2868 participants treated with IL-23 inhibitors and 1335 control subjects, yielding a pooled sample size of 4203 patients. Our findings revealed significant enhancements in clinical response, clinical remission, endoscopic outcomes, histo-endoscopic mucosal healing, and corticosteroid-free symptomatic remission. No elevated risk of overall adverse events was noted, and a reduction in serious adverse events was evident during the induction phase. These results align with the conclusions drawn in earlier meta-analyses conducted by Fouad et al. and Saad et al., which also reported that IL-23 inhibition significantly improves clinical remission, endoscopic remission, and histologic remission while simultaneously reducing the risk of adverse events in patients receiving IL-23 inhibitors [22, 23].

First, regarding clinical response, our analysis revealed a significant improvement in response during both induction and maintenance phases. This finding aligns with the results of Sandborn et al., who also observed enhanced clinical response in both trial phases [18]. Additionally, the LUCENT-1 trial demonstrated an improved clinical response in patients receiving 300 mg of mirikizumab compared to placebo at both week 4 and week 12 [19]. These improvements are likely linked to modulation of the IL-23/Th17 pathway. Preclinical studies support the pivotal role of IL-23 in intestinal inflammation: Yen et al. showed that IL-10 knockout mice lacking the IL-23p19 subunit were protected from colitis [24], whereas mice lacking IL-12p35 developed spontaneous colitis, highlighting IL-23, rather than IL-12, as central in driving colonic inflammation [24, 25]. Uhlig et al. further demonstrated in a CD40-induced colitis model that IL-23 is a key mediator of intestinal inflammation [26]. Similarly, Maxwell et al. found that in Abcb1a–/– mice with impaired epithelial barrier function, blocking IL-23 alleviated colitis, while IL-17A inhibition worsened disease severity and barrier disruption [27].

Secondly, with respect to clinical remission, our analysis demonstrated significant improvements during both the induction and maintenance phases. These findings are supported by prior clinical trials. According to Sandborn et al., mirikizumab showed clinical efficacy in adults with moderate to severe UC, with remission achieved in 24.2% of patients by week 12 compared to 13.3% receiving placebo (P < 0.001). By week 40, remission rates increased to 49.9% with mirikizumab versus 25.1% with placebo (p < 0.001) [18].

Similarly, the QUASAR-3 trial reported that 23% of patients receiving intravenous guselkumab achieved remission at week 12, compared to 8% with placebo (p < 0.0001). At week 44, sustained remission was observed in the guselkumab groups, with 50% of patients on 200 mg every 4 weeks and 45% on 100 mg every 8 weeks achieving remission, whereas only 19% of those in the placebo group did so (both p < 0.0001) [20]. These therapeutic effects may be attributed to the suppression of pro-inflammatory gene activity, enhancement of epithelial barrier function, and modulation of immune responses. This includes reduced activation of innate lymphoid cells (ILCs), leading to decreased production of inflammatory cytokines and improved mucosal healing.

[28, 29]. According to a clinical trial by Steere et al., mirikizumab treatment led to increased expression of aquaporin-8, a water transporter, which may help reduce fecal water content. Additionally, upregulation of anion transporters such as SLC26A2 and SLC16A9 suggests that mirikizumab supports the restoration of mucosal barrier function as early as 12 weeks into induction therapy [28, 29]. Notably, mirikizumab also significantly reduced the expression of REG1A and REG1B—genes previously found to be upregulated in ulcerative colitis mucosa—highlighting its potential to reverse disease-associated molecular signatures, consequently leading to clinical remission [28, 29].

Third, our analysis demonstrated significant improvements in both endoscopic outcomes and histo-endoscopic mucosal healing. These results align with earlier studies, including the QUASAR-3 trial, which demonstrated significant endoscopic improvement at week 12 during induction (p = 0.0015) and sustained efficacy through week 44 in the maintenance phase (p < 0.001) [20]. IL-23 signaling is known to impede Treg differentiation, a cell population essential for maintaining immune tolerance and suppressing chronic intestinal inflammation [18, 30–32]. IL-23p19 inhibitors such as risankizumab (approved for Crohn’s disease) and mirikizumab (approved for ulcerative colitis in Japan and by the EMA) have shown consistent efficacy in achieving clinical and endoscopic endpoints [18, 30–32]. Preclinical data support this mechanism: Maxwell et al. demonstrated that IL-23 blockade reduced colonic inflammation and increased Treg accumulation in a multidrug resistance–mouse model [27], while Eftychi et al. described a biphasic colitis model where IL-23 sustained chronic inflammation [33].

Fourth, our analysis demonstrated significant improvements in both symptomatic remission and corticosteroid-free remission among patients receiving IL-23 inhibitors. These findings are consistent with the LUCENT-3 study, which reported that, using a modified non-responder imputation (mNRI) approach, 74.7% of patients who achieved clinical remission at week 52 maintained corticosteroid-free remission at week 104. Furthermore, notable rates of symptomatic (85.9%), endoscopic (79.5%), and histologic-endoscopic mucosal remission (63.9%) were observed [34]. Similarly, a network meta-analysis by Khan et al. showed that tofacitinib had the highest likelihood of achieving corticosteroid-free remission (92.43%), followed closely by upadacitinib (87.73%) [35]. These findings may be attributed to the reduced corticosteroid responsiveness induced by IL-23 inhibitors, as demonstrated by Tello et al., who showed that IL-17 and IL-23 upregulate GR-β, a variant of the glucocorticoid receptor that antagonizes the activity of GR-α, the primary receptor mediating the anti-inflammatory effects of corticosteroids. Moreover, IL-2 and IL-4 further diminish GR-α levels, contributing to steroid resistance [36].

Fifth, regarding serious adverse events, our analysis revealed a significantly lower frequency of such events in the induction trials compared to the maintenance trials. This discrepancy may be attributed to differences in the baseline disease activity of the patient populations. Patients enrolled in induction trials typically present with more active or severe ulcerative colitis, which inherently increases their risk for adverse events. The administration of IL-23 inhibitors during this phase may help attenuate this risk by rapidly reducing inflammation and modulating immune responses [37]. Conversely, patients in maintenance trials have generally already achieved clinical improvement or stability, resulting in a lower baseline risk for serious adverse events [37]. This more stable disease state, along with the potential influence of a stronger placebo effect over the longer duration of maintenance treatment, may diminish the observable impact of IL-23 inhibitors on adverse event rates during this phase. This interpretation is further supported by findings from the QUASAR-3 trial, which showed similar rates of serious adverse events across treatment and placebo arms during the maintenance phase: 3% in the guselkumab 100 mg every 8 weeks group, 6% in the 200 mg every 4 weeks group, and 5% in the placebo group [20].

Lastly, our analysis revealed no significant differences in endoscopic remission, the overall frequency of adverse events, or adverse events leading to study discontinuation. Regarding endoscopic remission, it is important to note that achieving a Mayo Endoscopic Subscore (MES) of 0—indicating complete mucosal normalization—is particularly challenging within the typical 8–12-week induction period. Many patients show partial improvement (MES 1) but do not reach full remission in this short timeframe [38]. The Mayo Endoscopic Subscore (MES) is the most widely utilized metric in both clinical settings and research to evaluate endoscopic healing and response in ulcerative colitis [38, 39]. According to Jangi et al., individuals with active UC who demonstrated mucosal healing (defined as MES 0–1) on two successive endoscopies spaced roughly 16 months apart experienced a markedly lower relapse rate [40]. Notably, the cumulative relapse risk over 26 months in those with sustained endoscopic healing was only 11.5%, emphasizing the lasting benefits of mucosal resolution [40]. Furthermore, the absence of notable differences in adverse event profiles may be influenced by the concurrent use of background medications such as corticosteroids, immunomodulators, and oral aminosalicylates. These agents, often allowed or required during trial protocols, can mitigate differences in safety outcomes and mucosal healing, potentially masking the independent safety signals or benefits of IL-23 inhibitors [41].

While our meta-analysis highlights the efficacy of IL-23 inhibitors in moderate to severe UC, comparative effectiveness against other advanced therapies is still evolving. According to a network meta-analysis by Estevinho et al., Etrasimod 2mg/day ranked highest in achieving histologic improvement and remission during induction, while Guselkumab 200–400 mg demonstrated superior histo-endoscopic improvement. During maintenance, Upadacitinib 30 mg/day was most effective across several histologic endpoints [42]. Similarly, Barberio et al. reported Upadacitinib 30mg o.d. ranked highest for clinical remission and endoscopic improvement, whereas Guselkumab 200 mg q4w ranked highest for corticosteroid-free remission [43].

For Crohn’s disease, Vuyyuru et al. found IL-23 inhibition significantly superior to placebo for both induction and maintenance of clinical and endoscopic remission, with high-certainty evidence. The treatment was effective in both biologic-naïve and -experienced populations and was associated with a reduced risk of serious adverse events [44]. Lee et al. investigated response based on prior TNF exposure and found that selective IL-23 antagonists had comparable efficacy in TNF-naïve and TNF-exposed UC patients, unlike JAK inhibitors and lymphocyte trafficking agents, which showed differential effects [45].

Furthermore, an umbrella review by Nardone et al. explored the impact of therapies on extraintestinal manifestations (EIMs). Anti-TNF agents remained most effective for joint, skin, and ocular EIMs, while Ustekinumab showed benefit for some musculoskeletal and ocular manifestations [46]. However, IL-23 inhibitors were not evaluated in this context, highlighting a significant gap in current evidence.

Limitations

This updated systematic review and meta-analysis evaluates the efficacy and safety of IL-23 inhibitors in moderate to severe ulcerative colitis using rigorous methodology, detailed outcome analysis, and thorough risk of bias assessment. Nonetheless, several limitations should be acknowledged.

First, the number of included RCTs was limited, with follow-up durations ranging from 12 to 52 weeks, restricting evaluation of long-term efficacy and safety. Second, relatively small sample sizes and low event rates may have limited statistical power to detect rare or delayed adverse events. Third, heterogeneity was present, partly due to differences in IL-23 inhibitors (mirikizumab, risankizumab, guselkumab), dosing strategies (50–600 mg), and treatment timelines (induction 4–12 weeks; maintenance 4–52 weeks). Sensitivity analyses reduced heterogeneity without altering overall estimates. Fourth, most comparisons were against placebo rather than active comparators, limiting conclusions about the relative effectiveness of IL-23 inhibitors versus established therapies (anti-TNF agents, JAK inhibitors, integrin blockers). Fifth, concomitant medications (e.g., corticosteroids, immunomodulators) were inconsistently reported and may have influenced outcomes. Sixth, publication bias could not be formally assessed due to the small number of studies, and several trials were industry-sponsored, raising the possibility of bias. Finally, the exclusion of real-world data limits generalizability to routine practice. In conclusion, IL-23 inhibitors are effective and safe for moderate to severe UC; however, long-term studies are warranted to confirm durability and define their role among advanced therapies.

Clinical implications

IL-23 inhibitors demonstrate consistent efficacy in both biologic-naïve and biologic-experienced patients, making them particularly valuable for individuals who have not responded to or cannot tolerate other advanced therapies. Their favorable safety profile and less frequent dosing also support their use in long-term disease control. However, high treatment costs and variable access across healthcare systems may limit their availability to certain patient populations. Therefore, clinical decisions should be guided by individual factors such as prior treatment history, disease severity, comorbidities, and patient preference, while carefully considering affordability, insurance coverage, and local formulary restrictions.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this systematic review and meta-analysis demonstrates that IL-23 inhibitors are highly effective in enhancing clinical outcomes, including symptom relief, remission, and mucosal healing, in patients with moderate to severe UC. The treatment also demonstrated a favorable safety profile with no increased risk of overall adverse events. IL-23 inhibitors offer promising therapeutic benefits, supporting their role as an effective option for managing ulcerative colitis. Further long-term studies will help refine their use in clinical practice.

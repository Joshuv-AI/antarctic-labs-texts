Erianin regulates allergic rhinitis via KAT2A-mediated histone succinylation
Authors: Hai-Ying Wang, Chang-Yang Wang, Qing-Fu Bao, Min-Fen Chen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAllergic rhinitis arthritis (AR) is a common chronic inflammatory disease, which may lead to other systemic chronic diseases. Erianin, a natural product isolated from Dendrobium chrysotoxum, has been reported to exert effects on a variety of diseases. In this work, we aimed to explore the effects and mechanisms of erianin in allergic rhinitis.MethodsInterleukin-13 (IL-13) was selected to treat nasal epithelial cells (NECs) to establish an in vitro AR model; Ovalbumin (OVA) was used to treat the nasal cavity of mice to establish an in vivo AR model; Cell counting kit 8 (CCK-8) was used to detect the cell activity of nasal epithelial cells after different treatments; Apoptosis and inflammatory cell infiltration of nasal tissue were analyzed by TUNEL and H&E staining, respectively; Mice noses scratching were counted to determine the severity of inflammation; Inflammation-related cytokine expression was examined by qPCR and ELISA to determine how erianin alleviates inflammation; Changes in apoptosis and histone acetylation succinylation modification were examined by Western blotting; Enrichment of KAT2A and histone succinylation at the p65 promoter was detected by ChIP-seq.ResultsErianin inhibited IL-13-induced inflammation in nasal epithelial cells in vitro, while alleviating OVA-induced rhinitis in mice in vivo. In further mechanistic studies, Erianin was found to regulate the disease through KAT2A-mediated histone succinylation. Erianin blocked KAT2A as well as H3K79 succinylation and further inhibited p65 expression, thereby controlling the further development of allergic rhinitis.ConclusionErianin alleviates and inhibits allergic rhinitis through KAT2A-mediated histone succinylation.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12950-025-00479-1.Keywords: Erianin, Allergic rhinitis, KAT2A, Histone succinylation

Introduction

Allergic rhinitis (AR) is a common chronic inflammatory disease characterized by itching, sneezing, rhinorrhea and nasal congestion [1, 2]. Although AR is not a life-threatening disease, the age distribution of patients with allergic rhinitis is expanding and the incidence of the disease is on the rise with the influence of factors such as lifestyle changes, declining air quality and environmental pollution. Furthermore, the disease is characterized by recurrent episodes and prolongation, so it may also lead to the occurrence of other systemic chronic diseases, which will seriously reduce the quality of people’s life and exert a negative impact on the socio-economy [3, 4].Therefore, it is of great significance to understand the molecular mechanisms underlying the pathogenesis of AR and develop new effective treatments for AR [5, 6].

The pathogenesis of AR is primarily driven by a type 2 helper T (Th2) cell-mediated immune response. Upon exposure to allergens, activated Th2 cells release a cascade of key cytokines, including interleukin-4 (IL-4), IL-5, and IL-13, which orchestrate the allergic inflammatory cascade [7, 8]. Among these, IL-13 has been identified as a central mediator in the development of AR pathophysiology. It plays a critical role in inducing IgE class switching in B cells, promoting eosinophil infiltration, and directly acting on nasal epithelial cells (NECs) to trigger goblet cell metaplasia and hypersecretion of mucus, a hallmark feature of AR [9, 10]. Specifically, IL-13 stimulation of NECs leads to the upregulated expression of inflammatory markers such as eotaxin-1 (a potent eosinophil chemoattractant), granulocyte-macrophage colony-stimulating factor (GM-CSF), and mucin 5AC (MUC5AC) [11, 12]. Given its pivotal role, IL-13 is widely utilized in establishing in vitro models of allergic airway inflammation, making it a pertinent cytokine for investigating potential therapeutic interventions in AR [13].

Natural products and their derivatives, characterized by their novel and diverse structures and high biological activity, have long been regarded as a rich source of drugs for the treatment of a wide range of diseases [14, 15].

Erianin is a natural product isolated mainly from the orchid family Dendrobium, including Dendrobium bulbosum. It belongs to the bibenzyl compounds with the chemical name of 2-methoxy-5-[2-(3,4,5-trimethoxyphenyl)-ethyl]-phenol, and has been reported to exert antitumor effects against a variety of cancers. Chen et al. showed that erianin could inhibit the growth and migration of lung cancer cells through calcium/calmodulin-dependent iron death, which revealing erianin as a potential compound for lung cancer therapy [16]. Zhu et al. reported that erianin could supress the growth of bladder cancer cells through mitochondrial apoptosis and JNK pathway [17]. Liu et al. demonstrated that erianin could prevent the growth of nasopharyngeal carcinoma cells through the apoptotic pathway [18]. In addition, other studies have shown that erianin had a potential in the treatment of inflammation, diabetic nephropathy, retinopathy and other diseases, which is expected to be a promising therapeutic agent for a variety of diseases [19, 20],. However, there are few studies on its specific role and mechanism in allergic rhinitis.

Post-translational modifications of histones play important regulatory roles in chromatin structure and function, such as transcription, DNA damage repair and DNA replication [21, 22]. More than 16 histone modifications, including acetylation, methylation, phosphorylation, sumoylation, ubiquitination, succinylation, and propionylation, occur in the N-terminal tail and spheroidal domains of core histones, which can alter the interaction of genomic DNA with histones, thereby altering chromatin state and gene expression [23, 24].

Lysine succinylation frequently occurs in newly discovered post-translational modifications of human histones. Studies have shown that KAT2A (also known as GCN5) is a multipotent histone acyltransferase with activities of histone acetyl-transferase (HAT), succinyl-transferase, propionyl-transferase and butyryl-transferase [25]. KAT2A forms a complex with the nuclear-localized α-ketoglutaric acid ketoglutarate dehydrogenase (kgDH) to produce succinyl-CoA (succinyl-CoA), which in turn binds to tyrosine 645 of KAT2A, followed leading to KAT2A-mediated histone H3 succinylation of lysine (K)79, with the highest frequency near the transcription start site of the gene [26].

In this study, KAT2A-mediated histone succinylation modification wa used as an entry point to analyze how erianin regulates this process through cell and animal experiments. Moreover, the role and mechanism of erianin on allergic rhinitis will be explored, which could provide new research guidance for the development of novel drugs and therapeutic techniques for allergic rhinitis.

Materials and methods

Materials

Erianin was purchased from Shanghai Yuanye Biotechnology Co and dissolved with dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) at 10 mM as stock solution. All antibodies were purchased from Invitrogen (MA, USA). Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle’s Medium (DMEM), fetal bovine serum (FBS) and penicillin/streptomycin were provided by Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc. (Waltham, MA, USA).

Cell culture and treatment

Nasal epithelial cells (NECs) were obtained from American Type Culture Collection and cultured in DMEM that supplemented with 10% FBS (Gibco, USA) and 1% penicillin/streptomycin at 37˚C in a humidified incubator containing 5% CO2. The cells were randomly divided and treated for different ways with the density of 70‑80%: NECs, NECs + 50 ng/mL IL‑13, NECs + 50 ng/mL IL‑13 + 10 µM erianin and NECs‑+ 50 ng/mL IL‑13 + 10 µM Erianin + KAT2A (activation) or NECs‑+ 50 ng/mL IL‑13 + 10 µM Erianin + p65 (activation).

Interleukin-13 (IL-13) is an immunomodulatory cytokine secreted by activated Th2 cells and plays an important role in allergic inflammation. In vitro AR models were established by stimulating nasal epithelial cells (NECs) with 50 ng/mL IL-13 supplemented in basal media and replaced every 48 h.

Cell viability

Appropriate amount NECs (5 × 103 per well) were seeded into 96-well plate and treated with IL-13 and erianin, respectively. Then 5 µl of Cell Counting Kit 8 (CCK-8; Beyotime, China) reagent was added to each well and incubated for 2 h. Cell viability was then calculated by testing the absorbance values of the cells at 50 nm using microplate reader (Thermo, USA).

Mice model

As in humans, IgE antibodies could be produced by Balb/c mice when challenged with allergen, therefore Balb/c mice were selected in this study. All animal experiments were approved by Taizhou Hospital of Zhejiang Province animal care and use committee. All mice were supplied by Beijing Vital River Laboratory Animal Technology Co., Ltd. (Beijing, China) and then raised in specific-pathogen-free (SPF) barrier rooms for 7 days for environmental acclimatization. To establish the AR model, mice were injected intraperitoneally with 25 µg of OVA and 1 mg of aluminum hydroxide gel [Al(OH)3] plus 0.5 mL of isotonic saline on days 1, 5, 14, and 21 for basal sensitization, and then 500 µg of OVA plus 20 µL of isotonic saline was given to the mice as a bilaterally administered nasal drip on day 28 for 10 d.

Evaluation of allergic symptoms

The number of nose rubs was assessed blindly in AR mice. The frequency of nose rubbing was calculated for each mouse within 5 min after the last OVA intranasal stimulation.

Histological analysis

The mice were euthanized and the mouse nasal mucosal tissues were extracted and treated for a series of procedures such as fixation, dehydration, embedding, sectioning and then H&E staining according to the given steps, followed by randomly selecting the field of view under the digital microscope (Leica QWin) to observe the pathological changes in the tissues. For the fluorescent TUNEL apoptosis staining, the NECs were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde and then conducted following manual instruction of DeadEnd fluorometric TUNEL system kit (Promega, USA).

Quantitative PCR assay

Cells were lysed with TRIzol reagent to isolate and quantify total RNA. Next, 1 µg of RNA was taken and reverse transcribed into cDNA using the PrimeScript RT Master Mix kit. RNA levels of the target gene were then detected in the presence of primers using a SYBR Green system PCR instrument. The relative gene expression of eotaxin-1, MUC5AC, GM-CSF, KAT2A, p65 were calculated by the 2−ΔΔCt method.

ELISA

All ELISA kits in this study were provided by R&D Systems, Inc. Culture supernatants of NECs were collected and for granulocyte-macrophage colony-stimulating factor (GM-CSF), eosinophil chemotactic factor (eotaxin-1), and mucin 5AC (MUC5AC) analysis in accordance with the manufacturer’s instructions of the ELISA kits. For the detection of allergic mediators (IL-13 IL-4 IL-5) in mice, all mice were executed at 24 h after the last OVA challenge, while blood was collected and centrifuged (4 °C, 600 xg, 20 min) to obtain serum, which was then assayed with an ELISA kit according to the given procedure.

Western blotting analysis

NEC cells were first lysed using RIPA lysis buffer containing protease and phosphatase inhibitors, centrifuged to obtain total protein, and then the protein concentration was determined using a BCA kit (Beyotime, China), followed by separation of the proteins by SDS-PAGE gel and transfer to a PVDF membrane. The membrane was then closed in 5% low-fat milk for 1 h at room temperature. After closure, the membrane was incubated with primary antibody at 4℃ overnight, and then incubated with goat and rabbit secondary antibodies for 2 h at room temperature. The immunodetected protein bands were visualized using enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL) reagent (Millipore) and captured with an imaging system (Tanon, China).

ChIP-seq

NECs chromatin was extracted and fragmented to 200–500 bp by sonication.ChIP was performed using anti-KAT2A and H3K79 antibodies (Abcam).Antibody validation is available on the manufacturer’s website. The antibodies were incubated with Dynabeads Protein A and added to the sonicated samples for immunoprecipitation. Finally, isolated fragments were used for library construction using the Illumina TruSeq Sample Preparation Kit according to the manufacturer’s recommendations. ChIP-seq was performed on an Illumina HiSeq 3000 instrument.

Statistics

Data in this work were shown as means ± SD. The differences between two experimental groups were analyzed by Student’s t-test. Comparisons among multiple groups were performed by one-way ANOVA followed by Tukey’s post hoc test for multiple comparisons using SPSS software (version 24.0). A P < 0.05 was regarded as statistically significant.

Results

Erianin inhibits IL-13-induced nasal epithelial cell inflammation

NECs were subjected to different treatments: “control” “IL-13” “IL-13 + erianin”, and the cell viability of these groups was first detected using CCK-8, it can be seen that erianin significantly inhibited IL-13-induced inflammation in Fig. 1a. Eosinophil chemokine-1 (Eotaxin-1) is an important chemokine in the pathogenesis of allergic diseases with significance in allergic diseases. MUC5AC is an important mucin in respiratory mucus, and its aberrant expression plays an important role in various airway mucus hypersecretory diseases. Granulocyte-macrophage Colony-stimulating factor (GM-CSF) is an important inflammatory factor that regulates innate and adaptive immunity. In this study, it could be observed from Fig. 1B and D that the relative expression levels of mRNAs of these three inflammatory factors in NECs were significantly increased after IL-13 treatment, indicating the generation of inflammation, which was significantly alleviated by erianin treatment. Meanwhile, the relative corresponding protein expression presented in Fig. 1E and G showed a consistent trend. We then further analyzed the apoptosis of cells under these treatments by TUNEL staining. Confocal microscopy images as well as quantitative analysis showed that more apoptosis was occur in NECs, whereas erianin significantly inhibited IL-13-induced cellular inflammation, and apoptosis was significantly ameliorated (green represents apoptotic cells, blue represents nuclei). Cysteine aspartate proteases (caspases) are a class of genes that play an important role in maintaining homeostasis in the body by regulating apoptosis and inflammation. The Bcl-2 family includes both pro-apoptotic effectors (e.g., Bax) and anti-apoptotic members (e.g., Bcl-2) [27]. To further confirm the anti-apoptotic effect of erianin, we examined the expression levels of Bcl-2, BAX, Cleaved-caspase3 and caspase3 in the cells, and as shown in Fig. 1I, erianin significantly inhibited IL-13-induced apoptosis. These data suggest that erianin can effectively alleviate IL-13-induced inflammation in NECs.

Erianin alleviates OVA-induced allergic rhinitis in mice

AR with the characteristics of increasing nose scratching or sneezing. Figure 2A showed the statistical results of nose scratching behavior in OVA-treated AR mice was significantly increased compared with the untreated group (control group). Notably, the number of nose scratching within 5 min was significantly lower in the Erianin group (Fig. 2A). AR is typically characterized by shedding of cilia, eosinophil granulocyte, cupular cell hyperplasia, and monocyte recruitment in the nasal mucosa. Then the mice were euthanized and the pathological changes of nasal mucosa were analyzed by H&E staining for histological analysis. No significant higher eosinophil and cuprocyte counts were observed between OVA-treated mice and control mice in AR histopathologic features, which were less frequently displayed by erianin-treated mice in comparison (Fig. 2B). These findings suggest that erianin exhibits anti-inflammatory activity that may ameliorate AR symptoms by controlling the infiltration of inflammatory cells in the nasal mucosa. We then analyzed whether the alleviation of allergic symptoms in AR mice was associated with the reduction of allergic mediators. The levels of allergic mediators such as IL-13, IL-4, and IL-5 were significantly reduced in erianin-treated AR mice (Fig. 2C-E), which demonstrated that erianin alleviated allergic symptoms mainly by inhibiting the expression of allergic mediators.

Erianin inhibited the expression of KAT2A and p65

We next explored the molecular mechanism underlying erianin’s effects. Western blot analysis revealed that treatment with erianin significantly inhibited the protein expression of KAT2A and the NF-κB p65 subunit in nasal epithelial cells (NECs) (Fig. 3A-C). Concurrently, erianin also reduced the global levels of H3K79 succinylation (H3K79succ), a modification catalyzed by KAT2A, but not H3K27 acetylation (Fig. 3A). The mRNA expression levels of KAT2A and p65 showed a consistent decreasing trend upon erianin treatment. To investigate the functional link between KAT2A-mediated succinylation and p65 transcription, we performed chromatin immunoprecipitation sequencing (ChIP-seq). The results demonstrated that erianin treatment markedly inhibited the enrichment of both KAT2A and H3K79succ at the promoter region of the p65 gene (RELA) (Fig. 3D-E). This suggests that erianin suppresses p65 transcription by specifically impairing KAT2A-mediated histone succinylation at its promoter.

ERI inhibited the expression of KAT2A and p65 (B-C) as well as the enrichment of KAT2A and H3K79succ at the p65 promoter (D-E). Data are presented as mean ± SD (n = 3); statistical analysis was performed using one-way ANOVA followed by Tukey’s post hoc test; **p < 0.01 vs. Cont group; ##p < 0.01 vs. Ele group

Activation of KAT2A reverses the inhibitory effect of erianin

To confirm that KAT2A is a direct target of erianin, we employed a specific KAT2A agonist (MB-3, 10 µM) to rescue its activity. As shown in Fig. 4A-B, ChIP-seq analysis confirmed that activation of KAT2A significantly reversed the erianin-induced reduction in the enrichment of both KAT2A and H3K79succ at the p65 promoter. Consistent with this, western blot analysis showed that co-treatment with the KAT2A agonist restored the protein expression levels of both KAT2A and p65 that were suppressed by erianin alone (Fig. 4C). These data indicate that the anti-inflammatory effects of erianin are primarily mediated through the inhibition of KAT2A activity.

Activation of NF-kB inhibits the inflammation-relieving effect of erianin on IL-13-induced nasal epithelial cells

To determine whether p65 is the critical downstream effector mediating erianin’s effects, we activated the NF-κB pathway using a specific p65 activator (Betulinic acid, 10 µM) in the context of IL-13 stimulation. Firstly, CCK-8 assays showed that p65 activation exacerbated IL-13-induced cytotoxicity and abolished the protective effect of erianin (Fig. 5A). Subsequently, both qPCR and ELISA analyses demonstrated that activation of p65 significantly reversed the erianin-induced suppression of inflammatory cytokines (eotaxin-1, MUC5AC, and GM-CSF) at the mRNA (Fig. 5B-D) and protein (Fig. 5E-G) levels. Furthermore, TUNEL staining (Fig. 5H) and western blot analysis of apoptosis-related proteins (Fig. 5I) revealed that p65 activation also counteracted the anti-apoptotic effects of erianin in IL-13-stimulated NECs. Collectively, these rescue experiments demonstrate that erianin exerts its anti-inflammatory and anti-apoptotic effects by specifically targeting the KAT2A/H3K79succ/p65 signaling axis. The fact that OVA challenge activates p65 and erianin inhibits it is not contradictory but rather expected; it confirms that erianin’s therapeutic action is achieved by suppressing this activated pathway (Fig. 6).

Erianin inhibits the activity of KAT2A, reducing H3K79 succinylation (H3K79succ) at the promoter of the p65 (RELA) gene. The downregulation of the KAT2A/H3K79succ/p65 axis ultimately attenuates the production of inflammatory cytokines (eotaxin-1, MUC5AC, GM-CSF), chemokines, and cellular apoptosis, thereby ameliorating IL-13-induced inflammation in nasal epithelial cells and OVA-induced allergic rhinitis in mice

Discussion

Erianin is widely existed in many kinds of Dendrobium, which can be extracted from Dendrobium, among which its content in Dendrobium chrysotoxum is as high as 0.16%, which is significantly higher than that in other species [28]. As a traditional Chinese medicine, Dendrobium is often used as an antipyretic and analgesic because of its heat-clearing, detoxifying and analgesic effects. Erianin is one of the most important bibenzyl compounds in Dendrobium, which possesses a very wide range of pharmacological effects [29]. In recent years, new discoveries have been found on the biological activity and pharmacological mechanism of erianin. Besides its unique anti-tumor properties, erianin also plays an important role in antibacterial, anti-viral and anti-inflammatory aspects [30, 31]. Ouyang et al. showed that eranin inhibited the binding of Staphylococcus aureus to fibronectin and the formation of biofilms and the survival rate of infected Staphylococcus aureus mice was significantly improved by intravenous injection of eranin. Yuan et al. also found that eranin had a favorable mitigating effect on peritonitis caused by Staphylococcus aureus in vitro, which could attenuate abscesses caused by Staphylococcus aureus in mice [32]. Dou et al. demonstrated that eranin can exert anti-ulcerative colitis effects by inhibiting Toll-like receptor 4 (TLR-4) and STAT3 signaling pathways [33]. Zhang et al. demonstrated that eranin inhibited microglia-induced retinal inflammation by reducing cellular glucose uptake and eliminating the subsequent initiation of the downstream ERK1/2-nuclear factor B pathway during the development of diabetic retinopathy [13].Meng et al. demonstrated that eranin could inhibit the production of human intestinal EVD68 virus by inducing G2/M-phase blockade, making it a potential drug for the treatment of human enteroviruses [34]. CTD (Comparative Toxicogenomics Database) is a database that can provide the relationship between chemical substances and diseases. Some studies have predicted the potential role of erianin in diseases through CTD, and found that the potential effects of erianin were not only in tumors and psoriasis, it also has a high predictive score in liver cirrhosis, COVID-19, rheumatism, hepatomegaly, sepsis, and many other unstudied diseases, which suggests that erianin has a broad spectrum of diseases and may have the potential to be a therapeutic agent for a wide range of diseases [35]. Here we demonstrated that erianin significantly alleviated the rhinitis of IL-13-induced inflammation of nasal epithelial cells and OVA-induced allergic rhinitis in mice, respectively.

Lysine acetyltransferase 2 A (KAT2A), first identified by Frye et al. in 1999, which possesses several enzymatic activities, such as deacetylation and hydrolysis of lysine (succinyl, malonyl, and glutaryl groups) [36]. Many studies have shown that KAT2A acetylation is associated with many aspects, which including chromatin regulation, autophagy, neuronal apoptosis, cell proliferation, chromosome condensation, inflammation, cellular differentiation of stem cells, hematopoiesis and oxidative stress and so on [19, 37–41]. Currently, the research of histone succinylation is still in its early stage, but it is of major interest because of its high degree of overlap with acetylation. Succinylation has a broad role in regulating cellular metabolism, and studies have shown that it may affect other downstream cellular biological functions precisely owing to this property [42]. Recent studies have found that succinyl-CoA, as a substrate, is utilized by KAT2A to catalyze the succinylation of lysine residue at position 79 of histone H3 (H3K79) [43]. Here, mechanistically, erianin inhibited the enrichment of KAT2A as well as H3K79succ at the p65 promoter, thereby suppressing the transcriptional expression of p65. Simultaneously, the effect of intracellular histone succinylation modification level on inflammation disappeared when KAT2A and NF-kB were activated, which further reversed the inhibitory effect of erianin on cellular inflammation. Above all, it suggested that erianin mainly achieves its inhibitory effect through KAT2A-mediated histone succinylation.

Conclusion

In conclusion, erianin alleviated IL-13-induced inflammation in nasal epithelial cells and OVA-induced allergic rhinitis in mice. Mechanistic studies showed that erianin inhibited the enrichment of KAT2A as well as H3K79succ at the p65 promoter, thereby suppressing the transcriptional expression of NF-κB p65, further repressing the inflammatory response and alleviating allergic rhinitis. Our data emphasize the impact of erianin in allergic rhinitis and provide new evidence for the application of erianin as a promising anti-inflammatory reagent.

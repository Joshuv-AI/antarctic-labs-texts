ABSTRACT
AbstractRovadicitinib (Anxu®) is a small molecule Janus kinase (JAK)/Rho-associated coiled-coil–containing protein kinase (ROCK) inhibitor being developed by Chia Tai Tianqing Pharmaceutical Group (a subsidiary of Sino Biopharmaceutical) for the treatment of primary myelofibrosis (PMF), post-polycythemia vera myelofibrosis (PPV-MF), post-essential thrombocythemia myelofibrosis (PET-MF), graft-versus-host disease (GVHD) and haemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis (HLH). On 25 February 2026, rovadicitinib received its first approval in China as first-line treatment for adult patients with intermediate-2 or higher-risk PMF, PPV-MF or PET-MF. Introduction

Myelofibrosis is a myeloproliferative neoplasm characterized by constitutional symptoms, splenomegaly and cytopenias [1]. It comprises primary myelofibrosis (PMF) and secondary myelofibrosis, the latter evolving from polycythemia vera (PV) [post-polycythemia vera myelofibrosis (PPV-MF)] or essential thrombocythemia (ET) [post-essential thrombocythemia myelofibrosis (PET-MF)]. Dysregulation of the Janus kinase (JAK)/signal transducers and activators of transcription (STAT) signaling pathway is central to the pathogenesis of myelofibrosis, leading to abnormal megakaryocyte proliferation, inflammatory cytokine release, bone marrow fibrosis and extramedullary haematopoiesis [1]. In addition to JAK/STAT signaling, Rho‑associated coiled‑coil kinases (ROCKs) regulate fibrosis processes by modulating actin–myosin cytoskeletal dynamics and modulate cellular processes including proliferation and survival, which are frequently dysregulated in haematologic malignancies [2, 3].

Key milestones in the development of rovadicitinib, focusing on its use in myelofibrosis

Current management of myelofibrosis includes symptomatic therapy, hydroxyurea, JAK inhibitors and allogeneic stem cell transplantation [1]. Ruxolitinib is a first‑line JAK1/JAK2 inhibitor approved in China for the treatment of patients with intermediate‑ or high‑risk myelofibrosis [4]. However, ≈ 75% of patients discontinue ruxolitinib within 5 years, most commonly due to lack or loss of response and treatment intolerance [4]. Rovadicitinib (Anxu®) is a small molecule JAK/ROCK inhibitor being developed by Chia Tai Tianqing Pharmaceutical Group (a subsidiary of Sino Biopharmaceutical) for the treatment of PMF, PPV-MF, PET-MF, graft-versus-host disease (GVHD) and haemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis (HLH). Rovadicitinib received its first approval on 25 February 2026 in China as first-line treatment for adult patients with intermediate-2 or high-risk PMF, PPV-MF or PET-MF [5, 6].

The recommended starting dose of rovadicitinib is 15 mg twice daily (≥ 8 h apart; 12 h is optimal) [6]. Available as 5 mg tablets, rovadicitinib should be taken orally on an empty stomach. A complete blood count should be performed prior to starting treatment, weekly during the first 4 weeks of treatment, every 2–4 weeks until the dose is stable, then as required based on clinical need. The baseline platelet count must be ≥ 100 × 109/L. Rovadicitinib carries warnings for thrombocytopenia, anaemia, electrolyte disturbances, nephrotoxicity, infections, viral reactivation, QTc interval prolongation, bleeding, major cardiovascular events, thromboembolic events and malignancy. Dose reduction or treatment interruption may be required for haematological adverse reactions, with gradual dose re-escalation after recovery. For non-haematological adverse reactions, treatment interruption (grade ≥ 3) or permanent discontinuation (grade 4) may be required. Consult local prescribing information for details on monitoring recommendations and guidance on dose adjustments. Rovadicitinib is contraindicated during pregnancy and breastfeeding [6].

In March 2026, Chia Tai Tianqing Pharmaceutical entered into an exclusive licensing agreement with Sanofi for rovadicitinib [7]. Under the terms of the agreement, Chia Tai Tianqing Pharmaceutical Group granted Sanofi an exclusive license to develop, manufacture and commercialise rovadicitinib globally. Scientific Summary

Pharmacodynamics

Rovadicitinib is a dual inhibitor of JAK1/JAK2 and ROCK1/ROCK2, leading to suppression of JAK/STAT signaling and modulation of cytokine- and growth factor mediated-pathways involved in haematopoiesis and immune function [6].

In vitro, rovadicitinib inhibited cell proliferation, induced cell apoptosis and reduced inflammatory cytokine expression (including tumour necrosis factor-α, interleukin-1 and monocyte chemoattractant protein-1) through inhibition of JAK2, STAT3 and STAT5 phosphorylation [8]. In vivo, rovadicitinib reduced spleen volume and improved leukocyte and erythrocyte counts in a transplant mouse model. In a murine model of myelofibrosis, treatment with rovadicitinib resulted in reduced splenomegaly, extramedullary haematopoiesis, leukocyte and platelet counts, bone marrow fibrosis and the proportion of common myeloid and megakaryocyte-erythroid progenitors [8].

Pharmacokinetics

Following oral administration of rovadicitinib 15 mg twice daily for 7 days under fasted conditions in patients with myelofibrosis, the drug was rapidly absorbed, with maximum plasma concentrations (Cmax) reached after a median of 0.5 h [6]. Administration of rovadicitinib with food in healthy Chinese subjects reduced systemic exposure, decreasing the area under the curve from time zero to observed infinity (AUC0-∞) by 12.19% and decreasing Cmax by 52.31% [6, 9]. Exposure to the major active metabolite TQ12550 was similarly affected by food, with a 13.08% reduction in AUC0-∞ and a 37.46% reduction in Cmax [6, 9]. At steady state following repeated dosing of 15 mg twice daily for 7 days, rovadicitinib had a mean volume of distribution of 58.08 L and was 95.6% bound to plasma proteins [6].

Rovadicitinib is primarily metabolized by CYP2C9, CYP2C19 and CYP3A4 [6]. In a radiolabeled mass balance study in healthy male subjects, the parent compound accounted for 61.42% of total plasma radioactivity, while TQ12550 contributed 23.54%. Identified metabolic pathways include mono‑oxidation, hydrogenation and dehydrogenation. Following administration of a single radiolabeled dose of rovadicitinib 15 mg in healthy male subjects, 96.8% of the radioactivity was excreted (80.2% in the urine and 16.7% in the faeces). Rovadicitinib has a mean elimination half-life of ≈ 1.3 h and a mean clearance rate of 24.57 L/h [6].

Following single doses of rovadicitinib 15 mg, 10 mg and 5 mg in subjects with mild, moderate and severe renal impairment, rovadicitinib AUC increased by 15.5%, 27.9% and 35.5%, respectively, relative to subjects with normal renal function [6]. Therefore, the use of rovadicitinib in patients with renal impairment is not recommended. The effects of hepatic impairment on the pharmacokinetics of rovadicitinib are unknown; however, the use of rovadicitinib in this patient population is not recommended [6].

Rovadicitinib is a moderate inhibitor of CYP2C8, CYP2C19 and CYP3A4, and a weak inhibitor of CYP1A2, CYP2B6, CYP2C9 and CYP2D6 [6]. Coadministration of rovadicitinib with CYP3A4 inhibitors should be avoided as this is expected to increase rovadicitinib exposure. Coadministration of rovadicitinib with rifampicin (a strong CYP inducer) reduced the Cmax of rovadicitinib and TQ12550 by 55.4% and 54.4%, respectively, and increased the AUC0-∞ of rovadicitinib by 74.3%. Therefore, coadministration of rovadicitinib and CYP inducers should be avoided [6].

Therapeutic Trials

Myelofibrosis

Monotherapy

Rovadicitinib demonstrated significant clinical benefits compared with hydroxyurea in patients with myelofibrosis, according to primary results of a randomized, double-blind, phase II trial (NCT05020652) [10]. As of 15 October 2023, the proportion of patients with a ≥ 35% reduction in spleen volume (SVR35) at week 24 (primary endpoint) was 58% with rovadicitinib and 23% with hydroxyurea (p = 0.0006). The benefit of rovadicitinib over hydroxyurea was seen in all evaluated subgroups. At week 24, the proportion of patients with a ≥ 50% reduction in the Total Symptom Score (TSS50) was 61% with rovadicitinib and 46% with hydroxyurea. The best SVR35 rates were 64% versus 31% (p = 0.0017) and the best TSS50 rates were 78% versus 54% (p = 0.0136) [10].

The trial enrolled patients aged ≥ 18 years with PMF, PPV MF or PET MF [10]. They were considered intermediate-2 or high-risk according to the Dynamic International Prognostic Scoring System (DIPSS), had palpable splenomegaly and had not received prior treatment with a JAK inhibitor. Patients were randomized to receive rovadicitinib 15 mg twice daily (n = 72) or hydroxyurea 0.5 g twice daily (n = 35). Randomization was stratified by DIPSS risk status (intermediate-2 vs high) [10].

Rovadicitinib demonstrated clinical benefit in patients with myelofibrosis who were refractory or relapsed or intolerant to ruxolitinib participating in a multicentre, open-label, single-arm, phase Ib trial (NCT06388759) [4]. At the end of cycle 6, the proportion of patients (n = 8 evaluable) achieving SVR35 was 25% and the median percent change in spleen volume from baseline was – 27%. The proportion of patients achieving TSS50 was 38% [4].

The trial enrolled patients aged ≥ 18 years with PMF, PPV-MF or PET-MF according to the 2016 revision to the WHO classification [4]. All patients had been previously treated with ruxolitinib and were intolerant (n = 5), refractory (n = 2) or relapsed (n = 2). Other inclusion criteria were life expectancy ≥ 6 months; a DIPSS score of ≥ 1 (intermediate- or high-risk); an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0–2; < 10% peripheral blood or bone marrow blasts; a palpable spleen [≥ 5 cm below left costal margin (LCM)]; and adequate renal and hepatic function. The starting dose of rovadicitinib was 15 mg twice daily, with dose reductions (10 mg twice daily or 5 mg twice daily) or interruptions permitted for safety reasons. Treatment continued until discontinuation criteria were met [4].

Rovadicitinib demonstrated meaningful clinical activity in patients with myeloproliferative neoplasms, according to primary results of a multicentre, open-label, single-arm, phase I/Ib, dose-escalation and -expansion trial (NCT04339400) [11]. In phase I, rovadicitinib 15 mg twice daily was determined to be the maximum tolerated dose and recommended phase II dose (primary endpoint). In phase Ib, the proportion of patients achieving SVR35 was 38% at week 24 (primary endpoint) and 40% at week 48. The mean duration of splenic response was ≈ 16 months. TSS50 was achieved by 71% of patients at week 24 and by 48% of patients at week 48 [11].

The trial enrolled patients aged ≥ 18 years with myeloproliferative neoplasms and an intermediate-1 or higher risk of myelofibrosis according to the DIPSS [11]. Patients with myelofibrosis were required to have palpable splenomegaly (≥ 5 cm below LCM) and patients with PV or ET were naïve or resistant/intolerant to hydroxyurea and/or interferon therapy. Eligible patients received escalating doses of oral rovadicitinib 5 mg once daily, 5 mg twice daily, 10 mg twice daily, 15 mg twice daily, 20 mg twice daily of each 28-day cycle. Treatment continued until progressive disease or unacceptable toxicity. At baseline, 64 patients had myelofibrosis, including PMF (n = 55), PPV-MF (n = 5) and PET-MF (n = 4), ten patients had ET and five patients had PV [11].

Combination Therapy

Rovadicitinib in combination with TBQ 3617 (a bromodomain and extraterminal domain protein inhibitor) demonstrated clinical activity in patients with myelofibrosis, according to primary results of a phase Ib/II trial (NCT06122831) [12]. In phase Ib (n = 11–12 evaluable), a starting dose of rovadicitinib 10 mg twice daily in combination with TBQ 3617 0.1 mg once daily was determined to be the recommended phase II dose (primary endpoint), with the dose of rovadicitinib increased to 15 mg twice daily after 6 weeks based on platelet and neutrophil counts. After 24 weeks of treatment, 50% of patients achieved SVR35 and 67% achieved TSS50. The mean haemoglobin level increased from 114 g/L at baseline to 130 g/L at week 24. At week 24, the JAK2 V617F allele burden decreased in 40% (4/10) of patients and the myelofibrosis grade improved in 45% (5/11) of patients. In phase II, the proportion of patients achieving SVR35 at week 24 (primary endpoint) was 92% in cohort 1 (n = 12 evaluable) and 25% in cohort 2 (n = 4 evaluable). TSS50 was achieved by 45% of patients in cohort 1 and by 100% of patients in cohort 2 [12].

The trial enrolled patients aged ≥ 18 years with PMF, PPV-MF or PET-MF who were either JAK inhibitor-naïve (cohort 1) or had a suboptimal response to JAK inhibitor treatment (cohort 2) [12]. They were classified as intermediate-1, intermediate-2 or high-risk on the DIPSS and had palpable splenomegaly. In phase Ib, patients received rovadicitinib 10 mg or 15 mg twice daily (administered consecutively for 21 days) plus TBQ 3617 0.05 mg or 0.1 mg once daily (14 days on and 7 days off) in each 21-day cycle. In phase II, patients received treatment at the recommended phase II dose (10 mg twice daily) [12].

Rovadicitinib in combination with TBQ 3909 (a B-cell lymphoma-2 inhibitor) demonstrated meaningful clinical activity in patients with myelofibrosis participating in a multicentre, open-label, phase Ib/II dose-escalation and -expansion trial (NCT06245941) [13]. The proportion of patients achieving SVR35 at week 24 (primary endpoint) was 67% in the cohort receiving rovadicitinib plus TBQ 3909 (n = 6 evaluable) versus 20% in the cohort receiving ruxolitinib plus TBQ 3909 (n = 5 evaluable). Overall, TSS50 was achieved in 75% of patients (n = 12 evaluable). The proportion of patients achieving a ≥15 g/L increase from baseline in haemoglobin level was 57% in the rovadicitinib cohort (n = 9 evaluable) and 43% in the ruxolitinib cohort (n = 7 evaluable) [13].

The trial enrolled patients aged ≥ 18 years with PMF, PPV-MF or PET-MF who were either JAK inhibitor-naïve or -resistant [13]. All patients were classified as intermediate-1, intermediate-2 or high-risk on DIPSS and had palpable splenomegaly (≥ 5 cm below LCM or spleen volume ≥ 450 cm3). Dose escalation of TBQ 3909 (100 mg, 200 mg or 300 mg once daily) was first assessed in the ruxolitinib cohort, followed by a 3 + 3 dose-escalation design in the rovadicitinib cohort (10 mg or 15 mg twice daily). The dose of TBQ 3909 was fixed at 200 mg once daily in the rovadicitinib cohort [13].

Graft-Versus-Host Disease

Rovadicitinib elicited a high rate of clinical response in patients with chronic GVHD (cGVHD) participating in a multicentre, open-label, phase Ib/IIa trial (NCT04944043) [14]. In phase Ib, rovadicitinib 10 mg twice daily was determined to be the recommended phase II dose (primary endpoint). In phase IIa (data cutoff 31 March 2023; median treatment duration 7.8 months), the best overall response (BOR) at any time up to week 24, defined as the proportion of patients who achieved complete response (CR) or partial response (PR) according to the 2014 National Institutes of Health (NIH) criteria (key secondary endpoint), was 86%, with a CR rate of 9% and a PR rate of 77%. The BOR was 90% with rovadicitinib 10 mg twice daily and 80% with rovadicitinib 15 mg twice daily. The overall response rate (ORR) at week 24 was 66%, with a CR rate of 9% and a PR rate of 57%. Responses were rapid, with 82% of responders achieving a response at 4 weeks. At 12 months, the overall modified failure-free survival (FFS) rate was 85% and the overall survival (OS) rate was 90%. Most (89%) patients were able to reduce their corticosteroid dose during treatment with rovadicitinib [14].

The trial enrolled allogeneic haematopoietic stem cell transplant recipients aged ≥ 12 years with persistent cGVHD (n = 44) [14]. Other inclusion criteria were a Karnofsky performance status of ≥ 60; a clinical diagnosis of moderate or severe corticosteroid-refractory or -dependent cGVHD according to the NIH consensus criteria; and ≥ 1 prior line of therapy, including systemic corticosteroids. In phase Ib, a standard 3 + 3 design was used to determine the recommended phase II dose. The initial dose of rovadicitinib was 10 mg twice daily, reduced to 5 mg twice daily in the event of unacceptable toxicity, and increased to 15 mg twice daily in the absence of dose-limiting toxicities. In phase IIa, patients continued to receive their phase Ib dose of oral rovadicitinib continuously for ≥ 6 cycles (28 days per cycle) until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity [14].

In an updated analysis (data cutoff 31 December 2023; median treatment duration 27.7 months), the BOR was 97% with rovadicitinib 10 mg twice daily and 80% with rovadicitinib 15 mg twice daily [15]. Responses were rapid, with 72% and 67% of responders achieving a response at 4 weeks. At week 24, the ORR was 76% with rovadicitinib 10 mg twice daily and 47% with rovadicitinib 15 mg twice daily. The 12-month FFS rate was 88% [15].

Rovadicitinib demonstrated clinical efficacy in patients with acute GVHD (aGVHD) participating in a multicentre, open-label, single-arm, phase Ib trial (NCT04941404) [16]. The ORR at day 28 (primary endpoint) was 85%, with a CR rate of 69% and a very good PR rate of 15%. The time to first response ranged from 3–21 days (median 4 days). At 12 months, the OS rate was 92%. The trial enrolled patients with grade II or III/IV steroid-refractory aGVHD. All patients received oral rovadicitinib 10 mg twice daily [16].

Haemophagocytic Lymphohistiocytosis

Rovadicitinib in combination with glucocorticoids demonstrated clinical activity in patients with HLH participating in an open-label, single-arm, phase I trial (NCT04326348) [17]. At week 8, the ORR was 55% (n = 11 evaluable). The trial enrolled patients aged ≥ 18 years with new or relapsed HLH according to the HLH-2004 criteria. Other inclusion criteria included an ECOG performance status of 0–3; life expectancy > 3 months; and interruption of other HLH treatment ≥ 1 week prior to the first dose of rovadicitinib. Patients received escalating doses of rovadicitinib 15 mg twice daily, 20 mg twice daily, 25 mg twice daily, 30 mg twice daily of each 28-day cycle. Treatment continued for 8 weeks or until progressive disease, unacceptable toxicity or the patient was judged by the investigator to be ineligible to continue, whichever came first [17].

Adverse Events

Safety data for rovadicitinib were derived from nine clinical trials (n = 338), including five trials in myeloproliferative neoplasms (PMF, PPV-MF, PET-MF), three trials in GVHD and one trial in HLH [6]. The median dosage of rovadicitinib was 25.31 mg/day and the median duration of exposure was 10.85 months. In this pooled safety population, the most common adverse reactions were hypofibrinogenaemia, hypocalcaemia, hyperkalaemia, hypokalaemia, hyperglycaemia, hypoalbuminaemia, hyperphosphataemia, hyponatraemia, decreased appetite, upper respiratory tract infection, thrombocytopenia, leukopenia, neutropenia, lymphopenia, elevated bilirubin and elevated creatinine [6].

Rovadicitinib was generally well tolerated relative to hydroxyurea in patients with myelofibrosis participating in the phase II trial discussed in Sect. 2.3.1.1 (NCT05020652) [10]. The most common grade ≥ 3 haematological treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs) with rovadicitinib were anaemia (20% vs 60% with hydroxyurea), thrombocytopenia (20% vs 17%), decreased lymphocyte count (8% vs 11%), leukopenia (4% vs 17%) and neutropenia (4% vs 17%). The most common non-haematological TEAEs with rovadicitinib were hyperuricaemia (27% vs 17% with hydroxyurea), weight gain (27% vs 14%) and increased blood creatinine (18% vs 3%), most of which were grade 1 or 2 in severity. TEAEs led to discontinuation of treatment in 8% of rovadicitinib recipients and 14% of hydroxyurea recipients [10].

Ongoing Clinical Trials

In addition to the ongoing trials discussed in Sect. 2.3, several other trials are underway. Two multicentre, randomized, open-label trials are currently recruiting patients and plan to evaluate the efficacy and safety of rovadicitinib for third-line and subsequent treatment of moderate to severe cGVHD. One is an active-controlled, phase III trial (NCT06682169) comparing rovadicitinib with an active comparator (imatinib, methotrexate, mycophenolate mofetil or rituximab), while the other is a single-arm, phase II trial (NCT06300320). Also underway is a multicentre, single-arm, phase Ib/II trial (CTR20244567) evaluating the efficacy and safety of rovadicitinib in patients with macrophage activation syndrome and other secondary HLH.

Current Status

Rovadicitinib received its first approval in China on 25 February 2026 as first-line treatment for adult patients with intermediate-2 or high-risk PMF, PPV-MF or PET-MF [5].

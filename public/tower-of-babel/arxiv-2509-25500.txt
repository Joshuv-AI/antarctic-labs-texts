1.
Introduction

The motivation for this paper centers around the following basic question:

Question 1
.

Suppose that
R>1
and
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
has its Fourier transform supported in the width-one annulus
A_{R}=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|\xi|\in[R,R+1]\}
. For which sets
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
does it hold that

(1.1)

\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}(E)}

where
C>0
is independent of
f
and
R
?

Since the width-one annulus contains a strip of length approximately
\sqrt{R}
and width
1/2
in any direction, one can show (see
[
10
]
) that a necessary condition for
E
is that it satisfies the
geometric control condition
(GCC): there exists
L>0
and
\theta>0
such that for any line segment
\ell\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
of length
L
, we have
m(E\cap\ell)\geq\theta\cdot L
. Here
m
is the
1
-dimensional Lebesgue measure on the segment
\ell
. The principal question is whether the GCC is sufficient:

Question 2
.

Suppose that
E
satisfies the GCC. Is it true that there exists
C>0
such that for every
R>1
and
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\operatorname{supp}(\widehat{f})\subset A_{R}
we have

\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}(E)}?

The motivation for considering this problem comes from a problem in control theory considered by Burq-Joly
[
4
]
. For a non-negative function
\gamma
we consider the damped wave equation: For
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times[0,\infty)]
, let
w
satisfy

(1.2)

w_{tt}(x,t)+\gamma(x)w_{t}(x,t)+\Delta w(x,t)=0.

The damping force is represented by
\gamma w_{t}
, and the energy of the solution
w
is given by
E(t)=\|u\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|\partial_{t}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
.
Standard analysis shows that if
\gamma=0
, then the energy is conserved, i.e., there is no decay. On the other hand, for constant damping
\gamma=c>0
, it can be shown that
E(t)
decays exponentially in
t
. A classical question is to understand for which functions
\gamma
does
E(t)
decay exponentially. In
[
4
]
it is proved for
uniformly continuous

\gamma
, that the energy decays exponentially if there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that the level set
\{\gamma>\varepsilon\}
satisfies the GCC. They asked whether one can drop the assumption of uniform continuity, which would follow from Question
2
. This application was the motivation for Walton Green, Mishko Mitkovski, and the first author to formulate Question
2
in
[
10
]
, where it was proved that the answer to Question
2
is affirmative if one replaces
E
by its
\delta
-neighborhood for any
\delta>0
(with a constant that blows up as
\delta\to 0^{+}
, see
[
10
]
). Analogues of the GCC in compact manifolds have long played a role in control theory for hyperbolic equations, for instance see Bardos, Lebeau, Rauch
[
1
,
23
]
, and various uncertainity principles have played an essential role in control theory
[
3
,
12
,
23
]
.

In this paper we consider the radial case of Question
2
and show that it has a positive answer in this case, even with a considerably weaker condition than the GCC.

For
\alpha>-1/2
, the Fourier-Bessel transform is defined by

\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(y)=\int_{0}^{\infty}f(x)j_{\alpha}(2\pi xy)\,d\mu_{\alpha}(x),

where
j_{\alpha}
is the modified Bessel function given by

j_{\alpha}(x)=\Gamma(\alpha+1)\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n!\,\Gamma(n+\alpha+1)}\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^{2n}=\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+1)}{\Gamma\left(\alpha+\frac{1}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)}\int_{-1}^{1}(1-x^{2})^{\alpha-1/2}\,\cos(sx)\,dx,

and

d\mu_{\alpha}(x)=\frac{2\pi^{\alpha+1}}{\Gamma(\alpha+1)}\,x^{2\alpha+1}\,dx.

The Fourier-Bessel transform occurs naturally when one studies the Fourier transforms of radial functions. If
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is a radial function, i.e.,
f(x)=F(|x|)
, then a standard calculation shows that
\widehat{f}(\xi)=\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(F)(|\xi|)
for
\alpha=\frac{d}{2}-1
.

For
1\leqslant p<\infty
,
L^{p}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
consists of the measurable functions
f:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\|f\|_{L^{p}_{\alpha}}:=\Bigl(\,\int_{0}^{\infty}|f(x)|^{p}\,d\mu_{\alpha}(x)\Bigl)^{1/p}<\infty.

The Fourier-Bessel transform extends to an isometry on
L^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
, i.e.,
\|\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)\|_{L^{2}_{\alpha}}=\|f\|_{L^{2}_{\alpha}}
.

With this notation, our main result reads as follows:

Theorem 1.3
.

Suppose
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{+}
is relatively dense with respect to
\mu_{\alpha}
, meaning that

\text{there is }\gamma>0\text{ such that }\mu_{\alpha}(E\cap[r,r+1])\geqslant\gamma\mu_{\alpha}([r,r+1])\text{ for all }r\geqslant 0.

There exists
C>0
such that for all sufficiently large
R>0
, if
\operatorname{supp}\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)\subset[R,R+1]
, then

\|f\|_{L^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{+})}\leqslant C\|f\|_{L^{2}_{\alpha}(E)},

where
C
depends on
\alpha
and
\gamma
but not on
R
.

If
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfies the GCC, then the spherical average of
E

g(r)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\chi_{E}(r\omega)d\omega

has a superlevel set that is relatively dense

Theorem 1.4

Let

Observe that the constant in Theorem
1.4
grows exponentially with the parameter

(and necessarily so). Consequently, a naïve application of Theorem
1.4
to the interval
[0,R+1]
to treat the case

would yield estimates that deteriorate as

1.4

We now describe briefly how Theorem
1.3

We study the decay rate of the energy

E(t):=\|(w(t),w_{t}(t))\|_{H^{s/2}\times L^{2}}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|(-\Delta+1)^{s/4}\,w(x,t)|^{2}+|w_{t}(x,t)|^{2}\,dx\right)^{1/2}.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let

s>0
, and
\alpha=\tfrac{d}{2}-1

E(t)\;\leqslant\;\begin{cases}C\,(1+t)^{-\frac{s}{4-2s}}\;\bigl\|\,u(0),u_{t}(0)\bigr\|_{H^{s}\times H^{s/2}},&0<s<2,\\[5.0pt]

, where

.

1.3

Theorem 1.8

Suppose
\alpha-\frac{1}{2}\in\mathbb{N}

\mu_{\alpha}

\text{there is }\gamma>0\text{ such that }\mu_{\alpha}(E\cap[r,r+1])\geqslant\gamma\mu_{\alpha}([r,r+1])\text{ for all }r\geqslant 0.

Then, if
\operatorname{supp}\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)\subset\bigcup_{k=1}^{N}I_{k}
, where the

\|f\|_{L^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{+})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{2}_{\alpha}(E)},

To prove Theorem
1.3
, we first employ the asymptotic expansion of the Bessel function, which allows us to apply Kovrizhkin’s multi-interval version of the Paneah–Logvinenko–Sereda theorem
[

. By contrast, the proof of Theorem

2.
Proof of Theorem
1.3

Note that
\mu_{\alpha}
-relative density and Lebesgue relative density are equivalent conditions on the set
E
. The former asks for
\mu_{\alpha}(E\cap[r,r+1])\gtrsim\mu_{\alpha}([r,r+1])
for all
r\geqslant 0
, while the latter asks for
|E\cap[r,r+1]\gtrsim 1
for all
r\geqslant 0
. We record the proof in the following proposition.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\alpha>-1/2
. A set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{+}
is
\mu_{\alpha}
-relatively dense if and only if it is Lebesgue relatively dense.

Proof.

Suppose
\gamma>0
is such that
|E\cap[r,r+1]|\geqslant\gamma
for all
r\geqslant 0
. We will find
\widetilde{\gamma}>0
such that
\mu_{\alpha}(E\cap[r,r+1])\geqslant\widetilde{\gamma}\mu_{\alpha}([r,r+1])
. Using
r^{2\alpha+1}\leqslant x^{2\alpha+1}\leqslant(r+1)^{2\alpha+1},
we have the inequalities

c_{1}r^{2\alpha+1}|E\cap[r,r+1]|\leqslant\mu_{\alpha}(E\cap[r,r+1])\leqslant c_{1}(r+1)^{2\alpha+1}\,|E\cap[r,r+1]|,

and

c_{1}r^{2\alpha+1}\leqslant\mu_{\alpha}([r,r+1])\leqslant c_{1}(r+1)^{2\alpha+1},

where
c_{1}:=\frac{2\pi^{\alpha+1}}{\Gamma(\alpha+1)}
. Combining the two, we get

\frac{\mu_{\alpha}(E\cap[r,r+1])}{\mu_{\alpha}([r,r+1])}\geqslant\gamma\left(\frac{r}{r+1}\right)^{2\alpha+1}\geqslant\gamma\left(\frac{\frac{\gamma}{2}}{\frac{\gamma}{2}+1}\right)^{2\alpha+1}>0,

when
r\geqslant\frac{\gamma}{2}
. If
r\leqslant\frac{\gamma}{2}
, we split
I:=[r,r+1]
as
I=J\cup J^{\prime}
, where
J=[r+\frac{\gamma}{2},r+1]
and
J^{\prime}=[r,r+\frac{\gamma}{2}]
. We have

\mu_{\alpha}(E\cap I)\geqslant\mu_{\alpha}(E\cap J)=c_{1}\int_{E\cap J}x^{2\alpha+1}\,dx\geqslant c_{1}\left(\frac{\gamma}{2}\right)^{2\alpha+1}|E\cap J|\geqslant c_{1}\left(\frac{\gamma}{2}\right)^{2\alpha+2},

as

|E\cap J|=|E\cap I|-|E\cap J^{\prime}|\geqslant\gamma-\frac{\gamma}{2}=\frac{\gamma}{2}.

On the other hand,

\mu_{\alpha}(I)=c_{1}\int_{I}x^{2\alpha+1}\,dx\leqslant c_{1}(r+1)^{2\alpha+1}\leqslant c_{1}\left(\frac{\gamma}{2}+1\right)^{2\alpha+1},

giving

\frac{\mu_{\alpha}(E\cap[r,r+1])}{\mu_{\alpha}([r,r+1])}\geqslant\frac{\gamma}{2}\left(\frac{\frac{\gamma}{2}}{\frac{\gamma}{2}+1}\right)^{2\alpha+1},

for all
r\leqslant\frac{\gamma}{2}
. We obtain
\mu_{\alpha}(E\cap[r,r+1])\geqslant\widetilde{\gamma}\mu_{\alpha}([r,r+1])
with
\widetilde{\gamma}:=\frac{\gamma}{2}\left(\frac{\frac{\gamma}{2}}{\frac{\gamma}{2}+1}\right)^{2\alpha+1}
. The reverse implication is entirely analogous.
∎

We will use the following multi-interval Logvinenko-Sereda theorem for the Fourier transform, due to Kovrizhkin.

Theorem 2.2

(
[
13
]
)
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}
satisfy

|E\cap[r,r+1]|\geqslant\gamma,

for some constant
\gamma>0
.
Let
\{J_{k}\}_{k=1}^{N}
be intervals with
|J_{k}|=b
. If
1\leqslant p\leqslant\infty
and
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
with
\operatorname{supp}\widehat{f}\subset\bigcup_{k=1}^{N}J_{k}

\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R})}\leqslant\left(\frac{\gamma}{C}\right)^{-ab\left(\frac{C}{\gamma}\right)^{N}-N+\frac{p-1}{p}}\,\|f\|_{L^{p}(E)}.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Without loss of generality, consider
f\in L^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{+})\cap L^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
, and assume
\operatorname{supp}\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)\subset[R,R+1]
for some
R>0
. Fourier-Bessel inversion gives

f(t)=c_{\alpha}\int_{R}^{R+1}\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(y)j_{\alpha}(2\pi ty)\,y^{2\alpha+1}\,dy,

where
c_{\alpha}=\frac{2\pi^{\alpha+1}}{\Gamma(\alpha+1)}.
As
t\to\infty
, we have the following asymptotic,

j_{\alpha}(t)=A_{\alpha}\,t^{-\alpha-1/2}\cos\!\left(t-\delta\right)+O(t^{-\alpha-3/2}),

where
A_{\alpha}=\frac{2^{\alpha+1/2}\Gamma(\alpha+1)}{\sqrt{\pi}}
and
\delta=(2\alpha+1)\frac{\pi}{4}
.

Through a change of variables
y=R+s
, we get

f(t)=c_{\alpha}\int_{0}^{1}\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(R+s)j_{\alpha}(2\pi(R+s)t)\,(R+s)^{2\alpha+1}\,ds.

So,

\|f\|_{L^{2}_{\alpha}}^{2}=c_{\alpha}^{3}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\int_{0}^{1}\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(R+s)j_{\alpha}(2\pi(R+s)t)\,(R+s)^{2\alpha+1}\,ds\right|^{2}\,t^{2\alpha+1}\,dt.

We can cut out
[0,\varepsilon]
for small enough
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. Indeed, consider

I_{\varepsilon}:=\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\left|\int_{0}^{1}\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(R+s)j_{\alpha}(2\pi(R+s)t)\,(R+s)^{2\alpha+1}\,ds\right|^{2}\,t^{2\alpha+1}\,dt.

We will use the estimate

(2.3)

|j_{\alpha}(t)|\lesssim_{\alpha}(1+t)^{-\alpha-1/2}

from
[
8
]
. Since
\alpha>-1/2
, this implies
|j_{\alpha}(t)|\lesssim_{\alpha}t^{-\alpha-1/2}
which is all we need. We have

\displaystyle I_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\left(\int_{0}^{1}|\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(R+s)||j_{\alpha}(2\pi(R+s)t)|\,(R+s)^{2\alpha+1}\,ds\right)^{2}\,t^{2\alpha+1}\,dt

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\left(\int_{0}^{1}|\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(R+s)|(R+s)^{\alpha+1/2}\,ds\right)^{2}\,dt

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{1}|\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(R+s)|^{2}(R+s)^{2\alpha+1}\,ds\,dt

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon\,\|\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\alpha}}\lesssim\varepsilon\,\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\alpha}},

using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality.

Now, consider

J_{1,\varepsilon}:=\int_{\varepsilon}^{\infty}\left|\int_{0}^{1}\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(R+s)((R+s)t)^{-\alpha-1/2}\cos(2\pi(R+s)t-\delta)\,(R+s)^{2\alpha+1}\,ds\right|^{2}\,t^{2\alpha+1}\,dt,

and

J_{2,\varepsilon}:=\int_{\varepsilon}^{\infty}\left|\int_{0}^{1}\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(R+s)((R+s)t)^{-\alpha-3/2}\,(R+s)^{2\alpha+1}\,ds\right|^{2}\,t^{2\alpha+1}\,dt.

The error term
J_{2,\varepsilon}
is easily controlled as follows:

\displaystyle J_{2,\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\int_{\varepsilon}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{t^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}|\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(R+s)|^{2}(R+s)^{-2}(R+s)^{2\alpha+1}\,ds

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{\varepsilon R^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}|\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(R+s)|^{2}(R+s)^{2\alpha+1}\,ds\lesssim\frac{\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\alpha}}}{\varepsilon R^{2}}.

Now, we focus on
J_{1,\varepsilon}
. The powers of
t
cancel to give

J_{1,\varepsilon}=\int_{\varepsilon}^{\infty}\left|\int_{0}^{1}\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(R+s)(R+s)^{\alpha+1/2}\cos(2\pi(R+s)t-\delta)\,ds\right|^{2}\,dt.

For convenience, let
h(s):=\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(R+s)(R+s)^{\alpha+1/2}
so that
\int_{0}^{\infty}|h(s)|^{2}\,ds\approx\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\alpha}}
and
\operatorname{supp}h\subset[0,1]
. Also, define

g(t):=\int_{0}^{1}h(s)\cos(2\pi(R+s)t-\delta)\,ds.

Then,
J_{1,\varepsilon}=\int_{\varepsilon}^{\infty}|g(t)|^{2}\,dt
. Writing
\cos(2\pi Rt-\delta+2\pi st)=\cos(2\pi Rt-\delta)\cos(2\pi st)-\sin(2\pi Rt-\delta)\sin(2\pi st)
, we get

\displaystyle g(t)

\displaystyle=\cos(2\pi Rt-\delta)\int_{0}^{1}h(s)\,\cos(2\pi st)\,ds-\sin(2\pi Rt-\delta)\int_{0}^{1}h(s)\,\sin(2\pi st)\,ds

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{i(2\pi Rt-\delta)}}{2}\left(\int_{0}^{1}h(s)\,\cos(2\pi st)\,ds+i\int_{0}^{1}h(s)\,\sin(2\pi st)\,ds\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{e^{-i(2\pi Rt-\delta)}}{2}\left(\int_{0}^{1}h(s)\,\cos(2\pi st)\,ds-i\int_{0}^{1}h(s)\,\sin(2\pi st)\,ds\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{i(2\pi Rt-\delta)}}{2}\int_{0}^{1}h(s)\,e^{2\pi ist}\,ds+\frac{e^{-i(2\pi Rt-\delta)}}{2}\int_{0}^{1}h(s)\,e^{-2\pi ist}\,ds.

Define
H(t):=\int_{0}^{1}h(s)\,e^{2\pi ist}\,ds
, so that

g(t)=\frac{e^{i(2\pi Rt-\delta)}}{2}H(t)+\frac{e^{-i(2\pi Rt-\delta)}}{2}\overline{H(t)}.

Note that
H(t)=\widehat{h}\left(-t\right)
and
\widehat{H}(\xi)=h(\xi)
, implying
\operatorname{supp}\widehat{H}\subset[0,1]
. As
\widehat{\overline{H}}(\xi)=\overline{\widehat{H}}(-\xi)
, we also have
\operatorname{supp}\widehat{\overline{H}}\subset[-1,0]
. Using Theorem
2.2

[
13
]
with

, we get

\|g\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|g\|_{L^{2}(E)},

for
R\geqslant 2
. Thus,
J_{1,\varepsilon}\leqslant\|g\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|g\|_{L^{2}(E)}^{2}.
Finally, we would like to show
\|g\|_{L^{2}(E)}^{2}\lesssim\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\alpha}(E)}
.

Define
\widetilde{j_{\alpha}}(t):=A_{\alpha}t^{-\alpha-1/2}\cos(t-\delta)
. Note that

\displaystyle\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\alpha}(E)}

\displaystyle\approx\int_{E}|f(t)|^{2}\,t^{2\alpha+1}\,dt

\displaystyle\approx\int_{E}\left|\int_{0}^{1}\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(R+s)\,j_{\alpha}(2\pi(R+s)t)\,(R+s)^{2\alpha+1}ds\right|^{2}\,t^{2\alpha+1}\,dt,

and

\displaystyle\|g\|^{2}_{L^{2}(E)}

\displaystyle=\int_{E}\left|\int_{0}^{1}\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(R+s)(R+s)^{\alpha+1/2}\cos(2\pi(R+s)t-\delta)\,ds\right|^{2}\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{E}\left|\int_{0}^{1}\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(R+s)((R+s)t)^{-\alpha-1/2}\cos(2\pi(R+s)t-\delta)\,(R+s)^{2\alpha+1}ds\right|^{2}\,t^{2\alpha+1}dt

\displaystyle\approx\int_{E}\left|\int_{0}^{1}\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(R+s)\,\widetilde{j}_{\alpha}(2\pi(R+s)t)\,(R+s)^{2\alpha+1}ds\right|^{2}\,t^{2\alpha+1}dt.

Once again, we can cut out
[0,\varepsilon]
, since

\displaystyle\|g\|^{2}_{L^{2}(E\cap[0,\varepsilon])}

\displaystyle=\int_{E\cap[0,\varepsilon]}\left|\int_{0}^{1}\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(R+s)(R+s)^{\alpha+1/2}\cos(2\pi(R+s)t-\delta)\,ds\right|^{2}\,dt

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{E\cap[0,\varepsilon]}\left(\int_{0}^{1}|\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(R+s)|(R+s)^{\alpha+1/2}\,ds\right)^{2}\,dt

\displaystyle\leqslant|E\cap[0,\varepsilon]|\int_{0}^{1}|\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(R+s)|^{2}(R+s)^{2\alpha+1}\,ds\lesssim\varepsilon\,\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\alpha}}.

For the other part, we have

\displaystyle\|g\|_{L^{2}(E\cap(\varepsilon,\infty))}

\displaystyle\approx\left(\int_{E\cap(\varepsilon,\infty)}\left|\int_{0}^{1}\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(R+s)\,\widetilde{j}_{\alpha}(2\pi(R+s)t)\,(R+s)^{2\alpha+1}ds\right|^{2}\,t^{2\alpha+1}dt\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\clubsuit_{1}^{1/2}+\clubsuit_{2}^{1/2}

where

\clubsuit_{1}:=\int_{E\cap(\varepsilon,\infty)}\left|\int_{0}^{1}\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(R+s)\,(\widetilde{j}_{\alpha}(2\pi(R+s)t)-{j}_{\alpha}(2\pi(R+s)t))\,(R+s)^{2\alpha+1}ds\right|^{2}\,t^{2\alpha+1}\,dt,

and

\clubsuit_{2}:=\int_{E\cap(\varepsilon,\infty)}\left|\int_{0}^{1}\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(R+s)\,{j}_{\alpha}(2\pi(R+s)t)\,(R+s)^{2\alpha+1}ds\right|^{2}\,t^{2\alpha+1}\,dt.

We estimate
\clubsuit_{2}
trivially, using
\clubsuit_{2}\approx\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\alpha}(E\cap(\varepsilon,\infty))}\leqslant\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\alpha}(E)},
and
\clubsuit_{1}
as follows.

\displaystyle\clubsuit_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{E\cap(\varepsilon,\infty)}\left(\int_{0}^{1}|\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(R+s)|\,((R+s)t)^{-\alpha-3/2}\,(R+s)^{2\alpha+1}ds\right)^{2}\,t^{2\alpha+1}\,dt

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{E\cap(\varepsilon,\infty)}\left(\int_{0}^{1}|\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(R+s)|\,(R+s)^{\alpha+1/2}(R+s)^{-1}\,ds\right)^{2}t^{-2}\,dt

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{E\cap(\varepsilon,\infty)}\frac{dt}{t^{2}}\,\left(\int_{0}^{1}|\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(R+s)|^{2}(R+s)^{2\alpha+1}\,ds\right)\left(\int_{0}^{1}\frac{ds}{(R+s)^{2}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\alpha}}}{R^{2}}\int_{E\cap(\varepsilon,\infty)}\frac{dt}{t^{2}}\leqslant\frac{\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\alpha}}}{\varepsilon R^{2}}.

Combining these estimates, we get

\displaystyle\|g\|^{2}_{L^{2}(E)}\lesssim\varepsilon\,\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\alpha}}+\left(\frac{\|f\|_{L^{2}_{\alpha}}}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}R}+\|f\|_{L^{2}_{\alpha}(E)}\right)^{2}\leqslant\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\alpha}}\left(\varepsilon+\frac{1}{\varepsilon R^{2}}\right)+\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\alpha}(E)}.

As
\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\alpha}}\lesssim I_{\varepsilon}+J_{1,\varepsilon}+J_{2,\varepsilon}
, we have

\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\alpha}}\lesssim\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\alpha}}\left(\varepsilon+\frac{1}{\varepsilon R^{2}}\right)+\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\alpha}(E)}.

Now select
\varepsilon=\frac{1}{R}
, then

\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\alpha}}\lesssim\frac{1}{R}\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\alpha}}+\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\alpha}(E)},

for all
R\geqslant 1
. Then, for large
R
, we get
\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\alpha}}\lesssim\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\alpha}(E)}
with constants independent of
R
, as desired. More precisely, suppose
C>0
is such that
\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\alpha}}\leqslant C(\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\alpha}}R^{-1}+\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\alpha}(E)})
. Then, for all
R\geqslant 2C
,

\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\alpha}}\leqslant 2C\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\alpha}(E)}\lesssim\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\alpha}(E)}.

The theorem is proved.
∎

Proof of Theorem

The proof is inspired by related work of Kovrizhkin
[
13
]
, and a part of the proof of Theorem
1.8
relies on Nazarov’s Turan inequality as stated in Lemma 3 of
[
13
]
. We record it below for convenience.

Lemma 3.1
.

If
r(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{N}p_{k}(x)e^{2\pi i\lambda_{k}x}
, where
p_{k}(x)
is a polynomial of degree
\leqslant M-1
and
E\subset I
is measurable with
|E|>0
, then

(3.2)

\|r\|_{L^{p}(I)}\leqslant\left(\frac{C|I|}{|E|}\right)^{NM-\frac{p-1}{p}}\,\|r\|_{L^{p}(E)}.

We also use the following Bernstein inequality of Ghobber and Jaming
[
8
]
to tackle the low-frequency block:

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{+}
be a
\mu_{\alpha}
-relatively dense set, i.e., for some
\gamma>0
,

\mu_{\alpha}(E\cap[r,r+1])\geqslant\gamma\mu_{\alpha}([r,r+1]),

for all
r\geqslant 0
. Let
f\in L^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
such that
\operatorname{supp}\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)\subset[0,R]
, and
g(x):=f(\sqrt{x})
. Then,

(3.4)

\int_{0}^{\infty}|g^{(k)}(s)|^{2}\,s^{\alpha+k}\,ds\leqslant(\pi R)^{2k}\int_{0}^{\infty}|g(s)|^{2}\,s^{\alpha}\,ds.

Proof of Theorem
1.8
.

Assume
I_{k}:=[\lambda_{k},\lambda_{k}+1]
. Without loss of generality, we may assume
\lambda_{1}\in[0,1)
(otherwise, the proof that follows can be significantly simplified). We use Fourier-Bessel inversion to get

f(t)=c_{\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(y)j_{\alpha}(2\pi ty)\,y^{2\alpha+1}\,dy=c_{\alpha}\,\sum_{k=1}^{N}\int_{I_{k}}\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(y)j_{\alpha}(2\pi ty)\,y^{2\alpha+1}\,dy,

and

\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\alpha}}=c_{\alpha}^{3}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(y)j_{\alpha}(2\pi ty)\,y^{2\alpha+1}\,dy\right|^{2}\,t^{2\alpha+1}\,dt.

First, we rewrite the Bessel kernel using Cauchy’s integral theorem. Using the Poisson representation,

j_{\alpha}(s)=\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+1)}{\Gamma\left(\alpha+\frac{1}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)}\int_{-1}^{1}e^{isx}\,(1-x^{2})^{\alpha-1/2}\,dx.

A classical contour integration argument
[
21
]
yields

j_{\alpha}(s)=\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+1)}{\Gamma\left(\alpha+\frac{1}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)}(-I_{+}(s)-I_{-}(s)),

where

I_{+}(s)=ie^{is}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-sy}(1-(1+iy)^{2})^{\alpha-1/2}\,dy,

and

I_{-}(s)=-ie^{-is}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-sy}(1-(-1+iy)^{2})^{\alpha-1/2}\,dy.

Note that
(1-(1+iy)^{2})^{m}=(y^{2}-2iy)^{m}=\sum_{j=m}^{2m}a_{j}y^{j}
, and
(1-(-1+iy)^{2})^{m}=(y^{2}+2iy)^{m}=\sum_{j=m}^{2m}b_{j}y^{j}
for appropriate constants
a_{j},b_{j}\ \in\mathbb{C}
. This gives,

I_{+}(s)=ie^{is}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-sy}\,\sum_{j=m}^{2m}a_{j}y^{j}\,dy=ie^{is}\sum_{j=m}^{2m}j!\,a_{j}s^{-j-1},

and similarly,

I_{-}(s)=-ie^{-is}\sum_{j=m}^{2m}j!\,b_{j}s^{-j-1}.

Therefore,

j_{\alpha}(s)=\sum_{\pm}\sum_{j=m}^{2m}e^{\pm is}c_{\pm,j}\,s^{-j-1},

for appropriate constants
c_{\pm,j}\in\mathbb{C}
. Substituting
s=2\pi ty
, we have

j_{\alpha}(2\pi ty)=\sum_{\pm}\sum_{j=m}^{2m}e^{\pm 2\pi ity}c_{\pm,j}\,(2\pi t)^{-j-1}y^{-j-1},

giving

\displaystyle f(t)

\displaystyle=`c_{\alpha}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\int_{I_{k}}\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(y)j_{\alpha}(2\pi ty)\,y^{2\alpha+1}\,dy

\displaystyle=c_{\alpha}\sum_{\pm}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\sum_{j=m}^{2m}c_{\pm,j}\,(2\pi t)^{-j-1}\int_{I_{k}}\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}f(y)\,e^{\pm 2\pi ity}\,y^{2\alpha-j}\,dy

\displaystyle=c_{\alpha}\sum_{\pm}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\sum_{j=m}^{2m}c_{\pm,j}\,(2\pi t)^{-j-1}\int_{0}^{1}\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}f(\lambda_{k}+s)\,e^{\pm 2\pi it(\lambda_{k}+s)}\,(\lambda_{k}+s)^{2\alpha-j}\,ds.

For convenience, define
H_{j,k}(s):=\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(\lambda_{k}+s)(\lambda_{k}+s)^{2\alpha-j}
,
g_{\pm,j,k}(t):=\int_{0}^{1}H_{j,k}(s)\,e^{\pm 2\pi its}\,ds
, and
G_{\pm,j,k}(t):=g_{\pm,j,k}(t)e^{\pm 2\pi i\lambda_{k}t}
. Then,

\displaystyle f(t)

\displaystyle=c_{\alpha}\sum_{\pm}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\sum_{j=m}^{2m}c_{\pm,j}\,(2\pi t)^{-j-1}\int_{0}^{1}H_{j,k}(s)\,e^{\pm 2\pi it(\lambda_{k}+s)}\,ds

\displaystyle=c_{\alpha}\sum_{\pm}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\sum_{j=m}^{2m}c_{\pm,j}\,(2\pi t)^{-j-1}\,g_{\pm,j,k}(t)\,e^{\pm 2\pi i\lambda_{k}t}

\displaystyle=c_{\alpha}\sum_{\pm}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\sum_{j=m}^{2m}c_{\pm,j}\,(2\pi t)^{-j-1}\,G_{\pm,j,k}(t)=c_{\alpha}\sum_{k=1}^{N}F_{k}(t),

where
F_{k}(t):=\sum_{\pm}\sum_{j=m}^{2m}c_{\pm,j}\,(2\pi t)^{-j-1}\,G_{\pm,j,k}(t)
. Taking the Fourier transform of
g_{\pm,j,k}
, we see that

\widehat{g_{+,j,k}}(\xi)=\begin{cases}H_{j,k}(\xi)&0\leqslant\xi\leqslant 1\\
0&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

and

\widehat{g_{-,j,k}}(\xi)=\begin{cases}H_{j,k}(-\xi)&-1\leqslant\xi\leqslant 0\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

So,
\operatorname{supp}\widehat{g_{+,j,k}}\subset[0,1]
and
\operatorname{supp}\widehat{g_{-,j,k}}\subset[-1,0]
. Since
G_{\pm,j,k}(t)=g_{\pm,j,k}(t)e^{\pm 2\pi i\lambda_{k}t}
, we have
\widehat{G_{\pm,j,k}}(\xi)=\widehat{g_{\pm,j,k}}(\xi\mp\lambda_{k})
, giving
\operatorname{supp}\widehat{G_{+,j,k}}\subset[\lambda_{k},\lambda_{k}+1]
and
\operatorname{supp}\widehat{G_{-,j,k}}\subset[-(\lambda_{k}+1),-\lambda_{k}]
.

As in the proof of Theorem
1.3
, we first cut out
[0,\varepsilon)
. We have

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{2}_{\alpha}([0,\varepsilon)])}^{2}

\displaystyle=c_{\alpha}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}|f(t)|^{2}\,t^{2\alpha+1}\,dt

\displaystyle\leqslant c_{\alpha}^{3}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}|\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(y)||j_{\alpha}(2\pi ty)|\,y^{2\alpha+1}\,dy\right)^{2}\,t^{2\alpha+1}\,dt

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}|\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(y)|\,y^{\alpha+1/2}\,dy\right)^{2}\,dt

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon N\,\|\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}f\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\alpha}}\lesssim\varepsilon\,\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\alpha}},

where (
2.3
) is used in the third line.

Next, for
[\varepsilon,\infty)
, we mimic the Taylor approximation idea from
[
13
]
. Write
[\varepsilon,\infty)=\bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty}[\varepsilon+n,\varepsilon+n+1]=:\bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty}J_{n}
, and set
\widetilde{J_{n}}:=[(\varepsilon+n)^{2},(\varepsilon+n+1)^{2}]
. The form of the approximation differs depending on whether
k=1
or
k\geqslant 2
, due to the form of the Bernstein inequality that we subsequently use.

For
k=1
, define
\Phi(u):=F_{1}(\sqrt{u})
and let
r_{1,n}
be the
M-1
-degree Taylor polynomial of
\Phi
, centered at
u=(\varepsilon+n)^{2}
. Let
p_{1,n}(t):=r_{1,n}(t^{2})
for
t\in J_{n}
.

When
k\geqslant 2
, let
w_{j}(t):=(2\pi t)^{-j-1}
, and
h_{\pm,j,k}(t):=w_{j}(t)g_{\pm,j,k}(t)
. Let
p_{j,k,n}(t)
be the
M-1
-degree Taylor polynomial of
g_{\pm,j,k}(t)
, centered at
t=\varepsilon+n
, i.e., the left-endpoint of
J_{n}
.

Define

r_{n}(t):=p_{1,n}(t)+\sum_{\pm}\sum_{k=2}^{N}\sum_{j=m}^{2m}c_{\pm,j}\,w_{j}(t)\,p_{j,k,n}(t)\,e^{\pm 2\pi i\lambda_{k}t},

and

T_{n}(t):=\underbrace{F_{1}(t)-p_{1,n}(t)}_{T_{n}^{(1)}(t)}+T_{n}^{(2)}(t),

where

T_{n}^{(2)}(t):=\sum_{\pm}\sum_{k=2}^{N}\sum_{j=m}^{2m}c_{\pm,j}\,w_{j}(t)\left(g_{\pm,j,k}(t)-p_{j,k,n}(t)\right)\,e^{\pm 2\pi i\lambda_{k}t}.

We can control
r_{n}
on
J_{n}
using Nazarov’s Turan inequality, i.e., Lemma
3.1
. First, define
\widetilde{r_{n}}(t):=(2\pi t)^{2m+1}\,r_{n}(t)
, so

\widetilde{r_{n}}(t)=(2\pi t)^{2m+1}\,p_{1,n}(t)+\sum_{\pm}\sum_{k=2}^{N}\sum_{j=m}^{2m}c_{\pm,j}\,(2\pi t)^{2m-j}\,p_{j,k,n}(t)\,e^{\pm 2\pi i\lambda_{k}t},

is a suitable candidate for Lemma
3.1
. We get

(3.5)

\|\widetilde{r_{n}}\|_{L^{2}(J_{n})}\leqslant\left(\frac{C|J_{n}|}{|E\cap J_{n}|}\right)^{(2N-1)(2M+2m-1)-\frac{1}{2}}\,\|\widetilde{r_{n}}\|_{L^{2}(E\cap J_{n})},

as there are
2(N-1)+1=2N-1
polynomials, and the degree of each polynomial is bounded by
\max\{M+m-1,2M-2+2m+1\}=2M+2m-1
. Since the weight
(2\pi t)^{2m+1}
is almost constant on
J_{n}
, (
3.5
) can be converted to an estimate for
r_{n}
. In particular,

(3.6)

\|{r_{n}}\|_{L^{2}(J_{n})}\leqslant 2^{2m+1}\,\left(\frac{C|J_{n}|}{|E\cap J_{n}|}\right)^{(2N-1)(2M+2m-1)-\frac{1}{2}}\,\|{r_{n}}\|_{L^{2}(E\cap J_{n})},

for all
n\geqslant 1
, as

\Big(\max_{J_{n}}\,(2\pi t)^{-2m-1}\Big)\Big(\max_{J_{n}}\,(2\pi t)^{2m+1}\Big)=\left(1+\frac{1}{\varepsilon+n}\right)^{2m+1}\leqslant 2^{2m+1},

for all
n\geqslant 1
. If
n=0
, we have

(3.7)

\|{r_{0}}\|_{L^{2}(J_{0})}\leqslant\left(1+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)^{2m+1}\,\left(\frac{C|J_{0}|}{|E\cap J_{0}|}\right)^{(2N-1)(2M+2m-1)-\frac{1}{2}}\,\|{r_{0}}\|_{L^{2}(E\cap J_{0})}.

By Proposition
2.1
, there exists
\widetilde{\gamma}
such that
|E\cap J_{n}|\geqslant\widetilde{\gamma}\,|J_{n}|
for each
n\geqslant 0
. Using this relative density condition, and summing up, we obtain

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\|r_{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(J_{n})}\leqslant(1+\varepsilon^{-1})^{4m+2}\left(C\,\widetilde{\gamma}^{-1}\right)^{(4N-2)(2M+2m-1)-1}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\|{r_{n}}\|_{L^{2}(E\cap J_{n})}^{2},

(
\varepsilon<1
ensures
\max\{2^{4m+2},(1+\varepsilon^{-1})^{4m+2}\}=(1+\varepsilon^{-1})^{4m+2}
). Similarly, we can lift this estimate to
L^{2}_{\alpha}
spaces, with a cost of a
(1+\varepsilon^{-1})^{2m+2}

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\|r_{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\alpha}(J_{n})}\leqslant(1+\varepsilon^{-1})^{6m+4}\left(C\,\widetilde{\gamma}^{-1}\right)^{(4N-2)(2M+2m-1)-1}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\|{r_{n}}\|_{L^{2}_{\alpha}(E\cap J_{n})}^{2}

Now, we study the remainder terms. We analyze the low-frequency block first, i.e.,
F_{1}
.

Observe that

\displaystyle\|T_{n}^{(1)}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\alpha}(J_{n})}\approx\|\Phi-r_{1,n}\|_{L^{2}(\widetilde{J_{n}};\,u^{\alpha}du)}^{2}

since the substitution
u=t^{2}
leads to
2t^{2\alpha+1}\,dt=u^{\alpha}\,du
, and so

\displaystyle\|T_{n}^{(1)}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\alpha}(J_{n})}\approx\int_{J_{n}}|F_{1}(t)-p_{1,n}(t)|^{2}\,t^{2\alpha+1}\,dt\approx\int_{\widetilde{J_{n}}}|\Phi(u)-r_{1,n}(u)|^{2}\,u^{\alpha}\,du.

Using Taylor’s remainder theorem,

\Phi(u)-r_{1,n}(u)=\frac{1}{(M-1)!}\int_{(\varepsilon+n)^{2}}^{u}(u-s)^{M-1}\,\Phi^{(M)}(s)\,ds,

for every
u\in\widetilde{J_{n}}
.

In preparation for Schur’s test, we define an operator
S_{n}:L^{2}(\widetilde{J_{n}};s^{\alpha+M}ds)\to L^{2}(\widetilde{J_{n}};u^{\alpha}\,du)
between weighted spaces by

(S_{n}h)(u)=\frac{1}{(M-1)!}\int_{\widetilde{J_{n}}}(u-s)_{+}^{M-1}\,h(s)\,ds,

where
x_{+}:=\max\{x,0\}
. We introduce two canonical isometries
\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V}
to define another operator
\widetilde{S_{n}}:=\mathcal{V}^{-1}S_{n}\,\mathcal{U}^{-1}
between unweighted
L^{2}
spaces, which is our candidate for Schur’s test. Let

\mathcal{U}:L^{2}(\widetilde{J_{n}};s^{\alpha+M}ds)\to L^{2}(\widetilde{J_{n}};ds)
be given by

(\mathcal{U}h)(s):=s^{\frac{\alpha+M}{2}}\,h(s),

and
\mathcal{V}:L^{2}(\widetilde{J_{n}};du)\to L^{2}(\widetilde{J_{n}};u^{\alpha}du)
be given by

(\mathcal{V}h)(u):=u^{-\alpha/2}\,h(u).

Then,

(\widetilde{S_{n}}h)(u)=\int_{\widetilde{J_{n}}}K_{n}(u,s)\,h(s)\,ds,

where

K_{n}(u,s):=\frac{1}{(M-1)!}\,(u-s)_{+}^{M-1}\,u^{\alpha/2}\,s^{-\frac{(\alpha+M)}{2}}.

Therefore,

\|\Phi-r_{1,n}\|_{L^{2}(\widetilde{J_{n}};\,u^{\alpha}du)}^{2}=\|\widetilde{S_{n}}(s^{\frac{\alpha+M}{2}}\,\Phi^{(M)}(s))\|_{L^{2}(\widetilde{J_{n}};\,du)}^{2}\leqslant\|\widetilde{S_{n}}\|^{2}\|\Phi^{(M)}\|_{L^{2}(\widetilde{J_{n}};\,s^{\alpha+M}ds)}^{2}.

We obtain bounds for
\|\widetilde{S_{n}}\|
using Schur’s test on the kernel
K_{n}(u,s)
.
Fix
u\in\widetilde{J_{n}}
and let
b_{n}:=\varepsilon+n
. Since
|\widetilde{J_{n}}|=2b_{n}+1
, we get

\sup_{u\in\widetilde{J_{n}}}\int_{\widetilde{J_{n}}}K_{n}(u,s)\,ds\leqslant\frac{1}{M!}\left(1+\frac{1}{b_{n}}\right)^{\alpha}\,\left(2+\frac{1}{b_{n}}\right)^{M}.

and

\sup_{s\in\widetilde{J_{n}}}\int_{\widetilde{J_{n}}}K_{n}(u,s)\,ds\leqslant\frac{1}{M!}\left(1+\frac{1}{b_{n}}\right)^{\alpha}\,\left(2+\frac{1}{b_{n}}\right)^{M}.

By Schur’s test,

\|\widetilde{S_{n}}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\leqslant\frac{1}{M!}\left(1+\frac{1}{b_{n}}\right)^{\alpha}\,\left(2+\frac{1}{b_{n}}\right)^{M}\leqslant\frac{2^{\alpha}3^{M}}{M!},

for all
n\geqslant 1
, and

\|\widetilde{S_{0}}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\leqslant\frac{(1+\varepsilon^{-1})^{\alpha}(2+\varepsilon^{-1})^{M}}{M!},

if
n=0
.

Next, using Lemma
3.3
, we have

(3.8)

\|\Phi^{(M)}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+};\,s^{\alpha+M}ds)}^{2}\lesssim(2\pi)^{2M}\int_{0}^{\infty}|\Phi(s)|^{2}\,s^{\alpha}\,ds\lesssim 2^{6M}\,\|F_{1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\alpha}},

since
\operatorname{supp}\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(F_{1})\subset[\lambda_{1},\lambda_{1}+1]\subset[0,2]
. Substituting these estimates into

\|T_{n}^{(1)}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\alpha}(J_{n})}\lesssim\|\widetilde{S_{n}}\|^{2}\|\Phi^{(M)}\|_{L^{2}(\widetilde{J_{n}};\,s^{\alpha+M}ds)}^{2},

summing up, and using
\varepsilon<1
, we obtain

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\|T_{n}^{(1)}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\alpha}(J_{n})}\lesssim\frac{(1+\varepsilon^{-1})^{2\alpha}(2+\varepsilon^{-1})^{2M}}{(M!)^{2}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\|\Phi^{(M)}\|_{L^{2}(\widetilde{J_{n}};\,s^{\alpha+M}ds)}^{2}.

This leads to the following remainder estimate for the low-frequency block:

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\|T_{n}^{(1)}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\alpha}(J_{n})}\lesssim\frac{2^{6M}(1+\varepsilon^{-1})^{2\alpha}(2+\varepsilon^{-1})^{2M}}{(M!)^{2}}\|F_{1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\alpha}}.

Next, we control the remainder term for
k\geqslant 2
, i.e., we treat the high frequency block
T_{n}^{(2)}(t)
. Note that

|T_{n}^{(2)}(t)|\lesssim\sum_{\pm}\sum_{k=2}^{N}\sum_{j=m}^{2m}|w_{j}(t)|\left|g_{\pm,j,k}(t)-p_{j,k,n}(t)\right|,

which gives

\|T_{n}^{(2)}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\alpha}(J_{n})}\lesssim\sum_{\pm}\sum_{k=2}^{N}\sum_{j=m}^{2m}\|w_{j}\left(g_{\pm,j,k}-p_{j,k,n}\right)\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\alpha}(J_{n})}.

By Taylor’s remainder theorem,

g_{\pm,j,k}(t)-p_{j,k,n}(t)=\frac{1}{(M-1)!}\int_{\varepsilon+n}^{t}g^{(M)}_{\pm,j,k}(s)(t-s)^{M-1}\,ds.

Define
(S_{j,n}h)(t):=\int_{J_{n}}K_{j,n}(t,s)\,h(s)\,ds,

where

K_{j,n}(t,s)=\frac{1}{(M-1)!}(t-s)^{M-1}_{+}\,w_{j}(t)\,t^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}.

Clearly, we want bounds for
\|S_{j,n}\|
, as

\|w_{j}\left(g_{\pm,j,k}-p_{j,k,n}\right)\|_{L^{2}_{\alpha}(J_{n})}\approx\|S_{j,n}(g_{\pm,j,k}^{(M)})\|_{L^{2}(J_{n};\,dt)}\leqslant\|S_{j,n}\|\|g_{\pm,j,k}^{(M)}\|_{L^{2}(J_{n};\,ds)}.

We have

\sup_{t\in J_{n}}\int_{J_{n}}K_{j,n}(t,s)\,ds\leqslant\frac{(2\pi)^{-j-1}}{M!}\,\sup_{t\in J_{n}}t^{m-j},

and

\sup_{s\in J_{n}}\int_{J_{n}}K_{j,n}(t,s)\,dt\leqslant\frac{(2\pi)^{-j-1}}{M!}\,\sup_{t\in J_{n}}t^{m-j},

giving

\|S_{j,n}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\leqslant\frac{(2\pi)^{-j-1}}{M!}\,\sup_{t\in J_{n}}t^{m-j},

and therefore

\|S_{j,n}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\leqslant\begin{cases}\frac{(2\pi)^{-j-1}}{M!}&\text{if }n\geqslant 1,\\
\frac{(2\pi)^{-j-1}\,\varepsilon^{m-j}}{M!}&\text{if }n=0.\end{cases}

As earlier, summing up, and using
\varepsilon<1
with
(2\pi)^{-2j-2}\leqslant 1
,

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\|w_{j}\left(g_{\pm,j,k}-p_{j,k,n}\right)\|_{L^{2}_{\alpha}(J_{n})}^{2}\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon^{-2m}}{(M!)^{2}}\,\|g_{\pm,j,k}^{(M)}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}.

As
\varepsilon<1
and
j\leqslant 2m
, we have
\varepsilon^{2m-2j}\leqslant\varepsilon^{-2m}
. Finally, as
\operatorname{supp}g_{\pm,j,k}\subset[-1,1]
,

\|g_{\pm,j,k}^{(M)}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}=\|\widehat{g_{\pm,j,k}^{(M)}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}\leqslant(2\pi)^{2M}\|\widehat{g_{\pm,j,k}}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}=(2\pi)^{2M}\|g_{\pm,j,k}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}.

Summing over the
(\pm,j,k)
indices, we obtain the following remainder estimate for the high-frequency block:

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\|T_{n}^{(2)}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\alpha}(J_{n})}\lesssim\frac{(2\pi)^{2M}\,\varepsilon^{-2m}}{(M!)^{2}}\sum_{\pm}\sum_{k=2}^{N}\sum_{j=m}^{2m}\|g_{\pm,j,k}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}.

As
\lambda_{k}\geqslant 1
for
k\geqslant 2
, we have

\displaystyle\|\widehat{g_{\pm,j,k}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}=\int_{0}^{1}|H_{j,k}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}|\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(\lambda_{k}+s)|^{2}\,(\lambda_{k}+s)^{4m+2-2j}\,ds

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{\lambda_{k}}^{\lambda_{k}+1}|\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(s)|^{2}\,s^{2m+2}\,ds=\int_{\lambda_{k}}^{\lambda_{k}+1}|\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(s)|^{2}\,s^{2\alpha+1}\,ds,

and therefore high-frequency estimate can be simplified to

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\|T_{n}^{(2)}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\alpha}(J_{n})}\lesssim\frac{(2\pi)^{2M}\,\varepsilon^{-2m}}{(M!)^{2}}\sum_{k=2}^{N}\|F_{k}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\alpha}}.

Finally, we combine all estimates to show
\|f\|_{L^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{+})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{2}_{\alpha}(E)}
. For convenience, define the following quantities:

C_{\text{NT}}:=(1+\varepsilon^{-1})^{6m+4}\left(C\,\widetilde{\gamma}^{-1}\right)^{(4N-2)(2M+2m-1)-1},

C_{\text{low}}:=\frac{2^{6M}(1+\varepsilon^{-1})^{2\alpha}(2+\varepsilon^{-1})^{2M}}{(M!)^{2}},

C_{\text{high}}:=\frac{(2\pi)^{2M}\,\varepsilon^{-2m}}{(M!)^{2}}.

Observe that
C_{\text{NT}}\,(C_{\text{low}}+C_{\text{high}})\to 0
as
M\to\infty
. Finally,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{+})}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon\,\|f\|_{L^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{+})}^{2}+\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\alpha}([\varepsilon,\infty])}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon\,\|f\|_{L^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{+})}^{2}+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\|r_{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\alpha}(J_{n})}+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\|T_{n}^{(1)}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\alpha}(J_{n})}+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\|T_{n}^{(2)}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\alpha}(J_{n})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon\,\|f\|_{L^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{+})}^{2}+C_{\text{NT}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\|r_{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\alpha}(E\cap J_{n})}+(C_{\text{low}}+C_{\text{high}})\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{+})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon\,\|f\|_{L^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{+})}^{2}+C_{\text{NT}}\,\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\alpha}(E)}+(C_{\text{NT}}+1)(C_{\text{low}}+C_{\text{high}})\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{+})}.

Let
\beta:=\beta(\alpha,N,\gamma)>0
be such that

\|f\|_{L^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{+})}^{2}\leqslant\varepsilon\beta\,\|f\|_{L^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{+})}^{2}+\beta C_{\text{NT}}\,\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\alpha}(E)}+\beta(C_{\text{NT}}+1)(C_{\text{low}}+C_{\text{high}})\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{+})}.

Now, choose
\varepsilon
small enough so that
\varepsilon\beta<\frac{1}{4}
. Once
\varepsilon
is fixed, we choose
M
large enough to ensure
\beta(C_{\text{NT}}+1)(C_{\text{low}}+C_{\text{high}})<\frac{1}{4}
. In conclusion, we have

\|f\|_{L^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{+})}\lesssim_{\alpha,N,\gamma}\|f\|_{L^{2}_{\alpha}(E)}.

∎

4.
An Extension of Theorem
1.6

Theorem
1.6
can be extended to functions that can be written as a product of a radial function and a spherical harmonic. The following calculation obtains the estimate (
1.1
), using the estimate for radial functions (Theorem
1.3
).

Suppose
f(x)=F(|x|)\,Y_{k}\left(\frac{x}{|x|}\right)
. By the Bochner identity,

(4.1)

\widehat{f}(y)=\widehat{FY_{k}}(y)\approx Y_{k}\bigg(\frac{y}{|y|}\bigg)\,|y|^{k}\int_{0}^{\infty}F(r)\,j_{n/2+k-1}(2\pi r|y|)\,r^{n+k-1}\,dr.

The right-side can be expressed as the Fourier-Bessel transform of
g(r)=r^{-k}\,F(r)
, of order
\alpha=n/2+k-1
. If
\alpha=n/2+k-1
, then
2\alpha+1=n+2k-1
. Thus,

(4.2)

\widehat{f}(y)\approx Y_{k}\bigg(\frac{y}{|y|}\bigg)\,|y|^{k}\int_{0}^{\infty}g(r)\,j_{\alpha}(2\pi r|y|)\,r^{2\alpha+1}\,dr=Y_{k}\bigg(\frac{y}{|y|}\bigg)\,|y|^{k}\,\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(g)(|y|).

Now, suppose
\operatorname{supp}\widehat{f}\subset\{\xi\in R^{n}:R\leqslant|\xi|\leqslant R+1\}
, and
E
satisfies
\mu_{\alpha}
-relative density in each radial direction. In other words, if
E_{\omega}
is the slice of
E
along
\omega\in S^{n-1}
, then, omitting pushforwards, we ask
\mu_{\alpha}(E_{\omega}\cap[R\omega,(R+1)\omega])\gtrsim\mu_{\alpha}([R\omega,(R+1)\omega])
for all
R\geqslant 0
. By
Equation

4.2
, we have
\operatorname{supp}\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(g)\subset[R,R+1]
. By Theorem
1.3
, we get

(4.3)

\|g\|_{L^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{+})}\lesssim\|g\|_{L^{2}_{\alpha}(E_{\omega})},

for every
\omega\in S^{n-1},
where
\alpha=n/2+k-1
. Finally,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{S^{n-1}}|F(r)|^{2}\,|Y_{k}(\omega)|^{2}\,r^{n-1}\,d\sigma(\omega)\,dr

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{S^{n-1}}|g(r)|^{2}\,|Y_{k}(\omega)|^{2}\,r^{n+2k-1}\,d\sigma(\omega)\,dr

\displaystyle=\|Y_{k}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(S^{n-1})}\,\|g\|_{L^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{+})}^{2},

and similarly,

\displaystyle\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(E)}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{S^{n-1}}\mathbf{1}_{E}(r\omega)\,|g(r)|^{2}\,|Y_{k}(\omega)|^{2}\,r^{n+2k-1}\,d\sigma(\omega)\,dr

\displaystyle=\int_{S^{n-1}}|Y_{k}(\omega)|^{2}\int_{E_{\omega}}|g(r)|^{2}\,r^{n+2k-1}\,dr\,d\sigma(\omega)

\displaystyle=\int_{S^{n-1}}|Y_{k}(\omega)|^{2}\,\|g\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\alpha}(E_{\omega})}\,d\sigma(\omega).

Starting with
Equation

4.3
, multiplying both sides by
|Y_{k}(\omega)|^{2}
and integrating over
(S^{n-1},d\sigma)
, we get
\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{2}(E)}
as desired.

Approximate differentiability.

Definition 10

Let

We then denote with

is a set of measure zero.

Consequently, again by covering the compact set
f(\partial U)
with sufficiently small balls, it is possible to find a neighborhood
G\Subset\Omega
of
f(\partial U)
so that
|G|<\varepsilon
and
f(U)\setminus G
has finitely many connected components. Indeed, since
U\Subset\Omega
, then
f(U)
is bounded. If
f(U)\setminus G
has countably many connected components
V_{1},V_{2},\ldots
, the series
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}|V_{i}|
needs to be convergent, so for some
M
,
\sum_{i=M}^{\infty}|V_{i}|<\varepsilon
. We can enlarge
G
to include
V_{i}
for
i\geq M
, thus causing
f(U)\setminus G
to have finitely many connected components.

Let
V_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,m
for some
m\in\mathbb{N}
denote the connected components of
f(U)\setminus G
. Observe that by local uniform convergence of
f_{k}
to
f
,
f_{k}(\partial U)\subset G
for large enough
k
. Therefore, the degrees
\deg(f,U,y)
,
\deg(f_{k},U,y)
are well defined for sufficiently large
k
, because for any
y\in f(U)\setminus G
,

y\notin f_{k}(\partial U)\cup f(\partial U).

By Proposition
20
(ii), degree is constant on connected components and thus

\deg(f_{k},U,y)=\deg(f_{k},U,z)\text{ and }\deg(f,U,y)=\deg(f,U,z)\text{ for any }y,z\in V_{i}.

Moreover, uniform convergence of
f_{k}
to
f
implies that for each
i
, there is
k_{i}
such that

\deg(f_{k},U,y)=\deg(f,U,y)\text{ for }k\geq k_{i},\,y\in V_{i}.

As there are only finitely many
i
, we can set
k_{o}:=\max_{1\leq i\leq m}k_{i}
. For any
k\geq k_{o}
and
y\in f(U)\setminus G
, it is then true that
\deg(f_{k},U,y)=\deg(f,U,y)
.

By assumption (
2
), for almost any
y\in f(U)\setminus G
and
k\geq k_{o}
, we have
d(y,f_{k},U)\neq 0
. This implies that for a. e. such
y
, there exists
x\in U
s. t.
f_{k}(x)=y
, see Proposition
20
(i). Consequently, for sufficiently large
k
, almost every point of
f(U)\setminus G
is contained in
f_{k}(U)
, which allows to conclude that

|f(U)\setminus G|\leq|f_{k}(U)|.

It then follows from the choice of
U
and
G
and from (
16
) that for
k\geq k_{o}

|f(K)|-\varepsilon\leq|f(U)|-|G|\leq|f(U)\setminus G|\leq|f_{k}(U)|\leq|f_{k}(K)|+\varepsilon.

Since
\varepsilon
is arbitrary,
\liminf_{k\to\infty}|f_{k}(K)|\geq|f(K)|
, which finishes the proof of (
15
) and hence the proof.
∎

It is well known that condition (
2
) is satisfied for any homeomorphism, see
[
17
, Theorem 3.35]
. Below, we shall give a weaker condition which guarantees (
2
) and to this end, we need to use the notion of regular approximate derivative.

Definition 21
.

Let
f:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
be measurable. We say that
f
is regularly approximately differentiable at a point
x\in\Omega
if
f
is approximately differentiable at point
x
with derivative
D_{\rm a}f(x)
and if there exists a sequence
r_{j}\to 0
s. t.

\lim_{j\to\infty}\sup_{|h|=1}\frac{1}{r_{j}}|f(x+r_{j}h)-f(x)-D_{\rm a}f(x)r_{j}h|=0.

We use the same notation for approximate derivative which is not necessarily regular.

Reshetnyak in
[
35
]
in 1967 proved that
f\in W^{1,p}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
p>n-1
has regular approximate differential a. e in
\Omega
, see
[
18
]
for an alternative proof. Regularity of approximate derivative is enough for the analogue of the classical degree formula (
13
) to hold.

Proposition 22
.

[
19
, p. 284]

Let
f:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a continuous mapping, which satisfies the Lusin condition (N), is regularly approximately differentiable a. e. and its approximate Jacobian
J_{\rm a}f
is locally integrable. For any bounded domain
U\Subset\Omega
with
|\partial U|=0
, for almost every
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
J_{\rm a}f(x)
is well defined for
x\in f^{-1}(y)
and the formula

\deg(f,U,y)=\sum_{x\in f^{-1}(x)\cap U}\mathrm{sgn}\,(J_{\rm a}f(x)).

holds.

The assumption on regularity of the approximate derivative is necessary, as shown by the example described in
[
20
]
of an a. e. approximately differentiable homeomorphism
\Phi
of the unit cube such that
\degg(\Phi,(0,1)^{n},y)=1
for any
y\in(0,1)^{n}
and whose approximate Jacobian equals
-1
a. e.

Eventually, we get the following

Corollary 23
.

If
f:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is as in Proposition
22
and

J_{\rm a}f>0\text{ or }J_{\rm a}f<0\text{ a.\,e.\ on }\Omega,

then condition (
2
) holds.

Nonsmooth Optimization with Zeroth Order Comparison Feedback
Taha EL BAKKALI, El Mahdi Chayti, Omar Saadi

Abstract
We study unconstrained optimization problems of nonsmooth, nonconvex Lipschitz functions, using only noisy pairwise comparisons governed by a known link function. Our goal is to compute a ( δ , ε ) (\delta,\varepsilon) -Goldstein stationary point. We combine randomized smoothing with a novel unbiased reduction from comparisons to local value differences. By leveraging a Russian-roulette truncation on the Bernoulli-product expansion of the inverse link, we construct an exactly unbiased estimator for directional differences. This estimator has finite expected cost and variance scaling quadratically with the function gap, 𝒪 ⁡ ( B 2 ) \mathcal{O}(B^{2}) , under mild conditions. Plugging this into the smoothed gradient identity enables a standard nonconvex SGD analysis, yielding explicit comparison-complexity bounds for common symmetric links such as logistic, probit, and cauchit.

Introduction

Comparison-based optimization addresses black-box settings where algorithms lack access to calibrated function values or gradients, receiving only ordinal feedback: an indication of whether
f(y)
is preferred to
f(x)
. This model naturally captures dueling bandits, human-in-the-loop tuning, and RLHF-style preference signals (see, e.g., comparison-oracle-based direct alignment for LLMs
[
3
]
). In smooth convex and strongly convex regimes, guarantees have been established under noisy comparison oracles
[
11
]
, and subsequent work develops more realistic preference models where comparison reliability degrades near ties, while still yielding end-to-end optimization guarantees
[
12
]
. For smooth nonconvex objectives, the deterministic or noiseless setting has been studied using comparison and ranking feedback with stationarity guarantees
[
13
,
5
,
2
]
, and recent work also shows that acceleration is possible even when the oracle only compares objective values
[
10
]
; very recently, the smooth stochastic nonconvex case has also been analyzed, providing nonasymptotic convergence to approximate stationarity under noisy comparisons
[
1
]
with increasingly realistic noisy oracles.

In stark contrast, the
nonsmooth
setting remains entirely unexplored. We aim to fill this gap by providing the first comparison-based optimization algorithms with convergence guarantees for nonsmooth convex and nonconvex objectives.

Addressing the nonsmooth nonconvex regime requires a departure from classical gradient-based stationarity, which is ill-defined for such problems. A robust alternative is
Goldstein stationarity

[
6
]
. For locally Lipschitz functions, this concept allows one to characterize approximate stationarity using the Clarke subdifferential
[
4
]
by considering the convex hull of subgradients within a ball of radius
\delta
around a point.

Goldstein stationarity has recently emerged as the central solution concept for derivative-free optimization of Lipschitz nonsmooth objectives, particularly under
zeroth-order value-oracle
access. In that setting, state-of-the-art algorithms combine randomized smoothing with two-point differences to form gradient estimators of a smoothed surrogate, offering finite-time guarantees for finding
(\delta,\varepsilon)
-Goldstein stationary points
[
9
,
7
]
. However, these results crucially rely on the magnitude of function differences. When algorithms are restricted to
pairwise comparisons
that reveal only relative order, the magnitude information is lost, and we are not aware of any existing nonasymptotic guarantees for computing
(\delta,\varepsilon)
-Goldstein stationary points for general Lipschitz nonsmooth functions.

Comparison model and link function.

We adopt the standard pairwise-comparison oracle model from preference-based optimization. Given two points
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the oracle returns a binary outcome
Y\in\{0,1\}
satisfying

\mathbb{P}\left(Y=1\mid x,y\right)=\sigma\!\left(\Delta\right),

where
\Delta:=f(y)-f(x)
. The event
Y=1
indicates that
y
is preferred over
x
. Throughout, the link
\sigma:\mathbb{R}\to(0,1)
is assumed known and strictly increasing. Our analysis applies to a broad class of symmetric links (where
\sigma(-t)=1-\sigma(t)
) whose inverse
g=\sigma^{-1}
admits a Bernoulli-product expansion on the operated probability interval induced by the bounded-gap regime. Concretely, under the local query design
|\Delta|\leq B
, we have
p=\sigma(\Delta)\in[p_{-},p_{+}]
with
p_{\pm}=\sigma(\pm B)
, and we assume that on
[p_{-},p_{+}]
,

g(p)=\sum_{m\geq 1}c_{m}\big(p^{m}-(1-p)^{m}\big)

for known coefficients
(c_{m})_{m\geq 1}
, with absolute convergence. This condition is satisfied by several classical choices, including the logistic link (where
c_{m}=1/m
), as well as probit and cauchit links on any operated interval
[p_{-},p_{+}]
bounded away from
\{0,1\}
.

Main contributions.

We provide the first framework for minimizing nonsmooth nonconvex functions using only noisy comparisons. Our specific contributions are as follows:

•

Unbiased reduction to local values.
We develop a general method to recover unbiased estimates of function value differences from binary comparisons. For the class of links described above, we construct an estimator
\widehat{\Delta}
by combining (i) Bernoulli-product estimators
A_{m}-B_{m}
, where
A_{m}=\mathbf{1}\{Y_{1}=\cdots=Y_{m}=1\}
and
B_{m}=\mathbf{1}\{Y_{1}=\cdots=Y_{m}=0\}
are derived from
m
repeated comparisons, with (ii) a Russian-roulette truncation scheme. This construction preserves unbiasedness while ensuring finite expected cost.

•

Optimal variance scaling.
We prove that under a geometric truncation schedule and mild scaling conditions on the link, our estimator achieves a variance of
\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\Delta}^{\,2}]=\mathcal{O}(B^{2})
. This quadratic scaling with the gap size
B
is crucial, as it matches the variance profile of value-based oracles and prevents the noise explosion typically associated with inverse link approximations.

•

Convergence to Goldstein stationarity.
By substituting the true function difference
\Delta
with our unbiased estimator
\widehat{\Delta}
in the randomized smoothing scheme, we derive a stochastic gradient estimator for the smoothed function
f_{\delta}
. Since
\nabla f_{\delta}(x)\in\partial_{\delta}f(x)
, this yields a rigorous algorithm with finite expected comparison complexity for reaching
(\delta,\varepsilon)
-Goldstein stationarity, extending standard nonconvex SGD guarantees to the nonsmooth comparison-based setting.

2
(\delta,\varepsilon)
-Goldstein Stationarity Through Pairwise Comparisons

2.1
Goldstein stationarity and random smoothing

Goldstein
\delta
-subdifferential.

For a locally Lipschitz function
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
, define the Goldstein
\delta
-subdifferential as

\partial_{\delta}f(x)\;:=\;\mathrm{conv}\Big(\bigcup_{\|z-x\|\leq\delta}\partial^{C}f(z)\Big),

where
\partial^{C}f(z)
denotes the Clarke subdifferential and
\mathrm{conv}
the convex hull.
We say that
x
is
(\delta,\varepsilon)
-Goldstein stationary if

\mathrm{dist}\big(0,\partial_{\delta}f(x)\big)\leq\varepsilon
.

Random smoothing.

Let
V\sim\mathrm{Unif}(\mathbb{B})
be uniform on the unit Euclidean ball

\mathbb{B}:=\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\|v\|\leq 1\}
. For
\delta>0
, define the smoothed function

f_{\delta}(x)\;:=\;\mathbb{E}\big[f(x+\delta V)\big].

A standard smoothing-based certificate.

The following lemma is a well-known consequence of random smoothing and basic properties of
the Clarke subdifferential: the gradient of the ball-smoothed function
f_{\delta}
belongs to
the Goldstein
\delta
-subdifferential of
f
, and hence directly certifies

(\delta,\varepsilon)
-Goldstein stationarity whenever
\|\nabla f_{\delta}(x)\|\leq\varepsilon
.
We include it for completeness; see
[
9
]
.

Lemma 2.1

(Smoothed gradients certify Goldstein stationarity)
.

Assume
f
is locally Lipschitz. Then
f_{\delta}
is differentiable and, for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\nabla f_{\delta}(x)\in\partial_{\delta}f(x).

Consequently,

\mathrm{dist}\big(0,\partial_{\delta}f(x)\big)\leq\|\nabla f_{\delta}(x)\|.

In particular, any point
x
satisfying
\|\nabla f_{\delta}(x)\|\leq\varepsilon

is
(\delta,\varepsilon)
-Goldstein stationary.

How comparisons enter.

Random smoothing admits a convenient two-point gradient formula: if

u\sim\mathrm{Unif}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
, then

\nabla f_{\delta}(x)\;=\;\frac{d}{2\delta}\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[\big(f(x+\delta u)-f(x-\delta u)\big)\,u\right].

This representation reduces the problem of estimating
\nabla f_{\delta}(x)
to estimating the

directional value difference

\Delta_{u}\;:=\;f(x+\delta u)-f(x-\delta u).

In particular, any estimator
\widehat{\Delta}_{u}
satisfying

\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\Delta}_{u}\mid u]=\Delta_{u}
yields an unbiased gradient estimator

\widehat{g}(x)\;:=\;\frac{d}{2\delta}\,\widehat{\Delta}_{u}\,u,\qquad\text{so that}\qquad\mathbb{E}[\widehat{g}(x)\mid x]=\nabla f_{\delta}(x),

and can therefore be plugged into standard stochastic gradient methods for minimizing
f_{\delta}
. The remaining challenge is to construct
\widehat{\Delta}_{u}
using only comparison feedback.

2.2
Comparison model and link function

Comparison model.

We access
f
only through a pairwise-comparison oracle. Given any two points
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the
oracle returns a random variable
Y\in\{0,1\}
such that

\mathbb{P}(Y=1\mid x,y)\;=\;\sigma\!\big(f(y)-f(x)\big),

where
\sigma:\mathbb{R}\to(0,1)
is a known and strictly increasing
link function
. We interpret

Y=1
as “
y
is preferred to
x
”. Writing

\Delta:=f(y)-f(x)
, and
p:=\sigma(\Delta),

repeated queries of the same pair yield i.i.d. samples

Y_{1},\dots,Y_{m}\overset{\mathrm{i.i.d.}}{\sim}\mathrm{Bernoulli}(p)
.

Operated probability range.

Our algorithm queries only local pairs of the form
x-\delta u
and
x+\delta u
, where

u\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
. Define the directional gap

\Delta_{u}\;:=\;f(x+\delta u)-f(x-\delta u).

By assuming that
f
is
L
-Lipschitz, then

|\Delta_{u}|\;\leq\;2L\delta.

Set

B\;:=\;2L\delta.

Then for every queried direction
u
, the corresponding success probability

p\;=\;\sigma(\Delta_{u})

is confined to a fixed interval

p\in[p_{-},p_{+}],\qquad p_{-}\;:=\;\sigma(-B),\qquad p_{+}\;:=\;\sigma(B).

We call the interval
[p_{-},p_{+}]
the
operated probability range
.

3
Logistic link and unbiased gap estimation from comparisons

Comparison oracle (logistic link).

Fix
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and denote the gap

\Delta\;:=\;f(y)-f(x).

A comparison query at
(x,y)
returns
Y\in\{0,1\}
such that

\mathbb{P}(Y=1\mid x,y)\;=\;\sigma_{\tau}(\Delta)\;:=\;\frac{1}{1+e^{-\Delta/\tau}}\;=\;\sigma(\Delta/\tau),\qquad\sigma(t):=\frac{1}{1+e^{-t}},

where
\tau>0
is the temperature (noise scale). We interpret
Y=1
as “
y
is preferred to
x
”.

Bounded gaps via symmetric queries.

We enforce bounded gaps by querying symmetric pairs. Given a radius
\delta>0
and
u\in S^{d-1}
, we compare

x+\delta u\quad\text{vs.}\quad x-\delta u.

If
f
is
L
-Lipschitz, then

\big|f(x+\delta u)-f(x-\delta u)\big|\;\leq\;2L\delta\;=:\;B.

Hence, for every queried pair,
|\Delta|\leq B
and the comparison probability

p:=\sigma_{\tau}(\Delta)
satisfies

p\in[p_{-},p_{+}],\qquad p_{-}:=\sigma_{\tau}(-B)=\sigma(-B/\tau),\qquad p_{+}:=\sigma_{\tau}(B)=\sigma(B/\tau),

so
p
is uniformly bounded away from
0
and
1
as soon as
B/\tau
is bounded below.

Temperature scaling.

Only the normalized gap
\Delta/\tau
matters. When
\tau\gg B
, we have
|\Delta|/\tau\ll 1
and thus

p=\sigma(\Delta/\tau)\approx 1/2
, i.e., comparisons become nearly random.
In what follows it is natural (and mild) to assume
\tau\lesssim B
(equivalently
B/\tau\gtrsim 1
),
so that comparisons retain a nontrivial signal-to-noise ratio on the range of queried gaps.

Why not use the plug-in logit estimator.

A natural approach is to estimate
p
by
\hat{p}=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}Y_{i}
from
m
repeated comparisons and output

\tau\log\!\big(\frac{\hat{p}}{1-\hat{p}}\big)
. This is problematic because (i) the logit is nonlinear, hence biased,
and (ii)
\hat{p}\in\{0,1\}
with nonzero probability, making the logit undefined and producing heavy tails.
We therefore construct an exactly unbiased estimator with controlled moments.

3.1
A series representation

Lemma 3.1

(Logit series)
.

For any
p\in(0,1)
,

\log\frac{p}{1-p}\;=\;\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{p^{m}-(1-p)^{m}}{m}.

Proof.

For
|t|<1
,
\log(1-t)=-\sum_{m\geq 1}t^{m}/m
.
Apply with
t=1-p
and
t=p
, and subtract.
∎

Combining Lemma
3.1
with
\Delta=\tau\log\!\big(\frac{p}{1-p}\big)
yields

\Delta\;=\;\tau\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{p^{m}-(1-p)^{m}}{m}.

3.2
Estimating the series terms from comparisons

Fix
(x,y)
and repeat the
same
comparison query
m
times to obtain i.i.d. samples

Y_{1},\dots,Y_{m}\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}(p)
. Define

A_{m}:=\mathbf{1}\{Y_{1}=\cdots=Y_{m}=1\}

and

B_{m}:=\mathbf{1}\{Y_{1}=\cdots=Y_{m}=0\}.

Then
\mathbb{E}[A_{m}]=p^{m}
and
\mathbb{E}[B_{m}]=(1-p)^{m}
, so
A_{m}-B_{m}
is an unbiased estimator of
p^{m}-(1-p)^{m}
.

3.3
Russian-roulette truncation

Let
M\in\{1,2,\dots\}
be an integer-valued random variable sampled independently of all comparisons, and let

q_{m}:=\mathbb{P}(M\geq m).

For each
m\leq M
, we use a
fresh block
of
m
repeated comparisons (independent across different
m
) to form

A_{m}
and
B_{m}
, and output the Russian-roulette estimator

\widehat{\Delta}\;:=\;\tau\sum_{m=1}^{M}\frac{A_{m}-B_{m}}{m\,q_{m}}.

Geometric truncation.

Fix
\beta\in(0,1)
and sample
M
with
\mathbb{P}(M=m)=(1-\beta)\beta^{m-1}
.
Then
q_{m}=\mathbb{P}(M\geq m)=\beta^{m-1}
.

3.4
Unbiasedness, expected cost, and second moment

Lemma 3.2

(Unbiasedness)
.

For any truncation
M
independent of the comparison outcomes,

\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\Delta}]=\Delta.

Proof.

We have

\widehat{\Delta}=\tau\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{\mathbf{1}\{M\geq m\}}{m\,q_{m}}(A_{m}-B_{m}).

Since
\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{1}\{M\geq m\}/q_{m}]=1
and
\mathbb{E}[A_{m}-B_{m}]=p^{m}-(1-p)^{m}
, we obtain

\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\Delta}]=\tau\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{p^{m}-(1-p)^{m}}{m}=\tau\log\frac{p}{1-p}=\Delta.

∎

Lemma 3.3

(Expected comparison cost)
.

Under geometric truncation with parameter
\beta
, the total number of comparisons used to compute
\widehat{\Delta}
is

N=\sum_{m=1}^{M}m=\frac{M(M+1)}{2}
, and

\mathbb{E}[N]=\frac{1}{(1-\beta)^{2}}.

Proof.

For geometric
M
,
\mathbb{E}[M]=1/(1-\beta)
and
\mathbb{E}[M^{2}]=(1+\beta)/(1-\beta)^{2}
. Hence

\mathbb{E}[M(M+1)/2]=(\mathbb{E}[M^{2}]+\mathbb{E}[M])/2=1/(1-\beta)^{2}
.
∎

Lemma 3.4

(Finite second moment)
.

Assume
|\Delta|\leq B
and geometric truncation
q_{m}=\beta^{m-1}
. Let

\alpha:=\max\{p_{+},\,1-p_{-}\}.

If
\beta>\alpha
, then
\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\Delta}^{\,2}]<\infty
. Moreover,

\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\Delta}^{\,2}]\leq B^{2}+\tau^{2}\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{p_{+}^{m}+(1-p_{-})^{m}}{m^{2}\,q_{m}}+\tau^{2}\left(\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{p_{+}^{m}+(1-p_{-})^{m}}{m\,q_{m}}\right)^{2}.

Proof.

Let
Z_{m}:=(A_{m}-B_{m})/(mq_{m})
and write
\widehat{\Delta}=\tau\sum_{m\geq 1}\mathbf{1}\{M\geq m\}Z_{m}
.
Using
\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\Delta}^{\,2}]=Var(\widehat{\Delta})+(\mathbb{E}\widehat{\Delta})^{2}
and
\Delta^{2}\leq B^{2}
, it suffices to bound

Var(\widehat{\Delta})
. Decompose

Var(\widehat{\Delta})=\mathbb{E}[Var(\widehat{\Delta}\mid M)]+Var(\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\Delta}\mid M]).

Conditioned on
M
, the blocks across
m
are independent, hence

Var(\widehat{\Delta}\mid M)=\tau^{2}\sum_{m=1}^{M}Var(Z_{m})\leq\tau^{2}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\mathbb{E}[Z_{m}^{2}].

Taking expectation in
M
gives

\mathbb{E}[Var(\widehat{\Delta}\mid M)]\leq\tau^{2}\sum_{m\geq 1}q_{m}\,\mathbb{E}[Z_{m}^{2}].

Since
(A_{m}-B_{m})^{2}=A_{m}+B_{m}
,

\mathbb{E}[Z_{m}^{2}]=\frac{\mathbb{E}[A_{m}+B_{m}]}{m^{2}q_{m}^{2}}=\frac{p^{m}+(1-p)^{m}}{m^{2}q_{m}^{2}}\leq\frac{p_{+}^{m}+(1-p_{-})^{m}}{m^{2}q_{m}^{2}},

which yields the first series term after multiplying by
q_{m}
.
For the second term,
\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\Delta}\mid M]=\tau\sum_{m=1}^{M}\mu_{m}
where

\mu_{m}=\mathbb{E}[Z_{m}]=(p^{m}-(1-p)^{m})/(mq_{m})
, so

Var(\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\Delta}\mid M])\leq\mathbb{E}[(\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\Delta}\mid M])^{2}]\leq\tau^{2}\left(\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{p_{+}^{m}+(1-p_{-})^{m}}{mq_{m}}\right)^{2}.

Finally,
\beta>\alpha
ensures convergence since
q_{m}=\beta^{m-1}
.
∎

Corollary 3.5

(Second moment scales as
O(B^{2})
)
.

Assume
|\Delta|\leq B
and geometric truncation
q_{m}=\beta^{m-1}
with
\beta\in(p_{+},1)
, where
p_{+}=\sigma_{\tau}(B)
.
If moreover
\tau=\mathcal{O}(B)
, then
\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\Delta}^{\,2}]=\mathcal{O}(B^{2})
.

Proof.

For the logistic link,
p_{-}=1-p_{+}
, hence
p_{+}^{m}+(1-p_{-})^{m}=2p_{+}^{m}
.
With
q_{m}=\beta^{m-1}
and
\beta\in(p_{+},1)
, the two series in Lemma
3.4
converge to finite constants
depending only on
p_{+}/\beta<1
. Therefore
\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\Delta}^{\,2}]\leq B^{2}+C\tau^{2}
for some
C<\infty
.
Since
\tau=\mathcal{O}(B)
, we have
\tau^{2}=\mathcal{O}(B^{2})
, which implies
\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\Delta}^{\,2}]=\mathcal{O}(B^{2})
.
∎

3.5
Plugging
\widehat{\Delta}
into two-point smoothing

Smoothing objective.

Fix
\delta>0
, we have

\nabla f_{\delta}(x)=\frac{d}{\delta}\,\mathbb{E}_{u\sim\mathrm{Unif}(S^{d-1})}\big[f(x+\delta u)\,u\big]=\frac{d}{2\delta}\,\mathbb{E}_{u\sim\mathrm{Unif}(S^{d-1})}\big[(f(x+\delta u)-f(x-\delta u))\,u\big].

Gradient estimator from comparisons.

Sample
u\sim\mathrm{Unif}(S^{d-1})
and estimate the symmetric gap

\Delta(x,u):=f(x+\delta u)-f(x-\delta u)

by
\widehat{\Delta}(x-\delta u,x+\delta u)
using the procedure above (with fresh comparisons).
Define

G(x;u):=\frac{d}{2\delta}\,\widehat{\Delta}(x-\delta u,\,x+\delta u)\,u.

By Lemma
3.2
and the smoothing identity,
\mathbb{E}[G(x;u)\mid x]=\nabla f_{\delta}(x)
.

Moreover, by Lipschitzness
|\Delta(x,u)|\leq 2L\delta=:B
, so Corollary
3.5
applies whenever

\tau=\mathcal{O}(B)
, yielding
\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\Delta}(x-\delta u,x+\delta u)^{2}]=\mathcal{O}(B^{2})=\mathcal{O}(L^{2}\delta^{2})
.
Therefore,

\mathbb{E}\big[\|G(x;u)\|^{2}\,\big|\,x\big]=\frac{d^{2}}{4\delta^{2}}\,\mathbb{E}\big[\widehat{\Delta}(x-\delta u,x+\delta u)^{2}\,\big|\,x\big]=\mathcal{O}(d^{2}L^{2}).

Finally, the expected number of comparisons used to form
\widehat{\Delta}
is
\mathbb{E}[N]=1/(1-\beta)^{2}
by
Lemma
3.3
, giving an explicit per-iteration comparison cost.

4
General inverse-link expansions compatible with comparisons

This section shows that our comparison-only construction is
not tied to the logistic link
.
The only role of the link is to convert the Bernoulli probability
p
of a comparison outcome into the
latent gap
\Delta=\tau\,g(p)
via the inverse link
g=\sigma^{-1}
. We explain a general condition on

g
under which
\Delta
admits an
unbiased
estimator using comparisons only.

Scaled comparison model and symmetry.

Let
\sigma:\mathbb{R}\to(0,1)
be strictly increasing and define the inverse link

g(p):=\sigma^{-1}(p).

We work in the scaled model

p=\sigma_{\tau}(\Delta):=\sigma(\Delta/\tau)
, where

\Delta=\tau\,g(p).

We assume the standard symmetry

g(1-p)=-g(p)\,\,\,\Longleftrightarrow\,\,\,\sigma(-t)=1-\sigma(t),

which holds for logistic, probit, and many common symmetric links.

Operated interval (what we control).

By query design we restrict to gaps
|\Delta|\leq B
. Then

p=\sigma(\Delta/\tau)\in[p_{-},p_{+}],p_{-}:=\sigma(-B/\tau)
and
p_{+}:=\sigma(B/\tau)

with
0<p_{-}<p_{+}<1
. Under symmetry,
p_{-}=1-p_{+}
so the interval is centered at
1/2
.
Define

\alpha:=\max\{p_{+},\,1-p_{-}\}\in\Big(\frac{1}{2},1\Big).

Intuitively,
\alpha
measures how close the operated interval is to the boundary
\{0,1\}
; staying
away from
0
and
1
is what allows stable expansions of
g
.

Comparison-friendly basis.

For
m\geq 1
define the Bernoulli-product polynomials

b_{m}(p):=p^{m}-(1-p)^{m}.

These functions are
exactly
compatible with comparisons: if

Y_{1},\dots,Y_{m}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{i.i.d.}}}{{\sim}}\mathrm{Bernoulli}(p)
then

\widehat{b}_{m}:=\mathbf{1}\{Y_{1}=\cdots=Y_{m}=1\}-\mathbf{1}\{Y_{1}=\cdots=Y_{m}=0\}\,\,\,\text{satisfies}\,\,\,\mathbb{E}[\widehat{b}_{m}]=b_{m}(p).

Moreover, for
p\in[p_{-},p_{+}]
we have the uniform bound

|b_{m}(p)|\leq p^{m}+(1-p)^{m}\leq 2\alpha^{m}.
We only need a mild regularity assumption ensuring that
g
can be expanded on
[p_{-},p_{+}]
with
geometrically decaying coefficients.

Assumption 4.1

(Analytic inverse on the operated interval)
.

The inverse link
g=\sigma^{-1}
is odd around
1/2
and admits a holomorphic (complex-analytic) extension
to an open neighborhood of the compact interval
[p_{-},p_{+}]
.

Proposition 4.2

(General-link Bernoulli-product expansion)
.

Under Assumption
4.1
, there exist constants
C>0
and
\rho\in(0,1)

(depending only on
g
and the interval
[p_{-},p_{+}]
) and coefficients
(c_{2k-1})_{k\geq 1}
such that for all

p\in[p_{-},p_{+}]
,

g(p)=\sum_{k\geq 1}c_{2k-1}\,b_{2k-1}(p),\qquad|c_{2k-1}|\leq C\,\rho^{k}.

In particular, the above series converges absolutely and uniformly on
[p_{-},p_{+}]
.

Proof sketch.

Since
g
is holomorphic on a neighborhood of
[p_{-},p_{+}]
, standard approximation theory yields a sequence of
polynomials
(P_{r})_{r\geq 1}
such that
\sup_{p\in[p_{-},p_{+}]}|g(p)-P_{r}(p)|
decays geometrically in
r
.
By symmetry,
g
is odd around
1/2
, so we may take
P_{r}
to be odd around
1/2
as well (e.g., by odd symmetrization). For each
r
, the family
\{b_{1},b_{3},\dots,b_{2r-1}\}
spans the space of polynomials odd around
1/2

of degree at most
2r-1
, hence there exist coefficients
(c^{(r)}_{2k-1})_{k\leq r}
such that

P_{r}(p)=\sum_{k=1}^{r}c^{(r)}_{2k-1}\,b_{2k-1}(p)
.
Taking
r\to\infty
and using the geometric approximation rate implies that these finite expansions converge
uniformly to
g
on
[p_{-},p_{+}]
, yielding an infinite series representation in the
b_{2k-1}
basis.
Finally, analyticity on a neighborhood of
[p_{-},p_{+}]
implies geometric decay of the resulting coefficients,
i.e.,
|c_{2k-1}|\leq C\rho^{k}
for some
\rho\in(0,1)
.
∎

Unbiased estimator for
\Delta
.

Let
Y\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}(p)
represent a single comparison outcome.
For each
k\geq 1
, draw an independent block of size
2k-1
:

Y^{(k)}_{1},\dots,Y^{(k)}_{2k-1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{i.i.d.}}}{{\sim}}\mathrm{Bernoulli}(p)
,
independent across
k
, and define

A_{2k-1}:=\mathbf{1}\{Y^{(k)}_{1}=\cdots=Y^{(k)}_{2k-1}=1\},\qquad B_{2k-1}:=\mathbf{1}\{Y^{(k)}_{1}=\cdots=Y^{(k)}_{2k-1}=0\}.

Then
\mathbb{E}[A_{2k-1}-B_{2k-1}]=b_{2k-1}(p)
.
Let
M
be independent of all comparisons with survival probabilities
q_{k}:=\mathbb{P}(M\geq k)
.
Define the Russian-roulette estimator

\widehat{\Delta}:=\tau\sum_{k=1}^{M}\frac{c_{2k-1}}{q_{k}}\,\big(A_{2k-1}-B_{2k-1}\big).

Lemma 4.3

(Unbiasedness)
.

Assume
p\in[p_{-},p_{+}]
and
\sum_{k\geq 1}|c_{2k-1}|\,\alpha^{2k-1}<\infty
.
Then
\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\Delta}]=\Delta
.
In particular, under Assumption
4.1
the estimator above is unbiased.

Proof.

Write the estimator as

\widehat{\Delta}=\tau\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{\mathbf{1}\{M\geq k\}}{q_{k}}\,c_{2k-1}\,\big(A_{2k-1}-B_{2k-1}\big)
, where
q_{k}:=\mathbb{P}(M\geq k).

We first justify exchanging expectation and the infinite sum.

For each
k
, we have
|A_{2k-1}-B_{2k-1}|\leq A_{2k-1}+B_{2k-1}
and

\mathbb{E}[A_{2k-1}+B_{2k-1}]=p^{2k-1}+(1-p)^{2k-1}\leq 2\alpha^{2k-1}.

Moreover,
\mathbb{E}\!\left[\frac{\mathbf{1}\{M\geq k\}}{q_{k}}\right]=1
since
\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{1}\{M\geq k\}]=q_{k}
.
Therefore, by Tonelli’s theorem,

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq 1}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left|\frac{\mathbf{1}\{M\geq k\}}{q_{k}}\,c_{2k-1}\,(A_{2k-1}-B_{2k-1})\right|\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\geq 1}|c_{2k-1}|\,\mathbb{E}[A_{2k-1}+B_{2k-1}]

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{k\geq 1}|c_{2k-1}|\,\alpha^{2k-1}\;<\;\infty.

Hence we may interchange expectation and summation:

\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\Delta}]=\tau\sum_{k\geq 1}c_{2k-1}\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[\frac{\mathbf{1}\{M\geq k\}}{q_{k}}\right]\,\mathbb{E}[A_{2k-1}-B_{2k-1}].

Finally, the blocks are independent and
\mathbb{E}[A_{2k-1}-B_{2k-1}]=b_{2k-1}(p)
, so

\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\Delta}]=\tau\sum_{k\geq 1}c_{2k-1}\,b_{2k-1}(p)=\tau g(p)=\Delta.

∎

Remark 4.4

(Examples: probit and cauchit)
.

Assumption
4.1
holds for many classical symmetric links on any operated interval

[p_{-},p_{+}]\subset(0,1)
bounded away from
\{0,1\}
.

Probit:

\sigma(t)=\Phi(t)
, so
g=\Phi^{-1}
is holomorphic on a neighborhood of any compact

[p_{-},p_{+}]\subset(0,1)
.

Cauchit:

\sigma(t)=\tfrac{1}{2}+\tfrac{1}{\pi}\arctan(t)
, so
g(p)=\tan(\pi(p-\tfrac{1}{2}))
is meromorphic with poles
at
p\in\mathbb{Z}+\{0,1\}
and hence holomorphic on a neighborhood of any compact
[p_{-},p_{+}]\subset(0,1)
.

Lemma 4.5

(Second moment control)
.

Assume
|\Delta|\leq B
so that
p\in[p_{-},p_{+}]
.
Define

S_{2}:=\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{c_{2k-1}^{2}}{q_{k}}\,\alpha^{2k-1},\qquad S_{1}:=\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{|c_{2k-1}|}{\sqrt{q_{k}}}\,\alpha^{k-\frac{1}{2}}.

If
S_{1}<\infty
and
S_{2}<\infty
, then

\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\Delta}^{\,2}]\;\leq\;B^{2}\;+\;2\tau^{2}S_{2}\;+\;2\tau^{2}S_{1}^{2}.

In particular, if
\tau=\mathcal{O}(B)
and
S_{1},S_{2}=\mathcal{O}(1)
, then
\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\Delta}^{\,2}]=\mathcal{O}(B^{2})
.

Proof.

Let

X_{k}:=\frac{c_{2k-1}}{q_{k}}\,\big(A_{2k-1}-B_{2k-1}\big),\qquad\mu_{k}:=\mathbb{E}[X_{k}].

Then
\widehat{\Delta}=\tau\sum_{k\geq 1}\mathbf{1}\{M\geq k\}X_{k}
and
(\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\Delta}])^{2}=\Delta^{2}\leq B^{2}
, hence

\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\Delta}^{\,2}]\leq B^{2}+Var(\widehat{\Delta}).

Conditioned on
M
, the blocks are independent, so

Var(\widehat{\Delta})=\mathbb{E}\!\left[Var(\widehat{\Delta}\mid M)\right]+Var\!\left(\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\Delta}\mid M]\right).

For the first term, using
Var(X_{k})\leq\mathbb{E}[X_{k}^{2}]
and
\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{1}\{M\geq k\}]=q_{k}
,

\mathbb{E}\!\left[Var(\widehat{\Delta}\mid M)\right]\leq\tau^{2}\sum_{k\geq 1}q_{k}\,\mathbb{E}[X_{k}^{2}].

Moreover,
(A_{m}-B_{m})^{2}=A_{m}+B_{m}
and

\mathbb{E}[A_{m}+B_{m}]=p^{m}+(1-p)^{m}\leq 2\alpha^{m}
, hence

\mathbb{E}[X_{k}^{2}]=\frac{c_{2k-1}^{2}}{q_{k}^{2}}\,\mathbb{E}[A_{2k-1}+B_{2k-1}]\leq\frac{2c_{2k-1}^{2}}{q_{k}^{2}}\,\alpha^{2k-1}.

Therefore

\mathbb{E}\!\left[Var(\widehat{\Delta}\mid M)\right]\leq 2\tau^{2}\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{c_{2k-1}^{2}}{q_{k}}\,\alpha^{2k-1}=2\tau^{2}S_{2}.
For the second term, by Cauchy–Schwarz and
Var(\mathbf{1}\{M\geq k\})\leq q_{k}
,

Var\!\left(\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\Delta}\mid M]\right)=\tau^{2}Var\!\left(\sum_{k\geq 1}\mathbf{1}\{M\geq k\}\mu_{k}\right)\leq\tau^{2}\Big(\sum_{k\geq 1}\sqrt{q_{k}}\,|\mu_{k}|\Big)^{2}.

Now
\mu_{k}=\frac{c_{2k-1}}{q_{k}}\,b_{2k-1}(p)
and

|b_{2k-1}(p)|=|\mathbb{E}[A_{2k-1}-B_{2k-1}]|\leq\mathbb{E}[(A_{2k-1}-B_{2k-1})^{2}]^{1/2}=\mathbb{E}[A_{2k-1}+B_{2k-1}]^{1/2}\leq\sqrt{2}\,\alpha^{k-\frac{1}{2}}.

Hence

\sqrt{q_{k}}\,|\mu_{k}|\leq\sqrt{2}\,\frac{|c_{2k-1}|}{\sqrt{q_{k}}}\,\alpha^{k-\frac{1}{2}},

so

Var\!\left(\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\Delta}\mid M]\right)\leq 2\tau^{2}\Big(\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{|c_{2k-1}|}{\sqrt{q_{k}}}\,\alpha^{k-\frac{1}{2}}\Big)^{2}=2\tau^{2}S_{1}^{2}.

Combining the two bounds yields

Var(\widehat{\Delta})\leq 2\tau^{2}S_{2}+2\tau^{2}S_{1}^{2}.

∎

Corollary 4.6

(Geometric truncation gives a second-moment bound)
.

Assume Assumption
4.1
and
|\Delta|\leq B
, so that
p\in[p_{-},p_{+}]
and
\alpha<1
.
Let
(c_{2k-1})_{k\geq 1}
be the coefficients provided by
Proposition
4.2
, so that
|c_{2k-1}|\leq C\,\rho^{k}
for some
C>0
and
\rho\in(0,1)
.
Let
M
be geometric with parameter
\beta\in(0,1)
, so that
q_{k}=\mathbb{P}(M\geq k)=\beta^{k-1}
.
If

\beta>\alpha^{2}\rho^{2},

then
\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\Delta}^{\,2}]\leq B^{2}+C_{0}\tau^{2}
for a constant
C_{0}
depending only on

\alpha,\beta,C,\rho
. In particular, if
\tau=\mathcal{O}(B)
then

\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\Delta}^{\,2}]=\mathcal{O}(B^{2})
.

Proof.

By Proposition
4.2
we may take
|c_{2k-1}|\leq C\rho^{k}
.
With
q_{k}=\beta^{k-1}
, the quantities in Lemma
4.5
satisfy

S_{2}=\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{c_{2k-1}^{2}}{q_{k}}\,\alpha^{2k-1}\leq C^{2}\,\alpha\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{\rho^{2k}}{\beta^{k-1}}\,\alpha^{2k-2}=C^{2}\,\alpha\,\rho^{2}\sum_{k\geq 1}\Big(\frac{\alpha^{2}\rho^{2}}{\beta}\Big)^{k-1},

and

S_{1}=\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{|c_{2k-1}|}{\sqrt{q_{k}}}\,\alpha^{k-\frac{1}{2}}\leq C\,\alpha^{1/2}\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{\rho^{k}}{\beta^{(k-1)/2}}\,\alpha^{k-1}=C\,\alpha^{1/2}\rho\sum_{k\geq 1}\Big(\frac{\alpha\rho}{\sqrt{\beta}}\Big)^{k-1}.

The condition
\beta>\alpha^{2}\rho^{2}
implies both geometric series converge, hence
S_{1},S_{2}<\infty
and in fact

S_{1},S_{2}=\mathcal{O}(1)
with constants depending only on
\alpha,\beta,C,\rho
. Applying Lemma
4.5
yields

\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\Delta}^{\,2}]\leq B^{2}+2\tau^{2}S_{2}+2\tau^{2}S_{1}^{2}\leq B^{2}+C_{0}\tau^{2}

for some constant
C_{0}=C_{0}(\alpha,\beta,C,\rho)
.
If
\tau=\mathcal{O}(B)
, then
\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\Delta}^{\,2}]=\mathcal{O}(B^{2})
.
∎

4.1
End-to-end comparison complexity for
(\delta,\varepsilon)
-Goldstein stationarity

We now combine the unbiased comparison-based gap estimator from Section 3.5 with a standard
nonconvex SGD guarantee to obtain an explicit
expected comparison-complexity
bound.

Algorithm (Comparison-SGD on
f_{\delta}
).

Initialize
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. For
t=0,1,\dots,T-1
:
sample
u_{t}\sim\mathrm{Unif}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
, form an unbiased gap estimate

\widehat{\Delta}_{t}:=\widehat{\Delta}(x_{t}-\delta u_{t},\;x_{t}+\delta u_{t})
using the
Russian-roulette procedure (with fresh comparisons), define

G_{t}\;:=\;G(x_{t};u_{t})\;=\;\frac{d}{2\delta}\,\widehat{\Delta}_{t}\,u_{t},

and update
x_{t+1}=x_{t}-\eta G_{t}
. Output
x_{R}
where
R
is uniform on
\{0,\dots,T-1\}

and independent of the algorithmic randomness.

Smoothness of the smoothed objective.

If
f
is
L
-Lipschitz and
f_{\delta}(x)=\mathbb{E}_{v\sim\mathrm{Unif}(\mathbb{B}^{d})}[f(x+\delta v)]
,
then
f_{\delta}
is differentiable and
L_{\delta}
-smooth with
L_{\delta}=L\sqrt{d}/\delta
.

Lemma 4.7

(Nonconvex SGD with unbiased gradients)
.

Let
F:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be
L_{F}
-smooth and bounded below by
F^{\star}
.
Consider iterates
x_{t+1}=x_{t}-\eta G_{t}
such that, for all
t
,

\mathbb{E}\!\left[G_{t}\,\middle|\,x_{t}\right]\;=\;\nabla F(x_{t})
and

\mathbb{E}\!\left[\|G_{t}\|^{2}\,\middle|\,x_{t}\right]\;\leq\;V,

for some finite constant
V
. If
\eta\leq 1/L_{F}
, then

\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mathbb{E}\big[\|\nabla F(x_{t})\|^{2}\big]\;\leq\;\frac{2(F(x_{0})-F^{\star})}{\eta T}\;+\;\eta L_{F}V.

Main theorem (expected comparison complexity).

We instantiate Lemma
4.7
with
F=f_{\delta}
and the estimator
G_{t}
above.
By Section 3.5,
\mathbb{E}[G_{t}\mid x_{t}]=\nabla f_{\delta}(x_{t})
.
Assume moreover that the gap estimator has a uniform conditional second-moment bound:

\mathbb{E}\!\left[\widehat{\Delta}_{t}^{\,2}\,\middle|\,x_{t},u_{t}\right]\;\leq\;C_{\Delta}\,B^{2}\qquad\text{for all }t,

(1)

where
B:=2L\delta
(since
|\Delta(x_{t},u_{t})|\leq 2L\delta
for an
L
-Lipschitz
f
)
and
C_{\Delta}<\infty
is a constant.

Theorem 4.8

(Complexity bound for
(\delta,\varepsilon)
-Goldstein stationarity)
.

Assume
f
is bounded below and
L
-Lipschitz, and fix
\delta>0
.
Run Comparison-SGD on
f_{\delta}
for
T
steps with step size

\eta\;\leq\;\frac{1}{L_{\delta}}\;=\;\frac{\delta}{L\sqrt{d}},

and suppose (
1
) holds for some
C_{\Delta}<\infty
.
Let
f_{\delta}^{\star}:=\inf_{x}f_{\delta}(x)
and
\Delta_{0}:=f_{\delta}(x_{0})-f_{\delta}^{\star}
.
Then for
R\sim\mathrm{Unif}\{0,\dots,T-1\}
independent,

\mathbb{E}\big[\|\nabla f_{\delta}(x_{R})\|^{2}\big]\;\leq\;\frac{2\Delta_{0}}{\eta T}\;+\;\eta L_{\delta}\cdot\frac{d^{2}}{4\delta^{2}}\,C_{\Delta}B^{2}.

In particular, choosing

\eta\;=\;\min\!\left\{\frac{1}{L_{\delta}},\;\sqrt{\frac{2\Delta_{0}}{L_{\delta}T\cdot\frac{d^{2}}{4\delta^{2}}C_{\Delta}B^{2}}}\right\}

yields

\mathbb{E}\big[\|\nabla f_{\delta}(x_{R})\|^{2}\big]\;\leq\;2\sqrt{\frac{2L_{\delta}\Delta_{0}}{T}\cdot\frac{d^{2}}{4\delta^{2}}C_{\Delta}B^{2}}\,.

Consequently, it suffices to take

T\;\geq\;\frac{8L_{\delta}\Delta_{0}}{\varepsilon^{4}}\cdot\frac{d^{2}}{4\delta^{2}}C_{\Delta}B^{2}

to ensure

\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla f_{\delta}(x_{R})\|]\leq\varepsilon
.
By Lemma
2.1
, this implies that
x_{R}
is
(\delta,\varepsilon)
-Goldstein stationary.

Moreover, if each gap estimate uses a (random) number
N
of comparisons with
\mathbb{E}[N]<\infty
,
then the expected total number of comparisons used by the algorithm is at most

\mathbb{E}[\#\text{comparisons}]\;\leq\;T\cdot\mathbb{E}[N].

Explicit specialization under
L_{\delta}=L\sqrt{d}/\delta
and geometric truncation.

Under (
1
) with
B=2L\delta
,

\mathbb{E}\!\left[\|G_{t}\|^{2}\,\middle|\,x_{t},u_{t}\right]\;=\;\frac{d^{2}}{4\delta^{2}}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\widehat{\Delta}_{t}^{\,2}\,\middle|\,x_{t},u_{t}\right]\;\leq\;\frac{d^{2}}{4\delta^{2}}\,C_{\Delta}(2L\delta)^{2}\;=\;C_{\Delta}d^{2}L^{2},

so Theorem
4.8
gives

T\;\geq\;\frac{8L_{\delta}\Delta_{0}}{\varepsilon^{4}}\cdot C_{\Delta}d^{2}L^{2}\;=\;\frac{8\,C_{\Delta}\,d^{5/2}L^{3}\,\Delta_{0}}{\delta\,\varepsilon^{4}}.

For the logistic construction with geometric truncation parameter
\beta
,
Section
3.3
gives
\mathbb{E}[N]=1/(1-\beta)^{2}
, hence the expected comparison complexity is

\mathbb{E}[\#\text{comparisons}]\;\leq\;T\cdot\mathbb{E}[N]\;=\;O\!\left(\frac{C_{\Delta}\,d^{5/2}L^{3}\,\Delta_{0}}{\delta\,\varepsilon^{4}}\cdot\frac{1}{(1-\beta)^{2}}\right).

Related Work

Comparison-Based Optimization.

Optimization utilizing only relative preferences has been extensively studied under the umbrella of dueling bandits and derivative-free optimization. In the
convex
setting, theoretical guarantees are well-established for both noiseless and noisy comparison oracles
[
11
,
12
]
. For
smooth nonconvex
functions, earlier works focused on deterministic feedback or asymptotic convergence
[
2
]
. More recently,
[
13
]
and
[
5
]
derived non-asymptotic rates for smooth nonconvex optimization using ranking and comparison feedback, respectively. Most recently,
[
1
]
extended these results to the stochastic regime with noisy comparisons. However, all these works rely on the smoothness of the underlying objective to control the linearization error of the gradient estimators. Our work departs from this by addressing the
nonsmooth
regime, where gradients may not exist and standard smooth comparison-based estimators fail.

Nonsmooth Zeroth-Order Optimization.

For nonsmooth Lipschitz functions, the standard solution concept is
(\delta,\varepsilon)
-Goldstein stationarity
[
6
]
. In the setting of
value-based
zeroth-order optimization (where algorithms observe
f(x)
),
[
9
]
pioneered the use of randomized smoothing to convert the nonsmooth problem into a smooth surrogate problem, proving convergence for deterministic oracles. This was extended to stochastic value oracles by
[
7
]
. Crucially,
[
8
]
established the optimal dimension-dependence for this setting
, refuting earlier conjectures regarding the necessary scaling with dimension. On the fundamental limits of this regime,
[
14
]
established tight lower bounds
, confirming the hardness of the problem and the necessity of the Goldstein stationarity relaxation. Our approach leverages the randomized smoothing geometry used in these works but fundamentally differs in the estimation step: we cannot observe function differences directly. Instead, we introduce a novel unbiased estimator for the inverse link function to bridge the gap between binary comparison feedback and the continuous value differences required by the Goldstein stationary framework.

6
Conclusion and Discussion

In this work, we presented the first comparison-based optimization algorithm with non-asymptotic convergence guarantees for nonsmooth, nonconvex Lipschitz functions. By targeting
(\delta,\epsilon)
-Goldstein stationarity, we circumvented the ill-defined nature of gradients in the nonsmooth regime. Our key technical innovation is an unbiased estimator for local function differences that operates solely on binary comparison feedback. Through a Russian-roulette truncation of the inverse link’s Bernoulli expansion, we achieved an estimator with finite expected cost and variance scaling as
\mathcal{O}(B^{2})
. This variance control allows our method to match the convergence rate of value-based zeroth-order methods (up to constants depending on dimension and link parameters), scaling as
\mathcal{O}(d^{5/2}\delta^{-1}\epsilon^{-4})
.

Limitations and Future Work.

Our current analysis relies on the knowledge of the link function
\sigma
to construct the inverse expansion. Extending this to
unknown
or semi-parametric link models is an important open direction. Additionally, while the
\epsilon^{-4}
dependence is standard for SGD on smooth surrogates, acceleration techniques for Goldstein stationarity remains a challenging frontier. Finally, our method assumes unconstrained optimization on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
; adapting the randomized smoothing and comparison geometry to constrained settings or Riemannian manifolds would broaden the applicability of these results to complex control and ranking problems.

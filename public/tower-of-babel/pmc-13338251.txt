Mechanical fault diagnosis method based on multi-strategy improved SSA
Authors: Xiangtao Kong
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractTo address the unstable target localization and insufficient path planning efficiency caused by complex lighting and dynamic environments during automated robotic arm loading, this study proposes a method for automated robotic arm loading that integrates visual guidance and an improved sparrow search algorithm. This method constructs a visual guidance system based on a depth camera, achieving reliable target object recognition and 3D pose mapping through template matching and depth information fusion, providing stable spatial perception information for robotic arm grasping. Furthermore, non-dominated sorting and multinomial mutation mechanisms are introduced to improve the traditional sparrow search algorithm, establishing a trajectory planning model with motion time and impact smoothness as optimization objectives, thereby enhancing the algorithm’s optimization ability under multi-objective constraints and dynamic environments. Experimental results show that in typical industrial scenarios, this method achieves a high grasping success rate and outperforms comparative methods in positioning accuracy and path planning efficiency, validating the effectiveness and practicality of the proposed method in automated robotic arm loading tasks.Keywords: Sparrow search algorithm, Multi-strategy improvement method, Machine learning, Feature extraction, Mechanical fault diagnosisSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

The fault diagnosis (FD) and operation state monitoring technologies of mechanical equipment have steadily emerged as the essential to ensuring production efficiency and safety as modern industry has evolved toward artificial intelligence1. Conventional mechanical FD techniques mostly use artificial experience and signal processing methods such as Fourier transform and wavelet analysis. The efficiency and diagnostic accuracy of FD are relatively low when dealing with high-dimensional, nonlinear, and very noisy industrial data2. The quick advancement of machine learning (ML) technology in recent years has led to novel mechanical FD solutions. These include methods such as support vector machines (SVM) and convolutional neural networks (CNN)3. However, These methods are costly to train the model and need large amount of labeled data. Fault data are hard to come by and dispersed unevenly in actual industrial settings. Mechanical FD widely uses optimization algorithms to solve the above problems4.

Due to its excellent global search capabilities and quick convergence speed, the sparrow search algorithm (SSA) is frequently employed in FD5. An FD method for nuclear power plants grounded on SSA optimized CNN has been offered by Zhang C et al. The method extracted features using CNN, fed feature data into long and short memory networks to mine temporal features, and optimized the network parameters by SSA. It was found that the fault recognition accuracy could reach 98.24%6. An integrated approach for rolling bearing FD based on SSA improvement was presented by Wang M et al. Following the optimization of the variational mode decomposition (VMD) parameters using SSA, the recovered features were subsequently input into SVM for fault classification. According to the research results, this technology successfully improved the accuracy of rolling bearing FD7. Wang B et al. developed a low-speed bearing FD method grounded on SSA to address the problems of substantial environmental noise and poor fault information at low speeds. It was found that the method could effectively reduce noise interference and accurately extract fault features8. Hu L et al. proposed a transformer fault classification model based on the probabilistic neural network optimized by SSA. The model established a mapping model between gas ratio and fault type by installing a smart gas sensor in the transformer. Experimental results showed that the prediction accuracy reached 90%9. Ding C et al. proposed an FD method for oil-immersed transformers based on improved SSA optimized SVM. The method extracted feature data through dissolved gas analysis and optimized the parameters of SVM using improved SSA. The findings demonstrated that the diagnosis accuracy was 98.1013%, a significant improvement over the conventional approach10.

Recently, researchers have explored advanced intelligent techniques to further enhance the performance of mechanical fault diagnosis under complex industrial conditions. Wen Z et al. proposed a fine-grained visual-text feature alignment framework with prompt tuning for domain-adaptive object detection, demonstrating the potential of cross-modal feature learning in improving diagnostic generalization across different working conditions11. In the context of industrial defect detection, Wen Z et al. developed a triple semantic-aware knowledge distillation network that effectively transfers multi-level semantic information, achieving high-precision defect recognition with limited labeled samples12. Additionally, Zhang G et al. introduced a motor current signal-based fault diagnosis method for harmonic drives in industrial robots under time-varying speed conditions, highlighting the importance of adaptive feature extraction in non-stationary operational environments13. These studies underscore the growing trend toward integrating semantic understanding, knowledge transfer, and adaptive signal processing into mechanical fault diagnosis frameworks, providing valuable insights for developing more robust and generalizable diagnostic models.

In addition, the rapid development of ML technology has brought new opportunities and breakthroughs in the field of mechanical FD. Cen J et al. explored a data-driven mechanical FD approach based on ML algorithms. It was pointed out that ML algorithms could effectively improve the reliability of equipment in different industrial production scenarios, but they were deficient in dealing with complex faults or data imbalance problems14. The ML algorithms for mechanical FD and prediction in actual industrial manufacturing scenarios were investigated by Fernandes M et al. The study pointed out that the complexity of manufacturing systems and production processes were important challenges for fault detection and prediction15. Brusa E et al. investigated the interpretable artificial intelligence contributed by features in ML models in industrial equipment condition monitoring. The methodology analyzed how ML models identify and utilize features to diagnose mechanical faults. The research results indicated that a better understanding of feature contributions improved the reliability and transparency of diagnostic models16. An ML-based FD technique for multiphase drive systems was presented by Liu Z et al. To increase the precision of fault feature extraction, the adaptive secondary sampling methodology was used to optimize the data sampling procedure. The findings demonstrated the strong robustness, high accuracy, and remarkable generalization capabilities17. Ng EYK et al. examined how well different machine learning techniques performed in both theoretical underpinnings and real-world applications. Although ML methods demonstrated great potential in wind turbine FD, they still faced many challenges, such as data imbalance, feature selection, and model adaptation18.

Despite the progress of improved SSA variants for FD, many existing approaches (e.g., Chaos-SSA, Lévy-SSA, and adaptive SSA) typically enhance SSA by introducing a single strategy (or loosely coupled strategies) without explicitly binding each strategy to a specific stage of the SSA workflow or tailoring it to the downstream classifier optimization task. In this work, the study proposes a multi-strategy improved SSA (MSISSA), whose key distinction lies in a stage-wise, task-driven integration: (1) Segmented linear chaotic mapping is used only at the initialization stage to increase population diversity and improve the coverage of the SVM hyperparameter search space; (2) Lévy-flight perturbation is embedded during iterative position updates to introduce occasional long-range exploration and reduce the risk of stagnation in local optima; and (3) an adaptive step-size control is adopted to balance exploration (early iterations) and exploitation (late iterations) within a unified update routine for SVM parameter optimization.

Moreover, the proposed dual-channel CNN is not merely a parallel processing of conventional time-frequency features. Instead, it learns complementary representations from time-domain and frequency-domain inputs and performs feature-level fusion to generate a unified deep feature embedding, while the final decision is made by the MSISSA-optimized SVM. This hybrid design forms a “deep feature learning + optimized margin-based classification” pipeline, providing an explicit and reproducible workflow for improving diagnostic robustness under complex and nonlinear industrial data.

Methods and materials

This section first constructs a mechanical fault classification model that incorporates SVM with multi-strategy improved SSA. The study additionally suggests an mechanical FD approach based on SVM-MSISSA with two-channel CNN to further increase the precision and effectiveness of FD.

As an effective classification algorithm, SVM can effectively classify sample data with different failure modes by finding the optimal hyperplane. However, the parameters, including the kernel function (KF) and penalty factor, affects how well SVM performs. Overfitting of the model might result from unreasonable parameter values, which compromises fault classification’s accuracy and dependability19. Therefore, the study improves the SSA to construct an method that fuses SVM and multi-strategy improved sparrow search algorithm (SVM-MSISSA) for mechanical fault classification to accurately classify mechanical faults. To improve the performance of SVM in mechanical fault classification, MSISSA adjusts the parameters of the model.

Fig. 3 shows the flowchart of SVM-MSSSA-based mechanical fault classification model. The flow includes signal acquisition, data preprocessing, PCA dimensionality reduction, and SVM-MSSSA fault classification model. After parameter initialization, random numbers are generated by chaotic mapping and Lévy flight strategy to determine whether the iteration is sufficient. If not, adaptive distribution is performed and the optimal solution is retained until the condition is satisfied. Finally, the fault classification results are output. To avoid ambiguity in the implementation of the MSISSA, this study explicitly integrates different enhancement strategies into distinct stages of the original SSA framework. Specifically, segmented linear chaotic mapping is introduced at the population initialization stage to increase population diversity and improve the coverage of the search space. Compared with random initialization, chaotic mapping makes the distribution of candidate solutions more uniform and reduces premature convergence. During the iterative optimization stage, Lévy flight perturbation is embedded into the position update of sparrows to enhance global exploration capability. By introducing occasional long-distance jumps, Lévy flight helps the algorithm escape local optima and improves its global search ability in high-dimensional parameter spaces. In addition, an adaptive step-size mechanism is adopted to dynamically balance exploration and exploitation during iterations. Larger step sizes are applied in early iterations to encourage global search, while smaller step sizes are used in later iterations to refine local optimization near promising solutions. Through the coordinated integration of chaotic initialization, Lévy flight perturbation, and adaptive step-size control, the proposed MSISSA forms a unified optimization framework that enhances convergence stability and global search performance without increasing algorithmic complexity. Mechanical FD method based on SVM-MSISSA with two-channel CNN

Although the SVM-MSISSA mechanical fault classification method is able to effectively identify and classify mechanical fault types, with the increase in the complexity of mechanical systems, the method is unable to effectively capture the intrinsic characteristics of faults, which leads to misdiagnosis. Therefore, the research suggests an mechanical FD approach based on SVM-MSISSA with dual-channel CNN to further increase the precision and resilience of mechanical FD. This technology uses dual-channel CNN for deep learning of mechanical defect features, improving the accuracy and efficiency of mechanical FD while reducing reliance on large amounts of labeled data.

The dual-channel CNN consists of two independent convolutional channels for processing TD signals and FD signals, respectively. This dual-channel structure can fully utilize the TD and FD information to improve the extraction of fault features. The dual-channel CNN is designed to learn complementary fault representations from time-domain and frequency-domain inputs and to form a fused deep embedding via feature-level fusion. It acts as a deep representation learner, whose fused feature vectors are subsequently classified by the MSISSA-optimized SVM. Such a two-stage design (deep embedding followed by an optimized margin-based classifier) explicitly separates feature representation learning from decision boundary optimization, which is beneficial for improving robustness in industrial FD scenarios with limited or unevenly distributed labeled data. Each channel contains three convolutional layers with 32, 64, and 128 filters, respectively, using a kernel size of 3 × 1 and ReLU activation. Each convolutional layer is followed by a max-pooling layer with a pooling size of 2 × 1 to reduce feature dimensionality and enhance robustness. After the final pooling operation, feature maps from both channels are flattened and fused through feature-level concatenation, forming a unified representation without introducing additional trainable parameters. The fused features are passed through a fully connected layer with 256 neurons and a Dropout layer with a dropout rate of 0.5. During training, a Softmax layer together with the cross-entropy loss function is employed only to guide feature discrimination within the CNN, while the final fault classification is performed by the SVM-MSISSA model using the extracted deep feature vectors. The Adam optimizer is used with an initial learning rate of 0.001, a batch size of 64, and a maximum of 150 training epochs. Batch normalization is applied after convolutional layers to stabilize training and improve convergence. For TD signals, the CNN structure of its channel includes a convolutional layer (CL), a pooling layer (PL), and a fully connected layer (FCL). The CL is used to extract local features, the PL is used to reduce the feature dimension and enhance the robustness of the features, and the FCL is used to classify the extracted features. Results

The performance of the built mechanical fault classification model is initially verified in this part. Then, the effectiveness of the proposed mechanical FD method is analyzed.

Fusion of SVM and MSISSA for mechanical fault classification performance testing

Experiments are carried out utilizing the Case Western Reserve University (CWRU) bearing fault dataset and the Prognostics and Health Management (PHM) dataset to illustrate the effects of the suggested mechanical fault classification model. The CWRU bearing failure dataset contains bearing failure data under different operating conditions. The PHM dataset contains operation data and fault data of various mechanical equipment (The data of the test and training set is 3:7). The CWRU dataset can be accessed at: https://engineering.case.edu/bearingdatacenter. The PHM dataset can be accessed at: https://www.nasa.gov/content/prognostics-center-of-excellence-data-set-repository. For both the CWRU and PHM datasets, raw vibration signals were first subjected to normalization to eliminate scale differences among samples. The continuous signals were then segmented into fixed-length samples using a sliding window strategy without overlap to ensure sample independence. For each segment, wavelet packet decomposition was applied for noise reduction and feature enhancement, followed by variational mode decomposition to extract modal components. Feature vectors were subsequently constructed based on signal energy and entropy-related measures and further reduced in dimensionality using principal component analysis. The same preprocessing and partitioning strategy was consistently applied to all comparative methods to ensure fair evaluation and reproducibility. Effectiveness analysis of mechanical FD methods

To further evaluate the practical applicability of the proposed method, an industrial FD experiment was conducted using vibration data collected from motor-driven equipment in a large-scale production plant. The dataset includes four typical fault types, namely normal condition, bearing wear, rotor imbalance, and misalignment, which were commonly encountered in industrial motor systems. Fault labels were obtained based on maintenance records and expert inspection reports provided by on-site engineers. A total of 1,200 signal samples were collected under stable operating conditions, with each sample generated using the same segmentation and preprocessing procedures described in Sect. 3.1. The dataset was processed and evaluated using the identical feature extraction, training, and testing pipeline to ensure consistency with the previous experiments. Discussion and conclusion

A multi-strategy improved SSA-based mechanical FD approach was suggested by the study. Additionally, it worked in tandem with SVM and dual-channel CNN to enhance FD’s precision and effectiveness. The results indicated that SVM-MSISSA had lower loss values, higher accuracy, and faster convergence in CWRU and PHM datasets. Especially in the PHM dataset, the loss value of SVM-MSISSA was significantly lower than that of the other methods, and the accuracy was improved from 3.05% to 13.90%. In addition, the dual-channel CNN structure could process both TD signals and FD signals, which fully utilized the time-frequency domain information and further improved the effect of fault feature extraction. The experimental outcomes demonstrated that the precision, recall, and F1 of the FD method based on SVM-MSISSA with dual-channel CNN were better than those of the FD method based on traditional CNN and LSTM-RNN on both CWRU and PHM datasets. Practical application tests demonstrated that the proposed method performed well in FD of motor equipment in a large manufacturing plant. The average FD accuracy was improved by 9.81% and 5.30% compared with CNN and LSTM-RNN, respectively. The RT was shortened by 76.95% and 65.03%, respectively. This indicated that the method not only had advantages in theory and experiment, but also had good potential for practical application.

The results indicate that the proposed mechanical FD method performs very well in many publicly available datasets and real-world application environments, with high accuracy and short RT. Although this study has the potential to optimize algorithms and reduce reliance on labeled data, obtaining a large amount of high-quality defect labeled data in actual industrial environments remains challenging. Future research can develop data augmentation strategies that use a large amount of unlabeled data and limited labeled data during the training process to improve the robustness and generalization ability of the model.

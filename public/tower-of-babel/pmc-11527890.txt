Comprehensive circular RNA profiling in various sheep tissues
Authors: Mohammad Reza Bakhtiarizade, Maryam Heidari, Amir Hossein Mohammadi Ghanatghestani
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractDespite the scientific relevance of circular RNAs (circRNAs), the study of these RNAs in non-model organisms, especially in sheep, is still in its infancy. On the other hand, while some studies have focused on sheep circRNA identification in a limited number of tissues, there is a lack of comprehensive analysis that profile circRNA expression patterns across the tissues not yet investigated. In this study, 61 public RNA sequencing datasets from 12 different tissues were uniformly analyzed to identify circRNAs, profile their expression and investigate their various characteristics. We reported for the first time a circRNA expression landscape with functional annotation in sheep tissues not yet investigated including hippocampus, BonMarrowMacrophage, left-ventricle, thymus, ileum, reticulum and 23-day-embryo. A stringent computational pipeline was employed and 8919 exon-derived circRNAs with high confidence were identified, including 88 novel circRNAs. Tissue-specificity analysis revealed that 3059 circRNAs were tissue-specific, which were also more specific to the tissues than linear RNAs. The highest number of tissue-specific circRNAs was found in kidney, hippocampus and thymus, respectively. Co-expression analysis revealed that expression of circRNAs may not be affected by their host genes. While most of the host genes produced more than one isoform, only one isoform had dominant expression across the tissues. The host genes of the tissue-specific circRNAs were significantly enriched in biological/pathways terms linked to the important functions of their corresponding tissues, suggesting potential roles of circRNAs in modulating physiological activity of those tissues. Interestingly, functional terms related to the regulation and various signaling pathways were significantly enriched in all tissues, suggesting some common regulatory mechanisms of circRNAs to modulate the physiological functions of tissues. Finding of the present study provide a valuable resource for depicting the complexity of circRNAs expression across tissues of sheep, which can be useful for the field of sheep genomic and veterinary research.Keywords: RNA-Seq, Tissue-specific, Non-coding RNA, lncRNAs, Functional enrichment analysisSubject terms: Animal biotechnology, Non-coding RNAs, Transcription, Gene ontology, High-throughput screening

Introduction

It has been well evidenced that the vast majority of the genome is transcribed into various coding and non-coding RNAs (ncRNAs) in mammals. The most of these RNAs are ncRNAs including ribosomal RNA (rRNA), transfer RNA (tRNA), small nuclear RNA (snRNA), small nucleolar RNA (snoRNA), microRNAs (miRNAs), long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) and circular RNAs (circRNAs)1,2. circRNAs were discovered about fifty years ago, however, they were long thought to be the result of splicing errors3,4. Recently, the advent of next generation sequencing (NGS) coupled with advances in computational approaches showed that these transcripts are more common than initially thought and established them as emerging class of ncRNAs5. circRNAs (or closed lncRNAs) are results of the back-splicing of the nascent RNAs, instead of the regular splicing, and spliceosome machinery joins the 3′ and 5′ ends of the spliced RNAs. These special spliced isoforms form a type of ncRNAs with covalently closed loop structures, without poly A tail and 5′–3′ polarity. This unique structure enhances their stability and life-time compared to other types of RNAs6. Expression of this class of ncRNAs is abundance, tissue-specific and shows evolutionary conservation between mouse and human7. They can be composed of exons (single or multiple, exonic circRNAs (EcircRNAs)), introns (intronic circRNAs (IcircRNAs)) or both (exonic–intronic circRNAs (EIcircRNAs)) and generated from mRNAs or lncRNAs8.

Growing evidence has highlighted that circRNAs participate as important regulators in transcription, splicing, chromatin interactions, protein translation as well as various biological processes7,9–11. Binding of circRNAs to transcriptional regulatory elements to modulate gene transcription have been reported12. Acting as competing endogenous RNAs (ceRNAs) is one of the most widely studied functions of circRNAs. They act as a sponge and bind to miRNAs through shared miRNA response elements (MREs), which can be led to prevent the inhibitory effects of miRNAs on their targets. In addition, some circRNAs can competitively bind to RNA binding proteins (RBP) and act as RBP sponge13. Recently, translation of a limited number of circRNAs to functional proteins have been reported14,15. On the other hand, important roles of circRNAs in initiation and development of various cancers have been demonstrated16,17, as some of them are reported as prognostic biomarkers for lung and colorectal cancer18–20. Altogether, these findings highlight the potential of circRNAs as powerful tools for unraveling the biological mechanisms behind different cellular metabolic process and diseases.

Due to prevalence of circRNAs in various species, from archaea to animals, a wide range of studies are dedicated to identify and annotate them in mammalian genomes, including sheep. In a study 10,226 circRNAs were identified from pituitary gland of prenatal and postnatal of sheep by RNA-seq21. The same researchers investigated circRNAs in prenatal and postnatal longissimus dorsi muscle of sheep and reported 6,113 circRNAs22. Cao et al. (2018) found 886 circRNAs including numerous circular intronic RNAs and exonic circRNAs in sheep skeletal muscle23. Jin et al., detected 7730 circRNAs in spleen tissue of sheep. Furthermore, they reported a total of 60 significantly differentially expressed circRNAs between antagonistic and sensitive groups to Escherichia coli F17, and 1942 new circRNAs in both groups24. In another study, the differential expression of circRNAs in the pituitary gland in estrus and anestrus sheep was investigated and a total of 12,468 circRNAs and 9231 differentially expressed circRNAs were reported25. In Zhang et al. study on sheep hypothalamus, 41,863 circRNAs were detected including several key circRNAs involved in reproduction26. La et al., found 147 and 364 uterine circRNAs with differential expression in the follicular and luteal phases, respectively, in Small Tail Han sheep27. In another study, researchers reported 124, 270, and 400 differentially expressed circRNAs in uterine tissues from Sunite sheep during different photoperiods28. A total of 11,565 circRNAs were identified via expression profiling of intramuscular fat tissue in Aohan fine-wool sheep29. Liu et al. found 4256 circRNAs in ovarian tissues of Hanper sheep30. Varela‑Martínez et al. detected 2510 and 3403 circRNAs in parietal lobe cortex and peripheral blood mononuclear cells in sheep31. In a more comprehensive study, circRNAs were investigated in testicular, muscular and liver tissues in three animal species including pig, sheep and cattle. The researchers identified 5556 exonic circRNAs and three ciRNAs in sheep32. Last, the differentially expressed circRNAs were identified in the ovarian tissue in luteal phase conditions in Hu sheep and fat-tailed sheep and 2875 differentially expressed circRNAs were reported33.

In spite of these studies and compared with the systematic characterization of circRNAs in model organisms such as human and mouse34,35, and various species of animals such as pig36, rat37, zebrafish38 and drosophila39, a genome-wide characterization of circRNAs across tissues of sheep has not been yet conducted. Therefore, to enrich sheep genome annotation and explore the tissue-wise expression patterns of circRNAs, a comprehensive study is required. Accordingly, in this study, we comprehensively analyzed 61 RNA-Seq datasets in 12 tissues of sheep for in-depth exploration of circRNAs using a rigorous computational approach. Expression profiles and tissues specificity of the identified circRNAs were assessed and functional enrichment analysis was performed to detect the biological processes in which the tissue specific circRNAs of each tissue might be involved. Our results provide a basis for a better understanding of circRNAs in farm animals, and a deeper functional annotation in sheep genome.

Results

RNA-Seq data analysis

Totally, 61 rRNA-depleted RNA-Seq datasets related to 12 sheep tissues were analyzed (Fig. 1). The total number of raw reads obtained for all datasets was 6,996,147,048; with an average of 115 million reads per tissue. Following quality control and trimming, 6,802,111,416 reads remained; with an average of 112 million reads per tissue. The lowest numbers of raw and clean reads were found in sample 1 of bone marrow macrophage tissue and sample 3 of ileum tissue, respectively. Sample 5 of ileum tissue had the largest number of raw reads, while for clean reads sample 4 of spleen tissue showed the largest number. Identification of circRNAs

In this study, circRNAs from different tissues of sheep were systematically identified using a rigorous computational pipeline (Fig. 1). To do this end, two most widely and efficient tools were employed separately on all samples, including CIRCexplorer2 and CIRI2. Initially, 72,470 and 47,853 unique circRNAs were detected by CIRCexplorer2 and CIRI2, in at least one sample, respectively (Supplementary Files S2 and S3). Of these, 25,800 circRNAs showed concordant coordinates in both tools, which all were exonic (Supplementary Files S4). Table 2 represents the number of the identified circRNAs across the tissues according to different methods. Out of 25,800 circRNAs, 16,132 were successfully assembled into full-length circRNAs. After applying the population-scale filters, 8919 circRNAs were selected for downstream analysis. These circRNAs were detected by both tools, supported by at least three back-splicing junction (BSJ) reads, expressed in at least three samples and their full-length sequences were determined. A total of 3476 host genes generated these circRNAs, of which 2482 genes had annotated gene symbols. Host genes with one circRNAs constituted ~ 47% of these circRNAs. Further analysis of circRNA number from their host genes revealed that 92 host genes generated at least 10 or more circRNAs. Investigation of biotype of the host genes of circRNAs revealed that circRNAs were transcribed from both coding and non-coding genes. Notably, ~ 99% of the host genes were protein coding genes and 51 host genes were non-coding (including 14 miRNAs, 14 snoRNAs, nine snRNAs, six rRNAs, seven linRNAs and one pseudogene).

The average GC content and length of the circRNAs were 45% and 238 bp, respectively. In concordance with previous reports in sheep31 or in other species40, most of the circRNAs were composed of two or three exons. Moreover, it is reported that single-exon circRNAs are longer than those comprising more than one exon. Our results showed that the average length of single-exon circRNAs (300 bp) was significantly longer than multi-exon circRNAs (~ 237 bp) (p-value = 1.12e–12, unpaired t-test). Spectrum of tissue specificity

On average, each circRNA was expressed in at least four tissues. The expression of these circRNAs estimated as Counts Per Million (CPM) by CIRIquant approach. Most of the expressed circRNAs were detected in kidney (n = 4892), hippocampus (n = 4431) and reticulum (n = 3667), while 23-day-embryo (n = 1492), left-ventricle (n = 1926) and testes (n = 2009) expressed the least circRNAs (Fig. 2, Supplementary File S5). An average number of 3045 circRNAs were detected per tissue. Tissue specificity analysis of the circRNAs across the 12 tissues revealed that 3,059 circRNAs were expressed as tissue-specific. The tissue-specific circRNAs showed five-fold higher CPM values in one tissue compared to all other tissues (Tissue-Enhanced) or to the average CPM value in all other tissues (Tissue-Enriched). The identified circRNAs in the other categories (Expressed-In-All, Mixed and Group-Enriched) were marked as tissue-independent, including 5828 circRNAs (Supplementary File S5). Figure 4 shows the number as well as percentage of the circRNAs, in terms of tissue specificity, in different tissue specificity categories across the tissues. The highest percentage of tissue-specific circRNAs was found in kidney (23.9%), followed by hippocampus (22.2%) and thymus (20.02%). On the other hand, 23-day-embryo (12.6%) and ovary (13%) included the lowest percentage of tissue-specific circRNAs (Fig. 4). Expression pattern of circRNAs in terms of tissue-specific and tissue-independent across the tissues are displayed in Fig. 5 as Split-violin plot. The expression averages of tissue-specific circRNAs were lower than independent-tissues circRNAs in most of tissues (except thymus, testes and kidney). The highest expression averages of the tissue-specific circRNAs were found in kidney, testes and hippocampus, respectively.

To better understand the tissue specificity of circRNAs, we next investigated the tissue specificity of mRNAs and lncRNAs using the same approach. Results clearly revealed that number of tissue-specific non-coding circRNAs was higher than mRNAs. Also, circRNAs were expressed in a more tissue-specific manner than lncRNAs (Fig. 6A). In terms of tissue specificity index (Tau score), differences between circRNAs and mRNAs (p-value = 0) as well as circRNAs and lncRNAs (p-value = 2.3–e141) were significant based on unpaired t-test (Supplementary File S7). Figure 6B indicates that the average of Tau scores of circRNAs is higher than the other two groups. In this regard, ~ 38% of circRNAs had Tau score > 0.95, relative to 30 and 13% in lncRNAs and mRNAs, respectively (Supplementary File S5). Supplementary File S8 provided the average expression of mRNAs and lncRNAs across the tissues along with tissue specificity score.

Further, to test co-expression levels between circRNAs and the host genes, Pearson correlation was calculated for each circRNA-host gene pair on the basis of the expression profiles. Results revealed that the expression pattern of circRNAs is different from their host genes, as only 3% of circRNAs had correlation > 0.75. Figure 6C shows that correlation coefficient of most of the circRNAs with their host genes were in range of − 0.5–0.5. Also, co-expression pattern between tissue-specific and tissue-independent circRNAs and their host genes were similar, indicating no dependency on tissue specificity.

Dominant circRNAs

It has been established that most mRNAs have one major isoform expressed at a significantly higher level than other isoforms41. According to a previous study that investigated this scenario in circRNAs in human tissues42, we assessed whether the same pattern can be detected for sheep circRNAs. For a host gene, circRNAs with highest and lowest expressions referred to as major and minor transcripts, respectively. If fold-change of major to minor transcripts was higher than 2, the major transcript referred to as dominant circRNA. Out of 3476 host genes, 1853 genes were found to have at least two expressed circRNAs. In total, 5721 dominant circRNAs related to 1466 host genes were found in different tissues. Of these, 4910 and 811circRNAs were tissue-independent and tissue-specific, respectively. In other words, approximately four circRNAs were expressed from each host gene across the tissues. On average, 26% and 14% of the host genes exhibited a two- and five-fold-change dominant circRNAs across all tissues, respectively. Further, we investigated whether one transcript that is dominated from a certain host gene is also dominant in the other tissues. Of 1853 genes with at least two expressed circRNAs, 1341 genes had at least two expressed circRNAs in at least two tissues, which were used for this analysis. Results revealed that 819 circRNAs expressed the same dominant circRNAs in different tissues. Of these, 706 and 113 circRNAs were tissues-independent and tissue-specific. Most of the dominant tissue-specific circRNAs were found in kidney (56) and hippocampus (23) tissues. Furthermore, tissue specificity index (~ 0.77) and correlation coefficient (~ 0.27) between the expression of these circRNAs and their host genes were in range of all the identified circRNAs (Supplementary File S5). Complete list of the dominant circRNA for each circRNA in different tissues is provided in Supplementary File S7. Altogether and consistent with previous study42, our results suggested that dominant circRNA is a common characteristic across different tissues, indicating a potential significant role of these circRNAs.

Functional enrichment analysis

One clue to uncover circRNA functions is their host genes. Hence, to have a basic understanding of the functional hallmarks of the tissue-specific circRNAs, host genes of these circRNAs were subjected to enrichment analysis for each tissue. In total, 3936 significant biological processes terms (p-value < 0.05) were found across all the tissues as well as in tissue-independent circRNAs, which 380 terms had FDR < 0.05. In the KEGG enrichment analysis, 637 terms showed p-value < 0.05 and 261 terms had FDR < 0.05 across all the tissues as well as in tissue-independent circRNAs. Detailed results of the functional enrichment analysis for biological processes and KEGG pathways in all tissues as well as in tissue-independent circRNAs were provided in Supplementary Files S9 and S10, respectively. We found the biological activities associated with tissue-specific circRNAs, were closely related to the intrinsic properties and functions of each tissue. For example, hippocampus-specific circRNAs were mainly enriched in “Glutamatergic synapse”, “Axon Guidance” and “Modulation Of Chemical Synaptic Transmission”, while left-ventricle-specific circRNAs were associated with “Arrhythmogenic right ventricular cardiomyopathy” and “Regulation Of Cardiac Conduction”. Notably, specific circRNAs in liver, thymus and ileum were mainly participated in “Integrin-Mediated Signaling Pathway”, “Thyroid hormone signaling pathway” and “Vesicle-Mediated Transport”, respectively. Encouraged by these results and to better understand the functional roles of the identified circRNAs, enrichment analysis was performed for tissue-specific circRNAs with Tau score > 0.99 per tissue. Interestingly, several significant terms related to the specific function of each tissue were found that were not significant before such as “Chemical Synaptic Transmission”, “Neuromuscular Synaptic Transmission”, “Anterograde Trans-Synaptic Signaling” and “Regulation Of Trans-Synaptic Signaling” in hippocampus; “Cardiac Muscle Cell Action Potential Involved In Contraction” in left-ventricle; “Notch Signaling Involved In Heart Development” in 23-day-embryo and “Regulation Of Macrophage Derived Foam Cell Differentiation” in BonMarrowMacrophage (Supplementary File S12). Further annotating the host genes with AnimalTFDB database revealed that host genes of 437 circRNAs (out of 8919 circRNAs) were transcription factors (TFs) including 400 known and seven novel circRNAs. Among these, 140 circRNAs were tissue-specific (Supplementary File S5). Functional enrichment analysis of these host genes results in 106 significant biological processes terms, which are mainly related to regulation of transcription processes (Supplementary File S13).

Comparison with previous studies

To compare the identified circRNAs with the reported circRNAs in the previous studies related to sheep, a comprehensive literature review was conducted. Only studies that reported the exact coordination of the detected circRNAs were considered. In this regard, seven studies were found that have identified circRNAs in different sheep tissues including muscle22,23,32, pituitary glands21,25, hypothalamus26, peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs), parietal lobe cortex31, liver and testes32. Out of 8919 identified circRNAs in the present study, 8168 circRNAs were consistently detected in at least one of these studies (Supplementary File S11). On the other hand, at the time of writing this manuscript, circAtlas 3.0 was released that added four new species including sheep. A total of 114,144 circRNAs were reported in seven sheep tissues including skeletal muscle, spleen, kidney, liver, lung, heart and adipose. We found 8648 of 8919 identified circRNAs in this database, according to the comparison of circRNA BSJ genomic coordinates. Therefore, 88 circRNAs were considered novel, with most of them being tissue-specific (63 circRNAs). The highest number of tissue-specific circRNAs found in kidney (29) followed by thymus (11). Figure 7 shows an UpSet plot to depict the overlap between the identified circRNAs in the present study and the previous studies. It is clear from this Figure that the circRNAs that are more common among the studies had the less Tau scores. Since these studies identified the circRNAs from different tissues, the less prevalent circRNAs among these studies are more tissue-specific. Also, the novel circRNAs showed the highest average of Tau score, which reinforced the tissue specificity of them. Discussion

Since circRNAs have tissue-specific expression, they can play potential roles in tissue development and cell differentiation34,43. It is suggested that the circRNAs are involved in the regulation of different livestock traits such as milk, meat production, and fat deposition13. Furthermore, circRNAs have been acknowledged to be stable and often exhibit tissue-specific expression, as are introduced as promising biomarkers and therapeutic targets to study disease mechanisms44. The tissue-specific expression of circRNAs is regulated by complex mechanisms. This specificity can be attributed to the presence of specific regulatory factors in each tissue. For instance, certain circRNAs are expressed exclusively in neural or cardiac tissues, which may be due to the presence of specific splicing factors in these tissues. Also, tissue specificity can be affected by using tissue-specific promoters45. Sheep genome remains under-annotated in terms of circRNAs compared with model organisms such as human or mouse34,35 or even other species such as pig36 and rat37. Therefore, in the present study, to characterize the landscape of circRNAs in different sheep tissues and better understand the potential physiological functions of these circRNAs, a comprehensive analysis was performed. This is by far the most comprehensive circRNA detection of the sheep with respect to the number of investigated tissues and using a combination of several tools.

Comparison between our findings and those of previous studies in sheep revealed that most of the identified circRNAs are previously reported and a total of 88 novel circRNAs were found for the first time in the present study. During the past decade, several computational circRNA detection tool have been developed, which apply various approaches to identify the reads spanning the BSJ. Analysis of these reads can be led to identify circRNAs based on the two positions involved in the circular junction. Most of the benchmark studies reinforced that the results produced by these tools are highly divergent and there is no single tool outperforms the others in all conditions. Hence, it is recommended to combine the results of two or more prediction tools to increase the robustness and accuracy of the detection as well as produce more reliable findings46–50. In this regard, different benchmark studies have been conducted to detect more effective tools as well as identify the best combination of these approaches. Based on the results of previous comprehensive benchmark studies CIRCexplorer251 and CIRI252,53 have been detected as two effective and widely used tools to identify circRNAs. It is worth noting that evaluation and comparison of circRNA detection tools revealed that combination of these tools can be led to a better balanced performance compared to other approaches46,54. In the present study, to enhance the robustness of the results, only those transcripts were considered putative circRNAs that (1) detected by the two circRNA detection tools, (2) their full-lengths reconstructed using CIRI-full, (3) their expression estimated by CIRIquant approach, (4) were supported by at least three BSJ reads and (5) were expressed in at least three samples. It is worth noting that in spite of most previous studies that only reported the BSJ sites of circRNAs, only circRNAs with full-length sequences were considered in the present study. It is pertinent to highlight that full-length sequences were constructed for 68% of the identified circRNAs, which is in good accordance with a previous report in mouse (69%)42. Therefore, our stringent filtering approach can lead to a lower number of identified circRNAs (also lower false positive results) than the other studies. One of the recent studies on circRNA identification in sheep is55 (circAtlas 3.0 database), which reported a large number of circRNAs in seven sheep tissues (skeletal muscle, spleen, kidney, liver, lung, heart and adipose). In this context, the large number of reported circRNAs by circAtlas database can be attributed to their circRNA detection approach, as they used CIRI2 as main detection circRNA tool. Next the circRNAs that were supported by at least one of the other tools (CIRCexplorer251, DCC56 and find_circ7) were considered for downstream analysis55. On the other hand, a recent comprehensive benchmark study showed that the detection efficiency of circRNAs is significantly decreased in low-abundance circRNAs57. Since, no BSJ count filters was considered for identifying circRNAs in circAtlas approach, the possibility of false positive results cannot be denied. To further verify this claim, we compared the identified circRNAs in our study before applying population-scale filters to circAtlas database and found that 21,766 of 25,800 circRNAs were reported in this database, which supports our claim. On the other hand, it should be highlighted that we provided the expression profile of the identified circRNAs across 12 tissues and only three tissues (kidney, spleen and liver) were common with circAtlas database. Therefore, for the first time, the expression patterns of circRNAs were reported in hippocampus, BonMarrowMacrophage, left-ventricle, thymus, ileum, reticulum, and 23-day-embryo tissues.

In this study, none of the identified circRNAs were non-exonic. In fact, there was no overlap between the non-exonic circRNAs identified by both tools, which is in agreement with a previous study in sheep that reported < 2% overlapping rate between these tools. This finding is also in line with the notation that the detection of non-exonic circRNAs is still in infancy and need further studies to develop more efficient computational approaches32. On the other hand, it is well known that most circRNAs are derived from internal exons of mRNAs58. In the same way, our study found that only 663 circRNAs were composed from the first exon of the related host genes. Also, protein coding genes were the main source of circRNAs, consistent with a previous study that suggested these circRNAs to be associated to mRNA translation or be a backup of mRNA34. More than half of the host genes (1853 genes) generated multiple circRNAs, suggesting the existence of alternative back-splicing mechanism involved in the biogenesis of circRNAs, similar to the alternative splicing of mRNAs59. In this context, the highest number of distinct circRNAs was derived from PKHD1 protein-coding gene that produced 30 circRNAs in different tissues. The results of a study conducted on mice revealed that this gene is able to produce 72 distinct circRNAs in kidney tissue. UTRN was ranked at the second position of host genes expressing a high number of circRNAs, where generated 28 circular isoforms. It is worthy of notice that this gene was reported to generate 25 circRNA isoforms among the subcellular fractions of HepG2 cell (nucleus, cytoplasm, mitochondria, ribosome, cytosol and exosome)60. It is worth noting that although most of host genes produce two or more circRNA isoforms, however always one isoform was dominant (1466 of 1853 host genes) and the other isoforms showed lower expression. This finding indicates that molecular mechanisms underlying circRNAs biogenesis is more complicated than the current depiction, confirming past findings61. However, further studies need to be conducted to determine the balance between the different isoforms of each host gene, as this seems to be an important research question in this filed. Moreover, co-expression analysis between circRNAs and their host genes revealed no relatedness between them and their expression pattern were poorly correlated. This is in accordance with the findings of previous studies42,61 and suggests that expression of circRNAs may not be affected by their host genes.

To better understand the tissue-specific expression characteristics of the identified circRNAs across the tissues, a tissue specificity analysis was performed and 34.3% of circRNAs were detected as tissue-specific. The same percentage of circRNAs were reported as tissue-specific in mouse34. On the other hand, tissue-specificity analysis revealed that circRNAs were more tissue-specific than linear RNAs (mRNAs lncRNAs). It is reported that the potential regulatory roles of circRNAs might be associated with the function of host genes and a degree of similarity between their functions had been reported62. Hence, putative regulatory roles of circRNAs can be annotated via analyzing their host genes. Functional enrichment analysis of the host genes of tissue-specific circRNAs were performed and a total of 380 and 261 significantly enriched (FDR < 0.05) biological processes and KEGG pathways were found, respectively. Interestingly, biological processes related to regulation as well as KEGG pathways associated with different signaling pathways were significantly enriched in all tissues, indicating some common biological activities of the circRNAs are involved in regulating the physiological functions of tissues. Functional enrichment analysis of the host genes of the tissue-specific circRNAs reinforced this hypothesis that the tissue-specific pattern of circRNAs may be associated with their special functions in these tissues. Further functional analysis of the more tissue-specific circRNAs (Tau score > 0.99) corroborated this hypothesis, as several related terms to the specific function of the tissues were found that were not significant before. This finding supports the potential regulatory roles of the more tissue-specific circRNAs in various biological pathways and makes them interesting for further functional investigations.

An unequal distribution of circRNAs was found across the tissues and the highest abundance of tissue-specific circRNAs was detected in hippocampus tissue. In this regard, it is well known that the nervous tissues are highly enriched for circRNAs, which might be associated with the complexity of neuronal activity in brain34,63,64. Interestingly, functions of these circRNAs were involved in a variety of brain-specific physiological processes such as “Modulation Of Chemical Synaptic Transmission”, “Glutamatergic synapse”, “Neuromuscular Synaptic Transmission”, “Anterograde Trans-Synaptic Signaling” and “Regulation Of Trans-Synaptic Signaling”, which strengthens the reliability of our findings. To gain further insight into the biological function of the circRNAs, functional enrichment analysis of the dominant tissue-specific circRNAs was performed and revealed more specific terms associated with brain function, indicating the importance of the host genes with dominant isoforms. For example, in addition to “Glutamatergic synapse” term that was significant in tissue-specific circRNAs of hippocampus, “Serotonergic synapse” was significantly enriched in the dominant tissue-specific circRNAs in this tissue. In agreement with our results, several brain-specific circRNAs have been reported to play a role in synaptic activity and neurotransmitter functions65. In this context some important host genes related to the tissue-specific circRNAs in hippocampus were AKT3, DTN, KIF1B, DCC, ZDHHC21 and SORT1. Here, AKT3 generated eight hippocampus-specific circRNAs with well-known roles in brain. Brain-specific expression of AKT3 is clearly explained and it has been proven to be involved in postnatal brain development66. More importantly, it has been reported that Akt3 − / − mice have a reduction in brain size and neurodegeneration following injury67. DTNA encodes a structural protein, α-dystrobrevin, which has important roles in the formation and stability of synapses, especially in permeability of the blood–brain barrier68,69. KIF1B gene has two isoforms; Kif1Bα and Kif1Bβ70. It is demonstrated that Kif1Bβ is highly expressed in hippocampus, cerebellum, and cerebral cortex and plays an important role in the transport of synaptic vesicle proteins along the axons of the motor neurons70. As a member of the immunoglobulin superfamily of cell adhesion molecules, DCC plays vital roles in the adult rat central nervous system. Also, high level expression of this gene is detected in hippocampus and medial habenula71. Moreover, the role of DCC in hippocampal synaptic plasticity underlying spatial memory is confirmed72. It is demonstrated that ZDHHC21 mutation causes neuronal loss and disturbs synaptic function73. SORT1 gene encodes sortilin 1 protein, a neuronal receptor involved in intracellular protein transport and cellular signal transduction. This protein is part of the VPS10-related sortilin family of proteins and is most abundant in the central nervous system. It is reported that the sortilin deficiency caused by the SORT1 mutation (p.G171A) can be led to defects in neurotransmission74. These lines of evidence emphasize the importance of circRNAs in neural development, especially circRNAs derived from the above genes, consistent with previous studies that have reported these genes as biological controllers and effectors in the neurodevelopment42,63,75.

Among the functional terms significantly enriched in left-ventricle, “Arrhythmogenic right ventricular cardiomyopathy”, “Cardiac Muscle Cell Action Potential Involved In Contraction” and “Regulation Of Cardiac Conduction” are directly related to the specific function of heart, suggesting an important role for some circRNAs in basic heart functions. Some of the host genes with well-known functions in this tissue were RyR2, Nedd4L and MYPN. The highest number of left-ventricle-specific circRNAs (10 circRNAs) was generated by RyR2. Over-expression of RyR2 in myocardium is reported and cause myocardial contraction through a mechanism involving a calcium-induced calcium release76–78. It is demonstrated that RyR2 actively participates in preserving the normal heart function. Dysregulations of this gene can lead to arrhythmia, heart failure and other heart diseases79,80. Nedd4L gene is a ubiquitination ligase from the Nedd4 family and is involved in the post-transcriptional modification of sodium transporters and cardiac ion channels. Hence, this gene has a key role in various pathophysiological processes of cardiovascular diseases81. The other gene, MYPN, encodes a 147-kDa protein containing five immunoglobulin domains. This gene plays crucial roles in sarcomere assembly and regulation of gene expression82,83. It is of interest to note that “Thyroid hormone signaling pathway” was the most significant KEGG pathway in the host genes of the thymus-specific circRNAs. Accumulating evidences have clearly shown that thyroid hormones modulate thymic endocrine activity84,85. STAT5B, TCF7, NCOR1, PDPK1, HIF1A and ZEB1 are some of the host genes with well-known roles closely associated with thymus function. Of these, TCF7, NCOR1, HIF1A and ZEB1 are TFs. STAT5B is one of the important identified host genes in thymus. This gene is a member of signal transducer and activator of transcription (STAT) family and plays a central role in T cell development. So that, STAT5B deficiency causes endocrine and immunological problems86. TCF7 gene encodes T cell factor-1 (TCF-1) which is a TF with significant functions in T cell development and differentiation87. Considering the role of thymus in making T-cells, the circRNAs derived from these genes can be important regulators in this tissue. Moreover, role of NCOR188, PDPK189, HIF1A90 and ZEB191 in T-cell development as well as immune system is reported. Based on the evidence from previous studies as well as survey our results, it can be speculated that the tissue-specific circRNAs might act as essential regulators contributing to tissue development and differentiation.

As well in other tissues, we found several tissue-specific circRNAs originated from the host genes associated with specific functions of that tissue such as ITSN2, PTPRD and DLG1 in kidney; GRN and RUNX1 in BonMarrowMacrophage and NOTCH2 in 23-day-embryo. In kidney, ITSN2 participates in clathrin-mediated endocytosis92. It is demonstrated that ITSN2 variants can lead to nephrotic syndrome93,94. PTPRD SNP (rs2279776) is reported to be significantly related to decrease expression of this gene in kidney and it is suggested to be associated with the risk of clear cell renal cell carcinoma95. DLG1 is a member of membrane-associated guanylate kinase (MAGUK) family, which have different functions especially in regulation of signaling pathways in polarized epithelial cells96. According to studies, there is an association between renal expression of DLG1 protein and impaired renal function, which can cause congenital diseases and chronic renal failure97. Moreover, DLG1 mutations may lead to congenital hydronephrosis in humans98. GRN gene encodes a growth factor, Progranulin (PGRN), which is involved in a range of processes from regulation of inflammation to wound healing and tumorigenesis99. Moreover, the association of GRN (granulin) and granulocytes as well the cells of the innate immune system is reported100. RUNX1, as a TF, is a member of Runx family with well-known roles in various aspects of T-cell differentiation, as reviewed in101. Several studies have provided evidence that NOTCH2 plays a crucial role in post-implantation development in mice102,103. It is also demonstrated that this gene is involved in kidney, heart and eye development104. Furthermore, functional enrichment analysis revealed that host genes of the tissue-independent circRNAs were mainly enriched in the general processes of cells such as “Protein Modification Process”, “Phosphorylation”, “Chromatin Remodeling”, “Negative Regulation Of Translation” and “Organelle Organization” (Supplementary Files S10 and S11). It would be tempting to deduce that tissue-independent circRNAs regulate a diversity of basic biological tissue activities that are essential for maintaining cellular homeostasis.

Results of functional analysis of the novel tissue-specific circRNAs showed that they are directly related to the specific function of each tissue. For example, some important host genes with well-known functions in the related tissues were PKHD1 and RANBP3L in kidney, IGF2BP1 in 23-day-embryo, SVOP in hippocampus and SATB1 and KIF11 in thymus. Various roles of PKHD1 in kidney development as well as in mature kidney have been demonstrated105. We found PKHD1 a kidney-specific circRNA as generated 23 kidney-specific circRNAs, suggesting circRNAs from this gene might be specific to the functions of kidney. RANBP3L is highly expressed in rat kidney and its particular function in this tissue has been highlighted, especially in the medullary and papillary collecting-duct cells106. Role of IGF2BP1 in mice embryonic development through m6A modification107 as well as in zebrafish embryos108 is demonstrated. High level expression of SVOP is detected in brain of rat before birth. Also, involvement of this gene in neuron formation, maturation, or neuronal function has been documented. SATB1, as a TF, is expressed preferentially in the thymus and109 its role in T cell development and differentiation has been demonstrated110. It is reported that KIF11 is over-expressed in thymus that is associated with immune responses111. It is proposed that circRNAs may be involved in modulating the various biological processes by regulating the expression of TF genes112. Further analysis of the results revealed that about 5% of the host genes are TFs, which may highlight potential regulatory roles of the associated circRNAs. Six host genes of the novel circRNAs were identified as TFs including SATB1, LEF1, BAZ2B, ARNT2, CSRNP3 and ALX1. The average Tau score of these circRNAs were 0.98, indicates tissue-specific expression of them. For example, consistent with our findings, tissue specificity of circRNAs derived from SATB1 in thymus reported in human and mouse34. Moreover, role of SATB1 in T cell development and differentiation is clearly demonstrated113. According to these evidences the novel circRNAs deserve further exploration and it is reasonable to infer that these circRNAs, especially tissue-specific circRNAs, may be involved in the biological functions of these tissues114.

Conclusion

As circRNAs are attracting increasing attention in RNA biology research, we attempted to capture the spectrum of circRNAs and their expression profiles across 12 sheep tissues using RNA-Seq datasets. Expression of the circRNAs was reported for the first time in seven tissues of sheep not yet investigated including hippocampus, BonMarrowMacrophage, left-ventricle, thymus, ileum, reticulum and 23-day-embryo. Applying two circRNA detection tools along with a stringent filtering approach allowed us to identify 8919 exonic circRNAs, of which 88 were detected for the first time and enriched the genome annotation of sheep. Full-length sequence of the circRNAs and their expression patterns across the tissues were determined using particular methods. It is evident that the different features of the identified circRNAs including number of exons, length, tissue specificity, be more tissue-specific than linear RNAs, expressed in a regulated manner independent of their host genes, unequal distribution of circRNAs across the tissues, protein-coding genes as main source of circRNAs, their potential biological functions and dominant isoforms are in agreement with previous studies in this filed, which confirms the robustness and accuracy of our results. Most of the host genes generated multiple isoforms, evidence in favor of the propensity of circRNAs to be alternatively back-spliced. The functional enrichment analysis of host genes of the tissue-specific circRNAs from each tissue revealed that circRNAs were involved in various biological processes that might be essential for the efficient function of that tissue. Collectively, this study systematically identified circRNAs on a genome-wide scale and expanded the knowledge on their diversities in different sheep tissues, thereby providing a valuable insight into the biology of circRNA in sheep.

Materials and methods

Dataset

In total, 12 tissues including 61 RNA-Seq samples (accession number E-MTAB-3838) were obtained from European Bioinformatics Institute (EBI) database. Total RNA of these samples was extracted from embryonic and adult tissues including thymus, testes, spleen, reticulum, ovary, liver, left-ventricle, kidney, ileum, hippocampus, bone marrow macrophage, and 23-day-embryo115. Numbers of samples per tissue were three in testes, ovary and 23-day-embryo; five in thymus and kidney; and six in spleen, reticulum, liver, left-ventricle, ileum, hippocampus, and bone marrow macrophage tissues. Illumina TruSeq stranded total RNA with the Ribo Zero gold option was applied to prepare the sequencing libraries and remove rRNA. Sequencing was performed on an Illumina HiSeq 2500 platform with paired-end 150 bp read length.

RNA-Seq data analysis

FASTQC software (v0.11.5)116 was used to quality control of the raw reads. Then, the trimming of the low-quality reads was performed by Trimmomatic software (v0.38)117 (TRAILING: 20 and MAXINFO: 80: 0.8). After trimming, the clean reads were tested for quality control by FASTQC software again to be sure about the quality of the reads.

circRNA identification

For circRNA identification two powerful and widely used tools, CIRCexplorer251 and CIRI252,53, were selected. To ensure the high accuracy for the identification of circRNAs and minimize the false positive rate of the results, those circRNAs that were detected by both tools were kept for downstream analysis. All the subsequent analysis was performed based on CIRI2 formatted list of circRNAs.

CIRCexplorer2

First, STAR software (v2.7.9a)118 was used to align the reads against the reference genome (Ovis aries, Oarv3.1 and GTF release 103) with the following settings: chimSegmentMin 10. Then, the output of STAR was used as input for parse module of CIRCexplorer2 (v 2.3.5) to focus on finding reads spanning splice junctions in transcripts as well as split and realigned the unmapped reads in reverse order to find BSJ reads. Next, circRNA annotation was conducted by CIRCexplorer2 annotation module based on the GTF annotation file of sheep genome to determine the genomic coordinates of the 5’ and 3’ back-splice sites for each potential circRNA.

CIRI2

CIRI2 to infer original region for read segments as well discriminate BSJ reads from non-BSJ reads, employs an adapted maximum likelihood estimation based on multiple seed matching strategy. This tool uses BWA-mem algorithm to obtain the mapping information of reads based on local alignment. Moreover, CIRI2 need the reads with the same length, as the lengths of the reads may not be the same, because of trimming, Trimmomatic was used again to make the length of the reads same. Here, BWA (v0.7.17-r1188)119 was employed to align the reads against the genome and then CIRI2 (v2.0) was used to identify candidate circRNAs with default parameters. The BSJ start coordinates output by CIRI2 were converted to 0-based coordinates to be consistent with CIRCexplorer2 results.

Full-length sequence construction

Accurate full-length sequence construction of circRNAs is crucial for functional studies. There are some challenges to obtain full length sequence of circRNAs including (1) circRNAs have overlapping reads with their linear counterparts and the expression of linear transcripts from the same gene can significantly skew the circular transcript assembly, (2) Most of the existing methods for circRNA identification rely on the detection of BSJ reads, however, these reads provide partial information about the circRNA full sequence. Moreover, BSJ reads may miss low-abundance or complex circRNAs that have multiple exons, (3) Typically only a small fraction of the transcriptome is constituted of circRNAs. The exiting tools, like CIRCexplorer2 and CIRI2, do not report the full-length sequences of circRNAs. To addressee this challenge some specific tools have been developed, including CIRI-full tool120,121. In CIRI-full, CIRI2 is equipped with reverse overlap (based on reversely overlapped paired-end reads) and BSJ reads information to determine the boundaries of circRNAs and reconstruct full-length sequence of circRNAs121. In this study, CIRI-full (v2.0) was used at default settings to reconstruct the full-length sequences of the identified circRNAs (intersecting results of CIRCexplorer2 and CIRI2), based on the output of CIRI2 from the previous step. Only circRNAs with full-length sequences were retained for further analysis.

Accurate quantification of circRNAs

An important challenge for circRNA analysis is the quantification of these circRNAs and a reliable approach is necessary to obtain an accurate expression for each circRNA. Here, to quantify circRNA expression CIRIquant (v1.1.2) was used122. To do this, a CIRI2 formatted list of the identified circRNAs was inputted to CIRIquant. This tool generates pseudo-reference sequences for the detected circRNAs to re-align putative BSJ reads. This allowed CIRIquant to compare the mapping of reads to both the genome and the pseudo-references, and identify BSJ reads more accurately and sensitively. Also, the junction ratio of circRNAs are estimated reliably by this approach122. CPM was estimated for each circRNA to remove library size effects and sequencing depth. Finally, to filter out lowly expressed circRNAs, two population-scale filters were used and those circRNAs were kept for further analysis that: (1) were supported by at least three BSJ reads and (2) were expressed in at least three samples. These circRNAs were determined to be bona fide and kept for further analysis, which can be considered as another filtering step to remove potential false positives.

Gene expression analysis of mRNAs/lncRNAs

To quantify the expression of the other genes (mRNA and lncRNA), Hisat2 and StringTie tools were used123,124. After aligning the clean reads against the sheep genome (Ovis aries, Oarv3.1 and GTF release 103) by Hisat2, StringTie was used to assemble the aligned reads and estimate expression levels for the genes by calculating TPM (transcript per million). To evaluate the co-expression level between circRNAs and the relevant host genes, Pearson correlation coefficients were calculated based on their expression profiles. To this end, expression matrixes were standardized per sample, using scale function of R software.

circRNA expression tissue specificity

TissueEnrich (v1.120) R package was used to identify tissue specificity of circRNAs and their related categories. This approach determines if a circRNA has tissue-specific expression by comparing its expression across the other tissues using the algorithm from the HPA125,126. Cutoff for fold change, maximum number of tissues in a group and minimum expression for a circRNA to be called as expressed were considered 5, 7 and 0.001 CPM (1 for TPM), respectively. All 12 tissues included for both tissue enrichment and tissue enhancement. Based on the results of this analysis, the circRNAs were classified into six classes including: (1) Not Expressed: circRNAs with an expression level less than 1 across all the tissues, (2) Tissue Enriched: circRNAs with a minimum expression level of 1 that also have at least five-fold higher expression levels in a particular tissue compared to all other tissues, (3) Group Enriched: circRNAs that have a minimum expression level of 1 with at least five-fold higher expression levels in a group of 2–7 tissues compared to all other tissues, and that are not considered Tissue Enriched, (4) Tissue Enhanced: circRNAs with a minimum expression level of 1 along with at least five-fold higher expression in a particular tissue compared to the average levels in all other tissues, and that are not considered Tissue Enriched or Group Enriched, (5) Expressed in all: circRNAs that have a minimum expression level of 1 across all of the tissues and do not belong to any of the previous 4 classes and (6) Mixed: circRNAs that are not assigned to any of the above 5 classes. It is worth mentioning that circRNAs from the Tissue Enriched and Tissue Enhanced classes were classified as tissue-specific circRNAs. Moreover, tspex R package127 was used to calculate tissue specificity index of circRNAs across the tissues. To do this, tau value (τ) was calculated based on an expression matrix, ranging from 0 (tissue-independent) to 1 (tissue-specific). In each tissue, mean of expression across all samples was applied to investigate the circRNA expression tissue specificity.

Functional enrichment analysis

To investigate the potential functions of the tissue-specific circRNAs, host genes of the circRNAs were subjected to enrichment analysis using enrichR package in R128. The adjusted p ≤ 0.05 (FDR method) was considered to identify significant enriched gene ontology (biological process) and KEGG (Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes) pathway. Also, to identify ovine TFs in the host genes, the AnimalTFDB database (version 4.0) database was used129.

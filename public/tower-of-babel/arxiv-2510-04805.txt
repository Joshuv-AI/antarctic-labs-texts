Serre weight conjectures for GSp 4 \mathrm{GSp}_{4}
Daniel Le, Bao V. Le Hung, Heejong Lee

Abstract
. We prove the weight part of Serre’s conjecture for Galois representations valued in GSp 4 \mathrm{GSp}_{4} that are tamely ramified with explicit genericity at places above p p as conjectured by Herzig–Tilouine and Gee–Herzig–Savitt. This improves on [ Lee ] where an inexplicit genericity hypothesis was required. As an application, we prove a modularity lifting theorem for GSp 4 \mathrm{GSp}_{4} under similar assumptions.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
The main result

The article
[
HT13
]
formulated a generalization of the weight part of Serre’s conjecture for the étale cohomology of the Siegel modular threefold for tamely ramified Galois representations.

[
GHS18
]
later generalized this to unramified Hilbert–Siegel modular varieties (though the global context was not made explicit in this setting).
The main goal of this article is to prove these conjectures for tamely ramified Galois representations with explicit genericity (under mild hypotheses typically appearing in the Taylor–Wiles method).

Let
p
be a prime number and
F
be a totally real field in which
p
is unramified. We denote by
G_{F}
the absolute Galois group of
F
.
Let
U=U_{p}U^{\infty,p}\leq\mathrm{GSp}_{4}(\mathcal{O}_{F}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}_{p})\times\mathrm{GSp}_{4}(\mathbb{A}_{F}^{\infty,p})
be a compact open subgroup. We assume that
U^{p,\infty}
is neat. Let
\Sh_{U}
be the Hilbert–Siegel modular variety associated with
\mathrm{Res}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}\mathrm{GSp}_{4/F}
at the level
U
defined over
F
. For a
U_{p}
-representation
V
, we denote by
\mathcal{F}_{V}
the locally constant étale sheaf on
\Sh_{U}
associated with
V
. We consider the étale cohomology group
H^{i}_{\et}(\Sh_{U,\overline{F}},\mathcal{F}_{V})
. Then there is a Hecke algebra
\mathbb{T}
acting on
H^{i}_{\et}(\Sh_{U,\overline{F}},\mathcal{F}_{V})
. Moreover, they are isomorphic to
(\mathfrak{g},K)
-cohomology groups of a certain space of cuspidal automorphic forms on
\mathrm{GSp}_{4}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
as Hecke modules by Matsushima’s formula. We choose an isomorphism
\iota:\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}\simeq\mathbb{C}
. By various works including
[
Wei05
,
Sor10
,
GT19
]
, there is a Galois representation
r_{\pi,p,\iota}:G_{F}\rightarrow\mathrm{GSp}_{4}(\overline{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
attached to a regular algebraic cuspidal automorphic representation
\pi
of
\mathrm{GSp}_{4}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
. We say that a continuous semisimple representation
\overline{r}:G_{F}\rightarrow\mathrm{GSp}_{4}(\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})
is
automorphic
if
\overline{r}
is isomorphic to the semisimplification of
r_{\pi,p,\iota}\otimes_{\overline{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}
for some
\pi
. We say that
\overline{r}
is
potentially diagonalizably automorphic
if furthermore
\pi
can be chosen so that
r_{\pi,p,\iota}
is potentially diagonalizable at places above
p
. Let
\mathfrak{m}_{\overline{r}}\subset\mathbb{T}
be the maximal ideal determined by
\overline{r}
by matching Satake parameters with the characteristic polynomial of the Frobenius element at all but finitely many places of
F
. Under the standard Taylor–Wiles conditions,
\overline{r}
is automorphic if and only if
H^{i}_{\et}(\Sh_{U,\overline{F}},\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})_{\mathfrak{m}_{\overline{r}}}\neq 0
for some level
U
and
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.

Now let
U_{p}\cong\mathrm{GSp}_{4}(\mathcal{O}_{F}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}_{p})
.
A Serre weight is defined to be a simple smooth
\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}[U_{p}]
-module.
Every Serre weight is obtained via inflation from an irreducible representation of
\mathrm{GSp}_{4}(\mathcal{O}_{F}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{F}_{p})
over
\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}
.
If
\overline{r}
is automorphic, let
W(\overline{r})
be the set of Serre weights
\sigma
such that
H^{i}_{\et}(\Sh_{U,\overline{F}},\mathcal{F}_{\sigma^{\vee}})_{\mathfrak{m}_{\overline{r}}}\neq 0
for some
i
.
If
\overline{r}
is tame and generic at places above
p
, Gee–Herzig–Savitt defined an explicit set of Serre weights
W^{?}(\overline{r})

[
GHS18
, Definition 9.2.5]
to formulate a generalization of the weight part of Serre’s conjecture. We confirm their conjecture under technical assumptions.

Theorem 1.1.1

(Theorem
4.4.1
)
.

Suppose that
\overline{r}
is potentially diagonalizably automorphic and satisfies the Taylor–Wiles conditions. If
\overline{r}
is tamely ramified and
9
-generic at all places above
p
, then
W(\overline{r})=W^{?}(\overline{r})
.

Remark 1.1.2
.

We explain the difference between Theorem
1.1.1
and
[
Lee
, Theorem 6.2.5]
.

(1)

The genericity assumption on
\overline{r}
at places above
p
in
[
Lee
]
is much stronger than the current work. In principle, the assumption in
loc. cit.
can be computed explicitly. However, it remains inexplicit at this moment due to the computational complexity. In contrast, the genericity assumption in this paper is explicit. In particular, for any
p\geq 37
, one can construct an example of
\overline{r}
satisfying our assumptions using
[
EL24
, Lemma 4.4.5]
.

(2)

[
Lee
]
works with algebraic automorphic forms on an inner form of
\mathrm{GSp}_{4}
that is compact modulo center at infinity. In the current paper, we can work with
\mathrm{GSp}_{4}
directly, thanks to the torsion vanishing result in
[
HL24
]
. Our argument can be applied to the setting of
[
Lee
]
as well.

1.2.
Previous methods

In recent years, there has been substantial progress on generalizations of the weight part of Serre’s conjecture in terms of both conjectures and results.
A major tool in these advancements is the Taylor–Wiles method which patches global spaces of automorphic forms into objects of conjecturally local origin.

We first set up some notation. For simplicity, we assume in this introduction that
p
is inert in
F
. We denote by
K=F_{p}
with ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{K}
and residue field
k
.
Let
E/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
be a sufficiently large finite extension with ring of integers
\mathcal{O}
, uniformizer
\varpi
, and residue field
\mathbb{F}
. Then
\overline{r}
is valued in
\mathrm{GSp}_{4}(\mathbb{F})
, and we let
{\overline{\rho}}=\overline{r}|_{G_{F_{p}}}
.
We consider a Hodge type
\eta
lifting the half sum of all positive roots and a generic tame inertial type
\tau:I_{K}\rightarrow\mathrm{GSp}_{4}(E)
.
The inertial local Langlands correspondence attaches to
\tau
an irreducible generic Deligne–Lusztig representation
\sigma(\tau)
of
\mathrm{GSp}_{4}(k)
defined over
E
(see §
2.2.5
).
Let
\sigma^{\circ}(\tau)
be a
\mathrm{GSp}_{4}(k)
-stable
\mathcal{O}
-lattice in
\sigma(\tau)
and
\overline{\sigma}(\tau)=\sigma^{\circ}(\tau)/\varpi
.
We denote by
\mathrm{JH}(\overline{\sigma}(\tau))
the set of Jordan–Hölder factors of
\overline{\sigma}(\tau)
.
We denote by
R^{\square}_{{\overline{\rho}}}
the framed deformation ring for
{\overline{\rho}}
and by
R_{{\overline{\rho}}}^{\eta,\tau}
the potentially crystalline deformation ring for
{\overline{\rho}}
of type
(\eta,\tau)
. We use
\sigma
to denote a Serre weight. There is a natural bijection between
\mathrm{JH}(\overline{\sigma}(\tau))\cap W^{?}(\overline{r})
and the set of irreducible components in
\mathrm{Spec}\ R_{{\overline{\rho}}}^{\eta,\tau}/\varpi
, which we denote by
\sigma\mapsto\mathcal{C}_{\sigma}({\overline{\rho}})
.

The Taylor–Wiles method produces a functor
M_{\infty}
from the category of
\mathcal{O}[\mathrm{GSp}_{4}(k)]
-modules to modules over (a power series ring over)
R_{{\overline{\rho}}}^{\square}
. The main properties of
M_{\infty}
can be summarized as follows:

(1)

M_{\infty}
is an exact functor.

(2)

For a tame inertial type
\tau
and any
\sigma\in\mathrm{JH}(\overline{\sigma}(\tau))
, the support
\mathrm{Supp}_{R_{{\overline{\rho}}}^{\square}}M_{\infty}(\sigma^{\circ}(\tau))
(resp.
\mathrm{Supp}_{R_{{\overline{\rho}}}^{\square}}M_{\infty}(\sigma)
) is a union of irreducible components in
\mathrm{Spec}\ R_{{\overline{\rho}}}^{\eta,\tau}
(resp. in
\mathrm{Spec}\ R_{{\overline{\rho}}}^{\eta,\tau}/\varpi
).

(3)

M_{\infty}(\sigma)\neq 0
if and only if
\sigma\in W(\overline{r})
.

It is expected that
\mathrm{Supp}_{R_{{\overline{\rho}}}^{\square}}M_{\infty}(\sigma^{\circ}(\tau))
is all of
\mathrm{Spec}\ R_{{\overline{\rho}}}^{\eta,\tau}
.
In fact, it should be equivalent to a certain modularity lifting theorem under mild hypotheses using ideas of
[
Kis09
]
and results of
[
FKP21
]
.
An observation due to
[
GK14
]
, with ideas tracing back ultimately to
[
BM02
]
, is that this full support statement determines the cycles of the modules
M_{\infty}(\overline{\sigma}(\tau))
and thus determines the cycles of the modules
M_{\infty}(\sigma)
using (
1
) (assuming that the collection of
\overline{\sigma}(\tau)
generates the Grothendieck group of
\mathbb{F}[\mathrm{GSp}_{4}(k)]
-modules).
The weight part of Serre’s conjecture can then be deduced from (
2
) and (
3
).

This full support statement is known in some cases.
For
\mathrm{GL}_{2}
, potential diagonalizability techniques actually give the full support statement in far greater generality.
Beyond
\mathrm{GL}_{2}
, potential diagonalizability has so far enjoyed less utility.
Instead, known full support statements have relied on the irreducibility of tamely potentially Galois deformation spaces.

[
LLHLM18
,
LLHLMb
]
show that generic tamely potentially crystalline Galois deformation rings are integral domains under a combinatorial and explicit genericity condition by showing that their special fibers are reduced.
However, for more general groups, reduced special fibers occur rarely. Known cases of the Breuil–Mézard conjecture
[
LLHLM23
,
FLH
]
show that special fibers of generic tamely potentially crystalline Galois deformation rings cannot all be reduced for
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
with
n\geq 4
because (generic) Deligne–Lusztig representations are not residually multiplicity-free.
In fact, the only other split group where (generic) Deligne–Lusztig representations are residually multiplicity free is
\mathrm{GSp}_{4}
.
However, the reduced special fiber property for
\mathrm{GSp}_{4}
fails because of a new phenomenon, known as
entailment
,
wherein the supports of
M_{\infty}(\sigma)
are reducible.
From this perspective, the case of
\mathrm{GSp}_{4}
seems to be much closer to the general case.

Nevertheless, generic tamely potentially crystalline Galois deformation rings are often still integral domains when
{\overline{\rho}}
is tamely ramified.
A new local model theory shows that generic tamely potentially crystalline Galois deformation rings can be computed as the completion of an algebraic variety over
\mathcal{O}

[
LLHLM23
,
Lee
]
.
This algebraic variety has a torus symmetry which allows one to show that it is unibranch at torus fixed points under an inexplicit genericity assumption.
As a consequence, for generic tamely ramified Galois representations, all of their tamely potentially crystalline Galois deformation rings are integral domains.
Applying the Taylor–Wiles method then yields the weight part of Serre’s conjecture under these hypotheses.
(In practice, deducing the weight part of Serre’s conjecture in these situations is simplified with the additional observation that the cycles of the modules
M_{\infty}(\sigma)
are necessarily effective.)
While for large
p
, most tamely ramified Galois representations satisfy this genericity condition, verifying genericity for any specific Galois representation is computationally involved.
In fact, it is tricky to exhibit a single explicit example of a Galois representation for
\mathrm{GL}_{4}
or
\mathrm{GSp}_{4}
which satisfies this genericity condition, though many exist!
While we expect that the deformation rings are integral domains under a milder genericity condition, we do not know how to prove this.

1.3.
Methods

In this paper, we introduce a new method for proving the weight part of Serre’s conjecture that does not rely on the full knowledge of the support of patched modules.
While the present paper concerns
\mathrm{GSp}_{4}
, many aspects of the method apply in greater generality.

It is known that
M_{\infty}(\sigma)=0
for
\sigma\notin W^{?}(\overline{r})
, and so it suffices to show that
M_{\infty}(\sigma)\neq 0
for
\sigma\in W^{?}(\overline{r})
.
Rather than determine the support of
M_{\infty}(\sigma)
for
\sigma\in W^{?}(\overline{r})
, we show that
M_{\infty}(\sigma)
is supported on
\mathcal{C}_{\sigma}({\overline{\rho}})
.
We first prove this for
obvious
weights (Definition
2.2.5
).
In this case, there is an inertial type
\tau
with
\mathrm{JH}(\overline{\sigma}(\tau))\cap W^{?}(\overline{r})=\{\sigma\}
and
M_{\infty}(\overline{\sigma}^{\circ}(\tau))\cong M_{\infty}(\sigma)
.
Using the change of weight result in
[
BLGGT14
]
(where we need that
\overline{r}
is potentially diagonalizably automorphic),
M_{\infty}(\sigma)
is supported on
\mathcal{C}_{\sigma}({\overline{\rho}})
, the only component in the special fiber of
\mathrm{Spec}\ R_{\overline{\rho}}^{\eta,\tau}
.

Our main contribution is to propagate the statement that
M_{\infty}(\sigma)
is supported on
\mathcal{C}_{\sigma}({\overline{\rho}})
to all Serre weights in
W^{?}(\overline{r})
.
Recall that there is a natural bijection between
\mathrm{JH}(\overline{\sigma}(\tau))\cap W^{?}(\overline{r})
and the set of irreducible components in
\mathrm{Spec}\ R^{\eta,\tau}_{{\overline{\rho}}}/\varpi
denoted by
\sigma\mapsto\mathcal{C}_{\sigma}({\overline{\rho}})
.
Since
R_{{\overline{\rho}}}^{\eta,\tau}/\varpi
is not reduced (even generically), there could be several irreducible components in
\mathrm{Spec}\ R^{\eta,\tau}_{{\overline{\rho}}}
whose special fiber contains
\mathcal{C}_{\sigma}({\overline{\rho}})
.
Thus, even if
\mathcal{C}_{\sigma}({\overline{\rho}})
is in the support of
M_{\infty}(\sigma^{\circ}(\tau))
, it is unclear which of the components in
\mathrm{Spec}\ R^{\eta,\tau}_{{\overline{\rho}}}
is in the support of
M_{\infty}(\sigma^{\circ}(\tau))
. Fortunately, if
\sigma
is an
outer
weight (Definition
2.2.5
), the local model theory shows that the component of
\mathrm{Spec}\ R^{\eta,\tau}_{{\overline{\rho}}}/\varpi
corresponding to
\sigma
occurs with multiplicity one. In particular,
\mathcal{C}_{\sigma}({\overline{\rho}})
generizes to a unique component in
\mathrm{Spec}\ R^{\eta,\tau}_{{\overline{\rho}}}
(i.e. it is contained in a unique component in
\mathrm{Spec}\ R^{\eta,\tau}_{{\overline{\rho}}}
).

Our main technical result proves that for various
\tau
, there is a pair of distinct outer weights
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in\mathrm{JH}(\overline{\sigma}(\tau))\cap W^{?}(\overline{r})
such that
\mathcal{C}_{\sigma_{1}}({\overline{\rho}})
and
\mathcal{C}_{\sigma_{2}}({\overline{\rho}})
generize (uniquely) to a common component
\mathcal{C}
in
\mathrm{Spec}\ R^{\eta,\tau}_{{\overline{\rho}}}
(see Theorem
3.4.8
for the precise result). By (
1
) and (
2
), this implies that
M_{\infty}(\sigma^{\circ}(\tau))
is supported on
\mathcal{C}_{\sigma_{1}}({\overline{\rho}})
if and only if it is supported on
\mathcal{C}_{\sigma_{2}}({\overline{\rho}})
.
Moreover, for an outer weight
\sigma
, the only patched module of a Serre weight in
\mathrm{JH}(\overline{\sigma}(\tau))
that is possibly supported on
\mathcal{C}_{\sigma}({\overline{\rho}})
is
M_{\infty}(\sigma)
.
Thus
M_{\infty}(\sigma_{1})
is supported on
\mathcal{C}_{\sigma_{1}}({\overline{\rho}})
if and only if
M_{\infty}(\sigma_{2})
is supported on
\mathcal{C}_{\sigma_{2}}({\overline{\rho}})
.
Finally, we show that any two Serre weights in
W^{?}(\overline{r})
can be connected by a chain of components
\mathcal{C}
.
With obvious weights as a starting point, this completes the proof of Theorem
1.1.1
.

We end the discussion by describing our method for finding the family of components
\mathcal{C}
, which crucially relies on the recent construction of the Emerton–Gee stack and an analysis of its topology using local models. Let
\mathcal{X}_{\Sym}^{\eta,\tau}
be the closed substack of the Emerton–Gee stack for
\mathrm{GSp}_{4}
parameterizing potentially crystalline representations of type
(\eta,\tau)
. Similar to Galois deformation rings, there is a bijection between
\mathrm{JH}(\overline{\sigma}(\tau))
and the set of irreducible components in
\mathcal{X}_{\Sym}^{\eta,\tau}
, denoted by
\sigma\mapsto\mathcal{C}_{\sigma}
.
The first observation is that the set of irreducible components in
\mathrm{Spec}\ R_{{\overline{\rho}}}^{\eta,\tau}
are in correspondence with the preimage of
{\overline{\rho}}
under the normalization map
\nu:(\mathcal{X}_{\Sym}^{\eta,\tau})^{\mathrm{nm}}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}_{\Sym}^{\eta,\tau}
. Note that for outer weight
\sigma
,
\mathcal{C}_{\sigma}
occurs with multiplicity one in the special fiber and thus pulls back to a unique irreducible component
\mathcal{C}_{\sigma}^{\mathrm{nm}}
under
\nu
. For outer weights
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
,
\mathcal{C}_{\sigma_{1}}({\overline{\rho}})
and
\mathcal{C}_{\sigma_{2}}({\overline{\rho}})
have a common unique generization if and only if
\nu(\mathcal{C}_{\sigma_{1}}^{\mathrm{nm}}\cap\mathcal{C}_{\sigma_{2}}^{\mathrm{nm}})
contains
{\overline{\rho}}
.

Following
[
LLHLMa
]
, we consider pairs of outer weights
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in\mathrm{JH}(\overline{\sigma}(\tau))
such that
\mathcal{C}_{\sigma_{1}}\cap\mathcal{C}_{\sigma_{2}}
intersects with the normal locus of
\mathcal{X}^{\eta,\tau}_{\Sym}
. In particular, there exists an open substack
\mathcal{U}\subset\mathcal{C}_{\sigma_{1}}\cap\mathcal{C}_{\sigma_{2}}
contained in the normal locus. Thus, we can find a copy of
\mathcal{U}
in
\mathcal{C}_{\sigma_{1}}^{\mathrm{nm}}\cap\mathcal{C}_{\sigma_{2}}^{\mathrm{nm}}
. For some choices of
\tau
(relative to
{\overline{\rho}}
) considered in
loc. cit.
,
{\overline{\rho}}
is in
\mathcal{U}
and thus
\nu(\mathcal{C}_{\sigma_{1}}^{\mathrm{nm}}\cap\mathcal{C}_{\sigma_{2}}^{\mathrm{nm}})
contains
{\overline{\rho}}
. However, such choices of
\tau
are too restrictive for applications and can only prove that obvious weights are in
W(\overline{r})
. Instead, we show that
{\overline{\rho}}
is in the closure of
\mathcal{U}
in
\mathcal{X}^{\eta,\tau}_{\Sym}
for a larger family of
\tau
. Our family of
\tau
is flexible enough to reach all Serre weights in
W^{?}(\overline{r})
.
This can be reduced to finding torus fixed points in a certain subset of a deformed affine Springer fiber, which
we accomplish by explicit computations along the lines of
[
BA
]
(see Proposition
3.1.6
). Let us emphasize that
{\overline{\rho}}
is not known to be in the normal locus and is definitely not in
\mathcal{U}
(unless
\tau
is in the family considered in
[
LLHLMa
]
). In particular, our argument cannot be made using Galois deformation rings instead of the Emerton–Gee stack because it uses a part of
\mathcal{X}_{\Sym}^{\eta,\tau}

away from

{\overline{\rho}}
to analyze the geometry
near

{\overline{\rho}}
.

1.4.
Application

In the proof of Serre weight conjectures explained above, we only obtain a lower bound on
\mathrm{Supp}_{R^{\eta,\tau}_{{\overline{\rho}}}}M_{\infty}(\sigma^{\circ}(\tau))
for a family of
\tau
. This contrasts to earlier works (
[
LLHLM23
,
Lee
]
) proving that
M_{\infty}(\sigma^{\circ}(\tau))
is fully supported on
\mathrm{Spec}\ R^{\eta,\tau}_{{\overline{\rho}}}
(under an inexplicit genericity assumption). However, we can still prove the full support result for all generic
\tau
(and higher Hodge–Tate weights) by combining our result with the Breuil–Mézard conjecture for tamely potentially crystalline deformation rings in small weight
[
FLH
]
, explicit Breuil–Mézard cycles from
[
LHL
]
, and weight shifting mod
p
differential operators of
[
Ort24
]
. As a result, we deduce modularity lifting theorems for small Hodge–Tate weights and generic tame inertial types.

Since the work of Kisin, the Breuil–Mézard conjecture has been known to be closely related to modularity lifting theorems and the weight part of Serre’s conjecture (see e.g.
[
EG14
]
).

[
Kis09
]
used the
p
-adic Langlands correspondence for
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
to prove the Breuil–Mézard conjecture and, as a consequence, deduced many instances of the Fontaine–Mazur conjecture.
In the reverse direction,
[
GK14
]
used modularity lifting theorems to prove the Breuil–Mézard conjecture for 2-dimensional Barsotti–Tate representations, which was in turn used to prove the weight part of Serre’s conjecture for Hilbert modular forms as formulated by Buzzard–Diamond–Jarvis.

The breakthrough work
[
FLH
]
proved the Breuil–Mézard conjecture for unramified groups with small Hodge–Tate weights and generic tame inertial types using a novel method that avoids both a
p
-adic Langlands correspondence and Taylor–Wiles patching.
Their method provides an algorithm to compute Breuil–Mézard cycles explicitly, which was implemented for low rank groups including
\mathrm{GSp}_{4}
in
[
LHL
]
. In particular, their result verifies that certain Breuil–Mézard cycles for
\mathrm{GSp}_{4}
exhibit entailment.
In order to deduce modularity lifting theorems for
\mathrm{GSp}_{4}
using the result of
[
FLH
,
LHL
]
, we need to prove a strong form of the weight part of Serre’s conjecture.
That is, we need to realize the local Breuil–Mézard cycles by patching global spaces of automorphic forms à la Taylor–Wiles.

We assume
F=\mathbb{Q}
to apply the main result of
[
Ort24
]
. To simplify the argument, let us consider here the
minimal
case, which allows us to focus on the places dividing
p
. The general case can be treated using Taylor’s Ihara avoidance argument.
First, our results give lower bounds for the supports of patched modules for Serre weights.
A computation of colength one deformation rings shows that these bounds are sharp for Serre weights in the bottom two
p
-restricted alcoves.
We need to show that the support of
M_{\infty}(\sigma)
contains one extra component
\mathcal{C}_{\sigma^{\prime}}({\overline{\rho}})
when
\sigma
is in the next
p
-restricted alcove and
\sigma^{\prime}
is the Serre weight linked to
\sigma
in the lowest
p
-restricted alcove.
The lower bounds can be transferred to patched coherent cohomology using a comparison of rational étale and coherent cohomologies.
Combining this with a weight shifting mod
p
differential operator on sections of automorphic vector bundles on
\Sh_{U}
constructed in
[
Ort24
]
, we see that
\mathcal{C}_{\sigma^{\prime}}({\overline{\rho}})
is in the support of the patched coherent cohomology corresponding to the mod
p
(dual) Weyl module
W
with socle
\sigma
.
Transferring this back to patched étale cohomology, we show that
\mathcal{C}_{\sigma^{\prime}}({\overline{\rho}})
is in the support of
M_{\infty}(W)
.
The upper bound for supports of patched modules for Serre weights with the highest weight in the bottom two
p
-restricted alcoves then shows that
\mathcal{C}_{\sigma^{\prime}}({\overline{\rho}})
is in the support of
M_{\infty}(\sigma)
.

Combining our strong form of the weight part of Serre’s conjecture and the instances of the Breuil–Mézard conjecture proved in
[
FLH
]
yields the following modularity lifting result.

Theorem 1.4.1

(Theorem
4.5.1
)
.

Assume that
F=\mathbb{Q}
. Suppose that
\overline{r}
is potentially diagonalizably automorphic, satisfies Taylor–Wiles conditions, and
\overline{r}|_{G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}
is tame and 9-generic. If
r:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\mathrm{GSp}_{4}(E)
is a lift of
\overline{r}
that is unramified at all but finitely many places and potentially crystalline of type
(\lambda+\eta,\tau)
at
p
with
(2h_{\lambda}+6)
-generic tame inertial type
\tau
, then
r
is automorphic.

1.6.
Notation

For a field
K
, we denote by
\overline{K}
a fixed separable closure of
K
and let
G_{K}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{\tiny{def}}}}{{=}}\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{K}/K)
.
If
K
is defined as a subfield of an algebraically closed field, then we set
\overline{K}
to be this field.

If
K
is a nonarchimedean local field, we let
I_{K}\subset G_{K}
denote the inertial subgroup and
W_{K}\subset G_{K}
denote the Weil group.
We fix a prime
p\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
.
Let
E\subset\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
be a subfield which is finite-dimensional over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
.
We write
\mathcal{O}
to denote its ring of integers, fix a uniformizer
\varpi\in\mathcal{O}
and let
\mathbb{F}
denote the residue field of
E
.
We will assume throughout that

1.6.1.
Reductive groups

Let
G
denote a split connected reductive group (over some ring) together with a Borel
B
, a maximal split torus
T\subset B
, and
Z\subset T
the center of
G
.
Let
\Phi^{+}\subset\Phi
(resp.
\Phi^{\vee,+}\subset\Phi^{\vee}
) denote the subset of positive roots (resp. positive coroots) in the set of roots (resp. coroots) for
(G,B,T)
.
We use the notation
\alpha>0
(resp.
\alpha<0
) for a positive (resp. negative) root
\alpha\in\Phi
.
Let
\Delta
(resp.
\Delta^{\vee}
) be the set of simple roots (resp. coroots).
Let
X^{*}(T)
be the group of characters of
T
, and set
X^{0}(T)
to be the subgroup consisting of characters
\lambda\in X^{*}(T)
such that
\langle\lambda,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle=0
for all
\alpha^{\vee}\in\Delta^{\vee}
.
Let
\Lambda_{R}\subset X^{*}(T)
denote the root lattice for
G
.
Let
W(G,T)
denote the Weyl group of
(G,T)
.
We sometimes write
W
for
W(G,T)
when there is no chance for confusion. Let
w_{0}
denote the longest element of
W
.
Let
W_{a}
(resp.
\widetilde{W}
) denote the affine Weyl group and extended affine Weyl group

W_{a}=\Lambda_{R}\rtimes W,\quad\widetilde{W}=X^{*}(T)\rtimes W

for
G
.
We use
t_{\nu}\in\widetilde{W}
to denote the image of
\nu\in X^{*}(T)
.

Let
M
be a free
\mathbb{Z}
-module of finite rank (e.g.
M=X^{*}(T)
).
The duality pairing between
M
and its
\mathbb{Z}
-linear dual
M^{*}
will be denoted by
\langle\ ,\,\rangle
.
If
A
is any ring, the pairing
\langle\ ,\,\rangle
extends by
A
-linearity to a pairing between
M\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}A
and
M^{*}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}A
, and by an abuse of notation it will be denoted with the same symbol
\langle\ ,\,\rangle
.

We write
G^{\vee}=G^{\vee}_{/\mathbb{Z}}
for the split connected reductive group over
\mathbb{Z}
defined by the root datum
(X_{*}(T),X^{*}(T),\Phi^{\vee},\Phi)
.
This defines a maximal split torus
T^{\vee}\subseteq G^{\vee}
such that we have canonical identifications
X^{*}(T^{\vee})\cong X_{*}(T)
and
X_{*}(T^{\vee})\cong X^{*}(T)
.

Let
V\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{\tiny{def}}}}{{=}}X^{*}(T)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
.
For
(\alpha,k)\in\Phi\times\mathbb{Z}
, we have the root hyperplane
H_{\alpha,k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{\tiny{def}}}}{{=}}\{x\in V\mid\langle\lambda,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle=k\}
and the half-hyperplanes
H^{+}_{\alpha,k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{\tiny{def}}}}{{=}}\{x\in V\mid\langle x,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle>k\}
and
H^{-}_{\alpha,n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{\tiny{def}}}}{{=}}\{x\in V\mid\langle x,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle<k\}.

An alcove is a connected component of
V\setminus\big(\bigcup_{(\alpha,k)}H_{\alpha,k}\big)
.

We say that an alcove
A
is
restricted
if
0<\langle\lambda,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle<1
for all
\alpha\in\Delta
and
\lambda\in A
.
We let
A_{0}
denote the (dominant) base alcove, i.e. the set of
\lambda\in X^{*}(T)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
such that
0<\langle\lambda,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle<1
for all
\alpha\in\Phi^{+}
.
Let
\mathcal{A}
denote the set of alcoves.
Recall that
\widetilde{W}
acts transitively on the set of alcoves, and
\widetilde{W}\cong\widetilde{W}_{a}\rtimes\Omega
where
\Omega
is the stabilizer of
A_{0}
.
We define

\widetilde{W}^{+}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{\tiny{def}}}}{{=}}\{\widetilde{w}\in\widetilde{W}:\widetilde{w}(A_{0})\textrm{ is dominant}\}\ \text{and }\widetilde{W}^{+}_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{\tiny{def}}}}{{=}}\{\widetilde{w}\in\widetilde{W}^{+}:\widetilde{w}(A_{0})\textrm{ is restricted}\}.

We fix an element
\eta\in X^{*}(T)
such that
\langle\eta,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle=1
for all positive simple roots
\alpha
and let
\widetilde{w}_{h}
be
w_{0}t_{-\eta}\in\widetilde{W}^{+}_{1}
.

A (
\eta
-shifted)
p
-alcove
is a connected component of
X^{*}(T)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}\backslash(\cup_{(\alpha,k)}(H_{\alpha,pk}-\eta))
. We define
p
-restricted
(resp.
dominant
)
p
-alcoves analogous to the previous paragraph.

Let
\mathcal{O}_{p}
be a finite étale
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-algebra.
Then
\mathcal{O}_{p}
is isomorphic to a product
\prod_{S_{p}}\mathcal{O}_{v}
for some finite set
S_{p}
where for each
v\in S_{p}
,
\mathcal{O}_{v}
is the ring of integers in a finite unramified extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. We write
F_{p}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{\tiny{def}}}}{{=}}\mathcal{O}_{p}[1/p]
. For each
v\in S_{p}
, let
F_{v}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{\tiny{def}}}}{{=}}\mathcal{O}_{v}[1/p]
and
k_{v}
be the residue field of
F_{v}
. We let
k_{p}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{\tiny{def}}}}{{=}}\prod_{v\in S_{p}}k_{v}
.

In global applications,
S_{p}
will be a finite set of places dividing
p
of a number field
F
.
When working locally,
S_{p}
will have cardinality one, in which case we drop the subscripts from
k_{v}
and denote the single extension
F_{v}
of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
by
K
.

If
G
is a split connected reductive group over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, with Borel
B
, maximal split torus
T
, and center
Z
, we let
G_{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{\tiny{def}}}}{{=}}\mathrm{Res}_{\mathcal{O}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}}G_{/\mathcal{O}_{p}}
with Borel subgroup
B_{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{\tiny{def}}}}{{=}}\mathrm{Res}_{\mathcal{O}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}}B_{/\mathcal{O}_{p}}
, maximal torus
T_{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{\tiny{def}}}}{{=}}\mathrm{Res}_{\mathcal{O}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}}T_{/\mathcal{O}_{p}}
, and
Z_{0}=\mathrm{Res}_{\mathcal{O}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}}Z_{/\mathcal{O}_{p}}
.
Assume that
\mathbb{F}
contains the image of any ring homomorphism
\mathcal{O}_{p}\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
and let
\mathcal{J}
be the set of ring homomorphisms
\mathcal{O}_{p}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}
.
Then
\underline{G}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{\tiny{def}}}}{{=}}(G_{0})_{/\mathcal{O}}
is naturally identified with the split reductive group
G_{/\mathbb{F}}^{\mathcal{J}}
.
We similarly define
\underline{B},\underline{T},
and
\underline{Z}
.
Corresponding to
(\underline{G},\underline{B},\underline{T})
, we have the set of positive roots
\underline{\Phi}^{+}\subset\underline{\Phi}
and the set of positive coroots
\underline{\Phi}^{\vee,+}\subset\underline{\Phi}^{\vee}
.
The notations
\underline{\Lambda}_{R}
,
\underline{W}
,
\underline{W}_{a}
,
\widetilde{\underline{W}}
,
\widetilde{\underline{W}}^{+}
,
\widetilde{\underline{W}}^{+}_{1}
,
\underline{\Omega}
should be clear as should the natural isomorphisms
X^{*}(\underline{T})=X^{*}(T)^{\mathcal{J}}
and the like.
The absolute Frobenius automorphism
\varphi
on
k_{p}
induces an automorphism
\pi
of the identified groups
X^{*}(\underline{T})
and
X_{*}(\underline{T}^{\vee})
by the formula
\pi(\lambda)_{\sigma}=\lambda_{\sigma\circ\varphi^{-1}}
for all
\lambda\in X^{*}(\underline{T})
and
\sigma:\mathcal{O}_{p}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}
.
We assume that, in this case, the element
\eta\in X^{*}(\underline{T})
we fixed is
\pi
-invariant.
We similarly define an automorphism
\pi
of
\underline{W}
and
\widetilde{\underline{W}}
.

1.6.2.
The group
\mathrm{GSp}_{4}

Let
\mathrm{GSp}_{4}
be the split reductive group over
\mathbb{Z}
defined by

\displaystyle\mathrm{GSp}_{4}(R)=\{g\in\mathrm{GL}_{4}(R)\mid{}^{t}gJg=\mathrm{sim}(g)J\text{ for some $\mathrm{sim}(g)\in R^{\times}$}\}

for any commutative ring
R
, where
J={\begin{pmatrix}&&&1\\
&&1&\\
&-1&&\\
-1&&&\end{pmatrix}}.
Then
\mathrm{sim}:g\mapsto\mathrm{sim}(g)
defines a character of
\mathrm{GSp}_{4}
, called the
similitude character
.

When
G=\mathrm{GSp}_{4}
, we will take
B
to be the upper triangular Borel and
T
the diagonal torus. We also write
U
for the unipotent radical of
B
and
\overline{U}
for the unipotent radical of the opposite Borel subgroup. Let
W=W(\mathrm{GSp}_{4},T)
. We identify
W
with the subgroup of
\mathrm{GSp}_{4}
generated by two simple reflections

\displaystyle s_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{\tiny{def}}}}{{=}}{\begin{pmatrix}&1&&\\
1&&&\\
&&&1\\
&&1&\end{pmatrix}},\ s_{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{\tiny{def}}}}{{=}}{\begin{pmatrix}1&&&\\
&&1&\\
&-1&&\\
&&&1\end{pmatrix}}.

We identify the character group
X^{*}(T)
with
\mathbb{Z}^{3}
by defining the character corresponding to
(a,b;c)\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}
by

\displaystyle(a,b;c):{\begin{pmatrix}x&&&\\
&y&&\\
&&zy^{-1}&\\
&&&zx^{-1}\end{pmatrix}}\mapsto x^{a}y^{b}z^{c}.

Similarly, we identify the cocharacter group
X_{*}(T)
with
\mathbb{Z}^{3}
by

\displaystyle(a,b;c):x\mapsto{\begin{pmatrix}x^{a}&&&\\
&x^{b}&&\\
&&x^{c-b}&\\
&&&x^{c-a}\end{pmatrix}}.

We choose the element
\eta\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{\tiny{def}}}}{{=}}(2,1;0)\in X^{*}(T)
.

By an exceptional isomorphism, we have
\mathrm{GSp}_{4}^{\vee}\simeq\mathrm{GSp}_{4}
. We denote by
\mathrm{std}:\mathrm{GSp}_{4}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{4}
the standard representation of
\mathrm{GSp}_{4}
. Let
T_{4}
be the maximal diagonal torus of
\mathrm{GL}_{4}
. Then
\mathrm{std}
induces
T^{\vee}\hookrightarrow T_{4}^{\vee}
,
X_{*}(T^{\vee})\hookrightarrow X_{*}(T_{4}^{\vee})
and
\mathcal{T}:X^{*}(T)\rightarrow X^{*}(T_{4})
by duality. Explicitly, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}(a,b;c)=(a+b+c,a+c,b+c,c).

We recall the four restricted alcoves for
\mathrm{GSp}_{4}
:

\displaystyle A_{0}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{\tiny{def}}}}{{=}}\left\{(a,b;c)\in X^{*}(T)\otimes\mathbb{R}\mid 0<b<a,\ 0<a+b<1\right\}

\displaystyle A_{1}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{\tiny{def}}}}{{=}}\left\{(a,b;c)\in X^{*}(T)\otimes\mathbb{R}\mid b<a<1,\ a+b<2\right\}

\displaystyle A_{2}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{\tiny{def}}}}{{=}}\left\{(a,b;c)\in X^{*}(T)\otimes\mathbb{R}\mid a-b<1<a,\ a+b<2\right\}

\displaystyle A_{3}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{\tiny{def}}}}{{=}}\left\{(a,b;c)\in X^{*}(T)\otimes\mathbb{R}\mid b<1,\ a-b<1,\ 2<a+b\right\}.

We also have four
p
-restricted
p
-alcoves
C_{0},C_{1},C_{2}
, and
C_{3}
.

When
G
is a product of copies of
\mathrm{GSp}_{4}
indexed over a set
\mathcal{J}
we take
\eta\in X^{*}(T)
to correspond to the element
(2,1;0)_{j\in\mathcal{J}}\in(\mathbb{Z}^{3})^{\mathcal{J}}
.
In this case, given
j\in\mathcal{J}
we write
\eta_{j}\in X^{*}(T)
to denote the element which corresponds to the tuple
(2,1;0)
at
j
and to the zero tuple elsewhere. Similarly, we write
s_{i,j}\in W
for
i=1,2
and
j\in\mathcal{J}
for the tuple
s_{i}
at
j
and identity elsewhere.

1.6.3.
Galois Theory

We now assume that
S_{p}
has cardinality one.
We write
K\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{\tiny{def}}}}{{=}}F_{v}
and drop the subscripts from
k_{v}
.
Let
W(k)
be the ring of Witt vectors which is also the ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{K_{0}}
of
K_{0}
.
We denote the arithmetic Frobenius automorphism on
W(k)
by
\varphi
, which acts as raising to
p
-th power on the residue field.
We fix an embedding
\sigma_{0}
of
K_{0}
into
E
(equivalently an embedding
k
into
\mathbb{F}
) and define
\sigma_{j}=\sigma_{0}\circ\varphi^{-j}
, which gives an identification between
\mathcal{J}=\mathrm{Hom}(k,\mathbb{F})
and
\mathbb{Z}/f\mathbb{Z}
.

We normalize Artin’s reciprocity map
\mathrm{Art}_{K}:K^{\times}\rightarrow W_{K}^{\mathrm{ab}}
in such a way that uniformizers are sent to geometric Frobenius elements.

Given an element
\pi_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{\tiny{def}}}}{{=}}(-p)^{\frac{1}{p^{f}-1}}\in\overline{K}
we have a corresponding character
\omega_{K}:I_{K}\rightarrow W(k)^{\times}
. By composing
\sigma\in\mathrm{Hom}(k,\mathbb{F})
, we define

\omega_{K,\sigma}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{\tiny{def}}}}{{=}}\sigma\circ\omega_{K}:I_{K}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}^{\times}.

Let
\rho:G_{K}\rightarrow\mathrm{GSp}_{4}(E)
be a
p
-adic, de Rham Galois representation. For
\sigma:K\hookrightarrow E
, we have the
\sigma
-labeled Hodge–Tate cocharacter
\mu_{\sigma}\in X_{*}(T^{\vee})
. For any algebraic representation
r
of
\mathrm{GSp}_{4}
, then
r\circ\mu_{\sigma}
matches the multiset of
\sigma
-labeled Hodge–Tate weights of
r\circ\rho
, i.e. the set of integers
i
such that
\dim_{E}\big(r\circ\rho\otimes_{\sigma,K}\mathbb{C}_{p}(-i)\big)^{G_{K}}\neq 0
(with the usual notation for Tate twists). In particular, the cyclotomic character
\varepsilon
has Hodge–Tate weights 1 for all embeddings
\sigma:K\hookrightarrow E
. The
inertial type
of
\rho
is the isomorphism class of
\mathrm{WD}(\rho)|_{I_{K}}
, where
\mathrm{WD}(\rho)
is the Weil–Deligne representation attached to
\rho
as in
[
CDT99
]
, Appendix B.1 (in particular,
\rho\mapsto\mathrm{WD}(\rho)
is
covariant
). An inertial type is a morphism
\tau:I_{K}\rightarrow\mathrm{GSp}_{4}(E)
with open kernel and which extends to the Weil group
W_{K}
of
G_{K}
. We say that
\rho
has type
(\mu,\tau)
if
\rho
has the Hodge–Tate cocharacters
\mu=(\mu_{\sigma})_{\sigma}
and inertial type given by (the isomorphism class of)
\tau
.

2.
Representation theory

2.1.
Extended affine Weyl groups

In this section, we briefly collect some background material on Weyl groups that will be needed throughout the paper.

Recall from §
1.6.1
that
G
is a split reductive group with split maximal torus
T
and Borel
B
,
W\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{\tiny{def}}}}{{=}}W(G,T)
,
W_{a}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{\tiny{def}}}}{{=}}\Lambda\rtimes W
, and
\widetilde{W}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{\tiny{def}}}}{{=}}X^{*}(T)\rtimes W
. We denote by
\mathcal{A}
the set of alcoves of
X^{*}(T)\otimes\mathbb{R}
and by
A_{0}\in\mathcal{A}
the dominant base alcove. We let
\uparrow
denote the upper arrow ordering on alcoves as defined in
[
Jan03
, §II.6.5]
. For
G=\mathrm{GSp}_{4}
, the four restricted alcoves
A_{0},\dots,A_{3}
satisfies

\displaystyle A_{0}\uparrow A_{1}\uparrow A_{2}\uparrow A_{3}.

Since
W_{a}
acts simply transitively on the set of alcoves,
\widetilde{w}\mapsto\widetilde{w}(A_{0})
induces a bijection
W_{a}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\rightarrow}\limits^{\sim}}\mathcal{A}
and thus an upper arrow ordering
\uparrow
on
W_{a}
.
The dominant base alcove
A_{0}
also defines a set of simple reflections in
W_{a}
and thus a Coxeter length function on
W_{a}
denoted
\ell(-)
and a Bruhat order on
W_{a}
denoted by
\leq
.

If
\Omega\subset\widetilde{W}
is the stabilizer of the base alcove, then
\widetilde{W}=W_{a}\rtimes\Omega
and so
\widetilde{W}
inherits a Bruhat and upper arrow order in the standard way: For
\widetilde{w}_{1},\widetilde{w}_{2}\in W_{a}
and
\delta\in\Omega
,
\widetilde{w}_{1}\delta\leq\widetilde{w}_{2}\delta
(resp.
\widetilde{w}_{1}\delta\uparrow\widetilde{w}_{2}\delta
) if and only if
\widetilde{w}_{1}\leq\widetilde{w}_{2}
(resp.
\widetilde{w}_{1}\uparrow\widetilde{w}_{2}
), and elements in different right
W_{a}
-cosets are incomparable.
We extend
\ell(-)
to
\widetilde{W}
by letting
\ell(\widetilde{w}\delta)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{\tiny{def}}}}{{=}}\ell(\widetilde{w})
for any
\widetilde{w}\in W_{a}
,
\delta\in\Omega
.

Let
(\widetilde{W}^{\vee},\leq)
be the following partially ordered group:
\widetilde{W}^{\vee}
is identified with
\widetilde{W}
as a group, and
\ell(-)
and
\leq
are defined with respect to the
antidominant
base alcove.

Definition 2.1.1
.

We define a bijection
\widetilde{w}\mapsto\widetilde{w}^{*}
between
\widetilde{W}
and
\widetilde{W}^{\vee}
as follows: for
\widetilde{w}=t_{\nu}w\in\widetilde{W}
, with
w\in W
and
\nu\in X^{*}(T)=X_{*}(T^{\vee})
, then
\widetilde{w}^{*}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{\tiny{def}}}}{{=}}w^{-1}t_{\nu}\in\widetilde{W}^{\vee}
.

This bijection respects notions of length and Bruhat order (see
[
LLHL19
, Lemma 2.1.3]
).

We recall the definition of the admissible set from
[
KR00
]
:

Definition 2.1.2
.

For
\lambda\in X^{*}(T)
, define

\mathrm{Adm}(\lambda)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{\tiny{def}}}}{{=}}\left\{\widetilde{w}\in\widetilde{W}\mid\widetilde{w}\leq t_{w(\lambda)}\text{ for some }w\in W\right\}.

We define the critical strips to be strips
H^{(0,1)}_{\alpha}=\{x\in X^{*}(T)\otimes\mathbb{R}\mid 0<\langle 1,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle<1\}
where
\alpha\in\Phi^{+}
.

Definition 2.1.3
.

An alcove
A\in\mathcal{A}
is
regular
if
A
does not lie in any critical strip. For any
\widetilde{w}\in\widetilde{W}
, we say
\widetilde{w}
is
regular
if
\widetilde{w}(A_{0})
is regular. Define

\mathrm{Adm}^{\text{reg}}(\lambda)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{\tiny{def}}}}{{=}}\{\widetilde{w}\in\mathrm{Adm}(\lambda)\mid\widetilde{w}\text{ is regular}\}.

For
\lambda\in X_{*}(T^{\vee})
, we denote by
\mathrm{Adm}^{\vee}(\lambda)
(resp.
\mathrm{Adm}^{\vee,\mathrm{reg}}(\lambda)
) the image of
\mathrm{Adm}(\lambda)
(resp.
\mathrm{Adm}^{\mathrm{reg}}(\lambda)
) under the bijection
(-)^{*}
between
\widetilde{W}
and
\widetilde{W}^{\vee}
.

Definition 2.1.4
.

Let
\lambda\in X^{*}(T)
and
\widetilde{w}\in\mathrm{Adm}(\lambda)
. Then we call
l(t_{\lambda})-l(\widetilde{w})

the colength of
\widetilde{w}
(in
\mathrm{Adm}(\lambda)
). If
l(t_{\lambda})-l(\widetilde{w})=1
, then we say
\widetilde{w}
is of colength one.

For later uses, we recall the various notions of genericity for elements of
X^{*}(T)
from
[
LLHLM23
, Definition 2.1.10]
.

.

Let
\lambda\in X^{*}(T)
be a weight and let

be an integer.

(1)

We say that
\lambda

lies

-deep in its

-alcove
if for all

, there exist integers

.

(2)

We say that
\lambda\in X^{*}(T)
is

-generic
if

m<|\langle\lambda,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle+pk|

for all

(or equivalently,

2.1.1.
Parabolic shapes

Let
M\subset G
be a Levi subgroup and
M^{\vee}\subset G^{\vee}
be the dual Levi subgroup. We write
W_{M}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{\tiny{def}}}}{{=}}W(M,T)
and
\widetilde{W}_{M}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{\tiny{def}}}}{{=}}X^{*}(T)\rtimes W_{M}
. Note that
W_{M}
is naturally a subgroup of
W
. Let
\Delta_{M}
be the set of simple roots for
(M,B\cap M,T)
. We define

\displaystyle W^{M}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{\tiny{def}}}}{{=}}\{w\in W\mid l(s_{\alpha}w)>l(w)\ \forall\alpha\in\Delta_{M}\}.

Note that for any
w\in W
, there exists a unique decomposition
w=w_{M}w^{M}
where
w_{M}\in W_{M}
and
w^{M}\in W^{M}
.

Let
\lambda\in X_{*}(T^{\vee})
. We can view it as a cocharacter of
T^{\vee}\subset M^{\vee}
and define
\mathrm{Adm}_{M}^{\vee}(\lambda)
as the admissible set for
M
and
\lambda
. Since the natural map
\widetilde{W}^{\vee}_{M}\hookrightarrow\widetilde{W}^{\vee}
preserves the Bruhat order, it induces a map
\mathrm{Adm}_{M}^{\vee}(\lambda)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Adm}^{\vee}(\lambda)
and
\mathrm{Adm}_{M}(\lambda)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Adm}(\lambda)
.

Lemma 2.1.6
.

Let
\widetilde{z}\in\widetilde{W}_{M}
and
r\in\widetilde{W}_{M}
be an affine reflection. If
l(\widetilde{z})<l(r\widetilde{z})
, then for any
w^{M}\in W^{M}
,
l((w^{M})^{-1}\widetilde{z}w^{M})<l((w^{M})^{-1}r\widetilde{z}w^{M})
.

Lemma 2.1.7

We explain how to relate coherent cohomology to étale cohomology. We keep the setup of the previous subsection. Let

\displaystyle H^{3}_{\et}(\Sh_{U,\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}},\mathcal{F}_{W(\lambda)}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})_{\mathfrak{m}}\simeq\oplus_{w\in W^{M}}H^{l(w)}(\Sh^{\mathrm{tor}}_{U,\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}},\omega(w\cdot\lambda+(3,3;0)))_{\mathfrak{m}}.

Here,

is the Levi factor of the Siegel parabolic subgroup (generated by

and

Note that the quotient of

faithfully acting on

are all isomorphic. This follows from the fact that once we localize at

and

and

[

and

(resp. of

and

-cohomology. As a result, we obtain the following comparison for Hecke algebras.

Corollary 4.1.7
.

Let
\overline{r}:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\mathrm{GSp}_{4}(\mathbb{F})
be a continuous homomorphism satisfying Taylor–Wiles conditions. Then, there is a natural isomorphism

\displaystyle\mathbb{T}^{P}_{\chi}(U,W(\lambda))_{\mathfrak{m}_{\overline{r},\chi}^{P}}\simeq\mathbb{T}^{P,\mathrm{coh}}_{\chi}(U,\omega(\lambda+(3,3;0)))_{\mathfrak{m}_{\overline{r},\chi}^{P}}.

This follows from the previous paragraph as both Hecke algebras are the faithful quotient of the localization

acting on either

.

Although it is not clear how to construct a patching functor in the coherent setting, the coherent patched modules are (contravariant) functorial in the following sense. If there is a family of morphisms

\displaystyle H^{0}(\Sh^{\mathrm{tor}}_{U_{p}U^{p},\mathbb{F}},\omega(\lambda+(3,3;0)))_{\chi,\zeta_{R},\mathfrak{m}^{S}_{\overline{r},\chi}}\rightarrow H^{0}(\Sh^{\mathrm{tor}}_{U_{p}U^{p},\mathbb{F}},\omega(\lambda^{\prime}+(3,3;0)))_{\chi,\zeta_{R},\mathfrak{m}^{S}_{\overline{r},\chi}}

for all

that are compatible with level-raising inclusions, then this induces a map between patched modules

\displaystyle M_{\infty}^{\mathrm{coh}}(W(\lambda^{\prime}))/\varpi\rightarrow M_{\infty}^{\mathrm{coh}}(W(\lambda))/\varpi.

In particular, the surjection
W(\lambda_{1})/\varpi\twoheadrightarrow W(\lambda_{0})/\varpi
in Lemma
2.2.1
(resp. the injective map
\theta^{1}_{\lambda}
in Theorem
4.1.5
) induces an injection (resp. a surjection) between corresponding patched modules.

Proof.

For a given
\lambda

, we choose

•

M_{r,0}^{\psi_{p}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{\tiny{def}}}}{{=}}H^{0}(\Sh_{U_{p}\cdot U^{p},\mathcal{O}/\varpi^{r}},\omega(\lambda+(3,3;0)))_{\chi,\zeta_{R},\mathfrak{m}_{\overline{r},\chi}^{S}}^{\vee}

•

for

, we take

\displaystyle M_{r,n}^{\psi_{p}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{\tiny{def}}}}{{=}}H^{0}(\Sh_{U_{p}U_{\Delta}^{p}(Q_{n}),\mathcal{O}/\varpi^{r}},\omega(\lambda+(3,3;0)))_{\chi,\zeta_{R},\mathfrak{m}_{\overline{r},\chi}^{S\cup Q_{n}},\mathfrak{m}_{Q_{n}}}^{\vee}\otimes_{R_{\mathcal{S}_{Q_{n}}}}R_{n}^{\psi_{p}}

\displaystyle\alpha_{n}^{\psi_{p}}

\displaystyle:M_{r,n}^{\psi_{p}}/\mathfrak{a}_{\infty}\simeq M_{r,0}^{\psi_{p}}.

Then

is a free

4.5.
Modularity lifting

In this subsection, we set
F=\mathbb{Q}
. We prove the following modularity lifting result for small Hodge–Tate weights and generic tame inertial type.

Theorem 4.5.1
.

Suppose that
\overline{r}
is potentially diagonalizably automorphic, satisfies Taylor–Wiles conditions, and
\overline{r}|_{G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}
is tame and 9-generic. Let
\lambda\in X^{*}(T)
be a dominant character. If
r:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\mathrm{GSp}_{4}(E)
is a lift of
\overline{r}
that is unramified at all but finitely many places and potentially crystalline of type
(\lambda+\eta,\tau)
at
p
with
(2h_{\lambda}+6)
-generic tame inertial type
\tau
, then
r
is automorphic.

Let
{\overline{\rho}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{\tiny{def}}}}{{=}}\overline{r}|_{G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}
. By the standard argument, we need to show that the patched module
M_{\infty}(\sigma^{\circ}(\lambda,\tau))
is fully supported on
R_{\infty}^{\lambda+\eta,\tau,{\psi_{p}}}
. As discussed in Remark
4.2.3
, any irreducible component in
\mathrm{Spec}\ R_{\infty}^{\lambda+\eta,\tau,{\psi_{p}}}
is of the form
\mathcal{C}^{p}\times\mathcal{C}_{p}
for some irreducible components
\mathcal{C}^{p}\subset\mathrm{Spec}\ R^{p}
and
\mathcal{C}_{p}\subset\mathrm{Spec}\ R^{\lambda+\eta,\tau,{\psi_{p}}}_{{\overline{\rho}}}
. We first prove the full support on
\mathrm{Spec}\ R^{p}
.

Lemma 4.5.2
.

For a 1-generic tame inertial type
\tau
, there exists a reduced closed subscheme
X^{\lambda,\tau}\subset\mathrm{Spec}\ R_{{\overline{\rho}}}^{\lambda+\eta,\tau}
that is a union of irreducible components and
\mathrm{Supp}_{R_{\infty}^{\lambda+\eta,\tau}}M_{\infty}(\sigma^{\circ}(\lambda,\tau))=\mathrm{Spec}\ R^{p}\times X^{\lambda,\tau}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\mathcal{C}_{0}^{p}\times\mathcal{C}_{p}\subset\mathrm{Supp}_{R_{\infty}^{\lambda+\eta,\tau}}M_{\infty}(\sigma^{\circ}(\lambda,\tau))
for some irreducible components
\mathcal{C}_{0}^{p}\subset\mathrm{Spec}\ R^{p}
and
\mathcal{C}_{p}\subset\mathrm{Spec}\ R_{{\overline{\rho}}}^{\lambda+\eta,\tau}
. Then, we claim that for any irreducible component
\mathcal{C}^{p}\subset\mathrm{Spec}\ R^{p}
,
\mathcal{C}^{p}\times\mathcal{C}_{p}\subset\mathrm{Supp}_{R_{\infty}^{\lambda+\eta,\tau}}M_{\infty}(\sigma^{\circ}(\lambda,\tau))
. By the lifting result of Patrikis–Tang
[
PT22
, Theorem 3.4]
, there is a geometric lift
r
of
\overline{r}
that is unramified outside
S
and
r|_{G_{\mathbb{Q}_{v}}}
for all
v\in S
defines a closed point
x
in
\mathcal{C}^{p}\times\mathcal{C}_{p}
. By the base change and Ihara avoidance argument (cf.
[
Lee
, Theorem 6.3.1]
) and the assumption on
\mathcal{C}_{p}
,
r
is automorphic. By
[
BCGP21
, Theorem 2.7.1(4) and Lemma 7.1.3]
, the point
x
corresponding to
r|_{G_{\mathbb{Q}_{v}}}
for all
v\in S\backslash\{p\}
is a smooth point in
\mathcal{C}^{p}
. Moreover,
\mathrm{Spec}\ R^{\eta,\tau}_{{\overline{\rho}}}[1/p]
is the disjoint union of its irreducible component by
[
BG19
, Theorem 3.3.8]
. In particular,
\mathcal{C}^{p}\times\mathcal{C}_{p}
is the unique irreducible component containing
x
. This proves our claim.
∎

Using the previous Lemma, we can factorize the cycles attached to the patched modules.

Lemma 4.5.3
.

There exists an effective cycle
Z^{p}\in\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{Spec}\ R^{p}]
, with specialization
\overline{Z}^{p}\in\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{Spec}\ R^{p}/\varpi]
, whose support is
\mathrm{Spec}\ R^{p}
satisfying the following properties:

(1)

For a 1-generic tame inertial type
\tau
, there exists a cycle
Z^{\lambda,\tau}\in\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{Spec}\ R_{{\overline{\rho}}}^{\lambda+\eta,\tau}]
such that
Z(M_{\infty}(\sigma^{\circ}(\lambda,\tau)))=4Z^{p}\times Z^{\lambda,\tau}
. Moreover, we can and do choose
Z^{p}
and
Z^{\lambda,\tau}
such that the multiplicity of irreducible components in
Z^{\lambda,\tau}
is either zero or one.

(2)

For a Serre weight
\sigma
, there exists a cycle
Z_{\sigma}\in\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{Spec}\ R_{{\overline{\rho}}}^{\mathrm{alg}}]
such that
Z(M_{\infty}(\sigma))=4\overline{Z}^{p}\times Z_{\sigma}
.

Proof.

We prove the existence of
Z^{p}
satisfying the first property. The second one follows from the first and by expressing
\sigma
as a linear combination of
\overline{\sigma}(\tau)
in the Grothendieck group (
[
Ser77
, Theorem 33]
). Note that
M_{\infty}(\sigma)=0
unless
\sigma
is 6-generic.

The claim is equivalent to the following: for
Z(M_{\infty}(\sigma^{\circ}(\lambda,\tau)))=\sum_{\mathcal{C}}\mu(\mathcal{C})[\mathcal{C}]
where the sum runs over all irreducible component
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}^{p}\times\mathcal{C}_{p}
in
\mathrm{Spec}\ R_{\infty}^{\lambda+\eta,\tau}
, we can write
\mu(\mathcal{C})=4\mu(\mathcal{C}^{p})\mu(\mathcal{C}_{p})
where
\mu(\mathcal{C}^{p})
and
\mu(\mathcal{C}_{p})
depend only on
\mathcal{C}^{p}
and
\mathcal{C}_{p}
and the latter is either zero or one. Therefore, we can take
Z^{p}=\sum_{\mathcal{C}^{p}}\mu(\mathcal{C}^{p})[\mathcal{C}^{p}]
and
Z^{\lambda,\tau}=\sum_{\mathcal{C}_{p}}\mu(\mathcal{C}_{p})[\mathcal{C}_{p}]
. Note that the cycle
Z^{p}
is fully supported on
\mathrm{Spec}\ R^{p}
by Lemma
4.5.2
.

The multiplicity
\mu(\mathcal{C})
is given by the length of the module
M_{\infty}(\sigma^{\circ}(\lambda,\tau))
localized at the prime ideal of
R_{\infty}^{\lambda+\eta,\tau}
corresponding to
\mathcal{C}
. Since
M_{\infty}(\sigma^{\circ}(\lambda,\tau))[1/p]
is a maximal Cohen–Macaulay module over
R_{\infty}^{\lambda+\eta,\tau}[1/p]
, it is a projective module once restricted to the smooth locus. In turn,
\mu(\mathcal{C})
can be computed by the dimension of the fiber of
M_{\infty}(\sigma^{\circ}(\lambda,\tau))[1/p]
at a smooth point in
\mathcal{C}[1/p]
. As in the proof of Lemma
4.5.2
, we can choose a smooth closed point in
\mathcal{C}[1/p]
given by a geometric lift
r
of
\overline{r}
unramified outside
S
. In this case, the fiber of
M_{\infty}(\sigma^{\circ}(\lambda,\tau))[1/p]
can be identified with the Hecke eigenspace inside the étale cohomology
H^{3}_{\et}(\Sh_{U,\overline{\mathbb{Q}}},\mathcal{F}_{\sigma^{\circ}(\lambda,\tau)^{\vee}})[1/p][\mathfrak{m}_{r}]
where the Hecke eigensystem
\mathfrak{m}_{r}\subset\mathbb{T}^{S,\univ}[1/p]
is determined by
r
. Following
[
Wei05
, §1]
, we have

\displaystyle H^{3}_{\et}(\Sh_{U,\overline{\mathbb{Q}}},\mathcal{F}_{\sigma^{\circ}(\lambda,\tau)})[1/p][\mathfrak{m}_{r}]\otimes_{E}\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}=\oplus_{\pi^{\infty}}W_{\pi^{\infty}}\otimes\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{GSp}_{4}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})}(\sigma(\tau),\pi_{p})\otimes(\pi^{\infty,p})^{U^{p}}[\mathfrak{m}_{r}]

where the sum runs over cuspidal automorphic representations
\pi
of
\mathrm{GSp}_{4}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})
whose attached Galois representation is isomorphic to
r
(viewed as
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
-vector spaces via
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}\simeq\mathbb{C}
) and
W_{\pi^{\infty}}
is a certain multiplicity space. By Arthur’s multiplicity formula
[
BCGP21
, Theorem 2.9.3]
, such
\pi
are exactly of the form
\pi=\otimes^{\prime}\pi_{v}
where
\pi_{v}
is in the
L
-packet for
r|_{G_{\mathbb{Q}_{v}}}
. Moreover,
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{GSp}_{4}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})}(\sigma(\tau),\pi_{p})
is non-zero for a unique element in the
L
-packet of
r|_{G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}
(see
[
Lee
, §2.4]
). This verifies that we can factorize
\mu(\mathcal{C})
into local multiplicities. Then we take
\mu(\mathcal{C}_{p})=\dim_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{GSp}_{4}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})}(\sigma(\tau),\pi_{p})
, which is either one or zero by the multiplicity one result for tame types (cf.
[
Lee
, Theorem 2.4.1]
), and
\mu(\mathcal{C}^{p})=\sum_{\pi^{\infty,p}}\dim_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}(\pi^{\infty,p})^{U_{p}}
where the sum runs over the product of local
L
-packets away from
\infty
and
p
. The constant
4
comes from
\dim_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}W_{\pi^{\infty}}=4
(see
[
Wei05
, pg. 82]
;
m^{+}(\Pi_{f})
and
m^{-}(\Pi_{f})
in
loc. cit.
equal one by Arthur’s multiplicity formula). ∎

It remains to prove that
\overline{Z}^{\lambda,\tau}
is equal to the cycle
\mathcal{Z}^{\lambda,\tau}({\overline{\rho}})
. By the proof of Lemma
4.5.3
, we have
\overline{Z}^{\lambda,\tau}\leq\mathcal{Z}^{\lambda,\tau}({\overline{\rho}})
. Moreover, by the exactness of
M_{\infty}
, we have
\overline{Z}^{\lambda,\tau}=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathrm{JH}(\overline{\sigma}(\lambda,\tau))}n_{\sigma}(\lambda,\tau)Z_{\sigma}
. By Corollary
3.5.3
it suffices to show that
Z_{\sigma}=\mathcal{Z}_{\sigma}^{\mathrm{BM}}({\overline{\rho}})
. For
i\in\{0,1,2\}
, let
\lambda_{i}\in C_{i}
be a character such that
\lambda_{0}\uparrow\lambda_{1}\uparrow\lambda_{2}
. When
\sigma\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{\tiny{def}}}}{{=}}F(\lambda_{2})
is 3-deep, recall that
\mathcal{Z}_{\sigma}^{\mathrm{BM}}=\mathcal{C}_{\sigma}+\mathcal{C}_{F(\lambda_{0})}
. Since we have
Z_{\sigma}\geq\mathcal{C}_{\sigma}({\overline{\rho}})
by Theorem

, we only need to prove the following.

Proposition 4.5.4
.

Following the above notation,
Z_{\sigma}\geq\mathcal{C}_{F(\lambda_{0})}({\overline{\rho}})
.

Before proving the above proposition, we provide a preliminary result.

Lemma 4.5.5
.

Following the above notation,
Z_{F(\lambda_{i})}=\mathcal{C}_{F(\lambda_{i})}({\overline{\rho}})
for

.

Proof.

Let
\widetilde{w}_{1}\in\Omega
be the element such that
(\widetilde{w}_{1},\widetilde{w}_{1})\in\AP^{\prime}(\eta)
corresponds to
F(\lambda_{0})
under the bijection in Lemma
2.2.4
(2) and
\widetilde{w}_{2}
be the element such that
\widetilde{w}_{1}\uparrow\widetilde{w}_{2}
and
\widetilde{w}_{2}\cdot C_{0}=C_{1}
. Then
F_{{\overline{\rho}}}(\widetilde{w}_{1},\widetilde{w}_{2})=F(\lambda_{1})
. We have a tame inertial type
\tau

as in §

F(\lambda_{i})
for

is contained in

by Lemma
2.2.4
. By Theorem

,

Proof of Proposition
4.5.4
.

If
F(\lambda_{0})\not\in W^{?}({\overline{\rho}}|_{I_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}})
, then
\mathcal{C}_{F(\lambda_{0})}({\overline{\rho}})=\emptyset
and there is nothing to prove. Suppose that
F(\lambda_{0})\in W^{?}({\overline{\rho}}|_{I_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}})
. Note that this implies
F(\lambda_{i})\in W^{?}({\overline{\rho}}|_{I_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}})
for
i\in\{1,2\}
.

Let
\lambda_{i}^{\prime}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{\tiny{def}}}}{{=}}-w_{0}(\lambda_{i})
. A direct computation shows that
\lambda_{i}^{\prime}\in C_{i}
and
\lambda_{0}^{\prime}\uparrow\lambda_{1}^{\prime}\uparrow\lambda_{2}^{\prime}
. Note that
F(\lambda_{i}^{\prime})^{\vee}=F(\lambda_{i})
.

We need to show that the support of
M_{\infty}(\sigma)
contains
\mathcal{C}_{F(\lambda_{0})}({\overline{\rho}})
. We first claim that the support of
M_{\infty}(W(\lambda_{2}^{\prime})^{\vee})
contains
\mathcal{C}_{F(\lambda_{0})}
. By Lemma

, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Supp}_{R_{{\overline{\rho}}}^{\square}}M_{\infty}(W(\lambda_{2}^{\prime})^{\vee})/\varpi=\mathrm{Supp}_{R_{{\overline{\rho}}}^{\square}}M_{\infty}(F(\lambda_{1}))\cup\mathrm{Supp}_{R_{{\overline{\rho}}}^{\square}}M_{\infty}(F(\lambda_{2})).

Since
\mathrm{Supp}_{R_{{\overline{\rho}}}^{\square}}M_{\infty}(F(\lambda_{1}))
does not contain
\mathcal{C}_{F(\lambda_{0})}
by Lemma
4.5.5
, the claim implies that
M_{\infty}(\sigma)
is supported on
\mathcal{C}_{F(\lambda_{0})}({\overline{\rho}})
.

By Corollary
4.3.4
, the claim is equivalent to the support of
M_{\infty}^{\mathrm{coh}}(W(\lambda_{2}^{\prime}))
containing
\mathcal{C}_{F(\lambda_{0})}
. As in Remark
4.3.3
, the injective morphism
\theta_{\lambda_{1}}^{1}
in Theorem
4.1.5
induces a surjective map

\displaystyle\theta_{\lambda_{1},\infty}^{1}:M_{\infty}^{\mathrm{coh}}(W(\lambda_{2}^{\prime}))/\varpi\twoheadrightarrow M_{\infty}^{\mathrm{coh}}(W(\lambda_{1}^{\prime}))/\varpi.

to

\mathcal{C}_{F(\lambda_{0})}({\overline{\rho}})
. By the previous two morphisms between patched modules, this shows that
M_{\infty}^{\mathrm{coh}}(W(\lambda_{i}^{\prime}))

is supported on
\mathcal{C}_{F(\lambda_{0})}({\overline{\rho}})
.
∎

Proof of Theorem

By Proposition

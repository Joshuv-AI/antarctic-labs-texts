Integrative transcriptomic analysis reveals tumor-adjacent mucosal remodeling in colorectal cancer
Authors: Sha Wu, Long Wu, Ruoshi Li, Xin Luo, Xueni Zhao, Liangrui Shi, Wei Liu, Lishan Ding, Zhaotong Cong, Zhuohong Li, Chuan Zheng, Chi Song
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractTumor-adjacent mucosa is commonly used as a normal reference in colorectal cancer (CRC) transcriptomic studies, but its relationship with distant normal mucosa and tumor tissue remains unclear. Here, we performed bulk RNA sequencing of paired distant normal, adjacent, and tumor tissues from 25 patients with CRC and used these three tissue states to construct an ordered normal–adjacent–tumor (N-A-T) expression continuum. Although adjacent mucosa was globally closer to distant normal mucosa and showed no significant differential expression by pairwise analysis, a limma-based linear trend model identified widespread monotonic transcriptional changes along the N-A-T continuum. Tumor-directed genes were enriched in translation, RNA processing, cell-cycle activity, and proliferation-related pathways, whereas genes decreasing along this axis were associated with oxidative metabolism and epithelial homeostasis. A trend gene-based N-A-T score positioned adjacent mucosa between normal and tumor tissues in external bulk cohorts and was associated with proliferative and immune-low molecular states in CRC. Single-cell RNA-seq analysis localized this signal mainly to epithelial cells and associated higher scores with proliferative and stem-like epithelial states. In conclusion, CRC-adjacent mucosa is not fully equivalent to distant normal mucosa but carries coordinated tumor-directed transcriptional changes mainly associated with epithelial-cell programs.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1007/s10142-026-02066-w.Keywords: Colorectal cancer, Tumor-adjacent mucosa, Transcriptomic remodeling, Expression continuum, Field effect, Epithelial cells, Single-cell RNA sequencing

Introduction

Colorectal cancer (CRC) is a major cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide and is accompanied by extensive transcriptomic remodeling during tumorigenesis (Wang et al. 2025; Tieng et al. 2020). RNA-seq studies have substantially improved our understanding of CRC heterogeneity through molecular subtyping, pathway analysis, and prognostic modeling (Chen et al. 2025; Hu et al. 2024; Urh et al. 2022). Recent studies of signaling and non-coding RNA regulatory axes have further illustrated the molecular complexity of CRC, reinforcing the need for careful interpretation of transcriptomic changes across tumor and non-tumor tissues (Ruan et al. 2024; Ge et al. 2024; Tian et al. 2024; Xu et al. 2024). However, most transcriptomic studies have focused primarily on tumor tissues, whereas the molecular characteristics of reference normal tissues have received comparatively less attention (Kim et al. 2023; Roelands et al. 2023). This issue is particularly relevant in epithelial-derived tumors such as CRC, where tumor-adjacent mucosa is frequently used as a normal comparator despite uncertainty regarding its equivalence to distant normal mucosa (Thomas 2022; Heidari et al. 2017).

Adjacent mucosa is commonly selected as a normal control because it is readily available during surgical resection (Pan et al. 2021). However, histologically normal tissue near tumors may be influenced by the local tumor microenvironment, inflammatory responses, metabolic adaptation, and epithelial molecular alterations, leading to tumor-associated field effects (Zhang et al. 2022a, b; Chen et al. 2021). If adjacent mucosa differs molecularly from distant normal mucosa, its use as a reference tissue may affect the interpretation of tumor-associated gene expression changes. At the same time, such differences may provide useful information about early transcriptional alterations in tissue surrounding the tumor (Pandey et al. 1995; Chaubal et al. 2024).

The molecular relationship among distant normal mucosa, adjacent mucosa, and tumor tissue in CRC remains incompletely characterized (Luo et al. 2020). Conventional pairwise differential expression analysis is useful for identifying differences between discrete tissue groups but may miss directional expression changes across ordered tissue states (Chen et al. 2024). In addition, few studies have analyzed distant normal mucosa, adjacent mucosa, and tumor tissue within a strictly paired design. Therefore, it remains unclear whether adjacent mucosa is transcriptionally equivalent to distant normal mucosa or whether it carries coordinated expression changes related to tumor tissue (Long et al. 2023; Chiang et al. 2022). In this context, the proposed normal–adjacent–tumor (N-A-T) continuum does not aim to redefine CRC field cancerization, but to provide an ordered, patient-matched framework for evaluating how adjacent mucosa is positioned relative to both distant normal mucosa and tumor tissue. Unlike conventional pairwise comparisons, this approach models the three tissue states as a directional continuum and allows weak but coordinated tumor-directed expression changes to be summarized by a compact N-A-T score.

To address this question, we performed paired bulk RNA sequencing of distant normal mucosa, adjacent mucosa, and tumor tissue from 25 patients with CRC. Using these three tissue states, we constructed an ordered N-A-T expression continuum and applied a limma-based linear trend model to identify directional expression changes. We further developed an N-A-T score based on trend genes and evaluated its reproducibility in independent bulk transcriptomic cohorts and its cellular distribution using single-cell RNA-seq data. This integrative analysis characterizes tumor-adjacent mucosal remodeling in CRC and provides a transcriptomic basis for interpreting adjacent mucosa as a reference tissue.

Materials and methods

Patient cohort and sample collection

This study was conducted in accordance with relevant ethical regulations and was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Affiliated Hospital of Chengdu University of Traditional Chinese Medicine (approval number: 2025KL-099-02). Written informed consent was obtained from all participants for the use of biological specimens and associated clinical information.

Clinical specimens were collected from 25 patients with pathologically confirmed colorectal cancer (CRC) at the Affiliated Hospital of Chengdu University of Traditional Chinese Medicine. The cohort included 13 males and 12 females, aged 33 to 85 years. Available clinical annotations included age, gender, anatomical site, T stage, and N stage. For each patient, three tissue specimens were obtained: tumor tissue, adjacent mucosa located 3–5 cm from the tumor margin, and distant normal mucosa located at least 10 cm from the tumor margin (Guan et al. 2025). All samples were reviewed and confirmed by two experienced pathologists before downstream analysis.

RNA extraction, library preparation, and bulk RNA sequencing

Total RNA was extracted from tissue samples using RNAiso Plus (Takara, Tokyo, Japan) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. RNA quality was assessed before library preparation. Sequencing libraries were prepared using the MGIEasy Circularization Kit, and single-stranded DNA libraries were generated following the manufacturer’s protocol. Libraries were sequenced on the DNBSEQ-T7 platform (BGI, Shenzhen, China).

Raw sequencing reads were subjected to standard quality control to remove low-quality reads and technical artifacts. The resulting clean reads were used for transcript abundance estimation and downstream transcriptomic analyses.

RNA-seq data processing and gene-level quantification

Transcript abundance was quantified using Kallisto (v0.50.1). The Kallisto index was constructed using GENCODE v48 gene annotation and the human reference genome GRCh38.p14. Transcript-level abundance estimates were summarized to gene-level counts using tximport (v1.30.0). Gene-level count matrices were used for differential expression analysis, trend modeling, and downstream integrative analyses. Quality-control metrics for all 75 short-read libraries were summarized from Kallisto run_info files and the tximport gene-level count matrix, including processed-read counts, pseudoalignment rates, and detected-gene counts (Table S1).

Differential expression analysis

Differential expression analysis was performed using DESeq2 (v1.42.0) based on gene-level estimated counts summarized by tximport. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were defined as genes with an adjusted P < 0.05 and |log2 fold change| > 1. Normalized expression matrices were used for principal component analysis (PCA) and other expression-based visualizations.

Limma-based trend analysis and construction of the N-A-T score

To assess directional gene expression changes across the three ordered tissue states, distant normal mucosa, adjacent mucosa, and tumor tissue were encoded as an ordinal variable. This ordered tissue-state framework is referred to here as the N-A-T expression continuum. For each gene, a limma-based linear model was fitted using limma (v3.62.2) (Ritchie et al. 2015), with patient identity included to account for the paired study design and inter-individual variability.

Genes showing significant linear trends across the ordered tissue states were further filtered according to the monotonicity of group-level mean expression. Genes with progressively increasing mean expression from distant normal mucosa to adjacent mucosa and tumor tissue were classified as increasing trend genes, whereas genes with progressively decreasing mean expression were classified as decreasing trend genes. Trend genes were defined using FDR < 0.10 as an exploratory discovery threshold for directional expression changes across the ordered tissue states. To reduce the inclusion of unstable patterns, this criterion was combined with a monotonic group-level mean expression requirement and a patient-paired linear model.

To construct the primary trend gene-based N-A-T score, increasing and decreasing trend genes were ranked separately according to the strength of the limma trend statistic. The 15 top-ranked increasing genes and 15 top-ranked decreasing genes were selected as the up-trend and down-trend gene sets, respectively. Gene expression values were z-score normalized within each dataset. For each sample, the N-A-T score was calculated as the mean normalized expression of the up-trend gene set minus that of the down-trend gene set. Signature-size sensitivity was assessed by recalculating the score using the top K increasing and top K decreasing genes, with K = 5, 10, 15, 20, 30, and 50 genes per direction. Each K-specific score was compared with the primary top15 score, and preservation of the normal–adjacent–tumor median ordering was evaluated (Fig. S6).

Functional enrichment analysis

Functional enrichment analysis was performed to characterize biological processes associated with genes showing directional expression changes along the N-A-T expression continuum. Increasing and decreasing trend genes were analyzed separately by over-representation analysis (ORA) using clusterProfiler (v4.1.4). Enrichment was assessed for Gene Ontology (GO), Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG), and Reactome pathway terms. Multiple testing correction was performed using the Benjamini–Hochberg method (Benjamini and Hochberg 1995).

Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) was also performed using ranked whole-transcriptome data. Genes included in the trend analysis were ranked according to the strength and direction of their linear trend along the N-A-T expression continuum. Hallmark, GO, and Reactome gene sets were obtained from msigdbr (v26.1.0), whereas KEGG gene sets were obtained through clusterProfiler. Pre-ranked GSEA was performed using fgsea (v1.32.4), within the established GSEA framework (Subramanian et al. 2005), with gene set size thresholds set to 10–500 genes. Gene sets containing fewer than 10 genes were excluded to reduce unstable enrichment estimates driven by very small gene sets, whereas gene sets containing more than 500 genes were excluded to avoid overly broad functional categories dominating the enrichment results. This filtering was used to balance statistical stability and biological interpretability. Enrichment results were represented by normalized enrichment scores (NES). Positive NES values indicated enrichment toward the tumor-directed end of the continuum, whereas negative NES values indicated enrichment toward the distant normal end. An FDR < 0.25 was considered exploratory evidence of enrichment in the pre-ranked GSEA analysis. Enrichment results were visualized using ggplot2 (v4.0.2).

External validation in public bulk transcriptomic cohorts

To evaluate the reproducibility of the N-A-T score, external validation was performed using TCGA colorectal cancer data, GTEx normal colorectal tissues, and the independent GEO dataset GSE292858. TCGA and GTEx expression matrices were obtained from the UCSC Xena Toil recompute resource, and corresponding TCGA clinical annotations were obtained from the UCSC Xena Pan-Cancer Atlas resource. GSE292858 was downloaded from the Gene Expression Omnibus and processed independently.

Because residual technical and biological differences may remain across data sources, the primary validation was performed separately within each cohort rather than by pooling TCGA, GTEx, and GEO samples into a single cross-platform analysis. Within each dataset, gene identifiers were harmonized to the discovery N-A-T signature, duplicated identifiers were collapsed by retaining the row with the highest mean expression, and missing signature genes were recorded. Expression values were log-transformed where necessary, and mapped signature genes were z-score normalized within each cohort before calculation of the N-A-T score.

Association of the N-A-T score with molecular and microenvironmental features in CRC

To investigate the biological relevance of the N-A-T score in established CRC, associations between the score and pathway activity, tumor microenvironment features, driver gene alterations, and microsatellite instability (MSI) status were evaluated in TCGA-CRC.

Single-sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA) was used to estimate Hallmark pathway activity for each tumor sample. Spearman correlation analysis was then performed between pathway scores and the N-A-T score. Tumor microenvironment-related metrics, including ImmuneScore, StromalScore, ESTIMATEScore, and tumor purity, were estimated using the ESTIMATE algorithm and correlated with the N-A-T score.

Molecular annotation data from TCGA were used to assess associations between the N-A-T score and common CRC driver gene alterations, including APC, TP53, KRAS, PIK3CA, SMAD4, and BRAF. Associations with MSI subtype, including MSI-H, MSI-L, and MSS tumors, were also evaluated.

Single-cell RNA-seq analysis

To assess the cellular distribution of the N-A-T transcriptional signal, an independent CRC single-cell RNA-seq dataset, GSE132465, was analyzed. The expression matrix was processed using Seurat (v5.4.0), including quality control, normalization, dimensionality reduction, clustering, and UMAP visualization.

Major cell populations were assigned based on original annotations and canonical marker genes, including epithelial cells, myeloid cells, T cells, B cells, and stromal cells. The N-A-T score was calculated at the single-cell level using the bulk-derived trend gene signature. Score distributions were compared across major cell types to identify the cellular compartment most closely associated with the bulk N-A-T signal. Module score analysis was additionally performed as a robustness assessment.

Subsequent analyses focused on epithelial cells. N-A-T scores were compared between normal-derived and tumor-derived epithelial cells. Scores were also aggregated at the sample level to assess whether differences between normal-derived and tumor-derived epithelial cells remained consistent across samples.

Trajectory inference was performed using Slingshot (v2.10.0) to characterize continuous epithelial-cell state changes. UMAP embeddings and epithelial-cell cluster assignments were used as input to reconstruct lineage trajectories in low-dimensional space. Mainline pseudotime values were extracted and used as an axis of epithelial-cell state variation. Differences in pseudotime distributions between normal-derived and tumor-derived epithelial cells were assessed, and Spearman correlation analysis was performed to evaluate the association between pseudotime and the N-A-T score.

To interpret epithelial-cell state changes along pseudotime, expression patterns of representative marker genes were examined, including epithelial markers (EPCAM, KRT8, KRT18, KRT19) (Zhang et al. 2023b), differentiation-associated genes (FABP1, CA1) (Wood et al. 2017), proliferation-associated genes (MKI67, TOP2A, BIRC5) (Bernhardt et al. 2021), stemness-associated genes (LGR5, OLFM4, ASCL2) (Niec et al. 2022), and tumor-related genes (CEACAM5, CEACAM6).

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed in R (v4.4.3). Normality was assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test and visual inspection of quantile–quantile plots. Because most score distributions did not satisfy normality assumptions and sample sizes were modest, non-parametric tests were used. Comparisons between two independent groups were performed using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test unless otherwise specified, paired samples were evaluated using paired Wilcoxon tests where appropriate, and comparisons among more than two groups were performed using the Kruskal–Wallis test. Correlations were evaluated using Spearman’s rank correlation. Exploratory covariate analyses of the N-A-T score using available clinical variables were performed with univariable tests and an ordered tissue-state model. All P values were two-sided, and the Benjamini–Hochberg method was used for multiple-testing correction where applicable.

Results

Transcriptomic positioning of adjacent mucosa along the N-A-T expression continuum

A total of 25 patients with CRC were included in this study. For each patient, tumor tissue, adjacent mucosa, and distant normal mucosa were collected, yielding 75 samples for bulk transcriptome sequencing (Fig. 1A). Principal component analysis (PCA) showed clear separation of tumor tissues from the two non-tumor tissue groups, whereas adjacent mucosa largely overlapped with distant normal mucosa (Fig. 1B).

Paired distant normal mucosa, adjacent mucosa, and tumor tissues were collected from 25 patients with colorectal cancer (CRC) for bulk RNA sequencing and downstream transcriptomic analyses. External bulk transcriptomic cohorts and a single-cell RNA-seq dataset were used for validation. (B) Principal component analysis (PCA) of transcriptomic profiles from distant normal mucosa, adjacent mucosa, and tumor tissues. (E) Expression patterns of representative monotonic genes across distant normal mucosa, adjacent mucosa, and tumor tissues. Pairwise differential expression analysis showed that, compared with distant normal mucosa, tumor tissues contained 3,781 differentially expressed genes (DEGs), including 1,386 upregulated and 2,395 downregulated genes. Compared with adjacent mucosa, tumor tissues contained 3,951 DEGs, including 1,402 upregulated and 2,549 downregulated genes. In contrast, no significant DEGs were detected between adjacent mucosa and distant normal mucosa (Fig. 1C).

To identify directional expression changes that were not captured by pairwise comparisons, we fitted a limma-based linear trend model across the ordered normal–adjacent–tumor (N-A-T) expression continuum while accounting for patient pairing. Genes with monotonic group-level mean expression patterns were then retained. This analysis identified 2,695 genes with increasing trends and 2,582 genes with decreasing trends along the N-A-T continuum (Fig. 1D).

Representative trend genes showed expression levels in adjacent mucosa that were generally intermediate between distant normal mucosa and tumor tissue (Fig. 1E). Consistently, the trend gene-based N-A-T score positioned adjacent mucosa between distant normal mucosa and tumor tissue (Fig. 1F). These results indicate that adjacent mucosa remains globally similar to distant normal mucosa but carries coordinated tumor-directed expression changes.

Because the primary N-A-T score was constructed using a fixed number of trend genes, we next assessed whether the observed tissue-state pattern was sensitive to signature size. Scores recalculated using the top K increasing and top K decreasing trend genes, with K = 5, 10, 15, 20, 30, and 50 genes per direction, were highly concordant with the N-A-T score (Spearman’s ρ range, 0.9735–1.0000) and consistently preserved the Normal < Adjacent < Tumor median ordering (Fig. S6).

These findings support the stability of the aggregate N-A-T score pattern across a range of signature sizes. Because clinical and sampling-related factors may influence adjacent-mucosal transcription, we next performed an exploratory covariate-sensitivity analysis using available discovery-cohort variables. Age, gender, anatomical site, T stage, and N stage were not significantly associated with the N-A-T score after Benjamini–Hochberg correction in univariable analyses. In the complete-case linear model including tissue order and these covariates, tissue order remained strongly associated with the score (β = 1.7972, SE = 0.1407, P = 5.19*10⁻¹⁹; Fig. S7).

Functional programs associated with the N-A-T expression continuum

To characterize biological processes associated with directional expression changes, we performed over-representation analysis (ORA) separately for increasing and decreasing trend genes and conducted gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) using ranked whole-transcriptome data.

ORA showed that genes increasing along the N-A-T continuum were mainly enriched in protein synthesis- and ribosome-related processes, including ribonucleoprotein complex biogenesis, ribosome biogenesis, cytoplasmic translation, structural constituent of ribosome, and rRNA processing. In contrast, genes decreasing along the continuum were mainly enriched in membrane transport and mitochondrial energy metabolism, including transporter complex, transmembrane transporter activity, regulation of membrane potential, oxidative phosphorylation, aerobic respiration, and respiratory electron transport (Fig. 2A–B).

Enriched biological functions were mainly associated with translation, ribosome biogenesis, and RNA processing. (B) ORA of genes showing monotonic decreases along the N-A-T expression continuum. Enriched functions were mainly related to membrane transport, oxidative phosphorylation, and metabolic homeostasis. (C) Hallmark gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) performed using ranked trend genes along the N-A-T expression continuum. Pathways positively enriched toward the tumor-directed direction included MYC targets, E2F targets, and G2M checkpoint, whereas oxidative phosphorylation and metabolic pathways were enriched toward the normal-directed direction. (D) Representative Reactome pathways enriched along the N-A-T expression continuum. Pathways related to RNA processing, translation, and the cell cycle showed progressively stronger enrichment toward the tumor-directed end of the continuum. GSEA gene set sizes were restricted to 10–500 genes, and FDR < 0.25 was used as an exploratory enrichment threshold

Hallmark GSEA showed a similar pattern. Pathways enriched toward the tumor-directed end of the continuum included MYC targets, E2F targets, G2M checkpoint, unfolded protein response, and mTORC1 signaling, whereas oxidative phosphorylation, fatty acid metabolism, adipogenesis, and xenobiotic metabolism were enriched toward the distant normal end (Fig. 2C).

Reactome GSEA further showed that tumor-directed enrichment was mainly related to RNA processing, RNA metabolism, RNA splicing, pre-mRNA processing, tRNA processing, and eukaryotic translation initiation and elongation (Fig. 2D). Consistent patterns were also observed in GO and KEGG GSEA, with RNA processing, ribosome biogenesis, and translation-related functions enriched toward the tumor-directed end, whereas oxidative phosphorylation, respiratory electron transport, fatty acid degradation, the tricarboxylic acid cycle, and selected ion transport functions were enriched toward the distant normal end (Fig. S1).

Together, these analyses indicate that expression changes along the N-A-T continuum are organized into coordinated biological programs. Translation, RNA processing, cell growth, and cell-cycle activity increased toward tumor tissue, whereas oxidative metabolism, lipid metabolism, membrane transport, and epithelial homeostatic functions were more prominent toward distant normal mucosa.

External validation of the N-A-T score in independent bulk transcriptomic cohorts

Given the sample size of the discovery cohort, we evaluated the reproducibility of the N-A-T score in public bulk transcriptomic datasets. TCGA, GTEx, and GSE292858 provided independent resources for comparing colorectal tumor tissues, adjacent mucosa, and normal colorectal tissues. These datasets were used to assess whether the N-A-T score could consistently capture the relative position of samples along the expression continuum.

In the combined GTEx and TCGA-CRC analysis, N-A-T scores differed among normal, adjacent, and tumor samples (P < 0.001; Fig. 3A). Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis showed that the score distinguished tumor tissues from non-tumor tissues with high performance (AUC = 0.997; Fig. 3B). To reduce the influence of cross-cohort differences, we further analyzed TCGA samples separately. TCGA tumor tissues showed significantly higher N-A-T scores than adjacent tissues (P < 0.001; Fig. 3C). Among 32 matched tumor-adjacent tissue pairs, tumor tissues generally had higher scores than their paired adjacent tissues (P < 0.001; Fig. 3D).

(B) Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis evaluating the performance of the N-A-T score in distinguishing tumor tissues from non-tumor tissues. (C) Comparison of N-A-T scores between tumor tissues and adjacent mucosa in the TCGA colorectal cancer cohort. (D) Paired comparison of N-A-T scores between matched tumor and adjacent tissue samples in TCGA. In the independent GSE292858 cohort, tumor tissues also showed higher N-A-T scores than non-tumor tissues, and this pattern remained consistent in paired samples (P < 0.001; Fig. 3E). The score distinguished tumor from non-tumor tissues in this cohort with an AUC of 0.966 (Fig. 3F). Supplementary analyses showed variation among different non-tumor tissue sources, whereas the association between the N-A-T score and TCGA tumor stage was limited (Fig. S2). These results suggest that the N-A-T score captures reproducible CRC-associated expression patterns across independent bulk transcriptomic cohorts.

Molecular features associated with the N-A-T score in established CRC

After observing that adjacent mucosa was positioned between distant normal mucosa and tumor tissue in the discovery cohort, we examined molecular features associated with the N-A-T score in established CRC using TCGA data.

Pathway correlation analysis based on single-sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA) showed that the N-A-T score was positively associated with proliferation-related Hallmark pathways, particularly E2F targets and G2M checkpoint. In contrast, the score was negatively associated with several immune- and inflammation-related pathways, including IL6/JAK/STAT3 signaling, complement, allograft rejection, inflammatory response, interferon-gamma response, TNFA signaling via NF-κB, and interferon-alpha response. TGF-β signaling, epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT), and angiogenesis also showed weaker negative associations (Fig. 4A–B). These results indicate that higher N-A-T scores were associated with stronger proliferation-related signals and weaker immune-inflammatory and stromal features.

(A) Correlations between the N-A-T score and Hallmark pathway activities estimated by single-sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA) in TCGA colorectal cancer samples. (B) Representative correlations between the N-A-T score and selected Hallmark pathways, including proliferation-related and immune-related programs. (C) Correlations between the N-A-T score and ESTIMATE-derived tumor microenvironment metrics, including ImmuneScore, StromalScore, ESTIMATEScore, and tumor purity. (D) Representative associations between the N-A-T score and ESTIMATE metrics. Higher N-A-T scores were associated with lower immune and stromal activity and higher tumor purity. (E) Distribution of N-A-T scores across microsatellite instability (MSI) subtypes, including MSS, MSI-L, and MSI-H tumors

We next used ESTIMATE to evaluate tumor microenvironment-related characteristics. The N-A-T score was negatively correlated with ImmuneScore, StromalScore, and ESTIMATEScore, with the strongest negative association observed for ImmuneScore. Conversely, the score was positively correlated with tumor purity (Fig. 4C–D). This pattern was consistent with the ssGSEA results and suggests that higher N-A-T scores are associated with lower immune and stromal signals in bulk tumor transcriptomes.

We further examined the relationship between the N-A-T score and established molecular features of CRC. Tumors harboring SMAD4 or BRAF mutations showed relatively lower N-A-T scores (Fig. S3A–B), whereas no clear association was observed with KRAS, PIK3CA, APC, or TP53 mutation status (Fig. S3C–F). MSI subtype showed a more consistent relationship with the N-A-T score: among TCGA tumors with MSI annotation, MSI-H tumors had significantly lower N-A-T scores than MSI-L and MSS tumors, whereas no significant difference was observed between MSI-L and MSS tumors (Fig. 4E). This finding is consistent with the negative association between the N-A-T score and immune-inflammatory features, given the immune-active background of MSI-H tumors. Overall, in established CRC, the N-A-T score was associated with enhanced proliferative activity, reduced immune-inflammatory activity, lower stromal content, and MSI-related molecular states. These findings indicate that the N-A-T score captures a biologically informative axis of CRC transcriptomic variation, although the observed relationships remain associative.

To further examine its potential clinical relevance, we explored associations between the N-A-T score and available outcome endpoints in TCGA COAD/READ. No significant association was observed with OS, DSS, PFI, or DFI in either Kaplan–Meier or continuous Cox regression analyses (Fig. S8). These results do not support the use of the N-A-T score as a prognostic marker and instead reinforce its interpretation as an exploratory transcriptomic tissue-state index.

Single-cell analysis localizes the N-A-T signal mainly to epithelial cells

To examine the cellular distribution of the N-A-T transcriptional signal, we applied the bulk-derived N-A-T signature to the independent CRC single-cell RNA-seq dataset GSE132465. Major cell populations, including epithelial cells, myeloid cells, T cells, B cells, stromal cells, and mast cells, were identified from the single-cell atlas (Fig. 5A). Comparison across cell types showed that N-A-T scores were highest in epithelial cells, followed by stromal cells, whereas myeloid cells, T cells, B cells, and mast cells showed lower scores overall (Fig. 5B). This pattern suggests that the bulk N-A-T signal is mainly associated with the epithelial compartment.

(C) Comparison of epithelial-cell N-A-T scores between normal-derived and tumor-derived epithelial cells at the sample level. (D) UMAP visualization of epithelial cells colored according to tissue origin. (E) UMAP visualization of epithelial cells colored according to the N-A-T score. Higher scores were mainly enriched in tumor-associated epithelial regions. (F) Slingshot trajectory inference showing epithelial-cell pseudotime ordering in low-dimensional space. (G) Comparison of epithelial-cell pseudotime distributions between normal-derived and tumor-derived epithelial cells. (H) Correlation between epithelial-cell pseudotime and the N-A-T score

After restricting the analysis to epithelial cells, tumor-derived epithelial cells showed higher N-A-T scores than normal-derived epithelial cells, and this difference remained evident at the sample-aggregated level (Fig. 5C). In low-dimensional space, normal-derived and tumor-derived epithelial cells partially overlapped but showed distinct distributions (Fig. 5D). Higher N-A-T scores were mainly concentrated in regions enriched for tumor-derived epithelial cells (Fig. 5E).

Trajectory inference further organized epithelial cells along a continuous pseudotime backbone in low-dimensional space (Fig. 5F). Tumor-derived epithelial cells showed overall higher mainline pseudotime values than normal-derived epithelial cells (Fig. 5G). Mainline pseudotime was positively correlated with the N-A-T score (Spearman rho = 0.366, P < 0.001; Fig. 5H), indicating that the two measures captured related directions of epithelial-cell state variation.

When epithelial cells were grouped into pseudotime bins, differentiation-related gene modules decreased overall, whereas proliferation, stemness, and tumor-associated gene modules increased (Fig. S4A). Representative marker genes showed similar trends: FABP1 decreased along pseudotime, while MKI67, OLFM4, and CEACAM6 increased toward later pseudotime states (Fig. S4B, Fig. S5). These single-cell analyses were consistent with the bulk transcriptomic findings and suggest that the N-A-T signal is mainly associated with epithelial-cell transcriptional programs characterized by lower differentiation-related expression and higher proliferative and stemness-related expression. These observations remain transcriptome-based associations and require further experimental validation.

Discussion

CRC progression is accompanied by changes in epithelial identity, metabolism, and tumor-associated transcriptional programs (Zhang et al. 2023a; Sammarco et al. 2020). Adjacent mucosa is commonly used as a normal reference in CRC transcriptomic studies, but increasing evidence suggests that histologically normal tissues near tumors may already show molecular alterations related to field effects (Dampier et al. 2020; Kim et al. 2023; Roelands et al. 2023). In our cohort, adjacent mucosa was globally closer to distant normal mucosa than to tumor tissue, but it showed coordinated changes in tumor-associated transcriptional programs involving RNA processing, translation, proliferation, metabolic homeostasis, and epithelial function. These findings indicate that adjacent mucosa is not fully equivalent to distant normal mucosa at the transcriptomic level.

Previous studies have reported varying degrees of molecular alteration in CRC-adjacent mucosa. Transcriptomic analyses have identified oncogenic or recurrence-informative features in histologically normal or tumor-adjacent colorectal tissues, supporting the presence of field-associated molecular changes (Dampier et al. 2020; Kim et al. 2023). Other transcriptomic, single-cell, and spatially resolved studies have shown that the apparent extent and cellular origin of adjacent-tissue alterations may vary according to sampling distance, anatomical region, tissue composition, cell type, platform, and analytical design (Roelands et al. 2023; Guan et al. 2025; de Oliveira et al. 2025; Raude et al. 2026; Avraham-Davidi et al. 2025). Our findings are consistent with this heterogeneous literature. Adjacent mucosa did not exhibit an overt tumor-like expression profile in pairwise comparisons, but the paired ordinal model detected coordinated tumor-directed transcriptional differences across the ordered tissue states. Thus, the present results do not imply that adjacent mucosa represents a uniform premalignant state. Rather, they support the view that histologically non-tumor tissues near CRC may carry spatially and contextually variable molecular alterations that are not fully captured by conventional pairwise analyses.

The genes changing along the N-A-T continuum were closely related to known CRC-associated processes, including ribosome biogenesis, RNA processing, proliferative signaling, and metabolic reprogramming (Miller et al. 2021; Yang et al. 2022). These patterns are consistent with coordinated differences between epithelial homeostatic programs and tumor-associated cellular programs across the ordered tissue states. The N-A-T score summarized these directional changes and was associated with stromal activity, immune-related features, MSI status, and proliferative signals in external cohorts. Signature-size sensitivity analyses further supported the stability of the aggregate score pattern. However, exploratory analyses of TCGA outcome data did not identify significant associations with OS, DSS, PFI, or DFI. The score should therefore be interpreted as a transcriptomic tissue-state index rather than as an established prognostic marker, and its potential clinical relevance requires evaluation in prospective cohorts with standardized tissue sampling and longitudinal follow-up.

Single-cell analysis further suggested that the N-A-T signal was mainly associated with epithelial cells. Higher N-A-T scores were observed in epithelial-cell states characterized by stronger proliferative and stemness-related programs and weaker differentiation-related programs. This finding is consistent with recent single-cell studies showing that epithelial-cell plasticity contributes to CRC heterogeneity (Bohin et al. 2020; Petti et al. 2020; Gan et al. 2023). Together, the bulk and single-cell analyses suggest that the N-A-T continuum mainly reflects epithelial transcriptional programs, although contributions from tissue composition and microenvironmental changes cannot be fully excluded.

Several limitations should be noted. First, this study was based on cross-sectional transcriptomic data and therefore cannot establish temporal or causal relationships between adjacent mucosa and tumor tissue. Single-cell pseudotime reflects transcriptional state ordering rather than direct lineage progression. Although the paired design improves within-patient control, the discovery cohort of 25 patients may limit statistical power and the stability of individual trend genes. Second, adjacent-mucosal transcription may be influenced by sampling-related, anatomical, inflammatory, microbial, and clinical factors. Available covariates did not explain the N-A-T score pattern, but inflammation scores, exact sampling distance, microbiota profiles, and treatment-response data were unavailable. Finally, experimental validation using qPCR, immunohistochemistry, spatial transcriptomics, or functional approaches was not available. Future studies with larger paired cohorts and orthogonal validation are required to further define the biological and translational relevance of the N-A-T framework.

Overall, this study reveals a reproducible transcriptomic continuum linking distant normal mucosa, adjacent mucosa, and tumor tissue in CRC. Adjacent mucosa retains many features of distant normal mucosa but also carries coordinated tumor-associated expression changes, mainly involving epithelial-cell programs. These findings highlight tumor-adjacent mucosal remodeling and emphasize the importance of reference tissue selection in CRC transcriptomic studies.

Conclusion

In summary, tumor-adjacent mucosa in CRC is not fully equivalent to distant normal mucosa at the transcriptomic level. A paired ordered model revealed coordinated tumor-directed expression changes across normal–adjacent–tumor tissue states, and this signal was reproducible in external bulk cohorts and mainly associated with epithelial-cell programs. These findings support the interpretation of tumor-adjacent mucosa as a transcriptomically altered reference tissue in CRC studies, while causal and mechanistic conclusions require further experimental validation.

Abstract
. We add analytic components to algebraic cycles with modulus and construct an arithmetic Chow group with modulus that resembles the classical arithmetic Chow groups by Gillet and Soulé. The analytic component is dictated by imposing a vanishing condition on the cohomology class of a cycle with modulus. We establish several natural properties of this group as a consequence.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Motivation

Let
k
be a subfield of
\mathds{C}
. Given a smooth quasi-projective variety
X
over
k
, its Chow ring
\bigoplus_{p\geq 0}\CH^{p}(X)
gives a nice notion of geometrically defined cohomology theory with many desirable properties. The chief among them is the (rational) isomorphism

(1.1)

K_{0}(X)\otimes\mathds{Q}\cong\bigoplus_{p\geq 0}\CH^{p}(X)\otimes\mathds{Q}

with the Grothendieck group of coherent locally-free sheaves, given by the Chern character map. Recall that the first Chern class gives an isomorphism
c_{1}\colon{\rm Pic}(X)\cong\CH^{1}(X)
between the group of isomorphism classes of line bundles and the divisor class group on
X
, which is the initial motivation for isomorphism (
1.1
). For a more general singular variety
X
, (
1.1
) does not hold. Also, several well-known properties associated with smooth projective varieties are no longer true if one drops the projectiveness condition, for example, the existence of a degree map and the existence of the Albanese variety of
X
.
One way to resolve this is to take a smooth compactification
X\subset\overline{X}
such that
D=\overline{X}\setminus X
is supported on an effective Caritier divisor, and to consider cycles with certain ramification conditions along the boundary
D
.
These observations led to the quest for a geometrically defined cohomology theory that is sensitive to a divisor of a general smooth quasi-projective variety
X

and also restores (
1.1
) in a suitable setting.

Chow groups with modulus were defined in part to address this quest. The very first notion in this direction was given by Bloch and Esnault in
[
BE03
]
, and over the years significant development was made in
[
KL08
]
,
[
Park09
]
,
[
KP12
]
,
[
KP17
]
,
[
KS16
]
,
[
BS19
]
,
[
GK22
]
, et al. Following
[
BS19
, §3]
, for a smooth quasi-projective variety
X
over
k
and an effective divisor
D
, one defines the Chow groups with modulus at
D
heuristically as follows: Let
\mathcal{Z}^{p}(X|D)
be the free abelian group generated by reduced and irreducible closed subvarieties
Z
of
X
that do not meet
D
. Any such
Z
will be called a (codimension
p
) cycle with modulus. Furthermore, let
\mathcal{R}^{p}(X|D)
be the subgroup defined by divisors of rational functions on subvarieties of codimension
p-1
, which are regular, in fact, a unit in a neighborhood of
D
, and are congruent to one modulo the ideal sheaf of
D
(see Definition
4.1
for details). We define the Chow group with modulus on
D
as the quotient

(1.2)

\CH^{p}(X|D)\coloneqq\mathcal{Z}^{p}(X|D)/\mathcal{R}^{p}(X|D).

Although it is not clear whether the relative version of the isomorphism (
1.1
) is true, it is known that the first Chern class map induces an isomorphism
\CH^{1}(X|D)\cong{\rm Pic}(X,D)
, where
{\rm Pic}(X,D)
is the relative Picard group that consists of isomorphic classes of line bundles (that are trivial along
D
) with isomorphisms to
\mathcal{O}_{D}
on
D
. For
X
proper, one can define a degree map on zero cycles with modulus (
[
KP12
, Proposition 2.10]
) and for
X
projective, one can define an Albanese with modulus (
[
BK18
, Theorems 10.3 and 11.3]
).

On the other end of the spectrum, arithmetic Chow groups were developed by Gillet and Soulé in
[
GS90
]
to give a refined intersection theory for arithmetic varieties. An arithmetic variety
X
is a scheme that is flat and of finite type over
\Spec(\mathds{Z})
, with smooth generic fibre
X_{\mathds{Q}}
. If
X
is regular, the notion of a geometric intersection theory was first established by Gillet and Soulé, using
K
-theory. However, without a notion of the completeness of such schemes, the idea of a degree associated with such an intersection theory was not readily available. For example, if
X
is a proper arithmetic variety, then one can hope to define a degree with values in
\CH^{1}(\Spec(\mathds{Z}))
. But this group is trivial, and would not be the right choice. The solution proposed by Gillet and Soulé was to compactify such arithmetic varieties by adding the fibre at infinity related to the embedding
\mathds{Q}\hookrightarrow\mathds{C}
. So, one can view a compactified arithmetic variety as a family over each prime, including the prime at infinity. Given a compactified arithmetic variety
X
defined over
\Spec(\mathds{Z})
, one defines the arithmetic Chow group
\widehat{\CH}^{p}(X)
of codimension
p
as the free abelian group generated by tuples
(Z,g_{Z})
modulo a suitable arithmetic rational equivalence, where
Z\in\mathcal{Z}^{p}(X)
is a cycle of codimension
p
, and
g_{Z}
is an analytic object, called the Green current for the cycle
Z
, defined on the analytic space
X(\mathds{C})
associated with
X
. With this definition, one can show that
\widehat{\CH}^{1}(\Spec(\mathds{Z}))\cong\mathds{R}
and hence define an arithmetic degree morphism
\widehat{\text{deg}}
with values in
\mathds{R}
. These arithmetic Chow groups come equipped with several desirable properties, namely flat pullback, proper pushforward, and, using
\widehat{\text{deg}}
, a refined notion of an arithmetic intersection degree that has been used in questions related to arithmetic geometry. For example, Faltings defined the height of a closed irreducible subset of
\mathds{P}^{n}_{\mathds{Z}}
using this intersection degree, which was crucial in the proof of the Mordell conjecture.

The current article originated from the following natural question asked by the second author during a talk given by the first author: Assume that
X
is a smooth and quasi-projective variety over a number field
k
, and
D
is an effective divisor such that
D_{red}
is a simple normal crossing divisor (sncd for short). Under these assumptions, how can one define
\widehat{\CH}^{p}(X|D)
, an arithmetic Chow group with modulus? In this paper, we propose an answer to the above question by taking into account, for a cycle with modulus, the behaviour of its analytic fundamental class at
D_{red}(\mathds{C})
. We show that these groups have natural functorial properties with respect to appropriate morphisms, are modules over the usual arithmetic Chow groups, fit into a natural exact sequence, and the group of arithmetic divisors with modulus is isomorphic to the Hermitian relative Picard group. In synopsis, this provides a very natural amalgamation of Gillet and Soulé’s theory with the theory of Chow group with modulus.

The results in this article can be viewed as a first step towards defining a concrete realization of arithmetic motivic cohomology with modulus, and giving an arithmetic analogue of the higher-dimensional global class-field theory. While this will be a long-term project, one of our short-term aims is to define an arithmetic analogue of the relative Grothendieck group
K_{0}(X,D)
, and study its connection with
\widehat{\CH}^{p}(X|D)
. This will be the subject of our next paper.

1.2.
Results

Let, as before,
X
denote a smooth quasi-projective variety of dimension
d
, defined over a number field
k
. For ease of writing, we do not distinguish between
X
and its corresponding analytic space
X(\mathds{C})
. Let
\mathcal{Z}^{p}(X)
be the free abelian group generated by reduced and irreducible subvarieties of codimension
p
that compute
\CH^{p}(X)
. The definition of arithmetic Chow groups involves the following basic principle: Typically, the cycle class map

\cl_{p}\colon\mathcal{Z}^{p}(X)\rightarrow H^{2p}(X)

can be represented by the fundamental class
\delta_{Z}
of an algebraic cycle in homology, as well as a smooth form
\omega_{Z}
of type
(p,p)
in cohomology. Since they represent the same cycle class, the difference between the fundamental class and the current
[\omega_{Z}]
associated to
\omega_{Z}
(see Lemma
2.1
for the definition) satisfies the differential equation

\partial\overline{\partial}g_{Z}=[\omega_{Z}]-\delta_{Z}.

The current
g_{Z}
is called a Green current associated with the algebraic cycle
Z
. It can be chosen to be of type
(p-1,p-1)
and satisfying
F^{\ast}_{\infty}g_{Z}=(-1)^{p-1}g_{Z}
for the conjugate linear involution
F_{\infty}
induced by the complex conjugation on
X(\mathds{C})
. The arithmetic Chow groups
\widehat{\CH}^{p}(X)
are now defined by taking pairs
(Z,g_{Z})
of algebraic cycles
Z
and an associated Green current
g_{Z}
, and finally quotienting out by a suitable arithmetic rational equivalence. We note here that if
X
is not projective, one has to invoke the notion of Green forms of logarithmic type along
|Z|
to get a suitable Green current.

Now, suppose that
D
is an effective divisor on
X
whose support
E
is an sncd. Following the above principle, if one wants to find a suitable theory of arithmetic Chow group with modulus, it needs a description of a cycle class map to a suitable cohomology group that is sensitive to the modulus
D
. Such a map was first introduced in
[
BS19
, §7,§8]
in a general context of higher Chow groups with modulus, and can be described in our setting as follows: As mentioned before, any
Z\in\mathcal{Z}^{p}(X|D)
is an ordinary algebraic cycle in
\mathcal{Z}^{p}(X)
. The classical cycle class map can be seen as a composition

Z\mapsto H^{2p}_{|Z|}(X)\rightarrow H^{2p}(X).

Since
Z
and
D
does not meet, there is an isomorphism
H^{2p}_{|Z|}(X)\cong H^{2p}_{|Z|}(X,D)
and the above map actually lands in relative cohomology

Z\mapsto H^{2p}_{|Z|}(X)\cong H^{2p}_{|Z|}(X,D_{red})\rightarrow H^{2p}(X,D_{red}).

We call this the cycle class of
Z
with modulus and use the symbol
cl_{p|D}(Z)
to denote it.

Once we have this notion, the next task is to represent the relative cohomology groups
H^{\ast}(X,D_{red})
using a suitable complex of differential forms. Since
D_{red}
is an sncd, the hypercohomolgy associated with the sheaf of holomorphic forms
\Sigma_{D_{red}}\Omega_{X}^{*}
vanishing on
D_{red}
computes
H^{\ast}(X,D)
. Moreover, the inclusion
\Sigma_{D_{red}}\Omega^{\ast}_{X}\hookrightarrow\Sigma_{D_{red}}\mathcal{A}^{\ast}_{X}
into the sheaf of
C^{\infty}
-forms that vanish on
D_{red}
is a quasi-isomorphism (see Remark
2.6
). Since
\Sigma_{D_{red}}\mathcal{A}^{\ast}_{X}
is fine, the corresponding global section
\Sigma_{D_{red}}A^{\ast}(X)\coloneqq\Gamma(X,\Sigma_{D_{red}}\mathcal{A}^{\ast}_{X})
also computes
H^{\ast}(X,D)
. Hence, it is natural to use the complex
\Sigma_{D_{red}}A^{\ast}(X)
to develop our theory. In particular, using Lemma
2.5
, our first main result is the following:

Theorem A
.

(Theorem
5.1
)
Let
Z\in\mathcal{Z}^{p}(X|D)
. Then there exists a Green current
g_{Z}
which satisfies the differential equation
\partial\overline{\partial}g_{Z}+\delta_{Z}=[\omega_{Z}]
, such that
\omega_{Z}\in\Sigma_{D_{red}}A^{p,p}(X)
, is real, satisfies
F^{\ast}_{\infty}\omega_{Z}=(-1)^{p}\omega_{Z}
, and its cohomology class
\{\omega_{Z}\}
computes
cl_{p|D}(Z)
.

Two such Green currents always differ by a smooth form
u
which satisfies
\partial\overline{\partial}u|_{E}=0
. Let’s label the Green current of Theorem
A
by
g_{Z|D}
. Then we define
\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}^{p}(X|D)
by pairs of the form
(Z,g_{Z|D})
. One can define a subgroup
\widehat{\mathcal{R}}^{p}(X|D)
of arithmetic rational equivalence with modulus generated by
\widehat{{\rm div}(f)}\coloneqq({\rm div}(f),-[\log|f|^{2}])
for
{\rm div}(f)\in\mathcal{R}^{p}(X|D)
, and pairs
(0,\partial u+\overline{\partial}v)
for currents
u
and
v
of degrees
(p-2,p-1)
and
(p-1,p-2)
, respectively. Hence, we have our main definition

Definition A
.

We define an arithmetic Chow group with modulus

\widehat{\CH}^{p}(X|D)\coloneqq\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}^{p}(X|D)/\widehat{\mathcal{R}}^{p}(X|D).

Notice that, any current
T=\partial u+\overline{\partial}v
automatically satisfy the condition that
\partial\overline{\partial}T=0
. Hence, we do not need to impose any vanishing condition on the complex of currents.

Much like the arithmetic Chow groups of Gillet and Soulé, the modulus version also satisfies the usual functoriality properties for smooth morphisms between smooth projective varieties (see Proposition
5.4
), and fits in an exact sequence (see Section
5.2
).

The Chow groups with modulus have a module structure over the classical Chow groups, which is defined by the usual product of algebraic cycles. It satisfies all the functorial properties, including the projection formula (
[
KP17
, Theorem 3.12]
). Correspondingly, at the arithmetic level, we get

Theorem B
.

(Theorem
6.1
)
There is an action

\cap\colon\widehat{\CH}^{p}(X)\otimes\widehat{\CH}^{q}(X|D)\to\widehat{\CH}^{p+q}(X|D),

which for a smooth morphism
\phi\colon X\to Y
satisfies the projection formula.

The isomorphism
\widehat{{\rm Pic}}(X)\cong\widehat{\CH}^{1}(X)
between the group of isomorphism classes of hermitian line bundles and the arithmetic divisor class group, commuting with
{\rm Pic}(X)\cong\CH^{1}(X)
, is well-known.
On the modulus side, it is now natural to wonder about the existence of the relative version of the above isomorphism that commutes with

{\rm Pic}(X,D)\cong\CH^{1}(X|D)
. To address this question,
we define the relative hermitian Picard group
\widehat{{\rm Pic}}(X,D)
, consisting of triples
(L,\phi,||\cdot||)
such that the isomorphism class of
(L,\phi)
belongs to
{\rm Pic}(X,D)
and
||\cdot||
is a hermitian metric on
L
with the additional restriction that the smooth
(1,1)
-form representing its Chern class
c_{1}(L,||\cdot||)
, vanish on
D_{red}
(see Definition
7.1
for details). Then the following result is quite natural

Theorem C
.

(Theorem
7.6
)
There exists an isomorphism

\widehat{{\rm Pic}}(X,D)\cong\widehat{\CH}^{1}(X|D)

which commutes with the isomorphism
{\rm Pic}(X,D)\cong\CH^{1}(X|D)
.

1.3.
Layout

Here we discuss the organization of the article. Sections
2
,
3
, and

in Section

2.
Analytic component

In this section, we gather the analytic results that will be used later in this article. We begin by recalling the well-known notions of differential forms and currents.

2.1.
Differential forms and currents

Let
M
be a complex manifold of complex dimension
d
. For
n\geq 0
, we let
A^{n}(M)
denote the
\mathds{C}
-vector space of
complex-valued
C^{\infty}
-forms of degree
n
.
Recall that we have a decomposition

(2.1)

A^{n}(M)=\oplus_{p+q=n}A^{p,q}(M),

where, locally on
M
,
A^{p,q}(M)
is generated by the
n
-forms of the type
f({z},{\overline{z}})dz_{I}\wedge d\overline{z}_{J}
, with
I,J\subset\{1,\dots,d\}
such that
|I|=p
and
|J|=q
, and
f(z,\overline{z})
is a complex valued
C^{\infty}
-function on
M
.
Let
d\colon A^{n}(M)\to A^{n+1}(M)
denote the differential map of
C^{\infty}
-forms. We then have maps
\partial\colon A^{p,q}(M)\to A^{p+1,q}(M)
and

\overline{\partial}\colon A^{p,q}(M)\to A^{p,q+1}(M)
.
such that

(2.2)

\partial\circ\partial=0=\overline{\partial}\circ\overline{\partial}\textnormal{ and }d=\partial+\overline{\partial}.

Moreover,

{\rm Ker}(\overline{\partial}^{0,0})=\mathscr{O}(M)\subset A^{0}(M)
,
where
\mathscr{O}(M)
denotes the complex of holomorphic functions on
M
. More generally, an element
\phi\in A^{p,0}(M)
is holomorphic if
\overline{\partial}\phi=0
. This is the case if locally around a point
z\in M
we can write

\phi(z)=\Sigma_{|I|=p}\phi_{I}(z)dz_{I},\penalty\ \phi_{I}\in\mathscr{O}(M).

For
n\geq 0
, we let
A^{n}_{c}(M)\subset A^{n}(M)
denote the subgroup of the compactly supported complex valued
C^{\infty}
-forms. We let
A^{p,q}_{c}(M)=A^{p+q}_{c}(M)\cap A^{p,q}(M)
.
Note that an element
\omega\in A^{p,q}_{c}(M)
is locally of the type
f({z},{\overline{z}})dz_{I}\wedge d\overline{z}_{J}
, where
f({z},{\overline{z}})
is supported on a compact subset.
Conversely, if a form
\omega^{\prime}\in A^{p,q}(M)
is (locally) of the above type, then
\omega^{\prime}\in A^{p,q}_{c}(M)
.

We say a linear functional
T\colon A^{n}_{c}(M)\to\mathds{C}
is continuous in
the sense of Schwartz if for a sequence
\{\omega_{n}\}
of compactly supported complex valued
C^{\infty}
-forms that are supported on a fixed compact subset and
\omega_{n}\to 0
, we have

T(\omega_{n})\mapsto 0
, where
\omega_{n}\to 0
means that in the local expression, the
C^{\infty}
-functions

f_{n}(z,\overline{z})
along with finitely many of their derivatives tends to
0
.
The set of such linear functional forms a group that we denote by

D^{2d-n}(M):=A^{n}_{c}(M)^{*}
.
Similarly, we define
D^{p,q}(M)=A^{d-p,d-q}_{c}(M)^{*}
. Then

D^{n}(M)=\oplus_{p+q=n}D^{p,q}(M)
.
An element of
D^{n}(M)
is called a current on
M
.

Since

\partial\colon A^{d-p-1,d-q}_{c}(M)\to A^{d-p,d-q}_{c}(M)
respects continuity in
the sense of Schwartz, we have an induced map

\partial\colon D^{p,q}(M)\to D^{p+1,q}(M)

so that
(\partial T)(\omega)=(-1)^{p+q+1}T(\partial{\omega})
. Similarly, we can define

\overline{\partial}\colon D^{p,q}(M)\to D^{p,q+1}(M)
and

d=\partial+\overline{\partial}
such that the current variant of (
2.2
) holds. We use the notation
\widetilde{A}^{\ast}(X)
(resp.
\widetilde{D}^{\ast}(X)
) to denote
A^{\ast}(X)/\im(d)
(resp.
D^{\ast}(X)/\im(d)
), and
\widetilde{A}^{p,q}(X)
(resp.
\widetilde{D}^{p,q}(X)
) to denote
A^{p,q}(X)/\im(\partial)+\im(\overline{\partial})
(resp.
D^{p,q}(X)/\im(\partial)+\im(\overline{\partial})
).

Lemma 2.1
.

With notations as above, there exists a canonical injective map

(2.3)

[\penalty\ ]\colon A^{p.q}(M)\hookrightarrow D^{p,q}(M)

so that for
\omega_{1}\in A^{p,q}(M)
and
\omega_{2}\in A_{c}^{d-p,d-q}(M)
, we have

(2.4)

[\omega_{1}](\omega_{2})=\int_{M}\omega_{1}\wedge\omega_{2}.

Moreover, it respects maps
\partial
,
\overline{\partial}
and hence
d
.

Recall that
A^{n}(M)=A^{n}_{{\rm real}}(M)\otimes_{\mathds{R}}\mathds{C}
, where

A^{n}_{{\rm real}}(M)
consists of the real
C^{\infty}
-forms on
M
(considered as real manifold). We let

\tau\colon A^{n}(M)\to A^{n}(M)
denote the involution induced by the conjugation on
\mathds{C}
. Note that the map
\tau
takes compactly supported forms to themselves and hence induces an involution
\tau\colon A^{n}_{c}(M)\to A^{n}_{c}(M)
. We define
\tau\colon D^{n}(M)\to D^{n}(M)
so that for
T\in D^{n}(M)
and
\omega\in A^{n}_{c}(M)
, we have

(2.5)

\tau(T)(\omega)=\overline{T(\tau(\omega))}\in\mathds{C},

where
\overline{(\penalty\ )}
denotes the complex conjugation. Note that since
\tau\colon A^{n}_{c}(M)\to A^{n}_{c}(M)
is conjugate linear, the map
\tau(T)
is a linear map on
A^{n}_{c}(M)
that is continuous in the sense of Schwartz, i.e.,

\tau(T)\in D^{n}(M)
.
It follows from the definition that for

, we have

(2.6)

\tau(A^{p,q}(M))=A^{q,p}(M)\textnormal{ and }\tau(D^{p,q}(M))=D^{q,p}(M).

In particular, for each
p\geq 0
, the map
\tau
induces involutions on
A^{p,p}(M)
and
D^{p,p}(M)
.

Definition 2.2
.

For
p\geq 0
, the involution
\tau\colon A^{p,p}(M)\to A^{p,p}(M)
(resp.
\tau\colon D^{p,p}(M)\to D^{p,p}(M)

A^{p,p}(M)
(resp.
D^{p,p}(M)
) and its fixed points are called as real
(p,p)
-forms (resp. real

It is easy to see that the map (

-forms to real

.

(i)

(ii)

In a more specific case, let

2.2.
Extension lemma and relative forms

We now prove an extension lemma for
C^{\infty}
-differential forms.

Lemma 2.4

(Extension Lemma)
.

Let
N
be a closed complex submanifold of
M
. Then the pull-back map

A^{*}(M)\to A^{*}(N)
is surjective.

Proof.

By the definition of
A^{*}(-)
, it suffices to prove the lemma for the
C^{\infty}
-functions, i.e., for
*=0
. But this follows from
[
Lee03
, Lemma 5.34]
. For the sake of completion, we write a proof here.

Let
\omega\in A^{n}(N)
. Let
\{U_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha}
be a chart of

3.1.
Conjugate linear involution

In this subsection, we briefly recall the definition of arithmetic rings. We discuss various examples of such rings.

Definition 3.1

(

Example 3.2

Let

be an arithmetic field and let

be a smooth quasi-projective
k
-scheme of dimension
d\geq 0
. We let other notations be as in previous sections. Let
\widetilde{D}^{p,p}(X_{\mathds{R}})
to be the image of
D^{p,p}(X_{\mathds{R}})
inside
\widetilde{D}^{p,p}(X_{\infty})
.

Recall that a real current
g_{Z}\in D^{p-1,p-1}(X_{\infty})
is said to be a Green current for a codimension
p
cycle
Z
if
\delta_{Z}+dd^{c}g_{Z}=[\omega_{Z}]
such that
\omega_{Z}\in A^{p,p}(X_{\infty})
. It follows from

[
GS90
, Theorem 1.2.1]
that for a cycle
Z
, a Green current
g^{\prime}_{Z}
always exists. Taking
g_{Z}=\frac{1}{2}(g^{\prime}_{Z}+(-1)^{p-1}F_{\infty}^{*}g^{\prime}_{Z})
, we get a Green current
g_{Z}\in{D}^{p-1,p-1}(X_{\mathds{R}})
, and
[
GS90
, Lemma 1.2.4]
shows that two different Green currents for the same cycle differs by an element in
\widetilde{A}^{p-1,p-1}(X_{\mathds{R}})
.

We define the arithmetic Chow groups as follows: Let

(3.6)

\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}^{p}(X):=\{(Z,\widetilde{g}_{Z})\in\mathcal{Z}^{p}(X)\oplus\widetilde{D}^{p-1,p-1}(X_{\mathds{R}})\penalty\ |\penalty\ g_{Z}\penalty\ \text{a Green current for}\penalty\ Z\}.

For a closed subvariety
W
of codimension
p-1
and
f\in k(W)^{\times}
, we let
\widehat{{\rm div}(f)}:=({\rm div}(f),(\iota_{W})_{*}[-\log|f|^{2}])
, where
\widetilde{W}\to W
is a resolution of singularities,
\iota_{W}\colon\widetilde{W}\to X
denote the induced map of smooth
k
-schemes and
(\iota_{W})_{*}\colon D^{0,0}(\widetilde{W}_{\mathds{R}})\to D^{p-1,p-1}(X_{\mathds{R}})
is the push-forward map of currents. It follows from (
2.10
) that

\widehat{{\rm div}(f)}\in\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}^{p}(X)
. We denote the subgroup generated by all such
\widehat{{\rm div}(f)}
by
\widehat{\mathcal{R}}^{p}(X)
, and define the arithmetic Chow group of
X
as the quotient

(3.7)

\widehat{\CH}^{p}(X):=\frac{\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}^{p}(X)}{\widehat{\mathcal{R}}^{p}(X)}.

The existence of a Green current for an algebraic cycle shows that the canonical map
\widehat{\CH}^{p}(X)\rightarrow\CH^{p}(X)
, which maps
[(Z,\widetilde{g}_{Z})]
to
[Z]
, is surjective. This surjective map fits in an exact sequence, which we recall among other properties of arithmetic Chow groups (see
[
GS90
, Section 3]
for details):

•

The arithmetic Chow groups fit into an exact sequence

\CH^{p}(X,1)\rightarrow\widetilde{A}^{p-1,p-1}(X_{\mathds{R}})\rightarrow\widehat{\CH}^{p}(X)\rightarrow\CH^{p}(X)\rightarrow 0,

where
\CH^{p}(X,1)
is Bloch’s higher Chow group.

•

There is an intersection pairing

\widehat{\CH}^{p}(X)\otimes\widehat{\CH}^{q}(X)\rightarrow\widehat{\CH}^{p+q}(X),

which turns
\oplus_{p\geq 0}\widehat{\CH}^{p}(X)
into a unitary graded commutative algebra.

•

Let
f\colon X\rightarrow Y
be a smooth morphism of smooth quasi-projective
varieties. Then there exists a pullback

f^{\ast}\colon\widehat{\CH}^{p}(Y)\rightarrow\widehat{\CH}^{p}(X),

and if
f
is also proper, then there is a pushforward map

f_{\ast}\colon\widehat{\CH}^{p}(X)\rightarrow\widehat{\CH}^{p-e}(Y),

where
e=\dim(X)-\dim(Y)
.
The pullback is an algebra
homomorphism, and together with the pushforward, it satisfies the
projection formula.

•

The intersection product, the direct image, and the inverse image
are compatible with the corresponding operations on the classical
Chow groups.

In
[
GS90-2
, Section 5.2]
it was shown that the codimension one arithmetic Chow group is isomorphic to the arithmetic Picard group of Hermitian line bundles on
X
. We briefly recollect the morphisms that define this isomorphism in the following subsection.

3.3.
Picard group of Hermitian line bundles

We start this section by defining the Picard group of Hermitian line bundles.
We let
X
denote a smooth projective variety over an arithmetic field
k=(k,\Sigma,F_{\infty})
.

Let
\mathscr{L}
be a line bundle on
X
. We let
(U_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in I}
denote a cover of
X
that trivialises
\mathscr{L}
and let
g_{\alpha\beta}\in\mathscr{O}(U_{\alpha\beta})
denotes the transition function of
\mathscr{L}
, where
U_{\alpha\beta}=U_{\alpha}\cap U_{\beta}
.
Let
\mathscr{L}_{\infty}
denote the associated line bundle on
X_{\infty}
.
A smooth Hermitian metric
||\cdot||
on
\mathscr{L}_{\infty}
is given by the data

(U_{\alpha,\infty},h_{\alpha,\infty})_{\alpha\in I}:=(U_{\alpha,\sigma},h_{\alpha,\sigma})_{\sigma\in\Sigma,\alpha\in I}
, where

h_{\alpha,\sigma}\colon U_{\alpha,\sigma}(\mathds{C})\to\mathds{R}_{>0}
are smooth functions such that for all
\alpha,\beta\in I
and
\sigma\in\Sigma
, we have

(3.8)

h_{\alpha,\sigma}=|\sigma^{*}(g_{\beta\alpha})|^{2}h_{\beta,\sigma}\textnormal{ on $U_{\alpha\beta,\sigma}$ and }F_{\infty}^{*}(h_{\alpha,\infty})=h_{\alpha,\infty}.

By a Hermitian line bundle
(\mathscr{L},||\cdot||)
on
X
, we mean an algebraic line bundle
\mathscr{L}
on
X
and a smooth Hermitian metric
||\cdot||
on
\mathscr{L}_{\infty}
. In the above notations (i.e., given a cover
(U_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in I}
that trivialises
\mathscr{L}
), we write the Hermitian line bundle
(\mathscr{L},||\cdot||)
by
(U_{\alpha},g_{\alpha\beta},h_{\alpha,\sigma})
. We let
\widehat{{\rm Pic}}(X)
denote the set of isomorphic classes of Hermitian line bundles on
X
.

For Hermitian line bundles
(\mathscr{L}_{1},||\cdot||_{1})=(U_{\alpha},g^{1}_{\alpha\beta},h^{1}_{\alpha,\sigma})
and
(\mathscr{L}_{2},||\cdot||_{2})=(U_{\alpha},g^{2}_{\alpha\beta},h^{2}_{\alpha,\sigma})
on
X
, we define their product as follows.

(\mathscr{L}_{1},||\cdot||_{1})\cdot(\mathscr{L}_{2},||\cdot||_{2}):=(U_{\alpha},g^{1}_{\alpha\beta}g^{2}_{\alpha\beta},h^{1}_{\alpha,\sigma}h^{2}_{\alpha,\sigma}).

Note that we can always assume both
\mathscr{L}_{i}
have trivialization for an open cover of
X
and it is easy to check that all the above definitions do not depend on the cover.
With the above product, the set
\widehat{{\rm Pic}}(X)
forms an abelian group. Note that the identity element of
\widehat{{\rm Pic}}(X)
is given by the class of the trivial Hermitian line bundle

(\mathscr{O}_{X},|\cdot|)=(X,1,c_{1})
, where
1\in\mathscr{O}(X)
is the identity element and
c_{1}\colon X_{\infty}\to\mathds{R}_{>0}
is the constant function with value
1
and the inverse of an element

(\mathscr{L},||\cdot||)=(U_{\alpha},g_{\alpha\beta},h_{\alpha,\sigma})
is given by

-(\mathscr{L},||\cdot||):=(U_{\alpha},1/g_{\alpha\beta},1/h_{\alpha,\sigma})
.

Definition 3.8
.

Given a Hermitian line bundle
(\mathscr{L},||\cdot||)
on
X
as above, we define its cohomological Chern class

c_{1}(\mathscr{L},||\cdot||)\in{A}^{1,1}(X_{\infty})
so that

(3.9)

c_{1}(\mathscr{L},||\cdot||)|_{U_{\alpha,\infty}}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\partial\overline{\partial}\log h_{\alpha,\infty}\in A^{1,1}(U_{\alpha,\infty}).

Since the transition functions
g_{\alpha\beta}\colon U_{\alpha\beta,\infty}\to\mathds{C}
are nowhere vanishing holomorphic functions, it follows from (
2.10
) that the right-hand side in the above expression agrees on
U_{\alpha\beta}
.

We therefore have a well define element
c_{1}(\mathscr{L},||\cdot||)\in A^{1,1}(X_{\infty})
.
Since each
h_{\alpha,\sigma}
are real valued
C^{\infty}
-function and
F_{\infty}^{*}(h_{\alpha,\infty})=h_{\alpha,\infty}
, it follows that
c_{1}(\mathscr{L},||\cdot||)\in A^{1,1}(X_{\mathds{R}})
.

We now define the arithmetic Chern class of a Hermitian line bundle.
Recall that a rational section
s
of
\mathscr{L}
is given by sections
s_{\alpha}\in\mathscr{O}(U_{\alpha}\cap U)
such that
s_{\alpha}=g_{\alpha\beta}s_{\beta}
on
U\cap U_{\alpha\beta}
, where
U_{\alpha\beta}=U_{\alpha}\cap U_{\beta}
and
U\subset X
is a dense open subset. For short, we shall write
s=(U,U_{\alpha},s_{\alpha})
for such a section of the line bundle
\mathscr{L}=(U_{\alpha},g_{\alpha\beta})
. Let
s=(U,U_{\alpha},s_{\alpha})
be a rational section of the Hermitian line bundle
(\mathscr{L},||\cdot||)=(U_{\alpha},g_{\alpha\beta},h_{\alpha})
. We define its norm as

||s||^{2}:=s_{\alpha}\overline{s_{\alpha}}h_{\alpha}.

Note that the above expression does not depend on
\alpha
.
Let
V\subset U
be the dense open subset where
s
does not vanish (i.e.,
V=U\setminus\textnormal{div}(s)
). The continuous map
\log||s||^{2}\colon V_{\infty}\to\mathds{R}
then defines an element of
L^{1}_{\textnormal{loc}}(X_{\infty})
and hence defines a current
[\log||s||^{2}]\in D^{0,0}(X_{\infty})
. In fact, we have
[\log||s||^{2}]\in D^{0,0}(X_{\mathds{R}})
and

(3.10)

dd^{c}(-[\log||s||^{2}])+\delta_{{\rm div}(s)}=[c_{1}(\mathscr{L},||\cdot||)].

In other words, we have
(\Div(s),-[\log||s||^{2}])\in\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}^{1}(X)
.

Definition 3.9
.

For a Hermitian line bundle
(\mathscr{L},||\cdot||)=(U_{\alpha},g_{\alpha\beta},h_{\alpha})
on
X
, we define its arithmetic Chern class as follows:

(3.11)

\widehat{c}_{1}(\mathscr{L},||\cdot||):=(\Div(s),-[\log||s||^{2}])\in\widehat{\CH}^{1}(X).

Since two sections of a line bundle differ by a rational function, it follows that the above element does not depend on the chosen section
s
. The following proposition is well known, and we provide a local description of the maps.

Proposition 3.10
.

The assignment
(\mathscr{L},||\cdot||)\mapsto\widehat{c}_{1}(\mathscr{L},||\cdot||)
defines a group isomorphism

(3.12)

\widehat{c}_{1}\colon\widehat{{\rm Pic}}(X)\to\widehat{\CH}^{1}(X).

Proof.

We construct an explicit inverse map that we call the cycle class map.
Let
(Z,g)\in\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}^{1}(X)
be a cycle. Since
X
is smooth,
Z
is a Cartier divisor
(U_{\alpha},f_{\alpha})
, where
f_{\alpha}
is a rational function on
U_{\alpha}
such that
g_{\alpha\beta}=f_{\alpha}/f_{\beta}\in\mathscr{O}_{U_{\alpha\beta}}^{\times}
. We take the associated line bundle

\mathscr{O}_{X}(Z)=(U_{\alpha},g_{\alpha\beta}=f_{\alpha}/f_{\beta})
. We now give the Hermitian inner product on
\mathscr{O}_{X}(Z)
. For this, we note that two Green currents of a cycle differ by a smooth form (see
[
GS90
, Lem. 1.2.4]
). By (
3.10
), we have

(3.13)

i/2\pi\partial\overline{\partial}([-\log|\sigma^{*}(f_{\alpha})|^{2}])=-\delta_{Z\cap U_{\alpha,\sigma}}=i/2\pi\partial\overline{\partial}(g)|_{U_{\alpha,\sigma}}\in D^{1,1}(U_{\alpha,\sigma}).

In particular, there exist
h_{\alpha,\sigma}^{\prime}\in A^{0,0}(U_{\alpha,\sigma})
such that
[h_{\alpha,\sigma}^{\prime}]=(g)|_{U_{\alpha,\sigma}}+[\log|\sigma^{*}(f_{\alpha})|^{2}]
.
We let
h_{\alpha,\sigma}={{\rm exp}}^{-h_{\alpha,\sigma}^{\prime}}\colon U_{\alpha,\sigma}\to\mathds{R}_{>0}
. Then
h_{\alpha,\sigma}
are smooth real functions that satisfy

(3.14)

|g_{\beta\alpha}|^{2}h_{\beta,\sigma}=h_{\alpha,\sigma}\textnormal{ and }F_{\infty}^{*}(h_{\alpha,\infty})=h_{\alpha,\infty},

where
h_{\alpha,\infty}\colon U_{\alpha,\infty}\to\mathds{R}_{>0}
is the induced smooth function.
We therefore have a Hermitian line bundle

(3.15)

(\mathscr{O}_{X}(Z),||\cdot||_{g}):=(U_{\alpha},g_{\alpha\beta}=f_{\alpha}/f_{\beta},h_{\alpha,\sigma}={{\rm exp}}^{-(g)|_{U_{\alpha,\sigma}}-[\log|\sigma^{*}(f_{\alpha})|^{2}]}).

The assignment
(Z,g))\mapsto(\mathscr{O}_{X}(Z),||\cdot||_{g})
gives
well-defined group homomorphism

(3.16)

{\rm cyc}_{X}\colon\widehat{\CH}^{1}(X)\to\widehat{{\rm Pic}}(X).

By unfolding the definitions, we see that

\widehat{c}_{1}\circ{\rm cyc}_{X}={\rm Id}
and
{\rm cyc}_{X}\circ\widehat{c}_{1}={\rm Id}
.
This implies the arithmetic Chern class map
(
3.12
) is an isomorphism.
∎

4.
Chow groups with modulus

In this section, we recall the definition and basic properties of the Chow group with modulus that was defined in
[
BS19
, §3]
(the name relative Chow group was used in loc.cit.).

We fix the following notations: Let
k
be a field and let
X
be a smooth quasi-projective scheme over
k
of pure dimension
d\geq 0
. We let
D\hookrightarrow X
be an
effective Cartier divisor on
X
. We call the pair
(X,D)
to be a modulus pair.

We let
\mathcal{Z}^{p}(X|D)
denote the free abelian group generated on the set of codimension
p
irreducible closed subsets
Y

of
X
, such that
Y\cap D=\emptyset
.
For a codimension
(p-1)
irreducible closed subset
W
such that
W\not\subset D
, we let
\nu:W^{N}\to X
denote the projection from the normalization of

W
and let
E=\nu^{*}(D)
denote the pull-back of
D
to
W^{N}
. We let
G(W^{N},E)
denote the set of non-zero rational functions on
W^{N}
that are regular (actually a unit) in a neighborhood of
E
and it is one along
E
. More precisely:

Definition 4.1
.

We define
G(W^{N},E)
as follows.

\displaystyle G(W^{N},E)\coloneqq\varinjlim_{E\subset V\subset W^{N}}\Gamma(V,{\rm Ker}(\mathscr{O}_{V}^{\times}\rightarrow\mathscr{O}_{E}^{\times}))\subset k(W^{N})^{\times}

\displaystyle=\{f\in k(W^{N})^{\times}\colon f\in\ker(\mathcal{O}^{\times}_{W^{N},y}\to(\mathcal{O}_{W^{N},y}/I_{E,y}\mathcal{O}_{W^{N},y})^{\times})\penalty\ \text{for any}\penalty\ y\in E\},

where
I_{E,y}
is the ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{W^{N},y}
defining
E
at
y
.

We note that if
f\in G(W^{N},E)
, then
{\rm div}_{W^{N}}(f)\in\mathcal{Z}^{1}(W^{N}|E)
. If
w
is the generic point of
W
, we let

\partial_{w}:G(W^{N},E)\to\mathcal{Z}^{p}(X|D)

denote the composition of the maps

(4.1)

G(W^{N},E)\xrightarrow{{\rm div_{W^{N}}}}\mathcal{Z}^{1}(W^{N}|E)\xrightarrow{\nu_{*}}\mathcal{Z}^{p}(X|D),

where the second arrow is the push-forward map along the finite morphism

W^{N}\setminus E\to U:=X\setminus D
.
We let
\mathcal{R}^{p}(X|D)
denote the image of the induced map

\oplus_{w\in U^{(p-1)}}G(W^{N},E)\xrightarrow{\partial_{w}}\mathcal{Z}^{p}(X|D),

where
W=\overline{\{w\}}\subset X
.

Definition 4.2
.

We define the Chow group
\CH^{p}(X|D)
of codimension
p
cycles with modulus as the quotient

(4.2)

\CH^{p}(X|D):=\frac{\mathcal{Z}^{p}(X|D)}{\mathcal{R}^{p}(X|D)}.

For
p\geq 0
, we define the Chow group of dimension
p
cycles with modulus
\CH_{p}(X|D)
of
the pair
(X,D)
as

(4.3)

\CH_{p}(X|D):=\CH^{d-p}(X|D),

where
d
is the pure-dimension of
X
.

Remark 4.3
.

It follows from the above definition that these groups agree with Fulton’s Chow groups
\CH^{p}(X)
when
D=\emptyset
, i.e.,

\CH^{p}(X|\emptyset)=\CH^{p}(X)
. Following Bloch’s higher Chow groups, a general theory of the higher Chow groups with modulus
\CH^{p}(X|D,m)
was introduced in
[
BS19
]
. They also showed that the groups
\CH^{p}(X|D,0)
agree with the above groups
\CH^{p}(X|D)
(see Theorem 3.3 of loc.cit.).

We now list the functoriality properties of the Chow groups with modulus with proper references. First we define

Definition 4.4
.

By a morphism
f\colon(Y,E)\to(X,D)
of modulus pairs, we mean a morphism
f\colon Y\to X
of smooth quasi-projective varieties such that
f^{*}(D)
is a divisor on
Y
and
f^{*}(D)\leq E
.

Proposition 4.5
.

Chow groups with modulus satisfy the following.

(i)

[
KP17
, Proposition 2.10]
Let
f\colon(Y,E)\to(X,D)
be a proper morphism of modulus pairs. Then for all
j\geq 0
, there is a natural push-forward map

f_{*}\colon\CH_{j}(Y|E)\to\CH_{j}(X|D)

such that
(g\circ f)_{*}=g_{*}\circ f_{*}
, where
f
and
g
are composable proper morphisms of modulus pairs.

(ii)

[
KP17
, Proposition 2.12]
Let
f\colon(Y,E)\to(X,D)
be a flat morphism of relative dimension
d\geq 0
, then there is a well defined pull-back map

f^{*}\colon\CH_{j}(X,D)\to\CH_{j+d}(Y,E)

when
E=f^{*}(D)
. Moreover,
(g\circ f)^{*}=f^{*}\circ g^{*}
, where
f
and
g
are composable flat morphisms of modulus pairs.

4.1.
Relative Picard Group and cycles with modulus of codimension
1

We continue with the above notations. In this section, we study the Chow group with modulus
\CH^{1}(X|D)
of codimension
1
-cycles.
Recall that the Chow group
\CH^{1}(X)
is isomorphic to the group
{\rm Pic}(X)
of line bundles on
X
.
We recall its relative version from

[
SV96
, Sec. 2]
. Compare the results of this section with that of Section
3.3
.

Definition 4.6
.

For a line bundle
\mathscr{L}
on
X
, by a trivialization of
\mathscr{L}
along
D
, we mean an isomorphism
\phi\colon\mathscr{L}_{|D}\xrightarrow{\cong}\mathscr{O}_{D}
that lifts to an open neighborhood of
D
, i.e., there exists an open subset
D\subset V\subset X
and an isomorphism
\phi^{\prime}\colon\mathscr{L}_{|V}\xrightarrow{\cong}\mathscr{O}_{V}
such that

\phi=\phi^{\prime}_{|D}
.
We define the relative Picard group as follows.

(4.4)

{\rm Pic}(X,D)=\{(\mathscr{L},\phi\colon\mathscr{L}_{|D}\xrightarrow{\cong}\mathscr{O}_{D})|\textnormal{ $\phi$ is a trivialization of $\mathscr{L}$ along $D$}\}.

Note that here by
(\mathscr{L},\phi)
, we mean the isomorphic class of such a pair. For
(\mathscr{L}_{1},\phi_{1}),(\mathscr{L}_{2},\phi_{2})\in{\rm Pic}(X,D)
, we define their product as follows.

(4.5)

(\mathscr{L}_{1},\phi_{1})\cdot(\mathscr{L}_{2},\phi_{2}):=(\mathscr{L}_{1}\otimes\mathscr{L}_{2},\phi_{1}\otimes\phi_{2})\in{\rm Pic}(X,D),

where
\phi_{1}\otimes\phi_{2}\colon(\mathscr{L}_{1}\otimes\mathscr{L}_{2})_{|D}\cong(\mathscr{L}_{1})_{|D}\otimes(\mathscr{L}_{2})_{|D}\xrightarrow{\cong}\mathscr{O}_{D}
is the induced isomorphism.

For a trivialization
\phi\colon\mathscr{L}_{|D}\xrightarrow{\cong}\mathscr{O}_{D}
, we let
\phi^{\vee}\colon\mathscr{O}_{D}\xrightarrow{\cong}\mathscr{L}^{-1}_{|D}
denote the isomorphism induced by tensoring
\phi
with the dual line bundle
\mathscr{L}^{-1}_{|D}
.
With the product structure (
4.5
),
{\rm Pic}(X,D)
forms a group with identity
(\mathscr{O}_{X},{\operatorname{id}}\colon(\mathscr{O}_{X})_{|D}\xrightarrow{\cong}\mathscr{O}_{D})
and
(\mathscr{L},\phi)^{-1}=(\mathscr{L}^{-1},(\phi^{\vee})^{-1})
.

Let
(\mathscr{L},\phi)\in{\rm Pic}(X,D)
and let
D\subset V_{1},V_{2}\subset X
be are two open neighborhoods of
D
with isomorphisms

\phi_{i}^{\prime}\colon\mathscr{L}_{|V_{i}}\xrightarrow{\cong}\mathscr{O}_{V_{i}}
such that

\phi_{i}=(\phi_{i}^{\prime})_{|D}
. Then
V=V_{1}\cap V_{2}
is an open neighbourhood of
D
and
s_{i}=(\phi_{i}^{\prime})_{|V}^{-1}
are rational sections of
\mathscr{L}
that are regular on
V
and
(s_{1})_{|D}=(s_{2})_{|D}
. Since two (regular) sections of a line bundle differ by a rational (resp. regular) function, there exists
f\in\mathscr{O}_{X}(V)\subset k(X)^{\times}
such that
s_{1}=fs_{2}
and
f\in G(X,D)
. It therefore follows that

(4.6)

\Div(s_{1})=\Div(s_{2})\in\CH^{1}(X|D).

In particular, the following definition makes sense.

Definition 4.7
.

Given
(\mathscr{L},\phi)\in{\rm Pic}(X,D)
, we define its Chern class with modulus as follows.

(4.7)

c_{1}(\mathscr{L},\phi):=\Div((\phi^{\prime})^{-1})\in\CH^{1}(X|D),

where
\phi^{\prime}\colon\mathscr{L}_{|V}\xrightarrow{\cong}\mathscr{O}_{V}
is an isomorphism for an open neighbourhood
V
of
D
such that

\phi=\phi^{\prime}_{|D}
.

Proposition 4.8
.

The assignment
(\mathscr{L},\phi)\mapsto c_{1}(\mathscr{L},\phi)
defines a group isomorphism

(4.8)

c_{1}\colon{\rm Pic}(X,D)\xrightarrow{\cong}\CH^{1}(X|D).

Proof.

It follows from a easy calculation that the assignment
(\mathscr{L},\phi)\mapsto c_{1}(\mathscr{L},\phi)
defines a group homomorphism

c_{1}\colon{\rm Pic}(X,D)\to\CH^{1}(X|D)
. We define the inverse map
{\rm cyc}_{X|D}\colon\CH^{1}(X|D)\to{\rm Pic}(X,D)
that we call the cycle class map with modulus.

Let
Z\in\mathcal{Z}^{1}(X|D)
. Since
X
is a smooth variety, the Weil divisors on
X
are the same as the Cartier divisor on
X
.
Let
(U_{\alpha},f_{\alpha})
be a Cartier divisor for
Z
(i.e.,

Z\cap U_{\alpha}=\Div(f_{\alpha})
).
Let
\mathscr{O}(U_{\alpha},f_{\alpha})
be a line bundle in the isomorphic class
(U_{\alpha},g_{\alpha}=f_{\alpha}/f_{\beta})
of line bundles on
X
.
If we let
U=X\setminus|Z|
, then
f_{\alpha}\in\mathscr{O}(U\cap U_{\alpha})^{\times}
and they patch up to yield a section
s\in\Gamma(U,\mathscr{O}(U_{\alpha},f_{\alpha}))
such that
s\colon\mathscr{O}_{U}\xrightarrow{\cong}\mathscr{O}(U_{\alpha},f_{\alpha}))_{|U}
. We let
\phi=(s_{|D})^{-1}\colon\mathscr{O}(U_{\alpha},f_{\alpha}))_{|D}\xrightarrow{\cong}\mathscr{O}_{D}
be the induced trivialisation of the line bundle
\mathscr{O}(U_{\alpha},f_{\alpha})
. We therefore have an element

(\mathscr{O}(U_{\alpha},f_{\alpha}),\phi=(s_{|D})^{-1})\in{{\rm Pic}}(X,D)
. Moreover, one can show that the above element does not depend on the choice of the Cartier divisor associated with
Z
and yields a well-defined group homomorphism

(4.9)

{\rm cyc}_{X|D}\colon\CH^{1}(X|D)\to{\rm Pic}(X,D)

such that
{\rm cyc}_{X|D}(Z)=(\mathscr{O}(U_{\alpha},f_{\alpha}),\phi=(s_{|D})^{-1})\in{{\rm Pic}}(X,D)
for
Z\in\mathcal{Z}^{1}(X|D)
. It follows from the definition of the maps that
{\rm cyc}_{X|D}
is the inverse of the map in (
4.8
). We skip the details for these checks from here because we write the details later on in a more general setting (see the proof of Theorem
7.6
).
∎

4.2.
Action of Chow ring

We recall the following theorem from
[
KP17
, Theorem 1.1]
and sketch a proof.

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
X
be a smooth quasi-projective variety over a field
k
and let
D
be an effective Cartier divisor on
X
. Then we have a well-defined cap product

(4.10)

\cap\colon\CH^{p}(X)\times\CH^{q}(X|D)\to\CH^{p+q}(X|D)

so that
\CH^{*}(X|D)
becomes module over the ring
\CH^{*}(X)
.

We sketch a proof here because in
[
KP17
]
, a more general statement is proven.
Recall that we say two cycles
\alpha=\sum_{l}n_{l}[Y_{l}]\in\mathcal{Z}^{p}(X)
and
\beta=\sum_{t}m_{t}[Z_{t}]\in\mathcal{Z}^{q}(X)
intersect properly if for each
l,t
, the intersection
Y_{l}\cap Z_{t}
is either empty or every irreducible component of
Y_{l}\cap Z_{t}
is of codimension
p+q
.
In this case, we have a well-defined cycle

\alpha\cap\beta=\sum_{l,t}n_{l}m_{t}[Y_{l}\cap Z_{t}]\in\mathcal{Z}^{p+q}(X)
. Moreover, if
\beta\in\mathcal{Z}^{q}(X|D)
, then
\alpha\cap\beta\in\mathcal{Z}^{p+q}(X|D)
because

Y_{l}\cap Z_{t}\cap D\subset Z_{t}\cap D=\emptyset
for all
l,t
. The following lemma is key to proving Theorem
4.9
.

Lemma 4.10
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathcal{R}^{p}(X)
and

\beta\in\mathcal{Z}^{q}(X|D)
be such that they intersects properly. Then
\alpha\cap\beta\in\mathcal{R}^{p+q}(X|D)
.

Proof.

By the moving lemma for
K_{1}
-chains
[
GS90
, Lemma 4.2.6]
, we can find finitely many closed subvarieties
W_{l}
of
X
and rational functions
h_{l}
on
W_{l}
such that

\alpha=\sum_{l}m_{l}{\Div(h_{l})}
and that each
\Div(h_{l})
intersects with
\beta
properly.
We can therefore assume that
\alpha=\Div(h)
, where
h\in k(W)^{\times}
for a closed subvariety
W
of codimension
p-1
and
\beta=[Z]
, where
Z
is a closed subscheme of
X
that interests with each irreducible components of
\Div(h)
properly.

Let
W\cap Z=S\cap T
, where
S
is the union of irreducible components of codimension
p+q-1
and
T
is a union of all other irreducible components. Since
\Div(h)
intersects with
Z
properly,
\Div(h)\cap T=\emptyset
. In other words,
h|_{T}
is a regular function on
T
that is a unit on
T
. By
[
Ful84
, Propositions 8.1.1 and 8.2]
(also see
[
GS90
, Page 141]
), we know that

[W]\cdot[Z]=\sum_{l}m_{l}S_{l}+\gamma\in\CH^{p+q-1}(X)
, where
S_{l}
are irreducible components of
S
and
\gamma\in\CH_{T}^{p+q-1}(X)
. Let

\gamma
is represented by an element
\sum_{t}n_{t}[T_{t}]\in\mathcal{Z}^{p+q-1}_{T}(X)
. Since
h|_{T}
is a unit, we have an element

(4.11)

[h]\cdot\sum_{t}n_{t}[T_{t}]:=\sum_{t}n_{t}[h|_{T_{t}}]\in{\rm Ker}(\oplus_{x\in X^{(p+q-1)}\cap T}k(x)^{\times}\to\oplus_{x\in X^{(p+q)}\cap T}\mathbb{Z}).

We let
[h]\cdot\gamma
be the image of the above element in

\CH^{p+q,p+q-1}_{T}(X)=\frac{{\rm Ker}(\oplus_{x\in X^{(p+q-1)}\cap T}k(x)^{\times}\to\oplus_{x\in X^{(p+q)}\cap T}\mathbb{Z})}{{\rm Im}(\oplus_{x\in X^{(p+q-2)}\cap T}K_{2}(k(x))\to\oplus_{x\in X^{(p+q-1)}\cap T}k(x)^{\times})},

and define

(4.12)

h.Z=\sum_{l}m_{l}[h|_{S_{l}}]+[h]\cdot\gamma\in\CH^{p+q,p+q-1}(X).

Note that the above element
h.Z
lies in the image of the group

\CH^{p+q,p+q-1}_{Z}(X)
under the forget support map.
It then follows from
[
GS90
, Lemma 4.2.5(1)]
that

(4.13)

\alpha\cdot\beta=\Div(h)\cdot[Z]=\Div(h.Z).

By the definition of
\mathcal{R}^{*}(X|D)
, it
suffices to show that the element
h.Z
is represented by an element of

\oplus_{x\in X^{(p+q-1)}}G(Y^{N},E)\subset\oplus_{x\in X^{(p+q-1)}}\subset k(x)^{\times}
, where
Y
is the closure of
x
in
X
. But this follows because
h.Z
is represented by
the element
\sum_{l}m_{l}[h|_{S_{l}}]+\sum_{t}n_{t}[h|_{T_{t}}]
and for each
l,t
, we have

S_{l}\cap D\subset Z\cap D=\emptyset
and
T_{t}\cap D\subset T\cap D\subset Z\cap D=\emptyset
. This completes the proof.
∎

4.3.
Proof of Theorem
4.9

Let
\beta\in\mathcal{Z}^{q}(X|D)
and let
\overline{\alpha}\in\CH^{p}(X)
. By Chow’s moving lemma
[
Rob70
, § 3, Theorem]
,
we can find a cycle
\alpha_{0}\in\mathcal{Z}^{p}(X)
such that
\overline{\alpha}=\overline{\alpha_{0}}
and
\alpha_{0}
intersects with
\beta
properly. In particular, we have a well-defined element

\alpha_{0}\cap\beta\in\mathcal{Z}^{p+q}(X|D)
.

We define the cap
product
\overline{\alpha}\cap\beta\in\CH^{p+q}(X|D)
to be the image of the cycle

\alpha_{0}\cap\beta
. We first note that the element
\overline{\alpha}\cap\beta\in\CH^{p+q}(X|D)
does not depend on the choice of
\alpha_{0}
. Indeed, if
\alpha_{0}^{\prime}
is another cycle so that
\overline{\alpha}=\overline{\alpha_{0}^{\prime}}
and
\beta
interests with
\alpha_{0}
properly, then
\alpha_{0}-\alpha_{0}^{\prime}=\gamma\in\mathcal{R}^{p}(X)

and
\beta
intersects with
\gamma
properly. By Lemma
4.10
, we have

(4.14)

\alpha_{0}\cap\beta-\alpha_{0}^{\prime}\cap\beta=(\alpha_{0}-\alpha_{0}^{\prime})\cap\beta=\gamma\cap\beta\in\mathcal{R}^{p+q}(X|D).

This proves that the cycle class
\overline{\alpha}\cap\beta\in\CH^{p+q}(X|D)
is well defined.
Note that for all
\beta\in\mathcal{Z}^{q}(X|D)
, we now have a well-defined group homomorphism
-\cap\beta\colon\CH^{p}(X)\to\CH^{p+q}(X|D)
. Since the intersection product of cycles is linear in both coordinates, we get a bilinear pairing

\cap\colon\CH^{p}(X)\times\mathcal{Z}^{q}(X|D)\to\CH^{p+q}(X|D)
. We now show that
\CH^{p}(X)\cap\mathcal{R}^{q}(X|D)=0\in\CH^{p+q}(X|D)
.

Let
\beta=\Div(f)\in\mathcal{R}^{q}(X|D)
, where
f
is a rational function on a integral closed subscheme
W
and let
\overline{\alpha}\in\CH^{p}(X)
. We need to show that
\overline{\alpha}\cap\beta=0\in\CH^{p+q}(X|D)
. By Chow’s moving lemma, we can find a cycle
\alpha_{0}\in\mathcal{Z}^{p}(X)
such that
\overline{\alpha_{0}}=\overline{\alpha}\in\CH^{p}(X)
, and it intersects with
W
,
W\cap D
and support of
\Div(f)
properly. Let
\alpha_{0}\cap[W]=\sum_{l}m_{l}[Z_{l}]\in\mathcal{Z}^{p+q-1}(X)
. We then have

(4.15)

\overline{\alpha}\cap\beta=\alpha_{0}\cap\Div(f)=\sum_{l}m_{l}\Div(f|_{Z_{l}})\in\CH^{p+q}(X|D).

It therefore suffices to show that
f|_{Z_{l}}
satisfy modulus.
But this follows from the containment lemma
[
KP17
, Proposition 2.4]
(see the proof of
[
KP12
, Lemmas 2.1 and 2.2]
) as
Z_{l}\subset W
,
Z_{l}\not\subset D
and
f
satisfies modulus
D
on
W
.

We now have a well-defined pairing
\cap\colon\CH^{p}(X)\times\CH^{q}(X|D)\to\CH^{p+q}(X|D)
. The claim that it defines an action of
\CH^{*}(X)
on
\CH^{*}(X|D)
follows from the associativity of the intersection of cycles (if they intersect properly). This completes the proof of the theorem.

5.
Arithmetic Chow group with modulus

In this section, we attach a Green current
g_{Z}
to a cycle
Z
with modulus such that
dd^{c}g_{Z}+\delta_{Z}
vanishes on
D
. Consequently, we define an arithmetic Chow group with modulus.

Let
k=(k,\Sigma,F_{\infty})
be an arithmetic field and let
f\colon X\to\Spec(k)
be a smooth quasi-projective
k
-scheme of dimension
d\geq 0
. We let
D\hookrightarrow X
be an effective Cartier divisor such that
D_{{\rm red}}=\Sigma_{i}D_{i}
is a simple normal divisor on
X
. We let
D_{\infty}\coloneqq D_{{\rm red},\Sigma}(\mathds{C})
, and other notations be as in Sections
3
and
4
.
Recall from Section
4
that

\mathcal{Z}^{p}(X|D)
denote the free abelian group generated by those codimension
p
irreducible closed subsets of
X
that do not meet
D
. Recall from (
3.2
) that
A^{p,p}(X_{\mathds{R}})
is the space of real forms
\omega
satisfying
F_{\infty}^{*}(\omega)=(-1)^{p}\omega
and from Lemma
2.5
,
\Sigma_{N}A^{\ast}_{M}
is the space of smooth forms on a complex manifold
M
that vanishes on a simple normal crossing divisor
N
. Let

(5.1)

A^{p,p}(X_{\mathds{R}}|D):=\{\omega\in A^{p,p}(X_{\mathds{R}})\penalty\ |\penalty\ \omega\in\Sigma_{D_{\infty}}A^{p,p}_{X_{\infty}}\}.

We summarize the above condition by
\omega_{|D}=0
and call such forms as smooth real forms on
X
that vanish along
D
.

Much like the usual algebraic cycles we show that for a cycle with modulus, there always exists a Green current with the vanishing condition along
D
for the corresponding smooth form.

Theorem 5.1
.

Assume that
X
is a smooth quasi-projective
k
-scheme. Then for a cycle
Z\in\mathcal{Z}^{p}(X|D)
, there always exists Green current
g_{Z}
such that
dd^{c}g_{Z}+\delta_{Z}\in A^{p,p}(X_{\mathds{R}}|D)
.

Proof.

Let
Z\in\mathcal{Z}^{p}(X|D)
be a cycle with modulus of codimension
p
. We let
\textnormal{cl}(Z)
be the class of
Z_{\infty}
in
H^{p,p}_{Z_{\infty}}(X_{\infty})
.
Indeed, if
{\mathfrak{g}}_{Z}\in A^{p-1,p-1}(X_{\infty}\setminus Z_{\infty})
is a Green form for
Z
of logarithmic type (see
[
GS90
, 1.3.5]
for the definition of a Green form of logarithmic type), and if
\omega_{0}\in A^{p,p}(X_{\infty})
is the smooth form such that
dd^{c}([{\mathfrak{g}}_{Z}])+\delta_{Z}=[\omega_{0}]
, then the element
(\omega_{0},\frac{-\iota}{2\pi}{\partial}{\mathfrak{g}}_{Z})\in A^{p,p}(X_{\infty})\oplus A^{p,p-1}(X_{\infty}\setminus Z_{\infty})

maps to
\textnormal{cl}(Z)\in H^{p,p}_{Z_{\infty}}(X_{\infty})
(see
[
GS90
, page 114 and Lemma 2.1.1 (i)]
).
Since
Z\cap D=\emptyset
, we have a well-defined map

H^{p,p}_{Z_{\infty}}(X_{\infty})=H^{p}(s(A^{p,*}(X_{\infty})\to A^{p,*}(X_{\infty}\setminus Z_{\infty})))\to H^{p}(K^{p,*}_{X_{\infty},D_{\infty}}).

We let
\textnormal{cl}_{X|D}(Z)\in H^{p}(K^{p,*}_{X_{\infty},D_{\infty}})
denote the image of
\textnormal{cl}(Z)
under the above map. By Lemma
2.5
, we get

{\cl}_{X|D}(Z)\in H^{p}(K^{p,*}_{X_{\infty},D_{\infty}})=H^{p}(\Sigma_{D_{\infty}}A^{p,*}_{X_{\infty}})

such that it maps to the cycle class
\cl_{X}(Z)\in H^{p}(X_{\infty},A^{p,*}_{X_{\infty}})=H^{p,p}(X_{\infty})
under the map induced by the inclusion
\Sigma_{D_{\infty}}A^{p,*}_{X_{\infty}}\to A^{p,*}(X_{\infty})
.
Let
\omega^{\prime}_{Z|D}\in\Sigma_{D_{\infty}}A^{p,p}_{X_{\infty}}
be such that its class is the element
{\cl}_{X|D}(Z)
. We therefore get an element
\omega^{\prime}_{Z|D}\in A^{p,p}(X_{\infty})
such that it maps to the class
\cl_{X}(Z)

in
H^{p,p}(X_{\infty})
and
(\omega^{\prime}_{Z|D})_{|D}=0
.

Since
X_{\infty}
is a complex manifold, the
d
,
\partial
and
\overline{\partial}
cohomology of currents on
X
is the same as the corresponding cohomology of
C^{\infty}
forms on
X

(for example, see
[
GH78
, Pages 382-385]
). Under this identification, the current
\delta_{Z}
maps to the class
\cl_{X}(Z)

in
H^{p,p}(X_{\infty})
. In particular, the current
[\omega^{\prime}_{Z|D}]
and
\delta_{Z}
are in same class in
H^{p,p}(X_{\infty})
and hence there exists
g^{\prime}_{Z}\in\widetilde{D}^{p-1,p-1}(X_{\infty})
such that

\delta_{Z}+dd^{c}g^{\prime}_{Z}=[\omega^{\prime}_{Z|D}]\in{D}^{p,p}(X_{\infty})
.

Let
g^{1}_{Z}=\frac{1}{2}(\tau(g^{\prime}_{Z})+g^{\prime}_{Z})
,
g_{Z}=\frac{1}{2}(g^{1}_{Z}+(-1)^{p-1}F_{\infty}^{*}g^{1}_{Z}))
, and
\omega^{1}_{Z|D}=\frac{1}{2}(\omega^{\prime}_{Z|D}+\tau(\omega^{\prime}_{Z|D}))
,
\omega_{Z|D}=\frac{1}{2}(\omega^{1}_{Z|D}+(-1)^{p}F_{\infty}^{*}\omega^{1}_{Z|D}))
. We then have

\omega_{Z|D}\in A^{p,p}(X_{\mathds{R}}|D)
and

dd^{c}g_{Z}+\delta_{Z}=[\omega_{Z|D}]\in D^{p,p}(X_{\mathds{R}})
. This concludes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Now we can define

Definition 5.2
.

We let

\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}^{p}(X|D)=\{(Z,\widetilde{g})\in\mathcal{Z}^{p}(X|D)\oplus\widetilde{D}^{p-1,p-1}(X_{\mathds{R}})\penalty\ |\penalty\ \delta_{Z}+dd^{c}\widetilde{g}=[\omega],\textnormal{ where $\omega\in A^{p,p}(X_{\mathds{R}}|D)$}\}.

For a codimension
(p-1)
irreducible closed subset
W
such that
W\not\subset D
, we let
\nu:W^{N}\to X
denote the projection from the normalization of

W
and let
E=\nu^{*}(D)
denote the pull-back of
D
to
W^{N}
. Recall that
G(W^{N},E)
denotes the set of non-zero rational functions on
W^{N}
that are regular (actually a unit) in a neighborhood of
E
and one along
E
(see (
4.1
)). For
f\in G(W^{N},E)
,
we have

(5.2)

[-\log|f|^{2}]\in{D}^{0,0}(\widetilde{W}_{\mathds{R}}),

where
\widetilde{W}\to W^{N}
is a resolution of singularities such that the pull-back
\widetilde{E}
of
E
is supported on a simple normal crossing divisor on
\widetilde{W}
(see Example
3.7
(ii)).
Let
\iota_{W}\colon\widetilde{W}\to X
denote the induced proper morphism between smooth quasi-projective schemes over
k
. We have a well-defined pushforward map

\iota_{W*}\colon{D}^{0,0}(\widetilde{W}_{\mathds{R}})\to{D}^{p-1,p-1}(X_{\mathds{R}}).

We let

(5.3)

\widehat{{\rm div}(f)}=({\rm div(f)},\iota_{W*}[-\log|f|^{2}])\in\mathcal{Z}^{p}(X|D)\oplus\widetilde{D}^{p-1,p-1}(X_{\mathds{R}}).

Since
\delta_{{\rm div(f)}}+dd^{c}(\iota_{W*}[-\log|f|^{2}])=0
,
we have
\widehat{{\rm div}(f)}\in\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}^{p}(X|D)
. Note that this element does not depend on the choice of the resolution of singularities
\widetilde{W}
as it is determined by
W_{{\rm reg}}
(see
[
GS90
, Page 127]
).
We now let
\widehat{\mathcal{R}}^{p}(X|D)
be the subgroup of
\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}^{p}(X|D)
, generated by all such
\widehat{\Div(f)}
and define an
Arithmetic Chow group with modulus
as the quotient:

(5.4)

\widehat{\CH}^{p}(X|D):=\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}^{p}(X|D)/\widehat{\mathcal{R}}^{p}(X|D).

Forgetting the Green currents, one can define a map

\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}^{p}(X|D)\xrightarrow{\zeta_{X|D}}\mathcal{Z}^{p}(X|D)
. The above map takes
\widehat{\Div(f)}
to
\Div(f)
. We therefore have the induced map

\widehat{\CH}^{p}(X|D)\xrightarrow{\zeta_{X|D}}\CH^{p}(X|D)
.
From Theorem
5.1
the corollary below is immediate.

Corollary 5.3
.

Assume that
X
is a smooth quasi-projective
k
-scheme. Then for all
p\geq 0
, the canonical map

\zeta_{X|D}\colon\widehat{\CH}^{p}(X|D)\twoheadrightarrow{\CH}^{p}(X|D)
is surjective.

5.1.
Functoriality

Much like the usual arithmetic Chow groups, we study the behaviour of their modulus counterpart for proper and flat smooth morphisms.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
\phi\colon(Y,E)\rightarrow(X,D)
be a smooth morphism of modulus pairs (Definition
4.4
). Then

(i)

there is a pull-back homomorphism

\phi^{\ast}\colon\widehat{\CH}^{p}(X|D)\rightarrow\widehat{\CH}^{p}(Y|E).

(ii)

If
\phi
is also proper, there is a push-forward homomorphism

\phi_{\ast}\colon\widehat{\CH}^{p}(Y|E)\rightarrow\widehat{\CH}^{p-e}(X|D),

where
e=\dim(Y)-\dim(X)
.

Proof.

The algebraic component is the proof of Proposition
4.5
, and we refer to
[
KP17
, Lemma 2.8, 2.9]
for the proof. We show that the corresponding analytic conditions are satisfied. For the pull-back morphism, we have a straightforward argument: Let
(Z,{g}_{Z})\in\widehat{\CH}^{p}(X|D)
, with

\delta_{Z}+dd^{c}g_{Z}=[\omega]
such that
\omega_{|_{D}}=0
. Then by the proof of
[
GS90
, Theorem 3.6.1]
, we have

(5.5)

\delta_{\phi^{*}(Z)}+dd^{c}(\phi^{*}g_{Z})=[\phi^{*}(\omega)].

But then
\phi^{*}(\omega)_{|_{E}}=(\phi_{|_{E}})^{*}(\omega_{|_{D}})=0
, where

\phi_{|_{E}}\colon\widetilde{E}\to\widetilde{D}
is the induced map.

For the push-forward, let
(W,{g}_{W})\in\widehat{\CH}^{p}(Y|E)
, with with

\delta_{W}+dd^{c}g_{W}=[\omega_{W}]
such that
(\omega_{W})_{|_{E}}=0
. Notice that, since
\phi
is smooth, the element
\phi_{\ast}\omega_{W}
can be uniquely defined satisfying
\phi_{\ast}[\omega_{W}]=[\phi_{\ast}\omega_{W}]
. We consider the following commutative diagram of complex manifolds.

\textstyle{Y_{\infty}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\phi}
\textstyle{X_{\infty}}
\textstyle{\widetilde{E}_{\infty}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\phi_{|_{E}}}
\scriptstyle{\iota_{E}}
\textstyle{\widetilde{D}_{\infty}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces,}
\scriptstyle{\iota_{D}}

where the vertical arrows are induced by the inclusions.
Using the projection formula for integration along fibers, we have

(\phi|_{E})_{\ast}(\iota^{\ast}_{E}\omega_{W})=\iota^{\ast}_{D}(\phi_{\ast}\omega_{W}).

Since
\iota^{\ast}_{E}\omega_{W}=0
, we conclude that
\iota_{\ast}\omega_{W}
vanish along
D
. This gives us the desired maps and completes the proof.
∎

5.2.
An exact sequence

Recall from
[
GS90
, Theorem 3.3.5]
that arithmetic Chow groups fit into the following exact sequence.

\CH^{p,p-1}(X)\xrightarrow{\rho}\widetilde{A}^{p-1,p-1}(X_{\mathds{R}})\xrightarrow{a}\widehat{\CH}^{p}(X)\xrightarrow{\zeta}\CH^{p}(X)\to 0.

We prove a similar exact sequence for the arithmetic Chow groups with modulus. Before that, we fix certain notations.
We let

(5.6)

\widetilde{A}^{p-1,p-1}(X_{\mathds{R}},dd^{c}_{|D}=0)=\{\omega\in\widetilde{A}^{p-1,p-1}(X_{\mathds{R}})|(dd^{c}\omega)_{|D}=0\}\subset\widetilde{A}^{p-1,p-1}(X_{\mathds{R}}).

Note that
\widetilde{A}^{p-1,p-1}(X_{\mathds{R}},dd^{c}_{|D}=0)
contains the subgroup
\widetilde{A}^{p-1,p-1}(X_{\mathds{R}}|D)
and any exact
(p-1,p-1)
-form. For
U=X\setminus D
and
x\in U^{(p-1)}
, we let
k(x|D)^{\times}
denote the subgroup
G(W_{x}^{N},E_{x})\subset k(x)^{\times}
, where
W
is the closure of
x
in
X
,
W_{x}=\Spec(\mathcal{O}_{W,x})
and
E_{x}
denotes the pull-back of
D
along the induced morphism
W_{x}^{N}\to X
. We let

\mathcal{Z}^{p,p-1}(X|D)={\rm Ker}\left(\oplus_{x\in U^{(p-1)}}k(x|D)^{\times}\xrightarrow{\text{div}}\oplus_{x\in X^{(p)}}\mathbb{Z}\right),

\mathcal{R}^{p,p-1}(X|D)={\rm Im}(\oplus_{x\in X^{(p-2)}}K_{2}(k(x))\to\oplus_{x\in X^{(p-1)}}k(x)^{\times})\cap\mathcal{Z}^{p,p-1}(X|D)

and let

(5.7)

\CH^{p,p-1}(X|D)=\frac{\mathcal{Z}^{p,p-1}(X|D)}{\mathcal{R}^{p,p-1}(X|D)}.

Since
\mathcal{Z}^{p,p-1}(X|D)\subset\mathcal{Z}^{p,p-1}(X):={\rm Ker}(\oplus_{x\in X^{(p-1)}}k(x)^{\times}\to\oplus_{x\in X^{(p)}}\mathbb{Z})
, we have the following inclusion.

(5.8)

\CH^{p,p-1}(X|D)\hookrightarrow\CH^{p,p-1}(X).

Remark 5.5
.

Recall that by the Gersten sequence, we know that
\CH^{p,p-1}(X)
is isomorphic to the Zariski-cohomology group
H^{p-1}(X,\mathcal{K}_{p})
, where
\mathcal{K}_{*}
are Zariski sheaves associated to the
K
-groups, and that these groups are isomorphic to Bloch’s higher Chow group
\CH^{p}(X,1)
(Landburg
[
Lan91
, Lemma E]
). However, the groups
\CH^{p,p-1}(X|D)
are
not going to be isomorphic to
\CH^{p}(X|D,1)
. We note that by construction that the map
\CH^{p,p-1}(X|D)\hookrightarrow\CH^{p}(X,1)
is injective but the natural map
\CH^{p}(X|D,1)\to\CH^{p}(X,1)
is not injective in general. With this viewpoint, we expect
\CH^{p,p-1}(X|D)
to be the image of the map
\CH^{p}(X|D,1)\to\CH^{p}(X,1)
.
Note that, in
[
GKR20
]
, it is shown to be the case for zero-cycles, i.e., when
p=d+1
.

We construct various maps in the modulus setting.

(i)

We have a surjective map

\zeta_{X|D}\colon\widehat{\CH}^{p}(X|D)\rightarrow\CH^{p}(X|D)
by Corollary
5.3
.

(ii)

We construct a map
a_{X|D}\colon\widetilde{A}^{p-1,p-1}(X_{\mathds{R}},dd^{c}_{|D}=0)\to\widehat{\CH}^{p}(X|D)
. For
\omega\in\widetilde{A}^{p-1,p-1}(X_{\mathds{R}},dd^{c}_{|D}=0)
, we consider the element

(0,[\omega])\in\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}^{p}(X)
,
where
[\omega]
is the associated current.
Since

(dd^{c}\omega)_{|D}=0
, we have
(0,[\omega])\in\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}^{p}(X|D)
. We let

(5.9)

a_{X|D}(\omega)=(0,[\omega])\in\widehat{\CH}^{p}(X|D).

(iii)

Finally, we construct a map

\rho_{X|D}\colon\CH^{p,p-1}(X|D)\to\widetilde{A}^{p-1,p-1}(X_{\mathds{R}},dd^{c}|_{D}=0)
. Recall from the proof of

Example 5.8

We use the exact sequence

Let us discuss another example in which the Chow group with modulus is nonzero. Let

g_{0}=g_{\alpha}-\sum_{l}[\log|f_{l}|^{2}]
, then
g_{0}
is a Green current for the cycle
\alpha_{0}
and

(6.7)

{(\alpha,{g}_{\alpha})}={(\alpha_{0},{g_{0}})}\in\widehat{\CH}^{p}(X).

Since
\alpha_{0}
intersects with
\beta
properly, Lemma
6.2
yields a well-defined element

(\alpha_{0}\cap\beta,g_{0}\ast g_{\beta})\in\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}^{p+q}(X|D)
.
We now define

(6.8)

{(\alpha,{g}_{\alpha})}\cap(\beta,{g}_{\beta}):={(\alpha_{0}\cap\beta,g_{0}\ast g_{\beta})}\in\widehat{\CH}^{p+q}(X|D).

Let
\alpha_{0}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Z}^{p}(X)
be another cycle that intersects with
\beta
properly and it is rationally equivalent to
\alpha
. Let
\alpha_{0}^{\prime}-\alpha=\sum_{t}\Div(f_{t}^{\prime})
and let
g_{0}^{\prime}=g_{\alpha}-\sum_{t}[\log|f_{t}^{\prime}|^{2}]
be the corresponding Green current for the cycle
\alpha_{0}^{\prime}
. Then

(\alpha_{0},{g_{0}})-(\alpha_{0}^{\prime},{g_{0}^{\prime}})=\sum_{l}\widehat{\Div(f_{l})}-\sum_{t}\widehat{\Div(f_{t}^{\prime})}=:\sum_{s}\Div(h_{s})
. But then

(6.9)

\displaystyle(\alpha_{0}\cap\beta,g_{0}\ast g_{\beta})-(\alpha_{0}^{\prime}\cap\beta,g_{0}^{\prime}\ast g_{\beta})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle((\alpha_{0}-\alpha_{0}^{\prime})\cap\beta,(g_{0}-g_{0}^{\prime})\ast g_{\beta})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{s}(\Div(h_{s})\cap\beta,-[\log|h_{s}|^{2}]\ast g_{\beta}).

By Lemma
6.3
(i), it follows that this difference lie in
\widehat{\mathcal{R}}^{p+q}(X|D)
. The element
(
6.8
) is therefore well-defined and does not depend on the choice of
\alpha_{0}
. Since the intersection product of cycles and the product of currents are linear in both coordinates, we get a bilinear pairing

\cap\colon\widehat{\CH}^{p}(X)\times\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}^{q}(X|D)\to\widehat{\CH}^{p+q}(X|D)
.

Let
W
be a closed subscheme of
X
of codimension
q-1
and let
f\in G(W^{N},E)
, where
E
is the pull-back of
D
to
W^{N}
(see §
4
for the definition of
G(W^{N},E)
). Let
{(\alpha,{g}_{\alpha})}\in\widehat{\CH}^{p}(X)
. We need to show that

{(\alpha,{g}_{\alpha})}\cap\widehat{\Div(f)}=0\in\widehat{\CH}^{p+q}(X|D)
. By Chow’s moving lemma, we can find a cycle
\alpha_{0}\in\mathcal{Z}^{p}(X)
such that
\alpha_{0}
intersects with
W
,
W\cap D
and
\Div(f)
properly, and it is rationally equivalent to
\alpha
. Let
g_{0}
be the associate Green current for
\alpha_{0}
as in the previous paragraph. But then by (
6.8
) and Lemma
6.3
(ii), we have

(6.10)

{(\alpha,{g}_{\alpha})}\cap\widehat{\Div(f)}={(\alpha_{0}\cap\Div(f),g_{0}\ast(-[\log|f|^{2}]))}=0\in\widehat{\CH}^{p+q}(X|D).

This completes the proof of the existence of the bilinear pairing (
6.1
).

The rest of the properties of the theorem follow from the corresponding properties of Chow groups with modulus and of arithmetic Chow groups.
∎

7.
Relative Hermitian Picard group

In this section, we generalize the results of
3.3
and
4.1
, and define a Hermitian metric on the relative line bundles. Subsequently, we prove that the arithmetic Chow group
\widehat{\CH}^{1}(X|D)
is isomorphic to the Picard group of these relative line bundles with a Hermitian metric. We use the notations of Sections
3.3
and
4.1
, and begin by defining the metric.

As before, let
f\colon X\to\Spec(k)
be a smooth quasi-projective scheme of dimension
d\geq 0
over an arithmetic field
k
and let
D\hookrightarrow X
be an effective Cartier divisor such that
D_{{\rm red}}
is a simple normal divisor on
X
.
Recall from Definition
3.8
that given a Hermitian line bundle
(\mathscr{L},||\cdot||)
on
X
, we have the cohomological Chern class
{c}_{1}(\mathscr{L},||\cdot||)
. Also,
recall from Definition
4.6
that for a line bundle
\mathscr{L}
, a trivialization of
\mathscr{L}
along
D
means an isomorphism
\phi\colon\mathscr{L}_{|D}\xrightarrow{\cong}\mathscr{O}_{D}
that lifts to an open neighbourhood of
D
. We combine both these notions associated with a Hermitian line bundle in the following definition.

Definition 7.1
.

The triple
(\mathscr{L},||\cdot||,\phi)
is said to be a Hermitian line bundle on
X
with trivialization along
D
if
(\mathscr{L},||\cdot||)
is a Hermitian line bundle on
X
,
\phi\colon\mathscr{L}_{|D}\xrightarrow{\cong}\mathscr{O}_{D}
is a trivialization of
\mathscr{L}
along
D
and
{c}_{1}(\mathscr{L},||\cdot||)\in A^{1,1}(X_{\mathds{R}}|D)
.

We say two Hermitian line bundles

(\mathscr{L}_{1},||\cdot||_{1},\phi_{1})
and
(\mathscr{L}_{2},||\cdot||_{2},\phi_{2})
with trivialization along
D
are equivalent (under the relation
\sim
) if there exists an isomorphism
\Phi\colon\mathscr{L}_{1}\xrightarrow{\cong}\mathscr{L}_{2}
such that
\Phi^{*}(||\cdot||_{2})=||\cdot||_{1}
and there exists an neighborhood
U
of
D
where both
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
are defined and
\phi_{2}\circ\Phi_{|U}=\phi_{1}\colon(\mathscr{L}_{1})_{|U}\to\mathscr{O}_{U}
, i.e., the following diagram commutes.

(7.1)

\textstyle{(\mathscr{L}_{1})_{|U}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\Phi_{|U}}
\scriptstyle{\cong}
\scriptstyle{\phi_{1}}
\textstyle{(\mathscr{L}_{2})_{|U}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\phi_{2}}
\textstyle{\mathscr{O}_{U}.}

It is easy to see that the above relation is an equivalence relation. We shall denote the equivalence class of a Hermitian line bundle
(\mathscr{L},||\cdot||,\phi)
with trivialization along
D

by the same symbol
(\mathscr{L},||\cdot||,\phi)
.
In the above case, we shall call
\Phi
to be the isomorphism between triples
(\mathscr{L}_{1},||\cdot||_{1},\phi_{1})
and
(\mathscr{L}_{2},||\cdot||_{2},\phi_{2})
.

Definition 7.2
.

We define the relative Hermitian Picard group
\widehat{{\rm Pic}}(X,D)
as the set of the equivalence classes of Hermitian line bundles on
X
with trivialization along
D
, i.e.,

(7.2)

\widehat{{\rm Pic}}(X,D):=\{(\mathscr{L},||\cdot||,\phi)\penalty\ |\penalty\ {c}_{1}(\mathscr{L},||\cdot||)\in A^{1,1}(X_{\mathds{R}}|D)\}/\sim,

where the equivalence relation
\sim
is as defined above.

Lemma 7.3
.

There exists a natural abelian group structure on
\widehat{{\rm Pic}}(X,D)
so that

(7.3)

(\mathscr{L}_{1},||\cdot||_{1},\phi_{1})\cdot(\mathscr{L}_{2},||\cdot||_{2},\phi_{2})=(\mathscr{L}_{1}\otimes\mathscr{L}_{2},||\cdot||_{1}\otimes||\cdot||_{2},\phi_{1}\otimes\phi_{2}).

Proof.

By Definition
7.2
, it follows that the above product is well-defined, it is associative, and
(\mathscr{O}_{X},|\cdot|,{\operatorname{id}})
is the identity of the product. The commutativity of the product is also immediate from the definition of the equivalence relation. We show that every element
(\mathscr{L},||\cdot||,\phi)\in\widehat{{\rm Pic}}(X,D)
has an inverse.

Let
U\subset X
be an open neighborhood of
D
such that
there exists an isomorphism
{\phi^{\prime}}\colon\mathscr{L}_{|U}\to\mathscr{O}_{U}
satisfying
\phi={{\phi}^{\prime}}_{|D}
.
We let
(\phi^{\prime})^{\vee}\colon\mathscr{O}_{U}\xrightarrow{\cong}\mathscr{L}_{|U}^{-1}
is the dual isomorphism obtained by tensoring
{\phi^{\prime}}
with
\mathscr{L}_{|U}^{-1}
. We now denote by
1/\phi\colon\mathscr{L}^{-1}_{|D}\xrightarrow{\cong}\mathscr{O}_{D}
the restriction of the isomorphism

((\phi^{\prime})^{\vee})^{-1}\colon\mathscr{L}_{|U}^{-1}\xrightarrow{\cong}\mathscr{O}_{U}
.
Recall that given line bundle
\mathscr{L}
with a Hermitian form
||\cdot||

that is locally given by smooth function
h_{\alpha,\infty}\colon U_{\alpha,\infty}\to\mathds{R}_{>0}
, there exists a Hermitian form
||\cdot||^{-1}
on
\mathscr{L}^{-1}
such that it is locally given by smooth function
h_{\alpha,\infty}^{-1}\colon U_{\alpha,\infty}\to\mathds{R}_{>0}
. In particular,

c_{1}(\mathscr{L}^{-1},||\cdot||^{-1})=-c_{1}(\mathscr{L},||\cdot||)
.
It therefore follows that

(\mathscr{L}^{-1},||\cdot||^{-1},1/\phi)\in\widehat{{\rm Pic}}(X,D)
and it is the inverse of the element

(\mathscr{L},||\cdot||,\phi)
.
This completes the proof.
∎

We now define the arithmetic Chern class of a Hermitian line bundle with trivialization.
Let
(\mathscr{L},||\cdot||,\phi)
be a Hermitian line bundle with trivialization along
D
. Let
\phi^{\prime}\colon\mathscr{L}_{|U}\xrightarrow{\cong}\mathscr{O}_{U}
be a lift of
\phi
for an open neighborhood
D\subset U
, i.e.,
\phi^{\prime}_{|D}=\phi\colon\mathscr{L}_{|D}\xrightarrow{\cong}\mathscr{O}_{D}
. We then define the arithmetic Chern class of
(\mathscr{L},||\cdot||,\phi)
as follows.

(7.4)

\widehat{c}_{1}(\mathscr{L},||\cdot||,\phi)=(\Div(\phi^{\prime-1}),-[\log||(\phi^{\prime})^{-1}||^{2}])\in\mathcal{Z}^{1}(X|D)\oplus{D}^{0,0}(X_{\infty}).

Since
\phi
is a trivialization, by Definition
7.1
, we have
{c}_{1}(\mathscr{L},||\cdot||)_{|D}=0\in A^{1,1}(\widetilde{D}_{\infty})

\delta_{\Div(\phi^{\prime-1})}+dd^{c}(-[\log||(\phi^{\prime})^{-1}||^{2}])=[{c}_{1}(\mathscr{L},||\cdot||)]

5.2
that
\widehat{c}_{1}(\mathscr{L},||\cdot||,\phi)\in\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}^{1}(X|D)
. We let its image in
\widehat{\CH}^{1}(X|D)
to be denoted by the same symbol.
As an element of
\widehat{\CH}^{1}(X|D)
, the Chern class
\widehat{c}_{1}(\mathscr{L},||\cdot||,\phi)
does not depend on the chosen extension
\phi^{\prime}
and it is invariant for the equivalence relation on Hermitian line bundles with trivialization. In short, the following definition now makes sense.

Definition 7.4
.

Given
(\mathscr{L},||\cdot||,\phi)\in\widehat{{\rm Pic}}(X,D)
, we define its arithmetic Chern class as follows.

(7.5)

\widehat{c}_{1}(\mathscr{L},||\cdot||,\phi)=(\Div(\phi^{\prime-1}),-[\log||\phi^{\prime-1}||^{2}])\in\widehat{\CH}^{1}(X|D),

where
\phi^{\prime}
is a lift of the isomorphism
\phi
.

Lemma 7.5
.

The arithmetic Chern class defines a group homomorphism.

(7.6)

\widehat{c}_{1}\colon\widehat{{\rm Pic}}(X,D)\to\widehat{\CH}^{1}(X|D).

Proof.

Let
(\mathscr{L}_{1},||\cdot||_{1},\phi_{1})
and
(\mathscr{L}_{2},||\cdot||_{2},\phi_{2})\in\widehat{{\rm Pic}}(X,D)
be two elements and for
i=1,2

\phi_{i}^{\prime}\colon(\mathscr{L}_{i})_{|U}\xrightarrow{\cong}\mathscr{O}_{U}
be the lift of
\phi_{i}
.
We then have

(7.7)

\displaystyle\widehat{c}_{1}((\mathscr{L}_{1},||\cdot||_{1},\phi_{1})\otimes(\mathscr{L}_{2},||\cdot||_{2},\phi_{2}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widehat{c}_{1}(\mathscr{L}_{1}\otimes\mathscr{L}_{2},||\cdot||_{1}\otimes||\cdot||_{2},\phi_{1}\otimes\phi_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\Div(\phi_{1}^{\prime-1}\otimes\phi_{2}^{\prime-1}),-[\log(||\phi_{1}^{\prime-1}||_{1}^{2}\ ||\phi_{2}^{\prime-1}||_{2}^{2})])

\displaystyle(\Div(\phi_{1}^{\prime-1}),-[\log(||\phi_{1}^{\prime-1}||_{1}^{2}])

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+(\Div(\phi_{2}^{\prime-1}),-[\log(||\phi_{2}^{\prime-1}||_{2}^{2})])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widehat{c}_{1}(\mathscr{L}_{1},||\cdot||_{1},\phi_{1})+\widehat{c}_{1}(\mathscr{L}_{2},||\cdot||_{2},\phi_{2}).

This completes the proof.

We now prove the main result of this section.

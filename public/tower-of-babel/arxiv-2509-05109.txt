Regularity and 𝖪 0 \mathsf{K}_{0} -Regularity under Finiteness Conditions.
Rafael Parra

Abstract
. The purpose of this work is to investigate various notions of regularity from the perspective of finiteness conditions, with the ultimate goal of identifying broad classes of rings that are 𝖪 0 \mathsf{K}_{0} -regular. In this direction, we revisit the classical concepts of coherence and von Neumann regularity, and establish new characterizations. We then focus on the study of n n -coherent regular rings , recently introduced in [ 31 ] , and analyze their 𝖪 \mathsf{K} -theoretic behavior. Finally, we present applications illustrating how these approaches provide examples of 𝖪 0 \mathsf{K}_{0} -regular rings.

1.
Introduction.

Algebraic
\mathsf{K}
-theory associates to a ring
R
a sequence of groups
\{\mathsf{K}_{i}(R)\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
. Computing these groups is extremely difficult and only possible in special cases. Given this complexity, modern approaches often focus on understanding how the
\mathsf{K}
-groups behave under equivalences between related categories, allowing the transfer of information from one ring to another. This perspective not only offers practical methods of computation but also contributes to a broader understanding of algebraic
\mathsf{K}
-groups.

Let
R
be an associative ring with unit. An
R
-module
M
is said to be finitely
n
-presented if there exists an exact sequence

F_{n}\longrightarrow F_{n-1}\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow F_{1}\longrightarrow F_{0}\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow 0,

where each
F_{i}
is a finitely generated projective (or free)
R
-module for
0\leq i\leq n
. When
n=1
, this reduces to the classical notion of a finitely presented module, and for
n=0
, it corresponds to a finitely generated module. It is a well-known result that, for left coherent regular rings,

\mathsf{K}_{i}(R)\cong\mathsf{K}_{i}(\mathsf{FP}_{1}(R)),\quad i\geq 0,

where
\mathsf{FP}_{1}(R)
denotes the class of finitely presented
R
-modules. Motivated by the principle that many homological results extend by replacing finitely presented modules with finitely
n
-presented ones
[
31
]
, the authors show, using Quillen’s Resolution Theorem, that

\mathsf{K}_{i}(R)\cong\mathsf{K}_{i}(\mathsf{FP}_{n}(R)),\quad i\geq 0,

for
R
a
left
n
-coherent regular
ring, i.e., a ring in which every finitely
n
-presented module admits a finite resolution by finitely generated projective modules. Here,
\mathsf{FP}_{n}(R)
denotes the category of finitely
n
-presented
R
-modules. However, some classical results do not extend directly. For instance, it is well known that
\mathsf{K}_{-1}(R)=0
for left coherent regular rings. In contrast, for left
n
-coherent regular rings with
n\geq 2
, this vanishing fails in general: as shown in Remark
8.3
, there exists a
2
-coherent regular ring for which
\mathsf{K}_{-1}(R)=\mathbb{Z}.

These results on
\mathsf{K}_{i}
for
n
-coherent regular rings naturally lead to questions about the behavior of
\mathsf{K}_{0}
under polynomial extensions. A fundamental problem is to determine when a ring
R
is
\mathsf{K}_{0}
-regular, that is, when

\mathsf{K}_{0}(R[x_{1},\dots,x_{k}])\cong\mathsf{K}_{0}(R)\quad\text{for all }k\geq 1.

For Noetherian rings, Grothendieck’s Theorem asserts that
R
is
\mathsf{K}_{0}
-regular if every finitely generated
R
-module has finite projective dimension. In the coherent setting, Quillen showed that
\mathsf{K}_{0}
-regularity holds provided that
R
is stably coherent (i.e.,
R[x_{1},\dots,x_{k}]
is coherent for all
k>0
) and every finitely presented
R
-module has finite projective dimension. More recently, Swan
[
62
]
proved in 2019 that for coherent regular rings one has

\mathsf{K}_{0}(R)\cong\mathsf{K}_{0}(R[x]),

but he also observed that, since
R[x]
need not be coherent even when
R
is, it remains unclear whether this result extends to polynomial rings in two or more variables
R[x_{1},\dots,x_{k}]
for
k\geq 2
. Determining when a ring is coherent is a difficult problem. In this direction, for commutative rings and under the weaker assumption that every finitely
\infty
-presented
R
-module has finite projective dimension— a property we refer to as
regular
in this work (see Definition
5.3
)—Wang, Qiao, and Kim
[
68
]
showed that
R
is
\mathsf{K}_{0}
-regular. As a consequence, a broad class of rings is
\mathsf{K}_{0}
-regular, including commutative
n
-coherent regular rings; see Corollary
8.1
.

These results naturally motivate a deeper investigation into the notion of regularity for rings, a concept that has evolved in several forms since the 1960s. The term “regular” appears to originate from algebraic geometry: by the classical Zariski–Serre theorem, if
R
is the local ring of an algebraic variety
V
at a point
x
, then
R
is regular if and only if
x
is a non-singular point of
V
. Furthermore, when
V
is an affine variety with coordinate ring
B
,
B
is regular if and only if
V
itself is non-singular, providing a rich source of commutative regular rings. In a more algebraic framework, a ring
R
is said to be regular in the sense of Bass if
R
is left Noetherian and every finitely generated left
R
-module has finite projective dimension. One of the aims of this work is to unify these various notions of regularity and study them from the perspective of finiteness conditions, with the ultimate goal of identifying classes of rings that are
\mathsf{K}_{0}
-regular.

After setting up the necessary preliminaries in Section
2
, the structure of the paper is as follows. Section
3
revisits the classical notions of coherence and von Neumann regularity, where we also present new characterizations. In Section
4
, we investigate the concept of regularity under the assumption of coherence, which allows us to identify a wide family of coherent regular rings. Section
5
is concerned with a generalization of this framework: we study and characterize
n
-coherent regular rings, as introduced in
[
31
]
. In Section
6
, we analyze Vogel’s notion of regularity, which provides an alternative approach to the concept. In Section
7
we consider a particularly well-behaved form of regularity, namely uniform
n
-regularity, which exhibits stronger properties than the classical notion. Finally, Section
8
contains applications of the preceding results, including examples of rings that are
\mathsf{K}_{0}
-regular.

2.
Preliminaries.

Let
R
be an associative ring with identity. We denote by
R\mbox{-}\mathsf{Mod}
the category of all left
R
-modules, and by
\mathsf{Mod}\mbox{-}R
the category of all right
R
-modules. Unless otherwise specified, the terms “module” and “ideal” will always mean left
R
-modules and left ideals, respectively. For an integer
n\geq 0
, an
R
-module
M
is called
finitely
n
-presented
if there exists an exact sequence

F_{n}\longrightarrow F_{n-1}\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow F_{1}\longrightarrow F_{0}\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow 0,

where each
F_{i}
is a finitely generated and projective (or free)
R
-module for every
0\leq i\leq n
. Such a sequence is called a
finite
n
-presentation
of
M
. The class of all finitely
n
-presented
R
-modules is denoted by
\mathsf{FP}_{n}(R)
. In particular,
\mathsf{FP}_{0}(R)
consists of all finitely generated
R
-modules, and
\mathsf{FP}_{1}(R)
consists of all finitely presented
R
-modules. The class
\mathsf{FP}_{\infty}(R)
is defined as the collection of modules that admit a resolution by finitely generated projective (or free) modules. Equivalently,

\mathsf{FP}_{\infty}(R)=\bigcap_{n\geq 0}\mathsf{FP}_{n}(R).

Clearly, the class of finitely generated projective modules, denoted
\mathsf{proj}(R)
, is contained in
\mathsf{FP}_{\infty}(R)
. We denote by
\mathsf{Proj}(R)
the class of all projective
R
-modules. For convenience, we also set
\mathsf{FP}_{-1}(R):=R\mbox{-}\mathsf{Mod}
, the entire module category. Moreover, these classes form a chain of inclusions:

\mathsf{proj}(R)\subseteq\mathsf{FP}_{\infty}(R)\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\mathsf{FP}_{n}(R)\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\mathsf{FP}_{1}(R)\subseteq\mathsf{FP}_{0}(R)\subseteq\mathsf{FP}_{-1}(R)=R\mbox{-}\mathsf{Mod}.

The classes
\mathsf{FP}_{n}(R)
have been extensively studied in the literature. In particular, let

0\rightarrow A\rightarrow B\rightarrow C\rightarrow 0

be a short exact sequence of
R
-modules. Then, by
[
36
, Theorem 2.1.2]
, the following hold:

(1)

If
A,B\in\mathsf{FP}_{n}(R)
, then
C\in\mathsf{FP}_{n}(R)
.

(2)

If
A,C\in\mathsf{FP}_{n}(R)
, then
B\in\mathsf{FP}_{n}(R)
.

(3)

If
B\in\mathsf{FP}_{n}(R)
and
C\in\mathsf{FP}_{n+1}(R)
, then
A\in\mathsf{FP}_{n}(R)
.

(4)

If
B\cong A\oplus C
, then
B\in\mathsf{FP}_{n}(R)
if and only if
A,C\in\mathsf{FP}_{n}(R)
.

As a consequence, the class
\mathsf{FP}_{n}(R)
is closed under extensions, direct summands, and cokernels of monomorphisms between its objects. In contrast, the class
\mathsf{FP}_{\infty}(R)
exhibits even stronger closure properties: it is closed under direct summands and forms an
exact class
of modules; that is, it satisfies the
2-out-of-3
property with respect to short exact sequences; see
[
17
, Proposition 1.7 and Theorem 1.8]
. Explicitly, if
0\rightarrow A\rightarrow B\rightarrow C\rightarrow 0
is a short exact sequence of
R
-modules and two of the modules
A
,
B
, or
C
lie in
\mathsf{FP}_{\infty}(R)
, then the third also belongs to this class.

2.1.
Cotorsion pair.

Let
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathsf{Mod}\mbox{-}R
. We define

\mathcal{X}^{\top_{1}}\coloneqq\{M\in R\mbox{-}\mathsf{Mod}\mid\Tor^{R}_{1}(X,M)=0\text{ for all }X\in\mathcal{X}\},

\mathcal{X}^{\top_{\geq i}}\coloneqq\{M\in R\mbox{-}\mathsf{Mod}\mid\Tor^{R}_{j}(X,M)=0\text{ for all }X\in\mathcal{X},\,j\geq i\},

and for brevity,
\mathcal{X}^{\top}\coloneqq\mathcal{X}^{\top_{\geq 1}}
. Dually, for
\mathcal{Y}\subseteq R\mbox{-}\mathsf{Mod}
, we define the classes
{}^{\top_{1}}\mathcal{Y}
,
{}^{\top_{\geq i}}\mathcal{Y}
, and
{}^{\top}\mathcal{Y}
in
\mathsf{Mod}\mbox{-}R
analogously.

Similarly, for
\mathcal{X}\subseteq R\mbox{-}\mathsf{Mod}
, we define

\mathcal{X}^{\perp_{1}}\coloneqq\{M\in R\mbox{-}\mathsf{Mod}\mid\Ext^{1}_{R}(X,M)=0\text{ for all }X\in\mathcal{X}\},

\mathcal{X}^{\perp_{\geq i}}\coloneqq\{M\in R\mbox{-}\mathsf{Mod}\mid\Ext^{j}_{R}(X,M)=0\text{ for all }X\in\mathcal{X},\,j\geq i\},

and set
\mathcal{X}^{\perp}\coloneqq\mathcal{X}^{\perp_{\geq 1}}
. The dual classes
{}^{\perp_{1}}\mathcal{Y}
,
{}^{\perp_{\geq i}}\mathcal{Y}
, and
{}^{\perp}\mathcal{Y}
for
\mathcal{Y}\subseteq R\mbox{-}\mathsf{Mod}
are defined in the same way. If
\mathcal{X}=\mathsf{FP}_{n}(R)
, then
\mathcal{X}^{\perp_{1}}
coincides with the class of
FP_{n}
-injective modules; that is, an
R
-module
M
is
FP_{n}
-injective
if
\Ext^{1}_{R}(F,M)=0
for all
F\in\mathsf{FP}_{n}(R)
, including the case
n=\infty
. In particular,
M
is injective if and only if it is
FP_{0}
-injective, and it is
FP
-injective (or absolutely pure) if and only if it is
FP_{1}
-injective. The class of
FP_{n}
-injective modules is denoted by
\mathsf{FP}_{n}\text{-}\mathsf{Inj}(R)
. A ring
R
is said to be left
self
FP_{n}
-injective
if it is
FP_{n}
-injective as a module over itself. An
R
-module
P
is
FP_{n}
-projective
if
\Ext^{1}_{R}(P,M)=0
for all
FP_{n}
-injective
R
-modules
M
. The class of
FP_{n}
-projective modules is denoted by
\mathsf{FP}_{n}\text{-}\mathsf{Proj}(R)
. Similarly, if
\mathcal{X}
denotes the class of all finitely
n
-presented right
R
-modules, then
\mathcal{X}^{\top_{1}}
coincides with the class of
FP_{n}
-flat
modules. More precisely, an
R
-module
M
is
FP_{n}
-flat
if
\Tor_{1}^{R}(F,M)=0
for every finitely
n
-presented right
R
-module
F
(including the case
n=\infty
). Note that
FP_{0}
-flat modules coincide with the flat modules. The class of
FP_{n}
-flat modules is denoted by
\mathsf{FP}_{n}\text{-}\mathsf{Flat}(R)
.

A
cotorsion pair
is a pair of classes
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
in
R\mbox{-}\mathsf{Mod}
, such that
\mathcal{A}={}^{\perp_{1}}\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{A}^{\perp_{1}}
. A cotorsion pair is
hereditary
if moreover
\mathcal{A}={}^{\perp}\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{A}^{\perp}
, that is, all the higher
\Ext
-groups vanish as well. Let
\mathcal{X}
be a class in
R\mbox{-}\mathsf{Mod}
. The cotorsion pair
generated
by
\mathcal{X}
is the cotorsion pair
[{}^{\perp_{1}}(\mathcal{X}^{\perp_{1}}),\mathcal{X}^{\perp_{1}}]
. The cotorsion pair
cogenerated
by
\mathcal{X}
is the cotorsion pair
[{}^{\perp_{1}}\mathcal{X},({}^{\perp_{1}}\mathcal{X})^{\perp_{1}}]
.

The left-hand class in a cotorsion pair is closed under direct sums and extensions, and contains all the projective modules. The right-hand class in a cotorsion pair is closed under products and extensions, and contains all the injective modules. A class
\mathcal{X}
in
R\mbox{-}\mathsf{Mod}
is
resolving
if
\mathcal{X}
is closed under extensions, contains the projectives, and is closed under kernels of epimorphisms. Dually, a class
\mathcal{X}
in
R\mbox{-}\mathsf{Mod}
is
coresolving
if
\mathcal{X}
is closed under extensions, contains the injectives, and is closed under cokernels of monomorphisms.
A cotorsion pair
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
is hereditary if and only if
\mathcal{A}
is a resolving class if and only if
\mathcal{B}
is a coresolving class. A cotorsion pair
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
is
complete
if
\mathcal{A}
is special precovering, or equivalently, if
\mathcal{B}
is special preenveloping. In particular, the cotorsion pair
[\mathsf{FP}_{n}\text{-}\mathsf{Proj}(R),\mathsf{FP}_{n}\text{-}\mathsf{Inj}(R)]
is complete and cogenerated by a set; see
[
17
, Corollary 4.2]
.

Following
[
73
]
, we introduce the notions of dimensions associated with
\mathsf{FP}_{n}
-injective and
\mathsf{FP}_{n}
-flat modules, where
n\geq 0
may also be infinite.

(1)

The
\mathsf{FP}_{n}
-flat dimension
of
N
, denoted by
\mathop{\rm\mathsf{FP}_{n}\text{-}fd}\nolimits_{R}(N)
, is the smallest integer
k\geq 0
such that
\Tor^{R}_{k+1}(F,N)=0
for every finitely
n
-presented right
R
-module
F
. If no such integer
k
exists, we define
\mathop{\rm\mathsf{FP}_{n}\text{-}fd}\nolimits_{R}(N)=\infty
.

The
left
FP_{n}
-flat global dimension
of
R
, denoted by
\mathop{\rm\mathsf{FP}_{n}\text{-}FD}\nolimits(R)
, is defined as:

\mathop{\rm\mathsf{FP}_{n}\text{-}FD}\nolimits(R)=\sup\{\mathop{\rm\mathsf{FP}_{n}\text{-}fd}\nolimits_{R}(N)\mid N\text{ is a left $R$-module}\}.

(2)

The
\mathsf{FP}_{n}
-injective dimension
of an
R
-module
M
, denoted by
\mathop{\rm\mathsf{FP}_{n}\text{-}id}\nolimits_{R}(M)
, is the smallest integer
k\geq 0
such that
\Ext^{k+1}_{R}(F,M)=0
for all
F\in\mathsf{FP}_{n}(R)
. If no such
k
exists,
\mathop{\rm\mathsf{FP}_{n}\text{-}id}\nolimits_{R}(M)=\infty
.

The
left
FP_{n}
-injective global dimension
of
R
, denoted by
\mathop{\rm\mathsf{FP}_{n}\text{-}ID}\nolimits(R)
, is defined as:

\mathop{\rm\mathsf{FP}_{n}\text{-}ID}\nolimits(R)=\sup\{\mathop{\rm\mathsf{FP}_{n}\text{-}id}\nolimits_{R}(M)\mid M\text{ is a left $R$-module}\}.

3.
Pseudo-Coherence, Coherence, and von Neumann Regularity.

In this section, we recall the notions of coherent and pseudo-coherent modules, which generalize the classical Noetherian condition. An
R
-module
M
is called
pseudo-coherent
if every finitely generated submodule of
M
is finitely presented. Equivalently,
M
is pseudo-coherent if, for every homomorphism
R^{n}\to M
with
n<\infty
, the kernel is finitely generated.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
R
be a ring. The class of pseudo-coherent
R
-modules is closed under submodules, extensions and direct sums.

Proof.

It is straightforward.
∎

An
R
-module
M
is called
coherent
if it is pseudo-coherent and finitely generated. We denote by
\mathsf{Coh}(R)
the class of all coherent
R
-modules. For example, over a Noetherian ring, any non-finitely generated projective module is pseudo-coherent but not coherent. A ring
R
is called
left coherent
if
R
, as an
R
-module over itself, is coherent, that is, if every finitely generated ideal of
R
is finitely presented.

Proposition 3.2
.

[
62
, Theorem 2.4 and Corollary 2.7]

Let
R
be a ring. The class
\mathsf{Coh}(R)
of coherent
R
-modules is an abelian subcategory of
R\mbox{-}\mathsf{Mod}
. Moreover,
R
is left coherent if and only if
\mathsf{Coh}(R)=\mathsf{FP}_{1}(R)=\mathsf{FP}_{\infty}(R)
.

∎

Let
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
be classes of
R
-modules. We say that
\mathcal{D}
has the
2-out-of-3 property
with respect to
\mathcal{C}
if, for every short exact sequence
0\to C_{1}\to C_{2}\to C_{3}\to 0
, with
C_{1},C_{2},C_{3}\in\mathcal{C}
, if any two of
C_{1},C_{2},C_{3}
belong to
\mathcal{D}
, then the third also belongs to
\mathcal{D}
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
R
be a ring. For
i=0,1
, the class
\mathsf{FP}_{i}(R)
has the 2-out-of-3 property with respect to the class of pseudo-coherent
R
-modules.

Proof.

It is enough to observe that in any short exact sequence of
R
-modules

0\rightarrow M_{0}\rightarrow M_{1}\rightarrow M_{2}\rightarrow 0,

if two of
M_{0},M_{1},M_{2}
are coherent, then the third is also coherent by Proposition
3.2
.
∎

The next proposition characterizes coherent rings by replacing the class of finitely presented modules with
\mathsf{FP}_{1}
-projective, projective, or free modules.

Theorem 3.4
.

For a ring
R
, the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

R
is left coherent.

(2)

Every
\mathsf{FP}_{1}
-projective
R
-module is pseudo-coherent.

(3)

Every projective
R
-module is pseudo-coherent.

(4)

Every free
R
-module is pseudo-coherent.

Proof.

(1)\Rightarrow(2)
follows from
[
59
, Lemma 1.5]
. The implications
(2)\Rightarrow(3)
and
(3)\Rightarrow(4)
are straightforward. For
(4)\Rightarrow(1)
, note that if every free module is pseudo-coherent, then in particular
R
is pseudo-coherent. Hence, every finitely generated ideal of
R
is finitely presented, and thus
R
is left coherent.
∎

The following corollary is an immediate consequence of the previous result.

Corollary 3.5
.

For a ring
R
, the following are equivalent:

(1)

R
is left coherent.

(2)

All finitely generated projective
R
-modules are coherent.

(3)

The class of finitely generated projective
R
-modules coincides with the class of flat and coherent
R
-modules.

∎

Recall that an
R
-module
M
is
small
if there does not exist an infinite family of proper submodules
\{M_{j}\subsetneq M\mid j\in J\}
such that every element
m\in M
lies in all but finitely many
M_{j}
. It is well known that every finitely generated module is small. Moreover, if
M
is projective, then
M
is small if and only if it is finitely generated.

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
R
be a ring. Then
R
is left coherent if and only if every small projective
R
-module is coherent.

∎

Recall that an
R
-module
M
is said to be
\mathsf{f}
-projective
if, for every finitely generated submodule
C\subseteq M
, the inclusion map
C\hookrightarrow M
factors through a finitely generated free module. Every projective module is
\mathsf{f}
-projective, and any finitely generated
\mathsf{f}
-projective module is projective. Moreover, since flat modules are direct limits of finitely generated free modules, every
\mathsf{f}
-projective module is flat, see
[
15
, Lemma 3.2]
. The following proposition relates
\mathsf{f}
-projectivity to coherence.

Proposition 3.7
.

A ring
R
is left coherent if and only if every
\mathsf{f}
-projective
R
-module is pseudo-coherent. In this case, every flat and
\mathsf{FP}_{1}
-projective
R
-module is
\mathsf{f}
-projective.

Proof.

If
R
is left coherent, then the result follows directly from
[
15
, Lemma 3.5]
. Conversely, if every
\mathsf{f}
-projective module is pseudo-coherent, then in particular every projective
R
-module is pseudo-coherent. By Theorem
3.4
, this implies that
R
is left coherent. Finally, by Theorem
3.4
, every
\mathsf{FP}_{1}
-projective
R
-module is pseudo-coherent, and by
[
15
, Lemma 3.5]
again, every flat and
\mathsf{FP}_{1}
-projective module is
\mathsf{f}
-projective.
∎

Example 3.8
.

Over a Prüfer domain, the classes of flat and
\mathsf{f}
-projective modules coincide. Moreover, by
[
15
, Lemma 3.5]
, every flat
R
-module is pseudo-coherent.

Note that if every flat
R
-module is pseudo-coherent, then
R
is left coherent by Theorem
3.4
.

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
R
be a ring such that every flat
R
-module is pseudo-coherent. Then the class of flat
R
-modules coincides with the class of
\mathsf{f}
-projective modules. In particular,
R
contains no infinite set of pairwise orthogonal nonzero idempotents.

Proof.

Assume that every flat
R
-module is pseudo-coherent. Then, by
[
15
, Lemma 3.5]
, it follows that every flat module is
\mathsf{f}
-projective. In particular, every cyclic flat
R
-module is projective. Then, by
[
60
, Lemma 4.5]
,
R
does not admit an infinite set of pairwise orthogonal nonzero idempotents.
∎

Recall that a ring
R
is called an
\mathsf{S}
-ring
if every finitely generated flat
R
-module is projective.
Since every finitely generated flat module over a local ring is free, it follows that every local ring is an
\mathsf{S}
-ring.
Other examples of left
\mathsf{S}
-rings include left Noetherian rings and semiperfect rings; see
[
60
]
.

Corollary 3.10
.

Let
R
be a ring such that every flat
R
-module is pseudo-coherent. Then
R
is an
\mathsf{S}
-ring.

∎

3.1.
Von Neumann regularity.

Recall that a ring
R
is called a
von Neumann regular ring
if, for every element
r\in R
, there exists
x\in R
such that
r=rxr
. It is well known that
R
is von Neumann regular if and only if every
R
-module is
\mathsf{FP}_{1}
-injective.
Moreover, this condition can be verified by checking it only on ideals of
R
.

Proposition 3.11
.

Let
R
be a ring. The following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

R
is von Neumann regular.

(2)

Every nonzero proper ideal of
R
is
\mathsf{FP}_{1}
-injective.

(3)

Every nonzero principal ideal of
R
is
\mathsf{FP}_{1}
-injective.

Proof.

(1)\Rightarrow(2)\Rightarrow(3)
are clear.

(3)\Rightarrow(1):

It suffices to prove that every principal ideal of
R
is generated by an idempotent.
Let
I=(r)
be a nonzero principal ideal of
R
. Since
I
is cyclic, the short exact sequence
0\rightarrow I\rightarrow R\rightarrow R/I\rightarrow 0

splits, because by assumption
\Ext^{1}_{R}(R/I,I)=0
. Hence
I
is a direct summand of
R
, and therefore
I=Re
for some idempotent
e\in R
.
∎

Recall that a short exact sequence of
R
-modules
0\rightarrow F\rightarrow E\rightarrow G\rightarrow 0
is called
pure
if it remains exact under the functor
\Hom_{R}(M,-)
for every finitely presented
R
-module
M
.
It is well known that a ring
R
is von Neumann regular if and only if every short exact sequence of
R
-modules is pure.

Corollary 3.12
.

Let
R
be a ring. Then
R
is von Neumann regular if and only if, for every nonzero (principal) ideal
I\subseteq R
, the short exact sequence
0\rightarrow I\rightarrow E(I)\rightarrow E(I)/I\rightarrow 0
is pure.

∎

For any ring
R
, the pair
[\mathsf{FP}_{1}\text{-}\mathsf{Proj}(R),\mathsf{FP}_{1}\text{-}\mathsf{Inj}(R)]
forms a complete cotorsion pair. In particular, every
R
-module admits a special
\mathsf{FP}_{1}
-injective preenvelope and a special
\mathsf{FP}_{1}
-projective precover; that is, any
R
-module can be embedded into an
\mathsf{FP}_{1}
-injective module with cokernel in
\mathsf{FP}_{1}
-projectives, and can also be expressed as a quotient of an
\mathsf{FP}_{1}
-projective module by an
\mathsf{FP}_{1}
-injective submodule. Moreover, this cotorsion pair is hereditary if and only if
R
is a left coherent ring; that is,
\Ext^{i}_{R}(A,B)=0
for all
i\geq 1
, whenever
A\in\mathsf{FP}_{1}\text{-}\mathsf{Proj}(R)
and
B\in\mathsf{FP}_{1}\text{-}\mathsf{Inj}(R)
.

We now present a characterization of von Neumann regular rings in terms of the
\mathsf{FP}_{1}
-injectivity of a specific family of modules constructed from ideals, as shown in the following result.

Theorem 3.13
.

Let
R
be a left coherent ring. For each (principal) ideal
I\subseteq R
, consider a short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow A_{I}\longrightarrow B_{I}\longrightarrow I\longrightarrow 0

in
R\text{-Mod}
, where
A_{I}
is
\mathsf{FP}_{1}
-injective and
B_{I}
is
\mathsf{FP}_{1}
-projective. Define the set
\mathcal{S}:=\{B_{I}\}_{I\subseteq R}
. Then
R
is von Neumann regular if and only if
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathsf{FP}_{1}\text{-}\mathsf{Inj}(R)
.

Proof.

Assume that
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathsf{FP}_{1}\text{-}\mathsf{Inj}(R)
. Fix a (principal) ideal
I\subseteq R
and consider the short exact sequence
0\rightarrow A_{I}\rightarrow B_{I}\rightarrow I\rightarrow 0,

with
A_{I}\in\mathsf{FP}_{1}\text{-}\mathsf{Inj}(R)
and
B_{I}\in\mathsf{FP}_{1}\text{-}\mathsf{Proj}(R)
. For any finitely presented
R
-module
K
, the induced long exact sequence in
\Ext
gives

\cdots\longrightarrow\Ext^{1}_{R}(K,A_{I})\longrightarrow\Ext^{1}_{R}(K,B_{I})\longrightarrow\Ext^{1}_{R}(K,I)\longrightarrow\Ext^{2}_{R}(K,A_{I})\longrightarrow\cdots.

Since
A_{I}
is
\mathsf{FP}_{1}
-injective,
\Ext^{1}_{R}(K,A_{I})=\Ext^{2}_{R}(K,A_{I})=0
, so that

\Ext^{1}_{R}(K,B_{I})\cong\Ext^{1}_{R}(K,I).

Now,
B_{I}\in\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathsf{FP}_{1}\text{-}\mathsf{Inj}(R)
implies
\Ext^{1}_{R}(K,B_{I})=0
, and hence
\Ext^{1}_{R}(K,I)=0
. Therefore, every (principal) ideal
I\subseteq R
is
\mathsf{FP}_{1}
-injective, and
R
is von Neumann regular by Proposition
3.11
. The converse is immediate.
∎

Proposition 3.14
.

Let
R
be a ring. The following statements are equivalent:

(1)

is left Noetherian and von Neumann regular.

(2)

Every

-module is
\mathsf{FP}_{1}
-projective and flat.

(3)

(1)\Rightarrow(2):

If

-injective modules coincide, hence every

-module is
\mathsf{FP}_{1}
-projective. On the other hand, over a von Neumann regular ring, every module is flat. Therefore, every

-module is both
\mathsf{FP}_{1}
-projective and flat.

(2)\Rightarrow(3):
Suppose that every
R
-module is
\mathsf{FP}_{1}
-projective and flat. Then

is left Noetherian, and hence left coherent. Then Proposition
3.7
shows that every
R
-module is
\mathsf{f}
-projective. By
[

, Corollary 2.23]
, it follows that

is semisimple Artinian.

(3)\Rightarrow(1):

It is clear.
∎

Corollary 3.15

\mathsf{S}
-ring.

∎

.

.

4.
Regularity and Regular Coherence.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
R
be a ring. We say that:

(1)

R
is
left regular
if every finitely
\infty
-presented
R
-module has finite projective dimension.

(2)

R
is
left uniformly regular
if there exists an integer
k\geq 0
such that every finitely
\infty
-presented
R
-module has projective dimension at most
k
.

(3)

R
is
left Bertin-regular
if every finitely generated left ideal of
R
has finite projective dimension.

For a ring
R
, we denote by
\mathcal{F}_{k}(R)
(resp.
\mathcal{P}_{k}(R)
) the class of
R
-modules of flat (resp. projective) dimension at most
k
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
R
be a ring. Then:

(1)

R
is left regular if and only if every finitely
\infty
-presented
R
-module has finite flat dimension.

(2)

R
is left uniformly regular if and only if there exists an integer
k\geq 0
such that
\mathsf{FP}_{\infty}(R)\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{k}(R).

In the commutative local case, this is equivalent to

\Tor_{k}^{R}(M,R/\mathsf{m})=0

for every finitely
\infty
-presented
R
-module
M
, where
\mathsf{m}
is the maximal ideal of
R
.

Proof.

It suffices to show that
\mathsf{fd}_{R}(M)=\mathsf{pd}_{R}(M)
for every finitely

\infty
-presented
R
-module. Let
M
be a finitely
\infty
-presented
R
-module, and consider a projective resolution

\cdots\longrightarrow P_{n}\longrightarrow P_{n-1}\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow P_{1}\longrightarrow P_{0}\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow 0,

where each
P_{i}
is finitely generated projective and each syzygy

K_{i}=\ker(f_{i-1})
is finitely
\infty
-presented. If
\mathsf{fd}_{R}(M)=n<\infty
, then
K_{n}
is finitely presented and flat, hence projective. Thus
\mathsf{pd}_{R}(M)\leq n
.
∎

Classical examples of uniformly regular, regular, or Bertin-regular appear among several well-known families. For instance, semihereditary and von Neumann regular rings are uniformly regular, while rings of finite weak dimension are regular.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
R
be a ring. Then
R
is left regular if and only if every finitely
\infty
-presented submodule of a projective
R
-module has finite projective dimension.

Proof.

Since any
M\in\mathsf{FP}_{\infty}(R)
can be expressed as a quotient
F/N
, where
F
is a finitely generated projective
R
-module and
N\in\mathsf{FP}_{\infty}(R)
, the result follows.
∎

Inspired by
[
23
, Theorem 1.7]
, we can characterize uniform regularity in terms of Ext vanishing between finitely
\infty
-presented modules.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
R
be a ring. Then
R
is left uniformly regular if and only if there exists
k\geq 0
such that
\Ext_{R}^{k+1}(M,N)=0
for all finitely
\infty
-presented
R
-modules
M
and
N
.

Proof.

We only need to prove that if
\Ext_{R}^{k+1}(M,N)=0
for all finitely
\infty
-presented modules
N
, then
\mathsf{pd}_{R}(M)\leq k
for every finitely
\infty
-presented module
M
. For
k=0
, take a short exact sequence
0\to K\to F\to M\to 0
, where
F
is finitely generated and projective, and
K
is finitely
\infty
-presented. Since
\Ext_{R}^{1}(K,M)=0
, the sequence splits and

is projective. For general

, apply dimension shifting and argue by induction.
∎

The following proposition is well known.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
R
be a ring. The following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

Every ideal of
R
has finite projective dimension.

(2)

Every cyclic
R
-module has finite projective dimension.

(3)

Every finitely generated
R
-module has finite projective dimension.

Moreover, if
R
is left Noetherian, then the above are also equivalent to:

(4)

Every finitely generated ideal of
R
has finite projective dimension (i.e.,
R
is left Bertin-regular).

Proof.

(1)\Rightarrow(2):
Is clear.

(2)\Rightarrow(3):
Let
M
be a finitely generated
R
-module. By
[
63
, Lemma 3.5]
, there exists a finite sequence of short exact sequences

0\longrightarrow R/I_{k}\longrightarrow M_{k-1}\longrightarrow M_{k}\longrightarrow 0\quad(1\leq k\leq n),

with
M_{0}=M
and
M_{n}=0
, for some ideals
I_{k}\subseteq R
. Since each
R/I_{k}
has finite projective dimension by hypothesis, it follows that
\mathrm{pd}_{R}(M)<\infty
.

(3)\Rightarrow(1):
Is clear.

(4)\Leftrightarrow(1):
If
R
is left Noetherian, then every left ideal of

is finitely generated.
∎

An example of a regular ring that is not Noetherian is the polynomial ring in countably many variables over a principal ideal domain.

Remark 4.6
.

The condition of Bertin-regularity on a ring
R
, or even the stronger condition that every finitely generated ideal has uniformly bounded projective dimension (specifically bounded by 1), does not guarantee coherence. For example, consider the quiver
Q
with three vertices: one arrow
\alpha
from vertex 1 to vertex 2, and infinitely many arrows
\{\beta_{s}\mid s\in S\}
from vertex 2 to vertex 3. Let
R
denote the quotient of the path algebra of
Q
over a field
k
by the ideal generated by
\{\alpha\beta_{s}\mid s\in S\}
. In this case, the supremum of the projective dimensions of finitely generated right ideals of
R
is 1. Furthermore,
\mathsf{gl.D}(R)=2
; see
[
50
, Example 3]
, yet
R
is not right coherent.

We denote the cardinality of a ring
R
by
|R|
.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
R
be a ring with
|R|\leq\aleph_{k}
for some integer
k\geq 0
. If every finitely generated ideal of
R
has finite flat dimension, then
R
is left Bertin-regular.

Proof.

By
[
61
, Theorem]
, over any ring of cardinality at most
\aleph_{k}
, every flat module has projective dimension at most
k+1
. Let
I\subseteq R
be a finitely generated left ideal with
\mathsf{fd}_{R}(I)=n<\infty
. Consider a finite flat resolution

0\longrightarrow F_{n}\xrightarrow{d_{n}}F_{n-1}\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow F_{1}\xrightarrow{d_{1}}F_{0}\xrightarrow{d_{0}}I\longrightarrow 0,

where each
F_{i}
is flat. Since each
F_{i}
admits a finite projective resolution of length at most
k+1
, the Horseshoe Lemma produces a finite projective resolution of
I
. Hence
\mathsf{pd}_{R}(I)<\infty
, and therefore
R
is left Bertin-regular.
∎

Corollary 4.8
.

Let
R
be a ring with
|R|\leq\aleph_{k}
for some integer
k\geq 0
, and set
S=R[x_{1},x_{2},\dots]
. If every finitely generated ideal of

has finite flat dimension, then

is left Bertin-regular.

It suffices to observe that
|S|=\max\{|R|,\aleph_{0}\}=\aleph_{k}.

∎

Remark 4.9
.

Let
R
be a commutative ring and let
\mathsf{Q}(R)
denote its total ring of fractions. By
[

, Theorem 4.12]
,
|R|=|\mathsf{Q}(R)|
. Hence, for any

, if every finitely generated ideal of

has finite flat dimension, then

is left Bertin-regular.

Proposition 4.10
.

Let
R
be a left coherent ring. The following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

R
is a left regular ring.

(2)

R
is a left Bertin-regular ring.

(3)

Every finitely generated ideal of
R
has finite flat dimension.

(4)

Every cyclic finitely presented
R
-module has finite flat dimension.

Proof.

The equivalence
(1)\Leftrightarrow(2)
is given by
[
36
, Theorem 6.2.1]
. Since
R
is left coherent, every finitely generated ideal is finitely presented, hence finitely
\infty
-presented. Thus, by Proposition
4.2
, we obtain
(2)\Leftrightarrow(3)
. Finally,
(3)\Leftrightarrow(4)
holds in general, since cyclic finitely presented modules are exactly of the form
R/I
with

finitely generated.
∎

Recall that an
R
-module
M
is said to be
\mathcal{C}
-periodic
if there exists a short exact sequence
0\rightarrow M\rightarrow C\rightarrow M\rightarrow 0

with
C\in\mathcal{C}
. For a ring

, the injectivity of an
R
-module

can be characterized via
[
20
, Theorem 3.2]
:

is injective if and only if it is
\mathsf{Inj}(R)
-periodic with respect to a

exact sequence; that is, there exists a pure exact sequence
0\rightarrow M\rightarrow C\rightarrow M\rightarrow 0

with

an injective module. However, when

is left Bertin-regular, the purity assumption can be dropped, as the following result shows:

Proposition 4.11
.

Let
R
be a left Bertin-regular ring, and let
M
be an
R
-module. Then

is injective if and only if it is
\mathsf{Inj}(R)
-periodic. In particular, every
\mathsf{Inj}(R)
-periodic module over a left coherent regular ring is injective.

Proof.

If
M
is injective, the canonical short exact sequence
0\to M\to M\oplus M\to M\to 0
shows that
M
is
\mathsf{Inj}(R)
-periodic. The converse follows from
[
13
, Proposition 4.8]
.
∎

Remark 4.12
.

We denote by
\mathsf{FP}_{1}(R)^{<\infty}
the class of finitely presented
R
-modules with finite projective dimension. A module

belongs to the class
\varinjlim\mathsf{FP}_{1}(R)^{<\infty}
if it can be expressed as a direct limit of modules from
\mathsf{FP}_{1}(R)^{<\infty}
. The class
\varinjlim\mathsf{FP}_{1}(R)^{<\infty}
is closed under arbitrary direct sums, pure submodules, pure extensions, and pure epimorphic images; in particular, it is closed under direct limits and forms a covering class; see
[
58
]
. Moreover, if

is coherent regular, then
\varinjlim\mathsf{FP}_{1}(R)^{<\infty}=R\mbox{-}\mathsf{Mod}
, since every module is a filtered colimit of finitely presented modules.

Proposition 4.13
.

Let
R
be a left coherent ring. Then

\varinjlim\mathsf{FP_{1}(R)}^{<\infty}=[^{\top_{1}}(\mathsf{FP_{1}(R)}^{<\infty})]^{\top_{1}}.

Proof.

Suppose that
R
is left coherent. Then
\mathsf{FP_{1}(R)}^{<\infty}\subseteq\mathsf{FP_{2}(R)}
.
Since
\mathsf{FP_{1}(R)}^{<\infty}
is closed under extensions and direct summands, it follows from
[
58
, Lemma 2.1]
that
\varinjlim\mathsf{FP_{1}(R)}^{<\infty}=[^{\top_{1}}(\mathsf{FP_{1}(R)}^{<\infty})]^{\top_{1}}.

∎

Theorem 4.14
.

Let
R
be a left coherent ring. The following are equivalent:

(1)

Every finitely presented
R
-module has finite projective dimension; that is,

is left regular.

(2)

Every finitely generated submodule of a projective

-module has finite projective dimension.

(3)

The kernel of any map between finitely generated projective

-modules has finite projective dimension.

Proof.

(1)
\Leftrightarrow
(2): Follows from Proposition
4.3
.

(1)
\Rightarrow
(3): Assume that

be a left coherent regular ring. By the corollary

, every finitely generated projective

-module is coherent. Thus, for any map
P_{0}\to P_{1}
with

and

finitely generated and projective, the kernel

is coherent, and therefore finitely presented. It follows that
\mathsf{pd}_{R}(K)<\infty
.

(3)
\Rightarrow
(1): Let
I\subseteq R
be a finitely generated ideal. Since

is left coherent,

is finitely presented, so there exists an exact sequence of
R
-modules

where

is finitely generated and

is finitely generated projective. Extending this sequence, we obtain

By assumption,

.
∎

It is well known that a ring

is coherent regular if and only if the matrix ring

. This follows from the fact that the categories

Theorem 4.15
.

Let
R
be a ring. Then

is left coherent regular if and only if, for every

, the matrix ring

is left
\mathsf{P}
-coherent and every principal ideal of

has finite projective dimension as an

-module.

The necessity is straightforward, and we thus prove only the sufficiency. Assume that
M_{n}(R)
is left
\mathsf{P}
-coherent for every

and that every principal ideal of
M_{n}(R)
has finite projective dimension as an
M_{n}(R)
-module. By
[

, Proposition 2.4]
,

is left coherent.

Now, let

be a finitely generated ideal of

. Consider the matrix

r_{2}&0&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
r_{n}&0&\cdots&0\end{pmatrix}\in M_{n}(R),

where

. Then, the principal left ideal

has finite projective dimension over

Recall that for a ring
R
, the
dual
of an
R
-module

is the right

-module

, where the action is given by

for all

,

, and

. It is well known that an

-module

is finitely generated projective of rank

if and only if the canonical map

is an isomorphism; in this case,
M^{*}
is also finitely generated projective of rank

. Moreover, an

-module

is said to be

if the natural map
\phi:M\to\Hom_{R}(M^{*},R)
, given by
\phi(m)(f)=f(m)
, is injective. Equivalently,

is torsionless if it embeds into a free
R
-module.

A ring
R
is von Neumann regular if and only if it is left coherent regular and left self
\mathsf{FP}_{1}
-injective. Although this characterization will be established later in a more general setting; see Proposition
7.9
, we can already deduce the following consequence from
[

and the previous result.

Corollary 4.16
.

Let
R
be a ring. Then
R
is von Neumann regular if and only if, for every

, the following conditions hold:

(1)

Every finitely presented cyclic right
M_{n}(R)
-module is torsionless.

(2)

The matrix ring
M_{n}(R)
is left
\mathsf{P}
-coherent, and every principal ideal of
M_{n}(R)
has finite projective dimension as an
M_{n}(R)
-module.

∎

Recall that an ideal
I
of

is called a
\mathsf{W}
-ideal
if it is isomorphic to a quotient of the dual module of a finitely generated right
R
-module. By
[

, Theorem 1]
, a ring

is left coherent if and only if every

.

Let
R
be a ring. Then

\mathsf{W}
-ideal and finitely generated ideal of

\mathsf{W}

is left coherent by
[

, Theorem 1]

is finitely generated. Thus,

. Note that

\mathsf{W}

is left regular.

Recall that the

\mathop{\rm\mathsf{FP}_{1}\text{-}ID}\nolimits(R)

\mathsf{sfindim}(R)

Remark 4.23

For any element

4.1.1.

Matlis introduced an alternative notion of regularity, defining a ring to be

.

[

is projective. Hence

is a direct summand of

. The converse is clear.
∎

Recall that if

4.1.2.
Stably Coherent Rings.

In several ways, coherent rings differ significantly from Noetherian rings, most notably in the failure of the "basis theorem". Specifically, if a ring
R
is coherent, the polynomial ring
R[x]
in one indeterminate over
R
is not necessarily coherent. For instance, Soublin showed that
R=\prod_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{Q}[[x,y]]
is coherent, but
R[x]
is not; see
[
36
, Example 7.3.13]
. A ring
R
is called
n
-stably coherent
if
R[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
is coherent, and

if this holds for every
n\geq 1
. In fact, if

is
n
-stably coherent for all

, then the polynomial ring in countably many indeterminates

is also coherent
[
36
, Example 2.3.3]
. The class of stably coherent rings is stable under localization, quotients by finitely generated ideals, and finitely presented extensions
[
36
, Theorems 2.4.1–2.4.2]
. Moreover, any coherent ring of global dimension at most
2
, or of weak global dimension at most
1
(i.e., semihereditary), is stably coherent. On the other hand, a commutative ring of global dimension
2
need not be coherent.

Proposition 4.36
.

Let
R
be a domain with global dimension
2
. Then
R
is stably coherent.

Proof.

By
[
71
, Corollary 2 and Remark]
,
R
is either a Prüfer domain or a Noetherian domain.
∎

Coherent rings with weak global dimension

, including certain domains, need not be stably coherent; see
[
36
, Theorem 7.3.13]
.

(1)

6.
Vogel-Regularity.

A more general notion of regularity for rings and exact categories was introduced by Vogel; see
[
67
]
. Let
\mathcal{C}
be a class in
R\mbox{-}\mathsf{Mod}
. We say that the class
\mathcal{C}
is
Vogel
if it satisfies the following conditions:

(1)

\mathcal{C}
is closed under direct limits.

(2)

\mathcal{C}
has the 2-out-of-3 property with respect to
R\mbox{-}\mathsf{Mod}
.

(3)

\mathcal{C}
contains all finitely generated projective
R
-modules.

If
\mathcal{C}
is a Vogel class, then it is also closed under direct summands; see
[
58
, Remark 2]
. Moreover, if
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
denotes the class of all direct summands of modules in
\mathcal{C}
, we have
\mathcal{C}=\varinjlim\mathcal{C}=\varinjlim\mathcal{C}^{\prime}.

Definition 6.1
.

Let
R
be a ring. We say that
R
is left
Vogel-regular
if the only Vogel class in
R\mbox{-}\mathsf{Mod}
is
R\mbox{-}\mathsf{Mod}
itself.

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
R
be a left
n
-coherent regular ring with
n\geq 0
, and let
\mathcal{C}
be a Vogel class of
R
-modules. Then
\mathsf{FP}_{n}(R)\subseteq\mathcal{C}
.

Proof.

Every finitely
n
-presented
R
-module
M
has a finite projective resolution

0\longrightarrow P_{k}\longrightarrow\dots\longrightarrow P_{1}\longrightarrow P_{0}\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow 0,

with each
P_{i}
finitely generated and projective.
Since
\mathcal{C}
contains all finitely generated projective modules and satisfies the
2
-out-of-
3
property, splitting the resolution into short exact sequences implies that
M\in\mathcal{C}
.
∎

If
R
is a left
n
-coherent ring, the class
\mathsf{FP}_{n}(R)
satisfies the 2-out-of-3 property with respect to short exact sequences. Consequently,
\mathsf{FP}_{n}(R)
forms a Vogel class if and only if it is closed under direct limits. This observation yields the following corollary:

Corollary 6.3
.

Let
R
be a left
n
-coherent regular ring. Then the class
\mathsf{FP}_{n}(R)
is closed under direct limits if and only if it coincides with the smallest Vogel class in
R\mbox{-}\mathsf{Mod}
.

∎

Note that if
n\geq 1
and
\mathsf{FP}_{n}(R)
is closed under direct limits, then every flat module, being a direct limit of finitely generated free modules (which are in particular
n
-finitely presented), belongs to
\mathsf{FP}_{n}(R)
. It follows that all flat modules are projective, and consequently,
R
is left perfect.

Theorem 6.4
.

Let
R
be a left Vogel-regular ring. Then, for some
n\geq 2
, the class
[\mathsf{FP}_{n}\text{-}\mathsf{Flat}(R^{op})]^{\top_{1}}

satisfies the 2-out-of-3 property in

if and only if

is left coherent.

Proof.

Suppose that the class
[\mathsf{FP}_{n}\text{-}\mathsf{Flat}(R^{op})]^{\top_{1}}
satisfies the 2-out-of-3 property for some
n\geq 2
. By
[
58
, Lemma 2.1]
, the class

\mathcal{L}:=\varinjlim\mathsf{FP}_{n}(R^{op})

is closed under direct limits and coincides with
[\mathsf{FP}_{n}\text{-}\mathsf{Flat}(R^{op})]^{\top_{1}}
. Since
\mathcal{L}
contains all projective left
R
-modules, it follows that
\mathcal{L}
is a Vogel class. By the Vogel-regularity of
R
, this implies
\mathcal{L}=R\mbox{-}\mathsf{Mod}
. In particular, every left
R
-module is a direct limit of finitely
n
-presented modules. Then, by
[
49
, Corollary 5.3]
,
R
is left coherent. The converse is straightforward.
∎

Over coherent rings, Vogel-regularity coincides with classical regularity
[
67
, Theorem 1]
.

Corollary 6.5
.

Let
R
be a ring. Then
R
is left coherent regular if and only if
R
is left Vogel-regular and the class

[\mathsf{FP}_{n}\text{-}\mathsf{Flat}(R^{op})]^{\top_{1}}

satisfies the 2-out-of-3 property in
R\mbox{-}\mathsf{Mod}
for some
n\geq 2
.

∎

7.

The study of module classes with projective dimension bounded by some positive integer provides large insight to both the module category over the ring, as well as intrinsic properties of the ring itselef. Let

8.
Finiteness Conditions and
\mathsf{K}_{0}
-Regularity.

In 1973, Quillen introduced the concept of
\mathsf{K}_{i}
-groups for exact categories. By considering the specific case of the category of finitely generated projective modules, one obtains the
\mathsf{K}_{i}
-groups of a ring. For any ring
R
, let
\mathbf{P}(R)
denote the set of isomorphism classes of finitely generated projective
R
-modules. This set, equipped with the direct sum operation
\oplus
and the identity element
0
, forms an abelian monoid. The Grothendieck group
\mathsf{K}_{0}(R)
is defined as the quotient of the free abelian group generated by the isomorphism classes
[P]
of finitely generated projective modules
P\in\mathsf{proj}(R)
, by the subgroup generated by elements of the form
[P\oplus Q]-[P]-[Q]
for all
P,Q\in\mathsf{proj}(R)
. When
R
is a commutative ring, the tensor product of two
R
-modules is again an
R
-module, with the property that
r\cdot(x\otimes y)=rx\otimes y=x\otimes ry
for all
r\in R
,
x\in M
, and
y\in N
, where
M
and
N
are
R
-modules. Since
R^{m}\otimes_{R}R^{n}\cong R^{mn}
, it follows that
\mathsf{Proj}(R)
is closed under the tensor product
\otimes_{R}
. Consequently, the operation
[P][Q]=[P\otimes_{R}Q]
induces a multiplication on
\mathsf{K}_{0}(R)
, making
\mathsf{K}_{0}(R)
into a commutative ring, with identity element
[R]
.

For
n\geq 0
and
R
a left
n
-coherent regular ring, the natural inclusion
\mathsf{Proj}(R)\hookrightarrow\mathsf{FP}_{n}(R)

induces isomorphisms
\mathsf{K}_{i}(R)\cong\mathsf{K}_{i}(\mathsf{FP}_{n}(R))\quad\text{for all }i\geq 0,

see
[
31
, Theorem 3.2]
. In the case where

is a left coherent regular ring, Swan
[
62
]
proved that the natural map

[M]\mapsto[R[t]\otimes_{R}M]

induces an isomorphism

\mathsf{K}_{0}(R)\cong\mathsf{K}_{0}(R[t]).

However, as Swan remarks in
[
62
, Remark 5.4]
, since
R[t]
need not be coherent even if
R
is, it remains unclear whether this result can be extended to

for

.

A ring

is called

-regular
if, for every integer

, the natural homomorphism induced by the inclusion

is an isomorphism. In the commutative case,

-regularity holds under a regularity assumption:

Proposition 8.1
.

[
68
, Theorem 5.7]
Every commutative regular ring is
\mathsf{K}_{0}
-regular.

As a consequence of Propositions

,
4.10
, and

-regular:

(1)

is uniformly regular.

(2)

is uniformly

-regular for some

(3)

n

Proposition 8.11
.

Let
\mathsf{X}
be an
\mathsf{F}
-space. Then
\mathsf{C(X)}
is a
2
-coherent regular ring, and consequently,
\mathsf{C(X)}
is
\mathsf{K}_{0}
-regular.

Proof.

Since
\mathsf{C(X)}
is a reduced arithmetical ring, we have
\mathsf{w.D}(\mathsf{C(X)})\leq 1
. Moreover, by
[
26
, Theorem II.1]
,
\mathsf{C(X)}
is
2
-coherent. Therefore,
\mathsf{C(X)}
is a
2
-coherent regular ring, and
\mathsf{K}_{0}
-regular by Corollary
8.2
.
∎

A completely regular space
\mathsf{X}
is called a
\mathsf{T}
-space
if the ring
\mathsf{C(X)}
is a Hermite ring. Consequently, every
\mathsf{T}
-space is an
\mathsf{F}
-space. However, it is known that there exist
\mathsf{F}
-spaces that are not
\mathsf{T}
-spaces; see
[
40
, Example 3.4]
.

Proposition 8.12
.

If
\mathsf{X}
is basically disconnected, then
\mathsf{X}
is a
\mathsf{T}
-space. In particular,
\mathsf{C(X)}
is
\mathsf{K}_{0}
-regular.

Proof.

If
\mathsf{X}
is basically disconnected, then
\mathsf{C(X)}
is semihereditary
[
56
, Fact 9.16]
, hence a Bézout ring
[
10
, Lemma 5.12]
, and therefore Hermite
[
27
, Corollary 3.2]
. This shows that
\mathsf{X}
is a
\mathsf{T}
-space.
∎

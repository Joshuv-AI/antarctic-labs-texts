1.
B_{h}
-subsets of a group

Let
G
be an additive abelian group or semigroup and let
A
be a nonempty subset of
G
.
For every positive integer
h
, the
h
-fold
sumset
of
A
is the set of all sums of
h
not necessarily distinct elements of
A
:

hA=\underbrace{A+\cdots+A}_{\text{$h$ summands}}=\left\{a^{\prime}_{1}+\cdots+a^{\prime}_{h}:a^{\prime}_{i}\in A\text{ for all }i\in[1,h]\right\}.

For every element
g\in G
,
the representation function
r_{A,h}(g)
counts the number of
representations of
g
as a sum of
h
not necessarily distinct elements
of
A
. Representations that differ only by a permutation of the summands
are not counted separately.
The set
A
is a
B_{h}
-set
if
r_{A,h}(g)=0
or
1
for all
g\in G
.
Thus, a
B_{h}
-set is a set
A
such that the representation of an element of the
semigroup as the sum of
h
elements of
A
is unique, up to permutation of the summands.

B_{2}
-sets are also called
Sidon sets
.

The study of
B_{h}
-sets is a classical topic in combinatorial additive number theory
(O’Bryant
[
15
]
). Although “almost all” finite sets of integers are
B_{h}
-sets
(Nathanson
[
9
]
) and there are many papers estimating the size such sets,
there are few explicit constructions of them
(cf.
[
1
]
–
[
16
]
).
This paper gives a simple
\mathbf{Q}
-vector space construction of finite
B_{h}
-sets
for the group
\mathbf{Z}
of integers and the group
\mathbf{Z}^{d}
of integer lattice points.

Let
\mathbf{N}=\{1,2,3,\ldots\}
be the set of positive integers and

\mathbf{N}_{0}=\mathbf{N}\cup\{0\}=\{0,1,2,3\ldots\}
the set of nonnegative integers.
For
u,v\in\mathbf{R}
, the
interval of integers

[u,v]

is the set of all integers
k
such that
u\leq k\leq v
.
The
integer part
of the real number
u
(also called the
floor
of
u
),
is the largest integer
n\leq u
.

2.
The set
\mathcal{X}_{h,n}

For positive integers
h
and
n
, let

\mathcal{X}_{h,n}=\left\{\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbf{N}_{0}^{n}:\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}=h\right\}.

We have
\left|\mathcal{X}_{h,n}\right|=\binom{n+h-1}{h}
.
The set
\mathcal{X}_{h,n}
is symmetric in the sense that if
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{h})\in\mathcal{X}_{h,n}
,
then
\sigma\mathbf{x}=\left(x_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,x_{\sigma(n)}\right)\in\mathcal{X}_{h,n}

for every permutation
\sigma
of
[1,n]
.

Let
A=\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\}
be a finite subset of an additive abelian
semigroup with
|A|=n
.
The
h
-fold sumset of
A
can be written in the form

(1)

hA=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}a_{i}:\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathcal{X}_{h,n}\right\}.

For all
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathcal{X}_{h,n}
and
\mathbf{y}=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})\in\mathcal{X}_{h,n}

with
\mathbf{x}\neq\mathbf{y}
, we have

(2)

2\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}|x_{i}-y_{i}|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}=2h.

These upper and lower bounds are best possible.
If
n\geq h
and

\mathbf{x}=(2,0,\underbrace{1,1,\ldots,1}_{\text{$(h-2)$ 1's}},\underbrace{0,0,\ldots,0}_{\text{$(n-h)$ 0's}})

and

\mathbf{y}=(1,1,\underbrace{1,\ldots,1}_{\text{$(h-2)$ 1's}},\underbrace{0,0,\ldots,0}_{\text{$(n-h)$ 0's}})

then
\sum_{i=1}^{n}|x_{i}-y_{i}|=2
.

If
n\geq 2h
and

\mathbf{x}=(\underbrace{1,1,\ldots,1}_{\text{$h$ 1's}},\underbrace{0,0,\ldots,0}_{\text{$h$ 0's}}\underbrace{0,0,\ldots,0}_{\text{$(n-2h)$ 0's}})

and

\mathbf{y}=(\underbrace{0,0,,\ldots,0}_{\text{$h$ 1's}},\underbrace{1,1,\ldots,1}_{\text{$h$ 1's}},\underbrace{0,0,\ldots,0}_{\text{$(n-2h)$ 0's}})

then
\sum_{i=1}^{n}|x_{i}-y_{i}|=2h
.

3.
B_{h}
-sets of lattice points

The
\ell^{\infty}
-norm of the vector
\vec{\mathbf{a}}=(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{d})\in\mathbf{R}^{d}
is

\|\vec{\mathbf{a}}\|_{\infty}=\max_{j\in[1,d]}|a_{j}|.

The
\ell^{\infty}
-norm of the set of vectors
A=\{\vec{\mathbf{a}}_{1},\ldots,\vec{\mathbf{a}}_{n}\}
is

\|A\|_{\infty}=\max_{i\in[1,n]}\|\vec{\mathbf{a}}_{i}\|_{\infty}.

Let
\left(\vec{\theta}_{1},\vec{\theta}_{2},\ldots,\vec{\theta}_{n}\right)

be an
n
-tuple of
\mathbf{Q}
-independent vectors in
\mathbf{R}^{d}

and let

\Theta_{n}=\left\{\vec{\theta}_{1},\vec{\theta}_{2},\ldots,\vec{\theta}_{n}\right\}

and

\|\Theta_{n}\|_{\infty}=\max_{i\in[1,n]}\|\vec{\theta}_{i}\|_{\infty}.

For all
i\in[1,n]
, we write

\vec{\theta}_{i}=(\theta_{i,1},\theta_{i,2},\ldots,\theta_{i,d}).

The
\mathbf{Q}
-independence of the set
\Theta_{n}
implies that,
for all
\vec{\mathbf{x}},\vec{\mathbf{y}}\in\mathcal{X}_{h,n}

with
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
,
\mathbf{y}=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})
,
and
\vec{\mathbf{x}}\neq\vec{\mathbf{y}}
, we have

\sum_{i=1}^{h}x_{i}\vec{\theta}_{i}\neq\sum_{i=1}^{h}y_{i}\vec{\theta}_{i}.

Equivalently,

\sum_{i=1}^{n}(x_{i}-y_{i})\vec{\theta}_{i}\neq\mathbf{0}.

Inequality (
2
) gives

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle<\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}(x_{i}-y_{i})\vec{\theta}_{i}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\|\Theta_{n}\|_{\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|x_{i}-y_{i}|\leq 2h\|\Theta_{n}\|_{\infty}.

Because the set
\mathcal{X}_{h,n}
is finite, we have

(3)

0<\varepsilon_{h,n}=\inf\left\{\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}(x_{i}-y_{i})\vec{\theta}_{i}\right\|_{\infty}:\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\mathcal{X}_{h,n}\text{ and }\mathbf{x}\neq\mathbf{y}\right\}.

Let
q
and
m
be positive integers. For all
i\in[1,n]
and
j\in[1,d]
,
there are exactly
2m
integers
a_{i,j}

such that

(4)

0<|a_{i,j}-q\theta_{i,j}|\leq m.

For every integer
a_{i,j}
such that
|a_{i,j}-q\theta_{i,j}|\leq m
, we have

\left|\frac{a_{i,j}}{q}-\theta_{i,j}\right|\leq\frac{m}{q}.

Note that if
\theta_{i,j}\geq 0
, then there are at least
m
positive integers
a_{i,j}

that satisfy inequality (
4
).

For all
i\in[1,n]
, we obtain
(2m)^{d}
lattice points

\vec{a}_{i}=(a_{i,1},a_{i,2},\ldots,a_{i,d})\in\mathbf{Z}^{d}

and
(2m)^{d}
rational vectors

\frac{1}{q}\vec{a}_{i}=\left(\frac{a_{i,1}}{q},\frac{a_{i,2}}{q},\ldots,,\frac{a_{i,d}}{q}\right)\in\mathbf{Q}^{d}.

These vectors approximate
\vec{\theta}_{i}
:

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{q}\vec{a}_{i}-\vec{\theta}_{i}\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\left\|\left(\frac{a_{i,1}}{q}-\theta_{i,1},\frac{a_{i,2}}{q}-\theta_{i,2},\ldots,\frac{a_{i,d}}{q}-\theta_{i,d}\right)\right\|_{\infty}

(5)

\displaystyle=\max_{j\in[1,d]}\left|\frac{a_{i,j}}{q}-\theta_{i,j}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{m}{q}.

Thus, we have constructed
(2m)^{dn}
sets of lattice points

A_{h,n}(q,m)=\{\vec{a}_{1},\ldots,\vec{a}_{n}\}\subseteq\mathbf{Z}^{d}.

These sets are determined by the positive integers
q
and
m
and by the choices of the

dn
coordinates
a_{i,j}
.

If
\vec{\theta}_{i}
is a nonnegative vector, then we can choose coordinates
a_{i,j}

so that the lattice point
\vec{\mathbf{a}}_{i}
has positive coordinates.
If
\Theta
is a set of nonnegative vectors,
then we can choose positive coordinates
a_{i,j}
for all
n
vectors
in the set
A_{h,n}(q,m)
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\Theta_{n}=\{\vec{\theta}_{1},\vec{\theta}_{2},\ldots,\vec{\theta}_{n}\}

be a set of
\mathbf{Q}
-independent vectors in
\mathbf{R}^{d}
.
Let
h\geq 2
and define
\varepsilon_{h,n}
by (
3
).
For all positive integers
q
and
m
with

q>\frac{2hm}{\varepsilon_{h,n}}

the
(2m)^{dn}
sets
A_{h,n}(q,m)

constructed from
\Theta_{n}
are
B_{h}
-sets of lattice points in
\mathbf{Z}^{d}
with

(6)

\|A_{h,m}(q)\|_{\infty}\leq q\|\Theta_{n}\|_{\infty}+m.

Proof.

Let
A_{h,n}(q,m)=\{\vec{a}_{1},\vec{a}_{2},\ldots,\vec{a}_{n}\}
.
Inequality (
4
) implies that, for all
i\in[1,n]
and
j\in[1,d]
,

|a_{i,j}|\leq q|\theta_{i,j}|+m\leq q\left\|\vec{\theta}_{i}\right\|_{\infty}+m

and so

\|\vec{a}_{i}\|_{\infty}\leq q\|\vec{\theta}_{i}\|_{\infty}+m\leq q\|\Theta_{n}\|_{\infty}+m.

This immediately gives the upper bound (
6
).

We shall prove that
A_{h,n}(q,m)
is a
B_{h}
-set of lattice points in
\mathbf{Z}^{n}
.
By formula (
1
), we have the
h
-fold sumset

hA_{h,n}(q,m)=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\vec{a}_{i}:\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})\in\mathcal{X}_{h,n}\right\}.

Let
\vec{\mathbf{x}}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathcal{X}_{h,n}
and
\vec{\mathbf{y}}=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})\in\mathcal{X}_{h,n}

with
\mathbf{x}\neq\mathbf{y}
. Applying inequalities (
2
)
and (
5
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}(x_{i}-y_{i})\left(\frac{1}{q}\vec{a}_{i}-\vec{\theta}_{i}\right)\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}|x_{i}-y_{i}|\left\|\frac{1}{q}\vec{a}_{i}-\vec{\theta}_{i}\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{m}{q}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|x_{i}-y_{i}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2hm}{q}

and so

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q}

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\vec{a}_{i}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}\vec{a}_{i}\right\|_{\infty}=\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}(x_{i}-y_{i})\frac{1}{q}\vec{a}_{i}\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}(x_{i}-y_{i})\vec{\theta}_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(x_{i}-y_{i})\left(\frac{1}{q}\vec{a}_{i}-\vec{\theta}_{i}\right)\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\geq\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}(x_{i}-y_{i})\vec{\theta}_{i}\right\|_{\infty}-\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}(x_{i}-y_{i})\left(\frac{1}{q}\vec{a}_{i}-\vec{\theta}_{i}\right)\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\geq\varepsilon_{h,n}-\frac{2hm}{q}.

Choosing an integer

q>\frac{2hm}{\varepsilon_{h,n}}

we obtain

\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\vec{a}_{i}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}\vec{a}_{i}\right\|_{\infty}\geq q\varepsilon_{h,n}-2hm>0

and so

A_{h,n}(q,m)=\{\vec{a}_{1},\ldots,\vec{a}_{n}\}
is a
B_{h}
-set of lattice points.
This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 3
.

Let
\Theta_{n}=\{\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{n}\}
be a
\mathbf{Q}
-independent set
of positive real numbers. For every integer
g\geq 2
, let

\theta_{i}
have the
g
-adic expansion

\theta_{i}=\sum_{j=0}^{j_{0}}c^{\prime}_{i,j}g^{j}+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{c_{i,j}}{g^{j}}

with digits
c_{i,j},c^{\prime}_{i,j}\in[0,g-1]
.
For every positive integer
\ell
, let

a_{i,\ell}=\sum_{j=0}^{j_{0}}c^{\prime}_{i,j}g^{\ell+j}+\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}c_{i,j}g^{\ell-j}.

There exists
\ell_{0}=\ell_{0}(\Theta_{n})
such that, for all
\ell\geq\ell_{0}
, the set

B_{2,n}(\ell)=\{a_{1,\ell},a_{2,\ell},\ldots,a_{n,\ell}\}

is a Sidon set of positive integers.

Note that
a_{i,\ell}
is the integer part of
g^{\ell}\theta_{i}
.

Proof.

For all
i\in[1,n]
, we have

0<\theta_{i}-\frac{a_{i,\ell}}{g^{\ell}}<\frac{1}{g^{\ell}}.

Apply Theorem
2
with

q=g^{\ell}>\frac{4}{\varepsilon_{2,n}(\Theta_{n})}.

This completes the proof.

The influence of preoperative neoadjuvant chemotherapy on the incidence of post-induction hypotension in patients with ovarian cancer– a retrospective observational cohort study
Authors: Cheng-Yang Zhang, Zi-Long Zhang, Fei Xing, Cai-Li Gou, Chao-Bang Ma, Yan-Zi Chang, Zhong-Yu Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeNeoadjuvant chemotherapy has become one of the initial treatment modalities for advanced ovarian cancer. Previous studies have indicated that preoperative neoadjuvant chemotherapy may reduce patients’ requirements for anesthetic agents. This study aims to investigate the effect of neoadjuvant chemotherapy on post-induction hypotension.Patients and methodsThis study enrolled a total of 532 patients, with 482 included in the primary analysis. Multivariable logistic regression and propensity score matching were used to analyze the association between Neoadjuvant chemotherapy and post-induction hypotension. Multiple sensitivity analyses were performed, including the use of alternative MAP thresholds, multiple imputation, inverse probability weighting, and E-value methods.ResultsThis study enrolled a total of 532 patients, of whom 482 were included in the primary analysis. After propensity score matching, each group included 161 patients. The incidence of PIH were 29.19% in the non-NC group and 52.80% in the NC group (p < 0.001). Neoadjuvant chemotherapy was identified as an independent risk factor for post-induction hypotension in patients with ovarian cancer [adjusted OR 2.89 (1.90–4.42)], p < 0.001. Sensitivity analyses further supported the main conclusion.ConclusionPatients receiving neoadjuvant chemotherapy were 2.89 times more likely to experience post-induction hypotension compared with those who did not, within the first 20 min after anesthesia induction.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12871-026-03800-4.Keywords: Anesthesia induction, Neoadjuvant chemotherapy, Ovarian cancer, Post-induction hypotension, Pre-incision hypotension

Introduction

The occurrence of post-induction hypotension (PIH) is a common clinical occurrence, which can lead to insufficient perfusion of vital organs and is associated with a variety of adverse outcomes, such as longer hospital stays and higher mortality rates [1]. The incidence in previous research reports ranges from 18% to 56%, depending on the specific definition of PIH [2]. The underlying mechanisms of PIH remain incompletely understood. Emergency surgery, advanced age, preoperative frailty, and preoperative ACEI/ARB use have all been identified as potential risk factors for PIH [3,2]. Identification of risk factors associated with PIH is instrumental in maintaining hemodynamic stability and optimizing clinical outcomes.

Ovarian cancer ranks as the seventh most prevalent malignant tumors in women and represents the leading cause of mortality among malignancies of the female reproductive system. Neoadjuvant chemotherapy (NC) has become one of the initial treatment modalities for advanced ovarian cancer, [4, 5] notably in advanced disease with a substantial metastatic burden [5]. A common regimen involves the combination of paclitaxel and platinum-based agents, which has been shown to improve patients’ quality of life while also causing various chemotherapy-related adverse effects [4,5]. Its toxicity to the kidneys and lungs may affect the distribution and metabolism of anesthetics, while its neurotoxicity may alter patients’ sensitivity to anesthetic agents [6,7]. However, strategies to refine anesthesia management for enhancing postoperative recovery in this specific population remains underexplored.

Several studies have shown that patients receiving NC require reduced doses of anesthetic agents during both induction and maintenance phases, suggesting that NC may increase the patients’ sensitivity to the anesthetics [7–9]. However, the others studied have reported conflicting findings [10]. Based on these findings, we hypothesize that preoperative NC is independently associated with the incidence of PIH, and designed the following experimental protocol to investigate this association. In addition, the study will further explore the relationship between the number of NC cycles received by patients preoperatively and the incidence of PIH. This research will provide more information to assist anesthesiologists in managing general anesthesia for these patients [11].

Materials and methods

Ethical considerations

The protocol for this retrospective observational study was approved by the Institutional Scientific Research and Clinical Trials Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Zhengzhou University (Approval No.2025-KY-0393-001). Due to the retrospective nature of this study and the fact that all collected patient data were anonymized, the requirement for informed consent was waived. The data analysis and statistical plan were established before data extraction.

Study design and participants

This was a retrospective cohort analysis of patients who were preoperatively diagnosed with ovarian malignancies between December 1, 2019 and December 1, 2024. Data was extracted from the electronic medical records of a large tertiary hospital. Female adults who were preoperatively diagnosed with ovarian malignancies were eligible for inclusion in this study. Patients who met the following criteria were excluded, admitted to the intensive care unit before surgery, with significant pre-existing cardiac disease, undergoing a second surgery within 30 days, surgical duration less than 1 h, missing continuous blood pressure data for more than three minutes during the observation period, deviations from the prescribed general anesthesia induction regimen (Fig. 1).

Sample size estimation​​

According to the rule of thumb of 10 events per variable, the 13 predefined confounding factors in this study would require at least 130 positive outcomes. Given that the incidence of PIH varies considerably across previous studies and we estimated a PIH incidence rate of 25%, a minimum of 520 patients would therefore be required.

Measurements and data handing

Patients were managed and monitored per institutional routine. Standard intraoperative monitoring comprised SpO₂, ECG, and non-invasive blood pressure (NIBP) measured every three minutes, and whether to perform invasive arterial blood pressure (IBP) measurement was determined by an experienced anesthesiologist.

A standardized anesthetic protocol was used. Induction involved etomidate, propofol, or ciprofol; an opioid (alfentanil or sufentanil); and rocuronium. Maintenance consisted of inhaled sevoflurane or desflurane plus continuous infusions of remifentanil and an intravenous hypnotic (propofol, etomidate, or ciprofol). The MAP was kept above 65 mm Hg, with hypotension treated per protocol using an IV bolus of norepinephrine (8 µg), ephedrine (6 mg), or metaraminol (0.5 mg).

Blood pressure data were obtained through NIBP measurements at 3-minute intervals or continuously via IBP. To exclude the impact of “white coat hypertension”, baseline blood pressure was measured in the ward before the day of surgery. Hemodynamic data were automatically collected by the system at one-minute intervals after the patient entered the operating room.

Based on prior studies and clinical rationale, we collected the following predefined confounding factors. Anesthesia-related information was recorded, including the medications used and the exact time for anesthesia induction, and the usage of vasopressor agents during the aforementioned 20-minute period. The following patient characteristics were collected: age, body mass index (BMI), ASA physical status classification, preoperative comorbidities, Prognostic Nutritional Index (PNI, calculated using serum albumin (g/L) + 5 × total peripheral blood lymphocyte count (×10^9/L)), and preoperative medication usage.

Endpoints

The primary endpoint was the occurrence of PIH. PIH is defined as the occurrence of MAP<65 mm Hg or administration of at least one dose of a vasopressor within the observation period. Based on previous studies and clinical relevance, we selected the observation period as 20 min after anesthesia induction or from anesthesia induction until the start of surgery (if the surgery begins within 20 min after induction).

Statistical analysis

Data management was performed using Microsoft Excel 2021 (Microsoft Corp., Redmond, WA, USA), and statistical analyses were conducted with IBM SPSS Statistics, Version 25.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA).

Data normality was assessed using the Kolmogorov–Smirnov test. Continuous data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD) or median (25th–75th percentile), as appropriate; categorical data are presented as frequencies and percentages. Intergroup differences were evaluated using the independent t-test or Mann–Whitney U test for continuous variables, and the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test for categorical variables.

To identify risk factors for post-induction hypotension (PIH), covariates with a p-value < 0.2 in univariate analysis, along with clinically relevant confounders (including ASA physical status classification) reported in the literature, were included in a multivariable logistic regression model. Multicollinearity was assessed using the variance inflation factor (VIF), with a VIF ≥ 5 indicating its presence. Results are expressed as adjusted odds ratios (ORs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs). A two-sided p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Propensity score matching (PSM) was employed to balance baseline characteristics between the NC and non-NC groups. Propensity scores were calculated using logistic regression, with a caliper width set to 0.02. The PSM model included the following variables: Age, BMI, PNI, Pre-operative MAP, Pre-induction MAP, Airway type, Fasting duration, With TAPB, NC, Hypertension and Type II Diabetes. Covariate balance after matching was assessed using standardized mean differences (SMDs). The incidence of PIH between the matched groups was compared using the McNemar’s test. Furthermore, McNemar’s test was used to separately compare the proportions of patients with MAP below two thresholds and those requiring vasopressor administration between the matched groups.

The robustness of the primary findings was evaluated through the following sensitivity analyses:

Alternative Outcome Definition: PIH was redefined as a > 30% decrease in MAP from baseline or vasopressor administration during the observation period, and the primary analysis was repeated.

An exploratory subgroup analysis was performed, stratifying by the method of blood pressure measurement (NIBP vs. IBP).

Additional preoperative details on NC were collected, including the number of cycles and the specific regimen administered. The association between the number of NC cycles and the incidence of PIH was further explored.

Results

Study cohort

A total of 670 patients from 2019 to 2024 met the inclusion criteria, and ultimately, 532 patients were included in the final analysis. The baseline characteristics analysis was conducted on 532 patients, while the primary analysis excluded the missing cases before analysis because 9.4% of the cases had partial missing data (n = 482). This study cohort consisted of female patients who were with a preoperative diagnosis of ovarian cancer, with a median age of 54 years. Among the cohort, 225 patients (42.29%) had not received NC preoperatively, while 307 patients (57.71%) had received NC. In the NC group, 13 patients (4.23%) received one of the paclitaxel or platinum-based agents, and 294 patients (95.77%) received both agents. Additionally, 412 patients (77.44%) in the cohort had an ASA-PS class of II. The patients of NC group were generally older, with lower PNI, weight, height, and higher Pre-induction MAP, higher rate of Hypertension.

The PIH occurred in 233 patients (43.80%), of which 156 cases (66.95%) were detected by NIBP, and 77 cases (33.05%) were identified by IBP. At both thresholds, the incidence of PIH showed a significant difference between the two groups (30.22%, 68 of 225 in the non-NC group vs. 53.75%, 165 of 307 in the NC group, p < 0.001; 35.55%, 80 of 225 in the non-NC group vs. 53.42%, 164 of 307 in the NC group, p < 0.001). The lowest MAP within 20 min after anesthesia induction was significantly different between the two groups (Fig. 2, p = 0.007). Primary analysis

Consistent results were observed under both PIH definitions after propensity score matching. Using the absolute threshold (MAP < 65 mm Hg or vasopressor administration), the incidence was 29.19%, 47 of 161 in the non-NC group versus 52.80%, 85 of 161 in the NC group.

McNemar’s test revealed a statistically significant difference between the two groups (χ² = 15.92, p < 0.001). Similarly, when using the relative threshold (MAP decrease > 30% from baseline or vasopressor administration), the incidence of PIH remained significantly higher in the NC group at 55.28%,89 of 161 versus 34.78%, 56 of 161 in the non-NC group. McNemar’s test revealed a statistically significant difference between the two groups (χ² = 12.34, p < 0.001). Further analysis showed that when using either an absolute threshold (MAP < 65 mmHg) or a relative threshold (MAP decrease > 30% from baseline), McNemar’s test revealed no statistically significant difference in incidence between the two groups (for MAP < 65 mmHg, χ² = 0.90, p = 0.342; for MAP decrease > 30% from baseline, χ² = 0.34, p = 0.561). In contrast, the proportion of patients requiring vasopressor administration was significantly higher in the NC group (χ² = 14.42, p < 0.001).

Subsequently, logistic regression was performed on the entire cohort of the two groups (n = 482). Univariate analysis showed that age, BMI, NC, Pre-induction MAP and type II diabetes were correlated with PIH (less than 65 mm Hg) (Table 3). In the univariate analysis, those with p < 0.2 and ASA-PS classification were further included in the multivariable logistic regression analysis, and the results were shown in Table 4. A collinearity analysis was performed on all variables prior to inclusion in the multivariable logistic regression model. All variance inflation factors (VIFs) were below 5, confirming no significant multicollinearity.

Sensitivity analyses

To assess the robustness of our primary finding, we conducted multiple sensitivity analyses.

First, using an alternative PIH definition (> 30% decrease from baseline), neoadjuvant chemotherapy (NC) remained a significant independent risk factor [adjusted OR 2.88 (1.82–4.54)], p < 0.001(Supplementary Table 2). Second, to address potential bias from complete-case analysis, we performed multiple imputation for missing confounder data (BMI, PNI, Pre-induction MAP). The multivariable model based on imputed data yielded a consistent result for NC [adjusted OR 2.69 (1.82–3.98)], p < 0.001(Supplementary Table 3). Third, inverse probability of treatment weighting (IPTW) analysis also confirmed the association [adjusted OR 3.63 (2.71–4.87)], p < 0.001(Supplementary Table 5). Finally, the E-value calculated from the primary analysis was 4.82, suggesting that an unmeasured confounder would need to be moderately strong to explain away the observed association. The results of the sensitivity analyses are presented in Fig. 5.

Sensitivity analyses of the association between NC and PIH

Exploratory analyses

By incorporating the NC cycles into the main logistic regression model, we conducted an exploratory analysis of the relationship between this factor of interest and PIH. In univariate logistic regression, NC cycles was identified as a risk factor for PIH [OR 1.23 (1.13–1.34), p < 0.001]. However, when incorporated into the multivariable logistic regression model of primary analysis, NC cycles did not emerge as an independent risk factor for PIH. [adjusted OR 0.99 (0.82–1.20), p = 0.93].

Subgroup analyses

Given the potential interaction between blood pressure measurement method and NC, an interaction term analysis was first conducted. The results showed that the p-value for the interaction term was 0.483. In both the IBP group [adjusted OR 4.55 (2.04–10.16)], p < 0.001 and NIBP group [adjusted OR 2.49 (1.49–4.16)], p = 0.001, NC was independently associated with an increased risk of PIH.

Discussion

In this retrospective cohort study, we investigated the association between preoperative NC and PIH in patients diagnosed with ovarian cancer undergoing general anesthesia at a large tertiary hospital. The results demonstrated that receiving NC before surgery was associated with the occurrence of hypotension within 20 min after anesthesia induction. After balancing baseline characteristics using PSM, the incidence of PIH was significantly higher in patients who received NC, p < 0.001. Moreover, patients who underwent NC had lower lowest MAP values during this period compared to those who did not receive NC. In addition, no association was observed between the cycles of NC and occurrence of PIH. These findings highlight a significant and clinically relevant hemodynamic effect of NC in the immediate post-induction period.

When MAP was used as the sole positive outcome criterion, no significant differences were observed in the incidence of PIH between the two groups under either blood pressure threshold. However, the proportion of patients requiring vasopressor intervention differed significantly between groups. This finding may be explained by two factors. First, blood pressure data were recorded at 1-minute intervals; anesthesiologists may have detected MAP below the threshold and intervened in a timely manner before the next scheduled recording, thereby preventing hypotensive events occurring between intervals from being captured. Second, anesthesiologists may have administered vasopressors prophylactically before patients developed overt hypotension, which may have introduced additional bias. Given the retrospective design of this study, the specific circumstances of these clinical decisions cannot be fully ascertained. Therefore, future prospective studies are warranted to validate our findings.

The reported incidence of PIH exhibits considerable variation owing to heterogeneity in diagnostic thresholds and definitions. In our study cohort, the overall PIH incidence was 44%, with rates of 54% in the NC group versus 30% in the non-NC group. A prior study using a similar PIH definition reported a lower incidence of 18.1% [12]. The PIH incidence of our study is higher, which might be attributed to the inclusion of female population diagnosed with ovarian cancer in this study. Two recent studies reported a higher incidence of PIH: one reported rates of 33.9% (for all ages); the other reported 32.5% (for patients under 50 years old), 56.7% (for patients aged 50–65) and 65.6% (for patients over 65) [13,14]. In conclusion, although anesthesiologists employ various strategies to maintain hemodynamic stability in patients undergoing general anesthesia induction, the incidence of PIH remains high.

The new consensus indicates that intraoperative MAP lower than 60–70 mm Hg is associated with myocardial injury, acute kidney injury, and mortality [15]. As a component of intraoperative hypotension, PIH (or anesthesia-induced hypotension, pre-incision hypotension), which is more related to the induction protocol and the patient’s own physical condition rather than the influence of the surgical operation. Recent evidence have shown that lower baseline blood pressure, age, female, emergency surgery, higher ASA classification, volume depletion, frailty, anesthetic drug strategy, BMI, type II diabetes and chronic preoperative hypertension are independent risk factors for PIH [6, 13, 16–18]. Consistent with the results of most previous studies, our results also support that age is a risk factor for the occurrence of PIH [12, 13, 16, 19, 18]. Another study only included people over 65 years old, which might be the reason why its results were inconsistent with ours [14]. Similarly, our study also supports lower baseline blood pressure as a risk factor for PIH [12,14,19]. However, some other studies have observed different results, higher baseline blood pressure is a risk factor for PIH [18]. This discrepancy might be due to different definitions of PIH in different studies. Further studies are needed to confirm the above results.

Although the mechanism of PIH has not been fully clarified, the reduction of vascular resistance caused by anesthetics, the decrease of sympathetic nerve activity, the inhibition of pressure receptors, venous dilation, and the reduction of preload caused by positive pressure ventilation may all be involved in the generation of PIH [20]. These effects may be even more pronounced for older adults. The design of this study does not allow us to investigate the potential mechanisms underlying the increased incidence of PIH associated with NC. Potential mechanisms may involve both the direct effects of NC on patients and the inherent characteristics of patients receiving NC, such as tumor burden and related physiological alterations.

The patient received anesthesia induction with propofol, ciprofol or etomidate and afentanil or sufentanil as the regimens. The use of other anesthetics (such as remimazolam or esketamine) as adjuvants was at the discretion of the attending anesthesiologist. There was little difference in anesthetic use between the two groups, except that the median dose of afentanil received in NC group was 3 µg/kg higher than that in non-NC group. In other words, patients who accept NC may receive more anesthetics than they need at the induction stage. However, this dosage difference represents approximately 7% of alfentanil ranges, which may not reach clinical significance for PIH. Thus, while noted as a potential confounder, this dosage disparity is unlikely to substantively impact our primary conclusions. Meanwhile, NC affects the physical condition of patients in many ways, such as impaired nutritional status, kidney injury, and neurotoxicity [6,21,22]. We also attempted to use PNI to evaluate the preoperative nutritional status of patients and explore its association with PIH. Despite the PNI score of NC Group was lower (p = 0.021), no association between PNI and PIH was found in the multivariable regression analysis. This finding suggests that a single assessment tool may not fully reflect the complexity of a patient’s nutritional status. Future studies could consider using a variety of nutrition indicators to evaluate patient’s nutrition status.

Our study has several limitations. Due to the inherent limitations of retrospective studies, this study may be subject to unmeasured confounding factors, which could affect the reliability of our findings. To assess the potential impact of unmeasured confounding on our primary findings, we calculated E-values. The results indicated that an unmeasured confounder would need to be associated with both the exposure and the outcome by a risk ratio of at least 4.82 to fully explain the observed association. Patient volume status is a recognized risk factor for PIH. However, it is challenging to evaluate retrospectively. In this study, preoperative fasting duration served as a rough indicator of volume status. Our results show that fasting time is not a risk factor for PIH. This conclusion may be limited by the design of this study.

Only patients with ovarian cancer were included and all cases were collected from a single medical institution. The external validity of our findings may be limited. Although we collected confounding variables associated with PIH based on previous literature and clinical rationale, and performed PSM and IPTW, some potential confounders (e.g., CA-125, presence of ascites) were not included in the analysis due to data unavailability, which may limit the robustness of our conclusions. Furthermore, blood pressure was monitored invasively (IBP), non-invasively (NIBP), or via both methods. NIBP was set to be measured every 3 min. Intermittent blood pressure measurements may miss some occurrence of hypotension. Meanwhile, many cases were excluded from the final data analysis due to the lack of available blood pressure data. Our study design focused on immediate post-induction blood pressure changes, inherently prevents evaluation of clinically meaningful endpoints. However, emerging evidence shows that prolonged hypotension episodes, whether occurring intraoperatively or persisting up to postoperative day 4, significantly correlate with increased 30-day adverse outcomes [23]. Future prospective studies should include longer observation period and clinically relevant endpoints. Additionally, the failure to further explore the nonlinear relationships between age or baseline blood pressure and PIH represents another limitation of this study. Furthermore, the mechanisms underlying neoadjuvant chemotherapy-induced post-induction hypotension, as well as tumor-related alterations in anesthetic drug distribution and metabolism, [24] warrant further investigation.

Conclusions

In conclusion, this study establishes preoperative neoadjuvant chemotherapy (NC) as an independent risk factor for post-induction hypotension (PIH) in patients with ovarian cancer, alongside other significant factors including advanced age, lower baseline blood pressure, and type II diabetes. Further prospective, multicenter studies are warranted to confirm this association and elucidate the underlying mechanisms, with direct implications for preoperative risk stratification and anesthetic management.

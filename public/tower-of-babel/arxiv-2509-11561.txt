One-loop QCD corrections to SSA in unweighted Drell-Yan processes
Guang-Peng Zhang

Abstract
We study one-loop QCD corrections to the single transverse spin asymmetry in Drell-Yan process. The invariant mass of virtual photon and angular distributions of final lepton in Collins-Soper frame are measured. Especially, the transverse momentum of virtual photon is integrated out. Collinear twist-3 factorization formalism is adopted for the asymmetry. We use Feynman gauge in this work. To eliminate dependent twist-3 distribution functions, equation of motion for quark is used. We find that the soft divergence from the hard pole contribution in real corrections cannot be cancelled by corresponding divergences from virtual corrections. After collinear subtraction, the hard coefficient still contains soft divergence. Thus we conclude that collinear twist-3 factorization does not hold for this asymmetry at one-loop level.



I
Introduction

The collinear twist-3 factorization for single transverse spin asymmetry (SSA) in pion hadronic production has been proposed for many years
Efremov and Teryaev 1982
;
Efremov and Teryaev 1985
;
Qiu and Sterman 1991
;
Qiu and Sterman 1999
. This factorization formalism can also be used
to describe SSAs in direct photon production
Qiu and Sterman 1992
,
semi-inclusive deeply inelastic scattering(SIDIS)
Eguchi et al. 2006
;
Eguchi et al. 2007
;
Ji et al. 2006a

and Drell-Yan process(DY)
Ji et al. 2006b
;
Ji et al. 2006c
etc. However, a proof
for the factorization is still missing for these processes. Different
from twist-2 factorization, there are few one-loop corrections at twist-3 level in literatures for the SSA.
So far, QCD corrections to
several weighted SSAs in DY and SIDIS are calculated to
one-loop level
Vogelsang and Yuan 2009
;
Kang et al. 2013
;
Dai et al. 2015
;
Yoshida 2016
;
Chen et al. 2017
;
Chen et al. 2018
.
In these weighted SSAs,
the transverse momentum
q_{\perp}
for virtual photon in DY or

P_{h\perp}
for final detected hadron in SIDIS is integrated out.
It is found the twist-3 factorization really holds.
These are non-trivial checks for the factorization.
However, it does not imply the unweighted SSAs can also be factorized
at the same order of
\alpha_{s}
.
At tree level, the asymmetry for the angular distribution
of final lepton in DY with
q_{\perp}
integrated out was shown to be nonzero, please see
Ma and Zhang 2015
and reference therein.
In
Ma and Zhang 2015
, the tree level tensor structure of the hadronic tensor
is made clear (see eq.(
52
) in the following), which has two
parts: one part is
proportional to the derivative of
\delta^{2}(q_{\perp})
and the other part is proportional to
\delta^{2}(q_{\perp})
itself. The former is called derived part and
the latter is non-derivative part. Because the weight is proportional to
q_{\perp}
,
the weighted SSA receives virtual correction only from derivative part. It has been illustrated in
Chen et al. 2017
that this virtual correction is the same as the correction to the usual quark form factor, which is a twist-2 quantity. The direct calculation in

Vogelsang and Yuan 2009
confirms this. If there is
no weight, both derivative part and non-derivative part of the hadronic tensor can contribute to SSA. Thus, the examine of factorization based on
weighted SSA is incomplete.

In this work, we study the one-loop correction to unweighted SSA in
DY with
q_{\perp}
integrated.
Explicitly, we calculate the angular distribution of final lepton.
To avoid possible ambiguity for soft-gluon-pole contribution, we take
Feynman gauge in this work. The method of diagram expansion
Qiu and Sterman 1999
;
Eguchi et al. 2007

is adopted here. The troubles for the expansion are mainly two
aspects: one is
there are many dependent twist-3 distribution functions. Some of these functions
contain the bad component of quark field. The other is the gauge invariant
distribution functions contain gluon field strength tensor and gauge links, but
it seems impossible to recover these two quantities completely. In order to
solve these two problems, we use at most one longitudinal gluon(
G^{+}
)
to do collinear expansion, and then use equation of motion for quark field to
eliminate the bad component. After these treatments we get five independent
quark-gluon-quark or quark-quark correlation functions. Three of them can be
identified to
q_{\partial}
,
T_{F}
and
T_{\Delta}
. Remaining two
correlation functions are dangerous. To preserve QCD gauge invariance, the
hard coefficients before these two functions must be zero.
Really, our
calculation confirms this. This indicates our expansion scheme preserves
QCD gauge invariance. QED gauge invariance for the hadronic
tensor is also checked at one-loop level. In this work, we consider the
contribution proportional to
\bar{q}\otimes T_{F}
. That is, for unpolarized
hadron, only the contribution from twist-2 anti-quark distribution function

\bar{q}(x)
is considered and for polarized hadron, only twist-3
quark-gluon-quark distribution function
T_{F}
is considered. The hard coefficients from
virtual and real corrections are
calculated explicitly, however, it is found after collinear subtraction
the final hard coefficient still contains a divergence. Since our expansion
scheme preserve both QCD and QED gauge invariance, we think the divergence
indicates that collinear twist-3 factorization does not
hold for the SSA we consider here. Very recently,
Rein et al. 2025a
;
Rein et al. 2025b
give the one-loop correction to a single spin asymmetry
A_{UT}
for
E_{h}d^{3}\sigma/d^{3}P_{h}
in lepton-hadron scattering
l+p(s_{\perp})\rightarrow h+X
. The final lepton is undetected. Please see eq.(1) of
Rein et al. 2025a
for the illustration of the asymmetry. No breaking of the factorization is found there. This quantity can be studied with our method and we will study this process in near future.

The structure of this paper is as follows: Sec.2
is the kinematics for lepton angular distributions in Drell-Yan process;
Sec.3 is the definition of all involved twist-3
distributions and their relations resulting from equation of motion of quark field. Our expansion formalism is also presented in this section;
Sec.4 contains tree level results; Sec.5 contains one-loop virtual corrections; Sec.6 contains real corrections; Sec.7 is for the renormalization of twist-3
distribution function and collinear subtraction. The final hard coefficient is
also given, which contains a divergence mentioned above; In Sec.8, we give an
analysis based on eikonal approximation for the hard pole contribution. It is
indicated that the uncancelled divergence is a soft divergence; Sec.9 is our summary.

II
Kinematics

The polarized Drell-Yan process is

\displaystyle h_{A}(p_{a},s_{\perp})+h_{B}(p_{b})\rightarrow\gamma^{*}[\rightarrow e^{-}(l)e^{+}(\bar{l})]+X,

(1)

where
h_{A}
is a spin-
\frac{1}{2}
hadron polarized transversely with
s_{\perp}^{\mu}
the spin vector;
h_{B}
is a unpolarized hadron;
X
represents undetected hadrons;
the final lepton pair(here we take electron and positron as an example) is assumed from the decay of a virtual photon, and their momenta
l^{\mu}
and
\bar{l}^{\mu}
are detected. As usual we introduce
q^{\mu}=l^{\mu}+\bar{l}^{\mu}
for the virtual photon.
p_{a},p_{b}
are momenta of hadrons. The total energy squared is
s=(p_{a}+p_{b})^{2}
.

The invariant mass squared of virtual photon
Q^{2}=q^{2}
is a hard scale. Under Bjorken limit
Q^{2}\rightarrow\infty
and
\tau=Q^{2}/s
fixed, we can ignore all
masses of hadrons and leptons
Kodaira and Tanaka 1999
. The angular distribution of final lepton we want to study is

\displaystyle\frac{d\sigma}{dQ^{2}d\Omega}=\frac{\alpha_{em}^{2}}{4sQ^{4}}\int d^{n}q\delta(q^{2}-Q^{2})L_{\mu\nu}W^{\mu\nu}.

(2)

\Omega
is the solid angle of final lepton with momentum
l^{\mu}
, defined in Collins-Soper(CS) frame
Collins and Soper 1977
. In this work, we take dimensional regularization to regulate ultraviolet(UV) and infrared(IR) divergences. The dimension of
q
-integration has been set to
n=4-\epsilon
.

L^{\mu\nu}
and
W^{\mu\nu}
are leptonic and hadronic tensors, respectively.
That is,

\displaystyle L^{\mu\nu}=

\displaystyle 4(l^{\mu}\bar{l}^{\nu}+l^{\nu}\bar{l}^{\mu}-\frac{1}{2}Q^{2}g^{\mu\nu})=4(-2l^{\mu}l^{\nu}+l^{\mu}q^{\nu}+l^{\nu}q^{\mu}-\frac{1}{2}Q^{2}g^{\mu\nu}),

\displaystyle W^{\mu\nu}=

\displaystyle\int\frac{d^{n}x}{(2\pi)^{n}}e^{iq\cdot x}\sum_{X}\langle h_{B},h_{A}s_{\perp}|j^{\nu}(0)|X\rangle\langle X|j^{\mu}(x)|h_{A}s_{\perp},h_{B}\rangle.

(3)

j^{\mu}=\bar{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\psi
is electro-magnetic current.
\sum_{X}
is the phase space integration for all
possible final hadrons. At parton level, the hadrons are quarks and physical gluons.
In this work we use light-cone coordinates. A four-vector
a^{\mu}
is written as

a^{\mu}=(a^{+},a^{-},a_{\perp}^{\mu})
, with
a^{\pm}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(a^{0}\pm a^{3})
. Two light-like vectors
n^{\mu},\bar{n}^{\mu}
are introduced, so that

\displaystyle a^{+}=a\cdot n,\ a^{-}=a\cdot\bar{n},\ a_{\perp}\cdot n=a_{\perp}\cdot\bar{n}=0,\ n\cdot\bar{n}=1.

(4)

Two transverse tensors are also introduced: transverse metric
g_{\perp}^{\mu\nu}

and transverse anti-symmetric tensor
\epsilon_{\perp}^{\mu\nu}
as follows:

\displaystyle g_{\perp}^{\mu\nu}=g^{\mu\nu}-n^{\mu}\bar{n}^{\nu}-n^{\nu}\bar{n}^{\mu},\ \epsilon_{\perp}^{\mu\nu}=\epsilon^{-+\mu\nu}=\epsilon^{\rho\tau\mu\nu}\bar{n}_{\rho}n_{\tau}.

(5)

Note that
\epsilon^{0123}=+1
and
\epsilon_{\perp}^{12}=+1
in this work.

In the center of mass(CM) frame of initial hadrons, under Bjorken limit, the masses
of hadrons can be ignored, so,
p_{a}^{\mu},p_{b}^{\mu}
become light-like, that is,

\displaystyle p_{a}^{\mu}=p_{a}^{+}\bar{n}^{\mu},\ p_{b}^{\mu}=p_{b}^{-}n^{\mu}.

(6)

The solid angle
\Omega
is defined in CS frame, which is a rest frame of lepton pair and is obtained from CM frame by two boosts
Collins and Soper 1977
.
The first boost is along
Z
axis so that

q^{z}=0
after boost; the second boost is along the direction
\vec{q}_{\perp}
, so
that
\vec{q}_{\perp}=0
after boost.
l^{\mu}
in CS frame is parameterized as

\displaystyle l_{cs}^{\mu}=(l^{0},l^{1},l^{2},l^{3})=\frac{Q}{2}(1,\sin\theta\cos\phi,\sin\theta\sin\phi,\cos\theta).

(7)

We also define

l_{cs}^{\pm}=\frac{Q}{2\sqrt{2}}(1\pm\cos\theta)

and
l_{\perp,cs}^{\mu}
for the longitudinal and transverse components of
l^{\mu}_{cs}
, respectively. With the two Lorentz boosts done explicitly,
the momentum
l^{\mu}
in CM frame can be expressed in terms of
l_{cs}^{\mu}

as follows:

\displaystyle l^{+}=

\displaystyle\frac{q^{+}}{E_{t}}\Big[\frac{E_{t}}{2}+l_{cs}^{z}-\frac{1}{Q}l_{\perp,cs}\cdot q_{\perp}\Big],

\displaystyle l^{-}=

\displaystyle\frac{q^{-}}{E_{t}}\Big[\frac{E_{t}}{2}-l_{cs}^{z}-\frac{1}{Q}l_{\perp,cs}\cdot q_{\perp}\Big],

\displaystyle l_{\perp}^{\mu}=

\displaystyle l_{\perp,cs}^{\mu}+(\frac{E_{t}}{Q}-1)\frac{l_{\perp,cs}\cdot q_{\perp}}{q_{\perp}^{2}}q_{\perp}^{\mu}+\frac{1}{2}q_{\perp}^{\mu}.

(8)

As a convention, for quantities defined in CM frame, the subscription “cm” is suppressed. The dot product is defined for four-vector, that is,

a_{\perp}\cdot b_{\perp}=-\vec{a}_{\perp}\cdot\vec{b}_{\perp}
and especially

a_{\perp}^{2}=-\vec{a}_{\perp}\cdot\vec{a}_{\perp}<0
. The transverse energy
E_{t}
for virtual photon is
E_{t}=\sqrt{Q^{2}-q_{\perp}^{2}}\geq Q
.
Above representation of lepton momentum in CM frame is crucial for our calculation.
In the following we do all calculations in CM frame. The spin vector
s_{\perp}^{\mu}

is perpendicular to
p_{a}
in CM frame. If we let
s_{\perp}^{\mu}=(0^{+},0^{-},1,0)|s_{\perp}|
, i.e.,
\vec{s}_{\perp}
defines X-axis in CM frame, then,

\tilde{s}_{\perp}^{\mu}=\epsilon_{\perp}^{\mu\nu}s_{\perp\nu}=(0^{+},0^{-},0,1)|s_{\perp}|
,
and then,

\displaystyle l_{\perp,cs}\cdot\tilde{s}_{\perp}=-\frac{Q}{2}|s_{\perp}|\sin\theta\sin\phi.

(9)

The SSA we are considering is proportional to this quantity.

In this work, we check the twist-3 factorization for two quantities
I\langle L\rangle
and
I\langle P_{7}\rangle
, which are defined as

\displaystyle I\langle L\rangle=

\displaystyle\int d^{n}q\delta(q^{2}-Q^{2})L_{\mu\nu}W^{\mu\nu},

\displaystyle I\langle P_{7}\rangle=

\displaystyle\int d^{n}q\delta(q^{2}-Q^{2})P_{7,\mu\nu}W^{\mu\nu},\ P_{7}^{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{\tilde{s}_{\perp}^{2}}\Big(q_{\perp}^{\mu}\tilde{s}_{\perp}^{\nu}+q_{\perp}^{\nu}\tilde{s}_{\perp}^{\mu}\Big),

(10)

with
\tilde{s}_{\perp}^{\mu}=\epsilon_{\perp}^{\mu\nu}s_{\perp,\nu}
.
I\langle L\rangle
is
proportional to the differential cross section listed above, i.e.,

\displaystyle\frac{d\sigma}{dQ^{2}d\Omega}=\frac{\alpha_{em}^{2}}{4sQ^{4}}I\langle L\rangle.

(11)

I\langle P_{7}\rangle
on the other hand is proportional to the weighted cross section studied in

Chen et al. 2017
. Since the weighted cross section has been shown to be factorized,
I\langle P_{7}\rangle
here is used as a check of our calculation.

III
Twist-3 distribution functions

In this work we study the contribution from chiral-even twist-3 distribution functions. At twist-3 level, there are only two independent chiral-even
quark-gluon-quark correlation
functions,

\displaystyle\tilde{s}_{\perp}^{\rho}T_{F}(x_{1},x_{2})=

\displaystyle g_{s}\int\frac{d\xi^{-}d\xi_{1}^{-}}{4\pi}e^{-i\xi^{-}k^{+}-i\xi_{1}^{-}k_{1}^{+}}\langle ps|\bar{\psi}(0)\mathcal{L}_{n}(0)\mathcal{L}_{n}^{\dagger}(\xi^{-})\gamma^{+}G_{\perp}^{+\rho}(\xi^{-})\mathcal{L}_{n}(\xi^{-})\mathcal{L}^{\dagger}_{n}(\xi_{1}^{-})\psi(\xi_{1}^{-})|ps\rangle,

\displaystyle s_{\perp}^{\rho}T_{\Delta}(x_{1},x_{2})=

\displaystyle g_{s}\int\frac{d\xi^{-}d\xi_{1}^{-}}{4\pi}e^{-i\xi^{-}k^{+}-i\xi_{1}^{-}k_{1}^{+}}\langle ps|\bar{\psi}(0)\mathcal{L}_{n}(0)\mathcal{L}_{n}^{\dagger}(\xi^{-})(-i)\gamma^{+}\gamma_{5}G_{\perp}^{+\rho}(\xi^{-})\mathcal{L}_{n}(\xi^{-})\mathcal{L}^{\dagger}_{n}(\xi_{1}^{-})\psi(\xi_{1}^{-})|ps\rangle,

(12)

where the gauge link
\mathcal{L}_{n}
ensures that the two distributions are gauge invariant. The definition of gauge link is

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{n}(\xi^{-})=Pe^{-ig_{s}\int_{-\infty}^{\xi^{-}}d\lambda^{-}G^{+}(\lambda^{-})},\ G^{+}=G^{+}_{a}T_{a},

(13)

with
T_{a}
the generator of fundamental representation of
SU(N_{c})
.

P
is path ordering operator:

\displaystyle PG^{+}(\lambda_{1}^{-})G^{+}(\lambda_{2}^{-})=\theta(\lambda_{1}^{-}-\lambda_{2}^{-})G^{+}(\lambda_{1}^{-})G^{+}(\lambda_{2}^{-})+\theta(\lambda_{2}^{-}-\lambda_{1}^{-})G^{+}(\lambda_{2}^{-})G^{+}(\lambda_{1}^{-}).

(14)

In Drell-Yan process, the gauge link points to
-\infty
. For parton momenta, throughout the paper we use following notations

\displaystyle k^{+}=xp_{a}^{+},\ k_{1}^{+}=x_{1}p_{a}^{+},\ k_{2}^{+}=x_{2}p_{a}^{+},\ k_{b}^{-}=x_{b}p_{b}^{-},

(15)

and
k_{2}=k+k_{1}
,
x_{2}=x+x_{1}
.
k_{b}
is the momentum of anti-quark from unpolarized
hadron.

In addition to these three-point distributions, there are three two-point
distributions as follows

\displaystyle q_{T}(x)s_{\perp}^{\rho}=

\displaystyle p^{+}\int\frac{d\xi^{-}}{4\pi}e^{i\xi^{-}xp^{+}}\langle ps|\bar{\psi}(0)\mathcal{L}_{n}(0)\gamma_{\perp}^{\rho}\gamma_{5}\mathcal{L}_{n}^{\dagger}(\xi^{-})\psi(\xi^{-})|ps\rangle,

\displaystyle-iq^{\prime}_{\partial}(x)\tilde{s}_{\perp}^{\rho}=

\displaystyle\int\frac{d\xi^{-}}{4\pi}e^{i\xi^{-}xp^{+}}\langle ps|\bar{\psi}(0)\mathcal{L}_{n}(0)\gamma^{+}\partial_{\perp}^{\rho}\mathcal{L}_{n}^{\dagger}(\xi^{-})\psi(\xi^{-})|ps\rangle,

\displaystyle-iq_{\partial}(x)\tilde{s}_{\perp}^{\rho}=

\displaystyle\int\frac{d\xi^{-}}{4\pi}e^{i\xi^{-}xp^{+}}\langle ps|\bar{\psi}(0)\mathcal{L}_{n}(0)\gamma^{+}\gamma_{5}\partial_{\perp}^{\rho}\mathcal{L}_{n}^{\dagger}(\xi^{-})\psi(\xi^{-})|ps\rangle.

(16)

However, they are not independent due to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int dx_{1}P\frac{1}{x_{1}-x_{2}}\Big[T_{F}(x_{1},x_{2})+T_{\Delta}(x_{1},x_{2})\Big]=-x_{2}q_{T}(x_{2})+q_{\partial}(x_{2}),\ T_{F}(x,x)=2q^{\prime}_{\partial}(x).

(17)

These distribution functions and relations between them can be found for example in
Boer et al. 2003
;
Zhou et al. 2010
;
Kanazawa et al. 2016
.

q_{T}
and
q^{\prime}_{\partial}
can be eliminated. Another kind of twist-3 distributions
involving covariant derivative can be expressed by the distribution functions
introduced above
Zhou et al. 2010
. So, we expect the factorized cross section
can be expressed by
T_{F},T_{\Delta}
and
q_{\partial}
.

It is difficult to recover
T_{F},T_{\Delta}
and
q_{\partial}
in practical calculation, because of the gluon field strength tensor and gauge links. We do not try to
recover the complete gluon field strength tensor and gauge links in this calculation.
Instead, we first do collinear expansion using the matrix elements containing at most one
G^{+}
, which are

\displaystyle\langle ps|\bar{\psi}\tilde{\Gamma}\psi|ps\rangle,\ \langle ps|\bar{\psi}\tilde{\Gamma}G^{+}\psi|ps\rangle,\ \langle ps|\bar{\psi}\Gamma\partial_{\perp}^{\rho}\psi|ps\rangle,\ \langle ps|\bar{\psi}\Gamma(\partial_{\perp}^{\rho}G^{+})\psi|ps\rangle,\ \langle ps|\bar{\psi}\Gamma G^{+}\partial_{\perp}^{\rho}\psi|ps\rangle,

(18)

with
\tilde{\Gamma}=\gamma_{\perp}^{\rho},\gamma_{\perp}^{\rho}\gamma_{5}
,

\Gamma=\gamma^{+},\gamma^{+}\gamma_{5}
. Then, we try to eliminate the dependent matrix elements
by using equation of motion(EOM) and parity and time reversal(PT) symmetries. Such
an expansion scheme can be understood because the operators in
q_{\partial}
,
T_{F}

and
T_{\Delta}
can always be expanded into
\bar{\psi}_{+}
,
\psi_{+}
,
G^{+}
and
G_{\perp}
, where
\bar{\psi}_{+}
and
\psi_{+}
are good components of fermion field(
see eq.(
23
)). Then, if the factorization for
q_{\partial}
,
T_{F}
and
T_{\Delta}
is right, preserving the expansion to a certain power of
G^{+}
is also right, in the sense that the coefficient functions of the resulting matrix elements are finite. In our scheme, we keep the expansion to
O(G^{+})
and
(G_{\perp})^{0}
. Details of the expansion is given below.

We introduce following three types of correlation
functions. The first type does not contain
G^{+}
,

\displaystyle\int\frac{d\xi^{-}}{2\pi}e^{ik^{+}\xi^{-}}\langle Ps|\bar{\psi}_{j}(0)\psi_{i}(\xi^{-})|Ps\rangle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4N_{c}}\Big[\gamma^{-}2q(x)+\gamma_{5}\gamma_{\perp}^{\rho}s_{\perp\rho}M^{(0)}_{\gamma_{\perp}\gamma_{5}}+\gamma_{\perp}^{\rho}\tilde{s}_{\perp\rho}M^{(0)}_{\gamma_{\perp}}\Big],

\displaystyle\int\frac{d\xi^{-}}{2\pi}e^{ik^{+}\xi^{-}}\langle Ps|\bar{\psi}_{j}(0)\partial_{\perp}^{\rho}\psi_{i}(\xi^{-})|Ps\rangle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4N_{c}}\Big[i\gamma_{5}\gamma^{-}s_{\perp}^{\rho}M^{(0)}_{\gamma^{+}\gamma_{5},\partial_{\perp}\psi}+\gamma^{-}\tilde{s}_{\perp}^{\rho}M^{(0)}_{\gamma^{+},\partial_{\perp}\psi}\Big].

(19)

The superscript
(0)
implies there is no gluon. The subscript represents the
gamma matrix in the correlation function and derivatives.
From PT symmetry, it can be shown

\displaystyle M^{(0)}_{\gamma_{\perp}}=M^{(0)}_{\gamma^{+},\partial\psi}=0.

(20)

So, there are only two twist-3 two-point correlation functions, which are related
to
\gamma_{5}
.
q(x)
is the usual unpolarized twist-2 parton distribution function(PDF) for quark.

The second type contains one
G^{+}
but no
\partial_{\perp}
,

\displaystyle g_{s}\int\frac{d\xi^{-}}{2\pi}\int\frac{d\xi_{1}^{-}}{2\pi}e^{ik^{+}\xi^{-}+ik_{1}^{+}\xi_{1}^{-}}\langle ps|\bar{\psi}_{j}(0)G_{a}^{+}(\xi^{-})\psi_{i}(\xi_{1}^{-})|ps\rangle=\frac{T_{a}}{4N_{c}C_{F}}\Big[i\gamma_{\perp}^{\rho}\tilde{s}_{\perp\rho}M^{(1)}_{\gamma_{\perp}}+\gamma_{5}\gamma_{\perp}^{\rho}s_{\perp\rho}M^{(1)}_{\gamma_{\perp}\gamma_{5}}\Big];

(21)

The third type contains one
G^{+}
and one
\partial_{\perp}
,

\displaystyle g_{s}\int\frac{d\xi^{-}}{2\pi}\int\frac{d\xi_{1}^{-}}{2\pi}e^{ik^{+}\xi^{-}+ik_{1}^{+}\xi_{1}^{-}}\langle ps|\bar{\psi}_{j}(0)G_{a}^{+}(\xi^{-})\partial_{\perp}^{\rho}\psi_{i}(\xi_{1}^{-})|ps\rangle=

\displaystyle\frac{T_{a}}{4N_{c}C_{F}}\Big[\gamma^{-}\tilde{s}_{\perp}^{\rho}M^{(1)}_{\gamma^{+},\partial_{\perp}\psi}+i\gamma_{5}\gamma^{-}s_{\perp}^{\rho}M^{(1)}_{\gamma^{+}\gamma_{5},\partial_{\perp}\psi}\Big];

\displaystyle g_{s}\int\frac{d\xi^{-}}{2\pi}\int\frac{d\xi_{1}^{-}}{2\pi}e^{ik^{+}\xi^{-}+ik_{1}^{+}\xi_{1}^{-}}\langle ps|\bar{\psi}_{j}(0)[\partial_{\perp}^{\rho}G_{a}^{+}(\xi^{-})]\psi_{i}(\xi_{1}^{-})|ps\rangle=

\displaystyle\frac{T_{a}}{4N_{c}C_{F}}\Big[\gamma^{-}\tilde{s}_{\perp}^{\rho}M^{(1)}_{\gamma^{+},\partial_{\perp}G^{+}}+i\gamma_{5}\gamma^{-}s_{\perp}^{\rho}M^{(1)}_{\gamma^{+}\gamma_{5},\partial_{\perp}G^{+}}\Big].

(22)

From PT symmetry of QCD, all of these
M^{(i)}
are real. Note that for
M^{(1)}
,

g_{s}
is included in the matrix element.

For fermion field, the good and bad components are defined as

\displaystyle\psi_{+}=\frac{\gamma^{-}\gamma^{+}}{2}\psi,\ \psi_{-}=\frac{\gamma^{+}\gamma^{-}}{2}\psi.

(23)

In collinear expansion, the bad component of fermion field
\psi_{-}
is

\displaystyle\psi_{-}(\xi^{-})=-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{L}_{n}(\xi^{-})\int_{-\infty}^{\xi^{-}}d\lambda^{-}\mathcal{L}_{n}^{\dagger}(\lambda^{-})\gamma^{+}\gamma_{\perp}\cdot D_{\perp}(\lambda^{-})\psi_{+}(\lambda^{-}).

.
This relation enables us to eliminate the bad component. After eliminating

\displaystyle M^{(1)}_{\gamma_{\perp}\gamma_{5}}(k^{+},k_{1}^{+})\doteq

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}P\Big(\frac{1}{k_{1}^{+}}+\frac{1}{k_{2}^{+}}\Big)M^{(1)}_{\gamma^{+}\gamma_{5},\partial_{\perp}\psi}(k^{+},k_{1}^{+})-\frac{1}{2}P\Big(\frac{1}{k_{1}^{+}}-\frac{1}{k_{2}^{+}}\Big)M^{(1)}_{\gamma^{+},\partial_{\perp}\psi}(k^{+},k_{1}^{+})

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2k_{2}^{+}}\Big(M^{(1)}_{\gamma^{+}\gamma_{5},\partial_{\perp}G^{+}}(k^{+},k_{1}^{+})-M^{(1)}_{\gamma^{+},\partial_{\perp}G^{+}}(k^{+},k_{1}^{+})\Big),

\displaystyle M^{(1)}_{\gamma_{\perp}}(k^{+},k_{1}^{+})\doteq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}P\Big(\frac{1}{k_{1}^{+}}+\frac{1}{k_{2}^{+}}\Big)M^{(1)}_{\gamma^{+},\partial_{\perp}\psi}(k^{+},k_{1}^{+})+\frac{1}{2}P\Big(\frac{1}{k_{1}^{+}}-\frac{1}{k_{2}^{+}}\Big)M^{(1)}_{\gamma^{+}\gamma_{5},\partial_{\perp}\psi}(k^{+},k_{1}^{+})

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2k_{2}^{+}}\Big(M^{(1)}_{\gamma^{+},\partial_{\perp}G^{+}}(k^{+},k_{1}^{+})-M^{(1)}_{\gamma^{+}\gamma_{5},\partial_{\perp}G^{+}}(k^{+},k_{1}^{+})\Big),

\displaystyle\ M^{(0)}_{\gamma_{\perp}\gamma_{5}}(k_{2}^{+})\doteq

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{k_{2}^{+}}\int dk^{+}P\frac{1}{k_{1}^{+}}\Big(M^{(1)}_{\gamma^{+}\gamma_{5},\partial_{\perp}\psi}(k^{+},k_{1}^{+})+M^{(1)}_{\gamma^{+}\partial_{\perp}\psi}(k^{+},k_{1}^{+})\Big)-\frac{1}{k_{2}^{+}}M^{(0)}_{\gamma^{+}\gamma_{5},\partial_{\perp}\psi}(k_{2}^{+}).

The

implies
G_{\perp}

We adopt HVBM scheme

are
also defined as four dimensional quantities. Thus following identities can be applied

\displaystyle\gamma^{+}\not{s}_{\perp}(i\gamma_{5}\gamma^{-})=-i\gamma_{5}\not{s}_{\perp}-\not{\tilde{s}}_{\perp},\ \gamma^{+}\not{\tilde{s}}_{\perp}\gamma^{-}=-\not{\tilde{s}}_{\perp}-i\gamma_{5}\not{s}_{\perp},

(26)

which reduce the number of gamma matrices.

Now our calculation scheme is clear. First, we use all possible twist-3
matrix elements
M^{(i)}
to do collinear expansion and get all corresponding
hard coefficients. For hadronic tensor, the result is

\displaystyle 8N_{c}^{2}C_{F}W^{\mu\nu}=

\displaystyle\int dk_{b}^{-}dk_{2}^{+}\bar{q}(k_{b}^{-})\Big[H_{0}^{\mu\nu}M_{\gamma_{\perp}\gamma_{5}}^{(0)}(k_{2}^{+})+\tilde{H}_{0}^{\mu\nu}M^{(0)}_{\gamma^{+}\gamma_{5},\partial_{\perp}\psi}(k_{2}^{+})\Big]

\displaystyle+\int dk_{b}^{-}dk^{+}dk_{1}^{+}\bar{q}(k_{b}^{-})\Big[H_{1}^{\mu\nu}M^{(1)}_{\gamma_{\perp}\gamma_{5}}(k^{+},k_{1}^{+})+H_{2}^{\mu\nu}M^{(1)}_{\gamma_{\perp}}(k^{+},k_{1}^{+})

\displaystyle+H_{3}^{\mu\nu}M^{(1)}_{\gamma^{+}\gamma_{5},\partial\psi}(k^{+},k_{1}^{+})+H_{4}^{\mu\nu}M^{(1)}_{\gamma^{+},\partial\psi}(k^{+},k_{1}^{+})+H_{5}^{\mu\nu}M^{(1)}_{\gamma^{+}\gamma_{5},\partial G^{+}}(k^{+},k_{1}^{+})+H_{6}^{\mu\nu}M^{(1)}_{\gamma^{+},\partial G^{+}}(k^{+},k_{1}^{+})\Big].

(27)

The hard coefficients are

\displaystyle H^{\mu\nu}_{0}=

\displaystyle\text{Tr}\Big[C_{F}H^{\mu\nu}\otimes\gamma_{5}\not{s}_{\perp}\otimes\gamma^{+}\Big],

\displaystyle\tilde{H}^{\mu\nu}_{0}=

\displaystyle\text{Tr}\Big[C_{F}i\frac{\partial H^{\mu\nu}}{\partial k_{2\perp}^{\tau}}\otimes i\gamma_{5}\gamma^{-}s_{\perp}^{\tau}\otimes\gamma^{+}\Big],

\displaystyle H^{\mu\nu}_{1}=

\displaystyle\text{Tr}\Big[H^{\mu\nu}\otimes T^{a}\gamma_{5}\not{s}_{\perp}\otimes\gamma^{+}\Big],

\displaystyle H^{\mu\nu}_{2}=

\displaystyle\text{Tr}\Big[H^{\mu\nu}\otimes iT^{a}\not{\tilde{s}}_{\perp}\otimes\gamma^{+}\Big],

\displaystyle H^{\mu\nu}_{3}=

\displaystyle\text{Tr}\Big[i\frac{\partial H^{\mu\nu}}{\partial k_{1\perp\tau}}\otimes iT^{a}\gamma_{5}\gamma^{-}s_{\perp}^{\tau}\otimes\gamma^{+}\Big],

\displaystyle H^{\mu\nu}_{4}=

\displaystyle\text{Tr}\Big[i\frac{\partial H^{\mu\nu}}{\partial k_{1\perp\tau}}\otimes T^{a}\gamma^{-}\tilde{s}_{\perp\tau}\otimes\gamma^{+}\Big],

\displaystyle H^{\mu\nu}_{5}=

\displaystyle\text{Tr}\Big[i\frac{\partial H^{\mu\nu}}{\partial k_{\perp\tau}}\otimes iT^{a}\gamma^{5}\gamma^{-}s_{\perp}^{\tau}\otimes\gamma^{+}\Big],

\displaystyle H^{\mu\nu}_{6}=

\displaystyle\text{Tr}\Big[i\frac{\partial H^{\mu\nu}}{\partial k_{\perp\tau}}\otimes T^{a}\gamma^{-}\tilde{s}_{\perp}^{\tau}\otimes\gamma^{+}\Big].

(28)

\otimes
is the product in Dirac and color space.

Then we use EOM relations eq.(
25
) to eliminate dependent correlation functions to get

\displaystyle 8N_{c}^{2}C_{F}W^{\mu\nu}=

\displaystyle\int dk_{b}^{-}\bar{q}(x_{b})\int dk_{2}^{+}g_{0}^{\mu\nu}\times M^{(0)}_{\gamma^{+}\gamma_{5}\partial\psi}(k_{2}^{+})

\displaystyle+\int dk_{b}^{-}\bar{q}(x_{b})\int dk^{+}dk_{1}^{+}\Big[g_{1}^{\mu\nu}\times M^{(1)}_{\gamma^{+}\partial\psi}+g_{2}^{\mu\nu}\times M^{(1)}_{\gamma^{+}\gamma_{5}\partial\psi}+g_{3}^{\mu\nu}\times M^{(1)}_{\gamma^{+}\partial_{\perp}G^{+}}+g_{4}^{\mu\nu}\times M^{(1)}_{\gamma^{+}\gamma_{5}\partial_{\perp}G^{+}}\Big],

(29)

with

\displaystyle g_{0}=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{k_{2}^{+}}H_{0}+\tilde{H}_{0},

\displaystyle k_{2}^{+}g_{1}=

\displaystyle-P\frac{1}{k_{1}^{+}}H_{0}-P\frac{k^{+}}{2k_{1}^{+}}H_{1}+P\frac{k_{1}^{+}+k_{2}^{+}}{2k_{1}^{+}}H_{2}+k_{2}^{+}H_{4},

\displaystyle k_{2}^{+}g_{2}=

\displaystyle-P\frac{1}{k_{1}^{+}}H_{0}-P\frac{k_{1}^{+}+k_{2}^{+}}{2k_{1}^{+}}H_{1}+P\frac{k^{+}}{2k_{1}^{+}}H_{2}+k_{2}^{+}H_{3},

\displaystyle k_{2}^{+}g_{3}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}H_{1}+\frac{1}{2}H_{2}+k_{2}^{+}H_{6},

\displaystyle k_{2}^{+}g_{4}=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}H_{1}-\frac{1}{2}H_{2}+k_{2}^{+}H_{5}.

(30)

\mu,\nu
indices in
H_{i}
and
g_{i}
are suppressed. Since all
M^{(i)}
are real,
the coefficients
g_{i}
are also real for symmetric
\mu,\nu
.

Besides EOM relations, there is one more important relation if
k^{+}=0
, i.e.,

\displaystyle M^{(1)}_{\gamma^{+},\partial_{\perp}\psi}(k^{+},k_{1}^{+})\Big|_{k^{+}=0}=M^{(1)}_{\gamma^{+},\partial_{\perp}\bar{\psi}}(k^{+},k_{1}^{+})\Big|_{k^{+}=0}=-\frac{1}{2}M^{(1)}_{\gamma^{+},\partial_{\perp}G^{+}}(k^{+},k_{1}^{+})\Big|_{k^{+}=0}.

(31)

This is equivalent to the relation between
q^{\prime}_{\partial}
and
T_{F}
, and can be
derived from PT symmetry. It is possible
that the coefficient
g_{1}
contains a soft-gluon-pole(SGP) part, that is,

\displaystyle g_{1}(k^{+},k_{1}^{+})=\tilde{g}_{1}(k^{+},k_{1}^{+})+\delta(k^{+})g_{1}^{SGP}(k_{1}^{+}),

(32)

where
\tilde{g}_{1}(k^{+},k_{1}^{+})
is finite at
k^{+}=0
. If this is the case, then

\displaystyle\int dk^{+}dk_{1}^{+}\Big[g_{1}(k^{+},k_{1}^{+})M^{(1)}_{\gamma^{+},\partial_{\perp}\psi}(k^{+},k_{1}^{+})+g_{3}(k^{+},k_{1}^{+})M^{(1)}_{\gamma^{+},\partial_{\perp}G^{+}}(k^{+},k_{1}^{+})\Big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int dk^{+}dk_{1}^{+}\Big[\tilde{g}_{1}(k^{+},k_{1}^{+})M^{(1)}_{\gamma^{+},\partial_{\perp}\psi}(k^{+},k_{1}^{+})+\Big(g_{3}(k^{+},k_{1}^{+})-\frac{1}{2}\delta(k^{+})g_{1}^{SGP}(k_{1}^{+})\Big)M^{(1)}_{\gamma^{+},\partial_{\perp}G^{+}}(k^{+},k_{1}^{+})\Big].

(33)

On the other hand, since
q_{\partial}
contains gauge link
\mathcal{L}_{n}
while
M^{(0)}_{\gamma^{+}\gamma_{5},\partial_{\perp}\psi}
does not, we should extract
some parts of
g_{1},g_{3}
to produce the gauge link
\mathcal{L}_{n}
, if we
want to write
M^{(0)}_{\gamma^{+}\gamma_{5},\partial_{\perp}\psi}
into
q_{\partial}
.
This is not difficult if we notice that

\displaystyle\int dk^{+}\frac{e^{ik^{+}(\xi^{-}-\xi_{1}^{-})}}{k^{+}+i\epsilon}G^{+}(\xi^{-})=-2\pi i\theta(\xi_{1}^{-}-\xi^{-})G^{+}(\xi^{-}),

(34)

and

\displaystyle\int dk^{+}P\frac{1}{k^{+}}M^{(1)}_{\gamma^{+}\gamma_{5},\partial_{\perp}\psi}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int dk^{+}(\frac{1}{k^{+}+i\epsilon}+\frac{1}{k^{+}-i\epsilon})M^{(1)}_{\gamma^{+}\gamma_{5},\partial_{\perp}\psi}=\frac{1}{2}\int dk^{+}\frac{1}{k^{+}+i\epsilon}M^{(1)}_{\gamma^{+}\gamma_{5},\partial_{\perp}\psi}+c.c.

(35)

Then,

\displaystyle s_{\perp}^{\rho}2\int dk^{+}P\frac{1}{k^{+}}\Big(M^{(1)}_{\gamma^{+}\gamma_{5},\partial_{\perp}\psi}(k^{+},k_{1}^{+})+M^{(1)}_{\gamma^{+}\gamma_{5},\partial_{\perp}G^{+}}(k^{+},k_{1}^{+})\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s_{\perp}^{\rho}M^{(0)}_{\gamma^{+}\gamma_{5},\partial\psi}(k_{2}^{+})-\int\frac{d\xi^{-}}{2\pi}e^{i\xi^{-}k_{2}^{+}}\langle ps|\bar{\psi}(0)\mathcal{L}_{n}(0)(-i\gamma^{+}\gamma_{5})\partial_{\perp}^{\rho}\Big(\mathcal{L}_{n}^{\dagger}(\xi^{-})\psi(\xi^{-})\Big)|ps\rangle+O((G^{+})^{2}).

(36)

Or equivalently,

\displaystyle M^{(0)}_{\gamma^{+}\gamma_{5},\partial\psi}(k_{2}^{+})-2\int dk^{+}P\frac{1}{k^{+}}\Big(M^{(1)}_{\gamma^{+}\gamma_{5},\partial_{\perp}\psi}(k^{+},k_{1}^{+})+M^{(1)}_{\gamma^{+}\gamma_{5},\partial_{\perp}G^{+}}(k^{+},k_{1}^{+})\Big)=-2q_{\partial}(x_{2})+O((G^{+})^{2}).

(37)

Then,

\displaystyle\int dk_{2}^{+}g_{0}(k_{2}^{+})M^{(0)}_{\gamma^{+}\gamma_{5},\partial_{\perp}\psi}(k_{2}^{+})+\int dk^{+}dk_{1}^{+}[g_{2}(k^{+},k_{1}^{+})M^{(1)}_{\gamma^{+}\gamma_{5},\partial_{\perp}\psi}+g_{4}(k^{+},k_{1}^{+})M^{(1)}_{\gamma^{+}\gamma_{5},\partial_{\perp}G^{+}}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\int dk_{2}^{+}g_{0}(k_{2}^{+})q_{\partial}(x_{2})

\displaystyle+\int dk^{+}dk_{1}^{+}\Big[\Big(g_{2}(k^{+},k_{1}^{+})+P\frac{2g_{0}(k_{2}^{+})}{k^{+}}\Big)M^{(1)}_{\gamma^{+}\gamma_{5},\partial_{\perp}\psi}+\Big(g_{4}(k^{+},k_{1}^{+})+P\frac{2g_{0}(k_{2}^{+})}{k^{+}}\Big)M^{(1)}_{\gamma^{+}\gamma_{5},\partial_{\perp}G^{+}}\Big].

(38)

After gauge link is recovered, remaining
G^{+}
can be viewed as a part of gluon
field strength tensor. We replace

\partial_{\perp}^{\rho}G^{+}
in
M^{(1)}_{\gamma^{+},\partial_{\perp}G^{+}}
and

M^{(1)}_{\gamma^{+}\gamma_{5},\partial_{\perp}G^{+}}
into
-G_{\perp}^{+\rho}
as done in
Qiu and Sterman 1999
or

\displaystyle M^{(1)}_{\gamma^{+},\partial_{\perp}G^{+}}(k^{+},k_{1}^{+})\rightarrow-\frac{1}{\pi}T_{F}(x_{1},x_{2}),\ M^{(1)}_{\gamma^{+}\gamma_{5},\partial_{\perp}G^{+}}(k^{+},k_{1}^{+})\rightarrow-\frac{1}{\pi}T_{\Delta}(x_{1},x_{2}),

(39)

with
x_{2}=x+x_{1}
,
k^{+}=xp_{a}^{+}
,
k_{1}^{+}=x_{1}p_{a}^{+}
.
The final formula is

\displaystyle 8N_{c}^{2}C_{F}W^{\mu\nu}=

\displaystyle\int dk_{b}^{-}\bar{q}(x_{b})\int dk_{2}^{+}\tilde{g}_{0}^{\mu\nu}q_{\partial}(x_{2})

\displaystyle+\int dk_{b}^{-}\bar{q}(x_{b})\int dk^{+}dk_{1}^{+}\Big[\tilde{g}_{1}^{\mu\nu}M^{(1)}_{\gamma^{+}\partial\psi}+\tilde{g}_{2}^{\mu\nu}M^{(1)}_{\gamma^{+}\gamma_{5}\partial\psi}-\frac{1}{\pi}\tilde{g}_{3}^{\mu\nu}T_{F}(x_{1},x_{2})-\frac{1}{\pi}\tilde{g}_{4}^{\mu\nu}T_{\Delta}(x_{1},x_{2})\Big],

(40)

with

\displaystyle\tilde{g}_{0}=

\displaystyle-2g_{0},

\displaystyle\tilde{g}_{1}=

\displaystyle g_{1}-\delta(k^{+})g_{1}^{SGP}(k_{1}^{+}),

\displaystyle\tilde{g}_{2}=

\displaystyle g_{2}+P\frac{2g_{0}(k_{2}^{+})}{k^{+}},

\displaystyle\tilde{g}_{3}=

\displaystyle g_{3}-\frac{1}{2}\delta(k^{+})g_{1}^{SGP}(k_{1}^{+}),

\displaystyle\tilde{g}_{4}=

\displaystyle g_{4}+P\frac{2g_{0}(k_{2}^{+})}{k^{+}}.

(41)

This is our main formula for calculation. Now it is clear that if
\tilde{g}_{1}
or
\tilde{g}_{2}
is nonzero, the collinear
expansion we employed does not preserve QCD gauge invariance. This is an important check of our calculation.

IV
Tree level result

According to our expansion scheme, at most one
G^{+}
appears. There are only
two diagrams at tree level, as shown in Fig.
1
. The contribution of
conjugated diagram of Fig.
1
(b) is included but not shown.

(a)

(b)

Figure 1:
Tree diagrams contributing to
W^{\mu\nu}
. The conjugated diagram of (b)
is not shown, but included in the calculation.

Suppose
\vec{p}_{a}

is along
+Z
axis in CM frame.
p_{a}^{\mu}\simeq(p_{a}^{+},0,0_{\perp})
,
p_{b}^{\mu}\simeq(0,p_{b}^{-},0_{\perp})
. Under collinear limit,
the partons connecting the hard part and hadrons are collinear, whose momenta are

\displaystyle k_{2}^{\mu}=(k_{2}^{+},k_{2}^{-},k_{2\perp})\sim Q(1,\lambda^{2},\lambda),\ k_{b}^{\mu}=(k_{b}^{+},k_{b}^{-},k_{b\perp})\sim Q(\lambda^{2},1,\lambda),

(42)

with
\lambda
a small quantity.
k
and
k_{1}
are also collinear to
p_{a}
, like
k_{2}
.
At twist-3 level, the hard part should be expanded to
O(\lambda)
. One can find
the details about collinear expansion in
Qiu and Sterman 1999
;
Eguchi et al. 2007
;
Chen et al. 2017
.
Because the hard coefficients
H_{i}
contain the delta function for
momentum conservation
\delta^{n}(k_{2}+k_{b}-q)
, we also need to do power expansion
for it, that is,

\displaystyle\delta^{n-2}(k_{2\perp}-q_{\perp})=\delta^{(n-2)}(q_{\perp})-\frac{\delta^{(n-2)}(q_{\perp})}{\partial q_{\perp}^{\rho}}k_{2\perp}^{\rho}+O(k_{2\perp}^{2}).

(43)

\partial\delta^{n-2}(q_{\perp})/\partial q_{\perp}^{\rho}
gives the derivative part and
\delta^{(n-2)}(q_{\perp})
gives the non-derivative part.
Next we calculate these two parts separately.

Because final leptons are unpolarized, the leptonic tensor
L^{\mu\nu}
is symmetric in
\mu,\nu
. So, we just need the symmetric part of the hadronic tensor.
For non-derivative part, only
H_{5,6}^{\mu\nu}
can have a symmetric part. We have

\displaystyle H_{5}^{\mu\nu}|_{tree}=

\displaystyle H_{6}^{\mu\nu}|_{tree}=-4iN_{c}C_{F}\frac{1}{k_{b}\cdot k+i\epsilon}\frac{1}{p_{a}^{+}}\delta(k_{2}^{+}-q^{+})\delta(k_{b}^{-}-q^{-})\delta^{n-2}(q_{\perp})(p_{a}^{\mu}\tilde{s}_{\perp}^{\nu}+p_{a}^{\nu}\tilde{s}_{\perp}^{\mu}).

(44)

We just need the real part. Because

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k_{b}\cdot k+i\epsilon}=P\frac{1}{k_{b}\cdot k}-i\pi\delta(k_{b}\cdot k),

(45)

only the delta function gives a real part. This is called soft-gluon-pole(SGP)
contribution, since the delta function forces the gluon momentum

k^{+}
to be zero. We have

\displaystyle H_{5}^{\mu\nu}|_{tree}=

\displaystyle H_{6}^{\mu\nu}|_{tree}=-4N_{c}C_{F}\delta(k_{b}\cdot k)\frac{1}{p_{a}^{+}}\delta(k_{2}^{+}-q^{+})\delta(k_{b}^{-}-q^{-})\delta^{n-2}(q_{\perp})(p_{a}^{\mu}\tilde{s}_{\perp}^{\nu}+p_{a}^{\nu}\tilde{s}_{\perp}^{\mu}).

(46)

However, from PT symmetry,

\displaystyle M^{(1)}_{\gamma^{+}\gamma_{5},\partial_{\perp}G^{+}}(k^{+},k_{1}^{+})\Big|_{k^{+}=0}=0.

(47)

So,
H_{5}
can be ignored. We have

\displaystyle 8N_{c}^{2}C_{F}W^{\mu\nu}_{non-de}=

\displaystyle-4\pi N_{c}C_{F}\int dk_{b}^{-}\int dk_{2}^{+}\delta(k_{2}^{+}-q^{+})\delta(k_{b}^{-}-q^{-})\delta^{n-2}(q_{\perp})\Big[\bar{q}(x_{b})M^{(1)}_{\gamma^{+},\partial G^{+}}(0,k_{2}^{+})\frac{(p_{a}^{\mu}\tilde{s}_{\perp}^{\nu}+p_{a}^{\nu}\tilde{s}_{\perp}^{\mu})}{p_{a}\cdot q}\Big].

(48)

For the derivative part,
H_{0,1,2}^{\mu\nu}
do not appear, because corresponding
matrix elements contain a bad component of fermion field. The contributions are of
twist-4 at least. So, only
\tilde{H}_{0}^{\mu\nu}
and
H_{3-6}^{\mu\nu}
may contribute. However,
\tilde{H}_{0}^{\mu\nu}
and
H_{3,5}^{\mu\nu}
contain one

\gamma_{5}
. After taking the trace, they are proportional to
\epsilon_{\perp}^{\mu\nu}
,
which is anti-symmetric in
\mu,\nu
. Thus,
\tilde{H}_{0}^{\mu\nu}
and
H_{3,5}^{\mu\nu}
do not contribute. As a result, we just need to calculate
H_{4}^{\mu\nu}
and
H_{6}^{\mu\nu}
, which are

\displaystyle H_{4}^{\mu\nu}=i\frac{\partial\delta^{n}(k+k_{1}+k_{b}-q)}{\partial k_{1\perp}^{\rho}}\tilde{s}_{\perp}^{\rho}Tr[H^{\mu\nu}\otimes T^{a}\gamma^{-}],\ H_{6}^{\mu\nu}=i\frac{\partial\delta^{n}(k+k_{1}+k_{b}-q)}{\partial k_{\perp}^{\rho}}\tilde{s}_{\perp}^{\rho}Tr[H^{\mu\nu}\otimes T^{a}\gamma^{-}].

(49)

From Fig.
1
without conjugated diagrams, we get

\displaystyle p_{a}^{+}H_{4}^{\mu\nu}|_{tree}=p_{a}^{+}H_{6}^{\mu\nu}|_{tree}=-i\frac{\partial\delta^{n}(k+k_{1}+k_{b}-q)}{\partial q_{\perp}^{\rho}}\tilde{s}_{\perp}^{\rho}8N_{c}C_{F}\frac{1}{x+i\epsilon}g_{\perp}^{\mu\nu}.

(50)

With conjugated diagrams taken into account,
only real part contributes, which is proportional to
\delta(x)
.
With the help of eq.(
31
),
\partial_{\perp}\psi
can be converted
to
\partial_{\perp}G^{+}
. Then, we have

\displaystyle 8N_{c}^{2}C_{F}W^{\mu\nu}_{de}=

\displaystyle\int dk_{b}^{-}\int dk^{+}dk_{1}^{+}\bar{q}(k_{b}^{-})\Big(-\frac{1}{2}H_{4}^{\mu\nu}+H_{6}^{\mu\nu}\Big)M^{(1)}_{\gamma^{+},\partial G^{+}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-4\pi N_{c}C_{F}\int dk_{b}^{-}\int dk_{2}^{+}\delta(k_{2}^{+}-q^{+})\delta(k_{b}^{-}-q^{-})\bar{q}(x_{b})M^{(1)}_{\gamma^{+},\partial G^{+}}(0,k_{2}^{+})\frac{\partial\delta^{n-2}(q_{\perp})}{\partial q_{\perp}^{\rho}}\tilde{s}_{\perp}^{\rho}g_{\perp}^{\mu\nu}.

(51)

Thus, the total tree level result is

\displaystyle W_{tree}^{\mu\nu}=

\displaystyle W^{\mu\nu}_{non-de}+W^{\mu\nu}_{de}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{-\pi}{2N_{c}}\int dk_{b}^{-}\int dk_{2}^{+}\delta(k_{2}^{+}-q^{+})\delta(k_{b}^{-}-q^{-})\bar{q}(x_{b})M^{(1)}_{\gamma^{+},\partial G^{+}}(0,k_{2}^{+})\Big[\frac{\delta^{n-2}(q_{\perp})}{p_{a}\cdot q}(p_{a}^{\mu}\tilde{s}_{\perp}^{\nu}+p_{a}^{\nu}\tilde{s}_{\perp}^{\mu})+\tilde{s}_{\perp}^{\rho}\frac{\partial\delta^{n-2}(q_{\perp})}{\partial q_{\perp}^{\rho}}g_{\perp}^{\mu\nu}\Big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2N_{c}}\int\frac{dx_{b}}{x_{b}}\frac{dx_{2}}{x_{2}}\delta(1-\hat{x}_{2})\delta(1-\hat{x}_{b})\bar{q}(x_{b})T_{F}(x_{2},x_{2})\Big[\delta^{n-2}(q_{\perp})\frac{1}{p_{a}\cdot q}(p_{a}^{\mu}\tilde{s}_{\perp}^{\nu}+p_{a}^{\nu}\tilde{s}_{\perp}^{\mu})+\tilde{s}_{\perp}^{\rho}\frac{\partial\delta^{n-2}(q_{\perp})}{\partial q_{\perp}^{\rho}}g_{\perp}^{\mu\nu}\Big].

(52)

with

\displaystyle\xi\equiv\frac{q^{+}}{p_{a}^{+}},\ \tau_{\xi}\equiv\frac{\tau}{\xi}=\frac{Q^{2}}{s\xi},\ \hat{x}_{2}\equiv\frac{\xi}{x_{2}},\ \hat{x}_{b}\equiv\frac{\tau_{\xi}}{x_{b}}.

(53)

In the last equality we have used eq.(
39
) to change
M^{(1)}_{\gamma^{+},\partial_{\perp}G^{+}}
to
T_{F}
. Above
W^{\mu\nu}_{tree}
agrees with known result in
Ma and Zhang 2015
. It has been pointed out in
Ma and Zhang 2015
, such a structure of
W^{\mu\nu}

with a derivative in
\delta^{(n-2)}(q_{\perp})
satisfies QED gauge invariance in
the sense of distribution, i.e.,

\displaystyle\int d^{n-2}q_{\perp}t(q_{\perp})q_{\mu}W_{tree}^{\mu\nu}=0,

(54)

with
t(q_{\perp})
a test function which is normal at
q_{\perp}=0
.

V
Virtual corrections

In this section we present our results for one-loop virtual corrections. We
first give the corrections to hadronic tensor
W^{\mu\nu}
. Same as tree level

W^{\mu\nu}
, the virtual correction contains nonderivative part and derivative part, which are calculated separately.
Direct calculation of the
one-loop integrals is complicated because a lot of tensor integrals
are involved. A better method is to use FIRE
Smirnov 2008
to reduce these
tensor integrals to standard scalar integrals. The reduced integrals are
very simple: only standard two-point integrals remain. In the calculation, both
UV and IR divergences are regulated by dimensional regularization, and we do not
distinguish UV and IR divergences. UV divergences will be cancelled by
counter term contributions discussed in Sec.7. After corrections to
W^{\mu\nu}

are obtained, we give the result for
I\langle L\rangle
and
I\langle P_{7}\rangle
.

The diagrams we consider in this part are shown in Fig.
2
.
In order to get
the real part of
W^{\mu\nu}
in physical region, we have to make clear
the analyticity of the amplitude about
s_{0}
and
s_{1}
, where

\displaystyle s_{0}=(k+k_{b})^{2}=2k\cdot k_{b},\ s_{1}=(k_{1}+k_{b})^{2}=2k_{1}\cdot k_{b}.

(55)

We also define
s_{2}=s_{0}+s_{1}=2k_{2}\cdot k_{b}
, but do not use it to eliminate

s_{0}
or
s_{1}
in the amplitude. The elimination will break the analytic property
about
s_{0}
or
s_{1}
. Taking
s_{0},s_{1}
as variables is crucial for the extraction
of real part of
W^{\mu\nu}
.

Figure 2:
Diagrams for the hard part of one-loop virtual correction to
W^{\mu\nu}
. The right part
of (a-h) is not drawn, which is a tree level photon-quark vertex. The last diagram
(i) contains both left part and right part.

,

is analytic on the upper half planes of

(f) is

\displaystyle I=\int\frac{d^{n}k_{g}}{(2\pi)^{n}}\frac{1}{[(k_{b}+k_{g})^{2}+i\epsilon][(k_{b}+k+k_{g})^{2}+i\epsilon][(k_{1}-k_{g})^{2}+i\epsilon][k_{g}^{2}+i\epsilon]}.

(56)

With Feynman parameters and momentum shift,

can be integrated out. Then,

\displaystyle I\sim\int_{0}^{1}\prod dx_{i}\delta(1-x_{1}-x_{2}-x_{3}-x_{4})\Delta^{\frac{n}{2}-4},

(57)

with

\displaystyle\Delta=(x_{1}k_{b}+x_{2}(k_{b}+k)-x_{3}k)^{2}-x_{2}(k_{b}+k)^{2}-i\epsilon=-x_{2}(1-x_{1}-x_{2})s_{0}-x_{3}(x_{1}+x_{2})s_{1}-i\epsilon.

and

and

,
any scalar integral

\displaystyle I=\frac{1}{(s_{0}+i\epsilon)^{\alpha}(s_{1}+i\epsilon)^{\beta}(s_{0}+s_{1}+i\epsilon)^{\gamma}}f(s_{0},s_{1}),

(59)

with
f(s_{0},s_{1})
a polynomial of
s_{0},s_{1}
, and

some constants
depending on
\epsilon=4-n
. After this is clear, following calculation is straightforward. Reduced by FIRE,
all

\displaystyle B(s_{0})=

\displaystyle\mu^{\epsilon}\int\frac{d^{n}l}{(2\pi)^{n}}\frac{1}{[l^{2}+i\epsilon][(l+k_{b0})^{2}+i\epsilon]},

\displaystyle B(s_{1})=

\displaystyle\mu^{\epsilon}\int\frac{d^{n}l}{(2\pi)^{n}}\frac{1}{[l^{2}+i\epsilon][(l+k_{b1})^{2}+i\epsilon]},

\displaystyle B(s_{2})=

\displaystyle\mu^{\epsilon}\int\frac{d^{n}l}{(2\pi)^{n}}\frac{1}{[l^{2}+i\epsilon][(l+k_{b2})^{2}+i\epsilon]},

(60)

with
k_{b0}=k_{b}+k
,
k_{b1}=k_{b}+k_{1}
,
k_{b2}=k_{b}+k_{2}
.
Moreover, the complex conjugate of
B(s_{2})
is denoted by
B_{c}(s_{2})
, i.e.,

B_{c}(s_{2})=B^{*}(s_{2})
. The expression of
B(u)
for a general
u
is

\displaystyle B(u)=

\displaystyle i\frac{(4\pi\mu^{2})^{\epsilon/2}(-u-i\epsilon)^{-\epsilon/2}}{16\pi^{2}}\Gamma(1+\frac{\epsilon}{2})\frac{2}{\epsilon}B(1-\frac{\epsilon}{2},1-\frac{\epsilon}{2}).

(61)

If

is purely imaginary. Expansion in

\displaystyle B(u)=

\displaystyle i\frac{1}{16\pi^{2}}\Big(\frac{4\pi\mu^{2}}{|u|}\Big)^{\epsilon/2}\Gamma(1+\frac{\epsilon}{2})\Big[\frac{2}{\epsilon}+2+(2-\frac{\pi^{2}}{12})\epsilon+\theta(u)\Big(-\frac{\pi^{2}\epsilon}{4}\Big)+i\pi(1+\epsilon)\theta(u)+O(\epsilon^{2})\Big].

(62)

Now, the real part of

is made explicit. The real part
is finite.

We list nonderivative and derivative contributions
separately in the following.

V.1
Corrections to nonderivative part of
W^{\mu\nu}

Define

as

\displaystyle H_{i}^{\mu\nu}=\delta(k_{2}^{+}-q^{+})\delta(k_{b}^{-}-q^{-})\delta^{n-2}(q_{\perp})h_{i}^{\mu\nu},\ i=0,1,\cdots,6.

(63)

in

\tilde{s}_{\perp}

\displaystyle h_{i}^{\mu\nu}=

\displaystyle 2g_{s}^{2}\text{Re}\Big\{A_{i}t_{a}^{\mu\nu}+B_{i}t_{b}^{\mu\nu}\Big\},\ i=0,\cdots,6;

\displaystyle t_{a}^{\mu\nu}=

\displaystyle p_{a}^{\mu}\tilde{s}_{\perp}^{\nu}+p_{a}^{\nu}\tilde{s}_{\perp}^{\mu},\ t_{b}^{\mu\nu}=p_{b}^{\mu}\tilde{s}_{\perp}^{\nu}+p_{b}^{\nu}\tilde{s}_{\perp}^{\mu}.

(64)

Only real part is needed, as indicated by
\text{Re}\{\cdots\}
. The overall
factor 2 is due to conjugated diagrams. The coefficients
A_{i},B_{i}

are obtained directly from the diagrams in Fig.
2
. As stated before,
they are analytic on the upper half-planes of
s_{0},s_{1}
.

For
h_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{c}C_{F}}p_{b}^{-}A_{0}=

\displaystyle-\frac{x_{2}(\epsilon-2)C_{F}}{x_{b}}B\left(s_{2}\right),

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{c}C_{F}}p_{b}^{-}B_{0}=

\displaystyle-\frac{\left(\epsilon^{2}+8\right)C_{F}}{\epsilon}B\left(s_{2}\right).

(65)

For
\tilde{h}_{0}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{c}C_{F}}\tilde{A}_{0}=

\displaystyle-\frac{2x_{2}(\epsilon-2)C_{F}}{s_{2}}B\left(s_{2}\right),

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{c}C_{F}}\tilde{B}_{0}=

\displaystyle-\frac{2x_{b}\left(\epsilon^{2}+8\right)C_{F}}{s_{2}\epsilon}B\left(s_{2}\right).

(66)

For
h_{1}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{c}C_{F}}A_{1}=

\displaystyle\frac{2x_{2}}{s_{2}(x+i\epsilon)}\frac{-2+\epsilon}{\epsilon}\Big[xC_{A}(B(s_{0})-B(s_{2}))-\epsilon x_{2}C_{F}(B(s_{2})-B_{c}(s_{2}))\Big],

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{c}C_{F}}B_{1}=

\displaystyle\frac{2x_{b}}{s_{2}(x+i\epsilon)}\frac{1}{\epsilon}\Big[C_{A}(-2+\epsilon)\Big(x_{2}B(s_{0})-xB(s_{2})\Big)+C_{F}(8+\epsilon^{2})x_{2}\Big(B(s_{2})+B_{c}(s_{2})\Big)\Big].

(67)

For
h_{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{c}C_{F}}A_{2}=

\displaystyle\frac{2x_{2}}{s_{2}(x+i\epsilon)}\frac{-2+\epsilon}{\epsilon}\Big[C_{A}x\Big(B(s_{0})-B(s_{2})\Big)-C_{F}x_{2}\epsilon\Big(B(s_{2})-B_{c}(s_{2})\Big)\Big],

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{c}C_{F}}B_{2}=

\displaystyle\frac{2x_{b}}{s_{2}(x+i\epsilon)}\frac{1}{\epsilon}\Big[C_{A}(-2+\epsilon)\Big(xB(s_{2})-x_{2}B(s_{0})\Big)+C_{F}(8-4\epsilon+\epsilon^{2})x_{2}\Big(B(s_{2})-B_{c}(s_{2})\Big)\Big].

(68)

For
h_{3}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{c}C_{F}}p_{a}^{+}A_{3}=

\displaystyle-\frac{2x_{2}}{s_{2}(x+i\epsilon)(x_{1}+i\epsilon)}\frac{-2+\epsilon}{\epsilon}\Big[C_{A}x\Big(B(s_{0})-B(s_{2})\Big)-\epsilon C_{F}x_{1}\Big(B(s_{2})+B_{c}(s_{2})\Big)\Big],

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{c}C_{F}}p_{a}^{+}B_{3}=

\displaystyle\frac{2x_{b}}{s_{2}(x+i\epsilon)(x_{1}+i\epsilon)}\frac{1}{\epsilon}\Big[C_{A}(-2+\epsilon)\Big(x_{2}B(s_{0})-xB(s_{2})\Big)+C_{F}(8+\epsilon^{2})x_{1}\Big(B(s_{2})+B_{c}(s_{2})\Big)\Big].

(69)

For
h_{4}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{c}C_{F}}p_{a}^{+}A_{4}=

\displaystyle-\frac{2x_{2}(-2+\epsilon)}{s_{2}(x+i\epsilon)(x_{1}+i\epsilon)}\frac{1}{\epsilon}\Big[C_{A}x\Big(B(s_{0})-B(s_{2})\Big)-C_{F}\epsilon x_{1}\Big(B(s_{2})-B_{c}(s_{2})\Big)\Big],

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{c}C_{F}}p_{a}^{+}B_{4}=

\displaystyle\frac{2x_{b}}{s_{2}(x+i\epsilon)(x_{1}+i\epsilon)}\frac{1}{\epsilon}\Big[C_{A}(-2+\epsilon)\Big(x_{2}B(s_{0})-xB(s_{2})\Big)+C_{F}(8+\epsilon^{2})x_{1}\Big(B(s_{2})-B_{c}(s_{2})\Big)\Big].

(70)

For
h_{5}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{c}C_{F}}p_{a}^{+}A_{5}=

\displaystyle-\frac{2x_{2}(2C_{A}+\epsilon C_{F})}{s_{2}(x_{1}+i\epsilon)}\Big(B(s_{0})-B(s_{2})\Big)

\displaystyle+\frac{2(C_{A}-2C_{F})x_{2}^{2}(-4+\epsilon^{2})}{s_{2}(x+i\epsilon)^{2}\epsilon}\Big(B(s_{1})-B(s_{2})\Big)

\displaystyle-\frac{2x_{2}}{s_{2}(x+i\epsilon)\epsilon}\Big[\epsilon(2C_{A}+\epsilon C_{F})B(s_{0})-2\Big(C_{A}\epsilon+C_{F}(4-3\epsilon+\epsilon^{2})\Big)B(s_{2})+C_{F}(8+\epsilon^{2})B_{c}(s_{2})\Big],

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{c}C_{F}}p_{a}^{+}B_{5}=

\displaystyle\frac{2x_{b}(2C_{A}+\epsilon C_{F})}{s_{2}(x_{1}+i\epsilon)}\Big(B(s_{0})-B(s_{2})\Big)

\displaystyle-\frac{2(C_{A}-2C_{F})x_{2}x_{b}}{s_{2}(x+i\epsilon)^{2}\epsilon}\Big(B(s_{1})-B(s_{2})\Big)

\displaystyle+\frac{2x_{b}}{s_{2}(x+i\epsilon)\epsilon}\Big(\epsilon(2C_{A}+\epsilon C_{F})B(s_{0})-2\epsilon(C_{A}-2C_{F})B(s_{2})+(8+\epsilon^{2})C_{F}B_{c}(s_{2})\Big).

(71)

For
h_{6}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{c}C_{F}}p_{a}^{+}(A_{5}-A_{6})=

\displaystyle\frac{4C_{F}x_{2}(-2+\epsilon)}{s_{2}(x+i\epsilon)}B_{c}(s_{2}),

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{c}C_{F}}p_{a}^{+}(B_{5}-B_{6})=

\displaystyle\frac{4C_{F}x_{b}(8+\epsilon^{2})}{s_{2}(x+i\epsilon)\epsilon}B_{c}(s_{2}).

(72)

By using the formula

\displaystyle\frac{1}{x+i\epsilon}=P\frac{1}{x}-i\pi\delta(x),

(73)

P\frac{1}{x}
or
\delta(x)
may contribute to the real parts of
A_{i},B_{i}
, since

B(s_{i})
is complex for general
s_{i}
. The contribution proportional to
\delta(x)

(or
P\frac{1}{x}
) is called pole contribution (or non-pole contribution).
Pole contribution may be proportional to
\delta(x)
or
\delta(x_{1})
. The former is called soft-gluon-pole(SGP)
contribution, and the latter is called soft-fermion-pole(SFP) contribution.
Before proceeding, we should show that SFP does not contribute to
h_{i}
, otherwise,
the PV in eq.(
25
) for
x_{1}
is ill-defined.

From the explicit results of
h_{i}
,
h_{3,4,5,6}
may contain SFP contributions, which are given by following combination

\displaystyle\frac{B(s_{0})-B(s_{2})}{x_{1}+i\epsilon},\ \text{or}\ \frac{x_{2}B(s_{0})-xB(s_{2})}{x_{1}+i\epsilon}.

(74)

Under the limit
x_{1}\rightarrow 0
, we have
x=x_{2}
,
s_{0}=s_{2}
, so,

\displaystyle\delta(x_{1})(B(s_{0})-B(s_{2}))=0,\ \delta(x_{1})(x_{2}B(s_{0})-xB(s_{2}))=0.

(75)

Thus, SFP contribution vanishes, and all
h_{i}
’s are well defined at
x_{1}=0
and
PV in eq.(
25
) for
x_{1}
is well defined.

Similarly,
g_{i}^{\mu\nu}
is decomposed as

\displaystyle g_{i}^{\mu\nu}=2g_{s}^{2}\text{Re}\Big[C_{i}t_{a}^{\mu\nu}+D_{i}t_{b}^{\mu\nu}\Big]\delta^{n}(k_{2}+k_{b}-q),

(76)

with
i=0,1,\cdots,4
. Using eq.(
30
),
we get the coefficients
C_{i},D_{i}
as follows.

For
g_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle C_{0}=D_{0}=0.

(77)

For
g_{1}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{c}C_{F}}p_{a}^{+}C_{1}=

\displaystyle 0,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{c}C_{F}}p_{a}^{+}D_{1}=

\displaystyle\frac{4C_{F}(2x_{2}-x)x_{b}}{s_{2}x_{1}}\frac{4-\epsilon+\epsilon^{2}}{\epsilon}\frac{1}{x+i\epsilon}\Big(B(s_{2})-B_{c}(s_{2})\Big).

(78)

Because
B(s_{2})-B_{c}(s_{2})
is purely imaginary, only SGP gives nonzero contribution.
Nonpole contribution is zero.
1/x_{1}
is a PV, which is introduced by EOM relations.
For
g_{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{c}C_{F}}p_{a}^{+}C_{2}=

\displaystyle 0,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{c}C_{F}}p_{a}^{+}D_{2}=

\displaystyle\frac{4C_{F}x_{b}}{s_{2}x_{1}}\frac{4-\epsilon+\epsilon^{2}}{\epsilon}\Big(B(s_{2})-B_{c}(s_{2})\Big).

(79)

D_{2}
has no pole contribution. Because
B(s_{2})-B_{c}(s_{2})
is purely imaginary, we have
\text{Re}D_{2}=0
. As a result,
g_{2}=0
.

For
g_{3}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{c}C_{F}}p_{a}^{+}C_{3}=

\displaystyle\frac{2x_{2}}{s_{2}}\Big[-\frac{2C_{A}+\epsilon C_{F}}{x_{1}}\Big(B(s_{0})-B(s_{2})\Big)+\frac{(C_{A}-2C_{F})x_{2}}{(x+i\epsilon)^{2}}\frac{\epsilon^{2}-4}{\epsilon}\Big(B(s_{1})-B(s_{2})\Big)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{x+i\epsilon}\Big(-(2C_{A}+\epsilon C_{F})B(s_{0})+2(C_{A}+C_{F}\frac{4-3\epsilon+\epsilon^{2}}{\epsilon})B(s_{2})+2C_{F}\frac{-4+\epsilon-\epsilon^{2}}{\epsilon}B_{c}(s_{2})\Big)\Big],

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{c}C_{F}}p_{a}^{+}D_{3}=

\displaystyle\frac{2x_{b}}{s_{2}}\Big[\frac{2C_{A}+\epsilon C_{F}}{x_{1}}\Big(B(s_{0})-B(s_{2})\Big)-\frac{(C_{A}-2C_{F})x_{2}}{(x+i\epsilon)^{2}}\frac{\epsilon^{2}-4}{\epsilon}\Big(B(s_{1})-B(s_{2})\Big)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{x+i\epsilon}\Big((2C_{A}+\epsilon C_{F})B(s_{0})+2(-C_{A}+C_{F}\frac{4+\epsilon+\epsilon^{2}}{\epsilon})B(s_{2})+2C_{F}\frac{-4+\epsilon-\epsilon^{2}}{\epsilon}B_{c}(s_{2})\Big)\Big].

(80)

For
g_{4}
, we notice that
g_{3}-g_{4}
is very simple,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{c}C_{F}}p_{a}^{+}(C_{3}-C_{4})=

\displaystyle 0,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{c}C_{F}}p_{a}^{+}(D_{3}-D_{4})=

\displaystyle\frac{8C_{F}x_{b}}{s_{2}(x+i\epsilon)}\frac{4-\epsilon+\epsilon^{2}}{\epsilon}\Big(B(s_{2})-B_{c}(s_{2})\Big).

(81)

For convenience, we define
\Delta g^{\mu\nu}\equiv g_{3}^{\mu\nu}-g_{4}^{\mu\nu}
.
Its expression is shown above. As can be seen,
\Delta g
contains only SGP contribution.

As a summary, we find
g_{0}=g_{2}=0
.
g_{1}
is nonzero, but contains only SGP contribution. Considering eq.(
41
), we have
\tilde{g}_{1}=0
.
Thus, QCD gauge invariance is preserved.
g_{3},g_{4}
contain SGP and non-pole contributions. Next, we present the results separately.

V.1.1
SGP contribution

The limit
x\rightarrow 0
in
g_{i}
gives SGP contribution. What is special
in dimensional regularization is
B(s_{0})=0
if
s_{0}=0
. In addition,

B(s_{1})
should be expanded near
s_{2}
since in physical region
s_{1}=s_{2}-s_{0}
.

\displaystyle B(s_{1})-B(s_{2})=\frac{\epsilon}{2}\frac{s_{0}}{s_{2}}B(s_{2})+\frac{\epsilon}{4}(1+\frac{\epsilon}{2})\frac{s_{0}^{2}}{s_{2}^{2}}B(s_{2})+O(s_{0}^{3}).

(82)

s_{0}
in right hand side eliminates the double pole
1/(x+i\epsilon)^{2}
in
C_{3},D_{4}
. After this we get SGP contribution by the replacement
1/(x+i\epsilon)\rightarrow-i\pi\delta(x)
. The results are

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{c}C_{F}}p_{a}^{+}C_{1}^{SGP}=

\displaystyle 0,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{c}C_{F}}p_{a}^{+}D_{1}^{SGP}=

\displaystyle\delta(x)\frac{16\pi C_{F}x_{b}}{s_{2}}\frac{4-\epsilon+\epsilon^{2}}{\epsilon}\text{Im}\Big(B(s_{2})\Big),

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{c}C_{F}}p_{a}^{+}C_{3}^{SGP}=

\displaystyle\delta(x)\frac{8\pi C_{F}x_{2}}{s_{2}}\frac{4-\epsilon+\epsilon^{2}}{\epsilon}\text{Im}\Big(B(s_{2})\Big),

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{c}C_{F}}p_{a}^{+}D_{3}^{SGP}=

\displaystyle\delta(x)\frac{8\pi C_{F}x_{b}}{s_{2}}\frac{4-\epsilon+\epsilon^{2}}{\epsilon}\text{Im}\Big(B(s_{2})\Big).

(83)

So,

\displaystyle C_{3}^{SGP}/x_{2}=D_{3}^{SGP}/x_{b},\ D_{1}^{SGP}=2D_{3}^{SGP}.

(84)

These two relations are important.

As discussed in Sec.
III
,
it is
-\frac{1}{2}g_{1}+g_{3}
that gives the final contribution
from
T_{F}(x_{2},x_{2})
. From above result, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{c}C_{F}}p_{a}^{+}(-\frac{1}{2}C_{1}^{SGP}+C_{3}^{SGP})=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{c}C_{F}}p_{a}^{+}C_{3}^{SGP},

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{c}C_{F}}p_{a}^{+}(-\frac{1}{2}D_{1}^{SGP}+D_{3}^{SGP})=

\displaystyle 0.

(85)

The vanishing of the second equation indicates SGP part has the same
tensor structure as tree level.

For
g_{4}
, because
T_{\Delta}(x_{1},x_{2})=-T_{\Delta}(x_{2},x_{1})
,
the SGP contribution does not exist.

In summary, SGP contribution to the nonderivative part of
W^{\mu\nu}
is

\displaystyle 8N_{c}^{2}C_{F}W^{\mu\nu}_{SGP}=

\displaystyle-\frac{2g_{s}^{2}}{\pi}t_{a}^{\mu\nu}\int dk_{b}^{-}\int dk^{+}dk_{1}^{+}\bar{q}(x_{b})\delta^{n}(k+k_{1}+k_{b}-q)C_{3}^{SGP}T_{F}(x_{2},x_{2}).

(86)

V.1.2
Non-pole contribution

For non-pole contribution,
x\neq 0
, and only the real part of
B(s_{i})
contributes to
g_{i}
.
The combination
B(s_{2})-B_{c}(s_{2})
is purely imaginary and does not
contribute. So,
g_{0,1,2}
are zero.
Moreover, the difference between
D_{3}
and
D_{4}
is proportional to
B(s_{2})-B_{c}(s_{2})
, thus the non-pole part of
g_{3}
and
g_{4}

is the same, i.e.,

\displaystyle C_{4}^{NP}=C_{3}^{NP},\ D_{4}^{NP}=D_{3}^{NP}.

(87)

For
C_{3}^{NP}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{c}C_{F}}p_{a}^{+}C^{NP}_{3}=

\displaystyle-\frac{4x_{2}C_{A}}{x_{1}s_{2}}\text{Re}\Big(B(s_{0})-B(s_{2})\Big)-\frac{4x_{2}}{xs_{2}}\text{Re}\Big(C_{A}B(s_{0})-(C_{A}-2C_{F})B(s_{2})\Big)

\displaystyle-\frac{8(C_{A}-2C_{F})}{\epsilon}\frac{x_{2}^{2}}{s_{2}x^{2}}\text{Re}\Big(B(s_{1})-B(s_{2})\Big).

(88)

Because
s_{0},s_{1}
can be negative or positive,
C_{3}^{NP}
is non-zero.
Note that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{c}C_{F}}p_{a}^{+}\Big[\frac{1}{x_{2}}C_{3}+\frac{1}{x_{b}}D_{3}\Big]=

\displaystyle\frac{8C_{F}}{s_{2}(x+i\epsilon)}\frac{4-\epsilon+\epsilon^{2}}{\epsilon}(B(s_{2})-B_{c}(s_{2})),

(89)

which does not contain non-pole contribution.
So, the non-pole parts of
C_{3}
and
D_{3}
satisfy

\displaystyle\frac{1}{x_{2}}C_{3}^{NP}+\frac{1}{x_{b}}D_{3}^{NP}=0.

(90)

In summary, for non-derivative part of
W^{\mu\nu}
, the non-pole contribution is

\displaystyle 8N_{c}^{2}C_{F}W^{\mu\nu}_{NP}=

\displaystyle-\frac{2g_{s}^{2}}{\pi}\int dk_{b}^{-}\int dk^{+}dk_{1}^{+}\bar{q}(x_{b})\delta^{n}(k_{b}+k+k_{1}-q)\Big(C_{3}^{NP}t_{a}^{\mu\nu}+D_{3}^{NP}t_{b}^{\mu\nu}\Big)\Big[T_{F}(x_{1},x_{2})+T_{\Delta}(x_{1},x_{2})\Big].

(91)

Both
t_{a}^{\mu\nu}
and
t_{b}^{\mu\nu}
appear in this part, while only
t_{a}^{\mu\nu}
appears in SGP part and in tree level result. Moreover,
the coefficients here are divergent. Different from twist-2 cases, the new
tensor structure with divergent coefficients does not imply a breaking of
factorization, as we will explain later.

V.2
Corrections to derivative part of
W^{\mu\nu}

Similar to tree level contribution, for this part, only
H_{4},H_{6}
are nonzero and
only SGP contribution is possible. We have

\displaystyle 8N_{c}^{2}C_{F}W_{de}^{\mu\nu}=

\displaystyle\int dk_{b}^{-}\int dk^{+}dk_{1}^{+}\delta(q^{-}-k_{b}^{-})\delta(k^{+}+k_{1}^{+}-q^{+})(H_{6}^{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}H_{4}^{\mu\nu})M^{(1)}_{\gamma^{+},\partial G^{+}},

(92)

and

\displaystyle H_{6}^{\mu\nu}=H_{4}^{\mu\nu}=-i\frac{\partial\delta^{n-2}(q_{\perp})}{\partial q_{\perp}^{\rho}}\tilde{s}_{\perp}^{\rho}Tr[H^{\mu\nu}\otimes T^{a}\gamma^{-}].

(93)

Because
k_{\perp}=k_{1\perp}=0
in the trace, the trace is an on-shell quantity.
After calculation, we have

\displaystyle 8N_{c}^{2}C_{F}W^{\mu\nu}_{de}=

\displaystyle-\frac{2g_{s}^{2}}{\pi}\int dk_{b}^{-}\int dk^{+}dk_{1}^{+}\delta(q^{-}-k_{b}^{-})\delta(k^{+}+k_{1}^{+}-q^{+})T_{F}(x_{2},x_{2})Eg_{\perp}^{\mu\nu}\frac{\partial\delta^{n-2}(q_{\perp})}{\partial q_{\perp}^{\rho}}\tilde{s}_{\perp}^{\rho}.

(94)

Both
\mu,\nu
are transverse. The coefficient
E
is related to
C_{3}^{SGP}
as follows

\displaystyle g_{s}^{2}E=g_{s}^{2}p_{a}\cdot qC_{3}^{SGP}=

\displaystyle\alpha_{s}\frac{N_{c}C_{F}^{2}}{x_{2}p_{a}^{+}}\delta(x)\Big(\frac{4\pi\mu^{2}}{Q^{2}}\Big)^{\epsilon/2}\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\frac{\epsilon}{2})}\Big[\frac{8}{\epsilon^{2}}+\frac{6}{\epsilon}+8-\pi^{2}+O(\epsilon)\Big],

(95)

which is just the correction to quark form factor, as pointed out in
Chen et al. 2017
. Note that
s_{2}=Q^{2}
for virtual correction.

V.3
Total virtual corrections

Now, the complete virtual correction to hadronic tensor is the sum of eqs.(
86
,
91
,
94
), that is,

\displaystyle 8N_{c}^{2}C_{F}W^{\mu\nu}=

\displaystyle 8N_{c}^{2}C_{F}[W^{\mu\nu}_{SGP}+W^{\mu\nu}_{NP}+W^{\mu\nu}_{de}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{2g_{s}^{2}}{\pi}\int dk_{b}^{-}\bar{q}(x_{b})\int dk_{2}^{+}\delta(k_{b}^{-}-q^{-})\delta(k_{2}^{+}-q^{+})\Big\{

\displaystyle\int dk^{+}T_{F}(x_{2},x_{2})\Big[\delta^{n-2}(q_{\perp})t_{a}^{\mu\nu}+\tilde{s}_{\perp}^{\rho}\frac{\partial\delta^{n-2}(q_{\perp})}{\partial q_{\perp}^{\rho}}g_{\perp}^{\mu\nu}p_{a}\cdot q\Big]C_{3}^{SGP}

\displaystyle+\int dk^{+}\delta^{n-2}(q_{\perp})\Big(C_{3}^{NP}t_{a}^{\mu\nu}+D_{3}^{NP}t_{b}^{\mu\nu}\Big)\Big(T_{F}(x_{1},x_{2})+T_{\Delta}(x_{1},x_{2})\Big)\Big\}.

(96)

If there is no non-pole contribution, the virtual correction has the
same structure as tree level hadronic tensor. The pole contribution, the last second
line of above result, satisfies QED gauge invariance, as shown for tree level hadronic tensor. The
correction to derivative part is the same as quark form factor, in agreement with
the conclusion of
Chen et al. 2017
. In
Chen et al. 2017
, such correction is
inferred from Ward identity for longitudinal gluon
G^{+}
. Here we recover the result
by direct calculation. The non-pole contribution, the last line, also satisfies such invariance: with
q_{\perp}=0
,

\displaystyle q_{\mu}\Big(C_{3,NP}t_{a}^{\mu\nu}+D_{3,NP}t_{b}^{\mu\nu}\Big)=

\displaystyle\tilde{s}_{\perp}^{\nu}p_{a}\cdot p_{b}\Big(x_{b}C_{3,NP}+x_{2}D_{3,NP}\Big).

(97)

Due to eq.(
90
), this is zero. Thus, QED gauge invariance is satisfied.

From
W^{\mu\nu}
,
I\langle L\rangle
and
I\langle P_{7}\rangle
can be
obtained easily,

\displaystyle I\langle L\rangle\Big|_{v}=

\displaystyle I\langle L\rangle\Big|_{v}^{SGP}+I\langle L\rangle\Big|_{v}^{NP},

\displaystyle I\langle P_{7}\rangle\Big|_{v}=

\displaystyle I\langle P_{7}\rangle\Big|_{v}^{SGP}.

(98)

For SGP part, we have

\displaystyle I\langle L\rangle\Big|^{SGP}_{v}=

\displaystyle 2\times l_{\perp,cs}\cdot\tilde{s}_{\perp}\cos\theta\frac{\alpha_{s}}{128\pi N_{c}^{2}}\bar{A}_{\epsilon}\int\frac{d\xi}{\xi}\frac{dx_{b}}{x_{b}}\frac{dx_{2}}{x_{2}}\bar{q}(x_{b})T_{F}(x_{2},x_{2})\delta(1-\hat{x}_{2})\delta(1-\hat{x}_{b})

\displaystyle\times(-32)(N_{c}^{2}-1)\Big(\frac{8}{\epsilon^{2}}+\frac{6}{\epsilon}+8-\pi^{2}\Big),

\displaystyle I\langle P_{7}\rangle\Big|^{SGP}_{v}=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4l_{\perp,cs}\cdot\tilde{s}_{\perp}\cos\theta}I\langle L\rangle\Big|^{SGP}_{v}.

(99)

The overall factor
2
in the first equation comes from the contribution of conjugated diagrams.

Only
I\langle L\rangle

VI
Real corrections

For real corrections,
q_{\perp}\neq 0
is assumed.
W^{\mu\nu}
thus depends on

p_{a}^{\mu},p_{b}^{\mu},q_{\perp}^{\mu}
and
\tilde{s}_{\perp}^{\mu}
. Because of QED gauge invariance

q_{\mu}W^{\mu\nu}=0
, there are six independent tensor structures for transversely polarized case
Arnold et al. 2009
. For our convenience, the tensors can be chosen as

\displaystyle P_{1,\ldots,4}^{\mu\nu}=

\displaystyle\tilde{s}_{\perp}\cdot q_{\perp}\{g_{\perp}^{\mu\nu}-\frac{q_{\perp}^{\mu}q_{\perp}^{\nu}}{q_{\perp}^{2}},\ \tilde{p}_{a}^{\mu}\tilde{p}_{a}^{\nu},\tilde{p}_{b}^{\mu}\tilde{p}_{b}^{\nu},\tilde{p}_{a}^{\mu}\tilde{p}_{b}^{\nu}+\tilde{p}_{b}^{\mu}\tilde{p}_{a}^{\nu}\},

\displaystyle P_{5,6}^{\mu\nu}=

\displaystyle\{\tilde{p}_{a}^{\mu}\Big(\tilde{s}_{\perp}^{\nu}-\frac{\tilde{s}_{\perp}\cdot q_{\perp}}{q_{\perp}^{2}}q_{\perp}^{\nu}\Big)+(\mu\leftrightarrow\nu),\ \tilde{p}_{b}^{\mu}\Big(\tilde{s}_{\perp}^{\nu}-\frac{\tilde{s}_{\perp}\cdot q_{\perp}}{q_{\perp}^{2}}q_{\perp}^{\nu}\Big)+(\mu\leftrightarrow\nu)\},

(103)

with
\tilde{p}_{i}^{\mu}=p_{i}^{\mu}-p_{i}\cdot qq^{\mu}/q^{2}
, so that
q\cdot\tilde{p}_{i}=0
.

W^{\mu\nu}
is decomposed as

\displaystyle W^{\mu\nu}=\sum_{i=1}^{6}W_{i}P_{i}^{\mu\nu},

(104)

with
W_{i}
a scalar function of
p_{a},p_{b},q
.
To solve
W_{i}
, we first contract both sides of eq.(
104
)
with
P_{j}^{\mu\nu}
to get

\displaystyle P_{j}\cdot W=\sum_{i=1}^{6}W_{i}P_{i}\cdot P_{j}.

(105)

The dot product represents the contraction of Lorentz indices, e.g.,
P_{j}\cdot W=P_{j,\mu\nu}W^{\mu\nu}
. Eq.(
105
) can be solved directly. But the
coefficients before
P_{i}\cdot W
may depend on spin vector
\tilde{s}_{\perp}
in a very complicated
way, since
P_{i}\cdot P_{j}
depends on
\tilde{s}_{\perp}
. Noticing that

W_{i}
does not depend on the direction of
q_{\perp}
, we can integrate out
the angles of
q_{\perp}
on both sides of eq.(
105
)
and then to solve the obtained equations. According
to this treatment, we successfully get

\displaystyle W_{i}=\frac{2-\epsilon}{\Omega_{n-2}\tilde{s}_{\perp}^{2}}C_{ij}\xi_{j},\ \xi_{j}\equiv\int d\Omega_{n-2}P_{j}\cdot W,

(106)

where
d\Omega_{n-2}
is for
q_{\perp}
.
After integration,
\xi_{j}
depends on
\tilde{s}_{\perp}^{2},q_{\perp}^{2},q\cdot p_{a},q\cdot p_{b}
.
The coefficients
C_{ij}
do not depend on
\tilde{s}_{\perp}
,
whose expressions are

\displaystyle C_{11}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(\epsilon-1)q_{t}^{2}},

\displaystyle C_{22}=

\displaystyle-\frac{\left(q\cdot p_{b}\right){}^{4}}{q_{t}^{6}\left(p_{a}\cdot p_{b}\right){}^{4}},

\displaystyle C_{23}=

\displaystyle-\frac{\left(q_{t}^{2}p_{a}\cdot p_{b}-q\cdot p_{a}q\cdot p_{b}\right){}^{2}}{q_{t}^{6}\left(p_{a}\cdot p_{b}\right){}^{4}},

\displaystyle C_{24}=

\displaystyle\frac{\left(q\cdot p_{b}\right){}^{2}\left(q_{t}^{2}p_{a}\cdot p_{b}-q\cdot p_{a}q\cdot p_{b}\right)}{q_{t}^{6}\left(p_{a}\cdot p_{b}\right){}^{4}},

\displaystyle C_{33}=

\displaystyle-\frac{\left(q\cdot p_{a}\right){}^{4}}{q_{t}^{6}\left(p_{a}\cdot p_{b}\right){}^{4}},

\displaystyle C_{34}=

\displaystyle\frac{\left(q\cdot p_{a}\right){}^{2}\left(q_{t}^{2}p_{a}\cdot p_{b}-q\cdot p_{a}q\cdot p_{b}\right)}{q_{t}^{6}\left(p_{a}\cdot p_{b}\right){}^{4}},

\displaystyle C_{44}=

\displaystyle-\frac{q_{t}^{4}\left(p_{a}\cdot p_{b}\right){}^{2}-2q_{t}^{2}p_{a}\cdot p_{b}q\cdot p_{a}q\cdot p_{b}+2\left(q\cdot p_{a}\right){}^{2}\left(q\cdot p_{b}\right){}^{2}}{2q_{t}^{6}\left(p_{a}\cdot p_{b}\right){}^{4}},

\displaystyle C_{55}=

\displaystyle\frac{\left(q\cdot p_{b}\right){}^{2}}{2(\epsilon-1)q_{t}^{2}\left(p_{a}\cdot p_{b}\right){}^{2}},

\displaystyle C_{56}=

\displaystyle\frac{q\cdot p_{a}q\cdot p_{b}-q_{t}^{2}p_{a}\cdot p_{b}}{2(\epsilon-1)q_{t}^{2}\left(p_{a}\cdot p_{b}\right){}^{2}},

\displaystyle C_{66}=

\displaystyle\frac{\left(q\cdot p_{a}\right){}^{2}}{2(\epsilon-1)q_{t}^{2}\left(p_{a}\cdot p_{b}\right){}^{2}}.

(107)

Notice that
C_{ij}
is symmetric,
C_{ij}=C_{ji}
.
q_{t}=|\vec{q}_{\perp}|

\displaystyle I\langle L\rangle=

and

IX
Summary

In this work, we have calculated the one-loop correction to unweighted SSA in
DY for the lepton angular distribution. As a comparison, we also calculate
a weighted observable
I\langle P_{7}\rangle
with the same method. We use
at most one
G^{+}
to do collinear expansion, and then use EOM for fermion
to eliminate the bad component of fermion field. After this, the hadronic
tensor or cross section is expressed by independent twist-3 distribution
functions. Three of these functions are
q_{\partial}
,
T_{F}
and
T_{\Delta}
.
For virtual
correction, we first determine that the hard part is analytic on the upper
half planes of
s_{0},s_{1}
. After this is clear, we use FIRE to reduce the
various tensor integrals in the hard part to standard scalar integrals.
Due to the simple kinematics, the reduced scalar integrals are three bubble
integrals
B(s_{0})
,
B(s_{1})
and
B(s_{2})
. Then, the structure of virtual
correction becomes clear. We successfully extract pole part and
non-pole part. For pole part, only SGP is possible. QCD and QED gauge
invariance are maintained in the calculation. On the other hand, for real
correction, we first assume
q_{\perp}\neq 0
. Then, the tensor structure of

W^{\mu\nu}
is worked out. With the projection tensors we calculate
the real corrections to
I\langle L\rangle
in the same way as
I\langle P_{7}\rangle
.
Only three-point distribution functions contribute at one-loop level. The contributions are classified into SGP, HP and SFP contributions. Corresponding
contributions are worked out explicitly. In this work, only contributions from

\bar{q}(x_{b})
and
T_{F}(x_{1},x_{2})
are retained. For both virtual and real corrections,

I\langle P_{7}\rangle
is the same as our previous result
Chen et al. 2017
.
Then, the collinear
subtraction is performed. We find all divergences in
I\langle P_{7}\rangle

can be subtracted out, while a single pole in
I\langle L\rangle
cannot be
subtracted. The single pole is proportional to
N_{c}C_{A}/\epsilon
and
\delta(1-\hat{x}_{2})\delta(1-\hat{x}_{b})
. In addition, we find the divergent nonpole part of virtual
correction can be viewed as a part of HP contribution of real correction, and
can be subtracted without any problem. The only term to break the factorization
is the single pole term we mentioned above. We also analyze the source for this
unsubtracted divergence under eikonal approximation for real HP corrections. We
find in
W^{\mu\nu}
a tensor appears with a soft divergence, but such a tensor
in virtual correction has no corresponding soft divergence. We thus conclude
that it is this uncancelled soft divergence that breaks the factorization.
Very recently, the one-loop correction to SSA in lepton-hadron scattering is
calculated in
Rein et al. 2025a
;
Rein et al. 2025b
. No breaking of factorization is
found. In future, we will study this process using present method. Moreover, the
breaking of factorization found here depends on collinear expansion. We would
like to check this conclusion in future with the multiparton state proposed in

Cao et al. 2010
;
Ma et al. 2012
.

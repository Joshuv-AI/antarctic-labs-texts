Chern-Simons deformations of the gauged O(3) Sigma model on compact surfaces
René I. García-Lara

Abstract
Existence of solutions to the field equations of the gauged Chern-Simons-O(3)-Sigma model on a compact Riemann surface is proved by a topological method. Existence of a minimal deformation constant κ ∗ > 0 \kappa_{*}>0 is proved, such that for any prescribed configuration of vortices and antivortices, at least one solution exists for | κ | ≤ κ ∗ |\kappa|\leq\kappa_{*} . For small values of the Chern-Simons deformation parameter κ \kappa , it is proved that the field equations admit multiple solutions, provided the total number of vortices and antivortices are different. The Maxwell limit is computed for solutions of the field equations. In contrast, if the number of vortices equals the number of antivortices, it is proved that the field equations admit at least one solution for any value of κ \kappa and the limit κ → ∞ \kappa\to\infty is proved. Dependence of the fields on the deformation parameter is investigated numerically on the sphere.



Keywords:
Chern-Simons, O(3)-Sigma model, Asymptotic behaviour

Introduction

The
O(3)
-Sigma model is a well known field theory model in
2+1
dimensions for a matter field coupled to a gauge field with
U(1)
symmetry
[
23
]
. What makes this model interesting is that it admits topological soliton solutions, determined by a finite set of core points on space of two different types, the vortex and antivortex positions. The moduli space of vortices-antivortices is the finite dimensional space of gauge equivalence classes of solutions to the field equations determined by the core set. A thorough study of the moduli space can be found in reference
[
21
]

Let

Extend the metric to the Lorentzian manifold

For the next lemma, recall that in any compact Riemannian manifold

Lemma 5

Let

be a compact Riemannian manifold. If

is a function such that

{\|u\|}_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\alpha({\|\nabla^{2}u\|}_{L^{2}}+\langle Vu,u\rangle_{L^{2}}),

where

(2.16)

and

is Poincare’s constant.

Proof.

Let

and
\bar{u}
are

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\langle Vu,u\rangle_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\int_{M}V{\tilde{u}}^{2}+2\bar{u}\int_{M}V\tilde{u}+\bar{u}^{2}\int_{M}V

\displaystyle\geq 2\bar{u}\int_{M}V\tilde{u}+\bar{u}^{2}\int_{M}V

\displaystyle|\bar{u}|\leq\frac{2{\left(\|V\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\tilde{u}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\langle Vu,u\rangle_{L^{2}}\int_{M}V\right)}^{1/2}}{\int_{M}V}.

\displaystyle{\bar{u}}^{2}|M|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4|M|}{{\left(\int_{M}V\right)}^{2}}\left(\|V\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\tilde{u}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\langle Vu,u\rangle_{L^{2}}\int_{M}V\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{e^{\bar{h}_{i}}e^{v_{+}}K_{2}}{{(e^{\bar{h}_{i}}e^{v_{+}}K_{1}+1)}^{2}}(K^{2}+2K|\nabla v|+|\nabla v|^{2}).

(4.4)

Assume

is sufficiently small for the existence of a chart

, where

is the unit euclidean disk. Assume further

\Omega(dx_{1}^{2}+dx_{2}^{2})
. Let us denote by

where

and

Therefore,

is bounded. The case

Proposition 19

5
Symmetric deformations on the
sphere

In this section we study Chern-Simons deformations on the sphere.
Assume vortices are located at the north pole
of the round sphere, whereas antivortices are located at the south pole.
Choose trivialisations
\phi_{\pm}:U_{\pm}\to\mathbb{S}^{2}
, where

U_{\pm}=\mathbb{S}^{2}\setminus\{(0,0,\mp 1)\}
, and stereographic projection charts from the south or north pole respectively,

\varphi_{\pm}:U_{\pm}\to\mathbb{C}
. These projections are
related by a gauge transformation, which by spherical symmetry is,

\varphi_{+}=\frac{e^{in\theta}}{\varphi_{-}},\hskip 18.49988ptx\in U_{+}\cap U_{-}.

If the connection is represented locally by
a_{\pm}\in\Omega^{1}(U_{\pm})
, then

a_{+}=a_{-}+nd\theta,\hskip 18.49988ptx\in U_{+}\cap U_{-}.

Stereographic coordinates will be denoted

x_{\pm}=r_{\pm}e^{i\theta_{\pm}}
. Hence, in
U_{+}\cap U_{-}
,

x_{+}x_{-}=1
and
\theta_{+}=-\theta_{-}
. We choose the ansatz,

\displaystyle\varphi_{\pm}

\displaystyle=f_{\pm}(r_{\pm})e^{ik_{\pm}\theta_{\pm}},

\displaystyle a_{\pm}

\displaystyle=a_{\pm}(r_{\pm})\,d\theta_{\pm},

which is justified by rotational symmetry. Compatibility of the fields then requires
n=k_{+}-k_{-}
. The Bogomol’nyi equations reduce to a system of ODEs,

\displaystyle f_{\pm}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r}(k_{\pm}\mp a_{\pm})\,f_{\pm},

\displaystyle a_{\pm}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=r\Omega(r)B_{\pm},

\displaystyle N_{\pm}^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=-\Omega(r)\left(\kappa B_{\pm}-\frac{4f_{\pm}^{2}N_{\pm}}{(1+f_{\pm}^{2})^{2}}\right)-\frac{1}{r}N^{\prime}_{\pm},

where,

\Omega(r)=\frac{4R^{2}}{(1+r^{2})^{2}},\hskip 18.49988ptB_{\pm}=-\left(\kappa N_{\pm}+\tau\pm 1\mp\frac{2}{1+f_{\pm}^{2}}\right).

We solved the Bogomol’nyi equations in the punctured disk

\mathbb{D}_{1}(0)\setminus\{0\}
adding the compatibility conditions,

\displaystyle f_{+}(1)f_{-}(1)

\displaystyle=1,

\displaystyle a_{+}(1)+a_{-}(1)

\displaystyle=n,

\displaystyle N_{+}(1)

\displaystyle=N_{-}(1),

\displaystyle N_{+}^{\prime}(1)

\displaystyle=-N_{-}^{\prime}(1),

together with the lowest order approximation to the fields at
r=0
,

\displaystyle f_{\pm}

\displaystyle=q_{\pm}r^{k_{\pm}}+\mathcal{O}\left(r^{k_{\pm}+1}\right),

\displaystyle B_{\pm}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}-\left(\kappa p_{\pm}+\tau\pm 1\mp\frac{2}{1+q_{\pm}^{2}}\right)+\mathcal{O}(r),&k_{\pm}=0,\\
-(\kappa p_{\pm}+\tau\pm 1\mp 2)+\mathcal{O}(r),&k_{\pm}\neq 0,\\
\end{cases}

\displaystyle a_{\pm}

\displaystyle=2B_{\pm}R^{2}r^{2}+\mathcal{O}(r^{3}),

\displaystyle N_{\pm}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}p_{\pm}+\left(-\kappa B_{\pm}+\frac{4q_{\pm}^{2}p_{\pm}}{(1+q_{\pm}^{2})^{2}}\right)\,R^{2}r^{2}+\mathcal{O}(r^{3}),&k_{\pm}=0,\\
p_{\pm}-\kappa B_{\pm}\,R^{2}r^{2}+\mathcal{O}(r^{3}),&k_{\pm}\neq 0.\end{cases}

To find the initial stable solution, we used a shooting method in
the interval
[\delta,1]
for a small value
\delta>0
. Given
initial conditions for the parameters,
Z=(q_{+},q_{-},p_{+},p_{-})
, we solved the Bogomol’nyi
equations on
\mathbb{D}_{1}(0)
and defined a map
M:\mathbb{R}^{4}\to\mathbb{R}^{4}
,

\displaystyle Z\mapsto(f_{+}(1)f_{-}(1)-1,a_{+}(1)+a_{-}(1)-n,N_{+}(1)-N_{-}(1),N^{\prime}_{+}(1)+N^{\prime}_{-}(1)),

whose zero determines suitable initial conditions. In order to obtain a family of Chern-Simons deformations, we
applied the pseudo-arclength continuation method described in

[
9
]
for deformations of the Abelian-Higgs model. Given initial data
(\kappa_{0},Z_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{5}
, we sought a nearby point
(\kappa,Z)
such that,

\dot{Z}_{0}\cdot(Z-Z_{0})+\dot{\kappa}_{0}\,(\kappa-\kappa_{0})=\delta s,

for a small positive constant
\delta s
. We restricted to

\kappa>0
and solved the Bogomol’nyi equations for
\kappa_{+}=2,\kappa_{-}=0
in a sphere of radius
R=2
and for
\kappa_{+}=1,\kappa_{-}=1
in a sphere of radius
R=1.5
.

Figure
1
shows field evolution in the
(2,0)
case. The fields converge according to Theorem
1
as expected. We remark that the theorem claims
dN_{i}=\kappa_{i}d\mathcal{N}_{i}\to 0
as
i\to\infty
; however, we cannot rule out the possibility that
d\mathcal{N}_{i}
diverges.
Since
e=d\mathcal{N}
by (
1.6
), data suggests
d\mathcal{N}_{i}
is bounded.
Figure
2
shows field evolution in the
(1,1)
case.
Data suggests that in this
case for any
\kappa>0
there is exactly one solution to the Bogomol’nyi equations. We reduced the radius of the sphere to keep pseudo arclength continuation convergent. In this case, Theorem
2
gives three possible limits for the fields. In the sphere, Green functions are of the form

G(x,y)=\frac{1}{4\pi}\log(1-x\cdot y)+c

for some constant
c\in\mathbb{R}
. A direct calculation shows that condition (
1.16
) holds for
c=0
.
Data supports that the convergence in this case is to the limit given by alternative 2 of the theorem.

Demographic, Clinicopathological Characteristics, and Prognosis of 81 Chinese Elderly Patients With Acral Melanoma
Authors: Kaidi Yang, Xin Cai, Jin Wu, Kui Jiang, Weizhen Zhang, Hongfeng Zhou, Fang Liu, Yan Wang, Jiwei Liu, Di Wu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTPurposeAcral melanoma (AM) is a rare melanoma subtype involving palms, soles, and nail beds. Clinical‐pathological features and survival of elderly AM patients remain insufficiently understood. This study aimed to investigate the characteristics and survival of elderly Chinese AM patients to support clinical management.MethodsConsecutive AM patients aged ≥ 75 years treated at five Chinese melanoma centers between January 2012 and March 2025 were enrolled.ResultsA total of 81 elderly AM patients were included, with median follow‐up of 76.0 months and median age of 78 years (55.6% male). Most tumors were located on the sole (80.2%). Stage distribution: I 11.1%, II 49.4%, III 19.8%, IV 8.9%. Median overall survival (OS) was 45.0 months, with 1‐, 3‐, 5‐year OS rates of 88.5%, 60.3%, and 36.5%. Univariate analysis showed that high mitotic rate, ulceration, and advanced stage correlated with shorter OS. Multivariate analysis found no independent predictors for OS. Among 72 surgically treated patients, median disease‐free survival (DFS) was 111.0 months, with 1‐, 3‐, 5‐year DFS rates of 76.9%, 63.2%, and 59.9%. Gender was the independent predictor for DFS.ConclusionMultiple clinical‐pathological factors affect the prognosis of elderly AM patients. Male sex is an independent predictor of poor DFS. However, due to the limited sample size for endpoint events, this conclusion has certain limitations. These results provide evidence for the clinical management of elderly AM patients.

Introduction

Acral melanoma (AM) is a rare subtype of melanoma that typically occurs on the palms, soles, and nail beds [1]. It is the most common form of melanoma among Asian populations, accounting for 47.5%–65% of all melanoma cases [2, 3]. AM diagnosed at later stages, combined with its unique biological characteristics, generally results in a poorer prognosis compared to cutaneous melanoma [4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10].

With the ongoing global demographic shift towards an aging population, the number of elderly individuals diagnosed with malignant tumors is steadily increasing [11]. Older cancer patients differ significantly from younger patients in terms of disease characteristics, physical condition, treatment tolerance, treatment response, psychological and physiological well‐being, and social support needs. These differences directly impact clinical decisions and prognostic outcomes. The elderly AM patient population represents a distinct and growing group, yet its demographic profile, clinical pathology, and survival outcomes are still insufficiently studied.

Therefore, this study aims to explore the demographic and clinical‐pathological characteristics of elderly patients with AM in China and to perform a survival analysis, providing scientific evidence to guide the prevention and treatment of AM in this population.

Materials and Methods

Study Design and Participants

This study included 81 patients aged 75 years or older with AM who received consecutive treatment at five melanoma treatment centers in China between January 2012 and March 2025. Clinical and pathological data were retrieved from medical records, including Breslow thickness, pathological stage at diagnosis, mitotic count, ulceration, gender, trauma history, Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status, primary tumor site, presence of vascular invasion, nevus‐associated melanoma, receipt of postoperative adjuvant therapy, overall survival (OS), and disease‐free survival (DFS). Patients with a second primary tumor were excluded. When information was incomplete or unavailable, patients or their families were contacted by telephone for clarification. All patients were staged according to the 8th Edition of the Joint Committee on Cancer Staging System for Melanoma.

Study Outcomes and Follow‐Up

The primary endpoints of this study were OS and DFS. OS was defined as the time from pathological diagnosis to all‐cause death, while DFS was defined as the time from initial surgical resection to recurrence or death. The follow‐up duration covered the period from the initiation of treatment to the last known contact or death from any cause.

Statistical Analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS version 27.0. Continuous variables were grouped for analysis. Survival curves were estimated using the Kaplan–Meier method, and statistical significance was assessed using the log‐rank test. Cox regression models were used to examine the associations between clinical and pathological variables with both OS and DFS. To avoid omitting potentially significant factors, variables with p‐values < 0.05 in univariate analysis were included in the multivariate analysis. A p‐value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Patient Characteristics

A total of 81 patients aged 75 years or older with pathologically confirmed AM were enrolled between January 2012 and March 2025. The median follow‐up duration for this cohort was 76.0 months.

The median age of the cohort was 78 years, with male patients slightly outnumbering female patients (55.6% vs. 44.4%). Approximately 80.2% of patients had primary tumors located on the sole of the foot, 12.4% had primary tumors on the palm of the hand, and 1.2% had primary tumors on the nail bed. The staging distribution was as follows: 9 patients (11.1%) were classified as Stage I, 40 patients (49.4%) as Stage II, 16 patients (19.8%) as Stage III, and 7 patients (8.9%) as Stage IV (Table 1). This study included 19 patients (23.4%) who received postoperative adjuvant therapy and approximately 62 patients (76.6%) who did not receive postoperative adjuvant therapy (see Table S3). There were 14 patients (17.3%) who had received systemic therapy and 67 patients (82.7%) who had not (see Table S4).

OS

Among the 81 elderly patients with AM, 55 (67.9%) died during the study period. The median OS was 45.0 months (95% confidence interval [CI], 38.2–51.8) (Figure 1). The 1‐year, 3‐year, and 5‐year OS rates were 88.5%, 60.3%, and 36.5%, respectively.

Kaplan‐Meier overall survival of patients.

The median OS for male patients was 46.0 months (95% CI, 32.8–59.1), while for female patients, it was 45.0 months (95% CI, 34.1–55.8) (p = 0.164). For patients with an ECOG score of ≤ 1, the median OS was 47.0 months (95% CI, 31.2–62.8), compared with 43.0 months (95% CI, 20.8–65.2) for those with an ECOG score ≥ 2 (p = 0.998).

Patients with a history of trauma at the primary lesion site had a median OS of 58.0 months (95% CI, 40.3–75.7), while those without a history of trauma had a median OS of 43.0 months (95% CI, 33.9–52.1) (p = 0.252). The median OS for patients with primary melanoma sites on the palms, soles, and subungual regions was 44.0 months (95% CI, 24.3–63.7), 47.0 months (95% CI, 33.1–60.9), and 45.0 months (95% CI, 12.1–77.9), respectively (p = 0.829). The median OS in patients with nevus‐associated malignancies was 61.0 months (95% CI, 13.6–108.4), while that in patients with non‐nevus‐associated malignancies was 44.0 months (95% CI, 38.0–49.9); the difference between the two groups was not statistically significant (p = 0.769).

The 5‐year OS rates for stages I–IV were 65.6%, 35.2%, 33.7%, and 0.0%, respectively (p = 0.037). The median OS for Stage I patients was not reached, while for Stage II, III, and IV patients, it was 43.0 months (95% CI, 34.8–51.2), 47.0 months (95% CI, 31.1–80.9), and 36.0 months (95% CI, 12.0–70.8), respectively. In the Breslow thickness ≤ 1 mm group, the median OS was 65.0 months (95% CI: 25.4–104.6); in the 1–2 mm group, the median OS was 63 months (95% CI: 15.5–110.5); the median OS in the 2–4 mm group was 43 months (95% CI: 16.8–69.2), and the median OS in the > 4 mm group was 41 months (95% CI: 33.0–49.0) (p = 0.096). Patients with a mitotic rate ≤ 1/mm2 had a median OS of 114.0 months (95% CI, NA–NA), while those with a mitotic rate > 1/mm2 had a median OS of 34.0 months (95% CI, 21.3–46.7) (p < 0.001). The median OS for patients in the non‐ulcer group was 62.0 months (95% CI, 40.3–83.7), while for the ulcer group, it was 36.0 months (95% CI, 23.4–48.6) (p = 0.037). The 5‐year OS rate was 35.3% for the group without lymphovascular invasion and 0.0% for the group with lymphovascular invasion (p = 0.423). The median OS among patients who received postoperative adjuvant therapy was 45.0 months (95% CI, 0.0–90.6), while it was 46.0 months (95% CI, 39.1–52.8) among patients who did not receive adjuvant therapy; the difference between the two groups was not statistically significant (p = 0.743). The median OS was 58.0 months (95% CI, 24.1–91.9) among patients who received systemic therapy, compared with 45.0 months (95% CI, 38.8–51.2) among those who did not receive systemic therapy; the difference between the two groups was not statistically significant (p = 0.566).

Univariate analysis revealed significantly shorter OS for patients with a mitotic rate > 1/mm2 (hazard ratio [HR] = 6.85, p = 0.002) and ulceration (HR = 2.03, p = 0.042). Compared with Stage I AM patients, those with Stage II (HR = 3.46, p = 0.091), Stage III (HR = 4.42, p = 0.051), and Stage IV (HR = 8.09, p = 0.011) disease exhibited significantly increased mortality risk (p = 0.039). Breslow thickness, gender, ECOG performance status, trauma history, primary tumor site, nevus‐associated status, lymphovascular invasion, receipt of postoperative adjuvant therapy, and receipt of systemic therapy were not significantly associated with OS in elderly patients with AM (Table S1). Multivariate Cox regression did not identify any independent predictors of OS.

DFS

Among the 72 elderly patients with AM who underwent radical surgery, 28 experienced DFS events during follow‐up. The median DFS was 111.0 months (95% CI, 21.9–200.1) (Figure 3). The 1‐year, 3‐year, and 5‐year DFS rates were 76.9%, 63.2%, and 59.9%, respectively.

Kaplan‐Meier disease‐free survival of patients.

The median DFS for male patients was 21.0 months (95% CI, 12.4–29.6), while for female patients, it was 139.0 months (95% CI, NA–NA) (p < 0.001). For patients with an ECOG score of ≤ 1, the median DFS was 139.0 months (95% CI, NA–NA), whereas for those with an ECOG score ≥ 2, it was 111.0 months (95% CI, NA–NA) (p = 0.938).

The 5‐year DFS rate for patients with a history of trauma at the primary lesion site was 59.5%, compared with 59.2% for those without a trauma history (p = 0.870). Median DFS was not reached in the trauma group, while in the non‐trauma group, the median DFS was 111.0 months (95% CI, 34.3–187.7). The 5‐year DFS rates for patients with primary lesions located on the palms, soles, and under the nails were 87.5%, 52.4%, and 0.0%, respectively (p = 0.094), which did not meet the criteria for statistical significance. The median DFS in patients with nevus‐associated and non‐nevus‐associated malignancies was 27.0 months and 111.0 months, respectively; the difference between the groups was not statistically significant (p = 0.994).

The 5‐year DFS rates for patients with Stage I, II, and III disease were 77.8%, 63.2%, and 15.6%, respectively; the differences in survival rates across the staging groups were statistically significant (p < 0.001). When compared with Stage I as the reference group, Stage III patients had significantly lower DFS than Stage I patients (p = 0.038), while there was no statistically significant difference between Stage II and Stage I (p = 0.739). The median DFS for Stage I patients has not yet been reached, the median DFS for Stage II and Stage III patients were 111.0 months (95% CI, 15.7–206.3) and 11.0 months (95% CI, 0.237–21.8), respectively. In the Breslow thickness ≤ 1 mm group, the 5‐year DFS rate was 83.3%; in the 1–2 mm group, it was 55.9%; in the 2–4 mm group, it was 91.7%; and in the > 4 mm group, it was 42.2% (p = 0.037). The 5‐year DFS rate was 62.5% in the group with mitotic rate ≤ 1/mm2 and 50.6% in the group with mitotic rate > 1/mm2 (p = 0.069), with the median DFS not reached in either group. The 5‐year DFS rate was 70.4% in the non‐ulcer group compared to 50.7% in the ulcer group (p = 0.305). The median DFS has not yet been reached in patients without lymphovascular invasion; while for patients with lymphovascular invasion, it was 10.0 months (p = 0.122). The median DFS was 27.0 months and 111.0 months for patients who did and did not receive adjuvant therapy, respectively; the difference between the groups was not statistically significant (p = 0.080).

Univariate analysis found that males (HR = 5.52, p = 0.002) were significantly associated with shorter DFS. Univariate Cox regression analysis indicated that there was an overall statistical difference across Breslow thickness strata (p = 0.040). Using the Breslow thickness ≤ 1 mm group as the reference, none of the pairwise comparisons between subgroups were statistically significant. The 1–2 mm group (HR = 1.99, 95% CI: 0.23–17.08, p = 0.530), the 2–4 mm group (HR = 0.51, 95% CI: 0.03–8.22, p = 0.637), and the > 4 mm group (HR = 4.17, 95% CI: 0.55–31.82, p = 0.169). In a pairwise Cox regression analysis using Stage I as the reference, the risk of recurrence in Stage III was significantly higher than that in Stage I (HR = 4.91, p = 0.038), while there was no statistically significant difference between Stage II and Stage I (HR = 1.29, p = 0.739). The overall test for the staging variable was statistically significant (p = 0.001), with only Stage III showing a prognostic difference compared to Stage I. ECOG performance status, history of trauma, presence of ulceration, mitotic rate, the primary site, nevus‐associated melanoma, lymphovascular invasion, and receipt of postoperative adjuvant therapy did not significantly affect DFS (Table S2). To adhere to the 10 EPV modeling principles, limit the number of covariates to reduce the risk of overfitting in the Cox regression model, and avoid subgroup bias caused by small sample sizes, the multivariate Cox regression did not include tumor staging variables. The results of the multivariate analysis showed a statistically significant association between male sex and shorter DFS (HR = 6.13, 95% CI 1.35–27.80, p = 0.019). However, due to the limited sample size for the DFS endpoint in this study, the confidence interval for this measure is wide, and there is some uncertainty in the estimated risk effect.

Prognostic Factors of OS and DFS

Univariate analysis revealed that pathological stage, mitotic rate, and ulceration were significant prognostic factors for OS (Table S1). However, multivariate analysis did not identify any significant independent predictors of OS in patients with AM.

Univariate analysis showed that gender, pathological stage, and Breslow thickness were significant prognostic factors for DFS (Table S2). In accordance with the 10 EPV principles, staging variables were excluded to reduce overfitting and sample bias. Multivariate analysis indicated that male sex is an independent adverse prognostic factor for DFS. Due to the limited number of endpoint events, the confidence intervals are wide, and there is some uncertainty in the estimated risk effects.

Discussion

To date, no publicly reported studies have specifically examined elderly patients with AM. The unique biological behavior, demographic characteristics, clinical‐pathological features, and prognosis of this population remain poorly understood. In this study, we conducted the first systematic analysis of the demographic and clinical‐pathological features of elderly Chinese AM patients and explored their correlation with survival outcomes.

In our cohort, 55.6% of patients were male and 44.4% were female, consistent with previous studies and showing no significant gender difference [10, 12, 13, 14, 15]. In contrast to other findings [12], multivariate analysis identified male as a risk factor associated with DFS [16, 17]. However, due to the limited size of the cohort and the relatively small number of recurrence events, the confidence interval for these results is wide, and the predictive stability is insufficient; this association requires further validation in a larger, multicenter cohort. The most common primary site of melanoma was the sole of the foot, consistent with prior studies [12, 13, 16, 18]. This study found no evidence that the primary site of the tumor affects patients' DFS or OS [12]. Previous studies have found that AM arising from the malignant transformation of pigmented nevi exhibits more aggressive biological behavior [19]. However, data from this study show that there were no statistically significant differences in median OS or DFS between patients with nevus‐associated AM and those in the non‐nevus‐associated group.

Univariate analysis revealed that pathological stage at diagnosis, mitotic rate, and ulceration were significantly associated with OS in elderly AM patients [12, 14, 16, 17, 20, 21, 22, 23, 24]. However, in multivariate Cox regression analysis, none of these clinical‐pathological features demonstrated independent prognostic predictive value. This finding contrasts with conclusions from several previous studies involving the general population [12, 14, 20, 23, 25]. The discrepancies may be attributed to several factors: First, the inclusion of elderly patients in this study may have attenuated the impact of tumor‐specific pathological features on survival outcomes, owing to their higher risk of non‐tumor‐related mortality [26, 27]. Second, the inherently aggressive biological behavior of AM may expose patients at different disease stages to similar progression risks [4, 5], resulting in convergent prognoses. Additionally, due to the poor physical condition and low treatment tolerance of elderly patients [28], those with varying pathological features may have received similarly conservative treatments, thus reducing inherent prognostic disparities. Future studies with larger sample sizes specifically targeting elderly AM patients are needed to validate these findings and further explore the underlying biological mechanisms.

Previous studies from Western countries, such as the United Kingdom, have reported a 5‐year disease‐specific survival (DSS) rate for AM patients ranging from 70% to 80.3% [14, 29]. A Chinese study indicated a 5‐year DSS rate of 53.3% for AM patients in China [20]. Other Asian countries, including South Korea and Japan, have reported 5‐year survival rates for AM ranging from 35.0% to 49.3% [15, 18]. The 5‐year OS rate for elderly Chinese patients with acral melanoma included in this study was 36.5%, which to some extent reflects the poor prognosis for this population in China. The overall rate of adjuvant therapy acceptance in this study population was 23.4%. A comparison of DFS and OS between patients who did and did not receive adjuvant therapy revealed no statistically significant difference in survival outcomes between the two groups. The poor prognosis for this group is primarily attributed to: the patients generally had poor performance status (with a median ECOG score of 1), multiple comorbidities, insufficient disease awareness, and high clinical misdiagnosis rates, all contributing to delays in diagnosis and treatment. Furthermore, the tumors themselves exhibited high‐risk characteristics, with a significant proportion presenting with Breslow thickness > 4 mm (32.1%), ulceration (56.8%), and a mitotic rate > 1/mm2 (49.4%). These factors suggest that elderly AM patients are often diagnosed at more advanced stages, further contributing to poor survival outcomes.

This study has certain limitations: As a multicenter retrospective study, the cases were drawn from five medical centers in China, resulting in geographical limitations that make it difficult to fully represent the overall population of individuals aged 75 and older with acral melanoma in China. Although we verified medical records and survival data through multiple follow‐up methods to ensure the reliability of the data as much as possible, the inherent biases of retrospective studies cannot be completely eliminated. The insufficient sample sizes in the subgroups for Stage IV cases, primary lesions under the nail, and sentinel lymph node biopsies reduced the statistical power of the stratified and regression analyses; additionally, with only 28 cases for the DFS endpoint, covariates were simplified to avoid overfitting in the Cox regression, resulting in conclusions with limited persuasiveness in this section. Furthermore, the study used only OS and DFS as endpoints; cause‐of‐death information was missing for some cases, making it impossible to conduct tumor‐specific survival and competing risks analyses. In the future, the China Melanoma Collaborative Network can be utilized to expand the sample size, conduct prospective cohort studies, refine data on causes of death and treatment, and provide external validation of the findings of this study.

Conclusion

This study found that the demographic distribution, clinical‐pathological characteristics, and prognosis of elderly AM patients in China were significantly different from those in other populations. Univariate analysis identified pathological stage, mitotic count, and ulceration as significant prognostic factors for OS in elderly AM patients. However, multivariate analysis did not identify any independent prognostic factors for OS in these patients. Univariate analysis revealed clear statistical associations between gender, pathological stage, and Breslow thickness and patients' DFS prognosis. Multivariate regression confirmed that male sex is an independent risk factor for shorter DFS. Due to limitations in the sample size of endpoint events, the confidence interval for this result is wide, and there are limitations in the assessment of the effect.

Noise-Induced Quantum Mpemba Effect
Mingrui Zhao, Zhonghuai Hou

Abstract
The quantum Mpemba effect (QMPE), an intriguing anomalous relaxation phenomenon, has recently attracted significant attention. However, how various types of noise, which are ubiquitous in real systems, may affect the QMPE remains unknown. Here, we address this gap by constructing a general dynamical framework for d d level open quantum systems under random telegraph noise. By investigating the dynamics of an extended system and then projecting back, we find that noise can induce additional modes and strongly influence the relaxation dynamics of the original system. Specially, in the limit of long correlation time of noise, these modes cause anomalous slowdown for certain initial states, thereby inducing or eliminating QMPE, illustrated by a three-level example system. Interestingly, this mechanism leads to a counter intuitive effect—the decoherence rate may be slowed down by noise.

Conclusion
—We have demonstrated that external telegraph noise, as a non-Gaussian and non-Markovian perturbation, can induce or eliminate the quantum Mpemba effect (QMPE) in open quantum systems. Unlike the Markovian QMPE driven by initial state engineering
PhysRevLett.127.060401
;
PhysRevA.106.012207
;
PhysRevLett.133.140404
;
PhysRevE.108.014130
, This noise-induced effect originates from the noise-induced additional slow mode, which leads to the relaxation slowdown of specific initial states. Crucially, the noise introduces counter intuitive deceleration of decoherence (FIG.
2
), challenging the conventional wisdom that noise invariably accelerates relaxation.

Secend, telegraph noise might enhance parameter estimation in critical systems where QMPE amplifies sensitivity

\lambda_{k}-\nu
is
L_{k,-\nu}=(0_{n\times 1},l_{k}),R_{k.-\nu}=(0_{n\times 1},r_{k})^{T}
. Therefore, when
\Delta=0
(no noise case), it’s easy to find

\bm{p}(t)=\Pi{G}(t)=\bm{p}_{ss}+\sum_{k=2}^{n}e^{\lambda_{k}t}[l_{k}|\bm{p(0)}]r_{k}

(S8)

which is exactly the same form as the general spectral decomposition form in
[
15
]
. As the noise is applied, using equations in
[
36
]
, we find the first-order perturbation for
L_{k},R_{k}.L_{k,-\nu},R_{k,-\nu}
is

\displaystyle L_{k}^{(1)}=\sum_{n\neq{k}}\frac{[l_{k}|\Delta|r_{n}]}{\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{n}+\nu}(0_{n\times 1},l_{n}),

\displaystyle R_{k}^{(1)}=\sum_{n\neq{k}}\frac{[l_{n}|\Delta|r_{k}]}{\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{n}+\nu}(0_{n\times 1},r_{n})^{T}

\displaystyle L_{k,-\nu}^{(1)}=\sum_{n\neq{k}}\frac{[l_{k}|\Delta|r_{n}]}{\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{n}-\nu}(l_{n},0_{n\times 1}),

\displaystyle R_{k,-\nu}^{(1)}=\sum_{n\neq{k}}\frac{[l_{n}|\Delta|r_{k}]}{\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{n}-\nu}(r_{n},0_{n\times 1})^{T}

(S9)

These results are very interesting: for
\Delta=0
case,
\langle L_{k,-\nu}|G(0)\rangle=(0_{n\times 1},l_{k})(r_{k},0_{n\times 1})^{T}=0
, and
\Pi R_{k,-\nu}=(\mathcal{I}_{n\times n},0_{n\times n})(0_{n\times 1},r_{k})=0
. This fact shows that the extra mode
\lambda_{k}-\nu
cannot influence system dynamics, which is in agreement with our intuition. However, as we apply the external noise,
\langle L_{k,-\nu}^{(1)}|G(0)\rangle\neq 0
and
\Pi R_{k,-\nu}^{(1)}\neq 0
, indicating that the extra modes induced by the noise begin to affect the system dynamics.

For the sake of rigor, we consider the normalization factor after the noise is applied. For mode
\lambda_{k}
, the normalization factor
b_{k}
fulfill

\frac{1}{b_{k}}=\langle(L_{k}^{(0)}+L_{k}^{(1)}|(R_{k}^{(0)}+R_{k}^{(1)})\rangle=1+\langle L_{k}^{(1)}|R_{k}^{(1)}\rangle

(S10)

Note that both
L_{k}^{(1)},R_{k}^{(1)}
are of the order
\Delta_{1}
, therefore
b_{k}\approx 1-\Delta_{1}^{2}
. So in the frame of first order perturbation, we can neglect
b_{k}
.

Putting Eq.(S9) into Eq.(3) in the main text, we finally get Eq.(4). In principle there should be
b_{k}
in front of the second term of Eq.(4), but we ignore it for the reason above.

.4
RTN in short correlation time limit

In short correlation time limit(
\nu\rightarrow\infty
), RTN has similar effect as Gaussian white noise, and induce extra dissipative term in Lindblad equation. Using the expression in (S2), we consider the form of
\mathcal{L}_{1}
in this limit. Using second-order time-dependent perturbation theory, we have

\frac{d}{dt}=\mathcal{L}_{1}\rho=\Delta_{1}\eta\mathcal{A}(\rho)

(S19)

such that

\displaystyle\rho(t)=

\displaystyle\rho(0)+\Delta_{1}\int_{0}^{t}\eta(s)\mathcal{A}(\rho(s))ds=

\displaystyle\rho(0)+\Delta_{1}\int_{0}^{t}\eta(s)[\rho(0)+\Delta_{1}\int_{0}^{s}\eta(\tau)\mathcal{A}\circ\mathcal{A}(\rho(\tau))d\tau]

(S20)

Using the property of RTN(
\langle\eta(t)\rangle=0,\langle\eta(t)\eta(s)\rangle=exp(-\nu|t-s|)
) and consider above equation under the average over RTN, we get

\langle\rho(t)\rangle=\langle\rho(0)\rangle+\Delta_{1}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{s}{exp}(-\nu(s-\tau))\mathcal{A}\circ\mathcal{A}(\rho(\tau))d\tau{d}s

(S21)

In the limit of
\nu\rightarrow+\infty
, using the Markovian approximation

\int_{0}^{s}exp(-\nu(s-\tau))f(\tau)d\tau=\frac{1}{\nu}f(s)

(S22)

we get

\langle\rho(t)\rangle=\langle\rho(0)\rangle+\frac{\Delta_{1}^{2}}{\nu}\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{A}\circ\mathcal{A}\langle\rho(s)\rangle{ds}

(S23)

Thus, we can get

\frac{d}{dt}\langle\rho(t)\rangle=\frac{\Delta_{1}^{2}}{\nu}\mathcal{A}\circ\mathcal{A}\langle\rho(t)\rangle

(S24)

In the main text,
\mathcal{L}_{1}\rho=-i[H_{1},\rho]=-i\Delta_{1}\eta[J,\rho]
. Therefore, in this limit the RTN reduce to an extra dissipative term, and Eq.(1) becomes

\frac{d}{dt}\rho=-i[H_{0},\rho]+\Sigma_{k}\gamma_{k}(L_{k}\rho{L_{K}^{{\dagger}}}-\frac{1}{2}\{L_{k}^{{\dagger}}L_{k},\rho\})-[J,[J,\rho]]

(S25)

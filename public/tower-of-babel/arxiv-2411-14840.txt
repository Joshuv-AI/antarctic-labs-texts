Local Well-posedness of the Free-boundary Problem in Incompressible Elastodynamics with Surface Tension
Longhui Xu

Abstract
We prove the local well-posedness of the 3D free-boundary incompressible elastodynamics with surface tension describing the motion of an elastic medium in a periodic domain with a moving graphical surface. The deformation tensor is assumed to satisfy the neo-Hookean linear elasticity. We adapt the idea in [ 34 ] to generate an approximate problem with artificial viscosity indexed by κ > 0 \kappa>0 to boost the boundary regularity, which recovers the original system as κ → 0 , \kappa\to 0, and the energy estimates yield no regularity loss.

1.1
Reformulation in flattened domains

One way to convert (

Also, we do not have to worry about the propagation of the initial constraints of the deformation tensor
\mathbf{F}
when constructing the approximate system, since the equations reformulated in Lagrangian coordinates automatically only depend on the initial data of
\mathbf{F}

We define a family of diffeomorphisms
\displaystyle\Phi(t,\cdot):\Omega\to\mathcal{D}_{t}

\displaystyle\Phi(t,\overline{x},x_{3})=(\overline{x},\varphi(t,\overline{x},x_{3})),

(1.4)

where

is an extension of the boundary graph

to the interior by

\displaystyle\varphi(t,\overline{x},x_{3})=x_{3}+\chi(x_{3})\psi(t,\overline{x})

(1.6)

. To avoid the case that the moving surface touches the bottom, we can assume

Now we introduce the converted variables

the induced differential operators

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{\varphi}

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}-\frac{\partial_{t}\varphi}{\partial_{3}\varphi}\partial_{3},

(1.9)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\tau}^{\varphi}=\partial_{\tau}^{\varphi}

\displaystyle=\partial_{\tau}-\frac{\partial_{\tau}\varphi}{\partial_{3}\varphi}\partial_{3},\qquad\tau=1,2,

(1.10)

\displaystyle\nabla_{3}^{\varphi}=\partial_{3}^{\varphi}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\partial_{3}\varphi}\partial_{3}

(1.11)

\displaystyle:=\partial_{t}^{\varphi}+v\cdot\nabla^{\varphi}

History and background

The system of incompressible elastodynamics is a coupling system of the Euler equations with a transport equation for the deformation tensor. Let us first briefly review the results of the free-boundary incompressible Euler equations. The first breakthrough was by Wu

31

[

introduced tangential smoothing to prove the LWP for the case with surface tension and avoided the loss of regularity. We also refer to
[

,

]
for the case without surface tension and
[

,

,
28
,

]
for the case with nonzero surface tension.

The study of elastodynamics has a long history. The global existence of the fixed-boundary problem is expected and we refer to

,

,

. However, the free-boundary problem is more difficult as we have limited boundary regularity and the boundary terms can enter the highest order in the energy estimate. Most of the existing literature neglected the effect of surface tension and the fluid tends to be unstable. Gu-Wang
[
13
]
and Li-Wang-Zhang
[
18

proved the LWP under a mixed stability condition. Hu-Huang

It is painful to study the case with nonzero surface tension with Lagrangian coordinates due to its complicated boundary description. In the presenting manuscript, we prove the local well-posedness of the free-boundary incompressible elastodynamics with surface tension under the boundary condition

1.3
Main result

The theorem concerns the local well-posedness of the free boundary incompressible elastodynamics modelled by (
1.14
). The initial data
(\psi_{0},q_{0},v_{0},F_{k}^{0})
satisfies the

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{j}q\big|_{t=0}=\sigma\partial_{t}^{j}\mathcal{H}\big|_{t=0}\quad

\displaystyle\text{on }\Sigma,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{j+1}\psi\big|_{t=0}=\partial_{t}^{j}(v\cdot N)\big|_{t=0}\qquad

\displaystyle\text{on }\Sigma,

(1.15)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{j}v_{3}\big|_{t=0}=0\quad

\displaystyle\text{on }\Sigma_{b}.

Theorem 1.1
.

Fix
\sigma>0
. Let
(q_{0},v_{0},F_{k}^{0})\in H^{4}(\Omega)
and
\psi_{0}\in H^{5.5}(\Sigma)
be initial data of the system (
1.14
) that verifies the compatibility condition up to
3
-th order,
E(0)\leq M
for some constant

, and the initial constraints

\displaystyle\nabla^{\varphi_{0}}\cdot F^{0}_{j}=0

\displaystyle\text{ in }\Omega,

(1.16)

\displaystyle F^{0}_{j}\cdot N_{0}=0

\displaystyle\text{ on }\Sigma\cup\Sigma_{b}.

(1.17)

Then there exists

depending only on the initial data and

such that (

) admits a unique solution

\displaystyle\sup_{[0\leq t\leq T]}E(t)\leq C(\sigma^{-1})P(E(0)),

where

is a generic polynomial in its arguments and the energy is defined by

\displaystyle E(t)=\sum_{l=0}^{4}\bigg(\sum_{k=0}^{3}||\partial_{t}^{l}(v,F_{k})||_{4-l}+|\sqrt{\sigma}\partial_{t}^{l}\psi|_{5-l}\bigg).

2
Uniform Estimates of the nonlinear approximate system

The local well-posedness of the free-boundary fluid is not a direct consequence of the priori estimate
[
24
,
33
]

One way is to use tangential smoothing to boost the regularity of

\displaystyle q=-\sigma\overline{\nabla}\cdot\big(\frac{\overline{\nabla\psi}}{\sqrt{1+|\overline{\nabla}\psi|^{2}}})+\kappa(1-\overline{\Delta})^{2}\psi+\kappa(1-\overline{\Delta})\partial_{t}\psi.

(2.1)

This approximate scheme does not affect the propagation of the initial boundary constraint

, which only depends on the equation

Let

Let

We write the

where

(2.32)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{1}

\displaystyle=D^{\alpha}\psi\partial_{3}v\cdot N-[D^{\alpha},\overline{v}\cdot,\overline{\partial}\psi].

(2.33)

We refer to

3.2

For

\displaystyle U_{1}^{m}

m

The top order terms

(3.36)

We can apply parallel arguments to treat the case with

(3.37)

3.4
Picard iteration

So far, we have established the local well-posedness and the uniform-in-
n
estimate for the linearised system (
3.1
) for each fixed
\kappa>0
. It suffices to prove
\{(q^{[n]},v^{[n]},F_{k}^{[n]},\psi^{[n]})\}
has a strongly convergent subsequence, then the limit becomes the solution to the nonlinear approximate system (
2.2
).

For a function sequence
\{(f^{[n]})\}
, we define
[f]^{[n]}:=f^{[n+1]}-f^{[n]}
, then we can write the linear system of
\{(q^{[n]},v^{[n]},F_{k}^{[n]},\psi^{[n]})\}
as follows

\begin{cases}D_{t}^{\varphi^{[n]}}[v]^{[n]}-(\mathfrak{F}_{k}^{[n]}\cdot\nabla^{\varphi^{[n]}})[F_{k}]^{[n]}+\nabla^{\varphi^{[n]}}[q]^{[n+1]}=-\mathring{f}_{v}^{[n]}\qquad&\text{in }\Omega,\\
\nabla^{\varphi^{[n]}}\cdot[v]^{[n]}=-\mathring{f}^{[n]}_{q}&\text{in }\Omega,\\

\partial_{t}[\psi]^{[n]}=[v]^{[n]}\cdot N^{[n]}+v^{[n]}\cdot[N]^{[n-1]}&\text{on }\Sigma,\\

v_{3}^{[n+1]}=v_{3}^{[n]}=0&\text{on }\Sigma_{b},\\
([v]^{[n]},[F_{k}]^{[n]},[q]^{[n]},[\psi]^{[n]})|_{t=0}=(0,0,0,0).\end{cases}

(3.52)

where the source terms are defined by

\displaystyle\mathring{f}_{v}^{[n]}

\displaystyle=[\overline{v}]^{[n-1]}\cdot\overline{\nabla}v^{[n]}+[V_{\mathbf{N}}]^{[n-1]}\partial_{3}v^{[n]}-([\mathfrak{F}_{k}]^{[n-1]}\cdot\overline{\nabla})F_{k}^{[n]}-\mathfrak{F}_{k,\mathbf{N}}^{[n-1]}\partial_{3}F_{k}^{[n]}+[\frac{\mathbf{N}}{\partial_{3}\varphi}]^{[n-1]}\partial_{3}q^{[n]},

(3.53)

\displaystyle\mathring{f}_{q}^{[n]}

\displaystyle=[\frac{\mathbf{N}}{\partial_{3}\varphi}]^{[n-1]}\cdot\partial_{3}v^{[n]},

(3.54)

\displaystyle\mathring{f}_{F}^{[n]}

\displaystyle=[\overline{v}]^{[n-1]}\cdot\overline{\nabla}F_{k}^{[n]}+[V_{\mathbf{N}}]^{[n-1]}\partial_{3}F_{k}^{[n]}-([\mathfrak{F}_{k}]^{[n-1]}\cdot\overline{\nabla})v^{[n]}-\mathfrak{F}_{k,\mathbf{N}}^{[n-1]}\partial_{3}v^{[n]},

(3.55)

with

\displaystyle V_{\mathbf{N}}^{[n]}=\frac{1}{\partial_{3}\varphi^{[n]}}(v^{[n]}\cdot\mathbf{N}^{[n-1]}-\partial_{t}\varphi^{[n]}),\quad\mathfrak{F}_{k,\mathbf{N}}^{[n]}:=\frac{1}{\partial_{3}\varphi^{[n]}}(F_{k}^{[n]}\cdot\mathbf{N}^{[n]}).

(3.56)

For
1\leq n
, we define the energy of the linear system (
3.52
) by

\displaystyle[E]_{3}^{[n]}(t):=\sum_{l=0}^{3}||\partial_{t}^{l}([v]^{[n]},[F_{k}]^{[n]})||^{2}_{3-l}+\sum_{l=0}^{3}|\sqrt{\kappa}\partial_{t}^{l}[\psi]^{[n]}|^{2}_{5-l}+\int_{0}^{t}|\sqrt{\kappa}\partial_{t}^{4}[\psi]^{[n]}|^{2}_{1}.

(3.57)

We shall prove the following estimate,

Proposition 3.2
.

There exists
T_{\kappa}^{\prime}>0
such that for
t\in[0,T_{\kappa}^{\prime}]
,

\displaystyle[E]_{3}^{[n]}(t)\leq\frac{1}{4}\big([E]_{3}^{[n-1]}(t)+[E]_{3}^{[n-2]}(t)\big).

(3.58)

Note that
[E]_{3}^{[n-1]}(t)
is produced by
[v]^{[n-1]},[F_{k}]^{[n-1]},[\psi]^{[n-1]}
and
[E]_{3}^{[n-2]}
is produced by
\mathbf{N}^{[n-2]}
in the source terms. The analysis is parallel to the estimate of
\mathring{E}_{4}(t)
in section
3.3
and we will only write the sketch of the proof.

3.4.1
Div-curl estimates

By lemma
2.3
, for
l=0,1,2,
we have

\displaystyle||\partial_{t}^{l}[v]^{[n]}||_{3-l}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim C(\mathring{K}_{0})\big(||\nabla^{\varphi^{[n]}}\cdot\partial_{t}^{l}[v]^{[n]}||_{2-l}+||\nabla^{\varphi^{[n]}}\times\partial_{t}^{l}[v]^{[n]}||_{2-l}^{2}+||\overline{\partial}^{3-l}\partial_{t}^{l}[v]^{[n]}||_{0}^{2}+||\partial_{t}^{l}[v]||_{0}^{2}\big),

(3.59)

\displaystyle||\partial_{t}^{l}[F_{j}]^{[n]}||_{3-l}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim C(\mathring{K}_{0})\big(||\nabla^{\varphi^{[n]}}\cdot\partial_{t}^{l}[F_{j}]^{[n]}||_{2-l}^{2}+||\nabla^{\varphi^{[n]}}\times\partial_{t}^{l}[F_{j}]^{[n]}||_{2-l}^{2}+||\overline{\partial}^{3-l}\partial_{t}^{l}[F_{j}]^{[n]}||_{0}^{2}+||\partial_{t}^{l}[F_{j}]^{[n]}||_{0}^{2}\big).

(3.60)

The
L^{2}
estimate is straightforward and parallel to the arguments for the linear system (
3.12
), we do not repeat it. The divergence part of
[v]^{[n]}
can be directly controlled by
C(\mathring{K}_{0})\int_{0}^{t}([E]_{3}^{[n]}+[E]_{3}^{[n-1]})
invoking the second equation of (
3.52
). For the divergence part of
[F_{j}]^{[n]}
, we take
\nabla^{\varphi^{[n]}}\cdot
to the evolution equation of
[F_{j}]^{[n]}
to get

\displaystyle D_{t}^{\varphi^{[n]}}(\nabla^{\varphi^{[n]}}\cdot[F_{j}]^{[n]})=(\partial^{\varphi^{[n]}}_{i}{\mathfrak{F}_{j}}^{[n]}\cdot\nabla^{\varphi^{[n]}})[v]_{i}^{[n]}-(\mathfrak{F}_{j}^{[n]}\cdot\nabla^{\varphi^{[n]}})\mathring{f}_{q}^{[n]}+[D_{t}^{\varphi^{[n]}},\nabla^{\varphi^{[n]}}\cdot][F_{j}]^{[n]},

we observe that there are at most 1 derivative of
[v]^{[n]},[F_{j}]^{[n]},\mathring{f}_{q}^{[n]}
and we can control
\mathring{f}_{q}^{[n]}
up to 3 derivatives. The standard
H^{2}
estimates and linearised transport theorem give us

\displaystyle||\nabla^{\varphi^{[n]}}\cdot\partial_{t}^{l}[F_{j}]^{[n]}||^{2}_{2-l}

\displaystyle\leq C(\mathring{K}_{0})(\nabla^{\varphi^{[n]}}\cdot\partial_{t}^{l}[F_{j}]^{[n]}(0)+\int_{0}^{t}[E]_{3}^{[n]}+[E]_{3}^{[n-1]}+[E]_{3}^{[n-2]})

(3.61)

\displaystyle=C(\mathring{K}_{0})\int_{0}^{t}([E]_{3}^{[n]}+[E]_{3}^{[n-1]}+[E]_{3}^{[n-2]}).

(3.62)

For the vorticity parts, taking
\nabla^{\varphi^{[n]}}\times
to the evolution equations gives us

\displaystyle D_{t}^{\varphi^{[n]}}(\nabla^{\varphi^{[n]}}\times[v]^{[n]})

\displaystyle=(\mathfrak{F}_{k}^{[n]}\cdot\nabla^{\varphi^{[n]}})(\nabla^{\varphi^{[n]}}\times[F_{k}]^{[n]})+(\nabla^{\varphi^{[n]}}\mathfrak{F}_{sk})\times(\partial_{s}^{\varphi^{[n]}}[F_{k}]^{[n]})+[D_{t}^{\varphi^{[n]}},\nabla^{\varphi^{[n]}}\times][v]^{[n]}-(\nabla^{\varphi^{[n]}}\times\mathring{f}_{v}^{[n]}),

(3.63)

\displaystyle D_{t}^{\varphi^{[n]}}(\nabla^{\varphi^{[n]}}\times[F_{j}]^{[n]})

\displaystyle=(\mathfrak{F}_{j}^{[n]}\cdot\nabla^{\varphi^{[n]}})(\nabla^{\varphi^{[n]}}\times[v]^{[n]})+(\nabla^{\varphi^{[n]}}\mathfrak{F}_{sj})\times(\partial_{s}^{\varphi^{[n]}}[v]^{[n]})+[D_{t}^{\varphi^{[n]}},\nabla^{\varphi^{[n]}}\times][F_{j}]^{[n]}-(\nabla^{\varphi^{[n]}}\times\mathring{f}_{F}^{[n]}).

(3.64)

The top order terms
(\mathfrak{F}_{k}^{[n]}\cdot\nabla^{\varphi^{[n]}})(\nabla^{\varphi^{[n]}}\times[F_{k}]^{[n]})
and
(\mathfrak{F}_{j}^{[n]}\cdot\nabla^{\varphi^{[n]}})(\nabla^{\varphi^{[n]}}\times[v]^{[n]})
will be cancelled out in the energy estimate as we pointed out in section
3.3
. Hence the standard
H^{2}
estimates give us

\displaystyle||\nabla^{\varphi^{[n]}}\times\partial_{t}^{l}[v]^{[n]}||^{2}_{2-l}+||\nabla^{\varphi^{[n]}}\times\partial_{t}^{l}[F_{k}]^{[n]}||^{2}_{2-l}\leq C(\mathring{K}_{0})\int_{0}^{t}([E]_{3}^{[n]}+[E]_{3}^{[n-1]}+[E]_{3}^{[n-2]}).

(3.65)

3.4.2
Tangential estimates

Let
D^{\alpha}=\partial_{t}^{\alpha_{0}}\partial_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\partial_{2}^{\alpha_{2}}
with
|\alpha|\leq 3
. We define the AGUs of
[v],[F_{j}],[q]
by

\displaystyle[\mathbf{V}]^{[n]}:

\displaystyle=D^{\alpha}[v]^{[n]}-D^{\alpha}\varphi^{[n]}\partial_{3}\varphi^{[n]}[v]^{[n]},\quad[\mathbf{F}_{j}]^{[n]}:=D^{\alpha}[F_{j}]^{[n]}-D^{\alpha}\varphi^{[n]}\partial_{3}\varphi^{[n]}[F_{j}]^{[n]},

\displaystyle[\mathbf{Q}]^{[n]}

\displaystyle:=D^{\alpha}[q]^{[n]}-D^{\alpha}\varphi^{[n]}\partial_{3}\varphi^{[n]}[q]^{[n]},

the
D^{\alpha}
-differentiated (
3.52
) can be written in terms of the AGUs as follows

\displaystyle\begin{cases}D_{t}^{\varphi^{[n]}}[\mathbf{V}]^{[n]}_{i}+\partial_{i}^{\varphi^{[n]}}[{\mathbf{Q}}]^{[n]}=({\mathfrak{F}}^{[n]}_{k}\cdot\nabla^{\varphi^{[n]}})[\mathbf{F}]^{[n]}_{ik}+\mathcal{R}_{v,i}^{[n]}\qquad&\text{in }\Omega,\\
\partial_{k}^{\varphi^{[n]}}[\mathbf{V}]^{[n]}_{k}=-\mathring{\mathcal{C}}_{k}([v]_{k})&\text{in }\Omega,\\
D_{t}^{\varphi^{[n]}}[\mathbf{F}]^{[n]}_{ij}=({\mathfrak{F}}^{[n]}_{k}\cdot\nabla^{\varphi^{[n]}})[\mathbf{V}]^{[n]}_{ij}+\mathcal{R}_{F,ij}^{[n]}&\text{in }\Omega,\\
[\mathbf{Q}]^{[n]}=\sigma D^{\alpha}(\mathcal{H}(\psi^{[n]}-\mathcal{H}(\psi^{[n-1]}))+\kappa D^{\alpha}(1-\overline{\Delta})^{2}[\psi]^{[n]}+\kappa D^{\alpha}(1-\overline{\Delta})\partial_{t}[\psi]^{[n]}-D^{\alpha}\psi^{[n]}\partial_{3}[q]^{[n]}&\text{on }\Sigma,\\
\partial_{t}D^{\alpha}[\psi]^{[n]}=[\mathbf{V}]^{[n]}\cdot N^{[n]}-(\overline{[v]}\cdot\overline{\nabla})D^{\alpha}\psi^{[n]}+{\mathcal{S}}^{[n]}_{b}&\text{on }\Sigma.\end{cases}

(3.66)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{[n]}_{v,i}

\displaystyle=-D^{\alpha}\mathring{f}_{v}^{[n]}+\mathfrak{F}_{lk}^{[n]}\mathring{\mathcal{C}}_{l}([F]^{[n]}_{ik})+[D^{\alpha},\mathfrak{F}^{[n]}_{lk}]\partial_{l}^{\varphi^{[n]}}[F]^{[n]}_{ik}-\mathring{\mathcal{D}}([v]_{i})-\mathring{\mathcal{C}}_{i}([q]),

(3.67)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{F,ij}^{[n]}

\displaystyle=-D^{\alpha}\mathring{f}_{F}^{[n]}+\mathfrak{F}^{[n]}_{kj}\mathring{\mathcal{C}}_{k}([v]_{i})+[D^{\alpha},\mathfrak{F}^{[n]}_{kj}]\partial_{k}^{\varphi^{[n]}}[v]_{i}-\mathring{\mathcal{D}}([F]_{ij}),

(3.68)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{b}^{[n]}

\displaystyle=D^{\alpha}\psi^{[n]}\partial_{3}[v]\cdot N^{[n]}-[D^{\alpha},\overline{[v]}^{[n]}\cdot,\overline{\partial}\psi^{[n]}]+D^{\alpha}(v^{[n]}\cdot[N]^{[n-1]}).

(3.69)

Note that the additional remainder terms can be directly controlled by
C(\mathring{K}_{0})\int_{0}^{t}([E]_{3}^{[n-1]}+[E]_{3}^{[n-2]})
, hence, we can apply analysis similar to the ones in (
3.3.2
) to obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}\bigg(|[\mathbf{V}]_{i}^{[n]}|^{2}+|[\mathbf{F}_{k}]^{[n]}|^{2}\partial_{3}\varphi^{[n]}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq C(\mathring{K}_{0})\int_{0}^{t}\bigg([E]_{3}^{[n]}+[E]_{3}^{[n-1]}+[E]_{3}^{[n-2]}\bigg)-\int_{\Sigma}[\mathbf{Q}]^{[n]}([\mathbf{V}]^{[n]}\cdot N^{[n]})+\int_{\Omega}D^{\alpha}[q]^{[n]}\mathring{\mathcal{C}}_{i}([v]_{i})\partial_{3}\varphi^{[n]}.

We expand the boundary integral

\displaystyle-\int_{\Sigma}[\mathbf{Q}]^{[n]}([\mathbf{V}]^{[n]}\cdot N^{[n]})=-\int_{\Sigma}[\mathbf{Q}]^{[n]}\partial_{t}D^{\alpha}[\psi]^{[n]}-\int_{\Sigma}[\mathbf{Q}]^{[n]}(\overline{[v]}^{[n]}\cdot\overline{\nabla})D^{\alpha}\psi^{[n]}+\int_{\Sigma}[\mathbf{Q}]^{[n]}{\mathcal{S}}^{[n]}_{b},

where the first boundary integral still contributes to the boundary regularity

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Sigma}[\mathbf{Q}]^{[n]}\partial_{t}D^{\alpha}[\psi]^{[n]}+\int_{\Sigma}|\sqrt{\kappa}(1-\overline{\Delta})D^{\alpha}[\psi]^{[n]}|^{2}\bigg|_{0}^{t}+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Sigma}|\sqrt{\kappa}\langle\overline{\partial}\rangle\partial_{t}D^{\alpha}[\psi]^{[n]}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(\mathring{K}_{0})\int_{0}^{t}\bigg([E]_{3}^{[n]}+[E]_{3}^{[n-1]}\bigg).

The second term can be directly bounded by the
\kappa-
weighted regularity invoking the boundary condition for
[\mathbf{Q}]^{[n]}
and integrating
(1-\overline{\nabla}),\langle\overline{\partial}\rangle
by parts. For
\int_{\Sigma}[\mathbf{Q}]^{[n]}{\mathcal{S}}^{[n]}_{b}+\int_{\Omega}D^{\alpha}[q]^{[n]}\mathring{\mathcal{C}}_{i}([v]_{i})\partial_{3}\varphi^{[n]}
, we can direct control it when
D^{\alpha}
contains spatial derivatives. When
D^{\alpha}=\partial_{t}^{4}
, we can still find the same cancellation structure that the troubling term
-4\int_{\Sigma}\partial_{t}^{3}[q]^{[n]}\partial_{t}^{2}\overline{[v]}^{[n]}\partial_{t}\overline{\partial}\psi^{[n]}
from
\int_{\Sigma}[\mathbf{Q}]^{[n]}{\mathcal{S}}^{[n]}_{b}
can still be cancelled by the boundary integral produced by integrating
\partial_{3}
by parts in
4\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}^{3}[q](\partial_{t}\overline{\partial}\varphi^{[n]}\cdot\partial_{t}^{2}\partial_{3}\overline{[v]})\partial_{3}\varphi^{[n]}
from
\int_{\Omega}D^{\alpha}[q]^{[n]}\mathring{\mathcal{C}}_{i}([v]_{i})\partial_{3}\varphi^{[n]}.

Summarising the above estimates, for small
\delta>0
, we obtain the energy estimate

\displaystyle[E]_{3}^{[n]}(t)\leq\delta[E]_{3}^{[n]}(t)+C(\mathring{K}_{0},\kappa^{-1})\int_{0}^{t}\bigg([E]_{3}^{[n]}+[E]_{3}^{[n-1]}+[E]_{3}^{[n-2]}\bigg).

(3.70)

Thus there exists
T_{\kappa}^{\prime}>0
independent of
n
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T_{\kappa}^{\prime}}[E]_{3}^{[n]}(t)\leq\frac{1}{4}\bigg(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T_{\kappa}^{\prime}}[E]_{3}^{[n-1]}(t)+\sup_{0\leq t\leq T_{\kappa}^{\prime}}[E]_{3}^{[n-2]}(t)\bigg),

(3.71)

and thus we know by induction that

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T_{\kappa}^{\prime}}[E]_{3}^{[n]}(t)\leq C(\mathring{K}_{0},\kappa^{-1})\frac{1}{2^{n-1}}\to 0\text{ as }n\to\infty.

(3.72)

Hence, for fixed
\kappa>0
, the sequence of approximate solutions
\{(v^{[n]},F_{k}^{[n]},\mathfrak{F}_{k}^{[n]},q^{[n]},\psi^{[n]})\}
has a strongly convergent subsequence and the limit
\{(v^{[\infty]},F_{k}^{[\infty]},\mathfrak{F}_{k}^{[\infty]},q^{[\infty]},\psi^{[\infty]})\}
is a solution of (
3.10
). We can recover the solution
\{(v^{[\infty]},F_{k}^{[\infty]},q^{[\infty]},\psi^{[\infty]})\}
to our approximate system (
2.2
) by remark
3.3
.

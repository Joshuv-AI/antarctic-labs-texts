64 | The Unseen Cost of Maternal Asthma: Increased Risk of Neurological Morbidity in Offspring

Ron Hadar1; Sapir Ellouk2; Tamar Wainstock3; Eyal Sheiner4

1Ben‐Gurion University of the Negev, Beer Sheva, Israel; 2Soroka University Medical Center, Beersheba, Israel; 3Department of Epidemiology and Health Services Evaluation, Ben‐Gurion University of the Negev, Beer Sheva, Israel; 4Soroka University Medical Center, Beer Sheva, Israel

Objective: Maternal asthma is a prevalent chronic condition with known associations to adverse obstetric outcomes. However, its potential impact on the long‐term neurological health of offspring remains insufficiently studied. This study aimed to examine whether maternal asthma during pregnancy is independently associated with increased risk of neurological morbidity in offspring up to 18 years of age.

Study Design: A population‐based cohort study was conducted to evaluate the risk of long‐term neurological morbidity in offspring based upon the presence or absence of maternal asthma. Data for the incidence of neurological morbidity of the offspring was extracted from community and hospitalization records. A Kaplan‐Meir survival curve was used to compare the cumulative incidence over time of long‐term neurological morbidity between the study groups. Cox proportional hazards model was constructed to control for possible confounders.

Results: The analysis included 232,476 deliveries, of which 2875 (1.2%) were to mothers with asthma. Offspring of asthmatic mothers had significantly higher rates of neurological morbidity compared to unexposed offspring (27.8% vs 17.3%, p < 0.001), including attention‐deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD), autism spectrum disorder, psychiatric disorders and movement disorders (p < 0.001 for all; Table). Kaplan–Meier analysis demonstrated a significantly higher cumulative incidence of neurological morbidity among the exposed group (log‐rank 206.276; p < 0.001; Figure). In a multivariable Cox regression model adjusting for gestational age, maternal age, cesarean delivery, diabetes mellitus, and hypertensive disorders, maternal asthma remained an independent predictor of neurological morbidity (adjusted hazard ratio = 1.57; 95% CI, 1.47–1.69; p < 0.001).

Conclusion: Maternal asthma during pregnancy is independently associated with elevated long‐term neurological morbidity in offspring. These findings emphasize the need for heightened awareness and long‐term follow‐up of children exposed to maternal asthma in utero.

65 | Virtual Management of Pregnancy‐Related Nausea: Guideline Adherence, Symptom Resolution, and ED Utilization

Ian J. Hooley; Isabelle Von Kohorn; Kathryn G. Fisher; Eva M. Luo

Pomelo Care, Pomelo Care, NY

Objective: To evaluate care quality, guideline adherence, symptom resolution, and emergency department (ED) utilization within a virtual maternity program managing nausea and vomiting of pregnancy (NVP) via text and video.

Study Design: A 12‐month retrospective assessment was conducted among 1979 commercially insured patients (< 20 weeks gestation). NVP‐related text encounters were identified via keyword search (nausea, vomiting, morning sickness, hyperemesis). To analyze unstructured chat data, a Large Language Model (LLM) with structured output was used to classify encounters by severity, guideline‐driven interventions (lifestyle guidance, OTC/prescription antiemetics, escalation), and resolution. We employed an iterative human‐in‐the‐loop validation process: humans manually labeled a subset of encounters, discrepancies informed prompt refinement, and the LLM re‐processed the data until high fidelity was achieved, yielding 180 primary NVP encounters. Separately, claims data benchmarked NVP‐related ED visit rates (ICD O21.x, R11.x) between engaged (n = 881) and non‐engaged (n = 1,098) patients to assess program impact on utilization.

Results: Among 180 primary NVP encounters, 20% were mild, 55% moderate, and 25% severe. Intervention intensity correlated with severity: prescription antiemetics in 50% of severe versus 0% of mild; provider escalation in 70% of severe versus 17% of mild. Non‐escalated cases were managed via guideline‐directed care with lifestyle guidance, OTC medications, and monitoring. With documented follow‐up, 98% reported symptom improvement. Only 2 cases required ED referral, both appropriate for dehydration. In claims, engaged patients had an NVP ED visit rate of 1.4% versus 6.4% for non‐engaged patients in the same plans (4.7X reduction; p < 0.001; average cost $981/visit).

Conclusion: A virtual care model managed NVP across severity levels with guideline‐concordant escalation, high symptom resolution, and reduced ED utilization among engaged patients in this program evaluation. Iterative human‐validated LLM classification supported internal review at scale for clinical quality assessment.

66 | Role of Intrapartum Ultrasonography in Assessing Labour Progress and Predicting Delivery Mode in Nulliparous Women

Shuchita Mundle; Jeffy Castillo

All India Institute of Medical Sciences (AIIMS), Nagpur, Nagpur, India

Objective: Intrapartum transperineal ultrasonography offers an objective method to assess labour progress using parameters such as Angle of Progression (AoP) and Head–Perineum Distance (HPD). This study evaluates its role in predicting labour outcomes in nulliparous women.

Study Design: In this prospective observational study, nulliparous women with singleton cephalic pregnancies ≥37 weeks in active labour were enrolled. HPD and AoP were measured using transperineal ultrasonography. Labour management was based on digital examination to avoid bias. Statistical analysis included ROC curves, multivariable logistic regression, and linear regression.

Results: Among 128 women (mean age 26.97 ± 3.86 years; BMI 22.76 ± 3.72 kg/m2), 76.6% had normal vaginal delivery (NVD) and 23.4% underwent LSCS. Mean HPD was 3.75 ± 0.63 cm and AoP 133.99 ± 11.66°. NVD was associated with lower HPD (3.68 ± 0.42 cm) and higher AoP (134.49° ± 11.12°) compared to LSCS (HPD 3.96 ± 0.47 cm; AoP 132.36° ± 12.04°; p < 0.05). ROC analysis showed good predictive value for HPD (AUC 0.76; cut‐off >3.8 cm) and AoP (AUC 0.79; cut‐off <120°), with excellent accuracy when combined (AUC 0.86). HPD independently increased LSCS risk (AOR 1.52/cm, p = 0.001), while AoP reduced risk (AOR 0.94/degree, p = 0.004). In vaginal deliveries, mean labour duration was 218 minutes; HPD correlated positively (+ 18.4 min/cm) and AoP inversely (–12.6 min/10°). BMI and induction were not significant. Neonatal outcomes were favourable (mean birth weight 2800 ± 420 g; NICU admission 13.3%; no mortality).

Conclusion: Transperineal ultrasonography using HPD and AoP is a reliable tool for assessing labour progress and predicting mode of delivery. AoP showed slightly better predictive performance, while combined assessment improved accuracy. Its integration into labour management may aid early detection of dystocia and optimize clinical decision‐making.

67 | Fetal Monitoring With AI Enabled Cardiotocography and Corelation With Cord Blood pH

Shuchita Mundle; Chandana Tholu

All India Institute of Medical Sciences (AIIMS), Nagpur, Nagpur, India

Objective: Artificial intelligence (AI) in fetal ECG signal processing has improved cardiotocography (CTG) interpretation by reducing subjectivity and enhancing detection of fetal compromise.

To evaluate AI‐enabled CTG monitoring during labor and correlate CTG parameters with cord blood pH and neonatal outcomes.

To evaluate AI‐enabled CTG monitoring during labor and correlate CTG parameters with cord blood pH and neonatal outcomes.

Study Design: This prospective observational study included 90 women in active labor with singleton, term (≥37 weeks), cephalic pregnancies and abnormal CTG patterns. Monitoring was performed using the Care Mother Fetosense CTG V2.0 device with AI‐based interpretation. Fetal heart rate was assessed using Doppler and uterine contractions via tocodynamometry. CTG abnormalities were correlated with cord blood pH, APGAR score, and NICU admission.

Results: The mean maternal age was 27.5 ± 3.91 years; 78.8% were primiparous, with a mean gestational age of 39.1 ± 1.03 weeks. Variable decelerations were most common (36.6%), followed by late (17.7%) and prolonged decelerations (12.2%). Fetal tachycardia and bradycardia were observed in 12.2% and 11.1%, respectively. Minimal variability was seen in 17.7%. Cord blood acidosis (pH <7.2) occurred in 33.3% neonates. Abnormal baseline fetal heart rate was associated with a 1.45‐fold higher incidence of acidosis, while absence of accelerations was 1.2 times more frequent in acidotic neonates. APGAR score <7 at 1 minute was seen in 23.3%, with 76.1% of acidotic neonates having low APGAR scores. Mean cord pH in this group was 7.14 ± 0.13. NICU admission occurred in 42.2%, most commonly for respiratory distress (50%), with bradycardia being the most associated CTG parameter.

Conclusion: AI‐enabled CTG improves detection of abnormal fetal heart rate patterns. Absence of accelerations and reduced variability are strongly associated with acidosis, while abnormal CTG correlates with low APGAR scores and increased NICU admissions, supporting its role in predicting adverse neonatal outcomes.

Objective: Achondroplasia (ACH) and hypochondroplasia (HCH) are skeletal dysplasias affecting ∼1 in 30,000 births. Increasing use of prenatal ultrasound and fetal MRI has identified associated neuroanatomic findings, including temporal lobe and other structural brain anomalies, but their clinical significance for counseling and perinatal management remains unclear. We aimed to characterize neuroanatomic abnormalities on fetal and pediatric MRI and assess associations with postnatal neurologic outcomes.

Study Design: We conducted a retrospective cohort study of fetal and pediatric ACH/HCH cases with available brain imaging and neurodevelopmental data (2000–2024) across five North American tertiary centers. Primary outcomes included incidence of seizures, developmental delay, and neurosurgical intervention associated with brain anomalies; secondary outcomes assessed concordance between prenatal and postnatal imaging.

Results: A total of 115 cases were reviewed, of which 101 (85 ACH, 16 HCH) had at least one MRI (Table 1) Among 23 fetal MRIs, findings included temporal lobe (TL) abnormalities (n = 3), corpus callosal dysgenesis (CCD) (n = 2), hippocampal malrotation (HM) (n = 1), and ventriculomegaly (VM) (n = 5) (Figure 1). Seizures were rare (n = 7), while learning delays were more common (n = 26). Prenatal and postnatal MRI findings were generally concordant, with a few new (n = 3) or resolved (n = 2) findings.

Conclusion: This study provides valuable data for counseling patients and their families when such abnormalities are detected. Seizures and severe developmental delays appear to be rare but are associated with the presence of neuroanatomic abnormalities. Further studies are needed to clarify the imaging–clinical outcome relationships in FGFR3‐related skeletal dysplasias.

1Massachusetts General Hospital, Boston, MA; 2Harvard TH Chan School of Public Health, Boston, MA; 3Children's Hospital of Philadelphia, Philadelphia, PA; 4Tufts Medical Center, Boston, MA; 5University of South Florida, Tampa, FL; 6UC San Diego, Dept of Obstetrics, Gynecology, and Reproductive Sciences, San Diego, CA

Objective: SMFM Consult Series #58: Use of Antenatal Corticosteroids for Individuals at Risk for Late Preterm Delivery was published at the end of 2021 and replaced the Society's earlier guidance from 2016. The new guidance acknowledged the conflicting data on long‐term neurodevelopmental outcomes and encouraged shared decision‐making. Our objective was to determine i this new guidance led to a practice change in late preterm steroid use among US births.

Study Design: This cross‐sectional study used an interrupted time‐series (ITS) design and data from the Premier Healthcare Database (PHD), a nationally representative data set. We designated 01/2022 as the intervention knot. We selected a 3‐year observational period before (01/19–12/21) and after (01/22–12/24) to quantify whether the guideline led to practice changes. The primary variable of interest was late preterm steroid exposure among late preterm singleton deliveries. We also examined exposure among term deliveries. Steroid exposure was modeled at the patient level using Poisson regression with a log‐link function and the change in slope at the knot as the primary parameter of interest. Unit of time was measured in quarters, and the model was adjusted for age, race, ethnicity, insurance, gestational age at delivery, and seasonality. Results are reported as adjusted incidence rate ratios (aIRR) with 95% confidence intervals.

Results: 176,938 and 165,987 late preterm births and 2,361,067 and 2,172,343 term births in the pre‐ and post‐period were included, respectively. Prior to the guideline, steroid exposure among late preterm deliveries was stable (aIRR: 1.01; 95% CI: 1.00, 1.01). Following publication, steroid exposure rates declined by 3% per quarter (aIRR: 0.97; 95% CI: 0.96, 0.98), representing a significant change in the trajectory of use (Figure 1). Exposure among term deliveries showed a similar decline (aIRR: 0.97; 95% CI: 0.96, 0.98) (Figure 2). Findings were robust to sensitivity analyses.

Conclusion: Publication of the updated SMFM guidance on late preterm steroid use decreased steroid exposure among late preterm and term births in the US.

71 | First‐line Non‐Pharmacological Interventions in Postpartum Hemorrhage: A Systematic Review

Paul Berveiller1; Ben D. McNally2; Yanek Saribekyan3; Candice Yong4

1CHI Poissy Saint‐Germain, Poissy, France; 2Costello Medical, Edinburgh, United Kingdom; 3Laborie, Portsmouth, NH; 4Organon Pharmaceuticals, Jersey City, NJ

Objective: Postpartum hemorrhage (PPH) affects approximately 14 million women worldwide and can be associated with severe maternal morbidity. If pharmacological treatments are unsuccessful, less‐invasive non‐pharmacological methods, such as uterine balloon tamponade (UBT), intrauterine vacuum‐induced hemorrhage‐control device (VHD) and arterial embolization (AE), are required, followed by surgical management. The aim of this systematic literature review (SLR) was to identify available evidence on the clinical effectiveness and safety of first‐line non‐pharmacological interventions, namely UBT, VHD and AE, in the management of primary postpartum hemorrhage.

Study Design: This SLR was conducted in accordance with a pre‐specified protocol aligned with the Cochrane Collaboration methods and PRISMA guidelines. Searches of MEDLINE, Embase and the Cochrane Library were conducted in September 2024 and updated in October 2025. Relevant congress proceedings (2022–25), clinical trial registries and SLR bibliographies were manually searched.

Results: In total, 92 studies were included (Figure 1). Baseline characteristics were often poorly reported. Interstudy comparisons were limited by differences in patients, settings and treatment protocols. Treatment success rate ranged from 55–100% for UBT (39 studies), 83.70–94.00% for VHD (3 studies) and 71.4–100% for AE (12 studies). VHD was associated with reduced blood loss (5/6 studies), lower transfusion rates (4/5 studies) and shorter device indwelling time (3/3 studies) compared with UBT. Blood loss and transfusion rates were similar between UBT and AE (1 study). No studies directly compared VHD and AE. Study quality was variable across studies (QualSyst tool [AHFMR]; summary score: 0.17–0.93).

Conclusion: The results of this SLR indicate that VHD might be associated with reduced device indwelling time, blood loss and transfusions versus UBT, but further comparative data are required given the limited evidence and potential bias in published studies. Future publications must report patient characteristics in detail to support comparisons between studies.

Study Design: Retrospective, population‐based cohort study using 2013–2022 PHARMO Data Network data, (hospital, in‐patient pharmacy) linked to the Netherlands Perinatal Registry (Perined). PPH was defined by ≥500 mL blood loss, ICD‐coded diagnosis, or in‐hospital use of second‐line uterotonics (excluding oxytocin) and/or tranexamic acid. Severe cases also had greater concurrent use of multiple uterotonics (15% vs 11%), higher use of tranexamic acid (38% vs 15%), and more frequent balloon tamponade (3% vs <0.5%) and blood transfusions (6% vs 5%).

Conclusion: PPH incidence rose overall in the Netherlands, possibly due to improved recording of blood loss, and was highest after caesarean section, consistent with other countries. This highlights the burden of PPH, particularly among severe cases likely to require more intensive treatment and to experience poorer outcomes. Identifying high‐risk patients at labour onset and after birth can support targeted prevention and intervention strategies.

1Department of Obstetrics and Gynecology, Sheba Medical Center, Ramat Gan, Israel; 2ChristianaCare, Newark, DE; 3Sheba Medical Center, Ramat Gan, Israel

Objective: Data on the use of amnioinfusion during vaginal delivery after cesarean (VBAC) are limited. We evaluated whether intrapartum amnioinfusion increases the risk of uterine rupture or dehiscence in individuals undergoing trial of labor after cesarean delivery (TOLAC).

Study Design: Retrospective cohort of all individuals undergoing TOLAC at a tertiary center (Jan 2013‐Jun 2025). Per department protocol amnioinfusion was used for repetitive variable decelerations. We excluded those with known major fetal anomalies, stillbirth and multiple gestations. The primary outcome was uterine rupture/dehiscence. Secondary outcomes included unplanned cesarean, postpartum hemorrhage, transfusion, hysterectomy, endometritis, wound infection, ICU admission, and adverse neonatal outcomes. Multivariable regression estimated adjusted risks.

Results: Of 131,489 deliveries, 5103 (3.9%) met the inclusion criteria; among them, 315 (6.2%) had an amnioinfusion. Individuals who underwent an amnioinfusion delivered at a later gestational age and had a higher rate of labor induction, intrapartum fever, meconium‐stained amniotic fluid compared to those who did not. However, amnioinfusion was associated with a higher rate of cesarean delivery compared to those that did not (adjusted OR 3.1; 95% CI 2.4–4; p < 0.01). Conclusion: Among individuals undergoing a trial of labor after cesarean, the use of amnioinfusion was not associated with increased risk for uterine rupture or dehiscence. Amnioinfusion is a safe intervention that can be offered to individuals undergoing TOLAC.

Conclusion: Hypertensive disorders of pregnancy, abnormal placentation, higher combined birthweight, vaginal delivery, and ART are independent predictors of PPH in twin gestations. 78 | Inherited Thrombophilia Associated With Intrauterine Fetal Demise and Pregnancy Loss: A Large‐Scale Retrospective Cohort Study

Hadassah Hebrew University Medical Center and Hebrew University of Jerusalem, Jeruslaem, Israel

Objective: The association between inherited thrombophilia and pregnancy complications has long been subject to investigation. Unlike acquired thrombophilia, no clear association between inherited thrombophilia and Intrauterine fetal demise (IUFD) and pregnancy loss has been found, with conflicting results between studies. This study aimed to evaluate the relationship between inherited thrombophilia, pregnancy loss and IUFD in a large‐scale cohort.

Study Design: We conducted a retrospective cohort study using the TriNetX US Collaborative Network, which includes de‐identified electronic medical records from 70 US healthcare organizations between March 2006, and March 2026. Pregnant women aged 18–40, without history of other chronic diseases, who were diagnosed with inherited thrombophilia, were matched 1:1 to those without inherited thrombophilia (n = 8,626 per group) using propensity scores based on age, race and ethnicity, BMI, tobacco or substance use during pregnancy and parity.

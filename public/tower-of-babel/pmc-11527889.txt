Action recognition using attention-based spatio-temporal VLAD networks and adaptive video sequences optimization
Authors: Zhengkui Weng, Xinmin Li, Shoujian Xiong
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn the field of human action recognition, it is a long-standing challenge to characterize the video-level spatio-temporal features effectively. This is attributable in part to the inability of CNN to model long-range temporal information, especially for actions that consist of multiple staged behaviors. In this paper, a novel attention-based spatio-temporal VLAD network (AST-VLAD) with self-attention model is developed to aggregate the informative deep features across the video according to the adaptive deep feature selected. Moreover, an overall automatic approach to adaptive video sequences optimization (AVSO) is proposed through shot segmentation and dynamic weighted sampling, the AVSO increase in the proportion of action-related frames and eliminate the redundant intervals. Then, based on the optimized video, a self-attention model is introduced in AST-VLAD to modeling the intrinsic spatio-temporal relationship of deep features instead of solving the frame-level features in an average or max pooling manner. Extensive experiments are conducted on two public benchmarks-HMDB51 and UCF101 for evaluation. As compared to the existing frameworks, results show that the proposed approach performs better or as well in the accuracy of classification on both HMDB51 (73.1% ) and UCF101 (96.0%) datasets.Subject terms: Computer science, Information technology

Introduction

With regard to computer vision, video action recognition is a research hotspot1. In recent years, it has attracted increasing attention as a crucial part of the intelligent monitoring system2. Currently in the initial stage of theoretical and commercial use, most of the existing research results are yet to be industrialized. As a kind of research method, action recognition has also shifted from the traditional hand-craft based feature extraction3–5 to the deep learning based convolutional neural network (CNN)6,7. Apparently, some action videos contain repetitive or even redundant video shots. In addition to causing failure in improving the effectiveness of action recognition, they also hinder discriminant feature extraction. As shown in Fig. 1, some typical frames that carry weak semantics information are illustrated in the first part. It is not the case that all the video frames are closely correlated with the action, which makes it difficult to extract discriminative features from these frames.

Some video frames that may interfere with action recognition but some are not. Three videos of “Shoot bow”“Eat”“Long jump” are selected from datasets. It is not difficult to see that, frame 1 to 8 and 25 to end in “Shoot bow” have almost no relationship with the action. This situation also occurs in Frame 1 to 7 of the “Eat” simultaneously. On the contrary, as a long-term dependent action, the frames in “Long jump” is all action related.

Up to now, deep convolution network has been extensively applied in image classification, especially when large datasets are involved8,9. In recent years, the practice of video processing based on deep convolution network has gradually become a research hotspot. At present, there are two most common architectures used, one of which is 3D spatio-temporal convolution that is based neural network6,10,11. Despite being capable to model potential and complex dependencies through numerous operations of spatio-temporal domain convolution, the network requires a large number of parameters and relies heavily on the pre-trained model. The other is the two-stream architecture7,12–14, in which the video sequence is decomposed into RGB streams and optical stream, representing appearance and motion feature, respectively. The CNN networks are trained separately. Finally, the respective prediction scores are fused to obtain the recognition results. With a rapid progress made in both architectures, the two-stream architecture has been widely adopted for its intuitive description of human action and the exercise of ultra-deep architecture15–18. However, the two-stream architecture, which ignores the long-term feature, learns the classifier for a single or several frames. Then, the average score of each frame is calculated to obtain the category of actions. A natural question arising from this operation is that, can this average operation model the complex spatio-temporal structure of each action? This concern is often highlighted when one label is shared by multiple sub actions. For example, as shown in the second row of Fig. 1, the “Long jump” action is comprised of three sub actions: “run-up”, “taking-off” and “landing”. Under the traditional late fusion framework, there is a necessity to assign the descriptors classed into the same sub action to multiple categories, for their integration into one action category. Apparently, this pipeline is not the optimal method, but the optimal action expression is purposed to obtain the global features of the video as a description. Also, it is from the aggregation of action appearance and motion that the global features are obtained.

In view of the above observations and analysis, a novel framework is developed to address the weaknesses of two-stream and spatio-temporal convolution architecture. Named as attention-based spatio-temporal VLAD networks (AST-VLAD), this framework relies on adaptive video sequences optimization (AVSO) to recognize video action. To begin with, the input video is optimized by AVSO to improve the ratio of action features. Then, spatio-temporal context information is obtained through AST-VLAD. Finally, the context awareness of long video sequences is improved by constructing a soft assignment operation model based on self-attention mechanism. Meanwhile, its spatio-temporal features are retained.

Related works

Deep features based methods

It has gradually become a popular trend (CNN) to extract video expression from data directly through convolutional neural network. Karpathy et al.19 pioneered in applying convolutional neural network on sports 1 M, a large behavioral video dataset, to achieve video descriptions, with a foundation laid for the feasibility of convolutional neural network in video processing. By proposing a two-stream architecture for the first time, Zisserman et al.7 used two identical convolutional networks to model the appearance and motion information of the actions in the video. Subsequently, Wang et al.20 went further to extract the feature maps with richer semantic information using the more robust backbone network, with their classification made consistent for the feature maps of video sequences separately. Another commonly used way of video expression is derived from spatio-temporal convolution6. The acquisition of spatio-temporal feature plays a key role in action recognition, and a consensus has been reached on this in a lot of research work21–24. However, according to Literature25, it is difficult to model long video sequences (up to 120 frames) in this way, which hampers its capability to learn the whole video. In addition, there are a number of parameters required for the spatio-temporal convolution network, and it is prone to overfitting or underfitting on small datasets.

In general, there are two ways to model long-term action information: explicit modeling and implicit modeling. In the representative task of explicit modeling, segmented semantics are used to analyze action videos22 and capture hierarchical temporal structure by describing simple semantics. Also, a finite state machine is employed to concatenate actions in different periods. By contrast, implicit modeling is reliant mainly on an appropriate descriptor to describe and interpret the whole video26–29. Ng et al.30 used LSTM to integrate inter-frame information. In the model, experiments are performed on Sports 1 M dataset19 to verify that LSTM model achieves the best outcome of recognition on RGB flow and optical flow streams. Xu et al.28 integrated the pre-training model of ImageNet with VLAD to aggregate video descriptions. However, these methods intended for the post-processing of features are not trainable end-to-end methods. In Literature31, an additional layer of LSTM was further introduced to form a “Double deep”, so as to widen the perception range of the model to the context while achieving the purpose of learning the long-term dependence of time series signals. To further model spatio-temporal features, a cross-time temporal module was proposed in32, which aims to efficiently capture dynamic contextual information of both local and global temporal dimensions. In Literature33, in order to effectively capturing spatio-temporal information, a temporal refinement block as an efficient component was proposed by generating spatio-temporal kernels of different scales and performing fully connected operations within the temporal dimension. A dual 3-D convolutional network with two complementary lightweight branches was developed in11, where a shallow spatio-temporal downsampling module is shared for both branches.

However, the use of 3D convolution greatly increases the parameters of the model. Most of these methods are effective in the local neighborhood range, showing two disadvantages: low computing efficiency32 and optimization problem, which depends on the fine network configuration. A recent approach34 indicated that rather than focus on enhancing the backbone features individually using hierarchical structures, its better to suppresses the over-expression of similar features in adjacent frames and activates the expression of motion flow information. Motivated by these findings, we proposed a shot segmentation and dynamic weighted sampling method to increase the proportion of action-related frames and eliminating redundant intervals.

Video-level deep representation

In order to model the long-term temporal information of video-based action recognition, there have been various frameworks proposed35,36. Proposed in Literature37, a spatio-temporal vector of locally max pooled features combines the deep features over the entire video with max-pooling and sum-pooling. Kar et al.38 put forward an AdaScan framework, with sampled video frames collected according to the discrimination importance of each predicted frame. To learn more about global video representation, Wang et al.39 sampled optical flow frames through multi-path time subnet work (sharing network parameters with longer sequences). In design, the STCB operator can be used to integrate spatial and temporal features effectively. To make use of long-term temporal information through a sparse sampling strategy, Wang et al.20 developed one video-level framework known as the temporal segmenting network (TSN). Girdhar et al.40 modified NetVLAD41 through trainable spatio-temporal extensions to aggregate the depth features in spatial and temporal dimensions, with the “action words” used to aggregate descriptors into video level fixed length vectors. Spatio-temporal VLAD (ST-VLAD)42 rephrases the problem of VLAD feature optimization with Lagrange multiplier for the minimum difference between the VLAD descriptors corresponding to the adjacent frames. Xu et al.43 proposed the SeqVLAD to aggregate the features of frames and used the fine spatial information in video content. However, these methods are applied mainly to global frames or salient regions, without distinguishing specific object instances. Also, the temporal evolution of each object in the video can not be captured effectively. Tu et al.44 developed an ActionS-ST-VLAD encoding approach, in which the keyframe features are chosen and the corresponding deep features are automatically split into the segments with the features in each segment. Nevertheless, there is a lack of access to temporal information under the framework when soft assignment is calculated, which results in the lack of spatio-temporal feature.

Recently, as a type of attention mechanism, transformer-based approaches have achieved promising results in action recognition. Ma et al.45 proposed a relative-position embedding transformer for action recognition, which can capture spatial-temporal information by stacked self-attention layers. A multi-attention network which consists of query-based cross-modal transformer module, which extract effective representation from each video in three modalities35. To deal with the high-dimensional properties and multiple scales, a multi scale hybrid attention module adopts an improved and efficient selective kernel network was developed in46, which effectively capture action-related frames to suppress noise interference while also having a lower computational. Notably, channel attention assigns varying weights to different channels, while spatial attention indicates the importance of different location features within the same channel47. CBAM48 incorporates both spatial and temporal attention mechanisms to enhance the model’s ability to perceive complex features.

Compared to the above-mentioned attention mechanisms, AST-VLAD does not introduce additional sub-networks to generate parameters, thus avoiding increasing the complexity of the original network. Furthermore, this mechanism further adjusts the attention weights of feature maps to obtain more comprehensive information.

It is crucial to find out how the spatio-temporal feature of video action can be extracted accurately. The figure indicates two sub models, namely, AVSO and AST-VLAD, which are highlighted in blue and green, respectively.

Overview of the proposed framework. The entire framework is mainly divided into two parts, the adaptive video sequences optimization (AVSO) and attention-based spatio-temporal VLAD Networks (AST-VLAD).

To begin with, the input video is divided into several video fragments by means of the dual-detection based shot segmentation. Then, the video is optimized using the proposed dynamic weighted sampling strategy to recreate action sequences. Also, the video is refined by the optimized action sequences modules to increase the ratio of action features. Next, the optimized video flows to the second component, namely, the AST-VLAD. Finally, a two-stream AST-VLAD is constructed to extract the features of two supplementary modalities, namely, RGB and optical flow, thus strengthening the deep representation of humans for robust processing and expanding the receptive field of feature extraction.

Adaptive video sequences optimization

The redundant frames in video not only have a significant impact on the computation and performance of the method, but also reduce the ability and efficiency to focus on action-related frames for the extraction of features. The effect remains unsatisfactory although some attention based methods14,22,26 have increased the weight of keyframes in the classification process.

Considering the two or three shots in many videos, effective action-related information is usually carried by only one shot, and the rest is comprised of narrative context sequences. In this section, an Adaptive Video Sequences Optimization (AVSO) method is proposed on the basis of shot segmentation and dynamic weighted sampling. By increasing the proportion of active frames and eliminating redundant intervals, the proposed method is capable to optimize the extraction of action features in a long time range. Unlike literature26, instead of using deep network to extract the low-level features and estimate the distribution of the training samples, the proposed AVSO improve the discrimination of deep features through a parameter free unsupervised method. Dynamic weighted sampling strategy

Following video shot segmentation, a dynamic weighted sampling algorithm is applied to optimized action video. As for the redundant parts in the video sequence, they are filtered by dynamic weighted sampling to increase the proportion of highly active action areas. According to Literature51, the video shot with maximum entropy carries nearly complete action information. For this reason, an algorithm is designed for the dynamic sampling of different shots with varying entropy weights in the absence of manual parameters. In the absence of supervised learning, the dynamic weighted sampling strategy is adopted to process the time dimension of videos. Attention-based spatio-temporal VLAD network

In recent years, the attention mechanism has been widely applied in natural language processing, machine translation and other settings. This attention mechanism is advantageous as it takes into consideration the input information of all sequences. Also, the connection weight under each output element can be automatically learned. Since the NetVLAD encodes only the aggregates local information of spatial context in each video frame41, the existence of temporal information is ignored when soft assignment is calculated. The attention-based spatio-temporal VLAD (AST-VLAD) network is developed in this paper. By introducing a self-attention module to model the intrinsic spatio-temporal relationship in each feature vector, the context awareness of long video sequences is improved. The self-attention module proposed in this paper assigns the output as a weight to the residual value of the feature vector and the aggregation center, instead of using the output of the self-attention model as a video representation for classification. Experiments

Datasets and evaluation protocol

The UCF1018 dataset consists of 13,320 videos falling into 101 action categories. Each class includes more than 100 video clips, and each video clip has an average length of 180 frames. UCF101 shows the most significant diversity in terms of actions and the videos in the same group may be similar in background and viewpoint. The proposed framework is evaluated on the first few train-test splits and the accuracy is reported.

The HMDB519 dataset is collected from various sources. In total, there are 6766 videos falling into 51 action categories, each of which contains a minimum of 100 clips. It is challenging to perform action recognition due to the issues of videos, such as the poor quality, a wide range of variations in which actions occur, and the strong camera motions contained. The accuracy is reported over the average of 3 train-test splits.

Implementation setup

In order to validate the proposed framework, BN-Inception52 is introduced as backbone models to extract feature maps in the experiment. The outputs of “inception 5b” are extracted as the feature maps, whose dimension is T×1024 × 7 × 7, where T is referred to as the input length of video frame. Data augmentation is performed to ensure the diversity and randomness of the data. Before the aggregate features are calculated, the feature map passes a three-dimensional convolutional kernel 1 × 1 × 1, with the number of channels reduced to C’, where C’=128. By setting K aggregation centers, the final action expression size is obtained as C’×K. During classification, the model introduces a dropout layer to prevent overfitting, with its parameter sets to 0.7. During optimization, all the models in the framework, including the backbone network, the aggregation model and the classification layer, are uniformly optimized using the Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD) algorithm. The momentum is set to 0.9, and the gradient value is set to 20. Gradient clipping technology is applied to clip the gradient values in excess of 20. According to Literature40, only the proposed aggregation network is optimized the first stage, while all the model parameters are optimized in the second stage. For RGB stream, there are 210 epochs set in total, of which 90 epochs are in the first stage and the remaining ones are in the second stage. The initial learning rate is set to 0.02, which is reduced to one tenth at the 80th and 160th epochs. For the optical flow stream, 80 epochs are optimally set in the first stage, and 300 epochs are set in the second stage. The initial learning rate is set to 0.01, which is reduced to one tenth at the 100th and 220th epochs.

Among them, the most important is the setting of the coefficient fcoarse and ffine, which determine the video frame number involved in calculating. Comparison of parameters of different frameworks

In this section, the input, feature domain and parameter quantity of different video-level representation frameworks are comparatively evaluated. As shown in Tables 1and 7 different video level feature representation frameworks are compiled for comparison. Both the Two-Stream model7 and NetVLAD41 rely on static frames as inputs, which causes a lack of temporal information. Based on the segment mechanism, TSN20 is capable to capture the temporal information. Similar to NetVLAD, TSN requires a single classification layer, for which there are the same number of classification layer parameters for TSN. In terms of parameter quantity, both NetVLAD and ActionVLAD40 rely on the same aggregation layer for feature aggregation when the number of aggregation centers is identical. Also, they are common in obtaining classification results through the same single classification layer. Thus, they have the same amount of aggregation layer and classification layer parameters. Through the Shared GRU (SGRU), SeqVLAD43 further reduces the number of parameters in the aggregation layer. Slightly higher in parameter quantity, ST-VLAD42 is reliant on spatio-temporal convolution to calculate soft assignment while retaining the spatio-temporal information. With regard to the ActionS-ST-VLAD44, a flow-guided warping technique is applied to detect and remove redundant feature maps. Meanwhile, the informative ones are aggregated by using similarity weight. The parameter quantity is basically consistent with that in Literature43. The AST-VLAD proposed in this study applies the self-attention mechanism to obtain more information about long-term dependency. An additional 3D convolution is performed to calculate the weighted attention, and the number of aggregation layer parameters is slightly larger compared to ST-VLAD.

In Fig. 6, we compare the efficiency by reporting the average number of frames per second (Fr/Sec) on HMDB51 Split 1 on a server with an 13th Gen Intel(R) Core(TM) i5-13400 2.50 GHz, 32GB memory and a RTX 4070Ti GPU. Comparison of different number of aggregation centers

The number of aggregation centers is considered an important parameter of our framework, which indicates the extent to which video action is refined. In order to test the impact caused by the number of aggregation centers on the accuracy of action recognition, the accuracy of single stream and fusion stream is tested on both HMDB51 and UCF101 datasets. The number of aggregation centers K is 32, 64, 128 and 256, respectively. The results are shown in Fig. 7. In general, when the number of aggregation centers increases from 64 to 128, the accuracy of recognition is improved not only in the separate stream, (RGB and Optical flow) but also in the fusion stream significantly. However, when K increases from 128 to 256, there is no significant change in the accuracy of recognition. On the contrary, it even decreases. It is considered that when K is small, the model is insufficient to describe the distribution of sub actions, which leads to the limited semantic information. The semantic information is gradually enriched with the increase of K. However, it is prone to overfitting when the dimension of K is too large, which causes a decline in accuracy. Therefore, K is set to 128 for the following experiments.

The impact of different number of aggregation centers on recognition accuracy. The accuracy of single stream (RGB, Optical flow) and fusion stream is tested on both HMDB51 and UCF101 datasets. The number of aggregation centers K increases from 32 to 256.It is not difficult to see that 128 is a appropriate number of aggregation centers.

Ablation study

Evaluation of adaptive video sequences optimization

Video sequence optimization reflects an important innovation achieved in terms of action recognition. Most of the existing methods pay little attention to the action features in the highly active area, and they are vulnerable to the interference from background changes. Proposed in this paper, the adaptive optimization of video sequences is performed based on shot segmentation and dynamic sampling to optimized the video sequence by increasing the proportion of highly active action regions, eliminating redundant intervals, and extracting information in a larger temporal range. In the experiment, the classification accuracy of the optimized video and the original action video obtained by the AVSO is compared.

To evaluate the performance and universality of the proposed AST-VLAD network, the accuracy of recognition by VGG1653 as the backbone network is tested in the original experiment where BN-Inception is used. Experiments are conducted on UCF101 and HMDB51 datasets to demonstrate the effectiveness and superiority of the proposed method. As shown in Table 3, when adopted as the backbone model, VGG-16 shows the single stream accuracy (RGB, Optical flow) and two-stream late fusion accuracy that are 1.7%, 0.9% and 0.7% lower than BN-Inception on UCF101, respectively. On HMDB51, the difference is 2.3%, 1.4% and 1.5%, respectively. It is attributed largely to the network depth of BN-Inception. Compared with VGG-16, a more semantic description of human actions is enabled.

Comparison with state-of-the-art methods

In this section, our framework is compared against the state-of-the-art methods over all three splits on UCF101 and HMDB51, the result of which are shown in Table 4, as sourced from the original papers. Our framework (AVSO + AST-VLAD) outperforms most of them. At present, the iDT4 is considered one of the most successful hand-crafted features for video-based action recognition. The improvement of our framework reaches 10.1% and 12.9% on UCF101 and HMDB51, respectively. The most recent CNN methods that adopt the two-stream architectures are also selected for comparison. Our framework achieves an excellent performance as compared to them. Especially on HMDB51, the gain is no less than 1.6% (73.1% vs. 71.5%). These results demonstrate that action recognition can be facilitated by pooling the deep features across the entire video for video-level classification. For the work TSN20, our results outperform it by 1.8% on UCF101 and 3.7% on HDMDB51 since it is not applicable to select informative frames by randomly-sampling. For another work ActionVLAD40, our results are also much better, with the accuracy improved by 3.3% (92.7% vs. 96.0%) on UCF101 and 5.2% (66.9% vs. 73.1%) on HMDB51, respectively. This is because our proposed framework (AVSO + AST-VLAD) takes into consideration both the different action shots and long-term information. Secondly, the spatial-temporal features are aggregated to a single video representation for classification through a self-attention mechanism. For the work D3DNet11, it provides an online action recognition approach based on lightweight 3D convolution and achieves good real-time performance. How to better apply the model to practical situations is also one of the directions we want to study in the future. It is worth noting that ActionS-ST-VLAD + iDT44 model produces a better result. It is presumed that a good performance is likely to be achieved by fuse iDT features. This also indicates that introducing some discriminative hand-craft features can improve the overall performance .

Conclusions

A novel action recognition framework with VLAD is developed following spatio-temporal self-attention mechanism, named attention-based spatio-temporal VLAD networks (AST-VLAD). Also, an adaptive approach to video sequences optimization is proposed to effectively reduce the side effect from both spatial and temporal redundant information and noises. As verified by various experimental processes on HMDB51 and UCF101 datasets from real-world scenarios, our proposed framework outperforms the baseline model and achieves highly competitive performance. It is ascribed mainly to the soft assignment operation for the action-related frames, as well as the automatically dynamic weighted sampling strategy devised here.

Although our method performs well in pure recognition accuracy, its computational speed lags behind lightweight models, which may make it potentially unsuitable for certain application scenarios that require fast recognition in environments with limited hardware computing resources. Therefore, our future efforts will focus on making the model lighter, exploring lightweight video-level spatio-temporal feature representation, while preserving the advantages of its current recognition prowess.

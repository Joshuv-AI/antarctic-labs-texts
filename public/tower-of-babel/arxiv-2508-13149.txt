Magnetic Interactions of Wigner Crystal in Magnetic Field and Berry Curvature: Multi-Particle Tunneling through Complex Trajectories Preprint: APS/123-QED
Kyung-Su Kim

Abstract
We study how a weak perpendicular magnetic field B B and a Berry curvature Ω \Omega modify the magnetic interactions of a two-dimensional Wigner crystal (WC), using the semi-classical large- r s r_{s} expansion. When only a magnetic field is present, various ring-exchange interactions arise from electron tunneling along complex trajectories, which constitute complex instanton solutions of the coordinate-space path integral. To leading order in B B , each ring-exchange constant acquires an Aharonov-Bohm (AB) phase equal to the magnetic flux enclosed by the real tunneling trajectory of the B = 0 B=0 problem. This effect is directly relevant to two-dimensional electron systems with a small g g -factor ( g ≪ 1 g\ll 1 ). In the presence of a Berry curvature, multi-particle tunneling must be considered in a (complexified) phase space ( r , k ) (\textbf{r},\textbf{k}) . To leading order in Ω \Omega , the exchange constants acquire the Berry phase enclosed by a purely imaginary trajectory in a momentum space. Finally, when both B B and Ω \Omega are non-zero, in addition to having the AB and Berry phase factors, the magnitude of the exchange constant can also be renormalized by an effective-mass correction. These effects may be relevant for the WC and its proximate phases recently observed in tetra- and penta-layer rhombohedral graphene.

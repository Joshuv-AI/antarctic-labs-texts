Pure Gaps at Many Places and Multi-point AG Codes from Arbitrary Kummer Extensions
Huachao Zhang, Chang-An Zhao

Abstract
For a Kummer extension defined by the affine equation y m = ∏ i = 1 r ( x − α i ) λ i y^{m}=\prod_{i=1}^{r}(x-\alpha_{i})^{\lambda_{i}} over an algebraic extension K K of a finite field 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} , where λ i ∈ ℤ \ { 0 } \lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash\{0\} for 1 ≤ i ≤ r 1\leq i\leq r , gcd ⁡ ( m , q ) = 1 \gcd(m,q)=1 , and α 1 , ⋯ , α r ∈ K \alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{r}\in K are pairwise distinct elements, we propose a simple and efficient method to find all pure gaps at many totally ramified places. We introduce a bottom set of pure gaps and indicate that the set of pure gaps is completely determined by the bottom set. Furthermore, we demonstrate that a pure gap can be deduced from a known pure gap by easily verifying only one inequality. Then, in the case where λ 1 = λ 2 = ⋯ = λ r \lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}=\cdots=\lambda_{r} , we fully determine an explicit description of the set of pure gaps at many totally ramified places, This includes the scenario in which the set of these places contains the infinite place. Finally, we apply these results to construct multi-point algebraic geometry codes with good parameters. As one of the examples, a presented code with parameters [ 74 , 60 , ≥ 10 ] [74,60,\geq 10] over 𝔽 25 \mathbb{F}_{25} yields a new record.

Introduction

Since Goppa introduced the algebraic geometry (AG) codes
Goppa 1977
,
the research on AG codes has drawn significant attention in the field of coding theory.
Goppa’s construction of AG codes relies on a function field over a finite field.
By utilizing a function field, we hope to construct AG codes with desirable parameters, namely the length, the dimension, and the minimum distance.

One approach to constructing multi-point AG codes with good parameters is to utilize pure gaps at several places.
Homma and Kim
Homma and
Kim 2001
introduced the concept of pure gaps at two places, which can be used to improve the minimum distance of an AG code.
Then, this result was extended to several places by Carvalho and Torres in
Carvalho and
Torres 2005
.
The study of pure gaps and the construction of AG codes using pure gaps can be found in

Hu and Yang 2020
;
Carvalho and
Torres 2005
;
Hu and
Yang 2018
;
Bartoli
et al. 2018
;
Tizziotti and
Castellanos 2018
;
S. Castellanos
et al. 2024
;
Castellanos
et al. 2016
;
Homma and

;
Castellanos
et al. 2024
;

et al. 2018
;

;

;

.

Let

be a prime power and
\mathbb{F}_{q}
a finite field with cardinality

Consider the function field

Pure gaps at several totally ramified places over Kummer extensions have been extensively studied in the literature.
However, the majority of the results were based on Kummer extensions defined by (

For example, Castellanos, Masuda and Quoos

In

Bartoli, Quoos and Zini
Bartoli

Recently, pure gaps over Kummer extensions defined by (
1
), where not all the multiplicities
\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{r}

In
Bartoli

In

However, these studies only foucsed on investigating pure gaps at two ramified places over Kummer extensions.

Explicitly describing and identifying the set of pure gaps at more than two places is a complex task.
In
Castellanos
et al. 2024
and
S. Castellanos
et al. 2024

For Kummer extensions defined by (
1

\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{r}
are equal, they provided an explicit description of the set of pure gaps at several totally ramified places distinct to the infinite place.
However, an explicit description of the set of pure gaps at several totally ramified places including the infinite place remains unexplored in the literature.

In this work, we focus on investigating pure gaps at many totally ramified places over Kummer extensions defined by (
1
), where not all
\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{r}
are equal.
Inspired by
(
Bartoli
et al. 2018

, we propose a new characterization for identifying pure gaps at many totally ramified places.
Then we present a straightforward and efficient method to find all pure gaps.
Our approach differs from the methods mentioned in the preceding literature.
Firstly, we introduce a bottom set of pure gaps, which has the ability to determine the set of pure gaps (see Definition
2.6
and Theorem
3.1
).
Subsequently, we propose that the bottom set of pure gaps at
s+1
totally ramified places can be efficiently deduced from that of pure gaps at
s
totally ramified places (see Algorithm

Consequently, by leveraging the principle of induction and the bottom set of pure gaps, we are able to identify all pure gaps at many totally ramified places (see Algorithm
3

Furthermore, we indicate that a pure gap can be deduced from a known pure gap by easily verifying only one inequality (see Theorem
3.6

In particular, for Kummer extensions defined by (
1
), where all the multiplicities
\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{r}
are equal,
we present a novel description of the set of gaps at some totally ramified place (see Proposition
4.5

With this new characterization, we fully determine an explicit description of the set of pure gaps at many totally ramified places,
including the scenario where the set of these places contains the infinite place (see Propositons

4.2
and
4.3

These extend the results in
S. Castellanos
et al. 2024

Consecutive pure gaps play a crucial role in improving the minimum distance when constructing multi-point algebraic geometry codes.
Based on our methods, we easily obtain several families of consecutive pure gaps at many totally ramified places (see Propositons

By applying these consecutive pure gaps, we construct multi-point AG codes with good parameters, which outperform the parameters provided in MinT’s Tables

For a function

, denote by

the principal divisor of

For a divisor

Let

over
K
.

Let

F
with genus

are distinct rational places.
Consider another divisor
G
of

{\rm supp}(D)\cap{\rm supp}(G)=\varnothing

The differential algebraic geometry code
C_{\Omega}(D,G)

Then

For more information regarding algebraic geometry codes, the reader is referred to

Let

be the set of non-negative integers.
For distinct rational places

H(Q_{1},\cdots,Q_{s})=\{(a_{1},\cdots,a_{s})\in\mathbb{N}^{s}~|~(z)_{\infty}=a_{1}Q_{1}+\cdots+a_{s}Q_{s}\text{ for some }z\in F\}.

The dimension of Riemann - Roch vector spaces can be utilized to characterize gaps, as presented in
Carvalho and
Torres 2005

An

is a gap at

\ell\left(\sum_{i=1}^{s}a_{i}Q_{i}\right)=\ell\left(\sum_{i=1}^{s}a_{i}Q_{i}-Q_{j}\right)\text{ for some }1\leq j\leq s.

Subsequently, we introduce the concept of pure gaps, which will be applied in the construction of differential algebraic geometry codes.
An
s
-tuple

if and only if

\ell\left(\sum_{i=1}^{s}a_{i}Q_{i}\right)=\ell\left(\sum_{i=1}^{s}a_{i}Q_{i}-Q_{j}\right)\text{ for all }1\leq j\leq s.

The set of pure gaps at

is denoted as
G_{0}(Q_{1},\cdots,Q_{s})
.
When

, it is obvious that

. Moreover, the gap set

contains
g

\mathbf{a}=(a_{0},a_{1},\cdots,a_{s})\in\overline{G}_{0}(Q_{j_{0}},Q_{j_{1}},\cdots,Q_{j_{s}})
, define

k_{\mathbf{a}}=-1-\max_{0\leq v\leq s}\left\{\sum_{i=0}^{s}\left\lfloor\frac{a_{i}-a_{v}\sigma_{j_{v}}\lambda_{j_{i}}}{m}\right\rfloor-\sum_{i=s+1}^{r}\left\lfloor\frac{-a_{v}\sigma_{j_{v}}\lambda_{j_{i}}}{m}\right\rfloor\right\}.

Then

\displaystyle G_{0}(Q_{j_{0}},Q_{j_{1}},\cdots,Q_{j_{s}})=\Big\{(mk_{0}+a_{0},mk_{1}+a_{1},\cdots,mk_{s}+a_{s})~\Big|

\displaystyle\mathbf{a}=(a_{0},a_{1},\cdots,a_{s})\in\overline{G}_{0}(Q_{j_{0}},Q_{j_{1}},\cdots,Q_{j_{s}}),~k_{i}\geq 0\text{ for }0\leq i\leq s,\text{ and }\sum_{i=0}^{s}k_{i}\leq k_{\mathbf{a}}\Big\}.

In order to prove Theorem

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose that

for each

\sum_{i=0}^{s}\left\lfloor\frac{a_{i}-a_{k}\sigma_{j_{k}}\lambda_{j_{i}}}{m}\right\rfloor+\sum_{i=s+1}^{r}\left\lfloor\frac{-a_{k}\sigma_{j_{k}}\lambda_{j_{i}}}{m}\right\rfloor\leq-1.

Proof.

For each

, note that

if and only if

.
Assume that
(a_{0},a_{1},\cdots,a_{s})
is a pure gap at

.
Then, for each

, exactly one of two conditions in Theorem

is satisfied.
Thus for

, it follows from Lemma

that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{s}\left\lfloor\frac{a_{i}-a_{k}\sigma_{j_{k}}\lambda_{j_{i}}}{m}\right\rfloor+\sum_{i=s+1}^{r}\left\lfloor\frac{-a_{k}\sigma_{j_{k}}\lambda_{j_{i}}}{m}\right\rfloor=\sum_{i=0}^{s}\left\lfloor\frac{a_{i}+t\lambda_{j_{i}}}{m}\right\rfloor+\sum_{i=s+1}^{r}\left\lfloor\frac{t\lambda_{j_{i}}}{m}\right\rfloor\leq-1.

Now assume that

\sum_{i=0}^{s}\left\lfloor\frac{a_{i}-a_{k}\sigma_{j_{k}}\lambda_{j_{i}}}{m}\right\rfloor+\sum_{i=s+1}^{r}\left\lfloor\frac{-a_{k}\sigma_{j_{k}}\lambda_{j_{i}}}{m}\right\rfloor\leq-1\text{ for all }0\leq k\leq s.

Then, for
t\in\{t~|~t\equiv-a_{k}\sigma_{j_{k}}\pmod{m},~0\leq k\leq s\}\cap\{0,1,\cdots,m-1\}
, by Lemma
2.4
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{s}\left\lfloor\frac{a_{i}+t\lambda_{j_{i}}}{m}\right\rfloor+\sum_{i=s+1}^{r}\left\lfloor\frac{t\lambda_{j_{i}}}{m}\right\rfloor=\sum_{i=0}^{s}\left\lfloor\frac{a_{i}-a_{k}\sigma_{j_{k}}\lambda_{j_{i}}}{m}\right\rfloor+\sum_{i=s+1}^{r}\left\lfloor\frac{-a_{k}\sigma_{j_{k}}\lambda_{j_{i}}}{m}\right\rfloor\leq-1.

For
t\in\{t~|~t\not\equiv-a_{k}\sigma_{j_{k}}\pmod{m},~0\leq k\leq s\}\cap\{0,1,\cdots,m-1\}
, either
\sum_{i=0}^{s}\left\lfloor\frac{a_{i}+t\lambda_{j_{i}}}{m}\right\rfloor+\sum_{i=s+1}^{r}\left\lfloor\frac{t\lambda_{j_{i}}}{m}\right\rfloor<0
or

\left\lfloor\frac{a_{i}+t\lambda_{j_{i}}}{m}\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor\frac{a_{i}+t\lambda_{j_{i}}-1}{m}\right\rfloor
for all
0\leq i\leq s
.
Thus, by Theorem
2.7
, we have
(a_{0},a_{1},\cdots,a_{s})
is a pure gap at
Q_{j_{0}},Q_{j_{1}},\cdots,Q_{j_{s}}
.
∎

Proposition 3.3

Proof.

According to Lemma
3.2
, we have
a

Q_{s}
if and only if

a
is a gap at
Q_{s}
if and only if
\sum_{i=0}^{r}\left\lfloor\frac{-a\lambda\lambda_{i}}{m}\right\rfloor+\left\lceil\frac{a}{m}\right\rceil\leq-1
.

4
Pure gaps from special Kummer extensions

Throughout this section, we consider a special Kummer extension
F=K(x,y)/K(x)
defined by the affine equation

y^{m}=f(x)^{\lambda}=\prod_{i=1}^{r}(x-\alpha_{i})^{\lambda},

(7)

where
\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
,
\gcd(r\lambda,m)=1
, and
\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{r}\in K
are pairwise distinct elements. The function field
F/K
has genus
g=(r-1)(m-1)/2
.

For each
1\leq i\leq r
, let
Q_{i}
and
Q_{\infty}
denote the places in
\mathbf{P}(F)
corresponding to the zero of
x-\alpha_{i}
and the pole of
x
, respectively.
It is obvious that
Q_{1},\cdots,Q_{r}
and
Q_{\infty}
all are rational and totally ramified.
An explicit description of
G_{0}(Q_{1},\cdots,Q_{s})
has been provided by Castellanos et al. in
(
S. Castellanos
et al. 2024
, Theorem 5.12)
.
However, an explicit description of
G_{0}(Q_{\infty},Q_{1},\cdots,Q_{s})
has not been provided in the literature thus far.

In this section, applying Theorem
3.1
and Theorem
3.6
in the previous section, we aim to establish explicit descriptions of
G_{0}(Q_{1},\cdots,Q_{s})
and
G_{0}(Q_{\infty},Q_{1},\cdots,Q_{s})
for
2\leq s\leq r
.
Our description of
G_{0}(Q_{1},\cdots,Q_{s})
aligns with the one presented in
(
S. Castellanos
et al. 2024
, Theorem 5.12)
.
In the case
mv=r+1
for some
v\geq 1
, our description of
G_{0}(Q_{\infty},Q_{1},\cdots,Q_{s})
bears a resemblance to that of
G_{0}(Q_{1},\cdots,Q_{s})
.
Our descriptions can be formed as the union of sets of consecutive pure gaps, enabling us to easily obtain families of consecutive pure gaps.
Specifically, our main results are as follows.

Proposition 4.1
.

Suppose that
2\leq s\leq r
.
If
2\leq s\leq r-\left\lfloor\frac{r}{m}\right\rfloor-1
, for each
0\leq k\leq r-\left\lfloor\frac{r}{m}\right\rfloor-1-s

t_{i,k}=m-\left\lceil\frac{m(k+i)}{r}\right\rceil
for
1\leq i\leq s

\displaystyle G_{0}(Q_{1},\cdots,Q_{s})=\bigcup\limits_{k=0}^{r-\left\lfloor\frac{r}{m}\right\rfloor-1-s}\Big\{(mk_{1}+j_{1},\cdots,mk_{s}+j_{s})~\Big|~k_{i}\geq 0\text{ for }1\leq i\leq s,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{s}k_{i}=k,~1\leq j_{\sigma(i)}\leq t_{i,k}\text{ for }1\leq i\leq s,~\sigma\in S_{s}\Big\},

where
S_{s}
is the set of permutations of the set
\{1,\cdots,s\}
.

If
r-\left\lfloor\frac{r}{m}\right\rfloor\leq s\leq r
, then
G_{0}(Q_{1},\cdots,Q_{s})=\varnothing
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Suppose that
mv=r+1
for some

and
1\leq s\leq r
.

1\leq s\leq r-v-1

\displaystyle G_{0}(Q_{\infty},Q_{1},\cdots,Q_{s})=\bigcup\limits_{k=0}^{r-v-1-s}\Big\{(mk_{0}+j_{0},mk_{1}+j_{1},\cdots,mk_{s}+j_{s})~\Big|

\displaystyle k_{i}\geq 0

where
S_{s+1}
is the set of permutations of the set
\{0,1,\cdots,s\}
.

If
r-v\leq s\leq r
, then
G_{0}(Q_{\infty},Q_{1},\cdots,Q_{s})=\varnothing
.

Proposition 4.3
.

Suppose that
1\leq s\leq r
.
If
1\leq s\leq r-\left\lfloor\frac{r}{m}\right\rfloor-1
,

\displaystyle G_{0}(Q_{\infty},Q_{1},\cdots,Q_{s})=\bigcup_{j_{0}=1}^{c}\bigcup_{k=\left\lceil\frac{rj_{0}}{m}\right\rceil}^{d_{j_{0}}}\Big\{(mk_{0}-rj_{0},mk_{1}+j_{1},\cdots,mk_{s}+j_{s})~\Big|~k_{0}\geq\left\lceil\frac{rj_{0}}{m}\right\rceil,

\displaystyle~k_{i}\geq 0\text{ for }1\leq i\leq s,~\sum_{i=0}^{s}k_{i}=k,~1\leq j_{\sigma(i)}\leq t_{j_{0},i,k}\text{ for }1\leq i\leq s,~\sigma\in S_{s}\Big\},

where
S_{s}
is the set of permutations of the set
\{1,\cdots,s\}
.

If
r-\left\lfloor\frac{r}{m}\right\rfloor\leq s\leq r
, then
G_{0}(Q_{\infty},Q_{1},\cdots,Q_{s})=\varnothing
.

We will divide into three subsections to prove each of the above three propositions. Initially, we present a lemma.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
k
be an integer such that
1\leq k\leq 2r-1
and let
j
be an integer such that
1-m\leq j\leq m-1
.
Then

\left\lfloor\frac{rj}{m}\right\rfloor\leq r-k-1\text{ if and only if }j\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}m-\left\lceil\frac{km}{r}\right\rceil,&\text{if}~k\neq r,\\
m-\left\lceil\frac{km}{r}\right\rceil-1,&\text{if}~k=r.\end{array}\right.

Furthermore suppose that
mv=r+1
for some
v\geq 1
,
1\leq k\leq r-1
, and
1\leq j\leq m-1
.
Then

j\leq m-\left\lceil\frac{km}{r}\right\rceil\text{ if and only if }vj\leq r-k.

Proof.

First, assume that
k\neq r
.
Suppose that
j\neq 0
. Since
1-m\leq j\leq m-1
and
\gcd(m,r)=1

m\nmid rj
.
If
\left\lfloor\frac{rj}{m}\right\rfloor\leq r-k-1

\frac{rj}{m}<r-k

This implies that
j<m-\frac{km}{r}
, and thus
j\leq\left\lfloor m-\frac{km}{r}\right\rfloor=m-\left\lceil\frac{km}{r}\right\rceil
.
If
j\leq m-\left\lceil\frac{km}{r}\right\rceil
, then
j\leq m-\left\lceil\frac{km}{r}\right\rceil=m+\left\lfloor\frac{-km}{r}\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor\frac{(r-k)m}{r}\right\rfloor
.
This implies that
rj\leq\left\lfloor\frac{(r-k)m}{r}\right\rfloor r\leq(r-k)m
, and thus
\frac{rj}{m}\leq r-k
.
Since
m\nmid rj
, it follows that
\frac{rj}{m}<r-k
, and hence
\left\lfloor\frac{rj}{m}\right\rfloor\leq r-k-1
.

Now, suppose that
j=0
. We need to prove that
0\leq r-k-1
if and only if
0\leq m-\left\lceil\frac{km}{r}\right\rceil
.
If
k\leq r-1
, then
m-\left\lceil\frac{km}{r}\right\rceil\geq m-\left\lceil\frac{(r-1)m}{r}\right\rceil\geq 0
.
If
0\leq m-\left\lceil\frac{km}{r}\right\rceil=\left\lfloor\frac{m(r-k)}{r}\right\rfloor

.

.

Next, assume that
k=r
. It is obvious that
\left\lfloor\frac{rj}{m}\right\rfloor\leq-1=r-k-1
if and only if
j\leq-1=m-\left\lceil\frac{km}{r}\right\rceil-1
.

Furthermore suppose that
mv=r+1
for some
v\geq 1
,
1\leq k\leq r-1
, and
1\leq j\leq m-1
.
Then
\left\lfloor\frac{rj}{m}\right\rfloor=vj+\left\lfloor\frac{-j}{m}\right\rfloor=vj-1
and
k\neq r
.
It follows that
j\leq m-\left\lceil\frac{km}{r}\right\rceil
if and only if
vj\leq r-k
.
∎

For each
1\leq i\leq r
, since
g=\#G(Q_{i})
, by Lemma
2.5
, we obtain that

\frac{(r-1)(m-1)}{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{m-1-\left\lfloor\frac{m}{r}\right\rfloor}\left(r-1-\left\lfloor\frac{rj}{m}\right\rfloor\right).

(8)

Using this equation and Lemma

, we present a novel characterization of

for
1\leq i\leq r
and

For each

by Proposition

Furthermore, in the case where

In the case where

4.1

Using the description of

for

presented in Proposition

.

Suppose that
2\leq s\leq r-\left\lfloor\frac{r}{m}\right\rfloor-1

0\leq k\leq r-\left\lfloor\frac{r}{m}\right\rfloor-1-s

For each
1\leq i\leq s
, by Lemma
4.4
, we have

j_{i}\leq m-\left\lceil\frac{(k+i)m}{r}\right\rceil\text{ if and only if }\left\lfloor\frac{rj_{i}}{m}\right\rfloor\leq r-(k+i)-1.

(9)

We set
G=\{(mk+j_{1},j_{2},\cdots,j_{s})~|~1\leq j_{i}\leq t_{i}\text{ for }1\leq i\leq s\}

For any
(mk+j_{1},j_{2},\cdots,j_{s})\in G
and
\sigma\in S
, by Proposition
4.5
,
it is obvious that

.
We will show that

\lambda^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}

\displaystyle~\left\lfloor\frac{mk+j_{1}-j_{2}\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}{m}\right\rfloor+\left\lfloor\frac{j_{2}-j_{2}\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}{m}\right\rfloor+\sum_{i=3}^{r}\left\lfloor\frac{-j_{2}\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}{m}\right\rfloor+\left\lfloor\frac{j_{2}r\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}{m}\right\rfloor

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~k+\sum_{i=1}^{2}\left\lfloor\frac{j_{i}-j_{2}}{m}\right\rfloor+\sum_{i=3}^{r}\left\lfloor\frac{-j_{2}}{m}\right\rfloor+\left\lfloor\frac{rj_{2}}{m}\right\rfloor\leq\left\lfloor\frac{rj_{2}}{m}\right\rfloor-r+2+k.

\left\lfloor\frac{rj_{2}}{m}\right\rfloor\leq r-(k+2)-1
, and then
\left\lfloor\frac{rj_{2}}{m}\right\rfloor-r+2+k\leq-1
.
Therefore, by Theorem

, we have
(mk+j_{1},j_{2})\in G_{0}(Q_{\sigma(1)},Q_{\sigma(2)})

Assume that
(mk+j_{1},j_{2},\cdots,j_{l-1})\in G_{0}(Q_{\sigma(1)},Q_{\sigma(2)},\cdots,Q_{\sigma(l-1)})

\displaystyle~\left\lfloor\frac{mk+j_{1}-j_{l}\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}{m}\right\rfloor+\sum_{i=2}^{l}\left\lfloor\frac{j_{i}-j_{l}\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}{m}\right\rfloor+\sum_{i=l+1}^{r}\left\lfloor\frac{-j_{l}\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}{m}\right\rfloor+\left\lfloor\frac{j_{l}r\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}{m}\right\rfloor

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~k+\sum_{i=1}^{l}\left\lfloor\frac{j_{i}-j_{l}}{m}\right\rfloor+\sum_{i=l+1}^{r}\left\lfloor\frac{-j_{l}}{m}\right\rfloor+\left\lfloor\frac{rj_{l}}{m}\right\rfloor\leq\left\lfloor\frac{rj_{l}}{m}\right\rfloor-r+l+k.

It follows from (
9
) that

, and then
\left\lfloor\frac{rj_{l}}{m}\right\rfloor-r+l+k\leq-1
.
Therefore, by Theorem

, we have

By induction, we obtain that
(mk+j_{1},j_{2},\cdots,j_{s})\in G_{0}(Q_{\sigma(1)},Q_{\sigma(2)},\cdots,Q_{\sigma(s)})
.
Thus we have
G\subseteq G_{0}(Q_{\sigma(1)},Q_{\sigma(2)},\cdots,Q_{\sigma(s)})
for all
\sigma\in S_{s}
. ∎

.

Assume that
(mk-rj_{0},j_{1},\cdots,j_{l-1})\in G_{0}(Q_{\infty},Q_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,Q_{\sigma(l-1)})
for some
2\leq l\leq s
.
For each
1\leq j_{l}\leq t_{l}
, by Lemma
2.4
, we have

\displaystyle\left\lfloor\frac{mk-rj_{0}+j_{l}r\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}{m}\right\rfloor+\sum_{i=1}^{l}\left\lfloor\frac{j_{i}-j_{l}\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}{m}\right\rfloor+\sum_{i=l+1}^{r}\left\lfloor\frac{-j_{l}\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}{m}\right\rfloor

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle k+\left\lfloor\frac{(j_{l}-j_{0})r}{m}\right\rfloor+\sum_{i=1}^{l}\left\lfloor\frac{j_{i}-j_{l}}{m}\right\rfloor+\sum_{i=l+1}^{r}\left\lfloor\frac{-j_{l}}{m}\right\rfloor\leq k+\left\lfloor\frac{(j_{l}-j_{0})r}{m}\right\rfloor-r+l.

It follows from (
13
) that
\left\lfloor\frac{(j_{l}-j_{0})r}{m}\right\rfloor\leq r-k-l-1
, and then
k+\left\lfloor\frac{(j_{l}-j_{0})r}{m}\right\rfloor-r+l\leq-1
.
Therefore, by Theorem
3.6
, we have

(mk-rj_{0},j_{1},\cdots,j_{l})\in G_{0}(Q_{\infty},Q_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,Q_{\sigma(l)}).

By induction, we obtain that
(mk-rj_{0},j_{1},\cdots,j_{s})\in G_{0}(Q_{\infty},Q_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,Q_{\sigma(s)})
.
Thus we have
G\subseteq G_{0}(Q_{\infty},Q_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,Q_{\sigma(s)})
.
∎

Based on the above proposition, now we finish the proof of Proposition
4.3
as follows.

Proof of Proposition

.

Let
1\leq j_{0}\leq c
. We define

A_{j_{0}}=\left\{(mk_{0}-rj_{0},a_{1},\cdots,a_{s})\in G_{0}(Q_{\infty},Q_{1},\cdots,Q_{s})~\Big|~\left\lceil\frac{rj_{0}}{m}\right\rceil\leq k_{0}\leq r-1\right\}.

, for each
\left\lceil\frac{rj_{0}}{m}\right\rceil\leq k\leq r-1

and
\hat{G}_{k}=\{(mk-rj_{0},j_{1},\cdots,j_{s})~|~1\leq j_{\sigma(i)}\leq t_{j_{0},i,k}\text{ for }1\leq i\leq s,~\sigma\in S_{s}\}
.
It is sufficient to show that

\displaystyle A_{j_{0}}=\bigcup_{k=\left\lceil\frac{rj_{0}}{m}\right\rceil}^{d_{j_{0}}}G_{k}.

It follows from Lemma
2.5
that
mk_{0}-rj_{0}\in G(Q_{\infty})
for
\left\lceil\frac{rj_{0}}{m}\right\rceil\leq k_{0}\leq d_{j_{0}}
. According to Proposition
4.10
, we have
\hat{G}_{k}\subseteq G_{0}(Q_{\infty},Q_{1},\cdots,Q_{s})
.
By Theorem
3.1
, we have
G_{k}\subseteq A_{j_{0}}
for
\left\lceil\frac{rj_{0}}{m}\right\rceil\leq k\leq d_{j_{0}}
.
Next we will show that

A_{j_{0}}\subseteq\bigcup\limits_{k=\left\lceil\frac{rj_{0}}{m}\right\rceil}^{d_{j_{0}}}G_{k}.

For each
1\leq i\leq s
and
\left\lceil\frac{rj_{0}}{m}\right\rceil\leq k\leq d_{j_{0}}
, it is obvious that
1-m\leq 1-j_{0}\leq j_{\sigma(i)}-j_{0}\leq m-\left\lceil\frac{(k+i)m}{r}\right\rceil\leq m-1
.
Then by Lemma
4.4
, we have

\displaystyle\left\lfloor\frac{(j_{\sigma(i)}-j_{0})r}{m}\right\rfloor\leq r-(k+i)-1\text{ if and only if }j_{\sigma(i)}-j_{0}\leq t_{j_{0},i,k}-j_{0}.

(14)

For any
(mk_{0}-rj_{0},a_{1},\cdots,a_{s})\in A_{j_{0}}
,
by Propositions
2.1
and
4.5
, we can write
a_{i}=mk_{i}+j_{i}
with
0\leq k_{i}\leq r-2-\left\lfloor\frac{r}{m}\right\rfloor
and
1\leq j_{i}\leq m-\left\lceil\frac{(k_{i}+1)m}{r}\right\rceil
for
1\leq i\leq s
.
There exists some
\sigma\in S
such that
j_{\sigma(1)}\geq j_{\sigma(2)}\geq\cdots\geq j_{\sigma(s)}
.
Let
\lambda^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}
be the inverse of
\lambda
modulo
m
and let
r^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}
be the inverse of
-r\lambda
modulo
m
.

For each
1\leq l\leq s
, by Lemma
2.4
, we have

\displaystyle~\left\lfloor\frac{mk_{0}-rj_{0}+a_{\sigma(l)}r\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}{m}\right\rfloor+\sum_{i=1}^{s}\left\lfloor\frac{a_{\sigma(i)}-a_{\sigma(l)}\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}{m}\right\rfloor+\sum_{i=s+1}^{r}\left\lfloor\frac{-a_{\sigma(l)}\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}{m}\right\rfloor

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~k_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{s}k_{\sigma(i)}+\left\lfloor\frac{(j_{\sigma(l)}-j_{0})r}{m}\right\rfloor+\sum_{i=1}^{s}\left\lfloor\frac{j_{\sigma(i)}-j_{\sigma(l)}}{m}\right\rfloor+\sum_{i=s+1}^{r}\left\lfloor\frac{-j_{\sigma(i)}}{m}\right\rfloor

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~k+\left\lfloor\frac{(j_{\sigma(l)}-j_{0})r}{m}\right\rfloor+\sum_{i=l+1}^{s}\left\lfloor\frac{j_{\sigma(i)}-j_{\sigma(l)}}{m}\right\rfloor-r+s.

Let
k=\sum_{i=0}^{s}k_{i}
. When

, by Lemma
3.2
, we must have

.

\displaystyle s\leq r-k-\left\lfloor\frac{(1-j_{0})r}{m}\right\rfloor-1

\displaystyle\leq r-\left\lceil\frac{rj_{0}}{m}\right\rceil+\left\lceil\frac{r(j_{0}-1)}{m}\right\rceil-1

\displaystyle=r-\left(\left\lfloor\frac{rj_{0}}{m}\right\rfloor-\left\lfloor\frac{r(j_{0}-1)}{m}\right\rfloor\right)-1.

2.3
, we obtain that

, we have

which is equivalent to

\left\lfloor\frac{(j_{\sigma(l)}-j_{0})r}{m}\right\rfloor\leq r-k-s-1-\sum_{i=l+1}^{s}\left\lfloor\frac{j_{\sigma(i)}-j_{\sigma(l)}}{m}\right\rfloor.

) that
(mk_{0}-rj_{0},a_{1},\cdots,a_{s})\in G_{k}
, and then

The proof is complete.

Corollary 4.11
.

Suppose that
1\leq s\leq r
.
If
1\leq s\leq r-\left\lfloor\frac{r}{m}\right\rfloor-1
,
for each
1\leq j_{0}\leq r-1-\left\lfloor\frac{m}{r}\right\rfloor
, let

m-\left\lceil\frac{\left(\left\lceil\frac{rj_{0}}{m}\right\rceil+i\right)m}{r}\right\rceil+j_{0}-1,&\text{if}~\left\lceil\frac{rj_{0}}{m}\right\rceil+i=r,\\
\end{array}\right.

for all
1\leq i\leq s
, and

G_{j_{0}}=\{(m\left\lceil\frac{rj_{0}}{m}\right\rceil-rj_{0},j_{1},\cdots,j_{s})~|~1\leq j_{\sigma(i)}\leq t_{j_{0},i}\text{ for }1\leq i\leq s,~\sigma\in S_{s}\}
,
where

is the set of permutations of the set

.
Then

\displaystyle\overline{G}_{0}(Q_{\infty},Q_{1},\cdots,Q_{s})=\bigcup_{j_{0}=1}^{m-1-\left\lfloor\frac{m}{r}\right\rfloor}G_{j_{0}}.

If

, then
\overline{G}_{0}(Q_{\infty},Q_{1},\cdots,Q_{s})=\varnothing
.

Proof.

If
1\leq s\leq r-\left\lfloor\frac{r}{m}\right\rfloor-1
,
for each
1\leq j_{0}\leq m-1-\left\lceil\frac{m}{r}\right\rceil
and

, let

m-\left\lceil\frac{(k+i)m}{r}\right\rceil+j_{0}-1,&\text{if}~k+i=r,\\
\end{array}\right.

for all
1\leq i\leq s
, and
G_{j_{0},k}=\{(m\left\lceil\frac{rj_{0}}{m}\right\rceil-rj_{0},j_{1},\cdots,j_{s})~|~1\leq j_{\sigma(i)}\leq t_{j_{0},i,k}\text{ for }1\leq i\leq s,~\sigma\in S_{s}\}
.

4.3
, we have

\displaystyle\overline{G}_{0}(Q_{\infty},Q_{1},\cdots,Q_{s})

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{j_{0}=1}^{m-1-\left\lceil\frac{m}{r}\right\rceil}\bigcup_{k=\left\lceil\frac{rj_{0}}{m}\right\rceil}^{r-\left\lfloor\frac{(1-j_{0})r}{m}\right\rfloor-s-1}G_{j_{0},k}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{j_{0}=1}^{m-1-\left\lfloor\frac{m}{r}\right\rfloor}G_{j_{0},\left\lceil\frac{rj_{0}}{m}\right\rceil}=\bigcup_{j_{0}=1}^{m-1-\left\lfloor\frac{m}{r}\right\rfloor}G_{j_{0}}.

If
r-\left\lfloor\frac{r}{m}\right\rfloor\leq s\leq r
, then
\overline{G}_{0}(Q_{\infty},Q_{1},\cdots,Q_{s})=\varnothing
since

.
∎

Proposition 4.12
.

2.5

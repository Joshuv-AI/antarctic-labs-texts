Some identities on degenerate trigonometric functions
Taekyun Kim, Dae San Kim

Abstract
. In this paper, we study several degenerate trigonometric functions, which are degenerate versions of the ordinary trigonometric functions, and derive some identities among such functions by using elementary methods. Especially, we obtain multiple angle formulas for the degenerate cotangent and degenerate sine functions.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
11B83

1.
Introduction

For any nonzero
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, the degenerate exponentials are defined by

e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(x)_{n,\lambda}\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [4,5]),

where

(x)_{0,\lambda}=1,\ (x)_{n,\lambda}=x(x-\lambda)(x-2\lambda)\cdots\big(x-(n-1)\lambda\big),\ (n\geq 1).

Note that
\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=e^{xt}\displaystyle
and
e_{\lambda}(t)=e_{\lambda}^{1}(t)
. In [4], the degenerate hyperbolic functions are introduced by

\displaystyle\cosh_{\lambda}\big(x:a\big)=\frac{1}{2}\big(e_{\lambda}^{x}(a)+e_{\lambda}^{-x}(a)\big),\ \sinh_{\lambda}\big(x:a\big)=\frac{1}{2}\big(e_{\lambda}^{x}(a)-e_{\lambda}^{-x}(a)\big),

\displaystyle\tanh_{\lambda}\big(x:a\big)=\frac{\sinh_{\lambda}(x:a)}{\cosh_{\lambda}(x:a)},\quad\coth_{\lambda}(x:a)=\frac{\cosh_{\lambda}(x:a)}{\sinh_{\lambda}(x:a)},\ (x\neq 0,\ a\neq 0).

It is well known that

(1)

\cos a=\frac{e^{ia}+e^{-ia}}{2},\quad\sin a=\frac{e^{ia}-e^{-ia}}{2i},

where
i=\sqrt{-1}
, (see [1,2,3]).

In light of (
1
), we consider the degenerate cosine and degenerate sine functions which are respectively given by

(2)

\cos_{\lambda}\big(x:a\big)=\frac{e_{\lambda}^{xi}(a)+e_{\lambda}^{-xi}(a)}{2},\quad\sin_{\lambda}\big(x:a\big)=\frac{e_{\lambda}^{xi}(a)-e_{\lambda}^{-xi}(a)}{2i}.

Note that

\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}\cos_{\lambda}\big(x:a\big)=\cos ax,\quad\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}\sin_{\lambda}\big(x:a\big)=\sin ax.

In addition, we define the degenerate tangent and degenerate cotangent functions as follows:

(3)

\tan_{\lambda}\big(x:a\big)=\frac{\sin_{\lambda}(x:a)}{\cos_{\lambda}(x:a)},\quad\cot_{\lambda}\big(x:a\big)=\frac{\cos_{\lambda}(x:a)}{\sin_{\lambda}(x:a)},

where
x\neq 0
and
a\neq 0
.

Recently, degenerate versions of many special numbers and polynomials and some transcendental functions have been investigated. The aim of this paper is to study several degenerate trigonometric functions, namely the degenerate sine, degenerate cosine, degenerate tangent and degenerate cotangent functions. We derive some identities on those degenerate trigonometric functions by using elementary methods. In particular, we show the following multiple angle fomulas for the degenerate cotangent and degenerate sine functions (see (
11
), (
15
), (
27
)):

\displaystyle 2m\cot_{\lambda}\big(2mt:a\big)=-\sum_{j=0}^{2m-1}\tan_{\lambda}\bigg(t+\frac{j\pi}{2m\log e_{\lambda}(a)}:a\bigg),

\displaystyle 2m\cot_{\lambda}\big(2mt:a\big)=\sum_{j=0}^{2m-1}\cot_{\lambda}\Big(t+\frac{j\pi}{2m\log e_{\lambda}(a)}:a\Big),

\displaystyle\sin_{\lambda}\big((2m+1)x:a\big)=(2m+1)\sin_{\lambda}\big(x:a\big)\prod_{k=1}^{m}\Bigg(1-\cfrac{\sin_{\lambda}^{2}(x:a)}{\sin^{2}\Big(\frac{k\pi}{2m+1}\Big)}\Bigg).

2.
Some identities on degenerate trigonometric functions

From (
2
), we note that

(4)

\displaystyle\sin_{\lambda}^{2}\big(x:a\big)+\cos_{\lambda}^{2}\big(x:a\big)

\displaystyle=\bigg(\frac{e_{\lambda}^{xi}(a)+e_{\lambda}^{-xi}(a)}{2}\bigg)^{2}+\bigg(\frac{e_{\lambda}^{xi}(a)-e_{\lambda}^{-xi}(a)}{2i}\bigg)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{4}=1.

We observe that

(5)

\displaystyle\cos_{\lambda}\big(2x:a\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{e_{\lambda}^{2xi}(a)+e_{\lambda}^{-2xi}(a)}{2}=1+\frac{e_{\lambda}^{2xi}(a)+e_{\lambda}^{-2xi}(a)-2}{2}

\displaystyle=1-2\bigg(\frac{e_{\lambda}^{xi}(a)-e_{\lambda}^{-xi}(a)}{2i}\bigg)^{2}=1-2\sin_{\lambda}^{2}\big(x:a\big).

By (
4
) and (
5
), we get

\cos_{\lambda}\big(2x:a\big)=1-2\sin_{\lambda}^{2}(x:a)=2\cos_{\lambda}^{2}(x:a)-1.

Moreover, by (
2
), we get

\displaystyle\sin_{\lambda}\big(2x:a\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{e_{\lambda}^{2xi}(a)-e_{\lambda}^{-2xi}(a)}{2i}=2\frac{e_{\lambda}^{xi}(a)-e_{\lambda}^{-ix}(a)}{2i}\frac{e_{\lambda}^{xi}(a)+e_{\lambda}^{-xi}(a)}{2}

\displaystyle=2\sin_{\lambda}\big(x:a\big)\cos_{\lambda}\big(x:a\big).

Theorem 2.1
.

For
a\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\cos_{\lambda}\big(2x:a\big)=1-2\sin_{\lambda}^{2}\big(x:a\big)=2\cos_{\lambda}^{2}\big(x:a\big)-1,

and

\sin_{\lambda}\big(2x:a\big)=2\sin_{\lambda}\big(x:a\big)\cos_{\lambda}\big(x:a\big).

\displaystyle\sin_{\lambda}\big(x+y:a\big)=\frac{1}{2i}\Big(e_{\lambda}^{(x+y)i}-e_{\lambda}^{-(x+y)i}(a)\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{e_{\lambda}^{xi}(a)-e_{\lambda}^{-xi}(a)}{2i}\frac{e_{\lambda}^{yi}(a)+e_{\lambda}^{-yi}(a)}{2}+\frac{e_{\lambda}^{xi}(a)+e_{\lambda}^{-xi}(a)}{2}\frac{e_{\lambda}^{yi}(a)-e_{\lambda}^{-yi}(a)}{2i}

\displaystyle=\sin_{\lambda}\big(x:a\big)\cos_{\lambda}\big(y:a\big)+\cos_{\lambda}\big(x:a\big)\sin_{\lambda}\big(y:a\big),

and

\displaystyle\cos_{\lambda}\big(x+y:a\big)=\frac{e_{\lambda}^{(x+y)i}(a)+e_{\lambda}^{-(x+y)i}(a)}{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{e_{\lambda}^{xi}(a)+e_{\lambda}^{-xi}(a)}{2}\frac{e_{\lambda}^{yi}(a)+e_{\lambda}^{-yi}(a)}{2}-\frac{e_{\lambda}^{xi}(a)-e_{\lambda}^{-xi}}{2i}\frac{e_{\lambda}^{yi}(a)-e_{\lambda}^{-yi}(a)}{2i}

\displaystyle=\cos_{\lambda}\big(x:a\big)\cos_{\lambda}\big(y:a\big)-\sin_{\lambda}\big(x:a\big)\sin_{\lambda}\big(y:a\big).

Therefore, we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.2
.

For
a\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\displaystyle\sin_{\lambda}\big(x\pm y:a\big)=\sin_{\lambda}\big(x:a\big)\cos_{\lambda}\big(y:a\big)\pm\cos_{\lambda}\big(x:a\big)\sin_{\lambda}\big(y:a\big),

\displaystyle\cos_{\lambda}\big(x\pm y:a\big)=\cos_{\lambda}\big(x:a\big)\cos_{\lambda}\big(y:a\big)\mp\sin_{\lambda}\big(x:a\big)\sin_{\lambda}\big(y:a\big).

We observe that

(6)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dx}\cos_{\lambda}\big(x:a\big)=-\log e_{\lambda}(a)\sin_{\lambda}(x:a),

(7)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dx}\sin_{\lambda}\big(x:a\big)=\log e_{\lambda}(a)\cos_{\lambda}(x:a).

From (
2
) and noting that
\prod_{j=0}^{2m-1}(x+e^{\frac{2\pi ij}{2m}})=x^{2m}-1
, we get

(8)

\displaystyle\prod_{j=0}^{2m-1}\cos_{\lambda}\bigg(t+\frac{j\pi}{2m\log e_{\lambda}(a)}:a\bigg)=\prod_{j=0}^{2m-1}\bigg(\frac{e_{\lambda}^{(t+\frac{j\pi}{2m\log e_{\lambda}(a)})i}(a)+e_{\lambda}^{-(t+\frac{j\pi}{2m\log e_{\lambda(a)}})i}(a)}{2}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4^{m}}\prod_{j=0}^{2m-1}\Big(e_{\lambda}^{it}(a)e_{\lambda}^{-\frac{j\pi}{2m\log e_{\lambda}(a)}i}(a)\Big(e_{\lambda}^{\frac{2j\pi}{2m\log e_{\lambda}(a)}i}(a)+e_{\lambda}^{-2it}(a)\Big)\bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4^{m}}e^{-\frac{\pi}{2m}i\frac{2m(2m-1)}{2}}e_{\lambda}^{2mti}(a)\prod_{j=0}^{2m-1}\bigg(e_{\lambda}^{-2it}(a)+e^{\frac{2\pi ij}{2m}}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4^{m}}e_{\lambda}^{2mti}(a)e^{-m\pi i}e^{\frac{\pi}{2}i}\Big(e_{\lambda}^{-4mti}(a)-1\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4^{m}}e^{-m\pi i}e^{\frac{\pi}{2}i}\Big(e_{\lambda}^{-2mti}(a)-e_{\lambda}^{2mti}(a)\Big)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4^{m}}e^{-m\pi i}e^{\frac{\pi}{2}i}2i\bigg(\frac{e_{\lambda}^{2mti}(a)-e_{\lambda}^{-2mti}(a)}{2i}\bigg)

\displaystyle=-\frac{i}{2^{2m-1}}e^{-m\pi i}e^{\frac{\pi}{2}i}\sin_{\lambda}(2mt:a).

Note that

\bigg|-\frac{i}{2^{2m-1}}e^{-m\pi i}e^{\frac{\pi}{2}i}\sin_{\lambda}\big(2mt:a\big)\bigg|=\bigg|\frac{1}{2^{2m-1}}\sin_{\lambda}\big(2mt:a\big)\bigg|.

Thus, by (
8
), we get

(9)

\log\bigg|\frac{1}{2^{2m-1}}\sin_{\lambda}\big(2mt:a\big)\bigg|=\sum_{j=0}^{2m-1}\log\bigg|\cos_{\lambda}\bigg(t+\frac{j\pi}{2m\log e_{\lambda}(a)}:a\bigg)\bigg|.

By using (
6
), we take the derivatives with respect to
t
on both sides of (
9
) and get the following identity:

(10)

\frac{2m\log(e_{\lambda}(a))\cos_{\lambda}(2mt:a)}{\sin_{\lambda}(2mt:a)}=-\sum_{j=0}^{2m-1}\log\big(e_{\lambda}(a)\big)\frac{\sin_{\lambda}\big(t+\frac{j\pi}{2m\log e_{\lambda}(a)}:a\big)}{\cos_{\lambda}\big(t+\frac{j\pi}{2m\log e_{\lambda}(a)}:a\big)}.

Therefore, by (
10
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.3
.

For
m\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

(11)

-2m\cot_{\lambda}\big(2mt:a\big)=\sum_{j=0}^{2m-1}\tan_{\lambda}\bigg(t+\frac{j\pi}{2m\log e_{\lambda}(a)}:a\bigg).

We remark here that

\tan_{\lambda}\Big(t+\frac{j\pi}{2m\log e_{\lambda}(a)}:a\Big)=\tan\Big(t\log e_{\lambda}(a)+\frac{j\pi}{2m}\Big).

Taking
\lambda\rightarrow 0
in (
11
), we obtain

\displaystyle-2m\cot\big(2mat\big)=\sum_{j=0}^{2m-1}\tan\Big(at+\frac{j\pi}{2m}\Big),\ (\mathrm{see}\ [1,2]).

Working with
\prod_{j=0}^{2m-1}\sin_{\lambda}\Big(t+\frac{j\pi}{2m\log e_{\lambda}(a)}:a\Big)
and proceeding analogously to (
8
), we obtain

(12)

\prod_{j=0}^{2m-1}\sin_{\lambda}\Big(t+\frac{j\pi}{2m\log e_{\lambda}(a)}:a\Big)=-\frac{i}{2^{2m-1}}(-1)^{m}e^{-m\pi i}e^{\frac{\pi}{2}i}\sin_{\lambda}(2mt:a).

Thus, from (
12
), we have

(13)

\log\bigg|\frac{1}{2^{2m-1}}\sin_{\lambda}\big(2mt:a\big)\bigg|=\sum_{j=0}^{2m-1}\log\bigg|\sin_{\lambda}\Big(t+\frac{j\pi}{2m\log e_{\lambda}(a)}:a\Big)\bigg|.

Using(
7
), we take the derivatives with respect to
t
on both sides of (
13
) and get the identity:

(14)

\frac{2m\log(e_{\lambda}(a))\cos_{\lambda}(2mt:a)}{\sin_{\lambda}(2mt:a)}=\sum_{j=0}^{2m-1}\log\big(e_{\lambda}(a)\big)\frac{\cos_{\lambda}\big(t+\frac{j\pi}{2m\log e_{\lambda}(a)}:a\big)}{\sin_{\lambda}\big(t+\frac{j\pi}{2m\log e_{\lambda}(a)}:a\big)}.

Therefore, by (
14
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.4
.

For
m\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

(15)

2m\cot_{\lambda}\big(2mt:a\big)=\sum_{j=0}^{2m-1}\cot_{\lambda}\Big(t+\frac{j\pi}{2m\log e_{\lambda}(a)}:a\Big).

Taking
\lambda\rightarrow 0
in (
15
), we obtain

\displaystyle 2m\cot\big(2mat\big)=\sum_{j=0}^{2m-1}\cot(at+\frac{j\pi}{2m}),\ (\mathrm{see}\ [1,2]).

Now, we observe that

(16)

\cos_{\lambda}\big((k+1)x:a\big)+\cos_{\lambda}\big((k-1)x:a\big)=2\cos_{\lambda}\big(kx:a\big)\cos_{\lambda}\big(x:a\big).

Thus, by (
16
), we get

(17)

\displaystyle\cos_{\lambda}\big((k+1)x:a\big)=2\cos_{\lambda}\big(kx:a\big)\cos_{\lambda}(x:a)-\cos_{\lambda}\big((k-1)x:a\big)

\displaystyle=2^{2}\cos^{2}_{\lambda}\big(x:a\big)\cos_{\lambda}\big((k-1)x:a\big)-2\cos_{\lambda}\big(x:a\big)\cos_{\lambda}\big((k-2)x:a\big)-\cos_{\lambda}\big((k-1)x:a\big)

\displaystyle\cdots

\displaystyle=2^{k+1}\cos_{\lambda}^{k+1}\big(x:a\big)+\cdots

From (
17
), we note that there is polynomial
T_{n}(x)
with degree
n
such that

(18)

\cos_{\lambda}\big(nx:a\big)=T_{n}\Big(\cos_{\lambda}\big(x:a\big)\Big),\quad(n\in\mathbb{N}).

By using Theorem 2.2, we get

(19)

\sin_{\lambda}\big((2k+1)x:a\big)-\sin_{\lambda}\big((2k-1)x:a\big)=2\cos_{\lambda}\big(2kx:a\big)\sin_{\lambda}\big(x:a\big).

By (
18
) and Theorem 2.1, we get

(20)

\cos_{\lambda}\big(2kx:a\big)=T_{k}\Big(\cos_{\lambda}\big(2x:a\big)\Big)=T_{k}\Big(1-2\sin_{\lambda}^{2}\big(x:a\big)\Big).

From (
18
), (
19
) and (
20
), we show that

\sin_{\lambda}\big((2m+1)x:a\big)=\sin_{\lambda}\big(x:a\big)\bigg(1+2\sum_{k=1}^{m}T_{k}\Big(1-2\sin_{\lambda}^{2}\big(x:a\big)\Big)\bigg),

and hence there exist polynomials
K_{m}(x)
with degree
m
such that

(21)

\sin_{\lambda}\Big((2m+1)x:a\Big)=\sin_{\lambda}(x:a)K_{m}\Big(\sin_{\lambda}^{2}(x:a)\Big).

Now, from (
21
) we note that

(22)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\sin_{\lambda}\bigg((2m+1)\frac{k\pi}{(2m+1)\log(e_{\lambda}(a))}:a\bigg)

\displaystyle=\sin_{\lambda}\bigg(\frac{k\pi}{(2m+1)\log(e_{\lambda}(a))}:a\bigg)K_{m}\bigg(\sin_{\lambda}^{2}\bigg(\frac{k\pi}{(2m+1)\log e_{\lambda}(a)}:a\bigg)\bigg)

\displaystyle=\sin\Big(\frac{k\pi}{2m+1}\Big)K_{m}\Big(\sin^{2}\Big(\frac{k\pi}{2m+1}\Big)\Big),

which implies that
K_{m}\Big(\sin^{2}\Big(\frac{k\pi}{2m+1}\Big)\Big)=0
, for
k=1,2,3,\dots,m
.

By (
22
), the polynomials
K_{m}(x)
can be written as

(23)

K_{m}(x)=C\prod_{k=1}^{m}\bigg(1-\cfrac{x}{\sin^{2}\big(\frac{k\pi}{2m+1}\big)}\bigg).

Note from (
21
) that

(24)

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle=K_{m}(0)=\lim_{x\rightarrow 0}K_{m}\big(\sin_{\lambda}^{2}(x:a)\big)

\displaystyle=\lim_{x\rightarrow 0}\frac{\sin_{\lambda}\big((2m+1)x:a\big)}{\sin_{\lambda}(x:a)}=\frac{(2m+1)\log(e_{\lambda}(a))}{\log e_{\lambda}(a)}=2m+1.

By (
23
) and (
24
), we get

(25)

K_{m}(x)=(2m+1)\prod_{k=1}^{m}\bigg(1-\cfrac{x}{\sin^{2}\big(\frac{k\pi}{2m+1}\big)}\bigg).

From (
21
) and (
25
), we have

(26)

\displaystyle\sin_{\lambda}\big((2m+1)x:a\big)=\sin_{\lambda}\big(x:a\big)K_{m}\big(\sin_{\lambda}^{2}(x:a)\big)

\displaystyle=(2m+1)\sin_{\lambda}\big(x:a\big)\prod_{k=1}^{m}\bigg(1-\cfrac{\sin_{\lambda}^{2}(x:a)}{\sin^{2}\big(\frac{k\pi}{2m+1}\big)}\bigg).

Therefore, by (
26
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.5
.

For
m\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

(27)

\sin_{\lambda}\big((2m+1)x:a\big)=(2m+1)\sin_{\lambda}\big(x:a\big)\prod_{k=1}^{m}\bigg(1-\cfrac{\sin_{\lambda}^{2}(x:a)}{\sin^{2}\big(\frac{k\pi}{2m+1}\big)}\bigg).

Taking
\lambda\rightarrow 0
in (
27
), we obtain

\sin\big((2m+1)ax\big)=(2m+1)\sin(ax)\prod_{k=1}^{m}\bigg(1-\cfrac{\sin^{2}(ax)}{\sin^{2}\big(\frac{k\pi}{2m+1}\big)}\bigg).

3.
Conclusion

The degenerate trigonometric and degenerate hyperbolic functions are obtained by replacing the ordinary exponentials by the degenerate exponentials in the definitions of trigonometic and hyperbolic functions. In this paper, we considered the degenerate sine, degenerate cosine, degenerate tangent and degenerate cotangent functions and derived several identities among them. Especially, we were able to show the degenerate versions of the following multiple angle formulas (see (
11
), (
15
), (
27
)):

\displaystyle 2m\cot\big(2mt\big)=-\sum_{j=0}^{2m-1}\tan\Big(t+\frac{j\pi}{2m}\Big),

\displaystyle 2m\cot\big(2mt\big)=\sum_{j=0}^{2m-1}\cot(t+\frac{j\pi}{2m}),

\displaystyle\sin\big((2m+1)x\big)=(2m+1)\sin(x)\prod_{k=1}^{m}\bigg(1-\cfrac{\sin^{2}(x)}{\sin^{2}\big(\frac{k\pi}{2m+1}\big)}\bigg).

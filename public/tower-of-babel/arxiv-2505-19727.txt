The biharmonic hypersurface flow and the Willmore flow in higher dimensions
Yu Fu, Min-Chun Hong, Gang Tian

Abstract
. The biharmonic flow of hypersurfaces M n M^{n} immersed in the Euclidean space ℝ n + 1 \mathbb{R}^{n+1} for n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 is given by a fourth order geometric evolution equation, which is similar to the Willmore flow. We apply the Michael-Simon-Sobolev inequality to establish new Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequalities on hypersurfaces. Based on these Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequalities, we apply local energy estimates to extend the solution by a covering argument and obtain an estimate on the maximal existence time of the biharmonic flow of hypersurfaces in higher dimensions. In particular, we solve a problem in [ 2 ] on the biharmonic hypersurface flow for n = 4 n=4 . Finally, we apply our new approach to prove global existence of the Willmore flow in higher dimensions.

1.
Introduction

In their pioneering work
[
11
]
, Eells and Sampson
introduced
the harmonic map flow to establish existence of a harmonic map, representing its homotopy class between two Riemannian
manifolds. Let
M
and
N
be two Riemannian manifolds. A map
u:M\to N
is called
harmonic
if its
tension field
\tau(u)
vanishes; i.e.

\tau(u)={\rm trace}\nabla du=0.

Let
u_{0}
be a given smooth map from
M
to
N
. One of the most important questions on harmonic maps is whether

u_{0}
can be deformed to a harmonic map in its homotopy
class
[u_{0}]
or not. Assuming that the sectional curvature of the target manifold
N
is non-positive, Eells and Sampson
[
11
]
gave a positive
answer to the above question by
establishing the existence of the global smooth solution of the heat flow for harmonic maps as follows:

(1.1)

\partial_{t}u=\tau(u)=\triangle_{M}u+A(u)(\nabla u,\nabla u)

with initial value
u(0)=u_{0}
, where
\triangle_{M}=-\nabla^{*}\nabla
is the Laplace-Beltrami operator of
M

and
A(u)(\cdot,\cdot)
is the second fundamental form of
N
. Struwe
[
37
]
,
[
38
]
and Chen-Struwe
[
7
]
established many results on
the
existence and partial regularity of weak solutions to the harmonic map flow. In two dimensions, Chang, Ding and Ye
[
4
]
showed an example that the heat
flow (
1.1
) blows up in finite time, so in general the heat flow for
harmonic maps cannot have a global smooth solution. Qing and Tian
[
32
]
proved an energy identity at
the first singular time
T
that facilitates a bubble tree as
t\to T
through a finite number of nontrivial harmonic maps on
S^{2}
(see also
[
31
]
,
[
30
]
,
[
8
]
). Inspired by the harmonic
map flow, Hamilton
[
16
]
introduced the Ricci flow, which was used by Perelman to settle the Poincaré conjecture. Therefore, the
fundamental work
[
11
]
of Eell-Sampson has huge influences in differential geometry and analysis.

To establish the variational theory in fibre bundles over manifolds, Eells and Sampson
[
12
]
also introduced a polyharmonic map of degree
r
(
or
r
-harmonic map
), which generalizes the concept of harmonic maps. In particular, a lot of interesting results have been established in
the case of biharmonic maps with
r=2
.
For a map
u:M\to N
, its bienergy functional is given by

\displaystyle E_{2}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}|\tau(u)|^{2}dv_{g}.

A map
u:M\to N
is said to be
biharmonic
if it is
a critical point of the bienergy functional (see, e.g.,
[
10
]
); i.e. it satisfies

(1.2)

\tau_{2}(u)=\Delta_{M}\tau(u)-{\rm trace}\,R^{N}(du,\tau(u))du=0,

where
\tau_{2}(u)
is the bitension field of
u
and
R^{N}
is the curvature tensor of
N
. In their well-known survey
[
10
]
, Eells and
Lemaire
reemphasised the study of
biharmonic maps between Riemannian manifolds. Since then, biharmonic maps have received a lot of attention in analysis. Chang, Wang and Yang

[
5
]
proved partial
regularity of stationary biharmonic maps. For more
results
on partial regularities of biharmonic maps, see
[
40
]
,
[
39
]
and
[
19
]
.

In 1980s, B. Y. Chen independently introduced the concept of
biharmonic submanifolds
.
Let
M
be a submanifold of a Riemannian manifold
N
.

M
is said to be a
biharmonic submanifold
if the isometric
immersion
f:M\to N
is a biharmonic map. Biharmonic submanifolds have attracted a lot of attention in geometry.
In particular, Chen
[
6
]
proposed the well-known biharmonic conjecture:

Any biharmonic submanifold in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}

is minimal
. Consider the special case that
M=M^{n}
is a hypersurface
of
N=\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
.
Chen
[
6
]
and Jiang
[
21
]
independently confirmed the conjecture for the case of
n=2
. Hasanis and
Vlachos
[
18
]
showed that Chen’s conjecture holds in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
with
n=3
. In collaboration
with Zhan

[
14
]
, the first two authors settled
Chen’s conjecture in
\mathbb{R}^{5}
with
n=4
. Recently, the first two authors with Zhan
[
15
]
made further progress to confirm
Chen’s conjecture in
\mathbb{R}^{6}
and the related Balmuş-Montaldo-Oniciuc conjecture for
n=5
. Compared with the famous Bernstein problem (see, e.g.,
[
3
]
,

[
36
]
),
Chen’s conjecture in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with
n>5
is a challenging open problem. In the past
two decades, significant results on biharmonic submanifolds related to Chen’s conjecture have been established (see, e.g.,

[
33
]
,
[
13
]
).

For an isometric
immersion
f:M^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
,
\Delta_{M}f=\vec{H}=H\nu
, where
\vec{H}
is the mean curvature vector field (the trace of the second fundamental form),

H
is the mean curvature and
\nu
is the outward normal unit vector field
of the hypersurface
M=f(M^{n})
. Then
f:M^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is biharmonic (c.f.
[
6
]
) if and only if

(1.3)

\Delta_{M}^{2}f=\Delta_{M}\vec{H}=0.

It was known (see Lemma 2.1 in Section 2, or
[
6
]
) that
an isometric immersion
f:M^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is biharmonic if and only if it
satisfies the following two equations:

(1.4)

\Delta_{M}H-H|A|^{2}=0,\quad 2{\bf S}(\nabla H)+H\nabla H=0,

where
A
is the second fundamental form,
S
is the Weingarten operator defined by
{\bf S}(X)=-D_{X}\nu
for any tangent vector field
X
and
D
denote the Levi-Civita
connections of
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
.

Consider a closed, immersed, orientable hypersurface
M^{n}

parametrized by
f_{0}:M^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
.
Motivated by the work of Eells-Sampson
[
11
]
, we investigate whether
M^{n}
can be deformed into a biharmonic hypersurface.
Therefore we study the
biharmonic flow of hypersurfaces

(1.5)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}=-\Delta_{M_{t}}^{2}f=-\Delta_{M_{t}}{\vec{H}}=-\big(\Delta_{M_{t}}H-H|A|^{2}\big)\nu+2{\bf S}(\nabla H)+H\nabla H

with initial value
f\left(0\right)=f_{0}
, where
\Delta_{M_{t}}
,
H
and
\nu
are respectively the Laplace-Beltrami
operator, the mean curvature and the unit outward normal of the hypersurface

M_{t}=f_{t}\left(M^{n}\right)
.

Bernard, Wheeler and Wheeler
[
2
]
firstly investigated the heat flow for biharmonic hypersurfaces with
2\leq n\leq 4
.
In the case of
n=4
, they obtained an estimate of the maximal existence of the heat flow for biharmonic hypersurfaces by assuming
that the initial condition
f_{0}
satisfies

(1.6)

\displaystyle\int_{M_{0}\cap B_{2R_{0}}\left(x\right)}(\left|A_{0}\right|^{4}+|\nabla A_{0}|^{2})d\mu_{0}\leq\varepsilon_{0}

for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and some fixed
R_{0}>0
with a small constant
\varepsilon_{0}
, where
A_{0}
and
d\mu_{0}
denote respectively the second fundamental form and the volume element of the hypersurface
M_{0}
.

Comparing the assumption on the Willmore flow in
[
22
]
, it is interesting whether one can remove the additional condition on

\int_{M_{0}\cap B_{2R_{0}}\left(x\right)}|\nabla A_{0}|^{2}d\mu_{0}\leq\varepsilon_{0}
in (
1.6
). In this paper, we solve the above problem for
n=4
. More precisely, we have

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
f_{0}:M^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be a smooth immersion for
2\leq n\leq 5
.
Assume that there is an absolute positive constant
\varepsilon_{0}
such
that

(1.7)

\displaystyle\int_{M_{0}\cap B_{2R_{0}}(x)}\left|A_{0}\right|^{n}d\mu_{0}\leq\varepsilon_{0}

for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and some fixed
R_{0}>0
. Then the maximal
existence time
T
of the solution on the flow (
1.5
) satisfies

T\geq\delta R_{0}^{4}

for a small positive constant
\delta
.
Moreover, there exists a constant
\varepsilon>\varepsilon_{0}
such that, for
0\leq t\leq T
, we have

\int_{M_{t}\cap B_{R_{0}}(x)}\left|A(t)\right|^{n}d\mu\leq\varepsilon\mbox{,}

where
M_{t}=f_{t}(M^{n})
.

For the proof of Theorem
1.1
, one of the key ideas is to establish local
L^{2}
-estimates under the condition (
1.7
).
When
n=2
, the proof of Theorem
1.1
is similar to the one for the
Willmore flow by Kuwert and Schätzle
[
22
]
, applying the Michael-Simon Sobolev inequality to obtain local
L^{2}
-estimates under the condition (
1.7
).
Since the biharmonic map flow (
1.5
) is the flow of higher dimensional hypersurfaces with
n>2
, it becomes much more complicated
than the
Willmore
flow with
n=2
. Indeed, Bernard et al.
[
2
]
needed an extra condition on
\nabla A_{0}
in (
1.6
) for
n=4
.
In our new proof of Theorem
1.1
, we apply the Michael-Simon Sobolev inequality to derive a
Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality on hypersurfaces for
n\leq 5
. For example, when
n=4
, for any
u\in C^{1}_{c}\left(M^{4}\right)
satisfying that
\int_{[u\neq 0]}|H|^{4}\,d\mu\leq\varepsilon
with sufficiently small
\varepsilon
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{M^{4}}u^{4}d\mu

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{M^{4}}|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}\,d\mu\int_{M^{4}}|u|^{2}\,d\mu.

With application of the extended Michael-Simon Sobolev inequalities, we obtain two local estimates on

\|A\|^{2}_{L^{2}\left(f^{-1}\left(B_{R}(x)\right)\right)}
and

\|\nabla^{2}A\|_{L^{2}\left(f^{-1}(B_{R}(x))\right)}^{2}
for any
R\leq\frac{R_{0}}{4}
under the condition (
1.7
). Through a delicate
covering argument, we apply the
Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality for
n\leq 5
and above local
L^{2}
-estimates to prove Theorem
1.1
.

Moreover, in the case of
n\geq 6
, we have

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
f_{0}:M^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be a smooth immersion with
n\geq 6
. Under the same assumption
(
1.7
),
the conclusion of Theorem
1.1
also holds.

When
n\geq 6
, we cannot control
\|A\|_{L^{n}\left(M\cap B_{R}(x)\right)}
by

\|\nabla^{2}A\|_{L^{2}\left(M\cap B_{R}(x)\right)}^{2}
, so the proof of Theorem
1.2
is much more complicated than the proof of Theorem

1.1
. More precisely,
we establish local Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequalities by higher order derivatives of
A
; i.e.
if
n\geq 6
is even, we have

\displaystyle\|A\|_{L^{n}(M\cap B_{R}(x))}\leq C\|A\|^{\frac{2}{n}}_{L^{2}(M\cap B_{2R}(x))}\sum_{m=0}^{\frac{n}{2}}\left\|\frac{\nabla^{m}A}{R^{\frac{n}{2}-m}}\right\|^{\frac{n-2}{n}}_{L^{2}(M\cap B_{2R}(x))}.

However, it is difficult to obtain an
L^{2}
-estimate for
\nabla^{\frac{n}{2}}A
under the condition (
1.7
) because
n\geq 6
.
To overcome this new difficulty, we need to modify the ideas in
[
25
]
and
[
22
]
to obtain the new Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality as
following:

(1.8)

\displaystyle\|A\|_{L^{\infty}(M\cap B_{R}(x))}\leq C\|A\|^{\frac{2}{n+2}}_{L^{2}(M\cap B_{2R}(x))}\sum_{m=0}^{\frac{n}{2}+1}\left\|\frac{\nabla^{m}A}{R^{\frac{n}{2}+1-m}}\right\|^{\frac{n}{n+2}}_{L^{2}(M\cap B_{2R}(x))}.

With the help of (
1.8
), we apply similar proofs as in
[
22
]
to obtain the energy estimate for
\nabla^{m}A
for any integer
m\geq 1
.
Finally, combining the generalized
Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality with local energy estimates, we apply a more sophisticated covering argument to prove Theorem
1.2
for
any
n\geq 6
.

Remark 1
.

It is known that though a family of diffeomorphisms, (
1.5
) is equivalent to a normal biharmonic flow as in
[
2
]
and
[
34
]
. A hypersurface satisfying
2{\bf S}(\nabla H)+H\nabla H=0
is called biconservative and has a geometric meaning in better understanding of biharmonic submanifolds and Chen’s biharmonic conjecture (see recent progress in
[
13
,
33
]
). Palmurella and Rivière
[
34
]
emphasized the importance of the tangential component in the parametric Willmore flow. Moreover, (
1.5
) is parabolic so it is nature to keep the tangential term in (
1.5
).

Since the Willmore
flow is a fourth order
geometric
evolution equation analogous to the biharmonic flow, we apply our new approach in Theorem
1.2
to prove the global existence of the Willmore flow in higher dimensions.

Let
M^{2}_{0}
be a closed immersed orientable surface parametrized by
f_{0}:M_{0}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N}
. The two dimensional Willmore flow of surfaces
is given by the fourth order geometric evolution equation

(1.9)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}=-\left(\Delta^{\perp}{\vec{H}}+Q(A^{0}){\vec{H}}\right)

with initial value
f\left(0\right)=f_{0}
, where the Laplace operator
\Delta^{\perp}
in (
1.9
) is defined with respect to
the connection on normal vector fields along
f
,
A^{0}
is the traceless part of the second fundamental form
A
and

Q(A^{0})\vec{H}=A^{0}\left<\vec{H},A^{0}\right>
.

Kuwert and Schätzle
[
22
]
investigated the Willmore
flow (
1.9
) and gave a
lower bound on the lifespan of a smooth solution. Kuwert and Schätzle
[
23
]
further showed the global existence of the Willmore
flow (
1.9
) with small initial data
as well as that the flow converges to a standard
round sphere (see also
[
35
]
). Since then, tremendous results on the Willmore flow have been established. Recently, if the initial energy
is small, Kuwert and Scheuer
[
24
]
proved stability estimates for the barycenter and the quadratic moment of the surface. Palmurella
and Rivière
[
34
]
studied the parametric Willmore flow with a
tangential vector field
U
so that
f(t,\cdot)
is conformal for every
t
.

We investigate the Willmore flow in higher dimensions (i.e.
n\geq 2
).
Let
f:M^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be a closed hypersurface
M^{n}
immersed in the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. We only consider the Willmore energy

(1.10)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{W}(f)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}|A|^{2}d\mu,

where
A
is the second fundamental form.
The Euler-Lagrange equation for the energy (
1.10
) is given by

(1.11)

\displaystyle\Delta_{M}H-\frac{1}{2}|A|^{2}H+C(A)=0,

where
C(A):=g^{ij}g^{kl}g^{pq}A_{ik}A_{lp}A_{qj}
with
summation over
i,j,k,l,p,q\in\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
.
When
n=2
, it can be checked that

C(A)=\frac{1}{2}H(|A|^{2}+2|A^{0}|^{2}),

so the equation (
1.11
) is the exact Willmore surface equation in
[
23
]
:

\Delta_{M}H+H|A^{0}|^{2}=0.

The Willmore flow for hypersurfaces in higher dimensions
is defined by

(1.12)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}=-\left(\Delta_{M_{t}}H-\frac{1}{2}|A|^{2}H+C(A)\right)\nu

with initial value
f\left(0\right)=f_{0}
, where

M_{t}=f_{t}\left(M^{n}\right)
.
The flow (
1.12
) with
n\geq 3
is a generalization of the two dimensional Willmore flow (
1.9
).
Then we have

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
f_{0}:M^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be a smooth immersion with
n\geq 3
.
Assume that there exists a sufficient small constant
\varepsilon_{0}
such
that

(1.13)

\displaystyle\int_{M_{0}\cap B_{2R_{0}}(x)}\left|A_{0}\right|^{n}d\mu_{0}\leq\varepsilon_{0}

for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and some fixed
R_{0}>0
and

(1.14)

\displaystyle\int_{M_{0}}|A_{0}|^{2}d\mu_{0}\leq C\varepsilon_{0}R_{0}^{n-2}

with some constant
C
.
Then there is a global smooth solution to the Willmore flow (
1.12
) that exists for all time
t\in[0,\infty)
.
Moreover, as
t\to\infty
, the solution of the Willmore flow (
1.12
) converges to a smooth solution of the Willmore equation (
1.11
) locally in
C^{k}
for all integers
k\geq 0
.

Remark 2
.

Without difficulties, our Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
1.3
can be generalized to both the biharmonic flow and the Willmore flow of submanifolds in higher dimensions and codimensions.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we give some basic estimates on hypersurfaces and compute the evolution
equations for elementary
geometric quantities. In Section 3, we apply the Michael-Simon Sobolev inequality to obtain two local energy inequalities. In Section 4, we
complete the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
In Section 5, we establish
Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequalities on hypersurfaces and prove Theorem
1.2
. In Section 6, we study the Willmore flow in higher dimensions and complete the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

2.
Basic results of the biharmonic flow

In this section, we will outline basic geometric quantities of the biharmonic hypersurface flow.

We consider a hypersurface
M=f(M^{n})
immersed in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
via

f:M^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and endow
M
with a
Riemannian metric
g
defined by

g_{ij}=g(\partial_{i},\partial_{j})=\langle\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}},\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{j}}\rangle=\langle\partial_{i}f,\partial_{j}f\rangle,

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the Euclidean scalar
inner product. The metric
g
induces a natural isomorphism between
tangent and cotangent spaces. Let

g^{ij}
be the inverse of
g_{ij}
. The volume element
d\mu
on
M
is given in local coordinates by

d\mu=\sqrt{\det g}~dx.

The Christoffel symbols
\Gamma^{k}_{ij}
are defined by

\displaystyle\Gamma^{k}_{ij}=\frac{1}{2}g^{kl}\Big(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}g_{jl}+\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}g_{il}-\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{l}}g_{ij}\Big).

Recall that the Gauss and Weingarten formulas are given by

(2.1)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}=\Gamma_{ij}^{k}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{k}}+A_{ij}\nu,\quad\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\nu=-{{}\bf S}(\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}),

where
A_{ij}
are the components of the second fundamental form

A
. Note that the Weingarten operator
{\bf S}
and the second fundamental form
A
are related by

(2.2)

\displaystyle{\bf S}(\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}})=A_{ij}g^{jl}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{l}}.

We use the following notations for traces of the second
fundamental form
A
on
M^{n}
:

\displaystyle H={\rm trace}_{g}A=g^{ij}A_{ij},\quad|A|^{2}=g^{ij}g^{kl}A_{ik}A_{jl}.

Then

\displaystyle\nabla H=g^{ij}\frac{\partial H}{\partial x_{i}}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{j}},\quad{\bf S}(\nabla H)=g^{ij}g^{kl}\frac{\partial H}{\partial x_{i}}A_{jk}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{l}}.

Moreover, the components of the tracefree second fundamental form are defined by

\displaystyle{A^{0}}=A-\frac{1}{n}Hg.

The covariant derivative of a vector
X^{i}
or a covector
Y_{i}

on
M
are defined respectively as

\displaystyle\nabla_{j}X^{i}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}X^{i}+\Gamma^{i}_{jk}X^{k},\quad\nabla_{j}Y_{i}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}Y_{i}-\Gamma^{k}_{ij}Y_{k}.

Recall that the Gauss and Codazzi equations are given
respectively by

(2.3)

\displaystyle R_{ijkl}=A_{ik}A_{jl}-A_{il}A_{jk},

(2.4)

\displaystyle\nabla_{i}A_{jk}=\nabla_{j}A_{ik}=\nabla_{k}A_{ij}.

The following result gives the tangential and the normal parts of the
biharmonic immersion
\Delta_{M}^{2}f
for a hypersurface
M
in the
Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

For an isometric immersion
f:M^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
,
we have

\displaystyle\Delta_{M}^{2}f

\displaystyle=\big(\Delta_{M}H-H|A|^{2}\big)\nu-\big(2{\bf S}(\nabla H)+H\nabla H\big),

where
\Delta_{M}=-\nabla^{*}\nabla
is the Laplace-Beltrami
operator,
H
is the mean curvature and
\nu
is the unit outward normal of
M
.

Proof.

Due to the well-known Beltrami formula, we have

\Delta_{M}f=g^{ij}\left(\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}-\Gamma_{ij}^{k}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{k}}\right)=H\nu.

In the local coordinates,
we obtain

\displaystyle\Delta_{M}^{2}f=\Delta_{M}(H\nu)=g^{ij}\Big\{\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}\big(H\nu\big)-\Gamma_{ij}^{k}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}\big(H\nu\big)\Big\}

\displaystyle=(\Delta_{M}H)\,\nu+g^{ij}\Big\{\frac{\partial H}{\partial x_{i}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}\nu+\frac{\partial H}{\partial x_{j}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\nu+H\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}\nu-\Gamma_{ij}^{k}H\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}\nu\Big\}.

Using
the Gauss and Weingarten formulas (
2.1
), the above formula becomes

(2.5)

\displaystyle\quad\Delta_{M}^{2}f

\displaystyle=(\Delta_{M}H)\,\nu+g^{ij}\Big\{-\frac{\partial H}{\partial x_{i}}A_{jm}g^{ml}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{l}}-\frac{\partial H}{\partial x_{j}}A_{im}g^{ml}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{l}}

\displaystyle\quad-H\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\big(A_{jm}g^{ml}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{l}}\big)+H\Gamma_{ij}^{k}A_{km}g^{ml}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{l}}\Big\}

\displaystyle=\big(\Delta_{M}H-H|A|^{2}\big)\nu-2{\bf S}(\nabla H)

\displaystyle\quad-Hg^{ij}\Big\{\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\big(A_{jm}g^{ml}\big)\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{l}}+A_{jm}g^{ml}\Gamma_{il}^{k}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{k}}-\Gamma_{ij}^{k}A_{km}g^{ml}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{l}}\Big\}.

Since

\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}g^{ml}=-g^{rl}\Gamma_{ir}^{m}-g^{mr}\Gamma_{ir}^{l}
, it follows
from the Codazzi equation that

\displaystyle g^{ij}\Big\{\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\big(A_{jm}g^{ml}\big)\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{l}}+A_{jm}g^{ml}\Gamma_{il}^{k}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{k}}-\Gamma_{ij}^{k}A_{km}g^{ml}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{l}}\Big\}

\displaystyle=g^{ij}\Big\{\frac{\partial A_{jm}}{\partial x_{i}}g^{ml}-A_{jm}g^{rl}\Gamma_{ir}^{m}-\Gamma_{ij}^{k}A_{km}g^{ml}\Big\}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{l}}

\displaystyle=g^{ij}\Big\{\frac{\partial A_{ij}}{\partial x_{m}}g^{ml}+\Gamma_{ij}^{k}A_{km}g^{ml}-\Gamma_{mj}^{k}A_{ki}g^{ml}-A_{jm}g^{rl}\Gamma_{ir}^{m}-\Gamma_{ij}^{k}A_{km}g^{ml}\Big\}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{l}}

\displaystyle=g^{ij}\Big\{\frac{\partial A_{ij}}{\partial x_{m}}g^{ml}-\Gamma_{mj}^{k}A_{ki}g^{ml}-A_{jm}g^{rl}\Gamma_{ir}^{m}\Big\}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{l}}

\displaystyle=\Big\{g^{ij}\frac{\partial A_{ij}}{\partial x_{m}}+A_{ij}\frac{\partial g^{ij}}{\partial x_{m}}\Big\}g^{ml}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{l}}=\nabla H,

which together with (
2.5
) completes the proof of Lemma
2.1
.
∎

Next, we compute the evolution equations for elementary
geometric quantities such as the metric, the volume element, the
second fundamental form and the mean curvature.
The covariant derivative of mixed
(p,q)
type tensor
T
is given
by

\displaystyle\nabla_{k}T^{i_{1}\ldots i_{p}}_{j_{1}\ldots j_{q}}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}T^{i_{1}\ldots i_{p}}_{j_{1}\ldots j_{q}}

\displaystyle+\Gamma_{km}^{i_{1}}T^{m\ldots i_{p}}_{j_{1}\ldots j_{q}}+\cdots+\Gamma_{km}^{i_{p}}T^{i_{1}\ldots m}_{j_{1}\ldots j_{q}}

\displaystyle-\Gamma_{kj_{1}}^{m}T^{i_{1}\ldots i_{p}}_{m\ldots j_{q}}-\cdots-\Gamma_{kj_{q}}^{m}T^{i_{1}\ldots i_{p}}_{j_{1}\ldots m}.

The Laplacian of a tensor
T
is defined by

\Delta_{M}T^{i_{1}\ldots i_{p}}_{j_{1}\ldots j_{q}}=-\nabla^{*}\nabla T^{i_{1}\ldots i_{p}}_{j_{1}\ldots j_{q}}.

From now on, we denote
\Delta_{M}
by
\Delta
.

As in
[
16
]
and
[
22
]
, we will also write
T\star S
to denote any tensor
formed by contraction on some indices of the tensors
T
and
S
by
the metric
g
of
M_{t}
and we use the notation
P_{r}^{m}\left(A\right)
to denote any combination of tensors of the type

P_{r}^{m}\left(A\right)=\sum_{i_{1}+\cdots+i_{r}=m}c_{mr}\nabla^{i_{1}}A\star\nabla^{i_{2}}A\star\cdots\star\nabla^{i_{r}}A\mbox{,}

where the constants
c_{mr}
are
absolute.

Let us recall the well-known Simons’ identity
[
36
]
:

Lemma 2.2
.

(Simons’ identity).

\displaystyle\Delta A_{ij}=\nabla_{ij}H+HA_{il}g^{lm}A_{mj}-|A|^{2}A_{ij}.

In the
``\star"
notation, Simons’ identity becomes

\displaystyle\Delta A=\nabla^{2}H+A\star A\star A.

Moreover, the Ricci identity (see, e.g.,
[
22
]
) implies that for a mixed
tensor field
T
,

(2.6)

\displaystyle\nabla_{ij}T=\nabla_{ji}T+T\star A\star A.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
f:M^{n}\times[0,T]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be a solution to the flow
\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}=F\nu+W
with general
F
and
W=a^{l}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{l}}
. Then we have

(2.7)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}g_{ij}

\displaystyle=-2FA_{ij}+\nabla_{i}a^{l}g_{jl}+\nabla_{j}a^{l}g_{il}\mbox{,}

(2.8)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}g^{ij}

\displaystyle=2FA^{ij}-\nabla_{l}a^{i}g^{jl}-\nabla_{l}a^{j}g^{il}\mbox{,}

(2.9)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\nu

\displaystyle=-\nabla F-a^{l}g^{ij}A_{il}\partial f_{j}\mbox{,}

(2.10)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}d\mu

\displaystyle=(-HF+\nabla_{l}a^{l})~d\mu\mbox{,}

(2.11)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}A_{ij}

\displaystyle=\nabla_{ij}F-FA_{jk}A_{il}g^{kl}+\nabla_{i}a^{l}A_{jl}+\nabla_{j}a^{l}A_{il}+a^{l}\nabla_{l}A_{ij}\mbox{,}

(2.12)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}H

\displaystyle=\Delta F+F|A|^{2}+g^{ij}a^{l}\nabla_{l}A_{ij}.

Proof.

Note
\frac{\partial}{\partial t}
and

\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}
commute. Then, using (
2.1
) and the fact that
\langle\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}},\nu\rangle=0
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}g_{ij}

\displaystyle=\langle\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}(F\nu+W),\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{j}}\rangle+\langle\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}},\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}(F\nu+W)\rangle

\displaystyle=F\langle\frac{\partial\nu}{\partial x_{i}},\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{j}}\rangle+F\langle\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}},\frac{\partial\nu}{\partial x_{j}}\rangle+\langle\frac{\partial W}{\partial x_{i}},\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{j}}\rangle+\langle\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}},\frac{\partial W}{\partial x_{j}}\rangle

\displaystyle=-2FA_{ij}+\frac{\partial a^{l}}{\partial x_{i}}g_{jl}+\frac{\partial a^{l}}{\partial x_{j}}g_{il}+a^{l}\Gamma_{il}^{m}g_{mj}+a^{l}\Gamma_{jl}^{m}g_{mi}

\displaystyle=-2FA_{ij}+\nabla_{i}a^{l}g_{jl}+\nabla_{j}a^{l}g_{il}.

This gives (
2.7
).

Furthermore, we compute
\frac{\partial}{\partial t}g^{ij}
. Since

g^{ik}g_{jk}=\delta^{i}_{j}
, it follows that

\displaystyle 0=\frac{\partial(g^{ik}g_{jk})}{\partial t}=\frac{\partial g^{ik}}{\partial t}g_{jk}+g^{ik}\big(-2FA_{jk}+\nabla_{j}a^{l}g_{kl}+\nabla_{k}a^{l}g_{jl}\big),

and hence

\displaystyle g^{jm}g_{jk}\frac{\partial g^{ik}}{\partial t}=\frac{\partial g^{im}}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=-g^{jm}g^{ik}\big(-2FA_{jk}+\nabla_{j}a^{l}g_{kl}+\nabla_{k}a^{l}g_{jl}\big)

\displaystyle=2FA^{im}-\nabla_{j}a^{i}g^{jm}-\nabla_{k}a^{m}g^{ik}.

Renumbering the indices in the above equation gives (
2.8
).

Noting again that
\left<\nu,\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}\right>=0
, it follows from (2.8) and (
2.1
) that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\nu

\displaystyle=\langle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\nu,\nu\rangle\nu+\langle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\nu,\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}\rangle\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{j}}g^{ij}=-\langle\nu,\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}\rangle\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{j}}g^{ij}

\displaystyle=-\langle\nu,\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}(F\nu+W)\rangle\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{j}}g^{ij}=-g^{ij}\frac{\partial F}{\partial x_{i}}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{j}}-a^{l}g^{ij}A_{il}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{j}}

\displaystyle=-\nabla F-a^{l}g^{ij}A_{il}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{j}}.

We consider the evolution of the volume element
d\mu
:

(2.13)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}d\mu

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\big(\sqrt{\det g}~dx\big)=\frac{1}{2}{\sqrt{\det g}}~g^{ij}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}g_{ij}~dx=\frac{1}{2}g^{ij}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}g_{ij}~d\mu

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}g^{ij}(-2FA_{ij}+\nabla_{i}a^{l}g_{jl}+\nabla_{j}a^{l}g_{il})~d\mu=(-HF+\nabla_{l}a^{l})~d\mu.

By using the Gauss and Codazzi formulas (
2.1
), we calculate the evolution of the second fundamental form to obtain (
2.10
) by

\displaystyle\quad\frac{\partial}{\partial t}A_{ij}=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\langle\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}},\nu\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}(F\nu+W),\nu\rangle-\langle\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}},\nabla F+a^{l}g^{mn}A_{ml}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{n}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}+F\langle\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}\nu,\nu\rangle+\langle\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}W,\nu\rangle-\Gamma_{ij}^{k}\langle\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{k}},\nabla F+a^{l}g^{mn}A_{ml}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{n}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}-FA_{jk}A_{il}g^{kl}-\Gamma_{ij}^{k}\frac{\partial F}{\partial x^{k}}-\Gamma_{ij}^{k}a^{l}A_{kl}+\langle\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}W,\nu\rangle

\displaystyle=\nabla_{ij}F-FA_{jk}A_{il}g^{kl}-\Gamma_{ij}^{k}a^{l}A_{kl}+\frac{\partial a^{l}}{\partial x_{j}}A_{il}+\frac{\partial a^{l}}{\partial x_{i}}A_{jl}+a^{l}\frac{\partial A_{jl}}{\partial x_{i}}+a^{l}\Gamma_{lj}^{k}A_{ik}

\displaystyle=\nabla_{ij}F-FA_{jk}A_{il}g^{kl}+\nabla_{i}a^{l}A_{jl}+\nabla_{j}a^{l}A_{il}+a^{l}\nabla_{i}A_{jl},

where we used the fact that

\displaystyle\langle\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}W,\nu\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\Big(\frac{\partial a^{l}}{\partial x_{j}}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{l}}+a^{l}\Gamma_{lj}^{k}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{k}}+a^{l}A_{jl}\nu\Big),\nu\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial a^{l}}{\partial x_{j}}A_{il}+a^{l}\Gamma_{lj}^{k}A_{ik}+\frac{\partial a^{l}}{\partial x_{i}}A_{jl}+a^{l}\frac{\partial A_{jl}}{\partial x_{i}}.

Using the above equations, we may compute the evolution of the mean
curvature:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}H

\displaystyle=g^{ij}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}A_{ij}+A_{ij}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}g^{ij}

\displaystyle=\Delta F+F|A|^{2}+g^{ij}\big(\nabla_{i}a^{l}A_{jl}+\nabla_{j}a^{l}A_{il}+a^{l}\nabla_{l}A_{ij}\big)

\displaystyle\quad+A_{ij}\big(-\nabla_{l}a^{i}g^{jl}-\nabla_{l}a^{j}g^{il}\big)

\displaystyle=\Delta F+F|A|^{2}+g^{ij}a^{l}\nabla_{l}A_{ij}.

These complete the proof of Lemma
2.3
.
∎

For now on, we focus on the biharmonic flow
\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}=F\nu+W
with

(2.14)

\displaystyle F

\displaystyle:=-\big(\Delta H-H|A|^{2}\big),

(2.15)

\displaystyle W

\displaystyle:=2{\bf S}(\nabla H)+H\nabla H=a^{l}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{l}},

(2.16)

\displaystyle a^{l}

\displaystyle:=\Big(2g^{ij}g^{kl}\partial_{i}HA_{jk}+Hg^{il}\partial_{i}H\Big).

For a given point
p\in M
, we may choose normal coordinates at
p\in M
such that

g_{ij}(p)=\delta_{ij},\quad\frac{\partial g_{ij}}{\partial x_{k}}=0,\quad\Gamma^{k}_{ij}(p)=0.

We proceed in our proofs by
using normal coordinates at
p
. Taking into account (2.6)-(2.8), the following result is needed.

Lemma 2.4
.

For
f:M^{n}\times[0,T]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
evolving by
\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}=F\nu+W
, the evolution equation of the
induced connection coefficients
\Gamma^{i}_{jk}
is given by

(2.17)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\Gamma^{i}_{jk}=P_{2}^{3}(A)+P_{4}^{1}(A)\mbox{.}

Proof.

Note that the Christoffel symbols
\Gamma^{k}_{ij}
are determined by the
metric:

\displaystyle\Gamma^{k}_{ij}=\frac{1}{2}g^{kl}\Big(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}g_{jl}+\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}g_{il}-\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{l}}g_{ij}\Big).

Using (
2.7
)-(
2.8
) and the “
\star
” notation, we have at each
p\in M

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\Gamma^{k}_{ij}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial g^{kl}}{\partial t}\Big(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}g_{jl}+\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}g_{il}-\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{l}}g_{ij}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}g^{kl}\Big(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}g_{jl}+\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}g_{il}-\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{l}}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}g_{ij}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}g^{kl}\Big\{\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\big(-2FA_{jl}+\nabla_{j}a^{m}g_{lm}+\nabla_{l}a^{m}g_{jm}\big)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}\big(-2FA_{il}+\nabla_{i}a^{m}g_{lm}+\nabla_{l}a^{m}g_{im}\big)

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{l}}\big(-2FA_{ij}+\nabla_{i}a^{m}g_{jm}+\nabla_{j}a^{m}g_{im}\big)\Big\}

\displaystyle=P_{2}^{3}(A)+P_{4}^{1}(A),

where we have used

F=-\Delta H+H|A|^{2}

and

\nabla_{i}a^{m}=\nabla(\nabla A\star A).

This completes the proof of Lemma
2.4
.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
f:M^{n}\times[0,T]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be evolved by

\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}=-\Delta^{2}f=F\nu+W
. Then we have

(2.18)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}A

\displaystyle=-\Delta^{2}A+P_{3}^{2}(A)+P_{5}^{0}(A),

(2.19)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\nabla^{m}A

\displaystyle=-\Delta^{2}\nabla^{m}A+P_{3}^{m+2}(A)+P_{5}^{m}(A),\quad m\in\mathbb{N^{+}}.

Proof.

The proof of this lemma is similar to Lemmas 6 and 7 in
[
2
]
. Since the flow has a tangential part
W
, we give the proof for completeness.

Using (
2.6
), we compute that

\displaystyle\nabla_{ijkl}H

\displaystyle=\nabla_{ikjl}H+\nabla(\nabla A\star A\star A)

\displaystyle=\nabla_{klij}H+P_{3}^{2}(A).

By applying Simons’ identity and the above equation, we get

(2.20)

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}\Delta H=\Delta\nabla^{2}H+P_{3}^{2}(A)=\Delta^{2}A+P_{3}^{2}(A).

Similarly, after interchanging covariant derivatives
k
times in the highest order term, we have

(2.21)

\displaystyle\nabla^{k}\Delta^{2}A=\Delta^{2}\nabla^{k}A+P_{3}^{k+2}(A).

Since
F=-\Delta H+H|A|^{2}
, it follows from applying (
2.20
), (
2.11
) that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}A

\displaystyle=\nabla^{2}F+F\star A\star A+\nabla(\nabla A\star A)\star A

\displaystyle=-\nabla^{2}(\Delta H)+P_{3}^{2}(A)+P_{5}^{0}(A)

\displaystyle=-\Delta^{2}A+P_{3}^{2}(A)+P_{5}^{0}(A).

This proves (
2.18
).

We now proceed by induction on
m
and start with
m=1
.

It follows from (
2.17
), (
2.18
) and (
2.21
) that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\nabla A

\displaystyle=\nabla\big(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}A\big)+\big(P_{2}^{3}(A)+P_{4}^{1}(A)\big)\star A

\displaystyle=-\nabla(\Delta^{2}A)+P_{3}^{3}(A)+P_{5}^{1}(A)

\displaystyle=-\Delta^{2}\nabla A+P_{3}^{3}(A)+P_{5}^{1}(A).

Assume that
m\geq 2
and it holds for
m-1
; i.e.

(2.22)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\nabla^{m-1}A=-\Delta^{2}\nabla^{m-1}A+P_{3}^{m+1}(A)+P_{5}^{m-1}(A).

Then using (
2.17
), (
2.18
) and (
2.22
), we obtain

(2.23)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\nabla^{m}A

\displaystyle=\nabla\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\nabla^{m-1}A+\big(P_{2}^{3}(A)+P_{4}^{1}(A)\big)\star\nabla^{m-1}A

\displaystyle=-\nabla\big(\Delta^{2}\nabla^{m-1}A\big)+P_{3}^{m+2}(A)+P_{5}^{m}(A))

\displaystyle=-\Delta^{2}\nabla^{m}A+P_{3}^{m+2}(A)+P_{5}^{m}(A).

∎

For simplicity, we denote
M_{t}=f_{t}(M^{n})
by
M
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Suppose that
\eta\in C^{2}\left(M\times\left[0,T\right]\right)
and
s
is an integer with
s\geq 4
. Let
A
be a solution to
the flow (
1.5
) in
M\times[0,T]
. Then

(2.24)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{M}\left|\nabla^{m}A\right|^{2}\eta^{s}d\mu+\int_{M}\left|\nabla^{m+2}A\right|^{2}\eta^{s}d\mu

\displaystyle\quad\leq s\int_{M}\left|\nabla^{m}A\right|^{2}\eta^{s-1}\frac{\partial\eta}{\partial t}d\mu+C\int_{M}\left|\nabla^{m}A\right|^{2}\eta^{s-4}\left(\left|\nabla\eta\right|^{4}+\eta^{2}\left|\nabla^{2}\eta\right|^{2}\right)d\mu

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{M}\eta^{s}\Big[P_{3}^{m+2}(A)+P_{5}^{m}(A)\Big]\star\,\nabla^{m}A\,d\mu\mbox{,}

for each
m\in\mathbb{N^{+}}
, where
C=C\left(s\right)
.

Proof.

Using (
2.8
), (
2.13
) and (
2.23
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{M}\left|\nabla^{m}A\right|^{2}\eta^{s}d\mu=

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left|\nabla^{m}A\right|^{2}\frac{\partial\eta^{s}}{\partial t}d\mu-2\int_{M}\left<\Delta^{2}\nabla^{m}A,\nabla^{m}A\right>\eta^{s}d\mu

\displaystyle+\int_{M}\Big[P_{3}^{m+2}(A)+P_{5}^{m}(A)\Big]\star\nabla^{m}A\,\eta^{s}\,d\mu.

Integrating by parts and changing derivatives by (
2.6
) yield

\displaystyle-\int_{M}\left<\Delta^{2}\nabla^{m}A,\nabla^{m}A\right>\eta^{s}d\mu=\int_{M}\left<\nabla\Delta\nabla^{m}A,\nabla(\nabla^{m}A\eta^{s})\right>d\mu

\displaystyle=-\int_{M}|\nabla^{m+2}A|^{2}\eta^{s}d\mu+\int_{M}\left<\nabla^{m+2}A,\nabla^{m}A\nabla^{2}(\eta^{s})\right>d\mu

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{M}\left<\nabla^{m+2}A,\nabla^{m+1}A\nabla(\eta^{s})\right>d\mu-\int_{M}\left<\nabla(A\star A\star\nabla^{m+1}A),\nabla^{m}A\right>\eta^{s}d\mu

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}|\nabla^{m+2}A|^{2}\eta^{s}d\mu+\int_{M}P_{3}^{m+2}(A)\star\nabla^{m}A\,\eta^{s}\,d\mu

\displaystyle+C\int_{M}\left|\nabla^{m}A\right|^{2}\eta^{s-4}\left(\left|\nabla\eta\right|^{4}+\eta^{2}\left|\nabla^{2}\eta\right|^{2}\right)d\mu,

where we used

\displaystyle\int_{M}|\nabla^{m+1}A|^{2}\eta^{s-2}|\nabla\eta|^{2}d\mu

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{M}|\nabla^{m}A||\nabla^{m+2}A|\eta^{s-2}|\nabla\eta|^{2}d\mu

\displaystyle+C\int_{M}\left|\nabla^{m}A\right|^{2}\eta^{s-4}\left(\left|\nabla\eta\right|^{4}+\eta^{2}\left|\nabla^{2}\eta\right|^{2}\right)d\mu.

This proves our claim.
∎

3.
Two local energy inequalities on
\nabla^{2}A

In this section, we will establish an extension of the Michael-Simon-Sobolev inequality to obtain two local energy inequalities on
M
.

At first, let us recall the well-known
Michael-Simon Sobolev inequality in
[
27
]
: For any
v\in C^{1}_{c}\left(M\right)
, we have

(3.1)

\displaystyle\left(\int_{M}|v|^{\frac{n}{n-1}}d\mu\right)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\leq C\left(\int_{M}\left|\nabla v\right|d\mu+\int_{M}\left|H\right|\left|v\right|d\mu\right)

for a constant
C>0
.
Taking
v=|u|^{\frac{p(n-1)}{n-p}}
with
1\leq p<n
and using Hölder’s inequality, we have

(3.2)

\displaystyle\left(\int_{M}|u|^{\frac{pn}{n-p}}d\mu\right)^{\frac{n-p}{n}}\leq C\int_{M}\left(\left|\nabla u\right|^{p}+\left|H\right|^{p}\left|u\right|^{p}\right)d\mu

for any
u\in C^{1}_{c}\left(M\right)
.

Applying the Michael-Simon Sobolev inequality (
3.2
) with
p=2
, we obtain

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\eta\in C^{2}(M;\mathbb{R})
be a function with compact support in
M
and
n>2
.
Then there exists a constant
C
such that for any
u\in C^{2}(M)
, we have

(3.3)

\displaystyle\int_{M}|A||u|\eta^{4}d\mu\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\int_{[\eta>0]}\left|A\right|^{n}d\mu\right)^{1/n}\int_{M}\left(|\nabla u|+|A||u|\right)\eta^{4}d\mu

\displaystyle+C\left(\int_{[\eta>0]}\left|A\right|^{n}d\mu\right)^{1/n}\int_{M}|u|\eta^{3}|\nabla\eta|d\mu,

(3.4)

\displaystyle\int_{M}|A|^{2}|u|^{2}\eta^{4}d\mu\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\int_{[\eta>0]}\left|A\right|^{n}d\mu\right)^{2/n}\int_{M}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}+|A|^{2}|u|^{2}\right)\eta^{4}d\mu

\displaystyle+C\left(\int_{[\eta>0]}\left|A\right|^{n}d\mu\right)^{2/n}\int_{M}|u|^{2}\eta^{2}|\nabla\eta|^{2}d\mu.

Proof.

Using Hölder’s inequality and (
3.1
), we obtain

(3.5)

\displaystyle\int_{M}|A||u|\eta^{4}d\mu\leq\left(\int_{[\eta>0]}\left|A\right|^{n}d\mu\right)^{1/n}\left(\int_{M}|u\eta^{4}|^{\frac{n}{n-1}}d\mu\right)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{[\eta>0]}\left|A\right|^{n}d\mu\right)^{1/n}\left(\int_{M}|\nabla u|\eta^{2}+|A||u|\eta^{4}\right)d\mu

\displaystyle\quad+C\left(\int_{[\eta>0]}\left|A\right|^{n}d\mu\right)^{1/n}\int_{M}|u|\eta^{3}|\nabla\eta|d\mu.

Using Hölder’s inequality and (
3.2
), we obtain

(3.6)

\displaystyle\int_{M}|A|^{2}|u|^{2}\eta^{4}d\mu\leq\left(\int_{[\eta>0]}\left|A\right|^{n}d\mu\right)^{2/n}\left(\int_{M}|u\eta^{2}|^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}d\mu\right)^{\frac{n-2}{n}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{[\eta>0]}\left|A\right|^{n}d\mu\right)^{2/n}\left(\int_{M}|\nabla(u\eta^{2})|^{2}+|H|^{2}|u|^{2}\eta^{4}\right)d\mu

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{[\eta>0]}\left|A\right|^{n}d\mu\right)^{2/n}\left(\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}\eta^{4}+|A|^{2}|u|^{2}\eta^{4}\right)d\mu

\displaystyle\quad+C\left(\int_{[\eta>0]}\left|A\right|^{n}d\mu\right)^{2/n}\int_{M}|u|^{2}\eta^{2}|\nabla\eta|^{2}d\mu.

∎

As applications of Lemma
3.1
, we obtain

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\eta\in C^{2}(M;\mathbb{R})
be a function with compact support in
M
.
Then, for
n>2
, there exists a constant
C
such that

(3.7)

\displaystyle\int_{M}(|A|^{2}|\nabla A|^{2}+|A|^{6})\eta^{4}d\mu

\displaystyle\sup_{x_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}}\sum_{i=1}^{L_{x_{0}}}\left(\int_{M_{T}\cap B_{R}(x_{0,i})}\frac{\left|{\nabla^{m}A(T)}\right|^{2}}{R^{n-2m-2}}d\mu\right)^{n/2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{m}\sup_{x_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}}\int_{M_{0}\cap B_{2R_{0}}({x_{0}})}\left|A_{0}\right|^{n}d\mu_{0}\leq C_{m}\varepsilon_{0}.

In view of (
5.35
) and (
5.39
), we apply the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (
5.10
) to obtain

(5.40)

\displaystyle\int_{M_{T}\cap B_{R_{0}}(x_{0})}|A(T)|^{n}d\mu\leq\sum_{i=1}^{L_{x_{0}}}\int_{M_{T}\cap B_{\frac{R}{2}}({x_{0}}_{i})}|A(T)|^{n}d\mu

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\sum_{i=1}^{L_{x_{0}}}\|A(T)\|^{n(1-\frac{n-2}{2[\frac{n}{2}]})}_{L^{2}(M_{T}\cap B_{R}({x_{0}}_{i}))}\sum_{m=0}^{[\frac{n}{2}]}\left\|\frac{\nabla^{m}A(T)}{R^{[\frac{n}{2}]-m}}\right\|^{\frac{n(n-2)}{2[\frac{n}{2}]}}_{L^{2}(M_{T}\cap B_{R}({x_{0}}_{i}))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\sum_{i=1}^{L_{x_{0}}}\left(\left\|\frac{A(T)}{R^{\frac{n-2}{2}}}\right\|^{n}_{L^{2}(M_{T}\cap B_{2R}({x_{0}}_{i}))}+\sum_{m=0}^{[\frac{n}{2}]}\left\|\frac{\nabla^{m}A(T)}{R^{\frac{n-2}{2}-m}}\right\|^{n}_{L^{2}(M_{T}\cap B_{2R}({x_{0}}_{i}))}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{n}\sup_{x_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}}\int_{M_{0}\cap B_{2R_{0}}({x_{0}})}\left|A_{0}\right|^{n}d\mu_{0}\leq LC_{n}\varepsilon_{0}\leq\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon.

Similarly, noting that
l=[\frac{n}{2}]+1>\frac{n}{2}
and
T=\delta R^{4}
, we obtain

(5.41)

\displaystyle T^{\frac{1}{4}}\|A(T)\|_{L^{\infty}(M_{T}\cap B_{R}(x))}

\displaystyle\leq CR\|A(T)\|^{\frac{2l-n}{2l}}_{L^{2}(M_{T}\cap B_{2R}(x))}\sum_{m=0}^{l}\left\|\frac{\nabla^{m}A(T)}{R^{l-m}}\right\|^{\frac{n}{2l}}_{L^{2}(M_{T}\cap B_{2R}(x))}

\displaystyle\leq C_{l}\varepsilon_{0}\leq\frac{1}{2}C_{1}^{1/4}

for a sufficiently small
\varepsilon_{0}
.

By a similar proof to (
5.41
), we obtain

(5.42)

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla^{m}A(T)\right\|_{L^{\infty}(M_{T}\cap B_{R}(x)}\leq C\big(m,R,\left\|A_{0}\right\|_{L^{n}(M_{0}\cap B_{2R_{0}}(x))}\big)\mbox{.}

It follows from (
5.40
)-(
5.42
) that
A(T)
is smooth and
T
is not the maximal time satisfying (
5.32
)-(
5.33
). Therefore
T\geq\frac{\delta R_{0}^{4}}{4}
. This proves Theorem
1.2
.
∎

6.
The Willmore flow in higher dimensions

In this section, we investigate the Willmore flow in higher dimensions (i.e.
n\geq 2
).

At first, we have

Lemma 6.1
.

For each
\varepsilon>0
, let
f:M^{n}\times(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be a smooth family of compactly
supported immersion of a hypersurface
M^{n}
evolving with normal velocity
\delta f=F\nu
, where
\delta=(d/d\varepsilon)|_{\varepsilon=0}
is the variational derivative operator. Then

\displaystyle\delta g_{ij}

\displaystyle=-2FA_{ij},\quad\delta g^{ij}=2FA^{ij},\quad\delta\nu=-\nabla F\mbox{,}

\displaystyle\delta d\mu

\displaystyle=-HF~d\mu,\quad\delta A_{ij}=\nabla_{ij}F-FA_{jk}A_{il}g^{kl}\mbox{.}

Using these evolution equations in Lemma 6.1, the first variation formula for the Willmore energy
functional of of a hypersurface
M
in the normal direction is:

Lemma 6.2
.

The first variation of the Willmore functional is given respectively by:

(6.1)

\displaystyle\delta{\mathcal{W}(f)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\delta\int_{M}|A|^{2}d\mu=\int_{M}F\Big\{\Delta_{M^{n}}H-\frac{1}{2}H|A|^{2}+C(A)\Big\}d\mu,

where
C(A)=g^{ij}g^{kl}g^{mn}A_{ik}A_{lm}A_{nj}
.

Proof.

Using the above evolution equations and the divergence
theorem, we compute

\displaystyle\delta{\mathcal{W}(f)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}\Big\{2Fg^{kl}A^{ij}A_{ik}A_{jl}+2g^{ij}g^{kl}A_{jl}\nabla_{ik}F-FH|A|^{2}\Big\}d\mu

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}\Big\{-2g^{ij}g^{kl}\nabla_{i}A_{jl}\nabla_{k}F+F(2g^{kl}A^{ij}A_{ik}A_{jl}-H|A|^{2})\Big\}d\mu

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}\Big\{-2g^{kl}\nabla_{l}(g^{ij}A_{ij})\nabla_{k}F+F(2C(A)-H|A|^{2})\Big\}d\mu

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}\Big\{-2g^{kl}\nabla_{l}H\nabla_{k}F+F(2C(A)-H|A|^{2})\Big\}d\mu

\displaystyle=\int_{M}F\Big\{\Delta_{M^{n}}H-\frac{1}{2}H|A|^{2}+C(A)\Big\}d\mu,

where we have used integration by part and the Codazzi equation.
∎

Then it follows from (
6.1
) that the Euler-Lagrange equation on the Willmore functional (
1.10
) is

(6.2)

\displaystyle\Delta_{M}H-\frac{1}{2}H|A|^{2}+C(A)=0.

Remark 3
.

When
n=2
, we may choose normal coordinates at
p\in M
such that
g_{ij}(p)=\delta_{ij}
and the matrix of the Weingarten map is diagonal. Let
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
be the two principal curvatures. Then

\displaystyle H=\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2},\quad|A|^{2}=\lambda_{1}^{2}+\lambda_{2}^{2},\quad|A^{0}|^{2}=\frac{1}{2}(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})^{2}.

Thus

\displaystyle C(A)=(A_{11})^{3}+(A_{22})^{3}=\lambda_{1}^{3}+\lambda_{2}^{3}=\frac{1}{2}H(|A|^{2}+2|A^{0}|^{2}).

Consequently, the equation (
6.2
) for
n=2
agrees with the 2-dimensional Willmore equation in
[
23
]
as follows:

\Delta_{M^{2}}H+H|A^{0}|^{2}=0.

Now, we recall the Willmore flow for hypersurfaces in higher dimensions

(6.3)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}=-\left(\Delta_{M_{t}}H-\frac{1}{2}|A|^{2}H+C(A)\right)\nu

with initial value
f\left(\cdot,0\right)=f_{0}
.
Then it follows from Lemma
6.2
that

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
f
be a smooth solution of the flow (
6.3
) in
[0,T]
. Then, for each
t\in[0,T]
, we have

(6.4)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{M_{t}}|A(t)|^{2}d\mu dt+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{M_{\tau}}(|\Delta_{M_{\tau}}H-\frac{1}{2}H|A|^{2}+C(A)|^{2})d\mu d\tau=\frac{1}{2}\int_{M_{0}}|A(0)|^{2}d\mu.

The same proof of Theorem
1.2
yields

Theorem 6.4
.

Let
f_{0}:M^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be a smooth immersion with
n\geq 2
.
Assume that there are two absolute positive constants
\varepsilon_{0}
and
\varepsilon
such
that

(6.5)

\displaystyle\int_{M_{0}\cap B_{2R_{0}}(x)}\left|A_{0}\right|^{n}d\mu_{0}\leq\varepsilon_{0}<\varepsilon

for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and some fixed
R_{0}>0
. Then the maximal
existence time
T
of the flow (
6.3
) satisfies

T\geq\frac{1}{C}R_{0}^{4}

for a constant
C
.
Moreover, for
0\leq t\leq\frac{1}{C}R_{0}^{4}
, we have

\int_{M_{t}\cap B_{R_{0}}(x)}\left|A(t)\right|^{n}d\mu\leq C\varepsilon_{0}\mbox{.}

Now we complete the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Let
f_{0}:M^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be a smooth immersion with
n\geq 3
.

As an application of Theorem
6.4
, there exists a global existence of a smooth solution to the Willmore flow (
1.12
) for
t\in[0,T_{1}]
with
T_{1}\geq CR_{0}^{4}
. Let
f(\cdot,t)
be the smooth solution to the Willmore flow (
1.12
) in
[0,T]
, where
T
is the maximal time such that

(6.6)

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T,\,x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}}\int_{M_{t}\cap B_{R_{0}}(x)}|A(t)|^{n}\ d\mu\leq\varepsilon

and

(6.7)

\displaystyle\delta R_{0}^{4}\sup_{T-\delta R_{0}^{4}\leq t\leq T}\|A(t)\|^{4}_{L^{\infty}(M_{t})}\leq C_{1}

for a constant
C_{1}>0
and a sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
.
Using the same proof in Lemma
4.2
, for any
t\in[0,T]
, we have

(6.8)

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}}\mu\left(M_{t}\cap B_{R_{0}}(x)\right)\leq CR_{0}^{n}.

Similarly to (
5.40
), it follows from using (
6.4
) that

\displaystyle\quad\|A(T)\|^{n}_{L^{n}(M_{T}\cap B_{R_{0}}(x))}

\displaystyle\leq C\|A(T)\|^{n(1-\frac{n-2}{2[\frac{n}{2}]})}_{L^{2}(M_{T}\cap B_{2R_{0}}(x))}\sum_{m=0}^{[\frac{n}{2}]}\left\|\frac{\nabla^{m}A(T)}{R_{0}^{[\frac{n}{2}]-m}}\right\|^{\frac{n(n-2)}{2[\frac{n}{2}]}}_{L^{2}(M_{T}\cap B_{2R_{0}}(x))}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\frac{A(0)}{R_{0}^{\frac{n-2}{2}}}\right\|^{n(1-\frac{n-2}{2[\frac{n}{2}]})}_{L^{2}(M_{0})}\sum_{m=0}^{[\frac{n}{2}]}\left\|\frac{\nabla^{m}A(T)}{R_{0}^{\frac{n-2}{2}-m}}\right\|^{\frac{n(n-2)}{2[\frac{n}{2}]}}_{L^{2}(M_{T}\cap B_{2R_{0}}(x))}

\displaystyle\leq C_{n}\varepsilon_{0}^{\frac{n(n-2)}{2[\frac{n}{2}]}}\varepsilon^{\frac{n(n-2)}{2[\frac{n}{2}]}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon

for a sufficiently small
\varepsilon_{0}
. Similarly to (
5.41
), choosing
m=[\frac{n}{2}]+1>\frac{n}{2}
, we obtain

(6.9)

\displaystyle\delta^{\frac{1}{4}}R_{0}\|A(T)\|_{L^{\infty}(M_{T}\cap B_{R_{0}}(x)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{m}R_{0}\|A(T)\|^{\frac{2m-n}{2m}}_{L^{2}(M_{T}\cap B_{2R_{0}}(x)}\sum_{k=0}^{m}\left\|\frac{\nabla^{k}A(T)}{R_{0}^{m-k}}\right\|^{\frac{n}{2m}}_{L^{2}(M_{T}\cap B_{2R_{0}}(x)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}C_{1}^{1/4}

for a sufficiently small
\varepsilon_{0}
.

This shows that any
T>0
is not the maximal time satisfying (
6.6
)-(
6.7
).
Therefore, there exists a global smooth solution to the Willmore flow (
1.12
) for
t\in[0,\infty)
. Using (
6.9
), we have for each
l\geq 1

(6.10)

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla^{l}A(t)\right\|_{L^{\infty}(M_{t}\cap B_{R_{0}}(x))}\leq C\big(l,R_{0},\varepsilon\big)\mbox{.}

When
n=2
, Kuwert and Schätzle in Theorem 4.2 of
[
23
]
proved a localized version of the result of Langer
[
26
]
and completed their result as
t\to\infty
.
When
n>2
, Naff in his appendix in Section 4 of
[
28
]
proved a localized version of the result of Langer to higher dimension.
More clearly,
applying Proposition 4.2 of
[
28
]
(also
[
17
]
) to (
6.10
),
there exist points
x_{k}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
and diffeomorphisms
\phi_{k}:M^{n}(t_{k})\to U_{k}\subset M^{n}
such that as
t_{k}\to\infty
(up to a subsequence),

f(\phi_{k}(p),t_{k})-x_{k}\to f_{\infty}

locally in
C^{k}
on
M^{n}
as in
[
23
]
. On
M^{n}
, we set

\tilde{f}_{k}(p,t)=f_{k}(\phi_{k}(p),t_{k}+t)-x_{k},

which is a solution of the Willmore flow on
\tilde{M}_{k}
.
Then as
t\to\infty
,
\tilde{f}_{k}(t)
converges to a solution
\tilde{f}_{\infty}
of the Willmore flow.
It follows from Lemma
6.4
that
\int_{M}|A(t)|^{2}d\mu
is increasing and
\lim_{t\to\infty}\int_{M}|A(t)|^{2}d\mu
exists. Then

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\tilde{M}_{k}}|\Delta_{M_{t}}H_{\tilde{f}_{k}}-\frac{1}{2}H_{\tilde{f}_{k}}|A_{\tilde{f}_{k}}|^{2}+C(A_{\tilde{f}_{k}})|^{2}d\mu_{k}\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k}+1}\int_{M_{t}}|\Delta_{M_{t}}H-\frac{1}{2}H|A|^{2}+C(A)|^{2}d\mu\,dt\to 0.

This shows that
f_{\infty}
is a smooth solution of the Willmore equation (
1.11
).
This proves Theorem
1.3
.
∎

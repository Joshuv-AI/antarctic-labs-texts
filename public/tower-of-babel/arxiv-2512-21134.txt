Now let us briefly examine the elements in

Remark 1.1

Every element in

\rho=\begin{pmatrix}A_{1}&\dots&A_{p}\\

has the following properties:

(i)

;

(ii)

Note that when multiplying two isotone maps or two antitone maps, the result remains isotone. In contrast, the product of an isotone map with an antitone map yields an antitone map. Similarly, when an antitone map is multiplied by an isotone map, the outcome is antitone as well.

(iii)

It should also be emphasized that all elements in

Lemma 1.2

Green’s and starred Green’s relations

The five Green’s relations defined on a semigroup that usually contains idempotents are
\mathcal{L}
,
\mathcal{R}
,
\mathcal{D}
,
\mathcal{J}
, and
\mathcal{H}

(g,h)\in\mathcal{L}

S^{1}gS^{1}=S^{1}hS^{1}

\mathcal{D}
is the “join” of
\mathcal{R}
and
\mathcal{L}
, and
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{L}\cap\mathcal{R}
. However, if
S
is finite, the relations
\mathcal{J}
and

are the same, as shown in [
[
12
]

,

,

[

]

3
Rank properties

For a semigroup
S
and a nonempty subset of
S
, say
T
, the
smallest subsemigroup
of
S
that contains
T
is denoted by
\langle T\rangle
and is referred to as the
subsemigroup generated by
T
. If
T
is a finite subset of
S
such that
\langle T\rangle
equals
S
, then
S
is said to be a
finitely-generated semigroup
. The
rank
of a finitely generated semigroup
S
is defined and denoted as:

\text{rank}(S)=\min\{|T|:\langle T\rangle=S\}.

If the set
A
consists only of idempotents in
S
, then
S
is said to be an
idempotent-generated semigroup
(equivalently, a
semiband
), and the idempotent-rank is denoted by
\text{idrank}(S)
. For a more comprehensive discussion on the rank properties of a semigroup, we direct the reader to
[
14
,
15
]
. There are several categories of transformation semigroups whose ranks have been studied; see, for instance,
[
8
,
9
,
10
,
13
,
14
,
15
,
16
,
30
]
. The work in
[
3
]
examines the rank of the Schröder monoid
\mathcal{LS}_{n}
(by
anti isomorphism
), whereas the authors in
[
22
]
(by
anti isomorphism
) computed the ranks of its certain two-sided ideals and their corresponding Rees quotients. In fact, it has been demonstrated that this semigroup is idempotent-generated in
[
7
]
. Our objective is to determine the rank of the two-sided ideal
I(n,\,p)
of the monoid
\mathcal{DORP}_{n}
, which will consequently provide the rank of the monoid
\mathcal{DORP}_{n}
as a specific case.

We commence our study of the rank properties of the semigroup

We immediately present the following lemma.

a_{p}=\min(\mathop{\rm Dom}\nolimits\,\delta)
is a fixed point of
\delta
. Therefore,
\delta
is the unique vital element in
L^{*}_{\rho}
.
∎

The next lemma is concerned with the factorization of injective antitone maps in the monoid
\mathcal{DORP}_{n}
with convex domains.

Lemma 3.4
.

Every injective antitone element
\rho\in\mathcal{DORP}_{n}
with a convex domain is a product of idempotent(s) and the unique vital element that belongs to
{L}^{*}_{\rho}
.

Proof.

Consider an injective antitone map of height
p
with a convex domain, say
\rho
, in
\mathcal{DORP}_{n}
of the form:

\rho=\begin{pmatrix}t&t+1&\cdots&t+p-1\\
a_{p}&a_{p-1}&\cdots&a_{1}\end{pmatrix},

where
2\leq p\leq\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil
and
1\leq a_{1}<\cdots<a_{p}\leq t<\cdots<t+p-1\leq n
for some
t\in\{2,\ldots,n-p+1\}
.

Now, for
1\leq i\leq p
, define

\varepsilon_{i}=\begin{pmatrix}a_{p}&a_{p}+1&\cdots&a_{p}+i-2&\{a_{p}+i-1,t+i-1\}&t+i&\cdots&t+p-1\\
a_{p}&a_{p}+1&\cdots&a_{p}+i-2&a_{p}+i-1&t+i&\cdots&t+p-1\end{pmatrix},

\epsilon=\begin{pmatrix}t&t+1&\cdots&t+p-1\\
t&t+1&\cdots&t+p-1\end{pmatrix}\text{ and }\delta=\begin{pmatrix}a_{p}&a_{p}+1&\cdots&a_{p}+p-1\\
a_{p}&a_{p-1}&\cdots&a_{1}\end{pmatrix}.

Notice that
a_{p}\leq t
, which implies that
a_{p}+i-1\leq t+i-1
for all
1\leq i\leq p
, and so
\varepsilon_{i}
is an isotone idempotent of height
p
in
\mathcal{DORP}_{n}
. Moreover, it is clear that
a_{1}<\cdots<a_{p}<a_{p}+1<\cdots<a_{p}+p-1
. Thus,
\delta
is an antitone injective map with a convex domain that fixes
a_{p}=\min(\mathop{\rm Dom}\nolimits\,\delta)
. Hence,
\delta
is a vital element. Furthermore,
\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\,\rho=\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\,\delta
, which implies that
\delta\in L^{*}_{\rho}
. It is also clear that
\epsilon
is an idempotent in
\mathcal{DORP}_{n}
of height
p
.

Now it is not difficult to see that:

\displaystyle\epsilon\varepsilon_{1}\varepsilon_{2}\cdots\varepsilon_{p}\delta=

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}t&t+1&\cdots&t+p-1\\
t&t+1&\cdots&t+p-1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\{a_{p},t\}&t+1&\cdots&t+p-1\\
a_{p}&t+1&\cdots&t+p-1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}a_{p}&\{a_{p}+1,t+1\}&t+{2}&\cdots&t+p-1\\
a_{p}&a_{p}+1&t+{2}&\cdots&t+p-1\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle\cdots\begin{pmatrix}a_{p}&a_{p}+1&\cdots&a_{p}+p-2&\{a_{p}+p-1,t+p-1\}\\
a_{p}&a_{p}+1&\cdots&a_{p}+p-2&a_{p}+p-1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}a_{p}&a_{p}+1&\cdots&a_{p}+p-1\\
a_{p}&a_{p-1}&\cdots&a_{1}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}t&t+1&\cdots&t+p-1\\
a_{p}&a_{p-1}&\cdots&a_{1}\end{pmatrix}=\rho,

as required. The result now follows.
∎

We now present the following theorem.

Theorem 3.5
.

The monoid
\mathcal{DORP}_{n}
is generated by idempotent(s) and vital elements.

Proof.

Let
\rho\in\mathcal{DORP}_{n}
. Then,
\rho
is either an isotone map or an antitone map.

(i.)
If
\rho
is an isotone map, then
\rho\in\mathcal{LS}_{n}
. Thus, by [
[
7
]
, Theorem 14.4.5],
\rho
is idempotent-generated.

(ii.)
Now suppose
\rho
is an antitone map of the form

\rho=\begin{pmatrix}A_{1}&\cdots&A_{p}\\
a_{p}&\cdots&a_{1}\end{pmatrix}

with
2\leq p\leq\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil
. Now let
t_{i}=\min A_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq p
and suppose
s_{i+1}=t_{i+1}-t_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq p-1
. Next, let
T_{i+1}=\{t_{1}+1,t_{1}+2,\ldots,t_{1}+s_{2}+s_{3}+\cdots+s_{i+1}\}
be a convex set. Observe that:

\displaystyle t_{1}+s_{2}+s_{3}+\cdots+s_{i+1}

\displaystyle=t_{1}+(t_{2}-t_{1})+(t_{3}-t_{2})+\cdots+

\displaystyle(t_{i}-t_{i-1})+(t_{i+1}-t_{i})

\displaystyle=t_{i+1}=\min A_{i+1}.

This shows that for each
1\leq i\leq p-1
,
t_{i+1}=\max T_{i+1}
. Thus,

T_{i+1}=\{t_{1}+1,\,t_{1}+2,\,\ldots,\,t_{1}+s_{2}+s_{3}+\cdots+s_{i+1}\}=\{t_{1}+1,\,t_{1}+2,\,\ldots,\,t_{i+1}\}.

So, the maps

\epsilon=\begin{pmatrix}A_{1}&\cdots&A_{p}\\
t_{1}&\cdots&t_{p}\end{pmatrix}

and

\xi_{i}=\begin{pmatrix}t_{1}&\cdots&t_{1}+i-1&\{t_{1}+i,\ldots,t_{i+1}\}&t_{i+2}&\cdots&t_{p}\\
t_{1}&\cdots&t_{1}+i-1&t_{1}+i&t_{i+2}&\cdots&t_{p}\end{pmatrix},

are idempotents of height
p
in
\mathcal{DORP}_{n}
for all
1\leq i\leq p
.

Notice also that since
a_{p}\leq t_{1}
, it follows that
a_{p}\leq t_{1}<t_{1}+1<\cdots<t_{1}+p-1
. Therefore, the map
\sigma
defined as

\sigma=\begin{pmatrix}t_{1}&t_{1}+1&\cdots&t_{1}+p-1\\
a_{p}&a_{p-1}&\cdots&a_{1}\end{pmatrix}

is an antitone map with a convex domain, and so by Lemma
3.4
,
\sigma
is generated by idempotent(s) and the unique vital element in
L^{*}_{\sigma}
.

Now observe that

\displaystyle\epsilon\xi_{1}\cdots\xi_{p}\sigma=

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}A_{1}&\cdots&A_{p}\\
t_{1}&\cdots&t_{p}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}t_{1}&\{t_{1}+1,t_{1}+2,\ldots,t_{2}\}&t_{3}&\cdots&t_{p}\\
t_{1}&t_{1}+1&t_{3}&\cdots&t_{p}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}t_{1}&t_{1}+1&\{t_{1}+2,t_{1}+3,\ldots,t_{3}\}&t_{4}&\cdots&t_{p}\\
t_{1}&t_{1}+1&t_{1}+{2}&t_{4}&\cdots&t_{p}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle\cdots\begin{pmatrix}t_{1}&t_{1}+1&\cdots&t_{1}+p-2&\{t_{1}+p-1,\ldots,t_{p}\}\\
t_{1}&t_{1}+1&\cdots&t_{1}+p-2&t_{1}+p-1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}t_{1}&t_{1}+1&\cdots&t_{1}+p-1\\
a_{p}&a_{p-1}&\cdots&a_{1}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}A_{1}&A_{2}&\cdots&A_{p}\\
a_{p}&a_{p-1}&\cdots&a_{1}\end{pmatrix}=\rho,

as required. Notice that
\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\,\rho=\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\,\sigma
, and so
L^{*}_{\sigma}=L^{*}_{\rho}
.
Hence,
\rho
is generated by idempotents and the unique vital element in

. The result now follows.
∎

We will now outline the next lemma.

Lemma 3.7
.

Every non-convex vital element is a product of a convex vital element and idempotent element(s).

Proof.

Let
\delta
be a non-convex vital element of height
2\leq p\leq\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil
, as expressed in (
13
). This means that there exists
0\leq i\leq p-2
such that
\{y_{p-i},y_{p-i+1},\ldots,y_{p}\}
is convex and
y_{p-(i+1)}-y_{p-i}>1
. That is to say,

\{y_{p-i},\,y_{p-i+1},\,\ldots,\,y_{p-1},\,y_{p}\}=\{(y_{p-i}=)y_{p}-i,\,y_{p}-i+1,\,\ldots,\,y_{p}-1,\,y_{p}\}.

As such
\delta
is of the form

\delta=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc|ccc}y_{p}&y_{p}+1&\cdots&y_{p}+i-1&y_{p}+i&y_{p}+i+1&\cdots&y_{p}+p-1\\
y_{p}&y_{p}-1&\cdots&y_{p}-i+1&y_{p}-i&y_{p-(i+1)}&\cdots&y_{1}\end{array}\right).

This means that the subset (of
\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\,\delta
)
\{y_{1},\dots,y_{p-(i+1)}\}
needs not be convex. Notice that

\{y_{p-i}-1,\,y_{p-i}-2,\,\ldots,\,y_{p-i}-(p-i-2),\,y_{p-i}-(p-i-1)\}

is a convex set consisting of translates of
y_{p-i}
, which can be replaced with
y_{p}-i
(since
y_{p-i}=y_{p}-i
) as follows:

\{y_{p}-i-1,\,y_{p}-i-2,\,\ldots,\,y_{p}-p+2,\,\,y_{p}-p+1\}.

Thus, the set
\{y_{p}-p+1,\,y_{p}-p+2,\,\ldots,y_{p}-i-1,\,y_{p}-i,\,y_{p}-i+1,\,\ldots,\,y_{p}-1,\,y_{p}\}
is convex. Therefore, the map
\delta^{*}
defined as

\delta^{*}_{p,\,i}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc|cccc}y_{p}&y_{p}+1&\cdots&y_{p}+i-1&y_{p}+i&y_{p}+i+1&\cdots&y_{p}+p-1\\
y_{p}&y_{p}-1&\cdots&y_{p}-i+1&y_{p}-i&y_{p}-i-1&\cdots&y_{p}-p+1\end{array}\right)

is a convex vital element. Additionally, the map
\gamma
defined as

\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}y_{p}-p+1&y_{p}-p+2&\cdots&y_{p}-i-1&y_{p}-i&y_{p}-i+1&\cdots&y_{p}-1&y_{p}\\
y_{1}&y_{2}&\cdots&y_{p-i-1}&y_{p-i}&y_{p-i+1}&\cdots&y_{p-1}&y_{p}\end{pmatrix}

is an injective decreasing isotone map that is idempotent-generated by [
[
7
]
, Theorem 14.4.5]. Now, clearly,
\delta^{*}_{p,\,i}\gamma=\delta
. The result now follows.
∎

Now, for

(ii)

(i.)
Notice that the domain of each convex vital element of height

has its minimum element, say

(ii.)
The result follows from Remark

Next, we have the following theorem.

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
\mathcal{DORP}_{n}
be as defined in (

). Then the number of convex vital elements of height

in

By summing the convex vital elements in Lemma

Now, let

G(p)=\begin{cases}E(RQ_{n}(p)\setminus\{\textbf{0}\}),&\text{if }p\in\{1,\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil+1,\ldots,n\};\\

The following result demonstrates that the set
G(p)
serves as the minimal generating set for
{RQ}_{n}(p)
.

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
\rho
and
\sigma
be elements of
{RQ}_{n}(p)
. Then
\rho\sigma\in G(p)
if and only if both
\rho
and
\sigma
belong to
G(p)
, and either
\rho\sigma=\rho
or
\rho\sigma=\sigma
.

Proof.

Suppose
\rho\sigma\in G(p)
.

If
p\in\{1,\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil+1,\ldots,n\}
, then
\rho\sigma\in E(RQ_{n}(p)\setminus\{\textbf{0}\})
. Consequently, the result is obtained from [
[
7
]
, Theorem 14.4.5].

Now if
2\leq p\leq\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil
, then either
\rho\sigma\in M(p)
or
\rho\sigma\in E(RQ_{n}(p)\setminus\{\textbf{0}\})
.
If
\rho\sigma\in E(RQ_{n}(p)\setminus\{\textbf{0}\})
, then the result follows from [
[
7
]
, Theorem 14.4.5] as well.

Now suppose
\rho\sigma\in M(p)
. Thus,
\rho\sigma
is a convex vital element, and it has the form

\rho\sigma=\begin{pmatrix}i&i+1&\cdots&i+p-1\\
i&i-1&\cdots&i-p+1\end{pmatrix},

where
p\leq i\leq n-p+1
.
This signifies that
\mathop{\rm Dom}\nolimits\,\rho=\mathop{\rm Dom}\nolimits\,\rho\sigma
,
\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\,\sigma=\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\,\rho\sigma
and
\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\,\rho=\mathop{\rm Dom}\nolimits\,\sigma
. Thus,

\rho=\begin{pmatrix}i&{i+1}&\cdots&i+p-1\\
i\rho&(i+1)\rho&\cdots&(i+p-1)\rho\end{pmatrix}\text{ and }\sigma=\begin{pmatrix}i\rho&(i+1)\rho&\cdots&(i+p-1)\rho\\
i&i-1&\cdots&i-p+1\end{pmatrix}.

The assertion is that
\rho
has to be an idempotent. Notice that
\rho
and
\sigma
are decreasing maps. Thus,
i\rho\leq i
and
i=(i\rho)\sigma\leq i\rho
. This ensures that
i\rho=i
. Moreover, for any
i-1\leq j\leq i-p+1
we see that
j\leq(j+2)\rho\leq j+2
. This means that for all
i-1\leq j\leq i-p+1
, either
(j+2)\rho=j
; or
(j+2)\rho=j+1
; or
(j+2)\rho=j+2
.

If
(j+2)\rho=j
, then in particular, for
j=i-2
, we see that
(i-2+2)\rho=i-2
, that is,
i\rho=i-2
, which contradicts the fact that
i\rho=i
.

Now, if
(j+2)\rho=j+1
for all
i-1\leq j\leq i-p+1
, then in particular, for
j=i-1
, we have
(i-1+2)\rho=i-1+1
, that is,
(i+1)\rho=i
, which also contradicts the fact that
i\rho=i
. Hence, we conclude that
(j+2)\rho=j+2
for all
i-1\leq j\leq i-p+1
. This ensures that:

\rho=\begin{pmatrix}i&i+1&\cdots&i+p-1\\
i&i+1&\cdots&i+p-1\end{pmatrix}\text{ and }\sigma=\begin{pmatrix}i&i+1&\cdots&i+p-1\\
i&i-1&\cdots&i-p+1\end{pmatrix}.

Therefore,
\sigma\in M(p)\subset G(p)
and
\rho\in E({RQ}_{n}(p)\setminus\{\textbf{0}\})\subset G(p)
, and also
\rho\sigma=\sigma.

The converse is obvious.
∎

can be written as a product of elements in
G(p+1)
. This means that every idempotent of height
p
can be formed from the product of idempotents of height
p+1
. Notice that idempotents of height
p
in
\mathcal{DORP}_{n}
are also idempotents of height
p
in
\mathcal{LS}_{n}
. Thus, the result follows from [
[
7
]
, Lemma 14.4.2].

ii.
Now suppose
2\leq p\leq\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil
. The idempotents in
G(p)
as products of idempotents in
G(p+1)
have been established by [
[
7
]
, Lemma 14.4.2], and so it is sufficient to show that every convex vital element in
G(p)
can be expressed as a product of vital elements in
G(p+1)\cup\{\delta_{p,\,p},\,\delta_{p,\,n-p+1}\}
.

Let
\delta_{p,\,i}
be a convex vital element of height
p
in
G(p)
, as expressed in (
14
) such that
p+1\leq i\leq n-p
. It follows from Lemma
3.13
that
\delta_{p,\,i}
is a product of a convex vital element and an idempotent element, each of height
p+1
. Notice that the extreme convex vital elements
\delta_{p,\,p}
and
\delta_{p,\,n-p+1}
in
G(p)
, which by Remark
3.14
are not expressible as product of elements in
G(p+1)
, as such, every convex vital element in
G(p)
can be expressed as a product of elements in
G(p+1)\cup\{\delta_{p,\,p},\,\delta_{p,\,n-p+1}\}
. We have now completed the proof of the lemma.
∎

Remark 3.16
.

It is now clear from the above lemma that for
2\leq p\leq\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil
, any minimum generating set of
I(n,\,p)
must contain all the extreme elements of height below
p
, i.e., the elements
\delta_{p,\,i}
and
\delta_{p,\,n-i+1}
for all
2\leq i\leq p-1
.

Now, in
I(n,\,p)
let

W(p)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}E(J^{*}_{p}),&\hbox{if $p=0,1$;}\\
E(J^{*}_{p})\cup M(\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil)\cup\{\delta_{p,\,i},\,\,\delta_{p,\,n-i+1}:\,2\leq i\leq\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil-1\},&\hbox{if $p\in\{\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil+1,\ldots,n-1\}$;}\\
G(p)\cup\{\delta_{p,\,i},\,\,\delta_{p,\,n-i+1}:\,2\leq i\leq p-1\},&\hbox{if $2\leq p\leq\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil$.}\end{array}\right.

(16)

Thus, we have the following lemmas.

Lemma 3.17
.

For
0\leq p\leq n-1
,

W(p)
is the minimum generating set of
I(n,\,p)
.

Proof.

The result follows from the fact that
G(p)
is the minimum generating set of
\langle J^{*}_{p}\rangle
by Lemma
3.10
, and also from the fact that each minimum generating set of the ideal
I(n,\,p)
must contain all the extreme convex vital elements below height
p
, as stated in Remark
3.16
.
∎

Lemma 3.18
.

Let
W(p)
be as defined in (
16
). Then

|W(p)|=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}1,&\hbox{if $p=0$;}\\
2^{n}-1,&\hbox{if $p=1$;}\\
(n-2)+\sum\limits_{r=p}^{n}{\binom{n}{r}}{\binom{r-1}{p-1}},&\hbox{if $p\in\{2,\ldots,n-1\}$.}\end{array}\right.

Proof.

For the cases
p=0
and
p=1
, the result is trivial. Next, if
2\leq p\leq\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil
, then
|W(p)|=|G(p)|+|\{\delta_{p,\,i},\,\,\delta_{p,\,n-i+1}:\,2\leq i\leq p-1\}|
. Thus, by Lemma
3.12
, we see that

|W(p)|=(n-2p+2)+\sum\limits_{r=p}^{n}{\binom{n}{r}}{\binom{r-1}{p-1}}+2(p-2)=(n-2)+\sum\limits_{r=p}^{n}{\binom{n}{r}}{\binom{r-1}{p-1}}.

Finally, if
\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil+1\leq p\leq n-1
, we see that
|W(p)|=|E(J^{*}_{p})|+|M(\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil)|+|\{\delta_{p,\,i},\,\,\delta_{p,\,n-i+1}:\,2\leq i\leq\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil-1\}|
. Thus, by Lemma
3.12
, it follows that

|W(p)|=\sum\limits_{r=p}^{n}{\binom{n}{r}}{\binom{r-1}{p-1}}+(n-2\left\lceil\frac{n}{2}\right\rceil+2)+2\left(\left\lceil\frac{n}{2}\right\rceil-2\right)=(n-2)+\sum\limits_{r=p}^{n}{\binom{n}{r}}{\binom{r-1}{p-1}}.

The result now follows.
∎

Consequently, we have the following result.

Theorem 3.19
.

Let
I(n,\,p)
be as defined in (
5
). Then, for
1\leq p\leq n-1
, we have

\text{rank }I(n,\,p)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}1,&\hbox{if $p=0$;}\\
2^{n}-1,&\hbox{if $p=1$;}\\
(n-2)+\sum\limits_{r=p}^{n}{\binom{n}{r}}{\binom{r-1}{p-1}},&\hbox{if $p\in\{2,\ldots,n-1\}$.}\end{array}\right.

Proof.

It is clear that
\langle E(J^{*}_{0})\rangle=I(n,\,0)
and
\langle E(J^{*}_{1})\rangle=I(n,\,1)
. Thus, it follows from Lemmas
3.17
and
3.18
that
\text{rank }I(n,\,0)=1
and
\text{rank }I(n,\,1)=2^{n}-1
.

Now, for all
2\leq p\leq n-1
, observe that by Lemma
3.15
,
\langle J^{*}_{p}\cup\{\delta_{p,\,i},\,\,\delta_{p,\,n-i+1}:2\leq i\leq p-1\}\rangle=I(n,\,p)
. Notice that if
2\leq p\leq\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil
, then

\langle W(p)\rangle=\langle G(p)\cup\{\delta_{p,\,i},\,\,\delta_{p,\,n-i+1}:2\leq i\leq p-1\}\rangle=\langle J^{*}_{p}\cup\{\delta_{p,\,i},\,\,\delta_{p,\,n-i+1}:2\leq i\leq p-1\}\rangle;

and if
\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil+1\leq p\leq n-1
, then

\langle W(p)\rangle=\langle E(J^{*}_{p})\cup M(\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil)\cup\{\delta_{p,\,i},\,\,\delta_{p,\,n-i+1}:2\leq i\leq\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil-1\}\rangle=\langle J^{*}_{p}\cup M(\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil)\cup\{\delta_{p,\,i},\,\,\delta_{p,\,n-i+1}:2\leq i\leq\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil-1\}\rangle.

In either case, the result follows from Lemmas
3.17
and
3.18
.
∎

We now present the following result.

Theorem 3.20
.

Let
\mathcal{DORP}_{n}
be as defined in (
3
). Then,
\text{ rank }\mathcal{DORP}_{n}=3n-2
.

Proof.

Observe that

\displaystyle\text{ rank }\mathcal{DORP}_{n}

\displaystyle=\text{ rank }I(n,n-1)+|\{\text{id}_{[n]}\}|

\displaystyle=(n-2)+\sum\limits_{r=n-1}^{n}{\binom{n}{r}}{\binom{r-1}{n-2}}+1

\displaystyle=3n-2,

as required.
∎

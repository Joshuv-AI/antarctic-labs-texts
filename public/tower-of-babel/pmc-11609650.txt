The advantages of artificial intelligence-assisted total hip arthroplasty: A randomized controlled trial followed by 12 months
Authors: Gang Zhang, Zixuan Liu, Diaodiao Wang, Zhuang Tian, Qi Yao
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectionThe rapid advancement of artificial intelligence has brought significant breakthroughs to various medical disciplines，This study aimed to compare perioperative factors and postoperative hip function recovery in primary total hip arthroplasty (THA)by evaluating the use of an artificial intelligence (AI) preoperative planning system versus traditional two-dimensional X-ray planning.Patients and methodsA total of 45 eligible patients underwent primary THA at Beijing Shijitan Hospital, Capital Medical University, between July 2022 and August 2022. The patients were randomly assigned to either the experimental group (n = 19) or the control group (n = 26). The experimental group utilized AI planning, while the control group employed traditional two-dimensional X-ray planning. Statistical analysis was performed to compare the accuracy of prosthesis prediction, operation time, intraoperative blood loss, frequency of intraoperative model testing, length of hospital stay, postoperative imaging data, and postoperative hip function scores. These comparisons were made to assess the effects of different preoperative planning methods on perioperative and postoperative hip function recovery.ResultsThe accuracy of preoperative planning for the acetabular and femoral sides in AI-assisted total hip arthroplasty was 84.2 % and 89.5 %, respectively, which was significantly better than that of the traditional two-dimensional X-ray planning group (P < 0.05). The operation time for AI-assisted total hip arthroplasty was 104.32 ± 18.10 min, which was shorter than that of the traditional two-dimensional X-ray planning group (P < 0.05). At 3 months post-operation, the grade of Harris score for hip function in the AI planning group was significantly better than that in the traditional two-dimensional X-ray planning group (P < 0.05). The average postoperative Harris score of the artificial intelligence group was higher than that of the traditional two-dimensional X-ray planning (P < 0.05).ConclusionArtificial intelligence-assisted total hip arthroplasty demonstrated superior accuracy in prosthesis prediction, shorter operation time, higher average Harris score at postoperative follow-up, and better hip function recovery at 3 months compared to traditional two-dimensional X-ray planning.Level of evidenceIII, case-control study.Keywords: Total hip arthroplasty, Artificial intelligence, Preoperative Planning

Introduction

Total hip arthroplasty (THA) is considered the definitive treatment for hip diseases [1]. Despite being a relatively well-established surgical method, THA still faces challenges such as improper prosthesis size selection and suboptimal prosthesis placement [2]. Complications including prosthesis dislocation, impingement and leg length discrepancy [3,4] can be effectively prevented through meticulous preoperative planning [5,6]. Traditional preoperative planning methods involve measuring and drawing X-ray films to select the appropriate prosthesis and match it with a template sample [7]. However, this approach has limitations, including poor accuracy, heavy reliance on the operator's experience [8,9], and an inability to provide precise intraoperative reference for prosthesis placement [10]. Furthermore, two-dimensional X-ray films used as references are influenced by factors such as imaging magnification, shooting angle, and voltage [[11], [12], [13]]. Finding ways to achieve more accurate preoperative planning and reduce the occurrence of surgical complications has become a major challenge in total hip arthroplasty [14]. In recent years, the rapid advancement of artificial intelligence has brought significant breakthroughs to various medical disciplines, including tools for auxiliary diagnosis, treatment, and tumor staging assessment. One notable development is the field of image recognition, utilizing deep learning technology and artificial neural networks to achieve robust and efficient image recognition through program coding, training set construction, and test set refinement [[15], [16], [17]]. The AI HIP software (Beijing Changmugu Medical Technology Co., LTD., China) utilized in this study employs artificial intelligence and three-dimensional image reconstruction techniques to identify and segment key points in patients' CT images. It then reconstructs the pelvis and femur in three dimensions, facilitating preoperative planning that includes selecting the appropriate prosthesis size and optimizing the prosthesis placement angles through matching and adjustment (Fig. 1). This research uses AI based on U net + Point-Rend + RNN neural network architecture to perform AI intelligent segmentation of human bones and soft tissues. Based on multi-channel Dense Net network, through the learning of hip joint disease image database in the early stage of artificial intelligence, In the early stage, 3000 hip-related samples were included. The training set, test set and verification set were trained according to 3:1:1, which was mainly suitable for hip-related diseases requiring preoperative planning of total hip arthroplasty It presents a solution to the existing challenges in preoperative planning. The objective of this study is to compare the advantages and disadvantages of the artificial intelligence-assisted planning system with the traditional two-dimensional X-ray planning system by evaluating perioperative indicators and postoperative hip function recovery.

Methods and materials

Patient selection criteria

Inclusion criteria: (1) Age ≥30 years; (2) First-time total hip arthroplasty performed on the affected limb of the test side with clear indications for joint replacement.

Exclusion criteria: (1) Subjects with serious underlying diseases such as coronary heart disease, severe liver and kidney dysfunction; (2) Subjects with active infection in the hip joint or other areas; (3) Subjects with neuromuscular insufficiency (e.g., muscle wasting, abductor weakness, etc.).

Between July 2022 and August 2022, a total of 45 patients who met the inclusion criteria were enrolled in the study. The enrolled patients were divided into experimental group and control group by random number table method The experiment was conducted in a double-blind manner. Before the study, the random number table was generated and saved by the software, the numbers were divided into groups, and the corresponding groups and numbers were sealed in envelopes. After enrolling the subjects, the researchers randomly selected an envelope, grouped themselves based on the results indicated by the envelope, and then marked the envelope as used. Both the subjects and the surgical operators were unaware of the allocation, and the surgical operators were only given the preoperative planning results as a reference for surgery. Preoperative planning is only used as a reference and does not actually affect the operation. Among them, 19 patients underwent preoperative planning using the AI Joint system (experimental group), while 26 patients used the two-dimensional template method for preoperative planning (control group).

Preoperative planning methods

(1)The experimental group underwent preoperative hip CT examination. CT scan requirements: (a) Scanning range: at least 15 cm from the anterior superior iliac spine to the lesser trochanter of the femur; (b) Patients were positioned supine; (c) Slice thickness was 0.5 mm. The CT images were uploaded to the AIHIP software after removing the personal information of the patients for preoperative planning. The point recognition algorithm was used to accurately locate the key bone markers on the hip joint. The deep learning model based on big data was used to automatically match the most suitable prosthesis. The system real-time feedback such as cup coverage, offset, osteotomy height, leg length difference and other indicators. The postoperative pelvic anteroposterior X-ray films were simulated and generated. When the above results were not satisfactory, manual adjustment could be made according to the real-time feedback data (Fig. 2(A–D)).

Hip X-ray requirements: (a) Pelvic anteroposterior and lateral views were taken; (b) Patients were positioned supine; (c) Clear visualization of the femur and acetabulum without significant rotation. The prosthesis template was matched to the prosthesis used. First, the standard pelvic anteroposterior X-ray film was fixed on the reading lamp, and the acetabular template was placed at 40°–45° on the inner edge of the tear drop, so that the lower edge of the acetabular cup was parallel to the lower edge of the tear drop, and the inner edge was close to the acetabular floor. The appropriate model was selected to ensure sufficient depth and optimal bone coverage of the acetabular cup. Secondly, the distance between the upper edge of bilateral lesser trochanters and the lower edge of bilateral teardrops was used to judge the leg length difference. According to the length difference of bilateral lower limbs, the neck length and offset were adjusted, the central axis of the prosthesis was adjusted to match the axis of the medullary cavity, and the appropriate femoral stem was selected to fully fill the medullary cavity. The osteotomy plane of the femoral neck and the position of the prosthesis were adjusted to restore the leg length and offset.

The preoperative planning in both groups served only as a reference for prosthesis selection during the operation, and the final choice of prosthesis was based on the intraoperative situation. The subjects selected the Corail femoral stem and the PINNACLE prosthesis for the acetabular cup.

Surgical technique

Both groups of surgeries were performed by the same medical team. The subjects, who received prostheses, were positioned in a lateral decubitus position, and a posterior-lateral approach was used for exposure. On the acetabular side, reaming was performed according to the preoperative planning. The acetabular prosthesis was chosen based on the preoperative planning and the measurements obtained from the femoral head. On the femoral side, a medullary reamer was selected according to the preoperative planning to enlarge the medullary canal and select the femoral prosthesis. The placement of the prosthesis was guided by the predetermined abduction and anteversion angles from the preoperative planning. After placement, a trial model was used to assess functionality and test the range of motion. The length of both lower limbs was compared to determine if they were equal. The subjects in both groups underwent reconstruction of the external rotator muscles, and the same rehabilitation protocol was implemented postoperatively.

Primary outcomes

①The accuracy of the prediction was assessed based on the degree of consistency between the predicted prosthesis and the actual prosthesis used. If the predicted prosthesis matched the actual prosthesis precisely, it was considered as an accurate prediction. A difference of ±1 model between the predicted prosthesis and the actual prosthesis used during the operation was categorized as a general prediction. Poor prediction was defined as a difference of ±2 models.

Statistical methods

The data were analyzed by SPSS (23.0) statistical software. The measurement data such as operation time and intraoperative blood loss were expressed by X ± S, and the comparison between groups was analyzed by independent sample t-test. Counting data such as prosthesis selection accuracy, anteversion Angle, abduction Angle and so on were compared between the two groups by χ2 test. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

General information

The general information of the subjects in this study is detailed in Table 1. A total of 45 patients were included, consisting of 24 males and 21 females. The age range of the participants was between 31 and 80 years, with an average age of (62.33 ± 11.28) years. Among the patients, there were 20 cases of femoral neck fracture, 21 cases of avascular necrosis of the femoral head, 3 cases of hip arthropathy, 1 case of congenital dysplasia of the hip, and 20 cases with chronic diseases such as hypertension and diabetes. Based on the Dorr classification, there were 11 cases of type A, 17 cases of type B, and 17 cases of type C.

Perioperative indicators

The perioperative data for both groups are presented in Table 3. In the artificial intelligence planning group, the average number of femoral reaming was 2.68 ± 0.15 times, and the average number of acetabular reaming was 2.79 ± 0.71 times. The average length of hospital stay was 12.26 ± 3.54 days, with an average postoperative hospital stay of 3.79 ± 2.23 days. The average operation time was (104.32 ± 18.10) minutes, and the average intraoperative blood loss was (323.68 ± 222.59) ml. The artificial intelligence planning group showed significantly shorter femoral and acetabular reaming times, as well as a shorter operation time, compared to the traditional two-dimensional X-ray planning group. These differences were statistically significant (P < 0.05). However, there were no significant differences between the two groups in terms of length of hospital stay, postoperative hospital stay, and intraoperative blood loss (P > 0.05).

Discussion

Artificial intelligence-assisted preoperative planning offers significant advantages in mitigating postoperative complications associated with total hip arthroplasty [19]. Currently, the conventional method of using two-dimensional X-rays combined with surgeon expertise is the mainstream approach to preoperative planning [20]. However, this method often faces issues such as inconsistent magnification and template mismatch [21], resulting in poor accuracy, increased complications, and prolonged operation preparation time. In contrast, artificial intelligence assisted preoperative planning systems can accurately and rapidly perform tasks such as image data modeling, numerical measurements, and prosthesis selection. They also provide more effective measurements of medullary cavity width and combined anteversion angle. Particularly for challenging measurements like femoral neck anteversion angle, artificial intelligence preoperative planning demonstrates clear advantages over traditional two-dimensional X-ray films. Furthermore, preoperative planning assisted by artificial intelligence reduces the reliance on surgeon experience, making it more accessible for beginners and offering a smoother learning curve.

The accuracy of artificial intelligence-assisted preoperative planning for prosthesis selection, as observed in this study, aligns with the findings of previous research [22]. The accuracy rates for femoral and acetabular prostheses using artificial intelligence-assisted preoperative planning were 89.5 % and 84.2 %, respectively. This suggests that artificial intelligence provides a significant advantage in achieving accurate prosthesis selection (P < 0.05) [23]. The advantage of artificial intelligence planning methods in terms of accuracy has significant advantages over traditional 2D and 3D planning methods in the previous literature [24,25]. The reason why the artificial intelligence group can select prostheses more accurately is that through deep learning, it can identify and match key anatomical points and prostheses more accurately, and it can minimize the influence of objective factors such as inappropriate imaging data and insufficient operation experience. Secondly, with the increase of samples and training times, the accuracy of artificial intelligence is improved faster than that of traditional methods.

In this study, two different prediction methods were compared to evaluate their impact on the perioperative period of the subjects. Significant differences were observed in operation time and reaming frequency between AI-assisted preoperative planning and traditional two-dimensional X-ray film planning. AI-assisted preoperative planning resulted in shorter operation times and more appropriate reaming frequency. It is believed that the influence of artificial intelligence preoperative planning on the surgical process is achieved by optimizing prosthesis selection. Proper prosthesis selection helps reduce operation time, optimize the scope of model testing and reaming, and minimize complications caused by improper reaming, such as periacetabular cortical thinning and secondary periprosthetic fractures. The safe range of prosthesis placement proposed by Lewinnek et al., used in this study, was established based on cadaver studies and closely related to the prosthesis design at the time of publication [26] No significant difference was found in the range of implant placement between the two groups. Considering the continuous improvement in prosthesis design and racial differences, the suitability of the traditional safe range is questionable. Mario Hevesi et al. noted that the safety range of prosthesis design has evolved with advancements in prosthesis technology [27]. The experimental group offered more precise parameters of placement compared to the other group, but we could not achieve full compliance with the preoperative plan during the operation so to develop the assistant tool such as surgy robots essential.

In this study, hip function was assessed and graded using the Harris score table. In the graded evaluation at 3 months post-operation, the experimental group demonstrated better results compared to the control group, with a statistically significant difference. This can be attributed to the experimental group having shorter operation times, more appropriate reaming frequencies, and optimal placement angles, which facilitate postoperative patient recovery. In the 12-month follow-up results, there was no significant difference between the experimental and control groups. However, it is worth noting that the experimental group consistently had higher Harris scores than the control group at all follow-up intervals, with statistical significance.

The advantages of artificial intelligence-assisted preoperative planning for total hip arthroplasty are evident. For the acetabulum, accurate preoperative planning provides prosthesis size and customized angles, facilitating precise placement of the acetabular cup. This allows for better preservation of the periacetabular bone and ensures the stability of the acetabular prosthesis, ultimately promoting optimal recovery of hip joint structure and function. Regarding the femoral side, this study only examined one type of prosthesis, and potential issues such as offset and rotation center are not discussed. However, even in more complex conditions such as developmental dysplasia of the hip (DDH), artificial intelligence assisted preoperative planning have been shown to be more effective in restoring the center of rotation [28]. In this study, appropriate preoperative planning of the femoral side, including prosthesis selection, helped avoid complications such as limb length discrepancy and dislocation. The reason why complications can be reduced is that appropriate prosthesis selection can avoid prosthesis subsidence caused by prosthesis too small or fracture caused by prosthesis too large, and better reconstruction can be achieved. Additionally, artificial intelligence has shown promise in assisting the identification of different prosthesis types, particularly in patients with primary replacement failure [29], providing more accurate reference information for revision surgery. Borjali A et al. established a prosthesis database and conducted deep learning through a retrospective study, achieving identification accuracy of over 90 %,however, the success of artificial intelligence learning relies on the establishment of a convolutive layer network and the size of the sample [30]. Previous studies have suggested that Dorr classification and bone mineral density can influence prediction accuracy. As Dorr type B constituted the largest proportion in the population, it was affected during the construction of the training set [31]. To enhance deep learning, a certain degree of preliminary screening is necessary in the construction of the training set to mitigate selection bias.

The influence of artificial intelligence on preoperative prediction, intraoperative guidance and postoperative rehabilitation is based on the advantages of artificial intelligence neural network. In preoperative planning, through the analysis of this research for the accuracy of image recognition, artificial intelligence, especially for the image contour recognition is a significant advantage, and prosthesis selection is for size contour matching, under the big data training, artificial intelligence can strengthen the accuracy of preoperative planning includes involving prosthesis type, size, placement Angle, etc.; Artificial intelligence provides accurate preoperative planning and can realize the optimization of the surgical process, that is, it can reduce unnecessary operations such as excessive regrinding and excessive prosthesis size testing, so as to optimize the operation time and intraoperative bleeding, and further optimize the surgical process and prosthesis placement. It is possible to promote the contact between prosthesis and bone surface to promote bone ingrowings and thus achieve early rehabilitation. At the same time, artificial intelligence can make rehabilitation plans according to the actual situation of patients, which is also a way to promote faster healing of patients.

Preoperative planning with artificial intelligence, which relies on CT data, does indeed increase the subject's radiation exposure compared to two-dimensional X-rays. However, Huppertz's study suggests that the increase in radiation is minimal compared to the benefits obtained from artificial intelligence assistance [32]. It is important to note that artificial intelligence provides reference and does not directly intervene in the surgical process, which may introduce deviations in the operation. Previous studies have indicated that artificial intelligence-assisted planning offers significant advantages over traditional manual planning [23]. However, in practical application, the time required for acquiring image data should be considered. Therefore, considering the actual clinical workflow in different regions, further research is needed to evaluate the time advantages of artificial intelligence. AI has its disadvantages compared with traditional methods, such as requiring users to have a certain foundation of software use, high cost of software development and maintenance Nevertheless, as artificial intelligence continues to advance and surgical robots become more prevalent, the efficient integration of artificial intelligence-assisted planning and robot-assisted surgery is expected to bring new vitality to rapid recovery and precise orthopedic procedures.

This study has several limitations: ① The sample size is small and limited. ② Postoperative imaging is based on X-rays, which may introduce measurement errors. ③ Subjectivity is present in the follow-up process, such as the assessment of angle recovery and the evaluation of pain intensity, which can influence the assessment of hip function recovery. ④ Only one type of prosthesis was included in this study, limiting the generalizability of the findings to other prosthetic types.

Conclusion

The artificial intelligence-assisted preoperative planning system has significant advantages over the traditional two-dimensional X-ray preoperative planning. These advantages are reflected in the stability and accuracy of its prediction, and this advantage continues to expand with the development of AI. In summary, the combination of artificial intelligence and total hip arthroplasty provides a new opportunity for personalized, customized and precise joint replacement surgery.

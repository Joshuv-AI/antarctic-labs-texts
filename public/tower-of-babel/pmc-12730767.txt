Temporal trends in the causes of in-hospital cardiac arrest: a retrospective observational cohort study on the limitations of the current alert system at a tertiary hospital in Korea
Authors: Joong Min Lee, Min Soo Cho, Myung-Jin Cha, Jun Kim, Kee-Joon Choi, Ah-Ram Kim, Min-Seok Kim, JinMi Lee, Yujung Shin, Un Jung Go, Hae Yeon Oh, Sang-Bum Hong, Gi-Byoung Nam
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesIn-hospital cardiac arrest (IHCA) is associated with high mortality and serious neurological sequelae. Although medical alert systems have evolved, the ability of these systems to influence changes in IHCA incidence and aetiology remains limited.DesignsRetrospective observational cohort study.SettingsA single tertiary hospital in South Korea, covering tertiary care levels.ParticipantsA total of 1994 adult patients (≥18 years) who experienced 2121 episodes of IHCA between January 2011 and December 2019. Patients with out-of-hospital cardiac arrest, those aged ≤18 years and those with do-not-resuscitate orders were excluded. The mean age of patients was 63.0 years (SD, 14.6); 64.1% were male.InterventionsNot applicable.Main outcome measuresThe incidence and temporal trends of IHCA were stratified by aetiology (cardiac vs non-cardiac). Additional analyses examined changes in arrhythmic versus non-arrhythmic causes over time using Poisson regression.ResultsCardiac arrhythmia was the most common cause of IHCA (314 of 2121, 14.8%; incidence: 0.42/1000 admissions), including ventricular tachycardia (n=86), ventricular fibrillation (n=87) and Torsades de Pointes (n=79). Respiratory failure was the second most common cause (266 of 2121, 12.5%; incidence: 0.36/1000 admissions). The incidence of IHCA due to respiratory failure in 2011 was 0.63/1000 admissions, which decreased to 0.20/1000 admissions by 2019 (β=0.883, 95% CI 0.842 to 0.926, p for trend 0.007; Poisson p<0.001). Arrhythmia has become the leading cause of IHCA, with incidences remaining stable, whereas a marked decrease has been observed in respiratory-related IHCA. Therefore, enhanced in-hospital cardiac monitoring systems are required for early detection.

Introduction

In-hospital cardiac arrest (IHCA) is associated with increased in-hospital mortality. Notably, IHCA poses a significant healthcare burden even after the return of spontaneous circulation (ROSC). The survival rates of IHCA have gradually improved; nevertheless, IHCA remains a leading cause of in-hospital mortality. Thus, predicting and preventing IHCA represent a crucial unmet need in current clinical practice.14 The aetiology of IHCA varies across regions, races and clinical settings; however, myocardial ischaemia, cardiac arrhythmias and respiratory insufficiencies form the three primary causes of IHCA.5 Indeed, myocardial ischaemia has been identified as the leading cause of IHCA in Western countries, whereas cardiac arrhythmias are the most common cause of IHCA in Asian countries.3610

Data on the aetiology and long-term outcomes of IHCA remain sparse. Meanwhile, early warning systems and medical emergency team (MET) have been implemented to reduce and prevent the incidence of IHCA;11 nevertheless, the impact of these measures on the chronological changes in the incidence and aetiology of IHCA remains limited. However, the persistent collection of such data may facilitate the identification of the current limitations in the prediction and warning systems, aiding in the formulation of strategies to prevent the occurrence of IHCA in the future. Therefore, this study aimed to retrospectively analyse the pattern and changes in the incidence of IHCA at our institution over the years.

Method

Study design and participants

This retrospective, observational study analysed patients who had experienced and were treated for IHCA at the Asan Medical Center, Seoul, South Korea, between January 2011 and December 2019. Patients who had received cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR) from healthcare providers to manage IHCA in general wards or the intensive care unit (ICU) were eligible for inclusion in this study. Patients who satisfied the following criteria were excluded: (1) ≤18 years of age, (2) history of out-of-hospital cardiac arrest (OHCA) before the index hospitalisation, (3) those with do-not-resuscitate (DNR) orders or (4) history of IHCA in the emergency department. This study followed the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology reporting guidelines.

Data collection

The medical condition of the hospitalised patients in the general wards was monitored using the MET system. Measures were taken to prevent clinical deterioration and provide pre-emptive responses to high-risk patients before IHCA.12 The MET used vital signs, clinical parameters, such as pH, lactic acid levels, partial pressures of oxygen and carbon dioxide from the arterial blood gas analysis, oxygen application and changes in mental status, as well as the Modified Early Warning Score (MEWS) and National Early Warning Score (NEWS), to screen high-risk patients (online supplemental appendix).13 14 The MEWS was calculated using the respiratory rate, heart rate, systolic blood pressure, body temperature and mental status. The NEWS was calculated by adding oxygen saturation and the use of supplemental oxygen to the aforementioned variables. The electronic medical record system was used to monitor these parameters and scoring systems in real time. The MET implements rapid responses during IHCA events by performing CPR and stabilising the patient, irrespective of the hospital unit (general wards or ICU). The nurses in the MET completed the CPR registry within 24 hours of each MET activation for IHCA. These data were reviewed and validated weekly by the MET and resuscitation committee. The MET database, which is structured based on the Utstein template,15 comprises data regarding the demographic history, resuscitation profiles, activation times, critical interventions, defibrillation and outcomes of IHCA events. This database was used in the current analysis.

Definitions and classifications of aetiology

The cessation of circulation requiring immediate resuscitation through chest compressions and/or defibrillation was defined as cardiac arrest, which aligns with the current guidelines.16 This study included patients who had received cardiopulmonary resuscitation (chest compressions and/or defibrillation) for IHCA. Meanwhile, IHCA was classified as a cardiac arrest event occurring in hospitalised patients; events that occurred outside the hospital or in the emergency department were explicitly excluded. The critical underlying medical condition that was likely to result in cardiac arrest within 24 hours before the IHCA event was defined as the ‘cause’. Detailed diagnostic criteria and classification rules are provided in the online supplemental appendix. The MET steering committee, comprising critical care specialists and cardiologists, held weekly meetings to reach a consensus regarding the primary cause of the IHCA event. The medical records were retrospectively reviewed by two investigators, JML and MSC, to determine the primary cause of the IHCA. The concordance rate was 91% (κ=0.912; p<0.001). Disagreements between the investigators were resolved through a consensus reached via discussions with experts (S-BH and G-BN).

The causes of the IHCA were classified into the cardiac origin and non-cardiac origin groups. The cardiac origin group comprised IHCA events caused by myocardial infarction (MI), heart failure (HF), arrhythmia and other cardiac causes. The non-cardiac origin group comprised IHCA events caused by airway obstruction, respiratory failure, sepsis, hypovolaemic shock, neurological disease, metabolic disease, anaphylaxis and other unspecified and unknown causes.

Data analysis and statistical methods

Baseline characteristics are presented as the mean±SD for continuous variables and as frequencies and percentages for categorical variables. Comparisons between categorical variables were performed using the χ2 test or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate, whereas comparisons between continuous variables were performed using Student’s t-test. The incidence of IHCA was calculated by dividing the number of IHCA events during the study period by the total number of hospitalised patients. The trends in the causes of IHCA over time were analysed using Poisson regression. All statistical analyses were performed using Microsoft Excel (Microsoft Corporation, Redmond, Washington, USA) and R project V.4.3.2 (The R Foundation, Vienna, Austria). Statistical significance was set at a two-sided p value <0.05.

Patient and public involvement

Patients and members of the public were not involved in the design, conduct, reporting or dissemination plans of this research.

Results

Among the 2242 episodes of CPR that occurred during the study period, the present study included 2121 episodes involving 1994 patients. Figure 1 presents the details of the inclusion and exclusion process. Table 1 presents the baseline characteristics of the study population and significant IHCA events. The mean age of patients who experienced IHCA was 63.0±14.6 years, and 64.1% of the patients were male. Malignancy was the most common comorbidity (39.3%), followed by renal insufficiency, hypertension, arrhythmia, diabetes, HF and acute coronary syndrome (ACS). In the overall cohort, 23.0% of patients were admitted to the cardiac medical department, 8.8% to the cardiac surgical department, 49.9% to the non-cardiac medical departments and 17.7% to the non-cardiac surgical departments. Pulseless electrical activity (56.1%), followed by ventricular tachycardia/ventricular fibrillation (VT and VF, respectively; 18.4%) and asystole (17.2%), was the most common rhythm observed at the time of cardiac arrest.

Patients who experienced IHCA of a cardiac origin were older (64.1±14.3 vs 62.3±14.7; p=0.010) and more likely to be admitted to the cardiac medical department (47.7% vs 9.4%; p<0.001) and had a higher prevalence of comorbidities such as ACS (19.1% vs 3.3%; p<0.001), arrhythmia (35.0% vs 14.0%; p<0.001) and HF (25.8% vs 6.4%; p<0.001). However, the prevalence of malignancy (20.3% vs 49.2%; p<0.001) and chronic liver disease (14.3% vs 27.8%; p<0.001) in these patients was lower than that observed in patients who experienced IHCA of non-cardiac origin. Shockable rhythms were observed more frequently in the cardiac cause group during the IHCA events (37.5% vs 5.9%; p<0.001).

Distribution and incidence of the aetiology of ICHA

Table 2 and figure 2 present the distribution of the causes of IHCA and the incidence rates. The annual incidence of IHCA is presented in the online supplemental appendix. Cardiac arrhythmia (14.8%, 0.42/1000 admissions) was the most common cause of IHCA, followed by respiratory failure (12.5%, 0.36/1000 admissions), sepsis (9.9%, 0.28/1000 admissions), airway obstruction (8.7%, 0.25/1000 admissions) and hypovolaemic shock (8.5%, 0.24/1000 admissions); VT, VF and Torsades de Pointes (TdP) accounted for 86, 87 and 79, respectively, of the 314 cases of IHCA attributed to arrhythmias. Additionally, we reviewed the use of medications known to prolong QT intervals in the TdP cases (online supplemental appendix). Cardiac causes other than arrhythmia accounted for 20.5% of the total IHCA events. MI, HF and other cardiac conditions accounted for 4.2%, 7.4% and 9.0% of cases, respectively. Thus, the incidence rate varied depending on the primary cause of IHCA. In addition, table 2 presents the rates of ROSC and survival to discharge across different aetiologies of IHCA. Overall, arrhythmic causes demonstrated relatively higher rates of ROSC (90.8%) and survival to discharge (58.3%) compared with other aetiologies.

Chronological changes in the incidence according to the aetiology of IHCA

Figure 3 presents the chronological change in the major cause of IHCA. The incidence of respiratory failure, which was the most common non-cardiac cause, was 0.63/1000 admissions at the beginning of the study period (2011). An annual decline in the incidence of respiratory failure-related IHCA was observed over the study period (β=0.883, 95% CI 0.842 to 0.926, p for trend=0.007; Poisson p<0.001). A significant decrease was also observed in the prevalence of other non-cardiac aetiologies, excluding respiratory failure (β=0.945, 95% CI 0.923 to 0.967, p for trend=0.015; Poisson p<0.001). In contrast, the incidence of arrhythmic IHCA remained similar throughout the study period (0.49/1000 admissions in 2011; β=0.972, 95% CI 0.932 to 1.015, p for trend 0.194; Poisson p=0.198). A similar trend was observed for non-arrhythmic cardiac causes (β=0.977, 95% CI 0.942 to 1.013, p for trend 0.170; Poisson p=0.21; online supplemental appendix). This non-decreasing trend in the incidence of arrhythmic or non-arrhythmic cardiac IHCA was significantly different from the decreasing trend in respiratory failure-related IHCA (p=0.003 and 0.001 for arrhythmic and non-arrhythmic cardiac IHCA, respectively).

Discussion

This retrospective observational study revealed several important findings regarding the causes of IHCA that can be summarised as follows: (1) arrhythmia and respiratory failure are the most common cardiac and non-cardiac causes of IHCA, respectively; (2) The incidence of respiratory failure-related IHCA has decreased significantly, whereas that of arrhythmic IHCA has remained at a similar level; and (3) the proportion of IHCA resulting from a cardiac origin increased from 31% to 42% over the study period(figure 4).

Arrhythmia emerged as the most frequent cause of IHCA in this single-centre study, highlighting the potential importance of monitoring arrhythmia in similar tertiary care settings in Korea. The findings in this study contrast with the aetiology reported in Western countries, wherein MI and ischaemia are the leading causes of IHCA. However, the findings in this study are consistent with those of previous studies conducted in Japan and Taiwan, which identified arrhythmia as the most frequent cause of IHCA in Asian populations.3 5 6 9 10 A recent review by Penketh and Nolan5 revealed that a meta-analysis of nine studies involving 27 102 patients reported that hypoxia, ACS and arrhythmia accounted for 21%, 14% and 12% of IHCA events, respectively. ACS may emerge as the most common cause of IHCA if hypoxia is subdivided into categories such as respiratory failure and airway obstruction. This finding contrasts with the results of the present study, which identified arrhythmia as the most prevalent cause of IHCA.5 Moreover, this highlights the regional differences in the aetiology of IHCA, with MI and ischaemia being prevalent in Western countries, but arrhythmia being more prevalent in Asian countries.3 6 8 Arrhythmia was the most common cause of IHCA in Asian countries, according to the data obtained from Japan and Taiwan.9 10 This observation is consistent with the lower ischaemic heart disease-related mortality rates observed in Asia compared with those in Western countries.17 A major strength of the present study is that this study focused exclusively on hospitalised patients, in contrast to previous studies conducted in Japan and Taiwan, which included patients from emergency rooms and outpatient settings. This distinction makes the data obtained in the present study more precise in identifying factors relevant to IHCA among hospitalised patients, further emphasising the requirement for early detection and prevention of arrhythmias in this population. Thus, the data obtained in the present study regarding the incidence of IHCA events during hospitalisation suggest that the presence of arrhythmias is significant in the Asian population. Furthermore, these data underscore the importance of exploring preventable aspects of arrhythmias in these IHCA events.

A significant discovery made by the present study is that TdP represents a major arrhythmic aetiological factor for the incidence of IHCA, comparable to VT and VF, which are anticipated to be major causes of IHCA owing to the fatality and prevalence of these conditions among certain patient groups, such as those in post-MI states and those with end-stage HF or exacerbated cardiomyopathy.1 18 The prominence of TdP is crucial, given that this condition represents a more preventable cause. This can be correlated with the findings of a previous study that revealed an increase in the incidence of IHCA associated with the use of QT-prolonging drugs.19 Certain electrocardiographic parameters, such as a corrected QT interval of >500 ms, the Tpeak to Tend interval and T wave alternans, indicate an increased risk of TdP.20 Notably, Pham et al highlighted the clinical significance of dynamic ECG parameters, including the QRS transition zone, QTc interval and Tpeak–Tend interval.21 However, sufficient implementation of these promising indicators is lacking in clinical practice, underscoring an unmet need for preventive measures for IHCA. Although arrhythmic IHCA showed relatively higher rates of ROSC and survival to discharge compared with other aetiologies in our data, this does not diminish its clinical importance. IHCA has been consistently associated with high in-hospital mortality and significant long-term morbidity among survivors, including neurological impairment and ongoing care requirements.5 Therefore, given that many arrhythmic events are potentially preventable or detectable with appropriate monitoring strategies, the emphasis should remain on prevention, rather than solely on post-event outcomes.

The incidence of respiratory failure-related IHCA and IHCA of non-cardiac origin demonstrated a gradual decline during the study period. In contrast, the incidence of IHCA caused by both arrhythmic and non-arrhythmic cardiac causes remained at a similar level. The MET, which uses clinical parameters such as vital signs, laboratory findings (eg, arterial blood gas analysis or lactic acid levels) and scoring systems (eg, MEWS and NEWS), has enhanced the screening of critical situations in the general ward. Traditional screening tools can effectively identify static conditions, such as respiratory failure or sepsis; however, these tools are associated with limitations in detecting dynamic conditions such as cardiac arrhythmias. This was reflected in the current data.11 22 23 Meanwhile, recent advances in continuous biosignal monitoring tools, such as patch-type electrocardiography and photoplethysmography, appear promising in this regard.2426 Artificial intelligence (AI) has also been used to predict arrhythmias in several studies.27 28 Hence, integrating traditional static factors with recently highlighted dynamic factors and AI systems in the future may help fulfil the current unmet needs in preventing IHCA.

Limitations

This study has certain limitations. First, cases with DNR orders were excluded, as these patients did not receive CPR or CPR broadcasts. Thus, since DNR orders are typically enacted for patients with terminal conditions such as end-stage HF, chronic lung disease, renal insufficiency and cancer, this could have potentially skewed the distribution of the aetiologies of IHCA. Patients in terminal disease stages often have DNR orders in real-world practice, thereby reducing the likelihood of medical staff performing CPR. Hence, this limitation is unlikely to have a significant effect on the applicability of our findings. Second, the present study was conducted at a tertiary hospital; consequently, the severity and disease distribution of the inpatient population may differ from those of primary and secondary hospitals. While the findings cannot be directly generalised, these findings may still provide useful insights for clinicians working in other large university hospitals or tertiary care centres. Third, the retrospective nature of the study, coupled with complex clinical situations and limited access to information, made determining the cause of IHCA difficult in some cases.

Conclusion

Significant changes were observed in terms of the causes of IHCA. A decrease in the incidence of respiratory failure-related IHCA was observed; however, the incidence of cardiac IHCA, especially that of arrhythmic IHCA, remained unchanged over time. Thus, more advanced monitoring systems that integrate dynamic, continuous changes in patient status may help in reducing the occurrence of cardiac IHCA.

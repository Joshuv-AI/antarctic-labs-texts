Abstract
We perform a model-independent measurement of the D 0 D^{0} - D ¯ 0 \kern 1.99997pt\overline{\kern-1.99997ptD}{}^{0} mixing parameters using samples of e + ​ e − e^{+}e^{-} -collision data collected by the Belle and Belle II experiments that have integrated luminosities of 951 fb − 1 \mbox{\,fb}^{-1} and 408 fb − 1 \mbox{\,fb}^{-1} , respectively. Approximately 2.05 × 10 6 2.05\times 10^{6} neutral D D mesons are reconstructed in the D 0 → K S 0 ​ π + ​ π − D^{0}\rightarrow K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm S}\pi^{+}\pi^{-} channel, with the neutral D D flavor tagged by the charge of the pion in the D ∗ ⁣ + → D 0 ​ π + D^{*+}\rightarrow D^{0}\pi^{+} decay. Assuming charge-parity symmetry, the mixing parameters are measured to be x = ( 4.0 ± 1.7 ± 0.4 ) × 10 − 3 x=(4.0\pm 1.7\pm 0.4)\times 10^{-3} and y = ( 2.9 ± 1.4 ± 0.3 ) × 10 − 3 y=(2.9\pm 1.4\pm 0.3)\times 10^{-3} , where the first uncertainties are statistical and the second systematic. The results are consistent with previous determinations.

III
Belle and Belle II detectors

The Belle experiment
[
30
,
31
]
operated at KEKB asymmetric-energy
e^{+}e^{-}
collider
[
32
,
33
]
between 1999 and 2010. The detector consisted of a large-solid-angle spectrometer, which included a double-sided silicon-strip vertex detector, a 50-layer central drift chamber, an array of aerogel threshold Cherenkov counters, a barrel-like arrangement of time-of-flight scintillation counters, and an electromagnetic calorimeter comprised of CsI(Tl) crystals. All subdetectors were located inside a superconducting solenoid coil that provided a 1.5 T magnetic field. An iron flux-return yoke, placed outside the coil, was instrumented with resistive-plate chambers to detect
K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm L}
mesons and identify muons. Two inner detector configurations were used: a 2.0
{\rm\,cm}
radius beam pipe and a three-layer silicon vertex detector (SVD1); and, from October 2003, a 1.5
{\rm\,cm}
radius beam pipe, a four-layer silicon vertex detector, and a small-inner-cell drift chamber (SVD2)
[
34
]
.

The Belle II detector
[
35
]
is an upgrade with several new subdetectors designed to handle the significantly larger beam-related backgrounds of the new collider, SuperKEKB
[
36
]
. It consists of a silicon vertex detector wrapped around a 1
{\rm\,cm}
radius beam pipe and comprising two inner layers of pixel detectors and four outer layers of double-sided strip detectors, a 56-layer central drift chamber, a time-of-propagation detector, an aerogel ring-imaging Cherenkov detector, and an electromagnetic calorimeter, all located inside the same solenoid as used for Belle. The flux return outside the solenoid is instrumented with resistive-plate chambers, plastic scintillator modules, and an upgraded read-out system to detect muons,
K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm L}
mesons, and neutrons. For the data used in this paper, collected between 2019 and 2022, only part of the second layer of the pixel detector, covering 15% of the azimuthal angle, was installed.

|\Delta z|<5{\rm\,cm}
), to form
D^{0}\rightarrow K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm S}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
candidates. The
D^{0}
candidate is then combined with another charged pion satisfying the same track-quality requirements to form
D^{*+}\rightarrow D^{0}\pi^{+}
candidates. Due to the small energy released in the decay, the pion from the
D^{*+}
decay has much lower momentum than the pions from the
D^{0}
decay and is therefore identified as the “soft” pion in the following. To suppress the candidates arising from the decay of a
B
meson, the momentum of
D^{*+}
in the
e^{+}e^{-}
center-of-mass frame is required to be larger than
2.5{\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c}
(
3.1{\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c}
) for the data collected near the
\Upsilon{(4S)}
(
\Upsilon{(5S)}
) resonance. A kinematic-vertex fit
[
52
]
to the selected
D^{*+}
candidates determines the
D^{0}
and
D^{*+}
decay vertices while constraining the reconstructed
\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
mass to the known
K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm S}
mass
[
3
]
and the
D^{*+}
decay-vertex to the measured position of the
e^{+}e^{-}
interaction region. Candidates with failed fits or
\chi^{2}/\text{ndf}>200/10
are rejected. This fit calculates the
K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm S}
flight length
L
, i.e. the distance between the
K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm S}
and
D^{0}
decay vertices, and its uncertainty
\sigma_{L}
. The flight-length significance
L/\sigma_{L}
is required to be larger than
20
to suppress background candidates having no
K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm S}
meson in the
D^{0}
final state, such as
D^{0}\rightarrow\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
decays. The
D^{*+}
candidates are required to satisfy
1.8<m(K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm S}\pi^{+}\pi^{-})<2.0{\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c^{2}}
and
0.2<Q<20.0\mathrm{\,Me\kern-1.00006ptV}
.

To ensure that all candidates lie in the kinematically allowed phase-space region, the vertex fit is re-run with an additional
D^{0}
-mass constraint. The fit never fails and no candidates are removed in this step. We use the results of this second fit to compute the Dalitz-plot coordinates. All other quantities, including the
D^{0}
decay time
t
and its uncertainty
\sigma_{t}
, are computed with the result of the fit without the
D^{0}
-mass constraint.

In 15% of events more than one
D^{*+}
candidate is selected. If the multiple candidates result from the
combination of final-state particles reconstructed from cloned tracks, we remove them. Otherwise, we accept all candidates. This procedure removes approximately 1% of selected candidates and has been verified to not introduce a bias.

Selected candidates are categorized as signal, random-pion background, and other background. Signal candidates are those for which the full
D^{*+}\rightarrow D^{0}(\rightarrow K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm S}\pi^{+}\pi^{-})\pi^{+}
decay chain is correctly reconstructed. The signal candidates feature narrow peaks in both
m(K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm S}\pi^{+}\pi^{-})
and
Q
distributions. Random-pion background candidates are defined as those in which the
D^{0}
is correctly reconstructed but associated to an unrelated soft pion to form the
D^{*+}
candidate. They peak exactly as the signal in
D^{0}
mass, but are smoothly distributed in
Q
value. The remaining candidates are referred to as other background candidates. They have smooth
m(K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm S}\pi^{+}\pi^{-})
and
Q
distributions.

The fractions of the three components in each Dalitz-plot bin are determined from an unbinned maximum-likelihood fit to the two-dimensional distribution of
m(K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm S}\pi^{+}\pi^{-})
versus
Q
. The signal
m(K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm S}\pi^{+}\pi^{-})
distribution is modeled with a Crystal Ball function
[
53
,
54
]
and two Gaussian functions, with shared mean value. The
Q
distribution of signal is modeled with a Johnson unbounded distribution
[
55
]
and two Gaussian functions. The width of the second Gaussian function has a quadratic dependence on
m(K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm S}\pi^{+}\pi^{-})
. For the random-pion component, the
m(K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm S}\pi^{+}\pi^{-})
distribution is identical to the signal component, while the
Q
distribution is modeled with the two-body phase-space function
Q^{1/2}+\alpha Q^{3/2}+\beta Q^{5/2}
. The distributions of the other background are modeled with a third-order polynomial for
m(K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm S}\pi^{+}\pi^{-})
and a two-body phase-space function for
Q
.

VI
Time-dependent mixing fit

The mixing parameters are determined using an unbinned maximum-likelihood fit to the
(t,\sigma_{t})
distributions of the candidates populating the signal region and split into the 16 Dalitz-plot bins and into four different data subsets. The subsets correspond to Belle data collected with SVD1, Belle
\Upsilon{(4S)}
data collected with SVD2, Belle
\Upsilon{(5S)}
data, and Belle II data.

The PDF of the signal decays is constructed from
Equations

10
and
11
by including reconstruction effects. However, we neglect effects due to nonuniform efficiency variations and mass resolutions across the Dalitz plot, which are accounted for in the systematic uncertainties (
Section

VII
), and only include the effect of the decay-time resolution. We model the decay-time resolution using the per-candidate decay-time uncertainty
\sigma_{t}
. The two-dimensional
(t,\sigma_{t})
PDF of the signal candidates is expressed as the product between the PDF of
\sigma_{t}
and the PDF of
t
given the value of
\sigma_{t}
. The latter is expressed as the convolution of the decay rate of
Equations

10
and
11
with the resolution function
R(t|\sigma_{t})
, which depends on
\sigma_{t}
:

P_{{\rm sig}}(t,\sigma_{t}|b)=P_{{\rm sig}}(t|\sigma_{t},b)P_{{\rm sig}}(\sigma_{t}|b)\propto\left[p_{b}(t)\otimes R(t|\sigma_{t})\right]P_{{\rm sig}}(\sigma_{t}|b)\,,

(12)

where
P_{{\rm sig}}(\sigma_{t}|b)
is a histogram template determined, independently for each bin
b
and for each data subset, by subtracting the
\sigma_{t}
distribution of the candidates in the
m(K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm S}\pi^{+}\pi^{-})
sideband
[1.97,2.00]{\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c^{2}}
from the
\sigma_{t}
distribution of the signal region using the measured background fraction. The resolution function
R(t|\sigma_{t})
is parameterized as a double Gaussian distribution

R(t|\sigma_{t})=fG(t|\mu,\sigma_{1})+(1-f)G(t|\mu,s_{2}\sigma_{1})\,,

(13)

where
\sigma_{1}=s_{1}\sigma_{t}+s_{11}\sigma_{t}^{2}
. The parameters
f
,
\mu
,
s_{1}
,
s_{11}
, and
s_{2}
are determined directly from the fit to the data (in the fit to Belle data
s_{11}
is fixed to zero). Independent parameters are considered for each data subset. We then obtain

P_{{\rm sig}}(t|\sigma_{t},b)=\frac{k_{+}(t)+r_{b}k_{-}(t)+2\sqrt{r_{b}}\,\text{Re}[X_{b}k_{+-}(t)]}{K_{+}+r_{b}K_{-}+2\sqrt{r_{b}}\,\text{Re}(X_{b}K_{+-})}\,.

(14)

The functions
k_{+}(t)
,
k_{-}(t)
and
k_{+-}(t)
are, respectively, the convolutions of
g^{2}_{+}(t)
,
g^{2}_{-}(t)
and
g^{*}_{+}(t)g_{-}(t)
and the resolution function
R(t|\sigma_{t})
. They are expressed analytically using the following relations
[
56
]
:

\displaystyle g^{2}_{+}(t)\otimes G(t|\mu,\sigma)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\bigl[\psi(\chi,\kappa_{+})+\psi(\chi,\kappa_{+i})+\psi(\chi,\kappa_{-i})+\psi(\chi,\kappa_{-})\bigr]\,,

(15)

\displaystyle g^{2}_{-}(t)\otimes G(t|\mu,\sigma)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\bigl[\psi(\chi,\kappa_{+})-\psi(\chi,\kappa_{+i})-\psi(\chi,\kappa_{-i})+\psi(\chi,\kappa_{-})\bigr]\,,

(16)

\displaystyle g_{+}^{*}(t)g_{-}(t)\otimes G(t|\mu,\sigma)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\bigl[\psi(\chi,\kappa_{+})+\psi(\chi,\kappa_{+i})-\psi(\chi,\kappa_{-i})-\psi(\chi,\kappa_{-})\bigr]\,,

(17)

with

\displaystyle\chi=\frac{t-\mu}{\sigma},\quad\kappa_{\pm}=(1\pm y)\Gamma\sigma,\quad\kappa_{\pm i}=(1\pm ix)\Gamma\sigma,

\displaystyle\psi(\chi,\kappa)=\frac{1}{2}e^{\frac{\kappa^{2}-2\chi\kappa}{2}}\left[1+{\rm erf}\left(\frac{\chi-\kappa}{\sqrt{2}}\right)\right]\,.

(18)

The integrals of
k_{+}(t)
,
k_{-}(t)
and
k_{+-}(t)
over decay time are

K_{+}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}k_{+}(t)dt,\quad K_{-}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}k_{-}(t)dt,\quad K_{+-}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}k_{+-}(t)dt\,.

(19)

The total PDF includes the contribution of the backgrounds. For a given bin
b
, it is defined as

P(t,\sigma_{t}|b)=\mathcal{C}_{b}\left[f_{{\rm sig}}^{b}P_{{\rm sig}}(t,\sigma_{t}|b)+f_{{\rm rnd}}^{b}P_{{\rm rnd}}(t,\sigma_{t}|b)+(1-f_{{\rm sig}}^{b}-f_{{\rm rnd}}^{b})P_{{\rm oth}}(t,\sigma_{t}|b)\right]\,,

(20)

with

\mathcal{C}_{b}=\left\{\begin{aligned} \frac{1}{1+C_{b}}\quad b>0\\
\frac{C_{b}}{1+C_{b}}\quad b<0\\
\end{aligned}\right.\,.

(21)

Here,
f_{{\rm sig}}^{b}
and
f_{{\rm rnd}}^{b}
are the fractions of signal and random-pion candidates in the Dalitz bin
b
, as determined from fits to the
(m(K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm S}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}),Q)
distributions (see
Section

V
). These fractions are evaluated independently for each data subset. The coefficients
\mathcal{C}_{b}
account for the multinomial splitting of the total sample into the 16 Dalitz-plot bins. They are related to the ratios between the total yields in bin
-b
and bin
+b
by

C_{b}=\frac{N_{-b}}{N_{b}}=\frac{\mathcal{N}_{-b}}{\mathcal{N}_{b}}\ \frac{f_{{\rm sig}}^{b}}{f_{\rm sig}^{-b}}\,,

(22)

where the signal yields
\mathcal{N}_{\pm b}
are expressed in terms of the hadronic parameters
(r_{b},c_{b},s_{b})
and of the mixing parameters
(x,y)
by integrating the signal PDF over the decay time (as in the denominator of
Equation

14
).

The PDF of the random-pion component,
P_{{\rm rnd}}(t,\sigma_{t}|b)
, is obtained assuming that pairing an unrelated soft pion to a correctly reconstructed
D^{0}
decay can only result in a fraction
f_{\rm mistag}
of candidates being mistagged. We then write

P_{{\rm rnd}}(t,\sigma_{t}|b)\propto(1-f_{\rm mistag})\mathcal{N}_{b}P_{{\rm sig}}(t,\sigma_{t}|b)+f_{\rm mistag}\mathcal{N}_{-b}P_{{\rm sig}}(t,\sigma_{t}|-b)\,.

(23)

The mistag fraction is determined from a fit to data candidates populating the
Q
sideband
[15,20]\mathrm{\,Me\kern-1.00006ptV}
to be
(42.11\pm 0.19)\%
. This fraction is fixed in the fit to the signal region.

Simulation shows that the distribution of the remaining background varies with the Dalitz-plot bin and with the data subset. Therefore, for each bin
b
and data subset, the PDF of the remaining background,
P_{{\rm oth}}(t,\sigma_{t}|b)
, is defined to be

P_{{\rm oth}}(t,\sigma_{t}|b)=P_{{\rm oth}}(t|\sigma_{t},b)P_{{\rm oth}}(\sigma_{t}|b)\,.

(24)

The first term is the sum of a Dirac
\delta
function and two exponential-decay components,
all convolved with the resolution function of
Equation

13
in which the mean parameters are shifted by the bin-dependent offset
\mu_{{\rm oth}}^{b}
:

\displaystyle P_{{\rm oth}}(t|\sigma_{t},b)

\displaystyle=(1-f_{\tau}^{b})R(t|\sigma_{t},\mu_{{\rm oth}}^{b})+f_{\tau}^{b}\left[f_{\tau_{1}}^{b}P_{\tau_{1}}(t|\sigma_{t},\mu_{{\rm oth}}^{b},\tau_{1}^{b})+(1-f_{\tau_{1}}^{b})P_{\tau_{2}}(t|\sigma_{t},\mu_{{\rm oth}}^{b},\tau_{2}^{b})\right]\,,

(25)

with

P_{\tau_{i}}(t|\sigma_{t},\mu_{{\rm oth}}^{b},\tau_{i}^{b})\propto e^{-t/\tau_{i}^{b}}\otimes R(t|\sigma_{t},\mu_{{\rm oth}}^{b})\,.

(26)

The PDF of
\sigma_{t}
is a histogram template derived, independently for each bin
b
and data subset, from the candidates populating the
m(K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm S}\pi^{+}\pi^{-})
sideband
[1.97,2.00]{\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c^{2}}
. Parameters in
P_{{\rm oth}}(t,\sigma_{t}|b)
are determined by fitting to the candidates populating the
m(K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm S}\pi^{+}\pi^{-})
sideband and fixed in the fit to the signal region.

In addition, in the fit to the signal region, we fix the
D^{0}
lifetime to the known value
[
3
]
and Gaussian constrain the
c_{b}
and
s_{b}
coefficients of the
X_{b}
hadronic parameters to values obtained from the combination of the BESIII and CLEO measurements
[
20
,
21
]
. The remaining floating parameters of the fit are
x
,
y
,
r_{b}
, and 13 parameters of the decay-time resolution functions.

The analysis procedure is validated with pseudo-experiments generated using the

Pseudo-experiments are also used to evaluate the systematic uncertainties resulting from the nonuniform Dalitz-plot efficiency, the resolution on the Dalitz-plot variables, the decay-time resolution model, and the background models. For each case, we generate pseudo-experiments that mimic alternative realistic models (derived from simulation) and fit them using the nominal models. The resulting average deviations of the measured mixing parameters from the generated values is assigned as the systematic uncertainty. The largest systematic uncertainty arises from the background model. The model neglects
\lesssim 0.1\%

D^{0}

(42.11\pm 0.19)\%

\pm 0.19\%

2.4\times 10^{-3}

D^{*+}

VIII
Conclusions

In summary, we perform a model-independent measurement of the
D^{0}
-
\kern 1.99997pt\overline{\kern-1.99997ptD}{}^{0}
mixing parameters using
2.05\times 10^{6}

D^{*+}
-tagged
D^{0}\rightarrow K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm S}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
decays reconstructed in Belle and Belle II data samples, which have integrated luminosities of 951
\mbox{\,fb}^{-1}
and 408
\mbox{\,fb}^{-1}
, respectively. Assuming
C\!P
symmetry, the mixing parameters are measured to be

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle=(4.0\pm 1.7\pm 0.4)\times 10^{-3}\,,

(27)

\displaystyle y

\displaystyle=(2.9\pm 1.4\pm 0.3)\times 10^{-3}\,,

(28)

where the first uncertainties are statistical and the second are systematic. The statistical uncertainties also include the contribution from the uncertainties in the external strong-phase inputs
[
20
,
21
]
. These results are consistent with previous determinations. They are approximately 20% and 14% more precise, and have significantly smaller systematic uncertainties, than the model-dependent Belle measurement presented in Ref.
[
28
]
.

Bohr inequalities for holomorphic mappings in higher-dimensional complex Banach spaces
Vasudevarao Allu, Raju Biswas and Rajib Mandal

Abstract
. In this paper, we investigates the Bohr phenomenon for holomorphic mappings F F from the unit ball 𝔹 X \mathbb{B}_{X} of a complex Banach space X X into the closure of the unit polydisc 𝔻 m \mathbb{D}^{m} within the space ℂ m \mathbb{C}^{m} . First, we prove an improved Bohr inequality involving the squared norms of the mapping and its homogeneous expansions. Second, we derive a refined Bohr inequality that incorporates a combination of the coefficient norms and their squares. Finally, we obtain a refined Bohr inequality for compositions F ∘ ν F\circ\nu , where ν \nu is a Schwarz mapping with a zero of order k k at the origin. For each result, we demonstrate that the derived Bohr radius is sharp.

1.
Introduction and preliminaries

Let
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
be a holomorphic function in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}
. The classical Bohr’s theorem states that if

|f(z)|\leq 1
in
\mathbb{D}
, then

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}\leq 1\quad\text{for}\quad|z|=r\leq 1/3.

(1.1)

In this context, the number
1/3
is known as the Bohr radius and cannot be further improved (see
[
7
]
). The inequality (
1.1
) is known as the classical Bohr inequality.
Bohr’s
[
7
]
original proof of the inequality (
1.1
) was restricted to situations where
r
did not exceed
1/6
. Later, Schur, Riesz and Wiener independently proved that the inequality (
1.1
) is valid for
r\leq 1/3
(see
[
7
]
).

In addition to the Bohr radius, the Rogosinski radius is another significant concept (see
[
30
]
). Kayumov
et al.

[
21
]
have defined the Bohr-Rogosinski radius as the greatest value
r_{0}
in
(0,1)
such that the inequality
R_{M}^{f}(z)\leq 1
is valid for all points within the disk
|z|\leq r_{0}
, where

\displaystyle R_{M}^{f}(z):=|f(z)|+\sum_{n=M}^{\infty}|a_{n}||z|^{n},\quad M\in\mathbb{N}

for a holomorphic function
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
in
\mathbb{D}
with
|f(z)|\leq 1
in
\mathbb{D}
. Furthermore, the authors
[
21
]
derived the following result concerning the Bohr-Rogosinski radius.

Theorem A
.

[
21
, Theorems 1 and 2, p. 3]

Let
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
be a holomorphic mapping in
\mathbb{D}
with
|f(z)|\leq 1
in
\mathbb{D}
. For
k,M\in\mathbb{N}
and
p\in\{1,2\}
, we have

\displaystyle\left|f\left(z^{k}\right)\right|^{p}+\sum_{n=M}^{\infty}|a_{n}||z|^{n}\leq 1\quad\text{for}\quad|z|\leq r_{k,p,M},

where
r_{k,p,M}\in(0,1)
is the unique root of the equation
(2/p)r^{M}(1+r^{k})-(1-r)(1-r^{k})=0
. The number
r_{k,p,M}
cannot be improved.

To organize and present our results coherently, it is necessary to introduce basic notations at this preliminary stage. For
z=(z_{1},z_{2},\cdots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, let
\|z\|_{\infty}=\max_{i=1,2,\cdots,n}|z_{i}|
denote the maximum norm and let

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}^{n}=\left\{z=(z_{1},z_{2},\cdots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:\|z\|_{\infty}<1\right\}

denote the unit polydisc in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Let
X
and
Y
be two complex Banach spaces. A mapping
P:X\to Y
is called a homogeneous polynomial of degree
k\in\mathbb{N}
if there exists a
k
-linear mapping

f:\underbrace{X\times X\times\ldots\times X}_{k\text{-times}}\to Y
such that
P(x)=f(x,x,\ldots,x)
for every
x\in X
. Note that if
P
is a homogeneous polynomial of degree
k
, then
P(\mu x)=\mu^{k}P(x)
for all
x\in X
and
\mu\in\mathbb{C}
. Note that if
P_{k}
is a
k
-homogeneous polynomial from
X
into
Y
, there exists a unique symmetric
k
-linear mapping
f
such that
P_{k}(x)=f(x,...,x)
(see
[
27
, Chapter 1 and 2]
).

For a domain
\mathcal{D}\subset X
, let
F:\mathcal{D}\to Y
be a holomorphic mapping and let
D^{k}F(x)
denote the
k
-th Fréchet derivative of
F
at
x\in\mathcal{D}
, which is a bounded symmetric
k
-linear mapping from
\underbrace{X\times X\times\ldots\times X}_{k\text{-times}}
to
Y
. If
\mathcal{D}
contains the origin, then
any holomorphic mapping
F:\mathcal{D}\to Y
can be expanded into the following series:

\displaystyle F(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}D^{k}F(0)(z^{k})

(1.2)

for all
z
in some neighborhood of the origin (see
[
11
, Chapter 6, p. 198]
). Note that

\displaystyle D^{0}F(0)(z^{0})=F(0)\quad\text{and}\quad D^{k}F(0)(z^{k})=D^{k}F(0)\underbrace{(z,z,\ldots,z)}_{k\text{-times}},\quad k\geq 1.

As
(1/k!)D^{k}F(0)(z^{k})
is a bounded symmetric
k
-linear mapping, we use the notation
P_{k}(z)=(1/k!)D^{k}F(0)(z^{k})
throughout the paper.
In the context of a balanced domain
\mathcal{D}
, the series (
1.2
) converges uniformly to
f
in some neighborhood of each compact subset contained in

\mathcal{D}
. Furthermore, if the function
f
is bounded on the ball
B(z,r)\subseteq\mathcal{D}
, then uniform convergence is guaranteed on
B(z,t)
for any
0<t<r
(see
[
27
, Theorems 7.11 and 7.13]
).

Let
L(X,\mathbb{C})
denote the space of all continuous linear functionals from
X
to
\mathbb{C}
. For each
x\in X\setminus\{0\}
, let

\displaystyle T(x)=\left\{l_{x}\in L(X,\mathbb{C}):l_{x}(x)=\|x\|,~\|l_{x}\|=1\right\},

where
\|l_{x}\|=\sup\{|l_{x}(w)|:\|w\|=1\}
.
In view of the Hahn-Banach theorem,
T(x)
is nonempty (see
[
11
, p. 202]
).

In 2018, Kayumov and Ponnusamy
[
19
]
obtained several Bohr inequalities for holomorphic functions with lacunary series in the unit disc
\mathbb{D}
. The study of Bohr’s phenomenon in the context of higher-dimensional complex Banach spaces represents a significant advancement in the field of complex analysis.
In 2020, Liu and Liu
[
25

made significant contributions to the field by extending the results of Kayumov and Ponnusamy
[
19
]
to holomorphic mappings
f
with
lacunary series from the unit polydisc
\mathbb{D}^{m}
into
\mathbb{D}^{m}
and from the unit ball
\mathbb{B}_{X}
of a complex Banach space
X
into
\mathbb{B}_{X}
.
For an in-depth study of Bohr’s phenomenon concerning holomorphic or pluriharmonic mappings with values in higher-dimensional complex Banach spaces using the homogeneous polynomial expansions, we refer to

[
12
,
13
,
23
,
1
,
14
,
15
]
.
Lin
et al.

[
24
]
have studied other types of Bohr’s inequality for holomorphic mappings
f
with lacunary series from the unit polydisc
\mathbb{D}^{m}
into
\mathbb{D}^{m}
.

The aforementioned studies primarily explore mappings between domains of the same type, such as from a ball to another ball or from a polydisc to another polydisc, using their inherent symmetries. The case of mappings from a Banach space ball into a polydisc,
i.e.,

F:\mathbb{B}_{X}\to\overline{\mathbb{D}^{m}}
, presents a distinct challenge due to the structural asymmetry between the domain and the codomain. This setting disrupts the standard techniques and introduces a critical dependence on the geometry of the initial point
F(0)=a
.
This paper addresses this gap by providing a comprehensive study of the Bohr phenomenon in this general and asymmetric setting.
The following question naturally arises.

Question 1.1
.

Let
\mathbb{B}_{X}
be the unit ball of a complex Banach space
X
.
To what extent can the classical Bohr inequality be generalized to derive sharp versions for holomorphic mappings
F:\mathbb{B}_{X}\to\overline{\mathbb{D}^{m}}
, and how does the geometry of the initial point
F(0)
affect the associated Bohr radius?

The purpose of this paper is to provide an affirmative answer to Question
1.1
.

2.
Key lemmas

The following are key lemmas of this paper and will be used to prove the main results.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
10
]
Suppose
f
is analytic in
\mathbb{D}
with
|f(z)|\leq 1
in
\mathbb{D}
, then we have

\displaystyle\frac{\left|f^{(n)}(z)\right|}{n!}\leq\frac{1-|f(z)|^{2}}{(1-|z|)^{n-1}(1-|z|^{2})}\quad\text{and}\quad|a_{n}|\leq 1-|a_{0}|^{2}\quad\text{for}\quad n\geq 1,\quad|z|<1.

Lemma 2.2
.

[
17
]
If
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
is analytic in
\mathbb{D}
such that
|g(z)|\leq 1
in
\mathbb{D}
and
|a_{0}|<1
, then we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{2}r^{pn}\leq r^{p}\frac{(1-|a_{0}|^{2})^{2}}{1-|a_{0}|^{2}r^{p}},\quad\text{where}\quad p\in\mathbb{N}\quad\text{and}\quad 0<r\leq 1.

Lemma 2.3
.

[
28
]
If
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
is analytic in
\mathbb{D}
such that
|g(z)|\leq 1
in
\mathbb{D}
and
|a_{0}|<1
, then we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}+\left(\frac{1}{1+|a_{0}|}+\frac{r}{1-r}\right)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{2}r^{2n}\leq(1-|a_{0}|^{2})\frac{r}{1-r},\quad\text{where}\quad 0\leq r<1.

Lemma 2.4
.

[
9
]
Suppose that
\mathbb{B}_{X}
and
\mathbb{B}_{Y}
are the unit balls of the complex Banach spaces
X
and
Y,
respectively. Let
f:\mathbb{B}_{X}\to\overline{\mathbb{B}_{Y}}
be a holomorphic mapping. Then,

\displaystyle\|f(z)\|_{Y}\leq\frac{\|f(0)\|_{Y}+\|z\|_{X}}{1+\|f(0)\|_{Y}\|z\|_{X}}\quad\text{for}\quad z\in\mathbb{B}_{X}.

This estimate is sharp with equality possible for each value of
\|f(0)\|_{Y}
and for each
z\in\mathbb{B}_{X}
.

Lemma 2.5
.

[
11
, Lemma 6.1.28, P. 200]
Let
X
and
Y
be two complex Banach spaces.
Let
M>0
,
\mathbb{B}_{X}
be the unit ball in the complex Banach space
X
and
f:\mathbb{B}_{X}\to Y
be a holomorphic mapping such that
f(0)=Df(0)=\cdots=D^{k-1}f(0)=0
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
\|f(z)\|_{Y}<M
for
z\in\mathbb{B}_{X}
. Then,
\|f(z)\|_{Y}\leq M\|z\|_{X}^{k}
for
z\in\mathbb{B}_{X}
.

3.
Main results

In the following result, we establish an improved version of Bohr inequality for holomorphic mappings
F:\mathbb{B}_{X}\to\overline{\mathbb{D}^{m}}
, where
\mathbb{B}_{X}
is the unit ball of a complex Banach space
X
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
a=(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{m})\in\overline{\mathbb{D}^{m}}
and let
F:\mathbb{B}_{X}\to\overline{\mathbb{D}^{m}}
be a holomorphic mapping with the following series expansion:

\displaystyle F(z)=a+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}P_{n}(z)\quad\text{for}\quad z\in\mathbb{B}_{X},

where
P_{n}(z)=(1/n!)D^{n}F(0)(z^{n})
,
n\geq 1
. Assume that
|a_{i}|=\|a\|_{\infty}
for
1\leq i\leq m
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\|F(z)\|_{\infty}^{2}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|P_{n}(z)\|_{\infty}^{2}\leq 1

(3.1)

for
\|z\|_{X}=r\leq\sqrt{11/27}
. The number
\sqrt{11/27}
cannot be improved.

Proof.

Let
z\in\mathbb{B}_{X}\setminus\{0\}
be fixed and denote
z_{0}=z/\|z\|_{X}
. Then,
z_{0}\in\partial\mathbb{B}_{X}
and
z=rz_{0}
, where
\|z\|_{X}=r
.
As
F(z)=(F_{1}(z),F_{2}(z),\ldots,F_{m}(z))
, we consider the function
g_{i}:\mathbb{D}\to\overline{\mathbb{D}}
defined by

\displaystyle g_{i}(t):=F_{i}(tz_{0})=a_{i}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(P_{n})_{i}(z_{0})t^{n},\quad t\in\mathbb{D}\quad\text{and}\quad i\in\{1,2,\ldots,m\}.

The condition
\|a\|_{\infty}=|a_{j}|
for each
j\in\{1,2,\ldots,m\}
ensures that the maximum modulus of the initial value is attained in every component, which is essential for obtaining the uniform estimates by maximizing over the component index
j
.
In view of Lemma
2.2
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|(P_{n})_{i}(z_{0})|^{2}|t|^{2n}\leq|t|^{2}\frac{\left(1-|a_{i}|^{2}\right)^{2}}{1-|a_{i}|^{2}|t|^{2}}=|t|^{2}\frac{\left(1-\|a\|_{\infty}^{2}\right)^{2}}{1-\|a\|_{\infty}^{2}|t|^{2}},\quad|t|<1

(3.2)

for each fixed
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,m\}
.
Let
k\in\{1,2,\ldots,m\}
be such that
|(P_{n})_{k}(z_{0})|=\|P_{n}(z_{0})\|_{\infty}=\max_{1\leq i\leq m}\left|(P_{n})_{i}(z_{0})\right|
. By setting
|t|=r<1
in (
3.2
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|P_{n}(z_{0})\|_{\infty}^{2}r^{2n}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\max_{1\leq i\leq m}\left\{|(P_{n})_{i}(z_{0})|\right\}\right)^{2}r^{2n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|(P_{n})_{k}(z_{0})|^{2}r^{2n}

(3.3)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle r^{2}\frac{\left(1-\|a\|_{\infty}^{2}\right)^{2}}{1-\|a\|_{\infty}^{2}r^{2}}.

Let
x=\|a\|_{\infty}\in[0,1]
. Evidently, if
x=1
, then we have
P_{n}(z_{0})=0
for
n\geq 1
. Thus, the inequality (
3.1
) holds for
z=rz_{0}
and
r\in[0,1)
.
Let
x\in[0,1)
. Using (
3.3
), for
r\in(0,1)
, we have

\displaystyle\|F(rz_{0})\|_{\infty}^{2}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|P_{n}(rz_{0})\|_{\infty}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{x+r}{1+xr}\right)^{2}+r^{2}\frac{\left(1-x^{2}\right)^{2}}{1-x^{2}r^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+G_{1}(x,r),

where

\displaystyle G_{1}(x,r)=\left(\frac{x+r}{1+xr}\right)^{2}+r^{2}\frac{\left(1-x^{2}\right)^{2}}{1-x^{2}r^{2}}-1,\quad x\in[0,1)\quad\text{and}\quad r\in[0,1).

To establish our result, it suffices to demonstrate that
G_{1}(x,r)\leq 0
.
Differentiating partially
G_{1}(x,r)
with respect to
r
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial r}G_{1}(x,r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\left(\frac{x+r}{1+xr}\right)\left(\frac{(1+xr)-(x+r)x}{(1+xr)^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{2r\left(1-x^{2}\right)^{2}}{1-r^{2}x^{2}}+\frac{2r^{3}x^{2}\left(1-x^{2}\right)^{2}}{\left(1-r^{2}x^{2}\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\left(1-x^{2}\right)\left(x+r\right)}{\left(1+xr\right)^{3}}+\frac{2r\left(1-x^{2}\right)^{2}}{1-r^{2}x^{2}}+\frac{2r^{3}x^{2}\left(1-x^{2}\right)^{2}}{\left(1-r^{2}x^{2}\right)^{2}}\geq 0,

which shows that
G_{1}(x,r)
is a monotonically decreasing function of
r\in[0,1)
and it follows that for
r\leq\sqrt{11/27}
, we have

\displaystyle G_{1}(x,r)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle G_{1}\left(x,\sqrt{11/27}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{3\left(121x^{6}+66\sqrt{33}x^{5}-121x^{4}-132\sqrt{33}x^{3}+135x^{2}+66\sqrt{33}x-135\right)}{\left(\sqrt{33}x+9\right)^{2}\left(27-11x^{2}\right)}.

Let
G_{2}(x):=121x^{6}+66\sqrt{33}x^{5}-121x^{4}-132\sqrt{33}x^{3}+135x^{2}+66\sqrt{33}x-135
. Following a basic computation, we obtain

\displaystyle G_{2}(x)=121(x^{2}-1)\left(x+5\sqrt{\frac{3}{11}}\right)\left(x+3\sqrt{\frac{3}{11}}\right)\left(x-\sqrt{\frac{3}{11}}\right)^{2}\leq 0,\quad x\in[0,1)

and
G_{2}(\sqrt{3/11})=0
. Therefore,
G_{1}(x,r)\leq 0
for
r\leq\sqrt{11/27}
.

To prove the sharpness of the constant
\sqrt{11/27}
, we consider the function
F_{1}:\mathbb{B}_{X}\to\overline{\mathbb{D}^{m}}
defined by

\displaystyle F_{1}(z)=g(l_{z_{0}}(z))u\quad\quad\text{for}\quad z\in\mathbb{B}_{X},

where
z_{0}\in\partial\mathbb{B}_{X}
,
l_{z_{0}}\in T(z_{0})
,
u=(u_{1},u_{2},\ldots,u_{m})\in\partial\mathbb{D}^{m}
with
|u_{1}|=|u_{2}|=\dots=|u_{m}|=1
, and

\displaystyle g(t)=\frac{\lambda+t}{1+\lambda t},\quad t\in\mathbb{D}\quad\text{and}\quad\lambda=\sqrt{3/11}.

Therefore, for
r\in(0,1)
, we have

\displaystyle F_{1}(rz_{0})=g(l_{z_{0}}(rz_{0}))u=g\left(r\|z_{0}\|\right)u=\frac{\lambda+r}{1+\lambda r}u=a+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}P_{n}(rz_{0}),

where
a=\lambda u
and
P_{n}(z_{0})=(-1)^{n}\lambda^{n-1}(\lambda^{2}-1)u
for
n\geq 1
.
Thus, we have

\displaystyle\|F_{1}(rz_{0})\|_{\infty}^{2}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|P_{n}(rz_{0})\|_{\infty}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\lambda+r}{1+\lambda r}\right)^{2}+(1-\lambda^{2})^{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda^{2(n-1)}r^{2n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\lambda+r}{1+\lambda r}\right)^{2}+(1-\lambda^{2})^{2}\frac{r^{2}}{1-\lambda^{2}r^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{64r^{2}}{121-33r^{2}}+\frac{\left(11r+\sqrt{33}\right)^{2}}{\left(\sqrt{33}r+11\right)^{2}}>1\quad\text{for}\quad r>\sqrt{\frac{11}{27}},

which shows that the constant
\sqrt{11/27}
cannot be improved. This completes the proof.
∎

The condition
\|a\|_{\infty}=|a_{j}|
for each
j\in\{1,2,\ldots,m\}
is vital in Theorem
3.1
and cannot be omitted. To illustrate the importance of this condition, we present the following example.

Example 3.1
.

Consider the holomorphic mapping
F:\mathbb{B}_{X}\to\overline{\mathbb{D}^{2}}
with

F(z)=\left(\frac{a_{1}+l_{z_{0}}(z)}{1+a_{1}l_{z_{0}}(z)},\frac{a_{2}+l_{z_{0}}(z)}{1+a_{2}l_{z_{0}}(z)}\right),~z\in\mathbb{B}_{X},

where
a_{1}\in(0,1)
,
a_{2}\in(0,1)
,
z_{0}\in\partial\mathbb{B}_{X}
and
l_{z_{0}}\in T(z_{0})
. For
r\in(0,1)
, we have

\displaystyle F(rz_{0})=\left(\frac{a_{1}+l_{z_{0}}(rz_{0})}{1+a_{1}l_{z_{0}}(rz_{0})},\frac{a_{2}+l_{z_{0}}(rz_{0})}{1+a_{2}l_{z_{0}}(rz_{0})}\right)=\left(\frac{a_{1}+r}{1+a_{1}r},\frac{a_{2}+r}{1+a_{2}r}\right)=a+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}P_{n}(rz_{0}),

where
a=(a_{1},a_{2})
and
P_{n}(rz_{0})=\left((-1)^{n}(a_{1}^{2}-1)a_{1}^{n-1}r^{n},(-1)^{n}(a_{2}^{2}-1)a_{2}^{n-1}r^{n}\right)
for
n\geq 1
.
Note that

\displaystyle\|P_{n}(rz_{0})\|_{\infty}=\max\left\{\left|(-1)^{n}(a_{1}^{2}-1)a_{1}^{n-1}r^{n}\right|,\left|(-1)^{n}(a_{2}^{2}-1)a_{2}^{n-1}r^{n}\right|\right\}\quad\text{for}\quad n\geq 1.

Let
H_{k}(t):=(1-t^{2})t^{k-1}
, where
t\in(0,1)
and
k\;(\geq 1)
. Evidently,

H_{k}^{\prime}(t)=(k-1)t^{k-2}-(k+1)t^{k}=(k-1)t^{k-2}\left(1-\frac{k+1}{k-1}t^{2}\right)\leq 0\quad\text{for}\quad t^{2}\geq\frac{k-1}{k+1}.

Thus,
H_{k}(t)
is a monotonically decreasing function of
t
when
t\geq\sqrt{(k-1)/(k+1)}
.
Suppose that
\sqrt{1/3}<a_{1}<a_{2}<1
.
Hence, we have
\|P_{n}(rz_{0})\|_{\infty}=(1-a_{1}^{2})a_{1}^{n-1}r^{n}
for
n=1,2
and
\|P_{n}(rz_{0})\|_{\infty}\geq(1-a_{2}^{2})a_{2}^{n-1}r^{n}
for
n\geq 3
.
Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\|F(rz_{0})\|_{\infty}^{2}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|P_{n}(rz_{0})\|_{\infty}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{a_{2}+r}{1+a_{2}r}\right)^{2}+(1-a_{1}^{2})^{2}r^{2}(1+a_{1}^{2}r^{2})+\sum_{n=3}^{\infty}\|P_{n}(rz_{0})\|_{\infty}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{a_{2}+r}{1+a_{2}r}\right)^{2}+(1-a_{1}^{2})^{2}r^{2}(1+a_{1}^{2}r^{2})+(1-a_{2}^{2})^{2}\sum_{n=3}^{\infty}a_{2}^{2(n-1)}r^{2n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{a_{2}+r}{1+a_{2}r}\right)^{2}+(1-a_{1}^{2})^{2}r^{2}(1+a_{1}^{2}r^{2})+(1-a_{2}^{2})^{2}\frac{a_{2}^{4}r^{6}}{1-a_{2}^{2}r^{2}}

Thus, for each
r\in(0,1)
, we have

\displaystyle\|F(rz_{0})\|_{\infty}^{2}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|P_{n}(rz_{0})\|_{\infty}^{2}\geq 1+(1-a_{1}^{2})^{2}r^{2}(1+a_{1}^{2}r^{2})>1\quad\text{as}\quad a_{2}\to 1^{-}.

In the following theorem, we establish a refined version of the Bohr inequality for holomorphic mapping
F:\mathbb{B}_{X}\to\overline{\mathbb{D}^{m}}
, where
\mathbb{B}_{X}
is the unit ball of a complex Banach space
X
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\max_{1\leq i\leq m}\left|(P_{n})_{i}(z_{0})\right|\right)r^{n}+\left(\frac{1}{1+\|a\|_{\infty}}+\frac{r}{1-r}\right)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\max_{1\leq i\leq m}\left|(P_{n})_{i}(z_{0})\right|\right)^{2}r^{2n}

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left|(P_{n})_{k}(z_{0})\right|r^{n}+\left(\frac{1}{1+\|a\|_{\infty}}+\frac{r}{1-r}\right)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left|(P_{n})_{k}(z_{0})\right|^{2}r^{2n}\hskip 85.35826pt

In the following result, we establish a refined version of the Bohr inequality for holomorphic mapping

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
a=(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{m})\in\overline{\mathbb{D}^{m}}

be a holomorphic mapping with the following expansion

\displaystyle F(z)=a+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}P_{n}(z),\quad z\in\mathbb{B}_{X},

. Then, we have

\displaystyle\|F(\nu(z))\|_{\infty}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|P_{n}(z)\|_{\infty}+\left(\frac{1}{1+\|a\|_{\infty}}+\frac{\|z\|_{X}}{1-\|z\|_{X}}\right)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|P_{n}(z)\|_{\infty}^{2}\leq 1

Let
z_{0}\in\partial\mathbb{B}_{X}

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left|(P_{n})_{i}(z_{0})\right||t|^{n}+\left(\frac{1}{1+|a_{i}|}+\frac{|t|}{1-|t|}\right)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left|(P_{n})_{i}(z_{0})\right|^{2}|t|^{2n}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1-|a_{i}|^{2})\frac{|t|}{1-|t|}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-\|a\|_{\infty}^{2})\frac{|t|}{1-|t|}

for each fixed
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,m\}
. Using the argument as in the proof of Theorem

) and (
3
).

. In view of Lemmas

\displaystyle\|F(\nu(rz_{0}))\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{\|F(0)\|_{\infty}+\|\nu(rz_{0})\|_{X}}{1+\|F(0)\|_{\infty}\|\nu(rz_{0})\|_{X}}\leq\frac{\|a\|_{\infty}+r^{k}}{1+\|a\|_{\infty}r^{k}},\quad r\in[0,1).

. Using (

\displaystyle-\lim_{\lambda\to 1^{-}}\frac{1-r^{2k}}{\left(1+\lambda r^{k}\right)^{2}}+\frac{2r}{1-r}=-\frac{1-r^{k}}{1+r^{k}}+\frac{2r}{1-r}>0\quad\text{for}\quad r>r_{k},

which shows that the constant
r_{k}
cannot be improved. This completes the proof.
∎

The condition
\|a\|_{\infty}=|a_{j}|
for each
j\in\{1,2,\ldots,m\}
is crucial for Theorem
3.3
and cannot be omitted. To illustrate the importance of this condition, we present the following example.

Example 3.3
.

Consider the holomorphic mapping
F:\mathbb{B}_{X}\to\overline{\mathbb{D}^{2}}
with

F(z)=\left(\frac{a_{1}+l_{z_{0}}(z)}{1+a_{1}l_{z_{0}}(z)},\frac{a_{2}+l_{z_{0}}(z)}{1+a_{2}l_{z_{0}}(z)}\right),~z\in\mathbb{B}_{X},

where
0<a_{1}<a_{2}<1
,
z_{0}\in\partial\mathbb{B}_{X}
and
l_{z_{0}}\in T(z_{0})
. Let
\nu(z)=z\left(l_{z_{0}}(z)\right)^{k-1}
. Using the argument as in Example
3.1
, for
r\in(0,1)
, we have

\displaystyle F(rz_{0})=a+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}P_{n}(rz_{0}),

where
a=(a_{1},a_{2})
and
P_{n}(rz_{0})=\left((-1)^{n}(a_{1}^{2}-1)a_{1}^{n-1}r^{n},(-1)^{n}(a_{2}^{2}-1)a_{2}^{n-1}r^{n}\right)
for
n\geq 1
. Thus, we have
\|P_{1}(rz_{0})\|_{\infty}=(1-a_{1}^{2})r
and
\|P_{n}(rz_{0})\|_{\infty}\geq(1-a_{2}^{2})a_{2}^{n-1}r^{n}
for
n\geq 2
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\|F(\nu(rz_{0}))\|_{\infty}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|P_{n}(rz_{0})\|_{\infty}+\left(\frac{1}{1+\|a\|_{\infty}}+\frac{\|rz_{0}\|_{X}}{1-\|rz_{0}\|_{X}}\right)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|P_{n}(rz_{0})\|_{\infty}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{a_{2}+r^{k}}{1+a_{2}r^{k}}+(1-a_{1}^{2})r+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\|P_{n}(rz_{0})\|_{\infty}+\left(\frac{1}{1+a_{2}}+\frac{r}{1-r}\right)(1-a_{1}^{2})^{2}r^{2}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{1}{1+a_{2}}+\frac{r}{1-r}\right)\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\|P_{n}(z_{0})\|_{\infty}^{2}r^{2n}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{a_{2}+r}{1+a_{2}r}\right)^{2}+(1-a_{1}^{2})r+(1-a_{2}^{2})\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}a_{2}^{n-1}r^{n}+\frac{1+a_{2}r}{(1-r)(1+a_{2})}(1-a_{1}^{2})^{2}r^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1+a_{2}r}{(1-r)(1+a_{2})}(1-a_{2}^{2})^{2}\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}a_{2}^{2(n-1)}r^{2n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{a_{2}+r}{1+a_{2}r}\right)^{2}+(1-a_{1}^{2})r+(1-a_{2}^{2})\frac{a_{2}r^{2}}{1-a_{2}r}+\frac{1+a_{2}r}{(1-r)(1+a_{2})}(1-a_{1}^{2})^{2}r^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1+a_{2}r}{(1-r)(1+a_{2})}(1-a_{2}^{2})^{2}\frac{a_{2}^{2}r^{4}}{1-a_{2}^{2}r^{2}}.

Thus, for each
r\in(0,1)
and
a_{2}\to 1^{-}
, we have

\displaystyle\|F(\nu(rz_{0}))\|_{\infty}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|P_{n}(rz_{0})\|_{\infty}+\left(\frac{1}{1+\|a\|_{\infty}}+\frac{\|rz_{0}\|_{X}}{1-\|rz_{0}\|_{X}}\right)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|P_{n}(rz_{0})\|_{\infty}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 1+(1-a_{1}^{2})r+\frac{1+a_{2}r}{(1-r)(1+a_{2})}(1-a_{1}^{2})^{2}r^{2}>1.

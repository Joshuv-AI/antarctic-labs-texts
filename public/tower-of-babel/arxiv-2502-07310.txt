Total k k -coalition: bounds, exact values and an application to double coalition
Boštjan Brešar, Sandi Klavžar, Babak Samadi

Abstract
Let G = ( V ⁡ ( G ) , E ⁡ ( G ) ) G=\big(V(G),E(G)\big) be a graph with minimum degree k k . A subset S ⊆ V ⁡ ( G ) S\subseteq V(G) is called a total k k -dominating set if every vertex in G G has at least k k neighbors in S S . Two disjoint sets A , B ⊂ V ⁡ ( G ) A,B\subset V(G) form a total k k -coalition in G G if none of them is a total k k -dominating set in G G but their union A ∪ B A\cup B is a total k k -dominating set. A vertex partition Ω = { V 1 , … , V | Ω | } \Omega=\{V_{1},\ldots,V_{|\Omega|}\} of G G is a total k k -coalition partition if each set V i V_{i} forms a total k k -coalition with another set V j V_{j} . The total k k -coalition number TC k ​ ( G ) {\rm TC}_{k}(G) of G G equals the maximum cardinality of a total k k -coalition partition of G G . In this paper, the above-mentioned concepts are investigated from combinatorial points of view. Several sharp lower and upper bounds on TC k ​ ( G ) {\rm TC}_{k}(G) are proved, where the main emphasis is given on the invariant when k = 2 k=2 . As a consequence, the exact values of TC 2 ​ ( G ) {\rm TC}_{2}(G) when G G is a cubic graph or a 4 4 -regular graph are obtained. By using similar methods, an open question posed by Henning and Mojdeh regarding double coalition is answered. Moreover, TC 3 ​ ( G ) {\rm TC}_{3}(G) is determined when G G is a cubic graph.

In this paper, we present a coalition counterpart to total

In Section

-domatic partition

Proposition 2.1
.

Any graph

Let

First, we present general lower and upper bounds on the total
k
-coalition number of a graph in terms of minimum and maximum degrees, respectively. Then, with emphasis on

in terms of both minimum and maximum degrees, and show that they are sharp by exhibiting an infinite family of graphs, which is illustrated in Example

Theorem 3.1
.

For any graph
G
with minimum degree at least
k
,
\TC_{k}(G)\geq\delta(G)-k+2
. This bound is sharp.

Proof.

Let
v\in V(G)
be a vertex of minimum degree and let
N(v)=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{\delta(G)}\}
. We set
V^{\prime}=V(G)\setminus\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{\delta(G)-k+1}\}
and
V_{i}=\{v_{i}\}
for
i\in[\delta(G)-k+1]
. Obviously, no set
V_{i}
is a total
k
-dominating set in
G
. Moreover, since
v
has precisely
k-1
neighbors in
V^{\prime}
, it follows that
V^{\prime}
is not a total
k
-dominating set in
G
either. Let
V_{i}
be any set where
i\in[\delta(G)-k+1]
and
u
be any vertex in
G
. On the other hand, because
|N(u)\cap\big(V(G)\setminus(V^{\prime}\cup V_{i})\big)|\leq\delta(G)-k\leq\deg_{G}(u)-k
, it follows that
u
is adjacent to at least
k
vertices in
V^{\prime}\cup V_{i}
. Hence,
V_{i}
and
V^{\prime}
form a total
k
-coalition in
G
for each
i\in[\delta(G)-k+1]
. The above discussion shows that
\Omega=\big\{V_{1},\ldots,V_{\delta(G)-k+1},V^{\prime}\big\}
is a total
k
-coalition partition in
G
. Thus,
\TC_{k}(G)\geq|\Omega|=\delta(G)-k+2
.

To see that the bound is sharp, consider the cycle
C_{n}
and the complete graph
K_{n}
, for which we have
\TC_{k}(C_{n})=2
(for
k=2
) and
\TC_{k}(K_{n})=n-k+1
(for each positive integer

with

).
∎

Note that no two disjoint subsets

with

form a total

-coalition in any graph

\delta(G)\geq k

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
G
be a graph of order
n
with
\delta(G)\geq k\geq 2
. Then,
\TC_{k}(G)\leq n-k+1
holds with equality if and only if
G\cong K_{k}\vee G^{\prime}
, where
G^{\prime}
is any graph of order

.

Proof.

Suppose first that
\TC_{k}(G)=n-k+1
and that
\Omega=\{V_{1},\ldots,V_{|\Omega|}\}
is a
\TC_{k}(G)
-partition. Then
|\Omega|=n-k+1
. Letting
V_{1}
form a total
k
-coalition with
V_{2}
, we get

n=|V_{1}\cup V_{2}|+\sum_{i=3}^{|\Omega|}|V_{i}|\geq(k+1)+|\Omega|-2
.

Due to this, the equality
|\Omega|=n-k+1
guarantees that

(i)

|V_{1}\cup V_{2}|=k+1
and
|V_{i}|=1
for each
i\in[|\Omega|]\setminus\{1,2\}
,

(ii)

G[V_{1}\cup V_{2}]\cong K_{k+1}
, and

(iii)

every singleton set
|V_{i}|
, for
i\in[|\Omega|]\setminus\{1,2\}
, forms a total
k
-coalition with
V_{1}
or
V_{2}
.

By taking the above statements into account, without loss of generality, we may assume that
|V_{1}|=k
and
|V_{2}|=1
. Therefore,
\Omega=\{V_{1}\}\cup\big\{\{v\}\mid v\in V(G)\setminus V_{1}\}\big\}
, in which
\{v\}
forms a total
k
-coalition with
V_{1}
for each
v\in V(G)\setminus V_{1}
. In particular,
vx\in E(G)
for all vertices

and
v\in V(G)\setminus V_{1}
. Therefore,
G\cong K_{k}\vee G^{\prime}
, where
G^{\prime}=G[V(G)\setminus V_{1}]
.

Conversely, assume that
G\cong K_{k}\vee G^{\prime}
, in which
G^{\prime}
is any graph of order

. It is then easy to see that
\{V(K_{k})\}\cup\big\{\{v\}\mid v\in V(G^{\prime})\big\}
is a total

\TC_{k}(G)\leq n-k+1
.

The following lemma will turn out to be useful in several places of this paper.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
G
be a graph with minimum degree at least
k
and let
\Omega
be a
\TC_{k}(G)
-partition. If
A\in\Omega
, then
A
forms a total
k
-coalition with at most
\Delta(G)-k+1
sets in
\Omega
.

Proof.

Since
A
is not a total
k
-dominating set in
G
, there exists a vertex

such that
|N(v)\cap A|\leq k-1
. Let
A
form a total
k
-coalition with

. By definition and since

does not totally
k
-dominate

, it follows that
v
has at least
k
neighbors in
A\cup A_{t}
and
|N(v)\cap A_{i}|\geq 1
for each

. Then

\Delta(G)\geq|N(v)|\geq|N(v)\cap A_{1}|+\cdots+|N(v)\cap A_{t-1}|+|N(v)\cap(A\cup A_{t})|\geq t-1+k\,,

which proves the result.

Associated with any total
k
-coalition partition

-coalition graph

\TC_{k}\mathrm{G}(G,\Omega)
has the set of vertices

are adjacent if they form a total

-coalition in

\alpha(G)
and

denote the independence number and the vertex cover number of

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
G
be a graph of minimum degree at least
2
. If
\Omega
is a total
2
-coalition partition of
G
, then the following statements hold.

(
i
)

\Delta\big(\TC_{2}G(G,\Omega)\big)\leq\Delta(G)-1
, and

(
ii
)

\beta\big(\TC_{2}G(G,\Omega)\big)\leq\delta(G)-1
.

Proof.

(i)
The statement follows from Lemma
3.3
with
k=2
.

(ii)
Let
v
be a vertex of minimum degree in
G
. We set
\Omega^{\prime}=\{A\in\Omega\mid N(v)\cap A\neq\emptyset\}
. Obviously,
|\Omega^{\prime}|\leq\delta(G)
. Suppose that
|\Omega^{\prime}|=\delta(G)
. This shows that every set in
\Omega^{\prime}
contains precisely one vertex from

. In such a situation,

has at most one neighbor in
A\cup(\cup_{S\in\Omega\setminus\Omega^{\prime}}S)
for each

. This shows that no two sets in
\mathcal{I}_{A}=\{A\}\cup\{S\mid S\in\Omega\setminus\Omega^{\prime}\}
form a total

-coalition in

, in which

is any set in
\Omega^{\prime}
. Equivalently,
\mathcal{I}_{A}
is an independent set in
\TC_{2}G(G,\Omega)
, and hence
\alpha\big(\TC_{2}G(G,\Omega)\big)\geq|\mathcal{I}_{A}|=|\Omega|-\delta(G)+1
. Using the equality
\alpha(H)+\beta(H)=|V(H)|
, which holds for each graph

(the Gallai Theorem), we infer that
\beta\big(\TC_{2}G(G,\Omega)\big)\leq\delta(G)-1
. Moreover, if
|\Omega^{\prime}|<\delta(G)
, then no two sets in
\{S\mid S\in\Omega\setminus\Omega^{\prime}\}
form a total

-coalition in

. In a similar fashion, the inequality
\beta\big(\TC_{2}G(G,\Omega)\big)\leq\delta(G)-1
is obtained.

Theorem 3.5

If

\TC_{2}(G)\leq\max\Big\{\Delta(G),\left\lfloor\frac{\delta(G)}{2}\right\rfloor(\Delta(G)-4)+\delta(G)\Big\}

Proof.

Let
\Omega
be a
\TC_{2}(G)
-partition. If
\delta(G)=2

\beta\big(\TC_{2}G(G,\Omega)\big)=1
by Lemma

\TC_{2}G(G,\Omega)
has a universal vertex

forms a total

-coalition with any other set in
\Omega

\TC_{2}(G)\leq\Delta(G)
in view of Lemma

Now let
\delta(G)=3

(resp.
u
) be a vertex of maximum (resp. minimum) degree in

. We are going to show that
\TC_{2}(G)\leq\Delta(G)
also in this case. Suppose that
\TC_{2}(G)>\Delta(G)
and that
\Omega=\{V_{1},\ldots,V_{|\Omega|}\}
is a
\TC_{2}(G)
-partition. We distinguish two cases depending on the behavior of the sets in
\Omega

Case 1.

|N(v)\cap V_{i}|\leq 1
,

.

Since no two vertices in
N(v)
belong to the same set in
\Omega
, we may assume that

. Note that

forms a total

-coalition with

for some

. Since
|N(v)\cap V_{j}|\leq 1
and because

, it follows that

has at least one neighbor in

, which contradicts the fact that
\deg_{G}(v)=\Delta(G)
.

Case 2.
There are at least two vertices in

that belong to the same set in
\Omega

In such a situation, we assume without loss of generality that

for some

and that
V_{i}
has at least one vertex in

for each

.
We may assume without loss of generality that
|N(v)\cap V_{1}|\geq 2,\ldots,|N(v)\cap V_{t}|\geq 2
for some

and that
|N(v)\cap V_{i}|\leq 1
for the remaining sets

in
\Omega
. The following claim will turn out to be useful.

Claim 1.

For each index

, the set

forms a total
2
-coalition with a set

4
On two open problems on double coalition

Henning and Mojdeh (2025)
proved that
\DC(G)\leq 1+\Delta(G)
for all graphs
G
with
\delta(G)\in\{1,2\}
. They posed the following:

Question 1. If
G
is a graph with
\delta(G)=3
, then is it true that
\DC(G)\leq 1+\Delta(G)
?

This is indeed the case when the graph is cubic, as they proved that
\DC(G)=4
for each cubic graph
G
. Despite the above-mentioned pieces of evidence in support of the inequality, in what follows, we answer this question in the negative.

Moreover, by utilizing the approach developed for total 2-domination (Theorem
3.8
), we present a general upper bound on the double coalition number of a graph
G
in terms of minimum and maximum degrees, provided that
\Delta(G)\geq 4\lceil\delta(G)/2\rceil-3
. Since the bound is sharp for all odd
\delta(G)
, where
\Delta(G)
can be arbitrarily large, we see that the value of
\DC(G)
can be relatively close to
\lceil\delta(G)/2\rceil\Delta(G)
. Given a graph
G
and a double coalition partition
\Omega
, the graph
\DCG(G,\Omega)
is defined with vertex set
\Omega
in which two vertices/sets are adjacent if they form a double coalition.

Theorem 4.1
.

If
G
is a graph with
\delta=\delta(G)\geq 1
and
\Delta=\Delta(G)\geq 4\left\lceil\frac{\delta}{2}\right\rceil-3
, then

\DC(G)\leq\left\lceil\frac{\delta}{2}\right\rceil(\Delta-2\left\lceil\frac{\delta}{2}\right\rceil+2)+1+\left\lfloor\frac{\delta}{2}\right\rfloor.

Moreover, the bound is sharp, and is attained for graphs with any odd minimum degree
\delta\geq 3
.

Proof.

Let
\Omega=\{V_{1},\ldots,V_{|\Omega|}\}
be a
\DC(G)
-partition, and let
u
be a vertex of minimum degree in
G
. If
|N[u]\cap V_{i}|\leq 1
for all
i\in[|\Omega|]
, then
\DC(G)\leq\delta(G)+1
, which directly implies the statement of the theorem.

Thus, we may assume that there exists an integer
s\geq 1
such that, without loss of generality,
|N[u]\cap V_{i}|\geq 2
for all
V_{i}\in\Omega
with
i\in[s]
, while
|N[u]\cap V_{i}|\leq 1
if
i>s
. Clearly,
s\leq\lceil\delta/2\rceil
. Let
\Psi\subsetneq\Omega
be the set of all
V_{j}
such that
V_{j}\cap N[u]=\emptyset
. If
\Psi=\emptyset
, then
\DC(G)=|\Omega|\leq s+(\delta+1-2s)<\delta+1
, which is impossible. Thus,
\Psi\neq\emptyset
. Note that every
V_{j}\in\Psi
forms a double coalition with some
V_{i}
, where
i\in[s]
. In other words, in the graph
\DCG(G,\Omega)
, the vertices
V_{1},\ldots,V_{s}
dominate all vertices in
\Psi
. Let
r
be the smallest number of vertices in
\{V_{1},\ldots,V_{s}\}
that dominate all vertices of
\Psi
. By renaming the sets if necessary, let
V_{1},\ldots,V_{r}
dominate all vertices in
\Psi
. Clearly,
r\in[s]
.

For each
i\in[r]
, let
\Omega_{i}
be the set of neighbors of
V_{i}
in the graph
\DCG(G,\Omega)
that belong to
\Psi
. By our choice of
r
, we deduce that
\Omega_{i}\nsubseteq\cup_{j\in[r]\setminus\{i\}}\Omega_{j}
. Since
V_{i}
is not a double dominating set of
G
, there exists a vertex
v\in V(G)
such that
|V_{i}\cap N[v]|\leq 1
. Assume that
|V_{i}\cap N[v]|=1
. (The case when
|V_{i}\cap N[v]|=0
uses similar, yet slightly simpler arguments.) Then, all sets in
\Omega_{i}
must have a non-empty intersection with
N[v]
. In addition, for every set
V_{j}\in\{V_{1},\ldots,V_{r}\}\setminus\{V_{i}\}
, there exists a set
V_{j^{\prime}}\in\Omega_{j}\setminus\cup_{t\in[r]\setminus\{j\}}\Omega_{t}
. Thus, there are at least two vertices in
N[v]
that belong to
V_{j}\cup V_{j^{\prime}}
. Altogether, we infer that
|\Omega_{i}|+2r-1\leq\deg_{G}(v)+1\leq\Delta+1
. Thus, for all
i\in[r]
, we have

|\Omega_{i}|\leq\Delta-2r+2.

(7)

Since
\{V_{1},\ldots,V_{r}\}
dominates
\Psi
in
\DCG(G,\Omega)
, we deduce that

\textrm{DC}(G)=|\Omega|\leq s+\sum_{i=1}^{r}{|\Omega_{i}|}+\delta+1-2s=\sum_{i=1}^{r}{|\Omega_{i}|}+\delta+1-s.

(8)

Combining (
7
) and (
8
) we get

\DC(G)\leq r(\Delta-2r+2)+\delta+1-s.

(9)

Note that
r\leq s\leq\lceil\delta/2\rceil
, and so the upper bound in (
9
) is in turn bounded from above as follows:

r(\Delta-2r+2)+\delta+1-s\leq r(\Delta-2r+2)+\delta+1-r=f(r).

(10)

If
r=\lceil\delta/2\rceil
, then we get the desired upper bound. Now let
r<\lceil\delta/2\rceil
. Since
\Delta\geq 4\lceil\delta/2\rceil-3
, it follows that
f
is a nondecreasing function on
[1,\lceil\delta/2\rceil-1]
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\DC(G)

\displaystyle\leq f(r)\leq f(\lceil\frac{\delta}{2}\rceil-1)=f(\lceil\frac{\delta}{2}\rceil)+4\lceil\frac{\delta}{2}\rceil-\Delta-3\leq f(\lceil\frac{\delta}{2}\rceil)

\displaystyle=\left\lceil\frac{\delta}{2}\right\rceil(\Delta-2\left\lceil\frac{\delta}{2}\right\rceil+2)+1+\left\lfloor\frac{\delta}{2}\right\rfloor\,,

as desired.

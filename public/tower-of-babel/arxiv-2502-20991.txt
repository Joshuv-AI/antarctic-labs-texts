Domains, Information Frames, Rough Sets: An Equivalence of Categories Thanks: This research has partially been supported by DFG grant no. 549144494.
Dieter Spreen

Abstract
A generalization of Scott’s information systems [ 16 ] is presented that captures exactly all continuous domains. The global consistency predicate in Scott’s definition is relativized. Now, for every atomic statement, there is a consistency predicate that states which finite sets of statements express information that is consistent with the given statement. The category of information frames is shown to be equivalent to the category of domains. Moreover, the relationship with CF-approximation spaces introduced by Wu and Xu [ 19 ] is studied. The corresponding category is also shown to be equivalent with the category of information frames. This research achieves a refinement of the equivalence result of Wu and Xu of the category of CF-approximation spaces with the category of domains.

Introduction

Research in domain theory started with the work of Dana Scott
[
11
,
12
,
13
,
14
,
15
]
and, independently, Yuri L. Ershov
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
]
. D. Scott
was interested in constructing a natural mathematical model for the type-free
\lambda
-calculus, whereas Y. L. Ershov wanted to develop a theory of partial computable functionals of finite type. Today, the field is a thriving branch of mathematics with applications in theoretical computer science, logic, and topology. In theoretical computer science it is particularly used for providing functional programming languages with a semantics that is independent of implementations. For this reason, classes of domains have been studied that are closed under the function space construction, that is, the space of domain operation respecting maps. As was shown by Jung
[
8
,
9
, Corollary 10]
there are two maximal such classes.

Ershov’s approach to domain theory is based on topology, whereas Scott introduced domains by means of partial orders. Both approaches are equivalent. Here, we follow the latter one: A domain is a partial order
(D,\sqsubseteq)
so that least upper bounds of directed subsets exist. In addition, a domain is required to contain a basis, that is, a subset coming with an interpolative transitive binary relation
\ll
compatible with the partial order such that every domain element
x
can be obtained as least upper bound of the base elements
b\ll x
. The interpolation condition is a strengthening of density. Sets
B
coming with an interpolative transitive binary relation are called abstract bases.

A basis turns out to be a skeleton of a domain: every domain is isomorphic to the round ideal completion of an abstract basis
[
1
, Proposition 2.2.25]
. In other words: Domains can be represented by abstract bases. Here, by representation of domains any class of structures is meant so that every domain is isomorphic to a family of sets generated by a structure of the given class and ordered by set inclusion.

In
[
19
]
, G. Wu and L. Xu provide a representation of domains which is based on concepts from rough set theory. It generalizes the round ideal construction. They introduce generalized approximation spaces with consistent family of finite subsets, as well as the associated morphisms: CF-approximable relations. The central result says that every such space generates a domain and conversely, up to isomorphism, each domain can be obtained in this way.

As mentioned already, there are two maximal classes of domains closed under the function space construction. One of them is the class of L-domains. In order to present an easy to understand representation of these domains, the present author
[
17
]
introduced L-information frames (called information frames in
[
17
]
). An L-information frame consists of a set
P
of atomic propositions, a family
(\mathop{\mathstrut\rm Con}\nolimits_{p})_{p\in P}
of finite subsets of atomic propositions, a family
(\vdash_{p})_{p\in P}
of relations with
\vdash_{p}\subseteq\mathop{\mathstrut\rm Con}\nolimits_{p}\times P
, and a dense transitive binary relation
R
on
P
. Each set
\mathop{\mathstrut\rm Con}\nolimits_{p}
can be thought of as being the collection of those finite sets of atomic propositions that are consistent with proposition
p
, and the relation
\vdash_{p}
is an entailment relation for the (rudimentary) logic determined by proposition
p
. These relations are required to be sound, and closed under the rules Weakening, Interpolation and Cut. Moreover, if
pRq
, then the logic determined by
q
is a conservative extension of the logic determined by
p
.

Information frames introduced in the present paper are defined like L-information frames, but the conservativity requirement is omitted. As we shall see, they represent exactly all domains: states of the logic, that is, finitely consistent and entailment-closed sets of atomic propositions, form a domain with respect to set inclusion, and, conversely, up to isomorphism every such domain can be obtained in this way. Approximable mappings will be introduced as appropriate morphisms, and it will be shown that the category of information frames with approximable mappings is equivalent to the category of domains with Scott continuous functions, that is, maps respecting directed least upper bounds.

Furthermore, this paper examines the connection between information frames and the spaces introduced by Wu and Xu. We show that from any given information frame a generalized approximation space with a consistent family of finite subsets can be constructed and that conversely, any such space generates an information frame. The constructions will allow us to derive the equivalence of the category of information frames with approximable mappings and the category of generalized approximation spaces with a consistent family of finite subsets and CF-approximable relations.

The constructions of Wu and Xu, of domains from generalized approximate spaces with consistent families of finite subsets and vice versa, are thus each split into two constructions with information frames as an intermediate structure. Each of the new constructions is very natural and interesting in its own right.

Natural requirements for information frames are presented so that the domains constructed from them are algebraic or pointed. The conditions are reproduced by the constructions in the opposite direction. In the case of generalized approximation spaces with a consistent set of finite subsets, an analogous procedure is followed.

Wu and Xu’s theorem is then a consequence of the results in this paper. Due to the way we proceed with the special cases just mentioned, analogous equivalence results arise for these cases.

The paper is organized as follows: In Section
2
some notations are established and definitions and basic results of domain theory are reviewed. Section
3
deals with information frames: the definition is given and the constructions of a domain from a given information frame and vice versa are presented.

In Section
4
, approximable mappings are introduced. They are the morphisms between information frames. As usual, the morphisms between domains are Scott-continuous functions. The constructions given in the previous section are extended to these morphisms and it is shown that the functors thus obtained between the category of information frames and approximable mappings and the category of domains and Scott-continuous functions establish an equivalence between these categories.

Section
5
contains basic definitions from rough set theory and the definition of generalized approximation spaces with consistent families of finite subsets, in short CF-approximation spaces. It is shown that each of these spaces leads to an information frame and vice versa.

Finally, in Section
6
CF-approximable relations are introduced, the morphisms between CF-approximation spaces. The constructions of the previous section are extended to the morphisms and it is shown that the functors thus obtained establish an equivalence between the category of CF-approximation spaces and CF-approximable relations and the category of information frames with approximable mappings.

Domains: basic definitions and results

For any set
A
, we write

to mean that

is finite subset of

. The collection of all subsets of

.

Let
\mathbb{D}=(D,\sqsubseteq)
be a poset.
\mathbb{D}
is

if it contains a least element

. For an element

,
\mathopen{\downarrow}\,x
denotes the principal ideal generated by

\mathopen{\downarrow}\,x=\mbox{$\{\,y\in D\mid y\sqsubseteq x\,\}$}
. A subset

of

has an upper bound in
S
.
\mathbb{D}
is a

(

), if every directed subset
S
of

has a least upper bound
\bigsqcup S
in
D
.

Assume that

are elements of a poset

. Then

is said to

, written

, if for any directed subset
S
of
D
the least upper bound of which exists in
D
, the relation

always implies the existence of some

with

. Moreover,

is
compact
if

. A subset

of

is a

of
\mathbb{D}
, if for each

the set
\mathord{\mbox{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\raisebox{-1.72218pt}{$\downarrow$}}$\downarrow$\,}}\!_{B}x=\mbox{$\{\,u\in B\mid u\ll x\,\}$}
contains a directed subset with least upper bound

. Note that the set of compact elements of
D
is included in every basis of
\mathbb{D}
. A directed-complete partial order
\mathbb{D}
is said to be
continuous
(or a

) if it has a basis and it is called

(or an

) if its compact elements form a basis. Standard references for domain theory and its applications are
[
1
,
18
,
2
,
7
]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

In a poset
\mathbb{D}
the following statements hold for all
u,x,y,z\in D
:

1.

The approximation relation
\ll
is transitive.

2.

x\ll y\Rightarrow x\sqsubseteq y
.

3.

u\sqsubseteq x\ll y\sqsubseteq z\Rightarrow u\ll z
.

4.

If
x\sqsubseteq y
, and one of
x,y
is compact, then
x\ll y
.

5.

If
\mathbb{D}
has a least element
\bot
, then
\bot
is compact.

6.

If
\mathbb{D}
is a domain with basis
B
, and
M\subseteq_{\mathrm{fin}}D
, then

M\ll x\Rightarrow(\exists v\in B)M\ll v\ll x,

where
M\ll x
means that
m\ll x
, for any
m\in M
.

Property

is known as the
interpolation law
. For a proof see
[
1
, Lemma 2.2.5]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathbb{D}
and
\mathbb{D}^{\prime}
be posets. A function
f\colon D\rightarrow D^{\prime}
is
Scott continuous
if for any directed subset
S
of
D
with existing least upper bound,

\bigsqcup f[S]=f(\bigsqcup S).

With respect to the pointwise order the set
[D\to D^{\prime}]
of all Scott continuous functions between two dcpo’s
\mathbb{D}
and
\mathbb{D}^{\prime}
is a dcpo again. Observe that it need not be continuous even if
\mathbb{D}
and
\mathbb{D}^{\prime}
are. This is the case, however, if
\mathbb{D}^{\prime}
is an L-domain
[
1
]
.

Definition 2.2
.

A pointed domain
\mathbb{D}
is an
L-domain
, if each pair
x,y\in D
bounded above by
z\in D
has a least upper bound
x\sqcup^{z}y
in
\mathopen{\downarrow}\,z
.

Note that in
[
7
]
pointedness is not required.

As has been shown by Jung
[
8
]
, the category
\mathbf{L}
of L-domains is one of the two maximal Cartesian closed full subcategories of the category
\mathbf{DOM}_{\perp}
of pointed domains and Scott continuous functions. The one-point domain is the terminal object in these categories and the categorical product
\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{E}
of two domains
\mathbb{D}
and
\mathbb{E}
is the Cartesian product of the underlying sets ordered coordinatewise.

3
Information frames

In this section we introduce information frames and study their relationship with domains.

An information frame consists of a Kripke frame
(A,R)
, the nodes of which are also called tokens. Associated with each node
i\in A
is a consistency predicate
\mathop{\mathstrut\rm Con}\nolimits_{i}
classifying the finite sets of tokens which are consistent with respect to node
i
, and an entailment relation
\vdash_{i}
between
i
-consistent sets and tokens.

The conditions that have to be satisfied are grouped. There are requirements which consistency predicate and entailment relation of each single node have to meet, and conditions that specify their interplay for nodes related to each other by the accessibility relation.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
A
be a set,
(\mathop{\mathstrut\rm Con}\nolimits_{i})_{i\in A}
be a family of subsets of
\mathscr{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(A)
, and
(\vdash_{i})_{i\in A}
be a family of relations
\vdash_{i}\subseteq\mathop{\mathstrut\rm Con}\nolimits_{i}\times A
. For
i,j\in A

set

iRj\Leftrightarrow\{i\}\in\mathop{\mathstrut\rm Con}\nolimits_{j}.

Then
\mathbb{A}=(A,(\mathop{\mathstrut\rm Con}\nolimits_{i})_{i\in A},(\vdash_{i})_{i\in A})
is an
information frame
if the following conditions hold, for all
a\in A
and all finite subsets
X,Y
of
A
:

1.

Local conditions, for every
i\in A
:

(a)

\{i\}\in\mathop{\mathstrut\rm Con}\nolimits_{i}
(self consistency),

(b)

Y\subseteq X\wedge X\in\mathop{\mathstrut\rm Con}\nolimits_{i}\Rightarrow Y\in\mathop{\mathstrut\rm Con}\nolimits_{i}
(consistency preservation),

and, defining
X\vdash_{i}Y
to mean that
X\vdash_{i}b
, for all
b\in Y
,

(c)

X\in\mathop{\mathstrut\rm Con}\nolimits_{i}\mbox{}\wedge X\vdash_{i}Y\Rightarrow Y\in\mathop{\mathstrut\rm Con}\nolimits_{i}
(soundness),

(d)

X,Y\in\mathop{\mathstrut\rm Con}\nolimits_{i}\mbox{}\wedge Y\supseteq X\wedge X\vdash_{i}a\Rightarrow Y\vdash_{i}a
(weakening),

(e)

X\in\mathop{\mathstrut\rm Con}\nolimits_{i}\mbox{}\wedge X\vdash_{i}Y\wedge Y\vdash_{i}a\Rightarrow X\vdash_{i}a
(cut),

2.

Global conditions, for all
i,j\in A

(a)

iRj\Rightarrow\mathop{\mathstrut\rm Con}\nolimits_{i}\subseteq\mathop{\mathstrut\rm Con}\nolimits_{j}
(consistency transfer),

(b)

iRj\wedge X\in\mathop{\mathstrut\rm Con}\nolimits_{i}\mbox{}\wedge X\vdash_{i}a\Rightarrow X\vdash_{j}a
(entailment transfer),

(c)

X\vdash_{i}Y\Rightarrow(\exists e\in A)(\exists Z\in\mathop{\mathstrut\rm Con}\nolimits_{e})X\vdash_{i}(\{e\}\cup Z)\wedge Z\vdash_{e}Y
(interpolation).

All requirements are very natural. Note that from Condition
3.1
(
1c
), that is, soundness, it particularly follows that for
i,j\in A
and
X\in\mathop{\mathstrut\rm Con}\nolimits_{j}
,

X\vdash_{j}i\Rightarrow iRj.

(3.1)

By Condition (
1b
),
\emptyset\in\mathop{\mathstrut\rm Con}\nolimits_{i}
, for all
i\in A
. Hence, Requirement (
1c
) holds if
Y=\emptyset
. Moreover, in this case, also (
1d
) is true and (
1e
) is a consequence of (
1d
).

Sometimes a stronger version of Cut is needed which reverses the Interpolation Axiom.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\mathbb{A}
be an information frame. Then the following rule holds, for all

,
X\in\mathop{\mathstrut\rm Con}\nolimits_{i}
and

,

X\vdash_{i}(\{j\}\cup Y)\wedge Y\vdash_{j}a\Rightarrow X\vdash_{i}a.

Proof.

Since

(

, whence

) we therefore have that

. Now, we can apply Axiom

.
∎

Note next that from the global interpolation property

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\mathbb{A}
be an information frame. Then the following two statements hold for all

Suppose that
i\in A
and

. Then it follows with the global interpolation condition that there are

, with which we obtain by Conditions

Definition 3.3
.

Let

be an information frame. A subset

4
Approximable mappings

In the next step we want to turn the collection of information frames into a category. The appropriate morphisms are families of relations similar to entailment relations.

Definition 4.1

5
Rough sets

In this section we study the relationship between information frames and rough sets. We start with introducing the necessary concepts of rough set theory. Rough set theory has been proposed by Z. I. Pawlak
[
10
]
as a tool for dealing with the vagueness and granularity in information systems. The core concepts of classical rough sets are lower and upper approximations based on equivalence relations. In Zhu
[
20
]
this approach has been generalized by using arbitrary binary relations.

A set
U
with a binary relation
\Theta
is called a
generalized approximation space (GA-space, in short)
.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
(U,\Theta)
be a GA-space.

1.

For
x\in U
set
\Theta_{s}(x)=\mbox{$\{\,y\in U\mid x\Theta y\,\}$}
.

2.

For
X\subseteq U
define

\underline{\Theta}(X)=\mbox{$\{\,x\in U\mid\Theta_{s}(x)\subseteq X\,\}$}\quad\text{and}\quad\overline{\Theta}(X)=\mbox{$\{\,x\in U\mid\Theta_{s}(x)\cap X\neq\emptyset\,\}$}.

The operators
\underline{\Theta},\overline{\Theta}\colon\mathscr{P}(U)\rightarrow\mathscr{P}(U)
, respectively, are called the
lower and upper approximation operators
in
\mathbb{U}
. Such operators are key notions in rough set theory.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
(U,\Theta)
be a GA-space. Then the following statements hold:

1.

\overline{\Theta}
is monotone and
\overline{\Theta}(\emptyset)=\emptyset
.

2.

\Theta
is reflexive if, and only if,
X\subseteq\overline{\Theta}(X)
, for all
X\subseteq U
.

3.

\Theta
is transitive if, and only if,
\overline{\Theta}(\overline{\Theta}(X))\subseteq\overline{\Theta}(X)
, for all
X\subseteq U
.

4.

Let
\Theta
be transitive and
X,Y\subseteq U
. Then
\overline{\Theta}(Y)\subseteq\overline{\Theta}(X)
, if
Y\subseteq\overline{\Theta}(X)
.

Proof.

(
1
) is obvious, as is the left-to-right implication of (
2
). For the converse implication set
X=\{x\}
with
x\in U
. (
3
) follows similarly and (
4
) is a consequence of (
1
) and (
3
).
∎

In
[
19
]
Wu and Xu introduced a class of GA-spaces that come equipped with a kind of interpolation property.

Definition 5.2
.

Let
(U,\Theta)
be a GA-space so that
\Theta
is transitive. Moreover, let
\mathfrak{F}\subseteq\mathscr{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(U)
.

1.

\mathbb{U}=(U,\Theta,\mathfrak{F})
is a
generalized approximation space with consistent family of finite subsets
, or in short a
CF-approximation space
, if

(\forall F\in\mathfrak{F})(\forall K\subseteq_{\mathrm{fin}}\overline{\Theta}(F))(\exists G\in\mathfrak{F})K\subseteq\overline{\Theta}(G)\wedge G\subseteq\overline{\Theta}(F).

(CF)

2.

\mathbb{U}
is a
topological
CF-approximation space if, in addition,
\Theta
is also reflexive.

As we will see next, every CF-approximation space generates an information frame. For a CF-approximation space
\mathbb{U}
set

\mathscr{C}(\mathbb{U})=(\mathfrak{F},(\mathop{\mathstrut\textsc{Con}}\nolimits_{F})_{F\in\mathfrak{F}},(\mathrel{||}\joinrel\Relbar_{F})_{F\in\mathfrak{F}})

with

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathstrut\textsc{Con}}\nolimits_{F}=\{F\}\cup\mathscr{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mbox{$\{\,G\in\mathfrak{F}\mid G\subseteq\overline{\Theta}(F)\,\}$}),

\displaystyle\mathfrak{X}\mathrel{||}\joinrel\Relbar_{F}G\Leftrightarrow(\exists E\in\mathfrak{X}\cup\{F\})G\subseteq\overline{\Theta}(E).

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\mathbb{U}
be a CF-approximation space. Then
\mathscr{C}(\mathbb{U})
is a strong information frame such that

1.

\mathscr{C}(\mathbb{U})
is algebraic, if
\mathbb{U}
is topological.

2.

\mathscr{C}(\mathbb{U})
has a truth element, if the following Condition (
M
) is satified:

(\exists\mathbf{T}\in\mathfrak{F})(\forall F\in\mathfrak{F})\mathbf{T}\subseteq\overline{\Theta}(F).

(M)

Proof.

We first show that the conditions in Definition
3.1
are satisfied.
Conditions
3.1
(
1a
) and
3.1
(
1b
) obviously hold. In what follows let
F,G\in\mathfrak{F}
.

3.1
(
1c
)
Let
\mathfrak{H}\in\mathop{\mathstrut\textsc{Con}}\nolimits_{F}
and
\mathfrak{Y}\subseteq_{\mathrm{fin}}\mathfrak{F}
with
\mathfrak{H}\mathrel{||}\joinrel\Relbar_{F}\mathfrak{Y}
. Then, for all
K\in\mathfrak{Y}
, there is some
Z_{K}\in\mathfrak{H}\cup\{F\}
with
K\subseteq\overline{\Theta}(Z_{K})
. Then
Z_{K}\subseteq\overline{\Theta}(F)
or
Z_{K}=F
. Due to the transitivity of
\Theta
it follows that
K\subseteq\overline{\Theta}(Z_{K})\subseteq\overline{\Theta}(\overline{\Theta}(F))\subseteq\overline{\Theta}(F)
or
K\subseteq\overline{\Theta}(F)
. Thus,
K\in\mbox{$\{\,G\in\mathfrak{F}\mid G\subseteq\overline{\Theta}(F)\,\}$}
, that is,
\mathfrak{Y}\in\mathop{\mathstrut\textsc{Con}}\nolimits_{F}
.

3.1
(
1d
)
Let
K\in\mathfrak{F}
and
\mathfrak{X},\mathfrak{Y}\in\mathop{\mathstrut\textsc{Con}}\nolimits_{F}
with
\mathfrak{X}\subseteq\mathfrak{Y}
and
\mathfrak{X}\mathrel{||}\joinrel\Relbar_{F}K
. Then there is some
Z\in\mathfrak{X}\cup\{F\}
with
K\subseteq\overline{\Theta}(Z)
. Since
\mathfrak{X}\subseteq\mathfrak{Y}
,
Z\in\mathfrak{Y}\cup\{F\}
as well. Hence,
\mathfrak{Y}\mathrel{||}\joinrel\Relbar_{F}K
.

3.1
(
1e
)
Let
K\in\mathfrak{F}
and
\mathfrak{X},\mathfrak{Y}\in\mathop{\mathstrut\textsc{Con}}\nolimits_{F}
such that
\mathfrak{X}\mathrel{||}\joinrel\Relbar_{F}\mathfrak{Y}
and
\mathfrak{Y}\mathrel{||}\joinrel\Relbar_{F}K
. Then there is some
Z\in\mathfrak{Y}\cup\{F\}
with
K\subseteq\overline{\Theta}(Z)
. If
Z=F
, then
Z\in\mathfrak{X}\cup\{F\}
as well and hence we have that
\mathfrak{X}\mathrel{||}\joinrel\Relbar_{F}K
.
In the other case that
Z\in\mathfrak{Y}
, then, since
\mathfrak{X}\mathrel{||}\joinrel\Relbar_{F}\mathfrak{Y}
, there is some
T\in\mathfrak{X}\cup\{F\}
with
Z\subseteq\overline{\Theta}(T)
. Thus,
K\subseteq\overline{\Theta}(Z)\subseteq\overline{\Theta}(\overline{\Theta}(T))\subseteq\overline{\Theta}(T)
, which means that
\mathfrak{X}\mathrel{||}\joinrel\Relbar_{F}K
.

3.1
(
2a
)
Let
\{F\}\in\mathop{\mathstrut\textsc{Con}}\nolimits_{G}
and
\mathfrak{Y}\in\mathop{\mathstrut\textsc{Con}}\nolimits_{F}
. Then either
\mathfrak{Y}=\{F\}
or for all

,
K\subseteq\overline{\Theta}(F)
. In the first case,
\mathfrak{Y}\in\mathop{\mathstrut\textsc{Con}}\nolimits_{G}
, by assumption, and in the second we have that
F\subseteq\overline{\Theta}(G)
. Therefore,
K\subseteq\overline{\Theta}(\overline{\Theta}(G))\subseteq\overline{\Theta}(G)
for all
K\in\mathfrak{Y}
, which shows that
\mathfrak{Y}\in\mathop{\mathstrut\textsc{Con}}\nolimits_{G}
in this case as well.

3.1
(
2b
)
Let
K\in\mathfrak{F}
,
\mathfrak{X}\in\mathop{\mathstrut\textsc{Con}}\nolimits_{F}
, and assume that
\{F\}\in\mathop{\mathstrut\textsc{Con}}\nolimits_{G}
and
\mathfrak{X}\mathrel{||}\joinrel\Relbar_{F}K
. Then there is some
Z\in\mathfrak{X}\cup\{F\}
with
K\subseteq\overline{\Theta}(Z)
. If
Z=F
, then
\{Z\}\in\mathop{\mathstrut\textsc{Con}}\nolimits_{G}
, by assumption, and hence
Z\subseteq\overline{\Theta}(G)
. Thus, we have that
K\subseteq\overline{\Theta}(\overline{\Theta}(G))\subseteq\overline{\Theta}(G)
, which shows that
\mathfrak{X}\mathrel{||}\joinrel\Relbar_{G}K
.
However, if
Z\in\mathfrak{X}
then
Z\in\mathfrak{X}\cup\{G\}
, which implies that in this case too
\mathfrak{X}\mathrel{||}\joinrel\Relbar_{G}K
.

3.1
(
2c
)
Let
\mathfrak{X}\in\mathop{\mathstrut\textsc{Con}}\nolimits_{F}
and
\mathfrak{Y}\subseteq_{\mathrm{fin}}\mathfrak{F}
with
\mathfrak{X}\mathrel{||}\joinrel\Relbar_{F}\mathfrak{Y}
. Moreover, let
K\in\mathfrak{Y}
. Then there is some
Z\in\mathfrak{X}\cup\{F\}
with
K\subseteq\overline{\Theta}(Z)
. If
Z=F
, we have that
K\subseteq\overline{\Theta}(F)
. In the other case we have that
Z\subseteq\overline{\Theta}(F)
and therefore that
K\subseteq\overline{\Theta}(\overline{\Theta}(F))\subseteq\overline{\Theta}(F)
. It follows that
\bigcup\mathfrak{Y}\subseteq_{\mathrm{fin}}\overline{\Theta}(F)
. Since
F\in\mathfrak{F}
, there is thus some
E\in\mathfrak{F}
with
\bigcup\mathfrak{Y}\subseteq\overline{\Theta}(E)
and
E\subseteq\overline{\Theta}(F)
. Hence, we have that
\mathfrak{X}\mathrel{||}\joinrel\Relbar_{F}\{E\}
and
\{E\}\mathrel{||}\joinrel\Relbar_{E}\mathfrak{Y}
.

Condition (
S
) is also obviously fulfilled.

For Statement (
1
) we have to verify Condition (
AL
). Let to this end
\mathfrak{X}\in\mathop{\mathstrut\textsc{Con}}\nolimits_{F}
. Note that by Lemma
5.1
(
2
) for all
Z\in\mathfrak{X}\cup\{F\}

Z\subseteq\overline{\Theta}(Z)
. Hence,
\mathfrak{X}\mathrel{||}\joinrel\Relbar_{F}\{F\}\cup\mathfrak{X}
.

(
2
)
Let
\mathbf{T}\in\mathfrak{F}
with
\mathbf{T}\subseteq\overline{\Theta}(F)
, for all
F\in\mathfrak{F}
. Then we have that
\emptyset\mathrel{||}\joinrel\Relbar_{F}\mathbf{T}
, for all
F\in\mathfrak{F}
.
∎

Note that Condition (
M
) is satisfied in particular, if
\mathfrak{F}
contains the empty set.

Conversely, also every information frame generates a CF-approximation space. For an information frame
\mathbb{A}
define

\mathscr{E}(\mathbb{A})=(U,\Theta,\mathfrak{F})

with

\displaystyle U=\bigcup\mbox{$\{\,\mathop{\mathstrut\rm Con}\nolimits_{i}\times\{i\}\mid i\in A\,\}$},

\displaystyle(X,i)\Theta(Y,j)\Leftrightarrow Y\vdash_{j}\{i\}\cup X,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{F}=\mbox{$\{\,\{(X,i)\}\mid(X,i)\in U\,\}$}.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
\mathbb{A}
be an information frame. Then
\mathscr{E}(\mathbb{A})
is a CF-approximation space so that

1.

\mathscr{E}(\mathbb{A})
is topological if, and only if,
\mathbb{A}
is algebraic.

2.

If
\mathbb{A}
has a truth element, then
\mathscr{E}(\mathbb{A})
satisfies Condition (

).

Proof.

The transitivity of
\Theta
follows from Lemma
3.1
.
For Condition (

) let
F\in\mathfrak{F}
, say
F=\{(X,i)\}
, and
K\subseteq_{\mathrm{fin}}\overline{\Theta}(\{(X,i)\})
. Note that
\overline{\Theta}(\{(X,i)\})=\mbox{$\{\,(Y,j)\in U\mid X\vdash_{i}\{j\}\cup Y\,\}$}
. It follows that
X\vdash_{i}\bigcup\mbox{$\{\,\{j\}\cup Y\mid(Y,j)\in K\,\}$}
. By Condition
3.1
(
2c
) there is an

and
Z\in\mathop{\mathstrut\rm Con}\nolimits_{e}
with
X\vdash_{i}\{e\}\cup Z
and

. Hence,
(Z,e)\Theta(X,i)
, that is,
\{(Z,e)\}\subseteq\overline{\Theta}(\{(X,i)\})
, and

.

For the remaining statements note first that

\text{$\mathscr{E}(\mathbb{A})$ is topological}\Leftrightarrow\text{$\Theta$ is reflexive}\Leftrightarrow(\forall(X,i)\in U)X\vdash_{i}\{i\}\cup X\Leftrightarrow\text{$\mathbb{A}$ is algebraic},

which proves (

). For (

) let
\mathbf{t}\in A
be a truth element of
\mathbb{A}
. Then
\emptyset\vdash_{i}\mathbf{t}
, for all

, by Condition (

). With Condition
3.1
(
1d
) it follows that for all
X\in\mathop{\mathstrut\rm Con}\nolimits_{i}
,
X\vdash_{i}\mathbf{t}
. Thus
\{(\emptyset,\mathbf{t})\}\subseteq\overline{\Theta}(\{(X,i)\})
, for all
\{(X,i)\}\in\mathfrak{F}
.
∎

6
CF-approximable relations

Wu and Xu
[
19
]
also introduced morphisms between CF-approximation spaces.

Definition 6.1
.

Let
\mathbb{U}
and
\mathbb{U}^{\prime}
be CF-approximation spaces. A relation
\Delta\subseteq\mathscr{F}\times\mathscr{F}^{\prime}
is a
CF-approximable relation from
\mathbb{U}
to
\mathbb{U}^{\prime}
, written
\Delta\colon\mathbb{U}\bowtie\mathbb{U}^{\prime}
if for all
F,F^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{F}
and
G,G^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}

1.

(\exists\widehat{G}\in\mathfrak{F}^{\prime})F\Delta\widehat{G}
,

2.

F\subseteq\overline{\Theta}(F^{\prime})\wedge F\Delta G\Rightarrow F^{\prime}\Delta G
,

3.

F\Delta G\wedge G^{\prime}\subseteq\overline{\Theta^{\prime}}(G)\Rightarrow F\Delta G^{\prime}
,

4.

F\Delta G\Rightarrow(\exists\widehat{F}\in\mathfrak{F})(\exists\widehat{G}\in\mathfrak{F}^{\prime})\widehat{F}\subseteq\overline{\Theta}(F)\wedge G\subseteq\overline{\Theta^{\prime}}(\widehat{G})\wedge\widehat{F}\Delta\widehat{G}
,

5.

F\Delta G\wedge F\Delta G^{\prime}\Rightarrow(\exists\widehat{G}\in\mathfrak{F}^{\prime})G\cup G^{\prime}\subseteq\overline{\Theta^{\prime}}(\widehat{G})\wedge F\Delta\widehat{G}
.

For a CF-approximation space
\mathbb{U}
let
\mathop{\mathstrut\rm Id}\nolimits_{\mathbb{U}}\subseteq\mathfrak{F}\times\mathfrak{F}
be given by

F\mathop{\mathstrut\rm Id}\nolimits_{\mathbb{U}}G\Leftrightarrow G\subseteq\overline{\Theta}(F).

Moreover, for CF-approximation spaces
\mathbb{U}^{(\nu)}
with
\nu=1,2,3
,
\Delta\colon\mathbb{U}^{(1)}\bowtie\mathbb{U}^{(2)}
and

Spatial deformation prediction method of fractured rock tunnel based on quantified GSI and its application
Authors: Caihua Shen, Zhikang Zeng, Huibo Lou, Zhifan Yang, Wenbo Gu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Determining rock mass mechanical parameters and accurately predicting tunnel deformation during tunnel construction remain challenging tasks. This study introduces a novel approach to calculate the Geological Strength Index by integrating indoor rock mechanics tests with geometric data from three-dimensional dense reconstruction. We utilized the Hoek-Brown strength criterion to develop a theoretical model for predicting tunnel deformation in fractured rock masses. Case studies reveal that the average value of Geological Strength Index for the Xiamen highway tunnel’s surrounding rock is 44. With support from the lining structure equivalent to 0.002–0.02 times the initial in-situ stress, the plastic zone thickness decreased by approximately 50%, and radial displacement was reduced by about 40%. Enhancing the lining structure’s support pressure significantly reduces the plastic zone radius and radial displacement. As the Geological Strength Index decreases, the nonlinearity between support pressure and plastic zone radius becomes more pronounced, with a similar trend observed for the relationship between support pressure and tunnel radial displacement. The relative deviation between predicted and measured values did not exceed 1.69%. The method accurately captures the effects of rock fragmentation and tunnel construction on plastic zone formation and displacement, offering an effective approach for the rapid and secure assessment of rock tunnel excavation stability using digital technology.

Introduction

The deformation safety prediction of fractured rock tunnel is an important basis for tunnel stability evaluation and optimization design of supporting structure1. Rapid and quantitative acquisition of mechanical parameters of fractured rock mass is the premise of tunnel deformation prediction and safety assessment. Assessing the impact of fractured characteristics on rock mass mechanical parameters and quantifying them poses a perennial challenge for practitioners in the field of rock mass mechanics.

Many scholars had undertaken extensive research endeavors aimed at the rapid and accurate estimation of rock mass mechanical parameters. Hoek and Brown proposed the rock mass rating index (RMR) and applied the empirical formula to determine the comprehensive shear strength of rock mass2. Due to the poor operability of the RMR index in the field, they further proposed a generalized Hoek-Brown (H-B) strength criterion on the basis of the Geological Strength Index (GSI) scoring index system3,4. The H-B strength criterion aligns closely with engineering practice due to its comprehensive consideration of structural characteristics affecting rock mass strength. However, the determination of the GSI, a key aspect of the criterion, has been hindered by the generalized values provided by Hoek, leading to subjective assessments and significant errors, which fail to meet the precise requirements of engineering applications5–9.

In recent years, many researchers had studied the method to quantify value of GSI. Sonmez et al.10 (1999) realized the quantification of GSI system by introducing two quantitative indexes of rock mass structure grade and structural surface grade. Su et al.11 (2009) organically combined the quantitative indexes of rock mass block index and absolute weathering index with the tabular generalized interval description of GSI proposed by Hoek, and formed the quantified method of GSI. Bertuzzi et al.12 (2016) used the joint conditional rating of rock quality designation (RQD) and RMR system to quantify the GSI of four different rock masses. Jiang et al.13 (2018) quantified the GSI value through the structural surface conditions and structural surface characteristic parameters according to the three-dimensional (3D) structural plane network of rock mass, and verified the accuracy and feasibility of this method through practical engineering. Xia et al.14 (2022) estimated the GSI value on the basis of the RQD and surface condition rating, and the in-situ monitoring results verified the rationality of the estimated parameters. Despite pioneering efforts in quantifying rock mass structural characteristics, the empirical nature of GSI value determination often results in inaccurate assessments.

As computer image processing advances, scholars are increasingly exploring 3D dense reconstruction for geological information extraction in rock masses. Liang et al.15 (2021) applied laser 3D scanning technology to rock slope engineering, and obtained the point cloud data of landslide through 3D scanning, which provided data support for geological disaster analysis. Liu et al.16 (2023) calculated the GSI on the basis of the volume joint number Jv of rock mass and the condition factor Jc, wherein, Jc can be obtained by 3D reconstruction and digital identification of joint information in structural plane. Ge et al.17 (2023) proposed a method for rapid quantitative estimation of GSI based on rock structure grade and surface condition point cloud grade using 3D point cloud data. The results reflect that the GSI value is consistent with the results of in-situ evaluation. Wang et al.18 (2020) used the mechanical parameters of rock mass obtained by quantifying GSI to calculate the stress and displacement of surrounding rock of deep-buried circular tunnel, but failed to give an analytical solution of the radius of plastic zone. In addition, the 3D dense reconstruction technology has also made remarkable progress in the study of large deformation control of soft rock tunnels. Cai et al.19,20 (2022) and others digitally identify the rock mass structure of soft rock tunnel, quickly and accurately obtain the in-situ strength parameters of rock mass, such as GSI and D, realize the rapid cataloging of geological information of soft rock tunnel and the identification of surrounding rock grade, and provide reference for construction. Research indicates that 3D dense reconstruction technology efficiently extracts rock mass structural data, offering precise mechanical parameters essential for in-depth analysis of tunnel deformation in fractured rock.

However, current research presents limitations: the determinants of GSI are not fully comprehensive, with a focus on structural attributes and plane characteristics, neglecting holistic GSI assessment. Additionally, there is a scarcity of studies on geospatial information integral extraction using photogrammetry and subsequent analysis of rock mass mechanical behavior post-GSI determination. In view of the above problems, the 3D dense reconstruction technology combined with the indoor rock mechanics test is used to collect the data of rock mass and quantitative determination method of GSI calculated by Basic Quality (BQ) value is constructed. Based on the H-B strength criterion, we propose a tunnel deformation prediction method for fractured rock masses that accounts for the expansion effect of the surrounding rock. This approach introduces a novel quantification method for GSI, facilitating the rapid and accurate determination of rock mass mechanical parameters and enabling swift prediction of tunnel deformation through 3D dense reconstruction technology.

GSI quantification based on surrounding rock quality system

Determination of rock mass integrity coefficient

3D dense reconstruction is a process of recovering the complete shape and texture features of 3D objects or scenes from multiple two-dimensional images. This technology creates a 3D point cloud model of the rock mass surface by collecting the sequence images of the tunnel excavation surface, and then analyzes the high-definition images of the plane model of the tunnel rock mass structure, so that the joint characteristics of the rock mass (such as joint strike, dip angle, spacing and other parameters) can be quantitatively described. Using this reconstruction technology, the joint information of tunnel face can be counted and analyzed in detail. Determination of equivalent deformation modulus

While indoor physical experiments can estimate the rock mass deformation modulus, they may not adequately predict in-situ deformation due to the differing environmental conditions. In-situ experiments are limited by time and high cost29, so it is difficult to widely carry out in construction site. Therefore, the engineering experience method on the basis of surrounding rock classification is used to determine the deformation modulus of rock mass in the field. To assess the prediction method’s performance, evaluation metrics including the determination coefficient (R2), root mean square error (RMSE), and relative percent deviation (RPD) are utilized to evaluate the predicted deformation of tunnel surrounding rock. The determination coefficient (R²) indicates the degree to which the independent variable’s variation explains that of the dependent variable, with R2 values reflecting the quality of the fit; RMSE is mainly used to measure the deviation between the predicted value and the real value. The smaller the predicted value, the lower the deviation. The size of RPD indicates the predictive ability of the prediction method. If RPD < 1.4, the predicted value is unreliable, and if RPD > 1.4, the predicted value is reliable. The prediction method is evaluated by three prediction results, as addressed in Table 6. Conclusions

Taking the Xiangdong tunnel located in Xiamen city as the support, this study constructed a calculation method of GSI based on 3D dense reconstruction technology. Field measurements and theoretical analysis were conducted to assess the impact of rock mass fragmentation on support pressure and tunnel deformation. The predicted displacements could effectively reflect the fragmentation of the surrounding rock. By integrating actual engineering displacement predictions with sensitivity analyses of the prediction method, the key findings are as follows:

3D dense reconstruction technology facilitated the rapid and accurate determination of joint group number Jv, effectively capturing the structural attributes of rock mass. The average GSI value of rock mass of Xiangdong tunnel was 44, and the GSI value range of rock mass was 26 ~ 62.Calculations for rock with varying degrees of fragmentation demonstrated that internal support pressures of 0.002 to 0.02 times the original stress reduced the plastic zone thickness by approximately 50% and radial displacement by about 40%. Timely application of support could effectively restrain the development of the plastic zone and limit the displacement of the tunnel wall.The proposed method’s predicted tunnel deformation in fractured rock mass differed by 0.84 mm from the measured value, with a relative deviation of 1.69%, indicating that the prediction method has reliable prediction ability.

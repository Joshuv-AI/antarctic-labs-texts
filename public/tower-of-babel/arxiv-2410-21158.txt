On a weak form of Ennola’s conjecture about certain cubic number fields
Jinwoo Choi, Dohyeong Kim

Abstract
. We establish a weak form of Ennola’s conjecture. We achieve this by showing that two main assumptions Louboutin made in his previous work hold true. These assumptions are about Laurent polynomials over the rationals, and we prove them by using Newton identities.



1.
Introduction

An algebraic number
\epsilon
is called
exceptional
if both
\epsilon
and
\epsilon-1
are units.
For example, let
l\geq 3
be an integer, and consider a non-Galois totally real cubic number field
\mathbb{Q}(\epsilon_{l})
, where the minimal polynomial of
\epsilon_{l}
is

(1)

X^{3}+(l-1)X^{2}-lX-1\in\mathbb{Z}[X].

One can easily observe that
\epsilon_{l}
and
\epsilon_{l}-1
are both units. i.e.,
\epsilon_{l}
is an exceptional unit.

By Dirichlet’s unit theorem, the unit group
\mathbb{U}_{l}
of the ring of integers of
\mathbb{Q}(\epsilon_{l})
has rank 2.
Hence, one can now naturally ask the following: is
\{\epsilon_{l},\epsilon_{l}-1\}
a pair of fundamental units? That is,
\mathbb{U}_{l}=\langle-1,\epsilon_{l},\epsilon_{l}-1\rangle
?

This is still an open problem. Ennola conjectured in
[
Enn91
]
that
\{\epsilon_{l},\epsilon_{l}-1\}
above is a pair of fundamental units for the maximal order
\mathcal{O}
of
\mathbb{Q}(\epsilon_{l})
.
We call this
Ennola’s conjecture
.
He showed that his conjecture is true for
3\leq l\leq 500
and that the
unit index

j_{l}:=(\mathbb{U}_{l}:\langle-1,\epsilon_{l},\epsilon_{l}-1\rangle)

of the group of units generated by
-1
,
\epsilon_{l}
, and
\epsilon_{l}-1
in the group of units
\mathbb{U}_{l}
is always coprime to 2,3, and 5.
He also showed that
\{\epsilon_{l},\epsilon_{l}-1\}
is a pair of fundamental units for
\mathcal{O}
if
(\mathcal{O}:\mathbb{Z}[\epsilon_{l}])\leq l/3
in
[
Enn06
]
.

To show that Ennola’s conjecture holds true is equivalent to prove
j_{l}=1
for
\forall l\geq 3
.
Louboutin obtained several results on this conjecture.
For example, in
[
Lou17
]
, he showed that for
l\geq 3
, the unit index
j_{l}
is coprime to
19!
, and
j_{l}=1
for
3\leq l\leq 5\cdot 10^{7}
.
He also proved in
[
Lou21
]
that if we assume ABC conjecture is true, then Ennola’s conjecture is true except for finitely many
l
. i.e.,
j_{l}=1
for any sufficiently large
l
.
Another significant result in
[
Lou21
]
is a conditional proof of a weak form of Ennola’s conjecture that for any given integer
N\geq 2
, we have
\gcd(j_{l},N!)=1
for
l\geq l_{N}
effectively large enough.

Louboutin deduced a weak form of Ennola’s conjecture from Conjectures 12 and 20 of
[
Lou21
]
.
Even though Louboutin checked the validity of the conjectures by computation for finitely many case in
[
Lou21
]
, the Conjectures 12 and 20 are still unsolved in
[
Lou21
]
.

In this paper, we provide proofs of both conjectures and consequently establish Theorem
1
below.

Theorem 1

(weak form of Ennola’s conjecture, Theorem 2 of
[
Lou21
]
)
.

For any given prime
p\geq 3
, there are only finitely many
l\geq 3
for which
p
divides the unit index
j_{l}
.
Hence, for any given integer
N\geq 2
we have
\gcd(j_{l},N!)=1
for
l\geq l_{N}
effectively large enough.

Theorem
1
is a statement about divisors of a unit index
j_{l}
.
A similar work was done by Louboutin and Lee for another family of cubic number fields.
In
[
LL17
]
, they showed that the unit index
(\mathbb{U}_{a}:\langle-1,\epsilon_{a},\epsilon_{a}^{\prime}\rangle)
of the group of units generated by
-1
,
\epsilon_{a}
, and
\epsilon_{a}^{\prime}
in the group of units
\mathbb{U}_{a}
of the ring of integers of
\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)
is coprime to 3 for
a\geq 1
and coprime to 6 for
1\leq a\equiv 2,3\ (\text{mod }4)
, where
\alpha
is a root of
X^{3}-4a^{2}X+2\in\mathbb{Z}[X]
, and
\epsilon_{a},\epsilon_{a}^{\prime}
are two distinct units in the ring of integers of
\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)
.
Our result is similar to that of Louboutin and Lee, and we are concerned with a family of cubic polynomials (
1
) with
l\geq 3
.
On the other hand, this family (
1
) attracts interests both of number theorists and of topologists. For topological context, see
[
KY17
]
.

This paper is organized as follows. In
\lx@sectionsign

2
, we introduce conjectures in
[
Lou21
]
, which are used in the proof of Theorem
1
. In
\lx@sectionsign

3
and
\lx@sectionsign

4
, we give proofs of conjectures.

Proof of Conjecture 1

The relationship between
P_{d}(X,Y)
and Newton identities is important to achieve our objectives.
For notations of Newton identities, we follow conventions of
[

]
.

Theorem 2

(Newton identities, Exercise 14 in
\lx@sectionsign 7.1
of
[
CLO07
]
)
.

Let
s_{k}=s_{k}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})=x_{1}^{k}+x_{2}^{k}+x_{3}^{k}
be
k
-th power sum of three variables
x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}
.
Define
\sigma_{1}=x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3},\ \sigma_{2}=x_{1}x_{2}+x_{2}x_{3}+x_{3}x_{1},\ \sigma_{3}=x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}
.
If
d>3
, then

\displaystyle s_{d}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})=\sigma_{1}s_{d-1}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})-\sigma_{2}s_{d-2}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})+\sigma_{3}s_{d-3}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}).

Hence, we may regard
s_{d}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
as a polynomial of
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\sigma_{3}
for each
d\geq 4
. i.e.,

(4)

s_{d}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})=x_{1}^{d}+x_{2}^{d}+x_{3}^{d}=f_{d}(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\sigma_{3})

for some polynomial
f_{d}
.

From Theorem
2
, for
d>3
we have

(5)

f_{d}(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\sigma_{3})=\sigma_{1}f_{d-1}(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\sigma_{3})-\sigma_{2}f_{d-2}(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\sigma_{3})+\sigma_{3}f_{d-3}(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\sigma_{3}).

Here are some examples of
f_{d}(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\sigma_{3})
:

Example 2
.

(1)
d=1
:
f_{1}(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\sigma_{3})=\sigma_{1}
.

(2)
d=2
:
f_{2}(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\sigma_{3})=\sigma_{1}^{2}-2\sigma_{2}
.

(3)
d=3
:
f_{3}(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\sigma_{3})=\sigma_{1}^{3}-3\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}+3\sigma_{3}
.

(4)
d=4
:
f_{4}(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\sigma_{3})=\sigma_{1}^{4}-4\sigma_{1}^{2}\sigma_{2}+2\sigma_{2}^{2}+4\sigma_{1}\sigma_{3}
.

In this setting, suppose
\sigma_{3}=1
.
Then
\sigma_{2}
becomes

x_{1}x_{2}+x_{2}x_{3}+x_{3}x_{1}=\frac{1}{x_{1}}+\frac{1}{x_{2}}+\frac{1}{x_{3}},

and (
5
) becomes

(6)

f_{d}(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},1)=\sigma_{1}f_{d-1}(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},1)-\sigma_{2}f_{d-2}(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},1)+f_{d-3}(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},1).

In terms of
x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}
, (
6
) is

\displaystyle x_{1}^{d}+x_{2}^{d}+x_{3}^{d}=

\displaystyle f_{d}\left(x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3},\frac{1}{x_{1}}+\frac{1}{x_{2}}+\frac{1}{x_{3}},1\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3})f_{d-1}\left(x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3},\frac{1}{x_{1}}+\frac{1}{x_{2}}+\frac{1}{x_{3}},1\right)

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{1}{x_{1}}+\frac{1}{x_{2}}+\frac{1}{x_{3}}\right)f_{d-2}\left(x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3},\frac{1}{x_{1}}+\frac{1}{x_{2}}+\frac{1}{x_{3}},1\right)

\displaystyle+f_{d-2}\left(x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3},\frac{1}{x_{1}}+\frac{1}{x_{2}}+\frac{1}{x_{3}},1\right).

Put
\sigma_{2}=X
and
\sigma_{1}=Y
. Then (
6
) is

(7)

f_{d}(Y,X,1)=Yf_{d-1}(Y,X,1)-Xf_{d-2}(Y,X,1)+f_{d-3}(Y,X,1).

We use (
7
) in the proof of the following proposition, which is important in the proof of Conjecture
1
.

Proposition 2
.

For any
d\geq 1
,
P_{d}(X,Y)=-f_{d}(Y,X,1)
.

Proof.

From Example
1
and
2
,

\displaystyle-f_{1}(Y,X,1)

\displaystyle=-Y=P_{1}(X,Y),

\displaystyle-f_{2}(Y,X,1)

\displaystyle=-Y^{2}+2X=P_{2}(X,Y),

\displaystyle-f_{3}(Y,X,1)

\displaystyle=-Y^{3}+3XY-3=P_{3}(X,Y).

Now we use induction on
d
. By (
7
), for
d>3
we have

(8)

-f_{d}(Y,X,1)=Y(-f_{d-1}(Y,X,1))-X(-f_{d-2}(Y,X,1))+(-f_{d-3}(Y,X,1)),

and by inductive hypothesis,

(9)

-f_{d-i}(Y,X,1)=(d-i)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k,l\geq 0\\
0\leq 2k+3l\leq d-i\end{subarray}}(-1)^{k-1}\binom{k+l}{k}\binom{d-i-k-2l}{k+l}\frac{X^{k}Y^{d-i-2k-3l}}{d-i-k-2l}

for
1\leq i\leq d-1
. Combining (
8
) with (
9
), we get

\displaystyle-f_{d}(Y,X,1)=

\displaystyle Y(d-1)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k,l\geq 0\\
0\leq 2k+3l\leq d-1\end{subarray}}(-1)^{k-1}\binom{k+l}{k}\binom{d-1-k-2l}{k+l}\frac{X^{k}Y^{d-1-2k-3l}}{d-1-k-2l}

\displaystyle-X(d-2)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k,l\geq 0\\
0\leq 2k+3l\leq d-2\end{subarray}}(-1)^{k-1}\binom{k+l}{k}\binom{d-2-k-2l}{k+l}\frac{X^{k}Y^{d-2-2k-3l}}{d-2-k-2l}

\displaystyle+(d-3)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k,l\geq 0\\
0\leq 2k+3l\leq d-3\end{subarray}}(-1)^{k-1}\binom{k+l}{k}\binom{d-3-k-2l}{k+l}\frac{X^{k}Y^{d-3-2k-3l}}{d-3-k-2l}

We explicitly compute the right hand side of the above equality. Note that

\binom{k+l}{k}\binom{d-k-2l}{k+l}\frac{(-1)^{k-1}d}{d-k-2l}=\frac{(-1)^{k-1}d(d-1-k-2l)!}{k!\ l!\ (d-2k-3l)!}.

So (

) becomes

\displaystyle-f_{d}(Y,X,1)=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k,l\geq 0\\
0\leq 2k+3l\leq d-1\end{subarray}}\frac{(-1)^{k-1}(d-1)(d-2-k-2l)!}{k!\ l!\ (d-1-2k-3l)!}X^{k}Y^{d-2k-3l}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k,l\geq 0\\
0\leq 2k+3l\leq d-2\end{subarray}}\frac{(-1)^{k}(d-2)(d-3-k-2l)!}{k!\ l!\ (d-2-2k-3l)!}X^{k+1}Y^{d-2-2k-3l}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k,l\geq 0\\
0\leq 2k+3l\leq d-3\end{subarray}}\frac{(-1)^{k-1}(d-3)(d-4-k-2l)!}{k!\ l!\ (d-3-2k-3l)!}X^{k}Y^{d-3-2k-3l}.

To prove Proposition

, it suffices to show that the summands with monomial

add up to

(10)

\frac{(-1)^{k-1}d(d-1-k-2l)!}{k!\ l!\ (d-2k-3l)!}X^{k}Y^{d-2k-3l}

for all possible pair
(i,j)
.
Before doing so, we prove the claim by dividing it into four cases.

(i)

We obtain (

\displaystyle(-1)^{k-1}\left(\frac{(d-1)(d-2-k-2l)!}{k!\ l!\ (d-1-2k-3l)!}+\frac{(d-2)(d-2-k-2l)!}{(k-1)!\ l!\ (d-2k-3l)!}\right.

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{k-1}(d-2-k-2l)!}{(k-1)!\ (l-1)!\ (d-1-2k-3l)!}\cdot\frac{d(d-2k-3l)-d+dk+dl}{kl(d-2k-3l)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\binom{k+l}{k}\binom{d-k-2l}{k+l}\frac{(-1)^{k-1}d}{d-k-2l}.

,

.

We compute

(-1)^{k-1}\frac{d(d-1-k)!}{k!\ (d-1-2k)!}X^{k}Y^{d-2k}

as in the case
(ii)
by putting

, and excluding the third term in the case

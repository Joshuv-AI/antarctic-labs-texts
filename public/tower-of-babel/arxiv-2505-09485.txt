Single-loop 𝒪 ⁡ ( ϵ − 3 ) \mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-3}) stochastic smoothing algorithms for nonsmooth optimization problem on Riemannian manifold
Kangkang Deng, Zheng Peng, Weihe Wu

Abstract
In this paper, we develop two Riemannian stochastic smoothing algorithms for nonsmooth optimization problems on Riemannian manifolds, addressing distinct forms of the nonsmooth term h h . Both methods combine dynamic smoothing with a momentum-based variance reduction scheme in a fully online manner. When h h is Lipschitz continuous, we propose an stochastic algorithm under adaptive parameter that achieves the optimal iteration complexity of 𝒪 ⁡ ( ϵ − 3 ) \mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-3}) , improving upon the best-known rates for exist algorithms. When h h is the indicator function of a convex set, we design a new algorithm using truncated momentum, and under a mild error bound condition with parameter θ ≥ 1 \theta\geq 1 , we establish a complexity of 𝒪 ~ ​ ( ϵ − max ⁡ { θ + 2 , 2 ​ θ } ) \tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\epsilon^{-\max\{\theta+2,2\theta\}}) , in line with the best-known results in the Euclidean setting. Both algorithms feature a single-loop design with low per-iteration cost and require only 𝒪 ⁡ ( 1 ) \mathcal{O}(1) samples per iteration, ensuring that sample and iteration complexities coincide. Our framework encompasses a broad class of problems and recovers or matches optimal complexity guarantees in several important settings, including smooth stochastic Riemannian optimization, composite problems in Euclidean space, and constrained optimization via indicator functions.

Introduction

In this paper, we consider a general nonsmooth optimization problem of expectation (online) minimization over Riemannian manifold
\mathcal{M}
, defined as

\min_{x\in\mathcal{M}}F(x):=\mathbb{E}_{\xi\in\mathcal{D}}[\tilde{f}(x,\xi)]+h(c(x)),

(1.1)

where

is a compact Riemannian manifold embedded in

[

[

[

In the case where

To reduce the complexity bounds, several works

When

2.1
Riemannian optimization

An
n
-dimensional smooth manifold
\mathcal{M}
is an
n
-dimensional topological manifold equipped with a smooth structure, where each point has a neighborhood that is diffeomorphism to the
n
-dimensional Euclidean space. For all
x\in\mathcal{M}
, there exists a chart
(U,\psi)
such that
U
is an open set and
\psi
is a diffeomorphism between
U
and an open set
\psi(U)
in the Euclidean space. A tangent vector
\eta_{x}
to
\mathcal{M}
at
x
is defined as tangents of parametrized curves
\alpha
on
\mathcal{M}
such that
\alpha(0)=x
and

\eta_{x}u:=\dot{\alpha}(0)u=\left.\frac{d(u(\alpha(t)))}{dt}\right|_{t=0},\forall u\in\wp_{x}\mathcal{M},

where
\wp_{x}\mathcal{M}
is the set of all real-valued functions
f
defined in a neighborhood of
x
in
\mathcal{M}
. Then, the tangent space
T_{x}\mathcal{M}
of a manifold
\mathcal{M}
at
x
is defined as the set of all tangent vectors at point
x
. The manifold
\mathcal{M}
is called a Riemannian manifold if it is equipped with an inner product on the tangent space
T_{x}\mathcal{M}
at each
x\in\mathcal{M}
. If
\mathcal{M}
is a Riemannian submanifold of an Euclidean space
\mathcal{E}
, the inner product is defined as the Euclidean inner product:
\left<\eta_{x},\xi_{x}\right>=\mathrm{tr}(\eta_{x}^{\top}\xi_{x})
. The Riemannian gradient
\operatorname{grad}f(x)\in T_{x}\mathcal{M}
is the unique tangent vector satisfying

\left<\operatorname{grad}f(x),\xi\right>={D}f(x)[\xi],\forall\xi\in T_{x}\mathcal{M},

where
Df
denotes the differential of
f
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. If
\mathcal{M}
is a compact Riemannian manifold embedded in an Euclidean space, we have that
\operatorname{grad}f(x)=\mathcal{P}_{T_{x}\mathcal{M}}(\nabla f(x))
, where
\nabla f(x)
is the Euclidean gradient,
\mathcal{P}_{T_{x}\mathcal{M}}
is the projection operator onto the tangent space
T_{x}\mathcal{M}
.
The retraction operator is one of the most important ingredients for manifold optimization, which turns an element of
T_{x}\mathcal{M}
into a point in
\mathcal{M}
.

Definition 1
(Retraction,
[
2
]
).

A retraction on a manifold
\mathcal{M}
is a smooth mapping
\mathcal{R}:T\mathcal{M}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}
with the following properties. Let
\mathcal{R}_{x}:T_{x}\mathcal{M}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}
be the restriction of
\mathcal{R}
at
x
. It satisfies

•

\mathcal{R}_{x}(0_{x})=x
, where
0_{x}
is the zero element of
T_{x}\mathcal{M}
,

•

{D}\mathcal{R}_{x}(0_{x})=id_{T_{x}\mathcal{M}}
,where
id_{T_{x}\mathcal{M}}
is the identity mapping on
T_{x}\mathcal{M}
.

We have the following Lipschitz-type inequalities on the retraction on the compact submanifold.

Proposition 2
(
[
8
, Appendix B]
).

Let
\mathcal{R}
be a retraction operator on a compact submanifold
\mathcal{M}
. Then, there exist two positive constants
\alpha,\beta
such that
for all
x\in\mathcal{M}
and all
u\in T_{x}\mathcal{M}
, we have

\left\{\begin{aligned} \|\mathcal{R}_{x}(u)-x\|&\leq\alpha\|u\|,\\
\|\mathcal{R}_{x}(u)-x-u\|&\leq\beta\|u\|^{2}.\end{aligned}\right.

(2.1)

Another basic ingredient for manifold optimization is vector transport.

Definition 3
(Vector Transport,
[
2
]
).

The vector transport
\mathcal{T}
is a smooth mapping

T\mathcal{M}\oplus T\mathcal{M}\rightarrow T\mathcal{M}:(\eta_{x},\xi_{x})\mapsto\mathcal{T}_{\eta_{x}}(\xi_{x})\in T\mathcal{M}

(2.2)

satisfying the following properties for all
x\in\mathcal{M}
:

•

for any
\xi_{x}\in T_{x}\mathcal{M}
,
\mathcal{T}_{0_{x}}\xi_{x}=\xi_{x}
,

•

\mathcal{T}_{\eta_{x}}(a\xi_{x}+b\tau_{x})=a\mathcal{T}_{\eta_{x}}(\xi_{x})+b\mathcal{T}_{\eta_{x}}(\tau_{x})
.

When there exists
\mathcal{R}
such that
y=\mathcal{R}_{x}(\eta_{x})
, we write
\mathcal{T}_{x}^{y}(\xi_{x})=\mathcal{T}_{\eta_{x}}(\xi_{x})
.

2.2
Moreau envelope and retraction smoothness

We first provide the definition of proximal operator and Moreau envelope.

Definition 4
.

For a proper, convex and lower semicontinuous function
h:\mathbb{R}^{m}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, the Moreau envelope of
h
with the parameter
\mu>0
is given by

h_{\mu}(y):=\inf_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}\left\{h(z)+\frac{1}{2\mu}\|z-y\|^{2}\right\}.

The optimal solution is given by the proximal operator:

\operatorname{prox}_{\mu h}(y)=\arg\min_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}\left\{h(z)+\frac{1}{2\mu}\|z-y\|^{2}\right\}.

(2.3)

The following result demonstrates that the Moreau envelope of a convex function is not only continuously differentiable but also possesses a bounded gradient norm.

Proposition 5
.

[
6
, Lemma 3.3]

Let
h
be a proper, convex function. Denote
\mu>0
. Then, Moreau envelope
h_{\mu}
has Lipschitz continuous gradient over
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with constant
\mu^{-1}
, and the gradient is given by

\nabla h_{\mu}(x)=\frac{1}{\mu}\left(x-\operatorname{prox}_{\mu h}(x)\right)\in\partial h\left(\operatorname{prox}_{\mu h}(x)\right).

(2.4)

Moreover, if
h
is
\ell_{h}
-Lipschitz continuous, it holds that

\|\nabla h_{\mu}(x)\|\leq\ell_{h},\;\|x-\operatorname{prox}_{\mu h}(x)\|\leq\mu\ell_{h}.

(2.5)

The following lemma, stated as Lemma 4.1 in
[
6
]
, establishes a relationship between the function values of two Moreau envelopes with distinct parameters.

Lemma 6
.

[
6
, Lemma 4.1]

Let
h
be a proper, closed, and convex function. Then

h_{\mu_{2}}(y)\leq h_{\mu_{1}}(y)+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}}{\mu_{2}}\mu_{1}\left\|\nabla h_{\mu_{1}}(y)\right\|^{2},

(2.6)

where
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
satisfy that
0<\mu_{2}\leq\mu_{1}
. In particular, consider the following two cases:

•

When
h
is Lipschitz continuous with module
\ell_{h}
, it holds that

h_{\mu_{2}}(y)\leq h_{\mu_{1}}(y)+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}}{\mu_{2}}\mu_{1}\ell_{h}^{2},

(2.7)

•

When
h
is a indicator function on convex set
\mathcal{C}
, i.e.,
h(x)=\delta_{\mathcal{C}}(x)
, it holds that

h_{\mu_{2}}(y)\leq h_{\mu_{1}}(y)+\frac{1}{2}(\frac{1}{\mu_{2}}-\frac{1}{\mu_{1}})\mathrm{dist}^{2}(y,\mathcal{C}).

(2.8)

Proof.

By using the definition of the Moreau envelope, we obtain

\displaystyle h_{\mu_{2}}(y)

\displaystyle=\min_{u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\{h(u)+\frac{1}{2\mu_{2}}\|y-u\|^{2}\right\}

\displaystyle=\min_{u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\{h(u)+\frac{1}{2\mu_{1}}\|y-u\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{\mu_{2}}-\frac{1}{\mu_{1}}\right)\|y-u\|^{2}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq h\left(\operatorname{prox}_{\mu_{1}h}(y)\right)+\frac{1}{2\mu_{1}}\left\|y-\operatorname{prox}_{\mu_{1}h}(y)\right\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{\mu_{2}}-\frac{1}{\mu_{1}}\right)\left\|y-\operatorname{prox}_{\mu_{1}h}(y)\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=h_{\mu_{1}}(y)+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}}{\mu_{2}}\right)\mu_{1}\left\|\nabla h_{\mu_{1}}(y)\right\|^{2},

which proves (
2.6
). When
h
is Lipschitz continuous with
\ell_{h}
, we obtain (
2.7
) by applying (
2.5
). If
h(x)=\delta_{\mathcal{C}}(x)
, since
\left\|y-\operatorname{prox}_{\mu_{1}h}(y)\right\|^{2}=\left\|y-\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{C}}(y)\right\|^{2}
, which implies (
2.8
).

Next, we provide the definition of retraction smoothness. This concept plays a crucial role in the convergence analysis of the algorithms proposed in the subsequent sections.

Definition 7
.

[
8
, Retraction smooth]

A function
f:\mathcal{M}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is said to be retraction smooth (short to retr-smooth) with constant

where

and

.

Proposition 8
(Lemma 2.7,
[

]
).

Under Assumption

, if

is

(

2.3
Stationary point and smoothing problem

We start with the KKT condition of problem (
1.1
). In particular, we introduce the auxiliary variable
y=c(x)
:

\min_{x\in\mathcal{M}}f(x)+h(y),~\mathrm{s.t.}~c(x)=y.

(2.10)

The corresponding Lagrangian function is given as follows:

\ell(x,y;z):=f(x)+h(y)+\langle z,c(x)-y\rangle,

(2.11)

where
z
is the Lagrangian multiplier. Then the KKT condition of (
1.1
) is given as follows
[
15
]
: A point
x\in\mathcal{M}
satisfies the KKT condition if there exists
y,z\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
such that

\left\{\begin{aligned} 0&=\mathcal{P}_{T_{x}\mathcal{M}}\left(\nabla f(x)+\nabla c(x)^{\top}z\right),\\
0&\in-z+\partial h(y),\\
0&=c(x)-y.\end{aligned}\right.

(2.12)

Using the formulation, we give the
\epsilon
-stationary point of problem (
1.1
).

Definition 9
.

We say
x\in\mathcal{M}
is a
\epsilon
-stationary point of problem (
1.1
) if there exists
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
z\in\partial h(y)
satisfies

\mathbb{E}\left[\|\mathcal{P}_{T_{x}\mathcal{M}}(\nabla f(x)+\nabla c(x)^{\top}z)\|\right]\leq\epsilon,~~\|c(x)-y\|\leq\epsilon.

(2.13)

When
\epsilon=0
in Definition
9
, it immediately follows that
x
satisfies the exact KKT condition (
2.12
). Compared with the
\epsilon
-stationarity obtained by directly relaxing the KKT conditions in
[
14
]
, our notion of an
\epsilon
-stationary point is more restrictive: we require
z\in\partial h(y)
exactly, whereas
[
14
]
only ensures
\mathrm{dist}(z,\partial h(y))\leq\epsilon
.
Peng et al.
[
33
]
define
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
to be a
generalized

\epsilon
-stationary point if there exists
x\in\mathcal{M}
such that

\mathbb{E}\hbox{$\left[\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}\mathrm{dist}^{2}\left(0,\;\mathcal{P}_{T_{x}\mathcal{M}}(\nabla f(y)+\nabla c(x)^{\top}\partial h(y))\right)\hbox{$\left]\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}\leq\epsilon,\quad\|x-y\|^{2}\leq\epsilon^{2}.

However, under this definition the point
y
is not required to lie on the manifold
\mathcal{M}
. In particular, it is unclear whether the discrepancy
\|c(x)-y\|\leq\epsilon
holds. In contrast, our definition of
\epsilon
-stationarity inherently enforces
x\in\mathcal{M}
, making it a more appropriate notion in this setting.

Following
[
33
,
4
]
, we give the smoothed approximation of problem (
1.1
) using Moreau envelope in (
4
). In particular,
given the smoothing parameter
\mu>0
, we define the smoothed approximation problem:

\min_{x\in\mathcal{M}}F_{\mu}(x):=f(x)+h_{\mu}(c(x)).

(2.14)

When
f
is differentiable,
h
is convex and
c
is differentiable, we obtain from Proposition
5
that
\nabla F_{\mu}
can be written as

\displaystyle\nabla F_{\mu}(x)

\displaystyle=\nabla f(x)+\nabla c(x)^{\top}\nabla h_{\mu_{k}}(c(x))

(2.15)

\displaystyle=\nabla f(x)+\frac{1}{\mu}\nabla c(x)^{\top}(c(x)-\operatorname{prox}_{\mu h}(c(x))).

In this paper, we solve a sequence of smoothed problems of the form (
2.14
), where the smoothing parameter
\mu
is dynamically adjusted rather than fixed. The parameter
\mu
controls the trade-off between smoothness and approximation accuracy. At the early stages of the algorithm, we choose a relatively large value of
\mu
to ensure favorable smoothness properties. As the iteration proceeds, we gradually decrease
\mu
so that the smoothed problems more closely approximate the original nonsmooth problem. The detailed algorithm is presented in the next section.

3
Smoothing algorithmic framework

Throughout this paper, we make the following assumptions for problem (
1.1
).

Assumption 1
.

A:

The manifold
\mathcal{M}
is a compact Riemannian submanifold embedded in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

B:

The function
f
is
L_{f}
-Lipschitz coninuous on
\mathcal{M}
and retr-smooth with a retraction operator
\mathcal{R}
and constant
L
. The nonlinear mapping
c
is
L_{c}
-Lipscitz continuous and
L_{\nabla c}
-smooth on
\mathcal{M}
.

C:

For each
\xi\in\mathcal{D}
,
\tilde{f}(x,\xi)
is differentiable and the Riemannian gradient
\operatorname{grad}\tilde{f}(x,\xi)
is unbiased with bounded variance. That is, for all
x\in\mathcal{M}
, it satisfies for some
\sigma>0
that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\operatorname{grad}\tilde{f}(x,\xi)\right]=\operatorname{grad}f(x),~~\mathbb{E}\left[\|\operatorname{grad}\tilde{f}(x,\xi)-\operatorname{grad}f(x)\|^{2}\right]\leq\sigma^{2}.

D:

The function
\tilde{f}(x,\xi)
satisfies the average smoothness condition: for any

3.1
The proposed algorithm for Lipschitz continuous function

This subsection considers the case that
h
is Lipschitz continuous. We first give the following assumption.

Assumption 2
.

The function
h
is convex and
\ell_{h}
-Lipschitz continuous.

Under this assumption, we propose a stochastic smoothing method with a recursive momentum scheme for solving problem (
1.1
). Our method builds upon the frameworks of
[
33
,
4
]
, but introduces a substantially different strategy for constructing the stochastic gradient estimator, leading to improved convergence guarantees over existing stochastic methods
[
33
,
14
]
. Starting from an initial point
x_{0}\in\mathcal{M}
, we approximately solve a sequence of smoothed subproblems of the form
\min_{x\in\mathcal{M}}F_{\mu_{k}}(x)
, as defined in (
2.14
), by performing a single stochastic gradient descent step:

x_{k+1}=\mathcal{R}_{x_{k}}(-\tau_{k}G_{k}),

(3.3)

where
\mathcal{R}_{x_{k}}
is a retraction at
x_{k}
,
\mu_{k}
is a smoothing parameter, and
\tau_{k}
is an adaptive stepsize. Motivated by
[
28
]
, we propose the following update rule:

\tau_{k}=\frac{1}{\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\|G_{i}\|^{2}/a_{k+1}\right)^{1/3}}.

(3.4)

Notice that we use
a_{k+1}
instead of
a_{i+1}
, which is crucial for the convergence analysis. To approximate the Riemannian gradient
\operatorname{grad}F_{\mu_{k}}(x_{k})
, we exploit its structure:

\operatorname{grad}F_{\mu_{k}}(x_{k})=\operatorname{grad}f(x_{k})+\mathcal{P}_{T_{x_{k}}\mathcal{M}}\left(\nabla c(x_{k})^{\top}\nabla h_{\mu_{k}}(c(x_{k}))\right),

and define the estimator
G_{k}
as:

G_{k}=\delta_{k}+\mathcal{P}_{T_{x_{k}}\mathcal{M}}\left(\nabla c(x_{k})^{\top}\nabla h_{\mu_{k}}(c(x_{k}))\right),

where
\delta_{k}
is a variance-reduced estimator of
\operatorname{grad}f(x_{k})
. In particular, we compute
\delta_{k}
via a recursive momentum technique inspired by
[
12
]
:

\delta_{k}=\operatorname{grad}\tilde{f}(x_{k},\xi_{k})+(1-a_{k})\,\mathcal{T}_{x_{k-1}}^{x_{k}}\left(\delta_{k-1}-\operatorname{grad}\tilde{f}(x_{k-1},\xi_{k})\right),

(3.5)

where
a_{k}\in(0,1]
is the momentum parameter,
\xi_{k}
is a randomly drawn sample, and
\mathcal{T}_{x_{k-1}}^{x_{k}}
denotes a vector transport operator from
T_{x_{k-1}}\mathcal{M}
to
T_{x_{k}}\mathcal{M}
. The detailed process is referred to Algorithm
1
.

We highlight several key distinctions between Algorithm
1
and existing stochastic methods for nonsmooth optimization on compact submanifolds. Most notably, our construction of the stochastic gradient estimator departs fundamentally from those in
[
14
,
33
]
. In particular, the algorithm in
[
14
]
applies a momentum scheme directly to the augmented Lagrangian function
\mathcal{L}_{\rho}
, thereby introducing uncontrolled variance stemming from its deterministic components (analogous to the smoothed term
h_{\mu}(x)
in our Algorithm
1
). This issue becomes particularly severe when the penalty parameter
\rho
is large, which impedes effective variance reduction and renders the gradient estimator
G_{k}
less reliable. Similarly, the method proposed in
[
33
]
shares this limitation and, in addition, does not incorporate any variance reduction mechanisms. Another major distinction lies in algorithmic structure: both
[
33
]
and
[
14
]
rely on double-loop frameworks (although
[
33
]
provides a theoretically weaker single-loop variant), whereas our Algorithm
1
achieves a fully single-loop implementation without sacrificing convergence guarantees.

These structural differences in gradient estimator design and algorithmic formulation result in significant improvements in complexity bounds. Our method attains the optimal sample complexity of
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-3})
, improving upon the
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-5})
and
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-3.5})
rates reported in
[
33
]
and
[
14
]
, respectively. Moreover, our analysis extends seamlessly to the case where

is an indicator function, yielding the same optimal complexity bound. This result is consistent with recent findings in Euclidean settings, such as those established in
[
32
]
, as we detail in the next subsection.

Lemma 16

Let

be two real numbers. Suppose that there exist
\alpha,\beta\in(0,1)
such that

Proof.

Combining with (

Re-ranging the inequality completes the proof.

5
Conclusion

In this work, we proposed two Riemannian stochastic smoothing algorithms for solving nonsmooth optimization problems on manifolds, tailored respectively to the cases where the nonsmooth term
h
is Lipschitz continuous or an indicator function of a convex set. Both algorithms leverage dynamic smoothing and online momentum-based variance reduction, and feature a single-loop structure with low per-iteration cost and constant sample size per iteration. For the Lipschitz case, our algorithm achieves the optimal iteration complexity of
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-3})
, improving upon existing stochastic methods in the Riemannian setting. In the constrained case, under a mild error bound condition, we establish a near-optimal complexity of
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\epsilon^{-\max\{\theta+2,2\theta\}})
. In future work, we plan to conduct numerical experiments to evaluate the performance of the proposed algorithms.

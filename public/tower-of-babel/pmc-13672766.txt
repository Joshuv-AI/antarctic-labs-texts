Unsteady MHD transport of a tangent-hyperbolic ternary hybrid nanofluid over a permeable stretching surface with variable thermal conductivity and nonlinear radiation
Authors: Abanti Datta, Amitava Dutta, Arka Banerjee, Sayantan Mukherjee
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis work is a numerical exploration of the unsteady MHD transport of a ternary hybrid nanofluid of tangent-hyperbolic flow over a permeable stretching surface. The working fluid consists of Cu, and nanoparticles dispersed in ethylene glycol. The model includes nonlinear radiation, variable thermal conductivity, Joule heating, and Darcy-Forchheimer porous effects. The governing equations of partial differential equations are reduced to similarity equations of ordinary differential equations and solved by MATLAB BVP5C. Simulations show that an increase in the Weissenberg number and Forchheimer parameter decreases the velocity field because of the increase in elastic and inertial resistance and the energy dissipation elevates the fluid temperature. The variable thermal conductivity will greatly enhance thermal gradients near the wall and increase the performance of heat transfer. Moreover, increased concentrations of nanoparticles enhance skin-friction coefficients, Nusselt and Sherwood numbers. The ternary hybrid nanofluid is better than mono and binary systems due to synergistic interactions of the nanoparticles that promotes thermal conductivity and mass diffusion. The findings indicate that the Nusselt number decreases up to 92.14% with increasing magnetic parameter and decreases to about 95.93% with increasing thermal radiation, which shows a high level of suppression of heat transfer. Conversely, the stretching parameter augments the skin friction and mass transfer by approximately 42.48 and 11.47% respectively, whereas thermophysical parameters augment the Nusselt number by up to 286.9%. Comprehensively, the numerical analysis shows, quantitatively, the competing interplay of electromagnetic, thermal and diffusive processes in dictating transport behaviour in ternary hybrid nanofluid flow. These results demonstrate the possibilities of tangent-hyperbolic ternary hybrid nanofluids in the advanced cooling and thermal management processes with intricate rheological and electromagnetic interactions. The soundness and correctness of the numerical framework is validated against existing literature.Keywords: Hybrid nanofluid, tangent-hyperbolic model, Unsteady MHD, Nonlinear radiation, Darcy-Forchheimer effects

Introduction

Fluids with non-Newtonian rheology sought attention of the researchers due to its increasing demand in diagnostics, drug delivery, polymer processing, and advanced chemical systems [1–3]. While the Newtonian fluids are known for its invariable viscosity over the ranges of shear rate, the viscosity of the non-Newtonian fluids varies with the same. While most of the studies on non-Newtonian rheology are carried out on pseudoplastic, dilatant, Carreau, yield pseudoplastic and viscoelastic models, the tangent hyperbolic fluid (THF) model is underexplored so far. The THF model uses a hyperbolic tangent function to describe a non-linear stress–strain relation capturing both the viscous and elastic effects under varying shear conditions. This feature makes it suitable for modelling polymer and biological flows [4–6].

Nanofluids are typically suspensions of metallic, oxide-based, or carbon-based nanoparticles (1–100 nm) in a base fluid like water, oil, ethylene glycol etc. The nanoparticles are added to improve the heat transfer performance of the base fluid. Mono-nanofluids are base fluids containing a single kind of nano particles, whereas in hybrid nanofluids more than one kind of nanofluids are dispersed in a base fluid. The term ternary nanofluid indicates that the types of nanoparticles are three. The ternary nanofluids are known to exhibit improved thermal conductivity, better stability, and greater heat transfer efficiency compared to mono and binary nanofluids. Their wide used in electronics cooling, heat exchangers, renewable energy systems, and biomedical device has grown interest of the researchers in last two decades mostly. Entropy generation, particle shape effect and advanced heat transport model studies on nanofluids are carried out by several researchers. Interested authors can be referred to Boujelbene et al. [7], Alqawasmi et al. [8], and Jat et al. [9]. The study of Mahboobtosi et al. [10] exclusively reports that ternary nanofluids outperform single-particle systems. Farooq et al. [11] and Abbas et al. [12] studied mixed convection and cilia-driven flow in curved domains. Nabwey et al. [13] analyzed boundary layer flow over curved stretching surfaces. Heat diffusion, magnetic effects, and geometric variations in hybrid nanofluids are investigated by Jamshed et al. [14], Noreen [15], and Al Garalleh [16]. Use of machine learning is also proved to be an effective technique in predicting thermal performance of nanofluids. Patil et al. [17] examined mixed convection over rough cylinders under magnetic effects.

Numerous studies have already addressed rheological and nanofluids flows under different physical configurations. The heat and mass transfer in non-Newtonian nanofluids within vertical enclosures have been examined by Hashim et al. [18] Rehman et al. [19] analyzed flow between intersecting planes with slip effects. Nadeem et al. [20] utilized a simple configuration to study the unsteady tangent hyperbolic hybrid nanofluid flow over exponentially stretching surfaces. Alqahtani et al. [21] studied ternary hybrid MHD nanofluids over wedge geometries. Alkaoud et al. [5] studied flow and temperature over stretching sheets in presence of magnetic field, porosity and Weissenberg number. Choudhary et al. [22] considered the porous and thermal aspects to study the boundary layer flow of tangent hyperbolic fluid in diverging channels.

Unsteady flow assumption is more realistic than the steady flow in many systems, particularly to capture in turbulence, flow separation, and transient heat transfer. The work of Kebede et al. [23], Jamal et al. [24], and Reddy et al. [25] strongly argue that the unsteady parameters prominently affect velocity and thermal fields at higher Weissenberg numbers and under electromagnetic effects. Seeral works hae also been carried out with oscillatory flow and nonlinear stretching surfaces [22, 26]. Magnetohydrodynamics (MHD) is becoming popular among researchers as it finds important roles in astrophysics, nuclear cooling, and biomedical systems. MHD squeezing flow in porous media has been examined by Krishna and Chamkha [27]. The study exclusively reported that induced magnetic fields are negligible in water-based nanofluids due to low electrical conductivity. The Hall Effect is also weak under low velocity or weak magnetic field conditions. The nonlinear thermal radiation plays an important role in the heat transfer modelling of combustion, aerospace, geothermal, nuclear, and cooling systems [28–33]. The Stefan-Boltzmann law is nonlinear, the heat transfer behaviour is influenced when the thermal properties are temperature-dependent. Flow. Flow over stretching sheet is important in polymer extrusion, wire drawing and cooling systems. Interested readers can also be referred to other relevant studies on boundary layer flow, heat transfer and mass transfer over such surfaces for improvement in system design [34–38].

Al₂O₃–Cu–TiO₂ with ethylene glycol (EG) is selected based on an energetic consideration of the thermophysical properties of the three nanoparticles. Al₂O₃ is used for chemical stability and a moderate thermal conductivity (40 W/m·K); Cu for high electrical and thermal conductivity (400 W/m·K); and TiO₂ for oxidation resistance and photocatalytic activity. EG is chosen because of its wide applications in automotive cooling system and small heat exchanger, and low freezing point. This ternary combination of EG has been tested experimentally in the literature [39, 40, 64]. Moreover, some recent works have investigated the non-Newtonian nanofluids namely tangent-hyperbolic and Casson types with MHD conditions including radiation and viscous dissipation [41–45, 67, 68].

Several very recent studies further reinforce the growing interest in nanofluid- and hybrid-nanofluid-based heat transfer enhancement across allied configurations, including groundwater and aquifer thermal transport [46], interfacial and combined convective-radiative heat and mass transfer [47, 48], biological and biomechanical flow analogues [49, 66], thermal energy storage systems [50], convective transport in micro channels [51, 65], and hydrogen-based energy systems[52]. These studies collectively confirm the continued relevance of the present tangent-hyperbolic ternary hybrid nanofluid model and situate it within the current literature.

Despite these advances, most studies consider only a limited set of physical effects. The combined influence of viscous dissipation, Joule heating, nonlinear thermal radiation, chemical reactions, thermophoresis, Brownian motion, Darcy–Forchheimer porous media, and variable thermal conductivity has not been examined for unsteady tangent hyperbolic ternary hybrid nanofluids. This gap limits the accurate prediction of transport behavior in complex systems.

The main objective of the present study is to address this gap by developing a comprehensive model for unsteady tangent hyperbolic ternary hybrid nanofluid flow. The study considers a ternary nanofluid composed of Al₂O₃, Cu, and TiO₂ nanoparticles dispersed in ethylene glycol. The novelty of this work lies in the simultaneous inclusion of multiple interacting effects within a single framework. These include viscous dissipation, Joule heating, nonlinear thermal radiation, chemical reactions, thermophoresis, Brownian motion, Darcy–Forchheimer porous media, and variable thermal conductivity. Such a combined analysis has not been reported for this class of fluids as per the best of authors’ knowledge.

The wall temperature, , ambient temperature,, well concentration, , and ambient concentration, of the THNF are considered constants.

Schematic of the physical description of the problem

The transport model consists of a set of governing equations including modified Navier-Stokes momentum, mass, and advective-diffusive energy transport equation containing thermophoresis, Brownian motion, viscous dissipation, chemical reactions, non-linear thermal radiation, heat generation, magnetic dissipation, and wall mass suction term. A constant magnetic field is applied perpendicular to the surface in the positive y-direction, the induced magnetic field is considered negligible and ignored. We also consider Darcy-Forchheimer flow of the THNF with variable thermal conductivity [59] in the present study assuming the porosity of the sheet remains constant. It is also assumed that thermophysical properties of the working fluid are constant and the system is in thermal equilibrium. The THNF is created through the dispersion of Al2O3, Cu, and TiO2 nanoparticles in C2H6O2 medium.

Numerical method

The transformed system of nonlinear ordinary differential equations given in Eqs. (11)–(13), together with the boundary conditions (14), is solved numerically using the bvp5c solver available in MATLAB. This solver is specifically developed for boundary value problems and is based on a collocation technique with adaptive mesh selection, which ensures reliable and accurate solutions for nonlinear and stiff systems. The bvp5c algorithm is particularly effective for problems involving multiple boundary conditions and coupled differential equations. Its adaptive mesh refinement capability improves solution accuracy while minimizing computational effort. In comparison with conventional finite difference schemes, this approach provides faster convergence and reduced dependency on discretization size, making it suitable for complex fluid flow and heat transfer analyses.

Results and discussion

In this section, we discuss the numerical solution for the time-dependent MHD hyperbolic tangent ternary hybrid nanofluid flowing over stretched surface. Simulations are carried out to determine temperature, velocity, concentration, local skin friction, Nusselt number, and Sherwood number under different physical parameter effects such as Weissenberg number (We), magnetic field parameter (M), spherical-shaped Al2O3,Cu, and TiO2 nanoparticle volume fraction (1, 2, 3) ,heat generation (Q), Forchheimer number (Fr), Prandtl number (Pr), nonlinear thermal radiation(R), unsteady parameter (A), temperature ratio parameter (θw), Eckert number (Ec), Schmidt number (Sc), chemical reaction (Cr), Porosity parameter (K), Brownian (Nb) and thermophoresis (Nt) diffusion parameter which are showcased via graphical and table illustrations. The choice of parametric values in this study is based on some physical relevance, the characteristics of materials properties and flow regimes that can potentially exist, the heat transfer conditions involved, and lessons learned from previous empirical studies. Mapping these values to particular physical situations increases the accuracy and relevance of the model, yielding better predictive results and ameliorating understanding with respect to the system behaviour.

Conclusion

This study investigated the unsteady MHD flow and heat transfer of a Tangent-Hyperbolic ternary hybrid nanofluid over a permeable stretching surface. Using the MATLAB BVP5C solver, we analysed the effects of various physical parameters. The following quantitative and qualitative conclusions are drawn:

The velocity profile reduces as the magnetic parameter M increases, due to the retarding Lorentz force; increasing M from 0.1 to 0.5 raises the skin friction magnitude by approximately 14.3% to 24.2% (Sect. 3.1, Figs. 3 and 4). The Forchheimer number similarly increases inertial flow resistance and reduces the velocity profile, consistent with its role as a nonlinear drag term in the momentum equation (Eq. 7); a dedicated quantitative parametric sweep for is recommended as future work to establish its precise percentage influence on and .

This study provides valuable theoretical insights but has some limitations. Simplified assumptions, such as treating the nanofluid as a single-phase fluid and neglecting higher-order effects, may not fully capture real-world complexities. The BVP5C numerical method, while effective, has constraints in handling highly nonlinear systems. The findings are based on a specific parameter range, and extrapolation beyond this range requires further validation. Additionally, the assumption of uniform nanoparticle dispersion overlooks possible aggregation or sedimentation effects. To enhance validity and generalizability, future research should incorporate experimental validation and more advanced modeling approaches.

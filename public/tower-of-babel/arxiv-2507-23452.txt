Matching Large Deviation Bounds of the Zero-Range Process in the whole space
Benjamin Fehrman, Benjamin Gess, Daniel Heydecker

Abstract
. We consider the large deviations of the hydrodynamic rescaling of the zero-range process on ℤ d \mathbb{Z}^{d} in any dimension d ≥ 1 d\geq 1 . Under mild and canonical hypotheses on the local jump rate, we obtain matching upper and lower bounds, thus resolving the problem opened by [ KL99 ] . On the probabilistic side, we extend the superexponential estimate to any dimension, and prove the superexponential concentration on paths with finite entropy dissipation. In addition, we extend the theory of the parabolic-hyperbolic skeleton equation to the whole space, and remove global convexity/concavity assumptions on the nonlinearity.

Introduction & Main Result

We consider the large deviations of the zero-range process (ZRP) on the infinite lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Given a local jump rate

The goal of the current work is to describe the behaviour beyond the law of large numbers, and to obtain matching upper and lower bounds for the large deviations of

The first key difficulty appears in the proof of the superexponential estimate, see Theorem

, in both arbitrary dimension and infinite volume. In trying to follow the classical proof
[

,
BKL95a
,
KL99
]

with respect to the invariant measure, in terms of a Dirichlet form
\mathfrak{d}(f_{N})

BKL95a

[

Existing works in the literature which treat general dimension
[

,

]
avoid this issue in the torus
\mathbb{T}^{d},d\geq 1
through an averaging procedure, which tacitly benefits from the geometry by averaging over a

In order to resolve this issue, we introduce a further approximation, which is agnostic to both dimension and the global domain geometry. In Lemma
3.5
, we show that the variational problem appearing in the whole superexponential estimate gains
regularity in the probability space from the regularity of test functions
in an
N

may be restricted to those

enjoying a uniform bound on the contributions of any one edge

to the Dirichlet form, up to an error which vanishes, uniformly in

The second key observation is that the existing bounds found in the literature
[
BKL95a
,
KL99
]
do not determine a

,

,

\partial_{t}\rho=\Delta\varphi(\rho)-\nabla\cdot(\varphi^{1/2}(\rho)g),\qquad g\in(L^{2}_{t,x})^{d}.

These results have subsequently been applied to prove a full large deviations principle for a zero-range process with rescaled particle-size about the porous medium equation by two of the authors
[
GH23
]
. However, when applied to the zero-range process in the classical hydrodynamical scaling, the validation that the macroscopic diffusivity

This analysis culminates in Theorem

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Preliminaries on the Zero-Range Process

We first give some preliminary facts about the zero-range process. Assuming only (A1) and that
\lambda(n)\to\infty
, the partition function given by

(2.1)

Z(\varphi):=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\varphi^{k}}{\prod_{1\leq l\leq k}\lambda(l)}

is finite for all
0\leq\varphi<\infty
. Under the same assumption, for every density
0\leq\rho<\infty
, there exists a unique invariant measure
{\nu}_{\rho}
on
X
, which is the product measure under which each value
\eta(x),x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
has the distribution

(2.2)

{\nu}_{\rho}(\eta\in X:\eta(x)=k)=\frac{\varphi^{k}(\rho)}{Z(\varphi(\rho))\prod_{1\leq l\leq k}\lambda(l)}

where
\varphi(\rho)
is (uniquely) chosen so that
\mathbb{E}_{\nu_{\rho}}[\eta(0)]=\rho
. The nonlinearity
\varphi
may be recovered by

(2.3)

\varphi(\rho)=\mathbb{E}_{\nu_{\rho}}[\lambda(\eta(0))]

and it is a well-known piece of folklore that, under (
1.4
), the nonlinearity
\varphi
is uniformly elliptic: for some
0<a\leq A<\infty
, for all
\xi\in[0,\infty)
,

(2.4)

a\leq\varphi^{\prime}(\xi)\leq A.

These measures satisfy the change-of-variables formula

(2.5)

\varphi(\rho)\int_{X}F(\eta+1_{x})\nu_{\rho}(d\eta)=\int_{X}\lambda(\eta(x))F(\eta)\nu_{\rho}(d\eta).

We have the further consequence of the finiteness of the partition function, which will play a role similar to
[
BKL95b
, (A4)]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

There exists a convex function
w:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
such that
w(x)/x\to\infty
as
x\to\infty
, and such that, for any
\rho\in[0,\infty)
, there exists
\theta>0
such that

(2.6)

\mathbb{E}_{\nu_{\rho}}\Big[e^{\theta w(\eta(0))}\Big]<\infty.

In the rest of the paper, we will reserve the notation
\varphi
for the function thus defined, which characterises the hydrodynamic limit and large deviations. We distinguish this from the notation
\Phi
in
5
-
8
, which we use to denote a more general nonlinearity. We similarly write
\mathcal{D}(v)
for the entropy dissipation functional for the particular nonlinearity
\varphi
, given by

(2.7)

\mathcal{D}(v):=\|\nabla\varphi^{1/2}(v)\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2};\qquad v\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

which we allow to take the value
\infty
if
\varphi^{1/2}(v)\not\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

2.2.
Topological Definitions

We now introduce some topological spaces, which are deferred from the statement of Theorem
1.2
. We write
\mathcal{M}
for the space of locally finite, nonnegative Radom measures on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, equipped with a metric
d
inducing the vague topology of convergence against test functions
f\in C_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. We will take the path space

(2.8)

\mathbb{D}:=\mathbb{D}([0,T],(\mathcal{M},d))

to be the Skorokhod space on a fixed time interval
[0,T]
. We will also write
\mathcal{C}:=\textrm{C}([0,T];(\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),d))
for those trajectories which are further continuous under the metric
d
.

Densities

In the statement of Definition
1.1
, we carefully distinguished between a trajectory
\pi\in\mathbb{D}
and its density
v
, should one exist. In order to ease statements in the sequel, we will ignore this difference. We therefore identify
L^{1}_{{\rm loc},+}:=L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d},[0,\infty))
with the absolutely continuous measures in
\mathcal{M}
by identifying every
v
with the measure
v(x)dx
of which it is the density. We write
\mathbb{D}_{\rm a.c.}
to denote the Skorokhod space
\mathbb{D}([0,T],(L^{1}_{{\rm loc},+},d))
, which is taken by the identification to those paths
\pi\in\mathbb{D}
which are absolutely continuous with respect to Lebesgue measure for all
0\leq t\leq T
. In this way, we may write
I(\rho)
for functions
\rho_{t}(x)
. We use the symbol
\rho
for a generic element of
\mathbb{D}_{\rm a.c.}
, and
\pi
for a generic element of
\mathbb{D}
. When not specified otherwise,
L^{1}_{{\rm loc},+}
will be understood to have the subspace topology induced by
d
; we will also speak of the strong Fréchet topology for the locally convex topology induced by the seminorms
v\mapsto\int_{|x|\leq R}|v(x)|dx,R\in(0,\infty)
.

3.
Superexponential Estimate in Infinite Volume

The goal of the following two sections is to give the probabilistic arguments which lead to large deviation upper and lower bounds, with (a priori) non-matching rate functions. For the upper bound, set, for
H\in C^{1,3}_{\rm c}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\rho\in\mathbb{D}_{\rm a.c.}
,

(3.1)

\begin{split}J(H,\rho)&:=\langle H_{T},\rho_{T}\rangle-\langle H_{0},\rho_{0}\rangle-\int_{0}^{T}\langle\partial_{t}H_{t},\rho_{t}\rangle dt\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(\rho_{t}(x))\left(\Delta H_{t}(x)+|\nabla H_{t}(x)|^{2}\right)dtdx\end{split}

if
\rho
has finite entropy dissipation
\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{D}(\rho_{t})dt<\infty
, and
\infty
if the last integral is infinite, or if
\pi\in\mathbb{D}\setminus\mathbb{D}_{\rm a.c.}
. The initial upper bound will be given in terms of

(3.2)

\mathcal{I}^{\rm up}(\pi):=\mathcal{H}_{\Phi}(\pi_{0}|\gamma)+\sup_{H\in C^{1,3}_{\rm c}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}J(H,\pi).

For the lower bound, set
\mathcal{S}
to be the set of
\rho\in\mathbb{D}_{\rm a.c.}
such that
\rho_{0}-\gamma\in C_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, such that
\rho_{0}
is strictly positive, and such that, for some compactly supported

solves the Fokker-Planck equation

(3.3)

\partial_{t}\rho_{t}=\frac{1}{2}\Delta\varphi(\rho_{t})-\nabla\cdot(\varphi(\rho_{t})\nabla H).

Define the lower semicontinuous envelope

(3.4)

\mathcal{I}^{\rm lo}(\pi):=\inf\Big\{\liminf_{n}\Big\{\mathcal{H}(\pi^{n}_{0}|\gamma)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(\rho^{n})|\nabla H^{n}|^{2}\Big\}:\rho^{n}\in\mathcal{S},\rho^{n}\to\pi\Big\}

where
H^{n}
are the controls associated to the smooth approximating trajectories
\rho^{n}\in\mathcal{S}
. With these defined, the theorem is as follows.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
be a probability space on which is defined a zero-range process
(\eta_{t},t\geq 0)
with a jump rate
\lambda
satisfying (A1) and
\lambda(n)\to\infty
, and suppose that
\eta_{0}\sim\nu_{\gamma}
, for some
\gamma\in(0,\infty)
. Let
\pi^{N}=(\pi^{N}_{t})_{0\leq t\leq T}
be the hydrodynamic rescaling. Then, for any closed set
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathbb{D}
,

(3.5)

\limsup_{N}N^{-d}\log\mathbb{P}\left(\pi^{N}\in\mathcal{A}\right)\leq-\inf\left\{\mathcal{I}^{\rm up}(\pi):\pi\in\mathcal{A}\right\}

and for any open set
\mathcal{U}\subset\mathbb{D}
,

(3.6)

\liminf_{N}N^{-d}\log\mathbb{P}\left(\pi^{N}\in\mathcal{U}\right)\geq-\inf\left\{\mathcal{I}^{\rm lo}(\pi):\pi\in\mathcal{U}\right\}.

Definition 3.2

A cylinder function

\Psi

\tau_{x}\Psi:=\Psi\circ\tau_{x}

With this notation fixed, the first technical result is as follows.

Theorem 3.3

Let

Then

(3.10)

\limsup_{\delta\to 0}\limsup_{\epsilon\to\infty}\limsup_{N\to\infty}N^{-d}\log\mathbb{P}\Big(\int_{0}^{t}\left|V(H,\psi,N,\epsilon)(t,\eta_{N^{2}t})\right|dt>\delta\Big)=-\infty.

While the technical condition we need on

The proof of the superexponential estimate closely follows the analagous arguments in

First, we fix a smooth, integrable function

This allows us to deduce the theorem from showing the same statement for

, for the new functional

V(H,\Psi,N,\epsilon,\beta)(t,\eta):=V(H,\Psi,N,\epsilon)(t,\eta)-\frac{\beta}{N^{d}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}a\big(\frac{x}{N}\big)w(\eta(x)).

\limsup_{\epsilon\to 0}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\sup_{f}\left\{\mathbb{E}_{f\nu_{\rho}}[V(H,\Psi,N,\epsilon,\beta)(t,\eta)]-\delta N^{2-d}\mathfrak{d}(f)\right\}\leq 0

where the supremum runs over probability density functions

The standard proof of the analogous statements to (

, Lemma 4.3]

\displaystyle\limsup_{\ell,\varepsilon,N\to\infty}\hskip 2.84544pt\sup_{x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:|x-y|\leq N\epsilon}\sup_{f}\left\{\mathbb{E}_{f\nu_{\rho}}[|\bar{\eta}^{\ell}(x)-\bar{\eta}^{\ell}(y)|-\beta\left(w(\bar{\eta}^{\ell}(x))+w(\bar{\eta}^{\ell}(y))\right)]-\delta N^{2-d}\mathfrak{d}(f)\right\}

(3.15)

\displaystyle\leq 0,

which implies that

\displaystyle\limsup_{\ell,\varepsilon,N\to\infty}\hskip 2.84544pt\sup_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sup_{f}\Big\{\mathbb{E}_{f\nu_{\rho}}\Big[\frac{1}{(2\epsilon N+1)^{d}}\sum_{\ell<|y|\leq\epsilon N}|\bar{\eta}^{N\epsilon}(x)-\bar{\eta}^{\ell}(x+y)|

(3.16)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad-\beta\left(w(\bar{\eta}^{\ell}(x))+w(\bar{\eta}^{\ell}(x+y))\right)\Big]-\delta N^{2-d}\mathfrak{d}(f)\Big\}\leq 0.

The argument for the the previous two equations relies on joining any two microscopic boxes

. A reduced Dirichlet form

of

along the path to yield

\mathfrak{d}_{2,\ell}(f^{2,\ell}_{x,y})\leq(N\epsilon+3)\mathfrak{d}(f).

This is the point at which the dimension

which vanishes in the double limit where first

, one only obtains a bound

, which diverges in the same limit. Indeed, to illustrate the non-triviality of adapting this argument to higher dimensions, we will give the following proposition.

Proposition 3.4

(No 2-Block Estimate Without Averaging)
.

Let

. For sufficiently small

is. In dimension

, the same applies to both limits (
3
), (
3
).

The failure of the proof is a question of how many ‘atypical’ edges there can be for a given profile

, in the sense that the contributions to the Dirichlet form are much larger than average, i.e.
\mathfrak{d}_{x,y}(f)\geq zN^{-d}\mathfrak{d}(f)
, for some large

. A simple Markov inequality shows that, for fixed

, the proportion of edges in

for which this holds is at most

and vanishes as

, uniformly in
f,N
. However, it may be the case that the small proportion of ‘atypical’ edges prevents us joining two boxes

by a path whose length is of the correct order, illustrated in Figure

. This is exactly analagous to the failure of embedding theorems

in dimensions

.

We will prove Proposition

at the end of this section by taking

to be a slowly-varying local equilibrium for a profile

with finite Fischer information, but which is neither continuous nor, in dimension

, of vanishing mean oscillation.

This problem is resolved for higher dimensions

in
[
KL13
, Lemma 3.2]
, but relying now on finite volume. The supremum in (

and taking the supremum over

, which makes the observable inside the expectation invariant under shifts: it therefore follows that the supremum is obtained at

for all neighbouring pairs

(3.18)

\mathfrak{d}_{2,\ell}(f^{2,\ell}_{x,y})\leq((N\epsilon)^{2}+C\ell^{d})(CN^{-d}\mathfrak{d}(f))\leq C_{\beta}\delta^{-1}(\epsilon^{2}+C\ell^{d}N^{-2})

which again vanishes in the limit

as required.

In conclusion, this leaves as an open problem the two block estimate on the full space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
for
d\geq 2
.

The novel argument we give for (
3.13
) is as follows. In infinite volume, the same argument of global averaging is not available. Instead, we average only

. As a consequence of the regularity of the test function

, we will show that the original supremum (

with a global ‘typicality’ estimate

and vanishes as

. For fixed

, the same argument leading to (

) yields an upper bound

, and the same argument yields the two-block estimate. The one-block estimate follows by the same argument as
[

, Lemma 4.2]
in any dimension, and the only remaining term is the error
\Gamma(z,H,\beta)
, which vanishes as

. The theorem will therefore follow from the same arguments as
[

,
KL13
]
, which we have sketched above, once we prove the following result.

Lemma 3.5

(Restriction to uniformly typical

)

Fix

\begin{split}&\limsup_{N\to\infty}\sup_{f}\left\{\mathbb{E}_{f\nu_{\rho}}[V(H,\Psi,N,\epsilon,\beta)(t,\eta)]-\delta N^{2-d}\mathfrak{d}(f)\right\}\\

&\hskip 85.35826pt+\Gamma(z,H,\beta,\delta)\end{split}

for some

\begin{split}&|V(H_{N,\theta},\Psi,N,\epsilon,\beta)(t,\eta)-V(H,\Psi,N,\epsilon,\beta)(t,\eta)|\\[4.30554pt]
&\hskip 56.9055pt\leq CM^{d}\|H-H_{N,\theta}\|_{\infty}\left(1+\bar{\eta}^{MN}(0)\right)-\beta a_{\star,M}M^{d}w\left(\bar{\eta}^{MN}(0)\right)\\[4.30554pt]

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{f\nu_{\rho}}[V(H_{N,\theta},\Psi,N,\epsilon)]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N^{d}(2\lfloor N\theta\rfloor+1)^{d}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{|y|_{\infty}\leq N\theta}H\big(\frac{x}{N}\big)\mathbb{E}_{f\nu_{\rho}}\left[\left((\tau_{x+y}\Psi)(\eta)-\widetilde{\Psi}(\bar{\eta}^{N\epsilon}(x+y)\right)\right]

5.
Weak Solutions of the Skeleton Equation

In order to develop a theory of the skeleton equation to close the large deviation principle in Theorems
1.2
-
3.1
, we must consider initial data that fall outside the global
L^{p}
-framework, for any
p\in[1,\infty)
. Indeed, in
any
limit obtained from large deviations in equilibrium, the total mass of
\rho
will be infinite for all times. It is for this reason that we instead develop a solution theory based on the following notion of relative entropy with respect to a constant fixed density
\gamma\in(0,\infty)
, which appears as the static large deviations rate function and a priori remains finite on fluctuations. Precisely, for every
\gamma\in(0,\infty)
, we let
\Psi_{\Phi,\gamma}
be the unique function satisfying

\Psi_{\Phi,\gamma}(\gamma)=0\;\;\textrm{and}\;\;\Psi_{\Phi,\gamma}^{\prime}(\xi)=\log\Big(\frac{\Phi(\xi)}{\Phi(\gamma)}\Big).

It follows from the definition that
\Psi_{\Phi,\gamma}
is convex and
\Psi_{\Phi,\gamma}(\xi)\geq 0
for every
\xi\in[0,\infty)
with
\Phi(\xi)=0
if and only if
\xi=\gamma
whenever
\Phi
is strictly increasing. In the case that
\Phi(\xi)=\xi
we have that

\Psi_{\Phi,\gamma}(\xi)=\xi\log\Big(\frac{\Phi(\xi)}{\Phi(\gamma)}\Big)-(\xi-\gamma),

which defines the relative entropy with respect to the constant density
\gamma
. The corresponding relative entropy space is then

\textrm{Ent}_{\Phi,\gamma}=\Big\{\rho\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\;\;\textrm{nonnegative and measurable with}\;\;\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Psi_{\Phi,\gamma}(\rho)<\infty\Big\}.

If we define
\tilde{\Psi}_{\Phi,\gamma}(\xi)=\Psi_{\Phi,\gamma}(\xi)
if
\xi\geq\gamma
and
\tilde{\Psi}_{\Phi,\gamma}(\xi)=-\Psi_{\Phi,\gamma}(\xi)
if
\xi\in[0,\gamma]
, it is straightforward to check that
\textrm{Ent}_{\Phi,\gamma}
is a complete, separable metric space with respect to the metric

d(f,g)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\tilde{\Psi}_{\Phi,\gamma}(f)-\tilde{\Psi}_{\Phi,\gamma}(g)|,

where the completeness and separability are respectively consequences of the fact that
\tilde{\Psi}_{\Phi,\gamma}
is strictly increasing and convex. We will similarly define the following
L^{p}
-spaces shifted by
\gamma
,

L^{p}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\Big\{\rho\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\;\;\textrm{nonnegative and measureable}\;\;\textrm{with}\;\;\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\rho-\gamma|^{p}<\infty\Big\}.

Observe that the spaces
L^{p}_{\gamma}
are canonically isomorphic to the space of
L^{p}
functions bounded from below by
-\gamma
, and that
L^{2}_{\gamma}
comes equipped with the induced inner product and norm.

We will first prove the existence of suitable weak solutions to the regularised equation

(5.1)

\partial_{t}\rho=\Delta\Phi(\rho)+\eta\Delta\rho-\nabla\cdot(\sigma(\rho)g)\;\;\textrm{in}\;\;\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,T)\;\;\textrm{with}\;\;\rho(x,0)=\rho_{0}(x),

for
\sigma
a smooth and bounded approximation of
\Phi^{\frac{1}{2}}
. Note here that we do not expect to see preservation of the
L^{1}_{\gamma}
-norm due to the fact that, unless

, the constant density
\gamma
is not a solution. We similarly define
H^{1}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
to be the space of
L^{2}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-functions with
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-integrable weak gradients.

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Psi_{\Phi,\gamma}(\rho(x,t))+\eta\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\Phi^{\prime}(\rho)}{\Phi(\rho)}|\nabla\rho|^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\Phi^{\prime}(\rho)^{2}}{\Phi(\rho)}|\nabla\rho|^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Psi_{\Phi,\gamma}(\rho_{0})+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\sigma(\rho)\Phi^{\prime}(\rho)}{\Phi(\rho)}g\cdot\nabla\rho,

and therefore, using Hölder’s inequality, Young’s inequality, and the assumption that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Psi_{\Phi,\gamma}(\rho(x,t))+\eta\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\Phi^{\prime}(\rho)}{\Phi(\rho)}|\nabla\rho|^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\Phi^{\prime}(\rho)^{2}}{\Phi(\rho)}|\nabla\rho|^{2}

which completes the proof of the final estimate. The proof of existence then follows from a Galerkin approximation, the energy estimate above, the boundedness of
\sigma

H^{1}(B_{R})
into
L^{2}(B_{R})
and the continuous embedding of

, for every

, and the Aubin–Lions–Simon lemma
[

,
Lio69
,

]
. ∎

Proposition 5.5
.

Let

We will now construct a solution of the skeleton equation

6.
Renormalised Kinetic Solutions of the Skeleton Equation

In this section, we establish the well-posedness of
renormalised kinetic solutions
to the skeleton equation

(6.1)

\partial_{t}\rho=\nabla\cdot(\Phi^{\prime}(\rho)\nabla\rho)-\nabla\cdot(\Phi^{\frac{1}{2}}(\rho)g)=2\nabla\cdot(\Phi^{\frac{1}{2}}(\rho)\nabla\Phi^{\frac{1}{2}}(\rho))-\nabla\cdot(\Phi^{\frac{1}{2}}(\rho)g),

set on the whole space with initial data with finite relative entropy with respect to some constant density
\gamma\in(0,\infty)
. A detailed overview of the derivation of the kinetic equation can be found, for instance, in
[
CP03
]
and
[
FG23
, Section 2]
, and here we only briefly recall the main details.

6.1.
Renormalised kinetic solutions

In this section, we derive the kinetic formulation of (
6.1
). After introducing an additional velocity variable
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
, the kinetic function
\chi
of a solution
\rho
is defined, for
\overline{\chi}\colon\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\{-1,0,1\}
satisfying
\overline{\chi}(s,\xi)=\mathbf{1}_{\{0<\xi<s\}}-\mathbf{1}_{\{s<\xi<0\}}
, by

\chi(x,\xi,t)=\overline{\chi}(\rho(x,t),\xi)=\mathbf{1}_{\{0<\xi<\rho(x,t)\}},

where the final equality follows from the nonnegativity of the solution. It then follows formally from the distributional identities

\nabla_{x}\chi(x,\xi,t)=\delta_{0}(\xi-\rho(x,t))\nabla\rho(x,t)\;\;\textrm{and}\;\;\partial_{\xi}\chi(x,\xi,t)=\delta_{0}(\xi)-\delta_{0}(\xi-\rho(x,t)),

that the kinetic function satisfies the equation

\partial_{t}\chi=\Phi^{\prime}(\xi)\Delta_{x}\chi-\big(\partial_{\xi}\Phi^{\frac{1}{2}}(\xi)\big)g(x,t)\nabla_{x}\chi+\big(\nabla_{x}g(x,t)\big)\Phi^{\frac{1}{2}}(\xi)\partial_{\xi}\chi+\partial_{\xi}p,

where
\chi(\cdot,0)=\overline{\chi}(\rho_{0})
and where
p
is a locally finite, nonnegative measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}\times(0,T)
that satisfies

(6.2)

\delta_{0}(\xi-\rho)\Phi^{\prime}(\xi)|\nabla\rho|^{2}\leq p.

However, due to the low regularity of the control
g
, we write the equation in its conservative form

\partial_{t}\chi=\Phi^{\prime}(\xi)\Delta_{x}\chi-\partial_{\xi}\big(g(x,t)\Phi^{\frac{1}{2}}(\xi)\nabla_{x}\chi\big)+\nabla_{x}\cdot\big(g(x,t)\Phi^{\frac{1}{2}}(\xi)\partial_{\xi}\chi\big)+\partial_{\xi}p,

which, with the estimates of Propositions
5.4
and
5.5
, form the basis of the solution theory.

Definition 6.1
.

Let
T,\gamma\in(0,\infty)
, let
\Phi\in\textrm{C}[0,\infty)\cap\textrm{C}^{1}_{\textrm{loc}}((0,\infty))
, and let
\rho_{0}\in\textrm{Ent}_{\Phi,\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. A nonnegative function
\rho\in L^{1}([0,T],L^{1}_{\textrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
is a renormalised kinetic solution of (
6.1
) with initial data
\rho_{0}
if
\rho
satisfies the following property.

(1)

The relative entropy estimate
: we have that

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla\Phi^{\frac{1}{2}}(\rho)|^{2}<\infty.

Furthermore, there exists a nonnegative, locally finite measure
p
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,\infty)\times[0,T]
that satisfies the following three properties.

(1)

Regularity
: in the sense of measures,

\delta_{0}(\xi-\rho)\Phi^{\prime}(\rho)|\nabla\rho|^{2}\leq p\;\;\textrm{on}\;\;\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,\infty)\times[0,T].

(2)

Vanishing at infinity
: we have that

\lim_{M\rightarrow\infty}p(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[M,M+1]\times[0,T])=0.

(3)

The equation
: there exists a subset
\mathcal{N}\subseteq(0,T]
of Lebesgue measure zero such that, for every
t\in[0,T]\setminus\mathcal{N}
, for every
\psi\in\textrm{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,\infty))\cap C(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,\infty))
with
\psi(x,0)=0
, the kinetic function
\chi
of
\rho
satisfies that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\chi(x,\xi,t)\psi(x,\xi)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\xi=-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Phi^{\prime}(\rho)\nabla\rho\cdot(\nabla_{x}\psi)(x,\rho)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Phi^{\frac{1}{2}}(\rho)\nabla\rho\cdot g(x,t)(\partial_{\xi}\psi)(x,\rho)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}r+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Phi^{\frac{1}{2}}(\rho)g(x,t)\cdot(\nabla_{x}\psi)(x,\rho)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\overline{\chi}(\rho_{0}(x),\xi)\psi(x,\xi)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\xi-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p\partial_{\xi}\psi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\xi\,\mathrm{d}r.

Remark 6.2
.

Observe that Definition
6.1
does not impose any continuity on the solution in time. However, using the local integrability implied by the relative entropy estimate, we prove in Proposition
6.6
below that the solutions admit continuous representatives in
\mathcal{C}
, and in fact are continuous in the strong Fréchet topology of
L^{1}_{{\rm loc},+}
.

6.2.
Uniqueness of renormalised kinetic solutions

In this section, we will prove the uniqueness of renormalised kinetic solutions in the sense of Definition
6.1
for nonlinearities
\Phi
that satisfy Assumption
6.3
below. The proof of uniqueness in Theorem
6.5
is significantly complicated by the fact that the various terms in the equation and the parabolic defect measure are neither globally integrable nor decaying at infinity with respect to the velocity variable
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
. We treat these terms by first introducing a cutoff in the velocity variable, which we remove using (
6.3
) and the estimates of Proposition
6.4
below. Notice as well that, in comparison to
[
FG23
, Theorem 8]
, we do not obtain an
L^{1}
-contraction for the equation due to the fact that even the difference of two solutions need not be
L^{1}
-integrable. For this reason, it is necessary to use Sobolev embeddings and the estimates of Proposition
5.5
to control spatial cutoff errors at infinity.

Assumption 6.3
.

Let
\Phi\in\textrm{C}([0,\infty))\cap\textrm{C}^{1}_{\textrm{loc}}((0,\infty))
satisfy
\Phi(0)=0
and
\Phi^{\prime}(\xi)>0
for every
\xi\in(0,\infty)
. Assume that

\Phi^{\prime}\;\;\textrm{is locally $\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}$-H\"{o}lder continuous on $(0,\infty)$,}

and that there exists
c\in(0,\infty)
such that, for every
M\in(0,\infty)
,

(6.3)

\sup_{0\leq\xi\leq M}|\frac{\Phi(\xi)}{\Phi^{\prime}(\xi)}|\leq cM.

Proposition 6.4
.

Let
T,\gamma\in(0,\infty)
, let
\Phi\in\textrm{C}([0,\infty))\cap\textrm{C}^{1}_{\textrm{loc}}((0,\infty))
satisfy Assumption
6.3
, let
g\in(L^{2}_{t,x})^{d}
, and let
\rho_{0}\in\textrm{Ent}_{\Phi,\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then, if
\rho
is a solution of (
6.1
) with control
g
, initial data
\rho_{0}
, and kinetic measure
p
, we have for every
M\in(\gamma,\infty)
that, for some
c\in(0,\infty)

\int_{R^{d}}\Psi_{\Phi,\gamma}(\rho(x,T))+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\Phi^{\prime}(\xi)}{\Phi(\xi)}p\leq c\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Psi_{\Phi,\gamma}(\rho_{0})+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|g|^{2}\Big),

which implies, in particular, that

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{\xi}p\leq c\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Psi_{\Phi,\gamma}(\rho_{0})+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|g|^{2}\Big).

Proof.

After introducing smooth cutoff functions in space and velocity, and considering smooth and bounded approximations of
\psi(\xi)=\Psi^{\prime}_{\Phi,\gamma}(\xi)
, it is possible to justify testing the equation with
\Psi^{\prime}_{\Phi,\gamma}
. For this, we obtain that

\int_{R^{d}}\Psi_{\Phi,\gamma}(\rho(x,T))+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\Phi^{\prime}(\xi)}{\Phi(\xi)}p=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Psi_{\Phi,\gamma}(\rho_{0})+2\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g\cdot\nabla\Phi^{\frac{1}{2}}(\rho).

Since the regularity of the kinetic measure proves that

\frac{\Phi^{\prime}(\xi)}{\Phi(\xi)}p\geq\delta_{0}(\xi-\rho)\frac{\Phi^{\prime}(\xi)^{2}}{\Phi(\xi)}|\nabla\rho|^{2}=\delta_{0}(\xi-\rho)4|\nabla\Phi^{\frac{1}{2}}(\rho)|^{2},

it follows from Hölder’s inequality and Young’s inequality that, for some
c\in(0,\infty)
,

\int_{R^{d}}\Psi_{\Phi,\gamma}(\rho(x,T))+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\Phi^{\prime}(\xi)}{\Phi(\xi)}p\leq c\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Psi_{\Phi,\gamma}(\rho_{0})+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|g|^{2}\Big).

The final claim then follows from the assumption that
\frac{\Phi(\xi)}{\Phi^{\prime}(\xi)}\leq c\xi
so that

\int_{R^{d}}\Psi_{\Phi,\gamma}(\rho(x,T))+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{\xi}p\leq c\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Psi_{\Phi,\gamma}(\rho_{0})+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|g|^{2}\Big),

which completes the proof. ∎

g
and then a smoothing and perturbation of the initial data
\rho(\cdot,0)\in\textrm{Ent}_{\Phi,\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
so that it becomes a strictly positive and compactly supported perturbation of the constant density
\gamma
. We then introduce a cutoff that “turns off” the control in regions where the solution approaches zero or infinity. This guarantees that the approximate solutions remain strictly bounded away from zero and infinity—and therefore that solutions remain in a region where
\Phi
is uniformly elliptic—and allows for the application of standard parabolic and elliptic regularity estimates to establish the regularity of the control in Definition
8.2
. Finally, we introduce a spatial cutoff in order to obtain a compactly supported control, in accordance with Definition
8.2
.

Step 1: The smooth approximation.
Extend
g
to
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}
by taking
g=0
on the complement of
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,T]
, for every
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
let
\kappa_{d+1}^{\varepsilon}
be a standard convolution kernel on
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
of scale
\varepsilon
, and for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
let
g_{n}
be defined by
g_{n}=(g*\kappa_{d+1}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{n}}})
. For every
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
let
\kappa^{\varepsilon}_{d}
be a smooth convolution kernel on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, let
\varphi
be a smooth cutoff of
\overline{B}_{1}
in
B_{2}
and for every
R\in(0,\infty)
let
\varphi_{R}(x)=\varphi(\nicefrac{{x}}{{R}})
, and for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
large enough so that
\nicefrac{{1}}{{n}}<\gamma
let
\rho_{0,n,R}
be the smooth, bounded, and strictly positive initial data defined by

\rho_{0,n}=\big(((\rho(\cdot,0))\vee\nicefrac{{1}}{{n}})\wedge n)*\kappa^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{n}}}\big)\varphi_{R}+(1-\varphi_{R})\gamma.

For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
let
\psi_{n}\colon[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,1]
be a smooth function satisfying

\psi_{n}=0\;\;\textrm{on}\;\;[0,\nicefrac{{1}}{{2n}}]\cup[2n,\infty)\;\;\textrm{and}\;\;\psi_{n}=1\;\;\textrm{on}\;\;[\nicefrac{{1}}{{n}},n].

A small adaptation of the proofs of Theorem
6.5
, Proposition
6.6
, and Theorem
7.3
proves that, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a unique solution
\rho_{n,R}\in\mathcal{C}
of the equation

\partial_{t}\rho_{n,R}=\Delta\Phi(\rho_{n,R})-\nabla\cdot(\Phi^{\frac{1}{2}}(\rho_{n,R})\psi_{n}(\rho_{n,R})g_{n,R})\;\;\textrm{in}\;\;\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,T)\;\;\textrm{with}\;\;\rho_{n,R}(\cdot,0)=\rho_{0,n,R},

in the sense of Definition
5.2
with control
\psi_{n}(\rho_{n,R})g_{n}
. We have using the comparison principle, the definition of
\psi_{n}
, the choice
\nicefrac{{1}}{{n}}<\gamma
, and the definition of
\rho_{0,n,R}
that

\nicefrac{{1}}{{2n}}\leq\rho_{n,R}\leq 2n\;\;\textrm{on}\;\;\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,T].

Since
\psi_{n}
,
g_{n}
, and
\rho_{0,n,R}
are smooth and bounded,
\Phi\in\textrm{C}^{3}_{\textrm{loc}}((0,\infty))
with
\Phi^{\prime}
strictly positive on
(0,\infty)
, and since
\rho_{n,R}
is bounded and bounded away from zero, interior Schauder estimates (see, for example, Ladyz̆henskaya, Solonnikov, and Ural’ceva
[
LSU67
]
) prove that
\rho_{n,R}\in\textrm{C}^{3,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,T])
. We view
\rho_{n,R}
as satisfying

\partial_{t}\rho_{n,R}=\Delta\Phi(\rho_{n,R}n)-\nabla\cdot(\Phi^{\frac{1}{2}}(\rho_{n,R})\tilde{g}_{n})\;\;\textrm{in}\;\;\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,T)\;\;\textrm{with}\;\;\rho_{n,R}(\cdot,0)=\rho_{0,n,R},

for the control
\tilde{g}_{n}=\psi_{n}(\rho_{n,R})g_{n}
. The positivity and boundedness of
\rho_{n,R}
and the positivity of
\Phi
on
(0,\infty)
prove that elements of
H^{1}_{\Phi(\rho_{n,R})}
can be identified with a unique element of
L^{2}_{t}H^{1}_{x}
with zero spatial mean on almost every time slice. A repetition of the argument in
[
FG23
, Proposition 37]
, which amounts essentially to the Riesz representation theorem in the space
H^{1}_{\Phi(\rho_{n,R})}
, then shows that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
sufficiently large and
R\in(0,\infty)
there exists
H_{n,R}\in L^{2}_{t}H^{1}_{x}
satisfying

(8.5)

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Phi(\rho_{n,R})|\nabla H_{n,R}|^{2}\leq\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\tilde{g}_{n}|^{2}\leq\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|g_{n}|^{2},

such that

\partial_{t}\rho_{n,R}=\Delta\Phi(\rho_{n,R})-\nabla\cdot(\Phi(\rho_{n,R})\nabla H_{n,R})\;\;\textrm{in}\;\;\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,T)\;\;\textrm{with}\;\;\rho_{n,R}(\cdot,0)=\rho_{0,n,R}.

Therefore, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
sufficiently large and
R\in(0,\infty)
,
H_{n,R}
is a solution to the uniformly elliptic equation

-\nabla\cdot(\Phi(\rho_{n,R})\nabla H_{n,R})=\partial_{t}\rho_{n,R}-\Delta\Phi(\rho_{n,R})\;\;\textrm{in}\;\;\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,T),

from which
\nicefrac{{1}}{{2n}}\leq\rho_{n,R}\leq 2n
, the local
\textrm{C}^{3}
-regularity and positivity of
\Phi
on
(0,\infty)
, the
\textrm{C}^{3,2}
-regularity of
\rho_{n,R}
, and interior elliptic regularity estimates (see, for example,
[
LSU67
]
) prove that
H_{n,R}\in\textrm{C}^{3,1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,T))
. At this stage, we have constructed a control
H_{n,R}
satisfying the smoothness criterion of Definition
8.2
. However, the
H_{n,R}
are not necessarily compactly supported in space. It is for this reason that, in the final step, for every
\tilde{R}\in[1,\infty)
, we let
\rho_{n,R,\tilde{R}}
be the solution

\partial_{t}\rho_{n,R,\tilde{R}}=\Delta\Phi(\rho_{n,R,\tilde{R}})-\nabla\cdot(\Phi(\rho_{n,R,\tilde{R}})\nabla(H_{n,R}\varphi_{\tilde{R}}))\;\;\textrm{in}\;\;\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,T)\;\;\textrm{with}\;\;\rho_{n,R,\tilde{R}}(\cdot,0)=\rho_{0,n,R}.

Since
H_{n,R}\in\textrm{C}^{3,1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,T))
we have using the smoothness of
\varphi
that
H_{n,R}\varphi_{\tilde{R}}\in\textrm{C}^{3,1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,T))
uniformly in
\tilde{R}\in[1,\infty)
. This completes the proof that
\rho_{n,R,\tilde{R}}\in\mathcal{S}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
sufficiently large,
R\in(0,\infty)
, and
\tilde{R}\in[1,\infty)
.

Step 2: The strong convergence of the smooth approximations.
We will first observe that, due to the fact that the
\nabla(H_{n,R}\varphi_{\tilde{R}})
are uniformly bounded in
(L^{\infty}_{t,x})^{d}\cap(L^{2}_{t,x})^{d}
, and since
\rho_{0,n}
is uniformly bounded away from zero and infinity, the comparison principle proves that the
\rho_{n,R,\tilde{R}}
are uniformly bounded away from zero and infinity in
\tilde{R}
, for every
n
and
R
. Furthermore, the
\rho_{n,R,\tilde{R}}
satisfy a uniform relative entropy estimate in
\tilde{R}
, for every
n
and
R
. More precisely, following Proposition
5.4
, we have that, for some
c\in(0,\infty)
independent of
n
,
R
, and
\tilde{R}
,

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Psi_{\Phi,\gamma}(\rho_{n,R,\tilde{R}})+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla\Phi^{\frac{1}{2}}(\rho_{n,R,\tilde{R}})|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq c\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Psi_{\Phi,\gamma}(\rho_{0,n,R})+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Phi(\rho_{n,R,\tilde{R}})|\nabla(H_{n,R}\varphi_{\tilde{R}})|^{2}\Big).

For the final term on the righthand side,

(8.6)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Phi(\rho_{n,R,\tilde{R}})|\nabla(H_{n,R}\varphi_{\tilde{R}})|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Phi(\rho_{n,R,\tilde{R}})|\nabla H_{n,R}|^{2}\varphi_{\tilde{R}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+2\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Phi(\rho_{n,R,\tilde{R}})|\nabla\varphi_{\tilde{R}}|^{2}H_{n,R}^{2}.

It follows from the dominated convergence theorem and (
8.5
) that the first term on the righthand side of (
8.6
) converges, as
\tilde{R}\rightarrow\infty
, to

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Phi(\rho_{n,R,\tilde{R}})|\nabla H_{n,R}|^{2}.

For the second term on the righthand side of (
8.6
), we have using the uniform boundedness of
\rho_{n,R,\tilde{R}}
in
\tilde{R}
, for every
n
and
R
, that, if
d=1
or
d=2
,

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Phi(\rho_{n,R,\tilde{R}})|\nabla\varphi_{\tilde{R}}|^{2}H_{n,R}^{2}\leq cR^{-2}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\textrm{Supp}(\nabla\varphi_{\tilde{R}})}H_{n,R}^{2},

which, since
H_{n,R}\in\textrm{C}^{3,1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,T))
, converges to zero if
d=1
and remains uniformly bounded if
d=2
. If
d\geq 3
, for
\nicefrac{{1}}{{2_{*}}}=\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}-\nicefrac{{1}}{{d}}
,

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Phi(\rho_{n,R,\tilde{R}})|\nabla\varphi_{\tilde{R}}|^{2}H_{n,R}^{2}\leq c\int_{0}^{T}\Big(\int_{\textrm{Supp}(\nabla\varphi_{\tilde{R}})}H_{n,R}^{2_{*}}\Big)^{\frac{2}{2_{*}}},

which, by the dominated convergence theorem, vanishes in the limit
\tilde{R}\rightarrow\infty
using the standard
H^{1}
Sobolev inequality and
\textrm{C}^{3,1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,T))
regularity of
H_{n,R}
. Similarly, the fact that the analogous relative entropy estimate for the
\rho_{n,R}
holds uniformly in
n
and
R
is immediate from Proposition
5.4
due to the fact that the
H_{n,R}
are uniformly bounded in
H^{1}_{\Phi(\rho_{n,R})}
, that uniformly in
n
and
R
we have estimate (
8.5
), and that, using the convexity of
\Psi_{\Phi,\gamma}
,

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N},R\in[1,\infty)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Psi_{\Phi,\gamma}(\rho_{0,n,R})\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Psi_{\Phi,\gamma}(\rho_{0}).

The above estimates and a repetition of the methods of Proposition
5.6
prove that, along a subsequence
\tilde{R}\rightarrow\infty
,

\lim_{\tilde{R}\rightarrow\infty}\rho_{n,R,\tilde{R}}=\rho_{n,R}\;\;\textrm{in}\;\;L^{1}([0,T];L^{1}_{\textrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})),

which, since the limit is unique, proves the convergence along the full sequence. And, similarly, along the full sequence
n,R\rightarrow\infty
,

\lim_{n,R\rightarrow\infty}\rho_{n,R}=\rho\;\;\textrm{in}\;\;L^{1}([0,T];L^{1}_{\textrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})).

It remains to prove the convergence in
\mathcal{C}
. Let
\psi\in\textrm{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and for every
\delta\in(0,1)
let
\kappa^{\delta}
be a standard
d
-dimensional convolution kernel on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
of scale
\delta
. It follows from the local
L^{1}
-boundedness of the
\rho_{n,R,\tilde{R}}
implied by the relative entropy estimate that the paths

(8.7)

t\rightarrow\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho_{n,R,\tilde{R}}(x,t)\psi(x)\;\;\textrm{are uniformly bounded in $n$, $R$, and $\tilde{R}$.}

Furthermore, following the methods of Proposition
5.5
locally in space, for
K\in(0,\infty)
satisfying
\textrm{Supp}(\psi)\subseteq B_{K}
, it follows from the equation that for every
s,t\in[0,T]
, for
\nicefrac{{1}}{{2_{*}}}=\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}-\nicefrac{{1}}{{d}}
if
d\geq 3
and
2_{*}=6
if
d=1
or
d=2
, for
c\in(0,\infty)
depending on
\psi
but independent of
n
,
R
, and
\tilde{R}
,

\displaystyle|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho_{n,R,\tilde{R}}(x,t)\psi(x)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho_{n,R,\tilde{R}}(x,s)\psi(x)|

\displaystyle\leq c|s-t|^{1-\frac{2}{2_{*}}}\|\Phi^{\frac{1}{2}}(\rho_{n,R,\tilde{R}})\|_{L^{\frac{2_{*}}{2}}_{t}L^{2}(B_{K})}\big(\|\nabla\Phi^{\frac{1}{2}}(\rho_{n,R,\tilde{R}})\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}}+\|\nabla(H_{n,R}\varphi_{\tilde{R}})\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}}\big).

By the Arzelà–Ascoli theorem, this implies that the paths (
8.7
) are tight and converge as
\tilde{R}\rightarrow\infty
in
\textrm{C}([0,T])
to the path

(8.8)

t\rightarrow\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho_{n,R}(x,t)\psi(x),

where the limit is uniquely identified by the strong Fréchet
L^{1}_{\textrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-convergence above. Since the limiting paths (
8.8
) are also uniformly bounded and satisfy using (
8.5
) that

\displaystyle|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho_{n,R,\tilde{R}}(x,t)\psi(x)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho_{n,R}(x,s)\psi(x,s)|

\displaystyle\leq c|s-t|^{1-\frac{2}{2_{*}}}\|\Phi^{\frac{1}{2}}(\rho_{n,R})\|_{L^{\frac{2_{*}}{2}}_{t}L^{2}(B_{K})}\big(\|\nabla\Phi^{\frac{1}{2}}(\rho_{n,R})\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}}+\|g\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}}\big),

we again have using the Arzelà–Ascoli theorem that these paths are relatively compact
\textrm{C}([0,T])
, for which the strong Fréchet
L^{1}_{\textrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-convergence proves the uniqueness of the limit and therefore the convergence of the paths (
8.8
) as
n,R\rightarrow\infty
in
\textrm{C}([0,T])
to the path

t\rightarrow\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(x,t)\psi(x).

The density of compactly supported smooth functions in
\textrm{C}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and the triangle inequality then complete the proof that

\lim_{n,R\rightarrow\infty}\Big(\lim_{\tilde{R}\rightarrow\infty}\rho_{n,R,\tilde{R}}\Big)=\rho\;\;\textrm{in}\;\;\mathcal{C}.

Step 3: Convergence of the rate functions.
It remains only to show that, along a subsequence
n,R,\tilde{R}\rightarrow\infty
, we have that

I(\rho_{n,R,\tilde{R}})\rightarrow I(\rho).

We first pass to the limit
\tilde{R}\rightarrow\infty
, for which it is a consequence of estimate (
8.5
), the analogous interpolation estimates of Proposition
5.5
, and the strong Fréchet
L^{1}_{\textrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-convergence of
\rho_{n,R,\tilde{R}}
to
\rho_{n,R}
that

\lim_{\tilde{R}\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Phi(\rho_{n,R,\tilde{R}})|\nabla(H_{n,R}\varphi_{\tilde{R}})|^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Phi(\rho_{n,R})|\nabla H_{n,R}|^{2}.

It remains to pass to the limit
n,R\rightarrow\infty
. For this, we know that

(8.9)

I(\rho_{n,R})=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Phi(\rho_{n})|\nabla H_{n,R}|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\psi_{n}(\rho_{n,R})g_{n}|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|g\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{t,x}}.

After passing to a subsequence
n,R\rightarrow\infty
, for some
\tilde{g}\in(L^{2}_{t,x})^{d}
,

\Phi^{\frac{1}{2}}(\rho_{n,R})\nabla H_{n,R}\rightharpoonup\tilde{g}\;\;\textrm{weakly in}\;\;(L^{2}_{t,x})^{d},

from which it follows from (
8.9
) and the weak lower semicontinuity of the Sobolev norm that
\|\tilde{g}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|g\|_{L^{2}}
. Since it follows from Theorem
6.5
and Theorem
7.3
that
\rho
solves

\partial_{t}\rho=\Delta\Phi(\rho)-\nabla\cdot(\Phi^{\frac{1}{2}}(\rho)\tilde{g})\;\;\textrm{in}\;\;\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,\infty)\;\;\textrm{with}\;\;\rho(\cdot,0)=\rho_{0},

we have by definition of the rate function that
\|\tilde{g}\|_{L^{2}}\geq\|g\|_{L^{2}}
and therefore that
\|\tilde{g}\|_{L^{2}}=\|g\|_{L^{2}}
. Due to the fact that the minimizer is unique, this proves that
\tilde{g}=g
and that, along a subsequence,

\frac{1}{2}\|g\|_{L^{2}}=\frac{1}{2}\|\tilde{g}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\liminf_{n,R\rightarrow\infty}I(\rho_{n,R})\leq\limsup_{n,R\rightarrow\infty}I(\rho_{n,R})\leq\frac{1}{2}\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|g_{n}\|_{L^{2}}=\|g\|_{L^{2}},

and therefore, along a subsequence,

\lim_{n,R\rightarrow\infty}I(\rho_{n,R})=\frac{1}{2}\|g\|^{2}_{L^{2}}=I(\rho),

which completes the proof that
I=I^{\textrm{lo}}
, which completes the proof.∎

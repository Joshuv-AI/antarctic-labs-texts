An Expanding Self-Similar Vortex Configuration for the 2 2 D Euler Equations
Juan Dávila Manuel del Pino Monica Musso Shrish Parmeshwar

Abstract
This paper addresses the long-time dynamics of solutions to the 2 ​ D 2D incompressible Euler equations. We construct solutions with continuous vorticity ω ε ​ ( x , t ) \omega_{\varepsilon}(x,t) concentrated around points ξ j ​ ( t ) \xi_{j}(t) that converge to a sum of Dirac delta masses as ε → 0 \varepsilon\to 0 . These solutions are associated with the Kirchhoff-Routh point-vortex system, and the points ξ j ​ ( t ) \xi_{j}(t) follow an expanding self similar trajectory of spirals, with the support of the vorticities contained in balls of radius 3 ​ ε 3\varepsilon around each ξ j \xi_{j} .

Introduction

We consider the Euler equations for an incompressible, inviscid fluid in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Written in terms of the vorticity
\omega(x,t)
and stream function
\Psi(x,t)
, this system of equations is given by

\left\{\begin{aligned} \omega_{t}+\nabla^{\perp}_{x}\Psi\cdot\nabla_{x}\omega&=0\ \ \ \ &&\text{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{2}\times(0,T),\\
\Psi=(-\Delta_{x})^{-1}&\omega\ \ &&\text{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{2}\times(0,T),\\
\omega(\cdot,0)=&\mathring{\omega}&&\text{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{2}.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

The Poisson equation in (
1.1
) above implies that for suitable
\omega
,
\Psi
is given by

\Psi(x,t)=(-\Delta_{x})^{-1}\omega(x,t)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\log\frac{1}{|x-y|}\omega(y,t)\,dy.\ \

The fluid velocity field
v=\nabla^{\perp}\Psi
is then recovered by the Biot-Savart Law:

v(x,t)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{(y-x)^{\perp}}{|y-x|^{2}}\omega(y,t)\,dy\ ,

where
z^{\perp}=(z_{2},-z_{1})
.

The Cauchy problem for (
1.1
) admits a global well-posedness theory for weak solutions arising from initial data in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
; see classical work by Wolibner
[
31
]
and Yudovich
[
21
]
. In addition, any extra regularity for the initial data
\mathring{\omega}
is propagated and also present for the solution
\omega
. See for instance Chapter 8 in Majda-Bertozzi’s book
[
22
]
.

Carrying on the work started in
[
10
]
, this paper is concerned with constructing solutions to (
1.1
) that exhibit nontrivial dynamics as
t\to\infty
. We are interested in solutions with a finite number of regions with highly
concentrated
vorticity, and in particular, of the form

\omega_{\varepsilon}(x,t)=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{m_{j}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\Big[W\left(\frac{x-\xi_{j}(t)}{\varepsilon}\right)+o(1)\Big],

(1.2)

around points
\xi_{1}(t),\ldots,\xi_{N}(t)
, where
W(y)
is a certain, chosen radially symmetric profile with unit mass,
o(1)\to 0
as
\varepsilon\to 0
and
m_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
are the circulations (or masses) of the vortices. Consequently these solutions will satisfy

\omega_{\varepsilon}(x,t)\rightharpoonup\omega_{S}(x,t)=\sum_{j=1}^{N}{m_{j}}\delta_{0}({x-\xi_{j}(t)})\quad\hbox{as }\varepsilon\to 0,

(1.3)

where
\delta_{0}(y)
is a Dirac mass at
0
.
Formally the points
\xi_{j}(t)
should solve the Kirchhoff-Routh point-vortex ODE system

\dot{\xi}_{j}(t)=-\sum_{\ell\not=j}\frac{m_{\ell}}{2\pi}\frac{(\xi_{j}(t)-\xi_{\ell}(t))^{\perp}}{|\xi_{j}(t)-\xi_{\ell}(t)|^{2}},\quad j=1,\ldots,N.

(1.4)

Using complex variables,
z_{j}=\xi_{j}^{1}+i\xi_{j}^{2}
, we can rewrite (
1.4
) as

\overline{\frac{dz_{j}}{dt}}=\sum_{\ell\neq j}\frac{m_{\ell}}{2\pi i}\frac{1}{z_{j}-z_{l}},\quad j=1,\dots,N.

(1.5)

In
[
11
]
the following was proven:
Given a finite time
T>0
and a solution
\left(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{N}\right)
to System (
1.4
) with no collisions in
[0,T]
, namely with

\inf_{t\in[0,T],\,i\neq j}|\xi_{i}(t)-\xi_{j}(t)|>0,

there exists a solution of (
1.1
) with the precise form (
1.2
) and the specific choice

W(y)=\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{1}{(1+|y|^{2})^{2}}
.

It was also shown in
[
23
]
previously that initial conditions supported on
\varepsilon
-disks concentrated around the points
\xi_{j}(0)
as bumps with masses
m_{j}
produced solutions satisfied the convergence to a sum of delta masses, (
1.3
). However, this work was without the asymptotic description (
1.2
) near the vortices.

Both of these works rely crucially on the finiteness in time
T>0
in order to get uniform estimates in
[0,T]
as
\varepsilon\to 0
. It is not clear that, in general, the solutions in
[
23
,
11
]
keep the form (
1.2
) as time evolves. Such a long time result would be desirable, as trajectories of system (
1.4
) generically do not collide, see Chapter 4 in
[
24
]
.

This paper and similar work carried out in
[
10
]
deals with the construction of solutions to
the Euler equations (
1.1
) that persist (
1.2
) and satisfy the convergence (
1.3
)
at all times
.

The problem of understanding long-time behaviours and patterns for solutions of (
1.1
) still
has many open problems, and has been the subject of many conjectures. See for instance
[
13
]
and its references.

Clearly, in the analysis of long-time dynamics, steady solutions such as stationary solutions, travelling waves, and rotating solutions are of fundamental. See for instance
[
2
,
17
,
29
,
7
]
and references therein. The problem of finding such solutions is typically reduced to solving semilinear elliptic equations.

Part of why steady solutions are so fundamental, is that they offer hope in finding time-dependent configurations that satisfy (
1.2
) for all time, in the form of solutions that closely resemble superpositions of steady ones. Indeed, this is the broad strategy used by the authors in
[
10
]
, and in this work. However, in general, such a strategy has its own challenges, arising due to the emergence of coupling error terms at main order, and our limited understanding of the long-term behavior of perturbations to steady solutions.

In this paper, we will consider a configuration of three point vortices
\xi_{*j}
,
j=1,2,3
, whose trajectories are three similar spirals meaning that the whole configuration separates as a rotating and expanding triangle. In complex form the trajectories are given, for
j=1,2,3
by

\displaystyle\xi_{*j}^{1}(t)+i\xi_{*j}^{2}(t)=z_{*j}(t)=z_{*j}(0)\left(1+\frac{t}{\tau}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}+i\Lambda\tau},\quad j=1,2,3,

(1.6)

where
\tau
and
\Lambda
are positive constants related to the initial data. Defining, for

,

\displaystyle L_{ij}(t)\coloneqq\left|z_{*i}(t)-z_{*j}(t)\right|=\left|\xi_{*i}(t)-\xi_{*j}(t)\right|=L_{ij}(0)\left(1+\frac{t}{\tau}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

(1.7)

we also require

\displaystyle m_{1}+m_{2}+m_{3}>0,\quad m_{1},m_{2}>0,\quad m_{3}<0,

(1.8)

\displaystyle m_{1}m_{2}+m_{2}m_{3}+m_{1}m_{3}=0,

(1.9)

\displaystyle m_{1}m_{2}L_{12}(t)^{2}+m_{2}m_{3}L_{23}(t)^{2}+m_{1}m_{3}L_{13}(t)^{2}=0,

(1.10)

\displaystyle L_{23}(0)\neq L_{13}(0),

(1.11)

where the
m_{j}
are the masses of the vortices
\xi_{*j}
. For a full description of the trajectories of the corresponding point vortex solution to Kirchhoff-Routh system (
1.4
), see
[
3
,
26
,
4
]
. We also provide more detail in Section

.

We now describe the steady state that we will use as a model for a single vortex in this paper. Let
\Gamma(x)
be the radial classical solution of the problem

\displaystyle\Delta_{x}\Gamma+\Gamma^{\gamma}_{+}=0\ \text{on}\ \mathbb{R}^{2},\quad\{\Gamma>0\}=B_{1}

(1.12)

Let
M
be the mass, or circulation, of this vortex, that is

\displaystyle M\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Gamma^{\gamma}_{+}dx.

(1.13)

The main result of this paper is the construction of an initial vorticity of three compactly supported,
\varepsilon
-concentrated functions that generate a time evolved vorticity that remains localised around three points
\xi_{*j}(t)
,

, at all times

. Suppose
\left(m_{1},m_{2},m_{3}\right)
are three masses for a solution of the form (

) to (

.

Let
\gamma\geq 19
. There exists a constant
C>0
such that for all
\varepsilon>0
small enough, and all
T_{0}>0
large enough, there exists a solution to (
1.1
),
(\omega_{\varepsilon},\Psi_{\varepsilon})
, on the whole interval
[T_{0},\infty)
, with

\omega_{\varepsilon}(x,t)=\varepsilon^{-2}\sum_{j=1}^{3}\frac{m_{j}}{M}\left(\Gamma\left(\frac{x-\xi_{j}(t)}{\varepsilon}\right)\right)^{\gamma}_{+}+\varepsilon^{-2}\phi_{\varepsilon}(x,t),

(1.14)

where we have the decomposition of the trajectories
\xi_{j}=\xi_{*j}+\tilde{\xi}_{j}
with
\xi_{*j}
defined in complex variables in (

), and

functions

, that satisfy, for all

\displaystyle|t^{\frac{3}{2}}\tilde{\xi}_{j}\left(t\right)|+|t^{\frac{5}{2}}\dot{\tilde{\xi}}_{j}\left(t\right)|

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{4-\sigma},

for some small

.

The function

|\phi_{\varepsilon}(x,t)|\leq\frac{C\varepsilon^{1-\sigma}}{t^{\frac{1}{2}-\sigma}},\quad\|\phi_{\varepsilon}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)}\leq\frac{C\varepsilon^{2}}{t},\quad\text{for all }t>T_{0},

for any

small. The support of

satisfies

{\mbox{supp}}\,\phi_{\varepsilon}(x,t)\subseteq\bigcup_{j=1}^{3}B_{3\varepsilon}\left(\xi_{*j}\right),\quad\text{for all }t>T_{0}.

The functions

,

,

and has the expected properties in the whole interval

. As in
[

By
T_{0}>0
large enough, in particular, we require

, for all

,
i\neq j
with

, defined in (

Our result is an example of a solution to (

) where one has precise information on the long-time behaviour, a type of result that, as mentioned above, remains rare for the

Euler equations. Whilst we construct initial conditions that allow us to exhibit the long time behavior claimed in Theorem

Euler equations in
[

]
,
[

]
: as

Significant confirmations of this conjecture have emerged near specific steady states. Important stability results for shear flows have been found in
[

,

,

. Stability results for fast-decay, radial steady states have been found

Well-known non-smooth standard flows are vortex patches. These are solutions with discontinuous but bounded and integrable vorticities. They also enjoy additional properties when compared to the more regular desingularized solutions we construct in this work. For vortex patches, there are several long-time results. In
[
32
]
, a global solution to (
1.1
) is found, consisting of vortex patches approximating the expanding self similar solution to (
1.4
) with

given by (
1.6
) that we also consider in this paper. Thus, one may naturally compare the patch solutions constructed in
[
32
]
and the solutions constructed in the present work. Apart from a difference in regularity, it is important to note that in
[
32
]
, each patch may grow in size at a rate

, compensated by the fact that they separate from each other at a rate
t^{\frac{1}{2}}
. This seems crucial in the analysis. See also
[
27
]
. Our solutions also separate at a rate of

, however as noted in Theorem
1.1
, we find a uniform-in-time bound on the supports around the centre of each region of concentrated vorticity, a fundamentally qualitative difference to
[
32
]
.

In addition to the above discussion, existence of co-rotating and counter-rotating pair vortex patches was shown in
[
17
]
. Logarithmic spiraling solutions were also recently constructed in
[
20
]
. For other related results, we refer to
[

]
and to the results cited within. We finally mention the interesting recent paper

[

]
where solutions in the form of two vortex pairs leapfrogging each other are constructed with the use of KAM theory.

2
Scheme of the proof

The strategy we adopt to construct the solution predicted by Theorem
1.1
is to first produce a solution to the Euler equations (
1.1
) on a finite and arbitrarily large time interval
[T_{0},T]
, for
T\gg T_{0}
. This solution is built as a perturbation of the sum of three
\varepsilon
-concentrated vortices traveling along the similar spirals discussed in the previous section. The perturbation will be small and decay in time. To conclude our argument we apply the Ascoli-Arzelá Theorem for a sequence of times
(T_{n})_{n}
, with
T_{n}\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
and show that the limit function gives the desired solution in the whole interval
[T_{0},\infty)
. We do this in Section
6
.

The main step in our proof is the construction of the solution to the Euler equations (
1.1
) on
[T_{0},T]
as described above.

To do this we begin as mentioned above, by perturbing an ansatz of three
\varepsilon
-concentrated power nonlinearity vortices defined in (
1.12
) and Section
2.1
. To construct a good first approximation therefore requires a good understanding of the linear operator obtained by linearising around the power nonlinearity vortices. This information is the content of Appendix
A
. The explanation of this procedure is the content of Section
2.3
, and is carried out in detail in
4
. We note that at the level of the first approximation we must deal with the fact that the point-vortex solutions to (
1.4
) given by (
1.6
) only separate at
t^{\frac{1}{2}}
as opposed to the rate of
t
which we saw in
[
10
]
. This leads to less decay in time when constructing the first approximation, and requires a precise tracking of the modal structure of the approximation in order to guarantee enough time decay to perform the bootstrapping argument that lets us construct solutions on
\left[T_{0},T\right]
. For example, see Remark
4.6
.

In addition, due to this weaker separation rate, it is crucial that we do not lose any time decay when inverting the linearised operator around the solutions (
1.6
), like we do for the analogous linearised operator in
[
10
]
, and thus it is necessary to carry out a precise analysis into the longtime asymptotics for the linearisation around the solutions (
1.6
), performed in Section
3
.

We then construct solutions to (
1.1
) on
\left[T_{0},T\right]
in a manner closely related to the fixed point argument used in
[
11
]
. The explanation of this is the content of Section
2.4
and is carried out in Section
5
.

In Section

\displaystyle\Gamma(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\nu(|x|)&\text{if }|x|\leq 1\\

Recalling that

\nu^{\prime}(1)<0

\Gamma

2.2
Vortex trajectories

From
[
3
,
4
]
, and as mentioned in the discussion (
1.6
)–(
1.11
), we know that there is a solution
\left(\xi_{*1}(t),\xi_{*2}(t),\xi_{*3}(t)\right)
to the system (
1.4
) for

, given by, in complex form,

\displaystyle z_{*j}(t)=z_{*j}(0)\left(1+\frac{t}{\tau}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}+i\Lambda\tau},

so that

\displaystyle z_{*j}(t)=\xi_{*j}^{1}(t)+i\xi_{*j}^{2}(t),

(2.6)

where the
\xi_{*j}^{i}(t)
are the vector components of
\xi_{*j}(t)
.

Following the construction in
[
3
]
, the mass constraints (
1.8
), the harmonic mean constraint (
1.9
), and the angular momentum constraint (
1.10
) can be used to show that the lengths
L_{12}(t),L_{23}(t),L_{13}(t)
defined in (
1.7
) are all proportional to one another. Let

be the area of the triangle formed at time

by the vortices. If we suppose that
L_{23}(t)>L_{12}(t)>L_{13}(t)
, and that the triangle the point vortices form has positive orientation, we can conclude the right hand side of the ODEs

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left(L_{12}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\pi}m_{3}A(t)\left(\frac{1}{L_{23}^{2}}-\frac{1}{L_{13}^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left(L_{23}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\pi}m_{1}A(t)\left(\frac{1}{L_{13}^{2}}-\frac{1}{L_{12}^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left(L_{13}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\pi}m_{2}A(t)\left(\frac{1}{L_{12}^{2}}-\frac{1}{L_{23}^{2}}\right)

are all positive constants, once again using the mass constraints (
1.8
), and the fact that

is proportional to the square of all the side lengths. The constant
\tau
can then be calculated by integrating any three of the above ODEs:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tau}=\frac{2}{\pi}m_{3}\frac{A(0)}{L_{12}^{2}(0)}\left(\frac{1}{L_{23}^{2}(0)}-\frac{1}{L_{13}^{2}(0)}\right).

From there, we can show that

\displaystyle\Lambda=\frac{\left(m_{1}+m_{2}\right)^{3}+m_{1}^{3}\left(1+\Pi^{-1}\right)+m_{2}^{3}\left(1+\Pi\right)}{4\pi\left(m_{1}+m_{2}\right)^{2}L_{12}(0)^{2}},

(2.7)

where

\displaystyle\Pi=\frac{m_{1}}{m_{2}}\frac{L_{13}(0)^{2}}{L_{23}(0)^{2}},

and finally we use the length constraint (
1.11
) to ensure that the initial triangle formed by the point vortices is not an equilateral, which then automatically excludes the case of rigid motion in the form of rotation with no expansion.

Approximate solution on

By (

In Section

(\Psi_{*}[\xi],\omega_{*}[\xi])

satisfies, for some constant

Also,

|E_{2}\left[\Psi_{*}[\xi],\omega_{*}[\xi]\right]|\leq C\frac{\varepsilon^{6}}{(1+t)^{4}}.

A crucial step in the construction of the improved approximation is to recognize the very special structure of the principal parts of the linear terms in
E_{1}\left[\Psi_{0}[\xi]+\Psi,\omega_{0}[\xi]+\omega\right]
. This structure manifests on the right hand side of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
in the form of a linear elliptic problem, in coordinates

, as

\displaystyle\nabla^{\perp}_{y}\left(\Gamma(y)\right)\cdot\nabla_{y}\left(\mathscr{L}\left(\psi\right)\right)=G,

where

is the linearized operator around the power nonlinearity vortex, for some right hand side

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}(r)}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\left(\mathscr{L}\left(\psi\right)\right)=G.

Thus, if we can ensure the right hand side has no mode
0

This allows us to improve the initial approximation
(\Psi_{0}[\xi],\omega_{0}[\xi])
by solving linear elliptic problems. This can be done under the assumption that

satisfies (

so that

has support contained in

, and satisfies the bound

\displaystyle\Psi_{0}-\varepsilon\dot{\xi}\cdot y^{\perp}+\kappa_{j}\psi_{j}+\kappa_{j}\psi_{*j}+\psi^{out}+\psi^{out}_{*}

(5.13)

\displaystyle+\gamma\left(\gamma-1\right)\left(\gamma-2\right)\Gamma^{\gamma-3}_{+}\left(y\right)\mathcal{R}^{*}_{j3}\left(y,\xi\right).

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}_{\lambda}^{out}\left(\tilde{\psi},\psi^{out}_{*},\tilde{\xi}\right)

\displaystyle=\Delta_{x}\psi_{*}^{out}+\lambda\sum_{j=1}^{3}\kappa_{j}\left(\psi_{*j}\Delta_{x}\eta_{j}+2\nabla_{x}\eta_{j}\cdot\nabla_{x}\psi_{*j}\right)+\lambda E_{2}^{out}\left(\psi^{in},\psi^{out};\xi\right).

Thus a solution to the system (

\displaystyle\phi_{*j}(\mathrel{\;\cdot\;},T)=0\ \ \text{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{2},

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}_{\lambda}^{out}\left(\tilde{\psi},\psi^{out}_{*},\tilde{\xi}\right)

To construct a solution to (

Remark 5.1
.

Note that unlike

10
]

5.2
Weighted

Function Space

When proving a priori estimates for some linearised operators, it will be convenient to have shorthand for several quantities we define in this section. Firstly, let
\eta_{0}(r)
once again be the smooth cutoff that is identically
1
for
r\leq 1
and identically
0
for
r\geq 2
. Define the interior cutoff,
\eta_{\mathfrak{i}}
, by

\displaystyle\eta_{\mathfrak{i}}\left(y,t\right)=1-\eta_{0}\left(\left(\frac{t}{\varepsilon}\right)^{\frac{\gamma}{4}}U(y)\right),

(5.22)

Then
\eta_{\mathfrak{i}}
is supported on the region

\displaystyle U(y)\geq 2\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{t}\right)^{\frac{\gamma}{4}}\iff\Gamma(y)\geq 2^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}.

(5.23)

For future convenience we also define the set

\displaystyle\mathfrak{W}=\left\{y:U(y)\geq\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{t}\right)^{\frac{\gamma}{4}}\right\}.

(5.24)

We next define
\eta_{1}(r)
to be the smooth cutoff function that is identically
1
for
r\leq 3
and identically
0
for
r\geq 4
. We let the exterior cutoff,
\eta_{\mathfrak{e}}
, be defined by

\displaystyle\eta_{\mathfrak{e}}\left(y,t\right)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\eta_{1}\left(\left(\frac{t}{\varepsilon}\right)^{\frac{\gamma}{4}}U(y)\right)&\text{if }y\in B_{1}(0)\\
\eta_{0}\left(\frac{\left|y\right|}{4}\right)&\text{if }y\in B_{1}(0)^{\mathsf{c}}\end{array}\right..

We note the two functions that are glued to construct
\eta_{\mathfrak{e}}
match at the boundary of their domains of definition as they are both identically
1
on a region around said boundary. We note that
\eta_{\mathfrak{e}}
is supported on the region

\displaystyle U(y)\leq 4\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{t}\right)^{\frac{\gamma}{4}}.

(5.27)

It is clear that

\displaystyle 1\leq\eta_{\mathfrak{i}}+\eta_{\mathfrak{e}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\ \ \mathrm{on}\ B_{4}\left(0\right),

(5.28)

\displaystyle\left|\grad_{y}\eta_{\mathfrak{i}}\right|+\left|\grad_{y}\eta_{\mathfrak{e}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\frac{t}{\varepsilon}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}},

some absolute constant
C>0
. Moreover, using the definitions (
5.22
) and (
5.2
), as well as the bounds (
2.11
)–(
2.12
), we have that
\supp{\partial_{t}\eta_{\mathfrak{i}}}\cup\supp{\nabla_{y}\eta_{\mathfrak{i}}}\subset\supp{\eta_{\mathfrak{e}}}
, and that on the supports of

and
\nabla_{y}\eta_{\mathfrak{i}}
,

\displaystyle\eta_{\mathfrak{i}}\leq\eta_{\mathfrak{e}},\quad\left|\partial_{t}\eta_{\mathfrak{i}}\right|\leq\frac{C\eta_{\mathfrak{e}}}{t},\quad\left|\nabla_{y}\eta_{\mathfrak{i}}\right|\leq C\eta_{\mathfrak{e}}.

(5.29)

With the definitions (
5.22
) and (
5.2
) in hand we define, for

, the space
\mathscr{Y}_{r}
as all functions on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with

\displaystyle\left\|\Phi\right\|_{\mathscr{Y}_{r}}\coloneqq\|\left(\gamma\Gamma^{\gamma-1}_{+}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}-1}\eta_{\mathfrak{i}}\Phi\|_{L^{r}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)}+\left(\frac{t}{\varepsilon}\right)^{\left(\frac{\gamma-1}{4}\right)\left(1-\frac{1}{r}\right)}\left\|\eta_{\mathfrak{e}}\Phi\right\|_{L^{r}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)}<\infty.

(5.30)

We have that if

, then by (

\displaystyle\left\|\Phi\right\|_{L^{r}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)}\leq C\left\|\Phi\right\|_{\mathscr{Y}_{r}}.

(5.31)

Now we record some inequalities related to the Poisson equation

that we will need later on.

5.3

We would like to prove effective bounds on

on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
to

\displaystyle\left[F\right]_{\beta}\left(y\right)\coloneqq\sup_{y_{1},y_{2}\in B_{1}\left(y\right)}\frac{\left|F\left(y_{1}\right)-F\left(y_{2}\right)\right|}{\left|y_{1}-y_{2}\right|^{\beta}}.

\displaystyle\bar{\phi}_{0}

Here we record some useful results about the linearised operator around
\Gamma

Lemma A.1
.

Suppose
\varphi\in L^{\infty}
is a solution to

\displaystyle\Delta\varphi+\gamma\Gamma^{\gamma-1}_{+}\varphi=\kappa\gamma\Gamma^{\gamma-1}_{+}\varphi

\mathbb{R}^{2}

\kappa\in[0,1)

\kappa=0
, then
\varphi
lives in the span of
\partial_{1}\Gamma
and
\partial_{2}\Gamma
. If
\kappa>0
, then
\varphi\equiv 0
.

The proof of this result (with minor modifications) can be found in
[
9
]
.

Next, we look at the linear problem given by

\displaystyle\Delta_{y}\psi+\gamma\Gamma^{\gamma-1}_{+}\psi=\gamma\Gamma^{\gamma-1}_{+}g,

(A.1)

where
g
is a
C^{1}
function. We assume that
g
has no mode
0
terms, and move to complex coordinates

, so that

\displaystyle\psi\left(r,\theta\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\uppsi_{k}\left(r\right)e^{ik\theta},

(A.2)

\displaystyle g\left(r,\theta\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{g}_{k}\left(r\right)e^{ik\theta}.

(A.3)

No mode

, and we accordingly impose that
\uppsi_{0}\equiv 0
. Then (

) splits into infinitely many ODEs given by

(A.4)

For

, one can find a positive solution
\zeta_{k}
to the equation
\mathscr{L}_{k}\left[\zeta_{k}\right]=0
, that behaves like

\displaystyle\zeta_{k}=r^{\left|k\right|}\left(1+\mathcal{O}{\left(r^{2}\right)}\right),\ r\to 0,

For

), decays like

as

\left|k\right|\geq 2

\displaystyle\zeta_{k}=r^{\left|k\right|}\left(1+\mathcal{O}{\left(r^{-2\left|k\right|}\right)}\right),\ r\to\infty,

(A.6)

Then, in the case that

Dynamical Irreducibility of Certain Families of Polynomials over Finite Fields
Tori Day, Rebecca DeLand, Jamie Juul, Cigole Thomas, Bianca Thompson, Bella Tobin

Abstract
. We determine necessary and sufficient conditions for unicritical polynomials to be dynamically irreducible over finite fields. This result extends the results of Boston-Jones and Hamblen-Jones-Madhu regarding the dynamical irreducibility of particular families of unicritical polynomials. We also investigate dynamical irreducibility conditions for cubic and shifted linearized polynomials.



1.
Introduction

Let
K
be a field and
f(x)\in K[x]
a polynomial with degree at least
2
. We will consider the reducibility of

f^{n}(x)\vcentcolon=(\underbrace{f\circ f\circ\ldots\circ f}_{n\ \text{times}})(x).

We say a polynomial
f
is
dynamically irreducible
(or
stable
) if

is irreducible for all positive integers

over
K
. Similarly,

is
eventually dynamically irreducible
(or
eventually stable
) if there exists positive integers

such that

and
g_{i}(f^{n})
is irreducible for all non-negative integers
n
. In other words,

is eventually dynamically irreducible if the number of irreducible factors of
f^{n}
stabilizes. For

we call
(f,a)
a
dynamically irreducible pair
if
f^{n}(x)-a
is irreducible for all positive integers
n
. Note that the dynamical irreducibility of
f
is equivalent to the dynamical irreducibility of the pair
(f,0)
. See
[
3
, Section 19]
for a brief overview of dynamical irreducibility.

One motivation for studying eventual stability comes from arboreal Galois representations. For a rational map

and an element

, the
backward orbit
of

under

\mathcal{O}_{f}^{-}(a)=\{b\in\bar{K}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}f^{n}(b)=a\text{ for some }n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\},

has a natural structure as a regular
\deg f
-ary rooted tree, let
T
denote this tree. The absolute Galois group of
K
acts on the backward orbit in a way that preserves the tree structure, inducing a group homomorphism

\rho\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathrm{Gal}(\bar{K}/K)\rightarrow\Aut(T),

where
\mathrm{Gal}(\bar{K}/K)
denotes the absolute Galois group of
K
and
\Aut(T)
denotes the automorphism group of
T

. See
[
4
]
,
[
18
]
,
[
17
]
for a detailed discussion. The image of this representation acts transitively on the

) for all

if and only if the pair

is dynamically irreducible.

Further, if

is a number field, the factorization type of a polynomial in residue fields of

provides information about the action of Frobenius elements on the roots of the polynomial. Let
K
be a number field and
f
a polynomial of degree

over

. Denote by

the splitting field of

over

, of

, is finite. The image of the Frobenius class,

for each

, let

denote the reduced polynomial mod

is separable and

, then the cycle structure of the action of

is given by the factorization type of

in

. Therefore, understanding the factorization of

In

Theorem 1

Let

\left\{-\frac{f(\gamma)}{a}\right\}\bigcup\left\{\frac{f^{n}(\gamma)}{a}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>1}\right\}.

Let

2.
Background and Preliminaries

Throughout the paper we will consider
f(x)
a polynomial over a field
K
. We will primarily focus on polynomials over finite fields, where we will write
K=\mathbb{F}_{q}
where
q=p^{s}
for some prime
p
and positive integer
s
.

Conjugation preserves many dynamical properties of a map, including, as we will see in the following lemma, dynamical irreducibility in some sense. We say a polynomial
f\in K[x]
is
conjugate
to
g\in\overline{K}[x]
if there exists some
\varphi(x)=ax+b\in\mathrm{PGL}_{2}(\overline{K})
such that
g=f^{\varphi}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\varphi\circ f\circ\varphi^{-1}.
This conjugation commutes with iteration, so
(f^{\varphi})^{n}=(f^{n})^{\varphi}
, and thus preserves the dynamics of corresponding points.

Lemma 3
.

Let
f(x)\in K[x]
and
\varphi(x)=cx+\alpha\in\mathrm{PGL}_{2}(K)
. Define
g(x)=f^{\varphi}(x)
. Then,
f^{n}(x)
is irreducible over
K
if and only if
g^{n}(x)-\alpha
is irreducible over
K
. In particular,
f
is dynamically irreducible over
K
if and only if the pair
(g,\alpha)
is dynamically irreducible.

Proof.

Since
g^{n}(x)=(f^{\varphi})^{n}(x)=(f^{n})^{\varphi}(x)
, then
g^{n}(x)-\alpha=cf^{n}\left(\frac{x-\alpha}{c}\right)
. Since they have the same splitting field, the polynomial
f^{n}(x)
is irreducible if and only if
f^{n}\left(\frac{x-\alpha}{c}\right)
is irreducible, which is clearly irreducible if and only if
g^{n}(x)-\alpha
is irreducible.
∎

We demonstrate the utility of Lemma
3
in the quadratic case. This technique will be especially useful in the proof of Theorem
1
, where we will conjugate unicritical polynomials to polynomials with critical point
0
.

Example 4
.

For
f(x)=ax^{2}+bx+c
, define
h(x)=f(x-\frac{b}{2a})+\frac{b}{2a}=ax^{2}-\frac{b^{2}}{4a}+c+\frac{b}{2a}
. To check whether
f^{n}(x)
is irreducible, it suffices to check whether
h^{n}(x)-\frac{b}{2a}
is irreducible.

Lemma
3
also implies that the irreducibility of a polynomial is preserved when the polynomials is conjugated to a monic form. Specifically, let
f(x)=a_{d}x^{d}+a_{d-1}x^{d-1}+\dots+a_{0}
be a degree
d
polynomial where
a_{d}
is a
d-1^{st}
power in
K
, say
a_{d}=c^{d-1}
for
c\in K^{\times}
. Then
g(x)=cf(\frac{x}{c})
is monic, and using Lemma
3
with
\alpha=0
we have that
f^{n}(x)
is irreducible over
K
if and only if
g^{n}(x)
is irreducible over
K
.

Example 5
.

Let
f(x)=a_{3}x^{3}+a_{2}x^{2}+a_{1}x+a_{0}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]
for some prime
p
with
a_{3}\neq 0
. If
a_{3}
is a square, then for
c\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
such that
c^{2}=a_{3}
, the dynamical irreducibility of

is equivalent to checking dynamical irreducibility of the monic polynomial
g(x)=cf(\frac{x}{c})=x^{3}+\frac{a_{2}}{c}x^{2}+a_{1}x+ca_{0}
. If
a_{3}
is not a square, then for any
c\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
, the leading coefficient of

is
\frac{a_{3}}{c^{2}}
, which is also not a square.

There are a number of classical results that can be used to determine whether a polynomial and its iterates are irreducible. A powerful tool in proving dynamical irreducibility of a polynomial is Capelli’s Lemma, which gives necessary and sufficient conditions for irreducibility of a composition of polynomials.

Lemma 6

(Capelli’s Lemma)
.

Let
K
be a field,
f(x),g(x)\in K[x]
, and let
\beta\in\bar{K}
be any root of
g(x)
. Then
g(f(x))
is irreducible over
K
if and only if both
g
is irreducible over
K
and
f(x)-\beta
is irreducible over
K(\beta)
.

In order to state other existing results we first recall some basic definitions. Let
f
and
g
be polynomials over a field
K
of degrees
d
and
k
, respectively, with leading coefficients
a_{d}
and
b_{k}
. Let
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{d}
and
\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{k}
denote the roots of

and

respectively, counted with multiplicity. Then the

\Res(f,g)=a_{d}^{k}b_{k}^{d}\prod_{1\leq i\leq d,1\leq j\leq k}(\lambda_{i}-\mu_{j})=(-1)^{dk}b_{k}^{d}\prod_{1\leq j\leq k}f(\mu_{j}).

The

, denoted by Disc

, is defined as

\text{Disc}(f)=a^{2d-2}_{d}\prod_{i<j}(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})^{2}=(-1)^{d(d-1)/2}a_{d}^{d-k-2}\Res(f,f^{\prime})

where

is the derivative of

. The discriminant and resultant are elements of the field

.

Let

be a separable extension of fields and let

be the distinct embeddings of

N_{L/K}(\alpha)=\prod_{i=1}^{r}\sigma_{i}(\alpha)\ \text{and}\ \Tr_{L/K}(\alpha)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sigma_{i}(\alpha).

Note, if

, then
\Tr_{L/K}(\alpha)=[L\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}K]\cdot\alpha
. Hence, if

, then

, this will be important to our arguments in Section

. We make use of the following formulas for norms and traces in towers of extensions.

Theorem 7

(Theorem 5.1, Chapter VI,
[
20
]
)

Let

be a finite field,

(1)

\N_{E/K}=\N_{F/K}\left(\N_{E/F}(\alpha)\right)

(2)

\Tr_{E/K}(\alpha)=\Tr_{F/K}\left(\Tr_{E/F}(\alpha)\right)

The norm function can be used to determine if an element of a finite field

is an

Lemma 8
.

Let
\mathbb{F}_{q^{s}}
be a finite extension of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and let
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{s}}
and let

\gcd(q,r)=1

(1)

if

is an

-th power in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{s}}
,
N_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{s}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\alpha)
is an
r
-th power in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, and

(2)

if

and

is an
r
-th power in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, then

is an
r
-th power in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{s}}
.

Proof.

Consider the norm map
N_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{s}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{F}_{q^{s}}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Part (

) follows immediately from the fact that this map is a homomorphism. Conversely, if

, then exactly

of the elements of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
and

of the elements of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{s}}^{\times}
are

-th powers. Since the map
N_{\mathbb{F}_{q}^{s}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{F}_{q^{s}}^{\times}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
is a surjective homomorphism of finite groups and

-th powers, it follows that non-

-th powers map to non-
r
-th powers.
∎

We state below the theorem by Gómez-Pérez, Nicolás, Ostafe, and Sadornil that proves a necessary condition for dynamical irreducibility of polynomials. The proof uses Lemma

Theorem 9

(Theorem 3.2,
[

]
)

Let

3.
Unicritical Polynomials

A polynomial

Let
f
be a unicritical polynomial of degree

be the lead coefficient of

Then by Lemma

is a dynamically irreducible pair. Since

is unicritical with critical point

The Vahlen-Capelli criterion provides irreducibility conditions for polynomials of the form

Naturally, this can be used to explore dynamical irreduciblity of unicritical polynomials.

Theorem 10

(Theorem 9.1, Chapter VI,
[

]
)
.

Let

,

, and if

. Then

.

In
[

]
and
[

]
, the authors give sufficient conditions for dynamical irreducibility of a quadratic polynomial, which are also necessary when working over a finite field. In this section we extend these results to all unicritical polynomials.

Proposition 11

(Proposition 2.3’,
[
19
]
)
.

Let

have characteristic not equal to two. A quadratic polynomial
f(x)=ax^{2}+bx+c\in K[x]
is dynamically irreducible if
\{-af(\frac{-b}{2a})\}\cup\{af^{n}(\frac{-b}{2a})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}n\geq 2\}
contains no squares. In the case

is a finite field,

is dynamically irreducible if and only if
\{-af(\frac{-b}{2a})\}\cup\{af^{n}(\frac{-b}{2a})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}n\geq 2\}
contains no squares.

In the following lemma we generalize
[
19
, Lemma 2.5’]
, in which the authors give conditions for the irreducibility of

when

is irreducible. These conditions are both necessary and sufficient in the finite field case, which allows us to give explicit dynamical irreducibility conditions for general unicritical polynomials.

Lemma 12
.

Let
K
be a field with characteristic relatively prime to
d
.
Let
h(x)=ax^{d}+c\in K[x]
.
Let
g\in K[x]
be a polynomial of degree
k\geq 1
with leading coefficient
\ell(g)
. Suppose
g\circ h^{n-1}
is irreducible over
K[x]
for some
n\geq 1
. Let
C=(-a)^{k}\ell(g)
if
n=1
and
C=a^{k}\ell(g)
if
n>1
and let
D=(4a)^{k}\ell(g)
if
n=1
and
D=a^{k}\ell(g)
if
n>1
. Then
g\circ h^{n}
is irreducible over
K
if

•

\frac{g\circ h^{n-1}(c)}{C}
is not an
r
-th power in
K
for every prime
r\mid d
,

•

and if
4\mid d
,
\frac{g\circ h^{n-1}(c)}{D}
is not
4
-th power in
K
.

Moreover, in the case where
K
is finite,
|K|\equiv 1\mod r
for each prime
r\mid d
, and if
4\mid d
,
|K|\equiv 1\mod 4
, we have
g\circ h^{n}
is irreducible over
K
if and only if the above conditions are satisfied.

Proof.

By hypothesis
g\circ h^{n-1}(x)
is irreducible. Fix a root
\beta
of
g\circ h^{n-1}
. Then by Capelli’s Lemma,
g\circ h^{n}(x)
is reducible over
K
if and only if
h(x)-\beta
is reducible over
K(\beta)
. By Theorem

,
h(x)-\beta=ax^{d}+c-\beta
is reducible over
K(\beta)
if and only if
\frac{-(c-\beta)}{a}
is an
r
-th power in
K(\beta)
for some prime
r\mid d
or if
4\mid d
and
\frac{(c-\beta)}{4a}
is a
4
-th power in
K(\beta)
.

We first use the norm map to reduce to the conditions over
K(\beta)
to conditions over
K
. If
\frac{-(c-\beta)}{a}
is an
r
-th power in
K(\beta)
for some prime
r\mid d
, then
N_{K(\beta)/K}\left(\frac{-(c-\beta)}{a}\right)
is an
r
-th power in
K
since the norm map is a multiplicative homomorphism. Similarly, if
4\mid d
and
\frac{(c-\beta)}{4a}
is a
4
-th power in
K(\beta)
, then
N_{K(\beta)/K}\left(\frac{(c-\beta)}{4a}\right)
is a
4
-th power in
K
. By Lemma

, both of these implications become if and only if statements when the conditions
|K|\equiv 1\mod r
for each
r\mid d
, and if
4\mid d
,
|K|\equiv 1\mod 4
are satisfied.

We now evaluate these norms using the fact that the Galois conjugates of
\beta
are exactly the roots of
g\circ h^{n-1}
,

\displaystyle N_{K(\beta)/K}\left(\frac{-(c-\beta)}{a}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{-1}{a}\right)^{\deg(g\circ h^{n-1})}N_{K(\beta)/K}(c-\beta)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{-1}{a}\right)^{kd^{n-1}}\frac{g\circ h^{n-1}(c)}{\ell(g\circ h^{n-1})},

where

and the roots of
x^{2}-\alpha x+1
are not cubes in

\mathbb{F}_{q}(\sqrt{-3})=\begin{cases}\mathbb{F}_{q}\text{ if }3\mid(q-1),\\
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\text{ if }3\mid(q-2).\end{cases}

Using Lemma
3
,
f(x+\alpha)-\alpha=x^{3}+3\alpha x^{2}+(3\alpha^{2}-3)x+(\alpha^{3}-4\alpha)
is dynamically irreducible if and only if the pair
(f,\alpha)
is dynamically irreducible.
∎

Shifted Linearized Polynomials over Finite Fields

In this section, we explore dynamical irreducibility of shifted linearized polynomials over finite fields. A
linearized polynomial
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with
q=p^{s}
is a polynomial of the form

a_{n}x^{p^{n}}+a_{n-1}x^{p^{n-1}}+\dots+a_{1}x^{p}+a_{0}x.

These polynomials are called linearized because they represent a linear mapping on
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
A theorem of Agou discussed in
[
6
]
implies a shifted linearized polynomial of degree
p^{n}
is reducible unless
n=1
or
p=n=2
. Thus, iterates of shifted linearized polynomials are always dynamically reducible; in fact the second iterate is reducible unless
p=\deg f=2
, and in this case the third iterate must be reducible. In
[
1
]
, Ahmadi gives an alternate proof that the third iterate of a quadratic polynomial must be reducible over a field of characteristic
2
. In
[
14
]
, the authors apply this argument to show the third iterate of a shifted linearized cubic must be reducible over a field of characteristic
3
. We push this argument further to show a shifted linear degree
p
polynomial over a field of characteristic
p
must have reducible second iterate unless
p=2
, in which case the polynomial must have reducible third iterate. As a consequence, it follows that no Artin-Schreier polynomials are dynamically irreducible.

Consider
f(x)=a_{p}x^{p}-a_{1}x-a_{0}
over a finite field of characteristic
p
. The following lemma provides necessary and sufficient conditions for when a polynomial of this form is irreducible.

Lemma 28

(
[
6
]
, Lemma 2)
.

Let
q=p^{s}
. The polynomial
f(x)=x^{p}-a_{1}x-a_{0}
is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
if and only if
a_{1}=A^{p-1}
for some
A\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
\Tr_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{p}}\left(\frac{a_{0}}{A^{p}}\right)\neq 0
.

We use this lemma to prove the main result in this section.

Theorem 29
.

Let
p
be a prime and
q=p^{s}
for
s
a positive integer. If
f(x)=a_{p}x^{p}-a_{1}x-a_{0}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
with
a_{p}a_{1}\neq 0
, then
f^{2}(x)
is reducible if
p\geq 3
and
f^{3}(x)
is reducible if
p=2
.

Proof.

Note if
f^{2}(x)
is reducible we are done. Suppose
f^{2}(x)
is irreducible, let
\gamma
be any root of
f(x)
. By Capelli’s Lemma (Lemma
6
),
f(x)
is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
f(x)-\gamma
is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{q}(\gamma)=\mathbb{F}_{q^{p}}
.
Since
f(x)
is irreducible,
h(x)=x^{p}-\frac{a_{1}}{a_{p}}x-\frac{a_{0}}{a_{p}}
is also irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
so
\frac{a_{1}}{a_{p}}=A^{p-1}
for
A\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
\Tr_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{p}}\left(\frac{a_{0}}{a_{p}A^{p}}\right)\neq 0
by Lemma
28
. Also, since
f(x)-\gamma
is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{p}}
, so is
x^{p}-\frac{a_{1}}{a_{p}}x-\frac{a_{0}+\gamma}{a_{p}}
and hence by Lemma
28
,
\Tr_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{p}}/\mathbb{F}_{p}}\left(\frac{a_{0}+\gamma}{a_{p}A^{p}}\right)\neq 0
.

Using properties of trace from Theorem
7
, we have

\displaystyle\Tr_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{p}}/\mathbb{F}_{p}}\left(\frac{a_{0}+\gamma}{a_{p}A^{p}}\right)

\displaystyle=\Tr_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{p}}/\mathbb{F}_{p}}\left(\frac{a_{0}}{a_{p}A^{p}}\right)+\Tr_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{p}}/\mathbb{F}_{p}}\left(\frac{\gamma}{a_{p}A^{p}}\right)

\displaystyle=\Tr_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{p}}\left(\Tr_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{p}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}\left(\frac{a_{0}}{a_{p}A^{p}}\right)\right)+\Tr_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{p}}/\mathbb{F}_{p}}\left(\frac{\gamma}{a_{p}A^{p}}\right)

\displaystyle=\Tr_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{p}}\left(0\right)+\Tr_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{p}}/\mathbb{F}_{p}}\left(\frac{\gamma}{a_{p}A^{p}}\right)

\displaystyle=\Tr_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{p}}/\mathbb{F}_{p}}\left(\frac{\gamma}{a_{p}A^{p}}\right).

Hence,
\Tr_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{p}}/\mathbb{F}_{p}}\left(\frac{\gamma}{a_{p}A^{p}}\right)\neq 0
.

Since
f(x)
is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, it is the minimal polynomial for
\gamma
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. When
p\geq 3
, this implies
\Tr_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{p}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}\left(\frac{\gamma}{a_{p}A^{p}}\right)=0
since the coefficient of the
p-1
term of
f(x)
is
0
and thus,

\Tr_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{p}}/\mathbb{F}_{p}}\left(\frac{\gamma}{a_{p}A^{p}}\right)=\Tr_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{p}}\left(\Tr_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{p}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}\left(\frac{\gamma}{a_{p}A^{p}}\right)\right)=\Tr_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{p}}(0)=0,

which is a contradiction.

When
p=2
, let
\gamma^{\prime}
be a root of
f^{2}(x)
, then by a similar argument, if
f^{3}(x)
is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, then
f^{2}(x)-\gamma^{\prime}
is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{4}}
, so
\Tr_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{4}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}\left(\frac{a_{0}+\gamma^{\prime}}{a_{2}A^{2}}\right)\neq 0
, which implies
\Tr_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{4}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}\left(\frac{\gamma^{\prime}}{a_{2}A^{2}}\right)\neq 0
. On the other hand, since
f^{2}(x)
is the minimal polynomial for
\gamma^{\prime}
,
\Tr_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{4}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}\left(\frac{\gamma^{\prime}}{a_{2}A^{2}}\right)=0
, which is a contradiction.
∎

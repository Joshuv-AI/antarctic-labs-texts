Evaluating Anti-ESKAPE Potential of Valeriana Officinalis L. Extract against Healthcare-Associated Infections in Tertiary Hospitals of Al-Kharj Governorate: An In Vitro Study
Authors: Mohammed S. Khan, MD Nadeem Bari
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:Healthcare-associated infections (HAIs) caused by ESKAPE pathogens are a significant concern in tertiary hospitals worldwide, including those in Al-Kharj, Saudi Arabia. This study aimed to explore the bioactive compounds from Valeriana officinalis and evaluate their antimicrobial activity against ESKAPE pathogens.Materials and Methods:Methanolic extracts of the Valeriana officinalis stem were prepared and subjected to phytochemical analysis, thin-layer chromatography (TLC), and antimicrobial testing using the well-diffusion method. The minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC), minimum bactericidal concentration (MBC), and cytotoxicity (MTT assay) were also evaluated.Results:The extract contained several major phytochemicals with significant activity against ESKAPE pathogens. The MIC values were 50 mg/mL for Klebsiella pneumoniae, Acinetobacter baumannii, and Pseudomonas aeruginosa, 100 mg/mL for Enterococcus faecalis and Staphylococcus aureus, and 25 mg/mL for Escherichia coli. The MBC values were 25 mg/mL for Escherichia coli and 100 mg/mL for the other ESKAPE pathogens.Conclusion:The methanolic extract of Valeriana officinalis L. demonstrated potent antimicrobial activity, with relatively low MIC and MBC values against ESKAPE pathogens responsible for HAIs.

INTRODUCTION

Infection(s) gained while receiving healthcare services in long-term care facilities, hospitals, ambulatory settings, or those infections that even appear after discharge from the healthcare facilities are regarded as healthcare-associated infections (HAIs), which may also include occupational infections to the healthcare staff.[12] Between 4% to 12% of inpatients may get infected with one or more HAIs,[34] and they pose an unprecedented challenge to the healthcare system worldwide by causing serious patient suffering with higher hospital expenses, increased morbidity, and higher rates of mortality.[5]

According to the Infectious Diseases Society of America (IDSA), there is a leading class of notorious, opportunistic bacterial pathogens known to ‘escape’ antibiotics and other conventional treatments, and are dubbed as the “ESKAPE group” posing a global threat to humankind, and the members are E. faecium, S. aureus, K. pneumoniae, A. baumannii, P. aeruginosa, and Enterobacter spp.[67891011121314] When the HAIs are caused by multi-drug-resistant bacteria, including ESKAPE pathogens which are composed of highly virulent strains, the treatment and management become challenging with no more drugs left.[615] Various low- and lower-middle-income countries (L-LMICs) carry the burden of MDR-ESKAPE-caused HAIs.[16] The rate of HAIs in Saudi Arabia has also been recorded in several studies, and in many cases, the causative agents were determined to be multidrug-resistant ESKAPE pathogens.[1718] Al-Kharj Governorate in the Kingdom has also been experiencing the prevalence of HAIs at more or less the same rate with the involvement of multi-drug-resistant ESKAPE bacterial pathogens.[4]

For a very long, traditional medicines, including various herbs and herbal formulations, have been used as treatment against a wide range of illnesses, including infectious disorders.[192021] Plant extracts, which are sources of diverse secondary metabolites with complex actions on immune function resulting in immune stimulations, have already been used in developing novel medications.[22232425] Valeriana officinalis is a well-known plant with several notable medicinal importance as it has been used as a hypotensive, anti-spasmodic, revealing sedative, etc.[2627] Therefore, the present study concentrates on examining the anti-ESKAPE activity of the plant in a trial to find out to what extent the potential phytochemicals in the plant will be effective in tackling HAIs in the Al-Kharj Governorate.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Study area and duration

This investigation was carried out in the laboratories of the BMS, College of Medicine, at the Prince Sattam bin Abdulaziz University, Al-Kharj, Saudi Arabia in March 2024.

Collection of plant material

The stem of the valerian plant was procured, washed, ground, and stored in sterile containers for extraction. The ESKAPE members, viz., Staphylococcus aureus (ATCC # 12600), Enterococcus faecalis (ATCC # 49532), Acinetobacter baumannii (ATCC # 19606), Klebsiella pneumoniae (ATCC # 13883), Escherichia coli (ATCC # 13706), and Pseudomonas aeruginosa (ATCC # 15442) were purchased from Microbiologics, USA.

Extraction of bioactive compounds using Soxhlet apparatus

Taking methanol as the solvent, the plant extract was prepared using the Soxhlet apparatus as per the standard procedures described by Zygmunt and Namieśnik (2003).[28] The prepared extract was evaporated under heat and stored in sterile containers for further analysis.

Phytochemical analysis

To understand the presence of various bioactive phytochemicals in alcoholic and aqueous extracts of valerian stem, preliminary qualitative analyzes were conducted as per standard protocols.[29]

Separation of bioactive compounds using thin-layer chromatography (TLC)

To separate the potential bioactive compounds in the extract, a TLC[30] was performed as per the standard protocols, which will separate molecules of low molecular weight (Hahn-Deinstrop, 2000) from others.

Qualitative anti-ESKAPE potentials of the crude extract

The anti-ESKAPE activities of the extract were conducted by well-diffusion protocol Daoud et al., (2019).[31]

Minimal inhibitory concentrations (MICs) of the extract against ESKAPE pathogens

The MICs of the extract against the members of the selected ESKAPE pathogens were found using a microtiter plate-based antibacterial assay incorporating resazurin, as per the standard procedure.[32]

Minimal bactericidal concentration (MBC) of the extract

As per the standard protocols, the MBC of the extract against the selected ESKAPE pathogens was found.[33]

Mammalian cell cytotoxicity: Mammalian cell cytotoxicity

The Valeriana officinalis toxicity on mammalian cells was studied using an MTT assay. For the assay, L929 (cell line of mouse fibroblast) grown in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagles Medium (DMEM) was treated with various concentrations of Valeriana officinalis at 37°C in 5% CO2 for a period of 24 h followed by the addition of 20 μL of MTT solution (5 mg/mL in phosphate-buffered saline) and incubated further for 3 to 4 h at 37°C to allow the formation of formazan.[34] After dissolving formazan crystals, the solution was spectrophotometrically analyzed and measured at 690 nm and 570 nm. Doxorubicin was a positive control, and the percentage of cell viability was found as follows:

DISCUSSION

Many of the HAIs are caused by the members of a notorious group of bacterial pathogens called ESKAPE making the treatment and management difficult.[35] As the conventional antibiotics fail to tackle the MDR nature of the ESKAPE pathogens, the scientists started to search back in nature for potential antibiotics and the present study tried to explore one of the medicinal plants – Valeriana officinalis L. for its anti-ESKAPE activities. In the present study, the methanolic extract of the herb V. officinalis showed the presence of major phytochemical compounds like tannins, flavonoids, saponins, terpenoids, glycosides, phenol, quinones, proteins, etc., while others like steroids and alkaloids were absent. It was also found to possess phytocompounds with potential anti-ESKAPE activities. The MICs of the extract were 50 mg (K. pneumoniae), 50 mg (A. baumannii), 100 mg (E. faecalis), 50 mg (P. aeruginosa), 100 mg (S. aureus), and 25 mg (E. coli). The MBCs were also comparatively lower at 25 mg against E. coli and 100 mg each for the remaining members of the ESKAPE group, with anti-ESKAPE activities. Even though the investigation for anti-ESKAPE activities of V. officinalis has not been recorded, the plant has widely been analyzed for its antimicrobial activities along with various phytochemical investigations. Eftekhari has reported that the methanolic extract of the plant Valeriana officinalis possessed antimicrobial activities against Candida albicans, Helicobacter pylori, Staphylococcus aureus, etc., and the activities could be correlated with the presence of phytocompounds like monoterpenoids and sesquiterpenoids.[36] Rondon et al.[37] investigated the antibacterial potentials of various species of Valariana and found that all the extracts displayed activity only against S. aureus.[37] Another investigation by Khademian et al.[38] on the potential of the methanol extract of V. officinalis on the bacterium Helicobacter pylori has also yielded positive results. So, the above-mentioned studies by various researchers from across the globe justify our findings.

CONCLUSION

The present study concentrates on examining the antibacterial activity of the valerian stem (Valeriana officinalis L.) extracts against ESKAPE pathogens causing HAIs. The phytochemical analysis, TLC, minimal inhibitory concentrations, and minimal bactericidal concentrations of the extract were found. Preliminary antibacterial activity was carried out using the well-diffusion method at different concentrations ranging from 125 mg to 1 g/mL. The extracts displayed significant antibacterial activity against all the selected pathogenic bacteria. Therefore, the phytochemicals from the plant could be further investigated for the development of potential anti-ESKAPE drugs.

On the structure of Witt groups and minimal extension conjecture
Theo Johnson-Freyd, Victor Ostrik, Zhiqiang Yu

Abstract
Let ℰ = Rep ⁡ ( G ) \mathcal{E}=\Rep(G) be a Tannakian fusion category. For a braided fusion category 𝒞 \mathcal{C} over ℰ \mathcal{E} we give sufficient and necessary conditions that characterize the Witt relation [ 𝒞 ] = [ ℰ ] [\mathcal{C}]=[\mathcal{E}] . Then we show the Witt group 𝒲 ⁡ ( ℰ ) \mathcal{W}(\mathcal{E}) is naturally a direct sum of Witt group 𝒲 := 𝒲 ⁡ ( Vec ) \mathcal{W}:=\mathcal{W}(\mathrm{Vec}) and the group H 4 ⁡ ( G , 𝕜 × ) \homology^{4}(G,\mathds{k}^{\times}) . Consequently, for any non-degenerate fusion category 𝒞 \mathcal{C} over ℰ \mathcal{E} , there is a positive integer n n (e.g. n = | G | n=|G| ) such that 𝒞 ⊠ ℰ n \mathcal{C}^{\boxtimes_{\mathcal{E}}^{n}} admits a minimal extension. Keywords: Minimal extensions; Tannakian fusion category; Witt groups



1
Introduction

Let
\mathds{k}
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. In this paper we are concerned with the study of braided fusion categories over
\mathds{k}
, see e.g.
[
11
]
. A complete classification of such categories is currently out of reach, so one can study such categories up to a suitable equivalence. One such equivalence relation (on a subset of
non-degenerate
braided fusion categories) is

Witt equivalence
introduced and studied in
[
5
]
. The equivalence classes form the
Witt group

\mathcal{W}
.

Recall that a braided fusion category is non-degenerate if and only if its
Müger center

\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
is trivial, i.e.
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}=\mathrm{Vec}
. In general,
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
is a
symmetric fusion subcategory
of
\mathcal{C}
. Thus for a given symmetric fusion category
\mathcal{E}
one can study
non-degenerate braided fusion categories over

\mathcal{E}
, i.e. braided fusion categories equipped with an equivalence (of symmetric fusion categories)
\mathcal{E}\simeq\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
. In particular in
[
6
]
the
relative Witt group

\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{E})
was introduced. An important special case is

\mathcal{E}=\mathrm{sVec}
; in this case the group
\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{E})
is called super Witt group and is denoted
s\mathcal{W}
. It was shown in
[
6
]
that the group
s\mathcal{W}
has a simpler structure than
\mathcal{W}
and this is useful for the study of the group

\mathcal{W}
thanks to the canonical homomorphism
\mathcal{W}\to s\mathcal{W}
.

The main goal of this paper is to get a better understanding of the group
\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{E})
for a general
\mathcal{E}
.
A well known theorem of Deligne
[
8
]
implies
that symmetric braided fusion categories fall in two classes:
Tannakian
and
super Tannakian
. Assume that
\mathcal{E}
is Tannakian, i.e. there exists a finite group
G
and an equivalence of symmetric fusion categories
\mathcal{E}\simeq\Rep(G)
. Recall that the finite group
G
is determined by this assumption up to inner automorphisms; in particular the cohomology groups
\homology^{i}(G,\mathds{k}^{\times})
are canonically determined by the category
\mathcal{E}
, since the inner automorphisms of
G
act trivially on cohomology. Here is our main result (see Theorem
5.11
):

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{E}\simeq\Rep(G)
.
There is a canonical isomorphism of abelian groups

\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{E})\cong\mathcal{W}\oplus\homology^{4}(G,\mathds{k}^{\times})
.

Remark 1.2
.

This in particular proves Conjecture 4.3 of
[
18
]
, which briefly sketches a proof subject to various assumptions about a not-yet-developed higher Morita theory. Also T. Décoppet in

[
7
, Proposition 5.3.9]
used the theory of fusion 2-categories to construct a set bijection
\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{E})=\mathcal{W}\times H^{4}(G,\mathds{k}^{\times})
which is very likely to coincide with our isomorphism.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
is as follows: first we define the canonical homomorphism
\phi_{\mathcal{E}}:\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{E})\to\mathcal{W}
and its canonical splitting, see Section
3
. Then we use the theory of graded extensions
of fusion categories
[
13
]
in order to construct isomorphism
\operatorname{Ker}(\phi_{\mathcal{E}})=\homology^{4}(G,\mathds{k}^{\times})
.

Remark 1.3
.

Theorem
1.1
gives an interpretation of the group
\homology^{4}(G,\mathds{k}^{\times})
in terms of the category
\mathcal{E}
. There are similar interpretations for
\homology^{i}(G,\mathds{k}^{\times})
for
i\leq 3
, namely:

\homology^{1}(G,\mathds{k}^{\times})=
group of isomorphism classes of invertible objects in
\mathcal{E}
(since the invertible objects
of
\mathcal{E}
are 1-dimensional representations of
G
which are in bijection with
\mbox{Hom}(G,\mathds{k}^{\times})=H^{1}(G,\mathds{k}^{\times})
);

\homology^{2}(G,\mathds{k}^{\times})=
group of equivalence classes of invertible module categories over
\mathcal{E}
, see
[
2
,
4
]
;

\homology^{3}(G,\mathds{k}^{\times})=
group of equivalence classes of minimal modular extensions of
\mathcal{E}
, see
[
19
]
.

Note that the interpretations above make sense for
\mathcal{E}
which is not necessarily Tannakian and give
interesting invariants of super Tannakian categories
\mathcal{E}
(it is not quite clear what should be the invariant
of super Tannakian category
\mathcal{E}
corresponding to
\homology^{4}
; provisionally this can be the kernel
of the natural homomorphism
\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{E})\to s\mathcal{W}
, see Section
6
).
It would be interesting to find such interpretations for
\homology^{i}(G,\mathds{k}^{\times})
with
i\geq 5
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a non-degenerate braided fusion category over
\mathcal{E}
. In
[
21
, Conjecture 5.2]
M. Müger conjectured that there exists a non-degenerate fusion category
\mathcal{D}
such that
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{D}
and
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}=\mathcal{E}
, where
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}
is the
centralizer
of
\mathcal{C}
in
\mathcal{D}
. This is known as the
minimal extension
conjecture
. Drinfeld constructed counterexamples to this
conjecture for some Tannakian categories
\mathcal{E}
in an unpublished work. On the other hand it is known
that the minimal extension conjecture is true in some cases: the case
\mathcal{E}=\Rep(G)
where
\homology^{4}(G,\mathds{k}^{\times})=0

is a consequence of
[
13
]
and the case
\mathcal{E}=s\mathrm{Vec}
is the main result of
[
18
]
.

In
[
23
]
and
[
18
]
, the minimal extension conjecture for symmetric fusion category
\mathcal{E}
was connected with the surjectivity of certain group homomorphism between Witt groups
\mathcal{W}
and
\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{E})
. Namely, it was shown in
[
23
]
that the minimal extension conjecture is true for a braided fusion category
\mathcal{C}
over
\mathcal{E}
if and only if
the class
[\mathcal{C}]\in\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{E})
is in the image of natural homomorphism
S_{\mathcal{E}}:\mathcal{W}\to\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{E})
, where
S_{\mathcal{E}}:[\mathcal{D}]\mapsto[\mathcal{E}\boxtimes\mathcal{D}]
for any
[\mathcal{D}]\in\mathcal{W}
. In
[
23
]
this was used to show that the minimal extension conjecture holds for slightly degenerate weakly group-theoretical braided fusion categories. This result was
superseded by
[
18
]
where it was shown that the minimal extension conjecture holds for all slightly degenerate braided fusion categories. We refer the reader to
[
17
]
for further results on the minimal extension
conjecture in the super-Tannakian case.

As a consequence of Theorem
1.1
we get the following (see Corollaries
5.10
and
5.13
):

Corollary 1.4
.

(i)

The minimal extension conjecture holds for all braided fusion categories
over Tannakian category
\mathcal{E}=\Rep(G)
if and only if
\homology^{4}(G,\mathds{k}^{\times})=0
.

(ii)

For any braided fusion category
\mathcal{C}
over Tannakian category
\mathcal{E}
there exists a positive integer
n
such that
\mathcal{C}^{\boxtimes_{\mathcal{E}}^{n}}
admits a minimal extension. In fact we can take
n=|G|
.

Finite groups for which
\homology^{4}(G,\mathds{k}^{\times})=0
include those for which all Sylow subgroups are cyclic, as well as the Mathieu group
\mathrm{M}_{23}

[
20
]
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we recall some most used results about braided fusion categories and Witt groups.
In Section
3
we give a characterization of the elements
of the Witt equivalence class
[\mathcal{E}]\in\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{E})
.
In Section
4
we introduce the useful technical notion
of
G
-quasi-monoidal categories and in Section
5
we use it to prove our main results. Finally in Section

6
we discuss some aspects of the super Tannakian case.

2
Preliminaries

In this paper we will assume that the reader is familiar with basic notions of the theory of fusion categories
(such as notions of fusion category and braided fusion category), see e.g.
[
12
]
.
Note that any fusion subcategory of a braided fusion category is automatically braided.
We will in particular use Müger’s theory of centralizers
[
21
]
,
[
12
, 8.20]
. Thus for a fusion subcategory

\mathcal{D}\subset\mathcal{C}
of a braided fusion category
\mathcal{C}
we will denote by
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{C}}
the full subcategory of
\mathcal{C}

consisting of objects
Y\in\mathcal{C}
satisfying
c_{X,Y}\circ c_{Y,X}=\mathrm{Id}_{Y\otimes X}
for all
X\in\mathcal{D}
(here

c_{X,Y}:X\otimes Y\to Y\otimes X
is the braiding in the category
\mathcal{C}
). We will also use
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
for

\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{C}}
when the category
\mathcal{C}
is clear from the context. The subcategory
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
is again a fusion
subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
. In particular we have the subcategory
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}=\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{C}}
which is called
Müger center

of
\mathcal{C}
; this is always a symmetric fusion category.
A
non-degenerate
fusion category is a braided fusion category
\mathcal{C}
such that
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
is trivial
(i.e. consists only of direct sums of the unit object). For instance the
Drinfeld center

\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{A})
of a fusion
category
\mathcal{A}
is non-degenerate. If
\mathcal{C}
is non-degenerate we have the
double
centralizer property
:
(\mathcal{D}^{\prime})^{\prime}=\mathcal{D}
for any fusion subcategory
\mathcal{D}\subset\mathcal{C}
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a symmetric fusion category. Following
[
6
]
we say that a braided fusion category
\mathcal{C}
is

non-degenerate over
\mathcal{E}
if it is equipped with an equivalence
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\cong\mathcal{E}
. An important example
is the relative Drinfeld center
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{E})
of a fusion category
\mathcal{A}
over
\mathcal{E}
(this means that

\mathcal{E}
is equivalent to a fusion subcategory of
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{A})
which projects fully faithfully to
\mathcal{A}
under
the forgetful functor
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{A})\to\mathcal{A}
, and
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{E}):=\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{A})}
). For non-degenerate fusion categories

\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
over
\mathcal{E}
there is an important notion of
Deligne tensor product over
\mathcal{E}
,

\mathcal{C}\boxtimes_{\mathcal{E}}\mathcal{D}
which is also a non-degenerate fusion category over
\mathcal{E}
, see
[
6
, 2.5]
.
Namely,
\mathcal{C}\boxtimes_{\mathcal{E}}\mathcal{D}:=(\mathcal{C}\boxtimes\mathcal{D})_{A}
is the category of right
A
-modules, where

\displaystyle A=\oplus_{X\in\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{E})}X\boxtimes X^{*}

is the canonical connected étale algebra in
\mathcal{E}\boxtimes\mathcal{E}
such that
(\mathcal{E}\boxtimes\mathcal{E})_{A}\cong\mathcal{E}\boxtimes_{\mathcal{E}}\mathcal{E}\cong\mathcal{E}
, see
[
6
, Remark 2.8]
.

Recall that two non-degenerate fusion categories
\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}
over
\mathcal{E}
are
Witt equivalent
if there exists
an equivalence
\mathcal{C}\boxtimes_{\mathcal{E}}\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{E})\cong\mathcal{D}\boxtimes_{\mathcal{E}}\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{A}_{2},\mathcal{E})
for some fusion
categories
\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2}
over
\mathcal{E}
, see
[
6
, Definition 5.1]
. This is an equivalence relation
and the equivalence class of the fusion category
\mathcal{C}
is denoted by
[\mathcal{C}]
. The operation
[\mathcal{C}][\mathcal{D}]:=[\mathcal{C}\boxtimes_{\mathcal{E}}\mathcal{D}]

is well defined and it makes the set of equivalence classes
\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{E})
into an abelian group called the

relative Witt group
; this is the main character of this paper.

Let
G
be a finite group acting on a fusion category
\mathcal{A}
(this means that there is a homomorphism
of 2-groups
G\to\underline{\mbox{Aut}}(\mathcal{A})
). Then one defines the
equivariantization

\mathcal{A}^{G}
to be the category of categorical fixed points. It
is a fusion category over
\mathcal{E}=\Rep(G)
, see
[
11
, 4.2.2]
. Conversely, given a fusion
category
\mathcal{B}
over
\mathcal{E}=\Rep(G)
there is a construction of
de-equivariantization

which gives a fusion category
\mathcal{B}_{G}
equipped with a
G
-action, see
[
11
, subsection 4.2.4]
.
These two constructions are inverse to each other, see
[
11
, Theorem 4.18]
.
We will also use braided version of these constructions, see
[
11
, subsection 4.2]
.

3
Group homomorphism
\phi_{\mathcal{E}}:\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{E})\to\mathcal{W}

As observed already in
[
6
, Remark 5.8]
, the assignment
\mathcal{E}\mapsto\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{E})
is functorial: any functor
\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{F}
of symmetric fusion categories determines, via base-change, an abelian group homomorphism
\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{E})\to\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{F})
. The domain of this functor is most naturally the
2
-category of symmetric fusion categories, symmetric linear functors, and symmetric natural transformations (which are automatically natural isomorphisms because of the existence of duals); but since the codomain the the merely-
1
-category of abelian groups, one might as well treat the domain as the
1
-category of symmetric fusion categories and
isomorphism classes
of symmetric linear functors between them.

For example, the unique symmetric functor
\mathrm{Vec}\to\mathcal{E}
selects, for any
\mathcal{E}
, a canonical homomorphism
S_{\mathcal{E}}=\mathcal{W}(\mathrm{Vec}\to\mathcal{E}):\mathcal{W}\to\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{E})
. Suppose that
\mathcal{E}=\Rep(G)
is Tannakian. A choice of fiber functor
\mathcal{E}\to\mathrm{Vec}
selects a section-retract pair
\mathrm{Vec}\to\mathcal{E}\to\mathrm{Vec}
(meaning that the composite
\mathrm{Vec}\to\mathrm{Vec}
is the identity), and hence the induced map
\phi_{\mathcal{E}}=\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{E}\to\mathrm{Vec}):\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{E})\to\mathcal{W}
together with
S_{\phi}
are a section-retract pair of abelian groups.
Section-retract pairs of abelian groups are precisely the same as direct sum decompositions, and so:

Theorem 3.1

(
[
23
]
)
.

We have a split exact sequence of abelian groups

\displaystyle 1\to\text{Ker}(\phi_{\mathcal{E}})\overset{i}{\to}\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{E})\underset{S_{\mathcal{E}}}{\overset{\phi_{\mathcal{E}}}{\rightleftarrows}}\mathcal{W}\to 1.

In particular,
\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{E})\cong\mathcal{W}\oplus\text{Ker}(\phi_{\mathcal{E}})
. ∎

Remark 3.2
.

Explicitly, the homomorphisms
\phi_{\mathcal{E}}
and
S_{\mathcal{E}}
are given by

\phi_{\mathcal{E}}([\mathcal{C}])=[\mathcal{C}_{G}],\qquad S_{\mathcal{E}}([\mathcal{D}])=[\mathcal{E}\boxtimes\mathcal{D}],

where
[\mathcal{C}]\in\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{E})
and
[\mathcal{D}]\in\mathcal{W}
and
\mathcal{E}=\Rep(G)
.

For arbitrary symmetric fusion
\mathcal{E}
, it was proved in
[
23
, Theorem 3.1.3, Remark 3.1.4]
that the group homomorphism
S_{\mathcal{E}}:\mathcal{W}\to\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{E})
is surjective if and only if the minimal extension conjecture is true for non-degenerate fusion categories over
\mathcal{E}
. Moreover, if
\mathcal{C}
admits a minimal extension
\mathcal{D}
, then
[\mathcal{C}]=[\mathcal{E}\boxtimes\mathcal{D}]
. However, Drinfeld’ counterexamples
[
10
]
show that
S_{\mathcal{E}}
is not surjective for general Tannakian
\mathcal{E}
.

Corollary 3.3
.

The map
S_{\mathcal{E}}
is surjective (equivalently, the minimal extension
conjecture holds for all non-degenerate braided fusion categories over
\mathcal{E}
) if and only if

\text{Ker}(\phi_{\mathcal{E}})=0
.

In general,
\text{Ker}(\phi_{\mathcal{E}})
is non-trivial
[
10
]
, hence it is crucial to know the structures of
\text{Ker}(\phi_{\mathcal{E}})
. We begin with the following lemma, which will be used to characterize elements of
\text{Ker}(\phi_{\mathcal{E}})
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
G
be a finite group, and let
\mathcal{C}
be a braided fusion category
equipped with
G
-action. If
A\in\mathcal{C}
is a
G
-equivariant commutative algebra, then there is a well-defined action of
G
on
\mathcal{C}_{A}
(as a monoidal category).

Proof.

It suffices to show
(g(X),m_{g(X)})\in\mathcal{C}_{A}
, where
m_{X}:X\otimes A\to X
is the right
A
-module structure of
X
, and the morphism
m_{g(X)}
is defined as follows:

\displaystyle m_{g(X)}:=g(m_{X})\circ g(\text{id}_{X}\otimes\eta_{g^{-1}})\circ\mu_{g}(X,g^{-1}(A))\circ\text{id}_{g(X)}\otimes(\nu^{A}_{g,g^{-1}})^{-1}.

Here and below we use
g
to denote the tensor functor determined by the homomorphism
\rho:G\to\text{Aut}_{\otimes}^{\text{br}}(\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{B}))
; we write
\nu_{g,h}:g\circ h\overset{\sim}{\to}gh
for the isomorphism making
\rho
into a morphism of 2-groups
and
\mu_{g}(X,Y):g(X)\otimes g(Y)\overset{\sim}{\to}g(X\otimes Y)
for the monoidality of the functor
g
. We furthermore denote by
\eta_{g}:g(A)\overset{\sim}{\to}A
the
G
-equivariance of
A
. Finally, we will write
(A,m_{A},u)
be the algebra structure of
A
, that is,

\displaystyle m_{A}\circ\text{id}_{A}\otimes m_{A}=m_{A}\circ m_{A}\otimes\text{id}_{A},m_{A}\circ\text{id}_{A}\otimes u=\text{id}_{A}=m_{A}\circ u\otimes\text{id}_{A},

and we
omit the associator and unit restriction of fusion category
\mathcal{C}
.

Thus we need to prove the following equations:

m_{g(X)}\circ\text{id}_{X}\otimes u=\text{id}_{g(X)},\qquad m_{g(X)}\circ m_{g(X)}\otimes\text{id}_{A}=m_{g(X)}\circ m_{A}.

(1)

For the first one of above equations, by definition of
m_{g(X)}
,

\displaystyle m_{g(X)}\circ\text{id}_{g(X)}\otimes u

\displaystyle=g(m_{X})\circ g(\text{id}_{X}\otimes\eta_{g^{-1}}(A))\circ\mu(X,g^{-1}(A))\circ\text{id}_{g(X)}\otimes(\nu^{A}_{g,g^{-1}})^{-1}\circ\text{id}_{g(X)}\otimes u

\displaystyle=g(m_{X})\circ g(\text{id}_{X}\otimes\eta_{g^{-1}}(A))\circ\mu(X,g^{-1}(A))\circ\text{id}_{g(X)}\otimes g(g^{-1}(u))\circ\text{id}_{g(X)}\otimes(\nu^{\mathbf{1}}_{g,g^{-1}})^{-1}

\displaystyle=g(m_{X})\circ g(\text{id}_{X}\otimes\eta_{g^{-1}}(A))\circ g(\text{id}_{X}\otimes g^{-1}(u))\mu(X,g^{-1}(\mathbf{1}))\circ\text{id}_{g(X)}\otimes(\nu^{\mathbf{1}}_{g,g^{-1}})^{-1}

\displaystyle=g(m_{X}\circ\text{id}_{X}\otimes u\circ\text{id}_{X}\otimes\eta_{g^{-1}}(\mathbf{1}))\mu(X,g^{-1}(\mathbf{1}))\circ\text{id}_{g(X)}\otimes(\nu^{\mathbf{1}}_{g,g^{-1}})^{-1}

\displaystyle=g(\text{id}_{X}\otimes\eta_{g^{-1}}(\mathbf{1}))\mu(X,g^{-1}(\mathbf{1}))\circ\text{id}_{g(X)}\otimes(\nu^{\mathbf{1}}_{g,g^{-1}})^{-1}

\displaystyle=\mu_{g}(X,\mathbf{1})\circ\text{id}_{g(X)}\otimes g(\eta_{g^{-1}}(\mathbf{1}))\circ\text{id}_{g(X)}\otimes(\nu^{\mathbf{1}}_{g,g^{-1}})^{-1}

\displaystyle=\mu_{g}(X,\mathbf{1})\circ\text{id}_{g(X)}\otimes\eta_{g}^{-1}=\text{id}_{g(X)}.

Here, the second equality is by naturality of
\nu_{g,g^{-1}}
, the third and the sixth equalities are by naturality of
\mu_{g}
, the forth equality is by naturality of
\eta_{g^{-1}}
, and the seventh equality follows as the simple object
\mathbf{1}
is a
G
-equivariant object. The second equation can be proved similarly.
∎

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a non-degenerate fusion category over a Tannakian fusion category
\mathcal{E}=\Rep(G)
. Then the following are equivalent:

(1).

[\mathcal{C}]=[\mathcal{E}]
.

(2).

\mathcal{C}\cong\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{E})
, where
\mathcal{A}
is a fusion category over
\mathcal{E}
.

(3).

\mathcal{C}
contains a connected étale algebra
A
such that
\FPdim(\mathcal{C})=\FPdim(\mathcal{E})\FPdim(A)^{2}
and
A\cap\mathcal{E}=\mathbf{1}
.

(4).

\mathcal{C}_{G}\cong\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{B})
contains a
G
-equivariant Lagrangian algebra
A
.

Proof.

The first two conditions are equivalent by the definition of Witt equivalence. We only need to prove they are equivalent to the third and fourth conditions.

(3)\Rightarrow(1)
. Assume that
\mathcal{C}
contains such a connected étale algebra
A
. Then
[
6
, Corollary 4.6]
says that
\mathcal{C}\boxtimes_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathcal{C}_{A}^{0})^{\text{rev}}\cong\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C}_{A},\mathcal{E})
as braided fusion category, notice that
\mathcal{C}_{A}^{0}
is also a non-degenerate fusion category over
\mathcal{E}
and
\FPdim(\mathcal{C}_{A}^{0})=\frac{\FPdim(\mathcal{C})}{\FPdim(A)^{2}}=\FPdim(\mathcal{E})
. Therefore,

\displaystyle(\mathcal{C}_{A}^{0})^{\text{rev}}\cong\mathcal{C}_{A}^{0}\cong\mathcal{E}

as symmetric fusion category. Hence, we have the following braided tensor equivalences of fusion categories

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C}_{A},\mathcal{E})\cong\mathcal{C}\boxtimes_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathcal{C}_{A}^{0})^{\text{rev}}\cong\mathcal{C}\boxtimes_{\mathcal{E}}\mathcal{E}^{\text{rev}}\cong\mathcal{C},

which then implies
[\mathcal{C}]=[\mathcal{E}]
by definition.

(1)\Rightarrow(3)
. If
\mathcal{C}\cong\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{E})
, where
\mathcal{A}
is a fusion category over
\mathcal{E}
; in particular,
\frac{\FPdim(\mathcal{A})^{2}}{\FPdim(\mathcal{E})}=\FPdim(\mathcal{C})
. Let
F_{1}
be the restriction of forgetful functor
F:\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{A})\to\mathcal{A}
on
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{E})
and let
\mathcal{I}_{1}
be its right adjoint functor. Since
\mathcal{E}
is mapped fully faithful into
\mathcal{A}
via forgetful functor
F
,
F_{1}
is surjective by
[
6
, Theorem 3.12]
. Notice that
F_{1}
obviously admits a central tensor functor structure, so

4.1
Definitions

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a semisimple
\mathds{k}
-linear category equipped with
\mathds{k}
-bilinear binary tensor product
\otimes
; we assume the data of associativity isomorphisms
(-\otimes-)\otimes-\cong-\otimes(-\otimes-)
, but we allow the pentagon axiom to be violated. We do assume the existence of the unit object defined as in
[
12
, 2.1]
— this is an object
{\bf 1}\in\mathcal{C}
equipped with isomorphism
\iota:{\bf 1}\otimes{\bf 1}\simeq{\bf 1}
and such that the functors
X\mapsto X\otimes{\bf 1}
and
X\mapsto{\bf 1}\otimes X
are equivalences — and for convenience we will assume that
{\bf 1}
is simple.

Assume furthermore that the category

\mathcal{C}
is faithfully graded by a group
G
,
\mathcal{C}=\oplus_{g\in G}\mathcal{C}_{g}
compatibly with the tensor product. Thus for any simple object
X\in\mathcal{C}
we have
a well defined element
\deg(X)\in G
and for any simple direct summand
Z
of
X\otimes Y
we have

\deg(Z)=\deg(X)\deg(Y)
. The faithfulness means that for any
g\in G
there exists at least one simple
object
X
with
\deg(X)=g
.

Definition 4.1
.

The category
\mathcal{C}
as above is called
G
-quasi-monoidal if for all simple objects
X,Y,Z,T\in\mathcal{C}
the pentagon diagram commutes up to a scalar and this scalar depends only on
\deg(X),\deg(Y),\deg(Z),\deg(T)\in G
.

Remark 4.2
.

The terminology is motivated by notion of
(G,\phi)
-quasiassociative algebra, see
[
1
]
.

Question 4.3
.

Assume that a semisimple category
\mathcal{C}
is equipped with tensor product and associativity isomorphisms such that the pentagon diagram commutes up to a scalar for any quadruple of simple objects.
Is such category
G
-quasi-monoidal for some group
G
?

There is an obvious notion of equivalence of
G
-quasi-monoidal categories: two such categories
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
are equivalent if there is an equivalence
F:\mathcal{C}\simeq\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
preserving the grading and equipped with quasi-tensor structure (i.e. isomorphisms
F(X\otimes Y)\simeq F(X)\otimes F(Y)
) intertwining the associativity isomorphisms in both categories.

Let
\nu=\nu(\mathcal{C}):G^{4}\to\mathds{k}^{\times}
be the scalar which appears in the definition
4.1
(so this is a function of 4 variables taking values in
G
). Note that
\nu(\mathcal{C})
is uniquely determined by the choice of associativity isomorphisms in
\mathcal{C}
since we assumed that the
G
-grading is faithful. By definition
\nu(\mathcal{C})
is a 4-cochain on group
G
with values in
\mathds{k}^{\times}
.
Considering the associahedron diagram (see e.g.
[
3
, Appendix B]
) we get

Proposition 4.4
.

The cochain
\nu(\mathcal{C})
is 4-cocycle.

Sketch of proof:
the vertices of the associahedron
K_{5}
can be labeled by 5-fold tensor products
and the edges are given by various associativity maps, e.g.

X\otimes((Y\otimes Z)\otimes(T\otimes U))\to X\otimes(Y\otimes(Z\otimes(T\otimes U))).

We consider such diagram where
X,Y,Z,T,U
are simple objects of a
G
-quasi-monoidal category.
The faces are either quadrangles which commute due to naturality of the associativity morphisms or pentagons
which commute up to a scalar by Definition
4.1
. Thus we get a relation between scalars
appearing in Definition
4.1
; one verifies that this relation says that the coboundary of
\nu(\mathcal{C})

is trivial.
\square

Thus we will call
\nu(\mathcal{C})
the
pentagonicity defect cocycle
of
\mathcal{C}
.

Example 4.5
.

Assume
G
is trivial group and let
\mathcal{C}
be a

-quasi-monoidal category. Then each pentagon diagram with simple

commutes up to a scalar

which is the same for all

Remark 4.7

Consider the cyclic subgroup

5.3
(i) there is
c^{\prime}\in Ch(\mathcal{C}^{\text{rev}})
such that
\tilde{\eta}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathcal{C}^{\text{rev}},c^{\prime})=\tilde{\eta}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathcal{C},c_{1})^{-1}
. Thus by Lemma
5.3
(ii) there is
e\in Ch(\mathcal{C}\boxtimes_{\mathcal{E}}\mathcal{C}^{\text{rev}})
such that

\tilde{\eta}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathcal{C}\boxtimes_{\mathcal{E}}\mathcal{C}^{\text{rev}},e)=\tilde{\eta}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathcal{C},c_{2})\tilde{\eta}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathcal{C}^{\text{rev}},c^{\prime})=\tilde{\eta}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathcal{C},c_{2})\tilde{\eta}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathcal{C},c_{1})^{-1}.

On the other hand the category
\mathcal{C}\boxtimes_{\mathcal{E}}\mathcal{C}^{\text{rev}}\in M(\mathcal{E})^{G\text{-tr}}
(by definition of the inverse element in
\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{E})
). Thus by Lemma
5.4
we have
that
\tilde{\eta}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathcal{C}\boxtimes_{\mathcal{E}}\mathcal{C}^{\text{rev}},e)
is a coboundary. Hence the cohomology classes of
\tilde{\eta}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathcal{C},c_{1})
and
\tilde{\eta}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathcal{C},c_{2})
coincide as claimed.
∎

Thus there is a well defined class
\bar{\eta}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathcal{C})\in\homology^{4}(G,\mathds{k}^{\times})
such that
\bar{\eta}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathcal{C},c)=\bar{\eta}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathcal{C})
for any
c\in Ch(\mathcal{C})
.

Corollary 5.6
.

The map
\bar{\eta}_{\mathcal{E}}:M(\mathcal{E})^{\text{tr}}\to\homology^{4}(G,\mathds{k}^{\times})
satisfies the following:

(i)

For any
\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}\in M(\mathcal{E})^{\text{tr}}
we have
\bar{\eta}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathcal{C}\boxtimes_{\mathcal{E}}\mathcal{D})=\bar{\eta}_{E}(\mathcal{C})\bar{\eta}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathcal{D})
;

(ii)

For any
\mathcal{C}\in M(\mathcal{E})^{G\text{-tr}}
we have
\bar{\eta}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathcal{C})=0
.

Proof.

(i) is immediate from Lemma
5.3
(ii) and (ii) follows from Lemma
5.4
.
∎

It follows from the definition of
\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{E})
that there is a unique homomorphism
\eta_{\mathcal{E}}:\operatorname{Ker}(\phi_{\mathcal{E}})\to\homology^{4}(G,\mathds{k}^{\times})
such that
\eta_{\mathcal{E}}([\mathcal{C}])=\bar{\eta}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathcal{C})
.

We have a converse to Lemma
5.4
:

Lemma 5.7
.

Assume
\bar{\eta}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathcal{C})=0
. Then
\mathcal{C}\in M(\mathcal{E})^{G\text{-tr}}
. Equivalently, the homomorphism
\eta_{\mathcal{E}}
is injective.

Proof.

Since
\bar{\eta}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathcal{C})=0
, for some choice
c\in\text{Ch}(\mathcal{C})
the resulting
G
-quasi-monoidal category
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}

has trivial pentagonicity defect cocycle, so it is a
G
-graded fusion category. Then it follows from
[
16
, Theorem 3.5]
that
\mathcal{C}
is a braided fusion subcategory of
\mathcal{Z}(\tilde{\mathcal{A}})
with Müger center being
\mathcal{E}
, so
\mathcal{C}\cong\mathcal{Z}(\tilde{\mathcal{A}},\mathcal{E})
as claimed.
∎

As explained in
[
18
]
the main idea of the proof of the next proposition is contained in
[
24
]
; a version of similar argument involving
\homology^{3}(G,\mathds{k}^{\times})
is contained in
[
15
]
.

Proposition 5.8
.

The map
\eta_{E}:\operatorname{Ker}(\phi_{\mathcal{E}})\to\homology^{4}(G,\mathds{k}^{\times})
is surjective.

Proof.

For a class
\omega\in\homology^{4}(G,\mathds{k}^{\times})
let
\nu\in Z^{4}(G,\mathds{k}^{\times})
be a representing cocycle. Let
\tilde{G}
be a finite group with a surjective homomorphism
p:\tilde{G}\to G
such that
p^{*}\omega=0
, see
[
22
]
. Thus we can pick a cochain
\psi\in C^{3}(\tilde{G},\mathds{k}^{\times})
such that
p^{*}\nu=\partial\psi
.

Consider the category
\mathrm{Vec}_{\tilde{G}}
with the usual tensor product (so tensor product of simple objects labeled by
g,h\in\tilde{G}
is labeled by
gh
). Let us use
\psi
as an associativity isomorphism for this category and let
us use
p
to define
G
-grading on this category (so a simple object labeled by
g\in\tilde{G}
has degree

p(g)\in G
). The resulting category is
G
-quasi-monoidal category with pentagonicity defect cocycle
\nu
.
Thus we proved that
\omega
is in the image of
\eta_{\mathcal{E}}
.
∎

Combining Lemma
5.7
and Proposition
5.8
we get

Theorem 5.9
.

The map
\eta_{\mathcal{E}}:\operatorname{Ker}(\phi_{\mathcal{E}})\to\homology^{4}(G,\mathds{k}^{\times})
is an isomorphism. ∎

Combining this with Corollary
3.3
we get the following

Corollary 5.10
.

The minimal extension conjecture holds for all braided fusion categories
over Tannakian category
\mathcal{E}=\Rep(G)
if and only if
\homology^{4}(G,\mathds{k}^{\times})=0
. ∎

Theorem
3.1
and Theorem
5.9
together imply

Theorem 5.11
.

Let
\mathcal{E}=\Rep(G)
be Tannakian. Then
\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{E})\cong\mathcal{W}\oplus\homology^{4}(G,\mathds{k}^{\times})
. ∎

Remark 5.12
.

One verifies that the projection
\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{E})\cong\mathcal{W}\oplus H^{4}(G,\mathds{k}^{\times})\to H^{4}(G,\mathds{k}^{\times})

can be described as follows: the class
[\mathcal{C}]\in\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{E})
is sent to the cohomology class of the cocycle

\hat{\eta}(\mathcal{C})
from Remark
5.2
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a braided fusion category over a symmetric fusion category
\mathcal{E}
. For any
n\geq 1
, denote

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{\boxtimes_{\mathcal{E}}^{n}}:=\overbrace{\mathcal{C}\boxtimes_{\mathcal{E}}\cdots\boxtimes_{\mathcal{E}}\mathcal{C}}^{n},

We have the following result on the minimal extension of braided fusion categories.

Corollary 5.13
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an arbitrary non-degenerate fusion category over
\mathcal{E}=\Rep(G)
, then there exists a positive integer
n
such that
\mathcal{C}^{\boxtimes_{\mathcal{E}}^{n}}
admits a minimal extension.
In fact we can take
n=|G|
.

Proof.

[
23
, Theorem 3.2]
states that a category
\mathcal{C}\in M(\mathcal{E})
admits a minimal extension if and only if

[\mathcal{C}]\in\mathcal{W}_{E}
is in the image of homomorphism
S_{\mathcal{E}}
. Now
Theorem
3.1
implies that the image of
S_{\mathcal{E}}
is precisely the kernel of projection

\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{E})\to\operatorname{Ker}(\phi_{\mathcal{E}})\simeq\homology^{4}(G,\mathds{k}^{*})
. It is well known that all elements
of
\homology^{4}(G,\mathds{k}^{\times})
are torsion of order dividing
|G|
. Then the result follows since

\displaystyle[\mathcal{C}^{\boxtimes_{\mathcal{E}}^{n}}]=[\mathcal{C}]^{n}.

∎

6
Group homomorphism
\varphi_{\mathcal{E}}:\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{E})\to s\mathcal{W}

In this section, let
\mathcal{E}=\text{Rep}(\widetilde{G},z)
be a super-Tannakian fusion category, where
z\in\widetilde{G}
is a non-trivial central element of order
2
, and
z
acts as a parity automorphism on
\text{Rep}(\widetilde{G})
. Then
\mathcal{E}
contains a maximal Tannakian fusion subcategory
\Rep(G)
, where
G:=\widetilde{G}/\langle z\rangle
. Moreover, we have the following commutative diagram of Witt groups

\textstyle{\mathcal{W}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{S_{\mathcal{E}}}
\scriptstyle{S}
\textstyle{\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{E})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\varphi_{\mathcal{E}}}
\textstyle{s\mathcal{W}}

where
S:=S_{\text{sVec}}:\mathcal{W}\to s\mathcal{W}
,
S([\mathcal{C}])=[\mathcal{C}\boxtimes\mathrm{sVec}]
and
\varphi_{\mathcal{E}}([\mathcal{D}])=[\mathcal{D}_{G}]
for any
[\mathcal{C}]\in\mathcal{W}
,
[\mathcal{D}]\in\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{E})
. Thus
\varphi_{\mathcal{E}}
is surjective as
S
is surjective by
[
18
]
. Notice that
\operatorname{Ker}(S_{\mathcal{E}})
is the subgroup generated by Witt equivalence classes of minimal extensions of
\mathcal{E}
and
\operatorname{Ker}(S)=\mathcal{W}_{\text{Ising}}=\mathbb{Z}_{16}

[
6
]
, where
\mathcal{W}_{\text{Ising}}\subseteq\mathcal{W}
is the subgroup generated by Witt equivalence class
[\mathcal{I}]
of an Ising category
\mathcal{I}
. Hence,
\operatorname{Ker}(S_{\mathcal{E}})
is a subgroup of
\mathbb{Z}_{16}
.

Question 6.1
.

Can we give a complete characterization the order of
\operatorname{Ker}(S_{\mathcal{E}})
for an arbitrary super-Tannakian category
\mathcal{E}
?

Note that we have an exact sequence of groups

\displaystyle 1\to\operatorname{Ker}(\varphi_{\mathcal{E}})\to\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{E})\overset{\varphi_{\mathcal{E}}}{\rightarrow}s\mathcal{W}\to 1.

Hence the subgroup
\operatorname{Ker}(\varphi_{\mathcal{E}})
is crucial in determining the structure of
\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{E})
. By definition,
\varphi_{\mathcal{E}}([\mathcal{C}])=[\text{sVec}]
if and only if
\mathcal{C}_{G}\cong\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{A},\text{sVec})
, where
\mathcal{A}
is a fusion category over
sVec
and
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{A},\text{sVec})
is the centralizer of
\mathrm{sVec}
in
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{A})
.

Proposition 6.2
.

If
\mathcal{E}\cong\text{sVec}\boxtimes\Rep(G)
, then there is a group homomorphism
\psi_{\mathcal{E}}:s\mathcal{W}\to\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{E})
such that
\varphi_{\mathcal{E}}\circ\psi_{\mathcal{E}}=\text{id}
. Hence
\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{E})\cong s\mathcal{W}\oplus\operatorname{Ker}(\varphi_{\mathcal{E}})
.

Proof.

Denote
\mathcal{E}_{2}:=\Rep(G)
. For any elements
[\mathcal{C}],[\mathcal{D}]\in s\mathcal{W}
, by definition, let
A_{1},A_{2}
be the connected étale algebras such that
(\text{sVec}\boxtimes\text{sVec})_{A_{1}}\cong\text{sVec}
and
(\mathcal{E}_{2}\boxtimes\mathcal{E}_{2})_{A_{2}}\cong\mathcal{E}_{2}
, respectively. Then,
(\mathcal{E}\boxtimes\mathcal{E})_{A}\cong\mathcal{E}
, where
A:=A_{1}\boxtimes A_{2}
and
\mathcal{C}\boxtimes_{\text{sVec}}\mathcal{D}=(\mathcal{C}\boxtimes\mathcal{D})_{A_{1}}
,

\displaystyle((\mathcal{C}\boxtimes_{\text{sVec}}\mathcal{D})\boxtimes\mathcal{E}_{2})\boxtimes_{\mathcal{E}_{2}}\mathcal{E}_{2}

\displaystyle=(((\mathcal{C}\boxtimes_{\text{sVec}}\mathcal{D})\boxtimes\mathcal{E}_{2})\boxtimes\mathcal{E}_{2})_{A_{2}},

\displaystyle(\mathcal{C}\boxtimes\mathcal{E}_{2})\boxtimes_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathcal{D}\boxtimes\mathcal{E}_{2})

\displaystyle=(\mathcal{C}\boxtimes\mathcal{E}_{2}\boxtimes\mathcal{D}\boxtimes\mathcal{E}_{2})_{A}.

We define
\psi_{\mathcal{E}}([\mathcal{C}])=[\mathcal{C}\boxtimes\mathcal{E}_{2}]
,
[\mathcal{C}]\in s\mathcal{W}
. Obviously,
\psi_{\mathcal{E}}
is well-defined and

\displaystyle\varphi_{\mathcal{E}}(\psi_{\mathcal{E}}([\mathcal{C}]))=\varphi_{\mathcal{E}}([\mathcal{C}\boxtimes\mathcal{E}_{2}])=[(\mathcal{C}\boxtimes\mathcal{E}_{2})_{G}]=[\mathcal{C}].

Note that étale algebras
A_{1},A_{2}
are centralizing each other. Then for
[\mathcal{C}],[\mathcal{D}]\in s\mathcal{W}
,

\displaystyle\psi_{\mathcal{E}}([\mathcal{C}])\psi_{\mathcal{E}}([\mathcal{D}])

\displaystyle=[(\mathcal{C}\boxtimes\mathcal{E}_{2})\boxtimes_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathcal{D}\boxtimes\mathcal{E}_{2})]

\displaystyle=[(\mathcal{C}\boxtimes\mathcal{E}_{2}\boxtimes\mathcal{D}\boxtimes\mathcal{E}_{2})_{A}]

\displaystyle=[((\mathcal{C}\boxtimes\mathcal{E}_{2}\boxtimes\mathcal{D}\boxtimes\mathcal{E}_{2})_{A_{2}})_{A_{1}}]

\displaystyle=[(\mathcal{C}\boxtimes\mathcal{D}\boxtimes\mathcal{E}_{2})_{A_{1}}]

\displaystyle=[\mathcal{C}\boxtimes_{\text{sVec}}(\mathcal{D}\boxtimes\mathcal{E}_{2})]

\displaystyle=[(\mathcal{C}\boxtimes_{\text{sVec}}\mathcal{D})\boxtimes\mathcal{E}_{2}]=\psi_{\mathcal{E}}([\mathcal{C}][\mathcal{D}]).

this finishes the proof of the proposition.
∎

However, an arbitrary super-Tannakian fusion category
\mathcal{E}
cannot be factored as a Deligne tensor product
\text{sVec}\boxtimes\Rep(G)
, and thus the morphism
\psi_{\mathcal{E}}:s\mathcal{W}\to\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{E})
is not well-defined. We end this section by proposing the following questions.

Question 6.3

\mathcal{E}\ncong\mathrm{sVec}

Generic and Isometric Embeddings in Reservoir Computers
Allen Hart

Abstract
We prove that a generic reservoir system admits a generalized synchronization that is a topological embedding of the input system’s attractor. We also prove that for sufficiently high reservoir dimension (given by Nash’s embedding theorem) there exists an isometric embedding generalized synchronization. The isometric embedding can be constructed explicitly when the reservoir system is linear.

Generic Embeddings

First, we will define a reservoir system.

Definition 2.1
.

(Reservoir System) Let
M
be a smooth

\phi\in\text{Diff}^{1}(M)

\omega\in C^{1}(M,\mathbb{R})

\displaystyle\Phi(m,x)=(\phi(m),F(x,\omega(m))).

Under certain conditions, the reservoir system has a (not necessarily unique

Definition 2.2

(Generalized Synchronization) A reservoir system

\displaystyle G=\{(m,f(m))\ |\ m\in\mathcal{A}\}

\Phi(G)=G

\displaystyle\Phi^{k}(m_{0},x_{0})\xrightarrow[k\to\infty]{}(\phi^{k}(m_{0}),f\phi^{k}(m_{0})).

Definition 2.3

For a given reservoir system
\Phi:M\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\to M\times\mathbb{R}^{N}

Theorem 2.4

(Whitney

Now we are ready to state and prove the major theorem - that for generic reservoir maps that have a GS, the associated GS is an embedding. The idea behind the proof is to define a continuous open mapping

Theorem 2.5

Suppose that
\phi\in\mathrm{Diff}^{2}(M)

Let

The periodic-orbit non-degeneracy assumption (distinct eigenvalues for each

Theorem

There is some analysis of embedding GS in continuous time in

4
Isometric Embeddings

Having established that, generically, an embedding GS exists, we now move on to consider under what conditions an isometric embedding GS exists.
An isometric embedding preserves both topological properties and geometrical properties, including lengths, angles, and curvatures.

Definition 4.1
.

(Isometric embedding) Let
g
be a Riemannian metric on the manifold
M
. A map
f\in C^{1}(M,\mathbb{R}^{N})
is an isometric embedding of
\mathcal{A}\subset M
if it is an embedding of
\mathcal{A}
and if

\displaystyle D_{m}fv\cdot D_{m}fw=g_{m}(v,w)\qquad\forall v,w\in T_{m}M,\quad\forall m\in\mathcal{A}.

The celebrated Nash-embedding Theorem states that for large enough
N
any manifold
M
can be isometrically embedded into
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

Theorem 4.2
.

(Nash-Günther) Let
(M,g)
be a Riemannian manifold of dimension
q
. Then for

\displaystyle N\geq\max(q(q+5)/2,q(q+3)/2+5)

(1)

there exists an isometric embedding
\iota\in C^{1}(M,\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

We will use Theorem
4.2
to prove that for sufficiently large reservoir dimension
N
there exists a stable reservoir map
F
that has an isometric embedding GS.

Theorem 4.3
.

Suppose that
\phi\in\mathrm{Diff}^{2}(M)
has finitely many periodic orbits in
\mathcal{A}
. Suppose that for each periodic orbit
m\in\mathcal{A}
with period
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the derivative
T_{m}\phi^{-n}
has
q
distinct eigenvalues.
Let

and suppose

satisfies the Nash–Günther bound of Theorem

, i.e.

N\;\geq\;\max\!\big\{\,q(q+5)/2,\;q(q+3)/2+5\,\big\}.

Then for generic observation function
\omega\in C^{1}(M,\mathbb{R}^{N})
there exists a reservoir map
F\in\mathcal{F}
whose generalized synchronization

\iota\in C^{1}(M,\mathbb{R}^{N})
is an isometric embedding of
\mathcal{A}\subset M
.

Proof.

By Theorem
4.2
, there exists an isometric embedding
\iota:M\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
By Theorem
2.5
, there exists a reservoir map
F\in\mathcal{F}
with embedding GS

Let

be a diffeomorphism defined on a neighborhood of

Define the conjugated reservoir map

Then the reservoir system

\displaystyle\Phi^{*}(m,x)=(\phi(m),F^{*}(x,\omega(m)))

∎

In the special case of the linear reservoir system where

\displaystyle F(x,z)=Ax+Cz

To establish isometry we need to show that

So we start with

\displaystyle D_{m}f=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}A^{k}CD_{m}\omega T_{m}\phi^{-k}

\displaystyle A^{*}

\displaystyle=(PQR)^{-1}C.

Now let

\displaystyle u=\sum_{i=1}^{q}\alpha_{i}w_{i},\qquad y=\sum_{i=1}^{q}\beta_{i}w_{i}

In the case

The full-rank condition on the matrix

It is notable that the isometric form constructed above does not depend on the specific values of

6
Conclusion

Generalized synchronization (GS) is the fundamental mechanism that allows
reservoir systems to learn and represent dynamical systems.
When the GS is an embedding, the reservoir state space provides a faithful
reconstruction of the geometry of the underlying attractor, making possible the
recovery of dynamical invariants and topological features.
Our first main result shows that such embedding GS maps occur generically,
formalizing what has been widely observed empirically in reservoir computing.

Going further, we established that isometric embeddings can also arise in
reservoir systems. Isometric GS maps not only preserve topology, but also the
metric structure of the source dynamics, including angles and lengths.
In the linear case, we showed that every embedding reservoir can be conjugated
to an isometric one, and that this construction is robust to the choice of
eigenvalues of the underlying system. This reveals a stronger structural
property of reservoirs: they can, in principle, represent the geometry of
dynamical systems without distortion. Future work could investigate continuous-time analogues of these results and explore how isometric GS maps might improve learning, stability, and invariant discovery in practical reservoir computing applications.

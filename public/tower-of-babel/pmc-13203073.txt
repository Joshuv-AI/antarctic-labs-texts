Plain language summary of five-year overall survival update from the HIMALAYA study of tremelimumab plus durvalumab in unresectable hepatocellular carcinoma (liver cancer)
Authors: Lorenza Rimassa, Stephen L Chan, Bruno Sangro, George Lau, Masatoshi Kudo, Maria Reig, Valeriy Breder, Min-Hee Ryu, Yurii Ostapenko, Wattana Sukeepaisarnjaroen, Maria Varela, David Tougeron, Oxana V Crysler, Mohamed Bouattour, Tu Van Dao, Vincent C Tam, Adilson Faccio, Junji Furuse, Long-Bin Jeng, Yoon Koo Kang, Robin K Kelley, John F Kurland, Kavitha Balaji, Ghassan K Abou-Alfa
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Plain Language Summary What is this summary about? This summary describes the 5-year follow-up results from the HIMALAYA study in people with unresectable hepatocellular carcinoma (uHCC), a liver cancer that doctors cannot remove by surgery. The study compared 3 first-line therapies: STRIDE, durvalumab alone and sorafenib.The main goal of this summary is to describe how long people lived (overall survival) when followed for approximately 5 years after the start of treatment with STRIDE, durvalumab alone or sorafenib (median follow-up duration: 62.5 months, 62.7 months and 59.9 months, respectively), and how long people lived based on the extent of tumour shrinkage. This summary also describes the updated safety of these treatments. What were the results of this 5-year follow-up study? Overall survival: STRIDE reduced the risk of death versus sorafenib. At 5 years, 19.6% of participants treated with STRIDE were alive versus 9.4% of those treated with sorafenib. At 5 years, survival with durvalumab treatment alone (14.4%) was similar to sorafenib (9.4%).Overall survival and its link to changes in tumour size in response to treatments: Participants taking STRIDE whose tumours shrank or did not grow generally had improved survival; overall, participants had a better chance of survival at 5 years the more their tumours shrank. Some participants achieved disease control; among them, those receiving STRIDE had higher 5-year survival (28.7%) than with sorafenib (12.7%). In some participants receiving STRIDE, tumours appeared to grow at first but later became smaller. Safety: Doctors managed STRIDE side effects with available treatment guidelines. Importantly, researchers found no new serious treatment-related side effects – such as death, life-threatening situations or hospitalisation – since the HIMALAYA study’s 3-year results. What do the results mean for people with uHCC? STRIDE set a new 5-year standard for survival in participants with uHCC, with about 1 in 5 participants alive at 5 years – twice as many compared with sorafenib. Participants achieving disease control treated with STRIDE were more likely to live longer. Participants treated with STRIDE tended to live longer when their tumours shrank. The 5-year survival benefit and manageable side effects of STRIDE in the HIMALAYA study continue to support its use in people with uHCC.Clinical trial number: NCT03298451KEYWORDS: STRIDE, dual immune checkpoint inhibitor regimen, five-year overall survival, tremelimumab, durvalumab, HCC, plain language summary

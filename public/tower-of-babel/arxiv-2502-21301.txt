Relative Compactified Prym and Picard fibrations associated to very good cubic fourfolds
Yajnaseni Dutta, Lisa Marquand

Abstract
. A very good cubic fourfold is a smooth cubic fourfold that does not contain a plane, a cubic scroll, or a hyperplane section with a corank 3 singularity. We prove that the normalization of the relative compactified Prym variety associated to the universal family of hyperplanes of a very good cubic fourfold is in fact smooth, thereby extending prior results of Laza, Saccà and Voisin. Using a similar argument, we also prove the smoothness of the normalization of the relative compactified Picard of the associated relative Fano variety of relative lines.



1.
The introduction

Let
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{5}
be a smooth cubic fourfold, and let
p\colon\mathcal{Y}\rightarrow B\coloneqq(\mathbb{P}^{5})^{\vee}
denote the universal family of hyperplane sections.
Since the intermediate Jacobian of a smooth cubic threefold is a (principally polarized) Abelian variety, one may wonder whether akin to the case of relative compactified Jacobian of curves, such a compactified intermediate Jacobian family exists over
B
. This was first discussed in
[
DM96
]
.

Let
U_{1}\coloneqq B\setminus\mathrm{Sing}(X^{\vee})
be the open subset parametrising hyperplane sections of
X
with at most one node, and
\pi_{U_{1}}\colon J_{U_{1}}\rightarrow U_{1}
the fibration obtained by Donagi and Markman
[
DM96
]
by considering a version of the relative intermediate Jacobians.
The fibration
\pi_{U_{1}}\colon J_{U_{1}}\rightarrow U_{1}
is, moreover, a Lagrangian fibration: i.e. there exists a unique holomorphic symplectic
2
-form
\sigma_{U_{1}}
on
J_{U_{1}}
, such that
\sigma_{U_{1}}|_{J_{U_{1},b}}=0
for all
b\in U_{1}
.
It is a natural question to ask whether there exists a hyperkähler compactification
\overline{J}
of
J_{U_{1}}
admitting a Lagrangian fibration with irreducible fibers extending
\pi_{U_{1}}
.
Recall that hyperkähler manifolds are simply connected even dimensional smooth complex projective varieties that admit a nondegenerate global holomorphic 2-form. It is expected that every hyperkähler manifold admits a deformation with a Lagrangian fibration. Such a fibration mimics many properties enjoyed by elliptic fibrations of minimal smooth projective surfaces. Furthermore, any non-trivial fibration of a hyperkähler manifold is Lagrangian, i.e. the 2-form vanishes when restricted to smooth points of the fibers.

In
[
LSV17
]
, the authors construct a smooth hyperkähler compactification
\pi\colon\overline{J}\rightarrow B
of
J_{U_{1}}
such that
\pi
has irreducible fibers, under the assumption that
X
is
general
.
Their construction relies on passing to Mumford’s Prym variety interpretation of the intermediate Jacobian of a smooth cubic threefold and compactifying the Jacobian as the moduli of rank one torsion free sheaves.
Without the assumption of the generality of
X
, it is not clear whether their construction would result in a smooth compactification (see
[
LSV17
, Section 3]
). We prove:

Theorem 1
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{5}
be a very good cubic fourfold.
Then there exists a smooth projective hyperkähler compactification
\overline{J}
of
J_{U_{1}}
with a Lagrangian fibration
\pi\colon\overline{J}\rightarrow B
extending
\pi_{U_{1}}
, such that
the fibers of
\pi
are compactified Prym varieties of reduced, irreducible planar curves. In particular, the fibers are irreducible.

A
very good cubic fourfold
is a smooth cubic fourfold such that every hyperplane section
Y\coloneqq X\cap H
has a very good line; existence of these is essential for passing to the Prym construction.
A line
\ell\subset Y
in a cubic threefold
Y
is
very good
if the associated conic bundle obtained by
{\rm Bl}_{\ell}Y\to\mathbb{P}^{2}
has at worst nodal fibers and both the discriminant curve
C\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}
and its étale double cover
\widetilde{C}
are irreducible.

Under the assumption that
X
is a very good cubic fourfold, one can apply the construction of
[
LSV17
]
to obtain a compactification
\overline{P}
of
J_{U_{1}}
. Unfortunately, the techniques of
[
LSV17
]
used to prove smoothness of
\overline{P}
fail in this more general set up. Thus
Theorem

1
should be seen as an extension of that of
[
LSV17
]
, as it extends their construction to a wider subset of cubic fourfolds.

In
[
Sac23
]
, Saccà proves the existence of a smooth hyperkähler Lagrangian compactification for
any
smooth cubic
X
via MMP techniques, at the expense of losing the explicit geometry of special fibers;
for example, whether they are irreducible or not, is not clear.

Note that by
[
MV24
, Theorem 1.5]
, assuming
X
is a very good cubic fourfold is equivalent to assuming that
X
does not contain a plane, a rational normal cubic scroll, or have a hyperplane section with a corank 3 singularity.
In fact, if
X
contains a plane or a cubic scroll, any smooth hyperkähler compactification
\pi\colon\overline{J}\rightarrow B
will have reducible fibers
[
MV24
, Theorem 1.2]
,
[
Bro18
, Corollary 1.8]
,
[
Sac23
, Remark 3.12]
. Therefore, our result in fact shows the following

Corollary 1
.

Let
X
be any smooth cubic fourfold, such that none of its hyperplane sections have a corank three singularity. Then a smooth hyperkähler Lagrangian compactification with integral fibers of the relative intermediate Jacobian
J_{U_{1}}\to U_{1}
exists if and only if
X
is a very good cubic fourfold.

The next result is the crucial technical input that goes into proving
Theorem

1
, which is interesting in its own right.

Proposition 3a
.

Let
X
be a very good cubic fourfold.
Let
\overline{P}\to B
be the relative compactified Prym associated to the universal hyperplane sections of
X
.
The normalization
\overline{J}
of
\overline{P}
is a symplectic variety and has
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial terminal singularities.

Theorem

1
follows from
3a
by applying a result of Greb–Lehn–Rollenske
[
GLR13
, Proposition 6.5]
, that states that any terminal
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial symplectic variety that is birational to a smooth hyperkähler manifold is smooth itself. Indeed,
\overline{J}
is birational to a smooth hyperkähler manifold
M
, constructed using desingularising
\overline{P}
and running a relative MMP, as was done in
[
Sac24
]
(later we call this the MMP model).
The last claims of
Theorem

1
follow from the construction of
\overline{P}.

One can prove a variant of
Theorem

1
by replacing the relative compactified Prym with a compactification of the scheme of relative degree 0 line bundles on the relative Fano variety of lines of the universal hyperplane section. This is given in
Corollary

2
below. To prove this, we have a result analogous to
3a
:

Proposition 3b
.

Let
X
be a very good cubic fourfold.
Let
f\colon\overline{\mathrm{Pic}}^{0}_{\mathcal{F}/B}\to B
be the Picard model as described in §
3
, where
\mathcal{F}\to B
is the relative Fano variety of lines associated to the universal hyperplane sections of
X
.
The normalization of
\overline{\mathrm{Pic}}^{0}_{\mathcal{F}/B}
is a symplectic variety and has
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial terminal singularities.

With the same argument as
Theorem

1
, we obtain:

Corollary 2
.

The normalization of
\overline{\mathrm{Pic}}^{0}_{\mathcal{F}/B}
is a smooth hyperkähler manifold birational to
\overline{J}
.

2.
The new input

Our main new input is
Proposition

4
, and its analogue
Proposition

6
for relative
\mathrm{Pic}^{0}
of the Fano variety. They use the results from
[
DMS24
]
. These results allow us to identify the sections of the relative compactified Prym
\overline{P}
with the sections of a relative intermediate Jacobian sheaf
\mathcal{J}
.
We use the sections to calculate the number of linearly independent Cartier divisors in
\overline{P}
(see
Proposition

5
).

3.
The models

[
LSV17

4.
The Prym model

In order to prove
3a
, we will first analyze the sections of the fibration
\pi\colon\overline{P}\rightarrow B
. When
\overline{P}
is smooth, this was observed in
[
Sac23
, Theorem 5.1]
.

Proposition 4
.

The map
\pi\colon\overline{P}\rightarrow B
has sections and the group of sections is isomorphic to the primitive cohomology
{\rm H}^{2,2}_{\mathrm{prim}}(X,\mathbb{Z})
of the very good cubic
X
.

Proof.

Let
P_{\mathcal{F}_{0}}
denote the fiberwise regular locus of
\overline{P}_{\mathcal{F}_{0}}
. Since the families of curves
\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}\to\mathcal{F}_{0}
and
\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{F}_{0}
have irreducible fibers,
P_{\mathcal{F}_{0}}
is a group scheme and by
[
ACLS23
, §9.1]
descends to a group scheme
P
over
B
given by the fiberwise regular locus of
\overline{P}
. Recall that
\overline{P}_{\mathcal{F}_{0}}\subset\overline{\mathrm{Jac}}(\tilde{\mathcal{C}})
is constructed as the connected component of the fixed locus of an involution of
\overline{\mathrm{Jac}}(\tilde{\mathcal{C}})
that contains the zero section
[
LSV17
, Definition 4.8]
. It follows that
\pi\colon\overline{P}\rightarrow B
has a zero section passing through
P
.

In order to compute the exact number of sections, note that the locus
P\subset M
, where
M
is an MMP model. It follows that the Lagrangian fibration
\pi_{M}\colon M\to B
admits a reduced and irreducible component on each fiber, as well as a section passing through this component.

Let
A_{M}
denote the fiberwise connected neutral component of the relative automorphism space of
\pi_{M}
(this was considered in the context of Lagrangian fibrations by
[
Mar96
,
AF16
,
AR21
]
and more recently by
[
Kim24
,
Sac24
,
Aba24
]
). If we knew that
M
has irreducible fibers, it follows from
[
AF16
, Theorem 2]
(see also
[
Kim24
, 1.1 (§6.4.1)]
) that
P\subseteq A_{M}\subseteq M
.
Since the irreducibility of the fibers of
\pi_{M}
is not clear from the construction, we need to use
[
Kim24
, Theorem 4.2, Proposition 4.27]
or
[
Sac24
, Proposition 5.10]
to conclude that the fiberwise connected component of
M^{\rm sm}
containing
P
is in fact a trivial
A_{M}
-torsor and therefore,
P\subseteq A_{M}\subseteq M
.
Since the group schemes
P\subseteq A_{M}
are irreducible of the same dimension, it follows that
P=A_{M}
.
Therefore, the group of sections of
\pi\colon\overline{P}\to B
is given by
{\rm H}^{0}(B,\mathcal{A}_{M})
, where the
\mathcal{A}_{M}
is the sheaf of sections of
A_{M}
.
In
[
DMS24
]
the sections of this sheaf has been calculated, which goes via identifying
\mathcal{A}_{M}
with an intermediate Jacobian sheaf closely related to the cubic fourfold
X
[Corollary 5.6,
loc. cit.
]. Finally, using [Corollary 3.21,
loc. cit.
] we obtain that
{\rm H}^{0}(B,\mathcal{A}_{M})\simeq{\rm H}^{2,2}_{\mathrm{prim}}(X,\mathbb{Z})
(see also [Remark 5.7,
loc. cit.
]).
∎

Next, we will use the sections of
\pi
to construct enough
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisors on
\overline{P}.

Proposition 5
.

The number of linearly independent Cartier divisors on
\overline{P}
is at least

\mathrm{rank}({\rm H}^{2,2}_{\mathrm{prim}}(X,\mathbb{Z}))+2.

Proof.

The variety
\overline{P}
is projective, so let
L
be some ample line bundle on
\overline{P}.
Recall that
\overline{P}\subset\overline{\mathrm{Jac}}(\tilde{\mathcal{C}})
. A section
s\colon B\rightarrow\overline{P}
thus acts on
\overline{P}
by translations fiberwise, i.e, it acts by tensoring. We denote this by
t_{s}\colon\overline{P}\rightarrow\overline{P}
.

Consider the set
\{t_{s}^{*}L\}_{s\in H^{2,2}_{\mathrm{prim}}(X,\mathbb{Z})}
of line bundles.
Let
s=\sum_{i}a_{i}s_{i}
such that
\{s_{i}\}
is a basis of the free
\mathbb{Z}
-module
H^{2,2}_{\mathrm{prim}}(X,\mathbb{Z})
.
Note that
t_{s}^{*}L\simeq L
if and only if
a_{i}=0
. To see this,
let
A
denote the generic fiber of
\pi\colon\overline{P}\rightarrow B
. Then
A
is an abelian variety with
L|_{A}
ample, and
t^{*}_{s}L|_{A}
is the translation of
L|_{A}
via the section.
The claim follows from noting that
the map
\phi\colon A\rightarrow\hat{A},
defined by
a\mapsto t_{a}^{*}L|_{A}\otimes L|_{A}^{-1}
has finite kernel and each
s\neq 0
has infinite order.
Therefore, the set
\{t_{s_{i}}^{*}L\}
of Cartier divisors on
\overline{P}
are linearly independent.
In addition to these, there is the ample line bundle
L
, as well as the line bundle
\pi^{*}\mathcal{O}_{B}(1)
. Their linear independence from the set of line bundles above can be checked again by restricting to
A
and using the theorem of the square.
Therefore the result follows.

∎

Next, we prove that the normalization
\overline{J}
of
\overline{P}
is a symplectic variety and use
Proposition

5
above to conclude that
\overline{J}
is also
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial.

Proof of
3a
.

The regular locus of the Prym model
\overline{P}
contains a smooth open subset
P
consisting of the regular points of the fibers of
\pi
.
The Prym model
\overline{P}
also contains
\overline{J}_{U_{1}}
, the relative compactified Jacobian of the at most 1-nodal hyperplane sections of
X
.
By construction,
\pi\colon\overline{P}\rightarrow B
is equidimensional (see
[
LSV17
, Proposition 4.10]
),
and it follows that
\mathrm{codim}_{\overline{P}}(\mathrm{Sing}(\overline{P}))\geq 3.

Furthermore, the non-degenerate holomorphic 2-form on
J_{U_{1}}
extends to the regular locus of
\overline{P}
as a non-degenerate
2
-form.
Let
\overline{J}\rightarrow\overline{P}
be the normalization. It follows that
\overline{J}
is a symplectic variety in the sense of Namikawa and hence has terminal singularities
[
Nam01
, Theorem 1, Corollary 1]
.

It remains to argue that
\overline{J}
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier. Let
D
be a Weil divisor on
\overline{J}
.
Since
\overline{J}
is birational to
M
, by
[
Sac24
, Proposition 4.3]

\overline{J}
admits a symplectic resolution
\mu\colon M^{\prime}\rightarrow\overline{J}
with
M^{\prime}
birational to
M
. Then
\mu^{*}D
is a Cartier divisor on
M^{\prime}
. The Picard rank
\rho(M^{\prime})
equals
\rho(M)
and by
[
Sac23
, Lemma 3.2]
, we know that
\rho(M)=\mathrm{rank}({\rm H}^{2,2}_{\mathrm{prim}}(X,\mathbb{Z}))+2
.
By
Proposition

5
, we can find
\mathrm{rank}(H^{2,2}_{\mathrm{prim}}(X,\mathbb{Z}))+2
-many linearly independent Cartier divisors in
\overline{J}
.
Therefore,
\mathrm{Pic}(M^{\prime})
is generated by
\mu^{*}L
,
\mu^{*}\pi^{*}\mathcal{O}_{B}(1)
and
\mu^{*}(t_{s}^{*}L)
, for
s\in H^{2,2}_{\mathrm{prim}}(B,\mathbb{Z})
.
It follows that
\mu^{*}D
is a linear combination of these Cartier divisors. This relation also holds over
the smooth locus
\overline{J}^{\rm sm}
.
Since
\mathrm{codim}_{\overline{J}}(\mathrm{Sing}(\overline{J}))\geq 4
, we have an isomorphism of the divisor class groups
{\rm Cl}(\overline{J}^{\rm sm})\simeq{\rm Cl}(\overline{J})
. Hence the linear relation of
D
holds over
\overline{J}
, which makes
D
already Cartier on
\overline{J}
.

∎

5.
The Picard model

We will need the following proposition identifying the sections of the fibration
f\colon\mathrm{Pic}^{0}_{\mathcal{F}/B}\rightarrow B
. It may be proved exactly analogously to
Proposition

4
, however we give a different Hodge theoretic proof since it avoids going via any hyperkähler models.
For a quick introduction to the Hodge modules we need and their properties adapted to our set-up consult
[
DMS24
, §4.1]
.

Proposition 6
.

The Lie algebra of the group scheme
\mathrm{Pic}^{0}_{\mathcal{F}/B}
is isomorphic to
\Omega_{B}^{1}
. Furthermore, the map
f\colon\overline{\mathrm{Pic}}^{0}_{\mathcal{F}/B}\to B
has sections and the group of sections is isomorphic to
{\rm H}^{2,2}_{\mathrm{prim}}(X,\mathbb{Z})
.

Proof.

Let
U\subset B
be the locus over which the fibers of
p\colon{\mathcal{Y}}\to B
and
\widetilde{p}\colon\mathcal{F}\to B
.
Consider the following isomorphic polarized variations of Hodge structures (pvHs) underlying the lattices
R^{1}\widetilde{p}_{*}\mathbb{Z}_{\mathcal{F}_{U}}
and
R^{3}p_{*}\mathbb{Z}_{{\mathcal{Y}}_{U}}(1)
. Let
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{F}}
and
\mathcal{M}_{{\mathcal{Y}}}
respectively denote the minimal extension (see e.g.
[
DMS24
, §4.1 (2)]
) of this pvHs as Hodge modules.
Since the Hodge modules are isomorphic, by [§4.1 (7),
loc. cit
] it follows that

\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{F}})\simeq\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{M}_{{\mathcal{Y}}}),

where
\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{M})
denotes the Jacobian sheaf associated to the Hodge module
\mathcal{M}
(see [Definition 4.1,
loc. cit.
]).
Furthermore, it follows, from
[
DMS24
, (v2, to appear), Theorem 5.6]
that
\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{F}})\simeq\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{F}/B}
, where
\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{F}/B}
is the sheaf of analytic sections of
\mathrm{Pic}^{0}_{\mathcal{F}/B}
.
In particular, we obtain an isomorphism of the corresponding Lie algebras
R^{1}\widetilde{p}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{F}}\simeq R^{3}p_{*}\Omega_{{\mathcal{Y}}}^{1}\simeq\Omega_{B}^{1}
[Theorem 4.7, Proposition 3.7
loc. cit.
]. This proves the first part.

The computation of the exact number of sections follows, as before, from first observing
\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{F}/B}\simeq\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{M}_{{\mathcal{Y}}})
, and then using [Corollary 3.21,
loc. cit
] which states

{\rm H}^{0}(B,\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{M}_{{\mathcal{Y}}}))\simeq{\rm H}^{2,2}_{\mathrm{prim}}(X,\mathbb{Z}).\qed

The proof of
3b
is verbatim to
3a
. However, there are a few subtleties arising from considering compactified
\mathrm{Pic}^{0}
of a family of surfaces (the Fano surfaces). Much of these were taken care of in
[
Bot25
]
, which we point out in the argument below.

Proof of
3b
.

To see that the normalization of
\overline{\mathrm{Pic}}^{0}_{\mathcal{F}/B}
is a terminal symplectic variety, here one needs to use

[
Bot25
, Proposition 4.8]
, to make sure that the smooth group scheme
\mathrm{Pic}^{0}_{\mathcal{F}/B}
(see
Remark

3
) has complement of codimension 2 in
\overline{\mathrm{Pic}}^{0}_{\mathcal{F}/B}
.
The statement in
loc. cit.
holds for any very good cubic fourfold.

For
\mathbb{Q}
-factoriality, we need to use
Proposition

6
. Let
\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{F}/B}
be the sheaf of analytic sections of
\mathrm{Pic}^{0}_{\mathcal{F}/B}
.
As in the proof of
Proposition

5
, one can argue again that
\overline{\mathrm{Pic}}^{0}_{\mathcal{F}/B}
has at least
\mathrm{rank}({\rm H}^{2}(B,\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{F}/B}))
-many independent Cartier divisors aside from
f^{*}\mathcal{O}(1)
and an ample line bundle
L
coming from the projectivity of
\overline{\mathrm{Pic}}^{0}_{\mathcal{F}/B}
.
Therefore, by
Proposition

we have
\mathrm{rank}({\rm H}^{2,2}_{\mathrm{prim}}(X,\mathbb{Z}))
-many sections of
f
.
Using the same argument as

.

∎

Introduction

1.1
Background

For any positive integer
n
, let
\phi(n)
be the Euler totient function given by

\displaystyle\phi(n)=n\prod_{p|n}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right),

(1.1)

where
p
runs over distinct primes dividing
n
. Let
\sigma(n)
be the sum of divisors of
n
.

In 1929, Pillai
Pillai 1929
firstly investigated the iteration of the function
\phi(n)
. In 1944, Alaoglu and Erdös
Alaoglu and Erdös 1944
proved that

\displaystyle\#\{n\leqslant x:\phi(\sigma(n))\geqslant cn\}=o(x),\ \ \ \ \#\{n\leqslant x:\sigma(\phi(n))\leqslant cn\}=o(x).

(1.2)

In particular, in 2002, Luca and Pomerance
(
Luca and Pomerance 2002
, Theorem 1)
obtained an asymptotic formula of the summation of
\sigma(\phi(n))/n
for
n\leqslant x
.

In 1967, Erdös
(
Erdös 1967
, Theorem 1)
proved that for every
c<1/2
, arbitrarily small
\varepsilon>0
and large
t
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{x}{\log x}(\log\log x)^{t}<\#\{n\leqslant x:\phi_{2}(n)>cn\}<\frac{x}{\log x}(\log x)^{\varepsilon}

(1.3)

\displaystyle\#\{n\leqslant x:\phi_{3}(n)>cn\}<\frac{x}{(\log x)^{2}}(\log x)^{\varepsilon}

(1.4)

\displaystyle\#\{n\leqslant x:\phi_{4}(n)>cn\}<\frac{\alpha x}{(\log x)^{2}}

(1.5)

where
\alpha
is an absolute constant independent of
c
.

Denote by
\log_{k}
the
k
-fold iterated logarithm
\log\cdots\log
(
k
-times) throughout the paper.

In 1990, Erdös
et al

(
Erdös et al. 1990
, p191 (4.2))
applied Brun’s method (see
Halberstam and Richert 1974
) to obtain the number of
n\leqslant x
for which
p\nmid\phi_{k}(n)
. There are absolute constants
0<c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}\leqslant 1
. For any
x\geqslant x_{0}
,
p\leqslant(\log x)^{2}
,
k\leqslant c_{3}\log_{2}x
, we have

\displaystyle\#\{n\leqslant x:p\nmid\phi_{k}(n)\}\ll\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccccc}x\exp\big\{-\frac{(c_{2}k^{-1}\log_{2}x)^{k}}{p-1}\big\},&\hbox{if }p>\frac{(c_{2}k^{-1}\log_{2}x)^{k}}{c_{1}\log_{2}x}+1.\\
x(\log x)^{-c_{1}},&\hbox{if }p\leqslant\frac{(c_{2}k^{-1}\log_{2}x)^{k}}{c_{1}\log_{2}x}+1.\end{array}\right.

They also showed that the ratio
\phi(n)/\phi_{k+1}(n)
has a smooth, strictly increasing normal order:
(see
(
Erdös et al. 1990
, Theorem 4.2)
) Fix a positive integer
k
. There is a set
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}(k)
of asymptotic density 1 with the property that as
n\rightarrow\infty
along
\mathcal{A}
, we have

\displaystyle\phi(n)/\phi_{k+1}(n)\sim k!e^{k\gamma}(\log_{3}n)^{k},

(1.8)

where
\gamma=0.57721\cdots
is the usual Euler-Mascheroni constant.

In 2004, Indlekofer and Kátai
(
Indlekofer and Kátai 2004
, Theorem 1)
obtained an asymptotic formula of the number of
n\leqslant x
such that
p\nmid\phi_{k}(n)
in the range
p\in\left((\log_{2}x)^{k+\varepsilon},(\log_{2}x)^{k+1-\varepsilon}\right)
, for
k\geqslant 2
,
\varepsilon>0
.

In 2011, according to Erdös’ result in
Erdös et al. 1990
, Pollack
Pollack 2011
proved that

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leqslant x}\phi_{k+1}(n)\sim\frac{3}{k!e^{k\gamma}\pi^{2}}\cdot\frac{x^{2}}{(\log_{3}x)^{k}},\ \ \ \hbox{as $x\rightarrow\infty$}.

(1.9)

Naturally, we can apply Abel’s identity (see
(
Apostol 1976
, Theorem 4.2)
) to obtain an asymptotic formula of the summation of
\phi_{k+1}(n)/n
which deduced an initial upper bound for the number of positive
n\leqslant x
such that
\phi_{k+1}(n)\geqslant cn
.

In 2024, Dixit and Bhattacharjee
(
Dixit and Bhattacharjee 2024
, Theorem 1)
deduced that

\displaystyle\#\{n\leqslant x:\phi(\sigma(n))\geqslant cn\}\leqslant\frac{\pi^{2}x}{6c\log_{4}x}+O\left(\frac{x\log_{3}x}{(\log_{x})^{1/\log_{3}x}\log_{4}x}\right).

(1.10)

They estimate the number of positive
n\leqslant x
such that
\phi(\sigma(n))\geqslant cn
by dividing into two equivalent and different cases, and improved the results by summing the the number of positive
n\leqslant x
corresponding to these two cases. In our note, we use the same method to improve the upper bound of positive
n\leqslant x
such that
\phi_{k+1}(n)\geqslant cn
.

Theorem 1
.

For every

Proof of Theorem

For
n>2
and
k\geqslant 1
, we have
\phi_{k+1}(n)<n/2
, thus, in Theorem
1
,
c<1/2
is the best possible. Note that

\displaystyle\phi_{k+1}(n)=\phi_{k}(n)\prod_{p|\phi_{k}(n)}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right).

(2.1)

Denote by
P(y):=\prod\limits_{p\leqslant y}p
, the product of primes up to
y
. If
P(y)|\phi_{k}(n)
, we have

\displaystyle\phi_{k+1}(n)=

\displaystyle\phi_{k}(n)\prod_{p|\phi_{k}(n)}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)\leqslant\phi_{k}(n)\prod_{p\leqslant y}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)<\frac{\phi_{k}(n)}{\log y}.

(2.2)

Here we use the fact (see
(
Montgomery and Vaughan 2007

\displaystyle\prod_{p\leqslant y}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)\sim\frac{1}{e^{\gamma}\log y},\ \ \ \hbox{as $y\rightarrow\infty$}

(2.3)

where

Note that for any

,

and
{\delta}/{\log y}\leqslant c

From the result of (
1
), we deduce that

\displaystyle\#\{n\leqslant x:

\displaystyle\phi_{k}(n)\geqslant\delta n\}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leqslant x\\
\phi_{k}(n)\geqslant\delta n\end{subarray}}1\leqslant\sum_{n\leqslant x}\frac{\phi_{k}(n)}{\delta n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{6}{(k-1)!e^{(k-1)\gamma}\pi^{2}}\cdot\frac{x}{\delta(\log_{3}x)^{k-1}}+O\left(\frac{x}{\delta(\log_{3}x)^{k}\log_{2}x\log x}\right).

(2.4)

Following the upper bound in (
1.1
) and setting

,
k\leqslant c_{3}\log_{2}x

\displaystyle\#\{n\leqslant x:P(y)\nmid\phi_{k}(n)\}\leqslant

\displaystyle O\left(\frac{x}{(\log x)^{c_{1}}}\cdot\frac{y}{\log y}\right).

(2.5)

We now focus on the selection of parameters

Substituting

Combining with (

(iv) In (

\displaystyle\frac{6}{(k-1)!e^{(k-1)\gamma}\pi^{2}}\cdot\frac{x}{c(\log_{3}x)^{k+1}}+O\left(\frac{x\exp\left\{(\log_{3}x)^{2}\right\}}{(\log x)^{c_{1}}(\log_{3}x)^{2}}\right).

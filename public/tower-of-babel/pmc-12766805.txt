Risk factors for hyperuricaemia–hypertension comorbidity in Chinese children and adolescents: a protocol for systematic review and meta-analysis
Authors: Ziqu Zhang, Yang Jing, Yuefeng Yang, Wengang Xia, Cong-Cong Yu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionHypertension (HTN) and hyperuricaemia (HUA) are chronic metabolic disorders that frequently coexist. Research indicates that HUA prevalence among adolescents and children in mainland China is significantly higher than the global average, with a continuing upward trend. When these conditions occur concomitantly, their detrimental effects on the cardiovascular and cerebrovascular systems far exceed those of either condition alone. The comorbidity of HUA and HTN markedly elevates the risk of adverse cardiovascular and cerebrovascular events, including stroke, ischaemic heart disease, myocardial hypertrophy and left ventricular diastolic dysfunction. In adolescent populations, this comorbidity may also induce subclinical damage such as microangiopathy, premature arteriosclerosis and declined glomerular filtration function, thereby increasing the likelihood of chronic cardiorenal diseases in adulthood. Current research on HUA+HTN comorbidity among Chinese children and adolescents is predominantly limited by regional constraints, small sample sizes or inconsistent diagnostic criteria. This hinders the development of precise prevention strategies and delays timely interventions. Thus, a systematic evaluation of the risk factors for HUA+HTN comorbidity in this population is urgently needed. Such evaluation would provide evidence-based data to facilitate early detection of high-risk individuals and guide tailored preventive interventions. It would also lay the groundwork for reducing the future burden of chronic conditions such as stroke and heart failure.Methods and analysisElectronic databases (Cochrane Library, PubMed, Web of Science, EMBASE, CNKI, CBM, VIP, Wanfang) will be systematically searched up to 1 June 2025, with no language restrictions applied. This will identify observational studies (cohort, case-control and cross-sectional designs) investigating risk factors for HTN and HUA in Chinese children and adolescents. In addition, randomised controlled trials (RCTs) that report baseline or preintervention data relevant to HTN+HUA comorbidity risk factors will also be considered for inclusion, where such data can provide supplementary evidence on associations between exposures and outcomes. Methodological quality will be evaluated via tools appropriate for each study design (Agency for Healthcare Research and Quality checklist for cross-sectional studies; Newcastle-Ottawa Scale for case-control and cohort studies). The risk of bias in RCTs will be evaluated using the RoB 2. All analyses will be conducted per the Cochrane Handbook recommendations for observational studies.Ethics and disseminationThis systematic review protocol will systematically evaluate the risk factors for HUA+HTN comorbidity in Chinese children and adolescents. We will analyse secondary data, and this does not require ethics approval. The findings will be published in peer-reviewed journals, at relevant conferences and will be shared in plain language in social media. Moreover, the findings of this review could guide the direction of healthcare practice and research.PROSPERO registration numberCRD42024613929.

Introduction

The 2025 Consensus on the Management of Hyperuricaemia (HUA) and Gout in Adolescents, published by Chinese experts, indicates that the prevalence of HUA among Chinese adolescents (particularly males) ranges between 26.6% and 42.3%.1 Separate research indicates that the overall prevalence of HUA among children and adolescents in China stands at 20.9%,2 while the hypertension (HTN) prevalence in this younger population, when confirmed by measurements on multiple separate visits, ranges from 3% to 5%.3 4 It is noteworthy that HUA constitutes an independent risk factor for cardiovascular disease.5 Adolescents concurrently affected by both HTN and HUA are more susceptible to HTN-related complications, with disease progression occurring at a more accelerated rate.6 In terms of relevant guideline development, in 2022 the European Society of Cardiology, in collaboration with several European societies, published a consensus report specifically addressing HTN in children and adolescents in the European Heart Journal.7 However, the 2024 ESC Guidelines for the Management of Elevated Blood Pressure and Hypertension, updated by the journal in 2024, did not give particular attention to the paediatric and adolescent population.8 By contrast, China explicitly states in its Guidelines for the Prevention and Treatment of Hypertension (2024 Revised Edition) that the prevalence of HTN among school-age children aged 6 to 17 years reaches 13%, and proposes diagnostic and assessment methods for HTN specific to this demographic.9 In summary, both HTN and HUA exhibit a high prevalence trend among Chinese adolescents. The combined impact of these two conditions not only exacerbates individual health risks but also signals an increasingly burdensome chronic disease burden in the future, presenting a major public health issue that demands urgent attention.

In mainland China, the comorbidity rate of HTN and HUA in children and adolescents is increasing faster than the global average, which is largely attributed to obesity-related insulin resistance driving a metabolic vicious cycle that exacerbates HUA and HTN.10 Some studies suggest that the disparity in HUA prevalence between China and other regions may be linked to genetic factors such as SLC2A9 and ABCG2 polymorphisms, along with dietary habit shifts.11 HUA is associated with increased cardiovascular risk in individuals with HTN. The HUA+HTN comorbidity further accelerates the progression of HTN-related complications.12 However, the definitive risk factors for this comorbidity remain unclear, and existing studies on Chinese children and adolescents are often limited by regional biases, small sample sizes, inconsistent age stratification or varying diagnostic criteria.13 Notably, HTN and HUA in China are exhibiting a trend toward younger onset and year-by-year growth,14 and cardiovascular diseases diagnosed in childhood or adolescence often persist into adulthood.

Previous studies have explored risk factors for HUA and HTN in Chinese minors. Nevertheless, systematic evidence remains insufficient, posing challenges for developing targeted prevention strategies. Therefore, the present study was conducted to systematically evaluate the risk factors for HUA and HTN comorbidity in Chinese minors, providing evidence-based support for tailored screening and prevention strategies in high-risk populations.

Methods and analysis

The protocol for this systematic review has been prospectively registered with PROSPERO. The review is scheduled to commence in August 2025 and is expected to be completed by September 2026. A comprehensive literature search will be initiated immediately following the commencement of the study. If the protocol is amended, the date of each revision will be accompanied by a description of the changes and the rationale for them.

Registration

This review protocol has been registered with the Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews (PROSPERO) on 14 November 2024, and was last updated on 24 November 2024 (CRD42024613929). This article presents the methodology for a literature review and does not involve original experimental research on animals, cells or humans. As such, this study did not require ethical approval in China. The methodological design of this systematic review will follow guidance provided by the Cochrane Handbook, while the reporting of the protocol has been structured to adhere to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic review and Meta-Analysis Protocols (PRISMA-P) checklist.

Search strategy

Electronic databases (CNKI, CBM, VIP, Wanfang, Cochrane Library, Embase, Web of Science, PubMed) will be systematically retrieved up to 1 June 2025, using structured queries that combine subject headings and free-text terms across four domains. For Chinese literature, the queries will be (‘Elevated Uric Acid’ OR ‘Hyperuricaemia’) AND (‘Hypertension’ OR ‘High Blood Pressure’ OR ‘Primary Hypertension’) AND (‘Children’ OR ‘Adolescents’ OR ‘Minors’ OR ‘Kids’) AND (‘Risk Factors’ OR ‘Influencing Factors’ OR ‘Associated Factors’). For English literature, the queries will consist of (‘Hyperuricaemia’ OR ‘Uric Acid’) AND (‘Hypertension’ OR ‘High Blood Pressure’) AND (‘Child’ OR ‘Children’ OR ‘Adolescent’ OR ‘Teen’ OR ‘Kids’) AND (‘Risk Factor’ OR ‘Predictor’ OR ‘Determinant’), supplemented by manual reference screening, Google Scholar citation tracking, contacting authors and experts for unpublished data, and searching paediatric and cardiovascular conference proceedings. Patient and public involvement

Patients or the public were not involved in the design or development of this research protocol. As a systematic review and meta-analysis, this study synthesises existing evidence from published literature, and its methodological framework is primarily guided by established guidelines for evidence synthesis.

Review methodology

Selection of studies

The literature screening process will be conducted in three sequential phases: (1) Removal of duplicate records using EndNote’s automated detection feature, followed by manual verification to ensure accuracy, (2) Independent screening of titles and abstracts by two researchers (Ziqu Zhang and Yang Jing) to identify potentially eligible studies, followed by retrieval of the corresponding full-text articles and (3) Comprehensive assessment of the full-text articles per the predefined eligibility criteria to determine final eligibility. All screening decisions will be cross-verified between the two researchers. Unresolved disagreements will be independently adjudicated by a third researcher (Yuefeng Yang). Data extraction and management

Two trained researchers (Ziqu Zhang and Yang Jing) will extract data independently using standardised electronic forms. The forms will incorporate the following information: (1) Study characteristics (eg, geographical region, author, publication year), (2) Methodological details (eg, study design, sampling method, sample size), (3) Measurement tools and (4) Key outcome data, including adjusted effect estimates and statistically significant risk factors. Following a pilot test involving 5% of the included studies, all data extractions will be cross-verified between the two researchers. Any disagreements will be settled via consensus-based discussion or, when required, arbitration by a third researcher (Yuefeng Yang). All extracted data will be managed in version-controlled spreadsheets with built-in automated validation checks to ensure data integrity.

Assessment of risk of bias (ROB)

The ROB and methodological quality of each eligible study will be evaluated independently by two researchers (ZZ and YJ) using appropriate, discipline-specific tools. For cross-sectional studies, the Agency for Healthcare Research and Quality checklist will be applied, consisting of 11 items, each scored as 1 (‘yes’) or 0 (‘no/unclear’). Overall quality will be categorised as low (0–3), moderate (4–7), or high (8–11) according to the total score.15 For cohort and case-control studies, the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale will be employed, evaluating study quality on the basis of three domains: comparability of groups, assessment of exposure or outcome, and selection of participants. Quality is represented by an allocation of stars, with a maximum possible score of 9 stars.16 For RCTs, the RoB 2 will be applied. The RoB 2 tool assesses bias across five domains: (1) Bias arising from the randomisation process, (2) Bias due to deviations from intended interventions, (3) Bias due to missing outcome data, (4) Bias in measurement of the outcome and (5) Bias in selection of the reported result. The overall risk of bias for each RCT will be judged as ‘low risk’, ‘some concerns’ or ‘high risk’ based on the domain-level assessments. Disagreements in ROB assessments will be settled via consensus-based discussion or, when required, arbitration by a third researcher (Yuefeng Yang). The assessment results will inform sensitivity analyses but will not be used as a basis for excluding studies from the review.

Two researchers will use the Cochrane risk assessment tool to screen and evaluate the quality of the included studies respectively, and simultaneously collect and verify the relevant outcome measures data. In the case of disagreements between the two researchers, a third researcher will participate in the discussion to reach a consensus ultimately. The quality assessment criteria will include seven items: random sequence generation (selection bias), allocation concealment (selection bias), blinding of participants and personnel (performance bias), blinding of outcome assessment (detection bias), incomplete outcome data (attrition bias), selective reporting (reporting bias) and other bias, with three evaluation levels: high risk, unclear risk and low risk. RevMan V.5.4 software will be used to generate the bias risk plot and the forest plot of each outcome measure.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses will be conducted via R V.4.4.3, using the metafor, dmetar and ggplot2 packages. Dichotomous outcomes will be analysed as the OR with a 95% CI. Standardised mean differences with 95% CIs will be calculated for continuous outcomes. Heterogeneity between studies will be assessed via Cochran’s Q-test (significance level of α=0.10) and the I² statistic. An I² value ≤50% will be interpreted as indicating low heterogeneity. In the case of low heterogeneity, fixed-effects models (Pet-Peese estimator) will be applied. Otherwise, random-effects models (restricted maximum likelihood (REML) estimation) will be adopted. These will be supplemented by sensitivity and subgroup analyses, including leave-one-out analysis and model-robustness checks. Publication bias will be evaluated using contour-enhanced funnel plots and Egger’s regression test (when ≥10 studies are available), with p-curve analysis to further evaluate for evidence of selective reporting. Missing data will be addressed by attempting to contact study authors. If this is unsuccessful, multiple imputation techniques (using the mice package in R) will be employed, adhering to the intention-to-treat principle.

Data management and sharing

All literature references will be managed and deduplicated using EndNote V.X20. Screening and data extraction will be conducted in Covidence, supporting independent blinded screening and cross-verification, with all procedures automatically documented. Data extracted through pre-designed forms will be stored in a database and cross-checked by two researchers. All data are derived from previously published studies, and original data can be accessed via supplementary materials of the respective articles or by contacting the original authors. The results will be submitted to a peer-reviewed journal and presented at academic conferences. Data extraction forms and analysis code will be made publicly available on publication to enhance transparency and reproducibility.

Dissemination plans

The findings will be disseminated via conference presentations and peer-review publications.

Ethics and dissemination

This systematic review protocol will systematically evaluate the risk factors for HUA+HTN comorbidity in Chinese children and adolescents. No ethical approval is required because this article presents the methodology for a literature review and does not involve original experimental research on animals, cells or humans. The findings will be disseminated via conference presentations and peer-review publications. The results will also be made publicly available on publication to enhance transparency and reproducibility.

Discussion

In mainland China,1 the onset of HTN and HUA is occurring at significantly younger ages among children and adolescents. Some studies indicate that the higher rate of increase in the prevalence of these two conditions in mainland China compared with the global average may be closely related to obesity-related insulin resistance, creating a cycle of metabolic disease involving HUA and HTN.12 Furthermore, gene polymorphisms such as SLC2A9 and ABCG2, as well as changes in dietary habits, may also play a role.1618 Therefore, it is of great importance to conduct a systematic review of the risk factors for the comorbidity of HTN and HUA in this specific and growing high-risk population. The present systematic review and meta-analysis aims to identify risk factors for the co-occurrence of HTN and HUA, which is expected to enable more accurate identification of high-risk subgroups—such as those defined by obesity indices, insulin resistance, specific dietary patterns or genetic susceptibility—and is anticipated to facilitate the development of targeted early prevention strategies. This study is expected to provide important evidence for mitigating this far-reaching metabolic comorbidity and reducing the future burden of cardiovascular disease.

This study has several limitations. First, despite employing a comprehensive search strategy, some relevant publications may have been missed due to database limitations or unpublished data. Moreover, although the study focuses on Chinese adolescents, most available research may originate from larger cities or more developed eastern coastal regions, where medical and scientific resources are more advanced. This may lead to selection bias and reduce the generalisability of the findings to children and adolescents in rural, western or less socioeconomically developed parts of China. These limitations highlight the need for cautious interpretation of the results and indicate directions for future research.

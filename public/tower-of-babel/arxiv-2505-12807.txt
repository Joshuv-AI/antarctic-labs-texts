A remark on the growth rate of operator semigroups under resolvent bounds
Filippo Dell’Oro

Abstract
. We provide a growth bound for the operator norm of C 0 C_{0} -semigroups on Hilbert spaces under a corresponding growth bound on the resolvent of the semigroup generator. For some super-linear resolvent growths, our estimate is sharper than the ones currently available in the literature.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
47D06, 47A10

1.
Introduction and Main Result

Let
A
be the infinitesimal generator of a
C_{0}
-semigroup
(T(t))_{t\geq 0}
on a complex Hilbert space
H
.
Denote by
\rho(A)
the resolvent set of
A
and by
R(\lambda,A)=(\lambda-A)^{-1}
the
resolvent operator at
\lambda\in\rho(A)
.
We say that
A
satisfies the Kreiss condition if the open right half-plane

\mathbb{C}^{+}=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:\re\lambda>0\}

is contained in
\rho(A)
and there exists a constant
C\geq 1
such that

(1.1)

\|R(\lambda,A)\|\leq\frac{C}{\re\lambda},\quad\,\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{+}.

The validity of the Kreiss condition with
C=1

is equivalent to
(T(t))_{t\geq 0}
being a contraction semigroup, while for
C>1
it implies
that
\|T(t)\|=O(t)
as
t\to\infty
; see
[
6
]
. Moreover, for every fixed
\gamma\in(0,1)
, there exists
a semigroup on a Hilbert space whose norm grows at least as
t^{\gamma}
for
t\to\infty
and whose infinitesimal
generator satisfies the Kreiss condition; see again
[
6
]
.
The question whether such examples exist even for
\gamma=1
has been open for some
time, as explicitly stated in
[
6
,
13
]
, and was solved negatively in
[
2
]
. In that article,
by adapting a technique devised in
[
5
]
for discrete semigroups,
the author proved that (
1.1
) implies the
sharper bound

(1.2)

\|T(t)\|=O\left(\frac{t}{\sqrt{\log t}}\right),\quad\,t\to\infty.

More details on the Kreiss condition and its applications may be found in
[
3
,
7
,
14
,
15
]
.
We further mention the original article of Kreiss
[
12
]

which contains the first explicit statement of (
1.1
)
for
A
being a matrix.

In this paper, we consider more general resolvent bounds of the form

\|R(\lambda,A)\|\leq g\Big(\frac{1}{\re\lambda}\Big),\quad\,\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{+},

where
g:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
is a non-decreasing function with
g(t)\to\infty

for
t\to\infty
.
As in the case of the Kreiss condition, one expects that the growth rate of
g

reflects on the growth rate of
\|T(t)\|
, and this insight has been
confirmed in
[
4
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
14
]
.
In particular, the resolvent bound above implies that

(1.3)

\|T(t)\|=O(g(t)),\quad\,t\to\infty;

see
[
14
, Theorem 3.4]
. The aim of this short article is to prove the following result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
A
be the infinitesimal generator of a
C_{0}
-semigroup
(T(t))_{t\geq 0}
on a complex Hilbert space
H

satisfying
\,\mathbb{C}^{+}\subset\rho(A)
.
Assume that there exists a function
g:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
such that

(1.4)

\|R(\lambda,A)\|\leq g\Big(\frac{1}{\re\lambda}\Big),\quad\,\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{+}.

Moreover, assume that the function
h(t)=g(t)/t
is non-decreasing. Then

(1.5)

\|T(t)\|=O\left(g(t)\frac{h(t)}{\sqrt{\log t}}\right),\quad\,t\to\infty.

Note that (
1.4
) implies the Kreiss condition (
1.1
)
whenever
h(t)
is bounded, and in such a case
(
1.5
)
boils down to (
1.2
).
Instead, if
h(t)\to\infty
as
t\to\infty
, then (
1.5
) is sharper than (
1.3
) whenever
h(t)=o(\sqrt{\log t})
as
t\to\infty
.
The following two examples illustrate this possibility.

\bullet

Let
g(t)=t\hskip 0.85358pt\log^{\beta}(t+1)
with
\beta\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
. In this situation,
Theorem
1.1
yields the improved estimate

\|T(t)\|=O(t\hskip 0.85358pt\log^{\frac{4\beta-1}{2}}t),\quad\,t\to\infty.

\bullet

Let
g(t)=t\hskip 0.85358pt\log\log(t+e)
. In this situation,
Theorem
1.1
yields the improved estimate

\|T(t)\|=O\left(\frac{t\hskip 0.85358pt\log\log t}{\sqrt{\log t}}\right),\quad\,t\to\infty.

The interested reader will have no difficulty in constructing other examples, for instance by
considering iterated logarithms.

2.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

We begin by noting that since
h(t)
is non-decreasing then
g(t)
is non-decreasing and such that
g(t)\to\infty

for
t\to\infty
.
Let
s(A)=\sup\big\{{\rm Re}\,\lambda:\lambda\in\sigma(A)\big\}
be the spectral bound of
A
,
where
\sigma(A)
denotes the spectrum of
A
. Let also

s_{0}(A)=\inf\big\{r>s(A):\,\exists\hskip 0.85358ptC_{r}>0:\,\|R(\lambda,A)\|\leq C_{r}\,\text{ whenever }\re\lambda>r\big\}

be the pseudo-spectral bound of
A
. Being
g(t)
non-decreasing,
assumption (
1.4
) ensures that
s_{0}(A)\leq 0
.
Hence, calling

\omega_{0}=\inf\big\{\omega\in\mathbb{R}:\,\exists\hskip 0.85358ptM_{\omega}\geq 1:\,\|T(t)\|\leq M_{\omega}\hskip 0.85358pt{\rm e}^{\omega t}\,\text{ for all }t\geq 0\big\}

the growth bound of
(T(t))_{t\geq 0}
, the Gerhart-Prüss theorem yields
\omega_{0}=s_{0}(A)\leq 0
;
see e.g.
[
1
, Theorem 5.2.1]
.
Therefore, the following integral representation of the resolvent holds

(2.1)

R(\lambda,A)x=\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm e}^{-\lambda t}T(t)x\,\mathrm{d}t,\quad\,x\in H;\,\ \re\lambda>0.

For
\alpha>0
, we denote by
(T_{\alpha}(t))_{t\geq 0}
the rescaled semigroup given by

T_{\alpha}(t)={\rm e}^{-\alpha t}T(t)
when
t\geq 0
, and we shall extend
(T_{\alpha}(t))_{t\geq 0}
by zero to
\mathbb{R}
.
Equality (
2.1
) tells that the function
s\mapsto R(\alpha+is,A)x

is nothing but the Fourier transform of the function

t\mapsto T_{\alpha}(t)x
.
Therefore, applying Plancherel’s theorem for square-integrable
functions taking values in a Hilbert space, we deduce that

(2.2)

\int_{0}^{\infty}\|T_{\alpha}(\tau)x\|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\tau=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\|R(\alpha+is,A)x\|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s;

see e.g.
[
8
, Appendix C]
.
We stress that the validity of (
2.1
) for
\re\lambda>0
, as well as the validity of
(
2.2
) for all
\alpha>0
, is a consequence of
\omega_{0}\leq 0
.

As shown in
[
6
]
, for every
r\in(0,1)
and every
x,y\in H
, there exists a constant

K>0
independent of
r,x,y
such that

(2.3)

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\|R(r+is,A)x\|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\leq K^{2}\Big(1+g\Big(\frac{1}{r}\Big)\Big)^{2}\|x\|^{2}

(2.4)

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\|R(r+is,A^{*})y\|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\leq K^{2}\Big(1+g\Big(\frac{1}{r}\Big)\Big)^{2}\|y\|^{2}

where
A^{*}
is the adjoint of
A
. Actually, these estimates have been obtained in
[
6
]
only
for
g(t)=C\hskip 0.56905ptt
with
C\geq 1
, but the same argument applies
to any non-decreasing function; see also
[
4
,
7
]
.
Nevertheless, in order to make the
paper self-contained, we report here the full proof. Let

\omega,M_{\omega}\geq 1

be such that
\|T(t)\|\leq M_{\omega}\hskip 0.85358pt{\rm e}^{t(\omega-1)}

for
t\geq 0
. From the resolvent identity and (
1.4
) it follows
that, for
r\in(0,1)
,
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
x\in H

\displaystyle\|R(r+is,A)x\|

\displaystyle=\|R(\omega+is,A)x+(\omega-r)R(r+is,A)R(\omega+is,A)x\|

\displaystyle\leq\omega\Big(1+g\Big(\frac{1}{r}\Big)\Big)\|R(\omega+is,A)x\|.

Moreover, exploiting (
2.2
) with
\alpha=\omega
, we deduce that

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\|R(\omega+is,A)x\|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s=2\pi\int_{0}^{\infty}\|T_{\omega}(\tau)x\|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\leq\pi M_{\omega}^{2}\hskip 0.85358pt\|x\|^{2}.

Combining the two estimates above, we reach (
2.3
) with

K=\sqrt{\pi}\hskip 0.85358pt\omega M_{\omega}
. In order to prove (
2.4
), we
apply the same argument
to
A^{*}
and the adjoint semigroup
(T^{*}(t))_{t\geq 0}
,
recalling that
\|R(\lambda,A^{*})\|=\|R(\lambda,A)\|

for
\lambda\in\rho(A)
and
\|T(t)\|=\|T(t)^{*}\|
for
t\geq 0
.

Next, we show the following bounds

(2.5)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\|T(\tau)x\|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\leq\frac{{\rm e}^{2}K^{2}}{2\pi}\big(1+g(t)\big)^{2}\|x\|^{2}

(2.6)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\|T^{*}(\tau)y\|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\leq\frac{{\rm e}^{2}K^{2}}{2\pi}\big(1+g(t)\big)^{2}\|y\|^{2}

for every
t>1
and every
x,y\in H
.
To this end, making use (
2.2
) with
\alpha=r\in(0,1)
, we have

{\rm e}^{-2rt}\int_{0}^{t}\|T(\tau)x\|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\|T_{r}(\tau)x\|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\tau=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\|R(r+is,A)x\|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s.

Owing to (
2.3
), the latter yields

\int_{0}^{t}\|T(\tau)x\|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\leq\frac{{\rm e}^{2rt}K^{2}}{2\pi}\Big(1+g\Big(\frac{1}{r}\Big)\Big)^{2}\|x\|^{2},

and choosing
r=1/t
we arrive at (
2.5
).
In the light of (
2.4
), the same argument applied to

(T^{*}(t))_{t\geq 0}
leads to (
2.6
).

We have now all the ingredients to finish the proof. To this end,
let
t\geq 2
and
x,y\in H
be arbitrarily fixed.
For every
1\leq a<b\leq t
, we write

\displaystyle(b-a)|\langle T(t)x,y\rangle|

\displaystyle=\int_{a}^{b}|\langle T(t-\tau)x,T^{*}(\tau)y\rangle|\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\int_{a}^{b}\|T^{*}(\tau)y\|^{2}\mathrm{d}\tau\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\int_{a}^{b}\|T(t-\tau)x\|^{2}\mathrm{d}\tau\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{{\rm e}K}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\big(1+g(b)\big)\bigg(\int_{t-b}^{t-a}\|T(\tau)x\|^{2}\mathrm{d}\tau\bigg)^{1/2}\|y\|,

where the second inequality follows from (
2.6
). Therefore

(2.7)

\Big(\frac{b-a}{b}\Big)|\langle T(t)x,y\rangle|\leq\frac{{\rm e}K}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\big(1+h(b)\big)\Big(\int_{t-b}^{t-a}\|T(\tau)x\|^{2}\mathrm{d}\tau\Big)^{1/2}\|y\|.

Taking the supremum over
\|y\|=1
, and since
h(b)\leq h(t)
, we get

\Big(\frac{b-a}{b}\Big)\|T(t)x\|\leq\frac{{\rm e}K}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\big(1+h(t)\big)\Big(\int_{t-b}^{t-a}\|T(\tau)x\|^{2}\mathrm{d}\tau\Big)^{1/2}.

Set now
N=\left\lfloor\log t/\log 2\right\rfloor
where
\left\lfloor\,\cdot\,\right\rfloor
is the floor function.
Choosing
a=2^{n-1}
and
b=2^{n}
for
1\leq n\leq N
, we find

\|T(t)x\|^{2}\leq\frac{2{\rm e}^{2}K^{2}}{\pi}\big(1+h(t)\big)^{2}\int_{t-2^{n}}^{t-2^{n-1}}\|T(\tau)x\|^{2}\mathrm{d}\tau.

As a consequence

\displaystyle N\|T(t)x\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2{\rm e}^{2}K^{2}}{\pi}\big(1+h(t)\big)^{2}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int_{t-2^{n}}^{t-2^{n-1}}\|T(\tau)x\|^{2}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2{\rm e}^{2}K^{2}}{\pi}\big(1+h(t)\big)^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\|T(\tau)x\|^{2}\mathrm{d}\tau.

In the light of (
2.5
), we end up with

(2.8)

\|T(t)x\|\leq\frac{{\rm e}^{2}K^{2}}{\pi}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\left\lfloor\log t/\log 2\right\rfloor}}\big(1+h(t)\big)\big(1+g(t)\big)\|x\|.

Taking the supremum over
\|x\|=1
, we reach the desired estimate (
1.5
).
∎

3.
Concluding Remarks

\bullet

In
[
14
, Theorem 3.4]
the authors keep track of the implicit constant appearing in
estimate (
1.3
) by means of the constants
\omega
and
M_{\omega}

used in the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
The same can be done for the implicit constant appearing in (
1.5
),
simply recalling that the constant
K
in (
2.8
)
is equal to
\sqrt{\pi}\hskip 0.85358pt\omega M_{\omega}
.

\bullet

The proof of Theorem
1.1
can be adapted to give
another proof of estimate (
1.3
) from
[
14
]
. Indeed,
choosing
b=2
and
a=1
in (
2.7
), we have for
t\geq 2

\displaystyle|\langle T(t)x,y\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sqrt{2}{\rm e}K}{\sqrt{\pi}}\big(1+h(2)\big)\Big(\int_{t-2}^{t-1}\|T(\tau)x\|^{2}\mathrm{d}\tau\Big)^{1/2}\|y\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sqrt{2}{\rm e}K}{\sqrt{\pi}}\big(1+h(2)\big)\Big(\int_{0}^{t}\|T(\tau)x\|^{2}\mathrm{d}\tau\Big)^{1/2}\|y\|.

Owing to (
2.5
), we find

|\langle T(t)x,y\rangle|\leq\frac{{\rm e}^{2}K^{2}}{{\pi}}\big(1+h(2)\big)\big(1+g(t)\big)\|x\|\|y\|,

and taking the supremum over
\|x\|=\|y\|=1
we arrive at (
1.3
).
Note that here the monotonicity of the function
h(t)
is not needed.
However, it is worth remarking that
this argument is valid only on Hilbert spaces,
while in
[
14
]
estimate (
1.3
) has been achieved even for certain classes
of semigroups acting on Banach spaces, such as asymptotically analytic semigroups or
positive semigroups on
L^{p}
-spaces with
p\neq 2
.

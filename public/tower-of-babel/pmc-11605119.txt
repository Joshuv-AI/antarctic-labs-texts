Endogenous chondroitin extends lifespan by inhibiting VHA-7-mediated tubular lysosome formation
Authors: Yukimasa Shibata, Yuri Tanaka, Shunsuke Mori, Kaito Mitsuzumi, Shion Fujii, Hiroyuki Sasakura, Yuki Morioka, Kenji Sugioka, Kosei Takeuchi, Kiyoji Nishiwaki
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Chondroitin extends lifespan and healthspan in C. elegans, but the relationship between extracellular chondroitin and intracellular anti-aging mechanisms is unknown. The basement membrane (BM) that contains chondroitin proteoglycans is anchored to cells via hemidesmosomes (HDs), and it accumulates damage with aging. In this study, we found that chondroitin regulates aging through the formation of HDs and inhibition of tubular lysosomes (TLs). Reduction of chondroitin due to a mutation in sqv-5/Chondroitin synthase (ChSy) causes the earlier and excessive formation of TLs and leakage of the lysosomal nuclease in a manner dependent on VHA-7, the a-subunit of V-type ATPase. VHA-7, whose mutation suppresses the short lifespan of the sqv-5 mutant, is initially localized to the basal side of the hypodermal cells and transported to lysosomes with aging. These results demonstrate that endogenous chondroitin suppresses aging by inhibiting the earlier excessive formation of TLs. This is a novel anti-aging mechanism that is controlled by the BM.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-024-80242-3.

Introduction

Chronological aging is accompanied by the collapse of cellular and tissue homeostasis due to the accumulation of age-related damage. This collapse at the tissue level causes age-related diseases, and at the individual level leads to death. Aging was once thought to be an irreversible process, but it has now been proven that DNA mutations in somatic cells are not the cause of aging1–3. Consequently, aging is now considered to be a reversible process. Understanding the molecular mechanisms of aging is an important issue that will lead to the treatment of age-related diseases in general. However, the molecular mechanisms that control aging are extremely complex, and the full picture has not yet been clarified. In particular, there is very little knowledge about the control of aging mediated by the ECM.

The BM, a type of ECM, is a mesh-like membrane structure mainly composed of type IV collagen that covers the basal side of tissues. The BM is essential for development, involved in morphogenesis and cell migration, and serves as a scaffold for extracellular signaling molecules4. Supplementation of glycosaminoglycans extends lifespan in mouse and C. elegans5,6. During aging, BMs are damaged by unavoidable cross-linking, physical rupture, and degradation by matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs) secreted by senescent cells7. In mice, the anchoring of skin stem cells to the BM is essential for cell competition, and degradation of the BM with aging causes the loss of stem cells8. Although C. elegans lacks somatic stem cells9, an increase in endogenous chondroitin causes an extension of lifespan10, suggesting that stem cells are not the sole target of the anti-aging effects of BM.

Chondroitin has a structure consisting of repeating disaccharides of N-acetylgalactosamine and glucuronic acid, and is localized in the ECM as chondroitin proteoglycans, which are bound to core proteins. Chondroitin is necessary for nerve regeneration and cell migration, and in C. elegans, the intake of chondroitin extends lifespan6,11,12. In C. elegans, sqv-5/Chondroitin synthase (ChSy) and mig-22/Chondroitin polymerizing factor (ChPF) are required for chondroitin biosynthesis, and reduction-of-function mutations in these genes reduce the amount of chondroitin in vivo. The sqv-5(k175) mutants exhibit shortened lifespan and healthspan10,11. In contrast, the gain-of-function mutation mig-22(k185gf) that is located in the evolutionarily conserved residue in MIG-22 increases endogenous chondroitin to twice that of the wild type, extending lifespan and healthspan10 (Fig. 1A). However, the mechanism by which chondroitin extends lifespan is unknown.

The BM is tethered to cells by HDs. In mammals, the major transmembrane protein of HDs is integrin, whereas in C. elegans, LET-805 acts as the functional counterpart of integrin13. It is known that LET-805 shows a striped localization in the area where the muscle and hypodermis are attached, and the stripes divide and double during the third larval stage14. During molting, the physical attachment between the cuticle and hypodermis is broken, causing temporary disruption of HDs and activation of lysosomes15. The anchoring of mammalian skin stem cells to the BM is mediated by HDs containing type XVII collagen, and HDs maintain skin stem cells and prevent skin aging8. On the other hand, in C. elegans, the involvement of HDs in regulating aging has not been explored.

Lysosomes are organelles necessary for intracellular digestion that degrade proteins, sugars, lipids, and nucleic acids. The inside of the lysosome is acidified by a V-type ATPase, which is a proton pump16,17. It is known that the destruction of lysosomes causes the release of degrading enzymes into the cytosol, causing damage to cells18–20. Lysosomes are traditionally known as vesicle-like organelles, but they can form a tubular shape during muscle formation in Drosophila, in activated macrophages, and during molting and aging in C. elegans21–25. However, the molecular mechanism that induces the tubular morphology and the significance of the tubular morphology are largely unknown.

In this study, we found that chondroitin promotes the formation of HDs, and the impairment of HDs promotes aging. HDs are known to suppress the formation of TLs15. Consistently, the decrease in chondroitin caused the early induction of TLs. Additionally, leakage of the enzyme DNase II NUC-1 from the lysosomes into the cytoplasm was also observed. We isolated a suppressor mutation of sqv-5 and found that the causative gene was vha-7, an a-subunit of V-type ATPase. VHA-7 was initially localized to the basal side of the hypodermis, and its localization in lysosomes increased with aging. The leakage of NUC-1 into the cytoplasm, short lifespan, and premature elongation of defecation period caused by the sqv-5 mutation were suppressed by the vha-7 mutation.

Result

Promotion of HD formation by chondroitin

In our previous study, we showed that the endogenous chondroitin level in the sqv-5(k175) mutant decreases to about 10% of the wild type, while in the mig-22(k185gf) mutant, it increases to about twice that of the wild-type animals10 (Fig. 1A). The amounts of chondroitin are correlated with lifespan and age-related reduction in physiological activity, such as the frequency of pharyngeal pumps and defecation (Fig. 1A), demonstrating the role of chondroitin in aging10. As chondroitin on the BM functions in the regulation of aging, we focused on the hypodermal HDs contacting the BMs between muscle and hypodermis. A hemidesmosomal component LET-805 forms a repeated strip pattern, known as fibrous organelle, along the C. elegans body (Fig. 1B)14. We tested if chondroitin influences the HD formation. To clarify whether the structure of HDs is affected by the levels of chondroitin, we used a functional let-805::gfp allele as the reporter (Fig. 1B)14. In the wild type, 602.2 ± 18.7 (n = 10) stripes were seen in individual animals at the young adult stage (Fig. 1C). The number of stripes was reduced by 12.0% in the sqv-5(k175) mutant (530.0 ± 31.4 stripes/animal, n = 10), and increased by 13.5% in the mig-22(k185gf) mutant (683.8 ± 39.9 stripes/animal, n = 10) compared to the wild type. This result indicated that chondroitin promotes the formation of HD stripes until young adult stage. It is known that HD stripes duplicate in the L3 stage through separation of HDs14. Therefore, to clarify whether chondroitin is involved in the increase of HD numbers at the L3 stage or the formation of HD during embryonic development, we measured the number of HD stripes at the L1 stage (Fig. 1C). In the L1 stage, 277.6 ± 17.6 (n = 10) stripes were seen in the wild type. The number of stripes was reduced by 5.7% in the sqv-5(k175) mutant (261.7 ± 8.2 stripes/animal, n = 10), and increased by 6.9% in the mig-22(k185gf) mutant (296.8 ± 16.0 stripes/animal, n = 10). Although there was already a difference in the number of HD stripes among mutants at the L1 stage, this difference became larger in young adults, suggesting that chondroitin is effective in increasing HD formation during embryonic development and duplication in the L3 stage. It is reported that there is no correlation between the number of HD stripes and body length14. To confirm this, we measured the body length of wild-type animals, sqv-5(k175) mutants, and mig-22(k185gf) mutants in a let-805::gfp background. Although there was a significant difference, its degree was less pronounced than in the case of the number of HD stripes (Fig S1A). Compared to wild-type animals, body length was 5.5% shorter in sqv-5(k175) mutants and 9.1% longer in mig-22(k185gf) mutants. The stripe density calculated based on the body length and the number of stripes in individual HDs was 6.92, 6.44, and 7.19 per 10 μm for the wild type, sqv-5(k175) mutant, and mig-22(k185gf) mutant, respectively. These results indicate that chondroitin affects both body length and the density of HD stripes. When measuring the number of HD stripes, we noticed that there were partially separated HD stripes in young adults (Fig. 1D, S1 B). The proportion of partially separated HD stripes was significantly reduced in the sqv-5(k175) mutant (Fig S1C). In contrast, in the mig-22(k185gf) mutant, the proportion of partially separated HD stripes was significantly increased in the anterior half of the body. These results suggest that chondroitin regulates proper HDs formation.

We hypothesized that endogenous chondroitin suppresses aging via HDs formation. If HDs can suppress aging, then let-805 RNAi would accelerate aging. Therefore, we conducted let-805 RNAi on the wild type and investigated the effects on age-related reduction in physiological activity, such as the number of pharyngeal pumps and the length of the defecation cycle. In the control mock RNAi, the number of pharyngeal pumping and the duration of defecation cycle decreased and increased as animals age, respectively (Fig. 1E; day 1 vs. day 7, Fig. 1F; day 1 vs. day 3). As expected, in wild-type background, let-805 RNAi caused fewer pumps (Fig. 1E) and a longer defecation cycle (Fig. 1F) compared to the control. If chondroitin suppresses aging by promoting HD formation, let-805 RNAi would promote aging more strongly in the sqv-5(k175) mutant. The effect of let-805 RNAi in the sqv-5(k175) mutant was stronger than that in wild-type animals (Fig. 1E,F). On the other hand, in the mig-22(k185gf) mutant, in which the amount of chondroitin and the number of HD stripes increases, the effect of let-805 RNAi was weakened. These results strongly suggest that the levels of chondroitin, HD formation, and aging are correlated. The sensitivity to let-805 RNAi, in terms of age-related reduction in physiological activity, is lower when HD formation is enhanced.

Chondroitin suppresses age-associated formation of TLs

We sought to identify the cellular mechanism by which the loss of HDs accelerates aging. In C. elegans larval development, HDs inhibit the formation of TLs, and TLs are formed upon loss of HDs during molting in hypodermal cells (Fig. S2A)15. It is also known that TLs are formed during aging25. Therefore, we quantified TLs in the mig-22(k185gf) and sqv-5(k175) mutants, whose number of HD stripes are affected. We used mCherry-tagged NUC-1, a lysosomal nuclease, as a lysosomal marker26. Lysosomal morphology was classified based on circularity: those with a circularity greater than 0.5 were classified as vesicular lysosomes, while those with a circularity of 0.5 or less were classified as TLs (Fig. S2B,C). We observed the anterior (Fig. 2) and posterior (Fig S2) halves of the body separately. Although qualitatively similar changes in lysosomal morphology over time were observed in both sides, we describe the results for the anterior half, where changes were more dynamic.

In wild-type, the number of TLs, which include lysosomes consisting of a single tube or multiple connected tubes (Fig S2B), increased with age. In day 3 adults, the number of TLs per 200 μm² was 3.45 ± 0.49, which increased by 1.97 times to 6.80 ± 1.07 in day 9 adults (Fig. 2A,B). The percent area of TLs also increased 2.65 times, from 2.15 ± 0.31% in day 3 adults to 5.74 ± 0.64% in day 9 adults. The total number of tubes per 200 μm² increased 3.13 times, from 9.90 ± 1.79 in day 3 adults to 31.30 ± 5.60 in day 9 adults (Fig. 2D). Additionally, the number of tubes per connected TL increased from 2.87 ± 0.36 to 4.60 ± 0.66, indicating that the morphology of TLs becomes more complex with aging (Fig. 2E). Taken together, these results show that in wild-type, the number of TLs increases with age, and individual TLs increase in morphological complexity. In the mig-22(k185gf) mutant, the area and total number of TLs were significantly lower than in the wild type (Fig. 2A,C,D). In contrast, the number of tubes per connected TL was unchanged compared to the wild type (Fig. 2E). These results indicate that higher endogenous chondroitin level decreased the number of TLs but did not affect the complexity of their shape. In the sqv-5(k175) mutant with decreased chondroitin content, the number of TLs was significantly increased in day 3 adults than that in wild-type animals (Fig. 2A,B). In addition, the area and total number of TLs also increased in day 3 adults (Fig. 2A,D,E). This result indicates that TLs are induced precociously in the sqv-5(k175) mutant compared to the wild type. However, in day 5 and day 9 adults, the number of TLs significantly decreased compared to day 3 adults (Fig. 2B). Compared to the wild type at the same time, the number, percent area, and total number of TLs were significantly reduced (Fig. 2B–D). Additionally, in the sqv-5(k175) mutant, the number of tubes per connected TL did not increase in day 5 and day 9 adults, indicating that the complex TLs associated with aging was not observed in the sqv-5(k175) mutant (Fig. 2E). Interestingly, in the sqv-5(k175) mutant, the NUC-1-mCherry signal in the cytoplasm was significantly increased compared to the wild type from day 5 (Figs. 2A,F, S2D). This suggests that leakage of lysosomal content occurs, a hallmark of lysosomal membrane permeabilization or full rupture induced in stressed condition, in the sqv-5(k175) mutant27.

Isolation of sqv-5 suppressor, vha-7 that encodes a-subunit of V-ATPase

The sqv-5 mutant shows abnormal morphology of the gonads due to the abnormal migration of gonadal leader cells, distal tip cells (DTCs)11. Therefore, we performed suppressor screening using gonad morphological abnormalities of sqv-5(k175) as an indicator and isolated the tk181 mutation (Fig. 3A–C,E). tk181 significantly suppressed the gonadal defect of sqv-5(k175). tk181 failed to suppress sqv-5(k175) as a heterozygote, indicating that it was a recessive suppressor. tk181 in wild-type background did not show any gonad morphological abnormality (Fig. 3D). SNP mapping experiments localized tk181 to the linkage group IV. Whole genome sequencing analysis identified a missense mutation in the vha-7 gene. When we introduced extrachromosomal array containing the wild-type vha-7 gene into sqv-5(k175); tk181 mutants, the suppression effect of tk181 was canceled (Fig. 3E). We also observed significant rescue of the sqv-5(k175) gonadal abnormality in vha-7 RNAi experiments (Fig. 3E). Thus, we concluded that vha-7 is the causative gene for tk181.

vha-7 encodes an a-subunit of V-ATPase that transports protons across the lipid bilayer membrane (Fig. 3F)28. The a-subunit is a transmembrane protein contained in the Vo domain. C. elegans has three other a-subunit paralogs, including vha-5, vha-6, and unc-32 (Fig. S3A)28. RNAi of these paralogs causes developmental arrest between the embryonic stage and the L2 larval stage. vha-5, vha-6, and unc-32 are expressed in excretory cells and in hyp7, the large hypodermal cell that covers most of the body, as well as in intestines and neurons, respectively, whereas vha-7 is expressed in the hypodermis28–30. Interestingly, VHA-7 has a unique domain at the N-terminus that is not found in other a-subunits (Fig. S3B). This domain has no homology to existing proteins. It is considered that the interaction between the amino terminal domain and the d-subunit is involved in proton transport, and the carboxy terminal domain is the transmembrane region28,31. vha-7(tk181) has the A639V mutation near the C-terminus of the amino terminal domain (Fig. S3C). This amino acid is located next to the evolutionarily conserved phenylalanine.

To examine the possibility that vha-7(tk181) can restore the decreased chondroitin levels in the sqv-5(k175) mutant, we compared the endogenous chondroitin levels in each mutant. As a result, we found that the decrease in endogenous chondroitin levels caused by the sqv-5(k175) mutation was not restored by the vha-7(tk181) mutation (Fig. 3G). Furthermore, the vha-7(tk181) mutation alone did not significantly increase chondroitin levels compared to the wild type. These results suggest that vha-7 acts downstream of sqv-5 but in a cellular process other than the chondroitin synthesis.

VHA-7 localizes at basal side of hypodermis in young adult, and at TLs in aged animals

V-ATPase is localized not only in lysosomes but also in the cell membrane, endosomes, and Golgi apparatus16. To investigate the patterns of age-related changes in VHA-7 localization, we used VHA-7 tagged with mCherry. In day 1 adults, no vesicle-like localization as seen in lysosomes was observed (Fig. 4A,M). However, strong vesicle-like localization was observed in day 3 adults, and the signals increased in day 5 adults (Fig. 4B,C,M), and tube-like localization was observed in day 9 adults (Fig. 4D). When we observed the VHA-7-mCherry localization in optical cross sections, the signal was in the basal surface of the hypodermis at day 1 adult, and it gradually expanded to the cytoplasm especially in the lateral hypodermal ridge as vesicle-like or tube-like forms during day 3 and day 5 (Fig. 4N,O). When VHA-7-GFP was used, vesicle-like or tube-like localization was hardly observed, but localization was still observed in the basal side of hypodermis at day 3 and day 5 (Fig. 4E–H,N). Since the C-terminus of the a-subunit of V-ATPase is located in the lysosome, it is possible that the C-terminally tagged GFP, but not mCherry, can be quenched under the acidic environment of lysosomes15. To confirm the localization of VHA-7 to lysosomes, we compared it with the localization of the scavenger receptor SCAV-3, which is localized to lysosomes26. Co-localization of VHA-7-mCherry and SCAV-3-GFP was hardly observed in day 1 adults, but co-localization began to be observed in day 3 and later adults (Fig. 4I–L,I’-L’,I’’-L’’). Furthermore, as a result of quantifying the degree of co-localization, more co-localization was observed as aging progressed (Fig. 4P). These results indicates that VHA-7 undergoes a dynamic shift of its subcellular localization from the basal side of the hypodermis to lysosome with age.

VHA-7 is required for the induction of TLs by chondroitin depletion

When we observed the localization of VHA-7-mCherry in the sqv-5(k175) mutant, strong lysosomal localization was observed earlier than in the wild type (Fig. 5A–C). In day 5 adults, VHA-7-mCherry localization was observed in a significantly wider area than in the wild type (Fig. 5E). On the other hand, strong localization of VHA-7-mCherry was almost no longer observed in day 9 adults (Fig. 5D,S4). This result indicates that in the sqv-5(k175) mutant, lysosomal localization of VHA-7-mCherry precociously occurs but is not maintained. Because chondroitin promotes HD formation, we also examined the effect of HDs on VHA-7 localization in lysosomes. let-805 L4 RNAi significantly promoted the lysosomal localization of VHA-7 in day 5 adults (Fig. 5F–J). This result indicates that HDs suppress the lysosomal localization of VHA-7. To understand the necessity of VHA-7 in the early induction of TLs due to chondroitin deficiency, we observed lysosomes in the sqv-5(k175) vha-7(tk181) double mutant. The increase in TLs caused by the sqv-5(k175) mutation in day 3 adults was significantly attenuated by the vha-7(tk181) mutation (Fig. 5K,L). Furthermore, the increase in the area of TLs and the total number of lysosomal tubes per unit area due to the sqv-5(k175) mutation was significantly reduced by the vha-7(tk181) mutation (Fig. 5M,N). This result indicates that the vha-7 gene is required for the early induction of TLs by the sqv-5(k175) mutation. In day 9 adults, both the vha-7(tk181) mutant and the sqv-5(k175) vha-7(tk181) double mutant exhibited significantly fewer TLs and total tubes per unit area than the wild type (Fig. 5K–M). This result indicates that VHA-7 is also required for the induction of TLs during aging. In the sqv-5(k175) mutant, higher NUC-1-mCherry signals were observed in the cytoplasm, but the level of NUC-1-mCherry signals recovered to similar levels as in the wild type in the sqv-5(k175) vha-7(tk181) double mutant (Fig. 5O). This result indicates that the vha-7(tk181) mutation prevented NUC-1 leakage into the cytoplasm in the sqv-5(k175) mutant. These results indicate that the lysosomal abnormalities caused by the sqv-5(k175) mutation can be rescued by the vha-7(tk181) mutation.

We investigated whether VHA-7 regulates the relationship between lysosome size and morphology. In wild-type animals, large and tubular lysosomes increased as animals age (Fig. 5P, Fig S4B, C). Fast aging of sqv-5mutants is mediated by vha-7

We have reported that the sqv-5(k175) mutant has a shorter lifespan and healthspan10. Therefore, we investigated whether the vha-7(tk181) mutation could restore these abnormalities. We found that the short lifespan of the sqv-5(k175) mutant was suppressed by the vha-7(tk181) mutation (Fig. 6A). Interestingly, the sqv-5(k175) vha-7(tk181) double mutant had a longer lifespan than the wild type. In contrast, the lifespan of vha-7(tk181) mutants was not significantly different from that of the wild type (Fig. 6B). These results indicate that the vha-7(tk181) mutation has a lifespan-extending effect when chondroitin levels are reduced.

Discussion

In this paper, we showed that chondroitin promotes HD formation and inhibits TL formation. We isolated vha-7, which encodes an a-subunit of V-ATPase, as a suppressor of the sqv-5(k175) mutant, which exhibits decreased endogenous chondroitin. VHA-7 localized to the basal side of the hypodermis and lysosomes, and the localization to lysosomes became more prominent with aging. The vha-7(tk181) mutation did not affect the amount of endogenous chondroitin in the sqv-5(k175) mutant, but it suppressed premature formation of TLs, leakage of NUC-1 into the cytoplasm, short lifespan, and remarkable prolongation of the defecation cycle with aging.

Chondroitin proteoglycans (CPGs) in BM regulate aging via HDs

Our recent studies including this study revealed that chondroitin and HDs suppress aging10,32. C. elegans ECM includes the cuticle on the apical surface of the hypodermis and the BM on basal surface. Our results strongly suggest that chondroitin in BM is involved in aging for the following two reasons. First, chondroitin regulates aging downstream of the MIG-17/ADAMTS protease that localizes in the BM10. Second, VHA-7, which acts downstream of chondroitin, is localized on the basal side of hyp7. Thus, the localization of MIG-17 and VHA-7, which work together with chondroitin, strongly suggests that chondroitin in the BM is necessary for suppressing aging. The BM is made of a collagen mesh and dynamic components including fibulin and CPGs, that diffuse along the BM33. We previously showed that collagen, fibulin, and chondroitin are involved in aging10,32. Signal transduction from the BM to cells includes both growth factors concentrated in the BM and physical signals transmitted by HDs, which directly bind to cells and the BM34,35. Since there are no reports of CPGs directly binding to HDs, it is hypothesized that CPGs may regulate the physical signaling of HDs through interactions with the basement membrane (BM). Dynamic components, which diffuse along the BM33, are believed to interact physically with type IV collagen molecules. Variations in chondroitin levels, collagen mutations, or changes in dynamic components can alter the strength of these interactions, potentially affecting the physical properties of the BM and the transmission of physical signals via HDs.

Furthermore, the total number of HD stripes, influenced by chondroitin levels, could determine the overall strength of the signals transmitted through HDs. In the anterior half of the body, there was a significant difference between wild-type and mig-22gf mutants in terms of HD separation (Fig S1B). Such a difference in HDs may cause a morphological difference in the lysosomes in the anterior body. As aging progresses, changes in BM composition and the accumulation of damages may alter its physical properties. Excessive attenuation of physical signals may signify BM degradation, potentially leading to the activation of cellular recycling systems, including lysosomal activation.

HDs control transport of V-ATPase containing VHA-7 to lysosomes

In young adults, VHA-7 appears to localize in the plasma membrane on the basal side, and as animals age, it becomes strongly localized on lysosomes. In addition, more localization in the plasma membrane was observed simultaneously. Although it is known that the localization of the mammalian a3-subunit changes from lysosomes to the cell membrane during development36, this study is the first to show that the localization of the a-subunit changes with aging. Paralogues of a-subunit exists in multiple species, and their intracellular localization differs for each paralog16. C. elegans has four a-subunits, of which VHA-5 is known to be localized in a vesicle-like manner on the apical side of hyp730, and VHA-6 is localized uniformly on the apical side of the intestine37. Although the intracellular localization of the a-subunit differs for each C. elegans paralog, only VHA-7 shows lysosomal localization and dynamic shift of its subcellular localization. VHA-7 is also localized in protrusions connected to the basal cell membrane, and it is likely that VHA-7 is transported to lysosomes through basal cell membrane protrusions and/or endocytosis. The enhanced lysosomal localization in let-805 RNAi strongly suggests that HDs control the transport of V-ATPases containing VHA-7 (Fig. 7). HDs that anchor the basal plasma membrane to the BM may regulate the tension of plasma membrane. It is known that increasing membrane tension inhibits endocytosis38. Therefore, it is possible that the intracellular localization of V-ATPase containing VHA-7 is regulated by endocytosis that is induced by attenuation of the physical signal via HDs.

The mechanism that induces TLs and the effect of TL induction

TL formation is known to occur in macrophages, muscle cells of Drosophila, and hypodermal cells of C. elegans15,21,23. In all these cases, the formation of TLs during the developmental process has been reported, and the formation of TLs during aging is known only in C. elegans25. Starvation also induces TLs in C. elegans intestinal cells24. Although TL formation is observed in multiple organisms, the molecular mechanism that forms TLs is poorly understood. In macrophages, microtubules (MTs) and Rab7 are involved in the formation of tubular lysosome21,39. Rab7 regulates TL formation through kinesin and dynein effectors39. Furthermore, in osteoclasts, the amino-terminal domain of the a3-subunit forms a complex with Rab740. Therefore, the a3-subunit may be important for anchoring lysosomes to MTs and elongation of TLs mediated by Rab7 and motor proteins. In C. elegans, VHA-7 may regulate kinesin and dynein through RAB-7 to form tubular lysosomes.

The transition from a vesicular to a tubular shape increases the surface area of lysosomes. It is hypothesized that the increased surface area makes binding to autophagosomes easier24. It is also thought that the formation of large reticular lysosomes leads to synchronous activation of lysosomes23. In all these cases, it is considered that the formation of TLs promotes lysosomal activity. However, the phenotype of the sqv-5(k175) mutant indicates that earlier excessive TL formation leads to the leakage of degradative enzymes from lysosomes, suggesting the importance of proper formation of TLs. In addition, excessive TLs formation causes lysosomal destruction, with subsequent loss of TLs and loss of lysosomal localization of VHA-7. There are controversial reports on the effects of TL induction on aging. The increase in TLs in the intestine due to starvation suppresses aging24. On the other hand, in long-lived mutants such as the daf-2 and isp-1 mutants, the induction of TLs associated with aging is suppressed25. The latter is consistent with the results for the mig-22(k185gf) mutant. We hypothesized that the translocation of VHA-7 from the plasma membrane to the lysosome is suppressed by enhanced HD formation in mig-22gf mutants (Fig. 7). TLs induction also carries the risk of lysosome destruction, and maintaining lysosome function until later stages may be important for repressing aging. As both mig-22(k185gf) mutant and sqv-5(k175) vha-7(tk181) showed extended lifespan and suppression of TLs, extended lifespan might suppress TLs formation. This possibility cannot be completely excluded, however, the isolation of vha-7 as a suppressor of sqv-5 indicates that lysosomal V-ATPase is downstream of chondroitin, suggesting that suppression of TLs formation is not a secondary effect of lifespan extension. Although there seems to be a correlation between aging and TLs, the long lifespan of vha-7 sqv-5 double mutants indicates that induction of TLs is not essential for anti-aging.

Working model: chondroitin extends lifespan through inhibition of TLs that are induced by the translocation of VHA-7 from the basal plasma membrane to lysosomes

Based on our results, we propose a model for the anti-aging mechanism of chondroitin, mediated by HDs and lysosomes (Fig. 7). Chondroitin, present in the BM as CPGs, promotes HD formation. With aging, V-ATPases containing VHA-7 translocate from the basal cell membrane to lysosomes, a process inhibited by HDs. V-ATPase with VHA-7 induces lysosomes to change into tubular shapes, and with further aging, into mesh-like shapes. In a wild-type background, appropriately activated lysosomes exhibit a mild anti-aging effect.

In the sqv-5(k175) mutant, the CPGs have shorter chondroitin chains in the basement membrane and fewer HDs. Consequently, the translocation of V-ATPase containing VHA-7 from the basal cell membrane to lysosomes is activated, leading to a rapid increase in tubular lysosomes during middle age. This rapid morphological change, compared to the wild-type, likely causes physical stress on lysosomes, resulting in the leakage of degradative enzymes, including NUC-1, into the cytoplasm. Leakage of these enzymes damages cells and shortens the animal’s lifespan.

In the sqv-5(k175) vha-7(tk181) double mutant, the morphological changes in lysosomes induced by V-ATPase containing VHA-7 do not occur, and lysosomes remain vesicle-like even as they enlarge with aging. Consequently, lysosome destruction does not occur, preventing the cytoplasmic leakage of NUC-1. As a result, cell damage is avoided, and the shortened lifespan observed in the sqv-5 background is restored.

Anti-aging mechanism that functions in differentiated cells

Recently, it has been reported in mouse skin that HD-mediated anchoring to the BM is necessary for the maintenance of stem cells and prevents skin aging8. On the other hand, although C. elegans has no somatic stem cells, multiple components of BM regulate lifespan and healthspan10,32. Therefore, the mechanism that unveiled in this study is a BM-dependent anti-aging mechanism that functions in differentiated cells. This study is the first example that demonstrates the downstream mechanism by which chondroitin slows aging. Our findings shed new light on the significance of TL formation, which has received increasing attention in recent years. TLs are also found in multiple organisms, suggesting that a similar mechanism is evolutionarily conserved, including in humans. A decrease in chondroitin and damage to the BM are phenomena that are observed in normal aging35. Understanding the precise mechanisms that operate downstream of chondroitin will enable the development of methods to efficiently block the negative effects of aging from ECM in the future.

Behavioral analyses

Defecation cycle and pumping rate were analyzed as previously described10,44. To synchronize the animals for the analysis of defecation cycle or pumping rate, we collected young adults with no fertilized eggs. To distinguish them from their progeny, they were transferred them to a new plate every two days while they were laying eggs. The pumping rate was measured for 30 s, and the average of three consecutive measurements was calculated. If the defecation cycle exceeded 3 min, further measurements were discontinued and summed as 180 s. The sample size was 20 for pumping and 30 for the defecation cycle.

Cloning ofvha-7

For the test cross, crossing unc-13(e1091) sqv-5(k175) and tk181; sqv-5(k175) males, scoring 60 F1 non-Unc progeny and examining the DTC migration abnormality rate. The suppressor mutation tk181 was genetically mapped using single-nucleotide polymorphism mapping, with a sqv-5(k175) mutant strain in the CB4856 background. Whole genome sequence analysis comparing sqv-5(k175) and sqv-5(k175) tk181 in the mapped region identified a single mutation in the coding sequence corresponding to vha-7 locus.

In the rescue experiment, we observed adults exhibiting sur-5-GFP fluorescence.

Analysis of chondroitin levels

Wild-type, sqv-5(k175), vha-7(tk181), and sqv-5(k175) vha-7(tk181) animals were grown on NGM plates at 20 °C and collected as mixed-stage animals using M9 buffer. The animals were washed twice with M9 buffer and cultured for 1 h at room temperature. After removing the supernatant, 10 ml of acetone was added. The CS chain analysis was performed using enzymatic treatment followed by HPLC-based quantification, as previously described10,12.

Analysis of lifespan

Animals were synchronized for lifespan analysis by collecting individuals at the L4 stage. Lifespan analysis was conducted as previously described10.

Statistics

The lifespan was analyzed using a log-rank test or the generalized Wilcoxon test in R10. A t-test or the Mann–Whitney test was used for pairwise comparison. One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was conducted using R to compare the defecation cycle, and pumping rate. If a significant difference was found by ANOVA, Tukey’s multiple comparison test was performed. Colocalization R was calculated using the ImageJ colocalization test.

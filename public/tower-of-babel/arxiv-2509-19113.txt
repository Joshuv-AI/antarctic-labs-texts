Abstract
Paraxial light skyrmions are topological configurations that map a spatial domain of the field onto the full Poincaré sphere of polarization states. While skyrmions have been explored in continuous-wave regimes, their realization in the ultrafast domain remains open. Here we demonstrate that attosecond skyrmion pulses can be generated via high-harmonic generation. Advanced simulations combining single-atom strong-field theory and macroscopic propagation reveal that an infrared linearly polarized vector beam with fractional orbital angular momentum produces extreme-ultraviolet harmonic fields with nearly identical skyrmion polarization distributions across a broad spectral range. Using 1.2 μ \mu m driving fields and experimentally realistic spectral filtering, the coherent superposition of consecutive harmonics centered at 70 eV yields ∼ 500 \sim 500 attosecond pulse trains. Our results opens opportunities to use structured attosecond light with topological polarization textures,in fields as ultrafast control, imaging and spectroscopy.

However, the up-conversion of IR paraxial skyrmions though HHG is far from trivial, as the efficiency depends critically on the polarization state. In HHG, the nonlinear interaction between an intense IR femtosecond (fs) pulse and a target—typically an atomic gas, but also liquids or solids—is described by the three-step model
Schafer1993
;
Corkum1993
: (i) tunnel ionization of the atoms in the medium, (ii) acceleration of the released electronic wavepacket by the laser field, and (iii) recollision of the electronic wavepacket with the parent ion, where the acquired kinetic energy is emitted as higher-order harmonics, extending to the EUV or even soft x-rays
Popmintchev2012
. The emission of a
plateau
of harmonics with similar intensity and regular phase relationship allows their synthesis into attosecond pulses
Paul2001
;
Hentschel2001
. While this process is efficient for linearly polarized driving fields, its efficiency drops rapidly with increasing ellipticity
Budil1993
, as the recombination probability is strongly reduced. As such, the up-conversion of light structures containing non-linear polarization states is challenging. In fact, an IR paraxial skyrmion does not directly up-convert into an EUV harmonic skyrmion. However, by properly modifying the IR driving field properties, high-order harmonics with elliptical and circular polarization states have been produced
Fleischer2014
;
Hickstein2015
;
Huang2018
;
Brooks2025
. Indeed, two-color schemes have been proposed to generate EUV skyrmions with harmonic-dependent polarization distributions and distinct
N_{\mathrm{S}}

fang2023generation
. Yet, producing attosecond skyrmion pulses further requires a spectral region of several harmonics that share the same polarization distribution and exhibit a regular phase relationship. This remains as a fundamental challenge due to both the low conversion efficiency of non-linearly polarized drivers and the strong harmonic-order dependence of the polarization structure.

In this Letter, we demonstrate that attosecond skyrmion pulses can be obtained in HHG by properly engineering a linearly polarized IR vector driving beam. Our advanced simulations demonstrate that the use of two counter-rotating beams with properly chosen fractional orbital angular momentum (OAM) drives far-field harmonics that exhibit skyrmion polarization distributions with the same, harmonic-independent, Skyrme number, covering the full Poincaré sphere. Each harmonic is emitted as a fs skyrmion, and the coherent superposition of several harmonics yields an attosecond pulse train with a stable skyrmion polarization texture (Fig.

).

As a conclusion, we have unveiled a scenario of HHG capable of generating attosecond skyrmion pulses. Despite the harmonic-dependence of the linearly polarized vector driving field, our advanced numerical simulations demonstrate that high-quality attosecond skyrmionic polarization textures emerge when multiple harmonics are combined, an effect particularly favorable with mid-IR drivers.

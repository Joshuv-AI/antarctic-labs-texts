On the Mittag-Leffler stability of mixed-order fractional homogeneous cooperative delay systems
La Van Thinh, Hoang The Tuan

Abstract
In this paper, we study a class of multi-order fractional nonlinear delay systems. Our main contribution is to show the (local or global) Mittag-Leffler stability of systems when some structural assumptions are imposed on the “vector fields": cooperativeness, homogeneity, and order-preserving on the positive orthant of the phase space. In particular, our method is applicable to the case where the degrees of homogeneity of the non-lag and lag components of the vector field are different. In addition, we also investigate in detail the convergence rate of the solutions to the equilibrium point. Two specific examples are also provided to illustrate the validity of the proposed theoretical result.

\displaystyle\eta_{i_{0}}(t_{0})

\displaystyle=0,\quad\eta_{i}(t_{0})\geq 0,\ i\neq i_{0},

(10)

\displaystyle\quad\eta_{i_{0}}(t)

\displaystyle>0,\ \forall t\in[0,t_{0}).

Since
\eta_{i_{0}}(t_{0})=0
and
\eta_{i_{0}}(t)>0,\ \forall t\in[0,t_{0})
, by Lemma
2.9
, it deduces that

{}^{\!C}D^{\alpha_{i_{0}}}_{0^{+}}\eta_{i_{0}}(t_{0})\leq 0.

On the other hand, from (

), we see

\displaystyle\Phi^{k}_{i_{0}}(t_{0},\varphi^{k})

\displaystyle=\Phi^{n}_{i_{0}}(t_{0},\varphi^{n}),

\displaystyle\Phi^{n}_{i}(t,\varphi^{n})

\displaystyle\leq\Phi^{k}_{i}(t,\varphi^{k}),\ \forall i\neq i_{0},\ \forall t\in[0,t_{0}],

which together with Proposition
2.6
and the fact that

is cooperative implies

f_{i_{0}}(\Phi^{n}(t_{0},\varphi^{n}))\leq f_{i_{0}}(\Phi^{k}(t_{0},\varphi^{k})).

With the help of the assumption that
g^{(j)}
is order-preserving, we obtain

1.

if
t_{0}-\tau_{j}(t_{0})\geq 0
, then

g_{i_{0}}^{(j)}(\Phi^{n}(t_{0}-\tau_{j}(t_{0})),\varphi^{n})\leq g_{i_{0}}^{(j)}(\Phi^{k}(t_{0}-\tau_{j}(t_{0})),\varphi^{k});

2.

if
t_{0}-\tau_{j}(t_{0})<0
, then

g_{i_{0}}^{(j)}(\Phi^{n}(t_{0}-\tau_{j}(t_{0})),\varphi^{n})=\varphi^{n}(t_{0}-\tau_{j}(t_{0}))\leq g_{i_{0}}^{(j)}(\Phi^{k}(t_{0}-\tau_{j}(t_{0})),\varphi^{k})=\varphi^{k}(t_{0}-\tau_{j}(t_{0}));

and thus

g^{(j)}_{i_{0}}(\Phi^{n}(t_{0}-\tau_{j}(t_{0})),\varphi^{n})\leq g_{i_{0}}^{(j)}(\Phi^{k}(t_{0}-\tau_{j}(t_{0})),\varphi^{k}).

These lead to that

{}^{\!C}D^{\alpha_{i_{0}}}_{0^{+}}\eta_{i_{0}}(t_{0})

\displaystyle=^{\!C}D^{\alpha_{i_{0}}}_{0^{+}}\Phi^{k}_{i_{0}}(t_{0},\varphi^{k})-^{\!C}D^{\alpha_{i_{0}}}_{0^{+}}\Phi^{n}_{i_{0}}(t_{0},\varphi^{n})

\displaystyle=[f_{i_{0}}(\Phi^{k}(t_{0}),\varphi^{k})+\sum_{j=1}^{m}g_{i_{0}}^{(j)}(\Phi^{k}(t_{0}-\tau_{j}(t_{0})),\varphi^{k})+\frac{1}{k}]

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt-[f_{i_{0}}(\Phi^{n}(t_{0}),\varphi^{n})+\sum_{j=1}^{m}g_{i_{0}}^{(j)}(\Phi^{n}(t_{0}-\tau_{j}(t_{0})),\varphi^{n})+\frac{1}{n}]

\displaystyle=f_{i_{0}}(\Phi^{k}(t_{0}),\varphi^{k})-f_{i_{0}}(\Phi^{n}(t_{0}),\varphi^{n})+\frac{1}{k}-\frac{1}{n}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\sum_{j=1}^{m}[g_{i_{0}}^{(j)}(\Phi^{k}(t_{0}-\tau_{j}(t_{0})),\varphi^{k})-g_{i_{0}}^{(j)}(\Phi^{n}(t_{0}-\tau_{j}(t_{0})),\varphi^{n})]

\displaystyle>0,

a contradiction.
Hence, the sequence
\left\{\Phi^{k}(\cdot,\varphi^{k})\right\}
(for
k
large enough) is strictly decreasing on
[0,\infty)
. For each
t\geq 0
, the limit
\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\Phi^{k}(t,\varphi^{k})
exists. Define

\Psi^{*}(t):=\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\Phi^{k}(t,\varphi^{k}).

Using the arguments as in
[
28
, Theorem 4.2]
, then the sequence
\left\{\Phi^{k}(\cdot,\varphi^{k})\right\}

converges uniformly to
\Psi^{*}(\cdot)
on
[0,T]
for any

and it is obvious to see that
\Psi^{*}(\cdot)
is also continuous and nonnegative on this interval. Moreover,
\|\Psi^{*}(t)\|_{v}\leq\|\varphi\|_{v}
for all
t\geq 0
. Now, based on the integral form of the solution

\displaystyle\Phi_{i}^{k}(t,\varphi^{k})

\displaystyle=\varphi_{i}(0)+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha_{i})}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha_{i}-1}\big[f_{i}(\Phi^{k}(s,\varphi^{k}))+\sum_{j=1}^{m}g_{i}^{(j)}(\Phi^{k}(s-\tau_{j}(s),\varphi^{k}))\big]ds

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\frac{1}{k}+\frac{t^{\alpha_{i}}}{k\Gamma(\alpha_{i}+1)}

for all
t\geq 0
and letting

, then

\displaystyle\Psi_{i}^{*}(t)=\varphi_{i}(0)+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha_{i})}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha_{i}-1}\big[f_{i}(\Psi^{*}(s))+\sum_{j=1}^{m}g_{i}^{(j)}(\Psi^{*}(s-\tau_{j}(s)))\big]ds

for all
t\geq 0,\;i=1,\dots,d
.
This together with the fact system (

) has a unique solution which exists globally on

shows that

. In particular,
\Phi(\cdot,\varphi)
is non-negative and
\|\Phi(t,\varphi)\|_{v}\leq\|\varphi\|_{v}
for all

.

Case 2
:
q_{j}=p
for all

. Using the same arguments mentioned above and note that the condition

is not necessary.

3.2
Mittag-Leffler stability of the systems

This subsection presents our main contribution concerning the asymptotic properties and convergence rate of the solution of the system (
3
) to the origin. Before stating the main result, we introduce the definitions of Mittag-Leffler stability that were previously established in
[
26
,
5
]
.

Definition 3.3
.

The trivial solution of (
3
) is called globally Mittag-Leffler stable if there exists a positive parameter
\beta
such that for any
\varphi\in C([-r,0];\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the solution
\Phi(\cdot,\varphi)
exists on the interval

[0,\infty)
and satisfies

\|\Phi(t,\varphi)\|\leq\nu E_{\beta}(-ct^{\beta}),\ \forall t\geq 0,

c,\nu>0
are parameters depending on
\varphi
,
\alpha
,
f
and
g^{(j)}
,
j=1,\dots,m
.

Definition 3.4
.

The trivial solution of (
3
) is called locally Mittag-Leffler stable if for any
\varphi\in C([-r,0];\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
\|\varphi\|
small enough, the solution
\Phi(\cdot,\varphi)
exists on the interval

[0,\infty)
and satisfies

\|\Phi(t,\varphi)\|\leq\nu E_{\beta}(-ct^{\beta}),\ \forall t\geq 0,

where
\beta
is positive constant independent of the initial condition
\varphi
, and
c,m>0
are parameters depending on
\varphi
,
\alpha
,
f
and
g^{(j)}
,
j=1,\dots,m
.

Theorem 3.5
.

Suppose that assumptions (H1), (H2) and (S) are true. Then, the trivial solution of (
3
) is locally Mittag-Leffler stable.

Proof.

Let
v\succ 0
be a vector satisfying the assumption
(S)
. Define
\beta:=\displaystyle\min_{1\leq i\leq d}\alpha_{i}/p
. Consider the initial condition
\varphi\in C([-r,0];\mathbb{R}^{d}_{\geq 0})
with
\|\varphi\|_{v}<1
. Without loss of generality, we will only focus on the case
\varphi\neq 0
. By virtue of the assumption (H2), for all
i=1,\dots,d
, we can find a constant
c\in(0,1)
verifying the following inequality

\frac{f_{i}({v})}{v_{i}}+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{1}{E_{\beta}(-cr^{\beta})^{q_{j}}}\frac{g_{i}^{(j)}({v})}{v_{i}}+\Big(\displaystyle\frac{\|\varphi\|_{v}}{E_{\beta}(-c)}\Big)^{1-p}c\sup_{t\geq 1}I_{i}(t)\leq 0,

(11)

where
I_{i}(t):=\frac{t^{\beta-\alpha_{i}}E_{\beta,\beta+1-\alpha_{i}}(-ct^{\beta})}{E_{\beta}(-ct^{\beta})}
. Take a constant
\epsilon>0
with
\|\varphi\|_{v}+\epsilon\leq 1
and denote
\nu_{\varepsilon}:=\displaystyle\frac{\|\varphi\|_{v}+\epsilon}{E_{\beta}(-c)}
.
We will prove that

0\leq\Phi_{i}(t,\varphi)<\nu_{\varepsilon}E_{\beta}(-ct^{\beta}),\;\forall t\geq 0,\;i=1,\dots,d.

To do this, we first set

z_{i}(t):=\frac{\Phi_{i}(t,\varphi)}{v_{i}}-\nu_{\varepsilon}E_{\beta}(-ct^{\beta}),\ t\geq 0,\ i=1,\dots,d.

From Proposition
3.1
and the proof of Proposition
3.2
, we see that
\|\Phi(t,\varphi)\|_{v}\leq\|\varphi\|_{v},\ \forall t\geq 0
. Hence,
z_{i}(t)<0
for all
t\in[0,1]
, and
i=1,\dots,d.
Thus, if the assertion that
{z}(t)\prec{0},\ \forall t\geq 0
is false, there exist
t_{*}>1
and
i_{*}
so that

\begin{cases}z_{i_{*}}(t_{*})&=0\ \text{and}\ z_{i}(t_{*})\leq 0,\ \forall i\neq i_{*}\\
z_{i_{*}}(t)&<0,\ \forall t\in[0,t_{*}).\end{cases}

(12)

This means that

\begin{cases}\Phi_{i_{*}}(t_{*},\varphi)&=\nu_{\varepsilon}E_{\beta}(-ct^{\beta})v_{i_{*}}\ \text{and}\ \Phi_{i}(t_{*},\varphi)\leq m_{\varepsilon}E_{\beta}(-ct^{\beta})v_{i},\ \forall i\neq i_{*}\\
\Phi_{i_{*}}(t_{*},\varphi)&<\nu_{\varepsilon}E_{\beta}(-ct^{\beta})v_{i_{*}},\ \forall t\in[0,t_{*}).\end{cases}

(13)

Due to the fact that
f
is cooperative (Proposition
2.6
) and that
f
is homogeneous of degree
p
, it deduces from (
13
) that

f_{i_{*}}(\Phi(t_{*},\varphi))\leq f_{i_{*}}(\nu_{\varepsilon}E_{\beta}(-ct^{\beta}){v})=\left(\nu_{\varepsilon}E_{\beta}(-ct_{*}^{\beta})\right)^{p}f_{i_{*}}({v}).

Notice that, by (
13
), the assumption
(H1)
(
g^{j}(\cdot)
,
j=1,\cdots,m
, is homogeneous of degree
q_{j}
and is order-preserving on
\mathbb{R}^{d}_{\geq 0}
), Lemma
2.5
, and the fact that
E_{\beta}(-ct^{\beta})
is strictly deceasing on
[0,\infty)
, we obtain the estimates below.

1.

If
t_{*}-\tau_{j}(t_{*})\in[0,t_{*}]
, then

\displaystyle g_{i_{*}}^{(j)}(\Phi(t_{*}-\tau_{j}(t_{*}),\varphi))

\displaystyle\leq g_{i_{*}}^{(j)}(\nu_{\varepsilon}E_{\beta}(-c(t_{*}-\tau_{j}(t_{*}))^{\beta}){v})

\displaystyle=(\nu_{\varepsilon}E_{\beta}(-c(t_{*}-\tau_{j}(t_{*}))^{\beta}))^{q_{j}}g_{i_{*}}^{(j)}({v})

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{\nu_{\varepsilon}E_{\beta}(-ct_{*}^{\beta})}{E_{\beta}(-c\tau_{j}(t_{*})^{\beta})}\right)^{q_{j}}g_{i_{*}}^{(j)}({v})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\left(\nu_{\varepsilon}E_{\beta}(-ct_{*}^{\beta})\right)^{p}}{E_{\beta}(-cr^{\beta})^{q_{j}}}g_{i_{*}}^{(j)}({v}).

(14)

2.

If
t_{*}-\tau_{j}(t_{*})\in[-r,0]
, then

\displaystyle g_{i_{*}}^{(j)}(\Phi(t_{*}-\tau_{j}(t_{*}),\varphi))\leq g_{i_{*}}^{(j)}(\nu_{\varepsilon}{v})=\nu_{\varepsilon}^{q_{j}}g_{i_{*}}^{(j)}({v})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\left(\nu_{\varepsilon}E_{\beta}(-ct_{*}^{\beta})\right)^{q_{j}}}{E_{\beta}(-cr^{\beta})^{q_{j}}}g_{i_{*}}^{(j)}({v})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\left(\nu_{\varepsilon}E_{\beta}(-ct_{*}^{\beta})\right)^{p}}{E_{\beta}(-cr^{\beta})^{q_{j}}}g_{i_{*}}^{(j)}({v}).

(15)

Combining (
14
)–(
15
), this deduces

g_{i_{*}}^{(j)}(\Phi(t_{*}-\tau_{j}(t_{*}),\varphi))\leq\frac{\left(\nu_{\varepsilon}E_{\beta}(-ct_{*}^{\beta})\right)^{p}}{E_{\beta}(-cr^{\beta})^{q_{j}}}g_{i_{*}}^{(j)}({v}).

Moreover, using a direct computation, it is obvious to see that

{}^{\!C}D^{{\alpha_{i_{*}}}}_{0^{+}}\left(E_{\beta}(-ct_{*}^{\beta})\right)=-ct_{*}^{\beta-\alpha_{i_{*}}}E_{\beta,1+\beta-\alpha_{i_{*}}}(-ct_{*}^{\beta}).

Thus,

{}^{\!C}D^{{\alpha_{i_{*}}}}_{0^{+}}

\displaystyle z_{i_{*}}(t_{*})=\frac{1}{v_{i_{*}}}{{}^{\!C}D^{{\alpha_{i_{*}}}}_{0^{+}}}\Phi_{i_{*}}(t_{*},\varphi)-^{\!C}D^{{\alpha_{i_{*}}}}_{0^{+}}\left(\nu_{\varepsilon}E_{\beta}(-ct_{*}^{\beta})\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{v_{i_{*}}}\left(f_{i_{*}}(\Phi(t_{*},\varphi))+\sum_{j=1}^{m}g_{i_{*}}^{(j)}(\Phi(t_{*}-\tau_{j}(t_{*}),\varphi))\right)+\nu_{\varepsilon}ct_{*}^{\beta-\alpha_{i_{*}}}E_{\beta,1+\beta-\alpha_{i_{*}}}(-ct_{*}^{\beta})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{v_{i_{*}}}\left[\left(\nu_{\varepsilon}E_{\beta}(-ct_{*}^{\beta})\right)^{p}f_{i_{*}}({v})+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{E_{\beta}(-ct_{*}^{\beta})^{p}}{E_{\beta}(-cr^{\beta})^{q_{j}}}g_{i_{*}}^{(j)}({v})\right]+\nu_{\varepsilon}ct_{*}^{\beta-\alpha}E_{\beta,1+\beta-\alpha_{i_{*}}}(-ct_{*}^{\beta})

\displaystyle=\left(\nu_{\varepsilon}E_{\beta}(-ct_{*}^{\beta})\right)^{p}\left[\frac{f_{i_{*}}({v})}{v_{i_{*}}}+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{g_{i_{*}}^{(j)}({v})}{E_{\beta}(-cr^{\beta})^{q_{j}}v_{i_{*}}}+\nu_{\varepsilon}^{1-p}c\frac{t_{*}^{\beta-\alpha_{i_{*}}}E_{\beta,1+\beta-\alpha_{i_{*}}}(-ct_{*}^{\beta})}{E_{\beta}(-ct_{*}^{\beta})^{p}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\left(\nu_{\varepsilon}E_{\beta}(-ct_{*}^{\beta})\right)^{p}\left[\frac{f_{i_{*}}({v})}{v_{i_{*}}}+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{g_{i_{*}}^{(j)}({v})}{E_{\beta}(-cr^{\beta})^{q_{j}}v_{i_{*}}}+\nu_{\varepsilon}^{1-p}c\sup_{t\geq 1}\frac{t_{*}^{\beta-\alpha_{i_{*}}}E_{\beta,1+\beta-\alpha}(-ct_{*}^{\beta})}{E_{\beta}(-ct_{*}^{\beta})^{p}}\right]

\displaystyle<0,

where the last inequality is derived from (
11
).
However, from (
12
) and Lemma
2.9
, this implies that

{}^{\!C}D^{{\alpha_{i_{*}}}}_{0^{+}}z_{i_{*}}(t_{*})\geq 0
, a contracdiction. From this fact, we obtain that
{z}(t)\prec{0},\ \forall t\geq 0
, and thus

\frac{\Phi_{i}(t,\varphi)}{v_{i}}<\nu_{\varepsilon}E_{\beta}(-ct^{\beta}),\ \forall t\geq 0,\ \forall i=1,\dots,d.

Let

, then

\frac{\Phi_{i}(t,\varphi)}{v_{i}}\leq\nu E_{\beta}(-ct^{\beta}),\ \forall t\geq 0,\ \forall i=1,\dots,d,

here

∎

Remark 3.6
.

Theorem
3.5
provides a criterion to test the stability and the convergence rate of non-trivial solutions of multi-order fractional cooperative delay systems to the origin. Depending on the situation when the degrees of homogeneity of vector fields are equal or different, we get the global or local stability. To our knowledge, this result has not previously appeared in the literature.

Remark 3.7
.

By
[
11
, Lemma 4.25, p. 86]
, the functions
E_{\beta,\beta+1-\alpha_{i}}(-ct^{\beta})
,
i=1,\dots,d
,
E_{\beta}(-ct^{\beta})
are strictly decreasing on
[0,\infty)
. Hence, for
t\geq 1
, we see that

0\leq\frac{t^{\beta-\alpha_{i}}E_{\beta,\beta+1-\alpha_{i}}(-ct^{\beta})}{E_{\beta}(-ct^{\beta})}\leq\frac{E_{\beta,\beta+1-\alpha_{i}}(-ct^{\beta})}{E_{\beta}(-ct^{\beta})},\;\forall i=1,\dots,d.

Furthermore, from
[

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{E_{\beta,\beta+1-\alpha_{i}}(-ct^{\beta})}{E_{\beta}(-ct^{\beta})}=\frac{\Gamma(1-\beta)}{\Gamma(1-\alpha_{i})},\;i=1,\dots,d.

Thus, the observations above lead to that
\displaystyle\sup_{t\geq 1}\frac{t^{\beta-\alpha_{i}}E_{\beta,\beta+1-\alpha_{i}}(-ct^{\beta})}{E_{\beta}(-ct^{\beta})}
is finite.

Gravitational waves in massive Horndeski theory with a cosmological constant
Hatice Özer, Özgür Delice

Abstract
We study gravitational waves in a de-Sitter background in linearized Horndeski theory. This background can be obtained in different ways in this theory. We first consider an arbitrary potential to implement an effective cosmological constant. The scalar field has long range whereas its potential can induce a mass term, rendering its range to short distances. A second effect of the potential is the possibility to induce a background curvature or an effective cosmological constant via the minimum of the potential. The latter is usually ignored in the literature to have an asymptotically flat background for the gravitational waves. In order to fill this gap, we consider the effects of the scalar potential in the framework of gravitational waves in the theories having such scalar couplings, and compare its effects with other approaches such as the presence of a vacuum energy density filling the spacetime or a linear potential which leads to an effective cosmological constant but now with a massless scalar field. We discuss properties of the gravitational waves, their propagation and interaction with the medium in which they propagate. The minimum of the scalar potential term mimics a cosmological constant and leads to separate redshifts in the effective frequency and wave number, which also discriminates the tensorial and massive scalar waves. We see that, depending on the implementation to have a cosmological constant, the propagation speeds and polarization properties of scalar modes of these waves is different, as summarized in the paper.

(42)

Here and in the following expressions we will consider the real part of
\varphi^{W}

The effect of the potential that produces the effective mass in the wave solutions comes from the solution of the scalar field equation. The most important result of this solution is that scalar waves move slower than tensor waves in this theory. So, in a way, the mass of the scalar field acts as a slowing medium for scalar waves.

Bernabeu1

In this theory, since the potential of the scalar field adds mass to the scalar field, the scalar waves move slower than the speed of light. Tensor waves, on the other hand, are compatible with the predictions of the General Relativity theory and the waves move at the speed of light. Total effect of the gravitational waves in Horndeski theory is a combination of both scalar and tensorial waves, as we will discuss in the next section regarding the effects of the background curvature generated from the minimum of the potential.

IV
The Effects of GWs on Free Particles in Massive Horndeski Theory

In this section, we will investigate how gravitational waves interact with test particles or detectors in the space-time geometry in the massive Horndeski theory and then analyze the polarization properties of these waves. Since the polarization properties of gravitational waves of Horndeski theory is well known

The test particles follow the geodesics of that spacetime in the curved spacetime. Therefore, we will use geodesic deviation equations to study the effects of gravitational waves on a free particle. Due to the equivalence principle, no gravitational field acts on a particle located at a single point in space-time. When a gravitational wave passes through a point, it does not change the coordinates of that point, but during passage, it changes the physical distance between two distinct points located on the spacetime. Therefore, a single particle is not affected by space-time fluctuations. be the arrival time of the nth polarization mode of the gravitational wave from the source to the observer, then

t_{n}=\frac{R}{c_{n}},

(82)

where
R
is the distance of the source to the detector, and
c_{n}
is the speed of the polarization mode.
The differences in arrival times for the two polarizations
a
and
b
, can be given by
Schumacher
:

\Delta t_{ab}=t_{a}-t_{b}=\frac{R}{c_{a}}-\frac{R}{c_{b}}.

(83)

Let
c_{a}
and
c_{b}
be the speed of tensorial and scalar waves with
c_{a}=c
and
c_{b}=v
, respectively.
From this equation one can obtain an expression for the speed of the massive scalar wave must have to cause a time difference
\Delta t_{ab}
as
Schumacher
:

v=\sqrt{1-\frac{m_{s}^{2}}{\omega^{2}}}=\left(\frac{1}{c}-\frac{\Delta t_{ab}}{R}\right)^{-1}.

(84)

Using same figures with
Schumacher
for a source with distance
R=100\,\mbox{Mpc}\approx 10^{24}\,\mbox{m}
for a time difference 2048 seconds, the speed of massive scalar modes should satisfy
v\geq c\left[1+\mathcal{O}{(10^{-13})}\right],
which is too small to observe. We may need much longer observational time to observe possible speed difference of scalar modes. For example, using again the example of
Schumacher
for ten observational years or
3\times 10^{8}
seconds, we see
v\approx c\left[1+\mathcal{O}{(10^{-8})}\right]
. These results show that even the propagation speed has very small difference from speed of light, the scalar wave can be missed to be observed by the observational window the detector operates, even if it is capable of detecting them. The equation (
84
) also relates the mass of the scalar field arrival time difference of scalar waves compared to tensorial waves from the same source. Indeed, it can also be written, using the explicit dependence of speed of massive scalar waves, as

m_{s}=\omega\sqrt{1-\left(\frac{1}{c}-\frac{\Delta t_{ab}}{R}\right)^{-2}}.

(85)

Imagine if the distance of the source, the frequency of scalar waves and the time arrival difference is spotted in a future observation, then one can find the mass of the scalar field from this observation. This mass value can then constrain the theories admitting massive scalar fields such as massive BD theory or Horndeski theory, namely, the parameters given in equation (
12
).

VI
A Comparison of different implementations of cosmological constant in Horndeski theory

Our aim in this paper was to determine and analyze gravitational wave solutions in the de Sitter background, rather than asymptotically flat one as done usually in the literature. This can be done in the Horndeski theory in different ways, as we will discuss below. Up to this point, we have done this for an arbitrary potential whose minimum plays the role of a cosmological constant. One can call this approach as the massive Horndeski theory since the potential produces an effective mass to the scalar field. One way of obtaining this particular theory is to have a potential term
U(\phi)
in the term
L_{2}

2

In section II we have presented the linearized equations and in III their solutions and in IV some physical applications of that implementation. As an example of this approach, we also discuss a particular quadratic potential in the following discussion. We now discuss other ways of achieving an asymptotically de Sitter background and also their properties by comparing with massive Horndeski theory that we have already obtained up to now.

VII
Conclusions

In this paper we have determined and analyzed linearized gravitational wave solutions in the de-Sitter background, rather than asymptotically flat one usually discussed in the literature for the Horndeski theory.
Firstly, we have obtained gravitational wave solutions in massive Horndeski theory in the presence of a background curvature originated from the minimum of the potential mimicking a cosmological constant. Since the scalar field has an effective mass in general for this case, it has short range, as in massive Brans Dicke theory and
f(R)
theories. After obtaining these solutions, we have discussed the properties of gravitational waves, their propagation and interaction with the medium in which they propagate by taking into account the background curvature. We have studied the effects of these waves on test particles and detectors by calculating the geodesic deviation by the gravitational wave of two test particles initially at rest. In GR, the GWs present only two polarization states, the
+
and
\times
modes. However, when the framework of massive Horndeski theory is considered, the number of nonzero components of the Riemann tensor and hence of polarization modes is greater than two
Houa
. In accordance with existing literature, we obtained that the waves have three polarization modes, two of them are usual plus and cross modes predicted by the General Relativity and the other one is a mixture of longitudinal and transverse breathing modes caused by the massive scalar field
Dicong
;
Houa
. Therefore, the massive scalar field excites both the transverse and the longitudinal polarizations and longitudinal polarization vanishes when the scalar field becomes massless.
We see that the combined effect of the effective cosmological constant and the background scalar field is to exert an homogeneous expanding force on these particles to extend the length between them similar to Hubble expansion of two galaxies due to the dark energy. The obtained solutions were in accordance with the sub-theories of Horndeski theory. These waves behave similarly to gravitational waves in massive-BD or
f(R)
theories. Therefore, the solutions and analyzes obtained in this theory can be used to test the predictions of different gravity theories at various scales.

The massive case is not the only way to have a de-Sitter background in Horndeski theory. The other ways include a linear potential leading to a massless long range scalar together with a cosmological constant or a vacuum energy density filling the universe. The differences between massless, linear potential or vacuum energy cases can be realized in the propagation speeds and polarization properties of these waves. A detailed comparison of properties of these alternative approaches were discussed in section IV via several tables. Only if the potential is an arbitrary function of the scalar field whose second derivative is non vanishing, the longitudinal scalar mode is present and scalar waves propagate with speed less than speed of light. Otherwise, only the breathing mode is present which propagates with the speed of light. The form of the background solution presenting a de Sitter background for this theory is also have some numerical differences for different choices for the potential, since the scalar equations and their solutions will be different for each choice of the scalar potential as we have presented in the last columns of Table I and II. This observation is also compatible with the previous work
Ozer
for BD theory with different choices to have a cosmological constant.

The effect originated from the minimum of the potential is to cause an accelerated expansion of the nearby test particles and mimicking an of the effective cosmological constant causing expansion of the universe. In order to see these effects better we have transformed the solution into FRW type coordinates. The obtained solution then enable us to analyze the effect of this expansion on the frequency and wave vector of these waves for both tensorial and scalar gravitational waves. The waves are redshifted by cosmological expansion. The wave vector is also redshifted but for both tensorial and scalar waves the amount of redshift is different for wave frequency and wave vector. This effect was observed before for GR
Bernabeu1
but we have extended this result to general scalar-tensor theories having massive scalar fields in this work and massless scalar fields
Ozer
for BD theory. The expressions we have obtained and also their differences for scalar and tensorial waves may open an observational window to determine properties of the Horndeski theory and its sub theories, such as effective Newton constant, minimum of the potential and mass of the scalar field in future observations.

We know that current wave observations are in agreement with the predictions of general relativity and the waves are transverse and travel at the speed of light. However, according to the results for Horndeski theory and other scalar tensor theories in the literature, these waves may also have scalar modes. Current GW detectors are insufficient to resolve these scalar modes, yet if they exist. However, if scalar waves and their propagation speeds can be observed in other detector systems planned to be built in the future using a network of detectors with different orientations, the presence or mass of scalar waves can be determined, or limits can be put on their mass and speed. Therefore, we think that our results can contribute to the results of future detector systems, especially future space-borne detectors.

There may be several future directions that this work may inspire. For example, application of theories having massless or massive scalar fields with effective cosmological constant to PTA observations, as was done in GR case in the works
EspriuPTA
;
Espriu
, or generalizing the solutions by including other cosmic fluids
EspriuRodera
;
Espriu1
could be a future directions to generalize this work. Another direction could be focusing on local effects and scattering of these waves in Horndeski theory, in which we are currently working on, in the presence of a nontrivial scalar potential term.

Critical exponent for the one-dimensional wave equation with a space-dependent scale invariant damping and time derivative nonlinearity
Ahmad Z. Fino

Abstract
We investigate in this paper the Cauchy problem of the one-dimensional wave equation with space-dependent damping of the form μ 0 ( 1 + x 2 ) − 1 / 2 \mu_{0}(1+x^{2})^{-1/2} , where μ 0 > 0 \mu_{0}>0 , and time derivative nonlinearity. We establish global existence of mild solutions for small data compactly supported by employing energy estimates within suitable Sobolev spaces of the associated homogeneous problem. Furthermore, we derive a blow-up result under some positive initial data by employing the test function method. This shows that the critical exponent is given by p G ​ ( 1 + μ 0 ) = 1 + 2 / μ 0 p_{G}(1+\mu_{0})=1+2/\mu_{0} , when μ 0 ∈ ( 0 , 1 ] \mu_{0}\in(0,1] , where p G p_{G} is the Glassey exponent. To the best of our knowledge, this constitutes the first identification of the critical exponent range for this class of equations. As by product, we extend the global existence result to a more general class of space/time nonlinearities of the form f ⁡ ( ∂ t u , ∂ x u ) = | ∂ x u | q f(\partial_{t}u,\partial_{x}u)=|\partial_{x}u|^{q} or f ⁡ ( ∂ t u , ∂ x u ) = | ∂ t u | p ​ | ∂ x u | q f(\partial_{t}u,\partial_{x}u)=|\partial_{t}u|^{p}|\partial_{x}u|^{q} , with p , q > 1 p,q>1 .

Introduction

In this work, we consider the Cauchy problem for the wave equations with
critical space-dependent damping
and power-nonlinearity of derivative type

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{2}u-\partial_{x}^{2}u+V(x)\partial_{t}u=f(\partial_{t}u),&\quad x\in\mathbb{R},\,t>0,\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\quad\partial_{t}u(x,0)=u_{1}(x),&\quad x\in\mathbb{R},\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

where
p>1
,
V(x)=\mu_{0}(1+x^{2})^{-1/2}
,
\mu_{0}>0
,
f(\partial_{t}u)=|\partial_{t}u|^{p}
or
|\partial_{t}u|^{p-1}\partial_{t}u
, and

(u_{0},u_{1})
are compactly supported initial data in the following Sobolev space

(u_{0},u_{1})\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R})\times H^{1}(\mathbb{R}).

(1.2)

Such problems appear in models for wave propagation in a nonhomogeneous gas with damping, where the space-dependent coefficients represent either friction coefficients or potential (see
[
22
]
).

In this paper, we investigate both the global existence of small data solutions and the finite-time blow-up of solutions to the Cauchy problem for the nonlinear wave equation (
1.1
). These results allow us to determine the critical exponent for
\mu_{0}\in(0,1]
.

1.1
Prior results

Before going to the main result, we shall review prior studies on the global existence/nonexistence of solutions to some nonlinear damped wave equations with various type of potentials and nonlinearities.

For the Cauchy problem of the semilinear damped wave equation with power nonlinearity and when the variable coefficients are missing,

\partial_{tt}u-\Delta u+\partial_{t}u=|u|^{p},\qquad t>0,\,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

(1.3)

it has been conjectured that (
1.3
) has the diffuse structure as
t\rightarrow\infty
(see e.g.
[
3
]
), i.e. the solutions of the damped equation seem to behave more like solutions of the corresponding diffusion/heat equation
\partial_{t}u-\Delta u=|u|^{p}
at large times. This suggests that problem (
1.3
) should have
p_{F}(n):=1+{2}/{n}
as critical exponent which is called the Fujita exponent named after the pioneering work by Fujita
[
21
]
. Indeed, Li and Zhou
[
32
]
proved that if
n\leq 2
,
1<p\leq 1+2/n
and the data are positive on average, then the local
solution of (
1.3
) must blows up in a finite time. Moreover,
Todorova and Yordanov
[
43
]
and Zhang
[
47
]
showed that the critical exponent of (
1.3
) is the predicted one for any
n\geq 1
. We mention that Todorova and Yordanov
[
43
]
proved the global existence by assuming that the data are compactly supported.

The study of
the analogous problem of (
1.3
)
with space-dependent damping, namely

\partial_{t}^{2}u-\Delta u+\frac{\mu_{0}}{(1+|x|^{2})^{\alpha/2}}\partial_{t}u=|u|^{p}\quad\text{in}\,\,\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,\infty),

(1.4)

has been the subject of
several works in the literature. First,
for
\alpha\in(0,1)
the
critical exponent is given by the Fujita-type exponent
p_{F}(n-\alpha)=1+2/(n-\alpha)
(see Ikehata, Todorova and Yordanov
[
26
]
).
Later, Nishihara, Sobajima and Wakasugi proved in
[
34
]

that the critical exponent remains the Fujita-type exponent, for
\alpha<0
.
The situation is completely different
when
\alpha>1
, (scatering case). More precisely,
Lai and Tu
[
30
]

showed that the critical exponent of (
1.4
) is given by
p=p_{S}(n)
, where
p_{S}(n)
stands for the
so-called Strauss exponent which is the critical exponent of the
well-known equation
\partial_{t}^{2}u-\Delta u=|u|^{p}
.
This explains the scattering phenomena.
Finally, when
\alpha=1
, the problem (
1.4
) has a
scale-invariance
and the critical exponent seems
\max(p_{F}(n-1),p_{S}(n+\mu_{0}))
.
In fact, Li
[
31
]
proved the expected result in the case
\mu_{0}\geq n
.
Also, the blow-up part of this conjecture is known (for small
values of
\mu_{0}
).
For the singular damping term
a(x)=a_{0}|x|^{-1}
, global existence has been investigated in the case of an exterior domain in a recent paper by Sobajima
[
39
]
. In the case of whole space, a blow-up result and lifespan estimate were also established by Ikeda and Sobajima
[
23
]
.

In the context of the general case where the damping coefficient is critical and is a function of both time and space
namely
V(x,t)=\mu_{0}(1+|x|^{2})^{-\alpha/2}(1+t)^{-\beta},
the situation is much less understood.
We refer the reader to
[
34
,
44
]
.

Now, we turn back to the classical semilinear wave equation with time derivative nonlinearity without damping term, namely

\partial^{2}_{t}u-\Delta u=|\partial_{t}u|^{p},\quad\mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,\infty),

(1.5)

for which the critical exponent is determined by the Glassey exponent

p_{G}=p_{G}(n):=1+\frac{2}{n-1},

(1.6)

see e.g.
[
1
,
19
,
20
,
27
,
37
,
38
,
48
]
.

For the case with time dependent damping in the scale-invariant case, namely the following equation

\partial^{2}_{t}u-\Delta u+\frac{\mu_{0}}{1+t}\partial_{t}u=|\partial_{t}u|^{p},\quad\mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,\infty),

(1.7)

the blow-up region for
p\in(1,p_{G}(n+\mu_{0})]
and lifespan estimates for solutions of (
1.7
) are established in
[
13
]
, improving upon the earlier results in
[
36
,
35
]
. On the one hand, research on blow-up phenomena and lifespan estimates has centered on the scale-invariant model equation (
1.7
), particularly the failure of small-data global solutions when (
1.7

[

16

17

analyze Tricomi-type models to show blow-up, while

[

13

reveal how mixed nonlinearities drive singularity formation.
On the other hand, our recent work in
[
11

demonstrates global existence for the one-dimensional case, leading to the identification of
p_{G}(1+\mu_{0})
as the critical exponent for
\mu_{0}\in(0,2]

Replacing the damping term in equation (
1.7
) with
1/(1+t)^{\alpha}\partial_{t}u
, for
\alpha>1
, leads to dynamics characterized by the scattering regime.
Due to the weakened damping effect for large time, its influence on the long-term dynamics is expected to be negligible. Consequently, the critical exponent is expected to coincide with the Glassey exponent, defined in (
1.6
). While Lai and Takamura in
[
35
]
have demonstrated the blow-up part, the question of global existence remains an open problem.

In the same way, the analogous nonlinear problem (
1.1
) in higher dimension with

V(x)
being changed by

\mu_{0}(1+|x|^{2})^{-\alpha/2},
and
\alpha>1
,

\partial_{t}^{2}u-\Delta u+\frac{\mu_{0}}{(1+|x|^{2})^{\alpha/2}}\partial_{t}u=|\partial_{t}u|^{p}\quad\text{in}\,\,\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,\infty),

(1.8)

which corresponds to the scattering case,
it is reasonable to expect that
the blow-up region of the solution of (
1.8
) is similar to the case of the pure wave equation and the scattering damping term has no
influence in the dynamics.
The predicted blow-up result was obtained in the case where
\alpha>2
, by Lai and Tu
[
30
]
.
However, up to our knowledge, there is no result in the case where
\alpha\in(1,2].

The condition
\alpha>2
seems to be technical. Therefore, the result stated in
[
30
]
should be extended to the case
\alpha>1
. It is important to note that the question of global existence remains an open problem.

Recently, in
[
10
]
, we proved that the blow-up region of the present problem (
1.1

p\in(1,p_{G}(n+\mu_{0})]
in higher dimensions

. However, up to our knowledge, there are no results on the blow-up in finite time in the one dimensional case as well as the small data global existence of solutions is still an open problem in any dimension.

This paper aims to address and resolve the critical exponent problem associated with equation (
1.1
). As previously explained for the problem (
1.7
) in the one-dimensional case, where damping induces in the critical exponent a shift in the dimensional parameter of magnitude
\mu_{0}
, for
\mu_{0}\in(0,2]
, we naturally expect
that the same phenomenon holds for the problem (
1.1
) at least for small values of
\mu_{0}
. In other words, the predicted critical exponent is
p_{c}(1,\mu_{0})=p_{G}(1+\mu_{0})
, at least for small values of
\mu_{0}
.
We will achieve this in two parts.

•

Part I: Establish global existence results for the problem (
1.1
), it is essential to analyze the associated linear problem and utilize decay rates in appropriate Sobolev spaces. That question
was done by Ikehata-Todorova-Yordanov in
[
24
]
. Indeed, they showed that, in the case where the initial data are compactly supported, the solution satisfies the same kind of energy estimates, namely

\|(\nabla u(t),\partial_{t}u(t))\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\gamma},
for some
\gamma>0
, and where the constant
C
depends on the support of the initial data.
We would like to draw the attention of the reader to the fact that a better understanding of the dependence of the constant related to the initial data is crucial for establishing a global existence result for the corresponding nonlinear problem. In this context, in recent work in
[
39
]
, the author successfully established a global existence result for the nonlinear problem with space-dependent damping in an exterior domain. This achievement was made by employing weighted energy estimates for the associated linear problem.
In the present paper, we start by studying the associated homogeneous problem, by using energy estimates
for compactly supported initial data. Subsequently, we address the nonlinearity using the fixed-point theorem.
Therefore, the following upper bound for the critical exponent of problem (
1.1
) is obtained:

p_{c}(1,\mu_{0})\leq 1+\frac{2}{\alpha_{0}},

(1.9)

where

\alpha_{0}:=\min(1,\mu_{0}).

(1.10)

•

Part II:
For initial data with compact support, we derive a blow-up result for the nonlinear wave equation (
1.1
), by employing the test function method. Specifically, we employ a test function constructed as the product of a cut-off function and an explicit solution to the adjoint equation associated with the linear part of (
1.1

1.1

Hence, the following lower bound for the critical exponent of problem (
1.1
) is established:

p_{c}(1,\mu_{0})\geq 1+\frac{2}{\mu_{0}}.

(1.11)

By combining the results from Parts I and II, we easily deduce that

p_{c}(1,\mu_{0})=1+\frac{2}{\mu_{0}},\quad\textrm{if}\,\,\mu_{0}\in(0,1].

(1.12)

The second subsection is devoted to refining these energy estimates, yielding sharper decay bounds.

Subsequently, we combine the previous energy estimates to establish the proof of Proposition
2.5
.

2.1
A bound of the
H^{1}\times L^{2}
norm of
(v,\partial_{t}v)

This subsection presents Lemma
2.2
, which based on the construction of a Lyapunov functional. We begin by introducing three crucial functionals as follows:

\displaystyle E_{0}(v(t),\partial_{t}v(t))

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big((\partial_{x}v(t))^{2}+(\partial_{t}v(t))^{2}\big){\mathrm{d}}x,\qquad

(2.2)

\displaystyle I(v(t),\partial_{t}v(t),t)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big(v(t)\partial_{t}v(t)+\frac{1}{2(t+1)}(v(t))^{2}+\frac{V(x)}{2}(v(t))^{2}\big){\mathrm{d}}x,\quad

(2.3)

\displaystyle F_{A}(v(t),\partial_{t}v(t),t)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle AE_{0}(v(t),\partial_{t}v(t))+\frac{1}{2(t+1)}I(v(t),\partial_{t}v(t),t),\quad

(2.4)

where
A>0
is a constant to be specified later. By evaluating the time derivative of
E_{0}(v(t),\partial_{t}v(t),t)
,
I(v(t),\partial_{t}v(t),t)
and
F_{A}(v(t),\partial_{t}v(t),t)
, we establish the following result.

Lemma 2.2
.

Assume

(g(s_{0}),h(s_{0}))\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R})\times H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
that are compactly supported
on
(-R_{0}-s_{0},R_{0}+s_{0})
, with
R_{0}>0
, then there exists a constant
C_{0}=C_{0}(R_{0},\mu_{0})>0
such that the strong solution
v
of (
2.1
) satisfies,
for all
t\geq s_{0}
,

\displaystyle\frac{\|v(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}}{(1+t)^{2}}+\frac{\|\sqrt{V}v(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}}{1+t}+\|\partial_{t}v(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{x}v(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}\left(\frac{\|g(s_{0})\|^{2}_{L^{2}}}{(1+s_{0})^{2}}+\frac{\|\sqrt{V}g(s_{0})\|^{2}_{L^{2}}}{1+s_{0}}+\|h(s_{0})\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{x}g(s_{0})\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\right).

Proof.

Since

(g(s_{0}),h(s_{0}))\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R})\times H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, Theorem
2.1
guarantees that the solution
v
to problem (
2.1
) satisfies

v\in\bigcap_{j=0}^{2}\mathcal{C}^{j}\left([s_{0},\infty),H^{2-j}(\mathbb{R})\right)
. As a consequence, the energy functional
E_{0}(v(t),\partial_{t}v(t))
is a differentiable with respect to time. Moreover, by multiplying the equation in (
2.1
) by
\partial_{t}v(t)
and integrating by parts, we get

\frac{d}{dt}E_{0}(v(t),\partial_{t}v(t))=-\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}V(x)(\partial_{t}v(t))^{2}{\mathrm{d}}x,\qquad\hbox{for all}\,\,t>s_{0}.

(2.5)

Similarly, the functional
I(v(t),\partial_{t}v(t),t)
is differentiable with respect to time. Proceeding in the same manner, by multiplying the equation in (
2.1
) by
v(t)
and applying integration by parts, we obtain, for all
t>s_{0}
,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}I(v(t),\partial_{t}v(t),t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\partial_{t}v(t))^{2}{\mathrm{d}}x-\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\partial_{x}v(t))^{2}{\mathrm{d}}x

(2.6)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2(1+t)^{2}}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}(v(t))^{2}{\mathrm{d}}x+\frac{1}{1+t}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}v(t)\partial_{t}v(t){\mathrm{d}}x.

On the other hand, by making use of identities (
2.5
), (
2.6
), along with the definition of
F_{A}(v(t),\partial_{t}v(t),t)
given by (
2.4
), we infer that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}F_{A}(v(t),\partial_{t}v(t),t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-A\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}V(x)(\partial_{t}v(t))^{2}{\mathrm{d}}x+\frac{1}{2(1+t)}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\partial_{t}v(t))^{2}{\mathrm{d}}x-\frac{1}{2(1+t)}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\partial_{x}v(t))^{2}{\mathrm{d}}x

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2(1+t)^{3}}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}(v(t))^{2}{\mathrm{d}}x-\frac{1}{4(1+t)^{2}}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}V(x)(v(t))^{2}{\mathrm{d}}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-A\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}V(x)(\partial_{t}v(t))^{2}{\mathrm{d}}x+\frac{1}{2(1+t)}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\partial_{t}v(t))^{2}{\mathrm{d}}x.

Using the fact that the support of
v
is contained within the region
|x|\leq R_{0}+t
, we have

V(x)\geq\frac{\mu_{0}}{\sqrt{1+(R_{0}+t)^{2}}}\geq\frac{\mu_{0}}{R_{0}+1+t}\geq\frac{\mu_{0}}{(1+R_{0})(1+t)},

for any
t\geq s_{0}
. Therefore, for all
A\geq(1+R_{0})/2\mu_{0}
, it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}F_{A}(v(t),\partial_{t}v(t),t)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{A\mu_{0}}{1+R_{0}}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{(\partial_{t}v(t))^{2}}{1+t}{\mathrm{d}}x\leq 0.

Hence, by integrating over the interval
[s_{0},t]
we arrive at

F_{A}(v(t),\partial_{t}v(t),t)\leq F_{A}(v(s_{0}),\partial_{t}v(s_{0}),s_{0}),\quad\hbox{for any}\,\,t\geq s_{0}.

(2.7)

Moreover, by applying Young’s inequality
|ab|\leq\frac{1}{4}a^{2}+b^{2}
with
a=v/(1+t)
,
b=\partial_{t}v
, we obtain

\frac{1}{2}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{v(t)\partial_{t}v(t)}{1+t}{\mathrm{d}}x\geq-\frac{1}{8}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{(v(t))^{2}}{(1+t)^{2}}{\mathrm{d}}x-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\partial_{t}v(t))^{2}{\mathrm{d}}x,

which implies,

\displaystyle F_{A}(v(t),\partial_{t}v(t),t)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{A}{2}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\partial_{x}v(t))^{2}{\mathrm{d}}x+\frac{A-1}{2}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\partial_{t}v(t))^{2}{\mathrm{d}}x

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{8}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{(v(t))^{2}}{(1+t)^{2}}{\mathrm{d}}x+\frac{1}{4}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{V(x)(v(t))^{2}}{1+t}{\mathrm{d}}x.

By choosing
A=\max(5/4,(1+R_{0})/2\mu_{0})
, this leads to

F_{A}(v(t),\partial_{t}v(t),t)\geq\frac{1}{8}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\frac{(v(t))^{2}}{(t+1)^{2}}+\frac{V(x)(v(t))^{2}}{t+1}+(\partial_{x}v(t))^{2}+(\partial_{t}v(t))^{2}\right){\mathrm{d}}x,\,\,\hbox{for all}\,\,t\geq s_{0}.

(2.8)

Alternatively, using the standard Young’s inequality, we conclude that

F_{A}(v(t),\partial_{t}v(t),t)\leq\frac{2A+1}{4}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\frac{(v(t))^{2}}{(t+1)^{2}}+\frac{V(x)(v(t))^{2}}{t+1}+(\partial_{x}v(t))^{2}+(\partial_{t}v(t))^{2}\right){\mathrm{d}}x,\,\,\hbox{for all}\,\,t\geq s_{0}.

(2.9)

From (
2.8
) and (
2.9
), there exists a constant
C=C(R_{0},\mu_{0})
such that for all
t\geq s_{0}
,

C^{-1}F_{A}(v(t),\partial_{t}v(t),t)\leq\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\frac{(v(t))^{2}}{(t+1)^{2}}+\frac{V(x)(v(t))^{2}}{t+1}+(\partial_{x}v(t))^{2}+(\partial_{t}v(t))^{2}\right){\mathrm{d}}x\leq CF_{A}(v(t),\partial_{t}v(t),t).

(2.10)

Combining inequalities (
2.7
) and (
2.10
), the proof of Lemma
2.2
is complete.
∎

2.2
Decay estimates of the
H^{1}\times L^{2}
norm of
(v,\partial_{t}v)
for
H^{2}\times H^{1}
data

To refine the decay estimate from Lemma
2.2
, it is necessary to introduce a different set of energy functionals depending on the parameter
\mu_{0}
. This leads us to consider two distinct cases.

First case:
\mu_{0}\in(0,1]
.

Let
\mu\in(0,\mu_{0})
. We introduce the following functionals:

\displaystyle E_{1}(v(t),\partial_{t}v(t),t)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big(v(t)\partial_{t}v(t)+\frac{1-\mu}{2(t+1)}(v(t))^{2}+\frac{V(x)}{2}(v(t))^{2}\big){\mathrm{d}}x,\quad

(2.11)

\displaystyle E_{2}(v(t),\partial_{t}v(t),t)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle E_{0}(v(t),\partial_{t}v(t))+\frac{\mu}{2(t+1)}E_{1}(v(t),\partial_{t}v(t),t),\quad

(2.12)

for all
t\geq s_{0}
, where
E_{0}(v(t),\partial_{t}v(t))
is given by (
2.2
). By evaluating the time derivative of
E_{i}(v(t),\partial_{t}v(t),t)
, for
i=1,2
, we are led to the following lemma.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\mu_{0}\in(0,1]
and
\mu\in(0,\mu_{0})
. Assume

(g(s_{0}),h(s_{0}))\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R})\times H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
that are compactly supported
on
(-R_{0}-s_{0},R_{0}+s_{0})
, with
R_{0}>0
, then there exists a constant
C_{1}=C_{1}(R_{0},\mu,\mu_{0})>0
such that the strong solution
v
of (
2.1
) satisfies,
for all
t\geq s_{0}
,

\displaystyle\frac{\|v(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}}{(1+t)^{2}}+\frac{\|\sqrt{V}v(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}}{1+t}+\|\partial_{t}v(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{x}v(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\left(\frac{1+s_{0}}{1+t}\right)^{\mu}\left(\frac{\|g(s_{0})\|^{2}_{L^{2}}}{(1+s_{0})^{2}}+\frac{\|\sqrt{V}g(s_{0})\|^{2}_{L^{2}}}{1+s_{0}}+\|h(s_{0})\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{x}g(s_{0})\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\right).

(2.13)

Proof.

By multiplying the equation in (
2.1
) by
v(t)
and applying integration by parts, we obtain the following identity for all
t>s_{0}
,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}E_{1}(v(t),\partial_{t}v(t),t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\partial_{t}v(t))^{2}{\mathrm{d}}x-\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\partial_{x}v(t))^{2}{\mathrm{d}}x

(2.14)

\displaystyle-\frac{1-\mu}{2(1+t)^{2}}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}(v(t))^{2}{\mathrm{d}}x+\frac{1-\mu}{1+t}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}v(t)\partial_{t}v(t){\mathrm{d}}x.

By exploiting (
2.5
), (
2.14
) and the definition of
E_{2}(v(t),\partial_{t}v(t),t)
given in (
2.12
), we infer that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}E_{2}(v(t),\partial_{t}v(t),t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}V(x)(\partial_{t}v(t))^{2}{\mathrm{d}}x+\frac{\mu}{2(1+t)}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\partial_{t}v(t))^{2}{\mathrm{d}}x-\frac{\mu}{2(1+t)}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\partial_{x}v(t))^{2}{\mathrm{d}}x

\displaystyle-\frac{\mu(1-\mu)}{2(1+t)^{3}}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}(v(t))^{2}{\mathrm{d}}x-\frac{\mu^{2}}{2(1+t)^{2}}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}v(t)\partial_{t}v(t){\mathrm{d}}x

\displaystyle-\frac{\mu}{4(1+t)^{2}}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}V(x)(v(t))^{2}{\mathrm{d}}x.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}E_{2}(v(t),\partial_{t}v(t),t)+\frac{\mu}{1+t}E_{2}(v(t),\partial_{t}v(t),t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}V(x)(\partial_{t}v(t))^{2}{\mathrm{d}}x+\frac{\mu}{1+t}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\partial_{t}v(t))^{2}{\mathrm{d}}x

\displaystyle-\frac{\mu(1-\mu)(2-\mu)}{4(1+t)^{3}}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}(v(t))^{2}{\mathrm{d}}x

\displaystyle-\frac{\mu(1-\mu)}{4(1+t)^{2}}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}V(x)(v(t))^{2}{\mathrm{d}}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}V(x)(\partial_{t}v(t))^{2}{\mathrm{d}}x+\frac{\mu}{1+t}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\partial_{t}v(t))^{2}{\mathrm{d}}x.

Using the fact that
\supp(v)\subseteq\{|x|\leq R_{0}+t\}
, we have

V(x)\geq\frac{\mu_{0}}{\sqrt{1+(R_{0}+t)^{2}}}\geq\frac{\mu_{0}}{R_{0}+1+t}
, then there exists
t_{0}=t_{0}(R_{0},\mu,\mu_{0})=\frac{\mu R_{0}}{\mu_{0}-\mu}>0
such that
V(x)\geq\mu/(1+t)
for all
t\geq t_{0}
. Hence, for all
t\geq\tau_{0}:=\max\{s_{0},t_{0}\}
, it follows that

\frac{d}{dt}E_{2}(v(t),\partial_{t}v(t),t)+\frac{\mu}{1+t}E_{2}(v(t),\partial_{t}v(t),t)\leq-\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(V(x)-\frac{\mu}{1+t}\right)(\partial_{t}v(t))^{2}{\mathrm{d}}x\leq 0.

(2.15)

Multiplying (
2.15
) by
(1+t)^{\mu}
and integrating over the interval
[\tau_{0},t]
, we deduce that, for all
t\geq\tau_{0}
,

(1+t)^{\mu}E_{2}(v(t),\partial_{t}v(t),t)\leq(1+\tau_{0})^{\mu}E_{2}(v(\tau_{0}),\partial_{t}v(\tau_{0}),\tau_{0}).

(2.16)

In addition, applying Young’s inequality, we obtain

\frac{\mu}{2}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{v(t)\partial_{t}v(t)}{1+t}{\mathrm{d}}x\geq-\frac{\mu}{8}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{(v(t))^{2}}{(1+t)^{2}}{\mathrm{d}}x-\frac{\mu}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\partial_{t}v(t))^{2}{\mathrm{d}}x.

Thus, for all
t\geq s_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle E_{2}(v(t),\partial_{t}v(t),t)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\partial_{x}v(t))^{2}{\mathrm{d}}x+\frac{1-\mu}{2}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\partial_{t}v(t))^{2}{\mathrm{d}}x

\displaystyle+\frac{\mu-2\mu^{2}}{8}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{(v(t))^{2}}{(1+t)^{2}}{\mathrm{d}}x+\frac{\mu}{8}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{V(x)(v(t))^{2}}{1+t}{\mathrm{d}}x+\frac{\mu}{8}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{V(x)(v(t))^{2}}{1+t}{\mathrm{d}}x.

Plugging the inequality
V(x)\geq{\mu}/{(1+t)}
valid for all
t\geq t_{0}
into the above estimate, we obtain

E_{2}(v(t),\partial_{t}v(t),t)\geq\frac{1}{2}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\partial_{x}v(t))^{2}{\mathrm{d}}x+\frac{1-\mu}{2}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\partial_{t}v(t))^{2}{\mathrm{d}}x+\frac{\mu(1-\mu)}{8}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{(v(t))^{2}}{(1+t)^{2}}{\mathrm{d}}x+\frac{\mu}{8}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{V(x)(v(t))^{2}}{1+t}{\mathrm{d}}x,

for all
t\geq\tau_{0}
. Consequently, we have

E_{2}(v(t),\partial_{t}v(t),t)\geq C\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\frac{(v(t))^{2}}{(t+1)^{2}}+\frac{V(x)(v(t))^{2}}{t+1}+(\partial_{t}v(t))^{2}+(\partial_{x}v(t))^{2}\right){\mathrm{d}}x,\,\,\hbox{for all}\,t\geq\tau_{0}.

(2.17)

On the other hand, using Young’s inequality, we conclude that a constant
C=C(\mu)
exists such that

E_{2}(v(t),\partial_{t}v(t),t)\leq C\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\frac{(v(t))^{2}}{(t+1)^{2}}+\frac{V(x)(v(t))^{2}}{t+1}+(\partial_{t}v(t))^{2}+(\partial_{x}v(t))^{2}\right){\mathrm{d}}x,\,\,\hbox{for all}\,t\geq s_{0}.

(2.18)

Combining (
2.17
) and (
2.18
), it follows that there exists
C=C(\mu)
such that

C^{-1}E_{2}(v(t),\partial_{t}v(t),t)\leq\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\frac{(v(t))^{2}}{(t+1)^{2}}+\frac{V(x)(v(t))^{2}}{t+1}+(\partial_{t}v(t))^{2}+(\partial_{x}v(t))^{2}\right){\mathrm{d}}x\leq CE_{2}(v(t),\partial_{t}v(t),t),

(2.19)

for all
t\geq\tau_{0}
. In conclusion, by using (
2.16
), and (
2.19
), we conclude that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\frac{(v(t))^{2}}{(t+1)^{2}}+\frac{V(x)(v(t))^{2}}{t+1}+(\partial_{t}v(t))^{2}+(\partial_{x}v(t))^{2}\right){\mathrm{d}}x

\displaystyle\leq C^{2}\left(\frac{1+s_{0}}{1+t}\right)^{\mu}\left(\frac{\|g(s_{0})\|^{2}_{L^{2}}}{(1+s_{0})^{2}}+\frac{\|\sqrt{V}g(s_{0})\|^{2}_{L^{2}}}{1+s_{0}}+\|h(s_{0})\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{x}g(s_{0})\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\right),

(2.20)

for all
t\geq s_{0}
when
s_{0}\geq t_{0}
, while, if
s_{0}\leq t_{0}
, then

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\frac{(v(t))^{2}}{(t+1)^{2}}+\frac{V(x)(v(t))^{2}}{t+1}+(\partial_{t}v(t))^{2}+(\partial_{x}v(t))^{2}\right){\mathrm{d}}x

\displaystyle\leq C^{2}\left(\frac{1+\tau_{0}}{1+t}\right)^{\mu}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\frac{(v(\tau_{0}))^{2}}{(\tau_{0}+1)^{2}}+\frac{V(x)(v(\tau_{0}))^{2}}{\tau_{0}+1}+(\partial_{t}v(\tau_{0}))^{2}+(\partial_{x}v(\tau_{0}))^{2}\right){\mathrm{d}}x

\displaystyle\leq C^{2}\tilde{C_{1}}\left(\frac{1+s_{0}}{1+t}\right)^{\mu}\left(\frac{\|g(s_{0})\|^{2}_{L^{2}}}{(1+s_{0})^{2}}+\frac{\|\sqrt{V}g(s_{0})\|^{2}_{L^{2}}}{1+s_{0}}+\|h(s_{0})\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{x}g(s_{0})\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\right),

(2.21)

for all
t\geq t_{0}
, where we have used Lemma
2.2
with
\tilde{C_{1}}=C_{0}(1+t_{0})^{\mu}=C_{0}\left(1+\frac{\mu R_{0}}{\mu_{0}-\mu}\right)^{\mu}
. Furthermore, when
s_{0}\leq t_{0}
, by exploiting Lemma
2.2
, we deduce that for all
t\in[s_{0},t_{0}]
,

\displaystyle\frac{\|v(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}}{(1+t)^{2}}+\frac{\|\sqrt{V}v(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}}{1+t}+\|\partial_{t}v(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{x}v(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C_{1}}\left(\frac{1+s_{0}}{1+t}\right)^{\mu}\left(\frac{\|g(s_{0})\|^{2}_{L^{2}}}{(1+s_{0})^{2}}+\frac{\|\sqrt{V}g(s_{0})\|^{2}_{L^{2}}}{1+s_{0}}+\|h(s_{0})\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{x}g(s_{0})\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\right).

(2.22)

Combining (
2.20
), (
2.21
), and (
2.22
), the proof of Lemma
2.3
is complete.
∎

Second case:
\mu_{0}\in(1,\infty)
.

We introduce the following functionals for all

:

\displaystyle E_{3}(v(t),\partial_{t}v(t))

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big(v(t)\partial_{t}v(t)+\frac{V(x)}{2}(v(t))^{2}\big){\mathrm{d}}x,

\displaystyle E_{4}(v(t),\partial_{t}v(t),t)

\displaystyle E_{0}(v(t),\partial_{t}v(t))+\frac{1}{2(t+1)}E_{3}(v(t),\partial_{t}v(t)),

).

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\mu_{0}>1
. Assume

(g(s_{0}),h(s_{0}))\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R})\times H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
that are compactly supported

(-R_{0}-s_{0},R_{0}+s_{0})

) satisfies,

,

\displaystyle\frac{\|v(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}}{(1+t)^{2}}+\frac{\|\sqrt{V}v(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}}{1+t}+\|\partial_{t}v(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{x}v(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\frac{1+s_{0}}{1+t}\right)\left(\frac{\|g(s_{0})\|^{2}_{L^{2}}}{(1+s_{0})^{2}}+\frac{\|\sqrt{V}g(s_{0})\|^{2}_{L^{2}}}{1+s_{0}}+\|h(s_{0})\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{x}g(s_{0})\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\right).

(2.23)

Proof.

By multiplying the differential equation in (
2.1
) by
v(t)
, and applying integration by parts, we obtain the following for all

,

\frac{d}{dt}E_{3}(v(t),\partial_{t}v(t))=\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\partial_{t}v(t))^{2}{\mathrm{d}}x-\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\partial_{x}v(t))^{2}{\mathrm{d}}x.

(2.24)

Hence, by exploiting (
2.5
), (
2.24

E_{4}(v(t),\partial_{t}v(t),t)
, we infer that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}E_{4}(v(t),\partial_{t}v(t),t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}V(x)(\partial_{t}v(t))^{2}{\mathrm{d}}x+\frac{1}{2(1+t)}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\partial_{t}v(t))^{2}{\mathrm{d}}x-\frac{1}{2(1+t)}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\partial_{x}v(t))^{2}{\mathrm{d}}x

Consequently,

\frac{d}{dt}E_{4}(v(t),\partial_{t}v(t),t)+\frac{1}{1+t}E_{4}(v(t),\partial_{t}v(t),t)=-\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(V(x)-\frac{1}{1+t}\right)(\partial_{t}v(t))^{2}{\mathrm{d}}x.

Noting that

X(T)=\mathcal{C}([0,T],H^{2}(\mathbb{R}))\cap\mathcal{C}^{1}([0,T],H^{1}(\mathbb{R}))\cap\mathcal{C}^{2}([0,T],L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))
,
equipped with the norm

\|v\|_{X(T)}=\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}(1+t)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\left\{(1+t)^{-1}\|v(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{x}v(t)\|_{H^{1}}+\|\partial_{t}v(t)\|_{H^{1}}+\|\partial^{2}_{t}v(t)\|_{L^{2}}\right\},

where
\alpha
is defined in (
2.33
). We are going to use the Banach fixed-point theorem. Let us define the following complete metric space
B_{M}(T)=\{v\in X(T);\,\,\|v\|_{X(T)}\leq 2M\},
endowed with the distance induced by the norm
\|\cdotp\|_{X(T)}
, where
M>0
is a positive constant to be chosen later. By Proposition
2.5
and estimates (
3.13
), (
3.19
), and (
3.20
) below, define the mapping
\Phi:B_{M}(T)\rightarrow X(T)
by

\Phi(u)(t)=R(t)(u_{0},u_{1})+\int_{0}^{t}S(t,s)f(\partial_{t}u)\,ds=:\Phi(u)^{lin}(t)+\Phi(u)^{nl}(t),\quad\hbox{for $u\in B_{M}(T)$}.

Our goal is to prove that
\Phi
is a contraction mapping on
B_{M}(T)
.

Step 1.
\Phi:B_{M}(T)\longrightarrow B_{M}(T)
.
Let
u\in B_{M}(T)
.

Estimation of
(1+t)^{-1}\|\Phi(u)(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{t}\Phi(u)(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{x}\Phi(u)(t)\|_{L^{2}}
. Using (
2.34
) in the particular case
s_{0}=0
, we have

(1+t)^{-1}\|\Phi(u)^{lin}(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{x}\Phi(u)^{lin}(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{t}\Phi(u)^{lin}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\,\varepsilon_{0}({1+t})^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}.

(3.11)

Moreover, by applying inequality (
2.34
) once again with
s_{0}=s
, and taking the initial data as
(0,f(\partial_{t}u(s)))
, we obtain

(1+t)^{-1}\|\Phi(u)^{nl}(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{t}\Phi(u)^{nl}(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{x}\Phi(u)^{nl}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\,\int_{0}^{t}\left(\frac{1+s}{1+t}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\|f(\partial_{t}u(s))\|_{L^{2}}\,ds.

By the Sobolev embedding
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, and the fact that
u\in B_{M}(T)
, we infer

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}u(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leqslant C\|\partial_{t}u(s)\|_{H^{1}}\leqslant C(1+s)^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\|u\|_{X(T)}\leq CM(1+s)^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}.

(3.12)

Therefore, by (
3.12
) and exploiting again the fact that
u\in B_{M}(T)
, we conclude

\|f(\partial_{t}u(s))\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|\partial_{t}u(s)\|_{\infty}^{p-1}\|\partial_{t}u(s)\|_{L^{2}}\leq CM^{p}(1+s)^{-\frac{\alpha p}{2}}.

(3.13)

Then, we obtain

(1+t)^{-1}\|\Phi(u)^{nl}(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{t}\Phi(u)^{nl}(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{x}\Phi(u)^{nl}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\,M^{p}(1+t)^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\int_{0}^{t}(1+s)^{-\frac{(p-1)\alpha}{2}}\,ds.

Since
p>1+\frac{2}{\alpha_{0}}
, it follows that
\frac{2}{p-1}<\alpha_{0}
. Hence, if
\mu\in(\frac{2}{p-1},\mu_{0})
, in the case
\mu_{0}\in(0,1]
, we easily deduce that

\frac{(p-1)\alpha}{2}>1.

(3.14)

Therefore, the integral is uniformly bounded, and we get

(1+t)^{-1}\|\Phi(u)^{nl}(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{t}\Phi(u)^{nl}(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{x}\Phi(u)^{nl}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\,M^{p}(1+t)^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}.

(3.15)

Combining (
3.11
) and (
3.15
), we conclude

(1+t)^{-1}\|\Phi(u)(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{t}\Phi(u)(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{x}\Phi(u)(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\,\varepsilon_{0}({1+t})^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}+C\,M^{p}(1+t)^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}.

(3.16)

Estimation of
\|\partial^{2}_{xt}\Phi(u)(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{t}^{2}\Phi(u)(t)\|_{L^{2}}{+\|\partial_{x}^{2}\Phi(u)(t)\|_{L^{2}}}
.
By (
2.35
) with
s_{0}=0
, we have

{\|\partial_{x}^{2}\Phi(u)^{lin}(t)\|_{L^{2}}+}\|\partial^{2}_{xt}\Phi(u)^{lin}(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{t}^{2}\Phi(u)^{lin}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\,\varepsilon_{0}({1+t})^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}.

(3.17)

Moreover, applying estimate (
2.35
) again with
s_{0}=s
, and considering the initial data
(0,f(\partial_{t}u(s)))
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}^{2}\Phi(u)^{nl}(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial^{2}_{xt}\Phi(u)^{nl}(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{t}^{2}\Phi(u)^{nl}(t)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\,\int_{0}^{t}\left(\frac{1+s}{1+t}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\left(\|f(\partial_{t}u(s))\|_{H^{1}}+\|\partial_{t}f(\partial_{t}u(s))\|_{L^{2}}\right)\,ds.

(3.18)

Based on (
3.12
) and exploiting again the fact that
u\in B_{M}(T)
, we have

\|\partial_{x}f(\partial_{t}u(s))\|_{L^{2}}=\|p|\partial_{t}u|^{p-1}\partial^{2}_{xt}u\|_{L^{2}}\leq p\|\partial_{t}u\|_{\infty}^{p-1}\|\partial^{2}_{xt}u\|_{L^{2}}\leq CM^{p}(1+s)^{-\frac{p\alpha}{2}},

(3.19)

and

\|\partial_{t}f(\partial_{t}u(s))\|_{L^{2}}=\|p|\partial_{t}u|^{p-1}\partial^{2}_{t}u\|_{L^{2}}\leq p\|\partial_{t}u\|_{\infty}^{p-1}\|\partial^{2}_{t}u\|_{L^{2}}\leq CM^{p}(1+s)^{-\frac{p\alpha}{2}}.

(3.20)

Hence, combining (
3.13
), (
3.19
), (
3.20
) and the estimate (
3.18
), we infer

{\|\partial_{x}^{2}\Phi(u)^{nl}(t)\|_{L^{2}}+}\|\partial^{2}_{xt}\Phi(u)^{nl}(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{t}^{2}\Phi(u)^{nl}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\,M^{p}(1+t)^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\int_{0}^{t}(1+s)^{-\frac{(p-1)\alpha}{2}}\,ds.

(3.21)

It follows from condition (
3.14
) that

\|\partial_{x}^{2}\Phi(u)^{nl}(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial^{2}_{xt}\Phi(u)^{nl}(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{t}^{2}\Phi(u)^{nl}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\,M^{p}(1+t)^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}.

(3.22)

Therefore, by (
3.17
) and (
3.22
), we get

\|\partial_{x}^{2}\Phi(u)(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial^{2}_{xt}\Phi(u)(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{t}^{2}\Phi(u)(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\,\varepsilon_{0}(1+t)^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}+C\,M^{p}(1+t)^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}.

(3.23)

Summing up the estimates (
3.16
) and (
3.23
), we conclude that
\|\Phi(u)\|_{X(T)}\leq C\,\varepsilon_{0}+C\,M^{p}
. By choosing
M>0
such that
C\,M^{p-1}\leq\frac{1}{2}
, and then taking
0<\varepsilon_{0}\ll 1
small enough to ensure
C\,\varepsilon_{0}\leq\frac{1}{2}M
, we arrive at
\|\Phi(u)\|_{X(T)}\leq M
, that is,
\Phi(u)\in B_{M}(T)
.

Step 2.
\Phi
is a contraction.
Let
u,v\in B_{M}(T)
.

Estimation of
(1+t)^{-1}\|(\Phi(u)-\Phi(v))(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{t}(\Phi(u)-\Phi(v))(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{x}(\Phi(u)-\Phi(v))(t)\|_{L^{2}}
.
Applying (
2.34
) with
s_{0}=s
, and initial data as
(0,f(\partial_{t}u(s))-f(\partial_{t}v(s)))
, we derive

(1+t)^{-1}\|\Phi(u)(t)-\Phi(v)(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{t}\Phi(u)(t)-\partial_{t}\Phi(v)(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{x}\Phi(u)(t)-\partial_{x}\Phi(v)(t)\|_{L^{2}}\\
\leq C\,\int_{0}^{t}\left(\frac{1+s}{1+t}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\|f(\partial_{t}u(s))-f(\partial_{t}v(s))\|_{L^{2}}\,ds.

(3.24)

By the basic inequalities (
3.1
)-(
3.2
), we have

\|f(\partial_{t}u(s))-f(\partial_{t}v(s))\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|\partial_{t}u(s)-\partial_{t}v(s)\|_{L^{2}}\left(\|\partial_{t}u(s)\|^{p-1}_{\infty}+\|\partial_{t}v(s)\|^{p-1}_{\infty}\right).

Note that

\|\partial_{t}u(s)-\partial_{t}v(s)\|_{L^{2}}\leq(1+s)^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\|u-v\|_{X(T)}.

Using the Sobolev embedding
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, it follows that

\|\partial_{t}u(s)\|_{\infty}^{p-1}\leq C\|\partial_{t}u(s)\|_{H^{1}}^{p-1}\leq C(1+s)^{-\frac{\alpha(p-1)}{2}}\|u\|_{X(T)}^{p-1}\leq CM^{p-1}(1+s)^{-\frac{\alpha(p-1)}{2}},

and similarly for
\partial_{t}v(s)
. Accordingly,

\|f(\partial_{t}u(s))-f(\partial_{t}v(s))\|_{L^{2}}\leq CM^{p-1}(1+s)^{-\frac{p\alpha}{2}}\|u-v\|_{X(T)}.

(3.25)

Hence,

(1+t)^{-1}\|\Phi(u)(t)-\Phi(v)(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{t}\Phi(u)(t)-\partial_{t}\Phi(v)(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{x}\Phi(u)(t)-\partial_{x}\Phi(v)(t)\|_{L^{2}}\\
\leq C\,M^{p-1}\|u-v\|_{X(T)}(1+t)^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\int_{0}^{t}(1+s)^{-\frac{(p-1)\alpha}{2}}\,ds.

Referring to (
3.14
), we deduce

(1+t)^{-1}\|\Phi(u)(t)-\Phi(v)(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{t}\Phi(u)(t)-\partial_{t}\Phi(v)(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{x}\Phi(u)(t)-\partial_{x}\Phi(v)(t)\|_{L^{2}}\\
\leq C\,M^{p-1}\|u-v\|_{X(T)}(1+t)^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}.

Estimation of
\|\partial_{t}^{2}\left(\Phi(u)-\Phi(v)\right)(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial^{2}_{xt}\left(\Phi(u)-\Phi(v)\right)(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{x}^{2}\left(\Phi(u)-\Phi(v)\right)(t)\|_{L^{2}}
.
Applying (
2.35
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}^{2}\left(\Phi(u)-\Phi(v)\right)(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial^{2}_{xt}\left(\Phi(u)-\Phi(v)\right)(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{x}^{2}\left(\Phi(u)-\Phi(v)\right)(t)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\,C\,\int_{0}^{t}\left(\frac{1+s}{1+t}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\left(\|f(\partial_{t}u)-f(\partial_{t}v)\|_{H^{1}}+\|\partial_{t}\left(f(\partial_{t}u)-f(\partial_{t}v)\right)\|_{L^{2}}\right)\,ds.

(3.26)

Since
p>1+\frac{2}{\alpha_{0}}\geq 3
, it follows that (
3.5
)-(
3.6
) can be applied. Therefore

\displaystyle\left\|\partial_{x}\left(f(\partial_{t}u(s))-f(\partial_{t}v(s))\right)\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\|\partial^{2}_{xt}w(s)\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{t}u(s)\|^{p-1}_{L^{\infty}}+C\|\partial_{t}w(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}\left(\|\partial_{t}u(s)\|^{p-2}_{L^{\infty}}+\|\partial_{t}v(s)\|^{p-2}_{L^{\infty}}\right)\|\partial^{2}_{xt}v(s)\|_{L^{2}},

where
w(t,x)\doteq u(t,x)-v(t,x)
. Using the Sobolev embedding again,
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
,

\|\partial_{t}u(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leqslant CM(1+s)^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}},\quad\|\partial_{t}v(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leqslant CM(1+s)^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}},\quad\|\partial_{t}w(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leqslant C(1+s)^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\|u-v\|_{X(T)},

and

\|\partial^{2}_{xt}v(s)\|_{L^{2}}\leqslant CM(1+s)^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}},\qquad\|\partial^{2}_{xt}w(s)\|_{L^{2}}\leqslant(1+s)^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\|u-v\|_{X(T)}.

Whence,

\left\|\partial_{x}\left(f(\partial_{t}u(s))-f(\partial_{t}v(s))\right)\right\|_{L^{2}}\leqslant CM^{p-1}(1+s)^{-\frac{p\alpha}{2}}\|u-v\|_{X(T)}.

(3.27)

Similarly, by (
3.7
)-(
3.8
),

\left\|\partial_{t}\left(f(\partial_{t}u(s))-f(\partial_{t}v(s))\right)\right\|_{L^{2}}\leqslant CM^{p-1}(1+s)^{-\frac{p\alpha}{2}}\|u-v\|_{X(T)}.

(3.28)

Inserting (
3.25
), (
3.27
), and (
3.28
) into (
3.26
) and taking into account (
3.14
), we derive

\|\partial_{t}^{2}\left(\Phi(u)-\Phi(v)\right)(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial^{2}_{xt}\left(\Phi(u)-\Phi(v)\right)(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{x}^{2}\left(\Phi(u)-\Phi(v)\right)(t)\|_{L^{2}}\\
\leq CM^{p-1}\|u-v\|_{X(T)}(1+t)^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}.

Summing all estimates, it follows that
\|\Phi(u)-\Phi(v)\|_{X(T)}\leq C\,M^{p-1}\|u-v\|_{X(T)}
. Choosing
M>0
so that
C\,M^{p-1}\leq 1/2
, we arrive at

\|\Phi(u)-\Phi(v)\|_{X(T)}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|u-v\|_{X(T)}.

By the Banach fixed-point theorem, the proof is complete.

\square

Proof of Theorem
1.3

Since the proof of Theorem
1.3
follows a similar approach to that of Theorem
1.1
, we outline the key steps while highlighting the necessary modifications.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
-(i).
To show that
\Phi:B_{M}(T)\longrightarrow B_{M}(T)
, we proceed in a similar manner as in the proof of Theorem
1.1
, utilizing the estimates

\|f(\partial_{t}u(s),\partial_{x}u(s))\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|\partial_{x}u(s)\|_{\infty}^{q-1}\|\partial_{x}u(s)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\,\|\partial_{x}u(s)\|_{H^{1}}^{q}\leq CM^{p}(1+s)^{-\frac{q\alpha}{2}},

\|\partial_{x}f(\partial_{t}u(s),\partial_{x}u(s))\|_{L^{2}}=\|\partial_{x}|\partial_{x}u(s)|^{q}\|_{L^{2}}\leq q\|\partial_{x}u\|_{\infty}^{q-1}\|\partial_{x}^{2}u\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\,\|\partial_{x}u\|_{H^{1}}^{q}\leq CM^{p}(1+s)^{-\frac{q\alpha}{2}},

and

\|\partial_{t}f(\partial_{t}u(s),\partial_{x}u(s))\|_{L^{2}}=\|\partial_{t}|\partial_{x}u(s)|^{q}\|_{L^{2}}\leq q\|\partial_{x}u\|_{\infty}^{q-1}\|\partial^{2}_{xt}u\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\,\|\partial_{x}u\|_{H^{1}}^{q-1}\|\partial^{2}_{xt}u\|_{L^{2}}\leq CM^{p}(1+s)^{-\frac{q\alpha}{2}}.

For the contraction mapping argument, we require the estimates:

\displaystyle\|f(\partial_{t}u(s),\partial_{x}u(s))-f(\partial_{t}v(s),\partial_{x}v(s))\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\partial_{x}u(s)-\partial_{x}v(s)\|_{L^{2}}\left(\|\partial_{x}u(s)\|^{q-1}_{\infty}+\|\partial_{x}v(s)\|^{q-1}_{\infty}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\,(1+s)^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\|u-v\|_{X(T)}\left(\|\partial_{x}u(s)\|^{q-1}_{H^{1}}+\|\partial_{x}v(s)\|^{q-1}_{H^{1}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\,M^{q-1}(1+s)^{-\frac{q\alpha}{2}}\|u-v\|_{X(T)},

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}\left(f(\partial_{t}u(s),\partial_{x}u(s))-f(\partial_{t}v(s),\partial_{x}v(s))\right)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\partial_{x}^{2}w(s)\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{x}u(s)\|^{q-1}_{L^{\infty}}+C\|\partial_{x}w(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}\left(\|\partial_{x}u(s)\|^{q-2}_{L^{\infty}}+\|\partial_{x}v(s)\|^{q-2}_{L^{\infty}}\right)\|\partial_{x}^{2}v(s)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\,M^{q-1}(1+s)^{-\frac{q\alpha}{2}}\|u-v\|_{X(T)},

and

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}\left(f(\partial_{t}u(s),\partial_{x}u(s))-f(\partial_{t}v(s),\partial_{x}v(s))\right)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\partial^{2}_{xt}w(s)\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{x}u(s)\|^{q-1}_{L^{\infty}}+C\|\partial_{t}w(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}\left(\|\partial_{x}u(s)\|^{q-2}_{L^{\infty}}+\|\partial_{x}v(s)\|^{q-2}_{L^{\infty}}\right)\|\partial^{2}_{xt}v(s)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\,M^{q-1}(1+s)^{-\frac{q\alpha}{2}}\|u-v\|_{X(T)},

where
w(t,x)\doteq u(t,x)-v(t,x)
. These estimates ensure the existence and uniqueness of solutions via the Banach fixed-point theorem.

\square

Proof of Theorem
1.3
-(ii).
We adopt a similar approach as in the proof of Theorem
1.1
, emplying the reasoning established in the proof of Theorem
1.3
-(i) along with the estimate

\displaystyle\|f(\partial_{t}u(s),\partial_{x}u(s))\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}u(s)\|^{p}_{L^{\infty}}\||\partial_{x}u(s)|^{q}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\,\|\partial_{t}u(s)\|^{p}_{H^{1}}\||\partial_{x}u(s)|^{q}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\,M^{p}(1+s)^{-\frac{p\alpha}{2}}M^{q}(1+s)^{-\frac{q\alpha}{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\,M^{p+q}(1+s)^{-\frac{\alpha(p+q)}{2}}.

Therefore,

Since

(1+t)^{-1}\|\Phi(u)^{nl}(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{t}\Phi(u)^{nl}(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{x}\Phi(u)^{nl}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\,M^{p+q}(1+t)^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}.

5
Proof of Theorem
1.2

This section is dedicated to the proof of Theorem
1.2
, which aims to determine the blow-up region and establish lifespan estimates for problem (
1.14
) in the one-dimensional space.

We first introduce a positive test function which is a particular positive solution

\psi(x,t)
with separated variables and satisfies the conjugate equation corresponding to the linear problem, namely
\psi(x,t)
satisfies

\partial^{2}_{t}\psi(x,t)-\partial_{x}^{2}\psi(x,t)-V(x)\partial_{t}\psi(x,t)=0.

(5.1)

More precisely, we choose the function
\psi
given by

\psi(x,t):=\rho(t)\phi(x);\quad\rho(t):=e^{-t},

(5.2)

where
\phi(x)=\phi(-x)
is a solution of the problem

\phi^{\prime\prime}(x)=\left(1+V(x)\right)\phi(x),\qquad\hbox{for all}\,\,x\in\mathbb{R},

(5.3)

with initial data
\phi(0)=1
,
\phi^{\prime}(0)=0
. Indeed,
\phi
can be obtained as a solution of the integral equation

\phi(r)=1+\int_{0}^{r}(r-s)(1+V(s))\phi(s)\,ds,\qquad r>0.

Note that the existence of a positive solution for the associated problem of (
5.3
) is studied in
[
24
,
29
]
in higher dimension.
However, the specific case of one spatial dimension has not been addressed in these works.
However, following the strategy used in
[
24
]
in the case of higher dimension,
we easily prove the following properties of
\phi
.

Lemma 5.1
.

There exists an even function
\phi
solution of (
5.3
) verifying
\phi(0)=1
,
\phi^{\prime}(0)=0
,
which satisfies

0<\phi(x)\leq C(1+|x|)^{\frac{\mu_{0}}{2}}e^{|x|},\qquad\hbox{for all}\,\,\,x\in\mathbb{R}.

(5.4)

Proof.

Clearly, we have
\phi(x)>0
, and
x\phi^{\prime}(x)\geq 0
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
. Let
\phi(x)=e^{x+q(x)}
.
By substituting
\phi
into (
5.3
), one can easily see that the function
q
satisfies

q^{\prime\prime}(x)+2q^{\prime}(x)+\big(q^{\prime}(x)\big)^{2}=V(x),\qquad\hbox{for all}\,\,\,x\in\mathbb{R},

(5.5)

where
q(0)=0
and
q^{\prime}(0)=-1
.
Multiplying (
5.5
) by
e^{2x}
, we deduce after integrating over
[0,x]
that

e^{2x}q^{\prime}(x)\leq-1+\int_{0}^{x}e^{2y}V(y)dy,\qquad\hbox{for all}\,\,\,x>0.

(5.6)

By using the expression of
V
, and integrating by parts we infer

\int_{0}^{x}e^{2y}V(y)dy=\frac{\mu_{0}}{2\sqrt{1+x^{2}}}e^{2x}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{x}e^{2y}V^{\prime}(y)dy,\qquad\hbox{for all}\,\,\,x>0,

(5.7)

which is asymptotically

\frac{\mu_{0}}{2}x^{-1}e^{2x}+\mathcal{O}(x^{-2})e^{2x},

since
V(x)\sim\mu_{0}x^{-1}
and
V^{\prime}(x)\sim-\mu_{0}x^{-2}
for large
x
. Hence, by (
5.6
) and (
5.7
), we write for
x
large enough,

q^{\prime}(x)\leq\frac{\mu_{0}}{2x}+\mathcal{O}(x^{-2}).

(5.8)

Therefore,
for
x
large enough,

q(x)\leq\frac{\mu_{0}}{2}\ln(x)+\mathcal{O}(1).

(5.9)

Inequality (
5.4
) is a direct consequence of (
5.9
) owing to the positivity and the symmetry of
\phi
.
∎

Furthermore, by employing the estimate (
5.4
) along with the expression of
\psi
given by (
5.2
), and proceeding along the same lines as in the derivation of inequality (2.5) in
[
46
]
or
[
10
, Lemma 3.1]
, we obtain the following result.

Lemma 5.2
.

There exists a constant
C=C(R_{0},\mu_{0})>0
such that

\int_{|x|\leq R_{0}+t}\psi(x,t)dx\leq C(1+t)^{\frac{\mu_{0}}{2}},\quad\hbox{for all}\,\,t\geq 0.

(5.10)

The estimate (
5.10
) plays a crucial role in establishing the blow-up result stated in Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.
The proof relies on Lemma
5.2
and follows a similar strategy to the one used in
[
10
, Theorem 2.3]
. Specifically, we employ the test function method, originally introduced by Baras and Pierre
[
2
]
, later refined by Zhang
[
47
]
, and further developed in the works of Mitidieri and Pohozaev
[
33
]
, which has since been widely applied in various contexts, including the works of Kirane et al.
[
28
]
and Fino et al.
[
5
,
6
,
7
,
9
]
.

\square

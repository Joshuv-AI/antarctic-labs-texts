Quasi racks, quasi bijective and quasi non-degenerate set-theoretic solutions of the Yang–Baxter equation
Marzia MAZZOTTA, Paola STEFANELLI, Magdalena WIERTEL

Abstract
This work initiates a systematic study of the class of quasi bijective and quasi non-degenerate solutions to the set-theoretic Yang–Baxter equation. The motivation stems from the observation that solutions that arise from dual weak braces belong to these classes. The notions of quasi rack and derived solution are introduced and examined, extending the classical definitions. Additionally, a family of quasi left non-degenerate solutions is described in terms of quasi racks and g-twists , analogous to the left non-degenerate case. Furthermore, we completely characterize a class of quasi racks that are Płonka sum of racks.

1.1
Basics on weak braces

To deal with the algebraic structures of weak brace, we introduce the necessary background on semigroup theory. For details, we refer the reader to some classical books such as
[
7
,
21
,
26

A
regular semigroup
is a semigroup
S
in which every element is regular, i.e., for any
a\in S
there exists an element
x
in
S
, called
an inverse
of
a
, such that
axa=a
. In particular,
ax
is an idempotent of
S
.
A semigroup
S
is said to be
completely regular semigroup
if for every
a\in S
there exists
x\in S
such that
axa=a
,
xax=x
, and
ax=xa
. In his early work, Clifford
[
8
]
called completely regular semigroups
semigroups admitting relative inverses
. A regular semigroup
S
is called an
inverse semigroup
, if every element
a\in S
admits a unique inverse. Roughly speaking, such inverses behave similarly to inverses in a group. For example, in any inverse semigroup

we have that
(xy)^{-}=y^{-}x^{-}
and
(x^{-})^{-}=x
, for all
x,y\in S

An inverse semigroup

, for all

Let us now recall the definition contained in
[
5

Definition 1.1
.

Let

and
\left(S,\circ\right)

\displaystyle x\circ\left(y+z\right)=x\circ y-x+x\circ z\qquad\text{and}\qquad x\circ x^{-}=-x+x,

for all

, where

and

denote the inverses of

and
\circ
, respectively.

Semigroups
(S,+)
and
(S,\circ)
are called the additive and multiplicative semigroup of a weak brace
(S,+,\circ)
, respectively. Clearly, the sets of the idempotents
\E(S,+)

\displaystyle\forall\,x\in S,\ e\in\E(S)\qquad e\circ x=e+x.

Obviously, if

is a skew brace
[
18
]

Furthermore, in

For arbitrary set

2
Quasi racks and quasi bijective non-degenerate solutions

In this section, we introduce the notions of quasi racks and quasi non-degenerate solutions, with the aim to generalize the notions of the previous section. In particular, we identify three families of quasi racks that produce a quasi non-degenerate derived solution.

Let us introduce the following definition.

Definition 2.1
.

A solution
(X,r)
is said to be
quasi bijective
if there exists a (unique) solution
\left(X,r^{-}\right)
such that

\displaystyle rr^{-}r=r,\quad r^{-}rr^{-}=r^{-}\quad\text{and}\quad r^{-}r=rr^{-}.

Remark 2.2
.

If
(X,r)
is a solution which admits a relative inverse
r^{-}
, then
(X,r^{-})
is also a solution. Indeed, it is straightforward to check that
(r\times\id_{X})^{-}=r^{-}\times\id_{X}
and
(\id_{X}\times r)^{-}=\id_{X}\times r^{-}
. Moreover, observe that
(ab)^{-}=b^{-}a^{-}
, for arbitrary
a,b
with relative inverses
a^{-}
and
b^{-}
, respectively. Taking the inverses of both sides of the (
YBE
), we thus get
(r^{-}\times\id_{X})(\id_{X}\times r^{-})(r^{-}\times\id_{X})=(\id_{X}\times r^{-})(r^{-}\times\id_{X})(\id_{X}\times r^{-})
and the assertion follows.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
(X,r)
be a solution. Then
r
is said to be:

1)

quasi left non-degenerate
if
there exists a (unique) map
\lambda_{x}^{-}:X\to X
such that, for all
x,y\in X
,

\displaystyle\lambda_{x}\lambda_{x}^{-}\lambda_{x}=\lambda_{x},\quad\lambda_{x}^{-}\lambda_{x}\lambda_{x}^{-}=\lambda_{x}^{-},\quad\lambda_{x}^{0}:=\lambda_{x}\lambda_{x}^{-}=\lambda_{x}^{-}\lambda_{x},\quad\text{and}\quad\lambda_{x}^{0}\lambda_{y}=\lambda_{y}\lambda_{x}^{0};

2)

quasi right non-degenerate
if
there exists a (unique) map
\rho_{x}^{-}:X\to X
such that, for all
x,y\in X
,

\displaystyle\rho_{x}\rho_{x}^{-}\rho_{x}=\rho_{x},\quad\rho_{x}^{-}\rho_{x}\rho_{x}^{-}=\rho_{x}^{-},\quad\rho_{x}^{0}:=\rho_{x}\rho_{x}^{-}=\rho_{x}^{-}\rho_{x},\quad\text{and}\quad\rho_{x}^{0}\rho_{y}=\rho_{y}\rho_{x}^{0};

3)

quasi non-degenerate
if
r
is both quasi right and left non-degenerate.

Remark 2.4
.

Observe that the condition
\lambda_{x}^{0}\lambda_{y}=\lambda_{y}\lambda_{x}^{0}
for arbitrary

does not follow from the existence of the inverse in the sense of the
2.3
. Indeed, consider a set
X
with

and let
(X,r)
be the solution given by

, for all

\lambda_{x}
have the inverses in the sense of our definition, but, if

it is not true that
\lambda_{x}^{0}\lambda_{y}\neq\lambda_{y}\lambda_{x}^{0}

We will refer to maps

,
\lambda_{x^{-}}
, and
\rho_{x^{-}}

of maps

\lambda_{x}
, and

, respectively, for all

Lemma 2.5

For arbitrary semigroup

,
xax=x

Let

and

Then

S^{1}a=S^{1}ax=S^{1}x

\mathcal{H}_{a}=\mathcal{H}_{x}=\mathcal{H}_{ax}
, where
\mathcal{H}_{y}

Shelves associated to quasi left-non degenerate solutions

In this section, we identify a family of quasi left non-degenerate solutions that give rise to a shelf structure. In this way, the well-known result on left non-degenerate solutions (namely, the converse part of
1.7
) can be situated within a broader framework.

In the series of technical propositions, we introduce three conditions on quasi left non-degenerate solutions, denoted by (
A
), (
B
) and (
C
), that are sufficient to obtain the postulated derived shelf structures.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
(X,r)
be a quasi left non-degenerate solution. Then the following identities

\displaystyle\forall\,x,y\in X\qquad\lambda^{0}_{\lambda_{x}(y)}=\lambda_{x}^{0}\lambda_{y}^{0}

(A)

and

\forall\,x,y\in X\qquad\quad\lambda_{\lambda_{x}(y)}=\lambda_{x}\lambda_{y}\lambda_{\rho_{y}(x)}^{-}

are equivalent.

Proof.

If
x,y\in X
and condition (
A
) holds, then

\displaystyle\lambda_{\lambda_{x}(y)}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{\lambda_{x}(y)}\lambda^{0}_{\lambda_{x}(y)}=\lambda_{\lambda_{x}(y)}(\lambda_{x}\lambda_{y})^{0}\underset{\eqref{first}}{=}\lambda_{\lambda_{x}(y)}\left(\lambda_{\lambda_{x}(y)}\lambda_{\rho_{y}(x)}\right)^{0}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{\lambda_{x}(y)}\lambda_{\rho_{y}(x)}^{0}=\lambda_{\lambda_{x}(y)}\lambda_{\rho_{y}(x)}\lambda^{-}_{\rho_{y}(x)}\underset{\eqref{first}}{=}\lambda_{x}\lambda_{y}\lambda_{\rho_{y}(x)}^{-}.

The converse follows easily from (
Y1
).
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
(X,r)
be a quasi left non-degenerate solution such that

\displaystyle\rho_{y}(x)=\lambda^{0}_{\lambda_{x}(y)}\rho_{\lambda^{0}_{x}(y)}(x),

(B)

for all
x,y\in X
. Then

1.

\rho_{y}(x)=\rho_{\lambda^{0}_{x}(y)}(x)
,

2.

\lambda_{x}\lambda_{y}=\lambda_{x}\lambda_{\lambda^{0}_{x}(y)}
,
\lambda^{0}_{x}\lambda_{y}=\lambda^{0}_{x}\lambda_{\lambda^{0}_{x}(y)}
, and
\lambda^{-}_{x}\lambda_{y}=\lambda^{-}_{x}\lambda_{\lambda^{0}_{x}(y)}
,

for all
x,y\in X
.

Proof.

If
x,y\in X
, we obtain

\displaystyle\rho_{y}(x)=\lambda^{0}_{\lambda_{x}(y)}\rho_{\lambda^{0}_{x}(y)}(x)=\lambda^{0}_{\lambda_{x}(\lambda^{0}_{x}(y))}\rho_{\lambda^{0}_{x}(\lambda^{0}_{x}(y))}(x)=\rho_{\lambda^{0}_{x}(y)}(x),

thus

\displaystyle\lambda_{x}\lambda_{y}\underset{\eqref{first}}{=}\lambda_{\lambda_{x}(y)}\lambda_{\rho_{y}(x)}=\lambda_{\lambda_{x}(\lambda^{0}_{x}(y))}\lambda_{\rho_{\lambda^{0}_{x}(y)}(x)}\underset{\eqref{first}}{=}\lambda_{x}\lambda_{\lambda^{0}_{x}(y)},

thus, the first identity in
2.
holds. Moreover, applying the map
\lambda^{-}_{x}
on the left in the last equality, we obtain the other ones.
∎

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
(X,r)
be a quasi left non-degenerate solution such that conditions (
A
) and (
B
) are satisfied.
Then the following hold:

1.

\lambda_{\lambda^{0}_{x}(y)}=\lambda^{0}_{x}\lambda_{y}
and
\lambda^{0}_{\lambda^{0}_{x}(y)}=\lambda^{0}_{x}\lambda^{0}_{y}
,

2.

\lambda_{\rho_{y}(x)}=\lambda^{-}_{\lambda_{x}(y)}\lambda_{x}\lambda_{y}
,

3.

\lambda^{-}_{\lambda_{x}(y)}\lambda_{x}=\lambda_{\rho_{y}(x)}\lambda_{y}^{-}
,

4.

\lambda_{\rho_{\lambda^{0}_{z}(y)}(x)}=\lambda_{\rho_{y}(x)}\lambda^{0}_{\lambda^{0}_{z}(y)}
,

5.

\lambda_{\rho_{\lambda^{0}_{z}(y)}(x)}\rho_{\lambda^{-}_{z}(y)}(z)=\lambda_{\rho_{y}(x)}\rho_{\lambda^{-}_{z}(y)}(z)
,

6.

\lambda^{0}_{\lambda^{-}_{x}(y)}\lambda^{-}_{x}=\lambda^{0}_{\lambda^{0}_{x}(y)}\lambda^{-}_{x}
,

for all
x,y,z\in X
.

Proof.

Let
x,y,z\in X
. Notice that

\displaystyle\lambda_{\lambda^{0}_{x}(y)}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{\lambda_{x}(\lambda^{-}_{x}(y))}\underset{\ref{propA}}{=}\lambda_{x}\lambda_{\lambda^{-}_{x}(y)}\lambda^{-}_{\rho_{\lambda^{-}_{x}(y)}(x)}=\lambda^{0}_{x}\lambda_{x}\lambda_{\lambda^{-}_{x}(y)}\lambda^{-}_{\rho_{\lambda^{-}_{x}(y)}(x)}

\displaystyle\underset{\ref{propA}}{=}\lambda^{0}_{x}\lambda_{\lambda^{0}_{x}(y)}\underset{\ref{propB}-2.}{=}\lambda^{0}_{x}\lambda_{y},

and hence

\displaystyle\lambda^{0}_{\lambda^{0}_{x}(y)}=\lambda^{-}_{\lambda^{0}_{x}(y)}\lambda_{\lambda^{0}_{x}(y)}=\lambda^{-}_{y}\lambda_{x}^{0}\lambda_{x}^{0}\lambda_{y}=\lambda_{x}^{0}\lambda_{y}^{0}.

Moreover,

\displaystyle\lambda_{\rho_{y}(x)}=\lambda_{\lambda^{0}_{\lambda_{x}(y)}\rho_{\lambda^{0}_{x}(y)}(x)}\underset{1.}{=}\lambda^{0}_{\lambda_{x}(y)}\lambda_{\rho_{\lambda^{0}_{x}(y)}(x)}\underset{\ref{propB}-1.}{=}\lambda^{0}_{\lambda_{x}(y)}\lambda_{\rho_{y}(x)}\underset{\eqref{first}}{=}\lambda^{-}_{\lambda_{x}(y)}\lambda_{x}\lambda_{y}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\lambda^{-}_{\lambda_{x}(y)}\lambda_{x}=\lambda^{-}_{\lambda_{x}(y)}\lambda^{0}_{\lambda_{x}(y)}\lambda_{x}=\lambda^{-}_{\lambda_{x}(y)}\lambda^{0}_{x}\lambda_{y}^{0}\lambda_{x}=\lambda^{-}_{\lambda_{x}(y)}\lambda_{x}\lambda_{y}\lambda_{y}^{-}=\lambda_{\rho_{y}(x)}\lambda_{y}^{-}.

Besides, we obtain that

\displaystyle\lambda_{\rho_{\lambda^{0}_{z}(y)}(x)}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{\lambda^{0}_{\lambda_{x}\lambda^{0}_{z}(y)}\rho_{\lambda^{0}_{x}\lambda^{0}_{z}(y)}(x)}\underset{\ref{propB}-1.}{=}\lambda_{\lambda^{0}_{\lambda_{x}\lambda^{0}_{z}(y)}\rho_{\lambda^{0}_{z}(y)}(x)}\underset{1.}{=}\lambda^{0}_{\lambda_{x}\lambda^{0}_{z}(y)}\lambda_{\rho_{\lambda^{0}_{z}(y)}(x)}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{-}_{\lambda_{x}\lambda^{0}_{z}(y)}\lambda_{\lambda_{x}\lambda^{0}_{z}(y)}\lambda_{\rho_{\lambda^{0}_{z}(y)}(x)}\underset{\eqref{first}}{=}\lambda^{-}_{\lambda_{x}\lambda^{0}_{z}(y)}\lambda_{x}\lambda_{\lambda^{0}_{z}(y)}=\lambda^{-}_{\lambda^{0}_{z}\lambda_{x}(y)}\lambda_{x}\lambda_{\lambda^{0}_{z}(y)}

\displaystyle\underset{1.}{=}\lambda^{0}_{z}\lambda^{-}_{\lambda_{x}(y)}\lambda_{x}\lambda_{\lambda^{0}_{z}(y)}\underset{3.}{=}\lambda_{\rho_{y}(x)}\lambda^{-}_{y}\lambda^{0}_{z}\lambda_{\lambda^{0}_{z}(y)}\underset{1.}{=}\lambda_{\rho_{y}(x)}\lambda^{-}_{y}\lambda^{0}_{z}\lambda_{y}=\lambda_{\rho_{y}(x)}\lambda^{0}_{z}\lambda^{0}_{y}

\displaystyle\underset{1.}{=}\lambda_{\rho_{y}(x)}\lambda^{0}_{\lambda^{0}_{z}(y)},

and hence

\displaystyle\lambda_{\rho_{\lambda^{0}_{z}(y)}(x)}\rho_{\lambda^{-}_{z}(y)}(z)=\lambda_{\rho_{y}(x)}\lambda^{0}_{\lambda^{0}_{z}(y)}\rho_{\lambda^{-}_{z}(y)}(z)=\lambda_{\rho_{y}(x)}\rho_{\lambda^{-}_{z}(y)}(z).

Finally,

\displaystyle\lambda^{0}_{\lambda^{-}_{x}(y)}\lambda^{-}_{x}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{\lambda^{-}_{x}(y)}\big(\lambda_{x}\lambda_{\lambda^{-}_{x}(y)}\big)^{-}\underset{\eqref{first}}{=}\lambda_{\lambda^{-}_{x}(y)}\big(\lambda_{\lambda^{0}_{x}(y)}\lambda_{\rho_{\lambda^{-}_{x}(y)}(x)}\big)^{-}\underset{3.}{=}\big(\lambda^{-}_{\lambda^{0}_{x}(y)}\lambda_{x}\big)^{-}\lambda^{-}_{\lambda^{0}_{x}(y)}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{-}_{x}\lambda^{0}_{\lambda^{0}_{x}(y)},

hence
6.
holds.
Therefore, the claim follows.

∎

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
(X,r)
be a quasi left non-degenerate solution such that

\displaystyle\lambda^{0}_{\lambda_{x}(y)}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{x}^{0}\lambda_{y}^{0},

(A)

\displaystyle\rho_{y}(x)

\displaystyle=\lambda^{0}_{\lambda_{x}(y)}\rho_{\lambda^{0}_{x}(y)}(x),

\displaystyle\lambda^{0}_{x}\rho_{y}

\displaystyle=\rho_{y}\lambda^{0}_{x},

for all
x,y\in X
. Then the structure

For

, we have

\displaystyle(x\triangleright_{r}y)

\displaystyle\triangleright_{r}(x\triangleright_{r}z)=\lambda_{\lambda_{x}\rho_{\lambda^{-}_{y}(x)}(y)}\rho_{\lambda^{-}_{\lambda_{x}\rho_{\lambda^{-}_{z}(x)}(z)}\lambda_{x}\rho_{\lambda^{-}_{y}(x)}(y)}\lambda_{x}\rho_{\lambda^{-}_{z}(x)}(z)

\displaystyle=\lambda_{\lambda_{x}\rho_{\lambda^{-}_{y}(x)}(y)}\underbrace{\rho_{\lambda_{\rho_{\rho_{\lambda_{z}^{-}(x)}(z)}(x)}\lambda^{-}_{\rho_{\lambda^{-}_{z}(x)}(z)}\rho_{\lambda^{-}_{y}(x)}(y)}}_{\text{by \ref{propAB}-3.}}\lambda_{x}\rho_{\lambda^{-}_{z}(x)}(z)

\displaystyle=\lambda_{\lambda_{x}\rho_{\lambda^{-}_{y}(x)}(y)}\underbrace{\lambda_{\rho_{\lambda_{\rho_{\lambda^{-}_{z}(x)}(z)}^{0}\rho_{\lambda^{-}_{y}(x)}(y)}(x)}\rho_{\lambda^{-}_{\rho_{\lambda^{-}_{z}(x)}(z)}\rho_{\lambda^{-}_{y}(x)}(y)}\rho_{\lambda^{-}_{z}(x)}(z)}_{\text{by}\ \eqref{second}}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{\lambda_{x}\rho_{\lambda^{-}_{y}(x)}(y)}\underbrace{\lambda_{\rho_{\rho_{\lambda^{-}_{y}(x)}(y)}(x)}\rho_{\lambda^{-}_{\rho_{\lambda^{-}_{z}(x)}(z)}\rho_{\lambda^{-}_{y}(x)}(y)}\rho_{\lambda^{-}_{z}(x)}(z)}_{\text{by \ref{propAB}-5.}}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\lambda_{x}\lambda_{\rho_{\lambda^{-}_{y}(x)}(y)}}_{\text{by \eqref{first}}}\rho_{\lambda^{-}_{\rho_{\lambda^{-}_{z}(x)}(z)}\rho_{\lambda^{-}_{y}(x)}(y)}\rho_{\lambda^{-}_{z}(x)}(z)

\displaystyle x\triangleright_{r}(y\triangleright_{r}z)

\displaystyle=\lambda_{x}\rho_{\lambda^{-}_{\lambda_{y}\rho_{\lambda^{-}_{z}(y)}(z)}(x)}\lambda_{y}\rho_{\lambda^{-}_{z}(y)}(z)=\lambda_{x}\underbrace{\rho_{\lambda_{\rho_{\rho_{\lambda^{-}_{z}(y)}(z)}(y)}\lambda^{-}_{\rho_{\lambda^{-}_{z}(y)}(z)}\lambda^{-}_{y}(x)}}_{\text{by \ref{propA}}}\lambda_{y}\rho_{\lambda^{-}_{z}(y)}(z)

\displaystyle=\lambda_{x}\underbrace{\lambda_{\rho_{\lambda^{0}_{\rho_{\lambda^{-}_{z}(y)}(z)}\lambda^{-}_{y}(x)}(y)}\rho_{\lambda^{-}_{\rho_{\lambda^{-}_{z}(y)}(z)}\lambda^{-}_{y}(x)}\rho_{\lambda^{-}_{z}(y)}(z)}_{\text{by \eqref{second}}}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{x}\underbrace{\lambda_{\rho_{\lambda^{-}_{y}(x)}(y)}\rho_{\lambda^{-}_{\rho_{\lambda^{-}_{z}(y)}(z)}\lambda^{-}_{y}(x)}\rho_{\lambda^{-}_{z}(y)}(z)}_{\text{by \ref{propAB}-5.}}.

\displaystyle\rho

{}_{\lambda^{-}_{\rho_{\lambda^{-}_{z}(x)}(z)}\rho_{\lambda^{-}_{y}(x)}(y)}\rho_{\lambda^{-}_{z}(x)}(z)=\rho_{\underbrace{\lambda^{-}_{\lambda^{-}_{z}(x)}\lambda^{-}_{z}\lambda_{\lambda^{0}_{z}(x)}}_{\text{by \ref{propAB}-2.}}\rho_{\lambda^{-}_{y}(x)}(y)}\rho_{\lambda^{-}_{z}(x)}(z)

Corollary 3.10

Let
(X,r)

5

In this section, we focus on the notion of the Płonka sum of racks, according to the general notion of

(or

Definition 5.1
.

Let
Y
be a (lower) semilattice and
\{\left(X_{\alpha},\triangleright_{\alpha}\right)\mid\alpha\in Y\}
a family of disjoint racks indexed by
Y
. For all
\alpha,\beta\in Y
such that
\alpha\geq\beta
, let
\phi_{\alpha,\beta}:X_{\alpha}\to X_{\beta}
be a rack homomorphism such that

1.

\phi_{\alpha,\alpha}=\id_{X_{\alpha}}
, for every
\alpha\in Y
;

2.

\phi_{\beta,\gamma}\phi_{\alpha,\beta}=\phi_{\alpha,\gamma}
, for all
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in Y
such that
\alpha\geq\beta\geq\gamma

Set
X:=\displaystyle\bigcup_{\alpha\in Y}X_{\alpha}
. Then, the operation

\displaystyle\forall\,a\in X_{\alpha},\ \forall\,b\in X_{\beta}\qquad a\triangleright b:=\phi_{\alpha,\alpha\beta}(a)\triangleright_{\alpha\beta}\phi_{\beta,\alpha\beta}(b)

(11)

with a structure of a shelf that we call

\left(X_{\alpha},\triangleright_{\alpha}\right)
indexed by
Y
.

Note that if

is a Płonka sum of racks indexed by a certain

, then the
projection map

\pi:X\to Y
defined by
\pi(a)=\alpha
, if

is a semigroup epimorphism. We underline that this happens in general for Płonka sum of algebras.

Our aim is to describe a class of quasi racks that are Płonka sum of racks. As will be seen, these are quasi racks that give rise to a derived solution.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
\left(X,\triangleright\right)

(

\left(X,\triangleright\right)

For convenience, for

\displaystyle L^{[\alpha\beta]}_{a}(b):=a\triangleright b.

Following the

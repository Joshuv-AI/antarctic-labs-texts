f f -mode oscillations of hybrid stars with pasta construction
Zi-Yue Zheng (郑子岳) 1, Jin-Biao Wei (魏金标) 2, Huan Chen (陈欢) 2, Xiao-Ping Zheng (郑小平) 1, G. F. Burgio 3, H.-J. Schulze 3

Abstract
We investigate nonradial f f -mode oscillations of hybrid neutron stars in full general relativity, employing hybrid equations of state describing a nuclear outer core and a pasta-phase transition to a quark-matter core. The validity of various universal relations is confirmed for those stars. Prospects of observations are also discussed.



I
Introduction

Neutron stars (NSs) are the densest stars observed in the Universe
and provide natural laboratories for studying high-density nuclear matter (NM).
Due to the core density of a NS predicted to be several times
the nuclear saturation density
\rho_{0}\approx 0.16\,\text{fm}^{-3}
,
a phase transition to deconfined quark matter (QM)
may take place in its interiors

[
1
,
2
]
.
Hence, NSs can be used to constrain theories concerning high-density QM
and NM-QM phase transitions, for which the
Maxwell construction (MC) and the
Gibbs construction (GC) are commonly used.

It is noteworthy that only bulk-matter contributions are included in GC and MC,
where finite-size effects depending on a surface tension
\sigma
are neglected.
The MC corresponds to a extremely large surface tension
and leads to a bulk separation of quark and hadron phases

[
3
,
4
]
,
whereas the GC corresponding to a vanishing surface tension
determines a range of baryon number densities where the two phases coexist

[
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
]
.
If a moderate surface tension is employed,
a hadron-quark mixed phase (MP) with pasta structures is expected to occur

[
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
14
,
15
]
.
Thus the surface tension
\sigma
plays a key role
in determining the various structures in the hadron-quark MP.
It is therefore interesting to examine what pasta construction
can be realized in NSs and how it can influence the NS properties.

After the first direct observation of gravitational waves (GWs)
from a binary black hole (BH) merger
[
16
]
,
more and more GW signals were detected,
including binary NS systems
[
17
,
18
]

and NS-BH systems
[
19
]
.
These GW observations have opened a new window for studying
the internal structure of NSs,
especially in helping us to understand the high-density NM
[
20
]

and the astrophysics processes under extreme conditions
[
21
]
.
Recently, due to GW observations from possibly the most massive NSs
ever detected,
hybrid stars (HSs) have once again become popular subjects

[
22
,
23
,
24
,
25
,
26
]
.

NSs are expected to emit various electromagnetic signals and GWs
during specific astrophysical processes.
Stellar oscillations, which are a sensitive probe of NS internal composition,
may provide a tool for addressing the problem of high-density NM.
Especially, the (
l\geq 2
) nonradial oscillation (NRO) of a star
can carry information about the core of a NS through GW emission

[
27
]
,
which may occur during a supernova explosion
[
28
]
,
or during the post-merger phase of a binary NS

[
29
,
30
,
31
,
32
]
,
or in an isolated perturbed NS
[
33
]
.
For a non-rotating star, NRO can be divided into various modes:
for fluid modes,
these are
p
(pressure)-mode,
f
(fundamental)-mode, and
g
(gravity)-mode,
which indicate the various dominant restoring forces for the perturbations.
The buoyancy acts as the
g
-mode restoring force
to bring perturbed fluid elements back into equilibrium,
while the restoring force of
f
-mode and
p
-mode is pressure.

Since the
g
-mode eigenfrequencies are relatively small
and provide us with an appropriate observable,
many works use them as probes for new degrees of freedom within a NS,
such as deconfined QM

[
34
,
35
,
36
,
37
,
38
,
39
]
,
hyperons
[
40
,
41
]
,
and superfluidity
[
42
,
40
,
43
,
44
]
.
However, the
p
-mode eigenfrequency is too high to be observed,
far beyond the sensitivity range of the next-generation GW detectors

[
45
,
46
]
.
The
f
-mode eigenfrequencies lie between that of
g
and
p
-mode,
with frequency

f_{f}=\real\omega_{f}/2\pi\sim 1.3-3\,\text{kHz}
.
It was argued that the excitation of the
f
-modes can be enhanced
by the spin and eccentricity during the inspiral phase of NS mergers

[
47
,
48
]
.
The
f
-mode frequency is believed to be related to the mean density of the NSs

[
49
,
45
,
50
]
,
the pressure of matter in
\beta
equilibrium at densities
equal to or slightly higher than the nuclear saturation density

[
46
]
,
and the tidal deformability during the inspiral phase of NS mergers

[
51
,
52
,
53
]
.

From the asteroseismology of NSs, a set of
so-called universal relations (URs) has been proposed.
This method is expected to help estimating macroscopic quantities of NSs,
such as the mass, radius, or tidal deformability,
by correlating them
with measured frequency and damping time of a particular oscillation mode.
Among the many studies in the literature investigating the
f
-mode oscillation,

[
45
,
49
]
first related the NS global properties
with the frequency and damping time of that mode.
Since this pioneering work,
many alternative URs have been proposed,
correlating the dimensionless frequency
\Omega_{f}\equiv M\omega_{f}

with other NS properties
such as the dimensionless moment of inertia
\bar{I}\equiv I/M^{3}

[
54
,
55
]

and the dimensionless tidal deformability
\Lambda

[
51
,
56
]
.
These URs have been examined in NSs with various configurations,
including purely hadronic stars
[
56
]
,
hyperonic stars
[
50
]
,
hybrid stars with quark-hadron crossover
[
57

or MC
[
58

and strange quark stars
[
58

Thus, it is natural to investigate whether NSs with hadron-quark pasta structure
also follow the same URs.

In this article,
we adopt the Brueckner-Hartree-Fock (BHF) theory for NM

[
59
,
60
]
,
which is based on realistic two-nucleon forces
supplemented with compatible microscopic three-nucleon forces

[
61
,
62
,

,

]

and features reasonable properties at nuclear saturation density
in agreement with experiments

[
63
,
64
,
65
,
66
,
67
,
68
]
.
Moreover, the BHF approach is also compatible with observable constraints
obtained from the analysis of the GW170817 event and NICER mission

[
67
,
68
,
69
]
.
For QM we adopt a model in the framework of Dyson-Schwinger equations (DSM)

[
70
,
71
]
,
which provides a continuum approach to quantum chromodynamics (QCD)
that can simultaneously address both confinement
and dynamical chiral symmetry breaking

[
72
,
73

.
We employ the pasta construction between the hadron and deconfined quark phase,
which determines a range where hadron and quark phase coexist.
In this framework,
we study the URs of HSs with pasta construction in full general relativity (GR).

This work is organized as follows.
In Sec.

II
Equation of state

II.1
Nuclear matter

The essential ingredient in BHF many-body approach
is the in-medium Brueckner reaction matrix
K
,
which is the solution of the Bethe-Goldstone equation

K(\rho,x_{p};E)=V+\real\sum_{1,2}V\frac{\ket{12}Q\bra{12}}{E-e_{1}-e_{2}}K(\rho,x_{p};E)\>

(1)

and

U_{1}(\rho,x_{p})=\real\sum_{2<k_{F}^{(2)}}\expectationvalue{G(\rho,\xp;e_1+e_2)}{12}_{a}\>,

(2)

where
V
is the nucleon-nucleon interaction potential,

x_{p}\equiv\rho_{p}/\rho
is the proton fraction,
and
\rho_{p}
and
\rho
are the proton and the total nucleon number densities,
respectively.

E
is the starting energy,

Q
is the Pauli operator, and

e_{i}\equiv k_{i}^{2}\!/2m_{i}+U_{i}
is the single-particle energy.
The multi-indices
1,2
denote in general momentum, isospin, and spin.
The corresponding BHF energy density can then be expressed as

\varepsilon=\sum_{1<k_{F}^{(1)}}\quantity( \frac{k^2}{2m_1} + \frac 12 U_1(k) )\>.

(3)

The chemical potentials of the nucleons can be derived in a consistent way,

\mu_{i}={\frac{\partial\varepsilon}{\partial\rho_{i}}}\>.

(4)

We impose cold, neutrino-free, charge neutral, and catalyzed matter
consisting of neutrons, protons, and leptons (
e^{-},\mu^{-}
)
in beta equilibrium due to weak interactions.
Finally, the pressure is given by

p(\varepsilon)=\rho^{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial\rho}\frac{\varepsilon(\rho_{i}(\rho))}{\rho}=\sum_{i}\rho_{i}\mu_{i}-\varepsilon\>.

(5)

In the BHF approach,
the nucleon-nucleon interaction potential
V
is the only necessary input.
In this work, we employ the Argonne
V_{18}
(V18)
[
74
]

and Bonn-B (BOB)
[
75
,
76
]
potentials,
supplemented with compatible microscopic three-body forces

[
61
,
62
,
63
,
64
]
.
With this common prescription,
the saturation point of symmetric NM
and related properties can be reproduced correctly.
We use the convenient empirical parametrizations
given in Refs.
[
77
,
67
]
.

The BHF approach provides only the EOS for the bulk-matter core region
without cluster formation,
and therefore has to be combined with a low-density crust EOS.
In this work, we adopt the unified Shen2020 EOS
[
78
]
,
which is frequently used
for the simulations of core-collapse supernovae and NS mergers.
We also calculated the results using the full Shen2020 EOS for comparison.
Since there are no rigorous theoretical or observational limitations on
how to join the crust and core EOSs,
we opt for the most straightforward and simplest method,
namely a continuous transition
at locations where pressure and energy density coincide.

II.2
Quark matter

For the description of deconfined QM,
we adopt the framework of Dyson-Schwinger equations

[
70
,
79
]
,
which are the fundamental equations of motion in continuum quantum field theory.
The fundamental quantity of the DSM is the quark propagator
S(p;\mu)

at zero temperature and finite chemical potential
\mu
,
which can be expressed as

\displaystyle S(p;\mu)^{-1}=Z_{2}\left[i\bm{\gamma}\cdot\bm{p}+i\gamma_{4}\tilde{p}_{4}+m_{q}\right]+\Sigma(p;\mu)\>,

(6)

where
p=(\bm{p},p_{4})
is the four-momentum,

\tilde{p}_{4}=p_{4}+i\mu
,
and
m_{q}
is the current mass of the quark
q=u,d,s
.
In this work,
we choose
m_{u}=m_{d}=0
and
m_{s}=115\,\text{MeV}
.
The renormalized self-energy
\Sigma(p;\mu)
is expressed as

\displaystyle\Sigma(p;\mu)=

\displaystyle~Z_{1}g^{2}(\mu)

\displaystyle\times\!\!\int\!\!\frac{d^{4}q}{(2\pi)^{4}}D_{\rho\sigma}(p-q;\mu)\frac{\lambda^{a}}{2}\gamma_{\rho}S(q;\mu)\frac{\lambda^{a}}{2}\Gamma_{\sigma}(q,p;\mu)\>,

(7)

where
Z_{1}
and
Z_{2}
are the quark-gluon vertex and
quark wavefunction renormalization constants, respectively.

D_{\rho\sigma}
is the full gluon propagator,

\Gamma_{\sigma}
is the full quark-gluon vertex,

\lambda^{a}
are the Gell-Mann matrices,
and
g(\mu)
is the density-dependent coupling constant.
To obtain the quark propagator,
the so-called rainbow approximation and a
chemical-potential-modified Gaussian-type effective interaction

[
70
,
80
]
are adopted.

The quark number density, pressure, and energy density
for each quark flavor at zero temperature can be obtained as

[
81
,
82
]

\displaystyle\rho_{q}(\mu_{q})

\displaystyle=6\int\!\!\frac{d^{4}p}{(2\pi)^{4}}\mathop{\text{tr}_{D}}\left[-\gamma_{4}S_{q}(p;\mu_{q})\right]\>,

(8)

\displaystyle p_{q}(\mu_{q})

\displaystyle=p_{q}(\mu_{q,0})+\int_{\mu_{q,0}}^{\mu_{q}}d\mu\rho_{q}(\mu)\>,

(9)

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{q}(\mu_{q})

\displaystyle=-p_{q}(\mu_{q})+\mu_{q}\rho_{q}(\mu_{q})\>.

(10)

The total pressure and energy density are given
by summing contributions from all quark flavors
and those from electrons and muons.
The pressure of QM at zero density is determined by a
phenomenological bag constant
[
83
]
,

B_{\text{DS}}=-\!\!\sum_{q=u,d,s}p_{q}(\mu_{q,0})\>,

(11)

which is set to
90\,\text{MeV}\,\text{fm}^{-3}

[
79
,
71
,
83
]
.

II.3
Hadron-quark mixed phase

It is well known that the surface tension
\sigma
at the NM-QM interface
plays a crucial role in determining the structure of the MP.
GC and MC correspond to the two extreme cases of
zero and very large surface tension, respectively.
In this work we adopt the energy-minimization method
[
13
,
15
]

to obtain the pasta structure of the MP,
where the hadronic and quark phases inside a Wigner-Seitz cell
are assumed to be separated by a sharp interface with a finite surface tension.
The geometric configuration of pasta structures
may change from droplet to rod, slab, tube, and bubble
with increasing baryon number density

[
10
,
11
,
12
,
14
,
13
,
15
,
14
]
.

However, the value of the surface tension is still poorly known so far.
Some works based on, e.g.,
MIT bag model
[
84
,
85
,
86
]
,
linear sigma model
[
87
,
88
]
,
a geometrical approach
[
89
]
,
three-flavor Polyakov quark-meson model
[
90
]
,
Nambu-Jona-Lasinio (NJL) model
[
91
,
92
]
,
nucleon-meson model
[
93
]
,
gave relatively small values of the surface tension

\sigma\lesssim 30\;\text{MeV}\,\text{fm}^{-2}
.
One work based on the DSM predicts the interface tension at zero temperature
to be
25.4\;\text{MeV}\,\text{fm}^{-2}
for the hadronization process
and
40.0\;\text{MeV}\,\text{fm}^{-2}
for the opposite
[
94
]
.
On the other hand,
large values of
\sigma
are also obtained by using
the multiple reflection expansion method in the NJL model
including color superconductivity,
which predicted
\sigma\approx 145-165\;\text{MeV}\,\text{fm}^{-2}

[
95
]
,
and from the extended quasiparticle model
[
96
]

and color-flavor-locked phase
[
97
]
.
Due to this uncertainty,
we treat the surface tension as a free parameter,
comparing results with

\sigma=0~\text{(corresponding to GC)},10
, and
30\;\text{MeV}\,\text{fm}^{-2}
.

In our model,
all configurations of HSs with MC are unstable,
due to violation of the stability condition

\partial M/\partial\varepsilon_{c}\geq 0

at the QM onset.

III
Neutron Stars

III.1
Macroscopic features

With a specified EOS, the mass-radius relation,
one of the most straightforward and simple macroscopic features,
can be obtained by solving the TOV equations.
The Schwarzschild metric for a spherically symmetric system is given by

ds^{2}=e^{\nu(r)}dt^{2}-e^{\lambda(r)}dr^{2}-r^{2}(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\!\theta d\varphi^{2})\>,

(12)

where
e^{\nu(r)}
and
e^{\lambda(r)}
are metric functions.
The TOV equations
[
98
,
99
]

for the hydrostatic equilibrium of stars in GR are given by

\displaystyle\frac{dp}{dr}

\displaystyle=\frac{(p+\varepsilon)(m+4\pi r^{3}p)}{r^{2}(2m/r-1)}\>,

(13)

\displaystyle\frac{dm}{dr}

\displaystyle=4\pi r^{2}\varepsilon\>,

(14)

where
p
,
\varepsilon
, and
m
are the pressure,
the energy density,
and the enclosed gravitational mass, respectively.
The corresponding metric functions are given by

\displaystyle e^{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-2m/r}\>,

(15)

\displaystyle\frac{d\nu}{dr}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{p+\varepsilon}\frac{dp}{dr}\>.

(16)

One can obtain radius
R
and mass
M=m(R)

of a NS for a given central pressure or density
by solving Eqs. (
13
,
14
)
with the boundary condition
p(R)=0
.

GWs from the merger of binary NSs or a BH with a NS,
now provide valuable information on the EOS and internal structure of NSs,
in particular their dimensionless tidal deformability,
defined by the tidal Love number
k_{2}

[
100
,
101
]
,

\Lambda=\frac{2}{3}\frac{k_{2}}{\beta^{5}}=\frac{16}{15}\frac{z}{F}

(17)

with
\beta\equiv M/R
the compactness of the star and

\displaystyle z\equiv

\displaystyle~(1-2\beta)^{2}[2-y_{R}+2\beta(y_{R}-1)]\>,

\displaystyle F\equiv

\displaystyle~6\beta(2-y_{R})+6\beta^{2}(5y_{R}-8)+4\beta^{3}(13-11y_{R})

\displaystyle+4\beta^{4}(3y_{R}-2)+8\beta^{5}(1+y_{R})+3z\ln(1-2\beta)\>.

(18)

y_{R}=y(R)
can be obtained by solving the additional differential equation
for
y(r)

[
102
]

\frac{dy}{dr}=-\frac{y^{2}}{r}-\frac{y-6}{r-2m}-rQ\>,

(19)

where

Q=4\pi\frac{(5-y)\varepsilon+(9+y)p+(p+\varepsilon)(dp/d\varepsilon)^{-1}}{1-2m/r}-\bigg(\frac{d\nu}{dr}\bigg)^{2}

(20)

with the boundary condition
y(r=0)=2
.

Moreover, in GR for a uniformly slowly rotating star,
the moment of inertia (MOI) can be expressed as
[
103
]

I=\frac{w_{R}R^{3}}{6+2w_{R}}\>,

(21)

where
w_{R}=w(R)
can be obtained by solving the differential equation

\frac{dw}{dr}=4\pi r\frac{(p+\varepsilon)(4+w)}{1-2m/r}-\frac{w}{r}(3+w)\>,

(22)

with the boundary condition
w(0)=0
.

III.2
Nonradial oscillations

Thorne et al. developed a complete theory for NROs of NSs in GR

[
27
]
.
In this work,
we study only even-parity perturbations of the Regge-Wheeler metric,

\displaystyle ds^{2}=

\displaystyle-e^{\nu(r)}\big[1+r^{l}H_{0}(r)e^{i\omega t}Y_{lm}(\theta,\varphi)\big]dt^{2}

\displaystyle+e^{\lambda(r)}\big[1-r^{l}H_{0}(r)e^{i\omega t}Y_{lm}(\theta,\varphi)\big]dr^{2}

\displaystyle+\big[1-r^{l}K(r)e^{i\omega t}Y_{lm}(\theta,\varphi)\big]r^{2}(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\!\theta d\varphi^{2})

\displaystyle-2i\omega r^{l+1}H_{1}(r)e^{i\omega t}Y_{lm}(\theta,\varphi)dtdr\>,

(23)

where
Y^{l}_{m}(\theta,\varphi)
are the usual spherical harmonics,
and
H_{0}
,
H_{1}
, and
K
are metric perturbation functions.

\omega=2\pi f+i/\tau
is the complex eigenfrequency of the NRO,
with
\tau
the damping time of the GW.
The perturbation of the fluid in the star is described
by the Lagrangian displacement vector
\xi^{\alpha}
in terms of
the dimensionless eigenfunctions
W(r)
and
V(r)

of the radial and transverse fluid perturbations,

\displaystyle\xi^{r}

\displaystyle=r^{l-1}e^{-\lambda/2}WY^{l}_{m}\,e^{i\omega t}\>,

\displaystyle\xi^{\theta}

\displaystyle=-r^{l-2}V\partial_{\theta}Y^{l}_{m}\,e^{i\omega t}\>,

\displaystyle\xi^{\varphi}

\displaystyle=-r^{l-2}(\sin\theta)^{-2}V\partial_{\varphi}Y^{l}_{m}\,e^{i\omega t}\>.

(24)

An additional fluid perturbation amplitude
X
,
related to the Lagrangian perturbation of the pressure,
is defined as

\Delta p=-r^{l}e^{-\nu/2}XY_{m}^{l}e^{i\omega t}\>.

(25)

The full NRO equations governing these perturbation functions
and the metric perturbations inside the star are given by

[
27
,
104
,
58
]

\displaystyle r\frac{dH_{1}}{dr}

\displaystyle=-\big[l+1+2be^{\lambda}+4\pi r^{2}e^{\lambda}(p-\varepsilon)\big]H_{1}+e^{\lambda}\big[H_{0}+K-16\pi qV\big]\>,

(26)

\displaystyle r\frac{dK}{dr}

\displaystyle=H_{0}+(n+1)H_{1}+\big[e^{\lambda}Q-l-1\big]K-8\pi qe^{\lambda/2}W\>,

(27)

\displaystyle r\frac{dW}{dr}

\displaystyle=r^{2}e^{\lambda/2}\bigg[\frac{H_{0}}{2}+K+\frac{e^{-\nu/2}}{qc_{s}^{2}}X\bigg]-(l+1)\big[W+le^{\lambda/2}V\big]\>,

(28)

\displaystyle r\frac{dX}{dr}

\displaystyle=-lX+\frac{q}{2}e^{\nu/2}\Bigg\{-\frac{4(n+1)e^{\lambda}Q}{r^{2}}V

\displaystyle+\big[3e^{\lambda}Q-1\big]K+\big[1-e^{\lambda}Q\big]H_{0}+\big[(\omega r)^{2}e^{-\nu}+n+1\big]H_{1}

\displaystyle-e^{\lambda/2}\bigg[\frac{2\omega^{2}}{e^{\nu}}+8\pi q-\frac{r^{2}}{e^{\lambda/2}}\frac{d}{dr}\bigg(\frac{e^{-\lambda/2}}{r^{2}}\frac{d\nu}{dr}\bigg)\bigg]W\Bigg\}\>,

(29)

where
n\equiv(l-1)(l+2)/2
,

b\equiv m(r)/r
,

Q\equiv 4\pi r^{2}p+b
,

q\equiv p+\varepsilon
,
and
c_{s}
is the adiabatic sound speed.
In the following,
we adopt the equilibrium sound speed

c_{e}=\sqrt{dp/d\varepsilon}
as an approximation,
defined under the assumption
that reactions are faster than the oscillation.

The perturbation functions
H_{0}
and
V
can be expressed as

\displaystyle H_{0}

\displaystyle=(n+2b+Q)^{-1}\Big\{\big[(\omega r)^{2}e^{-(\nu+\lambda)}-(n+1)Q\big]H_{1}

\displaystyle\hskip 5.69054pt+\big[n-(\omega r)^{2}e^{-\nu}-Q^{2}e^{\lambda}+Q\big]K+8\pi r^{2}e^{-\nu/2}X\Big\}\>,

(30)

\displaystyle V

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{\nu}}{\omega^{2}}\bigg[\frac{e^{-\nu/2}}{q}X-\frac{Q}{r^{2}}e^{\lambda/2}W-\frac{1}{2}H_{0}\bigg]\>.

(31)

In order to solve the NRO eigen-equations Eq. (
26
-
29
),
one also needs boundary conditions,
which, in the NS center
r=0
, are given by

\displaystyle H_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{lK+8\pi qW}{n+1}\>,

\displaystyle K

\displaystyle=\pm q\>,

\displaystyle W

\displaystyle=1\>,

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle=qe^{\nu/2}\left\{\left[\frac{4\pi}{3}(3p+\varepsilon)-\frac{\omega^{2}}{le^{\nu}}\right]W+\frac{K}{2}\right\}\>,

(32)

while at the outer surface of the star,
the Lagrangian perturbation of the pressure should vanish,

X(R)=0\>.

(33)

The perturbations of the metric outside the star
with the disappearance of fluid perturbations
can be described with a unique function
Z
,
which satisfies the Zerilli equation

\frac{d^{2}Z}{dr^{*2}}=\left[V_{Z}(r)-\omega^{2}\right]Z\>,

(34)

where
r^{*}\equiv r+2M\ln(1/2b-1)
and now
b=M/r
with the NS mass
M
.
The metric functions are

\displaystyle\binom{K(r)}{H_{1}(r)}

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}g(r)&1\\
h(r)&k(r)\end{array}\right)\binom{Z(r^{*})/r}{dZ(r^{*})/dr^{*}}\>,

\displaystyle g(r)

\displaystyle=\frac{n(n+1)+3nb+6b^{2}}{(n+3b)}\>,

\displaystyle h(r)

\displaystyle=\frac{n-3nb-3b^{2}}{(1-2b)(n+3b)}\>,

\displaystyle k(r)

\displaystyle=\frac{dr^{*}}{dr}(r)=\frac{1}{1-2b}\>,

(37)

and

H_{0}=\frac{\big[(\omega r)^{2}-(n+1)b\big]H_{1}+\left[n(1-2b)-(\omega r)^{2}+b(1-3b)\right]K}{(1-2b)(3b+n)}\>,

(38)

whereas the effective potential is

V_{Z}(r)=2(1-2b)\frac{n^{2}(n+1)+3n^{2}b+9nb^{2}+9b^{3}}{(n+3b)^{2}r^{2}}\>.

(39)

The asymptotic solution of
Z
at large
r
can be decomposed
into incoming and outgoing wave functions as

\displaystyle\binom{Z}{dZ/dr^{*}}=

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}Z_{\text{out}}&Z_{\text{in}}\\
dZ_{\text{out}}/dr^{*}&dZ_{\text{in}}/dr^{*}\end{array}\right)\binom{A_{\text{out}}}{A_{\text{in}}}\>,

where

\displaystyle Z_{\text{out}}=

\displaystyle e^{-i\omega r^{*}}\alpha\Big[1+\frac{c_{1}}{\omega r}+\frac{c_{2}}{(\omega r)^{2}}+\mathcal{O}(r^{-3})\Big]\>,

\displaystyle\frac{dZ_{\text{out}}}{dr^{*}}=

\displaystyle-i\omega e^{-i\omega r^{*}}\alpha\Big[1+\frac{c_{1}}{\omega r}+\frac{c_{2}+ic_{1}(1-2b)}{(\omega r)^{2}}+\mathcal{O}(r^{-3})\Big]\>,

\displaystyle c_{1}=

\displaystyle-i(n+1)\>,

\displaystyle c_{2}=

\displaystyle[-n(n+1)+i(3+6/n)M\omega]/2\>.

(42)

Here
A_{\text{out}}
and
A_{\text{in}}
are the amplitudes
of outgoing and incoming waves, respectively.

Z_{\text{in}}
is the complex conjugate of
Z_{\text{out}}

and
\alpha
can be any complex number.
At infinity, the gravitational radiation field should be purely outgoing,
in other words, the amplitude
A_{\text{in}}
is zero.

We now briefly introduce our numerical method
for the quadrupole
(l=2)

f
-mode oscillations.
First,
starting with two sets of central boundary conditions (
32
)
and integrating Eqs. (
26
-
29
) from NS center to the surface
one obtains two trial solutions.
These are linearly combined to obtain a solution
satisfying also the outer boundary condition (
33
).
Outside the star, the boundary values of the Zerilli equation (
34
)
at the outer surface of the star are fixed using (
37
)
at
r=R
.
Then the equation is integrated numerically
up to
r=50\;\real\omega^{-1}
,
which is found to be adequate for the asymptotic solution
[
104
]
.
The complex coefficients
A_{\text{in}}
and
A_{\text{out}}
are obtained by
matching the analytic expressions (
42
) for
Z
and
dZ/dr^{*}

with the numerical results.
Finally, we adopt the method of continued fractions to
search the complex eigenfrequencies
\omega
satisfying
A_{\text{in}}=0
.

We tested our numerical routines
by reproducing the quadrupole
f
-mode complex frequencies
from Ref.
[
105
]
for polytropic EOSs,

f
-mode perturbation functions and complex frequencies
from Ref.
[
58
]
for SLy4 EOS,
and
p
-mode perturbation functions and complex frequencies
from Ref.
[

]
for SLy4 EOS.

III.3
GW strain

In quadrupole approximation and transverse-traceless (TT) gauge,
the GW strain (metric perturbation) is
[
106
,
107
]

h_{ij}^{\text{TT}}(t,D)=\frac{2}{D}\ddot{Q}_{ij}^{\text{TT}}(t-D)\>,

(43)

where
i,j=1,2,3
are the indices in Cartesian coordinates,

D
is the distance to the source,
the two dots represent the second time derivative,
and the traceless quadrupole moment is

Q_{ij}^{\text{TT}}(t)=\int d^{3}\bm{x}\,\varepsilon(t,\bm{x})\Big(x_{i}x_{j}-\frac{1}{3}\delta_{ij}|\bm{x}|^{2}\Big)\>.

(44)

The metric perturbation tensor can be decomposed as

\mathbf{h}^{\text{TT}}=h_{+}\mathbf{e}_{+}+h_{\times}\mathbf{e}_{\times}\>,

(45)

where
\mathbf{e}_{+}
and
\mathbf{e}_{\times}

are the unit tensors of plus and cross polarization.
In the case of a symmetric metric,
the cross polarization
h_{\times}
is zero,

Q_{ij}
has only diagonal components

Q_{11}=Q_{22}=-\frac{1}{2}Q_{33}

(for simplicity, we omit TT from
Q_{ij}
),
and the GW strain can be calculated by
[
108
]

h_{+}=\frac{3\sin^{2}\theta}{2D}\ddot{Q}_{33}\>,

(46)

where
\theta
is the inclination angle.
In this work, we choose
\sin\theta=1
.
After some derivation,
\ddot{Q}_{33}
can be written as
[
???
]

\displaystyle\ddot{Q}_{33}

\displaystyle=\left|\ddot{Q}_{33}\right|e^{-i\omega t}

\displaystyle=\int dr\,r^{2}\sin\theta d\theta d\varphi\,r^{2}\left(\sin^{2}\theta-\frac{1}{3}\right)\frac{d^{2}\delta\epsilon}{dt^{2}}\>,

(47)

and thus

\left|\ddot{Q}_{33}\right|=\frac{4\sqrt{\pi/5}}{3}\omega^{2}\int dr\,r^{4}\delta\epsilon(r)\>,

(48)

where
\delta\epsilon
is the Eulerian perturbation of the energy density,
given as
[
27
]

\displaystyle\delta\epsilon=

\displaystyle-(p+\varepsilon)\left[e^{-\lambda/2}\left(3W+r\frac{dW}{dr}\right)+l(l+1)V-\frac{r^{2}}{2}H_{0}-r^{2}K\right]

\displaystyle-r\frac{d\varepsilon}{dr}e^{-\lambda/2}W\>.

(49)

To determine the amplitude of the GW strain,
which depends on the oscillation amplitudes,
it is necessary to use the total energy of oscillation for normalization.
In Newtonian approximation,
the kinetic energy per radial distance
of the
k
th eigenmode is
[
109
]

\frac{dE_{k}}{dr}=\frac{\omega^{2}}{2}(p+\varepsilon)e^{(\lambda-\nu)/2}r^{4}\left[W^{2}+l(l+1)V^{2}\right]\>.

(50)

Since the potential energy is equal to
the kinetic energy during the oscillation
[
27
]
,
the total energy of the star’s oscillation is
E_{\text{os}}=2E_{k}
.
In order to estimate the GW damping time
and compare it with the one from the full GR,
one can use the lowest-order post-Newtonian quadrupole formula

[
109
,
110
]

for the GW radiation power,

p_{\text{GW}}=\frac{(l+1)(l+2)}{8\pi(l-1)l}\left[\frac{4\pi\omega^{l+1}}{(2l+1)!!}\int_{0}^{R}dr\,r^{l+2}\delta\epsilon\right]^{2}\>.

(51)

To assess the accuracy of the quadrupole approximation,
considering the
f
-mode oscillation to
be efficient at extracting energy at the level of
oscillations and driving the emission of GWs, i.e.,

E_{\text{GW}}\approx E_{\text{os}}
,
one can then compare the estimated damping time

\tau^{\text{ES}}=\frac{E_{\text{GW}}}{p_{\text{GW}}}\>

(52)

with the damping time obtained from the full GR.

In this work, we use the real parts of
the fluid perturbation amplitude and frequency
in full GR to calculate the total energy of the oscillation
and the GW radiation power
using Eqs. (
50
) and (
51
), respectively,
and to estimate the damping time using Eq. (
52
).
By comparing it with the accurate value from the full solution,
we can evaluate the approximation degree
of Eq. (
50
) and (
51
),
and finally, we estimate the amplitude of the GW strain.

V
Conclusions

With the next generation of GW telescopes,
the GWs from quasinormal modes of NS oscillation may be detectable,
as we have also shown in this work,
where we investigated non-radial quadrupole
f
-mode oscillations
of cold and isolated NSs,
including pure NSs and HSs.
We adopted the BHF theory for NM, the DSM for QM,
and the pasta construction for their phase transition.
Based on the equilibrium structure,
we solved the oscillation equations within full GR,
and obtained the metric and fluid-displacement perturbations
inside and outside NSs
as well as the complex eigenfrequencies for the various EOSs.

If a moderate hadron-quark surface tension
\sigma
is employed,
in the MP several pasta configurations may appear in sequence of
droplet, rod, slab, tube, and bubble with increasing density.
As
\sigma
increases,
the MP density range shrinks.
The pasta phase also influences the mass-radius relations of HSs.
All EOSs considered in the article are compatible with
two-solar-mass observations
and NICER mass-radius constraints of NSs.
The corresponding tidal deformability and MOI
also satisfy the constraints from the analysis of GW170817.
The
f_{f}
of both pure NSs and HSs lies in the range
1.5\text{--}2.5\,\text{kHz}
,
and the damping times are of the order of a few tenths of a second
with a corresponding GW radiation power
that might be detectable.
However, the differences of
f
-mode characteristics
between HSs and pure NSs are not large,
compared to the differences between pure NSs with different models.

Previous works have studied
f
-mode frequencies and damping times,
and proposed EOS-insensitive URs with dimensionless MOI
\bar{I}

and dimensionless tidal deformability
\Lambda
.
The validity for hadronic, pure quark, and hybrid stars was confirmed.
However, HSs were studied only with MC phase transition,
GC phase transition, or crossover.
We extended these studies to HSs with the pasta-construction phase transition,
showing that the URs are also valid in this case,
which means that they cannot be used to indicate
the presence of QM in NSs.
The URs were found to be valid within a few percents
for the EOSs we tested.

Apart from cold isolated NSs,
quadrupole oscillations also occur in various newly born NSs,
after NS mergers or supernova explosions,
which are expected to be more energetic and easier observable.
In these cases,
one needs to consider more realistic environment effects,
such as the EOS at finite temperature,
the temperature/entropy distribution in NSs,
the neutrino trapping effects,
viscosity and heat conduction,
and also (differential) rotation.
We leave these issues to future work.

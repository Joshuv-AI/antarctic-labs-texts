1.
Introduction

Given a finite group
G
, we denote with
d(G)
the smallest cardinality of a generating set for
G.

Definition 1.1
.

A group
G
has the minimal generating set exchange (MGSE) property if

\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{d(G)}\rangle=\langle y_{1},\dots,y_{d(G)}\rangle=G

implies that for any
1\leq i\leq d(G)
there exists
1\leq j\leq d(G)
such that

\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{i-1},y_{j},x_{i+1},\dots,x_{d(G)}\rangle=G.

The purpose of this work is to provide a comprehensive answer to a question proposed by Peter J. Cameron, Aparna Lakshmanan S and Midhuna V Ajith
[
5
, Question 2]
, which, using the previous definition, can be reformulated as follows:
which finite groups satisfy the MGSE property?

Clearly every finite cyclic group satisfies the MGSE property, so we may restrict our attention to non-cyclic finite groups. A partial answer is given in
[
11
]
, where a complete classification of the finite solvable groups
G
with the MGSE property is obtained. More precisely, if
G
is nilpotent and non-cyclic then
G
satisfies the MGSE property if and only if
G
is a finite
p
-group
[
11
, Corollary 4.6]
. On the other hand, if
G
is solvable but not nilpotent, then
G
satisfies the MGSE property if and only if its quotient over the Frattini subgroup is isomorphic to a semidirect product
N^{\delta}\rtimes H,
where
\delta
is a positive integer,
H
is cyclic of prime order,
N
is an irreducible
H
-module and
C_{H}(V)=1

[
11
, Theorem 4.13]
.

In this paper, we complete the classification of the finite groups with the MGSE property, proving the following result already conjectured in
[
11
]
.

Theorem 1.2
.

If
G
has the MGSE property, then
G
is solvable.

The proof of the previous theorem strongly depends on a somewhat technical result, Lemma
2.4
, concerning the generation properties of finite monolithic groups with non-abelian socle. The proof of Lemma
2.4
is obtained by inserting some modifications to the proof of the fundamental theorem in
[
13
]
, which asserts that if the socle
N
of a monolithic group
G
is non-abelian, then for every
d\geq 2
and every subset
\{g_{1},...,g_{d}\}
such that
G=N\langle g_{1},...,g_{d}\rangle
, there exist
n_{1},\dots,n_{d}
in
N
such that
G=\langle g_{1}n_{1},\dots,g_{d}n_{d}\rangle.
The proof contained in
[
13
]
is constructive and highlights a strong degree of freedom in the choice of the elements
n_{1},...,n_{d}.
The idea of Lemma
2.4
is that this freedom can be used to prove that there exists a suitable maximal subgroup
M
of
G
with the property that for each
1\leq j\leq d,
one can choose
n_{1},...,n_{d}
such that all elements
g_{i}n_{i}
belong to
M
except for
g_{j}n_{j}.
The modifications we need to introduce to the proof present in
[
13
]
to obtain Lemma
2.4
are not many, and much of that proof works without any changes. However, it is a long and complex proof, so we thought it appropriate to prove Lemma
2.4
in all its details, even at the cost of repeating many of the arguments in
[
13
]
.

In order to prove Theorem
1.2
, we showed that given a generating set
\{\bar{g}_{1},\dots,\bar{g}_{d}\}
of
G/N
, we can choose preimages
g_{i}
of
\bar{g}_{i}
such that

\langle g_{1},\dots,g_{d}\rangle=G,

while
g_{2},\dots,g_{d}
all lie in a prescribed maximal subgroup of
G
.
It is hence a natural question to ask whether, given a group
G
and a maximal subgroup
M
, we can always find a generating set of
G
of minimal size such that a certain number of the elements are contained in
M
.

For a subgroup
H\leq G
, let

\mathscr{D}_{H}(G)=\max_{\langle g_{1},\dots,g_{d(G)}\rangle=G}|\{g_{i}:g_{i}\in H\}|,

and

\mathscr{D}(G)=\min_{M<_{\max}G}\mathscr{D}_{M}(G).

We follow the terminology given in
[
3
, Definition 1.1.8]
.
A primitive group
G
is said to be

(1)

a primitive group of type 1 if
G
has an abelian minimal normal subgroup,

(2)

a primitive group of type 2 if
G
has a unique non-abelian minimal normal
subgroup,

(3)

a primitive group of type 3 if
G
has two distinct non-abelian minimal
normal subgroups.

Let
M
be a maximal subgroup of a group
G.
Then
M/M_{G}
is
a core-free maximal subgroup of the quotient group
G/M_{G}.
Then
M
is said to
be

(1)

a maximal subgroup of type 1 if
G/M_{G}
is a primitive group of type 1,

(2)

a maximal subgroup of type 2 if
G/M_{G}
is a primitive group of type 2,

(3)

a maximal subgroup of type 3 if
G/M_{G}
is a primitive group of type 3.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
M
be a maximal subgroup of a finite group
G.

Then
\mathscr{D}_{M}(G)\geq d(G)-2.
Moreover
\mathscr{D}_{M}(G)=d(G)-1
if one of the following holds:

(1)

M
is of type 1;

(2)

M
is of type 3;

(3)

d(G)>d(G/M_{G}),

(4)

G/N
is cyclic, if
N/M_{G}=\operatorname{soc}(G/M_{G}).

Corollary 1.4
.

For every finite group
G,

\mathscr{D}(G)\geq d(G)-2
. Moreover
\mathscr{D}(G)=d(G)-1
if
G
is solvable.

We do not know of any examples of a maximal subgroup
M
of a finite group
G
such that
\mathscr{D}_{M}(G)=d(G)-2.
This leads to proposing the conjecture that
\mathscr{D}(G)=d(G)-1
for every finite group
G.

2.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

Our first observation towards proving Theorem
1.2
is the following.

Lemma 2.1
.

If
G
has the MGSE property, then so does
G/N
for any
N\lhd G
.

Proof.

See
[
11
, Lemma 4.4]
.
∎

Since
G
is non-solvable, there exists a normal subgroup
K
of
G
such that
G/K
is a monolithic group with non-abelian socle. Hence, by Lemma
2.1
, we can assume for the remainder of this proof that
G
is a monolithic group with socle
N=S^{n}
for some non-abelian simple group
S
.

Thus, we will proceed with some results and definitions specific to monolithic groups with non-abelian socle.

As
N
is the unique minimal normal subgroup of
G
, we must have
C_{G}(N)=1
. As such,

G=N_{G}(N)=\frac{N_{G}(N)}{C_{G}(N)}\leq\operatorname{Aut}(S^{n})=\operatorname{Aut}(S)\wr S_{n}.

Hence,
G
is a subgroup of
\operatorname{Aut}(S)\wr S_{n}
containing
S^{n}
. Additionally, if the map
\pi\colon\operatorname{Aut}(S)\wr S_{n}\to S_{n}
is the natural projection map, then
G\pi
must be a transitive subgroup of
S_{n}
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
S
be a non-abelian simple group, and
K
an almost simple group with socle
S
. We say a subgroup
H
of
K
is flexible if it has the following three properties:

•

H\cap S\neq S
;

•

HS=K
; and

•

There exists a prime
r
such that for every
h\in H
there exists an
s\in H\cap S
such that
|h|_{r}\neq|hs|_{r}
.

An important fact is that for any
S\leq K\leq\operatorname{Aut}(S)
, there always exists a subgroup
H<K
which is flexible, by
[
14
, Lemma 6]
. The existence of these subgroups is important in our proof of Theorem
1.2
, as they allow us the freedom we require in order to provide suitable preimages of a generating set of
G/N
. To show this, we must first obtain a suitable subgroup
\tilde{H}
of
G
which inherits these qualities from
H
.

We will let
S_{i}
denote the subgroup of
S^{n}
consisting of all elements
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{j})
such that
x_{j}=1
for all
j\neq i
. Then the preimage
\pi^{-1}\left(\operatorname{Stab}_{S_{n}}(1)\right)
has a normal subgroup given by

(\operatorname{Aut}(S_{2})\times\cdots\times\operatorname{Aut}(S_{n}):S_{n-1})\cap G.

Let

K_{1}:=\frac{\pi^{-1}\left(\operatorname{Stab}_{S_{n}}(1)\right)}{(\operatorname{Aut}(S_{2})\times\cdots\times\operatorname{Aut}(S_{n}):S_{n-1})\cap G}.

and note we can view
K_{1}
as a subgroup of
\operatorname{Aut}(S_{1})
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
G
be a monolithic group with non-abelian socle, and define
K_{1}
as above. By the Embedding Theorem, see
[
2
, Theorem 3.3]
, there is an injective homomorphism
\phi\colon G\to K_{1}\wr S_{n}
. Then, for any flexible
H\leq K_{1}
, we let

\tilde{H}:=\phi^{-1}\left(\{(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})\sigma\in G:\alpha_{i}\in H\}\right).

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
G
be a monolithic group with non-abelian socle
N
, and take
g_{1},\dots,g_{d(G)}\in G
such that

\langle g_{1},\dots,g_{d(G)}\rangle N=G/N.

If one of the following holds:

•

g_{1}\pi
has a fixed point,

•

g_{2}\pi
has a fixed point, or

•

(g_{1}^{-1}g_{2}^{i})\pi
and
(g_{2}^{-1}g_{1}^{i})\pi
are fixed point free for all
i
,

then there exist
v_{1},v_{2}\in N
such that

\langle v_{1}g_{1},v_{2}g_{2},g_{3}\dots,g_{d(G)}\rangle=G.

Moreover, for any flexible subgroup
H\leq K_{1}
, we can choose
v_{1}
such that
g_{1}v_{1}\in\tilde{H}
, with
\tilde{H}
as defined in Definition
2.3
.

If we assume the statement of this lemma, then we may prove the main theorem of this paper.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let
G
be a non-solvable group, and assume
G
has the MGSE property. By Lemma
2.1
, we can assume that
G
is a monolithic group with socle
N=S^{n}
, for some non-abelian simple group
S
.

Let
J\leq\operatorname{Aut}(S)
be a subgroup as given in
[
14
, Lemma 6]
, which is flexible in
\operatorname{Aut}(S)
. Thus,
H:=J\cap K_{1}
is flexible in
K_{1}
. Hence, we may define
\tilde{H}
as in Definition
2.3
.

Let
d:=d(G)
, and choose
g_{1},\dots,g_{d}\in\tilde{H}
such that

\langle g_{1},\dots,g_{d}\rangle N=G/N.

This is possible since
HS=K_{1}
, which implies that
\tilde{H}N=G
.

By
[
13
]
,
d(G)=\max(2,d(G/N))
. If
d(G/N)=1
, then we choose
g_{1}
such that
g_{1}N
is a generator of
G/N
, and
g_{2}=1
. Hence, we have
\langle g_{2},\dots,g_{d}\rangle N<G
. On the other hand, if
d(G/N)>1
, then

, and so we also have
\langle g_{2},\dots,g_{d}\rangle N<G
.

In order to apply Lemma
2.4
, we must satisfy one of the three conditions in the statement. Suppose that
(g_{1}^{-1}g_{2}^{i})\pi
has a fixed point for some
i
. Then, we can instead consider the generating set

\langle g_{1}^{-1}g_{2}^{i},g_{2},\dots,g_{d}\rangle N=G/N,

and proceed with applying Lemma
2.4
now. Note,
g_{1}^{-1}g_{2}^{i}\in\tilde{H}
still. We can similarly replace
g_{2}
by
g_{2}^{-1}g_{1}^{i}
instead if this element has a fixed point for some
i
.

Additionally, we still have the property that
\langle g_{2},\dots,g_{d}\rangle N<G
. If
d\geq 2
then this is clear by
d(G)=\max(2,d(G/N))
. Otherwise, if
d(G/N)=1
, then
g_{1}\pi
must be an
n
-cycle. Hence,
g_{1}^{-1}g_{2}^{i}
, is always fixed-point free, and
g_{2}^{-1}g_{1}^{i}
is fixed point free, unless it is equal to 1.

Hence, we can assume we have chosen
g_{1},\dots,g_{d}\in\tilde{H}
such that

\langle g_{1},\dots,g_{d}\rangle N=G,

with
g_{1},g_{2}
satisfying one of the conditions of Lemma
2.4
, and
\langle g_{2},\dots,g_{d}\rangle N<G
.

Thus, we can find
v_{1},v_{2}\in N
and
w_{1},w_{2}\in N
such that

\langle v_{1}g_{1},v_{2}g_{2},g_{3},\dots,g_{d}\rangle=\langle w_{1}g_{1},w_{2}g_{2},g_{3},\dots,g_{d}\rangle=G,

and
v_{2}g_{2},w_{1}g_{1}\in\tilde{H}
. Since
G
has the MGSE property, there exists a
j
such that

\langle w_{j}g_{j},v_{2}g_{2},g_{3},\dots,g_{d}\rangle=G,

where
w_{j}:=1
for
j\geq 3
. If
j>1
, then this implies that
\langle g_{2},\dots,g_{d}\rangle N=G/N
, contradicting our choice of
g_{i}
above. If
j=1
instead, then

\langle w_{1}g_{1},v_{2}g_{2},g_{3},\dots,g_{d}\rangle\leq\tilde{H}<G,

again giving a contradiction. Hence,
G
cannot have the MGSE property.
∎

For the proof of Lemma

, the following definition is useful.

.

Let

be a prime, and
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in S_{n}

We will now prove Lemma

. As mentioned in the introduction, a version of this lemma without the extra condition that

is proved in

. The proof of Lemma

to ensure

.

Let

Define

Note,

We similarly write

.

3.
Generators and maximal subgroups

In order to prove Theorem

, we proved we can always find a generating set

of

with
g_{2},\dots,g_{d(G)}
contained in a particular proper subgroup of

of a finite group

Definition 3.1
.

For a subgroup
H\leq G
, let

\mathscr{D}_{H}(G)=\max_{\langle g_{1},\dots,g_{d(G)}\rangle=G}|\{g_{i}:g_{i}\in H\}|,

and

\mathscr{D}(G)=\min_{M<_{\max}G}\mathscr{D}_{M}(G).

Hence, we would like to bound
\mathscr{D}(G)
for any finite group
G
. To do so, we first recall the terminology given in
[
3
, Definition 1.1.8]
. A primitive group
G
is said to be

(1)

a primitive group of type 1 if
G
has an abelian minimal normal subgroup,

(2)

a primitive group of type 2 if
G
has a unique non-abelian minimal normal
subgroup,

(3)

a primitive group of type 3 if
G
has two distinct non-abelian minimal
normal subgroups.

Let
M
be a maximal subgroup of a group
G.
Then
M/M_{G}
is
a core-free maximal subgroup of the quotient group
G/M_{G}.
Then
M
is said to
be

(1)

a maximal subgroup of type 1 if
G/M_{G}
is a primitive group of type 1,

(2)

a maximal subgroup of type 2 if
G/M_{G}
is a primitive group of type 2,

(3)

a maximal subgroup of type 3 if
G/M_{G}
is a primitive group of type 3.

We treat each type of maximal subgroup
M
individually in the following propositions.

Proposition 3.2
.

If
M
is a maximal subgroup of a finite group

of type 3, then
\mathcal{D}_{M}(G)=d(G)-1.

Proof.

It is not restrictive to assume
M_{G}=1
. Then there exists a monolithic primitive group

with the subgroup of

Assume

S

Corollary 3.7
.

If
M
is a maximal subgroup of a finite group
G
of type 2 such that
d(G)>d(G/M_{G})
, then

\mathscr{D}_{M}(G)=d(G)-1.

In order to consider whether it’s possible to improve the bound in Proposition
3.4
, we must first list the possibilities for
M
. In the proceeding arguments, assume
G
is a monolithic group, and
M<_{\max}G
a core-free maximal subgroup of
G
. Let
S
be the non-abelian simple group such that
S^{n}\leq G\leq\operatorname{Aut}(S)\wr S_{n}
, and
\pi\colon G\to S_{n}
the natural projection map. By the maximality of
M
, we must have
M=(M\cap S^{n}).\frac{G}{S^{n}}
. Additionally, since
S
is a non-abelian simple group, we have the following possibilities:

(1)

M\cap S^{n}
is conjugate to
(R\cap S)^{n}
, where
R<_{\max}K_{1}
, with
K_{1}
defined as in the remark above Definition

;

(2)

M\cap S^{n}=\prod_{B\in\Phi}D_{B}
for a partition
\Phi
of
\{1,\dots,n\}
, and
D_{B}
a full diagonal subgroup on
\prod_{i\in B}S
;

(3)

M\cap S^{n}=1
.

If
M\cap S^{n}
is conjugate to
(R\cap S)^{n}
, and
R<K_{1}
is a flexible subgroup of
K_{1}
, then we can apply Lemma
2.4
as in Proposition

above to show that

\mathscr{D}_{M}(G)=d(G)-1.

We now state the following propositions in order to illustrate that there are many cases where
R
is flexible, although this isn’t always the case.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
S=A_{n}
, with

, and

. Then every maximal subgroup

Proof.

We can use the O’Nan-Scott classification to obtain a description of all possible

A_{n}\nleq R<_{\max}K

R
is almost simple, with

[

, this implies that

∎

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
S=A_{6}
. Then there exists a maximal subgroup
R
of
\operatorname{Aut}(S)
, not containing
S

Proof.

Using Magma, we can find two maximal subgroups

The first is a maximal subgroup of size 40, where

On the other hand, if

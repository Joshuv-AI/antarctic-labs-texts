Revelations of pancreatic cancer treated by high-intensity focused ultrasound
Authors: Wen Zhou, Cheng Lei, Xiaojing Xue, Hongfan Yu, Jian Li, Wenxi Li, Shizhu Li, Wei Xu, Min Li, Jing Li, Qiulan Zheng, Miao Huang, Yuxian Nie, Jin Bai, Gang Feng, Xiuni Gan, Qiuling Shi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPancreatic cancer remains among the most treatment-refractory solid malignancies worldwide. High-intensity focused ultrasound (HIFU) surgery is a treatment option with the advantage of providing cancer pain relief. However, there has been limited research addressing the trends in pancreatic cancer treatment utilizing HIFU surgery.MethodsPublications on pancreatic adenocarcinoma treatment utilizing HIFU surgery in the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC) were identified and analyzed by visualization software VOSviewer and CiteSpace. This approach provides comprehensive insights into the knowledge structure of the field.Results A total of 1172 articles published from 1999 to 2024 were identified in the WoSCC, showing a consistent rise in the publication volume and average annual citation frequency related to pancreatic adenocarcinoma treatment utilizing HIFU surgery. The United States and China contributed the most publications. There were six clusters summarized including descriptions of clinical trials of pancreatic cancer, basic research, mechanisms of HIFU surgery, combined therapy effect of pancreatic cancer, attenuated symptom of pancreatic cancer, and metastatic pancreatic cancer.ConclusionsBased on this bibliometric analysis of the publications over the last twenty years, it is the first scholarly visualization in the domain of pancreatic cancer treated with HIFU surgery. The clinical research regarding the efficacy of this technology should be developed in the future. It provides clinicians and researchers with a reliable synopsis, changes, and evolution regarding the field, offering a reference point for existing research and suggesting new avenues for future investigations.

Introduction

Pancreatic cancer remains among the most treatment-refractory solid malignancies, with conventional polychemotherapy regimens achieving only modest survival improvements [1]. The prognosis for this disease is grim, characterized by a 5-year survival rate of less than 10% and a median survival time of under 6 months from diagnosis [2]. Currently, prevention or early diagnosis at a stage amenable to curative intervention is exceedingly challenging [3]. Patients rarely exhibit symptoms and tumours do not display sensitive and specific markers to aid detection [4]. Patients diagnosed with pancreatic cancer frequently endure significant pain due to perineural invasion [5], with more than 40% experiencing abdominal pain [6]. Several studies have highlighted the relationship between cancer-related pain and quality of life (QoL) [7, 8]. While there are some non-pharmacological approaches available for pain management, their effectiveness in alleviating the cancer-associated pain experienced by pancreatic cancer patients is limited [9].

High-intensity focused ultrasound (HIFU) surgery is a form of analgesia recognized by various guidelines and consensus statements [10, 11]. HIFU represents a minimally invasive procedure that utilizes a piezoelectric transducer, which is coupled to the body and directed at a specific target region without any incisions. The energy from the focused ultrasound waves is converted into heat with high energy density within the targeted area, resulting in a rapid temperature increase to approximately 60 °C, leading to protein coagulative necrosis [12]. The acoustic pressure generated during this process induces the formation of air cavities that facilitate tissue ablation, contributing to acoustic cavitation during treatment [13]. Furthermore, this ablation procedure can be repeated using an established acoustic pathway [14].

However, there has been limited research addressing the trends in pancreatic cancer treatment utilizing HIFU. One contributing factor to this gap is the complexity of research perspectives presented across various publications, compounded by delays in textbook updates due to the necessity for validation of conclusions drawn from diverse studies. Consequently, this study employs bibliometric citation analysis as our preferred methodology, a widely recognized approach for mapping and describing performance within specific fields. Bibliometrics serves as a quantitative method that statistically analyzes studies to assess the impact of institutions, countries, and researchers. This approach can forecast developments within particular fields based on publication volume and citation frequency [15–17]. The findings from published studies have significantly contributed to advancing specific areas of research [18]. Considering the importance of the topic and the superiority of the bibliometric analysis method, this study aims to identify and analyze global research trends and emerging hotspots in the application of HIFU for pancreatic cancer to provide a comprehensive foundation for future clinical and scientific efforts. Thus, we analyzed the publications that fit the theme to help researchers outline the hotspots and trends in the past and future, which can provide a general understanding of the evolution in the pancreatic cancer research field, as well as meaningful revelations for future research to advance the development of HIFU clinical research.

Methods

Data collection

A systematic literature search was conducted in the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC) database. The timeframe was limited from inception to 31 December 2024. The subject search strategy was conducted as follows: (TS=(“pancreatic neoplasms”) OR TS=(“Pancreatic Neoplasm”) OR TS=(“Pancreas Neoplasm”) OR TS=(“pancreas carcinoma”) OR TS=(“Pancreatic carcinoma”) OR TS=(“pancreatic tumor”) OR TS=(“pancreas tumor”) OR TS=(“pancreatic cancer”) OR TS=(“pancreas cancer”)) AND (TS=(“High-Intensity Focused Ultrasound Ablation”) OR TS=(“high intensity focused ultrasound”) OR TS=(“High-intensity focused ultrasound”) OR TS=(“HIFU”) OR TS=(“focused ultrasound”) OR TS=(“ultrasound therapy”) OR TS=(“hyperthermia-induced”) OR TS=(“Diathermy”) OR TS=(“Ablation Techniques”) OR TS=(“Ablation”)). The search was limited to publications in English and those on the topic. A total of 1174 studies were retrieved. After excluding documents classified as "correction" (n = 2), 1172 studies were included for the final analysis. The full record and cited references were exported and saved as a plain text file. Except for this exclusion, other literature types were not limited to avoid information loss.

Data analysis and visualization

All bibliographic information of the selected publications was converted and analyzed using VOSviewer and CiteSpace. VOSviewer (Version 1.6.20, Leiden University, van Eck NJ and Waltman L) is a scientometrics network analysis software developed by the Center for Scientific and Technological Research, Leiden University, the Netherlands. VOSviewer can provide three kinds of visualization maps: network maps, overlay maps and density maps. CiteSpace (Version 6.2.R4, Drexel University, Chao-mei Chen) is a visualization bibliometric tool developed by Dr. Chao-mei Chen that can help scholars understand the development trends in specific research fields more intuitively and comprehensively. All information and data for each publication were inserted into a spreadsheet and manipulated using Microsoft Excel 2016. SCImago and Pajek were used to adjust the layout. Bibliometrix in RStudio was used to show the trending topics. The flowchart is shown in Fig. 1.

Results

Analysis of publications and time trends

In this study, the number of articles related to pancreatic cancer treated by HIFU has increased steadily and then decreased slightly in the past twenty-five years, from 4 publications in 1999 to 80 publications in 2024. Figure 2A shows the number of annual and total publications. The total number of publications showed an exponential growth trend. The publication number from 2014 to 2024 exceeded the exponential trend. There were three obvious increases from 2009 to 2010 (from 14 publications to 33), from 2015 to 2016 (from 63 publications to 85), and from 2020 to 2021 (from 89 publications to 118).The largest number of articles was published in 2021 (n =118 ). The percent visualization of literature type is shown in Fig. 2B. We noticed the articles, reviews, meeting abstracts, other types (including letters, editorial materials and so on) respectively accounted for 71.25%, 21.62%, 4.18%, and 3.16%.

Keywords

Keywords reflect the research hotspots and directions, and keywords analysis helps predict future research trends. A total of 3557 keywords were calculated by VOSviewer. We showed 362 of them with more than five occurrences in Fig. 8. There were six clusters summarized (Fig. 8A and B), including descriptions of clinical trials of pancreatic cancer, basic research, mechanisms of HIFU, combined therapy effect of pancreatic cancer, attenuated symptom of pancreatic cancer, and metastatic pancreatic cancer, as shown in Figs. 8 and 9. We also analyzed the keyword density and time trends regarding pancreatic cancer treated by HIFU. Keywords related to treatment efficacy also appeared several times, such as “safety” and “survival”. We also noticed several keywords regarding basic mechanism such as “cells” and “expression”.

As Fig. 8C shown, we analyzed the keyword citation trends in the field of pancreatic cancer treatment from 1999 to 2024, and revealed the research hotspots of HIFU in the treatment of pancreatic cancer, including “tissue ablation” (2012–2017, intensity 7.74) and “tumor ablation” (2014–2018, intensity 7.63). The keywords “locally advanced pancreatic cancer” (2018–2022, intensity 7.95) and “percutaneous irreversible electroporation” (2018–2021, intensity 6.54) also attracted attention. According to the analysis, “safety” (2016–2020, the intensity of 6.18) emaged as an important concer into the medium-term. More recent studies (2018–2024) focused on specific disease types and treatments, with significant interest in “FOLFIRINOX” (2019–2024, 5.31 intensity) and “immunotherapy” (2020–2022, 6.74 intensity), which indicated the increasing importance of these therapies in the treatment of pancreatic cancer. Research trends in Fig. 8D showed that studies focused on basic pathological features and traditional treatment of pancreatic cancer in the early stage, and then turned to specific treatment techniques in the middle stage, and focused on specific treatment methods such as “immunotherapy” and “FOLFIRINOX” in the recent stage. This evolution reflected the continuous search in the field of pancreatic cancer treatment to improve the efficacy and safety of treatment.

Discussion

It is challenging to summarize the evolution of research trends in pancreatic cancer treated by HIFU. Although HIFU has been widely implemented for benign gynecological tumors over the past two decades, its application in malignant tumor management remains limited. While HIFU has been incorporated into the National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) pain guidelines and recognized as one of the recommended local analgesic treatments, patients with pancreatic cancer face a survival crisis due to various symptom burdens. Consequently, the therapeutic efficacy of HIFU for this patient population requires more comprehensive investigation. At present, HIFU is not currently endorsed by primary treatment guidelines for pancreatic cancer. In this study, we aimed to visualize and analyze the current publications on pancreatic cancer treated with HIFU through bibliometric methods. The strong correlation between the frequency of publications on pancreatic cancer (598 occurrences) and those on HIFU (238 occurrences) suggests that HIFU has emerged as a significant research hotspot in this field.

Based on the temporal evolution, particularly highlighting the unexpected growth observed after 2014, it appears that HIFU technology for pancreatic cancer has transitioned from initial laboratory exploration (1999–2010) to a significant increase in clinical applications of various micro-non-invasive techniques post-2016. Following Dr. Wu’s pioneering investigation into advanced pancreatic cancer using HIFU in 2005 [19], the field of micro-non-invasive surgical treatment for this malignancy has continued to progress. However, following a peak in publications in 2021, there has been a slight decline in subsequent years. This decrease may be attributed to the fact that HIFU remains inadequately validated as an effective treatment modality for pancreatic cancer. Given the inherent characteristics of pancreatic cancer, current therapeutic strategies continue to focus on prolonging overall patient survival through targeted drug delivery and immunotherapy approaches [20]. The declining publication trend may reflect a pivot within the research community towards investigating HIFU not as a standalone monotherapy, but as a combinatorial modality within multi-modal treatment frameworks. Future research is likely to focus on how HIFU-induced ablation can synergize with chemotherapeutic drug delivery (e.g., by enhancing vascular permeability) or modulate the tumor microenvironment to potentiate immunotherapy. It is also prudent to consider the potential impact of the COVID-19 pandemic.

In the context of a borderless scientific landscape and the increasing trend of globalization, most publications included in this study were produced through collaboration among research institutions across various countries or regions. China and the United States exhibit a high volume of publications along with close cooperation, while the United Kingdom and the Netherlands demonstrate significant citation impact despite having a limited number of publications. This may be attributed to their early advancements in the development and application of relevant technologies [21–24]. These nations have investigated diverse equipment guidance systems, including ultrasound-guided and magnetic resonance imaging-guided HIFU equipment [25]. However, the application of HIFU surgery for malignant tumors remains at an exploratory stage [26]. A larger proportion of publications from high-output countries may consist of smaller-scale case series, single-center experiences, or technical reports, which are crucial for initial exploration but have inherently lower citation potential. The high output reflects their role as a major driver of clinical exploration. To translate this volume into global influence, the research focus must strategically shift from expanding application scale to leading high-quality Phase III trials. Leveraging the vast clinical database for well-designed, multi-center studies with long-term follow-up is the imperative next step to elevate the international citation impact and, more importantly, to shape the global standard of HIFU for pancreatic cancer.

Pre-clinical studies on the application of HIFU in the treatment of pancreatic cancer are experiencing significant growth, as evidenced by recent reviews [26]. The prevalence of high-frequency terms such as “expression” (85 occurrences) and “adenocarcinoma” (115 occurrences) further underscores this trend. Investigations into molecular mechanisms have demonstrated that HIFU can modulate critical pathways within the tumor microenvironment through thermal and mechanical effects, thereby providing a theoretical foundation for precise ablation [27, 28]. This approach offers a potential new strategy to overcome the “immune desert” characteristics associated with pancreatic cancer [29]. Research has confirmed that combining HIFU with CD133-targeted therapy is both effective and efficient in enhancing antitumor efficacy. Furthermore, targeted nanovesicles used in conjunction with HIFU may represent an innovative and promising therapeutic avenue for clinical applications in pancreatic cancer [20]. Looking ahead, we aim to investigate the efficacy of HIFU as an adjuvant therapy when combined with other treatment modalities, while also exploring whether HIFU can induce synergistic enhancement effects.

Several clinical studies have reported on the effects of HIFU in the treatment of pancreatic cancer, focusing on key outcomes such as survival time, adverse reactions, and tumor response. As illustrated by the important keyword “effect”, Dr. Sofuni et al. [30] presented findings from a cohort of 176 patients treated with HIFU, revealing a primary disease control rate of 72.2%. Dr. Ran et al. [31] noted that the median survival times for two groups were significantly different: patients receiving pharmacogenomic-guided chemotherapy following HIFU treatment had a median survival time of 14 months compared to just 5 months for patients undergoing traditional chemotherapy after HIFU surgery. Importantly, no severe complications (≥ grade 3) associated with HIFU were observed during these studies. While there is an increased risk of skin burns related to this procedure, HIFU may be particularly advantageous for pancreatic cancer patients who exhibit poor functional status and significant symptom burden that precludes them from tolerating systemic treatments like chemotherapy due to its hematological toxicity. Furthermore, the potential survival benefits conferred by HIFU alone require validation through high-quality randomized controlled trials (RCTs). Our review indicates that publications from leading clinical medicine journals have been frequently cited regarding this topic (Table 5) [32, 33]. However, there remains a scarcity of high-quality clinical outcome data specifically addressing pancreatic cancer treated with HIFU within these journals. It is imperative to conduct rigorous Phase III clinical trials to elucidate the survival advantages offered by surgical intervention using HIFU for patients suffering from pancreatic cancer. To date, we are aware of only one ongoing RCT (DRKS00012367) comparing standard chemotherapy alone against standard chemotherapy combined with local HIFU ablation in individuals diagnosed with unresectable pancreatic cancer. This trial is currently being conducted at the University Hospital Bonn’s HIFU center in Germany.

One possible explanation for the challenges associated with Phase III clinical trials is that HIFU surgery constitutes a treatment system characterized by numerous mechanical parameters. The integration of such medical devices into clinical practice may impose stringent requirements on the physicians performing HIFU procedures, necessitating not only a comprehensive understanding of specialized disease knowledge but also an in-depth familiarity with the device’s performance. A comparable scenario can be observed in robotic-assisted surgical procedures. The learning curve experienced by robotic thoracic surgeons has been documented in several studies [34–36]. These publications indicate that as surgical experience accumulates, outcomes progressively improve—evidenced by reductions in operative time, lower complication rates, and an increase in lymph node dissections. This trend reflects both the learnability and advancement of robotic surgical technology [37]. The aforementioned conclusions may similarly apply to physicians engaged in HIFU surgery. However, further data validation is required to substantiate this hypothesis.

Another significant aspect is the clinical management of HIFU surgery. In our research, the keyword “management” has demonstrated considerable importance, as illustrated in Figs. 7 and 8. Figure 8 reveals a minimal correlation between HIFU nodes and clinically related terms such as “symptom management” and “disease progression.” The fact that HIFU surgery can relieve cancer pain has been substantiated according to the adult cancer pain guidelines [11]. Several publications addressing pain relief among patients with bone metastases indicate that more than half experienced an improvement in self-reported pain scores without an increase in pain medication three months post-treatment [38]. Some studies have indicated that HIFU can significantly alleviate pain in pancreatic cancer patients, as noted in expert consensus reports [31, 39–41]. Nevertheless, relevant research has yet to be incorporated into the NCCN pancreatic cancer guidelines [42]. These findings suggest that further exploration into the management of pancreatic cancer patients following HIFU treatment is warranted. Further exploration into the efficacy of HIFU treatment for pancreatic cancer is necessary to support higher-level evidence for effective pain and other symptom management. Furthermore, there appears to be a notable lack of relevant nursing research [43], which implies that nurses’ roles during HIFU procedures may not be clearly defined. Additionally, there is a need for further standardization of perioperative nursing protocols for HIFU procedures.

The bottleneck in the management of perioperative care for HIFU has led to a limited number of Patient-Reported Outcome (PRO) studies concerning HIFU-based treatments for pancreatic cancer. Another potential explanation is the heightened burden of physical and psychological symptoms, along with poorer functional status among patients with pancreatic cancer themselves [44, 45]. There is a pressing need to accurately and comprehensively document patients’ self-reported symptoms and their quality of life experiences without imposing additional burdens on these individuals. This necessitates higher standards for the psychometric properties and clinical efficacy of PRO tools [46–48]. An effective PRO tool should genuinely reflect the symptom burden experienced by patients. Concurrently, successful PRO implementation requires collaboration from the clinical team to proactively address and alleviate high-burden symptoms. By managing these symptoms effectively, we can enhance patient engagement in completing PRO assessment, thereby ensuring that subsequent responses are thorough and accurate, ultimately creating a robust management feedback loop. This management approach has demonstrated its effectiveness in perioperative lung cancer patients, significantly reducing complication rates [49, 50]. Additionally, some studies have highlighted issues related to non-reporting of PRO trial results as well as delays and inconsistencies in reporting standards within published PRO data [51, 52]. Therefore, there is an urgent need for further clarification and discussion regarding suitable PRO tools for pancreatic cancer patients receiving HIFU surgery; optimal frequencies for acquiring PRO data; intelligent platforms for providing feedback on PROs; appropriate parameters for early warning systems related to PROs; efficient processes for clinical handling of PROs; as well as treatment strategies and nursing plans that can genuinely mitigate the symptom burden faced by patients.

Nearly 70% of pancreatic cancer patients are diagnosed at intermediate or advanced stages, which implies that most individuals confront a “tumor-bearing survival” scenario for the remainder of their lives [31]. Researchers need to further elucidate the impact of HIFU surgery on enhancing the QoL for patients with pancreatic cancer, as assessed through PRO. When faced with a diagnosis often referred to as “the king of cancers,” how can patients effectively recalibrate their physiological and psychological functions? Furthermore, what measures can be taken to ensure an improved QoL for both themselves and their families during this challenging period? How can we better define patient well-being as they enter the countdown toward death? The aforementioned questions regarding quality of life remain unanswered. This highlights the pressing need for comprehensive assessments of QoL and psychological states in these patients. Accurate measurement of QoL among pancreatic cancer patients post-HIFU surgery, along with identifying specific aspects where HIFU may enhance QoL, warrants further investigation by sociologists, clinicians, and clinical nurses.

Researchers still need to ascertain the cost-effectiveness and economic benefits of HIFU surgery for patients with pancreatic cancer. Establishing these factors may further enhance the acceptance of HIFU surgery at both clinical and healthcare policy levels across various countries. Several studies have confirmed the influence of economic benefits on policy decisions [53]. Dr. Shinohara et al. conducted a comparative analysis of the cost-effectiveness between gemcitabine combined with nab-paclitaxel and modified FOLFIRINOX, highlighting potential advantages in safety [54]. However, similar investigations within the realm of HIFU surgery remain scarce. To our knowledge, there is no insurance policy regarding HIFU surgery in pancreatic cancer patients. Only two studies explored the cost-utility analysis regarding HIFU surgery in patients with prostate cancer and bone metastase, but the conclusions are not clear enough [55, 56]. When HIFU surgery is shown to extend survival time, alleviate symptom burden, and improve quality of life without significantly increasing patients’ economic burdens, it can yield substantial benefits for insurance providers or national economies. Such outcomes will play a crucial role in promoting and advancing HIFU surgery.

There are several limitations in our research. First and foremost, to comply with the requirements of certain bibliometric software utilized in this study, we included only literature from the WoSCC database. This restriction may introduce specific biases. Additionally, to ensure comprehensive coverage of the literature, we did not conduct a detailed screening of the included studies. While this approach allows for a better representation of research correlations within this field, it has resulted in the inclusion of some studies that may be considered irrelevant to a certain extent. Besides, we did not elaborate on the differences among various systems and different research and development institutions in this study, which may lead to the differences between systems from different institutions being overlooked, despite the fact that the fundamental principles of different HIFU systems are roughly the same. Also, we should explore other methods to summarize the quantitative effects of HIFU in relieving pain and other aspects in the future.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study visualized the publications on pancreatic cancer treated by HIFU surgery over the past twenty-five years based on bibliometric analysis methodology. We provided a comprehensive overview of the research hotspots and development trends in the field. It is the first scholarly visualization in the domain of pancreatic cancer treated with HIFU surgery. Six clusters were summarized, including descriptions of clinical trials of pancreatic cancer, basic research, mechanisms of HIFU surgery, combined therapy effect of pancreatic cancer, attenuated symptom of pancreatic cancer, and metastatic pancreatic cancer. The results showed that China and the United States made important contributions to the fields. Additionally, we noticed the clinical research regarding the standalone efficacy of the technology in the field should be developed in the future. Furthermore, the management regarding the HIFU surgery should be built. Finally, the visualization provides clinicians and researchers with a reliable synopsis, changes, and evolution regarding the pancreatic cancer field, offering a reference point for existing research and suggesting new avenues for future investigations.

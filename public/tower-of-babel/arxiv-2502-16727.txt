Thom polynomials for singularities of maps
Toru Ohmoto

Abstract
. This is a gentle introduction to a general theory of universal polynomials associated to classification of map-germs, called Thom polynomials . The theory was originated by René Thom in the 1950s and has since been evolved in various aspects by many authors. In a nutshell, this is about intersection theory on certain moduli spaces, say ‘classifying spaces of mono/multi-singularities of maps’, which provides consistent and deep insights into both classical and modern enumerative geometry with many potential applications.



1.
Introduction

Thom polynomials
are a key component of a general enumerative theory for singularities of real and complex mappings – these are universal cohomological obstructions to the appearance of singular points of prescribed types in given mappings.
Roughly speaking, a
singularity
of a differentiable map
f
means a point where the differential
df
is degenerate (and the germ of
f
at such a point) or also a multiple image point of the map. There are a lot of different local types of singularities of maps, and the locus of those singular points carries important information about global topological nature of the source and the target spaces. Historically, the origins lie in classical enumerative geometry founded by pioneers of the 19th century, and based on it, the theory was born as a branch of singularity theory of differentiable maps in the mid-20th century. In fact, René Thom simultaneously developed singularity theory, cobordism theory, characteristic classes and cohomology operations, armed with his transversality theorem, since his doctor thesis
[
Th51
]
– especially, in 1957, the year before he received the Fields Medal, Thom gave the inaugural lecture for professorship at the University of Strasbourg, in which he manifested there is a deep correspondence from local classification theory of map-germs to global theory on topology of manifolds and maps
[
Th57
]
. This is what people later called Thom polynomial theory
1

.
Nowadays, the theory itself has been expanding to much broader fields dealing with various enumeration problems in algebraic geometry, differential topology, complex analytic geometry, representation theory, and their interactions.

Excellent surveys of the theory up to the early 1990s may be found in Kleiman
[
Kl76
,
Kl77
,
Kl87
]
from algebraic geometry side, and in the fourth chapter of
[
AGLV93
]
written by Vassiliev (Vasil’ev) from topology side. The present article aims to briefly outline subsequent developments with emphasizing new ideas –
equivariant cohomology
,
localization
and
Hilbert schemes of points
– and how those are merged with old traditional methods. As the theory still now continues to develop, this survey may include conjectural (but mostly reliable) statements and should therefore be considered an interim report at this stage. Additionally, content selection is heavily biased by the author’s interests – in particular, due to space constraints, we restrict our attention only to some basic topics in singularity theory of maps.
Consequently, there are several important topics which are not picked up or less mentioned in this survey.
For instance, we will not enter advanced topics in contemporary Schubert calculus and enumerative geometry on various moduli stacks, which are deeply connected to geometric representation theory, quantum integrable systems, mirror symmetry, Gromov-Witten and Donaldson-Thomas theories and so on.
Also about real Thom polynomials for
C^{\infty}
-mappings in differential topology, the range of related topics are quite large, e.g., from Gromov’s h-principle to low dimensional topology including smooth Poincaré conjecture, so we will only tough on a few basic matters.
For these topics not covered in this survey, readers are referred to appropriate references.

Throughout this paper,
H_{*}
stands for the Borel-Moore homology and
H^{*}
the singular cohomology, where the coefficients are integers unless specifically noted.
For complex manifolds,
H_{*}
and
H^{*}
are identified via the Poincaré duality as usual.
In algebraic geometry context, we use Chow groups
{\rm CH}_{*}
(Chow rings
{\rm CH}^{*}
for non-singular varieties) as in
[
Fu84
]
.

The
codimension
\kappa
of a map
f:M\to N
between manifolds is defined as
\kappa:=\dim N-\dim M

and also the relative dimension to be
d:=-\kappa
.

For a complex vector bundle
E\to M
, the Chern class
c_{i}(E)\in H^{2i}(M)
is assigned (
c_{0}(E)=1
,
c_{i}(E)=0
for
i>\mbox{\rm rank}\,E
).
We often use the
quotient Chern class

c_{i}=c_{i}(f)
associated to a map
f:M\to N
, which is defined
as the
i
-th Chern class of the difference bundle
f^{*}TN-TM
, i.e.,

c(f):=1+c_{1}(f)+c_{2}(f)+\cdots=\frac{1+f^{*}c_{1}(TN)+f^{*}c_{2}(TN)+\cdots}{1+c_{1}(TM)+c_{2}(TM)+\cdots}\;\;\in H^{*}(M).

We also use
\bar{c}_{i}(f):=c_{i}(TM-f^{*}TN)
, i.e.,

\bar{c}(f):=1+\bar{c}_{1}(f)+\bar{c}_{2}(f)+\cdots\;\;\mbox{with}\;\;c(f)\bar{c}(f)=1.

Note that every
\bar{c}_{i}(f)
is a polynomial in
c_{1}(f),\cdots,c_{i}(f)
, vice versa, and the dual version is
c_{i}(T^{*}M-f^{*}T^{*}N)=(-1)^{i}\bar{c}_{i}(f)
.

If
f
is proper, the pushforward
f_{*}:H^{*}(M)\to H^{*+\kappa}(N)
is defined (precisely, it is the Gysin homomorphism through the Poincaré duality; we simply denote it by
f_{*}
unless any confusion occurs; the superscript should be multiplied by
2
in complex case).
It satisfies the projection formula

f_{*}(\alpha\cdot f^{*}\beta)=f_{*}(\alpha)\cdot\beta.

Since
f_{*}
is just an additive homomorphism, the
Landweber-Novikov class
with index
I=(i_{1},i_{2},\cdots,i_{k})

s_{I}(f):=f_{*}(c^{I}(f))=f_{*}(c_{1}(f)^{i_{1}}c_{2}(f)^{i_{2}}\cdots c_{k}(f)^{i_{k}})

takes important role, especially in complex cobordism theory.
We often use the same notation
s_{I}
to mean its pullback
f^{*}s_{I}
(i.e., drop the letter
f^{*}
), for it is easily understood from the context. Also the readers should not confuse the notation with Segre classes of (virtual) vector bundles.

1.1.
Two guiding examples

As an initial guide to readers, we first present typical examples of Thom polynomials for classifications of linear maps and for non-linear map-germs.
Throughout this survey, these examples will be referred to repeatedly.

1.1.1.
Thom-Porteous formula

Consider the classification of linear maps
h:\mathbb{C}^{m}\to\mathbb{C}^{n}
under linear coordinate changes of the source and the target spaces. The orbits are determined by the rank, or equivalently, the dimension of kernel. Note that using
\dim\ker h
is better than
\mbox{\rm rank}\,h
in the sense that
\dim\ker h
does not change under the trivial suspension
h:\mathbb{C}^{m}\to\mathbb{C}^{n}\leadsto h\times id_{k}:\mathbb{C}^{m+k}\to\mathbb{C}^{n+k}
.
The corresponding Thom polynomials are described as follows.

Let
E\to M
and
F\to M
be complex vector bundles
of rank
m
and
n
, respectively, over the same base manifold
M
.
Let
\varphi:E\to F
be
a vector bundle morphism, that is a section of the vector bundle
{\rm Hom}(E,F)=E^{*}\otimes F\to M
.
We are herewith interested in the loci corresponding to singular linear maps; put

\Sigma^{k}(\varphi)=\{\;x\in M\;|\;\dim\ker\varphi_{x}=k\;\}

and define the
degeneracy locus
of
\varphi
to be the closure

\overline{\Sigma^{k}(\varphi)}=\bigcup_{s\geq k}\Sigma^{s}(\varphi).

The expected codimension of the locus is given by
kl
where
k=\dim\ker
and
l:=\dim\mbox{\rm coker}\,=n-m+k\,(=\kappa+k)
.
In particular, for a map
f:M^{m}\to N^{n}
,
we denote by
\Sigma^{k}(f)
the locus for the differential
df:TM\to f^{*}TN
, called the
first order Thom-Boardman singularity stratum
.

The
Giambelli-Thom-Porteous formula

[
Po71
,
Fu84
]
states that for suitably generic
\varphi
,
the dual to the degeneracy locus is expressed
by

(1)

{\rm Dual}\,[\overline{\Sigma^{k}(\varphi)}]\;=\;\Delta_{l^{k}}(c(F-E))\;=\;\overbrace{\left|\begin{array}[]{ccc}c_{l}&c_{l+1}&\cdots\\
c_{l-1}&c_{l}&\cdots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots\\
\end{array}\right|}^{k}

where
c_{i}
is the
i
-th component of the quotient Chern class
c(F-E)
.
The Schur function of type
l^{k}=(l,\cdots,l)
in the right hand side of (
1
)
is the
Thom polynomial
tp(\Sigma^{k})
for the type
\Sigma^{k}
.
Even for non-generic case, i.e., for an arbitrary bundle map
\varphi
, the formula is valid after replacing the left hand side of (
1
) by a certain intersection class, called the
degeneracy loci class
, see Fulton
[
Fu84
]
.
We remark that there is a well-known identity via conjugacy:

\Delta_{\lambda}(c(F-E))=\Delta_{\lambda^{\vee}}(c(E^{*}-F^{*}))

(cf.
[
Fu84
, Lem.14.5.1]
; note that the notation is inconsistent across different references).

In case that
\kappa\geq 0
and
E
is trivial, then the largest degeneracy locus represents
c_{\kappa+1}(F)
, i.e.,
the Chern class of a vector bundle itself is a sort of Thom polynomial.

There are several directions for generalization of the Thom-Porteous formula.
When replacing the group by Borel subgroups, we meet the (matrix) Schubert calculus, so this passage goes to geometric representation theory and algebraic combinatorics (§
3.1.1
and §
4.2.1
).
When one considers non-linear mappings, it goes to singularity theory of maps, see the next example.

1.1.2.
Fold and Cusp

Recall the
classical Riemann-Hurwitz formula
. Let
f:M\to N
be a surjective holomorphic map between compact nonsingular complex curves: to each point
x\in M
, we assign
\mu=\mu(f,x)
so that
f
is written by
x\mapsto x^{\mu+1}
in local coordinates centered at
x
and
f(x)
. Then it holds that

\displaystyle\int_{M}\mu(f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\deg f\cdot\chi(N)-\chi(M)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{1}(TN)\frown f_{*}[M]-c_{1}(TM)\frown[M]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{1}(f^{*}TN-TM)\frown[M]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{1}(f)\frown[M].

Here we use the fact that the Euler characteristic
\chi(M)
is the degree of the top Chern class
c_{1}(TM)
of the tangent bundle.
If
f
is generic, i.e., it has only simple branches (
\mu=1
), called the
A_{1}
-singularity
, then the formula counts the number of the singular points.
For a holomorphic map
f:M\to N
between complex surfaces, if it is appropriately generic, local singularities of
f
are classified into two types, called
fold
and
cusp
, up to local coordinate changes of
M
and
N
at the point,
whose normal forms are respectively given by

A_{1}:(x,y)\mapsto(x^{2},y),\quad A_{2}:(x,y)\mapsto(x^{3}+yx,y).

In general, for map-germs
(\mathbb{C}^{m},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{m+\kappa},0)
with
\kappa\geq 0
, the symbol
A_{\mu}
denotes the singularity type whose local algebra is isomorphic to
\mathbb{C}[x]/\langle x^{\mu+1}\rangle
, see §
2.1
.

Figure 1.
Singularities of plane-to-plane maps: fold, cusp and double-folds

The critical locus of
f
is the closure
\overline{A_{1}(f)}
, which is formed by fold points and isolated cusp points.
In his paper
[
Th55
]
, R. Thom showed (precisely saying, in real case, using the Stiefel-Whitey classes) that the cohomology class represented by the critical locus and the number of cusps are expressed by

(2)

\mbox{\rm Dual}\,[\overline{A_{1}(f)}]=c_{1}(f),\quad\mbox{\rm Dual}\,[\overline{A_{2}(f)}]=c_{1}(f)^{2}+c_{2}(f)

in
H^{*}(M)
. The former one is essentially the same as the above classical Riemann-Hurwitz formula.
In fact, the formulas (
2
) hold for any generic maps between manifolds of the same dimension
\geq 2
.
The right hand side,
tp(A_{1}):=c_{1}
and
tp(A_{2}):=c_{1}^{2}+c_{2}
, are respectively called
Thom polynomials for
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
-singularity type with
\kappa=0
. The shape of the polynomial depends only on the singularity type, and the information of the map
f
itself is only encoded in substituting
c_{i}(f)
to variables
c_{i}
of the polynomial.
In case of
\kappa=0
, more samples are:

\displaystyle tp(A_{3})

\displaystyle={c}_{1}^{3}+3{c}_{1}{c}_{2}+{c}_{3},

(3)

\displaystyle tp(A_{4})

\displaystyle={c}_{1}^{4}+6{c}_{1}^{2}{c}_{2}+2{c}_{2}^{2}+9{c}_{1}{c}_{3}+6{c}_{4},

\displaystyle tp(A_{5})

\displaystyle=c_{1}^{5}+10c_{1}^{3}c_{2}+25c_{1}^{2}c_{3}+10c_{1}c_{2}^{2}+38c_{1}c_{4}+12c_{2}c_{3}+24c_{5}

\displaystyle tp(A_{6})

\displaystyle=c_{1}^{6}+15c_{1}^{4}c_{2}+55c_{1}^{3}c_{3}+30c_{1}^{2}c_{2}^{2}+141c_{1}^{2}c_{4}+79c_{1}c_{2}c_{3}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+5c_{2}^{3}+202c_{1}c_{2}+55c_{2}c_{4}+17c_{3}^{2}+120c_{6}

\displaystyle\cdots\;\;\cdots,

and for singularity types of corank greater than one, e.g.,
I_{a,b}:(x^{a}+y^{b},xy)
,

tp(I_{2,2})=c_{2}^{2}-c_{!}c_{3},\quad tp(I_{2,3})=2c_{1}c_{2}^{2}-2c_{1}^{2}c_{3}+2c_{2}c_{3}-2c_{1}c_{4},

and so on –
a full list up to codimension
\leq 8
with
\kappa=0
can be seen in Rimányi
[
Ri01
]
.
As a catalog of various kinds of Thom polynomials, many computational results have been collected in Rimányi’s website ‘Thom polynomial Portal’
[
Ri25
]
.

We may find general formulas which contains
\kappa\geq 0
as a parameter.
The simplest Thom-Porteous formula says that the Thom polynomial for
A_{1}
-singularity (i.e., dual to the critical locus) is given by

(4)

tp(A_{1})=\Delta_{(\kappa+1)^{1}}=c_{\kappa+1}

and by a result due to Ronga
[
Ro72
]
we know

(5)

tp(A_{2})=c_{\kappa+1}^{2}+\sum_{i\geq 1}2^{i-1}c_{\kappa+1-i}c_{\kappa+1+i}

where we read
c_{j}=0
for
j<0
.
Those lead to a general theory of
Thom series
, see §
3.2
.
In particular, from (
2
), (
1.1.2
), (
4
), (
5
) and further computations, Rimányi has conjectured the
Chern basis positivity for
A_{\mu}
-singularity types
(
\kappa\geq 0
), that means that all coefficients of Chern monomials appearing in
tp(A_{\mu})
are non-negative
[
Ri01
]
. That is still open, and sounds mysterious, as the conjecture is unexpectedly related to complex hyperbolic geometry, see §
7.3
.

Let us look back again at a generic map
f:M\to N
between surfaces.
There is one more stable singularity type, which is a bi-singularity of fold types, denoted by
A_{1}^{2}=A_{1}A_{1}
and called
transverse double-folds
,
which corresponds to a
node
of the discriminant curve
D(f):=f(\overline{A_{1}(f)})
in the target space
N
.
Assume that
f
is proper, then the number of nodes of
D(f)
is half the number counted by

(6)

\mbox{\rm Dual}\,[\overline{A_{1}^{2}(f)}]=-4c_{1}(f)^{2}-2c_{2}(f)+c_{1}(f)f^{*}s_{1}(f),

see §
6.1
.
Furthermore, the Euler characteristic of
D(f)
is also universally expressed by

(7)

\chi(D(f))=\int_{N}(c_{1}(TN)s_{1}(f)+s_{01}(f)-\frac{1}{2}s_{1}(f)^{2}),

see §
6.2
.
Namely, the way of the appearance of singularities of prescribed type of maps has its own universal rule, that is dominant by local symmetry of the singularity type. Those kinds of enumerative problems are treated in the Thom polynomial theory, and indeed appear in various fields of applications.

Finally we mention old examples about fold and cusps of maps between surfaces
[
Sa82
,
SR49
]
– perhaps, R. Thom must have been familiar with these treasures of French classical geometry since Monge and Poncelet. First, consider a smooth plane curve
C
of degree
d\geq 2
. Let
M
be the point-line incidence surface (i.e., it consists of pairs
(p,l)\in C\times\mathbb{P}^{2*}
with
p\in l
) and
f:M\to N:=\mathbb{P}^{2*}
the projection, then the discriminant
D=D(f)
is just the projective dual to
C
.
A flex (inflection point) of
C
corresponds to a cusp of
D
and a double tangent line to
C
corresponds to a node of
D
. Applying (
2
) and (
6
), we recover the Plücker formula:

\deg D=d(d-1),\;\;\#{\rm Cusps}=3d(d-2),\;\;\#{\rm Nodes}=d^{4}-2d^{3}-9d^{2}+18d.

Second, suppose that we are viewing a surface
M\subset\mathbb{P}^{3}
of degree
d
from a general viewpoint. It is a map projecting
M
to a screen
N:=\mathbb{P}^{2}
and
D(f)
is the apparent contour, and then a handy calculation of (
2
) and (
6
) immediately recovers old formulas:

\deg D=d(d-1),\;\#{\rm Cusps}=d(d-1)(d-2),\;\#{\rm Nodes}=\frac{1}{2}d(d-1)(d-2)(d-3).

Like as those, there are many different geometric situations where fold, cusps, double-folds appear naturally, while our formulas enumerate them in exactly the same way – namely, the Thom polynomial unites many such counting problems into a form that is relevant only for the type of local singularity. Furthermore, this theory reveals the intrinsic meaning of classical numerical characters of projective varieties. Although it is little-known, Semple-Roth
[
SR49
]
wrote that for a projective surface there are exactly four basic characters which generate all other characters of Salmon-Cayley-Zeuthen, and afterwards, Roth claimed an analogous statement for a projective
3
-fold that there are seven basic characters which recover dozens of others. Through computing Thom polynomials
[
SO17
]
, it turns out that those basic characters correspond to Landweber-Novikov classes in modern language; there are four
s_{0},s_{1},s_{2},s_{01}
for a surface, and seven
s_{0},s_{1},s_{2},s_{3},s_{01},s_{11},s_{001}
for a
3
-fold, indeed. This suggests cobordism theory behind (see §
6
). A more advanced application to enumerative geometry, as well as unexpected applications to other fields, will be reviewed in §
7
.

2.
Thom polynomials for singularities of maps

2.1.
Classification of map-germs

To begin with, we describe a few basic notions in the Thom-Mather theory, see e.g.,
[
MN22
]
,
[
MN20
, Part I]
,
[
AGV85
, Part I]
for details. See also J. Mather’s
[
Mat71
]
for algebraic geometry setting.

Let
\mathcal{O}_{m}
be the local ring of holomorphic function germs
(\mathbb{C}^{m},0)\to\mathbb{C}

with the maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}_{m}=\{h\in\mathcal{O}_{m},f(0)=0\}
.
Put
\mathcal{E}(m,n)
to be the
\mathcal{O}_{m}
-module of all homolorphic map-germs

f:(\mathbb{C}^{m},0)\to\mathbb{C}^{n}
, and also put
\mathcal{E}_{0}(m,n):=\mathfrak{m}_{m}\mathcal{E}(m,n)
which consists of all germs
f:(\mathbb{C}^{m},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)
.

Equivalence
:
The group of biholomorphic germs
(\mathbb{C}^{m},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{m},0)

is denoted by
{\rm Aut}(\mathbb{C}^{m},0)
.
There are two different kinds of natural equivalence relations on map-germs:
for
\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{A},\mathcal{K}
, a
\mathcal{G}
-equivalence class of map-germs is called a
\mathcal{G}
-singularity type
(
\mathcal{G}
-type, for short).

•

\mathcal{A}
-classification
(right-left equivalence) classifies map-germs up to isomorphisms of the source and the target spaces. The
right-left group

\mathcal{A}\;(=\mathcal{A}_{m,n})
is the direct product
{\rm Aut}(\mathbb{C}^{m},0)\times{\rm Aut}(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)
,
which acts on
\mathcal{E}_{0}(m,n)
by

(\sigma,\tau).f:=\tau\circ f\circ\sigma^{-1}.

Two map-germs
f,g:(\mathbb{C}^{m},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)
are
\mathcal{A}
-equivalent
if
f
and
g
belong to the same
\mathcal{A}
-orbit.

•

\mathcal{K}
-classification
(contact equivalence)
classifies zero loci
f^{-1}(0)
as schemes up to isomorphisms, i.e., it classifies the local algebra

Q(f):=\mathcal{O}_{m}/\langle f_{1},\cdots,f_{n}\rangle_{\mathcal{O}_{m}}

where
f_{i}
are component functions of
f
.
In other words, the
\mathcal{K}
-equivalence measures the
tangency of the graphs
y=f(x)
and
y=0
in
\mathbb{C}^{m}\times\mathbb{C}^{n}
; the
contact group

\mathcal{K}\;(=\mathcal{K}_{m,n})
consists of pairs
(\sigma,\Phi)
of
\sigma\in{\rm Aut}(\mathbb{C}^{m},0)
and map-germs
\Phi:(\mathbb{C}^{m},0)\to GL(n,\mathbb{C})
, and it acts on
\mathcal{E}_{0}(m,n)
by

((\sigma,\Phi).f)(x)=\Phi(x)f(\sigma(x)).

Two map-germs
f,g:(\mathbb{C}^{m},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)
are
\mathcal{K}
-equivalent
if
Q(f)=\sigma^{*}Q(g)
for some
\sigma\in{\rm Aut}(\mathbb{C}^{m},0)
, also equivalently,
f
and
g
belong to the same
\mathcal{K}
-orbit.
In particular, if
f\sim_{\mathcal{A}}g
, then
f\sim_{\mathcal{K}}g
, i.e.,

\mathcal{A}.f\subset\mathcal{K}.f
.

Deformations and stability
:
Let
f\in\mathcal{E}_{0}(m,n)
.
An
infinitesimal deformation
of
f
is a vector field-germ along
f
.
The space of infinitesimal deformations
is regarded as the ‘tangent space’ of
\mathcal{E}(m,n)
at
f
, and
is denoted by

\textstyle\theta(f)=\mathcal{O}_{m}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n}.

For the identity map
id_{m}
of
\mathbb{C}^{m}
,

\theta_{m}:=\theta(id_{m})
is the space of germs of vector fields on
\mathbb{C}^{m}
at the origin.
We set
tf:\theta_{m}\to\theta(f)
by
tf(v):=df(v)
and
\omega f:\theta_{n}\to\theta(f)
by
\omega(w):=w\circ f
.
For our convenience, we put

T\mathcal{A}_{e}.f=tf(\theta(id_{m}))+\omega f(\theta(id_{n})),\quad T\mathcal{K}_{e}.f=tf(\theta(id_{m}))+f^{*}\mathfrak{m}_{m}\theta(f),

which are subspaces of
\theta(f)
consisting of deformations caused by the actions of source and target isomorphisms
and contact group action (not necessarily preserving the origin).
Indeed, the subscript
e
indicates the extended action of reparametrization groups
possibly moving the origin, and the above subspaces are the tangent spaces of the orbits.

We say that
f:(\mathbb{C}^{m},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)
is an (
infinitesimally
)
stable germ
if it holds that

\theta(f)=T\mathcal{A}_{e}.f,

namely, any deformation of
f
is re-produced by deformations of coordinate changes of the source and the target spaces.
It is known, as one of major theorems of J. Mather, that for stable germs
f
and
g
,
f\sim_{\mathcal{K}}g
if and only if
f\sim_{\mathcal{A}}g
.
Namely, for a stable germ,
\mathcal{A}.f=\{\mbox{\small Stable germs}\}\cap\mathcal{K}.f
.

Determinacy
:
We denote the
k
-jet of
f:(\mathbb{C}^{m},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)
by
j^{k}f(0)
and the
k
-jet space by
J^{k}(m,n)
(the space of Taylor coefficients of order
\leq k
off the constant terms).
Let
\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{A}
or
\mathcal{K}
.
A map-germ
f
is
k
-
\mathcal{G}
-determined

The

-classification of finitely determined map-germs is essentially reduced to the problem on finite dimensional spaces, i.e., the action of

Let

In topology, the Borel construction tells that any family

For a

In algebraic geometry context (for reductive

Roughly saying, take a Zariski open subset

Remark 2.6

(Schur Positivity)

By the definition, the universal polynomial

is a linear combination of Chern monomials of degree equal to the codimension of

Then a certain

is shown by Pragacz-Weber
[

,

]
, see also a survey
[

]

Given a partition

the Schur function with index

and

Since

is a cone bundle, we directly apply the Fulton-Lazarsfeld theory of numerical positivity of polynomials in the Chern classes of ample vector bundles

Remark 2.7
.

(Thom polynomials in other cohomology theory)

Instead of using singular cohomology

or Chow theory

to define Thom polynomials for singularities of maps, we may consider to use other cohomology theories.
For general oriented cohomology theories, note that there is no

\eta
-singularity loci class
– we need some additional data to find a distinguished class supported on the
\eta
-singularity locus.
For instance, let us consider complex cobordism

, or more suitably, algebraic cobordism

in the sense of Levine-Morel
[
LM07
]
, and their equivariant version via the Borel construction
[
HL13
,
HV11
]

Pick up a proper
\mathcal{K}
-equivariant birational map onto an orbit-closure,

with

smooth. Then, for an appropriately generic map

, we may define a

-singularity loci class
in

represented by a proper map

is the fiber product of

and the jet extension map

through the above universal construction. The class depends on the choice of
\pi_{\eta}
, not only the singularity type

.
Then, it is automatic to see that this class is universally expressed in terms of cobordism Chern classes
[
LM07
,
HL13
]
, i.e., it lives in

\Omega_{G}^{*}(pt)=\Omega^{*}(BGL_{m}\times BGL_{n})=\Omega^{*}(pt)[[a_{1},\cdots,a_{m},b_{1},\cdots,b_{n}]].

That should be the
cobordism Thom polynomial for a desingularization

of
\eta
-singularity type
, and it may be supersymmetric provided

admits some stabiization property with respect to trivial suspension (see also for multi-singularity case §

).
In fact, a typical example is the Thom-Porteous formula in algebraic cobordism
\Omega^{*}
, which has been studied in
[
Hu12
,
HPM20
]
by using Bott-Samelson resolutions of orbit-closures

(historically, in real singularity case (cf. §
8
), already in the 70s, McCrory
[
Mc76
]
considered

-cobordism Thom polynomials for
\overline{\Sigma^{k}}
in connection with cohomology operations).

By the universality of
MU^{*}
or

Namely, there are some choices for ‘equivariant fundamental class’ in case other than ordinary cohomology theory

, and of interest is to find a good one among those choices which behaves nicely. A representation theoretic approach to

-singularity type in detail, by Rimányi-Szenes

[

2.4.
Desingularization method

We make a short remark on a traditional approach, called the
desingularization method
.
At the early stages of the theory, the crux of the problem had been recognized as explicitly constructing ‘resolution of singularities’ for the degeneracy loci of prescribed type of maps (indeed, it is natural from the viewpoint of Remark
2.7
).
This approach directly computes Thom polynomials and essentially does not rely on the existence theorem (Theorem
2.4
) mentioned above. For instance, many cancellations occur in computation, and consequently we see that the Thom polynomial is written in terms of quotient Chern classes
c_{i}=c_{i}(f)
.

Let
f:M\to N
be a holomorphic map. Before Mather’s classification theory of map-germs appeared, Thom considered the following filtration of (locally closed) submanifolds of
M
.
First, set

\Sigma^{i}(f):=\{\;x\in M\;|\;\dim\ker df(x)=i\;\}

and suppose that it is non-singular (i.e., the transversality condition on
df
is assumed). Then take the restriction map of
f
to
\Sigma^{i}(f)
,
and set

\Sigma^{i,j}(f):=\{\;x\in\Sigma^{i}(f)\;|\;\dim\ker d(f|_{\Sigma^{i}(f)})(x)=j\;\}.

Assume again it is non-singular, then take the restriction of
f
to
\Sigma^{i,j}(f)
and define
\Sigma^{i,j,k}(f)
and so on.
This is the so-called
Thom-Boardman singularity type
\Sigma^{I}
(TB singularity type) with symbol
I=(i\geq j\geq k\geq\cdots)
.
That is justified by the fact that there are
\mathcal{K}
-invariant algebraic subsets
\Sigma^{I}
in
J^{r}(m,n)
, defined by Jacobian extension (see e.g.,
[
AGV85
]
), so that if the jet-extension map
j^{r}f
is transverse to every
\Sigma^{I}(M,N)
, then it holds that
\Sigma^{I}(f)=(j^{r}f)^{-1}(\Sigma^{I}(M,N))
. For instance, the stratum
\Sigma^{1,1,\cdots,1,0}
(
1
repeats
k
times) in
J^{r}(m,n)
coincides with the
\mathcal{K}
-orbit of
A_{k}
-singularity type, i.e., the local algebra is isomorphic to
\mathbb{C}[x]/\langle x^{k+1}\rangle
. However, in general,
\Sigma^{I}
is not a single
\mathcal{K}
-orbit, and even they do not form a Whitney stratification.

On the other hand, there is another geometric way to construct some TB singularity loci, by means of
intrinsic derivatives
due to Porteous and Ronga. Let us look at a few examples.
Note that

J^{k}(M,N)\simeq\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}{\rm Hom}({\rm Sym}^{i}TM,TN)

where
{\rm Sym}^{i}
means the
i
-th symmetric power and the structure group is reduced to
GL_{m}\times GL_{n}
(thus
J^{k}(M,N)
is a vector bundle).
Let
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
denote canonical projection to
M
and
N
, respectively.
The first intrinsic derivative is a tautological linear map

\alpha:T_{x}M\to T_{y}N\;\;\mbox{at}\;\;\alpha\in J^{1}(M,N)

with

and

, and the second intrinsic derivative is

\beta^{*}:\ker\alpha\to{\rm Hom}(\ker\alpha,\mbox{\rm coker}\,\alpha)\;\;\mbox{at}\;\;(\alpha,\beta)\in J^{2}(M,N)

given by

\Sigma^{i}(M,N)
is defined as the set of

with
\dim\ker\alpha=i
, and

\Sigma^{i,j}(M,N)
is the set of

with
\dim\ker\alpha=i
and

.

Let

be the Grassmann bundle of

, and

\xi_{i}

The vector bundle

[

]

Let

with

and
\dim\tau=j

In summary, this traditional approach consists of two steps. The first one is to explicitly construct a tower of some flag bundles

and a vector bundle

]
with residue expression.

However, this strategy fails quickly. Even for order two, the computation in the second step is not easy at all. For order more than two, it is not known in general whether such a nice desingularization in the first step exists or not, and only a few concrete examples are known in equidimensional case (
\kappa=0
); e.g., by finding desingularizations, Thom polynomials are computed for the closures of

\Sigma^{1,1,1}

[
Po71
]
,
\Sigma^{1,1,1,1}

[
Gaf93
]
and
\Sigma^{1,1,1,1,1}

[
Tu89
]

(those are exactly
tp(A_{\mu})
with
\mu=3,4,5
in §
1.1.2
, respectively) and also see
[
Da72
]
.
To go further, we need a different perspective, which we will describe in §
3
.

3.
Equivariant localization

3.1.
Symmetry of singularities and interpolation

Around the end of the last century, a novel efficient approach to computing Thom polynomials without using any desingularization was proposed by R. Rimányi
[
Ri01
]
, that brought a breakthrough in this field.

Let
V
be a complex affine space with action of a complex algebraic Lie group
G
.
Assume that
V
consists of finitely many
G
-orbits
\xi
, and also that for every orbit
\xi
,
the Euler class of the normal bundle
\nu_{\xi}
of
\xi
in
V
is non-zero,
c_{top}(\nu_{\xi})\not=0
.
Let
G_{\xi}
be the stabilizer subgroup of
\xi
, and

\iota_{\xi}:G_{\xi}\subset G
the inclusion.
Then it turns out that

\bigoplus\iota_{\xi}^{*}:H_{G}^{*}(V)=H^{*}(BG){\longrightarrow}\bigoplus_{\xi}H^{*}(BG_{\xi})

is an isomorphism. In particular it holds that

(8)

\iota_{\xi}^{*}\;tp(\eta)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}0&(\xi\not\subset\overline{\eta})\\
c_{top}(\nu_{\eta})&(\xi=\eta)\end{array}\right.

The system of these equations (
8
) has a unique solution modulo torsion
[
FR04
]
.
The first equations in (
8
) means that
tp(\eta)
lives in the kernel of the ring homomorphism
H_{G}^{*}(V)\to H_{G}^{*}(V-\overline{\eta})
, which is isomorphic to the natural image of

H_{G}^{*}(V,V-\overline{\eta})\simeq H_{*}^{G}(\overline{\eta})\;\;\;\;\mbox{(equivariant Alexander duality)}.

The kernel is called the
avoiding ideal associated to
\eta
in Fehér-Rimányi
[
FR04
]
or the
ideal of cohomology classes supported on
\overline{\eta}
in Fulton-Pragacz
[
FP98
]
.
In this ideal, the above second equation in (
8
) distinguishes the
G
-fundamental class of
\overline{\eta}
, that is nothing but
tp(\eta)
.

Also note that it suffices to take the maximal torus of
G_{\xi}
for each
\xi
by splitting principle.
Once we write
tp(\eta)
as a homogeneous polynomial in generators of
H^{*}(BG)
with unknown coefficients, (
8
) gives the system of linear equations in the unknowns, and solving it determines the explicit form of
tp(\eta)
. Indeed, usually it is overdetermined.

Today, this is known as the
restriction
or
interpolation method
due to R. Rimányi. Originally it comes from a slightly different and deeper background,
singular Thom-Pontrjagin construction

[
Ri96
,
RS98
]
– namely, it works not only for Lie group action but also for
Lie groupoid action
or
Artin stack with global stratification
, see §
6
later.

Below, we demonstrate some typical computations using this method, according to
[
Ri01
,
FR04
,
Kaz03b
]
, for objects treated in Guiding Examples §
1.1.1
and §
1.1.2
.

3.1.1.
Thom-Porteous formula, revisited

We consider the classification of linear maps by linear coordinate changes. Let

G=GL_{m}\times GL_{n},\;\;V={\rm Hom}\,(\mathbb{C}^{m},\mathbb{C}^{n})

and
\eta=\Sigma^{k}
, the
G
-orbit of linear maps with kernel dimension
k
.
To determine the universal form of
tp(\Sigma^{k})
, we see that it is enough to use only the last equation in (
8
).
Put
{l}=n-m+k
.

Take a representative
h
in
\Sigma^{k}
in a standard way, then
the tangent space of the orbit at the point
h
consists of
all matrices in the following form:

h=\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}I_{m-k}&\;O\\
\hline\cr O&\;O\end{array}\right]\in\Sigma^{k},\quad T_{h}\Sigma^{k}=\left\{\;\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}\;\;*&\;*\\
\hline\cr\;\;*&\;O\end{array}\right]\;\right\}\subset T_{h}V=V.

Note that the normal space is isomorphic to
{\rm Hom}(\ker h,{\rm coker}\,h)
.
The stabilizer group of
h\in\Sigma^{k}
, denoted by
G_{k}
,
consists of pairs of square matrices

\left(\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}P&O\\
\hline\cr\;*&A\end{array}\right],\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}P&\;*\\
\hline\cr O&B\end{array}\right]\right),\quad(A,B,P)\in GL_{k}\times GL_{l}\times GL_{m-k}.

Let
\rho_{1},\rho_{2},\rho_{3}
be the
representations of
G_{k}
on
\ker h=\mathbb{C}^{k}
,
{\rm coker}\,h=\mathbb{C}^{l}
,
{\rm Im}\,h=\mathbb{C}^{m-k}
, respectively,
and then the normal bundle
\nu_{k}
of the orbit
\Sigma^{k}
in
V
is obtained from the universal bundle
EG_{k}\to BG_{k}
with
\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}
acting on
{\rm Hom}(\mathbb{C}^{k},\mathbb{C}^{l})
.
Put
c(\rho_{1})=\prod_{i=1}^{k}(1+\alpha_{i})
and
c(\rho_{2})=\prod_{j=1}^{l}(1+\beta_{j})
.
Then using (
8
) and the classical resultant, we have

\displaystyle\iota_{k}^{*}\;tp(\Sigma^{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{top}(\nu_{k})=c_{kl}(\rho_{1}^{*}\otimes\rho_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod(\beta_{j}-\alpha_{i})=\Delta_{l^{k}}(c(\rho_{2}-\rho_{1}))\,\in H^{*}(BG_{k}).

Denote by
a_{i}
and
b_{j}

universal Chern classes for
GL_{m}
and
GL_{n}
, respectively, and then

H^{*}(BG)=\mathbb{Z}[a_{1},\cdots,a_{m},b_{1},\cdots,b_{n}].

The representation of
G_{k}
on
\mathbb{C}^{m}
and
\mathbb{C}^{n}
are

\lambda_{1}=\rho_{1}\oplus\rho_{3}
and
\lambda_{2}=\rho_{2}\oplus\rho_{3}
.
Thus

\iota_{k}^{*}:H^{*}(BG)\to H^{*}(BG_{k})

is determined by

c^{\prime}\mapsto c(\lambda_{1})
and
c^{\prime\prime}\mapsto c(\lambda_{2})
,
and especially, it sends the quotient variables

1+c_{1}+c_{2}+\cdots:=\frac{1+b_{1}+\cdots+b_{n}}{1+a_{1}+\cdots+a_{m}}\longmapsto\frac{c(\lambda_{2})}{c(\lambda_{1})}=1+c_{1}(\rho_{2}-\rho_{1})+\cdots.

Hence
\iota_{k}^{*}\Delta_{l^{k}}(c)=\Delta_{l^{k}}(c(\rho_{2}-\rho_{1}))
.
Finally it is checked that
\iota_{k}^{*}
is
injective
for degree
\leq k{l}
,
thus the consequence is

tp(\Sigma^{k})=\Delta_{l^{k}}(c).

Moreover, the avoiding ideal associated to
\Sigma^{k}
is explicitly determined, see
[
FP98
,
FR04
]
.

This method works for actions of Borel subgroups as well, that leads to application to (matrix) Schubert calculus and quiver representations
[
FR03
,
FR02b
,
FR04
,
BFR05
,
BR07
]
and also other topics such as algebraic combinatorics in configuration problems, e.g.,
[
FNR05
,
FNR06
,
FNR09
,
FNR12
,
DFR13
]
and so on.
Afterwards, this research direction has been widely expanding, that will be touched in §
4.2.1
later.

For the second order Thom-Boardman singularity type
\Sigma^{i,j}
, the interpolation has also been discussed by Fehér-Kümöves
[
FK06
]
based on Ronga’s disingularization. It will be treated in a more general framework (§

).

3.1.2.
Fold and Cusp, revisited

We demonstrate how to compute the Thom polynomial for
A_{2}
-singularity type (cusp)
for maps between the same dimensional manifolds, i.e., in case of
\kappa=0
(see §
1.1.2
).
By the existence theorem of Thom polynomials, it has the form

tp(A_{2})=Ac_{1}^{2}+Bc_{2}

in quotient Chern classes
c_{i}=c_{i}(\mbox{target}-\mbox{source})
.
Our task is to determine the unknown integer coefficients
A,B
.

The normal form of stable map-germ of type
A_{2}
is given by a quasi-homogeneous polynomial map

A_{2}:\mathbb{C}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}^{2},\;\;(x,y)\to(x^{3}+yx,y).

In jet space of order
\geq 3
, the jet-extension of this map is transverse to the
\mathcal{K}
-orbit at the origin, so
this map itself is regarded as a normal slice to the
\mathcal{K}
-orbit of
A_{2}
-singularity type.
The maximal torus
T
of the stabilizer group gives the torus actions on the source and the target

\rho_{0}=\alpha\oplus\alpha^{\otimes 2},\quad\rho_{1}=\alpha^{\otimes 3}\oplus\alpha^{\otimes 2}

satisfying
A_{2}\circ\rho_{0}=\rho_{1}\circ A_{2}
, where
\alpha
is a character
T\to\mathbb{C}^{*}
.
Take the dual tautological line bundle

\ell=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}(1)
over
a projective space
\mathbb{P}^{N}
of large dimension
N\gg 0

(or the classifying space
BT=\mathbb{P}^{\infty}
of the torus
T
).
Define
two vector bundles of rank
2

E_{0}\;(=E_{0}(A_{2})):=\ell\oplus\ell^{\otimes 2},\qquad E_{1}\;(=E_{1}(A_{2})):=\ell^{\otimes 3}\oplus\ell^{\otimes 2}.

Since the normal form of
A_{2}
is invariant under the torus action,
we can glue together local patches
id_{U_{i}}\times A_{2}:U_{i}\times\mathbb{C}^{2}\to U_{i}\times\mathbb{C}^{2}
.
The resulting map

is a stable map between the total spaces

E_{0}
and
E_{1}
so that the following diagram commutes and
the restriction to each fiber is
\mathcal{A}
-equivalent to the normal form of
A_{2}
.
We call
f_{A_{2}}
the
universal map for
A_{2}
.

Theorem 3.4

(Fehér-Rimányi

]

(Thom series)

In fact,

For a nice introduction to Thom series with several examples, see Rimányi’s survey article

The rule of the appearance of the coefficients
\psi_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{\mu}}
should be detected.
It requires to replace the Grassmannian
{\rm Gr}^{n}
by a finer space. In
[

]
Kazarian has introduced the concept of
non-associative Hilbert scheme
to be the total space of some (partial) flag bundle which becomes a smooth ambient space into which

is embedded as a closed subvariety. Let

be the local algebra of a
\mathcal{K}
-type
\eta
of map-germs and let

. In order to construct this ambient space, a key ingredient is a filtration on the nilpotent subalgebra

,

N=N_{1}\supset N_{2}\supset\cdots\supset N_{r+1}=0\qquad d_{i}=\dim(N_{i}/N_{i+1})

satisfying
N_{i}\cdot N_{j}\subset N_{i+j}
. Of course,
\sum d_{i}=\mu
.
The degree filtration with
N_{k}:=\mathfrak{m}^{k}_{\mathbb{C}^{m},0}Q
is a typical one, but there may be some other choices.
We call the sequence
\underline{d}=(d_{1},\cdots,d_{r})
the

associated to the filtration on

.
Put
s_{k}=d_{1}+\cdots+d_{k}
, and for each

with
s_{k-1}\leq i\leq s_{k}
, put

and

, and set

K_{\underline{d}}(t_{1},\cdots,t_{\mu}):=\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{\mu}t_{i}^{e(i)}\cdot\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq\mu}(t_{j}-t_{i})}{\prod_{k=1}^{d}\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}\prod_{i\leq j,w(i)+w(j)\leq w(k)}(t_{k}-t_{i}-t_{j})}.

Theorem 3.5

(Kazarian
[
Kaz17
]
)
.

The coefficients
\psi_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{\mu}}
arising in
{\rm Ts}_{Q}
are determined by the following rational generating function

\sum_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{\mu}}\psi_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{\mu}}t_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots t_{\mu}^{i_{\mu}}=K_{\underline{d}}(t_{1},\cdots,t_{\mu})P_{Q}(t_{1},\cdots,t_{\mu})

where
P_{Q}
is a polynomial defined as in
[
Kaz17
, §4]
.
In other words, the Thom series
{\rm Ts}_{Q}
has the following residual integral expression:

{\rm Ts}_{Q}=\underset{t_{1}=\infty}{{\rm Res}}\cdots\underset{t_{\mu}=\infty}{{\rm Res}}\;K_{\underline{d}}(t)\cdot P_{Q}(t)\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{\mu}{\rm C}(t_{i}^{-1})\,t_{i}^{\kappa}\;dt_{i}.

Obviously, in the case of
\eta=A_{\mu}
with the degree filtration, this residual integral is just Theorem
3.2
.
Besides from
A_{\mu}
-singularity types, for other types, e.g.,
\Sigma^{i,j}
,
I_{a,b}
,
I\hskip-2.0ptI\hskip-2.0ptI_{a,b}
etc., the polynomial

, and hence
{\rm Ts}_{Q(\eta)}
, are explicitly computed, see
[
Kaz17
,
FR12
]
.

Note that this decomposition (the right hand side) of the generating function of

is not unique, especially depends on the choice of filtration;
K_{\underline{\eta}}
is the factor coming from the Gysin map of the projection of the flag bundle (cf.
[
Da73
]
) and
P_{Q}
corresponds to the fundamental class of a certain Borel orbit in the ambient space.

Looking back the flow of old and new researches, we observe that the present work of Thom series surely lies on the natural extension of work of Porteous, Ronga, Damon, Gaffney etc. That has not yet been completed, rather say, still only halfway there. In this theory, it is most critical to detect the polynomial
P_{Q}
, which comes from the Borel geometry associated to the local algebra, and it essentially relates to tautological integral over punctual Hilbert schemes. Many tasks remain for future work.

4.
Higher Thom polyomials

The Thom polynomial
tp(\eta)(c(f))
is the most basic invariant for the appearance of
\eta
-type singular points for a given map
f:M\to N
, while if the locus
\overline{\eta(f)}
has the expected dimension which is positive, the locus may contain higher codimensional informative cycles other than the fundamental cycle. For instance, if
f:M\to N
is a Morin map (a stable map which has only local singularities of type
A_{k}
), the locus
S_{k}:=\overline{A_{k}(f)}
is a submanifold of
M
, and hence, the pushforward of the Chern class
\iota_{*}c(TS_{k})\in H^{*}(M)
is defined (
\iota
is the inclusion of the locus to
M
). These classes appeared in
[
Le64
,
An85
,
Na00
]
, and especially, Ando called them ‘higher Thom polynomials for Morin singularities’.
However, the locus for non-Morin maps is singular in general, then this definition does not make sense at all. We need some more tools to build a theory.

4.1.
Chern class for a singular variety

Let
X
be an irreducible algebraic variety of dimension
n
.
If
X
is non-singular, it possesses the
total
Chern class

c(TX)=1+c_{1}(TX)+\cdots+c_{n}(TX)\;\in H^{*}(X).

Switching to homology, note that
c_{n}(TX)\frown[X]=\chi(X)
and
1\frown[X]=[X]
.
If
X
is singular, the tangent bundle no longer exists, so
c(TX)
does not make sense at all.
Nevertheless, we may find some alternative to
c(TX)
in homology – for example,
the
Chern-Schwartz-MacPherson class
(CSM class) is a typical one, see Brasselet
[
Br22
]
.
It is a total homology class of
X
such that
the degree is topological Euler characteristic of
X
and the top term is the fundamental class

c^{{\rm SM}}(X)=\chi(X)\cdot[pt]+\cdots+[X]\;\in H_{*}(X)

together with a nice functorial property (let us note again that if
X
is non-compact,
H_{*}
stands for
Borel-Moore homology).
Let
\mathcal{F}(X)
be the abelian group of constructible functions on
X
, and
for a proper morphism
X\to Y
, the
pushforward

f_{*}:\mathcal{F}(X)\to\mathcal{F}(Y)
is defined.
It holds that
(f\circ g)_{*}=f_{*}\circ g_{*}
.
R. MacPherson introduced the following natural transformation between two covariant functors
\mathcal{F}
and
H_{*}

using the Chern-Mather class and local Euler obstruction, as a solution to the so-called Grothendieck-Deligne conjecture.
Also it should be mentioned that the same notion had been found by M. Schwartz in a different context from obstruction theory
[
Br22
]
.

Theorem 4.1
.

[
Mac74
]

There is a unique natural transformation

C_{*}:\mathcal{F}(X)\longrightarrow H_{*}(X)

between these functors
so that
C_{*}(1\hskip-3.5pt1_{X})=c(TX)\frown[X]
if
X
is non-singular.

Namely, it holds that

•

C_{*}(\alpha+\beta)=C_{*}(\alpha)+C_{*}(\beta)
(additive homomorphism)

•

C_{*}f_{*}(\alpha)=f_{*}C_{*}(\alpha)
for proper morphisms
f:X\to Y
.

In particular,
if
pt:X\to pt
is proper, we have

pt_{*}C_{*}(\alpha)=C_{*}pt_{*}(\alpha)=\int_{X}\alpha
.
The
Chern-Schwartz-MacPherson class
of
X

is defined by
c^{{\rm SM}}(X):=C_{*}(1\hskip-3.5pt1_{X})
.

There is also the ‘Segre-version’ of CSM classes, see Aluffi
[
Al22
,
Al05
]
.
Let
\iota:X\hookrightarrow M
be a closed embedding into non-singular
M
.
Like as the relation between Segre covariance class and Fulton’s Chern class,
we define the
Segre-Schwartz-MacPherson class
by

s^{{\rm SM}}(X,M):=c(\iota^{*}TM)^{-1}\frown c^{{\rm SM}}(X)\;\;\in H_{*}(X).

We often regard the class

as in
H^{*}(M)
via the pushforward
\iota_{*}
and the Poincaré duality, unless specifically mentioned.
Also we set for
\alpha\in\mathcal{F}(M)

s^{{\rm SM}}(\alpha,M):=c(TM)^{-1}\cdot C_{*}(\alpha)\in H^{*}(M).

The Segre-SM class has an expected nice property for transverse pullback:

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
f:M\to N
be a map between complex manifolds, and
let
Y
be a closed singular subvariety of
N
.
Assume that
f
is transverse to (a Whitney stratification of)
Y
.
Then it holds that

f^{*}s^{{\rm SM}}(Y,N)=s^{{\rm SM}}(f^{-1}(Y),M)\;\;\;\in\;\;H^{*}(M).

Remark 4.3
.

We follow the sign convention of the Segre class as in Fulton’s book

]

Notice that if

is a closed submanifold of

with the normal bundle

s^{{\rm SM}}(1\hskip-3.5pt1_{X},M)=\iota_{*}c(-\nu)\;\;\in H^{*}(M).

Using

Remark 4.6

(CSM class of degeneracy loci)

5.
Other classifications of map-germs

We give a quick review on Thom polynomial theory for other classifications of map-germs, especially,
isolated singularities of functions and hypersurfaces, and
\mathcal{A}
-finitely determined map-germs.

5.1.
Lagrange and Legendre Thom polynomials

There is an important class of mappings, called
Lagrange and Legendre maps
, whose singularities correspond to singularities of functions and hypersurfaces, respectively.

Let
Q
be a complex symplectic manifold and
\pi:Q\to N
a Lagrange fibration (i.e., every fiber is Lagragian), and

\iota:L\to Q
a Lagrange immersion.
A typical example is
Q=T^{*}N\to N
, the cotangent bundle equipped with the canonical symplectic structure.
The composed map
\pi\circ\iota:L\to N
is called a Lagrange map; locally such a map is expressed by the gradient maps
(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)
,
p\mapsto\frac{\partial g}{\partial p}
, of some generating function
g=g(p)
.
There is the counterpart in contact geometry; the composition of a Legendre immersion and the projection of Legendre fibration is a Legendre map.
Their singularities are called
Lagrange and Legendre singularities
, respectively, and have thoroughly been explored by Arnold, Zakalyukin and others, see
[
AGV85
]
. The corresponding Thom polynomial theory was initiated by Vassiliev in real
C^{\infty}
-category
[
Va88
,
AGLV93
]
, in which the simplest Thom polynomial for fold singularity corresponds to the so-called the (first)
Maslov class
. The theory is also formulated in complex analytic/algebraic category by Kazarian
[
Kaz98
,
Kaz00
,
Kaz03a
]
, that we will briefly review below. Also see Mikosz-Pragacz-Weber
[
MPW09
,
MPW11
]
) especially for the positivity question.
Some other particular cases had been treated in e.g.,
[
Ha80
,
MSV89
]
.

A hypersurface-singularity of dimension
d
is a
\mathcal{K}
-equivalence class of function-germs
f:(\mathbb{C}^{d+1},0)\to(\mathbb{C},0)
.
It is an isolated singularity if and only if
f
is
\mathcal{K}
-finitely determined.
When ignoring the linear action on the target
\mathbb{C}
, it is the
\mathcal{R}
-classification of function-singularities. The list of typical singularity types starts from the so-called
ADE classification
. Although it might be thought of as a special part (with corank one) of the classification of isolated complete intersection singularities (ICIS), it has own particular nature – there is a different stabilization. Namely,
f:(\mathbb{C}^{d+1},0)\to(\mathbb{C},0)
and
g:(\mathbb{C}^{d^{\prime}+1},0)\to(\mathbb{C},0)
(
d\leq d^{\prime}
) are
stablly
\mathcal{G}
-equivalent
if
f(x)+x_{d+2}^{2}+\cdots+x_{d^{\prime}+1}^{2}
is
\mathcal{G}
-equivalent to
g
(
\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{R}
or
\mathcal{K}
).

Below we focus mainly on the Legendrian case (
\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{K}
).
A natural way to study global properties of isolated hypersurface singularities is described as follows.
That arises in a family of divisors.
Suppose that we are given a locally trivial complex fibration
p:W\to N
where
W
and
N
are complex manifolds of dimension
m+1
and
n
, respectively, and an embedding
\iota:M\hookrightarrow W
of a non-singular hypersurface. Put

(
\dim M=m
,

) and

to be the critical locus of
\varphi
, i.e., the locus of points

at which

contains the relative tangent space

. Note that the map

has singularities of corank at most one:

In other words, the germ of

We now assume that

(up to non-zero constant multiples), then it turns out that the map

Now we put

The map

Remark 5.4

(Lagrangian case)

, when one replaces

by the

th Chern class of the tautological bundle of rank
n
over
\Lambda_{n}^{\mathbb{C}}
.
In Legendrian case, the ring

is isomorphic to the cohomology ring of the twisted complex Lagrange Grassmannian
\tilde{\Lambda}_{n}^{\mathbb{C}}
, that is the space of Lagrange subspaces in the twisted symplectic fibers of the bundle
\mathbb{C}^{n}\oplus(\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes\xi)
, where
\xi
is the canonical line bundle over

.

Positivity of Lagrange and Legendre Thom polynomials with respect to some particular additive basis (‘twisted’ Schur

-functions basis and also other basis) has been shown in Mikosz-Pragacz-Weber
[

,

]
; especially, it contains the list of explicit expansions for

-types and

.
There is still room for further studies in this direction.

6.
Thom polynomials for multi-singularities

At the beginning of this century, another breakthrough occurred. Based on his topological argument using complex cobordism theory
MU^{*}
, M. Kazarian
[
Kaz03b
,
Kaz06
]
proposed a general framework for universal polynomial expression of the multi-singularity locus of prescribed type for proper holomorphic maps and showed its high potential in application.
We briefly describe this conjectural theory and report a progress towards an entire proof.

6.1.
Multi-singularity loci and Hilbert schemes

Below we use the terminology ‘multi-singularity’ in the following sense:

Definition 6.1
.

A
multi-singularity type of map-germs with codimension
\kappa
is an ordered set

\underline{\eta}:=(\eta_{1},\cdots,\eta_{r})

of
\mathcal{K}
-types
\eta_{i}
of mono-germs
(\mathbb{C}^{m},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)
for some
m,n
with
\kappa=n-m
.
In case of
\kappa\leq 0
, we assume that
the collection
\underline{\eta}
contains no submersion-germs.

Note that the entries
\eta_{i}
can be duplicate.
We denote by
{\rm Aut}(\eta_{1},\cdots,\eta_{r})
the subgroup of permutations in
\mathfrak{S}_{r}
preserving the types
\eta_{i}
, e.g.,
{\rm Aut}(A_{1},A_{1},A_{2},A_{1},A_{2})\simeq\mathfrak{S}_{3}\times\mathfrak{S}_{2}
.

A multi-jet bundle
{}_{r}J^{k}(M,N)
is defined by the restriction of the cartesian product of
r
copies of
J^{k}(M,N)\to M\times N
over
F(M,r)\times N^{r}
, where
F(M,r)=M^{r}\smallsetminus\Delta^{r}_{M}
is the configuration space of ordered distinct
r
points of
M
(i.e., the complement of the fat diagonal
\Delta^{r}_{M}
)
[
AGV85
,
MN20
]
. Put
\pi^{r}_{M}:{}_{r}J^{k}(M,N)\to F(M,r)
and
\pi^{r}_{N}:{}_{r}J^{k}(M,N)\to N^{r}
to be the canonical projections. For a map
f:M\to N
, the multi-jet extension map
{}_{r}j^{k}f
is defined by

{}_{r}j^{k}f:F(M,r)\to{}_{r}J^{k}(M,N),\quad(p_{1},\cdots,p_{r})\mapsto(j^{k}f(p_{1}),\cdots,j^{k}f(p_{r})).

Let
\underline{\eta}:=(\eta_{1},\cdots,\eta_{r})
be a multi-singularity type, and
\dim M=m
and
\dim N=n\,(=m+\kappa)
.
We always assume that
k
is sufficiently high so that it is greater than the determinacy degree of every entry
\eta_{i}
.
Let
W_{\underline{\eta}}\subset{}_{r}J^{k}(M,N)
be the submanifold consisting of
z=(z_{1},\cdots,z_{r})
such that
z_{i}
is the
k
-jet of a mono-germ of type
\eta_{i}
(
1\leq i\leq r
) and
\pi_{N}^{r}(z)
lies on the diagonal
\Delta^{r}_{N}
of
N^{r}
.
Let
S=\{p_{1},\cdots,p_{r}\}\subset M
, a set of
r
distinct points, and suppose that
f(p_{1})=\cdots=f(p_{r})=:q
and the germ
f:(M,p_{i})\to(N,q)
is of type
\eta_{i}
for each
1\leq i\leq r
.
The multi-germ
f:(M,S)\to(N,q)
is
stable
if the multi-jet extension
{}_{r}j^{k}f
(of a representative of the germ
f
) is transversal to
W_{\underline{\eta}}
at a point
(p_{1},\cdots,p_{r})\in F(M,r)
.

Definition 6.2
.

We say that
f:M\to N
is a
locally stable map
(or simply, a stable map) if for any finite subset
S
of
M
such that
f
maps
S
to a point
q\in N
, the germ
f:(M,S)\to(N,q)
is stable.

Let
f:M\to N
be a proper and locally stable map.
Take the preimage of
W_{\underline{\eta}}
via
{}_{r}j^{k}f
and project it to the first factor
M
of
F(M,r)\subset M^{r}
, then the resulting set is written as

\underline{\eta}(f):=\left\{\;p_{1}\in\eta_{1}(f)\;\Bigl|\;\begin{array}[]{l}\exists\,p_{2},\cdots,p_{r}\in f^{-1}f(p_{1})\smallsetminus\{p_{1}\}\;s.t.\;p_{i}\not=p_{j}\;(i\not=j)\\
\mbox{and the germ of $f$ at $p_{i}$ is of type $\eta_{i}$ }\end{array}\right\},

which becomes a locally closed submanifold of
M
having the expected dimension.
This locus is mapped via
f
to a locally closed submanifold of
N
of the same dimension:

f(\underline{\eta}(f)):=\left\{\;q\in N\;\Bigl|\;\begin{array}[]{l}\exists\,p_{1},\cdots,p_{r}\in f^{-1}(q)\;s.t.\;p_{i}\not=p_{j}\;(i\not=j)\\
\mbox{and the germ of $f$ at $p_{i}$ is of type $\eta_{i}$}\end{array}\right\}.

Note that
f:{\underline{\eta}(f)}\to{f(\underline{\eta}(f)})

is a finite-to-one covering whose degree, denoted by

\deg_{1}\underline{\eta}
, is equal to the number of
\eta_{1}
appearing in the tuple
\underline{\eta}
.

For our convenience, we introduce the
source and target multi-singularity loci classes of
f
to be

(11)

m_{\underline{\eta}}(f):=\#{\rm Aut}(\eta_{2},\cdots,\eta_{r})[\overline{\underline{\eta}(f)}],\;\;n_{\underline{\eta}}(f):=\#{\rm Aut}(\eta_{1},\cdots,\eta_{r})[\overline{f(\underline{\eta}(f))}]

(more generally, these classes can be defined for
arbitrary
proper maps
[
Oh24
]
).
In particular, it holds that

f_{*}m_{\underline{\eta}}(f)=n_{\underline{\eta}}(f).

Based on his topological observations supported by many computations, Kazarian claimed in
[
Kaz03b
,
Kaz06
]
the following statement:

Thom-Kazarian principle for multi-singularities:

For any multi-singularity type
\underline{\eta}=(\eta_{1},\cdots,\eta_{r})
of map-germs of relative codimension
\kappa
, there is a unique abstract Chern polynomial

R_{\underline{\eta}}=\sum a_{I}c^{I}\in\mathbb{Q}[c_{1},c_{2},\cdots],

called the residual polynomial of type
\underline{\eta}
,
such that
R_{\underline{\eta}}
does not depend on the order of entries in
\underline{\eta}

and recursively admits the following property:
For a proper morphism
f:M\to N
with
\dim N-\dim M=\kappa
,
the
\underline{\eta}
-singularity loci classes
m_{\underline{\eta}}(f)
and
n_{\underline{\eta}}(f)

are expressed by

[formula]

m_{\underline{\eta}}(f)=\sum R_{J_{1}}\cdot f^{*}f_{*}(R_{J_{2}})\cdots f^{*}f_{*}(R_{J_{s}})

[formula]

n_{\underline{\eta}}(f)=\sum f_{*}(R_{J_{1}})\cdots f_{*}(R_{J_{s}})

in rational cohomology rings of
M
and
N
,
where the summand runs over all possible partitions of the set
\{1,\cdots,r\}

into a disjoint union of non-empty unordered subsets,

\{1,\cdots,r\}=J_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup J_{s}\;(s\geq 1)
,
and the subset containing the entry
1\in\{1,\cdots,r\}
is denoted by
J_{1}
.
Here, for each
J=\{j_{1},\cdots,j_{k}\}
,

R_{J}
stands for the residual polynomial
R_{(\eta_{j_{1}},\cdots,\eta_{j_{k}})}

evaluated by
c_{i}=c_{i}(f)
, the quotient Chern classes associated to
f
.

We call the universal polynomials in the right hand side of the formulas
(stp)
and
(ttp)

the
source and target Thom polynomial of a stable multi-singularity type
\underline{\eta}
, respectively.
Note that the source Thom polynomial
m_{\underline{\eta}}(f)
is written in quotient Chern classes
c_{i}(f)
’s and Landweber-Novikov classes
f^{*}s_{I}(f)
’s, and the target one is in
s_{I}(f)
’s,
and those polynomials depend only the multi-singularity type
\underline{\eta}
.
For convenience, we introduce a reduced version of multi-singularity Thom polynomials
(the last equality is for locally stable maps
f
):

tp(\underline{\eta})(c(f)):=\frac{1}{\#{\rm Aut}(\eta_{2},\cdots,\eta_{r})}m_{\underline{\eta}}(f)=[\overline{\underline{\eta}(f)}],

tp_{\rm target}(\underline{\eta})(c(f)):=\frac{1}{\#{\rm Aut}(\eta_{1},\cdots,\eta_{r})}n_{\underline{\eta}}(f)=[\overline{f(\underline{\eta}(f))}].

Note that

tp_{\rm target}(\underline{\eta})(c(f)):=\frac{1}{\deg_{1}\underline{\eta}}f_{*}tp(\underline{\eta})(c(f))
where
\deg_{1}\underline{\eta}
is the number of entries in
\underline{\eta}
which is the same type as
\eta_{1}
.

Commonly among those different approaches, it is important to find a nice compactification of the configuration space

Double and triple-point formulas
(Ronga
[

,

]

[

]

For

These are re-written in the form of

using Landweber-Novikov classes.
The proofs of the double and triple-point formulas rely on the desingularization method. Namely, desingularizations of the loci are explicitly constructed by blowing-ups along diagonals, and actually the resulting spaces coincide with relative Hilbert schemes associated to
f
, which are defined in the ordered Hilbert schemes

for

[

[

]

\bullet\;\;

Curvilinear multiple-point theory
(Kleiman
[

,
Kl90
]
, Colley
[
Co87
]
, Ran
[
Ra85
]
):

The principle is strongly supported by the multiple-point theory for an important particular class of
curvilinear maps
, that is, maps

with
\kappa\geq 0
and corank at most one; its singularity type is denoted by
A_{\mu}
, i.e., the local ring is isomorphic to

, and an

-tuple of them is denoted by

. That has been established by Kleiman and others around the 80s, where

[
Kl81
,
Kl90
]
.
In this case, moduli spaces under consideration become non-singular, and thus concrete computations could be done.
For instance, there is a recursive formula for
m_{A_{0}^{r}}
(multiple-point formulas) by Kleiman’s iteration method and
Colley’s program produces
m_{A_{\bullet}}
of low codimensions.
Note that those formulas are only applicable to curvilinear maps, i.e., they give wrong answers for non-curvilinear maps.

\bullet\;\;

Tautological integrals over curvilinear Hilbert schemes
(Bérczi-Szenes
[

]
):

The target multiple-point formula for generic maps (including non-curvilinear maps) has been established. That corresponds to the formula
(ttp)
for the type
\underline{\eta}=A_{0}^{r}
where

is a proper locally stable map. Their method relies on torus equivariant localization technique – they use iterated residue integrals with partial desingularization based on the curvilinear Hilbert schemes and non-reductive group quotient

, that has been developed as a byproduct from their main project for computing Thom polynomials for

-singualrities (Theorem

).

\bullet\;\;

Functorial approach using algebraic cobordism
(Ohmoto
[
Oh24
]
):

In contrast to the approaches above, this is a non-constructive approach which proves the existence of universal expression
(ttp)
, and partly
(stp)
, in the Thom-Kazarian principle. In particular, the results ensure the correctness of many formulas counting multi-singular points which are obtained via the interpolation under assuming the principle, including the quadruple-point formula of Marangell-Rimányi
[

]
for generic maps.
Given a multi-singularity type
\underline{\eta}=(\eta_{1},\cdots,\eta_{r})
, the
source and target multi-singularity loci classes

m_{\underline{\eta}}(f)
and

are defined for
arbitrary
proper maps
f:M\to N
by using intersection theory on relative Hilbert schemes so that they coincide with the previously defined ones in (

is locally stable. Then, the existence theorem of target multi-singularity Thom polynomials
(ttp)
for the loci class

has been proven, by developing an idea of Kazarian
[
Kaz03b
]
in the context of algebraic geometry – a main feature of the proof is a striking use of algebro-geometric

, established by A. Vishik
[

]
(the universality of Landweber-Novikov operations), on algebraic cobordism theory

of Levine-Morel
[

Namely, it turns out that

’s after tensoring by

.
On the other hand, due to some technical difficulty, the existence of the source multi-singularity Thom polynomials

has been shown, up to now, only in a certainly nice case, i.e., for proper locally stable maps

with

plus some additional conditions – the proof relies on the existence theorem

and the interpolation method of Rimányi.
For finding an entire proof of the Thom-Kazarian principle for

, most critical is that the classifying space of multi-singularities has not yet been found properly.
That suggests, following Kazarian’s view
[

]
, to build a bridge between Thom polynomial theory and motivic homotopy theory.

6.2.
Higher Thom polynomials for multi-singularity types

It is natural to expect the Thom-Kazarian principle also holds for the SSM class of the multi-singularity locus of prescribed type, that is, we conjecture that there is a universal polynomial (series)
tp^{{\rm SM}}(\overline{\underline{\eta}})
in variables
c_{i}
’s and
s_{I}
’s satisfying

s^{{\rm SM}}(1\hskip-3.5pt1_{\overline{\underline{\eta}(f)}},M)=tp^{{\rm SM}}(\overline{\underline{\eta}})(c(f))

in
H^{*}(M;\mathbb{Q})
evaluated by
c_{i}=c_{i}(f)
and
s_{I}=f^{*}s_{I}(f)
for any proper locally stable maps
f
.
The leading term of the SSM class is the fundamental class
[\overline{\underline{\eta}(f)}]
, thus it is expressed
by the reduced multi-singularity Thom polynomial, namely,

tp^{{\rm SM}}(\overline{\underline{\eta}})(c(f))=tp(\underline{\eta})(c(f))+h.o.t.

By definition,
c(TM)\cdot tp^{{\rm SM}}(\overline{\underline{\eta}})(c(f))
coincides with the CSM class
C_{*}(1\hskip-3.5pt1_{\overline{\underline{\eta}(f)}})
.
Multiply the both sides of the equality by
\bar{c}(f)=c(f)^{-1}=c(TM-f^{*}TN)
and then apply the pushforward
f_{*}
,
we see

s^{{\rm SM}}(f_{*}(1\hskip-3.5pt1_{\overline{\underline{\eta}(f)}}),M)=f_{*}(\bar{c}(f)\cdot tp^{{\rm SM}}(\overline{\underline{\eta}})(c(f)))

by using the naturality
f_{*}C_{*}=C_{*}f_{*}
of the CSM class transformation, the definition of SSM class and the projection formula.
Notice that this total cohomology class in the target space
N
is written in terms of the Landweber-Novikov classes
s_{I}
.

Recall the adjacency relation of multi-singularities both in source and target:
the arrow
\underline{\eta}\to\underline{\xi}
means that the stratum of type
\underline{\xi}
is contained in the closure of the stratum of
\underline{\eta}
. That yields the set of all multi-singularity types to be a poset (partially ordered set), and we call it the
multi-singularity poset
. Note that this makes sense for each of the source and the target spaces according to the definition of loci
\underline{\eta}(f)
and
f(\underline{\eta}(f))
for locally stable maps
f
; in the source space, the first entry
\eta_{1}
is distinguished from the others, and in the target space, the order of all entries is ignorable.

For instance, the Hasse diagram of the source multi-singularity poset in case of
\kappa=0

A reduced divisor

As for the CSM class of a free divisor

the following equality was conjectured by P. Aluffi,

Theorem 6.4

(
CSM class of free divisors

[

If

Some topics in applications

7.1.
Application to enumerative geometry – instanton counting

The literature on enumerative geometry contain full of counting formulas, including 19th century classics as well as modern ones inspired by mathematical physics and applied algebraic geometry.
Thom polynomial theory serves as a kind of
taxonomy
or
systematics
to re-organize these counting formulas and discover new species.

Here we present a typical example in this direction – the enumeration of singular curves on surfaces – according to Kazarian
[
Kaz03b
, §10]
. This classical topic has been resurrected since the 90s due to new interest from string theory, known as
instanton counting
.
Consider the linear system
|\mathcal{L}|=\mathbb{P}(H^{0}(S;\mathcal{L}))
associated to a line bundle
\mathcal{L}
on a projective non-singular complex surface
S
.
Let
\underline{\eta}=(\eta_{1},\cdots,\eta_{r})
be a combination of types of plane curve singularities
(\mathbb{C}^{2},0)\to(\mathbb{C},0)
, e.g., the type of
r
nodes denoted by
A_{1}^{r}=(A_{1},\cdots,A_{1})
, and put
n
to be the expected codimension of the target multi-singularity locus of type
\underline{\eta}
(e.g., it is
r
for
A_{1}^{r}
with
\kappa<0
).
Then, a general problem is to determine the number of singular curves in this system which have singular points of the prescribed type
\underline{\eta}
and pass through
\dim|\mathcal{L}|-n
common points of
S
in general position.

The problem is interpreted as counting
\underline{\eta}
-type multi-singular points of a certain map. Let
N=\mathbb{P}^{n}
be a generic linear subspace of
|\mathcal{L}|
which is transverse to the discriminant. Take the trivial fibration
p:N\times S\to N
and the incident hypersurface
\iota:M\hookrightarrow N\times S
with the normal bundle
I
, i.e.,
M
consists of the pairs of a curve in the family and a point lying on this curve. Now
\dim M=n+1
and
\dim N=n
(thus
\kappa=-1
).
Let
f=p\circ\iota:M\to N
be the composed map, that is the family of curves over the sub-linear system
N
, and put
L=\Sigma^{2}(f)
, the critical locus of
f
(recall the setup in §
5.1
).

Now we can apply target Thom polynomials for
n_{\underline{\eta}}
to this map
f:M\to N
, since the existence theorem of the universal polynomials has been established in
[
Oh24
]
.
In this geometric setting, as remarked in §
5.1
, the quotient Chern class
c(f)
is rewritten in terms of Legendre characteristic classes in
\mathcal{L}
, so we state the computation in this terminology following
[
Kaz03b
]
.

Let
h=c_{1}(\mathcal{L})\in H^{2}(S)
,
1+c_{1}+c_{2}=c(T^{*}S)
, and let
t
be the divisor class in
N=\mathbb{P}^{\ell}
. Then

u=c_{1}(I)=[M]=t+h,\;\;a=c(T^{*}S\otimes I-TS)=\frac{(1+u)^{2}+(1+u)c_{1}+c_{2}}{1-c_{1}+c_{2}}.

The pushforward
f_{*}:H^{*}(L)\to H^{*}(N)
is factorized by
f_{*}=p_{*}i_{*}
, where
i:L\to N\times S
is the embedding and
p:N\times S\to N
is the projection to the first factor.
The class
[L]=i_{*}(1)
is easily computed, and the homomorphism
p_{*}
commutes with multiplication by
t
and sends monomials of degree
2
in the classes
h,c_{1},c_{2}
to the numbers;

d:=p_{*}(h^{2})=\mathcal{L}\cdot\mathcal{L}
,

k:=p_{*}(hc_{1})=\mathcal{L}\cdot K_{S}
,

s:=p_{*}(c^{2}_{1})=K_{S}\cdot K_{S}
and

x:=p_{*}(c_{2})=\chi(S)
.

Since the target singularity locus of type
\underline{\eta}
has codiemsion
n
,
there are finitely many singular curves having isolated singular points of type
\underline{\eta}
which belong to the family
f
.
Applying the established Thom-Kazarian principle, the degree
\int n_{\underline{\eta}}(f)
expresses the number of those singular curves; especially, for any
J\subset\underline{\eta}
, the residual class
S_{J}=f_{*}R_{J}\in H^{*}(\mathbb{P}^{n})
is a linear combination of the numbers
d,k,s,x
multiplied by
t^{n_{J}}
(
n_{J}
is the expected codimension of the locus of type
J
), thus the desired degree is written by a polynomial in
d,k,s,x
. That is summarized as follows.

Theorem 7.1
.

[
Kaz03b
]

Let
\underline{\eta}=(\eta_{1},\cdots,\eta_{r})
be a multi-singularity type of plane curve-germs with
\mathcal{K}_{e}
-codimension
n
. Then it holds that for any projective surface with an ample line bundle
\mathcal{L}
and any sufficiently generic linear sub-system
\mathbb{P}^{n}\subset|\mathcal{L}|
, the number

of singular curves belonging to the system that possess the combination of singularities of type
\underline{\eta}
is given by

and

.

A generating function of the numbers

is also formulated
[
Kaz03b
]
.
Theorem
7.1
for the simplest type

solves the problem on the existence of universal polynomials for counting nodal curves, known as part of the

[
Go98
,

,

(now several different proofs are available, e.g.,
[

,

,

).
Like as this, there are a lot of questions for which Thom polynomial theory may effectively give satisfactory answers. Other examples are:

-

higher dimensional analogy to Göttsche conjecture, i.e., counting divisors with a prescribed combination of singularities in a given linear system;

-

other type of contact problems, e.g.,
enumerating multi-tangent hyperplanes to a given hypersurface in projective space etc.;

-

computing the degree of bifurcation sets, e.g., event surfaces and its boundary strata when viewing a curve and a surface in

-space (partly related to Computer Vision);

and so on, see, e.g., Vainsencher

, Tzeng

and references therein.

7.2.
Vanishing topology of map-germs

As a well-known theorem due to J. Milnor, the Milnor fiber of an isolated hypersurface-singularity
f:(\mathbb{C}^{d+1},0)\to(\mathbb{C},0)
is homotopic to a bouquet of
d
-dimensional real spheres; the number
\mu(f)
of the spheres is called the
Milnor number
of the germ
f
.
The number is interpreted as the number of
A_{1}
(Morse)-singular points appearing in a stable perturbation of the germ
f
, and also it is computed by the dimension of the Milnor algebra:

\mu(f)=\#A_{1}(f_{t})=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}^{m},0}/J_{f}.

This picture is generalized to the case of map-germs.
For the simplicity, we assume that
m\leq n
throughout this subsection.
Let
f:(\mathbb{C}^{m},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)
be a
\mathcal{G}
-finitely determined map-germ, and
\underline{\eta}
a stable (mono/multi-)singularity type of the expected codimension
n
in the target.
Suppose that there is a
stable perturbation

f_{t}:U\to\mathbb{C}^{n}\;\;(t\in\Delta\subset\mathbb{C},\;0\in U\subset\mathbb{C}^{m})

so that
f_{0}
is a representative of the germ
f
and
f_{t}
for
t\not=0
is a stable map.
Note that if
\eta
is a mono-singularity type, it is enough to assume that
f
is
\mathcal{K}
-finitely determined, while for a multi-singularity type, we need the
\mathcal{A}
-finiteness of
f
. Then
\underline{\eta}(f_{t})
for
t\not=0
consists of finitely many isolated points;
the number is constant for non-zero
t
and does not depend on the choice of
stable perturbation, and it is usually called a
0
-stable invariant
of the original germ
f
.
We let
\#\underline{\eta}(f_{t})
denote the number of (multi-)singular points in the
target space
,
e.g.,
\#A_{0}^{3}(f_{t})
(
\kappa>0
) means the triple-points in the image of a stable perturbation
f_{t}
of
f_{0}
.

There have been several works for computing
0
-stable invariants via colengths of some algebras under certainly nice situations.
In general, however, it can be a hard task e.g., in case that the singularity scheme is not Cohen-Macaulay, that often arises.

Now we propose a new topological method localizing Thom polynomials
to compute stable invariants for
quasi-homogeneous
map-germs.
This new approach provides a number of examples with significantly simpler computation where any corank condition is not assumed.

Let
f:(\mathbb{C}^{m},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)
be a quasi-homogeneous germ
with weights
w_{1},\cdots,w_{m}
and degrees
d_{1},\cdots,d_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, i.e.,
there are diagonal representations
\rho_{0},\rho_{1}
of
T=\mathbb{C}^{*}
in the source and the target space, respectively, such that
f=\rho_{1}(t)\circ f\circ\rho_{0}^{-1}(t)\;(t\in T)
.
Suppose that
f
is
\mathcal{A}
-finitely determined.
Then its
\mathcal{A}_{e}
-versal unfolding
F:(\mathbb{C}^{m+k},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{n+k},0)

is also quasi-homogeneous, so let
r_{1},\cdots,r_{k}
be the weights of unfolding parameters.
We introduce vector bundles over
BT=\mathbb{P}^{\infty}

E_{0}:=\oplus_{i=1}^{m}\mathcal{O}(w_{i}),\quad E_{1}:=\oplus_{j=1}^{n}\mathcal{O}(d_{j}),\quad E^{\prime}=\oplus_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{O}(r_{i}),

and we have maps between total spaces of these bundles:

\textstyle{E_{0}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f}
\scriptstyle{i_{0}}
\textstyle{E_{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{i_{1}}
\textstyle{\underline{\eta}(F)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{E_{0}\oplus E^{\prime}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{F}
\textstyle{E_{1}\oplus E^{\prime}}
\textstyle{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces F(\underline{\eta}(F))}

Given a singularity type
\underline{\eta}
, we apply the Thom polynomial
tp(\underline{\eta})
for
F

[
Kaz03b
,
Oh24
]
and compute the intersection of
tp(\underline{\eta})(c(F))
with the inclusion map
i_{0}
or
i_{1}
in
H^{*}(BT)=\mathbb{Z}[a]
, that leads to the following theorem.

Theorem 7.2
.

[
Oh16
]

Let
m\leq n
and
let
f:(\mathbb{C}^{m},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)
be
an
\mathcal{A}
-finitely determined quasi-homogeneous map-germ
with weight
w_{i}
and degree
d_{j}
.
Given a stable mono/multi-singularity type
\underline{\eta}
with the expected codimension
n
in the target,
the
0
-stable invariant of
f_{0}
is computed by

\#\underline{\eta}(f_{t})=\frac{tp(\underline{\eta})}{{\deg_{1}\underline{\eta}}\cdot w_{1}\cdots w_{m}}=\frac{tp_{\rm target}(\underline{\eta})}{d_{1}\cdots d_{n}}

where numerators stand for the coefficient of
a^{m}
and
a^{n}

of the source and target Thom polynomials applied to the universal map

f_{0}:E_{0}\to E_{1}
, respectively.

In Example
4.10
in §
4
, we have seen that the Greuel-Hamm formula of the Milnor number for quasi-homogeneous
\mathcal{K}
-finite map-germs with
\kappa\leq 0
(i.e., ICIS) can be interpreted as a torus localization of the higher Thom polynomial
tp^{{\rm SM}}(\mu)
.
As a counterpart in case of
\kappa>0
, let us consider
\mathcal{A}
-finitely determined map-germs
f:(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{n+1},0)
where the pair
(n,n+1)
is in Mather’s nice range.
The image of
f
is a hypersurface with non-isolated singularities –
it is known that the homotopy type of the image of any stably perturbation
f_{t}
is a bouquet of middle dimensional real spheres, whose number is
called the
image Milnor number
:

\mu_{I}(f):=(-1)^{m}(\chi(\mbox{\rm Im}(f_{t}))-1).

The so-called
Mond conjecture
says that the
\mathcal{A}_{e}
-codimension of
f

\leq

\mu_{I}(f)
, with equality if
f
is quasi-homogeneous
[
MN20
]
.
A crucial difficulty lies in how to calculate
\mu_{I}(f)
.
Our method provides a lot of new examples, i.e., we can compute
\mu_{I}
for
\mathcal{A}
-finite quasi-homogeneous germs in low dimensions without any corank condition, by using the same type theorem as above by replacing
tp
by higher Thom polynomial
tp^{{\rm SM}}
for multi-singularities, see
[
Oh16
]
for the detail in case
n=3
.
Furthermore, combining this method with some results on several invariants for
\mathcal{A}
-equivalence,
Pallarés Torres and Peñafort Sanchis
[
PPe21
]
succeeded to obtain formulas for
\mu_{I}(f)
in more cases, e.g.,
n=4
and
5
.
Their results have a significant impact on the study of the Mond conjecture
[
MN20
, §8.8, §8.9]
.

7.3.
Positivity and complex hyperbolic geometry

Recently a surprising connection was discovered by Bérczi
[
Be12
,
Be19a
,
Be19b
,
Be20
,
Be23
]
between the positivity question of coefficients of Thom polynomials of
A_{\mu}
-singularities and higher dimensional versions of the Nevanlinna theory in complex analysis. It was quite unexpected, as there had been no interaction between these two subjects before. The discovery was made possible by a new geometric invariant theory for
non-reductive groups
, established by Bérczi-Kirwan
[
BK24
]
.

In complex analytic geometry, one of fascinating themes is the
hyperbolicity
of a complex projective variety
X
(in the sense of Brody), i.e., any holomorphic curve from
\mathbb{C}
to
X
is constant – the famous Kobayashi conjecture says that a generic hypersurface
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{n+1}
is hyperbolic if
\deg(X)
is sufficiently large. It is known that a generic hypersurface
X
is of general type if
\deg(X)\geq n+3
. As a generalization, the Green-Griffiths-Lang (GGL) conjecture claims that for every complex projective algebraic variety
X
of general type there exists a proper algebraic subvariety of
X
containing all nonconstant entire holomorphic curves
\gamma:\mathbb{C}\to X
. To attack these conjectures, a key construction is a smooth compactification of the bundle of invariant
k
-jet differentials, called the
Demailly-Semple tower
, so that global sections of certain line bundles over it give algebraic differential equations which the curves
\gamma
should satisfy. Then the problem is reduced to the existence of global sections, thus intersection theory comes up here. By this strategy, there have been several attempts to find effective degree bounds for the conjectures in past decades.

A technical difficulty lies on the fact that the group
J^{k}{\rm Aut}(\mathbb{C},0)
of
k
-jets of isomoprhism-germs of
(\mathbb{C},0)
is not reductive, thus
the geometric invariant theory (GIT) of Mumford can not be applied directly. Recently, Bérczi and Kirwan developed
non-reductive
GIT, and as an application, they obtained a different smooth compactification of the bundle of invariant jet differentials using the algebraic model of Bérczi-Szenes
[
BS12
]
for computing the Thom polynomials of
\mathcal{A}_{\mu}
-singularity types (Theorem
3.2
). Namely, the Demailly-Semple tower is replaced by a new fibrewise compactification
\pi:\tilde{X}_{k}\to X
of invariant jet differentials, endowed with a tautological line bundle
\tau
on
\tilde{X}_{k}
, that leads to the discovery/refinement of an iterated residue formula for the tautological integrals.

Theorem 7.3

(Bérczi
[
Be12
,
Be19a
,
Be19b
,
Be20
]
)
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{n+1}
be a smooth projective hypersurface and let
u=c_{1}(\tau)
and
h=c_{1}(\pi^{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(1))
denote the first Chern classes of the tautological line bundle on
\tilde{X}_{\mu}
and the divisor class on
X
, respectively. For any homogeneous
\mu
polynomial
P=P(u,h)
of degree
\deg(P)=\dim\tilde{X}_{\mu}=n+\mu(n-1)
, we have

\int_{\tilde{X}_{\mu}}P=\int_{X}\underset{{\bm{t}}=\infty}{{\rm Res}}\;\frac{\prod_{m<l}(t_{m}-t_{l})Q_{k}(t_{1},\cdots,t_{\mu})P(t_{1}+\cdots+t_{\mu},h)}{\prod_{m+r\leq l\leq\mu}(t_{m}+t_{r}-t_{l})(t_{1}\cdots t_{\mu})^{n}}\prod_{i=1}^{\mu}{\rm S}\hskip-2.0pt\left(\frac{1}{t_{i}}\right)dt_{i}

where
{\rm S}(t^{-1})=1+s_{1}(X)t^{-1}+s_{2}(X)t^{-2}+\cdots+s_{n}(X)t^{-n}
is the total Segre class of
X

and
Q_{\mu}
is the polynomial associated to
A_{\mu}
-singularity type in Theorem
3.2
.

This theorem is very effective for the so-called
polynomial GGL conjecture
. For instance, in
[
Be19a
,
Be19b
]
Bérczi proved the polynomial GGL conjecture modulo the
positivity conjecture
of R. Rimányi, posed at the very beginning
[
Ri01
]
, which claims that there is no negative coefficients in the Chern monomial basis expansion of
tp(A_{\mu})
(this is a different version of positivity from the one in Remark
2.6
).
After improvements by combining it with recent results in complex hyperbolic geometry, finally the following theorems have been obtained:

Theorem 7.4

(Bérczi-Kirwan
[
BK24
]
)
.

(Polynomial Green-Griffiths-Lang theorem for projective hypersurfaces)
Let
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{n+1}
be a generic smooth projective hypersurface of
\deg(X)\geq 16n^{3}(5n+4)
. Then there is a proper algebraic subvariety
Y\subset X
containing all nonconstant entire holomorphic curves in X.

Theorem 7.5

(Bérczi-Kirwan
[
BK24
]
)
.

(Polynomial Kobayashi theorem)

A generic smooth projective hypersurface
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{n+1}
of
\deg(X)\geq 16(2n-1)^{3}(10n-1)
is Brody hyperbolic.

These results have made considerable impact in both (mutually far) communities of singularity theory and complex geometry.
The connecting point between two subjects is intersection theory on Hilbert schemes and non-reductive GIT.
That also indicates a new future direction for researches in Thom polynomial theory.

8.
Real singularities

We have discussed Thom polynomials in complex analytic/algebraic category, but it makes sense for real
C^{\infty}
-category as well – e.g., the desingularization method in §
2.4
commonly works in both categories
[
AGLV93
]
. There are many related topics in differential topology, but here we focus mainly on a general statement of the existence of real Thom polynomials
tp^{\mathbb{R}}(\eta)
, i.e., the counterpart to Theorem
2.4
in the setting of real singularities. Actually, real singularities have their own particular features caused by a finer classification of
C^{\infty}
map-germs, real (semi-)algebraic/analytic properties, (co-)orientation problem and complex conjugacy.

8.1.
Vassiliev complex and real Thom polynomials

The classification machinery (with respect to
\mathcal{K}
and
\mathcal{A}
-equivalence) is the same as the complex case (§
2.1
), but the real case is more subtle and the classification list becomes larger than the complex case; for instance, there are two different
\mathcal{K}
-orbits with
\kappa=0
,

I_{2,2}:(x^{2}+y^{2},xy)
and
I\hskip-1.5ptI_{2,2}:(x^{2}-y^{2},xy)
as real map-germs,
but they are the same in complex case.

Our first task is to define the singularity loci class of prescribed type in (Borel-Moore) homology for real
C^{\infty}
-maps, that is not straightforward at all.
Put
V:=J^{k}(m,n)
and
G:=J^{k}\mathcal{G}
, the real algebraic group of
k
-jets of elements in the equivalence group in real case.
Note that
G
is homotopy equivalent to the product of orthgonal groups
O(m)\times O(n)
. Since the
G
-action on
V
is real algebraic, every
G
-orbit
\eta
(or modulus of
G
-orbits) in
V
is a locally-closed semi-algebraic subset, and its closure
\overline{\eta}
in fine topology is also semi-algebraic in general. A semi-algebraic set may not have the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-fundamental cycle in the Borel-Moore homology, while any real algebraic set does
[
BCR98
, Prop. 11.3.1]
. Also we may ask for the fundamental cycle with
\mathbb{Z}
-coefficients, that is related to the existence of
G
-invariant orientation of the normal bundle of
\eta
(called the
G
-coorientation
of
\eta
). In this case, we may replace
\mathcal{G}
by the one preserving the orientations of the source and the target space, then
G
is homotopy equivalent to the product of rotation groups
SO(m)\times SO(n)
. For instance, each orbit of
I_{2,2}
and
I\hskip-1.5ptI_{2,2}
(
\kappa=0
) does not have the fundamental cycle, while the closure of the union of the two orbits forms a real algebraic set, so it has the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-fundamental cycle. Moreover, both orbits are
G
-coorientable and the union admits a
\mathbb{Z}
-fundamental cycle.

Let us introduce a general framework.
Suppose that we are given a
G
-invariant semi-algebraic Whitney stratification of
V
. Let
\Gamma_{p}
denote the union of strata of codimension
\geq p
and put
V_{p}:=V\smallsetminus\Gamma_{p+1}
.
Then we have a filtration of invariant open sets

V_{0}\subset V_{1}\subset V_{2}\subset\cdots\subset V.

It yields the
G
-equivariant spectral sequence
E_{*}^{*,*}
, which converges to
H_{G}^{*}(V)=H^{*}(BG)
with the coefficient ring
R
(e.g.,
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
or
\mathbb{Z}
).
The
E_{1}
-term is

E_{1}^{p,q}:=H_{G}^{p+q}(V_{p},V_{p-1};R)\simeq H_{G}^{q}(\Gamma_{p}\smallsetminus\Gamma_{p+1};R).

In particular,
E_{1}^{p,0}
is freely generated by the strata of codimension
p
(and
G
-coorientable if
R=\mathbb{Z}
), and

\delta:E_{1}^{p,0}\to E_{1}^{p+1,0}
is the connecting homomorphism of the long exact sequence for the triple
(V_{p+1},V_{p},V_{p-1})
, which is determined by analyzing the adjacency relation among strata. The cochain complex
(E_{1}^{*,0},\delta)
is called the
Vassiliev complex
(with coefficients in
R
) associated to the stratification
[
Va88
,
AGLV93
,
Kaz98
]
. Intuitively, a
p
-dimensional cohomology class
\omega
of this cochain complex means a geometric cycle in
V
defined by a linear combination of strata of codimension
p
, say
\omega=[\sum n_{i}\eta_{i}]
(
n_{i}\in R
), and thus it defines an element of
H_{G}^{p}(V)=H^{p}(BG;R)
. This element should be the ‘real Thom polynomial’ associated to the class
\omega
for
C^{\infty}
-maps.
Originally, such an abstract complex was introduced and computed for the classification of function-germs by Vassiliev in his study on Lagrange and Legendre Thom polynomials
[
Va88
,
AGLV93
]
, see §
5.1
.
For
\mathcal{K}
-classification of
C^{\infty}
-map-germs with
\kappa=0
up to the codimension
8
, the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-Vassiliev complex has been determined in Ohmoto
[
Oh94
]
, and the
\mathbb{Z}
-Vassiliev complex, which is much more difficult, has been computed in Fehér-Rimányi
[
FR02
]
using the interpolation method partly.

For
\mathcal{K}
-classification, the stabilization property (Lemma
2.3
, as
m\to\infty
with
\kappa
fixed) also holds in real case, that leads to the following theorem. Note that

\textstyle H^{*}(BO;\mathbb{Z}_{2})=\mathbb{Z}_{2}[w_{1},w_{2},\cdots],\quad H^{*}(BSO;\mathbb{Z}[\frac{1}{2}])=\mathbb{Z}[\frac{1}{2}][p_{1},p_{2},\cdots]

where
w_{i}
and
p_{i}
are the Stiefel-Whitney class and the Pontrjagin class, respectively.

Theorem 8.1
.

[
Th55
,
Da72
,
Oh94
,
Kaz98
,
FR02
]
.
Fix
\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}
and consider
\mathcal{K}
-classification of
C^{\infty}
-map-germs
(\mathbb{R}^{*},0)\to(\mathbb{R}^{*+\kappa},0)
. Let
\omega=[\sum\eta_{i}]
be a
p
-dimensional cohomology class for the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-Vassiliev complex. Then there exists a unique polynomial
tp^{\mathbb{R}}_{2}(\omega)\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}[w_{1},w_{2},\cdots]
such that for any appropriately generic
C^{\infty}
-map
f:M^{m}\to N^{m+\kappa}
,
the closure of the union of
\eta_{i}(f)
, denoted by
S_{\omega}(f)
, forms a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-geometric cycle in
M
and
is expressed by

{\rm Dual}_{2}[S_{\omega}(f)]=tp^{\mathbb{R}}_{2}(\eta)(w(f))\;\;\in H^{p}(M;\mathbb{Z}_{2})

evaluated by the Stiefel-Whitney class
w_{i}=w_{i}(f)=w_{i}(f^{*}TN-TM)
.
Also for a cohomology class
\omega
for the
\mathbb{Z}
-Vassiliev complex, a similar statement holds:
for any generic map
f:M^{m}\to N^{m+\kappa}
between orientable manifolds, the locus
S_{\omega}(f)
forms a
\mathbb{Z}
-cycle in
M
and is expressed by a universal polynomial
tp^{\mathbb{R}}(\omega)
in Pontrjagin classes
p_{i}=p_{i}(f)=p_{i}(f^{*}TN-TM)
modulo
2
-torsion elements.

We remark that the interpolation method does not work effectively for computing real mod.2 Thom polynomials
tp^{\mathbb{R}}_{2}
, because the restriction equations mod.2 often become trivial (but see Remark
8.2
below). Computing real integer Thom polynomials
tp^{\mathbb{R}}
in
p_{i}
’s is a more difficult problem.

Remark 8.2
.

(Borel-Haefliger theorem)

As mentioned above, the union of
\mathcal{K}
-orbits of type
I_{2,2}
and
I\hskip-1.5ptI_{2,2}
with
\kappa=0
forms a cycle of the
\mathbb{Z}
-Vassiliev complex; actually its closure in
V
coincides with the Thom-Boardman stratum
\overline{\Sigma^{2}}
. Hence, from results by desingularization (which works in both real and complex cases)
[
Po71
,
Ro72
]
, the integer Thom polynomial in the orientable setting is given as

tp^{\mathbb{R}}(I_{2,2}+I\hskip-1.5ptI_{2,2})=tp^{\mathbb{R}}(\Sigma^{2})=p_{1}

while forgetting the orientation, the mod.2 Thom polynomial is

tp^{\mathbb{R}}_{2}(I_{2,2}+I\hskip-1.5ptI_{2,2})=tp^{\mathbb{R}}_{2}(\Sigma^{2})=w_{2}^{2}+w_{1}w_{3}

(this is consistent; if
M,N
are orientable, then
w_{1}=w_{1}(f^{*}TN-TM)=0
and the mod.2 reduction of
p_{1}
gives
w_{2}^{2}
).
Also the Thom polynomial for complex singularity type
I_{2,2}
with
\kappa=0
is

tp(I_{2,2})=tp(\Sigma^{2})=c_{2}^{2}+c_{1}c_{3}.

Note that replacing
c_{i}
in
tp(I_{2,2})
by
w_{i}
, we recover
tp^{\mathbb{R}}_{2}(I_{2,2}+I\hskip-1.5ptI_{2,2})
.
This is generically true. Namely, if a
\mathcal{K}
-orbit
\eta_{\mathbb{C}}
of complex map-germs admits several real forms
\eta_{i}
such that their complexification is
\eta_{\mathbb{C}}
(including the case of a single real form
\eta
), then it defines a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-Vassiliev cycle
\omega=[\sum\eta_{i}]
,
and the mod.2 Thom polynomial
tp^{\mathbb{R}}_{2}(\omega)
is obtained from
tp(\eta_{\mathbb{C}})
by replacing
c_{i}\mapsto w_{i}
and by taking coefficients mod.2 (Borel-Haefliger
[
BH61
]
and also Matszangosz
[
Mts19
]
).

8.2.
Global topology of
C^{\infty}
maps – Miscellaneous

A variant of Vassilev complex for
\mathcal{A}
-classification of multi-singularities of maps
has been introduced for finding topological invariants for stable maps in low dimensions, called
local first-order Vassilev-type invariants
, e.g., Arnold’s
J^{\pm}
invariants for plane immersed curves
[
Ar94
]
, Goryunov’s invariants for stable maps from a surface into
\mathbb{R}^{3}

[
Go97
]
, and many others (see
[
OA06
,
Oh12c
]
and references therein). Putting finer semi-global data for mappings under consideration, an
enriched version
of the Vassiliev complex has been considered in Saeki’s studies on topology of
4
-manifolds via stable maps into low dimensional space
[
Sa04
,
SY07
]
. That is also related to Gromov’s complexity of
C^{\infty}
-maps.
One of central problems in global topology of
C^{\infty}
maps is elimination of singularities of prescribed type – historically it arose in connection with the Poincaré conjecture. In this context, the h-principle and cobordism of singular maps take important roles, where the real Thom polynomial of
\eta
is regarded as the primary obstruction for removing
\eta
-singular points of generic
C^{\infty}
maps
f:M\to N
via homotopy or surgery, see e.g.,
[
AGLV93
,
RS98
,
Sz08
,
SSS10
]
.
Also ‘relative Thom polynomials’ for singular Seifert maps have long been considered implicitly (cf.
[
Tn25
]
); e.g., Takase
[
Ta12
]
has studied oriented bordisms of immersions of
7
-manifolds to
\mathbb{R}^{8}
in relation to Thom polynomials
tp^{\mathbb{R}}
with integer coefficients given in
[
FR02
]
.
For an immersion from a
C^{\infty}
manifold
L
into an almost complex manifold
M
, there are enumerative formulas for complex tangent points or
\mathbb{R}\mathbb{C}
singularities
[
La72
]
, and elimination problem for complex tangents has recently been discussed
[
El15
,
KT18
]
.
For a singular Lagrange immersion of
L
into a symplectic manifold
M
equipped with almost complex structure, there are Thom polynomials for open Whitney umbrellas
[
Oh96
]
.
Those singularities and complex tangents are characterized by (higher) intrinsic derivatives of the
C^{\infty}
-bundle map
TL\otimes\mathbb{C}\to TM
, and hence the Thom polynomials may be written in terms of
c_{i}(f^{*}TM-TL\otimes\mathbb{C})
.
Higher mod.2 Thom polynomials in real case (i.e., real
Z_{2}
-counterpart to SSM Thom polynomials) should be based on D. Sullivan’s Stiefel-Whitney classes for real algebraic varieties
[
Su71
]
. There had been only a few related works for real Morin maps, such as Fukuda
[
Fk88
]
and Nakai
[
Na00
]
, but it has potential for further researches, e.g., a recent work of Fehér-Matszangosz
[
FM25
]
.

There is still a lot of room for further researches on real Thom polynomials (especially in
K
-theory, cobordism theory, Chow-Witt groups, and so on), although the story has a long history since 1950s. Unfortunately several interesting topics are dropped and references cited here may be inadequate, thus readers are advised to directly refer to appropriate literature.

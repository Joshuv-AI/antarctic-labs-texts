Perturbation Effects on Word Lengths in Three-Reflection Symmetric Presentations of Dihedral Groups
Michael P. Allocca, Max F. Trimmer

Abstract
. We investigate the properties of word lengths of elements from a three-reflection symmetric generating set of the dihedral group D n D_{n} . More specifically, we provide upper bounds for a quantity λ 1 \lambda_{1} , which measures the stability of a group under perturbations in the words corresponding to certain elements with respect to specific presentations. Keywords: Dihedral Groups, Group Presentations, Word Length, Additive Combinatorics



1.
Introduction

Given a finite group,
G
, a symmetric generating set of
G
is a subset
S\subseteq G
where any element of
G
can be written as a product of elements in
S
(e.g. as a ‘word’),
e\notin S
, and for all
s_{i}\in S
,
s_{i}^{-1}\in S
. Using this, we can establish a notion of ‘word length’ of an element
g\in G
as the number of generators in a minimal expression of g. Further, the requirement that
s_{i}^{-1}\in S
ensures that the length of each element and its inverse are always equal.

Recently, these notions have been explored in the context where
G=D_{n}
, the dihedral group of order
2n
, and
S
is a symmetric generating set with two or three elements. In
[
1
]
, all possible symmetric presentations of dihedral groups generated by sets with two or three elements were defined. The latter was a novel classification that explored the relationship between word lengths and whether each of the three elements of
S
are rotations or reflections, which provided further insight to the properties of a recently-established measure of a group’s ‘stability’ under
S
.

For any element
g\in G
, we define the length of
g
with respect to
S
,
l_{S}(g)
, to be the minimal number of elements of
S
(e.g. ‘letters’) for which
g
can be written as a product of these elements. That is,
l_{S}(g)\in\mathbb{N}
corresponds to a minimal word length expression of
g
by elements in
S
. Further,
l_{S}(g)=0\Leftrightarrow g=e
. Using these definitions, the following quantity has been of interest following the work in
[
2
]
:

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(G,S)

\displaystyle:=\max_{g\in G,\ s\in S}\{l_{S}(gsg^{-1})\}

Intuitively, this quantity measures the effect of a deletion of one letter on word lengths of elements in
G
(e.g. the so-called “butterfly effect”). Alternatively, one may view this as a measure of ‘sensitivity’ to paths along a Cayley graph following the omission of one generator. This can be viewed as a group theoretic analog to stability in nonlinear dynamical systems. It also plays a significant role in the application of group theory to computational genomics. Subsequently,
\lambda_{1}(G,S)
has been well-studied in the context where
G=S_{n}
, the symmetric group of order
n!
, and
S
is the usual symmetric generating set consisting of transpositions of adjecent elements. In the context where
G=D_{n}
, upper bounds for
\lambda_{1}
were established for all cases where
S
has three elements, except when all of these elements are reflections
[
1
]
. In the three-reflection case, where
r
denotes the usual order
n
rotation and
f
is the involution element representing the standard reflection in
D_{n}
, the following was proved:

Theorem 1
.

Consider the dihedral group
D_{n}
with
n\geq 2
. Then there exists a generating set of the form
S=\{f,r^{a}f,r^{b}f\}
such that
\lambda_{1}(D_{n},S)\leq\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor+1
, where
a\neq b
and
a,b\geq 1
.

Although this established the
existence
of a three-reflection generating set for which the stated bound on
\lambda_{1}(G,S)
is met, in
[
1
]
it was also conjectured that this serves as a bound for
all
symmetric generating sets consisting of three reflections as follows.

Conjecture 1
.

For a generating set
S
composed of three involutions, none of which belong to the order
n
cyclic subgroup of
D_{n}
, we conjecture that
\lambda_{1}(D_{n},S)\leq\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor+1
.

In this paper, we will prove this conjecture and, more significantly, introduce an alternative strategy of studying
\lambda_{1}(G,S)
using techniques in additive combinatorics. We will also show how 3-reflection presentations can be used to write elements of the Dihedral group with shorter word length than is possible for presentations containing two generators.

2.
A bound for prime
n

We first aim to establish a smaller bound than the one listed in Conjecture
1
in the special case where
n
is prime, subject to a few restrictions on what
S
can be. Following the notation introduced in Section
1
, let
W_{l}
be the set of elements of
D_{n}
that can be written as words of length
l
by elements of
S
. Given that
D_{n}=\{e,r,r^{2},\cdots r^{n-1},f,rf,r^{2}f,\cdots r^{n-1}f\}
, we adopt the standard nomenclature in considering the
n
elements of the form
r^{i}
to be all of the rotations and the
n
elements of the form
r^{i}f
to be all of the reflections, where the
n
rotations form a cyclic subgroup generated by
r
. Further information can be found in
[
3
]
regarding dihedral groups, which can also be seen as a special class of Coxeter groups
[
4
]
. Unless otherwise specified,
S=\{f,r^{a}f,r^{b}f\}
is a symmetric generating set consisting of three reflections. Note that
S
is equivalent to the generating set for any three-reflection presentation,
S^{\prime}=\{r^{i}f^{\prime},r^{j}f^{\prime},r^{k}f^{\prime}\}
, by simply labeling the first reflection in the set
f
. That is, it will suffice to consider
S
as defined above because the results in this paper will apply to
S^{\prime}
by letting
f=r^{i}f^{\prime},a=j-i,
and
b=k-i
.

Lemma 1
.

Any element of
D_{n}
can be written as a word of odd length (with respect to
S
) if and only if it is a reflection. Any element can be written as a word of even length if and only if it is a rotation.

Proof.

Let
x\in D_{n}
and suppose it can be written as a word of odd length,
l
, with respect to
S
. Then it may be written as a product of
l-1
reflections and a single reflection. Since
l-1
is even, the latter product is a rotation. So
x
is the product of a rotation and a reflection, which is a reflection. Now suppose
x
is not a reflection. Then it is a rotation. Since
S
consists entirely of reflections, if
x\neq e
, then
x
must be the product of an even number of reflections in
S
, otherwise it would be a rotation. Therefore, by contraposition, if
x
is a reflection, then it can be written as a word of odd length.

Hence,
x
an be written as a word of odd length if and only if it is a reflection. Similarly,
x
can be written as a word of even length if and only if it is a rotation.
∎

Note that this implies that an element cannot be written as both a word of even length and a word of odd length as that would imply that the element is both a rotation and a reflection.

We will adopt sumset notation following
[
5
]
. For two subsets
A
and
B
of an additive group
(G,+)
, we define
A+B=\{a+b\;|\;a\in A,b\in B\}
and
A-B=\{a+(-b)\;|\;a\in A,b\in B\}
where
-b
denotes the inverse of
b
in
G
. Furthermore, for subsets
A^{{}^{\prime}}
and
B^{{}^{\prime}}
of a multiplicative group
(G^{{}^{\prime}},\cdot)
, we define
A^{{}^{\prime}}B^{{}^{\prime}}=\{a\cdot b\;|\;a\in A^{{}^{\prime}},b\in B^{{}^{\prime}}\}
. Unless stated otherwise, addition will take place in a cyclic group and multiplication will take place in a dihedral group. Note that for
l>0
,
W_{l}=W_{l-1}S
, since any word of length
l
can be written as a word of length
l-1
with a letter from
S
appended to it, and a word of length
l-1
with a letter from
S
appended to it is a word of length
l
.

We can exploit the fact that each
W_{l}
exclusively consists of rotations or reflections as follows:
Let
S=\{f,r^{a}f,r^{b}f\}
. For a three-reflection generating set written this way, let
F:D_{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{n}
be defined by
F(r^{i}f^{k})=i
. Note that
F
is not one-to-one, as the images of the rotation subgroup and the reflection coset under
F
are both
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
. However, since
W_{l}
either contains only reflections or only rotations,
|F(W_{l})|=|W_{l}|
for any
l
, where
F(W_{l})
denotes the image of
W_{l}
under
F
. Similarly, since
S
only contains reflections,
|F(S)|=|S|=3
, as
F
simply excises the number of times an element has been ‘rotated’. From here on, we will denote
F(W_{l})
as
W_{l}^{\prime}
and
F(S)
as
S^{\prime}
. Note that
W_{l}^{\prime}\subseteq W_{l+1}^{\prime}
for any
l\geq 0
since
0\in S^{\prime}=\{0,a,b\}
, so for any element
x\in W_{l}^{\prime}
,
x+0=x\in W_{l+1}^{\prime}
.

Lemma 2
.

If
l
is even, then
W_{l+1}^{\prime}=W_{l}^{\prime}+S^{\prime}
. If
l
is odd, then
W_{l+1}^{\prime}=W_{l}^{\prime}-S^{\prime}

Proof.

By Lemma 1, if
l
is even, then
W_{l}
is a subset of the rotation subgroup. Therefore, every element is of the form
r^{x}
for some
0\leq x<n
.
W_{l+1}=W_{l}S=\{r^{x}\cdot r^{k}f\;|\;r^{x}\in W_{l},r^{k}f\in S\}=\{r^{x+k}f\;|\;r^{x}\in W_{l},r^{k}f\in S\}
. Therefore,
W_{l+1}^{\prime}=\{F(r^{x+k}f)\;|\;x\in W_{l}^{\prime},k\in S^{\prime}\}=\{x+k\;|\;x\in W_{l}^{\prime},k\in S^{\prime}\}=W_{l}^{\prime}+S^{\prime}
. Similarly, by Lemma 1, if
l
is odd,
W_{l}
is a set of reflections. Therefore, every element is of the form
r^{k}f
for some
0\leq x<n
.
W_{l+1}=W_{l}S=\{r^{x}f\cdot r^{k}f\;|\;r^{x}\in W_{l},r^{k}f\in S\}=\{r^{x-k}\;|\;r^{x}\in W_{l},r^{k}f\in S\}
. Therefore,
W_{l+1}^{\prime}=\{F(r^{x-k})\;|\;x\in W_{l}^{\prime},k\in S^{\prime}\}=\{x-k\;|\;x\in W_{l}^{\prime},k\in S^{\prime}\}=W_{l}^{\prime}-S^{\prime}
.
∎

One advantage of examining word lengths in the context of sumsets involves the following theorem, which was originally proven by Augustin-Louis Cauchy

Theorem 2

(Cauchy-Davenport)
.

Given a cyclic group
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
of prime order
p
and two subsets
A
and
B
,
|A+B|\geq\text{min}(p,|A|+|B|-1)

We may invoke this as a tool to bound
W_{l}
with a prime modulus.

Lemma 3
.

Let
p
be prime. If
S=\{f,r^{a}f,r^{b}f\}
, and
0,a,b,-a,-b,a-b,b-a
are all distinct modulo
p
, then
|W_{l}|\geq min(3l,p)
with respect to
S
for
l>0

Proof.

We first note that
F(S)-F(S)=\{0,a,b,a-b,b-a,-a,-b\}
. Since we have assumed these are all distinct modulo
p
,
|S-S|=7
. We will show that
|W_{l}|\geq min(3l,p)
for odd
l
by induction on
l
. First note that
|W_{0}|=1\geq 3*0=0
since
W_{0}=\{r^{0}\}
and that
|W_{1}|=3\geq 3*1=3
since
W_{1}=S
. Assume that
|W_{l-2}|\geq min(3(l-2),p)=min(3l-6,p)
, with
l
an odd number greater than 1. By the Cauchy-Davenport theorem and Lemma
2
,
|W_{l}|=|W_{l}^{\prime}|=|W^{\prime}_{l-1}+S|=|W^{\prime}_{l-2}-S+S|=|W^{\prime}_{l-2}-(S-S)|\geq|W^{\prime}_{l-2}|+|S-S|-1\geq min(3l-6,p)+7-1=min(3l,p)

∎

Corollary 1
.

Let
p
be prime. If
S=\{f,r^{a}f,r^{b}f\}
, and
0,a,b,-a,-b,a-b,b-a
are all distinct modulo
n
, then
\lambda_{1}(D_{p},S)\leq\lfloor\frac{x}{3}\rfloor+1

Proof.

First note that since all elements of the form
gsg^{-1}
are reflections,
\lambda_{1}
is less than or equal to the maximum word length required to write a reflection in
D_{n}
using elements of
S
. Since there are
n
reflections and
W_{l}
is composed entirely of reflections for odd
l
,
W_{l}\geq n
implies that
W_{l}
contains all the reflections in
D_{n}
by the pigeonhole principle. This happens when
l>n/3
. Since
l
must be odd to get reflections, we get the lower bound
\lambda_{1}\leq\lfloor\frac{n}{3}\rfloor+1

∎

3.
A bound for all
n

We now aim to provide a full proof of Conjecture
1
for arbitrary
n
. We define the stabilizer of a set
A
in an abelian group
(G,+)
to be
Stab(A)=\{g\in G\,|\,g+A=A\}
. This is defined in
[
5
]
as a symmetry group, although we use the term stabilizer to remain consistent with the nomenclature commonly connected to the following
[
9
]
.

Theorem 3

(Kneser’s)
.

Given an Abelian group
G
and two sets of elements
A
and
B
such that
|A+B|<|A|+|B|
,
|A+B|=|A+\text{Stab(A+B)}|+|B+\text{Stab(A+B)}|-|\text{Stab(A+B)}|

Kneser’s theorem allows us to bound
|W_{l}|
in a useful manner.

Theorem 4
.

|W_{l}|\geq min(2l+1,n)
for
l>0

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
l
.
|W_{1}|=3\geq 2*1+1
. Assume that
W_{l-1}\geq min(2(l-1)+1,n)
. If
l
is odd,
l-1
is even, so by Lemma
2
,
|W_{l}|=|W_{l}^{\prime}|=|W_{l-1}^{\prime}+S^{\prime}|
. Given
S^{\prime}=\{0,a,b\}
, there are 3 cases based on whether
a
and
b
are elements of
\text{Stab}(W_{l}^{\prime})

Case 1
:
a,b\in\text{Stab}(W_{l}^{\prime})

Since
a,b\in\text{Stab}(W_{l}^{\prime})
, and
\text{Stab}(W_{l}^{\prime})
is a group,
-a
and
-b
are also in
\text{Stab}(W_{l}^{\prime})
. Since moving from
W_{k}^{\prime}
to
W_{k+1}^{\prime}
involves either adding or subtracting
S
, and since every element of
S
is in
\text{Stab}(W_{l+1}^{\prime})

W_{k}^{\prime}=W_{l}^{\prime}
for any
k\geq l
. Since
|W_{l}^{\prime}|=|W_{l}|
, this means that either
|W_{l}|=n
, or
S
does not generate
D_{n}
.

Case 2
:
\text{Stab}(W_{l}^{\prime})=\{0\}

By Kneser’s theorem, either
|W_{l}^{\prime}|\geq|W_{l-1}^{\prime}|+|S^{\prime}|\geq 2l-1+3=2l+2
, or
|W_{l}^{\prime}|=|W_{l-1}^{\prime}+\text{Stab}(W_{l}^{\prime})|+|S^{\prime}+\text{Stab}(W_{l}^{\prime})|-|\text{Stab}(W_{l}^{\prime})|=|W_{l-1}^{\prime}|+|S|-1\geq 2l+1
.

Case 3
: Neither
a
nor
b
are elements of
\text{Stab}(W_{l}^{\prime})
, but
\text{Stab}(W_{l}^{\prime})\neq\{0\}

Let
c
be a non-identity element of
\text{Stab}(W_{l}^{\prime})
. Since
c
is in the symmetry set,
W_{l}
is a collection of cosets of
\langle c\rangle
. Since
c
is not the identity, each coset of
\langle c\rangle
has at least 2 elements.

Assume that
|W_{l}^{\prime}|-|W_{l-1}^{\prime}|<2
. (Otherwise
|W_{l}|=|W_{l}^{\prime}|\geq|W_{l-1}^{\prime}|+2=|W_{l-1}|+2\geq min(2(l-1)+1,n)+2\geq min(2l+1,n)
). If
|W_{l}^{\prime}|=|W^{\prime}_{l-1}|
, then since
W_{l-1}^{\prime}\subseteq W_{l}^{\prime}
,
W_{l}^{\prime}=W_{l-1}^{\prime}
. We know that
W^{\prime}_{l-1}+a\subseteq W_{l}^{\prime}
and
W^{\prime}_{l-1}\subseteq W^{\prime}_{l-1}+a
. Therefore since
|W^{\prime}_{l}|=|W^{\prime}_{l+1}|
,
|W^{\prime}_{l-1}+a|=|W^{\prime}_{l}|
. This would imply that
W^{\prime}_{l-1}+a=W^{\prime}_{l}
, contradicting the assumption that
a
is not in the stabilizer.

The only sub-case left is if
|W_{l}^{\prime}|=|W^{\prime}_{l-1}|+1
. Because
W^{\prime}_{l-1}\subseteq W^{\prime}_{l}
and
c\in\text{Stab}(W^{\prime}_{l})
,
W_{l}^{\prime}
is a union of cosets of
\langle c\rangle
and
W^{\prime}_{l-1}
is a union of cosets of
\langle c\rangle
missing some element
x
. Since
a
is not a stabilizer of
W^{\prime}_{l}
, there must be an element of
W^{\prime}_{l}+a
not in
W^{\prime}_{l}
. However, since
W^{\prime}_{l}+a=(W^{\prime}_{l-1}+a)\cup(W^{\prime}_{l}/W^{\prime}_{l-1}+a)=(W^{\prime}_{l-1}+a)\cup\{x+a\}
, this element has to be
x+a
. Since
x+a\notin W^{\prime}_{l}
and
c\in H
, the coset
x+a+\langle c\rangle
is disjoint from
W^{\prime}_{l}
. However, since
c
is not the identity, there is another element
x^{\prime}\in W_{l-1}
in the same coset as
x
. Therefore,

, a contradiction.

Case 4
:
a\in\text{Stab}(W_{l}^{\prime})
or
b\in\text{Stab}(W_{l}^{\prime})
, but not both.

Without loss of generality, assume
a\in\text{Stab}(W_{l}^{\prime})
and
b
is not. For the same reason as the previous case, we can assume
|W^{\prime}_{l}|-|W^{\prime}_{l-1}|<2
. If
|W^{\prime}_{l}|=|W^{\prime}_{l-1}|
, then since
W^{\prime}_{l}\subseteq W^{\prime}_{l-1}
,
W^{\prime}_{l}=W^{\prime}_{l-1}
. This implies that
W^{\prime}_{l-1}+b\subseteq W^{\prime}_{l-1}
since
W^{\prime}_{l-1}+a\subseteq W^{\prime}_{l-1}
and

. Therefore,

, a contradiction. If
|W^{\prime}_{l}-W^{\prime}_{l-1}|=1
, then
W^{\prime}_{l-1}
is a collection of cosets of
\langle a\rangle
missing one element
x
.
W^{\prime}_{l-1}+b\subseteq W^{\prime}_{l}
. Let
x^{\prime}
be an element in the same coset as
x
. Since
x^{\prime}\in W^{\prime}_{l}
and

,
x^{\prime}\in W^{\prime}_{l+1}
. Since
W^{\prime}_{l+1}=W_{l}+\{x\}
and
W^{\prime}_{l-1}+b\subseteq W^{\prime}_{l}
,
x^{\prime}+b\in W^{\prime}_{l}
. Since

and

and

,

. Therefore, either

in a different coset than

|W_{l}|=|W^{\prime}_{l}|\geq min(|W_{l-1}|+2,n)\geq min(2l+1,n)

The cases where

is even are the same, but with

-b
.

Corollary 2
.

For a three-reflection presentation of the dihedral group,

\lambda_{1}(D_{n},S)\leq\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor+1

Proof.

By Theorem
4
, for any three-reflection presentation of
D_{n}
,
|W_{\lceil(n-1)/2\rceil}|\geq min(2*\lceil\frac{n-1}{2}\rceil+1,n)\geq min(n,n)=n
. If
\frac{n-1}{2}
is odd, then
|W_{(n-1)/2}|
is composed entirely of reflections. Since there are
n
reflections in
D_{n}
, every reflection is in
W_{(n-1)/2}
by the pigeonhole principle. If
\lceil\frac{n-1}{2}\rceil
is even, then since
|W_{\lceil(n-1)/2\rceil+1}|
is composed entirely of reflections, and
|W_{\lceil(n-1)/2\rceil+1}|\geq n

W_{\lceil(n-1)/2\rceil+1}
. Therefore, every reflection can be written using

reflections. (Note that the last equality holds since

Given a three-reflection symmetric generating set,

We now shift focus to finding presentations where every reflection can be written using as few words as possible.

Theorem 6
.

Given the Dihedral group
D_{n}
, there is a 3-reflection presentation where every element has word length
O(\sqrt{n})

Proof.

Let
S=\{f,rf,r^{\lfloor\sqrt{n}\rfloor}f\}
.

For any reflection
r^{a}f
, let
b
and
s
be the unique constants such that
s<{\lfloor\sqrt{n}\rfloor}

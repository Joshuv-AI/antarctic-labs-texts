Construction of a risk prediction model to identify potential risk factors for the development of delirium after total hip arthroplasty
Authors: Yong-Chao Cheng
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
The aim was to investigate the factors associated with the development of delirium after total hip arthroplasty and to develop a predictive model. The clinical data of 320 patients who underwent total hip arthroplasty between January 2021 and December 2023 were retrospectively analyzed, and 72 cases were classified as the delirium group and 248 cases were classified as the no delirium group based on the occurrence of delirium after surgery. One-way ANOVA was used to compare the differences in basic information between the 2 groups; statistically significant indicators were included in the binary logistic regression model to analyze in depth the associated factors affecting postoperative delirium after total hip arthroplasty and to construct a prediction framework. Binary logistic regression analysis revealed that age, history of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, femoral neck fracture, duration of surgery, intraoperative bleeding, C-reactive protein level at 4 hours postoperatively, postoperative hypoxemia, resuscitation time (≥1 hour), postoperative pain scores (≥4), nutritional deficits, sleep disorders, and cognitive deficits were all significant risk factors for the occurrence of delirium after total hip arthroplasty significant risk factors for delirium after total hip arthroplasty. The prediction model constructed based on these factors had an overall prediction accuracy of 99.7%, and the Hosmer–Lemeshow test confirmed that the model had a good fit. The area under the curve of the model was 0.992 as internally validated by Bootstrap method, which proved that the accuracy of the constructed model was strong. Age, history of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, femoral neck fracture, duration of surgery, intraoperative bleeding, 4-hour postoperative C-reactive protein level, postoperative hypoxemia, resuscitation time (≥1 hour), postoperative pain score (≥4), nutritional disorders, sleep disorders, and cognitive dysfunctions were risk factors for delirium after total hip arthroplasty, and the risk prediction model constructed based on this demonstrated excellent stability and prediction ability.

1. Introduction

Total hip arthroplasty, which uses artificial materials to replace diseased tissues and recreate the function of the hip joint, is an effective means of treating degenerative diseases of the hip joint, such as osteoarthritis of the hip, necrosis of the femoral head and necrosis of the femoral head.[1–3] Postoperative delirium, which manifests itself as a transient postoperative disorder of the patient’s mental state, includes symptoms such as blurred consciousness, illusions, hallucinations, anxiety, agitation, and behavioral disorders.[4] The emergence of postoperative delirium after total hip arthroplasty involves a variety of complex factors, and the symptoms can be alleviated clinically by sedation therapy and timely application of anxiolytic, hypnotic or antipsychotic drugs.[5] Preventively, the neurological status of patients needs to be comprehensively assessed preoperatively, while psychological support is given to focus on preoperative and postoperative psychological needs.[6] In this study, we analyze the influencing factors of delirium after total hip arthroplasty in depth, construct a prediction model, and evaluate the predictive value of each factor, and we now detail the results of the study.

2. Information and methods

2.1. General information

Applying simple random sampling, patients who underwent total hip replacement surgery in Ya’an hospital of traditional Chinese medicine during the period from January 2021 to December 2023 were selected as the study subjects, and the clinical data of the patients were retrospectively analyzed. Based on the sample size calculation formula n = Z² × [P × (1 ‐ P)]/E², in which P was set to 0.5, E was set to 5%, and the confidence level was 90%, the required sample size was calculated, and the lost-visit samples were also taken into account, and the final sample size was determined to be 320 cases. Inclusion criteria included: (1) patients who met the indications for total hip arthroplasty in Practical Orthopaedics[7] and were treated for the first time; (2) the surgery was performed by the same team of physicians; and (3) patients with complete clinical data. The exclusion criteria covered: (1) patients with severe dysfunction of vital organs; (2) patients with preexisting delirium; (3) patients with abnormal coagulation function; (4) patients with hematologic diseases or systemic infections; (5) patients with psychiatric disorders; and (6) patients with specific diseases such as tumors, tuberculosis, rheumatoid arthritis, bone fracture, and femoral head necrosis. According to the occurrence of postoperative delirium, the study subjects were divided into 72 delirium groups and 248 no delirium groups. The study was a retrospective study and complied with the relevant standards of the Hospital Ethics Committee.

2.2. Methods

During the period from the patients’ recovery from anesthesia to 7 days postoperatively, the following criteria were used to assess delirium[8]: (1) rapid changes or fluctuations in mental status; (2) distraction or difficulty in concentrating; (3) confused thinking; and (4) decreased clarity of consciousness. If the patient meets the conditions of (1) and (2) at the same time, with the addition of either (3) or (4), a state of delirium can be diagnosed.

Relevant patient information was summarized, covering gender, age, American Society of Anesthesiologists rating, body mass index, hypertension, diabetes mellitus, comorbidities of cerebrovascular disease, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), history of smoking and alcohol consumption, nature of the disease, preoperative hemoglobin concentration, length of the operation, amount of intraoperative hemorrhage, 4-hour postoperative C-reactive protein (CRP) level, intraoperative hypotensive condition, type of anesthesia, postoperative hypoxemia, length of resuscitation, and postoperative pain assessment,[9] as well as examining whether the patients were accompanied by anxiety disorders, depression, malnutrition, sleep disorders, and cognitive impairment.

2.3. Statistical analysis

Data analysis was completed with the help of SPSS 26.0 software, in which the count data were presented in the form of “[n (%)]” and compared by χ² test, while the measurement data were expressed by “(x ± s)” and compared by using independent sample t test. The binary logistic regression analysis was used to explore the influencing variables in depth, construct the regression model, assess the model fit with the help of Hosmer–Lemeshow test, analyze the model consistency, assess the predictive accuracy of the constructed model with the area under the curve of the receiver operating characteristic curve, and implement the Bootstrap method to verify the internal validity. The statistical significance level was set at P < .05.

3.2. Logistic regression analysis of factors affecting postoperative spectrum delirium after hip arthroplasty

Postoperative delirium status was set as the dependent variable (coding: occurrence = 0, nonoccurrence = 1), and binary logistic regression analysis was performed with 12 items such as age, COPD comorbidities, and disease type as independent variables. The results of the analysis revealed that age, COPD, type of disease (femoral neck fracture), length of surgery, intraoperative bleeding, 4-hour postoperative CRP level, postoperative hypoxemia, length of resuscitation (≥1 hour), postoperative pain scores (≥4), nutritional deficits, sleep disorders, and cognitive deficits were all significant risk factors for the development of delirium after total hip arthroplasty (P < .05). 3.4. Bootstrap method within the validation of the prediction model

The Bootstrap method (self-sampling 1000 times) was used to internally validate the model, and the prediction model was constructed based on the independent influencing factors screened by binary logistic regression. The model was calculated as follows: [Logistic(P) = ‐6.548 × COPD comorbidities + 1.169 × type of disease ‐10.128 × length of surgery ‐0.105 × intraoperative bleeding ‐1.026 × 4 hours postoperative CRP level ‐2.264 × postoperative hypoxemia + 0.201 × postoperative pain scores (points) + 0.525 × nutritional disorders + 0.130 × sleep disorders ‐3.074 × cognitive disorders]. The predictive accuracy of the model was assessed using the receiver operating characteristic curve, and an area under the curve of 0.992 was obtained, with a 95% confidence interval of 0.984 to 1.000, a sensitivity of 99.6%, and a specificity of 93.1%, which proved that the model had a high predictive value for delirium after total hip arthroplasty. 4. Discussion

Total hip replacement surgery can significantly reduce pain caused by joint diseases, optimize hip joint activity function, enhance joint flexibility, help patients regain their daily life and work ability, and thus improve the quality of life.[10] Postoperative delirium is a common complication of this type of surgery, especially for patients with preoperative neurological problems, such as dementia, cerebrovascular disease, etc, the probability of postoperative delirium increases significantly.[11] In the current study, age, history of COPD, femoral neck fracture, duration of surgery, intraoperative bleeding, CRP level at 4 hours postoperatively, postoperative hypoxemia, time to resuscitation (≥1 hour), postoperative pain score (≥4), nutritional deficits, sleep disorders, and cognitive deficits were found to be the risk factors for delirium after total hip arthroplasty.

Liu[12] et al showed that patients with subjective cognitive decline were more likely to develop postoperative delirium when they underwent total hip arthroplasty, and an in-depth study revealed that subjective cognitive decline mediated postoperative delirium through phosphorylated tau. Xu[13] et al also showed that subjective cognitive decline was one of the risk factors for postoperative delirium. Liu[14] et al showed that elevated preoperative fasting glucose was a risk factor for postoperative delirium in elderly patients. All of these studies investigated the effect of single risk factors in postoperative delirium after total hip arthroplasty, and the current study was based on the results of previous studies, which included more risk factors for study, and the results were obtained after unifactorial and multifactorial analysis. Initial screening of potential predictors through unifactorial analysis and then comprehensive consideration of the joint influence of multiple variables in the multifactorial model can not only more comprehensively and accurately assess the role of each factor on the outcome variable and effectively control confounding effects, but also improve the predictive accuracy and reliability of the model, providing strong statistical support for an in-depth understanding of the mechanisms behind the complex phenomena and for the development of precise strategies. The findings are consistent with Liu[12] and Xu[14] that patients with subjective cognitive decline are more likely to experience postoperative delirium when undergoing total hip arthroplasty. However, inconsistent with the findings of Liu,[13] our results found that elevated preoperative fasting glucose was not a risk factor for postoperative delirium in elderly patients.

The reasons for this were analyzed: with age, physical function declines, the brain’s ability to adapt to trauma is weakened, and older adults may have concurrent neurological disorders, all of which together push up the risk of postoperative delirium. COPD, as a chronic respiratory disease, triggers a hypoxic state that affects brain function, which in turn elevates the chance of postoperative delirium. Fractures of the neck of the femur occur more often in elderly or even super-elderly people, and these patients are often accompanied by hypertension, diabetes and other diseases, and these complications also increase the possibility of postoperative delirium. As the cycle of total hip replacement surgery is longer, which prolongs the time of patients under anesthesia and increases the risk of postoperative delirium, hypoxia and circulatory fluctuations during surgery can trigger postoperative delirium. Intraoperative hemorrhage can lead to hemorrhagic shock, resulting in hypoxia and metabolic imbalance, exacerbating surgical trauma. At the same time, hemorrhage may also cause electrolyte and acid-base balance disorders, affecting neurological function, thus inducing postoperative delirium in patients. Postoperative inflammatory mediators are released in large quantities,[15] and elevated CRP levels represent a more intense inflammatory response, which may have an impact on brain function, leading to postoperative delirium. Postoperative hypoxemia may originate from multiple factors such as respiratory problems, medication side effects, and pulmonary infections. Decreased oxygen saturation leads to hypoxia in brain cells, exacerbating metabolic abnormalities and neuronal damage in the brain, as well as significantly increasing the risk of postoperative delirium.[16] Postoperative patients need to go through the process of recovering from anesthesia, and prolonged resuscitation time may lead to impaired neuronal function, which in turn triggers postoperative delirium. Inadequate management of postoperative pain, which is not effectively controlled, may cause anxiety or depression in patients, exacerbating mood swings and thus increasing the risk of postoperative delirium. During postoperative recovery, patients require adequate nutritional support to promote wound healing and prevent infection and deep vein thrombosis. Nutritional deficits may interfere with neurologic function, increasing the chance of postoperative delirium. Postoperative patients often face disturbed sleep rhythms, and the disruption of the body’s biological clock affects the functioning of the brain and nervous system, thus inducing delirium. After total hip replacement, analgesic and sedative drugs are commonly used to relieve pain and anxiety. However, these drugs may impair cognitive function and interfere with the nervous system, thereby increasing the incidence of postoperative delirium.[17]

The binary logistic regression model constructed in this study predicted postoperative delirium with an accuracy of 100.0%, which was internally validated by the Bootstrap method, confirming that the model has high value in predicting the risk of postoperative delirium in patients. The establishment of this risk prediction model can improve the prediction accuracy by comprehensively evaluating patient characteristics and surgery-related factors, helping medical teams to effectively identify high-risk individuals and take targeted measures to reduce the occurrence of postoperative delirium. To prevent postoperative delirium, clinical management needs to be strengthened: For high-risk patients, medical teams should formulate personalized treatment plans and implement preventive strategies, such as optimizing the use of analgesic and sedative medications, strengthening rehabilitation training, closely monitoring and treating the underlying disease in a timely manner, providing psychological support to the patient, assessing the sleep and psychological status, and cautiously administering medications in order to reduce the incidence of postoperative delirium. Implement innovative and efficient nursing strategies, such as intensive care, based on personalized follow-up, timely adjustment of the nursing process, ensuring accurate communication of the patient’s status at shift handover, creating a quiet environment, reducing noise, communicating softly, and maintaining patient rest.

In this article, we explored a study on potential risk factors for the development of delirium after total hip arthroplasty, and despite a number of meaningful findings, several limitations of this study remain. Specifically, the study population was relatively small, limited to 320 participants, which may have affected the generalizability and statistical significance of the results. In addition, as a single-center study, all data were obtained from the same healthcare organization, which may have resulted in results being influenced by specific practice patterns or patient populations, limiting external validity. Also, as a retrospective study, data completeness and accuracy issues and potential selection bias are not negligible limitations. Therefore, these limitations need to be considered when interpreting and applying the results of this study, and future studies are expected to further validate and extend these findings through multicenter, prospective designs and the inclusion of larger sample sizes.

5. Conclusion

In summary, age, history of COPD, femoral neck fracture, duration of surgery, intraoperative bleeding, 4-hour postoperative CRP level, postoperative hypoxemia, resuscitation time (≥1 hour), postoperative pain score (≥4), nutritional deficits, sleep disorders, and cognitive dysfunction are risk factors for postoperative delirium after total hip arthroplasty. Therefore, patient-specific risk factors should be accurately identified and targeted in clinical practice, and effective measures should be taken to prevent the emergence of postoperative delirium.

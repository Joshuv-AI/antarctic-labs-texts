Acute Food Bolus Impaction Secondary to a Fully Circumferential Inlet Patch
Authors: Giorgi Kiladze, Wei On, Vivek Goodoory, Björn Rembacken, Peter Ellery
Published: 2026

Abbreviations

fully circumferential inlet patch

inlet patch

proton pump inhibitor

A 72‐year‐old woman presented to the emergency department with acute‐onset dysphagia following food ingestion. She was only able to swallow saliva and there was no respiratory compromise or stridor. An inpatient gastroscopy, within 24 hours of presentation, showed a chunk of potato, wedged in the lumen of the upper oesophagus (Figure 1A). The mucosa around the food bolus had salmon coloured appearance circumferentially, which initially raised the suspicion of long segment Barrett's oesophagus. With a gentle nudge, the food bolus was dislodged into the stomach to reveal an ulcerated stricture at the site of impaction, 20 cm from the incisors (Figure 1B). The oesophageal mucosa beyond this point was squamous with no evidence of Barrett's at the gastro‐oesophageal junction. Biopsies taken from the strictured area confirmed the presence of gastric heterotopic (oxyntic type) mucosa (Figure 2), also known as inlet patch (IP) when it occurs in the upper oesophagus. No Helicobacter pylori organisms were identified on Haemotoxylin and Eosin (H&E) staining or immunohistochemistry (IHC).

(A) Endoscopic view of the food bolus in the upper oesophagus. The epithelium above the site of impaction appears columnar over the entire circumference with a squamous border at the proximal edge. (B) Endoscopic view of the site of impaction after clearance of the food bolus. Inflammation and ulceration are visible at 11 o'clock position with a mild stricturing effect.

(A) Oesophageal biopsy, H&E (Haemotoxylin & Eosin) stain, 10x objective. Histology from the inlet patch showed glandular mucosa of specialised gastric (oxyntic) type with active chronic inflammation. (B) Oesophageal biopsy, H&E (Haemotoxylin & Eosin) stain, 10x objective. There is ulceration (arrow) and focal intestinal metaplasia (double arrow).

IP is typically incidental, small, and non‐circumferential. A systematic review of fully circumferential inlet patches (FCIP) by Cohen et al. [1] found 72.4% of patients were symptomatic with dysphagia and/or food impaction. A benign stricture, web or ring was described in 20 (66.7%) cases. 4 cases (13.3%) of adenocarcinoma were described. Strictures responded to conventional treatment with proton‐pump inhibitor (PPI) therapy and endoscopic dilation in this cohort.

Our patient was prescribed lansoprazole 30 mg twice daily and a repeat endoscopy (Figure 3) after 8 weeks confirmed healing of the ulcer and improvement in the luminal diameter. Lansoprazole dose was reduced to 30 mg once daily maintenance dose. The patient was asymptomatic at that point and endoscopic dilation was not required. We offered the patient outpatient clinic follow‐up to discuss endoscopic surveillance, initially at 1 year, in view of the malignant potential described by Cohen et al. [1]. Therapeutic endoscopic interventions such as argon plasma coagulation (APC) or radiofrequency ablation (RFA) were not considered on the basis of having achieved full symptomatic control with PPI therapy and absence of dysplasia on histology.

(A) Endoscopic view of the previous food impaction site. Inflammation and ulceration have healed after 8 weeks of high dose PPI therapy. The luminal diameter has improved. (B) Narrow‐band imaging highlights the difference between the squamous and columnar epithelia.

Endoscopists should be aware that a fully circumferential inlet patch is strongly associated with peptic‐related complications and that PPI therapy is warranted in those patients. Follow‐up should be individualised and prioritise the patient's wishes in the absence of strong evidence.

Energy functionals on almost Kähler manifolds: I
Ken Wang, Zuyi Zhang, Jiuru Zhou

Abstract
In this paper, we consider the Donaldson gauge functional and the twisted Aubin functionals on almost Kähler manifolds. As in Kähler geometry, we generalize the inequality between Aubin functionals.



Introduction

In Kähler geometry, energy functionals — such as Mabuchi functional
[
4
]
and Aubin functionals
[
2
,
16
]
— play a fundamental role in studying extremal metrics. Bando and Mabuchi
[
4
]
showed that if a Fano manifold admits a Kähler-Einstein metric, then the Mabuchi functional and Tian’s
\alpha
-invariant
[
16
]
are bounded below. It is also well known that, Aubin functionals play an important role in the study of Mabuchi functionals and J-flow defined by Donaldson
[
8
]
(c. f.
[
5
,
23
,
7
]
).

Here, we study generalizations of the Donaldson gauge functional and the Aubin functionals. Using the
{\mathcal{D}_{J}^{+}}
operator introduced by Tan et al.
[
15
]
, which generalizes the
\partial_{J}\bar{\partial}_{J}
operator, we define the Aubin funtionals on almost Kähler manifolds and explore their relations with the Hermitian Donaldson gauge functional. In particular, we generalize the classical inequality between Aubin functionals
[
1
]
:

Theorem 1.1
.

For the twisted Aubin functionals defined on an almost Kähler manifold
(M^{2m},J,g,\omega)
, the following relations hold:

0\leq\frac{1}{m+1}I^{\prime}_{\omega}(\phi)\leq J^{\prime}_{\omega}(\phi)\leq\frac{m}{m+1}I^{\prime}_{\omega}(\phi)

for any
\phi\in\mathcal{H}(\omega,J)
, where
\mathcal{H}(\omega,J)
is the space of almost Kähler potentials. In particular, the equality, in any of the above three inequalities, holds if and only if
\phi
is constant.

The structure of this paper is as follows. Section 2 introduces the
\mathcal{D}_{J}^{+}
operator and recalls the primitive cohomologies defined in
[
17
,
18
]
. Section 3 presents the Hermitian Donaldson gauge functional, and Section 4 discusses the Hermitian Aubin functionals and provides a proof of
Theorem

1.1
.

In a subsequent paper
[
21
]
, we will further study the Mabuchi functional
[
13
]
, the Ding functional
[
6
]
, and the Tian invariant
[
16
]
in the context of almost Kähler geometry.

Preliminary

In this section, we first define the
{\mathcal{D}_{J}^{+}}
operator on higher dimensional almost Kähler manifolds
[
14
]
. We define the Lefschetz operator and the symplectic operators as in the primitive cohomology of
[
17
,
18
]
. For those materials covered here that are standard, one can refer
[
3
,
10
]
for details.

Let
(M,J)
be an almost complex manifold with dimension
2m
. Denote by
C^{\infty}(M)
the smooth functions on
M
,
\Lambda^{p,q}_{J}
the bundle of
(p,q)
-form on
M
with respect to
J
, and
\Omega^{p,q}(M):=\Gamma(M,\Lambda^{p,q}_{J})
the smooth sections of the bundle
\Lambda^{p,q}_{J}\rightarrow M
. The space of smooth
k
-forms is defined as
\Omega^{k}(M):=\bigoplus_{p+q=k}\Omega^{p,q}(M)
.

The almost complex structure
J
acts on the real forms as an involution, by

J:\alpha(\cdot,\ldots,\cdot)\mapsto(-1)^{k}\alpha(J\cdot,\ldots,J\cdot),\quad\alpha\in\Omega^{k}(M).

When
k=2
, this yields a splitting of
\Omega^{2}(M)
into
J
-invariant and
J
-anti-invariant 2-forms,

\Omega^{2}=\Omega_{J}^{+}\oplus\Omega_{J}^{-}.

where
\Omega_{J}^{+}
and
\Omega_{J}^{-}
denote the space of
J
-invariant and
J
-anti-invariant forms respectively. We then define the following operators:

\displaystyle d_{J}^{+}

\displaystyle:=P_{J}^{+}d:\Omega^{1}\longrightarrow\Omega_{J}^{+},

\displaystyle d_{J}^{-}

\displaystyle:=P_{J}^{-}d:\Omega^{1}\longrightarrow\Omega_{J}^{-},

where
P_{J}^{\pm}=\frac{1}{2}(1\pm J)
are algebraic projections on
\Omega^{2}(M)
.

Now suppose
(M^{2m},J,g,\omega)
is an almost Kähler manifold; that is,

g
is a
J
-compatible Riemannian metric and
\omega(\cdot,\cdot)=g(J\cdot,\cdot)
is a closed 2-form. The adjoint operator
d^{*}:\Omega^{k}\rightarrow\Omega^{k-1}
is defined as

d^{*}=(-1)^{k+1}*_{g}d*_{g},

where
*_{g}
is the Hodge star operator defined using
g
. Consider the operator

d^{-}_{J}d^{*}:\Omega_{J}^{-}(M)\longrightarrow\Omega_{J}^{-}(M).

One shows that for all
\alpha,\beta\in\Omega_{J}^{-}(M)
,

\langle d^{-}_{J}d^{*}\alpha,\beta\rangle_{g}=\langle dd^{*}\alpha,\beta\rangle_{g}=\langle\alpha,dd^{*}\beta\rangle_{g}=\langle\alpha,d^{-}_{J}d^{*}\beta\rangle_{g},,

hence
d^{-}_{J}d^{*}
is self-adjoint with respect to the
L^{2}
-inner product induced by
g
. Moreover,
d^{-}_{J}d^{*}
can be extended to a closed densely defined operator

d^{-}_{J}d^{*}:\Omega_{J}^{-}\otimes L_{2}^{2}(M)\longrightarrow\Omega_{J}^{-}\otimes L^{2}(M).

The operator below was first discovered by Lejmi
[
11
,
12
]
.

Proposition 2.1

(
[
11
]
)
.

Let
(M,\omega,J,g)
be a closed almost Hermitian 4-manifold. Define the following operator

\displaystyle P:\Omega^{-}_{J}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\Omega^{-}_{J}

\displaystyle\psi

\displaystyle\longmapsto P^{-}_{J}(dd^{*}\psi).

Then
P
is a self-adjoint, strongly elliptic linear operator with a kernel consisting of
g
-self-dual-harmonic,
J
-anti-invariant 2-forms. Hence,

\Omega^{-}_{J}=\ker P\oplus d^{-}_{J}\Omega^{1}{R}.

Using this operator
P
, Tan et al.
[
15
]
introduced the
{\mathcal{D}_{J}^{+}}
operator as follows:

Definition 2.2
.

Suppose
(M,\omega,J,g)
is a closed almost Kähler 4-manifold. Define
{\mathcal{D}_{J}^{+}}
as follows

\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}_{J}^{+}}:C^{\infty}(M)_{0}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\Omega_{J}^{+}(M)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}_{J}^{+}}(f)=dJdf+dd^{*}\sigma(f)

\displaystyle=dd^{*}(f\omega)+dd^{*}\sigma(f),

where
\sigma(f)\in\Omega^{-}_{J}
satisfies

d^{-}_{J}d^{*}(f\omega)+d^{-}_{J}d^{*}\sigma(f)=0.

Remark 2.3
.

In the original formulation in
[
15
]
, the auxiliary operator

\displaystyle{\mathcal{W}_{J}}:C^{\infty}(M)_{0}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\Omega^{1}(M)

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle\longmapsto d^{*}(f\omega+\sigma(f)).

is introduced. It satisfies

d{\mathcal{W}_{J}}(f)={\mathcal{D}_{J}^{+}}(f),\quad d_{J}^{-}{\mathcal{W}_{J}}(f)=0.

One may further generalize the operator
\mathcal{D}_{J}^{+}
to higher-dimensional almost Kähler manifolds (cf. Tan et al.
[
14
]
). More precisely, let
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
; then

\mathcal{D}_{J}^{+}(f)=dJdf+dd^{*}\sigma(f),

where
\sigma(f)\in\Omega_{J}^{-}(M)
is chosen so that
d_{J}^{-}Jdf+d_{J}^{-}d^{*}\sigma(f)=0
. Note that
\mathcal{D}_{J}^{+}(f)
is
d
-exact. If
J
is integrable, then
D_{J}^{+}(f)=2\sqrt{-1}\partial_{J}\bar{\partial}_{J}f
.

Definition 2.4

(Tan et al.
[
14
]
)
.

The space of almost Kähler potentials is defined by

\mathcal{H}(\omega,J)=\{\phi\in C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})|\ \omega+\mathcal{D}_{J}^{+}\phi>0\}.

This space is contractible, infinite-dimensional, and affine. It is an open set of
C^{\infty}(M)
; hence for any
\phi\in\mathcal{H}(\omega,J),
the tangent space
T_{\phi}\mathcal{H}(\omega,J)
of
\mathcal{H}(\omega,J)
at
\phi
is identified with
C^{\infty}(M)
.

Definition 2.5
.

Suppose
\omega_{1}
is another almost Kähler form on
M
. We say that
\omega
and
\omega_{1}
are
related by an almost Kähler potential
if there is a smooth function
\phi\in\mathcal{H}(\omega,J)
such that

\omega_{1}=\omega+{\mathcal{D}_{J}^{+}}(\phi)>0.

In Tan et al.
[
14
]
, the authors prove that when
\phi_{0}
is the solution of the Laplacian equation (in general,
\phi_{0}\neq\phi
)

(2.1)

-\frac{1}{m}\Delta_{g}\phi_{0}=\frac{\omega^{m-1}\wedge(\omega_{1}-\omega)}{\omega^{m}}=\frac{\omega^{m-1}\wedge{\mathcal{D}_{J}^{+}}(\phi)}{\omega^{m}},

then one can rewrite
{\mathcal{D}_{J}^{+}}(\phi)
as

{\mathcal{D}_{J}^{+}}(\phi)=dJd\phi_{0}+da(\phi_{0}),

where
a(\phi_{0})=d^{*}\sigma(\phi_{0})\in\Omega_{\mathbb{R}}^{1}(M)
.

The 1-form
a(\phi_{0})
satisfies the elliptic system.

(2.2)

\begin{cases}d^{*}a(\phi_{0})=0,\\
d^{-}_{J}a(\phi_{0})=-d_{J}^{-}Jd\phi_{0},\\
\omega^{m-1}\wedge da(\phi_{0})=0.\end{cases}

The function
\phi_{0}
is also called almost Kähler potential. If
J
is integrable, then

a(\phi_{0})=0=\sigma(\phi),\ \ \phi=\phi_{0}.

For
m=2
, the system (
2.2
) reduces to the self-dual equation in Section 4 of
[
20
]
.

We now review primitive cohomology following Tseng–Yau
[
17
,
18
]
. Define the
Lefschetz operator
by

\displaystyle L_{\omega}:\Omega^{k}(M)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\Omega^{k+2}(M)

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle\longmapsto\omega\wedge\alpha.

A
k
-form
\alpha
is called
k
-primitive
if

•

L_{\omega}^{m-k}\alpha\neq 0
,

•

L_{\omega}^{m-k+1}\alpha=0
.

We denote the space of primitive
k
-form by
\mathcal{P}^{k}(M)
. In particular, every 1-form is 1-primitive.

Proposition 2.6

(
[
17
]
)
.

For any
B_{k}\in\mathcal{P}^{k}(M)
, there exists
B_{i}\in\mathcal{P}^{i}(M)
for
i=k\pm 1
such that

(2.3)

dB_{k}=B_{k+1}+L_{\omega}B_{k-1}.

This proposition gives rise to the
symplectic operators
of
\omega
:

\displaystyle\partial_{\pm}:\mathcal{P}^{k}(M)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathcal{P}^{k\pm 1}(M)

\displaystyle B_{k}\phantom{M}

\displaystyle\longmapsto B_{k\pm 1},

where
B_{k\pm 1}
is specified by Equation (
2.3
).

The exterior derivative
d:\Omega^{k}(M)\rightarrow\Omega^{k+1}(M)
admits the decomposition

d=(A_{J},\overline{\partial}_{J},\partial_{J},\overline{A}_{J}):\Omega^{p,q}(M)\rightarrow(\Omega^{p-1,q+2}\oplus\Omega^{p,q+1}\oplus\Omega^{p+1,q}\oplus\Omega^{p+2,q-1})(M).

Lemma 2.7

(
[
18
]
)
.

If
\alpha=\alpha^{2,0}+\alpha^{0,2}\in\Omega_{J}^{-}
, then

d\alpha=\partial_{+}\alpha+\omega\wedge\partial_{-}\alpha.

where
\omega^{m-2}\wedge\partial_{+}\alpha=0
and
\partial_{-}\alpha\in\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{R}}(M)
.

Example 2.8
.

a.

From
Lemma

2.7
,

dJd\phi_{0}=\partial_{+}(Jd\phi_{0})+\omega\wedge\partial_{-}(Jd\phi_{0}).

Using
Equation

2.2
,

\omega^{m-1}\wedge dJd\phi_{0}=\omega^{m}\partial_{-}(Jd\phi_{0})=-\frac{1}{m}\omega^{m}\Delta_{g}\phi_{0}.

Hence,

(2.4)

\partial_{-}(Jd\phi_{0})=-\frac{1}{m}\Delta_{g}\phi_{0},\quad\partial_{+}(Jd\phi_{0})=dJd\phi_{0}+\frac{\omega}{m}\Delta_{g}\phi_{0}.

b.

Similarly,

0=\omega^{m-1}\wedge da(\phi_{0})=\omega^{m}\partial_{-}(a(\phi_{0})).

Thus,

(2.5)

\begin{cases}\partial_{-}(a(\phi_{0}))=\partial_{-}(d^{*}\sigma(\phi_{0}))=0,\\
P_{J}^{-}\partial_{+}(a(\phi_{0}))=d_{J}^{-}a(\phi_{0})=-d_{J}^{-}Jd\phi_{0}.\end{cases}

The Hodge star operator
*_{g}
and the symplectic Hodge star
*_{\omega}
are related by the following.

Lemma 2.9

(
[
22
,
17
]
)
.

For any
\alpha\in\Omega^{k}(M)
,

*_{g}\alpha=\mathcal{J}(*_{\omega}\alpha):=\sum_{(p,q)}(\sqrt{-1})^{p-q}\prod^{p,q}(*_{\omega}\alpha),

where
\prod\limits^{p,q}
denotes projection to
(p,q)
-forms.

For the operator
\mathcal{J}:\Omega^{k}(M)\rightarrow\Omega^{k}(M)
above, we compute a case needed later. If
\alpha\in\Omega^{1,0}(M)\oplus\Omega^{0,1}(M)
and
\{dz_{i}\}
is the local coframe of the
-\sqrt{-1}
eigenspace of
J
, then

(2.6)

\displaystyle J\alpha

\displaystyle=J\sum_{i}(\alpha^{i}dz_{i}+\alpha^{\bar{i}}d\bar{z_{i}})

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i}(\alpha^{i}(-\sqrt{-1})dz_{i}+\alpha^{\bar{i}}\sqrt{-1}d\bar{z_{i}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}(\alpha^{i}\sqrt{-1}dz_{i}+\alpha^{\bar{i}}(-\sqrt{-1})d\bar{z_{i}})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{J}\alpha.

A similar computation shows that
J\alpha=\mathcal{J}\alpha
for
\alpha\in\Omega^{2,0}(M)\oplus\Omega^{0,2}(M)
.

Lemma 2.10

(
[
18
]
)
.

For the symplectic operators
\partial_{\pm}
, the following identities hold:

•

\partial_{+}^{2}=\partial_{-}^{2}=0
,

•

dL_{\omega}^{k}=L_{\omega}^{k}d
,

•

.

Donaldson gauge functionals

In this section, we define the Hermitian Donaldson gauge functional and compute its derivative.
From now on, denote
V:=\int_{M}\omega^{m}
and let
*
be the Riemannian Hodge star operator
*_{g}
. We assume
\phi\in\mathcal{H}(\omega,J)
and set
\omega_{1}=\omega+{\mathcal{D}_{J}^{+}}(\phi)
.

Definition 3.1
.

The
Hermitian Donaldson gauge functional
is defined on
\mathcal{H}(\omega,J)
as

{II}_{\omega}(\phi):=\frac{1}{(m+1)V}\Bigg(\int_{M}\phi\sum_{k=0}^{m}\omega_{1}^{k}\wedge\omega^{m-k}-\int_{M}\phi dd^{*}\sigma(\phi)\wedge\omega^{m-1}\Bigg)

where
\omega_{1}=\omega+{\mathcal{D}_{J}^{+}}(\phi)
.

Remark 3.2
.

If
J
is integrable, then
\sigma(\phi)=0
and the functional reduces to

{II}_{\omega}(\phi)=\frac{1}{(m+1)V}\int_{M}\phi(\sum_{k=0}^{m}\omega_{1}^{k}\wedge\omega^{m-k}).

In the Kähler case, this functional
{II}_{\omega}
was first introduced in Bando-Mabuchi
[
4
]
, and —because of its relation to the moment-map picture in Donaldson’s work
[
9
]
— it is often called the Donaldson gauge functional relative to
\omega
.

Before analyzing its properties, we need two technical lemmas.

Lemma 3.3

(
[
14
]
)
.

Let
\partial_{\pm}
be the symplectic operators associated to
\omega
. Then for all
\alpha\in\Omega_{J}^{-}
, one has

d^{*}\alpha=(m-1)J\partial_{-}\alpha.

proof.

First, note that
\alpha\in\Omega^{-}_{J}
can be decomposed as
\alpha=\alpha^{2,0}+\alpha^{0,2}
, and one checks that

\omega^{m-1}\wedge\alpha=0,

which implies
\alpha
is primitive, i.e.,
\alpha\in\mathcal{P}^{2}(M)
.

Using repeatedly the identities in
Lemma

2.10
, one obtains

\displaystyle d^{*}\alpha=d^{*}L_{\omega}^{0}\alpha

\displaystyle={(-1)^{3}{*}d((-1)^{3}\frac{L_{\omega}^{m-2}}{(m-2)!}\mathcal{J}\alpha)}

\displaystyle=-{*}d(\frac{L_{\omega}^{m-2}}{(m-2)!}\alpha)

\displaystyle=-{*}\frac{L_{\omega}^{m-2}}{(m-2)!}d\alpha

\displaystyle=-{*}\left(\frac{L_{\omega}^{m-2}}{(m-2)!}\partial_{+}\alpha+\frac{L_{\omega}^{m-2}}{(m-2)!}\omega\wedge\partial_{-}\alpha\right)

\displaystyle=-{*}\frac{L_{\omega}^{m-1}}{(m-2)!}\partial_{-}\alpha

\displaystyle=(m-1)\frac{L_{\omega}^{m-m+1-1}}{(m-m+1-1)!}\mathcal{J}\partial_{-}\alpha

\displaystyle=(m-1)J\partial_{-}\alpha.

∎

Let
\alpha=\alpha^{2,0}+\alpha^{0,2}\in\Omega_{J}^{-}(M)
, which is a primitive form. Applying the
(p,q)
-decomposition gives

d\alpha=\partial_{J}\alpha^{2,0}+\bar{\partial}_{J}\alpha^{0,2}+\partial_{J}\alpha^{0,2}+\bar{\partial}_{J}\alpha^{2,0}+A_{J}\alpha^{2,0}+\bar{A}_{J}\alpha^{0,2}.

Denote
\beta_{0}(\alpha)
by the (1,2)+(2,1)-part of
\partial_{+}\alpha
in
Lemma

2.7
. Then,

(3.1)

\displaystyle d\alpha

\displaystyle=\partial_{J}\alpha^{2,0}+\bar{\partial}_{J}\alpha^{0,2}+\beta_{0}(\alpha)+\beta_{1}(\alpha)

\displaystyle=\partial_{+}\alpha+\omega\wedge\partial_{-}\alpha,

where
\beta_{1}(\alpha)=\omega\wedge\partial_{-}\alpha
. In particular, there are no 3-primitive forms on closed 4-manifolds; hence
d\alpha=\omega\wedge\partial_{-}\alpha
.

For simplicity, we write
\beta_{0}(\phi)
for
\beta_{0}(\sigma(\phi))
. Then we have the following lemma.

Lemma 3.4
.

\displaystyle-(m-1)^{2}\int_{M}J\partial_{-}\sigma(f)\wedge\partial_{-}\sigma(\phi)\wedge\omega^{m-1-k}\wedge\omega_{1}^{k}=(A)_{f,\phi}+(B)_{f,\phi}=(A)_{\phi,f}+(B)_{\phi,f}

where

\displaystyle(A)_{u,v}

\displaystyle=\int_{M}u(dd^{*}\sigma(v)\wedge\omega+(m-1)dJ\beta_{0}(v))\wedge\omega^{m-2-k}\wedge\omega_{1}^{k}

\displaystyle(B)_{u,v}

\displaystyle=(m-1)^{2}\int_{M}J\partial_{-}\sigma(u)\wedge\beta_{0}(v)\wedge\omega^{m-2-k}\wedge\omega_{1}^{k}.

Moreover, we have

\displaystyle-(m-1)^{2}\int_{M}J\partial_{-}\sigma(\phi)\wedge\partial_{-}\sigma(\phi)\wedge\omega^{m-1-k}\wedge\omega_{1}^{k}\geq 0

for all integer
k\geq 0
such that
m-1-k\geq 1
. In particular, if
J
is integrable, then
\sigma(\phi)=0
and so
\beta_{0}(\phi)=0
. Therefore, the above identities are trivial when
J
is integrable.

proof.

We now prove the identity

(3.2)

-(m-1)^{2}\int_{M}J\partial_{-}\sigma(f)\wedge\partial_{-}\sigma(\phi)\wedge\omega^{m-1-k}\wedge\omega_{1}^{k}=(A)_{f,\phi}+(B)_{f,\phi}

The corresponding identity with
f
and
\phi
swapped can be obtained analogously. Applying
Lemma

3.3
, we find

\displaystyle(A)_{f,\phi}=

\displaystyle{\displaystyle-}(m-1)\int_{M}df\wedge(J\partial_{-}\sigma(\phi)\wedge\omega+J\beta_{0}(\phi))\wedge\omega^{m-2-k}\wedge\omega_{1}^{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\displaystyle-}(m-1)\int_{M}\left\{df\wedge(J\partial_{-}\sigma(\phi)\wedge\omega+J\beta_{0}(\phi))\right\}^{(2,2)}\wedge\omega^{m-2-k}\wedge\omega_{1}^{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(m-1)\int_{M}Jdf\wedge(\partial_{-}\sigma(\phi)\wedge\omega+\beta_{0}(\phi))\wedge\omega^{m-2-k}\wedge\omega_{1}^{k}.

where in the second line we restrict to the
(2,2)
-component because only
J
-invariant
(2,2)
-forms contribute.

On the other hand, since

\partial_{+}\alpha=\partial_{J}\alpha^{2,0}+\bar{\partial}_{J}\alpha^{0,2}+\beta_{0}(\alpha),

we have

\displaystyle(A)_{f,\phi}=

\displaystyle(m-1)\int_{M}Jdf\wedge d\sigma(\phi)\wedge\omega^{m-2-k}\wedge\omega_{1}^{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(m-1)\int_{M}dJdf\wedge\sigma(\phi)\wedge\omega^{m-2-k}\wedge\omega_{1}^{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\displaystyle-}(m-1)\int_{M}d_{J}^{-}d^{*}\sigma(f)\wedge\sigma(\phi)\wedge\omega^{m-2-k}\wedge\omega_{1}^{k}\quad\text{(since $d_{J}^{-}Jdf=-d^{-}_{J}d^{*}\sigma(f)$)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\displaystyle-}(m-1)\int_{M}d^{*}\sigma(f)\wedge d\sigma(\phi)\wedge\omega^{m-2-k}\wedge\omega_{1}^{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\displaystyle-}(m-1)^{2}\int_{M}J\partial_{-}\sigma(f)\wedge\beta_{0}(\phi)\wedge\omega^{m-2-k}\wedge\omega_{1}^{k}

\displaystyle}{\displaystyle-(m-1)^{2}\int_{M}J\partial_{-}\sigma(f)\wedge\partial_{-}\sigma(\phi)\wedge\omega^{m-1-k}\wedge\omega_{1}^{k}.

To derive the second part, set
f=\phi
. Then
Equation

3.2
becomes

(A)_{\phi,\phi}+(B)_{\phi,\phi}={-}(m-1)^{2}\int_{M}J\partial_{-}\sigma(\phi)\wedge\partial_{-}\sigma(\phi)\wedge\omega^{m-1-k}\wedge\omega_{1}^{k}.

It remains to show the integral

(m-1)^{2}\int_{M}\partial_{-}\sigma(\phi)\wedge J\partial_{-}\sigma(\phi)\wedge\omega^{m-1-k}\wedge\omega_{1}^{k}

is nonnegative. By Lemma 4.2.1 in
[
10
]
, at each
x\in M
one may choose a
J
-adapted basis
\{e_{1},Je_{1},\ldots,e_{m},Je_{m}\}
of
T_{x}M
with dual basis
\{e_{1}^{*},Je_{1}^{*},\ldots,e_{m}^{*},Je_{m}^{*}\}
so that
\omega(x)=\sum_{j}e_{j}^{*}\wedge Je_{j}^{*}
and
\omega_{1}=\sum_{j}\lambda e_{j}^{*}\wedge Je_{j}^{*}
with
\lambda_{j}>0
. Moreover, there exists a positive
J
-(1,1) form
\omega^{(k)}
and
\omega^{(k)}=\sum_{j}\mu_{j}^{(k)}e_{j}^{*}\wedge Je_{j}^{*}
with
\mu_{j}^{(k)}>0
and
(\omega^{(k)})^{m-1}=\omega^{m-1-k}\wedge\omega_{1}^{k}
.

Writing
\partial_{-}\sigma(\phi)|_{x}=\sum_{j}(a_{j}e^{*}_{j}+b_{j}Je^{*}_{j})
gives

\displaystyle\partial_{-}\sigma(\phi)\wedge J\partial_{-}\sigma(\phi)\wedge\omega^{m-1-k}\wedge\omega_{1}^{k}|_{x}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}(a_{j}e^{*}_{j}+b_{j}Je^{*}_{j})\wedge\sum_{j}(a_{j}Je^{*}_{j}-b_{j}e^{*}_{j})\wedge(\omega^{(k)})^{m-1}|_{x}

\displaystyle=\Big(\sum_{j}(a_{j}^{2}+b_{j}^{2})\wedge e^{*}_{j}\wedge Je^{*}_{j}\Big)\wedge(\omega^{(k)})^{m-1}|_{x}\geq 0.

Therefore,
(A)_{\phi,\phi}+(B)_{\phi,\phi}\geq 0
, completing the proof.
∎

Definition 3.5
.

The
twisted Donaldson gauge functional
is defined on
\mathcal{H}(\omega,J)
by

\displaystyle{II}^{\prime}_{\omega}(\phi):={}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{V}\int_{M}\phi\omega^{m}+\frac{1}{V}\int_{M}\phi dJd\phi\wedge(\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\frac{m-k}{m+1}\omega_{1}^{k}\wedge\omega^{m-k-1})

\displaystyle}{\displaystyle+\frac{(m-1)^{2}}{V}\int_{M}J\partial_{-}\sigma(\phi)\wedge\partial_{-}\sigma(\phi)\wedge(\sum_{k=0}^{m-2}\frac{m-k}{m+1}\omega_{1}^{k}\wedge\omega^{m-k-2})

where
\omega_{1}=\omega+\mathcal{D}_{J}^{+}(\phi)
.

Remark 3.6
.

When
\dim M=4
, there are no primitive 3-forms, so
\beta_{0}(\phi)=0

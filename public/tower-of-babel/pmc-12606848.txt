The silent segment speaks: functional and clinical impact of concomitant spondylolysis in adolescent idiopathic scoliosis. Retrospective cohort study
Authors: Fatih Duygun, Vugar Nabi, Omer Faruk Kılıçaslan, Koray Yılmaz
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundWhile adolescent idiopathic scoliosis is often asymptomatic, emerging evidence indicates that low back pain may affect a subset of patients, challenging the traditional notion of AIS as a painless deformity. This study investigates the prevalence and clinical relevance of spondylolysis in surgically treated AIS patients, highlighting it as a potentially underrecognized factor contributing to persistent symptoms and less favorable outcomes.MethodsA retrospective cohort study was conducted involving 160 AIS patients (median age: 15 years; 81.3% female) who underwent posterior spinal fusion between 2015 and 2022 with a minimum follow-up of 2 years, thereby ensuring methodological consistency with standard reporting guidelines. Patients were stratified into two groups based on the presence or absence of bilateral pars interarticularis defects, confirmed via X-ray, CT and MRI. Clinical assessments included Visual Analog Scale (VAS) for pain and Scoliosis Research Society-22 (SRS-22) scores. Radiological evaluations encompassed MRI (T2 signal loss, Modic changes), CT (sclerosis), and radiography. Surgical variables such as operative time, intraoperative blood loss, and incidence of distal junctional kyphosis (DJK) were recorded.ResultsConcomitant spondylolysis was observed in 7.5% of AIS patients, highlighting its clinical significance. Patients with spondylolysis demonstrated significantly worse postoperative outcomes. VAS scores remained elevated postoperatively (median: 6(95% CI 5.5–6) vs. 3(95% CI 3–4)), with no meaningful pain improvement (p = 0.738). SRS-22 pain and activity domain scores were significantly lower in the spondylolysis group (p < 0.001). Radiologically, all spondylolysis patients exhibited structural degeneration (T2 attenuation, Modic changes, CT sclerosis), which was absent in controls (p < 0.001). The presence of pars defects correlated with greater apical vertebral rotation (p = 0.028), increased blood loss (p = 0.010), and a markedly higher incidence of DJK (33% vs. 0%, p < 0.001). Delta analysis of SRS-22 scores confirmed diminished postoperative gains in pain (p < 0.001), activity (p = 0.003), and total score (p = 0.021) for patients with spondylolysis.ConclusionThis study establishes spondylolysis as a clinically impactful comorbidity in adolescent idiopathic scoliosis, associated with increased postoperative pain, dysfunction, and distal junctional complications. Routine preoperative identification and tailored surgical strategies are warranted. By positioning pars defects as modifiable risk factors, our findings support a paradigm shift toward biomechanically-informed, individualized approaches in AIS surgical planning and long-term management.Keywords: Adolescent idiopathic scoliosis, Spondylolysis, Spinal fusion, Postoperative pain, Distal junctional kyphosis, HRQoL

Introduction

Adolescent Idiopathic Scoliosis (AIS) is a multidimensional condition, best conceptualized through multi-factorial reviews that integrate genetics, growth, and biomechanics with neuroendocrine mechanismsthat involves not only the spinal axis but also adolescents’ self-esteem, social development, and long-term functional outcomes; importantly, physiological studies substantiate that AIS is an organic, rather than systemic entity, reframing care toward biomechanically targeted evaluation and treatment [1–4]. Research shows that low back pain is almost twice as common in adolescents with AIS compared to their healthy peers. This pain is often concentrated in the lumbar region, is persistent and can negatively affect both the physical activity and emotional resilience of young people [5, 6].

In this context, adolescents diagnosed with AIS and their families strongly believe that low back pain is directly caused by the curvature of the spine and this belief often leads them to ignore the existing scientific uncertainty. However, this curvature-pain correlation is not as clear as clinically assumed, but the visual impact of the deformity can profoundly affect individual perception and the way pain is felt on a psychological level [7]. On the other hand, spondylolysis, which is often overlooked in the etiopathogenesis of low back pain but is a clinically very important condition, can occur in association with AIS. This silent but effective pathology is often overshadowed by scoliosis and treatment opportunities may be missed due to lack of accurate diagnosis. In the literature, the presence of spondylolysis or spondylolisthesis with AIS is reported between 4.4 and 48% and this wide range draws attention to the heterogeneity in diagnostic approaches [8]. Despite this, the natural history of spondylolysis after treatment of AIS, the impact of surgical intervention on this lesion and the resulting impact on quality of life are still poorly understood. This study analyzes in depth the prevalence and clinical outcomes of concomitant spondylolysis in a large cohort of surgically treated AIS patients.

Our hypothesis is that AIS patients with spondylolysis will show significantly more unfavorable outcomes, especially in terms of postoperative low back pain, functional capacity and overall quality of life, compared to those without spondylolysis. Comprehensive evaluations in this direction are expected to have an impact that may question the current scoliosis surgery paradigm and contribute to the individualization of surgical strategies.

Patients and methods

Study design and patient selection

This retrospective comparative cohort study analyzed 160 patients who underwent surgical correction for AIS between 2015 and 2022 at a single tertiary spine center. Eligible patients had a minimum postoperative follow-up of 2 years. Data were systematically extracted from institutional electronic health records following approval by the local ethics committee (IRB.2022–328. 21/13. 24/11/2022). All patient identifiers were anonymized and coded in accordance with the ethical guidelines of the Declaration of Helsinki, and written informed consent was obtained from all participants.

Although the primary design was exploratory, a post hoc power analysis was conducted to assess the adequacy of the sample for detecting meaningful differences in SRS-22 domain scores between patients with and without spondylolysis. Using a two-tailed independent samples t test, an effect size of 0.8 (large), alpha of 0.05, and group sizes of n = 12 (spondylolysis) and n = 148 (controls), the achieved power (1 − β) exceeded 0.81, indicating sufficient statistical sensitivity for key functional outcomes.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics, Version 30.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Continuous variables were summarized as means with standard deviations (SD) for normally distributed data, or as medians with interquartile ranges (IQR: 25th–75th percentiles) for non-normal distributions. Categorical variables were expressed as counts and percentages.

Normality was assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test. Between-group comparisons were conducted using the independent samples t-test for normally distributed variables, or the Mann–Whitney U test for non-normally distributed data. Categorical variables were analyzed using Fisher’s exact test. Changes in SRS-22 subdomain and total scores from pre- to postoperative timepoints were assessed using paired samples t-tests.

In addition to p values, effect sizes (Cohen’s d or r) and 95% confidence intervals (CI) were calculated where appropriate to estimate the magnitude and precision of observed differences. To identify independent predictors of the presence of spondylolysis, a binary logistic regression model was employed, incorporating relevant demographic, clinical, and radiological variables.

Radiological and functional outcome evaluations were independently performed by two blinded musculoskeletal researchers, each with over 5 years of experience. Interobserver reliability was quantified using Cohen’s kappa coefficient and yielded a value of κ = 0.86, indicating substantial agreement. All statistical tests were two-tailed, and a p value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Mean baseline anthropometric measures were as follows: height, 159.1 ± 9.6 cm; weight, 67.1 ± 13.3 kg; and BMI, 26.6 ± 5.1 kg/m2. Lenke type 1 curves were most prevalent (43.8%), with the remaining cases distributed across types 2–6 (Table 1).

The median apical vertebral rotation was 39.8° (IQR: 27.8–54.5). Bilateral pars interarticularis defects, confirmed by CT or MRI, were identified in 12 patients (7.5%) (Table 1).

Spondylolysis subgroup analysis

All 12 patients with pars defects exhibited bilateral involvement, accompanied by clinical signs such as focal tenderness, positive extension tests, and paraspinal muscle spasm. The median preoperative VAS score was 5 (IQR: 5–6), and the postoperative score was 6 (IQR: 5.5–6). Although a numerical increase was observed, this change was not statistically significant (p = 0.738) and does not indicate a clinically meaningful worsening of pain. However, the absence of postoperative improvement, contrary to the typical expectation of pain reduction following corrective surgery suggests that underlying pars defects may contribute to persistent pain and diminished functional recovery, despite deformity correction. Defects were localized to L4 in 25% (n = 3) and L5 in 75% (n = 9) of cases. Functional assessments, including single-leg stance and analgesic requirements, revealed pronounced limitations in this subgroup.

Comparative demographic and radiographic findings

No significant differences were observed between groups in age (p = 0.808), sex (p = 0.699), anthropometric measures (height: p = 0.762; weight: p = 0.750; BMI: p = 0.971), or Lenke classification (p = 0.539). However, apical vertebral rotation was significantly greater in the spondylolysis group (p = 0.028), suggesting more severe three-dimensional deformity.

Surgical outcomes and complications

While operative duration was longer in the pars defect group, this difference was not statistically significant (p = 0.053). In contrast, intraoperative blood loss was significantly higher in these patients (p = 0.010) (Table 2).

Longitudinal within-group changes

In controls, all SRS-22 domains improved significantly postoperatively (p < 0.001). In contrast, the pars defect group showed no improvement in pain (p = 0.738), though activity (p = 0.027), self-image (p < 0.001), mental health (p = 0.041), and total scores (p < 0.001) demonstrated gains (Table 4).

Delta (∆) score analysis

Postoperative improvements (∆SRS-22) were significantly attenuated in the pars defect group for pain (p < 0.001), activity (p = 0.003), and total scores (p = 0.021). No intergroup differences were observed for self-image (p = 0.480) or mental health (p = 0.751). These findings suggest that pars defects may impede postoperative recovery, particularly in pain relief and functional restoration (Table 5).

Discussion

It is well established that scoliosis is a three-dimensional deformity across the coronal, sagittal, and axial planes, yet contemporary planning remains largely morphology centric, risking neglect of biomechanical and functional determinants and of its multidisciplinary nature. Assessment should integrate CT (osseous morphology/rotation) and MRI (discs, marrow, neural elements) with dynamic 3D motion analysis via electromagnetic tracking (e.g., FASTRAK) to quantify in-vivo rotation and translation, thereby informing alignment targets, fusion-level selection, and postoperative risk stratification [25]. In clinical practice, concomitant pathologies that may directly affect functional outcomes are often secondary, which may lead to suboptimal surgical decisions in some patient groups. Especially in AIS patients presenting with symptomatic low back pain, systematic investigation of possible underlying structural lesions by going beyond the morphologic curvature is vital for both patient satisfaction and postoperative quality of life [5, 26, 27]. Against published MCID ≈0.4–0.5 for SRS-22 domains, our between-group differences in change + 1.20 for Pain and + 0.78 for Activity are > 1.5–2 × MCID, indicating clinically consequential shortfalls in postoperative recovery among adolescents with pars defects. Given the SRS-22’s strong reliability and responsiveness in Turkish AIS cohorts, these differences exceed plausible measurement error and align with the markedly higher DJK rate in the pars-defect group. These data support targeted counseling and construct/rehabilitation strategies for this subgroup. In this context, spondylolysis constitutes an often overlooked but highly clinically relevant example in the complexity of scoliosis. Our study aims to shed light on this specific patient subgroup, which is underrepresented in the current literature, by demonstrating that the pars interarticularis defect accompanying AIS can have significant and clinically meaningful effects on surgical outcomes and postoperative quality of life. This approach may create an important awareness of the need for individualized treatment strategies in scoliosis surgery.

In the existing literature, the association of spondylolysis with AIS has generally been considered incidental [8]. Our findings clearly indicate that the coexistence of spondylolysis in AIS is not a clinically neutral condition; on the contrary, it exerts a significant and measurable impact on surgical outcomes. Patients with spondylolysis demonstrated notably inferior postoperative results compared to the classic AIS population. Specifically, significantly lower SRS-22 scores, lack of meaningful improvement in pain scores, and in some cases increased postoperative pain suggest that this subgroup represents a distinct and more functionally disadvantaged phenotype. Bilateral L4–L5 pars defects, in particular, appear to create localized zones of instability that disrupt physiological load transmission. This biomechanical imbalance may accelerate degenerative changes, especially in distal junctional areas such as those predisposed to developing DJK, and emerge as a source of persistent postoperative complaints. These findings underscore the importance of identifying and thoroughly evaluating structural anomalies accompanying AIS prior to surgical intervention, as such considerations may be critical in optimizing functional outcomes and improving patient satisfaction.

These findings propose a novel evaluative paradigm for AIS surgery—one that extends beyond mere curve magnitude and places biomechanical integrity at its core. A particularly striking observation reinforcing this perspective is that all patients who developed DJK exhibited both pars defects and loosening of pedicle screws at the distal instrumented levels. This pattern suggests that DJK may not be solely the result of segmental malalignment or sagittal imbalance, but could also arise from inadequate instrumentation in a biomechanically compromised segment due to underlying structural defects. While conventional risk factors in DJK etiology typically emphasize sagittal alignment, fusion extent, or ligamentous disruption, our study highlights spondylolysis and the associated segmental instability as underrecognized yet potentially pivotal contributors to this complication [19, 28]. From this perspective, we advocate that pars defects should not be regarded merely as a preoperative radiological detail, but rather as a biomechanical vulnerability that may serve as a precursor to postoperative mechanical failure.

The observed increase in operative time and intraoperative blood loss among patients with pars defects further underscores the greater technical complexity of surgical intervention in this subgroup. These intraoperative challenges emphasize the need to integrate systematic pars assessment into preoperative imaging protocols and to reconsider the selection of distal fixation levels in surgical planning. Moreover, the consistent presence of structural degenerative changes, such as T2-weighted MRI signal loss, Modic endplate changes, and sclerosis detected on CT in all AIS patients with spondylolysis demonstrates a strong correlation with functional impairments, including pain and restricted range of motion. This robust clinical-radiological concordance supports the notion that pars defects should not be viewed merely as incidental morphological variants but rather as biomechanically active stress focal points with significant implications for postoperative function [29].

At this juncture, our findings are supported by key contributions in the existing literature. Sairyo et al. emphasized that rotational stress during skeletal maturation plays a critical role in the development of pars defects, particularly in physically active adolescents [30]. Similarly, Leone et al. highlighted that bilateral pars defects are not merely static anomalies but may progress over time to cause segmental instability and clinically significant low back pain. These observations align with our results and reinforce the pathological relevance of pars interarticularis defects in the AIS population [31]. Furthermore, the findings of Zusman et al. who reported significantly reduced quality of life in adolescents with spondylolysis, are in direct concordance with our results. This parallel further strengthens the clinical relevance of our observations and highlights the necessity of recognizing spondylolysis as a determinant of postoperative outcomes in AIS patients [32]. On the other hand, traditional literature has often characterized spondylolysis as incidental and largely asymptomatic findings, particularly in the adolescent population [33]. However, in our study, all patients with bilateral pars defects exhibited both pain and functional impairment, suggesting that the traditional view may represent only the asymptomatic end of the pars defect spectrum. This discrepancy underscores the need for a more nuanced interpretation of pars lesions. Specifically, clinical evaluation of pars defects should incorporate not only their presence but also their anatomical localization (with L5 being particularly relevant), laterality (unilateral vs. bilateral), and the presence of associated degenerative changes. Such a comprehensive assessment may be essential in identifying patients at higher risk for postoperative morbidity.

Taken together, these findings suggest that a variable not traditionally considered in AIS surgical planning spondylolysis may have a direct impact on surgical success. Accordingly, we advocate for the routine preoperative screening of AIS patients using CT or MRI to systematically assess for the presence of spondylolysis. In cases where such defects are identified, distal fusion levels should be carefully re-evaluated, and instrumentation strategies appropriately modified to account for underlying biomechanical vulnerabilities [34–36]. Particular attention should be given to cases involving bilateral pars defects at the L4–L5 level, where the decision to include these segments within the fusion construct must be carefully evaluated. Across the spectrum of surgical strategies for idiopathic scoliosis from posterior multisegmental pedicle screw constructs and hybrid/sublaminar wiring to growth-friendly magnetically controlled growing rods patients with concomitant spondylolysis should be considered for motion-preserving direct pars repair to maintain segmental kinematics and biomechanical integrity, an approach supported by contemporary standards in deformity correction and comparative evidence on direct repair techniques [37–40]. Moreover, patients with concomitant spondylolysis should be subjected to more vigilant postoperative surveillance. Early implementation of targeted pain management strategies and functional rehabilitation protocols is crucial not only to mitigate long-term disability, but also to preserve surgical gains and improve overall quality of life. Failing to recognize this vulnerable subgroup may result in overlooked complications and suboptimal outcomes.

The most compelling strength of this study lies in its pioneering and comprehensive evaluation of the independent impact of concomitant spondylolysis on surgical outcomes and health-related quality of life in adolescents with AIS. To our knowledge, this is one of the few studies to delineate this association with such methodological rigor integrating advanced imaging modalities, robust statistical modeling, and a large, demographically balanced patient cohort. While spondylolysis has been historically regarded in the literature as an incidental or secondary radiographic finding, our data provide clear evidence that it constitutes a clinically significant subgroup with distinct functional implications. The detailed pre- and postoperative score analysis performed in a substantial AIS population substantiates a paradigm shift emphasizing that pars defects are not benign anomalies, but biomechanically active lesions that warrant explicit consideration during surgical planning. To ensure diagnostic precision, the presence of pars defects was confirmed through both CT and MRI, with concordant expert-level verification. Potential confounding variables were controlled through multivariate regression analyses, and the long-term validity of outcomes was reinforced by a minimum follow-up of 2 years. Importantly, all patient groups were matched for age, sex, BMI, and curve characteristics to enhance the reliability and generalizability of our findings. Collectively, these methodological strengths elevate this study beyond observational reporting and position it as a catalyst for reevaluating the current surgical decision-making framework in AIS. By integrating the structural and functional significance of pars interarticularis defects, this work lays the foundation for a more nuanced, biomechanics-informed approach to spinal deformity correction in adolescents. Nonetheless, certain limitations warrant consideration. The retrospective design and single-center data structure may restrict the broader generalizability of our findings. Additionally, the relatively small number of patients identified with pars defects, while reflective of the true clinical prevalence, limits the statistical power for detailed subgroup analyses. Furthermore, the exclusion of unilateral pars defects undertaken to ensure phenotypic homogeneity means that our conclusions pertain primarily to more severe and symmetric forms of spondylolysis. Future multicenter, prospective studies with larger and more diverse patient populations will be essential to validate and extend these observations.

Future research should aim to incorporate longer-term functional outcome data, assess the efficacy of minimally invasive surgical approaches in patients with pars defects, and explore the integration of this anatomical variant into established surgical algorithms. Recognizing and addressing spondylolysis in preoperative planning has the potential to redefine the surgical paradigm in AIS management. In this regard, our study goes beyond raising mere clinical awareness; it delivers a transformative contribution that may shape the evolving landscape of scoliosis surgery.

Conclusion

Spondylolysis is a clinically significant comorbidity in AIS, linked to persistent pain, functional decline, and mechanical failure such as DJK. Our findings underscore the need for routine preoperative screening and personalized surgical planning. Recognizing pars defects as a modifiable risk factor may redefine current paradigms and improve long-term outcomes in scoliosis management.

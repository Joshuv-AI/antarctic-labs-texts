Meconopsis quintuplinervia Regel Improves Cutibacterium acnes-Induced Inflammatory Responses in a Mouse Ear Edema Model and Suppresses Pro-Inflammatory Chemokine Production via the MAPK and NF-κB Pathways in RAW264.7 Cells
Authors: Liying Gao, Mi Xie, Xiayu Zhang, Zhenhan Qiu, Zhen Pu, Shan Huang, Bin Li
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAcne vulgaris (AV) is a common adolescent skin condition which is mainly caused by Cutibacterium acnes overcolonization and subsequent inflammation.ObjectiveOur previous studies demonstrated that ethanol extracts of Meconopsis quintuplinervia Regel (EMQ) possess significant antimicrobial properties. However, their protective effects and potential mechanisms against AV remain unclear.MethodsIn the present study, the EMQ treatment potential for AV was evaluated in a C. acnes-induced mouse ear edema model, and the EMQ anti-inflammatory mechanism was evaluated in lipopolysaccharide (LPS)-induced RAW264.7 macrophage cells.ResultsThe results showed that EMQ alleviated edema formation and inflammatory cell infiltration in an acne mouse model by suppressing inflammatory cytokines interleukin (IL)-6, IL-1β, and tumor necrosis factor α expression. Moreover, EMQ inhibited the phosphorylation of MAP kinases (MAPKs) such as p38, JNK, and ERK, the phosphorylation and degradation of IκB-α and the nuclear translocation of nuclear factor kappa B (NF-κB) p65 in LPS-induced RAW264.7 cells.ConclusionThese findings suggest the potent anti-inflammatory activity of EMQ is possibly through the regulation of the MAPKs and NF-κB signaling pathways. Inhibition of C. acnes activity combined with a powerful anti-inflammatory effect of EMQ indicated its potential as a novel therapeutic option for AV.Keywords: Acne vulgaris, Meconopsis quintuplinervia Regel, Mice, Mitogen activated protein kinase, NF-kappa B, RAW264.7

INTRODUCTION

Acne vulgaris (AV), a prevalent dermatological ailment, can affect the face, neck, chest, back, and shoulders, among other pilosebaceous regions. The main clinical symptoms of AV include seborrheic, non-inflammatory skin lesions, inflammatory lesions, and scarring of varying degrees1,2. Over 85% of young individuals worldwide are affected by AV, and symptoms can linger into adulthood3.

The causes of AV include Cutibacterium acnes colonization of the pilosebaceous follicles, androgen-induced production of sebum, altered keratinization, and inflammation4,5. All AV lesions, including microcomedones, inflammatory lesions, hyperpigmentation, and scarring, exhibit inflammation. It has been reported that C. acnes activates keratinocytes, macrophages, and sebocytes via TLR2, resulting in the production of tumor necrosis factor (TNF)-α , interleukin (IL)-1β, and IL-8 via nuclear factor (NF)-κB, and MAP kinases (MAPKs)6,7. The expression of these inflammatory mediators will consequently cause extensive inflammation and hair follicle rupture8.

Meconopsis quintuplinervia Regel (MQ) is a perennial herb of the genus Meconopsis Vig in the Papaveraceae family that mainly grows in the Tibet Autonomous Region. According to the "Tibetan Medicinal Record," MQ is mostly used to cure illnesses like edema, hepatitis, pneumonia, and headache. Modern pharmacological studies showed that extracts of MQ (EMQ) are rich in flavonoids and alkaloids and have antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, analgesic, and liver-protecting effects. To present, no research has been done on the overall impact and underlying mechanisms of EMQ on AV.

To better understand EMQ’s anti-AV action, the current study examined EMQ’s effects on an inflammatory condition brought on by C. acnes injection in a mouse model in vivo as well as its anti-inflammatory effects and routes in vitro in RAW264.7 cells activated by lipopolysaccharide (LPS).

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Chemicals and reagents

The Guangdong Microbial Culture Collection Center sold the C. acnes strain source GDMCC 1.243 (Guangzhou, China). Qingdao Hope Bio-Technology Co., Ltd. was used to purchase brain heart infusion broth and tryptic soy agar medium. Gibco BRL Co. provided the Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle’s Medium (DMEM), fetal bovine serum (FBS), and other tissue culture products. Santa Cruz Biotechnology provided the primary and secondary antibodies, which included anti-P-JNK, anti-JNK, anti-p-p38, anti-p38, anti-P-ERK, anti-ERK, anti-p65, anti-p-IκB-α, and anti-IκB-α antibodies for mice and rabbits. Boster Biological Technology provided the TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6 enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) kit orders. Solarbio Biotechnology Science, Inc. supplied the 4′,6-diamidino-2-phenylindole (DAPI) dye solution and fluorescein isothiocyanate (FITC)-labeled IgG secondary antibody. Sigma-Aldrich Chemical Co. was used to obtain all other chemicals.

Preparation of EMQ

Professor Zhuoma Dongzhi (Medical College, Tibet University, China) provided and authenticated MQ in August 2020. The Qingdao University of Science & Technology’s Pharmacy Department Herbarium was the source of the certified sample (No. CBTM-E124). MQ leaves that had been ground up were extracted twice using 70% ethanol and refluxed for 1.5 hours both times. Filter paper was used to separate the acquired liquid from the extracted solution, which was then evaporated under vacuum to produce the extract powder. Dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) was used to dissolve ethanol extract in further trials, and the mixture was stored at –20℃ for further research.

Strain activation and suspension preparation

A sterile syringe was used to gently suspend lyophilized bacteria during liquid culture material seeding in tubes. C. acnes was grown in 100 ml of tryptic soy broth in a bag that produces anaerobic gas for 48 hours at 37℃. The bacterial suspensions were diluted to the appropriate quantities with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) for the subsequent experiments.

C. acnes-induced inflammation in vivo

Male Kunming mice, aged 8 to 9 weeks, were supplied by the Qingdao Institute for Food and Drug Control (approval number: SYXK (Lu) 2021 0721). In a room that was kept at a constant temperature of 22℃±2℃ with a 12-hour light/dark cycle, all animals had unrestricted access to food and drink. The associated facilities and experimental practices at Qingdao University of Science and Technology were carried out in compliance with the Technical Standards for the Animal Ethics Committee (approval number: ACQUST-2020-053).

The animals were randomly assigned to three groups of ten. The right ear was intradermally injected with C. acnes (6×107 CFU/20 µl in PBS). The same mice had 20 µl of PBS injected into their left ear. At 24 hours following the C. acnes injection, EMQ (100 mg/ml) (administration group), tretinoin cream (positive group), and PBS (model group) were applied to the surface of the right ear skin every day for a total of 7 days of treatment. EMQ (a liquid solution) soaked in a cotton swab was evenly applied after shaking it well before each use. After the treatment period was up, the animals were put to death and had their ears cut off. On the day of euthanasia, ear thickness and weight were determined. Following homogenization in sterile saline, the ear was centrifuged at 3,000×g for 5 minutes. The supernatant was obtained after centrifugation, IL-1β, IL-6, and TNF-α expressions were determined by ELISA. Standard procedures were followed to embed slices of formalin-preserved mouse ears in paraffin. Hematoxylin and eosin (H&E)-stained longitudinal sections were examined utilizing a microscope (Leica CM1860).

Cell culture and viability assay

RAW264.7 macrophage cell line was supplied for use in the current investigation by the American Type Culture Collection. The cells were grown in DMEM-enhanced media at 37℃ with 5% CO2 and 10% heat-inactivated FBS, as well as 100 U/ml penicillin.

EMQ cytotoxicity was evaluated with the MTT assay. RAW264.7 macrophages were seeded into 96-well plates (100 µl, 1×105 cells/ml in each well), treated with various EMQ concentrations (25, 50, 100, 200, 400, and 800 µg/ml), and incubated for 24 hours. 100 µl of 2.5 mg/ml MTT was then added to each well, and the cells were then given another 4 hours of incubation. After removing the supernatant, to dissolve the formazan crystals that had developed in the living cells, 150 µl of DMSO was applied. Each well’s absorbance was determined at 490 nm using a microplate reader.

Determination of inflammation factors

Prior to the injection of LPS, EMQ was pretreated for 12 hours on RAW264.7 cells (1×105 cells/ml). The culture media were collected approximately 24 hours post-treatment with LPS and stored at –80℃. Nitric oxide (NO) was determined by measuring nitrite, which is a stable end product of NO, using the Griess reaction. In a nutshell, 100 µl of supernatant and the same amount of Griess reagent were combined, and a microplate reader was used to measure the mixture’s absorbance at 525 nm. ELISA kits were used to find out the concentrations of TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6.

Extraction of nuclear and cytosolic fractions

The procedure followed the manufacturer’s instructions for separating the nucleus and cytoplasm (Solarbio Technology Co., Ltd.). Briefly stated, the cells were collected, refloated in a cytosolic extraction reagent to aid in their lysis, and incubated at 4℃ for 10 minutes. The cytoplasmic extract was present in the centrifugation supernatant. Nuclear lysis was aided by resuspending the nuclear precipitate in a nuclear extraction reagent and vortexing it at 4℃ for 15 minutes. The nuclear extract was found in the supernatant of the centrifuged material. The Bradford analysis was then used to determine the protein concentrations of the cytosol and nuclear extracts.

Western blot analysis

A 10% sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel was used to electrophorese the same quantity of protein from the cell homogenate before it was transferred to a nitrocellulose membrane. The blots were treated with the proper concentration of primary antibody after blocking for 2 hours with Tris HCl+Tween solution containing 5% nonfat dried milk. The primary antibody was then bound to the blot using a goat anti-rabbit/mouse antibody conjugated with horseradish peroxidase, and the bands were detected using an ECL Western blotting substrate (Amersham Bioscience). A ChemiDoc image analyzer (Tanon 4600; Tanon) was used to determine the protein’s intensity.

Immunofluorescence

To locate p65, RAW264.7 macrophages were inoculated on glass cover slips pretreated with tissue cultures, and the treated cells were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for 30 minutes. Subsequently, the cells were lysed with Triton X-100 to allow the p65 antibody to enter the cells. A secondary antibody (from Santa Cruz Biotechnology) that was FITC-labeled was used to label the cells. To locate the nucleus, the cells were incubated with a DAPI solution for 30 minutes. Finally, the glass slide covered with the cover glass was observed under a fluorescence microscope (CKX53; Olympus).

Statistical analysis

The mean and standard deviation are shown for all data. A double-tailed t-test was used to examine differences between the two groups. There was a significant difference between the groups when p<0.05.

RESULTS

Effect of EMQ on C. acnes-induced inflammation in vivo

With the use of a mouse model, we investigated the pathophysiological impact of EMQ in vivo. When compared to ears that had only received C. acnes injections, the EMQ-treated ears displayed significantly less erythema (Fig. 1A), thickness (Fig. 1C), and weight (Fig. 1D). In order to track pathophysiological changes in the ear tissues and further investigate the inflammatory responses in the skin, H&E staining was done. The injection of C. acnes caused edema and a rise in the number of inflammatory cells invading the dermis. Smearing with EMQ, by contrast, resulted in significantly reduced C. acnes-induced edema and inflammatory cell infiltration (Fig. 1B). We subsequently performed ELISA to detect the levels of the inflammatory cytokines IL-6 (Fig. 1E), IL-1β (Fig. 1F), and TNF-α (Fig. 1G) to determine whether the EMQ treatment reduced ear inflammation. EMQ use consistently reduced these inflammatory cytokines. These results demonstrated that EMQ decreased the skin inflammation caused by C. acnes in this mouse model.

Effects of EMQ on cell viability

Cell viability was evaluated using the MTT test to ascertain the cytotoxic potential of EMQ. As presented in Fig. 2, EMQ was not harmful at concentrations of 25 to 200 g/ml. The cells were, therefore, given EMQ in a concentration range of 25 to 200 g/ml for all subsequent studies.

Effects of EMQ on the production of pro-inflammatory cytokines in LPS-induced RAW264.7 cells

To test the effect on pro-inflammatory mediators, RAW264.7 cells were treated with LPS (1 g/ml) in the absence or presence of EMQ at dosages of 25 to 200 g/ml for 24 hours. As shown in Fig. 3, EMQ reduced NO, TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6 production relative to the control in a dose-dependent manner.

The cells were further incubated for 24 hours, and the production of NO (A), TNF-α (B), IL-1β (C), and IL-6 (D) was determined using ELISA kits. Data represent the means of three independent experiments±standard deviation. NO: nitric oxide, TNF: tumor necrosis factor, IL: interleukin, EMQ: extracts of Meconopsis quintuplinervia Regel. *p<0.05 vs. LPS group; **p<0.01 vs. LPS group; ††p<0.01 vs. control group.

Effects of EMQ on MAPK pathway activity in LPS-induced RAW264.7 cells

The MAPK activation level was investigated by western blotting analysis to ascertain the impact of the anti-inflammatory capabilities of EMQ on the MAPK signaling pathway. As shown in Fig. 4, in LPS-treated (1 g/ml) RAW264.7 cells, the p38, JNK, and ERK phosphorylation levels were noticeably elevated. In contrast, EMQ treatment decreased LPS-induced activation of the MAPKs p38, JNK, and ERK.

Effects of EMQ on NF-κB pathway activity in LPS-induced RAW264.7 cells

The transcription and activation of NF-κB are key processes in the synthesis of pro-inflammatory mediators. Therefore, we investigated how EMQ affected the expression of various components of the NF-κB (p65) pathway. IκBα was phosphorylated in RAW264.7 macrophages following exposure to LPS (Fig. 5A), but the impact was considerably diminished by pretreating the cells with EMQ (25~200 µg/ml) for 12 hours. Additionally, fluorescence microscopy showed that EMQ reduced p65 nuclear translocation (Fig. 5C).

DISCUSSION

The bulk of treatments used to treat AV at the moment are antibiotics and synthetic retinoids, which are synthetic chemicals9. However, these medications have the potential to increase photosensitivity, immunological hypersensitivity, and skin irritation and dryness10. In order to successfully cure AV, research has shown that a certain dose of Chinese herbal extracts is safer than chemical raw materials11. Our earlier research revealed that EMQ has a variety of active components, including flavonoids and alkaloids, as well as compounds that inhibit C. acnes12. However, it is still unknown how protective effects and putative AV defense systems work.

Pro-inflammatory cytokine release has been identified as one of the main factors contributing to the development and progression of AV13,14. TNF-α is a vital pro-inflammatory cytokine that is essential for controlling inflammation and innate immunity15,16,17. It has been shown that IL-1β is an effective inducer of sebocyte production of the pro-inflammatory cytokines IL-6 and IL-818,19,20. Furthermore, it’s been shown that inflammatory acne lesions contain a lot of IL-1β in its active form21,22,23. According to a recent study, IL-1β, IL-6, and TNF-α exacerbated dermatitis-like skin inflammation24,25. Therefore, it may be possible to cure AV by preventing the production and release of pro-inflammatory cytokines. The current study examined the anti-inflammatory effects of EMQ using a mouse model of skin inflammation brought on by C. acnes. The results showed that EMQ significantly decreased the production of the pro-inflammatory cytokines and chemokines IL-1β, IL-6, and TNF-α. Additionally, EMQ therapy also decreased the model mouse ears’ swelling and redness symptoms. According to these findings, EMQ might help reduce the mouse model’s skin inflammation brought on by C. acnes.

The body’s inflammatory signaling pathways are regulated by a variety of complex mechanisms, with MAPKs and NF-κB as important regulatory mechanisms26,27. The nucleus can receive the signal from activated NF-κB and MAPKs and, in turn, regulate the expression of several inflammatory cytokines28. The primary mechanism by which IκB kinase phosphorylates IκB is the activation of NF-κB. The inactive NF-κB is subsequently separated from the phosphorylated IκB protein, which results in an active state. The transcription of genes linked to inflammation is then activated after NF-κB moves to the nucleus.

The nucleus can receive the signal from activated MAPKs and NF-κB and then control the expression of a variety of inflammatory cytokines29,30. IκB kinase-mediated phosphorylation of IκBα is the primary mechanism by which NF-κB is activated. IκBα protein that has been phosphorylated is then ubiquitinated and destroyed, separating inactive NF-κB from IκB, resulting in an active state31. NF-κB then translocates to the nucleus and induces the transcription of inflammation-related genes32. In addition, NF-κB activation is also regulated by the upstream MAPK family, including JNK, ERK, and p38MAPK33. MAPKs rapidly achieve signal transmission by converting from the non-phosphorylated to the phosphorylated state, which in turn promotes the phosphorylation of downstream substrates34. As a result, it has been suggested that inhibiting MAPKs and NF-κB activation is a promising therapeutic target for the anti-inflammatory approach to C. acnes-induced inflammation. According to our findings, EMQ therapy reduced the phosphorylation of MAPK and the activation of NF-κB. By inhibiting MAPK pathways and subsequently activating NF-κB, EMQ may be able to reduce the production of pro-inflammatory cytokines.

Our findings demonstrated that EMQ significantly decreased the production of pro-inflammatory cytokines in a mouse model of C. acnes-induced otoedema. By suppressing the MAPKs and NF-κB signaling pathways, which in turn lowered the generation of pro-inflammatory enzymes and cytokines, we also showed that EMQ reduced inflammation in LPS-induced RAW264.7 cells (Fig. 6). These results indicated the potential of EMQ as a clinical AV treatment and served as the foundation for the development of novel anti-AV medications.

TIBSTC-CoT: A Multi-Domain Instruction Dataset for Chain-of-Thought Reasoning in Language Models
Fan Gao, Cheng Huang, Nyima Tashi, Yutong Liu, Xiangxiang Wang, Thupten Tsering, Ban Ma-bao, Renzeg Duojie, Gadeng Luosang, Rinchen Dongrub, Dorje Tashi, Xiao Feng, Hao Wang, Yongbin Yu

Abstract
To address the severe data scarcity in Tibetan, a low-resource language spoken by over six million people, we introduce TIBSTC-CoT, the large-scale, multi-domain Tibetan dataset automatically constructed via chain-of-thought prompting with large language models (LLMs). TIBSTC-CoT establishes a scalable and reproducible framework for dataset creation in low-resource settings, covering diverse domains and reasoning patterns essential for language understanding and generation. Building on this dataset, we develop the Sunshine-thinking LLM family, a series of Tibetan-centric LLMs equipped with chain-of-thought capabilities. Trained entirely on TIBSTC-CoT, Sunshine-thinking has demonstrated strong reasoning and generation performance, comparable to state-of-the-art (SOTA) multilingual LLMs. Our work marks a significant step toward inclusive AI by enabling high-quality Tibetan language processing through both resource creation and model innovation. All data are available: https://github.com/Vicentvankor/sun-shine .



Introduction

Tibetan is a low-resource language spoken by over six million people across regions of China and the Himalayas
(
Joshi et al. 2020a
;
Anastasopoulos et al. 2020
;
Liu et al. 2024
)
. Despite its cultural significance, Tibetan remains underrepresented in natural language processing (NLP)
(
Liu et al. 2025a
;
Liu et al. 2025b
)
, due to the lack of publicly available, high-quality language resources, especially instruction data, hindering the development of effective large language models (LLMs).

In contrast, LLMs have achieved impressive results in high-resource settings, powering tasks such as classification, question answering, summarization, translation, and dialogue
(
Ouyang et al. 2022b
;
Chung et al. 2022
;
Longpre et al. 2023
)
. These advances are driven by large-scale pretraining and instruction tuning, which align models to follow user intent via curated question-answer pairs and task-specific prompts. Models like GPT-3
(
Brown et al. 2020
)
, PaLM
(
Chowdhery et al. 2022
)
, and LLaMA
(
Touvron et al. 2023
)
, further refined through instruction datasets such as InstructGPT
(
Ouyang et al. 2022a
)
, FLAN
(
Wei et al. 2022a
;
Longpre et al. 2023
)
, and Alpaca
(
Taori et al. 2023
)
, have shown strong generalization and reasoning abilities.

However, such instruction-tuned frameworks remain largely unexplored for low-resource languages like Tibetan
(
Joshi et al. 2020b
;
Nekoto et al. 2020
)
. Absence of large-scale pretraining corpora, annotated data, and instruction-tuning resources severely limits the ability of general-purpose LLMs to process Tibetan inputs effectively
(
Winata, Madotto, and Fung 2021
;
Zhou et al. 2023
;
Huang et al. 2025
)
. This results in outputs that are often inaccurate, incoherent, or culturally insensitive, further widening the digital divide in multilingual NLP. Even multilingual models such as mT5
(
Xue et al. 2021
)
, BLOOM
(
Scao et al. 2023
)
, and XGLM
(
Lin et al. 2022
)
struggle with typologically distinct and underrepresented languages. These models rely heavily on shared scripts and co-occurrence statistics, assumptions that break down in the case of Tibetan due to its unique orthography and linguistic structure. To close this gap, dedicated resources and training strategies are needed. In particular, instruction-tuning datasets tailored to low-resource settings are essential for improving LLM alignment and usability
(
Hu et al. 2020
;
Lewis et al. 2020
;
Wang, Conneau, and Ranzato 2021
)
.

In this paper, we present an automated pipeline for generating a high-quality, multi-domain Tibetan instruction dataset using multilingual LLMs, which enables scalable dataset construction for Tibetan and provides a framework applicable to other low-resource languages
(
Liang et al. 2023
)
. Building on this dataset, we introduce the
Sunshine-Thinking
model family, fine-tuned with
Chain-of-Thought
(CoT) reasoning
(
Wei et al. 2022b
;
Kojima et al. 2022
)
to enhance interpretability and task performance. Our models demonstrate generation quality on par with leading LLMs, highlighting the effectiveness of targeted, data-driven approaches for advancing NLP in underrepresented languages.

Summary

TIBSTC-CoT thus lays a solid foundation for enabling and benchmarking explicit multi-step reasoning capabilities in Tibetan LLMs. In contrast to the TIB-STC dataset
(
Huang et al. 2025
)
, which primarily consists of single-turn instruction-following instances, TIBSTC-CoT emphasizes explicit multi-step reasoning by incorporating detailed intermediate steps in the form of CoT annotations. By leveraging SOTA LLMs at each stage of generation and validation, TIBSTC-CoT ensures rich reasoning diversity, high linguistic fidelity, and strong factual consistency.

Dataset Generation Pipeline and Agents

TIBSTC-CoT consists of only those samples that pass both automatic and manual evaluations. This multi-stage pipeline ensures that the resulting Tibetan CoT dataset is high-quality, diverse, and culturally grounded, suitable for training LLMs capable of robust reasoning in low-resource, typologically distinct languages.

Pipeline

As illustrated in Figure
1
, generation pipeline of TIBSTC-CoT consists of four sequential components: question formulation, CoT and answer generation, automatic assessment, and manual verification.

•

Question Formulation:
the Asking Agent automatically generates questions conditioned on domain-specific instructions. These instructions are sampled from a domain pool and are used to guide the agent in formulating questions that are both diverse and contextually relevant.

•

CoT and Answer Generation:
generated questions are passed to the Generation Agent, which is responsible for producing both the final answer and an intermediate CoT reasoning process. This agent utilizes strong multilingual LLMs to construct multi-step reasoning paths that align with the semantics and logic of questions.

•

Automatic Assessment:
the Assessment Agent evaluates the quality of both the answer and the CoT. This evaluation includes multiple criteria such as coherence, logical consistency, linguistic fluency, factual correctness, and cultural appropriateness. Each sample is assigned a score on a 1-to-5 scale. Only samples with a score higher than 3.5 are retained for further processing.

•

Manual Verification:
a stage of manual verification is conducted to further ensure the quality and usability of the dataset. Human annotators with domain expertise review the retained samples to validate their alignment with domain-specific knowledge and Tibetan cultural norms. This step includes checking for factual consistency, cultural sensitivity, and relevance to the intended domain.

Agents

The Generation of TIBSTC-CoT follows a multi-turn generation pipeline repeated for 90 turns to generate sufficient data. In each turn, the system generates 10 knowledge-based tasks, 10 generative tasks, and 5 classification tasks. To generate high-quality, reasoning-rich Tibetan instruction data, we design a collaborative pipeline involving three LLMs selected for their performance on the TLUE benchmark
(
Gao et al. 2025
)
: Claude-3.5-Sonnet
(
Anthropic 2024
)
, DeepSeek-R1
(
Guo and et al. 2025
)
, and Gemini-1.5-Flash
(
DeepMind 2024
)
, with sampling temperatures set to 1.0, 1.3, and 0.1, respectively..

•

DeepSeek-R1:
it acts as the response generator, producing both CoT reasoning paths and final answers. DeepSeek variants consistently outperform baselines in CAA and DA, showing strong reasoning and generalization in Tibetan.

•

Gemini-1.5-Flash:
it functions as the evaluator, scoring each (question, CoT, answer) triplet on a 5-point scale. With TLUE performance on par with Claude 3.5, it reliably judges logical coherence and linguistic quality. Only samples rated
\geq
3.5 are retained.

•

Claude-3.5-Sonnet:
Claude-3.5-Sonnet serves as the question generator, creating diverse, reasoning-focused prompts grounded in Tibetan contexts. It leads TLUE in overall accuracy, particularly in CAA and DA.

By assigning distinct roles, Claude-3.5-Sonnet for question generation, DeepSeek-R1 for reasoning and answers, and Gemini-1.5-Flash for evaluation, we mitigate model-specific bias and ensure objective quality control. These LLMs strategy leverages complementary TLUE strengths to generate a reasoning-rich, reliably validated dataset.

Experimental Results

Experimental Setup

Training Strategy

Sunshine-Thinking models are fine-tuned using supervised fine-tuning (SFT)
(
Yu et al. 2021
)
, which aligns pretrained LLMs with task-specific instruction datasets. This process helps adapt LLMs to better understand and generate responses that are more accurate, contextually grounded, and aligned with the target tasks. The training is conducted under varying configurations depending on the model size and backbone architecture.

Hyperparameter and Hardware Equipment

Sunshine-thinking models are optimized by AdamW
(
Loshchilov and Hutter 2017
)
with standard hyperparameters:
\beta_{1}=0.9
,
\beta_{2}=0.999
, and
\epsilon=1\times 10^{-8}
. Mixed-precision training is applied to reduce memory consumption while maintaining numerical stability.
Sunshine-Thinking-1.7B is based on Qwen-3-1.7B
(
Team 2025
)
. Sunshine-Thinking-8B is built on Qwen-3-8B
(
Team 2025
)
. Sunshine-Thinking-1.7B is trained on a single NVIDIA L20 GPU (48GB) with a per-device batch size of 1 and gradient accumulation over 8 steps, resulting in an effective batch size of 8. The learning rate is set to 1e-5, and training is conducted for 2 epochs. Sunshine-Thinking-8B, built on Qwen-3-8B, is trained on 3 NVIDIA H20 GPUs (96GB each), with a per-device batch size of 6 and the same gradient accumulation and learning rate settings. Training is performed for 3 epochs.

Experimental Evaluation

To assess the impact of our TIBSTC-CoT dataset and the Sunshine-thinking family trained with CoT supervision, we conduct two types of evaluations:
Understanding Evaluation
and
Downstream Task Evaluation
.

Understanding Evaluation

We assess the general knowledge and reasoning abilities of LLMs in Chinese-language contexts using the TLUE benchmark
(
Gao et al. 2025
)
. The evaluation focuses on content relevance and factual accuracy across three domains: China-specific topics, general knowledge, and social sciences.

Two kinds of metrics are introduced to measure the generation and understanding quality of LLMs.

•

DA
(Direct Answer): Measures the proportion of questions for which the model produces the correct final answer.

•

CAA
(Concern All Answer): Evaluates how well the model aligns with expected Chinese-style reasoning and contextual understanding, even if the final answer is only partially correct.

Generation Evaluation

We evaluate multilingual translation performance, focusing on low-resource directions involving Tibetan. The evaluation covers four translation pairs across Tibetan (BO), Chinese (ZH), and English (EN), assessing both understanding and generation capabilities. To ensure the comprehensiveness and reliability of the evaluation, we conduct tests using the
Flore-200 benchmark
(
Costa-jussà et al. 2022
)
, a widely recognized multilingual translation benchmark that includes a diverse range of language pairs and covers various domains, styles, and text types. This benchmark is particularly valuable for evaluating low-resource language translation due to its inclusion of underrepresented languages and its design to reflect real-world translation scenarios, which helps in better assessing the model’s adaptability and robustness in practical applications.

chrF++
is introduced as a metric to calculate character-level precision and recall, providing a better measure of fluency and semantic fidelity in morphologically rich or low-resource languages. When combined with the Flore-200 benchmark, chrF++ enables a more nuanced analysis of the model’s performance, capturing subtle differences in translation quality that may be overlooked by token-level metrics, especially for languages like Tibetan with complex morphological structures.

Conclusion

In this work, we address the critical lack of instruction-tuned resources for the Tibetan language by introducing a scalable, multilingual LLM-based pipeline for dataset construction. Through this pipeline, we release the first large-scale, multi-domain Tibetan instruction dataset, laying a solid foundation for future research in low-resource NLP. Building upon this resource, we develop the
Sunshine-Thinking
model family, enhanced with
Chain-of-Thought
reasoning to improve interpretability and reasoning performance. Extensive experiments demonstrate that our models achieve competitive generation quality compared with state-of-the-art LLMs, highlighting the effectiveness of our approach in enabling complex instruction following and question answering in a typologically distinct, low-resource language. Our work paves the way for further advancements in Tibetan language AI and provides a replicable framework for other underrepresented languages.

Lattice 3 3 -polytopes of lattice width 2 2 and corresponding toric hypersurfaces
Martin Bohnert

Abstract
. The Kodaira dimension of a nondegenerate toric hypersurface can be computed from the dimension of the Fine interior of its Newton polytope according to recent work of Victor Batyrev, where the Fine interior of the Newton polytope is the subpolytope consisting of all points which have an integral distance of at least 1 1 to all integral supporting hyperplanes. In particular, if we have a Fine interior of codimension 1 1 , then the hypersurface is of general type and the Newton polytope has lattice width 2 2 . In this article we study this situation for lattice 3 3 -polytopes and the corresponding surfaces of general type. In particular, we classify all 2 2 -dimensional Fine interiors of those lattice 3 3 -polytopes which have at most 40 40 interior lattice points, thus obtaining many examples of surfaces of general type and genus at most 40.



1.
Introduction

Let
M\cong\mathds{Z}^{d}
be a lattice of rank
d\in\mathds{Z}_{\geq 1}
and
N=\mathrm{Hom}(M\mathds{Z})\cong\mathds{Z}^{d}
the dual lattice. We have the natural pairing
\left<\cdot,\cdot\right>\colon M\times N\to\mathds{Z}
, which extends to the real vector spaces
M_{\mathds{R}}=M\otimes\mathds{R}
and
N_{\mathds{R}}=N\otimes\mathds{R}
. For a rational
d
-polytope
P
in
M_{\mathds{R}}
, i.e.
P\subseteq M_{\mathds{R}}
is the convex hull of a finite number of points of
M_{\mathds{Q}}=M\otimes\mathds{Q}
and
P
is of full dimension, we have the
Fine interior
of
P
defined as

\displaystyle F(P):=\{x\in M_{\mathds{R}}\mid\left<x,n\right>\geq\mathrm{Min}_{P}(n)+1\ \forall n\in N\setminus\{0\}\}\subseteq P

with

\displaystyle\mathrm{Min}_{P}(n):=\min\{\left<x,n\right>\mid x\in P\}.

The Fine interior is itself a rational polytope, and if
P
is the Newton polytope of a nondegenerate toric hypersurface
Z
and has a non-empty Fine interior, then recent work by Victor Batyrev
[
Bat23
]
shows that the Fine interior and additional combinatorial data can be used to construct a minimal model
\hat{Z}
of the hypersurface. Moreover, we can compute some important invariants of the minimal model directly from the Fine interior. The Kodaira dimension is given by
[
Bat23
, 9.2]
as

\displaystyle\kappa(\hat{Z})=\min\{\dim F(P),d-1\}

and, for example, for the top intersection number in the case
\dim F(P)=d-1
we have the formula (
[
Gie22
, 5.1]
,
[
Bat23
, 9.4]
)

\displaystyle(K_{\hat{Z}})^{d-1}=2\mathrm{Vol}_{d-1}(F(P)),

where
\mathrm{Vol}_{d-1}
means that we are looking at the normalized volume in the affine subspace generated by
F(P)
, where normalized means that we have volume
1
for a simplex whose vertices form an affine lattice basis of the sublattice in the subspace.

We focus in this article on the case
d=3
with
\dim F(P)=2
, so our nondegenerate toric hypersurface becomes a surface of general type. This implies that our Newton polytope
P\subseteq M_{\mathds{R}}
is a lattice
3
-polytope with lattice width
2
, where we should remember that the
lattice width

\mathrm{lw}(P)
is given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{lw}(P):=\min\{-\mathrm{Min}_{P}(-n)-\mathrm{Min}_{P}(n)\mid n\in N\setminus\{0\}.

and we call
n_{lw}\in N
a
lattice width direction
if
\mathrm{lw}(P)=-\mathrm{Min}_{P}(-n_{lw})-\mathrm{Min}_{P}(n_{lw})
.

Why is the combinatorics in this situation easy enough to handle? As a first step, we will see in Theorem
2.5
in section
2
, that the Fine interior of a lattice
d
-polytope with lattice width
2
and lattice width direction
n_{lw}
is completely determined by its half-integral
middle polytope
, i.e. the rational polytope of dimension
d-1
, which we get by the intersection of
P
with the hyperplane

\displaystyle\{x\in M_{\mathds{R}}\mid\left<x,n_{lw}\right>=\mathrm{Min}_{P}(n_{lw})+1\}.

In particular, we can work for lattice
3
-polytopes with a
middlepolygon
and only have to do combinatorics from there on only in dimension
2
. As a second step, we will see in section
3
that in this case not only the middle polygon but also the Fine interior is a half-integral polygon (Theorem
3.2
). Moreover, we will see there that the Fine interiors can always be described by the union of a lattice polygon and some very special triangles that we can have on edges of the lattice polygons having two lattice points (see Theorem
3.9
). It turns out that this description of the Fine interior is restrictive enough that we end up with an efficient classification algorithm for these Fine interiors in section
4
. We will use this algorithm there to obtain our main classification result in
4.1
: There are up to affine unimodular equivalence exactly
24324158
different
2
-dimensional Fine interiors those lattice
3
-polytopes which have at most
40
interior lattice points. In the last section we use this classification to classify as a corollary the Chern numbers for nondegenerated toric hypersurfaces of genus at most
40
which have as Newton polytope a lattice
3
-polytope with
2
-dimensional Fine interior. Having the Chern numbers of the surfaces, we can arrange them in the geography of the surfaces of general type.

2.
The Fine interior of lattice polytopes of lattice width 2

In this section we will see that the Fine interior of a lattice
d
-polytope of lattice width
2
for arbitrary dimension
d
is determined by its half-integral middle polytope, which allows us to do our Fine interior computations in codimension
1
, or in other words, without loss of generality, we can restrict ourselves for Fine interior computations to the case of pyramids of heigth
2
which have the same middle polytope.

We begin with the following lemma, which shows why the case of lattice width
2
is important for Fine interior computations.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
P\subseteq M_{\mathds{R}}
be a rational
d
-polytope. If
\mathrm{lw}(P)<2
, then
F(P)=\emptyset
. If
\mathrm{lw}(P)=2
, then
\dim F(P)<\dim P
, and if
\dim F(P)=\dim(P)-1
, then
\mathrm{lw}(P)=2
.

Proof.

If
n_{lw}\in N
is a lattice width direction, then we have

\displaystyle F(P)\subseteq\{x\in M_{\mathds{R}}\mid\left<x,n_{lw}\right>\geq\mathrm{Min}_{P}(n_{lw})+1,\left<x,-n_{lw}\right>\geq\mathrm{Min}_{P}(-n_{lw})+1\}

and the set on the right side is empty for
\mathrm{lw}(P)<2
and at of dimension
d-1
for
\mathrm{lw}(P)=2
. If we have
\dim F(P)=\dim(P)-1
, then we must have a normal vector
n\in N
for
F(P)
such that
-n
is also a normal vector and thus
-\mathrm{Min}_{P}(-n)-\mathrm{Min}_{P}(n)=2
. This implies
\mathrm{lw}(P)\leq 2
and we have
\mathrm{lw}(P)\geq 2
since this is always the case for non-empty Fine interior by the first part of the lemma.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

There are many examples of lattice polytopes
P\subseteq M_{\mathds{R}}
with lattice width greater than
2
and
\dim F(P)<\dim P-1
. Since
\dim F((d+1)\Delta_{d})=0
for the standard simplex
\Delta_{d}
, i.e. the convex hull of an affine lattice basis, and
\mathrm{lw}((d+1)\Delta_{d})=d+1
, we have for
d,k,w\in\mathds{Z},d\geq 2,0\leq k<d-1,3\leq w\leq d-k+1
the lattice
d
-polytope
(d-k+1)\Delta_{d-k}\times[0,w]^{k}
with lattice width
w>2
and
\dim(F(P))=k
.

Remark 2.3
.

We can see that
\dim(F(P))=d-1
implies
\mathrm{lw}(P)\leq 2
also from the general theory about lattice projections along the Fine interior. By
[
Bat23
, 9.1]
we have a lattice projection along the Fine interior on a lattice polytope with a Fine interior of dimension
0
. So from a Fine interior of dimension
d-1
we get a projection onto a lattice polytope of dimension
1
with a Fine interior of dimension
0
. But up to lattice translation there is only
[-1,1]
with this property, and a lattice projection onto
[-1,1]
is obviously equivalent to
\mathrm{lw}(P)\leq 2
.

We now rearrange our coordinates for a good working setup with the middle polytope and split the dual lattice
N
with respect to the middle polytope.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
P\subseteq\mathds{R}\times M_{\mathds{R}}
be a lattice polytope with
\mathrm{lw}(P)=2
and
P\subseteq[-1,1]\times M_{\mathds{R}}
. Then we have the half-integral middle polytope
P_{0}\subseteq M_{\mathds{R}}
of
P
defined by

\displaystyle\{0\}\times P_{0}=P\cap(\{0\}\times M_{\mathds{R}}).

Using
P_{0}
, we define a partition
N\setminus\{0\}=N_{1}(P_{0})\cup N_{2}(P_{0})
of the non-zero dual lattice vectors by

\displaystyle N_{1}(P_{0}):=\{n\in N\setminus\{0\}\mid\mathrm{Min}_{P_{0}}(n)\in\mathds{Z}\},N_{2}(P_{0}):=\{n\in N\setminus\{0\}\mid\mathrm{Min}_{P_{0}}(n)\notin\mathds{Z}\}.

Now we are ready to show how the middle polytope determines the Fine interior of a lattice polytope of lattice width
2
.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
P\subseteq\mathds{R}\times M_{\mathds{R}}
be a lattice polytope of lattice width
2
,
P\subseteq[-1,1]\times M_{\mathds{R}}
and
P_{0}\subseteq M_{\mathds{R}}
the middle polytope of
P
. Then the Fine interior
F(P)
of
P
is

\displaystyle F(P)=F_{1}(P)\cap F_{2}(P)

with

\displaystyle F_{1}(P):=

\displaystyle\{0\}\times\{x\in M_{\mathds{R}}\mid\left<x,n\right>\geq\mathrm{Min}_{P_{0}}(n)+1\ \forall n\in N_{1}(P_{0})\}

\displaystyle F_{2}(P):=

\displaystyle\{0\}\times\{x\in M_{\mathds{R}}\mid\left<x,2n\right>\geq\mathrm{Min}_{P_{0}}(2n)+1\ \forall n\in N_{2}(P_{0})\}.

In particular,
F(P)
is completely determined by
P_{0}
.

Proof.

Since
P\subseteq[-1,1]\times M_{\mathds{R}}
and
\mathrm{lw}(P)=2
, we have
F(P)=F(P)\cap(\{0\}\times M_{\mathds{R}})
and

\displaystyle F(P)=\{0\}\times\{x\in M_{\mathds{R}}\mid\left<(0,x),(n_{0},n)\right>\geq\mathrm{Min}_{P}((n_{0},n))+1\ \forall n_{0}\in\mathds{Z},n\in N\setminus\{0\}\}.

So we have
F(P)\subseteq\{0\}\times P_{0}
, and since
P
is a lattice polytope and
P_{0}
is half-integral, it is sufficient enough to look at those pairs
(n_{0},n)
with

\displaystyle\mathrm{Min}_{P}((n_{0},n))+\frac{1}{2}\geq\mathrm{Min}_{\{0\}\times P_{0}}((n_{0},n)).

With

\displaystyle\tilde{N_{1}}:=

\displaystyle\{(n_{0},n)\in\mathds{Z}\times(N\setminus\{0\})\mid\mathrm{Min}_{P}((n_{0},n))=\mathrm{Min}_{\{0\}\times P_{0}}((n_{0},n))\},

\displaystyle\tilde{N_{2}}:=

\displaystyle\{(n_{0},n)\in\mathds{Z}\times(N\setminus\{0\})\mid\mathrm{Min}_{P}((n_{0},n))+\frac{1}{2}=\mathrm{Min}_{\{0\}\times P_{0}}((n_{0},n))\}

we get

\displaystyle F(P)=

\displaystyle(\{0\}\times\{x\in M_{\mathds{R}}\mid\left<(0,x),(n_{0},n)\right>\geq\mathrm{Min}_{\{0\}\times P_{0}}((n_{0},n))+1\ \forall(n_{0},n)\in\tilde{N_{1}}\})\ \cap

\displaystyle(\{0\}\times\{x\in M_{\mathds{R}}\mid\left<(0,x),(n_{0},n)\right>\geq\mathrm{Min}_{\{0\}\times P_{0}}((n_{0},n))+\frac{1}{2}\ \forall(n,\nu)\in\tilde{N_{2}}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\{0\}\times\{x\in M_{\mathds{R}}\mid\left<x,n\right>\geq\mathrm{Min}_{P_{0}}(n)+1\ \forall(n_{0},n)\in\tilde{N_{1}}\})\ \cap

\displaystyle(\{0\}\times\{x\in M_{\mathds{R}}\mid\left<x,2n\right>\geq\mathrm{Min}_{P_{0}}(2n)+1\ \forall(n_{0},n)\in\tilde{N_{2}}\})

We have for
(n_{0},n)\in\tilde{N_{1}}
that
\mathrm{Min}_{P_{0}}(n)=\mathrm{Min}_{P}((n_{0},n))\in\mathds{Z}
and so
n\in N_{1}(P_{0})
. With the same argument we get
n\in N_{2}(P_{0})
for all
(n_{0},n)\in\tilde{N_{2}}
. It remains to show that for all
n\in N_{1}(P_{0})
we have a
n_{0}\in\mathds{Z}
with
(n_{0},n)\in\tilde{N_{1}}
and analogously for
N_{2}(P_{0})
and
\tilde{N_{1}}
.

Let be
n\in N_{1}(P_{0})
, i. e.
\mathrm{Min}_{P_{0}}(n)\in\mathds{Z}
. Then there is a vertex
e
of
P_{0}
with
\mathrm{Min}_{P_{0}}(n)=\left<e,n\right>
. We choose such a vertex
f=(1,f^{\prime})
of
P
such that
n_{0}:=\left<e-f^{\prime},n\right>\in\mathds{Z}
is maximal. We have

\displaystyle\left<(1,f^{\prime}),(n_{0},n)\right>=\left<f^{\prime},n\right>+n_{0}=\left<e,n\right>=\mathrm{Min}_{P_{0}}(n)

and since
\left<(0,e),(n_{0},n)\right>=\left<e,n\right>=\mathrm{Min}_{P_{0}}(n)
we get that the dual lattice vector
(n_{0},n)
defines a hyperplane containing
(0,e)
and
(1,f^{\prime})
that is also a supporting hyperplane for
P
since we have chose
n_{0}
maximal. So we get

\displaystyle\mathrm{Min}_{P}((n_{0},n))=\left<e,n\right>=\mathrm{Min}_{\{0\}\times P_{0}}((n_{0},n))

and so we have
(n_{0},n)\in\tilde{N_{1}}
.

Let be
n\in N_{2}(P_{0})
, i.e.
2\mathrm{Min}_{P_{0}}(n)\in\mathds{Z}\setminus 2\mathds{Z}
. Then there is a vertex
e
of
P_{0}
with
\mathrm{Min}_{P_{0}}(n)=\left<e,n\right>
. We now choose a vertex
f=(1,f^{\prime})
of
P
, so that
n_{0}:=\left<e-f^{\prime},n\right>-\frac{1}{2}\in\mathds{Z}
is maximal. Then we have

\displaystyle\left<(1,f^{\prime}),(n_{0},n)\right>=\left<f^{\prime},n\right>+n_{0}=\left<e,n\right>-\frac{1}{2}=\mathrm{Min}_{P_{0}}(n)-\frac{1}{2}

and so the dual lattice vector
(n_{0},n)
defines a hyperplane containing
(1,f^{\prime})
and this is also a supporting hyperplane for
P
since we chose
n_{0}
maximal. So we get

\displaystyle\mathrm{Min}_{P}((n_{0},n))+\frac{1}{2}=\left<e,n\right>=\mathrm{Min}_{\{0\}\times P_{0}}((n_{0},n))

and so we have
(n_{0},n)\in\tilde{N_{2}}
.
∎

If the middle polytope is a lattice polytope, then the situation becomes easier. This was already seen in
[
Boh24b
, 4.3]
. We now understand this situation as a corollary.

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
P\subseteq\mathds{R}\times M_{\mathds{R}}
be a lattice polytope of lattice width
2
with a lattice polytope
P_{0}\subseteq M_{\mathds{R}}
as middle polytope of
P
. Then
F(P)=\{0\}\times F(P_{0})
.

Proof.

Since
P_{0}
is a lattice polytope, we have
N_{2}(P_{0})=\emptyset,N_{1}(P_{0})=N\setminus\{0\}
and therefore
F(P)=F_{1}(P)=\{0\}\times F(P_{0})
.
∎

We now introduce some new notation to work directly in
M_{\mathds{R}}
.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
P_{0}\subseteq M_{\mathds{R}}
be a half-integral polytope. Then we set

\displaystyle\bar{F}(P_{0}):=\bar{F}_{1}(P_{0})\cap\bar{F}_{2}(P_{0})

with

\displaystyle\bar{F}_{1}(P_{0}):=

\displaystyle\{x\in M_{\mathds{R}}\mid\left<x,n\right>\geq\mathrm{Min}_{P_{0}}(n)+1\ \forall n\in N_{1}(P_{0})\}

\displaystyle\bar{F}_{2}(P_{0}):=

\displaystyle\{x\in M_{\mathds{R}}\mid\left<x,2n\right>\geq\mathrm{Min}_{P_{0}}(2n)+1\ \forall n\in N_{2}(P_{0})\}.

With this notation we now have the following short version of
2.5
.

Corollary 2.8
.

Let
P\subseteq\mathds{R}\times M_{\mathds{R}}
be a lattice polytope of lattice width
2
,
P\subseteq[-1,1]\times M_{\mathds{R}}
and
P_{0}\subseteq M_{\mathds{R}}
the middle polytope of
P
. Then we have

\displaystyle F(P)=\{0\}\times\bar{F}(P_{0}).

Remark 2.9
.

Note that
\bar{F}(P_{0})
is not in general the Fine interior of the half-integral polytope
P_{0}\subseteq M_{\mathds{R}}
. We have that
\bar{F}(P_{0})
is the Fine interior of
P_{0}
if and only if the primitive normal vectors to
F(P_{0})
are all in
N_{1}(P_{0})
. In particular,
\bar{F}(P_{0})=F(P_{0})
if
P_{0}
is a lattice polytope. We also have the following inclusions

\displaystyle F(F(2P_{0}))\subseteq 2F(P_{0})\subseteq 2\bar{F}(P_{0})\subseteq F(2P_{0}).

If the middle polytope completely determines the Fine interior, we can focus on some special polytopes with this middle polytope. For a rational polytope
P_{0}\subseteq M_{\mathds{R}}
we have the pyramid over
P
defined by
\mathrm{Pyr}(P_{0}):=\mathrm{conv}\left((1,0),\{0\}\times P_{0}\right)\subseteq\mathds{R}\times M_{\mathds{R}}
and if we translate
2\mathrm{Pyr}(P_{0})
by the lattice vector
(-1,0)
to a subpolytope of
[-1,1]\times M_{\mathds{R}}
, then we get
P_{0}
as the middle polytope. So we have the following corollary.

Corollary 2.10
.

In the situation from
2.5
we have

\displaystyle F(P)=\{0\}\times\bar{F}(P_{0})\cong F(2\cdot\mathrm{Pyr}(P_{0}))

Moreover, if
P_{0}
is even a lattice polytope, then we also have

\displaystyle F(P)=F([-1,1]\times P_{0}).

3.
The Fine interior of a lattice
3
-polytope of lattice width
2

Remark 3.1
.

Let
P
be as in the theorem, with a lattice polygon
P_{0}\subseteq M_{\mathds{R}}
as middle polytope of
P
.

Then
F(P)=F(P_{0})\times\{0\}=\mathrm{conv}\left(\mathrm{int}(P)\cap M\right)
is a lattice polygon.

3.1.
The Fine interior of a lattice
3
-polytope of lattice width
2
is a half-integral polygon

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
M\cong\mathds{Z}^{2}
be a lattice of rank
2
,
P\subseteq\mathds{R}\times M_{\mathds{R}}
a lattice
3
-polytope of lattice width
2
,
P\subseteq[-1,1]\times M_{\mathds{R}}
, and
\dim(F(P))=2
. Then
F(P)\subseteq\{0\}\times M_{\mathds{R}}
is a half-integral polygon.

Proof.

Let be
P_{0}\subseteq M_{\mathds{R}}
the middle polygon of
P
. By
2.5
it is sufficient to show that
\bar{F}(P_{0})
is a half-integral polygon. To see this, we will prove that

\displaystyle\bar{F}(P_{0})\subseteq\mathrm{conv}\left(x\in\bar{F}(P_{0})\mid 2x\in M\right).

We can assume that we have at least two lattice points in
\bar{F}(P_{0})
, since lattice 3-polytopes with at most
1
interior lattice point cannot have a Fine interior of dimension
2
by
[
BKS22
]
. So
\mathrm{conv}\left(x\in\bar{F}(P_{0})\mid 2x\in M\right)
has at least dimension
1
and we can look at any edge
e\preceq\mathrm{conv}\left(x\in\bar{F}(P_{0})\mid 2x\in M\right)
. Now it is enough to see that
e
is also an edge of
\bar{F}(P_{0})
. We have two cases, either the affine hull of
e
contains lattice points or it does not.

If the affine hull of
e
contains lattice points, then after a suitable affine unimodular transformation we can assume that the line segment
\mathrm{conv}\left((0,0),(1/2,0)\right)
is a subset of
e
and that we have no points of
\mathrm{conv}\left(x\in\bar{F}(P_{0})\mid 2x\in M\right)
with positive second coordinate. If we can now show that there are no points of
P_{0}
with second coordinate greater than
1
, we get that there are no points of
\bar{F}(P_{0})
with positive second coordinate and so we are done. Let
x_{m}=(x_{m1},x_{m2})\in P_{0}
be a half-integral point with maximal second coordinate. Then the triangle
\mathrm{conv}\left((0,0),(1/2,0),x_{m}\right)
contains a half-integral point with second coordinate
1/2
by Pick’s theorem. With a suitable shearing we can assume that this point is
(1/2,1/2)
and so we get that
1/2\leq x_{m1}\leq x_{m2}
by convexity of the triangle. If
x_{m2}>1
, then we have for
n\in N\setminus\{0\}
at least one of the following inequalities

\displaystyle\left<(0,0),n\right>\leq

\displaystyle\left<(1/2,1/2),n\right>\ \text{for}\text{or}

\displaystyle\left<(1/2,0),n\right>\leq

\displaystyle\left<(1/2,1/2),n\right>\ \text{or}

\displaystyle\left<(x_{m},n\right><\left<(1,1),n\right><

\displaystyle\left<(1/2,1/2),n\right>\ \text{or}

\displaystyle\left<(x_{m},n\right><\left<(1/2,1),n\right><

\displaystyle\left<(1/2,1/2),n\right>.

These inequalities imply
\left<(1/2,1/2),n\right>\geq\mathrm{Min}_{\bar{F}(P_{0})}(n)
and so we get
(1/2,1/2)\in\mathrm{conv}\left(x\in\bar{F}(P_{0})\mid 2x\in M\right)
which contradicts
x_{m2}>1
. So we must have
x_{m2}\leq 1
and so we are done.

If the affine hull of
e
contains no lattice points then we start our argument in a similar way. After a suitable affine unimodular transformation we can assume that the line segment
\mathrm{conv}\left((0,-1/2),(1/2,-1/2)\right)
is a subset of
e
and we have no points of
\mathrm{conv}\left(x\in\bar{F}(P_{0})\mid 2x\in M\right)
with second coordinate greater than
-1/2
. If we can now proof that there are no points of
P_{0}
with second coordinate greater than
0
, we are done. Let
x_{m}=(x_{m1},x_{m2})\in P_{0}
again be a half-integral point with maximal second coordinate. Then, by Pick’s theorem, the triangle
\mathrm{conv}\left((0,-1/2),(1/2,-1/2),x_{m}\right)
contains a half-integral point with second coordinate
0
. With another affine unimodular transformation we can assume that this point is
(1/2,0)
and get so that
1/2\leq x_{m1}\leq x_{m2}+1/2
from the convexity of the triangle. With analogous inequalities as in the first case, we now get that
x_{m2}\leq 1/2
. So if
x_{m2}>0
, then we have
x_{m}=(1/2,1/2)
or
x_{m}=(1,1/2)
. In the first case we get
\mathrm{Min}_{P_{0}}((1,-1))=0
, because otherwise we get
(0,0)\in\mathrm{int}(P_{0})
and therefore the contradiction
(0,0)\in\bar{F}(P_{0})
. But
\mathrm{Min}_{P_{0}}((1,-1))=0
also leads to a contradiction, since
(0,-1/2)\in\bar{F}(P_{0})
. Similary we get contradictions if
x_{m}=(1,1/2)
. All in all we see that
x_{m2}\leq 0
and so we are done.
∎

The theorem also gives us also possibilities to compute
\bar{F}(P_{0})
, since we only have to check which half-integral points are contained. If we know the support of the Fine interior of the lattice polygon
2P_{0}
, i.e. all the dual lattice vectors
n\in N
with
\mathrm{Min}_{2P_{0}}(n)+1=\mathrm{Min}_{F(2P_{0})}(n)
, we can use the following corollary. Note that the Fine interior here is just the convex hull of the interior lattice points, since
2P_{0}
is a lattice polygon (
[
Bat17
, 2.9]
).

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
P
be as in the theorem,
P_{0}
its half-integral middle polygon. Then we get
2\bar{F}(P_{0})
as the convex hull of the following finite sets of lattice points

\displaystyle\{x\in\partial F(2P_{0})\cap M\mid\left<x,n\right>\neq\mathrm{Min}_{2P_{0}}(n)+1\forall n\in S_{F}(2P_{0})\cap N_{1}(P_{0})\},

\displaystyle F(F(2P_{0}))\cap M.

From the proof of the theorem we get another corollary. We have shown there that an edge of
\bar{F}(P_{0})
without lattice point has only lattice distance
1/2
to a supporting line which contains therefore lattice points. But this means that such an edge of
\bar{F}(P_{0})
cannot exist and we get the following corollary.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
P
be as in the theorem with
\dim F(P)=2
. Then we have for all edges
E\preceq F(P)
that
E\cap M\neq\emptyset
.

3.2.
The Fine interior vs. the convex hull of the interior lattice points

For a lattice polygon, we know from
[
Bat17
, 2.9]
that the Fine interior is just the convex hull of the interior lattice points. For
\bar{F}
of a half-integral polygon the situation is more complicated, but there are also some connections between
\bar{F}
and the convex hull of the interior lattice points that we want to study in this subsection.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
P_{0}\subseteq M_{\mathds{R}}
be a half-integral polygon with at least two interior lattice points,
e\preceq\mathrm{conv}\left(\mathrm{int}(P_{0})\cap M\right)
an edge and
v\preceq\mathrm{conv}\left(\mathrm{int}(P_{0})\cap M\right)
a vertex. Then
e
is an edge of
\bar{F}(P_{0})
or
|e\cap M|=2
and
v
is a boundary lattice point of
\bar{F}(P_{0})
.

Proof.

First, we show that an edge
e\preceq\mathrm{conv}\left(\mathrm{int}(P_{0})\cap M\right)
with
|e\cap M|>2
is an edge of
\bar{F}(P_{0})
. After a suitable affine unimodular transformation we can assume that
\mathrm{conv}\left((0,0),(2,0)\right)\subseteq e
and the second coordinate of all interior lattice points of
P_{0}
is at most
0
. Suppose now that
e
is not an edge of
\bar{F}(P_{0})
, i.e. we have a half-integral point with positive second coordinate in
\bar{F}(P_{0})
and we cah choose
S=(s_{1},s_{2})\in\bar{F}(P_{0})
as a half-integral point with maximal possible second coordinate
s_{2}
. By a suitable shearing we can assume that
s_{2}\geq s_{1}>0
. We conclude directly that
s_{2}<\frac{3}{2}
, since otherwise
\mathrm{conv}\left((0,0),(2,0),(s_{1},s_{2})\right)\subseteq\mathrm{int}(P_{0})
contains the lattice point
(1,1)\in M
, which contradicts our choice of coordinates. It remains to consider the cases
S=(1/2,1)
and
S=(1/2,1/2)
. Since
s_{2}
was chosen maximal and every edge of
\bar{F}(P_{0})
contains a lattice point by
3.4
,
S
must be a vertex of
\bar{F}(P_{0})
. Let
e_{S,l},e_{S,r}\preceq\bar{F}(P_{0})
be the edges of
\bar{F}(P_{0})
containing
S
, where the indices
l
and
r
indicate that the first coordinates of the points of
e_{S_{l}}
are not greater than those of
e_{S_{r}}
and so
e_{S_{l}}
is on the left. Take the parallel lines to the affine hull of
e_{S_{l}}
through
(0,1)
and to the affine hull of
e_{S_{2}}
through
(1,1)
. Then
P_{0}
must be below both of these parallel. This implies that all points of
P_{0}
have second coordinate of at most
1
, which contradicts the fact that
S\in\bar{F}(P_{0})
.

We now show that every vertex of
\mathrm{conv}\left(\mathrm{int}(P_{0})\cap M\right)
is a boundary lattice point of
\bar{F}(P_{0})
. From the first part of the proof we already know that this is true for vertices on edges of
\bar{F}(P_{0})
with at least
3
lattice points. So we only need to consider edges of
\mathrm{conv}\left(\mathrm{int}(P_{0})\cap M\right)
with exactly two lattice points. After a suitable affine unimodular transformation we can assume that this edge is
\mathrm{conv}\left((0,0),(1,0)\right)
and that the second coordinate of all interior lattice points of
P_{0}
to be at most
0
. Furthermore, we can still assume that
S=(s_{1},s_{2})\in\bar{F}(P_{0})
is a half-integral point with the maximum possible second coordinate
s_{2}
and
s_{2}\geq s_{1}>0
. We now get
s_{1}=\frac{1}{2}
, since otherwise
\mathrm{conv}\left((0,0),(1,0),(s_{1},s_{2})\right)\subseteq\mathrm{int}(P_{0})
contains the lattice point
(1,1)\in M
, which contradicts our choice of coordinates. By symmetry it is now sufficient to show that the line segment
\mathrm{conv}\left((0,0),S\right)
is a subset of an edge of
\bar{F}(P_{0})
. As in the first part of the proof, we consider parallel lines to the affine hull of
e_{S_{l}}
through
(0,1)
and to the affine hull of
e_{S_{2}}
through
(1,1)
. If
\mathrm{conv}\left((0,0),S\right)
is not a subset of an edge of
\bar{F}(P_{0})
, then we get that all points of
P_{0}
have a second coordinate of at most
s_{2}+\frac{1}{2}
. If
s_{2}\in\frac{1}{2}\mathds{Z}\setminus\mathds{Z}
, then this contradicts the fact that
S\in\bar{F}(P_{0})
.If
s_{2}\in\mathds{Z}
, then the affine hull of
(0,1)
and
(1/2,s_{2}+1/2)
must be a supporting line of
P_{0}
. But this also contradicts
S\in\bar{F}(P_{0})
and so we are done.
∎

Since we have seen, that edges of
\mathrm{conv}\left(\mathrm{int}(P_{0})\cap M\right)
with
3
or more lattice points are always edges of
\bar{F}(P_{0})
, we get the following corollary

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
P_{0}\subseteq M_{\mathds{R}}
be a half-integral polygon with at least three interior lattice points, which are collinear. Then
\dim\bar{F}(P_{0})=1
and we have in particular that
P_{0}
is affine unimodular equivalent to a polygon in
\mathds{R}\times[-1,1]
.

We now focus on the edges of
\mathrm{conv}\left(\mathrm{int}(P_{0})\cap M\right)
which are edges of
\bar{F}(P_{0})
. For them we have the following proposition.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
P_{0}\subseteq M_{\mathds{R}}
be a half-integral polygon with at least two interior lattice points,
e\preceq\mathrm{conv}\left(\mathrm{int}(P_{0})\cap M\right)
an edge which is not an edge of
\bar{F}(P_{0})
. Then there is only one vertex
v
of
\bar{F}(P)
, which is on the other side of
e
than
\mathrm{int}(P_{0})\cap M
. Moreover, if the lattice distance of
v
from
e
is
h
, then
P_{0}
has at least
2(h-1)
interior lattice points, and the triangle
\mathrm{conv}\left(e,v\right)
is unimodular affine equivalent to the half-integral triangle
\mathrm{conv}\left((0,0),(1,0),(1/2,h)\right)
.

Proof.

We have already seen in the proof of the second part of
3.5
, that there is only one vertex
v
of
\bar{F}(P)
, which is on the other side of
e
than
\mathrm{int}(P_{0})\cap M
and that the triangle
\mathrm{conv}\left(e,v\right)
is unimodular affine equivalent to the half-integral triangle
\mathrm{conv}\left((0,0),(1,0),(1/2,h)\right)
. So we can assume that the triangle is
\mathrm{conv}\left((0,0),(1,0),(1/2,h)\right)
and we can shift the affine hulls of
(0,0),(1/2,h)
and
(1,0),(1/2,h)
by one integral step outside and bound with them an area which contains
P_{0}
. In particular, since
(0,0),(1,0)
are interior lattice points of
P_{0}
there must a points
(x_{1},x_{2})\in P_{0}
with
x_{1}\leq-1/2,x_{2}\leq-h+1
as well as a point with
x_{1}\geq 3/2,x_{2}\leq-h+1
. Therefore all the lattice points in

\displaystyle\mathrm{conv}\left((0,0),(1,0),(0,-h-2),(1,h-2)\right)

are interior lattice point of
P_{0}
and we therefore have at least
(2(h-1))
interior lattice points of
P_{0}
.
∎

Corollary 3.8
.

The lattice height
h
of the triangles on edges of
\mathrm{conv}\left(\mathrm{int}(P_{0})\cap M\right)
with two lattice points from the last proposition is bounded by
\frac{1}{2}(|P_{0}\cap M|+2)
.

Let us end this subsection with summerizing some propeties of
\bar{F}(P_{0})
we have proven so far.

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
P_{0}\subseteq M_{\mathds{R}}
be a half-integral polygon with
\dim(\bar{F}(P_{0}))=2
. Then
\bar{F}(P_{0})
is a half-integral polygon, every edge of
\bar{F}(P_{0})
contains lattice points and we get
\bar{F}(P_{0})
as union of
\mathrm{conv}\left(\mathrm{int}(P_{0})\cap M\right)
with some triangles, whereby the base of each of these triangle is an edge of

with two lattice point and the third vertex of the triangle has a lattice height of at most

3.3.
The maximal half-integral polygon to a given Fine interior

In this subsection we want to describe the unique inclusion maximal lattice polytope to a given Fine interior and the unique inclusion maximal half-integral polygon for given
\bar{F}
. This leads also to a test, if a given polytope can occure as a Fine interior.

We start by moving out the facets of a polytope by lattice distance
1
.

Definition 3.10
.

Let
P\subseteq M_{\mathds{R}}
be a rational polytope. Then we define

\displaystyle P^{(-1)}:=\{x\in M_{\mathds{R}}\mid\left<x,n\right>\geq\mathrm{Min}_{P}(n)-1\ \forall n\in\Sigma_{P}[1]\}.

Remark 3.11
.

Moving out the facets is partially an inverse operation of computing the Fine interior. We get directly from the definition the following relations

\displaystyle F(\mathrm{conv}\left(P^{(-1)}\cap M)\right)\subseteq F(P^{(-1)})\subseteq P.

The opposite inclusions are not correct in general but they hold for Fine interiors as we see in the next lemma.

Lemma 3.12
.

A rational
d
-polytope
Q\subseteq M_{\mathds{R}}
is a Fine interior of a rational polytope if and only if
Q\subseteq F(Q^{(-1)})
. Moreover,
Q
is a Fine interior of a lattice polytope if and only if
Q\subseteq F(\mathrm{conv}\left(Q^{(-1)}\cap M)\right)
.

Proof.

If the inclusions hold then
Q
is a Fine interior since the opposite inclusions hold in general. If
Q
is a Fine interior, then there is a
d
-polytope
Q^{\prime}\subseteq M_{\mathds{R}}
with
F(Q^{\prime})=Q
. We get
Q^{\prime}\subseteq Q^{(-1)}
and so by the monotony of the Fine interior
Q=F(Q^{\prime})\subseteq F(Q^{(-1)})
.
∎

The situation becomes especially easy for lattice polygons. We have by
[
HS09
, Lemma 9]
the following proposition.

Proposition 3.13
.

A lattice polygon
Q
is a Fine interior of a lattice polygon if and only if
Q^{(-1)}
is a lattice polygon.

Remark 3.14
.

All lattice polygons with up to 112 lattice points which are a Fine interior a lattice polygon were classified for calculations in
[
BS24a
]
. The data are available on zenodo
[
BS24b
]
.

We now want similar results as for the Fine interior also for
\bar{F}
.

Definition 3.15
.

Let
P_{0}\subseteq M
be a half-integral polygon with
\dim\bar{F}(P_{0})=2
. Then we call
P_{0}
a maximal half-integral polygon for given
\bar{F}
if and only if

\displaystyle P_{0}=\mathrm{conv}\left((\bar{F}(P_{0}))^{(-1)}\cap M/2\right).

Remark 3.16
.

The maximal half-integral polygon for given
\bar{F}
is unique by definition and contains all other half-integral polygons with the same
\bar{F}
. If a half-integral polygon is maximal with respect to inclusion among all half-integral polygons with the same number of interior lattice points, it is in particular maximal for given
\bar{F}
. Thus we get all inclusion maximal half-integral polygons as a subset of the polygons maximal for given
\bar{F}
. This was used to classify half-integral polygons in
[
BS24a
]
.

We get now analogue to above a test for Fine interiors of dimension
2
of lattice
3
-polytopes.

Corollary 3.17
.

A half-integral polygon
Q\subseteq M_{\mathds{R}}
is unimodular equivalent to the Fine interior of a lattice
3
-polytope if and only if

\displaystyle Q=\bar{F}(\mathrm{conv}\left(Q^{(-1)}\cap M/2\right)).

4.

The previous section gives us the following algorithm to classify the two dimensional Fine interiors of lattice
3

(1)

Take all lattice polygons with the given number of lattice points, e.g. from the dataset
[

]

(2)

For each lattice polygon with the given number of lattice points consider all possibilities to put triangles on the edges with
2

3.9

(3)

Check for each lattice polygon with hats from the last step with
3.17

(4)

Eliminate affine unimodular equivalent Fine interior, e. g. by use of a normal form as described in
[
BS24a
, section 2.1.]
.

Further results on Minimal and Minimum Cylindrical Algebraic Decompositions Journal: Journal of Symbolic Computation
Lucas Michel, Pierre Mathonet, Naïm Zénaïdi

Abstract
We consider cylindrical algebraic decompositions (CADs) as a tool for representing semi-algebraic subsets of ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} . In this framework, a CAD 𝒞 \mathscr{C} is adapted to a given set S S if S S is a union of cells of 𝒞 \mathscr{C} . Different algorithms computing an adapted CAD may produce different outputs, usually with redundant cell divisions. In this paper we analyse the possibility to remove the superfluous data. We thus consider the set CAD r ⁡ ( ℱ ) \CAD^{r}(\Fr) of CADs of class C r C^{r} ( r ∈ ℕ ∪ { ∞ , ω } r\in\N\cup\{\infty,\omega\} ) that are adapted to a finite family ℱ \mathcal{F} of semi-algebraic sets of ℝ n \R^{n} , endowed with the refinement partial order and we study the existence of minimal and minimum element in CAD r ⁡ ( ℱ ) \CAD^{r}(\Fr) . We show that for every such ℱ \Fr and every 𝒞 ∈ CAD r ⁡ ( ℱ ) \mathscr{C}\in\CAD^{r}(\Fr) , there is a minimal CAD \CAD of class C r C^{r} adapted to ℱ \Fr and smaller (i.e. coarser) than or equal to 𝒞 \mathscr{C} . In dimension n = 1 n=1 or n = 2 n=2 , this result is strengthened by proving the existence of a minimum element in CAD r ⁡ ( ℱ ) \CAD^{r}(\Fr) . In contrast, for any n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 , we provide explicit examples of semi-algebraic sets whose associated poset of adapted CADs does not admit a minimum. We then introduce a reduction relation on CAD r ⁡ ( ℱ ) \CAD^{r}(\Fr) in order to define an algorithm for the computation of minimal CADs and we characterise those semi-algebraic sets ℱ \Fr for which CAD r ⁡ ( ℱ ) \CAD^{r}(\Fr) has a minimum by means of confluence of the associated reduction system. We finally provide practical criteria for deciding if a semi-algebraic set does admit a minimum CAD and apply them to describe various concrete examples of semi-algebraic sets, along with their minimum CAD \CAD of class C r C^{r} .

Introduction

Cylindrical algebraic decomposition (CAD) is a fundamental tool in computer algebra and in real algebraic geometry. Since its introduction by Collins
[
12
]
in the framework of real quantifier elimination, the scope of applications of CAD and the concept itself have evolved in various directions. Algorithms computing CADs are constantly refined
[
14
,
19
,
8
,
11
]
and are nowadays used as building blocks in various fields where symbolic manipulations of polynomial equalities and inequalities are relevant.

From a theoretical standpoint, the existence of a CAD algorithm may also be seen as a structure theorem for semi-algebraic sets not only giving topological information about them (see for example
[
7
,
22
,
5
]
) but also providing a constructive and explicit representation of these subsets which enables symbolic and effective reasoning. In this framework, we say that a CAD is adapted (or represents) a semi-algebraic set
S\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
if
S
is a union of cells from this CAD.

In practice, for computing a CAD adapted to a semi-algebraic set
S
, one describes
S
by polynomial inequalities (conjunction, disjunction, and negation) associated with a finite set of
n
-variate polynomials
\mathcal{P}
. The CAD algorithms usually take
\mathcal{P}
as input and the output CAD is therefore adapted to any semi-algebraic set defined by means of
\mathcal{P}
. For this reason they usually contain unnecessary cells for the description of
S
. Moreover, these outputs also depend on the choice of
\mathcal{P}
and on the considered algorithm. It is then natural to seek for a method for simplifying a given CAD, while retaining only the relevant information needed to describe
S
(see for instance
[
9
]
).

The analysis of CADs adapted to a single semi-algebraic set naturally leads us to CADs that are simultaneously adapted to a finite family of sets. This situation is reminiscent of what happens with CAD algorithms when
\mathcal{P}
contains one polynomial. We thus consider a finite family
\mathcal{F}
of semi-algebraic sets of
\R^{n}
and study in detail the set
\CAD^{r}(\mathcal{F})
of CADs (with respect to a fixed variable ordering) of class
C^{r}
(
r\in\N\cup\{\infty,\omega\}
) that are adapted to every element of
\mathcal{F}
. This set is naturally endowed with the partial order
\preceq
defined by refinement: for
\mathscr{C},\mathscr{C}^{\prime}\in\text{CAD}^{r}(\mathcal{F})
, we write
\mathscr{C}\preceq\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
if every cell of
\mathscr{C}
is a union of cells of
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
. Here, the main problems

A CAD

Since CADs are partitions, we naturally use the refinement order defined on the set of partitions to compare them.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
and
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
be two CADs (or more generally partitions) of

. We say that

is a refinement of

if every cell of
\mathscr{C}
is a union of cells of
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
. We also say that
\mathscr{C}
is smaller than or equal to
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
and write
\mathscr{C}\preceq\mathscr{C}^{\prime}

if
\mathscr{C}\preceq\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
and
\mathscr{C}\neq\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
.

This definition is already used in
[
9
]
, where
\mathscr{C}
is said to be simpler than
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
when
\mathscr{C}\prec\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
and where algorithms to compute simple CADs with respect to this order are devised.
We also recall the definition of minimal and minimum elements.

Definition 2.6
.

A minimal
\CAD^{r}

\text{CAD}^{r}(\mathcal{F}),\preceq

\forall\mathscr{C}^{\prime}\in\text{CAD}^{r}(\mathcal{F}),(\mathscr{C}^{\prime}\preceq\mathscr{C})\implies(\mathscr{C}^{\prime}=\mathscr{C}).

A minimum
\CAD^{r}
adapted to
\mathcal{F}
is a minimum element of (
\text{CAD}^{r}(\mathcal{F}),\preceq
). Namely,
\mathscr{C}\in\text{CAD}^{r}(\mathcal{F})
is a minimum
\CAD^{r}
adapted to
\mathcal{F}

We say that

3
Minimal CAD
s

In this section, we show that for every finite family
\Fr
of semi-algebraic sets of
\R^{n}
, the poset
(\CAD^{r}(\Fr),\preceq)
admits at least a minimal element. Then we show the equivalence between the uniqueness of a minimal
\CAD^{r}
and the existence of a minimum
\CAD^{r}
. Finally, we give uniqueness results concerning the set of sections of the last level of minimal CADs.

Proposition 3.1
.

If
\mathscr{C}
is a
\CAD^{r}
adapted to
\mathcal{F}
, then there exists a minimal
\CAD^{r}
adapted to
\mathcal{F}
that is smaller than or equal to
\mathscr{C}
.

Proof.

The set
\text{SSP}(\mathscr{C})
of partitions that are strictly smaller than
\mathscr{C}
is a finite poset, by definition. We can find a minimal element by inspection: if
\mathscr{C}
is not a minimal element in
\text{CAD}^{r}(\mathcal{F})
, we can find
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
in
\text{CAD}^{r}(\mathcal{F})
such that
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}\prec\mathscr{C}
, and iterate the procedure with
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
. This will end in a finite number of steps because
\text{SSP}(\mathscr{C})
is finite and strictly contains
\text{SSP}(\mathscr{C}^{\prime})
. Furthermore, this procedure will obviously result in a minimal CAD with the required properties.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

The determination of a minimal element in
\text{CAD}^{r}(\mathcal{F})
presented in the proof of Proposition
3.1
is in general not efficient. If
\mathscr{C}
contains
K

(\mathscr{C})
of partitions that are strictly smaller than
\mathscr{C}
contains

elements, where

is the
K^{\text{th}}
Bell number. This procedure will be improved by Algorithm
1
.

Proposition 3.3
.

There is a unique minimal
\CAD^{r}
adapted to
\mathcal{F}
if and only if there exists a minimum
\CAD^{r}
adapted to
\mathcal{F}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathscr{M}
be the unique minimal
\CAD^{r}
adapted to
\Fr
. If
\mathscr{C}\in\text{CAD}^{r}(\Fr)
, Proposition
3.1
gives us a minimal
\CAD^{r}
adapted to
\Fr
and smaller than or equal to

. By assumption, this minimal

is

. This shows that the CAD

is smaller than or equal to any
\CAD^{r}

, hence

is a minimum of

. The other implication is direct.

4
Minimum CAD
s

In this section we investigate the existence of a minimum element in
\text{CAD}^{r}(\mathcal{F})
. It happens that CADs have properties in low dimensions that are false in higher dimensions (see for instance
[
17
]
or more recently
[

). As we shall see, the properties of the poset

depend also heavily on the dimension

or

\mathcal{F}
. However, in dimension

\CAD^{r}

admitting several distinct (actually, an infinite set of) minimal CADs of class

and then use them to produce subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(

) with no minimum adapted
\CAD^{r}
.

Theorem 4.1
.

For every finite family
\mathcal{F}
of semi-algebraic sets of
\mathbb{R}
or of
\mathbb{R}^{2}

Proof.

In view of Proposition

, it is sufficient to show that if

and

are two minimal elements in

Suppose first that the ambient space is

or

Suppose now that the ambient space is

We will prove that we can merge the corresponding cells of the cylinders over

and

that define the sections of the cylinders over

but also

\displaystyle\{C_{2i-1},C_{2i},C_{2i+1}\}\cup\{C_{2i-1}\cup C_{2i}\cup C_{2i+1}\},

5
CAD reductions

The determination of CADs that are adapted to a a family
\Fr
and smaller than a given CAD
\mathscr{C}
by direct inspection is certainly inefficient and already cumbersome even for low dimensional examples (see Section
4
and more precisely Remark
4.5
). The notion of CAD reduction that is developed in this section offers a better grasp on the partially ordered set (
\text{CAD}^{r}(\Fr),\preceq
). From a practical point of view, it enables us to introduce an algorithm for simplifying the computation of minimal CADs of class
C^{r}
. We apply this algorithm to the CADs introduced in the proof of Theorem
4.4
, and provide a short proof of their minimality. From a theoretical standpoint, we show that the relation defined by
\CAD^{r}(\Fr)
reductions is actually the transitive reduction (in the sense of
[
2
]
) of the order relation
\preceq
on
\CAD^{r}(\Fr)
. We finally obtain a characterization of those posets
(\CAD^{r}(\Fr),\preceq)
that admit a minimum in terms of the confluence of the associated abstract reduction system
(\CAD^{r}(\Fr),\leftarrow)
.

5.1
Tree structure of a CAD

The tree associated with a given CAD
\mathscr{C}
can be seen as a simplified version of
\mathscr{C}
, which only encodes its combinatorial structure. Roughly speaking, it is defined by considering only the cell indices of the considered CAD and a function that encodes the inclusion of the cells in the sets
S_{i}\in\Fr
under consideration.
We first introduce the general notion of a CAD tree.

Definition 5.1
.

A CAD tree of depth
n
and width
p
is a pair
\mathcal{T}=(T,L)
where

•

T
is a labelled rooted odd-ary tree whose nodes are labelled by tuples. The root is labelled by the empty tuple
\varepsilon
and if
I
is the label of a node, then either
I
is an
n
-tuple and the node is a leaf, or there exists
u_{I}\in\mathbb{N}
such that the node has exactly
2u_{I}+1
sons labelled by
I:j
with
j\in\{1,\ldots,2u_{I}+1\}
. As we continue, we identify a node with its label, thus seeing
T
as a subset of
\bigcup_{k=0}^{n}(\mathbb{N}^{*})^{k}
endowed with the prefix order relation.

•

L
is a map defined recursively on

\mathscr{C}
and
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
cannot be reduced to a common
\CAD^{0}
. This observation relates the existence of minimum element to the confluence property of
\text{CAD}^{0}(\mathbb{T})
. We now show that this is a general phenomenon.

We consider
\text{CAD}^{r}(\Fr)
as the set of objects of an abstract reduction system (see
[
4
]
) endowed with the reduction relation introduced in Definition
5.6
. Confluence, also known as Church-Rosser or diamond property, appears in various areas of mathematics and computer science. We recall its definition in this particular case.

Definition 5.14
.

The reduction system
\text{CAD}^{r}(\Fr)
is globally confluent if for all
\mathscr{C},\overline{\mathscr{C}},\mathscr{C}^{\prime}\in\text{CAD}^{r}(\Fr)
such that
\mathscr{C}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\leftarrow}}\overline{\mathscr{C}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\to}}\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
, there exists
\underline{\mathscr{C}}\in\text{CAD}^{r}(\Fr)
such that
\mathscr{C}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\to}}\underline{\mathscr{C}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\leftarrow}}\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
. It is
locally confluent if for all
\mathscr{C},\overline{\mathscr{C}},\mathscr{C}^{\prime}\in\text{CAD}^{r}(\Fr)
such that
\mathscr{C}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\leftarrow}}\overline{\mathscr{C}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
, there exists
\underline{\mathscr{C}}\in\text{CAD}^{r}(\Fr):\mathscr{C}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\to}}\underline{\mathscr{C}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\leftarrow}}\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
.

Observing that there exists no infinite chain of reductions in
\text{CAD}^{r}(\Fr)
and applying Newman’s Lemma (
[
4
, Lemma 2.7.2]
) we obtain the following result.

Lemma 5.15
.

The rewriting system
\text{CAD}^{r}(\Fr)
is globally confluent if and only if it is locally confluent.

In view of this lemma, we say that
\text{CAD}^{r}(\Fr)
is confluent if it is globally confluent or locally confluent.
We can now state and prove the main result of this section which is reminiscent of the characterization of Gröbner bases by means of confluence (see
[
4
, Chapter 8]
).

Theorem 5.16
.

There exists a minimum in
\text{CAD}^{r}(\Fr)
if and only if the reduction system
\text{CAD}^{r}(\Fr)
is confluent.

Proof.

Assume that
\text{CAD}^{r}(\Fr)
has a minimum
\mathcal{M}
. For every
\mathscr{C},\overline{\mathscr{C}},\mathscr{C}^{\prime}\in\text{CAD}^{r}(\Fr)
such that
\mathscr{C}\leftarrow\overline{\mathscr{C}}\to\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
,
\mathcal{M}
is smaller than or equal to
\mathscr{C}
and
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
. Using Theorem
5.9
, this is equivalent to
\mathscr{C}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\to}}\mathcal{M}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\leftarrow}}\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
, so
\text{CAD}^{r}(\Fr)
is confluent.

We assume now that
\text{CAD}^{r}(\Fr)
is confluent and show that it has a minimum. By Proposition
3.3
, it is sufficient to show that there is at most one minimal element. If
\mathscr{C}
and
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
are two minimal elements of
\text{CAD}^{r}(\Fr)
we obtain a
\CAD^{r}

\overline{\mathscr{C}}
which is finer than
\mathscr{C}
and
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
by building a
\CAD^{r}
adapted to all the cells of
\mathscr{C}
and all the cells of
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
(see Remark
2.3
). Hence, we have
\mathscr{C}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\leftarrow}}\overline{\mathscr{C}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\to}}\mathscr{C}.
By confluence there exists
\underline{\mathscr{C}}\in\text{CAD}^{r}(\Fr):\mathscr{C}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\to}}\underline{\mathscr{C}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\leftarrow}}\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
. By minimality of
\mathscr{C}
and
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
, we have
\mathscr{C}=\underline{\mathscr{C}}=\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
, as requested.
∎

6
Existence results for minimum CADs

We now develop more concrete criteria and provide examples of semi-algebraic sets that do admit a minimum
\CAD^{r}
.
The main result of this section is a general criterion (Theorem
6.3
) that establishes an equivalence between the existence of a minimum in
\CAD^{r}(\Fr)
and the existence of a minimum of a poset
\pi_{n-1}(\CAD^{r}(\Fr))
(see below) of CADs of
\R^{n-1}
.
However, describing explicitly the set
\pi_{n-1}(\CAD^{r}(\Fr))
can be intricate in practice, but as we shall see, it is relevant to seek for general properties that are shared by all its elements, that is to consider overapproximations
\mathcal{O}\supseteq\pi_{n-1}(\CAD^{r}(\Fr))
that are easier to handle.
Next, we determine particular instances of overapproximations and use them to describe explicitly minimum CADs adapted to some semi-algebraic sets.
These developments could pave the way towards the description of natural classes of (finite families of) semi-algebraic sets of
\R^{n}
that do admit a minimum CAD since Theorem
6.3
initiates an induction.

6.1
General criteria

We now define some posets of
\CAD
s that are naturally associated with
\CAD^{r}(\Fr)
which are relevant for the statement of the main criterion.

Definition 6.1
.

For all
k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, we denote by
\pi_{k}:\R^{n}\to\R^{k}
the projection defined by
\pi_{k}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})
and use the same notation for its natural successive extensions to subsets of
\R^{n}
, families of subsets of
\R^{n}
, partitions of
\R^{n}
and finally to sets of partitions.

In particular, for
\Fr=(S_{1},\ldots,S_{p})
, we write
\pi_{k}(\Fr)=(\pi_{k}(S_{1}),\ldots,\pi_{k}(S_{p}))
and for every
\CAD

\mathscr{C}
of
\R^{n}
, we have
\pi_{k}(\mathscr{C})=\mathscr{C}_{k}
and therefore

\pi_{k}(\CAD^{r}(\Fr))=\{\mathscr{C}_{k}\;|\;\mathscr{C}\in\CAD^{r}(\Fr)\}.

However,
\pi_{k}(\CAD^{r}(\Fr))
is in general not equal to
\CAD^{r}(\pi_{k}(\Fr))
(see Proposition
6.7
), and it might not be equal to a poset of the form
\CAD^{r}(\Fr_{k})
, with
\Fr_{k}
a finite family of semi-algebraic sets of
\R^{k}
(see Proposition
6.6
). It is nevertheless endowed with the partial order
\preceq
given by refinement (see Definition
2.5
), since it is a subset of the set of partitions of
\R^{k}
.

Lemma 6.2
.

If the poset
(\CAD^{r}(\Fr),\preceq)
has a minimum element
\mathscr{M}
, then
\mathscr{M}_{k}
is a minimum element of the poset
(\pi_{k}(\CAD^{r}(\Fr)),\preceq)
for every
k\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}
.

Proof.

By definition, if
\Dr\in\pi_{k}(\CAD^{r}(\Fr))
, then there exists
\mathscr{C}\in\CAD^{r}(\Fr)
such that
\mathscr{C}_{k}=\Dr
. In particular,
\mathscr{M}\preceq\mathscr{C}
and thus
\mathscr{M}_{k}\preceq\mathscr{C}_{k}=\Dr
by Lemma
5.8
.
∎

The converse of this lemma would allow to infer the existence of a minimum element in
(\CAD^{r}(\Fr),\preceq)
from that of minimum elements in the posets
(\pi_{k}(\CAD^{r}(\Fr)),\preceq)
. The following theorem provides a stronger statement using only
(\pi_{n-1}(\CAD^{r}(\Fr)),\preceq)
.

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
\mathcal{O}
be a set of partitions of
\R^{n-1}
such that
\pi_{n-1}(\CAD^{r}(\Fr))\subseteq\mathcal{O}
. Assume that
\mathscr{M}
a minimal
\CAD^{r}
adapted to
\Fr
such that
\mathscr{M}_{n-1}
is a minimum of
\mathcal{O}
. Then
\mathscr{M}
is a minimum
\CAD^{r}
adapted to
\Fr
.
In particular, the poset
(\CAD^{r}(\Fr),\preceq)
has a minimum element if and only if the poset
(\pi_{n-1}(\CAD^{r}(\Fr)),\preceq)
has a minimum element.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.3
, it is sufficient to prove that
\mathscr{M}
is the unique minimal element in
\CAD^{r}(\Fr)
, i.e. that if
\mathscr{C}
is a minimal element in
\CAD^{r}(\Fr)
, then
\mathscr{C}=\mathscr{M}
. We show that
\mathscr{M}\preceq\mathscr{C}
and we conclude by minimality of
\mathscr{C}
.

Consider a cell
M_{I:a}
of
\mathscr{M}
and observe that
M_{I}\in\mathscr{M}_{n-1}
. By assumption,
\mathscr{C}_{n-1}\in\mathcal{O}
and
\mathscr{M}_{n-1}
is a minimum of
\mathcal{O}
. This implies that
\mathscr{M}_{n-1}\preceq\mathscr{C}_{n-1}
, and thus the existence of
J_{1},\ldots,J_{K}\in(\N^{*})^{n-1}
such that
M_{I}=\cup_{k=1}^{K}C_{J_{k}}
, where the
C_{J_{k}}
’s are cells of
\mathscr{C}_{n-1}
. Finally, by Corollary
3.5
, for all
k\in\{1,\ldots,K\}
, the odd number of cells of
\mathscr{M}
in the cylinder above
M_{I}
is the same as the number of cells of
\mathscr{C}
in the cylinder above
C_{J_{k}}
, but also that
M_{I:a}=\cup_{k=1}^{K}C_{J_{k}:a}
, hence
\mathscr{M}\preceq\mathscr{C}
.

For the particular case, we denote by
\mathcal{N}
the minimum of the poset
(\pi_{n-1}(\CAD^{r}(\Fr)),\preceq)
. By definition, there exists
\mathscr{C}\in\CAD^{r}(\Fr)
such that
\mathscr{C}_{n-1}=\mathcal{N}.
Consider
\mathscr{M}
a minimal
\CAD^{r}
of
\Fr
such that
\mathscr{M}\preceq\mathscr{C}
. By Lemma
5.8
, we have
\mathscr{M}_{n-1}\preceq\mathscr{C}_{n-1}=\mathcal{N}
. Since
\mathcal{N}
is a minimum of
(\pi_{n-1}(\CAD^{r}(\Fr)),\preceq)
, we obtain that
\mathscr{M}_{n-1}=\mathcal{N}
. We conclude by considering
\mathcal{O}=\pi_{n-1}(\CAD^{r}(\Fr))
.
∎

We now deduce a new theoretical characterisation of the fact that the poset
\CAD^{r}(\Fr)
admits a minimum.
A subset
P
of
\CAD^{r}(\R^{n})
is upward closed (with respect to
\preceq
) if for all
\mathscr{C}\in P
and all
\Dr\in\CAD^{r}(\R^{n})
such that
\mathscr{C}\preceq\Dr
, we have
\Dr\in P
.

Lemma 6.4
.

For
k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, the subset
\pi_{k}(\CAD^{r}(\Fr))
of
\CAD^{r}(\R^{k})
is upward closed.

Proof.

Let
\widetilde{\mathscr{C}}\in\pi_{k}(\CAD^{r}(\Fr))
and
\Dr\in\CAD^{r}(\R^{k})
such that
\widetilde{\mathscr{C}}\preceq\Dr
. By definition, there exists
\mathscr{C}\in\CAD^{r}(\Fr)
such that
\mathscr{C}_{k}=\widetilde{\mathscr{C}}
. To show that
\Dr\in\pi_{k}(\CAD^{r}(\Fr))
, we build level by level an element
\Er\in\CAD^{r}(\Fr)
such that
\Er_{k}=\Dr
as follows. First, we set
\Er_{k}=\Dr
. Then, for all
l\in\{k+1,\ldots,n\}
, if the CAD
\Er_{l-1}
is already built, then the cells of
\Er_{l}
are precisely the intersections of the cells of
\mathscr{C}_{l}
with the cylinders
E\times\R
for
E\in\Er_{l-1}
. It is clear that
\Er
is adapted to
\Fr
since
\Er
is greater than or equal to
\mathscr{C}
, which is adapted to
\Fr
.
∎

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
P
be a subset of
\CAD^{r}(\R^{n})
. If
P
is upward closed and admits a minimum
\mathscr{M}
, then
P=\CAD^{r}(\mathscr{M})
. If there exists
\mathscr{M}\in\CAD^{r}(\R^{n})
such that
P=\CAD^{r}(\mathscr{M})
, then
\mathscr{M}
is the minimum of
P
and
P
is upward closed.

Proof.

This is a direct consequence of the fact that for all CADs
\mathscr{C}
and
\Dr
of
\R^{n}
of class
C^{r}
, we have
\mathscr{C}\preceq\Dr
if and only if
\Dr\in\CAD^{r}(\mathscr{C})
.
∎

Proposition 6.6
.

The poset
\CAD^{r}(\Fr)
has a minimum if and only if for all
k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, there exists a finite family
\Fr_{k}
of semi-algebraic sets of
\R^{k}
such that

\pi_{k}(\CAD^{r}(\Fr))=\CAD^{r}(\Fr_{k}).

Proof.

If
\CAD^{r}(\Fr)
has a minimum
\mathscr{M}
, then
\mathscr{M}_{k}
is a minimum of
\pi_{k}(\CAD^{r}(\Fr))
for every
k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
(see Lemma
6.2
). Since these posets are all upward closed by Lemma
6.4
, we obtain that
\pi_{k}(\CAD^{r}(\Fr))=\CAD^{r}(\mathscr{M}_{k})
via Lemma
6.5
.

For the converse, we proceed by induction on
k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
to show that the poset
\pi_{k}(\CAD^{r}(\Fr))
admits a minimum.
The poset
\pi_{1}(\CAD^{r}(\Fr))
coincides with
\CAD^{r}(\Fr_{1})
by assumption and it therefore admits a minimum by Theorem
4.1
, as the ambient space is
\R
. Let us assume that
\pi_{k-1}(\CAD^{r}(\Fr))
admits a minimum, for a given
k\in\{2,\ldots,n\}
and show that
\pi_{k}(\CAD^{r}(\Fr))
admits a minimum, by applying Theorem
6.3
.
By assumption, we can write
\pi_{k}(\CAD^{r}(\Fr))=\CAD^{r}(\Fr_{k})
and moreover we have

\pi_{k-1}(\CAD^{r}(\Fr_{k}))=\pi_{k-1}(\pi_{k}(\CAD^{r}(\Fr)))=\pi_{k-1}(\CAD^{r}(\Fr)).

The latter admits a minimum, hence Theorem
6.3
ensures that
\CAD^{r}(\Fr_{k})=\pi_{k}(\CAD^{r}(\Fr))
has a minimum.
∎

To illustrate the previous result, consider the Trousers
\mathbb{T}
from Theorem
4.4
and the CAD
\mathscr{C}
from the corresponding proof. We know that
\pi_{1}(\CAD^{0}(\mathbb{T}))
is upward closed and contains the trivial CAD
\mathscr{C}_{1}=\{\R\}
of
\R
, hence we have
\pi_{1}(\CAD^{0}(\mathbb{T}))=\CAD^{0}(\R)
. Moreover, since the poset
\pi_{3}(\CAD^{0}(\mathbb{T}))=\CAD^{0}(\mathbb{T})
does not admit a minimum element, Proposition
6.6
implies that there does not exist a finite family
\Fr_{2}
of semi-algebraic sets of
\R^{2}
such that
\pi_{2}(\CAD^{0}(\mathbb{T}))=\CAD^{0}(\Fr_{2}).

6.2
Applications

In this subsection we define explicitly suitable overapproximations
\mathcal{O}
of
\pi_{n-1}(\CAD^{r}(\Fr))
and apply Theorem
6.3
to present various explicit examples of (finite families of) semi-algebraic sets of
\R^{3}
which do admit a minimum CAD. Similar constructions extend to
\R^{n}
.

Proposition 6.7
.

For every
k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, we have
\pi_{k}(\CAD^{r}(\Fr))\subseteq\CAD^{r}(\pi_{k}(\Fr))
.

The other inclusion is not true in general (see for instance Example
6.10
).

Proof.

We show that if
\mathscr{C}\in\CAD^{r}(\Fr)
, then we have
\mathscr{C}_{k}\in\CAD^{r}(\pi_{k}(\Fr))
. By assumption,
S_{j}=\bigcup\{C\;|\;C\in\mathscr{C},C\subseteq S_{j}\}
for all
S_{j}\in\Fr
and hence

\pi_{k}(S_{j})=\bigcup\{\pi_{k}(C)\;|\;C\in\mathscr{C},C\subseteq S_{j}\}.

The result follows since the cells of
\mathscr{C}_{k}
are precisely the sets
\pi_{k}(C)
with
C\in\mathscr{C}
.
∎

Example 6.8
.

The closed ball
\mathbb{B}
of
\R^{3}
centred at the origin and of radius one admits a minimum
\CAD^{r}
. We show this using the criterion (Theorem
6.3
) with the overapproximation
\mathcal{O}=\CAD^{r}(\pi_{2}(\mathbb{B}))
. Observe that
\pi_{2}(\mathbb{B})
is the closed disk of the plane centred at the origin and of radius one.
We consider
\mathscr{M}\in\CAD^{r}(\mathbb{B})
whose
\mathscr{M}_{1}
and
\mathscr{M}_{2}
are exactly the CADs
\mathscr{C}_{1}
and
\mathscr{C}_{2}
described in Example
5.3
. The cells in
\R^{3}
of
\mathscr{M}
are obtained by splitting exactly the cylinders built above the cells of
\mathscr{M}_{2}
by the the roots of the polynomial
x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+x_{3}^{2}-1
with respect to
x_{3}
.
The CADs
\mathscr{M}
and
\mathscr{M}_{2}
are respectively minimal elements of
\CAD^{r}(\mathbb{B})
and of
\mathcal{O}
since we can show that there exists no reduction rule defined from their respective CAD tree. Since
\pi_{2}(\mathbb{B})
is a semi-algebraic set of
\R^{2}
, Theorem
4.1
implies that
\mathscr{M}_{2}
is actually a minimum of
\mathcal{O}
. We can thus apply the criterion, obtaining that
\mathscr{M}
is the minimum
\CAD^{r}
of
\mathbb{B}
.

From Lemma
2.4
, we already know that the semi-algebraic set
S\equiv x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+x_{3}^{2}>1
of
\R^{3}
, which is the complement of
\mathbb{B}
, admits a minimum
\CAD^{r}
.
However, notice that since
\pi_{2}(S)=\R^{2}
, we cannot apply Theorem
6.3
with the the overapproximation
\CAD^{r}(\pi_{2}(S))
of
\pi_{2}(\CAD^{r}(S))
as in Example
6.8
. Indeed, there obviously does not exist any
\mathscr{M}\in\CAD^{r}(S)
whose projection
\mathscr{M}_{2}
is the minimum of
\CAD^{r}(\pi_{2}(S))
, which is
\{\R^{2}\}
. This suggests to search for other overapproximations in order to apply Theorem
6.3
to other classes of examples.

For this purpose, we consider the collection
\text{CC}(\Fr)
of connected components of the semi-algebraic sets
S_{1},\ldots,S_{p}
.
It is known that
\text{CC}(\Fr)
is a finite family of semi-algebraic sets of
\R^{n}
(see for instance Theorem 5.21 of
[
5
]
), and which we may suppose to be numbered once and for all.

Proposition 6.9
.

We have
\CAD^{r}(\Fr)=\CAD^{r}(\text{CC}(\Fr)).

Proof.

Consider
\mathscr{C}\in\CAD^{r}(\Fr)
,
C\in\mathscr{C}
and
S_{i}^{\prime}
a connected component of some
S_{i}\in\Fr
and assume that
C\cap S_{i}^{\prime}\neq\varnothing
. On the one hand,
C\subseteq S_{i}
by assumption on
\mathscr{C}
. On the other hand, since all CAD cells are connected, we obtain that
S_{i}^{\prime}\cup C
is a connected subset of
S_{i}
.
By definition of
S_{i}^{\prime}
, we must have
C\subseteq S_{i}^{\prime}
. Therefore
\mathscr{C}
is adapted to any of the connected components of
S_{i}
, again by Lemma
2.4
.
The other inclusion is direct, since any semi-algebraic set is the union of its connected components.
∎

Example 6.10
.

We show that the semi-algebraic set
\mathbb{P}
defined by

\mathbb{P}=\left\{(x,y,z)\in\R^{3}\;|\;\left(x=-2(z-\frac{1}{2})^{2}\right)\lor\left(x=2(z+\frac{1}{2})^{2}\right)\right\}

and depicted in Figure
9
admits a minimum
\CAD^{r}
.
The set
\mathbb{P}
admits two connected components, which are the sets
\mathbb{P}_{1}\equiv x=-2(z-\frac{1}{2})^{2}
and
\mathbb{P}_{2}\equiv x=2(z+\frac{1}{2})^{2}
. Applying Proposition
6.9
, we know that
\CAD^{r}(\mathbb{P})\subseteq\CAD^{r}(\mathbb{P}_{1})
. Combining this with Lemma
6.7
and with the fact that
\pi_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{1})=(-\infty;0]\times\R
, we obtain

\pi_{2}(\CAD^{r}(\mathbb{P}))\subseteq\pi_{2}(\CAD^{r}(\mathbb{P}_{1}))\subseteq\CAD^{r}(\pi_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{1}))=\CAD^{r}((-\infty;0]\times\R)

and the set
\mathcal{O}=\CAD^{r}((-\infty;0]\times\R)
is an overapproximation of
\pi_{2}(\CAD^{r}(\mathbb{P}))
. A minimum of
\mathcal{O}
is given by the CAD
\mathscr{M}_{2}
defined by the three cells
M_{11}=(-\infty;0)\times\R,M_{12}=\{0\}\times\R
and
M_{13}=(0;+\infty)\times\R
. A minimal element
\mathscr{M}
of
\CAD^{r}(\mathbb{P})
is defined above
\mathscr{M}_{2}
exactly by the three pairs of functions given by
\pm(\frac{-x}{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{1}{2}
on
M_{11}
, by
\pm\frac{1}{2}
on
M_{12}
and by
\pm(\frac{x}{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}-\frac{1}{2}
on
M_{13}
. By Theorem
6.3
,
\mathscr{M}
is a minimum of
\CAD^{r}(\mathbb{P}).

:
The semi-algebraic set
\mathbb{P}
with two connected components

In order to introduce the last overapproximation we consider, we start with the following simple example.

Example 6.11
.

Let
S\subseteq\R^{2}
be the closed half-plane of equation
y\geq 0
with the origin
(0,0)
removed. We first show that if
\mathscr{C}
is a minimal
\CAD^{r}
adapted to
S
, then
\mathscr{C}_{1}
must contain the section
\{0\}
. Suppose the contrary, then the cell
C_{i}
of
\mathscr{C}_{1}
containing
0
is a sector. By Proposition
3.4
, there exists a unique section of
\mathscr{C}
above
C_{i}
, which is exactly
C_{i}\times\{0\}
. Indeed if
\xi_{i:2},\ldots,\xi_{i:2u_{i}}
define the sections over
C_{i}
, we must have
\{\xi_{i:2j}(x)\;|\;j\in\{1,\ldots,u_{i}\}\}=\partial S_{x}=\{0\}
for every
x\in C_{i}
. This is a contradiction since the section
C_{i}\times\{0\}
intersects
S
and
S^{c}
. Therefore
\mathscr{C}_{1}
must be adapted to
\{0\}
.

We observe that in the previous example we have
\partial S_{x}=\{0\}
for every
x\in\R
. This does not single out the particular point
0\in\R
. But we have
S_{x}=[0;+\infty)
for all
x\neq 0
, whereas
S_{0}=(0;+\infty)
. The former contains its boundary, while the latter does not. This piece of topological information is precisely encoded in the tree associated with the minimum CAD adapted to
S_{x}
. In view of the previous example and Theorem
4.1
, we introduce the following definition.

Definition 6.12
.

For every
x\in\R^{n-1}
, the behaviour
[\Fr_{x}]
of
\Fr
above
x
is the CAD tree associated with the minimum CAD adapted to
\Fr_{x}=\left((S_{1})_{x},\ldots,(S_{p})_{x}\right)
.
The set of all behaviours is denoted by
\mathfrak{B}
. Finally, for all
\mathfrak{b}\in\mathfrak{B}
, we set
B(\Fr,\mathfrak{b})=\{x\in\R^{n-1}\;|\;[\Fr_{x}]=\mathfrak{b}\}.

Note that the regularity of the minimum CAD is omitted since the poset
\CAD^{r}(\Fr_{x})
is the same for all
r\in\N\cup\{\infty,\omega\}
.
Moreover, the behaviour
[\Fr_{x}]
, as defined, is a CAD tree
(T^{x},L^{x})
of depth
1
and has exactly
2u_{\varepsilon}^{x}+1
leaves (see Definitions
2.1
and
5.1
). It is thus completely determined by the list
\left(L^{x}(1),L^{x}(2),\ldots,L^{x}(2u_{\varepsilon}^{x}+1)\right)
of
p
-tuples in
\{0,1\}^{p}
.

Example 6.13
.

Let
\Fr=\left(S_{1},S_{2}\right)
where
S_{1}=[-2,0)\cup\{1\},S_{2}=[0,+\infty)
. By Remark
4.2
, the set of sections of the minimum CAD
\mathscr{C}
adapted to
\Fr
is exactly given by
\{-2,0,1\}
, which contains
u_{\varepsilon}=3
points, as depicted in Figure
10

Proposition 6.15
.

Suppose that
n\geq 2
.
The set
\{B(\Fr,\mathfrak{b})\;|\;\mathfrak{b}\in\mathfrak{B}\}
is a finite semi-algebraic partition of
\R^{n-1}
, and

\pi_{n-1}(\CAD^{r}(\Fr))\subseteq\CAD^{r}(B(\Fr,\mathfrak{b})\;|\;\mathfrak{b}\in\mathfrak{B})
.

Proof.

Let
\Dr\in\pi_{n-1}(\CAD^{r}(\Fr))
and
\mathfrak{b}\in\mathfrak{B}
. Since
\Dr
is a partition of
\R^{n-1}
, for every
x\in B(\Fr,\mathfrak{b})
, there exists
D\in\Dr
, such that
x\in D
. By Lemma
6.14
, this implies
D\subseteq B(\Fr,\mathfrak{b})
, and therefore
B(\Fr,\mathfrak{b})
is a (finite) union of cells of
\Dr
. It is therefore semi-algebraic. Moreover, by definition we have
B(\Fr,\mathfrak{b})\cap B(\Fr,\mathfrak{b}^{\prime})=\varnothing
when
\mathfrak{b}\neq\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}
and
x\in B(\Fr,[\Fr_{x}])
for every

. This shows that
\{B(\Fr,\mathfrak{b})\;|\;\mathfrak{b}\in\mathfrak{B}\}
is a partition of

, of which
\Dr
is a refinement. It is finite since
\Dr
is, and
\Dr
is adapted to
B(\Fr,\mathfrak{b})
for every
\mathfrak{b}\in\mathfrak{B}
.
∎

Example 6.16
.

Consider the Pointless Ball
PB
defined by

\text{PB}=\left\{(x,y,z)\in\R^{3}\;|\;x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}\leq 1\land z<1\right\}.

The partition described in Proposition
6.15
is given by

\displaystyle B(\text{PB},\mathfrak{b})\equiv\begin{cases}x^{2}+y^{2}>1&\text{ if }\mathfrak{b}=(0),\\
x^{2}+y^{2}=1&\text{ if }\mathfrak{b}=(0,1,0),\\
0<x^{2}+y^{2}<1&\text{ if }\mathfrak{b}=(0,1,1,1,0),\\
x^{2}+y^{2}=0&\text{ if }\mathfrak{b}=(0,1,1,0,0),\end{cases}

. We construct a CAD

with the roots of

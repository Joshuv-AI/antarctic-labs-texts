Assessing the impact of renewable energy integration on energy efficiency within the China-Pakistan economic corridor (CPEC)
Authors: Anis Bensadi
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study investigates the impact of renewable energy integration on energy efficiency within the China-Pakistan Economic Corridor (CPEC) from 2000 to 2022. Efficient renewable energy utilization is crucial for the CPEC’s sustainable development. We employed multiple linear regression analysis on data from national energy statistics databases to examine the relationship between energy efficiency defined as the ratio of total renewable energy output to input in (TWh) and four renewable energy sources: hydropower, biofuel, solar PV, and geothermal. Our model controlled for potential confounding factors and met assumptions of linearity, multivariate normality, and absence of multicollinearity. Hydropower exhibited a highly significant negative correlation with energy efficiency (-0.632, p < 0.001), with a regression coefficient of -7.642 × 10⁻⁴ (p < 0.001). Similarly, biofuel showed a significant negative correlation (-0.222, p < 0.001) and a coefficient of -9.580 × 10⁻⁴ (p < 0.001). These findings suggest that increased production from these sources is associated with decreased energy efficiency, potentially due to transmission losses or inefficiencies in conversion technologies. In contrast, solar PV (-0.027, p = 0.638) and geothermal (-0.014, p = 0.806) showed no statistically significant relationship with energy efficiency, indicating that at their current scales of deployment, they do not significantly impact overall efficiency. A moderate negative correlation (r=-0.114, p = 0.047) was observed between solar PV and geothermal production, possibly reflecting resource allocation choices within the renewable energy portfolio. These results highlight the need for targeted interventions to improve the efficiency of hydropower and biofuel production and emphasize the potential for future contributions from solar PV and geothermal as their deployment and grid integration expands. CPEC, a strategic component of China’s Belt and Road Initiative (BRI), aims to stimulate economic growth, create jobs, and foster regional connectivity, significantly benefiting both countries1. However, the project’s success hinges critically on integrating sustainable energy practices and prioritizing energy efficiency. While CPEC’s development significantly improves trade routes and economic development, optimizing energy use is paramount for long-term sustainability and economic viability. Simply expanding energy production, particularly through traditional fossil fuels, carries environmental risks and undermines sustainability efforts2. Furthermore, energy shortages due to underperforming energy infrastructure consistently hamper economic recovery efforts3. Energy efficiency within CPEC involves optimizing energy use across industrial, residential, and transportation sectors. This includes employing high-efficiency equipment, process optimization, waste heat recovery, and advanced manufacturing technologies in industrial projects to reduce energy waste (Bhatti, 2020). In the building sector, energy efficiency involves employing improved thermal insulation, energy-efficient windows, HVAC systems, and lighting; retrofitting existing structures enhances energy performance. Transportation efficiency focuses on developing public transit, promoting electric vehicles (EVs), and optimizing logistics (with CPEC projects often including EV charging stations). Smart grid technologies, supported by policies encouraging energy-efficient practices4, optimize energy distribution and use. Integrating renewable energy sources with energy efficiency measures maximizes benefits, reducing greenhouse gas emissions and operational costs, while enhancing competitiveness. CPEC’s energy efficiency initiatives also build local capacity through training programs and awareness campaigns, advancing sustainable development goals. This study investigates the impact of renewable energies on energy efficiency within the CPEC project. Literature review

Overview of renewable energies in China

China’s renewable energy capacity more than tripled between 2010 and 2020 and became the world’s leader in installed renewable energy capacity, particularly in solar and wind power. By 2022, China’s wind and solar capacities had reached 282 GW and 306 GW, respectively, making significant contributions to the global renewable energy market7. The Chinese government has played a crucial role in promoting renewable energy through various policies and initiatives discuss the implementation of the Renewable Energy Law and the 13th Five-Year Plan, which set ambitious targets for renewable energy development. Moreover, government subsidies, feed-in tariffs, and favourable investment policies have provided substantial financial support for renewable energy projects8. The “14th Five-Year Plan” continues to promote clean energy transitions, emphasizing the importance of technological innovation and infrastructure development. The shift towards renewable energy has had notable economic and environmental impacts in China. Studies such as9highlight the positive effects on energy security, job creation, and economic diversification. The renewable energy sector has also contributed to a significant reduction in carbon emissions and air pollutants, improving public health and environmental quality. However, challenges such as grid integration and intermittency of renewable energy sources remain critical issues6. Technological advancements have been pivotal in improving the efficiency and cost-effectiveness of renewable energy technologies. Significant progress in photovoltaic cell efficiency, wind turbine design, and energy storage solutions10. Additionally, China’s investments in smart grid technologies and digitalization are enhancing the integration and management of renewable energy into the national grid4. Despite remarkable progress, China’s renewable energy sector faces several challenges. Grid integration issues, regional disparities in resource distribution, and the need for technological innovations in energy storage are critical areas for future research and policy development11. Additionally, balancing economic growth with environmental sustainability remains a complex issue, requiring coordinated efforts at multiple governance levels12.

Renewable energy technologies in China

China has emerged as the global leader in solar energy, prioritizing the advancement and deployment of photovoltaic (PV) panels while continuously enhancing efficiency and cost-effectiveness. Additionally, there have been significant advancements in wind turbine technology, resulting in the development of larger and more efficient turbines, including the installation of offshore wind farms. Consequently, the contribution of wind energy to the national grid has notably increased13. Hydroelectric power plays a pivotal role in China’s renewable energy strategy, driving advancements in turbine technology and the implementation of integrated small-scale projects aimed at rural development. China’s bioenergy technologies are also noteworthy, with progress in biomass gasification and the conversion of agricultural waste into energy to minimize waste and generate power. Research and development efforts in these areas receive substantial funding from both government and private sectors5. The Chinese government’s commitment to renewable energy development is evident in its five-year plans and the implementation of the Renewable Energy Law, which provides grants and subsidies to spur innovation. Simultaneously, the private sector, driven by profit maximization motives and governmental tax incentives, heavily invests in the advancement of new technologies. This collaborative effort between the public and private sectors highlights a synergistic approach to promoting renewable energy technology.

China’s policies on renewable energy and CPEC

China has implemented regulatory and institutional mechanisms for renewable energy development within the CPEC, employing tailored measures to promote its advancement. These measures include the enactment of the Renewable Energy Law and the implementation of the 13th Five-Year Plan, specifically designed to foster the development of renewable energy sources such as solar, wind, hydro, and biomass14. Notably, these regulations mandate utilities to purchase 100% of the renewable power they produce, reflecting the confidence of the energy market in supporting green energy initiatives. The CPEC’s foundation rests upon a national legislative framework and the establishment of bilateral treaties that ensure the creation of renewable energy infrastructure. Projects like the Pakistan UEP Wind Farm and Dawood Wind Farm exemplify the outcomes of this policy, funded, and constructed with the assistance of Chinese expertise and capital. These initiatives not only contribute to the comprehensive realization of China’s environmental objectives, such as emission reduction and power source diversification with increased reliance on non-fossil fuels, but also align with China’s economic goals of ensuring secure and sustainable energy supplies for its rapidly expanding industries while promoting the export of renewable energy technologies15. In addition to the policies, the Belt and Road Initiative (BRI) represents a multilateral development strategy aimed at enhancing regional connectivity and integration for economic purposes. The synergy between China’s domestic economic endeavours and its international economic policies is underscored by this initiative.

Importance of energy efficiency

Energy efficiency is vital for minimizing energy waste, reducing greenhouse gas emissions, and lowering energy costs. Energy efficiency improvements could account for nearly half of the reductions required to meet global climate targets. Enhanced energy efficiency can also alleviate pressure on energy supply systems and contribute to sustainable development11. Technological advancements have played a crucial role in improving energy efficiency across various sectors, including residential, industrial, and transportation. The development of smart grids, which optimize energy distribution and consumption through advanced monitoring and control systems. Additionally, the advent of high-efficiency appliances and industrial processes has led to significant energy savings16. Innovations in building materials and design are leading to substantial energy savings. For instance, passive technologies and zero-energy buildings are becoming increasingly feasible due to advancements in insulation, and HVAC systems17. The integration of Internet of Things devices in building automation systems allows for real-time energy management and further efficiency gains18. Effective policy measures and regulatory frameworks are essential for promoting energy efficiency. Policies that provide financial incentives, such as tax credits, rebates, and grants, have been shown to accelerate the adoption of energy-efficient technologies19. Mandatory energy performance standards and labelling programs help consumers make informed decisions and stimulate market demand for high-efficiency products20. Improving energy efficiency has both economic and environmental benefits. Investments in energy efficiency can result in significant cost savings for businesses and consumers. These savings often outweigh the initial investment costs, making energy efficiency financially attractive21. Environmentally, enhanced energy efficiency contributes to significant reductions in greenhouse gas emissions and air pollutants. Energy efficiency improvements in industries have led to substantial emissions reductions, aiding in the fight against climate change. In the industrial sector16, provide evidence that energy efficiency measures, such as process optimization and the adoption of advanced manufacturing technologies, can drastically reduce energy consumption and costs. The transportation sector has also seen improvements, with electric and hybrid vehicles playing a key role in enhancing fuel efficiency22. Despite significant progress, numerous challenges remain. Energy efficiency improvements often face barriers such as high upfront costs, insufficient financing options, and a lack of awareness or information23. Overcoming these barriers requires comprehensive strategies that include robust policy support, stakeholder engagement, and continuous technological innovation.

Potential of energy resources in CPEC regions

The China-Pakistan Economic Corridor (CPEC) regions possess significant potential for renewable energy development, particularly in solar PV and geothermal resources. Pakistan, a key partner in CPEC, has abundant solar energy potential, with an average solar irradiation of 5.3 kWh/m²/day and more than 300 sunny days per year in many areas25. The southern regions of Pakistan, including Balochistan and Sindh, are especially suitable for large-scale solar PV installations due to their high solar insolation levels and vast open lands26. Recent feasibility studies have shown that implementing solar PV plants in these regions could significantly contribute to Pakistan’s energy mix and help meet growing electricity demands. Geothermal energy also presents a promising opportunity in certain CPEC regions. Pakistan has several geothermal prospects, particularly in the northern areas of Gilgit-Baltistan and parts of Balochistan27. While geothermal energy development is still in its early stages compared to solar PV, recent geological surveys and thermal spring assessments indicate significant potential for both electricity generation and direct use applications. The implementation of geothermal plants in these regions could provide a stable, baseload renewable energy source to complement the intermittent nature of solar and wind power28. The feasibility of implementing both solar PV and geothermal plants in CPEC regions is further enhanced by China’s expertise in renewable energy technologies and its commitment to green development within the Belt and Road Initiative (BRI) framework. However, challenges such as grid integration, initial high investment costs, and the need for specialized skills and technologies must be addressed to fully realize the potential of these resources.

Mathematical model

Dependent variable

Energy Efficiency Ratio: Energy output/input, also known as the energy efficiency ratio or coefficient, is a measure of how effectively a system converts input energy into useful output energy31,32-42. It is calculated as the ratio of energy output to energy input. It is determined by the amount of energy produced by the system relative to the amount of energy used by the system. A higher energy efficiency ratio indicates that a system has effectively converted a larger proportion of the input energy into useful output energy, reflecting a more efficient operation. On the other hand, a lower energy efficiency ratio signifies that a system has wasted more input energy and produced less useful output energy, indicating lower efficiency. Energy output/input is a crucial metric used in various industries and systems to evaluate the performance and efficiency of energy conversion processes, appliances, equipment, and overall energy systems. Increasing energy efficiency is essential for cost savings, environmental sustainability, and overall system optimization.

Independent variables

Hydro (TWh)

This variable quantifies electricity from hydroelectric power that is generated by harnessing the energy of flowing or falling water. This power is generated by converting the kinetic energy of flowing water into electrical energy using turbines.

Biofuel (TWh)

This variable quantifies biofuel derived from biological materials, often referred to as biomass. These materials can include plant matter, animal waste, and other organic substances. Biofuels are used as alternatives to fossil fuels in various applications, such as transportation, heating, and electricity generation and are considered as renewable energies.

Solar PV (TWh)

This variable quantifies the electricity generated from Solar PV (Photovoltaic) technology that converts sunlight directly into electricity using semiconductor material.

Geothermal (TWh)

This variable quantifies the energy source that harnesses the heat stored beneath the Earth’s surface to generate electricity or provide heating and cooling for buildings.

Plot of residuals vs. fitted values

Plotting residuals against fitted values is presented on Fig. 2 below:

Plotting residuals vs. fitted values.

Plotting residuals against fitted values provides an additional method to examine for constant variance (homoscedasticity) across all levels of fitted values, a crucial assumption for linear regression. The scatter plot displays randomly distributed residuals along the horizontal axis, indicating no discernible pattern. This absence of systematic errors is a positive indicator. Moreover, the plot does not reveal any discernible trend or curve, suggesting that the model adequately captures nonlinearities. However, there is a slight increase in residual variance for larger fitted values, possibly indicating heteroscedasticity. This observation suggests that the variance of residuals may not be consistent across all levels of fitted values. The approximately normal distribution of residuals strengthens the veracity of the model by providing the basis for the standard tests of coefficients, which are based on the normality assumption. This suggests that t-tests and F-tests can be used in regression analysis as they are suitable. The relatively larger size of residuals at higher fitted values suggests that the data may be heteroscedastic. This, in turn, may lead to the standard errors of the regression coefficient being less reliable, which in turn may lead to the hypothesis tests and confidence intervals being less reliable. One potential solution is the use of robust standard errors to correct for inference procedures in the presence of heteroscedasticity thus ensuring that the statistical conclusions are more reliable. The model provides a way of testing for the homoscedasticity assumption, and the possibility of heteroscedasticity should be subjected to further research, for instance, using tests created for this purpose or by applying corrective measures such as heteroscedasticity-consistent standard error estimators.

Discussion of findings

This study provides significant insights into the complex relationship between renewable energy production and energy efficiency within the CPEC, directly the study’s aim. Our analysis, employing multiple linear regression, reveals nuanced interactions challenging simplistic assumptions about renewable energy expansion’s impact on overall efficiency. Using data from 2000–2022, the analysis integrates descriptive statistics, regression, and correlation analysis. Firstly, the study quantifies this relationship. Descriptive statistics show considerable hydropower production variability (mean: 1190.22 TWh, standard deviation: 70.52 TWh), contrasting with the stability of biofuel (mean: 294.01 TWh, standard deviation: 17.30 TWh), solar PV (mean: 79.06 TWh, standard deviation: 4.43 TWh), and geothermal (mean: 0.125 TWh, standard deviation: 0.007 TWh) production. The energy efficiency ratio ranges from 0.98 to 1.39 (mean: 1.16), also exhibiting considerable variability. Regression analysis reveals significant negative correlations between energy efficiency and both hydropower (−7.642 × 10⁻⁴, p < 0.001) and biofuel production (−9.580 × 10⁻⁴, p < 0.001), indicating that increased production does not automatically translate to higher overall efficiency. Secondly, the analysis identifies specific challenges. The negative correlation between hydropower production and energy efficiency (−0.632, p < 0.001) highlights the need to address transmission losses and grid infrastructure limitations. The moderately negative correlation between biofuel production and energy efficiency (−0.222, p < 0.001) underscores the energy intensity of its production processes. These findings, supported by regression coefficients of −7.642 × 10⁻⁴ (p < 0.001) for hydropower and − 9.580 × 10⁻⁴ (p < 0.001) for biofuel, directly inform policy decisions regarding investment and infrastructure development. Thirdly, the study offers critical insights into the complex interplay between technology, production, and efficiency. The non-significant relationships between energy efficiency and both solar PV (regression coefficient: −5.235 × 10⁻⁴, p = 0.538) and geothermal production (regression coefficient: −0.655, p = 0.214) at their current scales highlight the need for a holistic approach encompassing technological advancements, infrastructure development, and grid integration to maximize efficiency gains. The weak correlations observed between energy efficiency and both solar PV (−0.027, p = 0.638) and geothermal (−0.014, p = 0.806) production further support this conclusion. However, several specific challenges hinder broader adoption of these technologies within the CPEC. These include geographical limitations restricting suitable locations for installation, constraints on grid capacity to integrate increased renewable energy, high upfront capital costs, a shortage of skilled personnel for installation and maintenance, and insufficient policy incentives to promote widespread deployment. Addressing these challenges is crucial for maximizing the efficiency benefits of solar PV and geothermal energy within the CPEC. Fourthly, the results underscore the need for a balanced approach to renewable energy development—optimizing energy conversion and transmission efficiency alongside increasing production capacity—to maximize the benefits of investment. Fifthly, this study significantly advances our understanding of renewable energy integration’s impact on efficiency, particularly within large-scale infrastructure projects like the CPEC. The findings are applicable beyond the CPEC, informing initiatives elsewhere. Sixthly, the identified variability in energy efficiency and inconsistencies between expected and actual outcomes highlight the need for further investigation to optimize renewable energy systems. Seventhly, by identifying areas where efficiency is lacking (e.g., hydropower transmission and biofuel production), the study provides critical guidance for targeted interventions to improve efficiency. Eighthly, this comprehensive, data-driven analysis is readily applicable to policymakers. Ninthly, the analysis showcases the importance of considering the total energy system in efficiency assessments; examining individual production efficiencies alone provides an incomplete picture. Tenthly, this research contributes substantially to our understanding of energy efficiency in large-scale renewable energy integration, offering critical information to support and refine policy interventions. In conclusion, this study successfully addresses the objectives, providing a comprehensive, data-driven analysis of renewable energy efficiency within the CPEC. The findings not only quantify relationships between production and efficiency but also identify challenges, inform policy, and guide future research and interventions. Finaly, The findings underscore the need for future funding in technological development, particularly in energy efficiency and waste reduction, which are pivotal aspects of global change mitigation. This issue is not an isolated assertion but rather a logical extension of the study’s key findings. While the core analysis focuses on the energy efficiency ratio, the results highlight significant inefficiencies within the CPEC’s renewable energy sector. These inefficiencies, particularly the negative correlations observed between energy efficiency and hydropower/biofuel production, translate into substantial energy losses. These losses, whether due to transmission inefficiencies in hydropower or energy-intensive processing in biofuel production, can be viewed as a form of energy waste. Addressing these inefficiencies, therefore, requires not just improving the efficiency of energy conversion, but also actively reducing energy losses throughout the entire energy production and utilization chain. This includes improvements in transmission infrastructure, advancements in biofuel production techniques, better grid management practices, and the development of energy storage solutions. Reducing this ‘waste’ is directly linked to improving overall energy efficiency and is a pivotal aspect of global change mitigation efforts. Further research into minimizing energy waste across the entire energy lifecycle is thus a logical and crucial next step supported by the findings of this study.

Conclusion

This study provides a comprehensive, data-driven analysis of renewable energy efficiency within the CPEC, utilizing data from 2000 to 2022. Our findings reveal a complex interplay between renewable energy production and overall energy efficiency, challenging simplistic assumptions about the direct link between increased production and improved efficiency. Specifically, our analysis, using multiple linear regression and correlation analysis, reveals significant negative correlations between energy efficiency and both hydropower (−0.632, p < 0.001) and biofuel (−0.222, p < 0.001) production. Regression analysis further quantifies these relationships, with negative coefficients of −7.642 × 10⁻⁴ (p < 0.001) for hydropower and − 9.580 × 10⁻⁴ (p < 0.001) for biofuel, highlighting the need to address transmission losses and the energy intensity of biofuel production, respectively. In contrast, solar PV and geothermal energy sources showed no statistically significant relationship with energy efficiency at their current scales of implementation, suggesting that further investment in grid infrastructure and technological advancements are crucial to maximize their potential contributions to overall efficiency. The observed variability in energy efficiency (ranging from 0.98 to 1.39), coupled with the varied production levels across different renewable sources, underlines the need for a comprehensive and holistic approach to policymaking that considers the entire energy system and not only individual energy sources. Despite the valuable insights generated by this study, several limitations must be acknowledged. The analysis relies on available data, which may not fully capture the complexities of energy production and consumption within the CPEC. The model employed simplifies a complex system, not explicitly accounting for external factors such as macroeconomic conditions, policy changes, and technological advancements that can impact energy efficiency. Furthermore, the exclusion of wind energy due to data limitations restricts the generalizability of some findings. Future research should address these limitations by incorporating more comprehensive data, refining the econometric model to include relevant control variables, and expanding the scope to encompass a broader range of renewable energy sources. In conclusion, while acknowledging these limitations, this study provides critical insights for policymakers and stakeholders involved in the CPEC’s energy sector. The findings emphasize the need for a more holistic approach to renewable energy development, considering not just production capacity but also transmission efficiency, waste reduction, and grid integration to maximize the benefits of renewable energy investments and enhance overall energy efficiency. Targeted interventions to address the specific challenges identified in this study, particularly in the hydropower and biofuel sectors, are crucial to achieving the CPEC’s sustainable development goals. Further research, incorporating broader datasets and more sophisticated models, should focus on comprehensively analyzing the interplay between renewable energy sources, technological innovation, and energy efficiency within large-scale infrastructure projects.

Limitations of the study

This study provides valuable insights into renewable energy efficiency within the CPEC, but several limitations should be acknowledged and their impact on the interpretation of results and recommendations considered. First, data availability and reliability pose a significant constraint. The dataset, while comprehensive for certain renewable energy sources (hydro, biofuel, solar PV, and geothermal), lacks consistently reliable data on actual wind energy production across the CPEC region during the study period (2000–2022). Inconsistent reporting standards and variations in data collection methodologies across different provinces/regions within China and Pakistan prevented its inclusion. This limitation may underestimate the overall impact of renewable energy on efficiency, as wind energy constitutes a substantial portion of renewable capacity in some regions. Furthermore, the dataset may not fully capture the nuanced impacts of diverse technological implementations within each energy source category, potentially leading to an oversimplified representation of energy efficiency. This could influence the recommendations by suggesting improvements for certain technologies while potentially overlooking other, equally important areas needing attention. Second, the econometric model employed, while suitable for analyzing the relationship between specific renewable energy sources and energy efficiency, inherently simplifies a complex system. The model does not explicitly account for the influence of external factors such as macroeconomic conditions, policy shifts, technological advancements, and environmental variables that can significantly impact energy production and consumption patterns. The omission of these factors may lead to incomplete or potentially biased estimates of the relationships under investigation, thus potentially leading to recommendations that are not fully aligned with the broader reality of the CPEC energy landscape. For example, recommendations concerning biofuel production efficiency may not be fully applicable without acknowledging potential changes in agricultural policies or crop yields. Future research should address these limitations by: (1) incorporating more comprehensive and consistently reported data, including a wider range of renewable energy sources (particularly wind energy, once reliably comparable data becomes available); (2) exploring more sophisticated modeling techniques that can account for the non-linearity and interdependencies often found within complex energy systems; and (3) explicitly including relevant control variables to account for the influence of macroeconomic conditions, policy interventions, and other factors. While the current analysis provides a valuable foundational understanding, these improvements would lead to a more robust and nuanced assessment of renewable energy efficiency within the CPEC, resulting in more targeted and effective recommendations.

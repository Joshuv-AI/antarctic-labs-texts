QTL mapping for hundred-seed weight in soybean using high-density SLAF-seq technology
Authors: Chunlei Zhang, Huilong Hong, Rongqiang Yuan, Kezhen Zhao, Bire Zha, Sobhi F Lamlom, Xiaoyu Xia, Honglei Ren, Lijuan Qiu, Jiajun Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Abstract Hundred-seed weight is a critical yield component and quality trait in soybean that directly influences grain yield potential and market value. To dissect the genetic architecture underlying seed weight variation, we conducted comprehensive quantitative trait loci (QTL) mapping using a recombinant inbred line (RIL) population of 325 F₂:₅ lines derived from a cross between Qihuang 34 (high seed weight parent, mean 23.2 g) and Dongsheng 16 (low seed weight parent, mean 18.6 g). High-density genotyping was performed using specific-locus amplified fragment sequencing (SLAF-seq), generating 2,841 polymorphic markers distributed across 20 linkage groups spanning 2,387.6 cM with an average marker density of 0.84 cM. Multi-environment field trials were conducted in Harbin (2022–2023) and Sanya (2023/2024) to evaluate hundred-seed weight across contrasting environmental conditions. QTL analysis identified 11 significant QTLs distributed across 8 chromosomes (4, 6, 7, 13, 15, 18, 19, and 20), with individual QTLs explaining 2.47–8.59% of phenotypic variance, modest but typical effect sizes for polygenic seed weight traits that collectively account for 62% of total phenotypic variation. The major QTL qSW19-1 on chromosome 19 was detected in 2024 with the highest significance (LOD = 9.72, PVE = 8.59%), representing a novel locus in an infrequently reported chromosomal region for seed weight. Two QTLs (qSW6-1 and qSW7-1) demonstrated cross-year stability, indicating reliable targets for marker-assisted selection. Broad-sense heritability for seed weight was estimated at 0.68, indicating substantial genetic control with moderate genotype × environment interactions. Candidate gene analysis within major QTL regions identified Glyma.19G195400 (cell wall invertase) as the most promising candidate, showing 3.8-fold higher expression in the high seed weight parent during seed filling and strong correlation with phenotype (r = 0.68). Additional validated candidates include Glyma.19G194300 (PEBP family protein), Glyma.19G193400 (bZIP transcription factor), and Glyma.06G095100 (Myb DNA-binding protein), suggesting complex regulatory networks control seed weight. qRT-PCR validation identified four genes with differential expression between contrasting parents, including a cell wall invertase gene with a 3.2-fold difference in expression. These findings expand the genetic landscape of soybean seed weight control, provide molecular markers for breeding applications, and identify functionally validated candidate genes for future mechanistic studies.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12870-025-07860-5.Keywords: Soybean, Hundred-seed weight, QTL mapping, SLAF-seq, Candidate gene, Molecular markers, Cell wall invertase

Introduction

Soybean (Glycine max L. Merr.) represents one of the most economically important legume crops globally, serving as a fundamental source of plant-based protein and vegetable oil for both human consumption and industrial applications [1]. The crop’s significance has steadily increased over recent decades, with global production reaching approximately 350 million metric tons annually, driven by expanding demand from food processing, livestock feed, and biofuel industries [2, 3]. Among the various agronomic traits that determine soybean productivity, hundred-seed weight stands out as a critical yield component that directly influences both grain yield potential and market value [4].

Hundred-seed weight (HSW) exhibits substantial genetic variation among soybean genotypes and serves as a primary determinant of final grain yield. Previous research has established positive correlations between individual seed weight and overall productivity, with heavier seeds generally contributing to higher per-plant yields when other yield components remain constant [5]. Beyond yield implications, seed weight significantly affects seed quality attributes, including germination rate, seedling vigor, and stress tolerance during establishment [6]. Furthermore, seed size requirements vary considerably among different end-use applications, with large-seeded varieties preferred for direct human consumption products such as edamame and tofu, while smaller seeds are typically utilized for oil extraction and animal feed formulations [5, 7].

The genetic architecture underlying seed weight control in soybean has been extensively investigated through quantitative trait loci (QTL) mapping studies, revealing complex polygenic inheritance patterns. Comprehensive surveys of published literature indicate that seed weight QTLs have been detected across all twenty soybean chromosomes, with effect sizes ranging from minor contributions explaining less than 5% of phenotypic variance to major loci accounting for more than 20% of trait variation [8–10]. Notable examples of stable, major-effect QTLs include the chromosome 17 locus qSw17-1, which consistently demonstrates significant effects across multiple genetic backgrounds and environmental conditions [11]. Meta-analyses of seed weight QTL studies have identified several hotspot regions on chromosomes 1, 4, 6, 13, 15, and 20, where multiple independent studies have reported significant associations [8]. However, the molecular mechanisms governing seed size determination remain incompletely understood compared to model systems such as rice and Arabidopsis [12–14].

Despite the abundance of reported seed weight QTLs in soybean, several critical knowledge gaps persist. First, chromosome 19 has received limited attention in seed weight genetics. SoyBase QTL database searches reveal only sporadic reports of seed weight associations in this chromosomal region and no consistently validated major-effect loci. Recent genome-wide association studies (GWAS) using large germplasm collections have similarly not identified strong signals in the central region of chromosome 19 [8–10], suggesting that valuable genetic variation in this region may remain underexploited. Second, the functional validation of candidate genes underlying seed weight QTLs remains limited, with only a handful of genes functionally characterized in soybean, including the ln (narrow leaf) gene influencing both seed number per pod and individual seed size [15], and the PP2C-1 gene encoding a protein phosphatase that regulates seed size through metabolic pathway modulation [16]. Third, traditional QTL mapping approaches using low to moderate marker densities (typically 100–500 markers) often achieve limited genetic resolution, constraining precise localization and subsequent gene identification efforts [17]. High-density molecular marker technologies such as specific-locus amplified fragment sequencing (SLAF-seq) offer significant advantages by providing thousands of evenly distributed markers across the genome, enabling more precise QTL localization and improved detection power for moderate-effect loci [18].

The present study employed a recombinant inbred line (RIL) population consisting of 325 F₂:₅ lines derived from a cross between Qihuang 34 and Dongsheng 16 to conduct comprehensive QTL mapping for hundred-seed weight using high-density SLAF-seq genotyping. Multi-environment field trials were conducted across two contrasting locations in China (Harbin and Sanya) over multiple growing seasons to assess genetic stability and environmental interactions. Our specific objectives were to: (1) construct a high-density genetic linkage map using SLAF-seq technology to achieve marker resolution suitable for precise QTL localization, (2) identify and characterize QTLs controlling hundred-seed weight and assess their effect sizes and statistical confidence, (3) evaluate QTL stability across environments to determine reliability for breeding applications, and (4) identify and validate candidate genes underlying major QTLs through comparative genomic analysis and gene expression profiling. These findings contribute to understanding soybean seed weight genetics and provide molecular markers and candidate genes for genomics-assisted breeding applications.

Materials and methods

Plant materials and population development

A recombinant inbred line (RIL) population consisting of 325 F₂:₅ lines was developed from a cross between two soybean (Glycine max L. Merr.) cultivars with contrasting seed weight phenotypes. The female parent, Qihuang 34, is a high-yielding cultivar with large seed size (mean hundred-seed weight: 23.2 g ± 1.1 across test environments) selected by the Crop Research Institute of Shandong Academy of Agricultural Sciences. This cultivar was developed for temperate production regions and represents germplasm adapted to northern Chinese growing conditions. The male parent, Dongsheng 16, was developed by the Agricultural Technology Center of Northeast Institute of Geography and Agroecology, Chinese Academy of Sciences, and exhibits a smaller seed size (mean hundred-seed weight: 18.6 g ± 0.9). The phenotypic difference of 4.6 g between parents (approximately 20% variation) provides sufficient contrast for detecting quantitative trait loci (QTLs) with moderate to large effects. The cross was made in 2017, and F₁ plants were confirmed for hybridity. The RIL population was advanced through the single-seed descent (SSD) method from F₂ to F₂:₅ generation over three generations (2018–2020) in both field (Harbin, summer season) and winter nursery (Sanya, winter season) conditions to accelerate generation advancement. Single seeds were randomly selected from individual F₂ plants to establish F₃ lines, and this process was repeated through F₅. At the F₂:₅ generation, seeds from individual F₅ plants were bulk harvested to establish each RIL for phenotypic evaluation and genotyping. Both parents share similar maturity groups (MG III-IV). They are agronomically adapted to northern Chinese soybean production zones, minimizing confounding effects from flowering time and maturity differences while maximizing genetic variation for seed weight traits.

Field experiments and phenotypic evaluation

Multi-environment field experiments were conducted at two locations over three growing seasons to evaluate hundred-seed weight and assess genotype × environment interactions thoroughly. The first location was the Soybean Research Institute Experimental Farm in Harbin, Heilongjiang Province (45°51’27"N, 126°47’22"E, elevation 142 m) during the 2022 and 2023 spring-summer growing seasons. The second location was the Tropical Agricultural Research Station in Sanya, Hainan Province (18°21’33"N, 109°10’03"E, elevation 8 m) during the 2023–2024 winter growing season. These locations represent contrasting environmental conditions: Harbin provides a typical temperate continental climate with distinct seasons and black soil (Mollisol), while Sanya offers a subtropical maritime climate with year-round warm temperatures and red soil (Ultisol). At both locations, experiments were set up using a randomized complete block design (RCBD) with three replications. Each experimental plot consisted of a single 3-meter row. In Harbin, plots had 5 cm plant spacing within rows and 65 cm between-row spacing (ridge width), while in Sanya, 5 cm plant spacing was maintained with 50 cm between-row spacing to match local farming practices. Planting dates were May 15, 2022, and May 18, 2023, for Harbin trials, and November 12, 2023, for the Sanya trial. Standard agronomic practices were followed according to local recommendations to optimize soybean production, including pre-planting fertilization (N: P₂O₅:K₂O = 30:60:60 kg/ha in Harbin; 40:80:80 kg/ha in Sanya), weed control through herbicides and manual weeding, and pest management as needed. Hundred-seed weight (HSW) was measured as the primary trait for genetic analysis. At physiological maturity (R8 stage, approximately 130–140 days after planting depending on environment), plants were hand-harvested by plot. After natural air-drying in the field for 7–10 days, seeds were mechanically threshed and cleaned to remove debris and damaged seeds. For HSW determination, 100 intact seeds were randomly selected from each plot and weighed using an analytical balance accurate to 0.001 g (Sartorius BSA224S, Germany). All seed samples were equilibrated to approximately 13% moisture content in a controlled environment room (20 °C, 60% relative humidity) for 48 h before weighing to ensure consistency across samples. Each plot measurement represents the mean of three technical replicates (three sets of 100 seeds weighed independently). Plot means were used for statistical analysis and QTL mapping.

DNA extraction, construction and genotyping of SLAF libraries

Fresh leaves from the two parents and all 325 RILs were collected into centrifuge tubes, frozen in liquid nitrogen, ground in a tissue grinder, and then stored at −80 °C. Total genomic DNA was extracted from each leaf sample using a modified CTAB method [19]. The quality and concentration of the extracted DNA were assessed using 1% agarose gel electrophoresis and a spectrophotometer (UV-Vis Spectrophotometer Q5000). Individual SLAF libraries for 325 RILs and their parents were prepared and sequenced. A combination of Rsa I and Hae III restriction endonucleases was selected to digest the genomic DNA. The SLAF markers of the 325 RILs were grouped and genotyped following the method of Sun et al. [20]. The high-density molecular tags developed for the RIL population were used to construct a genetic map with HighMap software [21].

Genetic linkage map construction

A high-density genetic linkage map was constructed using the filtered polymorphic SLAF markers, showing clear Mendelian segregation patterns in the RIL population. Marker genotypes were coded using standard notation: “A” for Qihuang 34 homozygous alleles, “B” for Dongsheng 16 homozygous alleles, and “H” for heterozygous genotypes. Markers with identical genotype patterns across all RILs (redundant markers) were identified and grouped into bins, with one representative marker selected per bin to reduce computational burden while maintaining map information content. Map distances were calculated using the Kosambi mapping function, and marker order was determined through regression mapping algorithms. Quantitative trait loci associated with seed weight traits were identified using QTL IciMapping V4.2 software [22]. The inclusive composite interval mapping (ICIM-ADD) method was employed with the following parameters: a scanning step size of 1 cM, a LOD threshold determined by 1,000 permutation tests at the 95% confidence level, and a probability for stepwise regression of 0.001.

Candidate gene identification and functional annotation

Candidate genes within QTL confidence intervals were identified through thorough bioinformatics analysis. Gene models within these regions were extracted from the Glycine max Wm82.a2.v1 annotation (Glyma2.0) available at Phytozome v13 (https://phytozome-next.jgi.doe.gov/) and SoyBase (https://www.soybase.org/). Functional annotations were sourced from several platforms: gene ontology (GO) terms from the Soybean Knowledge Base (SoyKB, https://soybase.org/soyKB/), (2) protein domain predictions from InterProScan v5.52 and, pathway assignments from KEGG.

Quantitative real-time PCR analysis

For validating gene expression, we collected samples from the parental lines Qihuang 34 (QH34) and Dongsheng 16 (DS16) during seed development. We collected developing seeds at 15, 25, 35, and 45 days after flowering (DAF), with three biological replicates per genotype and time point. We extracted total RNA from 100 mg of ground seed tissue using the FastPure Plant Total RNA Isolation Kit (Vazyme, China). We assessed RNA quality using NanoDrop spectrophotometry (A260/A280 = 1.8–2.1) and agarose gel electrophoresis. We synthesized first-strand cDNA from 1 µg of RNA using HiScript III RT SuperMix (Vazyme) and diluted it 1:10 for analysis. We designed gene-specific primers for eight candidate genes using Primer Premier 5.0, targeting 100–200 bp amplicons with melting temperatures between 58 and 62 °C. We verified primer specificity using BLAST and melting curve analyses. We used β-tubulin (Glyma. 05G157200) as the reference gene (Table S1). We conducted qRT-PCR using ChamQ Universal SYBR qPCR Master Mix (Vazyme) on a CFX96 Touch system (Bio-Rad). Our reactions (20 µL) contained 10 µL SYBR Master Mix, 0.4 µL of each primer (10 µM), 2 µL cDNA, and 7.2 µL nuclease-free water. The thermal cycling conditions included 95 °C for 30 s, followed by 40 cycles at 95 °C for 10 s and 60 °C for 30 s, and then melting curve analysis. We ran all samples in technical triplicates with no-template controls. We calculated relative expression levels using the 2^(-ΔΔCt) method, normalized to β-tubulin, with the high-seed-weight group at 15 DAF as the calibrator. We analyzed the data using a two-way ANOVA with Tukey’s HSD post hoc tests. We used Pearson’s correlation analysis to examine relationships between gene expression and seed weight phenotypes in R (v4.3.0; p < 0.05).

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using R software version 4.3.0 [23]. Descriptive statistics, including mean, standard deviation, coefficient of variation, and range, were calculated for all traits. Analysis of variance (ANOVA) was conducted to assess genotype, environment, and genotype × environment interaction effects using a mixed linear model. Broad-sense heritability (H²) was estimated using variance components from the mixed model analysis: H² = σ²G/(σ²G + σ²GE/e + σ²e/re), where σ²G is the genetic variance, σ²GE is the genotype × environment interaction variance, σ²e is the error variance, e is the number of environments, and r is the number of replications.

Results

Phenotypic variation and distribution analysis

The field experiments conducted across multiple locations and years revealed significant phenotypic variation in hundred-seed weight (HSW), highlighting the quantitative nature of this key agronomic trait (Table 1). The parental lines showed substantial contrast, with Qihuang 34 exhibiting higher seed weight (mean 23.2 g ± 1.1 across environments) than Dongsheng 16 (mean 18.6 g ± 0.9), a difference of 4.6 g (approximately 20% variation). This phenotypic contrast provided sufficient statistical power for detecting QTLs with moderate to significant effects in the RIL population. HSW in the RIL population showed continuous variation across all environments, with means of 20.49 g in 2023 Harbin, 22.50 g in 2024 Sanya, and 21.48 g in 2022 Harbin. The standard deviations remained relatively consistent across environments (2.97 g in 2023 Harbin, 2.81 g in 2024 Sanya, and 2.66 g in 2022 Harbin), indicating stable genetic variation despite differing environmental conditions. Distribution analysis demonstrated that HSW approximately followed normal distributions across all three environments (Fig. 1A-B), with histograms displaying characteristic bell-shaped curves overlaid by fitted normal density curves. The Q-Q plots (Fig. 1B) confirmed the normality assumption for most data points, although some deviations were observed in the tails, particularly for extreme values. This near-normal distribution suggests that HSW is controlled by multiple genes with additive effects, making it suitable for quantitative genetic analysis. Comprehensive analysis across the three environments revealed significant effects of location and year on HSW performance (Fig. 1C; Table 1). The 2024 Sanya winter season provided the most favorable conditions for seed development, with the highest mean HSW (22.50 g) and median (22.33 g), compared to 2022 Harbin (mean: 21.48 g, median: 21.58 g) and 2023 Harbin (mean: 20.49 g, median: 20.14 g). Box plot analysis (Fig. 1C) showed that the 2024 Sanya environment also had the widest interquartile range, indicating that the subtropical winter conditions permitted maximum expression of genetic potential for seed size. Correlation analysis among environments revealed strong positive relationships (Fig. 1D). The highest correlation was observed between the two Harbin seasons (2022 and 2023: r = 0.93), reflecting consistent genotype performance across years at the same site. Cross-location correlations were moderately strong, with r = 0.71 between 2023 Harbin and 2024 Sanya, and r = 0.92 between 2022 Harbin and 2024 Sanya. These correlation patterns indicate that within-location temporal variation has less impact on genotype rankings than cross-location environmental differences. The strong positive correlations across all environmental pairs suggest good genotype stability and indicate that HSW is a reliable trait for selection. However, the varying correlation strength (r = 0.71 vs. 0.93) underscores the importance of multi-environment testing for comprehensive genetic evaluation and broad adaptation breeding.

Heritability and genetic parameters

Analysis of variance (ANOVA) across the three environments revealed significant effects of genotype, environment, and genotype × environment interactions for hundred-seed weight (all p < 0.001), confirming the quantitative nature of this trait and the presence of substantial genetic variation within the RIL population (Table 2). Variance component analysis indicated that genetic effects (σ²G) contributed the most significant proportion of total phenotypic variance, followed by genotype × environment interactions (σ²GE) and residual error variance (σ²e). Broad-sense heritability (H²) for hundred-seed weight was estimated separately for each environment and across all environments (Table 3). Per-environment H² values ranged from 0.58 (2023 Harbin) to 0.75 (2024 Sanya), with 2022 Harbin showing intermediate heritability (0.72). The overall H² across the three environments was estimated at 0.68, indicating that approximately 68% of the phenotypic variation was attributable to genetic factors when accounting for genotype × environment interactions. This moderate to high heritability value suggests that seed weight is primarily under genetic control and would respond effectively to selection pressure. The variation in per-environment heritability estimates reflects differential expression of genetic variance across environments. The 2024 Sanya environment shows the highest heritability, consistent with its wider phenotypic range and more favorable growing conditions for seed development. The significant genotype × environment interaction variance component (p < 0.001) indicates that genotype rankings varied across environments, though the strong phenotypic correlations (r = 0.71–0.93) suggest these rank changes were relatively modest. The interaction effects explain the moderate cross-location correlation (r = 0.71 between 2023 Harbin and 2024 Sanya) compared to the high within-location correlation (r = 0.93 between Harbin years), highlighting that environmental factors and their interactions with genotypes play important roles in determining final seed weight phenotypes alongside the predominant genetic control.

High-density genetic map construction and quality assessment

SLAF-seq analysis generated approximately 2.8 million raw sequence tags across the RIL population, with an average sequencing depth of 12.97× for parental lines and 10.05× for RIL individuals. After quality filtering and clustering analysis, 8,247 high-quality SLAF markers were identified, of which 3,892 polymorphic markers showed clear segregation patterns suitable for genetic mapping (Fig. S1 and S2). The final genetic linkage map included 2,841 markers distributed across 20 linkage groups corresponding to the 20 soybean chromosomes, spanning a total genetic distance of 2,387.6 cM with an average marker density of one marker every 0.84 cM (Table S2-S4). Individual chromosome lengths ranged from 89.4 cM (chromosome 11) to 158.7 cM (chromosome 18). The largest gap between adjacent markers was 8.2 cM on chromosome 6, while most inter-marker distances were less than 2 cM, indicating high map resolution suitable for precise QTL localization.

Haplotype maps clearly demonstrate the high quality and accuracy of the constructed genetic map (Fig. S3). Visual inspection of the 20 linkage groups (Gm01-Gm20) revealed predominantly large, contiguous haplotype blocks with minimal color transitions, indicating low frequencies of double crossovers and accurate marker ordering. The RIL population at F₂:₅ generation showed 96.8% homozygous marker loci across all individuals (green for QiHuang34 alleles, blue for DongSheng16 alleles), with only 3.2% heterozygous calls (red) and minimal missing data (gray regions). This level of genetic fixation, while lower than populations advanced to F₆ or beyond, is adequate for accurate QTL detection given the high marker density. The residual heterozygosity of 3.2% is consistent with the expected value of approximately 3.1% for F₅-equivalent lines [(1/2)⁴ = 6.25% residual heterozygosity in F₅, with selection during line development further reducing this value]. Linkage groups Gm01, Gm02, Gm04, Gm07, Gm09, Gm10, Gm13, Gm16, Gm17, and Gm19 displayed pristine block structures with clear inheritance patterns from both parents. More complex recombination patterns were observed in Gm05, Gm06, Gm11, Gm12, Gm14, and Gm20, likely reflecting natural recombination hotspots rather than genotyping errors, as evidenced by their organized and non-random color transition patterns. The haplotype visualization confirmed minimal missing data and showed precise recombination breakpoints without excessive double crossovers, keeping the double-crossover frequency below 3% across all linkage groups. Comparison with the physical map revealed strong collinearity between genetic and physical positions, with only minor inversions typical of recombination hot and cold spots. The marker density achieved in this study represents a significant improvement over previous soybean genetic maps constructed with conventional marker systems (typically 100–500 markers). It provides a robust framework for high-resolution QTL analysis of quantitative traits.

QTL mapping for hundred-seed weight

Comprehensive QTL analysis using the inclusive composite interval mapping (ICIM-ADD) method identified 11 significant QTLs associated with hundred-seed weight, distributed across 8 chromosomes (chromosomes 4, 6, 7, 13, 15, 18, 19, and 20), with LOD scores ranging from 2.51 to 9.72 (Table 4; Figs. 2 and 3, and Fig. S4). The detected QTLs accounted for individual phenotypic variance ranging from 2.47% to 8.59% per locus. The total phenotypic variance explained by all QTLs in the full model was estimated at 62% based on the coefficient of determination (R²), representing a substantial portion of the genetic variation. However, individual QTL effects cannot be directly summed due to potential linkage and epistatic interactions among loci.

The most notable QTL, qSW19-1, was located on chromosome 19 at position 20.0 cM with an LOD score of 9.72, explaining 8.59% of the phenotypic variation. This QTL showed an adverse additive effect of −1.09 g, indicating that the Dongsheng 16 allele contributed to increased seed weight compared to the Qihuang 34 allele at this locus. The LOD-1 support interval for qSW19-1 spans approximately 5.5 cM (17.5–23.0 cM on the genetic map), corresponding to a physical region of approximately 1.8 Mb (43.0–44.8.0.8 Mb) on the reference genome (Wm82.a2.v1). The QTL was flanked by markers Marker1711920 and Marker1774597, which are separated by 11.4 kb in physical distance (44,839,859 − 44,851,229 bp), reflecting high local marker density in this chromosomal region. This narrow marker spacing should not be confused with the confidence interval itself, which represents the uncertainty in QTL position based on the LOD-1 support interval. Two other QTLs showed relatively large effects: qSW7-1 on chromosome 7 (LOD = 6.16, PVE = 6.16%) and qSW15-1 on chromosome 15 (LOD = 7.37, PVE = 6.61%). qSW7-1 exhibited a positive additive effect of 0.92 g, indicating Qihuang 34 alleles increased seed weight at this locus, while qSW15-1 showed a positive additive effect of 0.96 g. The qSW7-1 region covers about 1.22 Mb (from 4,982,829 to 6,207,742 bp). It includes QTLs identified in both 2023 and 2024, indicating either a single QTL with wide effects or several linked genes affecting seed weight within this chromosome segment.

Chromosome 6 harbored one QTL in two locations (qSW6-1) at position 111 cM, showing negative additive effects and explaining 2.74% and 2.47% of phenotypic variance, respectively. The physical interval spans 714 kb (6,958,441-7,672,604 bp). Similarly, chromosome 20 contained two QTLs (qSW20-1 and qSW20-2) in proximity (positions 31 and 36 cM), with moderate effects (PVE = 5.07–6.16%) and negative additive effects ranging from − 0.84 to −0.89 g. The remaining QTLs (qSW4-1, qSW13-1, and qSW18-1) exhibited more minor individual effects, each explaining approximately 3.0–3.5.0.5% of phenotypic variance, with LOD scores between 3.10 and 3.58. All three QTLs displayed negative additive effects, indicating that Dongsheng 16 alleles increased seed weight at these loci despite this parent having lower overall seed weight, illustrating the complex distribution of favorable alleles in quantitative trait inheritance.

QTL stability and environmental effects

Analysis of QTL detection patterns across years revealed important insights into temporal consistency and environment-specific expression. Among the 11 hundred-seed weight QTLs identified, two loci demonstrated cross-year stability, representing 18% of total detected QTLs. We defined stability as consistent QTL detection in both 2023 and 2024 at similar genetic positions (within 5 cM) with the same direction of allelic effects. qSW6-1 on chromosome 6 (position 111.0 cM) was consistently detected in both years with LOD scores of 2.51 (2023) and 3.21 (2024), explaining 2.47% and 2.74% of phenotypic variance, respectively. qSW7-1 on chromosome 7 showed stable expression across years at nearly identical positions (102.0 cM in 2023, 99.0 cM in 2024) with substantial effects (LOD = 6.52 and 6.16; PVE = 7.73% and 6.16%). Both stable QTLs maintained consistent negative additive effects in both years, confirming that Dongsheng 16 alleles confer increased seed weight at these loci. The remaining 9 QTLs (82%) showed year-specific expression patterns, indicating substantial genotype × environment interactions for most loci controlling seed weight. The major seed weight QTL qSW19-1 was detected only in 2024 with exceptionally high significance (LOD = 9.72, PVE = 8.59%), suggesting this locus may be activated under specific environmental conditions characteristic of the Sanya winter growing season or may require particular developmental conditions for expression. Several other QTLs demonstrated clear temporal specificity: qSW4-1, qSW13-1, and qSW18-1 were detected only in 2023 (Harbin environment), while qSW15-1 was detected exclusively in 2024 (Sanya environment). This pattern indicates that temporal environmental factors—including temperature regimes, photoperiod, water availability, and their interactions with developmental stage—significantly influence QTL expression and detection power.

The magnitude of detected QTL effects also varied between years and locations. The 2024 Sanya environment yielded generally higher individual QTL effects, including detection of the strongest QTL (qSW19-1) and qSW15-1 (LOD = 7.37), and showed the highest environmental heritability (H² = 0.75). This pattern suggests that the subtropical winter conditions in Sanya—characterized by moderate temperatures, long growing season, and optimal moisture availability—were more conducive to expressing genetic variation for seed weight and provided better conditions for QTL detection. In contrast, the 2023 Harbin environment showed lower heritability (H² = 0.58) and detected fewer large-effect QTLs, possibly due to environmental stresses or suboptimal growing conditions that increased residual variance and reduced genetic signal.

The identification of environmentally stable QTLs (qSW6-1 and qSW7-1) alongside environment-specific QTLs has important implications for breeding strategy. Stable QTLs are particularly valuable for marker-assisted selection programs targeting broad environmental adaptation, as they consistently contribute to seed weight across diverse growing conditions. Environment-specific QTLs, while less universally applicable, may be strategically deployed in breeding programs targeting specific production regions or when selection environments can be controlled (such as winter nurseries). The substantial proportion of environment-specific QTLs (82%) underscores the importance of multi-environment phenotyping for comprehensive characterization of genetic effects and for identifying the most reliable targets for molecular breeding applications.

Among these, several genes are categorized as functional based on gene ontology annotations and homology to characterized genes in other species, relevant to seed development and size determination.

The identification of transcription factors from multiple families (bZIP, Myb, Dof-type, AP2/B3-like) within QTL regions suggests complex transcriptional regulatory networks controlling seed weight traits. These candidate genes represent high-priority targets for functional validation through reverse genetics approaches and for allelic mining across diverse germplasm to identify functional variants.

Quantitative real-time PCR validation of candidate genes

Analysis of candidate genes via qRT-PCR revealed notable differences in expression patterns between the parental lines QH34, high seed weight, and DS16, low seed weight, during seed development (Fig. 4). Expression was assessed at four stages: 15, 25, 35, and 45 DAF, covering early development to maturation. Cell wall invertase (Glyma.19G195400) showed the most pronounced differential pattern, with QH34 consistently displaying higher expression than DS16 across all stages. The peak at 25 DAF in QH34 (3.87-fold increase) was roughly 2.7 times higher than DS16 at the same point. This heightened expression persisted at 35 DAF and 45 DAF, indicating a sustained role in seed development. Similarly, PEBP family protein (Glyma.19G194300) reached its maximum at 25 DAF in QH34 (2.74-fold), significantly surpassing DS16, with elevated levels at 35 DAF (p < 0.05) before declining at 45 DAF. The bZIP transcription factor (Glyma.19G193400) exhibited a comparable pattern, peaking at 25 DAF in QH34 (2.29-fold) with highly significant differences from DS16 (*p < 0.01) at 25 and 35 DAF, and at 45 DAF. Myb DNA-binding domain (Glyma.06G095100) demonstrated late-stage upregulation in QH34, showing similar levels at early stages but significantly higher expression at 35 DAF (*p < 0.01) and 45 DAF (p < 0.05), suggesting a role in late seed maturation. Genes such as the protein kinase superfamily (Glyma.19G191600), LRR transmembrane protein kinase (Glyma.06G090700), and DNA-binding transcription factors (Glyma.06G094700) showed stable, consistent expression across stages with modest differences between genotypes, implying roles in maintaining cellular homeostasis during seed filling. The convergence of peak expression at 25 DAF for cell wall invertase, a PEBP family protein, and a bZIP transcription factor in QH34 coincides with the critical seed-filling phase, indicating coordinated regulation underlying high seed weight. Persistent elevated expression of cell wall invertase in QH34 particularly highlights enhanced sugar metabolism and resource distribution as key factors in increasing seed weight.

Discussion

Genetic architecture and heritability of Hundred-Seed weight

The identification of 11 QTLs for hundred-seed weight distributed across 8 chromosomes confirms the polygenic nature of this trait in soybean, consistent with previous studies reporting complex quantitative inheritance patterns for seed size traits [24, 25]. The moderate individual effect sizes observed in this study (2.47–8.59% phenotypic variance explained per QTL) align with the typical genetic architecture of quantitative traits, where multiple genes contribute small to moderate effects rather than single major genes controlling the phenotype [26]. The collective phenotypic variance explained by all detected QTLs was estimated at 62% based on the coefficient of determination (R²) from the full QTL model, representing a substantial portion of the genetic variation. This suggests that our high-density SLAF-seq approach successfully captured most major genetic factors influencing seed weight in this population, though the presence of additional minor-effect QTLs below our detection threshold, epistatic interactions among loci, and genotype × environment interactions likely account for the remaining unexplained variance.

The broad-sense heritability estimate of 0.68 across environments indicates that genetic factors predominantly control seed weight variation, which is consistent with heritability values reported in other soybean populations ranging from 0.60 to 0.85 [27, 28]. This moderate to high heritability supports the potential for effective genetic improvement through both conventional phenotypic selection and marker-assisted breeding approaches. However, the significant genotype × environment interactions observed (p < 0.001) reveal important temporal and spatial variation in QTL expression. While correlations between environments were generally strong (r = 0.71–0.93 across all pairwise comparisons), the lower correlation between 2023 Harbin and 2024 Sanya (r = 0.71) compared to within-location correlations between years at the same site (2022 Harbin vs. 2023 Harbin: r = 0.93) indicates that cross-location environmental factors influence genotype performance rankings more substantially than temporal variation at the same location. These patterns emphasize the importance of multi-environment evaluation for accurately assessing genetic effects and their stability across different growing conditions, particularly when breeding for broad environmental adaptation [11, 29].

The environmental specificity of QTL detection observed in this study—with 82% of QTLs showing year-specific expression and only 18% demonstrating cross-year stability—highlights the dynamic nature of seed weight genetic control. Among the 11 detected QTLs, only two loci (qSW6-1 and qSW7-1) were consistently identified in both 2023 and 2024 at similar genetic positions (within 5 cM) with consistent allelic effect directions. Although this level of QTL stability appears low, it is comparable to previous multi-environment QTL studies in soybean and other crops, where 15–30% of identified QTLs typically show consistent expression across different environments [28, 30, 31]. The environmentally stable QTLs serve as particularly valuable targets for marker-assisted selection programs, as they are more likely to yield consistent genetic improvements across diverse production environments.

Modest QTL effect sizes and the polygenic architecture of seed weight

The QTL effect sizes (2.47–8.59% PVE) are modest compared to major-effect loci but are common for seed weight, a polygenic trait influenced by many genes with minor to moderate effects [32–34]. Seed weight results from various developmental processes such as maternal tissue growth, embryo and endosperm cell proliferation, cell expansion, storage compound biosynthesis, and water accumulation that are regulated by diverse genetic pathways, hormones, metabolism, transcriptional networks, and epigenetics [35]. This complex control disperses genetic influence across multiple loci, with even genes involved in specific biochemical steps showing only modest phenotypic effects [36]. Significant G×E interactions (p < 0.001, 24.4%) indicate that genetic effects vary across environments, often diminishing in multi-environment trials. For example, the strongest QTL, qSW19-1, was detected only under certain conditions, likely due to environmental specificity. Most QTLs display environment-dependent expression, with spatial differences having a greater impact than temporal variation. Residual errors from field variability, sampling, and measurement reduce heritability and detection power. Traits like seed size, which are strongly selected, tend to be polygenic, involving many small-effect loci, consistent with theoretical expectations. Most QTLs explain 2–6% of variance, with few exceeding 6%, supporting the idea that phenotypic variation results from numerous loci with effects inversely related to their age [37]. Modest individual QTL effects do not hinder breeding gains; their cumulative impact can lead to significant improvements [38]. Our results show nine QTL regions explain about 62% of phenotypic variance when modeled together, supporting marker-assisted selection. Studies in soybean and other crops demonstrate that selecting on 5–10 QTLs with 2–8% effects can produce progeny 10–15% better than parents via transgressive segregation. Stable QTLs (qSW6-1, qSW7-1) across environments offer reliable breeding targets. Effect sizes here align with or surpass recent soybean studies, where QTLs explain 2.1–7.8%, rarely exceeding 10% PVE. Our major QTL qSW19-1 (8.59% PVE) is among the strongest reported, comparable to loci like qSw17-1. Meta-analyses confirm most soybean seed weight QTLs explain under 10%, with few over 15% [11, 32]. These findings reflect the authentic polygenic architecture of seed weight, shaped by biological and environmental factors. While individual effects are modest, their cumulative impact enables meaningful gains, and stable QTLs serve as valuable markers for breeding programs aimed at improving seed weight and yield.

Novel QTL discovery on chromosome 19 and comparative analysis

The major QTL qSW19-1 on chromosome 19 represents the most significant finding of this study, explaining 8.59% of phenotypic variance with exceptionally high statistical confidence (LOD = 9.72). Several lines of evidence indicate this QTL represents a novel or underexploited genetic factor for seed weight control in soybean. Comprehensive literature review reveals that chromosome 19 has been infrequently and inconsistently associated with seed weight in previous mapping studies. Examination of the SoyBase QTL database (accessed October 2024) identified only three studies reporting seed weight-related QTLs on chromosome 19, all with smaller effect sizes (< 5% variance explained) and located in different chromosomal regions distant from qSW19-1 [citation needed upon database verification]. Recent genome-wide association studies utilizing large germplasm collections have similarly not identified significant associations in the 43–45 Mb physical region of chromosome 19, where qSW19-1 was detected [8–10]. This absence of consistent reports in the literature, combined with the strong statistical support and relatively large effect size observed in our study, suggests that qSW19-1 represents either a novel QTL or an underutilized source of genetic variation for seed weight improvement.

Several factors may explain why qSW19-1 has not been more frequently detected in previous studies. First, this QTL may harbor allelic variation that is relatively rare in commonly used breeding germplasm, with the favorable allele present in Dongsheng 16 potentially derived from distinct genetic lineages not extensively sampled in prior mapping populations. Second, the expression of qSW19-1 appears to show environmental sensitivity, as it was detected only in the 2024 Sanya environment in our study despite strong statistical support. This environmental specificity could cause the QTL to be missed in studies conducted in different locations or years. Third, many earlier QTL mapping studies utilized lower marker densities (typically 100–500 markers), which may have provided insufficient resolution to detect this QTL, particularly if the local recombination rate is low. Our high-density SLAF-seq approach, providing 2,841 markers with an average density of one marker every 0.84 cM, likely enhanced detection power for moderate-effect QTLs such as qSW19-1.

The genetic characteristics of qSW19-1 make it particularly valuable for breeding applications despite its environmental specificity. The LOD-1 support interval spans approximately 5.5 cM (17.5–23.0 cM on the genetic map), corresponding to a physical region of approximately 1.8 Mb (43.0–44.8.0.8 Mb on the reference genome Wm82.a2.v1). While this confidence interval is moderately large, it is substantially narrower than intervals typically obtained with conventional marker densities, facilitating more targeted candidate gene analysis. Notably, the immediate flanking markers (Marker1711920 and Marker1774597) are separated by only 11.4 kb in physical distance (44,839,859 − 44,851,229 bp), indicating high local marker density in this chromosomal region, though this marker spacing should not be confused with the confidence interval itself. The negative additive effect (−1.09 g) indicates that Dongsheng 16 alleles contribute to increased seed weight at this locus, illustrating the complex nature of quantitative inheritance where favorable alleles can be distributed across both parents regardless of their overall phenotypic performance [30].

QTL clustering was observed on chromosomes 6, 7, and 20, where multiple QTLs were detected in close proximity. The chromosome 7 region containing qSW7-1 and qSW7-2 is particularly noteworthy, spanning approximately 1.2 Mb (4.98–6.21 Mb) and suggesting either pleiotropic effects of a single gene with broad influence on seed development or tight linkage of multiple functionally related genes. Such QTL clustering has been reported previously in soybean and other crop species, often reflecting genomic regions that harbor numerous genes involved in related biological processes or ancient tandem duplications [28, 31]. The detection of qSW7-1 in both 2023 and 2024 (at 102 cM and 99 cM, respectively) with significant, consistent effects (LOD = 6.52 and 6.16; PVE = 7.73% and 6.16%) marks this region as a second-priority target for fine-mapping and functional characterization. Previous studies have also reported seed weight QTLs on chromosome 7, though typically in different areas, suggesting that chromosome 7 may harbor multiple independent genetic factors controlling seed size [39, 40].

Functional validation through gene expression analysis and candidate gene identification

The qRT-PCR analysis of candidate genes provides crucial molecular validation for the biological relevance of detected QTLs, particularly qSW19-1. The identification of four genes showing significant differential expression between contrasting seed weight parental lines strengthens the evidence that these QTLs contain functionally important genetic variation rather than statistical artifacts [41]. Among the validated candidates, the cell wall invertase gene (Glyma.19G195400) within the qSW19-1 region emerged as the most promising candidate based on multiple lines of evidence: (1) strongest differential expression between parents (up to 3.84-fold higher in high-seed-weight parent Qihuang 34 at 25 DAF), (2) highest correlation with seed weight phenotypes across developmental stages (r = 0.68, p < 0.001), (3) peak expression during the rapid seed filling stage (25–35 DAF) when maximum dry matter accumulation occurs, and (4) well-established functional role in seed development based on ortholog studies in other species.

Cell wall invertases play critical roles in sucrose metabolism and carbon allocation during seed development by catalyzing the irreversible hydrolysis of sucrose into glucose and fructose in the apoplast [42, 43]. This enzymatic activity directly influences sink strength—the capacity of developing seeds to import and utilize photoassimilates—which is a primary determinant of final seed size [44, 45]. In soybean, the seed filling period (approximately 15–45 DAF) is characterized by rapid accumulation of storage proteins and lipids, requiring substantial carbon import from maternal tissues. Enhanced cell wall invertase activity in the seed coat and embryo could increase hexose availability for metabolic processes and osmotic potential, thereby strengthening sink demand and promoting greater dry matter accumulation. Similar cell wall invertase genes have been functionally implicated in seed size control in other crop species, including OsINV2 in rice and ZmIVR2 in maize, suggesting evolutionary conservation of this metabolic pathway in seed development across monocots and dicots [46, 47]. The striking concordance between gene expression patterns and seed weight phenotypes in our parental lines with the high-seed-weight parent (Qihuang 34) showing consistently elevated Glyma.19G195400 expression throughout seed development—provides compelling evidence for a causal relationship.

The PEBP (phosphatidylethanolamine-binding protein) family gene (Glyma.19G194300) also within qSW19-1 represents another strong candidate with demonstrated differential expression (up to 2.66-fold at 25 DAF). PEBP proteins have well-established roles in developmental timing and organ size control in model plants, including flowering time regulation and meristem identity determination [48, 49]. Recent studies in legumes have revealed that PEBP genes can influence seed development by regulating the duration of seed filling or by modulating developmental transitions in reproductive structures. The developmentally regulated expression pattern observed for Glyma.19G194300—with peak differential expression during early to mid-seed development (25–35 DAF)—is consistent with a role in determining seed size potential during critical developmental windows when cell division and expansion rates are established.

The identification of transcription factors showing differential expression between parental lines—including the bZIP transcription factor (Glyma.19G193400) and Myb DNA-binding protein (Glyma.06G095100)—suggests that seed weight QTLs may operate through both metabolic and transcriptional regulatory mechanisms [50, 51]. The bZIP gene showed sustained differential expression throughout seed development, with the most pronounced differences at 25 DAF (2.28-fold), while the Myb gene demonstrated significant differences primarily during late seed development (35–45 DAF), indicating involvement in seed maturation processes. These transcription factors could regulate networks of downstream genes involved in cell proliferation, cell expansion, nutrient transport, or the biosynthesis of storage compounds. The convergence of evidence from metabolic genes (cell wall invertase), developmental regulators (PEBP family), and transcriptional regulators (bZIP, Myb) within major QTL regions suggest that seed weight determination involves coordinated regulation across multiple biological layers—from metabolic flux and hormone signaling to transcriptional networks—consistent with the complex polygenic architecture observed in QTL analysis. It is important to note that demonstrating differential gene expression between parental lines, while supportive, does not definitively establish causality. The candidates identified here should be considered as high-priority targets for further functional validation through approaches such as transgenic overexpression or knockout studies, allelic variation analysis across diverse germplasm, and fine-mapping with near-isogenic lines. Nevertheless, the strong concordance between QTL detection, physical map position, gene expression patterns, and known functional roles provides a solid foundation for pursuing these candidates in subsequent research.

Environmental factors affecting QTL detection power

The variation in QTL detection across environments observed in this study underscores the crucial role of environmental quality in successful QTL mapping. In 2022, the Harbin environment, despite using the same experimental design and population size as in other years, produced significantly fewer QTL detections, which we attribute to several interconnected factors. Meteorological data indicated that 2022 experienced more erratic rainfall patterns, with multiple heavy precipitation events followed by drought periods during key seed development stages, potentially introducing non-genetic variance that masked genetic signals (Table S4). These findings have important implications for experimental design in QTL mapping. First, conducting tests across multiple environments is vital not only for evaluating QTL stability but also for enhancing detection power, since adverse conditions in any single environment can considerably reduce sensitivity [52]. Second, controlling environmental factors such as irrigation, pest management, and harvest timing should be prioritized alongside population size and marker density as key factors influencing mapping resolution. Third, employing adaptive significance thresholds that account for environment-specific error variance may improve QTL detection while maintaining acceptable false discovery rates [53]. Despite identifying only one QTL in 2022, the consistent detection of major QTLs (qSW6-1, qSW7-1) across the other two environments highlights the importance of multi-year evaluation for discovering robust genetic factors suitable for breeding purposes.

Breeding applications and future directions

The QTLs and candidate genes identified in this study provide valuable resources for soybean improvement programs targeting seed weight and related yield components. The environmentally stable QTLs qSW6-1 and qSW7-1, detected consistently across years, represent the most reliable targets for immediate implementation in marker-assisted selection (MAS) schemes. Development of high-throughput marker assays (such as KASP or PACE markers) flanking these stable QTLs would enable breeders to select for favorable alleles in early segregating generations efficiently, reducing population sizes and accelerating breeding cycles [54, 55]. Even the major QTL qSW19-1, despite showing environment-specific expression in our study, may prove valuable for breeding programs targeting specific production environments or for populations segregating in similar genetic backgrounds. The identification of Dongsheng 16 as the source of favorable alleles at qSW19-1, qSW6-1, qSW13-1, qSW18-1, and qSW20-1/20 − 2, despite its lower overall seed weight compared to Qihuang 34, illustrates the potential for transgressive segregation where progeny can exceed both parental phenotypes through the strategic combination of complementary favorable alleles from different sources [56]. Beyond conventional MAS applications, the candidate genes identified, particularly the cell wall invertase (Glyma.19G195400) suggest potential metabolic engineering approaches that could complement traditional breeding efforts. Targeted manipulation of sink strength by modulating cell wall invertase activity or expression has shown promise in other crop species for enhancing seed size and yield [42, 57]. However, such approaches would require careful evaluation of potential pleiotropic effects on seed composition (protein and oil content) and other agronomic traits. The transcription factors identified (Glyma.19G193400, Glyma.06G095100) may offer alternative entry points for genetic engineering, potentially enabling coordinated regulation of multiple downstream targets in seed development pathways while minimizing unintended consequences.

Several research priorities arise from this study to further improve the understanding and application of seed weight genetics in soybean. First, fine-mapping the major QTL qSW19-1 using larger populations or advanced mapping designs (such as multi-parent advanced generation inter-cross populations) would narrow the confidence interval and facilitate positional cloning of the causal gene(s). Developing near-isogenic lines (NILs) that differ specifically for the qSW19-1 region through marker-assisted backcrossing would enable precise measurement of QTL effects across various environments and genetic backgrounds, as well as the evaluation of pleiotropic effects on seed composition and other traits. Second, functional validation of the top candidate genes through reverse genetics approaches, including CRISPR/Cas9-mediated knockout or knockdown studies, would conclusively establish causality and reveal molecular mechanisms. Third, allelic mining of the qSW19-1 region across diverse soybean germplasm collections would help identify additional functional variants and determine the prevalence and effect sizes of different alleles, which is vital for maximizing genetic gains through favorable allele deployment. Fourth, examining epistatic interactions among the detected QTLs, particularly between the significant loci on chromosomes 7, 15, and 19, could uncover genetic networks and provide insights into optimal allele combinations to increase seed weight.

The findings of this study also have implications beyond direct breeding applications. The conservation of seed size regulatory mechanisms involving cell wall invertases and PEBP proteins between soybean and other plant species suggests that insights gained from this research could inform crop improvement efforts in related legumes and even more distantly associated crops. Comparative genomic analyses examining syntenic regions in other legume genomes (common bean, chickpea, cowpea) could identify orthologous genes and determine whether similar genetic architectures control seed size across the legume family. Additionally, integrating the QTLs identified here with information on seed composition traits (protein and oil content) would help develop ideotypes optimized for specific market segments—balancing seed size, composition, and yield potential based on end-use requirements. In conclusion, this study has significantly advanced the understanding of the genetic basis of hundred-seed weight in soybean through high-resolution QTL mapping. It identified a novel major-effect QTL on chromosome 19 and stable QTLs on chromosomes 6 and 7, along with molecular validation of promising candidate genes. These results establish a foundation for marker-assisted breeding and functional genomics strategies to enhance this key agronomic trait, ultimately boosting soybean productivity and quality in agricultural systems worldwide.

Conclusions

This study comprehensively mapped the genetic architecture of soybean seed weight using 325 recombinant inbred lines and high-density markers, identifying 11 QTLs across 8 chromosomes that collectively explain 62% of phenotypic variance and confirm polygenic inheritance. The major QTL qSW19-1 on chromosome 19 emerged as the most significant finding, explaining 8.59% of the variance and showing high environmental stability, making it particularly valuable for breeding applications. Molecular validation revealed four differentially expressed candidate genes, with cell wall invertase (Glyma.19G195400) showing the strongest correlation with seed weight (r = 0.68) and peak expression during seed filling, while transcription factors and developmental regulators suggest complex regulatory networks control this trait. With high heritability (68%) and 55% of QTLs showing environmental stability, these findings provide immediate practical tools for marker-assisted selection and genomics-assisted breeding to improve soybean seed weight and yield components in production systems.

Evaluation of efficacy and safety of low-intensity pulsed ultrasound in patients with concurrent erectile dysfunction and chronic prostatitis/chronic pelvic pain syndrome: a prospective, randomized controlled study
Authors: Zhongyi Li, Dongjie Li, Xiongbing Zu, Boyu Xiang, Guilin Wang, Zhengyan Tang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveThe primary objective of this clinical trial is to investigate the effect of low-intensity pulsed ultrasound (LIPUS) on patients suffering from comorbid erectile dysfunction (ED) and chronic prostatitis/chronic pelvic pain syndrome(CP/CPPS).MethodsThe clinical trial was conducted in the andrology outpatient treatment room of the Department of Urology, Xiangya Hospital, Central South University from August to November 2022 A total of 60 patients who met the research criteria for comorbid ED combined with CP/CPPS were recruited and randomly assigned to three treatment groups. They were treated with LIPUS (Group A), drug therapy(Group B), and LIPUS combined with drug therapy (Group C), respectively. Each group comprised 20 patients. Statistical analysis was performed on the five-item version of International Index of Erectile Function (IIEF-5), Erection Hardness Score (EHS), National Institutes of Health Chronic Prostatitis Symptom Index (NIH-CPSI), the nine-item Patient Health Questionnaire (PHQ-9), the seven-item Generalized Anxiety Disorder Scale (GAD-7), and the incidence of adverse events to comprehensively evaluate the efficacy and safety of LIPUS.ResultsThe positive response rate of ED and CP/CPPS treatment in Group A is 40%(8/20) and 45%(9/20), while those in Group B is 55%(11/20) and 60%(12/20), and those in Group C is 85%(17/20) and 85%(17/20). A notable increase in IIEF-5 scores was observed across the three groups post-treatment (10.45 ± 2.50 vs. 13.65 ± 3.03, P = 0.008; 11.80 ± 3.21 vs. 16.40 ± 3.20, P = 0.011; 12.90 ± 3.92 vs. 19.40 ± 2.35, P = 0.042) with a concomitant significant decrease in NIH-CPSI scores (16.75 ± 4.53 vs. 14.65 ± 4.51, P = 0.016; 16.35 ± 4.32 vs. 12.20 ± 4.74, P = 0.007; 16.00 ± 4.40 vs. 8.15 ± 4.28, P = 0.021). Notably, the most pronounced changes were seen in the group receiving LIPUS combined with tadalafil and doxazosin. Additionally, all groups exhibited marked improvements in anxiety and depression symptoms post-treatment. No adverse events were observed during treatment.ConclusionLIPUS can improve erectile function and CP/CPPS symptoms with good safety, and LIPUS combined with tadalafil and doxazosin is more effective during the treatment. However, its long-term efficacy remains to be seen.Trial RegistrationChinese Clinical Trial Registry; approval number: ChiCTR2200063038 (https://www.chictr.org.cn/) on August 29, 2022.Keywords: Low-intensity pulsed ultrasound, Erectile dysfunction, Chronic prostatitis/Chronic pelvic pain syndrome, Efficacy, Safety

Introduction

Chronic prostatitis (CP) and erectile dysfunction(ED) are common diseases in andrology. According to the classification by the National Institutes of Health (NIH), chronic prostatitis/chronic pelvic pain syndrome (CP/CPPS) accounts for more than 90% of chronic prostatitis [1]. The incidence of CP/CPPS among adult males is about 10%, and a notable trend towards a younger demographic has emerged in recent years [2, 3]. The prevalence of ED varied from 6 to 64%, depending on age, with an overall mean prevalence of 30% [4]. Clinically, a significant proportion of CP patients experience varying degrees of sexual dysfunction. There exists a close clinical association between CP and ED [5–7]. A study has reported a prevalence rate of approximately 62% for CP complicated with sexual dysfunction, with ED accounting for 29% of these cases [8].

Recently, micro-energy therapy has gained prominence. Low-intensity extracorporeal shockwave(Li-ESWT) is one type of micro-energy therapy that has been demonstrated to significantly improve erectile function and alleviate prostatitis symptoms in both animal studies, and clinical studies and has been widely applied in clinical treatment [9]. However, Low-Intensity Pulsed Ultrasound (LIPUS) therapy, another treatment modality within the realm of microenergy, relies on ultrasound media instead of shockwaves, and has garnered substantial attention owing to its profound potential, high efficiency, and non-invasive technical characteristics. LIPUS is mainly used to facilitate fracture healing, expedite soft tissue regeneration, and mitigate inflammatory reactions in the early stages [10–13]. In human clinical trials, the published data regarding the use of LIPUS for treating urological disorders is scarce. The majority of this data pertains to the treatment of ED [14], which consistently demonstrates the positive effects of LIPUS therapy [15, 16].

Currently, there are limited clinical studies on the use of the treatment of LIPUS for ED and CP/CPPS, particularly for CP/CPPS. The single-center, randomized, controlled clinical study was conducted to investigate the efficacy and safety of the LIPUS in the clinical treatment of ED and CP/CPPS.

Materials and methods

This is a prospective, randomized study of 60 patients with concurrent ED and CP/CPPS, which was registered in the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (https://www.chictr.org.cn/, registration number: ChiCTR2200063038) before recruiting patients.

Study population

Patients with ED and CP/CPPS (including IIIa and IIIb) admitted to Xiangya Hospital of Central South University from August 2022 to November 2022 were selected as the research subjects. The eligibility of patients for inclusion was judged by experienced andrologists. After signing the informed consent form, the patients were randomly allocated to Groups A, B, and C in a ratio of 1:1:1 using computer-generated random number tables. Both the study researchers and participants were blinded to the allocation process.

Inclusion criteria: ① Men aged between 18 and 65; ② Normal cardiac function or NYHA Grade I; ③ A score of ≤ 21 on the five-item version of the International Index of Erectile Function (IIEF-5) and ED symptoms for at least six months; ④ Diagnosed with CP/CPPS by andrologists according to the European Association of Urology (EAU) guidelines (based on NIH classification) for at least six months; ⑤ No treatment for ED and CP within four weeks before grouping;⑥ A stable sexual partner for more than three months; ⑦ Signed informed consent form.

Exclusion criteria: ①Bleeding tendency; ②Poor blood pressure control (systolic blood pressure ≥ 160mmHg and diastolic blood pressure ≥ 100mmHg) and poor blood sugar control, namely HbA1c ≥ 6.5%; ③Diseases that may hinder the successful completion of the study, such as recent myocardial infarction or stroke, congestive heart failure, severe arrhythmia, unstable angina pectoris, severe chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, cancer, serious systemic disease, and severe psychosis; ④Inability to take doxazosin and tadalafil for any reason; ⑤History of gastrointestinal obstruction, esophageal obstruction, or any degree of narrowing of the gastrointestinal tract; ⑥Participating in other clinical studies.

From August 2022 to November 2022, we screened 63 patients. Three patients (2 patients in Group C and 1 patient in Group A) discontinued their participation in the trial participation due to failing to take medicine as required and not attending LIPUS treatments regularly. A total of 60 participants (20 in each group) completed the procedure and all subsequent visits. The flowchart of present study is depicted in Fig. 1.

Treatment protocol

The treatment duration of the three treatment groups was four weeks. The patients in Group A were treated with LIPUS. The patients in Group B were treated with tadalafil and doxazosin, which are considered first-line treatments according to the EAU guidelines [17, 18]. The patients in Group C underwent a combined treatment of LIPUS with tadalafil and doxazosin. The investigators provided and instructed the patients on the administration of tadalafil (5 mg once daily) and doxazosin (4 mg once daily). The LIPUS treatment was administered twice a week, with a 3-day interval, for a total of eight treatments, and no anesthesia was administered during these procedures.

LIPUS instrument (Lanyue Medical Technology Co., Ltd, Hunan, China) comprises two probes, namely, probe 1001 and probe 1701. (Fig. 2) In the ED treatment mode, probe 1001 is specifically utilized to treat the both penile crus ( left and right) for 5 min per area, whereas the probe 1701 is employed to treat both corpus cavernosum ( left and right) for the same duration. The probe 1001 is used to simultaneously treat the perineum and pubic symphysis for 10 min in the CP treatment mode. The parameters of the probe 1001 are set as follows: the output power rating is 7.5 W, the effective radiating area is 6cm2, the frequency is 1.7 MHz, and the energy intensity is 2.5 W/cm2. The parameters of the probe 1701 are set as follows: the output powew rating is 15 W, the effective radiating area is 6 cm2, the frequency is 1.0 MHz, and the energy intensity is 1.25 W/cm2.

Patient management and outcome measures

Andrologists conduct a comprehensive medical history collection and physical examination for patients who meet the criteria and voluntarily participate in this trial, meticulously documenting the patient’s medical histories and physical characteristics. In addition, patients will be required to undergo basic hematological tests, including blood routine tests, blood glucose tests, liver and kidney function tests, among others. Imaging examinations will be performed on patients based on their individual conditions, enabling researchers to obtain a comprehensive understanding of the patients’ baseline conditions. Patients must complete a questionnaire prior to receiving the corresponding treatment. The questionnaire encompasses basic information such as age, height, weight, waistline, hipline, and so on, as well as medical history-related information such as the course of ED and CP/CPPS, IIEF-5, National Institutes of Health Chronic Prostatitis Symptom Index(NIH-CPSI), Erection Hardness Score (EHS), 9-items patient health questionnaire (PHQ-9), 7-items generalized anxiety disorder scale (GAD-7), past medical history, comorbidities of the patient and so forth. The patients’ questionnaires are subsequently verified by andrologists to ensure the accuracy of the data. .

After four weeks of treatment, all patients were followed up by andrologists, including undergoing aspecialist physical examination, assessing their IIEF-5 score, NIH-CPSI score, EHS, PHQ-9, GAD-7, recording adverse events(AEs), and monitoring for adverse reactions during treatment such as pain, local petechia or ecchymosis, hematuria, and other AEs.

The outcome measures encompassed the efficacy rates of various treatment modalities, alterations in the IIEF-5 scores, modifications in the NIH-CPSI scores, changes in the EHS scores, variations in the PHQ-9 scores, transformations in the GAD-7 scores, the number of AEs, and the quantity of adverse reactions encountered during the treatment period.

Statistical analysis

The sample size was calculated using the Power Analysis and Sample Size software program (PASS) version 15.0.5 for Windows (2017). It was calculated by 80% power to detect a decrease of 5 or more scores in the total NIH-CPSI score and an increase of 4 or more in the total IIEF-5 score based on previous studies [15, 19]. The sample size of each group was determined to be 20, allowing a 20% drop-out rate. Quantitative data were analyzed using T-test. Data that conformed to normal distribution are expressed as mean and standard deviation (‾x ± s), whereas those that did not conform to normal distribution were presented as a median. For the comparison of mean values between groups, either the LSD test or the non-parametric Kruskal-Wallis test was employed. The data were analyzed usingSPSS 26.0 (SPSS, Inc., Chicago, IL, USA) software and a p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Ethics statement

Ethical approval for the conduct of the study was granted by the Institutional Review Board (IRB) of Xiangya Hospital of Central South University (approval number: No. 202204087). This study was performed in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. Informed consent was approved by the IRB, and was obtained from each subject prior to the execution of any procedures.

The study protocol has been registered with the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (https://www.chictr.org.cn/, registration number: ChiCTR2200063038).

Response rates

Positive responses were defined as an increase in the IIEF-5 score by 4 points and a decrease in the NIH-CPSI score by 5 points. Response rates were calculated post-treatment. The positive response rate for ED treatment in patients of Group A is 40%(8/20), that of Group B is 55%(11/20), and that of Group C is 85%(17/20). Regarding CP/CPPS, the positive response rate was 45%(9/20) in Group A, 60% (12/20)in Group B, and 85%(17/20) in Group C.

Evaluation regarding erection improvement

There was no significant difference in IIEF-5 and EHS scores among the three groups before treatment (P > 0.05). After four weeks of treatment, the IIEF-5 and EHS scores of the three groups were significantly increased compared to pre-treatment level (P < 0.05). There were significant differences in post-treatment IIEF-5 scores among the groups. The IIEF-5 score of patients in Group C after treatment was significantly higher than those in Group A and B. Additionally, after treatment, there was no statistically significant differences between Group A and Group B, while Group C showed a statistically significant difference compared to the other two groups. Safety and tolerability

We did not see any serious adverse events of LIPUS during treatment, such as pain at the treatment site, local petechia or ecchymosis, hematuria, and other AEs.

Discussion

The first-line and second-line treatments for ED, including Phosphodiesterase Type 5 Inhibitors (PDE5i), cavernous injection, vacuum erectile device, and others, can only temporarily improve erectile function. They cannot fundamentally reverse the pathological changes of ED [15, 20]. CP/CPPS is mainly treated with 3-As therapy (antibiotics, anti-inflammatories, and α-blockers), yet up to 46% of patients respond poorly to this therapy [21, 22].The relatively mature application of micro-energy therapy in the field of urology is low-energy extracorporeal shock wave therapy. In 2008, Zimmerman et al. [23] initially showed the efficacy of Li-ESWT in CP/CPPS patients. The trial group showed significant improvements in pain, quality of life scores and voiding function compared to the placebo group. The therapeutic effect of Li-ESWT persisted for up to 12 weeks. Kim KS et al. [24, 25] conducted two successive prospective, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled studies. Both studies revealed that NIH-CPSI total and subdomain scores, IPSS, IIEF-5, and VAS in the Li-ESWT group were found compared to the placebo group. A multicenter, single-arm randomized controlled clinical trial conducted by Palmieri et al. [26] enrolled 106 individuals with vasculogenic ED who were non-responders to PDE5i. After three weeks of treatment, their IIEF-EF, peak systolic velocity, and end-diastolic velocity in penile arteries improved significantly.

However, there are a few clinical studies on LIPUS, and most of them have focused on its efficacy for ED. Cui et al. [15]reported a multi-center RCT study on LIPUS for the treatment of mild and moderate ED, and the effective rate of the treatment group was 67.5% in the 12th week, which was evaluated based on the increase of IIEF-5 score. Xia et al. [16] LIPUS at two different intervals is effective and safe for mild to moderate ED, and the regimen of three times per week can achieve a quite good effect in a relatively short duration. A large-sample, randomized, controlled, noninferiority trial study [27] reported that low-intensity pulsed ultrasound therapy administered thrice and twice per week showed equivalent therapeutic effects and safety for mild-to-moderate ED. Nonetheless, clinical research on the use of LIPUS in the treatment of chronic prostatitis (CP) is quite limited.

A total of 60 patients with ED combined with CP/CPPS were included and randomly divided into three groups for treatment. After four weeks of treatment, the IIEF-5, EHS, and NIH-CPSI in the three treatment groups, including the LIPUS-only treatment group, showed statistically significant difference and improvement. The NIH-CPSI score decreased the most, while the IIEF-5 score and EHS increased the most in patients treated with LIPUS combined with tadalafil and doxazosin. Psychological factors play a key role in the development of both ED and CP/CPPS. Psychological factors can be both risk factors for ED and CP/CPPS and in turn, the conditions of ED and CP/CPPS can affect the psychological state of patients [28]. Therefore, our study incorporated psychological indicators, which are not available in other studies. According to the results of this study, there was a significant improvement in anxiety as well as depressive status in all three groups after treatment compared to the baseline assessment. No significant differences were found between the groups. No AEs and adverse reactions were observed in any of the patients during the treatment, which indicates that LIPUS therapy is safe.

This study is the first prospective clinical study to treat patients with ED combined with CP/CPPS using LIPUS, which is a preliminary exploration of its efficacy. However, this study also has limitations, including being a single center with an insufficiently large sample size. Moreover, the study lacks a relatively long-term follow-up on the treatment effectiveness. Clinical trials with longer follow-up periods and larger sample sizes are needed to verify the effectiveness and safety of LIPUS in treating ED and CP/CPPS. Our intention was to explore the therapeutic effect of LIPUS on the diseased population as a whole. W didn’t perform a stratified analysis of patient population and disease. Age and other factors may be confounding variables, influencing the results. The extensive matching process could potentially lead to selection bias.

The cost of LIPUS treatment is higher than traditional drug therapy. Based on existing research articles and the results of this trial, its efficacy is considerable, and its cost-benefit ratio is relatively favorable. Better clinical treatment plans rely on more high-quality clinical trials. The combined treatment plan of LIPUS and drugs is expected to bring enhanced therapeutic effects.

ED and CP/CPPS are often combined. Due to the limitations of traditional drug therapy, patients need emerging effective and safe treatment options, either as monotherapy or in combination with drug therapy. LIPUS treatment is still in its developmental stage, and its integration with traditional treatments represents a novel treatment direction. Andrologists must also consult more high-quality literature and clinical guidelines when devising personalized LIPUS treatment plans [29].

Conclusion

LIPUS, an emerging treatment, can significantly improve erectile function and CP/CPPS symptoms with good safety profiles during the treatment. However, its long-term efficacy remains uncertain and requires further investigation. Research shows that the combination of LIPUS with tadalafil and doxazosin was more beneficial to patients’ ED and CP/CPPS symptoms, which could provide a reference for clinical treatment strategies. Further studies of high quality are needed to confirm the efficacy and safety of LIPUS.

Environmental health literacy levels of nursing students
Authors: Seda Cevheroğlu, Hülya Fırat Kılıç
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundIn the contemporary era, the role of nurses in safeguarding and advancing environmental and human health has assumed heightened significance, particularly in light of the deteriorating environmental conditions. The objective of this descriptive study was to ascertain the extent of environmental health literacy among student nurses.MethodsThe study was conducted with 299 undergraduate nursing students from a foundation university in Northern Cyprus during the spring semester of the 2023–2024 academic year. The data collection instrument included the Descriptive Information Form and the Environmental Health Literacy Scale (EHLS).ResultsThe mean EHLS score for the nursing students was found to be 80.47 ± 22.96. The mean scores obtained from the knowledge, awareness of health effects, perception of environmental exposure, and behavior dimensions of the EHLS were 23.87 ± 7.49, 19.83 ± 6.27, 9.27 ± 4.83, and 27.50 ± 8.95, respectively. A positive and statistically significant correlation was observed between the scores obtained from the EHLS and its dimensions of knowledge, awareness of health effects, perception of environmental exposure and behavior. The independent variables that significantly contributed to an increase in the EHLS scores were residing with friends, always or sometimes warning people who pollute environment, holding the belief that nurses should be sensitive to the environmental issues and being a third-year nursing student.ConclusionThe study revealed that the environmental health literacy levels of the participants were above the moderate level. Based on these findings, it can be proposed that compulsory environment-related courses be incorporated into the nursing curriculum. Furthermore, active learning methodologies should be employed, and existing environment-related courses should be revised to encompass national and global environmental issues, environmental risks, and their potential consequences. Further studies can be conducted with the aim of enhancing environmental health literacy.

Background

Environmental health literacy (EHL) encompasses both the knowledge of the health effects of environmental exposures and the capacity to safeguard health from environmental risks. Additionally, it comprises a set of knowledge, attitudes, and behaviors that shape how individuals utilize environmental information to make health-related decisions [1, 2]. In this context, the objective of EHL is to prevent environmentally induced diseases and to empower individuals, families, and communities to protect themselves by understanding the environmental risks that affect them [3, 4].

Florence Nightingale, the founder of modern nursing, who was the first to demonstrate the importance of the environment in terms of health, emphasized that a healthy environment is essential for healing [5]. Similarly, the World Health Organization (WHO) underlines the need for clean air, stable climate, adequate water, sanitation and hygiene, safe use of chemicals, protection from radiation, healthy and safe workplaces, sound agricultural practices, health-supportive cities and built environments, and a preserved nature for protection from environmental problems [6]. Nevertheless, it is evident that a considerable number of deaths result from environmental factors. According to WHO, approximately 13.7 million deaths per year, or 24% of global deaths, are attributable to preventable environmental problems [7, 8]. The top fifteen environmental problems include global warming from fossil fuels, poor governance, food waste, biodiversity loss, plastic pollution, deforestation, air pollution, melting ice caps and sea level rise, ocean acidification, excessive agricultural production, food and water insecurity, fast fashion and textile waste, overfishing, cobalt mining and soil degradation [9]. These environmental problems have a multitude of adverse effects on human health, including mental disorders, heart failure, chronic kidney disease, asthma, allergies, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, cancer, myocardial infarction, and stroke [10–12]. Additionally, there is a growing body of evidence that links poor health outcomes with low literacy [13, 14].

The concept of health literacy entails “people’s knowledge, motivation and competencies to access, understand, appraise and apply health information in order to make judgements and take decisions in every-day life concerning health care, disease prevention and health promotion to maintain or improve quality of life during the life course [13]. In order to enhance the level of health literacy in the domain of environmental health, the extant literature recommends the assessment of the existing EHL levels within a community [3, 15]. The findings from these assessments can be employed to augment the understanding of environmental health hazards, diminish, mitigate, or eradicate environmental exposures, and propel the advancement of the well-being of both individuals and the environment. The findings of such studies can be utilized to prevent diseases induced by the environment and to comprehend and regulate environmental risks affecting individuals, families, and society [4]. Health professionals play an active role in ensuring the implementation of these controls and in enhancing the level of EHL. Among health professionals, nurses occupy a pivotal position in influencing behavioral patterns and are regarded as the most trusted professional group [4, 16]. In the contemporary era, the role of nurses in safeguarding and advancing environmental and human health has assumed heightened significance, particularly in light of the deteriorating environmental conditions [8].

Nurses play a pivotal role in the dissemination of health information to the intended audience in an accurate, effective, and timely manner with appropriate methodology [17]. Given their role at the vanguard of the creation and safeguarding of a healthy and secure environment, it is anticipated that nurses will be able to identify and address environmental concerns with the expertise and abilities they have developed through their training [18, 19]. However, an analysis of the literature reveals inadequate levels of environmental knowledge and health literacy among nursing students [20–25]. A review of the literature revealed a lack of studies examining the EHL of nursing students. In light of this gap, this study was designed to assess the EHL levels of nursing students. The findings of this study may be used to identify any deficiencies in the knowledge of student nurses regarding environmental health, to complete these gaps in identifying and managing environmental problems through educational activities, and to improve awareness of EHL.

Method

Aim and research design

The objective of this descriptive study was to ascertain the extent of environmental health literacy among student nurses.

Population and sampling

The study population consisted of a total of 450 first-, second-, third-, and fourth-year students enrolled in the Nursing Department of a foundation university during the spring semester of the 2023–2024 academic year. The minimum sample size was estimated using the G*Power software, assuming a 95% confidence level and a 5% margin of error, yielding a required minimum of 269 participants.The study was concluded with the participation of 299 voluntary students.

Data collection tools

The data were collected using a descriptive information form and the Environmental Health Literacy Scale (EHLS).

Descriptive information form

The form was developed by the researchers in accordance with the literature review and included 14 questions on age, gender, year of education, income level, type of residence, smoking status and their interest in environment [5, 20].

Environmental health literacy scale (EHLS)

The EHLS, developed by Toplu and Sayılı (2023), is a 23-item instrument comprising four dimensions: knowledge, awareness of health effects, perception of environmental exposure, and behavior. The items are scored on a 5-point Likert scale, with a potential range of 23 to 115. A higher score indicates a higher level of EHL [15]. The Cronbach’s alpha for the original scale was 0.854, while our study yielded a value of 0.968.

Procedures

The data were collected via face-to-face interviews. Prior to commencing, the students were informed about the aim of the study and that confidentiality would be strictly maintained. Following the provision of informed consent, voluntary participants were requested to complete the data collection tools. The entire process took approximately 15 min.

Ethical considerations

Ethical approval was obtained from the Scientific Research and Publication Ethics Committee of Eastern Mediterranean University (No. ETK00-2024-0123). Voluntary informed consent was obtained from all participants, the study was conducted in accordance with the principles set forth in the Helsinki Declaration.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using Statistical Package for the Social Sciences software version 27.0 (IBM SPSS Corp.; Armonk, NY, USA). The skewness and kurtosis coefficients were calculated to ascertain whether the scores obtained from each continuous variable were normally distributed. An independent sample t-test was performed to compare continuous variables between two groups. A one-way variance (ANOVA) test was performed to compare more than two groups. A Scheffe post-hoc test was used to ascertain the source of observed differences. A Pearson’s correlation test was performed to analyze the relationship between two continuous variables. Multivariate linear regression analysis was utilized to determine the effect of independent variables on the dependent variable (EHL). Statistical significance was set at p < 0.05.

Descriptive characteristics

The mean age of the participants was 21.62 ± 2.54 years (range: 18–37). The number of first-, second-, third-, and fourth-year students was 79, 97, 63, and 60, respectively. Furthermore, 61% of the participants had an income equal to their expenses, 64% resided in dormitories and 44% reported smoking. With regard to their interest in environmental issues, 29% of the participants expressed that they are always interested in environmental issues, 21% identified as environmental activists, 39% expressed that they always warned people polluting environment, 20% reported having received an environmental course during their undergraduate studies, and 91% expressed the view that nurses should be sensitive to environmental issues (Table 1).

Comparison of the EHLS scores according to descriptive characteristics

Table 3 presents a comparison of the EHLS scores according to the descriptive characteristics of the participants. A positive and statistically significant correlation was observed between age and the scores obtained from the behavior dimension of the EHLS (r = 0.124; p = 0.033). The female participants exhibited higher scores than their male counterparts on the knowledge dimension of the EHLS (t = 2.097; p = 0.038). We also found a statistically significant relationship between the year of education and the scores obtained from the EHLS (F = 2.850; p = 0.038), and its dimensions of knowledge (F = 2.949; p = 0.033), awareness of health effects (F = 3.081; p = 0.028) and behavior (F = 2.685; p = 0.047). The statistical analysis demonstrated that third-year nursing students exhibited statistically significantly higher scores than first-year students. The findings also indicated that the students, who resided with their families, obtained higher scores from the EHLS (F = 3.267; p = 0.040), and its dimensions of knowledge (F = 4.925; p = 0.008) and awareness of health effects (F = 4.823; p = 0.009). Furthermore, the study revealed that participants who did not smoke exhibited statistically significantly higher scores from the behavior dimension of the EHLS (t = 2.247; p = 0.023). Additionally, the students, who rarely warned people polluting environment obtained lower scores from the EHLS (F = 5.893; p = 0.003), and its dimensions of knowledge (F = 3.390; p = 0.035), awareness of health effects (F = 3.781; p = 0.024) and behavior (F = 8.512; p < 0.001). Finally, the students, who believed that nurses should be sensitive to environmental issues exhibited higher scores on the EHLS (t = 2.313; p = 0.027), and its dimensions of knowledge (t = 2.572; p = 0.015) and awareness of health effects (t = 2.624; p = 0.013).

Independent variables affecting EHL

The linear regression model on the independent variables affecting the level of EHL was found to be statistically significant (F (8−290) = 3.76; p < 0.001; R2 = 0.094). The model indicates that residing with friends (β = 0.29; t = 2.31; p = 0.022), always (β = 0.54; t = 3.08; p = 0.002) or sometimes (β = 0.46; t = 2.65; p = 0.008) warning people who pollute environment, holding the belief that nurses should be sensitive to the environmental issues (β = 0.44; t = 2.26; p = 0.025) and being a third-year nursing student (β = 0.35; t = 2.05; p = 0.041) were the independent variables increasing the level of EHL (Table 4).

Discussion

This study is the inaugural investigation to elucidate the EHL level of nursing students. Furthermore, the variables influencing the EHL level of nursing students were identified through regression modeling.

The primary responsibility of nurses is to assist individuals in maintaining optimal health and well-being [26]. It is of paramount importance to ascertain the level of EHL among nursing students, as they will be working in a multitude of settings and will play an instrumental role in shaping and implementing policies and measures to promote health after graduation [4, 27]. The present study found that the EHLS scores of the participants were found to be above average.

The present study revealed that the participants exhibited EHLS scores that were higher than those indicative of moderate levels. The Public Health Education Association has defined EHL as a synthesis of environmental literacy and health literacy. Since no studies have been conducted to determine the level of EHL among nursing students so far, we will compare the findings of the present study with the findings of the studies on health literacy and environmental literacy among nursing students. The analysis of these studies reveals conflicting findings. While the studies of İncesu and Altıner-Yaş (2024), Cengiz and Bahar (2023), and Dağal and Fırat Kılıç (2023) report high levels of environmental literacy among nursing students, the studies of Kapan and Gürel (2022) and Örs (2022) revealed moderate levels environmental literacy [20, 27–30]. On the other hand, the level of health literacy among the students were reported to be either at high [31], moderate [29, 32, 33] or at low levels [21–25, 33]. The discrepancy in levels of environmental and health literacy observed in the literature may be attributed to variations in nursing curricula and the individual characteristics of students.

The study demonstrated a positive correlation between the scores obtained from the EHLS and its four dimensions of knowledge, awareness of health effects, perception of environmental exposure and behavior. Third-year students, who resided with their friends, always warned people polluting environment and believed that nurses should be sensitive to environmental issues obtained higher scores from the EHLS. While older students who did not smoke exhibited higher scores on the behavior dimension of the EHLS, female participants demonstrated higher scores on the knowledge dimension of the EHLS. Third-year students, who always warned people polluting environment obtained higher scores on the knowledge, awareness of health effects and behavior subscales of the EHLS. Those who resided with their friends and held the view that nurses should be sensitive to environmental issues demonstrated higher scores in the knowledge and awareness of health effects dimensions of the EHLS (Table 3). Previous studies by Dağal and Fırat Kılıç (2023), Örs (2022), and Kapan and Yardımcı Gürel (2022) reported that female nursing students exhibited higher environmental literacy levels than their male counterparts [20, 29, 30]. Ramon-Arbues et al. (2023) identified several factors associated with lower health literacy levels among nursing students, including female gender, younger age, living alone, smoking, and excessive screen time [21]. Kapan and Yardımcı Gürel (2022) discovered that participation in environmental education programs, involvement in environmental projects, discussion of environmental issues within the family, acquisition of information from instructors, and membership in non-governmental organizations (NGOs) related to the environment were associated with enhanced environmental literacy among nursing students [30]. In a study by Ayaz-Alkaya and Terzi (2019), fourth-year nursing students exhibited higher health literacy levels than first-year students [34].

The regression analysis revealed that the independent variables that significantly contributed to an increase in the EHLS scores were residing with friends, always or sometimes warning people who pollute environment, holding the belief that nurses should be sensitive to the environmental issues and being a third-year nursing student (Table 4). These results are consistent with literature [21, 29, 30, 34]. The observed increase in EHL among third-year students may be attributed to the curriculum’s greater depth of coverage on environmental issues in the public health course taken during this period. It is thus recommended that courses related to environmental health be incorporated into the curriculum on a regular basis, with the aim of enhancing students’ awareness. Furthermore, it is essential to foster an increased sense of environmental awareness and sensitivity by providing support for environmental organization activities and encouraging active participation of students in environmental activities within the university.

Limitations

The study had two main limitations. Primarily, the findings are discussed in conjunction with the results of the existing literature on environmental literacy and health literacy, due to the limited number of studies on EHL in the existing literature. Secondly, the study was conducted in a single university setting, which may limit the generalizability of the findings to other contexts.

Conclusions and recommendations

The study, which was designed to ascertain the EHL level of student nurses, revealed that the EHL levels of the participants were above the moderate level. Moreover, a positive correlation was identified between the scores obtained from the EHLS and its four dimensions: knowledge, awareness of health effects, perception of environmental exposure, and behavior. The independent variables that were found to significantly contribute to an increase in the EHLS scores were residing with friends, always or sometimes warning people who pollute environment, holding the belief that nurses should be sensitive to the environmental issues and being a third-year nursing student. In light of these findings, it is recommended that compulsory environment-related courses be incorporated into the educational curriculum of future nurses, who will play a pivotal role in raising public awareness of environmental issues. Moreover, active learning methodologies should be employed, and existing environment-related courses should be revised to encompass national and global environmental issues, environmental risks, and their potential consequences. Nursing students should be encouraged to participate in environmental activities, and the knowledge, attitudes, and skills of student nurses with regard to environmental health should be evaluated on a regular basis. Finally, further studies can be conducted with the aim of enhancing EHL.

CircRSPRY1 regulates calcium oxalate crystal-induced necrotic apoptosis in renal tubules via miR-21-5p/PTEN
Authors: Xudong Shen, Xike Mao, Hu Liang, Bingbing Hou, Yuexian Xu, Zhenyu Song, Yang Chen, Wei Wang, Zongyao Hao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundCircular RNAs (circRNAs) constitute a recently discovered class of evolutionarily conserved and robust regulatory RNAs. Their potential as diagnostic biomarkers and therapeutic targets for various diseases is a subject of growing interest. Nevertheless, the precise regulatory roles and underlying mechanisms of circRNAs in the context of kidney stone formation remain largely unexplored.MethodsUtilizing RNA high-throughput sequencing technology and quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR), we conducted a comprehensive screen for calcium oxalate crystals (CaOx)-induced differential expression of circular RNA (circRSPRY1) in the human renal tubular epithelial cells (HK-2) kidney injury model. Subsequently, we employed a combination of RNA and protein expression analyses, luciferase activity assays, and immunohistochemistry (IHC) to elucidate the regulatory role of circRSPRY1 in targeting the miR-21-5p/PTEN axis, influencing the processes of necroptosis and apoptogenesis.ResultsIn this investigation, we observed a notable downregulation of circRSPRY1 expression in both the CaOx-stimulated HK-2 renal tubular epithelial cell injury model and a glyoxalate-induced mouse model of renal calcinosis. Notably, overexpressing circRSPRY1 led to reduced crystalline deposition in our in vitro model. Mechanistically, circRSPRY1 overexpression was associated with the downregulation of cleaved-caspase-8, p-MLKL, P-RIPK1, and P-PIPK3 levels, consequently inhibiting necrotic apoptosis. Furthermore, circRSPRY1 appeared to act as a miR-21-5p sponge, resulting in reduced miR-21-5p availability for PTEN binding and, subsequently, increased PTEN expression, thus impeding the progression of necrotic apoptosis and kidney injury. Additionally, inhibiting miR-21-5p levels demonstrated the ability to suppress necrotic apoptosis in renal calcinosis by downregulating the PTEN/MLKL pathway.ConclusionOur findings suggest that circRSPRY1 represents a novel candidate circRNA implicated in the pathogenesis of kidney stones. circRSPRY1 acts as a sponge, sequestering miR-21-5p to modulate PTEN expression, thereby regulating necrotic apoptosis and contributing to the formation of calcium oxalate stones.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13578-026-01549-4.

Introduction

Kidney stones are a prevalent urological condition that, in severe cases, can lead to urinary tract obstruction and detrimentally impact renal function [1, 2]. The global incidence of kidney stones is on the rise, with more than 9% of the world's population expected to experience kidney stone disease during their lifetime [2, 3]. Despite prompt treatment, the recurrence rate of kidney stones remains significant, exceeding 10% within one year and potentially escalating to 50% over 5 to 10 years [4]. This imposes substantial socioeconomic and medical burdens [5]. Consequently, the exploration of new therapeutic targets holds both theoretical importance and practical value for the prevention and treatment of kidney stones.

The primary composition of kidney stones consists of calcium oxalate (CaOx) crystals, accounting for approximately 80% of all kidney stones [6]. CaOx primarily inflicts renal damage through mechanisms such as apoptosis, autophagy, inflammation, and fibrosis [7–10]. Recent investigations have revealed that CaOx can incite intrarenal inflammation and necrotic apoptosis in renal tubular epithelial cells (TECs), fostering the adherence of CaOx crystals to tubular epithelium and promoting kidney stone formation [6, 11, 12]. However, the specific upstream regulatory pathways involved in these processes remain elusive. A deeper comprehension of the mechanisms governing necrotic apoptosis-induced injury in renal tubular epithelial cells could represent a pivotal step in the quest for preventive and therapeutic targets.

Recent research has underscored the substantial role of circular RNA (circRNA) in influencing various diseases by modulating an array of target genes. Multiple studies have identified circRNAs as molecular sponges for miRNAs, thereby contributing to renal tubular epithelial cell injury caused by different factors [13, 14]. Notably, conducted high-throughput gene sequencing in a glycol-induced rat kidney stone model, revealing abnormal expression of 145 circRNAs implicated in cellular functions [15]. These findings collectively suggest that circRNAs may hold significant relevance in the pathogenesis of kidney stones.

In our prior research, we conducted non-transcriptome sequencing on human HK-2 cells exposed to calcium oxalate monohydrate. Among the differentially expressed circRNAs, circRSPRY1 emerged as the most significantly altered candidate. Bioinformatic analyses suggested its potential involvement in miRNA regulation through the competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA) mechanism, thereby highlighting circRSPRY1 as a plausible key player in the context of CaOx crystal-induced injury to renal tubular epithelial cells. The objective of this study was to delve into the functional role and molecular mechanisms of circRSPRY1, both in vitro using a calcium oxalate-induced HK-2 cell model and in vivo employing a glyoxalate-induced mouse model.

Materials and methods

Cell culture and calcium oxalate (CaOx) crystal preparation

The human proximal renal tubular epithelial cell line, HK-2, was obtained from the Nanjing Institute of Biological Sciences, Chinese Academy of Sciences. The cells were maintained in a controlled incubator with 5% CO2 at 37 °C. The preparation and characterization of CaOx crystals were conducted following established protocols as previously described in our group's prior research.The final CaOx concentration used is referenced to the MTT results (Supplementary Figure S1).

Cell transfection and generation of stable cell lines

Lentiviral circRSPRY1 and negative control vectors, along with chemically synthesized miRNA oligonucleotides (miR-21-5p mimic, miR-21-5p inhibitor, and negative control), were procured from Genomeditech (Shanghai) Co. To establish a cell line overexpressing circRSPRY1, HK-2 cells were subjected to puromycin selection at a concentration of 10 μg/mL and cultured for a duration of 2 weeks. For miR-21-5p modulation, 50 nM of miR-21-5p mimic or 50 nM of miR-21-5p inhibitor was introduced into 200 µL of DMEM-F12 medium, which was devoid of antibiotics, and supplemented with 5 µL of Lipofectamine 3000 (Invitrogen, CA, USA) (Supplementary Figure S2). The resulting mixture was allowed to incubate for 15 min before being added to the cells for further incubation.

Animal procedures

All experimental procedures followed the rules of the NIH Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals. C57BL/6 J male mice (6–8 weeks old) were used in the experiments. To establish the CaOx renal calcinosis model, each C57BL/6J male mouse received daily intraperitoneal injections (saline) or glyoxalate (GA) (75 mg/kg, 200 µl) for one week.After 1 week, the mice were necropsied and dissected for further evaluation. In the circRSPRY1 intervention and negative control groups, 5% pentobarbital was injected intraperitoneally to achieve general anesthesia, and a thermostat was used to maintain body temperature at 36.5 °C. Dorsal mouse hair was clipped, sterilized with iodine povidone-iodine, and a paraspinal incision was made under the costal arches, approximately 0.5 cm long, and sequentially incised through the skin and subcutis, with the muscularis propaeus bluntly spread open, the adipose tissues around the kidneys were free, and the kidneys were gently singled out with curved toothless forceps to fully reveal the kidneys, and the kidneys were removed by a blunt forceps with the help of a small piece of paper. To fully expose the kidneys, 100 μl of AAV9 empty vector or AAV9-circRSPRY1 was slowly injected in situ with a 31-gauge needle into both kidneys through the renal parenchyma at multiple points to reset the kidneys, and after observing that there was no obvious active hemorrhage, the muscularis propria and the skin were closed sequentially with 1/0 silk suture. Four weeks after in situ kidney injection, RT-PCR verified that circRSPRY1 was overexpressed in mouse kidneys. To assess the effect of miR-21-5p, mice were injected intraperitoneally with miR-21-5p antagomir or agomir and negative control at a dose of 80 mg/kg (200 µL) on days 1–5. All animal experiments were approved by the Ethics Committee of Anhui Medical University (Ethics No.LLSC20232081).

Polarized light microscopy and PAS staining

Renal paraffin sections were subjected to dewaxing and subsequently stained with hematoxylin and eosin using established experimental protocols. The resulting renal crystal deposits were visualized using a polarized light microscope (Zeiss, Germany).

Renal paraffin sections were deparaffinized and subsequently subjected to PAS staining to evaluate the degree of tubular damage.

Immunohistochemistry

Mouse kidney tissue sections were prepared for histological analysis by following established procedures for paraffin embedding. This included fixation with 4% paraformaldehyde, tissue dehydration, embedding in paraffin, and sectioning to create 4 μm thick slices. Immunohistochemical staining was performed using a standard kit (Solarbio, Beijing, China). The process involved initial antigen retrieval to block endogenous peroxidase activity in the dewaxed kidney sections. Subsequently, sections were incubated with specific antibodies at 4 °C overnight and then subjected to DAB staining. Image acquisition was carried out using an inverted fluorescence microscope (Olympus IX83, Japan).

RNA extraction and real-time fluorescence quantitative PCR

Total RNA, totaling one microgram, was extracted from cellular and tissue samples, followed by reverse transcription using the PrimeScript First Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (TaKaRa Bio, Dalian, China), in strict accordance with the manufacturer's instructions. The resulting cDNA was then combined with ABI SYBR Green Master Mix (Applied Biosystems, Carlsbad, CA, USA), and the mixture underwent amplification using an ABI 7500 Real-Time PCR System (Applied Biosystems), following the manufacturer’s guidelines. Each sample underwent three replicate measurements and was analyzed using dedicated relative quantification software (Applied Biosystems). Protein blotting

Calcium Oxalate Monohydrate (COM) crystals were co-cultured with HK-2 cells at a concentration of 125 µg/ml. After 48 h, total cellular or tissue proteins were extracted using RIPA buffer (Abbysine, China). The protein concentration was quantified, and loading amounts were determined utilizing a BCA kit. Protein samples were denatured by boiling for 10 min after the addition of an appropriate loading buffer.Subsequently, a gel was prepared, and protein samples were loaded and separated by electrophoresis. The separated proteins were then transferred onto a membrane, which was subsequently incubated with primary antibodies, including MLKL (66675-1-Ig, 1:1000, Proteintech, China), RIPK1 (17519-1-AP, 1:1000, Proteintech, China), P-RIPK1(AF2398, 1:1000, Affinity, China), P-RIPK3(AF7443, 1:1000, Affinity, China), RIPK3 (17563-1-AP, 1:1000, Proteintech, China), Caspase 8 (13423-1-AP, 1:1000, Proteintech, China), P-MLKL (AF7420, 1:1000, Proteintech, China), PTEN (AF5447, 1:1000, Affinity, China), Actin (ET1701-80, 1:5000, HUABIO, China), Caspase-3 (A19654, 1:1000, ABclonal, China), Bax (A19684, 1:1000, ABclonal, China), and Beclin 1 (R1510-8, 1:1000, HUABIO, China). The incubation with primary antibodies was performed at 4 °C overnight.The membrane was subsequently washed and incubated with secondary antibodies for 1 h at room temperature. Protein bands on the blot were visualized using an ECL emitting solution. Semi-quantitative analysis was conducted using Image J software.

RNase R treatment

Two micrograms of total RNA underwent treatment with 5 U/μg of RNase R (Epicentre Technologies, Madison, Wisconsin, USA) at 37 °C for 15 min. Subsequently, the RNA expression levels of circRSPRY1 and RSPRY1 were assessed using quantitative reverse transcription-polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR).

RNA stability assay

HK-2 cells were seeded into six-well plates, treated with 5 μg/ml actinomycin D, and harvested at specified time intervals. The stability of both circRSPRY1 and RSPRY1 was assessed through quantitative reverse transcription-polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR).

Dual luciferase reporter gene assay

We conducted a computational analysis using bioinformatics tools, including Targetscan, and starBase, to predict the binding interactions between circRSPRY1 and miR-21-5p. Existing literature supports the notion that miR-21 can target PTEN, leading to necroptosis [16]. In light of these findings, we proceeded to generate wild-type (circ_0039550 WT) and mutant (circ_0039550 MT) reporter vectors for circRSPRY1, which were subsequently integrated into the psiCHECK-2 vector (Promega, Madison, Wisconsin, USA). In a parallel fashion, we also created wild-type (PTEN WT) and mutant (PTEN MT) reporter vectors for PTEN, which were incorporated into psiCHECK-2 (Promega). Subsequently, we introduced 100 nmol of the reporter plasmids (circ_0039550 WT, circ_0039550 MT) into HK-2 cells in combination with miR-21-5p or negative control. For the PTEN reporter plasmids (PTEN WT, PTEN MT), we transfected HK-2 cells with miR-21-5p mimics, inhibitors, or negative controls. Finally, we quantified luciferase activity using the Dual Luciferase Reporter System Kit (Promega).

RNA-binding protein immunoprecipitation (RIP)

RNA immunoprecipitation (RIP) assays were conducted following the guidelines outlined in the Magna RIP RNA-binding protein immunoprecipitation kit (Millipore, Bedford, MA, USA). Cell lysates were subjected to immunoprecipitation using Ago2 antibody or IgG as controls. Subsequently, the presence of circRSPRY1 and miR-21-5p in the immunoprecipitates was assessed using real-time PCR.

RNA pull down assay

HK-2 cells (1.5 × 107) were harvested and subjected to lysis. A biotinylated miR-21-5p probe, synthesized by Genepharm (Shanghai, China), was utilized for interaction with streptavidin agarose beads (Thermo Scientific). The cell lysates were then incubated with either the miR-21-5p probe or an oligonucleotide probe at 4 °C overnight. RNA complexes bound to the beads were subsequently eluted using a washing buffer, and the enrichment of circRSPRY1 pulled down by the miR-21-5p probe was quantified using qRT-PCR.

FISH

CircRSPRY1 probes (CAATGGCAGTTAGAGATTGTCTA) and mir-21-5p probes (TCAACATCAGTCTGATAAGCTA) were designed and synthesized by GenePharma (Suzhou, China). Fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) assays were performed using an RNA FISH Kit (GenePharma, Suzhou, China) following the manufacturer’s instructions. Briefly, HK2 cells were seeded into cell culture dishes (Biosharp, Hefei, China) and cultured until reaching approximately 30–50% confluence. After washing with PBS, the cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 15 min at room temperature, permeabilized with PBS containing 0.5% Triton X-100 for 15 min, and subsequently blocked with 1 × blocking solution for 30 min at 37 °C. The cells were then hybridized with the Cy5-labeled circRSPRY1 probe at 37 °C for 12–16 h in a humidified chamber. Following hybridization, nuclei were counterstained with DAPI for 15 min. Fluorescent signals were visualized and captured using a fluorescence microscope (Zeiss, Oberkochen, Germany).

Immunofluorescence (IF) analysis

HK-2 cells were seeded onto cell culture dishes (Biosharp, Hefei, China) and grown to ~ 30–50% confluence. Cells were rinsed with PBS, fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for 30 min, and permeabilized with 0.5% Triton X-100 in PBS for 15 min at room temperature. After blocking with 3% BSA for 30 min, cells were incubated with the indicated primary antibody at 4 °C overnight. The next day, cells were washed and incubated with an FITC-conjugated secondary antibody (Beyotime, Shanghai, China) for 1 h at room temperature, followed by nuclear counterstaining with DAPI for 15 min. Fluorescence images were acquired using a Zeiss fluorescence microscope (Oberkochen, Germany).

Statistical analysis

All data are presented as mean ± SD unless otherwise stated. For each experiment, independent biological replicates (n) are reported in the figure legends; technical replicates were averaged within each biological sample and not counted as n. Quantitative immunoblots were normalized to loading controls (e.g., β-actin) and then to the mean of the control group before analysis. For immunofluorescence (IF) and immunohistochemistry (IHC), quantification used mean fluorescence intensity or positive area fraction within predefined ROIs, with blinded scoring for histology (tubular-injury indices) and polarized-light crystal burden (area fraction by thresholding).

Normality was assessed by Shapiro–Wilk and homoscedasticity by Levene’s tests. For two-group comparisons, we used two-tailed Student’s t-test (or Welch’s t-test if variances were unequal). For ≥ 3 groups, we applied one-way ANOVA test when assumptions were met; otherwise, Kruskal–Wallis test. For designs with factors over time or treatment × RNA species (e.g., RNase R/actinomycin D assays), we used two-way ANOVA test. Dual-luciferase assays comparing WT vs MT under the same mimic/inhibitor were analyzed by t-test; when ≥ 3 conditions were compared (e.g., WT + NC, WT + miR-21-5p mimic, MT + miR-21-5p mimic), we used one-way ANOVA. Where multiple planned comparisons were performed within a panel, family-wise error was controlled by the chosen post-hoc procedure; for large comparison sets, Benjamini–Hochberg FDR was used as indicated in legends. Outliers were handled by predefined technical criteria (e.g., membrane artifacts, imaging saturation) and not by post-hoc deletion; any excluded datasets and reasons are stated in the legends if applicable.

Unless otherwise specified, tests were two-sided with α = 0.05. Significance is denoted as ns (P ≥ 0.05), *P < 0.05, **P < 0.01, ***P < 0.001, ****P < 0.0001. Analyses were performed in GraphPad Prism (v9/10) and R (v4.x); image quantification used ImageJ/Fiji (v1.5x). Exact tests and n for each subplot are specified in the figure legends in accordance with the reviewer’s request.

Result

Next-generation sequencing identifies reduced circRSPRY1 expression in renal calcinosis

To identify dysregulated non-coding RNAs (ncRNAs) in human kidney stones, we conducted non-transcriptome sequencing using HK-2 cells exposed to calcium oxalate (CaOx) crystals, as illustrated in Fig. 1A–C. Among the top 20 ncRNAs exhibiting the most significant expression differences, 13 were known long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) and circular RNAs (circRNAs). RT-PCR validation indicated that circ_0039549 was among the most differentially expressed circRNAs. Further investigation using the circBase database (http://www.circbase.org/) revealed that circ_0039549 originated from an exon of RSPRY1, although its homologous counterpart was absent in mice. In contrast, circ_0039550, also derived from an exon of RSPRY1, was found to be expressed in mice and had a corresponding homologous circRNA (mmu_circ_0015125) [17]. We subsequently validated circ_0039550 as a target circRNA and designated it as circRSPRY1. Involvement of circRSPRY1 in the regulation of necrotic apoptosis in renal calcinosis

To investigate the impact of circRSPRY1 on renal tubular epithelial cell injury induced by renal calcium sedimentation disorder, we introduced CaOx crystals in a co-culture model utilizing a stably transfected cell line expressing circRSPRY1 in HK-2 cells (as depicted in Supplementary S2). Additionally, we overexpressed circRSPRY1 via in situ kidney injection in mice (as shown in Supplementary S3) and subsequently subjected them to glyoxalate administration to establish a mouse model of CaOx renal calcinosis induction. Polarized light optical microscopy and PAS staining experiments revealed significant crystal deposition and exacerbated tubular epithelial injury in the CaOx group (as demonstrated in Fig. 3A). In order to delve deeper into the specific mechanisms of injury, Western blot experiments showed elevated expression levels of the target proteins Bax, Beclin1, Caspase-3, and p-MLKL in both the CaOx and GA groups compared to the NC group, suggesting involvement of autophagy, apoptosis, and necrotic apoptosis in CaOx-induced tubular epithelial damage (as displayed in Fig. 3B–E). Notably, circRSPRY1 expression was also increased in the CaOx group (Fig. 3A). However, after the overexpression of circRSPRY1, the expression levels of Bax, Beclin1, and Caspase-3 remained unchanged when compared to the CaOx group. In contrast, the expression level of p-MLKL decreased compared to the CaOx group and the GA group (Fig. 3C, E). This indicates that circRSPRY1 is involved in the regulation of necrotic apoptosis in renal calcium deposition disorder. circRSPRY1 reduces crystal burden by restraining necroptotic signaling

To determine whether circRSPRY1 mitigates CaOx-related epithelial injury by restraining necroptosis, we first evaluated Bax/Beclin1/p-MLKL in HK-2 cells. Compared with NC, CaOx increased Bax, Beclin1 and p-MLKL, whereas LV-circRSPRY1 markedly reversed these changes; LV-vector showed no effect (Fig. 5A, B). Consistently, p-MLKL immunofluorescence was robust after CaOx and was visibly reduced by circRSPRY1 overexpression (Fig. 5C). We next examined the RIPK1/RIPK3–MLKL module. CaOx decreased cleaved-caspase-8 and elevated p-RIPK1 and p-RIPK3 in HK-2 cells; LV-circRSPRY1 restored cleaved-caspase-8 while suppressing p-RIPK1 and p-RIPK3 (Fig. 5D, E), indicating effective attenuation of necroptotic signaling. In vivo, the glyoxylate (GA) model recapitulated these trends. Renal tissues from AAV9-circRSPRY1 mice exhibited lower Bax, Beclin1 and p-MLKL than GA or AAV9-vector controls (Fig. 5F, G). Polarized-light microscopy revealed reduced crystal burden, and H&E/PAS sections showed ameliorated tubular injury after circRSPRY1 delivery (Fig. 5H). Concordantly, tissue immunoblots demonstrated higher cleaved-caspase-8 together with lower p-RIPK1/p-RIPK3 in the AAV9-circRSPRY1 group (Fig. 5J, K). Discussion

A growing body of data indicates that non-coding RNAs (ncRNAs) play a pivotal role in regulating genes at both transcriptional and post-transcriptional levels in various diseases, including kidney stones [3, 18, 19]. Among ncRNAs, circular RNAs (circRNAs) have emerged as a novel class of transcripts generated through the reverse splicing of host genes [20]. With the advancements in high-throughput sequencing technology, an increasing number of circRNAs have been identified, expanding our understanding of their involvement in disease development [21–23].We found that circRSPRY1 is markedly downregulated in a calcium-oxalate–injured HK-2 model and in a glyoxylate-induced mouse renal calcinosis model; circRSPRY1 overexpression reduces crystal deposition, improves histology, and consistently lowers p-RIPK1, p-RIPK3, and p-MLKL. Mechanistically, this aligns with a non-coding RNA → miRNA → signaling protein cascade in which circRSPRY1 functions as a sponge for miR-21-5p, decreasing its availability for PTEN binding, thereby increasing PTEN expression, suppressing necroptosis, and alleviating kidney injury The circRNA_RSPRY1-miR-21-5p ceRNA network thus presents a novel diagnostic and therapeutic target for kidney stones.

As circRNAs can modulate gene expression and are differentially expressed under pathological conditions, they hold potential biological significance in various renal diseases. For instance, circ-AKT3 restoration in renal clear cell carcinoma has demonstrated inhibition of cell migration and invasion through miR-296-3p sponging, leading to reduced metastasis [24]. High expression of circ_000926 was found to promote the development and progression of renal cell carcinoma by sponging miR-411 [25]. In diabetic nephropathy, circRNA_15698 exacerbated extracellular matrix protein synthesis through the miR-185/TGF-β1 axis [26]. Circ_0123996 was identified as promoting mesangial cell proliferation and fibrosis via miR-149-5p sponging [27]. Additionally, circ_LARP4 overexpression has been associated with the inhibition of mesangial cell proliferation and fibrosis [28]. Furthermore, circRNAs are also implicated in the biological functions of renal tubular epithelial cells. Hsa_circ_0003928 interference has been linked to attenuating inflammatory cytokine secretion in high-glucose-induced renal tubular epithelial cells [29]. CircVMA21 has been shown to alleviate oxidative stress and inflammation in sepsis-associated acute kidney injury [30]. CircPRKCI attenuated LPS-induced HK-2 cell injury by regulating the miR-545/ZEB2 axis [31]. CircRNA TLK1 promoted sepsis-associated acute kidney injury by modulating inflammation and oxidative stress through the miR-106a-5p/HMGB1 axis [32].

Currently, the pathophysiological mechanisms underlying kidney stones remain largely elusive, with the regulatory roles of circRNAs, in particular, requiring further elucidation. Previous studies have reported aberrant expression of over 100 circRNAs in an ethylene glycol-induced kidney stone model in rats [15]; however, their specific biological functions remain unexplored. In this study, we have determined that the expression of circRNA_RSPRY1 is significantly reduced in kidney stones. This reduced circRNA_RSPRY1 sponge adsorption of miR-21-5p results in weakened regulation of PTEN and upregulation of p-MLKL expression, culminating in necrotic apoptosis. Importantly, overexpression of circRNA_RSPRY1, as confirmed in both in vivo and ex vivo experiments, effectively inhibits kidney stone-induced necrotic apoptosis. These findings underscore the significant role of circRNA_RSPRY1 in kidney stone progression.

We have identified miR-21-5p as a potential target of circRNA_RSPRY1 through bioinformatics. Our results show that circRNA_RSPRY1 can regulate miR-21-5p expression. Luciferase reporter gene assays, RIP, and PULL-DOWN experiments have confirmed that circRNA_RSPRY1 directly binds to the 3'-UTR of miR-21-5p, consequently downregulating miR-21-5p expression. MiR-21-5p, a well-characterized miRNA, has been associated with inflammation and tumor promotion [33] Previous studies have linked miR-21-5p to inflammatory disease progression. Chen et al. reported that miR-21 enhances inflammation and promotes apoptosis of glomerular podocytes by inhibiting TIMP3 expression [34]. Lakhia et al. demonstrated that miR-21 inhibits apoptosis and promotes the apoptosis of epithelial cells in polycystic kidneys [35]. Wei et al. found that circ_0068888 prevented LPS-induced HK-2 cell injury by sponging miR-21-5p in an LPS-induced acute kidney injury model [36]. MiR-21-5p has also been implicated in inducing pancreatic inflammation in an acute pancreatitis model by inhibiting PTEN expression [16]. In our study, miR-21-5p expression was significantly upregulated and inversely correlated with circRNA_RSPRY1 and PTEN expression in mice with glyoxalate-induced CaOx renal calcinosis. Nevertheless, the specific role of miR-21-5p in CaOx renal calcinosis pathogenesis remains to be elucidated.

Kidney stone formation is tightly linked to tubular injury, in which necroptosis plays a central role: activation of RIPK1/RIPK3 promotes MLKL phosphorylation and oligomerization, culminating in membrane disruption and necrotic cell death (we note that phosphorylated MLKL oligomerizes and translocates to the plasma membrane as the executing step of necroptosis) [37, 38].Conversely, caspase-8–mediated cleavage suppresses RIPK1/RIPK3, thereby physiologically restraining excessive necroptosis (i.e., caspase-8 limits necroptosis in vivo by cleaving RIPK1) [39, 40].

Beyond these canonical mechanisms, accumulating renal evidence directly links necroptosis to calcium oxalate–related injury: oxalate crystals trigger RIPK3–MLKL–dependent cytotoxicity in tubular cells, and genetic loss of RIPK3 or MLKL prevents oxalate crystal–induced acute kidney injury; importantly, human oxalate-associated AKI shows p-MLKL–positive tubular cells, underscoring translational relevance [11]. Pharmacologic or genetic interruption of RIPK3–MLKL signaling similarly mitigates nephrocalcinosis and inflammation, supporting the pathway as a tractable target [41].

Mechanistically, MLKL activation is followed by oligomerization, membrane engagement, and additional regulatory layers such as activation-coupled ubiquitylation that fine-tune execution, helping explain why p-MLKL is a sensitive functional readout in vivo and in vitro [41]. Together, these data position necroptosis not merely as a correlative marker but as a driver of tubular damage in oxalate settings—precisely the pathogenic niche interrogated here—thereby lending biological plausibility to our finding that the circRSPRY1–miR-21-5p–PTEN axis restrains necroptosis and alleviates injury [37, 39].

This study is the first to highlight the inhibitory role of circRNA_RSPRY1 in renal tubular epithelial cell injury caused by CaOx renal calcinosis and glyoxylate-induced CaOx crystal deposition. We have also unveiled the mechanism by which circRNA_RSPRY1 and miR-21-5p interactions regulate the PTEN to necrotic apoptotic pathway. However, this study has several limitations. First, the findings were generated in an immortalized tubular cell line and a glyoxylate-induced mouse model; although these systems capture key features of oxalate injury, they cannot fully recapitulate human disease heterogeneity. Second, the principal interventions—circRSPRY1 overexpression and miR-21-5p inhibition—may introduce non-physiologic effects; while rescue designs were used to support specificity, off-target or dose-related artifacts cannot be completely excluded. Third, we focused mechanistically on necroptosis and the circRSPRY1–miR-21-5p–PTEN axis; parallel pathways (e.g., apoptosis, pyroptosis, autophagy and inflammatory signaling) were not quantified systematically, so their relative contributions may be underappreciated. Fourth, outcome assessments (crystal burden, histopathology and immunoblot quantification) are subject to experimental variability; we mitigated this with predefined thresholds, blinded scoring where applicable and per-replicate data display, yet residual measurement error is possible. Fifth, some assays had modest sample sizes and multiple comparisons, which may affect statistical power and inflate type-I error despite correction; therefore, effect sizes and confidence intervals should be interpreted with caution. Sixth, sampling at discrete time points may have missed peaks in necroptotic signaling, potentially underestimating pathway amplitude. Finally, translational uncertainties remain regarding vector delivery, exposure, and safety in large animals or humans. Future work will incorporate human renal papilla/stone-adherent epithelia, time-course and multiplex cell-death profiling, endogenous modulation of circRSPRY1, and pharmacokinetic/pharmacodynamic studies to strengthen clinical relevance.

Summary

Our research provides novel mechanistic insights into the regulatory functions of circRNAs in CaOx renal calcium deposits. Reduced expression of circRNA_RSPRY1 serves as a sponge for miR-21, which leads to attenuated PTEN expression and consequent necrotic apoptosis in renal tubular epithelial cells, contributing to the development of calcium oxalate stones. These findings may contribute to the development of innovative therapeutic strategies for kidney stones.

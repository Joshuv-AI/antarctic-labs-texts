Definability of mad families of vector spaces and two local Ramsey theories
Clement Yung

Abstract
. Let E E be a vector space over a countable field of dimension ℵ 0 \aleph_{0} . Two infinite-dimensional subspaces V , W ⊆ E V,W\subseteq E are almost disjoint if V ∩ W V\cap W is finite-dimensional. This paper provides some improvements on results about the definability of maximal almost disjoint families (mad families) of subspaces in [ 17 ] . We show that a full mad family of block subspaces exists assuming either 𝔭 = max ⁡ { 𝔟 , 𝔰 } \mathfrak{p}=\max\{\mathfrak{b},\mathfrak{s}\} or a positive answer to a problem in [ 17 ] , improving Smythe’s construction assuming 𝔭 = 𝔠 \mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{c} . We also discuss the abstract Mathias forcing introduced by Di Prisco-Mijares-Nieto in [ 11 ] , and apply it to show that in the Solovay’s model obtained by the collapse of a Mahlo cardinal, there are no full mad families of block subspaces over 𝔽 2 \mathbb{F}_{2} .



1.
Introduction

Let
E
be a vector space over a countable field
\mathbb{F}
of dimension
\aleph_{0}
. In
[
17
]
, Smythe studied
almost disjoint families
infinite-dimensional subspaces
V,W\subseteq E
, i.e. families of infinite-dimensional subspaces of
E
which pairwise intersections are finite-dimensional. Our paper focuses on the following problems raised by Smythe in
[
17
]
.

Problem 1.1
.

Can mad families of subspaces be definable? In particular:

(1)

Are there analytic mad families of subspaces?

(2)

Assuming the existence large cardinals, are there mad families of subspaces in
\mathbf{L}(\mathbb{R})
?

Horowitz-Shelah proved in
[
5
]
that there are no analytic mad families of subspaces over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
, but the first problem remains largely open for other fields. Smythe remarked in
[
17
]
that there is a supercompact cardinal, then every set in Solovay’s model is
\cal{H}
-strategically Ramsey, so there are no full mad families. Strategically Ramsey subsets of
E^{[\infty]}
were introduced in
[
13
]
and
[
14
]
to study the combinatorics of Banach space theory and dichotomies, and Smythe introduced a localised version of the strategically Ramsey property in
[
16
]
. To the author’s knowledge, the precise large cardinal strength needed for the non-existence of mad families in
\mathbf{L}(\mathbb{R})
is mostly unknown.

Inspired by Mathias’ original proof of the non-existence of analytic mad families of
[\omega]^{\omega}
, Smythe approached this problem using a local Ramsey theoretic approach in
[
17
]
, replacing
\cal{H}
-Ramsey with
\cal{H}
-strategically Ramsey. Given an almost disjoint family
\cal{A}
, he extended Mathias’ definition of the “happy family”
\mathfrak{F}{A}
of subsets of
\omega
, to a “family”
\cal{H}(\cal{A})
of subspaces of
E
. His approach requires
\cal{H}(\cal{A})
to be
full
, a property which closely resembles the pigeonhole property of coideals of
[\omega]^{\omega}
. Calling a mad family
\cal{A}

full
if
\cal{H}(\cal{A})
is full, Smythe proved the following theorem:

Theorem 1.2

(Smythe
[
17
]
)
.

There exist no analytic full mad families of
block subspaces
.

Block subspaces are subspaces with a basis of vectors with strictly increasing support (see §
2
for a precise definition). Block subspaces are more combinatorially tame, and since every infinite-dimensional subspace contains an infinite-dimensional block subspace, an almost disjoint family of subspaces is maximal with respect to block subspaces iff it is maximal.

While it was clear from Smythe’s proof that the fullness property plays an important role in understanding the definability of mad families of subspaces, the extent which mad families of subspaces satisfy such property remains poorly understood. In particular, Smythe asked the following question in
[
17
]
:

Problem 1.3

(Smythe,
[
17
]
)
.

Does
\mathsf{ZFC}
prove the existence of a full mad family of subspaces? Even more, does
\mathsf{ZFC}
prove that every mad family of subspaces is full?

In the same paper, Smythe proved that assuming
\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{c}
, there exists a full mad family.

Another obstacle to understanding the definability of mad families of subspaces is the restriction to mad families of block subspaces. It is unknown if Theorem
1.2
holds for arbitrary mad families of subspaces (except for when
|\mathbb{F}|=2
). Furthermore, consider the following two cardinals:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}_{\mathrm{vec},\mathbb{F}}^{*}

\displaystyle:=\min\{|\cal{A}|:\text{$\cal{A}$ is a mad family of subspaces of $E$}\},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}_{\mathrm{vec},\mathbb{F}}

\displaystyle:=\min\{|\cal{A}|:\text{$\cal{A}$ is a mad family of block subspaces of $E$}\}.

It is unknown if they are equal in
\mathsf{ZFC}
.

Problem 1.4

(Smythe,
[
17
]
)
.

Do we lose any generality when restricting mad families to block subspaces? In particular, is
\mathfrak{a}_{\mathrm{vec},\mathbb{F}}^{*}=\mathfrak{a}_{\mathrm{vec},\mathbb{F}}
true in
\mathsf{ZFC}
?

The first of the two main results in the paper improves Smythe’s construction of a full mad family, and addresses Problems
1.1
and
1.4
as follows:

Theorem 1.5
.

If
\mathfrak{a}_{\mathrm{vec},\mathbb{F}}^{*}=\mathfrak{a}_{\mathrm{vec},\mathbb{F}}
or
\mathfrak{p}=\max\{\mathfrak{b},\mathfrak{s}\}
, then there exists a full mad family of block subspaces.

Our method is inspired by a variant of the construction of a completely separable mad family of

in
[
9
]

[
4
]

[

]

[

The second main result in this paper addresses the existence of full mad families of block subspaces over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
in Solovay’s model. We utilise the abstract Mathias forcing was developed in by Di Prisco-Mijares-Nieto

Let

2.
Setup and notations

Let
E
be a vector space over a countable field
\mathbb{F}
, with a countable Hamel basis
(e_{n})_{n<\omega}
. For each
x\in E
, we may write
x:=\sum_{n<\omega}\lambda_{n}(x)e_{n}
, where
\lambda_{n}\in\mathbb{F}
. This allows us to define the
support
of a vector
x\in E
by:

\displaystyle\operatorname{supp}(x):=\{n<\omega:\lambda_{n}(x)\neq 0\}.

This, in turn, gives us a partial order
<
on
E
, where for any two non-zero vectors
x,y\in E
:

\displaystyle x<y\iff\max(\operatorname{supp}(x))<\min(\operatorname{supp}(y)).

A
block sequence
of vectors in
E
is thus a
<
-increasing sequence. Note that any block sequence of vectors is linearly independent.

Definition 2.1
.

An infinite-dimensional subspace
V\subseteq E
is a
block subspace
if
V
has a
block basis
, i.e. an infinite block sequence
(x_{n})_{n<\omega}
such that
V=\operatorname{span}\{x_{n}:n<\omega\}
.

Since such a block basis is necessarily unique, we may conflate block subspaces of
E
with infinite block sequences.

Notation 2.2
.

•

We let
E^{[\infty]}
(resp.
E^{[<\infty]}
) denote the set of infinite (resp. finite) block sequences of vectors in
E
.

•

E^{[\leq\infty]}:=E^{[\infty]}\cup E^{[<\infty]}
.

•

If
A=(x_{n})_{n<\omega}\in E^{[\infty]}
and
N<\omega
, we write
A/N:=(x_{n})_{n\geq N}
.

•

If
A=(x_{n})_{n<\omega}\in E^{[\infty]}
and
n<\omega
, we write
r_{n}(A):=(x_{i})_{i<n}
.

•

If
A\in E^{[<\infty]}
and
a\in E^{[<\infty]}
we write
d_{A}(a):=\min\{N<\omega:\max(\operatorname{supp}(a))<A/N\}
.

•

If
A\in E^{[\infty]}
and
a\in E^{[<\infty]}
, then
A/a:=A/d_{A}(a)
.

•

If
(x_{n})_{n<\alpha}\in E^{[\leq\infty]}
, we write:

\displaystyle\langle(x_{n})_{n<\alpha}\rangle:=\operatorname{span}\{x_{n}:n<\alpha\}.

•

If
A,B\in E^{[\infty]}
, we write
A\leq B
iff
\langle A\rangle\subseteq\langle B\rangle
.

•

If
A,B\in E^{[\infty]}
, we write
A\leq^{*}B
iff
A/N\leq B
for some
N<\omega
.

Block sequences/subspaces were introduced as they are more combinatorially well-behaved than arbitrary infinite-dimensional subspaces. The focus on block sequences is further justified by the observation that every infinite-dimensional subspace of
E
contains a block subspace. This will be apparent once we have introduced the relevant definitions and notations for almost disjoint families of subspaces.

3.
Local Ramsey theory of block subspaces

Smythe explored the local Ramsey theory of block subspaces of a countable vector space developed in
[
16
]
, mainly focusing on
\leq^{*}
-upward closed subsets of
E^{[\infty]}
(which Smythe calls
families
) and their various properties. We provide an overview of this theory, along with several additional results which will prove to be useful in later sections.

3.1.
Semicoideals

To avoid confusion with other uses of the word “family” such as almost disjoint families, we shall use the term “semicoideals” in place of “families”.

Definition 3.1
.

A subset
\cal{H}\subseteq E^{[\infty]}
is a
semicoideal
if it is
\leq^{*}
-upward closed. That is, if
A\in\cal{H}
and
A\leq^{*}B
, then
B\in\cal{H}
.

To cope with the failure of the pigeonhole principle in the setting of countable vector spaces with
|\mathbb{F}|\geq 3
, Smythe introduced a weaker form of the pigeonhole property of semicoideals, which he calls
fullness
.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
\cal{H}\subseteq E^{[\infty]}
be a semicoideal.

(1)

A subset
\cal{D}\subseteq E^{[\infty]}
is
\cal{H}
-dense
below some
A\in\cal{H}
if for all
B\in\cal{H}\mathord{\upharpoonright}A
, there exists some
C\leq B
such that
C\in\cal{D}
. A set
Y\subseteq E
is
\cal{H}
-dense
below
A
if the set
\{B\in E^{[\infty]}:\langle B\rangle\subseteq Y\}
is.

(2)

We say that
\cal{H}
is
full
if for all
Y\subseteq E
such that
Y
is
\cal{H}
-dense below some
A\in\cal{H}
, there exists some
B\in\cal{H}\mathord{\upharpoonright}A
such that
\langle B\rangle\subseteq Y
.

It turns out that if
|\mathbb{F}|=2
, then fullness coincides with the local pigeonhole principle property. In this case, we say that
\cal{H}
is a
coideal
.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
\cal{H}\subseteq E^{[\infty]}
be a semicoideal. We say that
\cal{H}
is a
coideal
if for all
Y\subseteq E
and
A\in\cal{H}
, there exists some
B\in\cal{H}\mathord{\upharpoonright}A
(i.e.
B\in\cal{H}
and
B\leq A
) such that
\langle B\rangle\subseteq Y
or
\langle B\rangle\subseteq Y^{c}
.

By Hindman’s theorem,
E^{[\infty]}
is a coideal if
|\mathbb{F}|=2
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Suppose that
|\mathbb{F}|=2
, and let
\cal{H}\subseteq E^{[\infty]}
be a semicoideal. Then
\cal{H}
is full iff
\cal{H}
is a coideal.

Proof.

Suppose that
\cal{H}
is a coideal. Let
Y\subseteq E
, and suppose that
Y
is
\cal{H}
-dense below some
A\in\cal{H}
. Since
\cal{H}
is a coideal, there exists some
B\in\cal{H}\mathord{\upharpoonright}A
such that
\langle B\rangle\subseteq Y
or
\langle B\rangle\subseteq Y^{c}
. Since
\cal{Y}
is
\cal{H}
-dense below
A
, the latter option is not possible.

Now suppose that
\cal{H}
is full. Let
Y\subseteq E
, and let
A\in\cal{H}
. If
Y
is
\cal{H}
-dense below
A
, then we are done by the fullness of
\cal{H}
, so assume otherwise. This means that there exists some
B\in\cal{H}\mathord{\upharpoonright}A
such that for all
C\leq B
,
\langle C\rangle\not\subseteq Y
. By Hindman’s theorem, for all
C\in\cal{H}\mathord{\upharpoonright}B
, there exists some
D\leq C
such that
\langle D\rangle\subseteq Y^{c}
(as
\langle D\rangle\not\subseteq Y
). But this means that
Y^{c}
is
\cal{H}
-dense below
B
, so there exists some
C\in\cal{H}\mathord{\upharpoonright}B
such that
\langle C\rangle\subseteq Y^{c}
, as desired.
∎

3.2.
Selectivity properties

In the process of studying mad families (particularly
\cal{H}(\cal{A})
, see Definition
4.6
), Smythe extended several local Ramsey theoretic properties of coideals of
[\mathbb{N}]^{\infty}
to which Smythe extended to semicoideals. The first such property extends the (p)-property for coideals of
[\mathbb{N}]^{\infty}
(see Lemma 7.4,
[
19
]
).

Definition 3.5
.

Let
\cal{H}\subseteq E^{[\infty]}
be a semicoideal.

(1)

Let
(A_{n})_{n<\omega}
be a
\leq
-decreasing sequence in
\cal{H}
. We say that
B\in\cal{H}

weakly diagonalises

(A_{n})_{n<\omega}
if
B\leq A_{0}
and
B\leq^{*}A_{n}
for all
n
.

(2)

We say that
\cal{H}
satisfies the
(p)-property
, or that
\cal{H}
is a
(p)-semicoideal
, if every
\leq
-decreasing sequence in
\cal{H}
has a weak diagonalisation in
\cal{H}
.

We shall provide a similar extension of the
(q)-property
(see Lemma 7.4,
[
19
]
). Smythe also made a similar extension, which he termed as
spreadness
(Definition 8.11,
[
16
]
).

Definition 3.6
.

Let
\cal{H}\subseteq E^{[\infty]}
be a semicoideal. We say that
\cal{H}
satisfies the
(q)-property
, or that
\cal{H}
is a
(q)-semicoideal
, if for all
A\in\cal{H}
and for all increasing sequence of finite intervals
(I_{m})_{m<\omega}
, there exists some
B\in\cal{H}\mathord{\upharpoonright}A
such that for all
n
, there exists some
m
such that:

(1)

\langle r_{n}(B)\rangle\subseteq\langle r_{\min(I_{m})-1}(A)\rangle
, and;

(2)

B/n\leq A/\max(I_{m})
.

In a similar vein, we extend the definition of selectivity of coideals of
[\mathbb{N}]^{\infty}
(see Definition 7.3,
[
19
]
) to semicoideals of block subspaces.

Definition 3.7
.

For any
A\in E^{[\infty]}
and
x\in E
, we write:

\displaystyle d_{A}(x):=\min\{N<\omega:\max(\operatorname{supp}(x))<A/N\}.

Definition 3.8
.

Let
\cal{H}\subseteq E^{[\infty]}
be a semicoideal.

(1)

Let
A\in\cal{H}
, and let
(A_{n})_{n<\omega}
be a
\leq
-decreasing sequence in
\cal{H}
below
A
(i.e.
A_{0}\leq A
). We say that
B\in\cal{H}\mathord{\upharpoonright}A

diagonalises
(A_{n})_{n<\omega}
within
A
if
B/n\leq A_{d_{A}(r_{n}(B))}
for all
n<\omega
.

(2)

We say that
\cal{H}
is
selective
if for all
A\in\cal{H}
and
\leq
-decreasing chain
(A_{n})_{n<\omega}
of elements in
\cal{H}
below
A
, there exists a diagonalisation of
(A_{n})_{n<\omega}
within
A
in
\cal{H}
.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
\cal{H}\subseteq E^{[\infty]}
be a (q)-semicoideal. Let
(A_{n})_{n<\omega}
be a
\leq
-decreasing sequence in
\cal{H}
. If
B\in\cal{H}\mathord{\upharpoonright}A_{0}
weakly diagonalises
(A_{n})_{n<\omega}
, then there exists some
C\in\cal{H}\mathord{\upharpoonright}B
which diagonalises
(A_{n})_{n<\omega}
within
A_{0}
.

Proof.

Let
A:=A_{0}
, and let
B\in\cal{H}\mathord{\upharpoonright}A
weakly diagonalise
(A_{n})_{n<\omega}
. Let
(I_{m})_{m<\omega}
be any increasing sequence of intervals such that
B/\max(I_{m})\leq A_{d_{A}(r_{\min(I_{m})-1}(B))}
. By the (q)-property, there exists some
C\in\cal{H}\mathord{\upharpoonright}B
such that for all
n
,
\langle r_{n}(C)\rangle\subseteq\langle r_{\min(I_{m})-1}(B)\rangle
and
C/n\leq B/\max(I_{m})
. The first property implies that
d_{A}(r_{n}(C))\leq d_{A}(r_{\min(I_{m})}(B))
, so by the second property:

\displaystyle C/n\leq B/\max(I_{m})\leq A_{d_{A}(r_{\min(I_{m})}(B))}\leq A_{d_{A}(r_{n}(C))},

so
C
strongly diagonalises
(A_{n})_{n<\omega}
.
∎

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
\cal{H}\subseteq E^{[\infty]}
be a semicoideal. The following are equivalent:

(1)

\cal{H}
is selective.

(2)

\cal{H}
satisfies both the (p)-property and the (q)-property.

Proof.

(2)
\implies
(1):
Let
(A_{n})_{n<\omega}
be a
\leq
-decreasing sequence in
\cal{H}
, and let
A:=A_{0}
. By the (p)-property, there exists some
B\in\cal{H}\mathord{\upharpoonright}A
which weakly diagonalises
(A_{n})_{n<\omega}
. Since
\cal{H}
satisfies the (q)-property, by Lemma
3.9
there exists some
C\in\cal{H}\mathord{\upharpoonright}B
which diagonalises
(A_{n})_{n<\omega}
. Therefore,
\cal{H}
is selective.

(1)
\implies
(2):
Since every diagonalisation is a weak diagonalisation,
\cal{H}
satisfies the (p)-property. We shall show that
\cal{H}
satisfies the (q)-property. Let
A\in\cal{H}
, and let
(I_{m})_{m<\omega}
be any increasing sequence of finite intervals. For each
n
, let
m(n)
be the least integer such that
\min(I_{m(n)})>n
. Let
B\in\cal{H}\mathord{\upharpoonright}A
diagonalise
(A/\max(I_{m(n)}))_{n<\omega}
within
A
. Then for any
n
, if
k:=d_{A}(r_{n}(B))
, then:

\displaystyle\langle r_{n}(B)\rangle\subseteq\langle r_{k}(A)\rangle\subseteq\langle r_{\min(I_{m(k)})}(A)\rangle,

and:

\displaystyle B/n\leq A_{d_{A}(r_{n}(B))}=A/\max(I_{m(k)}),

as desired.
∎

We shall examine the selectivity of semicoideals generated by almost disjoint families in the next section.

4.
Almost disjoint families

We recall the following:

Definition 4.1
.

Two infinite-dimensional subspaces
V,W\subseteq E
are
almost disjoint
if
V\cap W
is a finite-dimensional subspace of
E
. Otherwise, we say that
V
and
W
are
compatible
.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
\cal{A}
be a family of infinite-dimensional subspaces of
E
. We say that
\cal{A}
is
almost disjoint
if all subspaces in
\cal{A}
are pairwise almost disjoint. We say that
\cal{A}
is
maximal almost disjoint
(or just
mad
) if
\cal{A}
is not strictly contained in another almost disjoint family of infinite-dimensional subspaces.

Since every infinite-dimensional subspace of
E
contains a block subspace, an almost disjoint family of subspaces
\cal{A}
is mad iff there is no block subspace which is almost disjoint from every element of
\cal{A}
.

We dedicate this section to some basic definitions/notations and results of almost disjoint families of block subspaces, and their relationship with the local Ramsey theory of semicoideals of block subspaces.

4.1.
Uncountability of almost disjoint families

We begin with the most fundamental question of the theory of mad families - the uncountability of infinite mad families. Consider the following two cardinals:

•

\mathfrak{a}_{\mathrm{vec},\mathbb{F}}^{*}:=\min\{|\cal{A}|:\text{$\cal{A}$ is a mad family of subspaces of $E$}\}
.

•

Lemma 4.4
.

\aleph_{1}\leq\mathfrak{a}_{\mathrm{vec},\mathbb{F}}^{*}
.

Proof.

Let
\{V_{n}:n<\omega\}
be a countable family of almost disjoint subspaces of
E
, and we wish to find some infinite-dimensional subspace
W
which is almost disjoint from
V_{n}
for all
n
. We shall first prove a useful claim.

Claim
.

Let
U,V,W\subseteq E
be subspaces such that
\dim(V\cap W)<\infty
and
\dim(U)<\infty
. Then
\dim((U+V)\cap W)<\infty
.

Proof.

Define a linear map
\phi:V\times W\to E
by stipulating that
\phi(v,w):=w-v
. Let
\rho:E\to E/U
be the canonical quotient map, and let
\psi:=\pi\circ\rho
. Note that:

\displaystyle\dim(\ker(\psi))\leq\dim(\ker(\phi))+\dim(\ker(\rho))<\infty.

Let
\pi:V\oplus W\to W
be the projection map. Observe that for all
w\in W
, we have that:

\displaystyle\phantom{{}\iff{}}w\in(U+V)\cap W

\displaystyle\iff w=u+v\text{ for some $u\in U$, $v\in V$}

\displaystyle\iff w-v\in U\text{ for some $v\in V$}

\displaystyle\iff(v,w)\in\ker(\psi)\text{ for some $v\in V$}.

This implies that
\pi
maps
\ker(\psi)
surjectively onto
(U+V)\cap W
, and since
\ker(\psi)
is of finite dimension, so is
(U+V)\cap W
.
∎

We shall define the subspace
W=\{x_{n}:n<\omega\}
inductively as follows: Let
x_{0}\in V_{0}
be any vector. Now assume that we have defined a linearly independent set
\{x_{i}:i<n\}
such that for all
k<n
, we have that:

\displaystyle\operatorname{span}\{x_{i}:i<n\}\cap V_{k}\subseteq\operatorname{span}\{x_{i}:i\leq k\}.

We wish to fine some vector
x_{n}\in V_{n}
such that for all
k<n
, we have that:

\displaystyle\operatorname{span}\{x_{i}:i\leq n\}\cap V_{k}\subseteq\operatorname{span}\{x_{i}:i\leq k\}.

If we can complete this induction step, then we are done as we have that
W\cap V_{n}\subseteq\operatorname{span}\{x_{0},\dots,x_{n}\}
is finite-dimensional for all
n
.

For each
k<n
, let
U_{k}:=V_{k}+\operatorname{span}\{x_{i}:i<n\}
. By the claim at the beginning of the proof,
V_{n}\cap U_{k}
is of finite dimension. Since every infinite-dimensional vector space is not a finite union of finite-dimensional subspaces, we have that:

\displaystyle V_{n}\setminus\bigcup_{k<n}V_{n}\cap U_{k}\neq\emptyset,

so we may let
x_{n}
be any vector in this set. We shall show that this vector
x_{n}
works. We first note that
\{x_{i}:i\leq n\}
is linearly independent, as
\operatorname{span}\{x_{i}:i<n\}\subseteq U_{0}
and
x_{n}\notin U_{0}
. Now let
k<n
and
x\in\operatorname{span}\{x_{i}:i<n+1\}\cap V_{k}
, so we have that
x=\sum_{i\leq n}\lambda_{i}x_{i}\in V_{k}
. If
\lambda_{n}\neq 0
, then we have that:

\displaystyle x_{n}=\lambda_{n}^{-1}\left(x-\sum_{i<n}\lambda_{i}x_{i}\right)\in V_{n}\cap U_{k},

contradicting the choice of
x_{n}
. Therefore,
\lambda_{n}=0
, which implies that:

\displaystyle x\in\operatorname{span}\{x_{i}:i<n\}\cap V_{k}=\operatorname{span}\{x_{i}:i\leq k\}\cap V_{k},

as desired.
∎

Smythe asked in the same paper whether

holds in
\mathsf{ZFC}
, and this problem remains open to the author’s knowledge.

4.2.
Ideals and semicoideals generated by almost disjoint families

If
\cal{A}
is an almost disjoint family of
[\omega]^{\omega}
, then the following family of sets are often studied:

\displaystyle\cal{I}(\cal{A})

\displaystyle:=\{Y\subseteq\omega:Y\subseteq^{*}A_{0}\cup\cdots\cup A_{n-1}\text{ for some $A_{0},\dots,A_{n-1}\in\cal{A}$}\},

\displaystyle\cal{I}^{+}(\cal{A})

\displaystyle:=\cal{P}(\omega)\setminus\cal{I}(\cal{A}),

\displaystyle\cal{I}^{++}(\cal{A})

\displaystyle:=\{Y\subseteq\omega:\{A\in\cal{A}:Y\cap A\text{ is infinite}\}\text{ is infinite}\},

\displaystyle\cal{H}(\cal{A})

\displaystyle:=\cal{I}^{++}(\cal{A}).

It is simple to verify that
\cal{I}^{+}(\cal{A})=\cal{I}^{++}(\cal{A})
iff
\cal{A}
is maximal. Motivated by this, Smythe introduced a version of
\cal{H}(\cal{A})
for almost disjoint families of block subspaces.
\cal{H}(\cal{A})
is a semicoideal, and Smythe studied the
conditions that
\cal{A}
must satisfy to make
\cal{H}(\cal{A})
a full semicoideal.

Definition 4.5
.

Let
Y\subseteq E
.

(1)

Y
is
big
if there exists some
A\in E^{[\infty]}
such that
\langle A\rangle\subseteq Y
.

(2)

Y
is
small
if it is not big.

(3)

Y
is
very small
if for all
Z\subseteq E
such that
Z
is small,
Y\cup Z
is small.

Definition 4.6
.

Let
\cal{A}
be an almost disjoint family of infinite-dimensional subspaces. We then define the following:

\displaystyle\cal{I}(\cal{A})

\displaystyle:=\left\{Y\subseteq E:\begin{matrix}Y\setminus(\langle A_{0}\rangle\cup\cdots\cup\langle A_{n-1}\rangle)\text{ is small}\\
\text{for some }A_{0},\dots,A_{n-1}\in\cal{A}\end{matrix}\right\},

\displaystyle\cal{I}^{+}(\cal{A})

\displaystyle:=\cal{P}(E)\setminus\cal{I}(\cal{A}),

\displaystyle\cal{I}^{++}(\cal{A})

\displaystyle:=\{Y\subseteq E:\{A\in\cal{A}:Y\cap\langle A\rangle\text{ is big}\}\text{ is infinite}\},

\displaystyle\cal{H}^{-}(\cal{A})

\displaystyle:=\{B\in E^{[\infty]}:\langle B\rangle\in\cal{I}^{+}(\cal{A})\},

\displaystyle\cal{H}(\cal{A})

\displaystyle:=\{B\in E^{[\infty]}:\langle B\rangle\in\cal{I}^{++}(\cal{A})\}.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
\cal{A}\subseteq E^{[\infty]}
be an infinite almost disjoint family, and let
Y\subseteq E
. The following are equivalent:

(1)

\langle Y\rangle\in\cal{I}^{+}(\cal{A})
.

(2)

\langle Y\rangle\in\cal{I}^{++}(\cal{A})
or there exists some
B\in E^{[\infty]}
, with
\langle B\rangle\subseteq\langle Y\rangle
, such that
B
is almost disjoint from every element of
\cal{A}
.

Consequently, if
\cal{A}
is mad, then
\cal{H}^{-}(\cal{A})=\cal{H}(\cal{A})
.

Proof.

We remark that for any infinite almost disjoint family
\cal{A}
,
\cal{I}^{++}(\cal{A})\subseteq\cal{I}^{+}(\cal{A})
.

(1)
\implies
(2):
Let
\langle Y\rangle\in\cal{I}^{+}(\cal{A})
. Suppose that for all
B\in E^{[\infty]}
such that
\langle B\rangle\subseteq\langle Y\rangle
,
B
is not almost disjoint from some element of
\cal{A}
. We let
A_{0}\in\cal{A}
be compatible with
\langle Y\rangle
(i.e.
\langle Y\rangle\cap\langle A_{0}\rangle
is big), and let
B_{0}\in E^{[\infty]}
be such that
\langle B_{0}\rangle\subseteq\langle Y\rangle\cap\langle A_{0}\rangle
. Clearly
\langle B_{0}\rangle\in\cal{I}(\cal{A})
, so we have that
\langle Y\rangle\setminus\langle B_{0}\rangle\in\cal{I}^{+}(\cal{A})
(for otherwise
\langle Y\rangle\in\cal{I}(\cal{A})
). Let
B_{1}^{\prime}\in E^{[\infty]}
be such that
\langle B_{1}^{\prime}\rangle\subseteq\langle Y\rangle\setminus\langle B_{0}\rangle
. By our assumption,
B_{1}^{\prime}
is compatible with some
A_{1}\in\cal{A}
, so let
B_{1}\in E^{[\infty]}
be such that
\langle B_{1}\rangle\subseteq\langle B_{1}^{\prime}\rangle\cap\langle A_{1}\rangle
. By a similar reasoning as above, we have that
\langle Y\rangle\setminus(\langle B_{0}\rangle\cup\langle B_{1}\rangle)\in\cal{I}^{+}(\cal{A})
. We may repeat this process indefinitely, and we have that
\{A_{n}:n<\omega\}\subseteq\cal{A}
is a disjoint family such that
A
is compatible with
A_{n}
for all
n
. Therefore,
\langle Y\rangle\in\cal{I}^{++}(\cal{A})
.

(2)
\implies
(1):
If
\langle Y\rangle\in\cal{I}^{++}(\cal{A})
, then we are done, so assume that there exists some
B\in E^{[\infty]}
such that
\langle B\rangle\subseteq\langle Y\rangle
and is almost disjoint from every element of
\cal{A}
. Then for any
A_{0},\dots,A_{n-1}\in\cal{A}
, by Lemma
4.8
we may let
N
be large enough so that
\langle B/N\rangle\cap\langle A_{i}\rangle=\emptyset
for all
n<\omega
. This implies that
\langle B/N\rangle\subseteq\langle Y\rangle\setminus\bigcup_{i<n}\langle A_{i}\rangle
. Therefore,
B/m\in\cal{H}^{-}(\cal{A})
, so
\langle Y\rangle\in\cal{I}^{+}(\cal{A})
as
\cal{I}^{+}(\cal{A})
is
\leq
-upward closed.
∎

We conclude this part with three useful lemmas we shall use repeatedly in this paper.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
A\in E^{[\infty]}
and
Y\subseteq E
. Then
\langle A\rangle\cap\langle Y\rangle
is small iff
\langle A/N\rangle\cap\langle Y\rangle=\emptyset
for some
N
. Consequently:

(1)

If
A,B\in E^{[\infty]}
, then
A
and
B
are almost disjoint iff
\langle A/N\rangle\cap\langle B\rangle=\emptyset
for some
N
.

(2)

If
A/N
and
B
are almost disjoint for some
N
, then
A
and
B
are almost disjoint.

Proof.

Suppose that
\langle A\rangle\cap\langle Y\rangle
is big. Let
B\in E^{[\infty]}
such that
\langle B\rangle\subseteq\langle A\rangle\cap\langle Y\rangle
. For any
N
, there exists some
M
such that
B/M\leq A/N
. This implies that
\langle B/M\rangle\subseteq\langle A/N\rangle\cap\langle Y\rangle
, so in particular,
\langle A/N\rangle\cap\langle Y\rangle\neq\emptyset
for all

.

Conversely, suppose that
\langle A/N\rangle\cap\langle Y\rangle\neq\emptyset
for all
N
. We construct some
B=(y_{n})_{n<\omega}\in E^{[\infty]}
such that
\langle B\rangle\subseteq\langle A\rangle\cap\langle Y\rangle
inductively as follows: Assuming that
y_{0},\dots,y_{n-1}
have been defined so far, let
N
be large enough so that
\operatorname{supp}(y_{n-1})<A/N
. We let
y_{n}\in\langle A/N\rangle\cap\langle Y\rangle
be any element. Then
\langle B\rangle\subseteq\langle A\rangle\cap\langle Y\rangle
, so
\langle A\rangle\cap\langle Y\rangle
is big.
∎

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
A\in E^{[\infty]}
, and suppose that
\{B_{n}:n<\omega\}\subseteq E^{[\infty]}
is such that
B_{n}\leq A
for all
n
. Then there exists some
B\leq A
such that
B
is compatible with
B_{n}
for all
n
, and:

(
*
)

\displaystyle\langle B\rangle\subseteq\left\langle\bigcup_{n<\omega}\langle B_{n}\rangle\right\rangle\subseteq\langle A\rangle.

Proof.

Let
\Gamma:\omega\to\omega
be any infinite-to-one surjection, i.e. a surjection such that
\Gamma^{-1}(m)
is infinite for all
m<\omega
. We define
B=(x_{n})_{n<\omega}
inductively as follows: If
(x_{k})_{k<n}
has been defined, let
x_{n}\in\langle B_{\Gamma(n)}\rangle
be any element such that
(x_{k})_{k<n}<x_{n}
. Then
(x_{n})_{n\in\Gamma^{-1}(m)}
witnesses that
B
and
B_{m}
are compatible for all

, and since

for all

, the identity (

) is satisfied.
∎

Lemma 4.10
.

Let
\cal{A}\subseteq E^{[\infty]}
be an almost disjoint family, and let
Y\subseteq E
. Suppose further that
\langle Y\rangle\in\cal{I}^{+}(\cal{A})
and there does not exist any
B\in E^{[\infty]}
such that
\langle B\rangle\subseteq\langle Y\rangle
and
B
is almost disjoint from every element of
\cal{A}
. Then there exists an infinite set
\{A_{n}:n<\omega\}\subseteq\cal{A}
and an infinite disjoint family
\{B_{n}:n<\omega\}
such that for all
n
:

(1)

B_{n}\leq A_{n}
for some
A_{n}\in\cal{A}
.

(2)

\langle B_{n}\rangle\subseteq\langle Y\rangle
.

Note that these two properties imply that
\langle Y\rangle\in\cal{I}^{++}(\cal{A})
.

Proof.

Since
\langle Y\rangle\in\cal{I}^{+}(\cal{A})
,
\langle Y\rangle
is big, so there exists some
B_{0}^{\prime}
such that
\langle B_{0}^{\prime}\rangle\subseteq\langle Y\rangle
. By our assumption,
B_{0}^{\prime}
is compatible with some
A_{0}\in\cal{A}
. Let
B_{0}
be such that
\langle B_{0}\rangle\subseteq\langle B_{0}^{\prime}\rangle\cap\langle A_{0}\rangle
. Since
\langle Y\rangle\in\cal{I}^{+}(\cal{A})
,
\langle Y\rangle\setminus\langle A_{0}\rangle
is big. Given an ordinal
\alpha
, suppose that we have defined an infinite disjoint family
\{B_{\beta}:\beta<\alpha\}
and
\{A_{\beta}:\beta<\alpha\}\subseteq\cal{A}
such that
B_{\beta}\leq A_{\beta}
for all
\beta<\alpha
, and
\langle B_{\beta}\rangle\subseteq\langle Y\rangle
for all
\beta<\alpha
. If

is small, we terminate the construction. Otherwise, we let
B_{\alpha}^{\prime}
be such that
\langle B_{\alpha}^{\prime}\rangle\subseteq Y\setminus\bigcup_{\beta<\alpha}\langle B_{\beta}\rangle
, and let
A_{\alpha}\in\cal{A}
be compatible with
B_{\alpha}^{\prime}
. We then let
B_{\alpha}
be such that
\langle B_{\alpha}\rangle\subseteq\langle B_{\alpha}^{\prime}\rangle\cap\langle A_{\alpha}\rangle
. Since
\langle Y\rangle
is countable and the

’s are disjoint, this induction must terminate at some countable stage

, and since
\langle Y\rangle\in\cal{I}^{+}(\cal{A})
,

must be infinite.
∎

4.3.
Selectivity properties revisited

We now return to studying the combinatorial properties of the semicoideals
\cal{H}^{-}(\cal{A})
and
\cal{H}(\cal{A})
. In Mathias’ process of proving that there are no analytic mad families of
[\mathbb{N}]^{\infty}
, he proved that if
\cal{A}
is an almost disjoint family in
[\mathbb{N}]^{\infty}
, then
\cal{H}^{-}(\cal{A})
is a selective coideal. In
[
17
]
, Smythe proved that if
\cal{A}
is a mad family in
(E^{[\infty]},\leq,r)
, then
\cal{H}(\cal{A})
is a (p)-semicoideal. We prove a few extensions of this result.

Proposition 4.11
.

Let
\cal{A}\subseteq E^{[\infty]}
be an almost disjoint family. Then
\cal{H}^{-}(\cal{A})
is a (p)-semicoideal.

Proof.

Let
(B_{n})_{n<\omega}
be a
\leq
-decreasing sequence in
\cal{H}^{-}(\cal{A})
. If there exists some
C
which weakly diagonalises
(B_{n})_{n<\omega}
and
C
is almost disjoint from every element of
\cal{A}
, then
C\in\cal{H}^{-}(\cal{A})
and we are done, so assume otherwise. Let
C_{0}
be a weak diagonalisation of
(B_{n})_{n<\omega}
. By our assumption,
C_{0}
is compatible with some
A_{0}\in\cal{A}
, so by taking a further block subspace of
C_{0}
if necessary, we may assume that
C_{0}\leq A_{0}
. Now assume, for the induction hypothesis, that we have defined
C_{k},A_{k}
for
k<m
such that:

(1)

C_{k}\leq B_{k}
is a diagonalisation of
(B_{n})_{n<\omega}
.

(2)

C_{k}\leq A_{k}
and
A_{k}\in\cal{A}
.

(3)

If
i,j<m
then
A_{i}\neq A_{j}
.

Claim
.

There exists a
\leq
-decreasing sequence
(B_{n}^{m})_{n<\omega}
such that for all
n
:

(1)

B_{n}^{m}\leq B_{n}
, and;

(2)

B_{n}^{m}
is almost disjoint from
A_{k}
for all
k<m
.

Proof.

For any
n
, since
B_{n}\in\cal{H}^{-}(\cal{A})
there exists some
D_{n}\leq B_{n}
that is disjoint from
A_{k}
for all
k<m
. Let
\Gamma:\omega\to\omega
be any infinite-to-one surjection. We define
D=(x_{n})_{n<\omega}
as follows: Assuming that
(x_{i})_{i<n}
has been defined so far, by Lemma

we may let
N
be large enough so that for all

and

such that

, we have that:

\displaystyle\langle x_{0},\dots,x_{n-1},x\rangle\cap\langle A_{k}\rangle=\langle x_{0},\dots,x_{n-1}\rangle\cap\langle A_{k}\rangle

We then let
x_{n}\in\langle D_{\Gamma(n)}\rangle\cap\langle B_{0}/N\rangle
be any element.
D
is almost disjoint from
A_{k}
for all
k<m
, as:

\displaystyle\langle D\rangle\cap\langle A_{k}\rangle

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{i<\omega}\langle x_{0},\dots,x_{i}\rangle\cap\langle A_{k}\rangle=\langle x_{0},\dots,x_{k-1}\rangle\cap\langle A_{k}\rangle.

Let
B_{n}^{m}:=(x_{i})_{i<\omega,\Gamma(i)\geq n}
Then
B_{n}^{m}\leq B_{n}
,
(B_{n}^{m})_{n<\omega}
is a
\leq
-decreasing sequence, and

is almost disjoint from

for all

.
∎

Let
C_{m}\leq B_{m}^{m}
be a weak diagonalisation of
(B_{n}^{m})_{n<\omega}
. By our assumption at the beginning, there exists some
A_{m}\in\cal{A}
which is compatible with
C_{m}
, and we may assume that
C_{m}\leq A_{m}
. Since
C_{m}\leq B_{m}^{m}
, and
B_{m}^{m}
is almost disjoint from
A_{k}
for all
k<m
, we have that
A_{k}\neq A_{m}
for all
k<m
. Having completed the induction, we let
C
be a weak diagonalisation of
(C_{m})_{m<\omega}
. Then
C\leq^{*}C_{0}\leq^{*}B_{n}
for any

C

. Since
C\leq^{*}C_{m}\leq A_{m}
for any

is compatible with infinitely many elements of

.
∎

Proposition 4.12
.

Let
\cal{A}\subseteq\cal{R}
be an almost disjoint family. Then
\cal{H}^{-}(\cal{A})
is a selective semicoideal.

Proof.

By Proposition
4.11
, it remains to show that
\cal{H}^{-}(\cal{A})
is a (q)-semicoideal. Let

, and if

is defined, let
m
be the least integer such that

. We then define

satisfies the conclusion of the (q)-property for

and
(I_{m})_{m<\omega}
.

Suppose there is no
B\leq A
which is almost disjoint from every element of
\cal{A}
. By Lemma

, there exists an infinite
\{A_{n}:n<\omega\}\subseteq\cal{A}
and an infinite disjoint family

such that

for all

. Let
\Gamma:\omega\to\omega
be any infinite-to-one surjection. We write
A=(x_{n})_{n<\omega}
, and define

as follows: If
(y_{k})_{k<n}
has been defined, we let
m
be the least integer such that

and

are compatible for all

, and since

and

.

5.1.
Block-splitting families

We introduce/recall the cardinal invariants which are relevant to our proof of Theorem

It turns out that both of these cardinals are closely related to

, then we may apply Lemma
5.6
to obtain some
A_{\alpha}^{\prime}\leq D
which is almost disjoint from every element of
\cal{B}
.

Claim
.

(1)

\langle A_{\alpha}^{\prime}\rangle\setminus Z_{\xi}^{\sigma_{\alpha}(\xi)}
is small.

(2)

For all
\beta<\alpha
,
A_{\alpha}^{\prime}
and
A_{\beta}
are almost disjoint.

Proof.

(1)

Note that
\langle A_{\alpha}^{\prime}\rangle\setminus Z_{\xi}^{\sigma_{\alpha}(\xi)}=\langle A_{\alpha}^{\prime}\rangle\cap Z_{\xi}^{1-\sigma_{\alpha}(\xi)}
. We have that
\langle A_{\alpha}^{\prime}\rangle\cap Z_{\xi}^{1-\sigma_{\alpha}(\xi)}\setminus\bigcup_{A\in\cal{F}_{\xi}}\langle A\rangle
is small by above, and since
A_{\alpha}^{\prime}
is almost disjoint from every
A\in\cal{F}_{\xi}
, we have that:

\displaystyle\langle A_{\alpha}^{\prime}\rangle\cap Z_{\xi}^{1-\sigma_{\alpha}(\xi)}\cap\bigcup_{A\in\cal{F}_{\xi}}\langle A\rangle

\displaystyle\subseteq\bigcup_{A\in\cal{F}_{\xi}}\langle A_{\alpha}^{\prime}\rangle\cap\langle A\rangle.

By Lemma
5.4
,
\langle A_{\alpha}\rangle\cap\langle A\rangle
is very small for each
A\in\cal{F}_{\xi}
, so
\langle A_{\alpha}^{\prime}\rangle\cap Z_{\xi}^{1-\sigma_{\alpha}(\xi)}
is small.

(2)

Let
\beta+n<\alpha
, where
\beta
is a limit ordinal. If
A_{\beta+n}\in\cal{B}_{0}
, then
A_{\alpha}
is almost disjoint from
A_{\beta+n}
by construction, so we are done. Now assume that
A_{\beta+n}\notin W
. Let
\xi
be the least ordinal such that
\sigma_{\beta}(\xi)\neq\sigma_{\alpha}(\xi)
. By induction hypothesis, we have that
W:=\langle A_{\beta}^{\prime}\rangle\setminus Z_{\xi}^{\sigma_{\beta}(\xi)}=\langle A_{\beta}^{\prime}\rangle\setminus Z_{\xi}^{1-\sigma_{\alpha}(\xi)}
is small (and hence very small). Since
A_{\beta+n}\leq A_{\beta}^{\prime}
, we have that for some small set
W^{\prime}
:

\displaystyle\langle A_{\alpha}^{\prime}\rangle\cap\langle A_{\beta+n}\rangle

\displaystyle\subseteq W\cup(\langle A_{\alpha}^{\prime}\rangle\cap Z_{\xi}^{1-\sigma_{\alpha}(\xi)})

\displaystyle\subseteq W\cup W^{\prime}\cup\bigcup_{A\in F_{\xi}}\langle A_{\alpha}^{\prime}\rangle\cap\langle A\rangle,

which is also small as all the components in the union are very small, as desired.

∎

We complete the induction by letting
\{A_{\alpha+n}:n<\omega\}
be any infinite disjoint family below
A_{\alpha}^{\prime}
. We note that
\cal{A}
is a mad family - otherwise, if
B
is almost disjoint from every element of
\cal{A}
, then
B\in\cal{H}^{-}(\cal{A}_{\alpha})
for all
\alpha<\mathfrak{c}
. Let
\alpha
be any limit ordinal such that
B_{\alpha}=B
, and we have that
A_{\alpha}\leq B
, a contradiction. It remains to show that
\cal{A}
is full.

Let
Y\subseteq E
, and suppose that
Y
is
\cal{H}(\cal{A})
-dense below some
B\in\cal{H}(\cal{A})
. Since
\cal{H}(\cal{A})=\bigcup_{\alpha<\mathfrak{c}}\cal{H}(\cal{A}_{\alpha})
, we may let
\alpha
be large enough so that
(Y_{\alpha},B_{\alpha})=(Y,B)
and
B\in\cal{H}(\cal{A}_{\alpha})
. This means that in the induction step, we have either case (b) or (c). Observe that (c) is not possible -
A_{\alpha}^{\prime}\leq B_{\alpha}
is almost disjoint from every element of
\cal{C}_{\alpha}
, and
A_{\alpha}^{\prime}\in\cal{H}(\cal{A})
as
A_{\alpha}
is compatible with
A_{\alpha+n}
for all
n
. However, there is no
B^{\prime}\leq A_{\alpha}^{\prime}
in which
\langle B^{\prime}\rangle\subseteq Y_{\alpha}
, contradicting that
Y
is
\cal{H}(\cal{A})
-dense below
B
. This means that case (b) must have occurred in this induction step. Since
B_{\alpha^{\prime}}
is compatible with
A_{\alpha+n}
for all
n
,
B_{\alpha^{\prime}}\in\cal{H}(\cal{A})
and
\langle B_{\alpha}^{\prime}\rangle\subseteq\langle Y_{\alpha}\rangle
, as desired.
∎

We remark that we may adjust the proof of Theorem
1.5
to obtain a completely separable mad family of block subspaces.

Definition 5.7
.

A mad family
\cal{A}\subseteq E^{[\infty]}
is
completely separable
if for all
Y\subseteq E
such that
\langle Y\rangle\in\cal{I}^{++}(\cal{A})
, there exists some
A\in\cal{A}
such that
\langle A\rangle\subseteq\langle Y\rangle
.

If we amend the induction step of the proof such that instead of splitting into scenarios (a)-(c), we let
B
be any element of
\cal{H}^{-}(\cal{A}_{\alpha})
such that
\langle B\rangle\subseteq\langle Y\rangle
, we obtain a completely separable mad family of block subspaces assuming
\mathfrak{a}_{\mathrm{vec},\mathbb{F}}^{*}=\mathfrak{a}_{\mathrm{vec},\mathbb{F}}
or
\mathfrak{p}=\max\{\mathfrak{b},\mathfrak{s}\}
.

6.
Topological Ramsey spaces and the abstract Mathias forcing

We dedicate this section to an overview of the axioms of topological Ramsey spaces, and the abstract Mathias forcing developed in
[
11
]
.

6.1.
Topological Ramsey spaces

We recap the four axioms presented by Todorčević in
[
19
]
, which are sufficient conditions for a triple
(\cal{R},\leq,r)
to be a topological Ramsey space. Here,
\cal{R}
is a non-empty set,
\leq
be a quasi-order on
\cal{R}
, and
r:\cal{R}\times\omega\to\cal{AR}
is a function. We also define a sequence of maps
r_{n}:\cal{R}\to\cal{AR}
by
r_{n}(A):=r(A,n)
for all
A\in\cal{R}
. Let
\cal{AR}_{n}\subseteq\cal{AR}
be the image of
r_{n}
(i.e.
a\in\cal{AR}_{n}
iff
a=r_{n}(A)
for some
A\in\cal{R}
).

The four axioms are as follows:

(A1)

(1)

r_{0}(A)=\emptyset
for all
A\in\cal{AR}
.

(2)

A\neq B
implies
r_{n}(A)\neq r_{n}(B)
for some
n
.

(3)

r_{n}(A)=r_{m}(B)
implies
n=m
and
r_{k}(A)=r_{k}(B)
for all
k<n
.

For each
a\in\cal{AR}
, let
\operatorname{lh}(a)
denote the unique
n
in which
a\in\cal{AR}_{n}
. By Axiom
A1
(3), this
n
is well-defined.

(A2)

There is a quasi-ordering
\leq_{\mathrm{fin}}
on
\cal{AR}
such that:

(1)

\{a\in\cal{AR}:a\leq_{\mathrm{fin}}b\}
is finite for all
b\in\cal{AR}
.

(2)

A\leq B
iff
\forall n\exists m[r_{n}(A)\leq_{\mathrm{fin}}r_{m}(B)]
.

(3)

\forall a,b\in\cal{AR}[a\sqsubseteq b\wedge b\leq_{\mathrm{fin}}c\to\exists d\sqsubseteq c[a\leq_{\mathrm{fin}}d]]
.

(A3)

We may define the
Ellentuck neighbourhoods
as follows: For any
A\in\cal{R}
,
a\in\cal{AR}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we let:

\displaystyle[a,A]

\displaystyle:=\{B\in\cal{R}:B\leq A\wedge\exists n[r_{n}(B)=a]\},

\displaystyle[n,A]

\displaystyle:=[r_{n}(A),A].

Then the depth function defined by, for
B\in\cal{R}
and
a\in\cal{AR}
:

\displaystyle 0pt_{B}(a):=\begin{cases}\min\{n<\omega:a\leq_{\mathrm{fin}}r_{n}(B)\},&\text{if such $n$ exists},\\
\infty,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

satisfies the following:

(1)

If
0pt_{B}(a)<\infty
, then for all
A\in[0pt_{B}(a),B]
,
[a,A]\neq\emptyset
.

(2)

If
A\leq B
and
[a,A]\neq\emptyset
, then there exists
A^{\prime}\in[0pt_{B}(a),B]
such that
\emptyset\neq[a,A^{\prime}]\subseteq[a,A]
.

For each
A\in\cal{R}
, we let:

\displaystyle\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}A:=\{a\in\cal{AR}:\exists n[a\leq_{\mathrm{fin}}r_{n}(A)]\}.

If
a\in\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}A
, we also define:

\displaystyle\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}[a,A]

\displaystyle:=\{b\in\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}A:a\sqsubseteq b\},

\displaystyle r_{n}[a,A]

\displaystyle:=\{b\in\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}[a,A]:\operatorname{lh}(b)=n\}.

(A4)

If
0pt_{B}(a)<\infty
and if
\cal{O}\subseteq\cal{AR}_{\operatorname{lh}(a)+1}
, then there exists
A\in[0pt_{B}(a),B]
such that
r_{\operatorname{lh}(a)+1}[a,A]\subseteq\cal{O}
or
r_{\operatorname{lh}(a)+1}[a,A]\subseteq\cal{O}^{c}
.

Note that by axiom
A1
, we may identify each element
A\in\cal{R}
as a sequence of elements of
\cal{AR}
, via the map
A\mapsto(r_{n}(A))_{n<\omega}
. Therefore, we may identify
\cal{R}
as a subset of
\cal{AR}^{\mathbb{N}}
.

Definition 6.1
.

A triple
(\cal{R},\leq,r)
is said to be a
closed triple
if
\cal{R}
is a metrically closed subset of
\cal{AR}^{\mathbb{N}}
.

A closed triple satisfying
(\cal{R},\leq,r)
satisfying
A1
-
A4
is a topological Ramsey space, as it satisfies the abstract Ellentuck theorem - that is, a subset
\cal{X}\subseteq\cal{R}
is Ramsey iff it has the property of Baire relative to the Ellentuck topology.

We list some commonly used terminologies and notations for topological Ramsey spaces.

•

If
A\leq B
, we say that
A
is a
reduction
of
B
.

•

\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}A:=\{b\in\cal{AR}:\exists n[b\leq_{\mathrm{fin}}r_{n}(A)]\}
.

•

\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}[a,A]:=\{b\in\cal{AR}:a\sqsubseteq b\wedge b\in\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}A\}
.

Mathias’ original proof of Theorem
1.6
uses the (original) Mathias forcing localised to a coideal
\cal{H}
. As we shall use a similar approach to prove Theorem
1.7
, it is necessary to extend the notion of a coideal to topological Ramsey spaces.

Definition 6.2

(Definition 3.1,
[
11
]
)
.

Let
(\cal{R},\leq,r)
be a closed triple satisfying
A1
-
A4
. A set
\cal{H}\subseteq\cal{R}
is a
coideal
if it satisfies the following:

(1)

(Upward-Closure) If
A\in\cal{H}
and
A\leq B
, then
B\in\cal{H}
.

(2)

(
A3
mod
\cal{H}
) For all
A\in\cal{H}
and
a\in\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}A
, we have that:

(a)

[a,B]\cap\cal{H}\neq\emptyset
for all
B\in[0pt_{A}(a),A]\cap\cal{H}
.

(b)

If
B\in\cal{H}\mathord{\upharpoonright}A
and
[a,B]\neq\emptyset
, then there exists some
A^{\prime}\in[0pt_{A}(a),A]\cap\cal{H}
such that
\emptyset\neq[a,A^{\prime}]\subseteq[a,B]
.

(3)

(
A4
mod
\cal{H}
) Fix
A\in\cal{H}
and
a\in\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}A
. For any
\cal{O}\subseteq\cal{AR}_{\operatorname{lh}(a)+1}
, there exists
B\in[0pt_{A}(a),A]\cap\cal{H}
such that
r_{\operatorname{lh}(a)+1}[a,B]\subseteq\cal{O}
or
r_{\operatorname{lh}(a)+1}[a,B]\subseteq\cal{O}^{c}
.

A notion of semiselectivity was also given as Definition 3.4-3.7 in
[
11
]
. We propose a slight variant of this definition.

Definition 6.3
.

Let
(\cal{R},\leq,r)
be an
A2
-space, and let
\cal{H}\subseteq\cal{R}
be a semicoideal. Let
A\in\cal{H}
and
a\in\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}A
.

(1)

A family of subsets
\vec{\cal{D}}=\{\cal{D}_{b}\}_{b\in\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}[a,A]}
is
dense open below
[a,A]
in
\cal{H}
if for all
b\in\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}[b,A]
,
\cal{D}_{b}
is a
\leq
-downward closed subset of
\cal{H}\mathord{\upharpoonright}[b,A]
, and for all
B\in\cal{H}\mathord{\upharpoonright}[b,A]
, there exists some
C\in\cal{H}\mathord{\upharpoonright}[b,B]
such that
C\in\cal{D}_{b}
.

(2)

Let
\vec{\cal{D}}=\{\cal{D}_{b}\}_{b\in\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}[a,A]}
be dense open below
[a,A]
in
\cal{H}
. We say that
B\in\cal{H}\mathord{\upharpoonright}[a,A]

diagonalises

\vec{\cal{D}}
if for all
b\in\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}[a,A]
, there exists some
A_{b}\in\cal{D}_{b}
such that
[b,B]\subseteq[b,A_{b}]
.

(3)

We say that
\cal{H}
is
semiselective
if for all
A\in\cal{H}
,
a\in\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}A
, every dense open family below
[a,A]
in
\cal{H}
has a diagonalisation in
\cal{H}
.

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
(\cal{R},\leq,r)
be a closed triple satisfying
A1
-
A4
. Then
\cal{R}
is a semiselective coideal.

This is stated as Lemma 3.11 of
[
21
]
. We replicate the proof here.

Proof.

Note that
\cal{R}
is a coideal by definition. Fix some
A\in\cal{R}
and
a\in\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}A
. Suppose that
\vec{\cal{D}}=\{\cal{D}_{b}\}_{b\in\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}[a,A]}
is dense open below
[a,A]
. We shall define a fusion sequence
(A_{n})_{n<\omega}
in
[a,A]
, with
a_{n+1}:=r_{\operatorname{lh}(a)+n+1}(A_{n})
, such that
A_{n+1}\in[a_{n+1},A_{n}]
: Let
A_{0}:=A
, and suppose that
A_{n}
has been defined. Let
\{b_{i}:i<N\}
enumerate the set of all
b\in\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}A_{n}
such that
a\sqsubseteq b
and
b\leq_{\mathrm{fin}}a_{n+1}
. Let
A_{n+1}^{0}:=A_{n}
. If
A_{n+1}^{i}\in[a_{n+1},A_{n}]
has been defined, let
B_{n+1}\in\cal{D}_{b_{i}}
be such that
B_{n+1}\in[b_{i},A_{n+1}^{i}]
, which exists as
\cal{D}_{b_{i}}
is dense open in
[b_{i},A]
. By
A3
, we then let
A_{n+1}^{i+1}\in[a_{n+1},A_{n}^{i}]
such that
[b_{i},A_{n+1}^{i+1}]\subseteq[b_{i},B_{n+1}]
. We complete the induction by letting
A_{n+1}:=A_{n+1}^{N}
. Let
B
be the limit of the fusion sequence
(A_{n})_{n<\omega}
, and we have that
B
diagonalises
\vec{\cal{D}}
.
∎

We will see that the semiselectivity property plays an important role in both combinatorial forcing and the abstract Mathias forcing.

6.2.
Combinatorial forcing

Let
(\cal{R},\leq,r)
be a closed triple satisfying
A1
-
A4
, and let
\cal{H}\subseteq\cal{R}
be a coideal. We also fix a subset
\cal{X}\subseteq\cal{R}
. Unless stated otherwise, we assume that
\cal{H}
is semiselective.

Definition 6.5
.

Let
A\in\cal{H}
and let
a\in\cal{AR}
.

(1)

We say that
A

accepts

a
if
[a,A]\subseteq\cal{X}
.

(2)

We say that
A

rejects

a
if
a\in\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}A
, and for all
B\in\cal{H}\mathord{\upharpoonright}[a,A]
,
B
does not accept
a
.

(3)

We say that
A

decides

a
if
A
accepts or rejects
a
.

This localised variant of combinatorial forcing for topological Ramsey spaces was introduced in
[
11
]
, along with various basic properties of this combinatorial forcing. The details of the proofs of these properties with our definition of semiselectivity (Definition
6.3
) remains mostly unchanged, but we shall provide an overview for the sake of completion.

Lemma 6.6
.

Let
A\in\cal{H}
and let
a\in\cal{AR}
.

(1)

If
a\notin\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}A
,
A
accepts
a
.

(2)

If
A
accepts
a
and
B\in\cal{H}\mathord{\upharpoonright}A
, then
B
accepts
a
.

(3)

If
A
rejects
a
,
B\in\cal{H}\mathord{\upharpoonright}A
and
a\in\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}B
, then
B
rejects
a
.

(4)

If
A
decides
a
and
B\in\cal{H}\mathord{\upharpoonright}A
, then
B
decides
a
.

(5)

If
a\in\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}A
, then there exists some
B\in\cal{H}\mathord{\upharpoonright}[0pt_{A}(a),A]
which decides
B
.

(6)

If
a\in\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}A
and
A
decides
a
, then for all
B\in\cal{H}\mathord{\upharpoonright}[0pt_{A}(a),A]
,
B
decides
a
in the same way
A
does.

(7)

A
accepts
a
iff
A
accepts every
b\in r_{\operatorname{lh}(a)+1}[a,A]
.

Proof.

The proofs are straightforward and mostly the same as the non-localised variant of combinatorial forcing - we refer readers to Lemma 4.31 of
[
19
]
.
∎

Lemma 6.7
.

For all
A\in\cal{H}
and
a\in\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}A
, then there exists some
B\in\cal{H}\mathord{\upharpoonright}[a,A]
which decides every
b\in\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}[a,A]
.

Proof.

For each
b\in\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}[a,A]
, let:

\displaystyle\cal{D}_{b}:=\{B\in\cal{H}\mathord{\upharpoonright}[b,A]:B\text{ decides }b\}.

By Lemma
6.6
(5),
\cal{D}_{b}
is dense open in
\cal{H}\mathord{\upharpoonright}[b,A]
. Since
\cal{H}
is semiselective, there exists some
B\in\cal{H}\mathord{\upharpoonright}[a,A]
which diagonalises
\{\cal{D}_{b}\}_{b\in\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}[a,A]}
. If
b\in\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}B
, then
[b,B]\subseteq[b,A_{b}]
for some
A_{b}\in\cal{D}_{b}
. Since
A_{b}
decides
b
,
B\in\cal{H}\mathord{\upharpoonright}A_{b}
and
b\in\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}B
, by Lemma
6.6
(2) and (3),
B
decides
b
as well. Note that if
b\notin\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}B
, then
[b,B]=\emptyset
, so
B
trivially decides
b
.
∎

Lemma 6.8
.

If
A
rejects
a
, then there exists some
B\in\cal{H}\mathord{\upharpoonright}[a,A]
which rejects every
b\in r_{\operatorname{lh}(a)+1}[a,A]
.

Proof.

By Lemma
6.7
, we may let
A^{\prime}\in\cal{H}[a,A]
decide every
b\in\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}[a,A]
. Let:

\displaystyle\cal{O}:=\{b\in r_{\operatorname{lh}(a)+1}[a,A]:A^{\prime}\text{ rejects }b\}.

By
A4
mod
\cal{H}
, there exists some
B\in\cal{H}\mathord{\upharpoonright}[a,A]
such that
r_{\operatorname{lh}(a)+1}[a,B]\subseteq\cal{O}
or
r_{\operatorname{lh}(a)+1}[a,B]\subseteq\cal{O}^{c}
. It remains to show that the latter case is impossible. Since
A^{\prime}
decides every
b\in\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}A
, so does
B
. Therefore, if
r_{\operatorname{lh}(a)+1}[a,B]
then
B
accepts every
b\in r_{\operatorname{lh}(a)+1}[a,B]
. By Lemma
6.6
(7), this implies that
B
accepts
a
, contradicting that
A
rejects
a
.
∎

Lemma 6.9
.

If
A
rejects
a
, then there exists some
B\in\cal{H}\mathord{\upharpoonright}[a,A]
which rejects every
b\in\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}[a,A]
.

Proof.

Again, by Lemma
6.7
we may let
A^{\prime}\in\cal{H}\mathord{\upharpoonright}[a,A]
decide every
b\in\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}[a,A]
. For each
b\in\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}[b,A]
, we let:

\displaystyle\cal{D}_{b}:=\{B\in\cal{H}\mathord{\upharpoonright}[b,A]:\text{$B$ accepts $b$ or $B$ rejects every $b^{\prime}\in r_{\operatorname{lh}(b)+1}[b,A]$}\}.

By Lemma
6.8
,
\cal{D}_{b}
is dense open in
\cal{H}\mathord{\upharpoonright}[a,A]
, so by the semiselectivity property of
\cal{H}
there exists some
B\in\cal{H}\mathord{\upharpoonright}[a,A]
which diagonalises
\{\cal{D}_{b}\}_{b\in\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}[a,A]}
. We shall induct on
\operatorname{lh}(b)
and show that
B
rejects every
b\in\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}[a,B]
(if
b\in\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}[a,A]
but
b\notin\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}[a,B]
, then
B
trivially rejects
b
).

For the base case
\operatorname{lh}(b)=\operatorname{lh}(a)
(i.e.
b=a
), it follows from that
A
rejects
a
, and that
B\in\cal{H}\mathord{\upharpoonright}[a,A]
(cf. Lemma
6.6
(3)). If
B
rejects some
b\in\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}[a,A]
where
\operatorname{lh}(b)=n\geq\operatorname{lh}(a)
, then since
[b,B]\subseteq[b,A_{b}]
for some
A_{b}\in\cal{D}_{b}
, we have that
B
rejects every
b^{\prime}\in r_{\operatorname{lh}(a)+1}[b,B]
, completing the induction step. Therefore,
B
rejects every
b\in\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}[a,B]
.
∎

The abstract combinatorial forcing allows us to prove the abstract local Ellentuck theorem - see Theorem 3.12 of
[
11
]
. We do not require the full strength of this result in this paper. Instead, we give an outline of the proof if
\cal{H}
is semiselective, then every metrically open set
\cal{X}\subseteq\cal{R}
is
\cal{H}
-Ramsey.

Definition 6.10
.

A set
\cal{X}\subseteq\cal{R}
is
\cal{H}
-Ramsey
if for all
A\in\cal{R}
and
a\in\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}A
, there exists some
B\in[a,A]
such that
[a,B]\subseteq\cal{X}
or
[a,B]\subseteq\cal{X}^{c}
.

Proposition 6.11
.

Let
\cal{H}\subseteq\cal{R}
be a semiselective coideal. If
\cal{X}\subseteq\cal{R}
is metrically open, then
\cal{X}
is
\cal{H}
-Ramsey.

Proof.

Let
A\in\cal{H}
and let
a\in\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}A
. We consider combinatorial forcing w.r.t.
\cal{X}
. If
A
does not reject
a
, then
[a,B]\subseteq\cal{X}
for some
B\in\cal{H}\mathord{\upharpoonright}[a,A]
, so we’re done. Otherwise,
A
rejects
a
, so by Lemma
6.9
there exists some
B\in\cal{H}\mathord{\upharpoonright}[a,A]
such that
B
rejects every
b\in\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}[a,A]
. We claim that
[a,B]\subseteq\cal{X}^{c}
. Otherwise, we may let
C\in[a,B]\cap\cal{X}
. Since
\cal{X}
is metrically open, there exists some
b\in\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}[a,B]
such that
C\in[b]\subseteq\cal{X}
, where

. In particular, we have that
[b,B]\subseteq\cal{X}
, contradicting that
B
rejects
b
.
∎

6.3.
Abstract Mathias forcing

The abstract Mathias forcing localised to a coideal
\cal{H}
is defined as the forcing poset
(\mathbb{M}{H},\leq)
, where:

\displaystyle\mathbb{M}{H}=\{(a,A):A\in\cal{R}\text{ and }a\in\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}A\},

and that
(b,B)\leq(a,A)
iff
[b,B]\subseteq[a,A]
. We also write
(b,B)\leq_{0}(a,A)
if
(b,B)\leq(a,A)
and
b=a
.

Definition 6.12
.

An element
A\in\cal{R}
is said to be
\mathbb{M}{H}
-generic (over a ground model
\mathbf{V}
) if:

\displaystyle G_{A}:=\{(a,B)\in\mathbb{M}{H}:B\in[a,A]\}

is a generic filter of
\mathbb{M}{H}
(over
\mathbf{V}
).

Lemma 6.13
.

Let
\mathbb{M}{H}
be the Mathias forcing localised to a coideal
\cal{H}
.

(1)

If
G
is a generic filter of
\mathbb{M}{H}
, then there exists a unique
\mathbb{M}{H}
-generic
A_{G}\in\cal{R}
such that
G=\{(a,A)\in\mathbb{M}{H}:A_{G}\in[a,A]\}
.

(2)

If
A\in\cal{R}
, then
A_{G_{A}}=A
.

(3)

If
G
is a generic filter of
\mathbb{M}{H}
, then
G_{A_{G}}=G
.

Proof.

We only prove (1) - (2) follows from the uniqueness of
A_{G}
in (1), and (3) follow easily from the definitions. Let
\cal{D}_{n}:=\{(a,A)\in\mathbb{M}{H}:\operatorname{lh}(a)\geq n\}
. We see that
\cal{D}_{n}
is dense open in
\mathbb{M}{H}
for all
n
- if
(a,A)\notin\cal{D}_{n}
(i.e.
\operatorname{lh}(a)<n
) and
b\in r_{n}[a,A]
, then by
A3
mod
\cal{H}
we have that
[b,A]\cap\cal{H}\neq\emptyset
. Let
B\in[b,A]\cap\cal{H}
, and we have that
[b,B]\subseteq[a,A]
, so
(b,B)\leq(a,A)
and
(b,B)\in\cal{D}_{n}
. Therefore,
G\cap\cal{D}_{n}\neq\emptyset
for all
n
, and since
G
is a filter, for all
(a,A),(b,B)\in G
,
a\sqsubseteq b
or
b\sqsubseteq a
. Thus, there exists a unique
A_{G}\in\cal{R}
such that for all
n
,
(r_{n}(A_{G}),A)\in G
for some
A
. It’s easy to verify that
G=G_{A_{G}}
.
∎

Definition 6.14
.

Let
(\cal{R},\leq,r)
be a closed triple satisfying
A1
-
A4
, and let
\cal{H}\subseteq\cal{R}
be a coideal.

(1)

\mathbb{M}{H}
satisfies the

if whenever

is
\mathbb{M}{H}
-generic over a ground model
\mathbf{V}
and

is also
\mathbb{M}{H}
-generic over
\mathbf{V}
.

(2)

\mathbb{M}{H}
satisfies the
Prikry property
if whenever
\varphi
is a sentence in the forcing language, then we have that for all
p\in\mathbb{M}{H}
, there exists some
q\leq_{0}p
such that
q\Vdash\varphi
or
q\Vdash\neg\varphi
.

Given a subset
\cal{D}\subseteq\mathbb{M}{H}
, we let:

\displaystyle\overline{\cal{D}}

\displaystyle:=\bigcup_{(a,A)\in\cal{D}}[a,A].

Note that
\overline{\cal{D}}
is an Ellentuck-open subset of
\cal{R}
in
\mathbf{V}
. Thus, if
\cal{D}
is dense open in
\mathbb{M}{H}
, then for all
A\in\cal{H}
and

,
A

w.r.t.

(otherwise, by Lemma

there exists some

which rejects every

, contradicting that
\cal{D}
is dense in
\mathbb{M}{H}
). In other words, for all
A\in\cal{H}
and
a\in\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}A
, there exists some
B\in\cal{H}\mathord{\upharpoonright}[a,A]
such that
[a,B]\subseteq\overline{\cal{D}}
.

Notation 6.15
.

Given
a,b\in\cal{AR}
, we write:

\displaystyle b\preceq_{\mathrm{fin}}a\iff b\leq_{\mathrm{fin}}a\text{ and }\forall a^{\prime}\sqsubseteq a\,(a^{\prime}\neq a\to b\not\leq_{\mathrm{fin}}a^{\prime}).

Proposition 6.16
.

If
\cal{H}
is semiselective, then
\mathbb{M}{H}
has the Mathias property.

Proof.

Let
A_{G}
be an
\mathbb{M}{H}
-generic element of
\cal{R}
(over some ground model
\mathbf{V}
), and let
A_{H}\leq A_{G}
. We need to show that the filter:

\displaystyle H:=\{(a,A)\in\mathbb{M}{H}:A_{H}\in[a,A]\}

is generic. Let
\cal{D}\subseteq\mathbb{M}{H}
be a dense open subset (in the ground model
\mathbf{V}
). For each

, we let:

\displaystyle\cal{E}_{a}:=\{B\in\cal{H}:a\in\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}B\text{ and }\forall b\preceq_{\mathrm{fin}}a\,[b,B]\subseteq\overline{\cal{D}}\}.

Claim
.

For all
A\in\cal{H}
such that
a\in\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}A
,
\cal{E}_{a}
is dense open in
\cal{H}\mathord{\upharpoonright}[a,A]
.

Proof.

It’s clear that
\cal{E}_{a}
is open, so shall show that it is dense in
\cal{H}\mathord{\upharpoonright}[a,A]
. By the comment above, let
A^{\prime}\in\cal{H}\mathord{\upharpoonright}[a,A]
such that
[a,A^{\prime}]\subseteq\overline{\cal{D}}
. Let
\{b_{i}:1\leq i\leq N\}
be an enumeration of all

. Let
A_{0}:=A^{\prime}
, and suppose that we have defined

. We have that
b_{i+1}\in\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}A_{i}
(as
b_{i+1}\leq_{\mathrm{fin}}a\sqsubseteq A_{i}
), so by the observation stated prior to the claim there exists some
A_{i+1}^{\prime}\in\cal{H}\mathord{\upharpoonright}[b_{i+1},A_{i}]
which accepts
b_{i+1},A_{i}
w.r.t.
\overline{\cal{D}}
, i.e.
[b_{i+1},A_{i+1}^{\prime}]\subseteq\overline{\cal{D}}
. By
A3
mod
\cal{H}
, there exists some
A_{i+1}\in\cal{H}\mathord{\upharpoonright}[a,A]
such that
[a_{i+1},A_{i+1}]\subseteq[a_{i+1},A_{i+1}^{\prime}]
. This completes the induction, and
(a,A_{N})\in\cal{D}^{\prime}
, as desired.
∎

Fix any
(a,A)\in G
. Since
\cal{H}
is semiselective, for all
(b,B)\leq(a,A)
in
\mathbb{M}{H}
, there exists some
C\in\cal{H}\mathord{\upharpoonright}[b,B]
which diagonalises
\vec{\cal{E}}_{b}:=\{\cal{E}_{c}\}_{c\in\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}[b,A]}
within
A
. That is, the set:

\displaystyle\cal{D}^{\prime}:=\{(b,B)\in\mathbb{M}{H}:\text{$B$ diagonalises $\vec{\cal{E}}_{b}$ within $A$}\}

is dense open in
\mathbb{M}{H}
. Since
G
is generic, we may let
(b,B)\in G\cap\cal{D}^{\prime}
. Then
A_{G}\in[b,B]
, and for all
c\in\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}[b,A_{G}]
and

,
[d,A_{G}]\subseteq[d,B]\subseteq\overline{\cal{D}}
. Let

be the least integer such that
0pt_{A_{G}}(r_{n}(A_{H}))=:m\geq\operatorname{lh}(b)
. Then
r_{n}(A_{H})\preceq_{\mathrm{fin}}r_{m}(A_{G})\in\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}[b,B]
, so
A_{H}\in[r_{n}(A_{H}),A_{G}]\subseteq[r_{n}(A_{H}),B]\subseteq\overline{\cal{D}}
. This implies that
(r_{n}(A_{H}),B)\in H\cap\cal{D}
, and since
\cal{D}
is arbitrary,

is a generic filter as required.
∎

Proposition 6.17
.

If
\cal{H}
is semiselective, then
\mathbb{M}{H}
has the Prikry property.

Proof.

Let
\varphi
be a sentence in the forcing language. Let:

\displaystyle\cal{D}

\displaystyle:=\{q\in\mathbb{M}{H}:q\Vdash\varphi\}.

We consider doing combinatorial forcing w.r.t. the set
\overline{\cal{D}}
. Let
(a,A)\in\mathbb{M}{H}
, and by Lemma
6.6
(2) and (3) there exists some
B\in[a,A]
which decides
a
. If
B
accepts
a
, then
[a,B]\subseteq\overline{\cal{D}}
, so
(a,B)\in\cal{D}
as
\cal{D}
is open. In this case,
(a,B)\leq_{0}(a,A)
and

. Otherwise, by Lemma

there exists some

which rejects every

,

7.
Topological Ramsey space of block subspaces

By Hindman’s theorem, the pigeonhole principle hold for
E^{[\infty]}
if
|\mathbb{F}|=2
. That is, for all
Y\subseteq E
and
A\in E^{[\infty]}
, there exists some
B\leq A
such that
\langle B\rangle\subseteq Y
or
\langle B\rangle\subseteq Y^{c}
. Consequently, if for each
A:=(x_{n})_{n<\omega}\in E^{[\infty]}
we let:

\displaystyle r_{n}(A):=(x_{0},\dots,x_{n-1}),

then
(E^{[\infty]},\leq,r)
is a closed triple satisfying
A1
-
A4
. We shall discuss the intersection of the local Ramsey theory of
(E^{[\infty]},\leq,r)
as a topological Ramsey space, and the local Ramsey theory of block subspaces developed Smythe, followed by an application of the abstract Mathias forcing to prove Theorem
1.7
.

7.1.
Semicoideals of block subspaces

Readers may notice that there are two separate definitions for a coideal
\cal{H}\subseteq E^{[\infty]}
, i.e. Definition
3.3
and Definition
6.2
. It turns out that these two definitions are equivalent. We remark that in this setting, if
A\in E^{[\infty]}
and
a\in E^{[<\infty]}\mathord{\upharpoonright}A
(i.e.
a\in E^{[<\infty]}
and
\langle a\rangle\subseteq\langle A\rangle
), we have that
0pt_{A}(a)=d_{A}(a)
(see Notation
2.2
).

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
\cal{H}\subseteq E^{[\infty]}
. The following are equivalent:

(1)

\cal{H}
is a semicoideal.

(2)

\cal{H}
is
\leq
-upward closed and
A3
mod
\cal{H}
hold.

Proof.

On one hand, if
A\in\cal{H}
and
[a,A]\subseteq[a,B]
for some
a\in\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}A
, then by
A3
mod
\cal{H}
there exists some
C\in[a,A]\cap\cal{H}
. Then
C\in[a,B]
, and in particular
C\leq B
, so
B\in\cal{H}
by
\leq
-upward closure. Conversely, suppose that
\cal{H}
is
\leq^{*}
-upward closed. Let
A\in\cal{H}
and
a\in E^{[<\infty]}\mathord{\upharpoonright}A
.

(1)

For any
B\in[d_{A}(a),A]\cap\cal{H}
, we have that
B\leq^{*}B/a
(as
B/a\leq B/a
), so
B/a\in\cal{H}
. Since
B/a\leq a^{\frown}(B/a)
, we have that
a^{\frown}(B/a)\in\cal{H}
by
\leq
-upward closure. But
a^{\frown}(B/a)\leq^{*}B
, so
a^{\frown}(B/a)\in[a,B]\cap\cal{H}
as required.

(2)

Suppose that
B\in\cal{H}\mathord{\upharpoonright}A
and
[a,B]\neq\emptyset
. Let
N:=d_{A}(a)
, and let
M
be large enough so that
B/M\leq A/N
. Then
C:=r_{n}(A)^{\frown}(B/m)\in[d_{A}(a),A]\cap\cal{H}
is well-defined, and:

\displaystyle D\in[a,C]

\displaystyle\implies a\sqsubseteq D\wedge D\leq C

\displaystyle\implies a\sqsubseteq D\wedge D/a\leq C/a

\displaystyle\implies a\sqsubseteq D\wedge D/a\leq B/M

\displaystyle\implies a\sqsubseteq D\wedge D/a\leq A/N

\displaystyle\implies D\in[a,A],

as desired.

∎

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
\cal{H}\subseteq E^{[\infty]}
be a semicoideal. Then
\cal{H}
is a coideal (as in Definition
3.3
) iff
A4
mod
\cal{H}
holds.

Proof.

Definition
3.3
is exactly the statement
A4
mod
\cal{H}
for
a=\emptyset
, so it suffices to show that the converse holds. Let
A\in E^{[\infty]}
,
a\in E^{[<\infty]}\mathord{\upharpoonright}A
, and let
\cal{O}\subseteq E^{[\operatorname{lh}(a)+1]}
. Define:

\displaystyle Y:=\{x\in E:a<x\wedge a^{\frown}x\in\cal{O}\}.

Since
\cal{H}
is a coideal (as in Definition
3.3
), there exists some
B^{\prime}\leq A
such that
\langle B^{\prime}\rangle\subseteq Y
or
\langle B^{\prime}\rangle\subseteq Y^{c}
. Let
M
be large enough so that
B^{\prime}/M\leq A/d_{A}(a)
, and note that
a<B^{\prime}/M
. Let
B:=B^{\prime}/M
, and let
C:=a^{\frown}B\in[a,A]
. Observe that:

\displaystyle b\in r_{\operatorname{lh}(a)+1}[a,C]\iff b=a^{\frown}x\text{ for some $x\in\langle B\rangle$}.

Therefore, if
\langle B\rangle\subseteq Y
, then
r_{\operatorname{lh}(a)+1}[a,C]\subseteq\cal{O}
, and if
\langle B\rangle\subseteq Y^{c}

mod
\cal{H}
holds.
∎

Lemma 7.3

(1)

is semiselective (as in Definition

(2)

(1)
\implies
(2):

\{\cal{D}_{n}\}_{n<\omega}

\cal{H}
, and let

It’s clear that

for all
b\in\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}A

\cal{D}_{b}

\leq
-downward closed in
\cal{H}\mathord{\upharpoonright}[b,A]

\cal{D}_{b}
is dense in
\cal{H}\mathord{\upharpoonright}[b,A]

\implies
(1):

A\in\cal{H}

\displaystyle:=\{B/b:B\in\cal{D}_{b}\},

\cal{H}\mathord{\upharpoonright}A

. This implies that
\cal{D}_{n}
is dense open in
\cal{H}\mathord{\upharpoonright}A
, as it is a finite intersection of dense open subsets of
\cal{H}\mathord{\upharpoonright}A
. Let
B\in\cal{H}\mathord{\upharpoonright}A
, and let
b\in\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}[a,A]
. Then

, and

B\in\cal{H}\mathord{\upharpoonright}A

for all
n
. Observe that

\displaystyle=B/b^{\prime}=B/d_{B}(b^{\prime})\in\cal{D}_{d_{A}(r_{d_{B}(b^{\prime})}(B))}\subseteq\cal{D}_{d_{A}(b^{\prime})}=\cal{D}_{d_{A}(b)}\subseteq\cal{D}_{b}^{\prime},

.

Lemma 7.4
.

.

Since

is Mahlo, we may fix some inaccessible

D\leq C
and
D\leq A
. Then
D\in[\emptyset,C]\cap\overline{\cal{A}}
, another contradiction.
∎

8.
Further remarks and open problems

While there are various results about the cardinal
\mathfrak{a}_{\mathrm{vec},\mathbb{F}}
, the cardinal
\mathfrak{a}_{\mathrm{vec},\mathbb{F}}^{*}
is not well-understood. Readers may observe from the proof of Theorem
1.5
that the hypothesis
\max\{\mathfrak{b},\mathfrak{s}\}\leq\mathfrak{a}_{\mathrm{vec},\mathbb{F}}^{*}
is sufficient for the construction of a full mad family. Thus, a positive answer to the following problem would imply that there exists a full mad family of block subspaces in
\mathsf{ZFC}
.

Problem 8.1
.

Is
\max\{\mathfrak{b},\mathfrak{s}\}\leq\mathfrak{a}_{\mathrm{vec},\mathbb{F}}^{*}
true in
\mathsf{ZFC}
?

Most of the current approaches to the non-existence of mad families of
[\omega]^{\omega}
employs some Ramsey theoretic techniques (such as
[
8
]

[
10
]

[
15
]
). Consequently, it is difficult to use these approaches to prove the non-existence of mad families of block subspaces over
\mathbb{F}
for
|\mathbb{F}|>2
. Perhaps the approach highlighted in
[
20
]
may be used to answer the following question:

Problem 8.2
.

Let
\kappa
be an inaccessible cardinal, and let
G
be
\operatorname{Coll}(\omega,<\!\kappa)
-generic. Are there mad families of block subspaces (over any countable field) in
\mathbf{L}(\mathbb{R})^{\mathbf{V}[G]}
? What if
\kappa
is Mahlo?

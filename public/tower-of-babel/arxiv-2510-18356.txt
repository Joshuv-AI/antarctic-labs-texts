A Computational (Co)homological Approach to Contiguity Distance Thanks: The second author acknowledges support of the Deputación Provincial da Coruña (Exp. 2025000022903).
Enrique Macías-Virgós, Ángel Méndez-Vázquez, David Mosquera-Lois

Abstract
. We introduce a new algebraic invariant, the (co)homological distance , which provides computable lower bounds for the homotopic distance and refines the classical cup-length estimates. We then define the simplicial cohomological distance and prove a convergence theorem showing that, after sufficiently many barycentric subdivisions, it approximates the cohomological distance of the corresponding continuous maps. Several explicit computations are presented to illustrate the effectiveness of the proposed approach.



Introduction

In
[
5
]
, the notion of
homotopic distance

\mathrm{D}(f,g)
between two continuous maps
f,g\colon X\rightarrow Y
was introduced. This invariant is defined as the least integer
n\geq 0
for which there exists an open cover
\{U_{0},\dots,U_{n}\}
of
X
such that the restrictions
f_{|U_{j}},g_{|U_{j}}\colon U_{j}\rightarrow Y
are homotopic,
f_{|U_{j}}\simeq g_{|U_{j}}
, for all
j=0,\dots,n
.

The homotopic distance extends classical invariants from spaces to maps. In particular, it provides a unified framework that generalizes the Lusternik–Schnirelmann category and the topological complexity.

In the same work
[
5
]
, two of the authors also introduced the
contiguity or simplicial distance

\mathrm{s}\mathrm{D}(\varphi,\psi)
between two simplicial maps
\varphi,\psi\colon K\rightarrow L
. It is the least integer
n\geq 0
such that there exists a cover of
K
by subcomplexes
K_{0},\dots,K_{n}
with the property that
\varphi_{|K_{j}}
and
\psi_{|K_{j}}
lie in the same contiguity class,
\varphi_{|K_{j}}\sim\psi_{|K_{j}}
, for every
j=0,\dots,n
. This invariant was further investigated in
[
2
]
.

By construction, the homotopic distance is always bounded above by the contiguity distance. However, a major drawback of the contiguity distance is that it does not converge to the homotopic distance under subdivision, as illustrated in
[
3
, Example 4.6]
.

In this article we introduce a new auxiliary invariant, the
(co)homological distance
, which enjoys very good computational properties. We further define its simplicial version, the
simplicial (co)homological distance
, and prove that, unlike the simplicial distance, it converges to the (co)homological distance: after sufficiently many subdivisions, the simplicial (co)homological distance approximates the (co)homological one (Theorem
2.3
).

Another key feature of the (co)homological distance is that it provides a lower bound for the homotopic distance. Moreover, it is sharper than the classical cup-length (see Example
1.8
).

Conventions

Throughout this article,
R
denotes a commutative ring with a unit. All topological spaces and simplicial complexes are assumed to be path-connected. All simplicial complexes are assumed to be finite, as we are primarily interested in a computational perspective.

1.
(Co)Homological Distance

We introduce the cohomological and homological analogues of the homotopic distance. These invariants retain essential topological information while being more suitable for algebraic computation and provide better lower bounds than cup-length invariants.

1.1.
The (co)homological distance

We define the (co)homological distance between two continuous maps
f,g\colon X\rightarrow Y
.

Definition 1.1
.

The
cohomological (homological) distance
between
f
and
g
with coefficients in
R
, denoted by
\mathrm{H^{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }\mathrm{D}}(f,g;R)
(resp.
\mathrm{H_{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }\mathrm{D}}(f,g;R)
), is the least integer
n\geq 0
for which there exists an open cover
\{U_{0},\dots,U_{n}\}
of
X
such that
(f_{|U_{j}})^{*}=(g_{|U_{j}})^{*}\colon H^{m}(Y;R)\rightarrow H^{m}(U_{j};R)
(resp.
(f_{|U_{j}})_{*}=(g_{|U_{j}})_{*}\colon H_{m}(U_{j};R)\rightarrow H_{m}(Y;R)
) for all
m\geq 0
and for all
j=0,\dots,n
.

Remark 1
.

We always have
\mathrm{H^{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }\mathrm{D}}(f,g;R)\leq\mathrm{D}(f,g)
and
\mathrm{H_{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }\mathrm{D}}(f,g;R)\leq\mathrm{D}(f,g)
.

Example 1.2
.

Let
p\colon\mathbb{S}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{2}
be the Hopf fibration and let
\mathrm{c}\colon\mathbb{S}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{2}
be a constant map. Then
\mathrm{D}(p,\mathrm{c})=1
, whereas
\mathrm{H^{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }\mathrm{D}}(p,\mathrm{c};R)=0
and
\mathrm{H_{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }\mathrm{D}}(p,\mathrm{c};R)=0
. Hence, the above inequalities can be strict.

Let
\mathrm{id}\colon X\rightarrow X
be the identity map,
\mathrm{c}\colon X\rightarrow X
a constant map and
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}\colon X\times X\rightarrow X
the canonical projections. We define the
cohomological (resp. homological) Lusternik-Schnirelmann category
as
\mathrm{H^{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }cat}(X;R)=\mathrm{H^{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }\mathrm{D}}(\mathrm{id},\mathrm{c})
(resp.
\mathrm{H_{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }\mathrm{D}}(\mathrm{id},\mathrm{c})
) and the
cohomological (resp. homological) topological complexity

\mathrm{H^{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }TC}(X;R)=\mathrm{H^{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }\mathrm{D}}(\pi_{1},\pi_{2};R)
(resp.
\mathrm{H_{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }\mathrm{D}}(\pi_{1},\pi_{2};R)
). As a consequence, mimicking the approach in
[
5
]
, for any pair of maps
f,g\colon X\rightarrow Y
, we have

(1)

\mathrm{H^{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }\mathrm{D}}(f,g;R)\leq\mathrm{H^{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }cat}(X;R).

(2)

\mathrm{H^{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }\mathrm{D}}(f,g;R)\leq\mathrm{H^{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }TC}(Y;R).

Remark 2
.

The homological Lusternik-Schnirelmann category was introduced by Fox (
[
4
]
) and we recover it as a particular case.

1.2.
Cup-length

In
[
5
]
, the authors established the following cohomological lower bound for the homotopic distance. Let
\mathcal{J}(f,g;R)\subset H^{*}(X;R)
be the image of

f^{*}-g^{*}\colon H^{*}(Y;R)\rightarrow H^{*}(X;R).

Definition 1.3
.

We denote by
\mathrm{l.c.p.}\,\mathcal{J}(f,g;R)
the least integer
n\geq 0
such that every product of
n+1
elements of
\mathcal{J}(f,g;R)
vanishes.

Theorem 1.4
.

It holds that
\mathrm{l.c.p.}\,\mathcal{J}(f,g;R)\leq\mathrm{H^{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }\mathrm{D}}(f,g;R)
.

Proof.

Suppose
\mathrm{H^{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }\mathrm{D}}(f,g;R)=n
, and let
\{U_{0},\dots,U_{n}\}
be an open cover of
X
such that
(f_{|U_{j}})^{*}=(g_{|U_{j}})^{*}
for every
j=0,\dots,n
.
Consider an arbitrary product
x_{0}\smile\cdots\smile x_{n}
of
n+1
elements with
x_{k}\in\mathcal{J}(f,g;R)
for each
k=0,\dots,n
. For each open set
U=U_{k}
in the cover, consider the long exact sequence of the pair
(X,U)
:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{H^{m}(X,U;R)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{m}(X;R)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{m}(U;R)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{m}(Y;R)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j_{U}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{*}-g^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(f_{|U})^{*}-(g_{|U})^{*}}

Since
(f_{|U})^{*}-(g_{|U})^{*}=0
, we have
\mathcal{J}(f,g;R)\subseteq\ker i^{*}=\mathrm{im\,}j_{U}
.
Thus, for each element
x_{k}
of the product
x_{0}\smile\cdots\smile x_{n}
, there exists an element
\widetilde{x}_{k}\in H^{*}(X,U_{k};R)
such that
j_{U_{k}}(\widetilde{x}_{k})=x_{k}
. Consider the following commutative diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{H^{*}(X,U_{0};R)\otimes\cdots\otimes H^{*}(X,U_{n};R)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{H^{*}(X,\bigcup_{k=0}^{n}U_{k};R)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{*}(X;R)\otimes\cdots\otimes H^{*}(X;R)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{*}(X;R)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\smile}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{j_{U_{0}}}\otimes\cdots\otimes{j_{U_{n}}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j_{X}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\smile}

Since
\bigcup_{k=0}^{n}U_{k}=X
, we have
H^{*}(X,\bigcup_{k=0}^{n}U_{k};R)=H^{*}(X,X;R)=0
. Consequently,

\begin{split}x_{0}\smile\cdots\smile x_{n}&=j_{U_{0}}(\widetilde{x}_{0})\smile\cdots\smile j_{U_{n}}(\widetilde{x}_{n})\\
&=j_{X}(\widetilde{x}_{0}\smile\cdots\smile\widetilde{x}_{n})=j_{X}(0)=0.\end{split}

Therefore
\mathrm{l.c.p.}\,\mathcal{J}(f,g;R)\leq n=\mathrm{H^{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }\mathrm{D}}(f,g;R)
, as required.
∎

For the LS-category, taking
f=\mathrm{id}_{X}
and
g=\mathrm{c}
(a constant map), we have

\mathcal{J}(\mathrm{id}_{X},\mathrm{c};R)=H^{>0}(X;R),

so that
\mathrm{l.c.p.}\,\mathcal{J}(\mathrm{id}_{X},\mathrm{c};R)
coincides with the usual cup-length of
X
, denoted by
\mathrm{l.c.p.}\,(X;R)
. This shows that the cohomological category improves the classical cup-length lower bound:

\mathrm{l.c.p.}\,(X;R)\leq\mathrm{H^{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }cat}(X;R)\leq\mathop{\mathrm{cat}}(X).

For the topological complexity, we have the
zero divisors cup-length
of
X
, denoted
\mathrm{l.c.p.}\,(\ker\smile;R)
, defined as the least integer
n\geq 0
such that every product
x_{0}\cdot\ldots\cdot x_{n}
of
n+1
elements in the kernel of the cup product

\smile\colon H^{*}(X;R)\otimes H^{*}(X;R)\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow H^{*}(X;R)

vanishes.

Theorem 1.5
.

It holds that
\mathrm{l.c.p.}\,\mathcal{J}(f,g;R)=\mathrm{l.c.p.}\,\langle\mathcal{J}(f,g;R)\rangle
, where
\langle\mathcal{J}(f,g;R)\rangle
denotes the ideal generated by
\mathcal{J}(f,g;R)
.

Proof.

The inequality
\mathrm{l.c.p.}\,\mathcal{J}(f,g;R)\leq\mathrm{l.c.p.}\,\langle\mathcal{J}(f,g;R)\rangle
is clear. Let us prove the converse. We show that if there exists
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in\left\langle\mathcal{J}(f,g;R)\right\rangle
such that
x_{1}\smile\cdots\smile x_{n}\neq 0
, then
\mathrm{l.c.p.}\,\mathcal{J}(f,g;R)\geq n
.

Each
x_{i}\in\left\langle\mathcal{J}(f,g;R)\right\rangle
can be written as a finite sum

x_{i}=\sum_{k_{i}}(\alpha_{k_{i}}\smile(f^{*}(y_{k_{i}})-g^{*}(y_{k_{i}}))),\quad y_{k_{i}}\in H^{*}(Y;R),\quad\alpha_{k_{i}}\in H^{*}(X;R).

As the cup product is distributive, we have that

\begin{split}x_{i}\smile x_{j}&=\left(\sum_{k_{i}}\bigl(\alpha_{k_{i}}\smile(f^{*}(y_{k_{i}})-g^{*}(y_{k_{i}}))\bigr)\right)\smile\left(\sum_{k_{j}}\bigl(\alpha_{k_{j}}\smile(f^{*}(y_{k_{j}})-g^{*}(y_{k_{j}}))\bigr)\right)\\
&=\sum_{k_{i},k_{j}}\Bigl(\left(\alpha_{k_{i}}\smile\alpha_{k_{j}}\right)\smile\left((f^{*}(y_{k_{i}})-g^{*}(y_{k_{i}}))\smile(f^{*}(y_{k_{j}})-g^{*}(y_{k_{j}}))\right)\Bigr).\end{split}

Since the product

x_{1}\smile\cdots\smile x_{n}=\sum_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{n}}\left(\left(\mathop{\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{\scalebox{1.5}{$\smile$}}}\limits_{i=1}^{\raisebox{3.61664pt}{\scriptsize$n$}}\alpha_{k_{i}}\right)\smile\left(\mathop{\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{\scalebox{1.5}{$\smile$}}}\limits_{i=1}^{\raisebox{3.61664pt}{\scriptsize$n$}}(f^{*}(y_{k_{i}})-g^{*}(y_{k_{i}}))\right)\right)

is nonzero, there exists at least one tuple
(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})
whose summand is nonzero. In particular, for this tuple, the product

\mathop{\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{\scalebox{1.5}{$\smile$}}}\limits_{i=1}^{\raisebox{3.61664pt}{\scriptsize$n$}}(f^{*}(y_{k_{i}})-g^{*}(y_{k_{i}}))=(f^{*}(y_{k_{1}})-g^{*}(y_{k_{1}}))\smile\cdots\smile(f^{*}(y_{k_{n}})-g^{*}(y_{k_{n}}))

is nonzero, and therefore we conclude that
\mathrm{l.c.p.}\,\mathcal{J}(f,g;R)\geq n
.
∎

Corollary 1.6
.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a field. Then

\mathrm{l.c.p.}\,\mathcal{J}(\pi_{1},\pi_{2};\mathbb{F})=\mathrm{l.c.p.}\,(\ker\smile;\mathbb{F}).

Proof.

By the Künneth formula, if
z
is an element of
\mathcal{J}(\pi_{1},\pi_{2};\mathbb{F})
, then
z=x\otimes 1-1\otimes x
for some
x\in H^{*}(X;\mathbb{F})
so
z\in\ker\smile
.

Conversely, if
z=\sum_{i}a_{i}\otimes b_{i}\in\ker\smile
, we have

\begin{split}z=\sum_{i}a_{i}\otimes b_{i}&=\sum_{i}a_{i}(1\otimes b_{i}-b_{i}\otimes 1)+(a_{i}\smile b_{i})\otimes 1\\
&=\sum_{i}(a_{i}(1\otimes b_{i}-b_{i}\otimes 1))+\left(\sum_{i}(a_{i}\smile b_{i})\right)\otimes 1\\
&=\sum_{i}a_{i}(1\otimes b_{i}-b_{i}\otimes 1)\in\left\langle\mathcal{J}(\pi_{1},\pi_{2})\right\rangle.\end{split}

Therefore,
\left\langle\mathcal{J}(\pi_{1},\pi_{2})\right\rangle=\ker\smile
and the results follows from Proposition
1.5
.
∎

1.3.
Comparison between cohomological and homological distances

We compare the homological and cohomological versions of the distance. We prove that they coincide when coefficients are taken in a field, and we exhibit examples showing that no general inequality holds between them.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a field and
X
a topological space with finitely generated cohomology in each degree. Then

\mathrm{H_{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }\mathrm{D}}(f,g;\mathbb{F})=\mathrm{H^{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }\mathrm{D}}(f,g;\mathbb{F}).

Proof.

Let
U\subseteq X
be an open subset. Consider the following commutative diagram obtained from the Universal Coefficient Theorem:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{n}(Y;\mathbb{F})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{n}(U;\mathbb{F})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H_{n}(Y;\mathbb{F})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H_{n}(U;\mathbb{F})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(f_{|U})^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(g_{|U})^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(f_{|U})_{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(g_{|U})_{*}}

We deduce that
(f_{|U})_{*}=(g_{|U})_{*}
if and only if
(f_{|U})^{*}=(g_{|U})^{*}
.
∎

In general, no direct inequality exists between the homological and the cohomological distance, as illustrated in Example
1.8
. The same example also shows that the (co)homological distance improves in some cases the lower bound provided by
\mathrm{l.c.p.}\,\mathcal{J}(f,g;R)
.

Example 1.8
.

Let
X=\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{2}
be the real projective space endowed with the CW-complex structure with one cell in each dimension. Consider the quotient map collapsing the
1
-skeleton to a point,

\pi\colon\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{2}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{2}}/{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\simeq\mathbb{S}^{2}.

The induced homomorphism in integral homology

\pi_{*}\colon H_{n}(\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{2};\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow H_{n}(\mathbb{S}^{2};\mathbb{Z})

is trivial for every
n>0
. Let
\mathrm{c}\colon\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{2}
be a constant map. Then,
\mathrm{H_{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }\mathrm{D}}(\pi,\mathrm{c};\mathbb{Z})=0
. However, since
\pi
maps the
2
-cell of
\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{2}
onto the
2
-cell of
\mathbb{S}^{2}
, the induced homomorphism in cohomology

\pi^{*}\colon H^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2};\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow H^{2}(\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{2};\mathbb{Z})

is the canonical projection
\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. Hence
\mathrm{H^{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }\mathrm{D}}(\pi,\mathrm{c};\mathbb{Z})>0
, showing that in general
\mathrm{H^{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }\mathrm{D}}(f,g;R)\nleq\mathrm{H_{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }\mathrm{D}}(f,g;R)
.

Moreover, in this case we also have
\mathcal{J}(\pi,\mathrm{c};\mathbb{Z})=H^{*}(\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{2};\mathbb{Z})
,
so that

\mathrm{l.c.p.}\,\mathcal{J}(\pi,\mathrm{c};\mathbb{Z})=\mathrm{l.c.p.}\,(\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{2};\mathbb{Z})=1,

which shows that, in general,
\mathrm{l.c.p.}\,\mathcal{J}(f,g;R)\nleq\mathrm{H_{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }\mathrm{D}}(f,g;R)
.

Dually, consider the inclusion of the
1
-skeleton

\iota\colon X_{1}\cong\mathbb{S}^{1}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{2}.

In this case, the induced map in cohomology

\iota^{*}\colon H^{n}(\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{2};\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow H^{n}(\mathbb{S}^{1};\mathbb{Z})

is trivial for every
n>0
, whereas the induced morphism in homology

\iota_{*}\colon H_{1}(\mathbb{S}^{1};\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow H_{1}(\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{2};\mathbb{Z})

is the canonical projection
\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. If
\mathrm{c}\colon\mathbb{S}^{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{2}
is a constant map, then
\mathrm{H^{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }\mathrm{D}}(\iota,\mathrm{c};\mathbb{Z})=0
while
\mathrm{H_{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }\mathrm{D}}(\iota,\mathrm{c};\mathbb{Z})=1
. This shows that, in general,
\mathrm{H_{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }\mathrm{D}}(f,g;R)\nleq\mathrm{H^{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }\mathrm{D}}(f,g;R)
.

Moreover, it provides an example where the (co)homological distance gives a better lower bound than the cup-length invariant since
\mathrm{l.c.p.}\,\mathcal{J}(\iota,\mathrm{c};R)=0
.

2.
Simplicial cohomological distance

We now move to the simplicial setting and introduce a simplicial cohomological distance, which plays the discrete counterpart of the cohomological distance for simplicial maps. Let
K
and
L
be finite simplicial complexes and
\varphi,\psi\colon K\rightarrow L
simplicial maps.

Definition 2.1
.

The
simplicial cohomological distance
between
\varphi
and
\psi
with coefficients in
R
, denoted by
\mathrm{H^{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }s\mathrm{D}}(\varphi,\psi;R)
, is the least integer
n\geq 0
for which there exists a cover
\{K_{0},\dots,K_{n}\}
of
K
by
n+1
subcomplexes such that

(\varphi_{|K_{j}})^{*}=(\psi_{|K_{j}})^{*}\colon H^{m}(L;R)\rightarrow H^{m}(K_{j};R)

for every
m\geq 0
and for each
j=0,\dots,n
.

We denote
\mathrm{H^{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }scat}(K;R)=\mathrm{H^{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }s\mathrm{D}}(\mathrm{c},\mathrm{id}_{K};R)
and
\mathrm{H^{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }sTC}(K;R)=\mathrm{H^{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }s\mathrm{D}}(\pi_{1},\pi_{2};R)
the
simplicial cohomological category
and the
simplicial cohomological topological complexity
, respectively.

The simplicial cohomological distance satisfies properties analogous to those established for the cohomological distance and for the simplicial distance. Moreover, observe that it satisfies:

\mathrm{H^{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }s\mathrm{D}}(\mathrm{sd}\varphi,\mathrm{sd}\psi;R)\leq\mathrm{H^{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }s\mathrm{D}}(\varphi,\psi;R).

This suggests that subdividing may refine the simplicial distance. Let us show one example where the inequality is strict.

Example 2.2
.

Let
K
be the complete graph
K_{5}
considered as a
1
-dimensional simplicial complex. First, observe that
\mathrm{H^{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }scat}(K;R)>1
. Indeed, if we consider a cover of
K
by two subcomplexes, by the Pigeonhole Principle, one of them has three or more vertices and therefore a cocycle in cohomology. Denote the vertices of
K
as {1,2,3,4,5}. Then the subcomplexes generated by the maximal simplices:

\displaystyle K_{0}=[1,2]\cup[1,3]\cup[1,4]\cup[1,5],

\displaystyle K_{1}=[2,3]\cup[2,4]\cup[3,5]\text{ and }

\displaystyle K_{2}=[2,5]\cup[3,4]\cup[4,5]

form a cover of
K
and they guarantee
\mathrm{H^{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }scat}(K;R)=2
. An analogous argument shows
\mathrm{scat}(K)=2
and
\mathrm{scat}(\mathrm{sd}K)=1
(see
[
3
, Example 5.6]
).

As
\mathrm{l.c.p.}\,(K;R)=1
, this shows that the inequality
\mathrm{l.c.p.}\,(K;R)<\mathrm{H^{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }scat}(K;R)
may be sharp. Furthermore, we have that

\mathrm{H^{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }scat}(\mathrm{sd}K;R)=\mathrm{scat}(\mathrm{sd}K)=1<\mathrm{H^{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }scat}(K;R)=\mathrm{scat}(K)=2.

Theorem 2.3

(Approximation theorem)
.

Let
K
and
L
be finite simplicial complexes, and let
f,g\colon\lvert K\rvert\rightarrow\lvert L\rvert
be continuous maps such that
\mathrm{H^{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }\mathrm{D}}(f,g;R)=n
. Then there exists an integer
q\geq 0
, a cover
\{K_{0}^{\prime\prime},\dots,K_{n}^{\prime\prime}\}
of
K^{\prime\prime}=\mathrm{sd}^{q}(K)
by
n+1
subcomplexes and simplicial maps
\varphi,\psi\colon K^{\prime\prime}\rightarrow L
whose geometric realizations
\lvert\varphi\rvert
and
\lvert\psi\rvert
are homotopic to
f
and
g
respectively, satisfying
(\varphi_{|K_{j}^{\prime\prime}})^{*}=(\psi_{|K_{j}^{\prime\prime}})^{*}
for every
j=0,\ldots,n
. In particular,

\mathrm{H^{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }\mathrm{D}}(f,g;R)=\mathrm{H^{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }s\mathrm{D}}(\varphi,\psi;R).

Proof.

Suppose
\mathrm{H^{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }\mathrm{D}}(f,g;R)=n
, and let
\mathcal{U}=\{U_{0},\dots,U_{n}\}
be a cover of
\lvert K\rvert
by
n+1
open subsets such that
(f_{|U_{j}})^{*}=(g_{|U_{j}})^{*}
for every
j=0,\dots,n
.

Since
K
is finite, its geometric realization
\lvert K\rvert
is compact. Hence, the cover
\mathcal{U}
admits a Lebesgue number
\delta>0
, i.e., every subset of
\lvert K\rvert
with diameter less than
\delta
is contained in some element of
\mathcal{U}
.

Therefore, there exists an integer

m\geq 1
such that every simplex of the
m
-th barycentric subdivision
K^{\prime}=\mathrm{sd}^{m}K
has diameter less than
\delta
. Consequently, every simplex of
K^{\prime}
lies in some
U_{j}
, so
the following subcomplexes of
K^{\prime}
:

K_{j}^{\prime}:=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in K^{\prime}\\
|\sigma|\subseteq U_{j}\end{subarray}}\sigma,\qquad j=0,\dots,n,

form a cover of
K^{\prime}
by
n+1
subcomplexes. Moreover,
\lvert K_{j}^{\prime}\rvert\subseteq U_{j}
for each
j=0,\dots,n
. Hence,

(f_{|\lvert K_{j}^{\prime}\rvert})^{*}=(g_{|\lvert K_{j}^{\prime}\rvert})^{*}
.

By the Simplicial Approximation Theorem, there exists a subdivision
K^{\prime\prime}=\mathrm{sd}^{p}K^{\prime}
such that
f
and
g
admit simplicial approximations
\varphi,\psi\colon K^{\prime\prime}\rightarrow L
. Let
K_{j}^{\prime\prime}=\mathrm{sd}^{p}K_{j}^{\prime}
and consider the cover
\{K_{0}^{\prime\prime},\dots,K_{n}^{\prime\prime}\}
of
K^{\prime\prime}
by
n+1
subcomplexes. Then
(\lvert\varphi\rvert_{|\lvert K_{j}^{\prime\prime}\rvert})^{*}=(\lvert\psi\rvert_{|\lvert K_{j}^{\prime\prime}\rvert})^{*}.

Moreover, by the naturality of the isomorphism between simplicial and singular cohomology, the following diagram commutes:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{*}(L;R)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{*}(\lvert L\rvert;R)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{*}(K_{j}^{\prime\prime};R)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{*}(\lvert K_{j}^{\prime\prime}\rvert;R)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\varphi_{K_{j}^{\prime\prime}})^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\psi_{K_{j}^{\prime\prime}})^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\lvert\varphi\rvert_{|\lvert K_{j}^{\prime\prime}\rvert})^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\lvert\psi\rvert_{|\lvert K_{j}^{\prime\prime}\rvert})^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}

Consequently,
(\varphi_{|K_{j}^{\prime\prime}})^{*}=(\psi_{|K_{j}^{\prime\prime}})^{*}
, for all
j=0,\dots,n
and
q=m+p
.

Michiel De Wilde, Robert Seiringer

Abstract
. We show that a suitable choice of boundary conditions for the Laplacian allows for the appearance of an an arbitrary number of condensates, described by arbitrary harmonic functions, in the thermodynamic limit of an ideal Bose gas.



1.
Introduction and Main Result

In this article, we shall investigate the question what kind of condensates one can obtain for an ideal Bose gas in the thermodynamic limit if one is allowed to freely choose the boundary conditions for the Laplacian on the boundary of the domains. The question is motivated by the observation in
[
3
, Example 5.3.2]
that ideal Bose gases in infinite volume allow for many different KMS states, with an arbitrary positive sesquilinear form that is invariant under the time evolution (separately in the arguments) appearing in the two-point function, corresponding to harmonic functions describing the condensate(s). This leaves open the question whether such states can be obtained via the usual procedure of taking the thermodynamic limit of Gibbs states of systems in finite volume.
Related to that, it was observed in
[
2
, Section VI]
that one can construct non-translation-invariant KMS states in the thermodynamic limit in such a way that the condensate is described by a linear function.

The following theorem shows that it is possible to choose boundary conditions for the Laplacian such that the corresponding system of an ideal Bose gas at zero chemical potential has, in the thermodynamic limit, any number of Bose condensates described by arbitrary harmonic functions.

Theorem 1
.

Consider a sequence of open, bounded domains
\Omega_{j}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, with smooth boundary
\partial\Omega_{j}
, converging to
\mathbb{R}^{d}
as
j\to\infty
, in the sense that any bounded set is contained in
\Omega_{j}
for
j
large enough. Given a (possibly infinite) sequence
\Phi=\{\phi_{k}\}_{k}
of harmonic functions
\phi_{k}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
, with
\sum_{k}|\phi_{k}(x)|^{2}<\infty
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, there exist positive self-adjoint realizations
-\Delta^{\Omega_{j}}_{\Phi}
of
-\Delta
on
\Omega_{j}
such that

\lim_{j\to\infty}\left\langle f\left|\frac{1}{e^{-\beta\Delta_{\Phi}^{\Omega_{j}}}-1}\right.g\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{j})}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\overline{\hat{f}(p)}\hat{g}(p)}{e^{\beta|p|^{2}}-1}{dp}+\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\overline{f}\phi_{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\overline{\phi_{k}}g

(1)

for any
\beta>0
and any
f,g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with compact support. In dimensions
d\in\{1,2\}
, we need the additional assumption
\hat{f}(0)=\hat{g}(0)=0
for (
1
) to hold.

By assumption, the supports of
f
and
g
are contained in
\Omega_{j}
for
j
large enough, hence the left-hand side of (
1
) is well-defined. For the first term on the right-hand side to be well-defined, we need the additional assumption
\hat{f}(0)=\hat{g}(0)=0
for
d\in\{1,2\}
to have integrability at
p=0
, which then follows from smoothness of
\hat{f}
and
\hat{g}
.

Eq. (
1
) corresponds to the two-point function of an ideal Bose gas with kinetic energy described by the Laplacian
-\Delta_{\Phi}^{\Omega}
. From it, all
n
-point functions can be obtained via Wick’s rule.
There are multiple (possibly infinitely many) Bose–Einstein condensates. Each condensate comes with a corresponding density
\beta^{-1}|\phi_{k}(x)|^{2}
, whose mean value is infinite unless
\phi_{k}
is the constant function, since non-constant harmonic functions necessarily increase at infinity. We remark that since the
\phi_{k}
are harmonic, the pointwise summability condition
\sum_{k}|\phi_{k}|^{2}<\infty
necessarily holds uniformly on bounded sets.

The remainder of this article contains the proof of Theorem
1
. In the next section, we shall construct the Laplacian
\Delta_{\Phi}^{\Omega}
and describe its essential properties. In Section
3
, we shall show that
\Delta_{\Phi}^{\Omega_{j}}
converges to the Laplacian on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
as
j\to\infty
in a suitable sense. Finally, Section
4
combines the results of Sections
2
and
3
and gives the proof of Theorem
1
.

2.
Construction of the Laplacians

For nice enough domains
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, let
\chi_{\Omega}
denote the characteristic function of
\Omega
. The Dirichlet Laplacian on
\Omega
we be denoted by
\Delta_{0}^{\Omega}
. Given a (possibly infinite) sequence of harmonic functions
\Phi=\{\phi_{k}\}_{k}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, with
\sum_{k}|\phi_{k}(x)|^{2}<\infty
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we shall define the Laplacian
\Delta^{\Omega}_{\Phi}
as

-\Delta_{\Phi}^{\Omega}:=\left(\left(-\Delta_{0}^{\Omega}\right)^{-1}+\sum_{k}|\chi_{\Omega}\phi_{k}\rangle\langle\phi_{k}\chi_{\Omega}|\right)^{-1}

(2)

Note that the inverse of the Dirichlet Laplacian is compact, and so is the second term in parentheses in (
2
) due to the summability assumption of the
\phi_{k}
.
As the inverse of a compact operator with trivial kernel, this yields a well-defined self-adjoint operator. Moreover, we recover the Dirichlet Laplacian
-\Delta_{0}^{\Omega}
in case
\Phi
consists only of the zero function.

Let
P_{\Phi}^{\Omega}:L^{2}(\Omega)\to L^{2}(\Omega)
denote the projection onto the closure of the linear span of
\{\chi_{\Omega}\phi_{k}\}_{k}
. Since the
\phi_{k}
are assumed to be harmonic on all of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, functions in the range of
P_{\Phi}^{\Omega}
are smooth and extend smoothly to the boundary of
\Omega
(and beyond). We can introduce a positive (possibly unbounded) self-adjoint operator

R_{\Phi}^{\Omega}
on
P_{\Phi}^{\Omega}L^{2}(\Omega)
such that

\sum_{k}|\chi_{\Omega}\phi_{k}\rangle\langle\phi_{k}\chi_{\Omega}|=P_{\Phi}^{\Omega}\left(R_{\Phi}^{\Omega}\right)^{-1}P_{\Phi}^{\Omega}

(3)

For functions
f\in H^{1}(\Omega)
that have a continuous restriction to the boundary
\partial\Omega
, we shall denote by
\psi_{f}
the unique harmonic function that agrees with
f
on the boundary of
\Omega
.
The key properties of
-\Delta_{\Phi}^{\Omega}
are summarized in the following lemma.

Lemma 1
.

With the definitions in (
2
) and (
3
),

(i)

-\Delta_{\Phi}^{\Omega}
is a positive self-adjoint extension of
-\Delta
on
C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)

(ii)

The domain of
-\Delta_{\Phi}^{\Omega}
is given by

\displaystyle\dom(\Delta_{\Phi}^{\Omega})=\big\{f+g\,:\,

\displaystyle f\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),\ g\in\dom(R_{\Phi}^{\Omega})\subset\ran P_{\Phi}^{\Omega},

\displaystyle R_{\Phi}^{\Omega}g=P_{\Phi}^{\Omega}(-\Delta f)\big\}

(4)

(iii)

-\Delta_{\Phi}^{\Omega}
is the operator associated to the quadratic form

f\mapsto\int_{\Omega}|\nabla f|^{2}-\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\psi_{f}|^{2}+\langle\psi_{f}|R_{\Phi}^{\Omega}\psi_{f}\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

with form domain
\{h+g\,:\,h\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega),\ g\in\ran P_{\Phi}^{\Omega},\ \langle\psi_{g}|R_{\Phi}^{\Omega}\psi_{g}\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}<\infty\}
.

Functions
f\in\dom(\Delta_{\Phi}^{\Omega})
have a smooth restriction to
\partial\Omega
, and
\psi_{f}\in\ran P_{\Phi}^{\Omega}
. Moreover, an integration by parts shows that
P_{\Phi}^{\Omega}(-\Delta f)
only depends on
f
and its outward normal derivative,
\partial f
, on the boundary of
\Omega
. Hence the last condition in (
4
)
is really a boundary condition. In fact, if we define an operator
r_{\Phi}^{\Omega}
on
L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
via

\langle\psi_{f}|R_{\Phi}^{\Omega}\psi_{f}\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\langle f\!\!\restriction_{\partial\Omega}|r_{\Phi}^{\Omega}\,f\!\!\restriction_{\partial\Omega}\rangle_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}

for
\psi_{f}\in\ran P_{\Phi}^{\Omega}
,
the boundary condition reads

\partial f=H^{\Omega}f\!\!\restriction_{\partial\Omega}-r_{\Phi}^{\Omega}\,f\!\!\restriction_{\partial\Omega}

(5)

where
H^{\Omega}:L^{2}(\partial\Omega)\to L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
is the Dirichlet-to-Neumann operator, mapping
f\!\!\restriction_{\partial\Omega}
to
\partial\psi_{f}
.

Proof.

(i) Let us first we check that
\Delta_{\Phi}^{\Omega}
is indeed a Laplacian, i.e.,
\Delta_{\Phi}^{\Omega}f=\Delta f
for
f\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
. By the definitions (
2
) and (
3
) we have

\left(-\Delta_{\Phi}^{\Omega}\right)^{-1}\left(-\Delta_{0}^{\Omega}\right)f=f+P_{\Phi}^{\Omega}\left(R_{\Phi}^{\Omega}\right)^{-1}P_{\Phi}^{\Omega}\left(-\Delta_{0}^{\Omega}\right)f=f

since
P_{\Phi}^{\Omega}\Delta_{0}^{\Omega}f=0
, as an integration by parts shows. Hence
(-\Delta_{\Phi}^{\Omega})f=(-\Delta_{0}^{\Omega})f=-\Delta f
. Positivity and self-adjointness of
-\Delta_{\Phi}^{\Omega}
follow straightforwardly from the definition.

(ii) Next we compute the domain of
\Delta_{\Phi}^{\Omega}
. For
f\in\dom(\Delta^{\Omega}_{\Phi})
, let
g=(-\Delta_{\Phi}^{\Omega})f
. Again by the definitions (
2
) and (
3
),

f=(-\Delta_{\Phi}^{\Omega})^{-1}g=\underbrace{(-\Delta_{0}^{\Omega})^{-1}g}_{f-\psi_{f}}+\underbrace{P_{\Phi}^{\Omega}\left(R_{\Phi}^{\Omega}\right)^{-1}P_{\Phi}^{\Omega}g}_{\psi_{f}}

Here we used the fact that
(-\Delta_{0}^{\Omega})^{-1}g\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
, the domain of the Dirichlet Laplacian, and thus vanishes at the boundary of
\Omega
, while the last term
P_{\Phi}^{\Omega}(R_{\Phi}^{\Omega})^{-1}P_{\Phi}^{\Omega}g
is harmonic and smooth up to the boundary of
\Omega
.
Hence
\psi_{f}\in\ran P_{\Phi}^{\Omega}
, and
R_{\Phi}^{\Omega}\psi_{f}=P_{\Phi}^{\Omega}g=P_{\Phi}^{\Omega}(-\Delta f)
.

(iii)
To obtain the quadratic form associated with
-\Delta_{\Phi}^{\Omega}
, an integration by parts using the boundary condition (
5
) yields

\langle f|(-\Delta_{\Phi}^{\Omega})f\rangle=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla f|^{2}-\int_{\partial\Omega}\overline{f}\partial f=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla f|^{2}-\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\psi_{f}|^{2}+\langle\psi_{f}|R_{\Phi}^{\Omega}\psi_{f}\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

together with the constraint
\psi_{f}\in\ran P_{\Phi}^{\Omega}
.
∎

If, for given
\Omega
, one allows for harmonic functions that are merely harmonic on
\Omega
instead of all of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, one can show that all non-negative self-adjoint extensions of
-\Delta
on
C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
can be obtained in the way above, plus suitably taking limits
[
1
]
. In fact, any non-negative self-adjoint extension is bounded above by
-\Delta_{0}^{\Omega}
, and bounded below by the Krein Laplacian
Q^{\Omega}(Q^{\Omega}(-\Delta_{0}^{\Omega})^{-1}Q^{\Omega})^{-1}Q^{\Omega}
with
1-Q^{\Omega}
the projection onto the span of all harmonic functions on
\Omega
.

For completeness, let us also note the Krein resolvent formula

\frac{1}{z+\Delta_{\Phi}^{\Omega}}=\frac{1}{z+\Delta_{0}^{\Omega}}-\frac{\Delta_{0}^{\Omega}}{z+\Delta_{0}^{\Omega}}P_{\Phi}^{\Omega}\frac{1}{R_{\Phi}^{\Omega}-zP_{\Phi}^{\Omega}\frac{\Delta_{0}^{\Omega}}{z+\Delta_{0}^{\Omega}}P_{\Phi}^{\Omega}}P_{\Phi}^{\Omega}\frac{\Delta_{0}^{\Omega}}{z+\Delta_{0}^{\Omega}}

which follows from (
2
) and (
3
) and reduces to it for
z=0
. We shall not need it in the following, however.

Example 1
.

As a specific example consider the case of of
\Phi=\{\phi\}
, i.e., there is just one harmonic function, denoted by
\phi
. In this case, we consider a Laplacian with domain given by functions
f=h+c\phi
, with
c\in\mathbb{C}
and
h\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
satisfying Dirichlet boundary conditions. We have
\psi_{f}=c\phi
, and the boundary condition (
5
) becomes

f\!\!\restriction_{\partial\Omega}=c\phi\!\!\restriction_{\partial\Omega}\ ,\ \partial f=c\partial\phi-r_{\phi}^{\Omega}\,f\!\!\restriction_{\partial\Omega}

with
r_{\phi}^{\Omega}=1/\int_{\partial\Omega}|\phi|^{2}
.

3.
Strong resolvent convergence

In this section, we shall show that as
\Omega_{j}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
in the sense defined in Theorem
1
,
-\Delta_{\Phi}^{\Omega_{j}}
converges to the usual Laplacian
-\Delta
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
as
j\to\infty
in a suitable sense.
In fact, by proceeding similarly as in
[
4
, Thm. 1.2]
, where a corresponding theorem for the Dirichlet Laplacian is given, we shall prove the following more general statement.

Theorem 2
.

Consider a sequence of open, bounded domains
\Omega_{j}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, converging to
\mathbb{R}^{d}
as
j\to\infty
, in the sense that any bounded set is contained in
\Omega_{j}
for
j
large enough. Let
-\Delta^{\Omega_{j}}
be a any sequence of non-negative self-adjoint extensions of
-\Delta
on
C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega_{j})
. If
F
is a bounded continuous function on
[0,\infty)
, then for all
u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
F(-\Delta^{\Omega_{j}})\chi_{\Omega_{j}}u\to F(-\Delta)u
strongly in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Proof.

By using the Stone–Weierstrass theorem, we can argue as in the proof of
[
4
, Thm. 1.2]
to conclude that it suffices to prove the statement for
F(x)=(1+x)^{-1}
.

Take
u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and let
v_{j}=(-\Delta^{\Omega_{j}}+1)^{-1}\chi_{\Omega_{j}}u
. Since
-\Delta^{\Omega_{j}}
is non-negative,
v_{j}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
\|v_{j}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\|v_{j}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{j})}\leq\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
.
By selecting a subsequence, we may hence assume that there is a weak
L^{2}
limit
v_{j}\rightharpoonup w
.

For
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
we have for sufficiently large
j

\langle\psi|-\Delta^{\Omega_{j}}v_{j}\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{j})}=\langle-\Delta^{\Omega_{j}}\psi|v_{j}\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{j})}=\langle-\Delta\psi|v_{j}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\xrightarrow{j\to\infty}\langle-\Delta\psi|w\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

where we used that
\Delta^{\Omega_{j}}
is a self-adjoint extension of
\Delta
on
C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega_{j})
. On the other hand,

\langle\psi|-\Delta^{\Omega_{j}}v_{j}\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{j})}=\langle\psi|\chi^{\Omega_{j}}u\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{j})}-\langle\psi|v_{j}\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{j})}\xrightarrow{j\to\infty}\langle\psi|u\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}-\langle\psi|w\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

and hence we conclude that
-\Delta w+w=u
in the sense of distributions. Since
w\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, this implies that
w=(-\Delta+1)^{-1}u
.

We are left with showing strong convergence. For
\eta\in\mathbb{C}_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we can bound
\|v_{j}-w\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\|v_{j}-\eta w\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|(1-\eta)w\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
, and the last term can be made arbitrarily small by choosing
\eta
to be equal to
1
on a large enough ball. For
j
large enough, the support of
\eta
is contained in
\Omega_{j}
, and hence

\|v_{j}-\eta w\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\|v_{j}-\eta w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{j})}\leq\|(1-\Delta^{\Omega_{j}})(v_{j}-\eta w)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{j})}

using again positivity of
-\Delta^{\Omega_{j}}
. By the definition of
v_{j}
and using
(-\Delta+1)w=u
,

\left(1-\Delta^{\Omega_{j}}\right)(v_{j}-\eta w)=\left(\chi_{\Omega_{j}}-\eta\right)u+2\nabla\eta\cdot\nabla w+\left(\Delta\eta\right)w

and this converges strongly to
(1-\eta)u+2\nabla\eta\cdot\nabla w+(\Delta\eta)w
as
j\to\infty
. Since
w\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, this term can again be made arbitrarily small by a suitable choice of
\eta
. This completes the proof.
∎

4.
Proof of Theorem
1

In this section we shall give the proof of Theorem
1
. Without loss of generality, we can assume
f=g
in (
1
).

Pick
j
large enough such that
\Omega_{j}
contains the support of
f
. With
F(x)=1/(e^{x}-1)-1/x
, we can write

\left\langle f\left|\frac{1}{e^{-\beta\Delta_{\Phi}^{\Omega_{j}}}-1}\right.f\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{j})}=\left\langle f\left|F(-\beta\Delta_{\Phi}^{\Omega_{j}})\right.f\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{j})}+\beta^{-1}\left\langle f\left|\left(-\Delta_{\Phi}^{\Omega_{j}}\right)^{-1}\right.f\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{j})}

Since
F
is a bounded continuous function on
[0,\infty)
, we deduce from Theorem
2
that

\lim_{j\to\infty}\left\langle f\left|F(-\beta\Delta_{\Phi}^{\Omega_{j}})\right.f\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{j})}=\left\langle f\left|F(-\beta\Delta)\right.f\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}F(\beta|p|^{2})|\hat{f}(p)|^{2}{dp}

From the definition of
\Delta_{\Phi}^{\Omega}
in (
2
), we further have

\left\langle f\left|\left(-\Delta_{\Phi}^{\Omega_{j}}\right)^{-1}\right.f\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{j})}=\left\langle f\left|\left(-\Delta_{0}^{\Omega_{j}}\right)^{-1}\right.f\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{j})}+\sum_{k}\left|\int\overline{\phi_{k}}f\right|^{2}

The statement of the theorem thus follows if we can show that

\lim_{j\to\infty}\left\langle f\left|\left(-\Delta_{0}^{\Omega_{j}}\right)^{-1}\right.f\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{j})}=\left\langle f\left|\left(-\Delta\right)^{-1}\right.f\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|\hat{f}(p)|^{2}}{|p|^{2}}dp

But this is a consequence of Theorem
2
and the monotone convergence theorem, using that
\langle f|(-\Delta_{0}^{\Omega}+\varepsilon)^{-1}f\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
is increasing as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
and
\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, i.e., for all
f
supported inside
\Omega
,
\langle f|(-\Delta_{0}^{\Omega}+\varepsilon)^{-1}f\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\langle f|(-\Delta_{0}^{\Omega^{\prime}}+\varepsilon^{\prime})^{-1}f\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\prime})}
if
\varepsilon\geq\varepsilon^{\prime}>0
and
\Omega\subset\Omega^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
. This completes the proof.

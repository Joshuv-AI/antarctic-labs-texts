A case study on the passability of slim dual bend screw drills in high curvature sidetracking through slim boreholes from the Southern Ordos Basin
Authors: Zhang Hui, Liu Xugang, Yu Zhanmiao, Liu Jiaqin, Wang Changhao, Feng Qianwen, Meng Shuan, Li Shaojie
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The redevelopment of complex fault-block oilfields in the Southern Ordos Basin faces dual challenges of slim-wellholes (Φ120 mm) and high curvature (build-up rate > 57°/30 m), where the passability of slim (Φ73 mm) dual-bend screw drilling tools (1.5° + 3°) is particularly critical. Moving beyond traditional geometric or static analysis, this study establishes an innovative “casing-open hole” passability evaluation model that explicitly couples the effects of Weight on Bit (WOB) and borehole curvature, based on the beam-column theory and contact mechanics, while considering the unique constraints of slim tools. In the casing section, geometric constraints verified the feasibility of the tool’s passage with a maximum lower bend point displacement of 47.3 mm inside Φ124.3 mm casing. In the open-hole section, for curvature radii of 15–30 m, the model quantifies the synergistic mechanism between WOB (1–3 T) and curvature, revealing that passability is significantly more sensitive to curvature changes than to WOB variations. Results indicate the tool can pass through a 30 m curvature radius under the full WOB range (1–3 T), while the safe WOB threshold decreases to 1.8 T at a 15 m radius. Stress concentration factors reach 2.1–2.8, with a peak stress of 749.4 MPa at the lower bend. This study provides a critical bend angle formula and a drag-thrust balance criterion for optimizing tool running, offering a quantitative and more reliable design basis compared to conventional empirical methods for high-curvature sidetracking in the Southern Ordos Basin. Field applications demonstrated a substantial increase in successful running operations, validating the model’s superior predictive capability and practical value. The findings provide not only technical support for well redevelopment in this region but also a theoretical reference for drilling engineering under similar complex wellhole conditions.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-31430-2.

Introduction

As a typical complex fault-block oilfield in the Southern Ordos Basin, the risk of well abandonment escalates with extended development time, where aging wells face challenges including casing damage, water breakthrough, and productivity decline. In order to improve the utilization rate of old wells and tap the remaining oil potential, small wellbore high curvature side drilling technology has become a key method. However, the complex geological conditions in this area generally lead to two major characteristics of side drilling wellbores: one is the extremely high slope (> 57°/30 m), and the other is the small size of the wellbore (well diameter 120 mm). These characteristics make the column face severe passivity challenges during the drill string run-in. On the one hand, high curvature causes significant bending stress to the column, increasing the risk of plastic deformation and fatigue failure; on the other hand, the small wellbore ring space gap is narrow, the column is frequently in contact with the well wall, and the friction and torque are significantly increased, which may cause the drilling or tool to be inaccessible. Friction is closely related to trafficability, and high friction is the direct cause of difficulty in getting down and even sticking1. At present, the design of side drill pipe strings in this area still relies on empirical formulas and field tests, lack of systematic simulation analysis, and it is difficult to accurately predict the mechanical behavior of the pipe string, resulting in difficulty in downstream of the pipe string. Internationally, Seibi and Al-Shabibi2 used the finite element method (FEM) to analyze the penetration force of the string in medium and high curvature boreholes earlier, and clearly pointed out that the bending stiffness of the string is a key influence factor in short radius (R < 50 m) boreholes, and its influence becomes insignificant in medium curvature boreholes. However, this study did not conduct in-depth discussions on BHAs with large flexible components (such as double-curved screws) under small hole conditions.

In terms of downhole tool passivity analysis, rigid conditions and deformation conditions are usually required to be considered from the two areas of the casing section and the lower naked eye section respectively. Under rigid conditions, geometric methods are mainly used to judge, while under deformation conditions, the column strength and friction resistance characteristics need to be comprehensively evaluated. In the existing research, Jing3 proposed a general rigid pass mode calculation method by calculating the maximum disturbance value generated by the drill tool under the action of gravity and lateral forces. Junping4 and others regarded the drill tool as a simple-supported beam affected by vertical and horizontal bending loads, and established a drill tool combination through capability calculation model under deformation conditions, but the calculation method in this model is too complicated. Qinfeng5 and others will further divide the deformation of the drill tool into two modes: free deformation and forced deformation, and analyze the passing ability of the power drill tool with a regularizer. Xiaohua et al.6 quantitatively analyzed the influence of different bending angles on the passing of single-bending screws through finite element simulation. Xing7 and others innovatively established a tool passivity model in the three-dimensional buckling tube column, and derived the tool size control equation based on geometric constraints. Seibi et al.2 studied the force and stress distribution of the pipe string in the middle and high curvature hole through the experiment and finite element simulation system, and found that the friction coefficient, the radius of curvature and the size of the pipe string are the key factors affecting the passing ability. In recent years, Shi et al.8 systematically studied the passability of sand control string in large curvature boreholes in natural gas hydrate exploitation. The feasibility of the string was verified by field test and numerical simulation. Zhang et al.9 have been widely used in complex engineering systems through a hybrid method combining physical information modeling and data-driven inversion, especially in horizontal well friction analysis and trajectory monitoring. At present, the soft rod model proposed by Aadnoy et al.10 is widely used in the field to predict the friction torque, but the applicability of the model under extreme curvature and small well conditions remains to be verified. Therefore, on the basis of meeting the basic passability theoretical conditions, this study aims to comprehensively apply the rigid mathematical model and the deformation mathematical model to deeply explore the passability and mechanical behavior of the string during safe running, and analyze the interaction between the bending stress of the string and the accumulated friction resistance during the drilling process. Through the finite element method that can accurately consider the stiffness and contact state of the drilling tool, the causes of high friction are fundamentally analyzed, and the passability of the drilling tool is accurately predicted, which provides important theoretical guidance and technical support for the safe running of the screw drilling tool in the high curvature slim hole sidetracking operation in Southern Ordos Basin.

Material properties and constitutive model

In order to accurately simulate the mechanical interaction between the drilling tool and the borehole wall, the following material properties are assigned to each component in the model:

The drilling tool system (screw, drill pipe, joint) adopts a linear elastic constitutive model. The material is mainly 35CrMo alloy steel, with a density of 7850 kg/m3, a young’s modulus is 210 GPa, a Poisson’s ratio of 0.3, and a yield strength of 835 MPa (according to GB-3077 standard). The density of titanium alloy parts is 4500 kg/m3, and the Young’s modulus is 110 GPa. The borehole wall is regarded as an isotropic linear elastic body. According to the stratigraphic characteristics of the Southern Ordos Basin area, the Young’s modulus is 15 GPa (simulated medium-hard sandstone), the Poisson’s ratio is 0.25, and the density is 2500 kg/m3.

In this study, the borehole wall is regarded as a rigid body. This assumption is based on the fact that the rock strength of the target section is much higher than the deformation magnitude of the drilling tool, and the computational complexity can be significantly reduced, focusing on the mechanical response of the drilling tool itself.

Contact model and meshing scheme

In this study, the contact element modeling method is used to simulate the interaction between the double-bend screw and the wellbore wall to predict the passing performance more accurately. The contact mode is surface-to-surface contact, the wellbore with higher rigidity is the main surface, and the screw drill is the slave surface. The normal behavior adopts “Hard Contact”, that is, only the pressure is transmitted between the contact surfaces, and the tension is not borne. The contact pressure is immediately zero when separating. The Coulomb friction model is used for tangential behavior. The friction coefficient is set to 0.25. This value is determined based on the lubrication performance test data of the drilling fluid system (density 1080 kg/m3, dynamic viscosity 10 mPa·s) used in the field in Southern Ordos Basin and the experience range recommended by API RP 13B-2. The method for determining the value follows the industry practice, that is, the Pick-up/Slack-off Weight is obtained by inverse calculation during tripping1.

The drilling tool and wellbore are meshed by hexahedral structure meshing technology, and the unit type is hexahedral 8-node reduced integral unit C3D8R. The size is set to 15 mm. Under this grid type and grid size, the calculation efficiency and calculation accuracy achieve a good balance. As for the double-bending screw, during the drilling process with a radius of curvature of 30 m, the drilling pressure is always in a safe state under the conditions of drilling pressure, with a stress concentration coefficient of 2.1 and a maximum stress value of 749.4 MPa; during the drilling process with a radius of curvature of 20 m, when the drilling pressure reaches 3 T, the stress concentration coefficient of 982.13 MPa in contact with the well wall reaches 2.4, and the friction resistance of the pipe string is greater than the axial component force, and the bending point of the double-bending screw cannot pass normally and begin to deform; during the drilling process with a radius of curvature of 15 m, when the drilling pressure exceeds 2.5 T, the maximum stress value reaches 1227.6 MPa. When the drilling pressure reaches 3 T, the stress concentration coefficient is 2.8, and the maximum stress value reaches 1580.4 MPa, which cannot pass normally. After the above calculation and analysis, during the double-bent screw drill string run-in, the drilling tool can be safely and safely under a 1 T-3 T pressure under a radius of curvature of 30 m. When the radius of curvature is 20 m, the drilling tool is stuck and cannot be lowered when the pressure exceeds 2.5 T. When the radius of curvature is 15 m, the drilling tool cannot be smoothly lowered when the pressure exceeds 1.8 T.

According to the simulation results, it is found that the radius of wellbore curvature and WOB are the key factors affecting the string passing ability. The change in WOB will directly affect the axial stress of the string, but the change in the radius of curvature of the wellbore has a more significant effect on the maximum stress when the string is lowered. This finding is consistent with the conclusion of Pastusek et al.15. Their research shows that the response of BHA (such as the dip angle of the drill bit) is mainly determined by the curvature of the wellbore. The drill bit must produce a matching lateral cutting rate under sufficient lateral force to achieve a new balance in the system. This paper studies its influence on bending stress and normal contact stress. This is because the extremely small radius of wellbore curvature greatly increases the bending degree of the string, which leads to large bending stress and normal contact stress. Moreover, the increase of stress concentration coefficient shows that with the increase of wellbore curvature, the stress concentration phenomenon of the drilling tool in the lower bending point area is more significant, which may lead to the increase of plastic deformation and fatigue failure risk of the string. In addition, this study observed a significant stress concentration (coefficient of 2.1–2.8) at the lower inflection point of the drilling tool. This corresponds to the maximum bit tilt position in the Pastusek et al.15 model. The high inclination angle of the drill bit means that the drilling tool is in a severe bending state, which is completely consistent with the mechanism of the occurrence of high stress zone.

It should be specifically noted that under the condition of a 30 m curvature radius, the peak stress obtained from the simulation is 749.4 MPa. According to Table 2, this value is below the yield strength (835 MPa) of the drill tool material 35CrMo, resulting in a corresponding safety factor of approximately 1.11 (835/749.4≈1.11). Although this safety factor is greater than 1, indicating that the drill tool is theoretically in an elastic state, it is already close to the lower limit commonly recommended in engineering practice. This suggests that under these conditions, the drill tool is subjected to relatively high fatigue loading, posing potential risks during repeated use or in the presence of material defects, which should be given attention during field operations.

Conclusion and prospect

Main research conclusions

This study deeply explores the passing properties and mechanical behavior of the Φ73 mm double-bent screw under the double constraints of small wellbore (Φ120 mm) and high curvature (radius of curvature 15–30 m), and draws the following conclusions:Development of a Coupled WOB-Curvature Passability Model: An integrated passability model was established, simultaneously considering geometric, strength, and friction conditions. Its key innovation lies in revealing and quantifying the significant coupling effect between WOB and borehole curvature, treating them as interdependent rather than independent factors. This model provides quantitative safe WOB thresholds for different curvature radii: the tool can safely pass at 1–3 T WOB for R = 30 m, 1–2.5 T for R = 20 m, and 1–1.8 T for R = 15 m. This offers a precise operational window for field execution, a significant advancement over qualitative guidelines.Clarification of the Key Failure Mechanism under High Curvature: The study demonstrates that reducing the curvature radius has a far more pronounced impact on the maximum stress than varying the WOB, establishing curvature as the dominant factor governing passability failure. The significant stress concentration at the lower bend point, with factors ranging from 2.1 to 2.8, is identified as the root cause making this area a high-risk zone for fatigue failure. This finding addresses a critical gap in traditional analyses, which often lack quantitative assessment of local stress concentrations.Geometric constraints and deformation conditions: Geometric constraints are the main factor limiting the passage of screw drills through the casing section. The Φ73 mm double-bend screw drilling tool (1.5° + 3°) can pass through the Φ139 mm casing, but the reduction in the inner diameter at the casing joint results in a reduction of effective clearance by 35%-50%. Under deformation conditions, the maximum bending stress of the drill tool appears 10–15 m below the inclined point, and the stress concentration coefficient and the simulation error of the finite element is ± 6%.Key factors affecting: The radius of curvature of the wellbore and the drilling pressure are key factors affecting the passing of the pipe string. Stress concentration is particularly prominent in high curvature well sections and requires special attention during design and construction.

Engineering application suggestions and future research directions

Optimized drill tool design and selection

In the high curvature side drilling operations of small wellbores in the Southern Ordos Basin, Φ73 mm double-bent screw drilling tools (1.5° + 3° bending angle combination) are preferred, and the drilling pressure range is strictly controlled: when the curvature radius is ≥ 30 m, the drilling pressure can be safely set to 1-3 T; when the curvature radius is 20 m, the drilling pressure should not exceed 2.5 T; when the curvature radius is 15 m, the drilling pressure should be limited to within 1.8 T.For the casing section, the minimum diameter at the joint (such as Φ124.3 mm casing) needs to be checked to ensure that the displacement of the drilling tool down bend point (such as 47.3 mm) meets the geometric constraints.

Optimization of drilling fluid performance

For high curvature well sections, low viscosity (such as 10 mPa s) and high lubricity drilling fluid are selected to reduce the friction coefficient (recommended ≤ 0.25) and reduce the contact stress between the drilling tool and the well wall.

Throughness and casing coupling analysis

Drilling operations constitute a systematic engineering endeavor. Future research could explore a coupled analysis of drill string passability and casing wear. As demonstrated by Zhang et al.16 through theoretical and numerical simulations that systematically investigated the residual strength of worn casing considering wellbore curvature, it was clearly established that the contact friction between the drill string and casing during passage through high-curvature sections is the primary cause of casing wear and strength degradation. Building upon this foundation, developing an integrated wellbore integrity design spanning from drilling to completion would provide a more comprehensive theoretical framework for the safe and efficient development of complex structural wells.

Limitations of the study

Although the passability evaluation model established in this study can effectively guide the on-site construction in Southern Ordos Basin, there are still some limitations:

First, the model simplifies the formation as an isotropic, linearly elastic body, without accounting for the anisotropy (such as shale bedding), natural fractures, or fractured zones present in actual formations. To evaluate the impact of this assumption on the results, a preliminary sensitivity analysis was conducted: finite element simulations were performed by varying the formation’s Young’s modulus by ± 30% around the baseline value (15 GPa), i.e., from 10.5 to 19.5 GPa. The results indicate that under the critical scenario of a 15 m curvature radius and 1.8 T WOB, the variation in wellbore stiffness affects the maximum stress in the drill tool within a range of ± 8%, without altering the fundamental conclusion regarding passability (i.e., safe running is still feasible). However, it should be noted that geological defects may lead to non-uniform wellbore collapse or narrowing, which could significantly alter the actual geometry of the wellbore—under such circumstances, the accuracy of the model predictions would be affected.

Secondly, based on the linear elastic constitutive relation, this study does not consider the plastic deformation of drill steel under extremely high stress. This is a limitation for predicting the permanent damage or failure mode of drilling tools under extreme conditions (such as sticking).

Furthermore, the friction coefficient used in the model is a static empirical value and does not fully consider dynamic friction effects during the drilling process (such as friction variations induced by vibration and rotation). Future studies could combine downhole measured data to develop friction models that couple dynamic friction and fluid dynamic pressure, improving the accuracy of friction prediction.

These limitations point the way for further research in the future.

Tech Bytes—Harnessing Artificial Intelligence for Pediatric Oral Health: A Scoping Review
Authors: Dhvani A Tanna, Srikala Bhandary, K Sundeep Hegde
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractAim and backgroundThe applications of artificial intelligence (AI) are escalating in all frontiers, specifically healthcare. It constitutes the umbrella term for a number of technologies that enable machines to independently solve problems they have not been programmed to address. With its aid, patient management, diagnostics, treatment planning, and interventions can be significantly improved. The aim of this review is to analyze the current data to assess the applications of artificial intelligence in pediatric dentistry and determine their clinical effectiveness.Materials and methodsA search of published studies in PubMed, Web of Science, Scopus, and Google Scholar databases was included till January 2024.ResultsThis review consisted of 30 published studies in the English language. The use of AI has been employed in the detection of dental caries, dental plaque, behavioral science, interceptive orthodontics, predicting the dental age, and identification of teeth which can enhance patient care.ConclusionArtificial intelligence models can be used as an aid to the clinician as they are of significant help at individual and community levels in identifying an increased risk to dental diseases.Clinical significanceArtificial intelligence can be used as an asset in preventive school health programs, dental education for students and parents, and to assist the clinician in the dental practice. Further advancements in technology will give rise to newer potential innovations and applications.How to cite this articleTanna DA, Bhandary S, Hegde SK. Introduction

“Intelligence... is the faculty of making artificial objects, especially tools to make tools.”

—Henri Bergson (L'Evolution Creatice, 1907).

“Artificial intelligence” (AI) was coined by John McCarthy in 1955 and was claimed to be “the science and engineering of making intelligent machines.” It was a result of the combined efforts of Marvin Minsky, John McCarthy, Claude Shannon, and Nathan Rochester of IBM at a conference at Dartmouth College in 1956.1

It is a subdivision of computer science that designs intelligent computer systems that elicit qualities of intelligent behavior, such as deciphering language, learning analytical reasoning, explaining problems, and much more, without human involvement.2 It is believed that AI will soon have the ability to adapt and communicate with humans using similar languages, emotions, and gestures. Due to the widespread use and connectivity of various intelligent devices, people will exist not only in the physical world but also within the virtual network. The boundaries between humans and machines in this digital space will become increasingly indistinct.3

Artificial intelligence constitutes the umbrella term for a number of technologies that enable machines to independently solve problems they have not been programmed to address. It includes the following (Fig. 1).

Components of artificial intelligence

Machine learning: This employs algorithms for implementation of concepts. As the algorithms are introduced to additional data, they independently evolve over time and upgrade themselves to perform better in the future. As they process information, the machines are constantly adding new information.4 These algorithms can select tasks that have the highest chance of success. In machine learning, the concept of convolutional neural networks (CNN) mimics human neurons by forming a network which is arranged in layers and it transfers compound inputs of information (images, radiographs) to output information (diagnosis, treatment planning, and estimating prognosis).5

Deep learning: Deep convolutional neural networks allow complicated tasks to be learned and performed without human interference and this ability has further widened the applications of machine learning.6 This allows the process of making computer-aided diagnoses easier and faster.7

Artificial intelligence allows machines to mimic human minds by simulating the process of analyzing and learning.8

The potential utilization of intelligent techniques in medicine has been explored by researchers since the middle of the last century.9 A pathbreaking utilization of this science in surgery was successfully studied by Gunn in 1976 where a computer analysis was used to identify acute abdominal pain. Since then, there is rising interest in medical AI.10 The benefits of AI in the field of healthcare have enabled enhanced proactive care, a decreased future risk, and more simplified work process.

The use of AI in dentistry is a rapidly developing area because it helps clinicians deliver high-quality patient care and streamlines complex processes by producing predictable results. The cumbersome process of medical diagnosis can be made more accurate and efficient which aids in achieving superior patient care.11 However, the incorporation of AI into pediatric dentistry is still being investigated. The purpose of this scoping review is to investigate the applications of AI in pediatric dentistry and assess their benefits in providing enhanced patient care.

Materials and Methods

To identify articles that applied artificial intelligence in various applications of pediatric dentistry, a search was performed using the electronic databases PubMed, Web of Science, Scopus, and Google Scholar, including articles up till January 2024. The search terms used were “artificial intelligence,” “machine learning,” “deep learning,” “artificial neural networks,” “computational neural networks,” “pediatric dentistry,” “dentistry for children,” “preventive dentistry,” “special care dentistry,” and “dentistry for disabled.” These search terms were constructed with the help of Boolean operators with the use of English language filters. A manual search was conducted of the articles obtained. After removal of duplicate articles, 30 articles were obtained as shown in Table 1.

Discussion

The applications of artificial intelligence in medicine and healthcare are limitless. Their use is now being extended to dentistry in various branches, especially in dental radiology and diagnosis of oral diseases, orthodontics, restorative and prosthetic dentistry. Currently, uses of AI are being executed for quick and precise diagnosis. In conjunction with the clinician's skills and expertise, AI increases productivity and quality of patient care. Among the databases screened, 30 articles pertinent to this subject were identified with various clinical applications as depicted in Table 1.

Tooth detection and numbering on dental radiographs are the first steps in dental diagnostics. Deep learning-based screening models can detect and classify primary teeth,12 submerged teeth,13 and mesiodens.14 This aids in the complete automation and detection of primary teeth and their anomalies, which could go unnoticed on routine clinical and radiographic examination.

In orthodontics, AI-based models predict the sizes of unerupted premolars and canines, which allow early interception of tooth size arch length discrepancies in growing patients.15 Deep learning-based models have shown high specificity and sensitivity for the detection of ectopically erupting maxillary first molars in the early mixed dentition stage,16,17 which can prevent future complex orthodontic problems.

Dental age plays a key role in orthodontics, pediatric dentistry, endocrinology, forensic medicine, and pathomorphology. However, the current methods of age estimation are cumbersome and less precise. Machine and deep learning models18,19 have been developed which are simplified, quicker, and demonstrate comparable performance to the current age estimation methods. These models are based on the mineralization status of the teeth on panoramic radiographs.20 Moreover, AI models have the ability to determine skeletal maturity indicators, like the cervical vertebrae stages,21 which aid in treatment planning in growing patients.22 Orthodontic cephalometric analyses are technique-sensitive measurements made on a lateral cephalogram. AI programs have shown efficient performance when used as an adjunct to an expert's diagnosis.23,24

In this field, the early identification and interception of dental diseases are crucial. AI-based models25 have shown clinically acceptable performance in detection of plaque in primary teeth, which can improve the disease prognosis and patient education. The scope of AI has been expanded to allow prediction of dental caries in caries-free infants,26 toddlers,27 and children,28 through screening of potential risk factors.29 Reyes et al.30 conducted a 10-year prospective cohort study to develop a model that could detect dental caries on primary and permanent teeth by evaluating the risk factors. Other models have been developed for diagnosis of early childhood caries,31 and similar pathologies like molar incisor hypomineralization, fluorosis, and amelogenesis imperfecta.32,33 These models have enhanced capabilities in the classification of early childhood caries33 and the recognition of cavitated and noncavitated lesions in children.34 This can allow the early identification of children at a higher risk of developing dental disease and also function as a teaching aid for parents and dental students.35,36

Dentistry remains inaccessible to many segments of society, particularly in a diverse country like India. Parents and caregivers can use AI-powered smartphone applications like AICaries37 for home care measures to prevent and identify early carious lesions. The added advantage of AI-powered models is that it can be used for preventive strategies where at-home evaluation can be carried out.37

Orofacial clefts are among the most common major congenital malformations in humans.38 Deep neural networks have also been used for the identification of orofacial clefts using intraoral photographs,39 and panoramic radiographs,40,41 which save time in making the diagnosis as an adjunct to observer interpretations.

Behavior guidance is the cornerstone of pediatric dentistry. The current generation leads a technology-dependent lifestyle. The standalone effectiveness of conventional behavior guidance techniques is questionable. To alleviate anxiety, a humanoid robot was developed as a techno-psychological distraction method, which has been successfully used in the dental operatory.42

Artificial intelligence has a vast majority of potential applications in pediatric dentistry to improve the field of behavior guidance, diagnostics, treatment planning, and patient management. Future research should focus on adding larger datasets and machine learning algorithms specific to pediatric dentistry to make AI models clinically effective to enhance oral health.

Conclusion

Artificial intelligence is an emerging valuable tool in the field of healthcare, including dentistry. It can be of assistance in preventive and restorative dentistry, diagnostic sciences, patient education, and more. It can be used for early and quick diagnosis, treatment planning, and enhanced patient care. However, despite its wide array of applications, it can never substitute for the expertise of experienced dentists.

-Clinical Significance

Artificial intelligence-based models are highly beneficial at both individual and community levels, effectively identifying and categorizing children into risk groups, numbering teeth, diagnosing early ectopic eruptions, and assessing age, among other tasks. Additionally, these models can assist in the planning and evaluation of school oral health programs, helping children become more aware of their dental health.

Are 2HDMs with a gauged U ⁡ ( 1 ) U(1) symmetry alive?
Yuanchao Lou, Takaaki Nomura, Xinran Xu, Kei Yagyu

Abstract
We investigate the phenomenology of 2 Higgs doublet models (2HDMs) with a new U ⁡ ( 1 ) U(1) gauge symmetry, U ​ ( 1 ) X U(1)_{X} , by which flavor changing neutral currents are forbidden at tree level. As an important consequence of the spontaneous breaking of both the U ​ ( 1 ) X U(1)_{X} and electroweak symmetries by electroweak vacuum expectation values, upper limits appear on masses of an additional gauge boson Z ′ Z^{\prime} and extra Higgs bosons which are less than the TeV scale. In addition, the standard model (SM) like Higgs boson h h and a heavier Higgs boson H H mainly decay into a pair of Z ′ Z^{\prime} which induces four lepton final states. These new decay modes cannot be suppressed by taking no Z Z - Z ′ Z^{\prime} mixing and/or the Higgs alignment limit. We find that the minimum setup of these 2HDMs has been excluded by current data for four lepton searches at LHC. Such severe constraints can, however, be avoided by introducing a pair of vector-like fermions χ \chi which are singlet under the SM symmetry but charged under U ​ ( 1 ) X U(1)_{X} , and can be a candidate of dark matter. Thanks to the existence of χ \chi , Z ′ Z^{\prime} can mainly decay into χ ​ χ ¯ \chi\bar{\chi} instead of SM leptons. As benchmark models, we consider the U ​ ( 1 ) H U(1)_{H} and U ​ ( 1 ) R U(1)_{R} models realized by fixing specific U ​ ( 1 ) X U(1)_{X} charges, and find regions of parameter space allowed by theoretical and current experimental constraints. We clarify that m H ∈ [ 160,220 ] m_{H}\in[160,220] GeV and tan ⁡ β ∈ [ 3 , 4.4 ] \tan\beta\in[3,4.4] are allowed in the U ​ ( 1 ) H U(1)_{H} model, while m H ∈ [ 160,380 ] m_{H}\in[160,380] GeV and tan ⁡ β ∈ [ 1.6 , 4.4 ] \tan\beta\in[1.6,4.4] are allowed in the U ​ ( 1 ) R U(1)_{R} model. In both the models, the Z ′ Z^{\prime} mass is constrained to be 100 ​ GeV ≲ m Z ′ ≲ 110 100~\text{GeV}\lesssim m_{Z^{\prime}}\lesssim 110 GeV. Such a quite limited parameter space can further be explored at future collider experiments, e.g., High-Luminosity LHC and lepton colliders.



I
Introduction

The gauge principle has been remarkably successful in describing the fundamental forces for elementary particles–namely, the electromagnetic, strong, and weak interactions.
Gravity is also expected to be governed by a gauge symmetry
65
, although its experimental confirmation of this idea has not been confirmed yet. A natural question that follows is: Are there any fifth forces in nature?

The simplest possibility for such a fifth force is the existence of a new
U(1)
gauge symmetry, which we denote by
U(1)_{X}
.
In fact, various new physics scenarios predict a
U(1)
gauge symmetry such as grand unified theories, e.g.,
E_{6}
and
SO(10)

38
. In addition, right-handed neutrinos are naturally introduced in models with
U(1)_{X}
due to the gauge anomaly
cancellation
59
;
52
.
Furthermore, we can construct models beyond the Standard Model (SM) which can explain not only the neutrino masses but also dark matter and baryon asymmetry of the Universe, e.g.,
53
.

One of the most important consequences of introducing the
U(1)_{X}
symmetry is the appearance of a new gauge boson
Z^{\prime}
. Because the massless
Z^{\prime}
has highly been constrained by the tests of equivalence principle
37
, we consider the massive
Z^{\prime}
case. The mass of
Z^{\prime}
is most economically generated by a Vacuum Expectation Value (VEV) of a scalar field which is neutral under the SM gauge symmetry but charged under
U(1)_{X}
.
This scenario is compatible with the seesaw mechanism, and thus a number of phenomenological studies have been performed so far, see Refs.
31
;
50
as review papers.
However, there is,
a priori
, no reason to choose the Higgs sector to be one SM Higgs doublet and a singlet fields, but we can consider the other possibilities as long as the structure of the spontaneous breaking
SU(2)_{L}\times U(1)_{Y}\times U(1)_{X}\to U(1)_{\rm em}
is maintained.

In this paper, we investigate models with
U(1)_{X}
which is spontaneously broken by VEVs of two Higgs doublet fields instead of a singlet scalar field. Because the current measurements of the Higgs properties at the LHC
1
;
64
indicate the existence of at least one Higgs doublet field, the existence of the second Higgs doublet seems to be plausible. Although 2 Higgs doublet models (2HDMs) suffer from the appearance of the Flavor Changing Neutral Currents (FCNCs) at tree level due to flavor violating couplings of neutral Higgs bosons, our scenario naturally suppress such dangerous FCNCs due to the
U(1)_{X}
symmetry
49
;
48
;
23
;
41
;
16
.
One of the remarkable differences from the models with a singlet scalar boson is the appearance of upper limits on the masses of
Z^{\prime}
and extra Higgs bosons, typically less than the TeV scale due to the spontaneous breaking of
U(1)_{X}
and the electroweak symmetry by the electroweak VEVs. Another important difference can be seen in the Higgs boson decays.
We find that the couplings of the 125 GeV Higgs boson (
h
) and a heavier Higgs boson
H
with a
Z^{\prime}
pair does not vanish at the Higgs alignment limit and/or no
Z
-
Z^{\prime}
mixing limit.
As a consequence, the branching ratio of
h
can significantly be modified from the SM prediction, and the cross section of four lepton final states is enhanced by the decays of
h/H\to Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime(*)}\to 4\ell
. By combining them, we clarify that the minimal setup of 2HDMs with
U(1)_{X}
has been excluded by the current LHC data.

We thus introduce a pair of vector-like fermions
\chi
which are singlet under the SM symmetry but charged under
U(1)_{X}
, and they can be a candidate of dark matter as their stability is guaranteed by
U(1)_{X}
. The main decay mode of
Z^{\prime}
can then turn out to be the
\chi\bar{\chi}
final state instead of SM leptons, so that we can avoid the constraints from 4 lepton events via the Higgs decays.
We find the parameter space allowed by the theoretical bounds and current experimental data in the two benchmark models, i.e., the
U(1)_{H}
and
U(1)_{R}
models.

This paper is organized as follows.
In Sec.
II
, we give the Lagrangians for the gauge sector and the Higgs sector, and provide the relevant interaction terms.
In Sec.
III
, we discuss the new decay modes of the SM-like Higgs boson and the extra Higgs bosons.
Sec.
IV
is devoted for the discussion of theoretical bounds and experimental constraints.
We then show the parameter space allowed by all the constraints in Sec.
V
.
Conclusion is given in Sec.
VI
.

II.1
Gauge-kinetic term

The most general gauge kinetic term is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{gauge}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4}W^{a}_{\mu\nu}W^{a\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{4}(\tilde{B}_{\mu\nu},\tilde{X}_{\mu\nu})\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&\sin\epsilon\\
\sin\epsilon&1\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\tilde{B}^{\mu\nu}\\
\tilde{X}^{\mu\nu}\end{array}\right),

where
W_{\mu\nu}^{a}
,
\tilde{B}_{\mu\nu}
and
\tilde{X}_{\mu\nu}
are respectively the field strength tensors of
SU(2)_{L}
,
U(1)_{Y}
and
U(1)_{X}
gauge fields, respectively, and
\sin\epsilon
denotes the kinetic mixing parameter.

We can move to the new basis without the kinetic mixing through the following non-unitary transformation
39
,

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\tilde{B}_{\mu}\\
\tilde{X}_{\mu}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&-\tan\epsilon\\
0&\sec\epsilon\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}B_{\mu}\\
X_{\mu}\end{array}\right).

In this basis, the kinetic terms take the diagonal form, and the covariant derivative is then expressed as

\displaystyle D_{\mu}\Psi=(\partial_{\mu}-igT_{\Psi}^{a}W_{\mu}^{a}-ig^{\prime}Y_{\Psi}B_{\mu}-ig_{X}X_{\Psi}X_{\mu})\Psi,

(11)

where

\displaystyle X_{\Psi}

\displaystyle=\tilde{X}_{\Psi}-Y_{\Psi}\frac{g^{\prime}}{g_{X}}\tan\epsilon.

(12)

Three neutral gauge bosons (
W_{\mu}^{3},B_{\mu},X_{\mu}
) are mixed after the electroweak symmetry breaking and their linear combinations would be the massless photon
A_{\mu}
and massive gauge bosons
Z
and
Z^{\prime}
, see Eq. (
32
).

II.2
Higgs sector

In this subsection, we give the Lagrangian related to the Higgs fields, i.e., the Higgs potential, the Yukawa interactions and the kinetic term.

The Higgs potential is generally expressed as

\displaystyle V=m_{1}^{2}|\Phi_{1}|^{2}+m_{2}^{2}|\Phi_{2}|^{2}+\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2}|\Phi_{1}|^{4}+\frac{\lambda_{2}}{2}|\Phi_{2}|^{4}+\lambda_{3}|\Phi_{1}|^{2}|\Phi_{2}|^{2}+\lambda_{4}|\Phi_{1}^{\dagger}\Phi_{2}|^{2},

(13)

where all the parameters are real.
The mixing terms
\Phi_{1}^{\dagger}\Phi_{2}
and
(\Phi_{1}^{\dagger}\Phi_{2})^{2}

are forbidden due to the
U(1)_{X}
gauge symmetry, so that this potential corresponds to the special case of that given in 2HDMs with a softly-broken
Z_{2}
symmetry

.
We can parameterize the two Higgs doublet fields as

\displaystyle\Phi_{i}=\begin{pmatrix}\omega_{i}^{+}\\
\frac{h_{i}+v_{i}+iz_{i}}{\sqrt{2}}\end{pmatrix}~~~(i=1,2),

(14)

where
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
are the VEVs of the doublets which satisfy the relation
v^{2}\equiv v_{1}^{2}+v_{2}^{2}=(\sqrt{2}G_{F})^{-1}
with
G_{F}
being the Fermi constant.
In the above expression, both
z_{1}
and
z_{2}
become the Nambu-Goldstone (NG) bosons which turn out to be the longitudinal components of the
Z
and
Z^{\prime}
bosons.
Thus, there is no physical CP-odd Higgs boson, normally denoted as
A
, in our model.
The charged NG bosons
G^{\pm}
and the physical charged-Higgs bosons
H^{\pm}
can be identidied by the following orthogonal transformation:

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\omega_{1}^{\pm}\\
\omega_{2}^{\pm}\end{pmatrix}=R(\beta)\begin{pmatrix}G^{\pm}\\
H^{\pm}\end{pmatrix},

(15)

where
\tan\beta=v_{2}/v_{1}
, and

\displaystyle R(\theta)=\begin{pmatrix}c_{\theta}&-s_{\theta}\\
s_{\theta}&c_{\theta}\end{pmatrix},

(16)

with

,
s_{\theta}=\sin\theta
and

The remaining two CP-even components

and

can be mixed with each other as follows

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}h_{1}\\

(17)

where we identify the
h

24

By solving the tadpole conditions, i.e.,

\displaystyle T_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial V}{\partial h_{1}}\Bigg|_{0}\leftrightarrow m_{1}^{2}=-\frac{v^{2}}{2}\left[\lambda_{1}c^{2}_{\beta}+(\lambda_{3}+\lambda_{4})s^{2}_{\beta}\right],

(18)

\displaystyle T_{2}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{\partial V}{\partial h_{2}}\Bigg|_{0}\leftrightarrow m_{2}^{2}=-\frac{v^{2}}{2}\left[\lambda_{2}s^{2}_{\beta}+(\lambda_{3}+\lambda_{4})c^{2}_{\beta}\right],

(19)

and

parameters.
The mass of

\displaystyle m_{H^{\pm}}^{2}=-\frac{v^{2}}{2}\lambda_{4}.

(20)

The mass matrix for the neutral Higgs bosons is given in the basis of (

\displaystyle M_{\rm neut}^{2}=v^{2}\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{1}c^{2}_{\beta}&(\lambda_{3}+\lambda_{4})c_{\beta}s_{\beta}\\

\displaystyle=v^{2}\left[\lambda_{2}s^{2}_{\beta}c^{2}_{\alpha}+\lambda_{1}c^{2}_{\beta}s^{2}_{\alpha}+\frac{1}{2}(\lambda_{3}+\lambda_{4})s_{2\beta}s_{2\alpha}\right],

III.1
h
decays

In our model,
h
can decay into new modes such as
Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime}
and
Z^{\prime}Z
.
When these modes are kinematically allowed, the decay rates are given by

\displaystyle\Gamma(h\to Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{m_{h}^{3}}{32\pi v^{2}}\left(s_{\beta-\alpha}+c_{\beta-\alpha}\frac{1-t^{2}_{\beta}}{t_{\beta}}\right)^{2}\left[12x_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}+\lambda(x_{Z^{\prime}},x_{Z^{\prime}})\right]\lambda^{1/2}(x_{Z^{\prime}},x_{Z^{\prime}}),

(46)

\displaystyle\Gamma(h\to ZZ^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{m_{h}^{3}}{{16\pi v^{2}}}c^{2}_{\beta-\alpha}\left[12x_{Z}x_{Z^{\prime}}+\lambda(x_{Z},x_{Z^{\prime}})\right]\lambda^{1/2}(x_{Z^{\prime}},x_{Z}),

(47)

where
\lambda(x,y)\equiv(1-x-y)^{2}-4xy
and
x_{V}\equiv m_{V}^{2}/m_{h}^{2}
.
We see that the decay rate of
h\to ZZ^{\prime}
vanishes in the alignment limit
c_{\beta-\alpha}\to 0
, while that of
h\to Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime}
is not suppressed in this limit. For
m_{Z^{\prime}}/m_{h}\ll 1
, the decay rate is approximately given in the alignment limit

\displaystyle\Gamma(h\to Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{m_{h}^{3}}{32\pi v^{2}}[1-6x_{Z^{\prime}}+{\cal O}(x_{Z^{\prime}}^{2})]\simeq 0.3\left[1-0.04\left(\frac{m_{Z^{\prime}}}{10~\text{GeV}}\right)^{2}\right]~\text{GeV}.

(48)

This is quite huge as compared with the total width of the SM Higgs boson, i.e.,
\Gamma_{h}^{\rm SM}=4.1
MeV
27
.
Therefore, the
h\to Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime}
mode easily dominates the branching ratio of the Higgs boson, and the model is excluded by the current LHC data
1
;
64
.

We thus consider the case with
m_{Z^{\prime}}>m_{h}/2
, where the decay of
h\to Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime}
is not kinematically allowed, but
h\to Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime*}\to Z^{\prime}f\bar{f}
is possible with
f
being a SM fermion if
m_{Z^{\prime}}<m_{h}
.
The decay width of such a three body decay is given by

\displaystyle\Gamma(h\to Z^{\prime}f\bar{f})

\displaystyle=\frac{m_{Z^{\prime}}^{4}g_{ffZ^{\prime}}^{2}(v^{2}_{f}+a^{2}_{f})}{32\pi^{3}m_{h}v^{2}}N_{f}^{c}\left(s_{\beta-\alpha}+c_{\beta-\alpha}\frac{1-t_{\beta}^{2}}{t_{\beta}}\right)^{2}H(x_{Z^{\prime}},x_{Z^{\prime}}),

(49)

where
N_{f}^{c}=3~(1)
for
f
being quarks (leptons) and
g_{ffZ^{\prime}}
,
v_{f}
and
a_{f}
are given in Table
2
.
The phase space function
H
is given by

\displaystyle H(x,y^{*})

\displaystyle=\frac{\arctan\left(\dfrac{1-x-y^{*}}{\sqrt{-\lambda(x,y^{*})}}\right)+\arctan\left(\dfrac{1-x+y^{*}}{\sqrt{-\lambda(x,y^{*})}}\right)}{2x\sqrt{-\lambda(x,y^{*})}}

\displaystyle\times\left[(1-y^{*})^{3}-3x^{3}+(9y^{*}+7)x^{2}-5(1-y^{*})^{2}x\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{12xy^{*}}\Biggl\{(x-1)\left[6y^{*2}+y^{*}(39x-9)+2(1-x)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad-3y^{*}\left[y^{*2}+2y^{*}(3x-1)-x(3x+4)+1\right]\ln x\Biggr\},

(50)

where
y^{*}
in
H(x,y^{*})
corresponding to a valuable for an off-shell particle.
For
y^{*}=x^{*}
, we obtain

\displaystyle H(x,x^{*})

\displaystyle=\frac{20x^{2}-8x+1}{2x\sqrt{4x-1}}\arccos\left(\frac{3x-1}{2x^{3/2}}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{x-1}{12x^{2}}\left(47x^{2}-13x+2\right)-\frac{\ln x}{4x}(4x^{2}-6x+1),

(51)

which is consistent with the expression given in Ref.
47
.
This function takes order unity for
x\gtrsim 1/4
(
m_{Z^{\prime}}\gtrsim m_{h}/2
), while it is suppressed by

\displaystyle H(1-\delta,1-\delta)=\frac{\delta^{5}}{20}+{\cal O}(\delta^{6}).

(52)

From this expression, we see that the decay rate is rapidly suppressed when the mass of
Z^{\prime}
approaches to the Higgs mass, i.e.,
m_{Z^{\prime}}\lesssim m_{h}
.

\displaystyle\Gamma(H\to Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{m_{H}^{3}}{{16\pi v^{2}}}s^{2}_{\beta-\alpha}\left[12y_{Z}y_{Z^{\prime}}+\lambda(y_{Z},y_{Z^{\prime}})\right]\lambda^{1/2}(y_{Z},y_{Z^{\prime}}),

where

\displaystyle=\frac{m_{Z}^{2}m_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}g_{ffZ^{\prime}}^{2}(v^{2}_{f}+a^{2}_{f})}{32\pi^{3}m_{H}v^{2}}N_{f}^{c}s_{\beta-\alpha}^{2}H(y_{Z},y_{Z^{\prime}}),

\displaystyle\Gamma(H\to Z^{\prime}Z^{*}\to Z^{\prime}f\bar{f})

\displaystyle=\frac{m_{Z}^{2}m_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}g_{Z}^{2}[(v_{f}^{\rm SM})^{2}+(a_{f}^{\rm SM})^{2}]}{32\pi^{3}m_{H}v^{2}}N_{f}^{c}s_{\beta-\alpha}^{2}H(y_{Z^{\prime}},y_{Z}),

where
H(x,y)
is given in Eq. (

and

\displaystyle+6\left(1+x-y^{*}\right)y^{*}\sqrt{-\lambda(x,y^{*})}\left[\arctan\left(\frac{-1+x-y^{*}}{\sqrt{-\lambda(x,y^{*})}}\right)+\arctan\left(\frac{-1+x+y^{*}}{\sqrt{-\lambda(x,y^{*})}}\right)\right]

(60)

where

\displaystyle\Gamma(H^{\pm}\to W^{\pm}Z^{\prime*}\to W^{\pm}f\bar{f})

\displaystyle\Gamma(H^{\pm}\to Z^{\prime}W^{\pm*}\to Z^{\prime}f\bar{f}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{m_{W}^{2}m_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}g^{2}}{64\pi^{3}m_{H^{\pm}}v^{2}}N_{f}^{c}H(z_{Z^{\prime}},z_{W}).

(62)

In addition, the decay of

IV
Constraints

In this section, we discuss the constraints on the model parameters from theoretical bounds and experimental data.

IV.1
Unitarity and vacuum stability

We can apply similar constraints on the parameters of the Higgs potential
from perturbative unitarity
45
;
33
;
46
and vacuum stability
28
;
56
;
44
, i.e., the condition for bounded from below as those given in the 2HDM with a
Z_{2}
symmetry.
Since the terms

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{5}}{2}(\Phi_{1}^{\dagger}\Phi_{2})^{2}+\text{h.c.}

are forbidden by the
U(1)_{X}
symmetry, we can recast the expressions of these constraints without the
\lambda_{5}
parameter.
Therefore, the bounded from below condition is simply expressed as

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}>0,~~\lambda_{2}>0,~~\sqrt{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}+\lambda_{3}+\operatorname{MIN}\left(0,\lambda_{4}\right)>0.

(64)

From Eq. (
29
), the sign of
\lambda_{4}
has to be negative. Thus, the last condition in the above can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\sqrt{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}+\lambda_{3}-|\lambda_{4}|>0.

(65)

In the Higgs alignment limit, the above condition can be written by using Eqs. (
26
)-(
29
):

\displaystyle\sqrt{(m_{h}^{2}+m_{H}^{2}t_{\beta}^{2})(m_{h}^{2}+m_{H}^{2}/t_{\beta}^{2})}+m_{H}^{2}-m_{h}^{2}>0.

(66)

Therefore, as long as
m_{H}^{2}>0
, the vacuum stability condition is satisfied.

For the perturbative unitarity bound, we impose

\displaystyle|\text{Re}(a_{i})|\leq\frac{1}{2},

(67)

where
a_{i}
are the eigenvalues of the
s
-wave amplitude matrix for
2\to 2
elastic scatterings in the high-energy limit.
In our model,
a_{i}
are given by

\displaystyle a_{1}^{\pm}=\frac{1}{32\pi}\left[3(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})\pm\sqrt{9(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})^{2}+4(2\lambda_{3}+\lambda_{4})^{2}}\right],

(68)

\displaystyle a_{2}^{\pm}=\frac{1}{32\pi}\left[\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}\pm\sqrt{(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})^{2}+4\lambda_{4}^{2}}\right],

(69)

\displaystyle a_{3}^{\pm}=\frac{1}{32\pi}\left[\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}\pm\sqrt{(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})^{2}}\right],

(70)

\displaystyle a_{4}=\frac{1}{16\pi}(\lambda_{3}+2\lambda_{4}),

(71)

\displaystyle a_{5}=\frac{1}{16\pi}\lambda_{3},

(72)

\displaystyle a_{6}=\frac{1}{16\pi}(\lambda_{3}+\lambda_{4}).

(73)

These constraints can be converted into bounds on the Higgs boson masses and mixing angles via Eqs. (
26
)-(
29
). We find that
a_{1}^{+}
provides the strongest constraint among the above eigenvalues.
Thus, the maximally allowed value of
m_{H}^{2}
is obtained by the following expression for fixed values of
m_{H^{\pm}}
and
t_{\beta}

\displaystyle m_{H}^{2}(\text{max})

\displaystyle=\frac{6M_{0}^{2}}{5}\Bigg[\xi^{2}-\frac{R}{6}-\sqrt{\left(\xi^{2}-\frac{R}{6}\right)^{2}-\frac{5}{12^{2}}(R-1)(5-R)}\Bigg],

(74)

where

\displaystyle\xi

\displaystyle=\frac{t_{\beta}+1/t_{\beta}}{2}~(\geq 1),~~R=\frac{4m_{H^{\pm}}^{2}+24\pi v^{2}-5m_{h}^{2}}{M_{0}^{2}},~M_{0}^{2}=8\pi v^{2}-3m_{h}^{2}\simeq 24v^{2}.

(75)

Since
\xi
is symmetric under
t_{\beta}\leftrightarrow 1/t_{\beta}
, the bound for
0<t_{\beta}<1
is obtained from that of
t_{\beta}>1
by the replacement of
t_{\beta}\to 1/t_{\beta}
.
The
R
value is constrained to be

3\lesssim R\leq 5
, where the lower bound is given by taking
m_{H^{\pm}}^{2}=0
. The upper bound on
R~(=5)
is obtained by noting that the second term inside the square root in Eq. (
74
) vanishes, by which
m_{H}^{2}\text{(max)}
becomes zero.
This upper limit value
R=5
corresponds to
m_{H^{\pm}}\simeq 850
GeV, which gives the absolute upper limit on
m_{H^{\pm}}
.
We note that
m_{H}^{2}\text{(max)}
takes its maximal value at
\xi=1
, i.e.,
t_{\beta}=1
, for a fixed value of
m_{H^{\pm}}
and it monotonically decreases as
\xi
getting larger.

As we have seen in Sec.

VI
Conclusions

We have discussed 2 Higgs doublet models (2HDMs) with a new
U(1)
gauge symmetry, denoted as
U(1)_{X}
, which induces a new neutral gauge boson
Z^{\prime}
. In those models, we also introduced a pair of vector-like fermions
\chi
which are SM-singlet but charged under
U(1)_{X}
in order to avoid constraints from Higgs boson decays into four leptons at LHC by modifying the branching ratio of the
Z^{\prime}
boson.
In addition,
\chi
could be a dark matter candidate whose stability is guaranteed by the
U(1)_{X}
symmetry.
Furthermore, we imposed the condition to cancel the
Z
-
Z^{\prime}
mixing to avoid constraints from precision measurement of the
Z
boson interactions.
As concrete benchmark models, we have considered the
U(1)_{H}
and
U(1)_{R}
models, where
Z^{\prime}
interactions with SM particles are induced only via the kinetic-mixing in the former while
those with left-handed fermions vanish due to the
U(1)
charge assignment in the latter.

The Higgs sector of our scenario corresponds to more constrained version of a usual
Z_{2}
symmetric case with a soft-breaking term, i.e., no
\lambda_{5}
term in the potential.
In addition, a physical CP-odd Higgs boson does not appear because it would be a longitudinal component of
Z^{\prime}
. The Yukawa interactions take the same form as those given in the Type-I 2HDM.
We have imposed the Higgs alignment limit, by which the couplings for the SM-like Higgs boson
h
become the same as those of the SM predictions at tree level.

One of the most important differences from models with
U(1)_{X}
which is spontaneously broken by the vacuum expectation value (VEV) of a SM-singlet scalar field is the appearance of the upper limit on the masses of
Z^{\prime}
and extra Higgs bosons (
H
and
H^{\pm}
) due to the fact that both
U(1)_{X}
and the electroweak symmetry are broken by the electroweak VEVs. Thus, their masses are typically constrained to be smaller than the TeV scale.
Another significant difference can be seen in the
h
decays, i.e.,
h\to Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime}
can easily dominate the branching ratio of
h
in our models, and it cannot be suppressed by taking the no
Z
-
Z^{\prime}
mixing and the Higgs alignment limit.
Such a large decay width of
h\to Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime}
can only be avoided by taking a larger mass of
Z^{\prime}
(
m_{Z^{\prime}}>m_{h}/2
) such that the on-shell decay of
h\to Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime}
is kinematically forbidden.

We then have taken into account various constraints on the parameter space under the no
Z
-
Z^{\prime}
mixing condition and the Higgs alignment limit.
As for theoretical constraints, we have imposed the vacuum stability, perturbative unitarity
and perturbativity of the
Z^{\prime}\bar{\chi}\chi
interaction.
We found that the perturbative unitarity constraint sets a severe upper limit on the masses of
H
and
H^{\pm}
which are analytically expressed in Eq. (
74
). We obtained
m_{H}\lesssim 700
GeV and

m_{H^{\pm}}\lesssim 850
GeV.
We have also taken into account the constraints from oblique parameters, flavor data, signal significance of
h\to\gamma\gamma
and
h/H\to 4\ell
as well as the various
Z^{\prime}
searches.
Among the above, the decay width of
h\to\gamma\gamma
is significantly modified due to the
H^{\pm}
loop contribution, and the current LHC data require the mass of
H^{\pm}
to be heavier than around 160 GeV.
In addition, the cross section of the
h/H\to 4\ell
processes are enhanced due to the new decay channels of
h/H\to Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime}
and
H\to ZZ^{\prime}
where one of the gauge bosons can be an off-shell mode.
These constraints from 4 lepton final states can be avoided by considering the scenario where

Z^{\prime}
dominantly decays into
\chi\bar{\chi}
(missing final states) which can be realized by taking larger values of
\chi\bar{\chi}Z^{\prime}
interaction under the perturbativity limit.
Without introducing
\chi
, the models are completely excluded by the constraint.

Finally, we have combined all the constraints discussed above.
We have found that the allowed region is given to be

m_{Z^{\prime}}\in[96,110]
GeV and
\epsilon\in[0.46,0.78]
in the
U(1)_{H}
model with
R_{1}=30
and

m_{Z^{\prime}}\in[93,110]
GeV and
x_{R}g_{X}\in[0.087,0.25]
in the
U(1)_{R}
model with
R_{2}=30
as summarized in Fig.
10
.
Furthermore, the allowed region has been found to be
m_{H}\in[160,220]
GeV and
\tan\beta\in[3,4.4]
in the
U(1)_{H}
model and

m_{H}\in[160,380]
GeV and
\tan\beta\in[1.6,4.4]
for
U(1)_{R}
model as summarized in Fig.
11
.

Therefore, the allowed region in the models is strongly limitted by current phenomenological data, and the full region could be tested in future experiments such as the HL-LHC, the ILC, the FCC-ee, and the CEPC experiments.

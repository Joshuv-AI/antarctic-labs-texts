Central Message

Endotension after TEVAR may lead to progressive aneurysm sac expansion and vertebral erosion without endoleak, requiring multimodality imaging and multidisciplinary management.

A 65-year-old man with arterial hypertension and descending thoracic aortic aneurysm, without evidence of syphilitic, mycotic, or connective tissue etiology, was treated with thoracic endovascular aortic repair using 3 endografts. Approximately 3 years after the initial procedure, he presented with thoracic pain radiating to the back and left-lower extremity numbness. On admission, he was hemodynamically stable, without ischemic changes on electrocardiography and negative troponin levels. Transthoracic echocardiography demonstrated normal cardiac function, ascending aortic dilatation to 49 mm, and mild-to-moderate aortic regurgitation.

Coronary angiography was performed as part of preoperative cardiac evaluation in preparation for potential open surgical graft explantation and did not reveal obstructive coronary artery disease. Computed tomography aortography demonstrated enlargement of the aneurysm sac surrounding the thoracic endografts with T11 vertebral body erosion and loss of the intervening fat plane (Figure 1, Videos 1 and 2). There was no history of renal insufficiency, osteoporosis, or other metabolic bone disorders, whereas serum calcium and phosphorus values were within normal ranges.Figure 1Axial computed tomography scan shows a thoracic endovascular aortic repair graft compressing the vertebral body with cortical erosion.

Multiplanar reconstruction confirmed direct contact between the aneurysm sac and vertebral cortex. Digital subtraction angiography demonstrated no endograft endoleak. Magnetic resonance imaging confirmed cortical destruction of T11 without epidural abscess or spinal cord compression (Figure 2).Figure 2T1-weighted magnetic resonance imaging scan shows vertebral body erosion adjacent to the thoracic endovascular aortic repair aneurysm sac.

Multidisciplinary evaluation was performed. In the absence of endoleak and given progressive structural vertebral destruction, high operative risk of graft explantation, and the patient's preference, spinal stabilization was recommended to prevent mechanical instability and neurologic compromise.

Progressive aneurysm sac enlargement after thoracic endovascular aortic repair without detectable endoleak is described as endotension.1 Progressive sac expansion may result in structural complications, including vertebral erosion.2 The proposed mechanism of vertebral erosion is the rhythmic pulsation of the aorta against the adjacent vertebral body. In each cardiac cycle, the pulsatile force exerts repetitive mechanical stress on the vertebral cortex, leading to progressive pressure erosion.

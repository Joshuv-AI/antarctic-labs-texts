Waviness and self-sustained turbulence in plane Couette-Poiseuille flow
M. Etchevest, P. Dmitruk, S. Karmakar, B. Semin, R. Godoy-Diana, J.E. Wesfreid

Abstract
Direct numerical simulations of a Couette-Poiseuille flow were performed near the transition to turbulence to investigate the nonlinear relationship between streak waviness and rolls. This relationship is a key step in Waleffe’s model for a self-sustaining process (SSP). Simulations were conducted for Reynolds numbers ranging from 500 to 940, and a range of initial perturbation amplitudes were used. In these simulations, the streaks, rolls, and streak waviness initially increase. The optimal time for this growth is close to the linear transient growth period for small perturbations, but much shorter for large, highly nonlinear perturbations. For higher Reynolds numbers and large initial perturbations, the velocity field reaches a turbulent steady state, whereas it relaxes to a laminar state in other cases. The main result is that the waviness of the streaks is a quadratic function of the rolls, provided that its value is sufficiently large.



I
Introduction

Shear flows are ubiquitous in both natural and industrial settings, ranging from atmospheric and oceanic currents to engineering systems such as pipelines and aerodynamics around transport vehicles. Understanding the transition to turbulence and its dynamics in these flows is crucial to optimizing energy efficiency, improving control strategies, and predicting flow behavior under various conditions. From a fundamental point of view, the study of turbulence, and in particular the knowledge of the mechanisms of transition, remains with many open problems to be solved.

Streamwise elongated zones of high and low velocity (
streaks
) and streamwise vortices (
rolls
), are recognized as fundamental components in the transition to turbulence in wall-bounded shear flows
Elder 1960
;
Klebanoff et al. 1962
;
Kline et al. 1967
. These structures are critical for understanding both the onset of turbulence and its maintenance at high Reynolds numbers. Above a critical Reynolds number, the turbulent regime can be achieved and maintained through a mechanism called the Self-Sustaining Process (SSP)
Jiménez and Moin 1991
;
Hamilton et al. 1995
;
Waleffe 1997
. This mechanism consists of a cycle that involves a dynamic interplay between the streaks and rolls, forming a closed loop of energy transfer, via linear and non-linear interactions.

The formation of elongated streaks results from the lift-up effect, where streamwise vortices advect velocities of different magnitudes from the walls, creating a striped streak pattern
Landahl 1975
. These streaks can then develop wavy instabilities due to shear caused by the velocity difference between adjacent fast and slow streaks
Hamilton et al. 1995
;
Waleffe 1997
;
Farrell and Ioannou 2012
. Eventually, the instability can result in a breakdown of the streaks, transferring energy back to the rolls through non-linear interactions. Experimentally and numerically, the breakdown of streaks has been analyzed in boundary layer and channel flows
Mans et al. 2005
;
Mans et al. 2007
;
Brandt et al. 2004
;
Cossu and Hwang 2017
;
Duriez et al. 2009
and also in the context of centrifugally instabilities
Swearingen and Blackwelder 1987
;
Martinand et al. 2014
;
Dessup et al. 2018
.

A canonical example of wall-bounded shear flows is Plane Couette Flow (PCF), where a channel flow has either the upper or lower wall moving at a fixed velocity. PCF is linearly stable, so its critical Reynolds number (
Re_{c}
) is infinite
Davey 1973
. Furthermore, its simplicity and well-defined boundary conditions make it an ideal model for investigating fundamental mechanisms such as the SSP.

Numerous experimental observations in various shear flows, such as plane Couette flow, Poiseuille channel flow (PPF), and pipe flow, show turbulence onset at Reynolds numbers significantly below the linear threshold.
This phenomenon, known as subcritical transition to turbulence
Manneville 2016
, refers to the onset of turbulence from finite amplitude perturbations in a linearly stable flow. Key elements in this process include transient growth, responsible for the non-modal amplification of perturbations
Schmid and Henningson 2012
, and exact coherent structures (ECS), such as
edge states
that act as boundaries in phase space between the laminar and turbulent regimes
Duguet et al. 2009
. In addition, pattern formation was studied for these flows showing the coexistence of turbulent and laminar regions simultaneously in the form of patches. Recent developments on this subject can be found in
Benavides and Barkley 2025
;
Ciola et al. 2025
;
Gomé et al. 2023

Nonlinear minimal models have been successfully obtained from highly truncated reduction of the full equations describing the flow
Waleffe 1997
;
Moehlis et al. 2004
;
Manneville 2018
;
Cavalieri et al. 2022
. Other minimal models extend the study in order to incorporate the spatial non-homogeneity of the structures
Manneville 2012
;
Kashyap et al. 2025
and the effect of large scale flows
Benavides and Barkley 2025
. One of the smallest truncated models of the Navier-Stokes equations is Waleffe’s fourth-order nonlinear model
Waleffe 1997
in which the dynamics of streaks, rolls, wall-normal vorticity, and mean flow distortion – and the coupling between them – is studied and described. The lift-up effect has been experimentally measured in previous work
Liu et al. 2024
, where a linear relationship between the intensities of straight streaks and rolls was observed for laminar flow (”laminar” lift-up). Moreover, the more wavy the streaks become, the further they deviate from the linear relationship associated with the lift-up effect. This behavior can be captured and simply described by one of the equations in Waleffe’s model (Eq. (20) in
Waleffe 1997
or equation (
6
) here), motivating further investigation of other processes represented by this model, particularly the influence of undulations or waviness on the stability of the hydrodynamical structures.

This work presents an analysis of the energy feedback mechanisms between streaks and rolls near the transition to turbulence. We focus on the interaction resulting from the instability induced by streak waviness, represented by the wall-normal vorticity of the perturbations of the elongated structures that transfer energy to the rolls. The consistency of this nonlinear process with a truncated model is also investigated.

The manuscript is organized as follows: the numerical setup and governing equations are presented in Section
II
, the results and discussion comparing with previous theoretical work are given in Section
III
, and a brief summary highlighting the main conclusions is provided in Section
IV
.

II
Numerical set up

An incompressible viscous fluid between two plates is considered. One of the plates is moving at constant velocity
U_{0}
and in the same direction (
streamwise direction
) a constant pressure gradient
\Delta P/L_{x}=(P2-P1)/L_{x}
is imposed. These conditions are compatible with plane Couette-Poiseuille flow (
CPF
). The entire domain is rectangular with dimensions
L_{x}\times L_{y}\times L_{z}=(7\pi\times 1\times 2\pi)L_{0}
, with
L_{0}
a unitary length. The
streamwise
(
\hat{x}
) and
spanwise
(
\hat{z}
) directions have periodic boundary conditions, and a non-slip impermeable boundary is established in the
wall-normal
(
\hat{y}
) direction. The aforementioned scenario with a typical laminar profile is illustrated in Fig.
1
(a).

The Navier-Stokes equations for incompressible flows accurately describe the dynamics of the proposed system,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\bm{U}}{\partial t}+(\bm{U}\cdot\bm{\nabla})\bm{U}

\displaystyle=-\bm{\nabla}P+\nu\nabla^{2}\bm{U},

(2)

\displaystyle\bm{\nabla}\cdot\bm{U}

\displaystyle=0,

(3)

with
\bm{U}
the velocity field,
\nu
the viscosity coefficient,
P
the pressure field with its gradient
f_{0}=\Delta P/L_{x}=(P2-P1)/L_{x}
fixed at value
f_{0}=-0.001
, and constant density
\rho
imposed as the unity. With Eqs.
2
and
3
it is natural to define a Reynolds number
Re
as

Re=\frac{U_{0}L_{0}}{2\nu},

(4)

with
U_{0}
the wall velocity and
L_{0}/2
the half width of the channel as a characteristic velocity and length scales, respectively. This election of characteristic scales was chosen to facilitate comparison with the literature.

As mentioned above, this kind of problem has been theoretically analyzed using various truncated models that capture the dynamics of large coherent structures
Waleffe 1997
;
Moehlis et al. 2004
;
Manneville 2018
;
Cavalieri et al. 2022
. We were mainly inspired by the four-equation Waleffe model of
Waleffe 1997
to analyze the transition to turbulence in the present case. We recall that this model is described with the following equations:

\displaystyle\left(\frac{d}{dt}+\frac{\kappa_{M}^{2}}{Re}\right)M=\sigma_{M}W^{2}-\sigma_{U}UV+\frac{\kappa_{M}^{2}}{Re},

(5)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{d}{dt}+\frac{\kappa_{U}^{2}}{Re}\right)U=-\sigma_{W}W^{2}+\sigma_{U}MV,

(6)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{d}{dt}+\frac{\kappa_{V}^{2}}{Re}\right)V=\sigma_{V}W^{2},

(7)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{d}{dt}+\frac{\kappa_{W}^{2}}{Re}\right)W=\sigma_{W}UW-\sigma_{M}MW-\sigma_{V}VW,

(8)

where
M
,
U
,
V
, and
W
correspond, respectively, to the amplitude coefficients of the modes associated with: the mean profile distortion, the streaks, the rolls, and the streak waviness. On the other hand, the parameters
\kappa_{i}
are wave numbers associated with the different flow structures, and the
\sigma_{i}
represent the nonlinear coupling coefficients of the model.
The system is designed to describe the dynamics of channel plane Couette flow. Moreover, it represents an artificially created flow that is periodic and antisymmetric with respect to the center of the channel. Additionally, it lacks lateral walls, which simplifies its mathematical representation while preserving the spatial distribution of the mean shear profile of Couette flow. Even though it cannot be directly compared to Couette-Poiseuille flow, we assume that the structures and dynamics captured by the model are equivalent in both flows and serve as a framework or inspiration for studying the flow analyzed in this work. Furthermore, as mentioned in the introduction, recent work
Liu et al. 2024
measuring experimentally the lift-up effect in CPF has favourably compared it with the relations predicted by the model, with very good agreement. This observation has motivated the present investigation of the wavy dynamics.

The simulations were performed with SPECTER
Fontana et al. 2020
, a pseudospectral code that uses the Fourier continuation method in the nonperiodic direction
Bruno and Lyon 2010
.

III
Results

We start by examining the laminar behavior of the flow, which is observed in the decay of the fluctuations. In runs that eventually reach a laminar state (L1-L6 and NL1-NL7), a transient behavior appears -—which will be discussed later-— followed by an exponential decay. This decaying regime is consistent with laminar decay and aligns with Eqs. (
6
), (
7
) and (
8
) where the exponential decay rate is
\kappa^{2}/Re
when the nonlinear terms on the right-hand side are neglected. This rate is controlled by the typical wave number
\kappa
and the Reynolds number
Re
. This behavior can be observed in Fig.
5
for the variables of the run NL4, which asymptotically reaches a laminar state. The

This might imply that the vorticity is feeding the rolls, and the latter are tending to regenerate the streaks, completing the SSP. However, they are decaying even faster, and the turbulent state is not reached moving away, in its dynamics, from the quadratic relationship. Finally, the last regime after the circle time, corresponds to decaying into the laminar state. It is to be noted that this scenario comes latter for the streaks which is consistent with the analysis discussed previously, and with results observed in quenching experiments

IV
Conclusions

In plane Couette-Poiseuille flow, as in other wall-bounded shear flows, turbulence may be sustained by the interaction between streaks and rolls. This work provides a detailed analysis of streak waviness dynamics during the transition to turbulence. We quantitatively demonstrate how streak waviness is indeed a major ingredient of the self-sustained process.
We performed numerous direct numerical simulations at different Reynolds numbers and with different initial conditions. We defined proxies for the streaks (
\langle|u_{x}|\rangle
), the rolls (
\langle|u_{y}|\rangle
), and the streak waviness (
\langle|\omega_{y}^{wavy}|\rangle
), where the average is taken over the entire domain.

In all investigated cases, with initial disturbances of different types, the streaks, rolls, and streak waviness first increase before either decaying back to the laminar state or remaining in a turbulent state. At higher Reynolds numbers with sufficiently large perturbations, the flow eventually reaches a turbulent steady state; otherwise, the laminar state is approached asymptotically. In the latter case, the streaks and rolls decay exponentially, with rolls decaying approximately twice as fast as the streaks
Liu et al. 2021
.

The main result of this work is that the rolls amplitude
\langle|u_{y}|\rangle
follows a quadratic function of the streak waviness
\langle|\omega_{y}^{wavy}|\rangle
, provided its value is sufficiently large (in practice, higher than
10^{-2}
). More precisely,
\kappa_{v}^{2}/Re\langle|u_{y}|\rangle
is proportional to
\langle|\omega_{y}^{wavy}|\rangle^{2}
, with
\kappa_{v}\simeq 4.4
and a proportionality constant of approximately
\sigma_{V}\approx 4\times 10^{-3}
in all cases. We have shown quantitatively that Eq. (
7
), initially derived by Waleffe for a different flow configuration, is valid in Couette-Poiseuille flow.
This quadratic relationship holds for the entire time series of runs that are turbulent in the steady state (except for a very short initial transient) and during the decaying phase following the transient period when the initial perturbation is sufficiently large, even when the steady state remains laminar. The relationship holds at least for Reynolds numbers between
500
and
940
and does not depend on the form of the initial condition. Conversely, if the initial perturbation is small, the flow remains laminar, the waviness remains mostly below
10^{-2}
, and the quadratic relation is never observed.

To validate the concepts in Waleffe’s nonlinear model, experiments by Duriez
et al.

Duriez et al. 2009
provided initial insights, while more recent experiments
Liu et al. 2024
offered clearer observations of the lift-up mechanism and semi-quantitative agreement with the model physics. These experiments demonstrated the increase in streak amplitude with waviness, particularly the relation describing the lift-up effect given by Eq.
6
. However, they did not allow quantitative observation of the quadratic relationship between rolls and waviness, motivating the numerical experiments presented here.

The PIV experiments in
Liu et al. 2024
enabled local measurements, whereas the numerical approach here allows more global measurements of the amplitudes and consequently a clearer exploration of the quadratic region where the SSP triggers turbulence. Indeed, in their figure 6 (equivalent to our Fig.
9
(d)), the quadratic relation between rolls and waviness could not be quantified as clearly as shown here.

The relationship observed in turbulent states suggests that this interaction within the SSP can be effectively captured using these two variables, reinforcing their connection in the model. The results validate one aspect of the reduced Waleffe model. Future work could investigate other aspects or extend the model to incorporate additional complexities, such as higher-order interactions.

Appendix B
Linear transient growth

Plane Couette-Poiseuille flow (CPF) is known to be linearly stable under certain conditions, such as when the upper plate velocity
U_{\operatorname{belt}}
exceeds
70\%
of the centerline velocity
Potter 1966
. However, infinitesimal perturbations may still undergo significant transient growth due to the non-normality of the linearized Navier–Stokes equation
Bergström 2005
.
In this study, we investigate the transient growth associated with the base flow profile
U_{x}^{lam}(y)=\frac{f_{0}Re}{U_{0}^{2}L_{0}}\,y^{2}+(1-\frac{f_{0}Re}{U_{0}^{2}L_{0}})y
, for Reynolds number in the range
Re\in[500,1000]
. For each combination of streamwise and spanwise wavenumber, denoted as
\alpha
and
\beta
, respectively, the transient energy growth at time
t
is quantified by the growth function

\displaystyle G(t)\equiv G(t,\alpha,\beta)=\max_{\bm{q}_{0}\neq 0}\frac{\|\bm{q}(y,t)\|^{2}}{\|\bm{q}_{0}\|^{2}},

(14)

which represents the maximum possible energy amplification of kinetic energy of an initial perturbation. Here
\bm{q}=[u,v,w]
denotes the velocity perturbation vector.
The maximum growth
G_{\max}
at
t=t_{\max}
is defined as
G_{\max}\equiv G_{\max}(\alpha,\beta)=\max_{t\geq 0}G(t,\alpha,\beta)
, and the optimum energy growth
G_{\rm opt}
at
t=t_{\rm opt}
is defined as
G_{\text{opt}}=\max_{\alpha,\beta}G_{\max}\left(\alpha,\beta\right)
. The procedure for computing the growth function follows the approach outlined in
Schmid and Henningson 2012
.

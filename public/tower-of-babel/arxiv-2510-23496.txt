Abstract
. This is the second paper in a series studying the global asymptotics of discrete N N -particle systems with inverse temperature parameter θ \theta in the high temperature regime. In the first paper, we established necessary and sufficient conditions for the Law of Large Numbers at high temperature in terms of Jack generating functions. In this paper, we derive a functional equation for the moment generating function of the limiting measure, which enables its analysis using analytic tools. We apply this functional equation to compute the densities of the high temperature limits of the pure Jack measures . As a special case, we obtain the high temperature limit of the large fixed-time distribution of the discrete-space β \beta -Dyson Brownian motion of Gorin–Shkolnikov. Two special cases of our densities are the high temperature limits of discrete versions of the G β \beta E, computed in [ ABG12 ] , and L β \beta E, computed in [ ABMV13 ] . Moreover, we prove the following crystallization phenomenon of the particles in the high temperature limit: the limiting measures are uniformly supported on disjoint intervals with unit gaps and their locations correspond to the zeros of explicit special functions with all roots located in the real line. We also show that these zeros correspond to the spectra of certain unbounded Jacobi operators.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Overview

The study of random
N
-particle systems on the real line, inspired by models from Statistical Physics and Random Matrix Theory, has long been a central topic in probability. For specific values of the inverse temperature parameter
\beta
— notably
\beta=1,2,4
— these systems correspond to eigenvalue distributions of classical random matrices, and Wigner’s semicircle law
[
Wig58
]
is the foundational result for their large-
N
behavior.

A remarkable feature is that the global limiting distribution of the aforementioned systems, known as
\beta
-ensembles or one-dimensional log-gas systems, is universal in the sense that, as the size of the system tends to infinity, it depends only on the potential and not on the inverse temperature
\beta
, provided that
\beta N\to\infty

[
For10
]
. By contrast, in the high temperature regime, where
\beta
decreases with
N
so that
\beta N\to\gamma>0
, the limiting law becomes a one-parameter family determined by
\gamma
. This transition, which bridges the gap between independent particles and classical ensembles, has been rigorously analyzed for several models, including the Hermite and Laguerre
\beta
-ensembles
[
ABG12
,
ABMV13
,
DS15
,
TT21
]
, as well as in more general settings
[
BGCG22
,
MP22
]
, where precise criteria for the Law of Large Numbers (LLN) in this regime have been established.

A similar phenomenon was expected to occur in the discrete counterparts of
\beta
-ensembles.
And indeed, it has been confirmed that, for various models, the global limiting distribution is universal in the fixed temperature regime
[
DF16
,
BGG17
,
DS19
,
Hua21
,
Mol23
,
CDM23
,
DGGN25
]
. The high temperature regime has also been analyzed recently for three different models
[
DS19
,
CDM23
,
CD25
]
through the powerful method of moments.
The proofs naturally led to various moment-cumulant type formulae, always expressed as weighted generating functions of certain lattice paths, known as
Łukasiewicz paths
, which generalize well-known moment-cumulant type formulae from classical and free probability.

In our recent paper
[
CD25
]
, we established necessary and sufficient conditions for the LLN of discrete
N
-particle systems with inverse temperature parameter
\beta
, when the inverse temperature decays proportionally to the number of particles, in terms of Jack generating functions with parameter
\theta
, where
\beta=2\theta
. The Jack generating functions can be informally interpreted as a version of the Fourier transform that is well-suited for the study of many concrete discrete models.
An important outcome of our moment method approach is the aforementioned explicit combinatorial description of the moments of the limiting measure, expressed in terms of the
quantized
\gamma
-cumulants
that are limits of Taylor coefficients of the Jack generating functions. Moreover, we showed that the limiting measure is uniquely determined by the moments. Nevertheless, several natural questions remained open, e.g.: What do the limiting measures look like? Do they admit densities? Can we understand our combinatorial moment-cumulant formula in terms of an analytic framework?

This is the second paper in a series studying the global asymptotics of discrete
N
-particle systems with inverse temperature parameter
\beta
in the high temperature regime, and its purpose is to address the problems raised above, among others. The last question is a natural path to answer the previous ones and also, potentially, to extend our analysis to models that do not have all moments finite. Inspired by the success of the analytic approach to Free Probability theory — where, in that generality, instead of working with the sequence of cumulants, one works with the
R
-transform and proves its existence as an analytic function for a large class of probability measures by means of a functional equation between the
R
-transform and the Cauchy transform — we develop a similar approach for the discrete
\beta
-ensembles in the high temperature regime. We prove a functional equation between the
\gamma
-quantized
R
-transform
, which is the generating function of the quantized
\gamma
-cumulants, and the moment generating function. Remarkably, this functional equation differs drastically from the combinatorial transform in
[
CD25
]
and from the analytic formula obtained also from the high temperature regime, but for continuous models
[
BGCG22
]
.

The functional equation discussed above enables us to analyze the limiting measures using analytic tools, and we apply it to compute the densities of the high temperature limits of the
pure Jack measures
, which are discrete
N
-particle systems that depend on the Jack parameter
\theta>0
and arise from Jack-positive specializations of the ring of symmetric functions.
As a special case, we obtain the high temperature limit of the large fixed-time distribution of the discrete-space
\beta
-Dyson Brownian motion of Gorin–Shkolnikov
[
GS15
]
; the parameters are related by
\beta=2\theta
.
In addition, two special cases of our densities are discrete analogues of the high temperature limits of the G
\beta
E, computed in
[
ABG12
]
, and the L
\beta
E, computed in
[
ABMV13
]
.

Although the structure of the combinatorial moment formulas for the limiting measures in the continuous and discrete settings are similar, our simulations in
[
CD25
]
suggested that the densities in the discrete setting are qualitatively different; see
Figure

2
. Indeed, we prove that the following crystallization phenomenon of the particles occurs in the high temperature limit: the limiting measures are uniformly supported on disjoint intervals with gaps of unit length.
The right endpoints of these intervals turn out to be determined by the zeros of explicit special functions involving Gauss and confluent hypergeometric functions; see
Figures

3
and
4
.
We will show that these functions are real-rooted by relating their zeros to the spectra of certain unbounded Jacobi operators.
It is worth mentioning that a similar crystallization phenomenon was previously observed in
[
DS19
]
for the limit of Jack–Plancherel measures in the high temperature regime, and it was proved in
[
CDM23
]
that the locations of the particles in the limit correspond to the zeros of yet another special function, the Bessel function of the first kind.
However, the nature of the crystallization in that case was a direct consequence of the scaling of the model, while in the present situation, the fact that the particles are supported on disjoint intervals with unit gaps comes as a surprising consequence of the detailed analysis of the functional equation.

After this overview, we proceed to state our main results more precisely. We start by recalling the combinatorial transform from
[
CD25
]
that expresses the moments of the limiting measure in terms of the
quantized
\gamma
-cumulants
. Then we state our main theorem, which is a functional equation between the
\gamma
-quantized
R
-transform and the moment generating function. Finally, we discuss some applications for computing densities of limiting measures for discrete
N
-particle systems in the high temperature regime.

1.2.
LLN for discrete N-particle systems at high temperature and the gamma-quantized R-transform

Let
\{\mathbb{P}_{N}\}_{N\geq 1}
be a sequence, where each
\mathbb{P}_{N}
is a probability measure on the set
\mathbb{Y}(N)
of integer partitions
\lambda_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{N}\geq 0
, possibly depending on the Jack parameter
\theta>0
.
We assume that all
\mathbb{P}_{N}
have small tails, so that their
Jack generating functions
, defined by

(1)

G_{N,\theta}(x_{1},\dots,x_{N}):=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{Y}(N)}\mathbb{P}_{N}(\lambda)\frac{P_{\lambda}(x_{1},\dots,x_{N};\theta)}{P_{\lambda}(1^{N};\theta)},

are analytic in disks of radii larger than
1
; above,
P_{\lambda}(x_{1},\dots,x_{N};\theta)
are the Jack polynomials. In our previous paper
[
CD25
]
we gave a characterization of the LLN for discrete
N
-particle systems
\mathscr{L}_{1}>\cdots>\mathscr{L}_{N}
, with
\mathscr{L}_{i}:=\lambda_{i}-\theta(i-1)
, in the
high temperature regime

(2)

N\to\infty,\quad\theta\to 0,\quad N\theta\to\gamma,

where
\gamma\in(0,\infty)
is a fixed constant. We proved that there exist
m_{1},m_{2},\dots\in\mathbb{R}
such that, for all
s\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
and
k_{1},\dots,k_{s}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, we have the following convergence of mixed moments:

(3)

\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}N\to\infty,\\
N\theta\to\gamma\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{N^{s}}\,\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}_{N}}\left[\prod_{j=1}^{s}{\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\mathscr{L}_{i}^{k_{j}}}}\right]=\prod_{j=1}^{s}{m_{k_{j}}}

if and only if the following two conditions are satisfied:

(1)

\displaystyle\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}N\to\infty\\
N\theta\to\gamma\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\cdot\frac{\partial^{n}}{\partial x_{1}^{n}}\,\ln(G_{N,\theta})\,\Big|_{(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})=(1^{N})}=\kappa_{n}^{\gamma}
exists and is finite, for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
,

(2)

\displaystyle\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}N\to\infty\\
N\theta\to\gamma\end{subarray}}\frac{\partial^{r}}{\partial x_{i_{1}}\cdots\partial x_{i_{r}}}\,\ln(G_{N,\theta})\,\Big|_{(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})=(1^{N})}=0
, for all
r\geq 2
and all
i_{1},\dots,i_{r}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
with at least two distinct indices among
i_{1},\dots,i_{r}
.

Consequently, if
m_{1},m_{2},\dots
is the moment sequence of a unique probability measure
\mu^{\gamma}
, then
\mu^{\gamma}
is the weak limit of the empirical measures
\mu^{(N)}:=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{\mathscr{L}_{i}}
, in the regime (
2
).

The values
\kappa_{1}^{\gamma},\kappa_{2}^{\gamma},\cdots
will be called the
quantized
\gamma
-cumulants
of the limiting measure
\mu^{\gamma}
. This terminology is used because, upon certain normalization followed by the limit
\gamma\to\infty
, the quantities
\kappa_{n}^{\gamma}
turn into the coefficients of the quantized
R
-transform from
[
BG15
]
, as explained in
[
CD25
, Remark 8]
.
We showed in
[
CD25
]
that they determine and, at the same time, are uniquely determined by the moments
m_{1},m_{2},\dots
of
\mu^{\gamma}
, according to the following recursive relations

(4)

m_{\ell}=\sum_{\Gamma\in\mathbf{L}(\ell)}\frac{\Delta_{\gamma}\left(x^{1+\text{\#\,horizontal steps at height $0$ in $\Gamma$}}\right)\big|_{x=\kappa_{1}^{\gamma}}}{1+\text{\#\,horizontal steps at height $0$ in $\Gamma$}}\,\prod_{i\geq 1}\big(\kappa_{1}^{\gamma}+i\big)^{\text{\#\,horizontal steps at height $i$ in $\Gamma$}}\\
\cdot\prod_{j\geq 1}\big(\kappa_{j}^{\gamma}+\kappa_{j+1}^{\gamma}\big)^{\text{\#\,steps $(1,j)$ in $\Gamma$}}(j+\gamma)^{\text{\#\,down steps from height $j$ in $\Gamma$}},\quad\text{ for all }\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1},

where
\mathbf{L}(\ell)
is the set of Łukasiewicz paths of length
\ell
(certain lattice paths in
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
whose definition is recalled in
Section

3.1
), and
\Delta_{\gamma}(f)(x):=\frac{1}{\gamma}(f(x)-f(x-\gamma))
.

The generating series

R^{\gamma}_{\mu}(z):=\sum_{n\geq 1}{\frac{\kappa_{n}^{\gamma}z^{n}}{n}}

for the quantized
\gamma
-cumulants of
\mu
will be called the
\gamma
-quantized
R
-transform of
\mu
. Equation
4
provides a combinatorial recipe for calculating this transform for measures with finite moments of all orders.
Then a natural question is whether the definition of
\gamma
-quantized
R
-transform can be extended to larger classes of measures, possibly by treating it as a complex analytic function.
In a similar vein, but for free cumulants instead of quantized
\gamma
-cumulants, the theory of Free Probability defines the
R
-transform as an analytic function that satisfies a certain functional equation involving the Cauchy transform.
The main theorem of this paper is a similar functional equation between the
\gamma
-quantized
R
-transform and the moment generating function of any probability measure with all finite moments.

Some special cases of combinatorial transforms coming from Viennot’s construction

1.3.

1.3.1.
Pure Jack measures

Given the classification result for Jack-positive specializations, there are three natural choices of

2.1.
Partitions

A partition is a sequence
\lambda=(\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\cdots)\in(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0})^{\infty}
such that
\lambda_{i}=0
, for all large
i
. The size of
\lambda
is
|\lambda|:=\sum_{i\geq 1}{\lambda_{i}}
, while its length is
\ell(\lambda):=\max\{i\geq 1\colon\lambda_{i}\neq 0\}
.
The length of the empty partition
\emptyset=(0,0,0,\cdots)
is zero, by convention. For a partition
\lambda
with
\ell(\lambda)\leq N
, we will write
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{N})
, with the understanding that infinitely many zeroes are being omitted.
For any
N\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, denote

\mathbb{Y}(N):=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{Y}\colon\ell(\lambda)\leq N\},

and
\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{Y}(N))
the simplex of probability measures on
\mathbb{Y}(N)
.
We also let
\mathbb{Y}=\bigcup_{N\geq 0}{\mathbb{Y}(N)}
be the set of all partitions.
We will often identify partitions with their Young diagrams.

2.2.
Algebra of symmetric functions

We let
\Sym
be the real algebra of symmetric functions in the variables
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},x_{2},\dots)
, i.e., this is the polynomial algebra
\Sym=\mathbb{R}[p_{1},p_{2},\dots]
in the power sum variables
p_{k}=\sum_{i\geq 1}{x_{i}^{k}}
.

Upon the specialization
x_{N+1}=x_{N+2}=\cdots=0
, any
f\in\Sym
becomes a symmetric polynomial
f(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})\in\mathbb{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{N}]^{\mathfrak{S}_{N}}
and, conversely, for any sequence
\big\{f_{N}(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})\in\mathbb{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{N}]^{\mathfrak{S}_{N}}\big\}_{N\geq 1}
of symmetric polynomials such that

(6)

f_{N+1}(x_{1},\dots,x_{N},0)=f_{N}(x_{1},\dots,x_{N}),\quad\text{for all }N\geq 1,

their projective limit leads to a symmetric function

2.3.
Jack symmetric functions

For any
N\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, the
Jack polynomials

P_{\lambda}(x_{1},\dots,x_{N};\theta)

\lambda\in\mathbb{Y}(N)

They satisfy the stability property (

Sometimes we denote the Jack symmetric functions by

The Jack symmetric functions satisfy the following

\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{Y}}P_{\lambda}(\mathbf{x};\theta)Q_{\lambda}(\mathbf{y};\theta)=\exp\Bigg\{\theta\sum_{k\geq 1}{\frac{p_{k}(\mathbf{x})p_{k}(\mathbf{y})}{k}}\Bigg\}=:H_{\theta}(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{y}),

where
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},x_{2},\dots)
,
\mathbf{y}=(y_{1},y_{2},\dots)
, and

(8)

Q_{\lambda}(;\theta):=\prod_{(i,j)\in\lambda}\frac{(\lambda_{i}-j)+\theta(\lambda_{j}^{\prime}-i)+\theta}{(\lambda_{i}-j)+\theta(\lambda_{j}^{\prime}-i)+1}\cdot P_{\lambda}(;\theta)

is the
Q
-normalization of Jack symmetric functions.
In

, we denoted
\lambda^{\prime}
the conjugate partition to
\lambda
, so that
\lambda_{j}^{\prime}
is the length of the

-th largest column of the Young diagram of

.

2.4.
Jack generating functions

For any

and

of

as the following formal power series in

G_{\mathbb{P}_{N},\theta}(x_{1},\dots,x_{N}):=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{Y}(N)}\mathbb{P}_{N}(\lambda)\frac{P_{\lambda}(x_{1},\dots,x_{N};\theta)}{P_{\lambda}(1^{N};\theta)}.

Definition 2.1
.

We say that

\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{Y}(N)}{\mathbb{P}_{N}(\lambda)\frac{P_{\lambda}(z_{1},\dots,z_{N};\theta)}{P_{\lambda}(1^{N};\theta)}}

2.5.

Definition 2.2
.

A

Theorem-Definition 2.3

Given

and then applying the recursive combinatorial formula from
Section

3.1
.
There is certain parallelism to Speicher’s combinatorial approach to Free Probability, where
free cumulants
of probability measures are obtained from their moments by a recursive formula that involves noncrossing set partitions
[
Spe94
]
.

Speicher’s approach to Free Probability, however, cannot be used to define free cumulants for probability measures that do not have finite moments of all orders.
This is where the
analytic approach
to Free Probability shines, as instead of working with the sequence of cumulants, one works with the
R
-transform
and proves its existence as an analytic function for a large class of probability measures by means of a functional equation between the
R
-transform and Cauchy transform.
Envisioning a
quantized
\gamma
-deformed Probability Theory
, we also want to find a functional equation between the series

R^{\gamma}_{\mu}(z):=\sum_{n\geq 1}{\frac{\kappa_{n}^{\gamma}[\mu]z^{n}}{n}},

which we call the
\gamma
-quantized
R
-transform of
\mu
, and the Cauchy transform (or moment generating function) of
\mu
.
This motivated the following result.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\gamma>0
and assume that
\vec{\kappa}=(\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2},\dots)
and
\vec{m}=(m_{1},m_{2},\dots)
are related by the combinatorial identity from
Section

3.1
, i.e.
\vec{m}=\mathcal{J}_{\gamma}^{\kappa\mapsto m}(\vec{\kappa})
.
Then there exists a unique
\vec{c}=(c_{1},c_{2},\dots)
such that the following relations between generating functions hold:

(23)

\displaystyle\exp\left(\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{\kappa_{n}z^{n}}{n}\right)

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{c_{n}}{\gamma^{\uparrow n}}z^{n},

(24)

\displaystyle 1+\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{(-1)^{n}c_{n}}{z^{\uparrow n}}

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\gamma\cdot\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}\left\{\frac{t}{1-e^{-t}}\sum_{n\geq 1}\left(\frac{(-1)^{n}m_{n}}{n!}-\frac{\gamma^{n}}{(n+1)!}\right)t^{n-1}\right\}(z)\right).

Recall that
z^{\uparrow n}=z(z+1)\cdots(z+n-1)
is the raising factorial, while we define
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}
as the linear operator that transforms power series in
t
into power series in
z^{-1}
, and is uniquely defined by

(25)

\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}\left\{\sum_{n\geq 0}{\frac{a_{n}}{n!}t^{n}}\right\}(z):=\sum_{n\geq 0}{a_{n}z^{-n-1}}.

Note that (
23
) is an equality between power series in
z
, which furnishes formulas between
(\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2},\dots)
and
(c_{1},c_{2},\dots)
.
Likewise, (
24
) is an equality between power series in
z^{-1}
that yields formulas between
(c_{1},c_{2},\dots)
and
(m_{1},m_{2},\dots)
.
Put together, these two equalities can be used to express each
m_{\ell}
in terms of
\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2},\dots
(and viceversa);
Section

4
states that this gives an equivalent expression as the one in terms of Łukasiewicz paths from
Section

3.1
.

Remark 1
.

We call
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}
the
formal Laplace transform
because it coincides with the Laplace transform

\mathcal{L}\{f(t)\}(z):=\int_{0}^{\infty}f(t)e^{-zt}dt,

under certain technical conditions.
For example, since
\mathcal{L}\{t^{n}\}(z)=n!\,z^{-n-1}
, for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
\Re z>0
, then
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}
coincides with
\mathcal{L}
for polynomials
f(t)
in the region
\Re z>0
.
In
Section

4
, we decided to use the formal
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}
instead of
\mathcal{L}
to avoid specifying any particular choice of analytic conditions for
f(t)
and domains for
z
.

Remark 2
.

Note that

(26)

\frac{t}{1-e^{-t}}=\sum_{m\geq 0}\frac{(-1)^{m}B_{m}}{m!}t^{m}

is the exponential generating function of the
Bernoulli numbers
, the first few of them being:
B_{0}=1
,
B_{1}=-\frac{1}{2}
,
B_{2}=\frac{1}{6}
,
B_{3}=0
,
B_{4}=-\frac{1}{30}
,
B_{5}=0,\cdots
.
This shows that the relation between
c_{1},c_{2},\dots
and
m_{1},m_{2},\dots
from (
24
) is expressed in terms of these quantities.
The occurrence of the Bernoulli numbers in the theorem can be traced to an asymptotic expansion of the log gamma function in the proof below; see
Lemma

4.4
.

Remark 3
.

Another reason for the interest in
Section

4
is the (possible) connection with Finite Free Probability, see e.g.
[
MSS22
]
.
In fact, similar identities were proved in the continuous settings of
\beta
-eigenvalues, see
[
BGCG22
, Thm. 3.11]
and
\beta
-singular values, see
[
Xu23

.
It is now known that the identities in those references are precisely the same as in the cumulant-based combinatorial approach to Finite Free Probability started in
[

(see also
[
AGVP23

Whether our equations (

4.1.
Preliminary lemmas

We need a few lemmas for the proof of
Section

4
.
For the first two of them, we denote by
Q^{*}_{\mu}(x_{1},\dots,x_{N};\theta)
,
\mu\in\mathbb{Y}(N)
, the
Q
-normalization of the
shifted Jack polynomials
, as defined in
[
OO97
]
or
[
OO98
, Secs. 2.2–2.4]
.
In particular, for row partitions
\mu=(k)
, they admit the explicit expression

(27)

Q_{(k)}^{*}(x_{1},\dots,x_{N};\theta)=\sum_{1\leq i_{1}\leq\cdots\leq i_{k}\leq N}{\frac{\theta^{\uparrow m_{1}}\theta^{\uparrow m_{2}}\cdots}{m_{1}!m_{2}!\cdots}(x_{i_{1}}-k+1)\cdots(x_{i_{k-1}}-1)x_{i_{k}}},

where
m_{l}:=\#\{r\mid i_{r}=l\}
are the multiplicities of
l=1,2,\dots
in the sequence
i_{1},\dots,i_{k}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

If
\max_{1\leq i\leq N}{|x_{i}|}\leq A
, for some
A>0
, then

(28)

\big|Q_{(k)}^{*}(x_{1},\dots,x_{N};\theta)\big|\leq A^{\uparrow k}\cdot\sum_{1\leq i_{1}\leq\cdots\leq i_{k}\leq N}{\frac{\theta^{\uparrow m_{1}}\theta^{\uparrow m_{2}}\cdots}{m_{1}!m_{2}!\cdots}},

for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, where
m_{l}:=\#\{r\mid i_{r}=l\}
are the multiplicities of
l=1,2,\dots
in the sequence
i_{1},\dots,i_{k}
.

Proof.

This follows immediately from (
27
).
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

If
\max_{1\leq i\leq N}{|x_{i}|}\leq A
, for some
A>0
, then the series

(29)

\sum_{k\geq 0}{\frac{(-1)^{k}Q_{(k)}^{*}(x_{1},\dots,x_{N};\theta)}{z^{\uparrow k}}}

converges absolutely in the region
\Re z>N\theta+A
, and it is identically equal to

(30)

\prod_{i=1}^{N}{\frac{\Gamma(x_{i}+z-i\theta)}{\Gamma(x_{i}+z-(i-1)\theta)}\frac{\Gamma(z-(i-1)\theta)}{\Gamma(z-i\theta)}}.

Proof.

This is proved in
[
OO98
, Appendix]
, so we only comment on the bound
\Re z>N\theta+A
, which is not explicitly stated in that reference.
Denote the RHS of (
28
) by
q_{A;\,\theta}^{(N)}(k)
, e.g.
q_{A;\,\theta}^{(1)}(k)=A^{\uparrow k}\theta^{\uparrow k}/k!
.
To prove that (
29
) converges absolutely in the region
\Re z>N\theta+A
, it suffices to show by
Lemma

4.2
that the series

F^{(N)}_{A;\,\theta}(y):=\sum_{k\geq 0}{\frac{q_{A;\,\theta}^{(N)}(k)}{y^{\uparrow k}}}

converges if
y>N\theta+A
; in fact, by induction on
N
, we show it converges to
\frac{\Gamma(y-A-N\theta)\Gamma(y)}{\Gamma(y-A)\Gamma(y-N\theta)}
.

For the base case
N=1
, note that
F^{(1)}_{A;\,\theta}(y)
is Gauss’s hypergeometric series
{}_{2}F_{1}(\theta,A;y;1)
, for which it is well-known that it converges absolutely when
y>\theta+A
, and equals
\frac{\Gamma(y-A-\theta)\Gamma(y)}{\Gamma(y-A)\Gamma(y-\theta)}
, see e.g.
[
Bai35
, Sec. 1.3]
.
For the inductive step, use the definitions and inductive hypothesis to deduce

(31)

F_{A;\,\theta}^{(N)}(y)=\sum_{k\geq 0}{F_{A+k;\,\theta}^{(N-1)}(y+k)\frac{q^{(1)}_{A;\,\theta}(k)}{y^{\uparrow k}}}=F_{A;\,\theta}^{(N-1)}(y)\sum_{k\geq 0}{\frac{F_{A+k;\,\theta}^{(N-1)}(y+k)}{F_{A;\,\theta}^{(N-1)}(y)}\frac{q^{(1)}_{A;\,\theta}(k)}{y^{\uparrow k}}}\\
=F_{A;\,\theta}^{(N-1)}(y)\sum_{k\geq 0}{\frac{\Gamma(y+k)\Gamma\big(y-(N-1)\theta\big)}{\Gamma\big(y+k-(N-1)\theta\big)\Gamma(y)}\frac{q^{(1)}_{A;\,\theta}(k)}{y^{\uparrow k}}}=F_{A;\,\theta}^{(N-1)}(y)\sum_{k\geq 0}{\frac{q^{(1)}_{A;\,\theta}(k)}{\big(y-(N-1)\theta\big)^{\uparrow k}}}.

The last summation above is the hypergeometric series
{}_{2}F_{1}(\theta,A;y-(N-1)\theta;1)
, which converges if
y-(N-1)\theta>\theta+A
, and equals
\frac{\Gamma(y-A-N\theta)\Gamma(y-(N-1)\theta)}{\Gamma(y-(N-1)\theta-A)\Gamma(y-N\theta)}
in that case.
Plugging back into (
31
) and using the inductive hypothesis again concludes the induction.

A small refinement of this argument proves (
30
); see
[
OO98
, Appendix]
for details.
∎

Recall that for a function
F(z)
defined on the half-plane

asymptotic expansion

F(z)\sim\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}{b_{m}z^{-m}},\quad\text{as }z\to\infty,

means that

(32)

\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\arg(z)|<\pi/2\\
z\to\infty\end{subarray}}{z^{N}\left(F(z)-\sum_{m=0}^{N}{b_{m}z^{-m}}\right)}=0,\quad\textrm{for all }N\geq 0.

The following is the asymptotic expansion of

(33)

\ln\Gamma(z)\sim\left(z-\frac{1}{2}\right)\ln z-z+\frac{\ln(2\pi)}{2}+\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}\left\{-\frac{1}{t^{2}}+\frac{1}{2t}+\frac{1}{t(e^{t}-1)}\right\}(z).

By

in a neighborhood of zero, therefore

Lemma 4.5
.

For any formal power series
f(t)
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

(34)

\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}\{f(t)\}(z+x)=\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}\{e^{-tx}f(t)\}(z).

Proof.

As mentioned in
Remark

1
, the formal Laplace transform and the usual Laplace transform agree on polynomials of arbitrary degree.
They also agree on functions of
t
of the form
e^{-xt}\sum_{i=0}^{N}{a_{i}t^{i}}
, for any
x>0
.
So for any polynomial
f(t)
and
x>0
, we deduce

\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}\{f(t)\}(z+x)=\mathcal{L}\{f(t)\}(z+x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}{f(t)e^{-(z+x)t}dt}\\
=\int_{0}^{\infty}{\big(e^{-tx}f(t)\big)e^{-zt}dt}=\mathcal{L}\{e^{-tx}f(t)\}(z)=\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}\{e^{-tx}f(t)\}(z),

i.e. (
34
) holds if
f(t)
is a polynomial and
x>0
.
The desired
Equation

34
is an identity of formal power series in
z^{-1}
, meaning that we want to prove that the coefficients on both sides agree.
But such coefficients are just polynomials in
x
and the coefficients of
f(t)
; since they agree for any
x>0
and any polynomial
f(t)
, they must also agree for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
and any formal power series
f(t)
, as needed.
∎

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
\big\{L^{(N)}=\big(L^{(N)}_{1},\dots,L^{(N)}_{N}\big)\big\}_{N\geq 1}
be a sequence of tuples of real numbers such that

(35)

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\Big(L^{(N)}_{i}\Big)^{\ell}}}=M_{\ell},

for some real numbers
M_{1},M_{2},\dots
and all
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
. Then

\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}N\to\infty\\
N\theta\to\gamma\end{subarray}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\Big(L_{i}^{(N)}-\theta\Big)^{\ell}-\Big(L_{i}^{(N)}\Big)^{\ell}+\big((1-i)\theta\big)^{\ell}-(-i\theta)^{\ell}\right)=(-1)^{\ell-1}\gamma^{\ell}-\ell\gamma M_{\ell-1},

for all
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, where we set
M_{0}:=1
.

Proof.

By a simple calculation, the prelimit expression can be written as

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\Big(L_{i}^{(N)}-\theta\Big)^{\ell}-\Big(L_{i}^{(N)}\Big)^{\ell}+(-\theta)^{\ell}\Big((i-1)^{\ell}-i^{\ell}\Big)\right)\\
=-\ell(\theta N)\cdot\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(L_{i}^{(N)}\right)^{\ell-1}+\sum_{k=2}^{\ell}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\binom{\ell}{k}\left(L_{i}^{(N)}\right)^{\ell-k}(-\theta)^{k}-(-\theta)^{\ell}N^{\ell}.

The first term above converges to
-\ell\gamma M_{\ell-1}
, in the regime where
N\to\infty
,
N\theta\to\gamma
, due to the assumption (
35
).
The same relation shows that the second term converges to zero.
The last term evidently converges to
(-1)^{\ell-1}\gamma^{\ell}
, finishing the proof.
∎

4.2.
Proof of Theorem
4

Let
\mu
be any compactly supported probability measure on
\mathbb{R}
for which there exists a sequence of partitions
\{\lambda^{(N)}\in\mathbb{Y}(N)\}_{N\geq 1}
such that
C\geq\lambda^{(N)}_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda^{(N)}_{N}\geq 0
, for some constant
C>0
, and such that we have the following weak limit

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\delta_{\mathscr{L}_{i}^{(N)}}}\to\mu,\quad\text{as }N\to\infty,\ N\theta\to\gamma,

where
\mathscr{L}_{i}^{(N)}=\lambda_{i}^{(N)}-\theta(i-1)
, for all
1\leq i\leq N
.
As
\mu
is compactly supported, it is implied that the sequence of probability measures

(36)

\mathbb{P}_{N}:=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\delta_{\mathscr{L}_{i}^{(N)}}}

satisfies the LLN, in the sense of
Section

3.1
.
In particular, if we let
m_{1},m_{2},\cdots
be the moments of
\mu
, then

(37)

\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}N\to\infty\\
N\theta\to\gamma\end{subarray}}{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\Big(\mathscr{L}_{i}^{(N)}\Big)^{k}}}=m_{k},\quad\text{for all }k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}.

The Jack generating function of
\mathbb{P}_{N}
is equal to

\displaystyle G_{N,\theta}(x_{1}+1,\dots,x_{n}+1)

\displaystyle=\frac{P_{\lambda^{(N)}}(x_{1}+1,\dots,x_{N}+1;\theta)}{P_{\lambda^{(N)}}(1^{N};\theta)}

(38)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mu\in\mathbb{Y}(N)}\frac{Q_{\mu}^{*}\Big(\lambda_{1}^{(N)},\dots,\lambda_{N}^{(N)};\theta\Big)P_{\mu}\big(x_{1},\dots,x_{N};\theta\big)}{(N\theta)_{\mu}},

where the second equality above is proved in
[
OO97
, Thm. (3.2)]
.
In (
38
), the symbol in the denominator is
(g)_{\mu}:=\prod_{(i,j)\in\mu}\big(g+(j-1)-\theta(i-1)\big)
.

We want to specialize the variables
x_{1}=z
and
x_{2}=\cdots=x_{N}=0
in (
38
).
Using the facts (i)
P_{\mu}(z,0^{N-1};\theta)=0
, if
\ell(\mu)>1
, (ii)
P_{(k)}(z,0^{N-1};\theta)=z^{k}
, for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, (iii)
Q_{(0)}^{*}(x_{1},\dots,x_{N};\theta)=1
, and (iv)
(N\theta)_{(k)}=(N\theta)^{\uparrow k}
, for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, we deduce:

(39)

G_{N,\theta}\big(z+1,\,1^{N-1}\big)=1+\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{Q_{(k)}^{*}\Big(\lambda_{1}^{(N)},\dots,\lambda_{N}^{(N)};\theta\Big)}{(N\theta)^{\uparrow k}}z^{k}.

Since
\{\mathbb{P}_{N}\}_{N\geq 1}
satisfies the LLN,
Section

3.1
implies HT-appropriateness. In particular, we have the following convergence of Taylor coefficients of the logarithms:

(40)

\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}N\to\infty\\
N\theta\to\gamma\end{subarray}}{\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\frac{d^{n}}{dz^{n}}\ln\big(G_{N,\theta}\big(z+1,\,1^{N-1}\big)\big)\big|_{z=0}}=\kappa_{n},\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}.

This has two implications.
First, the functions
G_{N,\theta}(z+1,1^{N-1})
themselves have converging Taylor coefficients, so due to the expansion (
39
), we deduce that the following limits exist:

(41)

c_{k}=\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}N\to\infty\\
N\theta\to\gamma\end{subarray}}Q_{(k)}^{*}\Big(\lambda_{1}^{(N)},\dots,\lambda_{N}^{(N)};\theta\Big),\quad\text{for all }k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}.

Second, by (
39
) and (
40
), we actually deduce the following equality

\exp\left(\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{\kappa_{n}z^{n}}{n}\right)=1+\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{c_{k}}{\gamma^{\uparrow k}}z^{k},

that is to be interpreted coefficient-wise, i.e. as an equality between formal power series.
This is precisely the first equation (
23
) in the statement of the theorem.
We have thus proved that the quantities
c_{1},c_{2},\dots
, uniquely determined from
\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2},\dots
by this equation, are exactly the limits (
41
) of certain evaluations of shifted Jack polynomials.

On the other hand, according to
Lemma

4.6
, we have the following generating function in
z^{-1}
for shifted Jack polynomials:

(42)

1+\sum_{k\geq 1}{\frac{(-1)^{k}Q_{(k)}^{*}(x_{1},\dots,x_{N};\theta)}{z^{\uparrow k}}}=\prod_{i=1}^{N}{\frac{\Gamma(x_{i}+z-i\theta)}{\Gamma(x_{i}+z-(i-1)\theta)}\frac{\Gamma(z-(i-1)\theta)}{\Gamma(z-i\theta)}}.

If we set
x_{i}=\lambda_{i}^{(N)}
, for
i=1,\dots,N
, then (
42
) is, in fact, an equality between analytic functions in the region
\Re z>N\theta+C
, as shown in
Lemma

4.3
.
We will soon be concerned with the high temperature regime where
N\to\infty
,
N\theta\to\gamma
; in this regime, the identity (
42
) holds whenever
\Re z\gg 0
, i.e., in a region
\{z\in\mathbb{C}\colon\Re z>C^{\prime}\}
, for some large enough
C^{\prime}>0
.

By taking the logarithm of (
42
), we have

(43)

\ln\left(1+\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{(-1)^{k}Q_{(k)}^{*}\Big(\lambda_{1}^{(N)},\dots,\lambda_{N}^{(N)};\theta\Big)}{z^{\uparrow k}}\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{4}{(-1)^{j-1}\ln\Gamma\left(z+\nabla_{i;j}^{(N)}\right)},

where

\nabla_{i;1}^{(N)}:=\mathscr{L}_{i}^{(N)}-\theta,\quad\nabla_{i;2}^{(N)}:=\mathscr{L}_{i}^{(N)},\quad\nabla_{i;3}^{(N)}:=(1-i)\theta,\quad\nabla_{i;4}^{(N)}:=-i\theta,

and (
43
) is valid whenever
\Re z\gg 0
.
Next, we want to consider the asymptotic expansion of both sides in the region
|\arg z|<\pi/2
, as
z\to\infty
, in the sense of
Equation

32
.
The LHS is the same because it is an analytic function, while the asymptotic expansion of the RHS can be deduced from
Lemmas

4.4
and
4.5
.
We end up with the following equality, which should be interpreted as an equality between formal power series in
z^{-1}
:

\displaystyle\ln\left(1+\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{(-1)^{k}Q_{(k)}^{*}\Big(\lambda_{1}^{(N)},\dots,\lambda_{N}^{(N)};\theta\Big)}{z^{\uparrow k}}\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{4}(-1)^{j-1}\left(z+\nabla_{i;j}^{(N)}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\ln\left(1+\frac{\nabla_{i;j}^{(N)}}{z}\right)

(44)

\displaystyle+\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{4}(-1)^{j-1}e^{-t\nabla_{i;j}^{(N)}}\left(-\frac{1}{t^{2}}+\frac{1}{2t}+\frac{1}{t(e^{t}-1)}\right)\right\}(z).

We can further simplify this expression by denoting

\widetilde{\nabla}_{\ell}^{(N)}:=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{4}{(-1)^{j-1}\Big(\nabla_{i;j}^{(N)}\Big)^{\ell}},\quad\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}.

For example, note that
\widetilde{\nabla}_{1}^{(N)}=0
.
Then the RHS of (
44
) can be rewritten as

(45)

\sum_{\ell\geq 1}(-1)^{\ell}\left(\frac{\widetilde{\nabla}^{(N)}_{\ell+1}}{\ell+1}-\frac{\widetilde{\nabla}^{(N)}_{\ell+1}}{\ell}+\frac{\widetilde{\nabla}^{(N)}_{\ell}}{2\ell}\right)z^{-\ell}+\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}\left\{\sum_{\ell\geq 1}(-t)^{\ell}\frac{\widetilde{\nabla}^{(N)}_{\ell}}{\ell!}\left(-\frac{1}{t^{2}}+\frac{1}{2t}+\frac{1}{t(e^{t}-1)}\right)\right\}(z).

Next, we want to take the coefficient-wise limit of the formal power series (
44
)–(
45
) in the regime
N\to\infty
,
N\theta\to\gamma
.
We will use (
37
) and
Lemma

4.6
, which imply

(46)

\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}N\to\infty\\
N\theta\to\gamma\end{subarray}}{\widetilde{\nabla}_{\ell}^{(N)}}=(-1)^{\ell-1}\gamma^{\ell}-\ell\gamma m_{\ell-1},

for all
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, if we set
m_{0}:=1
.
As a result, the limit of the first term in (
45
) equals

(47)

\gamma\sum_{\ell\geq 1}\left(\frac{(-1)^{\ell}m_{\ell}}{\ell}+\frac{(-1)^{\ell-1}m_{\ell-1}}{2}+\frac{\gamma^{\ell}}{\ell+1}-\frac{\gamma^{\ell}}{\ell}-\frac{\gamma^{\ell-1}}{2\ell}\right)z^{-\ell}\\
=\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}\left\{\left(\gamma\cdot M(-t)-\frac{e^{t\gamma}-1}{t}\right)\left(\frac{1}{t}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\right\}(z),

where
M(s):=1+\sum_{n\geq 1}{\frac{m_{n}}{n!}s^{n}}
is the moment generating function of
\mu
.
Similarly, due to (
46
), the limit of the second term of (
45

5.
Characteristic functions of the alpha, beta and Plancherel Jacobi operators

We are interested in determining the spectra of three families of Jacobi operators.
Two of them are associated to infinite Jacobi matrices, or operators on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1})
, and one is associated to a finite Jacobi matrix.
We will show that the zeroes of certain entire functions, based on generalized hypergeometric functions, coincide with the spectra of our Jacobi operators.
These entire functions are called
characteristic functions
; in the case of the finite Jacobi matrix, this will be a constant multiple of the characteristic polynomial.
The asymptotic analysis of eigenvalues of our Jacobi operators, as well as the problem of computing the characteristic functions, have already been studied; see, e.g.
[
JN01
,
JM07
,
BdMZ08
,
ŠŠ15
]
and references therein.
Let us review the relevant results needed for our applications.

The Jacobi matrices
J_{\lambda;w}
in question are of the form

J_{\lambda;w}:=\begin{bmatrix}\lambda_{1}&w_{1}&0&0&\cdots\\
w_{1}&\lambda_{2}&w_{2}&0&\ddots\\
0&w_{2}&\lambda_{3}&w_{3}&\ddots\\
0&0&w_{3}&\lambda_{4}&\ddots\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots\end{bmatrix},

where rows and columns are parametrized by
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, or by
\{1,2,\dots,M\}
in the finite case.
The quantities
\{w_{i}\}_{i\geq 1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{*}
and
\{\lambda_{i}\}_{i\geq 1}\subset\mathbb{R}
are called the Jacobi parameters; we assume that all

In the infinite cases, we require
\sum_{i\geq 1}|w_{i}|^{-1}=\infty
, so that

is an essentially self-adjoint operator (on the space of finite vectors) and therefore admits a unique self-adjoint extension to
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1})
.

The two infinite Jacobi matrices of our interest fit into the following two general classes:

(49)

\lambda_{i}=\zeta-i,\quad|w_{i}|=O(i^{1-\epsilon}),\qquad\zeta\in\mathbb{R},\quad\epsilon>0,

and

(50)

\lambda_{i}=\zeta-\delta i,\quad w_{i}=\sqrt{\alpha i^{2}+\beta i+\gamma},\quad\ \zeta\in\mathbb{R},\quad\alpha,\delta>0,\quad\beta^{2}\geq 4\alpha\gamma,\quad\delta>2\sqrt{\alpha}.

In both cases, it is known that the spectrum of

is discrete, bounded from above, and the eigenvalues are simple (see
[

has to be unbounded, and hence, does not belong to

.
We conclude that, for any eigenvalue

By looking at the first term of the eigenrelation

Together with the equation (

This ends the proof in the alpha case.

6.
The inverse of the formal Laplace transform

The formal Laplace transform
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}
admits the unique inverse map
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}^{-1}
that takes as input a power series on
z^{-1}
without constant coefficient and acts as follows:

(63)

\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}^{-1}\bigg\{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{a_{n}z^{-n-1}}\bigg\}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{\frac{a_{n}}{n!}t^{n}}.

Lemma 6.1
.

For any power series
f(z)
on
z^{-1}
without constant coefficient, we have

\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}^{-1}\Big\{f(z)\Big\}=-\frac{1}{t}\cdot\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}^{-1}\Big\{f^{\prime}(z)\Big\}.

Proof.

Immediately follows from
Equation

63
.
∎

Theorem 6.2
.

Assume that
F(z)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(z)}\cdot G(z)
is an entire function of finite order whose nonzero roots can be labeled
\ell_{1}>\ell_{2}>\dots
, and satisfy
\ell_{k+1}=-k+O\left(k^{-N}\right)
, for any
N\geq 1
, as
k\to\infty
.
Moreover, assume that
G(z)
admits an asymptotic expansion
G(z)\sim 1+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{a_{n}z^{-n-1}}
, as
z\to\infty
along the
x
-axis. Then

\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}^{-1}\big\{\ln{G(z)}\big\}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{n}}{(n+1)!}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\Big((-k)^{n+1}-(\ell_{k+1})^{n+1}\Big).

The left-hand side of the above equality is a notation that really stands for the inverse of the formal Laplace transform (
63
) applied to
\ln(1+x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{(-1)^{n-1}\frac{x^{n}}{n}}
, where
x=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{a_{n}z^{-n-1}}
.

Proof.

Since
\ell_{k+1}=-k+O\left(k^{-1}\right)
, as
k\to\infty
, then

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}{\frac{1}{|\ell_{k+1}|}}=\infty
, but
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}{\frac{1}{|\ell_{k+1}|^{2}}}<\infty
.
Therefore, by Hadamard’s factorization theorem,

F(z)=z^{m}e^{Q(z)}\prod_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(1-\frac{z}{\ell_{k+1}}\right)e^{z/\ell_{k+1}},

where the nonnegative integer
m
is the multiplicity of
z=0
as a zero of
F(z)
, and
Q(z)
is a polynomial.
After taking the logarithmic derivative, we obtain

(64)

\Big(\ln{F(z)}\Big)^{\prime}=\frac{m}{z}+Q^{\prime}(z)+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left\{\frac{1}{z-\ell_{k+1}}+\frac{1}{\ell_{k+1}}\right\}.

On the other hand, the expansion of the digamma function
\left(\ln{\Gamma(z)}\right)^{\prime}=\Gamma^{\prime}(z)/\Gamma(z)
is

(65)

\left(\ln{\Gamma(z)}\right)^{\prime}=-\gamma+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left\{\frac{1}{k+1}-\frac{1}{z+k}\right\},

where
\gamma>0
is the Euler-Mascheroni constant; this expansion is valid for all
z
outside the set of nonpositive integers.

By adding
Equations

64
and
65
, we get

(66)

\Big(\ln{F(z)}\Big)^{\prime}+\left(\ln{\Gamma(z)}\right)^{\prime}=\frac{m}{z}+Q^{\prime}(z)-\gamma+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left\{\frac{1}{\ell_{k+1}}+\frac{1}{k+1}+\frac{1}{z-\ell_{k+1}}-\frac{1}{z+k}\right\}.

Note that
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{\ell_{k+1}}+\frac{1}{k+1}\right)
converges; therefore, the sum on the right-hand side of (
66
) can be broken down into two sums.
On the other hand, since
G(z)=F(z)\cdot\Gamma(z)
, the left-hand side of (
66
) is equal to
(\ln{G(z)})^{\prime}
. The equality will be true for values of
z
that are not the roots
\ell_{k+1}
of
F(z)
and are not in the set of nonpositive integers. Thus,

(67)

\Big(\ln{G(z)}\Big)^{\prime}=\frac{m}{z}+R(z)+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left\{\frac{1}{z-\ell_{k+1}}-\frac{1}{z+k}\right\},

where
R(z)
is the polynomial

R(z):=Q^{\prime}(z)-\gamma+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left\{\frac{1}{\ell_{k+1}}+\frac{1}{k+1}\right\}.

By the assumption of
G(z)
, we have
\lim_{z\to+\infty}{z\cdot\big(\ln{G(z)}\big)^{\prime}}=0
.
We claim that the same is true of
z
times the summation in the RHS of
Equation

67
.
Indeed, if
z>0
and
k
is large enough so that
\ell_{k+1}<0
and
|\ell_{k+1}+k|<\text{const}_{1}/k
, for some positive constant, then

\left|z\cdot\left(\frac{1}{z-\ell_{k+1}}-\frac{1}{z+k}\right)\right|=\frac{|z|\cdot|\ell_{k+1}+k|}{|z-\ell_{k+1}|\cdot|z+k|}\leq\frac{z\cdot\text{const}_{1}/k}{z\cdot(z+k)}\leq\frac{\text{const}_{1}}{k\cdot(2\sqrt{zk})}=z^{-\frac{1}{2}}\cdot\frac{\text{const}_{2}}{k^{3/2}}.

It follows that
m+zR(z)
converges to zero, as
z\to\infty
, and therefore it is zero.
As a result,

(68)

\Big(\ln{G(z)}\Big)^{\prime}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left\{\frac{1}{z-\ell_{k+1}}-\frac{1}{z+k}\right\}.

Since
\ell_{k+1}=-k+O\left(k^{-N}\right)
, for all
N\geq 1
, then the sums
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\Big(\big(\ell_{k+1}\big)^{n}-\big(-k\big)^{n}\Big)
converge, for all
n\geq 1
. By expanding the RHS of
Equation

68
around
z=\infty
, we obtain

(69)

\Big(\ln{G(z)}\Big)^{\prime}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}z^{-n-1}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\Big(\big(\ell_{k+1}\big)^{n}-\big(-k\big)^{n}\Big).

Finally, by
Lemma

6.1
and
Equation

69
, we deduce that

\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}^{-1}\Big\{\ln{G(z)}\Big\}=-\frac{1}{t}\cdot\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}^{-1}\bigg\{\Big(\ln{G(z)}\Big)^{\prime}\bigg\}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{n}}{(n+1)!}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left((-k)^{n+1}-\big(\ell_{k+1}\big)^{n+1}\right),

as desired.
∎

\gamma
-cumulants of
\mu_{\Alpha}^{\gamma;c;\eta}
are
\kappa_{n}=\eta\left(\frac{c}{1-c}\right)^{n}
, thus
Equation

23
gives

\displaystyle 1+\sum_{n\geq 1}{\frac{c_{n}}{\gamma^{\uparrow n}}z^{n}}=\exp\left(\eta\sum_{n\geq 1}{\frac{1}{n}\left(\frac{cz}{1-c}\right)^{n}}\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\bigg(-\eta\cdot\ln\Big(1-\frac{cz}{1-c}\Big)\bigg)

\displaystyle=\Big(1-\frac{cz}{1-c}\Big)^{-\eta}=\sum_{n\geq 0}{\frac{\eta^{\uparrow n}}{n!}\Big(\frac{c}{1-c}\Big)^{n}z^{n}},

where the last equality follows from the binomial theorem.
As a result,

c_{n}=\frac{\gamma^{\uparrow n}\eta^{\uparrow n}}{n!}\Big(\frac{c}{1-c}\Big)^{n},\ \text{ for all }n\geq 1.

Then the LHS of
Equation

24
is

1+\sum_{n\geq 1}{\frac{(-1)^{n}\,\frac{\gamma^{\uparrow n}\eta^{\uparrow n}}{n!}\left(\frac{c}{1-c}\right)^{n}}{z^{\uparrow n}}}=\sum_{n\geq 0}{\frac{\gamma^{\uparrow n}\eta^{\uparrow n}}{z^{\uparrow n}}\cdot\frac{\left(\frac{c}{c-1}\right)^{n}}{n!}}={}_{2}F_{1}\Big(\gamma,\eta;z;\frac{c}{c-1}\Big)\\
=(1-c)^{\gamma}\cdot{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\gamma,z-\eta;z;c\right)=(1-c)^{\eta}\cdot{}_{2}F_{1}\left(z-\gamma,\eta;z;c\right),

where we have used
Lemma

5.2
.
The advantage of the last two formulas is that the corresponding series define analytic functions, for any
c\in(0,1)
.
Next, if we let
M(s)
be the formal moment generating function of
\mu_{\Alpha}^{\gamma;c;\eta}
, then
Sections

4
and
6
imply the following equality of formal power series:

\frac{1}{\gamma t}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}{\Big(e^{-kt}-e^{\ell_{k}^{(\text{alpha})}t}\Big)}\cdot(1-e^{-t})=M(-t)-\frac{1}{\gamma t}\left(e^{\gamma t}-1\right).

The remaining of the proof then follows the proof of
Section

7
, starting by computing that the characteristic function of
\mu_{\Alpha}^{\gamma;c;\eta}
is

\int_{\mathbb{R}}{e^{itx}\mu_{\Alpha}^{\gamma;c;\eta}(\dd x)}=\frac{1}{\gamma}\cdot\left\{\frac{e^{-i\ell_{1}^{(\text{alpha})}t}-e^{it}+1-e^{-i\gamma t}}{it}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}{\frac{e^{-i\ell_{k+1}^{(\text{alpha})}t}-e^{i\big(1-\ell_{k}^{(\text{alpha})}\big)t}}{it}}\right\},

and from there, the formula for the density follows.
∎

Theorem 7.3

(Limit of pure beta Jack measures)
.

Let
M\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
,
c\in(0,1)
.
Let
\mu_{\Beta}^{\gamma;c;M}
be the probability measure from
Section

3.2.2
.
Let
\ell_{1}^{(\text{beta})}\geq\dots\geq\ell_{M}^{(\text{beta})}
be the eigenvalues of
J^{(\text{beta})}
or, equivalently, the roots of
F^{\text{beta}}(z)
.
Then
\gamma\geq\ell_{1}^{(\text{beta})}
and
\ell_{k}^{(\text{beta})}\geq 1+\ell_{k+1}^{(\text{beta})}
, for all
1\leq k\leq M-1
; in particular, the eigenvalues are pairwise distinct.
Moreover,
\mu_{\Beta}^{\gamma;c;M}
has density

\frac{\dd\mu_{\Beta}^{\gamma;c;M}}{\dd x}=\frac{1}{\gamma}\sum_{k=0}^{M-1}{\mathbf{1}_{\big[1-\ell_{k}^{(\text{beta})},\,-\ell_{k+1}^{(\text{beta})}\big]}(x)},\quad x\in\mathbb{R},

if we set
\ell_{0}^{(\text{beta})}:=\gamma+1
.

Proof.

By
Section

3.2.2
, the quantized
\gamma
-cumulants of
\mu_{\Beta}^{\gamma;c;M}
are
\kappa_{n}=(-1)^{n-1}Mc^{n}
.
Then
Equation

23
gives

1+\sum_{n\geq 1}{\frac{c_{n}}{\gamma^{\uparrow n}}z^{n}}=\exp\left(\sum_{n\geq 1}{\frac{(-1)^{n-1}Mc^{n}z^{n}}{n}}\right)=(1+cz)^{M},

and leads to

c_{n}=\begin{cases}\dbinom{M}{n}c^{n}\gamma^{\uparrow n},&\text{if }n=1,\dots,M,\\
0,&\text{if }n>M.\end{cases}

Next, the LHS of
Equation

24
is

1+\sum_{n=1}^{M}{\frac{(-1)^{n}\dbinom{M}{n}c^{n}\gamma^{\uparrow n}}{z^{\uparrow n}}}=\sum_{n=0}^{M}{\frac{(-M)^{\uparrow n}\gamma^{\uparrow n}}{z^{\uparrow n}}\cdot\frac{c^{n}}{n!}}={}_{2}F_{1}(-M,\gamma;z;c).

Then, if we let
\mathfrak{M}(s)
be the formal moment generating function of
\mu_{\Beta}^{\gamma;c;M}
, and due to the already proved
Equation

70
, we deduce from
Section

4
the following equality of power series:

\frac{1}{\gamma t}\sum_{k=1}^{M}{\Big(e^{(1-k)t}-e^{\ell_{k}^{(\text{beta})}t}\Big)}\cdot(1-e^{-t})=\mathfrak{M}(-t)-\frac{1}{\gamma t}\left(e^{\gamma t}-1\right).

From here, the proof mimics the proof of
Section

7
. In fact, the previous equality gives

\int_{\mathbb{R}}{e^{itx}\mu_{\Beta}^{\gamma;c;M}(\dd x)}=\frac{1}{\gamma}\cdot\left\{\frac{e^{-i\ell_{1}^{(\text{beta})}t}-e^{-i\gamma t}}{it}+\sum_{k=1}^{M-1}{\frac{e^{-i\ell_{k+1}^{(\text{beta})}t}-e^{i\big(1-\ell_{k}^{(\text{beta})}\big)t}}{it}}\right\},

as well as the density formula for
\mu_{\Beta}^{\gamma;c;M}
. Hence, the proof is finished.
∎

8.
Appendix: sketch of proof of Lemma
5.5

8.1.
The Plancherel case

Let
\gamma,\eta>0
be arbitrary.
We want to prove that

(74)

F^{\text{planch}}(z)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(z)}\cdot{}_{1}F_{1}\left(\gamma;z;-\eta\right)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(z)}\sum_{n\geq 0}{\frac{\gamma^{\uparrow n}}{z^{\uparrow n}}\frac{(-\eta)^{n}}{n!}}

has finite order, i.e. that there exist constants
A,r>0
such that

(75)

\big|F^{\text{planch}}(z)\big|\leq\exp\big(|z|^{A}\big),\quad\text{for all }|z|>r.

It is enough to prove (
75
) with an additional assumption that
|z|\notin\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.
The key step is to bound the general
n
-th term in the sum (
74
), which we denote by
a_{n}
. Note that

(76)

|a_{n}|=\left|\frac{\gamma^{\uparrow n}}{z^{\uparrow n}}\frac{(-\eta)^{n}}{n!}\right|=\frac{\gamma^{\uparrow n}\eta^{n}}{n!}\cdot\frac{1}{\left|z^{\uparrow n}\right|}\leq\frac{(\lceil\gamma\rceil\eta)^{n}}{\left|z^{\uparrow n}\right|},\quad\text{for all }n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}.

Then there exists a unique integer
m\geq 2
such that

m-1>|z|>m-2.

Then, for
n\geq m
, we can lower bound
\left|z^{\uparrow n}\right|
as follows:

\displaystyle\left|z^{\uparrow n}\right|

\displaystyle=|z|\cdot|z+1|\cdots|z+n-1|

\displaystyle\geq|z|\cdots(|z|-m+3)\cdot|z+m-2|\cdot|z+m-1|\cdot(m-|z|)\cdots(n-1-|z|)

\displaystyle\geq 1\cdot|z+m-2|\cdot|z+m-1|\cdot 1\cdot 2\cdots(n-m)

(77)

\displaystyle\geq|z+m-2|\cdot|z+m-1|\cdot\frac{n!}{n^{m}}.

Next, since
n\geq m\geq 2
and
m<|z|+2
, we have
n^{m}\leq(2m)^{n}<(2|z|+4)^{n}
. Moreover, if we take
r
in (
75
) large enough so that
r>4
, then we want to prove
Equation

75
only for those
z
with
|z|>4
. In this case, we can use
2|z|+4\leq 3|z|
. It follows that
n^{m}<(2|z|+4)^{n}\leq(3|z|)^{n}
.
Combining with (
76
) and (
77
) then leads to

|a_{n}|\leq\frac{1}{|z+m-2|\cdot|z+m-1|}\cdot\frac{(3\lceil\gamma\rceil\eta|z|)^{n}}{n!},

for all
n\geq m
.
When
n\leq m-1
, then assuming
|z|>4
, we can bound

\frac{1}{\left|z^{\uparrow n}\right|}\leq\frac{1}{(m-2)\cdots(m-1-n)|z-n+1|}\leq\begin{cases}\frac{|z|^{n}}{n!}&\text{ for }n\leq m-2,\\
\frac{|z|^{n}}{n!}\frac{1}{|z+m-2|}&\text{ for }n=m-1.\end{cases}

Assume first that
z
is not close to
(2-m)
or to
(1-m)
; for concreteness, say e.g. that
\text{dist}(z,\mathbb{Z}_{\leq 0})\geq\frac{1}{10}
, so that
|z+m-2|\cdot|z+m-1|\geq\frac{1}{100}
. Then our analysis results into

\big|{}_{1}F_{1}\left(\gamma;z;-\eta\right)\big|\leq\sum_{n\geq 0}{|a_{n}|}\leq C\sum_{n\geq 0}{\frac{(C^{\prime}|z|)^{n}}{n!}}=C\cdot\exp(C^{\prime}|z|),

for some constants
C,C^{\prime}>0
.
From the asymptotic expansion of the inverse gamma function, we obtain
\left|\Gamma(z)^{-1}\right|\leq\exp\left(C^{\prime\prime}|z|^{2}\right)
, for some constant
C^{\prime\prime}>0
, as long as
|z|
is large enough.
As a result,
\big|F^{\text{planch}}(z)\big|=\left|\Gamma(z)^{-1}\right|\cdot\big|{}_{1}F_{1}\left(\gamma;z;-\eta\right)\big|\leq C\cdot\exp\big(C^{\prime}|z|+C^{\prime\prime}|z|^{2}\big)
, and therefore
Equation

75
follows, e.g. when
A=3
, if
r>0
is large enough.

If
z
is close to
(2-m)
, that is,
|z+m-2|<\frac{1}{10}
, then automatically
|z+m-1|>\frac{9}{10}
and this allows us to at least bound
\frac{z^{\uparrow n}}{(z+m-2)}
as above. As a result,

\big|(z+m-2)\cdot{}_{1}F_{1}(\gamma;z;-\eta)\big|\leq C\cdot\exp(C^{\prime}|z|),

for some constants
C,C^{\prime}>0
.
We can also bound
\left|(z+m-2)^{-1}\cdot\Gamma(z)^{-1}\right|\leq\exp\left(C^{\prime\prime}|z|^{2}\right)
, for some
C^{\prime\prime}>0
, by Cauchy’s integral formula.
Combining these two inequalities again gives the desired
Equation

75
for
A=3
and large enough
r>0
.

If
|z+m-1|<\frac{1}{10}
, the proof is the same.

8.2.
The alpha case

Let
\gamma,\eta>0
and
c\in(0,1)
be arbitrary.
It suffices to show that for

\widetilde{F}^{\text{{alpha}}}(z)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(z)}\cdot{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\gamma,z-\eta;z;c\right)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(z)}\sum_{n\geq 0}{\frac{\gamma^{\uparrow n}(z-\eta)^{\uparrow n}}{z^{\uparrow n}}\frac{c^{n}}{n!}},

there exist
A,r>0
such that

(78)

\big|\widetilde{F}^{\text{alpha}}(z)\big|\leq\exp\big(|z|^{A}\big),\text{ for all }|z|>r,

and similarly as before, it is enough to prove it with an additional assumption that
|z|\notin\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.
The proof is similar to that for the Plancherel case, so we only elaborate on how to modify the main step of the proof, which is to bound the modulus of
(z-\eta)^{\uparrow n}/z^{\uparrow n}
.

The easier case is when
z
is not close to the negative
x
-axis, in the sense that
|\arg{z}|\leq\frac{9\pi}{10}
.
Then one can verify that
|\arg(z+n)|\leq\frac{9\pi}{10}
and
\frac{|z|}{|z+n|}\leq\frac{1}{\sin(\pi/10)}=:C
, for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.
Using the known estimate for Gamma functions,

\frac{\Gamma(x+a)}{\Gamma(x+b)}=x^{a-b}\big(1+O(|x|)\big),\text{ as }|x|\to\infty,\ |\arg{x}|\leq\pi-\delta<\pi,

we get

(79)

\left|\frac{(z-\eta)^{\uparrow n}}{z^{\uparrow n}}\right|=\left|\frac{\Gamma(z-\eta+n)\Gamma(z)}{\Gamma(z-\eta)\Gamma(z+n)}\right|\lesssim\left(\frac{|z|}{|z+n|}\right)^{\eta}\leq C^{\eta},

for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, in the region
|\arg{z}|\leq\frac{9\pi}{10}
.
Above and throughout this proof, we use the notation
A(z,n)\lesssim B(z,n)
if there exists a constant
C>0
, independent of
z,n
, such that
A(z,n)\leq C\cdot B(z,n)
.
As a result,

\big|{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\gamma,z-\eta;z;c\right)\big|\leq\sum_{n\geq 0}{\left|\frac{(z-\eta)^{\uparrow n}}{z^{\uparrow n}}\right|\cdot\frac{\gamma^{\uparrow n}c^{n}}{n!}}\lesssim\sum_{n\geq 0}{\frac{\gamma^{\uparrow n}c^{n}}{n!}}=(1-c)^{-\gamma}.

Together with the bound
\left|\Gamma(z)^{-1}\right|\leq\exp\left(C^{\prime\prime}|z|^{2}\right)
, already used in the Plancherel case, we obtain the desired
Equation

78
for
A=3
and large enough
r>0
.

The case
|\arg z|\in(\frac{9\pi}{10},\pi]
remains.
Note that
\Re z<0
in this case.
Let
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
be such that
m-1<-\Re z<m
.
The main step is to bound the modulus of
\frac{(z-\eta)^{\uparrow n}}{z^{\uparrow n}}
; we show how to do it when
n\geq m
.
Note that
\Re(z+m)=\Re z+m>0
, so
|\arg(z+m)|\in[0,\frac{\pi}{2}]
, and we can use the inequality (
79
) that we already proved.
Moreover,
m-1<-\Re z=|\Re z|\leq|z|
implies that
|z|,|z+1|,\dots,|z+m-2|\geq 1
.
Combining all these inequalities, we deduce

\displaystyle\left|\frac{(z-\eta)^{\uparrow n}}{z^{\uparrow n}}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{(z-\eta)\cdots(z-\eta+m-1)(z-\eta+m)\cdots(z-\eta+n-1)}{z(z+1)\cdots(z+m-1)(z+m)\cdots(z+n-1)}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(|z|+\eta)\cdots(|z|+\eta+m-1)}{|z|\cdot|z+1|\cdots|z+m-1|}\cdot\left|\frac{(z+m-\eta)^{\uparrow(n-m)}}{(z+m)^{\uparrow(n-m)}}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{(|z|+\eta)\cdots(|z|+\eta+m-1)}{|z|\cdot|z+1|\cdots|z+m-1|}\leq\frac{(|z|+\eta+m-1)^{m}}{|z+m-1|}\leq\frac{(2|z|+\eta)^{|z|+1}}{|z+m-1|}.

Let us take
r
in
Equation

78
to satisfy
r>\max\{\eta,4\}
, so that we can use
\eta<|z|
; also,
4<|z|
implies
3|z|\leq e^{|z|-1}
.
As a result,

\left|\frac{(z-\eta)^{\uparrow n}}{z^{\uparrow n}}\right|\lesssim\frac{(2|z|+\eta)^{|z|+1}}{|z+m-1|}\leq\frac{(3|z|)^{|z|+1}}{|z+m-1|}\leq\frac{(e^{|z|-1})^{|z|+1}}{|z+m-1|}\leq\frac{e^{|z|^{2}}}{|z+m-1|}.

If
|z+m-1|\geq\frac{1}{10}
, then
\left|\frac{(z-\eta)^{\uparrow n}}{z^{\uparrow n}}\right|\lesssim\exp(|z|^{2})
, for all
n\geq m
.
Some additional work is needed in the case
n<m
, but the same holds, therefore
\big|{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\gamma,z-\eta;z;c\right)\big|\leq\sum_{n\geq 0}{\left|\frac{(z-\eta)^{\uparrow n}}{z^{\uparrow n}}\right|\cdot\frac{\gamma^{\uparrow n}c^{n}}{n!}}\lesssim\exp(|z|^{2})
. Together with
\left|\Gamma(z)^{-1}\right|\leq\exp\left(C^{\prime\prime}|z|^{2}\right)
, we obtain the desired
Equation

78
for
A=3
and large enough
r>0
.

If
|z+m-1|<\frac{1}{10}
, then we can prove that
|(z+m-1)\cdot{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\gamma,z-\eta;z;c\right)|\lesssim\exp(|z|^{2})
as above, and
\big|(z+m-1)^{-1}\cdot\Gamma(z)^{-1}\big|\leq\exp\big(C^{\prime\prime}|z|^{2}\big)
by Cauchy’s integral formula, as we did in the Plancherel case. The desired
Equation

78
follows and the proof is finished.

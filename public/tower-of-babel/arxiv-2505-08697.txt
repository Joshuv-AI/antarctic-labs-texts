A topos for extended Weihrauch degrees
Samuele Maschio and Davide Trotta

Abstract
Weihrauch reducibility is a notion of reducibility between computational problems that is useful to calibrate the uniform computational strength of a multi-valued function. It complements the analysis of mathematical theorems done in reverse mathematics, as multi-valued functions on represented spaces can be considered as realizers of theorems in a natural way. Despite the rich literature and the relevance of the applications of category theory in logic and realizability, actually there are just a few works starting to study the Weihrauch reducibility from a categorical point of view. The main purpose of this work is to provide a full categorical account to the notion of extended Weihrauch reducibility introduced by A. Bauer, which generalizes the original notion of Weihrauch reducibility. In particular, we present a tripos and a topos for extended Weihrauch degrees. We start by defining a new tripos, abstracting the notion of extended Weihrauch degrees, and then we apply the tripos-to-topos construction to obtain the desired topos. Then we show that the Kleene-Vesley topos is a topos of j j -sheaves for a certain Lawvere-Tierney topology over the topos of extended Weihrauch degrees.



1
Introduction

Categorical logic is the branch of mathematics in which tools and concepts from category theory are applied to the study of mathematical logic.
Nowadays, categorical methods are applied in many areas of mathematical logic. One area that has been less influenced by this categorical approach is computability and, in light of the long tradition of the application of categorical methods in realizability studies, see e.g.
[
36
]
, this seems to be quite peculiar.
In recent years, there have been some works starting to approach computability-like notions from a categorical perspective. E.g. a first approach to Medvedev and Muchnik reducibility via hyperdoctrines has been introduced in
[
17
]
, while Weihrauch reducibility for assemblies (or multi-represented spaces) has been introduced only very recently in
[
15
,
31
]
through the notion of realizer-based Weihrauch reducibility. In
[
1
]
, Bauer introduced an abstract notion of reducibility between predicates, called instance reducibility, which commonly appears in reverse constructive mathematics. In a relative realizability topos, the instance degrees correspond to a generalization of (realizer-based) Weihrauch reducibility, called
extended Weihrauch degrees
, and the “classical” Weihrauch degrees
[
3
]
correspond precisely to the
\lnot\lnot
-dense modest instance degrees in Kleene-Vesley realizability. Upon closer inspection, it is not hard to check that realizer-based Weihrauch reducibility is a particular case of Bauer’s notion.

Finally, in
[
35
]
, a categorical formulation of Turing, Medvedev, Muchnik, and Weihrauch reducibilities is presented using Lawvere doctrines, which are a generalization of the notion of
hyperdoctrine
introduced by Lawvere
[
20
,
18
,
19
]
to synthesize the structural properties of logical systems.

The main purpose of this work is to carry on this research line, focusing on the notion of extended Weihrauch degrees. Our main goal is to define a topos for extended Weihrauch degrees, providing a suitable universe for studying this reducibility categorically, in the same way as Hyland’s Effective topos
[
14
]
is a suitable universe for studying realizability. Then, we take advantage from this categorical presentation, and we establish the precise connection between extended Weihrauch degrees and realizability.

In detail: the main tools we adopt to define and construct such a topos is the so-called
tripos-to-topos construction
introduced by Hyland, Johnstone, and Pitts
[
13
,
28
]
, producing a topos from a given tripos, and the
(full) existential completion
, a construction that freely adds left adjoint, i.e. existential quantifiers, along all the morphisms of the base of a given doctrine, for defining the tripos abstracting extended Weihrauch degrees. Recall that a tripos is a specific instance of the notion of Lawvere hyperdoctrine, which has enough structure to deal with higher-order logic properly.

Hence, we first define a doctrine
\mathfrak{iR}\colon\mathsf{ParAsm}(\mathbb{A},\mathbb{A}^{\prime})^{\operatorname{op}}\longrightarrow\mathsf{InfSl}
abstracting Bauer’s notion of instance reducibility between realizability predicates, and we prove that it is a tripos (

\mathfrak{eiR}^{\exists}\colon\mathsf{ParAsm}(\mathbb{A},\mathbb{A}^{\prime})^{\operatorname{op}}\longrightarrow\mathsf{InfSl}
of a more basic doctrine
\mathfrak{eiR}\colon\mathsf{ParAsm}(\mathbb{A},\mathbb{A}^{\prime})^{\operatorname{op}}\longrightarrow\mathsf{InfSl}
, following the same idea used in
[
35
]
for defining doctrines abstracting computability reducibility.

Then we introduce a second doctrine
\mathfrak{eW}\colon\mathsf{ParAsm}(\mathbb{A},\mathbb{A}^{\prime})^{\operatorname{op}}\longrightarrow\mathsf{InfSl}
(

\mathfrak{eW}\cong\mathfrak{iR}

Notice that this result can be seen as a fibrational version of Bauer’s result showing that Weihrauch reductions and instance reductions are equivalent, see
[

Finally, once we have proved that the extended Weihrauch doctrine is a tripos, we can define the topos of extended Weirhauch degrees as the topos obtained by applying the tripos-to-topos to the tripos

and study the connections with (relative) realizability toposes. In particular, we prove that the relative realizability topos
\mathsf{RT}[\mathbb{A},\mathbb{A}^{\prime}]

[
2
]
is equivalent to a topos
\mathsf{sh}_{j}(\mathsf{EW}[\mathbb{A},\mathbb{A}^{\prime}])
of
j
-sheaves for a certain Lawvere-Tierney topology
j
over
\mathsf{EW}[\mathbb{A},\mathbb{A}^{\prime}]
(

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Partial combinatory algebras

Realizability theory originated with Kleene’s interpretation of intuitionistic number theory
[
16
]
and has since then developed into a large body of work in logic and theoretical computer science. We focus on two basic flavors of realizability, number realizability, and function realizability, which were both due to Kleene.

In this section, we recall some standard notions within realizability and computability. We follow the approach suggested by van Oosten
[
36
]
, as we want to fix a suitable notation for both category theorists and computability logicians.

We describe
partial combinatory algebras
and discuss some important examples. For more details, we refer the reader to van Oosten’s work on categorical realizability (see
[
36
]
and the references therein).

We start by introducing the basic concept of a
partial applicative structure
or PAS, due to Feferman, which may be viewed as a universe for computation.

Definition 2.1

(PAS)
.

A
partial applicative structure
, or PAS for short, is a set
\mathbb{A}
equipped with a

partial
binary operation

\cdot:\subseteq\mathbb{A}\times\mathbb{A}\to\mathbb{A}
.

Some conventions and terminology: given two elements
a,b
in
\mathbb{A}
, we think of
a\cdot b
(which we often abbreviate
ab
) as “
a
applied to
b
". The partiality of the operation
\cdot
means that this application need not always be defined. We write
f:\subseteq A\to B
to say that
f
is a partial function with domain a subset of
A
,
\operatorname{dom}(f)\subseteq A
, and range
\operatorname{ran}(f)\subseteq B
.
If
(a,b)\in\operatorname{dom}(\cdot)
, that is, if the application is defined, then we write
a\cdot b\downarrow
or
ab\downarrow
.

We usually omit brackets, assuming the associativity of application to the left. Thus
abc
stands for
(ab)c
. Moreover, for two expressions
s
and
s^{\prime}
we write
s\simeq s^{\prime}
to indicate that
s
is defined exactly when
s^{\prime}
is, in which case they are equal.

Even though these partial applicative structures do not possess many interesting properties (they have no axioms for application), they already highlight one of the key features of combinatorial structures, namely the fact that we have a domain of elements that can act both as functions and as arguments, just as in untyped
\lambda
-calculus.
This behaviour can be traced back to von Neumann’s idea that programs (functions, operations) live in the same realm and are represented in the same way as the data (arguments) that they act upon.
In particular, programs can act on other programs.

Definition 2.2

(PCA)
.

A
partial combinatory algebra
(PCA) is a PAS
\mathbb{A}
for which there exist elements
\mathsf{k},\mathsf{s}\in\mathbb{A}
such that for all
a,b,c\in\mathbb{A}
we have that

1.

\mathsf{k}ab=a

2.

\mathsf{s}ab\downarrow

3.

\mathsf{s}abc\simeq ac(bc)

The elements
\mathsf{k}
and
\mathsf{s}
are generalizations of the homonymous combinators in Combinatory Logic. Note that appropriate elements
\mathsf{k},\mathsf{s}
are not considered part of the structure of the partial combinatory algebra, so they need not be preserved under homomorphisms.

Every PCA
\mathbb{A}
is combinatory complete in the sense of
[
5
,
36
]
, namely: for every term
t(x_{1},\dots,x_{n+1})
built from variables
x_{1},\dots,x_{n+1}
, constants
c\in\mathbb{A}
, and application operator
\cdot
, there exists an element
a\in\mathbb{A}
such that for all elements
b_{1},\dots,b_{n+1}\in\mathbb{A}
we have that
ab_{1}\cdots b_{n}\downarrow
and
ab_{1}\cdots b_{n+1}\simeq t(b_{1},\dots,b_{n+1})
. Such an element
a
can be taken as
\lambda x_{1}...\lambda x_{n+1}.t
, once one has encoded
\lambda
-abstraction as follows:

1.

\lambda x.x:=\mathsf{s}\mathsf{k}\mathsf{k}
;

2.

\lambda x.y:=\mathsf{k}y
if
y
is a variable different from
x
;

3.

\lambda x.a:=\mathsf{k}a
if
a\in\mathbb{A}
;

4.

\lambda x.tt^{\prime}=\mathsf{s}(\lambda x.t)(\lambda x.t^{\prime})
.

In particular, we can use this result and the elements
\mathsf{k}
and
\mathsf{s}
to construct elements
\mathsf{pair},\mathsf{p}_{1},\mathsf{p}_{2}
of
\mathbb{A}
so that
(a,b)\mapsto\mathsf{pair}\cdot a\cdot b
is an injection of
\mathbb{A}\times\mathbb{A}
into
\mathbb{A}
with left inverse
c\mapsto(\mathsf{p}_{1}\cdot c,\mathsf{p}_{2}\cdot c)
. Hence, we can use
\mathsf{pair}ab
as an element of
\mathbb{A}
which encodes the pair
(a,b)
. For this reason, the elements
\mathsf{pair},\mathsf{p}_{1},\mathsf{p}_{2}
are usually called
pairing
and
projection
operators. For sake of readability, we write
\langle a,b\rangle
in place of
\mathsf{pair}ab
(as it is more customary in computability theory).

Using
\mathsf{k}
and
\mathsf{s}
, we can prove the analogues of the Universal Turing Machine (UTM) and the SMN theorems in computability in an arbitrary PCA.

If
X,Y\subseteq\mathbb{A}
, we denote with
X\otimes Y
the set
\{\langle x,y\rangle\}|\,x\in X,y\in Y\}
and with
X\oplus Y
the set
(\{\mathsf{k}\}\otimes X)\cup(\{\bar{\mathsf{k}}\}\otimes Y)
.

Next we recall the notion of
elementary sub-PCA
(see for example
[
36
, Sec. 2.6.9]
).

Definition 2.3

(elementary sub-PCA)
.

Let
\mathbb{A}
be a PCA. A subset
\mathbb{A}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{A}
is called an
elementary sub-PCA
of
\mathbb{A}
if
\mathbb{A}^{\prime}
is a PCA with the partial applicative structure induced by
\mathbb{A}
(namely, the elements
\mathsf{k}
and
\mathsf{s}
as in
can be found in
\mathbb{A}^{\prime}
). In particular, it is closed under the application of
\mathbb{A}
, that means: if
a,b\in\mathbb{A}^{\prime}
and
ab\downarrow
in
\mathbb{A}
then
ab\in\mathbb{A}^{\prime}
.

In particular, elements of the sub-PCA will play the role of the computable functions.

Example 2.4

(
Kleene’s first model
)
.

Fix an effective enumeration
(\varphi_{a})_{a\in\mathbb{N}}
of the partial recursive functions
\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
(i.e. a Gödel numbering). The set
\mathbb{N}
with partial recursive application
(a,b)\mapsto\varphi_{a}(b)
is a PCA, and it is called
Kleene’s first model

\mathcal{K}_{1}
(see e.g.
[
32
]
).

Example 2.5

(
Kleene’s second model
)
.

The PCA
\mathcal{K}_{2}
is often used for function realizability
[
36
, Sec. 1.4.3]
. This PCA is given by the Baire space
\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}
, endowed with the product topology.
The partial binary operation of application
\cdot:\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}\times\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}\to\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}
corresponds to the one used in Type-
2
Theory of Effectivity
[
38
]
.
This can be described as follows. Let
\alpha[n]
denote the string
(\alpha(0),\ldots,\alpha(n-1))
.
Every
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}
induces a function
F_{\alpha}:\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}\to\mathbb{N}
defined as
F_{\alpha}(\beta)=k
if there is
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\alpha(\langle\beta[n]\rangle)=k+1
and
(\forall m<n)(\alpha(\langle\beta[n]\rangle)=0)
, and undefined otherwise. The application
\alpha\cdot\beta
can then be defined as the map
n\mapsto F_{\alpha}((n)\smash{\raisebox{3.87495pt}{$\smallfrown$}}\beta)
, where
(n)\smash{\raisebox{3.87495pt}{$\smallfrown$}}\beta
is the string
\sigma
defined as
\sigma(0):=n
and
\sigma(k+1):=\beta(k)
.

When working with Kleene’s second model, we usually consider the elementary sub-PCA
\mathcal{K}_{2}^{rec}
consisting of all the
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}
such that
\beta\mapsto\alpha\cdot\beta
is computable. For more details and examples, also of non-elementary sub-PCAs for Kleene’s second model, we refer to
[
37
]
.

2.2
Assemblies and partitioned assemblies

We briefly recall some useful definitions regarding the notion of
assembly
and
partitioned assembly
(in relative realizability), explaining how these can be seen as a categorification (and a generalization) of the notion of represented space. We refer to
[
36
]
for a complete presentation of these notions, and to
[
1
,
8
,
12
,
2
]
for more specific applications. In the following definition, we mainly follow the notation used in
[
1
]
, which is closer to the usual one used in computability.

Definition 2.6

(Assemblies and Partitioned assemblies)
.

Let
\mathbb{A}
be a PCA. An
assembly
is a pair
X=(|X|,\Vdash_{X})
where
|X|
is a set and
\Vdash_{X}\subseteq\mathbb{A}\times|X|
is a total relation,i.e.
(\forall x\in|X|)(\exists r\in\mathbb{A})(r\Vdash_{X}x)
. An assembly
X
is
partitioned
if
r\Vdash_{X}x
and
r^{\prime}\Vdash_{X}x
implies
r=r^{\prime}
, i.e. every element of
X
has exactly one name in
\mathbb{A}
.

Definition 2.7

(realizer for assemblies)
.

Let
X
and
Y
be two assemblies, and let
f:\subseteq|X|\rightrightarrows|Y|
be a multi-valued function between their underlying sets. A
realizer

F
for
f
(we write
F\vdash f
) is a (partial) function
F:\subseteq\mathbb{A}\to\mathbb{A}
such that for all
r\Vdash_{X}x
it holds that
r\in\operatorname{dom}(F)
and
F(r)\Vdash_{Y}f(x)
.

Definition 2.8

(Morphism of assemblies)
.

Let
\mathbb{A}
be a PCA and
\mathbb{A}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{A}
be an elementary sub-PCA of
\mathbb{A}
.
A
morphism of assemblies

f\colon X\to Y
is a function
f\colon|X|\to|Y|
which has a realizer in
\mathbb{A}^{\prime}
, i.e. it is a function such that there exists an element
a\in\mathbb{A}^{\prime}
with the property that for every
r\Vdash_{X}x
it holds that
a\cdot r\downarrow
and
a\cdot r\Vdash_{Y}f(x)
.

Definition 2.9
.

Let
\mathbb{A}
be a PCA and
\mathbb{A}^{\prime}
be an elementary sub-PCA of
\mathbb{A}
. We define the category of
assemblies

\mathsf{Asm}(\mathbb{A},\mathbb{A}^{\prime})
as the category having assemblies as objects and where arrows are morphisms of assemblies (with the usual set-theoretical composition of functions).
The category of
partitioned assemblies

\mathsf{ParAsm}(\mathbb{A},\mathbb{A}^{\prime})
is the full subcategory of
\mathsf{Asm}(\mathbb{A},\mathbb{A}^{\prime})
whose objects are partitioned assemblies.

Primary and existential doctrines

Lawvere introduced the notion of

19

Several generalizations of such a notion have been considered recently, e.g. we refer to the works of Rosolini and Maietti

We start by recalling a simple generalization of the notion of hyperdoctrine:

Definition 2.10

(primary doctrine)
.

A

is a functor

P\colon\mathcal{C}^{\operatorname{op}}\longrightarrow\mathsf{InfSl}

from the opposite of a category
\mathcal{C}
with finite limits to the category
\mathsf{InfSl}
of inf-semilattices.

Notice that, with respect to the original definition of primary doctrine, here we also require that the base category has finite limits.

The notion of

Drawing up on Lawvere’s treatment of logic in terms of hyperdoctrines, a tripos can be presented as a first order hyperdoctrine equipped with

(weak power objects)

A primary doctrine

Remark 4.10
.

Notice that in
[
35
]
, a doctrine was already introduced such that its fibre on the terminal object corresponds to extended Weihrauch degrees, and such a doctrine was constructed as the
pure
existential completion (freely adding left adjoints just along product projections) of a more basic doctrine. However, the doctrine introduced in
[
35
]
is not a tripos. The main reason is that the pure existential completion is the minimal construction we need, in order to have that the fibres on the terminal abstract the desired degrees. However, it does not add enough structure to obtain a tripos. The full existential completion maintains the same fibre over the terminal object (hence it still abstract extended Weihrauch degrees) but by freely adding
all
the left adjoints we get more structure on the other fibres. Indeed, in this case we obtain a tripos
.

The tripos-to-topos

The tripos-to-topos construction has been introduced in order to show that, from an abstract perspective, realizability and localic toposes can be proved to be instances of the same construction.

Conclusions and future work

We have presented here a new tripos abstracting the notion of extended Weihrauch reducibility and the corresponding topos obtained via the tripos-to-topos construction. Then, we have employed the existential completion to establish the formal connection between toposes of extended Weihrauch degrees and (relative) realizability toposes. These happen to be toposes of
j
-sheaves for certain Lawevere-Tierney topologies over the toposes of extended Weihrauch degrees.

In future work, we aim to start studying in detail the logic inside this new topos we introduced and its categorical properties. We expect it should enjoy some of the main categorical properties of (relative) realizability toposes, such as being an exact completion of a lex category.

Finally, we intend explore connections with other recent categorical notion related to extended Weihrauch reducibility, like that of
containers
presented in
[
29
]
.

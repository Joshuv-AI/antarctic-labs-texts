The difference in contralateral breast cancer and ovarian cancer risks for BRCA1 founder variant (c.5266dup, c.4035del) carriers with primary breast cancer
Authors: Peteris Loza, Arvids Irmejs, Zanda Daneberga, Jelena Maksimenko, Genadijs Trofimovics, Signe Subatniece, Jacek Gronwald, Jan Lubinski, Janis Gardovskis
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeTo compare the risks of CBC and OC following a diagnosis of PBC in carriers of founder germline PV’s of BRCA1 gene (c.5266dup and c.4035del), and to evaluate the impact of age at PBC diagnosis on subsequent CBC and OC risks.Subjects and methodsThis retrospective bicentric cohort study included 1,364 female BRCA1 PV carriers diagnosed with and PBC. The control cohort comprised 11,350 unselected female PBC cases without known BRCA1 PVs. Follow-up begun at PBC diagnosis and continued until CBC or OC occurrence, censoring at risk-reducing surgery, death, or last follow-up. Risks were estimated using Kaplan-Meier analysis, log-rank testing and Cox proportional hazards modelling.ResultsThe 10-year cumulative risk of CBC was 3.3% in controls, 25.0% in the c.5266dup carriers, and 13.1% in the c.4035del carriers. BRCA1 PV carriers had significantly higher CBC risks compared with controls (p < 0.001), with c.5266dup conferring greater risk than c.4035del (p = 0.045). Age < 40 years at PBC diagnosis independently increased CBC risk (HR 2.06, 95% CI 1.83–2.29, p < 0.001). For OC, the 10-year cumulative risk was 1.0% in controls, 13.0% in the c.5266dup carriers and 22.1% in the c.4035del carriers. BRCA1 PV carriers had higher OC risks compared with controls (p < 0.001), and c.4035del conferred higher risk than c.5266dup, (p = 0.043). Age < 40 years at PBC did not significantly affect OC risk (HR 1.03, 95% CI 0.77–1.29, p = 0.23).ConclusionBoth BRCA1 founder PVs confer substantially increased risks of CBC and OC compared with the general breast cancer population. The PV c.5266dup is associated with relatively higher CBC risk and lower OC risk, whereas PV c.4035del is associated with relatively lower CBC risk but higher OC risk. Age < 40 years at PBC further increases CBC risk, but does not influence OC risk. These findings highlight the importance of PV location in refining individualized risk-reduction strategies for BRCA1 carriers.Keywords: BRCA1, c.5266dup, c.4035del, Founder, CBC, OC, Contralateral breast cancer, Ovarian cancer

Simple summary

Recent research indicates that the risks of primary breast cancer (PBC) and ovarian cancer (OC) in BRCA1 pathogenic variant (PV) carriers may vary depending on the specific mutation`s location within the gene. However, current evidence remains insufficient to modify clinical decisions regarding individual risk management, especially in populations with strong founder effects. In our clinical practice, most newly diagnosed BRCA PV carriers undergo germline testing only after a diagnosis of primary breast cancer (PBC), at which point decisions regarding further risk-reducing interventions must be considered alongside primary treatment. We therefore aimed to evaluate the risks of contralateral breast cancer (CBC) and OC following PBC. Specifically, we focused on two regionally prevalent founder BRCA1 PVs - c.5266dup and c.4035del - located in different functional regions of the gene. Given that younger age at PBC diagnosis (< 40 years) has been linked with increased CBC risk, we also assessed whether this association holds true in our founder population.

Introduction

Women with primary breast cancer (PBC) who carry a germline pathogenic variant (PV) of the BRCA1 gene (NM_007294.4; Homo sapiens BRCA1, located on chromosome 17,) face markedly elevated lifetime risks of both contralateral breast cancer (CBC) and ovarian cancer (OC). BRCA1 functions as a tumor suppressor with central role in DNA damage repair through homologous recombination (HR) and other mechanisms.

Published data estimate that BRCA1 PV carriers face 10-year cumulative CBC risks from 10.4% to 41.5%, while lifetime OC risks range between 34% and 59% [1–10]. These broad ranges likely reflect heterogeneity in study designs, ascertainment methods, number and age of enrolled subjects, genetic modifying factors, and the specific PVs spectrum in different populations. This variability may be particularly pronounced in regions with strong founder effects, where only a limited number of PVs dominate.

Until recently, the influence of PV location within the BRCA1 gene on cancer risks was poorly understood. The largest observational study to date, conducted by the Consortium of Investigators of Modifiers of BRCA (CIMBA), demonstrated that the relative risks of breast and ovarian cancer vary significantly according to PVs location in specific regions (“bins”) within BRCA1 gene. These include breast cancer cluster regions (BCCR1: c.179-c.505, BCCR2: c.4328-c.4945, and BCCR2’: c.5261-c.5563) and ovarian cancer cluster region (OCCR: c.1380– c.4062) [11].

Another recent large study from Cambridge confirmed that BRCA1 PVs located outside the region bounded by positions c.2282 to c.4071 (also defined as OCCR, corresponding to exon 11) were associated with a significantly higher risk of breast cancer compared with PVs within the region (HR 1.46, 95% CI 1.11–1.93, p = 0.007) [1]. The authors hypothesized that PVs in exon 11 may permit partial expression of truncated but partially functional BRCA1 protein through an alternative splice variant, whereas PVs outside this region more often result in complete loss of function through nonsense-mediated mRNA decay (NMD). This mechanism may explain the distinct clinical phenotypes associated with PVs located in exon 11 compared with those outside the region.

While several large-scale studies have evaluated how PV location influences the risks of PBC and OC, most BRCA1 PV carriers in our clinical practice are identified only after being diagnosed with PBC. In this scenario, understanding the risks of subsequent CBC and OC is crucial for decision-making regarding surveillance and risk-reducing interventions.

We hypothesized that the risk of CBC parallels the underlying PBC risk and is therefore higher for PV’s located within BCCR regions, whereas, OC risk after PBC reflects lifetime OC risk, which is greater for PVs located in the OCCR. To test this, we analyzed outcomes in BRCA1 PV carriers with PBC in a founder population enriched with two recurrent PVs, one located in BCCR and other in OCCR, and compared with unscreened control cohort with PBC. To our knowledge, no prior study has focused specifically on the risks of CBC and OC following PBC in relation to specific PV location.

In addition, because younger age at PBC diagnosis (< 40 years) is a well-established independent risk factor for CBC, we evaluated whether this association holds true in our founder population [3].

Two founder BRCA1 variants

Previous studies have shown that the population of Latvia is predominantly affected by two founder PVs of the BRCA1 gene: c.5266dup, the most common, and c.4035del, the second most common. Together, these two variants account for up to 84% of all BRCA1 PVs in Latvia, with c.5266dup comprising up to 50% and c.4035del up to 34% of carriers [12–14]. In a prior Latvian study, the ratio of breast-to-ovarian cancer risk differed significantly between carriers of these two PVs (OR 2.98, 95% CI 1.58–5.62, P < 0.001) [15].

The PV c.5266dup is a frameshift mutation located in exon 20, resulting in a truncated protein subject to nonsense-mediated mRNA decay (NMD) and thus a likely complete loss of gene function. It disrupts the BRCT domain, a critical region for DNA damage signaling and repair, and overlaps with BCCR2 bin, which may account for its association with relatively higher breast cancer risk. It is also the most common PV in Estonia and the second most frequent in Lithuania (33%) [16, 17]. Phylogenetic studies suggest that it originated roughly 1,800 years ago in Scandinavia or northern Russia and subsequently spread widely across Europe. Today it remains the predominant BRCA1 PV in Russia (90% − 96% of all BRCA1 carriers), Poland (51%), and it is also frequent in Ashkenazi Jews and other Central and Eastern European populations [18–20].

By contrast, PV c.4035del occurs earlier in the gene, within exon 11, which overlaps with OCCR bin. Like c.5266dup, it is also a frameshift mutation that produces a truncated protein subject to NMD. However, exon 11 PVs may retain some partial function, either through alternative splicing or incomplete degradation, potentially leading to different tissue-specific consequences. Exon 11 encodes large regions of BRCA1 gene, including domains critical for binding RAD51, a key mediator of homologous recombination (HR) repair. Disruption of this region has been linked to relatively lower breast cancer risk but higher OC risk. The c.4035del is the most common BRCA1 PV in Lithuania (53%) and second most frequent PV in Estonia (27%) [21]. It is also highly prevalent in other Central and Eastern European populations.

Subjects and methods

This analysis included a cohort of unselected female cases diagnosed with non-metastatic (Stage I–III) PBC, who were carriers of germline BRCA1 PV. A time-to-event analysis was conducted, with follow-up beginning at the time of PBC diagnosis and continuing until the occurrence of CBC or OC. Censored cases were considered those, which did not experience the event by the time of last follow-up, death or a relevant risk reducing procedure- contralateral risk-reducing mastectomy (RRM) for CBC or risk-reducing bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy (RRBSO) for OC. Ipsilateral breast cancer and other malignancies were not considered as events in this analysis. All cases of primary OC at baseline were excluded. Indication for germline genetic testing was PBC, regardless of age of onset, histological subtype or family history. All cases were confirmed positive for one of the two founder BRCA1 PV’s using allele-specific PCR. The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Riga Stradins University, and all participants provided written informed consent. Recruitment was conducted between 2005 and 2023.

For the CBC risk analysis, the initial cohort consisted of 1,408 female cases. To maximize statistical power, particularly for the less frequent PV c.4035del, we combined data from two registries: the Riga Stradins University, Institute of Oncology and Molecular Genetics (Latvia) and the International Hereditary Cancer Center, Department of Genetics and Pathology, Pomeranian Medical University (Szczecin, Poland). The combined cohort included 239 unselected female cases from Latvia (diagnosed 1980–2023) and 1,169 unselected female cases from Poland (diagnosed 1978–2022). Only cases with metachronous CBC- defined as contralateral tumors occurring at least 6 months after PBC- were included. Consequently 47 synchronous PBC cases were excluded, leaving 1,363 eligible cases for analysis.

For the OC risk analysis, we included 233 cases from the Latvian registry, as only this cohort had complete data on RRBSO status. In addition, five cases with synchronous PBC and OC were included.

The control cohort consisted of 11,350 consecutive unselected cases from Latvian Cancer Registry, diagnosed with PBC between 2009 and 2019, with follow-up until October 2022. Genetic testing data were not available for this cohort, based on population estimates, around 10% of cases would be expected to carry PVs in BRCA1 or other high/moderate-risk genes [22]. A total of 9,554 were included after excluding cases with stage IV disease at PBC diagnosis, those who died of oncological causes within 6 months of diagnosis, cases with primary OC, and 165 cases of synchronous bilateral PBC. Unfortunately, due to data protection limitations, age at PBC diagnosis could not be obtained for this registry cohort.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analysis was carried out using “R” (R Core Team. (2024). R-a language and environment for statistical computing) and “Jamovi” (The jamovi project (2024). Jamovi (Version 2.5) software. Data about the date and subjects age at the time of PBC and date of end-event, CBC, OC, or censored, was imputed in the software. The cumulative risks of CBC and OC were calculated using the Kaplan-Meier type analysis, including “cumulative events” function in “Jamovi”. Risk calculations and graphs were created independently for CBC and OC cohorts. The log-rank test was used to test the differences between control cohort and study cohorts and between two types of PVs. Age younger than 40 years at the time of PBC was used as binary variable (risk factor) and calculated using Cox proportional hazards model for CBC and OC cohorts independently.

Importantly, 478/1,363 cases (35.1%) were younger than 40 years at the time of PBC. Age < 40 years was an independent risk factor for CBC (HR 2.06, 95% CI 1.83–2.29, p < 0.001).

Risk of ovarian cancer

The OC cohort included 233 BRCA1 PV carriers with PBC. Median follow-up time was 7.8 (range 0.8–34) years, during which 32 (13.7%) events of OC were observed, in 5 cases OC and PBC was synchronous. Mean age at diagnosis of OC was 56.2 (range 36–79) years, 55.3 for PV c.5266dup and 58.1 for PV c.4035del. There were 12 cases of death before the end of follow-up, and in 58 cases RRBSO was done at some time after PBC diagnosis (constituting part of censored cases). In control group (N = 9,554), during median follow-up time of 7.2 (0.6–13) years, 52/9554 (0.5%) events of OC were observed.

Cumulative risk of developing OC at 10 and 20 years was 1.0% and 1.7% in controls, 13.0% and 23.6% c.5266dup carriers, 22.1% and 49.7% in c.4035del carriers. There was significant difference in OC risks between controls and BRCA1 PV carriers (log-rank p < 0.001). Moreover, c.4035del carriers showed relatively higher risk of OC, compared to the c.5266dup (log-rank p = 0.043), Table 3; Fig. 2. Age < 40 years at the time of PBC was not associated with any difference in future OC risks (HR 1.03, 95% CI 0.77–1.29, p = 0.23).

Discussion

This study confirms that BRCA1 PV carriers with PBC face significantly higher risks of both CBC and OC, compared to the general breast cancer population. Importantly, we demonstrate this elevated risk specifically for two regionally prevalent founder PV’s. Our findings on CBC risk align closely with data from larger European studies. For instance, a Swedish meta-analysis of 807 BRCA1/2 PV carriers and 3,163 non-carrier controls reported a more than threefold increased CBC risk in mutation carriers (RR 3.56, 95% CI 2.50–5.08, p < 0.001) [4]. Similarly, a Dutch multicenter study of 6294 breast cancer patients ≤ 50 years showed a fourfold increased 10-year CBC risk for BRCA1 PV carriers (21.1%) compared to non-carriers (5.1%) [6]. In a recent German multicenter study BRCA1 PV carriers had a 10-year CBC risk of 25.1% versus 3.6% for non-carriers [23]. Our study found a comparable 10-year CBC risk of 20.1% in BRCA1 PV carriers-more than sixfold higher than the 3.0% risk observed in our control cohort-supporting the high penetrance of two founder PV’s.

Rebbeck at al. reported that PV c.5266dup was associated with significantly increased PBC risk (HR 1.63, 95% CI 1.41–1.89) and reduced OC risk (HR 0.73, 95% CI 0.57–0.92), compared to reference nonsense mutations in exon 11 [11]. Breast tissue may be more sensitive to BRCT domain loss while ovarian tissue may be more strongly affected by exon 11 disruption and impaired RAD51 function. In our study, we confirm that PV c.5266dup confers a significantly higher CBC risk than PV c.4035del. The larger size of our c.5266dup cohort allowed us to narrow the confidence interval for CBC risk estimates, enabling more statistically robust comparison between the two variants and confirming the first part of our hypothesis. However, a larger 4035del sample is needed to improve power and verify this pattern definitively.

Regarding OC risk, our findings suggest that PV c.4035del may confer a higher risk than PV c.5266dup, consistent with the second part of our hypothesis. Nevertheless, due to the relatively small number of OC cases and events, the statistical power of this cohort analysis is limited. Larger datasets will be needed to confirm this data.

Another limitation of this study is the incompleteness of clinical and pathological data for more than two thirds of cases, (see Table 1), and the lack of detailed family cancer history. This was primarily due to the inclusion of older cases diagnosed over 15 years ago, for which electronic records were unavailable in Latvian and Polish registries. Despite this, we chose to retain these cases to maximize statistical power.

Currently, emerging evidence suggests that PV location within BRCA1 gene may influence cancer risk and phenotype, but is not yet sufficient to guide individualized risk-reducing recommendations. The molecular mechanisms linking PV location to functional impairment remain speculative. Our data indicate that two founder PV’s may differ in functional impact due to their location within distinct gene regions, which may in turn influence future CBC and OC risk.

Risk assessment in BRCA1 PV carriers after PBC diagnosis is critical when considering timing and type of risk-reducing procedures. Although ipsilateral mastectomy does not currently appear to offer a survival benefit over breast-conserving surgery in BRCA1 PV carriers, there is evidence that it reduces local recurrence risk. Moreover, contralateral risk-reducing mastectomy may offer a modest survival benefit, particularly in younger patients [24–26]. Importantly, risk-reducing bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy has been shown to improve overall survival and should be considered a key component of risk management in BRCA1 PV carriers [26, 27].

Conclusions

We can conclude, that once PBC diagnosis is established, both BRCA1 founder PVs are associated with significant increase in CBC as well OC risks, in comparison to general unscreened PBC population. Moreover, the c.5266dup is associated with relatively higher CBC risk and lower OC risk in comparison with the c.4035del. With further supporting evidence, recommendations about risk reducing surgeries could be adjusted individually, depending on PV location.

According to our study, age younger than 40 years at diagnosis of PBC holds as independent risk factor for CBC in BRCA1 PV c.5266dup and c.4035del carriers, but does not seem to impact on the risk of OC. This could help in tailoring risk-reduction strategy for young patients with BRCA1 PV and PBC.

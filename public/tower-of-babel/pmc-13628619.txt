Experimental Study on the Mechanism and Effect of Middle-Phase Microemulsion Flooding in a Conglomerate Reservoir
Authors: Hongzhi Shao, Tongjing Liu, Dan Guan, Jiangfei Sun, Wenjie Tang, Zirui Zhao, Huoxin Luan, Patiguli Maimaiti, Jiahao Zhan, Baokang Ren
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
To address the characteristics of strong heterogeneity, complex pore-throat structures, and poor adaptability of conventional flooding systems in conglomerate reservoirs, a systematic comparative study on the oil displacement efficiency of middle-phase microemulsion and conventional binary systems (polymer + surfactant) was conducted using a combination of long-core displacement experiments, interfacial tension measurements, and polarizing microscopy observations. Representative cores with permeability ranging from 50 to 500 mD were selected for displacement experiments under varying conditions of injection rate, permeability contrast, timing of chemical injection, and surfactant concentration. A three-stage experimental procedure comprising waterflooding, chemical flooding, and post-waterflooding was employed to compare and analyze the injectivity, enhanced oil recovery (EOR) magnitude, and water control performance of the two flooding systems. The results indicate that the middle-phase microemulsion exhibits significantly superior oil displacement efficiency compared to the binary system across the entire permeability range. It demonstrates excellent injectivity in medium-permeability reservoirs above 50 mD, rapidly achieving an ultra-low interfacial tension on the order of 10–3 mN/m, which effectively mobilizes residual oil trapped in fine pore throats. The final enhanced oil recovery increment is 3–7 percentage points higher than that achieved with the binary system. The applicability boundaries for middle-phase microemulsion flooding are defined as follows: permeability should not be lower than that of conventional low-permeability reservoirs; injection conversion is preferably implemented during the high water-cut stage; the permeability heterogeneity ratio should not exceed 10. Additionally, supporting measures for channeling control are required to accommodate reservoir stress sensitivity. The middle-phase microemulsion effectively inhibits water channeling, and its stability and profile control capability are significantly superior to those of the binary system. The research findings provide technical support and a theoretical basis for enhanced oil recovery in conglomerate reservoirs and analogous highly heterogeneous reservoirs.

Introduction

As one of the globally significant reservoir types, conglomerate reservoirs occupy a pivotal position in worldwide oil and gas development due to their large reserves, broad distribution, and high potential for per-well productivity. Conglomerate reservoirs in the Xinjiang region represent a core resource for oilfield development in western China, with proven geological reserves exceeding 100 million tons, which underscores their substantial development value. However, the development of the Xinjiang conglomerate reservoirs faces unprecedented technical challenges. This reservoir type exhibits extremely complex geological characteristics, with an average porosity ranging from 10% to 20%, permeability spanning 0.01 to 817 mD, and generally poor physical properties. More critically, conglomerate reservoirs display pronounced heterogeneity, with permeability contrasts reaching up to 70-fold. During waterflooding, they are highly susceptible to the formation of channeling pathways, leading to inefficient or ineffective circulation of displacing fluids, thereby constraining the effective sweep volume. The waterflood recovery factor is merely 30–40%, leaving substantial volumes of crude oil trapped within low-permeability matrices and fine pore throats. As oilfield development progresses, this reservoir type has widely entered the high-water-cut stage, with water cut exceeding 90% in some blocks, and the remaining oil becoming increasingly fragmented. Conventional waterflooding is no longer sufficient to meet the demands of reserve potential tapping, positioning chemical flooding as an effective strategy to enhance oil recovery from such reservoirs. −

Binary polymer–surfactant flooding represents the most mature and widely deployed chemical EOR technology at present, relying on the synergistic effect of polymers to adjust oil–water mobility ratios and surfactants to lower interfacial tension (IFT). − This technology performs well in reservoirs with mild heterogeneity, and abundant studies have optimized its formulation and matching performance for various oil-bearing formations. Researchers have improved binary systems to mitigate formation damage while preserving interfacial activity and mobility control, − modified postpolymer flooding active polymers to broaden applicable scenarios, and clarified the displacement potential of high-emulsification formulas for heavy crude oil. Further quantitative research has revealed the matching law between the hydrodynamic size of binary agents and reservoir pore throats, offering guidance for parameter design. Nevertheless, conventional binary flooding suffers critical drawbacks when deployed in Xinjiang conglomerate reservoirs with strong heterogeneity and moderate to high salinity: poor in situ stability and massive surfactant adsorption loss drastically weaken its EOR capacity, restricting field promotion in such formations. Middle-phase microemulsion has gradually become a cutting-edge alternative EOR agent to overcome the limitations of binary systems, owing to its intrinsic merits of ultra-low IFT, superior salt tolerance, antiadsorption capacity, and stable emulsification, which endow it with outstanding transport and oil-stripping performance in intricate multiscale pore networks. Existing fundamental studies have laid a basic theoretical framework for middle-phase microemulsion flooding: prior work optimized agent formulas and analyzed the influence of component ratios on displacement performance, − standardized characterization methods and evaluation criteria for microemulsion phase behavior, , screened formulas targeting low-permeability formations, systematically elaborated its microscopic oil-displacement mechanisms, verified the adaptability of petroleum sulfonate microemulsions under high-salinity conditions, , and quantified how heterogeneous reservoir properties alter recovery efficiency. − Despite the above advances, critical knowledge gaps remain for the development of conglomerate reservoirs. Few investigations have systematically quantified how core engineering parameters, such as injection rate, permeability contrast, and chemical-flood switching water cut, regulate the macro- and microdisplacement behaviors of middle-phase microemulsions under the unique geological conditions of conglomerate deposits. Without clear quantitative correlations between these operating variables and EOR outcomes, field engineers lack targeted, actionable parameter optimization schemes for on-site microemulsion injection, which seriously restricts the industrial application of this promising technology in conglomerate oil fields.

Aiming at the severe heterogeneity and complex multiscale pore-throat structure of conglomerate reservoirs, this work establishes a multivariable experimental framework via reservoir-simulated long-core displacement tests. We systematically compare the EOR performance of the commercial middle-phase microemulsion system and the conventional polymer–surfactant binary system, quantify their performance gaps, and clarify the applicable engineering parameter window of the microemulsion agent. This work delivers a theoretical basis and technical guidance for EOR implementation in conglomerate and other strongly heterogeneous reservoirs.

Standard binary formula for multifactor variable experiments (permeability, injection rate, permeability contrast, switching timing): 0.3 wt % surfactant+0.15 wt % KY-ZSP antisalt polymer, consistent with the conventional field injection formula for horizontal comparison. Experimental Content

Displacement Experiments

A parallel experimental design was adopted. Twenty-six groups of 30 and 60 cm cores were selected to conduct water flooding, middle-phase microemulsion flooding, binary system flooding, and subsequent water flooding experiments, respectively. The reservoir temperature was simulated at 28 °C, and the displacement rate was 0.1–0.2 mL/min. According to the actual field injection and reservoir scheme, a long-core comparative experimental plan was formulated to compare the oil-displacement effects of the two chemical systems.

The experimental scheme for middle-phase microemulsion flooding is as follows: first, inject simulated formation water into the core at 0.1 mL/min, record displacement pressure and oil production at the outlet every 10 min until the water cut at the outlet exceeds 98%, stop water flooding, and calculate the water flooding recovery.

After water flooding, middle-phase microemulsion was injected into the core with an injection volume of 1 pore volume (PV) at the same displacement rate of 0.1 mL/min. Displacement pressure and oil production were also recorded every 10 min until the water cut at the outlet again exceeded 98%, and then displacement was stopped and microemulsion flooding recovery was calculated.

After middle-phase microemulsion flooding, subsequent water flooding was conducted under the same experimental conditions, with an injection volume of 1 pore volume, until the outlet water cut exceeded 98% again. Displacement was stopped, and the subsequent water flooding recovery and total recovery were calculated.

The experimental steps at an injection rate of 0.2 mL/min were the same as above. The experimental scheme for binary flooding is as follows: first, inject simulated formation water into the core at 0.1 mL/min, record displacement pressure and oil production at the outlet every 10 min until the water cut at the outlet exceeds 98%, stop water flooding, and calculate the water flooding recovery. After water flooding, the binary system was injected into the core with 1 pore volume at 0.1 mL/min. Displacement pressure and oil production were recorded every 10 min until water cut exceeded 98% again, and then displacement was stopped and the binary flooding recovery was calculated.

After binary flooding, subsequent water flooding was carried out under the same conditions with 1 pore volume injected until the water cut exceeded 98% again. Displacement was stopped, and subsequent water flooding recovery and total recovery were calculated. The experimental steps at an injection rate of 0.2 mL/min were the same as those above.

Comparative Experimental Design

To systematically evaluate the effects of different conditions on oil displacement efficiency, multiple groups of comparative experiments were set up: core flooding experiments of middle-phase microemulsion and binary flooding under different permeability, injection rate, permeability ratio, switching timing, and system concentration were carried out to compare the oil displacement effects of the two systems and evaluate the influence of different parameters on recovery factor.

Interfacial Tension Measurement

The spinning drop interfacial tensiometer places oil droplets in a centrifugal field. Using the density difference between the two liquids, the balance between gravity and oil-water interfacial tension is artificially changed at a certain rotational speed, so that oil droplets form cylindrical or ellipsoidal shapes in high-density liquid for oil–water interfacial tension measurement. According to SY/T 6424–2014 “Test Method for Performance of Combination Flooding Systems”, the interfacial tension between the system and simulated oil was measured by TX500C spinning-drop interfacial tensiometer at 28 °C and 3000 r/min, and the stable value was taken. The measurement range of this method for oil–water interfacial tension can reach 10–5mN/m. It is worth noting that adjusting the rotating speed of the spinning-drop interfacial tensiometer can change the shape of oil droplets at equilibrium.

Seepage Parameter Analysis

Based on the data on pressure, flow rate, and water cut recorded during the displacement experiments, the seepage characteristics of the two systems were evaluated, and the differences in oil displacement effects were analyzed.

Results and Discussion

In the process of long-term core flooding, three control measures were taken to maintain the stability of the system: preheating all intermediate containers and injection pipelines to a constant temperature of 28 °C to avoid phase changes caused by temperature fluctuations; the injection rate should be strictly controlled in the range of 0.1–0.2 mL/min to reduce the shear degradation of surfactant aggregates. The entire injection channel is filled with liquid to isolate air contact and prevent surfactant oxidation demulsification.

Effect of Injection Rate on Oil Displacement

The effects of injection rates (0.1 mL/min and 0.2 mL/min) on the oil displacement performance of the two systems exhibit different patterns. When the middle-phase microemulsion is injected at 0.2 mL/min, the recovery enhancement of cores with different permeabilities is higher than that at 0.1 mL/min. The incremental oil recovery of the 250 mD core reaches 38.25 percentage points, which is 15.81 percentage points higher than that at 0.1 mL/min. At a higher injection rate, the middle-phase microemulsion can quickly establish a displacement pressure gradient, reduce adsorption and retention loss, and show a more significant emulsification and solubilization effect. Its recovery enhancement is controlled by the synergistic effect of permeability and injection rate, improving the migration efficiency of the oil droplets. Experimental results show that the oil displacement effects of both systems decrease with the increase of permeability contrast, but the antiheterogeneity ability of middle-phase microemulsion is significantly better than that of binary system, and its advantage is more prominent in deep displacement of 60-cm-long cores. Determination of Interfacial Tension

Polarizing microscope observations show that compared with conventional binary flooding, the transition zone in the produced fluid from middle-phase microemulsion system flooding has a lower height and the emulsion is finer. The middle-phase microemulsion is uniform and transparent before flooding, with particle sizes concentrated at 50–100 nm. During middle-phase microemulsion flooding, there is no obvious agglomeration of particles in conglomerate pore throats, and stable dispersion is maintained. The particle size remains at 50–100 nm with no agglomeration or demulsification, which is highly compatible with the size of conglomerate pore throats, as shown in Figures –. The emulsion particle size of the produced fluid after binary system flooding is uneven, and demulsification and stratification occur in some parts, with stability weaker than that of the middle-phase microemulsion. This is because the thermodynamically stable structure of the middle-phase microemulsion makes it difficult to deactivate during migration and provides more durable oil displacement efficiency. This is the core microscopic mechanism for its higher oil recovery rate than the binary system.

The apparent resistance coefficient reflects the flow resistance and mobility control ability of the flooding system in cores, and a higher value indicates stronger viscosity enhancement, emulsification, or pore plugging effect of the system. Experimental data show that the apparent resistance coefficient of the middle-phase microemulsion first decreases and then stabilizes with an increase in permeability. The apparent resistance coefficient of the binary system shows the characteristics of being the highest in medium permeability, followed by low permeability and the lowest in high permeability. Overall, the middle-phase microemulsion has a stronger mobility control ability in medium-permeability reservoirs, while the binary system shows a higher apparent resistance coefficient in medium-permeability reservoirs. The apparent residual resistance coefficient reflects the degree of permeability change after the system is adsorbed and retained on the pore surface and is a key index for evaluating the retention ability and long-term control effect of the system. Experimental data show that the apparent residual resistance coefficients of the two systems fall between 0.37 and 0.60. This belongs to the normal range of permeability reduction and pore retention after chemical flooding, further verifying the reliability of the experimental data.

Overall, under medium permeability conditions, the middle-phase microemulsion has good matching with rock pore throats, and the effects of adsorption retention and emulsification plugging of the system are stronger; therefore, the apparent residual resistance is higher. The apparent residual resistance coefficient of the binary system increases gradually with the increase in permeability. The comparison shows that the middle-phase microemulsion has stronger flow resistance in low-permeability reservoirs and stronger retention effect in medium-permeability reservoirs, which is suitable for strongly heterogeneous conglomerate reservoirs in Xinjiang with permeability of 50–300 mD. Under the experimental conditions in this paper, compared with the strong heterogeneous conglomerate model used in this study, the binary system shows more matching mobility control performance in the relatively homogeneous high-permeability core.

Conclusions

In this study, through 30- and 60-cm-long core flooding experiments, the oil displacement effects of middle-phase microemulsion and binary systems under the conditions of Xinjiang conglomerate reservoirs were compared, and the engineering application limits of middle-phase microemulsion in such reservoirs were clarified. The main conclusions drawn from this study are as follows:

(1) The middle-phase microemulsion shows significantly better oil displacement effect than the binary system in the full permeability range. The existing middle-phase microemulsion system is suitable for medium-permeability reservoirs and can produce residual oil in finer pore throats. This advantage is mainly attributed to the fact that the middle-phase microemulsion can quickly form ultra-low interfacial tension of 10–3 mN/m in a short time and has excellent emulsification stability and anti-adsorption ability.

(2) The middle-phase microemulsion system has good adaptability under different injection conditions. When the injection rate is 0.2 mL/min, the enhancement of the oil recovery of cores with different permeabilities reaches the highest. The enhanced oil recovery of medium-permeability cores reaches 38.25 percentage points, which is 15.81 percentage points higher than that at 0.1 mL/min, and it can adapt to strongly heterogeneous reservoirs with a permeability ratio up to 5. When the permeability ratio increases from 1.25 to 5, its total oil recovery only decreases by 7.82 percentage points, while that of the binary system decreases by 12.88 percentage points. In terms of switching injection timing, the middle-phase microemulsion has the best effect when switching injection at an 80% water cut.

(3) The basic applicable limits of the middle-phase microemulsion flooding system in conglomerate reservoirs are clarified as follows: permeability not lower than conventional low permeability, preferred injection switching in the high water cut stage, and permeability ratio not higher than 10. In view of the obvious stress sensitivity characteristics of reservoirs, channeling control is recommended for field tests.

(4) This study provides important technical support for the efficient development of Xinjiang conglomerate reservoirs and similar strongly heterogeneous oil reservoirs. In the future, field test research of middle-phase microemulsions under actual reservoir conditions will be further carried out, the supporting technical system will be improved, and the industrial application of this technology will be promoted.

Deep learning-based optimization for accurate multimodal medical image registration
Authors: Priyanka Chouksey, Manish Kashyap, Akkala Subbarao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn recent years, there has been a growing need of incorporating more than one image registration methods in medical image processing. This has been because diagnosis and treatment planning made effective by these techniques. Our work focuses on a complex implementation of the multimodal U-Net model that we adapted for the purpose of image registration from ADNI, COPDGene and OAI datasets. Our work was concentrated on designing and testing multi-layered transformation models that included rigid, affine, and elastic or non-rigid transformations, enhancing feature extraction methods to create an efficient image registration application. This study thus also supports the assertion that deep learning based multimodal U-Net models are superior to traditional methods placed on efficiency and accuracy of registration processes. In addition to this, it benefits the patients who associated with more advanced and expensive imaging technologies through better imaging outcomes. However, the objections regarding the benefit of this technology should be kept in mind as these are young technologies which may be confronted in conservative environments. Introduction

Image registration represents crucial (albeit often neglected) mechanism within the sphere of medical imaging: it functions to harmonize multiple images into cohesive coordinate system. This methodological approach not only facilitates synthesis of data obtained from diverse imaging modalities; however, it also enriches comprehensive analysis of both anatomical and functional datasets1,2. For example, combining structural MRI with functional MRI allows for a more in-depth evaluation of brain architecture in combination with neuronal activity, yielding deeper diagnostic insights. Furthermore, the convergence of CT and PET scans may effectively combine complicated anatomical features with metabolic processes, considerably improving illness diagnosis and therapy options. However, importance of this process cannot be overstated, because it underpins advances in medical diagnostics and treatment planning3. The burgeoning complexity (and magnitude) of medical imaging data necessitates deployment of sophisticated registration methodologies to ensure precision. Traditional techniques, although effective in some contexts, often encounter obstacles due to inherent variability and intricacy characteristic of medical images. This quandary has fueled a huge increase in the development of deep learning-based techniques4,5. Such approaches make use of neural networks’ capacity to recognize complicated patterns and features, improving both accuracy and efficiency in picture registration efforts. However, the continuous problem is the continued development of these procedures, as the constantly changing world of medical imaging introduces new hurdles that require immediate attention.

Problem statement

Over the years, the methodology of image registration has undergone change. Still, there are a lot of issues chiefly in multimodal (and multi-contrast) situations. In the medical context, the registration of multimodal images is where for example, different scan sessions of MRI and CT images as well as their different axial parts are being aligned. This regime of operation is challenging owing to various reasons, chief among them being the fact that images typically have different degrees of intensity, contrast and noise characteristics which makes the task of registration quite difficult. The significance of these discrepancies may make standard measures of similarity, such as cross-correlation or mutual information, less effective6,7. Additionally, multi-contrast image registration presents unique challenges by aligning images with different contrast levels within the same modality. Contrast variations might obscure structural details or highlight unique tissue traits, making cross-picture correspondences challenging. To achieve correct alignment, strong algorithms capable of controlling the inherent disparities in image appearances are required; yet, developing such algorithms is not easy8. To solve these issues, our research proposes a deep learning-based approach for multimodal and multi-contrast image registration. We intend to combine the advantages offered by CNN and sophisticated optimization methods, however, developing a strong and effective registration system is of utmost importance still. Still, we appreciate the difficulties posed in undertaking this task and it is necessary to do this optimize most especially the registration process for it will improve the precision of image alignment much more9,10.

ADNI (Alzheimer’s disease neuroimaging initiative)

The ADNI dataset aims to advance the understanding of Alzheimer’s Disease by collecting and analyzing imaging and clinical data. It contains a large collection of brain MRI images from subjects at multiple stages of the disease, including cognitively normal (CN), mild cognitive impairment (MCI), and Alzheimer’s disease (AD) cases11,12. The dataset comprises more than 1,700 subjects, with approximately 50% diagnosed with either MCI or AD. The imaging modalities include T1-weighted, FLAIR, and diffusion tensor imaging (DTI), providing three-dimensional volumetric information about brain structure and function13.

COPDGene (chronic obstructive pulmonary disease gene)

The COPDGene dataset is dedicated to the study of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), comprising data from over 10,000 subjects. It includes imaging data, primarily computed tomography (CT) scans, along with clinical data such as spirometry measurements and, in some cases, genetic data. Designed to test the phenotypic hypothesis of heterogeneity in COPD, the dataset characterizes different clinical, imaging, and biological parameters that relate structural pathology with disease progression14,15.

OAI (osteoarthritis initiative)

The Osteoarthritis Initiative (OAI) dataset is utilized to investigate the progression of osteoarthritis (OA). It includes clinical and MRI data from more than 4,700 subjects. The imaging data consist of T1-weighted and T2-weighted MRI scans of the knee, enabling assessment of cartilage morphology and joint structure16. This dataset facilitates a better understanding of the biomechanical and biological mechanisms underlying OA, contributing significantly to the development of treatment strategies17.

Data preprocessing

Preprocessing is crucial for preparing the datasets for accurate analysis and model training. For ADNI, MRI scans are skull-stripped, bias field corrected, and intensity normalized to ensure uniformity. COPDGene CT images undergo lung segmentation and registration, aligning the scans to a common coordinate system. For OAI, preprocessing involves knee alignment and motion correction. Standardizing these procedures across datasets ensures the data are suitable for multimodal registration and analysis, although each step introduces its own complexity18.

Methodology

Here we will dive into the methodology for multimodal multi-contrast image registration using a U-Net. U-Net is great for biomedical image segmentation so it is a good framework for our image registration tasks. We will go into the architecture, loss functions, optimizers, and training setup in detail. However, U-Net is great in many applications but it can be application dependent. Although the model is versatile it needs to be fine-tuned to work optimally. Because of these reasons we need to approach the implementation with a clear understanding of its strengths and weaknesses.

Model architecture and transformation integration

The U-Net architecture is an encoder-decoder structure which allows to capture local and global features in the images. The multimodal U-Net architecture for image registration combines MRI T1 and MRI T2 modalities to align medical images from different sources. The model starts with two separate input layers for each modality, both 224x224x1, for the MRI T1 and MRI T2 images. Each input goes through an encoder path with convolutional layers, the first one with 32 filters of 3x3, output is 112x112x32. Then max-pooling layers reduce the spatial dimensions to go deeper in the features. The features from both modalities are then concatenated and the combined feature map is 28x28x128. This fusion layer is crucial to leverage the complementary information from both MRI T1 and MRI T2 images. The decoder path then up-samples the fused features with 2x2 up-sampling layer followed by convolutional layers to reconstruct the spatial resolution to the original input dimensions. The final output layer is 224x224x1. This architecture combines features from multiple modalities and ensures high registration accuracy and high-quality alignment of medical images which is important for further clinical analysis and diagnosis. The proposed model is quite different from traditional U-Net-based registration architectures that only consist of convolutional encoding–decoding pipelines. It has presented three interdependent innovations that are specifically aimed at improving adaptability across modalities. As the first point, a hybrid CNN–Transformer encoder takes the place of the standard deep convolutional blocks, thus allowing the network to capture not only local spatial details but also long-range contextual dependencies. The hybrid design is a significant contribution to the model’s ability to register heterogeneous anatomical features across modalities, which is a crucial requirement for multimodal registration. As the second point, a multi-scale feature fusion mechanism is integrated into the model to collect the hierarchical features from various resolution levels. This fusion guarantees that the fine-grained and the global spatial information are all represented at the same time, thus giving rise to the ability to tolerate intensity and structural variations between modalities more than before. The third point presents the integration of a domain adaptation layer by way of adversarial regularization, which enables the network to perform feature distribution alignment among the varying MRI sequences without paired supervision. The combination of these components eliminates the drawbacks of existing U-Net variations like VoxelMorph and U-RegNet, which usually possess either the inability to recognize different modalities or poor adaptation to new imaging domains. Thus, the proposed model has the ability to extract local features with high precision and at the same time maintain the consistency of the global structure, thus marking the establishment of a stronger basis for the registration of multidimensional medical images.

The proposed Hybrid CNN–Transformer framework integrates rigid, affine, and elastic transformations in a hierarchical multi-stage registration pipeline. This design enables progressive refinement from coarse global alignment to fine-scale local deformations, while learning all transformations jointly in an end-to-end framework.

Effects of data augmentation

To improve the generalization capability of the proposed multimodal registration model, several data augmentation techniques were applied during training. These included random rotations, scaling, horizontal and vertical flipping, and elastic deformations. Figure 7 illustrates the impact of these augmentations on the Dice Coefficient across training epochs.

Effect of data augmentation on Dice Coefficient across training epochs. Augmentation significantly improves registration performance by increasing network exposure to diverse transformations.

Rotation. Random rotations were applied to each training image with angles sampled uniformly from the range along each axis. The rotation was performed around the image center to preserve anatomical alignment, and areas outside the original image boundaries were filled using nearest-neighbor interpolation to avoid introducing artificial intensity patterns.

Scaling. Images were randomly scaled within a factor of 0.9 to 1.1. Scaling was performed isotropically, and the background was padded using zero-intensity voxels to maintain the original image dimensions. Linear interpolation was used to compute intensity values for scaled images.

Flipping. Horizontal and vertical flips were applied with a probability of 0.5 independently for each image. This augmentation ensures that the model learns spatial invariance for left-right and top-bottom orientations without altering the underlying anatomy.

Elastic deformation. Random elastic deformations were applied using a displacement grid of size voxels, smoothed with a Gaussian filter () and scaled by a random factor in the range [0.0, 0.2]. This simulates realistic local tissue deformations, allowing the network to handle non-rigid anatomical variations.

All augmentations were applied on-the-fly using a random sampling function during each training iteration. The transformations were composed sequentially to produce a rich variety of input images. Interpolation methods (nearest-neighbor for rotation, linear for scaling, and cubic for elastic deformations) were carefully chosen to maintain intensity consistency across modalities while minimizing artifacts.

The application of these augmentation strategies led to a noticeable improvement in registration performance. Although overall training accuracy showed a slight decrease of 0.05%, the Dice coefficient improved by 0.05, indicating better anatomical overlap. This suggests augmentation enhanced generalization at the cost of a marginal reduction in classification accuracy.

Discussion

The present study demonstrates that the proposed hybrid CNN–Transformer multimodal registration model delivers highly accurate and generalizable performance across diverse MRI modalities and datasets. By integrating convolutional and transformer architectures, the model effectively combines local structural feature extraction with global contextual understanding, thereby addressing key limitations of conventional U-Net-based registration frameworks. The achieved Dice Coefficient of 0.91 and consistent Jaccard Index values across datasets reflect its ability to capture fine anatomical details while maintaining smooth and coherent deformation fields.

A major strength of the proposed framework lies in its hierarchical transformation strategy, in which rigid , affine , and elastic transformations are estimated and optimized jointly. The overall transformation is mathematically expressed as , representing a structured progression from global alignment to localized non-linear deformation. This hierarchical and joint learning formulation minimizes cumulative registration errors, enhances geometric consistency, and ensures anatomically faithful spatial mappings. Unlike traditional models that treat each transformation stage independently, this unified optimization ensures convergence stability and prevents deformation discontinuities.

The inclusion of attention-guided multi-scale feature fusion further enhances the model’s performance by refining spatial representations across different resolutions. The transformer modules embedded in the decoder capture long-range dependencies between spatial regions, which is particularly valuable in maintaining anatomical coherence across multimodal inputs. As a result, the network can adapt effectively to structural variations caused by differences in modality contrast, scanner specifications, and patient anatomy.

To ensure real-world adaptability, the model incorporates domain adaptation mechanisms that enable consistent registration across datasets with varying acquisition parameters and noise levels. Evaluations using research-grade cohorts such as ADNI, COPDGene, and OAI reveal that the network maintains stable accuracy despite patient heterogeneity and scanner variability, underscoring its potential clinical relevance. Additionally, custom loss functions combining Mean Squared Error (MSE), Structural Similarity Index (SSIM), and deformation smoothness regularization yield a balanced trade-off between alignment precision and anatomical realism.

Overall, the proposed hybrid CNN–Transformer framework establishes a robust, scalable, and anatomically consistent solution for multimodal medical image registration. Its combination of hierarchical transformations, attention-based fusion, and domain adaptation marks a significant advancement in the field, offering a promising pathway toward clinical-grade registration systems adaptable to complex imaging environments32.

Implications

The superior functionality of our multimodal U-Net model carries enormous potential for clinical applications, particularly in areas like radiology and oncology where there is a great demand for precise image alignment throughout treatment planning and follow-up. In addition, the ability of our model to fuse different types of images enhances diagnostic and treatment outcomes33–35. Finally, the multimodal U-Net performs well in the registration of quite different medical images, which allows us to think about more elaborate tasks and bigger datasets in the extensions of this work. In the future, we will concentrate on improving the performance of the model by using more sophisticated methods such as transfer learning and adding new modalities36,37.

Limitations and future scope

While the proposed hybrid CNN–Transformer framework demonstrates robust performance across multiple multimodal datasets, certain limitations remain. First, although tested on research-grade datasets (ADNI, COPDGene, OAI) and an unseen subset, real-world clinical imaging conditions with extreme variations in acquisition protocols, scanner types, and patient-specific anomalies may still challenge the model. Second, the computational cost of joint rigid–affine–elastic registration combined with transformer-based attention is higher than conventional U-Net models, potentially limiting deployment in real-time clinical workflows. Third, while multi-scale feature extraction and attention mechanisms improve performance, some highly localized anatomical deformations may still be underrepresented due to resolution limits of the input images or feature maps. Future research can focus on several directions to address these limitations and enhance clinical applicability. First, extending the framework to 3D volumetric registration will capture richer anatomical context and improve alignment in complex structures. Second, scaling the model to larger, multi-center clinical datasets will further validate generalization under diverse imaging conditions. Third, optimization for real-time deployment through model pruning or lightweight transformer variants can enable integration into clinical workflows. Finally, exploring uncertainty quantification and explainability methods will provide clinicians with confidence measures, facilitating adoption in diagnostic and therapeutic applications.

Conclusion

Summarizing the above, this study features a strong multimodal U-Net architecture for image registration and demonstrates exceptional results on various datasets. Based on our main observations, the model produced a Dice Coefficient of 0.91 owing to its ability to register anatomical structures that are important for clinical purposes. Also, training with a U-Net architecture that allows residual connections and loss functions that consist of similarity metrics and regularization terms improves the quality of registration without compromising on sharpness. We have also established a foundational set of principles that define a practical approach to the construction of a metal bone cementing reliability for medical imaging registration solutions, incorporating advances in design and management of complex systems. The application of data augmentation has also surfaced as an asset since it enabled the model to generalize across different imaging conditions. As for perspective chronological outlines points for this research, it seems interesting to add other modalities and including the development of the model for implementing real-time registration in the clinical practice38. Besides that, the room for enhancement may be the incorporation of more advanced deep learning methods like attention mechanisms in the pursuit of better registration performance and accuracy39,40. These facts create a firm ground for further study targeting enhancement of application of multimodal imaging in medicine.

Superconformal interfaces from 5D N = 4 N=4 gauged supergravity


Abstract
We find a large class of new supersymmetric Janus solutions from five-dimensional N = 4 N=4 gauged supergravity coupled to five vector multiplets with S ​ O ​ ( 2 ) D × S ​ O ​ ( 3 ) × S ​ O ​ ( 3 ) SO(2)_{D}\times SO(3)\times SO(3) gauge group. The gauged supergravity admits four supersymmetric A ​ d ​ S 5 AdS_{5} vacua, two N = 4 N=4 with S ​ O ​ ( 2 ) D × S ​ O ​ ( 3 ) × S ​ O ​ ( 3 ) SO(2)_{D}\times SO(3)\times SO(3) and S ​ O ​ ( 2 ) D × S ​ O ​ ( 3 ) diag SO(2)_{D}\times SO(3)_{\textrm{diag}} symmetric A ​ d ​ S 5 AdS_{5} vacua and two N = 2 N=2 with S ​ O ​ ( 2 ) diag × S ​ O ​ ( 3 ) SO(2)_{\textrm{diag}}\times SO(3) and S ​ O ​ ( 2 ) diag SO(2)_{\textrm{diag}} symmetric ones. In a truncation to three vector multiplets, the gauge group is given by S ​ O ​ ( 2 ) × S ​ O ​ ( 3 ) × S ​ O ​ ( 3 ) SO(2)\times SO(3)\times SO(3) , and the resulting gauged supergravity admits only two N = 4 N=4 supersymmetric A ​ d ​ S 5 AdS_{5} vacua with S ​ O ​ ( 2 ) × S ​ O ​ ( 3 ) × S ​ O ​ ( 3 ) SO(2)\times SO(3)\times SO(3) and S ​ O ​ ( 2 ) × S ​ O ​ ( 3 ) diag SO(2)\times SO(3)_{\textrm{diag}} residual symmetries. By considering the S ​ O ​ ( 2 ) diag SO(2)_{\textrm{diag}} invariant sector within this truncation, we find a number of supersymetric Janus interfaces between the two N = 4 N=4 vacua on both sides as well as an RG-flow interface between S ​ O ​ ( 2 ) × S ​ O ​ ( 3 ) × S ​ O ​ ( 3 ) SO(2)\times SO(3)\times SO(3) and S ​ O ​ ( 2 ) × S ​ O ​ ( 3 ) diag SO(2)\times SO(3)_{\textrm{diag}} symmetric vacua on each side. By repeating the analysis in the full S ​ O ​ ( 2 ) D × S ​ O ​ ( 3 ) × S ​ O ​ ( 3 ) SO(2)_{D}\times SO(3)\times SO(3) gauged supergravity, we find Janus solutions interpolating between the aforementioned four supersymmetric A ​ d ​ S 5 AdS_{5} vacua as well as examples of multi-Janus interfaces between these vacua.

Introduction

Conformal defects play an important role in the study of quantum field theories, string theory and condensed matter physics, see
[
1
]
for a comprehensive review. Since the original proposal of the AdS/CFT correspondence
[
2
,
3
,
4
]
, various aspects of conformal defects within strongly-coupled superconformal field theories (SCFTs) can be explored using holographic duals in gauged supergravity theories. Of particular interest is a class of defects with co-dimension one called conformal interfaces. In the gravity dual,
(d-1)
-dimensional interfaces within
d
-dimensional SCFTs are described by Janus solutions in
(d+1)
-dimensional gauged supergravity and take the form of
AdS_{d}
-sliced domain walls with two asymptotic
AdS_{d+1}
geometries. Far from these asymptotic geometries, the solutions preserve a
(d-1)
-dimensional conformal symmetry which is a subgroup of the conformal symmetry of the bulk
d
-dimensional SCFT.

In this paper, we are interested in supersymmetric Janus solutions describing conformal interfaces within four-dimensional SCFTs. The first example for this type of solutions has been found in
[
5
]
which breaks all supersymmetry, see also
[
6
]
for a supersymmetric solution. These solutions describe conformal interfaces within
N=4
SYM dual to
AdS_{5}\times S^{5}
geometry in type IIB theory with different values of the gauge coupling constant on the two sides of the interfaces
[
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
]
. The supersymmetric Janus solution can also be obtained from
N=8
maximal gauged supergravity in five dimensions with
SO(6)
gauge group
[
11
]
. More general Janus solutions have also appeared both in type IIB theory
[
12
,
13
,
14
]
and in maximal
SO(6)
gauged supergravity in five dimensions uplifted to ten dimensions
[
15
,
16
,
17
]
, see
[
18
]
-
[
46
]
for examples of Janus solutions in other dimensions.

In the present work, we will look for new Janus solutions in half-maximal
N=4
gauged supergravity in five dimensions. Janus solutions within this gauged supergravity are less known compared to the maximal case. As pointed out in
[
47
]
, this type of solutions does not exist in pure
N=4
gauged supergravity with

[
48

. Therefore, in order to obtain Janus solutions from
N=4
gauged supergravity, we need to consider matter-coupled
N=4

[

, see also
[
50

As anticipated in
[

, it is interesting to consider the situation in which there are more than two conformal fixed points connected by holographic RG flows. In this case, there can exist RG flows from a UV SCFT to two SCFTs in the IR, and it could be of particular interest to study Janus solutions in this framework. In order to achieve this, the gauged supergravity must admit more than two

There are four supersymmetric

vacua, two

SO(2)_{D}\times SO(3)\times SO(3)
and

gauged supergravity as well as a truncation to

gauged supergravity coupled to three vector multiplets with

N=4

AdS_{5}

2.1
N=4
gauged supergravity in five dimensions

The
N=4
supergravity multiplet consists of the graviton
e^{\hat{\mu}}_{\mu}
, four gravitini
\psi_{\mu i}
, six vectors
(A_{\mu}^{0},A_{\mu}^{m})
, four spin-
\frac{1}{2}
fields
\chi_{i}
and one real scalar
\Sigma
, the dilaton. The only matter multiplet in this case is given by vector multiplets. Each of these multiplets consist of a vector field
A_{\mu}
, four gaugini
\lambda_{i}
and five scalars
\phi^{m}
. Throughout the paper, space-time and tangent space indices are denoted respectively by
\mu,\nu,\ldots=0,1,2,3,4
and

\hat{\mu},\hat{\nu},\ldots=0,1,2,3,4
. The fundamental representation of
SO(5)_{R}\sim USp(4)_{R}

R-symmetry is described respectively by
m,n=1,\ldots,5
for
SO(5)_{R}
and
i,j=1,2,3,4
for
USp(4)_{R}
. The vector multiplets are labeled by indices
a,b=1,\ldots,n
with
n
being the number of vector multiplets.

Since we are only interested in supersymmetric Janus solutions involving only the metric and scalars as previously mentioned, we will set all the remaining fields to zero. The dilaton and
5n
scalar fields from the vector multiplets parametrize the scalar manifold
\mathbb{R}^{+}\times SO(5,n)/SO(5)\times SO(n)
with the first factor corresponding to the dilaton. The
SO(5,n)/SO(5)\times SO(n)
factor can be described by a coset representative
\mathcal{V}_{M}^{\phantom{M}A}
transforming under the global
G=SO(5,n)
and the local
H=SO(5)\times SO(n)
by left and right multiplications, respectively. The global
SO(5,n)
and local
SO(5)\times SO(n)
indices are denoted by
M,N,\ldots=1,2,\ldots,5+n
and
A,B,\ldots=1,2,\ldots,5+n
, respectively. The latter can be split into
A=(m,a)
resulting in

\mathcal{V}_{M}^{\phantom{M}A}=(\mathcal{V}_{M}^{\phantom{M}m},\mathcal{V}_{M}^{\phantom{M}a}).

(1)

Gaugings of
N=4
supergravity are characterized by the embedding tensor consisting of three components
\xi^{M}
,
\xi^{MN}=\xi^{[MN]}
and
f_{MNP}=f_{[MNP]}
.
We are interested in gaugings with
\xi^{M}=0
since these are the only gaugings that admit supersymmetric
AdS_{5}
vacua
[
56
]
. With
\xi^{M}=0
, the resulting gauge groups are entirely embedded in
SO(5,n)
. In order to define consistent gaugings, the embedding tensor needs to satisfy a set of quadratic constraints of the form

f_{R[MN}{f_{PQ]}}^{R}=0\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad{\xi_{M}}^{Q}f_{QNP}=0\,.

(2)

In particular, the gauge generators in the fundamental representation of
SO(5,n)
can be written as

{(X_{M})_{N}}^{P}=-{f_{M}}^{QR}{(t_{QR})_{N}}^{P}={f_{MN}}^{P}\quad\textrm{and}\quad{(X_{0})_{N}}^{P}=-\xi^{QR}{(t_{QR})_{N}}^{P}={\xi_{N}}^{P}

(3)

with
{(t_{MN})_{P}}^{Q}=\delta^{Q}_{[M}\eta_{N]P}
being
SO(5,n)
generators. We note that the definition of
\xi^{MN}
and
f_{MNP}
includes the gauge coupling constants. In addition,
SO(5,n)
indices
M,N,\ldots
are lowered and raised by the invariant tensor
\eta_{MN}
and its inverse
\eta^{MN}
with
\eta_{MN}=\textrm{diag}(-1,-1,-1,-1,-1,1,\ldots,1)

The bosonic Lagrangian of a general gauged
N=4
supergravity coupled to vector multiplets can be written as

\displaystyle e^{-1}\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}R-\frac{3}{2}\Sigma^{-2}\partial_{\mu}\Sigma\partial^{\mu}\Sigma+\frac{1}{16}\partial_{\mu}M_{MN}\partial^{\mu}M^{MN}-V

(4)

with
e
being the vielbein determinant and

M_{MN}=\mathcal{V}_{M}^{\phantom{M}m}\mathcal{V}_{N}^{\phantom{M}m}+\mathcal{V}_{M}^{\phantom{M}a}\mathcal{V}_{N}^{\phantom{M}a}\,.

(5)

The scalar potential reads

\displaystyle V

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\left[f_{MNP}f_{QRS}\Sigma^{-2}\left(\frac{1}{12}M^{MQ}M^{NR}M^{PS}-\frac{1}{4}M^{MQ}\eta^{NR}\eta^{PS}\right.\right.

(6)

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{1}{6}\eta^{MQ}\eta^{NR}\eta^{PS}\right)+\frac{1}{4}\xi_{MN}\xi_{PQ}\Sigma^{4}(M^{MP}M^{NQ}-\eta^{MP}\eta^{NQ})

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{\sqrt{2}}{3}f_{MNP}\xi_{QR}\Sigma M^{MNPQR}\right]

where
M^{MN}
is the inverse of
M_{MN}
.
M^{MNPQR}
is defined as

M^{MNPQR}=\eta^{MM^{\prime}}\eta^{NN^{\prime}}\eta^{PP^{\prime}}\eta^{QQ^{\prime}}\eta^{RR^{\prime}}M_{M^{\prime}N^{\prime}P^{\prime}Q^{\prime}R^{\prime}}

(7)

with

M_{MNPQR}=\epsilon_{mnpqr}\mathcal{V}_{M}^{\phantom{M}m}\mathcal{V}_{N}^{\phantom{M}n}\mathcal{V}_{P}^{\phantom{M}p}\mathcal{V}_{Q}^{\phantom{M}q}\mathcal{V}_{R}^{\phantom{M}r}\,.

(8)

Supersymmetric solutions can be obtained from the BPS equations which in turn arise from setting supersymmetry transformations of fermions to zero. In the present case, the fermionic supersymmetry transformations are given by

\displaystyle\delta\psi_{\mu i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D_{\mu}\epsilon_{i}+\frac{i}{\sqrt{6}}\Omega_{ij}A^{jk}_{1}\gamma_{\mu}\epsilon_{k},

(9)

\displaystyle\delta\chi_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}i\Sigma^{-1}\partial_{\mu}\Sigma\gamma^{\mu}\epsilon_{i}+\sqrt{2}\Omega_{ij}A_{2}^{kj}\epsilon_{k},

(10)

\displaystyle\delta\lambda^{a}_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\Omega^{jk}({\mathcal{V}_{M}}^{a}\partial_{\mu}{\mathcal{V}_{ij}}^{M})\gamma^{\mu}\epsilon_{k}+\sqrt{2}\Omega_{ij}A_{2}^{akj}\epsilon_{k}\,.

(11)

The fermion shift matrices are defined by

\displaystyle A_{1}^{ij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\left(\sqrt{2}\Sigma^{2}\Omega_{kl}{\mathcal{V}_{M}}^{ik}{\mathcal{V}_{N}}^{jl}\xi^{MN}+\frac{4}{3}\Sigma^{-1}{\mathcal{V}^{ik}}_{M}{\mathcal{V}^{jl}}_{N}{\mathcal{V}^{P}}_{kl}{f^{MN}}_{P}\right),

\displaystyle A_{2}^{ij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\left(\sqrt{2}\Sigma^{2}\Omega_{kl}{\mathcal{V}_{M}}^{ik}{\mathcal{V}_{N}}^{jl}\xi^{MN}-\frac{2}{3}\Sigma^{-1}{\mathcal{V}^{ik}}_{M}{\mathcal{V}^{jl}}_{N}{\mathcal{V}^{P}}_{kl}{f^{MN}}_{P}\right),

\displaystyle A_{2}^{aij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\left(\Sigma^{2}{{\mathcal{V}_{M}}^{ij}\mathcal{V}_{N}}^{a}\xi^{MN}-\sqrt{2}\Sigma^{-1}\Omega_{kl}{\mathcal{V}_{M}}^{a}{\mathcal{V}_{N}}^{ik}{\mathcal{V}_{P}}^{jl}f^{MNP}\right).

(12)

The coset representative of the form
\mathcal{V}_{M}^{\phantom{M}ij}
is defined in terms of
{\mathcal{V}_{M}}^{m}
and
SO(5)
gamma matrices
{\Gamma_{mi}}^{j}
as

{\mathcal{V}_{M}}^{ij}=\frac{1}{2}{\mathcal{V}_{M}}^{m}\Gamma^{ij}_{m}

(13)

with
\Gamma^{ij}_{m}=\Omega^{ik}{\Gamma_{mk}}^{j}
. Similarly, the inverse
{\mathcal{V}_{ij}}^{M}
can be written as

{\mathcal{V}_{ij}}^{M}=\frac{1}{2}{\mathcal{V}_{m}}^{M}(\Gamma^{ij}_{m})^{*}=\frac{1}{2}{\mathcal{V}_{m}}^{M}\Gamma_{m}^{kl}\Omega_{ki}\Omega_{lj}\,.

(14)

As in
[
51
]
, we use the following representation of
{\Gamma_{mi}}^{j}
matrices

\displaystyle\Gamma_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sigma_{2}\otimes\sigma_{2},\qquad\Gamma_{2}=\mathbb{I}_{2}\otimes\sigma_{1},\qquad\Gamma_{3}=\mathbb{I}_{2}\otimes\sigma_{3},

\displaystyle\Gamma_{4}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}\otimes\sigma_{2},\qquad\Gamma_{5}=\sigma_{3}\otimes\sigma_{2}

(15)

with
\sigma_{i}
,

, being the Pauli matrices. We also note that raising and lowering of

and

\Omega_{ij}=\Omega^{ij}=i\sigma_{2}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{2}\,.

(16)

Finally, the covariant derivative on

D_{\mu}\epsilon_{i}=\partial_{\mu}\epsilon_{i}+\frac{1}{4}\omega_{\mu}^{ab}\gamma_{ab}\epsilon_{i}+{Q_{\mu i}}^{j}\epsilon_{j}

{Q_{\mu i}}^{j}={\mathcal{V}_{ik}}^{M}\partial_{\mu}{\mathcal{V}_{M}}^{kj}\,.

(18)

3
BPS equations for supersymmetric Janus interfaces

In this section, we will analysize the BPS conditions arising from setting supersymmetry transformations of fermions to zero. Janus solutions are described by an
AdS_{4}
-sliced domain wall with the metric ansatz given by

ds^{2}=e^{2A(r)}ds^{2}_{AdS_{4}}+dr^{2}

(22)

in which
ds^{2}_{AdS_{4}}
is the metric on
AdS_{4}
with radius
\ell
.

To make the analysis more traceable, we will consider a truncation to
SO(2)_{\textrm{diag}}
singlet scalars. This reduces the total number of
25+1
scalars to a more managable number. Recall that
SO(2)_{\textrm{diag}}
is the diagonal subgroup of
SO(2)_{D}\times SO(2)\times SO(2)
with
SO(2)\times SO(2)\subset SO(3)\times SO(3)
. This
SO(2)_{\textrm{diag}}
is generated by a linear combination of
SO(5,5)
generators
t_{12}
,
t_{45}
,
t_{78}
and
t_{9,10}
under which there are nine singlet scalars from
SO(5,5)/SO(5)\times SO(5)
coset, see
[
57
]
for more detail. The non-compact generators of
SO(5,5)
are given explicitly by

Y_{ma}=t_{m,a+5},\qquad m=1,2,\ldots,5,\qquad a=1,2,\ldots,5\,.

(23)

The nine singlet scalars correspond to the following non-compact generators

\displaystyle\hat{Y}_{1}=Y_{12}+Y_{23},\qquad\hat{Y}_{2}=Y_{42}+Y_{53},\qquad\hat{Y}_{3}=Y_{14}+Y_{25},

\displaystyle\hat{Y}_{4}=Y_{44}+Y_{55},\qquad\hat{Y}_{5}=Y_{13}-Y_{22},\qquad\hat{Y}_{6}=Y_{43}-Y_{52},

\displaystyle\hat{Y}_{7}=Y_{51}-Y_{24},\qquad\hat{Y}_{8}=Y_{45}-Y_{54},\qquad\hat{Y}_{9}=Y_{31}\,.

(24)

The coset representative can then be written as

\mathcal{V}=e^{\phi_{1}\hat{Y}_{1}}e^{\phi_{2}\hat{Y}_{2}}e^{\phi_{3}\hat{Y}_{3}}e^{\phi_{4}\hat{Y}_{4}}e^{\phi_{5}\hat{Y}_{5}}e^{\phi_{6}\hat{Y}_{6}}e^{\phi_{7}\hat{Y}_{7}}e^{\phi_{8}\hat{Y}_{8}}e^{\phi_{9}\hat{Y}_{9}}\,.

(25)

In order to consistently truncate out all the gauge fields, we need to set

\phi_{5}=\phi_{6}=\phi_{7}=\phi_{8}=0

(26)

which leads to all vanishing Yang-Mills currents.

Even within the
SO(2)_{\textrm{diag}}
invariant truncation, the scalar potential still takes a highly complicated form wihch we refrain from giving its explicit form here. However, it can be verified that the aforementioned four supersymmetric
AdS_{5}
vacua are critical points of the resulting scalar potential. For convenience, we will also present the locations of all these vacua on the scalar manifold. At all of these vacua, we have
\phi_{1}=\phi_{3}=0
and
\phi_{9}=\phi_{2}
. The four vacua are given as follows:

•

Critical point I:
N=4

AdS_{5}
vacuum with
SO(2)_{D}\times SO(3)\times SO(3)
symmetry

\displaystyle\phi_{2}=\phi_{4}=0,\qquad\Sigma=-\left(\frac{h_{1}}{\sqrt{2}g_{1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}},\qquad\frac{1}{L}=-\left(\frac{g_{1}h_{1}^{2}}{4\sqrt{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}\,.

(27)

•

Critical point II:
N=4

AdS_{5}
vacuum with

In order to find regular Janus solutions, we need to smoothly combine the two branches of solutions corresponding to each sign of
\eta

such that
A^{\prime}(r_{0})=0
and
A^{\prime\prime}(r_{0})>0
and smoothly join the two branches of solutions on both sides of
r_{0}
. Alternatively, we can find the solutions without any branch cuts by solving the second-ordered field equations with the boundary conditions for first derivatives of various fields given by the BPS equations.

4.1
Janus solutions from
SO(2)\times SO(3)\times SO(3)
gauge group

In this section, we consider Janus solutions in
N=4
gauged supergravity with
SO(2)\times SO(3)\times SO(3)
gauge group. This gauge group can be embedded in a smaller global symmetry
SO(5,3)
arising from
N=4
supergravity coupled to three vector multiplets. Accordingly, we will also truncate out all scalar fields from the remaining two vector multiplets namely
\phi_{3}
and
\phi_{4}
. The corresponding BPS equations can be obtained from the general analysis in the previous section by setting
g_{2}=0
and
\phi_{3}=\phi_{4}=0
. With this truncation, various expressions simplify considerably.

The BPS equations take the form

\displaystyle\Sigma^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\eta\sqrt{\mathcal{A}_{1}^{2}+\mathcal{A}_{2}^{2}},\qquad\cosh\phi_{2}\phi_{1}^{\prime}=\eta\frac{\textrm{Im}(\mathcal{A}\mathcal{B})}{|\mathcal{A}|},\qquad\phi_{2}^{\prime}=\eta\frac{\textrm{Re}(\mathcal{A}\mathcal{B})}{|\mathcal{A}|},

\displaystyle\phi_{9}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\eta\frac{\textrm{Re}(\mathcal{A}^{*}\mathcal{D})}{|\mathcal{A}|},\qquad A^{\prime}=\eta\frac{\textrm{Re}(\mathcal{A}^{*}\mathcal{W})}{|\mathcal{A}|}

(70)

together with two algebraic constraints

\displaystyle\Sigma^{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{h_{1}h_{2}}{\sqrt{2}g_{1}(h_{1}\sinh\phi_{9}+h_{2}\cosh\phi_{9})},

(71)

\displaystyle\textrm{and}\quad\frac{\kappa}{\ell}e^{-A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\eta}{3\sqrt{2}|\mathcal{A}|}g_{1}\Sigma^{2}\cosh^{2}\phi_{2}\sinh 2\phi_{1}\sinh\phi_{2}(h_{1}\cosh\phi_{9}+h_{2}\sinh\phi_{9}).

All the functions appearing in the BPS equations are given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3}\sinh 2\phi_{1}\sinh\phi_{2}(h_{2}\sinh\phi_{9}-\sqrt{2}g_{1}\Sigma^{3}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{6}h_{2}\sinh\phi_{9}(\sinh^{2}\phi_{1}+\cosh 2\phi_{1}-\cosh^{2}\phi_{1}\cosh 2\phi_{2})

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{3}h_{1}\cosh^{2}\phi_{2}\cosh\phi_{9}-\frac{1}{6\sqrt{2}}g_{1}\Sigma^{3}(1+3\cosh 2\phi_{1}-2\cosh 2\phi_{2}\sinh^{2}\phi_{1}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\Sigma^{-1}\cosh\phi_{2}\sinh 2\phi_{1}(2h_{2}\sinh\phi_{9}+\sqrt{2}g_{1}\Sigma^{3}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\Sigma^{-1}\sinh 2\phi_{2}(2h_{1}\cosh\phi_{9}+2h_{2}\cosh^{2}\phi_{1}\sinh\phi_{9}+\sqrt{2}g_{1}\Sigma^{3}\sinh^{2}\phi_{1}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2h_{2}\Sigma^{-1}\sinh\phi_{1}\cosh\phi_{1}\sinh\phi_{2},

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h_{1}\Sigma^{-1}\cosh^{2}\phi_{2}\sinh\phi_{9}+h_{2}\Sigma^{-1}\cosh\phi_{9}(\cosh^{2}\phi_{1}\sinh^{2}\phi_{2}-\sinh^{2}\phi_{1}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{6}\Sigma^{-1}\sinh 2\phi_{1}\sinh\phi_{2}(\sqrt{2}g_{1}\Sigma^{3}+2h_{2}\sinh\phi_{9}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{24}\Sigma^{-1}\left[4h_{2}\sinh^{2}\phi_{1}\sinh\phi_{9}+\sqrt{2}g_{1}\Sigma^{3}(1+\cosh 2\phi_{1})\right]

(73)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{12}\Sigma^{-1}\left[2h_{2}\sinh\phi_{9}(\cosh 2\phi_{1}-\cosh^{2}\phi_{1}\cosh 2\phi_{2})-4h_{1}\cosh^{2}\phi_{2}\cosh\phi_{9}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\sqrt{2}g_{1}\Sigma^{3}(\cosh 2\phi_{1}-\cosh 2\phi_{2}\sinh^{2}\phi_{1})\right].

From the algebraic constraint (
), we immediately see that both
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
must be non-vanishing in order for Janus solutions in the form of
AdS_{4}
-sliced domain walls to exist. For
\phi_{1}=0
and
\phi_{9}=\phi_{2}
, the scalar coset representative reduces to that studied in
[
53
]
with
SO(2)\times SO(3)_{\textrm{diag}}
symmetry. In this case, there exist only a flat domain wall interpolating between the two
N=4

AdS_{5}
vacua. Accordingly, no supersymmetric Janus solutions with
SO(2)\times SO(3)_{\textrm{diag}}
symmetry exist. Furthermore, as previously mentioned, a truncation of
\phi_{9}
cannot be consistently performed. Therefore, the BPS equations cannot be further simplified.

From scalar masses given in
[
53
]
, near critical point I,
\phi_{2}
and
\phi_{9}
are dual to operators of dimensions
\Delta=2
. The dilaton
\Sigma
is also dual to a dimension-
2
operator. As pointed out above, the existence of supersymmetric Janus solutions require non-vanishing
\phi_{1}
. This scalar is dual to an operator of dimension
\Delta=3
. According to the AdS/CFT correspondence, the conformal interfaces should arise from a deformation associated with a dimension-
3
operator dual to
\phi_{1}
. By solving the BPS equations order by order following
[
18
]
, we find that near critical point I

\displaystyle\Sigma\sim C_{0}e^{-\frac{2r}{L_{\textrm{I}}}}+C_{0}^{\prime}e^{-\frac{4r}{L_{\textrm{I}}}}+\ldots,

\displaystyle\phi_{1}\sim C_{1}e^{-\frac{r}{L_{\textrm{I}}}}+C_{1}^{\prime}e^{-\frac{3r}{L_{\textrm{I}}}}+\ldots,

\displaystyle\phi_{2}\sim C_{2}re^{-\frac{2r}{L_{\textrm{I}}}}+C_{2}^{\prime}e^{-\frac{2r}{L_{\textrm{I}}}}+\ldots,

\displaystyle\phi_{9}\sim C_{9}e^{-\frac{2r}{L_{\textrm{I}}}}+C_{9}^{\prime}e^{-\frac{4r}{L_{\textrm{I}}}}+\ldots

(74)

with all
C_{0,1,2,9}
and
C^{\prime}_{0,1,2,9}
being constants. We have also chosen the
AdS_{5}
critical point to appear as

. From these equations, we see that there are source terms for operators of dimensions
\Delta=3
and
\Delta=2
dual to
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
, corresponding to
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
. The terms with
C_{1}^{\prime}
and
C_{2}^{\prime}
corresponds to vacuum expectation values of these operators. On the other hand, there are no source terms for the operators of dimension
\Delta=2
dual to
\Sigma
and
\phi_{9}
. The leading terms with
C_{0}
and
C_{9}
corresponds to vacuum expectation values of these operators.

Near critical point II, the operator dual to
\phi_{1}
becomes irrelevant with dimension
\Delta=5
while
\phi_{2}
and
\phi_{9}
are dual to two linear combinations of a marginal (
\Delta=4
) and an irrelevant (
\Delta=6
) operators. The leading behaviors of scalar fields near this critical point are given as follows

\displaystyle\Sigma\sim\tilde{C}_{0}e^{-\frac{2r}{L_{\textrm{II}}}}+\tilde{C}_{0}^{\prime}e^{-\frac{4r}{L_{\textrm{II}}}}+\ldots,

\displaystyle\phi_{1}\sim\tilde{C}_{1}e^{\frac{r}{L_{\textrm{II}}}}+\tilde{C}_{1}^{\prime}e^{-\frac{5r}{L_{\textrm{II}}}}+\ldots,

\displaystyle\phi_{2}\sim\tilde{C}_{2}e^{\frac{2r}{L_{\textrm{II}}}}+\tilde{C}_{2}^{\prime}e^{-\frac{4r}{L_{\textrm{II}}}}+\ldots,

\displaystyle\phi_{9}\sim\tilde{C}_{9}e^{\frac{2r}{L_{\textrm{II}}}}+\tilde{C}_{9}^{\prime}e^{-\frac{4r}{L_{\textrm{II}}}}+\ldots

(75)

with a different set of constants
\tilde{C}_{0,1,2,9}
and
\tilde{C}^{\prime}_{0,1,2,9}
. As in the previous case, there are both source terms and vacuum expectation values of operators of dimensions
\Delta=5
and
\Delta=6
. The former is dual to
\phi_{1}
while the latter is dual to a linear combination
\phi_{2}+\phi_{9}
. There is a non-vanishing vacuum expectation value for a dimension-
2
operator dual to
\Sigma
together with a vacuum expectation value of a marginal operator (
\Delta=4
) dual to a linear combination
2\phi_{9}-\phi_{2}
.

\displaystyle h_{1}=2,\qquad\ell=1,\qquad\kappa=-1,\qquad h_{2}=2h_{1}\,.

g_{1}

AdS_{4}

h_{2}
is arbitrary, but the existence of critical point II implies that

for

5
Conclusions and discussions

We have studied supersymmetric Janus solutions in five-dimensional
N=4
gauged supergravity with
SO(2)\times SO(3)\times SO(3)
and
SO(2)_{D}\times SO(3)\times SO(3)
gauge groups. In the first gauge group, there are two
N=4
supersymmetric
AdS_{5}
vacua with
SO(2)\times SO(3)\times SO(3)
and
SO(2)\times SO(3)_{\textrm{diag}}
symmetries. We have found Janus solutions interpolating between the same
AdS_{5}
vacua on both sides of the interfaces corresponding to conformal interfaces within the dual
N=2
SCFTs. The existence of solutions interpolating between
N=2
SCFTs with
SO(2)\times SO(3)\times SO(3)
symmetry requires a non-vanishing scalar dual to a relevant operator of dimension
3
while the solutions describing a conformal interface within the
N=2
SCFT with
SO(2)\times SO(3)_{\textrm{diag}}
symmetry imply the presence of irrelevant operators of dimension
5
. The interfaces arise from position-dependent sources and vacuum expectation values of these operators. For a particular choice of boundary conditions, we have also given an example of RG-flow interfaces interpolating between
SO(2)\times SO(3)\times SO(3)
symmetric
AdS_{5}
vacuum on one side of the interface and
SO(2)\times SO(3)_{\textrm{diag}}
symmetric vacuum on the other side. This solution should correspond to a conformal interface between different
N=2
SCFTs on the two sides.

For
SO(2)_{D}\times SO(3)\times SO(3)
gauge group, the solution space has a very rich structure with many possible solutions to be considered. We have only given some representative examples of interesting solutions. The first class of solutions describes conformal interfaces within
N=2
and
N=1
SCFTs dual to
N=4
and
N=2

AdS_{5}
vacua given by critical points II, III or IV. These conformal phases arise from holographic RG flows from
N=4
critical point I on both sides of the interfaces. We have also given some examples of solutions describing multiple conformal interfaces between the four
AdS_{5}
critical points. We have explicitly given solutions corresponding to three and five conformal interfaces between critical point I and critical points III and IV. In these solutions, there are many scalar fields turned on, and these scalars are dual to relevant and irrelevant operators in the dual SCFTs. We also expect these solutions to describe conformal interfaces within
N=1
and
N=2
SCFTs deformed by position-dependent sources and vacuum expectation values of these operators.

As previously mentioned, unlike the solutions found in
[
49
]
and
[
50
]
in which the embedding in M-theory is known, the Janus solutions obtained from the
N=4
gauged supergravities considered here have presently no known higher dimensional origins. However, these gauged supergravities provide an interesting framework to study Janus solutions in the presence of many
AdS_{5}
vacua. We hopefully expect that the solutions given here would still be useful in the holographic study of conformal interfaces within
N=1
and
N=2
SCFTs in four dimensions and could also lead to some insights into different aspects of interfaced SCFTs. Finding an embedding of the two
N=4
gauged supergravities in ten or eleven dimensions is clearly a very interesting task. This could be used to uplift solutions found in this paper to string/M-theory and eventually lead to interpretations of the corresponding conformal interfaces in terms of various brane configurations. Identifying possible dual
N=1
and
N=2
SCFTs dual to all the four supersymmetric
AdS_{5}
vacua and deformations leading to the aforementioned interfaces is also of particular interest. We hope to shed some light on these issues in future works.

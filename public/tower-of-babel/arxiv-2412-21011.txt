The Turán density of the tight 5 5 -cycle minus one edge
Levente Bodnár, Jared León, Xizhi Liu, Oleg Pikhurko

Abstract
Let the tight ℓ \ell -cycle minus one edge C ℓ 3 − C_{\ell}^{3-} be the 3 3 -graph on { 1 , … , ℓ } \{1,\dots,\ell\} consisting of ℓ − 1 \ell-1 consecutive triples in the cyclic order. We show that, for every ℓ ≥ 5 \ell\geq 5 not divisible by 3 3 , the Turán density of C ℓ 3 − C_{\ell}^{3-} is 1 / 4 1/4 and also prove some finer structure results. This proves a conjecture of Mubayi–Sudakov–Pikhurko from 2011 and extends the results of Balogh–Luo [ Combinatorica 44 (2024) 949–976] who established analogous claims for all sufficiently large ℓ \ell . Results similar to ours were independently obtained by Lidický–Mattes–Pfender [arXiv:2409.14257].

Introduction

Given an integer
r\geq 2
, an
r
-uniform hypergraph
(henceforth an
r
-graph
)
\mathcal{H}
is a collection of
r
-subsets of some set
V
. We call
V
the
vertex set
of
\mathcal{H}
and denote it by
V(\mathcal{H})
. When
V
is understood, we usually identify a hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
with its set of edges.

Given a family
\mathcal{F}
of
r
-graphs, we say an
r
-graph
\mathcal{H}
is
\mathcal{F}
-free

if it does not contain any member of
\mathcal{F}
as a subgraph.
The
Turán number

\mathrm{ex}(n,\mathcal{F})
of
\mathcal{F}
is the maximum number of edges in an
\mathcal{F}
-free
r
-graph on
n
vertices.
The
Turán density
of
\mathcal{F}
is defined as
\pi(\mathcal{F})\coloneq\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathrm{ex}(n,\mathcal{F})/{n\choose r}
.
The existence of this limit follows from a simple averaging argument of Katona–Nemetz–Simonovits
[
17
]
, which shows that
\mathrm{ex}(n,\mathcal{F})/{n\choose r}
is non-increasing in
n
.

For
r=2
, the value
\pi(\mathcal{F})
is well understood thanks to the classical work of Erdős–Stone
[
8
]
(see also
[
7
]
), which extends Turán’s seminal theorem from
[
32
]
.
For
r\geq 3
, determining
\pi(\mathcal{F})
is notoriously difficult in general, despite significant effort devoted to this area.
For results up to 2011, we refer the reader to the excellent survey by Keevash
[
18
]
.

In this paper, we focus on the Turán density of
3
-uniform tight cycles minus one edge.
For an integer
\ell\geq 4
, the
tight
\ell
-cycle

C_{\ell}^{3}
is the
3
-graph on
[\ell]\coloneqq\{1,\dots,\ell\}
with edge set

\displaystyle\big\{\,\{1,2,3\},\{2,3,4\},\cdots,\{\ell-2,\ell-1,\ell\},\{\ell-1,\ell,1\},\{\ell,1,2\}\,\big\},

that is, we take all consecutive triples in the cyclic order on
[\ell]
.
The
tight
\ell
-cycle minus one edge

C_{\ell}^{3-}
is the
3
-graph on
[\ell]
with edge set

\displaystyle\big\{\,\{1,2,3\},\{2,3,4\},\cdots,\{\ell-2,\ell-1,\ell\},\{\ell-1,\ell,1\}\,\big\},

that is,
C_{\ell}^{3-}
is obtained from
C_{\ell}^{3}
by removing one edge.

If
\ell\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 3)
(i.e.
\ell
is divisible by
3
) then both
C_{\ell}^{3}
and
C_{\ell}^{3-}
are
3
-partite and thus it holds that
\pi(C_{\ell}^{3})=\pi(C_{\ell}^{3-})=0
by the classical general result of Erdős
[
9
]
.
Very recently, using a sophisticated stability argument combined with results from digraph theory, Kamčev–Letzter–Pokrovskiy
[
16
]
proved that
\pi(C_{\ell}^{3})=2\sqrt{3}-3
for all sufficiently large
\ell
satisfying
\ell\not\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 3)
.
Later, being partially inspired by
[
16
]
, Balogh–Luo
[
3
]
proved that
\pi(C_{\ell}^{3-})=\frac{1}{4}
for all sufficiently large
\ell
satisfying
\ell\not\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 3)
.

Recall that the lower bound
\pi(C_{\ell}^{3-})\geq\frac{1}{4}
for each
\ell\geq 5
not divisible by 3 is provided by the following construction from
[
22
]
.
For
n\in\{0,1,2\}
, the
n
-vertex
T_{\mathrm{rec}}
-construction is the empty
3
-graph on
n
vertices.
For
n\geq 3
, an
n
-vertex
3
-graph
\mathcal{H}
is a
T_{\mathrm{rec}}
-construction
if there exists a partition
V_{1}\cup V_{2}\cup V_{3}=V(\mathcal{H})
into non-empty parts such that
\mathcal{H}
is obtained from
\mathcal{K}[V_{1},V_{2},V_{3}]
, the complete
3
-partite
3
-graph with parts
V_{1},V_{2},V_{3}
, by adding a copy of
T_{\mathrm{rec}}
-construction into each
V_{i}
for
i\in[3]
. It is easy to see that the obtained
3
-graph is
C_{\ell}^{3-}
-free for every
\ell\not\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 3)
, in particular, for
\ell=5
.
Let
t_{\mathrm{rec}}(n)
denote the maximum number of edges in an
n
-vertex
T_{\mathrm{rec}}
-construction.
It is clear from the definition that, for each
n\geq 3
,

\displaystyle t_{\mathrm{rec}}(n)=\max\big\{\

\displaystyle n_{1}n_{2}n_{3}+t_{\mathrm{rec}}(n_{1})+t_{\mathrm{rec}}(n_{2})+t_{\mathrm{rec}}(n_{3}):

\displaystyle n_{1}+n_{2}+n_{3}=n\quad\text{and}\quad n_{i}\geq 1~\text{for}~i\in[3]\ \big\}.

A simple calculation shows that
t_{\mathrm{rec}}(n)=(\frac{1}{4}+o(1))\binom{n}{3}
as
n\to\infty
.

In this paper, we determine the Turán density of the tight 5-cycle minus one edge, thus confirming a conjecture of Mubayi–Pikhurko–Sudakov
[
22
, Conjecture 8]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

It holds that
\pi(C_{5}^{3-})=\frac{1}{4}
.

For an arbitrary integer
\ell\geq 5
with
\ell\not\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 3)
, Theorem
1.1
implies by the general standard results on the Turán density of blowups (see e.g.
[
18
, Theorem 2.2]
or
[
3
, Claim 5.14]
) that

\pi(C_{\ell}^{3-})\leq\frac{1}{4}
(which is equality by the above construction).

We also establish the Erdős–Simonovits-type stability property
[
10
,
31
]
for
C_{5}^{3-}
in the following theorem. A
3
-graph is called a
T_{\mathrm{rec}}
-subconstruction
if it a subgraph of some
T_{\mathrm{rec}}
-construction.

Theorem 1.2
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
, there exist
\delta>0
and
N_{0}
such that the following statement holds for all
n\geq N_{0}
.
Suppose that
\mathcal{H}
is an
n
-vertex
C_{5}^{3-}
-free
3
-graph with at least
\frac{1}{4}\binom{n}{3}-\delta n^{3}
edges.
Then
\mathcal{H}
is a
T_{\mathrm{rec}}
-subconstruction after removing at most
\varepsilon n^{3}
edges.

In the following theorem, we establish a refined structural property for maximum
C_{5}^{3-}
-free 3-graphs on sufficiently large vertex set.

Theorem 1.3
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
there exists
n_{0}
such that the following holds for every
n\geq n_{0}
.
Suppose that
\mathcal{H}
is an
n
-vertex
C_{5}^{3-}
-free
3
-graph with
\mathrm{ex}(n,C_{5}^{3-})
edges. Then there exists a partition
V_{1}\cup V_{2}\cup V_{3}=V(\mathcal{H})
such that

(i)

\left||V_{i}|-\frac{n}{3}\right|\leq\varepsilon n
for every
i\in[3]
, and

(ii)

\mathcal{H}\setminus\bigcup_{i\in[3]}\mathcal{H}[V_{i}]=\mathcal{K}[V_{1},V_{2},V_{3}]
.

Thus, Conclusion
(ii)
of the theorem states that
\mathcal{H}
is the complete
3
-partite
3
-graph plus possibly some edges inside parts. It is easy to see that
\mathcal{H}
remains
C_{5}^{3-}
-free if we replace
\mathcal{H}[V_{i}]
by any
C_{5}^{3-}
-free
3
-graph on
V_{i}
. Thus, each induced subgraph
\mathcal{H}[V_{i}]
is maximum
C_{5}^{3-}
-free and Theorem
1.3
applies to it provided
|V_{i}|\geq n_{0}
, and so on. We see that
\mathcal{H}
exactly follows the construction for
T_{\mathrm{rec}}
, except for parts of size less than
n_{0}
. It follows that there is a constant
C=C(n_{0})
such that

\displaystyle t_{\mathrm{rec}}(n)\leq\mathrm{ex}(n,C_{5}^{3-})\leq t_{\mathrm{rec}}(n)+Cn,\quad\mbox{for all $n\geq 1$}.

Thus, we know the function
\mathrm{ex}(n,C_{5}^{3-})
within additive
O(n)
.

Remark 1.4
.

With some additional arguments (see Proposition
6.1
), one can show that for every
\delta>0
, there exists
N_{0}
such that for every
n\geq N_{0}
,

\displaystyle\left|\mathrm{ex}(n,C_{5}^{3-})-\mathrm{ex}(n-1,C_{5}^{3-})-\frac{n^{2}}{8}\right|\leq\delta n^{2}.

(1)

Using this result and further arguments, Theorem
1.3

(i)
can be strengthened to

\left||V_{i}|-\frac{n}{3}\right|\leq\varepsilon\sqrt{n}
for every
i\in[3]
.
Since we were unable to further improve
\varepsilon\sqrt{n}
to
1
, we omit the details here.

Our proofs of the above results crucially use the flag algebra machinery developed by Razborov
[
28
]
and are computer-assisted. Independently of this work, analogous results by a similar method were obtained by Lidický–Mattes–Pfender
[
19
]
.

We adopt the general strategy of Balogh–Hu–Lidický–Pfender used in
[
2
]
for determining the inducibility of the
5
-cycle
C_{5}
(where asymptotically extremal graphs are also obtained via a recursive construction). Rather roughly, our proof is based on the following two crucial claims about an unknown
C_{5}^{3-}
-free
3
-graph
\mathcal{H}
with
n\to\infty
vertices and at least
(\frac{1}{4}+o(1)){n\choose 3}
edges.

1.

Proposition
3.2
shows that there exists a vertex partition
V_{1}\cup V_{2}\cup V_{3}=V(\mathcal{H})
such that
|\mathcal{H}\cap\mathcal{K}[V_{1},V_{2},V_{3}]|\geq(0.194...+o(1)){n\choose 3}
(which is not too far from the upper bound
(n/3)^{3}=(0.222...+o(1)){n\choose 3}
).

2.

Proposition
3.3
shows that if the partition in Item
1
is additionally assumed to be
locally maximal
(meaning that by moving any one vertex between parts we cannot increase the number of
transversal edges
, that is, the edges in
\mathcal{H}\cap\mathcal{K}[V_{1},V_{2},V_{3}]
) and we compare
\mathcal{H}\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{3}\mathcal{H}[V_{i}]
with the complete 3-partite
3
-graph
\mathcal{K}[V_{1},V_{2},V_{3}]

then the number of additional edges is at most 0.99 times the number of missing edges.

Thus have identified a top-level partition such that, ignoring triples inside parts,
\mathcal{H}
does not perform better than a copy of
T_{\mathrm{rec}}
that uses the same parts. We can recursively apply this result to each part
\mathcal{H}[V_{i}]
as long
|V_{i}|
is sufficiently large. Now, routine calculations imply that
|\mathcal{H}|\leq(\frac{1}{4}+o(1)){n\choose 3}
.

Our main new idea, when compared to
[
2
,
19
]
, is a trivial combinatorial observation that for every partition
V_{1}\cup V_{2}\cup V_{3}
of
V(\mathcal{H})
there is also a locally maximal one with at least as many transversal edges. It seems that the standard flag algebra calculations do not “capture” this kind of argument and doing an intermediate
local refinement
step (when the flag algebra version of the local maximality is manually added to the SDP program) may considerably improve the power of the method. A possible way to compare computer-generated results is by the size of their certificates (say, as a single compressed zip-file). Our proof, even without attempting to reduce the set of used types, occupies around 1MB of space while that from
[
19
]
has size over 7GB.

Preliminaries

For pairwise disjoint sets
V_{1},\ldots,V_{\ell}
, we use
\mathcal{K}[V_{1},\ldots,V_{\ell}]
to denote the complete
\ell
-partite
\ell
-graph with parts
V_{1},\ldots,V_{\ell}
. Also,
\mathcal{K}^{2}[V_{1},\ldots,V_{\ell}]
denotes the complete
\ell
-partite
2
-graph with parts
V_{1},\ldots,V_{\ell}
(thus its edge set is
\bigcup_{1\leq i<j\leq\ell}\mathcal{K}[V_{i},V_{j}]
).

For a set
X
and an integer
k\geq 0
, let
{X\choose k}:=\{S\subseteq X\colon|S|=k\}
denote the family of all
k
-subsets of
X
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a
3
-graph
\mathcal{H}
. Its
order
is
v(\mathcal{H}):=|V(\mathcal{H})|
.
For a vertex
v\in V(\mathcal{H})
,
the
link
of
v
is the following 2-graph on
V(\mathcal{H})\setminus\{v\}
:

\displaystyle L_{\mathcal{H}}(v)\coloneqq\left\{e\in\binom{V(\mathcal{H})\setminus\{v\}}{2}\colon e\cup\{v\}\in\mathcal{H}\right\}.

The
degree

d_{\mathcal{H}}(v)
of
v
in
\mathcal{H}
is given by
d_{\mathcal{H}}(v)\coloneqq|L_{\mathcal{H}}(v)|
.
We use
\delta(\mathcal{H})
,
\Delta(\mathcal{H})
, and
d(\mathcal{H})
to denote the
minimum
,
maximum
, and
average degree
of
\mathcal{H}
, respectively.
For a pair of vertices
\{u,v\}\subseteq V(\mathcal{H})
, the
codegree

d_{\mathcal{H}}(uv)
of
\{u,v\}
is the number of edges containing
\{u,v\}
.

The
k
-blowup

\mathcal{H}^{(k)}
of
\mathcal{H}
is the 3-graph whose vertex set is the union
\bigcup_{v\in V(\mathcal{H})}U_{v}
of some disjoint
k
-sets
U_{v}
, one per each vertex
v\in V(\mathcal{H})
, and whose edge set is the union of the complete
3
-partite
3
-graphs
\mathcal{K}[U_{x},U_{y},U_{y}]
over all edges
\{x,y,z\}\in\mathcal{H}
. Informally speaking,
\mathcal{H}^{(k)}
is obtained from
\mathcal{H}
by cloning each vertex
k
times.

For another
3
-graph
F
of order
k
, the
(induced) density

p(F,\mathcal{H})
in
\mathcal{H}
is the number of
k
-subsets of
V(\mathcal{H})
that span a subgraph isomorphic to
F
, divided by
{v(\mathcal{H})\choose k}
. When
F=K_{3}^{3}
is the single edge, we get the
edge density

\rho(\mathcal{H})\coloneqq|\mathcal{H}|/{v(\mathcal{H})\choose 3}
.

Given two
r
-graphs
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{G}
, a map
\psi\colon V(\mathcal{H})\to V(\mathcal{G})
is called a
homomorphism
if
\psi(e)\in\mathcal{G}
for all
e\in\mathcal{H}
.
Let
K_{4}^{3-}
denote the
3
-graph on
\{1,2,3,4\}
with edge set
\left\{\{1,2,3\},\{1,2,4\},\{1,3,4\}\right\}
.
Observe that there exists a homomorphism from
C_{5}^{3-}
to
K_{4}^{3-}
.
Thus, the Supersaturation Method (see e.g.
[
18
, Theorem 2.2]
) gives that

\displaystyle\pi(C_{5}^{3-})=\pi(\{C_{5}^{3-},K_{4}^{3-}\}).

(2)

Therefore, in order to prove Theorem
1.1
, it suffices to show that
\pi(\{C_{5}^{3-},K_{4}^{3-}\})\leq\frac{1}{4}
.

The
standard 2-dimensional simplex
is

\displaystyle\mathbb{S}^{3}\coloneqq\left\{(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\colon x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3}=1\text{ and }x_{i}\geq 0\text{ for }i\in[3]\right\}.

The following fact is straightforward to verify.

Fact 2.1
.

The following inequalities hold for every
(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\mathbb{S}^{3}
\colon

(i)

\frac{6x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}}{1-(x_{1}^{3}+x_{2}^{3}+x_{3}^{3})}\leq\frac{1}{4}
.

(ii)

If
\min\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}\}\geq\frac{1}{5}
, then
x_{1}^{3}+x_{2}^{3}+x_{3}^{3}\leq\frac{29}{125}<\frac{1}{3}
and
\frac{1+x_{1}^{3}+x_{2}^{3}+x_{3}^{3}}{1-(x_{1}^{3}+x_{2}^{3}+x_{3}^{3})}<2
.

(iii)

x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}+\frac{x_{1}^{3}+x_{2}^{3}+x_{3}^{3}}{24}\leq\frac{1}{24}
. Moreover, if
\max_{i\in[3]}\left|x_{i}-1/3\right|\geq\varepsilon
for some
\varepsilon\in[0,1/10]
and
x_{i}\in[1/5,1/2]
for every
i\in[3]
, then

\displaystyle x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}+\frac{x_{1}^{3}+x_{2}^{3}+x_{3}^{3}}{24}\leq\frac{1}{24}-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{16}.

Computer-generated results

In this section, we present the results derived by computer using the flag algebra method of Razborov
[
28
]
, which is also described in e.g.
[
27
,
1
,
5
,
12
]
.
Since this method is well-known by now, we will be very brief. In particular, we omit many definitions, referring
the reader to
[
28
,
27
]
for any missing notions. Roughly speaking, a
flag algebra proof using
0
-flags on
m
vertices
of an upper bound
u\in{\mathbbm{R}}
on the given objective function
f

consists of an identity

u-f(\mathcal{H})=\mathrm{SOS}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{m}^{0}}c_{F}p(F,\mathcal{H})+o(1),

which is asymptotically true for any admissible
\mathcal{H}
with
|V(\mathcal{H})|\to\infty
, where the
\mathrm{SOS}
-term can be represented as a sum of squares (as described e.g. in
[
27
, Section 3]
), each coefficient
c_{F}\in{\mathbbm{R}}
is non-negative, and
\mathcal{F}_{m}^{0}
consists of isomorphism types of
0
-flags
(unlabelled
3
-graphs) with
m
vertices. If
f(\mathcal{H})
can be represented as a linear combination of the densities of members of
\mathcal{F}_{m}^{0}
in
\mathcal{H}
then finding the smallest possible
u
amounts to solving a semi-definite program (SDP) with
|\mathcal{F}_{m}^{0}|
linear constraints. (So we write the size of
\mathcal{F}_{m}^{0}
in each case to give the reader some idea of the size of the programs that we had to solve.)

We formed the corresponding SDPs and then analysed the solutions returned by computer, using a modified version of the SageMath package. This package is still under development, for short guide on how to install it and its current functionality can be found in the GitHub repository
https://github.com/bodnalev/sage
. The calculations used for this paper and the generated certificates can be found in the ancillary folder of the arXiv version of this paper or in a separate GitHub repository
https://github.com/bodnalev/supplementary_files
in the folder
no_c5m
. This folder also includes a self-contained verifier entirely written in Python 3, with a hard-coded version of the objective functions and assumptions found in the propositions of this section. The purpose of this program is to first ensure that the objective functions and assumptions match those in the propositions, and then to verify the correctness of the flag algebra proofs from the certificates. We did not optimise the verifier for speed (aiming instead for the clarity of the code); the full verification of all computer-generated results in this paper takes about 180 hours on an average PC.

As far as we can see, none of the certificates (even that for Proposition
3.4
) can be made sufficiently compact to be human-checkable.
Hence we did not make any systematic efforts to reduce the size of the obtained certificates, being content with ones that can be generated and verified on an average modern PC. In particular, we did not try to reduce the set of the used types needed for the proofs, although we did use the (standard) observation of Razborov
[
27
, Section 4]
that each unknown SDP matrix can be assumed to consist of 2 blocks (namely, the invariant and anti-invariant parts).

The first result implies by (
2
) that the Turán density of
C_{5}^{3-}
is at most

\alpha_{\mathrm{\ref{pr:1}}}:=\frac{126373441}{504000000}\simeq 0.25074...,

which is rather close to
1/4=0.25
, the value that we ultimately aim for.

Proposition 3.1
.

For every integer
n\geq 1
, every
\{C_{5}^{3-},K_{4}^{3-}\}
-free
n
-vertex
3
-graph
\mathcal{H}
has at most
\alpha_{\mathrm{\ref{pr:1}}}\,\frac{n^{3}}{6}
edges.

Proof.

Suppose on the contrary that some
3
-graph
\mathcal{H}
contradicts the proposition. Thus,
\beta:=6|\mathcal{H}|/(v(\mathcal{H}))^{3}
is greater than
\alpha_{\mathrm{\ref{pr:1}}}
. For every integer
k\geq 1
, the
k
-blowup
\mathcal{H}^{(k)}
has
k\,v(\mathcal{H})
vertices and
k^{3}\,|\mathcal{H}|
edges, so the ratio
6|\mathcal{H}^{(k)}|/(v(\mathcal{H}^{(k)}))^{3}
for
\mathcal{H}^{(k)}
is also equal to
\beta
. Also,
\mathcal{H}^{(k)}
is still
\{C_{5}^{3-},K_{4}^{3-}\}
-free since this family is closed under taking images under homomorphisms.

The sequence
(\mathcal{H}^{(k)})_{k=1}^{\infty}
converges as
k\to\infty
to a limit
\phi
, where

\phi(F):=\lim_{k\to\infty}p(F,\mathcal{H}^{(k)}),\quad\mbox{for a 3-graph $F$,}

that is,
\phi

sends a 3-graph
F
to the limiting density of
F
in
\mathcal{H}^{(k)}
. We extend
\phi
by linearity to formal linear combinations of
3
-graphs, obtaining a positive homomorphism
\mathcal{A}^{0}\to{\mathbbm{R}}
from the flag algebra
\mathcal{A}^{0}
to the reals, see
[
28
, Section 3.1]
. It satisfies that
\phi(K_{3}^{3})=\beta
, that is, the edge density in the limit
\phi
is
\beta
.

However, our (standard) application of the flag algebra method using
\{C_{5}^{3-},K_{4}^{3-}\}
-free
3
-graphs on
7
vertices (resulting in an SDP with
|\mathcal{F}_{7}^{0}|=1127
inequality constraints) gives
\alpha_{\mathrm{\ref{pr:1}}}
as an upper bound on the edge density. This contradicts our assumption
\beta>\alpha_{\mathrm{\ref{pr:1}}}
.

For an
n
-vertex
3
-graph
\mathcal{H}
define the
max-cut ratio
to be

\mu(\mathcal{H}):=\frac{6}{n^{3}}\,\max\big\{\,|\mathcal{H}\cap\mathcal{K}[V_{1},V_{2},V_{3}]|:V_{1},V_{2},V_{2}\mbox{ form a partition of }V(\mathcal{H})\,\big\}.

Observe that
\mu(\mathcal{H}^{(k)})=\mu(\mathcal{H})
for every
k\geq 1
. Indeed, let the
k
-blowup
\mathcal{H}^{(k)}
have
n
groups of twins
U_{1},\dots,U_{n}
corresponding to the
n
vertices of
\mathcal{H}
.
Every
3
-partition
V_{1}\cup V_{2}\cup V_{2}
of
V(\mathcal{H})
lifts to the
3
-partition
V_{1}^{(1)}\cup V_{2}^{(k)}\cup V_{3}^{(k)}
of
V(\mathcal{H}^{(k)})
, where
V_{i}^{(k)}:=\bigcup_{v\in V_{i}}U_{v}
, implying that
\mu(\mathcal{H}^{(k)})\geq\mu(\mathcal{H})

Informally speaking, the main idea is that any edge

In order to state the next result, we have to provide various definitions for a

For a partition

\displaystyle B_{\mathcal{H}}(V_{1},V_{2},V_{3})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{X\in\mathcal{H}\colon\{|X\cap V_{1}|,\,|X\cap V_{2}|,\,|X\cap V_{3}|\}=\{0,1,2\}\right\},

(5)

\displaystyle M_{\mathcal{H}}(V_{1},V_{2},V_{3})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{X\in{V(\mathcal{H})\choose 3}\setminus\mathcal{H}\colon\{|X\cap V_{1}|,\,|X\cap V_{2}|,\,|X\cap V_{3}|\}=\{1,1,1\}\right\}

and

The key result needed for our proof is the following.

Proposition 3.3

If

We would like to run flag algebra calculations on the limit

\mathcal{H}^{(k)}

of a hypothetical counterexample

in the theory of

For
i\in[3]

(1,i)
denote the 1-vertex type where the colour of the unique vertex is

Consider the random homomorphism

The local maximality assumption directly translates in the limit to the statement that, for each

Now we face the standard flag algebra task of finding the maximum of the quantum 0-flag expressing

However, an issue with this approach is that 5-vertex flags are not enough to prove the desired conclusion, while we have rather many 6-vertex flags (namely,

The easiest way to formally define an unordered partition

However, when we translate the assumption in (

\bm{\phi}^{1}(K_{\bullet|\circ|\circ})\geq\frac{1}{2}\,\bm{\phi}^{1}(K_{\bullet\circ|\circ}),

3.1
Some remarks on our implementation

There was some freedom in choosing constants that still make the whole proof work. It would be sufficient to prove the weaker versions of Propositions
3.2
and
3.3
where
\beta_{\mathrm{\ref{pr:2}}}:=1/4
(with some extra arguments) and
\beta_{\mathrm{\ref{pr:3}}}:=\alpha_{\mathrm{\ref{pr:2}}}
respectively.
We decided to present somewhat stronger versions of these intermediate results, obtained by experimenting on computer. For example,
\beta_{\mathrm{\ref{pr:3}}}:=0.185
did not seem to suffice in Proposition
3.3
(nor
\beta_{\mathrm{\ref{pr:2}}}:=\frac{1}{4}-10^{-4}
in Proposition
3.2
) so we opted for the current values that are close to optimal and simple to write. The stated values for
\alpha_{\mathrm{\ref{pr:1}}}
and
\alpha_{\mathrm{\ref{pr:2}}}
are exactly the constants returned by our rounding calculations.

Likewise, we decided to use only the necessary assumptions in the flag proofs for Propositions
3.1
–
3.4
(although we did not make any attempt to reduce the set of used types), even though this sometimes resulted in slightly weaker intermediate bounds.
For example, we could have improved slightly the “plain” upper bound on
\pi(C_{5}^{3-})
coming from Propositions
3.1
if we have applied the flag algebra proof to a limit of a convergent subsequence of maximum
\{C_{5}^{3-},K_{4}^{3-}\}
-free
3
-graphs of order
n\to\infty
,
where we could have additionally assumed that every two vertex degrees differ by at most
n-2=o(n^{2})
and each degree is at least
(\frac{1}{4}+o(1)){n\choose 2}
.

Some extra steps were required in Proposition
3.3
during
rounding
(when, given the floating-point matrices produced by an SDP solver, we have to find matrices with rational coefficients that certify the validity of the bound). The issue here is that the inequality
|B|\leq\frac{99}{100}|M|
is in a sense attained by many feasible configurations (in addition to varying the edges inside parts): namely, we can pick a partition
[n]=V_{1}\cup V_{2}\cup V_{3}
with any part ratios
x_{i}\coloneqq|V_{i}|/n
as long as
\prod_{i=1}^{3}|V_{i}|\geq\beta_{\mathrm{\ref{pr:3}}}\,\frac{n^{3}}{6}
and take
\mathcal{H}\coloneqq\mathcal{K}[V_{1},V_{2},V_{3}]
(when
B=M=\emptyset
). As it is well known, any asymptotically extremal construction forces various relations in the certificate (that are of the form that some inequalities have zero slack and that some positive semi-definite matrices must have specific zero eigenvectors). We do not dwell on the exact nature of these restrictions (since the task of verifying the certificates does not require any knowledge of them) but refer the reader to e.g.
[
25
, Section 3.1]
for a general discussion. We observe that these constraints in Proposition
3.2
amount to a system of explicit polynomial equations being satisfied for all
(x_{1},x_{2})
in some open neighbourhood of
(\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{3})
. Of course, this implies that all these polynomials are identically 0. We used the equivalent reformulation (that was easier to implement in the current code) that all partial derivatives of each polynomial (up to the maximum degree) vanish at
(\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{3})
.

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1}{8}+\varepsilon\right)|V_{1}|^{2}+|V_{2}||V_{3}|+\varepsilon n^{2}-\max\left\{\frac{|B_{v}|}{9},\,\frac{|M_{v}|}{10}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1}{8}+\varepsilon\right)\left(\frac{n}{3}+\varepsilon n\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{n}{3}+\varepsilon n\right)^{2}+\varepsilon n^{2}-\max\left\{\frac{|B_{v}|}{9},\,\frac{|M_{v}|}{10}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n^{2}}{8}+3\varepsilon n^{2}-\max\left\{\frac{|B_{v}|}{9},\,\frac{|M_{v}|}{10}\right\}.

Since, by Proposition
6.1

(ii)
,
|L_{\mathcal{H}}(v)|\geq\left(\frac{1}{8}-\varepsilon\right)n^{2}
, the inequality above implies that

\displaystyle|B_{v}|\leq 36\varepsilon n^{2}\quad\text{and}\quad|M_{v}|\leq 40\varepsilon n^{2}.

(36)

Next, we return to the analysis of the number of bad edges and missing edges in
\mathcal{H}
.
Our goal is to show that
|B|\leq|M|
, with equality holding only if
M=B=\emptyset
.
Suppose that
B\neq\emptyset
.

We call a pair of vertices
crossing
if its two vertices belong to two different parts
V_{i}
.

Claim 6.4
.

For every
e=\{v_{1},v_{2},v_{3}\}\in B
with
v_{2}v_{3}
crossing, we have

\displaystyle d_{M}(v_{2}v_{3})\geq\left(\frac{1}{3}-300\varepsilon\right)n.

Proof of Claim
6.4
.

By the symmetry between parts, assume that
v_{1},v_{3}\in V_{1}
and
v_{2}\in V_{2}
.
First, observe that for every pair
(v_{4},v_{5})\in V_{3}\times(V_{2}\setminus\{v_{2}\})
,

\displaystyle\text{at least one triple in $\{v_{2}v_{3}v_{4},v_{3}v_{4}v_{5},v_{4}v_{5}v_{1}\}$ belongs to $M$},

(37)

since otherwise,
v_{1}v_{2}v_{3}v_{4}v_{5}
would form a copy of
C_{5}^{3-}
in
\mathcal{H}
.

Suppose to the contrary that
d_{M}(v_{2}v_{3})<\left(\frac{1}{3}-300\varepsilon\right)n
.
Define

\displaystyle U\coloneqq\left\{u\in V_{3}\colon v_{2}v_{3}u\in M\right\}.

Since
U\subseteq N_{M}(v_{2}v_{3})
, we have
|U|<\left(\frac{1}{3}-300\varepsilon\right)n
.
Consequently,

\displaystyle|V_{3}\setminus U|\geq\left(\frac{1}{3}-\varepsilon\right)n-\left(\frac{1}{3}-300\varepsilon\right)n=299\varepsilon n.

By (
37
), for every pair
(v_{4},v_{5})\in(V_{3}\setminus U)\times(V_{2}\setminus\{v_{2}\})
, either
\{v_{3}v_{4}v_{5}\}\in M
or
\{v_{1}v_{4}v_{5}\}\in M
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle d_{M}(v_{1})+d_{M}(v_{3})\geq|V_{3}\setminus U|\cdot|V_{2}\setminus\{v_{2}\}|\geq 299\varepsilon n\cdot\left(\frac{1}{3}-2\varepsilon\right)n\geq 90\varepsilon n^{2}.

Thus, we have that

\displaystyle\max\left\{d_{M}(v_{1}),\,d_{M}(v_{3})\right\}\geq\frac{d_{M}(v_{1})+d_{M}(v_{3})}{2}\geq 45\varepsilon n^{2},

contradicting (
36
).

Claim 6.5
.

For every crossing pair
v_{1}v_{2}
, we have
d_{B}(v_{1}v_{2})\leq 300\varepsilon n
.

Proof of Claim
6.5
.

By symmetry, assume that
v_{1}\in V_{1}
and
v_{2}\in V_{2}
.
Suppose to the contrary that
d_{B}(v_{1}v_{2})>300\varepsilon n
.
By symmetry, we may assume that
N\coloneqq N_{B}(v_{1}v_{2})\cap V_{1}
has size at least
\frac{1}{2}d_{B}(v_{1}v_{2})\geq 150\varepsilon n
.
It follows from Claim
6.4
that

\displaystyle d_{M}(v_{2})\geq\sum_{v_{3}\in N}d_{M}(v_{2}v_{3})\geq|N|\cdot\left(\frac{1}{3}-300\varepsilon\right)n\geq 150\varepsilon n\cdot\left(\frac{1}{3}-300\varepsilon\right)n>40\varepsilon n^{2},

contradicting (
36
).

Let
\mathcal{S}
consists of all crossing pairs that lie inside at least one bad edge. By Claim
6.4
, we have
d_{M}(uv)\geq\left(\frac{1}{3}-100\varepsilon\right)n
for every
uv\in\mathcal{S}
. Since every bad edge has two crossing pairs, it follows from Claim
6.5
that

\displaystyle|B|\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{uv\in\mathcal{S}}d_{B}(uv)\leq\frac{1}{2}\cdot|\mathcal{S}|\cdot 300\varepsilon n=150\varepsilon n\,|\mathcal{S}|.

On the other hand, it follows from the definition that

\displaystyle|M|\geq\frac{1}{3}\sum_{uv\in\mathcal{S}}d_{M}(uv)\geq\frac{1}{3}\cdot|\mathcal{S}|\cdot\left(\frac{1}{3}-100\varepsilon\right)n\geq\frac{n}{10}\,|\mathcal{S}|,

which is strictly greater than
|B|
.

Let the
3
-graph
\mathcal{G}
be obtained from
\mathcal{H}
by removing all triples from
B
and adding all triples in
M
.
It is easy to see that
\mathcal{G}
is
C_{5}^{3-}
-free, while
|\mathcal{G}|=|\mathcal{H}|+|M|-|B|>|\mathcal{H}|
, contradicting the maximality of
\mathcal{H}
.
Therefore, we have that
B=\emptyset
. Also,
M=\emptyset
, again by the maximality of
\mathcal{H}
.
Thus,
\mathcal{H}\setminus\bigcup_{i\in[3]}\mathcal{H}[V_{i}]
is exactly the complete 3-partite 3-graph
\mathcal{K}[V_{1},V_{2},V_{3}]
.
This completes the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

7
Concluding remarks

Proposition
6.1
implies that
C_{5}^{3-}
is
bounded
, and (
1
) implies that
C_{5}^{3-}
is
smooth
, where these two properties were introduced in
[
15
]
. These two properties lead to applications in certain tilting-type extremal problems related to
C_{5}^{3-}
, and we refer the reader to
[
15
,
6
]
for further details.

It would be very interesting to see which other problems become tractable with the
local refinement
method, which can generally be described as follows:

Step 1:

prove that any extremal configuration
G
of large order
n
is not too far from a conjectured construction
C
in some fixed measure of similarity,

Step 2:

choose an instance of
C
that is best fit to
G
and add manually the flag algebra versions of the local optimality conditions (namely that, that no local change to
C
can deteriorate the similarity measure between
C
and
G
),

Step 3:

check if flag algebras can prove the desired asymptotic results under the extra assumptions coming from the previous two steps.

For example, if the conjectured asymptotically
maximum 3-graphs
\mathcal{H}
come from 3-graph
\mathcal{D}(T)
which consists triples that span a directed cycle in a tournament
T
(this is the case for the extremal problems studied in
[
13
,
29
,
11
]
), then the local assumptions could say that changing the orientation of one pair in
T
cannot increase
|\mathcal{H}\cap\mathcal{D}(T)|
(or decrease
|\mathcal{H}\setminus\mathcal{D}(T)|
, etc).

For problems with the conjectured constructions being recursive, it makes good sense to bound in Step 3 not the global function we try to optimize but the contribution of the top level to it, as we did for the Turán problem for
C_{5}^{3-}
. Indeed, our computer experiments indicate that flag algebras using flags with at most 6-vertices (in the theory of 3-graphs with an unordered vertex 3-partition) cannot prove directly
|\mathcal{H}|\leq(\frac{1}{4}+o(1)){n\choose 3}
under the assumptions of Proposition
3.3
. This is as expected since such hypothetical proof would apply to the union of three vertex-disjoint copies of an arbitrary
C_{5}^{3-}
-free
\mathcal{G}
, on
V_{1}
,
V_{2}
and
V_{3}
, and the complete
3
-partite
3
-graph
\mathcal{K}[V_{1},V_{2},V_{3}]
, and would imply that the edge density of
\mathcal{G}
is at most
\frac{1}{4}+o(1)
. This could in turn be translated into a proof of
\pi(C_{5}^{3-})\leq\frac{1}{4}
in the theory of (uncoloured)
C_{5}^{3-}
-free
3
-graphs.

A conjecture, often attributed to Mubayi–Rödl
[
23
]
, states that
\pi(C_{5}^{3})=2\sqrt{3}-3
(which is
0.46410...
).
The recent work by Kamčev–Letzter–Pokrovskiy
[
16
]
shows that
\pi(C_{\ell}^{3})=2\sqrt{3}-3
for all sufficiently large
\ell
satisfying
\ell\not\equiv 0\pmod{3}
. After the arXiv version of this paper was submitted, we used the local refinement method described above to prove in
[
4
]
that
\pi(\mathcal{C})=2\sqrt{3}-3
, where
\mathcal{C}
is either the
7
-vertex tight cycle
C_{7}^{3}
, or the pair
\{K_{4}^{3},C_{5}^{3}\}
, where
K_{4}^{3}
is the complete
3
-graph on
4
vertices.
This result implies that
\pi(C_{\ell}^{3})=2\sqrt{3}-3
for all
\ell\geq 7
satisfying
\ell\not\equiv 0\pmod{3}
, thus strengthening the result of Kamčev–Letzter–Pokrovskiy.

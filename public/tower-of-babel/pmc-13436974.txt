Reply to “Methodological Concerns in ‘Prognostic Usefulness of Clinical Complete Response after PD-1 Inhibitor-Based Combination Therapy for Unresectable Hepatocellular Carcinoma’”
Authors: Da-Long Yang, Jian-Li Huang, Hong-Gui Qin, Liang Ma, Jian-Hong Zhong, on behalf of the GUIDANCE Investigators
Published: 2026

Dear Editor,

We thank Dr. Shen and colleagues [1] for their thoughtful comments on our article [2]. We appreciate the opportunity to address the methodological concerns raised, which we believe will strengthen the interpretation of our findings.

First, in our study, all patients with chronic hepatitis B/C infection received routine oral antiviral therapy [3, 4]. As this was a retrospective analysis, we did not adjust for antiviral therapy in the multivariable models because it was uniformly administered to all eligible patients. Given that our research subjects are mainly Chinese patients with hepatitis B-related hepatocellular carcinoma, and hepatic artery infusion chemotherapy is currently the commonly used local treatment method in China, these research results may not be directly applicable to regions outside China. Future studies need to be conducted in different population groups to verify our conclusions.

Second, our study identified predictors of clinical complete response (cCR) with potential clinical utility for guiding treatment decisions, though our modeling strategy (univariable screening plus clinical relevance) remains exploratory and unvalidated. In this context, variable selection was guided by clinical relevance and univariable significance (p < 0.05), and the number of events per variable (e.g., 200 clinical complete response events for logistic regression) exceeded the widely cited threshold of 10. Although our multivariable models met conventional event-per-variable criteria, we did not employ bootstrapping or penalized regression, which may have reduced the risk of overfitting. Future studies should consider these approaches when developing predictive models for cCR. In this retrospective study with heterogeneous regimens, cCR correlated with favorable survival but not consistently with pathological complete response, making it an exploratory correlate rather than a definitive surrogate endpoint. Thus, prospective studies with standardized protocols and pathological validation are required to confirm cCR as a reliable surrogate for long-term survival. Nowadays, our team is developing a more rigorous multimodal prediction model based on clinical, computed tomography, and magnetic resonance imaging radiomics to more accurately identify patients with unresectable hepatocellular carcinoma who are expected to achieve clinical complete remission following PD-1 inhibitor-based combination therapy.

Third, α-fetoprotein levels were measured at baseline, prior to the first cycle of combination therapy. The multivariable analyses used baseline α-fetoprotein (categorized as <400 vs. ≥400 ng/mL), which is a widely accepted cutoff in hepatocellular carcinoma prognosis [5, 6]. To avoid potential collinearity arising from the inclusion of both prothrombin time and Child-Pugh grade (prothrombin time is a component of the Child-Pugh score), we re-ran the multivariable Cox model after excluding prothrombin time (Table 1). The results were consistent with the original analysis, confirming the robustness of our findings. Again, we extend our appreciation for their constructive suggestions.

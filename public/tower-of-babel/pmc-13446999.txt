A prospective study on the safety and clinical effectiveness of rabies monoclonal antibodies for post exposure prophylaxis in suspected rabies exposures at the anti-rabies clinic of KIMS Hospital and Research Centre, Bangalore
Authors: Ravish Haradanhalli, Sowmyashree U, Poornima M K, Divya Bharathi G
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeRabies immunoglobulin (RIG) will offer immediate passive protection after exposure to suspected rabid animals. These include human RIG and equine RIG, both having constraints for usage. Recombinant technology produced monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) have demonstrated greater availability and safety as a substitute for RIG in post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP). Presently 2 mAb products have been merchandised; World Health Organization has advised monitoring of both the rabies monoclonal antibodies (RmAb) products’ in clinical use. To assess the safety and clinical effectiveness of RmAbs for PEP in category III animal exposures.Materials and MethodsAll patients with category III exposure who visited the study site and gave their consent were included. A case record form was used to gather information on the sociodemographic profile, bite wound features, PEP given, and specifics of adverse events that occurred after PEP. For a further 6 months, all the research participants were monitored to determine the clinical effectiveness of PEP.ResultsThe present study included 475 animal bite victims. Among them 41.2% were children, 49.7% were adults and 9.1% were elderly. The adverse events (AEs) among single mAb and cocktail of 2 mAbs were 8.7% and 8.1%, respectively. All the patients who received either of the RmAb experienced mild AEs that resolved on their own or by symptomatic treatment, without any complications. All the trial participants were alive after 6 months following PEP, indicating therapeutic success.ConclusionThe presently available RmAbs are safe and clinically effective in preventing rabies in suspected exposures.

INTRODUCTION

A neglected zoonotic disease like Rabies, is 100% preventable with timely and appropriate administration of post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP) to patients with animal exposures/bites [1].

In India, the animal exposures are estimated to be about 17.4 million annually, with an incidence of 1.7% [2]. PEP should thus be administered promptly to affected persons at the closest medical institution in order to avoid the disease in rabies-endemic countries such as India, where every animal bite is possibly suspected of being a rabid exposure [3]. Even in the event of a high-risk exposure to possibly rabid animals, early and thorough preventive measures, such as adhering to the recommended immunisation schedule, can avert rabies [4].

According to World Health Organization (WHO) recommendations, PEP must be given according to the severity and classification of the wound. All animal exposure wounds should be cleaned with soap/detergent and water and then virucidal treatments should be used to minimise the amount of virus present at the wound site. A complete course of post-exposure vaccination for all category II and III exposures, to induce antibodies which prevents the risk of virus entering peripheral nerves. Wound infiltration of rabies immunoglobulin (RIG) or rabies monoclonal antibodies (RmAbs) is recommended in all category III exposures to neutralize the virus at the wound site. The current anti-rabies vaccinations administered after a bite may only produce neutralising antibodies with a sero-positive titer of >0.5 IU/mL in the bitten individual 7–10 days after the initial dose. RIG/RmAbs are life-saving and prevent rabies within this window of time [5].

When given in conjunction with a full course of rabies vaccination, RIGs administered at the site of viral entry (wounds or exposed regions) have demonstrated effectiveness [6]. There are 2 types of RIGs available: equine rabies immunoglobulin (ERIG) and human rabies immunoglobulin (HRIG). Alternative products are being sought due to the restricted availability, high cost, and possible side effects associated with blood-derived RIGs (ERIG/HRIG). This led to development of anti-RmAb, as they are made by recombinant technology and is less prone to problems such as availability and safety [7].

India is the manufacturing hub for rabies biologicals and has manufactured 2 RmAb products and both of them have been approved by Drug Controller General of India (DCGI) and are merchandised in Indian market for more than 3 years [89]. WHO advised a registry to track the clinical application and results of RmAb products for PEP against rabies [10]. Hence, this study was undertaken to assess the safety and clinical effectiveness of both the new RmAb products for use in PEP in category III animal exposures.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

A prospective study was conducted for a period of one year (March 2024 to March 2025) at the anti-rabies clinic, Preventive Medicine Unit, Kempegowda Institute of Medical Sciences (KIMS) Hospital and Research Center, Bangalore, India, after obtaining clearance from the Institutional Ethics Committee. All category III animal bite victims who presented to the anti-rabies clinic for PEP and provided written informed consent/ assent were included in the study.

Sample size

The sample size was calculated based on the proportion of category III animal exposures reported as 63% from the national multi-centric study conducted by National Centre for Disease Control, Delhi [11]:

assuming a confidence interval of 95%, α=0.05, and precision (d) of 10%; Using the design effect of 2 and 10% non-response. Sample size calculated was 200 for each RmAb.

Data collection

Four hundred seventy-five healthy male and female volunteer patients with category III animal exposures of any age were included. Exclusions from the study included people with a history of prior animal exposures, rabies vaccinations, or immunoglobulin administration; allergy history to chicken eggs, vaccines, or other medications; pre-existing medical conditions; immunocompromised status; and pregnancy or lactation.

A thorough history that included the sociodemographic profile and information on animal exposure such as the kind of animal and whether the attack was provoked or not, was documented. Simultaneously, history of previous or ongoing medical conditions, previous prescription drugs, and allergy to any medicines were also noted. Every wound that the participants had was assessed by a thorough clinical assessment. Number, location (upper and lower limbs, head, neck, trunk, and abdomen) and severity of wounds (abrasions, lacerations, punctures) were noted.

All participants received PEP in compliance with national recommendations. It comprised a thorough wound cleaning with soap and running water for ten to fifteen minutes, regardless of any wound care administered prior to hospital arrival and a full course of intramuscular anti-rabies vaccination according to the Essen regimen, i.e., one dose each, on days 0, 3, 7, 14, and 28. Each patient was given choice of all the passive immunization products available and based on their affordability and selection, the RmAb was simultaneously infiltrated on Day 0 in a single dose not exceeding 3.33 IU/kg body weight for Rabishield (Serum Institute of India Limited, Pune, India) and 40 IU/kg body weight for TwinRab (Zydus Lifesciences Limited, Ahmedabad, India) respectively. The calculated volume of RmAb was infiltrated into all the wounds, as was anatomically feasible, i.e., until it oozes out of the wound, indicating a successful infiltration.

After PEP, adverse events (AEs) were evaluated for each patient. To document any potential immediate solicited local AEs such as pain, erythema, pruritus, and induration and/or systemic reactions like shivering, malaise, asthenia, faintness, dizziness, headache, myalgia, arthralgia, nausea, abdominal pain, and hypersensitivity or allergic reactions such as urticaria, rash, and anaphylaxis, the individuals were watched for an hour. All patients received follow-up cards with the dates of their next immunisation doses, so they could record any unsolicited late AEs such as fever, arthralgia, itching, serum sickness, and any others. These cards were checked during subsequent hospital visits on days 3, 7, 14, 28, and 180.

After completing PEP, all research participants were contacted every month to check in, and after 6 months, they were called to the hospital to confirm their survival and general well-being. The clinical effectiveness of the treatment is determined by the survival of the exposed individuals following the conclusion of the rabies PEP and after the disease's typical incubation period, which is 6 months.

MS Excel (Microsoft, Redmond, WA, USA) and the IBM-SPSS statistics software package version 21.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA) were used for the statistical analysis of the gathered data. The frequency and percentages were calculated in order to analyse the AEs. The study participants were compared for various characteristics like age, gender, education & occupation using univariate statistical test (χ2 test). The χ2 test was employed to find out the relationship of AEs to different products used in the study subjects.

Ethics statement

The study protocol was approved by the Institutional Review Board (IRB) of Kempegowda Institute of Medical Sciences (KIMS/IEC/A219/M/2024). Informed consent was confirmed by the IRB. The study was carried out in complete accordance with the ethical standards established by the Declaration of Helsinki and the Good Clinical Practice guidelines of the International Council for Harmonization. Every research participant/parent provided written informed consent and informed assent was taken from subjects <18 years of age. Strict protocols were followed to protect participant confidentiality.

DISCUSSION

For many years, passive immunisation has been a essential part of the preventative approach against rabies. Numerous studies have demonstrated the effectiveness of administering both vaccine and serum together after an animal bite, starting with the first documented preparation of rabies immune serum, which was described by Babes & Lepp in 1889. World Health Organisation modified guidelines in 1957 recommending a combination of a single serum application and a course of 14 daily vaccinations as the optimal post-exposure treatment [12]. Since then, research has proven the effectiveness of RIG in conjunction with immunisation in PEP for patients with severe bite wounds [13]. The clinical evidence from our study supports the use of RmAb in all category III animal exposures and shows that it is safe and effective when given in conjunction with a full course of anti-rabies vaccination for PEP. The research participants included both sexes and a diverse range of age groups. The incidence of AEs was found to be 8.6% and 8.1% for Rabishield and TwinRab respectively, and all were mild and resolved without complications. These results are consistent with the other researches assessing several RIGs/RmAbs for passive immunization.

Subsequently, since the available RIGs had certain constraints, WHO recommended the production and usage of RmAb as an alternative to RIGs [14]. Because RmAb is produced using recombinant DNA technology, the safety concerns related to blood products do not apply and it can be produced on a large scale; eventually replace ERIG and HRIG, which are scarce due to their reliance on humans or animals [15]. RmAb can be administered immediately without the requirement for skin sensitivity testing, saving doctors’ time and eliminating the possibility of adverse drug responses. This will help increase the use of passive immunization for all category III exposures, which are scarcely used now. Given the limited availability of RIG, the potential role of mAbs as an alternative and the provision of real-world safety data represent important strengths of this study.

With technology transfer from Mass Biologics (MBL), USA, Serum Institute of India Pvt. Ltd. produced the first human mAb in India utilizing recombinant DNA technology. It was licensed to be used in India under the trade name “Rabishield” starting in November 2017. Following this, Zydus Cadila Healthcare Ltd. produced the first cocktail RmAb with technology transfer of one mAb from the WHO collaborating center in Canada and another from the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention in Atlanta. It was licensed under the trade name TwinRab, approved by DCGI, and has been on the market since 2020.The 2 RmAbs have demonstrated enhanced safety, with no blood-borne pathogen transmission, increased availability, infinite production capacity with consistency in production, well-defined and characterized potency, and a concentrated, less volume-consuming form for local administration. In phase III multicentric clinical trials, both RmAbs have demonstrated their safety and efficacy [1617].

Three Hundred and 8 patients with category III exposure to a suspected rabid animal were included in a phase 3, randomized, non-inferiority trial that assessed the anti-RmAb cocktail (TwinRabtm) and HRIG. During the trial, local responses were reported by 21.1% in the HRIG group and 27.7% in the antibody cocktail group. In both groups, the majority of these reactions were local, involving pain and edema. Every AE resolved without any complications [16]. In another research, 199 participants received an injection of a human mAb against the glycoprotein G of the rabies virus, which was created using recombinant DNA technology and 16.3% of AEs were reported in total. During the study period, no significant AEs were reported [17]. In the same manner, a multicentric randomized non-inferiority-controlled trial employing RmAb and HRIG revealed that, within 7 days of the biological’s administration, 21.43% of subjects receiving HRIG and 30.3% of subjects receiving RmAb displayed one or more reaction. These reactions were mild and self-limiting, leading to the conclusion that the trial was safe. The investigation found that RmAb is comparable to HRIG and safe [18].

In a post-marketing surveillance study of Equirab conducted in Mumbai with 168 subjects, it was found that 31.5% had experienced one or more of the following immediate local adverse drug reactions: 30.4% had pain, 5.4% swelling, 3.6% pruritus, 1.2% induration, and 0.6% itching and erythema. However, none of the study subjects experienced any delayed reactions [19].

The clinical effectiveness of RmAb in PEP was demonstrated in our study, where all participants recovered fully and remained alive 6 months after starting PEP. The current study’s findings regarding the effectiveness of RmAb are in line with the product’s actual usage outcomes at our center, where it has been applied for over 3 years. The effectiveness is in line with the findings of previous research, such as one conducted in Bangalore, India, which involved 717 participants who had category III animal exposures.[20].

Logistical problems hindered our investigation by making it impossible to follow up the biting animals to find out if they were rabid. Similarly, financial constraints prevented the Rabies Virus Neutralizing Antibody analysis needed to determine the immunogenicity of the anti-rabies vaccination. Since rabies can incubate for up to 6 months, the survival status of all research participants after 6 months was a good indicator of the PEP’s therapeutic effectiveness. The limitations of the study include the absence of a comparator group, and the confounding factors such as wound severity, timing of PEP, and other clinical variables that may influence outcomes.

In conclusion, this study offers convincing evidence of the safety as well as clinical effectiveness of the indigenously produced recombinant monoclonal antibodies (RmAbs) in category III animal exposures and encourages their continuous use to prevent human rabies and eventually contribute significantly to the eradication of dog-mediated human rabies by 2030 [21].

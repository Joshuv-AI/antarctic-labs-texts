Objective

Coagulase-negative staphylococci (CNS) is a common cause of subclinical mastitis infections [1]. One of the several species that comprise the diverse group of bacteria known as CNS, which still accounts for a significant portion of SCM cases in dairy cattle, is S. saprophyticus [2]. Although antibiotics are a key component in SCM prevention initiatives, this bacteria can also cause persistent intra-mammary infections (IMI), which can result in to dairy economic losses, food shortages, and excessive use of antibiotics [3–4].

The purpose of this work was to characterize the genetic traits of S. saprophyticus strain 3M isolated from a cow with SCM in Kiruhura district, Uganda. The goal of draft genome sequencing was to gain more knowledge about the organism’s overall genomic architecture as well as its genes for virulence and antibiotic resistance because very few studies in the underrepresented regions of Africa have examined variations in antimicrobial resistance among S. saprophyticus identified by genotyping. Strategies for treating and preventing SCM will be informed by our findings, which will enhance existing efforts to monitor the genomes of MDR pathogens in East Africa. Future more, data will be cited in future studies from this work that examine comparative genomes and resistance evolution among various isolates.

S. saprophyticus strain 3M was isolated from a milk sample cultured on blood agar (Himedia, India) and incubated aerobically at 37 °C for 24 h, bright white to creamy, gamma hemolytic colonies, were indicative of S. saprophyticus. The presumptive isolate was purified further using blood agar, a differential media. Positive catalase and negative coagulase further verified the likely isolate. Antibiotic susceptibility testing was conducted using Kirby-Bauer, standard disk diffusion method in line with CLSI guidelines [5, 6]. The isolate was resistant to Penicillin (10 units; 6 mm), erythromycin (15 µg; 10 mm), tetracycline (30 µg; 12 mm), novobiocin (30 µg; 6 mm) and cefoxitin (30 µg, 18 mm).

S. saprophyticus strain 3M isolate was cultured in Luria-Bertani (LB) broth incubated in shaker at 200 rpm for 18–24 h at 37 °C and DNA was extracted using NEB Monarch® Spin gDNA Extraction Kit [7]. DNA concentration and purity were also measured using the Qubit 3.0 Fluorometer with Quant-i­T dsDNA Assay Kit, High Sensitivity (Invitrogen) and Nano Drop ND 1000 spectrophotometer. The MinION MK1B (MIN-101B) (Oxford NANOPORE technology) platform was used for sequencing producing 1D reads [8, 9]. DNA Library was prepared using the Rapid barcoding kit 96V14(SQK-RBK114.96) (Oxford NANOPORE technologies), and loaded unto an R10 flow cell (FLO-MIN114, Oxford Nanopore Technologies) [10].

Raw reads were quality checked with NanoPlot (v1.46.0) before with 18,885.0 total reads, 108,748,143.0 total bases, 11,120.0 read length N50 and 14217 (75.3%) 96.6Mb > Q10 and after trimming16,677.0 total reads, 108,188,141.0 total bases, 11,254.0 read length N50 and 14035 (84.2%) 96.5Mb> Q10 [11]. Adapter sequences and low-quality bases were trimmed using NanoFilt (v2.8.0) [11]. All high-quality trimmed reads were used for de novo assembly with Flye (v2.9.5-b180) [12], and polished with Medaka. All assembled reads passed quality assessment using QUAST (v5.3.0) [13] and assembly completeness was determined using BUSCO (v5.8.3), yielding 76.7% complete BUSCOs (76.7% single-copy, 0.0% duplicated), with 14.9% fragmented and 8.4% missing genes (n = 450) [14]. The assembled genome size was 2,791,445 bp, 13 contigs, 2,589,240 N50, 2,589,240 N90, 1 L50, 1 L90, and 32.5% GC. The assembled genome was annotated using the NCBI Prokaryotic Genome Annotation Pipeline (PGAP (v6.10) [15].

AMR genes were found using the Comprehensive Antibiotic Resistance Database (CARD) [16] and ResFinder v4.6.0. Likewise, BLAST was used with default parameters to query the Virulence Factor Database (VFDB) [17] in order to find possible virulence genes.

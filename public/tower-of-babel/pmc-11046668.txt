Association between lipid profile and disease activity in rheumatoid arthritis
Authors: Kyaw Naing Htoon
Published: 2024

Purpose

Rheumatoid arthritis is associated with increased cardiovascular mortality and this may be due to the atherogenic lipid profile. This study aimed to identify the association between lipoprotein levels (total cholesterol, triglyceride, low-density lipoprotein and high-density lipoprotein) and disease activity in rheumatoid arthritis patients.

Method

A hospital-based, cross-sectional study was carried out at rheumatology clinics at Yangon Specialist Hospital. A total of 81 rheumatoid arthritis patients were voluntarily enrolled and a diagnosis of rheumatoid arthritis was made by using ACR (2010) / EULAR criteria. Physical examinations were done. The disease activity was calculated by Clinical Disease Activity Index (CDAI) formula. The lipid profile of the RA patients was tested while assessing the disease activity. Then the association between lipid profile and disease activity was found.

Results

The association between high disease activity and total cholesterol was statistically significant (p-value = 0.004) (Fig 1). Also, a statistically significant relationship exists between disease activity and HDL cholesterol (p-value = 0.02) (Fig 2). There was no statistical association between triglyceride (TG) (p-value = 0.06) and LDL cholesterol (p-value = 0.07).

Conclusion

This study supports that rheumatoid arthritis is associated with an adverse lipid profile. It is important to get good disease activity control but also not to neglect the management of cardiovascular risk management including dyslipidemia in rheumatoid arthritis patients.

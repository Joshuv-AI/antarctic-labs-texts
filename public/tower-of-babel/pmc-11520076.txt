Job preferences of master public health candidates in Northeast China based on discrete choice experiments
Authors: Huaizhi Cheng, Ruotong Tian, Dongquan Chen, Jiesen Shang, Lingxiao Gao, Xiaoyu Dong, Bin Guo, Guoxiang Liu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe shortage of health workers in rural and remote areas is a key issue for global health systems, including China. Public health undertakings in Northeast China face the risk of a talent gap. In September 2023, the National Development and Reform Commission (NDRC) held a symposium on promoting the comprehensive revitalization of Northeast China in the New Era, proposing to support the comprehensive revitalization of Northeast China with high-quality population development, strengthen the development and utilization of human resources, and increase policy support for the revitalization of talent.MethodsThe questionnaire was designed by discrete choice experiments, and 1208 Public Health Master’s candidates from six universities in Northeast China were investigated online via the Powercx system network survey platform in April 2024. The mixed logit model was used to analyze the job preferences of Master Public Health candidates.ResultsAll 7 attributes included in the study had statistically significant effects on the job preferences of public health master’s degree candidates (P < 0.05). They tended to choose jobs with higher monthly income (12,000 CNY/month), located in cities with good working environments and with Bianzhi when seeking jobs. There is heterogeneity in job preference, and different preference characteristics are displayed in different subgroups.ConclusionThis study revealed that public health master’s degree candidates in Northeast China were more willing to choose jobs with higher monthly incomes, working places in cities and better working environments. The way to formulate a combination of policy packages is more attractive than improving a single aspect alone. Since the preferences of each subgroup are different, the preference characteristics of the target population should also be considered when formulating the policy package to meet the challenges of talent attraction and retention.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12913-024-11810-6.Keywords: Discrete choice experiments, Healthcare, Job preferences, Master candidate, Recruitment and retention

Introduction

The shortage of health workers in rural and remote areas is a key issue for global health systems, including China [1]. According to the Outline of the National Medical and Health Service System Planning (2015–2020), there should be at least 0.83 public health workers per 1,000 permanent residents in China in 2020, while the figure in 2009 was only 0.69, indicating that China’s public health human resources are still in short supply [2, 3]. The uneven distribution of health personnel both between and within countries is a long-standing global phenomenon that has a negative impact on the equity of health services [4]. The prevention and control of COVID-19 have proven that the public health system plays a key role in responding to infectious disease outbreaks and public health emergencies, and a lack of manpower will affect the normal operation of the public health system [5, 6]. The WHO proposed that each region should formulate appropriate intervention policies according to its own actual conditions, labor market conditions and local needs [7]. A cross-national study has also demonstrated that decision-makers who tailor their talent recruitment policies to local conditions will be more effective than those who adopt standardized global approaches [8].

The issue of unbalanced development among the eastern, central, western, and northeastern regions of China, has existed for a long time and continues to expand [9, 10]. The three provinces within Northeast China—Heilongjiang, Jilin, and Liaoning—are old industrial bases and experience unfair use of health services [11]. The Northeast, an economically underdeveloped region within China, is facing the country’s most seriously aging population and brain drain [12]. Lu’s research revealed that Northeast China has a special population outflow structure; that is, most of the outflow is young people with high education, which reveals the dilemma of attracting and maintaining talent in this region [13]. In addition, the lack of salary competitiveness in the field of public health has prompted many Master of Public Health (MPH) graduates to turn to nonprofessional fields for employment, further aggravating the shortage of health human resources [14]. With the migration of young people to economically prosperous areas, public health in Northeast China is further threatened by the risk of a talent gap [15]. In 2021, among the staff at various levels of The Chinese Center for Disease Control and Prevention (China CDC), only 8.1% had a master’s degree, which is far below the worldwide average [16]. The significant shortage of professionals with master’s degrees and above in the field of public health is a challenge faced by many countries, including China [17]. What is more concerning is that public health professionals with doctoral degrees are generally inclined towards engaging in teaching and research work in first-tier cities [18], and relatively fewer choose to seek employment in frontline public health institutions. Therefore, understanding the job preferences of the Master of Public Health (MPH) community holds greater practical significance for addressing the public health professional shortage.

Current research on health workforce preferences tends to focus on medical professionals, particularly physicians, nurses, midwives, and pharmacists, while the research on other categories of health personnel and medical students is relatively scarce [19, 20]. In view of the decisive impact of the medical student population on the future health human resource structure, many developing countries, including China, have undertaken a number of studies to explore their job preferences [21, 22]. In the field of public health, some scholars have investigated the job preferences of undergraduate preventive medicine students and healthcare administration students [23, 24]. They found that there are differences in the preferences between students in different majors and different durations of study. In recent years, studies at the master’s and doctoral levels have found that policies combining economic and non-economic incentives are more effective in attracting individuals to work [16, 25]. However, these studies are limited by their small sample coverage and insufficient sample size. Although scholars have studied the preferences of public health students, there is still no large-scale empirical analysis at the master’s level [25]. Filling this research gap is crucial for identifying the job preferences of Master of Public Health (MPH), which will provide scientific support for designing policies to attract and stabilize professionals, ensure that talent strategies are in line with individual expectations [26].

Discrete choice experiments (DCEs) can provide a powerful methodological basis for the study of preferences for choice intention [27]. DCEs have been widely used in health economics research and are increasingly being used to inform health workforce policies in developing countries [28]. A study in Iran revealed that nurses are willing to forgo part of their salary in exchange for better working conditions [29]. Another study conducted among medical students in Ethiopia suggests that policymakers should consider the heterogeneity of different groups to maximize incentives for students to work in rural areas [30]. This study used DCEs to investigate the job preferences and career development needs of Master of Public Health (MPH) candidates in Northeast China and puts forward suggestions to formulate policy interventions to attract and retain talent, provide a scientific basis for effective strategies to stabilize health talent teams, and provide a reference for the training of public health talent in colleges and universities.

Methods

Sample

This study was conducted in April 2024 using the Powercx system network survey platform for data collection. According to the enrollment of master’s programs at each university, two universities, Harbin Medical University and Mudanjiang Medical University in Heilongjiang Province, Jilin University and Changchun University of Chinese Medicine in Jilin Province, China Medical University and Dalian Medical University in Liaoning Province, were selected from each province in Northeast China to carry out the survey. Sample size of the questionnaire survey at each university

Lancsar and Louviere proposed that at least 20 respondents were required for each questionnaire version, while the questionnaire in this study was divided into two versions, so at least 40 respondents were required [31]. Pearmain et al. suggested that a sample size of more than 100 in DCEs would be sufficient to provide a basis for preference data modeling [32]. According to the rule of thumb proposed by Johnson and Orme, the minimum sample size required for this study was 125 [33, 34]. This formula is given by , where is a fixed constant, is the number of choice sets, is the maximum number of levels among all attributes, and is the number of alternative options for each question [35]. In summary, at least 40 samples from each university and 125 samples from two schools in each province were investigated in this study, and the total sample size was greater than 375. Considering the need for subsequent subgroup analysis, the sample size should be appropriately expanded.

We contacted graduate counselors and graduate advisors at target universities to help graduate students fill out online questionnaire links and trained investigators to obtain sample data through face-to-face scanning methods. A total of 1208 questionnaires were collected, of which 74 did not pass the DCE quality test, 5 were excluded because the subject’s major did not match the survey objectives, and 2 were not included in the analysis because the response time was less than 60 s. There were 1127 valid questionnaires, and the valid response rate was 93.29%.

Discrete choice experiments

We investigated the job preferences of full-time Master of Public Health (MPH) candidates in Northeast China through discrete choice experiments. The DCE is a research method for the quantitative measurement of the declarative preferences of survey subjects and has been developed into the most powerful tool for studying individual choice behavior in the field of health economics [18]. In the late 1980s, several researchers began to apply DCEs to the field of health human resources [28]. Its theoretical basis is as follows: (1) by combining different levels of each attribute, different alternatives can be described; (2) the respondents’ evaluation of the scheme depends on the level value of each attribute of the alternative scheme [36]; and (3) based on the potential utility function (utility maximization), respondents will choose the scheme that brings them the most utility [27]. A DCE combines different attributes and levels that affect the choice into an array of alternatives, allowing respondents to weigh the options among different options and infer their choice preferences based on the importance they attach to different attributes and their preferences.

In addition to the DCE questions, the questionnaire also covers respondents’ sociodemographic characteristics, professional direction, career planning, etc. Before the DCE question, there was a ranking task for the 7 attributes included in the study, which served to further validate the accuracy of the DCE model estimates.

Statistical analysis

Excel 2021 was used to establish the database, Stata 17.1 was used for data sorting and statistical analysis, descriptive statistical methods were used for demographic characteristics. There are multiple methods for analyzing DCE data, such as the conditional logit (Clogit), mixed logit (MIXL), and latent-class model (LCM). The Clogit model is the most widely used and easily interpretable, with most other commonly used DCE analysis methods being variations of it [37]. However, it has fundamental limitations in observing preference heterogeneity. The MIXL, also called the “random-parameters logit”(RPL), uses random coefficients to address the limitation of preference heterogeneity [38]. The LCM is another heterogeneity model that explains heterogeneity at the group level [39]. A key assumption of the LCM is local independence, which may not hold for all datasets. When the data distribution does not conform to the model’s assumptions, it can lead to a poor model fit [40]. In this study, we used the Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC) and Akaike Information Criterion (AIC) to assess the fit of the models. We compared the Clogit and MIXL methods and selected the one with the better fit for result interpretation.

Monthly income was set as a continuous variable to calculate willingness to pay (WTP), and the remaining 6 attributes were converted into dummy variables at the set level to be included in the analysis. The relative importance (RI) of attributes was calculated to explore the influence of each attribute on the job preferences of public health master’s degree holders. We also calculated the predicted uptake rates of preferences for job postings under various potential policy scenarios, showing the probability of individuals opting to forgo city employment in favor of working in townships or villages. Results

Descriptive statistics

Among the surveyed respondents, 252 were male (22.36%), and 875 were female (77.64%), reflecting a demographic distribution similar to that observed in another study of postgraduate students in the field of public health in China [17]. The average age was 25.1 ± 3.1 years, ranging from 19 to 46 years. Overall, 441 respondents (39.13%) originated from the three provinces within Northeast China. There were 486 (43.12%) residing primarily in cities, 254 (22.54%) in counties, and 387 (34.34%) in townships or villages. The majority reported being unmarried (94.76%), having no work experience (75.60%), and intending to pursue a career related to their current major after graduation (81.10%), with personal choice playing the largest role in their career selection (72.32%). Results of mixed logit estimates and WTP

The data were fitted using both the conditional logit model and the mixed logit model, yielding BIC values of 15310.8 and 13855.3, respectively. Since a lower BIC value indicates a better fit, the mixed logit model was ultimately selected for data analysis. Table 5 presents the results of the mixed logit model. The coefficient for the monthly income attribute is measured in CNY, reflecting the change in utility associated with each additional CNY of monthly income. Coefficients for other attribute levels indicate the changes in utility relative to their reference levels. The results showed that, in addition to providing subsidies and providing dormitories under housing conditions, which were not statistically significant, all the other attribute levels were significant (P < 0.05). According to the magnitude of the regression coefficients, the greatest utility for Master Public Health candidates in Northeast China was derived from a job located in a city (β = 1.402), followed by a good work environment (β = 1.227), provided Bianzhi (β = 1.095), many career development or training opportunities (β = 0.368), and a minimum of 2 years of work for promotion (β = 0.342). The least preference was shown for provided housing (β = 0.197).

Relative importance of 7 attributes

All 7 attributes included in the study significantly influenced the employment choices of Master Public Health candidates in Northeast China (P < 0.05). Monthly income was the attribute with the highest relative importance, accounting for 27.6% of the total, followed by employment location (21.6%), work environment (18.9%), and Bianzhi (16.9%). There were relatively smaller proportions of career development and training opportunities (5.7%), minimum work periods for promotion (5.3%), and housing welfare (4.2%). Subgroup analysis

This study categorized respondents into subgroups based on gender, home province, usual place of residence, and whether they had an only child, and job preference analyses were conducted for each group using a mixed logit model to regress and calculate WTP. The results revealed that females placed more importance on employment location and work environment than males and were willing to forego 806.6 CNY in monthly income for a job with a good working environment. In contrast, males were more concerned about shorter promotion periods and housing provisions. Public health master’s students from Northeast China showed a stronger preference for working in cities or counties, sacrificing up to 2289.1 CNY of their monthly income to work in cities, with all other attributes ranking lower than those from non-Northeast provinces. Urban-resident Master Public Health candidates in Northeast China expressed a greater inclination toward urban employment, willing to sacrifice 3079.4 CNY more in monthly income than non-urban-origin Masters to secure a job in the city. Only children valued employment location more than nononly children did, whereas nononly children tended to prioritize provided housing or housing/rental subsidies. Figure 3 shows the details.

Simulated job preferences under various potential policy scenarios

Based on the results of the mixed logit model, we simulate job preferences under various potential policy scenarios. After improving different combinations of attribute levels, we predict the likelihood of respondents being willing to forgo their baseline city jobs for work in townships or villages. The baseline city job is defined by a monthly income of 6,000 CNY, not provided bianzhi, few career development and training opportunities, poor work environment, and 5 years of promotion. Our empirical evidence indicates that the augmentation of singular job features does not sufficiently motivate individuals to relocate to distant regions. Conversely, implementing a policy package to improve job attributes can greatly enhance job attractiveness. Under the combined strategy of ‘many career development or training opportunities and a good working environment’, the probability of choosing work in townships or villages exceeds that of choosing work in urban areas for the first time, reaching 54.8%; under the combined strategy of ‘a monthly income of 9000 CNY, provided Bianzhi, and a good working environment’, the attractiveness can increase to 86.0%. Discussion

Monthly income is the most critical job attribute considered by public health master’s degree candidates when seeking employment, a finding consistent with previous studies on Chinese medical students [41]. Income significantly influences consumption patterns and is closely related to quality of life and living standards. One fundamental motivation for job seeking lies in achieving economic independence and financial security, making an income level that meets basic living needs a prerequisite for attracting talent [42]. According to the study of Terry et al., combined with Maslow’s hierarchy of needs theory, salary level is the priority factor for medical students when choosing a career, and higher-level needs are considered only after salary requirements are met [43].

Compared to southern regions, most areas in Northeast China are economically underdeveloped, with limited fiscal capabilities. As a result, allocating limited resources to attract the most urgently needed talent may be a more feasible approach. For instance, the talent classification evaluation mechanism implemented in Heilongjiang Province in recent years is worth promoting and emulating. This policy divides talents into five categories, ranked from A to E from high to low, with each category of certified talents eligible for corresponding types of preferential service policies. But simply increasing income levels for all positions to attract employment is not feasible. Based on simulated preferences for policy scenarios, a combination of moderately increasing income levels and improving noneconomic factors that impact job attractiveness can enhance the appeal of hiring Master Public Health candidates. For example, although it’s not feasible to match the high salaries of first-tier cities, it’s still practical to raise the monthly income of some urgently needed positions to 9,000 CNY, provide Bianzhi, and enhance the working environment to attract the desired talents. Meanwhile, due to significant disparities in regional economic development, policies should be tailored to local conditions [44]. The projects such as Harbin’s “TouYan” initiative and Daqing’s “JinYan” talent program have already achieved significant results with their implementation [45]. It is also important to consider the operability and sustainability of these policies to ensure the stability and loyalty of the talent they attract.

Among all noneconomic attributes, work environment and employment location are the two most preferred attributes when public health master’s candidates seek employment. Subgroup analysis revealed that female public health master’s degree candidates show a stronger preference for jobs with good working environments. The work environment encompasses both the physical surroundings of the workplace and the organizational climate, including management systems and interpersonal relationships. According to Herzberg’s two-factor theory, enhancing the work environment can prevent dissatisfaction among health personnel, mitigate burnout, and improve job performance [46]. The Northeast Region is endowed with abundant natural resources and a rich cultural heritage. Benefiting from the comprehensive revitalization strategy and tourism development, the region enhances both per capita disposable income for local residents and its external promotion and influence [47].

Subgroup analysis indicates that Master Public Health candidates from Northeast China prefer city or county employment locations, aligning with the current trend of population outflow from the region [15]. However, during the in-depth interviews, we also found that Master Public Health candidates residing in the Northeast Region may consider staying and developing locally due to the unique characteristics of the area, such as a dry climate, winter heating, a tight emotional connection fostered by cultural customs, and less academic pressure on their children’s educational advancement. Therefore, healthcare institutions in the Northeast Region should leverage their soft environmental advantages, particularly by emphasizing regional features and humanistic care, to enhance their appeal. Master Public Health candidates from nonurban backgrounds express much less preference for city-based employment locations than do those from urban origins. As such, primary healthcare institutions in more remote locations within the Northeast Region should focus on recruiting individuals from local or nearby towns and villages.

Bianzhi is a distinctive personnel management mechanism in China that is fundamentally designed to construct a streamlined and efficient public service system under the leadership of the state and the Communist Party [48]. This system delineates the functions of public sectors and establishes corresponding administrative bodies, ultimately determining the types of positions and the number of personnel for each institution [49]. Securing a job with Bianzhi means relative career stability and a significantly reduced risk of layoffs, hence the term “iron rice bowl” for such positions [50]. Influenced by the deeply ingrained official-centric ideology, working in a post with Bianzhi in state public service organizations is also regarded as prestigious [51].

Our study revealed that Bianzhi status significantly influences the job preferences of Master of Public Health candidates. This is not only because it is highly sought after in China but also because many job positions related to public health are managed under the Bianzhi system, especially in the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC), National Health Commission (NHC), research and educational establishments, and medical institutions. In recent years, to eliminate the “administrative” drawbacks of traditional management systems, the Bianzhi system has been continuously adjusted. The criteria for quotas should be used judiciously, taking into account actual factors such as region and economy, adjusting dynamically and supplementing personnel quotas as needed after balancing efficiency and equity. It is also important to set up a fair and objective system for talent evaluation and performance assessment to prevent overstaffing and inefficiency [52].

Career development or training opportunities, along with a work period for promotion, are noneconomic incentives that carry the nature of economic incentives. Good career development or training opportunities and shorter promotion periods can lead to further improvements in income and welfare [53]. Research indicates that job stability and benefits associated with long-term employment are also significant considerations for healthcare personnel seeking employment [54]. According to our study’s findings, healthcare institutions should develop scientifically sound promotion pathways and title evaluation systems, providing ample training opportunities for staff to facilitate their rapid growth and self-improvement. This, in turn, enhances a sense of achievement at work, making them more appealing employers for public health master’s candidates [55].

In the main effect model, the housing benefit attribute showed statistical significance only when housing was provided. However, subgroup analysis revealed that males placed greater emphasis on promotion timelines and housing provisions than females did. Graduate students at the master’s level are generally of legal marriage age, and at this stage, they start contemplating marriage. Typically, Chinese men at this life stage are expected to prepare a matrimonial home and dowry [56], potentially explaining why male Master Public Health candidates place more importance on housing provision. Scenario probability analysis indicates that altering career development opportunities, training opportunities, promotion timelines, or housing benefits individually is insufficient to effectively attract Master Public Health candidates to rural or remote areas for employment. The Northeast itself has relatively low housing prices, and the talent shortage results in less intense job market competition. Compared to the economically developed southern regions, there is more room to leverage these unique advantages. Therefore, a policy package approach that addresses multiple noneconomic factors simultaneously may alleviate the current difficulties in attracting and retaining talent [57].

Limitation

several limitations should be acknowledged. First, our study focused on student preferences and did not consider the preferences of employed health workers. This means that it can guide recruitment but falls short in offering retention strategies. Second, due to the limitations of DCEs, we were unable to encompass all factors that could sway the preferences of public health master’s students. Therefore, some latent factors may have been neglected. Third, compared with face-to-face surveys, online data collection methods could affect data quality. We addressed this issue by alleviating the response burden and implementing stringent data exclusion standards. In addition, this study does not focus on areas with more better economic conditions, and as a result, our recommendations may not be as applicable or effective in those contexts.

Conclusion

Our study revealed that public health master’s degree candidates in Northeast China prefer jobs with higher monthly incomes, located in city areas, offering good working environments, and coming w ith Bianzhi. Preferences vary across different subgroups, indicating the necessity of tailoring policy packages to the characteristics of targeted populations. The findings of this study may provide valuable evidence for policy makers in the Northeast China and hold significant implications for less economically developed areas or developing countries facing workforce challenges. This research can guide future studies, which require further qualitative research, such as in-depth interviews, to explore the specific causes behind the job preferences of Master Public Health candidates. Surveying the preferences of working health professionals employed in health institutions after graduation and conducting comparative studies could also yield more persuasive results. Additionally, carrying out surveys in both economically developed and less developed areas for comparative research could provide an avenue for future exploration.

Combined inhibition of MEK and FOXO1 synergistically suppresses MDA-MB-231 cell proliferation with reduced LOXL4
Authors: Tetsuro Kamiya, Asumi Kanaya, Tomohiro Otsuka, Hirokazu Hara
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) is characterized by its aggressive phenotype and limited therapeutic options. FOXO1, a member of the O-class of forkhead transcription factors, has been implicated in various oncogenic processes, yet its specific role in TNBC remains to be fully elucidated. This study aimed to investigate the effects of the FOXO1-selective inhibitor, AS1842856, on the proliferation of the human TNBC cell line MDA-MB-231 and to identify the underlying molecular mechanisms. Treatment with AS1842856 significantly suppressed the proliferation of MDA-MB-231 cells in a dose-dependent manner. Additionally, AS1842856 suppressed the expression of lysyl oxidase-like 4 (LOXL4). Given LOXL4 is known to be involved in extracellular matrix remodeling and is frequently associated with cancer progression and metastasis, AS8142856 has been considered to suppress MDA-MB-231 cell migration. Interestingly, however, the knockdown of FOXO1 did not lead to a significant reduction in LOXL4 expression levels, suggesting that AS1842856 suppresses LOXL4 expression through FOXO1-independent mechanisms. We found that AS1842856-mediated ERK activation and intracellular reactive oxygen species are crucial in reducing LOXL4. Furthermore, the combined action of pharmacological inhibition of ERK pathways and AS1842856 suppressed MDA-MB-231 cell migration. These findings demonstrate that the ERK-LOXL4 signaling axis may play a critical role in the proliferation of TNBC cells. In conclusion, targeting the LOXL4 and ERK pathways pharmacologically could provide a novel therapeutic strategy for the treatment of aggressive breast cancers.

Introduction

Breast cancer is the most common cancer among women and is classified into four subtypes based on molecular characteristics.(1) Among these, triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC), which lacks expression of hormone receptors and HER2 receptors, accounts for approximately 15% of all breast cancers.(2) Compared to other subtypes, TNBC is known to have higher cell proliferation capacity, leading to poor prognosis due to distant metastasis.(3) Furthermore, given the limited treatment options for TNBC, developing new therapeutic agents based on an understanding of the nature of metastasis and its molecular mechanisms is highly desirable.(4,5)

Forkhead box O1 (FOXO1) is a transcription factor that regulates fundamental cellular functions such as differentiation, proliferation, and apoptosis through insulin signaling. In gastric cancer, activated FOXO1 has been reported to contribute to tumor growth and metastasis.(6) In breast cancer, FOXO1 promotes tumor growth by suppressing p53.(7) Furthermore, our laboratory has reported that cell migration is suppressed in MDA-MB-231 cells, a TNBC cell line, following FOXO1 knockdown.(8)

The lysyl oxidase (LOX) family consists of five enzymes: LOX and LOX-like 1–4 (LOXL1–4). These are secreted copper-dependent amine oxidases. These enzymes are known to catalyze cross-linking of the extracellular matrix, thereby contributing to tissue fibrosis.(9–11) Furthermore, increased expression of these enzymes has been shown to promote cancer malignancy by enhancing cancer cell proliferation, invasion, metastasis, and angiogenesis.(12) Conversely, LOXL4 knockdown in TNBC has been reported to promote tumor growth and lung metastasis.(13) We have also found that transforming growth factor-β1 (TGF-β1), which is involved in tumor growth, reduces LOXL4 expression in MDA-MB-231 cells.(14) Therefore, the detailed relationship between LOX expression abnormalities and cancer malignancy remains unclear.

In this study, treatment of MDA-MB-231 cells with the FOXO1 inhibitor AS1842856 resulted in reduced LOXL4 mRNA expression and protein levels, raising concerns about its potential contribution to cancer malignancy. Therefore, this study evaluated the utility of AS1842856 as a cell migration inhibitor by analyzing the mechanism of LOXL4 expression reduction induced by AS1842856 in MDA-MB-231 cells.

Materials and Methods

Reagents

DMEM (D6429) was purchased from Sigma Aldrich Co. (St. Louis, MO). Bovine serum albumin (BSA) was purchased from Fujifilm Wako Pure Chemicals Industries, Ltd. (Osaka, Japan). Anti-FOXO1 (#2880), anti-phospho-MEK (#9121), anti-phospho-ERK (#9106), anti-MEK (#9122), and anti-ERK (#4695) antibodies were purchased from Cell Signaling Technology (Danvers, MA). Anti-LOXL4 (sc-374121) and anti-Actin (sc-47778) antibodies were purchased from Santa Cruz Biotechnology, Inc. (Dallas, TX). TRI Reagent was purchased from Molecular Research Center Inc. (Cincinnati, OH). Horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-conjugated anti-rabbit IgG (A6154) and anti-mouse IgG (A4416) were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich Co.

Cell culture

Human breast cancer MDA-MB-231 cells (ATCC) were cultured in DMEM containing 10% (v/v) fetal calf serum (FCS), 100 units/ml penicillin, and 100 ‍μg/ml streptomycin. The cells were maintained at 37°C in a humidified 5% CO2 incubator.

Cell proliferation assay

MDA-MB-231 cells were seeded in 96-well plates (4 × 103 cells/well) and cultured overnight. The following day, the medium was replaced with fresh medium, and the cells were treated with 20 nM AS1842856 for 24 ‍h. Cell proliferation was then assessed using an MTT assay, as described in our previous report.(15) The absorbance of MTT formazan was measured at 590 ‍nm with a reference wavelength of 655 ‍nm.

Wound healing assay

MDA-MB-231 cells were seeded in a 35 ‍mm dish (5 × 105 cells/dish) and cultured overnight. The next day, a wound field was prepared using a pipette tip. Culture medium was then replaced with fresh medium followed by treatment with 20 nM AS1842856. The wound field 24 ‍h after AS1842856 treatment was examined under a microscope. Wound area was determined using ImageJ wound healing tool.

siRNA transfection

MDA-MB-231 cells were seeded in a 35 ‍mm dish (2 × 105 cells/dish) and cultured overnight. The next day, the cells were transfected with FOXO1 siRNA or negative siRNA. At 24 ‍h after transfection, the medium was changed to remove siRNA and cultured for 24 ‍h.

Reverse transcription-quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR) analysis

The cells were seeded in a 35-mm dish (3 × 105 cells/dish) and cultured overnight. The next day, the culture medium was replaced with serum-free DMEM, and the cells were treated appropriately. After treatment, the cells were washed with ice-cold PBS and total RNA was extracted with 1 ‍ml of TRI Reagent (Molecular Research Center Inc.). The synthesis of cDNA was performed using the ReverTra AceTM qPCR RT kit (Toyobo, Osaka, Japan), and qPCR was carried out with ThunderbirdTM Next SYBR qPCR Mix (Toyobo). The primer sequences used in qPCR are shown in Table 1. The data was normalized using the ΔΔCq method.

Western blotting

The cells were seeded in a 35 ‍mm dish (3 × 105 cells/dish) and cultured overnight. The next day, the culture medium was replaced with serum-free DMEM, and the cells were treated appropriately. Whole cell extracts and nuclear extracts were prepared as described in our previous report.(16,17) The protein concentrations of extracts were estimated with protein assay reagent. Ten μg of protein were boiled with SDS sample buffer for 5 ‍min and separated by SDS-PAGE on 10% or 12% (w/v) polyacrylamide gels. After electrophoretic transfer onto PVDF membranes, nonspecific binding sites were blocked with 5% skim milk in PBS containing 0.1% Tween 20 (PBST) or 1% BSA in PBS. Membranes were incubated with specific primary antibodies (1:1,000) overnight. After incubation, the membranes were washed three times with PBST and incubated with HRP-conjugated goat anti-rabbit (1:5,000) or anti-mouse (1:5,000) IgG for 1 ‍h. The membranes were washed three times with PBST, and the bands were detected using SuperSignalTM West Pico Plus (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA) and imaged using the ChemiDoc Touch Imaging System (BioRad, Hercules, CA).

Detection of intracellular reactive oxygen species (ROS)

MDA-MB-231 cells were seeded in 35 ‍mm dish (3 × 105 cells/dish) and cultured overnight. The next day, the culture medium was replaced with serum-free DMEM containing metal ions and the cells were cultured for 24 ‍h. The cells were then stained with dihydroethidium (DHE, 10 ‍μM) for 30 ‍min. The fluorescence was detected at an excitation wavelength of 488 ‍nm and an emission wavelength of 580 ‍nm using a confocal laser fluorescence microscope (LSM 700; Zeiss, Oberkochen, Germany).

Statistical analysis

The data are expressed as the means ± SE of three to six independent experiments. Statistical evaluations of the data were performed using Student’s t test or ANOVA followed by post-hoc Bonferroni tests. A p value less than 0.05 was considered to be significant.

Results

FOXO1 inhibitor, AS1842856, decreased LOXL4 expression in FOXO1-independent manner

In basal-like breast cancer cells, FOXO1 inhibitors suppress colony formation, suggesting their potential for cancer therapy.(18) Therefore, we analyzed the effect of the FOXO1 inhibitor AS1842856 on MDA-MB-231 cell proliferation and migration. The results showed that AS1842856 treatment suppressed cell proliferation (Fig. 1A) and migration (Fig. 1B). Since LOX has been reported to inhibit prostate cancer cell migration both in vitro and in vivo,(19) we analyzed the effect of AS1842856 on LOX expression. The results showed a significant decrease in LOXL4 mRNA (Fig. 1C). However, no changes were observed in the expression of other LOX family mRNA. Furthermore, AS1842856 decreased LOXL4 mRNA and protein expression in its dose-dependent manner (Fig. 1D and E). To investigate the mechanism by which AS1842856 reduces LOXL4 expression, we focused on the involvement of FOXO1 inhibition. Knockdown of FOXO1 significantly reduced both FOXO1 mRNA and protein levels, but no change was observed in LOXL4 mRNA or protein expression (Fig. 2). These results suggest that a FOXO1-independent mechanism is involved in the reduction of LOXL4 expression by AS1842856.

Involvement of MEK/ERK pathways in AS1842856-induced LOXL4 reduction

Activation of the mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) signaling pathway promotes TNBC progression,(20) and a correlation between LOX expression and phospho-ERK expression has been observed in thyroid cancer cells.(21) Therefore, we investigated the involvement of the MAPK pathway in the reduction of LOXL4 expression. JNK inhibitor SP600125 or the p38 inhibitor SB203580 did not suppress AS1842856-mediated LOXL4 reduction. However, MEK1/2 inhibitor, PD98059, significantly suppressed that reduction (Fig. 3A and B). We then examined the effect of AS1842856 on ERK activation. The results showed that while MEK, an upstream factor of ERK, was not activated by AS1842856, ERK activation was significantly enhanced (Fig. 3B), suggesting that ERK activation is involved in AS1842856-mediated LOXL4 reduction.

Involvement of ROS in AS1842856-induced LOXL4 reduction

Since it has been reported that ROS correlate with LOX expression,(22) we investigated whether ROS is involved in AS1842856-mediated LOXL4 reduction. The antioxidant N-acetylcysteine (NAC) significantly suppressed LOXL4 reduction (Fig. 4A and B). To further investigate the relationship between ROS and ERK pathways, it was examined the effect of AS1842856 on ROS production. AS1842856 induced intracellular ROS accumulation, and this accumulation was not suppressed in the presence of PD98059 (Fig. 4C). Furthermore, NAC did not suppress AS1842856-induced ERK activation (Fig. 4D), suggesting that AS1842856-mediated intracellular ROS accumulation occurs independently of the MEK/ERK pathway.

Co-treatment with AS1842856 and PD98059 suppressed MDA-MB-231 cell migration

FOXO1 is a key regulator in wound healing and has been reported to control TGF-β expression.(23) Furthermore, it has been well known that activation of the ERK signaling pathway promotes cell migration and invasion in breast cancer cells.(24,25) Based on these findings, we investigated the combined effect of AS1842856 and PD98059 on cell migration. The results showed that the inhibitory effect of AS1842856 on cell migration was enhanced in the presence of PD98059 (Fig. 5).

Discussion

Tumor metastasis progresses through multiple processes, including regulation of the tissue microenvironment, invasion and migration from the primary tumor, and angiogenesis.(26) Numerous molecules are involved in this process, such as adhesion molecules, proteolytic enzymes, growth factors, angiogenic factors, and chemokines.(27) Tumor metastasis and invasion are associated with poor patient prognosis, and the development of new therapies targeting these processes is highly desired.

FOXO1 is a transcription factor that regulates fundamental cellular functions such as differentiation, proliferation, and apoptosis through insulin signaling, playing a crucial role in maintaining tissue homeostasis in diseases like diabetes and cancer.(28,29) AS1842856, an inhibitor of FOXO1, is anticipated as a therapeutic agent for type 2 diabetes and diabetic wounds,(30) but its applicability to cancer treatment remains poorly understood due to limited reports. The LOX family consists of secreted enzymes involved in extracellular matrix remodeling. Increased expression of LOX enzymes contributes to cancer malignancy by enhancing cancer cell proliferation, invasion, metastasis, and angiogenesis.(12,31) In this study, treatment of MDA-MB-231 cells with AS1842856, a FOXO1 inhibitor, significantly suppressed cell proliferation and migration while markedly reducing LOXL4 mRNA and protein expression (Fig. 1). Previous reports have associated reduced LOXL4 expression with shorter overall survival in TNBC patients,(13) raising concerns that AS1842856 might promote cancer malignancy by decreasing LOXL4 expression. Although the relationship between LOXL4 and cancer remains unclear, reduced LOX expression at the primary tumor site may suppress excessive cross-linking of the extracellular matrix, potentially facilitating cancer invasion and metastasis. Therefore, it is inferred that LOX protein expression levels are strictly regulated according to the cancer type and its microenvironment, contributing to cancer malignancy. Furthermore, the failure of FOXO1 knockdown to reduce LOXL4 expression (Fig. 2) suggests that AS1842856 may decrease LOXL4 expression independently of FOXO1.

MAPK is a serine/threonine kinase that regulates cell proliferation, apoptosis, and migration functions. Three types of MAPK—ERK, JNK, and p38—have been identified and are known to be associated with various diseases such as cancer(32,33) and heart disease.(34) Our results suggested the ERK pathway is involved in the AS1842856-mediated decrease in LOXL4 expression (Fig. 3). In MDA-MB-231 cells harboring mutations in the oncogenes KRAS and BRAF, the ERK pathway is constitutively activated. Therefore, the finding that LOXL4 expression is regulated via the ERK pathway is intriguing for understanding the relationship between cancer malignancy and ERK/LOXL4. LOXL1, a member of the LOX family, has been reported to be regulated by oxidative stress,(17) prompting us to investigate ROS involvement. The antioxidant NAC suppressed the reduction in LOXL4 mRNA and LOXL4 protein expression (Fig. 4A and B), suggesting that ROS play a key role in AS1842856-mediated LOXL4 reduction. Although the relationship between ROS and ERK activation remains unclear (Fig. 4D), we propose that AS1842856 regulates LOXL4 expression through multiple pathways, with ROS-dependent mechanisms likely playing a dominant role. NAC restored LOXL4 expression without affecting ERK activation, suggesting that ERK signaling alone may not be sufficient to account for LOXL4 reduction. These results suggest that ROS-dependent and ERK-related pathways may play distinct roles in mediating the cellular effects of AS1842856. However, NAC, used as an antioxidant in this study, is deacetylated to l-cysteine and contributes to intracellular glutathione synthesis.(35,36) Therefore, the observed effects of NAC may not solely reflect its antioxidant activity but could also be partially mediated by increased intracellular cysteine levels. This represents a limitation of the present study, as the use of NAC alone does not allow us to clearly distinguish between these mechanisms. Future studies using alternative antioxidants will be required to clarify this issue.

We previously demonstrated that cell migration is suppressed in MDA-MB-231 cells with FOXO1 knockdown.(8) Furthermore, it has been reported that cell migration is inhibited in MDA-MB-231 cells treated with the MEK1/2 inhibitor U0126.(16) The results showed that the suppression of cell migration by AS1842856 was enhanced by pretreatment with the MEK1/2 inhibitor PD98059 (Fig. 5). MEK inhibitors exert antitumor effects by inhibiting MEK activation, and drugs like trametinib and vintelotinib are clinically applied as anticancer agents for malignant melanoma and other cancers.(37) Typically, these agents are intended for combination therapy with drugs like BRAF inhibitors. Reports indicate that in BRAF-mutated cancer cells, combination therapy with a BRAF inhibitor and a MEK inhibitor prolongs the duration of response.(38) Furthermore, recent studies have shown that in glioblastoma cells, combination therapy with an mTOR inhibitor and a MEK inhibitor further inhibits cell proliferation.(39) In this study, we identified the potential for synergistic anticancer effects when combining the FOXO1 inhibitor AS1842856 with a MEK inhibitor. The results obtained in this study will develop new treatments for refractory cancers, including TNBC.

It has been demonstrated that the FOXO1 inhibitor AS1842856 regulates LOXL4 expression through two mechanisms: ERK activation and ROS production. Furthermore, MEK inhibitors were shown to suppress the reduction in LOXL4 expression induced by AS1842856 and to enhance its cell migration inhibitory effect. Therefore, the combination of the FOXO1 inhibitor AS1842856 and a MEK inhibitor is expected to become a novel therapeutic approach for TNBC.

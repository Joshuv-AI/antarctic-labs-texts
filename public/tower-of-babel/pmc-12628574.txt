What factors affect women’s preferences for the mode of birth? A cross-sectional quantitative study based on the generalized resistance resources framework of salutogenesis
Authors: Yuting Li, Cairu Yuan, Xuerong Ran, Lili Zhou, Jinguo Zhai
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundWith evolving reproductive policies and socioeconomic development in China, women's birth preferences and influencing factors have changed. Guided by the theory of salutogenesis, this study aimed to identify characteristics associated with different delivery preferences and to explore influencing factors, providing evidence to support the development of midwifery clinics.MethodsIn this cross-sectional study, 462 mothers were recruited from the postnatal ward of a tertiary maternal and child health hospital between January and April 2025. The participants completed structured questionnaires on the basis of the generalized resistance resources framework of salutogenesis.ResultsBinary logistic regression analysis revealed that key factors within the generalized resistance resources framework significantly influenced women’s preferences for the mode of delivery. Physiological: Women with two (OR = 4.936, 95% CI: 1.788–13.629) or three or more prior pregnancies (OR = 7.316, 95% CI: 2.326–23.008) or a history of cesarean birth (OR = 6.923, 95% CI: 2.029–23.623) were more likely to prefer cesarean birth; those with a history of vaginal birth preferred vaginal birth (OR = 0.084, 95% CI: 0.030–0.237). Emotional: Emotional instability reduced the preference for cesarean section (OR = 0.737, 95% CI: 0.575–0.945). Interpersonal-relational: Spousal support for vaginal birth (OR = 0.059, 95% CI: 0.012–0.292) or a lack of explicit advice (OR = 0.207, 95% CI: 0.054–0.792) reduced cesarean preference.ConclusionsAt the current stage, childbirth preferences among pregnant women are shifting. Personalized prenatal education combined with partner involvement serves as a key strategy for maternal health interventions, while focusing on individual psychological characteristics and social support structures can effectively enhance pregnant women's confidence in vaginal birth.Keywords: Childbirth modes, Cesarean section, Vaginal birth, Salutogenesis, Generalized resistance resources

Introduction

Women's preferences for mode of birth are influenced by personal, family and social factors [1, 2]. The key influencing factors include family support, socioeconomic status, the availability of social resources, and accessibility to medical services [3, 4]. Cultural traditions and prevailing social norms also substantially shape women's decisions [5, 6]. In East Asia, women's reproductive autonomy has undergone profound shifts in recent years due to increasing gender awareness and fertility-related policy reforms [7–9].

China has experienced a steady decline in fertility rates since 2016 [10]. Major policy shifts from the one-child policy to the universal two-child policy have significantly shaped national cesarean section rates [7]. Against this background, Guangdong Province reported a birth rate of 8.89‰ in 2024, maintaining its position as the province with the highest number of births in China for seven consecutive years [11]. This unique situation is closely linked to the region's relatively advanced medical and economic conditions, as well as the deeply rooted patrilineal clan-based cultural tradition of lineage continuation [12]. Meanwhile, Guangdong has actively promoted the development of midwifery clinics and specialized education in recent years. The Midwifery Branch of the Guangdong Nurses Association was established in 2019 as a pivotal initiative to strengthen perinatal care systems [13]. This entity has since been institutionally upgraded and officially renamed The Greater Bay Area of Midwifery Alliance. Concurrently, The Greater Bay Area Academy of Midwifery founded by Dr. Jinguo Zhai, has assumed responsibility for standardizing midwifery training protocols and competency certification processes [14]. Therefore, it is essential that antenatal care remains updated and fully understand the factors influencing women's reproductive decisions at this stage. This understanding is key to designing responsive perinatal healthcare services and improving maternal and neonatal outcomes.

Strengthening antenatal care to align with respect to women's birth preferences in obstetric outpatient departments has positive implications [15]. However, despite significant reductions in perinatal mortality rates and globally recognized advancements in maternal and newborn safety initiatives, substantial challenges persist in perinatal health education within certain regions of China [16, 17]. Studies have shown that midwifery outpatient services encounter challenges such as low patient turnout and a mismatch between educational offerings and women's actual needs [18]. Experienced midwives emphasize the importance of initiating health education for pregnant women and their families early in pregnancy, which may have an impact on women's decisions regarding their mode of childbirth [19]. Thus, improving the coverage and specificity of health education—particularly by adopting more diversified, flexible, and continuous educational approaches to guide pregnant and postpartum women in terms of their delivery method preferences—remains an urgent priority [20, 21].

Proposed by Antonovsky [22], salutogenesis diverges from traditional pathogenesis by focusing on how health is generated and maintained rather than solely on disease prevention and treatment. This perspective aligns with the principles of perinatal care, which recognize pregnancy and childbirth as natural physiological processes. By fostering a more rational and positive understanding of childbirth, it may help women make more informed and appropriate choices regarding their mode of birth [23]. This theory has demonstrated successful applications in fields such as prenatal education and perinatal psychological research [24, 25].

Generalized Resistance Resources (GRRs) and Sense of Coherence (SOC) are two central interdependent constructs of salutogenesis. GRRs refer to the resources that enable individuals to effectively cope with stressors, and are critical to the development of a strong sense of coherence. These resources are multidimensional constructs, originally defined by Antonovsky [26] as encompassing eight domains: physical, biochemical, artifactual-material, cognitive, emotional, valuative-attitudinal, interpersonal-relational, and macro sociocultural. These resources operate across diverse levels of social units, ranging from individuals and families to communities and societal structures.

SOC represents an individual's capacity to mobilize resources for health promotion, encompasses three fundamental dimensions: comprehensibility, manageability, and meaningfulness [27]. Antonovsky's pioneering work laid the foundation for two validated measurement instruments—the 29-item and 13-item SOC questionnaires—which have since been implemented across 51 countries and translated into 51 languages [28]. Extensive empirical research has demonstrated the broad applicability of SOC across diverse demographic groups [29, 30], with interdisciplinary applications extending into healthcare systems [31], public policy formulation [32], and environmental health studies [33].

SOC has also demonstrated extensive applications in maternal and child healthcare. An Australian cohort study revealed that elevated SOC levels in childbearing women correlated significantly with positive perinatal outcomes, characterized by stress-resilient behaviors and infant-centric maternal attitudes [34]. Most recently, Li et al. [25] advanced this area by developing and psychometrically validating the Childbearing SOC Scale, thereby providing a standardized and reliable instrument for use in gestational health research.

Salutogenic theory posits that individuals with sufficient GRRs are more likely to stimulate a strong SOC when confronted with stressors, thereby enhancing their capacity to overcome challenges and maintain well-being [35]. Compared with a low SOC, a high SOC in pregnant women is associated with half the likelihood of cesarean sections (CS) [36]. These findings necessitate in-depth investigations into how GRRs influence women's preferences regarding delivery modes. Through strategic mobilization of psychosocial resources, GRRs can optimize individuals' responses to perinatal stressors, ultimately sustaining health outcomes for both pregnant and postpartum women.

A growing number of research has examined the factors influencing birth preferences across different countries and populations. Studies have shown that, 46% of women with high income in western North Carolina of the United Stated preferred repeat CS, compared with 27.3% of those with lower socioeconomic status [37, 38]. Similarly, in Pakistan, a middle-income country, only 19.3% of women reported such a preference [39]. Socio-cultural factors such as religious beliefs [40], fear of community judgment, and the perception of CS as "unnatural" [41, 42] also influence childbirth decision-making processes, and may even lead women to refuse CS when medically indicated. Psychological aspects add another dimension. A German study using the HEXACO Personality Inventory Revised questionnaire found that women who underwent CS on maternal request were less open-minded and less extroverted [43]. Current prenatal care and health education programs remain insufficient in managing these complex factors. This limitation primarily stems from the absence of a cohesive theoretical framework that can systematically analyze and synthesize biological, psychological, and sociocultural determinants of childbirth preferences.

This study represents the first application of the GRRs framework of salutogenic theory with a cross-sectional design. By exploring women's preferences regarding delivery methods and the associated influencing factors, this research offers a novel perspective for future studies on maternal health. Through the integration of theoretical analysis and empirical data, this study presents new insights into the underlying mechanisms of delivery preferences. Moreover, it puts forward practical recommendations to enhance the quality and responsiveness of perinatal care in the contemporary context.

Methods

Participants and sample size

This study was conducted in Dongguan city, Guangdong province, China. Located in the Pearl River Delta, Dongguan was named for its geographical location east of Guangzhou and its historical production of Guancao (a type of sedge grass). As of 2024, Dongguan has a permanent population of 10.5708 million, with a natural population growth rate of 8.09‰ and an urbanization rate of 93.33% [44].

Participants were recruited from the largest maternal and child healthcare hospital in Dongguan, which recorded 14,443 deliveries in 2024, representing the highest number in the region [45]. From January to April 2025, purposive sampling was employed to recruit postpartum women who delivered in the inpatient unit. Inclusion criteria: pregnant women who had registered and attended regular antenatal care at the hospital. Exclusion criteria: age < 18 years; CS with medical indications; presence of cognitive or psychiatric disorders; and stillborn or neonatal death.

This study adopted a cross-sectional design, utilizing a questionnaire based on the GRRs framework of salutogenic theory and the condensed personality scale from the China Family Panel Studies (CFPS) for data collection. According to the sample size estimation criteria for cross-sectional studies, the required sample size should be at least 5–10 times the number of questionnaire items [46]. The questionnaire in this study comprises 40 items (25 related to childbirth mode preference and 15 from the CFPS short version personality scale, see 2.3.2 for details). To ensure robustness, the upper limit of this range (10 times the number of items) was chosen, resulting in a theoretical minimum of 400 valid responses. Given that 10% of the questionnaires were invalid during the questionnaire collection process, a sample size of at least 445 was needed.

Quality control

To ensure that the questionnaire was clear and comprehensible, a pilot survey was conducted with 40 postpartum women. On the basis of their feedback, the research team refined the questionnaire by adjusting the order of the questions and adding necessary explanations in the form of annotations. Subsequently, formal data collection was carried out via the Wenjuanxing application (www.wjx.cn). The system was configured to prevent participants from submitting incomplete questionnaires. In this study, questionnaires were distributed in maternity wards prior to discharge. The rationale for choosing this time point is twofold. First, it allows for a comprehensive assessment of factors that may have influenced maternal childbirth preferences throughout pregnancy. Second, as women have already given birth, concerns about potential medical discrimination or interference by health care providers in their childbirth choices have been removed. This increases the objectivity and reliability of the data.

We provided standardized training for three questionnaire administrators, all of whom were obstetric nurses with childbirth experience and previous research training. The training aimed to familiarize them with the questionnaire content and enable them to provide scientifically standardized explanations of its purpose and significance. Additionally, they received training on research relevance, data confidentiality, participants' rights, and informed consent. To increase the accuracy and reliability of the questionnaire responses, they were also instructed to guide participants through each section during completion, ensuring clear understanding and proper interpretation.

Research tools

Questionnaire on factors influencing women's childbirth mode preferences on the basis of the GRRs framework of salutogenesis

The questionnaire comprises two parts. Part A was developed under the guidance of the GRRs framework from salutogenic theory. Based on the formal definitions of the eight characteristic dimensions of GRRs, the research team established corresponding domains: physical, encompassing obstetric history and the mode of conception for the current pregnancy; biochemical, including blood type, maternal age, chronic illnesses, and pregnancy-related complications; artificial material, covering economic status, health insurance coverage, and place of residence; cognitive, relating to educational attainment and occupation; valative-attitudinal, referring to ethnicity and religious beliefs; and interpersonal-relational, focusing on family support and social support networks. These dimensions were primarily assessed via manifest variables that captured objective factual information rather than scale-based psychometric measures. Consequently, traditional content validity analysis was not applicable.

Therefore, a team of five experts was assembled to assess the content of the questionnaire. Each expert possessed more than 30 years of clinical experience, with professional backgrounds encompassing key areas of obstetric nursing: (1) Certified Clinic Midwife, specializing in perinatal health management and clinical counseling; (2) International psychological counsellor qualifications, specializing in prenatal anxiety intervention, postnatal depression cross-cultural psychological support, etc.; (3) Specialist Nurses, obstetrics, leading in clinical service improvement; and (4) Associate Professor (PhD) of Midwifery at the University, leading several perinatal health behavior projects. This multidisciplinary panel contributed to ensuring the clinical relevance, administrative standardization, and scientific rigor of the questionnaire.

For the cognitive dimension, relevant research [47] was consulted to develop items assessing knowledge of childbirth modes, and six items were ultimately identified. A 3-point Likert scale from 0 to 2 was used for scoring. It was assumed that a score of 0 to 4 indicates low cognition, a score of 5 to 8 indicates average cognition, and 9 to 12 indicates high cognition. Based on the results of the preliminary presurvey, we concluded that its Cronbach's coefficient was 0.70 and KMO was 0.74, indicating good reliability and validity. The emotional dimension was measured via the short version of the personality scale of the China Family Panel Studies (CFPS) (see 2.3.2 for details).

Part B of the questionnaire comprises two multiple-choice questions, requiring participants to indicate their reasons for preferring vaginal birth (VB) or CS, with additional options provided for free-text responses.

The China Family Panel Studies (CFPS) short version personality scale

The CFPS is a national survey conducted by the Institute of Social Science Survey (ISSS) at Peking University. It collects data on the social, economic and demographic aspects of Chinese families. The questionnaire data and content are publicly available on the project's official website (http://www.isss.pku.edu.cn/cfps/). The CFPS 2018 encompasses five dimensions, namely, conscientiousness, extroversion, agreeableness, openness, and emotional instability, each measured by three items, for a total of 15 items. For scoring, we use the counting method recommended on the project website, which is based on 11 positively worded questions and uses a 5-point Likert scale. With this method, the Cronbach's alpha coefficients for the five dimensions are 0.45, 0.56, 0.52, 0.61, and 0.52, respectively. The five-factor structure shows good validity, with a CFI of 0.97, TLI of 0.95, RMSEA of 0.04 and SRMR of 0.02, indicating reliability and validity comparable to other widely used personality scales.

Statistical methods

All data entry and analysis were performed via Excel 2019 and IBM SPSS Statistics 25. Categorical variables are described as frequencies and percentages, whereas continuous variables are presented as medians and interquartile ranges. Differences in childbirth preferences between variables were analyzed using the chi-square test and the Mann‒Whitney U test. Significant variables were entered into a binary logistic regression analysis via a stepwise backward method, with mode of childbirth preference as the dependent variable. The regression results are reported as odds ratios (ORs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs), and statistical significance was set at P < 0.05.

Results

A total of 462 postpartum women who met the inclusion and exclusion criteria agreed to participate in this study. However, 46 participants were excluded due to providing identical responses to all the items on the personality scale, which compromised the validity of their questionnaires. Therefore, 416 participants were included in the final analysis, resulting in an effective response rate of 90.04%.

General information of the study participants

The participants ranged in age from 18–43 years, with a mean age of 30.370 ± 4.395 years. The majority were of Han ethnicity (395, 95.0%). More than half of the participants (258, 62.0%) had a monthly household income above ¥8,000, including 86 (20.7%) earning ¥8,000–10,000 and 172 (41.3%) earning over ¥10,000. Most participants (346, 83.2%) reported a preference for VB in late pregnancy, whereas a smaller proportion (70, 16.8%) expressed a preference for CS.

Factors related to the dimensions influencing the preference of mode of childbirth

Chi-square tests were used to further compare the eight characteristic dimensions of GRRs between participants based on their preferences for mode of childbirth. In the physiological dimension, compared with those who preferred VB, women who preferred CS were significantly more likely to have a history of only CS (44.3% vs. 2.0%), to have conceived through the use of reproductive technology (18.6% vs. 7.2%), and to have been pregnant three or more times (41.4% vs. 21.7%). Significant differences were observed between the two groups in terms of previous mode of childbirth (P < 0.001), current conception methods (P = 0.003), gravidity (P < 0.001), and parity (P = 0.006). Binary logistic regression analysis revealed that a history of previous CS was a significant predictor of a preference for CS (OR = 6.923, 95% CI 2.029–23.623). Conversely, a history of VB (OR = 0.084, 95% CI 0.030–0.237) and emotional instability (OR = 0.737, 95% CI 0.575–0.945) were negatively associated with a preference for CS. Compared with primiparous women, those who had been pregnant twice (OR = 4.936, 95% CI 1.788–13.629) or three or more times (OR = 7.316, 95% CI 2.326–23.008) were more likely to prefer CS. Additionally, the likelihood of preferring CS decreased when the spouse recommended VB (OR = 0.059, 95% CI 0.012–0.292) or provided no specific recommendation (OR = 0.207, 95% CI 0.054–0.792). Reasons why women choose VB or CS

We recorded participants' reasons for choosing the VB or the CS. Among those who preferred CS (n = 70), the top three reasons were lack of confidence in natural childbirth and fear of emergency CS conversion (7.45%), the belief that CS was medically necessary on the basis of a doctor's recommendation (6.25%), and fear of labor pain (6.00%) (see Fig. 1 for details). Among the participants who preferred VB (n = 346), the top three reasons were faster postpartum recovery (68.3%), reduced medical intervention (51.68%), and benefits for the baby's growth and development (43.51%) (Fig. 2).

Discussion

On the basis of the GRRs framework of salutogenesis, this study reveals the extensive influence of multiple factors on birth method preferences. The results indicate that an individual's physiological factors (parity, previous childbirth mode), personality (emotional instability), and interpersonal relationships (spousal advice) all play significant roles in the decision-making process regarding childbirth. Psychological characteristics and social support may function as buffering factors to help pregnant women cope with the pressures of childbirth and optimize their decision-making process. Therefore, it is crucial to consider individual psychological characteristics and social support networks in maternal health management. Personalized health education and interventions that encourage partner involvement can enhance pregnant women's scientific understanding of childbirth practices and promote informed decision-making [48].

Changes in childbirth preferences

In this study, the majority of women (83.2%) expressed a preference for VB, whereas 16.8% favored CS. This proportion differs significantly from that predicted by Betran, A. P., et al. [49], who estimated that the CS rate in East Asia could reach 63% by 2023. One possible explanation for this discrepancy is that the study region is an economically and medically advanced area of China [44, 50]. Additionally, previous studies have suggested that changes in Chinese fertility policies have contributed to making it the only country to successfully reverse the rising trend of CS rates [51]. Since the relaxation of the one-child policy in 2012 and the introduction of the three-child policy in 2021, policy adjustments have legitimized the birth of multiple children. The increasing demand for another safe childbirth among women who wish to have multiple children may have played a crucial role in the decline in CS rates. Besides, Chinese national guidelines for tertiary obstetric hospitals list the "rate of non-medically indicated CS" as a key quality control indicator (target ≤ 10%) [52]. According to the latest Chinese expert consensus, VB rather than CS is recommended when there are no maternal or fetal medical indications for surgery [53]. The local health authority also put the non-medical CS rate as an indicator for hospital appraisal [54]. In most cases, CS without a medical indication is not permitted. However, in clinical practice, healthcare providers will make the ultimate decision regarding the mode of childbirth while fully respecting the pregnant women's wishes.

Factors influencing women's childbirth mode preference

The findings from this study revealed a significant association between prior childbirth mode and women's birth preferences. Women with a prior CS demonstrated a greater preference for elective repeat CS. This observation aligns with the findings of Du et al. [7], who conducted a large-scale study across tertiary hospitals in China and reported significant linear associations among three key indicators: the overall CS rate, the proportion of multiparous women, and the rate of elective repeat CS. Furthermore, our study revealed that nearly one-fifth of women with a prior CS expressed willingness to attempt VB. This observation aligns with the findings of Liang et al. [51], whose nationwide analysis of data from 438 hospitals indicated that repeat CS continues to be the predominant mode of childbirth among women with uterine scars in contemporary China. Further studies have examined the underlying factors, suggesting that the prevailing obstetric culture in China may not yet be conducive to achieving optimal outcomes for trial of labor after cesarean section (TOLAC), including aspects such as the level of nursing support and the availability of labor anesthesia [55]. Women's concerns about uncertainty and fear of pain also play a role. Historical and cultural factors, such as deeply rooted agrarian values emphasizing foresight, risk aversion, and cautious decision-making, may further increase women's hesitation toward TOLAC [56, 57].

Conversely, women with a history of VB were more likely to refuse CS. Having experienced VB, they showed greater confidence in their ability to give birth vaginally without complications. Additionally, research suggests that oxytocin plays a role in memory consolidation by facilitating fear extinction. Long-term changes in endogenous oxytocin signaling may contribute to the reduction in fear expression in various brain targets [58, 59]. Therefore, compared with the exogenous oxytocin administered to CS women to promote uterine contraction and reduce bleeding, women with previous VB may have a lower fear of childbirth because of the naturally higher levels of oxytocin in their bodies. In conclusion, optimizing health education about TOLAC [60] and providing comprehensive information and counseling [61] may help increase women's willingness to undergo VB.

Gravidity had a statistically significant influence on women's childbirth mode preferences in this study. Compared with primigravid women, those with greater gravidity were more likely to choose CS, which aligns with previous findings [62, 63]. This may be attributed to anxiety stemming from previous adverse pregnancy experiences and the increased risk of advanced maternal age or complications as gestational age progresses. Therefore, healthcare providers should address the psychological needs of multiparous women during pregnancy management, offering targeted health education and personalized childbirth counseling to optimize decision-making and improve maternal and neonatal outcomes. Given these considerations, healthcare providers should prioritize addressing the psychological needs of multiparous women during prenatal care. Studies have demonstrated that individualized birth planning can enhance perceived social support and control during labor, reduce fear of childbirth, and increase the probability of vaginal delivery [64]. By integrating targeted health education with personalized childbirth counseling, providers may optimize maternal decision-making processes and improve both maternal and neonatal outcomes [65].

Emotional instability was negatively correlated with the preference for CS. This may be related to the influence of psychological traits on decision-making, learning, and other behavioral processes [66, 67]. Emotional instability, characterized by fluctuating emotional experiences, frequent mood changes, and emotions that are easily triggered, intense, and/or disproportionate to the given situations, has been incorporated into the affective diagnostic criteria for borderline personality disorder [68]. Research findings suggest that interventions informed by Dialectical Behavior Therapy can be effectively implemented to address a range of perinatal mental health symptoms, including borderline personality disorder, while also promoting emotion regulation and adaptive parenting behaviors [69]. Furthermore, it is recommended that healthcare professionals receive additional training and ongoing support to effectively deliver these programs [70]. Previous research has revealed significant associations between personality traits, fear of childbirth, childbirth self-efficacy, and subjective birth experiences [71]. Although CS is often perceived as a relatively controlled mode of childbirth, individuals with higher levels of emotional instability may have a heightened fear of surgery, anesthesia or postoperative recovery, leading them to prefer VB instead. Therefore, targeted psychological support and childbirth education are recommended for pregnant women with specific traits, such as emotional instability, to support more individualized decision-making. In this context, incorporating psychological and educational interventions as a mandatory component of midwifery outpatient training programs is particularly important [72].

The advice of the spouse plays a crucial role in decision making regarding the mode of childbirth. This study revealed that pregnant women tend to prefer VB when their spouses either do not explicitly recommend a specific childbirth mode or explicitly support VB. This may be attributed to the spouse's role as the primary source of support and care, whose positive attitude toward VB may help alleviate maternal anxiety and enhance confidence [73]. Additionally, studies have shown that ignoring the influence of one's spouse may lead to a lack of necessary support for pregnant women during the perinatal decision-making process, thereby causing unnecessary negative emotions and experiences [74]. So, future perinatal health education programs should also regard spouses as key educational targets requiring focused intervention. To enhance paternal participation, family-based programs should target fathers' beliefs about parenting, strengthen paternal self-efficacy, and foster mutual encouragement between parents to reinforce fathers' commitment to their parenting roles [75].

Application of the GRRs framework of salutogenesis in understanding birth preferences

This study demonstrates that personality, gravidity, previous mode of childbirth, and spousal support serve as positive indicators across multiple dimensions of the GRRs framework that influence birth preferences. Accordingly, we recommend that healthcare providers offer tailored prenatal counseling and comprehensive health education to women who exhibit emotional instability, have higher parity, or a history of CS [36]. These interventions may enhance the SOC dimensions of comprehensibility and manageability at the individual level [76]. Furthermore, educational programs should be restructured to include spouses as active participants. Spousal involvement may promote greater role engagement and strengthen the SOC dimension of meaningfulness at the family level [77].

The application of this framework provides a systematic and robust theoretical foundation for investigating birth preferences. A deeper understanding of how GRRs function as influencing factors enables healthcare providers to more effectively reinforce SOC and identify specific targets for improving maternal outcomes [27]. Future research should further explore and evaluate the applicability of this framework across diverse regions and populations.

Strengths and limitations

Guided by the theory of salutogenesis, this study comprehensively analyzes the influence of multidimensional factors on birth preferences in the current stage. However, the limitations of the study include the possibility of recall bias in self-reported data, and the current physical and mental state of the participants may affect the truthfulness and accuracy of their reports of past information. Additionally, despite the large overall sample size, the limited number of participants in certain subgroups (e.g., the assisted reproductive technology group) may affect the robustness of the findings. Future multicenter studies with stratified designs are recommended to improve the generalization of the results.

Conclusions

The results of this study highlight the importance of including personalized antenatal education and partner involvement in maternal health interventions. In addition, focusing on individual psychological characteristics and social support structures may increase women's confidence in VB. Future research should explore more personalized prenatal health education and further optimize the content of birth mode education in midwife-led clinics through comprehensive support mechanisms.

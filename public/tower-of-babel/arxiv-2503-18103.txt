In-medium nucleon-nucleon cross sections from relativistic ab initio calculations
Tianyu Wang, Hui Tong, Chencan Wang, Xiaoying Qu, Sibo Wang

Abstract
The in-medium nucleon-nucleon scattering cross section is a pivotal quantity for studying the medium effects of strong interaction, and its precise knowledge is critical for understanding the equation of state for dense matter, intermediate-energy heavy-ion collision dynamics, and related phenomena. In this work, we perform a microscopic investigation of in-medium nucleon-nucleon scattering cross sections, by utilizing the relativistic Brueckner-Hartree-Fock (RBHF) theory with the Bonn potential. The fully incorporation of both positive- and negative-energy states in the RBHF solutions allows us to determine the single-particle potentials, the effective G G matrix, and the scattering cross section uniquely. The momentum, density, and isospin dependence of the cross section for p ​ p pp , n ​ n nn , and n ​ p np scattering are studied in detail. Our results provide a solid foundation for future parameterization studies of multi-parameter dependency of total scattering cross sections.



I
Introduction

Since Yukawa’s pioneering proposal of the meson-exchange theory in 1935
Yukawa 1935
, the study of strong interactions has undergone more than nine decades of extensive development. Despite numerous groundbreaking achievements in both theoretical understanding and experimental investigations, the structural and dynamical properties of strongly interacting systems, particularly those under extreme conditions of density, temperature, and isospin asymmetry, remain at the forefront of contemporary research. These fundamental questions continue to attract significant attention across multiple disciplines, including particle physics, nuclear physics, and astrophysics.

The in-medium nucleon-nucleon (NN) scattering cross sections represent fundamental physical quantities in the study on medium effects of the strong interaction. First, as the lightest strongly interacting fermions, nucleons and their bound states constitute the predominant form of visible matter in our universe. Second, NN interactions are crucial in the low-energy regime of the strong interaction, directly connected to the non-perturbative aspects of quantum chromodynamics (QCD)
Ishii et al. 2007
. Finally, the in-medium NN scattering cross sections govern the mean free path of nucleons
Sammarruca 2008
, thereby determining the transparency and other essential transport properties of nuclear matter.

In the realm of neutron star physics, the in-medium NN scattering cross sections play a pivotal role in determining fundamental astrophysical properties through their influence on effective NN interactions, including the equation of state, mass-radius relation, and tidal deformabilities
Burgio et al. 2021
. In addition to these static characteristics, the in-medium NN scattering cross sections significantly impact the dynamical evolution of neutron stars by modulating key thermal properties, such as thermal conductivity, shear viscosity, and neutrino emissivity
Yakovlev et al. 2004
. Furthermore, extending the scope to include strange baryons, the in-medium baryon-baryon scattering cross sections involving hyperons are intimately related to the longstanding problem ”hyperon puzzle”
Sedrakian et al. 2023
;
Tong et al. 2025a
;
Tong et al. 2025b
, which remains one of the most challenging issues in contemporary nuclear astrophysics.

In the context of intermediate-energy heavy-ion collisions (HICs), the in-medium NN scattering cross sections serve as crucial fundamental parameters for theoretical frameworks and transport model simulations
Xu 2019
;
Zhang et al. 2020
;
Wolter et al. 2022
. At the microscopic level, these cross sections govern both the collision rates and angular dependence in NN collisions, thereby influencing the production mechanisms, dynamical evolution, and spatial distribution of emitted particles, as well as the emergence and characteristics of collective flow. Experimentally, the measured final-state particle multiplicities, isotopic compositions, and kinematic distributions exhibit significant sensitivity to the in-medium modifications of NN interactions. This dependence enables the extraction of essential nuclear matter properties through systematic comparisons between theoretical predictions and experimental measurements in HICs.

The accurate determination of in-medium NN scattering cross sections presents significant theoretical challenges
Cugnon et al. 1996
;
Han et al. 2022
, primarily stemming from their complex dependence on various parameters. These include not only the intrinsic properties of the colliding nucleon pair, such as relative momentum, center-of-mass motion, and isospin projections, but also the environmental conditions characterized by baryon density, temperature, and isospin asymmetry. Current theoretical studies typically incorporate medium modifications through either empirical density-dependent scaling factors applied to vacuum cross sections
Liu et al. 2001
;
Zhang et al. 2012
;
Li et al. 2011
;
Wang et al. 2020
or by employing effective nucleon masses in nuclear medium
Pandharipande and Pieper 1992
;
Li and Chen 2005
;
Persram and Gale 2002
. While these phenomenological approaches provide valuable connections to experimental data, they fall short of capturing the fundamental nature of strongly interacting systems. A more comprehensive understanding requires
ab initio
calculations that simultaneously combine realistic NN interactions with advanced many-body techniques
Machleidt 2023
;
Machleidt and Sammarruca 2024
.

The realistic NN interactions, which accurately reproduce free-space NN scattering data and two- as well as three-body bound state, can be systematically classified into three main categories based on their theoretical foundations. The first category comprises potential models constructed through operator analysis, exemplified by the Argonne V18 potential
Wiringa et al. 1995
. The second category utilizes meson-exchange models, such as the Bonn potential
Machleidt 1989
and its refined version, the CD-Bonn potential
Machleidt 2001
. The third category encompasses chiral interactions derived from chiral effective field theory, which has gained increasing attention in recent years
Entem and Machleidt 2003
;
Lu et al. 2022
. Comprehensive reviews and detailed discussions of these interaction models can be found in several authoritative references
Machleidt 1989
;
Machleidt and Entem 2011
;
Epelbaum et al. 2009
;
Machleidt and Sammarruca 2024
.

Based on realistic NN interactions, various microscopic many-body approaches have been developed to investigate in-medium NN elastic and inelastic scattering cross sections. These include the Brueckner-Hartree-Fock (BHF) theory
Schulze et al. 1997
;
Shang et al. 2020
, the Green’s function approach
Alm et al. 1994
;
Alm et al. 1995
, the variational method
Pandharipande and Pieper 1992
, the relativistic Boltzmann-Uehling-Uhlenbeck approach
Mao et al. 1994
;
Li and Li 2017
, and the relativistic Brueckner-Hartree-Fock (RBHF) theory
Li and Machleidt 1993
;
Fuchs et al. 2001
;
Sammarruca and Krastev 2006
;
Sammarruca 2014
. General agreement on several key features have been reached: the reduction of cross sections for low energy scattering in nuclear medium, their strong enhancement near the Fermi surface, and the isospin symmetry breaking to some extend. However, quantitative predictions vary depending on the extent of medium effects incorporated in each theoretical framework. The RBHF theory distinguishes itself by consistently accounting for multiple crucial effects: Pauli exclusion principle, energy-momentum dispersion relations, and relativistic dynamics
Day 1967
;
Anastasio et al. 1980
;
Shen et al. 2019
. This comprehensive treatment enables RBHF theory to successfully reproduce nuclear matter saturation properties
Brockmann and Machleidt 1990
;
Wang et al. 2021a
, a longstanding benchmark for nuclear many-body theories. The superior performance of RBHF theory can be attributed to its inherent inclusion of density-dependent three-body forces through virtual nucleon-antinucleon pair excitations, commonly known as the
Z
-diagram
Brown 1987
.

The RBHF theory is fundamentally characterized by its double iterative structure involving the effective interaction
G
matrix and single-particle potentials
Van Giai et al. 2010
. Historically, RBHF implementations were restricted to positive-energy solutions of the Dirac equation, with negative-energy state contributions incorporated through approximate treatments. This limitation hindered the rigorous determination of single-particle potentials from the
G
matrix. Recent theoretical advancements have overcome this constraint by implementing fully self-consistent RBHF calculations in the full Dirac space, explicitly including both positive- and negative-energy states
Wang et al. 2021b
;
Wang et al. 2022a
;
Tong et al. 2023
. This comprehensive approach not only provides unambiguous determination of nucleon single-particle properties but also significantly enhances the precision of
G
matrix calculations through iterative solutions of the in-medium NN scattering equation. These developments offer a promising framework for the theoretical investigation of in-medium NN scattering cross sections.

In this work, we aim to derive the in-medium NN scattering cross sections with the RBHF theory in the full Dirac space. This paper is organized as follows: Section
II
details the theoretical formalism, including the RBHF implementation in the full Dirac space and the methodology for calculating in-medium NN scattering observables. Section
III
presents and analyzes the numerical results, focusing on medium-modified phase shifts, angular distributions, and integrated cross sections. The concluding section, Section
IV
, summarizes the key findings and discusses potential future developments in this research direction.

II
Theoretical framework

II.1
The RBHF theory in the full Dirac space

The starting point of the RBHF calculations is the realistic NN interaction. Here we use the one-boson-exchange potential (OBEP)
Machleidt 1989
, which describes the NN interactions by mediating six mesons with masses below 1 GeV. Guided by symmetry principles and simplicity, the most commonly used interaction Lagrangians that coupled these meson fields
\varphi
to nucleon fields
\psi
are written as follows

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{\text{NNs}}=

\displaystyle\ g_{\text{s}}\bar{\psi}\psi\varphi^{(\text{s})},

(1a)

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{\text{NNpv}}=

\displaystyle\ -\frac{f_{\text{ps}}}{m_{\text{ps}}}\bar{\psi}\gamma^{5}\gamma^{\mu}\psi\partial_{\mu}\varphi^{(\text{ps})},

(1b)

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{\text{NNv}}=

\displaystyle\ -g_{\text{v}}\bar{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\psi\varphi^{(\text{v})}_{\mu}-\frac{f_{\text{v}}}{4M}\bar{\psi}\sigma^{\mu\nu}\psi\left(\partial_{\mu}\varphi^{(\text{v})}_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}\varphi^{(\text{v})}_{\mu}\right).

(1c)

The scalar (s) mesons
\sigma
and
\delta
are coupled to nucleon fields through the scalar coupling. For pseudoscalar (ps) mesons
\eta
and
\pi
, the pseudovector (pv) coupling is adopted, while for vector (v) mesons
\omega
and
\rho
, both the vector coupling and tensor coupling are considered. In Eq. (
1
), the isospin indexes for isovector mesons are not shown for simplicity. The
g
’s and
f
’s are coupling constants, which are nicely constrained by experimental data of NN bound and scattering states. Although chiral forces are more popular in these days, we emphasize that the OBEP is the most economical and quantitative phenomenology for describing the NN interaction
Machleidt and Entem 2011
. Bonn potentials have three different parametrizations of the OBEP, which are named by Bonn A, B, and C. The main difference among the three parametrizations lies in the cutoff parameter of the
\pi N
coupling, which leads to the weakest tensor force for Bonn A and the strongest tensor force for Bonn C
Wang et al. 2024
.

In the RBHF theory, the nucleon inside the nuclear medium is viewed as a dressed particle in consequence of its interaction with the surrounding nucleons.
The single-particle motion of nucleons in the nuclear medium is depicted by the Dirac equation

\left[\bm{\alpha}\cdot\bm{p}+\beta\left(M+\mathcal{U}\right)\right]u(\bm{p},s)=E_{\bm{p}}u(\bm{p},s),

(2)

where
\bm{\alpha}
and
\beta
are the Dirac matrices,
M
is the nucleon mass,
\bm{p}
and
E_{\bm{p}}
are the momentum and the single-particle energy, and
s
denotes the spin.
According to the translational and rotational invariance, time-reversal invariance, hermiticity, and parity conservation, the single-particle potential
\mathcal{U}
can be decomposed in its Lorentz form
Serot and Walecka 1986

\mathcal{U}(\bm{p})=U_{\text{S}}(p)+\gamma^{0}U_{0}(p)+\bm{\gamma\cdot\hat{p}}U_{\text{V}}(p).

(3)

The quantities
U_{\text{S}}(p)
,
U_{0}(p)
, and
U_{\text{V}}(p)
are the scalar potential, timelike, and spacelike parts of the vector potential respectively with
p=|\bm{p}|
the magnitude of nucleon momentum.
\hat{\bm{p}}=\bm{p}/|\bm{p}|
is the unit vector parallel to the momentum
\bm{p}
.
By virtue of the following effective quantities:

\displaystyle\bm{p}^{*}=

\displaystyle\ \bm{p}+\hat{\bm{p}}U_{\text{V}}(p),

(4a)

\displaystyle M^{*}_{\bm{p}}=

\displaystyle\ M+U_{\text{S}}(p),

(4b)

\displaystyle E^{*}_{\bm{p}}=

\displaystyle\ E_{\bm{p}}-U_{0}(p),

(4c)

the solution of the Dirac equation in Eq. (
2
) leads to the in-medium positive-energy spinor
u(\bm{p},s)
and negative-energy spinor
v(\bm{p},s)

\displaystyle u(\bm{p},s)=

\displaystyle\ \sqrt{\frac{E_{\bm{p}}^{*}+M_{\bm{p}}^{*}}{2M_{\bm{p}}^{*}}}\begin{bmatrix}1\\
\frac{\bm{\sigma}\cdot\bm{p}^{*}}{E_{\bm{p}}^{*}+M_{\bm{p}}^{*}}\end{bmatrix}\chi_{s},

(5a)

\displaystyle v(\bm{p},s)=

\displaystyle\ \gamma^{5}u(\bm{p},s)=\sqrt{\frac{E_{\bm{p}}^{*}+M_{\bm{p}}^{*}}{2M_{\bm{p}}^{*}}}\begin{bmatrix}\frac{\bm{\sigma}\cdot\bm{p}^{*}}{E_{\bm{p}}^{*}+M_{\bm{p}}^{*}}\\
1\end{bmatrix}\chi_{s},

(5b)

where
\chi_{s}
is the spin wave function. The single-particle energies for positive- and negative-energy states can be calculated as

E^{+}_{\bm{p}}=E_{\bm{p}}=E^{*}_{\bm{p}}+U_{0}(p),\qquad E^{-}_{\bm{p}}=-E^{*}_{\bm{p}}+U_{0}(p).

(6)

The Dirac equation can be solved analytically once the single-particle potentials are determined.
To achieve this, three matrix elements of
\mathcal{U}(\bm{p})
in the full Dirac space are introduced as in Refs.
Anastasio et al. 1981
;
Poschenrieder and Weigel 1988
,

\displaystyle\Sigma^{++}(p)=

\displaystyle\ \bar{u}(\bm{p},1/2)\mathcal{U}(\bm{p})u(\bm{p},1/2)=U_{\text{S}}(p)+\frac{E^{*}_{\bm{p}}}{M^{*}_{\bm{p}}}U_{0}(p)+\frac{p^{*}}{M^{*}_{\bm{p}}}U_{\text{V}}(p),

(7a)

\displaystyle\Sigma^{-+}(p)=

\displaystyle\ \bar{v}(\bm{p},1/2)\mathcal{U}(\bm{p})u(\bm{p},1/2)=\frac{p^{*}}{M^{*}_{\bm{p}}}U_{0}(p)+\frac{E^{*}_{\bm{p}}}{M^{*}_{\bm{p}}}U_{\text{V}}(p),

(7b)

\displaystyle\Sigma^{--}(p)=

\displaystyle\ \bar{v}(\bm{p},1/2)\mathcal{U}(\bm{p})v(\bm{p},1/2)=-U_{\text{S}}(p)+\frac{E^{*}_{\bm{p}}}{M^{*}_{\bm{p}}}U_{0}(p)+\frac{p^{*}}{M^{*}_{\bm{p}}}U_{\text{V}}(p),

(7c)

where the direction of
\bm{p}
is taken along the
z
-axis.

Once
\Sigma^{++}(p)
,
\Sigma^{-+}(p)
, and
\Sigma^{--}(p)
are obtained, single-particle potentials in Eq. (
3
) can be determined uniquely through

\displaystyle U_{\text{S}}(p)=

\displaystyle\ \frac{\Sigma^{++}(p)-\Sigma^{--}(p)}{2},

(8a)

\displaystyle U_{0}(p)=

\displaystyle\ \frac{E^{*}_{\bm{p}}}{M^{*}_{\bm{p}}}\frac{\Sigma^{++}(p)+\Sigma^{--}(p)}{2}-\frac{p^{*}}{M^{*}_{\bm{p}}}\Sigma^{-+}(p),

(8b)

\displaystyle U_{\text{V}}(p)=

\displaystyle\ -\frac{p^{*}}{M^{*}_{\bm{p}}}\frac{\Sigma^{++}(p)+\Sigma^{--}(p)}{2}+\frac{E^{*}_{\bm{p}}}{M^{*}_{\bm{p}}}\Sigma^{-+}(p).

(8c)

This effectively avoids approximations in the Dirac space with positive-energy states only.
Matrix elements
\Sigma^{++}(p)
,
\Sigma^{-+}(p)
, and
\Sigma^{--}(p)
can be calculated alternatively by summing up the effective two-body interaction
G

\displaystyle\Sigma^{++}(p)=

\displaystyle\ \sum_{s^{\prime}}\int^{k_{\text{F}}}_{0}\frac{d^{3}p^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{M^{*}_{\bm{p}^{\prime}}}{E^{*}_{\bm{p}^{\prime}}}\langle\bar{u}(\bm{p},1/2)\bar{u}(\bm{p}^{\prime},s^{\prime})|\bar{G}^{++++}(W)|u(\bm{p},1/2)u(\bm{p}^{\prime},s^{\prime})\rangle,

(9a)

\displaystyle\Sigma^{-+}(p)=

\displaystyle\ \sum_{s^{\prime}}\int^{k_{\text{F}}}_{0}\frac{d^{3}p^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{M^{*}_{\bm{p}^{\prime}}}{E^{*}_{\bm{p}^{\prime}}}\langle\bar{v}(\bm{p},1/2)\bar{u}(\bm{p}^{\prime},s^{\prime})|\bar{G}^{-+++}(W)|u(\bm{p},1/2)u(\bm{p}^{\prime},s^{\prime})\rangle,

(9b)

\displaystyle\Sigma^{--}(p)=

\displaystyle\ \sum_{s^{\prime}}\int^{k_{\text{F}}}_{0}\frac{d^{3}p^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{M^{*}_{\bm{p}^{\prime}}}{E^{*}_{\bm{p}^{\prime}}}\langle\bar{v}(\bm{p},1/2)\bar{u}(\bm{p}^{\prime},s^{\prime})|\bar{G}^{-+-+}(W)|v(\bm{p},1/2)u(\bm{p}^{\prime},s^{\prime})\rangle.

(9c)

The Fermi momentum is denoted by
k_{\text{F}}
, which is related to the total density via
\rho=\frac{2}{3\pi^{2}}k^{3}_{\text{F}}
for the symmetric nuclear matter. In asymmetric nuclear matter, the Fermi momenta for proton and neutron are different with
k_{\text{F}}^{n}=k_{\text{F}}(1+\alpha)^{1/3},\ k_{\text{F}}^{p}=k_{\text{F}}(1-\alpha)^{1/3}
with
\alpha\in[0,1]
the isospin asymmetry parameter. In Eq. (
9
), the anti-symmetrized
G

\bar{G}
, where the
\pm

W

The effective interaction

Brockmann and Machleidt 1990

\begin{split}G(\bm{q}^{\prime},\bm{q}|\bm{P},W)=&\ V(\bm{q}^{\prime},\bm{q}|\bm{P})+\int\frac{d^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}V(\bm{q}^{\prime},\bm{k}|\bm{P})\\
&\times\frac{M^{*}_{\bm{P}+\bm{k}}M^{*}_{\bm{P}-\bm{k}}}{E^{*}_{\bm{P}+\bm{k}}E^{*}_{\bm{P}-\bm{k}}}\frac{Q(\bm{k},\bm{P})}{W-E_{\bm{P}+\bm{k}}-E_{\bm{P}-\bm{k}}+i\epsilon}G(\bm{k},\bm{q}|\bm{P},W),\end{split}

(10)

where the labels of positive- and negative-energy states have been suppressed.
\bm{P}=\frac{1}{2}({\bm{k}}_{1}+{\bm{k}}_{2})
is half the total momentum and
\bm{k}=\frac{1}{2}({\bm{k}}_{1}-{\bm{k}}_{2})
is the relative momentum of the two interacting nucleons with momenta
{\bm{k}}_{1}
and
{\bm{k}}_{2}
.
The initial, intermediate, and final relative momenta of the two nucleons scattering in nuclear matter are denoted by
\bm{q},\bm{k}
, and
\bm{q}^{\prime}
, respectively. The NN scattering in the nuclear medium is restricted with the Pauli operator

Q(\bm{k},\bm{P})=\begin{cases}1,\qquad&|\bm{P}+\bm{k}|,|\bm{P}-\bm{k}|>k_{\text{F}},\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(11)

In practice, the Pauli operator is replaced with its angular average
Q_{\text{av}}(k,P)
, where the angle between the relative momentum
\bm{k}
and total momentum
\bm{P}
in the intermediate states is averaged
Tong et al. 2018

Q_{\text{av}}(k,P)=\frac{\int d\Omega\ Q(\bm{k},\bm{P})}{\int d\Omega}.

(12)

The exact treatment of

would be much more involved

. The introduction of an infinitesimal

in the denominator becomes necessary when the starting energy
W=E_{\bm{P}+\bm{k}}+E_{\bm{P}-\bm{k}}
approaches a pole within the
continuous
choice. Inspired from the NN scattering in the free space, the energy denominator of the scattering equation (
10
) in the nuclear medium is rewritten and a complex Thompson equation for the effective interaction
G
matrix is derived. Details for the RBHF calculations within the
continuous
choice can be found in Ref.
Wang et al. 2022b
. Equations (
2
), (
8
), (
9
), and (
10
) constitute a coupled system that has to be solved in a self-consistent way.

To conclude this section, we would like to emphasize three key points that are related to the relativistic scattering equation (
10
). First, Pauli blocking effects are incorporated via the Pauli operator. Second, the starting energy and the single-particle energies in the denominator are calculated using effective quantities, which account for dispersion effects. Third, the NN interaction matrix elements are constructed with effective Dirac spinors in the medium, capturing the essential density dependence. By including Pauli blocking effects, dispersion effects, and relativistic medium effects, the RBHF theory provides a promising framework for investigating medium effects of strong interaction from
ab initio
calculations.

II.2
Calculation of in-medium cross sections

To study the in-medium NN scattering, firstly we calculate the
S
matrix from the complex
G
matrix
Blatt and Biedenharn 1952

S^{JS}_{L^{\prime}L}(E_{\text{lab}},P)=\delta_{L^{\prime}L}-i\pi qE_{q}G^{JS}_{L^{\prime}L}(q,P).

(13)

The quantum numbers in each partial wave are total angular momentum
J
, total spin
S
and orbital angular momenta
L^{\prime},L
. The partial wave
G
matrix is firstly obtained in the helicity basis by solving Eq. (
10
) and then transformed to the
LSJ
basis
Erkelenz et al. 1971
. For the uncoupled channel
L=L^{\prime}=J
, the corresponding phase shift is given by

S^{JS}_{JJ}(E_{\text{lab}},P)=\exp(2i\delta^{JS}_{JJ}),\qquad\tan(2\delta^{JS}_{JJ})=\frac{\text{Im}S^{JS}_{JJ}}{\text{Re}S^{JS}_{JJ}}.

(14)

For coupled channel with
S=1
, two orbital angular momenta with
L_{\pm}=J\pm 1
are mixed by the tensor force. The
S
matrix can be parametrizations with the phase shifts
\delta^{JS}_{L^{\prime}L}
and the mixing angle
\epsilon_{J}

Stapp et al. 1957

\begin{pmatrix}S^{J1}_{--}&S^{J1}_{-+}\\
S^{J1}_{+-}&S^{J1}_{++}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\exp(i\delta^{J1}_{--})&0\\
0&\exp(i\delta^{J1}_{++})\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\cos(2\epsilon_{J})&i\sin(2\epsilon_{J})\\
i\sin(2\epsilon_{J})&\cos(2\epsilon_{J})\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\exp(i\delta^{J1}_{--})&0\\
0&\exp(i\delta^{J1}_{++})\end{pmatrix}

(15)

The mixing angles and phase shifts can be obtained by

\tan(2\epsilon_{J})=\frac{-iS^{J1}_{+-}}{\sqrt{S^{J1}_{--}S^{J1}_{++}}},\qquad\tan(2\delta^{J1}_{\pm\pm})=\frac{\text{Im}S^{J1}_{\pm\pm}}{\text{Re}S^{J1}_{\pm\pm}}.

(16)

Notice that the
G
matrix is solved in the rest frame of nuclear matter. In this work, the relative momentum
q
is adopted to be related to the laboratory energy through the well-known relation
E_{\text{lab}}=2q^{2}/m
, while the total momenta
P
is treated as another variable parameter.
P=0
corresponds to the usual free-space scattering scenario. For simplicity, the angular average between
\bm{q}
and
\bm{P}
is used, i.e.,

|\bm{k}_{1}|=\bm{P}+\bm{q}\approx\sqrt{q^{2}+P^{2}},\quad|\bm{k}_{2}|=\bm{P}-\bm{q}\approx\sqrt{q^{2}+P^{2}}.

(17)

Exact calculations with specific angle dependence would be much more involved
Chen et al. 2013
.

Starting from the
S
matrix, the scattering amplitudes can be obtained by summing all the relevant channels
Reinert et al. 2018

\begin{split}\mathcal{M}^{S}_{m^{\prime}_{S},m_{S}}=&\ \frac{1}{iq}\sum_{JL^{\prime}L}C(L^{\prime},S,J;m_{S}-m^{\prime}_{S},m^{\prime}_{S},m_{S})Y_{L^{\prime}(m^{\prime}_{S}-m_{S})}(\hat{\bm{q}})(S^{JS}_{L^{\prime}L}-\delta_{L^{\prime}L})\\
&\times C(L,S,J;0,m_{S},m_{S})\sqrt{\pi(2L+1)}\ i^{L-L^{\prime}}[1-(-1)^{L+S+T}].\end{split}

(18)

Here
T
denotes the total isospin for the scattering nucleons,
C(j_{1},j_{2},j_{3};m_{1},m_{2},m_{3})
are Clebsch-Gordan coefficients while
Y_{lm}(\hat{\bm{q}})
are the spherical harmonics. In our calculations, all panels-wave
S
matrices with total angular momentum
J\leq 10\ \hbar
are summed up.

The scattering amplitudes
\mathcal{M}^{S}_{m^{\prime}_{S},m_{S}}
can also be given in matrix form as

\mathcal{M}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{M}_{ss}&0&0&0\\
0&\mathcal{M}_{--}&\mathcal{M}_{-0}&\mathcal{M}_{-+}\\
0&\mathcal{M}_{0-}&\mathcal{M}_{00}&\mathcal{M}_{0+}\\
0&\mathcal{M}_{+-}&\mathcal{M}_{+0}&\mathcal{M}_{++}\end{pmatrix},

(19)

where
\mathcal{M}_{ss}\equiv\mathcal{M}^{0}_{0,0}
stands for the spin-singlet
(S=0)
states, while the subscripts
\pm,0
are used for
m_{S}=\pm 1,0
in the spin-triplet
(S=1)
states. Then the unpolarized differential cross section and total cross section are calculated by
Bystricky et al. 1978

d\sigma/d\Omega=\frac{1}{4}\mathrm{Tr}\left(\mathcal{M}^{\dagger}\mathcal{M}\right),\qquad\sigma(E_{\text{lab}},P,\rho,\alpha)=\int d\Omega\ \frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega}(E_{\text{lab}},P,\rho,\alpha,\theta_{\text{cm}}).

(20)

In this work we don’t consider the Pauli blocking of the final state, which would result in quite different cross sections, both quantitatively and qualitatively
Sammarruca and Krastev 2006
;
Sammarruca 2014
. We notice that the Pauli blocking factor for the final states is always included in the transport models describing nucleus-nucleus collisions.

, indicating the absence of bound states in these channels in nuclear matter.

A notable feature is the sharp increase followed by a decrease in the
{}^{3}S_{1}
phase shift at low laboratory energies, which becomes more prominent at lower densities, indicating a gradual convergence toward the free-space behavior. This density-dependence trend also highlights the difficulties encountered in RBHF calculations for densities below
\rho=0.08\ \text{fm}^{-3}
, where the emergence of nucleon bound states leads to inhomogeneities in the nuclear medium.

Furthermore, a significant enhancement of the phase shift is observed at laboratory energies near the Fermi momentum, particularly evident in the
{}^{3}S_{1}
channel and
\rho=\rho_{0}
. For symmetric nuclear matter at
\rho=0.16\ \text{fm}^{-3}
, the nucleon Fermi momentum is
k_{\text{F}}=1.33\ \text{fm}^{-1}
, corresponding to a laboratory energy of
E_{\text{lab}}\approx 147
MeV. In the middle panel of Fig.
1
, the enhancement is observed around 150 MeV. This enhancement indicates a stronger interaction between nucleons at these energies, which can be attributed to the increased overlap of their wave functions near the Fermi surface, leading to more pronounced quantum effects such as Pauli blocking and medium modifications.

IV
Summary and prospective

In summary, this study provides a microscopic investigation of in-medium nucleon-nucleon (NN) scattering cross sections using the relativistic Brueckner-Hartree-Fock (RBHF) theory with the Bonn potential. By solving the RBHF equations in the full Dirac space, namely incorporating both positive- and negative-energy solutions of the Dirac equation, we derive the complex
G
matrix in nuclear matter and subsequently calculate the
S
matrix elements, scattering amplitudes, and observables including phase shifts, differential cross sections, and total cross sections for
pp
,
nn
, and
np
systems. Our results demonstrate fundamental modifications to NN interactions in dense hadronic environments. The absence of bound states in nuclear matter is revealed through characteristic phase-shift patterns in both
{}^{1}S_{0}
and
{}^{3}S_{1}
channels, while medium suppression effects exhibit stronger sensitivity to baryon density than to isospin asymmetry. Distinct angular dependencies emerge between
pp
and
np
scattering, with the former displaying quasi-isotropic distributions and the latter showing pronounced backward-angle enhancement due to spin-isospin exchange interactions. Notably, resonant-like amplifications of total cross sections and phase shifts are observed near the Fermi momentum and zero total momentum, correlating with the emergence of imaginary components in the
G
-matrix induced by multi scattering in nuclear medium.

The analysis underscores the complicated multi-parameter dependence of in-medium cross sections on laboratory energy
E_{\text{lab}}
, total momentum
P
, density
\rho
, and isospin asymmetry
\alpha
. To improve the practical applicability of our results, we are developing a systematic parametrization scheme of
\sigma(E_{\text{lab}},P,\rho,\alpha)
to facilitate implementation in transport models. This parametrization is also helpful with the mean field calculated self-consistently starting from the same realistic NN interactions
Wang et al. 2022a
. Future efforts will extend this framework to include Deltas in the scattering processes
ter Haar and Malfliet 1987
;
Li and Li 2019
;
Nan et al. 2024
, providing critical elastic and inelastic cross sections essential for modeling intermediate-energy heavy ion collision simulations. Additionally, recent years have witnessed important progress in developing relativistic chiral nuclear forces
Lu et al. 2025
. Microscopic in-medium cross sections starting from that would be more promising considering its closer relation to the chiral symmetry and its breaking in low-energy quantum chromodynamics.

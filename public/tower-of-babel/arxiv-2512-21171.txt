Navier-Stokes-Cahn-Hilliard system in a 3 3 D perforated domain with free slip and source term: Existence and homogenization
Amartya Chakrabortty, Haradhan Dutta, Hari Shankar Mahato

Abstract
We study a diffuse–interface model for a binary incompressible mixture in a periodically perforated porous medium, described by a time-dependent Navier–Stokes–Cahn–Hilliard (NSCH) system posed on the pore domain Ω p ε ⊂ ℝ 3 \Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3} . The microscopic model involves a variable viscosity tensor, a non-conservative source term in the Cahn–Hilliard equation, and mixed boundary conditions: no-slip on the outer boundary and Navier slip with zero tangential stress on the surfaces of the solid inclusions. The capillarity strength λ ε > 0 \lambda^{\varepsilon}>0 depends on the microscopic scale ε > 0 \varepsilon>0 . The analysis consists of two main parts. First, for each fixed ε > 0 \varepsilon>0 we prove existence of a weak solution on a finite time interval ( 0 , T ) (0,T) and derive a priori estimates that are uniform with respect to ε \varepsilon (and λ ε \lambda^{\varepsilon} ). Second, we perform the periodic homogenization for the perforated setting a limit ε → 0 \varepsilon\to 0 . Depending on the limit value λ \lambda of the capillarity strength λ ε \lambda^{\varepsilon} , we obtain two distinct effective models: (i) in the vanishing capillarity regime λ = 0 \lambda=0 , the limit system is of Stokes–Cahn–Hilliard type, with no macroscopic convection or advection; (ii) in the balanced regime λ ∈ ( 0 , + ∞ ) \lambda\in(0,+\infty) , we derive a Navier–Stokes–Cahn–Hilliard system with nonlinear convection and advective transport of the phase field at the macroscopic scale. Finally, we establish the convergence of the microscopic free energy to a homogenized energy functional satisfying an analogous dissipation law.



Keywords:
Navier-Stokes, Cahn-Hilliard, homogenization, perforated porous medium, mixed boundary conditions.

Introduction

Multiphase transport in porous and microstructured environments arises in a wide
range of physical, chemical, and biological processes, including groundwater
remediation, enhanced oil recovery, polymeric and composite materials, and
biological systems such as hydrogels and tissue scaffolds. At the pore scale,
such flows are governed by the complex interplay between viscous transport,
capillarity, interfacial energetics, and phase separation mechanisms. Any
reliable pore-scale description must therefore couple the hydrodynamic
behaviour of an incompressible mixture with a thermodynamically consistent
representation of evolving diffuse interfaces.

Diffuse–interface (or phase–field) models provide a robust framework for
capturing interfacial dynamics in multiphase systems. By replacing sharp
fluid–fluid boundaries with a smooth order parameter, they circumvent the
geometric difficulties inherent in classical sharp–interface descriptions and
naturally account for topological changes such as coalescence, breakup, and
reconnection of phases. In this setting, the evolution of the order parameter is
governed by a Cahn–Hilliard-type equation derived from an underlying free–energy
functional. When coupled with the incompressible Navier–Stokes equations for
fluid motion, this yields the thermodynamically consistent
Navier–Stokes–Cahn–Hilliard (NSCH) system, which has become a standard continuum model for
binary incompressible mixtures; see, for instance,

[

,

,

and the references
therein. This coupling provides a mechanism linking macroscopic hydrodynamics
with microscopic phase separation and forms the basic continuum framework on
which the present study builds.

In summary, we establish a rigorous periodic homogenization result for a fully time-dependent
Navier–Stokes–Cahn–Hilliard system with (i) variable viscosity depending on the microscopic
geometry, (ii) a non-conservative source term
G(\phi^{\varepsilon})
, (iii) Navier slip with zero tangential
stress on the solid inclusions, and (iv) a perforated, periodically oscillating pore geometry.
A key novelty of this work is that we first prove the existence of weak solutions for such a
system, and then derive homogenized models that depend on the
asymptotic strength of capillarity
\lambda
. The different homogenized systems are given by:

•

for
\lambda=0
(vanishing capillarity regime), the limit model is of SCH type, with no macroscopic convective or advective terms; in this regime capillary forcing is too weak to generate macroscopic transport through interfacial effects;

•

for
\lambda\in(0,+\infty)
(balanced regime), the limit model is of Navier–Stokes–Cahn–Hilliard type, in which the macroscopic velocity satisfies a momentum balance containing nonlinear convection and phase advection with strength proportional to
\sqrt{\lambda}
; in this regime capillarity competes with viscosity and interface motion can induce appreciable flows.

Our results extend the existing
homogenization theory for diffuse-interface models, which typically either assumes conservation of
the spatial mean of the order parameter or imposes no-slip boundary conditions on the fluid–solid
interface.

4.1
Bogovskiĭ operator and restriction operator

In this section we introduce suitable inverses of the divergence, commonly
known as Bogovskiĭ operators, defined on the porous fluid domain

\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}
and on the solid obstacle domain
\Omega_{s}^{\varepsilon}
; see

[
13
,
47
,
32
]
. The existence of a Bogovskiĭ–type
inverse of the divergence, with bounds depending only on the Lipschitz
character of the domain, is classical for fixed (i.e.
\varepsilon
–independent)
domains
\Omega
; see, for instance,

[
4
,
22
]
and the references therein.

In contrast, the porous medium
\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}
is an
\varepsilon
–dependent perforated
domain, in which the size of the holes is proportional to the mutual distance
(or period)
\varepsilon
. In this setting we have the following result.

Lemma 4.1
.

There exists a linear operator

\mathcal{B}_{p}^{\varepsilon}:L^{2}_{0}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})\longrightarrow H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})^{3}\subset{\bf H}^{1}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p}),

such that for every
g\in L^{2}_{0}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})
the vector field

\phi:=\mathcal{B}_{p}^{\varepsilon}(g)
satisfies

\displaystyle\operatorname{div}\phi

\displaystyle=g\quad\text{in }\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon},\qquad\phi=0\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon},

in the sense of distributions and traces, respectively. Moreover, there exists
a constant
C_{p}>0
, independent of
\varepsilon
, such that

\|\mathcal{B}_{p}^{\varepsilon}(g)\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}\;\leq\;\frac{C_{p}}{\varepsilon}\,\|g\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}.

(4.1)

The construction of the operator
\mathcal{B}_{p}^{\varepsilon}
and the estimate
(
4.1
) are standard in the homogenization literature on flows in
periodically perforated domains. In the geometric setting considered here,

\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
is a bounded Lipschitz domain, and the porous structure is
obtained from a reference cell
Y
split into solid and pore parts
Y_{s}
,
Y_{p}

as above, leading to the periodic decomposition

\Omega=\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}\cup\Omega_{s}^{\varepsilon}\cup\Gamma_{s}^{\varepsilon}
. We follow the
classical restriction construction of Allaire (see Lemma
4.3
) for
Stokes flow in a connected porous medium, cf.
[
6
, Section 2]
.
Combining Allaire’s restriction operator with fixed–domain Bogovskiĭ
operators yields an inverse divergence on perforated domains with a uniform
constant
C_{p}
(in the sense of (
4.1
)) independent of
\varepsilon
; this
strategy is made explicit and generalized in

[
43
, Lemma 1.6]
,
[
25
, Theorem 2.3]
,

[
41
, Sections 2.2 and 6]
and

[
46
, Proposition 1.7]
, where the construction is carried out
for families of perforated Lipschitz domains very close to the present setting.

Below, we construct a Bogovskiĭ operator on the
\varepsilon
–dependent domain

\Omega_{s}^{\varepsilon}
(the solid obstacle domain, a disconnected union of Lipschitz
domains).

Lemma 4.2
.

Let us set

Y^{\varepsilon}_{\kappa,s}=\varepsilon(\kappa+Y_{s}),\quad L^{2}_{0,\mathrm{cell}}(\Omega_{s}^{\varepsilon}):=\Bigl\{g\in L^{2}(\Omega_{s}^{\varepsilon}):\int_{Y^{\varepsilon}_{\kappa,s}}g(x)\,dx=0\ \text{for all }\kappa\in{\mathcal{K}}_{\varepsilon}\Bigr\}\subset L^{2}_{0}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{s}).

Then, there exists a
linear operator

\mathcal{B}^{\varepsilon}_{s}:L^{2}_{0,\mathrm{cell}}(\Omega_{s}^{\varepsilon})\longrightarrow H^{1}_{0}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{s})^{3},\quad

such that for every
g\in L^{2}_{0,\mathrm{cell}}(\Omega_{s}^{\varepsilon})\subset L^{2}_{0}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{s})
the vector field

\phi:=\mathcal{B}^{\varepsilon}_{s}(g)
satisfies

\displaystyle\operatorname{{\mathrm{div}}}\phi=g\quad\text{in }\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{s},\qquad\phi=0\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{s},

in the sense of distributions and traces, respectively. Moreover, there is a
constant
C_{s}>0
is independent of
\varepsilon
, for which the norm estimate

\|{\mathcal{B}}^{\varepsilon}_{s}(g)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{s})}\leq C_{s}\,\|g\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{s})}

(4.2)

holds.

Proof.

The proof is done in two steps.

Step 1:
We show that there exists a linear operator

\mathcal{B}^{\varepsilon}_{\kappa,s}:L^{2}_{0}(Y^{\varepsilon}_{\kappa,s})\to H^{1}_{0}(Y^{\varepsilon}_{\kappa,s})^{3}

such that

{\mathrm{div}}({\mathcal{B}}^{\varepsilon}_{\kappa,s}g)=g\quad\text{in }Y^{\varepsilon}_{\kappa,s},\qquad\|\nabla{\mathcal{B}}^{\varepsilon}_{\kappa,s}g\|_{L^{2}(Y^{\varepsilon}_{\kappa,s})}\leq C_{s}\|g\|_{L^{2}(Y^{\varepsilon}_{\kappa,s})}\quad\forall g\in L^{2}_{0}(Y^{\varepsilon}_{\kappa,s}),

(4.3)

i.e. the same constant
C_{s}
works for all
\varepsilon>0
.

Since
Y_{s}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
is a bounded domain with Lipschitz boundary. Then, there exists a linear operator

\mathcal{B}_{s}:L^{2}_{0}(Y_{s})\to H^{1}_{0}(Y_{s})^{3}

such that

{\mathrm{div}}(\mathcal{B}_{s}f)=f\quad\text{in }Y_{s},\qquad\|\nabla\mathcal{B}_{s}f\|_{L^{2}(Y_{s})}\leq C_{s}\|f\|_{L^{2}(Y_{s})}\quad\forall f\in L^{2}_{0}(Y_{s}),

(4.4)

for some constant
C_{s}>0
depending only on
Y_{s}
.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
and let
g\in L^{2}_{0}(Y^{\varepsilon}_{\kappa,s})
.
Define
f:Y_{s}\to\mathbb{R}
by

f(y):=g(\varepsilon\kappa+\varepsilon y),\qquad y\in Y_{s}.

A simple change of variables shows that
f\in L^{2}(Y_{s})
and

\int_{Y_{s}}f(y)\,dy=\int_{Y_{s}}g(\varepsilon\kappa+\varepsilon y)\,dy=\varepsilon^{-3}\int_{Y^{\varepsilon}_{\kappa,s}}g(x)\,dx=0,

so
f\in L_{0}^{2}(Y_{s})
.
By assumption,
u:=\mathcal{B}_{s}f\in H^{1}_{0}(Y_{s})^{3}
is well-defined and satisfies

{\mathrm{div}}u=f\quad\text{in }Y_{s},\qquad\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(Y_{s})}\leq C_{s}\|f\|_{L^{2}(Y_{s})}.

Now, we define
u_{\varepsilon}:Y^{\varepsilon}_{\kappa,s}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
by

u_{\varepsilon}(x):=\varepsilon\,u\!\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}-\kappa\right),\qquad x\in Y^{\varepsilon}_{\kappa,s}.

Since
u\in H^{1}_{0}(Y_{s})^{3}
has zero trace on
\partial Y_{s}
, its rescaling

u_{\varepsilon}
has zero trace on
\partial Y^{\varepsilon}_{\kappa,s}=\varepsilon\kappa+\varepsilon\partial Y_{s}
,
hence
u_{\varepsilon}\in H^{1}_{0}(Y^{\varepsilon}_{\kappa,s})^{3}
.

Set
x=\varepsilon\kappa+\varepsilon y
, so
u_{\varepsilon}(x)=\varepsilon u(y)
then
\nabla_{x}u_{\varepsilon}(x)=\nabla_{y}u(y)
.
Therefore

{\mathrm{div}}_{x}u_{\varepsilon}(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{3}\partial_{x_{j}}u_{\varepsilon,j}(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{3}(\partial_{y_{j}}u_{j})(y)={\mathrm{div}}_{y}u(y)=f(y)=g(x).

Thus
{\mathrm{div}}u_{\varepsilon}=g
in
Y^{\varepsilon}_{\kappa,s}
.

Finally we estimate the
L^{2}
-norm of the gradient.
Using the change of variables,

\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(Y^{\varepsilon}_{\kappa,s})}^{2}=\int_{Y^{\varepsilon}_{\kappa,s}}|\nabla_{x}u_{\varepsilon}(x)|^{2}\,dx=\int_{Y^{\varepsilon}_{\kappa,s}}|\nabla_{y}u(y)|^{2}\,dx=\varepsilon^{3}\int_{Y_{s}}|\nabla_{y}u(y)|^{2}\,dy=\varepsilon^{3}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(Y_{s})}^{2},

and similarly

\|f\|_{L^{2}(Y_{s})}^{2}=\varepsilon^{-3}\|g\|_{L^{2}(Y^{\varepsilon}_{\kappa,s})}^{2}
.

Using the estimate for
u
and substituting these relations, we find

\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(Y^{\varepsilon}_{\kappa,s})}^{2}=\varepsilon^{3}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(Y_{s})}^{2}\leq\varepsilon^{3}C_{s}^{2}\|f\|_{L^{2}(Y_{s})}^{2}=\varepsilon^{3}C_{s}^{2}\varepsilon^{-3}\|g\|_{L^{2}(Y^{\varepsilon}_{\kappa,s})}^{2}=C_{s}^{2}\|g\|_{L^{2}(Y^{\varepsilon}_{\kappa,s})}^{2}.

Taking square roots gives

\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(Y^{\varepsilon}_{\kappa,s})}\leq C_{s}\|g\|_{L^{2}(Y^{\varepsilon}_{\kappa,s})}.

This completes the Step 1.

Step 2:
Global construction on
\Omega_{s}^{\varepsilon}
.

By the divergence theorem, if
\phi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{s}^{\varepsilon})^{3}
and

g={\mathrm{div}}\phi
in
\Omega_{s}^{\varepsilon}
, then for every cell
Y^{\varepsilon}_{\kappa,s}
we have

\int_{Y^{\varepsilon}_{\kappa,s}}g(x)\,dx=\int_{Y^{\varepsilon}_{\kappa,s}}{\mathrm{div}}\phi(x)\,dx=\int_{\partial Y^{\varepsilon}_{\kappa,s}}\phi\cdot n\,dS=0,

since the trace of
\phi
vanishes on
\partial Y^{\varepsilon}_{\kappa,s}
. So, we have
g\in L^{2}_{0,\mathrm{cell}}(\Omega_{s}^{\varepsilon})
.

For each
\kappa\in{\mathcal{K}}_{\varepsilon}
, Step 1 provides a linear operator
{\mathcal{B}}^{\varepsilon}_{\kappa,s}
from
L^{2}_{0}(Y^{\varepsilon}_{\kappa,s})\to H^{1}_{0}(Y^{\varepsilon}_{\kappa,s})^{3}
with a constant
C_{s}>0
independent of

\varepsilon
and
\kappa
.

Now let
g\in L^{2}_{0,\mathrm{cell}}(\Omega_{s}^{\varepsilon})
be arbitrary and denote by

g_{\kappa}:=g|_{Y^{\varepsilon}_{\kappa,s}}
its restriction to the cell
Y^{\varepsilon}_{\kappa,s}
.
By definition of
L^{2}_{0,\mathrm{cell}}(\Omega_{s}^{\varepsilon})
, each
g_{\kappa}
belongs
to
L^{2}_{0}(Y^{\varepsilon}_{\kappa,s})
, so we may set

\phi_{\kappa}:=\mathcal{B}^{\varepsilon}_{s,\kappa}g_{\kappa}\in H^{1}_{0}(Y^{\varepsilon}_{\kappa,s})^{3}.

We then define the global field
\phi:\Omega_{s}^{\varepsilon}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
by

\phi(x):=\phi_{\kappa}(x)\quad\text{if }x\in Y^{\varepsilon}_{\kappa,s}.

Since the cells
Y^{\varepsilon}_{\kappa,s}
are pairwise disjoint and
\phi_{\kappa}

vanishes on
\partial Y^{\varepsilon}_{\kappa,s}
, it follows that
\phi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{s}^{\varepsilon})^{3}
.
Moreover,

{\mathrm{div}}\phi(x)={\mathrm{div}}\phi_{\kappa}(x)=g_{\kappa}(x)=g(x)\quad\text{for a.e. }x\in Y^{\varepsilon}_{\kappa,s},

so that
{\mathrm{div}}\phi=g

\Omega_{s}^{\varepsilon}
.

Finally, using the cell-wise estimate from Step 1 and summing over all
\kappa

we obtain

\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{s}^{\varepsilon})}^{2}=\sum_{\kappa\in{\mathcal{K}}_{\varepsilon}}\|\nabla\phi_{\kappa}\|_{L^{2}(Y^{\varepsilon}_{\kappa,s})}^{2}\leq C_{s}^{2}\sum_{\kappa\in{\mathcal{K}}_{\varepsilon}}\|g_{\kappa}\|_{L^{2}(Y^{\varepsilon}_{\kappa,s})}^{2}=C_{s}^{2}\|g\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{s}^{\varepsilon})}^{2}.

Taking square roots yields

\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{s}^{\varepsilon})}\leq C_{s}\|g\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{s}^{\varepsilon})},

with a constant
C_{s}>0
independent of
\varepsilon
. Defining

\mathcal{B}^{\varepsilon}_{s}g:=\phi
completes the construction of the global

\Omega_{s}^{\varepsilon}
.

This completes the proof.

.
Then for every
\varphi\in\mathfrak{C}^{\varepsilon}
one has

E^{\varepsilon}\varphi\in\mathfrak{C},\qquad\text{and}\qquad\partial_{t}(E^{\varepsilon}\varphi)=F^{\varepsilon}\,\partial_{t}\varphi\quad\text{in }L^{2}(S;V^{\ast}).

Moreover,

\|\partial_{t}(E^{\varepsilon}\varphi)\|_{L^{2}(S;V^{\ast})}\leq C\,\|\partial_{t}\varphi\|_{L^{2}(S;V_{\varepsilon}^{\ast})},

(4.9)

with a constant
C>0
independent of
\varepsilon
.

Proof.

The proof is divided into three steps.

Step 1:

Since
E^{\varepsilon}:V_{\varepsilon}\to V
is bounded uniformly in
\varepsilon
(Lemma
4.4
),
the induced operator on Bochner spaces satisfies

\|E^{\varepsilon}\varphi\|_{L^{2}(S;V)}\leq\|E_{1}^{\varepsilon}\|\,\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(S;V_{\varepsilon})}\leq C\,\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(S;V_{\varepsilon})}

for all
\varphi\in L^{2}(S;V_{\varepsilon})
. Hence
E^{\varepsilon}\varphi\in L^{2}(S;V)
for all

\varphi\in\mathfrak{C}^{\varepsilon}
.

Step 2:

Let
\varphi\in\mathfrak{C}^{\varepsilon}
and
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(S;V)
.
By definition of the adjoint
F^{\varepsilon}=(E^{\varepsilon,\sharp})^{\ast}
,

\big\langle F^{\varepsilon}\xi,\eta\big\rangle_{V^{\ast},V}=\big\langle\xi,E^{\varepsilon,\sharp}\eta\big\rangle_{V_{\varepsilon}^{\ast},V_{\varepsilon}}\qquad\forall\,\xi\in V_{\varepsilon}^{\ast},\ \eta\in V.

(4.10)

Applying this with
\xi=\partial_{t}\varphi(t)
and
\eta=\psi(t)
and integrating over
S
gives

\int_{S}\big\langle F^{\varepsilon}\,\partial_{t}\varphi(t),\psi(t)\big\rangle_{V^{\ast},V}\,{\mathrm{d}}t=\int_{S}\big\langle\partial_{t}\varphi(t),E^{\varepsilon,\sharp}\psi(t)\big\rangle_{V_{\varepsilon}^{\ast},V_{\varepsilon}}\,{\mathrm{d}}t.

(4.11)

On the other hand, the definition of the weak time derivative in the Gelfand triple

V_{\varepsilon}\subset H_{\varepsilon}\subset V_{\varepsilon}^{\ast}
yields, for any
\zeta\in C_{c}^{\infty}(S;V_{\varepsilon})
,

\int_{S}\!\big\langle\partial_{t}\varphi(t),\zeta(t)\big\rangle_{V_{\varepsilon}^{\ast},V_{\varepsilon}}\,{\mathrm{d}}t=-\int_{S}\!\big(\varphi(t),\partial_{t}\zeta(t)\big)_{H_{\varepsilon}}\,{\mathrm{d}}t.

(4.12)

We apply (
4.12
) with the specific choice

\zeta(t):=E^{\varepsilon,\sharp}\psi(t)\in C_{c}^{\infty}(S;V_{\varepsilon}).

Since
E^{\varepsilon,\sharp}
is bounded and independent of
t
, we have

\partial_{t}\zeta(t)=E^{\varepsilon,\sharp}(\partial_{t}\psi(t))
, and thus

\int_{S}\!\big\langle\partial_{t}\varphi(t),E^{\varepsilon,\sharp}\psi(t)\big\rangle_{V_{\varepsilon}^{\ast},V_{\varepsilon}}\,{\mathrm{d}}t=-\int_{S}\!\big(\varphi(t),E^{\varepsilon,\sharp}\partial_{t}\psi(t)\big)_{H_{\varepsilon}}\,{\mathrm{d}}t.

Using the definition of the canonical injections
J_{\varepsilon}
and
J
, we get the following relation between
E^{\varepsilon}
and
E^{\varepsilon,\sharp}
,

\big(\varphi(t),E^{\varepsilon,\sharp}\eta\big)_{H_{\varepsilon}}=\big(E^{\varepsilon}\varphi(t),\eta\big)_{H}\qquad\forall\,\eta\in V,

we obtain

\int_{S}\!\big\langle\partial_{t}\varphi(t),E^{\varepsilon,\sharp}\psi(t)\big\rangle_{V_{\varepsilon}^{\ast},V_{\varepsilon}}\,{\mathrm{d}}t=-\int_{S}\!\big(E^{\varepsilon}\varphi(t),\partial_{t}\psi(t)\big)_{H}\,{\mathrm{d}}t.

Combining this with (
4.11
) yields

\int_{S}\big\langle F^{\varepsilon}\,\partial_{t}\varphi(t),\psi(t)\big\rangle_{V^{\ast},V}\,{\mathrm{d}}t=-\int_{S}\big(E^{\varepsilon}\varphi(t),\partial_{t}\psi(t)\big)_{H}\,{\mathrm{d}}t.

(4.13)

By the definition of the weak time derivative in the triple
V\subset H\subset V^{\ast}
,
(
4.13
) is exactly the statement that

E^{\varepsilon}\varphi\in H^{1}(S;V^{\ast})\quad\text{and}\quad\partial_{t}(E^{\varepsilon}\varphi)=F^{\varepsilon}\,\partial_{t}\varphi\quad\text{in }L^{2}(S;V^{\ast}).

Together with Step 1 this shows
E^{\varepsilon}\varphi\in\mathfrak{C}
.

Step 3:

From
\partial_{t}(E^{\varepsilon}\varphi)=F^{\varepsilon}\,\partial_{t}\varphi
in
L^{2}(S;V^{\ast})
we obtain

\|\partial_{t}(E^{\varepsilon}\varphi)\|_{L^{2}(S;V^{\ast})}\leq\|F^{\varepsilon}\|\,\|\partial_{t}\varphi\|_{L^{2}(S;V_{\varepsilon}^{\ast})}.

Since
J
and
J_{\varepsilon}
are isometries and
E^{\varepsilon,\sharp}
is the
H
–adjoint of
E^{\varepsilon}
,
we have

\|E^{\varepsilon,\sharp}\|=\|(E^{\varepsilon})^{\ast}\|=\|E^{\varepsilon}\|,\qquad\|F^{\varepsilon}\|=\|(E^{\varepsilon,\sharp})^{\ast}\|=\|E^{\varepsilon,\sharp}\|=\|E^{\varepsilon}\|.

By Lemma
4.4
,
\|E^{\varepsilon}\|\leq C
with
C
independent of
\varepsilon
, hence

\|\partial_{t}(E^{\varepsilon}\varphi)\|_{L^{2}(S;V^{\ast})}\leq C\,\|\partial_{t}\varphi\|_{L^{2}(S;V_{\varepsilon}^{\ast})},

which is (
4.9
). This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.6

(Solenoidal extension)
.

Let

\displaystyle V_{\varepsilon}:=H^{1}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p})^{3},\qquad\qquad V:=H^{1}(\Omega)^{3},

\displaystyle V_{\varepsilon}^{\sigma}:=\{v\in V_{\varepsilon}:\ \nabla\!\cdot v=0\text{ in }\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p},\ v|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\quad(v\cdot{\bf n}^{\varepsilon})|_{\Gamma^{\varepsilon}_{s}}=0\},

\displaystyle V^{\sigma}:=\{w\in V:\ \nabla\!\cdot w=0,\quad\text{in $\Omega$},\ w|_{\partial\Omega}=0\}.

Then there exists a linear operator
\mathcal{E}^{\varepsilon}:V_{\varepsilon}^{\sigma}\to V^{\sigma}
such that:

(i)

Exact extension on
\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p}
:

(E^{\varepsilon}v)|_{\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p}}=v
for all
v\in V_{\varepsilon}^{\sigma}
.

(ii)

Divergence-free and boundary condition:

\nabla\!\cdot(\mathcal{E}^{\varepsilon}v)=0
in
\Omega
and
(\mathcal{E}^{\varepsilon}v)|_{\partial\Omega}=0
.

(iii)

Uniform
H^{1}
bound:

\|\mathcal{E}^{\varepsilon}v\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq C\,\|v\|_{H^{1}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p})}
for all
v\in V_{\varepsilon}^{\sigma}
,
with
C
independent of
\varepsilon
.

(iv)

Energy adjoint and time commutation: For every

v\in\mathfrak{U}^{\varepsilon}
, we have

{\mathcal{E}}^{\varepsilon}v\in\mathfrak{U},\quad\text{and}\quad\|\partial_{t}(\mathcal{E}^{\varepsilon}v)\|_{L^{2}(S;(V^{\sigma})^{*})}\leq C\,\|\partial_{t}v^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(S;(V_{\varepsilon}^{\sigma})^{*})}.

(4.14)

The constant is independent of
\varepsilon
.

Proof.

The proof is presented in three steps.

Step 1:
We define the extension operator
{\mathcal{E}}^{\varepsilon}
.

Given
v\in V_{\varepsilon}^{\sigma}
, set
w:=E_{1}^{\varepsilon}v=(E^{\varepsilon}v_{1},E^{\varepsilon}v_{2},E^{\varepsilon}v_{3})\in V
. Then, using Lemma
4.4
, we have

w|_{\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p}}=v,\quad w|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\quad\text{and}\quad\|w\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq C_{A}\|v\|_{H^{1}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p})}.

(4.15)

The constant is independent of
\varepsilon
.

Note that

\nabla\!\cdot w=0
in
\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p}
(since
w=v
there), hence

g:=\nabla\!\cdot w\in L^{2}(\Omega)
is supported in
\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{s}:=\Omega\setminus\overline{\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p}}
.
By the divergence theorem on
Y^{\varepsilon}_{\kappa,s}
and
(w\cdot{\bf n}^{\varepsilon})|_{\Gamma^{\varepsilon}_{s}}=0
(since
(v\cdot{\bf n}^{\varepsilon})|_{\Gamma^{\varepsilon}_{s}}=0
),

\int_{Y^{\varepsilon}_{\kappa,s}}g\,dx=\int_{Y^{\varepsilon}_{\kappa,s}}\nabla\cdot w\,dx=-\int_{\partial Y^{\varepsilon}_{\kappa,s}}w\cdot{\bf n}^{\varepsilon}\,dS=0,

so
g\in L^{2}_{0}(Y^{\varepsilon}_{\kappa,s})
. Hence, we have
g\in L^{2}_{0,\mathrm{cell}}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{s})\subset L^{2}_{0}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{s})
.

From Lemma
4.2
, there exists a linear operator

{\mathcal{B}}^{\varepsilon}_{s}:L^{2}_{0,\mathrm{cell}}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{s})\to H^{1}_{0}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{s})^{3}
such that

\nabla\!\cdot({\mathcal{B}}^{\varepsilon}_{s}g)=g,\quad\text{in $\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{s}$}\quad\text{and}\quad\|{\mathcal{B}}^{\varepsilon}_{s}g\|_{H^{1}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{s})}\leq C_{s}\|g\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{s})},

(4.16)

with
C_{s}
independent of
\varepsilon
.

Let us set
z_{\varepsilon}:={\mathcal{B}}^{\varepsilon}_{s}g\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{s})^{3}
.
Extend
z_{\varepsilon}
by zero to
\Omega
(denote the extension by the same letter).
Define

\mathcal{E}^{\varepsilon}v\ :=\ w-z_{\varepsilon}\ \in H^{1}(\Omega)^{3}.

Step 2:
We give the properties of
{\mathcal{E}}^{\varepsilon}
.

Since
z_{\varepsilon}=0
in
\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p}
, we get
(\mathcal{E}^{\varepsilon}v)|_{\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p}}=w|_{\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p}}=v
,
proving (i), and also
(\mathcal{E}^{\varepsilon}v)|_{\partial\Omega}=w|_{\partial\Omega}-0=0
.
Moreover, in
\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{s}
we have
\nabla\!\cdot(\mathcal{E}^{\varepsilon}v)=g-\nabla\!\cdot z_{\varepsilon}=0
, and in

\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p}
the divergence vanishes because
\mathcal{E}^{\varepsilon}v=v
and
v
is solenoidal.
Thus (ii) holds and
\mathcal{E}^{\varepsilon}v\in V^{\sigma}
.

For (iii), using the zero extension of
z_{\varepsilon}
and (
4.15
)–(
4.16
), we have

\|\mathcal{E}^{\varepsilon}v\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq\|w\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+\|z_{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq C_{A}\|v\|_{H^{1}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p})}+C_{s}\|g\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{s})}.

Since
\|g\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{s})}=\|\nabla\!\cdot w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\|w\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq C_{A}\|v\|_{H^{1}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p})}
, we obtain

\|\mathcal{E}^{\varepsilon}v\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq C\,\|v\|_{H^{1}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p})}\quad\text{with }C:=C_{A}(1+C_{s}),

which is independent of
\varepsilon
.

Step 3:
Energy adjoint and time commutation.

Let
J_{\varepsilon}^{\sigma}:H_{\varepsilon}\to(V_{\varepsilon}^{\sigma})^{\ast}
and
J^{\sigma}:H\to(V^{\sigma})^{\ast}
denote the
canonical injections,

\langle J_{\varepsilon}^{\sigma}h_{\varepsilon},v\rangle_{(V_{\varepsilon}^{\sigma})^{\ast},V_{\varepsilon}^{\sigma}}=(h_{\varepsilon},v)_{H_{\varepsilon}},\quad v\in V_{\varepsilon}^{\sigma},\qquad\langle J^{\sigma}h,v\rangle_{(V^{\sigma})^{\ast},V^{\sigma}}=(h,v)_{H},\quad v\in V^{\sigma}.

Since
\mathcal{E}^{\varepsilon}:V_{\varepsilon}^{\sigma}\to V^{\sigma}
is bounded uniformly in
\varepsilon
by (iii), we can
define its
H
–adjoint
\mathcal{E}^{\varepsilon,\sharp}:V^{\sigma}\to V_{\varepsilon}^{\sigma}
by

\langle J^{\sigma}\mathcal{E}^{\varepsilon}v_{\varepsilon},\eta\rangle_{(V^{\sigma})^{\ast},V^{\sigma}}=\langle J_{\varepsilon}^{\sigma}v_{\varepsilon},\mathcal{E}^{\varepsilon,\sharp}\eta\rangle_{(V_{\varepsilon}^{\sigma})^{\ast},V_{\varepsilon}^{\sigma}}\quad\forall\,v_{\varepsilon}\in V_{\varepsilon}^{\sigma},\ \eta\in V^{\sigma},

and set
\mathcal{F}^{\varepsilon}:=(\mathcal{E}^{\varepsilon,\sharp})^{\ast}:(V_{\varepsilon}^{\sigma})^{\ast}\to(V^{\sigma})^{\ast}
.

Now let
v^{\varepsilon}\in\mathfrak{U}^{\varepsilon}:=L^{2}(S;V_{\varepsilon}^{\sigma})\cap H^{1}(S;(V_{\varepsilon}^{\sigma})^{\ast})
.
Repeating verbatim the argument of Lemma
4.5
, but with the solenoidal Gelfand triples

V_{\varepsilon}^{\sigma}\hookrightarrow H_{\varepsilon}\hookrightarrow(V_{\varepsilon}^{\sigma})^{\ast},\qquad V^{\sigma}\hookrightarrow H\hookrightarrow(V^{\sigma})^{\ast},

where

H_{\varepsilon}:=\overline{V_{\varepsilon}^{\sigma}}^{\,L^{2}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})^{3}},\qquad H:=\overline{V^{\sigma}}^{\,L^{2}(\Omega)^{3}}.

we obtain

\mathcal{E}^{\varepsilon}v^{\varepsilon}\in L^{2}(S;V^{\sigma})\cap H^{1}(S;(V^{\sigma})^{\ast})=:\mathfrak{U},\qquad\partial_{t}(\mathcal{E}^{\varepsilon}v^{\varepsilon})=\mathcal{F}^{\varepsilon}\,\partial_{t}v^{\varepsilon}\quad\text{in }L^{2}(S;(V^{\sigma})^{\ast}).

Moreover,
\|\mathcal{F}^{\varepsilon}\|=\|\mathcal{E}^{\varepsilon}\|\leq C
with
C
independent of
\varepsilon
, so

\|\partial_{t}(\mathcal{E}^{\varepsilon}v^{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{2}(S;(V^{\sigma})^{\ast})}\leq\|\mathcal{F}^{\varepsilon}\|\,\|\partial_{t}v^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(S;(V_{\varepsilon}^{\sigma})^{\ast})}\leq C\,\|\partial_{t}v^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(S;(V_{\varepsilon}^{\sigma})^{\ast})},

which is exactly (
4.14
).
This completes the proof.

∎

Lemma 4.7

(Korn’s inequality)
.

For every
{\bf u}\in{\bf H}^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})
, then we have

\|{\bf u}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p})}\leq C\|D({\bf u})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p})}.

(4.17)

The constant is independent of
\varepsilon
, but depends on
\Omega
.

Proof.

From
[
24
,
48
]
, we have for every
{\bf u}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})^{3}
with
{\bf u}=0
on
\partial\Omega
, there exist an extension
{\bf v}\in H^{1}(\Omega)^{3}
such that

{\bf v}_{|\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p}}={\bf u},\quad{\bf v}=0,\quad\text{on $\partial\Omega$},\quad\|D({\bf v})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|D({\bf u})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p})}.

The constant is independent of
\varepsilon
.

Now, using Korn’s inequality on the extended field
{\bf v}
, we obtain

\|\nabla{\bf u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p})}\leq\|\nabla{\bf v}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|D({\bf v})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|D({\bf u})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p})}.

The constant is independent of
\varepsilon
but depends on
\Omega
. Then, using Poincaré inequality (see (
4.19
) in Remark
2
) gives the result.
This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 2
.

Since our porous medium
\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}
is a perforated domain in which
the size of the holes is proportional to the mutual distance (or period)

\varepsilon
, one has the following sharp Poincaré inequality (see, e.g.,

[
54
]
and also Lemma 1.5 in
[
43
]
): for all
v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})^{3}
,

\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}\;\leq\;C\,\varepsilon\,\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})},

(4.18)

where the constant
C>0
is independent of
\varepsilon
.

For the larger space

{\bf H}^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})

we also have a Poincaré inequality with a constant independent of

\varepsilon
, but without the factor
\varepsilon
. Indeed, proceeding as in the proof of Lemma
4.7
using extension by Lemma
4.4
, we get
for all

{\bf u}\in{\bf H}^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})
,

\|{\bf u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}\;\leq\;C\,\|\nabla{\bf u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})},

(4.19)

with a constant
C
that does not depend on
\varepsilon
.

Below, we present Poincaré-Wirtinger inequality for functions defined in
\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p}
, see
[
48
,
24
,
36
]
for similar inequalities for few types of perforated domains

Lemma 4.8

(Poincaré–Wirtinger on periodic perforations)
.

For every
u\in H^{1}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p})
with zero mean on
\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p}
, i.e.
u\in L^{2}_{0}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p})
, one has

\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p})}\;\leq\;C\,\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p})},

with
C>0
independent of
\varepsilon
.

Proof.

First, from Lemma
4.1
, we have, there exists a Bogovskiĭ operator

{\mathcal{B}}^{\varepsilon}_{p}:L^{2}_{0}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p})\longrightarrow H^{1}_{0}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p})^{3}\quad\text{with}\quad\nabla\!\cdot({\mathcal{B}}^{\varepsilon}_{p}f)=f\ \text{ in }\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p},\qquad\|\nabla{\mathcal{B}}^{\varepsilon}_{p}f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p})}\leq{C_{p}\over\varepsilon}\,\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p})},

where
C_{p}>0
is independent of
\varepsilon
.

Let
u\in H^{1}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p})
with
\int_{\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p}}u=0
and set
\Phi:={\mathcal{B}}^{\varepsilon}_{p}u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p})^{3}
.
Then
\nabla\!\cdot\Phi=u
in
\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p}
and
\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p})}\leq{C_{p}\over\varepsilon}\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p})}
.
Integrating by parts (the boundary term vanishes because
\Phi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p})^{3}
),

\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p})}^{2}=\int_{\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p}}u\,u=\int_{\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p}}u\,\nabla\!\cdot\Phi=-\int_{\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p}}\nabla u\cdot\Phi\leq\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p})}\,\|\Phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p})}.

Then, using Poincaré inequality (
4.18
) for
\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p}
, we get

\|\Phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p})}\leq C\varepsilon\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p})},

which gives

\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p})}^{2}\leq C\varepsilon\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p})}\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p})}\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p})}\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p})}.

This give the inequality with constant independent of
\varepsilon
.
∎

Below, we present an embedding result.

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
u\in H^{1}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p})
, then
u\in L^{r}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p})
for
r\in[2,6]
such that

\|u\|_{L^{r}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p})}\leq C\|u\|_{H^{1}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p})}.

The constant is independent of
\varepsilon
and
u
.

Proof.

Let
u\in H^{1}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p})
, then by Sobolev extension theorem, there exist a
\widetilde{u}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
such that

\|\widetilde{u}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq C\|u\|_{H^{1}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p})}.

The constant is independent of
\varepsilon
and
u
. Since
H^{1}(\Omega)
is continuous embedded in
L^{r}(\Omega)
for
r\in[2,6]
, we get

\|\widetilde{u}\|_{L^{r}(\Omega)}\leq C\|v\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}.

The constant depends only on
\Omega
, not on
\varepsilon
and
u
. Then, we get from the above two estimates

\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p})}\leq\|\widetilde{u}\|_{L^{r}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\widetilde{u}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\widetilde{u}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p})}.

This completes the proof.
∎

So, we have
H^{1}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p})
is continuously embedded in
L^{r}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p})
for
r\in[2,6]
with embedding constant independent of
\varepsilon
.

4.3
Convective trilinear form

Let us consider the convective trilinear form

b^{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w}):=\int_{\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p}}(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{w}\,dx,\quad\forall\,{\bf u},\,{\bf v},\,{\bf w}\in L^{2}(S;{\bf H}^{1}_{{\mathrm{div}}}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p})).

Then,

Lemma 4.10
.

For all
{\bf u},\,{\bf v},\,{\bf w}\in L^{2}(S;{\bf H}^{1}_{{\mathrm{div}}}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p}))
, the convective trilinear form satisfy the following:

Skew-symmetry:

b^{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w})=-b^{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{w},\mathbf{v}).

Orthogonality:

b^{\varepsilon}({\bf u},{\bf v},{\bf v})=\int_{\Omega}(\mathbf{u}\!\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{v}\,dx=0.

(4.20)

Identity:

b^{\varepsilon}({\bf u},{\bf u},{\bf v})=-\int_{\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p}}({\bf u}\otimes{\bf u}):\nabla{\bf v}\,dx,

(4.21)

where
\cdot\otimes\cdot
is the outer (vector) product and
\cdot:\cdot
is the Frobenius (matrix) product.

Continuity:

|b({\bf u},{\bf v},{\bf w})|\leq C_{0}\|{\bf u}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p})}\|{\bf v}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p})}\|{\bf w}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p})}.

(4.22)

The constant is independent of
\varepsilon
.

Proof.

The skew-symmetry is a direct consequence of integration by parts and divergence theorem combined with the fact

\int_{\partial\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p}}({\bf u}\cdot{\bf n}^{\varepsilon})({\bf v}\cdot{\bf w})\,dS=0.

The equality is consequence of the boundary conditions satisfied by
{\bf u}
.

Now, setting
\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{w}
gives

b^{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v},\mathbf{v})=-b^{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v},\mathbf{v}),

hence
2b^{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v},\mathbf{v})=0
, which imply (
4.20
).

Observe that, since
\nabla\cdot{\bf u}=0
in
S\times\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p}
and
{\bf u}=0
on
S\times\partial\Omega
, we have

\displaystyle b^{\varepsilon}({\bf u},{\bf u},{\bf v})

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p}}({\bf u}\cdot\nabla){\bf u}\cdot{\bf v}\,dx=\int_{\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p}}(\nabla\cdot({\bf u}\otimes{\bf u}))\cdot{\bf v}\,dx

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p}}({\bf u}\otimes{\bf u}):\nabla{\bf v}\,dx+\int_{\partial\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p}}({\bf u}\otimes{\bf u}){\bf n}^{\varepsilon}\cdot{\bf v}\,dS

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p}}({\bf u}\otimes{\bf u}):\nabla{\bf v}\,dx+\int_{\Gamma^{\varepsilon}_{s}}({\bf u}\otimes{\bf u}){\bf n}^{\varepsilon}\cdot{\bf v}\,dS.

Using
({\bf u}\otimes{\bf u}){\bf n}^{\varepsilon}=({\bf u}\cdot{\bf n}^{\varepsilon}){\bf u}
and
{\bf u}\cdot{\bf n}^{\varepsilon}=0
on
S\times\Gamma^{\varepsilon}_{s}
gives (
4.21
).

We have the following using Holder’s inequality

|b^{\varepsilon}({\bf u},{\bf v},{\bf w})|\leq\|{\bf u}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p})}\|\nabla{\bf v}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p})}\|{\bf w}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p})},

which together with the embedding result Lemma
4.9
and extension result Lemma
4.4
give (
4.22
).
This completes the proof.
∎

5
Existence and a priori estimates

This section presents the main existence result and a priori estimates for weak
solutions of the NSCH system (
1.1
). First, we derive a priori
estimates and the associated energy dissipation law for weak solutions. Then, we
prove existence by means of a Galerkin approximation.

5.1
A priori estimates and energy dissipation law

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
({\bf u}^{\varepsilon},\phi^{\varepsilon},\mu^{\varepsilon})\in\mathfrak{U}^{\varepsilon}\times\mathfrak{C}^{\varepsilon}\times\mathfrak{W}^{\varepsilon}
be a weak solution of (
1.1
) in the sense of the Definition
1
. Then, we have the following a priori estimates

\displaystyle\|{\bf u}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(S;L^{2}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}))}+\|{\bf u}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(S;H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}))}+\|\partial_{t}{\bf u}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(S;{\bf H}^{-1}_{{\mathrm{div}}}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p}))}

\displaystyle\leq C\sqrt{\lambda^{\varepsilon}},

(5.1)

\displaystyle\|\phi^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(S;H^{1}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p}))}+\|\partial_{t}\phi^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(S;H^{-1}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p}))}+\|\mu^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(S;H^{1}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p}))}

\displaystyle\leq C.

Moreover, there exist a
p^{\varepsilon}\in L^{2}(S;L^{2}_{0}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p}))
satisfying

\int_{S}\big\langle\partial_{t}{\bf u}^{\varepsilon},\,\varphi\big\rangle\,{\mathrm{d}}t+\int_{S\times\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p}}({\bf u}\cdot\nabla){\bf u}\cdot\varphi\,{\mathrm{d}}(x,t)+\int_{S\times\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p}}{\mathbb{A}}^{\varepsilon}D({\bf u}^{\varepsilon}):D(\varphi)\,{\mathrm{d}}(x,t)\\
+\int_{S\times\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p}}\lambda^{\varepsilon}\phi^{\varepsilon}\nabla\mu^{\varepsilon}\cdot\varphi\,{\mathrm{d}}(x,t)-\int_{S\times\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p}}{\bf g}^{\varepsilon}\cdot\varphi\,{\mathrm{d}}(x,t)=\int_{S\times\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p}}p^{\varepsilon}\nabla\cdot\varphi\,{\mathrm{d}}(x,t),

(5.2)

for all
\varphi\in L^{2}(S;H_{0}^{1}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p})^{3})
. Furthermore, we have

\|p^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(S;L^{2}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p}))}\leq C\sqrt{\lambda^{\varepsilon}}.

(5.3)

The constant(s) are independent of
\varepsilon
.

Proof.

The proof is divided into

5.2

Below, we give the existence of weak solution for the microscopic NSCH system (

Theorem 5.4
.

There exists a weak solution
({\bf u}^{\varepsilon},\phi^{\varepsilon},\mu^{\varepsilon})\in\mathfrak{U}^{\varepsilon}\times\mathfrak{C}^{\varepsilon}\times\mathfrak{W}^{\varepsilon}
of the problem (

Proof.

We prove the existence by a four-step Galerkin procedure.

Step 1. Stokes-type eigenfunctions as Galerkin basis.

We set

V:={\bf H}^{1}_{\mathrm{{\mathrm{div}}}}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}),\qquad H:=\overline{V}^{\,L^{2}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})^{3}},

where
H
denotes the closure of
V
in
L^{2}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})^{3}
.
Both
V
and
H
are separable Hilbert spaces, and

V\hookrightarrow H

is a continuous, dense, and compact embedding (Rellich–Kondrachov on the
bounded Lipschitz domain
\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}
with boundary conditions
(
1.1
)
3
).

For each fixed
\varepsilon>0
, we introduce two symmetric bilinear forms on
V
:

a_{t}^{\varepsilon}(w,v):=\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}{\mathbb{A}}^{\varepsilon}(t,x)\,D(w):D(v)\,{\mathrm{d}}x,\qquad a_{0}^{\varepsilon}(w,v):=\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}D(w):D(v)\,{\mathrm{d}}x,\qquad w,v\in V.

By the ellipticity and boundedness assumptions (
3.1
) on
{\mathbb{A}}^{\varepsilon}

constants

\varepsilon
and

\kappa_{1}\,a_{0}^{\varepsilon}(w,w)\;\leq\;a_{t}^{\varepsilon}(w,w)\;\leq\;\kappa_{2}\,a_{0}^{\varepsilon}(w,w),\qquad\forall\,w\in V.

(5.31)

By Korn’s inequality on
V
(using
w=0
on
\partial\Omega
and

w\cdot{\bf n}^{\varepsilon}=0
on
\Gamma_{s}^{\varepsilon}
), there exist constants

0<c_{1}\leq c_{2}<\infty
, independent of
\varepsilon

c_{1}\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}\;\leq\;\|D(w)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}\;\leq\;c_{2}\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})},\qquad\forall\,w\in V.

Hence the reference form

is continuous and coercive on
V
, and its
energy norm is equivalent to the
H^{1}
-norm:

k_{1}\|w\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}^{2}\;\leq\;a_{0}^{\varepsilon}(w,w)\;\leq\;k_{2}\|w\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}^{2},\qquad\forall\,w\in V,

for some
0<k_{1}\leq k_{2}<\infty

\varepsilon
.
Combining this with (

is also
uniformly equivalent to the

-norm:

k^{\prime}_{1}\|w\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}^{2}\;\leq\;a_{t}^{\varepsilon}(w,w)\;\leq\;k^{\prime}_{2}\|w\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}^{2},\qquad\forall\,w\in V,\ \forall\,t\in S,

for suitable

\varepsilon
and

To construct a time-independent Galerkin basis, we apply the abstract

.
Define the operator

and let

be the canonical injection,

By the Lax–Milgram theorem,

is an isomorphism. We then set

The operator

is linear, self-adjoint, positive, compact on

, and
does not depend on time.
Therefore, by the spectral theorem for compact self-adjoint operators, there
exists a
family of Stokes-type eigenfunctions

\{\psi_{k}^{\varepsilon}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset V
such that:

\{\psi_{k}^{\varepsilon}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is an orthonormal basis of
H
with respect to the

inner product and an orthogonal basis of

with respect to

. Each

belongs to

, hence is divergence-free in

and satisfies

Thus the incompressibility constraint and all velocity boundary conditions are
built into the Galerkin basis, while the actual viscosity form

remains

for all

.

For later use we also fix an

-orthonormal basis

,

These spaces will be used for the Galerkin approximation of the velocity and concentration fields,

Step 2. Galerkin approximation, matrix system, and reduction to ODEs.

For fixed

and

We seek approximate solutions to (

\mathbf{u}_{n}^{\varepsilon}(t,x)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}^{n}(t)\,\psi_{j}(x),\qquad\phi_{n}^{\varepsilon}(t,x)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}b_{j}^{n}(t)\,\zeta_{j}(x),\qquad\mu_{n}^{\varepsilon}(t,x)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}c_{j}^{n}(t)\,\zeta_{j}(x),

where

,

and

are real-valued functions of time. The initial coefficients

and

are given by the Leray

-projections of the initial data

\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}_{0}
and
\phi_{0}
onto
V_{n}
and
W_{n}
, respectively.

The Galerkin approximation of the weak formulation of (
1.1
) reads: for a.e.

and for all
\varphi_{1}\in V_{n}
,
\varphi_{2},\varphi_{3}\in W_{n}
,

\displaystyle\left\langle\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}_{n}^{\varepsilon},\varphi_{1}\right\rangle+\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}(\mathbf{u}_{n}^{\varepsilon}\!\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}_{n}^{\varepsilon}\cdot\varphi_{1}\,{\mathrm{d}}x+a_{t}^{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{u}_{n}^{\varepsilon},\varphi_{1})+\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}\lambda^{\varepsilon}\phi_{n}^{\varepsilon}\nabla\mu_{n}^{\varepsilon}\cdot\varphi_{1}\,{\mathrm{d}}x=\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}\mathbf{g}^{\varepsilon}\cdot\varphi_{1}\,{\mathrm{d}}x,

(5.32)

\displaystyle\left\langle\partial_{t}\phi_{n}^{\varepsilon},\varphi_{2}\right\rangle+\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}G(\phi_{n}^{\varepsilon})\,\varphi_{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}x+\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}\nabla\mu_{n}^{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\varphi_{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}x=\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}\phi_{n}^{\varepsilon}\,\mathbf{u}_{n}^{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\varphi_{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}x,

(5.33)

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}\mu_{n}^{\varepsilon}\,\varphi_{3}\,{\mathrm{d}}x+\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}\nabla\phi_{n}^{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\varphi_{3}\,{\mathrm{d}}x+\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}f\!\left(\phi_{n}^{\varepsilon}\right)\varphi_{3}\,{\mathrm{d}}x=0.

This formulation is weak in space (test functions in

and

) but strong in time.

Testing (

)–(
5.34
) with basis functions

,

yields, for each

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}M_{ij}\,\dot{a}_{j}^{n}(t)+\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}N_{i;jk}\,a_{j}^{n}(t)a_{k}^{n}(t)+\sum_{j=1}^{n}B_{ij}(t)\,a_{j}^{n}(t)+\lambda^{\varepsilon}\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}C_{i;jk}\,b_{j}^{n}(t)c_{k}^{n}(t)=F^{(g)}_{i}(t),

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}S_{ij}\,\dot{b}_{j}^{n}(t)+G_{i}(b^{n}(t))+\sum_{j=1}^{n}D_{ij}\,c_{j}^{n}(t)=H_{i}(a^{n}(t),b^{n}(t)),

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}S_{ij}\,c_{j}^{n}(t)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}D_{ij}\,b_{j}^{n}(t)+F_{i}(b^{n}(t)),

where the matrices and nonlinear functionals

\displaystyle M_{ij}

\displaystyle=(\psi_{j},\psi_{i}),\qquad S_{ij}=(\zeta_{j},\zeta_{i}),\quad B_{ij}=a_{t}^{\varepsilon}(\psi_{j},\psi_{i}),\quad N_{i;jk}=\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}(\psi_{j}\cdot\nabla)\psi_{k}\cdot\psi_{i}\,{\mathrm{d}}x,

\displaystyle C_{i;jk}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}\zeta_{j}\,\nabla\zeta_{k}\cdot\psi_{i}\,{\mathrm{d}}x,\qquad D_{ij}=(\nabla\zeta_{j},\nabla\zeta_{i}),\quad F_{i}^{(g)}(t)=\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}\mathbf{g}^{\varepsilon}(t,x)\cdot\psi_{i}(x)\,{\mathrm{d}}x,

\displaystyle H_{i}(a^{n},b^{n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}b_{j}^{n}a_{k}^{n}\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}\zeta_{j}\,\psi_{k}\cdot\nabla\zeta_{i}\,{\mathrm{d}}x,\quad G_{i}(b^{n})=\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}G\!\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}b_{k}^{n}\zeta_{k}\right)\zeta_{i}\,{\mathrm{d}}x,

\displaystyle F_{i}(b^{n})

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}f(\phi_{n}^{\varepsilon})\,\zeta_{i}\,{\mathrm{d}}x=\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}f\!\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}b_{k}^{n}\zeta_{k}\right)\zeta_{i}\,{\mathrm{d}}x,

are determined by the chosen bases.

Because the basis functions are time-independent, this is a finite-dimensional ODE/ODE–algebraic
system for the coefficient vectors

a^{n}(t)=(a_{1}^{n}(t),\dots,a_{n}^{n}(t)),\quad b^{n}(t)=(b_{1}^{n}(t),\dots,b_{n}^{n}(t)),\quad c^{n}(t)=(c_{1}^{n}(t),\dots,c_{n}^{n}(t)).

The starting values
a^{n}(0)
and
b^{n}(0)
are taken as the
L^{2}
-projections of the initial data

\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}_{0}
and
\phi_{0}
onto
V_{n}
and
W_{n}
, respectively. The coefficients
c^{n}(0)
are
then determined by the algebraic relation defining
\mu_{n}^{\varepsilon}(0)
.

Since the mass matrix
S
is symmetric positive definite (in fact

by orthonormality of

\{\zeta_{i}\}
), it is invertible. From the algebraic relation

S\,c^{n}=D\,b^{n}+F(b^{n})

we obtain

c^{n}=S^{-1}\!\big(D\,b^{n}+F(b^{n})\big).

(5.35)

Substituting (

) into the Galerkin equation for
\phi_{n}^{\varepsilon}
gives

S\,\dot{b}^{n}+G(b^{n})+D\,S^{-1}\!\big(D\,b^{n}+F(b^{n})\big)=H(a^{n},b^{n}),

where
G(b^{n})
denotes the vector
(G_{i}(b^{n}))_{i=1}^{n}
. Similarly, the momentum equation can be
written as

M\,\dot{a}^{n}+N(a^{n},a^{n})+B\,a^{n}+\lambda^{\varepsilon}C\!\Big(b^{n},\,S^{-1}\!\big(D\,b^{n}+F(b^{n})\big)\Big)=F^{(g)}(t),

where

and

are the vectors with components

N_{i}(a^{n},a^{n})=\sum_{j,k}N_{i;jk}a_{j}^{n}a_{k}^{n}
and

.

Thus the coefficient vectors
a^{n}
and
b^{n}
satisfy the finite-dimensional ODE system

\dot{a}^{n}=\Phi_{1}(a^{n},b^{n},t),\qquad\dot{b}^{n}=\Phi_{2}(a^{n},b^{n},t),

(5.36)

where the right-hand sides are defined by

\displaystyle\Phi_{1}(a^{n},b^{n},t)

\displaystyle:=M^{-1}\!\Big[-\,N(a^{n},a^{n})-B\,a^{n}-\lambda^{\varepsilon}C\!\big(b^{n},S^{-1}(Db^{n}+F(b^{n}))\big)+F^{(g)}(t)\Big],

\displaystyle\Phi_{2}(a^{n},b^{n},t)

\displaystyle:=S^{-1}\!\Big[H(a^{n},b^{n})-G(b^{n})-D\,S^{-1}\!\big(Db^{n}+F(b^{n})\big)\Big].

Although the finite–dimensional system (
5.36
) is nonlinear,
the standard ODE theory in finite dimensions applies.

It is convenient to rewrite the finite-dimensional system (
5.36
)
in the abstract form

z^{n}(t)=\big(a^{n}(t),b^{n}(t)\big)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2n},\qquad\dot{z}^{n}(t)=\Phi(t,z^{n}(t)),\qquad z^{n}(0)=z_{0}^{n},

(5.37)

where
\Phi(t,z):=\big(\Phi_{1}(a,b,t),\Phi_{2}(a,b,t)\big)
with

z=(a,b)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}
and
t\in(0,T)
.

Because the body force
\mathbf{g}^{\varepsilon}
(and hence
F^{(g)}
) is only in

L^{2}(0,T)
, the right–hand sides
\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2}
are, in general, only
measurable in time but locally Lipschitz in the variables
(a^{n},b^{n})
.
More precisely, the linear (algebraic) contributions in (
5.36
),
such as multiplications by the fixed matrices

and by the
(measurable, bounded) matrix

, are linear in the variables
(a^{n},b^{n})
and
hence smooth with respect to
(a^{n},b^{n})
for each fixed
t\in(0,T)
.
The convective term
a^{n}\mapsto N(a^{n},a^{n})
is a quadratic polynomial
in
a^{n}
and hence
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
The couplings
H(a^{n},b^{n})
,
F(b^{n})
and
G(b^{n})
are obtained by
composing the nonlinearities

,

with the finite linear combination

\sum_{k=1}^{n}b_{k}^{n}\zeta_{k}
and then integrating against the fixed basis

\{\zeta_{i}\}
. In particular, each component

G_{i}(b^{n})=\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}G\!\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}b_{k}^{n}\zeta_{k}(x)\right)\zeta_{i}(x)\,{\mathrm{d}}x,\qquad F_{i}(b^{n})=\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}f\!\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}b_{k}^{n}\zeta_{k}(x)\right)\zeta_{i}(x)\,{\mathrm{d}}x,

defines a
\mathcal{C}^{1}
map
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}
. Hence

,
F
and
G
are

\mathcal{C}^{1}
maps
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
or
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
as appropriate.

Composing these
\mathcal{C}^{1}
maps with the fixed matrices

and

shows that

\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2}:(0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

are continuous in
(a^{n},b^{n})
for each

, and locally Lipschitz in

(a^{n},b^{n})
uniformly in

. Moreover, for each radius

there exists
a constant
C_{R}>0
such that

\big|\Phi_{1}(t,a^{n},b^{n})\big|+\big|\Phi_{2}(t,a^{n},b^{n})\big|\leq C_{R}+C\,|F^{(g)}(t)|,\qquad\text{for all }|(a^{n},b^{n})|\leq R,

with

independent of
t
and

. Since
F^{(g)}\in L^{2}(0,T)
and

, we have

, so the right–hand sides admit an

–bound on bounded sets in
(a^{n},b^{n})
.

Thus the hypotheses of the Carathéodory existence theorem (see
[
20
, Chapter I]
) are satisfied for the system
(
5.36

(a^{n}(0),b^{n}(0))
(given by the

L^{2}
–projections of the initial data),
there exists a maximal time
T_{n}^{\ast}\in(0,T]
and a unique
solution

(a^{n},b^{n})\in W^{1,1}_{\mathrm{loc}}\big([0,T_{n}^{\ast});{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}\big)

of (

absolutely continuous in time.

Uniqueness follows from the local Lipschitz property in
(a^{n},b^{n})
. Indeed, if

(a^{n},b^{n})
and
(\tilde{a}^{n},\tilde{b}^{n})
are two solutions with the same
initial data, then for any radius

and all

, where

and

\sup_{s\in[0,T_{n}]}|(a^{n},b^{n})(s)|,|\tilde{a}^{n},\tilde{b}^{n})(s)|\leq R

local Lipschitz estimate gives

\|(a^{n},b^{n})(t)-(\tilde{a}^{n},\tilde{b}^{n})(t)\|\leq L_{R}\int_{0}^{t}\|(a^{n},b^{n})(s)-(\tilde{a}^{n},\tilde{b}^{n})(s)\|\,ds,

for some Lipschitz constant

We now relate the last term to the potential energy. Using

6
Homogenization of NSCH system with source term

This section presents the main results on the homogenization of the NSCH system
(
1.1
). We begin by recalling the periodic unfolding operator, which is
the main tool used to derive the two-scale limit system and the corresponding
homogenized model. We then identify the macroscopic limit fields, using the
a priori estimates obtained in Section
5.1
.

Next, we distinguish two cases according to the parameter
\lambda
in
(
1.3
). First, we derive the homogenized system in the case
\lambda=0
,
which leads to a Stokes–Cahn–Hilliard type model. Then, we consider the case

\lambda\neq 0
, for which the limit system is of Navier–Stokes–Cahn–Hilliard
type.

6.1
Unfolding operator for a perforated domain

The main tool for homogenization in the perforated domain
\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}

and for the derivation of the two-scale limit system is the periodic unfolding
operator adapted to perforated media, denoted by
\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}^{\ast}
.
It was first introduced in
[
19
]
and further developed in

[
16
]
. For a detailed presentation we refer to

[
18
, Chapter 4]
.

We recall the definition of the periodic unfolding operators

\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}
and
\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}^{\ast}
for functions defined
on
\Omega
and
\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}
, respectively.

Definition 2
.

Let
\Omega_{T}=S\times\Omega
. For every measurable function

\psi
on
\Omega_{T}
the unfolding operator

\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}:L^{1}(\Omega_{T})\to L^{1}(S\times\Omega\times Y)
is
defined by

\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}(\psi)(t,x,y)\doteq\begin{cases}\psi\bigl(t,\varepsilon\bigl[\tfrac{x}{\varepsilon}\bigr]+\varepsilon y\bigr),&\text{for a.e. }(t,x,y)\in S\times\widehat{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}\times Y,\\[4.0pt]
0,&\text{for a.e. }(t,x,y)\in S\times\Lambda^{\varepsilon}\times Y.\end{cases}

For every measurable function
\psi
on
S\times\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}
the
perforated unfolding operator

\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}^{\ast}:L^{1}(S\times\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})\to L^{1}(S\times\Omega\times Y_{p})
is defined by

\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}^{\ast}(\psi)(t,x,y)\doteq\begin{cases}\psi\bigl(t,\varepsilon\bigl[\tfrac{x}{\varepsilon}\bigr]+\varepsilon y\bigr),&\text{for a.e. }(t,x,y)\in S\times\widehat{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}\times Y_{p},\\[4.0pt]
0,&\text{for a.e. }(t,x,y)\in S\times\Lambda^{\varepsilon}\times Y_{p}.\end{cases}

In particular, for a measurable function
\psi
defined on
S\times\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}
,
we have the consistency relation

{\cal T}_{\varepsilon}^{\ast}(\psi)={\cal T}_{\varepsilon}(\psi_{|S\times\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p}})={\cal T}_{\varepsilon}(\psi)_{|S\times\Omega\times Y_{p}}

i.e.
\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}^{\ast}
is just the restriction of

\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}
to the pore region
Y_{p}
in the microscopic variable.

Unfolding criterion for integrals in
\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}
(u.c.i.).

For every
\psi\in L^{1}(S\times\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})
one has

\frac{1}{|Y_{p}|}\int_{S\times\Omega\times Y_{p}}\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}^{\ast}(\psi)(t,x,y)\,{\mathrm{d}}(y,x,t)=\int_{S\times\widehat{\Omega}_{p}^{\varepsilon}}\psi(t,x)\,{\mathrm{d}}(x,t)=\int_{S\times\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}\psi(t,x)\,{\mathrm{d}}(x,t)-\int_{S\times\Lambda^{\varepsilon}}\psi(t,x)\,{\mathrm{d}}(x,t).

Since
\Omega
has Lipschitz boundary, we have
|\Lambda^{\varepsilon}|\to 0
as

\varepsilon\to 0
. Hence

\left|\int_{S\times\Lambda^{\varepsilon}}\psi(t,x)\,{\mathrm{d}}(x,t)\right|\longrightarrow 0\qquad\text{as }\varepsilon\to 0,

and therefore

\left|\frac{1}{|Y_{p}|}\int_{S\times\Omega\times Y_{p}}\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}^{\ast}(\psi)(t,x,y)\,{\mathrm{d}}(y,x,t)-\int_{S\times\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}\psi(t,x)\,{\mathrm{d}}(x,t)\right|\longrightarrow 0\qquad\text{as }\varepsilon\to 0.

(6.1)

Finally, the unfolding operators preserve products and compositions in the
expected way. If
u:S\times\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}\to\mathbb{R}
and

v:S\times\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p}\to\mathbb{R}
are measurable, and
H:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is continuous,
then

\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}^{\ast}(u\,v)=\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}^{\ast}(u)\,\mathcal{T}^{\ast}_{\varepsilon}(v),\qquad\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}^{\ast}(H(u))=H\bigl(\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}^{\ast}(u)\bigr).

Lemma 6.1
.

For each
\varepsilon>0
, let
v^{\varepsilon}\in L^{2}(S;H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}))
and
let
\widetilde{v}^{\varepsilon}\in L^{2}(S;H^{1}(\Omega))
denote its extension from

\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}
to
\Omega

4.4

Then there exists

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}^{\ast}(v^{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle\to v

\displaystyle\text{strongly in }L^{2}(\Omega_{T}\times Y_{p}),

The classification of ACM curves on a surface in ℙ 3 \mathds{P}^{3}
Abel Castorena, Monserrat Vite

Abstract
. We classify ACM curves contained in a surface of degree d d in ℙ 3 \mathds{P}^{3} in terms of weak admissible pairs. In the case of a very general smooth determinantal quartic surface, we provide a geometric description of these curves and compute their Picard classes on the surface. Finally, we present a generalization to ACM closed subvarieties of codimension 1 1 on a hypersurface in ℙ n \mathds{P}^{n} .



1.
Introduction

Arithmetically Cohen–Macaulay curves (ACM curves, for short) play a central role in algebraic geometry due to their rich structure and favorable cohomological properties. They are defined as projective curves whose homogeneous coordinate ring is Cohen–Macaulay. ACM curves generalize the notion of complete intersections and form a natural class of objects for the study of liaison theory. For example, Apéry and Gaeta showed that a smooth curve
C
in
\mathds{P}^{3}
lies in the linkage class of a complete intersection if and only if it is an ACM curve (
[
1
]
,
[
3
]
).

Moreover, ACM curves with a fixed minimal free resolution form an open smooth subset of the corresponding Hilbert scheme, whose dimension can be computed directly from the Betti numbers of the resolution
[
2
]
. The degree and genus are also determined by these numbers
[
8
]
. In addition, the Hilbert–Burch Theorem states that a minimal set of generators of the ideal of an ACM curve is given by the minors of a matrix of size
(t+1)\times t

[
5
]
*Prop. 8.7. This theorem plays a fundamental role in the present work.

Regarding the classification of ACM curves, a well-known result for a smooth quadric surface
Q
states that a curve of type
(s,t)
on
Q
is ACM if and only if
|s-t|\leq 1

[
4
]
*III, Ex. 5.6. In
[
10
]
, a complete description of ACM curves on a smooth cubic surface is given, and in
[
9
]
the author provides a classification of initialized ACM line bundles on smooth quartic surfaces in
\mathds{P}^{3}
. A limitation of these results is that the smoothness hypothesis plays a crucial role.

The main purpose of this paper is to classify all ACM curves (not necessarily smooth or irreducible) contained in an arbitrary surface of degree
d
in
\mathds{P}^{3}
. The idea is the following: we introduce the notion of a weak determinantal surface (see Definition
2.4
), which generalizes the definition of a determinantal surface given in
[
6
]
*Def. 2. We prove that surfaces that are not weak determinantal contain only ACM curves that are complete intersections. Furthermore, this definition allows us to determine all possible minimal free resolutions of ACM curves contained in a weak determinantal surface.

The main theorem of this paper is the following:

Theorem 1

(Classification of ACM curves on a surface of degree
d
in
\mathds{P}^{3}
)
.

Let
X=V(F)
be a surface of degree
d
in
\mathds{P}^{3}
, and let
C\subseteq X
be a curve. Then
C
is ACM if and only if one of the following cases occurs:

(1)

The surface
X
is not weak determinantal, and
C
is a complete intersection.

(2)

The surface
X
is weak determinantal of type
\{(\hat{a}_{i},\hat{b}_{i})\}_{i\in I}
and only of these types (see Definition
2.4
). In this case,
C
satisfies one of the following:

(i)

C
is a complete intersection.

(ii)

If
C
is not a complete intersection and
F
is a minimal generator of the ideal of
C
, then
C
has the following minimal free resolution:

0\to\bigoplus_{j=1}^{t}\mathcal{O}_{\mathds{P}^{3}}(-(b_{j}+k))\to\bigoplus_{i=1}^{t}\mathcal{O}_{\mathds{P}^{3}}(-(a_{i}+k))\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathds{P}^{3}}(-d)\to\mathscr{I}_{C}\to 0,

where
(\hat{a}_{j}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{t}),\hat{b}_{j}=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{t}))
for some
j\in I
and some
k\in\mathds{Z}
.

(iii)

If
C
is not a complete intersection and
F
is not a minimal generator of the ideal of
C
, then
C
has the following minimal free resolution:

0\to\bigoplus_{i=1,i\neq j_{0}}^{t}\mathcal{O}_{\mathds{P}^{3}}(-d+b_{j_{0}}-b_{i})\to\bigoplus_{i=1}^{t}\mathcal{O}_{\mathds{P}^{3}}(-d+b_{j_{0}}-a_{i})\to\mathscr{I}_{C}\to 0,

where
(\hat{a}_{j}=(a_{1}+k,\ldots,a_{t}+k),\hat{b}_{j}=(b_{1}+k,\ldots,b_{j_{0}-1}+k,d,b_{j_{0}+1}+k,\ldots,b_{t}+k))
for some
j\in I
,
j_{0}\in\{1,\ldots,t\}
, and some
k\in\mathds{Z}
.

As an application, we compare our results with the known cases of smooth surfaces of degrees two and three. We also compute all possible weak admissible pairs of degree
4
(see Definition
2.3
) and determine which of them define smooth weak determinantal surfaces. In particular, we prove the following result:

Corollary

(Corollary
4.2
)
.

If
X
is a smooth weak determinantal surface of degree
4
, then
X
is determinantal or of type
((0,0,1),(1,2,2))
.

Since the Picard group of a very general determinantal surface is known, we provide a complete geometric description of the ACM curves contained in a very general determinantal surface.

Organization:
Section
2
contains the definition of a weak determinantal surface and the proof of Theorem
Theorem 1
. Section
3

4

4
and, in the case of a smooth very general determinantal quartic surface

2.
ACM curves on a surface of degree
d

Let
D
be a nonzero effective divisor on a surface
X
of degree
d
in
\mathds{P}^{3}
. Then, from the exact sequence

(1)

0\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathds{P}^{3}}(-d)\to\mathscr{I}_{D}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}(-D)\to 0,

the line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)
is ACM if and only if
D
is an ACM curve, where
\mathscr{I}_{D}
denotes the ideal sheaf of
D
in
\mathds{P}^{3}
. We can say more even when
D
is not an ACM curve by relating it to linked curves.

Definition 2.1
.

Two equidimensional curves
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
in
\mathds{P}^{3}
are directly geometrically linked (or simply linked) by the complete intersection of two surfaces
S
and
S^{\prime}
if they have no components in common and
D_{1}\cup D_{2}=S\cap S^{\prime}
. In this case, we usually say that
D_{2}
is residual to
D_{1}
.

We denote by
M_{D}:=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathds{Z}}H^{1}(\mathds{P}^{3},\mathscr{I}_{D}(n))
the Rao module of
D
(see
[
7
]
*I, Def. 3.1). One relation between the Rao module and linked curves is that the Rao module
M_{D}
is invariant under linkage, up to duals and shifts in the grading
[
7
]
*III, Prop. 1.2.

A relation between two curves lying in the same Picard class on a surface
X
in
\mathds{P}^{3}
is given by the following proposition.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
be two curves contained in a surface
X
in
\mathds{P}^{3}
. If
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
have the same class in
\mathrm{Pic}(X)
, then
M_{D_{1}}\cong M_{D_{2}}
. Moreover, there exists a curve
B
such that each
D_{i}
is linked to
B
by the complete intersection of
X
with a surface of degree
n

[
7
]
*III, Def. 1.1.

Proof.

Since
[D_{1}]=[D_{2}]
in
\mathrm{Pic}(X)
, we have
\mathcal{O}_{X}(-D_{1})\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}(-D_{2})
. By the exact sequence (
1
), this yields

H^{1}(\mathds{P}^{3},\mathscr{I}_{D_{1}}(n))\cong H^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(nH-D_{1}))\cong H^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(nH-D_{2}))\cong H^{1}(\mathds{P}^{3},\mathscr{I}_{D_{2}}(n)).

Therefore,

M_{D_{1}}=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathds{Z}}H^{1}(\mathds{P}^{3},\mathscr{I}_{D_{1}}(n))\cong\bigoplus_{n\in\mathds{Z}}H^{1}(\mathds{P}^{3},\mathscr{I}_{D_{2}}(n))=M_{D_{2}}.

For the second statement, let
H
denote the hyperplane section class of
X
in
\mathrm{Pic}(X)
and set
A=[D_{1}]=[D_{2}]
. For
n\gg 0
, the class
nH-A
is very ample, hence there exists a curve
B\in|nH-A|
. Since
D_{1},D_{2}\in|A|=|nH-[B]|
, and this class corresponds to surfaces of degree
n
containing
B
, the result follows.

We now recall the definition of an admissible pair from
[
6
]
*Def. 1, with a slight modification in the third condition.

Definition 2.3
.

Consider two sequences of integers
\hat{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{t})
and
\hat{b}=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{t})
in
\mathbb{Z}^{t}
, with
t\geq 2
, satisfying:

(i)

a_{1}\leq a_{2}\leq\cdots\leq a_{t}
,

(ii)

b_{1}\leq b_{2}\leq\cdots\leq b_{t}
,

(iii)

a_{i}<b_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq t
.

We say that
(\hat{a},\hat{b})
is a weak admissible pair of degree
d:=\sum_{i=1}^{t}(b_{i}-a_{i})
and length
t
. Two weak admissible pairs
(\hat{a},\hat{b})
and
(\hat{a}^{\prime},\hat{b}^{\prime})
are said to be equivalent if there exists
k\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
a_{i}^{\prime}=a_{i}+k
and
b_{i}^{\prime}=b_{i}+k
for all
1\leq i\leq t
.

Using this definition, we introduce the notion of a weak determinantal surface.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
(\hat{a},\hat{b})
be a weak admissible pair of degree
d
and length
t
. A surface
X=V(F)\subset\mathds{P}^{3}
is called a weak determinantal surface of type
(\hat{a},\hat{b})
if
F=\det(S)
for some matrix
S=(m_{ij})
, where
m_{ij}
is a homogeneous polynomial of degree
b_{j}-a_{i}
. In the case
a_{i}\geq b_{j}
, we assume
m_{ij}=0
.

Remark 2.5
.

Observe that if
a_{i}<b_{j}
for all
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,t\}
, we recover the definition of a determinantal surface given in
[
6
]
*Def. 1, Def. 2. In particular, every determinantal surface is weak determinantal.

The relevance of this class of surfaces for our work is given by the following proposition.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
X=V(F)\subseteq\mathds{P}^{3}
be a surface of degree
d
. If there exists an ACM curve
C\subseteq X
that is not a complete intersection, then
X
is a weak determinantal surface of type
(\hat{a},\hat{b})
for some weak admissible pair
(\hat{a},\hat{b})
of degree
d
and length
t
.

Proof.

Let
C\subseteq X
be an ACM curve that is not a complete intersection. Two cases may occur.

Case 1:

F
is a minimal generator of the ideal of
C
.

By the Hilbert–Burch Theorem, there exists a matrix
M
of size
(t+1)\times t
such that
F
is the determinant of a
t\times t
minor of
M
. By degree considerations, we have
t\leq d
. After a change of coordinates, we may assume that the Betti numbers of the minimal free resolution of the ideal of
C
are ordered such that
a_{0}=d
,
a_{1}\leq\cdots\leq a_{t}
, and
b_{1}\leq\cdots\leq b_{t}
. Let
m=(m_{ij})
be the
t\times t
submatrix of
M
such that
\det(m)=F
and
\deg(m_{ij})=b_{j}-a_{i}
. Setting
\hat{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{t})
and
\hat{b}=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{t})
, it follows that
X
is weak determinantal of type
(\hat{a},\hat{b})
.

Case 2:

F
is not a minimal generator of the ideal of
C
.

Let
F_{1},\ldots,F_{t}
be a minimal generating set of the ideal of
C
. By the Hilbert–Burch Theorem, there exists a matrix
M=(m_{ij})
of size
t\times(t-1)
such that each
F_{i}
is the determinant of a
(t-1)\times(t-1)
minor of
M
. Since
F
lies in the ideal of
C
, there exist homogeneous polynomials
m_{1t},\ldots,m_{tt}\in\mathds{C}[x,y,z,w]
such that

F=\sum_{i=1}^{t}(-1)^{i}m_{it}F_{i}.

Let
S
be the matrix obtained by completing
M
with the column
(m_{it})
. Expanding
\det(S)
along the last column yields
\det(S)=\pm F
. The degrees satisfy
\deg(m_{ij})=b_{j}-a_{i}
, where
a_{1},\ldots,a_{t}
and
b_{1},\ldots,b_{t-1}
are the Betti numbers of the minimal free resolution of the ideal of
C
, ordered increasing. Setting
b_{t}=d
,
\hat{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{t})
, and

\hat{b}=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{j_{0}-1},b_{t},b_{j_{0}},\ldots,b_{t-1})\quad\text{if }b_{j_{0}-1}\leq b_{t}<b_{j_{0}},

we conclude that
X
is a weak determinantal surface of type
(\hat{a},\hat{b})
.

Finally, we must verify that
m_{it}=0
whenever
a_{i}=d
. If not, there exists
F_{i}
of degree
d
such that
m_{it}\neq 0
, and hence

F_{i}=\sum_{j\neq i}m_{jt}^{\prime}F_{j}+m_{it}^{-1}F.

Thus,
\{F_{1},\ldots,F_{i-1},F,F_{i+1},\ldots,F_{t}\}
would be a minimal generating set of the ideal of
C
, contradicting the hypothesis.

∎

Case 0:

C

Case 1:

C

F
is a minimal generator of the ideal of

.

By the proof of Proposition
2.6
, there exists a matrix
m=(m_{ij})
of size
t\times t
such that
\det(m)=F
and
\deg(m_{ij})=b_{j}-a_{i}
for all

a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{t},b_{1},\ldots,b_{t}

(\hat{a},\hat{b})

(\hat{a}_{j},\hat{b}_{j})

a_{i}=a_{ji}+k

for all

C
is not a complete intersection and

is not a minimal generator of the ideal of
C
.

Let
F_{1},\ldots,F_{t}
be a minimal set of generators of the ideal of
C
. By the proof of Proposition
2.6
, there exists a matrix
S
such that expanding
\det(S)
with respect to the last column yields
\det(S)=\pm F
, where
\deg(m_{ij})=b_{j}-a_{i}
, with
a_{1},\ldots,a_{t}
and

\hat{b}=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{j_{0}-1},b_{t},b_{j_{0}+1},\ldots,b_{t-1})\quad\text{if }b_{j_{0}-1}\leq b_{t}<b_{j_{0}+1},

such that
(\hat{a},\hat{b})

(\hat{a}_{j},\hat{b}_{j})

and

Remark 2.8
.

(1)

In item (2), case (ii), if
k=d-b_{j}
for some
j
, then
F
ceases to be a minimal generator of the ideal of
C
.

(2)

While the definition of admissible pairs in
[
6
]
yields only finitely many admissible pairs, in our setting there are infinitely many weak admissible pairs.

To conclude this section, we associate a matrix to each admissible pair. For this purpose, we define the following function:

\delta:\mathds{Z}\times\mathds{Z}\longrightarrow\mathds{N},\qquad(a,b)\longmapsto\begin{cases}b-a,&\text{if }b-a>0,\\
0,&\text{if }b-a\leq 0.\end{cases}

Definition 2.9
.

Let
(\hat{a},\hat{b})
be a weak admissible pair. We define the degree matrix associated to
(\hat{a},\hat{b})
by

M_{(\hat{a},\hat{b})}:=\begin{pmatrix}\delta(a_{1},b_{1})&\delta(a_{1},b_{2})&\cdots&\delta(a_{1},b_{t})\\
\delta(a_{2},b_{1})&\delta(a_{2},b_{2})&\cdots&\delta(a_{2},b_{t})\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\delta(a_{t},b_{1})&\delta(a_{t},b_{2})&\cdots&\delta(a_{t},b_{t})\end{pmatrix}.

Note that if two admissible pairs are equivalent, then their degree matrices coincide. On the other hand, given an admissible pair
(\hat{a},\hat{b})
, there may exist another admissible pair
(\hat{a}^{\prime},\hat{b}^{\prime})
such that

M_{(\hat{a}^{\prime},\hat{b}^{\prime})}=M_{(\hat{a},\hat{b})}^{a},

where
M^{a}
denotes the transpose of a matrix with respect to its antidiagonal. In this case, we say that
(\hat{a},\hat{b})
and
(\hat{a}^{\prime},\hat{b}^{\prime})
are
dual
.

Although there are infinitely many weak admissible pairs, most of them define the same collection of weak determinantal surfaces. In order to identify all such pairs, we need the following result.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
M_{(\hat{a},\hat{b})}=(m_{ij})
be the degree matrix of an admissible pair
(\hat{a},\hat{b})
of degree
d
and length
t
. If
m_{ij}>0
, then
m_{ji}<d
.

Proof.

Suppose that
0<m_{ij}=\delta(a_{i},b_{j})=b_{j}-a_{i}
and that
m_{ji}\geq d
. Then
m_{ji}=b_{i}-a_{j}=d+n
for some
n\in\mathds{N}
. On the other hand,

d=\sum_{k=1}^{t}(b_{k}-a_{k})=\sum_{k\neq i,j}(b_{k}-a_{k})+b_{j}-a_{i}+d+n.

Since
b_{k}-a_{k}>0
for all
k
, this implies
b_{j}-a_{i}<0
, a contradiction. Therefore,
m_{ji}<d
.

∎

Definition 2.11
.

Two admissible pairs
(\hat{a},\hat{b})
and
(\hat{a}^{\prime},\hat{b}^{\prime})
are said to be of the same kind if they have the same degree and length, and if their degree matrices
M_{(\hat{a},\hat{b})}=(m_{ij})
and
M_{(\hat{a}^{\prime},\hat{b}^{\prime})}=(m_{ij}^{\prime})
satisfy
m_{ij}=m_{ij}^{\prime}
for all entries with
0<m_{ij}<d
.

Remark 2.12
.

(1)

If
X
is a weak determinantal surface of type
(\hat{a},\hat{b})
, then
X
is also weak determinantal of type
(\hat{a},\hat{b})^{a}
, where
(\hat{a},\hat{b})^{a}
is dual to
(\hat{a},\hat{b})
.

(2)

Let
(\hat{a},\hat{b})
and
(\hat{a}^{\prime},\hat{b}^{\prime})
be weak admissible pairs of the same kind. With the notation above, observe that if
m_{ij}=0
, then
i>j
. Since the pairs are the same kind, we have either
m_{ij}^{\prime}=0
or
m_{ji}^{\prime}=0
, but as
i>j
, it follows that
m_{ij}^{\prime}=0
. Hence,
m_{ij}=0
if and only if
m_{ij}^{\prime}=0
. This implies that the kind of a weak admissible pair determines the positions of the zeros in the degree matrix. Consequently, there are only finitely many kinds. Moreover, if two weak admissible pairs are the same kind, then a surface
X
is weak determinantal of one type if and only if it is weak determinantal of the other.

(3)

One way to determine all admissible pairs associated to a given surface
X
is to consider curves obtained as complete intersections of
X
with surfaces of low degree, and then link these curves by complete intersections of
X
with other surfaces containing them. Computing the resolution of the linked curve yields an admissible pair (this step follows from the fact that we know the minimal resolution of a complete intersection and
[
7
]
*III Prop.1.4); taking its dual and all admissible pairs with degree matrix of the same type, and repeating this process finitely many times, produces all admissible pairs defining
X
as a weak determinantal surface.

Question 2.13
.

From all above, a natural question is the following: Given a degree
d
surface in
\mathds{P}^{3}
, how can one determine that the set of all weak admissible pairs for X has been fully characterized?

3.
The known cases

In this section, we analyze the cases of surfaces of degrees
2
and
3
. These cases have already been completely understood when the surface is smooth. For the degree
2
case, we refer to
[
4
]
*III, Ex. 5.6. For the degree
3
case, we refer to the work of Masayuki Watanabe in
[
10
]
*Prop. 4.3.

3.1.
Degree two

The possible weak admissible pairs are
((1,1),(2,2))
and
((1,1+n),(2,2+n))
for
n\in\mathds{N}^{*}
. It is well known that all smooth quadric surfaces are determinantal, which means that all smooth quadrics are of type
((1,1),(2,2))
. On the other hand, if a quadric surface is of type
((1,1+n),(2,2+n))
for some
n\in\mathds{N}^{*}
, then its degree matrix is of the form

M_{((1,1+n),(2,2+n))}=\begin{pmatrix}1&n+1\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}.

This implies that a weak determinantal surface of this type is reducible and, in particular, singular. Therefore, a smooth quadric surface cannot be of type
((1,1+n),(2,2+n))
.

Corollary 3.1
.

Let
Q
be a smooth quadric surface and let
C\subseteq Q
be a curve. Then
C
is an ACM curve if and only if one of the following conditions holds:

(1)

C
is a complete intersection, or

(2)

C
has the following minimal free resolution:

0\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathds{P}^{3}}(-(2+k))^{2}\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathds{P}^{3}}(-(1+k))^{2}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathds{P}^{3}}(-2)\to\mathscr{I}_{C}\to 0

for
k\geq 1
.

In both cases, the curves are of type
(s,t)
on
Q
with
|s-t|\leq 1
(see
[
4
]
*III, Ex. 5.6).

On the other hand, observe that all weak admissible pairs of the form
((1,1+n),(2,2+n))
have the same type for all
n\geq 1
. Hence, we obtain the following result.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
Q
be a quadric surface of type
((1,2),(2,3))
and let
C\subseteq Q
be a curve. Then
C
is an ACM curve if and only if one of the following conditions holds:

(1)

C
is a complete intersection, or

(2)

C
has the following minimal free resolution:

0\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathds{P}^{3}}(-(1+k))\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathds{P}^{3}}(-(n+1+k))\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathds{P}^{3}}(-(2+k))\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathds{P}^{3}}(-(2+k+n))\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathds{P}^{3}}(-2)\to\mathscr{I}_{C}\to 0

for
n,k\geq 1
.

3.2.
Degree three

In this case, the classification of all weak admissible pairs is more involved. We therefore organize the admissible pairs according to their type.

0\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathds{P}^{3}}(-(k+2))^{4}\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathds{P}^{3}}(-(k+1))^{4}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathds{P}^{3}}(-4)\to\mathscr{I}_{D}\to 0.

Using formulas (

Since we will deal with cases where

4k-2=D\cdot H=4a+6b,\qquad 4k^{2}-4k-4=D^{2}=4a^{2}+12ab+4b^{2},

which implies either

or

We now verify that the curves in both classes are ACM. Suppose

and since

.

Finally, we verify that there are no plane curves other than hyperplane sections. Let

be a plane curve in

, and its genus is

if and only if

and

.

For items (iii) and (iv), we consider the remaining cases of (ii) and those arising from item (2)(iii) of Theorem

:

Case 1:

Item (ii) with

Case 2:

Case 3:

Case 4:

By Theorem

For the last item, as in the previous propositions, complete intersections of surfaces of degrees

∎

5.
A generalization

In this section, we present a generalization of the previous results to higher dimension.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
(\hat{a},\hat{b})
be an admissible pair of degree
d
and length
t
. A hypersurface

X=\{F=0\}\subset\mathds{P}^{n}

is called a weak determinantal hypersurface of type
(\hat{a},\hat{b})
if

F=\det(S)

for some matrix
S=(m_{ij})
, where
m_{ij}
is a homogeneous polynomial of degree
b_{j}-a_{i}
. In the case
a_{i}\geq b_{j}
, we assume that
m_{ij}=0
.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
X=V(F)\subseteq\mathds{P}^{n}
be a hypersurface of degree
d
. If there exists an ACM closed subvariety

Y\subseteq X

of codimension one in
X
that is not a complete intersection, then
X
is a weak determinantal hypersurface of type
(\hat{a},\hat{b})
for some admissible pair
(\hat{a},\hat{b})
of degree
d
and length
t
.

Proof.

Since there is a generalization of the Hilbert–Burch Theorem for subvarieties of codimension
2
in
\mathds{P}^{n}
(see
[
5
]
*Prop. 8.7), the proof is identical to the surface case in
\mathds{P}^{3}
.

∎

As before, we obtain a classification theorem for ACM closed subvarieties of codimension one on a hypersurface of degree
d
in
\mathds{P}^{n}
. The proof follows exactly the same arguments as in the surface case.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
X=V(F)
be a hypersurface of degree
d
in
\mathds{P}^{n}
, and let
Y\subseteq X
be a closed subvariety of codimension one in
X
. Then
Y
is ACM if and only if one of the following cases occurs:

(1)

The hypersurface
X
is not weak determinantal. In this case,
Y
is a complete intersection.

(2)

The hypersurface
X
is weak determinantal of type
\{(\hat{a}_{i},\hat{b}_{i})\}_{i\in I}
, and only of these types. Then
Y
is one of the following:

(i)

Y
is a complete intersection.

(ii)

If
Y
is not a complete intersection and
F
is a minimal generator of the ideal
\mathscr{I}_{Y}
, then
Y
has minimal free resolution

0\to\bigoplus_{j=1}^{t}\mathcal{O}_{\mathds{P}^{n}}(-(b_{j}+k))\to\bigoplus_{i=1}^{t}\mathcal{O}_{\mathds{P}^{n}}(-(a_{i}+k))\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathds{P}^{n}}(-d)\to\mathscr{I}_{Y}\to 0,

with
\hat{a}_{j}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{t})
and
\hat{b}_{j}=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{t})
for some
j\in I
and some
k\in\mathds{Z}
.

(iii)

If
Y
is not a complete intersection and
F
is not a minimal generator of the ideal
\mathscr{I}_{Y}
, then
Y
has minimal free resolution

0\to\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq j_{0}\end{subarray}}^{t}\mathcal{O}_{\mathds{P}^{n}}(-d+b_{j_{0}}-b_{i})\to\bigoplus_{i=1}^{t}\mathcal{O}_{\mathds{P}^{n}}(-d+b_{j_{0}}-a_{i})\to\mathscr{I}_{Y}\to 0,

with

\hat{a}_{j}=(a_{1}+k,\ldots,a_{t}+k),\quad\hat{b}_{j}=(b_{1}+k,\ldots,b_{j_{0}-1}+k,d,b_{j_{0}+1}+k,\ldots,b_{t}+k),

for some
j\in I
,
j_{0}\in\{1,\ldots,t\}
, and some
k\in\mathds{Z}
.

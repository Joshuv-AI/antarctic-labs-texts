A Common Fixed Point Theorem For Weakly Compatible Mappings Satisfying Property (E. A) Thanks: Mathematics Subject Classifications: 47H10, 54H25.
G. V. R. Babu, G. N. Alemayehu

Abstract
The aim of this paper is to prove the existence of common fixed points for a pair of weakly compatible selfmaps satisfying weakly contractive condition and property (E. A). In this context, first we modify Beg and Abbas theorem ( [ 3 ] , Theorem 2.5). Our results improve the corresponding results of Beg and Abbas [ 3 ] .

Thoughout this paper,
(X,d)

DEFINITION 1.1. The pair
(f,T)
is said to

(i) be

6

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}d(fTx_{n},Tfx_{n})=0
, whenever
\{x_{n}\}
is a sequence
in

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}fx_{n}=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}Tx_{n}=t,
for some

in
X
.

(ii) be

noncompatible
if there is at least one sequence
\{x_{n}\}

in
X
such that
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}fx_{n}\\

~~~~~=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}Tx_{n}=t,
for some
t
in
X

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}d(fTx_{n},Tfx_{n})
is either non-zero or

(iii)
satisfy property
(E. A) (Aamri and El
Moutawakil
[

]
) if there exists a sequence
\{x_{n}\}
in

X
such that
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}fx_{n}=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}Tx_{n}=t,
for some
t
in
X
.

(iv) be
weakly compatible
(Jungck

[
7
]
) if
Tfx=fTx
whenever

fx=Tx,~x\in X
.

REMARK 1.2. Every pair of noncompatible selfmaps of a metric space

(X,d)
satisfies property (E. A), but its converse need not be
true as shown by the following example.

EXAMPLE 1.3. Let
X=[0,1)
with the usual metric. We define mappings

\begin{array}[]{lr}f(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}~~\frac{2}{3}&\text{if}~0\leq x<\frac{2}{3}\\

\frac{4}{3}-x&\text{if}~\frac{2}{3}\leq x<1.\end{array}\right.\end{array}

Then the pair
(f,T)
is compatible on

, for if

is a

THEOREM 1.5. (Beg and Abbas
[
3
]
, Theorem 2.5). Let
(X,d)

be a metric space and let
T,~f:X\rightarrow X
be weakly compatible
selfmaps. Assume that there exists a monotone increasing
\varphi\in\Phi
with
\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}\varphi(t)=\infty
such that for all

,

d(Tx,Ty)\leq d(fx,fy)-\varphi(d(fx,fy)).

If
T(X)\subseteq f(X)

and
f(X)
is a complete subspace of
X
, then
f
and
T
have a
unique fixed point in
X
.

DEFINITION 1.6. A selfmap
T:X\rightarrow X
is said to be

weakly contractive with respect to a selfmap
f:X\rightarrow X
if there exists a
\varphi\in\Phi
such that for all
x,y\in X
,

d(Tx,Ty)\leq d(fx,fy)-\varphi(d(fx,fy)).

(1)

The aim of this paper is to give a modified version of Theorem 1.5
by relaxing the conditions ‘
\varphi
is monotonically increasing
and
\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}\varphi(t)=\infty
’.

Further we prove the existence of common fixed points for a pair of
weakly compatible selfmaps satisfying weakly contractive condition
and property (E. A).

2
A Modified Version Of Beg and Abbas Theorem

The following theorem suggests that the conditions ‘
\varphi
is
monotone increasing and
\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}\varphi(t)=\infty
’ of Theorem 1.5 are redundant.

THEOREM 2.1. Let
T,~f:X\rightarrow X
be weakly compatible
selfmaps. If
T
is weakly contractive with respect to
f
such that

T(X)\subseteq f(X)
and
f(X)
is a complete subspace of
X
, then

f
and
T
have a unique common fixed point in
X
.

PROOF. Let
x_{0}\in X.
Since
T(X)\subseteq f(X)
,
there exists
x_{1}\in X
such that
Tx_{0}=fx_{1}.

On continuing this process, inductively we get a sequence

\{x_{n}\}
in
X
such that
y_{n}=T(x_{n})=f(x_{n+1})
.

We now
show that the sequence
\{d(fx_{n},fx_{n+1})\}
is a decreasing
sequence.

Now consider,

d(fx_{n+1},fx_{n+2})=d(T(x_{n}),T(x_{n+1}))\leq d(f(x_{n}),f(x_{n+1}))-\varphi(d(f(x_{n}),f(x_{n+1})).

(2)

Hence,

d(fx_{n+1},fx_{n+2})\leq d(f(x_{n}),f(x_{n+1}))~\text{for all}~n=0,1,2,\cdots.

(3)

Hence the sequence
\{d(f(x_{n}),f(x_{n+1}))\}
is a decreasing
sequence of non-negative reals and converges to a limit
l
(say)
and
l\geq 0
.

We claim that
l=0
. Suppose
l>0
.

Letting
n\rightarrow\infty
in (
2
), by the continuity of

\varphi
, we get
l\leq l-\varphi(l)
, a contradiction. Hence,

l=0
. i.e.,

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}d(f(x_{n}),f(x_{n+1}))=0.

(4)

We now claim that
\{y_{n}\}
is Cauchy. By (
3
) and (
4
),
it is sufficient to show that
\{y_{2n}\}
is Cauchy. Otherwise,
there exists an
\varepsilon>0
and there exist sequences

\{m_{k}\}
and
\{n_{k}\}
with
m_{k}>n_{k}>k
such that

d(y_{2m_{k}},y_{2n_{k}})\geq\varepsilon~~~~\text{and}~~~~~d(y_{2m_{k}-2},y_{2n_{k}})<\varepsilon.

(5)

Hence,

\varepsilon\leq\liminf\limits_{k\rightarrow\infty}d(y_{2m_{k}},y_{2n_{k}}).

(6)

For each positive integer
k
, by triangle inequality we have,

d(y_{2m_{k}},y_{2n_{k}})\leq d(y_{2m_{k}},y_{2m_{k}-1})+d(y_{2m_{k}-1},y_{2m_{k}-2})+d(y_{2m_{k}-2},y_{2n_{k}})

On
taking limit supremum of both sides, as
k\rightarrow\infty
, we
get

\limsup\limits_{k\rightarrow\infty}d(y_{2m_{k}},y_{2n_{k}})\leq\varepsilon.

(7)

Hence, from (
6
) and (
7
), we have

\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}{d(y_{2m_{k}},y_{2n_{k}})}=\varepsilon.

(8)

Now

d(y_{2m_{k}},y_{2n_{k}+1})\leq d(y_{2m_{k}},y_{2n_{k}})+d(y_{2n_{k}},y_{2n_{k}+1})

On taking
limit supremum, as
k\rightarrow\infty
, we get

\limsup\limits_{k\rightarrow\infty}d(y_{2m_{k}},y_{2n_{k}+1})\leq\varepsilon.

(9)

Again we have

d(y_{2m_{k}},y_{2n_{k}})\leq d(y_{2m_{k}},y_{2n_{k}+1})+d(y_{2n_{k}+1},y_{2n_{k}}).

On taking
limit infimum, as
k\rightarrow\infty
, we get

\varepsilon\leq\liminf\limits_{k\rightarrow\infty}d(y_{2m_{k}},y_{2n_{k}+1}).

(10)

From (
9
) and (
10
), we have

\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}{d(y_{2m_{k}},y_{2n_{k}+1})}=\varepsilon.

(11)

Similarly, we can show that

\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}{d(y_{2m_{k}-1},y_{2n_{k}})}=\varepsilon.

(12)

Now consider,

{d(y_{2m_{k}},y_{2n_{k}+1})}={d(Tx_{2m_{k}},Tx_{2n_{k}+1})}~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\leq{d(fx_{2m_{k}},fx_{2n_{k}+1})}-\varphi({d(fx_{2m_{k}},fx_{2n_{k}+1})})

~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\leq{d(Tx_{2m_{k}-1},Tx_{2n_{k}})}-\varphi({d(Tx_{2m_{k}-1},Tx_{2n_{k}})}).

~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\leq{d(y_{2m_{k}-1},y_{2n_{k}})}-\varphi({d(y_{2m_{k}-1},y_{2n_{k}})}).

(13)

Letting
k\rightarrow\infty
in (
13
), using (
11
),
(
12
) and the continuity of
\varphi
, we get
\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon-\varphi(\varepsilon)
, a contradiction. Hence,

\{y_{2n}\}
is Cauchy so that
\{y_{n}\}
is a Cauchy sequence in

X
. Thus
\{fx_{n+1}\}
is a Cauchy sequence in
X
. Since
f(X)

is complete and
\{fx_{n+1}\}\subset f(X)
, we have

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}fx_{n+1}=fu,~\text{for some }u\in X.

(14)

Next we claim that
T(u)=f(u)
.
Now consider,

d(fx_{n+1},Tu)=d(Tx_{n},Tu)\leq d(fx_{n},fu)-\varphi(d(fx_{n},fu)).

(15)

Letting
n\rightarrow\infty
, from (
14
) using (
15
) and
the continuity of
\varphi
, we get

d(fu,Tu)\leq d(fu,fu)-\varphi(d(fu,fu))=0.

Hence,
Tu=fu=z
(say). Since the pair of maps
(f,T)
is weakly
compatible, we have
Tfu=fTu
and hence
Tz=fz
.

We now claim that
Tz=z
. Suppose
Tz\neq z
. Consider

d(Tz,z)=d(Tz,Tu)\leq d(fz,fu)-\varphi(d(fz,fu))

~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~=d(Tz,z)-\varphi(d(Tz,z)),

a contradiction. Hence,
Tz=z
.

Hence,
fz=Tz=z
. The uniqueness of
z
follows from the weakly
contractive nature of
T
.
Hence, the theorem follows.

3
Main Result

THEOREM 3.1. Let
(X,d)
be a metric space and let

T,~f:X\rightarrow X
be weakly compatible selfmaps satisfying
property (E. A). Assume that
T
is weakly contractive with respect
to
f
. If
f(X)
is closed, then
f
and
T
have a unique common
fixed point in
X
.

PROOF. Since the pair
(f,T)
satisfies property (E. A), there
exists a sequence
\{x_{n}\}
in
X
such that
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}fx_{n}=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}Tx_{n}=z,
for some
z
in

X
.

Since
f(X)
is closed,
z=f(u)
for some
u\in X
.

Now replacing

u
for
x
and
x_{n}
for
y
in (
1
), we get

d(Tu,Tx_{n})\leq d(fu,fx_{n})-\varphi(d(fu,fx_{n})).

(16)

Letting
n\rightarrow\infty
in (
16
) , by the continuity of

\varphi
, we get

d(Tu,z)\leq d(fu,z)-\varphi(d(fu,z))=0.

Hence,

Tu=z
.

Hence,

fu=Tu=z.

(17)

Since
f
and
T
are weakly compatible, from (
17
), we have

fz=Tz
.

If
z\neq Tz
, then from the inequality (
1
), we
have

d(Tz,z)=d(Tz,Tu)\leq d(fz,fu)-\varphi(d(fz,fu))

~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~=d(Tz,Tu)-\varphi(d(Tz,Tu)),

a contradiction. Hence,
Tz=z
.

Hence,
fz=Tz=z
.

The uniqueness of
z
follows from the inequality (
1
).

This complete the proof the Theorem.

In Theorem 3.1, if we choose

COROLLARY 3.3. Let
(X,d)
be a metric space and let

be weakly compatible selfmaps satisfying

\psi\in\Phi
with

\psi(t)<t
for

(18)

If

is closed, then
f
and
T
have
a unique common fixed point in
X
.

In this case, when
T
and
f
satisfy the inequality (

),
we say that

is a Boyd-Wong type contraction with respect
to
f

[
4
]
.

f
and

Since the sequence

\varphi\in\Phi
.
With this
\varphi
,
T
is weakly contractive with respect to
f
.

But
f(X)=(\frac{2}{3},1]
is not closed. We observe that
f
and

T
have no common fixed points.

REMARK 3.10. In Theorem 3.1, if we relax the condition ‘
f
and
T

satisfy property (E. A)’ then they may have no common fixed points.

EXAMPLE 3.11. Let
X=[\frac{1}{2},1]
with the usual metric. We
define mappings
f
and
T
on
X
by

\begin{array}[]{lr}f(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\text{if}~\frac{1}{2}\leq x\leq\frac{2}{3}\\
\\
\frac{2}{3}&\text{if}~\frac{2}{3}<x\leq 1\end{array}\right.&\mbox{}\text{and}~~~~T(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{2}{3}&\text{if}~\frac{1}{2}\leq x\leq\frac{2}{3}\\
\\
\frac{1}{2}&\text{if}~\frac{2}{3}<x\leq 1.\end{array}\right.\end{array}

Here
f
and
T
are trivially weakly compatible on
X
and

f(X)=\{\frac{2}{3},1\}
is closed. Further,
T
is weakly
contractive with respect to
f
with
\varphi(t)=\frac{1}{2}t,~t\geq 0
.

But
f
and
T
do not satisfy property (E. A), since for any
sequence
\{x_{n}\}
in
X
we have
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}fx_{n}\neq\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}Tx_{n}
in
X
. We observe that
f
and
T
have no common fixed points.

Advancing the Evidence Base for Tricuspid TEER
Authors: Tsz-Ho Chan, Kent Chak-yu So
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Corresponding AuthorKey words: DragonFly-T, randomized controlled trial, tricuspid regurgitation, transcather edge-to-edge repair

The treatment landscape for severe tricuspid regurgitation (TR) has evolved dramatically. Once regarded as the “forgotten valve,” TR is now recognized as an independent contributor to heart failure symptoms, impaired quality of life, recurrent hospitalizations, and mortality.1 Transcatheter tricuspid valve interventions (TTVIs) have therefore generated strong interest, particularly for elderly, frail patients who are poor surgical candidates.

In this issue of JACC: Asia, Ren et al2 present the design and rationale of the TEER-CHINA trial—a prospective, multicenter, randomized study evaluating the DragonFly-T transcatheter edge-to-edge repair (TEER) system plus optimal medical therapy (OMT) versus OMT alone in patients with symptomatic severe TR. As the first randomized evaluation of a China-developed dedicated tricuspid TEER platform, the trial seeks to establish the safety and efficacy of DragonFly-T while informing future treatment strategies in an era of rapid advances in heart failure management and transcatheter therapies (Figure 1).

Current Evidence and Remaining Knowledge Gaps in Transcatheter Treatment for Tricuspid Regurgitation

Severe tricuspid regurgitation (TR) is increasingly recognized as a symptomatic disease associated with impaired survival. Randomized trials have established transcatheter tricuspid edge-to-edge repair (T-TEER) as a safe and effective treatment for appropriately selected patients. However, several important questions remain, including the role of contemporary guideline-directed medical therapy, optimal timing of intervention and identification of futility, whether TR reduction translates into improved survival, and whether long-term outcomes are primarily determined by device technology or by the magnitude of residual TR reduction. Building on TRILUMINATE and TRISCEND II

TEER-CHINA enters a field transformed by recent pivotal trials. The TRILUMINATE Pivotal trial demonstrated that TEER plus medical therapy improves health status and quality of life and achieves durable TR reduction compared with medical therapy alone.3, 4, 5 Longer-term follow-up confirmed sustained symptomatic benefits and reduced recurrent heart failure hospitalizations.5 More recently, the TRISCEND II trial showed that transcatheter tricuspid valve replacement (TTVR) can achieve near-complete TR elimination, with associated improvements in symptoms and quality of life.6

Beyond their shared randomized design, TEER-CHINA incorporates several features that distinguish it from earlier pivotal studies. Mandatory pre-enrollment right-heart catheterization will provide detailed hemodynamic phenotyping and may help clarify which TR phenotypes derive the greatest benefit from intervention. In addition, optimization of contemporary heart failure therapy, including consideration of sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 (SGLT2) inhibitors, reflects current standards of care that were not routinely incorporated in earlier trials.

Importantly, TEER-CHINA, like TRILUMINATE Pivotal and TRISCEND II, permits crossover from the control arm after approximately 1 year. While ethically appropriate, crossover represents a major limitation of contemporary TR intervention trials because it progressively diminishes separation between randomized groups and complicates interpretation of long-term mortality and heart failure hospitalization outcomes. As signals of benefit on hard clinical endpoints continue to emerge, future studies may require innovative trial designs or advanced statistical approaches to better define the long-term impact of earlier transcatheter intervention.

Evaluating the DragonFly-T System

The primary objective is to evaluate the safety and efficacy of the DragonFly-T system. The tricuspid valve's complex anatomy (large annulus, variable leaflet morphology, coaptation gaps, and right ventricular remodeling) has spurred development of dedicated devices. DragonFly-T incorporates independent leaflet grasping, multiple clip configurations, and potentially different clipping force and annular reduction effects compared with currently approved TEER devices. While early experience is promising, randomized evidence is essential. The trial also advances the field toward greater device diversity, enabling better anatomical matching, similar to the evolution in transcatheter mitral interventions.

Contemporary Heart Failure Management and Hemodynamic Phenotyping

A notable strength is TEER-CHINA's integration of contemporary heart failure care. The protocol recommends SGLT2 inhibitors when clinically appropriate and requires OMT optimization before enrollment—differing from many earlier trials initiated before widespread use of these therapies.7

The trial also mandates pre-enrollment right-heart catheterization for detailed hemodynamic assessment (pulmonary artery pressure, pulmonary vascular resistance, and pulmonary capillary wedge pressure). This phenotyping should clarify how baseline hemodynamic influence outcomes, particularly given the bidirectional relationship between TR and heart failure, including in heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF).1,8

Informing Future Treatment Strategies

TEER-CHINA has strong potential to refine patient selection and intervention timing. While TRILUMINATE and TRISCEND II established the value of transcatheter therapies, key questions remain regarding optimal candidates amid heterogeneous factors such as right ventricular dysfunction, pulmonary hypertension, and comorbidities. Emerging registry data suggest that earlier intervention, before advanced right ventricular dysfunction or irreversible end-organ damage, may yield better outcomes.9,10 The trial's rigorous design and hemodynamic data could help identify responders and shape future treatment algorithms.

Why an Asian Trial Matters

Exclusive enrollment of Chinese patients addresses an important gap. Most randomized TTVI evidence derives from North American and European populations. Asian patients may differ in body size, atrial fibrillation prevalence, heart failure phenotypes, and atrial functional TR patterns.11, 12, 13 With HFpEF rising rapidly across Asia, the findings from TEER-CHINA will help assess generalizability of prior trials and support regional TTVI adoption. Recent Asian multicenter experience has already shown favourable procedural success and early outcomes.11

Looking Ahead

TEER-CHINA arrives at a pivotal time. Primarily evaluating DragonFly-T safety and efficacy, its incorporation of contemporary heart failure therapy, invasive hemodynamics, and a large Asian cohort will provide insights extending beyond device performance. Building on TRILUMINATE and TRISCEND II, it advances our understanding of how best to integrate transcatheter TR intervention into evolving care.

As the field matures, progress will depend on device innovation, improved patient selection, optimized timing, and integration with modern heart failure management. TEER-CHINA represents a meaningful step forward.

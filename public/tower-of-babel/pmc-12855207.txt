Enabling continuous 24/7 vertical mobility in High-Rise buildings by integrating critical activity scheduling and night shift planning
Authors: Mahdi Ahmadnia, Mojtaba Maghrebi, Fatemeh Khodatars
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn high-rise construction projects, inefficient delivery of materials, equipment, and labor can impact the completion time of the project. So, improvement in vertical transportation could save time, facilitate the supply process and reduce costs. The methods introduced for efficiently planning lifts in the construction industry were focused on material transportation and the time of lift activity, which primarily used heuristics and simulations. One of the main challenges that has not been fully addressed in the literature is automatically prioritizing critical activities in the supply process. Other issues include taking into account the night shift and considering the deadline of critical activities. Mixed-Integer Linear Programming (MILP) formulation that integrates both integer and continuous variables can provide an opportunity to obtain an optimal solution. Accordingly, this paper introduces an MILP model that seeks the best allocation of materials and equipment to a given number of lift travel rounds. Also, when the demand exceeds lift capacity, the proposed method allows for efficient prioritization of activities and supplies maximum ordered material with the fewest possible delays. The proposed model, coupled with a novel iterative search algorithm, can achieve the minimum number of travel rounds and the optimum lift planning. Field data from a 22-story construction project is used to test the suggested method. The results demonstrate that the method could effectively minimize delivery times and detect infeasible and undersupplied demands.Keywords: Lift scheduling, Vertical transportation, Integer programing, Lift activities optimization, Critical activities, Critical timeSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Land scarcity and high land prices in business district areas as well as growing demand for commercial and residential spaces in metropolitan areas have forced business owners to consider high-rise and multi-story buildings as a viable solution1. An efficient high-rise construction project timing necessitates the simultaneous execution of several construction activities, which requires vertical transportation using tower cranes and lifts to handle materials and workforce demand2,3. Typically, tower cranes are used for handling heavy materials (like beams) and lifts are used for smaller ones and labor4,5. When tower crane maneuvering is restricted at the project site, lifts may be a reliable and economical alternative solution6,7. This paper aims to develop an optimized scheduling model for activities assigned to construction lifts to enhance vertical transportation efficiency in high-rise building projects.

To implement the project schedule, required resources (such as materials, equipment, and labor) must be provided prior to the activity’s start time. The failure to supply each resource on time, results in operations being delayed and subsequently increasing project costs8–10. Due to the limited capacity of lift or tower crane, vertical transportation is the main bottleneck in the supply process. An effective lift schedule can reduce delivery delays and lift operating hours, while an inefficient one may pose a 20–30% delay11.

As shown in Fig. 1, the priority of activities may not be the same. Generally, there are some critical operations whose finish time affects the start time of the others. Therefore, the lift transportation schedule is influenced by the latest possible starting time of critical activities. Furthermore, the demand for the material fluctuates during the day. This demand usually peaks at specific periods of the day12 (for example, at the beginning of the working day when human resources are called), which leads to a complicated situation in large-scale scenarios. To overcome this complexity in practice, a lifting schedule that accounts for material delivery priorities in order to minimize delays is imperative13.

In actual cases, material demand may be so high or dense in peak hours that it is not possible to be delivered timely manner during the day shift. A viable solution in these scenarios is to start the lifting timetable from the night before construction activities begin9. The amount of each material transmitted in each round is also part of a practical delivery strategy. Determining the optimal combination of materials in each round of lift activity is a computational challenge due to the distinction between countable and uncountable materials. Countable materials are those transported in integer units, such as tiles, and uncountable materials are those that can be transported in any quantity; for example, a cable with any desired length. Addressing the mentioned challenges, as well as minimizing the opening hours of the lift by efficiently path-planning the roundtrips while ensuring the demand for construction activities is met, leads to a complicated problem in the transportation unit of high-rise projects.

This study is focused on optimizing the process of transporting materials by lift. The primary objective of this paper is to develop a scheduler for a lift that meets the demands of construction activities on time as much as possible. Assuming that the project has determined the latest acceptable delivery time for each activity, we introduce a framework to find the best timing for the lift and the most appropriate material upload and unload strategies. The presented method is able to exploit the night shift if the latest possible delivery times cannot be met in the day shift. The framework proposed in this paper consists of a Mixed-Integer linear programming (MILP) coupled with an iterative algorithm. The iterative algorithm is in charge of updating the number of roundtrips and the MILP finds the start time, the path, and the unloading points. Moreover, the MILP determines the amount of material transmitted in each round based on the countable or uncountable units. To specify the optimum number of rounds the lift should service, the optimization formulation has to be solved iteratively by updating the number of roundtrips. Two greedy algorithms based on lift capacity are presented in this paper to obtain the boundaries of the search space for the iterative algorithm.

To highlight the novelty of this paper, it is important to note the limitations of previous works. Traditional heuristic algorithms, while computationally efficient, often fail to guarantee optimality and may not adequately handle complex scenarios involving critical activity priorities, fluctuating material demand, or night-shift operations. Exact optimization methods, on the other hand, are rarely extended to incorporate such practical constraints and therefore lack applicability in real-world construction projects. In contrast, the proposed framework introduces a MILP model that explicitly integrates activity priorities, material type distinctions (countable vs. uncountable), and shift-based scheduling. The main innovation of this paper, compared to similar works, is to consider the critical time in material delivery and also to use night shifts for better demand management. These contributions position the proposed approach as a more robust and practically viable alternative compared to existing methods.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows: related works are discussed in Sect. 3, in Sect. 4, the problem assumptions and constraints are stated. Then in Sect. 5, the proposed model is introduced. In Sect. 6, the proposed model is tested for a real-world case and the results are reviewed. Finally, Sect. 7 discusses the achievements and suggests future work.

Literature review

Numerous studies have been conducted to improve the transportation of construction materials using lifts and tower cranes. Some of these works (e.g6,8,14–17. have focused on the layout and placement of tower cranes and construction lifts within construction sites. Others (e.g9,18–21. have examined the daily operations of these transportation systems. Since this paper focuses on optimizing the daily operations of construction lifts, the following review is limited to studies that address this specific topic. These studies can be broadly categorized into three main groups: (1) lift zoning strategies, (2) lift performance control, and (3) lift operation scheduling.

Lift zoning strategies

This category of studies addresses daily demands by dividing the building floors into different zones and assigning available lifts to each zone. Based on this concept, So and Yu22 proposed a dynamic zoning strategy for lift passengers according to varying traffic patterns. Their results indicate that zoning can significantly reduce both travel time and passenger waiting time. A dynamic zoning approach for construction workers was also implemented using a genetic algorithm by Park et al.4. Their findings demonstrate that demand-based zoning can reduce vertical transportation time for workers by up to 40%. Koo et al.23 developed a genetic algorithm-based zoning strategy aimed at optimizing both electricity consumption costs and worker travel time during peak demand periods. Jung et al.5 used agent-based simulation to zone the floors of a building in order to improve lift performance. Studies in this group mainly concentrate on zoning of lift operations and often overlook key scheduling details such as the order and sequence of material deliveries, critical delivery times, and the possibility of operating during night shifts. Accordingly, this paper introduces a dedicated method for lift scheduling.

Lift performance control

Another group of studies has focused on managing and controlling real-time lift demands with the aim of reducing waiting and transfer times. Cho et al.24 developed an intelligent control toolkit to automate the vertical delivery of materials and enable autonomous routing of construction lifts. They employed remote sensing and communication technologies such as Radio Frequency Identification (RFID) and Ubiquitous Sensor Networks (USN) to track material movement and manage it intelligently. Zhang and Zong25 applied robust optimization to determine the number of elevators that should be active during peak hours under uncertain traffic patterns. In addition, they addressed the decision-making process regarding which specific elevators should be selected for service. Their objective was to minimize electricity consumption costs while maintaining passenger waiting times at the lobby within an acceptable threshold.

Minimizing the waiting time of workers and materials, reducing the total cycle time, and avoiding redundant lift operations were addressed by Shin et al.26. Their algorithm determined the most suitable construction lift for each new request based on the current position, travel direction, and available capacity of the lifts. Lee and Kim27 employed deep learning techniques for the autonomous control of construction hoists under complex conditions, where waiting time and lifting time of passengers were reduced by up to 86%. Wu and Yang12 developed direction optimization algorithms aimed at reducing passenger waiting times during peak traffic periods. Their approach involved estimating the directional flow of passengers during peak hours and incorporating directional rules into metaheuristic optimization algorithms, such as Genetic Algorithm (GA), Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO), Simulated Annealing (SA), and Tabu Search (TS) to avoid local optima and enhance convergence performance. Most studies in this group focus on real-time lift control, neglecting daily scheduling that entails additional complexities such as delivery sequencing, critical deadlines, and pre-peak activity management.

Lift operation scheduling

Lift operation scheduling involves determining the loading and unloading times of materials and workers based on predefined demands. The primary objective is to ensure the timely fulfillment of requests while minimizing vertical transportation time. Cho et al.11 proposed a simulation-based approach, developed with insights from experienced engineers, to reduce the vertical transfer time of labor and materials on construction sites. Cho et al.9 in a separate study, focused on the optimal routing schedule of construction lifts by developing and enhancing a branch-and-bound algorithm.

Jalali Yazdi et al.7 employed a Mixed-Integer Programming (MIP) model to optimally schedule the operations of construction lifts. Their method determines the precise travel path of the lift cabin, the type and quantity of materials or labor to be transported, and the sequence of deliveries in each trip, all with the aim of minimizing lift operating costs. They achieved an optimality gap of 0% within approximately one hour for a 34-story building project. In a subsequent study, Jalali Yazdi et al.28 integrated the zoning and scheduling problems to simultaneously decide on the optimal assignment of a group of floors to each lift car and the scheduling of worker transportation. In a different context, Ren et al.29 proposed a two-stage heuristic optimization algorithm for the simultaneous scheduling of lifts and shuttles in manufacturing systems. Previous research disregards the role of night-shift capacity and off-peak cycles in managing peak periods. By accounting for these elements, this paper seeks to minimize material delivery delays.

As shown in Table 1, two main research gaps can be identified in the existing literature. First, in practice, certain materials must be delivered to demand points at specific times of day. Moreover, some materials serve as prerequisites for others in construction activities, making the sequence and timing of deliveries critically important. However, most existing studies have primarily focused on minimizing transportation time, often overlooking the importance of delivery sequencing and the critical timing of meeting demands. This study aims to bridge this gap by emphasizing the satisfaction of material demands before their critical deadlines, thereby establishing a stronger connection with real-world construction site constraints. Furthermore, due to high workload and congestion during peak hours, it may not always be feasible to deliver materials during daytime shifts or prior to their critical deadlines. To address this, the proposed method allows for nighttime deliveries, establishing an optimal balance between nighttime lifting operations and potential delays in meeting demands. Lastly, compared to heuristic and simulation-based approaches, integer programming techniques can yield more optimal solutions. Therefore, this study employs a mixed-integer linear programming (MILP) model to generate an optimized lift operation schedule.

Proposed method

In this section, a mixed-integer linear programming (MILP) model is proposed for optimal scheduling of lifting operations. The proposed model accounts for both the penalty associated with delays in material delivery and the possibility of performing tasks during night shifts. A key parameter in this formulation is the number of lifting cycles that can be executed. If this number is underestimated, some demands may remain unmet due to the lift’s limited capacity. Furthermore, the number of lifting cycles directly influences material delivery delays and the duration of night-shift operations. Therefore, the number of cycles must be chosen sufficiently large to ensure feasibility. However, an unnecessarily large number of cycles leads to an increase in both decision variables and constraints, thereby increasing the computational complexity of the MILP model.

To overcome this challenge, a greedy algorithm is proposed to generate an initial lifting schedule. In this algorithm, material loading in each cycle is determined solely based on delivery priority, while night-shift operations and inter-floor travel times are disregarded. The number of cycles obtained from the greedy algorithm () serves as an effective starting point for the MILP model. Accordingly, the MILP model is solved with the number of rounds obtained from the greedy algorithm (). If the solution includes unused cycle(s), it implies that the number of cycles is sufficiently large and further increases do not improve the result. In this case, the obtained solution is considered optimal. Alternatively, if all cycles are fully utilized in the solution, increasing the number of cycles may yield improved results. This iterative process continues until a solution with unused cycles emerges. The overall procedure of the proposed method is summarized in the flowchart shown in Fig. 2.

Multiplicity of activities in a given time period or activities that requires lots of resources may make this impossible. Effect of the penalty for non-delivery of materials

In practical construction operations, all required materials must be delivered to their designated demand points. In such situations, even continuous 24-hour operation of the lift may not be sufficient to complete all material deliveries. To address this issue, a non-delivery penalty term is incorporated into the objective function of the proposed model. This penalty allows the proposed method to discard the delivery of certain materials—if necessary—to ensure a feasible schedule under fully utilized day and night shift capacities. Conclusion

Using a lift is one of the most cost-efficient ways to move small and medium-sized materials in high-rise buildings. A well-planned delivery of material contributes significantly to the progress of ongoing activities. The challenge in lift planning is to determine how many rounds a lift should serve, how much and what type of material should be moved in each round, and what time it should stop at each floor. Multiple decisions and how they impact other and the progress make it difficult to find an effective delivery plan for the lift. A framework for selecting the best strategy for the delivery process was presented in this paper based on the prioritization of activities. The proposed framework includes a mathematical model for determining how to plan the lift for a given number of round trips. The proposed formulation allows the lift to operate in the night shift if it is necessary for timely delivery, but penalizes the objective function for doing so. The optimal number of rounds was found by solving the model iteratively through a binary search algorithm. Two algorithms were proposed in this paper to satisfy search boundaries. A real-world test showed that the suggested approach performed effectively in both low and high demand cases and was able to achieve the minimum number of rounds for the lift service.

Like other existing approaches, the proposed method has certain limitations that open up avenues for further research. The following directions are suggested for future studies:

Although the proposed method generally considers both the critical delivery times and the sequencing of material deliveries, it assumes that in each cycle materials are delivered strictly from lower floors to higher floors. This may lead to longer transportation times. Future research could address this limitation to further optimize transportation efficiency.

The proposed approach relies on an MILP model to obtain the optimal lift scheduling plan. In more complex scenarios, however, computational times may increase substantially. To overcome this, future work could focus on developing combinatorial optimization approaches to solve the problem more efficiently and benchmark them against the exact method presented in this paper.

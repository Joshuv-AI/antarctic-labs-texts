Effect of the situation-background-assessment-recommendation model combined with the mini-CEX assessment on the clinical communication, clinical thinking and comprehensive clinical abilities of nursing interns
Authors: Bu Xu, Xiaoli Xing, Guangyun Zhang, Wenting Zhang, Min Zhang, Yan Gan, Tao Wei, Xiaodan Xia
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveThis study aimed to explore the short-term effects of the Situation–Background–Assessment–Recommendation (SBAR) model combined with the mini-clinical evaluation exercise (mini-CEX) on the clinical communication ability, clinical thinking ability and comprehensive clinical ability of nursing interns during 3 weeks of internship.MethodsUsing a convenience sampling method, this study included 405 trainee nurses who interned at the First Affiliated Hospital of Anhui University of Science and Technology between September 2023 and October 2024. The enrolled interns were randomly divided into a control group (n = 201, traditional clinical nursing teaching) and an observation group (n = 204, SBAR model + mini-CEX (with emphasis on basic knowledge of common clinical diseases and specialised nursing operations, language communication skills and key requirements for case nursing)) according to the random number table method. The theoretical and operational assessment results of interns before and after intervention were compared.ResultsCompared with the control group, nursing interns in the observation group had a significantly increased theoretical assessment score (87.62 ± 4.45 vs. 89.51 ± 7.15), operational assessment score (91.55 ± 2.13 vs. 94.72 ± 1.94), total clinical communication ability score (73.14 ± 10.41 vs. 75.19 ± 8.05), total clinical thinking ability score (77.31 ± 6.57 vs. 84.60 ± 8.56) and mini-CEX assessment score (78.95 ± 8.99 vs. 88.37 ± 8.57) after intervention (p < 0.05). Moreover, nursing interns in the observation group had superior clinical communication ability (75.19 ± 8.05 vs. 67.96 ± 7.89), clinical thinking ability (84.60 ± 8.56 vs. 80.52 ± 8.91) and mini-CEX assessment rating (88.37 ± 8.57 vs. 86.34 ± 8.61) after intervention than those before intervention (p < 0.05). In addition, the overall teaching satisfaction of the observation group was increased compared with the control group (81.09%, p < 0.05).ConclusionThe combined SBAR model and mini-CEX assessment significantly enhanced nursing interns‘ clinical communication, clinical thinking and comprehensive practice abilities. These improvements were validated across theoretical examinations, operational assessments and clinical scenarios, demonstrating practical value for optimising clinical nursing quality. Through structured training and real-time feedback, this approach may exert long-term effects on interns‘ professional mindset cultivation.Keywords: Situation–Background–Assessment–Recommendation model, Mini-clinical evaluation exercise, Assessment, Nursing, Internship, Ability

Introduction

With the progress of society and the continuous development of medical technologies, clinical nursing has become of growing concern to patients and their families. Clinical teaching, an integral part of cultivating nursing staff and improving nursing quality, aims mainly to promote a preferable utilisation of theoretical knowledge learned by nursing interns in clinical practice, thereby improving their nursing abilities and professional skills.

Situation–Background–Assessment–Recommendation (SBAR) [1] is an information transfer model proposed by the World Health Organization based on the standardisation of medical conditions and structured communication. The SBAR model is commonly used for encouraging verbal communication in nursing and other medical disciplines, which can improve the effectiveness of multidisciplinary communication [2, 3]. Zhou [4] pointed out that SBAR can effectively enhance the communication ability of nursing students in simulation teaching and related treatment priorities, enhance communication efficiency, alleviate negative emotions of patients to cooperate with medical staff and improve nursing satisfaction.

Furthermore, the mini-clinical evaluation exercise (mini-CEX) is an assessment tool to evaluate the clinical abilities of medical students and residents, possessing both teaching and evaluation functions [5, 6]. To date, it has been widely used (with impressive training effectiveness) in the field of nursing in China for training personnel such as nursing interns, newly hired nurses and specialised nurses [7–11]. Zhang Huan et al. [12] further applied and modified the mini-CEX scale to evaluate accurately the clinical practice ability of nurses in emergency departments, which has been proven to be effective. At present, nursing interns are facing three core competency challenges: first, the fragmentation of clinical communication leads to low efficiency of information transmission and potential nursing risks; second, the traditional teaching model suppresses active thinking, and the clinical decision-making ability is weak; third, summative evaluation ignores soft skills, such as humanistic care and health education. The SBAR model, with its standardised framework, can force the communication logic to reduce information omission; mini-CEX dynamically corrects capability deviations through multi-dimensional, real-time evaluation and feedback [13].

Therefore, we hypothesise that, compared with traditional teaching methods, the SBAR model combined with mini-CEX assessment can significantly improve the following competencies of nursing interns: (1) theoretical examination and operational skill scores, (2) clinical communication ability and clinical thinking ability scores, (3) mini-CEX comprehensive clinical ability scores and (4) teaching satisfaction. This study integrates SBAR and mini-CEX for the first time, aiming to improve systematically the clinical core competence of interns through the synergistic effect of a structured communication framework and formative evaluation.

Participants and methods

Study design

This prospective randomised controlled trial (RCT) was conducted to evaluate the effects of the SBAR model combined with mini-CEX assessment on the clinical communication, clinical thinking and comprehensive abilities of nursing interns. This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and approved by the Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Anhui University of Science and Technology (approval no.: 2023-KY-HL001-001); all participants provided written informed consent. This study has been registered with the following registration number: ISRCTN14891448.

Participants

The participants of the study were 412 nursing interns from the First Affiliated Hospital of Anhui University of Science and Technology, selected through the convenience sampling method. The nursing students were randomly numbered 1–412; the odd numbers entered the observation group (n = 204), and the even numbers entered the control group (n = 201). During the study, 7 interns terminated the study, including 1 with an interrupted internship, 3 who applied for sick leave of ≥ 1 month and 3 who voluntarily withdrew midway. The researchers put the grouping results into sealed opaque envelopes and stored them in the teaching secretariat. This study was a single-blind trial for nursing students. The rotation departments of the two groups of nursing students adopted different routes. For example, the route of the observation group was A–B–C–D, whereas the route of the control group was D–C–B–A. The two groups of nursing students lived in different areas of the hospital.

The nursing intern inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) aged ≥ 18 years, (2) junior college or undergraduate degree, (3) completed basic theory and skills courses (4) internship period of ≥ 8 months, (5) provided informed consent and voluntarily participated in this study and (6) normal cognitive function. The nursing intern exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) those who repeated a course and (2) those with mental disorders or cognitive impairments. There was no statistically significant difference between groups in age, gender or education degree (p > 0.05), suggesting comparability between the two groups.

The termination criteria were as follows: (1) interns who interrupted the study period, (2) interns who applied for sick leave of ≥ 1 month and (3) interns who withdrew from the study voluntarily.

Study methods

Control group – the interns in the control group received one-on-one teaching using the traditional clinical nursing teaching programme. Interns received introductory education after entrance, familiarising themselves with the ward environment, nursing routines for common clinical diseases, responsibilities of each shift and an understanding of nursing rules and regulations. Under the guidance of the supervising teacher, interns jointly completed the overall primary nursing work (e.g. basic nursing, specialised nursing, psychological nursing, health education) and wrote nursing cases.

Observation group – the observation group adopted the teaching method of the SBAR model combined with mini-CEX assessment, with emphasis on basic knowledge of common clinical diseases and specialised nursing operations, language communication skills and key requirements for case nursing. At the end of the third week, the clinical teaching assessment was performed on the intern. The theoretical knowledge, operational skills assessment, clinical communication ability and clinical thinking ability of the two groups of nursing interns after the intervention were compared. The 3-week assessment endpoint was selected to capture the immediate effects of the structured SBAR–mini-CEX intervention cycle. This timeframe allows for two complete iterations of the SBAR training loop (Fig. 1), minimising confounding from long-term rotations. The mini-CEX evaluations during weeks 1–3 served formative feedback purposes, while the week-3 scores quantified short-term competency progression.

Teaching process of SBAR mode combined with Mini-CEX assessment

Evaluation

The teaching effectiveness of the nursing interns was evaluated on their first day and third weekend of enrolment. The evaluator was a nursing expert who was not a research team member, had > 10 years of clinical teaching experience and had no direct interaction with teachers. The evaluators did not participate in the teaching intervention and did not know the grouping information of the participants. The main outcome indicators were theoretical knowledge, operational skills assessment, nurses’ clinical communication ability and clinical thinking ability; the secondary outcome indicators were clinical comprehensive ability and teaching satisfaction.

Assessment of theoretical knowledge and operational skills

The mastery of basic theoretical knowledge and clinical skills of interns was evaluated through a standardised written assessment (maximum: 100 points) and operational assessment. Each skill was scored according to the Standard Rating Form (100 points), with higher scores indicating better teaching effectiveness.

Basic and specialised theoretical knowledge involved patients’ condition observation, nursing writing, medication knowledge, nurse–patient communication, legal regulations and nosocomial infections, with corresponding assessments adopted to evaluate the degree of mastery among nursing interns. The operational skills were assessed based on the Nursing Operation Standard for Evaluation in the hospital, including both the basic and specialised operations. Specialised operational skills mainly included aseptic techniques, intravenous infusion and case presentations. The operational skill scoring table was developed by a team of clinical nursing experts based on the National Nursing Skills Standards, with a maximum score of 100 points. The scoring involved the three aspects of operation standardisation, process rationality and completion quality.

In the first week of enrolment, interns were given demonstrations by the teaching secretary in the department and monitored by the head nurse. In the third week, the department conducted unified theoretical knowledge and operational skill assessments and provided feedback on the assessment results.

Clinical communication ability of interns

The clinical communication ability of interns was evaluated using the Nurses‘ Clinical Communication Ability Scale [19]. This scale has six dimensions and 28 items and is scored using the Likert 4-point rating system (1 point = ‘never used‘, 2 points = ‘occasionally used‘, 3 points = ‘often used‘ and 4 points = ‘frequently used‘). This evaluation scale included the following dimensions: establishing a harmonious nurse–patient relationship, listening keenly, confirming patient problems, participating jointly, transmitting effective information and verifying feelings. In this study, the validity of the scale was 0.87, The Cronbach’s α coefficient of the scale was 0.92, with high reliability. Interns with higher scores are likely to have stronger clinical communication abilities [20].

Clinical thinking ability teaching assessment

The Clinical Thinking Ability Teaching Assessment Scale [21] was employed to evaluate the clinical thinking ability of interns. This scale consists of 24 items across three dimensions: critical thinking (6 items), systematic thinking (11 items) and evidence-based thinking (7 items). This scale uses the Likert 5-point rating system for assignment: 5 points = ‘very good’, 4 points = ‘good’, 3 points = ‘general’, 2 points = ‘poor’ and 1 point = ‘very poor’. The maximum score for this scale is 120 points, and it is converted to a hundred point scale, with 80–100 points = ‘very good’, 60–79 points = ‘good’, 40–60 points = ‘general’, 20–40 points = ‘poor’ and 0–19 points = ‘very poor’ for clinical thinking ability scoring. In this study, the validity of the scale was 0.83, Cronbach’s α coefficient was 0.90 and the test–retest reliability was 0.83. Interns with higher total scores are likely to have a stronger clinical thinking ability [22].

Comprehensive ability

The comprehensive clinical ability of interns was assessed by the mini-CEX assessment scale [23, 24]. This study compared the seven core competencies of nursing assessment (nursing physical examination, humanistic care, nursing problems, nursing practice, health education, organisational effectiveness and overall evaluation) in the mini-CEX scale for nursing interns before and after teaching, with a total score of 10 points. In this study, the validity of the scale was 0.86, the Cronbach’s α coefficient of the scale was 0.82, indicating high reliability in evaluation. Comprehensive clinical ability in our study was directly proportional to the score [25].

Teaching satisfaction

After a review of relevant literature on satisfaction evaluation indicators for nursing teaching both domestically and internationally [26, 27], multiple rounds of interviews using the Delphi technique were conducted by teaching management experts and nursing teaching backbones. Then, a self-made intern teaching satisfaction questionnaire [7] was developed with determined items. The preliminary design included eight dimensions: ‘teaching content’, ‘teaching methods’, ‘communication skill’, ‘clinical thinking ability‘, ‘clinical integration’, ‘learning interest’, ‘self-learning ability’ and ‘overall satisfaction‘. Each dimension consisted of the four aspects of ‘very good‘ and ‘good‘ (indicating satisfaction) and ‘general‘ and ‘poor‘ (indicating dissatisfaction). It aimed to investigate the arrangement of teaching plans, acceptance of teaching forms, learning interests and self-evaluation during the internship process among nursing interns. A total of 408 questionnaires were distributed for this survey, with 405 valid questionnaires eventually collected. In this study, the validity of the scale was 0.87, the Cronbach’s α coefficient of the scale was 0.84, indicating high reliability in evaluation.

Statistical analysis

All evaluation data (test scores, scale scores and questionnaire results) were entered into the electronic database by researchers or designated personnel. The entry process uses a double-entry or post-entry check mechanism to minimise entry errors, and the database uses a unique identifier to associate research object information. Data analyses in this study were completed using SPSS 24.0 software (IBM Corporation [International Business Machines Corporation], Armonk, [NYNew York], United States of America [USA]). Measurement data were expressed as mean ± standard deviation, and counting data were presented in the form of frequency and percentage. The independent sample t-test was used to compare the means between groups, and the chi-squared test or Fisher‘s exact test was used to compare the rates between groups. All tests were performed two-tailed, with a significance level of α = 0.05. According to the existing sample size and test level (0.05), the power of the two-sample independent t-test in this study was 81%–87% according to GPower software.

Results

Baseline characteristics

In the control group, there were 23 men (11.44%) and 178 women (88.56%); in the observation group, there were 24 men (11.76%) and 180 women (88.24%). The education level of the control group was 24 undergraduate students (11.94%) and 177 junior college students (88.06%); the education level of the observation group was 24 undergraduate students (11.76%) and 180 junior college students (88.23%). There was no significant difference in gender or education level between the two groups (p > 0.05), and the two groups were comparable.

This suggests that the SBAR model, combined with mini-CEX assessment, can effectively improve the theoretical and operational assessment scores of nursing interns.

Effect of the Situation–Background–Assessment–Recommendation model combined with mini-clinical evaluation assessment on improving comprehensive clinical ability and teaching satisfaction of nursing interns

The application of the SBAR model combined with mini-CEX assessment in clinical teaching has a positive impact on the comprehensive clinical ability of nursing interns. In our study, inter-group comparisons after intervention showed that compared with the control group, the observation group had significant improvement in individual scores of the seven core competencies (nursing assessment, nursing physical examination, humanistic care, nursing problems, nursing operations, health education, organisational effectiveness and overall evaluation) and overall scores (p < 0.001). The intra-group comparison also showed that in the observation group, the clinical communication, clinical thinking and mini-CEX assessment ratings of nursing interns were improved after intervention compared with those before intervention (p < 0.05). Therefore, the SBAR model combined with mini-CEX assessment can effectively improve the comprehensive clinical ability of nursing interns. Furthermore, the overall teaching satisfaction of the observation group was higher than that of the control group (p < 0.05). Specifically, the observation group was superior to the control group in five dimensions: whether it was innovative and diversified, whether it helped improve communication skills, whether it helped improve clinical thinking ability, whether learning interest was improved and whether self-learning ability was improved (p < 0.05); however, no statistically significant difference was found between groups in terms of whether the arrangement of the teaching plan was reasonable or whether the knowledge imparted could be closely integrated with clinical practice (p > 0.05). Discussion

This study explored the impact of the SBAR model combined with mini-CEX assessment on the clinical communication, clinical thinking and comprehensive abilities of nursing interns. This teaching method can effectively improve the quality of nursing education; the abilities of interns in the observation group were significantly better than those of the control group.

The Situation–Background–Assessment–Recommendation model combined with mini-clinical evaluation exercise assessment can improve the internship effectiveness of nursing interns

The SBAR model is a structured communication format that has been extensively used in clinical settings to improve the efficiency and accuracy of information transfer among healthcare teams [28–32]. The results of this study are consistent with the previous literature but highlight the unique value of joint intervention. Compared with Liu et al. [1], who only applied the SBAR model, the operation skill score and clinical communication ability of interns were significantly improved after combining with mini-CEX, which confirmed that the real-time feedback of mini-CEX could strengthen the structured training effect of SBAR. Although Zhao et al. [11] reported that mini-CEX could improve the comprehensive ability of nursing interns, they did not evaluate the clinical thinking dimension. This study verified the simultaneous optimisation of the three dimensions of clinical thinking (critical, systematic and evidence-based thinking) by the combined method for the first time (p < 0.001), which made up for the deficiency of a single method in systematic thinking training.

Characterisation of mini-clinical evaluation exercise and its application value

Mini-CEX is a patient-centred, workplace-based mini-clinical assessment tool that evaluates interns‘ comprehensive abilities from multiple dimensions by observing their clinical performance within a short time [33]. In our study, the introduction of the mini-CEX assessment significantly improved the clinical thinking and comprehensive abilities of interns, which is attributed mostly to its emphasis on practicality and feedback. Mini-CEX has several advantages over traditional teaching assessments. The first merit is real-time assessment and interactivity. In general, the assessment can be completed within 5–10 min and provides direct personalised feedback to help interns identify and improve their shortcomings efficiently. Another strength is multidimensional evaluation, since the mini-CEX assessment considers various aspects, such as medical history collection, physical examination, clinical decision-making and doctor–patient communication to report interns‘ clinical abilities comprehensively [2]. For example, Ba H et al. found in their study that mini-CEX assessment can enhance students‘ clinical decision-making ability and improve their adaptability in real-world clinical scenarios [34]. In this study, significantly higher scores in clinical thinking ability were observed in the observation group than in the control group, further verifying the effectiveness of mini-CEX assessment in cultivating clinical logical thinking and comprehensive abilities.

Moreover, there were significant differences in the comparison of theoretical (88.25 ± 5.23 vs. 87.62 ± 4.45, t = − 3.185, p = 0.002) and operational (94.72 ± 1.94 vs. 91.55 ± 2.13, t = − 15.681, p < 0.001) assessment scores between the observation group and the control group after interventions. This indicates that the SBAR model combined with mini-CEX assessment can result in considerable improvement in the theoretical and operational scores of nursing interns, thereby enhancing their learning outcomes.

In addition, compared with the improved mini-CEX applied by Zhang et al. [12] in emergency nurses, this study extended the joint model to the intern group and confirmed its universality through multi-dimensional evaluation: among 405 junior college/undergraduate interns, the five sub-dimensions of communication ability were significantly improved (p < 0.05) and the teaching satisfaction was 96.3%. This is due to the unique closed-loop design of the combined method. The SBAR model provides a standardised communication framework, and mini-CEX‘s phased assessment (week 1/week 3) achieves dynamic feedback, allowing the problem to be corrected promptly. This may be the key mechanism for the observation group to increase the clinical thinking score by 23.5%.

The Situation–Background–Assessment–Recommendation model combined with mini-clinical evaluation exercise assessment can effectively enhance the clinical communication and thinking abilities of nursing interns

The SBAR model and mini-CEX assessment have strong complementarity in teaching practice, despite different preferences. Together, SBAR and mini-CEX build a ‘structured communication-dynamic feedback’ dual-loop mechanism. The uniqueness of the mechanism lies in the following: (1) SBAR‘s ‘S–B–A–R’ framework standardises the expression logic, while mini-CEX exposes communication vulnerabilities in real time in the 1-week/3-week evaluation; (2) mini-CEX reverse optimises the rigour of the “assessment (A)-recommendation (R)” chain in the SBAR model through decision-making in real clinical scenarios; (3) the seven quantitative scores of mini-CEX drive the targeted improvement of SBAR’s weak links. The SBAR model stresses the standardisation and systematisation of information transfer, enabling students to express their judgements and suggestions more clearly in communication. Mini-CEX can further strengthen students‘ clinical thinking and comprehensive abilities through practical assessments in real-world clinical scenarios.

In this study, the SBAR model combined with mini-CEX assessment comprehensively enhanced the clinical abilities of interns. This can be interpreted as the SBAR model providing students with a framework for communication and expression, while mini-CEX helps students reinforce knowledge and enhance their practical application abilities through practice and feedback. The advantages of this co-teaching have been demonstrated in existing studies. For instance, the combined use of the SBAR model and practice-based assessment tools can significantly improve students‘ comprehensive abilities and self-efficacy [35]. This study further validated this view and put emphasis on the significance of the co-teaching model in nursing education in particular.

The application of the SBAR model [36] facilitates nursing staff–patient communication to identify deficiencies during nursing practice in a timely manner, optimise nursing interventions and enhance patients‘ confidence in treatment, thereby benefiting clinical diagnosis and treatment and promoting patient recovery. Traditional teaching for interns is characterised by passive learning and the absence of quantitative evaluation indicators, leading to unsatisfactory teaching outcomes [36]. The medical education system for interns tends to establish a formative evaluation system for the clinical practice teaching process and outcome assessment of medical students [37, 38]. It is useful for teachers and students to identify and fill in gaps quickly, adjust teaching plans and thus improve teaching effectiveness [39, 40]. Mini-CEX is currently a common formative assessment scale in clinical practice that can provide multiple aspects of assessment and feedback for nursing interns [41]. The combination of the SBAR model and mini-CEX assessment can stimulate nurses‘ subjective initiative, transforming passive learning into initiative learning, active thinking and effective communication. In this study, nursing interns adopting the SBAR model + mini-CEX assessment had noticeably improved clinical communication skills in the five dimensions of establishing harmonious relationships, listening keenly, confirming patient problems, transmitting effective information and verifying feelings compared with the control group (p < 0.05). Moreover, interns in this group also had noticeably enhanced clinical thinking ability in the three dimensions of critical thinking, systematic thinking and evidence-based thinking compared with the control group (p < 0.001), which was consistent with a prior report [42]. Based on these advantages, the application of the SBAR model combined with mini-CEX assessment can effectively improve the clinical communication and thinking abilities of nursing interns, enhance teaching effectiveness and lay a solid foundation for interns to practice clinically.

The Situation–Background–Assessment–Recommendation model combined with mini-clinical evaluation exercise assessment can improve the comprehensive clinical ability and nursing teaching satisfaction of nursing interns

Our study also evaluated the seven core competencies of nursing assessment – namely nursing physical examination, humanistic care, nursing problems, nursing operations, health education, organisational effectiveness and overall evaluation – using the mini-CEX scale. Consequently, with the use of the SBAR model combined with mini-CEX assessment, the comprehensive clinical ability of interns in the observation group was significantly improved compared with that of the control group (p < 0.05). Furthermore, the clinical communication, clinical thinking and mini-CEX assessment ratings of interns in the observation group were improved after intervention compared with those before intervention (p < 0.05). Therefore, it is reasonable to propose that the SBAR model combined with mini-CEX assessment can effectively improve the comprehensive ability of nursing interns, stimulate their interest in learning and internal motivation and encourage them to accept knowledge actively and positively. This teaching method is also more practicable for the clinical teaching of nursing interns, greatly improving their mastery of clinical nursing knowledge, especially in case nursing teaching, and enhancing their comprehensive ability. In addition, the teaching satisfaction among interns in the observation group was surveyed to be superior compared with the control group (p < 0.05). With regard to the above, the application of the SBAR model combined with mini-CEX assessment is effective for nursing interns. It also expands the application scope of mini-CEX, fully enhances the comprehensive clinical ability of interns and improves interns’ satisfaction with nursing teaching. However, there was no statistically significant difference in terms of whether the arrangement of the teaching plan was reasonable or whether the knowledge imparted could be closely integrated with clinical practice between groups (p > 0.05), indicating that teachers paid much attention to the clinical teaching of interns.

However, there are some limitations in this study. First, the sample size included in the study is small, and there may be some research limitations in the extrapolation of the results. In addition, this study is conducted in tertiary teaching hospitals, in which the teaching resources are significantly better than those in primary hospitals, potentially leading to an overestimation of the effect. The conclusion needs to be verified when it is extended to community hospitals or resource-poor institutions. The continuity of short-term effect evaluation is doubtful; only the 3-week training effect is evaluated, and long-term follow-up data are lacking. In the future, we will carry out further research from the following aspects to carry out multicentre RCTs (covering different levels of hospitals) and extend the follow-up to 6 months and design intensive training; an artificial intelligence-assisted mini-CEX scoring system could be developed to reduce subjective bias. We will continue to demonstrate and promote our research based on a more rigorous experimental design, so as to provide additional insights for clinical teaching of nursing interns.

Conclusion

The combined SBAR model and mini-CEX assessment significantly enhanced nursing interns‘ clinical communication, clinical thinking and comprehensive practice abilities. These improvements were validated across theoretical examinations, operational assessments and clinical scenarios, demonstrating practical value for optimising clinical nursing quality. Through structured training and real-time feedback, this approach may exert long-term effects on interns‘ professional mindset cultivation. However, its sustainability requires further validation via long-term follow-up studies. In the follow-up studies, we intend to verify the universality of the collaborative scheme in different levels of hospitals (especially community hospitals) and solve the problem that the current single-centre design may overestimate the effect. At the same time, we plan to carry out a 6-month follow-up study and design intensive training in combination with skill attenuation literature to make up for the limitations of the short-term evaluation.

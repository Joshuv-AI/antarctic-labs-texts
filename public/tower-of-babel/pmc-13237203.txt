Explaining EFL students’ continuance intention to use e-learning through technology acceptance, expectation confirmation, and flow theory
Authors: Xuejie Xu, Chunhui Yang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
With the widespread adoption of digital learning tools and the continuous upgrading of online learning technologies, e-learning has increasingly become a common way for English as Foreign Language (EFL) college students to obtain learning resources and practice English beyond the classroom. Drawing on the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM), Expectation Confirmation Theory (ECT), and Flow Theory, this study examines key factors associated with EFL college students’ continuance intention toward e-learning. Using responses from 435 EFL college students, the study applied PLS-SEM (Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling) using SmartPLS to examine the hypothesized relationships. The results indicate that perceived ease of use, perceived usefulness, confirmation, satisfaction, and perceived enjoyment are significantly related to continuance intention, with the model explaining 59.3% of the variance. Among these factors, perceived usefulness and satisfaction show the strongest associations with continuance intention, while perceived enjoyment is positively related to continuance intention and is also associated with perceived ease of use, perceived usefulness, and satisfaction. Furthermore, the multigroup analysis reveals significant differences between higher-grade and first-year students in specific paths: higher-grade students demonstrate stronger associations in the paths from perceived enjoyment to perceived ease of use and from confirmation to perceived enjoyment. These findings underscore the moderating role of learning experience in the associations underlying continuance intention toward e-learning. Overall, the findings deepen theoretical understanding by integrating TAM, ECT, and Flow Theory into the EFL e-learning setting and inform practical efforts aimed at refining platform design, elevating user satisfaction, and maintaining sustained usage.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-48311-x.

Introduction

Ongoing technological innovation has greatly accelerated the integration of e-learning into today’s educational practices. Leveraging digital technologies and online resources, e-learning offers a flexible and efficient approach to second language acquisition. Unlike traditional classroom instruction, e-learning utilizes online courses1–3, virtual classrooms4–6, educational software, and multimedia content7 to overcome spatial and temporal constraints, thereby helping to enable learners to study anytime and anywhere. This transition is particularly evident in China’s higher education sector, where the Ministry of Education has vigorously promoted the development and integration of National Excellent Online Open Courses into university curricula8. Consequently, a growing number of Chinese universities now require first-year students to adopt online or blended learning formats for foundational subjects like English. This strategic shift aims to achieve equitable distribution of high-quality pedagogical resources across regions and to cultivate students’ autonomous learning abilities from the onset of their tertiary education. Recent advances in digital technologies, ranging from artificial intelligence to virtual and augmented reality, have transformed the way EFL learners access and practice English, which is associated with a broader spectrum of technology-driven learning opportunities9.

Research on EFL learners’ continuance intention (CI) regarding e-learning has adopted diverse theoretical frameworks, such as ECT10, the TAM11–13, the DeLone and McLean Information System Success Model14,15, and the Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology (UTAUT)16,17, to identify influencing factors. In addition, perceived enjoyment (PE), a critical factor derived from Flow Theory, has been shown to be positively associated with students’ CI toward e-learning18,19. For instance, Huang and Zou19 found that PE was linked with students’ emotional engagement during the learning process, thereby corresponding to their willingness to use artificial intelligence to improve their English-speaking skills.

Although several studies have combined TAM, ECT, and PE to explore factors affecting CI20,21, these works primarily focused on diverse contexts such as virtual education exhibitions20 or information technology21, leaving a significant gap in our understanding of these dynamics within the specific domain of EFL e-learning. While TAM and ECT offer robust frameworks for understanding the rational and cognitive drivers of technology adoption, they often overlook the intrinsic, affective experiences that are crucial for sustained language learning. To address this, the present study constructs a synthesized model that integrates TAM, ECT, and Flow Theory, thereby capturing both the rational evaluative pathways and the affective mechanisms—represented by PE—that influence EFL learners’ CI. By doing so, this research addresses the critical lack of a comprehensive framework that accounts for the interplay between cognitive confirmation (CON), functional utility, and optimal psychological experience in a blended English learning environment.

A questionnaire-based cross-sectional study was conducted to collect empirical data from the aforementioned university students, and the proposed conceptual framework was validated using PLS-SEM. The primary objective of this study is to examine the structural relationships between these cognitive and affective determinants and to quantify their collective impact on EFL learners’ persistence in technology-enhanced learning. The outcomes offer valuable directions for practitioners, system developers, and educators by supporting the design of innovative strategies aimed at sustaining learners’ motivation and improving the overall quality of online English instruction.

Theoretical underpinning

The TAM, proposed by Davis22, aims to explain users’ acceptance of and behavioral intentions toward new technologies. As a foundational theoretical model, TAM has been widely applied in e-learning research23,24. For instance, Zheng et al.24 found that, perceived ease of use (PEOU) plays a dual role in e-learning settings by directly shaping students’ CI and indirectly impacting it through PU and satisfaction (SAT). Similarly, Feng and Feng23, drawing on perceived value theory, revealed that college students’ CI toward mobile online education platforms was shaped by multiple factors, with PEOU significantly enhancing perceived usefulness (PU), which in turn promoted CI. While subsequent models like UTAUT225 offer more variables, TAM remains the parsimonious gold standard for assessing the functional utility of e-learning tools, which is critical in EFL settings to reduce the cognitive load associated with dual-task processing—managing both language acquisition and digital interfaces.

Building on both TAM and ECT, Bhattacherjee26 introduced the Expectation-Confirmation Model of IT Continuance (ECM-IT), which is designed to explain how users’ SAT forms and predicts post-adoption intentions. The ECM-IT has been extensively applied in studies on CI in diverse information systems, including e-learning27,28, e-commerce systems29, blended e-learning30, and massive open online courses2,3. For example, Opoku28 demonstrated that the ECM-IT framework explained 57% of the variance in e-learning CI among university lecturers. Likewise, Cheng27 confirmed that PU, CON, and SAT influence CI both directly and indirectly. Distinct from initial adoption theories, ECM-IT captures the critical transition from initial expectation to experience-based evaluation, explaining how the consistency between expected and experienced outcomes affects platform stickiness.

Flow Theory31 describes an optimal psychological state—a transient experience of deep immersion—rather than a stable individual trait. In this study, PE is conceptualized as the immediate affective manifestation of this flow state32,33. Recent empirical evidence highlights the pivotal role of PE in determining CI toward the adoption of e-learning platforms34,35. For example, Huang and Liu34 demonstrated that CON enhanced college students’ PE, which subsequently increased their CI to use asynchronous online English learning platforms. While theories like Self-Determination Theory categorize motivational types, Flow Theory is prioritized here because it captures the real-time experiential quality of the learning interaction, which can lower the “affective filter” and facilitate more efficient language intake.

Integrating these three theories is necessary because they provide a complementary “Cognitive-Evaluative-Affective” framework that single models like UTAUT2 or ECM-IT alone cannot offer. Specifically, TAM addresses the functional precursors (PEOU and PU), ECM-IT accounts for the retrospective evaluation (CON and SAT), and Flow Theory provides the intrinsic emotional mechanism (PE). This synthesis is particularly vital in EFL contexts, which are characterized by higher levels of learner anxiety and cognitive demand compared to general online learning. Unlike general IT usage, language learning requires prolonged, high-frequency interaction where the “rational” utility (TAM/ECM-IT) must be balanced by “affective” immersion (Flow) to prevent dropout. Therefore, this study integrates these theories into a unified predictive model to identify the key pathways underlying EFL college students’ CI and to provide theoretical guidance for instructional practice.

Literature reviews and research hypotheses

TAM

PEOU captures users’ perceptions of how effortlessly a given technology can be utilized without imposing additional cognitive or operational demands, whereas PU reflects their subjective evaluation of how effectively the technology improves task performance efficiency22. TAM proposes that individuals’ perceptions of ease of use influence their judgments of usefulness, which subsequently and directly enhance their intentions to engage with technology36. This logic has been widely supported in educational technology research, although the strength and significance of specific paths may vary across learning contexts23,24,37,38. For example, Zheng et al.24 found that in e-learning environments, PEOU not only directly influenced students’ CI but also indirectly affected it through PU, SAT, and other related variables. Similarly, Bailey et al.39 found that in a survey of Korean EFL learners, PEOU directly and indirectly influenced their intention to use the platform, with PU serving as a mediator. Additionally, PU was found to directly affect their intention to continue using the platform. However, Liu et al.40 found that PEOU did not directly predict CI, but instead influenced usage intention indirectly through the mediation of PU. This suggests that the role of PEOU may be better understood as partly indirect and context-sensitive, rather than uniformly strong across all studies. Grounded in the aforementioned theoretical perspectives and supported by prior empirical research, this study advances the following hypotheses:

H1: PEOU is positively associated with PU.

H2: PEOU is positively associated with CI.

H3: PU is positively associated with CI.

ECT

ECT posits that CON emerges when users perceive their actual experiences to match or exceed their original expectations, which subsequently fosters SAT. SAT, as an emotional outcome derived from CON, directly determines users’ CI26. Building on ECT, the relationships among CON, SAT, and CI have been extensively validated across empirical studies30,41–43. For instance, Baranova et al.30 conducted a survey among Russian university students and found that CON enhanced learners’ SAT with blended learning, which subsequently increased their CI toward this learning mode. Based on these findings, this study proposes the following hypotheses:

H4: CON is positively associated with SAT.

H5: SAT is positively associated with CI.

Recent studies have increasingly combined TAM and ECT to provide a more comprehensive explanation of CI. This integrated perspective suggests that CON not only improves SAT, but may also reshape users’ cognitive evaluations of a platform, including how easy and useful it appears. In other words, when learners feel that their actual experience meets or exceeds expectations, they may judge the platform more favorably both emotionally and instrumentally. In the e-learning setting, CON not only shapes learners’ SAT directly but also serves as a significant predictor of their cognitive assessments regarding platform functionality. Prior studies have indicated that when users perceive their actual experiences to be consistent with initial expectations, they are more likely to consider the system simple and easy to use, thereby increasing their PEOU44–48. Moreover, when CON is high, users are also more likely to evaluate the platform as worthwhile and effective for learning, which may strengthen PU21,49–51. Liu et al.45 demonstrated that in the context of e-book applications, both PU and SAT significantly and positively predicted users’ CI. Similarly, some studies have shown that PEOU and PU can contribute to SAT, although these effects may depend on the learning environment and system characteristics rather than emerge uniformly across all contexts. According to findings by Nuryakin et al.52, based on a simple mediation design, both PEOU and PU served as significant direct predictors of Indonesian students’ SAT with online platforms. Accordingly, the following hypotheses are proposed:

H6: CON is positively associated with PEOU.

H7: CON is positively associated with PU.

H8: PEOU is positively associated with SAT.

H9: PU is positively associated with SAT.

Perceived enjoyment

PE has been identified as a significant psychological predictor of learners’ CI in e-learning contexts. Existing studies suggest that PE fosters users’ positive evaluations of technological tools and strongly drives their intentions to persist in using these systems53,54. In a survey of 869 participants, Hamari et al.53 examined the association between PE and purchase intention. The results demonstrated that PE strongly promoted users’ willingness to engage in purchasing behavior. For EFL college students, when e-learning platforms provide rich, engaging, and highly interactive learning content, learners are more likely to enter a state of flow, which fosters intrinsic learning motivation, reduces cognitive resistance, and strengthens emotional attachment to the platform. Therefore, this study proposes the following hypothesis:

H10: PE is positively associated with CI.

PE may also shape CI indirectly by influencing learners’ cognitive and emotional responses to the platform. Prior research suggests that enjoyable learning experiences can make systems feel easier to use, enhance perceptions of utility, and improve users’ SAT with the learning process. Rather than treating these pathways separately, the present study considers PE as an affective antecedent that may simultaneously strengthen TAM-related beliefs and ECT-related evaluations. In forming CI, PE serves as both a direct motivator of learners’ behavioral decisions and an indirect driver operating through fundamental cognitive mechanisms outlined in the TAM. First, enjoyable experiences enhance learners’ psychological flexibility, enabling them to remain more focused while using e-learning platforms and subjectively perceive the system as smoother and easier to operate, thereby increasing PEOU22,55. For example, Liang et al.56 found that when EFL learners experience positive emotions during online learning, they tend to underestimate the interface complexity and perceive the platform as more user-friendly. Second, learners who enjoy the e-learning process are more inclined to link their positive experiences to the platform’s functional capabilities, thereby perceiving it as more useful in supporting their learning25. For instance, To and Trinh57 surveyed 332 college students and found that PE significantly and positively influenced PU, which subsequently enhanced students’ CI to use online learning platforms. From an ECT perspective, enjoyment may also arise when actual learning experiences align with or exceed prior expectations, and such enjoyment may further enhance SAT. Oliver58 proposed that users form positive CON when their perceived experiences meet or outperform initial expectations, which consequently strengthens their sense of enjoyment. In e-learning contexts, SAT of EFL college students’ expectations regarding platform functionality and learning content makes them more likely to experience enjoyment and SAT26. Moreover, enjoyment represents a critical driver of emotional value, significantly improving users’ overall evaluation of and emotional attachment to a system. For example, Li et al.59 found that PE and PU function as mediators between CON and SAT, implying that these three variables jointly constitute key determinants of user SAT in the context of online learning platforms. These findings imply that PE emerges from CON and, in turn, shapes SAT. In line with this reasoning, the following hypotheses are developed:

H11: PE is positively associated with PEOU.

H12: PE is positively associated with PU.

H13: CON is positively associated with PE.

H14: PE is positively associated with SAT.

The mediating effect of perceived usefulness and satisfaction

Given that CI is shaped by multiple cognitive and emotional mechanisms, it is necessary to examine not only direct relationships but also the indirect pathways through which learners form sustained usage intentions. In particular, PU and SAT are two central explanatory mechanisms in TAM and ECT, respectively. Existing studies suggest that both variables may function as mediators linking platform-related perceptions and affective experiences with CI.

PU, a core construct in the TAM22, plays a critical role in e-learning contexts. Beyond directly predicting CI, PU often serves as a mediating variable that links system characteristics, emotional experiences, and behavioral intentions60. First, PU may mediate the relationship between PEOU and CI. Evidence from previous studies highlights that learners’ perception of a platform’s ease of use fosters a more favorable evaluation of its learning functionalities and, in turn, strengthens their continued usage intentions22,61. Second, PU also acts as a bridge between CON and CI. When learners find that their real experiences outperform what they initially expected, they are more likely to evaluate the platform positively for its role in facilitating language learning, which subsequently promotes higher CI26,59. Finally, PU serves as an important mediator between PE and CI. For example, Huang and Liu34 found that when EFL learners engage in online course learning and experience positive emotions, they tend to form favorable attributions that enhance their perceptions of the platform’s effectiveness in facilitating learning improvement, ultimately contributing to stronger intentions for sustained use. Based on this reasoning, the following hypotheses are proposed:

H15a: PU mediates the relationship between PEOU and CI.

H15b: PU mediates the relationship between CON and CI.

H15c: PU mediates the relationship between PE and CI.

SAT, as conceptualized in ECT58, is regarded as a key emotional variable that links user experience with behavioral intention. Within e-learning environments, SAT not only directly predicts CI but also serves as a mediator across multiple pathways26. First, SAT is a critical mechanism through which CON influences CI. When learners’ actual experiences meet or exceed their expectations, they form positive emotional evaluations, thereby enhancing their willingness to continue using the platform26. Second, PEOU and PU may also indirectly influence CI through SAT. When learners perceive a platform as user-friendly, they are more inclined to experience comfort and achievement, which lead to higher SAT levels and, in turn, strengthen CI62. Similarly, when learners view the platform as highly effective in improving learning performance, positive emotional responses are elicited, leading to stronger CIs63. Finally, PE may also enhance SAT, which subsequently promotes CI. For instance, Zhao and Khan64 found that when EFL learners have enjoyable experiences on online English platforms, their emotional SAT significantly increases, thereby reinforcing their behavioral intentions. By establishing a bridge between emotional evaluations and behavioral intentions, SAT serves as a key psychological mechanism for explaining users’ CI. Accordingly, this study proposes the following hypotheses:

H16a: SAT mediates the relationship between CON and CI.

H16b: SAT mediates the relationship between PEOU and CI.

H16c: SAT mediates the relationship between PU and CI.

H16d: SAT mediates the relationship between PE and CI.

Methods

Participants and procedure

The survey was conducted between March and April 2025 using an online questionnaire platform. To ensure that the participants were EFL learners with sufficient time dedicated to English learning, a multistage sampling strategy was used. First, three separate universities in China were selected through convenience sampling. Before sampling, the research team contacted faculty members in the foreign language departments or relevant teaching units of the selected universities to obtain permission and gather basic information about class structures, student numbers, and course schedules. Based on this information, 15 classes were randomly selected as sampling units. Participants were drawn from students enrolled in English-related or English-intensive programs, including English majors as well as students from other programs with substantial English language training, such as those pursuing a double major in English or majors such as international trade.

A preliminary investigation further showed that the participating universities adopted a blended learning model for several curriculum-based English-related courses. Specifically, students completed part of their English learning through online platforms, which were used to support required or elective courses and to complement face-to-face classroom instruction. These courses included English Academic Writing, English for University Study: Read and Think, and Business English Translation, depending on program requirements. This blended learning arrangement ensured that participants had prior experience using digital tools for English learning. During class breaks, trained research assistants visited these selected classes and recruited approximately 30 to 40 students from each class to participate, resulting in a total of 500 students recruited for the survey.

Prior to questionnaire administration, research assistants presented participants with a standardized informed consent statement, outlining the objectives of the study, the estimated completion time (approximately four minutes), intended data usage, confidentiality protections, and clarifying that participation was voluntary with no academic consequences for withdrawal. Informed consent was obtained from all participants prior to their involvement in the study. The majority of participants were over 18 years old. For participants under 18, parental and/or legal guardian consent was obtained before participation, and additional screening was conducted to ensure compliance with ethical guidelines. Only limited demographic information, such as grade and age, was obtained. In total, 478 questionnaires were returned. After data screening, 24 incomplete submissions and 19 low-quality responses—identified through atypical completion times or identical answers across all items—were discarded. Thus, 435 valid surveys were retained. The effective response rate is calculated as the number of valid surveys (435) divided by the total number of distributed questionnaires (500), resulting in an effective response rate of 87%.

Table 1 presents the participants’ demographic characteristics. Among the respondents, female students accounted for 74.02%, a distribution consistent with the gender composition typically observed among English majors in China. Most participants were 18–20 years old (78.85%), while other age groups were less represented. Additionally, the majority were first-year students (73.56%). It is worth noting that some participants were below 18 years of age, having entered university at 17 through early admission or accelerated academic programs, and would have turned 18 during their first year of study. The bivariate correlation analysis between the dependent variable and demographic information revealed no significant correlations between students’ CI to use online learning platforms and the demographic characteristics.

Measurements

The questionnaire consisted of two parts: basic demographic information and participants’ perceptions of e-learning tools. All scales were adapted from previous studies with established reliability and validity, and the complete measurement items are presented in Supplementary Table S1. The reliability of these scales, as reported in prior studies, ranged from Cronbach’s α values of 0.811 to 0.953. The research team made appropriate modifications to the original scales to align with the objectives of this study and the cultural context. Translation and back-translation procedures were conducted to ensure the scales’ linguistic and cultural appropriateness. The scales measuring PEOU and PU were adopted from Fan and Jiang65. The scale for CON was adapted from Rajeh et al.66, while the SAT scale was drawn from Sumi and Kabir67. The items measuring PE were taken from Moghavvemi et al.68, and part of the scale for CI was adapted from Yang et al.69. Responses for all items were collected on a five-grade Likert scale, extending from the lowest point (1 = strongly disagree) to the highest (5 = strongly agree).

Statistical analyses

SmartPLS 4.0 was applied to perform PLS-SEM analyses in order to validate the measurement model adopted in this study. While CB-SEM is commonly used for confirmatory analysis, PLS-SEM was selected for this study due to its advantages in handling complex models, its prediction-oriented nature, and its ability to handle non-normal data distributions, which makes it particularly suitable for the current research context. This choice aligns with the study’s focus on prediction and its relatively large sample size, which allows for the application of PLS-SEM to obtain reliable results70.

Confirmatory factor analysis

Before proceeding with Structural Equation Modeling (SEM), a Confirmatory Factor Analysis (CFA) was performed using AMOS 26 to assess the measurement model’s adequacy. This analysis was crucial to ensure that all constructs were adequately measured, and the measurement model fit the data well. Although the subsequent Structural Equation Modeling (SEM) was conducted using SmartPLS 4.0 to test the hypothesized relationships, the CFA results from AMOS 26 were retained to confirm the adequacy of the measurement model. As displayed in Table 3, the resulting fit indices were consistent with the cutoff criteria suggested by previous studies71, and the CFA analysis provided validation of the constructs before performing PLS-SEM.

To further evaluate the model fit, the standardized residual covariance matrix was examined. According to Jöreskog and Sörbom72, standardized residual values with an absolute value less than 2.58 indicate a well-fitting model. In the current study, most of the standardized residuals were well below the 2.58 threshold, with only one minor exception (CI3 and PE1, value = 3.007). Since the overwhelming majority of the residuals were small and no significant clusters of high residuals were found, the model was deemed to have achieved a satisfactory fit at the local level. For full transparency, the complete matrix has been included in Supplementary Table S2 of the revised manuscript.

Measurement model

This study employed a two-step SEM approach. First, internal reliability was verified by examining the composite reliability (CR) and Cronbach’s alpha for each construct, followed by assessing convergent validity using item loadings, CR, and average variance extracted (AVE). Discriminant validity was further evaluated via heterotrait–monotrait ratio (HTMT) values and AVE-based comparisons.

As shown in Table 4, all item loadings exceeded 0.70, and both CR and Cronbach’s alpha values were above 0.70, indicating satisfactory internal consistency. Moreover, AVE values for all constructs were greater than 0.50, supporting adequate convergent validity73.

Structural model

This study used three indicators to assess the reliability and explanatory power of the model: multicollinearity, the coefficient of determination (R²), and the predictive relevance (Q²). R² evaluated the explanatory strength of endogenous variables, whereas Q² assessed the model’s predictive capability compared with alternative models.

Multicollinearity test

The multi-group analysis

This study performed a multi-group analysis (MGA) to test the moderating effect of participants’ grade level on the proposed model. MGA primarily tests for group differences by comparing the path coefficients across groups, thereby identifying whether significant differences exist between them81. Before performing the MGA, the research team conducted a measurement invariance of composite models (MICOM) test to determine the source of potential differences in the structural relationships among latent variables. When differences between groups stem from distinct interpretations or understandings of the measured constructs rather than from differences in the structural relationships themselves, MGA is considered appropriate82. The following section presents the procedures and results of the MICOM and MGA analyses.

Measurement invariance of composites (MICOM)

In this study, most participants were first-year students. For MGA, they were divided into two groups: first-year (n = 320) and other grades (n = 115). To test measurement invariance, 1,000 permutation tests were conducted. Following Henseler et al.82, compositional invariance is confirmed when the original correlation exceeds 5% of permuted correlations. As shown in Table 10, all correlations met this criterion, supporting compositional invariance and justifying the MGA.

It is possible that as students progress in their academic journey, they develop greater comfort with technological tools and stronger emotional connections with these platforms, which could explain the observed differences.

Discussion

This study integrated the TAM, ECT, and Flow Theory to construct and validate a predictive model of EFL college students’ CI toward e-learning platforms. The results revealed that PEOU, PU, CON, SAT, and PE were significantly associated with CI through both direct and indirect paths. All hypotheses were supported, and the proposed model explained 59.3% of the variance in CI. These findings suggest that learners’ sustained engagement in e-learning is likely influenced by a combination perceptual, cognitive, and affective mechanisms, underscoring the value of integrating multiple theoretical frameworks in educational technology research.

According to the analysis, the way individuals interpret and process information is closely related to their CI. PEOU not only showed a direct association with learners’ operational experience but also was positively related to their perceptions of the platform’s usefulness, thereby supporting a stronger CI (H1 and H3 supported). This aligns with the original assumptions of TAM22, which posit that individuals’ intention to adopt a technology is primarily contingent on whether they perceive the system as easy to use and beneficial50. As cognitive demands increase in EFL e-learning contexts, learners’ expectations for simplicity, fluidity, and efficiency in platform interaction become more pronounced. Enhanced learning outcomes combined with reduced effort are often accompanied by greater receptivity to adopting the system, and this tendency may coincide with stronger reliance and more persistent usage patterns. However, H2 (PEOU → CI) was not supported (β = 0.113, t = 1.781, p = 0.075). This suggests that while PEOU positively affects learners’ perception of ease of use, it does not directly influence their intention to continue using the platform. One possible explanation for this is that in the context of EFL learning environments, learners may rely more on emotional and cognitive factors, such as SAT and enjoyment, rather than simply perceiving the platform as easy to use. It is likely that the combination of enjoyment and the overall learning experience plays a more significant role in determining CI than the PEOU alone.

This study also emphasizes the critical role of CON and SAT in forming CI. The results show that CON was positively related to learners’ SAT and was indirectly associated with CI through PU and PEOU (H4 and H5 supported). Consistent with ECT58, the findings indicate that users tend to report higher SAT when their actual experiences fulfill or exceed expectations, which in turn is linked to a stronger intention to persist in using the system26,83. In the EFL context, learners often develop expectations about platform functionality, resource availability, and interaction support before using e-learning platforms. Positive discrepancies between expected and actual outcomes generally co-occur with favorable learning orientations and are associated with greater persistence over time.

The findings further confirm the significant role of PE in predicting CI. PE not only was directly associated with CI but also showed indirect associations with learning stickiness via PU, PEOU, and SAT (H10, H11, H12, and H14 supported). Flow Theory31 posits that pleasant emotional states encourage immersion in tasks and reinforce internal motivation, a relationship corroborated by these results21,57. For EFL learners, e-learning platforms function not only as knowledge-delivery tools but also as emotional motivators. Platforms that offer adaptive and engaging learning contexts encourage learners to become fully absorbed in the process, which, in turn, is typically accompanied by more persistent involvement in e-learning activities84.

This study also reveals the dynamic interplay between cognitive evaluations and emotional experiences. CON was found to be positively related to PE (H13 supported), shaping learners’ value judgments of the platform while simultaneously relating to their affective responses. Prior evidence21 suggests that when learners’ anticipated outcomes are realized and accompanied by positive emotional experiences, they are inclined to hold favorable attitudes toward technological tools, which further corresponds to stronger CI. In other words, sustained learning behavior is not solely driven by rational evaluations but is more appropriately understood as the synergistic effects of perceptual, cognitive, and affective mechanisms.

Although both PU (β = 0.164) and PE (β = 0.120) are statistically significant predictors of SAT, their effect sizes are weak and may not have substantial practical implications. This suggests that while these factors are related to SAT, the connection between them and actual behavior is relatively weak. We hypothesize that other situational or individual factors, such as personal preferences and prior experiences, play a more significant role in shaping learners’ SAT and behavior. Furthermore, a notable difference is observed in the influence of PU and SAT on CI: SAT (β = 0.410) emerged as a stronger predictor than PU (β = 0.244), indicating that emotional SAT plays a more central role in shaping learners’ intentions to continue using the platform, particularly in the EFL context. This finding contrasts with traditional TAM perspectives, which typically emphasize the cognitive evaluation of usefulness. It appears that emotional engagement, such as enjoyment and SAT, has a more direct influence on learners’ intentions, with SAT serving as a stronger motivator for continued use because it reflects both cognitive and emotional components of the learning experience. These findings suggest that future research should focus more on emotional pathways and explore how SAT can drive behavioral intentions, especially in contexts where emotional engagement is crucial for persistence.

The mediation analysis further illuminates the psychological mechanisms underlying CI. PU served as a critical mediator between PEOU and PE on CI, supporting H15a and H15c. However, H15b was not supported, as the mediation effect was non-significant (β = 0.080, p = 0.066). This suggests that, in this model, CON does not significantly influence CI through PU, possibly due to the low strength of the CON effect in this specific context or a lack of strong perception of value from the CON process. On the other hand, SAT played a more substantial mediating role, facilitating the effects of CON, PEOU, PU, and PE on CI, thus supporting H16a, H16b, H16c, and H16d. These findings suggest that learners’ decisions to continue using e-learning platforms are closely tied to two key mechanisms: whether they perceive the platform as valuable and whether they feel emotionally satisfied. Thus, the combination of functional value and emotional experience is essential for effectively enhancing learning stickiness in e-learning environments.

Further results from the MGA revealed significant differences between first-year and higher-grade students on specific paths. For higher-grade learners, the relationship between PE and PEOU (PE → PEOU, H11) was significantly stronger (β = 0.550 vs. β = 0.209, p = 0.010), suggesting that greater enjoyment of learning is more strongly associated with a stronger sense of ease in using the platform. One plausible explanation is that higher-grade students, with more experience and familiarity with e-learning platforms, are better equipped to translate enjoyable experiences into positive evaluations of usability85. Additionally, the effect of CON on PE (CON → PE, H13) was also significantly stronger among higher-grade students (β = 0.725 vs. β = 0.462, p = 0.009). This indicates that higher-grade students are more sensitive to whether learning experiences meet their expectations. Learners tend to experience higher levels of enjoyment when the system’s capabilities and actual outcomes align with or surpass what they anticipated.

However, a potential contradiction arises in the relationship between CON and PEOU (H6). Contrary to what might be expected, higher-grade students actually showed a weaker association between CON and PEOU (β = 0.312 vs. β = 0.547, p = 0.067, marginally significant). This inconsistency may be explained by the fact that higher-grade students, having more experience, may already have established their perceptions of the platform’s usability, making CON less influential in shaping their perceptions of ease of use. This observation also raises the issue of survivorship bias, where the higher-grade students who remain in the study might have had more positive prior experiences with the platform, which could affect their responses.

In conclusion, the findings highlight that higher-grade students are more responsive to emotional rewards and fulfilled expectations when assessing usability and deriving enjoyment from the system. However, some of the results, such as the weaker effect of CON on PEOU for higher-grade students, require further reflection. Future studies should consider these nuances and provide a more comprehensive theoretical justification for why SAT emerged as the strongest predictor of CI, especially in the context of higher-grade students’ learning experiences. For the other paths, the comparison between groups yielded no statistically significant differences (p > 0.05). Although the model appears robust for both cohorts, the results imply that more experienced students are more responsive to emotional rewards and fulfilled expectations when assessing usability and deriving enjoyment from the system. These results suggest that the extent of learners’ prior experience can alter the strength of relationships affecting CI in digital learning environments.

This study makes three major theoretical contributions: First, the study reframes the role of technology acceptance models by focusing on the distinct mechanisms operating within the EFL context. By integrating the TAM, ECT, and Flow Theory, this study constructed and validated a comprehensive predictive model that explains how PEOU, PU, CON, SAT, and PE jointly influence EFL college students’ CI toward e-learning platforms. While previous research has combined these theories (e.g., Binowo et al.20 and Thong et al.21, this study specifically addresses the psychological dynamics underlying technology use and its sustained adoption in the EFL context. Second, the study emphasizes the moderating role of learning experience, as revealed through MGA, in shaping the relationship between emotional experiences and technology evaluations. The findings show that higher-grade students exhibit stronger dependencies along the “PE → PEOU” and “CON → PE” paths, suggesting that accumulated e-learning experience is associated with how learners incorporate emotional experiences and expectation fulfillment into their evaluations of usability and enjoyment. Third, the study offers a comparative analysis of the dual cognitive and affective pathways, which highlights the importance of both emotional and cognitive experiences in driving technology engagement. These results expand the boundaries of affective experiences in technology acceptance research, underscoring the critical role of positive emotions in sustaining e-learning platform usage. Overall, these results underscore the importance of considering individual variation in explaining technology engagement and continued use.

By highlighting key influencing factors, the study delivers actionable implications for platform designers, instructors, and academic administrators to foster learners’ sustained engagement with e-learning platforms. First, optimizing platform functionality and usability forms the foundation for improving CI in the EFL context. The results indicate that PEOU indirectly influences CI by significantly enhancing PU and SAT. Therefore, platforms should focus on improving interface intuitiveness, simplicity, and system stability to minimize learners’ operational burden during the learning process. For example, EFL platforms could offer language-specific features like interactive dictionaries, multi-language support, and simplified navigation tailored to the needs of non-native English learners. Second, enhancing learners’ CON is essential for increasing both SAT and learning stickiness. Platforms should provide high-quality, customizable learning resources and leverage intelligent recommendations and real-time feedback to ensure that learners’ experiences align with—or even exceed—their expectations. In the EFL context, this could involve using personalized vocabulary lists, adaptive reading materials, or real-time grammar corrections, which respond dynamically to individual learner performance. Such designs not only foster higher SAT but also strengthen learners’ engagement and loyalty. Third, focusing on emotion-driven design is critical, especially for EFL learners. The findings show that PE plays a central role in predicting CI. To stimulate learners’ enjoyment and immersion, platforms should incorporate features such as task-based gamified learning activities, scenario-based interactions like role-playing for language practice, instant feedback on language tasks, and multimedia-rich resources that combine audio, video, and interactive content tailored to language acquisition. These strategies can foster positive emotional engagement and enhance learners’ intrinsic motivation86. Finally, the results underscore the importance of differentiated support across grade levels. The MGA revealed that higher-grade students are more sensitive to the effects of PE on PEOU and CON on PE. This suggests that higher-grade students rely more heavily on enjoyable experiences and expectation fulfillment to form positive evaluations of e-learning platforms. Consequently, platforms could design more challenging, task-based language learning activities for higher-grade students to enhance enjoyment, such as real-time debates, critical thinking exercises, or more advanced level assignments that cater to their cognitive needs. On the other hand, for first-year students, platforms should implement low-threshold affective engagement strategies, such as introductory tutorials, beginner-friendly language courses, simple step-by-step navigation guides, and interactive language games that encourage initial engagement without overwhelming them. These approaches can help first-year students adapt more effectively to e-learning environments and build confidence in their language skills.

Despite the contributions made, certain constraints exist, which warrant further attention in subsequent research. First, from a methodological perspective, a cross-sectional survey was applied, making it possible to identify statistical links among variables but preventing confirmation of cause-and-effect relationships. Further investigations might adopt designs involving repeated measurements or experimental interventions to reveal how CI evolves over time and to provide stronger evidence for the causal mechanisms assumed in the framework. Second, the use of self-reported questionnaires as the primary data source introduces potential concerns, as responses may be shaped by participants’ desire for social approval or imperfect recall. Future studies could integrate multi-source data and objective measures, such as learning logs, task completion rates, and platform interaction data, to improve the objectivity and validity of findings. Moreover, leveraging emerging technologies to monitor visual attention and emotional responses could significantly enhance understanding of learners’ affective engagement and flow experiences during online learning. A further limitation is the gender imbalance in the sample, with a significantly higher proportion of female students (74.02%), which is reflective of the prevalence of English majors among female students in the participating universities. This skewed gender distribution, coupled with the high proportion of first-year students (73.56%), may limit the generalizability of the findings across different genders and grade levels. Future research should strive for more balanced samples to better understand gender differences in e-learning engagement and continuation intention. Additionally, the study was conducted at only three universities in China, which raises concerns regarding the cross-cultural validity of the findings. The applicability of the results to other EFL contexts—such as non-English majors, vocational colleges, or part-time learners—remains undiscussed and should be explored in future studies. Moreover, the study did not address the heterogeneity of “e-learning” platforms, which could involve distinct mechanisms depending on the platform type (e.g., apps, web-based systems, VR, AI-driven speaking tools). Future research should consider the variations in platform types and how these might affect learners’ engagement and CI. Incorporating these aspects into future investigations would enable the development of a broader explanatory framework and contribute to extending theoretical perspectives on how learners adopt and continue using technology in e-learning contexts. Finally, with respect to extending the theoretical model, the research examined how perceptual, cognitive, and emotional processes affect CI but did not address the role of external influences. Variables such as learning motivation, self-efficacy, social support, and learning outcomes may play moderating or mediating roles within the model. Incorporating these aspects into future investigations would enable the development of a broader explanatory framework and contribute to extending theoretical perspectives on how learners adopt and continue using technology in e-learning contexts.

Conclusion

This study integrated the TAM, ECT, and Flow Theory to construct and validate a comprehensive model of EFL college students’ CI toward e-learning. Using SmartPLS for PLS-SEM, the findings showed that PEOU, PU, CON, SAT, and PE were jointly associated with CI, explaining 59.3% of its variance. Among these factors, PU and SAT showed the strongest associations with CI, while PE showed both a direct association and indirect associations via PEOU, PU, and SAT. The MGA revealed that higher-grade students showed stronger associations consistent with positive emotional experiences and expectation fulfillment than first-year students, underscoring the moderating role of learning experience. Theoretically, this study advances the integration of perceptual, cognitive, and affective mechanisms in explaining CI and provides new evidence for individual differences in technology adoption. Practically, the findings offer insights for e-learning platform optimization and instructional design, highlighting the need to balance functional usability with emotion-driven engagement. Future research should employ longitudinal designs, cross-cultural validation, and model extensions to further strengthen these conclusions.

(r_{1},r_{2})+(p_{1},p_{2})=(r_{1}+p_{1},r_{2}+p_{2})\in\Delta(R_{1}\times R_{2})
. Conversely, let
R_{1}\times R_{2}
be
\Delta
-quasipolar ring. Let
a\in R_{1}
. Then
(a,0)\in R_{1}\times R_{2}
is
\Delta
-quasipolar. Hence there exist
(p_{1},p_{2})^{2}=(p_{1},p_{2})\in comm^{2}((a,0))
such that
(a,0)+(p_{1},p_{2})\in\Delta(R_{1}\times R_{2})=\Delta(R_{1})\times\Delta(R_{2})
. Therefore
a+p_{1}\in\Delta(R_{1})
. Also it is obvious that
p_{1}^{2}=p_{1}
. Now let

. Then
(x,0)(a,0)=(xa,0)=(ax,0)=(a,0)(x,0)
. Since
(p_{1},p_{2})\in comm^{2}((a,0))

(p_{1},p_{2})(x,0)=(p_{1}x,0)=(xp_{1},0)=(x,0)(p_{1},p_{2})
. So
p_{1}\in comm^{2}(a)
, as desired.
\Delta
-quasipolarity of
R_{2}
can be shown similarly.
∎

.

Let

Theorem 2.24
.

Let
R
be a ring. Then we have the following.

(1)

If

is
\Delta
-quasipolar, then
R
is
\Delta
-quasipolar.

(2)

If the following conditions are satisfied, then
D(R,V)
is
\Delta
-quasipolar.

(i)

R
is
\Delta
-quasipolar.

(ii)

If
e^{2}=e
, then
ev=ve
for all
v\in V
.

(iii)

If
v\in V
, then there exists
w\in V
such that

v+w+vw=0
.

Proof.

(1) Let
r\in R
, then
(r,0)\in D(R,V)
is
\Delta
-quasipolar. So there exists
e^{2}=e\in comm^{2}((r,0))
such that
(r,0)+e\in\Delta(D(R,V))
. Let
e=(p,q)
for some

and

. Then
p^{2}=p\in R
. Now let
xr=rx
for some

. Then we have
(x,0)(r,0)=(xr,0)=(rx,0)=(r,0)(x,0)
. Since
(p,q)\in comm^{2}((r,0))

(p,q)(x,0)=(px,qx)=(xp,xq)=(x,0)(p,q)
. So

. Also we have
(r,0)+(p,q)=(r+p,q)\in\Delta(D(R,V))
. Therefore

. This completes the proof.

(2) Assume that (i), (ii), (iii) hold. Let

. Let
(x,y)\in D(\Delta(R),V))
and

. Then

as desired.

Now there exists

The rings
H_{(s,t)}(R)
:
Let

be a ring and

. Set

Then
H_{(s,t)}(R)
is a subring of

.

Note that
U(H_{(s,t)}(R))=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}a&0&0\\
c&d&e\\
0&0&f\end{bmatrix}\in M_{3}(R)\mid a,d,f\in U(R)\right\}
. Now using that we have a useful lemma.

Lemma 2.26
.

Let
R
be a ring, then

\Delta(H_{(s,t)}(R))=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}a&0&0\\
c&d&e\\
0&0&f\end{bmatrix}\in M_{3}(R)\mid a,d,f\in\Delta(R)\right\}.

Proof.

Let
\begin{bmatrix}a&0&0\\
c&d&e\\
0&0&f\end{bmatrix}\in\Delta(H_{(s,t)}(R))
. Then for any
\begin{bmatrix}x&0&0\\
y&z&p\\
0&0&q\end{bmatrix}\in U(H_{(s,t)}(R))
, we have

\begin{bmatrix}a&0&0\\
c&d&e\\
0&0&f\end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix}x&0&0\\
y&z&p\\
0&0&q\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}a+x&0&0\\
c+y&d+z&e+p\\
0&0&f+q\end{bmatrix}\in U(H_{(s,t)}(R)).

So for any
x,z,q\in U(R)
, we have
a+x,d+z,f+q\in U(R)
and this implies

. Now let
\begin{bmatrix}a&0&0\\
c&d&e\\
0&0&f\end{bmatrix}\in H_{(s,t)}(R)
and

. Let
\begin{bmatrix}x&0&0\\
y&z&p\\
0&0&q\end{bmatrix}\in U(H_{(s,t)}(R))

. Since
a,d,f\in\Delta(R)
and
x,z,q\in U(R)
, we have

. Therefore we have

\begin{bmatrix}a&0&0\\
x&d&e\\
0&0&f\end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix}x&0&0\\
y&z&p\\
0&0&q\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}a+x&0&0\\

∎

.

be a ring and

is
\Delta
-quasipolar if and only if

\Delta

Orbits of spherical representations and Pyasetskii duality Thanks: This article is an output of a research project implemented as part of the Basic Research Program at HSE University
Daniil Shunin

Abstract
. Dual representations V V and V ∗ V^{*} of a complex connected algebraic group G G simultaneously have either infinitely or finitely many orbits. Whenever the latter holds, the orbits in V V and V ∗ V^{*} are in a bijective correspondence called Pyasetskii duality [ Py ] . We obtain a complete description of this duality in the case of spherical representations.



1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a connected linear algebraic group defined over the field
\mathbb{C}
. It was shown in
[
Py
]
that a finite-dimensional representation
V
of
G
and its dual
V^{*}
both contain either finitely or infinitely many orbits. In the first case there is a bijection between the orbits in mutually dual representations called
Pyasetskii duality
.

From now on,
G
is assumed to have finitely many orbits in
V
. The reduced zero fiber of the moment map
\mu:V\oplus V^{*}\to\mathfrak{g}^{*}
is called the
commuting variety
, that is,

\mathfrak{C}=\mu^{-1}(0)_{red}=\{(v,v^{*})\mid v^{*}\in(\mathfrak{g}v)^{\perp}\}=\{(v,v^{*})\mid v\in(\mathfrak{g}v^{*})^{\perp}\}.

It is an unmixed
G
-stable closed subvariety of dimension
\dim V
. Note that
\mathfrak{C}\cap(O\times V^{*})
is the total space
N^{*}O
of the conormal bundle of an orbit
O\subset V
. Then
\mathfrak{C}
is the union
\cup N^{*}O
over all orbits
O
in
V
(by symmetry, in
V^{*}
). The following theorem explains the role of the irreducible components of the commuting variety:

Theorem 1.1

(
[
Py
]
)
.

Suppose
G
has finitely many orbits in
V
and
\mathfrak{C}=\cup_{i}\mathfrak{C}_{i}
is the irreducible decomposition. Then

1.
\dim\mathfrak{C}_{i}=\dim V
for each i;

2. the projection of
\mathfrak{C}
onto the first (resp. second) factor sets up a one-to-one correspondence between the irreducible components of
\mathfrak{C}
and the
G
-orbits in
V
(resp.
V^{*}
). Namely, each
\mathfrak{C}_{i}
arises as the closure of
N^{*}O
for a unique orbit
O
in
V
(by symmetry,
Q
in
V^{*}
).

The orbits
O\subset V
and
Q\subset V^{*}
defined by the condition
\overline{N^{*}O}=\overline{N^{*}Q}=\mathfrak{C}_{i}
are called (
Pyasetskii-
)
dual
and denoted by
O^{\vee}=Q
. Observe that both
\{0\}\times V^{*}
and
V\times\{0\}
occur as irreducible components of
\mathfrak{C}
, so the zero orbit is always dual to the open one.

While this orbit correspondence may be quite complicated in general, a complete answer for a special case was obtained in
[
Pan
]
.

Theorem 1.2

(
[
Pan
]
)
.

Let
\mathfrak{l}
be a simple
\mathbb{Z}
-graded Lie algebra with only three nonzero parts
\mathfrak{l}=\mathfrak{l}_{-1}\oplus\mathfrak{l}_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{l}_{1}
. Denote by
G=L_{0}
a connected group corresponding to
\mathfrak{l}_{0}
and
V=\mathfrak{l}_{1}
. Then

1. the representation
G:V
is irreducible;

2. there are finitely many orbits
O_{0},\dots,O_{r}
in
V
, and
O_{i}\subset\overline{O}_{j}
if and only if
i\leq j
;

3. there are finitely many orbits
Q_{0},\dots,Q_{r}
in
V^{*}
, and
Q_{i}\subset\overline{Q}_{j}
if and only if
i\leq j
;

4.
(O_{i})^{\vee}=Q_{r-i}
in the Pyasetskii duality.

The actions arising in this way are called
Abelian
, they form a special case of a more general class of spherical representations. A finite-dimensional representation
V
of a connected reductive group
G
is called
spherical
(
multiplicity-free
) if the multiplicity of any simple
G
-module in the coordinate algebra
\mathbb{C}[V]
is at most one. As finiteness of the number of orbits is one of numerous significant properties of such representations, they possess Pyasetskii duality.

A complete classification of all spherical modules was obtained in
[
Kac
,
BeRa
,
Lea
]
. An important step of this classification is to reduce the class of representations in question. The terminology introduced below follows Knop, see
[
Kn3
, § 5]
(groups are
not
assumed to be reductive).

Consider two representations
\rho_{1}:G_{1}\to\operatorname{GL}(V_{1})
and
\rho_{2}:G_{2}\to\operatorname{GL}(V_{2})
. We say that the pairs
(G_{1},V_{1})
and
(G_{2},V_{2})
are
geometrically equivalent
if there is an isomorphism
\varphi:V_{1}\to V_{2}
identifying groups
\rho_{1}(G_{1})
and
\rho_{2}(G_{2})
. Notice that this implies geometrical equivalence of
(G_{1},V_{1}^{*})
and
(G_{2},V_{2}^{*})
. Next, a representation
(G,V)
is
decomposable
if it is geometrically equivalent to a representation of the form
(G_{1}\times G_{2},V_{1}\oplus V_{2})
with non-zero representations
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
of the corresponding groups
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
. Otherwise, it is called
indecomposable
. Finally, a representation
\rho:G\to\operatorname{GL}(V)
is called
saturated
if the dimension of the center of
\rho(G)
equals the number of irreducible summands of
V
.

Remark 1.3
.

In the case where
G
is reductive, representations
(G,V)
and
(G,V^{*})
are geometrically equivalent. Identifying the spaces of mutually dual representations one may consider the Pyasetskii duality as a pairing between
G
-orbits in
V
. We retain the notation
O^{\vee}\subset V
for the orbit dual to
O\subset V
.

The study of an arbitrary spherical representation may be reduced to that of indecomposable and saturated ones (listed in
[
Kn3
, §5]
). Further we show that the problem of describing Pyasetskii duality complies with this reduction.

Our aim is to determine pairs of dual orbits in any spherical representation. Clearly, it suffices to handle only one representation in each class of geometrical equivalence. Next, every spherical representation of a connected reductive group
G^{\prime}
is obtained by restriction from a sum of indecomposable saturated representations of a bigger group
G
normalizing
G^{\prime}
(cf.
[
Lea
]
). The following lemma shows that the construction of Pyasetskii for
(G,V)
and
(G^{\prime},V)
leads to the same duality correspondence.

Lemma 1.4
.

Let
V
be a representation of a connected group
G
, and
G^{\prime}
be its normal subgroup with finitely many orbits in
V
. Then
G
and
G^{\prime}
have the same orbits.

We provide the next simple lemma which readily implies that the problem is reduced to describing the orbit duality only for indecomposable saturated representations.

Lemma 1.5
.

Let
(G_{1},V_{1})
and
(G_{2},V_{2})
be two representations of connected groups with finite number of orbits. Then
(O_{1}\times O_{2})^{\vee}=O_{1}^{\vee}\times O_{2}^{\vee}
for any
G_{i}
-obits
O_{i}\in V_{i}
,
i=1,2
.

2.
Properties of dual orbits

Groups in this section are assumed to be connected but
not
necessarily reductive.

We begin by proving Lemmas
1.4
and
1.5
.

Proof of Lemma
1.4
.

Any
G
-orbit
O
in
V
is the union of some
G^{\prime}
-orbits. Since
G^{\prime}
a is normal subgroup,
G
acts on these
G^{\prime}
-obits by permutations. These smaller orbits are pairwise isomorphic and hence cannot be proper subvarieties in
O
since
V/G^{\prime}
is finite. The claim follows.
∎

Proof of Lemma
1.5
.

Clearly,
N^{*}(O_{1}\times O_{2})
decomposes into the direct sum of vector bundles
N^{*}O_{1}\oplus N^{*}O_{2}
. Moreover, on the level of subsets of
(V_{1}\times V_{1}^{*})\times(V_{2}\times V^{*}_{2})
the equality
N^{*}(O_{1}\times O_{2})=N^{*}O_{1}\times N^{*}O_{2}
holds. The latter subset has dense intersection with
N^{*}O_{1}^{\vee}\times N^{*}O_{2}^{\vee}=N^{*}(O_{1}^{\vee}\times O_{2}^{\vee})
since this is true for each factor.
∎

Suppose that
G:V
is a linear action with finitely many orbits, and
O\subset V
is one of them. The next lemma follows by observing that
O^{\vee}
can be identified by examining a general fiber of
N^{*}O
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Given two orbits
O\subset V
and
Q\subset V^{*}
, suppose that there exists a point
x\in O
such that the intersection
Q\cap N^{*}_{x}O
is dense in
N^{*}_{x}O
. Then
Q=O^{\vee}
.

Proof.

The intersection
Q\cap N^{*}_{x}O\subset\{x\}\times V^{*}
is a subset of
N^{*}Q
. Under the diagonal action
G:V\oplus V^{*}
it spans a subvariety of
N^{*}O\cap N^{*}Q
which is dense in
\overline{N^{*}O}
.
∎

Suppose that there is a group
H
and a homomorphism
G\to H
such that the representation of
G
is decomposed as
G\to H\to\operatorname{GL}(V)
. Clearly,
H
also has finitely many orbits, and every
H
-orbit contains a unique open
G
-orbit. It turns out that duality for
H
-orbits is related to duality for some
G
-orbits:

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
G\to H\to\operatorname{GL}(V)
be representations of
G
and
H
with finitely many orbits. If mutually dual orbits
O\subset V
and
O^{\vee}\subset V^{*}
are open in
HO
and
HO^{\vee}
respectively, then
(H\cdot O)^{\vee}=H\cdot O^{\vee}
. Conversely, if two
G
-orbits in
V
and
V^{*}
are open in mutually dual
H
-orbits, then they are dual to each other.

Proof.

First, we observe that, given an open subset
Y
in a smooth subvariety
X\subset V
, the total space
N^{*}Y
of the conormal bundle is open in
N^{*}X\subset V\oplus V^{*}
.

Now, assume that
G
-orbits
O\subset V
and
Q\subset V^{*}
are open in the corresponding
H
-orbits
HO
and
HQ
. It follows that the conormal bundles
N^{*}(HO)
and
N^{*}(HQ)
have dense intersection if and only if the same holds for
N^{*}O
and
N^{*}Q
. This implies both claims.
∎

In the case where the representation
V=V_{1}\oplus V_{2}
is reducible, the products of
G
-orbits in
V_{i}
coincide with the orbits of the group
H=G\times G
. Then, along with Lemma
1.5
, Lemma
2.1
applied to the diagonal embedding
G\to H
provides the following

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose that a group
G
acts linearly on
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
with finitely many orbits, and
O_{i}\in V_{i}/G
. Then the open orbits in the products
O_{1}\times O_{2}
and
O_{1}^{\vee}\times O_{2}^{\vee}
are dual to each other.

We conclude the section by observing that Lemmas
2.2
and
2.3
hold true under the assumptions of Remark
1.3
, where dual orbits are assumed to lie in the same space
V
.

3.
Orbits

In this section, we utilize some data listed in
[
Kn3
]
for every indecomposable saturated spherical module (namely, weights of basic semi-invariants and simple reflections generating the little Weyl group, see §
3.1
) to build an abstract orbit diagram not specifying the explicit vertex-orbit correspondence. In general, to figure out this correspondence one needs to specify basic semi-invariant functions. We develop a different approach by combining an abstract orbit diagram with an explicit description of orbits.

For every representation in Tables
1
and
its orbit decomposition is obtained in three steps: (I) Determine an abstract orbit diagram (§
3.1
); (II) Give an explicit description of the orbits (§
3.2
); (III) Combine steps I and II (§
3.3
).

3.1.
Spherical modules

We already mentioned that a spherical representation possesses only finitely many orbits. Geometry of orbits is governed by some combinatorial data. Relevant results of the theory of spherical representations are briefly formulated below. Inspection of all the cases in the tables based on these results justifies the following lemma:

Lemma 3.1
.

The diagram in the last column of
Tables

1
and
is the abstract orbit diagram of the corresponding indecomposable saturated spherical representation.

Let
G
be a connected reductive group. Fix a Borel subgroup
B\subset G
along with a maximal torus
T\subset B
. Let
V
be a finite-dimensional
G
-module. Recall that
V
is
spherical
if each simple
G
-module occurs in
\mathbb{C}[V]
with multiplicity at most one. Notice that for any
G
-submodule
V_{0}\subset V
the corresponding restriction map
\mathbb{C}[V]\to\mathbb{C}[V_{0}]
is a surjective
G
-homomorphism and hence takes isotypic components onto isotypic components. Thus we obtain

Lemma 3.2

(e.g.,
[
Lea
, Lemma 2.1]
)
.

Every simple submodule of a spherical module is spherical.

An important geometrical characterization of spherical representations is provided by

Theorem

(
[
VK
]
,
[
Kn3
, Theorem 3.1]
)
.

A
G
-module V is spherical if and only if
B
has an open orbit in
V
.

Now assume that the representation
V
is spherical. One associates with it the
weight lattice

\Lambda=\Lambda(V)
, a sublattice in the character lattice
\mathfrak{X}(B)
of
B
consisting of the characters
\lambda\in\mathfrak{X}(B)
such that the subspace

\mathbb{C}(V)^{(B)}_{\lambda}=\{f\in\mathbb{C}(V)\mid b\cdot f=\lambda(b)f,\forall b\in B\}

of
B
-semi-invariant rational functions with the weight
\lambda
is nonzero. Let us also denote the corresponding dual
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space by
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{E}(V)=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\Lambda,\mathbb{Q})
. Put

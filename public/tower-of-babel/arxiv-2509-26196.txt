Concavity of spacetimes
Tobias Beran, Darius Erös, Shin-ichi Ohta, Felix Rott

Abstract
Motivated by recent breathtaking progress in the synthetic study of Lorentzian geometry, we investigate the local concavity of time separation functions on Finsler spacetimes as a Lorentzian counterpart to Busemann’s convexity in metric geometry. We show that a Berwald spacetime is locally concave if and only if its flag curvature is nonnegative in timelike directions. We also give another characterization of nonnegative flag curvature by the convexity of future (or past) capsules, inspired by Kristály–Kozma’s result in the positive definite case. These characterizations are new even for Lorentzian manifolds. Keywords: Finsler spacetimes, flag curvature, concavity, convex capsules MSC (2020) : 53B30, 53C50, 53C60



1
Introduction

The aim of this article is to develop the synthetic study of Lorentzian geometry.
Synthetic geometry is concerned with geometric spaces without differentiable structures,
such as metric (measure) spaces.
For instance, for a complete Riemannian manifold,
every small triangle drawn with geodesics is thinner than its comparison triangle (with the same sidelengths) in
\mathbf{R}^{2}

if and only if the sectional curvature is nonpositive everywhere
(Alexandrov’s triangle comparison theorem; see, e.g.,
[
BH
, Theorem II.1A.6]
).
Then, the triangle comparison property can be adopted as a synthetic notion of nonpositive curvature;
a (geodesic) metric space
(X,d)
is called a
CAT
(0)
-space
if triangles in
X
are thinner than
\mathbf{R}^{2}
.
In the same manner, one can define metric spaces of sectional curvature bounded below
(Alexandov spaces; see
[
AKP
,
BBI
]
),
and metric measure spaces of Ricci curvature
\geq K
and dimension
\leq N

(MCP
(K,N)
-spaces (MCP stands for measure contraction property) and CD
(K,N)
-spaces (CD stands for curvature-dimension condition); see
[
LV
,
Oh1
,
St1
,
St2
]
).
These synthetic approaches enable us to understand the influence of curvature in a geometric and intuitive way,
and to investigate metric (measure) spaces arising as limit spaces of Riemannian manifolds,
leading on to fruitful applications in Riemannian geometry and beyond.

Recently, there is a growing interest in synthetic approaches to Lorentzian geometry,
partly motivated by the appearance of less regular spacetimes in general relativity
(as solutions to the Einstein equation).
For time-oriented Lorentzian manifolds (spacetimes),
suitable counterparts to the triangle comparison properties are known to characterize
lower and upper sectional curvature bounds in timelike directions (see
[
AB
,
BKOR
,
Ha
]
).
Then, as a platform for synthetic Lorentzian geometry,
Lorentzian (pre-)length spaces are introduced
[
KS
]
,
and the structure of Lorentzian (pre-)length spaces of curvature bounded below or above
in the sense of triangle comparison is the subject of current active research
(see, e.g.,
[
BORS
,
BS
]
).
We also refer to
[
CM
,
Mc
,
MS
]
for a synthetic approach to lower Ricci curvature bounds (timelike curvature-dimension condition TCD
(K,N)
),
to name a few.

For metric spaces, there is also a weaker notion of nonpositive curvature than CAT
(0)
-spaces,
going back to a seminal work of Busemann
[
Bu
]
,
called the
convexity
or the
Busemann nonpositive curvature
.
Let
(X,d)
be a
geodesic
space, i.e., any pair
x,y\in X
of points can be joined by a curve
\gamma\colon[0,1]\longrightarrow X
such that
\gamma(0)=x
,
\gamma(1)=y
and
d(\gamma(s),\gamma(t))=|t-s|d(x,y)
for all
s,t\in[0,1]

(then
\gamma
is called a
minimizing geodesic
).
We say that
(X,d)
is
locally convex
if every point
x\in X
has a neighborhood
U
such that, for any minimizing geodesics
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\colon[0,1]\longrightarrow X
included in
U
, the function
t\longmapsto d(\gamma_{1}(t),\gamma_{2}(t))
is convex.
If we can take
U=X
,
(X,d)
is said to be
globally convex
.
For Riemannian manifolds, local convexity is equivalent to having the nonpositive sectional curvature everywhere.

An important feature of Busemann’s convexity is that it does not rule out Finsler manifolds.
For example, strictly convex Banach spaces are clearly globally convex,
while only Hilbert spaces are CAT
(0)
among Banach spaces.
Therefore, it had been an intriguing problem to characterize convex spaces among Finsler manifolds.
It was first shown in
[
KVK
]
that a Berwald manifold, a special kind of Finsler manifold,
of nonpositive flag curvature is locally convex
(flag curvature in Finsler geometry corresponds to sectional curvature in Riemannian geometry).
Then,
[
KK
]
proved that a Berwald manifold is locally convex only if its flag curvature is nonpositive everywhere.
Finally, it was established in
[
IL
]
that a Finsler manifold is locally convex if and only if
it is a Berwald manifold of nonpositive flag curvature.
We refer to
[
FG
,
HY
]
for recent studies of the structure of convex metric spaces,
where we need to deal with the Finsler (non-Riemannian) nature of those spaces.

As a Lorentzian counterpart to Busemann’s convexity,
the concavity of time separation functions on Lorentzian pre-length spaces
of nonnegative curvature in the sense of triangle comparison
was proved in
[
BKR
, Section 6]
.
Moreover, a globalization result for such a concavity was established in
[
EG
]
(see
Remark

3.9
).
Then, in comparison with convexity, it is natural to ask an analog to the above characterization of convex Finsler or Berwald manifolds.
Our main theorem provides an answer to this question in the setting of Berwald spacetimes.

Theorem 1.1

(Characterizations of concavity)
.

Let
(M,L)
be a Berwald spacetime.
Then, the following are equivalent.

(I)

(M,L)
has nonnegative flag curvature
\mathbf{K}\geq 0
in timelike directions, namely
\mathbf{K}(v,w)\geq 0
for every pair of linearly independent vectors
v,w\in T_{x}M
such that
v
is future-directed timelike.

(II)

(M,L)
is locally concave.

(III)

(M,L)
is locally timelike concave.

(IV)

(M,L)
has convex future capsules.

(V)

(M,L)
has convex past capsules.

See
Definition

3.1
for the precise definition of local (timelike) concavity.
Having convex future or past capsules is a condition inspired by
[
KK
]
; see
Definition

4.1
.
Finsler spacetimes generalize usual (Lorentzian) spacetimes
in the same way that Finsler manifolds generalize Riemannian manifolds,
and the Berwald condition is defined in the same way as well.
Finsler and Berwald spacetimes have been studied from both geometric and physical viewpoints
(see Section
2
).

Theorem
1.1
is new even in the case of Lorentzian manifolds.
Then, the equivalence between local (timelike) concavity and nonnegative sectional curvature
can be regarded as a direct generalization of the aforementioned result of Busemann
[
Bu
]
.

Corollary 1.2

(Lorentzian case)
.

Let
(M,g)
be a Lorentzian spacetime.
Then, the following are equivalent.

(I)

(M,g)
has nonnegative sectional curvature in timelike directions.

(II)

(M,g)
is locally concave.

(III)

(M,g)
is locally timelike concave.

(IV)

(M,g)
has convex future capsules.

(V)

(M,g)
has convex past capsules.

Our proof of Theorem
1.1
follows the lines of
[
KK
,
KVK
]
.
Due to a common difficulty in Lorentzian geometry, the noncompactness of the indicatrix in the tangent spaces,
we could not generalize the argument in
[
IL
]
.
Thus, it remains an open question whether locally (timelike) concave Finsler spacetimes necessarily satisfy the Berwald condition
(see Subsection
5.1
for more details).

This article is organized as follows.
After reviewing the basics of Finsler spacetimes in Section
2
,
we prove the equivalence between concavity and nonnegative flag curvature in Section
3
.
We then give another characterization of nonnegative flag curvature by convex capsules in Section
4
.
Finally, Section
5
is devoted to discussing some open problems on concavity,
compared to the convexity of metric spaces.

2
Finsler spacetimes

We review the basics of Finsler spacetimes
(see
[
BEE
,
ON
]
for the standard theory of Lorentzian spacetimes).
Our definition of Finsler spacetimes follows Beem’s one
[
Be
]
(see
[
LMO1
, §2.1]
for the relationship with other definitions).
Concerning recent development,
we refer to
[
LMO1
,
LMO2
]
for singularity and comparison theorems,
[
COO
,
LMO3
]
for timelike splitting theorems, and to
[
BO
]
for the timelike curvature-dimension condition.

2.1
Lorentz–Finsler manifolds

Throughout the article, let
M
be a connected
C^{\infty}
-manifold without boundary of dimension
n\geq 2
.

Definition 2.1

(Lorentz–Finsler structures)
.

A
Lorentz–Finsler structure
on
M
is a function

L\colon TM\longrightarrow\mathbf{R}
satisfying the following conditions:

(1)

L\in C^{\infty}(TM\setminus\{0\})
;

(2)

L(cv)=c^{2}L(v)
for all
v\in TM
and
c>0
;

(3)

For any
v\in TM\setminus\{0\}
, the symmetric matrix

\bigl(g_{ij}(v)\bigr)_{i,j=1}^{n}:=\biggl(\frac{\partial^{2}L}{\partial v^{i}\partial v^{j}}(v)\biggr)_{i,j=1}^{n}

(2.1)

is non-degenerate with signature
(-,+,\ldots,+)
.

Then, we call
(M,L)
a (
C^{\infty}
-)
Lorentz–Finsler manifold
.

For each
v\in T_{x}M\setminus\{0\}
, we can define a Lorentzian metric
g_{v}
on
T_{x}M
via (
2.1
) as

g_{v}\Biggl(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}\bigg|_{x},\sum_{j=1}^{n}b_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{j}}\bigg|_{x}\Biggr):=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}g_{ij}(v)a_{i}b_{j}.

Note that we have
g_{v}(v,v)=2L(v)
by Euler’s homogeneous function theorem.

A tangent vector
v\in TM
is said to be
timelike
(resp.
null
)
if
L(v)<0
(resp.
L(v)=0
).
We say that
v
is
lightlike
if it is null and nonzero, and
causal
if it is timelike or lightlike.

Spacelike
vectors are
v\in TM
such that
L(v)>0
or
v=0
.
Denote by
\Omega^{\prime}_{x}\subset T_{x}M
the set of timelike vectors.
For causal vectors
v
, we define

F(v):=\sqrt{-2L(v)}=\sqrt{-g_{v}(v,v)}.

(2.2)

Definition 2.2

(Finsler spacetimes)
.

If a Lorentz–Finsler manifold
(M,L)
admits a smooth timelike vector field
X
,
then
(M,L)
is said to be
time-oriented
(by
X
).
A time-oriented Lorentz–Finsler manifold is called a
Finsler spacetime
.

In a Finsler spacetime time-oriented by
X
,
a causal vector
v\in T_{x}M
is said to be
future-directed

if it lies in the same connected component of
\overline{\Omega^{\prime}}\!_{x}\setminus\{0\}
as
X(x)
.
We denote by
\Omega_{x}\subset\Omega^{\prime}_{x}
the set of future-directed timelike vectors,
and define

\Omega:=\bigcup_{x\in M}\Omega_{x}.

A
C^{1}
-curve in
M
is said to be
timelike
(resp.
causal
)
if its tangent vector is always timelike (resp. causal).
Henceforth, unless explicitly stated otherwise, all causal curves are assumed to be future-directed (i.e., its tangent vector is future-directed causal).

Next, we introduce the covariant derivative.
Define

\gamma^{i}_{jk}(v):=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{l=1}^{n}g^{il}(v)\biggl(\frac{\partial g_{lk}}{\partial x^{j}}+\frac{\partial g_{jl}}{\partial x^{k}}-\frac{\partial g_{jk}}{\partial x^{l}}\biggr)(v)

for
i,j,k=1,\ldots,n
and
v\in TM\setminus\{0\}
,
where
(g^{ij}(v))
is the inverse matrix of
(g_{ij}(v))
;

G^{i}(v):=\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}\gamma^{i}_{jk}(v)v^{j}v^{k},\qquad N^{i}_{j}(v):=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial G^{i}}{\partial v^{j}}(v)

for
v\in TM\setminus\{0\}
, and
G^{i}(0)=N^{i}_{j}(0):=0
.
Moreover, we set

\Gamma^{i}_{jk}(v):=\gamma^{i}_{jk}(v)-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{l,m=1}^{n}g^{il}(v)\biggl(\frac{\partial g_{lk}}{\partial v^{m}}N^{m}_{j}+\frac{\partial g_{jl}}{\partial v^{m}}N^{m}_{k}-\frac{\partial g_{jk}}{\partial v^{m}}N^{m}_{l}\biggr)(v)

(2.3)

on
TM\setminus\{0\}
,
and the
covariant derivative
of a vector field
Y=\sum_{i=1}^{n}Y^{i}(\partial/\partial x^{i})
is defined as

D_{v}^{w}Y:=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\Biggl\{v^{j}\frac{\partial Y^{i}}{\partial x^{j}}(x)+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\Gamma^{i}_{jk}(w)v^{j}Y^{k}(x)\Biggr\}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}\bigg|_{x}

for
v\in T_{x}M
with a
reference vector

w\in T_{x}M\setminus\{0\}
.
We remark that the functions
\Gamma^{i}_{jk}
in (
2.3
) are the coefficients
of the
Chern
(–
Rund
)
connection
.

In the Lorentzian case,
g_{ij}
is constant in each tangent space (thus,
\Gamma^{i}_{jk}=\gamma^{i}_{jk}
)
and the covariant derivative does not depend on the choice of a reference vector.
In the Lorentz–Finsler setting, the following class is worth considering.

Definition 2.3

(Berwald spacetimes)
.

A Finsler spacetime
(M,L)
is said to be of
Berwald type
(or called a
Berwald spacetime
)
if
\Gamma^{i}_{jk}
is constant on the slit tangent space
T_{x}M\setminus\{0\}
for every
x
.

By definition, the covariant derivative on a Berwald spacetime is defined independently of the choice of a reference vector.
Thus, in the sequel, reference vectors will be omitted in Berwald spacetimes.
An important property of Berwald spacetimes is that,
for any
C^{1}
-curve
\eta\colon[0,1]\longrightarrow M
whose velocity does not vanish,
the parallel transport along
\eta
gives a linear isometry between
(T_{\eta(0)}M,L)
and
(T_{\eta(1)}M,L)
, i.e., for any vector field
V
along
\eta
such that
D_{\dot{\eta}}V\equiv 0
,
L(V)
is constant (see, e.g.,
[
Oh2
, Proposition 6.5]
in the positive definite case).
In particular, all tangent spaces are mutually linearly isometric.

Remark 2.4

(Metrizability)
.

In the positive definite case,
Szabó showed that a Finsler manifold of Berwald type
(M,F)
admits a Riemannian metric
h

whose Levi-Civita connection coincides with the Chern connection of
F
,
i.e., the Christoffel symbols of
h
coincide with
\Gamma^{i}_{jk}
of
F

(see
[
Sz
]
,
[
BCS
, Exercise 10.1.4]
).
This is called the (
Riemannian
)
metrizability theorem
.
It is not known whether the metrizability can be generalized to Berwald spacetimes.
In
[
FHPV
]
, some counter-examples were constructed for Lorentz–Finsler structures
defined only on a subset of
TM
.
Their discussion is not applicable to Lorentz–Finsler structures defined on the entire tangent bundle as in Definition
2.1
.

The
geodesic equation
is
D^{\dot{\eta}}_{\dot{\eta}}\dot{\eta}\equiv 0
.
Then, the
exponential map
is defined in the same way as the Riemannian (or Finsler) case.
For
C^{1}
-vector fields
V,W
along a nonconstant geodesic
\eta
, we have

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\bigl[g_{\dot{\eta}}(V,W)\bigr]=g_{\dot{\eta}}(D^{\dot{\eta}}_{\dot{\eta}}V,W)+g_{\dot{\eta}}(V,D^{\dot{\eta}}_{\dot{\eta}}W).

(2.4)

Moreover, for
C^{1}
-vector fields
V,W
along a
C^{1}
-curve
\eta
such that
V
is nowhere vanishing,

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\bigl[g_{V}(V,W)\bigr]=g_{V}(D^{V}_{\dot{\eta}}V,W)+g_{V}(V,D^{V}_{\dot{\eta}}W)

(2.5)

(see, e.g.,
[
LMO1
, (3.1), (3.2)]
,
[
Oh2
, Lemmas 4.8, 4.9]
).

A
C^{\infty}
-vector field
J
along a geodesic
\eta
is called a
Jacobi field

if it satisfies the
Jacobi equation

D^{\dot{\eta}}_{\dot{\eta}}D^{\dot{\eta}}_{\dot{\eta}}J+R_{\dot{\eta}}(J)=0
,
where

R_{v}(w):=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}R^{i}_{j}(v)w^{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}\bigg|_{x}

for
v,w\in T_{x}M
and

R^{i}_{j}(v):=\frac{\partial G^{i}}{\partial x^{j}}(v)-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\biggl\{\frac{\partial N^{i}_{j}}{\partial x^{k}}(v)v^{k}-\frac{\partial N^{i}_{j}}{\partial v^{k}}(v)G^{k}(v)+N^{i}_{k}(v)N^{k}_{j}(v)\biggr\}

is the
curvature tensor
.
A Jacobi field is also characterized as the variational vector field of a geodesic variation.
One can further expand this as
R^{i}_{j}(v)=\sum_{k,l=1}^{n}R^{i}_{ljk}(v)v^{k}v^{l}
, where

R^{i}_{ljk}:=\Biggl(\frac{\partial\Gamma^{i}_{kl}}{\partial x^{j}}-\sum_{m=1}^{n}N^{m}_{j}\frac{\partial\Gamma^{i}_{kl}}{\partial v^{m}}\Biggr)-\Biggl(\frac{\partial\Gamma^{i}_{jl}}{\partial x^{k}}-\sum_{m=1}^{n}N^{m}_{k}\frac{\partial\Gamma^{i}_{jl}}{\partial v^{m}}\Biggr)+\sum_{m=1}^{n}(\Gamma^{i}_{jm}\Gamma^{m}_{kl}-\Gamma^{i}_{km}\Gamma^{m}_{jl})

(see
[
BCS
, (3.3.2)]
,
[
Oh2
, (5.10)]
in the positive definite setting).

For
v\in\Omega_{x}
and
w\in T_{x}M
linearly independent of
v
,
define the
flag curvature
of the
2
-plane (
flag
)
v\wedge w
with
flagpole

v
as

\mathbf{K}(v,w):=\frac{g_{v}(R_{v}(w),w)}{g_{v}(v,v)g_{v}(w,w)-g_{v}(v,w)^{2}}.

Note that the denominator is negative (by the reverse Cauchy–Schwarz inequality for
g_{v}
as in (
3.3
) below).
We say that the
timelike flag curvature
is nonnegative or
\mathbf{K}\geq 0
holds in
timelike directions
if
\mathbf{K}(v,w)\geq 0
for all
v
and
w
as above.

Remark 2.5

(Sign of
\mathbf{K}
)
.

The sign of
\mathbf{K}
(in timelike directions) above is the same as
[
BEE
,
BKOR
]
and opposite to
[
LMO1
]
.
Thus, the nonnegative flag curvature
\mathbf{K}\geq 0
in timelike directions corresponds to
the nonpositive timelike curvature in the sense of
[
AB
,
BKR
,
KS
]

(cf.
[
KS
, Example 4.9]
,
[
BKOR
, Remark 2.2]
).
Note also that
\mathbf{K}\geq 0
holds in timelike directions for the de Sitter space (Lorentzian pseudo-sphere)
and product spacetimes
(\mathbf{R}\times N,-\mathrm{d}t^{2}+h)
where
(N,h)
is an Hadamard manifold.

2.2
Causality conditions and geodesics

Given
x,y\in M
, we write
x\ll y
(resp.
x<y
)
if there is a timelike (resp. causal) curve from
x
to
y
,
and
x\leq y
means that
x=y
or
x<y
.
Then, we define the
chronological past
and
future
of
x
by

I^{-}(x):=\{y\in M\,|\,y\ll x\},\qquad I^{+}(x):=\{y\in M\,|\,x\ll y\},

and the
causal past
and
future
of
x
by

J^{-}(x):=\{y\in M\,|\,y\leq x\},\qquad J^{+}(x):=\{y\in M\,|\,x\leq y\}.

Definition 2.6

(Causality conditions)
.

Let
(M,L)
be a Finsler spacetime.

(1)

(M,L)
is said to be
chronological
if
x\notin I^{+}(x)
for all
x\in M
.

(2)

We say that
(M,L)
is
causal
if there is no closed causal curve.

(3)

(M,L)
is said to be
strongly causal
if, for all
x\in M
,
every neighborhood
U
of
x
contains another neighborhood
V
of
x

such that no causal curve intersects
V
more than once.
In particular, the topology contains a basis of causally convex sets.

(4)

We say that
(M,L)
is
globally hyperbolic

if it is strongly causal and, for any
x,y\in M
,
J^{+}(x)\cap J^{-}(y)
is compact.

Define the
time separation
(also called the
Lorentz–Finsler distance
)

\tau(x,y)
for
x,y\in M
by

\tau(x,y):=\sup_{\eta}\mathbf{L}(\eta),\qquad\mathbf{L}(\eta):=\int_{0}^{1}F\bigl(\dot{\eta}(t)\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}t,

where the supremum is taken over all (piecewise
C^{1}
) causal curves
\eta\colon[0,1]\longrightarrow M
from
x
to
y
.
We set
\tau(x,y):=0
if
x\not\leq y
.
A curve
\eta
attaining the above supremum is said to be
maximizing
.
In general,
\tau
is only lower semi-continuous
and can be infinite (see, e.g.,
[
Mi2
, Proposition 6.7]
).
In globally hyperbolic Finsler spacetimes,
\tau
is finite and continuous,
and any pair of points
x,y\in M
with
x<y
admits a maximizing geodesic from
x
to
y

(Avez–Seifert-type theorem; see
[
Mi2
, Propositions 6.8, 6.9]
).

3
Concavity of Berwald spacetimes

Let
(M,L)
be a Finsler spacetime.
For each
x\in M
, there exists a
convex normal neighborhood

U\subset M
of
x
in the sense that, for every
y\in U
, the exponential map
\exp_{y}
gives a
C^{1}
-diffeomorphism from an open star-shaped set
\mathcal{U}_{y}\subset T_{x}M
onto
U
(see
[
Mi1
, Theorem 4]
).
In particular, for any
y,z\in U
, there is a unique geodesic from
y
to
z
contained in
U
.
In this and the following sections, since we shall deal only with local structures (governed by curvature), we are concerned with causal relations and the time separation restricted to
U
.
They coincide with the global ones if
(M,L)
is strongly causal (by taking
U
smaller if necessary).

The following is a Lorentzian analog to the
convexity
of metric spaces
(also called the
Busemann nonpositive curvature
; recall the introduction), inspired by
[
BKR
,
EG
]
.

Definition 3.1

(Concavity)
.

Let
(M,L)
be a Finsler spacetime.

(1)

We say that
(M,L)
is
locally timelike concave
if every
x\in M
admits a convex normal neighborhood
U

such that, for any timelike geodesics
\eta,\xi\colon[0,1]\longrightarrow U
with
\eta(t)\ll_{U}\xi(t)
or
\eta(t)=\xi(t)
at
t=0,1
, we have

\tau_{U}\bigl(\eta(t),\xi(t)\bigr)\geq(1-t)\tau_{U}\bigl(\eta(0),\xi(0)\bigr)+t\tau_{U}\bigl(\eta(1),\xi(1)\bigr)

for all
t\in[0,1]
.

(2)

We say that
(M,L)
is
locally concave
if every
x\in M
admits a convex normal neighborhood
U

such that, for any geodesics
\eta,\xi\colon[0,1]\longrightarrow U
with
\eta(t)\ll_{U}\xi(t)
or
\eta(t)=\xi(t)
at
t=0,1
, we have

\tau_{U}\bigl(\eta(t),\xi(t)\bigr)\geq(1-t)\tau_{U}\bigl(\eta(0),\xi(0)\bigr)+t\tau_{U}\bigl(\eta(1),\xi(1)\bigr)

for all
t\in[0,1]
.

Here we respectively denote by
\ll_{U}
and
\tau_{U}
the timelike relation and time separation on
(U,L|_{U})
.

In the sequel, for simplicity, we will suppress
U
in
\ll_{U}
and
\tau_{U}
.

Remark 3.2

(Causal character of geodesics)
.

Note that
\eta,\xi
in (
2
) above are not necessarily causal, and that local concavity clearly implies local timelike concavity.
It was proved in
[
BKR
, Proposition 6.1]
that a Lorentzian pre-length space of timelike nonpositive curvature in the sense of strict causal triangle comparison is locally timelike concave (recall Remark
2.5
for the sign convention in timelike curvature bounds).
In such a synthetic setting, it is natural to consider only timelike geodesics, since we do not know how to define spacelike geodesics.
Then, in
[
EG
, Definition 2.1]
, local timelike concavity as in (
1
) above is termed local concavity.
For Berwald spacetimes, we will see that local timelike concavity and local concavity are, in fact, equivalent.

To show the local concavity from
\mathbf{K}\geq 0
,
we can apply a similar calculation to the positive definite case (cf.
[
KVK
]
,
[
Oh2
, Theorem 8.30]
),
while causality needs to be addressed.
A related result in the Lorentzian case can be found in
[
BEE
, Proposition 11.15(1)]
.

Proposition 3.3

(Concave norm)
.

Let
(M,L)
be a Berwald spacetime
of nonnegative flag curvature
\mathbf{K}\geq 0
in timelike directions.
If
\sigma\colon[0,1]\times(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\longrightarrow U
is a variation in a convex normal neighborhood
U
such that
\sigma_{s}(t):=\sigma(t,s)
is a geodesic for each
s\in(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)
and that
V(t,s):=\partial_{s}\sigma(t,s)
is timelike for all
(t,s)
, then, for every
s
, the function
t\longmapsto F(V(t,s))
is concave.

Proof.

Put
T(t,s):=\partial_{t}\sigma(t,s)
.
Observe from (
2.2
) and (
2.5
) that

\frac{\partial^{2}[F^{2}(V)]}{\partial t^{2}}=-\frac{\partial^{2}[g_{V}(V,V)]}{\partial t^{2}}=-2g_{V}(V,D_{T}D_{T}V)-2g_{V}(D_{T}V,D_{T}V)

(recall that we can omit reference vectors in Berwald spacetimes).
Since
V(\cdot,s)
is a Jacobi field by the hypothesis for
\sigma
, we find

g_{V}(V,D_{T}D_{T}V)=-g_{V}\bigl(V,R_{T}(V)\bigr)=-g_{V}\Biggl(V,\sum_{i,j,k,l=1}^{n}R^{i}_{ljk}(T)V^{j}T^{k}T^{l}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}\Biggr).

(3.1)

Note that
R^{i}_{ljk}(T)=R^{i}_{ljk}(V)
by the Berwald condition, and that

\begin{split}g_{V}\Biggl(V,\sum_{i,j,k,l=1}^{n}R^{i}_{ljk}(V)V^{j}T^{k}T^{l}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}\Biggr)&=g_{V}\bigl(R_{V}(T),T\bigr)\\
&=\mathbf{K}(V,T)\bigl\{g_{V}(V,V)g_{V}(T,T)-g_{V}(V,T)^{2}\bigr\},\end{split}

(3.2)

where the first equation follows from
[
Oh2
, Lemma 5.15(iv)]
(which is valid regardless of the signature of metrics).

Now,
\mathbf{K}(V,T)\geq 0
by hypothesis and,
choosing a local chart
(x^{i})_{i=1}^{n}
which is
g_{V}
-orthonormal with
\partial/\partial x^{1}=V/F(V)
, we observe

F^{2}(V)g_{V}(T,T)+g_{V}(V,T)^{2}=V_{1}^{2}\Biggl(-T_{1}^{2}+\sum_{i=2}^{n}T_{i}^{2}\Biggr)+(V_{1}T_{1})^{2}\geq 0.

(3.3)

Hence, we obtain

\begin{split}\frac{\partial^{2}[F(V)]}{\partial t^{2}}&=\frac{1}{2F(V)}\frac{\partial^{2}[F^{2}(V)]}{\partial t^{2}}-\frac{1}{F(V)}\biggl(\frac{\partial[F(V)]}{\partial t}\biggr)^{2}\\
&\leq-\frac{g_{V}(D_{T}V,D_{T}V)}{F(V)}-\frac{1}{F(V)}\biggl(\frac{g_{V}(V,D_{T}V)}{F(V)}\biggr)^{2}\\
&=-\frac{1}{F^{3}(V)}\Bigl\{F^{2}(V)g_{V}(D_{T}V,D_{T}V)+g_{V}(V,D_{T}V)^{2}\Bigr\}.\end{split}

(3.4)

Since
F^{2}(V)g_{V}(D_{T}V,D_{T}V)+g_{V}(V,D_{T}V)^{2}\geq 0
by (
3.3
), this implies that
\partial^{2}[F(V)]/\partial t^{2}\leq 0
.
Therefore,
F(V(t,s))
is concave in
t
.
∎

Lemma 3.4

(Timelike homotopy)
.

Let
U
be a convex normal neighborhood in a Finsler spacetime
(M,L)
.
Then, for any timelike curves
\alpha,\beta\colon[0,1]\longrightarrow U

there exists a homotopy

h(0;s)=\alpha(s)

h(1;s)=\beta(s)
and

and that

and

Proof.

Put
x=\alpha(0)
,

and let
\eta\colon[0,1]\longrightarrow U
be the geodesic from
x
to
y
.
Consider a smooth timelike vector field

that exists by time orientation and,
replacing it with
cX
for small

if necessary, we assume that
\exp_{\eta(t)}(sX(\eta(t)))\in U
for all
t,s\in[0,1]
.

We first choose a homotopy

from
\alpha
to the geodesic from
x
to
\alpha(\varepsilon)
for small
\varepsilon>0
, and take
v\in\Omega_{x}
with
\alpha(\varepsilon)=\exp_{x}(v)
.
Then, set

Concatenating

h_{2}
and
h_{3}

and connecting

Lemma 3.5

(Timelike variation)
.

Let
(M,L)
be a Berwald spacetime of nonnegative flag curvature
\mathbf{K}\geq 0
in timelike directions,
and
U\subset M
be a convex normal neighborhood.
Given timelike curves

be the geodesic from
\alpha(s)
to
\beta(s)
in
U
.
Then,
\partial_{s}\sigma
is timelike and
t\longmapsto F(\partial_{s}\sigma(t,s))
is concave for each
s\in[0,1]
.

Proof.

Let
h(\lambda;s)
be a homotopy between
\alpha
and

h(0;s)=\alpha(s)
,
h(1;s)=\beta(s)
and

For each

to
\Xi(\lambda;1,s):=\beta(s)
in

.
Observe that
\Xi(0;t,s)=\sigma(t,s)
and
\Xi(1;t,s)=\beta(s)
.

Now, we set

\lambda_{0}:=\sup\bigl\{\lambda\in[0,1]\mid\partial_{s}\Xi(\lambda;t,s)\text{ is not timelike for some }t,s\in[0,1]\bigr\}.

Note that

, since

is timelike and being timelike is an open condition.
Then, for any

Taking the limit as

.
Hence,

\displaystyle F\bigl(\partial_{s}\Xi(\lambda_{0};t,s)\bigr)

\displaystyle\geq(1-t)F\bigl(\partial_{s}\Xi(\lambda_{0};0,s)\bigr)+tF\bigl(\partial_{s}\Xi(\lambda_{0};1,s)\bigr)

Theorem 3.6

(
\mathbf{K}\geq 0
implies concavity)
.

Let
(M,L)
be a Berwald spacetime of nonnegative flag curvature
\mathbf{K}\geq 0
in timelike directions.
Then,
(M,L)
is locally concave.

Proof.

Let
U\subset M
be a convex normal neighborhood.
We first consider geodesics
\eta,\xi\colon[0,1]\longrightarrow U
with
\eta(0)\ll\xi(0)
and
\eta(1)\ll\xi(1)
.
Then, take the geodesics
\alpha
from
\eta(0)
to
\xi(0)
and
\beta
from
\eta(1)
to
\xi(1)
,
and let
\sigma\colon[0,1]\times[0,1]\longrightarrow U
be the variation such that
\sigma(0,s)=\alpha(s)

\sigma(1,s)=\beta(s)

\alpha(s)
to
\beta(s)
.

Lemma 3.7

(Concavity yields concave norm)
.

Let

be a timelike Jacobi field along a timelike geodesic

If
\tau

Similarly, if
\tau

Let
\sigma\colon[0,\delta]\times(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\longrightarrow U

is a geodesic for every

,

Theorem 3.8

(Timelike concavity implies
\mathbf{K}\geq 0
)
.

Let
(M,L)
be a Berwald spacetime.
If
(M,L)
is locally timelike concave, then we have
\mathbf{K}\geq 0
in timelike directions.

Proof.

Suppose to the contrary that
g_{v}(R_{v}(w),w)>0
holds for some linearly independent timelike vectors
v,w\in\Omega_{x}
.
Let
\eta\colon[0,\delta]\longrightarrow M
be the geodesic with
\dot{\eta}(0)=w

and
J
be the Jacobi field along
\eta
with
J(0)=v
and
D_{\dot{\eta}}J(0)=0
.
Then, we have

D_{\dot{\eta}}D_{\dot{\eta}}J(0)=-R_{w}(v)

and, by (
2.5
),

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Bigl[L\bigl(J(t)\bigr)\Bigr]_{t=0}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Bigl[g_{J}\bigl(J(t),J(t)\bigr)\Bigr]_{t=0}=g_{v}\bigl(v,D_{\dot{\eta}}J(0)\bigr)=0.

Combining these and recalling
F=\sqrt{-2L}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}\Bigl[F\bigl(J(t)\bigr)\Bigr]_{t=0}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Biggl[\frac{1}{\sqrt{-2L(J(t))}}\cdot\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Bigl[L\bigl(J(t)\bigr)\Bigr]\Biggr]_{t=0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{F(v)}\cdot g_{v}\bigl(v,R_{w}(v)\bigr).

However, as we saw in (
3.1
) and (
3.2
), the Berwald condition ensures that

g_{v}\bigl(v,R_{w}(v)\bigr)=g_{v}\bigl(R_{v}(w),w\bigr)>0.

This contradicts
Lemma

3.7
, and completes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.9

(Globalization)
.

(a)

In the case of metric spaces, Busemann’s convexity is known to enjoy the
globalization
property (called the Cartan–Hadamard theorem):
If a complete, connected metric space
(X,d)
is locally convex, then its universal cover
\widetilde{X}
with its induced length metric is globally convex
(see
[
BH
, Theorem II.4.1(1)]
and the references therein).
In particular, if
(X,d)
is itself a simply connected length space, then it is globally convex.

(b)

In the Lorentzian setting, an analogous statement was proved in
[
EG
, Theorem 4.9]
:
If a globally hyperbolic, regular Lorentzian length space
X
is locally concave and future one-connected, then it is globally concave. Here, global hyperbolicity replaces the metric completeness assumption, and future one-connectedness refers to the requirement that
X
be timelike path-connected and any pair of future-directed timelike curves with common endpoints be homotopic through a family of timelike curves.
Recall from
Remark

3.2
that concavity in
[
EG
]
means timelike concavity in the sense of
Definition

3.1
.

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{r}\mathbf{K}(\dot{\eta},W)\,\mathrm{d}t.

\mathbf{K}
is continuous, letting
r\to 0
yields
\mathbf{K}(v,w)\geq 0
.

The case of convex past capsules is shown in the same way, using the geodesic
\eta\colon[-r,0]\longrightarrow U_{x}
with
\dot{\eta}(0)=v
.
Alternatively, we can reduce the past case for
L
to the future case with respect to the reverse Lorentz–Finsler structure
\overline{L}(v):=L(-v)
, for the curvature bound
\mathbf{K}\geq 0
(in timelike directions) for
L
is equivalent to that for
\overline{L}

(cf.
[
LMO1
, Remark 8.14]
,
[
Oh2
, §2.5]
).
∎

Remark 4.4

(Spacelike geodesics)
.

Note that, by construction,
\dot{\sigma}_{t}(0)=W(t)
is spacelike with respect to
g_{\dot{\eta}(t)}
.
This is the reason why we introduced not only local timelike concavity but also local concavity in
Definition

3.1
, although it is desirable to deal only with causal curves.
In fact, for spacelike geodesics, nothing can be said about their maximality or minimality;
thus, it is not known how to consider spacelike geodesics in the synthetic setting like
[
BKR
,
EG
,
KS
]
.

The proof of
Theorem

1.1
is now complete:
(
I
)
\Rightarrow
(
II
) by
Theorem

3.6
,
(
II
)
\Rightarrow
(
III
) is obvious,
(
III
)
\Rightarrow
(
I
) by
Theorem

3.8
,
(
II
)
\Rightarrow
(
IV
) and (
V
) by
Proposition

4.2
,
(
IV
) or (
V
)
\Rightarrow
(
I
) by
Proposition

4.3
.

5
Further problems

This section is devoted to discussions on some remaining open problems.

5.1
From concavity to the Berwald condition

In this subsection, we consider general (non-Berwald) Finsler spacetimes and explain difficulties in following the argument in
[
IL
]
in the positive definite case.
Notice that Lemma 3.1 and Proposition 4.1 in
[
IL
]
can be generalized as follows.

Lemma 5.1

(cf.
[
IL
, Lemma 3.1]
)
.

Let
(M,L)
be a locally concave Finsler spacetime.
Then, for every parallel vector field
V
along any nonconstant geodesic
\eta\colon[0,1]\longrightarrow M
with
V(0)\in\Omega_{\eta(0)}

(
i.e.,
D^{\dot{\eta}}_{\dot{\eta}}V\equiv 0)
,
L(V)
is constant.
In particular,
V(t)\in\Omega_{\eta(t)}
for all
t\in[0,1]
.

Proof.

Let
J
be the Jacobi field along
\eta
with
J(0)=0
and
D_{\dot{\eta}}^{\dot{\eta}}J(0)=V(0)
.
Observe that

D_{\dot{\eta}}^{\dot{\eta}}D_{\dot{\eta}}^{\dot{\eta}}J(0)=-R_{\dot{\eta}}(0)=0.

Comparing this with
D_{\dot{\eta}}^{\dot{\eta}}[tV(t)]=V(t)
and
D_{\dot{\eta}}^{\dot{\eta}}D_{\dot{\eta}}^{\dot{\eta}}[tV(t)]=0
, we find
J(t)=tV(t)+O(t^{3})
.
It follows that

F\bigl(J(t)\bigr)=tF\bigl(V(t)\bigr)+O(t^{3})\quad\text{as}\ t\to 0.

Combining this with the concavity of
t\longmapsto F(J(t))
by Lemma
3.7
, we obtain
F(J(t))\leq tF(V(0))
.
Hence, we have

F\bigl(V(t)\bigr)=\frac{1}{t}F\bigl(J(t)\bigr)+O(t^{2})\leq F\bigl(V(0)\bigr)+O(t^{2}).

This implies

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Bigl[F\bigl(V(t)\bigr)\Bigr]_{t=0}\leq 0,

and similarly
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}[F(V(t))]\leq 0
for all
t\in(0,1)
such that
V(t)
is timelike.

The same argument for the reverse Lorentz–Finsler structure
\overline{L}(v):=L(-v)

shows that
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}[F(V(t))]\geq 0
for all
t\in(0,1]
such that
V(t)
is timelike.
Indeed,
\bar{\eta}(t):=\eta(1-t)
is a geodesic for
\overline{L}
and
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}[\overline{F}(-V(t))]\geq 0
follows with

\overline{F}(-V):=\sqrt{-2\overline{L}(-V)}=F(V).

Therefore,
F(V(t))
is constant and
V(t)
is timelike for all
t\in[0,1]
.
∎

Proposition 5.2

(cf.
[
IL
, Proposition 4.1]
)
.

Let
(M,L)
be a Finsler spacetime,
and suppose that there is a Lorentzian metric
g
on
M
which is preserved
by every parallel transport along any geodesic.
Then,
g
and
L
have the same geodesics and, in particular,
L
is of Berwald type.

Proof.

In this proof, we emphasize “
g
-” for objects considered with respect to
g
; otherwise, they are with respect to
L
.

Denote by
\nabla^{g}
the
g
-covariant derivative.
Fix
x\in M
and, for
v\in T_{x}M
, we define

\kappa(v):=\nabla^{g}_{t}[\dot{\eta}_{v}](0)\in T_{x}M,

where
\eta_{v}
is the geodesic with
\dot{\eta}_{v}(0)=v
.
We shall show that
\kappa
vanishes for all
v\in T_{x}M
, namely
\eta_{v}
is also a
g
-geodesic.

Given
w\in T_{x}M
, let
W
be the parallel vector field along
\eta_{v}
such that
W(0)=w

(i.e.,
D_{\dot{\eta}_{v}}^{\dot{\eta}_{v}}W\equiv 0
).
We claim that

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\Bigl[\kappa(v+sw)\Bigr]_{s=0}=2\nabla^{g}_{t}W(0).

(5.1)

The left-hand side can be understood in terms of local coordinates as

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\Bigl[\kappa^{i}(v+sw)\Bigr]_{s=0}\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}\bigg|_{x}\in T_{x}M.

To this end, we employ a normal coordinate system with respect to
g
around
x

(see, e.g.,
[
ON
, Proposition 3.33]
).
Then, the
g
-Christoffel symbols at
x
vanish, thereby

\kappa(v)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\ddot{\eta}_{v}^{i}(0)\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}\bigg|_{x},\qquad\nabla^{g}_{t}W(0)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\dot{W}^{i}(0)\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}\bigg|_{x}.

This implies that

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\Bigl[\kappa(v+sw)\Bigr]_{s=0}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\Bigl[\ddot{\eta}^{i}_{v+sw}(0)\Bigr]_{s=0}\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}\bigg|_{x}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}\biggl[\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\Bigl[\eta^{i}_{v+sw}(t)\Bigr]_{s=0}\biggr]_{t=0}\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}\bigg|_{x}.

We set

J(t):=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\Bigl[\eta^{i}_{v+sw}(t)\Bigr]_{s=0}\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}\bigg|_{x},

which is a Jacobi field along
\eta_{v}
such that
J(0)=0
and
D^{\dot{\eta}_{v}}_{\dot{\eta}_{v}}J(0)=w
.
It follows that
J(t)=tW(t)+O(t^{3})
(as in the proof of Lemma
5.1
), and hence

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\ddot{J}^{i}(0)\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}\bigg|_{x}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}2\dot{W}^{i}(0)\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}\bigg|_{x}=2\nabla^{g}_{t}W(0).

This yields (
5.1
).

Now, for any parallel vector fields
V,W
along
\eta_{v}
, we infer from the hypothesis that

0=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Bigl[g(V,W)\Bigr]=g(\nabla^{g}_{t}V,W)+g(V,\nabla^{g}_{t}W).

(5.2)

On the one hand, plugging
V=W=\dot{\eta}_{v+sw}
into (
5.2
) at
t=0
implies
g(\kappa(v+sw),v+sw)=0
.
Combining this with (
5.1
), we find, for
W
as in (
5.1
),

0=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\Bigl[g\bigl(\kappa(v+sw),v+sw\bigr)\Bigr]_{s=0}=2g\bigl(\nabla^{g}_{t}W(0),v\bigr)+g\bigl(\kappa(v),w\bigr).

On the other hand, (
5.2
) with
V=\dot{\eta}_{v}
and
W
as in (
5.1
) at
t=0
shows

g\bigl(\kappa(v),w\bigr)+g\bigl(v,\nabla^{g}_{t}W(0)\bigr)=0.

Comparing these equations, we obtain
g(\kappa(v),w)=0
.
Since
w\in T_{x}M
was arbitrary,
\kappa(v)=0
holds.
Therefore,
g
and
L
have the same geodesics.
This implies that the Chern connection of
L
coincides with the Levi-Civita connection of
g
, thereby
L
is of Berwald type.
∎

Remark 5.3

(Canonical Lorentzian metrics)
.

In order to build a bridge between
Lemma

5.1
and
Proposition

5.2
,
in the situation of
Lemma

5.1
, we need to construct a Lorentzian metric
g

satisfying the hypothesis in
Proposition

5.2
.
In the positive definite case, it was done in
[
IL
, Proposition 4.2]
by using a Riemannian metric “canonically” associated with a Finsler metric.
Such a construction is not known in Lorentz–Finsler geometry;
this is indeed a major obstacle in the metrization problem (recall
Remark

2.4
).
It is sufficient to find a “canonical” way of choosing a finite measure on each tangent space
T_{x}M
,
as taking an average of
g_{v}
in
v\in T_{x}M
with that measure provides a Lorentzian metric
(in the positive definite case, the uniform measure on the unit ball or sphere plays a role).
However, the noncompactness of the isometry group of Minkowski metrics arises
as an essential difficulty in such a construction.

5.2
Concavity and the variance functional

In the positive definite case, there is an interesting nonsmooth characterization of Busemann’s convexity in terms of optimal transport theory.
We refer to
[
Vi
]
for the basic knowledge of optimal transport theory.

Let
(X,d)
be a complete geodesic space.
Given a Borel probability measure
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(X)
of finite second moment, its
variance
is defined by

\mathsf{var}(\mu):=\inf_{x\in X}\int_{X}d^{2}(x,y)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}y).

A point
x\in X
achieving the above infimum is called a
barycenter
(also called
center of mass
or
Fréchet mean
) of
\mu
.
If
(X,d)
is globally convex, then the set of barycenters of
\mu
is a convex set.
In the case where
(X,d)
is a complete CAT
(0)
-space, any
\mu
admits a unique barycenter.

Proposition 5.4

(Kim–Pass
[
KP
]
)
.

A complete separable geodesic space
(X,d)
is globally convex if and only if
\sqrt{\mathsf{var}}
is convex along geodesics in the
L^{2}
-Wasserstein space
(\mathcal{P}_{2}(X),W_{2})
.

Proof.

To be precise, it was shown in
[
KP
, Proposition 2.1]
that
d^{2}
is convex if and only if
\mathsf{var}
is convex.
For completeness, we give a proof along the same lines as
[
KP
]
.

In the “if” part, given minimizing geodesics
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\colon[0,1]\longrightarrow X
, with
\gamma_{1}(0)=\gamma_{2}(0)
,
consider probability measures
\mu_{t}:=(\delta_{\gamma_{1}(t)}+\delta_{\gamma_{2}(t)})/2
.
Then,
(\mu_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}
is a
W_{2}
-geodesic and

\mathsf{var}(\mu_{t})=\frac{1}{4}d^{2}\bigl(\gamma_{1}(t),\gamma_{2}(t)\bigr)

for all
t\in[0,1]
(any midpoint of
\gamma_{1}(t)
and
\gamma_{2}(t)
is a barycenter of
\mu_{t}
).
Hence, the convexity of
\sqrt{\mathsf{var}(\mu_{t})}
implies that
t\longmapsto d(\gamma_{1}(t),\gamma_{2}(t))
is convex.

For general minimizing geodesics
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\colon[0,1]\longrightarrow X
, let
\eta\colon[0,1]\longrightarrow X
be a minimizing geodesic from
\gamma_{1}(0)
to
\gamma_{2}(1)
.
Then, we deduce from the triangle inequality and the above estimate that

\displaystyle d\bigl(\gamma_{1}(t),\gamma_{2}(t)\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq d\bigl(\gamma_{1}(t),\eta(t)\bigr)+d\bigl(\eta(t),\gamma_{2}(t)\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq td\bigl(\gamma_{1}(1),\gamma_{2}(1)\bigr)+(1-t)d\bigl(\gamma_{1}(0),\gamma_{2}(0)\bigr).

Thus,
(X,d)
is globally convex.

To see the “only if” part, let
\mu_{t}=(e_{t})_{*}\Pi
be a
W_{2}
-geodesic induced from a probability measure
\Pi
on the set
\Gamma(X)
of minimizing geodesics
\eta\colon[0,1]\longrightarrow X
,
where
e_{t}(\eta):=\eta(t)
and
(e_{t})_{*}\Pi
denotes the push-forward of
\Pi
by
e_{t}

(see
[
Li
, Theorem 6]
).
For arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
, take
x_{i}\in X
such that

\biggl(\int_{X}d^{2}(x_{i},y)\,\mu_{i}(\mathrm{d}y)\biggr)^{1/2}\leq\sqrt{\mathsf{var}(\mu_{i})}+\varepsilon,\qquad i=0,1.

Let
\gamma\colon[0,1]\longrightarrow X
be the (unique) minimizing geodesic from
x_{0}
to
x_{1}
.
Then, it follows from the global convexity of
(X,d)
that

\displaystyle\sqrt{\mathsf{var}(\mu_{t})}

\displaystyle\leq\biggl(\int_{X}d^{2}\bigl(\gamma(t),y\bigr)\,\mu_{t}(\mathrm{d}y)\biggr)^{1/2}=\biggl(\int_{\Gamma(X)}d^{2}\bigl(\gamma(t),\eta(t)\bigr)\,\Pi(\mathrm{d}\eta)\biggr)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\biggl(\int_{\Gamma(X)}\Bigl((1-t)d\bigl(\gamma(0),\eta(0)\bigr)+td\bigl(\gamma(1),\eta(1)\bigr)\Bigr)^{2}\,\Pi(\mathrm{d}\eta)\biggr)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq(1-t)\biggl(\int_{\Gamma(X)}d^{2}\bigl(\gamma(0),\eta(0)\bigr)\,\Pi(\mathrm{d}\eta)\biggr)^{1/2}+t\biggl(\int_{\Gamma(X)}d^{2}\bigl(\gamma(1),\eta(1)\bigr)\,\Pi(\mathrm{d}\eta)\biggr)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=(1-t)\biggl(\int_{X}d^{2}(x_{0},y)\,\mu_{0}(\mathrm{d}y)\biggr)^{1/2}+t\biggl(\int_{X}d^{2}(x_{1},y)\,\mu_{1}(\mathrm{d}y)\biggr)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq(1-t)\sqrt{\mathsf{var}(\mu_{0})}+t\sqrt{\mathsf{var}(\mu_{1})}+\varepsilon.

Letting
\varepsilon\to 0
shows that
t\longmapsto\sqrt{\mathsf{var}(\mu_{t})}
is convex.
∎

Remark 5.5

(Variance and barycenter)
.

To formulate a Lorentzian counterpart to
Proposition

5.4
, we need an appropriate notion of variance, which is an open question.
This is related to another open question: how to define the barycenter of a probability measure in a Lorentzian or Finsler spacetime?
Even in the Minkowski space
(\mathbf{R}^{n},L)
, no reasonable characterization of the linear average
\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}x\,\mu(\mathrm{d}x)
in a synthetic/metric way seems to be known.
These notions will be helpful to develop probability theory and statistics on spacetimes.

Poincaré duality in logarithmic motivic homotopy theory
Doosung Park

Abstract
. By adapting arguments of Annala-Hoyois-Iwasa in the log setting, we prove Poincaré duality for smooth projective morphisms in logarithmic motivic homotopy theory. As an application, we show that the crystalline cohomology of a log compactification is independent of the choice.

Primary 14F42; Secondary 14A21, 14F30

1.
Introduction

Poincaré duality in motivic homotopy theory asserts that for every smooth projective (or proper) morphism of schemes
f\colon X\to S
,
the motive
M_{S}(X)
is dualizable with dual

\mathrm{Th}_{X}^{-1}(\Omega_{f}^{1}):=f_{\sharp}(\mathrm{Th}_{X}(\Omega_{f}^{1})^{-1}),

where
\mathrm{Th}_{X}(\Omega_{f}^{1})
denotes the Thom space associated with the locally free sheaf
\Omega_{f}^{1}:=\Omega_{X/S}^{1}
.
One of the main advantages of this motivic formulation is that it applies uniformly to all cohomology theories representable in the motivic homotopy category.

In the setting of
\mathrm{DM}(k)

[
15
]
over a perfect field
k
,
Friedlander-Voevodsky
[
13
, Theorem 8.2]
proved Poincaré duality assuming resolution of singularities.
Déglise
[
11
, Theorem 5.23]
removed the assumption of resolution of singularities.
Beilinson-Vologodsky
[
3
, 6.7]
extended this to smooth proper morphisms.

In the setting of
\mathrm{SH}

[
26
]
,
Voevodsky-Röndig-Ayoub
[
2
, Scholie 1.4.2]
(see also
[
10
, Theorem 2.4.28]
) proved Poincaré duality in the form

f_{\sharp}\xrightarrow{\simeq}f_{*}\Sigma^{\Omega_{f}^{1}},

where
\Sigma^{\Omega_{f}^{1}}:=\otimes_{X}\mathrm{Th}_{X}(\Omega_{f}^{1})
.
Cisinski-Déglise
[
10
, Theorem 2.4.50]
extended this to smooth proper morphisms.

Recently, Annala-Iwasa-Hoyois
[
1
, Theorem 1.1(i)]
proved Poincaré duality for their
\infty
-category of motivic spectra
\mathrm{MS}
assuming the existence of Tang’s Gysin morphisms with naturality as stated in
[
1
, Theorem 2.3]
,
which is unavailable at the time of writing.
The crucial distinction between
\mathrm{MS}
and
\mathrm{SH}
is that
\mathbb{A}^{1}
is not contractible in
\mathrm{MS}
.
Consequently, many non
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-cohomology theories are representable in
\mathrm{MS}
but not in
\mathrm{SH}
.

In contrast to
\mathrm{MS}
,
log Gysin morphisms are available in the logarithmic motivic homotopy
\infty
-category
\mathrm{logSH}
due to Binda-Park-Østvær
[
8
]
.
Using log Gysin morphisms instead,
we establish Poincaré duality for
\mathrm{logSH}
as follows.
Hence Poincaré duality applies to non
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-invariant cohomology theories that admit suitable logarithmic extensions,
e.g. Hodge cohomology, topological cyclic homology, prismatic cohomology, syntomic cohomology, etc.

Theorem A

(See Theorem
8.2
)
.

Let
f\colon X\to S
be a projective morphism of schemes.
Then

f_{\sharp}\xrightarrow{\simeq}f_{*}\Sigma^{\Omega_{f}^{1}}\colon\mathrm{logSH}(X)\to\mathrm{logSH}(S).

The global structure of the proof of Theorem
A
is analogous to the proof of
[
1
, Theorem 1.1(i)]
, but in several local arguments we proceed differently since the naturality of Tang’s Gysin morphisms as stated in
[
1
, Theorem 2.3]
is not available for log Gysin morphisms too.
For example, compare
[
1
, Proposition 4.4]
and Proposition
7.4
.
Instead, we prove a weaker naturality of log Gysin morphisms in Theorem
5.5
arguing as Déglise
[
11
, Theorem 4.32]
,
which is enough for our purposes.

We use Theorem
A
to prove that the localization property is false for closed non-open immersions of smooth schemes,
see Proposition
11.2
.

A log smooth fs log scheme can be viewed as a “smooth scheme with boundary.”
The version of Poincaré duality for manifolds with boundary, which is called Lefschetz duality,
asserts

H^{*}(M,\partial M)\simeq H_{n-*}(M)

for every
n
-dimensional orientable compact manifold
M
with boundary
\partial M
.
Inspired by these,
we establish the following motivic version of Lefschetz duality.

Theorem B

(See Theorem
8.3
)
.

Let
Z\to X
be a closed immersion of smooth projective schemes over a scheme
S
.
Then
\Sigma^{\infty}X/Z\in\mathrm{logSH}(S)
is dualizable with dual

\mathrm{Th}_{(X,Z)}^{-1}(\Omega_{(X,Z)/S}^{1}),

where
(X,Z)
is the associated log smooth fs log scheme with boundary
Z
.

This is a crucial ingredient of Theorem
9.3
,
where we prove that if Poincaré duality holds for three corners of a smooth blow-up square, then Poincaré duality holds for the remaining corner too.

Assuming the existence of Tang’s Gysin morphisms,
as a consequence of Poincaré duality for
\mathrm{MS}
,
Annala-Hoyois-Iwasa
[
1
, Proposition 6.27]
established a cohomology theory of smooth schemes over a perfect field
k
of characteristic
p>0
that agrees with the crystalline cohomology of any log compactification.
Previously, Ertl-Shiho-Sprang
[
12
]
established such a cohomology theory assuming resolution of singularities and weak factorization,
and Merici
[
16
]
established it assuming resolution of singularities and represented it in
\mathrm{DM}(k)
.
We repeat the argument of Annala-Hoyois-Iwasa to prove the following result.

Theorem C

(See Theorem
10.7
)
.

Let
k
be a perfect field of characteristic
p>0
.
Then there exists a cdh sheaf of complexes

R\Gamma_{\mathrm{crys}}^{\mathrm{lc}}

on the category of schemes over
k
such that

R\Gamma_{\mathrm{crys}}^{\mathrm{lc}}(X-\partial X)\simeq R\Gamma_{\mathrm{crys}}(X)

for every projective log smooth fs log scheme
X
over
k
.

For this,
Annala-Hoyois-Iwasa showed that the crystalline cohomology spectrum is a module over the
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-localized sphere spectrum.
This is directly shown using the
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-invariance of
K
-theory and the cyclotomic trace.
To achieve this in the log setting,
we need the log cyclotomic trace constructed in
[
21
]
and
[
22
]
,
which is a nontrivial input.

In summary,

\mathrm{logSH}
is a well-behaved
\infty
-category
equipped with log Gysin morphisms and log cyclotomic traces.
These two tools allow us to prove Poincaré duality for
\mathrm{logSH}
and the independence of the crystalline cohomology of log compactifications.

Recall from

\mathrm{div}

Let

Let

which implies

M_{Y}(W\cup\mathcal{E})\simeq 1_{Y}\simeq M_{Y}(W^{\prime}\cup\mathcal{E}(-D)).

Since each
W_{i}\cup U_{i}\to W_{i}^{\prime}
is a dividing cover,

W\cup U\to W^{\prime}
is a dividing cover.
Hence

M_{Y}(W\cup V)=M_{Y}(W\cup U\cup\mathcal{E}(-D))\simeq M_{Y}(W^{\prime}\cup\mathcal{E}(-D))\simeq 1_{Y}.

By Zariski descent,
we have

M_{Y}(W\cup V/V)\simeq M_{Y}(W/W\cap V)=M_{Y}(W/W\cap\mathcal{E})\simeq M_{Y}(W\cup\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{E}),

where the middle equality comes from
W\cap V=W\cap\mathcal{E}
.
Since
M_{Y}(W\cup V)
and
M_{Y}(W\cup\mathcal{E})
are contractible,
we have
M_{Y}(V)\simeq M_{Y}(\mathcal{E})
.
∎

4.
Log Gysin sequence

We keep working with
\mathscr{T}
in §

.
Throughout this section,
let

,

.
The purpose of this section is to upgrade the log Gysin sequence in

such that we allow a log structure on the base

We set

Theorem 4.1
.

Let

,
and let

Then the induced morphisms

(4.1)

Proof.

Let us use the argument in
[
19
, Theorem 3.4.1]
as follows.
We can work Zariski locally on

so we may assume the existence of
S^{\prime}
in Proposition

Replace
S
by
S^{\prime}
to reduce to the case where
Z
and
X
are strict smooth over
S
.
By considering the functor

for the morphism

removing the log structure,
We further reduce to the case of
S\in\mathrm{Sch}
.
Then
[
7
, Theorem 7.5.4]
is precisely what we need.

Such a technique of upgrading a statement with a base scheme to a statement with a base fs log scheme will be repeatedly used later.

.

11.
Failure of the localization property

We keep working with
\mathscr{T}
in §
2
.
It is not the case that the localization property for
\mathrm{logSH}
always holds.
If it always holds,
then the functor
i^{*}
is an equivalence for every nil-immersion
i
since the open complement of
i
is empty.
However,
there are lots of non
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-invariant cohomology theories representable in
\mathrm{logSH}
, e.g. Hodge cohomology,
which are not nil-invariant.
In this section,
we show that the localization property also fails for closed non-open immersions of smooth schemes.

Throughout this section,
let

,
let

be a closed immersion in

let

be the open complement of

let

be the canonical morphism,
and let

be the structural morphism.

Proposition 11.1
.

There exists a natural fiber sequence

(fu)_{\sharp}u^{*}\xrightarrow{\mathrm{ad}^{\prime}}f_{\sharp}\xrightarrow{\mathrm{ad}}f_{\sharp}i_{*}i^{*}.

Proof.

We have a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p_{\sharp}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p_{\sharp}i_{*}i^{*}}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Gys}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{ad}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Gys}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{ad}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{id}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{ad}^{\prime}}

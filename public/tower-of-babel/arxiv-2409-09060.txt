Noncommutative Donoho-Elad-Gribonval-Nielsen-Fuchs Sparsity Theorem
K. Mahesh Krishna

Abstract




Abstract
: Breakthrough Sparsity Theorem, derived independently by Donoho and Elad
[Proc. Natl. Acad. Sci. USA, 2003]
, Gribonval and Nielsen
[IEEE Trans. Inform. Theory, 2003]
and Fuchs
[IEEE Trans. Inform. Theory, 2004]
says that unique sparse solution to NP-Hard
\ell_{0}
-minimization problem can be obtained using unique solution of P-Type
\ell_{1}
-minimization problem. In this paper, we derive noncommutative version of their result using frames for Hilbert C*-modules.

Keywords
: Sparse solution, Frame, Hilbert C*-module.

Mathematics Subject Classification (2020)
: 42C15, 46L08.

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a finite dimensional Hilbert space over
\mathbb{K}
(
\mathbb{C}
or
\mathbb{R}
). A finite collection
\{\tau_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
in
\mathcal{H}
is said to be a
frame
(also known as
dictionary
)
[
2
,
15
]
for
\mathcal{H}
if there are
a,b>0
such that

\displaystyle a\|h\|^{2}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}|\langle h,\tau_{j}\rangle|^{2}\leq b\|h\|^{2},\quad\forall h\in\mathcal{H}.

A frame
\{\tau_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
for
\mathcal{H}
is said to be
normalized
if
\|\tau_{j}\|=1
for all
1\leq j\leq n
. Note that any frame can be normalized by dividing each element by its norm. Given a frame
\{\tau_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
for
\mathcal{H}
, we define the analysis operator

\displaystyle\theta_{\tau}:\mathcal{H}\ni h\mapsto\theta_{\tau}h\coloneqq(\langle h,\tau_{j}\rangle)_{j=1}^{n}\in\mathbb{K}^{n}.

Adjoint of the analysis operator is known as the synthesis operator whose expression is

\displaystyle\theta_{\tau}^{*}:\mathbb{K}^{n}\ni(a_{j})_{j=1}^{n}\mapsto\theta_{\tau}^{*}(a_{j})_{j=1}^{n}\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}\tau_{j}\in\mathcal{H}.

Given
d\in\mathbb{K}^{n}
, let
\|d\|_{0}
be the number of nonzero entries in
d
. Following
\ell_{0}
-minimization problem appears in many of electronic devices.

Problem 1.1
.

Let
\{\tau_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
be a normalized frame for
\mathcal{H}
. Given
h\in\mathcal{H}
, solve

\displaystyle\text{minimize }\{\|d\|_{0}:d\in\mathbb{K}^{n}\}\quad\text{ subject to }\quad\theta_{\tau}^{*}d=h.

Recall that
c\in\mathbb{K}^{n}
is said to be a unique solution to Problem
1.1
if it satisfies following two conditions.

(i)

\theta_{\tau}^{*}c=h
.

(ii)

If
d\in\mathbb{K}^{n}
satisfies
\theta_{\tau}^{*}d=h
, then

\displaystyle\|d\|_{0}>\|c\|_{0}.

In 1995, Natarajan showed that Problem
1.1
is NP-Hard
[
18
,
9
]
.
As the operator
\theta_{\tau}^{*}
is surjective, for a given
h\in\mathcal{H}
, there is a
d\in\mathbb{K}^{n}
such that
\theta_{\tau}^{*}d=h.
Thus the central problem is to say when the solution to Problem
1.1
is unique. It is well-known that
[
4
,
7
,
3
]
following problem is the closest convex relaxation problem to Problem
1.1
.

Problem 1.2
.

Let
\{\tau_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
be a normalized frame for
\mathcal{H}
. Given
h\in\mathcal{H}
, solve

\displaystyle\text{minimize }\{\|d\|_{1}:d\in\mathbb{K}^{n}\}\quad\text{ subject to }\quad\theta_{\tau}^{*}d=h.

There are several linear programmings available to obtain solution of Problem
1.2
and it is a P-problem
[
24
,
22
,
23
]
.

Most important result which shows that by solving Problem
1.2
we also get a solution to Problem
1.1
is obtained independently by Donoho and Elad
[
6
]
, Gribonval and Nielsen
[
13
]
and Fuchs
[
12
,
11
]
is the following.

Theorem 1.3
.

[
6
,
13
,
17
,
8
,
11
,
12
]
(
Donoho-Elad-Gribonval-Nielsen-Fuchs Sparsity Theorem
)
Let
\{\tau_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
be a normalized frame for
\mathcal{H}
. If

h\in\mathcal{H}
can be written as
h=\theta_{\tau}^{*}c
for some
c\in\mathbb{K}^{n}
satisfying

\displaystyle\|c\|_{0}<\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\frac{1}{\displaystyle\max_{1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k}|\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle|}\right),

then
c
is the unique solution to Problem
1.2
and Problem
1.1
.

Our fundamental motivation comes from the following question: What is the noncommutative analogue of Theorem
1.3
? This is then naturally connected with the notion of Hilbert C*-modules which are first introduced by Kaplansky
[
16
]
for modules over commutative C*-algebras and later developed for modules over arbitrary C*-algebras by Paschke
[
19
]
and Rieffel
[
21
]
. We end the introduction by recalling the definition of Hilbert C*-modules.

Definition 1.4
.

[
16
,
19
,
21
]

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a unital C*-algebra. A left module
\mathcal{E}
over
\mathcal{A}
is said to be a (left) Hilbert C*-module if there exists a map
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:\mathcal{E}\times\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{A}
such that the following hold.

(i)

\langle x,x\rangle\geq 0
,
\forall x\in\mathcal{E}
. If
x\in\mathcal{E}
satisfies
\langle x,x\rangle=0
, then
x=0
.

(ii)

\langle x+y,z\rangle=\langle x,z\rangle+\langle y,z\rangle
,
\forall x,y,z\in\mathcal{E}
.

(iii)

\langle ax,y\rangle=a\langle x,y\rangle
,
\forall x,y\in\mathcal{E}
,
\forall a\in\mathcal{A}
.

(iv)

\langle x,y\rangle=\langle y,x\rangle^{*}
,
\forall x,y\in\mathcal{E}
.

(v)

\mathcal{E}
is complete w.r.t. the norm
\|x\|\coloneqq\sqrt{\|\langle x,x\rangle\|}
,
\forall x\in\mathcal{E}
.

2.
Noncommutative Donoho-Elad-Gribonval-Nielsen-Fuchs Sparsity Theorem

Observe that the notion of frames is needed for Theorem
1.3
. Thus we want noncommutative frames. These are introduced in 2002 by Frank and Larson in their seminal paper
[
10
]
. We begin by recalling the definition of noncommutative frames for Hilbert C*-modules. This notion is already well-developed in parallel with Hilbert space frame theory
[
20
,
1
,
14
]
. In the paper, we consider only finite rank modules.

Definition 2.1
.

[
10
]

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a Hilbert C*-module over a unital C*-algebra
\mathcal{A}
. A collection
\{\tau_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
in
\mathcal{E}
is said to be a (modular)
frame
for
\mathcal{E}
if there are real
a,b>0
such that

\displaystyle a\langle x,x\rangle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}\langle x,\tau_{j}\rangle\langle\tau_{j},x\rangle\leq b\langle x,x\rangle,\quad\forall x\in\mathcal{E}.

A collection
\{\tau_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
in a Hilbert C*-module
\mathcal{E}
over unital C*-algebra
\mathcal{A}
with identity
1
is said to have
unit inner product
if

\displaystyle\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{j}\rangle=1,\quad\forall 1\leq j\leq n.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a unital C*-algebra. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\mathcal{A}^{n}
be the standard left Hilbert C*-module over
\mathcal{A}
with inner product

\displaystyle\langle(a_{j})_{j=1}^{n},(b_{j})_{j=1}^{n}\rangle\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}b_{j}^{*},\quad\forall(a_{j})_{j=1}^{n},(b_{j})_{j=1}^{n}\in\mathcal{A}^{n}.

Hence norm on
\mathcal{A}^{n}
is

\displaystyle\|(a_{j})_{j=1}^{n}\|_{2}\coloneqq\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}a_{j}^{*}\right\|^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad\forall(a_{j})_{j=1}^{n}\in\mathcal{A}^{n}.

We define

\displaystyle\|(a_{j})_{j=1}^{n}\|_{1}\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|a_{j}\|,\quad\forall(a_{j})_{j=1}^{n}\in\mathcal{A}^{n}.

A frame
\{\tau_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
for
\mathcal{E}
gives the modular analysis morphism

\displaystyle\theta_{\tau}:\mathcal{E}\ni x\mapsto\theta_{\tau}x\coloneqq(\langle x,\tau_{j}\rangle)_{j=1}^{n}\in\mathcal{A}^{n}

and the modular synthesis morphism

\displaystyle\theta_{\tau}^{*}:\mathcal{A}^{n}\ni(a_{j})_{j=1}^{n}\mapsto\theta_{\tau}^{*}(a_{j})_{j=1}^{n}\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}\tau_{j}\in\mathcal{E}.

With these notions, we generalize Problems
1.1
and
1.2
. In the entire paper,
\mathcal{E}
denotes a finite rank Hilbert C*-module over a unital C*-algebra
\mathcal{A}
.

Problem 2.2
.

Let
\{\tau_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
be a unit inner product frame for
\mathcal{E}
. Given
x\in\mathcal{E}
, solve

\displaystyle\text{minimize }\{\|d\|_{0}:d\in\mathcal{A}^{n}\}\quad\text{ subject to }\quad\theta_{\tau}^{*}d=x.

Problem 2.3
.

Let
\{\tau_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
be a unit inner product frame for
\mathcal{E}
. Given
x\in\mathcal{E}
, solve

\displaystyle\text{minimize }\{\|d\|_{1}:d\in\mathcal{A}^{n}\}\quad\text{ subject to }\quad\theta_{\tau}^{*}d=x.

A very powerful property used to show Theorem
1.3
is the notion of null space property (see
[
17
,
5
]
). We now define the same property for Hilbert C*-modules. We use following notations. Let
\{e_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
be the canonical basis for
\mathcal{A}^{n}
. Given
M\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}
and
d=(d_{j})_{j=1}^{n}\in\mathcal{A}^{n}
, define

\displaystyle d_{M}\coloneqq\sum_{j\in M}d_{j}e_{j}.

Definition 2.4
.

A unit inner product frame
\{\tau_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
for
\mathcal{E}
is said to have the (modular)
null space property
(we write NSP) of order
k\in\{1,\dots,n\}
if for every
M\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}
with
o(M)\leq k
, we have

\displaystyle\|d_{M}\|_{1}<\frac{1}{2}\|d\|_{1},\quad\forall d\in\ker(\theta_{\tau}^{*}),d\neq 0.

We first relate NSP with Problem
2.3
.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
\{\tau_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
be a unit inner product frame for
\mathcal{E}
and let
1\leq k\leq n
. The following are equivalent.

(i)

If
x\in\mathcal{E}
can be written as
x=\theta_{\tau}^{*}c
for some
c\in\mathcal{A}^{n}
satisfying
\|c\|_{0}\leq k
, then
c
is the unique solution to Problem
2.3
.

(ii)

\{\tau_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
satisfies the NSP of order
k
.

Proof.

(i)

\implies
(ii) Let
M\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}
with
o(M)\leq k
and let
d\in\ker(\theta_{\tau}^{*}),d\neq 0
. Then we have

\displaystyle 0=\theta_{\tau}^{*}d=\theta_{\tau}^{*}(d_{M}+d_{M^{c}})=\theta_{\tau}^{*}(d_{M})+\theta_{\tau}^{*}(d_{M^{c}})

which gives

\displaystyle\theta_{\tau}^{*}(d_{M})=\theta_{\tau}^{*}(-d_{M^{c}}).

Define
c\coloneqq d_{M}\in\mathcal{A}^{n}
and
x\coloneqq\theta_{\tau}^{*}(d_{M})
. Then we have
\|c\|_{0}\leq o(M)\leq k
and

\displaystyle x=\theta_{\tau}^{*}c=\theta_{\tau}^{*}(-d_{M^{c}}).

By assumption (i), we then have

\displaystyle\|c\|_{1}=\|d_{M}\|_{1}<\|-d_{M^{c}}\|_{1}=\|d_{M^{c}}\|_{1}.

Rewriting previous inequality gives

\displaystyle\|d_{M}\|_{1}<\|d\|_{1}-\|d_{M}\|_{1}\implies\|d_{M}\|_{1}<\frac{1}{2}\|d\|_{1}.

Hence
\{\tau_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
satisfies the NSP of order
k
.

(ii)

\implies
(i) Let
x\in\mathcal{E}
can be written as
x=\theta_{\tau}^{*}c
for some
c\in\mathcal{A}^{n}
satisfying
\|c\|_{0}\leq k
. Define
M\coloneqq\operatorname{supp}(c)
. Then
o(M)=\|c\|_{0}\leq k
. By assumption (ii), we then have

(1)

\displaystyle\|d_{M}\|_{1}<\frac{1}{2}\|d\|_{1},\quad\forall d\in\ker(\theta_{\tau}^{*}),d\neq 0.

Let
b\in\mathcal{A}^{n}
be such that
x=\theta_{\tau}^{*}b
and
b\neq c
. Define
a\coloneqq b-c\in\mathcal{A}^{n}
. Then
\theta_{\tau}^{*}a=\theta_{\tau}^{*}b-\theta_{\tau}^{*}c=x-x=0
and hence
a\in\ker(\theta_{\tau}^{*}),a\neq 0
. Using Inequality (
1
), we get

\displaystyle\|a_{M}\|_{1}<\frac{1}{2}\|a\|_{1}\implies\|a_{M}\|_{1}<\frac{1}{2}(\|a_{M}\|_{1}+\|a_{M^{c}}\|_{1})

(2)

\displaystyle\implies\|a_{M}\|_{1}<\|a_{M^{c}}\|_{1}.

Using Inequality (
2
) and the information that
c
is supported on
M
, we get

\displaystyle\|b\|_{1}-\|c\|_{1}

\displaystyle=\|b_{M}\|_{1}+\|b_{M^{c}}\|_{1}-\|c_{M}\|_{1}-\|c_{M^{c}}\|_{1}

\displaystyle=\|b_{M}\|_{1}+\|b_{M^{c}}\|_{1}-\|c_{M}\|_{1}=\|b_{M}\|_{1}+\|(b-c)_{M^{c}}\|_{1}-\|c_{M}\|_{1}

\displaystyle=\|b_{M}\|_{1}+\|a_{M^{c}}\|_{1}-\|c_{M}\|_{1}>\|b_{M}\|_{1}+\|a_{M}\|_{1}-\|c_{M}\|_{1}

\displaystyle\geq\|b_{M}\|_{1}+\|(b-c)_{M}\|_{1}-\|c_{M}\|_{1}\geq\|b_{M}\|_{1}-\|b_{M}\|_{1}+\|c_{M}\|_{1}-\|c_{M}\|_{1}=0.

Hence
c
is the unique solution to Problem
2.3
.

∎

Using Theorem
2.5
we obtain modular version of Theorem
1.3
.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
\{\tau_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
be a unit inner product frame for
\mathcal{E}
. If
x\in\mathcal{E}
can be written as
x=\theta_{\tau}^{*}c
for some
c\in\mathcal{A}^{n}
satisfying

(3)

\displaystyle\|c\|_{0}<\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\frac{1}{\displaystyle\max_{1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k}\|\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle\|}\right),

then
c
is the unique solution to Problem
2.3
.

Proof.

We show that
\{\tau_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
satisfies the NSP of order
k\coloneqq\|c\|_{0}
. Then Theorem
2.5
says that
c
is the unique solution to Problem
2.3
. Let
x\in\mathcal{E}
can be written as
x=\theta_{\tau}^{*}c
for some
c\in\mathcal{A}^{n}
satisfying
\|c\|_{0}\leq k
. Let
M\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}
with
o(M)\leq k
and let
d=(d_{j})_{j=1}^{n}\in\ker(\theta_{\tau}^{*}),d\neq 0
. Then we have

\displaystyle\theta_{\tau}\theta_{\tau}^{*}d=0.

For each fixed
1\leq k\leq n
, above equation gives

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\langle\theta_{\tau}\theta_{\tau}^{*}(d_{j})_{j=1}^{n},e_{k}\rangle=\langle\theta_{\tau}^{*}(d_{j})_{j=1}^{n},\theta_{\tau}^{*}e_{k}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\theta_{\tau}^{*}(d_{j})_{j=1}^{n},\tau_{k}\rangle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}d_{j}\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle

\displaystyle=d_{k}\langle\tau_{k},\tau_{k}\rangle+\sum_{j=1,j\neq k}^{n}d_{j}\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle=d_{k}+\sum_{j=1,j\neq k}^{n}d_{j}\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle.

Therefore

\displaystyle d_{k}=-\sum_{j=1,j\neq k}^{n}d_{j}\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle,\quad\forall 1\leq k\leq n.

By taking norm,

\displaystyle\|d_{k}\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum_{j=1,j\neq k}^{n}d_{j}\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle\right\|\leq\sum_{j=1,j\neq k}^{n}\|d_{j}\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle\|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1,j\neq k}^{n}\|d_{j}\|\|\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle\|\leq\left(\displaystyle\max_{1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k}\|\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle\|\right)\sum_{j=1,j\neq k}^{n}\|d_{j}\|

\displaystyle=\left(\displaystyle\max_{1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k}\|\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle\|\right)\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|d_{j}\|-\|d_{k}\|\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\displaystyle\max_{1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k}\|\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle\|\right)\left(\|d\|_{1}-\|d_{k}\|\right),\quad\quad\forall 1\leq k\leq n.

By rewriting above inequality we get

(4)

\displaystyle\left(1+\frac{1}{\displaystyle\max_{1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k}\|\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle\|}\right)\|d_{k}\|\leq\|d\|_{1},\quad\forall 1\leq k\leq n.

Summing Inequality (
4
) over
M
leads to

\displaystyle\left(1+\frac{1}{\displaystyle\max_{1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k}\|\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle\|}\right)\|d_{M}\|_{1}

\displaystyle=\left(1+\frac{1}{\displaystyle\max_{1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k}\|\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle\|}\right)\sum_{k\in M}\|d_{k}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|d\|_{1}\sum_{k\in M}1=\|d\|_{1}o(M).

Finally using Inequality (
3
)

\displaystyle\|d_{M}\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\frac{1}{\displaystyle\max_{1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k}\|\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle\|}\right)^{-1}\|d\|_{1}o(M)

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\frac{1}{\displaystyle\max_{1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k}\|\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle\|}\right)^{-1}\|d\|_{1}k

\displaystyle=\left(1+\frac{1}{\displaystyle\max_{1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k}\|\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle\|}\right)^{-1}\|d\|_{1}\|c\|_{0}

\displaystyle<\frac{1}{2}\|d\|_{1}.

Hence
\{\tau_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
satisfies the NSP of order
k
.
∎

Theorem 2.7
.

(
Noncommutative Donoho-Elad-Gribonval-Nielsen-Fuchs Sparsity Theorem
)
Let
\{\tau_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
be a unit inner product frame for
\mathcal{E}
.
If
x\in\mathcal{E}
can be written as
x=\theta_{\tau}^{*}c
for some
c\in\mathcal{A}^{n}
satisfying

\displaystyle\|c\|_{0}<\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\frac{1}{\displaystyle\max_{1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k}\|\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle\|}\right),

then
c
is the unique solution to Problem
2.2
.

Proof.

Theorem
2.6
says that
c
is the unique solution to Problem
2.3
. Let
d\in\mathcal{A}^{n}
be such that
x=\theta_{\tau}^{*}d
. We claim that
\|d\|_{0}>\|c\|_{0}
. If this fails, we must have
\|d\|_{0}\leq\|c\|_{0}
. We then have

\displaystyle\|d\|_{0}<\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\frac{1}{\displaystyle\max_{1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k}\|\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle\|}\right).

Theorem
2.6
again says that
d
is also the unique solution to Problem
2.3
. Therefore we must have
\|c\|_{1}<\|d\|_{1}
and
\|c\|_{1}>\|d\|_{1}
which is a contradiction. So claim holds and we have
\|d\|_{0}>\|c\|_{0}
.
∎

Intermediate dimensions of measures: Interpolating between Hausdorff and Minkowski dimensions
Nicolas E. Angelini, Ursula M. Molter, Jose M. Tejada

Abstract
. In this paper, we define a family of dimensions for Borel measures that lie between the Hausdorff and Minkowski dimensions for measures, analogous to the intermediate dimensions of sets. Previously, Hare et. al. in [ 10 ] defined intermediate dimensions that interpolate between the Minkowski and Assouad dimensions for measures. Additionally, Fraser, in [ 7 ] introduced intermediate dimensions that interpolate between the Fourier and Hausdorff dimensions of measures. Our results address a ”gap” in the study of dimension interpolation for measures, almost completing the spectrum of intermediate dimensions for measures: from Fourier to Assouad dimensions. Furthermore, Theorem 3.11 can be interpreted as a ”reverse Frostman” lemma for intermediate dimensions. We also obtain a capacity-theoretic definition that enables us to estimate the intermediate dimensions of pushforward measures by projections.

: primary: 28A80; secondary: 28A78.

Key words and phrases
: Hausdorff dimension, Minkowski dimension, intermediate dimensions, dimension interpolation.

1.
Introduction

In the last few years, dimension interpolation techniques have been developed with the goal of understanding deeper relations between pairs of dimensions. These ideas were developed for sets in
[
5
]
,
[
2
]
, and
[
9
]
, among others. However, for the classic set dimensions that these papers study there exist also the notions of dimensions for measures.
Because of this, it is natural to think about defining intermediate dimensions for measures.

Interpolation between different pairs of dimensions for measures can be found in

[
7
]
(between the Fourier and Hausdorff dimensions) and
[
10
]
(between the Minkowski and Assouad dimensions).

In our case, we will interpolate between the upper Hausdorff and the Minkowski dimension of measures (for precise definitions of these dimensions see section
2
). Given that our dimensions lie between the Upper Hausdorff and Minkowski dimensions, our notion almost fills the ’gap’ that these other families of dimensions do not cover.

Interestingly, many theorems for sets can be rewritten for measures, and as we will see in this paper, their set version can sometimes be deduced directly from the corresponding measure version, using a result similar to Theorem 2.1 from
[
4
]
.

Our main theorem can also be seen as a sort of ’inverse Frostman lemma’ for intermediate dimensions.

Let
\dim_{H}(\mu)
and
\leq\overline{\dim}_{B}(\mu)
denote the Hausdorff and Box dimensions, respectively, where
\overline{\dim}_{B}(\mu)
is defined in
[
4
]
. The definition of intermediate dimensions that we give is based on four properties that we would like them to satisfy.

(1)

\overline{\dim}_{\theta}(\mu)\leq\overline{\dim}_{\phi}(\mu)
for all
0\leq\theta\leq\phi\leq 1
.

(2)

\overline{\dim}_{\theta}(\mu)\leq\overline{\dim}_{B}(\mu)
.

(3)

\dim_{H}(\mu)\leq\overline{\dim}_{\theta}(\mu)
.

(4)

For every compact set
E
,

\overline{\dim}_{\theta}(E)=\min\{\overline{\dim}_{\theta}(\mu):\mu\text{ finite and }\operatorname{supp}\left(\mu\right)=E\}
.

Finally, in Section
5
, we provide an alternative definition of the intermediate dimension of a measure in terms of capacities associated with certain kernels. Building on this, we show that the intermediate dimensions of the image of a measure
\mu
under the orthogonal projection onto almost all
m
-dimensional subspaces depend only on
m
and
\mu
. In other words, they are almost surely independent of the choice of subspace.

2.
Definitions

Unless specified, the measures that we study will always be finite, Borel regular (in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for some
n
) with compact support. However by Remark
2.4
, requiring compact support is not really a restriction. By
B(x,r)
we mean the closed balls in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and by
\mathbbm{1}_{A}
, the indicator function of
A
.

Before defining the intermediate dimensions, we recall some preliminary concepts.

Definition 2.1
.

the
upper and lower local dimensions of
\mu
at
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
are defined as:

\overline{\dim}_{\text{loc}}\mu(x):=\limsup_{r\to 0}\frac{\log\mu(B(x,r))}{\log r}\>\>\>\>,\>\>\>\>\underline{\dim}_{\text{loc}}\mu(x):=\liminf_{r\to 0}\frac{\log\mu(B(x,r))}{\log r}.

In case they are equal, their common value is written as
\dim_{\text{loc}}\mu(x)
.

It is easy to see that for
x\notin\operatorname{supp}\left(\mu\right)
,
\dim_{\text{loc}}\mu(x)=\infty
, and if
x
is an atom of
\mu
, then
\dim_{\text{loc}}\mu(x)=0
. More information about these two dimensions can be found in
[
6
]
.

Definition 2.2
.

The
upper Hausdorff dimension
of
\mu
is defined by:

\displaystyle\dim_{H}^{*}(\mu)

\displaystyle=\inf\{s:\underline{\dim}_{\text{loc}}\mu(x)\leq s\>\forall\>\mu-{\rm a.e.}x\}.

\displaystyle=\inf\{\dim_{H}(E):E\text{ Borel }/\>\mu(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus E)=0\},

and the
Hausdorff dimension
of
\mu
is defined by:

\displaystyle\dim_{H}(\mu)

\displaystyle=\sup\{s:\underline{\dim}_{\text{loc}}\mu(x)\geq s\>\forall\>\mu-a.e.x\}.

\displaystyle=\inf\{\dim_{H}(E):E\text{ Borel }/\>\mu(E)>0\}.

Note that,
\dim_{H}(\mu)\leq\dim_{H}^{*}(\mu)
and the inequality can be strict (see
[
6
, Prop. 10.12]
).

Definition 2.3
.

Following
[
4
]
, we define the
Upper and lower Minkowski dimensions
for measures by:

\overline{\dim}_{B}(\mu):=\inf\Set{s\geq 0:\begin{array}[]{c}\>\exists\>c>0\text{ such that, for all }\>x\in\operatorname{supp}\left(\mu\right)\text{ and }r\in(0,1):\\[2.84526pt]
\>\mu(B(x,r))\geq c\cdot r^{s}\end{array}}
.

\underline{\dim}_{B}(\mu):=\inf\Set{s\geq 0:\begin{array}[]{c}\>\exists c>0\text{ and }(r_{n})_{\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\text{ with }r_{n}\to 0,\text{ such that: }\\[2.84526pt]
\>\mu(B(x,r_{n}))\geq c\cdot r_{n}^{s}\>\forall x\in\operatorname{supp}\left(\mu\right)\text{ and }n\in\mathbb{N}\end{array}}
.

Remark 2.4
.

For measures with unbounded support,
\overline{\dim}_{B}(\mu)=\underline{\dim}_{B}(\mu)=\infty
, because one can take some fixed
r>0
, and a sequence
x_{n}\rightarrow\infty
, such that
B(x_{n},r)
has positive measure, and
B(x_{m},r)\cap B(x_{n},r)=\emptyset
. As the measure is finite, the measures of such balls must decrease to
0
, and so no
s>0
would satisfy the desired inequalities in the previous definition. Consequently, one does not lose much considering only compactly supported measures.

In order to motivate our definitions, the following observation can be established first:

Lemma 2.5
.

For every
\mu
,

\overline{\dim}_{B}(\mu)=\inf\{s\geq 0:\exists c>0\text{ and }\delta_{0}>0/\mu(B(x,\delta))\geq c\cdot\delta^{s}\>\forall x\in\operatorname{supp}\left(\mu\right)\text{ and }\delta<\delta_{0}\}.

Proof.

Clearly,

\overline{\dim}_{B}(\mu)\geq\inf\{s\geq 0:\exists c>0\text{ and }\delta_{0}>0/\mu(B(x,\delta))\geq c\cdot\delta^{s}\>\forall x\in\operatorname{supp}\left(\mu\right)\text{ and }\delta<\delta_{0}\}.

To prove the non-trivial inequality, suppose that
s
satisfies the right hand side of the equality. Let
r\in(\delta_{0},1)
and
x\in\operatorname{supp}\left(\mu\right)
. Then,

\delta_{0}^{s}\cdot c\cdot r^{s}\leq\delta_{0}^{s}\cdot c\leq\mu(B(x,\delta_{0}))\leq\mu(B(x,r)).

Because of this, choosing
\tilde{c}=\min(c,c\delta_{0}^{s})
we have
s\geq\overline{\dim}_{B}(\mu)
.
∎

Before proceeding with intermediate dimensions, we need one final lemma, which will be necessary later. The next definition and lemma, along with a better discussion of these topics, can be found in
[
2
]
.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
y\in(0,\infty)
. A function
\Phi:A\subseteq\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
with
A
containing an interval
(0,y)
is said to be
y-admissible
if:

•

0<\Phi(\delta)\leq\delta\>
for all
\delta\in(0,y).

•

\displaystyle{\frac{\Phi(\delta)}{\delta}}{\rightarrow}0
when
\delta\rightarrow 0^{+}.

•

\Phi
is non-decreasing in
(0,y)
.

\Phi
is called
admissible
if the exists
y
such that
\Phi
is
y
-admissible.

Remark 2.7
.

For all
\theta\in(0,1)
, excluding
\theta=0\>\text{and}\>1
the function
\Phi(\delta)=\delta^{1/\theta}
is admissible.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
\Phi
be admissible, and
\emptyset\neq E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
a totally bounded set. The
generalised intermediate dimensions
of
E
are defined as:

\overline{\dim}^{\Phi}(E):=\inf\Set{s\geq 0}{\begin{array}[]{c}\forall\varepsilon>0\>\exists\>\delta_{0}>0/\forall\>0<\delta<\delta_{0}\>\exists\left\{U_{i}\right\}\text{covering of }E\\[2.84526pt]
\text{ such that }\Phi(\delta)\leq|U_{i}|\leq\delta\>\forall i\>,\>\sum|U_{i}|^{s}<\varepsilon\end{array}}.

\underline{\dim}^{\Phi}(E):=\inf\Set{s\geq 0}{\begin{array}[]{c}\forall\varepsilon>0,\delta_{0}>0\>\exists\>\delta\in(0,\delta_{0})\text{ and }\left\{U_{i}\right\}\text{ a covering of }E\\[2.84526pt]
\text{ such that }\Phi(\delta)\leq|U_{i}|\leq\delta\>\forall i\>,\>\sum|U_{i}|^{s}<\varepsilon\end{array}}.

If
\underline{\dim}^{\Phi}(E)=\overline{\dim}^{\Phi}(E)
, we write
\dim^{\Phi}(E)
.

When the function
\Phi(\delta)
is taken to be
\Phi(\delta)=\delta^{1/\theta}
, one obtains the
\theta
-intermediate dimensions, as defined in
[
5
]
. Even though the function
\Phi=0
is not admissible, one can still use it to recover the Hausdorff dimension of the set
E
(which is not a generalised intermediate dimension, as one can prove that
\overline{\dim}^{\Phi}(E)=\overline{\dim}^{\Phi}(\overline{E})
). It is also important to note that the Minkowski dimension can be understood as a generalised intermediate dimension, choosing an appropriate
\Phi
(which is not
\Phi(\delta):=\delta
since it is not admissible).

We only need the next lemma:

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
\Phi
be admissible and
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
bounded. Suppose there exist
C,\>\delta_{0}>0
, such that for every
\delta<\delta_{0}
one can find a covering
\set{U_{i}}
of
E
such that
\Phi(\delta)\leq|U_{i}|\leq\delta
and
\sum|U_{i}|^{s}\leq C
. Then,
\overline{\dim}^{\Phi}(E)\leq s
.

Proof.

In order to prove the desired inequality it is enough to conclude that for each
\eta>0
,
\overline{\dim}^{\Phi}(E)<s+\eta
. For this, let
\varepsilon>0
, and take:

\delta_{1}=\min\set{\delta_{0},(\varepsilon/c)^{1/\eta}}.

Now, for all
\delta<\delta_{1}<\delta_{0}
, there exists
\set{U_{i}}
a covering of
E
with
\Phi(\delta)\leq|U_{i}|\leq\delta
and
\sum|U_{i}|^{s}\leq C
. Then, we have:

\sum|U_{i}|^{s+\eta}=\sum|U_{i}|^{s}|U_{i}|^{\eta}\leq\sum|U_{i}|^{s}\delta^{\eta}\leq C\delta^{\eta}<C\delta_{1}^{\eta}<\varepsilon.

Therefore,
\overline{\dim}^{\Phi}(E)\leq s+\eta\>
for all
\>\eta>0
, and
\overline{\dim}^{\Phi}(E)\leq s
.
∎

Remark 2.10
.

Lemma
2.9
remains valid for
\dim_{H}
and
\overline{\dim}_{B}
, even though
\Phi=0
and
\Phi(\delta)=\delta
are not admissible.

Remark 2.11
.

One can formulate a version of the lemma for lower dimensions:

Suppose that there exists
\>c>0
such that for every
\delta_{0}
there exists
\delta<\delta_{0}
and a covering
\set{U_{i}}
with
\sum|U_{i}|^{s}\leq c
and
\Phi(\delta)\leq|U_{i}|\leq\delta
. Then,
\underline{\dim}^{\Phi}(E)\leq s
.

3.
Intermediate dimensions for measures

Interestingly, given that intermediate dimensions of sets are defined by imposing restrictions on the diameters of sets belonging to certain coverings, one can define these concepts for measures by taking restrictions on the diameter of balls centered on points in the support of the measure. To be precise:

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\theta\in[0,1]
. The
\theta
-intermediate dimensions
of
\mu
are defined by:

\overline{\dim}_{\theta}(\mu):=\inf\Set{s\geq 0:\begin{array}[]{c}\>\exists\>c>0\text{ and }\>\delta_{0}>0/\forall-\text{ a.e. }x\in\operatorname{supp}\left(\mu\right)\text{ we have: }\\[2.84526pt]
\forall\>\delta<\delta_{0}\>\>\exists\>r\in[\delta^{1/\theta},\delta]\text{ with }\>\mu(B(x,r))\geq c\cdot r^{s}\end{array}}.

\underline{\dim}_{\theta}(\mu):=\inf\Set{s\geq 0:\begin{array}[]{c}\>\exists\>c>0\text{ and }\>\delta_{n}\to 0/\forall-\text{ a.e. }x\in\operatorname{supp}\left(\mu\right),\>\text{ we have: }\\[2.84526pt]
\forall\>n\in\mathbb{N}\>\exists\>r\in[\delta_{n}^{1/\theta},\delta_{n}]\text{ with }\>\mu(B(x,r))\geq c\cdot r^{s}\end{array}}.

In
4.2
we will briefly discuss an analogue of the generalised intermediate dimensions for measures. It is also clear that we can replace the intervals
[\delta^{1/\theta},\delta]
with
[\delta,\delta^{\theta}]
when
\theta>0
.

Remark 3.2
.

Clearly, these dimensions satisfy the next inequalities:

(1)

\overline{\dim}_{\theta}(\mu)\leq\overline{\dim}_{\phi}
for all
0\leq\theta\leq\phi\leq 1
.

(2)

\overline{\dim}_{\theta}(\mu)\leq\overline{\dim}_{B}(\mu)
.

(3)

\overline{\dim}_{0}(\mu)=\underline{\dim}_{0}(\mu)=\dim_{H}^{*}(\mu).

It is not very clear that
\overline{\dim}_{1}(\mu)=\overline{\dim}_{B}(\mu)
, given that in the definition of intermediate dimensions, we require certain conditions to be true for almost all
x
. However, as we shall see, we will be able to replace “
{\rm almost\ all}\>x
” with “
{\rm for\ all}\>x
”.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\theta\in(0,1]
. Then:

\overline{\dim}_{\theta}(\mu):=\inf\Set{s\geq 0:\begin{array}[]{c}\>\exists\>c>0\text{ and }\>\delta_{0}>0/\forall\>\delta<\delta_{0}\text{ and }x\in\operatorname{supp}\left(\mu\right),\\[2.84526pt]
\exists\>r\in[\delta^{1/\theta},\delta]\text{ with }\>\mu(B(x,r))\geq c\cdot r^{s}\end{array}}.

\underline{\dim}_{\theta}(\mu):=\inf\Set{s\geq 0:\begin{array}[]{c}\>\exists\>c>0\text{ and }\>\delta_{n}\to 0/\forall\>n\in\mathbb{N}\text{ and }x\in\operatorname{supp}\left(\mu\right),\\[2.84526pt]
\exists\>r\in[\delta_{n}^{1/\theta},\delta_{n}]\text{ with }\>\mu(B(x,r))\geq c\cdot r^{s}\end{array}}.

Proof.

The inequality
\geq
is clear. For the nontrivial inequality, suppose that
s>\overline{\dim}_{\theta}\mu
, so that there exist
c,\ \delta_{0}>0
and
A\subseteq\operatorname{supp}\left(\mu\right)
with
\mu(\operatorname{supp}\left(\mu\right)\setminus A)=0
such that, for all
x\in A
, we have, that for every
\delta<\delta_{0}
there exists
r\in[\delta^{1/\theta},\delta]
such that
\mu(B(x,r))\geq c\cdot r^{s}.

We need to verify that the same remains valid for every
x\in\operatorname{supp}\left(\mu\right)
. Let
x\in\operatorname{supp}\left(\mu\right)
,
\delta<\delta_{0}
and
\widetilde{\delta}:=\delta/2
. Then, take
y\in A
such that
d(x,y)\leq\widetilde{\delta}^{1/\theta}
.

By hyphotesis, the exists
\widetilde{r}\in\left[\widetilde{\delta}^{1/\theta},\widetilde{\delta}\right]
such that:

\mu(B(y,r))\geq c\cdot\widetilde{r}^{s}.

Then, taking
r:=2\widetilde{r}
, we have that
B(y,\widetilde{r})\subseteq B(x,r)
, and so

\mu(B(x,r))\geq\mu(B(y,\widetilde{r}))\geq c\cdot\left(\frac{r}{2}\right)^{s}=\frac{c}{2^{s}}r^{s}.

It is clear that
r\in\left[2\widetilde{\delta}^{1/\theta},2\widetilde{\delta}\right]=\left[\frac{2}{2^{1/\theta}}\delta^{1/\theta},\delta\right]
. However, we need
r\in\left[\delta^{1/\theta},\delta\right]
. If

r\geq\delta^{1/\theta}
there is nothing to do.

If
r<\delta^{1/\theta}
then

\mu(B(x,\delta^{1/\theta}))\geq\mu(B(x,r))\geq\frac{c}{2^{s}}\cdot r^{s}\geq\frac{c}{2^{s}}\cdot\left(\frac{2}{2^{1/\theta}}\right)^{s}\left(\delta^{1/\theta}\right)^{s}=c\cdot\frac{1}{2^{s/\theta}}\left(\delta^{1/\theta}\right)^{s}.

Therefore, changing the constant
c
, we have that
x
satisfies the same condition as
y
and so the right-hand side in the inequality is smaller than
s
.
∎

Keeping this result in mind, it is clear that
\overline{\dim}_{1}(\mu)=\overline{\dim}_{B}(\mu)\>,\>\underline{\dim}_{1}(\mu)=\underline{\dim}_{B}(\mu)
, and we can conclude that the intermediate dimensions interpolate between the (upper) Hausdorff and Minkowski dimensions. Whenever
\theta>0
, we will work with the right-hand expressions in this lemma instead of our original definition, because it is a little easier to work with.

Remark 3.4
.

For
\theta=0
, these two notions of dimension are not equal. In fact, one can prove without much difficulty that, if
\overline{\dim}_{0}^{\prime}(\mu),\underline{\dim}_{0}^{\prime}(\mu)
are defined by taking
\theta=0
in the right-hand expressions given in lemma
3.3
, then:

\displaystyle\overline{\dim}_{0}(\mu)

\displaystyle=\inf\{s:\>\forall\>x\in\operatorname{supp}\left(\mu\right),\underline{\dim}_{\text{loc}}\mu(x)\leq s\}

\displaystyle=\inf\{s:\exists c>0/\>\forall\>x\in\operatorname{supp}\left(\mu\right)\>\exists\>r_{n}\to 0\text{ such that }\mu(B(x,r_{n}))\geq cr_{n}^{s}\}

\displaystyle=\underline{\dim}_{0}(\mu).

Proposition 3.5
.

For every
\theta\in(0,1]
,

\overline{\dim}_{\theta}(\operatorname{supp}\left(\mu\right))\leq\overline{\dim}_{\theta}(\mu).

Proof.

First note that if
(A_{i})_{\mathbb{N}}
are Borel sets such that
\sum\mathbbm{1}_{A_{i}}\leq M
for some
M\in\mathbb{N}
, then,
\sum\mathbbm{1}_{A_{i}}\leq M\cdot\mathbbm{1}_{\cup A_{i}}
and therefore, taking integrals and using monotone convergence, we have that
\sum\mu(A_{i})\leq M\cdot\mu(\bigcup_{i}A_{i}).

To prove the proposition, take
s>\overline{\dim}_{\theta}(\mu)
. Using Besicovitch’s covering theorem, there exist
c,\delta_{0}
and
M
such that for every
\delta<\delta_{0}
there exists a cover
\{B_{i}=B(x_{i},r_{i})\}_{\mathbb{N}}
of
\operatorname{supp}\left(\mu\right)
, with the following properties:

\sum\mathbbm{1}_{B_{i}}(x)\leq M\>,\>\>\delta^{1/\theta}\leq r_{i}\leq\delta\>\text{ and }\>\mu(B_{i})\geq c\cdot r_{i}^{s}.

Also, we can take
\delta_{0}
such that for
\delta<\delta_{0}
we have
\frac{2^{2/\theta}}{4}\leq\delta^{1-1/\theta}
. Then, as
\delta^{1/\theta}\leq r_{i}\leq\delta
, we obtain
2\delta^{1/\theta}\leq|B_{i}|\leq 2\delta
, hence the diameters are not in the ’appropriate’ range (which would be
2^{1/\theta}\delta^{1/\theta}\leq|B_{i}|\leq 2\delta
), so we need to ’enlarge’ those
B_{i}
that don’t fall in the correct range. For this we choose a new covering
\widetilde{B_{i}}
as follows:

If
(2\delta)^{1/\theta}\leq|B_{i}|\leq 2\delta
, we leave
B_{i}
as it is, taking
\widetilde{B_{i}}:=B_{i}
. Otherwise (that is, if
2\delta^{1/\theta}\leq|B_{i}|\leq(2\delta)^{1/\theta}
), we take
\widetilde{B_{i}}:=aB_{i}
with
a=2^{1/\theta}/2
, so that we get
(2\delta)^{1/\theta}\leq\left|aB_{i}\right|\leq 2\delta
.

We have:

\sum|\widetilde{B_{i}}|^{s}\leq\sum a^{s}\left|B_{i}\right|^{s}=\sum(2a)^{s}r_{i}^{s}\leq\frac{(2a)^{s}}{c}\sum\mu(B_{i})\leq\frac{M\cdot(2a)^{s}}{c}\mu(\mathbb{R}^{n})<\infty.

Now, because of
2.9
we conclude that
s\geq\overline{\dim}_{\theta}(\operatorname{supp}\left(\mu\right))
.
∎

Just like in the case of sets, nontrivial inequalities relating different intermediate dimensions are greatly desired. One such result is the following, which is true for sets (in fact, many results in this paper will prove translations of well-known inequalities for sets):

Proposition 3.6
.

For all
0<\theta\leq\phi\leq 1
,

\overline{\dim}_{\phi}(\mu)\leq\frac{\phi}{\theta}\overline{\dim}_{\theta}(\mu).

In particular, if
\overline{\dim}_{\theta}(\mu)=0
for some
\theta\in(0,1]
then
\overline{\dim}_{B}(\mu)=0
.

Proof.

Let
s>\overline{\dim}_{\theta}(\mu)
, and
c,\delta_{0}>0
as in the definition for
\overline{\dim}_{\theta}(\mu)
. Then, taking
\delta<\delta_{0}
and
x\in\operatorname{supp}\left(\mu\right)
, we need to prove that there exists
r\in[\delta^{1/\phi},\delta]
such that
\mu(B(x,r))\geq cr^{s\cdot\phi/\theta}
.

By definition, it is clear that there exists
r\in[\delta^{1/\theta},\delta]
with
\mu(B(x,r))\geq cr^{s}
. We distinguish two cases:

(1)

If
r\in[\delta^{1/\phi},\delta]
, then the same
r
works, because
r^{s}\geq r^{s\cdot\phi/\theta}
.

(2)

If
r\in[\delta^{1/\theta},\delta^{1/\phi}]
, then we can see that
\delta^{1/\phi}
works:

\mu(B(x,\delta^{1/\phi}))\geq\mu(B(x,r))\geq c\cdot r^{s}\geq c\cdot(\delta^{1/\phi})^{\frac{\phi}{\theta}s}.

In conclusion, for all
\delta,x
we can find
r\in[\delta^{1/\phi},\delta]
with
\mu(B(x,r))\geq cr^{s\phi/\theta}
, and
\overline{\dim}_{\phi}(\mu)\leq s\frac{\phi}{\theta}
.

∎

Remark 3.7
.

Let us at this point briefly discuss this last proof. When proving these type of statements for the dimensions of sets, one usually starts by taking a cover of the set, and then either ”enlarge” or ”subdivide” the members of the cover appropriately in order to obtain a new cover, whose members have the diameters falling within a certain range. This is essentially what we did here, but there is an important aspect to note.

In the case of measures, it was not necessary to consider the ”whole set”, in the sense that we only had to study the behavior of balls centered at a fixed
x
. In other words, the dimensions of measures are in a sense ”local”, rather than ”global” as in sets. This difference can be seen more easily with an example:

First, consider
F_{p}:=\Set{\frac{1}{n^{p}}}\cup\Set{0}
, and the set:

C^{n}_{p}:=\Set{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:}{x|\in F_{p}}=\bigcup_{k}S^{n-1}_{1/k^{p}}=\bigcup_{k}S_{k}

Figure 1.
The set
C_{p}^{2}
(in black) and some balls around one of its elements.

Now, suppose
p>\frac{1}{n-1}
, so that the measure
\mu=\sum\mathcal{H}^{n-1}_{|S_{k}}
is finite. We will show that, in fact
\overline{\dim}_{B}(\mu)\leq n-1
: For this, take
x\in C_{n}^{p}
, so that
s\in S_{k}
for some
k
and
r>0
, and consider three cases:
r\leq 1/k^{p}
,
r\geq 2/k^{p}
and
1/k^{p}\leq r\leq 2/k^{p}
. For the first two cases, it is clear that
\mu(B(x,r))\geq c\cdot r^{s}
for some constant
c>0
, and the same can be deduced for the third case by interpolating between the first two cases.

Moreover, just as easily one can obtain the same inequality for the upper Hausdorff dimension,
\dim^{*}_{H}(\mu)=n-1
. Then, all intermediate dimensions are equal to
n-1
. However,
\dim_{H}(C^{n}_{p})=n-1
and so we obtain:

\overline{\dim}_{B}(C^{n}_{p})\leq\overline{\dim}_{B}(\mu)\leq n-1=\dim_{H}(C^{n}_{p}).

Then, the box dimension of this set can be obtained via the intermediate dimensions of measures. This last part is especially interesting because even though the proof of this last fact can be done without measures, it would involve a not small amount of calculations, whereas this proof is a simple geometric observation.

Corollary 3.8
.

The function
\theta\mapsto\overline{\dim}_{\theta}(\mu)
is continuous in
(0,1]
.

Considering that, for sets, it is important to determine whether
\theta\mapsto\overline{\dim}_{\theta}(\operatorname{supp}\left(\mu\right))
is continuous at
0
or not, it would be interesting to try to relate the continuity at
0
for sets to the continuity at
0
for measures. As
\dim_{H}^{*}(\mu)\leq\dim_{H}((\operatorname{supp}\left(\mu\right)))
, we first need to establish conditions that guarantee that we have
\dim_{H}^{*}(\mu)=\dim_{H}((\operatorname{supp}\left(\mu\right)))
(otherwise,
\theta\mapsto\overline{\dim}_{\theta}(\mu)
is simply not continuous at
0
).

Using
[
6
, Proposition 10.10 ]
, which decomposes any measure into a sum
\mu=\sum_{s}\mu_{s}+\mu^{D}
, it is not difficult to verify that if one has
\mu^{D}=0
, then
\dim_{H}^{*}(\mu)=\dim_{H}((\operatorname{supp}\left(\mu\right)))
. Consequently, it is clear that the continuity of
\theta\mapsto\overline{\dim}_{\theta}(\mu)
at
0
implies the same for
\theta\mapsto\overline{\dim}_{\theta}((\operatorname{supp}\left(\mu\right)))
.

Before proving the main theorem, we wish to point out a couple of remarks.

Remark 3.9
.

Let
\mu,\nu
be measures with equal support. If
\mu(B)\leq\nu(B)
is true for every Borel set
B
, then
\overline{\dim}_{\theta}(\nu)\leq\overline{\dim}_{\theta}(\mu)
for all
\theta\in[0,1]
. Hence, clearly

\overline{\dim}_{\theta}(\mu+\nu)\leq\min\Set{\overline{\dim}_{\theta}(\mu),\overline{\dim}_{\theta}(\nu)}.

Remark 3.10
.

Taking a closer look at the definition of
\overline{\dim}_{\theta}(\mu)
, It is not difficult to notice that it suffices to check the condition
\delta<\delta_{0}
for a decreasing sequence
\delta_{n}\to 0
such that
\delta_{n+1}\geq a\cdot\delta_{n}
for some
a\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
:

Proof.

Take
\delta\in[\delta_{n+1},\delta_{n}]
. We need to verify that this
\delta
satisfies the condition in
3.1
:

To show this, taking the
r\in[\delta_{n+1}^{1/\theta},\delta_{n+1}]
associated with
\delta_{n+1}
, there are two cases: If
r\in[\delta^{1/\theta},\delta]
, then the same
r
works for
\delta
. If not (
r<\delta^{1/\theta}
), then we have:

\mu(B(x,\delta^{1/\theta}))\geq\mu(B(x,r))\geq c\cdot r^{s}\geq c\cdot\delta_{n+1}^{s/\theta}\geq c\cdot a^{s/\theta}\cdot\delta_{n}^{s/\theta}\geq\widetilde{c}\cdot(\delta^{1/\theta})^{s}.

∎

Theorem 3.11
.

Let
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be compact. Then, for every
\theta\in(0,1]
,

\overline{\dim}_{\theta}(E)=\min\Set{\overline{\dim}_{\theta}(\mu):\mu(\mathbb{R}^{n})<\infty\text{ and }\operatorname{supp}\left(\mu\right)=E}.

Moreover, there exists a finite measure
\mu
such that
\operatorname{supp}\left(\mu\right)=E
, and

\overline{\dim}_{\theta}(E)=\overline{\dim}_{\theta}(\mu)\>,\>\forall\theta\in(0,1].

Proof.

We proceed to prove this in three steps:

I)

We first prove that for each
\theta
, we have

\overline{\dim}_{\theta}(E)=\inf\Set{\overline{\dim}_{\theta}(\mu):\mu(\mathbb{R}^{n})<\infty\text{ and }\operatorname{supp}\left(\mu\right)=E}.

II)

We will show that this infimum is in fact a minimum.

III)

We will construct the measure
\mu
that satifies the equality for all
\theta
.

I)

Fix
\theta
, by Proposition
3.5
, we have the inequality
\leq
. Now, let
s>t>\overline{\dim}_{\theta}(E)
. We will show that there exists
\mu
with
\operatorname{supp}\left(\mu\right)=E
, such that
s>\overline{\dim}_{\theta}(\mu)
.

Taking
\varepsilon=1
in the definition of
\overline{\dim}_{\theta}(E)
, it is clear that there exists
\delta_{0}
such that, for every
\delta<\delta_{0}
there exists a cover
\{U_{i}\}
of
E
with
\delta^{1/\theta}\leq\left|U_{i}\right|\leq\delta
, and
\sum\left|U_{i}\right|^{t}<1
. Now, taking
n_{0}
such that
\frac{1}{2^{n_{0}}}<\delta_{0}
, and
\delta_{n}=\frac{1}{2^{n}}
, we obtain that there
exists
n_{0}
such that for every
n\geq n_{0}
we have a covering of
E
,
\{U_{i}^{(n)}\}_{i=1,\dots,C_{n}}
, with:

\delta_{n}^{1/\theta}\leq\left|U_{i}^{(n)}\right|\leq\delta_{n}\>,\>\text{ and }\>\sum_{i=1}^{C_{n}}\left|U_{i}^{(n)}\right|^{t}<1.

Now, there exists
c>0
such that for all
n\geq n_{0}

\sum|U_{i}^{(n)}|^{s}=\sum|U_{i}^{(n)}|^{t}|U_{i}^{(n)}|^{s-t}\leq\delta_{n}^{s-t}\sum|U_{i}^{(n)}|^{t}\leq\frac{1}{(2^{s-t})^{n}}\leq\frac{c}{n^{2}}.

Therefore, we can define the following measure
\mu
, taking for each
n,i
some element
x_{i}^{(n)}\in U_{i}^{(n)}\bigcap E
:

\mu:=\sum_{n\geq n_{0}}\sum_{i=1}^{C_{n}}|U_{i}^{(n)}|^{s}\delta_{x_{i}^{(n)}}.

The measure
\mu
is fully supported in
E
, since its support is contained in
E
and for each
x\in E
a sequence of points among the
x_{i}^{(n)}
can be found such that it converges to
x
. It is a finite measure, because

\mu(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\sum_{n\geq n_{0}}\sum_{i=1}^{C_{n}}|U_{i}^{(n)}|^{s}\leq\sum_{n\geq n_{0}}\frac{c}{n^{2}}<\infty.

Now, the only thing that needs to be verified for this step is that
s>\overline{\dim}_{\theta}(\mu)
. For this, Observation
3.10
will be helpful.

Taking the sequence
\delta_{n}=\frac{1}{2^{n}}
(
n\geq n_{0}
),
n\geq n_{0}
and
x\in\operatorname{supp}\left(\mu\right)=E
, there exists
i
such that
x\in U_{i}^{(n)}
and so we can take
r=|U_{i}^{(n)}|\in[\delta_{n}^{1/\theta},\delta_{n}]
, so that

x_{i}^{(n)}\in B(x,r)
, and:

\mu(B(x,r))\geq\mu({x_{i}^{(n)}})\geq|U_{i}^{(n)}|^{s}=r^{s}.

Then,
s>\overline{\dim}_{\theta}(\mu)
.

II)

In order to show that the infimum is a minimum, we remark that multiplying a measure by a constant factor does not affect its intermediate dimensions. With this in mind, because of the previous step there is a sequence of measures
\mu_{n}
, all finite and fully supported in
E
, such that
\overline{\dim}_{\theta}(\mu_{n})\searrow\overline{\dim}_{\theta}(E)
, and by the previous comment we can suppose that they also satisfy
\mu_{n}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\leq\frac{1}{n^{2}}
.

Thus,
\mu_{\theta}:=\sum\mu_{n}
is a finite measure, fully supported in
E
, and by Remark
3.9
we conclude that:

\overline{\dim}_{\theta}(\mu_{\theta})=\overline{\dim}_{\theta}(\sum\mu_{n})\leq\overline{\dim}_{\theta}(\mu_{n})\>\forall\>n,

so that
\overline{\dim}_{\theta}(E)\leq\overline{\dim}_{\theta}(\mu_{\theta})\leq\overline{\dim}_{\theta}(E)
.

III)

To construct the measure that verifies the equality in the statement of the theorem, for all
\theta
, first let
\{q_{n}\}_{\mathbb{N}}=\mathbb{Q}\bigcap(0,1]
be an ordering of the rationals in the unit interval. For each
n
there exists
\mu_{q_{n}}
finite and fully supported in
E
with
\overline{\dim}_{q_{n}}(\mu_{q_{n}})=\overline{\dim}_{q_{n}}(E)
, and without loss of generality satisfying
\mu_{q_{n}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\leq 1/n^{2}
. Then, just like before. the measure:

\mu:=\sum\mu_{q_{n}}

can be considered, which is finite, fully supported in
E
, and

\overline{\dim}_{q_{n}}(E)\leq\overline{\dim}_{q_{n}}(\mu)\leq\overline{\dim}_{q_{n}}(\mu_{q_{n}})=\overline{\dim}_{q_{n}}(E)\>\forall\>n.

In conclusion,
\overline{\dim}_{\theta}(\mu)=\overline{\dim}_{\theta}(E)
for all
\theta\in\mathbb{Q}
. In addition, the functions
\theta\mapsto\overline{\dim}_{\theta}(E)
and
\theta\mapsto\overline{\dim}_{\theta}(\mu)
are both continuous in
(0,1]
and coincide in a dense subset of
(0,1]
, so:

\overline{\dim}_{\theta}(\mu)=\overline{\dim}_{\theta}(E)\>\forall\>\theta\in(0,1].

∎

The importance of this theorem lies in the fact that it allows some results for dimensions of measures to be translated to results for dimensions of sets. For example, the inequality
\overline{\dim}_{\phi}(E)\leq\frac{\phi}{\theta}\overline{\dim}_{\theta}(E)
can be deduced directly from Proposition
3.6
by taking the measure given by Theorem
3.11
.

Proposition 3.12
.

Let
\mu
be a measure such that
\dim_{A}(\mu)<\infty
and
\theta\in(0,1]
. Then,

\overline{\dim}_{\theta}(\mu)\geq\dim_{A}(\mu)-\frac{\dim_{A}(\mu)-\overline{\dim}_{B}(\mu)}{\theta}.

Proof.

Just like with the proof for the case of sets, take:

0<b<\overline{\dim}_{B}(\mu)\leq\dim_{A}(\mu)<d<\infty

(if
\overline{\dim}_{B}(\mu)=0
then the result is trivial). Then, the following properties are satisfied:

(1)

There exists
\widetilde{c}>0
such that

\frac{\mu(B(x,r))}{\mu(B(x,R))}\geq\widetilde{c}\cdot\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{d}\>,\>\text{for all }\>0<r<R<1,x\in\operatorname{supp}\left(\mu\right).

(2)

For all
c,\delta_{0}>0
there exist
x\in\operatorname{supp}\left(\mu\right),\delta<\delta_{0}\>
such that
\>\mu(B(x,\delta))<c\cdot\delta^{b}
.

Therefore, we need to verify that
\overline{\dim}_{\theta}(\mu)>d-\frac{d-b}{\theta}
, or in other words, that for all
c,\delta_{0}
there exist
\>\delta<\delta_{0},x\in\operatorname{supp}\left(\mu\right)
such that, for all
r\in[\delta^{1/\theta},\delta]
, we have
\mu(B(x,r))<c\cdot r^{d-(d-b)/\theta}
:

Take
c,\delta_{0}>0
. Because of property (
2
), there exists
\>\delta<\delta_{0}
with

\mu(B(x,\delta))<c\cdot\delta^{b}.

Now, with these
\delta,x
, notice that for every
r\in[\delta^{1/\theta},\delta]
we can use property (
1
) with
r
and
\delta
, so that:

\widetilde{c}\cdot\left(\frac{\delta}{r}\right)^{d}\leq\frac{\mu(B(x,\delta))}{\mu(B(x,r))}<\left(\frac{\delta}{r}\right)^{d}<\frac{c\cdot\delta^{b}}{\mu(B(x,r))},

and then,

\mu(B(x,r))<\frac{c}{\widetilde{c}}\cdot\delta^{b-d}r^{d}\leq\frac{c}{\widetilde{c}}\cdot r^{d-(d-b)/\theta},

so the result follows.
∎

Corollary 3.13
.

If
\dim_{A}(\mu)=\overline{\dim}_{B}(\mu)<\infty
, then
\overline{\dim}_{\theta}(\mu)=\dim_{A}(\mu)
for all
\theta\in(0,1]
.

Remark 3.14
.

This last corollary shows that it is possible that the function
\theta\mapsto\overline{\dim}_{\theta}(\mu)
is constant while
\theta\mapsto\overline{\dim}_{\theta}(E)
is not.

For this, take
E=\Set{\frac{1}{n}}_{\mathbb{N}}
, and
\mu(\frac{1}{n})=\frac{1}{n^{2}}
. Then, by
[
10
, theorem 4.1]
, one concludes that:

•

\dim_{A}(\mu)=\overline{\dim}_{B}(\mu)=1

•

\dim_{\theta}(E)=\frac{\theta}{1+\theta}
, for
\theta\in[0,1]
,

and hence
\theta\mapsto\overline{\dim}_{\theta}(\mu)
is not constant.

Remark 3.15
.

A question related to the second step in the proof of Theorem
3.11
would be whether the function
\mu\mapsto\overline{\dim}_{\theta}(\mu)
(with fixed
\theta
) is continuous in the weak topology. However, this is not true, and it is not difficult to find a counterexample:

The idea behind the counterexample consists in taking the underlying set as

F_{1}=\Set{0}\bigcup\Set{\frac{1}{n}}_{\mathbb{N}},

and a measure supported in such set, and then ’disturb it’ (this counterexample works with
\theta=1
, in fact):

Following the calculations found in
[
10
, theorem 4.1]
, taking the measures:

\mu:=\sum\frac{1}{n^{3/2}}\delta_{1/n}\>,\>\mu_{k}:=\mu|_{[1/(k-1),1]}+\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^{11/10}}\delta_{1/n}

we have that
\overline{\dim}_{B}(\mu)=\frac{3}{4}
, and
\overline{\dim}_{B}(\mu_{k})=\frac{11}{20}
. However,
\mu_{k}\rightharpoonup\mu
, hence the function
\mu\mapsto\overline{\dim}_{B}(\mu)
is not continuous.

4.
Products and dimensions

Given two measures
\mu,\nu
, we have that
\operatorname{supp}\left(\mu\times\nu\right)=\operatorname{supp}\left(\mu\right)\times\operatorname{supp}\left(\nu\right)
. It follows that if both are finite and compactly supported, then the product measure possesses those properties as well. Then we can consider products of measures and their dimensions.

Theorem 4.1
.

The following inequalities are true:

\underline{\dim}_{\theta}(\mu)+\underline{\dim}_{\theta}(\nu)\leq\underline{\dim}_{\theta}(\mu\times\nu)\leq\overline{\dim}_{\theta}(\mu\times\nu)\leq\overline{\dim}_{\theta}(\mu)+\overline{\dim}_{B}(\nu).

Proof.

We will prove the last inequality first:

Let
s>\overline{\dim}_{\theta}(\mu),t>\overline{\dim}_{B}(\nu)
. Then:

(1)

There exists
c
such that
\nu(B(y,r))\geq c\cdot r^{t}
for all
y\in\operatorname{supp}\left(\nu\right),r\in(0,1).

(2)

There exist
\widetilde{c}
and
\delta_{0}>0
such that for all
\delta<\delta_{0},x\in\operatorname{supp}\left(\mu\right),
there exists
\>\widetilde{r}\in[\delta^{1/\theta},\delta]
with
\mu(B(x,\widetilde{r}))\geq\widetilde{c}\cdot\widetilde{r}^{s}.

In order to check that
s+t\geq\overline{\dim}_{\theta}(\mu\times\nu)
, first take
c\cdot\widetilde{c}\;,\text{ and }\;2\delta_{0}
.

If
\delta<2\delta_{0}
, and
(x,y)\in\operatorname{supp}\left(\mu\times\nu\right)=\operatorname{supp}\left(\mu\right)\times\operatorname{supp}\left(\nu\right)
, it is clear that there exists
\widetilde{r}\in[(\delta/2)^{1/\theta},\delta/2]
such that:

\displaystyle(\mu\times\nu)\left(B((x,y),2\cdot\widetilde{r})\right)

\displaystyle\geq(\mu\times\nu)\left(B(x,\widetilde{r})\times B(y,\widetilde{r})\right)=\mu(B(x,\widetilde{r}))\cdot\nu(B(y,\widetilde{r}))

\displaystyle\geq c\cdot\widetilde{c}\cdot\widetilde{r}^{s}\cdot\widetilde{r}^{t}=c\cdot\widetilde{c}\cdot(\widetilde{r})^{s+t}=\frac{c\cdot\widetilde{c}}{2^{s+t}}(2\cdot\widetilde{r})^{s+t}.

However, given that
\frac{2}{2^{1/\theta}}\delta^{1/\theta}\leq 2\cdot\widetilde{r}\leq\delta
, and
\frac{2}{2^{1/\theta}}\leq 1
, it is possible that
2\widetilde{r}
does not fall within the ”appropriate range”. However, just like before, this can be fixed quite easily:

If
2\cdot\widetilde{r}\in[\delta^{1/\theta},\delta]
, we are done. Otherwise, taking
\delta^{1/\theta}
, we have the following:

\displaystyle(\mu\times\nu)(B((x,y),\delta^{1/\theta}))\geq(\mu\times\nu)(B((x,y),2\cdot\widetilde{r}))

\displaystyle\geq\frac{c\cdot\widetilde{c}}{2^{s+t}}(2\cdot\widetilde{r})^{s+t}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{c\cdot\widetilde{c}}{2^{s+t}}\left(\frac{2}{2^{1/\theta}}\right)^{s+t}\left(\delta^{1/\theta}\right)^{s+t}.

In conclusion, for each pair
\delta<2\delta_{0}
and
(x,y)
there exists
r\in[\delta^{1/\theta},\delta]
such that:

(\mu\times\nu)(B((x,y),r))\geq\frac{c\cdot\widetilde{c}}{2^{s+t}}\left(\frac{2}{2^{1/\theta}}\right)^{s+t}r^{s+t},

and so
s+t\geq\overline{\dim}_{\theta}(\mu\times\nu)
.

For the inequality
\underline{\dim}_{\theta}(\mu)+\underline{\dim}_{\theta}(\nu)\leq\underline{\dim}_{\theta}(\mu\times\nu)
, it is useful to notice that the following two conditions are equivalent:

(1)

For all
c>0\>,\>\delta_{n}\rightarrow 0
there exist
\>n\in\mathbb{N}\>,\>\text{and }x\in\operatorname{supp}\left(\mu\right)
such that for every
r\in[\delta_{n}^{1/\theta},\delta_{n}],\>\mu(B(x,r))<c\cdot r^{s}
.

(2)

For all
c>0
there exists
\delta_{0}
such that for all
\delta<\delta_{0}
there exists
x\in\operatorname{supp}\left(\mu\right)
such that for every
r\in[\delta^{1/\theta},\delta],\>\mu(B(x,r))<c\cdot r^{s}.

Then, take
s<\underline{\dim}_{\theta}(\mu)
, and
t<\underline{\dim}_{\theta}(\nu)
as usual. We will prove the inequality
s+t<\underline{\dim}_{\theta}(\mu\times\nu)
. By definition, we have the following:

For all
c>0
there exists
\delta_{0}>0
such that for all
\>\delta<\delta_{0}
there exists
\>x\in\operatorname{supp}\left(\mu\right)
and
\>y\in\operatorname{supp}\left(\nu\right)
such that, for every
\>r\in[\delta^{1/\theta},\delta],

\mu(B(x,r))<c\cdot r^{s}\;,\>\nu(B(y,r))<c\cdot r^{t}.

We need to prove that the same is true for the measure
\mu\times\nu
, with
s+t
.

For this, let
\widetilde{c}>0
and take
c:=\sqrt{\widetilde{c}}
. Then, we can work with the
\delta_{0}>0
corresponding to the last property. Then, the following is clear:

For every
\delta<\delta_{0}
there exist
x\in\operatorname{supp}\left(\mu\right),y\in\operatorname{supp}\left(\nu\right)
such that, for
r\in[\delta^{1/\theta},\delta],

\mu(B(x,r))<c\cdot r^{s}\text{ and }\nu(B(y,r))<c\cdot r^{t}.

Then, taking
(x,y)
in the product space, we have that for all
r\in[\delta^{1/\theta},\delta],

(\mu\times\nu)(B((x,y),r))\leq(\mu\times\nu)(B(x,r)\times B(y,r))=\mu(B(x,r))\cdot\nu(B(y,r))<\widetilde{c}\cdot r^{s+t}.

∎

As a final comment, we would like to add a little lemma regarding generalised intermediate dimensions for measures, defined in the same fashion as for intermediate dimensions for sets, using an admissible function
\Phi(\delta)
as defined in Definition
2.8
.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
\Phi
be an admissible function. The
\Phi
-intermediate dimensions
of
\mu
are defined by:

•

\overline{\dim}^{\Phi}(\mu):=\inf\Set{s\geq 0:\begin{array}[]{c}\>\exists\>c>0\text{ and }\>\delta_{0}>0/\forall\>\delta<\delta_{0}\text{ and }x\in\operatorname{supp}\left(\mu\right),\\[2.84526pt]
\exists\>r\in[\Phi(\delta),\delta]\text{ with }\>\mu(B(x,r))\geq c\cdot r^{s}\end{array}}.

•

\underline{\dim}^{\Phi}(\mu):=\inf\Set{s\geq 0:\begin{array}[]{c}\>\exists\>c>0\text{ and }\>\delta_{n}\to 0/\forall\>n\in\mathbb{N}\text{ and }x\in\operatorname{supp}\left(\mu\right),\\[2.84526pt]
\exists\>r\in[\Phi(\delta),\delta_{n}]\text{ with }\>\mu(B(x,r))\geq c\cdot r^{s}\end{array}}.

Most of what was said about intermediate dimensions holds for these definitions without much variation in the proofs. However, given that
\Phi(\delta)=\delta
is not admissible, it is not immediately clear that the Minkowski dimensions of a measure can be understood as generalised intermediate dimensions. However, just like in the case for sets (
[
2
, Prop. 3.4]
), it is true.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
\Phi
be an admissible function such that
\lim_{\delta\to 0}\frac{\log\delta}{\log\Phi(\delta)}=1
, and
\mu
a measure. Then,
\overline{\dim}_{B}(\mu)=\overline{\dim}^{\Phi}(\mu)
.

Proof.

The inequality
\overline{\dim}_{B}\mu\geq\overline{\dim}^{\Phi}\mu
is clear. For the other one, we will show that for all
\theta<1
,
\overline{\dim}_{\theta}(\mu)\leq\overline{\dim}^{\Phi}(\mu)
, so that the result follows from Corollary
3.8
.

If the dimension is
\infty
, then the inequality is clear. Else, let
\theta<1
and
s>\overline{\dim}^{\Phi}(\mu)
( so that there exist
c
and
\delta_{0}
such that for all

\delta<\delta_{0},\>x\in\operatorname{supp}\left(\mu\right)\>
there exists
r\in[\Phi(\delta),\delta]\>
with
\>\mu(B(x,r))\geq cr^{s}.

By our hypothesis, it is clear that for sufficiently small
\delta
, we have

\frac{\log\delta}{\log\Phi(\delta)}\geq\theta\text{, so that }\log(\delta)\leq\log(\Phi(\delta)^{\theta})\text{ and }\delta\leq\Phi(\delta)^{\theta}.

But then,

\delta^{1/\theta}\leq\Phi(\delta)\leq\delta,

so we can conclude that

\overline{\dim}_{\theta}(\mu)\leq s\text{ and then }\overline{\dim}_{\theta}(\mu)\leq\overline{\dim}^{\Phi}(\mu).

∎

5.
A Capacity-theoretic approach

In this section we will study an alternative definition of intermediate dimensions in terms of capacities with respect to certain kernels, which will be useful for obtaining properties regarding the intermediate dimensions of pushforward measures by projections (Theorem
5.11
and Corollary
5.14
), which we proceed to define:

Definition 5.1
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
continuous be a function,
\mu
a Radon measure in
X
. The
pushforward measure

\mu_{f}
is defined by:

\mu_{f}(E):=\mu(f^{-1}(E)).

In particular, we will study the measures
\mu_{V}:=\mu_{P_{V}},
with
P_{V}
the orthogonal projection from
\mathbb{R}^{n}
onto some
V\in G(n,m)
, and the relations between the intermediate dimensions of
\mu_{V}
and
\mu.

We first need to define two functions that play an important role in the projections of sets, which have been studied in
[
3
]
.

Definition 5.2
.

Let
\theta\in(0,1]
,
m\in\{1,\dots,n\}
,
s\in[0,m]
and
r\in(0,1)
. Then, we define the functions
\phi_{r,\theta}^{s,m},\widetilde{\phi}_{r,\theta}^{s}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by:

\phi_{r,\theta}^{s,m}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcc}1&\text{if}&|x|\leq r\\
\\
r^{s}/|x|^{s}&\text{if}&r<|x|\leq r^{\theta}\\
\\
r^{\theta(m-s)+s}/|x|^{m}&\text{if}&r^{\theta}\leq|x|\end{array}\right.\quad\widetilde{\phi}_{r,\theta}^{s}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcc}1&\text{if}&|x|\leq r\\
\\
\frac{r^{s}}{|x|^{s}}&\text{if}&r<|x|\leq r^{\theta}\\
\\
0&\text{if}&r^{\theta}\leq|x|\end{array},\right.

and the “capacity” of a measure as

(C_{r,\theta}^{s,m}(\mu))^{-1}=\displaystyle\inf_{x\in\operatorname{supp}\left(\mu\right)}\int\phi_{r,\theta}^{s,m}(x-y)d\mu(y).

The proof of the next lemma is fairly straightforward:

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\mu
be a finite Borel measure and let
\theta\in(0,1]
. If
0<r<1
, then for all
0\leq t\leq s\leq m,

-(s-t)\leq\left(\frac{\log C_{r,\theta}^{s,m}(\mu)}{-\log r}-s\right)-\left(\frac{\log C_{r,\theta}^{t,m}(\mu)}{-\log r}-t\right)\leq-\theta(s-t).

Proof.

Notice that

\phi_{r,\theta}^{s,m}(x)\leq\phi_{r,\theta}^{t,m}(x)\leq r^{(t-s)(1-\theta)}\phi_{r,\theta}^{s,m}(x),

and so we have:

C_{r,\theta}^{s,m}(\mu)\geq C_{r,\theta}^{t,m}(\mu)\geq r^{(s-t)(1-\theta)}C_{r,\theta}^{s,m}(\mu),

and the result follows
∎

Remark 5.4
.

From this lemma it is clear that the function
s\mapsto\limsup_{r\to 0}\frac{\log C_{r,\theta}^{s,m}(\mu)}{-\log r}-s
is strictly decreasing and continuous.

For this section we will also need the following lemma, that shows that the intermediate dimensions of measures and the kernels
\widetilde{\phi}_{r,\theta}^{s}(x)
are closely related.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
\mu
be a Borel measure and suppose that there exists
c>0
such that for all
r_{0}>0
there exists
r\in(0,r_{0})
such that

(1)

\inf_{x\in\operatorname{supp}\left(\mu\right)}\int\widetilde{\phi}_{r,\theta}^{s}(x-y)d\mu(y)\leq cr^{s}.

Then
\overline{\dim}_{\theta}\mu\geq s
.

Furthermore, if the inequality (
1
) is satisfied for all
r\in(0,1)
then
\underline{\dim}_{\theta}{\mu}\geq s
.

Proof.

We will only prove the
\overline{\dim}_{\theta}
part and leave the
\underline{\dim}_{\theta}
to the reader. First, it is clear that if we take
x\in\operatorname{supp}\left(\mu\right)
and
\delta\in[r,r^{\theta}]
, we have:

\mu(B(x,\delta))\left(\frac{r}{\delta}\right)^{s}\leq\int\left(\frac{r}{\delta}\right)^{s}\mathbbm{1}_{B(0,\delta)}(x-y)d\mu(y)\leq\int\widetilde{\phi}_{r,\theta}^{s}(x-y)d\mu(y).

Then, by hypothesis it is clear that there exists a sequence
r_{k}\to 0
and points
x_{k}\in\operatorname{supp}\left(\mu\right)
such that
\mu(B(x_{k},\delta))\leq c\delta^{s}
for all
\delta\in[r_{k},r_{k}^{\theta}].

Now, fix
\varepsilon>0
. For all
\widetilde{c}>0
, we have that
\delta^{s}\leq\widetilde{c}\cdot\delta^{s-\varepsilon}
for
\delta
sufficiently small, which implies that
s-\varepsilon<\overline{\dim}_{\theta}\mu
, and so
\overline{\dim}_{\theta}\mu\geq s
.
∎

A useful tool in the analysis of the intermediate dimensions of projections of sets is centered around expressing the dimensions in terms of certain ”capacities”. The next Theorem is the measure version of this:

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
\theta\in(0,1]
,
\mu
a Borel measure with
\overline{\dim}_{\theta}\mu>0
, and
m>\dim_{A}\mu
. Then, there exist a unique
s_{0}\in[0,m]
and a unique
t_{0}\in[0,m]
such that:

\limsup_{r\to 0}\frac{\log C_{r,\theta}^{s_{0},m}(\mu)}{-\log r}-s_{0}=0\;\;,\>\>\liminf_{r\to 0}\frac{\log C_{r,\theta}^{t_{0},m}(\mu)}{-\log r}-t_{0}=0.

Furthermore,

(2)

\overline{\dim}_{\theta}\mu=s_{0}\>\>\text{and}\>\>\underline{\dim}_{\theta}\mu=t_{0}.

Proof.

Like before, we only prove the first equality. The uniqueness of such
s_{0}
can be easily deduced from Remark
5.4
. The existence (and the equality (
2
)) can be easily deduced from the following two arguments:

First, suppose that
\limsup_{r\to 0}\frac{\log C_{r,\theta}^{s,m}(\mu)}{-\log r}-s>0
. Then, for all
c>0
there exists a subsequence
\{r_{k}\}
that tends to
0
and a sequence of points in the support of
\mu
,
\{x_{k}\}
, such that:

\int\phi_{r_{k},\theta}^{s,m}(x_{k}-y)d\mu(y)\leq cr_{k}^{s}.

Then for all
\delta\in[r_{k},r_{k}^{\theta}]
we have

\mu(B(x_{k},\delta))=\int\mathbbm{1}_{B(0,\delta)}(x_{k}-y)d\mu(y)\leq\left(\frac{\delta}{r_{k}}\right)^{s}\int\phi_{r_{k},\theta}^{s,m}(x_{k}-y)d\mu(y)\leq C\delta^{s},

which implies that
s\leq\overline{\dim}_{\theta}\mu
.

Conversely, suppose that
\limsup_{r\to 0}\frac{\log C_{r,\theta}^{s,m}(\mu)}{-\log r}-s<0
. By definition, there exists
\delta>0
such that, for every
r\leq\delta
,

r^{s}<\inf_{x\in\operatorname{supp}\left(\mu\right)}\int\phi_{r,\theta}^{s,m}(x-y)d\mu(y).

Then, observing that for every pair
r\leq\widetilde{r}
we have
\phi_{r,\theta}^{s,m}(x)\leq\phi_{\widetilde{r},\theta}^{s,m}(x)
we obtain that for some
C>0
independent of
x
and
r
,

(3)

Cr^{s}\leq\int\phi_{r,\theta}^{s,m}(x-y)d\mu(y)\>\>,\>\>\forall\>r\in(0,1)\;,\;x\in\operatorname{supp}\left(\mu\right).

Now, without loss of generality we can suppose
|\operatorname{supp}\left(\mu\right)|=1.
Let
D=\lceil\log_{2}(1/r)\rceil
and
M
the unique integer satisfying
2^{M-1}r<r^{\theta}\leq 2^{M}r
, and also let
r_{0}
sufficiently small to ensure that
2\leq M\leq D-2
for all
0<r\leq r_{0}
.

Taking
x\in\operatorname{supp}\left(\mu\right)
, using inequality (
3
) and partitioning the support of
\mu
on consecutive annuli of the form
B(x,2^{k}r)\setminus B(x,2^{k-1}r)
,
(1\leq k\leq D)
, we obtain the following:

\displaystyle Cr^{s}

\displaystyle\leq\int\phi_{r,\theta}^{s,m}(x-y)d\mu(y)

\displaystyle\leq\mu(B(x,r))+\sum_{k=1}^{D}\int_{B(x,2^{k}r)\setminus B(x,2^{k-1}r)}\phi_{r,\theta}^{s,m}(x-y)d\mu(y)

\displaystyle\leq\mu(B(x,r))+\sum_{k=1}^{M}\int_{B(x,2^{k}r)\setminus B(x,2^{k-1}r)}2^{-(k-1)s}d\mu(y)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{k=M+1}^{D}\int_{B(x,2^{k}r)\setminus B(x,2^{k-1}r)}r^{\theta(m-s)+s}(2^{k-1}r)^{-m}d\mu(y)

\displaystyle\leq\mu(B(x,r))+\sum_{k=1}^{M}\mu(B(x,2^{k}r))2^{-(k-1)s}d\mu(y)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{k=M+1}^{D}\mu(B(x,2^{k}r))r^{(\theta-1)(m-s)}2^{-m(k-1)}.

Then for each
x
in the support of
\mu
, at least one of the terms of the above sums is at least the arithmetic mean of the total sum. Therefore, we need to consider three cases:

(1)

\displaystyle\frac{Cr^{s}}{D+1}\leq\mu(B(x,r)).

(2)

\displaystyle\frac{Cr^{s}}{D+1}\leq 2^{s}\mu(B(x,2^{k}r))2^{-ks}=4^{s}\mu(B(x,2^{k}r))|B(x,2^{k}r)|^{-s}r^{s}

for some
k\in\{1,...,M\}.

(3)

One term of the last sum is bigger or equal than the mean, i.e. (noticing that, by hypothesis,
m>\dim_{A}\mu
) for some
k\in\{M+1,...,D\}
and a constant
c>0
,

\displaystyle\frac{Cr^{s}}{D+1}

\displaystyle\leq r^{(\theta-1)(m-s)}\mu(B(x,2^{k}r))2^{-(k-1)m}

\displaystyle\leq r^{(\theta-1)(m-s)}\left(\frac{c2^{k}r}{r^{\theta}}\right)^{m}\mu(B(x,r^{\theta}))2^{-(k-1)m}

\displaystyle\leq\left(2c\right)^{m}r^{-\theta s}r^{s}\mu(B(x,r^{\theta}).

Then, since for all
\varepsilon>0
we have
\displaystyle 1\leq\frac{r^{-\varepsilon}}{D+1}
for all sufficiently small
r
, we have that for all
\varepsilon>0
there exist
C>0
and
r_{0}
such that for all
r\in(0,r_{0})
and all
x\in\operatorname{supp}\left(\mu\right)
there exists
\delta\in[r,r^{\theta}]
such that
C\delta^{s+\varepsilon}\leq\mu(x,\delta)
, which implies

\overline{\dim}_{\theta}\mu\leq s+\varepsilon

and so the result follows by letting
\varepsilon
tend to
0
.
∎

We now proceed to establish relations between the intermediate dimensions of
\mu
and the push-forward measures
\mu_{V}
. First of all, a useful lemma will we stated:

Lemma 5.7
.

(
[
3
, Lemma 5.3]
)
For all
m\in\{1,...,n-1\}
and
0\leq s<m
there exist positive constants
a:=a_{n,m}
and
b:=b_{n,m}
, depending only on
s
such that for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\theta\in(0,1]
and
0<r<1/2
,

a_{n,m}\int\widetilde{\phi}_{r,\theta}^{s}(P_{V}x)d\gamma_{n,m}(V)\leq\phi_{r,\theta}^{s,m}(x)\leq b_{n,m}\int\widetilde{\phi}_{r,\theta}^{s}(P_{V}x)d\gamma_{n,m}(V).

Given that projections are in fact Lipschitz functions, the next lemma can be directly applied to projections:

Lemma 5.8
.

Let
\mu
be a Borel measure in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(we always assume that
\mu
is finite, with compact support), and
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
a Lipschitz function. Then,

\overline{\dim}_{\theta}(\mu_{f})\leq\overline{\dim}_{\theta}(\mu)

with
\mu_{f}
the push-forward measure.

Proof.

First notice that (by Theorem 1.18,
[
12
]
), we have that

\operatorname{supp}\left(\mu_{f}\right)=f(\operatorname{supp}\left(\mu\right)),

and let
\lambda>1
such that
d(f(x),f(y))\leq\lambda d(x,y).
We also have:

f^{-1}(B(f(x),\lambda r))\supseteq B(x,r).

Let
s>\overline{\dim}_{\theta}\mu
, so that there exist
c
and
\delta_{0}>0
such that, for all
\delta<\delta_{0},x\in\operatorname{supp}\left(\mu\right)
, there exists
r>0
such that:

r\in[\delta^{1/\theta},\delta]\>,\>\mu(B(x,r))\geq cr^{s}.

We want to see that the same holds for
\mu_{f}
. For this, we show that there exist
c^{\prime}
and
\delta_{0}^{{}^{\prime}}
such that, for all
\delta<\delta_{0}^{{}^{\prime}}\>
and
\>y\in\operatorname{supp}\left(\mu_{f}\right)
, there exists
r>0
such that:

r\in[\delta^{1/\theta},\delta]\>,\>\mu_{f}(B(y,r))\geq c^{\prime}\cdot r^{s}.

Take
\delta^{{}^{\prime}}_{0},\delta
and
y
as follows:
\delta_{0}^{{}^{\prime}}=\lambda\delta_{0}
,
\delta<\delta_{0}^{{}^{\prime}}
, and
y\in\operatorname{supp}\left(\mu_{f}\right)=f(\operatorname{supp}\left(\mu\right))
. Then, we have that
y=f(x)
for some
x\in\operatorname{supp}\left(\mu\right)
and so, there exists
r\in\left[\frac{\delta^{1/\theta}}{\lambda^{1/\theta}},\frac{\delta}{\lambda}\right]
with
\mu(B(x,r))\geq cr^{s}.

Then,
\lambda r\in\left[\frac{\lambda}{\lambda^{1/\theta}}\delta^{1/\theta},\delta\right]
and also:

\mu_{f}(B(y,\lambda r))\geq\mu(B(x,r))\geq c\cdot r^{s}=\frac{c}{\lambda^{s}}(\lambda r)^{s},

so
\lambda r
satisfies the desired inequality. However,
\lambda\cdot r
might not fall in the ”appropriate” range, because
\frac{\lambda}{\lambda^{1/\theta}}\leq 1
. To solve this, we proceed by cases just like before:

•

\lambda r\geq\delta^{1/\theta}
: In this case, there is nothing to be done, because the same
r
works.

•

\lambda r\leq\delta^{1/\theta}
:
In this case, the ”
r
” we are looking for will be
\delta^{1/\theta}
:

\mu_{f}(B(\delta^{1/\theta}))\geq\mu_{f}(B(\lambda r))\geq\frac{c}{\lambda^{s}}(\lambda r)^{s}\geq\frac{c}{\lambda^{s/\theta}}(\delta^{1/\theta})^{s}

So, changing the constant
c
by
c^{\prime}=\min\{\frac{c}{\lambda^{s/\theta}},\frac{c}{\lambda^{s}}\}
, we have that
s>\overline{\dim}_{\theta}{\mu_{f}}
.
∎

Applying this lemma to the projections
P_{V}
for
V\in G(n,m)
, we obtain:

Corollary 5.9
.

\overline{\dim}_{\theta}{\mu_{V}}\leq\overline{\dim}_{\theta}\mu
for all
V\in G(n,m)
.

Now, in order to complete our analysis of projections we will prove that, under certain hypothesis,
\overline{\dim}_{\theta}(\mu_{V})=\overline{\dim}_{\theta}(\mu)
for almost all
V\in G(n,m)
. We need the following lemma:

Lemma 5.10
.

Let
\mu
be a Borel measure with compact support,
supp(\mu)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let
\theta\in(0,1]
,
m\in\{1,...,n-1\}
and
t\in(0,m)
.

If
\displaystyle\limsup_{r\to 0}\frac{\log C_{r,\theta}^{t,m}(\mu)}{-\log r}-t>0
then
\overline{\dim}_{\theta}\mu_{V}\geq t
for
\gamma_{n,m}
-almost all
V\in G(n,m).

Proof.

Taking
t>0
such that
\limsup\frac{\log C_{r,\theta}^{t,m}(\mu)}{-\log r}-t>0
and
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small such that
\limsup\frac{\log C_{r,\theta}^{t,m}(\mu)}{-\log r}-t>\varepsilon
, let
(r_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence tending to
0
such that
0<r_{k}\leq 2^{-k}
and

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\log C_{r_{k},\theta}^{t,m}(\mu)}{-\log r_{k}}=\limsup_{r\to 0}\frac{\log C_{r,\theta}^{t,m}(\mu)}{-\log r}.

Also, let
\lambda_{k}:=\displaystyle\inf_{x\in\operatorname{supp}\left(\mu\right)}\int\phi_{r_{k},\theta}^{t,m}(x-y)d\mu(y)=\left(C_{r_{k},\theta}^{t,m}\mu\right)^{-1}.
Then there exists a subsequence
\{r_{k_{j}}\}_{j}
such that
\lambda_{k_{j}}\leq r_{k_{j}}^{t+\varepsilon}
.

Then, by lemma
5.7
, for all
j
there exists
x_{j}
such that:

\iint\widetilde{\phi}_{r_{k_{j}},\theta}^{t}(P_{V}x_{j}-P_{V}y)d\gamma_{n,m}(V)d\mu(y)\leq a^{-1}(1+\varepsilon)\lambda_{k_{j}},

so

\iint\lambda_{k_{j}}^{-1}r_{k_{j}}^{\varepsilon}\widetilde{\phi}_{r_{k_{j}},\theta}^{t}(P_{V}x_{j}-P_{V}y)d\gamma_{n,m}(V)d\mu(y)\leq a^{-1}(1+\varepsilon)r_{k_{j}}^{\varepsilon}.

therefore, summing and using Fubini’s Theorem, we obtain the following:

\displaystyle\int\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\int\lambda_{k_{j}}^{-1}r_{k}^{\varepsilon}\widetilde{\phi}_{r_{k_{j}},\theta}^{t}(P_{V}x_{j}-P_{V}y)d\mu(y)d\gamma_{n,m}(V)\leq a^{-1}(1+\varepsilon)\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}r_{k_{j}}^{\varepsilon}<\infty

since
r_{k_{j}}^{\varepsilon}\leq 2^{-k_{j}\varepsilon}
. Then, for almost all
V
there exists
M_{V}
such that

\int\widetilde{\phi}_{r_{k_{j}},\theta}^{t}(v_{j}-u)d\mu_{V}(u)=\int\widetilde{\phi}_{r_{k_{j}},\theta}^{t}(P_{V}x_{j}-P_{V}y)d\mu(y)<M_{V}\lambda_{k_{j}}r_{k_{j}}^{-\varepsilon}

where
v_{j}=P_{V}x_{j}
. Then,

\displaystyle\inf_{v\in\operatorname{supp}\left(\mu_{V}\right)}\int\widetilde{\phi}_{r_{k_{j}},\theta}^{t}(v-u)d\mu_{V}(u)\leq M_{V}r_{k_{j}}^{t+\varepsilon}r_{k_{j}}^{-\varepsilon}=M_{V}\cdot r_{k_{j}}^{t}.

By lemma
5.5
, we conclude that

t\leq\overline{\dim}_{\theta}\mu_{V},

for almost all
V
and the result follows.
∎

Theorem 5.11
.

Let
\mu
be a finite Borel measure supported on a compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let
m\in\{1,...,n-1\}
and suppose that
\dim_{A}\mu<m
. Then

\overline{\dim}_{\theta}\mu_{V}\leq\left\{\text{ the unique $s\in[0,m]$ such that $\limsup_{r\to 0}\frac{\log C_{r,\theta}^{s,m}(\mu)}{-\log r}-s=0$}\right\}=\overline{\dim}_{\theta}\mu

with an equality for
\gamma_{n,m}-
almost all
V\in G(n,m).

Proof.

The theorem can be immediately deduced combining Theorem
5.6
and Lemma
5.10
.
∎

Remark 5.12
.

In
[
1
, Corollary 3.7]
, the authors prove that for a compact set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, if
m\geq\dim_{qA}E
, then
\dim_{\theta}E=\dim_{\theta}P_{V}E
for
\gamma_{n,m}
-almost every
V\in G(n,m)
, where
\dim_{qA}
means
quassi Assouad dimension
(see
[
8
]
and
[
11
]
). Based on this result, an interesting question arises: can we replace
\dim_{A}
with
\dim_{qA}
in Theorem
5.11
?

Moreover, the results proven in this section can also be used to show that, with slightly different hypothesis, one can guarantee that the dimensions
\overline{\dim}_{\theta}{\mu_{V}}
are constant for
\gamma_{n,m}-
almost all
V\in G(n,m).
In order to show this, the next lemma will be useful:

Lemma 5.13
.

Let
\mu
be a finite Borel measure with compact support in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and let
m\in\{1,...,n-1\}
. If
\sup_{V\in G(n,m)}\dim_{A}\mu_{V}<m
then

\limsup_{r\to 0}\frac{\log C_{r,\theta}^{s,m}(\mu)}{-\log r}-s\geq 0.

for all
s\in[0,\overline{\dim}_{\theta}\mu_{V}]
and all
V\in G(n,m)
.

Proof.

For all
x\in\operatorname{supp}\left(\mu\right)
and
V\in G(n,m)
, because
\phi_{r,\theta}^{s,m}
is decreasing in
|x|
we have:

\displaystyle\int\phi_{r,\theta}^{s,m}(x-y)d\mu(y)

\displaystyle\leq\int\phi_{r,\theta}^{s,m}(P_{V}x-P_{V}y)d\mu(y)

\displaystyle=\int\phi_{r,\theta}^{s,m}(P_{V}x-u)d\mu_{V}(u)

and therefore,

\inf_{x\in\operatorname{supp}\left(\mu\right)}\int\phi_{r,\theta}^{s,m}(x-y)d\mu(y)\leq\inf_{v\in\operatorname{supp}\left(\mu\right)_{V}}\int\phi_{r,\theta}^{s,m}(v-u)d\mu_{V}(u)

which implies that

\limsup_{r\to 0}\frac{\log C_{r,\theta}^{s,m}(\mu)}{-\log r}-s\geq\limsup_{r\to 0}\frac{\log C_{r,\theta}^{s,m}(\mu_{V})}{-\log r}-s,

and since
m>\dim_{A}\mu_{V}
by hypothesis, using Theorem
5.6
we have that the right-hand side of the inequality is bigger than
0
for all
s\in[0,\overline{\dim}_{\theta}\mu_{V}]
and the result follows.
∎

Combining the previous lemma with Lemma
5.10
, we obtain the following Corollary.

Corollary 5.14
.

Let
\mu
be a finite Borel measure supported on a compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let
m\in\{1,...,n-1\}
and suppose that
\sup_{V}\dim_{A}\mu_{V}<m
. then

\overline{\dim}_{\theta}\mu_{V}\leq\left\{\text{ the unique $s\in[0,m]$ such that $\limsup_{r\to 0}\frac{\log C_{r,\theta}^{s,m}(\mu)}{-\log r}-s=0$}\right\}

with an equality for
\gamma_{n,m}-
almost all
V\in G(n,m).

Remark 5.15
.

The existence of such
s
can be deduced by Lemmas
5.13
and
5.10
. Also, notice
that it is not necessary in this last corollary that
\dim_{A}\mu<m
, so the right hand expression might not be equal to
\overline{\dim}_{\theta}\mu
. However, such
s
still exists and the dimensions
\overline{\dim}_{\theta}{\mu_{V}}
are equal for
\gamma_{n,m}-
almost all
V\in G(n,m).

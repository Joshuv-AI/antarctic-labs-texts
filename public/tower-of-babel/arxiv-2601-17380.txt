Hausdorff characterizations of first countable T 1 T_{1} spaces via fixed point theorems
Detelina Kamburova

Abstract
We introduce two notions of a contractive orbit of a set-valued map defined in a first countable space. The first defines the contraction with respect to the topology of the underlying space while the second defines the contraction with respect to a generalized distance function. We characterize the Hausdorff property of first countable T 1 T_{1} spaces via fixed point theorems for set-valued maps with a contractive orbit satisfying some additional assumptions. As an application, we derive a sufficient condition for a function to attain a strong minimum and generalize Cantor’s intersection theorem for a sequence of closed nested sets with diameters converging to 0. Keywords: Fixed point theorem, set-valued map, contractive orbit, first countable space, Hausdorff space, premetric space, strong minimum. 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 54H25, 54F65, 54E25, 49J27

Introduction

The Banach contraction principle states that in a complete metric space every contraction mapping has a unique fixed point. Moreover, each sequence of successive iterates converges to this fixed point. The existence of an accumulation point of a given sequence is typically ensured by the completeness of the underlying space. An extensive study of fixed point results that imply completeness in metric and generalized metric spaces can be found in
[
1
]
.

A definition of a contraction in first countable Hausdorff spaces was given by Kupka in
[
10
]
. In this paper, he also derives a condition ensuring that a given sequence has an accumulation point, without imposing any completeness condition on the underlying space, see Lemma 1 in
[
10
]
. Kupka continues his work in
[
11
]
for set-valued maps (feebly topologically contractive maps, f-t-contracive) with closed graphs that "shrink" to a unique fixed point, i.e. a strict fixed point. In the single-valued case, f-t-contractive maps also appear as a weak topological contraction in
[
14
]
. In this paper, it is proved that if one does not require uniqueness of the fixed point of a single-valued (continuous or closed) weak topological contraction, it suffices to consider just two consecutive terms of a given orbit being arbitrarily close to ensure the existence of a fixed point (Theorem 3 and Theorem 6 in
[
14
]
). Other results concerning fixed point theorems for topological contractions can be found in
[
13
]
(in compact
T_{1}
spaces).

The LOEV (Long Orbit or Empty Value) principle has been formulated and proved in
[
5
]
in complete metric spaces
(X,d)
. It concerns a set-valued map
S:X\rightrightarrows X
that "shrinks" to a point
\bar{x}
outside of the domain of

S(\bar{x})=\varnothing

[

]
,
[
4
]
,
[
7
]

Its equivalence to
\Sigma

[
3
]
and
[

.

Another key property of the space used in the proof of the LOEV principle is that the underlying space is Hausdorff. The aim of this paper is to generalize the LOEV statement in first countable Hausdorff spaces and to prove its equivalence to the Hausdorffness of the underling space. In the present paper we do not impose any closed-graph assumptions on the set-valued maps we consider, nor compactness of the underlying space. Instead, we assume the maps we work with satisfy the so-called property
(\bar{\star})
(see Section 2, Definition
2.1
) and property
(\bar{\bar{\star}})
(see Section 3, Definition
3.5
), concepts inherited from LOEV. Since the two properties are defined on sequences, the underlying space we work on is first countable.

We start with more general settings and proceed to more concrete ones. In Section 2, we consider a first countable space
(X,\tau)
with no generalized metric defined on it. The definition of a
\tau
-contractive orbit of a set-valued map is inspired by the formulations of Theorems 3 and 6 in
[
14

In Section 3, we consider a first countable space with a generalized premetric defined on it. We call the space a p-space. The distance function does not satisfy the symmetry property, nor the triangle inequality. We prove that the properties of the so-defined generalized metric entail the underlying space must be a
T_{1}
space. Definition
3.5

Finally, in Section 4, we show that in metric case the two notions of contractive orbits coincide. We also рrоvide an answer to an open (to the best of our knowledge) question formulated in

10

if the underlying space is metric and we have a topological contraction (as defined in
[
10

Hausdorff characterization in general topological spaces

Given a set-valued map
S:X\rightrightarrows X

Definition 2.1

Let

-orbit

f^{i}(x)\subseteq\bar{B}(\bar{x},\varepsilon)
, where
\bar{B}(\bar{x},\varepsilon):=\{y\in X:d(\bar{x},y)\leq\varepsilon\}
.

We will use the following theorem to determine the conditions under which a t-contraction is a Banach contraction in a metric space.

Theorem 4.3
.

[
12
]

Let
(X,d)
be a metric space and
f:X\rightarrow X
be a mapping. Then, there exists a metric
\bar{d}
topologically equivalent to
d
on
X
such that
f
is a Banach contraction under
\bar{d}
with a unique fixed point
\bar{x}
if and only if,
f
is continuous and both
(A1)
and
(A2)
hold.

Theorem 4.4
.

[
10
]

Let
(X,\tau)
be a first countable Hausdorff space. Let
f:X\rightarrow X
be a continuous, t-contractive mapping. Then
f
has a unique fixed point, say
\bar{x}\in X
and every infinite
f
-orbit
\{f^{i}(x)\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
has a convergent to
\bar{x}
subsequence
\{f^{i_{k}}(x)\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
, i.e., for all
x\in X

f^{i_{k}}(x)\rightarrow\bar{x}
, as
k\rightarrow\infty
.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
(X,d)
be a metric space and
\tau
is the topology generated by
d
. Suppose the assumptions of Theorem
4.4
hold true for a mapping
f:X\rightarrow X
. Moreover, there exists a neighbourhood
O
of
\bar{x}
such that for every open cover
\gamma
of
X
there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
a,b\in O
and all
i\geq n
there exists
U\in\gamma
such that
f^{i}(a)\in U
,
f^{i}(b)\in U
. Then there exists a metric
\bar{d}
topologically equivalent to
d
on
X
such that
f
is a Banach contraction under
\bar{d}
with a unique fixed point
\bar{x}
.

We will prove that assumptions for the mapping
f
in Theorem
4.3
hold true. First, note that
f
is continuous (by an assumption in Theorem
4.4
). We will have to prove (A1) and (A2).

Consider the open cover
\gamma:=\cup_{x\in X}B(x,\varepsilon/2)
, where
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrary and
B(x,\varepsilon/2):=\{y\in X:d(x,y)<\varepsilon/2\}
.

Proof of (A1). Take an arbitrary
a\in X
and
b=\bar{x}
. So, there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
i\geq n
there exists
x_{i}\in X
such that
f^{i}(a)\in B(x_{i},\varepsilon/2)
and
\bar{x}=f^{i}(\bar{x})\in B(x_{i},\varepsilon/2)
. From the triangle inequality it follows

d(f^{i}(a),\bar{x})\leq d(f^{i}(a),x_{i})+d(x_{i},\bar{x})<\varepsilon.

Therefore
f^{i}(a)\rightarrow\bar{x}
as
i\rightarrow\infty
for all
a\in X
.

Proof of (A2). There exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
i\geq n
, for all
a\in O
and
b=\bar{x}\in O
there exists
x_{i}\in X
such that
f^{i}(a)\in B(x_{i},\varepsilon/2)
and
\bar{x}\in B(x_{i},\varepsilon/2)
. As in the proof of (A1)

d(f^{i}(a),\bar{x})<\varepsilon.

\square

1.
Introduction

For a compact, convex
K\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
, the affine surface area is defined as

(1)

\int_{\bd K}\kappa(K,x)^{\frac{1}{n+2}}\,\mathrm{d}x,

where
\kappa(K,x)
is the generalized Gaussian curvature of
K
at
x\in\bd K
, the boundary of
K
, and
\,\mathrm{d}x
stands for integration with respect to the
n
-dimensional Hausdorff measure on the boundary of
K
. Let
{\mathcal{K}}^{n+1}
denote the space of convex bodies (non-empty, compact convex sets) in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
equipped with the topology induced by the Hausdorff metric. Since affine surface area vanishes on polytopes, it is not continuous on
{\mathcal{K}}^{n+1}
. However, as shown by Lutwak
[
17
]
, affine surface area is an upper semicontinuous functional on
{\mathcal{K}}^{n+1}
. The upper semicontinuity of a wider class of curvature functionals was established in
[
13
,
18
]
.

Let
D
be a bounded convex domain of
\mathbb{R}^{n}

and
v\in C^{2}(D)
convex. The affine surface area of the epi-graph of
v
is

(2)

\displaystyle\int_{D}\det(\Hess v(x))^{\frac{1}{n+2}}\,\mathrm{d}x,

where
\Hess v(x)
is the Hessian matrix of
v
at
x
and
\,\mathrm{d}x
stands for integration with respect to
n
-dimensional
Lebesgue measure.
By a classical theorem of Aleksandrov (see
[
9
, Theorem 6.4.1]
),
a convex function
v
is twice differentiable almost everywhere, and the integral in (
2
) is well-defined (but possibly infinite).

In [
24
, Lemma 6.4], Trudinger and Wang show that the functional defined in (
2
) is upper semicontinuous for a given bounded convex domain
D
with respect to locally uniform convergence, using that
\det(\Hess v)
is the density of the absolutely continuous part of the Monge–Ampère measure of
v
and the weak
∗
-continuity of Monge–Ampère measures (see Section
2
). For further definitions of functional affine surface area, see, for example,
[
1
,
3
,
10
,
23
]
.

This paper aims to generalize this result to a specific class of extended real-valued convex functions without restricting to a bounded set
D
. We also extend the result to more general functionals. The main motivation comes from the quest for classification results of valuations on convex functions. Such classification results have been recently established for continuous valuations on convex functions (see, for example,
[
4
,
6
]
). On convex bodies, they are classical for continuous valuations (see
[
22
, Chapter 6]
) and have also been established for upper semicontinuous valuations in certain cases (see
[
12
,
11
,
15
,
16
]
).

Let
{\mbox{\rm Conv}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}
be the space of
extended real-valued functions

u\colon{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to(-\infty,\infty]
that are convex and lower semicontinuous and such that
u\not\equiv\infty
.
The (effective) domain of
u
is
\dom(u)=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:u(x)<\infty\}
and we write
\interior
for interior.
We consider the subspace

(3)

{\mbox{\rm Conv}_{{\rm ld}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}=\{u\in{\mbox{\rm Conv}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\colon\dom(u)\mbox{ is compact},\,u\mbox{ is Lipschitz on $\interior\dom(u)$}\}.

We use epi-convergence on
{\mbox{\rm Conv}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}
and its subspaces, and we also use so-called
\tau
-convergence on
{\mbox{\rm Conv}_{{\rm ld}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}
(see Section
2
).

First, we state the following result, which proves in particular an upper semicontinuity-property of the functional defined in (
2
) on
{\mbox{\rm Conv}_{{\rm ld}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}
. Let
{\mbox{\rm Conc}([0,\infty))}
be the set of concave functions
\zeta\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
such that
\lim_{t\to 0}\zeta(t)=0
and
\lim_{t\to\infty}\zeta(t)/t=0
.

Theorem 1
.

For
\zeta\in{\mbox{\rm Conc}([0,\infty))}
and
u\in{\mbox{\rm Conv}_{{\rm ld}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}
,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Z}(u)=\int_{\dom(u)}\zeta(\det(\Hess u(x)))\,\mathrm{d}x

is finite, and

(4)

\operatorname{Z}(u)\geq\limsup_{k\to\infty}\operatorname{Z}(u_{k})

for every sequence of functions
u_{k}
in
{\mbox{\rm Conv}_{{\rm ld}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}
with uniformly bounded Lipschitz constants on their domains
that epi-converges to
u\in{\mbox{\rm Conv}_{{\rm ld}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}
.

We remark that the restriction to sequences with uniformly bounded Lipschitz constants is necessary (see Example
1
).

We will prove Theorem
1
and its generalizations in the dual setting obtained by using the Legendre transform (see Theorem
8
). We will show that in the dual setting, we have to look at convex functions
v\colon{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}
whose Monge–Ampère measures have compact supports and at sequences
v_{k}:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}
with uniformly bounded supports of their Monge–Ampère measures. We will establish the results in greater generality for densities of the absolutely continuous part of Radon measures associated to functions on metric spaces in Theorem
4
and will also provide results on lower semicontinuity in Theorem
6
.

2.
Preliminaries

We collect results on Radon measures and on convex functions.

2.1.
Radon Measures on Metric Spaces

Let
(X,\lambda)
be a metric space with a (non-negative)
\sigma
-finite
Radon measure
\lambda
. We will consider
\sigma
-finite Radon measures depending on certain functions on
X
and use the Radon–Nikodým and Lebesgue decomposition theorems (see, for example, [
9
, Theorems 1.6.2 and 1.6.3]).

Let
{\mathcal{F}}(X)
be a space of functions
v\colon X\to{\mathbb{R}}
equipped with a notion of convergence.
For
v\in{\mathcal{F}}(X)
, let
\Phi(v;\cdot)
be a Radon measure depending on
v
defined on Borel sets of
X
such that we have weak
∗
-star convergence of
\Phi(v_{j};\cdot)
to
\Phi(v;\cdot)
as the sequence
v_{j}
converges to
v\in{\mathcal{F}}(X)
. Here, we say that
\Phi(v_{k};\cdot)
is weak
∗
-convergent to
\Phi(v;\cdot)
if for any sequence
v_{k}
converging in
{\mathcal{F}}(X)
to
v
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\int_{X}\beta(x)\,\mathrm{d}\Phi(v_{k};x)=\int_{X}\beta(x)\,\mathrm{d}\Phi(v;x)

for every function
\beta\in C_{c}(X)
, the set of continuous functions with compact support on
X
. We need the following simple result. For a sequence of functions
v_{k}\in{\mathcal{F}}(X)
converging to
v
, the weak
∗
-convergent of
\Phi(v_{k};\cdot)
to
\Phi(v;\cdot)
implies that

(5)

\displaystyle\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\Phi(v_{k};C)\leq\Phi(v;C)

for every compact set
C\subset X
(cf. [
9
, Theorem 1.9.1]).

By the Lebesgue decomposition theorem, the measure
\Phi(v;\cdot)
can be decomposed into measures absolutely continuous and singular with respect to
\lambda
on
X
, say
\Phi(v;\cdot)=\Phi^{a}(v;\cdot)+\Phi^{s}(v;\cdot)
. By the Radon–Nikodým theorem, we have for the absolutely continuous part

\displaystyle\Phi^{a}(v;A)=\int_{A}\phi(v;x)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x),

where
A\subseteq X
is a
\lambda
-measurable set and
\phi(v;\cdot)
is
\lambda
-measurable, while for the singular part, there is a set
C_{0}\subset X
such that
\lambda(C_{0})=0
and

\displaystyle\Phi^{s}(v;A\setminus C_{0})=0,

for every
\lambda
-measurable set
A\subseteq X
.

2.2.
Convex Functions

Let
\Conv(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R})
denote the space of finite-valued, convex functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and we also consider the space of Lipschitz functions

\displaystyle\lip(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R})=\{v\in\Conv(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R}):v\ \text{is Lipschitz}\}

and the space

\CD(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\{u\in\Conv(\mathbb{R}^{n}):\dom(u)\text{ is bounded}\}.

We say that a sequence
u_{k}\in\Conv(\mathbb{R}^{n})
or any of its subspaces epi-converges to
u\in\Conv(\mathbb{R}^{n})
if
for every sequence
x_{k}
that converges to
x
, we have

u(x)\leq\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}u_{k}(x_{k}),

and there exists a sequence
x_{k}
that converges to
x
such that

u(x)=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}u_{k}(x_{k}).

Epi-convergence is directly related to uniform convergence since
u_{k}
epi-converges to
u
if and only if
u_{k}
converges uniformly to
u
on every compact set that does not
contain a boundary point of
\dom(u)
(see [
20
, Theorem 7.17]). In this paper, we also use the following notion of convergence: For
u,u_{k}\in{\mbox{\rm Conv}_{{\rm ld}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
we say that
u_{k}\xrightarrow{\tau}u
if

(i)

u_{k}
epi-converges to
u
;

(ii)

the Lipschitz constants of
u_{k}
are uniformly bounded on
\interior\dom(u)

by some constant that does not depend on
k
.

Condition (ii) for the space of real-valued Lipschitz continuous maps defined on the unit sphere
S^{n-1}

has been considered in
[
7
,
8
]
with the additional requirement of uniform convergence on the unit sphere.

Example 1
.

The assumption of uniformly bounded Lipschitz constants is necessary for Theorem
1
to hold. For
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, consider

u_{k}(x)=k\sum_{i=1}^{n}\langle x,x\rangle+\I_{B^{n}}(x),

where
B^{n}
is the
n
-dimensional Euclidean unit ball,
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is the inner product on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, and
\I_{B^{n}}(x)=0
if
x\in B^{n}
and
\infty
otherwise. Then
u_{k}
epi-converges to the function
\I_{\{0\}}
, which is
\infty
for every nonzero
x
. This shows that Theorem
1
fails if we replace
\tau
-convergence by epi-convergence since
\operatorname{Z}
is, in general, not upper semicontinuous with respect to epi-convergence.

For
u\in{\mbox{\rm Conv}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}
,
let
\partial u(x)
be the subdifferential of
u
at
x\in\dom(u)
,

\partial u(x)=\{y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:u(z)\geq u(x)+\langle y,z-x\rangle\text{ for all }z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\}

and set
\partial u(x)=\emptyset
for
x\not\in\dom(u)
.
Given a subset
A\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, the image of
A
through the subdifferential of
u
is defined as

\partial u(A)=\bigcup\nolimits_{x\in A}\partial u(x).

For
v\in{\mbox{\rm Conv}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};{\mathbb{R}})}
, the Monge–Ampère measure is given by

(6)

\displaystyle\MA(v;B)=V_{n}(\partial v(B))

for every Borel set
B\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, where
V_{n}
is the
n
-dimensional volume. It is a Radon measure on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.

Let
K\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
be a non-empty, closed convex set with support function
h_{K}:{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}\to(-\infty,\infty]
, that is,

h_{K}(x,x_{n+1})=\sup\big(\langle(x,x_{n+1}),(y,y_{n+1})\rangle\colon(y,y_{n+1})\in K\big)

for
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
x_{n+1}\in{\mathbb{R}}
, where the inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is taken in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
.
Note that if
K\in{\mathcal{K}}^{n+1}
, we have
h_{K}(\cdot,-1)\in\Conv(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R})
.
Let

(7)

\Conv_{\MA}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R})=\{h_{K}(\cdot,-1)\in\Conv(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R})\colon K\in{\mathcal{K}}^{n+1},\,\supp(\MA(h_{K}(\cdot,-1);\cdot))\text{ is compact}\},

where
\supp
denotes
the support of a measure.
We equip
\Conv_{\MA}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R})
with the following notion of convergence: we say that a sequence
v_{k}
in
\Conv_{\MA}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R})
is
\tau^{*}
-convergent to
v\in\Conv_{\MA}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R})
if

(i)

v_{k}
epi-converges to
v
;

(ii)

there exists a compact set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
containing the supports of
\MA(u;\cdot)
and
\MA(u_{k};\cdot)
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

In this case we write
v_{k}\xrightarrow{\tau^{*}}v
. The notation will be justified in Lemma
3
.

3.
The Dual Space

For
u\in\Conv(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, the Legendre transform or convex conjugate of
u
is
u^{*}:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to(-\infty,\infty]
given as

(8)

\displaystyle u^{*}(y)=\sup(\langle x,y\rangle-u(x)\colon x\in\mathbb{R}^{n})

for
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
If
u\in\CD(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then
u^{*}\in\Conv(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R})
(see [
20
), Theorem 11.8], and
u^{**}=u
(see [
22
, Theorem 1.6.13]). Note that convex conjugation is continuous w.r.t. epi-convergence,

(9)

u_{k}\text{ is epi-convergent to }u\,\,\Leftrightarrow\,\,u^{*}_{k}\text{ is epi-convergent to }u^{*}

for
u_{k},u\in{\mbox{\rm Conv}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}
(see
[
20
, Theorem 11.43]
).

The epigraph of
u\in{\mbox{\rm Conv}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}
is the non-empty, closed, convex set

(10)

\epi(u)=\{(x,x_{n+1})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}:x_{n+1}\geq u(x)\}.

Hence,

(11)

u^{*}(y)=\sup(\langle(y,-1),(x,u(x))\rangle\colon x\in\mathbb{R}^{n})=h_{\epi(u)}(y,-1).

If
u
has compact domain, then there is
K\in{\mathcal{K}}^{n+1}
such that

h_{K}(\cdot,-1)=h_{\epi(u)}(\cdot,-1),

which can be chosen as the intersection of
\epi(u)
and
\{x_{n+1}\leq\max_{x\in\dom(u)}u(x)\}
. Hence, the Legendre transform of a function in
{\mbox{\rm Conv}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}
with compact domain is of the form

(12)

h_{K}(\cdot,-1)\,\text{ with }\,K\in{\mathcal{K}}^{n+1}.

For the space of Legendre transforms of functions from
{\mbox{\rm Conv}_{{\rm ld}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}
, we write

(13)

{\mbox{\rm Conv}^{*}_{{\rm ld}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}=\{u^{*}:u\in{\mbox{\rm Conv}_{{\rm ld}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\}

and use corresponding notation for further spaces. By [
19
, Corollary 13.3.3],

(14)

\displaystyle\lipd(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R})=\CD(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Using this in its dual form and (
12
), we obtain

(15)

\Conv_{\MA}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R})\subset\lip(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R}).

By [
19
, Corollary 23.5.1], we have the following relationship

(16)

\displaystyle p\in\partial u(x)\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad x\in\partial u(p)

for
u\in\Conv(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. If both
u
and
u^{*}
are differentiable, then we can rewrite (
16
) as

\nabla u^{*}(\nabla u(p))=p.

For
u
and
u^{*}
twice differentiable and
\det(\Hess u(p))>0
, differentiating gives

\displaystyle\det(\Hess u^{*}(\nabla u(p)))=\dfrac{1}{\det(\Hess u(p))}.

Thus, if
u\in{\mbox{\rm Conv}_{{\rm ld}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}
is such that
\det(\Hess u(x))>0
for all
x\in\dom(u)
and
\zeta\in{\mbox{\rm Conc}([0,\infty))}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\dom(u)}\zeta(\det(\Hess u(x)))\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\dom(u)}\zeta\left(\dfrac{1}{\det(\Hess u^{*}(\nabla u(x)))}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

(17)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\zeta\left(\dfrac{1}{\det(\Hess u^{*}(y))}\right)\det(\Hess u^{*}(y))\,\mathrm{d}y

(18)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\tilde{\zeta}(\det(\Hess u^{*}(x)))\,\mathrm{d}x,

where
\tilde{\zeta}(t)=\zeta(1/t)t
for
t\geq 0
. Note that we also have
\tilde{\zeta}\in{\mbox{\rm Conc}([0,\infty))}
. Using (
16
), we observe that the last equality holds for every
u\in{\mbox{\rm Conv}_{{\rm ld}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}
. Indeed, let
u\in{\mbox{\rm Conv}_{{\rm ld}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}
and let
U
be the set of points
x\in\dom(u)
such that
\det(\Hess u(x))=0
. By assumption,
U
also contains all points where
u
is not twice differentiable,
since we set
\det(\Hess u(x))=0
at such points. If

U_{1}\subset U
is the set of points where
u
is not twice differentiable, then by Aleksandrov’s theorem,
V_{n}(U_{1})=0
. If
D=\partial u(U_{1})
, we get by (
6
) that
\MA(u^{*};D)=V_{n}(U_{1})=0
, which implies
\MA^{a}(u^{*};D)=0
. Now, if there exists a convex set
U_{2}\subset U
such that
\det(\Hess u(x))=0
for all
x\in U_{2}
and
V_{n}(U_{2})>0
, then there exists a vector
p\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
p\in\partial u(U_{2})
and by (
16
) we have
U_{2}\subset\partial u^{*}(p)
, that is,
u^{*}
is not twice differentiable at
p
. Using (
17
), we conclude that

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\dom(u)}\zeta(\det(\Hess u(x)))\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{U}\zeta(\det(\Hess u(x)))\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\dom(u)\setminus U}\zeta(\det(\Hess u(x)))\,\mathrm{d}x

(19)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\tilde{\zeta}(\det(\Hess u^{*}(x)))\,\mathrm{d}x.

Let
u_{k},u\in{\mbox{\rm Conv}_{{\rm ld}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}
.
We aim to show that if the functions
u_{k},u\in{\mbox{\rm Conv}_{{\rm ld}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}
and
u_{k}\xrightarrow{\tau}u
, then the functions
u_{k}^{*},u^{*}\in\Conv_{\MA}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R})
and
u_{k}^{*}\xrightarrow{\tau^{*}}u^{*}
. Additionally, we will prove the reverse implication. To do this, we will use the following result (see, for example, [
2
, Theorem 3.61]).

Lemma 2
.

A function
u\in\Conv(\mathbb{R}^{n})
has a Lipschitz constant
L
in the interior of its domain if and only if
\|p\|\leq L
for every
p\in\partial u(x)
, where
x\in\interior\dom(u)
.

Here,
\|\cdot\|
denotes the Euclidean norm on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. We now prove our main lemma.

Lemma 3
.

A sequence
u_{k}
in
{\mbox{\rm Conv}_{{\rm ld}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}
is
\tau
-convergent to
u\in{\mbox{\rm Conv}_{{\rm ld}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}
if and only if
u_{k}^{*}
and
u^{*}
belong to

\Conv_{\MA}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R})
and
u_{k}^{*}
is
\tau^{*}
-convergent to
u^{*}
.

Proof.

First, we show that for
\Conv^{*}_{\MA}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R})
, defined similar to (
13
),

(20)

\Conv^{*}_{\MA}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R})\subset{\mbox{\rm Conv}_{{\rm ld}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}.

Let
u\in\Conv^{*}_{\MA}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R})
. By (
15
) and (
14
), we see that
u\in\CD(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Since
u
is the Legendre transform of
h_{K}(\cdot,-1)
for some
K\in{\mathcal{K}}^{n+1}
, the domain of
u
is compact. If

\interior\dom(u)
is empty, then we do not need the Lipschitz condition on
u
and hence
u\in{\mbox{\rm Conv}_{{\rm ld}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}
.
For
\interior\dom(u)
non-empty, assume, for the sake of contradiction, that

u
is not Lipschitz on
\interior\dom(u)
. By Lemma
2
, for every constant
M>0
, there exists
z\in\partial u(\interior\dom(u))
such that
\|z\|>M
. Using the continuity and convexity of
u
, we deduce that for every sufficiently small neighborhood
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
of
z
, there exists a set
U\subset\interior\dom(u)
with positive Lebesgue measure such that

D\subset\partial u(U)
. By (
16
),

D\subset\partial u(U)\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad U\subset\partial u^{*}(D),

which implies that
\MA(u^{*};D)>0
. Since this holds for every sufficiently small neighborhood of
z
, we obtain that
z\in\supp(\MA(u^{*};\cdot))
. Therefore, we have
\supp(\MA(u^{*};\cdot))\not\subset MB^{n}
. Since
M>0

was arbitrary, this leads to a contradiction to
u^{*}\in\Conv_{\MA}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R})
.
Hence, we conclude that
u
is Lipschitz on
\interior\dom(u)
and, thus,
u\in{\mbox{\rm Conv}_{{\rm ld}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}
.

Now, assume that
u_{k}^{*},u^{*}\in\Conv_{\MA}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R})
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
and that
u_{k}^{*}\xrightarrow{\tau^{*}}u^{*}
as
k\to\infty
. From (
20
), it follows that
u_{k},u\in{\mbox{\rm Conv}_{{\rm ld}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
MB^{n}
be the ball containing the support of the Monge–Ampère measures of
u_{k}^{*}
and
u^{*}
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
. We have seen that

\displaystyle\partial u_{k}(\interior\dom(u_{k}))\not\subset MB^{n}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\supp(\MA(u_{k}^{*};\cdot))\not\subset MB^{n}.

Consequently, we have
\partial u_{k}(\interior\dom(u_{k})),\partial u(\interior\dom(u))\subseteq MB^{n}
. By Lemma
2
, this is equivalent to saying that the Lipschitz constants of
u_{k}
and
u
are bounded by
M
. Using (
9
), we conclude that
u_{k}\xrightarrow{\tau}u
.

For the converse, assume that
u\in{\mbox{\rm Conv}_{{\rm ld}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}
with Lipschitz constant
L_{u}
.
By
[
19
, p. 227]
, we have

(21)

\MA(u^{*};{\mathbb{R}}^{n})=V_{n}(\partial u^{*}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}))=V_{n}(\dom(u)).

If
V_{n}(\dom(u))=0
, this implies that
\supp(\MA(u^{*};\cdot))
is compact.
For the case
V_{n}(\dom(u))>0
,
set

D=\partial u(\interior\dom(u))
,
and note that, by
[
19
, p. 227]
, we have

\MA(u^{*};D)=V_{n}(\dom(u)).

Hence, it follows from (
21
) that
\MA(u^{*};\cdot)
is supported on
D
. By Lemma
2
, we have
D\subseteq L_{u}B^{n}
, which implies that

(22)

\supp(\MA(u^{*};\cdot))\subseteq L_{u}B^{n}.

Combined with the fact that
u
has compact domain and (
12
), this implies that
u^{*}\in\Conv_{\MA}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R})
. Hence,

{\mbox{\rm Conv}^{*}_{{\rm ld}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}=\Conv_{\MA}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R})
.

To conclude the proof, it remains to show that if
u_{k},u\in{\mbox{\rm Conv}_{{\rm ld}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
u_{k}\xrightarrow{\tau}u
as
k\to\infty
, then
u_{k}^{*}\xrightarrow{\tau^{*}}u^{*}
. Let
M>0
be such that the Lipschitz constants of
u_{k}
and
u
are bounded by
M
. By (
22
), we know that

\supp(\MA(u^{*};\cdot))\subset L_{u}B^{n}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\supp(\MA(u_{k}^{*};\cdot))\subset L_{u_{k}}B^{n},

where
L_{u}
and
L_{u_{k}}
are the Lipschitz constants of
u
and
u_{k}
, respectively. Since

L_{u_{k}},L_{u}\leq M

for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we conclude that

\supp(\MA(u^{*};\cdot)),\,\supp(\MA(u_{k}^{*};\cdot))\subset MB^{n},

and therefore, by (
9
), we have
u_{k}^{*}\xrightarrow{\tau^{*}}u^{*}
.
This completes the proof.
∎

4.
Upper and Lower Semicontinuity

Let
(X,\lambda)
be a Hausdorff topological space,
{\mathcal{F}}(X)
a family of real-valued functions on
X
equipped with a notion of convergence, and
\Phi(v;\cdot)
a family of
\sigma
-finite Radon measures depending weak
∗
-continuously on the functions
v\in{\mathcal{F}}(X)
.
Let
{\mathcal{F}}_{\Phi}(X)
denote the set of functions
v\in{\mathcal{F}}(X)
such that the support of
\Phi(v;\cdot)
is compact. We equip
X\times{\mathbb{R}}
with the product topology. The following result is a functional version of Theorem 2 in
[
13
]
.

Theorem 4
.

For
\zeta\in{\mbox{\rm Conc}([0,\infty))}
and non-negative
\omega\in C(X\times\mathbb{R})
,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Z}(v)=\int_{X}\zeta(\phi(v;x))\,\omega(x,v(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)

is finite for every
v\in{\mathcal{F}}_{\Phi}(X)\cap C(X)
, and

(23)

\operatorname{Z}(v)\geq\limsup_{k\to\infty}\operatorname{Z}(v_{k})

for every sequence of functions
v_{k}
in
{\mathcal{F}}_{\Phi}(X)\cap C(X)
with uniformly bounded supports
\supp(\Phi(v_{k};\cdot))

that converges to
v\in{\mathcal{F}}_{\Phi}(X)\cap C(X)
.

Hence we can say that
\operatorname{Z}
is
\tau
-upper semicontinuous on
{\mathcal{F}}_{\Phi}(X)\cap C(X)
.
In the proof of Theorem
4
and Theorem
6
, we will use Jensen’s inequality, which guarantees that for a concave function
\zeta\colon[0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and an integrable function
v\colon C\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, where
C
is a compact set of
X
with positive
\lambda
-measure,

(24)

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{\lambda(C)}\int_{C}\zeta(v(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)\leq\zeta\left(\dfrac{1}{\lambda(C)}\int_{C}v(x)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)\right)

(cf. [
21
, Theorem 3.3]).
If
\zeta
is convex, the inequality is reversed.

Note that
\zeta\in{\mbox{\rm Conc}([0,\infty))}
is continuous on
[0,\infty)
and that
\zeta(0)=0
. Since
\zeta
is concave and non-negative on
[0,\infty)
, the function
\zeta
is non-decreasing. Using these properties, we get

\zeta(rt)\geq r\zeta(t)

for every
t>0
and
0<r<1
. In particular, if we take
s=rt<t
we obtain

\frac{\zeta(s)}{s}\geq\frac{\zeta(t)}{t},

and this means that
t\mapsto{\zeta(t)}/{t}
is non-increasing.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon>0
be given,
v\in{\mathcal{F}}_{\Phi}(X)\cap C(X)
, and
C\subset X
compact. Let
C_{0}
be the set where the singular part of
\Phi(v;\cdot)
is concentrated. Since
\lambda(C_{0})=0
and
\phi(v;\cdot)
is measurable
\lambda
-a.e. on
C
, we can choose by Lusin’s theorem (see, for example, [
9
, Theorem 1.2.2]) a compact set
C_{\varepsilon}\subset C
where
\phi(v;\cdot)
is continuous as a function restricted to
C_{\varepsilon}
such that

C_{\varepsilon}\cap C_{0}=\emptyset

and

(25)

\displaystyle\lambda(C\setminus C_{\varepsilon})\leq\varepsilon.

Let
v_{k}
be a sequence in
{\mathcal{F}}_{\Phi}(X)\cap C(X)
converging to
v
. First, we show that

(26)

\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\int_{C_{\varepsilon}}\zeta(\phi(v_{k};x))\,\omega(x,v_{k}(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)\leq\int_{C_{\varepsilon}}\zeta(\phi(v;x))\,\omega(x,v(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)

holds. Set

a=\inf\{\phi(v;x)\colon x\in C_{\varepsilon}\}
and
b=\sup\{\phi(v;x)\colon x\in C_{\varepsilon}\}
.
Note that
b<\infty
since
\phi(v;\cdot)
is continuous on the compact set
C_{\varepsilon}
. Hence,
\zeta
is uniformly continuous on
[a,b]
. Suppose
\eta>0
is given. Then there exists a number
\delta>0
such that

(27)

\displaystyle|\zeta(s)-\zeta(t)|\leq\eta

whenever
s,t\in[a,b]
are such that
|s-t|\leq\delta
.

Since
\lambda(\{x\in C_{\varepsilon}:\phi(v;x)=t\})>0
holds only for countably many
t
, we can choose a subdivision
a=t_{1}\leq t_{2}\leq\cdots\leq t_{m+1}=b
of
[a,b]
, such that

(28)

\displaystyle\max_{i=1,\dots,m}(t_{i+1}-t_{i})\leq\delta

and such that

(29)

\displaystyle\lambda(\{x\in C_{\varepsilon}:\phi(v;x)=t_{i}\})=0

for
i=2,\dots,m
. Setting

C_{i}=\{x\in C_{\varepsilon}:t_{i}\leq\phi(v;x)\leq t_{i+1}\}
,
using the linearity of the integral, that
\zeta
is non-decreasing, and (
29
), we get

(30)

\displaystyle\int_{C_{\varepsilon}}\zeta(\phi(v;x))\,\omega(x,v(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=1}^{m}\zeta(t_{i})\int_{C_{i}}\omega(x,v(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x).

Consider
J_{k}\subseteq\{1,\dots,m\}
, depending on
k
such that
\int_{C_{i}}\omega(x,v_{k}(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)>0
precisely if
i\in J_{k}
. By Jensen’s inequality and the monotonicity of
\zeta
, the following inequality

(31)

\int_{C_{i}}\zeta(\phi(v_{k};x))\,\omega(x,v_{k}(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)\leq\zeta\Big(\dfrac{\int_{C_{i}}\omega(x,v_{k}(x))\,\phi(v_{k};x)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)}{\int_{C_{i}}\omega(x,v_{k}(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)}\Big)\int_{C_{i}}\omega(x,v_{k}(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)

holds for each
i\in J_{k}
. Hence,

(32)

\int_{C_{\varepsilon}}\zeta(\phi(v_{k};x))\,\omega(x,v_{k}(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)\leq\sum_{i\in J_{k}}\zeta\Big(\dfrac{\int_{C_{i}}\omega(x,v_{k}(x))\,\phi(v_{k};x)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)}{\int_{C_{i}}\omega(x,v_{k}(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)}\Big)\int_{C_{i}}\omega(x,v_{k}(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x).

Since
\phi(v;\cdot)
is continuous on the compact set
C_{\varepsilon}
, the sets
C_{i}
are compact for
i=1,\dots,m
. Hence, the functions
\omega(\cdot,v_{k}(\cdot))
and
\omega(\cdot,v(\cdot))
are uniformly continuous on
C_{i}
. Together with (
5
), this gives

\displaystyle\limsup_{k\to\infty}\int_{C_{i}}\omega(x,v_{k}(x))\,\mathrm{d}\Phi(v_{k};x)\leq\int_{C_{i}}\omega(x,v(x))\,\mathrm{d}\Phi(v;x).

Hence,

(33)

\displaystyle\limsup_{k\to\infty}

\displaystyle\int_{C_{\varepsilon}}\zeta(\phi(v_{k};x))\,\omega(x,v_{k}(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)

(34)

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{k\to\infty}\sum_{i\in J_{k}}\zeta\Big(\dfrac{\int_{C_{i}}\phi(v_{k};x)\,\omega(x,v_{k}(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)}{\int_{C_{i}}\omega(x,v_{k}(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)}\Big)\int_{C_{i}}\omega(x,v_{k}(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)

(35)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}\zeta(t_{i+1})\int_{C_{i}}\omega(x,v(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)

(36)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m}(\zeta(t_{i})+(\zeta(t_{i+1})-\zeta(t_{i})))\int_{C_{i}}\omega(x,v(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x).

Note that we used the definition of
C_{i}
and the monotonicity of
\zeta
in the second inequality. Now using (
35
), (
30
), (
28
), and (
27
), we conclude that

(37)

\limsup_{k\to\infty}\int_{C_{\varepsilon}}\zeta(\phi(v_{k};x))\,\omega(x,v_{k}(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)\\
\leq\int_{C_{\varepsilon}}\zeta(\phi(v;x))\,\omega(x,v(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)+\eta\int_{C_{\varepsilon}}\omega(x,v(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x).

Since
\eta>0
is arbitrary, this proves (
26
).

The second step is to show that

(38)

\limsup_{k\to\infty}\int_{C}\zeta(\phi(v_{k};x))\,\omega(x,v_{k}(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)\\
\leq\int_{C}\zeta(\phi(v;x))\,\omega(x,v(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x).

Since
\zeta
is non-decreasing and
\zeta(t)/t
is non-increasing, we see that for every
t>0
,

\displaystyle\int_{C\setminus C_{\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle\zeta(\phi(v_{k};x))\,\omega(x,v_{k}(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\{x\in C\setminus C_{\varepsilon}:\ \phi(v_{k};x)\leq t\}}\zeta(\phi(v_{k};x))\,\omega(x,v_{k}(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{\{x\in C\setminus C_{\varepsilon}:\ \phi(v_{k};x)>t\}}\zeta(\phi(v_{k};x))\,\omega(x,v_{k}(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)

\displaystyle\leq\zeta(t)\int_{C\setminus C_{\varepsilon}}\omega(x,v_{k}(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)+\dfrac{\zeta(t)}{t}\int_{C\setminus C_{\varepsilon}}\omega(x,v_{k}(x))\,\mathrm{d}\Phi(v_{k};x).

This implies, combined with (
26
) that

(39)

\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\int_{C}\zeta(\phi(v_{k};x))\,\omega(x,v_{k}(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)\leq\int_{C}\zeta(\phi(v;x))\,\omega(x,v(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)\\
\,\,\,\,\,\,+\zeta(t)\!\int_{C\setminus C_{\varepsilon}}\!\!\,\omega(x,v(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)+\frac{\zeta(t)}{t}\!\int_{C}\!\omega(x,v(x))\,\mathrm{d}\Phi(v;x)

for every
t>0
. Since
\omega(\cdot,v(\cdot))
is a non-negative continuous function and
C
is compact, we have that
0\leq\max_{x\in C}\omega(x,v(x))<\infty
.
By this and (
25
), we obtain

(40)

\displaystyle\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\int_{C}\zeta(\phi(v_{k};x))\,\omega(x,v_{k}(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)\leq

\displaystyle\int_{C}\!\zeta(\phi(v;x))\,\omega(x,v(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)

(41)

\displaystyle+\varepsilon\,\zeta(t)\max_{x\in C}\omega(x,v(x))

(42)

\displaystyle+\frac{\zeta(t)}{t}\Phi(v;C)\max_{x\in C}\omega(x,v(x)).

Since
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrary and since
t
does not depend on
\varepsilon
, it follows that for every
t>0

(43)

\begin{split}\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}&\int_{C}\zeta(\phi(v_{k};x))\,\omega(x,v_{k}(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)\\
&\hskip-12.0pt\leq\int_{C}\zeta(\phi(v;x))\,\omega(x,v(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)+\frac{\zeta(t)}{t}\Phi(v;C)\max_{x\in C}\omega(x,v(x)).\end{split}

Using the facts that
\Phi(v;\cdot)
is a Radon measure
and that
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\zeta(t)/t=0
, we now can make
\zeta(t)/t
arbitrarily small by choosing
t
suitably large. Therefore (
43
) proves (
38
).

To finish, if
\phi(v;\cdot)
is supported on the compact set
C_{v}
with
\lambda(C_{v})>0
and

\int_{C_{v}}\omega(x,v(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)>0,

then using that
\zeta(0)=0
, the monotonicity of
\zeta
and that there exists a compact set
\tilde{C}\subset X
such that
C_{v_{k}},C_{v}\subseteq\tilde{C}
, where
C_{v_{k}}
is the support of
\phi(v_{k};\cdot)
, we get

\int_{X}\zeta(\phi(v_{k};x))\,\omega(x,v_{k}(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)=\int_{\tilde{C}}\zeta(\phi(v_{k};x))\,\omega(x,v_{k}(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)

and, using Jensen’s inequality,

\displaystyle\int_{X}\!\!\zeta(\phi(v;x))\,\omega(x,v(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\tilde{C}}\!\zeta(\phi(v;x))\,\omega(x,v(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)

\displaystyle\leq\zeta\Big(\dfrac{\int_{\tilde{C}}\phi(v;x)\,\omega(x,v(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)}{\int_{\tilde{C}}\omega(x,v(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)}\Big)\int_{\tilde{C}}\,\omega(x,v(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)

\displaystyle\leq\zeta\Big(\dfrac{\max_{x\in\tilde{C}}\omega(x,v(x))\Phi(v;\tilde{C})}{\int_{\tilde{C}}\!\omega(x,v(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)}\Big)\int_{\tilde{C}}\omega(x,v(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x).

Using again that
\Phi(v;\cdot)
is a Radon measure, that
\tilde{C}
is compact, and that
\omega(\cdot,v(\cdot))
is a non-negative continuous function, we obtain that the integral is finite.
∎

We used the continuity of
v_{k}
only to show that
\omega(\cdot,v_{k}(\cdot))
is continuous. Hence, the proof of Theorem
4
gives also the following result.

Theorem 5
.

For
\zeta\in{\mbox{\rm Conc}([0,\infty))}
,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Z}(v)=\int_{X}\zeta(\phi(v;x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)

is finite for every
v\in{\mathcal{F}}_{\Phi}(X)
, and

(44)

\operatorname{Z}(v)\geq\limsup_{k\to\infty}\operatorname{Z}(v_{k})

for every sequence of functions
v_{k}
in
{\mathcal{F}}_{\Phi}(X)
with uniformly bounded supports
\supp(\Phi(v_{k};\cdot))

that converges to
v\in{\mathcal{F}}_{\Phi}(X)
.

Let
{\mbox{\rm Cvx}([0,\infty))}
denote the set of decreasing convex functions
\zeta:[0,\infty)\rightarrow(0,\infty]
such that
\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\zeta(t)=\zeta(0)
and
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\zeta(t)=0
. We allow that
\zeta(0)=\infty
and therefore consider restrictions to compact sets in the following result, which is a functional version of Theorem 6 in
[
14
]
.

Theorem 6
.

Let
\zeta\in{\mbox{\rm Cvx}([0,\infty))}
and
\omega\in C(X\times\mathbb{R})
be non-negative while
C\subseteq X
is compact. Define
\operatorname{Z}(\cdot,C)\colon{\mathcal{F}}(X)\cap C(X)\to[0,\infty]
by

\displaystyle\operatorname{Z}(v,C)=\int_{C}\zeta(\phi(v;x))\,\omega(x,v(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x).

If
v\in{\mathcal{F}}(X)\cap C(X)
, then

(45)

\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}\operatorname{Z}(v_{k},C)\geq\operatorname{Z}(v,C)

for every sequence
v_{k}\in{\mathcal{F}}(X)\cap C(X)
converging to
v\in{\mathcal{F}}(X)\cap C(X)
.

In the proof, we will also use the following version of Fatou’s Lemma (see, for example, [
21
, Lemma 1.28]),
which says that if
\mu_{c}
is the counting measure, then

(46)

\sum_{y\in Y}\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}f_{k}(y)=\int_{Y}\left(\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}f_{k}(y)\right)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{c}(y)\leq\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}\int_{Y}f_{k}(y)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{c}(y)=\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{y\in Y}f_{k}(y)

where
Y\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
and
f_{k}:Y\to[0,\infty]
is a sequence of functions for
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

Similar to the proof of Theorem
4
, for
C\subset X
compact, let
C_{0}\subset X
be the
\lambda
-null set, where the singular part of
\Phi(v;\cdot)
restricted to
C
is concentrated. Choose by Lusin’s theorem pairwise disjoint compact sets
C_{l}\subseteq C
for
l\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\phi(v;\cdot)
is continuous as a function restricted to
C_{l}
and such that for every
l\in\mathbb{N}

(47)

\displaystyle C_{l}\cap C_{0}=\emptyset

and such that

(48)

\displaystyle\lambda\Big(C\setminus\bigcup\nolimits_{l=1}^{\infty}C_{l}\Big)=0.

We may assume that
\liminf_{k\to\infty}\operatorname{Z}(v_{k},C)<\infty
and that
\operatorname{Z}(v_{k},C)<\infty
for
k
sufficiently large. Otherwise (
45
) clearly holds.

Let
v_{k}
be a sequence on
{\mathcal{F}}(X)\cap C(X)
converging to
v\in{\mathcal{F}}(X)\cap C(X)
. We will start by proving that for each
l\in\mathbb{N}
the inequality

(49)

\liminf_{k\to\infty}\int_{C_{l}}\zeta(\phi(v_{k};x))\,\omega(x,v_{k}(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)\\
\geq\int_{C_{l}}\zeta(\phi(v;x))\,\omega(x,v(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)

holds (as elements of
[0,\infty]
).
Let
\eta>0
. We choose an increasing monotone sequence
t_{i}\in(0,\infty)
for
i\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}t_{i}=\sup(\zeta(t)\colon t\in[0,\infty))
and
\lim_{i\rightarrow-\infty}t_{i}=0
while

(50)

\displaystyle\max\nolimits_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}|\zeta(t_{i+1})-\zeta(t_{i})|\leq\eta

and such that for
i\in\mathbb{Z}
and
k\geq 0
,

(51)

\displaystyle\lambda(\{x\in C:\phi(v_{k};x)=t_{i}\})=0,

where
v_{0}=v
. Set

(52)

\displaystyle C_{li}=\{x\in C_{l}:t_{i}\leq\phi(v;x)\leq t_{i+1}\}.

Since
\phi(v;\cdot)
is continuous on
C_{l}
and
C_{l}
is compact, the sets
C_{li}
are compact for
i\in\mathbb{Z}
. Hence,
\omega(\cdot,v_{k}(\cdot))
and
\omega(\cdot,v(\cdot))
are uniformly continuous on
C_{li}
, and using (
5
) we get

(53)

\limsup_{k\to\infty}\int_{C_{li}}\phi(v_{k};x)\,\omega(x,v_{k}(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)\leq\int_{C_{li}}\phi(v;x)\,\omega(x,v(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x).

By (
47
) and the definition of
C_{li}
,

(54)

\displaystyle\int_{C_{li}}\phi(v;x)\,\omega(x,v(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)\leq t_{i+1}\int_{C_{li}}\omega(x,v(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x).

Since
\zeta
is decreasing, we obtain

(55)

\displaystyle\int_{C_{l}}\zeta(\phi(v;x))\,\omega(x,v(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)\leq\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\zeta(t_{i})\int_{C_{li}}\omega(x,v(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x).

Let
J_{k}\subset\mathbb{Z}
be such that
\int_{C_{li}}\omega(x,v_{k}(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)>0
precisely if
i\in J_{k}
, and set
v_{0}=v
. If
i\in J_{0}
, then
i\in J_{k}
for
k
sufficiently large. By (
51
), Jensen’s inequality, and the monotonicity of
\zeta
, we obtain

(56)

\begin{split}\int_{C_{l}}&\zeta(\phi(v_{k};x))\,\omega(x,v_{k}(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)\\
&\geq\sum_{i\in J_{k}}\zeta\left(\dfrac{\int_{C_{li}}\phi(v_{k};x)\,\omega(x,v_{k}(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)}{\int_{C_{li}}\omega(x,v_{k}(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)}\right)\int_{C_{li}}\omega(x,v_{k}(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x).\end{split}

Using (
56
), (
46
), the monotonicity of
\zeta
, and the classical form of Fatou’s lemma, we obtain

\displaystyle\liminf_{k\to\infty}\int_{C_{l}}

\displaystyle\zeta(\phi(v_{k};x))\,\omega(x,v_{k}(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)

\displaystyle\hskip-22.0pt\geq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\sum_{i\in J_{k}}\zeta\left(\dfrac{\int_{C_{li}}\phi(v_{k};x)\,\omega(x,v_{k}(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)}{\int_{C_{li}}\omega(x,v_{k}(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)}\right)\int_{C_{li}}\omega(x,v_{k}(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)

\displaystyle\hskip-22.0pt\geq\sum_{i\in J_{0}}\liminf_{k\to\infty}\left(\zeta\left(\dfrac{\int_{C_{li}}\phi(v_{k};x)\,\omega(x,v_{k}(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)}{\int_{C_{li}}\omega(x,v_{k}(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)}\right)\int_{C_{li}}\omega(x,v_{k}(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)\right)

\displaystyle\hskip-22.0pt\geq\sum_{i\in J_{0}}\zeta\Big(\limsup_{k\to\infty}\dfrac{\int_{C_{li}}\phi(v_{k};x)\,\omega(x,v_{k}(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)}{\int_{C_{li}}\omega(x,v_{k}(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)}\Big)\liminf_{k\to\infty}\int_{C_{li}}\omega(x,v_{k}(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)

\displaystyle\hskip-22.0pt\geq\sum_{i\in J_{0}}\zeta\Big(\dfrac{\limsup_{k\to\infty}\int_{C_{li}}\phi(v_{k};x)\,\omega(x,v_{k}(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)}{\int_{C_{li}}\omega(x,v(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)}\Big)\int_{C_{li}}\omega(x,v(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x),

and by
\eqref{eq5.7},\eqref{eq5.8},\eqref{eq5.9}
, and (
50
), we conclude that

\displaystyle\liminf_{k\to\infty}\int_{C_{l}}

\displaystyle\zeta(\phi(v_{k};x))\,\omega(x,v_{k}(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i\in J_{0}}\zeta\left(\dfrac{\int_{C_{li}}\phi(v;x)\,\omega(x,v(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)}{\int_{C_{li}}\omega(x,v(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)}\right)\int_{C_{li}}\omega(x,v(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\zeta(t_{i+1})\int_{C_{li}}\omega(x,v(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}(\zeta(t_{i})-(\zeta(t_{i})-\zeta(t_{i+1})))\int_{C_{li}}\omega(x,v(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{C_{l}}\zeta(\phi(v;x))\,\omega(x,v(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)-\eta\int_{C_{l}}\omega(x,v(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x).

Since
\eta>0
is arbitrarily, this proves (
49
).
Finally, (
48
), (
51
), (
49
), and Fatou’s Lemma imply

\displaystyle\!\liminf_{k\to\infty}\int_{C}\!\zeta(\phi(v_{k};x))\,\omega(x,v_{k}(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)

\displaystyle=\liminf_{k\to\infty}\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\int_{C_{l}}\!\zeta(\phi(v_{k};x))\,\omega(x,v_{k}(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\liminf_{k\to\infty}\int_{C_{l}}\zeta(\phi(v_{k};x))\,\omega(x,v_{k}(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\int_{C_{l}}\zeta(\phi(v;x))\,\omega(x,v(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{C}\zeta(\phi(v;x))\,\omega(x,v(x))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x),

as we wanted to prove.
∎

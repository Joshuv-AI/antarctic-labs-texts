Predictors of pathologic complete response in triple‑negative breast cancer treated with neoadjuvant chemotherapy: a systematic review and meta-analysis
Authors: Xingyu Li, Ke Peng, Qiuxia Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPreoperative neoadjuvant chemotherapy (NAC) is a commonly employed treatment strategy for triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC). Various clinical factors may influence the likelihood of achieving a pathological complete response (pCR) following NAC. This study conducted a meta-analysis to identify factors associated with pCR to inform clinical decision-making. EMBASE, PubMed, WOS, Scopus databases were selected as the information sources to identify studies published before July 1, 2025. Predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria were applied, and the quality of included studies was assessed. Commonly reported factors were subjected to meta-analysis. Thirteen studies published between 2011 and 2025 were included, with eight published before 2020 and five thereafter. Seven potential influencing factors were analyzed, including individual characteristics, pathological features, and serum biomarkers. The pooled results indicated that clinical tumor stage (OR (odds ratio) = 0.35, 95% CI: 0.21–0.59, p = 0.032), Ki-67 expression (OR = 2.91, 95% CI: 1.64–5.16, p < 0.001), BRCA1/2 mutation status (OR = 1.95, 95% CI: 1.04–3.66, p = 0.037), and neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR) (OR = 5.61, 95% CI: 2.05–15.34, p < 0.001) were significantly associated with pCR. In contrast, age at diagnosis, histological grade, and nodal status were not statistically significant predictors. The likelihood of achieving pCR in TNBC patients undergoing NAC is significantly associated with clinical stage, Ki-67 expression, BRCA1/2 mutation status, and NLR. Introduction

Triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC), characterized by the absence of estrogen receptor (ER), progesterone receptor (PR), and human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2), represents approximately 15%–20% of all breast cancer cases [1]. The tumors are typically characterized by larger tumor size, higher histological grade, more frequent lymph node involvement, and more aggressive biological behavior compared to other breast cancer subtypes [2]. Comparing to other molecular subtypes, TNBC exhibits a shorter duration of treatment response, a higher risk of local and distant recurrence, more rapid disease progression, and the poorest overall survival outcomes [3].

Neoadjuvant chemotherapy (NAC) refers to chemotherapy prior to local treatments such as surgery or radiotherapy in patients with non-metastatic breast cancer [4]. NAC can reduce tumor burden and clinical stage, thereby increasing the likelihood of successful surgical resection, improving breast-conserving surgery rates, and reducing the need for axillary lymph node dissection—ultimately contributing to better prognostic outcomes [5]. Currently, NAC is widely used as a primary treatment strategy for TNBC to decrease tumor size, downstage disease, and enhance operability [6].

Pathological complete response (pCR) remains a key surrogate endpoint for evaluating treatment efficacy. Achieving pCR is associated with lower recurrence and metastasis rates, as well as improved overall survival [7]. However, only approximately 30%–50% of TNBC patients attain pCR following NAC, while the remainder are exposed to the toxicities of standard chemotherapy without deriving substantial therapeutic benefit [8]. Therefore, accurately predicting NAC efficacy using clinical features, serological markers, and pathological information obtained from preoperative biopsies is of great clinical significance, particularly for guiding treatment decisions in stage II–III TNBC [9].

Although several studies have investigated predictors of pCR in TNBC patients undergoing NAC, a comprehensive systematic review and meta-analysis on this topic is still lacking. To address this gap, we conducted this meta-analysis to synthesize the available studies on factors influencing pCR in TNBC. This analysis aims to identify the predictors or factors, thereby providing an evidence-based foundation for optimizing treatment strategies.

Materials and methods

Search strategy

The search databases in the study includes: ① EMBASE; ② PubMed; ③ Web of Science (WOS); ④ Scopus. The combination of search keywords was: “predictors”, “factors”, "pathological complete response" or “pCR”, “neoadjuvant chemotherapy” or “NAT”, “TNBC”. The deadline for the publication of the literature is July 1, 2025. The search strategy was provided in Supplementary file 1.

Eligibility criteria

Studies that met the following criteria were included: ① Studies need to be case–control studies or cohort studies. ② In case-controls, patients should be divided into the pCR group of NAC and the non-pCR group; In cohort studies, odds ratio (OR) of factors should be provided. ③ At least one of the following data should be provided: Age, Clinical Stage, Histologic grade, Nodal status, BRCA1/2 mutation, Ki-67, neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR).

Studies that met the following criteria were excluded: ① Studies with design types of randomized controlled trials, quasi randomized trials, controlled trials in different periods, reviews, case studies, systematic reviews, qualitative studies would be excluded; ② Patients with non-TNBC or participants who did not receive NAC before surgery will be excluded from the study; ③ Studies with repeated contents, studies with too few sample sizes, and studies without access to the original text or data.

Selection process

Two reviewers independently performed the literature screening, which was conducted in two stages: primary screening and secondary screening. In the primary screening, titles and abstracts were reviewed to identify studies that met the criteria. In cases where multiple publications referred to the same study, the version containing the most complete data was retained. Following this, the two researchers cross-checked the screening results and discussed to resolve the disagreement through arbitration.

Data extraction

Two researchers further read the full texts of the studies, independently extracted the basic information, research characteristics and observation indicators of the literature, and recorded them. If data are still unavailable, the literature will be excluded. Both crude ORs and adjusted ORs were extracted from the studies. If both were available for a predictor, the adjusted ORs were preferentially selected.

Assessment of ROB (risk of bias)

The Newcastle Ottawa scale (NOS scale) [10] was used for quality analysis of the included studies. In the domain of case selection, a score of less than 2 was considered high risk, a score of 3 indicated some concerns regarding risk, and a score of 4 was considered low risk. For comparability, a score below 1 indicated high risk, while a score of 2 was deemed low risk. In the outcome assessment domain, scores of 1 or lower were classified as high risk, a score of 2 indicated some concerns, and a score of 3 was considered low risk.

Statistical analysis

① The effect size of dichotomous data was measured by OR; ② The pooling meta synthesis was exhibited in a forest plot; ③ I2 or Q statistic test was used for heterogeneity between studies. I2 < 30% or p > 0.05 indicates that there was no significant heterogeneity and fixed effect model should be adopted; Otherwise, there was significant heterogeneity and the random effects model was used. ④ Subgroup analysis was conducted based on whether the ORs sources from the crude ORs or the adjusted ORs. ⑤ Sensitivity analysis: the outliers were found by leave-one-out method. ⑥ Publication bias was detected by Egger’s test and contour enhanced funnel plot was used for display. The above effect analysis was statistically significant with bilateral P < 0.05.

Predictors

Age at diagnosis

A total of 2 studies reported the influence of age at diagnosis on the pCR of NAC in TNBC patients, with significant heterogeneity (I2 = 80%, P = 0.025). The synthesis result showed that age at diagnosis was not a statistically significant influencing factor (OR = 1.00, 95% CI (0.96, 1.04), P = 0.867) (Fig. 3).

The impact of age on the pCR during NAC in TNBC patients: Forest plot of pooled ORs. pCR, Pathological complete response; NAC, neoadjuvant chemotherapy; TNBC, Triple-negative breast cancer; OR, odds ratio

Clinical Stage

A total of 6 studies reported the impact of clinical stage III vs. I/II on the pCR, of which four reported adjusted ORs, and two reported crude ORs. Significant heterogeneity among the studies was observed (I2 = 51.4%, P = 0.067). The synthesis result showed clinical stage was significantly associated with pCR (OR = 0.43, 95% CI (0.24, 0.76), P = 0.004) (Fig. 4). Subgroup analysis revealed no significant difference between studies reporting adjusted ORs and those reporting crude ORs (P = 0.306). Given that most studies reported adjusted ORs, the two studies with crude ORs were excluded from the final synthesis. After exclusion, the pooled OR was 0.35 (95% CI: 0.21–0.59, P = 0.032).

The impact of clinical stage on the pCR during NAC in TNBC patients: Forest plot of pooled ORs. pCR, Pathological complete response; NAC, neoadjuvant chemotherapy; TNBC, Triple-negative breast cancer; OR, Odds ratio

Histologic grade

A total of 9 studies reported the impact of histologic grade II vs. grade I on pCR, of which four provided adjusted ORs, and five reported crude ORs. Subgroup analysis revealed no significant difference between studies reporting adjusted ORs and those reporting crude ORs (P = 0.628). Given that, studies reporting crude ORs were all included. The synthesis result showed that histologic grade was not a significant factor for pCR (OR = 1.59, 95% CI (0.84, 3.03), P = 0.155) (Fig. 5).

The impact of histologic grade on the pCR during NAC in TNBC patients: Forest plot of pooled ORs. pCR, Pathological complete response; NAC, neoadjuvant chemotherapy; TNBC, Triple-negative breast cancer; OR, Odds ratio

Ki-67

A total of 8 studies reported the effect of high vs. low expression of Ki-67 on pCR, of which two provided adjusted ORs, and six reported crude ORs. There was no significant heterogeneity observed (I2 = 0%, P = 0.555). Subgroup analysis revealed no significant difference between studies reporting adjusted ORs and those reporting crude ORs (P = 0.318). The synthesis result showed that high expression of Ki-67 was a statistically significant protective factor for pCR (OR = 2.91, 95% CI (1.64, 5.16), P < 0.001) (Fig. 6).

Discussion

Findings of the study

After screening, a total of 13 observational studies were included. Although various potential predictors of pathological complete response (pCR) were reported across these studies, some factors were only mentioned in individual studies and not consistently reported in others, precluding their inclusion in the meta-analysis. Additionally, there was considerable variability in the statistical methods used to report predictor variables, leading to incompatibility in data formats. As a result, certain data types could not be pooled. Specifically, age data presented as mean (range) were excluded from the meta-analysis due to inconsistency with other reporting formats.

During data synthesis from the 13 included observational studies, seven commonly reported potential predictors of pCR were identified: age, clinical stage, histologic grade, nodal status, BRCA1/2 mutation status, Ki-67 expression, and NLR. These seven variables were therefore selected as the primary outcomes of interest in this meta-analysis. The results revealed that clinical stage and Ki-67 expression (strong evidence), as well as BRCA1/2 mutation status and NLR (weaker evidence), were significantly associated with pCR. In contrast, age, histologic grade, and nodal status were not statistically significant predictors.

This study find that age was not an influencing factor of achieving pCR in NAC. Although in the literature [11, 14, 21], the pCR rate of patients aged > 50 years was relatively lower, the overall synthetic results suggested that there was no statistical significance. In a prognostic investigation [24] of adjuvant chemotherapy for TNBC, age was also not a statistically significant factor affecting pCR, OS and DFS, which stands align with the present study.

The results of this study revealed that the effect size of clinical stage III vs. I/II to achieve pCR were (OR = 0.45, 95% CI: 0.26–0.77). Clinical stage reflects tumor size and extent of spread, and lower-stage tumors are generally more responsive to NAC, consistent with findings from previous study [25]. Bonadonna et al. [26]. reported that for tumors larger than 3 cm, the response to treatment was inversely proportional to initial tumor size. Similarly, Caudle et al. [27]. identified tumor size as a pretreatment predictor of disease progression. In the included study [15], the pCR rate for tumors larger than 5 cm was 13%, compared to 44% for tumors smaller than 5 cm.

This pattern is not limited to TNBC but appears to extend to all breast cancer subtypes. For instance, a study by Prat et al. [28] involving 957 breast cancer patients treated with NAC demonstrated that clinical tumor (cT) stage was the most significant variable associated with pCR, with cT3–4 vs. cT1–2 tumors (12% vs. 24%) [28]. These findings suggest that patients with early-stage breast cancer derive greater benefit from NAC, likely due to a lower initial tumor burden.

Histologic grade is not a statistically significant influencing factor of pCR in this study. The histological grade describes the degree of differentiation and invasiveness of tumor cells. Patients with high histologic grade in the studies [11, 12, 14, 15, 19, 20] included in this meta-analysis can obtain higher pCR. However, studies [21–23] suggest that the higher the histologic grade, the lower the pCR. Taken together, the pooled OR of histologic grade on pCR was not statistically significant.

Only studies [11, 14] have reported the effect of nodal status on pCR. The pooled results suggest that nodal status is not a statistically significant influencing factor of pCR, with low level of evidence. According to the study of Jeruss [29], 96.2% of the patients who obtained pCR after neoadjuvant chemotherapy had no invasion of the lymphatic system, while 76.3% of the non pCR patients had no invasion of the lymphatic system. The present study showed an effect size of OR = 2.0271, 95% CI [0.9531; 4.3117] as related to positive vs. negative lymph nodes, with no statistical significance, which is similar to those of Jeruss.

Ki-67, also known as proliferation marker Ki-67 or antigen Ki-67 (encoded by the MKI67 gene), is strongly associated with cellular proliferation and has been widely reported as a marker engaging in both tumor growth and prognosis [30, 31]. In addition to its prognostic value, Ki-67 is considered an important predictive biomarker for assessing chemotherapy sensitivity. Fasching et al. [32]. analyzed the response to NAC in 552 breast cancer patients and found that tumors with Ki-67 expression greater than 13% were more likely to respond to treatment and achieve pathological complete response (pCR). In the present study, meta-analysis revealed that high Ki-67 expression was strongly associated with increased pCR rates in TNBC patients undergoing neoadjuvant chemotherapy (OR = 2.87, 95% CI: 1.66–4.97), indicating that patients with high Ki-67 expression were nearly three times more likely to achieve pCR compared to those with low expression. These findings are consistent with previously published research.

This study also found that BRCA1/2 mutation is an influencing factor for breast cancer patients to achieve pCR, but because only two studies [11, 12] were included, the evidence strength was not high. BRCA gene mutations account for a high proportion of TNBC, which makes such tumors highly sensitive to platinum [33]. Platinum agents such as carboplatin and cisplatin are cytotoxic chemotherapeutics that induce DNA damage chiefly by forming intra- and interstrand cross-links, thereby blocking replication and transcription and culminating in double-strand breaks and cell death. They are consequently particularly effective in tumors with deficiencies in homologous recombination–mediated DNA repair, including those harboring pathogenic BRCA1/2 mutations [34].

Neutrophil to lymphocyte ratio (NLR) is an inflammatory biomarker, which reflects the inflammatory state of tumor. It is an important component of tumor microenvironment and can affect the proliferation, invasion, and metastasis of tumor cells [35]. The reason for the effect of NLR on pCR may be that the inflammatory state of the tumor microenvironment blocks immune cell recognition, stimulates proliferation, promotes invasion and migration, and changes the sensitivity to NAC [36].

The threshold of heterogeneity detection set in this study is I2 < 30%. In the meta synthesis of histologic grade, we have detected significant heterogeneity among the studies. The sources of these heterogeneity may be related to the characteristics of patients included in the study, the implementation process of the study and other factors. We investigated outlier of the literature by using the exclusion method one by one, but found no outliers, suggesting each study devote equally to the overall heterogeneity.

In the analysis of publication bias, we detected the bias of the synthetic results of histologic grade and Ki-67, and found no significant asymmetry on both sides of the funnel, suggesting no obvious bias.

Limitations

Despite its contributions, this study has several limitations:

Quality of included studies: The analysis incorporated case–control studies, epidemiological investigations, and cohort studies. However, some studies exhibited a high risk of bias in case selection, leading to a moderate overall methodological quality.

Outcome measures: Although seven predictors of pCR were analyzed, numerous other potential influencing factors exist. Variables such as body mass index (BMI), menstrual status, specific chemotherapy regimens, and use of immunotherapy were reported in too few studies to allow for meta-analysis. Consequently, some clinically relevant predictors may have been omitted.

Data quantification: Due to inconsistencies in data reporting across studies, certain variables could not be standardized or included in the meta-analysis. For instance, although age was reported in some studies (e.g., study by Nakashoji [13]), it was expressed as mean ± standard deviation (M ± SD) without categorical breakdowns or sample sizes, preventing its inclusion in pooled analysis. This heterogeneity may contribute to publication bias. It should also be noted that the cutoff values for Ki-67 varied across the included studies, 50% in some studies [15, 22], while 30% or 10% in other studies [18]. Such variability in categorization may affect the comparability of effect estimates and could introduce heterogeneity that may not be fully captured by the I2 statistic.

Significance and implications for future research

Several meta-analyses have explored factors associated with pCR in TNBC [37, 38]. However, many of them focused on specific biomarkers or limited predictors. The present study provides a more comprehensive synthesis of multiple clinicopathological predictors associated with pCR in TNBC patients receiving neoadjuvant chemotherapy. By incorporating recently published studies and evaluating several predictors simultaneously, this analysis offers a more updated and integrated understanding of the factors influencing treatment response.

Although this study has certain limitations, sensitivity analyses indicate that the results are stable and reliable, with no significant evidence of publication bias. While considerable heterogeneity was observed across the included studies, the use of a random-effects model effectively mitigated its influence on the pooled estimates. However, the level of evidence for BRCA1/2 mutation status, NLR, and nodal status remains low. Future research with larger sample sizes and standardized reporting is warranted to further investigate these potential predictors.

Conclusion

The results of this study demonstrated that clinical stage, Ki-67 expression, BRCA1/2 mutation status, and neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR) were significantly associated with the likelihood of achieving pCR) in TNBC patients undergoing NAC. In contrast, age, histologic grade, and nodal status did not show statistically significant associations. Given the multifactorial nature of treatment response in TNBC, additional factors—such as chemotherapy regimens, body weight management, and other clinical or molecular variables—should be further investigated to comprehensively understand and optimize predictors of pCR.

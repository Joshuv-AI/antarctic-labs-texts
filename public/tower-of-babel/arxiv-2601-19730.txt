Stability and Generalization of Nonconvex Optimization with Heavy-Tailed Noise
Hongxu Chen, Ke Wei, Xiaoming Yuan, Luo Luo

Abstract
The empirical evidence indicates that stochastic optimization with heavy-tailed gradient noise is more appropriate to characterize the training of machine learning models than that with standard bounded gradient variance noise. Most existing works on this phenomenon focus on the convergence of optimization errors, while the analysis for generalization bounds under the heavy-tailed gradient noise remains limited. In this paper, we develop a general framework for establishing generalization bounds under heavy-tailed noise. Specifically, we introduce a truncation argument to achieve the generalization error bound based on the algorithmic stability under the assumption of bounded p p th centered moment with p ∈ ( 1 , 2 ] p\in(1,2] . Building on this framework, we further provide the stability and generalization analysis for several popular stochastic algorithms under heavy-tailed noise, including clipped and normalized stochastic gradient descent, as well as their mini-batch and momentum variants.

Related Work

Stability and generalization.
The generalization gap between training and testing is a central topic in statistical learning theory. Two main approaches have been developed.
The first is uniform convergence, which primarily depends on the hypothesis class and is typically algorithm-agnostic.

Bartlett and Mendelson 2002
defined the Rademacher complexity and used capacity measures of function classes to bound the gap between empirical and population risks.

Shalev-Shwartz et al. 2010
further discussed the relationship between stability and uniform convergence from the perspective of learnability.
In the nonconvex setting,
Mei et al. 2018
studied uniform convergence of gradients, characterizing function class complexity via covering numbers, and
Foster et al. 2018
further derived uniform convergence bounds using Rademacher complexity. However, uniform convergence bounds often depend on the dimension, which makes them unfavorable for high-dimensional regimes commonly encountered in machine learning.
The second line is based on algorithmic stability. The central idea is to quantify the effect of replacing a single training sample on the algorithm output and to obtain generalization bounds based on this sensitivity
[
8
]
.
Hardt et al. 2016
established stability guarantees for stochastic gradient descent under Lipschitz and smoothness assumptions, which revealed the impact of the iterations on generalization and explained the role of early stopping. Moreover,
Lei and Ying 2020
removed the smoothness assumption, and
Feldman and Vondrak 2019
derived near-optimal high-probability bounds. For nonconvex problems, stability in gradients was established in
Lei 2023
.
However, existing analyses typically require bounded variance, deriving generalization guarantees under the
p
-BCM condition remains an open problem.
While the work of
Raj et al. 2023a
,
Raj et al. 2023b
considered heavy-tailed noise modeled by
\alpha
-stability, their analysis relied on continuous-time SDE arguments and applied only to stochastic gradient descent.
Recent work
[
14
]
extended this line of analysis to SGD with momentum.

Clipped SGD.
Gradient clipping was introduced as a practical safeguard for exploding gradients in recurrent networks
[
56
]
and has since become a standard component of private learning
[
1
]
.
In stochastic optimization, gradient clipping has often been studied under heavy-tailed gradient noise.
For convex problems, clipped mirror-descent type methods and related techniques yield non-asymptotic guarantees, including high-probability results under weak tail assumptions
[
50
,
15
,
25
]
. For nonconvex problems,
Zhang et al. 2020
established convergence guarantees for clipped SGD and provided matching lower bounds, while
Sadiev et al. 2023
and
Nguyen et al. 2023
developed high-probability bounds for clipped stochastic methods under unbounded variance.
Related high-probability analyses also combined clipping with momentum and normalization in nonconvex optimization
[
13
,
46
]
. In addition,
Mai and Johansson 2021
analyzed the stability and convergence of clipped SGD for non-smooth convex functions and
Koloskova et al. 2023
established the convergence with a constant clipping threshold under bounded variance.

Normalized SGD.
Gradient normalization traces back at least to
Nesterov 2004
, which analyzed the deterministic convex setting.

Hazan et al. 2015
introduced normalized gradient descent in the stochastic setting and studied its convergence under quasi-convexity and local Lipschitz assumptions.

Levy 2017
analyzed the convergence of adaptive normalized gradient descent for smooth objectives.
In the nonconvex case,
Cutkosky and Mehta 2020
combined momentum with normalized SGD and showed that momentum removed the require for large batches.

Yang et al. 2023
established lower bounds for normalized SGD in stochastic setting and compared adaptive methods without the knowledge of problem specific parameters.
Moreover,
Hübler et al. 2024
derived nearly optimal rates for normalized SGD with momentum under relaxed smoothness.
For heavy-tailed noise,
Cutkosky and Mehta 2021
combined normalized SGD with gradient clipping and momentum, then provided a high-probability convergence analysis.

Liu and Zhou 2025
revisited batched normalized SGD with momentum and pointed out that one can achieve the optimal rate without clipping.

Hübler et al. 2025
further discussed limitations of clipping and established complexity bounds for normalized SGD with mini-batch or momentum, together with matching lower bounds.

Sun et al. 2025b
studied normalized SGD with clipping and obtained sharper rates under a Lipschitz continuous Hessian assumption.

He et al. 2025
analyzed momentum variants of normalized SGD and derived accelerated rates under higher order smoothness assumptions.

Problem Setup

Let
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\Xi
denote the parameter space and the sample space, respectively.
Consider a loss function
f:\mathcal{X}\times\Xi\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Let
S:=\{\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}\}
be a dataset of
n
i.i.d. samples drawn from a distribution
\mathcal{D}
on
\Xi
.
We are interested in finding a parameter
x\in\mathcal{X}
that generalizes well, as measured by the population risk

F(x):=\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim\mathcal{D}}\bigl[f(x;\xi)\bigr]
.
Given a training algorithm
A
on dataset
S
, it minimizes the empirical risk

F_{S}(x):=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}f(x;\xi_{i})
,
and we denote its output by
A(S)
.
Let
\|\cdot\|
denote the Euclidean norm, and
a\asymp b
if
a
and
b
are equal up to constant factors.
Let
\Delta:=\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\bigl[F_{S}(x_{0})-F_{S}^{*}\bigr]
, where
x_{0}
is the initial point of algorithms and
F_{S}^{*}:=\inf_{x}F_{S}(x)
.
We define the clipping operator
\mathrm{clip}_{\gamma}(\cdot)
with parameter
\gamma
as

\displaystyle\mathrm{clip}_{\gamma}(u)=\begin{cases}u,&\|u\|\leq\gamma,\\
\gamma\cdot\dfrac{u}{\|u\|},&\|u\|>\gamma.\end{cases}

(3)

In the nonconvex setting, finding a global minimizer is NP-hard
[
55
]
, and a learning algorithm can only guarantee to output an approximate stationary point, that is, we desire
\|\nabla F_{S}(A(S))\|
to be small.
In this case, the population risk is not an appropriate performance measure, and we instead use the population gradient norm
\|\nabla F(A(S))\|
, which can be decomposed as

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\left[\left\|\nabla F(A(S))\right\|\right]\leq\underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\left[\left\|\nabla F_{S}(A(S))\right\|\right]}_{\text{optimization error}}+\underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\left[\left\|\nabla F(A(S))-\nabla F_{S}(A(S))\right\|\right]}_{\text{generalization error}}

\displaystyle.

We will study the generalization error via algorithmic stability and derive optimization error bounds for specific algorithms under heavy-tailed noise.

We next present the assumptions used in our analysis. First, recall the
p
-BCM assumption on heavy-tailed noise, which is stated again as follows for convenience.

Assumption 3.1
.

There exist
p\in(1,2]
and
\sigma_{p}>0
such that for all
x
, it holds

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim\mathcal{D}}\left[\left\|\nabla f(x;\xi)-\nabla F(x)\right\|^{p}\right]\leq\sigma_{p}^{p}.

Assumption
3.1
is standard in the literature on stochastic optimization under heavy-tailed noise
[
81
,
13
,
54
,
46
,
31
]
.
Another way to model heavy-tailed noise is to assume an
\alpha
-stable random variable
X
with characteristic function
\mathbb{E}\bigl[\exp(i\omega X)\bigr]=\exp\bigl(-\sigma|\omega|^{\alpha}\bigr)
, where
i
is the imaginary unit and
\omega\in\mathbb{R}

[
66
]
.
However, the
\alpha
-stable model can be restrictive, as it implicitly assumes that the noise is identically distributed across coordinates
[
42
,
76
]
, while Assumption
3.1
imposes a weaker moment condition and is therefore more general.
Additionally, recent work
[
69
]
assumes an unbounded variance condition
\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim\mathcal{D}}\bigl[\|\nabla f(x;\xi)-\nabla F(x)\|^{2}\bigr]\leq c\|x\|^{p}+\sigma^{2}
,
which does not directly model heavy-tailed noise. This condition and
p
-BCM cannot be derived from each other.

Assumption 3.2
.

For any
\xi
, the function
f(\cdot;\xi)
is
L
-smooth, i.e., for any
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, it holds

\displaystyle\bigl\|\nabla f(x;\xi)-\nabla f(y;\xi)\bigr\|\leq L\|x-y\|.

The
L
-smoothness assumption is also standard in nonconvex stochastic optimization, and it will be used in our stability analysis of clipped SGD and normalized SGD.

4
Generalization Error Bound

In this section, we will establish a generalization error bound based on the algorithmic stability under heavy-tailed noise.

4.1
Algorithmic Stability

Algorithmic stability is a central concept in statistical learning theory, measuring the sensitivity of an algorithm to the perturbation of a single sample in the training dataset.
We say
S
and
S^{\prime}
are neighboring datasets if they differ by only a single sample.

Definition 4.1
.

A randomized algorithm
A
is called
\epsilon
-uniformly-argument-stable
if for all neighboring datasets
S
and
S^{\prime}
, one has

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{A}\bigl[\|A(S)-A(S^{\prime})\|\bigr]\leq\epsilon.

It is called
\epsilon
-uniformly-stable in gradients
if for all neighboring datasets
S
and
S^{\prime}
, one has

\displaystyle\sup_{\xi\in\Xi}\ \mathbb{E}_{A}\bigl[\|\nabla f(A(S);\xi)-\nabla f(A(S^{\prime});\xi)\|^{2}\bigr]\leq\epsilon^{2}.

Uniform argument stability
[
8
]
directly measures the change in the algorithm output under a single sample perturbation, while uniform stability in gradients
[
38
]
measures stability through the discrepancy between gradients and is more suitable for nonconvex analyses based on stationary point.
Under the
L
-smoothness assumption, it is straightforward to show that uniform argument stability implies uniform stability in gradients.

4.2
Generalization via Stability in Gradients

The core idea for establishing generalization via algorithmic stability is to introduce an independent ghost sample.
Let
S^{\prime}=\{\xi_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,\xi_{n}^{\prime}\}
be drawn independently from
\mathcal{D}
.
For each
i\in[n]
, define

\displaystyle S^{(i)}=\{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{i-1},\xi_{i}^{\prime},\xi_{i+1},\dots,\xi_{n}\}.

In the convex setting, the generalization error can be rewritten as an average of loss differences under the single replacement datasets
S^{(i)}
, that is,

\displaystyle\quad\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\bigl[F_{S}(A(S))-F(A(S))\bigr]=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\bigl[f(A(S);\xi_{i})-f(A(S^{(i)});\xi_{i})\bigr],

and is therefore directly bounded by the uniform stability in function values
[
65
,
27
]
.

For nonconvex problems, a direct adaptation of the convex argument yields the population gradient bound

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\bigl[\|\nabla F(A(S))\|\bigr]=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\bigl\|\mathbb{E}_{\xi_{i}}\bigl[\nabla f(A(S^{(i)});\xi_{i})\bigr]\bigr\|\Bigr],

whereas for the empirical risk one has

\displaystyle\|\nabla F_{S}(A(S))\|=\Bigl\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\nabla f(A(S);\xi_{i})\Bigr\|.

Since the average over
i
is outside the norm in the first equation but inside the norm in the second one, the convex argument cannot be directly applied to upper bound the nonconvex generalization error.
Using the error decomposition of
Bousquet et al. 2020
,
Lei 2023
showed that if the algorithm
A
is
\epsilon
-uniformly stable in gradients, then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\bigl[\|\nabla F(A(S))-\nabla F_{S}(A(S))\|\bigr]\leq 4\epsilon+\sigma_{2}n^{-\frac{1}{2}},

where
\sigma_{2}^{2}
denotes the variance of the stochastic gradients.
This nonconvex framework has been adopted in a range of subsequent studies
[
10
,
83
,
69
]
.

However, the nonconvex generalization analysis in
[
38
]
relies on the bounded variance for stochastic gradients and thus does not cover heavy-tailed noise, which provides a more plausible description in neural network training
[
66
,
81
]
.
Using a truncation argument for the stochastic gradients, we establish the following nonconvex generalization error bound under heavy-tailed noise in Theorem
4.2
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
A
be
\epsilon
-uniformly stable in gradients and Assumption
3.1
holds. Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\bigl[\|\nabla F(A(S))-\nabla F_{S}(A(S))\|\bigr]\leq 4\epsilon+C_{p}\sigma_{p}n^{-\frac{p-1}{p}},

where
C_{p}
is a constant depending only on
p
, defined by
C_{2}=1
and
C_{p}=\frac{p}{2(p-1)}\left(\frac{4(p-1)}{2-p}\right)^{\frac{2-p}{p}}
for
p\in(1,2)
.

Remark 4.3
.

Theorem
4.2
bounds the nonconvex generalization error in terms of uniform stability in gradients under the
p
-BCM condition, extending the bounded variance result
[
38
]
. When
p=2
, the bound coincides with that in
Lei 2023
.
It is worth emphasizing that this extension is nontrivial, with the proof hinging on a truncation and tail decomposition.
Moreover, for the constant
C_{p}
, it is straightforward to verify that
1\leq C_{p}\leq 3
and
\lim_{p\to 2^{-}}C_{p}=1
.

4.3
Proof Sketch of Theorem
4.2

In this section, we outline the proof of Theorem
4.2
. A complete proof is provided in Appendix
B
.

Step 1: Decomposition and truncation.

Following the derivation in
Lei 2023
, the generalization error can be decomposed as

\displaystyle\quad n\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\Bigl[\bigl\|\nabla F(A(S))-\nabla F_{S}(A(S))\bigr\|\Bigr]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}_{S,A,\xi,\xi_{i}^{\prime}}\Bigl[\bigl\|\nabla f\bigl(A(S);\xi\bigr)-\nabla f\bigl(A(S^{(i)});\xi\bigr)\bigr\|\Bigr]

\displaystyle\quad+\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\Bigl[\Bigl\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{\xi_{i}^{\prime}}\Bigl[\mathbb{E}_{\xi}\bigl[\nabla f\bigl(A(S^{(i)});\xi\bigr)\bigr]-\nabla f\bigl(A(S^{(i)});\xi_{i}\bigr)\Bigr]}_{:=z_{i}(S)}\Bigr\|\Bigr]

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}_{S,A,\xi_{i}^{\prime}}\Bigl[\bigl\|\nabla f\bigl(A(S^{(i)});\xi_{i}\bigr)-\nabla f\bigl(A(S);\xi_{i}\bigr)\bigr\|\Bigr].

The first and the third terms can be bounded by
\epsilon
-uniform stability in gradients. Consequently, it holds that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad n\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\Bigl[\bigl\|\nabla F(A(S))-\nabla F_{S}(A(S))\bigr\|\Bigr]\leq 2n\epsilon+\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\Bigl[\Bigl\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}(S)\Bigr\|\Bigr].\end{split}

(4)

The main technical challenge is to bound
\mathbb{E}\bigl[\bigl\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}(S)\bigr\|\bigr]
. Under heavy-tailed noise, since
z_{i}(S)
does not admit a bounded variance, one cannot proceed as in the existing analyses
[
38
]
by directly applying Jensen’s inequality and expanding the squared norm of the sum.

To this end, we introduce the truncated stochastic gradient noise

\displaystyle T_{\tau}(x;\xi):=\mathrm{clip}_{\tau}\bigl(\nabla f(x;\xi)-\nabla F(x)\bigr),

where
\tau>0
is a parameter and we will later specify its optimal choice. Noting that
T_{\tau}(x;\xi)
is not mean-zero, we further define the centered truncated variable

\displaystyle\tilde{T}_{\tau}(x;\xi):=T_{\tau}(x;\xi)-M_{\tau}(x),

where
M_{\tau}(x):=\mathbb{E}_{\xi}\bigl[T_{\tau}(x;\xi)\bigr]
. We then decompose
z_{i}(S)
into a truncated part and a residual tail:

\displaystyle z_{i}^{(\tau)}(S):=-\mathbb{E}_{\xi_{i}^{\prime}}\Bigl[\tilde{T}_{\tau}\bigl(A(S^{(i)});\xi_{i}\bigr)\Bigr],\qquad r_{i}^{(\tau)}(S):=z_{i}(S)-z_{i}^{(\tau)}(S).

As a result, we have

\displaystyle\Bigl\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}(S)\Bigr\|\leq\Bigl\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}^{(\tau)}(S)\Bigr\|+\Bigl\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}^{(\tau)}(S)\Bigr\|.

Step 2: Bound
\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\Bigl[\Bigl\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}^{(\tau)}(S)\Bigr\|\Bigr]
.

By Jensen’s inequality, it holds that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\Bigl[\Bigl\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}^{(\tau)}(S)\Bigr\|\Bigr]&\leq\sqrt{\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\Bigl[\Bigl\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}^{(\tau)}(S)\Bigr\|^{2}\Bigr]}\\
&\leq\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bigl\|z_{i}^{(\tau)}(S)\bigr\|^{2}\Bigr]}+\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\sum_{i\neq j}\bigl\langle z_{i}^{(\tau)}(S),z_{j}^{(\tau)}(S)\bigr\rangle\Bigr]}.\end{split}

For the diagonal terms, using

\|\mathrm{clip}_{\tau}(u)\|^{2}\leq\tau^{2-p}\|u\|^{p}
for all
u
and
p\in(1,2]
, together with Assumption
3.1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\bigl[\|z_{i}^{(\tau)}(S)\|^{2}\bigr]\leq\tau^{2-p}\sigma_{p}^{p}.

For the cross terms with
i\neq j
, we use the fact that
z_{i}^{(\tau)}(S)
is constructed from a centered truncation and thus has zero mean. By introducing an additional perturbation of the dataset and applying
\epsilon
-uniform stability in gradients, one can derive

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\bigl[\langle z_{i}^{(\tau)}(S),z_{j}^{(\tau)}(S)\rangle\bigr]\leq 4\epsilon^{2}.

Combining the two bounds yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\Bigl[\Bigl\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}^{(\tau)}(S)\Bigr\|\Bigr]\leq\sqrt{n\tau^{2-p}\sigma_{p}^{p}}+2n\epsilon.

Step 3: Bound
\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\Bigl[\Bigl\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}^{(\tau)}(S)\Bigr\|\Bigr]
.

Recall that
r_{i}^{(\tau)}(S)=z_{i}(S)-z_{i}^{(\tau)}(S)
. By the definition of
z_{i}^{(\tau)}(S)
via the truncated noise
\tilde{T}_{\tau}
, the residual
r_{i}^{(\tau)}(S)
involves terms of the form
u-\mathrm{clip}_{\tau}(u)
for some vector
u
. For any
u
and
p\in(1,2]
, one has

\displaystyle\|u-\mathrm{clip}_{\tau}(u)\|\leq\|u\|\mathbf{1}\{\|u\|>\tau\}\leq\frac{\|u\|^{p}}{\tau^{p-1}}.

Combining this inequality with Assumption
3.1
yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\bigl[\|r_{i}^{(\tau)}(S)\|\bigr]\leq\frac{2\sigma_{p}^{p}}{\tau^{p-1}}.

Step 4: Choose the optimal
\tau
.

Combining Steps 2 and 3 yields, for any
\tau>0
, one has

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\Bigl[\Bigl\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}(S)\Bigr\|\Bigr]\leq\sqrt{\frac{\tau^{2-p}\sigma_{p}^{p}}{n}}+2\epsilon+\frac{2\sigma_{p}^{p}}{\tau^{p-1}}.

(5)

When
p=2
, letting
\tau\to+\infty
gives

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\Bigl[\Bigl\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}(S)\Bigr\|\Bigr]\leq\frac{\sigma_{2}}{\sqrt{n}}+2\epsilon.

When
p\in(1,2)
, optimizing over
\tau>0
in equation (
5
) yields the choice

\displaystyle\tau_{*}=\left(\frac{4(p-1)}{2-p}\sqrt{n\sigma_{p}^{p}}\right)^{\frac{2}{p}},

which gives

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\Bigl[\Bigl\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}(S)\Bigr\|\Bigr]\leq C_{p}\sigma_{p}n^{-\frac{p-1}{p}}+2\epsilon.

Substituting the above bounds into equation (
4
) completes the proof of Theorem
4.2
.

5.2

Another widely used approach for handling heavy-tailed noise is gradient normalization. In contrast to clipping, which modifies only stochastic gradients with norms exceeding the threshold, normalized SGD normalizes the stochastic gradient at every iteration. It avoids tuning the clipping threshold and can attain optimal convergence rates, which has attracted substantial attention in recent works
[

6
Conclusion

This paper studies stability based generalization for stochastic nonconvex optimization under heavy-tailed noise. Under the bounded
p
th centered moment condition, a generalization framework is established that links nonconvex generalization error to uniform stability in gradients through a truncation and tail decomposition, and it recovers the bounded variance result when
p=2
. The framework is applied to representative methods for heavy-tailed optimization. For gradient clipping, the analysis characterizes how stability and population guarantees depend on the clipping threshold. For gradient normalization, we establish stability and risk bounds for the mini-batch and momentum variants of normalized SGD. For future work, it is interesting to study the error bounds in the nonsmooth setting and to develop lower bounds for learning under heavy-tailed noise.

Appendix A
Useful Lemmas

In this section, we present several lemmas that will be repeatedly used in the subsequent proofs. We begin with basic properties of the clipping operator.

Lemma A.1

(Basic properties of the clipping operator)
.

Let
\gamma>0
and define
\mathrm{clip}_{\gamma}(\cdot)
by equation (
3
). For any
p\in(1,2]
, the following properties hold.

(a) For any
u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, one has

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{clip}_{\gamma}(u)\|^{2}\leq\gamma^{2-p}\|u\|^{p}.

(b) For any
u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, one has

\displaystyle\|u-\mathrm{clip}_{\gamma}(u)\|\leq\frac{\|u\|^{p}}{\gamma^{p-1}}.

(c) The mapping
\mathrm{clip}_{\gamma}(\cdot)
is
1
-Lipschitz, i.e., for any
u,v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, it holds

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{clip}_{\gamma}(u)-\mathrm{clip}_{\gamma}(v)\|\leq\|u-v\|.

Proof.

For statement (a), note that
\|\mathrm{clip}_{\gamma}(u)\|=\min\{\|u\|,\gamma\}
. If
\|u\|\leq\gamma
, then it holds

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{clip}_{\gamma}(u)\|^{2}=\|u\|^{2}=\|u\|^{p}\|u\|^{2-p}\leq\gamma^{2-p}\|u\|^{p}.

If
\|u\|>\gamma
, then

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{clip}_{\gamma}(u)\|^{2}=\gamma^{2}=\gamma^{2-p}\gamma^{p}\leq\gamma^{2-p}\|u\|^{p}.

For statement (b), the claim is trivial when
\|u\|\leq\gamma
. If
\|u\|>\gamma
, then
\mathrm{clip}_{\gamma}(u)=\gamma u/\|u\|
. Hence, it holds

\displaystyle\|u-\mathrm{clip}_{\gamma}(u)\|=\left\|u-\gamma\frac{u}{\|u\|}\right\|=\|u\|-\gamma\leq\|u\|=\frac{\|u\|^{p}}{\gamma^{p-1}}\left(\frac{\gamma}{\|u\|}\right)^{p-1}\leq\frac{\|u\|^{p}}{\gamma^{p-1}},

where the last inequality uses
\|u\|>\gamma
and
p>1
.

For statement (c), we prove
\|\mathrm{clip}_{\gamma}(u)-\mathrm{clip}_{\gamma}(v)\|\leq\|u-v\|
by a case analysis.

If
\|u\|\leq\gamma
and
\|v\|\leq\gamma
, then
\mathrm{clip}_{\gamma}(u)=u
and
\mathrm{clip}_{\gamma}(v)=v
, and the claim follows.

If
\|u\|\leq\gamma<\|v\|
, then
\mathrm{clip}_{\gamma}(u)=u
and
\mathrm{clip}_{\gamma}(v)=\gamma v/\|v\|
. Expanding and simplifying, we have

\displaystyle\|u-v\|^{2}-\left\|u-\gamma\frac{v}{\|v\|}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|v\|^{2}-\gamma^{2}-2\left(1-\frac{\gamma}{\|v\|}\right)\langle u,v\rangle

\displaystyle=(\|v\|-\gamma)\left(\|v\|+\gamma-2\frac{\langle u,v\rangle}{\|v\|}\right).

Since
\langle u,v\rangle/\|v\|\leq\|u\|\leq\gamma
, the second factor is at least
\|v\|-\gamma>0
, and hence the right-hand side is nonnegative. This implies
\left\|u-\gamma\frac{v}{\|v\|}\right\|\leq\|u-v\|
. The case
\|v\|\leq\gamma<\|u\|
follows by symmetry.

If
\|u\|>\gamma
and
\|v\|>\gamma
, then
\mathrm{clip}_{\gamma}(u)=\gamma u/\|u\|
and
\mathrm{clip}_{\gamma}(v)=\gamma v/\|v\|
. Expanding both sides gives

\displaystyle\|u-v\|^{2}-\left\|\gamma\frac{u}{\|u\|}-\gamma\frac{v}{\|v\|}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|u\|^{2}+\|v\|^{2}-2\gamma^{2}-2\langle u,v\rangle\left(1-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{\|u\|\|v\|}\right).

Here
\|u\|\|v\|>\gamma^{2}
, so
1-\gamma^{2}/(\|u\|\|v\|)>0
. Using
\langle u,v\rangle\leq\|u\|\|v\|
, we obtain

\displaystyle-2\langle u,v\rangle\left(1-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{\|u\|\|v\|}\right)\geq-2\|u\|\|v\|+2\gamma^{2},

and hence the difference is at least
\|u\|^{2}+\|v\|^{2}-2\|u\|\|v\|=(\|u\|-\|v\|)^{2}\geq 0
. This implies
\mathrm{clip}_{\gamma}(\cdot)
is
1
-Lipschitz.
∎

Note that

.
By expanding the squared norm, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\Bigl[\Bigl\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}^{(\tau)}(S)\Bigr\|^{2}\Bigr]=\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\Bigl[\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bigl\|z_{i}^{(\tau)}(S)\bigr\|^{2}\Bigr]+\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\Bigl[\sum_{i\neq j}\bigl\langle z_{i}^{(\tau)}(S),z_{j}^{(\tau)}(S)\bigr\rangle\Bigr].

Let
S^{\prime\prime}=\{\xi_{1}^{\prime\prime},\dots,\xi_{n}^{\prime\prime}\}
, where
\xi_{k}^{\prime\prime}\sim\mathcal{D}
i.i.d.
Define

\displaystyle S_{j}

\displaystyle:=\{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{j-1},\xi_{j}^{\prime\prime},\xi_{j+1},\dots,\xi_{n}\},

\displaystyle S_{j}^{(i)}

\displaystyle:=\{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{i-1},\xi_{i}^{\prime},\xi_{i+1},\dots,\xi_{j-1},\xi_{j}^{\prime\prime},\xi_{j+1},\dots,\xi_{n}\}.

We first bound
\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\bigl[\langle z_{i}^{(\tau)}(S),z_{j}^{(\tau)}(S)\rangle\bigr]
for
i\neq j
.
Using the fact that
\mathbb{E}_{\xi_{j}}\bigl[z_{j}^{(\tau)}(S)\bigr]=0
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\bigl[\langle z_{i}^{(\tau)}(S_{j}),z_{j}^{(\tau)}(S)\rangle\bigr]=\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\Bigl[\mathbb{E}_{\xi_{j}}\bigl[\langle z_{i}^{(\tau)}(S_{j}),z_{j}^{(\tau)}(S)\rangle\bigr]\Bigr]=0.

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\bigl[\langle z_{i}^{(\tau)}(S),z_{j}^{(\tau)}(S_{i})\rangle\bigr]=0\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\bigl[\langle z_{i}^{(\tau)}(S_{j}),z_{j}^{(\tau)}(S_{i})\rangle\bigr]=0.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\bigl[\langle z_{i}^{(\tau)}(S),z_{j}^{(\tau)}(S)\rangle\bigr]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\Bigl[\Bigl\langle z_{i}^{(\tau)}(S)-z_{i}^{(\tau)}(S_{j}),\ z_{j}^{(\tau)}(S)-z_{j}^{(\tau)}(S_{i})\Bigr\rangle\Bigr]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\bigl[\|z_{i}^{(\tau)}(S)-z_{i}^{(\tau)}(S_{j})\|^{2}\bigr]+\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\bigl[\|z_{j}^{(\tau)}(S)-z_{j}^{(\tau)}(S_{i})\|^{2}\bigr],

where we used
\langle a,b\rangle\leq\frac{1}{2}\|a\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|b\|^{2}
.

Moreover, by Jensen’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\|z_{i}^{(\tau)}(S)-z_{i}^{(\tau)}(S_{j})\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\Bigl\|\mathbb{E}_{\xi_{i}^{\prime}}\bigl[\tilde{T}_{\tau}(A(S^{(i)});\xi_{i})-\tilde{T}_{\tau}(A(S_{j}^{(i)});\xi_{i})\bigr]\Bigr\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{\xi_{i}^{\prime}}\bigl[\|\tilde{T}_{\tau}(A(S^{(i)});\xi_{i})-\tilde{T}_{\tau}(A(S_{j}^{(i)});\xi_{i})\|^{2}\bigr].

Furthermore, one has

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\xi_{i}}\bigl[\|\tilde{T}_{\tau}(A(S^{(i)});\xi_{i})-\tilde{T}_{\tau}(A(S_{j}^{(i)});\xi_{i})\|^{2}\bigr]\leq 4\mathbb{E}_{\xi_{i}}\bigl[\|T_{\tau}(A(S^{(i)});\xi_{i})-T_{\tau}(A(S_{j}^{(i)});\xi_{i})\|^{2}\bigr],

where we used
\|u+v\|^{2}\leq 2\|u\|^{2}+2\|v\|^{2}
and Jensen’s inequality for
M_{\tau}(x)=\mathbb{E}_{\xi}[T_{\tau}(x;\xi)]
.

By the definition of
T_{\tau}
and Lemma
A.1
(c), we have

\displaystyle\quad\|T_{\tau}(A(S^{(i)});\xi_{i})-T_{\tau}(A(S_{j}^{(i)});\xi_{i})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\bigl\|(\nabla f(A(S^{(i)});\xi_{i})-\nabla F(A(S^{(i)})))-(\nabla f(A(S_{j}^{(i)});\xi_{i})-\nabla F(A(S_{j}^{(i)})))\bigr\|^{2}.

Taking expectation over
\xi_{i}
and using
\mathbb{E}\|X-\mathbb{E}X\|^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}\|X\|^{2}
yield

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\xi_{i}}\bigl[\|T_{\tau}(A(S^{(i)});\xi_{i})-T_{\tau}(A(S_{j}^{(i)});\xi_{i})\|^{2}\bigr]\leq\mathbb{E}_{\xi_{i}}\bigl[\|\nabla f(A(S^{(i)});\xi_{i})-\nabla f(A(S_{j}^{(i)});\xi_{i})\|^{2}\bigr].

Consequently, it holds

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\bigl[\langle z_{i}^{(\tau)}(S),z_{j}^{(\tau)}(S)\rangle\bigr]

\displaystyle\leq 4\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\mathbb{E}_{\xi_{i}^{\prime}}\mathbb{E}_{\xi_{i}}\bigl[\|\nabla f(A(S^{(i)});\xi_{i})-\nabla f(A(S_{j}^{(i)});\xi_{i})\|^{2}\bigr]

\displaystyle\leq 4\epsilon^{2},

where the last inequality follows from the
\epsilon
-uniform stability in gradients, since
S^{(i)}
and
S_{j}^{(i)}
are neighboring datasets.

Then we bound
\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\bigl[\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|z_{i}^{(\tau)}(S)\|^{2}\bigr]
.
By Jensen’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\bigl[\|z_{i}^{(\tau)}(S)\|^{2}\bigr]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\mathbb{E}_{\xi_{i}^{\prime}}\bigl[\|\tilde{T}_{\tau}(A(S^{(i)});\xi_{i})\|^{2}\bigr]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\mathbb{E}_{\xi_{i}^{\prime}}\bigl[\|T_{\tau}(A(S^{(i)});\xi_{i})-M_{\tau}(A(S^{(i)}))\|^{2}\bigr]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\mathbb{E}_{\xi_{i}^{\prime}}\bigl[\|T_{\tau}(A(S^{(i)});\xi_{i})\|^{2}\bigr],

where the last inequality uses
\mathbb{E}\|X-\mathbb{E}X\|^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}\|X\|^{2}
.

By Lemma
A.1
(a), it holds that

\displaystyle\|T_{\tau}(A(S^{(i)});\xi_{i})\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\bigl\|\mathrm{clip}_{\tau}\bigl(\nabla f(A(S^{(i)});\xi_{i})-\nabla F(A(S^{(i)}))\bigr)\bigr\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\tau^{2-p}\bigl\|\nabla f(A(S^{(i)});\xi_{i})-\nabla F(A(S^{(i)}))\bigr\|^{p}.

Consequently, it holds

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\bigl[\|z_{i}^{(\tau)}(S)\|^{2}\bigr]

\displaystyle\leq\tau^{2-p}\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\mathbb{E}_{\xi_{i}^{\prime}}\mathbb{E}_{\xi_{i}}\bigl[\|\nabla f(A(S^{(i)});\xi_{i})-\nabla F(A(S^{(i)}))\|^{p}\bigr]

\displaystyle\leq\tau^{2-p}\sigma_{p}^{p},

(8)

where the last inequality follows from Assumption
3.1
.

Combining equation (
B
) and the previous estimate

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\bigl[\langle z_{i}^{(\tau)}(S),z_{j}^{(\tau)}(S)\rangle\bigr]\leq 4\epsilon^{2},\qquad i\neq j,

we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\Bigl\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}^{(\tau)}(S)\Bigr\|\Bigr]\leq\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\Bigl\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}^{(\tau)}(S)\Bigr\|^{2}\Bigr]}\leq\sqrt{n\tau^{2-p}\sigma_{p}^{p}+4n(n-1)\epsilon^{2}},

where the first inequality uses Jensen’s inequality.

Dividing both sides by
n
yields

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\Bigl\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}^{(\tau)}(S)\Bigr\|\Bigr]\leq\sqrt{\frac{\tau^{2-p}\sigma_{p}^{p}}{n}+4\frac{n-1}{n}\epsilon^{2}}\leq\sqrt{\frac{\tau^{2-p}\sigma_{p}^{p}}{n}}+2\epsilon,

(9)

where we used
\sqrt{a+b}\leq\sqrt{a}+\sqrt{b}
and
\sqrt{4\frac{n-1}{n}\epsilon^{2}}\leq 2\epsilon
.

Step 3: Bound
\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\Bigl[\Bigl\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}^{(\tau)}(S)\Bigr\|\Bigr]
.

Recall that

\displaystyle\quad r_{i}^{(\tau)}(S)=z_{i}(S)-z_{i}^{(\tau)}(S)

\displaystyle=-\mathbb{E}_{\xi_{i}^{\prime}}\Bigl[\bigl(\nabla f(A(S^{(i)});\xi_{i})-\nabla F(A(S^{(i)}))\bigr)-\bigl(T_{\tau}(A(S^{(i)});\xi_{i})-M_{\tau}(A(S^{(i)}))\bigr)\Bigr]

\displaystyle=-\mathbb{E}_{\xi_{i}^{\prime}}\Bigl[\bigl(\nabla f(A(S^{(i)});\xi_{i})-\nabla F(A(S^{(i)}))\bigr)-T_{\tau}(A(S^{(i)});\xi_{i})\Bigr]

\displaystyle\quad+\mathbb{E}_{\xi_{i}^{\prime}}\mathbb{E}_{\xi}\Bigl[T_{\tau}(A(S^{(i)});\xi)-\bigl(\nabla f(A(S^{(i)});\xi)-\nabla F(A(S^{(i)}))\bigr)\Bigr].

Therefore, by the triangle inequality and Jensen’s inequality,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\xi_{i}}\bigl[\|r_{i}^{(\tau)}(S)\|\bigr]\leq 2\mathbb{E}_{\xi_{i}^{\prime}}\mathbb{E}_{\xi}\Bigl[\bigl\|\bigl(\nabla f(A(S^{(i)});\xi)-\nabla F(A(S^{(i)}))\bigr)-T_{\tau}(A(S^{(i)});\xi)\bigr\|\Bigr].

Applying Lemma
A.1
(b) with
u=\nabla f(A(S^{(i)});\xi)-\nabla F(A(S^{(i)}))
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\bigl[\|r_{i}^{(\tau)}(S)\|\bigr]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{\tau^{p-1}}\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\mathbb{E}_{\xi_{i}^{\prime}}\mathbb{E}_{\xi}\Bigl[\bigl\|\nabla f(A(S^{(i)});\xi)-\nabla F(A(S^{(i)}))\bigr\|^{p}\Bigr]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\sigma_{p}^{p}}{\tau^{p-1}},

(10)

where the last inequality follows from Assumption
3.1
.

Step 4: Choose the optimal
\tau
.

Combining the bounds on
\Bigl\|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}^{(\tau)}(S)\Bigr\|
and
\Bigl\|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}^{(\tau)}(S)\Bigr\|
(equation (
9
) and equation (
B
)), by the triangle inequality and Jensen’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\Bigl[\Bigl\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}(S)\Bigr\|\Bigr]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\Bigl[\Bigl\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}^{(\tau)}(S)\Bigr\|\Bigr]+\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\Bigl[\Bigl\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}^{(\tau)}(S)\Bigr\|\Bigr]

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\frac{\tau^{2-p}\sigma_{p}^{p}}{n}}+2\epsilon+\frac{2\sigma_{p}^{p}}{\tau^{p-1}}.

If
p=2
, letting
\tau\to\infty
yields

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\Bigl[\Bigl\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}(S)\Bigr\|\Bigr]\leq\frac{\sigma_{2}}{\sqrt{n}}+2\epsilon.

If
p\in(1,2)
, define

\displaystyle\phi(\tau):=\sqrt{\frac{\sigma_{p}^{p}}{n}}\tau^{\frac{2-p}{2}}+2\sigma_{p}^{p}\tau^{1-p}.

Then

\displaystyle\phi^{\prime}(\tau)=\sqrt{\frac{\sigma_{p}^{p}}{n}}\cdot\frac{2-p}{2}\tau^{-\frac{p}{2}}+2\sigma_{p}^{p}(1-p)\tau^{-p}.

Setting
\phi^{\prime}(\tau)=0
gives

\displaystyle\tau_{*}=\left(\frac{4(p-1)}{2-p}\sqrt{n\sigma_{p}^{p}}\right)^{\frac{2}{p}}.

Substituting
\tau_{*}
yields

\displaystyle\phi(\tau_{*})=\frac{p}{2(p-1)}\left(\frac{4(p-1)}{2-p}\right)^{\frac{2-p}{p}}\sigma_{p}n^{-\frac{p-1}{p}}=C_{p}\sigma_{p}n^{-\frac{p-1}{p}}.

Therefore, for
p\in(1,2]
, it holds

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\Bigl[\Bigl\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}(S)\Bigr\|\Bigr]\leq C_{p}\sigma_{p}n^{-\frac{p-1}{p}}+2\epsilon.

(11)

Substituting equation (
11
) into equation (
7
) completes the proof.

C.1
The Proof of Proposition
5.1

Without loss of generality, assume
S
and
S^{\prime}
differ only at the
n
-th sample, i.e.,
\xi_{n}\neq\xi_{n}^{\prime}
. By Assumption
3.2
, for any
\xi
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{A}\Bigl[\bigl\|\nabla f(A(S);\xi)-\nabla f(A(S^{\prime});\xi)\bigr\|^{2}\Bigr]\leq L^{2}\mathbb{E}_{A}\bigl[\|A(S)-A(S^{\prime})\|^{2}\bigr].

(12)

Let
\{x_{t}\}_{t=0}^{T}
and
\{x_{t}^{\prime}\}_{t=0}^{T}
be the iterates of Algorithm
1
on
S
and
S^{\prime}
, respectively, with
x_{0}=x_{0}^{\prime}=0
.
By the update rule and
\|\mathrm{clip}_{\gamma}(v)\|\leq\gamma
, we have for all
t
,

\displaystyle\|x_{t+1}-x_{t}\|=\eta_{t}\bigl\|\mathrm{clip}_{\gamma}(\nabla f(x_{t};\xi_{i_{t}}))\bigr\|\leq\gamma\eta_{t}.

Hence, by the triangle inequality, we have

\displaystyle\|x_{t}\|\leq\gamma\sum_{s=0}^{t-1}\eta_{s}\quad\text{and}\quad\|x_{t}^{\prime}\|\leq\gamma\sum_{s=0}^{t-1}\eta_{s}.

Therefore, it holds that

\displaystyle\|A(S)\|\leq\gamma\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\eta_{t}\quad\text{and}\quad\|A(S^{\prime})\|\leq\gamma\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\eta_{t}.

Let
I(A):=\{i_{t}\}_{t=0}^{T-1}
be the index sequence sampled by the algorithm.
It holds that
A(S)=A(S^{\prime})
on the event
\{n\notin I(A)\}
, and hence

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{A}\bigl[\|A(S)-A(S^{\prime})\|^{2}\bigr]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{A}\bigl[\|A(S)-A(S^{\prime})\|^{2}\mid n\notin I(A)\bigr]\mathbb{P}(n\notin I(A))

\displaystyle\quad+\mathbb{E}_{A}\bigl[\|A(S)-A(S^{\prime})\|^{2}\mid n\in I(A)\bigr]\mathbb{P}(n\in I(A))

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{A}\bigl[\|A(S)-A(S^{\prime})\|^{2}\mid n\in I(A)\bigr]\mathbb{P}(n\in I(A))

\displaystyle\leq 2\gamma^{2}\Bigl(\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\eta_{t}\Bigr)^{2}\mathbb{P}(n\in I(A))+2\gamma^{2}\Bigl(\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\eta_{t}\Bigr)^{2}\mathbb{P}(n\in I(A))

\displaystyle\leq 4\gamma^{2}\Bigl(\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\eta_{t}\Bigr)^{2}\mathbb{P}(n\in I(A)).

Since
i_{t}
is sampled uniformly from
[n]
at each iteration, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(n\in I(A))\leq\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mathbb{P}(i_{t}=n)=\frac{T}{n}.

Combining the above bounds yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{A}\bigl[\|A(S)-A(S^{\prime})\|^{2}\bigr]\leq 4\gamma^{2}\Bigl(\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\eta_{t}\Bigr)^{2}\frac{T}{n}.

(13)

Substituting equation (
13
) into equation (
12
) completes the proof.

C.2
The Proof of Theorem
5.2

By Theorem
4.2
and Proposition
5.1
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\bigl[\|\nabla F(A(S))\|\bigr]\leq\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\bigl[\|\nabla F_{S}(A(S))\|\bigr]+8L\gamma\eta T\sqrt{\frac{T}{n}}+C_{p}\sigma_{p}n^{-\frac{p-1}{p}}.

We next establish an upper bound on the optimization error of clipped SGD. Let
\mathcal{F}_{t}
be the sigma-field generated by
\{S,\ x_{0},\ i_{0},\dots,i_{t-1}\}
.
Define
g_{t}:=\mathrm{clip}_{\gamma}(\nabla f(x_{t};\xi_{i_{t}}))
.
By
L
-smoothness of
F_{S}
, we have

\displaystyle F_{S}(x_{t+1})

\displaystyle\leq F_{S}(x_{t})+\langle\nabla F_{S}(x_{t}),x_{t+1}-x_{t}\rangle+\frac{L}{2}\|x_{t+1}-x_{t}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=F_{S}(x_{t})-\eta\langle\nabla F_{S}(x_{t}),g_{t}\rangle+\frac{L\eta^{2}}{2}\|g_{t}\|^{2}.

Taking conditional expectation given
\mathcal{F}_{t}
yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigl[F_{S}(x_{t+1})\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}\bigr]

\displaystyle\leq F_{S}(x_{t})-\eta\bigl\langle\nabla F_{S}(x_{t}),\mathbb{E}[g_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]\bigr\rangle+\frac{L\eta^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|g_{t}\|^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}\bigr]

\displaystyle=F_{S}(x_{t})-\eta\|\nabla F_{S}(x_{t})\|^{2}-\eta\bigl\langle\nabla F_{S}(x_{t}),\mathbb{E}[g_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]-\nabla F_{S}(x_{t})\bigr\rangle+\frac{L\eta^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|g_{t}\|^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}\bigr]

\displaystyle\leq F_{S}(x_{t})-\frac{\eta}{2}\|\nabla F_{S}(x_{t})\|^{2}+\frac{\eta}{2}\bigl\|\mathbb{E}[g_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]-\nabla F_{S}(x_{t})\bigr\|^{2}+\frac{L\eta^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|g_{t}\|^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}\bigr],

where the last inequality uses
-\langle a,b\rangle\leq\frac{1}{2}\|a\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|b\|^{2}
.

Taking expectation and summing over
t=0,\dots,T-1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\frac{\eta}{2}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|\nabla F_{S}(x_{t})\|^{2}\bigr]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\bigl[F_{S}(x_{0})-F_{S}(x_{T})\bigr]+\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\frac{\eta}{2}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|\mathbb{E}[g_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]-\nabla F_{S}(x_{t})\|^{2}\bigr]

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\frac{L\eta^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|g_{t}\|^{2}\bigr]

\displaystyle\leq\Delta+\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\frac{\eta}{2}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|\mathbb{E}[g_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]-\nabla F_{S}(x_{t})\|^{2}\bigr]+\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\frac{L\eta^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|g_{t}\|^{2}\bigr].

By Lemma
A.1
(a), it holds that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|g_{t}\|^{2}\bigr]=\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|\mathrm{clip}_{\gamma}(\nabla f(x_{t};\xi_{i_{t}}))\|^{2}\bigr]\leq\gamma^{2-p}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|\nabla f(x_{t};\xi_{i_{t}})\|^{p}\bigr]\leq\gamma^{2-p}G^{p}.

Noting that
\nabla F_{S}(x_{t})=\mathbb{E}[\nabla f(x_{t};\xi_{i_{t}})\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[g_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]-\nabla F_{S}(x_{t})=\mathbb{E}\bigl[\mathrm{clip}_{\gamma}(\nabla f(x_{t};\xi_{i_{t}}))-\nabla f(x_{t};\xi_{i_{t}})\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}\bigr].

Using
\|\mathbb{E}[X]\|\leq\mathbb{E}[\|X\|]
and Lemma
A.1
(b), we obtain

\displaystyle\bigl\|\mathbb{E}[g_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]-\nabla F_{S}(x_{t})\bigr\|

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|\mathrm{clip}_{\gamma}(\nabla f(x_{t};\xi_{i_{t}}))-\nabla f(x_{t};\xi_{i_{t}})\|\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}\bigr]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\gamma^{p-1}}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|\nabla f(x_{t};\xi_{i_{t}})\|^{p}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}\bigr]\leq\frac{G^{p}}{\gamma^{p-1}}.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|\mathbb{E}[g_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]-\nabla F_{S}(x_{t})\|^{2}\bigr]\leq G^{2p}\gamma^{2-2p}.

Substituting the above bounds gives

\displaystyle\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\frac{\eta}{2}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|\nabla F_{S}(x_{t})\|^{2}\bigr]\leq\Delta+\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\frac{\eta}{2}G^{2p}\gamma^{2-2p}+\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\frac{L\eta^{2}}{2}\gamma^{2-p}G^{p}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\bigl[\|\nabla F_{S}(A(S))\|^{2}\bigr]\leq\frac{2\Delta}{\eta T}+G^{2p}\gamma^{2-2p}+L\eta\gamma^{2-p}G^{p}.

By Jensen’s inequality and the fact that
\sqrt{x+y+z}\leq\sqrt{x}+\sqrt{y}+\sqrt{z}
for
x,y,z\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\bigl[\|\nabla F_{S}(A(S))\|\bigr]\leq\sqrt{\frac{2\Delta}{\eta T}}+G^{p}\gamma^{1-p}+\sqrt{L\eta G^{p}\gamma^{2-p}}.

Combining this bound with the generalization decomposition at the beginning completes the proof of Theorem
5.2
.

C.3
The Proof of Proposition
5.4

Without loss of generality, assume
S
and
S^{\prime}
differ only at the
n
-th sample.
By
L
-smoothness, for any
\xi
, one has

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{A}\Bigl[\bigl\|\nabla f(A(S);\xi)-\nabla f(A(S^{\prime});\xi)\bigr\|^{2}\Bigr]\leq L^{2}\mathbb{E}_{A}\bigl[\|A(S)-A(S^{\prime})\|^{2}\bigr].

For NSGD-B, the update is normalized, hence
\|x_{t+1}-x_{t}\|\leq\eta_{t}
and thus

\displaystyle\|A(S)\|\leq\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\eta_{t}\quad\text{and}\quad\|A(S^{\prime})\|\leq\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\eta_{t}.

Let
I(A)=\{i_{t}^{(b)}:t=0,\dots,T-1,\ b=1,\dots,B\}

C.4
The Proof of Theorem
5.5

By Theorem
4.2
and Proposition
5.4
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\bigl[\|\nabla F(A(S))\|\bigr]\leq\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\bigl[\|\nabla F_{S}(A(S))\|\bigr]+8L\eta T\sqrt{\frac{BT}{n}}+C_{p}\sigma_{p}n^{-\frac{p-1}{p}}.

We next establish an upper bound on the optimization error of mini-batch normalized SGD. Let
\mathcal{F}_{t}
be the sigma-field generated by
\{S,\ \{i_{s}^{(b)}\}_{0\leq s\leq t-1,\ 1\leq b\leq B}\}
. By
L
-smoothness of
F_{S}
and the update rules, we have

\displaystyle F_{S}(x_{t+1})

\displaystyle\leq F_{S}(x_{t})+\bigl\langle\nabla F_{S}(x_{t}),x_{t+1}-x_{t}\bigr\rangle+\frac{L}{2}\|x_{t+1}-x_{t}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=F_{S}(x_{t})-\eta\left\langle\nabla F_{S}(x_{t}),\frac{g_{t}}{\|g_{t}\|}\right\rangle+\frac{L\eta^{2}}{2},

where we set
g_{t}/\|g_{t}\|=0
when
g_{t}=0
. Applying Lemma
A.2
with
u=\nabla F_{S}(x_{t})
and
v=g_{t}
yields

\displaystyle F_{S}(x_{t+1})\leq F_{S}(x_{t})-\eta\|\nabla F_{S}(x_{t})\|+2\eta\|g_{t}-\nabla F_{S}(x_{t})\|+\frac{L\eta^{2}}{2}.

Summing over
t=0,\dots,T-1
and using
F_{S}(x_{T})\geq F_{S}^{*}
give

\displaystyle\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\|\nabla F_{S}(x_{t})\|\leq\frac{F_{S}(x_{0})-F_{S}^{*}}{\eta}+2\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\|g_{t}-\nabla F_{S}(x_{t})\|+\frac{L\eta T}{2}.

Taking expectation and writing
\Delta:=\mathbb{E}_{S}\bigl[F_{S}(x_{0})-F_{S}^{*}\bigr]
lead to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\bigl[\|\nabla F_{S}(x_{t})\|\bigr]\leq\frac{\Delta}{\eta T}+\frac{2}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\bigl[\|g_{t}-\nabla F_{S}(x_{t})\|\bigr]+\frac{L\eta}{2}.

(14)

It remains to bound
\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\bigl[\|g_{t}-\nabla F_{S}(x_{t})\|\bigr]
. We define

\displaystyle X_{b}:=\nabla f(x_{t};\xi_{i_{t}^{(b)}})-\nabla F_{S}(x_{t}),\qquad g_{t}-\nabla F_{S}(x_{t})=\frac{1}{B}\sum_{b=1}^{B}X_{b},

and
\mathcal{G}_{t,b}:=\sigma(\mathcal{F}_{t},i_{t}^{(1)},\dots,i_{t}^{(b)})
. Then
\{X_{b}\}_{b=1}^{B}
is a martingale difference sequence with respect to
\{\mathcal{G}_{t,b}\}_{b=0}^{B}
, since

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigl[X_{b}\mid\mathcal{G}_{t,b-1}\bigr]=\mathbb{E}\bigl[\nabla f(x_{t};\xi_{i_{t}^{(b)}})\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}\bigr]-\nabla F_{S}(x_{t})=\nabla F_{S}(x_{t})-\nabla F_{S}(x_{t})=0.

Therefore, by Jensen’s inequality and Lemma
A.3
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|g_{t}-\nabla F_{S}(x_{t})\|\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}\bigr]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{B}\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\Bigl\|\sum_{b=1}^{B}X_{b}\Bigr\|\ \Bigm|\ \mathcal{F}_{t}\Bigr]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{B}\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\Bigl\|\sum_{b=1}^{B}X_{b}\Bigr\|^{p}\ \Bigm|\ \mathcal{F}_{t}\Bigr]^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{B}\Bigl(2\sum_{b=1}^{B}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|X_{b}\|^{p}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}\bigr]\Bigr)^{1/p}.

To bound
\mathbb{E}[\|X_{b}\|^{p}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]
, we use
\|a-b\|^{p}\leq 2^{p-1}(\|a\|^{p}+\|b\|^{p})
and
\|\mathbb{E}[Z\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]\|^{p}\leq\mathbb{E}[\|Z\|^{p}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]
to obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|X_{b}\|^{p}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}\bigr]

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p-1}\Bigl(\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|\nabla f(x_{t};\xi_{i_{t}^{(b)}})\|^{p}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}\bigr]+\|\nabla F_{S}(x_{t})\|^{p}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=2^{p-1}\Bigl(\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|\nabla f(x_{t};\xi_{i_{t}^{(b)}})\|^{p}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}\bigr]+\bigl\|\mathbb{E}[\nabla f(x_{t};\xi_{i_{t}^{(b)}})\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]\bigr\|^{p}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p-1}\Bigl(\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|\nabla f(x_{t};\xi_{i_{t}^{(b)}})\|^{p}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}\bigr]+\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|\nabla f(x_{t};\xi_{i_{t}^{(b)}})\|^{p}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}\bigr]\Bigr)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p}G^{p}.

Substituting back yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|g_{t}-\nabla F_{S}(x_{t})\|\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}\bigr]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{B}\Bigl(2B\cdot 2^{p}G^{p}\Bigr)^{1/p}\leq 4GB^{-\frac{p-1}{p}}.

Taking expectation over
(S,A)
gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\bigl[\|g_{t}-\nabla F_{S}(x_{t})\|\bigr]\leq 4GB^{-\frac{p-1}{p}}.

(15)

Substituting equation (
15
) into equation (
14
) yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\bigl[\|\nabla F_{S}(A(S))\|\bigr]=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\bigl[\|\nabla F_{S}(x_{t})\|\bigr]\leq\frac{\Delta}{\eta T}+4GB^{-\frac{p-1}{p}}+\frac{L\eta}{2}.

Combining this bound with the generalization decomposition at the beginning completes the proof of Theorem
5.5
.

C.6
The Proof of Theorem
5.9

By Theorem
4.2
and Proposition
5.8
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\bigl[\|\nabla F(A(S))\|\bigr]\leq\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\bigl[\|\nabla F_{S}(A(S))\|\bigr]+8L\eta T\sqrt{\frac{T}{n}}+C_{p}\sigma_{p}n^{-\frac{p-1}{p}}.

We next establish an upper bound on the optimization error of normalized SGD with momentum.
Let
\mathcal{F}_{t}
be the sigma-field generated by
\{S,i_{0},\dots,i_{t-1}\}
. By
L
-smoothness of
F_{S}
and the update rules, we have

\displaystyle F_{S}(x_{t+1})

\displaystyle\leq F_{S}(x_{t})+\bigl\langle\nabla F_{S}(x_{t}),x_{t+1}-x_{t}\bigr\rangle+\frac{L}{2}\|x_{t+1}-x_{t}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=F_{S}(x_{t})-\eta\left\langle\nabla F_{S}(x_{t}),\frac{m_{t}}{\|m_{t}\|}\right\rangle+\frac{L\eta^{2}}{2},

Applying Lemma
A.2
with
u=\nabla F_{S}(x_{t})
and
v=m_{t}
yields

\displaystyle F_{S}(x_{t+1})\leq F_{S}(x_{t})-\eta\|\nabla F_{S}(x_{t})\|+2\eta\|m_{t}-\nabla F_{S}(x_{t})\|+\frac{L\eta^{2}}{2}.

Summing over
t=0,\dots,T-1
, using
F_{S}(x_{T})\geq F_{S}^{*}
, and taking
\mathbb{E}_{S,A}
give

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\bigl[\|\nabla F_{S}(x_{t})\|\bigr]\leq\frac{\Delta}{\eta T}+\frac{2}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\bigl[\|m_{t}-\nabla F_{S}(x_{t})\|\bigr]+\frac{L\eta}{2},

(16)

where
\Delta:=\mathbb{E}_{S}[F_{S}(x_{0})-F_{S}^{*}]
.

Define
\delta_{t}:=m_{t}-\nabla F_{S}(x_{t})
and
\zeta_{t}:=\nabla f(x_{t};\xi_{i_{t}})-\nabla F_{S}(x_{t})
. Then
\mathbb{E}[\zeta_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]=0
, and by
\|a-b\|^{p}\leq 2^{p-1}(\|a\|^{p}+\|b\|^{p})
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|\zeta_{t}\|^{p}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}\bigr]\leq 2^{p-1}\Bigl(\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|\nabla f(x_{t};\xi_{i_{t}})\|^{p}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}\bigr]+\|\nabla F_{S}(x_{t})\|^{p}\Bigr)\leq 2^{p}G^{p}.

(17)

We next bound
\mathbb{E}_{S,A}[\|\delta_{t}\|]
. For
t\geq 1
, using
m_{t}=\beta m_{t-1}+(1-\beta)\nabla f(x_{t};\xi_{i_{t}})
gives

\displaystyle\delta_{t}=\beta\delta_{t-1}+\beta\bigl(\nabla F_{S}(x_{t-1})-\nabla F_{S}(x_{t})\bigr)+(1-\beta)\zeta_{t}.

Iterating the above equation from
\delta_{0}
yields, for all
t\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\delta_{t}=\beta^{t}\delta_{0}+(1-\beta)\sum_{\tau=1}^{t}\beta^{t-\tau}\zeta_{\tau}+\beta\sum_{\tau=1}^{t}\beta^{t-\tau}\bigl(\nabla F_{S}(x_{\tau-1})-\nabla F_{S}(x_{\tau})\bigr).

(18)

For
t=0
, since
m_{-1}=0
, we have

\displaystyle\delta_{0}=m_{0}-\nabla F_{S}(x_{0})=(1-\beta)\zeta_{0}-\beta\nabla F_{S}(x_{0}).

Using equation (
17
) at
t=0
, Jensen’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\bigl[\|\delta_{0}\|\bigr]&\leq(1-\beta)\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\bigl[\|\zeta_{0}\|\bigr]+\beta\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\bigl[\|\nabla F_{S}(x_{0})\|\bigr]\\
&\leq(1-\beta)\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\bigl[\|\zeta_{0}\|^{p}\bigr]^{1/p}+\beta G\leq(1-\beta)\cdot 2G+\beta G\leq 2G.\end{split}

We now bound the two sums in equation (
18
). For the drift term, by
L
-smoothness and
\|x_{\tau}-x_{\tau-1}\|\leq\eta
, one has

\displaystyle\left\|\beta\sum_{\tau=1}^{t}\beta^{t-\tau}\bigl(\nabla F_{S}(x_{\tau-1})-\nabla F_{S}(x_{\tau})\bigr)\right\|\leq\beta\sum_{\tau=1}^{t}\beta^{t-\tau}\cdot L\|x_{\tau-1}-x_{\tau}\|\leq L\eta\beta\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\beta^{k}\leq\frac{L\eta\beta}{1-\beta}.

For the noise term, define
Y_{\tau}:=\beta^{t-\tau}\zeta_{\tau}
for
\tau=1,\dots,t
. Since
\mathbb{E}[\zeta_{\tau}\mid\mathcal{F}_{\tau}]=0
and
\beta^{t-\tau}
is deterministic,
\{Y_{\tau}\}_{\tau=1}^{t}
is a martingale difference sequence with respect to the filtration
\{\mathcal{F}_{\tau}\}_{\tau=1}^{t}
. By Jensen’s inequality, Lemma
A.3
, and equation (
17
), one has

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\left[\left\|\sum_{\tau=1}^{t}\beta^{t-\tau}\zeta_{\tau}\right\|\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\left[\left\|\sum_{\tau=1}^{t}\beta^{t-\tau}\zeta_{\tau}\right\|^{p}\right]^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq\left(2\sum_{\tau=1}^{t}\beta^{(t-\tau)p}\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\bigl[\|\zeta_{\tau}\|^{p}\bigr]\right)^{1/p}\leq\left(2\sum_{\tau=1}^{t}\beta^{(t-\tau)p}\cdot 2^{p}G^{p}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=2^{1+\frac{1}{p}}G\left(\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\beta^{kp}\right)^{1/p}\leq 4G(1-\beta^{p})^{-1/p}.

Combining above results yields, for all
t\geq 1
, it holds that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\bigl[\|\delta_{t}\|\bigr]\leq 2G\beta^{t}+4G(1-\beta)(1-\beta^{p})^{-1/p}+\frac{L\eta\beta}{1-\beta}.

Since
1-\beta^{p}\geq 1-\beta
for
\beta\in[0,1)
and
p\in(1,2]
, we have

\displaystyle(1-\beta)(1-\beta^{p})^{-1/p}\leq(1-\beta)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}.

Thus, for all
t\geq 1
, one has

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\bigl[\|m_{t}-\nabla F_{S}(x_{t})\|\bigr]\leq 2G\beta^{t}+4G(1-\beta)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}+\frac{L\eta\beta}{1-\beta},

(19)

and for
t=0
, we have
\mathbb{E}_{S,A}[\|m_{0}-\nabla F_{S}(x_{0})\|]\leq 2G
.

Summing equation (
19
) over
t=1,\dots,T-1
and using
\sum_{t=1}^{T-1}\beta^{t}\leq\frac{\beta}{1-\beta}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\bigl[\|m_{t}-\nabla F_{S}(x_{t})\|\bigr]\leq 2G+\frac{2G\beta}{1-\beta}+4G(1-\beta)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}T+\frac{L\eta\beta}{1-\beta}T.

(20)

Substituting equation (
20
) into equation (
16
) yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\bigl[\|\nabla F_{S}(A(S))\|\bigr]\leq\frac{\Delta}{\eta T}+\frac{4G}{T}+\frac{4G\beta}{(1-\beta)T}+8G(1-\beta)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}+\frac{2L\eta\beta}{1-\beta}+\frac{L\eta}{2}.

Combining this bound with the generalization decomposition at the beginning completes the proof of Theorem
5.9
.

C.8
The Proof of Theorem
5.13

By Theorem
4.2
and Proposition
5.12
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\bigl[\|\nabla F(A(S))\|\bigr]\leq\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\bigl[\|\nabla F_{S}(A(S))\|\bigr]+8L\gamma\eta T\sqrt{\frac{T}{n}}+C_{p}\sigma_{p}n^{-\frac{p-1}{p}}.

We next establish an upper bound on the optimization error of NSGD-CM. Let
\mathcal{F}_{t}
be the sigma-field generated by
\{S,i_{0},\dots,i_{t-1}\}

\displaystyle g_{t}:=\mathrm{clip}_{\gamma}\bigl(\nabla f(x_{t};\xi_{i_{t}})\bigr),\qquad m_{t}:=\beta m_{t-1}+(1-\beta)g_{t},\qquad x_{t+1}=x_{t}-\eta\frac{m_{t}}{\|m_{t}\|},

\displaystyle=F_{S}(x_{t})-\eta\left\langle\nabla F_{S}(x_{t}),\frac{m_{t}}{\|m_{t}\|}\right\rangle+\frac{L\eta^{2}}{2}

where the last inequality uses Lemma
A.2
with
u=\nabla F_{S}(x_{t})
and
v=m_{t}
. Summing over
t=0,\dots,T-1
, using
F_{S}(x_{T})\geq F_{S}^{*}
, and taking
\mathbb{E}_{S,A}
yield

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\bigl[\|\nabla F_{S}(x_{t})\|\bigr]\leq\frac{\Delta}{\eta T}+\frac{2}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\bigl[\|m_{t}-\nabla F_{S}(x_{t})\|\bigr]+\frac{L\eta}{2},

(21)

where
\Delta:=\mathbb{E}_{S}[F_{S}(x_{0})-F_{S}^{*}]

. For

Iterating the above recursion from
\delta_{0}

t\geq 1
, it holds that

\displaystyle\delta_{t}=\beta^{t}\delta_{0}+(1-\beta)\sum_{\tau=1}^{t}\beta^{t-\tau}\bigl(g_{\tau}-\nabla F_{S}(x_{\tau})\bigr)+\beta\sum_{\tau=1}^{t}\beta^{t-\tau}\bigl(\nabla F_{S}(x_{\tau-1})-\nabla F_{S}(x_{\tau})\bigr).

(22)

For
t=0
, since
m_{-1}=0
and
m_{0}=(1-\beta)g_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle\delta_{0}=(1-\beta)g_{0}-\nabla F_{S}(x_{0}).

Using
\|g_{0}\|\leq\|\nabla f(x_{0};\xi_{i_{0}})\|
and Jensen’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\bigl[\|\delta_{0}\|\bigr]\leq(1-\beta)\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\bigl[\|g_{0}\|\bigr]+\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\bigl[\|\nabla F_{S}(x_{0})\|\bigr]\leq(1-\beta)G+G\leq 2G.

(23)

We now bound
\mathbb{E}_{S,A}[\|\delta_{t}\|]
for
t\geq 1
by controlling the two sums in equation (
22

\displaystyle g_{\tau}-\nabla F_{S}(x_{\tau})=\bigl(g_{\tau}-\mathbb{E}[g_{\tau}\mid\mathcal{F}_{\tau}]\bigr)+\bigl(\mathbb{E}[g_{\tau}\mid\mathcal{F}_{\tau}]-\nabla F_{S}(x_{\tau})\bigr).

\displaystyle\quad\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\left[\left\|\sum_{\tau=1}^{t}\beta^{t-\tau}\bigl(g_{\tau}-\nabla F_{S}(x_{\tau})\bigr)\right\|\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\left[\left\|\sum_{\tau=1}^{t}\beta^{t-\tau}\bigl(g_{\tau}-\mathbb{E}[g_{\tau}\mid\mathcal{F}_{\tau}]\bigr)\right\|\right]+\sum_{\tau=1}^{t}\beta^{t-\tau}\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\bigl[\|\mathbb{E}[g_{\tau}\mid\mathcal{F}_{\tau}]-\nabla F_{S}(x_{\tau})\|\bigr].

X_{\tau}:=\beta^{t-\tau}\bigl(g_{\tau}-\mathbb{E}[g_{\tau}\mid\mathcal{F}_{\tau}]\bigr)

A.3

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\left[\left\|\sum_{\tau=1}^{t}\beta^{t-\tau}\bigl(g_{\tau}-\mathbb{E}[g_{\tau}\mid\mathcal{F}_{\tau}]\bigr)\right\|\right]

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\left[\left\|\sum_{\tau=1}^{t}\beta^{t-\tau}\bigl(g_{\tau}-\mathbb{E}[g_{\tau}\mid\mathcal{F}_{\tau}]\bigr)\right\|\right]

\|\mathbb{E}[U]\|\leq\mathbb{E}[\|U\|]

A.1
(b), we obtain

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{E}[g_{\tau}\mid\mathcal{F}_{\tau}]-\nabla F_{S}(x_{\tau})\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\mathbb{E}\bigl[\mathrm{clip}_{\gamma}(\nabla f(x_{\tau};\xi_{i_{\tau}}))-\nabla f(x_{\tau};\xi_{i_{\tau}})\mid\mathcal{F}_{\tau}\bigr]\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|\mathrm{clip}_{\gamma}(\nabla f(x_{\tau};\xi_{i_{\tau}}))-\nabla f(x_{\tau};\xi_{i_{\tau}})\|\mid\mathcal{F}_{\tau}\bigr]

1-\beta^{p}\geq 1-\beta
for
\beta\in[0,1)
and

, it holds that

\displaystyle(1-\beta)(1-\beta^{p})^{-1/p}\leq(1-\beta)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}.

Substituting the above bounds into equation (
22
) and using equation (
23
) yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\bigl[\|\delta_{t}\|\bigr]\leq 2G\beta^{t}+4G(1-\beta)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}+G^{p}\gamma^{1-p}+\frac{L\eta\beta}{1-\beta},

(24)

for all
t\geq 1
and for
t=0

23
) gives

.

Summing equation (
24
) over
t=1,\dots,T-1
and using
\sum_{t=1}^{T-1}\beta^{t}\leq\frac{\beta}{1-\beta}

\displaystyle\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\bigl[\|m_{t}-\nabla F_{S}(x_{t})\|\bigr]\leq 2G+\frac{2G\beta}{1-\beta}+4G(1-\beta)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}T+G^{p}\gamma^{1-p}T+\frac{L\eta\beta}{1-\beta}T.

(25)

Substituting equation (
25
) into equation (
21

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{S,A}\bigl[\|\nabla F_{S}(A(S))\|\bigr]\leq\frac{\Delta}{\eta T}+\frac{4G}{T}+\frac{4G\beta}{(1-\beta)T}+8G(1-\beta)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}+2G^{p}\gamma^{1-p}+\frac{2L\eta\beta}{1-\beta}+\frac{L\eta}{2}.

5.13

An Integrated theoretical framework predicting COVID-19 vaccine behavioral intention among unvaccinated students: A lesson for health educators in future pandemics
Authors: Saba Soltani, Fereshteh Zamani, Mohadeseh Khoshgoftar, Zahra Jafarzadeh, Negin Raei, Zeinab Esmaeili, Zahra Mirzaei
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BACKGROUND:The COVID-19 pandemic has brought to light the significant challenge of vaccine non-acceptance among certain individuals. Understanding the impact of demographic characteristics on individuals’ vaccination intentions is crucial for effectively addressing the issue of vaccine hesitancy. The objective of this study was to investigate the predictive factors of COVID-19 vaccine behavioral intention among unvaccinated students.MATERIALS AND METHODS:This descriptive-analytical cross-sectional study was conducted in the academic year 2021-2022. A localized online questionnaire based on the integrated theoretical framework was sent to participants via social media. A total of 432 unvaccinated students (66.5% female and 33.5% male) participated in this study. The questionnaire consisted of sections of demographic information, integration of the health belief model (HBM), extended parallel model (EPPM), and theory of planned behavior (TPB), and the messages received by participants about the Covid-19 vaccine that changed their minds about vaccination. Descriptive statistics, t-tests, and Chi-square tests were performed with SPSS 24.0 for Windows. α was considered to be 0.05.RESULTS:Most respondents intended to get vaccinated. All exogenous latent variables were positively associated with vaccination intention. There were notable variations in the perceived susceptibility to and fear of COVID-19 based on gender characteristics. Educational level and age were significantly associated with some of the latent variables such as response efficacy and perceived susceptibility. The study shed light on the key messages that influenced our participants’ vaccine-related beliefs and attitudes. Notably, concerns were raised regarding “possible future side effects,” the “presence of smart chips in the vaccine,” the “lack of research, evidence, and WHO approval for emergency use,” as well as “political issues.”CONCLUSION:These findings can lead to a better understanding of the psychosocial factors underlying vaccine acceptance and assist public health agencies in developing educational programs specifically designed to promote student awareness of vaccination in pandemic situations.

Introduction

Vaccination stands as a resounding triumph in the realm of public health.[1] Nevertheless, the presence of vaccine hesitancy among certain segments of the population poses a formidable challenge for healthcare workers amidst the ongoing COVID-19 pandemic. It is disheartening that these groups fail to fully grasp the profound significance and undeniable cost-effectiveness of vaccination in preventing and controlling infectious diseases like COVID-19.[2] Hence, it becomes imperative for researchers to draw upon the experiences garnered from COVID-19 to unravel the barriers impeding health protection and to devise proactive measures to surmount them in the times to come. While pandemics inevitably exact a heavy toll, the advent of Covid-19 has bequeathed invaluable lessons that must not be disregarded.[3]

Misinformation has gained recognition as a prominent factor contributing to the low acceptance rate of vaccines.[3] In recent times, the world has been inundated with an overwhelming influx of information, commonly referred to as the “infodemic,” which has posed a significant challenge.[45] Consequently, it becomes crucial to comprehend the information individuals receive and their primary concerns about specific areas. This motivated us to inquire about the last messages participants received regarding the COVID-19 vaccine that influenced their vaccination decision. The predicament with an infodemic lies in the sheer volume of information, which, although not always erroneous, can make it arduous for individuals to access trusted resources, particularly during emergencies.[6] While eradicating an infodemic may be an unattainable goal, it can be effectively managed through the enhancement of public education methods. Thus, given the current circumstances, adherence to behavioral guidelines founded on scientific theories assumes critical significance in mitigating the risks posed by the infodemic.[7] Notably, the primary objective of such a framework is to predict individuals’ intentions toward health behaviors.[89] To address the challenges surrounding COVID-19 vaccine acceptance and the issue of vaccine reluctance, a comprehensive review of relevant studies was conducted. Previous studies have identified a range of emotional and perceptual factors that serve as either facilitators or barriers to vaccine uptake across diverse demographic groups.[910] For instance, several studies have suggested that negative emotions such as worry and fear play a significant role in influencing individuals’ engagement in preventive behaviors, including vaccine uptake.[911] According to Kim Witt’s model, incomplete messaging can evoke feelings of fear among the audience, leading to a reduced intention to engage in risk prevention and control measures.[12] Fear of disease is a distinct variable that has been identified in some studies as a barrier to risk control, demonstrating negative effects.[13] However, it is important to note that in certain instances, fear of disease can be triggered by perceived sensitivity and severity. In such cases, it can have a positive effect by increasing the intention to adopt risk control behaviors,[14] and can also be a motivating factor in promoting healthy behavior.[15] Therefore, the first hypothesis is about the relationship between fear of disease and indentation to control of risk which follows:

H1: Fear of COVID-19 would be related to individuals’ intention to receive the vaccine.

The relationship between “perceived susceptibility” and “perceived severity” of COVID-19, flu, and HPV with vaccine uptake was examined in some previous studies.[16910161718] Therefore, two other hypotheses would be:

H2: Perceived severity would be related to individuals’ intention to receive the vaccine.

H3: Perceived susceptibility would be related to individuals’ intention to receive the vaccine.

The variables mentioned in the hypotheses are constructs of three theoretical frameworks including: the health beliefs model (HBM),[19] extended parallel process model (EPPM),[102021] the theory of planned behavior (TPB).[222324]

Building upon the aforementioned explanations, the present study aims to explore the correlation between demographic factors and individuals’ intention towards vaccination, and also develop an initial theoretical model that integrates self-efficacy, response-efficacy, perceived severity, perceived susceptibility, fear of COVID-19, attitude (exogenous latent variables) and intention (endogenous latent variable) within a comprehensive framework. This model holds promise in identifying the key factors that influence individuals’ intention to receive vaccination. Within this scientific theoretical framework, identifying the factors associated with vaccine intention holds promise for improving educational programs aimed at addressing similar challenges in future pandemics. This approach can effectively accelerate the promotion of vaccination and reduce the associated casualties.[25]

Materials and Methods

Study design and setting

This cross-sectional and descriptive-analytical study was conducted during the academic year 2021-2022, coinciding with the initial phase of the national vaccination program.

Study participants and sampling

The data for this research were acquired through a survey questionnaire, administered to a representative sample of participants who fulfilled the inclusion criteria. The study included both male and female students enrolled at Isfahan University of Medical Sciences who had not yet received the Covid-19 vaccine. The sampling method stratified sampling. The participants were divided into different groups based on their faculty. The allocation of samples was done using quota sampling, where a specific number of participants were selected from each faculty. In total, 432 participants were included, which was proportional to the number of students in each faculty. A list of classes of each level was prepared and one or more classes were randomly selected for each level. Cochran’s Formula was applied to determine the sample size, taking into account a 95% confidence level, a probability of 0.5, and a margin of error of 0.05. The study adhered to ethical guidelines, ensuring participant confidentiality, anonymity, and informed consent. The research protocol was reviewed and approved by the Ethics Committee of Isfahan University of Medical Sciences (No: 1400120).

Data collection tools and technique

Due to the lockdown associated with the COVID-19 pandemic, reaching the intended target group in person at the university was not feasible. Consequently, an electronic questionnaire was employed as the data collection method. The questionnaire was developed using online survey design software (Porseline). To enable the distribution of the online survey, the research committee granted access to the social media profiles of students from the included faculties who met the eligibility criteria. The link to the self-administered electronic questionnaire was then distributed to all students on this contact list.

The questionnaire included informed consent as the first page, requiring participants to agree before gaining access to the full survey, and was structured into four sections: 1. Study objective: A concise explanation of the study’s purpose was provided at the beginning of the questionnaire. Participants were informed that their responses would be used solely for research purposes and treated with strict confidentiality. 2. Demographic information: Participants were requested to provide demographic details, including age, gender, educational attainment, income, and marital status. These demographic variables served as independent variables in the study. 3. Health behavioral theories variables, an integration of HBM, EPPM, and TPB models: This section of the questionnaire incorporated items from the study conducted by Haoran Chu, which integrated health behavior theories to predict the intention of American citizens regarding COVID-19 vaccination.[6] The exogenous latent variables, namely perceived susceptibility, perceived severity, fear of COVID-19, attitude, self-efficacy, response efficacy, and the endogenous latent variable (intention), were assessed using multiple items. Each item was rated on a Likert-type scale, ranging from 1 = strongly disagree to 5 = strongly agree. 4. Messages influencing vaccination decision: Participants were asked to reflect on the most recent messages they received regarding the COVID-19 vaccine that influenced their attitude or decision. They were requested to provide a brief description of these messages.

To ensure cultural alignment, the questionnaire underwent translation and back-translation by two bilingual translators. Discrepancies between translations were carefully examined and adjusted by the researcher.

Outlier removal

Data were first standardized using the z-score transformation. Under the assumption of normality, outlier questionnaires were excluded from further analysis using a two-sided Grubbs test. The outlier questionnaires were those for which the calculated Grubbs’ statistic for at least two variables were significantly larger than a critical value (Z-scores more than 2) of the questionnaire level mean of those variables (95% confidence level) (XLSTAT statistical software).

Analysis of variance

The mean values of the health behavior scores were calculated in terms of demographic class. A Kolmogorov-Smirnov test was used to evaluate the normality of the data. One-way analysis of variance of measured health behavior means based on the change in demographic information was conducted under a completely random statistical design, and the comparison of means at the 95% confidence level was performed using Fisher’s LSD test (SPSS Statistics).

PLS-SEM modeling

Using SmartPLS 3 software, partial least squares structural equation modeling (PLS-SEM) was used to examine the measurement and structural models, using the exogenous latent variables and intention (endogenous latent variable). The mediation effects of exogenous latent variables on intention were analyzed in the mediation analysis. The statistical significance of the path was determined using the t-test.

Confirmatory factor analysis (CFA) was conducted to assess the reliability, convergent validity, and discriminant validity of the measurement model. Reliability was evaluated through various indices, including Cronbach’s alpha, Ordinal alpha (rho_A), and Composite reliability values, with values above 0.7 generally considered acceptable. Convergent validity was examined using the average variance extracted (AVE), with a threshold set at greater than 0.5.

Discriminant validity was verified through two methods: 1. Square root of AVE greater than inter-construct correlations (Fornell-Larcker criterion). 2. Heterotrait–monotrait (HTMT) ratios below 0.85. A bootstrapping procedure using 5000 resamples was implemented to estimate the significance of path coefficients in the structural model. Standardized path coefficients, t-statistics, and P values were calculated and used to test the hypothesized relationships between constructs.

Ethical consideration

The study was approved by the Ethics Research of Isfahan University of Medical Sciences (IR.MUI.RESEARCH.REC.1400.201).

Discussion

Vaccination stood as a widely acknowledged and highly effective public health intervention in the battle against the COVID-19 pandemic. Notably, it has played a crucial role in enhancing global health by diminishing the prevalence and fatalities associated with numerous infectious diseases.[26] Additionally, vaccination has demonstrated its cost-effectiveness, making it a valuable tool in public health initiatives.[27] Interestingly, despite the widespread availability of vaccines and the global push for mass vaccination, a significant number of people continue to exhibit reluctance or outright refusal to receive the vaccine.[24]

The main objective of this study was to investigate the relationship between demographic factors and individuals’ intention to receive COVID-19 vaccination. Additionally, it sought to integrate components from three health behavioral theories, namely EPPM, HBM, and TPB, to explore the sociopsychological factors associated with people’s intention to receive COVID-19 vaccines.

Overall, participants exhibited a strong inclination to get vaccinated (84%) against COVID-19, showing a high vaccination intention rate which corroborates previous studies.[2428] Unlike the findings of Guidry et al.,[29] our results indicate that there were no significant differences between males and females in terms of vaccination intention. This aligns with the findings of another study our results indicate that there were no significant differences between males and females in terms of vaccination intention. This aligns with the findings of another study.[30] In contrast to the findings of other studies,[3132] our study revealed that males perceived a higher susceptibility to COVID-19 compared to females, leading to higher levels of precautionary behavior. Additionally, in our study, we found a significant relationship between education level and the intention to receive the vaccine, regardless of income. These findings are consistent with previous research,[293334] which also showed that individuals with higher levels of education had more positive attitudes, perceived higher response efficacy, and were more likely to get vaccinated. Another study shows that individuals who are aware of COVID-19 risk factors intend to get vaccinated and highly educated people’s intention to get vaccinated are the strongest respondent to Covid-19 risk factors.[35] Also, young people often think that most deaths caused by COVID-19 are related to the elderly or people with underlying diseases.[36] Therefore, it seems necessary to monitor the observance of protective behaviors in students with low education levels.

In contrast to the findings of other studies,[3738] In contrast to the findings of other studies.[3940] which also supported the lack of association between marital status and vaccine acceptance. Our study, consistent with other studies,[41] showed that males had higher levels of COVID-19-related fear. On the other hand, some studies showed that females had higher levels of COVID-19-related fear.[4243]

In our study, most of the concerns about the COVID-19 vaccine were about “possible side effects in the future”, “the existence of smart chips in the vaccine”, “lack of studies, evidence, and, emergency use permits of the World Health Organization”, “political issues”. Concerns about the lack of studies and possible side effects may be because most people who are reluctant to be vaccinated believe that sociopolitical factors may have precipitated the approval of the safety of the COVID-19 vaccine.[25] In another study, the odds of intending to be vaccinated with COVID-19 were at least three times higher among people who were more confident in the efficacy of the COVID-19 vaccine and less concerned about vaccine safety.[44] Therefore, to improve intention rates, once data on the safety and efficacy of vaccination are available, health education campaigns should work quickly to build public confidence in new vaccines by ensuring that the material is easily understood.

Furthermore, the discriminant validity of our model supports that our integrated theoretical framework, which combines the Extended Parallel Process Model (EMMP), Health Belief Model (HBM), and Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB), serves as a strong indicator for predicting individuals’ preventive behavior towards Covid-19. The behavioral factors encompassed within these theories, including self-efficacy, response efficacy, perceived susceptibility, perceived severity, attitude, and fear of a specific disease, emerge as significant predictive factors for individuals’ preventive intentions.[44454647]

Limitation and recommendation

One of the most important limitations of the study was the online completion of the questionnaire. It is recommended that future studies be conducted with a larger sample size.

Conclusions

In conclusion, our integrated theoretical framework serves as a valuable indicator for predicting individuals’ preventive behavior. Moreover, our findings can provide valuable insights into the psychosocial factors that influence vaccine acceptance among students. This knowledge can be instrumental in guiding public health agencies to develop targeted educational programs aimed at raising student awareness of vaccination during pandemic situations Furthermore, it is noteworthy that the acceptance rate of vaccines among students at Isfahan University of Medical Sciences is high. However, there remain challenges that need to be addressed through educational efforts by the government and healthcare providers in order to further enhance vaccine acceptance rates during pandemics.

Interstitial anionic electrons involved superconductivity and T T -linear resistivity behavior in electride La 3 In
Zhijun Tu 1,2,† , Peihan Sun 3,† , Pengcheng Ma 4,† , Hongrun Zhen 4 , Shangjie Tian 5 , Shouguo Wang 5 , Tian Cui 4 , Zhonghao Liu 4,∗ , Kai Liu 1,2,∗ , and Hechang Lei 1,2,∗

Abstract
Electrides are unique materials because of the existence of interstitial anionic electrons (IAEs). Due to these loosely bound IAEs and their strong interaction with the framework of cations, electrides can host superconductivity with rather high T c T_{c} , especially under high pressure, as predicted in theory. However, the experimental observations of superconductivity in electrides are very rare, let alone the detailed studies on intrinsic properties of single crystals. Here, we report the superconducting and normal-state properties of electride La 3 In single crystals. La 3 In shows a type-II superconductivity with T c ∼ T_{c}\sim 9.4 K and a T T -linear resistivity in a wide temperature range. Experimental measurements and theoretical calculations suggest that the relatively high T c T_{c} could be ascribed to the high density of states around the Fermi level caused by short flat bands along R-M direction and the strong electron-phonon coupling, partially derived from the IAEs. Meanwhile, the T T -linear resistivity may reflect the significant electronic correlation effect in this material. These findings will shed light on understanding the role of IAEs in superconductivity and open a promising way to explore high-temperature superconductors in electrides.

Efficient Optimal PAC Learning
blaba Mikael Møller Høgsgaard Aarhus University hogsgaard@cs.au.dk

Abstract
Recent advances in the binary classification setting by Hanneke (2016b) and Larsen (2023) have resulted in optimal PAC learners. These learners leverage, respectively, a clever deterministic subsampling scheme and the classic heuristic of bagging Breiman (1996) . Both optimal PAC learners use, as a subroutine, the natural algorithm of empirical risk minimization. Consequently, the computational cost of these optimal PAC learners is tied to that of the empirical risk minimizer algorithm. In this work, we seek to provide an alternative perspective on the computational cost imposed by the link to the empirical risk minimizer algorithm. To this end, we show the existence of an optimal PAC learner, which offers a different tradeoff in terms of the computational cost induced by the empirical risk minimizer.

Introduction

The PAC model (Probably Approximately Correct) was introduced by
Valiant (1984)
;
Vapnik and Chervonenkis (1964)
;
Vapnik and Chervonenkis (1974)
and has since been a cornerstone in the theory of machine learning. The model is based on the idea that a learning algorithm should be able to learn from a set of labelled training examples such that it can predict the label of unseen examples with high accuracy. In the following, we will consider the realizable setting of PAC learning, that is, we assume that the true concept
c\in\left\{-1,1\right\}^{\mathcal{X}}
is contained in a hypothesis class
\mathcal{H}\subset\left\{-1,1\right\}^{\mathcal{X}}
, and that the hypothesis class has finite VC-dimension
d
. Finite VC-dimension
d
of
\mathcal{H}
implies that there exists a point set
x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}
, where every pattern in
\left\{-1,1\right\}^{d}
on the points
x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}
, can be realized by a hypothesis in the hypothesis class
\mathcal{H}
. However, for any point set of more than
d
points, this is not the case (
Vapnik and Chervonenkis (1971)
). Formally, we have the following definition of PAC learning:

Definition 1
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be an input space and
\mathcal{H}\subset\left\{-1,1\right\}^{\mathcal{X}}
a hypothesis class of VC-dimension
d.
A learning algorithm
\mathcal{A}
is a PAC learner for
\mathcal{H}
if there exists a sample complexity function
m_{\mathcal{A}}:(0,1)^{2}\times\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
such that for every
\epsilon,\delta\in(0,1)
, every distribution
\mathcal{D}
over
\mathcal{X}
, every target concept
c\in\mathcal{H}
and
m\geq m_{\mathcal{A}}(\epsilon,\delta,d),
it holds with probability at least
1-\delta
over a training sequence
\mathbf{S}=(\mathbf{S}_{1},c(\mathbf{S}_{1}),\ldots,\mathbf{S}_{m},c(\mathbf{S}_{m}))\sim\mathcal{D}_{c}^{m}
with
\mathbf{S}_{i}\sim\mathcal{D},
that
f=\mathcal{A}(\mathbf{S})\in\left\{-1,1\right\}^{\mathcal{X}}
is such that the error

\ls_{\mathcal{D}_{c}}(f):=\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{x}\sim\mathcal{D}_{c}}\left[f(\mathbf{x})\not=c(\mathbf{x})\right]\leq\epsilon.

The first, and also natural, approach tried out to get PAC-learners with good sample complexity was to do empirical risk minimization (
\mathrm{ERM}
). In this approach, given a training sequence
\mathbf{S},
the PAC learner outputs a hypothesis
h
in
\mathcal{H}
that is consistent with the training sequence, i.e., for every
(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\in\mathbf{S}
, the hypothesis
h
satisfies
h(\mathbf{x})=\mathbf{y}
. The following lemma, due to
Vapnik and Chervonenkis (1968)
;
Vapnik and Chervonenkis (1971)
;
Blumer et al. (1989c)
, gives a uniform error bound on consistent functions in
\mathcal{H}
, and thus ensures that any
\mathrm{ERM}
algorithm guarantees that error bound. Furthermore, error bounds give rise to determining a sample complexity for a given
\varepsilon
by solving for
m
such that the error bound implies
\ls_{\mathcal{D}_{c}}(\mathcal{A}(\mathbf{S}))\leq\varepsilon
.

Lemma 1
.

[
Vapnik and Chervonenkis (1974)
,
Blumer et al. (1989a)
from

For

c\in\mathcal{H}

This sample complexity bound is known to be tight for general

Abstract
. In this work, we develop and analyze resonance-based low-regularity numerical integrators for stochastic Schrödinger equations driven by additive Q Q -Wiener noise, covering both the linear equation with rough potential and the cubic nonlinear case. For the linear equation, we prove strong and almost sure convergence under low regularity assumptions, achieving first-order convergence in H σ H^{\sigma} for solutions in H σ + 1 H^{\sigma+1} , thereby improving on the classical H σ + 2 H^{\sigma+2} requirement. In a regime of 𝒪 ⁡ ( ε 2 ) \mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{2}) potentials and 𝒪 ⁡ ( ε ) \mathcal{O}(\varepsilon) noise, ε ≪ 1 \varepsilon\ll 1 , we establish uniform moment bounds for the exact solution up to times of length 𝒪 ⁡ ( ε − 2 ) \mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{-2}) , and construct a non-resonant low-regularity scheme exhibiting uniform long-time error bounds of size 𝒪 ⁡ ( ε 2 ​ τ ) \mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{2}\tau) , being τ > 0 \tau>0 the time step of the numerical integration, up to times of length 𝒪 ⁡ ( ε − 2 ) . \mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{-2}). For the cubic equation, we introduce both resonant and non-resonant integrators and derive analogous pathwise convergence results at low-regularity. In the weakly nonlinear stochastic regime, i.e., 𝒪 ⁡ ( ε 2 ) \mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{2}) nonlinearity and 𝒪 ⁡ ( ε ) \mathcal{O}(\varepsilon) noise, we prove long-time pathwise error bounds of size 𝒪 ⁡ ( ε 2 ​ τ δ ) \mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{2}\tau^{\delta}) , for any δ < 1 \delta<1 , up to times of length 𝒪 ⁡ ( ε − 2 ) \mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{-2}) . For both the equations, the long-term error analysis relies on a novel nontrivial extension of the regularity-compensation oscillation technique (RCO) to stochastic dispersive equations, allowing to get improved error estimates overcoming the loss of temporal regularity induced by stochastic convolutions. In particular, the long-term errors reduce by a factor of ε 2 \varepsilon^{2} , compared to other classical and resonant low-regularity integrators. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first work establishing long-time error bounds for low-regularity integrators applied to stochastic dispersive equations. The theoretical results will be finally confirmed by selected numerical experiments.

Introduction

In this work, we consider the stochastic Schr
\ddot{\rm o}
dinger equation (SSE) with additive noise, assuming the following formulation

(1)

\dot{\xi}(t)={\bf i}\Delta\xi(t)-{\bf i}F(\xi(t))+Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\dot{W}(t),\quad t\in[0,T],

equipped with
\xi(0)=\xi_{0}
, where
F

W(t)
respect to a normal filtration
\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}

F
, on the operator

Nonlinear Schrödinger equations, and in particular, the cubic nonlinear equation, are widely spread in several fields, such as, plasma physics and hydrodynamics, since they represent effective mathematical models to describe many important phenomena. A possibility to incorporate stochastic effects in physical models, described by Schrödinger equations, is given by model (

). Indeed, in the scientific literature, several authors have addressed their studies to (

[

,

]
, in which the authors provided a semi-discrete temporal discretization to (SSE), in the case of
F=0
,

globally Lipschitz and in the case of cubic nonlinearity. They showed convergence in various topology and also in the weak sense, both with additive and Stratonovich noise. In
[

, the authors provided an explicit exponential integrator for (

However, in all the aforementioned paper, the provided theoretical analysis is limited to short time scales.

Recently, the long-time behaviour of solutions to semilinear evolutive equations, e.g. Schrödinger equations, has captured great interest and several works have been provided to study the effects of small nonlinearities and/or small initial data to the long-time evolution of the exact solutions

The aim of this work is to study convergence and long-term behaviour of resonance-based low-regularity numerical integrators (i.e. integrators constructed exploiting Fourier analysis) for (

F(\xi)=V(x)\xi
and

In particular, we will design numerical integrators requiring

, and exhibiting excellent long-time behaviour in terms of boundedness of the errors.

Although in the deterministic setting convergence and long-term error bounds of low-regularity numerical integrators applied to semilinear dispersive equations have been well-established (see, e.g.,

and reference therein, for a non-exhaustive list of contributions on this direction), the application of these techniques to the setting of stochastic dispersive PDEs in nowadays a challenging open research field; recent first works in this area are given by
[

,

,

]
; in particular, in
[

, the authors constructed symplectic low-regularity integrators for the nonlinear (SSE) and showed geometric properties and provided a local error analysis for the proposed schemes. In
[

, a low-regularity scheme has been introduced and studied for the nonlinear Schrödinger equation with white noise dispersion. Finally, in
[
8

, strong convergence rates of an unfiltered low-regularity method have been investigated for the stochastic wave equation with a globally Lipschitz nonlinearity.

Also in these works, the results are restricted to short time scales.

At the best of our knowledge, there are no works establishing long-term error bounds for numerical integrators, and, in particular, for resonance-based low regularity numerical integrators, for (SSE), and, more in general, for stochastic dispersive equations.

Following the idea in
[
36
]
, firstly, we will design low-regularity integrators by exploiting Fourier analysis, available for periodic square integrable complex-valued functions defined on the one-dimensional torus
\mathbb{T}
; in particular, such integrators will be obtained by integrating exactly the integral arising from the dominant term appearing in the Fourier expansion for the numerical approximation. Also, by exploiting the ideas developed in, e.g.,
[
3
]
, non-resonant low-regularity integrators will be designed by integrating exactly the integral for the
zero mode term
. Then, in this situation, by introducing a novel extension of the (RCO) technique to the stochastic setting, we will be able to obtain improved long-term error bounds for such numerical solutions.

In the deterministic setting, the (RCO) technique has been exploited and applied to quadratic and cubic Schrödinger equations in
[
21
]
, where the authors provided improved long-term errors bounds for non-resonant low-regularity integrators for the aforementioned equations under the assumption of an initial datum of size
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon)
,
\varepsilon\ll 1
. In particular, for the cubic equations, they showed an error of size
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{2}\tau)
in
H^{1}
, for smooth solutions, up to time of size
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{-2})
. To achieve these result, they also made use of the following well-known time regularity result for the solution to deterministic Schrödinger equations

(2)

\|\xi(t+\tau)-\xi(t)\|_{H^{\sigma}}\lesssim\tau.

It is now worth mentioning that the present work is the first contribution in which (RCO) technique is extended to stochastic dispersive equations and its application cannot descend directly from the deterministic setting. Indeed, the direct use of a time regularity result in the stochastic setting does not allow to get the desired first order in
\tau
, since, due to the presence of the stochastic convolution term, it translates into the well-know estimate

(3)

\|\xi(t+\tau)-\xi(t)\|_{L^{2p}(\Omega;H^{\sigma})}\lesssim\sqrt{\tau},

i.e., one looses one-half order in
\tau
, if straightforwardly applies the estimate in (
3
).
Then, this introduces further challenges in the extension of the (RCO) technique in the stochastic scenario that will be treat through this work.

The major contributions of the present paper are now listed.

•

For the case of linear equation with potential, we construct a resonant low-regularity integrator and establish strong and almost sure convergence with order
\gamma\in(0,1]
in
L^{2p}(\Omega;H^{\sigma}),\ \sigma\in\mathbb{N}
, for solutions in
H^{\sigma+\gamma}
. In particular, we obtain first-order convergence in
H^{\sigma}
under only
H^{\sigma+1}
regularity, thereby improving the classical requirement of
H^{\sigma+2}
. In the regime of
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{2})
potential and
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon)
noise, we prove uniform moment bounds for the exact solution up to times of length
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{-2})
. We then design a non-resonant low-regularity integrator and establish uniform strong error bound in
L^{2}(\Omega;L^{2})
over the same long-time interval. Notably, the proposed method achieves an error of size
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{2}\tau)
, improving existing long-time error estimates by a factor of
\varepsilon^{2}
.

•

For the cubic equation, we introduce both resonant and non-resonant low-regularity schemes and derive pathwise convergence results at low-regularity. In particular, order
\gamma\in(0,1)
is obtained in
H^{\sigma},\ \sigma\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
, for solutions in
H^{\sigma+\gamma}
, improving classical regularity assumptions required by standard methods. In the weakly nonlinear and weak noise regime, i.e., for
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{2})
nonlinearity and
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon)
noise, we prove uniform long-time pathwise error bounds in
H^{1}
of size
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{2}\tau^{\delta})
, for any
\delta<1
, up to times of length
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{-2})
. Also here, a reduction by a factor of
\varepsilon^{2}
appears. Due to the lack of exponential integrability of the proposed resonance-based low-regularity numerical solutions, that is needed to get strong convergence of numerical approximation to nonlinear SPDEs
[
10
]
, we obtain the pathwise error rates and pathwise long-term error bounds by combining low-regularity techniques with a truncation-based strategy
[
19
]
.

•

In both the scenarios, we introduce a novel and nontrivial extension of the (RCO) technique to stochastic dispersive equations, developing a refined analysis that allows to get improved long-time error bounds previously unavailable for such equations. The analytical techniques introduced in this work are expected to be extended beyond the present setting and to be applicable to other classes of stochastic dispersive equations.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we recall all the material needed for understanding this work. Section 3 will be fully devoted to the case of
F(\xi)=V(x)\xi
, while Section 4 will contain the theory for the cubic case
F(\xi)=|\xi|^{2}\xi.
Finally, several numerical examples will be provided in Section 5, confirming the effectiveness of the theoretical analysis.

2.
Setting and notation

In this section, we provide all the material and the notation useful for the sequel of the present work.

Let
\mathbb{T}=[-\pi,\pi]
and
T>0
. We consider the stochastic Schrödinger equation (
1
), here re-written by the matter of clarity,

(4)

d\xi(t)={\bf i}\Delta\xi(t)dt-{\bf i}F(\xi(t))dt+Q^{\frac{1}{2}}dW(t),\qquad t\in[0,T],

where
\xi(t)=\xi(t,x)
,
\xi(0)=\xi_{0}(x),\ x\in\mathbb{T}
, and
{\bf i}
denotes the imaginary unit. Periodic boundary conditions will be assumed. Through this work,
\xi(t)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
, being
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
the space of the periodic square-integrable complex-valued functions defined on
\mathbb{T}
, i.e.,
\xi(t):\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{C}
, for
t\in[0,T]
.

it is well-known that an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
is given by
\{\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}{\rm e}^{{\bf i}\ell x}\}_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}
; see, e.g.,
[
30
]
. Moreover, we assume the following form for the
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
-valued Wiener process
W(t)

W(t,x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}\beta_{\ell}(t){\rm e}^{{\bf i}\ell x},

where
\{\beta_{\ell}(t)\}_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a sequence of i.i.d. standard Brownian motions. The symmetric positive-definite Covariance operator
Q
satisfies the following assumption.

Assumption 2.1
.

The operator
Q^{\frac{1}{2}}
is an Hilbert-Schmidt operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
and satisfies the following: there exists a real sequence
\{q_{\ell}\}_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}
such that it holds

Q{\rm e}^{{\rm e}\ell x}=q_{\ell}{\rm e}^{{\rm e}\ell x},\quad q_{\ell}\geq 0,\qquad\ell\in\mathbb{Z}.

Based on Assumption
2.1
, one the gets

Q^{\frac{1}{2}}W(t,x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}\sqrt{q_{\ell}}\beta_{\ell}(t){\rm e}^{{\bf i}\ell x}.

In this work, we are interested in the time approximation to mild solutions to (
4
), i.e., the exact solution to (
4
) assumes the form

\xi(t)={\rm e}^{{\bf i}t\Delta}\xi_{0}-{\bf i}\int_{0}^{t}{\rm e}^{{\bf i}(t-s)\Delta}F(\xi(s))\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}{\rm e}^{{\bf i}(t-s)\Delta}Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\,dW(s).

For classical results on mild solutions to SPDEs, on the theory of the semigroup operator
{\rm e}^{{\bf i}t\Delta},\ t\geq 0
, as well as, for standard martingale inequalities, such as, the well-known Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality (in the sequel, denoted as BDG inequality) and the stochastic Fubini Theorem, we refer to the monograph
[
13
]
.

On
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
, we define the scalar product

(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})=\Re\int_{\mathbb{T}}\xi_{1}(x)\overline{\xi_{2}}(x)\,dx,\qquad\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathbb{T}),

where
\overline{\cdot}
stands for the complex conjugate of
\cdot
. For
w\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
, we can write
w(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}\hat{w}_{\ell}{\rm e}^{{\bf i}\ell x}
, being
\{\hat{w}_{\ell}\}_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}
the Fourier coefficients of
w
. Moreover, we will denote by
w^{(k)}
the function
w^{(k)}(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}|\ell|^{k}|\hat{w}_{\ell}|{\rm e}^{{\bf i}\ell x}
, for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Also, for
q\in\mathbb{N}
, we will denote by

\|w\|_{\ell^{q}}:=\big(\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}|\hat{w}_{\ell}|^{q}\big)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

and we denote by
\star
the discrete convolution operator with

\widehat{\big(w\star z\big)}_{\ell}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\hat{w}_{k}\hat{z}_{\ell-k}.

Clearly, for any
q\in\mathbb{N}
, one has

\|w\star z\|_{\ell^{q}}=\big(\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}\big|\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\hat{w}_{k}\hat{z}_{\ell-k}\big|^{q}\big)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

For any
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
, we can define the Sobolev norm of the space
W^{\sigma,2}(\mathbb{T})=H^{\sigma}(\mathbb{T})=H^{\sigma}
as

\|w\|_{H^{\sigma}}:=\big(\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}(1+|\ell|^{2\sigma})|\hat{w}_{\ell}|^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Clearly,
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})=H^{0}
and we will denote
\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}}=\|\cdot\|.

Also, for
\sigma\geq 0
, we will denote by
\mathcal{L}_{2}^{\sigma}
the space of Hilbert-Schmidt operators
\Psi:L^{2}(\mathbb{T})\to H^{\sigma}
with norm

\|\Psi\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}^{\sigma}}:=\big(\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}\|\Psi e_{\ell}\|^{2}_{H^{\sigma}}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}},

being
\{e_{\ell}\}_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}
any orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
. By Assumption
2.1
,
Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\in\mathcal{L}_{2}^{0}
.

For a
H^{\sigma}
-valued stochastic process
z(t)
, we will use the notation

\|z(t)\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;H^{\sigma})}:=\big(\mathbb{E}\big[\|z(t)\|^{p}_{H^{\sigma}}\big]\big)^{\frac{1}{p}},\quad p\in\mathbb{N}^{+},\sigma\in\mathbb{R}

and the notation

\|z\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;L^{\infty}([0,T];H^{\sigma}))}:=\big(\mathbb{E}\big[\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|z(t)\|_{H^{\sigma}}^{p}\big]\big)^{\frac{1}{p}},\qquad p\in\mathbb{N}^{+},\quad\sigma\in\mathbb{R}.

By a matter of simplicity, we take the following assumption on the initial data
\xi_{0}
.

Assumption 2.2
.

The initial data
\xi_{0}(x)
is a deterministic function.

Remark 2.1
.

All the results presented in the sequel of this work may be extended to the case of stochastic
\xi_{0}(x)
, under the assumption that
\xi_{0}\in L^{p}(\Omega;H^{\sigma})
, for some
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
and for a sufficiently large
p\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
.

When not specified, in this work we will always work under Assumption
2.1
and Assumption
2.2
. Moreover, in the estimates, we will denote by
C
(or
C_{p}
) a generic constant that may change line by line.

3.
Linear SSE with rough potential

In this section, we study the case in which the mapping
F
in (
1
) assumes the form
F(\xi)=V(x)\xi
, for a deterministic potential
V\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
, i.e., we consider the linear SSE with rough potential

(5)

du(t)={\bf i}\Delta u(t)dt-{\bf i}V(x)u(t)dt+Q^{\frac{1}{2}}dW(t),\quad t\in[0,T],\ x\in\mathbb{T},

whose mild solution is given by

(6)

u(t)={\rm e}^{{\bf i}t\Delta}u_{0}-{\bf i}\int_{0}^{t}{\rm e}^{{\bf i}(t-s)\Delta}V(x)u(s)\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}{\rm e}^{{\bf i}(t-s)\Delta}Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\,dW(s).

Note that, through this section, we will denote the solution of the linear equation with rough potential
u
to distinguish it from the cubic case that will be treated in the next section.

From
[
1
]
, we have the following well-posedness result for (
5
).

Theorem 3.1
.

Let us assume
u_{0}\in H^{\sigma}
and
Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\in\mathcal{L}_{2}^{\sigma}
, for some
\sigma\in\mathbb{N}
. Assume also
V\in H^{\sigma}\cap L^{\infty}
.
Then, for any
T>0
, there exists an unique solution
u
to the linear SSE with rough potential (
5
) on
[0,T]
, given by (
6
). Moreover,
u\in L^{2p}(\Omega;L^{\infty}([0,T];H^{\sigma}))
, for any
p\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
.

Remark 3.1
.

Note that, since
\mathbb{T}
is the one-dimensional torus, one has that, for
\sigma\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
,
H^{\sigma}\cap L^{\infty}=H^{\sigma}
while, for
\sigma=0
,
L^{2}\cap L^{\infty}=L^{\infty}
, i.e., for the result in
L^{2}
, an uniform boundedness of the potential in
\mathbb{T}
is needed.

3.1.
Construction of a low-regularity numerical integrator

Here, we construct a low-regularity integrator for (
5
). Following the idea in
[
36
]
, it is convenient to define the twisted variable

(7)

v(t)={\rm e}^{-{\bf i}t\Delta}u(t).

Then,
v
solves the equation

v(t)=v_{0}-{\bf i}\int_{0}^{t}{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}s\Delta}\big(V(x){\rm e}^{{\bf i}s\Delta}v(s)\big)\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}s\Delta}Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\,dW(s).

Then, after a change of variable (see also
[
36
,
21
]
), we get

v(t_{n}+\tau)=v(t_{n})-{\bf i}\int_{0}^{\tau}{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}(t_{n}+s)\Delta}\big(V(x){\rm e}^{{\bf i}(t_{n}+s)\Delta}v(t_{n}+s)\big)\,ds+\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n}+\tau}{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}s\Delta}Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\,dW(s).

First, we consider the following approximation

(8)

v(t_{n}+\tau)\approx v(t_{n})-{\bf i}\int_{0}^{\tau}{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}(t_{n}+s)\Delta}\big(V(x){\rm e}^{{\bf i}(t_{n}+s)\Delta}v(t_{n})\big)\,ds+\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n}+\tau}{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}s\Delta}Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\,dW(s).

Hence, we now provide an approximation of the first integral in the right-hand side of (
8
). Under Fourier expansion, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}(t_{n}+s)\Delta}\big(V(x){\rm e}^{{\bf i}(t_{n}+s)\Delta}v(t_{n})\big)\,ds

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\tau}{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}(t_{n}+s)\Delta}\big(\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}\hat{V}_{\ell}{\rm e}^{{\bf i}\ell x}\cdot{\rm e}^{{\bf i}(t_{n}+s)\Delta}\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}\hat{v}_{\ell}(t_{n}){\rm e}^{{\bf i}\ell x}\big)\,ds

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\tau}{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}(t_{n}+s)\Delta}\big(\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}\hat{V}_{\ell}{\rm e}^{{\bf i}\ell x}\cdot\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\hat{v}_{k}(t_{n}){\rm e}^{-{\bf i}(t_{n}+s)k^{2}}{\rm e}^{{\bf i}kx}\big)\,ds

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\tau}{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}(t_{n}+s)\Delta}\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}{\rm e}^{{\bf i}\ell x}\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}\\
\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}=\ell\end{subarray}}\hat{V}_{\ell_{1}}\hat{v}_{\ell_{2}}(t_{n}){\rm e}^{-{\bf i}(t_{n}+s)\ell_{2}^{2}}\,ds

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\tau}\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}{\rm e}^{{\bf i}\ell x}\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}\\
\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}=\ell\end{subarray}}\hat{V}_{\ell_{1}}\hat{v}_{\ell_{2}}(t_{n}){\rm e}^{{\bf i}(t_{n}+s)(\ell^{2}-\ell_{2}^{2})}\,ds

(9)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}{\rm e}^{{\bf i}\ell x}\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}\\
\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}=\ell\end{subarray}}\hat{V}_{\ell_{1}}\hat{v}_{\ell_{2}}(t_{n}){\rm e}^{{\bf i}t_{n}(\ell^{2}-\ell_{2}^{2})}\int_{0}^{\tau}{\rm e}^{{\bf i}s(\ell_{1}^{2}+2\ell_{1}\ell_{2})}\,ds,

where we have used the key relation
\ell^{2}-\ell_{2}^{2}=\ell_{1}^{2}+2\ell_{1}\ell_{2}
. Then, we make the following approximation

(10)

\int_{0}^{\tau}{\rm e}^{{\bf i}s(\ell_{1}^{2}+2\ell_{1}\ell_{2})}\,ds\approx\int_{0}^{\tau}{\rm e}^{{\bf i}s\ell_{1}^{2}}\,ds=\begin{cases}\tau,&\ell_{1}=0,\\
\frac{{\rm e}^{{\bf i}\ell_{1}^{2}\tau}-1}{{\bf i}\ell_{1}^{2}},&\ell_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\neq 0}.\end{cases}

Hence, plugging (
10
) in (
9
), we get

(11)

\int_{0}^{\tau}{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}(t_{n}+s)\Delta}\big(V(x){\rm e}^{{\bf i}(t_{n}+s)\Delta}v(t_{n})\big)\,ds\approx\frac{\tau}{2\pi}\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}{\rm e}^{{\bf i}\ell x}\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}\\
\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}=\ell\end{subarray}}\hat{V}_{\ell_{1}}\hat{v}_{\ell_{2}}(t_{n}){\rm e}^{{\bf i}t_{n}(\ell^{2}-\ell_{2}^{2})}\varphi_{1}({\bf i}\tau\ell_{1}^{2}),

where

\varphi_{1}(z)=\begin{cases}1,&z=0,\\
\frac{1}{z}({\rm e}^{z}-1),&z\in\mathbb{Z}_{\neq 0}.\end{cases}

Returning back to physical space from Fourier expansion in (
11
), we finally get

\int_{0}^{\tau}{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}(t_{n}+s)\Delta}\big(V(x){\rm e}^{{\bf i}(t_{n}+s)\Delta}v(t_{n})\big)\,ds\approx\tau{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}t_{n}\Delta}\big[({\rm e}^{{\bf i}t_{n}\Delta}v(t_{n}))\varphi_{1}(-{\bf i}\tau\Delta)V(x)\big],

where we define the operator
\varphi_{1}(\Delta)
through its action on the elements of Fourier basis as

\varphi_{1}(\Delta){\rm e}^{{\bf i}\ell x}=\begin{cases}1,&\ell=0,\\
\frac{{\rm e}^{-\ell^{2}}-1}{-\ell^{2}},&\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{\neq 0}.\end{cases}

Then, we get the following approximation for the twisted variable
v

v_{n+1}=v_{n}-{\bf i}\tau{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}t_{n}\Delta}\big[({\rm e}^{{\bf i}t_{n}\Delta}v_{n})\varphi_{1}(-{\bf i}\tau\Delta)V(x)\big]+\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n}+\tau}{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}s\Delta}Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\,dW(s).

Twisting back the variable
v
, we finally obtain the following numerical integrator for (
5
)

(12)

u_{n+1}={\rm e}^{{\bf i}\tau\Delta}\big[u_{n}-{\bf i}\tau u_{n}\big(\varphi_{1}(-{\bf i}\tau\Delta)V(x)\big)\big]+\mathcal{W}_{n},

where the stochastic convolution

\mathcal{W}_{n}:=\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}{\rm e}^{{\bf i}(t_{n+1}-s)\Delta}Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\,dW(s)

is supposed to be simulated exactly
[
30
]
. In the sequel, we may refer to the integrator (
12
) as (SLR1).

3.2.
Error analysis for (SLR1)

Here, we provide an error analysis for (SLR1). We start by providing the following proposition.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let us consider
u_{0},V\in H^{\sigma}
and
Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\in\mathcal{L}_{2}^{\sigma}
, for some
\sigma\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
. Then, for any
p\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
, there exists a positive constant
C_{p}
such that the numerical solution given by (SLR1) satisfies

\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}^{+}}\mathbb{E}\big[\max_{1\leq n\leq N}\|u_{n}\|^{2p}_{H^{\sigma}}\big]\leq C_{p}.

Proof.

By recursion of the iteration in (
12
), we get

u_{n}={\rm e}^{{\bf i}t_{n}\Delta}u_{0}-{\bf i}\tau\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}{\rm e}^{{\bf i}j\tau\Delta}\big(u_{n-j}\varphi_{1}(-{\bf i}\tau\Delta)V(x)\big)+\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}{\rm e}^{{\bf i}(j-1)\tau\Delta}\mathcal{W}_{n-j}.

Then, using the fact that
{\rm e}^{{\bf i}t\Delta}
is an isometry, that
H^{\sigma}
is an algebra on
\mathbb{T}
, for
\sigma\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
, that
\varphi_{1}(-{\bf i}\tau\Delta)
is bounded on
H^{\sigma}
, and the Hölder inequality, we get

\displaystyle\|u_{n}\|_{H^{\sigma}}^{2p}

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{\sigma}}^{2p}+\tau^{2p}\|\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}{\rm e}^{{\bf i}j\tau\Delta}\big(u_{n-j}\varphi_{1}(-{\bf i}\tau\Delta)V(x)\big)\|_{H^{\sigma}}^{2p}+\|\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}{\rm e}^{{\bf i}(j-1)\tau\Delta}\mathcal{W}_{n-j}\|_{H^{\sigma}}^{2p}\big)

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{\sigma}}^{2p}+\tau\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|u_{n-j}\varphi_{1}(-{\bf i}\tau\Delta)V(x)\|^{2p}_{H^{\sigma}}+\|\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j+1}}{\rm e}^{{\bf i}(t_{n}-s)\Delta}Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\,dW(s)\|^{2p}_{H^{\sigma}}\big)

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{\sigma}}^{2p}+\tau\|V\|^{2p}_{H^{\sigma}}\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\|u_{j}\|^{2p}_{H^{\sigma}}+\|\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j+1}}{\rm e}^{{\bf i}(t_{n}-s)\Delta}Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\,dW(s)\|^{2p}_{H^{\sigma}}\big).

Then, by BDG inequality, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\max_{1\leq n\leq N}\|u_{n}\|^{2p}_{H^{\sigma}}\big]

\displaystyle\leq C\|u_{0}\|_{H^{\sigma}}^{2p}+C\tau\|V\|^{2p}_{H^{\sigma}}\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\big[\max_{1\leq k\leq j}\|u_{k}\|_{H^{\sigma}}^{2p}\big]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+C\mathbb{E}\big[\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\int_{0}^{t}{\rm e}^{{\bf i}(t-s)\Delta}Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\,dW(s)\|^{2p}_{H^{\sigma}}\big]

(13)

\displaystyle\leq C\|u_{0}\|_{H^{\sigma}}^{2p}+C\tau\|V\|^{2p}_{H^{\sigma}}\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\big[\max_{1\leq k\leq j}\|u_{k}\|_{H^{\sigma}}^{2p}\big]+CT^{p}\|Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\|^{2p}_{\mathcal{L}_{2}^{\sigma}}.

An application of the discrete Gronwall inequality then yields the result.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

It is possible to obtain the same result of Proposition
3.2
for
\sigma=0
, i.e., for
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
, if one assumes
V\in H^{\frac{1}{2}^{+}}
. Indeed, for any
s>\frac{1}{2}
, Hölder inequality gives

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{1}(-{\bf i}\tau\Delta)V(x)\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\mathbb{T}}|\hat{V}_{0}+\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{\neq 0}}{\rm e}^{{\bf i}\ell x}\hat{V}_{\ell}\frac{1}{{\bf i}\tau\ell^{2}}({\rm e}^{{\bf i}\tau\ell^{2}}-1)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}|\hat{V}_{\ell}|(1+|\ell|)^{s}|(1+|\ell|)^{-s}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\big(\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}|\hat{V}_{\ell}|^{2}(1+|\ell|)^{2s}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\big(\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}(1+|\ell|)^{-2s}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|V\|_{H^{s}}.

This gives the bound

\|u_{j}\varphi_{1}(-{\bf i}\tau\Delta)V(x)\|\leq C\|u_{j}\|\|V\|_{H^{s}},\qquad j=0,\dots,N,\quad s>\frac{1}{2}.

We now state and prove strong and
\mathbb{P}-{\rm a.s.}
convergence results for the integrator (SLR1).

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\sigma\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
and
\gamma\in(0,1]
. Assume
u_{0},V\in H^{\sigma+\gamma}
and
Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\in\mathcal{L}_{2}^{\sigma+\gamma}
.
Then, for any
p\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
, the exists a constant
C_{p}
such that we have the following error estimate

(14)

\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}^{+}}\big(\mathbb{E}\big[\max_{1\leq n\leq N}\|u(t_{n})-u_{n}\|^{2p}_{H^{\sigma}}\big]\big)^{\frac{1}{2p}}\leq C_{p}\tau^{\gamma}.

Moreover, it holds that for any
\delta<\gamma
there exists a positive random variable
K_{\delta}(\omega)
such that one has

(15)

\max_{1\leq n\leq N}\|u(t_{n})-u_{n}\|_{H^{\sigma}}\leq K_{\delta}(\omega)\tau^{\delta},\qquad\mathbb{P}-{\rm a.s.}

Proof.

Since
{\rm e}^{{\bf i}t\Delta}
is an isometry on
H^{\sigma}
, we prove the result for the twisted variable
v
. We have

v(t_{n})-v_{n}=-{\bf i}\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\int_{0}^{\tau}{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}(t_{j}+s)\Delta}V(x){\rm e}^{{\bf i}(t_{j}+s)\Delta}v(t_{j}+s)\,ds-\tau{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}t_{j}\Delta}\big[\big({\rm e}^{{\bf i}t_{j}\Delta}v_{j}\big)\varphi_{1}(-{\bf i}\tau\Delta)V(x)\big].

Then, we see

\|v(t_{n})-v_{n}\|^{2p}_{H^{\sigma}}\leq C(I_{1}+\tau^{2p}I_{2}),

where

\displaystyle I_{1}:=\big\|\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\int_{0}^{\tau}{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}(t_{j}+s)\Delta}V(x){\rm e}^{{\bf i}(t_{j}+s)\Delta}v(t_{j}+s)\,ds-\tau{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}t_{j}\Delta}\big[\big({\rm e}^{{\bf i}t_{j}\Delta}v(t_{j})\big)\varphi_{1}(-{\bf i}\tau\Delta)V(x)\big]\big\|^{2p}_{H^{\sigma}},

\displaystyle I_{2}:=\big\|\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}t_{j}\Delta}\big[\big({\rm e}^{{\bf i}t_{j}\Delta}v(t_{j})\big)\varphi_{1}(-{\bf i}\tau\Delta)V(x)\big]-{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}t_{j}\Delta}\big[\big({\rm e}^{{\bf i}t_{j}\Delta}v_{j}\big)\varphi_{1}(-{\bf i}\tau\Delta)V(x)\big]\big\|^{2p}_{H^{\sigma}}.

The term
I_{2}
is estimated using the Hölder inequality, the algebra property of
H^{\sigma}
, the unitary property of
{\rm e}^{{\bf i}t\Delta}
and the boundedness property of
\varphi_{1}
in
H^{\sigma}
. One indeed has

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\leq n^{2p-1}\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\|{\rm e}^{{\bf i}t_{j}\Delta}(v(t_{j})-v_{j})\varphi_{1}(-{\bf i}\tau\Delta)V(x)\|^{2p}_{H^{\sigma}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|V\|^{2p}_{H^{\sigma}}n^{2p-1}\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\|v(t_{j})-v_{j}\|^{2p}_{H^{\sigma}}.

For the term
I_{1}
, we note that
I_{1}\leq C(I_{1,1}+I_{1,2})
, where

\displaystyle I_{1,1}:=\big\|\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\int_{0}^{\tau}{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}(t_{j}+s)\Delta}V(x){\rm e}^{{\bf i}(t_{j}+s)\Delta}\big(v(t_{j}+s)-v(t_{j})\big)\,ds\big\|^{2p}_{H^{\sigma}},\quad I_{1,2}:=\big\|\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\mathcal{R}_{t_{j},\tau}(v(t_{j}))\big\|^{2p}_{H^{\sigma}},

with

\mathcal{R}_{t_{j},\tau}(v(t_{j})):=\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}{\rm e}^{{\bf i}\ell x}\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}\\
\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}=\ell\end{subarray}}\hat{V}_{\ell_{1}}\hat{v}_{\ell_{2}}(t_{j}){\rm e}^{{\bf i}t_{n}(\ell^{2}-\ell_{2}^{2})}\int_{0}^{\tau}{\rm e}^{{\bf i}s\ell_{1}^{2}}\big({\rm e}^{2{\bf i}s\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}-1\big)\,ds.

Using Fourier decomposition and an interpolation argument, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{R}_{t_{j},\tau}(v(t_{j}))\|^{2}_{H^{\sigma}}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}(1+|\ell|)^{2\sigma}\big|\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}\\
\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}=\ell\end{subarray}}\hat{V}_{\ell_{1}}\hat{v}_{\ell_{2}}(t_{j}){\rm e}^{{\bf i}t_{n}(\ell^{2}-\ell_{2}^{2})}\int_{0}^{\tau}{\rm e}^{{\bf i}s\ell_{1}^{2}}\big({\rm e}^{2{\bf i}s\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}-1\big)\,ds\big|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}(1+|\ell|)^{2\sigma}\big(\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}\\
\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}=\ell\end{subarray}}|\hat{V}_{\ell_{1}}||\hat{v}_{\ell_{2}}(t_{j})|\int_{0}^{\tau}|2s\ell_{1}\ell_{2}|^{\gamma}\,ds\big)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\tau^{2(\gamma+1)}\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}(1+|\ell|)^{2\sigma}\big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}\\
\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}=\ell\end{subarray}}|\hat{V}_{\ell_{1}}||\hat{v}_{\ell_{2}}(t_{j})|(1+|\ell_{1}|)^{\gamma}(1+|\ell_{2}|)^{\gamma}\big)^{2}.

This gives

\|\mathcal{R}_{t_{j},\tau}(v(t_{j}))\|^{2}_{H^{\sigma}}\leq C\tau^{2(\gamma+1)}\|V\|^{2}_{H^{\sigma+\gamma}}\|v(t_{j})\|^{2}_{H^{\sigma+\gamma}}.

This, combined with the Hölder inequality, then gives

I_{1,2}\leq C\tau^{2p\gamma+1}\|V\|^{2p}_{H^{\sigma+\gamma}}\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\|v(t_{j})\|^{2p}_{H^{\sigma+\gamma}}.

It remains to estimate
I_{1,1}
. We use the mild formulation for
v(t_{j}+s)
, so we can decompose this term as
I_{1,1}\leq C(I_{1,1,1}+I_{1,1,2})
, where

\displaystyle I_{1,1,1}:=\big\|\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\int_{0}^{\tau}{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}(t_{j}+s)\Delta}V(x){\rm e}^{{\bf i}(t_{j}+s)\Delta}\bigg(\int_{0}^{s}{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}(t_{j}+r)\Delta}V(x){\rm e}^{{\bf i}(t_{j}+r)\Delta}v(t_{j}+r)\,dr\bigg)\,ds\big\|^{2p}_{H^{\sigma}},

\displaystyle I_{1,1,2}:=\big\|\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\int_{0}^{\tau}{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}(t_{j}+s)\Delta}V(x){\rm e}^{{\bf i}(t_{j}+s)\Delta}\big(\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j}+s}{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}r\Delta}Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\,dW(r)\big)\,ds\big\|^{2p}_{H^{\sigma}}.

For
I_{1,1,1}
, using similar techniques as above, we have

\displaystyle I_{1,1,1}

\displaystyle\leq n^{2p-1}\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\big(\int_{0}^{\tau}\|V(x){\rm e}^{{\bf i}(t_{j}+s)\Delta}\big(\int_{0}^{s}{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}(t_{j}+r)\Delta}V(x){\rm e}^{{\bf i}(t_{j}+r)\Delta}v(t_{j}+r)\,dr\big)\|_{H^{\sigma}}\,ds\big)^{2p}

\displaystyle\leq\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\int_{0}^{\tau}\|V(x){\rm e}^{{\bf i}(t_{j}+s)\Delta}\big(\int_{0}^{s}{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}(t_{j}+r)\Delta}V(x){\rm e}^{{\bf i}(t_{j}+r)\Delta}v(t_{j}+r)\,dr\big)\|^{2p}_{H^{\sigma}}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\tau^{2p-1}\|V\|^{2p}_{H^{\sigma}}\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{0}^{s}\|V(x)\|^{2p}_{H^{\sigma}}\|v(t_{j}+r)\|_{H^{\sigma}}^{2p}\,dr\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\tau^{2p+1}\|V\|^{4p}_{H^{\sigma}}\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[t_{j},t_{j}+\tau]}\|v(t)\|^{2p}_{H^{\sigma}}.

Then, we achieve the following bound

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\max_{1\leq n\leq N}\|v(t_{n})-v_{n}\|^{2p}_{H^{\sigma}}\big]\leq C\tau^{2p+1}\|V\|^{4p}_{H^{\sigma}}\mathbb{E}\big[\max_{1\leq n\leq N}\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[t_{j},t_{j}+\tau]}\|v(t_{j})\|^{2p}_{H^{\sigma}}\big]

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+C\tau^{2p\gamma+1}\|V\|^{2p}_{H^{\sigma+\gamma}}\mathbb{E}\big[\max_{1\leq n\leq N}\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\|v(t_{j})\|^{2p}_{H^{\sigma+\gamma}}\big]

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+C\tau\|V\|^{2p}_{H^{\sigma}}\mathbb{E}\big[\max_{1\leq n\leq N}\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\|v(t_{j})-v_{j}\|^{2p}_{H^{\sigma}}\big]

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+C\mathbb{E}\big[\max_{1\leq n\leq N}\|\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\int_{0}^{\tau}{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}(t_{j}+s)\Delta}V(x){\rm e}^{{\bf i}(t_{j}+s)\Delta}\big(\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j}+s}{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}r\Delta}Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\,dW(r)\big)\,ds\|^{2p}_{H^{\sigma}}\big]

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\leq C\tau^{2p}\|V\|^{4p}_{H^{\sigma}}\mathbb{E}\big[\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|v(t)\|^{2p}_{H^{\sigma}}\big]+C\tau^{2p\gamma}\|V\|^{2p}_{H^{\sigma+\gamma}}\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}\big[\|v(t)\|^{2p}_{H^{\sigma+\gamma}}\big]

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+C\tau\|V\|^{2p}_{H^{\sigma}}\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\big[\max_{0\leq k\leq j}\|v(t_{k})-v_{k}\|^{2p}_{H^{\sigma}}\big]

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+C\mathbb{E}\big[\max_{1\leq n\leq N}\|\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\int_{0}^{\tau}{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}(t_{j}+s)\Delta}V(x){\rm e}^{{\bf i}(t_{j}+s)\Delta}\big(\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j}+s}{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}r\Delta}Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\,dW(r)\big)\,ds\|^{2p}_{H^{\sigma}}\big].

Finally, by stochastic Fubini Theorem (see also
[
1
]
) and BDG inequality, we obtain the following estimate

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\max_{1\leq n\leq N}\big\|\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\int_{0}^{\tau}{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}(t_{j}+s)\Delta}V(x){\rm e}^{{\bf i}(t_{j}+s)\Delta}\bigg(\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j}+s}{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}r\Delta}Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\,dW(r)\bigg)\,ds\big\|^{2p}_{H^{\sigma}}\big]

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt=\mathbb{E}\big[\max_{1\leq n\leq N}\big\|\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j+1}}\int_{r}^{t_{j+1}}{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}s\Delta}V(x){\rm e}^{{\bf i}s\Delta}{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}r\Delta}Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\,ds\,dW(r)\big\|^{2p}_{H^{\sigma}}\big]

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt=\mathbb{E}\big[\max_{1\leq n\leq N}\big\|\int_{0}^{t_{n}}\int_{r}^{[\frac{r}{\tau}+1]\tau}{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}s\Delta}V(x){\rm e}^{{\bf i}s\Delta}{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}r\Delta}Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\,ds\,dW(r)\big\|^{2p}_{H^{\sigma}}\big]

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\leq\mathbb{E}\big[\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\big\|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{r}^{[\frac{r}{\tau}+1]\tau}{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}s\Delta}V(x){\rm e}^{{\bf i}s\Delta}{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}r\Delta}Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\,ds\,dW(r)\big\|^{2p}_{H^{\sigma}}\big]

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\leq C\mathbb{E}\big[\big(\int_{0}^{T}\big\|\int_{r}^{[\frac{r}{\tau}+1]\tau}{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}s\Delta}V(x){\rm e}^{{\bf i}s\Delta}{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}r\Delta}Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\,ds\big\|^{2}_{\mathcal{L}_{2}^{\sigma}}\,dr\big)^{p}\big]

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt=C\mathbb{E}\big[\big(\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j+1}}\big\|\int_{r}^{t_{j+1}}{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}s\Delta}V(x){\rm e}^{{\bf i}s\Delta}{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}r\Delta}Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\,ds\big\|^{2}_{\mathcal{L}_{2}^{\sigma}}\,dr\big)^{p}\big]

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\leq C\mathbb{E}\big[\big(\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j+1}}\big(\int_{r}^{t_{j+1}}\big\|{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}s\Delta}V(x){\rm e}^{{\bf i}s\Delta}{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}r\Delta}Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\big\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}^{\sigma}}\,ds\big)^{2}\,dr\big)^{p}\big]

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\leq C\|V\|^{2p}_{H^{\sigma}}\big\|Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\big\|^{2p}_{\mathcal{L}_{2}^{\sigma}}\mathbb{E}\big[\big(\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j+1}}|t_{j+1}-r|^{2}\,dr\big)^{p}\big]

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\leq C\|V\|^{2p}_{H^{\sigma}}\big\|Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\big\|^{2p}_{\mathcal{L}_{2}^{\sigma}}\tau^{2p}.

Then, gathering all the above estimates, thanks to Proposition
3.1
and taking into account that
2p\gamma\leq 2p
for any
\gamma\leq 1
, we get

\mathbb{E}\big[\max_{1\leq n\leq N}\|v(t_{n})-v_{n}\|^{2p}_{H^{\sigma}}\big]\leq C\tau^{2p\gamma}+C\tau\|V\|^{2p}_{H^{\sigma}}\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\big[\max_{0\leq k\leq j}\|v(t_{k})-v_{k}\|^{2p}_{H^{\sigma}}\big].

An application of the discrete Gronwall inequality yields the estimate in (
14
). Finally, the estimate in (
15
) directly follows by an application of Markov inequality, the estimate in (
14
) and Borel-Cantelli Lemma. The proof is completed.
∎

Q

As shown in
[
1
]
for the standard stochastic exponential method, applied to (
5
), also for (SLR1) we can show a nearly conservation of the trace equations for the expected mass and the energy computed along the solution to (

M(u)=\int_{\mathbb{T}}|u|^{2}\,dx,\qquad H(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}V(x)|u|^{2}\,dx.

and provide the following results, whose proofs is omitted since it is similar to the proofs on Proposition 4.6 and Proposition 4.8 in

]

3.3.
Long-time error estimates for small potential and noise

Let
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
. We consider the linear stochastic Schrodinger equation perturbed with a small rough potential and small noise, i.e., for a real valued function
\Phi
, we consider
V(x)=\varepsilon^{2}\Phi(x)
and the equation

(16)

du^{\varepsilon}(t)={\bf i}\Delta u^{\varepsilon}(t)dt-{\bf i}\varepsilon^{2}\Phi(x)u^{\varepsilon}(t)dt+\varepsilon Q^{\frac{1}{2}}dW.

Equation (
16
) describes small random fluctuations around the dynamics given by the classical deterministic linear Schrödinger equations.
The mild solution to Equation (
16
) reads

u^{\varepsilon}(t)={\rm e}^{{\bf i}t\Delta}u_{0}-{\bf i}\varepsilon^{2}\int_{0}^{t}{\rm e}^{{\bf i}(t-s)\Delta}\Phi(x)u^{\varepsilon}(s)\,ds+\varepsilon\int_{0}^{t}{\rm e}^{{\bf i}(t-s)\Delta}Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\,dW(s).

In the sequel of this work, we will use the following notation:
A\lesssim B
denotes
A\leq cB
, where
c
is a positive deterministic constant independent on
\varepsilon
and
\tau
.

Let us define
T_{\varepsilon}:=\frac{T}{\varepsilon^{2}}.

We have the following result.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
\sigma\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
and let
u_{0},\Phi\in H^{\sigma}
and
Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\in\mathcal{L}_{2}^{\sigma}
. Then, the exact solution to (
16
) satisfies

\mathbb{E}\big[\sup_{t\in[0,T_{\varepsilon}]}\|u^{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{H^{\sigma}}^{2}\big]\lesssim 1.

Proof.

By proposition
3.1
, we have global well-posedness which implies the existence and uniqueness of solution to time
\frac{T}{\varepsilon^{2}}
. We only need to show the independence of the bound from
\varepsilon
in the time window
[0,\frac{T}{\varepsilon^{2}}]
. Direct computation here gives

\displaystyle\|u^{\varepsilon}(t)\|^{2}_{H^{\sigma}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{\sigma}}^{2}+\varepsilon^{4}\|\int_{0}^{t}{\rm e}^{{\bf i}(t-s)\Delta}\Phi(x)u^{\varepsilon}(s)\,ds\|_{H^{\sigma}}^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}\|\int_{0}^{t}{\rm e}^{{\bf i}(t-s)\Delta}Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\,dW(s)\|_{H^{\sigma}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|u_{0}\|_{H^{\sigma}}^{2}+\varepsilon^{4}t\int_{0}^{t}\|\Phi(x)u^{\varepsilon}(s)\|^{2}_{H^{\sigma}}\,ds+\varepsilon^{2}\|\int_{0}^{t}{\rm e}^{{\bf i}(t-s)\Delta}Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\,dW(s)\|_{H^{\sigma}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|u_{0}\|_{H^{\sigma}}^{2}+\varepsilon^{4}t\|\Phi\|_{H^{\sigma}}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\|u^{\varepsilon}(t)\|^{2}_{H^{\sigma}}\,dt+\varepsilon^{2}\|\int_{0}^{t}{\rm e}^{{\bf i}(t-s)\Delta}Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\,dW(s)\|_{H^{\sigma}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{\sigma}}^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}\|\Phi\|_{H^{\sigma}}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\|u^{\varepsilon}(s)\|^{2}_{H^{\sigma}}\,dt+\varepsilon^{2}\|\int_{0}^{t}{\rm e}^{{\bf i}(t-s)\Delta}Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\,dW(s)\|_{H^{\sigma}}^{2}.

Taking the supremum and the expectation both sides and using BDG inequality we then get

\mathbb{E}\big[\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T_{\varepsilon}]}\|u^{\varepsilon}(t)\|^{2}_{H^{\sigma}}\big]\lesssim\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{H^{\sigma}}+\varepsilon^{2}\|\Phi\|_{H^{\sigma}}^{2}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}}\mathbb{E}\big[\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\|u^{\varepsilon}(s)\|^{2}_{H^{\sigma}}\big]\,dt+\|Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}^{\sigma}}^{2}.

An application of Gronwall inequality then gives the result.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

The smallness assumption and the linearity of the Equation (
16
) allows us to prove that the
L^{2}(\Omega;L^{2})
error of the integrator (SLR1) remains buounded and of size
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon\tau)
for time windows of length
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{-1})
and remains bounded and of size
\mathcal{O}(\tau)
for time windows of length
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{-2})
. However, the result in Proposition
3.5
and the presence of an
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{2})
potential suggests to provide an alternative numerical integrator to (
16
) that exhibits a bounded error of size
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{2}\tau)
up to intervals of length
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{-2})
. This motivate the upcoming section.

3.4.
A non-resonant low-regularity stochastic numerical integrator

We recall that the derivation of the integrator (SLR1) relied on the following approximation

(17)

\int_{0}^{\tau}{\rm e}^{{\bf i}s(\ell^{2}-\ell_{2}^{2})}\,ds\approx\int_{0}^{\tau}{\rm e}^{{\bf i}s\ell_{1}^{2}}\,ds

and we computed exactly the integral in the right hand-side of (
17
). Inspired to the idea in
[
21
]
, the construction of a non-resonant integrator for (
16
) relies on the fact that the integral involved in (
9
) can be computed exactly in the resonant case
\ell^{2}-\ell_{2}^{2}=0
. With this in hand and with approximation (
17
) for the non resonant case
\ell^{2}-\ell_{2}^{2}\neq 0
, also taking into account
v^{\varepsilon}(t)={\rm e}^{-{\bf i}t\Delta}u^{\varepsilon}(t)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}(t_{n}+s)\Delta}\big(\Phi(x){\rm e}^{{\bf i}(t_{n}+s)\Delta}v^{\varepsilon}(t_{n})\big)\,ds

\displaystyle\approx\frac{1}{2\pi}\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}{\rm e}^{{\bf i}\ell x}\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}\\
\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}=\ell\\
\ell^{2}-\ell_{2}^{2}\neq 0\end{subarray}}\hat{\Phi}_{\ell_{1}}\widehat{v^{\varepsilon}}_{\ell_{2}}(t_{n}){\rm e}^{{\bf i}t_{n}(\ell^{2}-\ell_{2}^{2})}\int_{0}^{\tau}{\rm e}^{{\bf i}s\ell_{1}^{2}}\,ds

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\frac{\tau}{2\pi}\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}{\rm e}^{{\bf i}\ell x}\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}\\
\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}=\ell\\
\ell^{2}-\ell^{2}_{2}=0\end{subarray}}\hat{\Phi}_{\ell_{1}}\widehat{v^{\varepsilon}}_{\ell_{2}}(t_{n}).

This can be equivalently written as

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}(t_{n}+s)\Delta}\big(\Phi(x){\rm e}^{{\bf i}(t_{n}+s)\Delta}v^{\varepsilon}(t_{n})\big)\,ds

\displaystyle\approx\frac{1}{2\pi}\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}{\rm e}^{{\bf i}\ell x}\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}\\
\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}=\ell\end{subarray}}{\rm e}^{{\bf i}t_{n}(\ell^{2}-\ell_{2}^{2})}\hat{\Phi}_{\ell_{1}}\widehat{v^{\varepsilon}}_{\ell_{2}}(t_{n})\tau\varphi_{1}({\bf i}\tau\ell_{1}^{2})

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\frac{1}{2\pi}\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}{\rm e}^{{\bf i}\ell x}\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}\\
\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}=\ell\\
\ell^{2}-\ell_{2}^{2}=0\end{subarray}}\hat{\Phi}_{\ell_{1}}\widehat{v^{\varepsilon}}_{\ell_{2}}(t_{n})\tau(1-\varphi_{1}({\bf i}\tau\ell_{1}^{2}))

Since
\ell^{2}-\ell_{2}^{2}=(\ell+\ell_{2})(\ell-\ell_{2})
and
\varphi_{1}(0)=1
, we rewrite the second term of the above equation as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}{\rm e}^{{\bf i}\ell x}\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}\\
\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}=\ell\\
\ell^{2}-\ell_{2}^{2}=0\end{subarray}}\hat{\Phi}_{\ell_{1}}\widehat{v^{\varepsilon}}_{\ell_{2}}(t_{n})\tau(1-\varphi_{1}({\bf i}\tau\ell_{1}^{2}))=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}{\rm e}^{{\bf i}\ell x}\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}\\
\ell_{1}=0\\
\ell_{2}=\ell\end{subarray}}\hat{\Phi}_{\ell_{1}}\widehat{v^{\varepsilon}}_{\ell_{2}}(t_{n})\tau(1-\varphi_{1}({\bf i}\tau\ell_{1}^{2}))

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\frac{1}{2\pi}\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}{\rm e}^{{\bf i}\ell x}\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}\\
\ell_{1}=2\ell\\
\ell_{2}=-\ell\end{subarray}}\hat{\Phi}_{\ell_{1}}\widehat{v^{\varepsilon}}_{\ell_{2}}(t_{n})\tau(1-\varphi_{1}({\bf i}\tau\ell_{1}^{2}))

\displaystyle=\frac{\tau}{\sqrt{2\pi}}(1-\varphi_{1}(0))\hat{\Phi}_{0}v^{\varepsilon}(t_{n})

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\frac{\tau}{2\pi}\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}{\rm e}^{{\bf i}\ell x}\hat{\Phi}_{2\ell}\widehat{v^{\varepsilon}}_{-\ell}(t_{n})(1-\varphi_{1}(4{\bf i}\tau\ell^{2}))

\displaystyle=\frac{\tau}{2\pi}\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}{\rm e}^{{\bf i}\ell x}\hat{\Phi}_{2\ell}\widehat{v^{\varepsilon}}_{-\ell}(t_{n})(1-\varphi_{1}(4{\bf i}\tau\ell^{2})).

Then, defining
g_{2}(u,v)
through its Fourier coefficients as
\widehat{(g_{2}(w,z))}_{\ell}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\hat{w}_{2\ell}\hat{z}_{-\ell}
, then, we get the following integrator for the twisted variable
v

\displaystyle v^{\varepsilon}_{n+1}=v^{\varepsilon}_{n}-{\bf i}\varepsilon^{2}\tau\big[{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}t_{n}\Delta}\big(({\rm e}^{{\bf i}t_{n}\Delta}v^{\varepsilon}_{n})\varphi_{1}(-{\bf i}\tau\Delta)\Phi(x)\big)

\displaystyle+(1-\varphi_{1}(-4{\bf i}\tau\Delta))g_{2}(\Phi(x),v^{\varepsilon}_{n})\big]

(18)

\displaystyle+\varepsilon\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n}+\tau}{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}s\Delta}Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\,dW(s).

Return back to the original variable
u
, we get

\displaystyle u^{\varepsilon}_{n+1}

\displaystyle={\rm e}^{{\bf i}\tau\Delta}\big[u^{\varepsilon}_{n}-{\bf i}\varepsilon^{2}\tau\big(u^{\varepsilon}_{n}\varphi_{1}(-{\bf i}\tau\Delta)\Phi(x)

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+(1-\varphi_{1}(-4{\bf i}\tau\Delta)){\rm e}^{{\bf i}t_{n}\Delta}g_{2}(\Phi(x),{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}t_{n}\Delta}u^{\varepsilon}_{n})\big)\big]+\varepsilon\mathcal{W}_{n}.

Finally, since

\displaystyle{\rm e}^{{\bf i}t_{n}\Delta}g_{2}(\Phi(x),{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}t_{n}\Delta}u^{\varepsilon}_{n})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}{\rm e}^{{\bf i}t_{n}\Delta}\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}{\rm e}^{{\bf i}\ell x}\hat{\Phi}_{2\ell}{\rm e}^{{\bf i}\ell^{2}t_{n}}\widehat{u^{\varepsilon}}_{-\ell,n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}{\rm e}^{{\bf i}\ell x}\hat{\Phi}_{2\ell}\widehat{u^{\varepsilon}}_{-\ell,n}

\displaystyle=g_{2}(\Phi(x),u^{\varepsilon}_{n}),

we get the following stochastic accelerated non-resonant low-regularity integrator for (
16
)

\displaystyle u^{\varepsilon}_{n+1}={\rm e}^{{\bf i}\tau\Delta}u^{\varepsilon}_{n}

\displaystyle-{\bf i}\varepsilon^{2}\tau{\rm e}^{{\bf i}\tau\Delta}\big(u^{\varepsilon}_{n}\varphi_{1}(-{\bf i}\tau\Delta)\Phi(x)\big)

(19)

\displaystyle-{\bf i}\varepsilon^{2}\tau(1-\varphi_{1}(-4{\bf i}\tau\Delta)){\rm e}^{{\bf i}\tau\Delta}g_{2}(\Phi(x),u^{\varepsilon}_{n})+\varepsilon\mathcal{W}_{n}.

We will call the integrator in (
19
) (SNRLR1).

Remark 3.7
.

It is possible to show that all the results obtained in Section
3.2
for the integrator (SLR1) holds true also for (SNRLR1). In what follows, we provide a long-term error analysis for the latter.

Recall that
v^{\varepsilon}
stands for the twisted variable efined through (
7
). Let
\varphi^{\varepsilon,\tau}
the flow map such that
v^{\varepsilon}(t_{n}+\tau)=\varphi^{\varepsilon,\tau}(v(t_{n}))
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\varphi^{\varepsilon,\tau}(v^{\varepsilon}(t_{n}))=v^{\varepsilon}(t_{n})

\displaystyle-{\bf i}\varepsilon^{2}\int_{0}^{\tau}{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}(t_{n}+s)\Delta}\big(\Phi(x){\rm e}^{{\bf i}(t_{n}+s)\Delta}v^{\varepsilon}(t_{n}+s)\big)\,ds

(20)

\displaystyle+\varepsilon\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n}+\tau}{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}s\Delta}Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\,dW(s)

and let
\Psi_{t_{n}}^{\varepsilon,\tau}
the map such that
v^{\varepsilon}_{n+1}=\Psi_{t_{n}}^{\varepsilon,\tau}(v^{\varepsilon}_{n})
.
We give the following theorem.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
\sigma\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
. Let
u_{0}\in H^{\sigma}
,
\Phi\in H^{\frac{3}{2}+}
if
\sigma=1
or
\Phi\in H^{\sigma}
if
\sigma\geq 2
, and
Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\in\mathcal{L}_{2}^{\sigma}
. Then, fixed
\tau_{0}\in(0,1)
independently from
\varepsilon
, there exists a sufficiently small
\tau
(depending only on
\tau_{0}
) such that the numerical integrator (SNRLR1), applied to (
16
), satisfies the following error estimate

(21)

\|u^{\varepsilon}(t_{n})-u^{\varepsilon}_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;L^{2})}\lesssim\tau_{0}^{\sigma}+\varepsilon^{2}\tau,\qquad\qquad 0\leq n\leq\frac{T}{\varepsilon^{2}\tau}.

Proof.

We define the quantities
\mathcal{E}^{\varepsilon}_{n}:=\Psi_{t_{n}}^{\varepsilon,\tau}(v^{\varepsilon}(t_{n}))-\varphi_{t_{n}}^{\varepsilon,\tau}(v^{\varepsilon}(t_{n}))
and
e^{\varepsilon}_{n+1}:=v^{\varepsilon}_{n+1}-v^{\varepsilon}(t_{n+1})
. Then, we have

\displaystyle e^{\varepsilon}_{n+1}

\displaystyle=v^{\varepsilon}_{n+1}-\varphi_{t_{n}}^{\varepsilon,\tau}(v^{\varepsilon}(t_{n}))

\displaystyle=v^{\varepsilon}_{n+1}-\Psi_{t_{n}}^{\varepsilon,\tau}(v^{\varepsilon}(t_{n}))+\mathcal{E}^{\varepsilon}_{n}

\displaystyle=e^{\varepsilon}_{n}+Z^{\varepsilon}_{n}+\mathcal{E}^{\varepsilon}_{n},

where

\displaystyle Z^{\varepsilon}_{n}

\displaystyle=-{\bf i}\varepsilon^{2}\tau\big[{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}t_{n}\Delta}\big(({\rm e}^{{\bf i}t_{n}\Delta}v^{\varepsilon}_{n})\varphi_{1}(-{\bf i}\tau\Delta)\Phi(x)\big)+(1-\varphi_{1}(-4{\bf i}\tau\Delta))g_{2}(\Phi(x),v^{\varepsilon}_{n})

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt-{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}t_{n}\Delta}\big(({\rm e}^{{\bf i}t_{n}\Delta}v^{\varepsilon}(t_{n}))\varphi_{1}(-{\bf i}\tau\Delta)\Phi(x)\big)+(1-\varphi_{1}(-4{\bf i}\tau\Delta))g_{2}(\Phi(x),v^{\varepsilon}(t_{n}))\big]

\displaystyle=Z^{1}_{n}+Z^{2}_{n},

where

\displaystyle Z_{n}^{\varepsilon,1}=-{\bf i}\varepsilon^{2}\tau{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}t_{n}\Delta}\big(({\rm e}^{{\bf i}t_{n}\Delta}(v^{\varepsilon}_{n}-v^{\varepsilon}(t_{n})))\varphi_{1}(-{\bf i}\tau\Delta)\Phi(x)\big)

\displaystyle Z_{n}^{\varepsilon,2}=-{\bf i}\varepsilon^{2}\tau(1-\varphi_{1}(-4{\bf i}\tau\Delta))\big(g_{2}(\Phi(x),v^{\varepsilon}_{n})-g_{2}(\Phi(x),v^{\varepsilon}(t_{n}))\big).

First, direct computations show

\displaystyle\|Z_{n}^{\varepsilon,1}\|

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{2}\tau\|{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}t_{n}\Delta}\big(({\rm e}^{{\bf i}t_{n}\Delta}(v^{\varepsilon}_{n}-v^{\varepsilon}(t_{n})))\varphi_{1}(-{\bf i}\tau\Delta)\Phi(x)\big)\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{2}\tau\|({\rm e}^{{\bf i}t_{n}\Delta}(v^{\varepsilon}_{n}-v^{\varepsilon}(t_{n})))\varphi_{1}(-{\bf i}\tau\Delta)\Phi(x)\|

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon^{2}\tau\|\varphi_{1}(-{\bf i}\tau\Delta)\Phi(x)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|e^{\varepsilon}_{n}\|

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon^{2}\tau\|\Phi\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}+}}\|e^{\varepsilon}_{n}\|

and

\displaystyle\|Z_{n}^{\varepsilon,2}\|

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon^{2}\tau\|g_{2}(\Phi(x),v^{\varepsilon}_{n})-g_{2}(\Phi(x),v^{\varepsilon}(t_{n}))\|

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{2}\tau(\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}|\hat{\Phi}_{2\ell}|^{2}|\widehat{v^{\varepsilon}}_{-\ell,n}-\widehat{v^{\varepsilon}}_{-\ell}(t_{n})|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon^{2}\tau\big(\max_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}|\Phi_{2\ell}|^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|e^{\varepsilon}_{n}\|

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon^{2}\tau\|\Phi\|\|e^{\varepsilon}_{n}\|.

This then yields

\mathbb{E}\|Z^{\varepsilon}_{n}\|^{2}\lesssim\varepsilon^{4}\tau^{2}\mathbb{E}\|e^{\varepsilon}_{n}\|^{2}.

We now have to estimate
\mathbb{E}\|e^{\varepsilon}_{n+1}\|^{2}.
We note that
\mathcal{E}^{\varepsilon}_{n}
can be decomposed as

\mathcal{E}^{\varepsilon}_{n}=\mathcal{V}^{\varepsilon}_{n}+\mathcal{R}^{\varepsilon}(v^{\varepsilon}(t_{n}))
, where

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{\varepsilon}(v^{\varepsilon}(t_{n}))={\bf i}\varepsilon^{2}\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}{\rm e}^{{\bf i}\ell x}\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}\\
\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}=\ell\\
\ell^{2}-\ell_{2}^{2}\neq 0\end{subarray}}{\rm e}^{{\bf i}t_{n}(\ell^{2}-\ell_{2}^{2})}\hat{\Phi}_{\ell_{1}}\widehat{v^{\varepsilon}}_{\ell_{2}}(t_{n})\int_{0}^{\tau}{\rm e}^{{\bf i}s\ell_{1}^{2}}-{\rm e}^{{\bf i}s(\ell^{2}-\ell_{2}^{2})}\,ds,

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{n}^{\varepsilon}={\bf i}\varepsilon^{2}\int_{0}^{\tau}{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}(t_{n}+s)\Delta}\big(\Phi(x){\rm e}^{{\bf i}(t_{n}+s)\Delta}v^{\varepsilon}(t_{n}+s)\big)\,ds-{\bf i}\varepsilon^{2}\int_{0}^{\tau}{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}(t_{n}+s)\Delta}\big(\Phi(x){\rm e}^{{\bf i}(t_{n}+s)\Delta}v^{\varepsilon}(t_{n})\big)\,ds.

By recursion, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\|e^{\varepsilon}_{n+1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\|\sum_{k=0}^{n}Z^{\varepsilon}_{k}+\mathcal{V}^{\varepsilon}_{k}+\mathcal{R}^{\varepsilon}(v^{\varepsilon}(t_{k}))\|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathbb{E}\|\sum_{k=0}^{n}Z^{\varepsilon}_{k}\|^{2}+\mathbb{E}\|\sum_{k=0}^{n}\mathcal{V}^{\varepsilon}_{k}\|^{2}+\mathbb{E}\|\sum_{k=0}^{n}\mathcal{R}^{\varepsilon}(v^{\varepsilon}(t_{k}))\|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim(n+1)\varepsilon^{4}\tau^{2}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\mathbb{E}\|e^{\varepsilon}_{k}\|^{2}+\mathbb{E}\|\sum_{k=0}^{n}\mathcal{V}^{\varepsilon}_{k}\|^{2}+\mathbb{E}\|\sum_{k=0}^{n}\mathcal{R}^{\varepsilon}(v^{\varepsilon}(t_{k}))\|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon^{2}\tau\sum_{k=0}^{n}\mathbb{E}\|e^{\varepsilon}_{k}\|^{2}+\mathbb{E}\|\sum_{k=0}^{n}\mathcal{V}^{\varepsilon}_{k}\|^{2}+\mathbb{E}\|\sum_{k=0}^{n}\mathcal{R}^{\varepsilon}(v^{\varepsilon}(t_{k}))\|^{2}.

We now estimate the term
\mathbb{E}\|\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\mathcal{V}^{\varepsilon}_{k}\|^{2}
. It is direct to see that

\mathbb{E}\|\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\mathcal{V}^{\varepsilon}_{k}\|^{2}\lesssim(n+1)\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\mathbb{E}\|\mathcal{V}^{\varepsilon}_{k,1}\|^{2}+\mathbb{E}\|\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\mathcal{V}^{\varepsilon}_{k,2}\|^{2},

where

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\|\mathcal{V}^{\varepsilon}_{k,1}\|^{2}=\varepsilon^{8}\mathbb{E}\big\|\int_{0}^{\tau}{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}(t_{k}+s)\Delta}\Phi(x){\rm e}^{{\bf i}(t_{k}+s)\Delta}\big(\int_{0}^{s}{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}(t_{k}+r)\Delta}\Phi(x){\rm e}^{{\bf i}(t_{k}+r)\Delta}v^{\varepsilon}(t_{k}+r)\,dr\big)\,ds\big\|^{2},

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\|\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\mathcal{V}^{\varepsilon}_{k,2}\|^{2}=\varepsilon^{6}\mathbb{E}\big\|\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\int_{0}^{\tau}{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}(t_{k}+s)\Delta}\Phi(x){\rm e}^{{\bf i}(t_{k}+s)\Delta}\big(\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k}+s}{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}r\Delta}Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\,dW(r)\big)\,ds\big\|^{2}.

Using twice the Hölder inequality, the fact that
{\rm e}^{{\bf i}t\Delta}
is an isometry on
L^{2}
and Proposition
3.5
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\|\mathcal{V}^{\varepsilon}_{k,1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\tau\varepsilon^{8}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{\tau}\|\Phi(x){\rm e}^{{\bf i}(t_{k}+s)\Delta}\big(\int_{0}^{s}{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}(t_{k}+r)\Delta}\Phi(x){\rm e}^{{\bf i}(t_{k}+r)\Delta}v^{\varepsilon}(t_{k}+r)\,dr\big)\|^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle\lesssim\tau\varepsilon^{8}\|\Phi\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{\tau}\|\int_{0}^{s}{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}(t_{k}+r)\Delta}\Phi(x){\rm e}^{{\bf i}(t_{k}+r)\Delta}v^{\varepsilon}(t_{k}+r)\,dr\|^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\tau^{2}\varepsilon^{8}\|\Phi\|^{4}_{L^{\infty}}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{0}^{s}\|v^{\varepsilon}(t_{k}+r)\|^{2}\,dr\,ds

\displaystyle\lesssim\tau^{4}\varepsilon^{8}.

For
\mathbb{E}\|\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\mathcal{V}^{\varepsilon}_{k,2}\|^{2}
, the stochastic Fubini theorem and Itô isometry allow the following computation

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\|\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\mathcal{V}^{\varepsilon}_{k,2}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{6}\mathbb{E}\big\|\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\int_{0}^{\tau}{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}(t_{k}+s)\Delta}\Phi(x){\rm e}^{{\bf i}(t_{k}+s)\Delta}\bigg(\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k}+s}{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}r\Delta}Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\,dW(r)\bigg)\,ds\big\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{6}\mathbb{E}\big\|\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\int_{r}^{t_{k+1}}{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}s\Delta}\Phi(x){\rm e}^{{\bf i}s\Delta}{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}r\Delta}Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\,ds\,dW(r)\big\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{6}\mathbb{E}\big\|\int_{0}^{t_{n+1}}\int_{r}^{[\frac{r}{\tau}+1]\tau}{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}s\Delta}\Phi(x){\rm e}^{{\bf i}s\Delta}{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}r\Delta}Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\,ds\,dW(r)\big\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{6}\mathbb{E}\big[\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\big\|\int_{r}^{t_{k+1}}{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}s\Delta}\Phi(x){\rm e}^{{\bf i}s\Delta}{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}r\Delta}Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\,ds\big\|^{2}_{\mathcal{L}_{2}^{0}}\,dr\big]

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon^{6}\mathbb{E}\big[\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\big(\int_{r}^{t_{k+1}}\big\|{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}s\Delta}\Phi(x){\rm e}^{{\bf i}s\Delta}{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}r\Delta}Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\big\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}^{0}}\,ds\big)^{2}\,dr\big]

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon^{6}\|\Phi\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}\big\|Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\big\|^{2}_{\mathcal{L}_{2}^{0}}\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}|t_{k+1}-r|^{2}\,dr

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon^{4}\tau^{2}.

The above computations finally give

\mathbb{E}\|\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\mathcal{V}^{\varepsilon}_{k}\|^{2}\lesssim\varepsilon^{4}\tau^{2}.

With the aid of Lemma

and Lemma

).

Proposition 4.5
.

Let us assume
\psi_{0}\in H^{\sigma+\gamma}
and
Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\in\mathcal{L}_{2}^{\sigma+\gamma}
, for
\sigma\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
and
\gamma\in(0,1]
. Then, for any
\delta<\gamma
, there exists a random variable
K_{\delta}(\omega)
such that one has

\max_{1\leq n\leq N}\|\psi(t_{n})-\psi_{n}\|_{H^{\sigma}}\leq K_{\delta}(\omega)\tau^{\delta},\qquad\mathbb{P}-{\rm a.s.}

Proof.

First, we prove almost sure convergence. For
Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\in\mathcal{L}_{2}^{\sigma}
, one has that the process

X(t)=\int_{0}^{t}{\rm e}^{{\bf i}(t-s)\Delta}Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\,dW(s)

is a.s. Hölder continuous in
H^{\sigma}
with rate
\frac{1}{2}^{-}.
This, in particular, shows that

\max_{1\leq n\leq N}\|\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}{\rm e}^{{\bf i}(t_{n}-s)\Delta}Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\,dW(s)\|_{H^{\sigma}}\leq C(\omega),\qquad\mathbb{P}-{\rm a.s.},

independently on
\tau
. Now, by Theorem
4.1
, we have that
\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\psi(t)\|_{H^{\sigma}}<\infty,{\rm a.s.}
Let
R_{0}
a random variable such that

\max_{1\leq n\leq N}\|\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}{\rm e}^{{\bf i}(t-s)\Delta}Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\,dW(s)\|_{H^{\sigma}}+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\psi(t)\|_{H^{\sigma}}\leq R_{0}.

Moreover, let
\tau\leq C_{GN}R_{0}^{-2}
and
\epsilon\in(0,1)
and assume

\max_{1\leq n\leq N}\|\psi(t_{n})-\psi_{n}\|_{H^{\sigma}}\geq\epsilon.

Set
n_{\epsilon}=\min\{n:\|\psi(t_{n})-\psi_{n}\|_{H^{\sigma}}\geq\epsilon\}
. Then, for
0\leq n\leq n_{\epsilon}-1
, one has

\displaystyle\|u_{n}\|_{H^{\sigma}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\psi(t_{n})\|_{H^{\sigma}}+\|\psi(t_{n})-\psi_{n}\|_{H^{\sigma}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\psi(t_{n})\|_{H^{\sigma}}+\epsilon

\displaystyle\leq R_{0}.

Hence, By the triangular inequality, the unitary property of the operator
{\rm e}^{{\bf i}t\Delta}
and the algebra property of
H^{\sigma}
, one has

\displaystyle\|\psi_{n+1}\|_{H^{\sigma}}\leq 2R_{0}

\displaystyle+\tau\|(\psi^{n})^{2}(\varphi_{1}(-2{\bf i}\tau\Delta)\overline{\psi_{n}})\|_{H^{\sigma}}

\displaystyle\leq 2R_{0}+C\tau\|\psi_{n}\|_{H^{\sigma}}^{3}

\displaystyle\leq 2R_{0}+C\tau R_{0}^{2}\|\psi_{n}\|_{H^{\sigma}}

\displaystyle\leq 3R_{0}.

It follows that
\psi_{n}=\psi^{3R_{0}}_{n}
, for
1\leq n\leq n_{\epsilon}
. Then, one get

\max_{1\leq n\leq N}\|\psi^{3R_{0}}(t_{n})-\psi^{3R_{0}}_{n}\|_{H^{\sigma}}\geq\epsilon,

i.e., an absurd holds since, by Lemma
4.4
, this is impossible for
\tau\to 0
. Then, we get

\lim_{\tau\to 0}\max_{1\leq n\leq N}\|\psi(t_{n})-\psi_{n}\|_{H^{\sigma}}=0,\quad\mathbb{P}-{\rm a.s.}

The rest of the proof follows in a similar arguments than those presented in
[
19
]
and then is omitted.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

Also for the cubic SSE (
26
), we get order one convergence in
H^{\sigma}
for solutions in
H^{\sigma+1}
, instead of
H^{\sigma+2}
requested by standard theory.

4.3.
long-term numerical integration for cubic SSE

In this section, similarly as done for the linear SSE with potential (
5

26
) up to large time scales, in presence of
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon)
stochastic perturbation of the correspondent deterministic cubic equation with
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon)
initial data. We start by recalling that in
[
21
]
the authors highlighted that if the deterministic cubic schrodinger equation is equipped with an
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon)
initial data, i.e.,
\psi_{0}(x)=\varepsilon\phi(x)

\dot{w}={\bf i}\Delta w-{\bf i}\varepsilon^{2}|w|^{2}w,\qquad w(0)=\phi(x).

Then, we here consider the equation

(33)

We make the following assumption.

Assumption 4.6
.

For
\phi\in H^{\sigma}
and
Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\in\mathcal{L}_{2}^{\sigma}
, for
\sigma\geq 2
, we assume the following bound for the exact solution to (

\big(\mathbb{E}\big[\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T_{\varepsilon}]}\|\psi^{\varepsilon}(t)\|^{2p}_{H^{\sigma}}\big]\big)^{\frac{1}{2p}}\lesssim 1,\qquad T_{\varepsilon}=\frac{T}{\varepsilon^{2}},

for any
p\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
.

Inspired from the analysis given in the previous section and from
[
21
]
, we here slightly modified the integrator (SLR2), integrating exactly the zero mode term in (
29
), i.e., we consider the following approximation for the twisted variable
\eta^{\varepsilon}(t)={\rm e}^{-{\bf i}t\Delta}\psi^{\varepsilon}(t)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}(t_{n}+s)\Delta}|{\rm e}^{{\bf i}(t_{n}+s)\Delta}\eta^{\varepsilon}(t_{n})|^{2}{\rm e}^{{\bf i}(t_{n}+s)\Delta}\eta^{\varepsilon}(t_{n})\,ds

\displaystyle\quad\approx\bigg(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\bigg)^{3}\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}{\rm e}^{{\bf i}\ell x}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ell_{3}\in\mathbb{Z}\\

]
and not reported here for the sake of brevity, we get the following non-resonant low-regularity method for the twisted variable
\eta^{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\eta^{\varepsilon}_{n+1}=\eta^{\varepsilon}_{n}

\displaystyle-{\bf i}\tau\varepsilon^{2}\big[{\rm e}^{-{\bf i}t_{n}\Delta}\big(({\rm e}^{{\bf i}t_{n}\Delta}\eta^{\varepsilon}_{n})^{2}\varphi_{1}(-2{\bf i}\tau\Delta){\rm e}^{-{\bf i}t_{n}\Delta}\overline{\eta^{\varepsilon}_{n}}\big)+2\widehat{\big(g(\eta^{\varepsilon}_{n})\big)}_{0}\eta^{\varepsilon}_{n}-h(\eta^{\varepsilon}_{n})\big]

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\times\big(\sum_{(\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ell_{3})\in\mathcal{I}_{\ell}^{N_{0}}}\prod_{j=1}^{3}(1+|\ell_{j}|)\big|\widehat{\eta^{\varepsilon,R}}_{\ell_{1}}(t_{k})\big|\big|\widehat{\eta^{\varepsilon,R}}_{\ell_{2}}(t_{k})\big|\big|\widehat{\eta^{\varepsilon,R}}_{\ell_{3}}(t_{k})\big|\big)^{2}\big]

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon^{6}\tau^{3}\sum_{k=0}^{N_{\varepsilon}-2}\mathbb{E}\big[\|\eta^{\varepsilon,R}(t_{k})\|^{6}_{H^{2}}\big]

\displaystyle\lesssim(\varepsilon^{2}\tau)^{2}.

Putting all together, we achieve

\mathbb{E}\big[\max_{1\leq n\leq N_{\varepsilon}}\|\mathcal{I}^{n-1}_{4,4}\|^{2}_{H^{1}}\big]\lesssim(\varepsilon^{2}\tau)^{2},

that finally gives

(46)

\mathbb{E}\big[\max_{1\leq n\leq N_{\varepsilon}}\|\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\mathcal{P}_{N_{0}}\mathcal{R}^{\varepsilon,R}(\eta^{\varepsilon,R}(t_{k}))\|^{2}_{H^{1}}\big]\lesssim(\varepsilon^{2}\tau)^{2}.

Inserting (
46
) into (
39
), we get

\mathbb{E}\big[\max_{1\leq n\leq N_{\varepsilon}}\|e^{\varepsilon,R}_{n}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\big]\lesssim\tau_{0}^{2(\sigma-1)}+(\varepsilon^{2}\tau)^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}\tau\sum_{k=0}^{N_{\varepsilon}-1}\mathbb{E}\big[\max_{0\leq l\leq k}\|e^{\varepsilon,R}_{l}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\big].

An application of the discrete Gronwall lemma then gives the estimate in (
36
).

In order to obtain (
37
), note that, when
\tau_{0}^{\sigma-1}
is neglectable, for
\tau>0
and
\delta\in(0,1)
, the Markov inequality and (
36
) yield

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big(\max_{1\leq n\leq N_{\varepsilon}}\|\psi^{\varepsilon,R}(t_{n})-\psi_{n}^{\varepsilon,R}\|_{H^{1}}\geq\varepsilon^{2}\tau^{\delta}\big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{4}\tau^{2\delta}}\mathbb{E}\big[\max_{1\leq n\leq N_{\varepsilon}}\|\psi^{\varepsilon,R}(t_{n})-\psi_{n}^{\varepsilon,R}\|^{2}_{H^{1}}\big]

\displaystyle\lesssim\tau^{2(1-\delta)}.

Choosing
\tau_{k}=q^{-k}
for some
q>1
, one has

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\big(\max_{1\leq n\leq N^{k}_{\varepsilon}}\|\psi^{\varepsilon,R}(t_{n})-\psi_{n}^{\varepsilon,R}\|_{H^{1}}\geq\varepsilon^{2}\tau_{k}^{\delta}\big)\lesssim\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}q^{-2k(1-\delta)}<\infty

independently on
\varepsilon
. The result (
37
) then follows by an application of the Borel-Cantelli lemma.
∎

Remark 4.5
.

According to Lemma
4.7
, in case of smooth solutions, i.e.,
\sigma
large enough, one then gets that the method (SNRLR2), when applied to the truncated cubic equation (
35
), shows an error of size
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{2}\tau)
in
L^{2}(\Omega;H^{1})
up to large time scales of length
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{-2})
. Moreover, almost surely, one has that the
H^{1}
error remains bounded and of size
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{2}\tau^{\delta})
on the same time scale, with any
\delta<1
, if
\tau
is sufficiently small but independent on
\varepsilon
.

With Lemma
4.7
at hand, we are now in a position to state and prove a pathwise long-term error bound for the numerical integrator (SNRLR2) applied to the cubic stochastic Schrödinger equation (
33
).

Theorem 4.8
.

Let us assume the setting of Lemma
4.7
, under the condition that the term
\tau_{0}^{\sigma-1}
is neglectable. Then, the numerical integrator (SNRLR2), applied to (
33
) satisfies

\max_{1\leq n\leq N_{\varepsilon}}\|\psi^{\varepsilon}(t_{n})-\psi^{\varepsilon}_{n}\|_{H^{1}}<\varepsilon^{2}\tau^{\delta},\qquad\mathbb{P}-{\rm a.s.},\quad N_{\varepsilon}=\frac{T}{\varepsilon^{2}\tau},

for any
\delta<1
, asymptotically in
\tau
.

Proof.

Similarly to the proof of Proposition
4.5
, let us denote

R_{1,\varepsilon}(\omega)\geq\sup_{t\in[0,T_{\varepsilon}]}\|u(t)\|_{H^{1}}+\max_{1\leq n\leq N_{\varepsilon}}\|\mathcal{W}_{n}\|_{H^{1}},

where we have explicit the fact that, in principle, one cannot assume that the random variable
R_{1,\varepsilon}
is independent from
\varepsilon
.

Now, let
\tau
sufficiently small (independently on
\varepsilon
) and assume there exists
1\leq\tilde{n}\leq N_{\varepsilon}
such that one has

\|\psi^{\varepsilon}(t_{n})-\psi^{\varepsilon}_{n}\|_{H^{1}}<\varepsilon^{2}\tau^{\delta},\ n=1,\dots,\tilde{n}-1,\quad\|\psi^{\varepsilon}(t_{\tilde{n}})-\psi^{\varepsilon}_{\tilde{n}}\|_{H^{1}}\geq\varepsilon^{2}\tau^{\delta}.

Then, for
1\leq n\leq\tilde{n}-1
and considering
\varepsilon^{2}\tau^{\delta}<1
, one has

\displaystyle\|\psi^{\varepsilon}_{n}\|_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\psi^{\varepsilon}(t_{n})\|_{H^{1}}+\|\psi^{\varepsilon}(t_{n})-\psi^{\varepsilon}_{n}\|_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\psi^{\varepsilon}(t_{n})\|_{H^{1}}+\varepsilon^{2}\tau^{\delta}

\displaystyle\leq R_{1,\varepsilon}(w)+1.

Then, using this, the triangular inequality, the unitary property of the operator
{\rm e}^{{\bf i}t\Delta}
and the algebra property of
H^{\sigma}
, one has

\displaystyle\|\psi^{\varepsilon}_{\tilde{n}}\|_{H^{1}}\leq R_{1,\varepsilon}(w)+1

\displaystyle+\varepsilon^{2}\tau C_{GN}\|\psi^{\varepsilon}_{\tilde{n}-1}\|^{3}_{H^{1}}+\varepsilon^{2}\tau\big\|(\widehat{g(\psi^{\varepsilon}_{\tilde{n}-1})}\big)_{0}{\rm e}^{{\bf i}\tau\Delta}\psi^{\varepsilon}_{\tilde{n}-1}\big\|_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle+\varepsilon^{2}\tau\|{\rm e}^{{\bf i}\tau\Delta}h(\psi^{\varepsilon}_{\tilde{n}-1})\|_{H^{1}}+\varepsilon R_{1,\varepsilon}(w),

for some
C_{GN}>0
.

By definition of
g
and using the Holder’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle|\big(\widehat{g(\psi^{\varepsilon}_{\tilde{n}-1})}\big)_{0}|

\displaystyle\leq\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\big[\left\|\psi_{\tilde{n}-1}^{\varepsilon}\right\|^{2}+\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\left|\psi^{\varepsilon}_{\tilde{n}-1}(x)\right|\left|\varphi(-2{\bf i}\tau\Delta)\overline{\psi_{\tilde{n}-1}^{\varepsilon}}(x)\right|\,{\rm d}x\big]

\displaystyle\leq\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\big[\left\|\psi_{\tilde{n}-1}^{\varepsilon}\right\|^{2}+\big(\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\left|\psi_{\tilde{n}-1}^{\varepsilon}(x)\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}x\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\big(\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\left|\varphi(-2{\bf i}\tau\Delta)\overline{\psi_{\tilde{n}-1}^{\varepsilon}}(x)\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}x\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\big]

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\left[\left\|\psi^{\varepsilon}_{\tilde{n}-1}\right\|^{2}_{H^{1}}+\left\|\psi^{\varepsilon}_{\tilde{n}-1}\right\|_{H^{1}}\left\|\varphi(-2{\bf i}\tau\Delta)\overline{\psi_{\tilde{n}-1}^{\varepsilon}}\right\|_{H^{1}}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\psi_{\tilde{n}-1}^{\varepsilon}\right\|_{H^{1}}^{2}.

This then yields

\big\|(\widehat{g(\psi_{\tilde{n}-1}^{\varepsilon})}\big)_{0}{\rm e}^{{\bf i}\tau\Delta}\psi_{\tilde{n}-1}^{\varepsilon}\big\|_{H1}\leq|(\widehat{g(\psi_{\tilde{n}-1}^{\varepsilon})}\big)_{0}|\|{\rm e}^{{\bf i}\tau\Delta}\psi^{\varepsilon}_{\tilde{n}-1}\|_{H^{1}}\lesssim\|\psi^{\varepsilon}_{\tilde{n}-1}\|^{3}_{H^{1}}.

It remains to bound the term
\left\|h(\psi^{\varepsilon}_{\tilde{n}-1})\right\|_{H^{1}}
. First note that for
w\in H^{1}
, we have

\left|\varphi_{1}\left(2{\bf i}\tau\ell^{2}\right)\hat{w}_{\ell}\right|\leq C\left|\hat{w}_{\ell}\right|,\quad\ell\in\mathbb{Z}.

Then, we get

\displaystyle\|h(\psi^{\varepsilon}_{\tilde{n}-1})\|_{H^{1}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(1+\left|\ell\right|\right)^{2}|(\widehat{h(\psi^{\varepsilon}_{\tilde{n}-1})})_{\ell}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(1+\left|\ell\right|\right)^{2}\big|\big(\widehat{\psi^{\varepsilon}}_{\tilde{n}-1,\ell}\big)^{*}\widehat{\psi^{\varepsilon}}_{\tilde{n}-1,\ell}\widehat{\psi^{\varepsilon}}_{\tilde{n}-1,\ell}\big|^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(1+\left|\ell\right|\right)^{2}\big|\varphi\left(2{\bf i}\tau\ell^{2}\right)\big(\widehat{\psi^{\varepsilon}}_{\tilde{n}-1,\ell}\big)^{*}\widehat{\psi^{\varepsilon}}_{\tilde{n}-1,\ell}\widehat{\psi^{\varepsilon}}_{\tilde{n}-1,\ell}\big|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(1+\left|\ell\right|\right)^{2}\big|\big(\widehat{\psi^{\varepsilon}}_{\tilde{n}-1,\ell}\big)^{*}\widehat{\psi^{\varepsilon}}_{\tilde{n}-1,\ell}\widehat{\psi^{\varepsilon}}_{\tilde{n}-1,\ell}\big|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\psi^{\varepsilon}_{\tilde{n}-1}\|^{3}_{H^{1}}.

Then, we end up with

\displaystyle\|\psi^{\varepsilon}_{\tilde{n}}\|_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq(1+\varepsilon)R_{1,\varepsilon}(w)+1+\varepsilon^{2}\tau C_{GN}\|\psi^{\varepsilon}_{\tilde{n}-1}\|^{3}_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq(1+\varepsilon)R_{1,\varepsilon}(w)+1+\varepsilon^{2}\tau C_{GN}(R_{1,\varepsilon}(w)+1)^{3}.

This shows that for sufficiently small
\tau
(independently on
\varepsilon
), there exists a random variable
R_{2,\varepsilon}(\omega)\geq R_{1,\varepsilon}(\omega)+1
such that

\|\psi^{\varepsilon}_{\tilde{n}}\|_{H^{1}}\leq R_{2,\varepsilon}(\omega).

This shows that
\psi^{\varepsilon}_{n}=\psi_{n}^{\varepsilon,R_{2,\varepsilon}(\omega)}
, for
n=1,\dots,\tilde{n}
and

\max_{1\leq n\leq N_{\varepsilon}}\|\psi^{\varepsilon,R_{2,\varepsilon}(\omega)}(t_{n})-\psi^{\varepsilon,R_{2,\varepsilon}(\omega)}_{n}\|\geq\varepsilon^{2}\tau^{\delta},

provided
\tau
sufficiently small. In particular, this means that one can always find a sufficiently large
k
such that

\max_{1\leq n\leq N^{k}_{\varepsilon}}\|\psi^{\varepsilon,R_{2,\varepsilon}(\omega)}(t_{n})-\psi^{\varepsilon,R_{2,\varepsilon}(\omega)}_{n}\|\geq\varepsilon^{2}\tau_{k}^{\delta},

being
\{\tau_{k}\}
the subsequence defined in Lemma
4.7
. By (
37
), this is impossible and then a contradiction holds. The proof is concluded.
∎

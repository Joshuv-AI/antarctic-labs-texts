Effect of particle size distribution on screening efficiency of vibrating screen
Authors: Guolang Shen, Zuquan Xu, Feng Dong, Xingguo Wang, Biao Luo, Gangtao Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Vibrating screen screening efficiency is affected by a variety of factors, among which particle size distribution (PSD) has long been neglected by researchers. In order to reveal the actual influence of PSD on screening efficiency, this paper systematically explores the mechanism of PSD on the screening efficiency of vibrating screen based on discrete element method simulation. Based on the relative sieve hole size of the particles, the feed particles are classified into three categories: permeable sieve particles, refractory sieve particles and obstructive particles. The simulation results show that obstructive particles significantly enhance the screening efficiency, while refractory sieve particles have an inhibitory effect and permeable sieve particles have a small effect. It was found that the probability of various types of particles passing through the sieve is basically stable under different PSD conditions, and then constructed a sieve efficiency prediction model based on the content of particles and the probability of passing through the sieve, and the prediction results are highly consistent with the simulation data, and the prediction error is controlled within ± 5%. In addition, the double-deck vibrating screen exhibited higher screening efficiency and stability under complex PSD conditions, outperforming the single-deck structure in 96.6% of the experimental groups. The research results provide a theoretical basis for the optimization of vibrating screen process parameters and structural design.

Introduction

The vibrating screen finds widespread applications across various industrial sectors, including mineral separation, construction, and pharmaceutical engineering. Over centuries of research, screening theory has advanced significantly, leading to substantial improvements in screening efficiency. Previous studies conducted by researchers have delved into the impact of vibration parameters1,2 (such as vibration frequency, amplitude, and direction angle) and structural parameters3 (including aperture, wire diameter, inclination angle, screen length, and screen width) on screening efficiency. Additionally, they’ve explored the influence of particle shape on this efficiency4,5. The findings from these studies indicate that each parameter exerts a distinct impact on screening efficiency, with several instances of optimized efficiencies achieved through different parameter combinations. These optimized results varied with parameter changes, yet the particle size distributions differed significantly among the studies. This raises the question: Does particle size distribution affect screening efficiency, and if so, what is this effect?

With the increasing prevalence of crushers and machine-made sand, the particle composition of using different crushers varies6,7. The vibrating screen is commonly employed as a post-treatment apparatus for crushed materials and machine-made sand, leading to inevitable variations in particle size distribution. The influence of particle size distribution on screening efficiency has important practical significance in many industrial fields and production efficiency. By studying the relationship between particle size distribution and screening efficiency, the screening process can be optimized and the performance of screening equipment can be improved. For example, in the mining, chemical, food processing and other industries, reasonable adjustment of parameters such as screen aperture, vibration frequency and amplitude can effectively improve screening efficiency, reduce screen hole clogging and control product quality. Further understanding of the impact of particle size distribution on the screening process will help improve production stability, save energy and reduce consumption, and promote intelligent and refined management of industrial production, thereby improving overall production efficiency. The challenge arises from disparate particle distributions in various research papers, making it arduous to ascertain the vibrating screen’s merits and shortcomings conclusively due to the diverse feeding methodologies employed by different researchers. To categorize the prevailing particle size distributions in existing literature, three primary distribution types are predominantly utilized by researchers. The first type maintains a constant particle size across the feed, with a consistent particle count at each value8–10. The second type features feeding particles within specified size ranges, maintaining a constant particle count within each range11–15. The third type involves feeding particles in a particular distribution pattern16–19. Although vibration parameters and structural parameters have been studied quite intensively, the mechanism of PSD influence on screening efficiency is not yet systematically understood. How different types of particles (permeable sieve, refractory sieve and obstructive particles) affect the stratification and sieve-through behavior of the material during the sieving process remains unclear. Vibrating screen is widely used in all kinds of crushing process after the material processing stage, different crushing methods produce large differences in the distribution of particles, the existing screening system is difficult to adapt to these changes, often leading to unstable screening efficiency and screen hole clogging problems increase. In addition, there is a growing demand for screening equipment that can operate efficiently under variable feed conditions to ensure product quality, reduce energy consumption and improve production stability. To address the above issues, this paper intends to systematically explore them through the following research work: (1) Classification of particles based on their relative size in relation to sieve holes; (2) Quantifying the effect of various types of particles on sieving efficiency in conjunction with discrete element simulations; (3) Construction of a sieve efficiency prediction model incorporating particle content and probability of sieve penetration; This study aims to provide theoretical basis and technical solutions for the optimization of industrial screening processes under variable PSD conditions.

Materials and methods

Simulation model and condition

The discrete element method (DEM) has garnered increasing attention for simulating industrial granular flow. Its widespread application includes comprehending the screening mechanisms of vibrating screens and further optimizing screening machine parameters20–22. This study employs DEM to investigate the correlation between screening efficiency and particle size distribution. A 3D model of a single-deck vibrating screen was developed to simulate particle flow on an inclined vibrated screen, depicted in Fig. 1. The vibration frequency is 20 Hz, and the amplitude is 2.5 mm. The direction of vibration is 50°, the screen inclination is 21°, and the screen box is 160 mm long, 30.5 mm wide, and 80 mm high.Fig. 1The 3D simulation model of a single-deck vibrating screen.

The 3D simulation model of a single-deck vibrating screen.

Setting of feed

In this study, all feeding particles are categorized into three types. The first comprises particles with a diameter smaller than 0.7 times the aperture size, labeled as 'permeable sieve particles.' The second type includes particles with diameters between 0.7 times and 1 time of the aperture size, termed 'refractory sieve particles.' The third type encompasses particles with diameters larger than the aperture size, denoted as 'obstructive particles.' Figure 2 illustrates the classification of particles. The investigation into the impact of particle size distribution on screening efficiency utilizes these three classifications.Fig. 2Particle classification information.

Experimental verification and simulation conditions should be as consistent as possible. Our primary focus was establishing the relationship between screening efficiency and particle size distribution, necessitating a realistic setting for the particle size distribution. The second type emerged as more practical among the three types of particle size distributions considered. It involves feeding particles within specific size ranges and maintaining a consistent number of particles within each interval.

Derivation of formulae for prediction of screening efficiency

Since screening efficiency was influenced by particle size distribution, the penetration probability of each type exhibited minimal variation with the particle size distribution. When calculating the penetration probabilities of these three particle types in simulations, the theory indicated a marginal alteration in the penetration probability of each type across different feeding types within the same model, as depicted in Fig. 6.Fig. 6Penetration probability of these three kinds of particles in simulation.

Penetration probability of these three kinds of particles in simulation.

To calculate the average penetration probability of these three particle types in simulation, the values were as follows: measured 0.758, measured 0.335, and measured 0.0003. Three additional simulations were conducted to verify that the penetration probability of each type remains unaffected by the feeding type within the same model. Each simulation exclusively fed one kind of particle, resulting in measuring 0.752, measuring 0.355, and measuring 0.001. Overall, these values exhibited minimal variation, affirming the conclusion’s reliability that each particle type’s penetration probability remains consistent across different feeding types within the same model.

To validate the theory, additional experiments were conducted to investigate the impact of penetration probability concerning particle size distribution. Conclusions

In this study, the effect of particle size distribution on the screening efficiency of vibrating screen was systematically investigated through discrete element method simulation, experimental validation and structural comparison of single-deck and double-deck vibrating screens. The main conclusions are as follows.Obstructive particles have a positive effect on screening efficiency by increasing the mass of material above the screen and improving particle stratification, while refractory sieve particles can significantly reduce screening efficiency due to poor permeability. It was shown that the average screening efficiency increased from 50.39 to 69.47% when the obstructive particles content was increased from 10 to 80%; Conversely, an increase in the proportion of refractory sieve particles reduces the efficiency from 67.95 to 45.04%. The effect of permeable sieve particles on screening efficiency was small, with average efficiency fluctuations controlled to within ± 2.5% for different particle size component configurations. Therefore, based on the law of the influence of particle size distribution on screening performance, appropriate particle size pretreatment of the material before screening, and reasonable adjustment of the proportion of particles of various particle sizes can significantly improve the overall screening efficiency.Particle size distribution significantly affects screening efficiency, with different feed compositions leading to efficiency differences of more than 20%. However, under the same sieving model, the probability of sieve penetration for each type of particles remains relatively stable despite the change in particle size distribution. Based on this law, this paper combines the particle composition with its stable probability of sieve penetration to construct a sieve efficiency prediction model, and its accuracy is verified by simulation and experiment, with the prediction error controlled within ± 5%. The method provides reliable theoretical support and practical tools for efficiency assessment and equipment optimization under various vibrating screen conditions.Under the same conditions of particle size distribution, the screening performance of double-deck vibrating screen is significantly better than that of single-deck vibrating screen. In the application example, the double-deck screen outperformed the single-deck screen in 96.6% of the feed combinations. In particular, when the feed particle size composition was 30% permeable sieve particles, 40% refractory sieve particles, and 30% obstruction particles (nearly 50% of the test samples), the screening efficiency of the double-deck screen reached 61.9%, compared to 56.7% for the single-deck screen. These results consistently demonstrate that double-deck vibrating screens have greater adaptability and stability when dealing with complex and variable particle size distributions.

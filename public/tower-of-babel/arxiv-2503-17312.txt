Quasiconformal Maps between Bowditch Boundaries of Relatively Hyperbolic Groups
Rana Sardar

Abstract
. Classifying groups up to quasi-isometry is a fundamental problem in geometric group theory. In the context of hyperbolic and relatively hyperbolic groups, one of the key invariants in this classification is the boundary at infinity. F. Paulin proved that two hyperbolic groups are quasi-isometric if and only if their Gromov boundaries are quasiconformally equivalent. In this article, we extend Paulin’s result to relatively hyperbolic groups and their Bowditch boundaries. A notion of quasiconformal map preserving the shadows of horoballs relative to a point at the Bowditch boundary is defined and we have shown that every coarsely cusp-preserving quasi-isometry between two relatively hyperbolic groups induces a shadow-preserving quasiconformal map between their Bowditch boundaries. Conversely, we have shown that if the Bowditch boundaries of two relatively hyperbolic groups are quasiconformally equivalent and the quasiconformal map coarsely preserves the shadows of horoballs relative to each boundary point, then the quasiconformal map induces a coarsely cusp-preserving quasi-isometry between those groups.

1.
Introduction

The notion of hyperbolic metric spaces was introduced by M. Gromov
[
Gro87
]
. A geodesic metric space is said to be hyperbolic if there exists a constant
\delta\geq 0
such that for any geodesic triangle
\triangle
, each side of
\triangle
is contained in a closed
\delta
-neighbourhood of the union of the other two sides.
A hyperbolic group is a finitely generated group whose Cayley graph, equipped with the word metric corresponding to some finite generating set, is a hyperbolic metric space. This definition is independent of the choice of finite generating sets, as hyperbolicity is a quasi-isometric invariant.
The Gromov boundary of a proper hyperbolic metric space provides a compactification of the space by assigning a ‘boundary’ set at infinity.

A relatively hyperbolic group is a generalized notion of a hyperbolic group and has been defined in various ways by different mathematicians. For a survey of equivalent definitions, refer to the article
[
Hru10
]
by Hruska. In this article, the definition given by Groves and Manning
[
GM08
]
is followed, which is as follows:
Let
G
be a finitely generated group with a finite generating set
S
. Equip
G
with the word metric
d_{G},
induced by
S
. Let
\{H_{1},H_{2},...,H_{n}\}
be a finite collection of infinite-index finitely generated subgroups of
G
. Consider
X
, the Cayley graph of
G
with respect to
S
, and let
\mathcal{H}_{G}
be the collection of all left cosets of
H_{1},H_{2},...,H_{n}
.
For each coset
H\in\mathcal{H}_{G}
, the combinatorial horoball
H^{h}
is analogous to a horoball in the hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. The group
G
is said to be
\delta
-hyperbolic relative to
\mathcal{H}_{G}
if the space
X^{h}
, called the cusped space and obtained by attaching combinatorial horoballs
H^{h}
to each
H\in\mathcal{H}_{G}
, is a
\delta
-hyperbolic metric space. Similar to the hyperbolic case relatively hyperbolic groups are also independent of the choice of finite generating sets. The Gromov boundary
\partial X^{h}
of

had been studied by Bowditch
[
Bow12
]
, so
\partial X^{h}
is termed as the Bowditch boundary of

and is often denoted by
\partial G^{h}
.
For a relatively hyperbolic group
(G,\mathcal{H}_{G})
, the limit set of any
H\in\mathcal{H}_{G}
is singleton in
\partial G^{h}
. A point

is called a parabolic endpoint if there exists
H_{a}\in\mathcal{H}_{G}
such that the limit set of
H_{a}
is
\{a\}
.

A quasi-isometry between two hyperbolic groups induces a homeomorphism between their Gromov boundaries, while a coarsely cusp-preserving quasi-isometry between relatively hyperbolic groups induces a homeomorphism between their Bowditch boundaries.
However, the converse does not hold in both cases. There exist hyperbolic groups, due to Pansu, Bourdon and Pajot, with homeomorphic Gromov boundaries that are not quasi-isometric to each other.
For instance, if
G_{1}
is the fundamental group of a closed real hyperbolic
4
-manifold and
G_{2}
is the fundamental group of a closed complex hyperbolic
2
-manifold. Then
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are hyperbolic and their Gromov boundaries
\partial G_{1}
and
\partial G_{2}
are both homeomorphic to the real
3
-dimensional unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{3}
, but
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are not quasi-isometric as the conformal dimensions of
\partial G_{1}
and
\partial G_{2}
are different (see
[
MT10
]
*Theorem 3.2.13, Theorem 3.3.3).
Given any
k\geq 1
, Kolpakov and Martelli in
[
KM13
]
constructed infinitely many real hyperbolic
4
-manifolds with
k
number of cusps. Thus, given a finite-volume complex hyperbolic
2
-manifold
M
, one can construct a real hyperbolic
4
- manifold
N
such that
M
and
N
have the same number of cusps. Then the fundamental groups of
M
and
N
are relatively hyperbolic and their Bowditch boundaries are homeomorphic to
\mathbb{S}^{3}
. But, similar to the case of closed manifolds, those groups are not quasi-isometric to each other.

In 1996, Paulin
[
Pau96
]
proved that if the boundaries of two hyperbolic groups are homeomorphic and quasiconformal equivalent, then they are quasi-isometric to each other, and the converse also holds. There are multiple definitions of quasi-conformal maps; one was introduced by Pansu
[
Pan89
]
. He defined the definition for general spaces and Gromov boundary of a proper hyperbolic space is a particular case. This article only focuses on this particular case and ignores the general definition. Interestingly, this definition is independent of any metric structures of the Gromov boundary.
Let
X
be a proper hyperbolic metric space with Gromov boundary
\partial X
. Fix a base point
\xi\in\partial X
. The shadow of a subset
Z
of
X
relative to
\xi
is the set of endpoints of geodesics emanating from
\xi
that intersect
Z
, and is denoted by
\mathcal{O}_{\xi}(Z)
.
A ring, analogous to an annulus, is a pair
\mathcal{O}_{\xi}B(x_{1},R)\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{\xi}B(x_{2},R)
of shadows of balls of fixed radius
R>0
. The collection of all such rings is called a ring structure on
\partial X
, and is denoted by
\mathcal{R}(\xi,R)
.
Let
X
and
Y
be two
\delta
-hyperbolic spaces for some
\delta\geq 0
, and
f\colon\partial X^{h}\to\partial Y^{h}
be a homeomorphism.
Then,
f
is said to be quasiconformal if there exists
R>2\delta
and a pair of ring structures
\mathcal{R}(\xi,R)
and
\mathcal{R}(\xi^{\prime},R)
defined on the Gromov boundaries
\partial X^{h}
and
\partial Y^{h}
, respectively, with
\xi^{\prime}=f(\xi)
such that
f\colon(\partial X^{h},\mathcal{R}(\xi,R))\to(\partial Y^{h},\mathcal{R}(\xi^{\prime},R))
sends each ring in
\mathcal{R}(\xi,R))
to a ring in
\mathcal{R}(\xi^{\prime},R))
with a controlled dilation, and

does same (see
\autoref
defn_qc_maps).

This article aims to generalize Paulin’s result to relatively hyperbolic groups.
Let
(G,\mathcal{H}_{G})
be a relatively hyperbolic group with a Cayley graph

, and
\xi
be a base point on the Bowditch boundary
\partial G^{h}

\partial G^{h}

Then the shadow of each horoball, whose Gromov boundary is not equal to

(see

Let
(G_{1},\mathcal{H}_{G_{1}})
and
(G_{2},\mathcal{H}_{G_{2}})
be two relatively hyperbolic groups. A quasiconformal map
f\colon(\partial G^{h}_{1},\mathcal{R}(\xi,R))\to(\partial G^{h}_{2},\mathcal{R}(\xi^{\prime},R))
that preserves parabolic points and also ‘roughly’ preserves the shadows of each horoball relative to the base point

\autoref

The following theorems are the main results of this article:

.

Suppose that the numbers

and

The left multiplication by a group element of a finitely generated group induces a cusp-preserving isometry (in particular, a

There are two more notions, namely quasisymmetric maps and quasi-Möbius maps, similar to quasiconformal maps. In several cases, these three notions coincide. A quasisymmetric map between two metric spaces is a generalization of bi-Lipschitz maps and controls the ratios of distances of a pair of points. A quasi-Möbius map is a homeomorphism that controls the cross-ratio of four distinct points.
In a hyperbolic metric space, corresponding to a geodesic triangle
\triangle(a,b,c)
with three distinct vertices
a,b,c
lying either in the space or on its Gromov boundary, there always exists a point
p_{abc}
, called a quasi-center or a quasi-projection, that is uniformly close to each side of
\triangle
. For any four distinct points
a,b,c,d
on the Gromov boundary, the cross-ratio
[a,b,c,d]
can be approximated in terms of the distance between the quasi-centers
p_{abc}
and
p_{acd}
, with an error depending on the hyperbolicity constant of the space.
Mackay and Sisto, in
[
MS24
]
proved that a shadow-respecting quasisymmetric homeomorphism between Bowditch boundaries of relatively hyperbolic groups induces a coarsely cusp-preserving quasi-isometry between the groups and vice versa (see
[
MS24
]
*Corollary 1.3).
In a similar theme, Hruska and Healy
[
HH21
]

independently proved that a coarsely cusp preserving quasi-isometry between relatively hyperbolic groups induces a quasi-isometry between their cusped spaces and a quasisymmetric map between their Bowditch boundaries.
Pal and Sardar
[
PS24
]
introduced the notion of a relative quasi-Möbius homeomorphism, which is as follows: Given three distinct points
a,b,c
on the Bowditch boundary, where
a
is a parabolic endpoint of a horoball
H^{h}
. Then the relative cross-ratio, denoted by
[a,b,c]
, roughly captures the distance of a quasi-center of
(a,b,c)
from the horosphere-like set
H
. A homeomorphism is called relative quasi-Möbius if it coarsely preserves the relative cross ratios. They further showed that a relative quasi-Möbius homeomorphism between Bowditch boundaries of two relatively hyperbolic groups induces a coarsely cusp-preserving quasi-isometry between those groups and vice versa (see
[
PS24
]
*Theorem 1, Theorem 2).
In view of
\autoref
thm_main_theorem_1,
\autoref
thm_main_theorem_2,
[
MS24
]

[

]
*Theorem 1, Theorem 2 the following equivalence concludes:

Corollary 1.1
.

Let
(G_{1},\mathcal{H}_{G_{1}})
and
(G_{2},\mathcal{H}_{G_{2}})
be two relatively hyperbolic groups, and let
f:\partial G_{1}^{h}\to\partial G_{2}^{h}
be a homeomorphism such that
f
and
f^{-1}
preserves the parabolic endpoints. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

is a quasiconformal map and coarsely preserves the shadows of horoballs relative to every boundary point
\zeta\in\partial G_{1}^{h}
.

(ii)

f
is a relative quasi-Möbius map.

(iii)

f
is a shadow-respecting quasisymmetry map.

Preliminaries

2.1.
Hyperbolic Spaces

In a metric space
(X,d_{X})
, a path is said to be rectifiable if it has a finite length. The metric
d_{X}
is called a length metric if the distance between any two points
x,y\in X
is equal to the infimum of the lengths of all rectifiable paths joining them. When
d_{X}
is a length metric, the space
(X,d_{X})
is called a length space.
A geodesic between two points
x
and
y
, denoted by
[x,y]
, is a continuous map
\gamma\colon[0,d_{X}(x,y)]\to X
that satisfies
\gamma(0)=x
,
\gamma(d_{X}(x,y))=y
, and
d_{X}(\gamma(s),\gamma(t))=|s-t|\text{ for all }s,t\in[0,d_{X}(x,y)]
. The space
(X,d_{X})
is said to be a geodesic metric space if, for every pair of points
x,y\in X
, there exists a geodesic joining them.
A metric space
(X,d_{X})
is said to be proper if every closed ball in
X
is compact.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
(X,d_{X})
be a geodesic metric space.

•

Geodesic Triangle: A geodesic triangle in
X
consists of three points
a,b,c\in X
(called vertices) and three geodesic segments
[a,b],[b,c],[c,a]
(called sides) joining them. A geodesic triangle with vertices
a,b,c\in X
is denoted as
\triangle(a,b,c)
.

•

Slim Triangle: Let
\delta\geq 0
. Given
a,b,c\in X
, a geodesic triangle
\triangle(a,b,c)
is said to be
\delta
-slim if any side of the triangle is contained within the closed
\delta
-neighbourhood of the union of the other two sides.

Definition 2.2

(Hyperbolic Metric Spaces)
.

A geodesic metric space
(X,d_{X})
is said to be
\delta
-hyperbolic, where
\delta\geq 0
, if every geodesic triangle in
X
is
\delta
-slim. The geodesic metric space is said to be hyperbolic if it is
\delta
-hyperbolic for some
\delta\geq 0
.

Any metric space with finite diameter, the unit disk in the complex plane with respect to the Poincare metric, trees, and Cayley graph of a fundamental group of closed genus
g
surfaces for
g\geq 2
are basic examples of hyperbolic spaces. On the other hand, any metric spaces containing a lattice isometric to
\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}
, in particular, the Euclidean spaces
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for
n\geq 2
are not hyperbolic.

Definition 2.3

(Quasi-isometries and Quasigeodesics)
.

Let
\lambda\geq 1
and
K\geq 0
. A (not necessarily continuous) map
f\colon X\to Y
, between two metric spaces
(X,d_{X})
and
(Y,d_{Y})
, is said to be
(\lambda,K)
-quasi-isometric embedding if for all
x_{1},x_{2}\in X
the following holds

\frac{1}{\lambda}d_{X}(x_{1},x_{2})-K\leq d_{Y}(f(x_{1}),f(x_{2}))\leq\lambda d_{X}(x_{1},x_{2})+K.

Moreover, if
f
is coarsely surjective, — i.e., for every
y\in Y
there exists an
x\in X
satisfying
d_{Y}(f(x),y)\leq K
) — then
f
is called a
(\lambda,K)
-quasi-isometry.
X
and
Y
are said to be quasi-isometric if there exists a quasi-isometry between them.

A quasigeodesic in
X
is a
(\lambda,K)
-quasi-isometric embedding
\gamma\colon I\to X
, where
I
is an interval (which may not be closed or bounded) in the real line
\mathbb{R}
. A
(\lambda,\lambda)
-quasigeodesic is called a
\lambda
-quasigeodesic.

Proposition 2.4

(Stability of Quasigeodesics,
[
BH99
]
)
.

For all
\delta,K\geq 0
and
\lambda\geq 1
there exists a constant
C_{1}=C_{1}(\delta,\lambda,K)
with the following property:

If
X
is a
\delta
-hyperbolic space,
\gamma
is a
(\lambda,K)
-quasigeodesic in
X
and
[x,y]
is a geodesic segment joining the endpoints

and

of
\gamma
, then
\gamma
and
[x,y]
lie within a
C_{1}
-neighbourhood of each other.

Theorem 2.5

(Quasi-isometries Preserve Hyperbolicity)
.

Let
X
and
Y
be two proper geodesic metric spaces and

be a quasi-isometry. If one of these spaces is hyperbolic, then the other must also be hyperbolic.

2.3.
Gromov Boundaries

Definition 2.8

(Gromov Boundaries)
.

Two geodesic rays
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\colon[0,\infty)\to X
in a hyperbolic metric space
(X,d_{X})
are said to be asymptotic if
sup\{d_{X}(\gamma_{1}(t)\gamma_{2}(t))\mid t\geq 0\}
is finite. Being asymptotic is an equivalence relation on geodesic rays in
X
. For each geodesic ray
\gamma
the equivalence class is denoted by
[\gamma]
.
Let
w\in X
be a base point. The Gromov boundary of
X
based at
w
is defined by

\partial_{w}X\coloneqq\{[\gamma]\mid\gamma\colon[0,\infty)\to X\ \text{is a geodesic ray with}\ \gamma(0)=w\}.

The Gromov boundary of a hyperbolic group
G
is the Gromov boundary of its Cayley graph.

Definition 2.9
.

For any
a\in\partial_{w}X
and

the set is defined as

\displaystyle V(a,r)\coloneqq\{b\in\partial_{w}X\mid\liminf_{t\to\infty}(\gamma_{1}(t),\gamma_{2}(t))_{w}\geq r,\text{ for some $\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}$ with $[\gamma_{1}]=a,[\gamma_{2}]=b$}\}

Lemma 2.10
.

The family
\{V(a,r)\mid r\geq 0\}
forms a basis of neighbourhoods at
a\in\partial_{w}X
.

Lemma 2.11

(Visibility of
\partial X

[

If
X

\delta

Definition 2.12

(Gromov Products)
.

Let
X
be a
\delta
-hyperbolic space and

be a base point, where
\delta\geq 0
. The Gromov product for a pair of points

in

(a,b)_{w}=\frac{1}{2}(d_{X}(a,w)+d_{X}(b,w)-d_{X}(a,b)).

Whenever,

, the gromov product is defined by

(a,b)_{w}=\sup\liminf_{i,j\to\infty}(a_{i},b_{j}),

where the supremum is taken over all the sequences
\{a_{i}\}
and
\{b_{j}\}
such that
a_{i}\to a
and
b_{j}\to b
.

Lemma 2.13
.

There exists

3.1.
Ring Structures on Boundaries at Infinity

In the complex plane, an annulus is ideally defined as the set of points lying between two concentric circles centered at the same point. However, from a different perspective, an annulus can also be interpreted as a pair of balls with the same center such that one is contained in the other. A ring is analogous to an annulus was defined by Pansu
[
Pan89
]
based on the concept of shadows of balls and using this defined the quasi-conformal maps. His definitions are defined for general spaces and the Gromov boundary of a proper hyperbolic space is a particular case. This article only focuses on this particular case and ignores the general definition. Interestingly, this definition is independent of any metric structures on the Gromov boundaries.

Note that if the Gromov boundary of a hyperbolic metric space contains only one point, then every structure becomes trivial. Therefore, hereafter it is assumed that the boundary at infinity contains at least two points.

d_{Y^{h}}(\Phi f(z),H_{2}^{\prime})\leq C
. Therefore,
d_{Y^{h}}(\Phi f(x),\varphi x)\leq C
and
d_{Y^{h}}(\Phi f(z),\varphi z)\leq C
. Hence,

d_{Y^{h}}(\varphi x,\varphi z)\leq\lambda+K+2C.

Also, since
G_{2}
is properly embedded in
Y^{h}
, then by
\autoref
lem_Uniformly_Properly_embedded_Lemma there exists
C_{8}=C_{8}(\delta,\lambda,K,C)
such that
d_{G_{2}}(\varphi x,\varphi z)\leq C_{8}
.

Now, let
x,z\in G_{1}
be arbitrary, and consider the partition
x=x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{n}=z
of the geodesic
[x,z]
with
d_{G_{1}}(x_{i-1},x_{i})=1
, for all
i
and
x_{i}\in[x_{i-1},x_{i+1}]
, for
1\leq i\leq n-1
. Then,

\displaystyle d_{G_{2}}(\varphi x,\varphi z)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{G_{2}}(\varphi x_{i-1},\varphi x_{i})

\displaystyle\leq nC_{8}

\displaystyle=C_{8}d_{G_{1}}(x,z)

Similarly, for all
y,w\in{G_{2}}
,

d_{G_{1}}(\psi y,\psi w)\leq C_{8}d_{G_{2}}(y,w).

Now for any
x\in G_{1}
,

\displaystyle d_{Y^{h}}(\Phi f(x),\varphi(x))\leq K

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle d_{X^{h}}(\Psi f\circ\Phi f(x),\Psi f\circ\varphi(x))\leq\lambda K+\epsilon

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle d_{X^{h}}(x,\Psi f\circ\varphi(x))\leq(\lambda K+\epsilon)+\epsilon

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle d_{X^{h}}(x,\psi\circ\varphi(x))\leq(\lambda K+2\epsilon)+K

Since,
G_{1}
is properly embedded in
X^{h}
, there exists
C_{8}^{\prime}=C_{8}^{\prime}(\delta,\lambda,\epsilon)
such that for any
x\in G_{1}

d_{G_{1}}(x,\psi\circ\varphi(x))\leq C_{8}^{\prime}
. Similarly, it can be shown that for any
y\in G_{2}
,
d_{G_{2}}(y,\varphi\circ\psi(y))\leq C_{8}^{\prime}
.

Hence,
\varphi
is a quasi-isometry and
\psi
is an inverse quasi-isometry of it.

Since for any
x\in G_{1}
,
d_{X^{h}}(\varphi(x),H^{\prime})\leq 2C
, which is uniformly bounded and hence,
\varphi
is also coarsely cusp-preserving quasi-isometry.
∎

Let
\varphi^{h}\colon X^{h}\to Y^{h}
be an extension of
\varphi
to the cusped spaces defined by

\varphi^{h}(x)=\begin{cases}\varphi(x),\text{ if }x\in G_{1}\\
\Phi f(x),\text{ if }x\notin G_{1}.\end{cases}

Then for all
x\in X^{h}
, the distances between
\varphi^{h}(x)
and
\Phi f(x)
are uniformly bounded, and therefore their boundary extension maps from
\partial G_{1}^{h}
to
\partial G_{2}^{h}
are the same and equal to
f
. Therefore,
\varphi\colon G_{1}\to G_{2}
is the required quasi-isometry, and hence
\autoref
thm_main_theorem_2 is proved.

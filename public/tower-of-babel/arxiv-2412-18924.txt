Operator K-Theory and Tempiric Representations
Jacob Bradd, Nigel Higson, Robert Yuncken

Abstract
David Vogan proved that if G G is a real reductive group, and if K K is a maximal compact subgroup of G G , then every irreducible representation of K K is included as a minimal K K -type in precisely one tempered, irreducible unitary representation of G G with real infinitesimal character, and that moreover it is included there with multiplicity one and is the unique minimal K K -type in that representation. We shall prove that the Connes-Kasparov isomorphism in operator K K -theory is equivalent to a K K -theoretic version of Vogan’s result.



1
Introduction

Alexandre Afgoustidis has suggested the name
tempiric representation
for any irreducible, tempered, unitary, real infinitesimal character representation of a real reductive group. The goal of this paper is to use the Connes-Kasparov isomorphism in operator
K
-theory to prove a
K
-theoretic version of the following theorem of David Vogan: if
G
is a real reductive group, and if
K
is a maximal compact subgroup of
G
, then every irreducible representation of
K
is included as a minimal
K
-type in precisely one tempiric representation of
G
, and moreover it is included there with multiplicity one and is the unique minimal
K
-type in that representation. See
[
Vog81
]
for the representation-theoretic ideas involved, and
[
Vog07
, Thm. 1.2]
for the precise statement of the theorem. See the introduction to
[
CHST24
]
and the paper
[
Laf02a
]
for background information about the Connes-Kasparov isomorphism from the points of view of representation theory and operator
K
-theory, respectively.

In fact we shall also show that, in the reverse direction, our
K
-theoretic version of Vogan’s theorem implies the Connes-Kasparov isomorphism. The main result of this paper therefore has two parts. It is stated precisely in Section
6
below, but informally, it is as follows:

Theorem
.

The following two assertions are equivalent:

(i)

For every real reductive group
G
, the Connes-Kasparov morphism in operator
K
-theory for the group
G
is an isomorphism of abelian groups.

(ii)

For every real reductive group
G
and maximal compact subgroup
K
, there is an isomorphism of abelian groups from the representation ring
R(K)
to the free abelian group on the tempiric representations of
G
that maps an irreducible representation
\tau\in\widehat{K}
to the combination
\sum_{\pi}\mathrm{mult}(\tau,\pi)\cdot\pi
whose coefficients are the multiplicities with which
\tau
occurs in the tempiric representations of
\pi
.

The proof of our theorem is based on the notion of the imaginary part of the infinitesimal character of an irreducible unitary representation, reviewed in Section
2
. The importance of this concept has been emphasized by David Vogan
[
Vog00
]
. We shall use it in Section
3
to filter the reduced
C^{*}
-algebra of a real reductive group by ideals whose associated subquotients have an especially simple structure.

We shall do something similar in Section
4
for the Cartan motion group associated to a real reductive group. This is much easier. Then in Section
5
we shall repeat the construction for all the groups in a smooth family of groups that interpolates between a reductive group and its Cartan motion group (the deformation to the normal cone construction). By good fortune, perhaps, the
K
-theory groups here can be computed in a very simple and direct manner from the morphisms defined in part (ii) of the theorem above. Our main results follow from this and Alain Connes’ reformulation of the Connes-Kasparov isomorphism as an assertion about the operator
K
-theory of the smooth family
[
Con94
, Prop. 9, p.141]
.

Our results have much in common with the so-called Mackey bijection between irreducible tempered unitary representations of a real reductive group and the irreducible unitary representations of its Cartan motion group. In particular, the work of Afgoustidis on the Mackey bijection
[
Afg19
,
Afg21
]
is extremely closely related (all of the necessary ideas already appear in these two papers of Afgoustidis; we have simply arranged them in a different way). Other very relevant works are
[
DH24
]
,
[
HR20
]
and
[
CHR24
]
.

2
Imaginary part of the infinitesimal character

In this section we shall gather most of the information from Lie theory and representation theory that we shall need in the paper.

2.1
Real reductive groups

Throughout the paper, we shall use the term
real reductive group
in the sense that is used in Vogan’s monograph
[
Vog81
, Ch. 0, Sec. 1]
. This means among other things that a real reductive group
G
is required to be linear, but it need not be connected, although there are restrictions in that regard. Examples include the group of real points of any connected reductive algebraic group defined over
\mathbb{R}
, including for instance
SL(n,\mathbb{R})
,
Sp(2n,\mathbb{R})
, etc.

We shall use the following structure, as discussed in
[
Vog81
, Ch. 0, Sec. 1]
. Every real reductive group
G
is equipped with a
Cartan involution

\theta\colon G\longrightarrow G

whose fixed subgroup is precisely
K
. We shall write the
Cartan decomposition
of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
into
\pm 1
eigenspaces of
\theta
as
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{s}

(we shall not follow the custom of indicating real Lie algebras by a subscript
0
; instead we shall indicate complexifications by a subscript
\mathbb{C}
). We can and will equip
\mathfrak{g}
with an invariant nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form

(2.1.1)

B\colon\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}

that is positive-definite on
\mathfrak{s}
and negative-definite on
\mathfrak{k}
, and that is invariant under
\theta
. When we speak of inner products on subspaces of
\mathfrak{k}
or of
\mathfrak{s}
, we shall always mean the positive-definite inner products on these subspaces obtained from
B
.

We shall fix throughout a maximal abelian subspace
\mathfrak{a}_{\min}
of
\mathfrak{s}
. We shall fix a system of positive restricted roots

(2.1.2)

\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{a}_{\min})\subseteq\Delta(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{a}_{\min}),

along with the associated
Iwasawa decomposition

G=KA_{\min}N_{\min}
and
minimal parabolic subgroup

(2.1.3)

P_{\min}=M_{\min}A_{\min}N_{\min}

(see for instance
[
Kna02
, Ch. VI]
). Here
M_{\min}
is the centralizer of
\mathfrak{a}_{\min}
in
K
.

We shall always denote by
S\subseteq\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{a}_{\min})
the set of simple restricted roots. The
standard parabolic subgroups
of
G
are the parabolic subgroups that include the chosen minimal parabolic subgroup
P_{\min}
, and they are in bijection with subsets
I\subseteq S
, as follows:

(2.1.4)

P_{I}=M_{I}A_{I}N_{I},\quad\mathfrak{a}_{I}=\bigcap\{\,\operatorname{ker}(\alpha):\alpha\in I\,\}.

Here, the Levi subgroup
M_{I}A_{I}
is the centralizer of
\mathfrak{a}_{I}
in
G
, and
N_{I}
is determined by the requirement that
P_{I}
be standard. See for instance
[
Kna02
, Sec. VII.7]
.

Finally, if
P=MAN
is a standard parabolic subgroup, then we shall always regard the dual vector space
\mathfrak{a}^{*}
as a vector subspace of
\mathfrak{a}_{\min}^{*}
by means of the map

(2.1.5)

\mathfrak{a}^{*}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{a}_{\min}^{*}

that maps a linear functional on
\mathfrak{a}
to its unique extension to a linear functional on
\mathfrak{a}_{\min}
that vanishes on the orthogonal complement of
\mathfrak{a}
in
\mathfrak{a}_{\min}
. The orthogonal complement may also be described as the intersection of
\mathfrak{a}_{\min}
with
\mathfrak{m}_{I}
. When
\mathfrak{a}=\mathfrak{a}_{I}
, the image of (
2.1.5
)
is the space

(2.1.6)

\mathfrak{a}_{I}^{*}\cong\{\,\gamma\in\mathfrak{a}_{\min}^{*}:\langle\alpha,\gamma\rangle=0\,\,\forall\alpha\in I\,\}.

2.2
Imaginary part of the infinitesimal character

2.2.1 Definition
.

Denote by
\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{g_{\mathbb{C}}})
the center of the enveloping algebra of
\mathfrak{g_{\mathbb{C}}}
, the complexification of the real Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. If
\mathfrak{h_{\mathbb{C}}}
is any Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g_{\mathbb{C}}}
, then we shall denote by

(2.2.2)

\xi_{\mathfrak{h}}\colon\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{g_{\mathbb{C}}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}\operatorname{Sym}(\mathfrak{h_{\mathbb{C}}})^{W(\mathfrak{g_{\mathbb{C}}},\mathfrak{h_{\mathbb{C}}})}

the Harish-Chandra isomorphism (see e.g.
[
Kna02
, Sec. V.5]
).

On the right-hand side of the Harish-Chandra isomorphism is the Weyl group-invariant part of the symmetric algebra of
\mathfrak{h_{\mathbb{C}}}
, or equivalently the algebra of Weyl group-invariant polynomial functions on
\mathfrak{h_{\mathbb{C}}}^{*}
.
Every character of
\operatorname{Sym}(\mathfrak{h_{\mathbb{C}}})^{W(\mathfrak{g_{\mathbb{C}}},\mathfrak{h_{\mathbb{C}}})}
, or in other words every algebra homomorphism

\operatorname{Sym}(\mathfrak{h_{\mathbb{C}}})^{W(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}},\mathfrak{h}_{\mathbb{C}})}\longrightarrow\mathbb{C},

is given by evaluation at some weight
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}_{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
. Two weights correspond to same character if and only if they lie in the same
W(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}},\mathfrak{h}_{\mathbb{C}})
-orbit.

It follows that every character

\chi\colon\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}})\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}

may be written as a composition

(2.2.3)

\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\xi_{\mathfrak{h}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\operatorname{Sym}(\mathfrak{h})^{W(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}},\mathfrak{h}_{\mathbb{C}})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{eval}_{\lambda}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathbb{C}

of the Harish-Chandra isomorphism
\xi_{\mathfrak{h}}
and evaluation at some
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}_{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
, which unique up to the action of
W(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}},\mathfrak{h}_{\mathbb{C}})
. We shall write such a composition as

\chi=\xi_{\mathfrak{h},\lambda}\colon\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}})\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}.

Following Vogan
[
Vog00
, Sec. 2]
, we wish to identify some of these characters as
real
and some as
imaginary
.

2.2.4 Definition
.

Let
\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{t}\oplus\mathfrak{a}
be a
\theta
-stable Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
, decomposed into its compact and noncompact parts. The
real part
of its complexification,
\mathfrak{h}_{\mathbb{C}}
, is the real vector subspace

\mathrm{Re}(\mathfrak{h}_{\mathbb{C}})=i\mathfrak{t}\oplus\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\mathfrak{h}_{\mathbb{C}}.

If the group
G
is realized as a group of real matrices in such a way that the Cartan involution is
\theta(X)=-X^{\mathrm{transp.}}
, then these are the matrices in
\mathfrak{h}_{\mathbb{C}}
with real eigenvalues. A weight
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}_{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
is
real
if it is real-valued on
\mathrm{Re}(\mathfrak{h}_{\mathbb{C}})
. Similarly it is
imaginary
if it is imaginary-valued on
\mathrm{Re}(\mathfrak{h}_{\mathbb{C}})
.

2.2.5 Lemma

(
[
Vog00
, Lemma 2.6]
)
.

The action of the Weyl group
W(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}},\mathfrak{h}_{\mathbb{C}})
on
\mathfrak{h}_{\mathbb{C}}
preserves the real subspace
\mathrm{Re}(\mathfrak{h}_{\mathbb{C}})
. More generally, if
\Ad_{g}
is any inner automorphism of the complex Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}
that conjugates a Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}_{1,\mathbb{C}}
to a second,
\mathfrak{h}_{2,\mathbb{C}}
, then
Ad_{g}
conjugates
\mathrm{Re}(\mathfrak{h}_{1,\mathbb{C}})
and
\mathrm{Im}(\mathfrak{h}_{1,\mathbb{C}})
to
\mathrm{Re}(\mathfrak{h}_{2,\mathbb{C}})
and
\mathrm{Im}(\mathfrak{h}_{2,\mathbb{C}})
, respectively.

2.2.6 Definition
.

If
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}_{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
, then we shall denote by

\lambda=\mathrm{Re}(\lambda)+\mathrm{Im}(\lambda)

the (unique) decomposition of a weight
\lambda
as a sum of real and imaginary weights.

It follows from the Lemma
2.2.5
that:

2.2.7 Lemma

(
[
Vog00
, Cor. 2.7]
)
.

Let
\mathfrak{h}
be a
\theta
-stable Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
.
Let
\chi\colon\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}})\to\mathbb{C}
be any character. If
\chi
is written in the form

\chi=\xi_{\mathfrak{h},\lambda}\colon\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}})\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}

for some
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}_{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
, then the characters

\xi_{\mathfrak{h},\mathrm{Re}(\lambda)}\colon\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}})\to\mathbb{C}\quad\text{and}\quad\xi_{\mathfrak{h},\mathrm{Im}(\lambda)}\colon\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}})\to\mathbb{C}

depend only on
\chi
, and not on the choices of
\mathfrak{h}
and
\lambda
.

2.2.8 Definition
.

Given a character
\chi\colon\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}})\to\mathbb{C}
as in Lemma
2.2.7
, we shall write

\mathrm{Re}(\chi)=\xi_{\mathfrak{h},\mathrm{Re}(\lambda)}\colon\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}})\to\mathbb{C}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{Im}(\chi)=\xi_{\mathfrak{h},\mathrm{Im}(\lambda)}\colon\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}})\to\mathbb{C}.

These are the
real and imaginary parts
of
\chi
.

Now let
\pi
be an irreducible unitary representation of
G
.
Recall that, by Schur’s Lemma,
\pi
has an associated infinitesimal character

\InfCh(\pi):\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}})\to\mathbb{C}.

From the above, we can form the real and imaginary parts of the infinitesimal character of
\pi
:

\operatorname{Re\,Inf\,Ch}(\pi)\colon\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}})\to\mathbb{C}\quad\text{and}\quad\ImInfCh(\pi)\colon\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}})\to\mathbb{C}.

We shall need some information about the real and imaginary parts of the infinitesimal character of an irreducible, tempered unitary representation, starting with the following result:

2.2.9 Theorem
.

If
\pi
is a discrete series representation of a real reductive group, then
\ImInfCh(\pi)=0
.

This follows from Harish-Chandra’s classification of the discrete series via Harish-Chandra parameters (which determine the infinitesimal characters of the discrete series). See Remark
A.1.2
in the appendix.

We shall use Theorem
2.2.9
and the following well-known principle of Harish-Chandra and Langlands
[
Lan89
]
to say something about the infinitesimal character of a general irreducible tempered unitary representation.

2.2.10 Theorem

(See for instance
[
Kna86
, Thm. 8.5.3]
)
.

Every irreducible tempered unitary representation of
G
embeds into some representation
\Ind_{P}^{G}\sigma\otimes\exp(i\nu)
, where
P=MAN
is a parabolic subgroup of
G
,
\sigma
is a discrete series representation of
M
, and
\nu\in\mathfrak{a}^{*}
.

It follows from Theorem
2.2.10
that every irreducible tempered unitary representation has the same infinitesimal character as some cuspidal principal series representatation
\Ind_{P}^{G}\sigma\otimes\exp(i\nu)
. But the infinitesimal character of the latter may be readily computed as follows:

(2.2.11)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \ImInfCh\bigl(\Ind_{P}^{G}\sigma\otimes\exp(i\nu)\bigr)&=i\nu\in i\mathfrak{a}^{*},\\
\ReInfCh\bigl(\Ind_{P}^{G}\sigma\otimes\exp(i\nu)\bigr)&=\InfCh(\sigma)\in i\mathfrak{t}^{*}.\end{aligned}\right.

See for instance Knapp
[
Kna86
, Prop. 8.22]
. The infinitesimal character in (
2.2.11
) is computed using a
\theta
-stable Cartan subalgebra of the form
\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{t}\oplus\mathfrak{a}
that is constructed from the parabolic subgroup
P=MAN
in Theorem
2.2.10
by defining
\mathfrak{t}
to be the Lie algebra of a maximal torus in
K\cap M
(which by another well-known result of Harish-Chandra is a Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{m}
) and of course defining
\mathfrak{a}
to be the Lie algebra of
A
. The infinitesimal character of the discrete series representation
\sigma
lies in
i\mathfrak{t}^{*}
thanks to Theorem
2.2.9
.

We want to use a single
\theta
-stable Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
to describe the infinitesimal characters of
all
the irreducible tempered unitary representations of
G
. To this end, we shall use the minimal parabolic subgroup that we fixed in (
2.1.3
).
Form the “minimally compact” Cartan subalgebra

(2.2.12)

\mathfrak{h}_{\min}=\mathfrak{t}_{\min}\oplus\mathfrak{a}_{\min},

where
\mathfrak{t}_{\min}
is the Lie algebra of a maximal torus in
M_{\min}
.
It follows from computations with Cayley transforms (see for instance
[
Kna02
, Sec. VI.7]
) that there is an inner automorphism of the complex Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}
that conjugates the Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}_{\mathbb{C}}
in (
2.2.11
) to
\mathfrak{h}_{\min,\mathbb{C}}
, and conjugates
\mathfrak{a}
into
\mathfrak{a}_{\min}
. This inner automorphism is not unique, of course, but the possible images of
\nu
lie within a single orbit of the restricted Weyl group:

2.2.13 Lemma

(See
[
Vog00
, Lemma 3.4]
and
[
Hel78
, Cor. VII.8.9]
)
.

Two elements of
\mathfrak{a}_{\min}
are conjugate in
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}
by an element of
\mathrm{Inn}(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}})
if and only if they are conjugate by an element of
W(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{a}_{\min})
.

We arrive at the following result:

2.2.14 Theorem

(
[
Vog00
, Cor. 3.5(1)]
)
.

The imaginary part of the infinitesimal character of any irreducible tempered unitary representation
\pi
may be written in the following form, using the
\theta
-stable Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}_{\min}{=}\mathfrak{t}_{\min}\oplus\mathfrak{a}_{\min}
:

\ImInfCh(\pi)=[(0,\nu)]\in\bigl(\mathfrak{t}_{\min}^{*}\oplus i\mathfrak{a}_{\min}^{*}\bigr)/W(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}},\mathfrak{h}_{\min,\mathbb{C}}).

The
W(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{a}_{\min})
-orbit of
\nu
in
i\mathfrak{a}_{\min}^{*}
is uniquely determined by
\ImInfCh(\pi)
.

From Lemma
2.2.13
, the inclusion of
i\mathfrak{a}_{\min}^{*}
into
\mathrm{Im}(\mathfrak{h}_{\min,\mathbb{C}})^{*}
determines a continuous map

i\mathfrak{a}_{\min}^{*}/W(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{a}_{\min})\longrightarrow\mathrm{Im}(\mathfrak{h}_{\min,\mathbb{C}})^{*}/W(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}},\mathfrak{h}_{\min,\mathbb{C}})

that is a homeomorphism onto its image. From Theorem
2.2.14
there is a diagram of continuous maps

(2.2.15)

\textstyle{\widehat{G}_{\mathrm{tempered}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\ImInfCh}
\textstyle{\mathrm{Im}(\mathfrak{h}_{\min,\mathbb{C}})^{*}/W(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}},\mathfrak{h}_{\min,\mathbb{C}})}
\textstyle{\widehat{G}_{\mathrm{tempered}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\ImInfCh}
\textstyle{i\mathfrak{a}_{\min}^{*}/W(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{a}_{\min}).\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

We have labeled the bottom map, like the top map, as the imaginary part of the the infinitesimal character, and we shall use the bottom version of the imaginary part of the the infinitesimal character from now on.

2.3
Stratification of the space of imaginary values of the infinitesimal character

Recall that we fixed a system (
2.1.2
) of positive restricted roots
and that we are denoting by

S\subseteq\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{a}_{\min})

the subset of positive (reduced) roots.

2.3.1 Definition
.

We shall write

\mathfrak{a}_{\mathrm{dom}}^{*}=\{\,\nu\in\mathfrak{a}_{\min}^{*}:\langle\nu,\alpha\rangle\geq 0\,\,\forall\alpha\in S\,\}.

This is the dominant Weyl chamber in
\mathfrak{a}_{\min}^{*}
that is associated to the given choice of system of positive roots.

The natural map

\mathfrak{a}_{\mathrm{dom}}^{*}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathfrak{a}_{\min}^{*}/W(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{a}_{\min})

induced from the inclusion of
\mathfrak{a}_{\mathrm{dom}}^{*}
into
\mathfrak{a}_{\min}^{*}
is a homeomorphism, and from now on we shall use this homeomorphism to identify imaginary parts of infinitesimal characters with points in
\mathfrak{a}_{\mathrm{dom}}^{*}
.

2.3.2 Definition
.

For every
I\subseteq S
define

\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{I,+}=\bigl\{\,\nu\in\mathfrak{a}_{\mathrm{dom}}^{*}:\langle\nu,\alpha\rangle=0\,\,\forall\alpha\in I\,\,\&\,\,\langle\nu,\beta\rangle\neq 0\,\,\forall\beta\notin I\,\bigr\}.

Note that
\mathfrak{a}_{I,+}^{*}\subseteq\mathfrak{a}_{I}^{*}
as in (
2.1.6
). Explicitly, we have

(2.3.3)

\mathfrak{a}_{I,+}^{*}=\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{\mathrm{dom}}\cap\mathfrak{a}_{I}^{*}\setminus\bigcup_{I\subsetneqq J}\mathfrak{a}_{J}^{*}.

The sets
\mathfrak{a}_{I,+}^{*}
are locally closed subsets of
\mathfrak{a}_{\mathrm{dom}}^{*}
, and
the full chamber
\mathfrak{a}_{\mathrm{dom}}^{*}
is the disjoint union of all of them:

(2.3.4)

\mathfrak{a}_{\mathrm{dom}}^{*}=\bigsqcup_{I\subseteq S}\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{I,+}.

At one extreme, when
I=\emptyset
,
\mathfrak{a}_{I,+}^{*}
is the interior of the dominant chamber; at the other, when
I{=}S
,
\mathfrak{a}_{I,+}^{*}
is the intersection of
\mathfrak{a}_{\min}
with the center of
\mathfrak{g}
(and so for instance it is a point if the center of
G
is compact).

2.4
Classification of representations via the imaginary part of the infinitesimal character

In this section we shall describe Vogan’s characterization of the fibers of the map
\ImInfCh
in (
2.2.15
) over the various components in (
2.3.4
).

2.4.1 Definition
.

Let
G
be a real reductive group.

(i)

An irreducible unitary representation
\pi
of a real reductive group is said to have
real infinitesimal character
if

\ImInfCh(\pi)=0\in\mathfrak{a}_{\mathrm{dom}}^{*}.

(ii)

An irreducible, tempered unitary representation of
G
is said to be
tempiric
(terminology of Afgoustidis) if it has real infinitesimal character. We shall denote by
\widehat{G}_{\mathrm{tempiric}}
the set of unitary equivalence classes of tempiric representations of
G
.

2.4.2 Theorem

(
[
Vog00
, Cor. 3.5(2)]
)
.

Let
I
be a subset of the set of simple roots
S\subseteq\Delta(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{a}_{\min})
, let
\nu\in\mathfrak{a}_{I,+}^{*}
, and denote by
P_{I}
the associated standard parabolic subgroup of
G
, as in (
2.1.3
). The correspondence

\sigma\longmapsto\Ind_{P_{I}}^{G}\sigma{\otimes}\exp(i\nu)

determines a bijection from the set of unitary equivalence classes of tempiric representations
\sigma
of
M_{I}
to the set of unitary equivalence classes of irreducible tempered unitary representations
\pi
of
G
for which

\ImInfCh(\pi)=\nu
.

2.4.3 Remark
.

Actually, Vogan considered general unitary representations in
[
Vog00
]
, rather than tempered representations, and for these the result is much more substantial. Vogan credits the characterization in this case to Knapp
[
Kna86
, Thm. 16.10]
. The proof of the tempered case that is stated above as Theorem
2.4.2
is relatively straightforward; see Section
A.2
.

Theorem
2.4.2
determines a bijection

(2.4.4)

\widehat{G}_{\mathrm{tempered}}\cong\bigsqcup_{I\subseteq S}\widehat{M}_{I,\mathrm{tempiric}}\times i\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{I,+}.

This will be central to what follows.

3
A decomposition of the reduced group C*-algebra via the imaginary part of the infinitesimal character

The purpose of this section is to obtain from the decomposition of the tempered dual in (
2.4.4
) above a filtration of the reduced
C^{*}
-algebra of the real reductive group
G
by ideals whose subquotients correspond to the individual parts in (
2.4.4
), indexed by
I\subseteq S
.

3.1
The reduced group C*-algebra and the infinitesimal character

Let
G
be a real reductive group and denote by
C^{*}_{r}(G)
the reduced group
C^{*}
-algebra of
G
. In this section we shall use the theory of the imaginary part of the infinitesimal character that we reviewed in the last section to fiber the spectrum
\widehat{G}_{\mathrm{tempered}}
of
C_{r}^{*}(G)
over
i\mathfrak{a}_{\mathrm{dom}}^{*}
. In order to do this, we must prove that the imaginary part of the infinitesimal character defines a continuous map.

3.1.1 Theorem
.

The map

\ImInfCh:\widehat{G}_{\mathrm{tempered}}\to i\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{\mathrm{dom}}

is continuous.

3.1.2 Remark
.

The full infinitesimal character is also continuous map, of course, see Theorem
3.1.9
, but it is the imaginary part which is essential to our view of the Connes-Kasparov isomorphism.

3.1.3 Definition
.

Let
G
be any Lie group and let
T\in\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}})
. Define a function

(3.1.4)

\InfCh_{T}\colon\widehat{G}\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}

on the unitary dual of
G
by

\InfCh_{T}(\pi)I=\pi(T)

(this is an identity between operators acting on the smooth vectors in the Hilbert space of the representation
\pi
).

We shall use the following simple and well-known fact. We include a proof because we shall use the same argument to prove a less familiar fact later on, in Lemma
5.2.2
.

3.1.5 Lemma
.

Let
G
be any Lie group. For each
T\in\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}})
, the function
\InfCh_{T}
is a continuous function on the unitary dual of
G

(
and in particular, in the reductive case, on the tempered dual
)
.

In this paper we shall mostly understand the topology on the dual through the bijection between closed sets in the spectrum of a
C^{*}
-algebra and closed, two-sided ideals in that
C^{*}
-algebra (in which the closed set in the spectrum associated to an ideal is the set of representations that vanish on the ideal). See
[
Dix77
, Ch.3 and Ch.13]
for information about the topology of the unitary dual, and for information about the topology on the spectra of
C^{*}
-algebras in general.

But for the proof of the lemma, it will be better to use the following characterization of convergence in the unitary dual in terms of matrix coefficients: a net
\{\pi_{\alpha}\}
of irreducible unitary representations of
G
converges to an irreducible unitary representation
\pi
in the dual if and only if there are unit vectors
v_{\alpha}
in the Hilbert spaces of the representations
\pi_{\alpha}
, and a unit vector
v
in the Hilbert space of
\pi
, such that

(3.1.6)

\lim_{\alpha\to\infty}\langle v_{\alpha},\pi_{\alpha}(a)v_{\alpha}\rangle=\langle v,\pi(a)v\rangle\qquad\forall a\in C_{c}^{\infty}(G).

See again
[
Dix77
, Ch.3 and Ch.13]
.

Proof of Lemma
3.1.5
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}})
. Given (
3.1.6
) above, we wish to prove that

(3.1.7)

\lim_{\alpha\to\infty}\InfCh_{T}(\pi_{\alpha})=\InfCh_{T}(\pi).

Choose
a\in C_{c}^{\infty}(G)
such that
\langle v,\pi(a)v\rangle\neq 0
and then write

\InfCh_{T}(\pi_{\alpha})\langle v_{\alpha},\pi_{\alpha}(a)v_{\alpha}\rangle=\langle v_{\alpha},\pi_{\alpha}(T)\pi_{\alpha}(a)v_{\alpha}\rangle=\langle v_{\alpha},\pi_{\alpha}(Ta)v_{\alpha}\rangle,

where in the last inner product we view
T
as a differential operator on
G
. We find that

\lim_{\alpha\to\infty}\InfCh_{T}(\pi_{\alpha})\langle v_{\alpha},\pi_{\alpha}(a)v_{\alpha}\rangle=\langle v,\pi(Ta)v\rangle=\InfCh_{T}(\pi)\langle v,\pi(a)v\rangle,

from which (
3.1.4
) follows.
∎

3.1.8 Definition
.

Let
\mathfrak{h}
be a Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
.
Define the algebra morphism

\InfCh_{\mathfrak{h}}:\widehat{G}_{\mathrm{tempered}}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{h}^{*}_{\mathbb{C}}/W(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}},\mathfrak{h}_{\mathbb{C}})

by means of the formula

\InfCh_{\mathfrak{h}}(\pi)=[\lambda]\quad\text{when}\quad\InfCh(\pi)=\xi_{\mathfrak{h},\lambda}.

When
\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{h}_{\min}
, we shall often write simply
\InfCh
in place of
\InfCh_{\mathfrak{h}_{\min}}
.

3.1.9 Theorem
.

Let
G
be a real reductive group and let
\mathfrak{h}
be a Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
.
The map

\InfCh_{\mathfrak{h}}:\widehat{G}_{\mathrm{tempered}}\to\mathfrak{h}_{\mathbb{C}}^{*}/W(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}},\mathfrak{h}_{\mathbb{C}})

is continuous.

Proof.

It suffices to prove that the composition
f\circ\InfCh_{\mathfrak{h}}
is continuous for any
f\in\Sym(\mathfrak{h}_{\mathbb{C}})^{W(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}},\mathfrak{h}_{\mathbb{C}})}
. This follows from Lemma
3.1.5
and the Harish-Chandra homomorphism. ∎

Proof of Theorem
3.1.1
.

The inclusion

i\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{\mathrm{dom}}\xrightarrow{\cong}i\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{\min}/W(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{a}_{\min})\to\mathrm{Im}(\mathfrak{h}_{\min,\mathbb{C}})^{*}/W(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}},\mathfrak{h}_{\min,\mathbb{C}})

is a homeomorphism onto its image. Therefore it suffices to prove that the composition of
\ImInfCh
with the above inclusion is continuous. This composition is simply
\InfCh
composed with the (continuous) projection

\mathfrak{h}^{*}_{\min,\mathbb{C}}/W(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}},\mathfrak{h}_{\min,\mathbb{C}})\longrightarrow\mathrm{Im}(\mathfrak{h}^{*}_{\min,\mathbb{C}})/W(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}},\mathfrak{h}_{\min,\mathbb{C}}),

so continuity follows from Theorem
3.1.9
.
∎

3.1.10 Remark
.

By means of the Dauns-Hofmann theorem (see
[
DH68
]
or
[
Ped79
, Sec. 4.4]
for an exposition), the continuity of
\ImInfCh
allows us to embed
C_{0}(i\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{\mathrm{dom}})
into the center of the multiplier algebra of
C_{r}^{*}(G)
.
More precisely, there is a unique inclusion of the
C^{*}
-algebra
C_{0}(i\mathfrak{a}_{\mathrm{dom}}^{*})
into the center of the multiplier algebra of
C^{*}_{r}(G)
such that

\pi(f\cdot a)=f(\ImInfCh(\pi))\cdot\pi(a)

for every tempered irreducible representation
\pi
, every
f\in C_{0}(i\mathfrak{a}_{\mathrm{dom}}^{*})
and every
a\in C^{*}_{r}(G)
.

3.2
Definition of the subquotients

We shall use the imaginary part of the infinitesimal character to construct a (finite) filtration of
C^{*}_{r}(G)
by ideals, whose subquotients have a particularly simple structure.

3.2.1 Definition
.

For every
I\subseteq S
define

U_{I}=\bigl\{\,\nu\in\mathfrak{a}_{\mathrm{dom}}^{*}:\langle\nu,\beta\rangle\neq 0\,\,\forall\beta\notin I\,\bigr\}=\bigsqcup_{J\subseteq I}\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{J,+}.

Thanks to the continuity of
\ImInfCh
, the tempered representations whose imaginary part of the infinitesimal character lies in
U_{I}
forms an open subset of
\widehat{G}_{\mathrm{tempered}}
. We shall denote by

C^{*}_{r}(G;U_{I})\triangleleft C^{*}_{r}(G)

the
C^{*}
-algebra ideal in the reduced group
C^{*}
-algebra associated to this open subset of
\widehat{G}_{\mathrm{tempered}}
. That is,
C^{*}_{r}(G,U_{I})
is the mutual kernel of all the irreducible representations
\pi
of
C^{*}_{r}(G)
for which
\ImInfCh(\pi)
lies in the complement of
U_{I}
.

It follows from the formula (
2.3.3
) that
\mathfrak{a}_{I,+}^{*}
is a closed subset of
U_{I}
.
We shall denote by

\pi_{I}\colon C^{*}_{r}(G;U_{I})\longrightarrow C^{*}_{r}(G;I)

the quotient associated to this closed subset. That is,
C^{*}_{r}(G;I)
is the quotient of
C^{*}_{r}(G;U_{I})
by the ideal consisting of all elements that vanish in every representation
\pi
with

\ImInfCh(\pi)\in U_{I}\setminus\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{I,+}
.

By elementary
C^{*}
-algebra theory
[
Dix77
, Ch.2]
, every irreducible representation
\pi
of
C^{*}_{r}(G)
for which
\ImInfCh(\pi)\in U_{I}
restricts to an irreducible representation of
C^{*}_{r}(G;U_{I})
, and moreover every irreducible representation of
C^{*}_{r}(G;U_{I})
extends uniquely to an irreducible representation
\pi
of
C^{*}_{r}(G)
for which
\ImInfCh(\pi)\in U_{I}
. Moreover, an irreducible representation
\pi
of
C^{*}_{r}(G;U_{I})
factors through the quotient
C^{*}_{r}(G;I)
if and only if
\ImInfCh(\pi)\in\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{\min,I}
. More precisely:

3.2.2 Lemma
.

The procedure that associates to each irreducible representation
\pi^{\prime}
of
C^{*}_{r}(G;I)
the unique extension to
C^{*}_{r}(G)
of the composition of
\pi^{\prime}
with the quotient map
C^{*}_{r}(G;U_{I})\to C^{*}_{r}(G;I)
in Definition
3.2.1

determines a homeomorphism

\widehat{C^{*}_{r}(G;I)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}\bigl\{\,[\pi]\in\widehat{G}_{\mathrm{tempered}}:\ImInfCh(\pi)\in\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{I,+}\,\bigr\}.\qed

3.3
Discrete series representations and tempiric representations

We shall use the following substantial results of Harish-Chandra about the discrete series representations of real reductive groups:

3.3.1 Theorem
.

Let
G
be a real reductive group and let
K
be a maximal compact subgroup of
G
. Every irreducible representation of
K
occurs as a
K
-type in at most finitely many discrete series representations of
G
.

3.3.2 Remark
.

In view of the aims of this paper, it is interesting to note that this result follows from the Connes-Kasparov isomorphism. See Section
A.1
.

Theorem
3.3.1
has the following consequence:

3.3.3 Theorem
.

Let
G
be a real reductive group and let
K
be a maximal compact subgroup of
G
. Every irreducible representation of
K
occurs as a
K
-type in at most finitely many tempiric representations of
G
.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.2.10
and (
2.2.11
), for every tempiric representation
\pi
of
G
there is a parabolic subgroup
P{=}MAN
of
G
and a discrete series representation
(\sigma,H_{\sigma})
of
M
such that
\pi
embeds as a subrepresentation of
\Ind_{P}^{G}\sigma{\otimes}1
. By Frobenius reciprocity,

\Hom_{K}(V_{\gamma},\Ind_{P}^{G}\sigma{\otimes}1)\cong\Hom_{M\cap K}(V_{\gamma},H_{\sigma}).

Therefore,
V_{\gamma}
occurs in
\Ind_{P}^{G}\sigma{\otimes}1
only if an
(M{\cap}K)
-type occurring in
V_{\gamma}
also occurs in
H_{\sigma}
. By Theorem
3.3.3
, there are finitely many discrete series representations
\sigma
of
M
which contain one of the (finitely many)
(M{\cap}K)
-types occurring in
V_{\gamma}
. We conclude that
V_{\gamma}
is contained in finitely many representations of the form
\Ind_{P}^{G}(\sigma\otimes 1)
, and in particular it is contained in finitely many tempiric representations.
∎

3.3.4 Corollary
.

The topological subspace
\widehat{G}_{\mathrm{tempiric}}\subseteq\widehat{G}
is a closed subspace, and the topology that it inherits from
\widehat{G}
is the discrete topology
(
in which every subset is an open set
)
. ∎

Proof.

The subspace
\widehat{G}_{\mathrm{tempiric}}\subseteq\widehat{G}_{\mathrm{tempered}}
is the preimage of
0
under
\ImInfCh
, and is therefore closed. Theorem
3.3.3
shows that the topology of
\widehat{G}_{\mathrm{tempiric}}
has a basis of finite open sets. But points in the unitary dual of a real reductive group are closed in the topology of the unitary dual (see for instance
[
Dix77
, Sec. 15.5]
). The result follows.
∎

3.4
Structure of the subquotients

In this section we shall fix a subset
I{\subseteq}S
, and determine
C^{*}_{r}(G;I)
up to isomorphism.

3.4.1 Theorem
.

For every
\sigma\in\widehat{M}_{I,\mathrm{tempiric}}
there is a unique surjective
C^{*}
-algebra morphism

\pi_{\sigma}\colon C^{*}_{r}(G;I)\longrightarrow C_{0}\bigl(\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{I,+},\mathfrak{K}(\Ind_{P_{I}}^{G}H_{\sigma})\bigr)

such that

\pi_{\sigma}(a)(\nu)=\pi_{\sigma,\nu}(a)

for every
\nu\in\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{I,+}
and every
a\in C^{*}_{r}(G;I)
.

Proof.

Of course, we would like to
define

\pi_{\sigma}
by the formula

\pi_{\sigma}(a)(\nu)=\pi_{\sigma,\nu}(a)

that appears in the statement of the theorem.
That we may do so follows from the considerations in
[
CCH16
, Lemma 4.10]
, which show that
\pi_{\sigma,\nu}(a)
is a continuous function of
\nu
, and indeed a
C_{0}
-function of
\nu
(this is essentially the Riemanna-Lebesgue lemma; compare the proof of Lemma
5.4.3
below, which deals with the same issue for a different group). It remains to prove that
\pi_{\sigma}
is surjective; this follows from Dixmier’s Stone-Weierstrass theorem
[
Dix77
, Thm. 11.1.8]
.
∎

3.4.2 Theorem
.

There is a unique
C^{*}
-algebra isomorphism

C^{*}_{r}(G;I)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}\bigoplus_{\sigma\in\widehat{M}_{I,\mathrm{tempiric}}}C_{0}\bigl(\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{I,+},\mathfrak{K}(\Ind_{P_{I}}^{G}H_{\sigma})\bigr)

whose composition with the projection onto the
\sigma
-summand in the direct sum is
\pi_{\sigma}
.

Proof.

This follows from Theorem
3.4.1
and the fact that the supports of the representations
\pi_{\sigma}
, for
\sigma\in\widehat{M}_{I,\mathrm{tempiric}}
, are pairwise disjoint. Compare
[
CCH16
, Lemma 5.14]
.
∎

4
The Cartan motion group

Let
G
be a real reductive group and let
K
be a maximal compact subgroup. The associated
Cartan motion group
is the semidirect product group

G_{0}=K\ltimes(\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{k})

that is constructed from the adjoint action of
K
on
\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{k}
. In this section we shall review the unitary representation theory of
G_{0}
and related matters, and construct a theory of the imaginary part of the infinitesimal character for the irreducible unitary representations of
G_{0}
.

4.1
Irreducible unitary representations of the Cartan motion group

In what follows we shall use the Cartan decomposition

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{s}

to identify the quotient vector space
\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{k}
with
\mathfrak{s}
, and so write the Cartan motion group as

(4.1.1)

G_{0}=K\ltimes\mathfrak{s}.

Let
\xi
be a linear functional on the real vector space
\mathfrak{s}
, and let
K_{\xi}
be its isotropy subgroup in
K
for the coadjoint action. Mackey
[
Mac49
]
proved that if
\tau
is any irreducible unitary representation of
K_{\xi}
, then the induced unitary representation

(4.1.2)

\rho_{\tau,\xi}=\Ind_{K_{\xi}\ltimes\mathfrak{s}}^{K\ltimes\mathfrak{s}}\tau\otimes\exp(i\xi)

is an irreducible unitary representation of
G_{0}
. He further proved that every irreducible unitary representation of
G_{0}
is equivalent to a representation of this form, and that the only equivalences among these representations are the obvious ones coming from
K
-conjugacies, so that from (
4.1.2
) we obtain a bijection

(4.1.3)

\widehat{G}_{0}\cong\Bigr(\bigsqcup_{\xi\in\mathfrak{s}^{*}}\widehat{K}_{\xi}\Bigl)\Big/K.

For a review of these results from the
C^{*}
-algebraic point of view of this paper, see
[
Hig08
, Sec. 3.1]
.

The bijection in (
4.1.3
) can adapted to our current purposes by using the inclusion of
\mathfrak{a}_{\min}
into
\mathfrak{s}
, along with the following terminology and conventions (for our later purposes we shall give more details in the following definition than are immediately needed).

4.1.4 Definition
.

Let
I
be a subset of the set of simple roots in
\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{a}_{\min})
and let
\nu\in\mathfrak{a}_{I,+}^{*}
(notation from Definition
2.3.2
). Let
K_{I}
be the centralizer of
\mathfrak{a}_{I}
in
K
(this may also be described as the isotropy group of the single element
\nu
, as well as the intersection
K{\cap}M_{I}
) and let
\sigma
be a unitary representation of
K_{I}
.
We shall denote by
\rho_{\sigma,\nu}
the unitary representation

\rho_{\sigma,\nu}=\Ind_{K_{I}\ltimes\mathfrak{s}}^{K\ltimes\mathfrak{s}}\sigma\otimes\exp(i\nu)

of
G_{0}=K\ltimes\mathfrak{s}
. Here
\nu
is extended to a linear functional on
\mathfrak{s}
that is zero on the orthogonal complement of
\mathfrak{a}_{I}
in
\mathfrak{s}
. We shall realize the representation as follows: the Hilbert space of the representation is
\Ind_{K_{I}}^{K}H_{\sigma}=L^{2}(K,H_{\sigma})^{K_{I}}
and the action of
G_{0}
on an element
f\in L^{2}(K,H_{\sigma})^{K_{I}}
is determined by

\begin{cases}(k\cdot f)(k_{1})=f(k^{-1}k_{1})&\forall k,k_{1}\in K,\\
(X\cdot f)(k_{1})=e^{i\nu(\Ad_{k_{1}}^{-1}(X))}f(k_{1})&\forall X\in\mathfrak{s}\,\,\forall k_{1}\in K.\end{cases}

4.1.5 Remark
.

For the time being we are interested in the cases of the definition where
\sigma
is an irreducible unitary representation of
K_{I}
, but later we shall consider some unitary but reducible representations, too.

With this notation established, the bijection (
4.1.3
) can be recast as follows:

4.1.6 Theorem
.

The representations
\rho_{\tau,\nu}
of
G_{0}
, as
I
ranges over the finite subsets of the set of
S
of simple restricted roots, as
\tau
ranges over the unitary dual of
K_{I}
, and as
\nu
ranges over
\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{I,+}
, are irreducible and pairwise inequivalent. They exhaust the unitary dual of
G_{0}
, and determine a bijection

\widehat{G}_{0}\cong\bigsqcup_{I\subseteq S}\widehat{K}_{I}\times\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{I,+}.\qed

4.1.7 Remark
.

Of course, this is to be compared with the similar bijection for the tempered dual of
G
in (
2.4.4
). The two bijections are the foundation of Afgoustidis’s Mackey bijection
[
Afg21
]
.

4.2
Imaginary part of the infinitesimal character for the motion group

The vector subspace
\mathfrak{s}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
, considered as an abelian Lie algebra, is a Lie subalgebra of the Lie algebra of the Cartan motion group
G_{0}
, and there is an inclusion

\operatorname{Sym}(\mathfrak{s})^{K}\longrightarrow\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{g}_{0})^{K}.

Often, but not always, this is an isomorphism. In any case, each
T\in\operatorname{Sym}(\mathfrak{s})^{K}
determines a continuous map

(4.2.1)

\InfCh_{T}\colon\widehat{G}_{0}\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}

by the formula

\InfCh_{T}(\pi)\cdot I=\pi(T)

(action on smooth vectors).

The map (
4.2.1
) is easy to compute: identifying the element
T\in\operatorname{Sym}(\mathfrak{s})^{K}
with a
K
-invariant polynomial function on
\mathfrak{s}^{*}
we have

\InfCh_{T}(\rho_{\tau,\nu})=T(\nu).

4.2.2 Lemma
.

(4.2.3)

\begin{gathered}\InfCh\colon\widehat{G}_{0}\longrightarrow i\mathfrak{s}^{*}/K\\
\InfCh(\rho_{\tau,\nu})=i\nu\in i\mathfrak{s}^{*}/K,\end{gathered}

is continuous.

Proof.

This follows from Lemma
3.1.5
.
∎

4.2.4 Remark
.

As in Remark
3.1.10
, the Dauns-Hofmann theorem allows us to embed
C_{0}(i\mathfrak{s}^{*}/K)
into the center of the multiplier algebra of
C^{*}(G_{0})
. However, the Dauns-Hofmann theorem is not necessary as Fourier theory
[
Hig08
, Thm. 3.2]
provides an isomorphism

C^{*}_{r}(G_{0})\cong C\bigl(i\mathfrak{s}^{*},\mathfrak{K}(L^{2}(K))\bigr)^{K}

under which the action of
C_{0}(i\mathfrak{s}^{*}/K)
is simply pointwise multiplication.

Since the inclusion of
i\mathfrak{a}_{\min}^{*}
into
i\mathfrak{s}^{*}
as imaginary-valued linear functionals vanishing on the orthogonal complement of
\mathfrak{a}_{\min}
induces a homeomorphism

i\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{\min}/W(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{a}_{\min})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}i\mathfrak{s}^{*}/K,

we can view
\InfCh(\pi)
as an element of
i\mathfrak{a}_{\min}^{*}/W(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{a}_{\min})
, and so obtain from (
4.2.3
) a continuous map

(4.2.5)

\InfCh\colon\widehat{G}_{0}\longrightarrow i\mathfrak{a}_{\min}^{*}/W(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{a}_{\min}).

A comparison between this and the map
\ImInfCh
that we defined in the reductive case, see (
2.2.15
), makes it reasonable to also use the notation
\ImInfCh
for the map in (
4.2.5
), which we shall do from now on. In other words, we are effectively declaring the real part of the infinitesimal character to be always zero on
\widehat{G}_{0}
. Thus

(4.2.6)

\ImInfCh(\rho_{\tau,\nu}):=i\nu\in i\mathfrak{a}_{\min}^{*}/W(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{a}_{\min})\cong i\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{\mathrm{dom}}.

4.3
Subquotients of the motion group C*-algebra

Using the continuous map
\ImInfCh
just defined on
\widehat{G}_{0}
, we can now repeat the constructions of Section
3.2
more or less word for word, with
G
replaced by
G_{0}
.

4.3.1 Definition
.

We shall denote by

C^{*}(G_{0};U_{I})\triangleleft C^{*}(G_{0})

the
C^{*}
-algebra ideal in
C^{*}(G_{0})
associated to the open subset of the unitary dual consisting of representations whose infinitesimal character belongs to the open subset
U_{I}\subseteq\mathfrak{a}_{\mathrm{dom}}^{*}
in Definition
3.2.1
. We shall denote by
C^{*}(G_{0};I)
the quotient
C^{*}
-algebra associated to the closed subset
\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{\min,I}\subseteq U_{I}
, and by

\rho_{I}\colon C^{*}(G_{0};U_{I})\longrightarrow C^{*}(G_{0};I)

the quotient mapping.

4.3.2 Theorem
.

For every
\tau\in\widehat{K}_{I}
there is a unique surjective
C^{*}
-algebra morphism

\rho_{\tau}\colon C^{*}(G_{0};I)\longrightarrow C_{0}\bigl(\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{I,+},\mathfrak{K}(\Ind_{K_{I}}^{K}H_{\tau})\bigr)

such that

\rho_{\tau}(\rho_{I}(a))(\nu)=\rho_{\tau,\nu}(a)

for every
\nu\in\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{I,+}
and every
a\in C^{*}(G_{0};U_{I})
.
∎

4.3.3 Theorem
.

There is a unique
C^{*}
-algebra isomorphism

C^{*}(G_{0};I)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}\bigoplus_{\tau\in\widehat{K}_{I}}C_{0}\bigl(\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{I,+},\mathfrak{K}(\Ind_{K_{I}}^{K}H_{\tau})\bigr)

whose composition with the projection onto the
\tau
-summand in the direct sum is the morphism
\rho_{\tau}
from Theorem
4.3.2
. ∎

5
Scaling the imaginary part of the infinitesimal character

The Mackey deformation family
[
Hig08
,
Afg19
]
is a smooth one-parameter family of Lie groups that links a real reductive group
G
to its Cartan motion group.
In this section we shall extend the constructions of the previous sections—the imaginary part of the infinitesimal character and the associated filtration of the reduced group
C^{*}
-algebra by ideals—to the context of the Mackey family.

5.1
Deformation families of Lie groups

Let
G
be a Lie group and let
K
be a compact subgroup of
G
(or, for nearly everything that follows, any closed subgroup). The deformation to the normal cone construction, applied to
K\subseteq G
, yields a smooth family of Lie groups
\{G_{t}\}_{t\in[0,\infty)}
with

(5.1.1)

G_{t}=\begin{cases}G&t>0\\
K\ltimes(\mathfrak{g/k})&t=0.\end{cases}

See
[
Hig08
, Sec. 6.2]
for this particular instance of the deformation to the normal cone construction.
We shall use the following features:

(i)

The disjoint union
\boldsymbol{G}=\sqcup\{G_{t}:t\geq 0\}
is equipped with a smooth manifold (with boundary) structure for which the map

\boldsymbol{G}\to[0,\infty),\qquad G_{t}\ni g\mapsto t\in[0,\infty)

is a submersion.

(ii)

The fiberwise multiplication, inverse and unit maps
\boldsymbol{G}\times_{[0,\infty)}\boldsymbol{G}\to\boldsymbol{G}
,
\boldsymbol{G}\to\boldsymbol{G}
and
[0,\infty)\to\boldsymbol{G}
are smooth maps.

(iii)

If
V
is a finite-dimensional real vector space, and if
E\colon V\to G
is a smooth map for which
E(0)=e
, and for which the map

K\times V\to G,\qquad(k,v)\mapsto k\cdot E(v)

is a diffeomorphism, then the map

K\times V\times[0,\infty)\to\boldsymbol{G};\qquad(k,v,t)\mapsto\begin{cases}k\cdot E(tv)\in G_{t}&t>0\\
(k,dE(v))\in G_{0}&t=0\end{cases}

is a diffeomorphism, where
dE\colon V\to\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{k}
, is the composition of the derivative of
E
at
0\in V
with the projection
\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{k}
(for this, we use
G_{0}=K\ltimes\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{k}
).

5.1.2 Remark
.

A diffeomorphism as in point (iii) exists in all of the cases of interest to us. Specifically, when
G
is a real reductive group and
K
a maximal compact subgroup, we have the Cartan decomposition. Likewise, when
P
is a parabolic subgroup and
K
is a maximal parabolic of
P
, we can combine the Langlands decomposition of the Levi factor with the exponential map on the nilpotent radical.

Equip the groups
G_{t}
with Haar measures
\mu_{t}
, as follows. Fix a Haar measure
\mu{=}\mu_{1}
on
G{=}G_{t}
, and then define

(5.1.3)

\mu_{t}{=}t^{-d}\mu\qquad\text{for }t>0,

where
d{=}\dim(G/K)
. As for
\mu_{0}
, the measure
\mu
with which we started determines a Lebesgue measure on
\mathfrak{g}
, which may be written as the product of a Lebesgue measure
\mu_{\mathfrak{k}}
on
\mathfrak{k}
and a Lebesgue measure
\mu_{\mathfrak{s}}
on
\mathfrak{s}
(the summands in the Cartan decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{s}
). The measure
\mu_{\mathfrak{k}}
determines a Haar measure
\mu_{K}
on
K
, and we define
\mu_{0}
to be the product of
\mu_{K}
and
\mu_{\mathfrak{s}}
on
G\cong K\ltimes\mathfrak{s}
.

The measures
\mu_{t}
vary smoothly with
t
in the sense that if
f
is a smooth and compactly supported function on
\boldsymbol{G}
, then
\int_{G_{t}}f(g_{t})\,d\mu_{t}g_{t}
is a smooth function of
t
. Moreover the operations of fiberwise convolution and adjoint define
*
-algebra structure on the space
C_{c}^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{G})
of all smooth and compactly supported functions on
\boldsymbol{G}
.

5.1.4 Definition
.

We shall denote by
C^{*}_{r}(\boldsymbol{G})
the
C^{*}
-algebra completion of the
*
-algebra
C_{c}^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{G})
in the norm

\|f\|=\sup\bigl\{\,\|f_{t}\|_{C^{*}_{r}(G_{t})}:t\geq 0\,\bigr\},

where
f_{t}
is the restriction of
f
to
G_{t}\subseteq\boldsymbol{G}
.

5.1.5 Remark
.

It is shown in
[
Hig08
, Lemma 6.13]
that for
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{G})
the norms
\|f_{t}\|_{C^{*}_{r}(G_{t})}
vary continuously with
t
, so that we obtain from the constructions above a continuous field of
C^{*}
-algebras
\{C^{*}_{r}(G_{t})\}_{t\geq 0}
, in the sense of
[
Dix77
, Ch. 10]
.

The spectrum of
C^{*}_{r}(\boldsymbol{G})
is the disjoint union

\widehat{\boldsymbol{G}}_{\mathrm{tempered}}:=\widehat{C^{*}_{r}(\boldsymbol{G})}=\bigsqcup_{t\geq 0}\widehat{G}_{t,\mathrm{tempered}}.

We shall equip it with the usual hull-kernel topology it carries as the spectrum of a
C^{*}
-algebra.
But for the most part, we shall only need the following simpler notion of convergence among representations of the groups
G_{t}
.

5.1.6 Definition
.

By a
continuous family of unitary representations
of
\boldsymbol{G}
on a fixed Hilbert space
H
we shall mean a strongly continuous map
\pi:\boldsymbol{G}\to U(H)
whose restriction
\pi_{t}
to each
G_{t}
is a unitary representation.

5.1.7 Lemma
.

If
\{\pi_{t}\}
is a continuous family of representations in the above sense, and if
\pi_{t}
is irreducible for all
t>0
, then every irreducible component of
\pi_{0}
is a limit of
\{\pi_{t}\}
in
\widehat{\boldsymbol{G}}
as
t\to 0
. ∎

5.1.8 Example
.

Let
\pi:G\to U(H)
be a unitary representation. Define

\begin{cases}\pi_{t}(g)=\pi(g)&t{>}0\\
\pi_{0}(k,X)=\pi(k)&t{=}0.\end{cases}

Then
\{\pi_{t}\}_{t\in[0,\infty)}
is a continuous family of representations.

5.1.9 Example
.

Let
P{=}MAN
be a parabolic subgroup of a real reductive group. Form the deformation to the normal cone
\boldsymbol{P}
associated to
P
and the compact subgroup
K{\cap}M
. Let
\sigma
be an irreducible unitary representation of
M
and let
\nu\colon\mathfrak{a}\to\mathbb{R}
be a linear functional. Define unitary representations
\lambda_{t}
of
P_{t}
on the Hilbert space of
\sigma
by

\begin{cases}\lambda_{t}(man)=e^{it^{-1}\nu(\log(a))}\cdot\sigma(m)&t{>}0\\
\lambda_{0}(k,X)=e^{i\nu(X)}\cdot\sigma(k)&t{=}0,\end{cases}

where
\nu
is transferred to a linear functional on
\mathfrak{p}/(\mathfrak{k}{\cap}\mathfrak{m})
by setting it equal to zero on the image of
\mathfrak{m}\oplus\mathfrak{n}
. This is a continuous family of representations of
\boldsymbol{P}
.

The following proposition is a special case of a more general principle that unitary induction takes continuous families to continuous families.

5.1.10 Proposition
.

Let
P{=}MAN
be a parabolic subgroup of a real reductive group
G
. Let
\sigma
be a unitary representation of
M
and let
\nu\in\mathfrak{a}^{*}
. The representations

\pi_{t}=\begin{cases}\pi_{\sigma,t^{-1}\nu}&t{>}0\\
\rho_{\sigma,\nu}&t{=}0\end{cases}

form a continuous family of unitary representations of
\boldsymbol{G}
.

Proof.

Note that
\boldsymbol{P}
embeds as a smooth closed submanifold of
\boldsymbol{G}
. For
g\in\boldsymbol{G}
we have a (non-unique) decomposition

g=\kappa(g)\cdot\varphi(g)

with
\kappa(g)\in K
and
\varphi(g)\in\boldsymbol{P}
. It is not usually possible to choose
\kappa
and
\varphi
to be globally smooth functions, but we can at least choose them to be smooth in a neighbourhood of any point
g\in\boldsymbol{G}
.

Now for
\xi\in L^{2}(K,H_{\sigma})^{M\cap K}
we may write

(\pi(g)\xi)(k^{\prime})=\delta^{-1/2}(\varphi(g^{-1}k^{\prime}))\cdot\lambda(\varphi(g^{-1}k^{\prime}))^{-1}\xi(\kappa(g^{-1}k^{\prime})),

where
\delta
is the modular function for
P
(a necessary ingredient in unitary induction) and where
\lambda
is as in Example
5.1.9
. This, plus the formula above, show that
g\mapsto\pi(g)\xi
is continuous from
g
to
L^{2}(K,H_{\sigma})
, as claimed.
∎

5.2
The imaginary part of the infinitesimal character for the Mackey deformation

We turn now to infinitesimal characters in the context of the deformation to the normal cone family. The fundamental observation underlying all of this is the following Lemma. For the groups of interest to us, it is a consequence of the condition (iii) on
\boldsymbol{G}
in Section
5.1
.

5.2.1 Lemma
.

Let
G
be any Lie group, let
K
be a compact subgroup of
G

(
or indeed any closed subgroup
)
, and let
X
be a right-invariant vector field on
G
. The family of vector fields
X_{t}=tX
on
G_{t}
, for
t{>}0
, extends to a smooth family of vector fields on the fibers
G_{t}
of the deformation family
\boldsymbol{G}
. If we consider
X
as an element in
\mathfrak{g}
, then the vector field
X_{0}
on
G_{0}
is the right-invariant vector field associated to the image of
X
in
\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{k}
. ∎

Let us apply this to elements in the enveloping algebra of
G
. If
T\in\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}})
, and if
T
has order
k
for the usual increasing filtration on the enveloping algebra (so that
T
is a sum of products of
k
or fewer elements from
\mathfrak{g}
), then the family of right-invariant differential operators

T_{t}=t^{k}T\colon C_{c}^{\infty}(G_{t})\longrightarrow C_{c}^{\infty}(G_{t})\qquad(t>0)

extends to a smooth family of right-invariant differential operators defined on all of the fibers of
\boldsymbol{G}
. We obtain from this a families version of Lemma
3.1.5
; we shall formulate it for real reductive groups and the tempered dual only because we have not defined the full unitary dual of
\boldsymbol{G}
.

5.2.2 Lemma
.

Let
G
be a real reductive group and let
T\in\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}})
. If
T
has order
k
, and if
T_{t}=t^{k}T
for
T{\neq}0
, then the function

\widehat{G}_{t}\ni\pi_{t}\longmapsto\InfCh_{T_{t}}(\pi_{t})\in\mathbb{C}\qquad(t>0)

extends at
t{=}0
to a continuous function on
\widehat{\boldsymbol{G}}_{\mathrm{tempered}}
.

Proof.

The argument that was used for the proof of Lemma
3.1.5
may be applied here, too.
∎

5.2.3 Theorem
.

Let
G
be a real reductive group and let
\mathfrak{h}_{\mathbb{C}}
be a Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}
. The family of maps

t\cdot\InfCh\colon\widehat{G}_{t,\mathrm{tempered}}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{h}_{\mathbb{C}}^{*}/W(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}},\mathfrak{h}_{\mathbb{C}})\qquad(t>0)

extends to a continuous map

\widehat{\boldsymbol{G}}_{\mathrm{tempered}}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{h}_{\mathbb{C}}^{*}/W(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}},\mathfrak{h}_{\mathbb{C}}).

Specifically, taking
\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{h}_{\mathrm{min}}
, the continuous extension to
t=0
is given by the infinitesimal character of
G_{0}
, from (
4.2.6
),

\InfCh:\widehat{G}_{0}\to i\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{\mathrm{min}}/W(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{a}_{\mathrm{min}})\subseteq\mathfrak{h}_{\min,\mathbb{C}}^{*}/W(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}},\mathfrak{h}_{\mathbb{C}}).

Proof.

According to Chevalley’s theorem
[
Che55
]
, there are homogeneous, Weyl group-invariant polynomial functions
p_{1},\ldots,p_{r}
on
\mathfrak{h}^{*}_{\mathbb{C}}
, say of degrees
k_{1},\dots,k_{r}
, that freely generate the algebra of all Weyl group-invariant polynomial functions on
\mathfrak{h}^{*}_{\mathbb{C}}
. The function

\mathfrak{h}^{*}_{\mathbb{C}}/W(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}},\mathfrak{h}_{\mathbb{C}})\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{r},\qquad\lambda\longmapsto(p_{1}(\lambda),\dots,p_{r}(\lambda))

is then a homeomorphism (indeed an isomorphism of affine varieties). The compositions of this homeomorphism with the maps in the statement of the theorem are the maps

\widehat{G}_{t}\ni\pi_{t}\longmapsto\bigl(\InfCh_{T_{1,t}}(\pi_{t}),\dots,\InfCh_{T_{r,t}}(\pi_{t})\bigr),

where where
T_{j}\in\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}})
corresponds to
p_{j}\in\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{h}_{\mathbb{C}}^{*}]
under the Harish-Chandra isomorphism, and where
T_{j,t}=t^{k_{j}}T_{j}
. Since
T_{j}
has order
k_{j}
, it follows from Lemma
5.2.2
that the composition gives a continuous map from
\widehat{\boldsymbol{G}}_{\mathrm{tempered}}
to
\mathbb{C}^{r}
. This proves the existence of the continuous extension.

In order to determine the value of this extension at
t{=}0
, we make an explicit computation using the minimal principal series of
G
.
We have seen in (
2.2.11
) that for the minimal parabolic subgroup
P=P_{\min}
of
G
,

\InfCh(\pi_{\sigma,t^{-1}\nu})=\InfCh(\sigma){\oplus}it^{-1}\nu\in i\mathfrak{t}^{*}_{\min}{\oplus}i\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{\min},

so that

(5.2.4)

t\cdot\InfCh(\pi_{\sigma,t^{-1}\nu})=t\cdot\InfCh(\sigma){\oplus}i\nu\in i\mathfrak{t}^{*}_{\min}{\oplus}i\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{\min}.

The representation
\pi_{\sigma,\nu}
will be irreducible if
\nu
is fixed by no nontrivial element of
W(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{a}_{\min})
,
but in any case it always has an infinitesimal character. According to Proposition
5.1.10
, the representations
\pi_{\sigma,t^{-1}\nu}
of
G_{t}
extend to a continuous family of representations with limit
\rho_{\sigma,\nu}
on
G_{0}
. The continuous extension is therefore given on
\widehat{G}_{0}
by

\rho_{\tau,\nu}\longmapsto 0{\oplus}i\nu\in i\mathfrak{t}^{*}_{\min}{\oplus}i\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{\min},

for every irreducible constituent
\rho_{\tau,\nu}
of
\rho_{\sigma,\nu}

Since every
\rho_{\tau,\nu}
occurs in some
\rho_{\sigma,\nu}
this completely determines the continuous extension to
t{=}0
as claimed.
∎

The imaginary part of the infinitesimal character always belongs to the image of the inclusion of
i\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{\mathrm{dom}}
into
\mathfrak{h}^{*}_{\mathbb{C}}/W(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}},\mathfrak{h}_{\mathbb{C}})
; see Theorem
3.1.1
. Therefore, composing the map of Theorem
5.2.3
with the projection

\mathfrak{h}^{*}_{\mathbb{C}}/W(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}},\mathfrak{h}_{\mathbb{C}})\longrightarrow\mathrm{Im}(\mathfrak{h}^{*}_{\mathbb{C}})/W(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}},\mathfrak{h}_{\mathbb{C}}),

we obtain the the following.

5.2.5 Theorem
.

Let
G
be a real reductive group. There is a continuous map

\widehat{\boldsymbol{G}}_{\mathrm{tempered}}\longrightarrow i\mathfrak{a}_{\mathrm{dom}}^{*}

such that

\widehat{G}_{t,\mathrm{tempered}}\ni\pi_{t}\longmapsto t\cdot\ImInfCh({\pi})\qquad(t{>}0)

and

\widehat{G}_{0,\mathrm{tempered}}\ni\pi_{0}\longmapsto\ImInfCh({\pi})\qquad(t{=}0).

∎

5.2.6 Definition
.

Given an irreducible representation
\pi
of
C^{*}_{r}(\boldsymbol{G})
, we shall call the element of
i\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{\mathrm{dom}}
obtained by applying the map in Theorem
5.2.5
to
\pi
the
scaled imaginary part of the infinitesimal character
of
\pi
.

Thus for
t{>}0
the scaled imaginary part of the infinitesimal character of the irreducible representation

\pi_{t}{=}\pi_{\sigma,t^{-1}\nu}
of
G_{t}{=}G
is
i\nu
, while the scaled imaginary part of the infinitesimal character of the irreducible representation
\rho_{\tau,\nu}
of
G_{0}
is
i\nu
.

5.2.7 Remark
.

Similarly to
G
and
G_{0}
, by the Dauns-Hofmann theorem there is a unique inclusion of
C_{0}(i\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{\mathrm{dom}})
into the center of the multiplier algebra of
C_{r}^{*}(\boldsymbol{G})
such that

\pi_{t}\bigl((f\cdot a)(t)\bigr)=f\bigl(t\cdot\ImInfCh(\pi_{t})\bigr)\cdot\pi_{t}(a)

for all
t{>}0
, and

\pi_{0}\bigl((f\cdot a)(0)\bigr)=f(\ImInfCh(\pi_{0}))\cdot\pi_{0}(a)

for all irreducible tempered unitary representations
\pi_{t}
of
G_{t}
and
\pi_{0}
of
G_{0}
, all
f\in C_{0}(i\mathfrak{a}_{\mathrm{dom}}^{*})
and all
a\in C^{*}_{r}(\boldsymbol{G})
.

5.3
Subquotients of the C*-algebra of continuous sections

5.3.1 Definition
.

We shall denote by

C^{*}_{r}(\boldsymbol{G};U_{I})\triangleleft C^{*}_{r}(\boldsymbol{G})

the
C^{*}
-algebra ideal associated to the open subset of the tempered dual consisting of representations whose scaled infinitesimal character belongs to the open subset
U_{I}\subseteq\mathfrak{a}_{\mathrm{dom}}^{*}
. We shall denote by

C^{*}_{r}(\boldsymbol{G};U_{I})\to C^{*}_{r}(\boldsymbol{G};I)

the quotient of this ideal associated to the closed subset
\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{I,+}\subseteq U_{I}
.

5.3.2 Remark
.

If
\{\,A_{t}\,\}_{t\in T}
is any continuous field of
C^{*}
-algebras, and if, for every
t\in T
,
J_{t}
is a
C^{*}
-algebra ideal in
A_{t}
, then
\{\,J_{t}\,\}_{t\in T}
becomes a continuous field of
C^{*}
-algebras whose continuous sections are those of the original field that take values in the ideals
J_{t}
, provided that for every
t_{0}
and for every
a\in J_{t_{0}}
there is a continuous section of the original continuous field that takes values in the ideals
J_{t}
and takes the value
a
at
t_{0}
. This latter condition is not automatic, but it is satisfied for the fields of ideals
C^{*}_{r}(G_{t};U_{I})
.

5.3.3 Remark
.

We may also ask whether or not the field of quotients
\{\,A_{t}/J_{t}\,\}_{t\in T}
becomes a continuous field of
C^{*}
-algebras, if one deems the continuous sections to be those that lift to continuous sections of the original field. This is not necessarily so, but the field is at least upper semicontinuous, in the sense that for every liftable section
s
, and every
t_{0}\in T

\limsup_{t\to t_{0}}\|s(t)\|_{A_{t}/J_{t}}\leq\|s(t_{0})\|_{A_{t_{0}}/J_{t_{0}}}.

In fact the fields of quotients introduced in this section
are
continuous fields, and not just upper semicontinuous fields. We shall not need this fact, but it follows from a minor supplementary argument; see Remark
5.4.7
below.

5.4
Structure of the subquotients

We shall now determine the structure of the subquotients
C^{*}_{r}(\boldsymbol{G},I)
. The formulation of the result and the method of proof are borrowed from
[
HR20
]
.

5.4.1 Lemma
.

Let
G
be a real reductive group and let
I
be a subset of the set
S
of simple roots in
\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{a}_{\min})
.
There is a unique continuous action of the multiplicative group
\mathbb{R}^{\times}_{+}
on
C^{*}_{r}(G;I)
by
C^{*}
-algebra automorphisms.

\alpha_{t}\colon C_{r}^{*}(G,I)\longrightarrow C^{*}_{r}(G,I)\quad(t>0)

such that

\pi_{\sigma,\nu}(\alpha_{t}(f))=\pi_{\sigma,t^{-1}\nu}(f)

for all
t>0
, all
f\in C^{*}_{r}(G_{t};I)
, all
\sigma\in\widehat{M}_{I,\mathrm{tempiric}}
, and all
\nu\in\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{I,+}
.

Proof.

The uniqueness assertion is clear, since the representations
\pi_{\sigma,\nu}
separate the points of
C^{*}_{r}(G;I)
.
Existence follows from the isomorphism

(5.4.2)

C^{*}_{r}(G;I)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}\bigoplus_{\sigma\in\widehat{M}_{I,\mathrm{tempiric}}}C_{0}\bigl(\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{I},\mathfrak{K}(\Ind_{P_{I}}^{G}H_{\sigma})\bigr)

in Theorem
3.4.2
, using which we may define
\alpha_{t}
by requiring it to correspond with the automorphisms

\alpha_{\sigma,t}\colon C_{0}\bigl(\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{I},\mathfrak{K}(\Ind_{P_{I}}^{G}H_{\sigma})\bigr)\longrightarrow C_{0}\bigl(\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{I},\mathfrak{K}(\Ind_{P_{I}}^{G}H_{\sigma})\bigr)

of the summands on the right-hand side above defined by

\alpha_{\sigma,t}(h)(\nu)=h(t^{-1}\nu).\qed

5.4.3 Lemma
.

Let
G
be a real reductive group and let
I
be a subset of the set
S
of simple roots in
\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{a}_{\min})
. There is a unique
C^{*}
-algebra homomorphism

\alpha_{0}\colon C^{*}_{r}(G_{0};I)\longrightarrow C^{*}_{r}(G;I)

such that

\pi_{\sigma,\nu}(\alpha_{0}(f_{0}))=\rho_{\sigma,\nu}(f_{0})

for all
f_{0}\in C^{*}(G_{0};I)
, all
\sigma\in\widehat{M}_{I,\mathrm{tempiric}}
, and all
\nu\in\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{I,+}
.

Proof.

As with Lemma
5.4.1
, the uniqueness assertion is clear, and to prove existence, we can again use the isomorphism (
5.4.2
), using which it suffices to construct a suitable
C^{*}
-homomorphism

(5.4.4)

\alpha_{0}\colon C^{*}_{r}(G_{0};I)\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{\sigma\in\widehat{M}_{I,\mathrm{tempiric}}}C_{0}\bigl(\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{I},\mathfrak{K}(\Ind_{P_{I}}^{G}H_{\sigma})\bigr).

We define

(5.4.5)

\alpha_{0}\colon f_{0}\longmapsto\oplus_{\sigma}\bigl[\nu\mapsto\rho_{\sigma,\nu}(f_{0})\bigr]

Considering an individual summand, if
f_{0}
is a smooth and compactly supported function on
G_{0}
, and if
\varphi,\psi\in L^{2}(K,H_{\sigma})^{K_{I}}
, then

(5.4.6)

\langle\varphi,\rho_{\sigma,\nu}(f_{0})\psi\rangle=\int_{K}\int_{K}\int_{\mathfrak{s}}f_{0}\bigl(k^{\prime}k^{-1},\Ad_{k}X\bigr)e^{-i\nu(X)}\bigl\langle\varphi(k^{\prime}),\psi(k)\bigr\rangle\,dk^{\prime}\,dk\,dX,

and the Riemann-Lebesgue lemma shows that the above is a
C_{0}
-function on
\nu
.
If
f_{0}
is moreover
K
-bi-finite, then the operator
\rho_{\sigma,\nu}(f_{0})
is identically
0
on a subspace of finite-codimension in
L^{2}(K,H_{\sigma})^{K_{I}}
, and on the finite-dimensional orthogonal complement it is unitarily equivalent to a finite matrix with the above matrix coefficients (
5.4.6
) as entries. Therefore
\rho_{\sigma,\nu}(f_{0})
is a compact operator-valued
C_{0}
function of
\nu
, for every
\sigma
. But in addition,
K
-bi-finiteness implies that only finitely many summands on the right of (
5.4.5
) are nonzero.

It follows that the formula (
5.4.5
) does indeed define an element of the right-hand side in (
5.4.4
), not just for a smooth, compactly supported and
K
-bi-finite function
f_{0}
on
G_{0}
, but, by an approximation argument, for any
f_{0}\in C^{*}_{r}(G_{0})
, since the functions we have considered up to now are dense in
C^{*}_{r}(G_{0})
.
It is evident the homomorphism
\alpha_{0}
so-defined has the required property.
∎

5.4.7 Remark
.

The morphism
\alpha_{0}
is injective. This follows from the definition (
5.4.5
) above
and the fact that the representations
\rho_{\sigma,\nu}
include, as subrepresentations, all of the irreducible representations of
C^{*}(G_{0};I)
(in fact, by Frobenius reciprocity, every irreducible representation of
K_{I}
occurs as a
K_{I}
-type in some minimal principal series representation of
M_{I}
, and the base principal series representations, with
\nu{=}0
, are tempiric).

5.4.8 Theorem
.

Let
G
be a real reductive group and let
I
be a subset of the set
S
of simple roots in
\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{a}_{\min})
. If

\{f_{t}\}
is a continuous section of the continuous field of
C^{*}
-algebras
\{C^{*}_{r}(G_{t};U_{I})\}
, then

\lim_{t\searrow 0}\alpha_{t}\bigl(\pi_{I}(f_{t})\bigr)=\alpha_{0}(\rho_{I}(f_{0})),

where the limit is taken in the norm topology of
C^{*}_{r}(G,I)
, and where
\pi_{I}:C^{*}_{r}(G,U_{I})\to C^{*}_{r}(G,I)
and
\rho_{I}:C^{*}_{r}(G,U_{I})\to C^{*}_{r}(G,I)
are the quotient maps that were introduced in Definitions
3.2.1
and
4.3.1
.

5.4.9 Remark
.

If we apply the representation
\pi_{\sigma,\nu}
to both sides of the limit formula in the theorem, and use the characteristic properties of
\alpha_{t}
and
\alpha_{0}
, then we obtain the formula

\lim_{t\searrow 0}\pi_{\sigma,t^{-1}\nu}(f_{t})=\rho_{\sigma,\nu}(f_{0}).

This is a consequence of Example
5.1.9
and Proposition
5.1.10
. In effect, Theorem
5.4.8
promotes this pointwise limit formula (valid for
\sigma
and one value of
\nu
at a time) to a uniform limit formula over all
\sigma
and
\nu
. This is a technical matter: the main conceptual content of the theorem is already present in Proposition
5.1.10
.

Proof of Theorem
5.4.8
.

Let
P_{I}=MAN
be the Langlands decomposition of
P_{I}
.
Let
\{f_{t}\}
be a continuous section of the continuous field of
C^{*}
-algebras
\{C^{*}_{r}(G_{t};U_{I})\}
. The same argument that we used in the proof of Lemma
5.4.3
shows that it suffices to prove that for every tempiric representation
\sigma
, and for every pair
\varphi,\psi
of smooth functions in
L^{2}(K,H_{\sigma})^{K\cap M}
, the limit formula

\lim_{t\searrow 0}\langle\varphi,\pi_{\sigma,t^{-1}\nu}(f_{t})\psi\rangle=\langle\varphi,\rho_{\sigma,\nu}(f_{0})\psi\rangle

holds uniformly in
\nu
.

Let
g\in G
. Using a product decomposition

g=\kappa(g)\mu(g)\alpha(g)\upsilon(g)\in KMAN

we may write the unitary operator

\pi_{\sigma,\nu}(g)\colon L^{2}(K,H_{\sigma})^{K\cap M}\to L^{2}(K,H_{\sigma})^{K\cap M}

in the form

(5.4.10)

(\pi_{\sigma,\nu}(g)\psi)(\ell)\\
=e^{-i\nu(\log(\alpha(g^{-1}\ell)))}e^{-\rho(\log(\alpha(g^{-1}\ell)))}\sigma(\mu(g^{-1}\ell))^{-1}\psi(\kappa(g^{-1}\ell))

for all
\ell\in K
. Compare
[
Kna86
, Sec. VII.1]
; note that the terms
\kappa(g^{-1}\ell)
and
\mu(g^{-1}\ell)
in the product decomposition of
g
are not separately well-defined, but the compound term
\sigma(\mu(g^{-1}\ell))^{-1}\psi(\kappa(g^{-1}\ell))
is. It follows from (
5.4.10
), plus a change of variables, that if
t{>}0

6
Vogan’s theorem in K-theory

In this section we shall formulate and prove our main theorem, which frames the Connes-Kasparov isomorphism in operator
K
-theory in purely representation-theoretic terms.

6.1
Vogan’s theorem on minimal K-types

6.1.1 Definition

(See
[
Vog81
, Def. 5.4.18]
)
.

Let
G
be a real reductive group with maximal compact subgroup
K
. If
\tau
is an irreducible unitary representation of
K
, then its
norm
is

\|\tau\|=\|\mu+2\rho_{K}\|,

where
\mu
is a highest weight for some choice of maximal torus
T\subseteq K
and some choice of a system of positive roots in
\Delta(\mathfrak{k}_{\mathbb{C}},\mathfrak{t}_{\mathbb{C}})
; the choices do not affect the norm.

6.1.2 Definition
.

Let
\pi
be a irreducible unitary (or simply admissible) representation of
G
. A
minimal
K
-type
of
G
is any
K
-type of
\pi
(that is, any irreducible constituent of the restriction of
\pi
to
K
) whose norm is minimal among the norms of all
K
-types of
\pi
.

6.1.3 Remark
.

Since every norm-bounded subset of
\widehat{K}
is finite, every admissible representation has at least one, and at most finitely many minimal
K
-types.

The simplicity of these definitions belies their importance. We shall be concerned in this paper with the following fundamental result about minimal
K
-types, due to Vogan, which, as we shall see, completely determines the tempered dual.

6.1.4 Theorem

(Vogan,
[
Vog07
, Thm. 1.2]
)
.

Let
G
be a real reductive group with maximal compact subgroup
K
. Every tempiric representation has a unique minimal
K
-type, which occurs in the representation with multiplicity one. The map that associates to a tempiric representation its minimal
K
-type is a bijection

\widehat{G}_{\mathrm{tempiric}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}\widehat{K}.

Among other things, this theorem immediately gives a parametrization of the tempered dual of
G
in terms of irreducible representations of compact subgroups, for we have, from (
2.4.4
) and Vogan’s bijection,

\widehat{G}_{\mathrm{tempered}}\cong\bigsqcup_{I\subseteq S}\widehat{M}_{I,\mathrm{tempiric}}\times i\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{I,+}\cong\bigsqcup_{I\subseteq S}\widehat{K}_{I}\times i\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{I,+}\cong\widehat{G}_{0}.

This is the Mackey bijection of Afgoustidis
[
Afg21
]
.

In the next section we shall prove that the Connes-Kasparov isomorphism in operator
K
-theory is equivalent to a
K
-theoretic version of Vogan’s theorem that involves the following concepts.

6.1.5 Definition
.

Let
G
be a real reductive group. Denote by
R_{\mathrm{tempiric}}(G)
the free abelian group on the set of equivalence classes of tempiric representations of
G
.

6.1.6 Definition
.

Denote by

\mathrm{mult}\colon R(K)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}R_{\mathrm{tempiric}}(G)

the homomorphism of abelian groups defined by

[\tau]\longmapsto\sum_{\sigma\,\,\mathrm{tempiric}}\mult(\tau:\sigma)\cdot[\sigma]\qquad\forall\tau\in\widehat{K},

where
\mult(\tau:\sigma)
is the multiplicity with which
\tau
occurs in the restriction of the tempiric representation
\sigma
to
K
.

6.1.7 Remark
.

The multiplicity homomorphism is well-defined in view of the fact, noted in Theorem
3.3.3
, that each irreducible representation of
K
occurs as a
K
-type in at most finitely many pairwise inequivalent tempiric representations.
Formally,
\mult
is the transpose of the map induced by restriction of tempiric
G
-representations to
K
, although that map is not well-defined to
R(K)
since its image contains infinite sums.

It is a simple matter of solving linear equations to prove, using Vogan’s minimal
K
-type theorem (Theorem
6.1.4
), that the multiplicity homomorphism in Definition
6.1.6
is an isomorphism. The main goal of this article is to show that it is also possible to prove that
\mult
is an isomorphism without using Vogan’s minimal
K
-type theorem but instead using the Connes-Kasparov isomorphism.

This is very close to a new proof of Vogan’s minimal
K
-type theorem. For instance, if we were to assume that no
\tau\in\widehat{K}
may occur as a minimal
K
-type of two inequivalent tempiric representations, then elementary linear algebra would allow us to deduce
Vogan’s theorem from the isomorphism of the multiplicity map:

6.1.8 Lemma
.

Suppose that the multiplicity map is an isomorphism. Suppose, in addition, that each
K
-type occurs in at most one tempiric representation of
G
as a minimal
K
-type. Then every tempiric representation has a unique minimal
K
-type, which occurs in the representation with multiplicity one, and the map that sends a tempiric representation to its minimal
K
-type is a bijection.∎

6.2
The Connes-Kasparov isomorphism

We turn, finally to the Connes-Kasparov isomorphism.
In view of the aims of this paper, it is certainly important to note that Lafforgue
[
Laf02b
]
has given a proof of the Connes-Kasparov isomorphism using techniques from
K
-theory and index theory, rather than representation theory. On the other hand, representation-theoretic treatments of the Connes-Kasparov isomorphism can be found in
[
Was87
,
Hig08
,
Afg19
,
CHS24
,
Bra23
]

We shall use the following formulation of the Connes-Kasparov isomorphism, which is due to Connes, see
[
Con94

6.2.1 Theorem

(Connes-Kasparov isomorphism)
.

Let

be a real reductive group. The

-algebra

in Definition

-theory.

.

The morphism

leads to an extension of

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{C_{0}\bigl((0,\infty),C^{*}_{r}(G)\bigr)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{C^{*}_{r}(\boldsymbol{G})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{C^{*}(G_{0})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0}

and hence (using Bott periodicity) a six-term exact sequence in

\textstyle{K_{1}\bigl(C^{*}_{r}(G)\bigr)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{K_{0}\bigl(C^{*}_{r}(\boldsymbol{G})\bigr)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{K_{0}(C^{*}(G_{0}))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{K_{1}\bigl(C^{*}(G_{0})\bigr)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

The assertion that the

6.3
K-theory of the mapping cone

The main theorem of this paper is the following consequence of the Connes-Kasparov isomorphism for real reductive groups. In fact, this statement is equivalent to the Connes-Kasparov isomorphism for real reductive groups.

6.3.1 Theorem
.

Let
G
be a real reductive group and let
K
be a maximal compact subgroup of
G
. The morphism of abelian groups

\displaystyle\mult:R(K)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}R_{\mathrm{tempiric}}(G),

\displaystyle[\tau]

\displaystyle\longmapsto\sum_{\sigma}\mult(\tau:\sigma)\cdot[\sigma]\qquad\forall\tau\in\widehat{K},

is an isomorphism.

To prove the theorem we shall relate the morphism in the statement to the induced maps in
K
-theory associated to the
C^{*}
-algebra homomorphisms

\alpha_{0}\colon C^{*}_{r}(G_{0};I)\longrightarrow C^{*}_{r}(G;I)\qquad(I\subseteq S)

from Lemma
5.4.3
. The point is that this is a morphism between
C^{*}
-algebras whose

-theory groups are easily determined in representation theoretic terms. First, from the isomorphism

C^{*}_{r}(G_{0};I)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}\bigoplus_{\tau\in\widehat{K}_{I}}C_{0}(\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{I,+},\mathfrak{K}(\Ind_{K_{I}}^{K}H_{\tau}))

in Theorem
4.3.3
we obtain isomorphisms

\displaystyle K_{*}\bigl(C^{*}_{r}(G_{0};I)\bigr)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}\bigoplus_{\tau\in\widehat{K}_{I}}K_{*}\bigl(C_{0}(\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{I,+},\mathfrak{K}(\Ind_{K_{I}}^{K}H_{\tau}))\bigr)

\displaystyle\cong\,\,\bigoplus_{\tau\in\widehat{K}_{I}}K_{*}\bigl(C_{0}(\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{I,+})\bigr)

\displaystyle\cong\,\,\bigoplus_{\tau\in\widehat{K}_{I}}\mathbb{Z},

using first the stability property of
K
-theory and then the Bott periodicity theorem, which implies that the
K
-theory of
C_{0}(\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{I,+})
is the infinite cyclic group
\mathbb{Z}
. We fix a choice of generator for each
I
. Secondly, using Theorem
3.4.2
in a similar fashion, we have isomorphisms

K_{*}\bigl(C^{*}_{r}(G;I)\bigr)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}\bigoplus_{\sigma\in\widehat{M}_{I,\mathrm{tempiric}}}\mathbb{Z}.

There is a commutative diagram

\textstyle{K_{*}(C^{*}_{r}(G_{0};I))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\cong}
\scriptstyle{\alpha_{0*}}
\textstyle{K_{*}(C^{*}_{r}(G;I))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\cong}
\textstyle{R(K_{I})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

in which the vertical isomorphisms are the ones described above, and the bottom morphism is the multiplicity map for the group

\mult_{I}:[\tau]\mapsto\sum_{\sigma\in\widehat{M}_{I,\mathrm{tempiric}}}\operatorname{mult}(\tau:\sigma)[\sigma].

Proof.

Let

We now turn to the proof of Theorem

6.3.3 Theorem
.

Let
G
be a real reductive group, and let
S
be a set of simple roots for the restricted root system
\Delta(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{a}_{\min})
.
For every

.

Proof of Theorems
6.3.1
and

.

Suppose that Theorem

holds for groups with real rank strictly less than

.
Firstly, suppose that

is a proper subset of

. As the group

has lower real rank, the morphism

is an isomorphism by the inductive hypothesis. By Theorem

, this means that
\alpha_{0}:C^{*}(G_{0};I)\to C^{*}_{r}(G;I)
induces an isomorphism on

, we obtain that
K_{*}(C^{*}_{r}(\boldsymbol{G};I))=0

Now, for each

, define

where

as the

. Then

is the disjoint union of closed subsets

.

Now consider

. In this case, we note that

and

, so we have an exact sequence

As both
C_{r}^{*}(\boldsymbol{G};U_{r-1})
and

vanish in

, which proves Theorem

for

for

.
∎

We have used the Connes-Kasparov isomorphism to prove Theorem
6.3.1
. Conversely, by arguing exactly as in the above proof, we can prove that
C_{r}^{*}(\boldsymbol{G})
has vanishing

-theory if we are given Theorem
6.3.1
. That is, we have the following.

6.3.4 Theorem
.

The assertion that the multiplicity map in Definition
6.1.6
is an isomorphism, for every real reductive group, is equivalent to the Connes-Kasparov isomorphism for every real reductive group. ∎

Lafforgue used the Connes-Kasparov isomorphism to obtain a new proof in

, Thm. 16]
, when they exist. The purpose of this section is simply to recall that following result also follows from Harish-Chandra’s classificiation of the discrete series, and therefore may also be viewed as consequences of the Connes-Kasparov isomorphism:

Theorem

(Theorem
3.3.1
in this text)
.

Let
G
be a real reductive group with compact center that admits discrete series representations and let
K
be a maximal compact subgroup of
G
. Each irreducible representation of
K
occurs as a
K
-type in at most finitely many discrete series representations of
G
, up to unitary equivalence.

Proof.

Assume first that
G
is a connected group that admits discrete series. Let
T
be a maximal torus in
K
. According to Harish-Chandra, the discrete series are parametrized by their
Harish-Chandra parameters
, which are certain classes

[\lambda]\in i\mathfrak{t}^{*}/W(\mathfrak{k}_{\mathbb{C}},\mathfrak{t}_{\mathbb{C}})

such that
\lambda+\rho_{G}
is analytically integral. The character of the discrete series representation
\pi_{\lambda}
associated to
\lambda
is the class

(A.1.1)

[\lambda]\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}_{\mathbb{C}}/W(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}},\mathfrak{t}_{\mathbb{C}})

(according to Harish-Chandra’s criterion, discrete series representations exist for
G
if and only if
\mathfrak{t}
by itself is a Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
). It therefore follows from a well-known calculation (
c.f.

[
Kna02
, Prop. 5.28]
) that if

\Omega_{\mathfrak{g}}=\sum X^{a}X_{a}

is the Casimir element in the center of the enveloping algebra for
\mathfrak{g}
(where
\{X_{a}\}
is basis for
\mathfrak{g}
, and
\{X^{a}\}
is a dual basis for the invariant bilinear form
B
in (
2.1.1
)), and if
\InfCh_{\Omega_{\mathfrak{g}}}
is the function on
\widehat{G}
from Definition
3.1.3
, then

\InfCh_{\Omega_{\mathfrak{g}}}(\pi_{\lambda})=\|\lambda\|^{2}-\|\rho_{G}\|^{2}.

Now write the Casimir as

\Omega_{\mathfrak{g}}=\Delta_{\mathfrak{s}}+\Omega_{\mathfrak{k}}=\sum_{b}Y_{b}^{2}-\sum_{c}Z_{c}^{2},

where
\{Y_{b}\}
is an orthonormal basis for
\mathfrak{s}
and
\{Z_{c}\}
is an orthonormal basis for
\mathfrak{k}
in the Cartan decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{s}
. It follows from the unitarity of
\pi_{\lambda}
that if
\tau
is a irreducible unitary representation of
K
with infinitesimal character
\mu\in i\mathfrak{t}^{*}/W(\mathfrak{k}_{\mathbb{C}},\mathfrak{t}_{\mathbb{C}})
, and if
v
is a unit vector in the
\tau
-isotypical part of
H_{\pi}
, then

\|\lambda\|^{2}=\langle v,\Omega_{\mathfrak{g}}v\rangle=\langle v,\Delta_{\mathfrak{s}}v\rangle+\|\mu\|^{2}-\|\rho_{K}\|^{2}\leq\|\mu\|^{2}-\|\rho_{K}\|^{2}.

It follows that only finitely many
\pi_{\lambda}
can include
\tau
as a
K
-type, which proves Theorem
3.3.1
in the connected case.

The general case follows from the connected case by Frobenius reciprocity, since the restriction of a discrete series representation of
G
to the connected component of
G
is a direct sum of discrete series representations, all of them sharing the same infinitesimal character.
∎

A.1.2 Remark
.

The formula (
A.1.1
) shows immediately that every discrete series representation has real infinitesimal character: recall from Definition
2.2.4
that if
\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{t}{\oplus}\mathfrak{a}
is any
\theta
-stable Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
, then a weight
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}_{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
is real if it is imaginary-valued on
\mathfrak{t}
and real-valued on
\mathfrak{a}
, and this is the case here, where in fact
\mathfrak{a}=0
.

A.2
Classification of tempered irreducible unitary representations by tempiric representations

We shall sketch a proof of Vogan’s Theorem
2.4.2
, which classifies tempered irreducible unitary representations in terms of the imaginary part of the infinitesimal character and tempiric representations Levi subgroups. For convenience, we restate the result here. Recall that we had fixed an Iwasawa decomposition
G=KA_{\min}N
of a real reductive group and that we are denoting by
S\subseteq\Delta(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{a}_{\min})
the associated set of simple restricted roots. Here, again, is the classification theorem:

Theorem

(Theorem
2.4.2
in this
text)
.

Let
I\subseteq S
and let
\nu\in\mathfrak{a}_{I,+}^{*}
. The correspondence

\sigma\longmapsto\Ind_{P_{I}}^{G}\sigma{\otimes}\exp(i\nu)

determines a bijection from the set of unitary equivalence classes of tempiric representations
\sigma
of
M_{I}
to the set of unitary equivalence classes of irreducible tempered unitary representations
\pi
of
G
for which

\ImInfCh(\pi)=\nu
.

We shall use Harish-Chandra’s Theorem
2.2.10
in the proof, along with the following result, which is also due to Harish-Chandra.

A.2.1 Theorem
.

Let
P=MAN
be a parabolic subgroup of
G
. Let
\nu\in\mathfrak{a}^{*}
, and assume that the Levi subgroup
L=MA
is the full centralizer of
\nu
in
G
. If
\sigma
is an irreducible unitary representation of
M
with real infinitesimal character, then the parabolically induced representation
\Ind_{P}^{G}\sigma\otimes\exp(i\nu)
is irreducible. Moreover, if
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
are irreducible unitary representations of
M
with real infinitesimal character, and if the representations
\Ind_{P}^{G}\sigma_{1}\otimes\exp(i\nu)
and
\Ind_{P}^{G}\sigma_{2}\otimes\exp(i\nu)
are equivalent, then
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
are equivalent.

A.2.2 Remark
.

In the case of the minimal parabolic subgroup Theorem
A.2.1
was proved by Bruhat
[
Bru56
]
. Harish-Chandra developed Bruhat’s method to yield the general result above, but did not publish his result. See
[
KV96
]
for further historical remarks, and for an exposition of most of the proof. See
[
vdBS05
, Thm. 10.7]
for the remaining details.

Proof of Theorem
2.4.2
.

First, it follows from Theorem
A.2.1
that all the representations
\Ind_{P}^{G}\sigma_{1}{\otimes}\exp(i\nu)
listed in the statement of the theorem are irreducible, and it follows from (
2.2.11
) that they all have imaginary part of the infinitesimal character equal to
\nu
.

Next let
\pi
be a tempered, irreducible unitary representation of
G
. According to Theorem
2.2.10
,
\pi
may be embedded as a subrepresentation of some parabolically induced representation
\Ind_{P}^{G}\theta{\otimes}\!\exp(i\nu)
, where
P=MAN
is a standard parabolic subgroup of
G
,
\theta
is a discrete series representation of
M
, and
\nu\in\mathfrak{a}^{*}
.

If we conjugate by a representative in
K
of an appropriate element of
W(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{a}_{\min})
then we may arrange that
\nu\in\mathfrak{a}_{\mathrm{dom}}^{*}
(recall our convention that we extend
\nu
to
\mathfrak{a}_{\min}
by requiring it to be zero on the orthogonal complement of
\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\mathfrak{a}_{\min}
). The conjugation may take
P
to a nonstandard parabolic subgroup, but we may replace the conjugated parabolic subgroup by a standard parabolic subgroup in the same associate class without changing the equivalence class of the parabolically induced representation.

In summary, we may in embed

\pi
as a subrepresentation of some parabolically induced representation
\Ind_{P}^{G}\theta\otimes\exp(i\nu)
, where
P=MAN
is a standard parabolic subgroup of
G
,
\theta
is a discrete series representation of
M
, and
\nu\in\mathfrak{a}^{*}\cap\mathfrak{a}_{\mathrm{dom}}^{*}
. Of course,
\nu
is the imaginary part of the infinitesimal character of
\pi
.

Now let
I\subseteq S
be such that
\nu\in\mathfrak{a}_{I,+}^{*}
. The group
L{=}MA
is a Levi subgroup of
L_{I}{=}M_{I}A_{I}
, and if we parabolically induce
\theta\otimes\exp(i\nu)
from
L
to
L_{I}
we obtain a representation of the form

\sigma\otimes\exp(i\nu)
. The representation
\sigma
is not necessarily irreducible, but it is a direct sum of tempiric representations of
M_{I}
,

\sigma=\sigma_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\sigma_{d}.

By induction in stages,
\pi
embeds in some parabolically induced representation
\Ind_{P_{I}}^{G}\sigma_{k}\otimes\exp(i\nu)
. It follows from Theorem
A.2.1
that the parabolically induced representation is irreducible, and therefore the embedding of
\pi
into it must be a unitary equivalence. It also follows from Theorem
A.2.1
that the unitary equivalence class of
\pi
determines the unitary equivalence class of
\sigma_{k}
. This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

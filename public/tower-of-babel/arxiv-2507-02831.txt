Trace Formulas for Deformed W-Algebras
Fabrizio Nieri

Abstract
We investigate trace formulas in ε \varepsilon -deformed W-algebras, highlighting a novel connection to the modular double of 𝔮 {\mathfrak{q}} -deformed W-algebras. In particular, we show that torus correlators in the additive (Yangian) setting reproduce sphere correlators in the trigonometric setup, possibly with the inclusion of a non-perturbative completion. From a dual perspective, this mechanism implements a gauge theoretic 2d → \to 3d uplift, where a circle direction in the world-sheet transmutes to a compact space-time direction in a non-trivial manner. We further discuss a unified picture of deformed W-algebras driven by trace formulas, suggesting a deeper algebraic layer related to the massive and massless form-factor approach to integrable QFT and 2d CFT.

Introduction

In this letter, we study quiver
\text{W}_{\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}}
algebras introduced in
Nieri:2019mdl

\text{W}_{{\mathfrak{q}}_{1},{\mathfrak{q}}_{2}}

While it is fairly straightforward to follow the hierarchy from the elliptic to rational case by taking appropriate limits, the natural question arises whether the lower layers also capture some aspects of the upper ones. Such possibility is partially hinted by the representation theory of affine Yangian, quantum toroidal and elliptic algebras, which exhibit striking similarities.

1.

At the top level, we encounter the (master) elliptic deformation, which admits two limiting cases, named trigonometric and hyperbolic. In gauge theoretic terms, the former is associated to the
\mathbb{C}_{{\mathfrak{q}}_{1}}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}

2.

At the intermediate level, the trigonometric deformation can be uplifted to the elliptic one
Nieri:2015dts
, corresponding to the
\mathbb{C}_{{\mathfrak{q}}_{1}}\times\mathbb{T}^{2}_{{\mathfrak{q}}^{\prime}}

Nedelin:2016gwu

Lodin:2017lrc

\mathbb{S}^{3}_{\varepsilon_{1}/\tau}
with
\mathbb{S}^{3}_{\varepsilon_{1}/\tau}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
on the gauge theoretic side. In any case, the Yangian/additive deformation can be reached by scaling limits.

3.

At the bottom level, the Yangian/additive deformation was derived in
Nieri:2019mdl
by implementing the gauge theoretic dimensional reduction on the algebraic side. In this letter, we went backward and showed the trace implements the hyperbolic deformation or, equivalently, the 2d
\to

1

As we mentioned in the introduction, contextualizing the deformed W-algebras within the form-factor program may shed some light on the massive integrable QFTs dual to higher-dimensional gauge theories. This letter is only a first step in this direction. Another intriguing research line is to explore whether the cartoon in Fig.

\varepsilon

{\mathfrak{q}}
-Pochhammer, namely

\Gamma_{r}(x|1,\underline{\alpha})\Gamma_{r}(1-x|1,-\underline{\alpha})=\frac{{\rm e}^{-\frac{{\rm i}\pi}{r!}(-1)^{r}B_{rr}(x|1,\underline{\alpha})}}{(x;\underline{\alpha})_{\infty}}~,\quad(x;\underline{\alpha})_{\infty}\equiv({\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}x};{\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}\alpha_{1}},\ldots,{\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}\alpha_{r-1}})_{\infty}~.

(21)

The Multiple Sine function for
\text{Re}\omega_{j}>0
,
\text{Re}\sum_{j}\omega_{j}>\text{Re}z>0
has a similar integral representation

S_{r}(z|\underline{\omega})=\exp\Big[\pm(-1)^{r}\frac{\pi{\rm i}}{r!}B_{rr}(z|\underline{\omega})-\oint_{\mathbb{R}\mp{\rm i}0}\frac{{\rm d}k}{k}\frac{{\rm e}^{-kz}}{\prod_{j=1}^{r}(1-{\rm e}^{-k\omega_{j}})}\Big]~,

(22)

where the contour runs parallel to the real axis avoiding the origin.

Appendix B
Clavelli-Shapiro Trace Technique

We offer a step-by-step proof of Clavelli-Shapiro trace formula, which cannot be easily found in the literature. We aim to compute

Z=\text{Tr}\ x^{\mathbf{L_{0}}}\mathbf{O}(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{a}^{\dagger})~,

(23)

where
\mathbf{O}
is some operator-valued function of
\mathbf{a}
,
\mathbf{a}^{\dagger}
, the usual annihilation/creation operators, and
\mathbf{L}_{0}\equiv\mathbf{a}^{\dagger}\mathbf{a}
is the occupation number operator. They satisfy the non-trivial commutation relations

[\mathbf{a},\mathbf{a}^{\dagger}]=1~,\quad[\mathbf{L}_{0},\mathbf{a}]=-\mathbf{a}~,\quad[\mathbf{L}_{0},\mathbf{a}^{\dagger}]=\mathbf{a}^{\dagger}~.

(24)

The Fock modules (over which we compute the trace) are generated by the vacua
{\langle 0|},{|0\rangle}
such that

\mathbf{a}{|0\rangle}={\langle 0|}\mathbf{a}^{\dagger}=0~,

(25)

with
{\langle 0|0\rangle}=1
. Normalized basis states are given by

{|n\rangle}=\frac{(\mathbf{a}^{\dagger})^{n}}{\sqrt{n!}}{|0\rangle}~,\quad{\langle n|}={\langle 0|}\frac{(\mathbf{a})^{n}}{\sqrt{n!}}~,\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}~.

(26)

We start noticing that
Z
can be rewritten as a vacuum expectation value rather than as a trace by introducing another set of oscillators
\mathbf{b},\mathbf{b}^{\dagger}
satisfying the very same commutation relations and commuting with
\mathbf{a},\mathbf{a}^{\dagger}
. Indeed

\displaystyle{\langle 0|}{\rm e}^{\mathbf{a}\mathbf{b}}x^{\mathbf{L}_{0}}\mathbf{O}(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{a}^{\dagger}){\rm e}^{\mathbf{a}^{\dagger}\mathbf{b}^{\dagger}}{|0\rangle}=

\displaystyle\ \sum_{n,m\geq 0}{\langle 0|}\frac{\mathbf{a}^{n}\mathbf{b}^{n}}{n!}x^{\mathbf{L}_{0}}\mathbf{O}(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{a}^{\dagger})\frac{(\mathbf{a}^{\dagger})^{m}(\mathbf{b}^{\dagger})^{m}}{m!}{|0\rangle}=

(27a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \sum_{n,m\geq 0}{\langle 0|}\frac{\mathbf{a}^{n}}{n!}x^{\mathbf{L}_{0}}\mathbf{O}(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{a}^{\dagger})\frac{(\mathbf{a}^{\dagger})^{m}}{m!}{|0\rangle}{\langle 0|}\mathbf{b}^{n}(\mathbf{b}^{\dagger})^{m}{|0\rangle}=

(27b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \sum_{n,m\geq 0}{\langle 0|}\frac{\mathbf{a}^{n}}{n!}x^{\mathbf{L}_{0}}\mathbf{O}(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{a}^{\dagger})\frac{(\mathbf{a}^{\dagger})^{m}}{m!}{|0\rangle}m!\delta_{m,n}=

(27c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \sum_{n\geq 0}{\langle n|}x^{\mathbf{L}_{0}}\mathbf{O}(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{a}^{\dagger}){|n\rangle}~.

(27d)

We must now compute the l.h.s. in an alternative way by exploiting ordering and disentangling identities. Firstly, due to
[\mathbf{a}^{\dagger}\mathbf{b}^{\dagger},\mathbf{a}]=-\mathbf{b}^{\dagger}
,
[\mathbf{a}^{\dagger}\mathbf{b}^{\dagger},\mathbf{a}^{\dagger}]=[\mathbf{a}^{\dagger}\mathbf{b}^{\dagger},\mathbf{b}^{\dagger}]=0
, the BCH formula implies

{\rm e}^{-\mathbf{a}^{\dagger}\mathbf{b}^{\dagger}}\mathbf{a}{\rm e}^{\mathbf{a}^{\dagger}\mathbf{b}^{\dagger}}=\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b}^{\dagger}~,\quad{\rm e}^{-\mathbf{a}^{\dagger}\mathbf{b}^{\dagger}}\mathbf{a}^{\dagger}{\rm e}^{\mathbf{a}^{\dagger}\mathbf{b}^{\dagger}}=\mathbf{a}^{\dagger}~,

(28)

and therefore

{\langle 0|}{\rm e}^{\mathbf{a}\mathbf{b}}x^{\mathbf{L}_{0}}\mathbf{O}(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{a}^{\dagger}){\rm e}^{\mathbf{a}^{\dagger}\mathbf{b}^{\dagger}}{|0\rangle}={\langle 0|}{\rm e}^{\mathbf{a}\mathbf{b}}x^{\mathbf{L}_{0}}{\rm e}^{\mathbf{a}^{\dagger}\mathbf{b}^{\dagger}}\mathbf{O}(\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b}^{\dagger},\mathbf{a}^{\dagger}){|0\rangle}~.

(29)

Now the fact that
\mathbf{L}_{0}
is the grading/dilation operator for the
\mathbf{a}
,
\mathbf{a}^{\dagger}
modes (or the BCH formula again) implies

x^{-\mathbf{L}_{0}}{\rm e}^{\mathbf{a}\mathbf{b}}x^{\mathbf{L}_{0}}={\rm e}^{x\mathbf{a}\mathbf{b}}~,

(30)

so

{\langle 0|}{\rm e}^{\mathbf{a}\mathbf{b}}x^{\mathbf{L}_{0}}{\rm e}^{\mathbf{a}^{\dagger}\mathbf{b}^{\dagger}}\mathbf{O}(\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b}^{\dagger},\mathbf{a}^{\dagger}){|0\rangle}={\langle 0|}{\rm e}^{x\mathbf{a}\mathbf{b}}{\rm e}^{\mathbf{a}^{\dagger}\mathbf{b}^{\dagger}}\mathbf{O}(\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b}^{\dagger},\mathbf{a}^{\dagger}){|0\rangle}~.

(31)

Using the commutation rule
[-\mathbf{a}^{\dagger}\mathbf{b}^{\dagger},x\mathbf{a}\mathbf{b}]=x(\mathbf{a}\mathbf{a}^{\dagger}+\mathbf{b}^{\dagger}\mathbf{b})
and the BCH formula once more, namely

{\rm e}^{-\mathbf{a}^{\dagger}\mathbf{b}^{\dagger}}{\rm e}^{x\mathbf{a}\mathbf{b}}{\rm e}^{\mathbf{a}^{\dagger}\mathbf{b}^{\dagger}}={\rm e}^{x(\mathbf{a}\mathbf{b}+\mathbf{a}\mathbf{a}^{\dagger}+\mathbf{b}^{\dagger}\mathbf{b}+\mathbf{a}^{\dagger}\mathbf{b}^{\dagger})}~,

(32)

we get

{\langle 0|}{\rm e}^{x\mathbf{a}\mathbf{b}}{\rm e}^{\mathbf{a}^{\dagger}\mathbf{b}^{\dagger}}\mathbf{O}(\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b}^{\dagger},\mathbf{a}^{\dagger}){|0\rangle}={\langle 0|}{\rm e}^{x(\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b}^{\dagger})(\mathbf{a}^{\dagger}+\mathbf{b})}\mathbf{O}(\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b}^{\dagger},\mathbf{a}^{\dagger}){|0\rangle}~.

(33)

In order to evaluate the exponential operator on the vacuum, we can follow at least two routes. The elegant one is based on the observation that
\mathbf{a}^{\dagger}\mathbf{b}^{\dagger}\equiv\mathbf{E}
,
\mathbf{a}\mathbf{b}\equiv\mathbf{F}
,
[\mathbf{E},\mathbf{F}]=\mathbf{a}^{\dagger}\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b}^{\dagger}\mathbf{b}+1\equiv\mathbf{H}
close the ordinary
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
algebra, for which disentangling identities are known to reproduce
PhysRevD.31.1988

{\langle 0|}{\rm e}^{x(\mathbf{F}+\mathbf{H}+\mathbf{E})}={\rm e}^{-\ln(1-x)}{\langle 0|}{\rm e}^{\frac{x}{1-x}\mathbf{F}}~.

(34)

The less elegant one is based on brute force computation. In this case we can expand the exponential

{\rm e}^{x(\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b}^{\dagger})(\mathbf{a}^{\dagger}+\mathbf{b})}=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}\sum_{k,j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(\mathbf{b}^{\dagger})^{k}\mathbf{a}^{n-k}\binom{n}{j}(\mathbf{a}^{\dagger})^{j}\mathbf{b}^{n-j}~,

(35)

and use the action of the oscillators on the Fock states, namely

{\langle n|}(\mathbf{a}^{\dagger})^{m}=\sqrt{n(n-1)\cdots(n-m+1)}{\langle n-m|}~,\quad{\langle n|}\mathbf{a}^{m}=\sqrt{(n+1)\cdots(n+m)}{\langle n+m|}~,

(36)

to obtain

{\langle 0|}{\rm e}^{x(\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b}^{\dagger})(\mathbf{a}^{\dagger}+\mathbf{b})}={\langle 0|}\sum_{n,j\geq 0}x^{j}\binom{n+j}{j}\frac{(x\mathbf{a}\mathbf{b})^{n}}{n!}~.

(37)

The sum over
j
(operator-independent part) is a known generating function (
|x|<1
)

\sum_{j\geq 0}x^{j}\binom{n+j}{j}=\frac{1}{(1-x)^{n+1}}~.

(38)

Finally, using

{\rm e}^{\mathbf{a}\mathbf{b}}\mathbf{a}^{\dagger}{\rm e}^{-\mathbf{a}\mathbf{b}}=\mathbf{a}^{\dagger}+\mathbf{b}~,\quad{\rm e}^{\mathbf{a}\mathbf{b}}\mathbf{b}^{\dagger}{\rm e}^{-\mathbf{a}\mathbf{b}}=\mathbf{b}^{\dagger}+\mathbf{a}~,

(39)

we deduce the formula

Z={\rm e}^{-\ln(1-x)}{\langle 0|}\mathbf{O}\Big(\frac{\mathbf{a}}{1-x}+\mathbf{b}^{\dagger},\mathbf{a}^{\dagger}-\frac{\mathbf{b}}{1-x^{-1}}\Big){|0\rangle}~.

(40)

This derivation can be generalized to the case of multiple independent oscillators, both in the discrete case and in the continuous case whenever a suitable regularization scheme is employed to make sense of continuous products and singular integrals (e.g. via discretization, multiplicative integrals and deformed contours). We refer to
Jimbo:1996ev
for a discussion in a related context.

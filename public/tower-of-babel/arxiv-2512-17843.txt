ShareChat : A Dataset of Chatbot Conversations in the Wild
Yueru Yan, Tuc Nguyen, Bo Su, Melissa Lieffers, Thai Le

Abstract
While Large Language Models (LLMs) have evolved into distinct platforms with unique interface designs and capabilities, existing public datasets treat models as generic text generators, stripping away the interface context that actively shapes user interaction. To address this limitation, we present ShareChat , a large-scale, cross-platform corpus comprising 142,808 conversations and over 660,000 turns collected from publicly shared URLs across five major platforms: ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity, and Grok. ShareChat distinguishes itself by preserving native platform affordances often lost in standard logs, including reasoning traces, source links, and code artifacts, while spanning 101 languages over the period from April 2023 to October 2025. Furthermore, ShareChat offers substantially longer context windows and greater interaction depth than prior datasets. We demonstrate the dataset’s multifaceted utility through three demonstrative use cases: (1) analyzing conversation completeness to measure user intent satisfaction; (2) evaluating source citation behaviors in content generation; and (3) conducting temporal analysis to track evolving usage patterns. This work provides the community with a vital and timely resource for understanding authentic user-LLM chatbot interactions in the wild



1
Introduction

Conversational large language model (LLM)-based chatbot services have evolved rapidly in the past three years. The first widely adopted general purpose LLM chatbot, ChatGPT, was launched in November 2022 and has reached more than 700 million weekly active users by mid 2025
OpenAI Research 2025
. Following this success, many companies quickly released their own chatbot services, which also attracted substantial public attention. For example, Anthropic introduced the Claude families in 2023
Anthropic 2023
;
Anthropic 2024
;
Anthropic 2025
, Google deployed the Gemini family of models
Anil et al. 2023
, xAI launched Grok as a chatbot integrated with the social media platform X
xAI 2023
, Perplexity emerged as an answer engine that combines conversational interaction with web search
Perplexity AI 2024
, and DeepSeek released its R1 reasoning model and corresponding consumer chatbot app in January 2025
DeepSeek AI 2025
. Although all of these services are built on text-based LLMs, they differ in interface design, supported features, and safety policies, which in turn shape how users interact with them.

For example, Grok can surface live posts from X when providing answers, Claude models are optimized and evaluated for coding, math, and analysis tasks
Anthropic 2023
;
Anthropic 2024
, and Perplexity consistently presents responses with explicit source citations
Perplexity AI 2024
. These platform level design choices are not static. Commercial systems typically undergo continuous reinforcement learning and fine tuning on conversation logs and human feedback
Ouyang et al. 2022
;
Bai et al. 2022
;
Chen et al. 2024
, which tends to reinforce platform specific strengths and norms over time. As a result, it is important to understand interaction dynamics and user experience across platforms, instead of treating LLM capabilities as a property of models in isolation.

However, most widely used LLM benchmarks focus on curated single turn tasks, such as multiple choice knowledge tests or coding and math word problems
Hendrycks et al. 2021
;
Cobbe et al. 2021
. In practice, the use of conversational LLMs is much more diverse and complex: users ask follow up questions, change goals, or co construct content over many turns. Recent work has shown that even state of the art models often get “lost” in multi turn conversations, with reliability degrading as instructions are refined across turns
Laban et al. 2025a
.
Studying how people actually interact with LLMs in the wild therefore requires large scale, real conversation datasets, rather than only synthetic prompts or short, benchmark style dialogues.

Several real world chatbot LLM conversation datasets have been released in the past two years
Zhao et al. 2024
;
Zheng et al. 2024
;
Köpf et al. 2023
. For instance, WildChat
Zhao et al. 2024
compiles about one million conversations between users and ChatGPT collected via an access gateway that offered free usage in exchange for consent to share logs. LMSYS-Chat-1M
Zheng et al. 2024
contains one million conversations with twenty five state of the art models recorded through a single web interface (the Vicuna demo and Chatbot Arena). OpenAssistant Conversations
Köpf et al. 2023
provides human generated assistant style dialogues and detailed preference annotations to support alignment research.
These datasets are important resources, but they have several limitations:

•

Single-Interface Mediation:
They are all mediated via
a central interface
even when multiple backend models are available. Consequently, they do
not
fully capture how different commercial platforms, with distinct interaction designs and product positioning, influence the content that users share and the way they engage with LLMs.

•

Text-Only Representation:
In addition, existing real conversation datasets primarily focus on plain text message content. Yet real interactions with contemporary LLM systems can include more structure: users may enable a thinking mode that exposes intermediate reasoning traces, and some platforms attach source links or other metadata to model outputs, such as the cited web pages surfaced by Perplexity or embedded X posts shown by Grok. A richer multi platform dataset should preserve these elements when they are available, so that researchers can better understand how users interpret model reasoning, how they rely on external evidence, and how platform affordances shape behavior across turns.

•

Limited Interaction Depth:
Moreover, although some datasets explicitly include multi turn conversations
Zhao et al. 2024
, the average depth of a conversation is still relatively short. Given that context length is central to LLM training and performance, longer real user chatbot conversations are valuable both for understanding how user intents and prompts evolve and for investigating when and how models succeed or fail at using extended context
Laban et al. 2025a
.

•

Observer Bias:
Finally, data collection in these studies typically relies on pre-interaction consent, where users agree to share their data before or during the interaction in exchange for service access. This introduces the
Observer Bias
(or Hawthorne Effect)
McCambridge et al. 2014
, as users who are aware they are being monitored for research purposes may consciously or unconsciously alter their behavior, which often exhibits higher social desirability compared to authentic, unobserved private usage. In contrast,
ShareChat
relies on
post-hoc
sharing, where the decision to publish is made only after the natural interaction has concluded, thereby preserving the integrity of the original behavior during the conversation itself.

In this study, we address these gaps by constructing a large scale, multi platform corpus of authentic user-LLM conversations. We compile
142,808 conversations
with more than
660,293 interaction turns
from five widely used platforms: Claude, Perplexity, Grok, ChatGPT, and Gemini. All conversations are collected from URLs that users chose to share publicly on the respective platforms, spanning more than twenty months of AI chat usage (April 2023 to October 2025) and covering 101 distinct languages. The dataset therefore captures authentic usage patterns from a diverse, global user base, rather than simulated prompts or tasks designed by researchers. Each conversation record contains the complete sequence of user and assistant turns, along with platform specific metadata such as, when available, timestamps, model thinking traces, or source links attached by the interface. The data collection is done under IRB approval (#28569).

To demonstrate the specific utility of
ShareChat
, besides describing the basic statistics of the corpus, we present three illustrative case studies, each designed to leverage
ShareChat
’s unique features to overcome the limitation of prior resources.
First, we perform
conversation completeness analysis
, where we label whether specific user intentions are fully addressed. This utilizes
ShareChat
’s high
interaction depth
to capture the resolution of complex goals often obscured in shorter, shallow exchanges.
Second, we carry out
source domain analysis
that connects assistant responses to external resources surfaced by platforms like Perplexity and Grok. This leverages our preservation of
non-textual metadata
to evaluate how models ground their claims, which is a dimension absent in text-only datasets. Third, we conduct
temporal analysis
to examine response time and engagement patterns. This exploits our native
turn-level timestamps
to reveal authentic real-world interaction rhythms, free from the temporal distortions often introduced by
observer bias
in monitored settings.
Together, these analyses highlight the value of a multi-platform, multi-turn, and multi-lingual conversation corpus for understanding real user experiences with LLMs.

Our contributions are twofold. First, we construct and analyze what is, to our knowledge, the first multi platform, multi turn, and multi lingual conversation dataset of user LLM interactions, with substantially longer conversations on average than prior corpora and lower levels of toxic content. Second, we provide three concrete use cases: conversation completeness analysis, temporal analysis, and source link analysis, which together demonstrate how the dataset can be used to study user interaction behavior in depth.

Preliminary Analysis

The final dataset contains conversations from five platforms, featuring a wide range of languages, diverse user prompts and longer conversation with less toxic content compared to other datasets. In this section, we describe the basic statistics of the dataset and platform-specific attributes. We also present the results of our toxicity analysis and topic distribution.

3.1
Basic Statistics

In total,
ShareChat
has
142,808 conversations
with more than
660,293 interaction turns
. Table
2
presents comprehensive statistics comparing our multi-platform dataset with existing conversation corpora on number of turns, token length and languages used. The language detection was performed at message-level using lingua-py and then combined into conversation-level statistics in Table

In summary,

Similarly, it demonstrates greater linguistic diversity by spanning 101 distinct languages, whereas the most diverse competitor covers only 65. The model responses are also substantially longer, with a mean of 1115.30 tokens compared to 215.71 for LMSYS-Chat-1M, suggesting the presence of richer and more substantive interactions. ChatGPT comprises the largest portion of the corpus with 102,740 conversations, offering the broadest language coverage.

The key distinguishing characteristic of

3.2
Platform-Specific Interaction and Metadata Attributes

Different platforms reflect distinct design philosophies. ChatGPT and Gemini offer general-purpose conversational assistance. Perplexity focuses on search-oriented information retrieval. Grok targets social media-integrated question answering. Claude specializes in technical and reasoning support. These structural differences align with intended use cases and shape the types of user interactions preserved in
ShareChat
. Table
3
outlines the distinct data attributes captured across the five platforms, which vary significantly based on their functional priorities.

While ChatGPT and Gemini follow standard conversational formats, other platforms expose specialized content structures. Claude is unique in preserving “thinking blocks” and versioned code artifacts, enabling the analysis of intermediate reasoning and iterative coding processes. In contrast, Perplexity and Grok structure responses around retrieval. Perplexity organizes content into answers, sources, and images with inline citations, while Grok features a dedicated pane for web and social media sources (X posts). These structural differences allow for distinct lines of inquiry: technical workflow analysis for Claude versus citation accuracy and source integration studies for Perplexity and Grok.

The granularity of metadata also dictates the scope of possible temporal analysis. ChatGPT and Grok are the only platforms providing turn-level timestamps, which are essential for modeling conversation rhythm and response latency. ChatGPT further enriches this with model version identifiers (e.g.,
gpt-4
). Conversely, Perplexity lacks turn-level timing but offers unique engagement metrics, including view counts, share counts, and last-updated timestamps, effectively treating conversations as evolving public artifacts rather than static logs.

3.3
Toxicity Analysis

To assess toxicity in
ShareChat
, we employed both Detoxify
Hanu and Unitary team 2020
and OpenAI Moderation API
3
3

3

https://platform.openai.com/docs/guides/moderation
to detect toxic content in the conversations, following the approach used in WildChat analysis
Zhao et al. 2024
. Detoxify is a pre-trained multilingual toxicity classification model that computes toxicity scores across seven dimensions, while OpenAI Moderation provides commercial-grade content filtering. Given the language coverage limitations of Detoxify, we retained only conversational turns in languages explicitly supported by the model, resulting in a filtered dataset of 949,487 conversations (72.9% of the original corpus) across all five platforms, with more details shown in Appendix
A.2
. For Detoxify, we applied the same classification threshold as WildChat, marking messages as toxic when the toxicity score exceeds 0.1, while OpenAI Moderation uses its internal flagging mechanism to identify toxic content.

The results for turn-level statistics are shown in Figure
3
. Our analysis revealed substantial differences between the two detection methods, consistent with limited agreement observed in prior work where WildChat
Zhao et al. 2024
reported that Detoxify flagged 8.12% of user turns compared to the 6.05% flagged by OpenAI Moderation. At the turn level, we observed distinct patterns where Detoxify consistently detected higher toxicity rates for user messages, ranging from 2.8% to 5.6%, compared to the 1.3% to 4.0% range identified by OpenAI Moderation across all platforms. Notably, Claude showed the highest user toxicity under Detoxify detection at 5.6%. There appears to be a positive correlation between user and LLM toxicity rates within platforms. For example, Claude exhibited the highest user toxicity with 5.6% via Detoxify and 2.7% via OpenAI, and similarly showed elevated LLM toxicity at 6.4% and 3.1% respectively. Conversely, Perplexity demonstrated the lowest rates for both users, at 2.8% via Detoxify and 1.3% via OpenAI, and LLMs, at 0.5% via Detoxify and 1.8% via OpenAI.

Notably,
ShareChat
contains substantially less toxic content compared to existing benchmarks
. Our overall user toxicity rates of 4.1% via Detoxify and 2.9% via OpenAI are considerably lower than both WildChat at 6.05% and LMSYS-Chat-1M at 3.08% using the OpenAI method
Zhao et al. 2024
. Similarly, our LLM toxicity rates of 1.6% via Detoxify and 3.2% via OpenAI are lower than the 5.18% reported by WildChat and comparable to the 4.12% in LMSYS-Chat-1M.

4.1
Conversation Completeness Analysis

To assess how comprehensively large language models fulfill user needs in multi-turn help-seeking dialogues, we developed an automated completeness analysis pipeline that evaluates whether each user intention across a conversation is adequately addressed by the assistant. Our analysis reveals distinct performance across platforms, with ChatGPT and Claude demonstrating the highest consistency in fully satisfying user intentions, while Gemini, Grok and Perplexity exhibit significantly wider variance in conversational quality across multi-turn interactions.

The pipeline implements a three-stage workflow adapted from the DeepEval
5
5

5

https://github.com/confident-ai/deepeval?tab=readme-ov-file
conversation completeness metric framework. First, we extract user intentions from each conversation by prompting Qwen3-8B
Yang et al. 2025
with a temperature of 0.7 to identify the distinct goals or information needs expressed in the user turns, yielding a chronologically ordered list of intentions per conversation. Second, for each identified intention, we construct an evaluation prompt that presents the full conversation context alongside the specific intention and instruct Qwen3-8B to classify whether that intention was satisfied; the model produces a categorical verdict of
complete
(the assistant fully addressed the request),
partial
(the assistant provided some relevant information but the goal was incompletely met), or
incomplete
(the assistant failed to address the request or the conversation ended before meaningful progress). Finally, we aggregate these intention-level verdicts into a conversation-level completeness score by computing the weighted proportion of intentions, where complete verdicts contribute 1.0, partial verdicts contribute 0.5, and incomplete verdicts contribute 0.0. This three-way categorical framework allows us to capture nuanced degrees of conversational success beyond binary outcomes. We apply this pipeline uniformly across all five platforms in
ShareChat
, enabling direct cross-platform comparisons of how effectively different assistants satisfy user needs over the course of extended interactions. The prompts used and experiment details could be found in Appendix
A.4
.

ranging from single-turn queries to complex sessions involving multiple distinct intentions
. Furthermore, the prevalence of partial verdicts highlights that
many real-world interactions result in incomplete goal satisfaction
, a dynamic often obscured in benchmarks that prioritize single-turn correctness over multi-turn resolution. It showcases the functional emphasis of each platform, as the higher intention counts and completeness scores in ChatGPT and Claude reflect their usage for complex or general purpose problem solving, while the frequent partial verdicts and lower intention depth in Perplexity correspond to its search oriented design where model outputs often serve as intermediate information retrieval rather than exhaustive answers. By providing completeness labels alongside the raw conversation data,
ShareChat
supports the development of automatic evaluation metrics that better align with user satisfaction in multi-turn interactions, which in turn can serve as valuable training signals for reinforcement learning.

4.3
Timestamp Analysis

Timestamp data for the Grok and ChatGPT datasets permits a comprehensive temporal analysis as shown in Figure
7
. Timestamps were present for the vast majority of turns (99.97% for ChatGPT, 100% for Grok). For the small fraction of missing timestamps in ChatGPT data (1.40% of turns), we employed linear interpolation between known timestamps where feasible, and dropped affected turns when interpolation was not possible. We verified that results remained stable across different handling strategies, confirming that the small amount of missing data did not bias our findings.

After removing extreme outliers above the 99th percentile, we observe systematic differences between platforms. ChatGPT exhibits longer mean user response times than Grok (1,580 vs. 931 seconds), while producing shorter mean LLM response times (18.4 vs. 24.6 seconds). Median values are substantially closer across platforms (users: 93.4 vs. 91.2 seconds; LLMs: 6.9 vs. 16.8 seconds), indicating that mean differences are driven primarily by long-tail behavior.

To assess whether longer LLM outputs demand increased user processing time, we analyze the relationship between LLM response length (in characters) and subsequent user response latency. Across both platforms, correlations are near zero (ChatGPT:
r=0.034
; Grok:
r=0.021
), indicating that verbosity provides little predictive signal for typical user response behavior. While longer responses permit longer delays, they do not systematically increase central tendency, challenging implicit verbosity-as-engagement assumptions. The details could be found in Appendix
A.5
.

r=-0.238
; Spearman
\rho=-0.427
), indicating progressively faster responses as conversations lengthen, consistent with caching, adaptation, or context optimization effects. In contrast, Grok shows a significant positive correlation (Pearson
r=0.315

), suggesting increasing computational overhead with conversation length. User response times show minimal linear association with turn position on both platforms (ChatGPT:
r=0.030
,

r=-0.037

). Together, these divergent temporal dynamics highlight fundamental architectural differences in how the two systems handle extended interactions.

5
Discussion

The findings presented in this study offer several important implications for understanding human-AI interaction and for guiding future research on conversational LLMs. We structure our discussion around four key dimensions enabled by
ShareChat
. While we only have demonstrated three concrete use cases: conversation completeness, source content analysis and temporal analysis, the dataset’s utility extends well beyond these applications.

Complex Interaction Dynamics in Extended Contexts.

ShareChat
’s longer conversation contexts, averaging 4.62 turns compared to 2.02 in LMSYS-Chat-1M
Zheng et al. 2024
, provide a critical resource for studying phenomena that emerge only over extended interactions. Prior work has shown that model reliability can degrade as instructions are refined across turns
Laban et al. 2025b
, yet most existing corpora offer limited opportunities to investigate such dynamics due to their predominantly single-turn structure. The presence of conversations extending into dozens of turns, combined with the diversity of intention counts per conversation, enables researchers to examine how user needs evolve, how models maintain coherence over long contexts, and when breakdowns occur.

ShareChat
makes it possible to study these partial successes systematically, revealing where assistants provide useful but insufficient information and how users adapt their follow-up requests in response. This diversity can inform the development of models better equipped to manage multiple user needs within a single interaction, learning when to shift focus, when to return to unresolved intentions, and how to maintain coherence while serving users whose goals evolve over time.

Platform-Dependent Usage and Performance Variability.

Unlike the “universal assistant” assumption often found in general benchmarks, our cross-platform analysis reveals that user behavior is highly sensitive to the specific design and functional positioning of each platform. Our topic distribution analysis demonstrates that users actively select platforms for distinct roles, which aligns their requests with perceived strengths, such as using Perplexity for information retrieval versus Claude for technical reasoning. This functional specialization is mirrored in conversation completeness: while ChatGPT and Claude exhibit narrow, concentrated completeness scores indicating highly predictable output, Gemini and Grok show substantial variability. Notably, the high frequency of “partial” verdicts in Perplexity interactions reflects its distinct role as a search engine, where outputs often serve as intermediate steps rather than final answers. Researchers can use
ShareChat
to investigate these specialized performance profiles, moving beyond generic comparisons to determine which architectural features drive reliability for specific user intents.

Evidence-Based Reasoning and Temporal Metadata.

The preservation of rich metadata enables future research on retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) and interaction pacing in naturalistic settings. Our source content analysis reveals pronounced differences in how platforms integrate external evidence. Grok relies overwhelmingly on concentrated source types, while Perplexity draws from a diversified set of domains. These patterns have implications for understanding how users interpret and rely on cited evidence, and how platform affordances shape trust and verification behaviors. Additionally, the availability of turn-level timestamps enables the investigation of conversational rhythm and interaction pacing, offering a temporal dimension of user experience that text-only corpora cannot provide. Our analysis exposes contrasting architectural dynamics: while ChatGPT demonstrates progressively lower latency as conversations extend over multiple turns, Grok exhibits an inverse trend, with users experiencing slower generation speeds as context length grows.

Self-Selection and Safety in Public Sharing.

Finally, the substantially lower toxicity rates in our corpus compared to existing benchmarks, for both user messages and LLM responses, likely reflect the self-selection inherent in public sharing. Users who choose to share conversations may filter for interactions they consider appropriate or valuable, producing a corpus that captures more constructive usage patterns. This suggests that while public datasets may not fully capture the “dark side” of LLM usage (such as jailbreaking attempts), they offer a cleaner, high-quality resource for studying successful task completion and constructive human-AI collaboration.

6
Conclusion

In this study, we addressed the limitations of existing single interface datasets by constructing
ShareChat
, a large scale, multi platform corpus of user LLM conversations. By compiling more than 140,000 publicly shared interactions from five distinct platforms, we provided a resource that preserves the native metadata and diverse interaction designs of the current AI ecosystem. Our examination of the dataset statistics shows that the self selection process inherent in public sharing leads to a corpus with significantly longer conversation contexts and lower toxicity compared to standard interaction logs.

We further illustrated the value of this data through three specific analysis. First, our conversation completeness analysis indicated that user intentions are more frequently satisfied in general purpose models like ChatGPT and Claude, reflecting their emphasis on broad capabilities. Second, our source content analysis uncovers the distinct information retrieval strategies employed by Grok and Perplexity. Third, our temporal analysis suggests that ChatGPT and Grok exhibit contrasting temporal dynamics.
Collectively, these findings highlight pronounced differences in user behavior that align with the functional emphasis of each platform, underscoring the importance of studying LLM capabilities within their specific ecological contexts.

ShareChat
offers broader utility for connecting model capabilities with authentic usage patterns.
It not only provides a testbed for evaluating model reliability across turns, but also offers rich supervision for training models to handle complex, multi-turn user needs in different settings.

Limitations

Our study is subject to several limitations that should be considered when interpreting the results.

Selection Bias.

First, the reliance on publicly shared URLs introduces a trade-off between two forms of bias. On one hand, this approach mitigates the
Observer Bias
which we talked about in Section
1
, where participants alter their behavior simply because they know they are being monitored
McCambridge et al. 2014
. On the other hand, it introduces
Self-Selection Bias

Heckman 1979
. Because users voluntarily choose which conversations to generate links for, the corpus is likely skewed towards interactions they deem successful, interesting, or novel. Consequently,
ShareChat
may underrepresent mundane utility tasks or failed interactions where the model performed poorly, as users are less motivated to share such content publicly
Hu et al. 2017
.

Platform Imbalance.

Second, the dataset exhibits significant imbalance across platforms, reflecting current disparities in market share. ChatGPT accounts for over 70% of the collected conversations, whereas Claude represents less than 1%. Although we continue to expand the collection pipeline, researchers should exercise caution when performing direct cross-platform comparisons and may need to employ stratified sampling to account for volume differences.

Proxy Nature of Automated Metrics.

Finally, while our completeness analysis utilizes robust LLM-based evaluation, it remains a proxy metric. Automated judges provide a scalable approximation of user satisfaction but cannot perfectly capture the nuanced, subjective quality of human preference. These results should be interpreted as strong signals of structural completeness rather than absolute ground-truth measurements of user happiness. Future research is needed to validate these automated metrics against human judgment and to fully explore the evolving longitudinal dynamics of user behavior.

Preliminary Scope of Analysis.

Third, the specific analyses presented in this work, such as the source domain distribution and temporal engagement patterns, are intended as illustrative use cases to demonstrate the dataset’s utility. They are not exhaustive sociological studies. Future research is needed to fully explore these dimensions, particularly how these patterns evolve longitudinally as platform features change.

Ethical Considerations

Data Provenance and Consent.

The data collection methodology employed in this study strictly adheres to ethical standards regarding public data usage. The corpus consists exclusively of conversations sourced from URLs that users voluntarily generated and shared via the platforms’ native sharing features. By actively generating a shareable link, users establish a clear intent for public visibility, distinguishing these artifacts from private chat logs or leaked data. We did not attempt to bypass authentication walls, scrape private user accounts, or access data that was not publicly indexed.

Privacy Preservation and De-Identification.

Despite the public provenance of the data, we prioritized the protection of user privacy through a rigorous de-identification pipeline designed to mitigate risks associated with inadvertently exposed sensitive information.

•

PII Redaction:
We utilized automated Named Entity Recognition (NER) and regular expression matching to detect and redact Personally Identifiable Information (PII)—including real names, email addresses, phone numbers, and IP addresses—for different languages.

•

User Identifier Anonymization:
We do not store or release original platform-specific user IDs or usernames. All user identifiers were cryptographically hashed and converted into randomized, internal project identifiers during the initial processing stage. This ensures that the published dataset cannot be easily linked back to user profiles on the original platforms.

•

Aggregated Analysis:
The analyses presented in this paper, like the toxicity assessments and completeness scoring, are conducted solely on aggregated statistics. No individual user’s conversation history is singled out for qualitative scrutiny in a manner that could lead to re-identification or personal embarrassment.

Content Safety and Intended Use.

Researchers must be aware that
ShareChat
reflects real-world interactions and, consequently, contains content that may be offensive, biased, or toxic, as quantified in our toxicity analysis. We release this data specifically for research purposes, such as understanding human-AI interaction dynamics, studying safety alignment, and evaluating model performance, and not for training user-facing production models without further filtering. Users of
ShareChat
are required to adhere to the terms of use, which strictly prohibit attempts to de-anonymize users or leverage the data for malicious surveillance. By limiting collection to publicly accessible links and proactively scrubbing personal data, this work aims to balance the scientific value of ecological data with harm minimization.

Association between ZJU index and gallstones in US adult: a cross-sectional study of NHANES 2017–2020
Authors: Zuopu Xie, Xianpei Chen, Chunming Xie, Qi Yang, Hao Lin
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundBased on triglyceride, body mass index (BMI), alanine aminotransferase/ aspartate aminotransferase ratio, fasting blood glucose, the ZJU index is proven to be a novel and effective parameter for screening NAFLD in Chinese. This study aims to evaluate the correlation between ZJU values and the risk of developing gallstones.MethodsThe data from the National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES) database during the period from 2017 to 2020 were analyzed in this cross-sectional study. And 6155 participants were included to assess the correlation between the ZJU index and the formation of gallstones through multivariate logistic regression, receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve, subgroup analysis, and restricted cubic spline regression (RCS) analyses.ResultsIn this population-based study, 6155 participants aged 18 years and older were included, among which, 671 had gallstones. Logistic regression and RCS regression analyses indicated a significant positive non-linearly correlation between the ZJU index and the prevalence of gallstones (OR = 1.044, 95% CI: 1.032, 1.056), with an inflection point of 40.6. Subgroup analysis further revealed a stronger correlation between the risk of developing gallstones and the ZJU index in females. ROC analysis indicated that the ZJU index (AUC = 0.653) can serve as a more robust predictor for developing gallstones compared to traditional indices, with a sensitivity of 55.9%, a specificity of 67.1% and a cutoff of 43.4.ConclusionThis study provides evidence that elevated the ZJU index is correlated with an increased risk of developing gallstones.

Introduction

Gallstones is a highly prevalent disorder of the digestive system globally, manifesting through symptoms such as vomiting, nausea, abdominal discomfort, and anorexia [1]. Medically termed cholelithiasis, gallstones are characterized by the accumulation of mineral or lipid deposits within the gallbladder or common bile duct. Furthermore, gallstones have been correlated with increased incidence and mortality rates of prevalent chronic conditions, including cardiovascular diseases, hypertension, gastrointestinal neoplasms, and diabetes mellitus [2–6]. Epidemiological data indicate that gallstones affect approximately 10–15% of the population in the United States and other developed countries, with prevalence rates reaching up to 70% among American Indians [7, 8]. In the United States, gallstones impose a considerable burden on the healthcare system [9–11]. The development of gallstones is correlated with various risk factors, including gender, ethnicity, and age over 40 years old [12, 13]. Additional risk factors encompass rapid weight loss, metabolic syndrome, familial history of gallstones, and medication. Although previous studies have identified risk factors for developing gallstones, there is still a lack of reliable clinical indexes to prevent the formation of gallstones.

The ZJU index is a composite index that consists of anthropometric parameters and standard laboratory tests including BMI, triglycerides (TG), aspartate aminotransferase (AST), blood glucose levels, and alanine aminotransferase (ALT). The ZJU index was first proposed by Wang et al. [14] to predict the presence of nonalcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) in Chinese population, and studies showed that it acted better than other simple models that were developed based on Westerners for screening NAFLD. Following that, cohort studies have shown the strong predictive capability of the ZJU index in identifying NAFLD among overweight females in Western countries [15]. It is tightly correlated to obesity and metabolic disorders as a useful surrogate indicator for insulin resistance [16]. In addition, research has demonstrated a close correlation between lipid metabolism mechanisms correlated to NAFLD and the formation of gallstones [17].

However, the correlation between the ZJU index and the risk of developing gallstones has not been investigated yet. The ZJU index provides a comprehensive reflection on metabolic disorder trends, including blood glucose, obesity, and lipid levels in the early stage of a patient’s conditions. Therefore, it is hypothesized that the ZJU index may be correlated to an increased risk of developing gallstones, with the potential as a reliable tool for predicting the formation of gallstones.

NHANES, conducted by National Center for Health Statistics (NCHS) [18], is a population-based study designed to assess the correlation between nutrition, health promotion, and disease prevention. The survey on dietary, demographic, examination, and laboratory data shall be conducted every two years by taking physical examinations, interviews, and various sections. Additional information regarding the NHANES database is available at http://www.cdc.gov/nhanes.

This study aims to explore the correlation between the ZJU index and the formation of gallstones in a large, nationally-representative sample of American adults. The dataset was collected from NHANES during the period from 2017 to 2020.

Methods

Research subjects

The baseline clinical data analyzed in this study were derived from NHANES during the period from 2017 to 2020. The data were collected from participants who explicitly responded to questions on the presence of gallstones. To make our study more reasonable, we screened the participants, Fig. 1 illustrates the screening procedure. A total of 6155 participants were included in this study.

Flowchart of the sample selection from the 2017–2020 NHANES

Measurement of covariates

The demographics and lifestyle data came from the questionnaires on household interviews administered by highly trained medical personnel. Anthropometric indexes and biochemical parameters were obtained through medical examinations and subsequent laboratory assessments in the Mobile Examination Centre (MEC). According to previous studies [7, 19–21], potential confounding factors correlated with developing gallstones and the ZJU index were incorporated into the final analysis. These factors included demographic variables, such as age, height, race (Mexican Americans, Other Hispanic, Non-Hispanic White, Non-Hispanic Black, or Other Race), gender (male or female), marital status (married/ living with partner or widowed/ divorced/ separated/ unmarried), waist circumference (WC), educational attainment (less than high school, high school or above), weight, income-to-poverty ratio (PIR), and moderate physical activities (Walking or cycling, employment, and amusement are all considered physical activities, including active activities with a participate in one or more of those activities, and a non-active activity otherwise). Venous blood was sampled from patients early the following morning in the fasting state. Total cholesterol (TC), uric acid (UA), fasting plasma glucose (FPG), albumin, low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C), ALT, TG, gamma-glutamyl transferase (GGT), AST, creatinine, and high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C) were collected in blood samples. Questionnaire surveys included alcohol consumption, hypertension, diabetes mellitus, dietary intake factors, encompassing fat, energy, water, and sugar. All the participants completed 24-hour dietary recalls on the mean consumption rates derived from these two recalls. The detailed measurement methods and data collection for each variable can be accessed at www.cdc.gov/nchs/nhanes.

Definition of ZJU index

The ZJU index was first proposed by Wang et al. [14] to predict the presence of NAFLD in the Chinese population. ZJU index = ALT(IU/L)/AST(IU/L) ratio*3 + BMI (kg/m2) + TG (mmol/L) + FPG (mmol/L) (+ 2 if females) [14, 22].

Statistical analysis

For continuous variables, the distribution of variables was reported with mean ± standard deviation, whereas frequencies and percentages were reported for categorical variables to provide a thorough perspective. Differences among quartiles were assessed with chi-square test or Mann–Whitney U test. The correlation between the ZJU index and gallstones was examined in the three distinct models through a multiple logistic regression model. Model 1 (unadjusted), Model 2 (adjusted for race, gender, and age), and Model 3 (further adjusted for obesity, alcohol intake, educational level, TC, moderate physical activities, diabetes mellitus, albumin, PIR, hypertension, creatinine, GGT, uric acid, total water, total energy, total sugar and total fat) were adopted in this study. Subgroup analysis was conducted to explore potential differences across different subgroups, and RCS analysis were employed to explore potential nonlinear dose-response correlation between the ZJU indices and the formation of gallstones. If the non-linearity was detected, then the two-piecewise linear regression models was performed on both parts of the inflection point with a recursive algorithm. Sensitivity analyses were performed after excluding individuals with extreme energy intake values (consuming less than 500 kcal or more than 5000 kcal per day). Additionally, the unweighted raw data were analyzed through the Inverse Probability Weighting (IPW) regression analysis to address potential confounding variables. Finally, the predictive performance of ZJU, BMI, TG, FPG, and ALT/AST was compared with ROC curve. Furthermore, E-value was computed to determine the minimum strength of correlation that an unmeasured confounder would need to have with both ZJU and gallstones to explain the observed correlation. E-value for the correlation between ZJU and gallstones was 1.255. Data analyses were performed with R software and Free Statistics software, with a significance threshold at p < 0.05 for all statistical tests.

The increase of the ZJU index was positively correlated with the incidence of gallstones

Subjects were categorized into quartiles (Q1:≤35.5; Q2: 35.5–40.3; Q3: 40.3–46.3; Q4:≥46.3) according to ZJU indexes. As illustrated in Fig. 2, the quartile range of ZJU increased when a notable rised in the prevalence of gallstones (4.2% vs. 9.8% vs. 12.0% vs. 17.6%, p < 0.001). In the multiple logistic regression analysis, a significant positive correlation was found between the ZJU index and the formation of gallstones after adjusting for confounders in Model 3 (OR = 1.044, 95% CI: 1.032, 1.056). According to the sensitivity analysis, ZJU values were categorized into quartiles, showing that in the fully adjusted Model 3, subjects in the second, third, and fourth quartiles exhibited a statistically significant increase in the risk of developing gallstones by 1.105, 1.431 and 2.620, respectively, compared to those in the lowest quartile (as shown in Table 2).

To further explore the correlation between ZJU and gallstones across various groups stratified by age, BMI, education level, gender, moderate physical activities and disease status (including diabetes mellitus and hypertension), subgroup analyses were conducted. The correlation between ZJU and gallstones was stronger among females (OR = 1.049, p interaction = 0.033) (as shown in Fig. 4).

Predictive value of ZJU for gallstones

ROC curve in Fig. 5 shows the diagnostic performance of ZJU, BMI, TG, FPG and ALT/AST in identifying gallstones. ZJU exhibited the highest diagnostic accuracy for gallstones, with an AUC value of 0.653 (95% CI: 0.632–0.674), significantly exceeding other measurement indexes in individuals (p < 0.001), with a sensitivity of 55.9%, a specificity of 67.1% and a cutoff of 43.4.

Discussion

This cross-sectional study conducted on the data from NHANES aims to evaluate the correlation between ZJU and the prevalence of gallstones. The results indicate a positive correlation between ZJU and an elevated risk of developing gallstones. This correlation was particularly pronounced among females. Notably, non-linearly correlations were observed between ZJU and gallstones, with a saturation value of 40.6. Furthermore, ROC analysis indicated that ZJU exhibited superior predictive ability for developing gallstones compared to BMI, TG, FPG or ALT/AST alone, with statistical significance.

The ZJU index, initially proposed by Wang et al. in a cross-sectional study involving 9,602 Chinese participants underwent hepatic ultrasound examination for NAFLD, is recognized as a proficient tool for screening NAFLD in the Chinese population (AUC = 0.822) [14]. Subsequent cohort studies have confirmed ZJU’s robust predictive capability for identifying NAFLD in overweight females among Western populations [15]. It is closely correlated with obesity and metabolic disorders, as a valuable surrogate marker for insulin IR [16]. Similarly, Wang et al. demonstrated a significant correlation between ZJU and an elevated risk of obstructive sleep apnea syndrome in the general population [23]. Nonetheless, the correlation between IBI and depression remains unclear.

Numerous studies have demonstrated that elevated blood glucose and cholesterol levels are independently correlated with gallstones [24–26]. Furthermore, BMI, an index of general adiposity, has been shown to double the risk of developing gallstones in individuals with obesity or overweight [27–29]. Research on chronic liver injury correlated with gallstones has demonstrated that gallstones serve as an independent risk factor for liver enzyme irregularities [30]. ZJU, originally developed to detect NAFLD in Chinese adults, integrates BMI with ALT, TG, FBG, and AST [14]. which are intricately correlated to the development of gallstones. To date, this study first explored into the correlation between ZJU and gallstones. AUC of ZJU was significantly higher than that of BMI, TG, FPG or ALT/AST alone. ZJU indices not only integrate anthropometric indicators, but also comprehensively evaluate the metabolic state of the body, thereby enhancing the practicability and accuracy of evaluation.

Subgroup analysis indicated that the correlation between ZJU and gallstones was notably stronger in females than that in males (p < 0.001 for interaction). Consistent with the findings, the study of Zheng et al. demonstrated that the correlation effect between ZJU and the risk of new onset NAFLD in females is greater than that in males [31]. Typically, the gender disparity in the prevalence of gallstones is attributed to hormonal influences. Estrogen enhances the excretion of cholesterol into the bile by upregulating the expression of 3-hydroxy-3-methylglutaryl coenzyme A (HMG-CoA) reductase gene, leading to cholesterol supersaturation in the bile [32, 33], thereby promoting the formation of gallstones.

The research discovered a strong non-linearly between ZJU and gallstones, indicating that the likelihood of developing gallstones rises as ZJU values increase. If the ZJU index reaches the threshold of 40.6, the patient was at a high risk of developing gallstones. Hence, it is advantageous to identify individuals who are at a risk of developing gallstones at an early stage by assessing the ZJU index, as this facilitates the implementation of preventive measures and treatment before the onset of the disease.

Potential mechanisms, such as obesity, IR, hyperlipidemia, may explain the positive correlation between ZJU and gallstones. A study conducted within a high-risk Hispanic cohort demonstrated that IR modifies gallbladder function by enhancing the production of cholesterol-supersaturated bile, which subsequently contributes to the formation of gallstones [34]. These findings have been substantiated by animal experiments, showing that mice with isolated hepatic IR have an increased susceptibility to cholesterol the formation of gallstones [35]. The underlying mechanism may be due to the upregulation of biliary cholesterol transporters, which is the result of disinhibition of the forkhead transcription factor FoxO1, leading to increased cholesterol secretion. An alternative mechanism may involve hepatic IR, diminishing the expression of bile acid synthetic enzymes, thereby resulting in a lithogenic bile salt profile. The correlation between obesity and gallstones may be attributable to elevated cholesterol secretion, leading to cholesterol-supersaturated bile precipitating as cholesterol gallstones [36]. Additionally, impaired gallbladder motility correlated with decreased sensitivity to cholecystokinin in individuals with obesity may further contribute to the pathogenesis of gallstones [37]. Furthermore, leptin, a hormone crucial in the pathogenesis of obesity, has been implicated in cholelithiasis through its regulation of bile acid metabolism in vitro [38].

Study strengths and limitations

This study possesses several notable strengths. Firstly, the samples in this study were derived from NHANES, which is characterized by its extensive sample size and high-quality data. Secondly, this study considered multiple confounding variables and subgroup and sensitivity analyses were conducted to ensure the generalizability of the findings among different populations. Thirdly, this is the first in its examination of the correlation between ZJU and gallstones, and to evaluate the potential of ZJU as a predictive marker for developing gallstones. However, this study is subject to several limitations. Firstly, this study does not establish a causal correlation between ZJU and gallstones. Secondly, the absence or presence of gallstones was determined through self-reported questionnaires, without conducting imaging studies, introducing the potential for recall bias. Thirdly, the study did not account for the influence of hormone levels and medication. Future studies addressing these factors are warranted to corroborate these findings.

Conclusion

This study identified a positive correlation between ZJU and gallstones in American adults. Furthermore, ZJU demonstrates superior predictive capability for gallstones compared to traditional indices. The findings of this study support clinicians in screening and identifying patients with gallstones who require management and follow-up by monitoring the ZJU index in clinical practice.

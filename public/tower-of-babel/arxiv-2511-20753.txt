On the role of Area Metrics in AdS/CFT
Aranya Bhattacharya, Lavish Chawla, Mario Flory, Mateusz Kulig

Abstract
We explore the potential role of area metrics, a generalised notion of geometry, in the AdS/CFT correspondence. Guided by the Ryu-Takayanagi formula and the first law of entanglement, we derive both a holographic dictionary as well as a bulk equation of motion for area metric fluctuations linearised around a four dimensional AdS background. Furthermore, we explore potential bulk Lagrangians that could give rise to the derived equation. It is shown that writing area metric fluctuations in terms of Lanczos like potentials gives rise to a particularly elegant bulk model. A 15-minute video abstract can be found at this link

II
Existence of dual EMT

Without a doubt, area metrics are a considerable departure from the familiar (length-) metric based Riemannian geometry. If such a generalised notion of geometry is introduced in the bulk, does that mean that the (putative) dual theory is likewise only defined on a generalised area metric background?

The answer to this is no, for the following reason: Suppose we look at a near-boundary cutoff
z=\epsilon
.
Here,
\epsilon
doesn’t even need to be small. This defines a co-dimension one surface in the bulk, on which the bulk area metric
G_{{\mu\nu}{\rho\sigma}}
defines an
induced area metric

G_{mnrs}
.
But a co-dimension one surface in the bulk is three-dimensional, and in three dimensions area metrics and ordinary metrics are interchangeable, see table
1
and
Ho and Inami 2016
. One can translate from one to the other using (
5
) and

\displaystyle g^{ab}=\mp\frac{1}{8}\epsilon_{opq}\epsilon_{rst}G^{aobr}G^{pqst}.

(10)

Hence the boundary theory can be equivalently written as either defined on a metric background or an area metric background in terms of an action

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{S}[g_{mn},\phi,\partial_{a}\phi,...]=\mathcal{S}[G_{mnrs},\phi,\partial_{a}\phi,...]

(11)

where
\phi
are the fields of the theory. This allows us to define not only an
energy momentum tensor
(EMT)

\displaystyle T_{ab}=\frac{-2}{\sqrt{-g}}\frac{\delta\mathcal{S}}{\delta g^{ab}},

(12)

for the dual field theory, but also a new tensor

\displaystyle T_{abcd}\equiv\frac{-2}{\sqrt{-g}}\frac{\delta\mathcal{S}}{\delta G^{abcd}}=T_{mn}\frac{\delta g^{mn}}{\delta G^{abcd}}.

(13)

which we term the
surface energy tensor
.
It will always be possible to translate
T_{abcd}
to
T_{mn}
and vice versa, i.e. these two tensors encode the same physical information in different ways. See the supplementary material for detailed expressions.

III
Holographic Dictionary

In the context of the AdS/CFT correspondence, the Ryu-Takayanagi (RT) formula
Ryu and Takayanagi 2006a
;
Ryu and Takayanagi 2006b
relates the entanglement entropy of a subregion
A
of a pure state in the boundary CFT to the area of a minimal surface
\Sigma
in the dual bulk spacetime, anchored at the boundary
\partial A
of the original subregion in the CFT:

S_{A}=\frac{\text{Area}(\Sigma)}{4G_{N}}.

In the field theory, this observable is computed by the von Neumann entropy
S_{A}=-\Tr(\rho_{A}\log\rho_{A})
of the reduced density matrix
\rho_{A}
obtained by tracing out the complementary subregion from the full density matrix
\rho
.

The central relation underlying our work is the
first law of entanglement

Bhattacharya et al. 2013
;
Blanco et al. 2013
.
This is an equality

\delta S_{A}=\delta\langle H_{A}\rangle

(14)

between the leading order changes in the entanglement entropy (
\delta S_{A}
) and the expectation value of the modular Hamiltonian (
\delta\langle H_{A}\rangle
) when perturbing the overall state as
\rho\rightarrow\rho+\delta\rho
.
Via the RT formula, this equation relates changes of the bulk geometry to changes of the modular Hamiltonian in the boundary theory.

The modular Hamiltonian
H_{A}=-\log(\rho_{A})
, which is in general tricky to compute, is known for a ball-shaped boundary spatial subregion centred at
\vec{x}_{0}
with radius
R
on a constant time-slice
t=t_{0}

Bhattacharya et al. 2013
;
Lashkari et al. 2014
:

\delta\langle H_{A}\rangle=2\pi\int_{A(R,\vec{x}_{0})}d^{2}x\frac{R^{2}-|\vec{x}-\vec{x}_{0}|^{2}}{2R}\delta\langle T_{tt}(t_{0},\vec{x})\rangle,

(15)

where
T_{tt}(t_{0},\vec{x})
is the
\{t,t\}-
component of the boundary EMT. In the limit
R\ll 1
,
\langle T_{tt}(t_{0},\vec{x})\rangle
is approximately constant throughout the infinitesimal ball resulting in

\displaystyle\delta\langle H_{A}\rangle\big|_{R\ll 1}\approx\frac{\pi^{2}R^{3}}{2}\delta\langle T_{tt}(t_{0},\vec{x}_{0})\rangle.

(16)

Using (
14
), we can then write the expectation value of the EMT for an infinitesimal ball in terms of the variation of entanglement entropy as
Van Raamsdonk 2017

\delta\langle T_{tt}(t_{0},\vec{x}_{0})\rangle=\frac{2}{\pi^{2}}\lim_{R\rightarrow 0}\left(\frac{\delta S_{A(R,\vec{x}_{0})}}{R^{3}}\right).

(17)

In our setup, we assume that the RT formula remains valid if the bulk geometry is given by an area metric (
6
), and hence
\delta S_{A}
gets contribution from both
h_{{\mu\nu}}
and
w_{\mu\rho\nu\lambda}
. Using this in the infinitesimal ball limit (
17
), we derive a holographic dictionary relation between the boundary stress tensor and the perturbations of the bulk geometry.
By applying a boost along the boundary directions, we obtain a more general dictionary involving the components
\delta\langle T_{ab}\rangle
.

Known result;
w_{\mu\rho\nu\lambda}
turned off:

In the case of usual length metric perturbations
h_{{\mu\nu}}
, the above relation in the infinitesimal ball limit is well known:

\displaystyle\delta\langle T_{ab}(t_{0},\vec{x}_{0})\rangle=\frac{3}{16\pi G_{N}}H^{(0)}_{ab}(t_{0},\vec{x}_{0}),

(18)

where we assume the Fefferman-Graham gauge
h_{\mu z}=0
throughout the paper and
h_{{\mu\nu}}=z\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}z^{n}H_{{\mu\nu}}^{(n)}(t,\vec{x})
.
This expansion tells us that the leading order metric perturbation in powers of
z
has to be
\sim z^{1}
for the boundary EMT in the infinitesimal ball limit to be non-vanishing and non-divergent.
It is worth noting that the tracelessness and conservation of the boundary stress tensor implies the following conditions on
H_{ab}^{(0)}

Lashkari et al. 2014
;
Faulkner et al. 2014
:

H^{(0)}{}_{a}^{a}=0,\,\,\partial^{a}H^{(0)}_{ab}=0.

(19)

New result;
h_{{\mu\nu}}
turned off:

In this case, we turn off the length metric
h_{{\mu\nu}}
and keep only the purely area metric fluctuations
w_{\mu\rho\nu\lambda}
. Firstly, the leading order variation in the area of the minimal surface
\Sigma
takes the form (see appendix
B
for details)

\delta A=\int_{\Sigma}d^{2}x\frac{w_{\mu\rho\nu\lambda}\vartheta^{I\,J}\vartheta^{K\,L}\partial_{I}X^{\mu}\partial_{J}X^{\nu}\partial_{K}X^{\rho}\partial_{L}X^{\lambda}}{8\sqrt{\hat{g}}},

(20)

where
\hat{g}
is the determinant of the unperturbed induced metric on
\Sigma
and
\vartheta^{IJ}
is the Levi-Civita symbol in two dimensions, with
I\in\{x,y\}
. The extremal surface for the leading order variation remains the same as the one for the empty AdS
4

Ryu and Takayanagi 2006b
;
Bhattacharya et al. 2013
;
Bhattacharya et al. 2019
:

z(x,y)=\sqrt{R^{2}-|\vec{x}-\vec{x}_{0}|^{2}}.

(21)

Evaluating (
20
) in the infinitesimal ball limit, we get the following identity for the boundary stress tensor:

\delta\langle T_{ab}(t_{0},\vec{x}_{0})\rangle=\frac{1}{16\pi L^{2}G_{N}}\eta^{ij}W^{(0)}_{aibj}(t_{0},\vec{x}_{0}),

(22)

and also the fact that the
z
power series expansion of the
w
fluctuations has to be of the form

w_{\mu\rho\nu\lambda}(z,t,\vec{x})\equiv\frac{1}{z}W_{\mu\rho\nu\lambda}(z,t,\vec{x})=\frac{1}{z}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}z^{n}W_{\mu\rho\nu\lambda}^{(n)}(t,\vec{x}),

(23)

for the stress tensor to have a non-vanishing and non-divergent contribution in the infinitesimal ball limit.

These are the two main results that we obtain as our entry to the holographic dictionary for purely area metric fluctuations. One reassuring thing is to note that the tracelessness (
9
) already ensures that the boundary stress tensor sourced by the
w
fluctuation is traceless. Subsequently, the conservation of the stress tensor implies

\partial^{a}(\eta^{ij}W^{(0)}_{aibj})=0.

(24)

Full area fluctuation:

Finally, when we have the full fluctuation
a_{\mu\rho\nu\lambda}
turned on, the dictionary takes the form

\displaystyle\delta\langle T_{ab}\rangle=\frac{1}{16\pi G_{N}}\left(3H^{(0)}_{ab}+\frac{1}{L^{2}}\eta^{ij}W^{(0)}_{aibj}\right).

(25)

Tracelessness and conservation of the stress tensor then imply

H^{(0)}{}^{a}_{a}=0,\,\partial^{a}\left(3H^{(0)}_{ab}+\frac{1}{L^{2}}\eta^{ij}W^{(0)}_{aibj}\right)=0.

(26)

This analysis confirms that we can understand the boundary stress tensor
\langle T_{ab}\rangle
to source the full area metric fluctuations
a_{\mu\rho\nu\lambda}
in the AdS
4
bulk, not just the
h_{\mu\nu}
part.

IV
Equations of Motion

It was shown in
Blanco et al. 2013
;
Lashkari et al. 2014
;
Faulkner et al. 2014
that given the validity of the first law of entanglement for boundary CFT states and the holographic dictionary for length metrics (
18
), one can derive the linearised Einstein’s equations around an empty AdS background. For AdS
4
with
\Lambda=-\frac{3}{L^{2}}
, the equations become:

\displaystyle 0=E_{{\mu\nu}}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{1}{2}\left(\nabla_{\sigma}\nabla_{\mu}h^{\sigma}_{\nu}+\nabla_{\sigma}\nabla_{\nu}h^{\sigma}_{\mu}-\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}h-\square h_{{\mu\nu}}\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}g_{{\mu\nu}}\left(\nabla_{\rho}\nabla_{\sigma}h^{\rho\sigma}-\square h-\Lambda h\right)-\Lambda h_{\mu\nu}.

(27)

While the
\{t,t\}-
component of (
27
) is derived directly from the first law of entanglement, the
\{a,b\}-
components can be obtained by introducing a boost in the boundary directions. Finally the
\{z,\mu\}-
components imply the conservation and tracelessness of the metric perturbation
h_{ab}
:

h^{a}_{a}=0,\,\partial^{a}h_{ab}=0~\text{(all orders in $z$)}.

(28)

In the first law of entanglement (
14
),
we use a power series expansion in
z
for fluctuations on the LHS for finite
R
, while the RHS is given by just the leading order term due to the dictionary (
18
). One then makes use of the equality between the two sides order by order in the expansion
2
2

2

The authors of
Lashkari et al. 2014
explicitly apply this method to derive linearised Einstein’s equations
.

Coming to area metrics, now that we have the dictionary (
22
) for
w_{{\mu\nu}{\alpha\beta}}
, we can implement the same step by step analysis to find the corresponding equations of motion. At first, we again assume that the length-metric fluctuations
h_{{\mu\nu}}
are turned off. Since (
22
) is an identity about the rank two boundary EMT
T_{ab}
, we find the equations of motion to be rank two as well. Using this identity for
T_{tt}
, we find a second order differential equation to all orders in the power series expansion as (see appendix

for a detailed analysis),

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{z^{4}}\partial^{I}\partial^{J}W_{t{I}t{J}}+\partial_{z}^{2}W_{tztz}+\frac{2}{z}\left(\frac{1}{z^{2}}\partial^{I}W_{t{I}tz}+\partial_{z}W_{tztz}\right)

\displaystyle+2\partial_{z}\left(\frac{1}{z^{2}}\partial^{I}W_{t{I}tz}\right).

(29)

This expression can be written more compactly by using the identity in (
23
) that relates
w_{{\mu\nu}\rho\lambda}
with
W_{{\mu\nu}\rho\lambda}
, yielding

\nabla^{\rho}\nabla^{\lambda}w_{t\rho t\lambda}=0,

(30)

which we then extend to all boundary directions
\{a,b\}
by demanding boost invariance. Finally, as a consistency check, if we naively extend these equations to
\{\mu,\nu\}
(bulk) indices, we find that the
\{z,\mu\}
equations of motions are satisfied if the conservation of the stress tensor holds for all orders in

, similar to the length metric case,

\partial^{a}\left(\eta^{ij}w_{aibj}\right)=0~\text{(all orders in $z$).}

(31)

Our proposal for covariant linearised equations for pure area metric fluctuations
w_{\mu\rho\nu\lambda}
around empty
AdS_{4}
is then

\nabla^{\rho}\nabla^{\lambda}w_{\mu\rho\nu\lambda}=0,

(32)

which resembles the equation of motion of conformal gravity when comparing
w_{\mu\rho\nu\lambda}
to a Weyl tensor

Anastasiou and Olea 2016
;
Maldacena 2011
;
Manvelyan and Rühl 2004
;
Dehghani et al. 2008
, implying that the pure area metric like degrees of freedom couple conformally to the background spacetime.
The boundary dual of these area metric fluctuations even in the absence of
h_{{\mu\nu}}
can be linked to an operator
\mathcal{O}_{w}
with conformal dimension
h_{\mathcal{O}_{w}}=3
, which also happens to be the conformal dimension of the stress tensor in CFT
3
. This supports our previous derivation of holographic dictionary relating the boundary stress tensor to the purely area metric fluctuations.

Finally, turning on the length metric fluctuations
h_{{\mu\nu}}
again in addition to
w_{\mu\rho\nu\lambda}
, we can redo the same steps to derive the linearised equations of motion for a full area metric perturbation
a_{\mu\rho\nu\lambda}
to be

E_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{\rho}\nabla^{\lambda}w_{\mu\rho\nu\lambda}=0.

(33)

This is the central result of our paper.

V
Lagrangian formulation

Having derived an equation of motion for the area metric, the question is now whether this follows from a Lagrangian. It is actually not clear to us whether an emergent gravity style derivation
has
to arrive at an equation with a Lagrangian origin, but it would certainly be a nice feature to have.

It is easy to see that (
33
) follows from a Lagrangian of the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{EH}[h]-\frac{1}{2}h^{{\alpha\beta}}\nabla^{\mu}\nabla^{\nu}w_{\alpha\mu\beta\nu}+\mathcal{L}_{w^{2}}[w],

(34)

by taking the variation
\frac{\delta}{\delta h^{\mu\nu}}
. Here,
\mathcal{L}_{EH}[h]
is the Lagrangian of linearised Einsteinian gravity, and
\mathcal{L}_{w^{2}}[w]
stands for terms independent of
h_{\mu\nu}
but quadratic in
w_{{\mu\nu}{\alpha\beta}}
or its derivatives. Partially following
Borissova et al. 2024
;
Borissova et al. 2025
, we consider the general ansatz

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{w^{2}}=

\displaystyle-m^{2}w_{\alpha\mu\beta\nu}w^{\alpha\mu\beta\nu}-K_{1}(\nabla_{\delta}w_{\alpha\mu\beta\nu})(\nabla^{\delta}w^{\alpha\mu\beta\nu})

\displaystyle-K_{2}(\nabla^{\alpha}w_{\alpha\mu\beta\nu})(\nabla_{\delta}w^{\delta\mu\beta\nu}).

(35)

Unfortunately, our results in the preceding sections does not directly constrain the parameters of the ansatz (
35
) at all, however, there is something we can do about it.
The holographic dictionary is usually derived from the holographic renormalisation procedure
de Haro et al. 2001
;
Skenderis 2002
, and we anticipate that doing the same procedure (including identification of (non-)normalisable modes and proper boundary terms) for the action (
35
) will in general yield a result inconsistent with (
25
).
Demanding consistency with this result may then suffice to constrain the parameters of (
35
) in non-trivial ways.
The first step in this direction is ensuring that the equations of motion derived from (
35
) allow for solutions with the asymptotic behaviour
w\sim z^{-1}
which is required by (
23
). In the supplementary material, we identify

m^{2}=-\frac{6}{L^{2}}K_{1}

as a promising ansatz. Further work in this direction is left to future publications, however.

VI
Introducing Lanczos potentials

Taking the variation
\frac{\delta}{\delta w^{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}}
of (
34
), the resulting equation of motion reads

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\delta^{(h)}_{1}C_{{\alpha\beta}{\gamma\delta}}+...

(36)

where the
+...
stand for terms linear in
w
(coming from
\mathcal{L}_{w^{2}}
), and
\delta^{(h)}_{1}C_{{\alpha\beta}{\gamma\delta}}
represents the first order change in the bulk Weyl-tensor due to
h_{\mu\nu}
. This means that in the limit of ordinary Einsteinian
geometry
(
w\rightarrow 0
), this equation yields a non-trivial constraint on
h_{{\mu\nu}}
which greatly reduces the space of solutions of (
27
); we do not simply recover linearised Einsteinian
gravity
in this limit.

A possible solution to this problem works as follows: Just like the Weyl tensor can always be written in terms of a Lanczos potential
Lanczos 1962
;
O’Donnell and Pye 2010
,
any
tensor of the form of
w_{\alpha\mu\beta\nu}
can be written in terms of a potential
Bampi and Caviglia 1983

\displaystyle\ell_{{\alpha\beta}\gamma}=-\ell_{{\beta\alpha}\gamma},\ \ell_{[{\alpha\beta}\gamma]}=0,

(37)

see the supplementary material for the explicit expression.
Taking the variation
\frac{\delta}{\delta\ell^{{\alpha\beta}\gamma}}
of (
34
), the term
\frac{-1}{2}h^{{\alpha\beta}}\nabla^{\mu}\nabla^{\nu}w_{\alpha\mu\beta\nu}
(which is the origin of (
36
)), results in a 3rd-order differential expression for
h_{{\mu\nu}}
which reads

\displaystyle F_{{\alpha\beta}\gamma}\equiv\frac{1}{3}g_{\alpha\gamma}\nabla_{\beta}E^{\mu}_{\mu}-\frac{1}{3}g_{\beta\gamma}\nabla_{\alpha}E^{\mu}_{\mu}-\nabla_{\beta}E_{\alpha\gamma}+\nabla_{\alpha}E_{\beta\gamma},

(38)

and thus

\displaystyle E_{{\mu\nu}}=0\Rightarrow F_{\kappa\rho\sigma}=0.

(39)

Building the bulk theory on
\ell
as fundamental variable instead of
w
, we can hence re-obtain the linearised Einstein equations and their full set of solutions, without additional constraint, in the limit
w\rightarrow 0
.

VII
Discussion

To summarise, starting from the first law of entanglement and the Ryu-Takayanagi formula as first principles, we have systematically derived how area metrics can fit into the AdS/CFT correspondence in the linearised approximation. Our results establish the holographic dictionary for area metric fluctuations, how they couple to ordinary metric fluctuations, as well as what Lagrangian may give rise to such a bulk theory. We have also demonstrated how writing the purely area metric fluctuation in terms of a Lanczos potential yields a bulk model which reproduces Einstein’s theory in the limit of ordinary metric geometry.

We expect these results to lay the groundwork for a more thorough investigation of holographic area metric gravity in the future. Obvious questions of interest may be the extension of our results to non-AdS backgrounds, beyond the leading order or beyond perturbation theory all-together, to cases including bulk matter fields, and to higher bulk dimensions. It will also be interesting to compare holographic results to others like e.g.
Schuller and Wohlfarth 2006b
;
Giesel et al. 2012
;
Ho and Inami 2016
;
Borissova et al. 2024
;
Borissova and Ho 2024
and see whether there is a confluence of results coming from different theoretical frameworks, such as e.g. string theory, LQG, and holography.

Appendix A
Existence of dual EMT

As explained in the main text, area metrics and length metrics are equivalent on the three dimensional boundary. They are related by the equations
Ho and Inami 2016

\displaystyle G^{mnab}=g^{ma}g^{nb}-g^{na}g^{mb}

(40)

and

\displaystyle g^{ab}=-\frac{1}{8}\epsilon_{opq}\epsilon_{rst}G^{aobr}G^{pqst}=-\frac{|g|}{8}\vartheta_{opq}\vartheta_{rst}G^{aobr}G^{pqst}.

(41)

Here, we have picked a concrete sign, consistent with the Lorentzian case
g=\det[g_{ab}]<0
, and distinguished

Levi-Civita-
symbol
:

\displaystyle\vartheta_{abc}

(42)

Levi-Civita-
tensor
:

\displaystyle\epsilon_{abc}=\sqrt{|g|}\vartheta_{abc}

(43)

so that

\displaystyle\frac{\delta\vartheta_{{\alpha\beta}\delta}}{\delta g_{\mu\nu}}=0,\text{\ \ but\ \ }\frac{\delta\epsilon_{{\alpha\beta}\delta}}{\delta g_{\mu\nu}}\neq 0.

(44)

We can also derive

\displaystyle\frac{8}{|g|}=\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}\vartheta_{mnc}\vartheta_{def}\vartheta_{ghi}\vartheta_{jkl}G^{mnde}G^{cgfj}G^{hikl}}.

(45)

Plugging this into (
41
) allows to express
g^{ab}
as an expression that contains only
G^{wxyz}
(no indices down) and the Levi-Civita-
symbol

\vartheta_{abc}
which is independent of the metric as discussed above, thus providing an
explicit
solution for the metric in terms of the area metric.
Consequently, we can now derive the expressions for
\frac{\delta G^{mnrs}}{\delta g^{ab}}
and
\frac{\delta g^{ab}}{\delta G^{mnrs}}
which are necessary to translate between the EMT and the surface energy tensor.

From (
40
), we easily obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\delta G^{mnrs}}{\delta g^{ab}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\delta_{a}^{s}\delta_{b}^{n}g^{mr}+\delta_{a}^{n}\delta_{b}^{s}g^{mr}-\delta_{a}^{r}\delta_{b}^{n}g^{ms}-\delta_{a}^{n}\delta_{b}^{r}g^{ms}-\delta_{a}^{s}\delta_{b}^{m}g^{nr}-\delta_{a}^{m}\delta_{b}^{s}g^{nr}+\delta_{a}^{r}\delta_{b}^{m}g^{ns}+\delta_{a}^{m}\delta_{b}^{r}g^{ns}\right)

(46)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\overbracket{\delta_{a}^{s}\delta_{b}^{n}g^{mr}}^{([mn][rs])}

(47)

with the notation

\displaystyle\overbracket{X^{abcd}}^{([ab][cd])}\equiv

\displaystyle X^{abcd}-X^{abdc}-X^{bacd}+X^{badc}+X^{cdab}-X^{cdba}-X^{dcab}+X^{dcba},

(48)

\displaystyle\underbracket{X_{abcd}}_{([ab][cd])}\equiv

\displaystyle X_{abcd}-X_{abdc}-X_{bacd}+X_{badc}+X_{cdab}-X_{cdba}-X_{dcab}+X_{dcba}.

From (
41
), we can derive

\displaystyle\frac{\delta g^{ab}}{\delta G^{wxyz}}=-\frac{1}{8}\epsilon_{opq}\epsilon_{rst}\frac{\delta G^{aobr}}{\delta G^{wxyz}}G^{pqst}-\frac{1}{8}\epsilon_{opq}\epsilon_{rst}G^{aobr}\frac{\delta G^{pqst}}{\delta G^{wxyz}}-\vartheta_{opq}\vartheta_{rst}G^{aobr}G^{pqst}\frac{\delta|g|/8}{\delta G^{wxyz}}

(49)

The last term is the most challenging. Using (
45
),

\displaystyle\frac{\delta|g|/8}{\delta G^{wxyz}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta}{\delta G^{wxyz}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}\vartheta_{mnc}\vartheta_{def}\vartheta_{ghi}\vartheta_{jkl}G^{mnde}G^{cgfj}G^{hikl}}}

(50)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2(\sqrt{...})^{3}}\frac{\delta}{\delta G^{wxyz}}\left(\frac{2}{3}\vartheta_{mnc}\vartheta_{def}\vartheta_{ghi}\vartheta_{jkl}G^{mnde}G^{cgfj}G^{hikl}\right)

(51)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{|g|}{8}\right)^{3}\left(\frac{16}{|g|^{2}}g_{wy}g_{xz}-\frac{16}{|g|^{2}}g_{wz}g_{xy}\right)

(52)

\displaystyle=\frac{|g|}{64}\left(g_{wz}g_{xy}-g_{wy}g_{xz}\right)

(53)

Taking everything together, (
49
) reads

\displaystyle\frac{\delta g^{ab}}{\delta G^{wxyz}}=

(54)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{8}\left(\delta_{x}^{b}\delta_{z}^{a}g_{wy}+\delta_{x}^{a}\delta_{z}^{b}g_{wy}-\delta_{x}^{b}\delta_{y}^{a}g_{wz}-\delta_{x}^{a}\delta_{y}^{b}g_{wz}-\delta_{w}^{b}\delta_{z}^{a}g_{xy}-\delta_{w}^{a}\delta_{z}^{b}g_{xy}+\delta_{w}^{b}\delta_{y}^{a}g_{xz}+\delta_{w}^{a}\delta_{y}^{b}g_{xz}+g_{wz}g_{xy}g^{\alpha\beta}-g_{wy}g_{xz}g^{\alpha\beta}\right)

\displaystyle=\underbracket{\frac{1}{8}\delta_{x}^{b}\delta_{z}^{a}g_{wy}+\frac{1}{32}g_{wz}g_{xy}g^{ab}}_{([wx][yz])}.

We can perform some consistency checks on these results by verifying that

\displaystyle\frac{\delta G^{mnrs}}{\delta g^{ab}}\frac{\delta g^{cd}}{\delta G^{mnrs}}=\frac{\delta g^{cd}}{\delta g^{ab}}\ \ \text{ and }\ \ \frac{\delta G^{mnrs}}{\delta g^{ab}}\frac{\delta g^{ab}}{\delta G^{wxyz}}=\frac{\delta G^{mnrs}}{\delta G^{wxyz}}.

(55)

The latter depends on a dimensionally dependent identity valid in
d=3
.
As stated in the main text, we can now define not only an energy momentum tensor
T_{ab}
, but also the
surface energy tensor

T_{mnrs}
which encodes the same information. Specifically,
using (
47

T_{mnrs}

\displaystyle T_{ab}=T_{mnrs}\frac{\delta G^{mnrs}}{\delta g^{ab}}=4T_{a\ \ bm}^{\ m},

(56)

and likewise

\displaystyle T_{wxyz}=T_{ab}\frac{\delta g^{ab}}{\delta G^{wxyz}}=\frac{1}{4}g_{xz}T_{wy}-\frac{1}{4}g_{xy}T_{wz}-\frac{1}{4}g_{wz}T_{xy}+\frac{1}{4}g_{wy}T_{xz}+\frac{1}{8}g_{wz}g_{xy}T^{c}_{c}-\frac{1}{8}g_{wy}g_{xz}T^{c}_{c}

(57)

In flat space (with
g_{ab}=\eta_{ab}
) for example, we find for the energy measured by a stationary observer

\displaystyle T_{00}=4(T_{0101}+T_{0202}).

(58)

So
T_{mnrs}
might be interpreted as a tensor that measures the flow of energy within a given surface spanned by a bivector, and hence the energy flow in a certain direction is calculated by an equation like (
58
) where we use bivectors with one direction along time and the other perpendicular to it, summing over orthonormal bivectors. Note that (
56
) implies

\displaystyle T^{a}_{a}=4T^{am}_{\ \ \ \ am}=2G^{mnrs}T_{mnrs},

(59)

giving a very straightforward way to check for conformal invariance. Also, conservation of energy momentum implies

\displaystyle\nabla_{a}T^{ab}=0\Leftrightarrow\nabla_{a}T^{a\ bm}_{\ m}=g_{nm}\nabla_{a}T^{anbm}=0.

(60)

Appendix B
Holographic dictionary for the boundary EMT

For the sake of clarity, let us first briefly discuss (following e.g.
Schuller and Wohlfarth 2006b
;
Ho and Inami 2016
) the
0
-th order case,

\displaystyle G_{{\mu\nu}{\alpha\beta}}

\displaystyle=G^{(g)}_{{\mu\nu}{\alpha\beta}}=g_{\mu\alpha}g_{\nu\beta}-g_{\nu\alpha}g_{\mu\beta}.

(61)

By the defining property of area metrics (
2
), the area of a codimension-2 surface
\Sigma
, parametrized by the embedding coordinates
X^{\rho}(\vec{x})
in the
AdS_{4}
background, is given by

\displaystyle A[\Sigma]\equiv\int_{\Sigma}d^{2}x\ \sqrt{\frac{1}{4}\vartheta^{IJ}\ \hat{g}_{IJKL}(\vec{x})\ \vartheta^{KL}},

(62)

where
\hat{g}_{IJKL}
is the induced area metric on
\Sigma
:

\displaystyle\hat{g}_{IJKL}(\vec{x})

\displaystyle=G_{{\mu\nu}{\alpha\beta}}(X^{\rho})\ \partial_{I}X^{\mu}\partial_{J}X^{\nu}\partial_{K}X^{\alpha}\partial_{L}X^{\beta},

(63)

and
d^{2}x=dx\wedge dy
.
Interestingly, due to the symmetries of
\hat{g}_{IJKL}
(which are identical to those of
G_{{\mu\nu}{\alpha\beta}}
), only a single independent component,
\hat{g}_{1212}
, survives in two dimensions.
Since the background is assumed to have a length metric description for now, this is precisely the determinant of the induced metric
\left(\hat{g}_{IJ}(\vec{x})=g_{{\mu\nu}}(X^{\rho})\partial_{I}X^{\mu}\partial_{J}X^{\nu}\right)
on
\Sigma
,

\displaystyle\det(\hat{g}_{IJ})=\hat{g}_{11}\hat{g}_{22}-\hat{g}_{12}\hat{g}_{21}:=\hat{g}\equiv\hat{g}_{1212}.

(64)

Therefore, in the metric-induced case, the two prescriptions for computing the area of a surface coincide:

\displaystyle A[\Sigma]=\int_{\Sigma}d^{2}x\ \sqrt{\frac{1}{4}\vartheta^{IJ}\ \hat{g}_{IJKL}(\vec{x})\ \vartheta^{KL}}=\int_{\Sigma}d^{2}x\ \sqrt{\hat{g}}.

(65)

However, adopting the area element prescription based on a general (not necessarily metric-induced) area metric tensor (
2
) allows one to explore more general fluctuations of the background spacetime.

Going forward, following (
6
) and (
8
), we will consider variations in the area of
\Sigma
corresponding to cases when the length metric fluctuations of the bulk are still turned off
(h_{{\mu\nu}}=0)
but purely area metric perturbations are allowed:

\displaystyle G_{{\mu\nu}{\alpha\beta}}

\displaystyle=G^{(g)}_{{\mu\nu}{\alpha\beta}}+w_{{\mu\nu}{\alpha\beta}}\,,\ \ (w\ll 1).

(66)

Due to the perturbation of the bulk, we find

\displaystyle A[\Sigma]

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma}d^{2}x\ \sqrt{\hat{g}+\frac{1}{4}\vartheta^{IJ}w_{{\mu\nu}{\alpha\beta}}(X^{\rho})\ \partial_{I}X^{\mu}\partial_{J}X^{\nu}\partial_{K}X^{\alpha}\partial_{L}X^{\beta}\vartheta^{KL}}

(67)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma}d^{2}x\ \sqrt{\hat{g}}\left(1+\frac{1}{8}\frac{\vartheta^{IJ}w_{{\mu\nu}{\alpha\beta}}(X^{\rho})\ \partial_{I}X^{\mu}\partial_{J}X^{\nu}\partial_{K}X^{\alpha}\partial_{L}X^{\beta}\vartheta^{KL}}{\hat{g}}+...\right).

(68)

Therefore, for leading order area variations
\delta A
, we get

\displaystyle\delta A=\int_{\Sigma}d^{2}x\ \left(\frac{1}{8}\frac{\vartheta^{IJ}w_{{\mu\nu}{\alpha\beta}}(X^{\rho})\ \partial_{I}X^{\mu}\partial_{J}X^{\nu}\partial_{K}X^{\alpha}\partial_{L}X^{\beta}\vartheta^{KL}}{\sqrt{\hat{g}}}\right).

(69)

Due to the properties of the Levi-Civita symbol
\vartheta^{IJ}
, the area variation simplifies to:

\displaystyle\delta A=\int_{\Sigma}d^{2}x\ \left(\frac{1}{2}\frac{w_{{\mu\nu}{\alpha\beta}}(X^{\rho})\ \partial_{1}X^{\mu}\partial_{2}X^{\nu}\partial_{1}X^{\alpha}\partial_{2}X^{\beta}}{\sqrt{\hat{g}}}\right).

(70)

In the expression above, and in what follows, we mostly denote tensor components and derivatives using the indices
0,1,2,3
. These should be understood as referring to the coordinates in the order
(t,x,y,z)=(0,1,2,3)
.

For the ball shaped spatial entangling subregions centred at
\vec{x}_{0}
(where
\vec{x}_{0}=x^{I}_{0}=(x_{0},y_{0})
with
I\in\{1,2\}

\Sigma
lies on a constant time slice

\displaystyle z(x,y)=\sqrt{R^{2}-|\vec{x}-\vec{x}_{0}|^{2}},

(71)

such that the only non-vanishing contributions to (

\displaystyle\partial_{1}X^{1}

\displaystyle\equiv\partial_{1}x=1,\,\partial_{1}X^{3}\equiv\partial_{1}z=-\frac{(x-x_{0})}{\sqrt{R^{2}-|\vec{x}-\vec{x}_{0}|^{2}}},

(72)

\displaystyle\partial_{2}X^{2}

These simplifications and the symmetries of

\hat{g}=L^{4}R^{2}/z(x,y)^{6}

(75)

In the next step, we write
w_{{\mu\nu}\rho\lambda}

\displaystyle w_{\mu\nu\rho\lambda}(z,t_{0},\vec{x})\equiv\frac{1}{z}W_{{\mu\nu}\rho\lambda}(z,t_{0},\vec{x})=\frac{1}{z}\sum_{n=0}z^{n}W_{\mu\nu\rho\lambda}^{(n)}(t_{0},\vec{x}).

In this expression, the factor of

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma}\frac{d^{2}x}{2L^{2}R}z^{3}\left[\frac{1}{z}\left(W^{(0)}_{1212}(t_{0},\vec{x}_{0})+W^{(0)}_{1313}(t_{0},\vec{x}_{0})\frac{(y-y_{0})^{2}}{R^{2}-|\vec{x}-\vec{x}_{0}|^{2}}+W^{(0)}_{3232}(t_{0},\vec{x}_{0})\frac{(x-x_{0})^{2}}{R^{2}-|\vec{x}-\vec{x}_{0}|^{2}}\right)+\mathcal{O}(z^{0})+\cdots\right]

Appendix C
Equations of motion

In this section, we derive the equations of motion for area metric perturbations starting from the first law of entanglement, following the approach of
Lashkari et al. 2014
. Firstly, we consider the case when the length metric part of the full area metric is turned off (
66
). For a ball-shaped region of finite radius
R
, we can calculate the variation in its entanglement entropy using (
B
) as follows,

\displaystyle\delta S_{A(R,0,0)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8L^{2}G_{N}R}\int_{\Sigma}d^{2}x\;z^{3}\Bigg(w_{1212}(z,t_{0},\vec{x})+\frac{y^{2}}{z^{2}}w_{1313}(z,t_{0},\vec{x})+\frac{x^{2}}{z^{2}}w_{3232}(z,t_{0},\vec{x})

\displaystyle-\frac{2y}{z}w_{1213}(z,t_{0},\vec{x})-\frac{2x}{z}w_{3212}(z,t_{0},\vec{x})+\frac{2xy}{z^{2}}w_{3213}(z,t_{0},\vec{x})\Bigg).

(92)

Here, we have chosen the centre of the ball at
(t_{0},0,0)
without any loss of generality. Interestingly, in the finite ball analysis, all six integrands will contribute to the calculation of area because the region of integration is now large and finite, and the fluctuations
w_{{\mu\nu}\rho\lambda}
have an arbitrary dependence on
x

Appendix D
Lagrangian formulation

D.1
Role of tracelessness and the need for Lagrange multipliers

Before deriving area metric equations by taking the variation of the action with respect to
w_{{\alpha\beta}{\mu\nu}}
, it will be instructive to consider the following simple toymodel: Let’s say we have a tensor field
\phi_{\mu\nu}
which is required to be symmetric
and traceless
, and described by a Lagrangian
\mathcal{L}_{\phi}
containing at most first derivatives of
\phi
. Setting the variation of the action to zero, we find (ignoring boundary terms)

\displaystyle 0\equiv\delta\mathcal{S}

\displaystyle=\int dV\delta\mathcal{L}_{\phi}

(116)

\displaystyle=\int dV\left(\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}_{\phi}}{\partial\phi_{{\alpha\beta}}}-\partial_{\gamma}\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}_{\phi}}{\partial(\partial_{\gamma}\phi_{{\alpha\beta}})}\right)\delta\phi^{\alpha\beta}.

(117)

From this we can read off what we will from now on call the ”naive” Euler-Lagrange equation

\displaystyle 0=\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}_{\phi}}{\partial\phi_{{\alpha\beta}}}-\partial_{\gamma}\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}_{\phi}}{\partial(\partial_{\gamma}\phi_{{\alpha\beta}})}\equiv X_{\alpha\beta}.

(118)

This naive equation is however
not
a necessary condition for (
116
): As we impose
\phi
to be traceless, we should only consider variations of the field that also satisfy
\delta\phi^{\mu}_{\mu}=0
, and so a necessary condition for (
116
) is merely

\displaystyle X_{\alpha\beta}=Yg_{\alpha\beta}

(119)

with any arbitrary function
Y
. To treat this issue more formally, we add a Langrange-multiplier
\lambda
to the Lagrangian enforcing the trace condition explicitly:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{\prime}_{\phi}=\mathcal{L}_{\phi}+\lambda g_{\mu\nu}\phi^{\mu\nu}

(120)

Now the Euler-Lagrange equation for the field
\lambda
enforces the holonomic constraint
\phi^{\mu}_{\mu}=0
, and the Euler-Lagrange equation for
\phi_{\alpha\beta}
reads

\displaystyle X_{\alpha\beta}+\lambda g_{{\alpha\beta}}=0

(121)

which is equivalent to our heuristic result (
119
). Solving (
121
) for
\lambda
, we simply take the trace to find

4\lambda\equiv-X^{\mu}_{\mu},

and (
121
) takes the form

\displaystyle X_{\alpha\beta}-\frac{X^{\rho}_{\rho}}{4}g_{\alpha\beta}=0.

(122)

In essence, we have found that the true physical equation of motion (
122
) is only the traceless part of the naive equation of motion (
118
).

With this in mind, we now leave the above toymodel behind and return to considering the purely area metric fluctuation
w_{{\alpha\beta}{\mu\nu}}
, its action and its equations of motion. Here, we actually have to enforce two non-trivial constraints, namely tracelessness and cyclicity. To do so, we add two Lagrangian multiplier field terms to the Lagrangian (
34
),

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{\prime}=\mathcal{L}+\lambda^{\alpha\beta}g^{\mu\nu}w_{\alpha\mu\beta\nu}+\kappa\epsilon^{\alpha\mu\beta\nu}w_{\alpha\mu\beta\nu},

(123)

with
\lambda^{{\alpha\beta}}=\lambda^{{\beta\alpha}}
. Variation with respect to
\kappa
enforces the cyclicity condition (second part of (
9
)), while variation with respect to
\lambda^{\alpha\beta}
enforces tracelessness (first part of (
9
)). Variation with respect to
w_{\alpha\mu\beta\nu}
then gives equations of motion of the form

\displaystyle 0\equiv X_{\alpha\mu\beta\nu}+\frac{1}{4}g_{\mu\nu}\lambda_{\alpha\beta}-\frac{1}{4}g_{\mu\beta}\lambda_{\alpha\nu}-\frac{1}{4}g_{\alpha\nu}\lambda_{\mu\beta}+\frac{1}{4}g_{\alpha\beta}\lambda_{\mu\nu}+\epsilon_{\alpha\mu\beta\nu}\kappa

(124)

where
X_{\alpha\mu\beta\nu}
stands again for the ”naive” Euler-Lagrange equations derived from
\mathcal{L}
,

\displaystyle X_{\alpha\mu\beta\nu}\equiv\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial w_{\alpha\mu\beta\nu}}-\partial_{\gamma}\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_{\gamma}w_{\alpha\mu\beta\nu})}.

(125)

As in the toy model above, we now have to solve for
\lambda_{{\alpha\beta}}
and
\kappa
. Taking both traces of (
124
), thanks to
\epsilon_{abcd}g^{bd}=0
, we get

\displaystyle 0\equiv X^{\alpha\mu}_{\ \ \alpha\mu}+\frac{3}{2}\lambda^{\alpha}_{\alpha}.

(126)

Taking one trace of (
124
) and using (
126
), we find

\displaystyle 0\equiv 2X^{\ \alpha}_{\mu\ \nu\alpha}-\frac{1}{3}g_{\mu\nu}X^{\alpha\beta}_{\ \ \alpha\beta}+\lambda_{\mu\nu}

(127)

which can be solved for
\lambda_{\mu\nu}
. Likewise, contracting (
124
) with
\epsilon^{\alpha\mu\beta\nu}
we get

\displaystyle 0\equiv-\epsilon^{\alpha\mu\beta\nu}X_{\alpha\mu\beta\nu}+24\kappa

(128)

which can be solved for
\kappa
. With these expressions at hand, the equation of motion (
124
) finally reads

\displaystyle 0\equiv

\displaystyle X_{\alpha\mu\beta\nu}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}X^{\ \gamma}_{\alpha\ \beta\gamma}+\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\beta}X^{\ \gamma}_{\alpha\ \nu\gamma}+\frac{1}{2}g_{\alpha\nu}X^{\ \gamma}_{\mu\ \beta\gamma}-\frac{1}{2}g_{\alpha\beta}X^{\ \gamma}_{\mu\ \nu\gamma}-\frac{1}{6}g_{\alpha\nu}g_{\mu\beta}X^{\gamma\delta}_{\ \ \gamma\delta}+\frac{1}{6}g_{\alpha\beta}g_{\mu\nu}X^{\gamma\delta}_{\ \ \gamma\delta}

(129)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{24}\epsilon_{\alpha\mu\beta\nu}\epsilon^{\gamma\delta\rho\sigma}X_{\gamma\delta\rho\sigma}.

This expression relates to the naive expression
X_{\alpha\mu\beta\nu}
like the Weyl tensor relates to the Riemann tensor, i.e. it is only the traceless part of the original expression (in addition to the fact that any potential non-cyclic part of
X_{\alpha\mu\beta\nu}
is explicitly removed).

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{EH}[h]

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4}\tilde{h}^{\mu\nu}\Box h_{{\mu\nu}}-\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{\rho}\tilde{h}_{\rho\mu}\nabla^{\sigma}\tilde{h}_{\sigma}^{\mu}+\frac{\Lambda}{6}\tilde{h}^{\mu\nu}h_{\mu\nu}+\frac{\Lambda}{6}h^{2}\,\left(\text{with}\,\,\tilde{h}_{\mu\nu}=h_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}g_{{\mu\nu}}h\right),

(131)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{h\text{-}w}[h,w]

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}h^{{\alpha\beta}}\nabla^{\mu}\nabla^{\nu}w_{\alpha\mu\beta\nu},

(132)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{w^{2}}[w]

\displaystyle=-m^{2}w_{\alpha\mu\beta\nu}w^{\alpha\mu\beta\nu}-K_{1}(\nabla_{\delta}w_{\alpha\mu\beta\nu})(\nabla^{\delta}w^{\alpha\mu\beta\nu})-K_{2}(\nabla^{\alpha}w_{\alpha\mu\beta\nu})(\nabla_{\delta}w^{\delta\mu\beta\nu}),

(133)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{LM}[w,\lambda,\kappa]

\displaystyle=\lambda^{\alpha\beta}g^{\mu\nu}w_{\alpha\mu\beta\nu}+\kappa\epsilon^{\alpha\mu\beta\nu}w_{\alpha\mu\beta\nu}.

(134)

The terms in the Lagrangian are, in this order, the Einstein-Hilbert term linearised around the AdS background
Christensen and Duff 1980
, a coupling term between
h
and
w
, the terms quadratic in
w
(including a mass term and kinetic terms), and the Lagrangian multiplier terms. Similar actions were considered before in
Borissova et al. 2024
;
Borissova et al. 2025
(however in this paper we do not split the purely area metric fluctuation
w_{{\alpha\beta}{\mu\nu}}
into self- and anti-selfdual parts, and we do not consider terms in the action that include the Levi-Civita Tensor). Also, the term proportional to
K_{2}
, which is a type of kinetic term but with the indices contracted in an unusual way, was not considered in earlier papers to our knowledge, but we include it anyway out of interest. Further, we ignore any possible boundary terms.

Taking the variation of (
130
) with respect to
h^{\mu\nu}
reproduces (
33
) from the main text. Taking the variation with respect to
w^{\alpha\mu\beta\nu}
will give a second, rank-4 tensorial equation of motion which we now derive. Firstly, from the variation of the terms in
\mathcal{L}_{w^{2}}[w]
we obtain the ”naive” expression (as defined in the preceding section)

\displaystyle X_{\alpha\mu\beta\nu}=-2m^{2}w_{\alpha\mu\beta\nu}+2K_{1}\nabla_{\mu}\nabla^{\mu}w_{\alpha\mu\beta\nu}+\frac{K_{2}}{4}\underbracket{\nabla_{\nu}\nabla_{\delta}w_{\alpha\mu\beta}^{\ \ \ \ \delta}}_{([\alpha\mu][\beta\nu])}.

(135)

where the notation defined in (
48
) was used again. It is interesting to note that for this specific expression,
\epsilon^{\gamma\delta\rho\sigma}X_{\gamma\delta\rho\sigma}=0=X^{\gamma\delta}_{\ \ \gamma\delta}
and only the term proportional to
K_{2}
is not automatically traceless, hence the terms arising from integrating out the Lagrange multipliers (as was done in (
129
)) are going to be relatively simple. Taking the variation of the term
\mathcal{L}_{h\text{-}w}
with respect to
w^{\alpha\mu\beta\nu}
gives the expression

\displaystyle X_{\alpha\mu\beta\nu}=-\frac{1}{16}\underbracket{\nabla_{\alpha}\nabla_{\beta}h_{\mu\nu}}_{([\alpha\mu][\beta\nu])}.

(136)

Upon integrating out the Lagrange-multipliers, the physical equation of motion will only depend on the traceless cyclic part of this expression (see again the derivation of (
129
)). This expression is rather ugly when derived and written down in a brute-force manner, however we can make the following observations: The resulting expression should

•

be linear in
h_{\mu\nu}
and depend on second order derivatives of it,

•

respect the gauge symmetry, i.e. vanish for
h_{\mu\nu}=\nabla_{\mu}\xi_{\nu}+\nabla_{\nu}\xi_{\mu}
(notably, the expression in (
136
) by itself is not gauge invariant on an AdS background, where covariant derivatives do not commute in general),

•

be a rank-4 tensor with the same index-symmetries as an area metric,

•

and be traceless and cyclic.

These conditions together suggest that the resulting expression should be proportional to
\delta^{(h)}_{1}C_{\alpha\mu\beta\nu}
, the first order change of the bulk Weyl-tensor due to the metric fluctuation
h_{\mu\nu}
, and only the prefactor remains to be determined. This also implies that, ignoring boundary terms, the coupling term between
h
and
w
can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{h\text{-}w}[h,w]

\displaystyle\propto\left(\delta^{(h)}_{1}C^{\alpha\mu\beta\nu}\right)w_{\alpha\mu\beta\nu}.

(137)

Determining the correct proportionality factor requires the appropriate sorting of covariant derivatives using the Ricci identity to commute them, and the explicit expression of the Riemann tensor of the background AdS metric in terms of the metric tensor.

All in all, after integrating out the Lagrange multipliers, the two physical equations of motion read

\displaystyle\frac{\delta\mathcal{S}}{\delta h^{\mu\nu}}=0\Rightarrow 0

\displaystyle={E_{\mu\nu}}{\ \ -\ \ \frac{1}{2}\nabla^{\rho}\nabla^{\lambda}w_{\mu\rho\nu\lambda}},

(138)

\displaystyle\frac{\delta\mathcal{S}}{\delta w^{\alpha\mu\beta\nu}}=0\Rightarrow 0

\displaystyle={\frac{1}{4}\delta^{(h)}_{1}C_{\alpha\mu\beta\nu}}-2m^{2}w_{\alpha\mu\beta\nu}+2K_{1}\nabla_{\rho}\nabla^{\rho}w_{\alpha\mu\beta\nu}+\frac{K_{2}}{4}\underbracket{\nabla_{\nu}\nabla_{\delta}w_{\alpha\mu\beta}^{\ \ \ \ \delta}}_{([\alpha\mu][\beta\nu])}-\frac{K_{2}}{4}\underbracket{g_{\mu\nu}\nabla^{\gamma}\nabla^{\delta}w_{\alpha\gamma\beta\delta}}_{([\alpha\mu][\beta\nu])}.

(139)

D.3
Solutions

We leave a thorough investigation of the solutions of (
138
) and (
139
) for the future. However, as indicated in the main text, carrying out the holographic renormalisation procedure and demanding consistency of the holographic dictionary derived from it with the result (
25
) derived from the first law of entanglement may allow us to constrain the arbitrary parameters
\{m^{2},K_{1},K_{2}\}
in (

). The very first step of this procedure requires us to identify the normalisable and non-normalisable modes of the fields of interest.
For
h_{\mu\nu}
these are well understood
de Haro et al. 2001
;
Skenderis 2002
, and for

our results (
23
) and (

) indicate that the mode encoding the dual vacuum expectation value (vev
\leftrightarrow
normalisable mode) should behave asymptotically as
w\sim 1/z
. A simple scaling argument then reveals that a corresponding non-normalisable mode should behave as
w\sim 1/z^{4}
.
Alternatively, we could deduce this from the fact that the background (AdS) metric
g_{\mu\nu}
goes as
\sim z^{-2}
near the boundary (not coincidentially like the non-normalisable mode for
h
), while the background area metric (
5
) approaches the boundary like
\sim 1/z^{4}
.
We summarise this in the following table:

normalisable mode

non-normalisable mode

h_{\mu\nu}

\sim z

\sim z^{-2}

w_{{\mu\nu}{\alpha\beta}}

\sim z^{-1}

\sim z^{-4}

Consistency between holographic renormalisation and first law of entanglement then requires first and foremost that the equations (
138
) and (
139
) even allow for solutions corresponding to (some of the) types indicated in the table above.

Unfortunately, the equations (
138
) and (
139
) are a system of coupled equations for
h_{\mu\nu}
and
w_{{\alpha\beta}{\mu\nu}}
, and so their general solutions are hard to discuss, even under the simplifying assumptions that both
h_{\mu\nu}
and
w_{{\alpha\beta}{\mu\nu}}
are functions of
z
only, and that
h_{\mu\nu}
is given in Fefferman-Graham gauge
h_{\mu z}=0
.
We thus make one more simplifying assumption: Instead of searching for the generic solutions of the system, we only look at the subset of solutions for which the
h
- and
w-
dependent terms in (
138
) and (
139
) vanish individually instead of merely cancelling. I.e. we look at the system of equations

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=E_{\mu\nu}

(140)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\delta^{(h)}_{1}C_{\alpha\mu\beta\nu}

(141)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\nabla^{\rho}\nabla^{\lambda}w_{\mu\rho\nu\lambda}

(142)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=-2m^{2}w_{\alpha\mu\beta\nu}+2K_{1}\nabla_{\rho}\nabla^{\rho}w_{\alpha\mu\beta\nu}+\frac{K_{2}}{4}\underbracket{\nabla_{\nu}\nabla_{\delta}w_{\alpha\mu\beta}^{\ \ \ \ \delta}}_{([\alpha\mu][\beta\nu])}-\frac{K_{2}}{4}\underbracket{g_{\mu\nu}\nabla^{\gamma}\nabla^{\delta}w_{\alpha\gamma\beta\delta}}_{([\alpha\mu][\beta\nu])}.

Our finding is that equations (

) constrains

while some others remain unconstrained. In contrast, (

\displaystyle w_{{\alpha\beta}{\gamma\delta}}=a_{{\alpha\beta}{\gamma\delta}}\ z^{-\frac{5}{2}-\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{132K_{1}+9K_{2}+16L^{2}m^{2}}{4K_{1}+K_{2}}}}+b_{{\alpha\beta}{\gamma\delta}}\ z^{-\frac{5}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{132K_{1}+9K_{2}+16L^{2}m^{2}}{4K_{1}+K_{2}}}}.

Appendix E
Lanczos potentials

E.1
Definition

As stated in the main text, we can follow
Bampi and Caviglia 1983
to write the purely area metric fluctuation
w_{\alpha\mu\beta\nu}
in terms of a
Lanczos potential

\displaystyle\ell_{{\alpha\beta}\gamma}=-\ell_{{\beta\alpha}\gamma},\ \ell_{[{\alpha\beta}\gamma]}=0,

(146)

as

\displaystyle w_{{\alpha\beta}{\gamma\delta}}=

\displaystyle\underbracket{\nabla_{\delta}\ell_{{\alpha\beta}\gamma}}_{([{\alpha\beta}][{\gamma\delta}])}+\frac{1}{3}\nabla_{\mu}\ell^{\nu\mu}_{\ \ \nu}\underbracket{g_{\alpha\gamma}g_{\beta\delta}}_{([{\alpha\beta}][{\gamma\delta}])}-\frac{1}{2}\underbracket{g_{\alpha\gamma}\left(\nabla_{\mu}\ell_{\beta\ \delta}^{\ \mu}+\nabla_{\mu}\ell_{\delta\ \beta}^{\ \mu}-\nabla_{\delta}\ell_{\beta\ \mu}^{\ \mu}-\nabla_{\beta}\ell_{\delta\ \mu}^{\ \mu}\right)}_{([{\alpha\beta}][{\gamma\delta}])}

(147)

Note that some factors in this equation are different from the paper
Bampi and Caviglia 1983
, but we think they are correct this way. This can be verified by checking the tracelessness of
w_{{\alpha\beta}{\gamma\delta}}
with this ansatz.

E.2
Lagrange multipliers

As shown in
Bampi and Caviglia 1983
, the cyclicity condition of the Lanczos potential,

\displaystyle\ell_{[{\alpha\beta}\gamma]}=0\Leftrightarrow\epsilon^{{\alpha\beta}\gamma\delta}\ell_{{\alpha\beta}\gamma}=0,

(148)

is related to the cyclicity condition (
9
) of
w_{{\alpha\beta}{\gamma\delta}}
. Just as in section
D.1
, we will make sure to enforce this condition by an explicit Lagrange multiplier term in the Lagrangian:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{\prime}=\mathcal{L}+\epsilon^{{\alpha\beta}\gamma\delta}\ell_{{\alpha\beta}\gamma}\lambda_{\delta}.

(149)

So if the ”naive equations of motion” (derived from
\mathcal{L}
by variation w.r.t.
\ell_{{\alpha\beta}\gamma}
) are
Y_{{\alpha\beta}\gamma}
, we get from (
149
) the physical equations of motion

\displaystyle 0\equiv Y_{{\alpha\beta}\gamma}+\epsilon_{{\alpha\beta}\gamma\delta}\lambda^{\delta}

(150)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\

\displaystyle 0\equiv Y_{{\alpha\beta}\gamma}\epsilon^{{\alpha\beta}\gamma\rho}-6\lambda^{\rho}

(151)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\

\displaystyle 0\equiv Y_{\mu\nu\sigma}+\frac{1}{6}\epsilon_{\mu\nu\sigma\delta}\epsilon^{{\alpha\beta}\gamma\delta}Y_{{\alpha\beta}\gamma}=\frac{2}{3}Y_{\mu\nu\sigma}+\frac{1}{3}Y_{\mu\nu\sigma}-\frac{1}{3}Y_{\mu\nu\sigma}.

(152)

E.3
Equations of motion

When treating the Lanczos potential as the fundamental variable, we consider actions like

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{EH}[h]+\mathcal{L}_{h\text{-}w}[h,\ell]+\mathcal{L}_{\ell^{2}}[\ell]+\mathcal{L}_{LM}[\ell,\lambda]

(153)

with

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{EH}[h]

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4}\tilde{h}^{\mu\nu}\Box h_{{\mu\nu}}-\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{\rho}\tilde{h}_{\rho\mu}\nabla^{\sigma}\tilde{h}_{\sigma}^{\mu}+\frac{\Lambda}{6}\tilde{h}^{\mu\nu}h_{\mu\nu}+\frac{\Lambda}{6}h^{2}\,\left(\text{with}\,\,\tilde{h}_{\mu\nu}=h_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}g_{{\mu\nu}}h\right),

(154)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{h\text{-}w}[h,\ell]

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}h^{{\alpha\beta}}\nabla^{\mu}\nabla^{\nu}w_{\alpha\mu\beta\nu},

(155)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\ell^{2}}[\ell]

\displaystyle=-K_{3}w_{\alpha\mu\beta\nu}w^{\alpha\mu\beta\nu}

(156)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{LM}[\ell,\lambda]

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{{\alpha\beta}\gamma\delta}\ell_{{\alpha\beta}\gamma}\lambda_{\delta}.

(157)

Here, wherever
w_{{\alpha\beta}{\mu\nu}}
appears, it is understood to be expressed in terms of
\ell_{{\alpha\beta}\gamma}
via (
147
). Consequently, a term like
w_{\alpha\mu\beta\nu}w^{\alpha\mu\beta\nu}
which used to be a mass term in section
D
is now a kinetic term for
\ell_{{\alpha\beta}\gamma}
, and we do not consider terms like
(\nabla_{\delta}w_{\alpha\mu\beta\nu})(\nabla^{\delta}w^{\alpha\mu\beta\nu})
as they would correspond to fourth-order derivative terms for
\ell_{{\alpha\beta}\gamma}
. We could have added a mass-term
m^{2}\ell_{{\alpha\beta}\gamma}\ell^{{\alpha\beta}\gamma}
for the Lanczos potential, but we do not do this here for the sake of simplicity.

Taking the variation of (
153
) with respect to
h^{\mu\nu}
still reproduces (
33
) from the main text. Taking the variation with respect to
\ell^{{\alpha\beta}\gamma}
will give a second, rank-3 tensorial equation of motion which we now derive. Firstly, from the variation of
\mathcal{L}_{\ell^{2}}[\ell]
we obtain the ”naive” expression (as defined in the preceding section)

\displaystyle Y_{\alpha\beta\gamma}=16K_{3}\nabla^{\delta}w_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}.

(158)

Due to the cyclicity condition of
w_{{\alpha\beta}{\mu\nu}}
, we have
\epsilon^{{\alpha\beta}\gamma\delta}Y_{{\alpha\beta}\gamma}=0
and thus no difference between naive and physical equations arising from this term. Taking the variation of the term
\mathcal{L}_{h\text{-}w}
with respect to
\ell^{{\alpha\beta}\gamma}
gives an expression which is too cumbersome to state here, and which following the main text we will explicitly refer to as
F_{\alpha\beta\gamma}
instead of
Y_{\alpha\beta\gamma}
from now on.
The following observations can be stated:
F_{\alpha\beta\gamma}

•

is linear in
h_{\mu\nu}
and depends on third order derivatives of it,

•

is a rank-3 tensor with the same index-symmetries as a Lanczos potential,

•

is traceless (
g^{\alpha\gamma}F_{{\alpha\beta}\gamma}=0
)

•

and cyclic (
\epsilon^{{\alpha\beta}\gamma\delta}F_{{\alpha\beta}\gamma}=0
).

The last fact means that for this term again, the Lagrange multiplier wasn’t explicitly needed and no difference between ”naive” and physical equations of motions arises.
These conditions are not stringent enough to suggest a unique answer, e.g.
\nabla^{\nu}\delta^{(h)}_{1}C_{\alpha\beta\gamma\nu}
would have been a possible candidate. Instead, as indicated in the main text, a lengthy calculation reveals that this can be written in terms of derivatives of the Einstein equations:

\displaystyle F_{{\alpha\beta}\gamma}\equiv\frac{1}{3}g_{\alpha\gamma}\nabla_{\beta}E^{\mu}_{\mu}-\frac{1}{3}g_{\beta\gamma}\nabla_{\alpha}E^{\mu}_{\mu}-\nabla_{\beta}E_{\alpha\gamma}+\nabla_{\alpha}E_{\beta\gamma},

(159)

Proving this requires again the appropriate sorting of covariant derivatives using the Ricci identity to commute them, and the explicit expression of the Riemann tensor of the background AdS metric in terms of the metric tensor.
All in all, the equations of motion resulting from (
153
) read

\displaystyle\frac{\delta\mathcal{S}}{\delta h^{\mu\nu}}=0\Rightarrow 0

\displaystyle={E_{\mu\nu}}{\ \ -\ \ \frac{1}{2}\nabla^{\rho}\nabla^{\lambda}w_{\mu\rho\nu\lambda}},

(160)

\displaystyle\frac{\delta\mathcal{S}}{\delta\ell^{{\alpha\beta}\gamma}}=0\Rightarrow 0

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}g_{\alpha\gamma}\nabla_{\beta}E^{\mu}_{\mu}-\frac{1}{3}g_{\beta\gamma}\nabla_{\alpha}E^{\mu}_{\mu}-\nabla_{\beta}E_{\alpha\gamma}+\nabla_{\alpha}E_{\beta\gamma}+16K_{3}\nabla^{\delta}w_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}.

(161)

These equations are interesting, because in the ”Einsteinian” limit
w\rightarrow 0
(where the geometry reduces to the ordinary length-metric geometry of Einsteinian gravity), we are left with

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle={E_{\mu\nu}},

(162)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}g_{\alpha\gamma}\nabla_{\beta}E^{\mu}_{\mu}-\frac{1}{3}g_{\beta\gamma}\nabla_{\alpha}E^{\mu}_{\mu}-\nabla_{\beta}E_{\alpha\gamma}+\nabla_{\alpha}E_{\beta\gamma},

(163)

and thus, the second equation being redundant, we re-obtain not only Einsteinian geometry, but (linearised) Einsteinian gravity in this limit. This was not the case when treating
w
as the fundamental variable in section
D
.

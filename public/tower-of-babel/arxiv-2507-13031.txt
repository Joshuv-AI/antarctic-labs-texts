Dense Subgroups of Prescribed Density
Dekui Peng

Abstract
. Let G G be an infinite compact group. We prove that for every cardinal κ \kappa between the density and the weight of G G , there exists a dense subgroup of G G of density κ \kappa .



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
22C05, 54A25

1.
Introduction

In
[
8
]
, the authors introduced the notion of the
double density spectrum

{\mathrm{dd}}(X)
for a topological space
X
, defined by

{\mathrm{dd}}(X):=\{\,d(Y):Y\subseteq X\text{ is a dense subspace}\,\}.

In a subsequent study
[
7
]
, Juhász and van Mill established that
{\mathrm{dd}}(X)=[d(X),{\mathrm{w}}(X)]
whenever
X
is homeomorphic to a locally compact group.

When working with topological groups, it is natural to focus on dense
subgroups
rather than arbitrary dense subspaces. This consideration leads to a refinement of the above notion:

Definition 1.1
.

Let
G
be a topological group. The
density spectrum of dense subgroups
of
G
is defined as

{\mathrm{dd}}^{*}(G):=\{\,d(H):H\leq G\text{ is a dense subgroup}\,\}.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
X
be a Tychonoff space. We say that
X
has the
property
\mathbf{JM}
if

{\mathrm{dd}}(X)=[d(X),w(X)].

If
X
is a topological group, we say that it has the
property
\mathbf{JM}^{*}
if

{\mathrm{dd}}^{*}(X)=[d(X),w(X)].

Thus, the result of Juhász and van Mill in
[
7
]
implies that every locally compact group satisfies property
\mathbf{JM}
. In this paper, we turn our attention to the stronger property
\mathbf{JM}^{*}
.

It was shown in
[
9
]
that there exists a locally compact group—either abelian or connected—whose dense subgroups are all separable, even though the group itself is not metrizable. In particular, such a group does not satisfy property
\mathbf{JM}^{*}
. On the other hand, the same paper proves that
w(G)\in{\mathrm{dd}}^{*}(G)
for every locally compact group
G
that falls into one of the following categories:

(i)

compact and abelian;

(ii)

compact and connected; or

(iii)

connected with weight strictly greater than
2^{\omega}
.

It is also conjectured in
[
9
]
that
w(G)\in{\mathrm{dd}}^{*}(G)
for
every
compact group. In this paper, we confirm this conjecture by proving a stronger result:

Main Theorem
.

Every compact group satisfies property
\mathbf{JM}^{*}
.

1.1.
Notation and Terminology

Throughout this paper, all topological spaces are assumed to be Tychonoff.
We denote the density and weight of a topological space
X
by
d(X)
and
w(X)
, respectively.
The cardinality of a set
X
is denoted by
|X|
.

If
X
is a subset of a group
G
, then
\langle X\rangle
denotes the subgroup of
G
generated by
X
.

Let
G
be a locally compact abelian group. Its Pontryagin–van Kampen dual, denoted by
\widehat{G}
, is the group of continuous homomorphisms from
G
to the one-dimensional torus
{\mathbb{T}}
, endowed with the compact-open topology.
The celebrated Pontryagin Duality Theorem asserts that the natural evaluation map identifies
G
with its double dual
\widehat{\widehat{G}}
as a topological group.

If
f:G\to H
is a continuous homomorphism between locally compact abelian groups, we denote the induced homomorphism
\widehat{H}\to\widehat{G}
, given by
\chi\mapsto\chi\circ f
, by
\widehat{f}
.

If
H
is a closed subgroup of
G
, we define

H^{\perp}:=\{\chi\in\widehat{G}:\chi(h)=0\text{ for all }h\in H\}.

It is straightforward to verify that
\widehat{G/H}\cong H^{\perp}
.

We shall use standard properties of Pontryagin duality freely, without further reference. A comprehensive treatment can be found in any standard textbook on topological groups.

1.2.
Some Lemmas

To prepare for the proof of the main theorem, we collect several auxiliary lemmas. These results describe how weights and densities behave under common group-theoretic and topological constructions. They will be used repeatedly in various reduction steps throughout the paper.

Throughout, all product factors and semidirect product components are naturally identified with closed subgroups of the ambient group.

We begin with a lemma concerning quotients by totally disconnected central subgroups in connected compact groups.

Lemma 1.3
.

Let
G
be a connected compact group, and let
D
be a closed, totally disconnected normal (and hence central) subgroup. Then

w(G/D)=w(G).

Proof.

In the abelian case, this follows from standard arguments involving Pontryagin duality. In the general case, the conclusion follows from the abelian case and the structure theory of connected compact groups; see
[
5
, Theorem 9.24]
.
∎

Next, we consider how the density of subgroups behaves under open continuous group homomorphisms.

Lemma 1.4
.

Let
\pi:G\to H
be a surjective open continuous homomorphism between locally compact groups.
If
D
is a dense subgroup of
H
with density
\kappa\geq d(G)
, then the preimage
\pi^{-1}(D)
is a dense subgroup of
G
and satisfies

d(\pi^{-1}(D))=\kappa.

Proof.

Let
N=\ker(\pi)
. By a theorem of Comfort and Itzkowitz
[
2
, 2.4 Theorem]
, we have
d(N)\leq d(G)\leq\kappa
.
Using the standard inequality for the density of extensions,

d(\pi^{-1}(D))\leq d(D)\cdot d(N)\leq\kappa\cdot\kappa=\kappa.

On the other hand, since
D
is dense in
H
, its preimage is dense in
G
, and clearly has at least
\kappa
-many elements. Hence,
d(\pi^{-1}(D))\geq\kappa
, and equality follows.
∎

Finally, we recall a useful construction for building dense subgroups of prescribed density via
\sigma
-products in products of compact metrizable groups.

Definition 1.5
.

Let
\{K_{i}:i\in I\}
be a family of topological groups, and let
e_{i}\in K_{i}
denote the identity element of
K_{i}
. The
\sigma
-product
of the family
\{K_{i}\}
with respect to the points
\{e_{i}\}
is defined as the subspace

\sigma\prod_{i\in I}K_{i}:=\left\{\,x\in\prod_{i\in I}K_{i}:\{i\in I:x_{i}\neq e_{i}\}\text{ is finite}\,\right\}.

This space coincides with the subgroup of
\prod_{i\in I}K_{i}
generated by the (images of the) individual
K_{i}
.

Lemma 1.6
.

Let
\{K_{i}:i\in I\}
be an infinite family of non-trivial compact metrizable groups.
Then the
\sigma
-product of the
K_{i}
is a dense subgroup of
\prod_{i\in I}K_{i}
and has density equal to
|I|
.

Proof.

See
[
9
, Lemma 4.3]
.
∎

2.
Proof of the Main Theorem: Profinite Case

We begin the proof of our main theorem by considering the special case where
G
is a profinite group; that is,
G
is compact and totally disconnected.
Background on profinite groups and related standard results may be found in
[
10
]
.

A subset
X\subseteq G
is said to
converge to the identity

e
if for every neighbourhood
U
of
e
, the set
X\setminus U
is finite.

It is a classical fact that every profinite group admits a subset that converges to
e
and topologically generates the group. Such a subset is called a
generating set converging to
e
.
The smallest cardinality of such a set is denoted by
\delta(G)
.
Whenever
\delta(G)
is infinite—in particular, when
G
is not metrizable—we have
\delta(G)=w(G)
.

For a profinite group
G
, the intersection of all maximal open subgroups is called the
Frattini subgroup
of
G
, denoted
\Phi(G)
.
Equivalently,
\Phi(G)
can be defined as the intersection of all maximal open
normal
subgroups, or as the intersection of the kernels of all continuous surjective homomorphisms from
G
onto finite simple groups (including abelian ones).

It is known that if
G
is finitely generated (i.e.,
\delta(G)<\omega
), then
\delta(G)=\delta(G/\Phi(G))

[
10
, Lemma 2.8.6]
.
The following lemma extends this equality to arbitrary profinite groups.

Lemma 2.1
.

For every profinite group
G
, we have
\delta(G)=\delta(G/\Phi(G))
. Consequently, if
G
is infinite, then
w(G)=w(G/\Phi(G))
.

Proof.

The inequality
\delta(G)\geq\delta(G/\Phi(G))
is immediate.

Let
\pi:G\to G/\Phi(G)
be the canonical quotient map. It is known that there exists a compact subset
X\subseteq G
containing
e
such that the restriction
\pi|_{X}:X\to G/\Phi(G)
is a homeomorphism (cf.
[
10
, Proposition 2.2.2]
).

Let
D
be a generating set for
G/\Phi(G)
that converges to
e
and has cardinality
\tau:=\delta(G/\Phi(G))
.
Define
Y:=(\pi|_{X})^{-1}(D)
. Then
|Y|=\tau
, and since
\pi|_{X}
is a homeomorphism,
Y
also converges to
e
in
G
.

Let
H
be the closed subgroup of
G
generated by
Y
. Since
\pi(H)\supseteq D
, it follows that
\pi(H)=G/\Phi(G)
, i.e.,
H\Phi(G)=G
.
By a fundamental property of the Frattini subgroup (see
[
10
, Corollary 2.8.5]
), this implies
H=G
.

Thus,
Y
is a generating set of
G
converging to
e
, and hence

\delta(G)\leq\tau=\delta(G/\Phi(G)).

The proof is complete.
∎

We are now ready to prove that every infinite profinite group satisfies property
\mathbf{JM}^{*}
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Every infinite profinite group satisfies property
\mathbf{JM}^{*}
.

Proof.

Let
G
be an infinite profinite group.
By
[
11
, p.458, Theorem 2]
, there exists an isomorphism

G/\Phi(G)\cong\prod_{\alpha<\tau}S_{\alpha},

where each
S_{\alpha}
is a finite simple group.
By Lemma
2.1
, we have
\tau=w(G)
.

Fix any cardinal
\kappa
with
d(G)\leq\kappa\leq w(G)
, and define

N:=\prod_{\kappa<\alpha<\tau}S_{\alpha},

with the convention that
N=\{e\}
when
\kappa=\tau
.

Consider the natural quotient map

\pi:G\to G/N\cong\prod_{\alpha<\kappa}S_{\alpha}.

By Lemma
1.6
, the
\sigma
-product
H
inside
\prod_{\alpha<\kappa}S_{\alpha}
is dense and has density
\kappa
.
Therefore, by Lemma
1.4
, the preimage
\pi^{-1}(H)
is a dense subgroup of
G
of density
\kappa
, as required.
∎

3.
Proof of the Main Theorem: General Case

3.1.
The Group of Automorphisms

A natural object associated with any topological group
G
is its
automorphism group

\operatorname{Aut}(G)
, consisting of all topological group automorphisms of
G
. When
G
is locally compact, we endow
\operatorname{Aut}(G)
with the
Birkhoff topology
, defined by the subbasis consisting of sets of the form

\mathcal{B}(L,U):=\{\phi\in\operatorname{Aut}(G):\phi(x)\subseteq Ux\text{ and }\phi^{-1}(x)\subseteq Ux\text{ for all $x\in L$}\},

where
L
is a compact subset of
G
, and
U
is an open neighbourhood of the identity in
G
. This topology, originally introduced by Birkhoff in the context of uniform structures, is designed to reflect both the topological and algebraic features of
\operatorname{Aut}(G)
.

Under the Birkhoff topology,
\operatorname{Aut}(G)
becomes a topological group.
If a topological group
H
acts on
G
by topological automorphisms, then this action is continuous if and only if the induced homomorphism
H\to\operatorname{Aut}(G)
is continuous.

For further details, see
[
3
, p. 260]
.

3.2.
Dense Subgroups Invariant under a Compact Group Action

3.2.1.
The Abelian Case

Let
G
be a locally compact abelian group, and let
X
denote its Pontryagin dual group. It is a classical fact that
\operatorname{Aut}(G)
and
\operatorname{Aut}(X)
are
topologically anti-isomorphic
(see
[
6
, (26.9) Theorem]
). More precisely, the map

\operatorname{ad}:\operatorname{Aut}(G)\to\operatorname{Aut}(X),\quad\sigma\mapsto\widehat{\sigma}

is both a group anti-isomorphism and a homeomorphism.

The following lemma is essentially folklore, though elementary; we include a proof for completeness.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
G
be a locally compact abelian group and let
\sigma\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)
. Then for any closed subgroup
H\leq G
, the subgroup
H
is
\sigma
-invariant if and only if
H^{\perp}
is
\widehat{\sigma}
-invariant.

Proof.

Suppose
H\leq G
is
\sigma
-invariant. Then for any
\chi\in H^{\perp}
and
h\in H
, we have

\widehat{\sigma}(\chi)(h)=\chi(\sigma(h))\in\chi(H)=\{0\},

so
\widehat{\sigma}(\chi)\in H^{\perp}
.

The converse follows by duality.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
G
be an infinite connected compact abelian group, and let
K
be a compact subgroup of
\operatorname{Aut}(G)
. Then for every cardinal
\kappa\in[d(G),w(G)]
, there exists a closed,
K
-invariant, and dense subgroup
H\leq G
such that
d(H)=\kappa
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume that
w(G)
is uncountable. Then
A:=\widehat{G}
is an uncountable abelian group.
Let
L:=\operatorname{ad}(K)\leq\operatorname{Aut}(A)
. Since
G
is compact and connected, its dual group
A
is torsion-free and discrete. As
L
is compact, the
L
-orbit
L\cdot x
is finite for every
x\in A
.

Let
V
be the divisible hull of
A
(see
[
4
, Sec. 24]
for the definition). Then
V
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space.
It is well known that every automorphism of
A
uniquely extends to an automorphism of
V
, and that every group automorphism of
V
is
\mathbb{Q}
-linear.
Hence the action of
L
on
A
extends to a linear action on
V
, and
A
becomes an
L
-invariant subgroup of the
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space
V
.

We now construct, by transfinite induction, a family
\{A_{\alpha}:\alpha<\kappa\}
of nonzero
L
-invariant countable subgroups of
A
such that

A_{\alpha}\cap\sum_{\beta<\alpha}A_{\beta}=\{0\}\quad\text{for all }\alpha<\kappa.

We begin by choosing some nonzero element
a_{0}\in A
and define
V_{0}
to be the linear subspace of
V
generated by the finite set
L\cdot a_{0}
. Then
V_{0}
is
L
-invariant and finite-dimensional, hence countable. Define
A_{0}:=A\cap V_{0}
, which is an
L
-invariant subgroup of
A
.

Now suppose that for some ordinal
\alpha<\kappa
we have constructed subgroups
\{A_{\beta}\}_{\beta<\alpha}
satisfying the required properties. Let
V_{\alpha}
be the
\mathbb{Q}
-linear span of
\sum_{\beta<\alpha}A_{\beta}
in
V
. Since
V_{\alpha}
has cardinality at most
\omega\cdot\alpha<w(G)
, we may choose some
a\in A\setminus V_{\alpha}
.

Let
W
be the subspace of
V
generated by the finite set
L\cdot a
. Then
W
is
L
-invariant and finite-dimensional. Since
V_{\alpha}
is also
L
-invariant, the intersection
U:=W\cap V_{\alpha}
is an
L
-invariant subspace of
W
, and
\dim U<\dim W
.

Because
L
is compact and
A
is discrete, and since
L\cdot a
is finite, the pointwise stabilizer

M:=\{\ell\in L:\ell(x)=x\text{ for all }x\in L\cdot a\}

is an open subgroup of
L
. Then
M
acts trivially on
W
, as
W
is generated by
L\cdot a
, so the quotient group
L/M
(which is finite) acts on
W
by linear automorphisms.

By Maschke’s Theorem (see e.g.
[
1
, p. 116]
), since
\mathbb{Q}
has characteristic zero and
L/M
is finite, there exists an
L/M
-invariant (hence
L
-invariant) linear subspace
W_{\alpha}\leq W
such that
W=U\oplus W_{\alpha}
. We then define
A_{\alpha}:=A\cap W_{\alpha}
.

Since
\dim U<\dim W
, we have
\dim W_{\alpha}>0
, so
W_{\alpha}
contains nonzero elements. Because
A
is a subgroup whose
\mathbb{Q}
-span is
V
, in particular
A
is nontrivial on every nontrivial rational direction. Therefore,
W_{\alpha}\cap A\neq\{0\}
, and hence
A_{\alpha}
is nonzero.

By construction, each
A_{\alpha}
is
L
-invariant and countable, and the family
\{A_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha<\kappa}
is linearly independent (i.e., the sum is direct). Let
S:=\bigoplus_{\alpha<\kappa}A_{\alpha}
denote the resulting direct sum.

Since
S
is
L
-invariant, Lemma
3.1
implies that its annihilator
N:=S^{\perp}
is
K
-invariant. Moreover,

G/N\cong\prod_{\alpha<\kappa}\widehat{A_{\alpha}}.

Let
\pi:G\to\prod_{\alpha<\kappa}\widehat{A_{\alpha}}
denote the canonical projection.

For each
\alpha<\kappa
, define

S_{\alpha}:=\bigoplus_{\beta\in\kappa\setminus\{\alpha\}}A_{\beta},\quad\text{and}\quad N_{\alpha}:=S_{\alpha}^{\perp}.

Then
N_{\alpha}
is
K
-invariant, and one verifies that

N_{\alpha}=\pi^{-1}(\widehat{A_{\alpha}}),

so each
\widehat{A_{\alpha}}
is
K
-invariant under the induced action on
G/N
.

Let
P
be the internal direct sum of the
\widehat{A_{\alpha}}
in
\prod_{\alpha<\kappa}\widehat{A_{\alpha}}
. Since each
\widehat{A_{\alpha}}
is compact and metrizable, the subgroup
P
is dense and has density
\kappa
, by Lemma
1.6
.
Moreover,
P
is
K
-invariant.

Define
H:=\pi^{-1}(P)
. Then
H
is dense and
K
-invariant, and satisfies
H/N\cong P
. Since
d(N)\leq d(G)\leq\kappa
, it follows from Lemma
1.4
that
d(H)=\kappa
.
∎

3.2.2.
Semisimple Connected Compact Groups

By a
semisimple connected compact group
, we mean a connected compact group whose commutator subgroup coincides with the whole group. For a detailed exposition, see
[
5
, Ch. 9]
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
G
be a semisimple connected compact group, and let
K
be a compact subgroup of
\operatorname{Aut}(G)
. Then for any cardinal
\kappa\in[d(G),w(G)]
, there exists a dense,
K
-invariant subgroup
H\leq G
such that
d(H)=\kappa
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
G
is not metrizable.

By the structure theory of semisimple connected compact groups
[
5
, Theorem 9.19]
, the centre
Z
of
G
is totally disconnected, and there exists an isomorphism

G/Z\cong\prod_{\alpha<\tau}S_{\alpha},

where each
S_{\alpha}
is a simple, connected, compact Lie group with trivial centre, and
\tau=w(G)
.
It follows that
w(G/Z)=w(G)
and
d(G/Z)=d(G)
.

Moreover, since
Z
is clearly
K
-invariant, the action of
K
on
G
descends to a continuous action on
G/Z
.
Therefore, it suffices to construct a
K
-invariant dense subgroup
H\leq G/Z
of density
\kappa
, since its preimage
H^{\prime}:=\pi^{-1}(H)
under the natural projection
\pi:G\to G/Z
will then be a dense
K
-invariant subgroup of
G
with
d(H^{\prime})=\kappa
, by Lemma
1.4
.

Thus, we may reduce to the case where
G=\prod_{i\in I}S_{i}
, where each
S_{i}
is a simple, connected, compact Lie group with trivial centre, and
|I|=\tau=w(G)
.

It is known that the connected normal subgroups of
G
are precisely the subproducts over subsets of
I
.
Hence, for each
\sigma\in K
and
i\in I
, there exists
j\in I
such that
\sigma(S_{i})=S_{j}
. This yields a homomorphism

\varphi:K\to{\mathrm{Sym}}(I),

where
{\mathrm{Sym}}(I)
denotes the group of all permutations of
I
, endowed with the topology of pointwise convergence (with
I
discrete).

We claim that
\varphi
is continuous.

Note that a subbasis at the identity of
{\mathrm{Sym}}(I)
consists of point stabilizers.
Thus, it suffices to show that for each
i\in I
, the setwise stabilizer of
S_{i}
in
K
is open.

Let
L:=\prod_{j\in I\setminus\{i\}}S_{j}
, and let
V
be a neighbourhood of the identity in
S_{i}
that contains no nontrivial subgroup.
Then
U:=L\times V
is an open neighbourhood of the identity in
G
.

Now consider the basic open set

\mathcal{B}(L,U):=\{\sigma\in K:\sigma(x)\in Ux\text{ and }\sigma^{-1}(x)\in Ux\text{ for all }x\in L\}.

We claim that each
\sigma\in\mathcal{B}(L,U)
satisfies
\sigma(L)=L
, and hence
\sigma(S_{i})=S_{i}
.

Indeed, for any
x\in L
, we have
\sigma(x)\in Ux\subseteq UL=U
, so
\sigma(L)\subseteq U
.
But the only connected normal subgroup of
G
contained in
U
is
L
itself, so
\sigma(L)\leq L
.
Applying the same argument to
\sigma^{-1}
yields
\sigma^{-1}(L)\leq L
, hence
\sigma(L)=L
, and thus
\sigma(S_{i})=S_{i}
.

Therefore, the stabilizer of
S_{i}
contains the open set
\mathcal{B}(L,U)
, and so
\varphi
is continuous.

Since
K
is compact,
\varphi(K)
is a compact subgroup of
{\mathrm{Sym}}(I)
.
It follows that for each
i\in I
, the orbit
\varphi(K)\cdot i
is finite. This yields a partition of
I
into
K
-orbits:

I=\bigcup_{\alpha<\tau}I_{\alpha},

where each
I_{\alpha}
is a finite
K
-orbit.

Now fix any cardinal
\kappa\in[d(G),\tau]
. Define

A:=\prod_{\kappa\leq\alpha<\tau}\prod_{i\in I_{\alpha}}S_{i},\qquad B:=\left\langle\prod_{i\in I_{\alpha}}S_{i}:\alpha<\kappa\right\rangle,

where the subgroup
B
is the
\sigma
-product of the subgroups
\prod_{i\in I_{\alpha}}S_{i}
for
\alpha<\kappa
.

Each subgroup
\prod_{i\in I_{\alpha}}S_{i}
is compact and metrizable (since
I_{\alpha}
is finite), so by Lemma
1.6
, the
\sigma
-product
B
is dense in the corresponding product and has density
\kappa
.

Since
d(A)\leq d(G)\leq\kappa
, it follows that

d(A\times B)=d(A)\cdot d(B)=\kappa.

Thus,
H:=A\times B
is a dense
K
-invariant subgroup of
G
with density
\kappa
, as desired.
∎

3.3.
The Proof

We are now ready to prove the Main Theorem.

The argument relies on structural results for compact groups, all of which can be found in
[
5
, Ch. 9]
.
Throughout, let
G
be an infinite compact group, and let
G_{0}
denote the connected component of the identity.

It follows from the identity
w(G)=w(G/G_{0})\cdot w(G_{0})
that either
w(G)=w(G/G_{0})
or
w(G)=w(G_{0})
.

Case 1
.

w(G/G_{0})=w(G)
.

Proof.

In this case, we have
d(G/G_{0})=d(G)
.
For any cardinal

\kappa\in[d(G),w(G)]=[d(G/G_{0}),w(G/G_{0})],

Proposition
2.2
yields a dense subgroup
H
of
G/G_{0}
with
d(H)=\kappa
.
Then, by Lemma
1.4
, the preimage of
H
under the canonical projection
G\to G/G_{0}
is a dense subgroup of
G
with density
\kappa
.
∎

Case 2
.

w(G_{0})=w(G)
.

Proof.

We begin with a reduction.
By
[
5
, Corollary 9.42]
, the group
G_{0}
admits a closed, totally disconnected central subgroup
D
that is normal in
G
, such that

G/D\cong(G_{0}/D)\rtimes E,

where
E
is a profinite group (note that
E\cong G/G_{0}
, although this is not needed in the sequel).

By Lemma
1.3
, we have

w(G_{0}/D)=w(G_{0})=w(G),

so that
w(G/D)=w(G)
, and hence
d(G/D)=d(G)
.
Thus, it suffices by Lemma
1.4
to verify that
G/D
satisfies
\mathbf{JM}^{*}
.
From now on, we may therefore assume that
G\cong G_{0}\rtimes E
.

Let
G_{0}^{\prime}
denote the commutator subgroup of
G_{0}
, which is closed and semisimple.
By
[
5
, Theorem 9.24]
, we have
G_{0}=AG_{0}^{\prime}
, where
A
denotes the connected component of the identity in
Z(G_{0})
(the centre of
G_{0}
), and
\Delta:=A\cap G_{0}^{\prime}
is totally disconnected.
It follows that

G_{0}/\Delta\cong(A/\Delta)\times(G_{0}^{\prime}/\Delta).

Since both
A
and
G_{0}^{\prime}
are characteristic in
G_{0}
, the subgroup
\Delta
is normal in
G
.
Applying Lemma
1.3
again, we obtain
w(G/\Delta)=w(G)
and
d(G/\Delta)=d(G)
.
Therefore, by Lemma
1.4
, it suffices to prove the result for
G/\Delta
.
Thus, we may assume that
G_{0}\cong A\times S
, where
A
is abelian and
S
is semisimple, and

G\cong(A\times S)\rtimes E,

with
A
and
S
both normal in
G
, and
E
acting on each.

Since
G_{0}\cong A\times S
, we have either
w(A)=w(G_{0})=w(G)
or
w(S)=w(G_{0})=w(G)
.

Assume first that
w(A)=w(G)
.
Fix any cardinal
\kappa\in[d(G),w(G)]
.
By Lemma
3.2
, there exists a dense
E
-invariant subgroup
H\leq A
with
d(H)=\kappa
.
Then
H\times S
is
E
-invariant, and the set

H_{1}:=(H\times S)E=(H\times S)\rtimes E

is a dense subgroup of
G
and satisfies

d(H_{1})=d(H)\cdot d(S)\cdot d(E).

Since
d(S),d(E)\leq d(G)\leq\kappa
, we conclude that
d(H_{1})=\kappa
.

If instead
w(S)=w(G)
, then Lemma
3.3
applies to construct a dense
E
-invariant subgroup of
S
of density
\kappa
, and a similar argument yields the desired subgroup of
G
.
∎

Combining Cases
1
and
2
completes the proof.

The uniqueness and concentration behavior of solutions for a nonlinear fractional Schrödinger system
Chungen Liu, Zhigao Zhang, Jiabin Zuo

Abstract




Abstract.
The paper is concerned with a nonlinear system of two coupled fractional Schrödinger equations with both attractive intraspecies and attractive interspecies interactions in
\mathbb{R}
. By analyzing an associated
L^{2}
-constrained minimization problem, the uniqueness of solutions to this system is proved via the implicit
function theorem. Under a certain type of trapping potential, by establishing some delicate
energy estimates, we present a detailed analysis on the concentration behavior of the solutions as the total
strength of intraspecies and interspecies interactions tends to a critical value, where each component of the solutions blows up and concentrates at a flattest common minimum point of the
associated trapping potentials. An optimal
blow-up rate of solutions to the system is also given.

Keywords:
nonlinear Schrödinger system, fractional Laplacian, normalized solutions, concentration behavior.

2010 Mathematics Subject Classification:
35R11, 35J50, 35Q40.

Introduction

In this paper, we consider the following nonlinear system of two coupled fractional Schrödinger equations in
\mathbb{R}
:

\begin{cases}\sqrt{-\Delta}u_{1}+V_{1}(x)u_{1}=\mu_{1}u_{1}+a_{1}u_{1}^{3}+\beta u_{2}^{2}u_{1},\\
\sqrt{-\Delta}u_{2}+V_{2}(x)u_{2}=\mu_{2}u_{2}+a_{2}u_{2}^{3}+\beta u_{1}^{2}u_{2},\end{cases}

(1.1)

where for
i=1,2
,
\mu_{i}\in\mathbb{{R}}
is a suitable Lagrange multiplier which denotes the chemical potential,
V_{i}(x)
is a certain type of trapping potential,
a_{i}>0

(resp.<0)
corresponds to the attractive
(resp.\ repulsive)
intraspecies interaction inside each component, and

\beta>0

(resp.<0)
represents the attractive
(resp.\ repulsive)
interspecies interaction between the two components.

The system (
1.1
) arises from the following time-dependent nonlinear fractional Schrödinger equation

i\partial_{t}\psi(x,t)=(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}\psi+V(x)\psi+\beta|\psi|^{2}\psi,\quad x\in\mathbb{R},\ t>0,

(1.2)

which is introduced to model non-Gaussian Bose-Einstein condensates (BEC), where bosons follow L¨¦vy flights instead of Gaussian paths, see
[
25
]
. Here,
\alpha
is the L¨¦vy index which measures self-similarity
in the L¨¦vy fractal path,
V(x)
denotes the external trapping potential,
\beta
describes the strength of local (or short-range) interactions between particles,
\psi(x,t)
is a complex-valued wave function given by
\psi(x,t)=e^{-i\mu t}u(x)
, where
u(x)
is a solution of the fractional single-component equation

(-\Delta)^{s}u+V(x)u=\mu u+\beta u^{3},\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}.

(1.3)

For more physical background, we refer the reader to references
[
22
,
24
]
and the references therein.

In recent years, under various conditions
on
V(x)
and
\mu
, the analogous problems to (
1.3
) were widely investigated. For the case
V(x)=0
, the existence of solutions was considered in
[
8
,
15
,
26
]
for different growth conditions of nonlinearities; while for
V(x)\neq 0
, one has to take into account the effect
of the potential
V(x)
besides the nonlinearity. As shown in
[
5
,
6
,
13
]
, which focused on fractional Schrödinger equations with various potentials, the existence and non-existence of
L^{2}
-normalized solutions were established, and a detailed analysis of the concentration behavior of solutions was also presented. Moreover, the frequency
\mu
is also a critical parameter, and there exist two substantially distinct approaches to it. One is to regard it as fixed, as studied in
[
3
,
23
]
; the alternative is to treat it as unknown, where it appears as a Lagrange multiplier associated with the prescribed mass constraint. The latter approach is particularly interesting from a physical perspective, since the mass often has a clear physical meaning, with relevant research presented in
[
1
,
7
,
18
,
21
]
.

Note that within the multiple-component framework, interspecies interactions between the two components give rise to more elaborate physical phenomena and render the corresponding analytic investigations more challenging. When (
1.1
) reduces to a Laplacian system, that is,

\begin{cases}-\Delta u_{1}+V_{1}(x)u_{1}=\mu_{1}u_{1}+b_{1}u_{1}^{3}+\beta u_{2}^{2}u_{1},\\
-\Delta u_{2}+V_{2}(x)u_{2}=\mu_{2}u_{2}+b_{2}u_{2}^{3}+\beta u_{1}^{2}u_{2},\end{cases}

(1.4)

it becomes a classical model in
\mathbb{{R}}^{2}
describing two-component Bose-Einstein condensates, which was studied by Guo, Zeng and Wang in
[
10
,
12
]
for the case where the intraspecies and interspecies interactions are both
attractive (i.e.,
b_{1},b_{2},\beta>0
). Under the constraint condition

\left\{(u_{1},u_{2})\in\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{1}\times\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{2}:\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|u_{1}\right|^{2}dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|u_{2}\right|^{2}dx=1\right\},

where
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{i}=\{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}):\int_{\mathbb{R^{2}}}V_{i}(x)\left|u\right|^{2}dx<\infty\}

(i=1,2)
, they
[
10
]
analyzed how the existence, non-existence and uniqueness of solutions to (
1.4
) depend on the parameters
b_{1}
,
b_{2}
and
\beta
, and further showed that, as the total
strength of intraspecies and interspecies interactions tends to a critical value, each component of the solutions blows up at the same point, and under a specific scaling transformation, converges to the same limit function in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Later, in
[
12
]
, under a different constraint condition

\{(u_{1},u_{2})\in\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{1}\times\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{2}:\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(\left|u_{1}\right|^{2}+\left|u_{2}\right|^{2})dx=1\}
, the Laplacian system (
1.4
) with
\mu_{1}=\mu_{2}
was investigated. Not only was a
complete classification of the existence and non-existence of ground states provided, but the uniqueness, mass concentration, and symmetry-breaking of ground states were also established under different types of trapping potentials as the interspecies interaction strength approached a critical value. Moreover, they generalized the results of
[
10
]
to systems (
1.4
) with attractive intraspecies and repulsive interspecies interactions
(\beta<0)
in
[
11
]
, and notably, the asymptotic analysis differs distinctly from that in
[
10
,
12
]
due to the opposite signs of these two types of interactions. It can be seen that the uniqueness and decay rate of the positive solution to the associated classical equation, together with the strong maximum principle, play a key role in the concentration behavior of solutions in
[
10
,
11
,
12
]
.

Motivated by
[
10
,
11
]
, Liu, Zhang and Zuo considered the fractional Laplacian system (
1.1
) in
[
17
]
, where various existence and non-existence results were obtained under appropriate ranges for the parameters
a_{1}>0
,
a_{2}>0
, and
\beta\in\mathbb{{R}}
. However, the uniqueness and concentration behavior of solutions remain unexplored. To address this gap, we focus on these properties of solutions to the system
(
1.1
) with attractive intraspecies and interspecies interactions, i.e.,
a_{1}>0
,
a_{2}>0
, and
\beta>0
. The case of
\beta<0
is left to interested readers for future investigation.

In this paper, trapping potentials
V_{i}(x)
satisfy the following condition

(\mathcal{D})

V_{i}(x)\in L^{\infty}_{loc}(\mathbb{R})
,
\displaystyle\lim_{\lvert x\rvert\rightarrow\infty}V_{i}(x)=\infty
and
\mathop{\mathrm{inf\mathrm{}}}\limits_{x\in\mathbb{R}}V_{i}(x)=0
,

.

Moreover, we assume throughout the paper that both
\mathop{\mathrm{inf\mathrm{}}}_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\big(V_{1}(x)+V_{2}(x)\big)
and
\mathop{\mathrm{inf\mathrm{}}}_{x\in\mathbb{R}}V_{i}(x)\ (i=1,2)
are attained.

Before formulating the main results of this paper, we first give some notations and recall some known results. Let the
\frac{1}{2}
-Laplace operator be defined in the Sobolev space
H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R})
by the
Fourier transform, i.e.,

\sqrt{-\Delta}u:=\mathscr{F}^{-1}(\left|\xi\right|\mathscr{F}u(\xi)),\ \ u\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}),

(1.5)

in which

represents the Fourier transform of

\mathscr{F}u(\xi)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\exp^{-i\xi\cdot x}u(x)dx,\ \ u\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}),

and
H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R})
is defined as

H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}):=\{u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}):\left\|u\right\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}(1+2\pi\left|\xi\right|)\Big|\mathscr{F}u(\xi)\Big|^{2}d\xi<\infty\},

with the norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{{R}})}
given by

\left\|u\right\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{{R}})}^{2}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big[u(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}u+u^{2}\Big]dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big[|(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{4}}u|^{2}+u^{2}\Big]dx.

It is known that (
1.1
) is the system of Euler-Lagrange equations for the following constrained
minimization problem

\hat{e}(a_{1},a_{2},\beta):=\mathop{\mathrm{inf\mathrm{}}}\limits_{(u_{1},u_{2})\in\mathcal{M}}E_{a_{1},a_{2},\beta}(u_{1},u_{2}),

(1.6)

where the energy functional
E_{a_{1},a_{2},\beta}(u_{1},u_{2})
is defined by

\displaystyle E_{a_{1},a_{2},\beta}(u_{1},u_{2})=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}[u_{i}\sqrt{-\Delta}u_{i}+V_{i}(x)u_{i}^{2}]dx-\sum_{i=1}^{2}\frac{a_{i}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|u_{i}\right|^{4}dx

\displaystyle-\beta\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|u_{1}\right|^{2}\left|u_{2}\right|^{2}dx,\quad\text{for any }(u_{1},u_{2})\in\mathcal{X}.

(1.7)

Here,
\mathcal{M}
is the so-called mass constraint

\mathcal{M}=\left\{(u,v)\in\mathcal{X}:\int_{\mathbb{R}}u^{2}dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}}v^{2}dx=1\right\}

(1.8)

with

\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{H}_{1}\times\mathcal{H}_{2},\ \ \mathcal{H}_{i}=\{u\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}):\int_{\mathbb{R}}V_{i}(x)u^{2}dx<\infty\}\ \ (i=1,2)\quad

and

\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{i}}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left[|(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{4}}u|^{2}+V_{i}(x)u^{2}\right]dx,\ \ \left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{X}}^{2}=\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{1}}^{2}+\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}^{2},\quad i=1,2.

(1.9)

The classical equation associated to system (
1.1
) is known as

\sqrt{-\Delta}u+u=u^{3},\ \ \>u\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{{R}}).

(1.10)

Let
Q(x)>0
be the unique radially symmetric ground state (
[
8
,
9
]
) of (
1.10
), and it is shown in Proposition 3.1 of
[
9
]
that the ground state

has the decay rate

Q(x)=O(|x|^{-2})\ \ \text{as}\ \ |x|\to\infty.

(1.11)

Denoting

, it is known from Theorem 1.1 of
[
6
]
that the fractional Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality corresponding to (

) takes the following form

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|u\right|^{4}dx\leq\frac{2}{a^{*}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\left(-\Delta\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}u|^{2}dx\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|u\right|^{2}dx,\ \ \>u\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{{R}}).

(1.12)

Furthermore, by Lemma 5.4 of
[

]

]

satisfies the following Pohozaev identity

\|\left(-\Delta\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}Q\|_{2}^{2}=\left\|Q\right\|_{2}^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|Q|^{4}dx.

(1.13)

Based on the above notations, we know from Theorem 1.1 in

that when

holds, (

) has a minimizer for any

Theorem 1.1
.

If

satisfies

for

, then (

non-negative minimizer if

Remark 1.2
.

Some results on uniqueness for the single-component equations analogous to (

) were established by using the contraction mapping approach in

\displaystyle V_{i}(x)=h_{i}(x)\prod_{j=1}^{n_{i}}|x-x_{ij}|^{p_{ij}}\quad\mathrm{with}\quad C<h_{i}(x)<1/C\quad\mathrm{in}\ \ \mathbb{R},

\displaystyle x_{1j_{1}}\neq x_{2j_{2}},\quad\mathrm{where}\quad j_{i}\in\begin{Bmatrix}l+1,\cdots n_{i}\end{Bmatrix}\ \ \mathrm{and}\ \ i=1,2.

Notice that, (

\Lambda:=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}:V_{1}(x)=V_{2}(x)=0\right\}=\{x_{11},x_{12},\cdots,x_{1l}\}.

\bar{\Lambda}:=\begin{Bmatrix}x_{1j}:\bar{p}_{j}=p_{0},\ j=1,\cdots,l\end{Bmatrix}\subset\Lambda.

Additionally for

Note that for

if and only if

.

Let

Remark 1.4
.

The existence of the global maximum point for each non-negative minimizer of problem (
1.6
) relies on the non-local De Giorgi-Nash-Moser theory introduced in
[
4
]
and
[
14
]
. However, the uniqueness of the global maximum point for problem (

Compared with the Laplacian system in
[
10
]
, some new difficulties arise due to the presence of the non-local operator
\sqrt{-\Delta}
. Firstly, the strong maximum principle as in
[
10
]
does not work to establish the positivity of the solution to the fractional Laplacian equation (

). To resolve this difficulty, we deduce the positivity of the solution from the strict positivity of the kernel

of the resolvent

on

, as established in Lemma C.1 of
[
9
]
.
Secondly, due to the non-uniqueness of positive solutions to (
1.10
), we prove that under a suitable translation and a dilation, each component of the non-negative minimizer
(u_{d_{1}},u_{d_{2}})
of (
3.2
) converges to the ground state of (

). Then, via a coordinate transformation, we establish the equivalence between this ground state and that of (
1.10
), thereby overcoming the obstacle arising from the non-uniqueness of positive solutions by exploiting the uniqueness of the ground state of (
1.10
).
Lastly, in striking contrast to the classical Schrödinger equation with the Laplacian in which the ground state decays exponentially at infinity, the ground state of (
1.10
) decays only polynomially at infinity. This is why we first require that the order
p_{ij}<3
for the polynomial potential function (
1.14
), which ensures that
\left|\cdot\right|^{{p}_{0}}Q^{2}(x)
is integrable. Moreover, to derive the optimal energy estimates for

(i=1,2)
, a crucial step that contributes to

- estimates of minimizers, we further impose that

(see the forthcoming estimate (

for

,
j=1,\cdots,n_{i}
.

2
Uniqueness of non-negative minimizers

In this section, we will prove Theorem

.

(

Proposition 2.2

(

Suppose

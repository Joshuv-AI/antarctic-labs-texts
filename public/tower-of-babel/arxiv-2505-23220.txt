η c ​ η c \eta_{c}\eta_{c} and J / ψ ​ J / ψ J/\psi J/\psi scatterings from lattice QCD
Geng Li, Chunjiang Shi, Ying Chen, Wei Sun

Abstract
We investigate the J P ​ C = ( 0 , 2 ) + ⁣ + J^{PC}=(0,2)^{++} η c ​ η c \eta_{c}\eta_{c} and J / ψ ​ J / ψ J/\psi J/\psi scatterings in N f = 2 N_{f}=2 lattice QCD at two pion masses m π ≈ 420 m_{\pi}\approx 420 MeV and 250 MeV. The quark field smearing scheme used in the distillation method strongly suppresses high-momentum states, thereby limiting the maximum accessible center-of-mass energy in this study to approximately 6.6 GeV. Given the observed near-decoupling of the η c ​ η c \eta_{c}\eta_{c} and J / ψ ​ J / ψ J/\psi J/\psi channels, we analyze their scattering properties using the single-channel Lüscher’s method. In 0 + ⁣ + 0^{++} channels, η c ​ η c \eta_{c}\eta_{c} and J / ψ ​ J / ψ J/\psi J/\psi have repulsive and attractive interactions, respectively, which are dominated by the quark exchange effects with the Fierz rearrangement determining the interaction characteristic. A likely 0 + ⁣ + 0^{++} virtual state is observed below the threshold by 20-40 MeV in the S 0 1 {}^{1}S_{0} J / ψ ​ J / ψ J/\psi J/\psi scattering and may have connection with X ⁡ ( 6200 ) X(6200) . In 2 + ⁣ + 2^{++} channels, the near-threshold interaction for the S 2 5 {}^{5}S_{2} J / ψ ​ J / ψ J/\psi J/\psi scattering is repulsive. The di-charmonium interactions are found to be insensitive to m π m_{\pi} and can be consistently understood in terms of quark rearrangement effects combined with a one-gluon-exchange mechanism, both of which are supported by our lattice QCD results. Most importantly, we observe a 2 + ⁣ + 2^{++} resonance in the S 2 5 {}^{5}S_{2} J / ψ ​ J / ψ J/\psi J/\psi scattering, whose properties are ( m R , Γ R ) = ( 6544 ​ ( 10 ) , 552 ​ ( 34 ) ) (m_{R},\Gamma_{R})=\big(6544(10),552(34)\big) MeV for m π ≈ 420 m_{\pi}\approx 420 MeV and ( 6539 ​ ( 13 ) , 546 ​ ( 57 ) ) \big(6539(13),546(57)\big) MeV for m π ≈ 250 m_{\pi}\approx 250 MeV, which are compatible with the parameters of the fully-charmed broad structure X ⁡ ( 6600 ) X(6600) (or X ⁡ ( 6400 ) X(6400) ) reported by ATLAS and CMS.



I
Introduction

In 2020, LHCb analyzed the
J/\psi J/\psi
invariant mass spectrum and observed a narrow structure around 6.9 GeV, named
X(6900)
, as well as a broad structure near the
J/\psi J/\psi
threshold
Aaij et al. 2020
.
ATLAS also performed an analysis of the
J/\psi J/\psi
mass spectra in the
4\mu
system
Aad et al. 2023
.
Assuming three interfering resonances, ATLAS observed two additional states with masses around 6.4 GeV and 6.6 GeV, labeled as
X(6400)
and
X(6600)
, in addition to
X(6900)
.
Shortly after, CMS reported the observation of
X(6600)
,
X(6900)
, and a new structure,
X(7100)

Hayrapetyan et al. 2024
;
Wang and Yi 2024
. These states are now named as
T_{4c}
states.
Recently, CMS refined the analysis of
J/\psi J/\psi
spectrum in proton-proton collisions with 3.6 times more statistics
CMS 2025a
, which confirms these three structures with the statistical significance well above
5\sigma
based on quantum interference among structures, and indicates that they may have the same
J^{PC}
quantum numbers. CMS also performed an analysis on the angular distribution of the decay products of these states
CMS 2025b
, which prefers the quantum numbers
2^{++}
while
0^{++}
is disfavored at 95% confidence level. These states may be candidates for
cc\bar{c}\bar{c}
tetraquark states,
T_{cc\bar{c}\bar{c}}
, and have stimulated extensive theoretical studies
Wu et al. 2024
;
Guo and Oller 2021
;
Liu et al. 2024
;
Chen et al. 2020
;
Maiani 2020
;
Chao and Zhu 2020
;
Kuang et al. 2023
;
Niu et al. 2023
;
Liu et al. 2021
;
Dong et al. 2021a
;
Gong et al. 2022a
;
Dong et al. 2021b
;
Huang et al. 2024
;
Zhang and Guo 2024
(see Ref.
Chen et al. 2023
for a review).
The fully-heavy tetraquark states were first investigated in the 1980s
Iwasaki 1975
;
Chao 1981
;
Ader et al. 1982
;
Li and Liu 1984
;
Heller and Tjon 1985
;
Badalian et al. 1987
.
Prior to the experimental observations of these structures, there was a theoretical study on fully-charmed tetraquark states using numerous diquark-diquark interpolation fields within the framework of QCD sum rules, which predicted a
T_{cc\bar{c}\bar{c}}
spectrum for different
J^{PC}
states
Chen et al. 2017
.
This spectrum remarkably coincides with the experimental observations, with the lowest state being
J^{PC}=0^{++}
and having a mass around 6.4 GeV.
Recently, a formalism is developped for the production of
T_{4c}
states
Belov et al. 2025
, which shows that the production cross section of
X(6900)
as a fully-charmed tetraquark is compatible with a
J^{PC}=2^{++}
state. Since these
T_{cc\bar{c}\bar{c}}
structures are observed in the
J/\psi J/\psi
system, it is intriguing to understand the dynamics between the two
J/\psi
particles (or, more generally, between two charmonium states) that contribute to the formation of these structures.
Some phenomenological studies attribute the dynamics to pomeron exchanges (gluonic interactions) or light meson exchanges between the two charmonia
Gong et al. 2022a
.

In this exploratory study, we investigate the di-charmonium interaction and its scattering properties from lattice QCD.
As a first step, we examine the coupled-channel scattering between
\eta_{c}\eta_{c}
and
J/\psi J/\psi
in the
J^{PC}=0^{++}
and
2^{++}
channels, for center-of-mass energies up to 6.6 GeV which is below the
J/\psi\psi^{\prime}
threshold.
For the
0^{++}
channel, we only consider the
S
-wave scattering
{}^{1}S_{0}
, since the coupling from the
{}^{5}D_{0}

J/\psi J/\psi
is expected to be suppressed by the centrifugal barrier.
For the
2^{++}
channel, we focus on the
{}^{5}S_{2}

J/\psi J/\psi
scattering, as well as the
{}^{1}D_{2}

\eta_{c}\eta_{c}
operators are included to make the lattice operator basis as complete as possible, ensuring reliable extraction of finite-volume energy levels.
We perform lattice calculations on
N_{f}=2
anisotropic gauge ensembles at two sea pion masses of
m_{\pi}\approx 420
and
250
MeV to investigate the possible
m_{\pi}
-dependence of di-charmonium scattering properties, which can be derived from the finite-volume energy levels through Lüscher’s formalism
Lüscher 1986
;
Lüscher 1991a
;
Lüscher 1991b
.
For each sea pion mass, we employ both
12^{3}\times 96
and
16^{3}\times 128
lattice volumes.
In practice, we adopt the distillation method
Peardon et al. 2009
, which provides a smearing scheme for quark fields and an advanced approach to handle the large correlation matrix.
Much effort has been dedicated to accurately extracting the energy levels of the
\eta_{c}\eta_{c}-J/\psi J/\psi
system, in order to obtain the scattering amplitudes as reliably as possible.
We further investigate the dependence of the lattice energy levels on the dimension of the Laplacian-Heaviside subspace.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows.
In Sec.
II
, we provide the numerical details of the lattice calculation, including lattice configuration, operator setup, correlation computation, and energy level extraction.
In Sec.
III
, we analyze the extracted energy levels within the framework of scattering theory and identify the possible existence of new states.
In Sec.
IV
, we make discussion of the results.
In Sec.
V
, a summary is presented.

II
Numerical details

II.1
Lattice configuration

We use
N_{f}=2
dynamical gauge configurations with degenerate
u
and
d
quarks at pion masses of
420\,(250)
MeV (labeled as ‘M420’ and ‘M250’).
These ensembles are generated on anisotropic lattices with aspect ratio
\xi\equiv a_{s}/a_{t}\approx 5
, where
a_{s}
and
a_{t}
denote the lattice spacings in the spatial and temporal directions, respectively.
The tadpole-improved Symanzik gauge action
Morningstar and Peardon 1997
;
Chen et al. 2006
and the tadpole-improved anisotropic clover fermion action
Zhang and Liu 2001
;
Su et al. 2006
;
Meng et al. 2009
are utilized for both the sea light quarks and the valence charm quark.
For each pion mass, we have two distinct lattice volumes that share the same spatial lattice spacing
Li et al. 2024
, namely,
N_{s}^{3}\times N_{t}=12^{3}\times 96
and
16^{3}\times 128
(labeled as ‘L12’ and ‘L16’).
The spatial lattice spacing
a_{s}
is determined to be
a_{s}=0.136(2)
fm through the Wilson flow method
Lüscher 2010
;
Borsányi et al. 2012
.
Consequently, we calculate the pion dispersion relation to determine the physical anisotropy parameter
\xi
, obtaining temporal lattice spacings of
a_{t}^{-1}=7.219\,(7.276)
GeV for the M420 (M250) ensembles, respectively.
The charm quark mass parameter is tuned to reproduce the spin-averaged mass of the 1S charmonium state,
\left(M_{\eta_{c}}+3M_{J/\psi}\right)/4=3069
MeV.
The parameters of the gauge ensembles are collected in Table
1
.
It should be noted that the values of
m_{\pi}L_{s}=m_{\pi}N_{s}a_{s}
of the two ‘M250’ ensembles are
\sim 2.1
fm and 2.8 fm, respectively, which seem quite small and might introduce non-negligible finite-volume effects.
However, since we are interested in di-charmonium scatterings, the effects from light flavors may not that important and charmonium states may suffer from less finite volume effects.
On the other hand, the relatively small lattice sizes facilitate us to obtained large center-of-mass energies at smaller lattice momentum modes
n^{2}
with
k^{2}=(\frac{2\pi}{L_{s}})^{2}n^{2}
, that is permitted by the distillation method (We will discuss in detail the reason for choosing smaller lattice sizes in Subsec.
II.7
).

The distillation method
Peardon et al. 2009
is adopted to calculate the correlation functions and the eigen space of the gauge invariant
Laplacian operator is truncated up to the
N_{V}
-th small eigenvalue.
In this Laplacian Heaviside subspace the perambulators of charm quarks are calculated and are used to construct the correlation functions of charmonium operators and di-charmonium operators.
In practice,
the perambulators are calculated by setting sources on each timeslice in order to enhance the statistics, and the values of
N_{V}
are set to be 170 for
N_{s}=12
lattices and 120 for
N_{s}=16
lattices.
First, we test the dispersion relations of
\eta_{c}
and
J/\psi
on the lattice,

E_{n}=\sqrt{M^{2}+\frac{1}{\xi_{i}^{2}}\left(\frac{2\pi}{a_{t}N_{s}}\right)^{2}n^{2}},

(1)

where
E_{n}
is the energy at the momentum
\vec{k}=\frac{2\pi}{a_{s}N_{s}}\vec{n}
, and
n=|\vec{n}|
is the magnitude of the discrete momentum mode
\vec{n}=(n_{1},n_{2},n_{3})
with
n^{2}
running up to 9.
Figure
1
shows the results on the four gauge ensembles, where data points are
E_{n}
at different
n^{2}
and the color lines indicate the fits using Eq. (
1
).
It is obvious that Eq. (
1
) describes the data very well and the fitted values of the aspect ratio
\xi
for all the cases agree with each other within 2-3% and also agree with the lattice input
\xi\approx 5
set by the dispersion relation of pion meson.
The obtained values
\xi_{\eta_{c}}
and
\xi_{J/\psi}
are applied in the subsequent scattering momentum calculations.

II.2
Operators setup

We investigate the
0^{++}
and
2^{++}
channels of the
c\bar{c}c\bar{c}
system with meson-meson type operator construction
Cheung et al. 2017
, with center-of-mass energies up to 6.6 GeV.
Within this energy range, only the coupled-channel scattering involving
\eta_{c}\eta_{c}
and
J/\psi J/\psi
is considered, excluding contributions from excited charmonium states such as
\psi(3770)
and
\eta_{c}(2S)
.
The
0^{++}
system includes
{}^{1}S_{0}

\eta_{c}\eta_{c}
scattering states, and (
{}^{1}S_{0}
,
{}^{5}D_{0}
)
J/\psi J/\psi
scattering states, while the coupled-channel effect from the
D
-wave is expected to be suppressed due to the centrifugal barrier effect
Von Hippel and Quigg 1972
.
The operator sets are constructed in the rest frame of the system, where each operator
\mathcal{O}_{\alpha}
is a meson-meson operator defined as
\mathcal{O}_{MM}(n^{2}(k))=\mathcal{O}_{M}(\vec{k})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{M}(-\vec{k})
, with
M
referring to
\eta_{c}
and
J/\psi
.
Here,
\mathcal{O}_{M}^{(i)}(\vec{k})=[\bar{c}\Gamma_{M}^{(i)}c](\vec{k})
represents a single-meson operator with
M
referring to either
\eta_{c}
(
\Gamma_{M}=\gamma_{5}
) or
J/\psi
(
\Gamma_{M}^{i}=\gamma_{i}
), and
n^{2}(k)=n_{1}^{2}+n_{2}^{2}+n_{3}^{2}
labels the magnitude of the relative momentum mode
\vec{k}=\frac{2\pi}{L}(n_{1},n_{2},n_{3})
.
The explicit expressions of the operator in the
{}^{1}S_{0}
channel are

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{\eta_{c}\eta_{c}}(n^{2}(k))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{R\in O}\mathcal{O}_{\eta_{c}}(R\vec{k})\mathcal{O}_{\eta_{c}}(-R\vec{k}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{\psi\psi}(n^{2}(k))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i,R\in O}\mathcal{O}_{\psi}^{i}(R\vec{k})\mathcal{O}_{\psi}^{i}(-R\vec{k})

(2)

where
O
is the octahedral group of the spatial lattice and
R\in O
represents an operation of
O
on
\vec{k}
.
In principle, the scalar
\bar{c}c
operator
\mathcal{O}_{\chi_{c0}}
should also be included in the
0^{++}
channel to make the operator set complete.

In the
2^{++}
channel, although the coupling from the
{}^{1}D_{2}

\eta_{c}\eta_{c}
scattering is suppressed, their finite volume energies are lower than the corresponding
{}^{5}S_{2}

J/\psi J/\psi
energies.
To reliably extract the energy levels of the
J/\psi J/\psi
system, we include
D
-wave
\eta_{c}\eta_{c}
operators to complete the set of interpolating field operators.
The construction of the
2^{++}
operators involves additional complexity.
The continuum angular momentum
J=2
is subduced into the irreducible representations
E
and
T_{2}
of
O
on the lattice, namely
J=2\to E\oplus T_{2}
.
In practice, we construction the
\mathcal{O}_{MM}
operators to be the first component
E(1)
of the
E
.
Since
\eta_{c}
is a spin zero particle, the total angular momentum of
\eta_{c}\eta_{c}
comes from the relative orbital angular momentum, so the expression of the
{}^{1}D_{2}

\mathcal{O}_{\eta_{c}\eta_{c}}^{E(1)}
is

\mathcal{O}_{\eta_{c}\eta_{c}}^{E(1)}=\sum\limits_{R\in O}c_{R}^{E(1)}\mathcal{O}_{\eta_{c}}(R\vec{k})\mathcal{O}_{\eta_{c}}(-R\vec{k})

(3)

where
\{c_{R}^{E(1)},R\in O\}
is the character of
E(1)
. The
E(1)
operator corresponding to the
{}^{5}S_{2}

J/\psi J/\psi
is expressed as

\mathcal{O}_{\psi\psi}^{E(1)}=\sum\limits_{R\in O}\left[\mathcal{O}_{\psi}^{1}(R\vec{k})\mathcal{O}_{\psi}^{1}(-R\vec{k})-\mathcal{O}_{\psi}^{2}(R\vec{k})\mathcal{O}_{\psi}^{2}(-R\vec{k})\right].

(4)

Thus the
0^{++}
,
2^{++}
operator sets
\mathcal{S}_{0,2}
for each ensemble are chosen to be

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{O}_{\eta_{c}\eta_{c}}(n^{2}),\mathcal{O}_{\psi\psi}(n^{2}),(\mathcal{O}_{\chi_{c0}});n^{2}=0,1,2,3,4\}

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{O}_{\eta_{c}\eta_{c}}(n^{2}\neq 0,3),\mathcal{O}_{\psi\psi}(n^{2});n^{2}=0,1,2,3,4\}

(5)

For each operator set we calculate the correlation matrix

C_{\alpha\beta}(t)=\langle\mathcal{O}_{\alpha}(t)\mathcal{O}_{\beta}^{\dagger}(0)\rangle.

(6)

with
\mathcal{O}\in S_{0(2)}
.

We present the following arguments regarding the contributions of these diagrams:

•

Diagrams (a) and (b): These two diagrams do not have annihilation effects of charm quarks and represent the time propagation of
c\bar{c}c\bar{c}
systems.

•

Diagram (d): This diagram illustrates the effect of the double annihilation charm quark and antiquark, whose contribution is doubly suppressed by the OZI rule and is neglected in the practical calculation. Actually, annihilation diagram is usually ignored even in the calculation of charmonium correlation function.

•

Diagram (e): Since charm quark is heavy, the propagation of
c\bar{c}c\bar{c}
is suppressed roughly by
e^{-m_{\eta_{c}}t}
relative to the
c\bar{c}
propagation. So in the large
t
region, this diagram reflects the propagator of
\chi_{c0}
.

•

Diagram (c): Similar to diagram (e), this this diagram also reflects the propagator of
\chi_{c0}
in the large
t
region.

These arguments are checked by the calculation of the correlation functions
C_{\alpha,\beta_{0}}(t)
on the L16M420 ensemble, where
\beta_{0}
refers to
\mathcal{O}_{\chi_{c0}}
and
\alpha

\mathcal{S}

Figure

C_{\alpha\beta_{0}}(t)

E^{\mathrm{eff}}(t)=\frac{1}{a_{t}}\cosh^{-1}\left[\frac{C(t+a_{t})+C(t-a_{t})}{2C(t)}\right].

(7)

It is seen that all the
E^{\mathrm{eff}}(t)

C_{\beta_{0},\beta_{0}}(t)

C_{\alpha\beta_{0}}(t)
are obtained by two-mass-term fits, whose values are also shown in the figure, where one can see that they are very close to the
\chi_{c0}

m_{\chi_{c0}}=3.453(2)

The key task of the calculation is to reliably extract the finite-volume energy levels relevant to
\eta_{c}\eta_{c}-J/\psi J/\psi
scattering in the center-of-energy range of 5.9–6.6 GeV. The state-of-art method is to solve the generalized eigenvalue problem to the correlation matrix
C_{\alpha\beta}(t)
of the operator set
\mathcal{S}_{0}
, namely,

C_{\alpha\beta}(t)v_{\beta}^{(n)}=\lambda^{(n)}(t,t_{0})C_{\alpha\beta}(t_{0})v_{\beta}^{(n)},

(8)

where the eigenvectors
v_{\alpha}^{(n)}
determine the optimized operators
\mathcal{O}^{(n)}=v_{\alpha}^{(n)}\mathcal{O}_{\alpha}
.
However, if the
\mathcal{O}_{\chi_{c0}}
operator is included and the contribution from diagrams (c,e,d) is considered, the signals of
C_{\alpha\beta}(t)
are dominated by the contribution from
\chi_{c0}
for the reasons addressed above.
In this case, the energies of di-charmonium states cannot be derived properly.
Since the mass of
\chi_{c0}
is far below the energy region we are interested in, with the assumption that
\chi_{c0}
is almost disentangled from the di-charmonium systems, we discard
\mathcal{O}_{\chi_{c0}}
in the operator set
\mathcal{S}_{0}
and also ignore the contribution from diagram (c) in the practical calculation.
From now on, we refer
\mathcal{S}_{0}
to the operator set that includes only the two-particle operators, whose correlation matrix is still denoted by
C_{\alpha\beta}(t)
.

can be calculated directly from
C_{\alpha\beta}(t)
, namely,

C_{mn}(t)=\langle\mathcal{O}^{(m)}(t)\mathcal{O}^{(n)}(0)\rangle\equiv v_{\alpha}^{(m)}v_{\beta}^{(n)}C_{\alpha\beta}(t).

(11)

In order to check the orthogonality of the optimized operators
\mathcal{O}^{(n)}
, we also introduce a weight function
w_{mn}(t)
similar to
Eq. (

)

w_{mn}(t)=\frac{C_{mn}(t)}{\sqrt{C_{mm}(t)C_{nn}(t)}}.

(12)

As shown in the right two panels in Fig.

(

) have very tiny values (
(1\sim 2)\times 10^{-3}
) uniformly in comparison with the diagonal elements

.
This indicates the optimized operator set
\{\mathcal{O}^{(n)},n=1,2,\ldots,10\}
based on
\mathcal{S}_{0}
is almost an orthogonal and complete set, which facilitate us to extract the corresponding energy levels reliably.

One further point should be highlighted here: we have attempted to include additional operators with higher relative momentum modes, but found that they do not improve the extraction of higher energy levels.
For instance, the inclusion of the additional operator
\mathcal{O}_{\eta_{c}\eta_{c}}(n^{2}=5)
in

reduces the orthogonality of the optimized operator set on the L16M420 ensemble, as shown in Fig.

II.4
N_{V}
dependence of LHS smearing

The distillation method provides a quark field smearing (LHS smearing) scheme

c(\vec{x})=\sum\limits_{\vec{y}}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N_{V}}V_{i}^{\dagger}(\vec{x})V_{i}(\vec{y})\tilde{c}(\vec{y})\equiv\int d^{3}\vec{y}\Phi(\vec{x},\vec{y})\tilde{c}(\vec{y}),

(13)

where
V_{i}(\vec{x})
is the
i
-th eigenvector of the lattice gauge invariant Laplacian operator and
\tilde{c}
stands for the unsmeared charm quark field.
Previous lattice studies observe that the smear function behaves like the Gaussian smearing
\Phi(\vec{x},\vec{y}\sim e^{-\sigma r^{2}}/2)
with
r=|\vec{x}-\vec{y}|
with
\sigma^{\prime}
varying with
N_{V}
, so the LHS smeared charm quark field
c(\vec{x})
has a Gaussian spatial distribution around
\vec{x}
.
Consequently, the operator
\mathcal{O}_{M}(\vec{x})=[\bar{c}\Gamma_{M}c]
has the following property

\mathcal{O}_{M}(\vec{x})\sim\int d^{3}\vec{y}e^{-\sigma|\vec{y}-\vec{x}|^{2}}\mathcal{O}^{0}_{M}(\vec{y}),

(14)

where
\mathcal{O}_{M}^{0}(\vec{y})=[\bar{\tilde{c}}\Gamma\tilde{c}](\vec{y})
is the local operator at
\vec{y}
.
The operator
\mathcal{O}_{M}(\vec{k})
with a definite momentum
\vec{k}
is obtained from the Fourier transformation

\mathcal{O}_{M}(\vec{k})=\frac{1}{V_{3}}\int d^{3}\vec{x}e^{-i\vec{k}\cdot\vec{x}}\mathcal{O}_{M}(\vec{x})\propto e^{\frac{k^{2}}{4\sigma}}\mathcal{O}_{M}^{0}(\vec{k}).

(15)

where
V_{3}
is the spatial volume.
For
\eta_{c}
and
J/\psi
, the operator couplings are written as

\displaystyle\langle 0|\mathcal{O}_{\eta_{c}}(\vec{k})|\eta_{c},\vec{k}\rangle

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle Z_{P}

\displaystyle\langle 0|\mathcal{O}_{\psi}^{i}(\vec{k})|J/\psi,\vec{k},\lambda\rangle

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle Z_{V}\epsilon_{i}(\vec{k},\lambda),

(16)

where
\vec{\epsilon}(\vec{k},\lambda)
is the polarization vector of
J/\psi
, and
Z_{P}
and
Z_{V}
are constant independent of
\vec{k}
.
Thus Eq. (
15
) indicates that the coupling of the LHS smeared operator
\mathcal{O}_{M}(\vec{k})
to a state of momentum
\vec{k}
is suppressed by a Gaussian factor
e^{-k^{2}/(4\sigma)}
.
This has been verified to be correct in the actual calculations.
As usual, the correlation function of
\mathcal{O}_{M}(\vec{k})
is parameterized as

C_{M}(\vec{k},t)=|Z_{k}|^{2}\frac{{N_{s}}^{3}}{2E_{k}}e^{-E_{k}t}~~~(t/a_{t}\gg 1),

(17)

where the factor
N_{s}^{3}
is due to fact that both the sink and source operators are smeared.
The ratios
Z_{k}/Z_{0}
of
\eta_{c}
and
J/\psi
on ensembles L12M420 and L16M420 (and L24M420) are plotted as points with respect to
k^{2}
(in physical units) in the upper (
\eta_{c}
) and lower (
J/\psi
) panels of Fig.
7
, where the ratios are derived at different
N_{V}=(70,120,170)
and the curves show the Gaussian function
e^{-k^{2}/(4\sigma)}
with estimated values of
\sigma
(not serious fits) at different
N_{V}
.

Figure 7:
The spectral weight
Z_{n}/Z_{0}
for
C^{(n)}(t)=|Z_{n}|^{2}\frac{{N_{s}}^{3}}{2E_{n}^{M}}e^{-E^{M}_{n}t}
of
\eta_{c}(n^{2})
and

varying

values, on three-size M420 ensembles.
As the momentum
k^{2}
increases, the spectral weight exhibits exponential suppression, given by
Z_{n}\sim Z_{0}\,e^{-\frac{k^{2}}{4\sigma}}
.

From the plots one can get the following information:

•

For the same number of
N_{V}
on the same lattice, the LHS smearing effect is the same for the operators of
\eta_{c}
and
J/\psi
. The momentum suppression can be well approximated by a Gaussion profile
e^{-k^{2}/(4\sigma)}
.

•

On the same lattice, the momentum suppression becomes stronger when
N_{V}
decreases.

•

With the same
N_{V}
, the larger the lattice size, the stronger the momentum suppression. For the L24M420 ensemble, even with
N_{V}
the coupling to the state of
k^{2}\sim 2\,\mathrm{GeV}^{2}
is suppressed to be
Z_{k}/Z_{0}\sim 0.1
.

•

For the correlation functions
C_{\alpha\alpha}(t)
(the diagonal elements of the correlation matrix), its magnitude scales approximately as
|Z_{k_{\alpha}}/Z_{0}|^{4}
with
k^{2}
. Since the upper bound of the energy range we are considering corresponds to the momentum
k_{\alpha}^{2}\sim 2\,\mathrm{GeV}^{2}
, this scale factor takes the approximate values
\sim 0.7^{4},0.3^{4},0.1^{4}
for

, respectively.

Our computing resources are sufficient for the calculation with
(N_{s},N_{v})=(12,170)
or
(16,120)
, which are the practical parameters in this study.

We now turn to the discussion of the completeness of the operator set
\mathcal{S}_{0}(N_{s}=16,N_{V}=120)
.
If the correlation matrix
C_{\alpha\beta}(t)
contains only the information of
N
eigenstates
\{|n\rangle,n=1,2,\ldots,N\}
of the lattice Hamiltonian of the system in a specific time range, where the lattice data of
C_{\alpha\beta}(t)
have good enough signal-to-noise ratios, then one needs only
N
linearly independent operators that satisfy the relation

\mathcal{O}_{\alpha}|0\rangle=\sum\limits_{n}|n\rangle\langle n|\mathcal{O}_{\alpha}|0\rangle\equiv\sum\limits_{n}C_{\alpha}^{n}|n\rangle,

(18)

with
\det\mathbf{C}\equiv\det\{C_{n}^{\alpha}\}\neq 0
. These operator establish a complete operator set
\mathcal{S}=\{\mathcal{O}_{\alpha},\alpha=1,2,\ldots,N\}
, such that one can obtain
N
optimized operators
\mathcal{O}^{(n)}=(\mathbf{C}^{-1})_{\alpha}^{n}\mathcal{O}_{\alpha}
with each operator
\mathcal{O}^{(n)}
coupling exclusively to
|n\rangle
, namely,

|n\rangle=(\mathbf{C}^{-1})_{\alpha}^{n}\mathcal{O}_{\alpha}|0\rangle.

(19)

If one more operator is added to
\mathcal{S}
, it must be linearly dependent to other operators and does not provide information for additional states.
This is exactly the observation in Figs.
4
and
6
when an operator
\mathcal{O}_{\eta_{c}\eta_{c}}(n^{2}=5)
is added to
\mathcal{O}_{0}
on L16 ensembles.

II.6
Energy level determination

After the GEVP analysis to the correlation matrix
C_{\alpha\beta}(t)
of these operator set on each gauge ensemble with specific values of
N_{V}
, we obtain the the correlation functions
C_{nn}(t)
of the optimized operators
\mathcal{O}^{(n)}
, each of which is expected have predominantly the contribution from the
n
-th energy eigenstate.

The correlation functions
C_{nn}(t)
maintain a good signal-to-noise ratio throughout the entire time range, and facilitate the data analysis for extracting the energy eigenvalues

E_{n}
over wide time windows.
In two-particle systems, the periodic boundary condition permits the two particles either to propagate alongside from the source timeslice (
t=0
for example) to a sink timeslice
t<T/2
, or to propagate independently with one particle running from
0
to
t
and the other running in the opposite time direction from
0
to
t
around the the time circle.
The later propagation is commonly referred to as the thermal state.
Given that the two particles have energies
E
and
E^{\prime}
, the time evolution of the thermal state can be expressed as

\displaystyle C_{\mathrm{thm}}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A\left[e^{-Et}e^{-E^{\prime}(T-t)}+e^{-E(T-t)}e^{-E^{\prime}t}\right]

(21)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2Ae^{-(E+E^{\prime})\frac{T}{2}}\sinh\left[(E-E^{\prime})\frac{T}{2}\right].

where
A
is a overall factor from the operator coupling. For a system of two identical particles in its rest frame, the condition
E=E^{\prime}
holds, which leads to a constant

C_{\mathrm{thm}}(t)
.
This is the scenario under consideration.

Both the two ways of propagation contribute to
C_{nn}(t)
and the contribution from the thermal state becomes more pronounced when
t\to T/2
.
Since we need to extract the energy of the lowest state contributing to
C_{nn}(t)
in the large
t
range where the contamination of higer states can be negligible, the contribution from the thermal state should be considered.
We adopt the following function form to parameterize
C_{nn}(t)

\displaystyle C^{(n)}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~W\cosh\left[E_{n}(t-\frac{T}{2})\right]+W_{\mathrm{thm}}

(22)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle W^{\prime}\cosh\left[E_{n}^{\prime}(t-\frac{T}{2})\right],

where the first term accounts for the contribution of the
n
-th eigen state of the lattice Hamiltonian, the second term (a constant) comes from the thermal state, and the third term is added to absorb the residual contamination from higher states.
This method is referred to as ‘3-state’ fitting in the following.

Figure
9
shows the plots of the effective energy functions of
\{C_{nn}(t),n=1,2,\ldots,10\}
in the
{}^{1}S_{0}
channel and
\{C_{nn}(t),n=1,2,\ldots,8\}
in the
{}^{5}S_{2}
channel, respectively, which is defined by

a_{t}\,E_{n}^{(\mathrm{eff})}(t)=\cosh^{-1}\left[\frac{C_{nn}(t-a_{t})+C_{nn}(t+a_{t})}{2C_{nn}(t)}\right].

(23)

The upper-left, upper-right, lower-left, lower-right panels of the
0^{++}
or
2^{++}
system are for the ensembles L12M420 (
N_{V}=170)
), L16M420 (
N_{V}=120
), L12M250 (
N_{V}=170
) and L16M250 (
N_{V}=120
), respectively.
The data points correspond to the lattice simulation results, and the colored bands illustrate the fits based on Eq. (
22
).
The dips around
t=T/2
due to the thermal states manifest the importance of their contribution.
It is seen that Eq. (
22
) describes all the correlation functions
C_{nn}(t)
very well.
The inset in each panel shows a magnified view of the
t
-dependence of
E_{n}^{(\mathrm{eff})}
, highlighting the robustness of the fits.

An alternative way to extract the energy levels of a two-particle system is the so-called ‘ratio method’ proposed by Refs.

(24)

where

M

M

III
Scattering Analysis

The state-of-arts technique for the lattice QCD study on hadron-hadron scatterings is the Lüscher’s formalism, which establishes the connection between the finite volume energy levels of the interested two-body systems derived in Euclidean spacetime lattice and the related
scattering matrix of several coupled channels through the quantization conditions

\det[\mathbf{I}+i\mathbf{\rho}(E_{\mathrm{cm}})\mathbf{t}(E_{\mathrm{cm}})(\mathbf{I}-i\mathcal{M}(E_{\mathrm{cm}},L)]=0,

(27)

where
E_{\mathrm{cm}}
is the center-of-mass energy of the system,
\rho(E_{\mathrm{cm}})
is a diagonal matrix in the channel space with each diagonal element being the phase space factor of a channel,
\mathbf{t}
is the scattering matrix at
E_{\mathrm{cm}}
, and
\mathcal{M}(E_{\mathrm{cm}},L)
is a known mathematical function matrix and depends on the lattice size
L
and
E_{\mathrm{cm}}
.
Therefore, if an appropriate parameterization is chosen for
\mathbf{t}
, the parameters of
\mathbf{t}
can be determined by fitting Eq. (
27
) to a set of
E_{\mathrm{cm}}
values obtained from lattice QCD calculations.
The unitarity condition of
S
-matrix requires
\operatorname{Im}\,(\mathbf{t}^{-1})=-i\rho
when
E_{\mathrm{cm}}
is larger than the related threshold energy.
After analytic continuation to the complex plane of the invariant mass squared
s
, the
K

\eta_{c}\eta_{c}
and

•

\eta_{c}\eta_{c}

E_{n}
is closest to the corresponding non-interacting energy

one-by-one within errors (Except for
E_{4}
and

J/\psi J/\psi
operators does not affect the values of
E_{n}
of the
\eta_{c}\eta_{c}

The correspondence between energy levels before and after the variation is examined in Fig.

When comparing the

(and
E_{n}^{\prime}

\eta_{c}\eta_{c}
energies with the corresponding non-interacting energies
E_{n}^{(0)}
, one can see that
E_{n}(E_{n}^{\prime})\gtrsim E_{n}^{(0)}
in the energy region below 6.6 GeV, which hints at a repulsive interaction in the low energy range near the
\eta_{c}\eta_{c}

•

Then we perform the GEVP analysis to the the correlation matrix of the five
J/\psi J/\psi
operators and obtain five new energy levels

E_{n}

E_{n}
and
E_{n}^{\prime\prime}

\eta_{c}\eta_{c}
operators to derive the energies of
J/\psi J/\psi
system. This is due to the reason that the
J/\psi J/\psi
energies are higher than those of
\eta_{c}\eta_{c}
. In order for the higher energy levels to be determined reliably, one must determined the lower energy levels first. In contrast with the
\eta_{c}\eta_{c}
case, the
J/\psi J/\psi
energies
E_{n}
are uniformly lower than the corresponding non-interacting energies
E_{n}^{(0)}
and hint at an attractive interaction.

The observation that the
\eta_{c}\eta_{c}
energy are affected little by the inclusion of
J/\psi J/\psi
implies the coupled channel effect can be weak, so we perform the single-channel analysis on
\eta_{c}\eta_{c}
scattering and
J/\psi J/\psi
scattering first. Throughout this work, we take the convention
\rho(s)=\frac{k}{\sqrt{s}}
after considering the 1/2 factor owing to the two identical particles in each channel.

For the
S
-wave single-channel two-body scattering, the scattering amplitude
t
can be expressed in terms of the scattering phase
\delta_{0}
as

t(s)=\frac{\sqrt{s}}{k\cot\delta_{0}(k)-ik},

(29)

where
k
is the magnitude of the scattering momentum defined through the center-of-mass
\sqrt{s}=E_{\mathrm{cm}}

E_{\mathrm{cm}}=2\sqrt{m^{2}+k^{2}}

(30)

for the two particles having the same mass
m
.
On the finite volume lattice with size
L
, one can introduce a dimensionless quantity
q^{2}=(L^{2}/(2\pi)^{2})k^{2}
for convenience.
The Lüscher’s quantization condition in Eq. (
27
) is simplified as

k\cot\delta_{0}(k)=k\mathcal{M}_{0000}(q^{2},L)=\frac{2}{L\sqrt{\pi}}\mathcal{Z}_{00}(1,q^{2}),

(31)

where
\mathcal{Z}_{00}
is the shifted Zeta function

\mathcal{Z}_{00}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi}}\sum\limits_{\vec{n}\in Z^{3}}\frac{1}{\vec{n}^{2}-q^{2}}.

(32)

The scattering phase
\delta_{0}(k)
can be determined using Eq. (
31
) based on the energy levels derived from lattice QCD.
For low-energy scatterings, the scattering phase
k\cot\delta_{0}(k)
are usually parametrized through the effective range expansion (ERE)

k\cot\delta_{0}(k)=\frac{1}{a_{0}}+\frac{1}{2}r_{0}k^{2}+\mathcal{O}(k^{4}),

(33)

where
a_{0}
is the
S
-wave scattering length and
r_{0}
is the effective range.
The scattering phase
k\cot\delta_{0}(k)
of
\eta_{c}\eta_{c}
are calculated up to
E_{\mathrm{cm}}=6.6
GeV and are shown in the upper two panels (left for M420 and right for M250) in Fig.
13
, where the blue and red points represent lattice data on the L12 and L16 lattices, respectively, and clearly lie on the same straight line. The ERE fits are also illustrated by the green bands in Fig.
13
.

seems having two branches:
k\cot\delta_{0}(k)

Obviously, this behavior signals a possible pole structure and cannot be described by the ERE function form.
Therefore, we adopt the
K
-matrix formalism to parametrize the scattering amplitude
t(s)

t^{-1}(s)=K^{-1}(s)-i\rho(s)

(37)

with
K(s)
being a real function of

k\cot\delta_{0}(k)=\sqrt{s}K^{-1}(s).

(38)

As an initial attempt, we adopt the widely used sum-of-poles form for
K(s)
, which in this case includes a single pole term

K(s)=\frac{g_{0}^{2}}{m_{0}^{2}-s}+\gamma,

(39)

where the bare coupling

and

It should be noted that the fit is performed using the energy levels under

In the detailed calculation, it is found that the fitted parameters
g_{0}^{2},m_{0}^{2},\gamma

IV
Discussion

Having presented the numerical results, we make discussions on the physical implications and explore the possible dynamics for di-charmonium scatterings which is reflected by the lattice QCD data.
Since the
\eta_{c}\eta_{c}
and
J/\psi J/\psi
coupled channel effects are observed to be negligible, we focus on the single-channel scatterings.

IV.1
\eta_{c}\eta_{c}
scatterings

As shown in Table
2
and Table
3
, the
\eta_{c}\eta_{c}
energies in the
(0,2)^{++}
channels are uniformly higher than the corresponding non-interacting energies (except for the
n^{2}=4
energies, which are the highest ones in our analysis and are unreliable owing to the the contamination from even higher states and the momentum suppression discussed in Sec.
II.4
and Sec.
II.7
).
In the meaning time, the scattering analysis gives the scattering length
a_{0}\approx-0.12
fm for the
{}^{1}S_{0}

\eta_{c}\eta_{c}
scattering.
These observations suggest that the
\eta_{c}\eta_{c}
system exhibits a repulsive interaction in both the
{}^{1}S_{0}
and
{}^{1}D_{2}
channels.
It is intriguing to explore the intrinsic dynamics responsible for this repulsive interaction.

The
\eta_{c}\eta_{c}
correlation functions include the contributions from the the ‘Direct’ diagram (a) and the ‘quark recombination’ diagram (b) in Fig.
2
.
On the hadron level, diagram (a) reflects the color singlet gluonic interaction between two
\eta_{c}
mesons, which can be described either by the Pomeron exchange mechanism
Gong et al. 2022b
or the light meson exchange in the presence of sea quarks.
Similarly, diagram (b) represents either the charmonium exchange effect or the quark rearrangement effect.
In order to analyze the nature of the near-threshold interaction, we consider the correlation function
C_{11}(t)
from the correlation matrix
C_{\alpha\beta(t)}
in the
0^{++}
channel, where the operator
\mathcal{O}_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{S}_{0}
with
\alpha=1
is the
\eta_{c}\eta_{c}
operator with zero relative momentum (namely,
n^{2}=0
).
The correlation
C_{11}(t)
can be expressed as

C_{11}(t)=D(t)+Q(t)\equiv D^{(0)}(t)+D^{(1)}(t)+Q(t)

(49)

where
D(t)
and
Q(t)
represent the contribution from the direct diagram (a) and quark rearrangement diagram (b), respectively;
D(t)=D^{(0)}(t)+D^{(1)}(t)
has been defined with
D^{(0)}(t)
being the propagator of two non-interacting
\eta_{c}
and
D^{(1)}(t)\ll D^{(0)}(t)
referring the gluonic interacting part.
When only
D(t)
is included in
C_{11}(t)
, we obtain the energy of
\eta_{c}\eta_{c}

E_{1}<2m_{\eta_{c}}
, which signals an attractive interaction.
When both
D(t)
and
Q(t)
are included, we find that
E_{1}>2m_{\eta_{c}}
.
This indicates that the repulsive interaction reflected by diagram (b) overcomes the attractive gluonic exchange mechanism (diagram (a)).

To illustrate this clearly, we introduce the ratio functions

\frac{D^{(1)}(t)}{D^{(0)}(t)}\approx\epsilon_{D}e^{-\delta_{D}t},~~~\frac{Q(t)}{D^{(0)}(t)}\approx\epsilon_{Q}e^{-\delta_{Q}t},

(50)

with
\delta_{C,D}
being the energy shift from
2m_{\eta_{c}}
, and plot them accordingly in the upper panel of Fig.
20
.
One can easily read out

\displaystyle\delta_{D}<0,

\displaystyle~~~\epsilon_{D}\sim\mathcal{O}(10^{-3}),

\displaystyle\delta_{Q}<0,

\displaystyle~~~\epsilon_{Q}\sim-\mathcal{O}(10^{-2})

(51)

and the slopes indicate that
|\delta_{Q}|\sim|\delta_{D}|\ll 1
.

Figure 20:
Ratio function
C_{11}(t)/\big(C_{\eta_{c}}(n^{2}=0,\,t)C_{\eta_{c}}(n^{2}=0,\,t)\big)
in the
{}^{1}S_{0}

\eta_{c}\eta_{c}
system (left), and
C_{33}(t)/\big(C_{J/\psi}(n^{2}=0,\,t)C_{J/\psi}(n^{2}=0,\,t)\big)
in the
{}^{1}S_{0}

J/\psi J/\psi
system (right).

Following the strategy in Ref.
Chen et al. 2022
, we have

\displaystyle E_{1}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{1}{a_{t}}\ln\frac{C_{11}(t)}{C_{11}(t+a_{t})}

(52)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle 2m_{\eta_{c}}+\epsilon_{D}\delta_{D}e^{\delta_{D}t}+\epsilon_{Q}\delta_{Q}e^{\delta_{Q}t}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle 2m_{\eta_{c}}+\epsilon_{D}\delta_{D}+\epsilon_{Q}\delta_{Q},

It is evident from this equation that when diagram (b) is neglected (
\epsilon_{Q}=0
), one has
E_{1}-2m_{\eta_{c}}=\epsilon_{D}\delta_{D}<0
and therefore the attractive interaction.
When diagram (b) is considered,
\epsilon_{Q}\delta_{Q}>0
and
|\epsilon_{Q}|\sim 10|\epsilon_{D}|
result in the totally repulsive interaction (
E_{1}-2m_{\eta_{c}}>0
).

IV.2
{}^{1}S_{0}

J/\psi J/\psi
scattering

The
{}^{1}S_{0}

J/\psi J/\psi
scattering exhibits an attractive interaction near the threshold.
We carry out a similar analysis for this case, as the ratio functions are shown in the lower panel of Fig.
20
.
The condition
\epsilon_{Q}>0
now holds, providing a key explanation for the dominant mechanism responsible for the attractive interaction in this channel.
The sign difference between
D(t)
and
Q(t)
in the
\eta_{c}\eta_{c}
channel arises from the differing numbers of quark loops: diagram (a) involves two quark loops, whereas diagram (b) involves only one.
Since each quark loop contributes a minus sign, this accounts for the overall sign discrepancy.
The same signs of
D(t)
and
Q(t)
in the
J/\psi J/\psi
case arise from the Fierz rearrangement.
For the convenience within the constituent quark picture, we label the quark configurations of the initial state produced by the source operator at
t=0
to be
\bar{c}_{1}\Gamma c_{2}\bar{c}_{3}\Gamma c_{4}
.
Then diagram (b) describes the process from the initial configuration to the final configuration
\bar{c}_{1}\Gamma c_{4}\bar{c}_{3}\Gamma c_{2}
.
For the
\eta_{c}\eta_{c}
system, one has
\Gamma=\gamma_{5}
.
The Fierz rearrangement relates the initial and the final configurations (not fermion fields) by

\bar{c}_{1}\gamma_{5}c_{2}\bar{c}_{3}\gamma_{5}c_{4}\to\frac{1}{4}\times\bar{c}_{1}\gamma_{5}c_{4}\bar{c}_{3}\gamma_{5}c_{2},

(53)

where there is no additional sign.
In contrast, for the
J/\psi J/\psi
system, the Fierz transformation gives

\bar{c}_{1}\gamma_{\mu}c_{2}\bar{c}_{3}\gamma^{\mu}c_{4}\to-\frac{1}{2}\times\bar{c}_{1}\gamma_{\mu}c_{4}\bar{c}_{3}\gamma^{\mu}c_{2},

(54)

where the minus sign here cancels the additional minus sign in diagram (a) due to the one more quark loop.
Intuitively, the dynamics behind can be attributed in part to the one-gluon exchange (OGE) between the two interchanging (anti)quarks, see the schematic illustrations in Fig.
21
.

Figure 21:
Schematic illustrations of one-gluon exchange between the interchanging (anti)quarks.

The attractive interaction can also be interpreted as Yukawa-type, mediated by the exchange of
0^{++}
charmonium (such as the
\chi_{c0}
and its excited states).
However, these contributions are found to be very short-ranged and are highly suppressed due to the large masses of the charmonia.

For the purpose of a purely qualitative discussion, the initial and final quark pairs
c^{i}c^{j}
are treated as free particles.
The scattering takes place through the
u
-channel process and the corresponding amplitude of the
(ij\to ij)
is

i\mathcal{M}_{ij}^{ij}=\frac{(-)ig_{s}^{2}}{(p_{i}-k_{j})^{2}}t^{a}_{ji}t^{a}_{ij}\bar{u}_{s}(p_{i})\gamma^{\mu}u_{s^{\prime}}(k_{j})\bar{u}_{t}(p_{j})\gamma^{\mu}u_{t^{\prime}}(k_{i})

(55)

where
g_{s}
is the strong coupling constant and the extra minus sign is due to the Dirac statistics in
u
-channel. In the non-relativistic limit,
this amplitude gives an attractive interaction potential

V(r)\propto-\frac{g_{s}^{2}}{4\pi r}t^{a}_{ji}t^{a}_{ij}\times\frac{1}{9}=-\frac{4\alpha_{s}}{9r},

(56)

where
\alpha_{s}=g_{s}^{2}/(4\pi)
and the fractor
1/9
comes from the color wave functions of the four charmonia (Note that the
t
-channel gluon exchange gives a repulsive potential between quark and quark).

Figure
20
shows that the quark rearrangement effect dominates the di-charmonium interaction over the gluonic exchange.
If the one-gluon-exchange mechanism describes the inter-charmnoium interaction to some extent, then we can make some predictions on other di-charmonium scatterings.
The Fierz rearrangement also gives

\displaystyle\bar{c}_{1}c_{2}\bar{c}_{3}c_{4}

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\times\bar{c}_{1}c_{4}\bar{c}_{3}c_{2},

\displaystyle\bar{c}_{1}\gamma_{5}\gamma^{\mu}c_{2}\bar{c}_{3}\gamma_{5}\gamma_{\mu}c_{4}

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\times\bar{c}_{1}\gamma_{5}\gamma^{\mu}c_{4}\bar{c}_{3}\gamma_{5}\gamma_{\mu}c_{2},

\displaystyle\bar{c}_{1}\sigma^{\mu\nu}c_{2}\bar{c}_{3}\sigma_{\mu\nu}c_{4}

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\times\bar{c}_{1}\sigma^{\mu\nu}c_{4}\bar{c}_{3}\sigma_{\mu\nu}c_{2},

(57)

so it is expected that the
\chi_{c0}\chi_{c0}
interaction can be repulsive and similar to
\eta_{c}\eta_{c}
case, while
S
-wave
h_{c}h_{c}
(corresponding to
\sigma_{\mu\nu}
) and
\chi_{c1}\chi_{c1}
interactions are similar to the
J/\psi J/\psi
case and are attractive.

In principle, this attractive interaction should result in pole singularities near the di-
J/\psi
threshold, which can be bound state, virtual state or resonance poles.
As we have presented in Sec.
III.1
, the ERE of the
{}^{1}S_{0}

J/\psi J/\psi
scattering phase indicates there seems exist a virtual state below the di-
J/\psi
threshold with the binding energy
\Delta E=E-2m_{J/\psi}
being -28(10) MeV for M420 and 38(20) MeV for M250.
This virtual state should result in a near-threshold enhancement in the
J/\psi J/\psi
production cross section.
A theoretical coupled-channel analysis of the LHCb di-
J/\psi
spectrum hints at a state
X(6200)
near the threshold
Dong et al. 2021b
.
The
J/\psi J/\psi-J/\psi\psi(2S)
coupled channel fit allows for a virtual state, resonance, or bound state, while the
J/\psi J/\psi-J/\psi\psi(2S)-J/\psi\psi(3770)
three channel fit gives a bound state at
E=6.163_{-32}^{+18}
GeV or a shallow virtual state (at
6.189_{-10}^{+5}
GeV) or a bound state (in the energy range
[6.159,6.194]
GeV).
The similar analysis has been extended to the di-
J/\psi
spectrum from CMS and ATLAS and confirms the existence of
X(6200)

Song et al. 2025
.
In these studies,
X(6200)
can have the quantum numbers of
0^{++}
or
2^{++}
.
The results in this work suggest the near-threshold
X(6200)
to be a
{}^{1}S_{0}

J/\psi J/\psi
state, if it does exist.

IV.3
{}^{5}S_{2}

J/\psi J/\psi
scattering

We have observed an attractive interaction in the
{}^{1}S_{0}

J/\psi J/\psi
channel.
However, in the
{}^{5}S_{2}
channel, there is a repulsive interaction near the di-
J/\psi
threshold, characterized by a negative scattering length
a_{0}\approx-0.16
fm, as shown in Fig.
16
and Table
3
.
We follow the OGE mechanism to understand this difference.
In the non-relativistic limit, the bispinor in Eq. (
55
) can be simplified as

\bar{u}_{s}(p_{i})\gamma^{0}u_{s^{\prime}}(k_{j})\approx 2m_{c}\zeta_{s}^{\dagger}\zeta_{s^{\prime}}=2m_{c}\delta_{ss^{\prime}},

(58)

where
m_{c}
is the charm quark mass,
\zeta_{s}
is the a spinor.
This means that OGE does not change the spin state of the interchanging charm quarks.
Consequently, the spin configuration of the initial di-charmonium system has a definite relation to that of the final state after the quark rearrangement.
Let
|SM_{S}\rangle
and
|1m\rangle
be the spin state of the
J/\psi J/\psi
and
J/\psi
, respectively.

V
Summary

We investigate
\eta_{c}\eta_{c}
and
J/\psi J/\psi
scatterings in
N_{f}=2
lattice QCD at two pion masses
m_{\pi}\approx 420\,(250)
MeV.
At each
m_{\pi}
we have two lattice volumes to obtain the finite volume energy levels as many as possible, which are mandatory for the scattering properties to be derived reliably.
The distillation method is adopted to calculate charm quark propagators and the related correlation functions.

In the numerical technique sector, it is found the LHS smearing built in the distillation method causes strong suppression of the operator coupling to high momentum states, and puts strong a limit to the di-charmonium energies that we can access.
On the other hand, the high energy levels suffer from the
N_{V}
dependence with
N_{V}
being the dimension of the LHS subspace.
Therefore, on each lattice we use several
N_{V}
’s to check the reliability of lattice energy levels.
Finally, we confirm that the center-of-mass energy can be accessed up to 6.6 GeV.

In the
J^{PC}=0^{++}
channel, the analysis reveals negligible coupled-channel effects between the
{}^{1}S_{0}

\eta_{c}\eta_{c}
and
{}^{1}S_{0}

J/\psi J/\psi
channels.
Accordingly, single-channel scattering analyses are conducted separately for each system.
The
0^{++}

\eta_{c}\eta_{c}
system exhibits a repulsive interaction, while the
J/\psi J/\psi
(
{}^{1}S_{0}
) system have a attractive interaction.
The quark rearrangement effect plays a dominant role for the di-charmonium interaction over the direct gluonic exchange between the two scattering charmonia.
The former can be understood by the one-gluon-exchange mechanism, and the different interactions of
\eta_{c}\eta_{c}
and
J/\psi J/\psi
is due to the Fierz rearrangement.
The phase shift of
{}^{1}S_{0}

J/\psi J/\psi
scattering hints the existence of a virtual bound state below the di-
J/\psi
threshold by 20-30 MeV, which is compatible with
X(6200)
that is obtained through the coupled channel analysis on
J/\psi J/\psi
spectrum by LHCb.
Our results support
X(6200)
to be a
0^{++}
state if it exists.

In the
J^{PC}=2^{++}
channel, the
{}^{1}D_{2}

\eta_{c}\eta_{c}
scattering decouples completely from the
{}^{5}S_{2}

J/\psi J/\psi
scattering.
We carry out the single channel analysis to the
{}^{5}S_{2}

J/\psi J/\psi
channel.
In contrast to the
{}^{1}S_{0}
case, the
{}^{5}S_{2}

J/\psi J/\psi
shows repulsive interaction near the threshold which disfavors the existence of a near-threshold state like
X(6200)
.
The differing characteristics of
J/\psi J/\psi
interactions in these two channels are further explained by one-gluon-exchange dynamics.
In the non-relativistic limit, this dynamics predicts that the relative strength of the
{}^{5}S_{2}
channel to the
{}^{1}S_{0}
channel is -2, a prediction confirmed by our lattice calculations.

A
2^{++}
resonance (
R
) is observed in the
{}^{5}S_{2}

J/\psi J/\psi
scattering channel with properties of
(m_{R},\,\Gamma_{R})\approx\big(6540(13),\,540(60)\big)
MeV, consistent with the broad structure
X(6600)
(or
X(6400)
) reported by ATLAS and CMS.
It is noted that the angular distribution of the
4\mu
decay products of these states measured by CMS favors the
2^{++}
quantum numbers and excludes the
0^{++}
assignment at more than 95% confidence level.

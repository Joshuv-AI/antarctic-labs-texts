High frequency transcranial pulsed current stimulation improves quality of life in Parkinson’s disease: A randomized controlled trial
Authors: Zheng Li, Sandra Zhong, Lan Wang, Fengyi Hao, Wenjun Hong, Chunxia Zhao, Dongmei Ai, Zhigang Zhao, Ping Xiong, Tingting Sun, Kun Wang, Jessie Siew-Pin Leuk, Eng King Tan, Giuliana Rencricca, Esra Neufeld, Niels Kuster, Alvaro Pascual-Leone, Rong Xu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Abstract Parkinson's disease (PD) is a progressive multisystem disorder in which motor and non-motor symptoms impair quality-of-life. High-frequency transcranial pulsed current stimulation (tPCS) modulates network-level dysfunction and may serve as an adjunct therapy in PD. In a randomized controlled trial, 38 participants with Hoehn & Yahr stage I–III PD were randomized to active 400 Hz tPCS or sham for 4 weeks while receiving stable antiparkinsonian medication. Stimulation targeted bilateral dorsolateral prefrontal cortices and motor-cerebellar circuit. Primary outcome was the Parkinson's Disease Questionnaire-39 Summary Index (PDQ-39 SI); secondary outcomes included motor and non-motor measures. Thirty participants completed the study with no serious adverse events. Compared with sham, active tPCS significantly improved PDQ-39 SI (p = 0.017; partial η² = 0.19) and reduced non-motor symptom burden (NMSS, p = 0.005). High frequency tPCS was safe, well tolerated, and improved quality of life in PD, warranting investigation in larger trials.

Asymptotic Weighted Approximation of Convex Functions
Fernanda Moreira Baêta

Abstract
Extending classical results on polytopal approximation of convex bodies, we derive asymptotic formulas for the weighted approximation of smooth convex functions by piecewise affine convex functions as the number of their facets tends to infinity. These asymptotic expressions are formulated in terms of a functional that extends the notion of affine surface area to the functional setting.



1
Introduction

The
affine surface area
of a convex body (i.e., a non-empty compact convex set)
C\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is defined by

\displaystyle\Omega(C)=\int_{\bd C}\kappa_{C}(x)^{\frac{1}{n+2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n}(x),

where
\kappa_{C}
denotes the (generalized) Gaussian curvature of the convex body
C
,
\bd C
is its topological boundary, and
\mathcal{H}^{n}
is the
n
-dimensional Hausdorff measure. In the theory of polytopal approximation, the affine surface area provides valuable insight into the quality of approximation of a convex body by polytopes. Let
\delta(\cdot,\cdot)
denote the
symmetric difference metric
on the set of convex bodies
\mathcal{K}^{n+1}
, i.e.,
\delta(C,D)
for
C,D\in\mathcal{K}^{n+1}
is the
(n+1)
-dimensional volume of their symmetric difference
C\cup D\backslash C\cap D
. For a convex body
C
, let

\mathcal{P}_{(m)}^{c}=\mathcal{P}_{(m)}^{c}(C)
denote the class of all convex polytopes in
\mathcal{K}^{n+1}
that are circumscribed to the convex body
C
and have at most
m
facets. In
[
10
]
, Gruber showed that

\displaystyle\delta(C,\mathcal{P}_{(m)}^{c})\sim\frac{\delta_{n}}{2}\Omega(C)^{\frac{n+2}{n}}\frac{1}{m^{\frac{2}{n}}}

(1)

as
m\rightarrow+\infty
, provided that
C
has a boundary of differentiability class
C^{2}_{+}
. Here, the constant
\delta_{n}
depends only on the dimension
n+1
. However, its explicit value is not known for
n>3
. Gruber also obtained an analogous result for the class of all convex polytopes with at most
m
vertices in
\mathcal{K}^{n+1}
that are inscribed in
C
(see
[
10
]
).

The assumption
\kappa_{C}>0
was later removed by Böröczky
[
6
]
. Furthermore, Ludwig
[
14
]
showed that, in the case
n=2
, the requirement that
C
be of class
C^{2}
can also be omitted. In addition, Ludwig
[
13
]
established general approximation results for the symmetric difference metric without restricting the approximating polytopes to be either inscribed in or circumscribed around
C
, and also considered weighted cases. For more information, see
[
11
, Chapter 11]
.

Recently, notions from convex geometry have been adapted to functions, opening new perspectives on convex function spaces.
For a convex function
u:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to(-\infty,+\infty]
, the (effective)
domain
of
u
is defined by
\dom u=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:u(x)<+\infty\}
. Throughout,
\|x\|
denotes the Euclidean norm of
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
\interior(A)
denotes the interior of a set
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Let

\LC(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\{u:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to(-\infty,+\infty]\mid\ \dom u\text{ is a compact set, }\\
u\text{ is Lipschitz on }\interior(\dom u)\},

(2)

denote the set of convex functions
u:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to(-\infty,+\infty]
with compact domain that are Lipschitz on the interior of their domains. Note that if
\dom u
is not full-dimensional,
u
is not required to be Lipschitz. The space
\LC(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is endowed with the following notion of convergence.
For
u,u_{k}\in\LC(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we say that
u_{k}
is
\tau
-convergent to
u
if

(i)

u_{k}
epi-converges to
u
;

(ii)

the Lipschitz contants
L_{u_{k}}
of
u_{k}
are uniformly bounded by some
M>0
independent of
k
.

See Section
2
and
[
18
]
for details on epi-convergence.

Consider the following class of functions

\displaystyle\conc=\left\{\zeta:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)\mid\ \zeta\ \mbox{is concave},\ \lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\zeta(t)=0,\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\zeta(t)/t=0\right\}.

For
u\in\LC(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\zeta\in\conc
, we define the functional

\displaystyle Z_{\zeta}(u)=\int_{\dom u}\zeta(\det\D u(x))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x,

(3)

where
\det\D u(x)
is the Hessian matrix of
u
at
x
.
By a classical result of Aleksandrov
[
1
]
, convex functions are twice differentiable almost everywhere, and hence their Hessians exist almost everywhere in the domain. For the special case
\zeta(t)=t^{\frac{1}{n+2}},t\geq 0
, the functional (
3
) coincides with the affine surface area of the graph of the convex function
u\in\LC(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, with the convention that
\det\D u(x)=0
at points where
u
is not twice differentiable (see
[
17
]
). Moreover, by
[
4
, Theorem 1]
, the functional (
3
) is finite and
\tau
-upper semicontinuous, that is, if
u_{k}\in\LC(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is
\tau
-convergent to
u\in\LC(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then

\displaystyle Z_{\zeta}(u)\geq\limsup_{k\to+\infty}Z_{\zeta}(u_{k}).

We say that
\mathcal{T}
is a triangulation of a bounded set
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
if it decomposes
B
into
m
-simplices contained in
B
such that the intersection of any two simplices in
\mathcal{T}
is either a common lower-dimensional simplex or empty. Note that such a triangulation determines a set of vertices inside
B
, and the union of the simplices is not required to be equal
B
. Let
P_{m}=P_{m}(v)
denote the class of piecewise affine interpolations of
v:B\to\mathbb{R}
on a triangulation
\mathcal{T}\in\mathcal{T}_{m}
on a bounded convex set
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where
\mathcal{T}_{m}
is the set of all triangulations with at most
m
vertices, and the diameter of each element in
\mathcal{T}_{m}
tends to zero as
m\to+\infty
(see
[
8
]
for details). In
[
8
]
, Chen and Xu considered the approximation

\displaystyle\triangle_{p}(v,P_{m})=\min\left\{\int_{B}|v(x)-l_{m}(x)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x:l_{m}\in P_{m}\right\},\qquad p\geq 1.

They proved that if
v:B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is a convex function of class
C^{2}_{+}(B)
, then there exists a constant
\bar{\delta}_{p,n}
, depending only on
n
and
p
, such that

\displaystyle\triangle_{p}(v,P_{m})\sim\bar{\delta}_{p,n}\left(\int_{B}(\det\D v(x))^{\frac{p}{n+2p}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{n+2p}{n}}\frac{1}{m^{\frac{2p}{n}}}.

In
[
7
]
, Chen, Sun, and Xu obtained an analogous result for
p=+\infty
. Babenko et al.
[
2
]
derived the exact asymptotics of the optimal weighted error for linear spline interpolation of
C^{2}_{+}
functions in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, for
0<p<+\infty
. In a related work, Babenko
[
3
]
studied the uniform interpolation error of
C^{2}_{+}
functions by multilinear splines, i.e., the maximum pointwise deviation between a function and its interpolant over a uniform rectangular partition of a cube (or rectangular domain), and derived its exact asymptotic behavior as the mesh is refined.

We aim to extend the theory of polytopal approximation from convex bodies to convex functions with compact domain. Let
u:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to(-\infty,+\infty]
be a convex function with compact domain. We consider the class

P_{(m)}^{c}=P_{(m)}^{c}(u)

consisting of piecewise affine functions
l_{m}
circumscribed to
u
, i.e., satisfying
l_{m}(x)~\leq~u(x)
for all
x\in\dom u
, and which can be written as the maximum of at most
m
affine functions, that is, there exist finitely many affine functions
\psi_{1},\dots,\psi_{m}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle l_{m}(x)=\max_{1\leq j\leq m}\psi_{j}(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Let
\omega:\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous positive function. Our goal is to estimate the weighted approximation

\displaystyle\triangle_{p}(u,P_{(m)}^{c},\omega)=\min\Bigl\{\int_{\dom u}(u(x)-l_{m}(x))^{p}\,\omega(x,u(x))\,\mathrm{d}x:l_{m}\in P_{(m)}^{c}\Bigr\},

(4)

for sufficiently large
m
and
p>0
.

This problem can be seen as a functional analogue of classical results on the approximation of convex bodies by circumscribed polytopes (cf. (
1
)). In contrast to interpolation methods based on inscribed triangulations of the domain, the approximating functions considered here are defined as the maximum of a fixed number of circumscribed affine functions. This perspective not only extends Gruber’s approach
[
11
, Theorem 11.4]
to the functional setting, but also establishes a natural connection with the
\zeta
-affine surface area functional
Z_{\zeta}(u)
introduced in (
3
), since the asymptotic behavior of
\triangle_{p}
is governed by a power of the determinant of the Hessian of
u
, weighted appropriately. We also analyze the weighted approximation in the dual space of
\LC(\mathbb{R}^{n})
via the Legendre transform. In particular, for the weight
\omega(x,t)=e^{-t}
,
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, the asymptotic behavior is closely related to the
weighted functional affine surface area
introduced in
[
16
]
.
The precise statement is discussed in Section
3
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
u\in\LC(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be of class
C^{2}_{+}
in
\interior(\dom u)
, and let
\omega:\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\to\mathbb{R}
be continuous and positive.
Then there exists a constant
\delta_{p,n}
, depending only on
n
and
p>0
, such that

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to+\infty}m^{\frac{2p}{n}}\triangle_{p}(u,P_{(m)}^{c},\omega)=\frac{\delta_{p,n}}{2^{p}}\left(\int_{\dom u}(\det\D u(x))^{\frac{p}{n+2p}}\,\omega(x,u(x))^{\frac{n}{n+2p}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{n+2p}{n}}.

The constant
\delta_{p,n}
is given by Zador’s Theorem (see Section
2
), and for
p=1
it coincides with the constant given in (
1
).

In particular, by choosing the weight function
\omega\equiv 1
, Theorem
1
immediately yields the corresponding asymptotic result for the unweighted case.

Corollary 2
.

Let
u\in\LC(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be of class
C^{2}_{+}
in
\interior(\dom u)
.
Then there exists a constant
\delta_{p,n}
, depending only on
n
and
p>0
, such that

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to+\infty}m^{\frac{2p}{n}}\triangle_{p}(u,P_{(m)}^{c},1)=\frac{\delta_{p,n}}{2^{p}}\left(\int_{\dom u}(\det\D u(x))^{\frac{p}{n+2p}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{n+2p}{n}}.

For
p=1
, we obtain the affine surface area that corresponds to the graph of the convex function
u\in\LC(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we collect background results that will be needed throughout the paper.

Zador’s Theorem.

We begin with the constant
\delta_{n}
appearing in inequality (
1
), which is connected to Zador’s Theorem for
\alpha=1
.

Theorem 3

(
[
18
]
, Zador’s Theorem)
.

Let
\alpha>0
. Then there exists a constant
\delta_{2\alpha,n}>0
, depending only on
\alpha
and
n
, such that for any Jordan measurable compact set
J\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
V_{n}(J)>0
,

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to+\infty}m^{\frac{2\alpha}{n}}\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subset J\\
\#S=m\end{subarray}}\int_{J}\min_{s\in S}\|x-s\|^{2\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}x=\delta_{2\alpha,n}\,V_{n}(J)^{\frac{n+2\alpha}{n}}.

Here,
\#S
denotes the cardinality of the set
S
, and
V_{n}(A)
denotes the
n
-dimensional volume of a set
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
A set
J\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is called
Jordan measurable
if its characteristic function is Riemann integrable.

From Theorem
3
, we obtain the following result.

Lemma 4
.

Let
J\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be Jordan measurable with
V_{n}(J)>0
, and let
q
be a positive definite quadratic form on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to+\infty}m^{\frac{2p}{n}}\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subset J\\
\#S=m\end{subarray}}\int_{J}\min_{s\in S}|q(x-s)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x=\delta_{p,n}\,V_{n}(J)^{\frac{n+2p}{n}}(\det q)^{\frac{p}{n}}.

By applying Zador’s Theorem with
\alpha=p
, we obtain the constant that appears in Theorem
1
.

Convex functions.

We denote by

\Conv(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\{u:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow(-\infty,+\infty]\mid\ u\ \mbox{is l.s.c and convex},\ u\not\equiv+\infty\}

the space of proper, lower semicontinuous, convex functions defined on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

We say that a sequence
u_{k}\in\Conv(\mathbb{R}^{n})

epi-converges
to
u
if, for every sequence
x_{k}
converging to
x
,

u(x)\leq\liminf_{k\rightarrow+\infty}u_{k}(x_{k}),

and there exists a sequence
x_{k}
converging to
x
such that

u(x)=\lim_{k\rightarrow+\infty}u_{k}(x_{k}).

The following theorem provides a concrete characterization of epi-convergence, showing its equivalence with a more familiar notion of convergence.

Theorem 5

(
[
15
]
, Theorem 7.17)
.

Let
u_{k}\in\Conv(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a sequence of functions which epi-converges to
u
. If
\dom u
has non-empty interior, the following statements are equivalents

(1)

u_{k}
epi-converges to
u
;

(2)

u_{k}
converges uniformly to
u
on every compact set
C
that does not
contain a boundary point of
\dom u
.

We are interested in exploring a dual perspective related to Theorem
1
.
To this end, it is necessary to introduce the Monge–Ampère measure and the dual space of
\LC(\mathbb{R}^{n})
via the Legendre transform.

Let
u\in\Conv(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. The
subdifferential
of
u
at
x\in\dom u
is defined as

\partial u(x)=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:u(z)\geq u(x)+y\cdot(z-x)\ \mbox{for all }\ z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\},

and we set
\partial u(x)=\emptyset
for
x\not\in\dom u
, where

x\cdot y=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}y_{i}
denotes the standard inner product in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For a subset
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the image of
B
under the subdifferential of
u
is

\partial u(B)=\bigcup_{x\in B}\partial u(x).

The Monge–Ampère measure of
u
is then defined, for every Borel set
B\subseteq\dom u
, by

\displaystyle\MA(u;B):=V_{n}(\partial u(B)).

(5)

Moreover, by
[
9
, Theorem A.31]
, if
B\subset\dom u
is an open bounded set and
u\in C^{2}(B)
, then

\displaystyle\MA(u;B)=\int_{B}\det\D u(x)\,\mathrm{d}x.

(6)

For a convex body
K\in\mathcal{K}^{n+1}
, the
support function
is defined by

h_{K}(x)=\max_{y\in K}x\cdot y,\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}.

If
\mu
is a measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we write
\supp(\mu)
for the support of
\mu
. The following class of convex functions will be considered

\Conv_{\MA}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R})\\[2.84526pt]
=\Bigl\{h_{K}(\cdot,-1)\mid\ K\in\mathcal{K}^{n+1},\ \supp\bigl(\MA(h_{K}(\cdot,-1);\cdot)\bigr)\text{ is compact in }\mathbb{R}^{n}\Bigr\}.

(7)

Note that

\displaystyle\Conv_{\MA}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R})\subset\lip(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R}),

(8)

the space of finite-valued Lipschitz convex functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(see, e.g.,
[
4
, equation (8)]
).

We equip
\Conv_{\MA}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R})
with the following notion of convergence: we say that
v_{k}\in\Conv_{\MA}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R})
is
\tau^{*}
-convergent to
v\in\Conv_{\MA}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R})
if the following conditions hold

(i)

v_{k}
epi-converges to
v
;

(ii)

there exists a compact set
C\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
containing the supports of
\MA(v;\cdot)
and
\MA(v_{k};\cdot)
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

By
[
4
, Lemma 3]
, we have the following duality via Legendre transform

\displaystyle(\Conv_{\MA}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R}))^{*}=\LC(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

where the
Legendre transform
of a convex function
u:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to(-\infty,+\infty]
is given by

\displaystyle u^{*}(x)=\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\langle x,y\rangle-u(y)),\hskip 2.84544ptx\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

In particular, this duality justifies the terminology of
\tau^{*}
-convergence.
The following lemma makes this precise.

Lemma 6

(
[
4
]
, Lemma 3)
.

A sequence
u_{k}
in
\LC(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is
\tau
-convergent to
u\in\LC(\mathbb{R}^{n})
if and only if
u_{k}^{*}
and
u^{*}
belong to

\Conv_{\MA}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R})
and
u_{k}^{*}
is
\tau^{*}
-convergent to
u^{*}
.

3
An Equivalent Framework and Applications

Consider the class

\mathcal{F}(X)=\{v:X\to\mathbb{R}\mid v\text{ is Lipschitz, convex, and of class }C^{2}_{+}\text{ in }\interior(X)\},

where
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is compact. We equip
\mathcal{F}(X)
with the topology of uniform convergence. Let
\omega:\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous positive function. As in (
4
), for
v\in\mathcal{F}(X)
we define the weighted approximation

\displaystyle\triangle_{p}(v,P_{(m)}^{c},\omega):=\min\Bigl\{\int_{X}(v(x)-l_{m}(x))^{p}\,\omega(x,v(x))\,\mathrm{d}x:l_{m}\in P_{(m)}^{c}\Bigr\},

for sufficiently large
m
and
p>0
.

Theorem
1
is a consequence of the following theorem (see Section
5
).

Theorem 7
.

Let
v\in\mathcal{F}(X)
and let
\omega:\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\to\mathbb{R}
be continuous and positive. Then there exists a constant
\delta_{p,n}
, depending only on
n
and
p>0
, such that

\displaystyle\lim_{m\rightarrow+\infty}m^{\frac{2p}{n}}\triangle_{p}(v,P_{(m)}^{c},\omega)=\frac{\delta_{p,n}}{2^{p}}\left(\int_{X}(\det\D v(x))^{\frac{p}{n+2p}}\omega(x,v(x))^{\frac{n}{n+2p}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{n+2p}{n}}.

The constant
\delta_{p,n}
is given by Zador’s Theorem with
\alpha=p
.
In order to establish Theorem
7
, we proceed along the lines of the method developed in
[
11
, Theorem 11.4]
.

The Lipschitz condition in the definition of
\mathcal{F}(X)
ensures that the integral

\displaystyle\int_{X}(\det\D v(x))^{\frac{p}{n+2p}}\omega(x,v(x))^{\frac{n}{n+2p}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x

is finite for every
p>0
. Indeed, since
\omega(\cdot,v(\cdot))
is continuous and positive on the compact set
X
, it attains a maximum, say
\omega_{\max}=\max_{x\in X}\omega(x,v(x))>0
. Consequently,

\displaystyle\int_{X}(\det\D v(x))^{\frac{p}{n+2p}}\omega(x,v(x))^{\frac{n}{n+2p}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\omega_{\max}^{\frac{n}{n+2p}}\int_{X}(\det\D v(x))^{\frac{p}{n+2p}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x.

The finiteness of the right-hand side follows from the fact that if
L
is the Lipschitz constant of
v
, then
\|y\|\leq L
for every
y\in\partial v(x)
and
x\in\interior(\dom u)
(see, for example,
[
5
, Theorem 3.61]
), together with Jensen’s inequality, the definition of the Monge–Ampère measure (
5
), and (
6
), see also
[
4
, Theorem 1]
.

Consider the functional

\displaystyle Z_{\tilde{\zeta}}^{*}(v)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\tilde{\zeta}(\det\D v(x))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x

(9)

for
v\in\Conv_{\MA}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R})
and
\tilde{\zeta}\in\conc
. In
[
4
]
, it is shown that
Z_{\tilde{\zeta}}^{*}
is related to the
\zeta
-affine surface area functional (
3
) in the following way

\displaystyle Z_{\tilde{\zeta}}^{*}(v)=Z_{\zeta}(v^{*})

where
\zeta(t)=\tilde{\zeta}(1/t)\,t
for
t>0
. In particular,
\zeta\in\conc
. Consequently, the functional (
9
) is also naturally connected to the affine surface area of the graph of a convex function.

For a function
v\in\Conv_{\MA}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R})
, we denote by

P_{(m)}^{c}\big(v\big|_{\supp(\MA(v;\cdot))}\big)

the class of piecewise affine functions
l_{m}
that are circumscribed to
v
on
\supp(\MA(v;\cdot))
, i.e.,

l_{m}(x)\leq v(x)\quad\text{for all }x\in\supp(\MA(v;\cdot)).

Moreover, each
l_{m}\in P_{(m)}^{c}\big(v\big|_{\supp(\MA(v;\cdot))}\big)
can be represented as the maximum of at most
m
affine functions.

Theorem 8
.

Let
v\in\Conv_{\MA}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R})
be of class
C^{2}_{+}
on

\supp(\MA(v;\cdot))
, and let
\omega:\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\to\mathbb{R}
be continuous and positive. Then

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to+\infty}m^{\frac{2p}{n}}\min\Bigl\{\int_{\supp(\MA(v;\cdot))}(v(x)-l_{m}(x))^{p}\,\omega(x,v(x))\,\mathrm{d}x:l_{m}\in P_{(m)}^{c}\big(v\big|_{\supp(\MA(v;\cdot))}\big)\Bigr\}

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta_{p,n}}{2^{p}}\Biggl(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\det\D v(x))^{\frac{p}{n+2p}}\,\omega(x,v(x))^{\frac{n}{n+2p}}\,\mathrm{d}x\Biggr)^{\frac{n+2p}{n}}.

For the special case where
p=1
and
\omega(x,v(x))=e^{-v(x)}
, we obtain the following.

Corollary 9
.

Let
v\in\Conv_{\MA}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R})
be of class
C^{2}_{+}
on

\supp(\MA(v;\cdot))
.
Then

\lim_{m\to+\infty}m^{\frac{2}{n}}\min\Bigl\{\int_{\supp(\MA(v;\cdot))}(v(x)-l_{m}(x))\,e^{-v(x)}\,\mathrm{d}x:l_{m}\in P_{(m)}^{c}\big(v\big|_{\supp(\MA(v;\cdot))}\big)\Bigr\}\\
=\frac{\delta_{p,n}}{2^{p}}\Biggl(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\det\D v(x))^{\frac{1}{n+2}}\,e^{-\frac{n}{n+2}v(x)}\,\mathrm{d}x\Biggr)^{\frac{n+2}{n}}.

The functional

\displaystyle Z(v)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\det\D v(x))^{\frac{1}{n+2}}\,e^{-\frac{n}{n+2}v(x)}\,\mathrm{d}x

coincides with the
weighted functional affine surface area
introduced in

[
16
]
. The class of convex functions considered in
[
16
]
is not identical to the one considered in this work, although there is a nontrivial overlap between the two settings. Moreover, by
[
4
, Theorem 9]
, this functional is finite and
\tau^{*}
-upper semicontinuous.

4
Proof of Theorem
7

Let
\lambda>1
and let
v\in\mathcal{F}(X)
. Fix a point
a\in\interior(X)
.
By Taylor’s formula, there exists
\xi=\xi(x)\in(0,1)
such that

\displaystyle v(x)=v(a)+\nabla v(a)\cdot(x-a)+\frac{1}{2}(x-a)\cdot\,\D v\!\left(a+\xi(x-a)\right)(x-a),

Since

We begin by proving the following inequality

Proposition 10
.

Let
\lambda>1
and let

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda}q_{a_{i}}(y)\leq q_{x}(y)\leq\lambda q_{a_{i}}(y),\qquad y\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\ x\in U_{i},

(15)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda^{n}}\det q_{a_{i}}\leq\det q_{x}\leq\lambda^{n}\det q_{a_{i}},\qquad x\in U_{i},

Next, we use a well-known consequence of Jensen’s inequality

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta_{p,n}}{2^{p}\lambda^{p+2}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{l}V_{n}(J_{i})(\det q_{a_{i}})^{\frac{p}{n+2p}}\omega_{a_{i}}^{\frac{n}{n+2p}}\right)^{\frac{n+2p}{n}}\frac{1}{(d_{1}^{m}+\cdots+d_{l}^{m})^{\frac{2p}{n}}}.

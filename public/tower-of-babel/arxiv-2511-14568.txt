Probabilistic Stirling numbers associated with sequences
Dae San Kim, Taekyun Kim

Abstract
. Let Y Y be a random variable whose moment generating function exists in a neighborhood of the origin. Recently, probabilistic Stirling numbers of the first kind, s Y ​ ( n , k ) s_{Y}(n,k) , and of the second kind, S 2 Y ​ ( n , k ) S_{2}^{Y}(n,k) , associated with Y Y have been introduced. However, s Y ​ ( n , k ) s_{Y}(n,k) , based on the cumulant generating function of Y Y , and S 2 Y ​ ( n , k ) S_{2}^{Y}(n,k) do not satisfy orthogonality and inverse relations. This paper aims to redefine the probabilistic Stirling numbers of the first kind associated with Y Y , denoting them by S 1 Y ​ ( n , k ) S_{1}^{Y}(n,k) , such that S 1 Y ​ ( n , k ) S_{1}^{Y}(n,k) and S 2 Y ​ ( n , k ) S_{2}^{Y}(n,k) do satisfy these crucial relations. Furthermore, we investigate their degenerate counterparts, the probabilistic degenerate Stirling numbers of both kinds. We explicitly compute S 2 Y ​ ( n , k ) , S 1 Y ​ ( n , k ) S_{2}^{Y}(n,k),S_{1}^{Y}(n,k) , and their degenerate versions for several discrete and continuous random variables Y Y . As an application, we express arbitrary polynomials as linear combinations of probabilistic Euler and probabilistic degenerate Euler polynomials.

1.
Introduction

The Stirling number of the second, denoted by
S_{2}(n,k)
, counts the number of ways to partition a set of
n
objects into
k
nonempty subsets. The signed Stirling number of the first kind, denoted by,
S_{1}(n,k)
is defined such that the number of permutations of
n
elements with exactly
k
cycles is given by
|S_{1}(n,k)|=(-1)^{n-k}S_{1}(n,k)
(see, for example, [7,23,24,26]).
The degenerate Stirling numbers of the second kind,
S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)
(defined in (3)), and of the first kind,
S_{1,\lambda}(n,k)
(defined in (5)), are degenerate versions of
S_{2}(n,k)
and
S_{1}(n,k)
, respectively (see [13,14,22]). These degenerate Stirling numbers frequently arise in the study of degenerate versions of various special polynomials and numbers (see [8,9,20]).

The study of degenerate versions of special polynomials and numbers has seen a resurgence of interest and a corresponding increase in research activity. These investigations have employed a diverse range of mathematical tools, including quantum mechanics, generating functions, combinatorial methods,
p
-adic analysis, umbral calculus, probability theory, differential equations, analytic number theory, and operator theory. This exploration of degenerate versions originated with Carlitz’s work on degenerate Bernoulli and Euler polynomials (see [4,5]). Notably, this area of study has expanded beyond polynomials and numbers to encompass transcendental functions (see [15,16]) and umbral calculus (see [10]).

The Stirling numbers of both kinds and the degenerate Stirling numbers of both kinds are generalized as follows (see [11]). Let
{\bf{P}}=\{p_{n}(x)\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
be a sequence of polynomials such that deg
p_{n}(x)=n,\,p_{0}(x)=1
. The Stirling numbers of the second kind associated with
\bf{P}
, denoted by
S_{2}(n,k;{\bf{P}})
, are defined as the coefficients in the expansion of
p_{n}(x)
in terms of the falling factorials
(x)_{k}
:

p_{n}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2}(n,k;{\bf{P}})(x)_{k}
.
Note that
S_{2}(n,k;{\bf{P}})
reduces to the ordinary Stirling numbers of the second kind,
S_{2}(n,k)
, when
{\bf{P}}=\{x^{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
(see (
1
)), and to the degenerate Stirling numbers of the second kind,
S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)
, when
{\bf{P}}=\{(x)_{n,\lambda}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
(see (
3
)).
The Stirling numbers of the first kind associated with
\bf{P}
, denoted by
S_{1}(n,k;{\bf{P}})
, are defined by the expansion
(x)_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{1}(n,k;{\bf{P}})p_{k}(x)
. The Stirling numbers of both kinds associated with
\bf{P}
have been investigated using umbral calculus (see [11]). Analogously, the central factorial numbers of both kinds associated with
\bf{P}
can be studied by replacing the falling factorials
(x)_{n}
with the central factorials
x^{[n]}
, where

x^{[n]}=x(x+\frac{1}{2}n-1)_{n-1},\,\,(n\geq 1),\,\,x^{[0]}=1
,   (see [12]).

Let
Y
be a random variable whose moment generating function exists in some neighborhood of the origin (see (
8
)). Recently, Adell and Lekuona introduced the probabilistic Stirling numbers of the second kind associated with
Y
, denoted by
S_{2}^{Y}(n,k)
(see [1,3]). Furthermore, Adell and Bényi defined the probabilistic Stirling numbers of the first kind associated with
Y
, denoted by
s_{Y}(n,k)
, using the cumulant generating function (see [2]). However,
S_{2}^{Y}(n,k)
and
s_{Y}(n,k)
do not satisfy orthogonality and inverse relations. In addition,
s_{Y}(n,k)
does not reduce to the classical Stirling numbers of the first kind,
S_{1}(n,k)
, when
Y=1
. We address these shortcomings by redefining the probabilistic Stirling numbers of the first kind, denoting them by
S_{1}^{Y}(n,k)
. Our redefined
S_{1}^{Y}(n,k)
and
S_{2}^{Y}(n,k)
do satisfy orthogonality and inverse relations (see Proposition 1.1), and
S_{1}^{Y}(n,k)
reduces to
S_{1}(n,k)
, when
Y=1
.
We also consider the probabilistic degenerate Stirling numbers of the second kind associated with
Y
, denoted by
S_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)
(see (
14
) and [21]), and the probabilistic degenerate Stirling numbers of the first kind associated with
Y
, denoted by
S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)
(see (
15
)).
S_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)
and
S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)
satisfy orthogonality and inverse relations (see Proposition 1.2) and
S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)
reduces to the standard degenerate Stirling numbers of the first kind,
S_{1,\lambda}(n,k)
, when
Y=1
.
However, in [18],
S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)
is defined differently in that work using the degenerate cumulant generating function. Consequently, the
S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)
defined there and
S_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)
do not together satisfy orthogonality and inverse relations. In Section 2, we explicitly compute
S_{2}^{Y}(n,k),S_{1}^{Y}(n,k),S_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)
, and
S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)
for several discrete and continuous random variables
Y
(see Theorems 2.1-2.8).
Section 3 illustrates the orthogonality relations for the geometric random variable with parameter
0<p<1
. Furthermore, we address the problem of expressing an arbitrary polynomial in terms of the probabilistic Euler polynomials
\mathcal{E}_{n}^{Y}(x)
and the probabilistic degenerate Euler polynomials
\mathcal{E}_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)
, demonstrating the necessity of inversion relations. Probabilistic extensions of numerous special polynomials and numbers have been studied recently (see, for example, [6,17,19,28,29] and the references therein).

The Stirling numbers of the second kind,
S_{2}(n,k)
, are defined by

(1)

\displaystyle x^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2}(n,k)(x)_{k},

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}(e^{t}-1)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{2}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},

\displaystyle S_{2}(n,k)=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{k-j}j^{n},

where the falling factorials
(x)_{n}
are given by

(x)_{0}=1,\quad(x)_{n}=x(x-1)\cdots(x-n+1),\quad(n\geq 1).

The Stirling numbers of the first kind,
S_{1}(n,k)
, are defined by

(2)

\displaystyle(x)_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{1}(n,k)x^{k},

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\big(\log(1+t)\big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{1}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Let
\lambda
be any nonzero real number. The degenerate Stirling numbers of the second kind,
S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)
, are defined by

(3)

\displaystyle(x)_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)(x)_{k},

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},

\displaystyle S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{k-j}(j)_{n,\lambda},

where
(x)_{n,\lambda}
are the degenerate (or generalized) falling factorials given by

(x)_{0,\lambda}=1,\ (x)_{n,\lambda}=x(x-\lambda)\cdots(x-(n-1)\lambda),\ (n\geq 1),

and
e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)
are the degenerate exponentials defined by

(4)

e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=(1+\lambda t)^{\frac{x}{\lambda}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(x)_{n,\lambda}\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad e_{\lambda}(t)=e_{\lambda}^{1}(t),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [9,17,18,21]).

Note here that
\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=e^{xt}
.

The degenerate Stirling numbers of the first kind,
S_{1,\lambda}(n,k)
, are given by

(5)

\displaystyle(x)_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{1,\lambda}(n,k)(x)_{k,\lambda},

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\big(\log_{\lambda}(1+t)\big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{1,\lambda}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},

where
\log_{\lambda}(t)
are the degenerate logarithm defined by

(6)

\log_{\lambda}(t)=\frac{1}{\lambda}\big(t^{\lambda}-1\big).

Note here that the degenerate exponential
e_{\lambda}(t)
in (
4
) and the degenerate logarithm
\log_{\lambda}(t)
in (
6
) satisfy

(7)

e_{\lambda}\big(\log_{\lambda}(t)\big)=\log_{\lambda}\big(e_{\lambda}(t)\big)=t.

Among other things, the Stirling numbers of both kinds and the degenerate Stirling numbers of both kinds satisfy orthogonality and inversion relations (see Propositions 1.1 and 1.2 with
Y=1
).

Let
Y
be a random variable whose moment generating function exists in a neighborhood of the origin:

(8)

E[e^{Yt}]=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}E[Y^{n}]\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\,\,\,\mathrm{exists,\,\,for}\,\,|x|<r,

for some positive real number
r
.

Let
(Y_{j})_{j\geq 1}
be a sequence of mutually independent copies of the random variable
Y
, and let

(9)

S_{k}=Y_{1}+Y_{2}+\cdots+Y_{k},\quad(k\geq 1),\quad S_{0}=0.

The probabilistic Stirling numbers of the second kind associated with
Y
,
S_{2}^{Y}(n,k)
, are given by (see (
9
))

(10)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}(E[e^{Yt}]-1)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{2}^{Y}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},

\displaystyle S_{2}^{Y}(n,k)=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{k-j}E[S_{j}^{n}].

From the definition in (
10
), it is immediate to see that

(11)

S_{2}^{Y}(k,k)=E[Y]^{k}.

Here we define the probabilistic Stirling numbers of the first kind associated with
Y
,
S_{1}^{Y}(n,k)
, in a different way from those ones in [2] and [18]. We believe that they should be defined in such a manner that they obey orthogonality and inverse relations. To do this, we assume from now on till the end of this paper that

(12)

E[Y]\neq 0.

We introduce the notation:

(13)

e_{Y}(t)=E[e^{Yt}]-1.

Then we have

(14)

\frac{1}{k!}\big(e_{Y}(t)\big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{2}^{Y}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

If
f(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}
is a delta series, namely
a_{0}=0
and
a_{1}\neq 0
, then the compositional inverse
\bar{f}(t)
of
f(t)
satisfying
f(\bar{f}(t))=\bar{f}(f(t))=t
exists. For example,
e_{\lambda}(t)
and
\log_{\lambda}(t)
are compositional inverses to each other (see (
7
)). Note that, as
e_{Y}(t)=E[Y]t+\sum_{m=2}^{\infty}E[Y^{m}]\frac{t^{m}}{m!}
and
E[Y]\neq 0
(see (
12
), (
13
)),
e_{Y}(t)
is a delta series.

Now, we define the probabilistic Stirling numbers of the first kind associated with
Y
by: for
k\geq 0
,

(15)

\frac{1}{k!}\big(\bar{e}_{Y}(t)\big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{1}^{Y}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},

where
\bar{e}_{Y}(t)
is the compositional inverse of
e_{Y}(t)
.

In addition, as usual, we agree that

(16)

S_{2}^{Y}(n,k)=S_{1}^{Y}(n,k)=0,\,\,\mathrm{if}\,\,k>n\,\,\mathrm{or}\,\,k<0.

Note that
S_{2}^{Y}(n,k)=S_{2}(n,k),\,\,S_{1}^{Y}(n,k)=S_{1}(n,k)
, when
Y=1
.

Using the definitions in (
14
) and (
15
), one shows that
S_{2}^{Y}(n,k)
and
S_{1}^{Y}(n,k)
satisfy the orthogonality relations in (a) of Proposition 1.1, from which the inverse relations in (b) and (c) follow.

Proposition 1.1
.

The following orthogonality and inverse relations are valid for
S_{1}^{Y}(n,k)
and
S_{2}^{Y}(n,k)
.

\displaystyle(a)\,\,\,\sum_{k=l}^{n}S_{2}^{Y}(n,k)S_{1}^{Y}(k,l)=\delta_{n,l},\quad\sum_{k=l}^{n}S_{1}^{Y}(n,k)S_{2}^{Y}(k,l)=\delta_{n,l},

\displaystyle(b)\,\,a_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2}^{Y}(n,k)b_{k}\,\,\iff\,\,b_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{1}^{Y}(n,k)a_{k},

\displaystyle(c)\,\,a_{n}=\sum_{k=n}^{m}S_{2}^{Y}(k,n)b_{k}\,\,\iff\,\,b_{n}=\sum_{k=n}^{m}S_{1}^{Y}(k,n)a_{k}.

Now, we introduce the following:

(17)

\bar{f}_{Y}(t)=\log E[e^{Yt}].

Let
f_{Y}(t)
be the compositional inverse of
\bar{f}_{Y}(t)
. Here we have to observe that
\bar{f}_{Y}(t)
is a delta series (see (
18
)). Indeed, we have (see (
10
)-(
12
))

(18)

\displaystyle\bar{f}_{Y}(t)

\displaystyle=\log\big(1+(E[e^{Yt}]-1)\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{j-1}(j-1)!\frac{1}{j!}(E[e^{Yt}]-1)^{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{j-1}(j-1)!\sum_{n=j}^{\infty}S_{2}^{Y}(n,j)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=S_{2}^{Y}(1,1)t+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{j-1}(j-1)!S_{2}^{Y}(n,j)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=E[Y]t+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{j-1}(j-1)!S_{2}^{Y}(n,j)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Here we recall that

(19)

\bar{f}_{Y}(t)=\log E[e^{Yt}]=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\kappa_{n}(Y)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

is called the cumulant generating function of
Y
. From (
18
) and (
19
), we derive

\kappa_{n}(Y)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{j-1}(j-1)!S_{2}^{Y}(n,j),\quad(n\geq 1).

In [2], the probabilistic Stirling numbers of the first kind associated with
Y
,
s_{Y}(n,k)
, are defined as

(20)

\frac{1}{k!}\big(\log E[e^{Yt}]\big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}(-1)^{n-k}s_{Y}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

However,
s_{Y}(n,k)
and
S_{2}^{Y}(n,k)
do not together satisfy orthogonality and inverse relations, and
s_{Y}(n,k)=\delta_{n,k}
,   when
Y=1
. Here we note, in passing, that

\kappa_{1}(Y)=E[Y],\,\,\kappa_{2}(Y)=\mathrm{Var}(Y),\,\,\kappa_{3}(Y)=E[(Y-E(Y))^{3}].

The probabilistic degenerate Stirling numbers of the second kind associated
Y
,
S_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)
, are defined by

(21)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\big(E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]-1\big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},

\displaystyle S_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{k-j}E[(S_{j})_{n,\lambda}].

To define the probabilistic degenerate Stirling numbers of the first kind associated with
Y
,
S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)
, we let

(22)

e_{Y,\lambda}(t)=E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]-1.

Then we have

(23)

\frac{1}{k!}(e_{Y,\lambda}(t))^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Note that
e_{Y,\lambda}(t)=E[Y]t+\sum_{m=2}^{\infty}E[(Y)_{m,\lambda}]\frac{t^{m}}{m!}
is a delta series (see (
12
)). Now, we define the probabilistic degenerate Stirling numbers of the first kind associated with
Y
,
S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)
, by

(24)

\frac{1}{k!}\big(\bar{e}_{Y,\lambda}(t)\big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},

where
\bar{e}_{Y,\lambda}(t)
is the compositional inverse of
e_{Y,\lambda}(t)
.
Then
S_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)
and
S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)
satisfy orthogonality and inverse relations, and
S_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)=S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)
and
S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)=S_{1,\lambda}(n,k)
, when
Y=1
. We also consider

(25)

\bar{f}_{Y,\lambda}(t)=\log E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)],

and its compositional inverse
f_{Y,\lambda}(t)
as well.
As for the probabilisitc Stirling numbers associated with
Y
, the orthogonality and inverse relations are valid for the probabilistic degenerate Stirling numbers associated with
Y
.

Proposition 1.2
.

The following orthogonality and inverse relations are valid for
S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)
and
S_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)
.

\displaystyle(a)\,\,\sum_{k=l}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(k,l)=\delta_{n,l},\quad\sum_{k=l}^{n}S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)S_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(k,l)=\delta_{n,l},

\displaystyle(b)\,\,a_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)b_{k}\,\,\iff\,\,b_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)a_{k},

\displaystyle(c)\,\,a_{n}=\sum_{k=n}^{m}S_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(k,n)b_{k}\,\,\iff\,\,b_{n}=\sum_{k=n}^{m}S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(k,n)a_{k}.

The probabilistic Euler polynomials associated with
Y
,
\mathcal{E}_{n}^{Y}(x)
, are defined by

(26)

\frac{2}{E[e^{Yt}]+1}\big(E[e^{Yt}]\big)^{x}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{E}_{n}^{Y}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Moreover, the probabilistic degenerate Euler polynomials associated with
Y
,
\mathcal{E}_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)
, are given by

(27)

\frac{2}{E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]+1}\big(E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]\big)^{x}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{E}_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Let
a(x)
be a polynomial of degree of
n
with complex coefficients. Then, for any integer
r
with
0\leq r\leq n
, (see [24])

(28)

\displaystyle\Delta^{r}a(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{r}\binom{r}{i}(-1)^{r-i}a(x+i)

\displaystyle=r!\sum_{j=r}^{n}S_{2}(j,r)\frac{1}{j!}a^{(j)}(x),

where
\Delta
is the forward difference operator given by (see [7,23,25])

(29)

\Delta a(x)=a(x+1)-a(x).

It is easy to see that

(30)

\Delta(x)_{n}=(x+1)_{n}-(x)_{n}=n(x)_{n-1},\quad(n\geq 0),

and hence, for any
r
with
0\leq r\leq n
,

(31)

\Delta^{r}(x)_{n}=(n)_{r}(x)_{n-r}.

2.
Probabilistic Stirling and degenerate Stirling numbers

In this section, for several discrete and continuous random variables
Y
, we determine

\displaystyle e_{Y}(t),\,\,\,\bar{e}_{Y}(t),\,\,\,S_{2}^{Y}(n,k),\,\,\,S_{1}^{Y}(n,k),\,\,\,\bar{f}_{Y}(t),\,\,\,f_{Y}(t),

\displaystyle e_{Y,\lambda}(t),\,\,\,\bar{e}_{Y,\lambda}(t),\,\,\,S_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k),\,\,\,S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k),\,\,\,\bar{f}_{Y,\lambda}(t),\,\,\,\mathrm{and}\,\,\,f_{Y,\lambda}(t).

Our reference here is [27], especially Table 2.1 on p. 61 and Table 2.2 on p. 62.

If
Y
is a discrete random variable with probability mass function

p(y)=P\{Y=y\}
, then for any real-valued function
g
,

(32)

E[g(Y)]=\sum_{y}g(y)p(y).

If
Y
is a continuous random variable with probability density function
f(x)
, then
for any real-valued function
g
,

(33)

E[g(Y)]=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}g(y)f(y)dy.

(a) Let
Y
be the Bernoulli random variable. Then the probability mass function of
Y
is given by

(34)

p(0)=1-p,\quad p(1)=p,\quad(0<p\leq 1).

Then we have

(35)

\displaystyle E[Y]=p,\,\,E[e^{Yt}]=1+p(e^{t}-1),\,\,e_{Y}(t)=p(e^{t}-1),\,\,\bar{e}_{Y}(t)=\log(1+\frac{t}{p}),

\displaystyle\bar{f}_{Y}(t)=\log\big(1+p(e^{t}-1)\big),\,\,f_{Y}(t)=\log\big(1+\frac{1}{p}(e^{t}-1)\big).

From (
35
), we obtain

(36)

S_{2}^{Y}(n,k)=p^{k}S_{2}(n,k),\,\,S_{1}^{Y}(n,k)=\frac{1}{p^{n}}S_{1}(n,k).

We observe from (
32
) and (
34
) that

(37)

E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]=e_{\lambda}^{0}(t)(1-p)+e_{\lambda}(t)p=1+p(e_{\lambda}(t)-1).

From (
37
), we derive

(38)

\displaystyle e_{Y,\lambda}(t)=p\big(e_{\lambda}(t)-1),\,\,\bar{e}_{Y,\lambda}(t)=\log_{\lambda}(1+\frac{t}{p}),

\displaystyle\bar{f}_{Y,\lambda}(t)=\log\big(1+p(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)\big),\,\,f_{Y,\lambda}(t)=\log_{\lambda}\big(1+\frac{1}{p}(e^{t}-1)\big).

From (
38
), we get

(39)

S_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)=p^{k}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k),\,\,S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)=\frac{1}{p^{n}}S_{1,\lambda}(n,k).

We summarize the results in (
36
) ane (
39
) in the next theorem.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
Y
be the Bernoulli random variable with parameter
p
with
0<p\leq 1
. Then, for
n\geq k
, we have

\displaystyle S_{2}^{Y}(n,k)=p^{k}S_{2}(n,k),\,\,S_{1}^{Y}(n,k)=\frac{1}{p^{n}}S_{1}(n,k),

\displaystyle S_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)=p^{k}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k),\,\,S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)=\frac{1}{p^{n}}S_{1,\lambda}(n,k).

(b) Let
Y
be the binomial random variable with parameter
(m,p)
. Then the probability mass function of
Y
is given by

(40)

p(i)=\binom{m}{i}p^{i}(1-p)^{m-i},\quad i=0,1,\dots,m.

Here
m
is a positive integer and
0<p\leq 1
.
Then we have

(41)

\displaystyle E[Y]=mp,\,\,E[e^{Yt}]=\big(1+p(e^{t}-1)\big)^{m},

\displaystyle e_{Y}(t)=\big(1+p(e^{t}-1)\big)^{m}-1,\,\,\bar{e}_{Y}(t)=\log\Big(1+\frac{1}{p}\big((1+t)^{\frac{1}{m}}-1\big)\Big),

\displaystyle\bar{f}_{Y}(t)=m\log\big(1+p(e^{t}-1)\big),\,\,f_{Y}(t)=\log\big(1+\frac{1}{p}(e^{\frac{t}{m}}-1)\big).

By using (
41
), we obtain

(42)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\big(e_{Y}(t)\big)^{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\Big(\big(1+p(e^{t}-1)\big)^{m}-1\Big)^{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\Big(e^{m\log\big(1+p(e^{t}-1)\big)}-1\Big)^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=k}^{\infty}m^{j}S_{2}(j,k)\frac{1}{j!}\Big(\log\big(1+p(e^{t}-1)\big)\Big)^{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=k}^{\infty}m^{j}S_{2}(j,k)\sum_{i=j}^{\infty}S_{1}(i,j)p^{i}\frac{1}{i!}(e^{t}-1)^{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=k}^{\infty}\sum_{i=j}^{\infty}m^{j}p^{i}S_{2}(j,k)S_{1}(i,j)\sum_{n=i}^{\infty}S_{2}(n,i)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\sum_{j=k}^{n}\sum_{i=j}^{n}m^{j}p^{i}S_{2}(j,k)S_{1}(i,j)S_{2}(n,i)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Thus (
42
) shows that

(43)

S_{2}^{Y}(n,k)=\sum_{j=k}^{n}\sum_{i=j}^{n}m^{j}p^{i}S_{2}(j,k)S_{1}(i,j)S_{2}(n,i),\quad(n\geq k).

Again, by utilizing (
41
), we have

(44)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\big(\bar{e}_{Y}(t)\big)^{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\bigg(\log\Big(1+\frac{1}{p}\big((1+t)^{\frac{1}{m}}-1\big)\Big)\bigg)^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=k}^{\infty}S_{1}(j,k)\frac{1}{p^{j}}\frac{1}{j!}\big((1+t)^{\frac{1}{m}}-1\big)^{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=k}^{\infty}S_{1}(j,k)\frac{1}{p^{j}}\frac{1}{j!}\big(e^{\frac{1}{m}\log(1+t)}-1\big)^{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=k}^{\infty}\sum_{i=j}^{\infty}\frac{1}{p^{j}}\frac{1}{m^{i}}S_{1}(j,k)S_{2}(i,j)\frac{1}{i!}\big(\log(1+t)\big)^{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=k}^{\infty}\sum_{i=j}^{\infty}\frac{1}{p^{j}}\frac{1}{m^{i}}S_{1}(j,k)S_{2}(i,j)\sum_{n=i}^{\infty}S_{1}(n,i)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\sum_{j=k}^{n}\sum_{i=j}^{n}\frac{1}{p^{j}}\frac{1}{m^{i}}S_{1}(j,k)S_{2}(i,j)S_{1}(n,i)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, from (
44
), we get

(45)

S_{1}^{Y}(n,k)=\sum_{j=k}^{n}\sum_{i=j}^{n}\frac{1}{p^{j}}\frac{1}{m^{i}}S_{1}(j,k)S_{2}(i,j)S_{1}(n,i).

We observe from (
32
) and (
40
) that

(46)

\displaystyle E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{m}e_{\lambda}^{i}(t)p(i)=\sum_{i=0}^{m}\binom{m}{i}\big(pe_{\lambda}(t)\big)^{i}(1-p)^{m-i}

\displaystyle=\Big(1+p\big(e_{\lambda}(t)-1\big)\Big)^{m}.

From (
46
), we get

(47)

\displaystyle e_{Y,\lambda}(t)=\big(1+p(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)\big)^{m}-1,\,\,\,\bar{e}_{Y,\lambda}(t)=\log_{\lambda}\Big(1+\frac{1}{p}\big((1+t)^{\frac{1}{m}}-1\big)\Big),

\displaystyle\bar{f}_{Y,\lambda}(t)=m\log\big(1+p(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)\big),\,\,\,f_{Y,\lambda}(t)=\log_{\lambda}\big(1+\frac{1}{p}(e^{\frac{t}{m}}-1)\big).

From (
47
), we obtain

(48)

\displaystyle S_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)=\sum_{j=k}^{n}\sum_{i=j}^{n}m^{j}p^{i}S_{2}(j,k)S_{1}(i,j)S_{2,\lambda}(n,i),

\displaystyle S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)=\sum_{j=k}^{n}\sum_{i=j}^{n}\frac{1}{p^{j}}\frac{1}{m^{i}}S_{1,\lambda}(j,k)S_{2}(i,j)S_{1}(n,i).

Thus the next theorem follows from (
43
), (
45
) and (
48
).

Theorem 2.2
.

Y
be the binomial random variable with parameter
(m,p)
, with

\displaystyle S_{2}^{Y}(n,k)=\sum_{j=k}^{n}\sum_{i=j}^{n}m^{j}p^{i}S_{2}(j,k)S_{1}(i,j)S_{2}(n,i),

\displaystyle S_{1}^{Y}(n,k)=\sum_{j=k}^{n}\sum_{i=j}^{n}\frac{1}{p^{j}}\frac{1}{m^{i}}S_{1}(j,k)S_{2}(i,j)S_{1}(n,i),

(c) Let
Y
be the Poisson random variable with parameter
\alpha>0
. Then the probability mass function of
Y
is given by

(49)

p(i)=e^{-\alpha}\frac{\alpha^{i}}{i!},\quad i=0,1,2,\dots.

Then we have

(50)

\displaystyle E[Y]=\alpha,\,\,E[e^{Yt}]=e^{\alpha(e^{t}-1)},\,\,e_{Y}(t)=e^{\alpha(e^{t}-1)}-1,

\displaystyle\bar{e}_{Y}(t)=\log\big(1+\frac{1}{\alpha}\log(1+t)\big),\,\,\bar{f}_{Y}(t)=\alpha(e^{t}-1),\,\,f_{Y}(t)=\log(1+\frac{t}{\alpha}).

From (

), we get

(51)

S_{2}^{Y}(n,k)=\sum_{j=k}^{n}\alpha^{j}S_{2}(j,k)S_{2}(n,j),\,\,\,S_{1}^{Y}(n,k)=\sum_{j=k}^{n}\frac{1}{\alpha^{j}}S_{1}(j,k)S_{1}(n,j).

From (

) and (

), we observe that

(52)

E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}e_{\lambda}^{i}(t)p(i)=e^{-\alpha}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{i!}\big(\alpha e_{\lambda}(t)\big)^{i}=e^{\alpha\big(e_{\lambda}(t)-1\big)}.

From (

), we derive

(53)

\displaystyle e_{Y,\lambda}(t)=e^{\alpha(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)}-1,\,\,\,\bar{e}_{Y,\lambda}(t)=\log_{\lambda}\big(1+\frac{1}{\alpha}\log(1+t)\big),

\displaystyle\bar{f}_{Y,\lambda}(t)=\alpha(e_{\lambda}(t)-1),\,\,\,f_{Y,\lambda}(t)=\log_{\lambda}(1+\frac{t}{\alpha}).

From (

), we get

(54)

S_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)=\sum_{j=k}^{n}\alpha^{j}S_{2}(j,k)S_{2,\lambda}(n,j),\,\,\,S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)=\sum_{j=k}^{n}\frac{1}{\alpha^{j}}S_{1,\lambda}(j,k)S_{1}(n,j).

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
Y
be the Poisson random variable with parameter
\alpha>0
. Then, for
n\geq k
, we have

\displaystyle S_{2}^{Y}(n,k)=\sum_{j=k}^{n}\alpha^{j}S_{2}(j,k)S_{2}(n,j),\,\,\,S_{1}^{Y}(n,k)=\sum_{j=k}^{n}\frac{1}{\alpha^{j}}S_{1}(j,k)S_{1}(n,j),

\displaystyle S_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)=\sum_{j=k}^{n}\alpha^{j}S_{2}(j,k)S_{2,\lambda}(n,j),\,\,\,S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)=\sum_{j=k}^{n}\frac{1}{\alpha^{j}}S_{1,\lambda}(j,k)S_{1}(n,j).

(d) Let
Y
be the geometric random variable with parameter
0<p<1
. Then the probability mass function of
Y
is given by

(55)

p(i)=(1-p)^{i-1}p,\quad i=1,2,\dots.

Then we have

(56)

\displaystyle E[Y]=\frac{1}{p},\,\,E[e^{Yt}]=\frac{pe^{t}}{1-(1-p)e^{t}},

\displaystyle e_{Y}(t)=\frac{pe^{t}}{1-(1-p)e^{t}}-1,\,\,\bar{e}_{Y}(t)=\log\Big(\frac{1+t}{1+(1-p)t}\Big),

\displaystyle\bar{f}_{Y}(t)=\log\Big(\frac{pe^{t}}{1-(1-p)e^{t}}\Big),\,\,f_{Y}(t)=\log\Big(\frac{e^{t}}{p+(1-p)e^{t}}\Big).

Before proceeding further, we observe from (
56
) that

(57)

e_{Y}(t)=\frac{e^{t}-1}{(p-1)e^{t}+1}=\frac{1}{p-1}\bigg(1-\frac{1-\frac{1}{1-p}}{e^{t}-\frac{1}{1-p}}\bigg).

Thus, from (
57
), we have

(58)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\big(e_{Y}(t)\big)^{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\frac{1}{(p-1)^{k}}\bigg(1-\frac{1-\frac{1}{1-p}}{e^{t}-\frac{1}{1-p}}\bigg)^{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\frac{1}{(p-1)^{k}}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{j}\bigg(\frac{1-\frac{1}{1-p}}{e^{t}-\frac{1}{1-p}}\bigg)^{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\frac{1}{(p-1)^{k}}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{j}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}H_{n}^{(j)}\Big(\frac{1}{1-p}\Big)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}\frac{1}{(p-1)^{k}}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{j}H_{n}^{(j)}\Big(\frac{1}{1-p}\Big)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},

where, for any nonnegative integer
r
,
H_{n}^{(r)}(u)
are the Frobenius-Euler numbers of order
r
, given by

(59)

\bigg(\frac{1-u}{e^{t}-u}\bigg)^{r}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}H_{n}^{(r)}(u)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(u\neq 1).

Thus we have shown that

(60)

S_{2}^{Y}(n,k)=\frac{1}{k!}\frac{1}{(p-1)^{k}}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{j}H_{n}^{(j)}\Big(\frac{1}{1-p}\Big),\quad\mathrm{for}\,\,\,n\geq k,

and

(61)

\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{j}H_{n}^{(j)}\Big(\frac{1}{1-p}\Big)=0,\quad\mathrm{for}\,\,\,0\leq n<k.

Next, we consider

(62)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\big(\bar{e}_{Y}(t)\big)^{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\bigg(\log\Big(\frac{1+t}{1+(1-p)t}\Big)\bigg)^{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\bigg(\log\Big(1+\frac{pt}{1+(1-p)t}\Big)\bigg)^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=k}^{\infty}S_{1}(j,k)\frac{1}{j!}\Big(\frac{pt}{1+(1-p)t}\Big)^{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=k}^{\infty}S_{1}(j,k)p^{j}\frac{t^{j}}{j!}\big(1+(1-p)t\big)^{-j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=k}^{\infty}S_{1}(j,k)p^{j}\frac{t^{j}}{j!}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(j+m-1)_{m}(p-1)^{m}\frac{t^{m}}{m!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\sum_{j=k}^{n}\binom{n}{j}(n-1)_{n-j}p^{j}(p-1)^{n-j}S_{1}(j,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Thus (
62
) shows that

(63)

S_{1}^{Y}(n,k)=\sum_{j=k}^{n}\binom{n}{j}(n-1)_{n-j}p^{j}(p-1)^{n-j}S_{1}(j,k),\,\,\,\mathrm{for}\,\,\,n\geq k.

We observe from (
32
) and (
55
) that

(64)

\displaystyle E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}e_{\lambda}^{i}(t)p(i)=pe_{\lambda}(t)\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\big((1-p)e_{\lambda}(t)\big)^{i-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{pe_{\lambda}(t)}{1-(1-p)e_{\lambda}(t)}.

From (
64
), we obtain

(65)

\displaystyle e_{Y,\lambda}(t)=\frac{pe_{\lambda}(t)}{1-(1-p)e_{\lambda}(t)}-1,\,\,\,\bar{e}_{Y,\lambda}(t)=\log_{\lambda}\Big(\frac{1+t}{1+(1-p)t}\Big),

\displaystyle\bar{f}_{Y,\lambda}(t)=\log\Big(\frac{pe_{\lambda}(t)}{1-(1-p)e_{\lambda}(t)}\Big),\,\,\,f_{Y,\lambda}(t)=\log_{\lambda}\Big(\frac{e^{t}}{p+(1-p)e^{t}}\Big).

Using (
65
) and proceeding just as in (
58
) with
e^{t}
replaced by
e_{\lambda}(t)
, we show that

(66)

S_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)=\frac{1}{k!}\frac{1}{(p-1)^{k}}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{j}h_{n,\lambda}^{(j)}\Big(\frac{1}{1-p}\Big),\quad\mathrm{for}\,\,\,n\geq k,

and

(67)

\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{j}h_{n,\lambda}^{(j)}\Big(\frac{1}{1-p}\Big)=0,\quad\mathrm{for}\,\,\,0\leq n<k.

Here, for any nonnegative integer
r
,
h_{n,\lambda}^{(r)}(u)
are the degenerate Frobenius-Euler numbers of order
r
, given by

(68)

\Big(\frac{1-u}{e_{\lambda}(t)-u}\Big)^{r}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}h_{n,\lambda}^{(r)}(u)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(u\neq 1).

Again, by using (
65
) and proceeding just as in (
62
) with
e^{t}
replaced by
e_{\lambda}(t)
, we get

(69)

S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)=\sum_{j=k}^{n}\binom{n}{j}(n-1)_{n-j}p^{j}(p-1)^{n-j}S_{1,\lambda}(j,k),\quad\mathrm{for}\,\,\,n\geq k.

.

(e) Let
Y
be the exponential random variable with parameter
\alpha>0
. Then the probability density function of
Y
is given by

(70)

f(y)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}\alpha e^{-\alpha y},&\textrm{if \,\,$y\geq 0$,}\\
0,&\textrm{if\,\, $y<0$}.\end{array}\right.

Then we have

(71)

\displaystyle E[Y]=\frac{1}{\alpha},\,\,\,E[e^{Yt}]=\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-t},\,\,\,e_{Y}(t)=\frac{t}{\alpha-t},

\displaystyle\bar{e}_{Y}(t)=\frac{\alpha t}{1+t},\,\,\,\bar{f}_{Y}(t)=\log\big(\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-t}\big),\,\,\,f_{Y}(t)=\alpha(1-e^{-t}).

By using (
71
), we have

(72)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\big(e_{Y}(t)\big)^{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\Big(\frac{t}{\alpha-t}\Big)^{k}=\frac{1}{k!}\frac{1}{\alpha^{k}}t^{k}\big(1-\frac{t}{\alpha}\big)^{-k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\frac{1}{\alpha^{k}}t^{k}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(k+n-1)_{n}\frac{1}{n!}\Big(\frac{t}{\alpha}\Big)^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(k+n-1)_{n}\frac{1}{n!k!}\frac{1}{\alpha^{n+k}}t^{n+k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\binom{n}{k}(n-1)_{n-k}\frac{1}{\alpha^{n}}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Thus (

(73)

S_{2}^{Y}(n,k)=\binom{n}{k}(n-1)_{n-k}\frac{1}{\alpha^{n}},\quad\mathrm{for}\,\,\,n\geq k.

Next, by using (
71
), we obtain

(74)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\big(\bar{e}_{Y}(t)\big)^{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\Big(\frac{\alpha t}{1+t}\Big)=\frac{\alpha^{k}}{k!}t^{k}(1+t)^{-k}

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha^{k}}{k!}t^{k}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}(k+n-1)_{n}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha^{k}}{k!}t^{k}\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}(-1)^{n-k}(n-1)_{n-k}\frac{t^{n-k}}{(n-k)!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{n-k}(n-1)_{n-k}\alpha^{k}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore (

(75)

S_{1}^{Y}(n,k)=(-1)^{n-k}\binom{n}{k}(n-1)_{n-k}\alpha^{k},\quad\mathrm{for}\,\,\,n\geq k.

From (
33
) and (
70
), we observe

(76)

\displaystyle E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e_{\lambda}^{y}(t)f(y)dy=\alpha\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-y\big(\alpha-\frac{1}{\lambda}\log(1+\lambda t)\big)}dy

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-\log e_{\lambda}(t)},\quad\Big(t<\frac{e^{\lambda\alpha}-1}{\lambda}\Big).

(77)

\displaystyle e_{Y,\lambda}(t)=\frac{\log(1+\lambda t)}{\alpha\lambda-\log(1+\lambda t)},\,\,\,\bar{e}_{Y,\lambda}(t)=\frac{1}{\lambda}\big(e^{\frac{\alpha\lambda t}{1+t}}-1\big),

\displaystyle\bar{f}_{Y,\lambda}(t)=\log\Big(\frac{\alpha\lambda}{\alpha\lambda-\log(1+\lambda t)}\Big),\,\,\,f_{Y,\lambda}(t)=\frac{1}{\lambda}\big(e^{\alpha\lambda(1-e^{-t})}-1\big).

77

72
) with
t
replaced by
\log(1+\lambda t)
and
\alpha
by
\alpha\lambda

(78)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\big(e_{Y,\lambda}(t)\big)^{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\bigg(\frac{\log(1+\lambda t)}{\alpha\lambda-\log(1+\lambda t)}\bigg)^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=k}^{\infty}\binom{j}{k}(j-1)_{j-k}\frac{1}{(\alpha\lambda)^{j}}\frac{1}{j!}\big(\log(1+\lambda t)\big)^{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=k}^{\infty}\binom{j}{k}(j-1)_{j-k}\frac{1}{(\alpha\lambda)^{j}}\sum_{n=j}^{\infty}S_{1}(n,j)\lambda^{n}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\sum_{j=k}^{n}\binom{j}{k}(j-1)_{j-k}\frac{1}{\alpha^{j}}\lambda^{n-j}S_{1}(n,j)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Thus (
78
) shows that

(79)

S_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)=\sum_{j=k}^{n}\binom{j}{k}(j-1)_{j-k}\frac{1}{\alpha^{j}}\lambda^{n-j}S_{1}(n,j),\quad\mathrm{for}\,\,\,n\geq k.

Using (
77
) and (
74
) with
\alpha
replaced by
\alpha\lambda
, we get

(80)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\big(\bar{e}_{Y,\lambda}\big)^{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda^{k}}\frac{1}{k!}\Big(e^{\frac{\alpha\lambda t}{1+t}}-1\Big)^{k}=\frac{1}{\lambda^{k}}\sum_{j=k}^{\infty}S_{2}(j,k)\frac{1}{j!}\Big(\frac{\alpha\lambda t}{1+t}\Big)^{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda^{k}}\sum_{j=k}^{\infty}S_{2}(j,k)\sum_{n=j}^{\infty}\binom{n}{j}(-1)^{n-j}(n-1)_{n-j}(\alpha\lambda)^{j}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\sum_{j=k}^{n}\binom{n}{j}(-1)^{n-j}(n-1)_{n-j}\alpha^{j}\lambda^{j-k}S_{2}(j,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore (
80
) shows that

(81)

S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)=\sum_{j=k}^{n}\binom{n}{j}(-1)^{n-j}(n-1)_{n-j}\alpha^{j}\lambda^{j-k}S_{2}(j,k),\quad\mathrm{for}\,\,\,n\geq k.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
Y
be the exponential random variable with parameter
\alpha>0
. Then, for
n\geq k
, we have

\displaystyle S_{2}^{Y}(n,k)=\binom{n}{k}(n-1)_{n-k}\frac{1}{\alpha^{n}},\,\,\,S_{1}^{Y}(n,k)=(-1)^{n-k}\binom{n}{k}(n-1)_{n-k}\alpha^{k},

(f) Let
Y
be the gamma random variable with parameters
\alpha,\beta>0
. Then the probability density function of
Y
is given by

(82)

f(y)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}\beta e^{-\beta y}\frac{(\beta y)^{\alpha-1}}{\Gamma(\alpha)},&\textrm{if\,\, $y\geq 0$,}\\
0,&\textrm{if \,\,$y<0$},\end{array}\right.

Then we have

(83)

\displaystyle E[Y]=\frac{\alpha}{\beta},\,\,\,E[e^{Yt}]=\Big(\frac{\beta}{\beta-t}\Big)^{\alpha},\,\,\,e_{Y}(t)=\Big(\frac{\beta}{\beta-t}\Big)^{\alpha}-1,

\displaystyle\bar{e}_{Y}(t)=\beta\big(1-(1+t)^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}\big),\,\,\,\bar{f}_{Y}(t)=\alpha\log\big(\frac{\beta}{\beta-t}\big),\,\,\,f_{Y}(t)=\beta\big(1-e^{-\frac{t}{\alpha}}\big).

Using (
83
), we get

(84)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\big(e_{Y}(t)\big)^{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\Big(\Big(\frac{\beta}{\beta-t}\Big)^{\alpha}-1\Big)^{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{k-j}\Big(\frac{1}{1-\frac{t}{\beta}}\Big)^{\alpha j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!\beta^{n}}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{k-j}(\alpha j+n-1)_{n}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Thus (
84
) shows that

(85)

S_{2}^{Y}(n,k)=\frac{1}{k!\beta^{n}}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{k-j}(\alpha j+n-1)_{n},\quad\mathrm{for}\,\,\,n\geq k,

and

(86)

\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{k-j}(\alpha j+n-1)_{n}=0,\quad\mathrm{for}\,\,\,0\leq n<k.

Next, by using (
83
), we consider

(87)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\big(\bar{e}_{Y}(t)\big)^{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\beta^{k}\big(1-(1+t)^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}\big)^{k}=\frac{1}{k!}\beta^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{j}(1+t)^{-\frac{j}{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\beta^{k}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\big(-\frac{1}{\alpha}\big)^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{j}\big(j+(n-1)\alpha)_{n,\alpha}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Thus (
87

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{k-j}e^{ajt}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}S_{2}(l+j,j)\frac{j!}{(l+j)!}\big((b-a)t\big)^{l}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{k-j}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{l=0}^{n}\binom{n}{l}\binom{l+j}{j}^{-1}(aj)^{n-l}(b-a)^{l}S_{2}(l+j,j)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\sum_{l=0}^{n}\binom{n}{l}\binom{k}{j}\binom{l+j}{j}^{-1}(-1)^{k-j}(aj)^{n-l}(b-a)^{l}S_{2}(l+j,j)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Thus (
114
) shows that, for
n\geq k
,

(115)

S_{2}^{Y}(n,k)=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\sum_{l=0}^{n}\binom{n}{l}\binom{k}{j}\binom{l+j}{j}^{-1}(-1)^{k-j}(aj)^{n-l}(b-a)^{l}S_{2}(l+j,j),

and, for
0\leq n<k
,

(116)

\sum_{j=0}^{k}\sum_{l=0}^{n}\binom{n}{l}\binom{k}{j}\binom{l+j}{j}^{-1}(-1)^{k-j}(aj)^{n-l}(b-a)^{l}S_{2}(l+j,j)=0.

In the special case of
a=0
, we have

(117)

S_{2}^{Y}(n,k)=\frac{1}{k!}b^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}\binom{n+j}{j}^{-1}(-1)^{k-j}S_{2}(n+j,j),\quad\mathrm{for}\,\,\,n\geq k,

and

(118)

\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}\binom{n+j}{j}^{-1}(-1)^{k-j}S_{2}(n+j,j)=0,\quad\mathrm{for}\,\,\,0\leq n<k.

Next, we consider the degenerate case. First, we observe that

(119)

\displaystyle E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e_{\lambda}^{y}(t)f(y)dy=\frac{1}{b-a}\int_{a}^{b}e_{\lambda}^{y}(t)dy

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{b-a}\int_{a}^{b}e^{y\log e_{\lambda}(t)}dy=\frac{e_{\lambda}^{b}(t)-e_{\lambda}^{a}(t)}{(b-a)\log e_{\lambda}(t)}.

From (
119
), we get

(120)

e_{Y,\lambda}(t)=\frac{e_{\lambda}^{b}(t)-e_{\lambda}^{a}(t)}{(b-a)\log e_{\lambda}(t)}-1,\,\,\,\bar{f}_{Y,\lambda}(t)=\log\bigg(\frac{e_{\lambda}^{b}(t)-e_{\lambda}^{a}(t)}{(b-a)\log e_{\lambda}(t)}\bigg).

From (
120
) and using (
114
) with
t
replaced by
\log e_{\lambda}(t)=\frac{1}{\lambda}\log(1+\lambda t)
, we have

(121)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\big(e_{Y,\lambda}(t)\big)^{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\Bigg(\frac{e_{\lambda}^{b}(t)-e_{\lambda}^{a}(t)}{(b-a)\log e_{\lambda}(t)}-1\bigg)^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\sum_{l=0}^{m}\frac{1}{k!}\binom{m}{l}\binom{k}{j}\binom{l+j}{j}^{-1}(-1)^{k-j}(aj)^{m-l}(b-a)^{l}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\times S_{2}(l+j,j)\frac{1}{\lambda^{m}}\frac{1}{m!}\big(\log(1+\lambda t\big)^{m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\sum_{l=0}^{m}\frac{1}{k!}\binom{m}{l}\binom{k}{j}\binom{l+j}{j}^{-1}(-1)^{k-j}(aj)^{m-l}(b-a)^{l}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\times S_{2}(l+j,j)\frac{1}{\lambda^{m}}\sum_{n=m}^{\infty}S_{1}(n,m)\frac{\lambda^{n}t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=0}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\sum_{l=0}^{m}\frac{1}{k!}\binom{m}{l}\binom{k}{j}\binom{l+j}{j}^{-1}(-1)^{k-j}(aj)^{m-l}(b-a)^{l}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\times\lambda^{n-m}S_{2}(l+j,j)S_{1}(n,m)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Thus (
121
) shows that, for
n\geq k
,

(122)

\displaystyle S_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{m=0}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\sum_{l=0}^{m}\binom{m}{l}\binom{k}{j}\binom{l+j}{j}^{-1}(-1)^{k-j}(aj)^{m-l}(b-a)^{l}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\times\lambda^{n-m}S_{2}(l+j,j)S_{1}(n,m),

and, for
0\leq n<k
,

(123)

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\sum_{l=0}^{m}\binom{m}{l}\binom{k}{j}\binom{l+j}{j}^{-1}(-1)^{k-j}(aj)^{m-l}(b-a)^{l}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\times\lambda^{n-m}S_{2}(l+j,j)S_{1}(n,m)=0.

In the special case of
a=0
, we get,  for
n\geq k
,

(124)

S_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{m=0}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}\binom{m+j}{j}^{-1}(-1)^{k-j}b^{m}\lambda^{n-m}S_{2}(m+j,j)S_{1}(n,m),

and,   for
0\leq n<k
.

(125)

\sum_{m=0}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}\binom{m+j}{j}^{-1}(-1)^{k-j}b^{m}\lambda^{n-m}S_{2}(m+j,j)S_{1}(n,m)=0.

From (
115
) and (
122
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
Y
be the uniform random variable over
(a,b)
, with
a+b\neq 0
. For
n\geq k
, we have

\displaystyle S_{2}^{Y}(n,k)=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\sum_{l=0}^{n}\binom{n}{l}\binom{k}{j}\binom{l+j}{j}^{-1}(-1)^{k-j}(aj)^{n-l}(b-a)^{l}S_{2}(l+j,j),

\displaystyle S_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{m=0}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\sum_{l=0}^{m}\binom{m}{l}\binom{k}{j}\binom{l+j}{j}^{-1}(-1)^{k-j}(aj)^{m-l}(b-a)^{l}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\lambda^{n-m}S_{2}(l+j,j)S_{1}(n,m).

In the special case of
a=0
, from (
117
) and (
124
), we have

Corollary 2.9
.

Let
Y
be the uniform random variable over
(0,b)
, with
b>0
. For
n\geq k
, we have

\displaystyle S_{2}^{Y}(n,k)=\frac{1}{k!}b^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}\binom{n+j}{j}^{-1}(-1)^{k-j}S_{2}(n+j,j),

\displaystyle S_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{m=0}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}\binom{m+j}{j}^{-1}(-1)^{k-j}b^{m}\lambda^{n-m}S_{2}(m+j,j)S_{1}(n,m).

Unlike the cases of
(a)-(g)
, we were not able to find some expressions for
\bar{e}_{Y}(t)
and
\bar{e}_{Y,\lambda}(t)
in the case of (h). Consequently, we were not able to determine
S_{1}^{Y}(n,k),\,\,\,f_{Y}(t),\,\,\,S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)
, and
f_{Y,\lambda}(t)
. We leave this as an open question to the reader.

3.
Some applications

By using Proposition 1.1 (a), Proposition 1.2 (a), and Theorem 2.4, we get the following proposition.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
p
be a real number satisfying
0<p<1
. Then we have the following orthogonality relations:

\displaystyle\sum_{k=l}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\sum_{m=l}^{k}(-1)^{j}\frac{1}{k!}\binom{k}{j}\binom{k}{m}(k-1)_{k-m}\Big(\frac{p}{p-1}\Big)^{m}H_{n}^{(j)}\big(\frac{1}{1-p}\big)S_{1}(m,l)=\delta_{n,l},

\displaystyle\sum_{k=l}^{n}\sum_{j=k}^{n}\sum_{m=0}^{l}(-1)^{m}\frac{1}{l!}\binom{n}{j}\binom{l}{m}(n-1)_{n-j}\Big(\frac{p}{p-1}\Big)^{j}(p-1)^{n-l}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times S_{1}(j,k)H_{k}^{(m)}\big(\frac{1}{1-p}\big)=\delta_{n,l},

\displaystyle\sum_{k=l}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\sum_{m=l}^{k}(-1)^{j}\frac{1}{k!}\binom{k}{j}\binom{k}{m}(k-1)_{k-m}\Big(\frac{p}{p-1}\Big)^{m}h_{n,\lambda}^{(j)}\big(\frac{1}{1-p}\big)S_{1,\lambda}(m,l)=\delta_{n,l},

\displaystyle\sum_{k=l}^{n}\sum_{j=k}^{n}\sum_{m=0}^{l}(-1)^{m}\frac{1}{l!}\binom{n}{j}\binom{l}{m}(n-1)_{n-j}\Big(\frac{p}{p-1}\Big)^{j}(p-1)^{n-l}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times S_{1,\lambda}(j,k)h_{k,\lambda}^{(m)}\big(\frac{1}{1-p}\big)=\delta_{n,l},

where
H_{n}^{(j)}(u)
are the Frobenius-Euler numbers of order
j
(see (
59
)) and
h_{n}^{(j)}(u)
are the degenerate Frobenius-Euler numbers of order
j
(see (
68
)).

Remark 3.2
.

The interested reader can obtain other orthogonality relations by using the results in Theorems 2.1-2.3 and Theorems 2.5-2.7. We note here that many important orthogonality and inverse relations can be found in Chapters 2 and 3 of [25] and Chapter 5 of [26].

From the definition of the probabilistic Euler polynomials
\mathcal{E}_{n}^{Y}(x)
in (
26
), we see that
\mathcal{E}_{n}^{Y}(x)
is Sheffer for the pair
\big(\frac{e^{t}+1}{2},f_{Y}(t)\big)
, with
\bar{f}_{Y}(t)=\log E[e^{Yt}]
. For the basics about umbral calculus, one refers to [26]. However, no knowledge about umbral calculus is needed in this paper. We observe that

(126)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\big(\mathcal{E}_{n}^{Y}(x+1)+\mathcal{E}_{n}^{Y}(x)\big)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=2\big(1+E[e^{Yt}]-1\big)^{x}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(x)_{k}\frac{1}{k!}\big(E[e^{Yt}]-1\big)^{k}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n}(x)_{k}S_{2}^{Y}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Thus (
126
) shows that

(127)

\big(\mathcal{E}_{n}^{Y}(x+1)+\mathcal{E}_{n}^{Y}(x)\big)=2\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2}^{Y}(n,k)(x)_{k}.

Let
q(x)
be any polynomial of degree
n
with complex coefficients, and let

(128)

q(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{k}\mathcal{E}_{k}^{Y}(x).

Now, we consider

(129)

a(x)=q(x+1)+q(x).

Then, by using (
127
)-(
129
), we have

(130)

\displaystyle a(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{k}\big(\mathcal{E}_{k}^{Y}(x+1)+\mathcal{E}_{k}^{Y}(x)\big)

\displaystyle=2\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{k}a_{k}S_{2}^{Y}(k,j)(x)_{j}.

From (
130
) and by using (
29
)-(
31
), we obtain

(131)

\Delta^{r}a(x)=2\sum_{k=r}^{n}\sum_{j=r}^{k}a_{k}S_{2}^{Y}(k,j)(j)_{r}(x)_{j-r}.

Hence, from (
131
), we get

(132)

\frac{1}{2r!}\Delta^{r}a(x)|_{x=0}=\sum_{k=r}^{n}S_{2}^{Y}(k,r)a_{k}.

From (
129
) and (
132
), and by invoking the inversion relation in Proposition 1.1, we have

(133)

\displaystyle a_{r}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=r}^{n}S_{1}^{Y}(j,r)\frac{1}{j!}\Delta^{j}a(x)|_{x=0}

(134)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=r}^{n}S_{1}^{Y}(j,r)\frac{1}{j!}\big(\Delta^{j}q(1)+\Delta^{j}q(0)\big).

From (
133
) and (
28
), we obtain the alternative expressions given by

(135)

\displaystyle a_{r}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=r}^{n}\sum_{i=0}^{j}(-1)^{j-i}\frac{1}{j!}\binom{j}{i}S_{1}^{Y}(j,r)\big(q(i+1)+q(i)\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=r}^{n}\sum_{l=j}^{n}S_{1}^{Y}(j,r)S_{2}(l,j)\frac{1}{l!}\big(q^{(l)}(1)+q^{(l)}(0)\big).

Thus, from (
134
) and (
135
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
q(x)
be any polynomial of degree
n
with complex coefficients, and let

q(x)=\sum_{r=0}^{n}a_{r}\mathcal{E}_{r}^{Y}(x).

Then the coefficients are determined by

\displaystyle a_{r}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=r}^{n}S_{1}^{Y}(j,r)\frac{1}{j!}\big(\Delta^{j}q(1)+\Delta^{j}q(0)\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=r}^{n}\sum_{i=0}^{j}(-1)^{j-i}\frac{1}{j!}\binom{j}{i}S_{1}^{Y}(j,r)\big(q(i+1)+q(i)\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=r}^{n}\sum_{l=j}^{n}S_{1}^{Y}(j,r)S_{2}(l,j)\frac{1}{l!}\big(q^{(l)}(1)+q^{(l)}(0)\big).

Proceeding analogously to the above, we can show the next theorem.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
q(x)
be any polynomial of degree
n
with complex coefficients, and let

q(x)=\sum_{r=0}^{n}a_{r}\mathcal{E}_{r,\lambda}^{Y}(x).

Then the coefficients are determined by

\displaystyle a_{r}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=r}^{n}S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(j,r)\frac{1}{j!}\big(\Delta^{j}q(1)+\Delta^{j}q(0)\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=r}^{n}\sum_{i=0}^{j}(-1)^{j-i}\frac{1}{j!}\binom{j}{i}S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(j,r)\big(q(i+1)+q(i)\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=r}^{n}\sum_{l=j}^{n}S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(j,r)S_{2}(l,j)\frac{1}{l!}\big(q^{(l)}(1)+q^{(l)}(0)\big).

Remark 3.5
.

We have established the necessity of inverse relations for expressing arbitrary polynomials in terms of the probabilistic Euler polynomials,
\mathcal{E}_{n}^{Y}(x)
, and the probabilistic degenerate Euler polynomials,
\mathcal{E}_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)
. Theorems 2.1 through 2.7 provide explicit formulas for
S_{1}^{Y}(j,r)
and
S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(j,r)
for several discrete and continuous random variables
Y
, thus enabling the explicit determination of these polynomial representations for those specific random variables. A more comprehensive analysis of such representations will be presented in subsequent publications.

4.
Conclusion

The study of Stirling numbers (of both kinds) and their degenerate versions has been extended to a probabilistic context. This involves defining probabilistic Stirling numbers of the first and second kind, both in standard and degenerate forms, associated with a random variable Y. These probabilistic versions are constructed to maintain important properties like orthogonality and inverse relations, which are crucial for applications like expressing polynomials in terms of probabilistic Euler polynomials. This builds upon prior work in defining probabilistic special numbers and polynomials, demonstrating a broader trend of incorporating probability into the study of these mathematical objects.

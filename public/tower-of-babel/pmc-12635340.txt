The application of channel attention mechanism for fusion of painting and live-action footage under deep learning and animation generation technology
Authors: Wenjing Zhu, Mengyao Zhou
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn animation generation combining paintings and live-action footage, the core challenge for cross-modal visual content creation lies in effectively coordinating feature discrepancies across multimodal data while achieving adaptive enhancement of key information. This study proposes a deep learning model based on the channel attention mechanism (CAM). It systematically addresses cross-modal fusion difficulties between artistic semantic features and live-action visual information through a dual-path feature fusion framework and dynamic weight allocation strategy. The study employs an encoder-decoder architecture where Residual Network-50 (ResNet-50) extracts multimodal features, channel attention modules perform importance weighting on feature channels, and Transformer decoders generate animation sequences. The framework incorporates feature alignment loss functions and dynamic weight ablation experiments to strengthen cross-modal feature integration capabilities. Experimental results demonstrate that the model achieves an 11.0% improvement in peak signal-to-noise ratio and an 8.8% enhancement in structural similarity index on the custom test set. This confirms high reconstruction accuracy at both pixel and structural levels. The Fréchet Inception Distance decreases by 23.4%. Meanwhile, multimodal fusion degree and cross-modal feature dynamic coupling index increase by 21.9% and 42.3% respectively, significantly optimizing feature distribution consistency and modal interaction efficiency. Ablation studies reveal that the CAM improves model performance by approximately 15%, and the dynamic weight strategy effectively enhances robustness against parameter perturbations. This study provides both theoretical foundations and technical solutions for virtual-real fusion in digital art creation, advancing animation generation toward intelligent and artistic development.Keywords: Painting, Live-action footage, Animation generation, Cross-modal, Attention mechanismSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Animation generation technology represents a core field in visual content creation during this era of deep convergence between digital art and multimedia technology. The field is currently experiencing a revolutionary transformation from conventional hand-drawn and live-action editing techniques toward intelligent and automated production methodologies1. The vigorous development of deep learning technology has injected strong impetus into the animation domain. Through its autonomous learning and feature extraction abilities from massive data, it has broken the bottlenecks of traditional creation models in terms of efficiency and creative expression2. The fusion technology combining paintings with live-action footage represents an emerging direction in animation production, seeking to integrate the imagination of artistic creation with the realism of real scenes. This approach constructs immersive visual experiences through the sophisticated interweaving of virtual and real elements. It has been widely applied in multiple fields such as film and television special effects, virtual reality (VR), and game production3.

However, in generating fusion animations of painting and live-action footage, accurately capturing and strengthening key visual information, and coordinating the feature expression of different modal data have become core issues restricting technological development4. As an important means to optimize network performance in deep learning, the channel attention mechanism (CAM) can focus on the key semantic information of images through adaptive weight allocation for feature channels. Meanwhile, it effectively improves the model’s ability to process complex visual data. Introducing the CAM into the animation generation technology that fuses painting and live-action footage is expected to solve the feature fusion problems caused by modal differences; it also brings visual effects with more layering, detailed expressiveness, and artistic appeal to animation works5.

Currently, deep learning has achieved significant results in image generation, video processing, and other directions. However, systematic research on the CAM in the scenario of fusing painting and live-action footage remains relatively scarce. Based on this research gap, this study explores the innovative application of the CAM in animation generation technology. The study aims to construct a more efficient and intelligent animation generation model and provide theoretical support and technical solutions for digital art creation. Thus, it can promote the animation field to develop in a higher-quality and more innovative direction.

Literature review

In the context of the deep integration of digital technology and artistic creation, the research and innovation of animation generation technology have become the focus of interdisciplinary fields. In recent years, related research achievements have provided vital theoretical and practical support for exploring the boundaries of animation technology.

Stadlinger et al. (2021) were the first to focus on the value of video animation in technology-enhanced learning. Through empirical research, they confirmed that animated visual expression could remarkably enhance knowledge transfer efficiency, laying a foundation for the application of animation technology in the education field6. Jiang et al. (2022) focused on film and television animation design. Through quantitative analysis, they found that digital media technology could effectively improve the visual rendering efficiency and artistic expressiveness of film and television animation, providing a key basis for the technical creation of animation7. Wang et al. (2022) further combined VR modeling with fractal deformation technology, proposing an animation design method for dynamic morphological topology transformation. They realized the collaborative optimization of virtual scene structure and motion trajectory and pushed immersive animation creation to a new height8.

With the deepening of research, Tang et al. (2022) proposed an online animation design studio teaching model under the background of artificial intelligence (AI). Through interdisciplinary collaboration and technology integration, they innovated the practical system of animation design education9. Kumar et al. (2023) integrated gamified learning theory with next-generation Artificial Intelligence of Medical Things (AIoMT) technology. With the help of three-dimensional (3D) animation and VR simulation, they constructed an interactive learning system with dynamic evaluation functions, expanding the application of animation technology in the field of educational assessment10. Zhen et al. (2023) reviewed the leading generation technologies in the domain of human-computer interaction. The expression migration model they proposed provided technical support for the dynamic expression of animated characters11.

Hashim et al. (2024) constructed an analytical framework for the application of animation technology in VR through a systematic literature review. This revealed the technical development laws in dimensions such as rendering efficiency, interaction logic, and immersive experience12. In the same year, Kumar (2024) further deeply integrated gamified training with AI, VR, and animation design. They proposed an efficient training paradigm based on immersive technology and provided an innovative path for professional skills training13. Zhu et al. (2024) first introduced edge intelligence and large model technology into animation design. They optimized model reasoning efficiency through distributed computing and promoted the development of animation generation towards intelligence14. In the latest research, Zhao (2025) focused on the field of film and television performance. Their work realized the accurate alignment of virtual characters and real performances through cross-modal fusion of motion capture technology and animation generation methods. This verified the important value of multi-modal data fusion in improving artistic realism15. Engelson et al. (2025) focused on the efficiency of three-dimensional (3D) graphic animation code generation and proposed a method to automatically generate efficient OpenGL animation code from Mathematica object-oriented models. By simplifying the code development process of 3D animation, they improved the technical implementation efficiency of animation generation. Their exploration of “underlying optimization of animation generation technology” had a logical consistency with this study’s pursuit of efficient implementation of cross-modal fusion animation. Additionally, the exploration provided a reference for the engineering feasibility of the proposed model architecture16. Zulkifli and Jamaludin (2025) combined animation technology with cultural content dissemination and visualized the biographies of Malaysian historical figures in the form of animation. They enhanced the content dissemination effect by strengthening the narrativity and visual appeal of animation. Their core goal of “enhancing visual expression with animation” echoes the direction of this study, which is “integrating painting and live-action footage to improve the artistic expressiveness of animation”. Meanwhile, it highlights the innovation of this study in introducing CAM to solve cross-modal feature differences and further optimize visual fusion effects. Moreover, it makes up for the deficiencies of existing studies in the aspect of multi-modal feature collaboration17.

The above studies have made significant progress in expanding the application scenarios of animation technology, innovating creation methods, and integrating intelligent technologies. However, there remains a key technical gap in the cross-modal fusion animation generation between live-action footage and painting. Existing studies lack an adaptive weight allocation mechanism for painting semantic features and live-action visual information, making it difficult to effectively coordinate feature differences between multi-modal data. This study focuses on this deficiency, explores the application of the CAM in fusion animation generation between painting and live-action footage. Meanwhile, the study constructs a more efficient animation generation model through selective enhancement and dynamic fusion of cross-modal features, offering innovative solutions for multi-modal animation creation.

After CAM processes the features extracted by ResNet-50 to screen key channels, they are then decoded by the Transformer. Compared with a unified Transformer system, this process makes it easier to balance the feature differences between the two modalities, avoid modal information confusion, and highlight the rationality and innovation of the architecture.

Experimental design and performance evaluation

Datasets collection

In research on animation generation and multi-modal data fusion, the selection of public datasets must consider the diversity, annotation completeness, and scene richness of both paintings and live-action footage. Currently, the combination of the Microsoft Common Objects in Context-Stuff (Microsoft COCO-Stuff) dataset and Google Open Images V6 can provide ideal experimental materials for this study31. The Microsoft COCO-Stuff dataset is an extension of the classic COCO dataset. It contains approximately 123,000 real-scene images with instance-level annotations, and adds pixel-level segmentation annotations for 91 “stuff” categories (such as grass, sky, and other background elements). It provides sufficient samples for the semantic feature extraction of live-action footage. Meanwhile, the dataset covers diverse scenes such as people, buildings, and natural landscapes, which can simulate common types of live-action materials in animation creation32.

To supplement painting modal data, the “Artwork” subset of the Google Open Images V6 dataset has a unique value. This subset contains over 100,000 artistic painting images, covering various painting styles such as oil painting, watercolor, and sketch. Meanwhile, this subset provides category labels and object bounding box annotations, which can effectively support the training and analysis of painting features. In addition, the dataset ensures data quality through a combination of crowdsourced annotation and manual review, reducing annotation errors caused by subjective differences in painting styles.

After integrating the two datasets, painting and live-action footage paired samples can be constructed through data augmentation techniques (such as random cropping, rotation, and color transformation). For example, live-action images of urban street scenes are selected from the COCO-Stuff dataset and matched with paintings depicting similar scenes in Open Images V6 to form multi-modal data pairs. Meanwhile, to adapt to animation generation tasks, images can be serialized into short video clips, and transition frames can be generated through dynamic interpolation algorithms to build training samples with temporal continuity. This dataset combination not only meets the modal complementarity needs of paintings and live-action footage but also provides a sufficient data basis for verifying the effectiveness of CAM in multi-modal feature fusion.

Experimental environment

This study’s experiments are built on a high-performance computing environment to ensure efficient training and testing of complex deep learning models. At the hardware level, an NVIDIA DGX A100 server is used as the core computing device. This server is equipped with 8 NVIDIA A100 Tensor Core Graphics Processing Units (GPUs), each with a memory capacity of 80 gigabytes (GB). It supports up to 312 Tera Floating-Point Operations Per Second (TFLOPS) of 16-bit floating-point computing power, providing sufficient computing resources for multi-modal data processing and model parallel computing. The central processing unit is an AMD EPYC 7742 64-Core Processor with a main frequency of 2.25 GHz, combined with 512GB Double Data Rate 4 (DDR4) memory, ensuring efficient data reading and preprocessing. The storage system uses a high-speed Non-Volatile Memory Express (NVMe) Solid State Drive (SSD) array with a total capacity of 4 terabytes (TB). This ensures fast loading of datasets and efficient storage and retrieval of intermediate calculation results.

In terms of software environment, the operating system adopts Ubuntu 20.04 Long-Term Support (LTS) to provide a stable open-source computing platform. The deep learning framework is developed based on PyTorch 1.12.1, which supports dynamic computation graph features, facilitating flexible adjustment and rapid iteration of model structures. Meanwhile, it combines Compute Unified Device Architecture (CUDA) 11.6 and CUDA Deep Neural Network library (cuDNN) 8.4.1 acceleration libraries to fully utilize the parallel computing capabilities of GPUs. Model visualization and data preprocessing rely on Open Source Computer Vision Library (OpenCV) 4.5.5 and Pillow 8.3.2 libraries to achieve efficient reading, enhancement, and visual analysis of image and video data. Moreover, Tensor Board 2.9.1 is used for real-time monitoring of the training process and visualization of performance indicators. The Weights & Biases (WandB) platform is employed for systematic recording and comparative analysis of experimental results, ensuring the reproducibility of experiments and the scientificity of parameter tuning.

Performance evaluation

In animation generation, the dynamic interaction of multi-modal features between painting and live-action footage in the spatiotemporal dimension directly affects the fusion effect and visual coherence. Traditional evaluation methods mostly focus on static indicators, making it difficult to quantify the temporal synergy and coupling efficiency of features. To address this, this study proposes a dynamic feature interaction evaluation system. Starting from the temporal evolution and spatial correlation of cross-modal features, it constructs innovative quantitative indicators to deeply analyze the model’s ability to process dynamic scenes. In Fig. 4, experimental data indicate that the proposed model performs prominently in pixel-level and structural similarity evaluations. The Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR) reaches 28.73dB (11.0% higher than the baseline model). The Structural Similarity Index (SSIM) is 0.891 (8.8% higher), showing a high degree of consistency between the generated animation and real samples. This confirms the proposed model’s ability to deeply fuse painting and live-action footage. The result of the Vision Transformer Perception Score (ViT-PScore) of 0.905 shows that the generated animation is closer to real samples in terms of high-level semantic features. This data difference clearly shows that traditional methods and baseline models are prone to “feature fusion imbalance” when weight parameters are fine-tuned. For example, an excessive bias toward the painting modality leads to the loss of live-action scene details (such as building outlines and road textures); an excessive bias toward the live-action modality weakens the painting’s artistic style (such as brush stroke blurring and color tension), both of which cause a sharp drop in PSNR. In contrast, the small fluctuation of the proposed model proves that it can effectively avoid such problems. In summary, the experimental results in Fig. 6 quantitatively verify the high robustness of the proposed model under parameter perturbation. Discussion

This study aims to explore the application of CAM in the generation of fusion animations between paintings and live-action footage. By constructing a deep learning model that integrates dual-path feature fusion and dynamic weight allocation, it addresses the problem of low fusion efficiency caused by feature differences in multi-modal data. Based on the encoder-decoder framework, the study combines the ResNet-50 and Transformer architectures. Through the channel attention module, it realizes adaptive weight allocation for painting semantic features and live-action visual information. At the same time, it introduces feature alignment loss and dynamic weight ablation experiments to strengthen the model’s ability to synergistically process cross-modal features.

Experimental results reveal that the proposed model performs prominently in quantitative indicators. The PSNR reaches 28.73dB, which is 11.0% higher than that of the baseline model. The SSIM achieves 0.891, with an increase of 8.8%, confirming high fidelity at the pixel and structural levels. In the evaluation of feature distribution and modal fusion, the FID value is 19.65, which is 23.4% lower than that of the traditional method. The MFD and the CMF-DCI are 0.762 and 0.812, which increase by 21.9% and 42.3%, respectively. This indicates that the model effectively promotes the deep coupling of painting and live-action features. Further verification through ablation experiments shows that removing CAM causes the PSNR to drop to 26.62dB and the MFD to 0.648. In the dynamic weight ablation experiment, the PSNR fluctuation range of the proposed model is only ± 0.8dB, remarkably lower than the ± 2.3dB of traditional methods. This demonstrates the key role of CAM in stabilizing feature fusion.

From a mechanism perspective, the channel attention module, through the combination of global pooling and MLP, realizes differential weight allocation for live-action scene details (e.g., texture edges) and painting brushstroke features (e.g., color saturation). When processing the fusion of Impressionist paintings and urban street scene footage, the proposed model automatically enhances the weights of high-saturation color channels in the paintings. Concurrently, it can strengthen the feature response of building outlines in the live-action footage. This dynamic adjustment enables the generated animation to retain artistic expressiveness while maintaining scene realism. The dual-path fusion strategy predicts fusion weights through a lightweight convolutional network, solving the problem of modal imbalance in traditional splicing methods. In the fusion scenario of anime characters and real street scenes, this strategy improves the naturalness of the transition between character edges and the background by 37%.

The innovative contributions of this study are reflected in three aspects. First, it proposes dynamic feature interaction evaluation indicators such as CMF-DCI and ViT-PScore, breaking through the limitations of traditional static indicators in evaluating temporal features. Second, it constructs a dual-path feature fusion framework based on channel attention, increasing the multi-modal feature fusion degree by 21.9%. Third, it verifies the model’s robustness to parameter disturbances through dynamic weight ablation experiments, offering a reliability basis for industrial deployment.

Conclusion

Research contribution

The core contribution of this study lies in constructing a cross-modal animation generation framework integrating CAM for paintings and live-action footage, breaking through the technical bottleneck of coordinating feature differences in traditional multi-modal fusion. The study implements a dual-path feature fusion strategy combined with a dynamic weight allocation mechanism to achieve adaptive integration between painting semantic features (e.g., brushstroke styles) and live-action visual information (e.g., scene textures). This approach yields a 21.9% improvement in MFD while attaining a CMF-DCI of 0.812. It proposes innovative evaluation indicators such as CMF-DCI and ViT-PScore, addressing the limitations of traditional static indicators in evaluating temporal features. Among them, ViT-PScore reaches 0.905, verifying the authenticity of generated animations in high-level semantics. Through dynamic weight ablation experiments, the model’s robustness under parameter disturbances is confirmed (PSNR fluctuation of ± 0.8dB), providing a quantitative basis for industrial deployment. Compared with existing studies, this study applies the CAM system to cross-modal animation generation for the first time, improving realism. Meanwhile, it retains artistic expressiveness and promotes the development of virtual-real fusion technology in digital art creation.

Future works and research limitations

This study has limitations in two aspects. First, the dynamic feature interaction evaluation system has not fully covered the subjective aesthetic dimension of artistic creation. Existing indicators insufficiently quantify the artistic expressiveness of painting brushstrokes and lack correlation analysis with human aesthetic preferences. Second, the model still has the problem of feature imbalance when processing high dynamic range scenes. When there is a large brightness difference between painting elements and live-action scenes, the CAM is prone to bias in weight allocation for high-brightness areas, leading to local overexposure or underexposure in generated animations.

Future research can be conducted in three aspects. First, optimize the temporal continuity of animation sequences. The current model has motion blur issues when processing long videos. Diffusion models can be introduced to strengthen inter-frame temporal consistency, and the quality of transition frame generation can be optimized by learning the probability distribution of dynamic scenes. Second, promote a lightweight model to adapt to edge computing scenarios. The current model has high inference latency. Hence, it is necessary to significantly compress the number of parameters through knowledge distillation, model pruning, and quantization strategies to achieve efficient deployment on mobile devices. Third, construct professional multi-modal datasets. The current experimental data lack paired samples of extreme styles, such as traditional ink paintings and impressionist paintings, with high-resolution live-action footage. In the future, art institutions can collaborate to collect cross-modal data containing stylized brushstrokes and complex light and shadow, improving the model’s adaptability to extreme style differences.

Fully discrete error analysis of finite element discretizations of time-dependent Stokes equations in a stream-function formulation
Dmitriy Leykekhman, Boris Vexler, Jakob Wagner

Abstract
In this paper we establish best approximation type error estimates for the fully discrete Galerkin solutions of the time-dependent Stokes problem using the stream-function formulation. For the time discretization we use the discontinuous Galerkin method of arbitrary degree, whereas we present the space discretization in a general framework. This makes our result applicable for a wide variety of space discretization methods, provided some Galerkin orthogonality conditions are satisfied. As an example, conformal C 1 C^{1} and C 0 C^{0} interior penalty methods are covered by our analysis. The results do not require any additional regularity assumptions beyond the natural regularity given by the domain and data and can be used for optimal control problems.



keywords

Stokes problem, time-dependent Stokes, parabolic problems, steamline formulation, finite elements, discontinuous Galerkin, error estimates, best approximation, fully discrete

Introduction

In this paper we consider the following time-dependent Stokes problem with no-slip boundary conditions,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}-\Delta\mathbf{u}+\nabla p

\displaystyle=\mathbf{g}\quad

\displaystyle\text{in }I\times\Omega,

(1)

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}

\displaystyle=0\quad

\displaystyle\text{in }I\times\Omega,

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{0}\quad

\displaystyle\text{on }I\times\partial\Omega,

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}(0)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{u}_{0}

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega.

We assume that
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
, is a bounded, polygonal and simply connected
domain,
T>0
and
I=(0,T]
.
The finite element approximation of Stokes and Navier-Stokes problems is a mature and well researched topic due to its importance for understanding and computing fluid problems. The literature is immense, we only would like to mention the monographs
[
12
,
15
,
31
]
.

The numerical, and in particular finite element approximation of the problem, has a long history and the research is still very active. There are many challenges one faces in order to construct proper finite element spaces. It is well-known that the spaces for the discrete velocity
\mathbf{u}_{h}
and the discrete pressure
p_{h}
can not be taken arbitrary and the corresponding finite element spaces must satisfy the
inf-sup
condition. In addition, the continuity equation, i.e.,
\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}=0
, is not trivial to satisfy on the discrete level pointwise, and usually requires either high order methods
[
4
,
14
,
29
]
or very special constructions
[
19
,
20
]
. The time discretization poses new challenges. For example, on non-matching meshes, a discretely divergence free function on
the previous mesh is not discretely divergence free on the next mesh and needs to be projected, which
leads to oscillations in the pressure.
All these challenges are well-known and are addressed in more or less satisfactory fashion and constitute the standard part of the finite element theory.
Recently, the finite element theory and practices were looked in a new light and many standard finite element approximation schemes faced some criticism. The main objection of many standard finite element methods, like Taylor-Hood or MINI elements, are the facts that the discrete pressure and velocity are coupled and the error estimates for the velocity contain a pressure term. As a result, in some situations, the perturbations of the source term by a gradient field is not completely absorbed by the discrete pressure alone and may lead to a large error for the computed velocity
[
21
]
. This observation had led recently to a development of various
pressure robust
methods
[
2
,
3
,
26
]
.

An old two dimensional approach, that checks all the above mentioned points is the streamline function method where the velocity
\mathbf{u}
is taken as vectorial curl of a scalar function
\psi

\mathbf{u}=\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}}(\psi):=(\partial_{2}\psi,-\partial_{1}\psi)^{T}.

(2)

Its dual operator is given by the scalar curl of a vector field
\mathbf{v}

\operatorname{curl}(\mathbf{v})=\partial_{2}\mathbf{v}_{1}-\partial_{1}\mathbf{v}_{2}.

For
\mathbf{v}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)^{2},\psi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, there hold the following identities, to be understood in

H^{-1}(\Omega)^{2}
and
H^{-1}(\Omega)
, respectively:

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}}(\operatorname{curl}(\mathbf{v}))

\displaystyle=(\partial_{2}^{2}\mathbf{v}_{1}-\partial_{2}\partial_{1}\mathbf{v}_{2},-\partial_{1}\partial_{2}\mathbf{v}_{1}+\partial_{1}^{2}\mathbf{v}_{2})^{T}=\Delta\mathbf{v}-\nabla(\text{div }\mathbf{v})

(3)

\displaystyle\operatorname{curl}(\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}}(\psi))

\displaystyle=\Delta\psi.

(4)

For velocities
\mathbf{u}
satisfying the form (
2
), it clearly holds
\text{div }\mathbf{u}=0
,
and taking formally the negative scalar curl of the equation
(
1
), we find that the scalar function
\psi
satisfies the following fourth order equation with
f=-\operatorname{curl}(\mathbf{g})

\displaystyle-\partial_{t}\Delta\psi+\Delta^{2}\psi

\displaystyle=f\quad

\displaystyle\text{in }I\times\Omega,

(5)

\displaystyle\psi=\partial_{n}\psi

\displaystyle=0\quad

\displaystyle\text{on }I\times\partial\Omega,

\displaystyle\psi(0)

\displaystyle=\psi_{0}

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega,

where
\psi_{0}
is the solution to

\displaystyle-\Delta\psi_{0}

\displaystyle=-\operatorname{curl}(\mathbf{u}_{0})\quad

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega,

(6)

\displaystyle\psi_{0}

\displaystyle=0\quad

\displaystyle\text{on }\partial\Omega.

For simply connected
\Omega
, the equations (
1
) and (
5
)
are equivalent, for a proof, we refer to
[
17
]
.
Although the stationary problem is well investigated, (cf. Parts 4 and 5 in
[
18
]
),
it seems there is no clean theory of fully discrete finite element error estimates for Galerkin methods for the time dependent problem.
For example, a relatively recent paper
[
1
]
treated backward Euler for virtual elements and derived some fully discrete error estimates in
L^{\infty}(H^{1})
norm.
However, in order to achieve these estimates, the authors rely on regularity in time for spacial error estimates
and had to impose overly strict assumptions on

\partial_{tt}\psi
and
\mathbf{g}\in L^{\infty}(I;H^{r-1}(\Omega)^{2})
for some
r>\frac{1}{2}
.

In this paper, we take a first step in the direction of developing an elegant theory for the fully discrete error estimates similarly to the one for the second order parabolic problems
[
23
,
24
]
. We establish a best approximation type error estimate for discontinuous Galerkin discretizations in time of arbitrary degree and finite element discretization in space (that include conformal
C^{1}
elements as well as
C^{0}
interior penalty methods), namely

\lVert{\rm NewA}(\psi-\psi_{{k}h})\rVert_{L^{2}(I\times\Omega)}\leq C\Big(\lVert{\rm NewA}(\psi-{\chi})\rVert_{L^{2}(I\times\Omega)}+\lVert{\rm NewA}(\psi-R_{h}\psi)\rVert_{L^{2}(I\times\Omega)}+\lVert{\rm NewA}(\psi-\pi_{k}\psi)\rVert_{L^{2}(I\times\Omega)}\Big),

where
\psi_{kh}
is the fully discrete Galerkin solution for (
5
),
R_{h}
is the Galerkin projection of the stationary biharmonic problem and
\pi_{k}
is a certain time projection, and
\chi
is an arbitrary element from the finite dimensional space.
Under low regularity assumptions, i.e.
f\in L^{2}(I;H^{-1}(\Omega))
and
\psi_{0}\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
,
using the approximation properties of the above mentioned projections, we can establish the following optimal convergence result

\lVert{\rm NewA}(\psi-\psi_{kh})\rVert_{L^{2}(I\times\Omega)}\leq C(k+h^{2})\Big(\|f\|_{L^{2}(I;H^{-1}(\Omega))}+\|\psi_{0}\|_{H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)}\Big)

(7)

on convex polygonal domains.
The minimal assumptions we put on the data are satisfied by the usual assumptions on data for the time-dependent Stokes problem, namely
\mathbf{g}\in L^{2}(I;\mathbf{H})
and
\mathbf{u}_{0}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)^{2}
with
\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}_{0}=0
.
For a precise introduction of the space
\mathbf{H}
, see Subsection
2.3
.
Indeed we will argue later, see (
11
) and
Lemma

4
,
that under those assumptions it holds
f\in L^{2}(I;H^{-1}(\Omega))
and
\psi_{0}\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
.
As a result, taking
\mathbf{u}_{kh}=\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}}(\psi_{kh})
we obtain for the velocity solution
of (
1
)

\displaystyle\lVert\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}_{kh}\rVert_{L^{2}(I\times\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\lVert\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}}(\psi-\psi_{kh})\rVert_{L^{2}(I\times\Omega)}=\lVert{\rm NewA}(\psi-\psi_{kh})\rVert_{L^{2}(I\times\Omega)}

(8)

\displaystyle\leq C(k+h^{2})\left(\lVert\mathbf{g}\rVert_{L^{2}(I\times\Omega)}+\lVert\mathbf{u}_{0}\rVert_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\right).

As we have formulated our main result in a best approximation type form, higher order estimates
are also possible, assuming higher regularity of
\psi
.
The remainder of the paper is structured as follows:
in Section
2
, we summarize some results of the continuous Stokes problem,
and establish some results regarding the associated stationary and time-dependent biharmonic equations.
In Section
3
we introduce the space and time discretizations, keeping the

for the fully discrete problem.
We proceed to prove the best approximation type error estimate in Section
4
, with
our main result being
Theorem

23
. After this, we discuss the specific orders of

We conclude with some numerical experiments in Section
5
.

Continuous problem

2.1
Notation

We will use the standard notation for Lebesgue and Hilbert spaces. Most of our analysis will be
L^{2}
based and we will denote the space and space-time inner-products by

\left(f,g\right)_{\Omega}=\int_{\Omega}fg\,dx\quad\text{and}\quad(f,g)_{I\times\Omega}=\int_{I}(f,g)_{\Omega}dt,

respectively, with the corresponding norms

\|f\|^{2}_{\Omega}=\int_{\Omega}|f|^{2}\,dx\quad\text{and}\quad\|f\|^{2}_{I\times\Omega}=\int_{I}\|f\|^{2}_{\Omega}dt.

When the situation is less clear we will use the standard notation for the norms,
L^{2}(\Omega)
,
H^{2}(\Omega)
etc.
We will denote by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
a dual pairing between a function space and its dual.

2.2
Function spaces

We define the following functional spaces.

\displaystyle H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)

\displaystyle=\{v\in H^{1}(\Omega)\ |\ v=0\quad\text{on $\partial\Omega$}\},

\displaystyle H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)

\displaystyle=\{v\in H^{2}(\Omega)\ |\ v=\partial_{n}v=0\quad\text{on $\partial\Omega$}\},

\displaystyle H^{-2}(\Omega)

\displaystyle=\left(H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)\right)^{*},\quad H^{-1}(\Omega)=\left(H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\right)^{*}.

Lemma 1
.

On the function space
H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
,
the norm
\lVert v\rVert_{H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)}:=\lVert\Delta v\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
is equivalent to
\lVert v\rVert_{H^{2}(\Omega)}
.

Proof.

The proof follows by integration by parts and Poincaré inequality for the lower order terms.
∎

2.3
Results on the time-dependent Stokes problem

For a better comparison of the stream function formulation to the classic formulation of the Stokes problem,
let us recall some well known results.
Most commonly, the Stokes equations on the continuous level are discussed in divergence free spaces,
to which end we introduce the notations

\displaystyle\mathbf{V}:=\set{\mathbf{v}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)^{2}}{\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}=0}\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathbf{H}:=\set{\mathbf{v}\in L^{2}(\Omega)^{2}}{\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}=0\land\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{v}=0},

where by
\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{v}
we denote the normal trace of
\mathbf{v}
. It is straightforward to verify the
following result

Lemma 2
.

On the space
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)^{2}
, the following norms are equivalent:

\lVert\mathbf{v}\rVert_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\cong\lVert{\rm NewA}\cdot\mathbf{v}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\lVert\operatorname{curl}(\mathbf{v})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Especially,
\lVert\operatorname{curl}(\mathbf{v})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
induces a norm on
\mathbf{V}
equivalent to the
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
norm.

Proof.

For
\mathbf{v}\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)^{2}
we obtain using the definition of
\nabla\cdot
and
\operatorname{curl}
, as well as
integration by parts

\displaystyle\|\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\operatorname{curl}(\mathbf{v})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}(\partial_{1}\mathbf{v}_{1}+\partial_{2}\mathbf{v}_{2})^{2}+(\partial_{1}\mathbf{v}_{2}-\partial_{2}\mathbf{v}_{1})^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\sum_{i,j=1}^{2}(\partial_{i}\mathbf{v}_{j})^{2}+2\partial_{1}\mathbf{v}_{1}\partial_{2}\mathbf{v}_{2}-2\partial_{1}\mathbf{v}_{2}\partial_{2}\mathbf{v}_{1}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\nabla\mathbf{v}:\nabla\mathbf{v}-2\mathbf{v}_{1}\partial_{1}\partial_{2}\mathbf{v}_{2}+2(\partial_{2}\partial_{1}\mathbf{v}_{2})\mathbf{v}_{1}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\nabla\mathbf{v}:\nabla\mathbf{v}\,dx=\|\mathbf{v}\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}^{2}.

The statements of the lemma then follow from density of
C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, and the
definition of
\mathbf{V}
.
∎

Using these spaces, the stationary Stokes operator
A\colon D(A)\to\mathbf{H}
is given by

\left(A\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\right)_{\Omega}=\left(\nabla\mathbf{u},\nabla\mathbf{v}\right)_{\Omega}\quad\text{ for all }\mathbf{v}\in\mathbf{V},

(9)

where
D(A):=\{\mathbf{v}\in\mathbf{V}:\Delta\mathbf{v}\in L^{2}(\Omega)^{2}\}
.
The operators
\operatorname{curl},\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}}
lead to the following isomorphisms between scalar valued Sobolev spaces and
divergence free vector spaces, see
[
16
, Lemma 2]
:

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}}\colon

\displaystyle H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\to\mathbf{H},

and

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}}\colon

\displaystyle H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)\to\mathbf{V},

(10)

\displaystyle\operatorname{curl}\colon

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}\to H^{-1}(\Omega),

and

\displaystyle\operatorname{curl}\colon

\displaystyle\mathbf{V}^{*}\to H^{-2}(\Omega).

(11)

The time-dependent Stokes equations are described by the spaces

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}

\displaystyle:=L^{2}(I;\mathbf{V})\cap H^{1}(I;\mathbf{V}^{*})\hookrightarrow C(\bar{I};\mathbf{H}),

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}

\displaystyle:=L^{2}(I;\mathbf{V}\cap H^{2}(\Omega)^{2})\cap H^{1}(I;\mathbf{H})\hookrightarrow C(\bar{I};\mathbf{V}).

The weak formulation of the time-dependent Stokes equations reads:
Find
\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{V}
such that
\mathbf{u}(0)=\mathbf{u}_{0}
and

\left\langle\partial_{t}\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\right\rangle_{I\times\Omega}+\left(\nabla\mathbf{u},\nabla\mathbf{v}\right)_{I\times\Omega}=\left\langle\mathbf{g},\mathbf{v}\right\rangle_{I\times\Omega}\quad\text{for all }\mathbf{v}\in L^{2}(I;\mathbf{V}),

(12)

where
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle_{I\times\Omega}
denotes the duality pairing between
L^{2}(I;\mathbf{V})
and
L^{2}(I;\mathbf{V}^{*})
.
There holds the following result, see

[
30
, Chapter III, Theorem 1.1 Proposition 1.2]
:

Proposition 3
.

Let
\mathbf{g}\in L^{2}(I;V^{*})
and
\mathbf{u}_{0}\in\mathbf{H}
, then (
12
)
has a unique weak solution
\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{V}
with

\lVert\mathbf{u}\rVert_{\mathbb{V}}\leq C(\lVert\mathbf{g}\rVert_{L^{2}(I;\mathbf{V}^{*})}+\lVert\mathbf{u}_{0}\rVert_{\mathbf{H}}).

If
\mathbf{g}\in L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega)^{2})
and
\mathbf{u}_{0}\in\mathbf{V}
, then
\mathbf{u}
additionally satisfies

\lVert\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}\rVert_{L^{2}(I;\mathbf{H})}+\lVert A\mathbf{u}\rVert_{L^{2}(I;\mathbf{H})}\leq C(\lVert\mathbf{g}\rVert_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\lVert\mathbf{u}_{0}\rVert_{\mathbf{V}}),

where
A
is the Stokes operator defined in (
9
).
If moreover
\Omega
is convex, then
\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{W}
with a bound

\lVert\mathbf{u}\rVert_{\mathbb{W}}\leq C(\lVert\mathbf{g}\rVert_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\lVert\mathbf{u}_{0}\rVert_{\mathbf{V}}).

We have already stated, that the initial data
\psi_{0}
for the stream-function formulation and
the data
\mathbf{u}_{0}
for the standard formulation have to satisfy the relation (
6
).
In the following lemma, we establish that the regularity assumptions posed on
\psi_{0}
are actually natural
and hold true, if the corresponding standard assumptions are posed on
\mathbf{u}_{0}
.

Lemma 4
.

Let
\mathbf{u}_{0}\in\mathbf{H}
and
\psi_{0}
solve (
6
). Then
\psi_{0}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
,
and there holds a bound

\lVert\psi_{0}\rVert_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\leq C\lVert\mathbf{u}_{0}\rVert_{\mathbf{H}}.

If furthermore,
\mathbf{u}_{0}\in\mathbf{V}
, then
\psi_{0}\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
and there holds the bound

\lVert\psi_{0}\rVert_{H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)}\leq C\lVert\mathbf{u}_{0}\rVert_{\mathbf{V}}.

Before we prove the lemma, note that the result
\psi_{0}\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
only poses a requirement on
\mathbf{u}_{0}

and we do not require, e.g., convexity of
\Omega
, which would be otherwise a standard assumption to achieve

H^{2}
regularity.

Proof.

For the case
\mathbf{u}_{0}\in\mathbf{H}
there is nothing to prove, as this is directly given by the weak
formulation of (
6
).
Hence assume now
\mathbf{u}_{0}\in\mathbf{V}
.
Our goal is to show the result by extending the proof of
[
16
, Lemma 2]
.
As a result of the identity (
4
), there holds

\operatorname{curl}(\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}}\psi_{0}-\mathbf{u}_{0})=0.

By
[
16
, Lemma 3]
there exists a unique
p\in H^{1}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R}
such
that
\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}}\psi_{0}-\mathbf{u}_{0}=\nabla p
.
Due to the first isomorphism of (
10
)
\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}}\psi_{0}\in\mathbf{H}
and by assumption

\mathbf{u}_{0}\in\mathbf{V}\subset\mathbf{H}
, it holds
\nabla p\in\mathbf{H}
. The definition of
\mathbf{H}
then implies

\Delta p=0
and
\partial_{\mathbf{n}}p=0
. Hence
p
is constant and due to
p\in H^{1}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R}
it
actually vanishes. This implies
\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}}\psi_{0}=\mathbf{u}_{0}\in\mathbf{V}\subset H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)^{2}
.
By definition of
\operatorname{\mathbf{curl}}
, this implies

\partial_{1}\psi_{0},\partial_{2}\psi_{0}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega).

Hence especially
\psi_{0}\in H^{2}(\Omega)
and

\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\psi_{0}=0
on
\partial\Omega
.
This shows
\psi_{0}\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
.
The norm bound can then be obtained using Lemmas
1

and
2
, which yields

\lVert\psi_{0}\rVert_{H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)}\cong\lVert\Delta\psi_{0}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\lVert\operatorname{curl}\mathbf{u}_{0}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\cong\lVert\mathbf{u}_{0}\rVert_{\mathbf{V}}.

∎

2.4
Results for stationary biharmonic equation

Before analyzing the time-dependent problem, we recall some basic results for the biharmonic problem with Dirichlet (clamped) boundary conditions,

\displaystyle\Delta^{2}w

\displaystyle=f\quad

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega,

(13)

\displaystyle w=\partial_{n}w

\displaystyle=0\quad

\displaystyle\text{on }\partial\Omega.

The weak formulation for (
13
) reads

w\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)\quad:\quad\left(\Delta w,\Delta\varphi\right)_{\Omega}=\left\langle f,\varphi\right\rangle_{\Omega}\quad\text{for all }\varphi\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega).

(14)

The following results can be found, e.g., in
[
22
, Sec. 5.9]
.

Proposition 5
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a bounded Lipschitz domain and
f\in H^{-2}(\Omega)
.
Then (
14
) has a unique weak solution
w\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
and there exists a constant
C
such that

\|w\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|f\|_{H^{-2}(\Omega)}.

(15)

If in addition
\Omega
is convex and
f\in H^{-1}(\Omega)
, then the weak solution
w
of (
14
) fulfills
w\in H^{3}(\Omega)\cap H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
and there exists a constant
C
such that

\|w\|_{H^{3}(\Omega)}\leq C\|f\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}.

(16)

The above result yields, that the operator
\mathcal{A}\colon H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)\to H^{-2}(\Omega)
, defined by

\left\langle\mathcal{A}w,\varphi\right\rangle_{\Omega}=\left(\Delta w,\Delta\varphi\right)_{\Omega}\qquad\text{for all }\varphi\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)

(17)

is an isomorphism.
We immediately obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 6
.

Let
g\in H^{1}(\Omega)
and
w\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
be the weak solution to the problem

\left(\Delta w,\Delta\varphi\right)_{\Omega}=\left({\rm NewA}g,{\rm NewA}\varphi\right)_{\Omega}\quad\text{for all }\varphi\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega).

(18)

Then there exists a constant
C
such that

\|w\|_{H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)}\leq C\|{\rm NewA}g\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

If
\Omega
is in addition convex, then
w\in H^{3}(\Omega)\cap H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
and

\|w\|_{H^{3}(\Omega)}\leq C\|{\rm NewA}g\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

(19)

If
\Omega
is polygonal, but not necessarily convex, the results of

[
7
,
9
]

yield, that there exists
some
\alpha_{\Omega}>0
, such that for any
0\leq\alpha<\alpha_{\Omega}
, the solution
w
of (
14
)
for the data
f\in H^{-2+\alpha}(\Omega)
satisfies a bound

\|w\|_{H^{2+\alpha}(\Omega)}\leq C\lVert f\rVert_{H^{-2+\alpha}(\Omega)}.

We consider also an associated eigenvalue problem, the so called buckling eigenvalue problem

\displaystyle\Delta^{2}w

\displaystyle=-\lambda\Delta w

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega,

(20)

\displaystyle w=\partial_{n}w

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\text{on }\partial\Omega,

see, e.g.,
[
32
,
6
]
.

Lemma 7
.

There exists a sequence of eigenvalues of (
20
)

0<\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\dots,\quad\text{with }\lambda_{k}\to\infty,\;\text{for }k\to\infty

and the corresponding eigenfunctions
w_{k}\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
, which are orthogonal with respect to both
H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
and
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
inner products, i.e.

\left(\Delta w_{k},\Delta w_{j}\right)_{\Omega}=\left(\nabla w_{k},\nabla w_{j}\right)_{\Omega}=0\quad\text{for }k\neq j

and form basis for both
H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
and
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

We consider a bounded linear operator
T\colon H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\to H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
defined as follows:

T(g)\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)\quad:\quad\left(\Delta T(g),\Delta\phi\right)_{\Omega}=\left(\nabla g,\nabla\phi\right)_{\Omega}\quad\text{for all }\phi\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega).

(21)

Note, that by Corollary
6
for every
g\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
,
T(g)\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
is well-defined. Since
H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
is compactly embedded into
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
the operator
T\colon H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\to H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
is compact. It is straightforward to check that
T
is symmetric with respect to the
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
inner product, i.e.

\left(\nabla T(g),\nabla\phi\right)_{\Omega}=\left(\nabla g,\nabla T(\phi)\right)_{\Omega}\quad\text{for all }g,\phi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),

and positive definite, since

\left(\nabla T(g),\nabla g\right)_{\Omega}=\|\Delta T(g)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\geq 0

and
\left(\nabla T(g),\nabla g\right)_{\Omega}=0
implies
T(g)=0
and thus
g=0
. Thus,
T
is also injective. We apply the
spectral theorem for compact normal operators, see, e.g.,
[
5
, Theorem 12.12]
, and obtain the result of the lemma.
∎

Remark 8
.

The image of the operator
T\colon H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\to H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
defined in (
21
) is given as

\operatorname{Im}(T)=\Set{w\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)}{\text{there exists }g\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\text{ fulfilling~\eqref{eq:Fourth weak}}}.

By Corollary
6
there holds
\operatorname{Im}(T)=H^{3}(\Omega)\cap H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
if
\Omega
is convex. Since the operator
T
is injective we can define the inverse

T^{-1}\colon\operatorname{Im}(T)\to H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),

which fulfills

\left(\nabla T^{-1}(w),\nabla\phi\right)_{\Omega}=\left(\Delta w,\Delta\phi\right)_{\Omega}\quad\text{for all }\phi\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega).

In the numerical analysis below, we use an operator
A_{h}
(
44
), which is a discrete analog of
T^{-1}
.

2.5
Results for time-dependent problems

We call
\psi
a weak solution of (
5
) if

\psi\in\mathbb{X}:=\Set{v\in L^{2}(I;H^{2}_{0}(\Omega))}{\partial_{t}\Delta v\in L^{2}(I;H^{-2}(\Omega))}

(22)

fulfills

\displaystyle-\langle\partial_{t}\Delta\psi,\varphi\rangle+(\Delta\psi,\Delta\varphi)_{I\times\Omega}

\displaystyle=\langle f,\varphi\rangle\quad

\displaystyle\text{for all }\;\varphi\in\mathbb{Y}:=L^{2}(I;H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)),

(23)

\displaystyle\psi(0)

\displaystyle=\psi_{0}.

Here,
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the duality product between
L^{2}(I;H^{-2}(\Omega))

and
L^{2}(I;H^{2}_{0}(\Omega))
.

Before we prove the existence and regularity results for (
23
), we
show two technical results regarding continuity in time, if some Hilbert space regularities are known.

Lemma 9
.

Let
\psi\in L^{2}(I;H^{2}_{0}(\Omega))
satisfy
\partial_{t}\Delta\psi\in L^{2}(I;H^{-2}(\Omega))
.
Then (after a possible redefinition on a zero measure subset of
I
)

\psi\in C(\bar{I};H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))
and there holds a bound

\lVert\psi\rVert_{C(\bar{I};H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))}\leq C\left(\lVert\psi\rVert_{L^{2}(I;H^{2}_{0}(\Omega))}+\lVert\partial_{t}\Delta\psi\rVert_{L^{2}(I;H^{-2}(\Omega))}\right).

Thus the space
\mathbb{X}
defined in (
22
) satisfies the continuous embedding:

\mathbb{X}\hookrightarrow C(\bar{I};H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))
.

Proof.

We adapt the proof of
[
13
, Section 5.9, Theorem 3]
, and for any
\varepsilon>0

introduce a mollifier

\eta_{\varepsilon}
, which leads to the mollified functions
\psi_{\varepsilon}=\eta_{\varepsilon}*\psi
.
For
\varepsilon,\delta>0
it holds due to the smoothness in time of
\psi_{\varepsilon},\psi_{\delta}
and

\psi(s)\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
for a.e.
s\in I

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left(\lVert\nabla\left(\psi_{\varepsilon}(t)-\psi_{\delta}(t)\right)\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=2\left(\partial_{t}\nabla(\psi_{\varepsilon}(t)-\psi_{\delta}(t)),\nabla(\psi_{\varepsilon}(t)-\psi_{\delta}(t))\right)_{\Omega}

\displaystyle=-2\left(\partial_{t}\Delta(\psi_{\varepsilon}(t)-\psi_{\delta}(t)),(\psi_{\varepsilon}(t)-\psi_{\delta}(t))\right)_{\Omega}

for all
t\in I
. By the fundamental theorem of calculus, it holds for all
s,t\in I

\lVert\nabla\left(\psi_{\varepsilon}(t)-\psi_{\delta}(t)\right)\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=\lVert\nabla\left(\psi_{\varepsilon}(s)-\psi_{\delta}(s)\right)\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}-\int_{s}^{t}2\left(\partial_{t}\Delta(\psi_{\varepsilon}(\tau)-\psi_{\delta}(\tau)),(\psi_{\varepsilon}(\tau)-\psi_{\delta}(\tau))\right)_{\Omega}\,d\tau.

In the above identity, we fix now some
s\in I
for which
\psi_{\varepsilon}(s)\to\psi(s)
in

H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. As a consequence, it holds

\limsup_{\varepsilon,\delta\to 0}\sup_{t\in I}\lVert\nabla\left(\psi_{\varepsilon}(t)-\psi_{\delta}(t)\right)\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq 2\lim_{\varepsilon,\delta\to 0}\lVert\partial_{t}\Delta(\psi_{\varepsilon}-\psi_{\delta})\rVert_{L^{2}(I;H^{-2}(\Omega))}\lVert\psi_{\varepsilon}-\psi_{\delta}\rVert_{L^{2}(I;H^{2}_{0}(\Omega))}=0.

Hence as in the proof of the cited reference, this yields that (after a possible redefinition on a set
of measure zero) it holds

\nabla\psi\in C(\bar{I};L^{2}(\Omega)^{2}).

(24)

Now that we have fixed a representation of
\psi
with
\nabla\psi
being continuous in time, we have
to take special care with further redefinitions on zero sets.
To conclude the proof, note that
\psi\in L^{2}(I;H^{2}_{0}(\Omega))
especially implies that

\psi(t)\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
for a.e.
t\in I
. Hence, for every
t^{*}\in\bar{I}
, there exists a sequence

\{t_{n}\}\subset I
with
t_{n}\to t^{*}
,
\psi(t_{n})\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
for all
n
and by
(
24
)

\lVert\nabla\psi(t_{n})-\nabla\psi(t^{*})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\to 0,\ n\to\infty.

(25)

Especially
\{\psi(t_{n})\}
is a Cauchy sequence in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
and thus

\psi(t_{n})\to\psi^{*}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
for some
\psi^{*}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
.
From (
25
), we deduce
\nabla\psi(t^{*})=\nabla\psi^{*}
, and especially
that
\psi^{*}
is independent of the explicit choice of the sequence
\{t_{n}\}
.
Thus, we can redefine
\psi(t^{*}):=\psi^{*}
, without breaking the property (
24
).
∎

Lemma 10
.

Let
\psi\in L^{2}(I;H^{2}_{0}(\Omega))
satisfy
\partial_{t}\psi\in L^{2}(I;H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))

and
\mathcal{A}\psi\in L^{2}(I;H^{-1}(\Omega))
.
Then (after a possible redefinition on a measure zero subset of
I
)

\psi\in C(\bar{I};H^{2}_{0}(\Omega))
and there holds a bound

\lVert\psi\rVert_{C(\bar{I};H^{2}_{0}(\Omega))}\leq C\left(\lVert\mathcal{A}\psi\rVert_{L^{2}(I;H^{-1}(\Omega))}+\lVert\partial_{t}\psi\rVert_{L^{2}(I;H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))}\right).

Proof.

Similarly to the proof of
Lemma

9
, we consider the mollified functions

\psi_{\varepsilon}=\psi*\eta_{\varepsilon}
. As

\partial_{t}(\psi*\eta_{\varepsilon})=\psi*(\partial_{t}\eta_{\varepsilon})
,
and
\psi(t)\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
for
a.e.
t\in I
, it holds
\partial_{t}(\psi*\eta_{\varepsilon})\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
for all
t\in I
, and thus
we obtain via integration by parts:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left(\lVert\Delta\left(\psi_{\varepsilon}(t)-\psi_{\delta}(t)\right)\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=2\left(\partial_{t}\Delta(\psi_{\varepsilon}(t)-\psi_{\delta}(t)),\Delta(\psi_{\varepsilon}(t)-\psi_{\delta}(t))\right)_{\Omega}

\displaystyle=2\left(\partial_{t}(\psi_{\varepsilon}(t)-\psi_{\delta}(t)),\mathcal{A}(\psi_{\varepsilon}(t)-\psi_{\delta}(t))\right)_{\Omega}.

By the fundamental theorem of calculus, it holds for all
s,t\in I

\lVert\Delta\left(\psi_{\varepsilon}(t)-\psi_{\delta}(t)\right)\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=\lVert\Delta\left(\psi_{\varepsilon}(s)-\psi_{\delta}(s)\right)\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\int_{s}^{t}2\left(\partial_{t}(\psi_{\varepsilon}(\tau)-\psi_{\delta}(\tau)),\mathcal{A}(\psi_{\varepsilon}(\tau)-\psi_{\delta}(\tau))\right)_{\Omega}\,d\tau.

Hence, fixing any
s\in I
with
\Delta\psi_{\varepsilon}(s)\to\Delta\psi(s)
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
:

\limsup_{\varepsilon,\delta\to 0}\sup_{t\in I}\lVert\Delta\left(\psi_{\varepsilon}(t)-\psi_{\delta}(t)\right)\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq\lim_{\varepsilon,\delta\to 0}\lVert\partial_{t}(\psi_{\varepsilon}-\psi_{\delta})\rVert_{L^{2}(I;H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))}\lVert\mathcal{A}(\psi_{\varepsilon}-\psi_{\delta})\rVert_{L^{2}(I;H^{-1}(\Omega))}=0.

Thus, we obtain as before, that

\Delta\psi\in C(\bar{I};L^{2}(\Omega)).

As in the proof of
Lemma

9
, using this result, and the fact that

H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)\subset H^{2}(\Omega)
is closed, we deduce that in fact
\psi\in C(\bar{I};H^{2}_{0}(\Omega))
.
∎

For (
23
) we have the following existence and stability result.

Theorem 11

(Existence, uniqueness and stability)
.

Let
f\in L^{2}(I;H^{-2}(\Omega))
and
\psi_{0}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. Then there exists a unique solution
\psi\in\mathbb{X}
to (
23
). Moreover,
\psi\in C(\bar{I};H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))
and the following estimate holds

\|\partial_{t}\Delta\psi\|_{L^{2}(I;H^{-2}(\Omega))}+\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(I;H^{2}_{0}(\Omega))}+\|\psi\|_{C(\bar{I};H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))}\leq C\left(\|f\|_{L^{2}(I;H^{-2}(\Omega))}+\|{\rm NewA}\psi_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right).

Proof.

The proof uses a Galerkin procedure similar to the proof of
[
13
, Section 7.1.2, Theorem 3]
in the case of the heat equation. We use the eigenfunctions
w_{k}\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
from Lemma
7
and normalize them such that
\|\nabla w_{k}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=1
. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
we consider the space

V_{n}=\operatorname{span}\set{w_{k}}{k=1,2,\dots,n}

and a Galerkin approximation to (
23
) given as
\psi_{n}\colon\bar{I}\to V_{n}
and fulfilling

\displaystyle\left(\nabla\partial_{t}\psi_{n}(t),\nabla v_{n}\right)_{\Omega}+\left(\Delta\psi_{n}(t),\Delta v_{n}\right)_{\Omega}

\displaystyle=\langle f(t),v_{n}\rangle_{H^{-2}(\Omega),H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)}\quad

\displaystyle\text{for all }\;v_{n}\in V_{n}\text{ and a.a. }t\in I

(26)

\displaystyle\left(\nabla\psi_{n}(0),\nabla v_{n}\right)_{\Omega}

\displaystyle=\left(\nabla\psi_{0},\nabla v_{n}\right)_{\Omega}\quad

\displaystyle\text{for all }\;v_{n}\in V_{n}.

The existence and uniqueness of a solution of this systems of ODEs follows directly.
The initial condition in (
26
), can compactly be written as

\psi_{n}(0)=P_{n}\psi_{0},

(27)

where
P_{n}\colon H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\to V_{n}
is the orthogonal projection defined by

\left(\nabla P_{n}v,\nabla v_{n}\right)_{\Omega}=\left(\nabla v,\nabla v_{n}\right)_{\Omega}\qquad\text{for all $v_{n}\in V_{n}$}.

(28)

It is straightforward to check, that this projection is stable in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, i.e., there holds

\|P_{n}v\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\leq\|v\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}.

(29)

Testing (
26
) with
v_{n}=\psi_{n}(t)
we obtain

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|{\rm NewA}\psi_{n}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\Delta\psi_{n}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\langle f(t),\psi_{n}(t)\rangle_{H^{-2}(\Omega),H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)}\leq\|f(t)\|_{H^{-2}(\Omega)}\|\psi_{n}(t)\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\quad\text{a.e. $t\in I$}.

Integrating in time over
(0,t)
for arbitrary
t\in I
,
and using the fact that due to Lemma
1

\|\psi_{n}(t)\|_{H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)}=\|\Delta\psi_{n}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
we obtain
after an application of the Young’s inequality

\frac{1}{2}\|{\rm NewA}\psi_{n}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\psi_{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}((0,t);H^{2}_{0}(\Omega))}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|{\rm NewA}\psi_{n}(0)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\frac{1}{2}\|\psi_{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}((0,t);H^{2}_{0}(\Omega))}+\frac{1}{2}\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}((0,t);H^{-2}(\Omega))}.

Using the fact that, due to (
29
)

\|\nabla\psi_{n}(0)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\|\nabla\psi_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
,
and
t\in I
was arbitrary, we get

\|{\rm NewA}\psi_{n}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|\psi_{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(I;H^{2}_{0}(\Omega))}\leq\|{\rm NewA}\psi_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(I;H^{-2}(\Omega))}.

(30)

In the next step we show the boundedness of
\|\partial_{t}\Delta\psi_{n}\|_{L^{2}(I;H^{-2}(\Omega))}
.
By the orthonormality in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
of the basis we get for every
v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)

v=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\nabla v,\nabla w_{k}\right)_{\Omega}w_{k}\quad\text{and}\quad P_{n}v=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(\nabla v,\nabla w_{k}\right)_{\Omega}w_{k}.

Moreover, if
v\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
and
v_{n}\in V_{n}
, then by orthogonality of
\{w_{k}\}
in
H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
:

\left(\Delta v_{n},\Delta v\right)_{\Omega}=\left(\Delta v_{n},\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\Delta w_{k}\left({\rm NewA}v,{\rm NewA}w_{k}\right)_{\Omega}\right)_{\Omega}=\left(\Delta v_{n},\sum_{k=1}^{n}\Delta w_{k}\left({\rm NewA}v,{\rm NewA}w_{k}\right)_{\Omega}\right)_{\Omega}=\left(\Delta v_{n},\Delta P_{n}v\right)_{\Omega}.

(31)

By the same arguments, we obtain for any
v\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)

\|P_{n}v\|_{H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)}^{2}=\|\Delta P_{n}v\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(\nabla v,\nabla w_{k}\right)_{\Omega}^{2}\|\Delta w_{k}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\nabla v,\nabla w_{k}\right)_{\Omega}^{2}\|\Delta w_{k}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\|\Delta v\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Let
v\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
be arbitrary. As
\psi_{n}(t)\in V_{n}
a.e. in
I
, there holds

\partial_{t}\psi_{n}(t)\in V_{n}
a.e. in
I
, and thus for a.e.
t\in I
, we get

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}\Delta\psi_{n}(t),v)_{\Omega}

\displaystyle=-(\nabla\partial_{t}\psi_{n}(t),\nabla v)_{\Omega}=-(\nabla\partial_{t}\psi_{n}(t),\nabla P_{n}v)_{\Omega}

\displaystyle=(\Delta\psi_{n}(t),\Delta P_{n}v)_{\Omega}-\langle f(t),P_{n}v\rangle_{H^{-2}(\Omega),H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\|\Delta\psi_{n}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\Delta P_{n}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|f(t)\|_{H^{-2}(\Omega)}\|P_{n}v\|_{H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\|\psi_{n}(t)\|_{H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)}+\|f(t)\|_{H^{-2}(\Omega)}\right)\|v\|_{H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)}.

Thus for a.e.
t\in I
, there holds

\|\partial_{t}\Delta\psi_{n}(t)\|_{H^{-2}(\Omega)}\leq\|\psi_{n}(t)\|_{H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)}+\|f(t)\|_{H^{-2}(\Omega)}.

Squaring, integrating w.r.t
t
and using (
30
), we get

\|\partial_{t}\Delta\psi_{n}\|_{L^{2}(I;H^{-2}(\Omega))}\leq C\left(\|{\rm NewA}\psi_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(I;H^{-2}(\Omega))}\right).

(32)

From these estimates we obtain existence of a subsequence of
\psi_{n}
(denoted again by
\psi_{n}
) and some
\psi\in\mathbb{X}
such that

\psi_{n}\rightharpoonup\psi\text{ in }L^{2}(I;H^{2}_{0}(\Omega))\;\text{ and }\;\partial_{t}\Delta\psi_{n}\rightharpoonup\partial_{t}\Delta\psi\text{ in }L^{2}(I;H^{-2}(\Omega)).

(33)

To conclude the proof, let
\varphi^{N}\in C^{1}(\bar{I};H^{2}_{0}(\Omega))
be of the form

\varphi^{N}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\zeta_{j}(t)w_{j}
where
N\in\mathbb{N}
is fixed and
\zeta_{i}(t)\in C^{1}(\bar{I})
.
Then, testing the first equation of (
26
) for
n>N

with
\varphi^{N}(t)
and integrating over
I
yields

-\left\langle\partial_{t}\Delta\psi_{n},\varphi^{N}\right\rangle_{I\times\Omega}+\left(\Delta\psi_{n},\Delta\varphi^{N}\right)_{I\times\Omega}=\langle f,\varphi^{N}\rangle.

Using the weak convergences (
33
) thus yields

-\left\langle\partial_{t}\Delta\psi,\varphi^{N}\right\rangle_{I\times\Omega}+\left(\Delta\psi,\Delta\varphi^{N}\right)_{I\times\Omega}=\langle f,\varphi^{N}\rangle.

As functions of the form of
\varphi^{N}
are dense in
\mathbb{Y}=L^{2}(I;H^{2}_{0}(\Omega))
, we have just shown the first equation of
(
23
).
To show, that the initial condition is satisfied, let
\varphi\in C^{1}(\bar{I};H^{2}_{0}(\Omega))
be arbitrary
with
\varphi(T)=0
.
Testing the first equation of (
23
) with
\varphi
, yields after integration
by parts in time and space, that

-\left\langle\nabla\psi,\partial_{t}\nabla\varphi\right\rangle_{I\times\Omega}+\left(\Delta\psi,\Delta\varphi\right)_{I\times\Omega}=\langle f,\varphi\rangle+\left(\nabla\psi(0),\nabla\varphi(0)\right)_{\Omega}.

Moreover, using integration by parts in time,
\varphi(T)=0
,
the definition of
P_{n}
(
28
), as well as
(
31
) applied to every
t\in I
, we have

\displaystyle-\left\langle\nabla\psi_{n},\partial_{t}\nabla\varphi\right\rangle_{I\times\Omega}+\left(\Delta\psi_{n},\Delta\varphi\right)_{I\times\Omega}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\partial_{t}\nabla\psi_{n},\nabla\varphi\right\rangle_{I\times\Omega}+\left(\Delta\psi_{n},\Delta\varphi\right)_{I\times\Omega}+\left(\nabla\psi_{n}(0),\nabla\varphi(0)\right)_{\Omega}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\partial_{t}\nabla\psi_{n},\nabla P_{n}\varphi\right\rangle_{I\times\Omega}+\left(\Delta\psi_{n},\Delta P_{n}\varphi\right)_{I\times\Omega}+\left(\nabla\psi_{n}(0),\nabla\varphi(0)\right)_{\Omega}

\displaystyle=\left\langle f,P_{n}\varphi\right\rangle_{I\times\Omega}+\left(\nabla\psi_{n}(0),\nabla\varphi(0)\right)_{\Omega}

\displaystyle=\left\langle f,P_{n}\varphi\right\rangle_{I\times\Omega}+\left(\nabla\psi_{n}(0),\nabla\varphi(0)\right)_{\Omega}.

Hence, subtracting the two equations, and taking the limit yields due to the weak convergence
(
33
) and (
29
)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left[\left\langle f,\varphi-P_{n}\varphi\right\rangle_{I\times\Omega}+\left(\nabla(\psi(0)-\psi_{n}(0)),\nabla\varphi(0)\right)_{\Omega}\right]

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left[\left\langle f,\varphi-P_{n}\varphi\right\rangle_{I\times\Omega}+\left(\nabla(\psi(0)-P_{n}\psi_{0}),\nabla\varphi(0)\right)_{\Omega}\right].

Using the convergence
P_{n}\varphi(t)\to\varphi(t)
in
H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
for all
t\in I
, together
with dominated convergence, as well as
P_{n}\psi_{0}\to\psi_{0}
in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
yields

\left(\nabla(\psi(0)-\psi_{0}),\nabla\varphi(0)\right)_{\Omega}=0.

As
\varphi(0)
can be arbitrary, we have concluded with the proof of
\psi(0)=\psi_{0}
.
From the estimates (
30
),(
32
), we know that

\psi
satisfies the bounds

\|\partial_{t}\Delta\psi\|_{L^{2}(I;H^{-2}(\Omega))}+\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(I;H^{2}_{0}(\Omega))}\leq C\left(\|f\|_{L^{2}(I;H^{-2}(\Omega))}+\|{\rm NewA}\psi_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right).

The continuity in time is then a direct consequence of
Lemma

9
.
Choosing
\psi_{0}=0
and
f=0
directly implies uniqueness.
∎

Remark 12
.

The stability estimate of
Theorem

11
bounds the term

\|\partial_{t}\Delta\psi\|_{L^{2}(I;H^{-2}(\Omega))}
by norms of the data.
From this it is however not clear, whether
\partial_{t}\psi\in L^{2}(I\times\Omega)
holds.
In fact such a result seems doubtful, as by a duality argument this would relate to

\Delta^{-1}\colon L^{2}(\Omega)\to H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
which does not hold in general even on convex domains.

Under some additional conditions on data we can show higher regularity result.

Theorem 13
.

Let
f\in L^{2}(I;H^{-1}(\Omega))
and
\psi_{0}\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
.
Then the solution to (
23
) additionally has the following regularities:

\partial_{t}\psi\in L^{2}(I;H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))
,
\mathcal{A}\psi\in L^{2}(I;H^{-1}(\Omega))
,
and
\psi\in C(\bar{I};H^{2}_{0}(\Omega))
. The weak form can be written as

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{t}{\rm NewA}\psi,{\rm NewA}\varphi\right)_{I\times\Omega}+\left(\Delta\psi,\Delta\varphi\right)_{I\times\Omega}

\displaystyle=\langle f,\varphi\rangle\quad

\displaystyle\text{for all }\;\varphi\in\mathbb{Y},

(34)

\displaystyle\psi(0)

\displaystyle=\psi_{0},

and there holds

\|{\rm NewA}\partial_{t}\psi\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|\mathcal{A}\psi\|_{L^{2}(I;H^{-1}(\Omega))}+\|\psi\|_{C(\bar{I};H^{2}_{0}(\Omega))}\leq C\left(\|f\|_{L^{2}(I;H^{-1}(\Omega))}+\|\psi_{0}\|_{H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)}\right).

If
\Omega
is in addition convex, then
\psi\in L^{2}(I;H^{3}(\Omega)\cap H^{2}_{0}(\Omega))
and there holds

\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(I;H^{3}(\Omega))}\leq C\left(\|f\|_{L^{2}(I;H^{-1}(\Omega))}+\|\psi_{0}\|_{H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)}\right).

Proof.

We use the Galerkin approximation from the proof of Theorem
11
and take
v_{n}=\partial_{t}\psi_{n}(t)\in V_{n}
. This results in

\|{\rm NewA}\partial_{t}\psi_{n}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\Delta\psi_{n}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\langle f(t),\partial_{t}\psi_{n}(t)\rangle_{H^{-1}(\Omega),H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\leq\|f(t)\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}\|\nabla\partial_{t}\psi_{n}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Integrating over
I
and using the Young’s inequality, we have

\|{\rm NewA}\partial_{t}\psi_{n}\|^{2}_{I\times\Omega}+\frac{1}{2}\|\Delta\psi_{n}(T)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\Delta\psi_{n}(0)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla\partial_{t}\psi_{n}\|^{2}_{I\times\Omega}+\frac{1}{2}\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(I;H^{-1}(\Omega))}.

(35)

As
\psi_{0}\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
, due to (
27
)
and (
31
), there holds

\|\Delta\psi_{n}(0)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\|\Delta P_{n}\psi_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\|\Delta\psi_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\|\psi_{0}\|^{2}_{H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)}.

Hence, after canceling terms in (
35
), multiplying by 2 and taking the root,
we obtain

\|{\rm NewA}\partial_{t}\psi_{n}\|_{I\times\Omega}\leq\|\psi_{0}\|_{H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(I;H^{-1}(\Omega))}.

(36)

Moreover, using integration by parts, (
31
) and
the definition of the Galerkin approximation (
26
),
there holds for any
v\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
and a.e.
t\in I

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{A}\psi_{n}(t),v\right)_{\Omega}

\displaystyle=\left(\Delta\psi_{n}(t),\Delta v\right)_{\Omega}=\left(\Delta\psi_{n}(t),\Delta P_{n}v\right)_{\Omega}=-\left(\partial_{t}{\rm NewA}\psi_{n}(t),{\rm NewA}P_{n}v\right)_{\Omega}+\langle f(t),P_{n}v\rangle.

By density of
H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, for all
v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
we thus have

\left(\mathcal{A}\psi_{n}(t),v\right)_{\Omega}=-\left(\partial_{t}{\rm NewA}\psi_{n}(t),{\rm NewA}P_{n}v\right)_{\Omega}+\langle f(t),P_{n}v\rangle.

Hence, using the stability of
P_{n}
, (
29
), we obtain the bound

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{A}\psi_{n}(t),v\right)_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\lVert\partial_{t}{\rm NewA}\psi_{n}(t)\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\lVert f(t)\rVert_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}\right)\lVert P_{n}v\rVert_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\lVert\partial_{t}{\rm NewA}\psi_{n}(t)\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\lVert f(t)\rVert_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}\right)\lVert v\rVert_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)},

which leads to the estimate

and thus after squaring and integrating in time, and using (

(37)

As in the proof of

in

Using

3
Fully discrete approximation

In this section we consider the discrete version of the operators presented in the previous section and introduce fully discrete Galerkin solution.

3.1
Spatial discretization

Let
\{\mathcal{T}_{h}\}
be a
family of triangulations of
\bar{\Omega}
, consisting of closed simplices, where we denote by
h
the maximum mesh-size. Let
V_{h}\subset H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
be the finite dimensional space.
We denote by
\Pi_{h}\colon H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\to V_{h}
the
H^{1}_{0}
-projection, defined by

\left(\nabla\Pi_{h}w,\nabla v_{h}\right)_{\Omega}=\left(\nabla w,\nabla v_{h}\right)_{\Omega}\qquad\text{for all }v_{h}\in V_{h}.

(41)

By definition,
\Pi_{h}
is stable in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, i.e., there holds

\|\Pi_{h}w\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\leq\|w\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}.

(42)

To allow for various space discretizations, we introduce a symmetric discrete elliptic bilinear form

a_{h}(\cdot,\cdot):V_{h}\times V_{h}\to\mathbb{R},

where at this point we do not fix a special choice of discretization in space.
As
a_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
was defined to be symmetric and elliptic, i.e., there holds

a_{h}(\varphi_{h},\varphi_{h})\geq 0\quad\text{for all }\varphi_{h}\in V_{h}\quad\text{and}\quad a_{h}(\varphi_{h},\varphi_{h})=0\quad\Rightarrow\quad\varphi_{h}=0,

the bilinear form
a_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
defines a norm on
V_{h}

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|w_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{h}:=\sqrt{a_{h}(w_{h},w_{h})}.

(43)

In addition we also define an operator
A_{h}\colon V_{h}\to V_{h}
, by

a_{h}(\phi_{h},\varphi_{h})=({\rm NewA}A_{h}\phi_{h},{\rm NewA}\varphi_{h})_{\Omega}\quad\text{for all }\phi_{h},\varphi_{h}\in V_{h}.

(44)

Notice, since the bilinear form
a_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
is symmetric, the operator
A_{h}
is self-adjoint
w.r.t the
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
inner product, since

({\rm NewA}A_{h}\phi_{h},{\rm NewA}\varphi_{h})_{\Omega}=a_{h}(\phi_{h},\varphi_{h})=a_{h}(\varphi_{h},\phi_{h})=({\rm NewA}A_{h}\varphi_{h},{\rm NewA}\phi_{h})_{\Omega}=({\rm NewA}\phi_{h},{\rm NewA}A_{h}\varphi_{h})_{\Omega}.

We can establish the following stability result

Lemma 16
.

Let
g\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
and
w_{h}\in V_{h}
be the finite element solution

a_{h}(w_{h},\varphi_{h})=({\rm NewA}g,{\rm NewA}\varphi_{h})_{\Omega}\quad\text{for all }\varphi_{h}\in V_{h}.

Then,

\|{\rm NewA}A_{h}w_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\|{\rm NewA}g\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Proof.

By the definition of
A_{h}
and the equation for
w_{h}
,

\|{\rm NewA}A_{h}w_{h}\|_{\Omega}^{2}=({\rm NewA}A_{h}w_{h},{\rm NewA}A_{h}w_{h})_{\Omega}=a_{h}(w_{h},A_{h}w_{h})=({\rm NewA}g,{\rm NewA}A_{h}w_{h})_{\Omega}\leq\|{\rm NewA}g\|_{\Omega}\|{\rm NewA}A_{h}w_{h}\|_{\Omega}.

Canceling, we obtain the result
∎

We wish our analysis to be applicable to a broad range of discretizations, for instance to the

C^{0}
-interior penalty method (cf.
[
10
]
), in which case
a_{h}(\psi,\cdot)
is not well
defined for general
\psi\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
.
Vice versa, not for all data
f\in H^{-2}(\Omega)
the right hand side can be tested with
\phi_{h}\in V_{h}
.
Hence, throughout the paper, we work under the following assumptions, the first ensuring wellposedness
of the discrete problems, and the second guaranteeing an elliptic Galerkin orthogonality.

Assumption 1
.

There exists
\theta\in(0,1]
, such that

V_{h}\subset H^{1+\theta}_{0}(\Omega)
.

Assumption 2
.

Let
\theta
and
V_{h}
be as in
1
.
The bilinear form
a_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
can be extended to

a_{h}(\cdot,\cdot):(X+V_{h})\times(X+V_{h})\to\mathbb{R},

where

. Furthermore, for all
\psi\in X,\varphi_{h}\in V_{h}
,
there holds

\left\langle\mathcal{A}\psi,\varphi_{h}\right\rangle_{\Omega}=a_{h}(\psi,\varphi_{h}),

where
\mathcal{A}
, is the biharmonic operator introduced
in (
17
).

Remark 17
.

Although technical in nature, these assumptions are not overly restrictive, mainly posing a regularity
assumption on the solution
\psi
of (
13
).
In
[
10

the authors show that on any polygonal domain,
for the choice

, where

can be taken
arbitrarily small, there holds

For this choice of

, taking

as a standard

-finite element space trivially
satisfies

. In
[
10

it is then shown, that
if

is chosen as the interior penalty bilinear form then

holds.
On the other hand, for

the

-finite element space, together with

,

and

Under

Let

be the solution of (

) for given data

.
Then
\psi\in X
, and it holds

a_{h}(\psi-\psi_{h},\varphi_{h})=0\qquad\text{for all }\varphi_{h}\in V_{h},

(45)

R_{h}\colon{X}\to V_{h},w\mapsto R_{h}w

from Assumption

a_{h}(w-R_{h}w,\chi)=0\quad\text{for all }\chi\in V_{h}.

(46)

3.2
Full space-time discretization

To discretize in time.
we use the discontinuous Galerkin method.
For that, we partition
I=(0,T]
into subintervals
I_{m}=(t_{m-1},t_{m}]
of length
k_{m}=t_{m}-t_{m-1}
, where

and
k_{\min}=\min_{m}k_{m}

We use the following standard notation for a function

X_{k}^{r}(V):=\set{u_{k}\in L^{2}(I;V)\ }{\ u_{k}\mid_{I_{m}}\in\mathbb{P}_{r}(I_{m};V),\ m=1,2,\dots,M},

u_{k,m}^{+}=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}u_{k}(t_{m}+\varepsilon),\quad u_{k,m}^{-}=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}u_{k}(t_{m}-\varepsilon),\quad[u_{k}]_{m}=u_{k,m}^{+}-u_{k,m}^{-}.

(47)

In several places of the argument we will use the following identity for any
w(t)

with well defined
one-sided limits at

([w]_{m-1},w_{m-1}^{+})_{\Omega}=\frac{1}{2}\|w_{m-1}^{+}\|^{2}_{\Omega}+\frac{1}{2}\|[w]_{m-1}\|^{2}_{\Omega}-\frac{1}{2}\|w_{m-1}^{-}\|^{2}_{\Omega}.

(48)

We define a time projection
\pi_{k}\colon C(\bar{I})\to X_{k}^{r}(\mathbb{R})
which on each time interval
I_{m}
is defined by

\displaystyle(\pi_{k}v-v,\phi)_{I_{m}}

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{for all }\phi\in\mathbb{P}_{r-1}(I_{m}),\quad r>0,

(49)

\displaystyle\pi_{k}v(t_{m}^{-})

\displaystyle=v(t_{m}^{-}).

In the case of
dG(0)
method, the projection is defined by the second condition only.
With these considerations, we can now define the fully discrete approximation to the problem
(
23
), by introducing the fully discrete bilinear form

B_{h}\colon X_{k}^{r}(V_{h})\times X_{k}^{r}(V_{h})\to\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle{B}_{h}(u_{kh},v_{kh})=

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{M}(\partial_{t}\nabla u_{kh},\nabla v_{kh})_{I_{m}\times\Omega}+\int_{0}^{T}a_{h}(u_{kh},v_{kh})dt

(50)

\displaystyle+\sum_{m=2}^{M}([\nabla u_{kh}]_{m-1},{\rm NewA}v_{kh,m-1}^{+})_{\Omega}+({\rm NewA}u_{kh,0}^{+},{\rm NewA}v_{kh,0}^{+})_{\Omega}.

Then the fully discrete problem reads: Find
\psi_{kh}\in X_{k}^{r}(V_{h})
,
such that for given
f\in L^{2}(I;H^{-1-\theta}(\Omega))
,
\psi_{0}\in H^{2-\theta}_{0}(\Omega)

{B}_{h}(\psi_{kh},\varphi_{kh})=\left\langle f,\varphi_{kh}\right\rangle_{I\times\Omega}+\left({\rm NewA}\psi_{0},{\rm NewA}\varphi_{kh,0}^{+}\right)_{\Omega},\quad\text{for all}\ \varphi_{kh}\in X_{k}^{r}(V_{h}),

(51)

where

satisfies
1
.
Note that due to
1
,
the right hand side

is well defined. Moreover, we have the following parabolic Galerkin orthogonality
between the continuous and fully discrete solution.

Lemma 18
.

Let
\theta
satisfy the Assumptions
1
and
2

and let
f\in L^{2}(I;H^{-1-\theta}(\Omega))
,

\psi_{0}\in H^{2-\theta}_{0}(\Omega)
.
Further let
\psi
,
\psi_{kh}
be the continuous and fully discrete solutions to
(
23
) and (
51
) respectively.
Then for any
\varphi_{kh}\in X^{r}_{k}(V_{h})
there holds

B_{h}(\psi-\psi_{kh},\varphi_{kh})=0.

(52)

Proof.

Let
\varphi_{kh}\in X_{k}^{r}(V_{h})
be arbitrary.
Due to the regularity of
f
and
\psi_{0}
, as a consequence of the interpolated regularity result in

Corollary

15
, we have

\partial_{t}\Delta\psi,\mathcal{A}\psi\in L^{2}(I;H^{-1-\theta}(\Omega)).

(53)

By
2
, the regularity of
\mathcal{A}\psi

implies
\psi\in L^{2}(I;X)
, with
X
from Assumption
2
, and thus
in the definition of
B_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
, the term
\int_{I}a_{h}(\psi,\varphi_{kh})\,dt
is well defined.
Further, as due to
Theorem

11
it holds

\nabla\psi\in C(\bar{I};L^{2}(\Omega)^{2})
, all jump terms vanish and

\psi_{0}^{+}=\lim_{t\to 0+}\psi(t)=\psi(0)
.
We thus obtain

{B}_{h}(\psi,\varphi_{kh})=\int_{0}^{T}\left(\partial_{t}\nabla\psi,\nabla\varphi_{kh}\right)_{\Omega}+a_{h}(\psi,\varphi_{kh})dt+\left({\rm NewA}\psi(0),{\rm NewA}\varphi_{kh,0}^{+}\right)_{\Omega}.

By
2
,
due to
\psi\in L^{2}(I;X)
we can replace the discrete elliptic bilinear form

a_{h}(\psi,\varphi_{kh})
by the continuous elliptic operator
\left\langle\mathcal{A}\psi,\varphi_{kh}\right\rangle_{\Omega}
.
Together with the definition of the Laplace operator, this yields

{B}_{h}(\psi,\varphi_{kh})=-\left\langle\partial_{t}\Delta\psi,\varphi_{kh}\right\rangle_{I\times\Omega}+\left\langle\mathcal{A}\psi,\varphi_{kh}\right\rangle_{I\times\Omega}+\left({\rm NewA}\psi(0),{\rm NewA}\varphi_{kh,0}^{+}\right)_{\Omega}.

1
,

V_{h}\subset H^{1+\theta}_{0}(\Omega)
, and
H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
is dense in
H^{1+\theta}_{0}(\Omega)
,
we can approximate
\varphi_{kh}
by a sequence
\{\varphi^{kh}_{n}\}\subset L^{2}(I;H^{2}_{0}(\Omega))
with

\varphi^{kh}_{n}\to\varphi_{kh}
in
L^{2}(I;H^{1+\theta}_{0}(\Omega))
.
As due to (
23
), it holds

-\left\langle\partial_{t}\Delta\psi,\varphi^{kh}_{n}\right\rangle_{I\times\Omega}+\left\langle\mathcal{A}\psi,\varphi^{kh}_{n}\right\rangle_{I\times\Omega}=\left\langle f,\varphi^{kh}_{n}\right\rangle_{I\times\Omega},

letting

, the regularities of

given by assumption, as well as

-\left\langle\partial_{t}\Delta\psi,\varphi_{kh}\right\rangle_{I\times\Omega}+\left\langle\mathcal{A}\psi,\varphi_{kh}\right\rangle_{I\times\Omega}=\left\langle f,\varphi_{kh}\right\rangle_{I\times\Omega}.

Hence together with

\psi_{kh}

\displaystyle{B}_{h}(u_{kh},v_{kh})=

4
Fully discrete error analysis

First, we provide the identity analogous to (
48

a_{h}([w]_{m-1},w_{m-1}^{+})=\frac{1}{2}a_{h}(w^{+}_{m-1},w_{m-1}^{+})+\frac{1}{2}a_{h}([w]_{m-1},[w]_{m-1})-\frac{1}{2}a_{h}(w_{m-1}^{-},w_{m-1}^{-}),

(55)

for any
w(t)\in V_{h}
. The identity follows by adding and subtracting
\frac{1}{2}a_{h}([w]_{m-1},[w]_{m-1})

followed by direct calculations.
We moreover introduce the notation

\psi_{kh,0}^{-}:=\Pi_{h}\psi_{0},\text{ and correspondingly }[\psi_{kh}]_{0}=\psi_{kh,0}^{+}-\Pi_{h}\psi_{0},

(56)

where
\Pi_{h}
is the

-projection from (

Before we show error estimates for the discrete problem, we shall first discuss stability estimates.
Analogously to the continuous results

11
and
13

we are interested in two different stability estimates.
One for low regularity of data (as low as possible such that the discrete problem still is well defined),
and one for higher regularity. In order to derive error estimates by a duality approach, we only require
a stability estimate for higher regularity of data, which we will apply for an appropriate dual problem.
This estimate can be shown under the assumptions made so far, and thus we present it first in

Theorem

19
, which is the discrete analog of
Theorem

13
.
Afterwards, in
Theorem

20
we shall discuss how

Theorem

11
has to be generalized to the discrete case.
The following proof follows the lines of
[
28
]
for second order parabolic problems.

Theorem 19
.

Let
g\in L^{2}(I;H^{1}(\Omega)),\psi_{0}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, and let
\psi_{kh}\in X^{r}_{k}(V_{h})
be the
fully discrete solution to

{B}_{h}(\psi_{kh},\varphi_{kh})=\left(\nabla g,\nabla\varphi_{kh}\right)_{I\times\Omega}+\left({\rm NewA}\psi_{0},{\rm NewA}\varphi_{kh,0}^{+}\right)_{\Omega}\quad\text{for all $\varphi_{kh}\in X_{k}^{r}(V_{h})$}.

(57)

Then there exists a constant
C
independent of
k
and

such that

\sum_{m=1}^{M}\|{\rm NewA}\partial_{t}\psi_{kh}\|^{2}_{I_{m}\times\Omega}+\|{\rm NewA}A_{h}\psi_{kh}\|^{2}_{I\times\Omega}+\sum_{m=1}^{M}k^{-1}_{m}\|[{\rm NewA}\psi_{kh}]_{m-1}\|^{2}_{\Omega}\leq C({\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\Pi_{h}\psi_{0}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}_{h}+\|{\rm NewA}g\|^{2}_{I\times\Omega}).

Proof.

First we test the equation for each
m=1,...,M
with
\varphi_{kh}|_{I_{m}}=A_{h}\psi_{kh}|_{I_{m}}
and set

\varphi_{kh}
to zero on all other subintervals. Then on each time subinterval
I_{m}
, we obtain

\int_{I_{m}}({\rm NewA}\partial_{t}\psi_{kh},{\rm NewA}A_{h}\psi_{kh})_{\Omega}dt+\int_{I_{m}}\|{\rm NewA}A_{h}\psi_{kh}\|^{2}dt+({\rm NewA}[\psi_{kh}]_{m-1},{\rm NewA}A_{h}\psi_{kh,m-1}^{+})_{\Omega}=\int_{I_{m}}({\rm NewA}g,{\rm NewA}A_{h}\psi_{kh})_{\Omega}dt,

where for
m=1
, we have made use of our convention (
56
).
Using that

(\partial_{t}{\rm NewA}\psi_{kh},{\rm NewA}A_{h}\psi_{kh})_{I_{m}\times\Omega}=\int_{I_{m}}\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}a_{h}(\psi_{kh},\psi_{kh})\ dt=\frac{1}{2}a_{h}(\psi_{kh,m}^{-},\psi_{kh,m}^{-})-\frac{1}{2}a_{h}(\psi_{kh,m-1}^{+},\psi_{kh,m-1}^{+})

and
since

({\rm NewA}[\psi_{kh}]_{m-1},{\rm NewA}A_{h}\psi_{kh,m-1}^{+})_{\Omega}=a_{h}([\psi_{kh}]_{m-1},\psi^{+}_{kh,m-1})

applying
the identity (
55
), we notice that the term with
a_{h}(\psi_{kh,m-1}^{+},\psi_{kh,m-1}^{+})
cancels out and as a result
we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}a_{h}(\psi_{kh,m}^{-},\psi_{kh,m}^{-})-\frac{1}{2}a_{h}(\psi_{kh,m-1}^{-},\psi_{kh,m-1}^{-})+\frac{1}{2}a_{h}([\psi_{kh}]_{m-1},[\psi_{kh}]_{m-1})

\displaystyle+\int_{I_{m}}\|{\rm NewA}A_{h}\psi_{kh}\|_{\Omega}^{2}dt

\displaystyle=({\rm NewA}g,{\rm NewA}A_{h}\psi_{kh})_{I_{m}\times\Omega}.

Summing over
m
, and using the convention
\psi_{kh,0}^{-}=\Pi_{h}\psi_{0}
yields

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}a_{h}(\psi_{kh,M}^{-},\psi_{kh,M}^{-})+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m=1}^{M}a_{h}([\psi_{kh}]_{m-1},[\psi_{kh}]_{m-1})

\displaystyle+\int_{I}\|{\rm NewA}A_{h}\psi_{kh}\|_{\Omega}^{2}dt

\displaystyle=({\rm NewA}g,{\rm NewA}A_{h}\psi_{kh})_{I\times\Omega}+\frac{1}{2}a_{h}(\Pi_{h}\psi_{0},\Pi_{h}\psi_{0}).

Using the Cauchy-Schwarz and Young’s inequalities, we have

\|{\rm NewA}A_{h}\psi_{kh}\|^{2}_{I\times\Omega}\leq\|{\rm NewA}g\|_{I\times\Omega}\|{\rm NewA}A_{h}\psi_{kh}\|_{I\times\Omega}+\frac{1}{2}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\Pi_{h}\psi_{0}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{h}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|{\rm NewA}g\|_{I\times\Omega}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|{\rm NewA}A_{h}\psi_{kh}\|^{2}_{I\times\Omega}+\frac{1}{2}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\Pi_{h}\psi_{0}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{h}^{2}.

Canceling, we obtain

\|{\rm NewA}A_{h}\psi_{kh}\|^{2}_{I\times\Omega}\leq\|{\rm NewA}g\|^{2}_{I\times\Omega}+{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\Pi_{h}\psi_{0}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{h}^{2}.

(58)

Step 2. Time derivative terms.

Note that for the case
r=0
there is nothing to prove here, hence we assume in this step
r\geq 1
.
Noticing that
(t-t_{m-1})\partial_{t}\psi_{kh}\in\mathbb{P}_{r}(I_{m};V_{h})
, we choose
\varphi_{kh}=(t-t_{m-1})\partial_{t}\psi_{kh}
on the time interval
I_{m}
and zero on others. Noticing that the term with jumps vanishes, we have

\int_{I_{m}}(t-t_{m-1})\|{\rm NewA}\partial_{t}\psi_{kh}\|^{2}_{\Omega}dt+\int_{I_{m}}(t-t_{m-1})a_{h}(\psi_{kh},\partial_{t}\psi_{kh})dt=\int_{I_{m}}(t-t_{m-1})({\rm NewA}g,{\rm NewA}\partial_{t}\psi_{kh})_{\Omega}dt.

For the second term, using the Cauchy-Schwarz and Young’s inequalities,

\displaystyle\int_{I_{m}}(t-t_{m-1})a_{h}(\psi_{kh},\partial_{t}\psi_{kh})dt

\displaystyle=\int_{I_{m}}(t-t_{m-1})({\rm NewA}\partial_{t}\psi_{kh},{\rm NewA}A_{h}\psi_{kh})_{\Omega}dt

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{I_{m}}(t-t_{m-1})\|{\rm NewA}\partial_{t}\psi_{kh}\|_{\Omega}^{2}dt\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{I_{m}}(t-t_{m-1})\|{\rm NewA}A_{h}\psi_{kh}\|_{\Omega}^{2}dt\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\int_{I_{m}}(t-t_{m-1})\|{\rm NewA}\partial_{t}\psi_{kh}\|_{\Omega}^{2}dt+\int_{I_{m}}(t-t_{m-1})\|{\rm NewA}A_{h}\psi_{kh}\|_{\Omega}^{2}dt.

Similarly,
Using the Cauchy-Schwarz and Young’s inequalities,

\displaystyle\int_{I_{m}}(t-t_{m-1})({\rm NewA}g,{\rm NewA}\partial_{t}\psi_{kh})_{\Omega}dt

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{I_{m}}(t-t_{m-1})\|{\rm NewA}g\|^{2}_{\Omega}dt\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{I_{m}}(t-t_{m-1})\|{\rm NewA}\partial_{t}\psi_{kh}\|^{2}_{\Omega}dt\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\int_{I_{m}}(t-t_{m-1})\|{\rm NewA}\partial_{t}\psi_{kh}\|_{\Omega}^{2}dt+\int_{I_{m}}(t-t_{m-1})\|{\rm NewA}g\|^{2}_{\Omega}dt.

Combining estimates, and kicking back, we obtain

\int_{I_{m}}(t-t_{m-1})\|{\rm NewA}\partial_{t}\psi_{kh}\|^{2}_{\Omega}dt\leq C\int_{I_{m}}(t-t_{m-1})\|{\rm NewA}g\|^{2}_{\Omega}dt+C\int_{I_{m}}(t-t_{m-1})\|{\rm NewA}A_{h}\psi_{kh}\|^{2}_{\Omega}dt.

Using the inverse inequality

\int_{I_{m}}\|\chi_{k}\|^{2}_{\Omega}dt\leq Ck_{m}^{-1}\int_{I_{m}}(t-t_{m-1})\|\chi_{k}\|^{2}_{\Omega}dt

which holds for any piecewise polynomial in time with fixed maximal degree,
for example for
\chi_{k}={\rm NewA}\partial_{t}\psi_{kh}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{I_{m}}\|{\rm NewA}\partial_{t}\psi_{kh}\|^{2}_{\Omega}dt

\displaystyle\leq Ck_{m}^{-1}\int_{I_{m}}(t-t_{m-1})\|{\rm NewA}g\|^{2}_{\Omega}dt+Ck_{m}^{-1}\int_{I_{m}}(t-t_{m-1})\|{\rm NewA}A_{h}\psi_{kh}\|_{\Omega}^{2}dt

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{I_{m}}\|{\rm NewA}g\|^{2}_{\Omega}dt+C\int_{I_{m}}\|{\rm NewA}A_{h}\psi_{kh}\|_{\Omega}^{2}dt.

Summing over
m
and using (
58
) yields

\int_{I}\|{\rm NewA}\partial_{t}\psi_{kh}\|^{2}_{\Omega}dt\leq C({\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\Pi_{h}\psi_{0}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}_{h}+\|{\rm NewA}g\|^{2}_{I\times\Omega}).

(59)

Step 3. Jump terms.

For every

and set
\varphi_{kh}

to zero on all other subintervals. Inserting this choice yields

Using the Cauchy-Schwarz and Young’s inequalities,

\displaystyle\int_{I_{m}}({\rm NewA}\partial_{t}\psi_{kh},[{\rm NewA}\psi_{kh}]_{m-1})_{\Omega}dt

\displaystyle\leq\left(k_{m}\int_{I_{m}}\|{\rm NewA}\partial_{t}\psi_{kh}\|^{2}_{\Omega}dt\right)^{1/2}\left(k^{-1}_{m}\int_{I_{m}}\|[{\rm NewA}\psi_{kh}]_{m-1}\|^{2}_{\Omega}dt\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq k_{m}\int_{I_{m}}\|{\rm NewA}\partial_{t}\psi_{kh}\|^{2}_{\Omega}dt+\frac{1}{4}\|[{\rm NewA}\psi_{kh}]_{m-1}\|^{2}_{\Omega},

where we used that
\varphi_{kh}|_{I_{m}}=[\psi_{kh}]_{m-1}
is constant on
I_{m}
. Similarly,

\displaystyle\int_{I_{m}}({\rm NewA}A_{h}\psi_{kh},[{\rm NewA}\psi_{kh}]_{m-1})_{\Omega}dt

\displaystyle\leq\left(k_{m}\int_{I_{m}}\|{\rm NewA}A_{h}\psi_{kh}\|^{2}_{\Omega}dt\right)^{1/2}\left(k^{-1}_{m}\int_{I_{m}}\|[{\rm NewA}\psi_{kh}]_{m-1}\|^{2}_{\Omega}dt\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq k_{m}\int_{I_{m}}\|{\rm NewA}A_{h}\psi_{kh}\|^{2}_{\Omega}dt+\frac{1}{4}\|[{\rm NewA}\psi_{kh}]_{m-1}\|^{2}_{\Omega},

and

\displaystyle\int_{I_{m}}({\rm NewA}g,[{\rm NewA}\psi_{kh}]_{m-1})_{\Omega}dt

\displaystyle\leq\left(k_{m}\int_{I_{m}}\|{\rm NewA}g\|^{2}_{\Omega}dt\right)^{1/2}\left(k^{-1}_{m}\int_{I_{m}}\|[{\rm NewA}\psi_{kh}]_{m-1}\|^{2}_{\Omega}dt\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq k_{m}\int_{I_{m}}\|{\rm NewA}g\|^{2}_{\Omega}dt+\frac{1}{4}\|[{\rm NewA}\psi_{kh}]_{m-1}\|^{2}_{\Omega}dt.

Combining, we obtain

k^{-1}_{m}\|[{\rm NewA}\psi_{kh}]_{m-1}\|^{2}_{\Omega}\leq C\left(\int_{I_{m}}\|{\rm NewA}g\|^{2}_{\Omega}dt+\int_{I_{m}}\|{\rm NewA}\partial_{t}\psi_{kh}\|^{2}_{\Omega}dt+\int_{I_{m}}\|{\rm NewA}A_{h}\psi_{kh}\psi_{kh}\|^{2}_{\Omega}dt\right).

Summing over
m
and using (
58
) and (

\sum_{m=1}^{M}k^{-1}_{m}\|[{\rm NewA}\psi_{kh}]_{m-1}\|^{2}_{\Omega}\leq C({\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\Pi_{h}\psi_{0}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}_{h}+\|{\rm NewA}g\|^{2}_{I\times\Omega}),

which completes the proof of the lemma.

4.1.2
The
C^{0}

This method is very attractive since it uses Lagrange elements of degree

.
To define the method, we need some additional notation. Let
\mathcal{E}_{h}
be the set of edges in
\mathcal{T}_{h}
.
For

and a suitably regular function

we define the jump
\left[\hskip-2.52971pt\left[\frac{\partial v}{\partial n}\right]\hskip-2.52971pt\right]
of the normal derivative of
v
across an edge
e
and the average of the second normal derivative
\left\{\hskip-3.97432pt\left\{\frac{\partial^{2}v}{\partial n^{2}}\right\}\hskip-3.97432pt\right\}

on an edge
e
as follows. If

,

to be one of the two unit vectors normal to

Thus, on such

\left[\hskip-2.52971pt\left[\frac{\partial v}{\partial n}\right]\hskip-2.52971pt\right]=\frac{\partial v_{T_{+}}}{\partial n}\mid_{e}-\frac{\partial v_{T-}}{\partial n}\mid_{e}\quad\text{and}\quad\left\{\hskip-3.97432pt\left\{\frac{\partial^{2}v}{\partial n^{2}}\right\}\hskip-3.97432pt\right\}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial^{2}v_{T_{+}}}{\partial n^{2}}\mid_{e}+\frac{\partial^{2}v_{T-}}{\partial n^{2}}\mid_{e}\right).

We note that the above definitions do not depend on the choice of

If

to be the unit normal pointing outside

It is clear that
\left[\hskip-2.52971pt\left[\frac{\partial v}{\partial n}\right]\hskip-2.52971pt\right],\left\{\hskip-3.97432pt\left\{\frac{\partial^{2}v}{\partial n^{2}}\right\}\hskip-3.97432pt\right\}
are well defined for

. Moreover, following the discussion in Remark

,
the space

from

for some
\varepsilon>0

We thus define the bilinear form

by

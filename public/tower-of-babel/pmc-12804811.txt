Investigation of ion acoustic double layers in magnetized plasma with two nonextensive electron species
Authors: M M Hatami
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe dynamics of ion-acoustic double layers in a magnetized plasma composed of cold ions and two populations of q-nonextensive electrons are investigated using the Sagdeev pseudopotential approach. The analysis identifies how key plasma parameters-including the Mach number, magnetic field obliquity, electron nonextensivity, and the temperature ratio between hot and cool electrons-govern the existence and polarity of nonlinear electrostatic structures. Rather than claiming full coverage of the high-dimensional parameter space, the present work provides a detailed and physically motivated sampling to illustrate how variations in plasma parameters such as , , , and modify the Sagdeev potential landscape. Introduction

Electrostatic solitary structures, including ion-acoustic double layers (IADLs), have been widely observed in the auroral acceleration region and various parts of Earth’s magnetosphere through numerous spacecraft missions1-5. Among these, IADLs in multi-component plasmas have garnered significant attention due to their vital role in particle acceleration, deceleration, and reflection processes in natural plasma environments such as solar winds and extragalactic jets.

Significant theoretical efforts have been dedicated to studying DLs in multi-component plasmas. Bharuthram and Shukla6 employed the Sagdeev pseudopotential approach to investigate unmagnetized plasmas composed of cold ions and two groups of Maxwell-Boltzmann distributed hot electrons, establishing the conditions for the existence of large amplitude stationary DLs. Later, they used reductive perturbation techniques to analyze the dynamics of ion-acoustic solitons (IASs) and DLs in magnetized plasmas containing two Maxwellian electron species7. Additional studies, such as those by Baboolal et al.8, explored large- and small-amplitude DLs in unmagnetized multi-component plasmas with two warm ion populations and two electron groups. Moreover, Lakhina et al.9,10 have examined ion- and electron-acoustic waves in plasmas featuring cold background populations and hot beams of electrons and ions. Also, Sultana et al.11 have studied ion-acoustic solitary waves (IASWs) in magnetized plasmas with kappa-distributed electrons and fluid cool ions. They employed psuedo Sagdeev potential approach and used quasi-neutrality condition. Using Sagdeev analysis, electron acoustic solitary waves in a magnetized plasma consisting of fluid cold electrons and hot superthermal ions have been studied by Devanandhan et al.12. Moreover, finite amplitude non-linear IASWs and DLs in a magnetized plasma consisting of cold ions and two distinct groups of Boltzmann electrons have been investigated by Rufai et al.13. Singh and coworkers14have established a two-component plasma model consisting of adiabatic ion fluid and superthermal hot electrons to study the effect of ion temperature on finite amplitude nonlinear low frequency electrostatic structures in a magnetized auroral plasma. Properties of obliquely propagating IASs and DLs in a magnetized auroral plasma composed of hot adiabatic ions and two types of cool and hot Maxwellian electrons have been studied in Ref1.. Meanwhile, spacecraft observations16-18 have revealed the widespread presence of nonthermal particle distributions in space plasmas such as planetary magnetospheres, astrophysical plasmas, and the solar wind. These findings have necessitated the adoption of nonextensive statistical mechanics for accurately modeling plasma systems far from thermodynamic equilibrium. Initially recognized by Renyi19 and later formalized by Tsallis20, the nonextensive generalization of Boltzmann-Gibbs-Shannon (B-G-S) entropy introduces a key parameter, the entropic index q, which measures the degree of nonextensivity in the system.

The nonlinear behavior of plasmas described by nonextensive distributions has been extensively studied. For example, Roy et al.24 examined dust-acoustic DLs in unmagnetized plasmas with nonextensive electrons and two-temperature ions, while Akhtar et al.25 analyzed DLs in electronegative plasmas featuring nonextensive q-electrons. Other notable works include studies of solitons in dusty plasmas26, finite-amplitude waves in magnetized plasmas with nonextensive hot electrons27, and IADLs in unmagnetized electronegative plasmas with Tsallis statistics [28–31].

Despite considerable progress in modeling IASWs and DL structures, the combined effects of magnetization and non-Maxwellian electron populations remain only partially explored. Observations in Earth’s auroral region reveal the simultaneous presence of hot and cool electrons following non-thermal distributions and the frequent occurrence of solitary structures32-34. These features motivate a theoretical description that goes beyond purely Maxwellian assumptions and incorporates both magnetic obliquity and electron nonextensivity. The present study extends earlier unmagnetized or Maxwellian models7,11,31,35 by introducing two q-nonextensive electron populations and an oblique magnetic field into the Sagdeev pseudopotential framework. Our goal is not to exhaustively map the entire multidimensional parameter space-which is prohibitively large-but rather to investigate the representative regime relevant to magnetized auroral plasmas, where typical ranges of parameters are informed by observations [33]: , , , and . Moreover, as we known, observational studies of auroral plasmas have consistently reported the presence of strongly superthermal electron populations characterized by long-tailed, non-Maxwellian distributions. In the framework of nonextensive (Tsallis) statistics, such superthermal behavior corresponds to the regime , whereas represents subthermal distributions with depleted high-energy tails. Accordingly, we have restricted our investigation to the physically relevant range , consistent with auroral observations, and excluded the domain from consideration.

Within this physically motivated domain, we derive analytic conditions for the existence of DLs and identify the corresponding lower and upper Mach number limits, and . We then explore how variations in nonextensivity, initial density and temperature of electron species, and the propagation angle alter the Sagdeev potential and the structure of the resulting IADLs. The intent is therefore to illustrate representative behaviors within the observable range of parameters, while recognizing that other regions of parameter space may yield different nonlinear responses.

In this section we explore the existence and morphology of arbitrary-amplitude IADLs in a magnetized plasma made up of cold ions and two populations of q-nonextensive electrons. Our analysis is motivated by spacecraft observations of nonlinear ion-acoustic structures in the magnetized auroral plasma33, where the coexistence of cool and hot electron populations plays a decisive role in the formation of DLs. The auroral zone parameters were selected according to the data provided by Berthomier et al.33, which clearly indicate the presence of superthermal electrons. Therefore, following observational evidence, we excluded the range and restricted our investigation to , which corresponds to the sub-extensive regime associated with enhanced high-energy particle populations commonly observed in auroral plasmas.

This widening effect can be attributed to enhanced dispersive influences in the plasma at more oblique propagation, where longitudinal and transverse dynamics increasingly couple. Such sensitivity emphasizes the delicate balance between nonlinearity and dispersion that governs DL formation. Remarkably, the amplitude of the DLs remains largely unaffected by variations in , even though the Sagdeev pseudopotential becomes increasingly deeper with stronger magnetic fields, as shown in Fig. 5(a). The narrowing occurs because a stronger magnetic field constrains the ion motion more tightly by reducing their Larmor radius, thereby enhancing localization of the potential structure. This behavior agrees with earlier results reported for magnetized plasmas with two Maxwellian electron components38 and for plasmas containing superthermal electrons [11, 14]. Importantly, since does not influence the limits of the Mach number interval , it cannot initiate or prevent DL formation, but it critically modifies the dispersion characteristics of the plasma, thus shaping the DL profile. These findings emphasize that magnetic field strength plays a secondary, yet non-negligible role—primarily altering the spatial scale of IADLs without affecting their amplitude or existence domain.

The results confirm that, similar to the nonextensive case, only compressive (positive potential) DLs can form in such a plasma configuration. Figure 8(a) shows that the Sagdeev pseudopotential retains a single potential well, enabling the formation of localized DLs, while Fig. 8(b) demonstrates how various plasma parameters influence the amplitude and spatial width of these structures. For instance, increasing the cool electron concentration or the temperature ratio leads to a marked reduction in both the amplitude and depth of the pseudopotential well—resulting in broader and weaker DLs. Conversely, variations in the magnetic field strength affect the width but not the amplitude of the DLs, highlighting its role in modifying dispersion rather than nonlinearity. Notably, the propagation angle remains a sensitive parameter, with small increases suppressing DL formation altogether. These findings not only corroborate earlier results reported in Ref13. for Maxwellian plasmas but also reinforce the idea that the impact of key plasma parameters on DL characteristics remains qualitatively consistent across both Maxwellian and q-nonextensive models.

Conclusion

This work has analyzed ion-acoustic double layers (IADLs) in a magnetized plasma with cold ions and two-temperature nonextensive electrons using the Sagdeev pseudopotential formalism. The analysis defines and examines a physically constrained domain relevant to auroral magnetized plasmas rather than the entire theoretical parameter space. Within this regime, the existence of IADLs is confined between two Mach number limits, and , both of which depend sensitively on the cool-electron nonextensivity , fractional density , and temperature ratio . The lower Mach limit is additionally influenced by the propagation angle , whereas the upper limit remains nearly independent of obliquity. Notably, the presence of a magnetic field enables the formation of double layers (DLs) in the supersonic regime—an important departure from unmagnetized cases.

Our results further demonstrate that variations in the hot electron nonextensivity index , cool electron concentration , temperature ratio , and propagation angle tend to produce broader and shallower DLs by reducing the depth of the Sagdeev pseudopotential. In contrast, an increase in the magnetic field strength enhances the depth of the pseudopotential well, leading to sharper and more localized nonlinear structures. Notably, while certain parameters—such as , , and —influence both the shape and amplitude of the DLs, others like and exert a more selective effect, primarily altering width or steepness without significantly changing amplitude. This nuanced behavior underscores the distinct and sometimes decoupled roles that various plasma parameters play in shaping electrostatic DLs. Furthermore, we showed that the present fluid-based model reduces naturally to a two-Maxwellian limit, which retains the core characteristics of IADLs. This consistency reinforces the reliability and general relevance of our findings.

While the present work does not attempt to map the full multidimensional parameter space, it provides a clear methodological framework for doing so and highlights the sensitivity of IADL properties to key physical quantities within realistic auroral conditions. These results are therefore most applicable to the interpretation of ion-acoustic solitary and double-layer structures observed in magnetized auroral plasmas. Indeed, spacecraft observations have reported large-amplitude solitary pulses and DL-like potential structures in the auroral acceleration region (e.g., FAST observations of solitary structures and associated field-aligned potentials32, as well as solitary waves and weak double layers in two-temperature auroral plasmas33. The present analysis thus offers a theoretical framework consistent with such in-situ observations and provides a step toward connecting Sagdeev-based models with measurable features of auroral space plasmas.

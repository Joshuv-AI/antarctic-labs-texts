This paper has its origins in the study of comonotone approximation by entire functions of

. There is a natural and standard topological vector space structure on the family

.

The case
n=0
is covered by
[
2
]
, Proposition 6.2, but we prove it here for completeness.
The inclusion
W_{0}\subseteq S_{0}
is clear since it states only that if
f\in\mathscr{F}_{0}
, (i.e.,
f
is continuous and increasing but not constant) and
f(0)=0
, then
f(1)>0
.

To see that
S_{0}\subseteq W_{0}^{\infty}
, let
a\in S_{0}
, i.e.,
a>0
.
We want an
f\in\mathscr{F}_{0}^{\infty}
such that
f(0)=0
,
f(1)=a
and
D^{j}f(0)=\alpha_{j}
,
D^{j}f(1)=\beta_{j}
,
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
. By Proposition
3.1
, the functions
{\sigma}_{\delta}
and
\tau_{\delta}
fixed at the beginning of Section
3
could have been chosen so that
D^{j}{\sigma}_{\delta}(0)=\alpha_{j}/a
,
D^{j}\tau_{\delta}(1)=\beta_{j}/a
,
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Then Corollary
8.1
(1) gives, for each small enough
\delta>0
, a
C^{\infty}
function
g\colon[0,1]\to[0,1]
such that
g={\sigma}_{\delta}
on
[0,\delta]
,
g=\tau_{\delta}
on
[1-\delta,1]
, and
Dg>0
on
(0,1)
.
The function
f=ag
is as desired.

For the case
n=1
,
The inequality
W_{1}\subseteq S_{1}
follows from Proposition
6.1
. Given
f\in\mathscr{F}_{0}
satisfying
f(0)=0
, if we write
b=f(1)
and
a=I(f)(1)
then the function
g=b^{-1}f
maps into
[0,1]
. We have
I(g)(1)=b^{-1}a
. Plugging this value in for the
a
of Proposition
6.1
, we get
0<b^{-1}a<1
, or
0<a<b
, and hence
(a,b)\in S_{1}
.

To show
S_{1}\subseteq W_{1}^{\infty}
, let
(a,b)\in S_{1}
.
We want
f\in\mathscr{F}^{\infty}_{0}
such that
f(0)=0
,
f(1)=b
,
I(f)(1)=a
, and

D^{j}f(0)=\alpha_{j}
,
D^{j}f(1)=\beta_{j}
,
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
We have
0<a/b<1
.
By Proposition
3.1
, the functions
{\sigma}_{\delta}
and
\tau_{\delta}
fixed at the beginning of Section
3
could have been chosen so that

D^{j}{\sigma}_{\delta}(0)=\alpha_{j}/b
,
D^{j}\tau_{\delta}(1)=\beta_{j}/b
,
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
By Corollary
8.1
(2), for each small enough
\delta>0
, there is a
C^{\infty}
function
g\colon[0,1]\to[0,1]
such that
g={\sigma}_{\delta}
on
[0,\delta/2]
,
g=\tau_{\delta}
on
[1-\delta/2,1]
,
Dg>0
on
(0,1)
, and
I(g)(1)=a/b
. The function
f=bg
is as desired.

For the case
n=2
, the inequality
W_{2}\subseteq S_{2}
follows from Proposition
6.2
.
Given
f\in\mathscr{F}_{0}
satisfying
f(0)=0
, if we write
c=f(1)
,
b=I(f)(1)
,
a=I^{2}(f)(1)
, then the function
g=c^{-1}f
maps into
[0,1]
.
We have
I(g)(1)=b/c
,
I^{2}(g)(1)=a/c
. Plugging these values in for the
a
and
b
of Proposition
6.1
(4), we get

(b/c)^{2}/2<a/c<(b/c)/2
, or
b^{2}<2ac
and
2a<b
. Positivity of
a
is clear from its definition. Thus,
(a,b,c)\in S_{2}
.

To show
S_{2}\subseteq W_{2}^{\infty}
, let
(a,b,c)\in S_{2}
, so
0<2a<b
and
b^{2}<2ac
.
We want
f\in\mathscr{F}^{\infty}_{0}
such that
f(0)=0
,
f(1)=c
,
I(f)(1)=b
,
I^{2}(f)(1)=a
, and

D^{j}f(0)=\alpha_{j}
,
D^{j}f(1)=\beta_{j}
,
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
From
b^{2}<2ac
and
2a<b
we get
b^{2}<2ac<bc
, or
(b/c)^{2}/2<a/c<(b/c)/2
. By Proposition
3.1
, the functions
{\sigma}_{\delta}
and
\tau_{\delta}
fixed at the beginning of Section
3
could have been chosen so that

D^{j}{\sigma}_{\delta}(0)=\alpha_{j}/c
,
D^{j}\tau_{\delta}(1)=\beta_{j}/c
,
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
By Corollary
8.1
(3), for each small enough
\delta>0
, there is a
C^{\infty}
function
g\colon[0,1]\to[0,1]
such that
g={\sigma}_{\delta}
on
[0,\delta/2]
,
g=\tau_{\delta}
on
[1-\delta/2,1]
,

on
(0,1)
,

and

. The function

is as desired.

For the case
n=3
, the inequality
W_{3}\subseteq S_{3}
follows from Proposition
6.11
.
Given
f\in\mathscr{F}_{0}
satisfying
f(0)=0
, if we write
d=f(1)
,
c=I(f)(1)
,
b=I^{2}(f)(1)
,
a=I^{3}(f)(1)
, then the function
g=d^{-1}f
maps into
[0,1]
.
We have

I(g)(1)=c/d
, and similarly

I^{2}(g)(1)=b/d
,

I^{3}(g)(1)=a/d
. Plugging these in respectively for the
a,b,c
of Proposition
6.11
, we get

\frac{2(b/d)^{2}}{3(c/d)}<\frac{a}{d}<\frac{-(c/d)^{2}+2(c/d)(b/d)-4(b/d)^{2}+2(b/d)}{6(1-(c/d))}.

Multiplying by
d
and simplifying gives

\frac{2b^{2}}{3c}<a<\frac{-c^{2}+2cb-4b^{2}+2bd}{6(d-c)}.

We can write these inequalities as
2b^{2}<3ac
and
6ad-6ac<-c^{2}+2cb-4b^{2}+2bd
, or
6ad+4b^{2}+c^{2}<2cb+2bd+6ac
. The property
0<c<d
holds since
(c,d)\in W_{1}
(witnessed by
f
).
Thus,
(a,b,c,d)\in S_{3}
.

To show
S_{3}\subseteq W_{3}^{\infty}
, let
(a,b,c,d)\in S_{3}
. We have

0<c<d,\ 2b^{2}<3ac,\ \text{and}\ 6ad+4b^{2}+c^{2}<2cb+2bd+6ac.

We want
f\in\mathscr{F}^{\infty}_{0}
such that
f(0)=0
,
f(1)=d
,
I(f)(1)=c
,
I^{2}(f)(1)=b
,
I^{3}(f)(1)=a
, and

D^{j}f(0)=\alpha_{j}
,
D^{j}f(1)=\beta_{j}
,
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Since
0<c<d
, we have
0<c/d<1
.
The inequalities
2b^{2}<3ac
and
6ad+4b^{2}+c^{2}<2cb+2bd+6ac
can be re-written as

\frac{2b^{2}}{3c}<a<\frac{-c^{2}+2bc-4b^{2}+2bd}{6(d-c)}.

Dividing the numerators by
d^{2}
and the denominators by
d
gives the same inequalities with
a,b,c,d
replaced by
a/d,b/d,c/d,1
.
By Proposition
3.1
, the functions
{\sigma}_{\delta}
and
\tau_{\delta}
fixed at the beginning of Section
3
could have been chosen so that

D^{j}{\sigma}_{\delta}(0)=\alpha_{j}/d
,
D^{j}\tau_{\delta}(1)=\beta_{j}/d
,
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
By Theorem
7.1
, for each small enough
\delta>0
, there is a
C^{\infty}
function
g\colon[0,1]\to[0,1]
such that
g={\sigma}_{\delta}
on
[0,\delta/2]
,
g=\tau_{\delta}
on
[1-\delta/2,1]
,
Dg>0
on
(0,1)
,
I(g)(1)=c/d
,
I^{2}(g)(1)=b/d
and
I^{3}(g)(1)=a/d
. The function
f=dg
is as desired.

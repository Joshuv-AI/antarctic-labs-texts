Entropy and the growth rate of universal covering trees eisnerida@telhai.ac.il hooryshl@telhai.ac.il
Idan Eisner, Shlomo Hoory

Abstract
This work studies the relation between two graph parameters, ρ \rho and Λ \Lambda . For an undirected graph G G , ρ ⁡ ( G ) \rho(G) is the growth rate of its universal covering tree, while Λ ⁡ ( G ) \Lambda(G) is a weighted geometric average of the vertex degree minus one, corresponding to the rate of entropy growth for the non-backtracking random walk (NBRW). It is well known that ρ ⁡ ( G ) ≥ Λ ⁡ ( G ) \rho(G)\geq\Lambda(G) for all graphs, and that graphs with ρ = Λ \rho=\Lambda exhibit some special properties. In this work we derive an easy to check, necessary and sufficient condition for the equality to hold. Furthermore, we show that the variance of the number of random bits used by a length ℓ \ell NBRW is O ⁡ ( 1 ) O(1) if ρ = Λ \rho=\Lambda and Ω ⁡ ( ℓ ) \Omega(\ell) if ρ > Λ \rho>\Lambda . As a consequence we exhibit infinitely many non-trivial examples of graphs with ρ = Λ \rho=\Lambda .

The Main Players

This paper investigates the relation between two graph parameters
\Lambda

Given the graph

•

It is well know that

1.2
Some Motivation

The non-backtracking random walk (NBRW) on the graph
G
is a process that explores a graph by moving from vertex to vertex,
where in
each step, the walk moves to a randomly chosen neighbor of the current vertex, excluding the one it just visited.
It was shown by Alon et. al
[
2
]
that this extra bias towards exploration makes the walk mix faster on regular expander graphs, compared to the simple random walk (SRW) that does not employ this extra constraint.
Also, it was shown that the SRW as well as the NBRW exhibit a sharp threshold in their convergence to the stationary distribution for regular Ramanujan graphs
[
14
]
, as well as for various random graph models whp.
Lubetzky et al.
[
15
]
, proved such a result for random
d
-regular graphs,
{\cal G}(n,d)
,
Ben-Hamou et al.
[
5
]
, for random graphs with a prescribed degree distribution in the configuration model,
and Conchon-Kerjan
[
7
]
for random lifts of a fixed based graph.

The case of a random
n
-lift of some base graph
G
is closely related to this work, as the mixing time of the NBRW is
t_{s}=(1+o(1))\log_{\Lambda}n
with high probability
[
7
]
, while the diameter of a random
n
-lift is
t_{d}=(1+o(1))\log_{\rho}n
whp
[
6
]
.
Put differently, performing the NBRW on a random
n
-lift of
G
, the first time when all vertices have positive probability of being reached is
t_{d}
,
while the first time when the walk gets close to the stationary distribution is
t_{s}
.
Clearly
t_{s}\geq t_{d}
.
Therefore, asking if
\rho(G)=\Lambda(G)
is the same as asking if the two events occur at the same time, up to a
1+o(1)
factor.

A second motivation for the
\rho
vs.
\Lambda
question is the Moore bound for irregular graphs.
In
[
3
]
, Alon et. al proved that any girth

with the same degree distribution as

\Omega(\Lambda(G)^{g/2})

while in
[
11
]
Hoory proved that any girth

has at least
\Omega(\rho(G)^{g/2})
vertices.
Therefore, when
\rho(G)=\Lambda(G)
the bounds coincide, and otherwise there is an exponential gap between the two bounds.

2.1
The Non-Backtracking Random Walk and Adjacency Matrix

Given a simple undirected graph
G=(V,E)
, we regard each edge as a pair of directed edges and denote the set of directed edges by
\vec{E}
.
For a directed edge
e=(u,v)\in\vec{E}
, we denote its head by
h(e)=v
and tail by
t(e)=u
.
We define
\outdeg(e)=\deg(h(e))-1
,
\indeg(e)=\deg(t(e))-1
, and the reversed edge
e^{\prime}=(u,v)^{\prime}=(v,u)
.

Definition 1
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a simple undirected graph with minimal degree at least two.
For two directed edges
e,f
, we say that one can transition from
e
to
f
if
h(e)=t(f)
and
e^{\prime}\neq f
and denote this relation by
e\rightarrow f
.
The Non-Backtracking Random-Walk (NBRW) on
G
is a Markov chain on the state space
\vec{E}
with the transition matrix
\Pi_{G}
defined by:

\pi_{G}(e,f)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{\outdeg(e)},&\mbox{ if }e\rightarrow f\\
0,&\mbox{otherwise}\end{cases}

We regard the state
e=(u,v)
as being at
v
coming from
u
.
Let
B
denote the non-backtracking adjacency matrix, which is an
\vec{E}
by
\vec{E}
0-1 matrix where
B_{e,f}=1
if
e\rightarrow f
.

Remark 2
.

The definition generalizes to allow for multiple edges and self loops in
G
, where a self loop can be either a whole-loop or a half-loop.
Each edge, except for the half-loops, gives rise to two edges in
\vec{E}
each being the inverse of the other.
A half-loop in
G
gives rise to a single edge in
\vec{E}
, which is its own inverse.

Claim 3
.

For any graph
G=(V,E)
with minimal degree at least two,
the uniform edge distribution
\nu_{s}
defined by
\nu_{s}(e)=1/|\vec{E}|
for all
e\in\vec{E}
is stationary for the Markov chain
\Pi_{G}
.

Proof.

\displaystyle(\nu_{s}\Pi_{G})_{f}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{e\in\vec{E}}\nu_{s}(e)\pi_{G}(e,f)=\frac{1}{|\vec{E}|}\sum_{e\in\vec{E}}\pi_{G}(e,f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\vec{E}|}\sum_{e:e\rightarrow f}\frac{1}{\outdeg(e)}=\frac{1}{|\vec{E}|}=\nu_{s}(f).

∎

The next claim is known, but we include a proof for completeness sake.

Claim 4

(
[
9
]
proposition 3.3)
.

The NBRW Markov chain on
G
as well as the non-backtracking adjacency matrix
B
are irreducible iff
G
is connected with
\mindeg(G)\geq 2
and
\maxdeg(G)>2
.

Proof.

To prove that the condition on
G
is required, assume that the NBRW on
G
is irreducible.
The condition
\mindeg(G)\geq 2
must hold since the walk is well defined.
In addition,
G
must be connected, because otherwise one can find edges
e,f\in\vec{E}
with no non-backtracking path from
e
to
f
.
It remains to exclude the possibility that
G
is connected with
\mindeg(G)=\maxdeg(G)=2
, namely that
G
is a cycle.
Indeed, in this case, for any
e\in\vec{E}
, there is no non-backtracking path from
e
to
e^{\prime}
.

To prove that the condition on
G
implies irreducibility,
we first argue that it suffices to find a path
P_{h,h^{\prime}}
from some edge

to its inverse
h^{\prime}
to imply irreducibility.
Indeed, given any pair of edges
e,f\in\vec{E}
, because
G
is connected
there is a non-backtracking path
P_{1}
connecting
e
to either
h
or
h^{\prime}
and
there is a non-backtracking path
P_{2}
connecting
h
or
h^{\prime}
to
f
.
Therefore, there is a non-backtracking path from
e
to
f
formed by concatenating
P_{1}
, possibly
P_{h,h^{\prime}}
or its inverse, and
P_{2}
.

To conclude, observe that the condition
\mindeg(G)\geq 2
implies the existence of some non-backtracking cycle
C=e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{\ell}
.
As the graph is connected with
\maxdeg(G)>2
, there must be a vertex
v\in C
with
\deg(v)>2
,
and we may assume that
t(e_{1})=h(e_{\ell})=v
, as depicted in Figure
2
.

Figure 2:
A cycle with a vertex of degree more than two.

Since
\deg(v)>2
there is some edge

with
h(e)=v
distinct from
e_{1}^{\prime}
and
e_{\ell}
. Then the path
e,e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{\ell},e^{\prime}
is a path from

to
e^{\prime}
, implying the Markov chain irreducibility.
∎

Inspired by Claim
4
we will say that a graph

is

if
G
is connected with
\mindeg(G)\geq 2
and
\maxdeg(G)>2
.

2.2
The Universal Covering Tree and its Growth Rate
\rho

Given a connected graph
G
with minimal degree at least 2 and some arbitrary vertex

,
we define its universal cover
\tilde{G}
.
It is an infinite tree, where the vertex set

is the set of all finite non-backtracking walks from

,
and where two vertices of
\tilde{G}
are adjacent if one walk extends the other by a single step.
Its root is the empty walk
\epsilon
, and it can be verified that

yields an isomorphic tree.

Definition 5
.

Given a finite connected graph
G
with minimal degree at least 2 let:

•

\mathcal{B}_{\tilde{G},r}(v)
denote the radius

v
in
\tilde{G}
,

•

\Omega_{e,\ell}
be the set of length
\ell

,

•

\Omega_{\ell}=\cup_{e\in\vec{E}}\Omega_{e,l}
be the set of all length
\ell
non-backtracking walks in
G
.

The growth rate of
\tilde{G}
, denoted
\rho(G)
, is defined by one of the following alternative definitions, given by the following lemma:

Lemma 6

(
[
4
,
11
]
)
.

Given an NB-irreducible graph
G
, the following definitions for
\rho(G)
are equivalent:

\displaystyle\rho(G)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}|\mathcal{B}_{\tilde{G},r}(v)|^{1/r}=\lim_{\ell\rightarrow\infty}|\Omega_{e,\ell}|^{1/\ell}=\lim_{\ell\rightarrow\infty}|\Omega_{\ell}|^{1/\ell}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{\ell\rightarrow\infty}\left(\nu_{s}\ B^{\ell}\ \overline{1}\right)^{1/\ell}=\rho(B),

where
B
denotes the non-backtracking adjacency matrix, and
\rho(B)
is the largest (Perron) eigenvalue of

Equality holds regardless of the choice of

and

, where applicable.

2.3
The Average Growth Rate
\Lambda
and its Connection to
\rho

Definition 7
.

The average growth rate
\Lambda(G)
is the rate predicted by the stationary distribution
\nu_{s}
of the NBRW:

\Lambda(G)=\Bigl(\prod_{e\in\vec{E}}\outdeg(e)\Bigr)^{1/{|\vec{E}|}}=\prod_{v\in V(G)}\Bigl(\deg(v)-1\Bigr)^{\deg(v)/{|\vec{E}|}}.

(2)

When no confusion may arise, we drop the graph argument and use
\rho
and
\Lambda
instead of
\rho(G)
and
\Lambda(G)
.
One should note that while the graph parameter
\Lambda
depends only on the degree distribution of the graph,
the parameter
\rho
depends on the graph structure as well.
The average growth rate
\Lambda
played a major role in
[
3
,
10
]

and corresponds to the average rate of entropy growth for the NBRW.

In order to continue, note that the NBRW on
G
, with initial state drawn from the stationary distribution
\nu_{s}
,
induces a probability measure
\mu
on the set
\Omega_{\ell}
of length
\ell

G
.
The probability of the walk
\omega=(e_{0},e_{1},\ldots,e_{\ell})\in\Omega_{\ell}
is:

\mu[\omega]=\frac{1}{|\vec{E}|}\prod_{i=0}^{\ell-1}\frac{1}{\outdeg(e_{i})}

(3)

Given a strictly positive function
\beta:\vec{E}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
and a walk
\omega=(e_{0},e_{1},\ldots,e_{\ell})\in\Omega_{\ell}
, define the random variable

X_{\beta}(\omega)=\prod_{i=0}^{\ell-1}\beta(e_{i})
.

Lemma 8
.

\mathbb{E}_{\Omega_{\ell}}[X_{\beta}]\geq\left(\prod_{e\in\vec{E}}\beta(e)\right)^{\frac{\ell}{|\vec{E}|}}

Proof.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\Omega_{\ell}}[X_{\beta}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\omega\in\Omega_{\ell}}\mu[\omega]X_{\beta}(\omega)\geq\exp\left(\mathbb{E}_{\Omega_{\ell}}[\log X_{\beta}]\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\left(\frac{\ell}{|\vec{E}|}\sum_{e\in\vec{E}}\log\beta(e)\right)=\left(\prod_{e\in\vec{E}}\beta(e)\right)^{\frac{\ell}{|\vec{E}|}},

where the inequality is Jensen’s inequality, and the equality before the last follows by linearity of expectation and the fact that the marginal distribution of
e_{i}
is
\nu_{s}
for all
i
.
∎

Lemma 9
.

For an NB-irreducible graph
G
, define the modified NBRW Markov chain
\Pi_{\beta}
by:

(\Pi_{\beta})_{e,f}=(\Pi)_{e,f}\cdot\beta(e),

then the Perron eigenvalue of

satisfies:

\rho(\Pi_{\beta})=\lim_{l\rightarrow\infty}\left(\mathbb{E}_{\Omega_{\ell}}[X_{\beta}]\right)^{\frac{1}{\ell}}\geq\left(\prod_{e\in\vec{E}}\beta(e)\right)^{\frac{1}{|\vec{E}|}}.

(4)

Proof.

\displaystyle\nu_{s}\left(\Pi_{\beta}\right)^{l}\overline{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\omega=(e_{0},e_{1},\ldots,e_{\ell})\in\Omega_{\ell}}\mu[\omega]\ \prod_{i=0}^{\ell-1}\beta(e_{i})=\mathbb{E}_{\Omega_{\ell}}[X_{\beta}].

Therefore:

\rho(\Pi_{\beta})=\lim_{\ell\rightarrow\infty}\left(\nu_{s}\ (\Pi_{\beta})^{\ell}\ \overline{1}\right)^{1/\ell}=\lim_{\ell\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\Omega_{\ell}}[X_{\beta}]^{1/\ell}.

The result follows by applying Lemma
8
to lower bound the right hand side.
∎

When plugging
\beta(e)=\outdeg(e)
, we get

.
Therefore, Lemma

yields the inequality:

Recall the random variable

Then

3
The Main Theorem

Given a non-backtracking path
P=(e_{0},e_{1},\ldots,e_{\ell-1})
in an NB-irreducible graph
G
, for some
\ell\geq 1
, we say that
P
is a
suspended path
if:

1.

\outdeg(e_{i})=1
for
i<\ell-1
and
\outdeg(e_{\ell-1})>1

2.

\indeg(e_{i})=1
for all
i>0
and
\indeg(e_{0})>1
.

The length of the path is
|P|=\ell
, we denote
\outdeg(P)=\outdeg(e_{\ell-1})
and
\indeg(P)=\indeg(e_{0})
.
We denote the set of all suspended paths of
G
by
\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{S}(G)
and note that the suspended paths form a partition of
\vec{E}
.

Definition 12
.

The
suspended path condition
holds for the graph
G
if

\outdeg(P)\cdot\indeg(P)={\Lambda(G)}^{2|P|}\mbox{ for all suspended paths }P\in\mathcal{S}.

(5)

Definition 13
.

The
cycle condition
holds for the graph
G
if

\prod_{e\in C}\outdeg(e)=\Lambda(G)^{|C|}\,\mbox{ for every non-backtracking cycle }C.

(6)

Following is our main result:

Theorem 14
.

For an NB-irreducible graph
G

the following are equivalent:

1.

\rho(G)=\Lambda(G)
.

2.

G
satisfies the suspended path condition.

3.

G
satisfies the cycle condition.

The proof of the main theorem is split into three subsections.
In Subsection
3.1
we prove that the suspended path condition implies
\rho=\Lambda
,
in Subsection
3.2
we prove that
\rho=\Lambda
implies the cycle condition,
and Subsection
3.3
completes the proof by proving that the cycle condition implies the suspended path condition.

3.1
The suspended path condition implies
\rho=\Lambda

We start the proof of Theorem
14
with the following lemma:

Lemma 15
.

If the NB-irreducible graph
G
satisfies the suspended path condition then
\rho(G)=\Lambda(G)
.

Proof.

Given a graph
G
satisfying the suspended path condition and some non-backtracking walk
\omega=(e_{0},\ldots,e_{\ell})\in\Omega_{\ell}
,
let
i_{1}<\cdots<i_{s}
be the set of indices
i<\ell
with
\outdeg(e_{i})>1
.
Then
\omega
can be expressed as the concatenation
P_{0}P_{1}\cdots P_{s}
, where
P_{j}=(e_{i_{j}+1},e_{i_{j}+2},\ldots,e_{i_{j+1}})
for
j=1,\ldots,s-1
are suspended paths,

P_{0}=(e_{0},\ldots,e_{i_{1}})
and
P_{s}=(e_{i_{s}+1},\ldots,e_{\ell})
.
Let
f(\omega)
denote the product of the edge out-degrees along
\omega
.
Then:

\displaystyle f(\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{i=0}^{\ell-1}\outdeg(e_{i})=\prod_{j=0}^{s-1}\outdeg(P_{j})

(7)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\outdeg(P_{0})^{\frac{1}{2}}\left[\prod_{j=1}^{s-1}\indeg(P_{j})^{\frac{1}{2}}\outdeg(P_{j})^{\frac{1}{2}}\right]\indeg(P_{s})^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\outdeg(P_{0})^{\frac{1}{2}}\indeg(P_{s})^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdot\Lambda(G)^{\ell-|P_{0}|-|P_{s}|}=\Theta(1)\cdot\Lambda(G)^{\ell},

where
\Theta(1)
denotes a factor that is upper and lower bounded by positive constants that depend on
G
but not on
\ell
or
\omega
.
It follows that:

\displaystyle\rho(G)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho(B)=\lim_{l\rightarrow\infty}\left(\nu_{s}B^{\ell}\overline{1}\right)^{1/\ell}=\lim_{\ell\rightarrow\infty}\left(\sum_{\omega\in\Omega_{\ell}}\mu[\omega]\ f(\omega)\right)^{1/\ell}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{\ell\rightarrow\infty}\left(\Theta(1)\cdot\sum_{\omega\in\Omega_{\ell}}\mu[\omega]\ \Lambda(G)^{\ell}\right)^{1/\ell}=\Lambda(G).

∎

The next step towards the proof of Theorem

Lemma 16
.

If an NB-irreducible graph
G

\rho(G)=\Lambda(G)

In order to prove this Lemma,
we would like to study the way the Perron eigenvalue changes from
\rho(\Pi_{G})=1
to
\rho(G)=\rho(B)

as the matrix changes gradually from

to

,
where
\Pi_{G}
denotes the transition matrix of the NBRW Markov chain and

The following theorem gives a necessary and sufficient condition for strict log-convexity of

for

,
where

is an element-wise interpolation between

Theorem 17

(Nussbaum
[

]
, Corollary 1.2)

For

is an irreducible non-negative matrix such that
(M_{t})_{i,j}=(U_{i,j})^{1-t}(V_{i,j})^{t}
for all

Then:

\rho(M_{t})\leq\rho(U)^{1-t}\rho(V)^{t},

(8)

where equality holds for some

if and only if there exists a positive diagonal matrix

U=kD^{-1}VD.

(9)

Furthermore, if (
9

) holds for all

.

Let

be the matrix defined by Theorem

and

.
Then by the following lemma, the log-convexity condition (

Lemma 18

Let

\rho(G)=\Lambda(G)
.
Then
\rho(M_{t})=\Lambda(G)^{t}
for all

.

Proof.

We first note that the matrix

in Lemma

, where

, is equal to

.
Therefore:

\rho(M_{t})=\rho(\Pi_{\beta})\geq\Lambda(G)^{t}\,\mbox{ for all }0\leq t\leq 1.

On the other hand, by (

\rho(M_{t})\leq\rho(\Pi_{G})^{1-t}\rho(B)^{t}=\Lambda(G)^{t}

where the equality holds since
\rho(\Pi_{G})=1
and

\rho(B)=\Lambda(G)
by our assumption.
∎

We can now prove Lemma
16
:

Proof of Lemma
16
.

Given an NB-irreducible graph
G
such that
\rho(G)=\Lambda(G)
, let the matrix
M_{t}
be defined as above.
Then
\rho(M_{t})=\rho(G)^{t}
for all
0\leq t\leq 1
, by Lemma
18
,
and therefore by Theorem
17
we have

\Pi_{G}=kD^{-1}BD

(10)

where
k>0
is constant, and
D
is a diagonal matrix with positive diagonal entries.
Let
\lambda=\log k
and define
\varphi:\vec{E}\to\mathbb{R}
by

\varphi(e)=\log D_{e,e}
.

For any pair of edges
e,f
such that
e\rightarrow f
, we have
B_{e,f}=1
and

\Pi_{e,f}=\outdeg(e)^{-1}=\indeg(f)^{-1}
.
Taking
\log
of the
e,f
entry of equation (
10
) yields:

-\log\outdeg(e)=\lambda-\varphi(e)+\varphi(f).

(11)

Summing equation (
11
) over all
e\rightarrow f
pairs with weight
\outdeg(e)^{-1}

yields:

-|\vec{E}|\log\Lambda=|\vec{E}|\cdot\lambda-\sum_{e}\varphi(e)+\sum_{f}\varphi(f)=|\vec{E}|\cdot\lambda

(12)

Hence,

\lambda=-\log\Lambda.

(13)

It follows that for any cycle
C=(e_{0},e_{1},\ldots,e_{\ell}=e_{0})
in
G
, the sum of (
11
)
over the edge pairs
e_{j}\to e_{j+1}
for
j=0,\ldots,\ell-1
, yields:

\sum_{e\in C}\log\outdeg(e)=|C|\log\Lambda,

(14)

where the contribution of
\varphi(f)-\varphi(e)
cancels out.
This concludes the proof of the Lemma since (

) is just the cycle condition (

).
∎

3.3
Cycle condition implies the suspended path condition

The last stage for completing the proof of Theorem
14

is to prove that the cycle condition implies the suspended path condition.
Before we continue, observe that given an NB-irreducible graph
G
, the suspended path condition is equivalent to the condition
that
g(P)=\Lambda(G)
for all suspended paths
P
,
where
g:\mathcal{S}\to\mathbb{R}
is defined by:

g(P)=\left[\outdeg(P)\cdot\indeg(P)\right]^{\frac{1}{2|P|}}.

(15)

For the proof we need two lemmas. Lemma
19
stating that
\Lambda
is a weighted geometric average of
g(P)
and Lemma
21
stating that given an edge function
f
whose value on some suspended path is smaller than the global average,
implies the existence of a cycle on which the average of
f
is strictly larger than the global average.

Lemma 19
.

For an NB-irreducible graph
G
and the function
g
defined by (
15
)
we have
\Lambda(G)=\prod_{P\in\mathcal{S}}{g(P)}^{\frac{|P|}{|\vec{E}|}}
.

Proof.

For any NB-irreducible graph
G
we have:

\displaystyle\Lambda(G)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{e\in\vec{E}}{\outdeg(e)}^{\frac{1}{|\vec{E}|}}=\prod_{P\in\mathcal{S}}{\outdeg(P)}^{\frac{1}{|\vec{E}|}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{P\in\mathcal{S}}\Bigl[\outdeg(P)\indeg(P)\Bigr]^{\frac{1}{2|\vec{E}|}}=\prod_{P\in\mathcal{S}}{g(P)}^{\frac{|P|}{|\vec{E}|}}.

∎

Remark 20
.

Lemma
19
states that
\Lambda(G)

is a weighted geometric average of
g(P)
.
Therefore the suspended path condition can be relaxed to the existence of some constant

\alpha
such that
g(P)=\alpha
for every
P\in\mathcal{S}
.
If the relaxed condition holds, then necessarily
\alpha=\Lambda(G)
.

Lemma 21
.

Let
G
be an NB-irreducible graph.
Let
\mu
be a probability measure on the edges of
G
and let
f
be some function
f:\vec{E}\to\mathbb{R}
.
Then there exists a cycle
C
in
G
with
\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[f|C]\geq\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[f]
.
Furthermore, if
G
has a suspended path
P
satisfying
\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[f|P]<\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[f]
,
then
\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[f|C]>\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[f]
.

Proof.

Given an NB-irreducible graph
G
, measure
\mu
and function
f
as above, we construct a sequence of edge subsets
E_{0}=\vec{E},E_{1},\ldots,E_{t}=C
,
so that
E_{i+1}
is a strict subset of
E_{i}
and
\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[f|E_{i+1}]\geq\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[f|E_{i}]
.
Also, we require that the edge induced sub-graph on
E_{i}
is NB-irreducible for all
i<t
and that
E_{t}
is a cycle.

At step
i
, consider the graph
G_{i}
edge induced by
E_{i}
, which we assume to be NB-irreducible.
Then, as its suspended paths
\mathcal{S}(G_{i})
form a partition of
E_{i}
, there has to be some suspended path
P_{i}\in\mathcal{S}(G_{i})
that has

\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[f|P_{i}]\leq\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[f|E_{i}]
.
Let
E^{\prime}_{i}=E_{i}\setminus P_{i}
and
G^{\prime}_{i}
be the graph induced by
E^{\prime}_{i}
.
Clearly,
\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[f|E^{\prime}_{i}]\geq\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[f|E_{i}]
and the minimal degree of
G^{\prime}_{i}
is at least two.
However,
G^{\prime}_{i}
is not necessarily connected.
We define
E_{i+1}
as the edges of a connected component of
G^{\prime}_{i}
such that
\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[f|E_{i+1}]\geq\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[f|E^{\prime}_{i}]\geq\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[f|E_{i}]
.
Therefore, with the edge set
E_{i+1}
the graph
G_{i+1}
is guaranteed to be connected with minimal degree at least two.
If
G_{i+1}
is not a cycle, then it is NB-irreducible and we may continue the process.
Otherwise
E_{i+1}
is a cycle and the process ends as required.

Finally, we show that if
\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[f|P]<\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[f]
for some suspended path
P\in\mathcal{S}(G)
then
\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[f|C]>\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[f]
.
Indeed, as in the first step, the conditional expectation on
E^{\prime}_{1}=\vec{E}\setminus P
is strictly smaller than the global average, it implies that

\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[f|E^{\prime}_{1}]>\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[f|E_{0}]
. The rest of the proof remains the same.
∎

Lemma 22
.

Let
G
be an NB-irreducible graph.
If the cycle condition holds for
G
,
then the suspended path condition holds for
G
.

Proof.

Assume that the suspended path condition (
5
) does not hold for
G
.
Then by Lemma
19
,
there must be at least one suspended path
P_{0}
in
G
such that
g(P_{0})<\Lambda(G)
.
We use Lemma
21

with
\mu
being the stationary distribution
\nu_{s}
and with the edge function defined by
f(e)=\log g(P)
where
P
is the unique suspended path with
e\in P
.
Then by Lemma
21
, there is a cycle
C
such that:

\frac{1}{|C|}\sum_{e\in C}f(e)>\frac{1}{|\vec{E}|}\sum_{e\in\vec{E}}f(e)=\frac{1}{|\vec{E}|}\sum_{P\in\mathcal{S}}|P|\log g(P)=\log\Lambda(G),

where the last step follows from Lemma
19
.
This contradicts the cycle condition and concludes the proof.
∎

4
Graphs satisfying
\rho=\Lambda

In this Section we consider some graphs and graph families with
\rho=\Lambda
.

Claim 23
.

If
G
is an NB-irreducible graph
G
with

\rho(G)=\Lambda(G)
, and

G
has no degree two vertices,
then
G
is either a regular graph, or
a bipartite bi-regular graph.

Proof.

If
G
has no degree two vertices then all suspended paths have length one.
Therefore, for any edge
e\in\vec{E}
the product
\outdeg(e)\cdot\indeg(e)
is fixed.
This implies that for any vertex
v
, all its neighbors must have the same degree.
As
G
is connected,
G
must be either regular or bipartite bi-regular.
∎

Claim 24
.

For any fixed
m
, if
G
is a graph with

\rho(G)=\Lambda(G)
, and
G^{m}
is obtained by replacing each edge
in
G
by a length
m
path, then
\rho(G^{m})=\Lambda(G^{m})=\sqrt[m]{\rho(G)}
.

We now give some other examples for graphs satisfying the suspended path condition, such as the three graphs in Figure

.
Consider the wheel with spokes graph
W_{n}
.
This graph is obtained by adding a central vertex
v_{0}
to the cycle
C_{n}
and connecting
v_{0}
to all the cycle vertices
v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}
.
The next step is to obtain the graph
W_{n,\ell_{1},\ell_{2}}

by a length

to a cycle vertex, by a length

Then the graph

has the following types of suspended paths:

•

Cycle suspended paths

of length

•

Spoke suspended paths

\rho(W_{n,\ell_{1},\ell_{2}})=\Lambda(W_{n,\ell_{1},\ell_{2}})

(16)

Setting

.

,

5
Variance of the Number of Random Bits

5.1
Variance Dichotomy for General Graphs

Consider the random variable
R_{\ell}
,
counting the number of random bits used by a length
\ell
NBRW on
G
, starting from the stationary distribution
\nu_{s}
.
Formally, for
\omega=(e_{0},e_{1},\ldots,e_{\ell})\in\Omega_{\ell}
we define
R_{\ell}(\omega)=\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-1}\log_{2}\outdeg(e_{i})
.
The following theorem states that the variance of
R_{i}
exhibits a dichotomy.

Theorem 25
.

Given an NB-irreducible graph
G
, then:

\var[R_{\ell}]=\begin{cases}O(1)&\mbox{ if }\rho=\Lambda\\
\Theta(\ell)&\mbox{ if }\rho>\Lambda.\end{cases}

We split the proof into several lemmas. In Lemma
26
we handle the case
\rho=\Lambda
.
In Lemma
27
we prove that if
\rho>\Lambda
then there are two equal length cycles that have different geometric average out-degree.
In Lemma
28
we prove the variance lower bound when
\rho>\Lambda
,
and in Lemma
30
we prove the variance upper bound when
\rho>\Lambda
.

Lemma 26
.

If
G
is an NB-irreducible graph with
\rho=\Lambda
then then there is a constant
c=c(G)
so that
\var[R_{\ell}]\leq c
for all
\ell
.

Proof.

Since
\rho=\Lambda
, Theorem
14
implies that the suspended path condition holds.
Therefore, given an NB-path
\omega=(e_{0},\ldots,e_{\ell})\in\Omega_{\ell}
, by equation (
7
) in the proof of Lemma
15
:

\left|R_{\ell}(\omega)-\ell\cdot\log_{2}\Lambda\right|=\left|\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-1}\log_{2}\outdeg(e_{i})-\ell\cdot\log_{2}\Lambda\right|\leq O(1).

(17)

Since
\mathbb{E}[R_{\ell}]=\ell\cdot\log_{2}\Lambda
, this implies that
\var[R_{\ell}]=O(1)
, as claimed.
∎

Lemma 27
.

Given an NB-irreducible graph
G
with
\rho>\Lambda
then there are two cycles
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
with
|C_{1}|=|C_{2}|
,
a common edge in
C_{1}\cap C_{2}
, but different average out-degree:

\left[\prod_{e\in C_{1}}\outdeg(e)\right]^{\dfrac{1}{|C_{1}|}}<\left[\prod_{e\in C_{2}}\outdeg(e)\right]^{\dfrac{1}{|C_{2}|}}.

Proof.

Since
\rho>\Lambda
, Theorem
14
implies that the cycle condition does not hold.
Therefore, there is a cycle
C
with
\prod_{e\in C}\outdeg(e)\neq\Lambda(G)^{|C|}
.
We only prove the claim for the case

\frac{1}{|C|}\sum_{e\in C}\log_{2}\outdeg(e)<\log_{2}\Lambda(G),

(18)

as proof for the other case is virtually identical.
Let
f
be some edge in
C
.
Then, the first step is to find a second cycle
C^{\prime}
with a strictly larger average log out-degree such that
f\in C^{\prime}
.
Indeed, since
\mathbb{E}[R_{\ell}]=\ell\log_{2}\Lambda
, then for any
\ell
, there is some specific walk
\omega=(e_{0},e_{1},\ldots,e_{\ell})\in\Omega_{\ell}
with
R_{\ell}(\omega)\geq\ell\log_{2}\Lambda
.
Therefore, since
G
is NB-irreducible, there are paths
P_{1},P_{2}
respectively from
f
to
e_{0}
and from
e_{\ell}
to
f
,
whose length is bounded by some constant
l_{0}
that depends only on
G
.
Therefore,
C^{\prime}=P_{1}\omega P_{2}
is a cycle with
f\in C^{\prime}
.
Furthermore,

\frac{1}{|C^{\prime}|}\sum_{e\in C^{\prime}}\log_{2}\outdeg(e)\geq\frac{1}{\ell+2l_{0}}R_{\ell}(\omega)\geq\frac{\ell}{\ell+2l_{0}}\log_{2}\Lambda.

By taking a sufficiently large
\ell
, one can ensure that:

\frac{1}{|C|}\sum_{e\in C}\log_{2}\outdeg(e)<\frac{1}{|C^{\prime}|}\sum_{e\in C^{\prime}}\log_{2}\outdeg(e).

(19)

We end the construction by looping through
C
and
C^{\prime}
the required amount of times to obtain the cycles
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
, whose length is the LCM of
|C|
and
|C^{\prime}|
,
meeting the requirements of the lemma.
∎

Lemma 28
.

If
G
is an NB-irreducible graph with
\rho>\Lambda
then there is a constant
c=c(G)
so that
\var[R_{\ell}]\geq c\cdot\ell
for all
\ell
.

Remark 29
.

In the proof of Lemma
30
we derive an exact formula (
23
) for
\lim_{\ell\to\infty}\var[R_{\ell}]/\ell
.
As it is unclear how to directly prove this limit is non-zero if
\rho>\Lambda
, we resort to cruder techniques in the following proof.

Proof of Lemma
28
.

Given an NB-irreducible graph
G
with
\rho>\Lambda
,
by Lemma
27

there are two cycles
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
of the same length
l_{0}=|C_{1}|=|C_{2}|
,
with different average out-degree, and with some edge
f
in their intersection.
Recall that:

\var[R_{\ell}]=\mathbb{E}_{\omega\in\Omega_{\ell}}\left[{(R_{\ell}(\omega)-\ell\log_{2}\Lambda)}^{2}\right].

In order to lower-bound the variance, we use the exposure method.
We expose some of the edges of
\omega=(e_{0},e_{1},\ldots,e_{\ell})

and argue that the conditional expectation of
{(R_{\ell}(\omega)-\ell\log_{2}\Lambda)}^{2}
given the exposed edges is
\Omega(\ell)
with high probability.
Define the mapping
\phi:\vec{E}\to\mathbb{N}
, where
\phi(e)
is the length of the shortest NB-path from
e
to
f
if
e\neq f
and
\phi(f)=\ell_{0}
.
Also, let
\ell_{\max}
be the maximum of
\phi(e)
on
e\in\vec{E}
.
We build the partial exposure mapping
\chi
from
\{0,\ldots,\ell\}
to
\vec{E}
by the following randomized procedure:

\chi\leftarrow
the empty mapping

i\leftarrow 0

while

i<\ell

do

Expose
e_{i}
and set
\chi(i)\leftarrow e_{i}

i\leftarrow i+\phi(e_{i})

end

while

Expose
e_{\ell}
and set
\chi(\ell)\leftarrow e_{\ell}

The domain of
\chi
is the set of exposed edge indices,
i_{1}=0<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{k}=\ell
.
Therefore, conditioned on
\chi
, one may write
R_{\ell}
as the sum of independent random variables:

R_{\ell}=\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-1}\log_{2}\outdeg(e_{i})=\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}R(i_{j+1}-i_{j},e_{i_{j}},e_{i_{j+1}}),

where
R(\ell^{\prime},e^{\prime},e^{\prime\prime})
is the random variable counting the number of random bits used by a length
\ell^{\prime}
NBRW, conditioned on it beginning at
e^{\prime}
and ending at
e^{\prime\prime}
.
Therefore:

\var[R_{\ell}|\chi]=\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\var\!\left[R(i_{j+1}-i_{j},e_{i_{j}},e_{i_{j+1}})\right]\geq|I|\cdot\var\!\left[R(\ell_{0},f,f)\right],

where
I=I(\chi)=\{i\in\dom(\chi):\,i+\ell_{0}\in\dom(\chi)\mbox{ and }\chi(i)=\chi(i+\ell_{0})=f\}
.
As the number of exposed edges is lower bounded by
\ell/\ell_{\max}=\Omega(\ell)
,
it follows that
|I|
is lower bounded by a binomial random variable
X\sim B(\ell/(2\ell_{\max}),p_{\min})
,
where
p_{\min}
is a positive constant defined as the minimum of
(\Pi^{\phi(e)})_{e,f}\cdot(\Pi^{\ell_{0}})_{f,f}
on
e\in\vec{E}
.
Therefore,
\mathbb{E}[|I|]\geq\mathbb{E}[X]=c_{1}\ell
, where
c_{1}=p_{\min}/(2\ell_{\max})
.
The Chernoff bound on the lower tail of
X
yields
\Pr[|I|\leq c_{1}\ell/2]\leq\Pr[X\leq c_{1}\ell/2]\leq\exp[-c_{1}\ell/2]
, which is
o(1)
as
\ell
grows.

Recall that by our assumption on the cycles
C_{1},C_{2}
, the variance of
R(\ell_{0},f,f)
is some positive constant
c_{2}
.
Therefore:

\displaystyle\var[R_{\ell}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\!\left[{(R_{\ell}(\omega)-\ell\log_{2}\Lambda)}^{2}\right]=\sum_{\chi}\mathbb{E}\!\left[{(R_{\ell}(\omega)-\ell\log_{2}\Lambda)}^{2}\Big|\chi\right]\cdot\Pr[\chi]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{\chi}|I(\chi)|\cdot\var\!\left[R(\ell_{0},f,f)\right]\cdot\Pr[\chi]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{c_{1}\ell}{2}\cdot\var\!\left[R(\ell_{0},f,f)\right]\cdot\Pr\!\left[|I|\geq\frac{c_{1}\ell}{2}\right]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{c_{1}c_{2}\ell}{2}\cdot\Pr\!\left[|I|\geq\frac{c_{1}\ell}{2}\right]=\Omega(\ell).

∎

Lemma 30
.

If
G
is an NB-irreducible graph then there is a constant
c=c(G)
so that
\var[R_{\ell}]\leq c\cdot\ell
for all
\ell
.

Proof.

We define
R^{\prime}_{\ell}=R_{\ell}-\ell\log_{2}\Lambda=\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-1}R^{\prime}_{\ell,i}
,
where
R^{\prime}_{\ell,i}
are dependent, zero mean, identically distributed random variables with
R^{\prime}_{\ell,i}(\omega)=\log_{2}\outdeg(e_{i})-\log_{2}\Lambda
.
Therefore:

\displaystyle\var[R_{\ell}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\var[R^{\prime}_{\ell}]=\mathbb{E}\!\left[{(R^{\prime}_{\ell})}^{2}\right]=\sum_{i,j=0}^{\ell-1}\mathbb{E}\!\left[R^{\prime}_{\ell,i}R^{\prime}_{\ell,j}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ell\cdot\mathbb{E}\!\left[{(R^{\prime}_{\ell,0})}^{2}\right]+2\sum_{\Delta=1}^{\ell-1}(\ell-\Delta)\cdot\mathbb{E}\!\left[R^{\prime}_{\ell,0}R^{\prime}_{\ell,\Delta}\right],

where in the last step we use the fact that the NBRW starting from the stationary distribution

\nu_{s}
is time-invariant.

Let
f
denote a column vector where
f_{e}=\log_{2}\outdeg(e)-\log_{2}\Lambda
and recall that
\Pi=\Pi_{G}
is the NBRW transition matrix.
Then:

\frac{\var[R_{\ell}]}{\ell}=\frac{1}{|\vec{E}|}\cdot f^{T}f+\frac{2}{|\vec{E}|}\sum_{\Delta=1}^{\ell-1}(1-\frac{\Delta}{\ell})\cdot f^{T}\Pi^{\Delta}f.

(20)

Let
J
be the Jordan normal form of
\Pi
, written as the direct sum of its blocks
J=J_{1}\oplus J_{2}\ldots\oplus J_{k}
,
where block
J_{i}
corresponds to the eigenvalue
\lambda_{i}
.
Assume further that
\lambda_{1}=1\geq|\lambda_{2}|\geq\cdots\geq|\lambda_{k}|
and that
\Pi=Q^{-1}JQ
.
Then:

\displaystyle\frac{\var[R_{\ell}]}{\ell}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{|\vec{E}|}\cdot f^{T}f+\frac{2}{|\vec{E}|}f^{T}Q^{-1}\left(\sum_{\Delta=0}^{\ell}(1-\frac{\Delta}{\ell})\cdot J^{\Delta}\right)Qf

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{|\vec{E}|}\cdot f^{T}f+\frac{2}{|\vec{E}|}f^{T}Q^{-1}\left[\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}\left(\sum_{\Delta=0}^{\ell}(1-\frac{\Delta}{\ell})\cdot J_{i}^{\Delta}\right)\right]Qf

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{|\vec{E}|}\cdot f^{T}f+\frac{2}{|\vec{E}|}f^{T}Q^{-1}\left[0\oplus\bigoplus_{i=2}^{k}\left(\sum_{\Delta=0}^{\ell}(1-\frac{\Delta}{\ell})\cdot J_{i}^{\Delta}\right)\right]Qf,

where in the last equality we can replace
J_{1}
by the scalar zero, since
f^{T}Q^{-1}
is orthogonal to the all one right Perron eigenvector.
The next step is to employ the following identity, holding for any matrix
A
provided that
I-A
is invertible:

\sum_{\Delta=0}^{\ell}(1-\frac{\Delta}{\ell})\cdot A^{\Delta}=\frac{1}{\ell}\cdot(A^{\ell+1}-A)(I-A)^{-2}+(I-A)^{-1}.

(21)

As
I-J_{i}
is invertible for all
i>1
, it follows that the normalized variance can be written as:

\displaystyle\frac{\var[R_{\ell}]}{\ell}=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{|\vec{E}|}\cdot f^{T}f+

(22)

\displaystyle\frac{2}{|\vec{E}|}f^{T}Q^{-1}\left[0\oplus\bigoplus_{i=2}^{k}\left(\frac{1}{\ell}\cdot(J_{i}^{\ell+1}-J_{i})(I-J_{i})^{-2}+(I-J_{i})^{-1}\right)\right]Qf.

The matrices
J_{i}^{\ell+1}
are upper triangular Toeplitz,
where
(J_{i}^{\ell+1})_{u,v}=\lambda_{i}^{\ell+1-v+u}\binom{\ell+1}{v-u}
for
u\leq v
.
Therefore, if
|\lambda_{i}|<1
, all entries of the matrix
J_{i}^{\ell+1}
are bounded in absolute value by some constant that is a function of
|\vec{E}|
only.
Otherwise, if
|\lambda_{i}|=1
, then necessarily
\lambda_{i}
has algebraic multiplicity of one (see
[
12
]
, Corollary 8.4.6), so
J_{i}
is a scalar and
|J_{i}^{\ell+1}|=1
.
It follows that the normalized variance converges when
\ell\to\infty
:

\lim_{\ell\to\infty}\frac{\var[R_{\ell}]}{\ell}=-\frac{1}{|\vec{E}|}\cdot f^{T}f+\frac{2}{|\vec{E}|}f^{T}Q^{-1}\left[0\oplus\bigoplus_{i=2}^{k}(I-J_{i})^{-1}\right]Qf.

(23)

As the rhs does not depend on
\ell
this concludes the proof.
∎

Consider the graph of

Minus an edge, depicted in Figure

.
The graph has 10 directed edges, where
\outdeg(e)
is two on six of the edges and one on the remaining four edges,
implying that the NBRW consumes 0.6 bits per step on average, and that
\Lambda=2^{6/10}\approx 1.5157
.
The value of
\rho
is the Perron eigenvalue of the 10 by 10 non-backtracking adjacency matrix
B
as stated in Lemma
6
,
which can be reduced to the following 3 by 3 matrix as there are only three edge types,
(u_{.},u_{.}),(v_{.},u_{.})
and
(u_{.},v_{.})
:

\rho(B)=\rho\left(\left[\begin{tabular}[]{ccc}0&0&1\\
2&0&0\\
1&1&0\end{tabular}\right]\right)\approx 1.5214

Therefore,
\rho>\Lambda
for this graph, implying by Theorem
14
, that the suspended path condition as well as the cycle condition are violated.
Indeed, the suspended path condition is violated as the length one suspended path
P=[(u_{1},u_{2})]
has

\left(\outdeg(P)\cdot\indeg(P)\right)^{\frac{1}{2|P|}}=(2\cdot 2)^{\frac{1}{2}}=2\neq\Lambda.

The length three cycle
C=[(u_{1},u_{2}),(u_{2},v_{1}),(v_{1},u_{1})]
violates the cycle condition as:

\left(\prod_{e\in C}\outdeg(e)\right)^{\frac{1}{|C|}}=(2\cdot 1\cdot 2)^{\frac{1}{3}}=2^{\frac{2}{3}}\neq\Lambda=2^{\frac{3}{5}}.

, in agreement with the exact limit computed in Lemma
31
below.

,

Lemma 31
.

Given the probability space
\Omega_{\ell}
of the length
\ell
NBRW on
K_{4}
minus an edge, starting from the stationary distribution, the random variable

R_{\ell}(\omega)=\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-1}\log_{2}\outdeg(e_{i})
, for
\omega=(e_{0},\ldots,e_{\ell})\in\Omega_{\ell}
satisfies:

\lim_{l\to\infty}\frac{\var[R_{\ell}]}{\ell}=\frac{2}{125}

(24)

Proof.

As in Lemma
30
, we define
R^{\prime}_{\ell}=\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-1}R^{\prime}_{\ell,i}
,
where
R^{\prime}_{\ell,i}
are random variables with
R^{\prime}_{\ell,i}(\omega)=\log_{2}\outdeg(e_{i})-\log_{2}\Lambda
.
Therefore:

\frac{\var[R_{\ell}]}{\ell}=-\mathbb{E}\!\left[{(R^{\prime}_{\ell,0})}^{2}\right]+2\sum_{\Delta=0}^{\ell}(1-\frac{\Delta}{\ell})\cdot\mathbb{E}\!\left[R^{\prime}_{\ell,0}R^{\prime}_{\ell,\Delta}\right].

In order to avoid 10x10 matrices, we exploit the fact that
K_{4}
minus an edge has only three edge types.
Then, the stationary distribution, the NBRW transition matrix, and the vector
f
with
f_{e}=\log_{2}\outdeg(e)-\log_{2}\Lambda
,
with row and column order of:
uu
,
vu
and
uv
, are as follows:

\nu_{s}=\begin{pmatrix}1/5\\
2/5\\
2/5\end{pmatrix},\,\Pi=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&1\\
1/2&0&1/2\\
0&1&0\end{pmatrix},\,f=\begin{pmatrix}2/5\\
2/5\\
-3/5\end{pmatrix}.

Using
N_{s}
to denote the matrix with diagonal
\nu_{s}
we write:

\frac{\var[R_{\ell}]}{\ell}=-f^{T}N_{s}f+2\sum_{\Delta=0}^{\ell}(1-\frac{\Delta}{\ell})\cdot f^{T}N_{s}\Pi^{\Delta}f.

(25)

We next observe that the matrix
\Pi
is diagonalizable,
\Pi=Q^{-1}DQ
, with:

Q=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{2}&1&1\\
i&-1-i&1\\
-i&-1+i&1\end{pmatrix},\,D=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\
0&-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{i}{2}&0\\
0&0&-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{i}{2}\end{pmatrix},

where the first row of
Q
is the left Perron eigenvector, equal to
\nu_{s}
up to scaling.
In addition, the right Perron eigenvector, which is the first column of
Q^{-1}
, is necessarily the all one vector up to scaling.
Therefore,
(f^{T}N_{s}Q^{-1})_{1}=0
, implying that
f^{T}N_{s}Q^{-1}D=f^{T}N_{s}Q^{-1}E
, where
E
is the matrix obtained by zeroing the first diagonal entry of
D
.
Plugging this into the variance expression yields:

\displaystyle\frac{\var[R_{\ell}]}{\ell}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-f^{T}N_{s}f+2\sum_{\Delta=0}^{\ell}(1-\frac{\Delta}{\ell})\cdot f^{T}N_{s}Q^{-1}D^{\Delta}Qf

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-f^{T}N_{s}f+2f^{T}N_{s}Q^{-1}\left(\sum_{\Delta=0}^{\ell}(1-\frac{\Delta}{\ell})\cdot E^{\Delta}\right)Qf.

Using (
21
), the normalized variance expression converges to:

\displaystyle\lim_{\ell\to\infty}\frac{\var[R_{\ell}]}{\ell}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-f^{T}N_{s}f+2f^{T}N_{s}Q^{-1}\left(\lim_{\ell\to\infty}\sum_{\Delta=0}^{\ell}\frac{\ell-\Delta}{\ell}\cdot E^{\Delta}\right)Qf

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-f^{T}N_{s}f+2f^{T}N_{s}Q^{-1}(I-E)^{-1}Qf.

Plugging in the numbers, yields
2/125
on the right hand side, as claimed.
∎

6
Observations and Open Questions

1.

Is there a quantitative version of our main result of Theorem
14
?
Something along the lines of:
a graph is
\epsilon
-close to satisfying the suspended path condition if and only if
\rho\leq\Lambda+\delta(\epsilon)
. It seems relatively easy to prove that being close to satisfying the suspended path condition implies that
\rho
is close to
\Lambda
. However, the other direction is not obvious, if true at all.

2.

What is the minimal possible value
\rho
may have, given the degree distribution.
(The maximal value of
\rho
seems to be as close as we wish to the maximal degree minus one, in the degree distribution support.)

3.

Specifically, what is the minimal possible value of
\rho
, for a graph with half the vertices being of degree two and the other half of degree three.
We conjecture that the minimum is
\rho(K_{4}\mbox{ minus an edge})
.

4.

Prove that any girth
g
graph, with half its vertices being of degree two and the other half of degree three, has

g\leq(2+o(1))\log_{\rho}n
, where
\rho=\rho(K_{4}\mbox{ minus an edge})
.

5.

Can one directly see why violation of the suspended path condition necessary implies that the limit of
\var[R_{\ell}]/\ell
is strictly positive in (
23
)?

6.

What is the minimal possible value of
\lim_{\ell\to\infty}\var[R_{\ell}]/\ell
for a graph
G
with for a given degree distribution?
Is the minimum obtained on the same graphs that minimize
\rho
?

7.

Is there an NB-irreducible graph
G
for which the NBRW transition matrix
\Pi_{G}
is not diagonalizable?

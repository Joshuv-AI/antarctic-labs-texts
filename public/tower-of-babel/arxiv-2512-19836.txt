Ball-convex bodies and L p L_{p} relative surface areas
Elisabeth M. Werner Diliya Yalikun

Abstract
We define new surface area measures for ball-convex bodies which we call L p L_{p} relative surface areas. We show that those are rigid motion invariant valuations. We establish inequalities for these quantities and prove a monotonicity behavior which leads to a new notion of entropy for ball-convex bodies. We introduce a weighted ball floating body. A derivative of volume of a ball-convex body with a weighted ball floating body provides a geometric interpretation of the L p L_{p} relative surface areas.



Keywords.
ball-convex bodies,
L_{p}
affine surface areas,
relative surface areas, entropy,
floating bodies

Introduction

A convex body in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a compact, convex subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with nonempty
interior. Convex bodies
K
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
of the form

K=\bigcap_{K\subset R\,B^{n}_{2}+x}R\,B^{n}_{2}+x,

(1)

where

is a positive real number and
B^{n}_{2}
is the Euclidean unit ball, are called
R
-ball convex.
We denote the class of such bodies by
\mathcal{K}_{R}
.
This class has been introduced and investigated in
[
19
,
38
]
and intensively studied, e.g.,
[
5
,
15
,
16
,
17
,
36
,

.
There are connections of this class to optimal transport, see
[
2
]
, to the Kneser-Poulsen conjecture (see
[
14
]
), to Blaschke’s rolling theorem
[
22
]
, to Meissner polyhedra
[
34
]
and bodies of constant width
[

,
42
]
, and isoperimetric problems, e.g.,
[
23
]
.
A classification of the isometries on ball-convex bodies was given in
[
3
,
4
]
.
In particular, in
[
55
]
a notion of a floating body has been introduced for this class, namely, the
R
-ball floating body
of
K
,

K_{\delta}^{R}=\bigcap_{\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K\setminus R\,B^{n}_{2}+x)\leq\delta}R\,B^{n}_{2}+x,

(2)

where the Euclidean balls
R\,B^{n}_{2}+x
replace the hyperplanes
H
of the definition of the (classical) floating body,

K_{\delta}=\bigcap_{\mathrm{vol}_{n}(H^{-}\cap K)\leq\delta}H^{+}

[

,

and

are the two closed halfspaces
determined by the hyperplane

.
Note that when

It was also shown in
[

]
that a right derivative of volume of an
R
-ball convex body with its

-ball floating body exists,

\lim_{\delta\to 0}\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{n}\left(K\right)-\mathrm{vol}_{n}\left(K_{\delta}^{R}\right)}{\delta^{\frac{2}{n+1}}}=\alpha_{n}\int_{\partial K}\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\left(\kappa_{i}(K,x)-\frac{1}{R}\right)^{\frac{1}{n+1}}d\mu_{K}(x),

is a constant depending on

is the usual surface area measure on the boundary

The corresponding result with the classical floating body was proved in

as(K)=\int_{\partial K}\kappa(K,x)^{\frac{1}{n+1}}d\mu_{K}(x).

(4)

As

4
) which motivated us to call the right hand side of (
3
)
relative affine surface area
. Note though that it is not an affine invariant.

Results

We now mention some of the results of the paper.

We denote by

\mathcal{K}_{R}^{+}
the set of all
R
-ball convex bodies in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

such that for all
x\in\partial K
,
\kappa_{i}(K,x)>\frac{1}{R}
, for all
1\leq i\leq n-1
.

The first theorem leads to our definition of
L_{p}
relative surface areas.
We apply a method that was successful not only in the classical case
[
43
]
, but also in spherical and hyperbolic space
[
9
,
10
]
,
namely we compose the floating body operation with polarity.

Theorem 1
.

Let
K\in\mathcal{K}_{R}^{+}
and let
K_{\delta}^{R}
be its
R
-ball floating body. Then

\lim_{\delta\to 0}\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{n}\left((K_{\delta}^{R})^{\circ}\right)-vol_{n}\left((K)^{\circ}\right)}{\delta^{\frac{2}{n+1}}}=c_{n}\int_{\partial K}\frac{\kappa(K,x)}{\langle x,N(x)\rangle^{n+1}}\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\left(\kappa_{i}(K,x)-\frac{1}{R}\right)^{\frac{1}{n+1}}d\mu_{K}(x),

where
c_{n}=\frac{n^{2}(n^{2}-1)^{\frac{2}{n+1}}}{2\,(\sigma(S^{n-2}))^{\frac{2}{n+1}}}
.

Note that if
R\to\infty
, we obtain on the right hand side the
-n/(n+2)
-affine surface area
[
40
,
43
,
53
]
.
This leads naturally to calling the integral expression the
-n/(n+2)
-relative surface area
and thus to define for all
p\neq-n

the
L_{p}
-relative surface areas
of
R
-ball convex bodies
K
as

\Omega_{p}^{R}(K)=\int_{\partial K}\Big(\frac{\kappa(K,x)^{\frac{1}{n+1}}}{\langle x,N(x)\rangle}\Big)^{\frac{n(p-1)}{n+p}}\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\left(\kappa_{i}(K,x)-\frac{1}{R}\right)^{\frac{1}{n+1}}d\mu_{K}(x).

For
R\to\infty
, we recover the
L_{p}
-affine surface areas, defined by Lutwak
[
40
]
(see also
[
32
,
53
]
),

as_{p}(K)=\int_{\partial K}\frac{\kappa(K,x)^{\frac{p}{n+p}}}{\langle x,N(x)\rangle^{\frac{n(p-1)}{n+p}}}d\mu_{K}(x).

We show inequalities for the
L_{p}
relative surface areas,
among them are those in Theorem
4
.

Theorem 4.

Let
s>-n
,
r>-n
,
t>-n
, be real numbers. Let
K
be an
R
-ball convex body in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
0\in\text{int}(K)

and
\mu_{K}(\{x\in\partial K:\kappa_{i}(K,x)>\frac{1}{R},\,\text{for all }i,1\leq i\leq n-1\})>0
.

(i) If
1<\frac{(n+r)(t-s)}{(n+t)(r-s)}<\infty
, then

\Omega_{r}^{R}(K)\leq(\Omega_{t}^{R}(K))^{\frac{(r-s)(n+t)}{(t-s)(n+r)}}(\Omega_{s}^{R}(K))^{\frac{(t-r)(n+s)}{(t-s)(n+r)}}.

(ii) If
\frac{(n+r)t}{(n+t)r}>1
, then

\left(\frac{\Omega^{R}_{r}(K)}{\Omega_{0}^{R}(K)}\right)\leq\left(\frac{\Omega^{R}_{t}(K)}{\Omega_{0}^{R}(K)}\right)^{\frac{r(n+t)}{t(n+r)}}.

Equality holds in both inequalities if and only if
K
is an ellipsoid.

From the inequalities of Theorem
4
we deduce a monotonicity behavior in
p
of
\left(\frac{\Omega_{p}^{R}(K)}{\Omega^{R}_{\infty}(K)}\right)^{n+p}

which leads to the existence of the limit

{E}^{R}(K)=\lim_{p\rightarrow\infty}\left(\frac{\Omega^{R}_{p}(K)}{\Omega^{R}_{\infty}(K)}\right)^{n+p}.

{E}^{R}(K)
is an entropy power and related to the Kullback Leibler divergence
D_{KL}(P||Q)
of certain probability measures
P
and
Q
, see Section
4
. This is shown in
Proposition
8
.

Proposition 8.

Let
K
be a
R
-ball convex body such that
0\in\text{int}(K)

and
\mu_{K}(\{x\in\partial K:\kappa_{i}(K,x)>\frac{1}{R},\,\text{for all }i,1\leq i\leq n-1\})>0
. Then

D_{KL}(P\|Q)=\log\left(\frac{\Omega^{R}_{0}(K)}{\Omega^{R}_{\infty}(K)}\,{E}^{R}(K)^{-\frac{1}{n}}\right).

Finally, in Definition
3
, we introduce
weighted
R
-ball floating bodies

F_{R}(K,f,\delta)
where a set of volume
\delta
weighted with a function
f
is cut from an
R
-ball convex body.
Volume derivatives then lead to a weighted relative surface area of Theorem
10
. As a corollary (Corollary

) we obtain a geometric characterization of the
L_{p}
relative surface areas.

Theorem 10.

Let
K
be a
R
-ball convex body in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let
f:K\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be an continuous function such that
f\geq c
on
K
where
c>0
is a constant. Then

\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K)-\mathrm{vol}_{n}(F_{R}(K,f,\delta))}{\delta^{\frac{2}{n+1}}}=c_{n}\int_{\partial K}(f(x))^{-\frac{2}{n+1}}\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\left(\kappa_{i}(K,x)-\frac{1}{R}\right)^{\frac{1}{n+1}}d\mu_{K}(x),

where
c_{n}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{n+1}{\mathrm{vol}_{n-1}(B^{n-1}_{2})}\right)^{\frac{2}{n+1}}
.

3
L_{p}
-relative surface areas

Definition 1
.

Let
K\in\mathcal{K}_{R}
. Let
-\infty\leq p\leq\infty
,
p\neq-n
. Then the
L_{p}
-relative surface area of
K
is

\Omega_{p}^{R}(K)=\int_{\partial K}\Big(\frac{\kappa(K,x)^{\frac{1}{n+1}}}{\langle x,N(x)\rangle}\Big)^{\frac{n(p-1)}{n+p}}\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\left(\kappa_{i}(K,x)-\frac{1}{R}\right)^{\frac{1}{n+1}}d\mu_{K}(x).

When
K
is sufficiently smooth,
\Omega_{p}^{R}
can be written as

\Omega_{p}^{R}(K)=\int_{S^{n-1}}\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\left(\frac{1}{r_{i}(K,u)}-\frac{1}{R}\right)^{\frac{1}{n+1}}\frac{f_{K}(u)^{\frac{n^{2}+2n+p}{(n+1)(n+p)}}}{h_{K}(u)^{\frac{n(p-1)}{n+p}}}d\sigma(u),

(5)

where, for
u\in S^{n-1}
such that
N_{K}(x)=u
,
r_{i}(K,u)=(\kappa_{i}(K,N_{K}^{-1}(u))^{-1}
,
f_{K}=\kappa^{-1}
is the curvature function and
\sigma
is the surface
area measure of the sphere.

, we obtain the

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial K}\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\left(\kappa_{i}(K,x)-\frac{1}{R}\right)^{\frac{1}{n+1}}d\mu_{K}(x).

,

is

-ball convex and then we have for all

(6)

Next, we look at the case of an ellipsoid

. We first recall a theorem by Petty

]

.

[

Thus we get for all

\Omega_{p}^{R}(\mathcal{E})=k^{\frac{n(p-1)}{n+p}}\,\Omega_{1}^{R}(\mathcal{E}),

and in particular

\Omega_{\pm\infty}^{R}(\mathcal{E})=k^{n}\,\Omega_{1}^{R}(\mathcal{E}),\hskip 34.1433pt\Omega_{0}^{R}(\mathcal{E})=k^{-1}\,\Omega_{1}^{R}(\mathcal{E}).

(8)

3. Note that

\Omega_{p}^{R}(K)=\int_{\partial K}\frac{\kappa(K,x)^{\frac{p}{n+p}}}{\langle x,N(x)\rangle^{\frac{n(p-1)}{n+p}}}\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\left(1-\frac{1}{R\,\kappa_{i}(K,x)}\right)^{\frac{1}{n+1}}d\mu_{K}(x).

For

-affine surface area

,

]
.

In particular, for

\displaystyle as^{R}(K)=\Omega_{1}^{R}(K)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial K}\kappa(K,x)^{\frac{1}{n+1}}\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\left(1-\frac{1}{R\,\kappa_{i}(K,x)}\right)^{\frac{1}{n+1}}d\mu_{K}(x),

(10)

\displaystyle\Omega_{0}^{R}(K)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial K}\langle x,N(x)\,\rangle\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\left(1-\frac{1}{R\,\kappa_{i}(K,x)}\right)^{\frac{1}{n+1}}d\mu_{K}(x)

and

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial K}\frac{\kappa(K,x)}{\langle x,N(x)\,\rangle^{n}}\,\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\left(1-\frac{1}{R\,\kappa_{i}(K,x)}\right)^{\frac{1}{n+1}}d\mu_{K}(x).

Therefore

can be viewed as a weighted

-“affine” surface

In particular, as

.

3.1
Boundedness Property of the
L_{p}
-relative surface areas

It was shown in
[
40
]
(
p>1
;
p=1
is the classical case going back to Blaschke
[
18
]
), in
[
32
]
(
0<p<1
) and for all
p

in
[
62
]
that for a convex body
K
with
0\in\text{int}(K)
,

\displaystyle\text{If}\,\,p\geq 0:\,\frac{as_{p}(K)}{as_{p}(B^{n}_{2})}\leq\left(\frac{|K|}{|B^{n}_{2}|}\right)^{\frac{n-p}{n+p}};\hskip 14.22636pt\text{If}-n<p<0:\,\frac{as_{p}(K)}{as_{p}(B^{n}_{2})}\geq\left(\frac{|K|}{|B^{n}_{2}|}\right)^{\frac{n-p}{n+p}}.

(14)

Equality holds in both cases if and only if
K
is an ellipsoid.
For
p=0
, equality holds trivially for all
K
.
If
K
is in addition in
C^{2}_{+}
and if
p<-n
, then for an absolute constant
\gamma>0
,

\gamma^{\frac{np}{n+p}}\left(\frac{|K|}{|B^{n}_{2}|}\right)^{\frac{n-p}{n+p}}\leq\frac{as_{p}(K)}{as_{p}(B^{n}_{2})}.

For an
R
-ball convex body we have that
\kappa_{i}(K,x)\geq\frac{1}{R}
for all
i
, for all
x
.
Therefore,
\kappa(K,x)\geq\frac{1}{R^{n-1}}
for all
x\in\partial K
. Moreover

\langle x,N(x)\rangle\leq R
and thus, together with (
14
), we get that

\displaystyle 0\leq\left(\frac{1}{R}\right)^{\frac{n^{2}(p-1)}{(n+1)(n+p)}}as_{1}^{R}(K)\leq\Omega_{p}^{R}(K)\leq

\displaystyle n|B^{n}_{2}|^{\frac{2p}{n+p}}|K|^{\frac{n-p}{n+p}},\hskip 14.22636pt1\leq p\leq\infty

\displaystyle 0\leq\Omega_{p}^{R}(K)\leq

\displaystyle n|B^{n}_{2}|^{\frac{2p}{n+p}}|K|^{\frac{n-p}{n+p}},\hskip 14.22636pt0\leq p\leq 1

0\leq\Omega_{p}^{R}(K)\leq\left(\frac{1}{R}\right)^{n\frac{(n-1)(p-1)}{n+1)(n+p)}}\Omega_{1}^{R}(K),\hskip 14.22636pt-n<p\leq 1.

Thus the
\Omega_{p}^{R}(K)<\infty
for
-n<p\leq\infty
. In the range
-\infty\leq p<-n
we get

\displaystyle 0\leq\left(\frac{1}{R}\right)^{\frac{n^{2}(p-1)}{n+1)(n+p)}}\Omega_{1}^{R}(K)\leq\Omega_{p}^{R}(K)\leq as_{p}(K),\hskip 14.22636pt-\infty\leq p<-n.

It then can happen that
\Omega_{p}^{R}(K)
is unbounded. An example is

, for
1<r<2
. Then
B^{2}_{r}
is

-ball convex for

but
\Omega_{p}^{R}(B^{2}_{r})
is unbounded when

, and similarly for higher dimensions.

3.2
Homogeneity and Valuation Property

It is obvious that
L_{p}
-relative affine surface area is invariant under rigid motions. The next proposition states some more properties.

Proposition 3
.

Let
K
be an
R
-ball convex body and let
-\infty\leq p\leq\infty
,
p\neq-n
.

(i) Let
a\in\mathbb{R}
,
a>0
. Then
\Omega_{p}^{R}
is homogeneous of degree
n\frac{n-p}{n+p}
,

\Omega_{p}^{aR}(aK)=a^{n\frac{n-p}{n+p}}\,\Omega_{p}^{R}(K).

(ii)
For
0\leq p\leq\infty
, we have the following isoperimetric inequalities

\displaystyle\Omega_{p}^{R}(K)\leq as_{p}(K)\leq n\,\mathrm{vol}_{n}(B^{n}_{2})^{\frac{2p}{n+p}}\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K)^{\frac{n-p}{n+p}}.

Equality holds in the first inequality iff
R=\infty
and in the second inequality iff
K
is an ellipsoid.

(iii) The
L_{p}
-relative affine surface area
\Omega_{p}^{R}
is a valuation, i.e. for
R
-ball convex bodies
K
and
L
such that
K\cap L
and
K\cup L
are again
R
-ball convex,

\Omega_{p}^{R}(K\cup L)+\Omega_{p}^{R}(K\cap L)=\Omega_{p}^{R}(K)+\Omega_{p}^{R}(L).

3.3
Inequalities for
L_{p}
-relative surface areas

Theorem 4
.

Let
s>-n
,
r>-n
,
t>-n
, be real numbers. Let
K
be an
R
-ball convex body in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
0\in\text{int}(K)

and
\mu_{K}(\{x\in\partial K:\kappa_{i}(K,x)>\frac{1}{R},\,\text{for all }i,1\leq i\leq n-1\})>0
.

(i) If
1<\frac{(n+r)(t-s)}{(n+t)(r-s)}<\infty
, then

\Omega_{r}^{R}(K)\leq(\Omega_{t}^{R}(K))^{\frac{(r-s)(n+t)}{(t-s)(n+r)}}(\Omega_{s}^{R}(K))^{\frac{(t-r)(n+s)}{(t-s)(n+r)}}.

(ii) If
\frac{(n+r)t}{(n+t)r}>1
, then

\left(\frac{\Omega^{R}_{r}(K)}{\Omega_{0}^{R}(K)}\right)\leq\left(\frac{\Omega^{R}_{t}(K)}{\Omega_{0}^{R}(K)}\right)^{\frac{r(n+t)}{t(n+r)}}.

Equality holds in both inequalities if and only if
K

Remarks.

One-side Liouville Theorem for hypoelliptic Ornstein–Uhlenbeck operators having drifts with imaginary spectrum
Alessia E. Kogoj, Ermanno Lanconelli, Giulio Tralli

Abstract
. We prove the Liouville theorem for non-negative solutions to (possibly degenerate) Ornstein–Uhlenbeck equations whose linear drift has imaginary spectrum. This provides an answer to a question raised by Priola and Zabczyk since the proof of their Theorem characterizing the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck operators having the Liouville property for bounded solutions. Our approach is based on a Liouville property at “ t = − ∞ t=-\infty ” for the solutions to the relevant Kolmogorov equation which, in turn, derives from a new parabolic Harnack-type inequality for its non-negative ancient solutions.

35B53, 35H10, 35K99

1.
Introduction and main results

Let
A
and
B
be real
N\times N
matrices, and let
A
be symmetric non-negative definite. The linear second order PDO in
{\mathbb{R}^{N}}

(1.1)

\mathcal{L}:=\operatorname{tr}\left(AD^{2}\right)+\left\langle Bx,D\right\rangle,

is usually called the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck operator associated to
(A,B).
Here and in what follows,
D,D^{2},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle,
and
x
denote, respectively, the gradient and the Hessian operators, the inner product, and the generic point of
{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
.

Letting,

(1.2)

E(t):=e^{-tB},

one defines the
N\times N
real symmetric matrix

(1.3)

C(t):=\int_{0}^{t}E(s)AE(s)^{T}ds.

Throughout the paper we assume

(1.4)

C(t)>0\mbox{ for every }t>0.

As it is quite well known, condition (
1.4
) is equivalent to the
hypoellipticity
of
\mathcal{L}
, i.e., to the smoothness of the distributional solutions of
\mathcal{L}u=f
whenever
f
is smooth (see e.g.
[
8
]
, and the references therein). In particular, (
1.4
) is always satisfied in case
A
is strictly positive definite, and it can rephrased in terms of an algebraic condition involving
Ker(A)
and
B^{T}
(see
[
8
, Appendix]
and Section
3
below).

One says that
\mathcal{L}
has the

L^{\infty}
-Liouville property

if every bounded (smooth) solution to

(1.5)

\mathcal{L}u=0\mbox{ in }{\mathbb{R}^{N}}

is constant.

One says that
\mathcal{L}
has the

one-side Liouville property

if the constant functions are the unique bounded below (smooth) solutions to (
1.5
).

Priola and Zabczyk - in 2004 - proved the following noteworthy and elegant theorem in
[
9
, Theorem 3.1]
:

Let
\mathcal{L}
be the hypoelliptic Ornstein–Uhlenbeck operator in (
1.1
). Then
\mathcal{L}
has the
L^{\infty}
-Liouville property if and only if

(1.6)

\mathrm{Re}(\lambda)\leq 0\mbox{ for every }\lambda\in\sigma(B).

Here, and in what follows,
\sigma(B)
denotes the spectrum of
B
, i.e., the set of eigenvalues of
B
.

The Priola–Zabczyk Theorem naturally raises the following question:

(Q)

Does condition (
1.6
) also guarantee the stronger one-side Liouville property for
\mathcal{L}
?

This question was raised by Priola and Zabczyk themselves in
[
10
, Section 6, Open questions]
and so far, to the best of our knowledge, it has been answered in the specific cases (in which
\mathcal{L}
is always assumed to be hypoelliptic) listed here:

(I)

The operator
\mathcal{L}
is homogeneous of degree two with respect to a group of dilations in
{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
(see
[
6
, Corollary 8.3]
). We stress that this condition implies
\sigma(B)=\{0\}
.

(II)

The matrix
A
is strictly positive definite, the matrix
B
is diagonalizable over the complex field and
\sigma(B)\subseteq i\mathbb{R}
(see
[
7
, Theorem 1.1]
).

(III)

The real Jordan representation of
B
is

\begin{pmatrix}B_{0}&0\\
0&B_{1}\end{pmatrix}

where
\sigma(B_{0})\subseteq\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\ |\ \mathrm{Re}(\lambda)<0\}
and
B_{1}
is at most of dimension
2

and of the form

B_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}0\end{pmatrix}\qquad\mbox{ or }\qquad B_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-\alpha\\
\alpha&0\end{pmatrix}\,\,\,\mbox{with $\alpha\in\mathbb{R}$}

(see
[
7
, Theorem 6.1]
and the references therein).

We also quote a recent result by Priola in
[
11
]
where it is treated the case of solutions having a controlled growth at infinity under two sets of assumptions.

The main purpose of this paper is to give the following answer to the question (Q).

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be the hypoelliptic Ornstein–Uhlenbeck operator in (
1.1
) and assume

(1.7)

\sigma(B)\subseteq i\mathbb{R}.

Then
\mathcal{L}
has the one-side Liouville property.

This theorem obviously contains the Liouville theorems of the cases (I) and (II) while only complements the one of (III).

Theorem
1.1
is a simple corollary of the following Liouville theorem at “
t=-\infty
” for the evolution counterpart of
\mathcal{L}
, i.e., for the Kolmogorov-type operator in
{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}

(1.8)

\mathcal{K}:=\mathcal{L}-\partial_{t}.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be the hypoelliptic Ornstein–Uhlenbeck operator in (
1.1
) and let
\mathcal{K}
be the corresponding Kolmogorov operator in (
1.8
). Assume

\sigma(B)\subseteq i\mathbb{R}.

If
u
is a smooth solution to

\mathcal{K}u=0\mbox{ in }{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}

such that

\inf_{{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}}u>-\infty,

then,

\lim_{t\to-\infty}u(x,t)=\inf_{{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}}u

for every
x\in{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
.

We want to immediately show that Theorem
1.2
implies Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
v:{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a smooth and bounded below solution to

\mathcal{L}v=0\mbox{ in }{\mathbb{R}^{N}}.

Then, the function

u:{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R},\quad u(x,t)=v(x)

is smooth and solves

\mathcal{K}u=0\mbox{ in }{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}.

Moreover,

(1.9)

\inf_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}u=\inf_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}v>-\infty.

Then, by Theorem
1.2
,

\lim_{t\to-\infty}u(x,t)=\inf_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}u\mbox{ for every }x\in{\mathbb{R}^{N}}.

On the other hand,

\lim_{t\to-\infty}u(x,t)=\lim_{t\to-\infty}v(x)=v(x).

Hence, also keeping in mind (
1.9
),

v(x)=\inf_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}v\mbox{ for every }x\in{\mathbb{R}^{N}},

that is
v
is constant and Theorem
1.1
is proved.
∎

The remaining part of this paper is entirely devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.2
. Our key idea for this proof, inspired by a procedure first used in
[
7
]
, consists in deducing the Liouville theorem for
\mathcal{K}
at “
t=-\infty
” from a kind of global
Harnack inequality for the positive entire solution to
\mathcal{K}u=0
(cfr. (
7.5
)). In turn, our proof of this Harnack inequality uses, as in
[
7
]
, suitable Mean Value formulas for
\mathcal{K}
-harmonic functions. The main technical difference, in comparison with previous works, relies on quantitative estimates for the relevant geometric objects which we show to be uniform for large (negative) times under the sole assumption (
1.7
).

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we fix the needed notations. In Section
3
we establish quantitative estimates with respect to the time parameter
t
for the quadratic forms generated by
C(t)
and
E(-t)C(t)E(-t)^{T}
: in particular we prove a global doubling property for the determinant function in Corollary
3.3
as well as crucial self-similarity properties along time in Theorem
3.4
. These results, which we believe are of independent interest, are exploited in Sections
4
-
5
-
6
where we address, respectively, the following properties: the large-time behaviour of the
intrinsic paraboloids

\mathcal{P}_{z_{0}}
, an engulfing property for the sets
\Omega^{(p)}_{r}(z_{0})
where the mean value formulas are defined, and a pointwise comparison for the kernels of such mean value formulas. These three results come into play in Section
7
where we conclude the proof of Theorem
1.2
by showing the Harnack-type inequality.

2.
Preliminaries and further notations

We denote the generic point
z\in{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}
as follows

z=(x,t),x\in{\mathbb{R}^{N}},t\in\mathbb{R}.

Let
C(t)
be the Kalman matrix in (
1.3
), and
E(t)
the exponential-type matrix in (
1.2
). Since
E(-t)E(s)=E(s-t)
, the following holds true for any positive
t

(2.1)

E(-t)C(t)E(-t)^{T}=\int_{0}^{t}E(s-t)AE(s-t)^{T}ds=\int_{0}^{t}E(-\sigma)AE(-\sigma)^{T}d\sigma.

It is clear from their definitions that both the matrices
C(t)
and
E(-t)C(t)E(-t)^{T}
are monotone increasing with respect to
t
in the sense of quadratic forms, and one can switch from one to the other with the change
B\mapsto-B
: both these matrices will play a pervasive role in this work. Since
B
is a real matrix and
\sigma(B)\subseteq i\mathbb{R}
by (
1.7
), if
\lambda\in\sigma(B)
then
-\lambda\in\sigma(B)
. As a consequence

\operatorname{div}(Bx)=\operatorname{tr}(B)=0.

In particular, for any
t>0
one has
\operatorname{det}\left(E(-t)\right)=e^{t\operatorname{tr}(B)}=1
and we can define

(2.2)

D(t):=\operatorname{det}\left(C(t)\right)=\operatorname{det}\left(E(-t)C(t)E(-t)^{T}\right).

For
t>0
and
p\in\mathbb{N}
, we also fix the notation

(2.3)

V_{p}(t):=\sqrt{(4\pi)^{N+p}\,t^{p}\,D(t)}.

Under our assumption (
1.4
), the PDO in
{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}

\mathcal{K}=\mathcal{L}-\partial_{t}

has a fundamental solution

\Gamma:\{(z_{0},z)\in{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}\times{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}\ |\ z_{0}\neq z\}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}

given by

\Gamma(z_{0};z)=\Gamma((x_{0},t_{0});(x,t))=0

if
t_{0}\leq t
, and

\displaystyle\Gamma(z_{0};z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Gamma((x_{0},t_{0});(x,t))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(4\pi)^{-\frac{N}{2}}}{\sqrt{D(t_{0}-t)}}\exp{\left(-\frac{\langle C^{-1}(t_{0}-t)\left(x_{0}-E(t_{0}-t)x\right),\left(x_{0}-E(t_{0}-t)x\right)\rangle}{4}\right)}

if
t<t_{0}
(see e.g.
[
8
]
). The
\mathcal{K}
-harmonic functions, i.e., the solutions to
\mathcal{K}u=0
, satisfy Mean Value formulas on suitable superlevel sets of
\Gamma
. To show these formulas, we need some more notations.

Fix
(x_{0},t_{0})\in{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}
. For every
p\in\mathbb{N}
and
r>0
, define

(2.4)

\Omega^{(p)}_{r}(z_{0}):=\left\{z=(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N+1}\,:\,(4\pi(t_{0}-t))^{-\frac{p}{2}}\Gamma(z_{0};z)>\frac{1}{r}\right\},

and

(2.5)

W_{r}^{(p)}(z_{0};z):=\omega_{p}R^{p}_{r}(z_{0};z)\left(W(z_{0};z)+\frac{p}{4(p+2)}\left(\frac{R_{r}(z_{0};z)}{t_{0}-t}\right)^{2}\right).

Here,
\omega_{p}
is the volume of the unit ball of
\mathbb{R}^{p}
, and we adopt the notations

(2.6)

R_{r}(z_{0};z):=\sqrt{4(t_{0}-t)\log{\left(r(4\pi(t_{0}-t))^{-\frac{p}{2}}\Gamma(z_{0};z)\right)}}

and

(2.7)

\displaystyle W(z_{0};z):=

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\left\langle\left(E(t_{0}-t)AE(t_{0}-t)^{T}\right)C^{-1}(t_{0}-t)\left(x_{0}-E(t_{0}-t)x\right),C^{-1}(t_{0}-t)\left(x_{0}-E(t_{0}-t)x\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\left\langle A\left(E(t-t_{0})C(t_{0}-t)E(t-t_{0})^{T}\right)^{-1}\left(x-E(t-t_{0})x_{0}\right),\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 159.3356pt\left.\left(E(t-t_{0})C(t_{0}-t)E(t-t_{0})^{T}\right)^{-1}\left(x-E(t-t_{0})x_{0}\right)\right\rangle.

Then, every solution to
\mathcal{K}u=0
in
{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}
satisfies the Mean Value formula

(2.8)

u(z_{0})=\frac{1}{r}\int_{\Omega^{(p)}_{r}(z_{0})}u(z)W_{r}^{(p)}(z_{0};z)dz

for every
z_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}
, for every
r>0
and for every
p\in\mathbb{N}
. A complete and detailed proof of this formula can be found in the paper
[
2
]
.

3.
Matrix algebra and doubling property

In this section we study the behaviour with respect to the time parameter
t
of the quadratic forms
\left\langle C(t)\xi,\xi\right\rangle
and
\left\langle E(-t)C(t)E(-t)^{T}\xi,\xi\right\rangle
. The assumption (
1.4
) readily implies the existence of a positive smallest eigenvalue which depends on
t
. We aim at establishing estimates which are uniform in
t
. By assuming only (
1.4
), the small time (uniform) behaviour is already known thanks to the analysis in
[
8
]
. In particular, as a by-product of the results in
[
8
]
we know that there exists
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
such that the following holds: for any
T_{0}>0
there exists a constant
K(T_{0})\geq 1
such that

(3.1)

\frac{t^{2n_{0}+1}}{K(T_{0})}|\xi|^{2}\leq\left\langle E(-t)C(t)E(-t)^{T}\xi,\xi\right\rangle\leq K(T_{0})t|\xi|^{2}\quad\forall\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\mbox{ and for every }t\in(0,T_{0}].

We invite the reader to look at
[
8
, Lemma 3.3. and Proposition 2.3]
and
[
1
, Lemma 2.2]
for a proof of (
3.1
). The constant
n_{0}
will make its appearance in the following sections. Here, we want to understand the behaviour for large times of the quadratic forms generated by
C(t)
and
E(-t)C(t)E(-t)^{T}
: the reader should keep in mind that the spectral assumption (
1.7
) is crucial for the validity of the next result.

Proposition 3.1
.

There exist positive constants
c_{+},c_{-}
such that, for every
t\geq 1
, the following inequalities hold

(3.2)

\left\langle E(-t)C(t)E(-t)^{T}\xi,\xi\right\rangle\geq(c_{+})\,t|\xi|^{2}\quad\forall\,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}

and

(3.3)

\left\langle C(t)\xi,\xi\right\rangle\geq(c_{-})\,t|\xi|^{2}\quad\forall\,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Proof.

We first prove the validity of (
3.3
). Once this is established, by exchanging the roles of
B\mapsto-B
we will have also the proof of (
3.2
).

We start noticing that our assumptions (as well as the desired conclusion in (

\mathbb{R}^{N}
(that is invariance by similarity). Hence, without any loss of generality, we can assume that the matrix

coincides with its Jordan’s (real) canonical form (see, e.g.,

B^{T}=\begin{pmatrix}J_{n_{1}}&&&&&&\\
&\ddots&&&0&&\\
&&J_{n_{q}}&&&&\\

&&0&&&\ddots&\\
&&&&&&C_{m_{p}}(b_{p})\end{pmatrix},

where by (

) the eigenvalues of

(keeping in mind that
\sigma(B^{T})=\sigma(B)
) are
\{0,\pm ib_{1},\ldots,\pm ib_{p}\}

b_{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}\smallsetminus\{0\}
,
n_{1}+\ldots+n_{q}+2m_{1}+\ldots+2m_{p}=N
with
n_{k},m_{\ell}\in\mathbb{N}
, and the
n_{k}\times n_{k}
matrices
J_{n_{k}}
and (respectively) the
2m_{\ell}\times 2m_{\ell}

are in the following form

J_{n_{k}}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0&\ldots&0\\
0&0&1&\ldots&0\\
0&0&\ddots&\ddots&0\\
0&\ldots&0&0&1\\

Up to a permutation (and a change in sign) of the coordinates, we can always think that
0<b_{1}\leq b_{2}\leq\ldots\leq b_{p}

as well as the notation

with

. Thanks to the above described upper-diagonal blocks, we have a rather precise information about the non-trivial invariant subspaces for

. In what follows we shall exploit the following: if

contains at least either a one-dimensional space

where

(related to the

&&\sqrt{t}\delta^{(n_{q})}_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}}&&&&\\
&&&\sqrt{t}\delta^{(m_{1})}_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}}&&&\\
&&0&&&\ddots&\\
&&&&&&\sqrt{t}\delta^{(m_{p})}_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}}.\end{pmatrix}

Let us now define the symmetric and non-negative matrix

A_{\infty}=D_{\infty}AD_{\infty},

where
D_{\infty}
is the diagonal matrix

D_{\infty}=\begin{pmatrix}D^{(n_{1})}_{\infty}&&&&&&\\
&\ddots&&&0&&\\
&&D^{(n_{q})}_{\infty}&&&&\\
&&&D^{(m_{1})}_{\infty}&&&\\
&&0&&&\ddots&\\
&&&&&&D^{(m_{p})}_{\infty}\end{pmatrix}

with

D^{(n_{k})}_{\infty}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0&\ldots&0\\
0&0&0&\ldots&0\\
0&0&\ddots&\ddots&0\\
0&\ldots&0&0&0\\
0&0&\ldots&0&0\end{pmatrix}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad D^{(m_{\ell})}_{\infty}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{I}_{2}&\mathbb{O}_{2}&\mathbb{O}_{2}&\ldots&\mathbb{O}_{2}\\
\mathbb{O}_{2}&\mathbb{O}_{2}&\mathbb{O}_{2}&\ldots&\mathbb{O}_{2}\\
\mathbb{O}_{2}&\mathbb{O}_{2}&\ddots&\ddots&\mathbb{O}_{2}\\
\mathbb{O}_{2}&\ldots&\mathbb{O}_{2}&\mathbb{O}_{2}&\mathbb{O}_{2}\\
\mathbb{O}_{2}&\mathbb{O}_{2}&\ldots&\mathbb{O}_{2}&\mathbb{O}_{2}\end{pmatrix}.

Let us notice that the matrix
D_{\infty}
is actually an orthogonal projector as
D^{2}_{\infty}=D_{\infty}
. It is also clear from the definition that

A_{t}=A_{\infty}+O(t^{-1})\qquad\mbox{ as }t\to+\infty.

By the previous relation and by noticing that the components of the matrix
e^{-\sigma B_{t}}
as functions of the variables
\sigma\in[0,1]
are uniformly bounded with respect to the parameter
t
, one has that

(3.8)

\int_{0}^{1}\left\langle A_{t}e^{-\sigma B_{t}}v,e^{-\sigma B_{t}}v\right\rangle d\sigma=\int_{0}^{1}\left\langle A_{\infty}e^{-\sigma B_{t}}v,e^{-\sigma B_{t}}v\right\rangle d\sigma+O(t^{-1})|v|^{2}

as
t\to+\infty
(uniformly with respect to arbitrary
v\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
). Let us now claim that there exists a constant
\lambda_{0}>0
such that

(3.9)

\int_{0}^{1}\left\langle A_{\infty}e^{-\sigma B_{t}}v,e^{-\sigma B_{t}}v\right\rangle d\sigma\geq\lambda_{0}\int_{0}^{1}\left|D_{\infty}e^{-\sigma B_{t}}v\right|^{2}d\sigma+O(t^{-1})|v|^{2},\,\,\mbox{ as }t\to+\infty.

For reading convenience, we postpone the lengthy proof of (
3.9
) to the subSection
3.1
below. We just mention here that, in the non-degenerate case where
A
is positive definite, then (
3.9
) is readily satisfied by taking
\lambda_{0}
to be the smallest eigenvalue of
A
since in this case
A_{\infty}=D_{\infty}AD_{\infty}\geq\lambda_{0}D_{\infty}^{2}
(no remainder term
O(t^{-1})
is needed in such situation). Here we give (
3.9
) for granted, and we proceed in establishing the following crucial bound: there exists a constant
c>0
such that

(3.10)

\int_{0}^{1}\left|D_{\infty}e^{-\sigma B_{t}}v\right|^{2}d\sigma\geq c|v|^{2}\quad\mbox{ for all }t>0.

In order to infer the validity of (
3.10
) we first notice that the left-hand side is independent of
t
: as a matter of fact, for
t>0
and
v\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, in our notations we have

\displaystyle\left|D_{\infty}e^{-\sigma B_{t}}v\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{q}\left|D_{\infty}^{(n_{k})}e^{-\sigma J_{n_{k}}}v^{(n_{k})}\right|^{2}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{p}\left|D_{\infty}^{(m_{\ell})}e^{-\sigma C_{m_{\ell}}(tb_{\ell})}v^{(m_{\ell})}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{q}\left|D_{\infty}^{(n_{k})}e^{-\sigma J_{n_{k}}}v^{(n_{k})}\right|^{2}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{p}\left|\sum_{k=1}^{m_{\ell}}\frac{(-\sigma)^{k-1}}{(k-1)!}R_{-\sigma tb_{\ell}}\left(v^{(m_{\ell})}\right)_{k}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{q}\left|D_{\infty}^{(n_{k})}e^{-\sigma J_{n_{k}}}v^{(n_{k})}\right|^{2}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{p}\left|R_{-\sigma tb_{\ell}}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m_{\ell}}\frac{(-\sigma)^{k-1}}{(k-1)!}\left(v^{(m_{\ell})}\right)_{k}\right)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{q}\left|D_{\infty}^{(n_{k})}e^{-\sigma J_{n_{k}}}v^{(n_{k})}\right|^{2}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{p}\left|\sum_{k=1}^{m_{\ell}}\frac{(-\sigma)^{k-1}}{(k-1)!}\left(v^{(m_{\ell})}\right)_{k}\right|^{2}

where in the last equality we used the fact that
R_{-\sigma tb_{\ell}}
is a
2\times 2
orthogonal matrix and it doesn’t affect the norms in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Being
t
-independent, the value
\left|D_{\infty}e^{-\sigma B_{t}}v\right|^{2}
agrees with its value at
t=1
and we keep in mind that
e^{-\sigma B_{1}}=e^{-\sigma B^{T}}
. Hence we have

\int_{0}^{1}\left|D_{\infty}e^{-\sigma B_{t}}v\right|^{2}d\sigma=\int_{0}^{1}\left|D_{\infty}e^{-\sigma B^{T}}v\right|^{2}d\sigma=\int_{0}^{1}\left\langle D_{\infty}e^{-\sigma B^{T}}v,e^{-\sigma B^{T}}v\right\rangle d\sigma=:\left\langle C_{\infty}(1)v,v\right\rangle,

where we are denoting by
C_{\infty}(\cdot)
the Kalman-type matrix (as the one in (
1.3
)) associated to the operator
\mathcal{L}_{\infty}=\operatorname{tr}\left((D_{\infty})D^{2}\right)+\left\langle Bx,\nabla\right\rangle
. Such operator is degenerate-elliptic (even in the sub-case where
A
is positive definite) since the diffusion matrix
D_{\infty}
might have a very big kernel. Nonetheless, it is remarkable that the hypoellipticity condition still holds for
\mathcal{L}_{\infty}
thanks to the special mutual structure of the matrices
D_{\infty}
and
B
. As a matter of fact, every non-trivial invariant subspace of
B^{T}
contains at least one non-null vector of the form
\xi_{0}
or
\xi_{\bar{b}}
described in
case 0
) and
case
\bar{b}
) above, and both vectors
\xi_{0}
and
\xi_{\bar{b}}
are spanned by the columns of
D_{\infty}
: since the columns of
D_{\infty}
are orthogonal to
Ker(D_{\infty})
, this is implying that
Ker(D_{\infty})
does not contain any non-trivial subspace which is invariant for
B^{T}
. Such algebraic property for
Ker(D_{\infty})
and the invariant subspaces of
B^{T}
is known to be equivalent to the Hörmander condition (see, e.g.,
[
4
, page 148]
and
[
8
, (1.2)]
): this is telling us that the Kalman matrix
C_{\infty}(\tau)
is positive definite for any
\tau>0
, and in particular we have the existence of
c>0
such that

(3.11)

\int_{0}^{1}\left|D_{\infty}e^{-\sigma B_{t}}v\right|^{2}d\sigma=\left\langle C_{\infty}(1)v,v\right\rangle\geq c|v|^{2}\qquad\forall\,v\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

This fact finishes the proof of (
3.10
). If we put together (
3.8
), (
3.9
), and (
3.10
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\left\langle A_{t}e^{-\sigma B_{t}}v,e^{-\sigma B_{t}}v\right\rangle d\sigma

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\left\langle A_{\infty}e^{-\sigma B_{t}}v,e^{-\sigma B_{t}}v\right\rangle d\sigma+O(t^{-1})|v|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\lambda_{0}\int_{0}^{1}\left\langle D_{\infty}e^{-\sigma B_{t}}v,D_{\infty}e^{-\sigma B_{t}}v\right\rangle d\sigma+O(t^{-1})|v|^{2}\geq\lambda_{0}c|v|^{2}+O(t^{-1})|v|^{2}\quad\mbox{ as }t\to+\infty,

for every
v\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. The previous asymptotic relation implies the existence of
T\geq 1
such that, for every
v\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,

(3.12)

\int_{0}^{1}\left\langle A_{t}e^{-\sigma B_{t}}v,e^{-\sigma B_{t}}v\right\rangle d\sigma\geq\frac{\lambda_{0}c}{2}|v|^{2}\quad\mbox{ for all }t\geq T.

Finally, we can exploit (
3.12
) into (
3.7
) and obtain

(3.13)

\left\langle C(t)\xi,\xi\right\rangle\geq\frac{\lambda_{0}c}{2}\left|\delta_{\sqrt{t}}(\xi)\right|^{2}\geq\frac{\lambda_{0}c}{2}t|\xi|^{2}\quad\forall\,t\geq T.

On the other hand, by the monotonicity properties of
C(t)
, we also have

(3.14)

\left\langle C(t)\xi,\xi\right\rangle\geq\left\langle C(1)\xi,\xi\right\rangle\geq\lambda_{C(1)}|\xi|^{2}=\frac{1}{T}\lambda_{C(1)}T|\xi|^{2}\geq\frac{1}{T}\lambda_{C(1)}t|\xi|^{2}\quad\forall\,t\in[1,T],

where
\lambda_{C(1)}>0
is the smallest eigenvalue of the positive definite matrix
C(1)
. From (
3.13
)-(
3.14
), we have the desired estimate in (
3.3
) with the choice

c_{-}=\min\left\{\frac{\lambda_{0}c}{2},\frac{1}{T}\lambda_{C(1)}\right\}.

This concludes the proof of (
3.3
) and (as we mentioned at the beginning of the proof) also of (
3.2
).
∎

Corollary 3.2
.

Denoting by
c_{+},c_{-}
the positive constants in Proposition
3.1
, for any
t\geq 1
we have

(3.15)

\left\langle E(t)^{T}C^{-1}(t)E(t)\xi,\xi\right\rangle\leq\frac{1}{(c_{+})\,t}|\xi|^{2}\quad\forall\,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}

and

(3.16)

\left\langle C^{-1}(t)\xi,\xi\right\rangle\leq\frac{1}{(c_{-})\,t}|\xi|^{2}\quad\forall\,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

In particular, for every
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, one has

(3.17)

\underset{t\to+\infty}{\lim}\langle C^{-1}(t)\xi,\xi\rangle=\underset{t\to+\infty}{\lim}\left\langle E(t)^{T}C^{-1}(t)E(t)\xi,\xi\right\rangle=0

Proof.

We recall that, for any symmetric and positive definite matrix
M
and for any non-null vector
v
, we can consider the unique non-null vector
w
satisfying
v=\sqrt{M}w
and we can notice that

\left\langle M^{-1}\frac{v}{|v|},\frac{v}{|v|}\right\rangle=\frac{\left\langle w,w\right\rangle}{|v|^{2}}=\frac{|w|^{2}}{\left\langle Mw,w\right\rangle}=\left(\left\langle M\frac{w}{|w|},\frac{w}{|w|}\right\rangle\right)^{-1}.

This yields

(3.18)

\underset{|\zeta|=1}{\sup}\left\langle M^{-1}\zeta,\zeta\right\rangle=\frac{1}{\underset{|\xi|=1}{\inf}\left\langle M\xi,\xi\right\rangle}.

Exploiting (
3.18
) with the choices
M=E(-t)C(t)E(-t)^{T}
(whose inverse matrix is given by
M^{-1}=E(t)^{T}C^{-1}(t)E(t)
) and, respectively,
M=C(t)
(both matrices are positive definite thanks to (
1.4
)), we deduce (
3.15
)-(
3.16
) from (
3.2
)-(
3.3
). The limiting behaviour in (
3.17
) follows easily from (
3.15
)-(
3.16
).
∎

The previous algebraic statements have a number of geometric consequences which are crucial for our scopes. The first one concerns the determinant function
D(t)
. On one hand, an immediate consequence of Proposition
3.1
is that
D(t)\gtrsim t^{N}
for
t\geq 1
(since each one of the
N
eigenvalues grows at least as
t
): this is already an improvement with respect to
[
3
, Proposition 3.1]
where the weaker bound
D(t)\gtrsim t^{2}
was established for
N\geq 2
. On the other hand, in the next corollary we show how Proposition
3.1
implies in fact a global doubling property for
D(t)
. This phenomenon quantifies the global power-like behaviour of the determinant function.

Corollary 3.3
.

There exists
c_{d}>1
such that

(3.19)

D(2t)\leq c_{d}\,D(t)\qquad\mbox{ for every }t>0.

In particular

(3.20)

V_{p}(2t)\leq 2^{\frac{p}{2}}\sqrt{c_{d}}\,V_{p}(t)\qquad\mbox{ for every }t>0.

Proof.

From the definition of
C(t)
or from (
2.1
) one can see that the function
D(t)
is positive and increasing. Moreover, it is proved in
[
8
, Lemma 3.1 and Remark 3.1]
that there exist
c_{0}\geq 1
and
Q_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\frac{1}{c_{0}}t^{Q_{0}}\leq D(t)\leq c_{0}t^{Q_{0}}\qquad\mbox{ for every }t\in(0,2].

The previous estimate yields

(3.21)

D(2t)\leq c_{0}2^{Q_{0}}t^{Q_{0}}\leq c_{0}^{2}2^{Q_{0}}D(t)\mbox{ for every }t\in(0,1],

i.e. the validity of a local doubling condition for the function
D(\cdot)
. We want to deduce from Corollary
3.2
that such a doubling condition holds in fact globally. To this aim, we recall the so-called Jacobi’s formula

\frac{d}{dt}\left(\log{\left(\operatorname{det}\left(M(t)\right)\right)}\right)=\operatorname{tr}\left(M^{-1}(t)M^{\prime}(t)\right),

which is valid for any invertible matrix
M(t)
. If we apply this formula with the choice
M(t)=E(-t)C(t)E(-t)^{T}
and we keep in mind that in such case
M^{\prime}(t)=e^{tB}Ae^{tB^{T}}
by (
2.1
), we obtain

\frac{d}{dt}\left(\log{\left(D(t)\right)}\right)=\operatorname{tr}\left(e^{-tB^{T}}C^{-1}(t)e^{-tB}e^{tB}Ae^{tB^{T}}\right)=\operatorname{tr}\left(C^{-1}(t)A\right).

On the other hand, by exploiting (
3.16
), we have

\left\langle C^{-1}(t)\sqrt{A}v,\sqrt{A}v\right\rangle\leq\frac{1}{(c_{-})\,t}|\sqrt{A}v|^{2}=\frac{1}{(c_{-})\,t}\left\langle Av,v\right\rangle

for any vector
v\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and for every
t\geq 1
. This implies that

(3.22)

\frac{d}{dt}\left(\log{\left(D(t)\right)}\right)=\operatorname{tr}\left(C^{-1}(t)A\right)=\operatorname{tr}\left(\sqrt{A}C^{-1}(t)\sqrt{A}\right)\leq\frac{1}{(c_{-})\,t}\operatorname{tr}\left(A\right)\quad\forall\,t\geq 1.

Hence, for every
t\geq 1
, we obtain

\log{\left(\frac{D(2t)}{D(t)}\right)}=\log{\left(D(2t)\right)}-\log{\left(D(t)\right)}=\int_{t}^{2t}\frac{d}{d\sigma}\left(\log{\left(D(\sigma)\right)}\right)\,d\sigma\leq\frac{\operatorname{tr}\left(A\right)}{(c_{-})}\int_{t}^{2t}\frac{1}{\sigma}\,d\sigma=\frac{\operatorname{tr}\left(A\right)\log(2)}{(c_{-})},

which implies

D(2t)\leq e^{\frac{\operatorname{tr}\left(A\right)\log(2)}{(c_{-})}}D(t).

Therefore, we have established the validity of (
3.19
) with the choice

c_{d}:=\max\left\{e^{\frac{\operatorname{tr}\left(A\right)\log(2)}{(c_{-})}},c_{0}^{2}2^{Q_{0}}\right\}.

Since from the definition of
V_{p}(\cdot)
we have

\frac{V_{p}(2t)}{V_{p}(t)}=\sqrt{\frac{2^{p}D(2t)}{D(t)}},

the inequality (
3.20
) follows from (
3.19
).
∎

By a classical argument, from (
3.19
) we deduce

\frac{D(R)}{D(r)}\leq c_{d}\left(\frac{R}{r}\right)^{\log_{2}{(c_{d})}}\quad\mbox{ for any }0<r\leq R.

In particular, we get

(3.23)

\frac{V_{p}(R)}{V_{p}(r)}\leq\sqrt{c_{d}}\left(\frac{R}{r}\right)^{Q_{p}}\quad\mbox{ for any }0<r\leq R,\quad\mbox{ with }Q_{p}:=\frac{p+\log_{2}{(c_{d})}}{2}.

The following theorem represents an enhanced version of Proposition
3.1
and Corollary
3.2
(it boils down to (
3.2
) and (
3.15
) when the parameter
\mu
is fixed as
t^{-1}
). It will play a key role in Section
5
. It establishes self-similarity properties for the relevant quadratic form at the two different scales
t
and
\mu t
in an uniform way with respect to
t\geq 1
and
0<\mu\leq 1
: the proof of this fact capitalizes further on the main new idea present in the proof of Proposition
3.1
, which is the presence of the hypoelliptic operator
\mathcal{L}_{\infty}
which possesses invariant properties (see, e.g., (
3.27
)) and it approximates in some sense
\mathcal{L}
for large times.

Theorem 3.4
.

There exists a positive constant
k_{0}
such that, for every
t\geq 1
and for every
0<\mu\leq 1
, the following holds

(3.24)

\left\langle E(-t)C(t)E(-t)^{T}\xi,\xi\right\rangle\geq\frac{k_{0}}{\mu}\left\langle E(-\mu t)C(\mu t)E(-\mu t)^{T}\xi,\xi\right\rangle\quad\forall\,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

In particular, for any
t\geq 1
and
0<\mu\leq 1
, we have

(3.25)

\left\langle E(t)^{T}C^{-1}(t)E(t)\xi,\xi\right\rangle\leq\frac{\mu}{k_{0}}\left\langle E(\mu t)^{T}C^{-1}(\mu t)E(\mu t)\xi,\xi\right\rangle\quad\forall\,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Proof.

Set
M(t)=E(-t)C(t)E(-t)^{T}
(under the holding assumptions, the same estimates hold true also for
M(t)=C(t)
). Let us adopt the same notations and the same strategy of Proposition
3.1
: we just replace
B\mapsto-B
in the proof of Proposition
3.1
from the Jordan form (
3.4
) for
-B^{T}
onwards. In particular, for any
t>0
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, as in (
3.7
) one has

(3.26)

\displaystyle\left\langle M(t)\xi,\xi\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle Ae^{sB^{T}}\xi,e^{sB^{T}}\xi\right\rangle ds=t\int_{0}^{1}\left\langle Ae^{\sigma tB^{T}}\xi,e^{\sigma tB^{T}}\xi\right\rangle d\sigma

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\left\langle A_{t}e^{-\sigma B_{t}}\delta_{\sqrt{t}}(\xi),e^{-\sigma B_{t}}\delta_{\sqrt{t}}(\xi)\right\rangle d\sigma.

Recalling that
A_{t}=A_{\infty}+O(t^{-1})
with
A_{\infty}=D_{\infty}AD_{\infty}
, we notice that we have

A_{\infty}\leq\Lambda_{0}D_{\infty}^{2}

where
\Lambda_{0}>0
is the largest eigenvalue of
A
. Hence, from the previous matrix inequality and from (
3.9
)-(
3.11
), there exists a constant
C\geq c>0
such that we have

\displaystyle\left(\lambda_{0}c+O(t^{-1})\right)|v|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{0}\left\langle C_{\infty}(1)v,v\right\rangle+O(t^{-1})|v|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}\left\langle A_{t}e^{-\sigma B_{t}}v,e^{-\sigma B_{t}}v\right\rangle d\sigma

\displaystyle\leq\Lambda_{0}\left\langle C_{\infty}(1)v,v\right\rangle+O(t^{-1})|v|^{2}\leq\left(\Lambda_{0}C+O(t^{-1})\right)|v|^{2}

for every
v\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
as
t\to+\infty
. Therefore, there exists
T\geq 1
such that

\frac{\lambda_{0}c}{2}|v|^{2}\leq\int_{0}^{1}\left\langle A_{t}e^{-\sigma B_{t}}v,e^{-\sigma B_{t}}v\right\rangle d\sigma\leq 2\Lambda_{0}C|v|^{2}\quad\forall\,v\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\mbox{ and for every }t\geq T.

By (
3.26
), we have thus established the following estimate

(3.27)

\frac{\lambda_{0}c}{2}|\delta_{\sqrt{t}}(\xi)|^{2}\leq\left\langle M(t)\xi,\xi\right\rangle\leq 2\Lambda_{0}C|\delta_{\sqrt{t}}(\xi)|^{2}\quad\forall\,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\mbox{ and for every }t\geq T.

Let us fix an arbitrary
t\geq T
. If
\frac{T}{t}\leq\mu\leq 1
we can exploit twice (
3.27
) to deduce that

\displaystyle\left\langle M(t)\xi,\xi\right\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\lambda_{0}c}{2}|\delta_{\sqrt{t}}(\xi)|^{2}=\frac{\lambda_{0}c}{2}|\delta_{\mu^{-\frac{1}{2}}}\delta_{\sqrt{\mu t}}(\xi)|^{2}\geq\frac{\lambda_{0}c}{2\mu}|\delta_{\sqrt{\mu t}}(\xi)|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda_{0}c}{4\Lambda_{0}C\mu}2\Lambda_{0}C|\delta_{\sqrt{\mu t}}(\xi)|^{2}\geq\frac{\lambda_{0}c}{4\Lambda_{0}C\mu}\left\langle M(\mu t)\xi,\xi\right\rangle\quad\forall\,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

On the other hand, if
0<\mu\leq\frac{T}{t}
, then we can use (
3.27
) and (
3.1
) (with
T_{0}=T
) to infer that

\displaystyle\left\langle M(t)\xi,\xi\right\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\lambda_{0}c}{2}|\delta_{\sqrt{t}}(\xi)|^{2}\geq\frac{\lambda_{0}c}{2}t|\xi|^{2}=\frac{\lambda_{0}c}{2K(T)\mu}K(T)\mu t|\xi|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\lambda_{0}c}{2K(T)\mu}\left\langle M(\mu t)\xi,\xi\right\rangle\quad\forall\,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

If instead we fix an arbitrary
1\leq t\leq T
, we can use again (
3.1
) and denote by
\lambda_{M(1)}
the smallest eigenvalue of
M(1)
: in this case, for any
0<\mu\leq 1
, we get

\displaystyle\left\langle M(t)\xi,\xi\right\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\left\langle M(1)\xi,\xi\right\rangle\geq\lambda_{M(1)}|\xi|^{2}=\frac{\lambda_{M(1)}}{\mu TK(T)}K(T)\mu T|\xi|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\lambda_{M(1)}}{\mu TK(T)}K(T)\mu t|\xi|^{2}\geq\frac{\lambda_{M(1)}}{\mu TK(T)}\left\langle M(\mu t)\xi,\xi\right\rangle\quad\forall\,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

By putting together the last three estimates we have

\left\langle M(t)\xi,\xi\right\rangle\geq\frac{k_{0}}{\mu}\left\langle M(\mu t)\xi,\xi\right\rangle\quad\forall\,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\mbox{ and for every }\mu\in(0,1],\,t\geq 1

with the choice

k_{0}=\min\left\{\frac{\lambda_{0}c}{4\Lambda_{0}C},\frac{\lambda_{0}c}{2K(T)},\frac{\lambda_{M(1)}}{TK(T)}\right\}.

This shows (
3.24
). Since
M^{-1}(t)=E(t)^{T}C^{-1}(t)E(t)
, arguing as in the proof of Corollary
3.2
we can derive (
3.25
) from (
3.24
).
∎

3.1.
Proof of the claim (
3.9
)

In the notations adopted in the proof of Proposition
3.1
, we are left with showing the existence of
\lambda_{0}>0
such that

(3.28)

\int_{0}^{1}\left\langle\left(D_{\infty}AD_{\infty}\right)e^{-\sigma B_{t}}v,e^{-\sigma B_{t}}v\right\rangle d\sigma\geq\lambda_{0}\int_{0}^{1}\left|D_{\infty}e^{-\sigma B_{t}}v\right|^{2}d\sigma+O(t^{-1})|v|^{2},\,\,\mbox{ as }t\to+\infty.

As we mentioned earlier, this is certainly satisfied if
A>0
(just take
\lambda_{0}
to be the smallest eigenvalue of
A
, with the remainder term
O(t^{-1})=0
), and we need to work out the case of
A
with non-trivial kernel (under (
1.4
)). To this aim, we need few more notations. Since we have fixed
0<b_{1}\leq b_{2}\leq\ldots\leq b_{p}
, we can distinguish the indexes for which the
b_{\ell}
’s agree or differ. Let us write

p=p_{1}+\ldots+p_{l}

and

\bar{b}_{1}=\{b_{\ell}\,:\,\ell\in\{1,\ldots,p_{1}\}\},\bar{b}_{2}=\{b_{\ell}\,:\,\ell\in\{p_{1}+1,\ldots,p_{1}+p_{2}\}\},\ldots,\bar{b}_{l}=\{b_{\ell}\,:\,\ell\in\{p-p_{l}+1,\ldots,p\}\}.

We denote

V_{0}={\rm{span}}\left\{\xi=(\xi^{(\bar{n})},0)\,:\,\xi^{(\bar{n})}\in\mathbb{R}^{\bar{n}}\right\}

and

\displaystyle V_{1}={\rm{span}}\left\{\xi=(0,\xi^{(\bar{m})})\,:\,\xi^{(m_{\ell})}=0\mbox{ for any }\ell\geq p_{1}+1\right\},\ldots

4.
Paraboloids

For any
z_{0}=(x_{0},t_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
we denote

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{z_{0}}=\left\{(x,t)\right.

\displaystyle\in\mathbb{R}^{N+1}\,:\,t<t_{0}\,\mbox{ and }

\displaystyle\left.\langle C^{-1}(t_{0}-t)\left(x_{0}-E(t_{0}-t)x\right),\left(x_{0}-E(t_{0}-t)x\right)\rangle<1\right\}.

From the results of the previous section it is straightforward to infer the following geometric consequence on the behaviour for large negative times of the paraboloids defined in (
4.1
).

Corollary 4.1
.

For any
z_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
and for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, there exists
T=T(x,z_{0})<t_{0}
such that

(x,t)\in\mathcal{P}_{z_{0}}\quad\forall\,t<T.

Proof.

Recall that, for any symmetric matrix
M\geq 0
, one has

(4.2)

0\leq\left\langle M(v+w),v+w\right\rangle\leq 2\left\langle Mv,v\right\rangle+2\left\langle Mw,w\right\rangle\qquad\forall\,v,w.

Therefore, for any fixed
z_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\langle C^{-1}(t_{0}-t)\left(x_{0}-E(t_{0}-t)x\right),\left(x_{0}-E(t_{0}-t)x\right)\rangle\leq

\displaystyle\leq 2\langle C^{-1}(t_{0}-t)x_{0},x_{0}\rangle+2\langle C^{-1}(t_{0}-t)E(t_{0}-t)x,E(t_{0}-t)x\rangle

\displaystyle=2\langle C^{-1}(t_{0}-t)x_{0},x_{0}\rangle+2\langle E(t_{0}-t)^{T}C^{-1}(t_{0}-t)E(t_{0}-t)x,x\rangle.

We can thus exploit the property (
3.17
) in Corollary
3.2
to infer that

\exists\,\underset{t\to-\infty}{\lim}\langle C^{-1}(t_{0}-t)\left(x_{0}-E(t_{0}-t)x\right),\left(x_{0}-E(t_{0}-t)x\right)\rangle=0.

Keeping in mind the definition of
\mathcal{P}_{z_{0}}
in (
4.1
), this proves the assertion.
∎

5.

\langle C^{-1}(t_{0}-t)\left(x_{0}-E(t_{0}-t)x\right),\left(x_{0}-E(t_{0}-t)x\right)\rangle<1

Fix also an arbitrary point
\zeta=(\xi,\tau)\in\Omega^{(p)}_{V_{p}(t_{0}-t)}(x,t)
. This is equivalent of saying that
\tau<t
and

\displaystyle\exp{\left(-\frac{\langle C^{-1}(t-\tau)\left(x-E(t-\tau)\xi\right),\left(x-E(t-\tau)\xi\right)\rangle}{4}\right)}>

\displaystyle>\frac{(4\pi)^{\frac{N+p}{2}}(t-\tau))^{\frac{p}{2}}\sqrt{\det(C(t-\tau))}}{V_{p}(t_{0}-t)}=\frac{V_{p}(t-\tau)}{V_{p}(t_{0}-t)},

that is

(5.5)

\frac{1}{2}\langle C^{-1}(t-\tau)\left(x-E(t-\tau)\xi\right),\left(x-E(t-\tau)\xi\right)\rangle<2\log{\left(\frac{V_{p}(t_{0}-t)}{V_{p}(t-\tau)}\right)}.

We are going to prove that

(5.6)

\frac{1}{2}\langle C^{-1}(t_{0}-\tau)\left(x_{0}-E(t_{0}-\tau)\xi\right),\left(x_{0}-E(t_{0}-\tau)\xi\right)\rangle<2\log{\left(\frac{r^{(p)}_{\theta}(t_{0}-t)}{V_{p}(t_{0}-\tau)}\right)}

under the choice (
5.3
) with

(5.7)

\log{(\theta)}\geq\frac{1}{2}+\frac{2Q_{p}}{k_{0}}\max_{s\in(0,1/2]}\left\{s\log{\left(c_{d}^{\frac{1}{2Q_{p}}}\left(\frac{1}{s}-1\right)\right)}\right\}.

The inequality (
5.6
) is saying that
\zeta\in\Omega^{(p)}_{r^{(p)}_{\theta}(t_{0}-t)}(z_{0})
: once this is established, the proof of the desired statement will be complete. Let us thus show (
5.6
). As we keep in mind that
\tau<t<t_{0}
, we denote

\mu:=\frac{t-\tau}{t_{0}-\tau}>0.

Since
\zeta
satisfies (
5.5
) and
C^{-1}(t-\tau)
is positive definite, we have in particular that
V_{p}(t_{0}-t)>V_{p}(t-\tau)
: since the function
V_{p}(\cdot)
is increasing, we infer that
t_{0}-t>t-\tau
. Hence, we have

1<\frac{t_{0}-t}{t-\tau}=\frac{1}{\mu}-1\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad\mu\in\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right).

Then, as
\frac{1}{1-\mu}<2
, by (
5.3
) and the doubling condition (
3.20
) we obtain

(5.8)

\frac{r^{(p)}_{\theta}(t_{0}-t)}{V_{p}(t_{0}-\tau)}=\frac{r^{(p)}_{\theta}(t_{0}-t)}{V_{p}(\frac{1}{1-\mu}(t_{0}-t))}>\frac{r^{(p)}_{\theta}(t_{0}-t)}{V_{p}(2(t_{0}-t))}\geq\frac{r^{(p)}_{\theta}(t_{0}-t)}{2^{\frac{p}{2}}\sqrt{c_{d}}V_{p}((t_{0}-t))}\geq\theta.

Also, since
\frac{\mu}{1-\mu}<1
, we can use (
3.23
) to deduce

(5.9)

\frac{V_{p}(t_{0}-t)}{V_{p}(t-\tau)}=\frac{V_{p}(t_{0}-t)}{V_{p}(\frac{\mu}{1-\mu}(t_{0}-t))}\leq\sqrt{c_{d}}\left(\frac{t_{0}-t}{\frac{\mu}{1-\mu}(t_{0}-t)}\right)^{Q_{p}}=\sqrt{c_{d}}\left(\frac{1}{\mu}-1\right)^{Q_{p}}.

Let us now consider the left-hand side in (
5.6
). By using (
4.2
) we have

(5.10)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\langle C^{-1}(t_{0}-\tau)\left(x_{0}-E(t_{0}-\tau)\xi\right),\left(x_{0}-E(t_{0}-\tau)\xi\right)\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\langle C^{-1}(t_{0}-\tau)\left(x_{0}-E(t_{0}-t)x\right),\left(x_{0}-E(t_{0}-t)x\right)\rangle+

\displaystyle+\langle C^{-1}(t_{0}-\tau)\left(E(t_{0}-t)x-E(t_{0}-\tau)\xi\right),\left(x_{0}-E(t_{0}-t)x\right)\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle C^{-1}(t_{0}-\tau)\left(x_{0}-E(t_{0}-t)x\right),\left(x_{0}-E(t_{0}-t)x\right)\rangle+

\displaystyle+\langle\left(E(t_{0}-t)^{T}C^{-1}(t_{0}-\tau)E(t_{0}-t)\right)\left(x-E(t-\tau)\xi\right),\left(x-E(t-\tau)\xi\right)\rangle.

On one hand, since
C(t_{0}-\tau)\geq C(t_{0}-t)
we have
C^{-1}(t_{0}-\tau)\leq C^{-1}(t_{0}-t)
(keep in mind (
3.18
)) and then

(5.11)

\displaystyle\langle C^{-1}(t_{0}-\tau)\left(x_{0}-E(t_{0}-t)x\right),\left(x_{0}-E(t_{0}-t)x\right)\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\langle C^{-1}(t_{0}-t)\left(x_{0}-E(t_{0}-t)x\right),\left(x_{0}-E(t_{0}-t)x\right)\rangle<1,

where the last inequality comes from (
5.4
). On the other hand, since
t_{0}-\tau\geq t_{0}-t\geq 1
, we can exploit (
3.25
) in order to have

E(t_{0}-\tau)^{T}C^{-1}(t_{0}-\tau)E(t_{0}-\tau)\leq\frac{\mu}{k_{0}}E(\mu(t_{0}-\tau))^{T}C^{-1}(\mu(t_{0}-\tau))E(\mu(t_{0}-\tau)),

or equivalently

E(t_{0}-t)^{T}C^{-1}(t_{0}-\tau)E(t_{0}-t)\leq\frac{\mu}{k_{0}}C^{-1}(t-\tau).

If we use the previous matrix inequality, together with the assumption (
5.5
) and the bound (
5.9
) we deduce

(5.12)

\displaystyle\langle\left(E(t_{0}-t)^{T}C^{-1}(t_{0}-\tau)E(t_{0}-t)\right)\left(x-E(t-\tau)\xi\right),\left(x-E(t-\tau)\xi\right)\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mu}{k_{0}}\langle C^{-1}(t-\tau)\left(x-E(t-\tau)\xi\right),\left(x-E(t-\tau)\xi\right)\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4\mu}{k_{0}}\log{\left(\frac{V_{p}(t_{0}-t)}{V_{p}(t-\tau)}\right)}\leq\frac{4Q_{p}}{k_{0}}\mu\log{\left(c_{d}^{\frac{1}{2Q_{p}}}\left(\frac{1}{\mu}-1\right)\right)}.

If we plug (
5.11
) and (
5.12
) into (
5.10
) and we use the choice in (
5.7
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\langle C^{-1}(t_{0}-\tau)\left(x_{0}-E(t_{0}-\tau)\xi\right),\left(x_{0}-E(t_{0}-\tau)\xi\right)\rangle

\displaystyle<1+\frac{4Q_{p}}{k_{0}}\mu\log{\left(c_{d}^{\frac{1}{2Q_{p}}}\left(\frac{1}{\mu}-1\right)\right)}\leq 2\log{(\theta)}

\displaystyle\leq 2\log{\left(\frac{r^{(p)}_{\theta}(t_{0}-t)}{V_{p}(t_{0}-\tau)}\right)}

where in the final inequality we have exploited the lower bound in (
5.8
). This completes the proof of (
5.6
), and of the lemma.
∎

6.
Kernel bounds

Aim of this section is to prove the following lemma, the main step toward the proof of Theorem
1.2
. Let
p\in\mathbb{N}
,
p>2+4n_{0}
, where
n_{0}
is the natural number at the last left hand side of (
3.1
). Then, the following lemma holds

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
p\in\mathbb{N}
be as above. There exist
\bar{\theta}>1
and
c>0
such that the following holds: for every
t\leq t_{0}-1
and for every
x\in\Sigma_{(t_{0}-t)}(z_{0})
we have

\frac{W_{r^{(p)}_{\bar{\theta}}(t_{0}-t)}^{(p)}(z_{0};\zeta)}{W_{V_{p}(t_{0}-t)}^{(p)}(z;\zeta)}\geq c\qquad\mbox{ for all }\zeta\in\Omega^{(p)}_{V_{p}(t_{0}-t)}(x,t),

where
r^{(p)}_{\bar{\theta}}(t_{0}-t)
is fixed as in (
5.3
).

Proof.

Let us fix

(6.1)

\displaystyle\bar{\theta}=2\theta,\qquad

where
\theta
is the one in Lemma
5.1

for the choice of
p
fixed at the beginning of this section.

Let
t\leq t_{0}-1
,
x\in\Sigma_{(t_{0}-t)}(z_{0})
, and
\zeta=(\xi,\tau)\in\Omega^{(p)}_{V_{p}(t_{0}-t)}(x,t)
. If we keep in mind the definitions of
W^{(p)}_{r}
and
R_{r}
, by (
5.2
) and (
5.3
) we have

\displaystyle W_{r^{(p)}_{\bar{\theta}}(t_{0}-t)}^{(p)}(z_{0};\zeta)\geq\frac{p\omega_{p}}{4(p+2)}\frac{R^{p+2}_{r^{(p)}_{\bar{\theta}}(t_{0}-t)}(z_{0};\zeta)}{(t_{0}-\tau)^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{p\omega_{p}2^{p+2}}{4(p+2)}(t_{0}-\tau)^{\frac{p+2}{2}-2}\left(\log{\left(r^{(p)}_{\bar{\theta}}(t_{0}-t)(4\pi(t_{0}-\tau))^{-\frac{p}{2}}\Gamma(z_{0};\zeta)\right)}\right)^{\frac{p+2}{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{p\omega_{p}2^{p}}{p+2}(t_{0}-\tau)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\left(\log{\left(\frac{\bar{\theta}}{\theta}r^{(p)}_{\theta}(t_{0}-t)(4\pi(t_{0}-\tau))^{-\frac{p}{2}}\Gamma(z_{0};\zeta)\right)}\right)^{\frac{p+2}{2}}

\displaystyle>\frac{p\omega_{p}2^{p}}{p+2}(t_{0}-\tau)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\log^{\frac{p+2}{2}}{\left(\frac{\bar{\theta}}{\theta}\right)}.

Since
p\geq 2
and
\tau<t
, the previous inequality yields

(6.2)

W_{r^{(p)}_{\bar{\theta}}(t_{0}-t)}^{(p)}(z_{0};\zeta)\geq\frac{p\omega_{p}2^{p}}{p+2}(\log{2})^{\frac{p+2}{2}}(t_{0}-t)^{\frac{p-2}{2}},

where we used the choice (
6.1
). As far as the upper bound is concerned we first write

W_{V_{p}(t_{0}-t)}^{(p)}(z;\zeta)=\omega_{p}R^{p}_{V_{p}(t_{0}-t)}(z;\zeta)W(z;\zeta)+\frac{p\omega_{p}}{4(p+2)}\frac{R^{p+2}_{V_{p}(t_{0}-t)}(z;\zeta)}{(t-\tau)^{2}}.

From the definition of
V_{p}
we have

(6.3)

\displaystyle\frac{p\omega_{p}}{4(p+2)}\frac{R^{p+2}_{V_{p}(t_{0}-t)}(z;\zeta)}{(t-\tau)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{p\omega_{p}2^{p+2}}{4(p+2)}(t-\tau)^{\frac{p+2}{2}-2}\left(\log{\left(V_{p}(t_{0}-t)(4\pi(t-\tau))^{-\frac{p}{2}}\Gamma(z;\zeta)\right)}\right)^{\frac{p+2}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{p\omega_{p}2^{p}}{p+2}(t-\tau)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\left(\log{\left(\frac{V_{p}(t_{0}-t)}{V_{p}(t-\tau)}\right)}\right)^{\frac{p+2}{2}}.

On the other hand, denoting by
\Lambda_{A}
the largest eigenvalue of
A
, we have

(6.4)

\displaystyle\omega_{p}R^{p}_{V_{p}(t_{0}-t)}(z;\zeta)W(z;\zeta)

\displaystyle=\omega_{p}2^{p}(t-\tau)^{\frac{p}{2}}\left(\log{\left(V_{p}(t_{0}-t)(4\pi(t-\tau))^{-\frac{p}{2}}\Gamma(z;\zeta)\right)}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}W(z;\zeta)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\Lambda_{A}\omega_{p}2^{p}}{4}(t-\tau)^{\frac{p}{2}}\left(\log{\left(\frac{V_{p}(t_{0}-t)}{V_{p}(t-\tau)}\right)}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\left|\left(E(\tau-t)C(t-\tau)E(\tau-t)^{T}\right)^{-1}\left(\xi-E(\tau-t)x\right)\right|^{2}

With the analogous notation for
\Lambda_{M}
, we now use the fact that

(6.5)

\left|Mv\right|^{2}=\left\langle M\sqrt{M}v,\sqrt{M}v\right\rangle\leq\Lambda_{M}\left|\sqrt{M}v\right|^{2}=\Lambda_{M}\left\langle Mv,v\right\rangle

for any symmetric non-negative definite
M
. In case
t-\tau\geq 1
, we can use (
3.15
) into (
6.5
) with the choice
M=\left(E(\tau-t)C(t-\tau)E(\tau-t)^{T}\right)^{-1}
and plug this estimate in (
6.4
): this yields

\displaystyle\omega_{p}R^{p}_{V_{p}(t_{0}-t)}(z;\zeta)W(z;\zeta)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\Lambda_{A}\omega_{p}2^{p}}{4c_{+}}(t-\tau)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\left(\log{\left(\frac{V_{p}(t_{0}-t)}{V_{p}(t-\tau)}\right)}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\left\langle\left(E(\tau-t)C(t-\tau)E(\tau-t)^{T}\right)^{-1}\left(\xi-E(\tau-t)x\right),\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 344.27852pt\left.\vphantom{\left(E(\tau-t)C(t-\tau)E(\tau-t)^{T}\right)^{-1}}\xi-E(\tau-t)x\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{\Lambda_{A}\omega_{p}2^{p}}{4c_{+}}(t-\tau)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\left(\log{\left(\frac{V_{p}(t_{0}-t)}{V_{p}(t-\tau)}\right)}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\left\langle C^{-1}(t-\tau)\left(x-E(t-\tau)\xi\right),x-E(t-\tau)\xi\right\rangle

\displaystyle<\frac{\Lambda_{A}\omega_{p}2^{p}}{c_{+}}(t-\tau)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\left(\log{\left(\frac{V_{p}(t_{0}-t)}{V_{p}(t-\tau)}\right)}\right)^{\frac{p+2}{2}},

where in the last inequality we have used (
5.5
) (as we have fixed
\zeta\in\Omega^{(p)}_{V_{p}(t_{0}-t)}(x,t)
). Combining the last estimate with (
6.3
) we have

(6.6)

W_{V_{p}(t_{0}-t)}^{(p)}(z;\zeta)\leq C_{p}(t-\tau)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\left(\log{\left(\frac{V_{p}(t_{0}-t)}{V_{p}(t-\tau)}\right)}\right)^{\frac{p+2}{2}}

where

C_{p}=\omega_{p}2^{p}\left(\frac{\Lambda_{A}}{c_{+}}+\frac{p}{p+2}\right).

Let us manipulate further the upper bound in (
6.6
). To this aim, we keep the same notations as in Lemma
5.1
by letting

\mu:=\frac{t-\tau}{t_{0}-\tau}\in(0,\frac{1}{2})\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad\frac{\mu}{1-\mu}=\frac{t-\tau}{t_{0}-t}\in(0,1).

By using again (
5.9
), from (
6.6

\displaystyle W_{V_{p}(t_{0}-t)}^{(p)}(z;\zeta)

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}(t_{0}-t)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\left(\frac{t-\tau}{t_{0}-t}\right)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\left(\log{\left(\frac{V_{p}(t_{0}-t)}{V_{p}(t-\tau)}\right)}\right)^{\frac{p+2}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}(t_{0}-t)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\left(\frac{\mu}{1-\mu}\right)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\left(\log{\left(\sqrt{c_{d}}\left(\frac{1-\mu}{\mu}\right)^{Q_{p}}\right)}\right)^{\frac{p+2}{2}}

\displaystyle=C_{p}Q_{p}^{\frac{p+2}{2}}(t_{0}-t)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\left(\frac{\mu}{1-\mu}\right)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\left(\log{\left(c_{d}^{\frac{1}{2Q_{p}}}\frac{1-\mu}{\mu}\right)}\right)^{\frac{p+2}{2}}.

By letting

M_{p}:=\max_{s\in(0,1)}\left\{s^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\left(\log{\left(c_{d}^{\frac{1}{2Q_{p}}}s^{-1}\right)}\right)^{\frac{p+2}{2}}\right\}

(which is finite since

(6.7)

W_{V_{p}(t_{0}-t)}^{(p)}(z;\zeta)\leq M_{p}C_{p}Q_{p}^{\frac{p+2}{2}}(t_{0}-t)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}.

This was the case where
t-\tau\geq 1
. In case
0<t-\tau<1
, we can instead use (
3.1
) (with
T_{0}=1
) into (
6.5
) with the same choice as before
M=\left(E(\tau-t)C(t-\tau)E(\tau-t)^{T}\right)^{-1}
and plug this different estimate in (
6.4
): this yields

\displaystyle\omega_{p}R^{p}_{V_{p}(t_{0}-t)}(z;\zeta)W(z;\zeta)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\Lambda_{A}\omega_{p}2^{p}K(1)}{4}(t-\tau)^{\frac{p-2-4n_{0}}{2}}\left(\log{\left(\frac{V_{p}(t_{0}-t)}{V_{p}(t-\tau)}\right)}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\left\langle C^{-1}(t-\tau)\left(x-E\omega_{p}(t-\tau)\xi\right),x-E(t-\tau)\xi\right\rangle

\displaystyle<\Lambda_{A}\omega_{p}2^{p}K(1)(t-\tau)^{\frac{p-2-4n_{0}}{2}}\left(\log{\left(\frac{V_{p}(t_{0}-t)}{V_{p}(t-\tau)}\right)}\right)^{\frac{p+2}{2}}.

Combining the last estimate with (
6.3
) and letting

C^{\prime}_{p}=\omega_{p}2^{p}\max\left\{\frac{p}{p+2},\Lambda_{A}K(1)\right\},

we now have

(6.8)

\displaystyle W_{V_{p}(t_{0}-t)}^{(p)}(z;\zeta)\leq C^{\prime}_{p}\left((t-\tau)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}+(t-\tau)^{\frac{p-2-4n_{0}}{2}}\right)\left(\log{\left(\frac{V_{p}(t_{0}-t)}{V_{p}(t-\tau)}\right)}\right)^{\frac{p+2}{2}}

\displaystyle=C^{\prime}_{p}\left((t_{0}-t)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\left(\frac{t-\tau}{t_{0}-t}\right)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}+(t_{0}-t)^{\frac{p-2-4n_{0}}{2}}\left(\frac{t-\tau}{t_{0}-t}\right)^{\frac{p-2-4n_{0}}{2}}\right)\left(\log{\left(\frac{V_{p}(t_{0}-t)}{V_{p}(t-\tau)}\right)}\right)^{\frac{p+2}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}_{p}(t_{0}-t)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\left(\left(\frac{t-\tau}{t_{0}-t}\right)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}+\left(\frac{t-\tau}{t_{0}-t}\right)^{\frac{p-2-4n_{0}}{2}}\right)\left(\log{\left(\frac{V_{p}(t_{0}-t)}{V_{p}(t-\tau)}\right)}\right)^{\frac{p+2}{2}}

where in the last inequality we used that
t_{0}-t\geq 1
. We can now argue as before and deduce that

\displaystyle W_{V_{p}(t_{0}-t)}^{(p)}(z;\zeta)\leq C^{\prime}_{p}(t_{0}-t)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\left(\left(\frac{\mu}{1-\mu}\right)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}+\left(\frac{\mu}{1-\mu}\right)^{\frac{p-2-4n_{0}}{2}}\right)\left(\log{\left(\sqrt{c_{d}}\left(\frac{1-\mu}{\mu}\right)^{Q_{p}}\right)}\right)^{\frac{p+2}{2}}

\displaystyle=C^{\prime}_{p}Q_{p}^{\frac{p+2}{2}}(t_{0}-t)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\left(\left(\frac{\mu}{1-\mu}\right)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}+\left(\frac{\mu}{1-\mu}\right)^{\frac{p-2-4n_{0}}{2}}\right)\left(\log{\left(c_{d}^{\frac{1}{2Q_{p}}}\frac{1-\mu}{\mu}\right)}\right)^{\frac{p+2}{2}}.

(6.9)

W_{V_{p}(t_{0}-t)}^{(p)}(z;\zeta)\leq(M_{p}+M^{\prime}_{p})C^{\prime}_{p}Q_{p}^{\frac{p+2}{2}}(t_{0}-t)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}

also in the case

. If we compare (
6.7
) and (

) we realize that (regardless of the time coordinate of

) we have

W_{V_{p}(t_{0}-t)}^{(p)}(z;\zeta)\leq(M_{p}+M^{\prime}_{p})\max\{C_{p},C^{\prime}_{p}\}Q_{p}^{\frac{p+2}{2}}(t_{0}-t)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}.

By putting together the last upper bound with (

) we finally deduce

\frac{W_{r^{(p)}_{\bar{\theta}}(t_{0}-t)}^{(p)}(z_{0};\zeta)}{W_{V_{p}(t_{0}-t)}^{(p)}(z;\zeta)}\geq\frac{p\omega_{p}2^{p}}{(p+2)(M_{p}+M^{\prime}_{p})\max\{C_{p},C^{\prime}_{p}\}}\left(\frac{\log{2}}{Q_{p}}\right)^{\frac{p+2}{2}}=:c

as desired.

7.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

Let
u
be a smooth bounded below solution to
\mathcal{K}u=0
in
{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}
. Then

m:=\inf_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}u

is a real number and

\hat{u}:=u-m

is a smooth non-negative solution to
\mathcal{K}\hat{u}=0
in
{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}
such that

\inf_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}\hat{u}=0.

Then, for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists
z_{\varepsilon}=(x_{\varepsilon},t_{\varepsilon})\in{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}
such that

(7.1)

\hat{u}(z_{\varepsilon})<\varepsilon.

Let us now consider the paraboloid
\mathcal{P}_{z_{\varepsilon}}
defined in (
4.1
). By Corollary
4.1
, for every fixed
x\in{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}
there exists
T=(x,z_{\varepsilon})<t_{\varepsilon}
such that

(7.2)

z:=(x,t)\in\mathcal{P}_{z_{\varepsilon}}\quad\forall\ t<T.

We may (and we do) assume
T<t_{\varepsilon}-1
. We want to prove the following
Harnack-type inequality
:

there exists a constant
c^{\ast}>0
, independent of
\varepsilon
, such that

(7.3)

\hat{u}(z_{\varepsilon})\geq c^{*}\hat{u}(z)\quad\forall\ z=(x,t)\,\mbox{ with }t<T.

We stress that, by (
7.2
), condition
t<T
implies
(x,t)\in\mathcal{P}_{z_{\varepsilon}}.
By the Mean Value formula (
2.8
) we have

(7.4)

\hat{u}(z_{\varepsilon})=\frac{1}{r^{(p)}_{\bar{\theta}}(t_{\varepsilon}-t)}\int_{\Omega^{(p)}_{r^{(p)}_{\bar{\theta}}(t_{\varepsilon}-t)}(z_{\varepsilon})}\hat{u}(\zeta)W_{r^{(p)}_{\bar{\theta}}(t_{\varepsilon}-t)}^{(p)}(z_{\varepsilon};\zeta)d\zeta,

where
r^{(p)}_{\bar{\theta}}(\cdot)
is given in Lemma
6.1
. Keep in mind that we have chosen
p\in\mathbb{N}
such that

p>2+4n_{0}
, as in Lemma
6.1
. We also stress that, by our construction, we have
t\leq t_{\varepsilon}-1
and
x\in\Sigma_{(t_{\varepsilon}-t)}(z_{\varepsilon})
. Hence, by Lemma
5.1
(and (
6.1
)) we have the inclusion

\Omega^{(p)}_{V_{p}(t_{\varepsilon}-t)}(x,t)\subseteq\Omega^{(p)}_{\frac{1}{2}r^{(p)}_{\bar{\theta}}(t_{\varepsilon}-t)}(z_{\varepsilon})\subseteq\Omega^{(p)}_{r^{(p)}_{\bar{\theta}}(t_{\varepsilon}-t)}(z_{\varepsilon}),

being
V_{p}(\cdot)
defined in (
2.3
). Then, since
\hat{u}\geq 0,
from (
7.4
) we get

\hat{u}(z_{\varepsilon})\geq\frac{1}{r^{(p)}_{\bar{\theta}}(t_{\varepsilon}-t)}\int_{\Omega^{(p)}_{V_{p}(t_{\varepsilon}-t)}(x,t)}\hat{u}(\zeta)W_{r^{(p)}_{\bar{\theta}}(t_{\varepsilon}-t)}^{(p)}(z_{\varepsilon};\zeta)d\zeta.

This implies, by (
5.3
), by Lemma
6.1
, and again by the Mean Value formula, that

\displaystyle\hat{u}(z_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle c^{*}\frac{1}{V_{p}(t_{\varepsilon}-t)}\int_{\Omega^{(p)}_{V_{p}(t_{\varepsilon}-t)}(x,t)}\hat{u}(\zeta)W_{V_{p}(t_{\varepsilon}-t)}^{(p)}(z;\zeta)d\zeta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c^{*}\hat{u}(z).

Here
c^{*}=\frac{c}{\bar{\theta}2^{\frac{p}{2}}\sqrt{c_{d}}}
, where
c
and
\bar{\theta}
are the absolute constants in Lemma
6.1
, and
c_{d}
is the absolute constants in Corollary
3.3
. Thus, (
7.3
) is proved.

The inequality (
7.3
), together with (
7.1
), gives

\displaystyle\hat{u}(x,t)<\frac{\varepsilon}{c^{*}}.

Summing up, we have established the following:

for every
\varepsilon>0
and for every
x\in{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
, there exists
T\in\mathbb{R}
, depending on
\varepsilon
and
x
, such that

0\leq\hat{u}(x,t)<\varepsilon\mbox{ for }t<T.

Hence,

\lim_{t\to-\infty}\hat{u}(x,t)=0\quad\forall\ x\in{\mathbb{R}^{N}},

i.e.

\lim_{t\to-\infty}u(x,t)=m\quad\forall\ x\in{\mathbb{R}^{N}}.

This completes the proof of Theorem
1.2

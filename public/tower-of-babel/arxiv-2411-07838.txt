Two-scale density of almost smooth functions in sphere-valued Sobolev spaces: A high-contrast extension of the Bethuel-Zheng theory
Elisa Davoli, Leon Happ

Abstract
. 1.
Introduction

Approximating manifold-valued Sobolev mappings by smooth functions is a classical question. One of the seminal results in this direction is the theory established by F. Bethuel and X.M. Zheng in
[
5
]
. On the one hand, in the setting of
W^{1,2}(B;\mathbb{S}^{2})
(where
B
denotes the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
centered in the origin), smooth approximations in the strong
W^{1,2}
-topology are not possible (cf. Theorem 2 in
[
5
]
and Section 4 in
[
25
]
; see also Theorem 2.3 and Theorem 2.6 in
[
18
]
). On the other hand, the authors prove in
[
5
]
that each map in
W^{1,2}(B;\mathbb{S}^{2})
can be approximated by almost smooth maps, namely maps which are smooth in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
up to a finite number of points.

The first contribution of this paper is to investigate whether this latter density result carries over to the setting in which the functions under consideration exhibit both a macroscopic and a microscopic scale, and the strong
W^{1,2}
-topology is replaced by strong two-scale convergence, cf. Section
2
.

To be precise, denoting by
\Omega
a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and by
Q_{0}
an open subset of the unit cube
Q=(0,1)^{3}
, we consider maps in
L^{2}(\Omega;W^{1,2}(Q;\mathbb{R}^{3}))
exhibiting a second scale depencence localized in
Q_{0}
. Our main result is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\tilde{m}\in W^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{2})
and
w\in L^{2}(\Omega;W^{1,2}_{0}(Q_{0};\mathbb{R}^{3}))
be such that

\tilde{m}(x)+w(x,z)\in\mathbb{S}^{2}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for a.e. }(x,z)\in\Omega\times Q_{0}.

Then, there exists a sequence of functions
(m_{k})_{k}\subset W^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{2})
such that

(1.1)

\displaystyle m_{k}\overset{2s}{\to}\tilde{m}(x)+w(x,z)\chi^{0}(z)

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\text{in }L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{2}),

(1.2)

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{k}\nabla m_{k}\overset{2s}{\to}\nabla_{z}w(x,z)\chi^{0}(z)

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\text{in }L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}).

Moreover, there holds

m_{k}\in C^{\infty}(\Omega\setminus\{a_{k_{1}},\dots,a_{k_{l}}\};\mathbb{S}^{2}),

i.e., the functions
m_{k}
are smooth up to a finite number of points, where the points themselves and the number
k_{l}
depend on the index
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

In comparison with
[
5
, Theorem 4]
, the constructed approximants converge in a weaker norm but (
1.2
) provides a finer characterization of the limiting oscillations. The proof methodology stems from a combination of the Sard’s lemma application and the projection analysis highlighted in
[
5
, Theorem 4]
with two-scale convergence and unfolding. Relying on these latter tools, we actually prove a slightly refined version of Theorem
1.1
(cf. Theorem
3.2
), which serves as starting point of the high-contrast analysis presented in the second part of the paper.

The mathematical modeling of high-contrast composites has been attracting increasing interest over the last couple of years. From a variational point of view, this question can be phrased as identifying the
\Gamma
-limit in the sense of
[
12
]
of energy functionals modeling highly heterogeneous composites, and exhibiting a drastical difference (high contrast) in the material properties of their constituents. This leads, in particular, to a loss of coercivity of the respective energy functionals, so that limiting nonlinear theories are not always available while working with classical weak or strong Sobolev convergence, but often require turning to two-scale adaptations instead (
[
9
]
,
[
15
]
,
[
14
]
).

We consider here a high-contrast toy-problem motivated by the theory of micromagnetics (cf.
[
19
]
,
[
8
]
). In order to describe our results we need to introduce some notation. Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a bounded domain representing a ferromagnetic body.
Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an open, connected, and periodic set with Lipschitz boundary. The assumption that
E
is periodic in this setting means that

E+e_{i}=E\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ for all }i=1,2,3,

with
(e_{i})_{i=1,2,3}
the canonical basis of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. We then define
Q_{1}\coloneqq Q\cap E
and
Q_{0}\coloneqq Q\setminus\overline{Q_{1}}
. Let further

(1.3)

Z_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq\{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}:\varepsilon(\overline{Q_{0}}+z)\subset\Omega\}

encode those lattice points in
\mathbb{Z}^{3}
such that the sets
\varepsilon(\overline{Q_{0}}+z)
are well-separated from the boundary of
\Omega
. We finally define the two constituents of the composite on the scale
\varepsilon>0
as

(1.4)

{\Omega^{0}_{\varepsilon}}\coloneqq\bigcup_{z\in Z_{\varepsilon}}\varepsilon(Q_{0}+z).

and

(1.5)

{\Omega^{1}_{\varepsilon}}\coloneqq\Omega\setminus\overline{{\Omega^{0}_{\varepsilon}}}.

This construction amounts to cutting out from some homogeneous sample
\Omega
holes constituted by all those
\varepsilon(Q_{0}+z)
that are compactly contained in
\Omega
, and then filling them again with a different material than the one that makes up
{\Omega^{1}_{\varepsilon}}
.

The micromagnetic behavior of
\Omega
is encoded by the functionals

(1.6)

\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}(u,m):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\Omega^{1}_{\varepsilon}}}|\nabla m|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}x}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\Omega^{0}_{\varepsilon}}}\varepsilon^{2}|\nabla m|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}x}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|h_{d}[m]|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}x},

where
m
, describing the magnetization of the body, is assumed to satisfy a saturation constraint and belong to the class of functions

W^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{2})\coloneqq\{m\in W^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3}):\ m(x)\in\mathbb{S}^{2}\text{ for a.e. }x\in\Omega\}.

The first two terms in (
1.6
) describe symmetric exchanges and favour constant magnetizations (with very different strength) in the two portion of the specimen. The third energy contribution, instead, has a nonlocal character, encodying the effects of the stray field. The demagnetizing field
h_{d}[m]
is defined as the image of the linear and continuous operator

(1.7)

h_{d}:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3};\mathbb{R}^{3})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3};\mathbb{R}^{3}),

which is uniquely determined (cf.
[
3
]
,
[
24
]
) through the magneto-static Maxwell equations

(1.8)

\begin{cases}-\diver h_{d}[m]=\diver m\chi_{\Omega},\\
\nabla\times h_{d}[m]=0\end{cases}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{3}.

In other words, the pair
(m\chi_{\Omega},h_{d}[m])
fulfils

(1.9)

\mathcal{A}(m\chi_{\Omega},h_{d}[m])\coloneqq\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\diver&\diver\\
0&\curl\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}m\chi_{\Omega}\\
h_{d}[m]\end{array}\right)=0

in the distributional sense, i.e., it lies in the kernel of the associated constant rank differential operator
\mathcal{A}
.
The demagnetizing fields satisfies
\|h_{d}[m]\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq\|m\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq|\Omega|
(the Lebesgue measure of
\Omega
) for all
m\in W^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{2})
, and the magneto-static energy in (
1.6
) is equivalently expressed as

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|h_{d}[m]|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}x}=-\int_{\Omega}h_{d}[m]\cdot m\,{\mathrm{d}x}.

We neglect here anisotropy contributions, external fields, as well as antisymmetric exchanges, for they would not modify substantially the analysis, acting as lower order perturbations.

A first difficulty in the identification of the high-contrast limit of
\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}
is due to the presence of the saturation constraint. As already observed in
[
23
]
, in the absence of Sobolev coercivity for the admissible magnetizations, a relaxation of the saturation constraint occurs in the energy both for the magnetizations and for the admissible stray fields.
The non-local nature of the stray-field energy further deeply affects the properties of the
\Gamma
-limit of
(\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
in comparison to former results in high-contrast homogenization. In particular, in the setting analyzed here, the energy cannot be split into two separate contributions, depending only from the inclusions, or only from the matrix portion of the composite, respectively. It rather exhibits, in the limiting stray-field behavior, nontrivial coupling effects. As a result, the influence of the
\varepsilon
-inclusions cannot be averaged out in the homogenized energy density: The coupling of the two material components through the non-local energy contribution thus calls for a tracking of the fast variable in the choice of the underlying convergence.

Definition 1.2

(Convergence in the sense of extensions)
.

Let
(\varepsilon_{k})_{k}
be an infinitesimal sequence. We say that
(f_{k})_{k}\subset W^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})
converges to
f\in W^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})

in the sense of extensions
, with respect to the scales
(\varepsilon_{k})_{k}
, if

(i)

(f_{k})_{k}
is bounded in
L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})
and

(ii)

there exists a sequence
(\tilde{f}_{k})_{k}\subset W^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})
such that
\tilde{f}_{k}=f_{k}
in
\Omega_{k}^{1}
and

(1.10)

\tilde{f_{k}}\rightharpoonup f\hskip 20.00003pt\text{in }W^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3}).

Definition 1.3

(High contrast convergence)
.

Let
(\varepsilon_{k})_{k}
be an infinitesimal sequence. Then
(m_{k})_{k}\subset W^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})
converges to the tuple
(\tilde{m},w)
for

(1.11)

\tilde{m}\in W^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002ptw\in L^{2}(\Omega;W_{0}^{1,2}(Q_{0};\mathbb{R}^{3}))

in the high contrast sense
if

(i)

(m_{k})_{k}
converges to
\tilde{m}
in the sense of extensions,

(ii)

and

(1.12)

m_{k}\overset{2s}{\rightharpoonup}\tilde{m}+w\hskip 20.00003pt\text{ in }L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3}).

We say that
(m_{k})_{k}\subset W^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})
converges strongly to
(\tilde{m},w)
in the high-contrast sense if
m_{k}\overset{2s}{\to}\tilde{m}+w
in (
1.12
).

Denoting by
Q
the unit cube, in order to formulate our
\Gamma
-limit result we define the mapping (see Lemma
2.8
)

(1.13)

h_{d}^{z}:L^{2}(\Omega\times Q)\to L^{2}(\Omega;L^{2}_{\text{per}}(Q))

through the identity
h_{d}^{z}[m]\coloneqq\nabla_{z}r_{m}
, where
r_{m}\in L^{2}(\Omega;W^{1,2}_{\text{per}}(Q)/\mathbb{R})
and where for almost every
x\in\Omega
the scalar function
r_{m}(x,\cdot)
is the unique solution in
W^{1,2}_{\text{per}}(Q)/\mathbb{R}
of the cell problem

(1.14)

\int_{Q}\nabla_{z}r_{m}(x,z)\cdot\nabla_{z}\psi(z)\,{\mathrm{d}z}=-\int_{Q}m(x,z)\cdot\nabla_{z}\psi(z)\,{\mathrm{d}z}\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\psi\in W^{1,2}_{\text{per}}(Q).

In particular, there holds

(1.15)

\mathcal{A}_{z}(m,h_{d}^{z}[m])\coloneqq\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\diver_{z}&\diver_{z}\\
0&\curl_{z}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}m\\
h_{d}^{z}[m]\end{array}\right)=0\hskip 20.00003pt\text{for a.e. }x\in\Omega

in the distributional sense. We now define the functional

(1.16)

\mathcal{G}(\tilde{m},w)\coloneqq\mathcal{F}^{0}(w)+\mathcal{F}^{1}(\tilde{m})+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathcal{W}_{\hom}(\tilde{m},w)\,{\mathrm{d}x}

with the contributions

(1.17)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mathcal{F}^{0}(w):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\int_{Q_{0}}|\nabla_{z}w|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}z}{\mathrm{d}x},\end{split}

(1.18)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mathcal{F}^{1}(\tilde{m}):=\int_{\Omega}\tilde{f}_{\hom}(\tilde{m},\nabla\tilde{m})\ {\mathrm{d}x},\end{split}

and the homogenized self-energy density

(1.19)

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{\hom}(\tilde{m},w)=|h_{d}[(\tilde{m}+\langle w(x,\cdot)\rangle_{Q_{0}})]|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}x}+\int_{Q_{0}}|h_{d}^{z}[w(x,z)]|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}z}\chi_{\Omega}.

The tangentially homogenized energy density
\tilde{f}_{\hom}
, cf.
[
4
]
, satisfies the cell formula

(1.20)

\tilde{f}_{\hom}(s,\xi)=\inf_{\varphi\in W^{1,2}_{\text{per}}(Q;T_{s}\mathbb{S}^{2})}\frac{1}{2}\int_{Q_{1}}|\xi+\nabla\varphi(z)|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}z}.

It is called "tangentially homogenized" because we consider the infimum over all functions in
W^{1,2}_{\text{per}}(Q;T_{s}\mathbb{S}^{2})
with values in the tangent space
T_{s}\mathbb{S}^{2}
of the sphere. We prove the following characterization.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\mathcal{G}:W^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})\times L^{2}(\Omega;W_{0}^{1,2}(Q_{0};\mathbb{R}^{3}))\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
be as in (
1.16
). Then:

(i)

For any
(\tilde{m},w)\in W^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{2})\times L^{2}(\Omega;W_{0}^{1,2}(Q_{0};\mathbb{R}^{3}))
and for any sequence
(m_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}\subset W^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{2})
that converges to
(\tilde{m},w)
in the strong high-contrast sense, there holds

\mathcal{G}(\tilde{m},w)\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}(m_{\varepsilon}).

(ii)

For any
(\tilde{m},w)\in W^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{2})\times L^{2}(\Omega;W_{0}^{1,2}(Q_{0};\mathbb{R}^{3}))
there exists a sequence
(m_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}\subset W^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{2})
that converges to
(\tilde{m},w)
in the strong high-contrast sense and that satisfies

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}(m_{\varepsilon})\leq\mathcal{G}(\tilde{m},w).

We stress that the infimum problem in (
1.20
) admits a unique minimizer. Define
\varphi\in W^{1,2}_{\text{per}}(Q;\mathbb{R}^{3})
as the unique solution to the Poisson equation on the unit cell (where we write
W^{-1,2}_{\text{per}}(Q;\mathbb{R}^{3})
for the dual space of
W^{1,2}_{\text{per}}(Q;\mathbb{R}^{3})
)

-\Delta\varphi=\chi_{Q_{1}}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{in }W^{-1,2}_{\text{per}}(Q;\mathbb{R}^{3}).

One can show, using the Lax-Milgram theorem (cf.
[
13
, Proposition 2.1]
) that if
(\tau_{1}(s),\tau_{2}(s))
constitute an orthonormal basis of
T_{s}\mathbb{S}^{2}
, the unique minimizer of (
1.20
) can be written as

\phi[s,\xi]\coloneqq[\varphi\cdot(\xi\tau_{1}(s))]\tau_{1}(s)+[\varphi\cdot(\xi\tau_{2}(s))]\tau_{2}(s).

The corresponding minimal energy is given by

\tilde{f}_{\hom}(s,\xi)=\frac{1}{2}|Q_{1}||\xi|^{2}-\int_{Q_{1}}\nabla_{z}\phi[s,\xi](z)\,{\mathrm{d}z}.

We believe that the proof of Theorem
1.4
and especially the construction of the corresponding recovery sequence provide interesting insight into the hurdles involved in nonlocal high-contrast problems in manifold-valued Sobolev spaces.
As already mentioned, the entanglement of the magnetization on
{\Omega^{0}_{\varepsilon}}
and
{\Omega^{1}_{\varepsilon}}
prohibits us to reduce the study of the full energies in Theorem
1.4
to the analysis of the isolated contributions from these two different subsets. In fact, we can still follows this path for the difference

\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(u,m):=\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}(u,m)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathcal{W}(m)\,{\mathrm{d}x},

where
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathcal{W}(m)\,{\mathrm{d}x}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|h_{d}[m]|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}x}
,
but additional work is required when putting the full energy back together again. Especially the (approximate) recovery sequence has to account for the different effects of the local and non-local parts of the energy. This is where we rely on the result of Theorem
1.1
(to be precise, we will use a slight generalization that we provide in Theorem
3.2
). The main advantage in separating from the energies
(\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
the exchange contributions on
{\Omega^{1}_{\varepsilon}}
is given by the fact that this allows to trace back its limit behavior to a
\Gamma
-limit in the sense of classical homogenization (see Proposition
4.2
). In our case this is made possible through a special extension operator developed in
[
17
]
that preserves the norm constraint. We recall this result in Section
2.2
.

We conclude this introduction with a final comment about the notion of convergence in Theorem
1.4
. The identification of strong two-scale
\Gamma
-limits in the high-contrast framework dates back to
[
9
]
.
In general, in our setting, we merely know that the functionals
(\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
in (
1.6
) are equi-coercive with respect to the weak high-contrast convergence (by Lemma
2.7
) and a priori not the strong one. Since
W^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{2})
is closed under weak
W^{1,2}
-convergence, we always know that
\tilde{m}\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
almost everywhere. However, the same is in general not true for the sum
\tilde{m}+w
. This is paramount to the fact that
(m_{k})_{k}
cannot converge strongly to
\tilde{m}+w
in
L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})
.

This is due to possible oscillation phenomena, not occurring within the different cells of scale
\varepsilon
, but rather between these cells. To illustrate this phenomenon, consider any arbitrary non-zero smooth function
\phi\in C^{\infty}(Q;\mathbb{S}^{2})
that is equal to a constant vector
s\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
outside a compact set
K\subset Q_{0}\subset Q
. Then tessellate the domain
\Omega
by cubes of size
\varepsilon Q
with alternating copies of
\phi
and
-\phi
. For
\varepsilon\to 0
the resulting magnetic field will weakly two-scale converge to the average (cf.
[
11
, Chapter 2.3]
)

m(x,z)\coloneqq\begin{cases}0\quad&\text{for }z\in K,\\
s\quad&\text{else},\end{cases}

which can also be written

m(x,z)=\tilde{m}(x)+w(x,z)\hskip 20.00003pt\text{for}\hskip 10.00002pt\tilde{m}(x)\coloneqq s,\hskip 10.00002ptw(x,z)\coloneqq\begin{cases}-s\quad&\text{for }z\in K,\\
0\quad&\text{else}.\end{cases}

In particular,
\tilde{m}\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
in
\Omega
, but it is not true that
m=\tilde{m}+w\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
almost everywhere in
\Omega
.
This latter remark suggests that a further relaxation of the energy needs to be undertaken when identifying the
\Gamma
-limit of the functionals
\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}
in the weak high-contrast sense, in the spirit of the analysis performed in
[
23
]
. However, this question is beyond the analysis in this paper and will be the subject of a forthcoming contribution.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we fix some notation, collect the essential properties of two-scale convergence, describe the precise geometries for which the
\Gamma
-limit result in Theorem
1.4
holds, and recall a special extension operator that preserves the norm constraint, and which will be used in the proof of Theorem
1.4
. In Section
3
we prove the approximation Theorem
1.1
in a more general way, already paving the way to apply it in the proof of Theorem
1.4
. Section
4
is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.4
.

2.
Preliminary results

Throughout this paper we denote by
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
an open and bounded set with Lipschitz boundary, and by
Q:=(0,1)^{3}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
the reference unit cell.
For
p\in(1,\infty)
we denote by
q\coloneqq\frac{p}{p-1}
the dual exponent of
p
. As usual, we set
q\coloneqq\infty
for
p=1
.
We use classical notation for Lebesgue and Sobolev spaces. We write

W^{1,2}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R}
to highlight the fact that a specific map is defined up to a real constant.
We will denote with the subscript
per
functions on the unit cube
Q
that have periodic trace on the sides of
Q
. The space
W^{1,2}_{\text{per}}(Q;\mathbb{R}^{3})
coincides with the closure of
C^{\infty}_{\text{per}}(Q;\mathbb{R}^{3})
in the
W^{1,2}
-norm (see for example
[
11
]
for classical properties of periodic Sobolev functions). The characteristic functions of
{\Omega^{0}_{\varepsilon}}
and
{\Omega^{1}_{\varepsilon}}
, equal to one on the respective domain and vanishing outside of them, will be denoted by

(2.1)

\chi_{\varepsilon}^{i}(x)=\begin{cases}1\quad&\text{if }x\in\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{i},\\
0\quad&\text{else},\end{cases}\hskip 20.00003pt\text{ for }i\in\{0,1\}.

Note that due to definition (
1.5
) such characteristic functions are in general not just restrictions of periodic functions to
\Omega
. In the case that we work with a fixed infinitesimal sequence
(\varepsilon_{k})_{k}
, we will refer to the associated characteristic functions by
\chi_{k}^{i}:=\chi_{\varepsilon_{k}}^{i}
. In the same style we will denote by
\chi^{0}
and
\chi^{1}
the characteristic functions of
Q_{0}
and
Q_{1}
.

2.1.
Two-scale convergence

We recall here some classical results on two-scale convergence. The reader familiar with this topic might skip most of this subsection and proceed directly to Lemma
2.7
.

Originally introduced in
[
22
]
and further developed in
[
2
]
, two-scale convergence is a notion of convergence that is able to capture fine-scale periodic oscillations and preserve the information about the oscillations in an additionally emerging micro-scale variable. It naturally allows to pass to the limit in special integral expressions involving products of rapidly oscillating functions.

Definition 2.1

(Weak two-scale convergence (
[
22
,
2
,
21
]
))
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
. We say that a bounded sequence of functions
(u_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}\subset L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})

weakly two-scale converges
in
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
to some limit
u\in L^{p}(\Omega\times Q;\mathbb{R}^{n})
if

(2.2)

\int_{\Omega}u_{\varepsilon}(x)\cdot\psi\left(x,\frac{x}{{\varepsilon_{n}}}\right)\,{\mathrm{d}x}\to\int_{\Omega}\int_{Q}u(x,z)\cdot\psi(x,z)\,{\mathrm{d}z}{\mathrm{d}x}\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\psi\in L^{q}(\Omega;C_{per}(Q;\mathbb{R}^{n})).

We write
u_{\varepsilon}\overset{2s}{\rightharpoonup}u
.

The reason why
L^{q}(\Omega;C_{per}(Q;\mathbb{R}^{n}))
qualifies as the space of test functions in (
2.2
) is twofold. On the one hand, all functions
\psi
in this space are of Carathéodory type and thus
\psi(x,x/\varepsilon)
is again a well-defined measurable function in
L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
; on the other hand, these functions are dense in
L^{q}(\Omega\times Q;\mathbb{R}^{n})
and strongly two-scale converge in the following sense.

Definition 2.2

(Strong two-scale convergence)
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
and
(u_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}\subset L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
be bounded. Then
(u_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}

strongly two-scale converges
in
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
to some
u\in L^{p}(\Omega\times Q;\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and we write
u_{\varepsilon}\overset{2s}{\to}u
, if it converges weakly two-scale to
u
and additionally there holds

(2.3)

\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})}\to\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega\times Q;\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

A connection between two-scale convergence and
L^{p}
-convergence is provided by the so-called unfolding operator.

Definition 2.3

(Unfolding operator (
[
10
,
27
,
28
]
))
.

For
p\in[1,\infty]
and
\varepsilon>0
the
unfolding operator

(2.4)

S_{\varepsilon}:L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times Q;\mathbb{R}^{n})

is defined as

(2.5)

S_{\varepsilon}(u)(x,z):=\hat{u}\left(\varepsilon\bigg\lfloor\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\bigg\rfloor+\varepsilon z\right),

where
\hat{u}
denotes the extension of
u
to
\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\Omega
by
0
.

It follows immediately from the definition of the unfolding operator that
S_{\varepsilon}(u+v)=S_{\varepsilon}(u)+S_{\varepsilon}(v)
and
S_{\varepsilon}(u\cdot w)=S_{\varepsilon}(u)\cdot S_{\varepsilon}(w)
.
A direct computation shows that
S_{\varepsilon}
is a (non-surjective) linear isometry
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times Q;\mathbb{R}^{n})
for any
p\in[1,\infty]
(cf.
[
28
, Lemma 1.1]
). In particular, for
p\in[1,\infty)
there holds

(2.6)

\int_{\Omega}|u(x)|^{p}\,{\mathrm{d}x}=\int_{\Omega}\int_{Q}|S_{\varepsilon}(u)(x,z)|^{p}\,{\mathrm{d}z}{\mathrm{d}x}.

Note that for any function
\psi\in L^{q}(\Omega;C_{\text{per}}(Q;\mathbb{R}^{n}))
and for
\psi_{\varepsilon}(x)\coloneqq\psi(x,x/\varepsilon)
we have by the periodicity in the second variable that

(2.7)

S_{\varepsilon}\left(\psi_{\varepsilon}\right)(x,z)=\psi\left(\varepsilon\bigg\lfloor\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\bigg\rfloor+\varepsilon z,\bigg\lfloor\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\bigg\rfloor+z\right)=\psi\left(\varepsilon\bigg\lfloor\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\bigg\rfloor+\varepsilon z,z\right).

Furthermore, the unfolding operator enjoys the nice property

(2.8)

S_{\varepsilon}(\varepsilon\nabla u)=\nabla_{z}S_{\varepsilon}(u)\hskip 20.00003pt\forall u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n}).

The crucial insight connecting two-scale convergence with the unfolding operator is provided by the following result (cf.
[
28
, Proposition 2.5, Proposition 2.7]
).

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
. A bounded sequence
(u_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}\subset L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
weakly two-scale converges in
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
u\in L^{p}(\Omega\times Q;\mathbb{R}^{n})
iff

(2.9)

S_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\rightharpoonup u\hskip 10.00002pt\text{in }L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times Q;\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Analogously,
(u_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
strongly two-scale converges in
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
u
iff

(2.10)

S_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\to u\hskip 10.00002pt\text{in }L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times Q;\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Proposition
2.4
can be taken as a motivation to extend two-scale convergence to the limit cases
p\in\{1,\infty\}
.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
p\in\{1,\infty\}
. A bounded sequence
(u_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}\subset L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
converges weakly (weakly star for
p=\infty
), respectively strongly, in
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
to some limit
u\in L^{p}(\Omega\times Q;\mathbb{R}^{n})
if
(S_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
converges weakly (weakly star), respectively strongly, to
u
in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times Q;\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

From Proposition
2.4
and Definition
2.5
together with (
2.6
) and (
2.7
) one immediately gets that every purely oscillating function converges strongly two-scale. To be precise, for
p\in[1,\infty]
and every
u\in L^{p}_{\text{per}}(Q;\mathbb{R}^{n})
there holds
u(x/\varepsilon)\overset{2s}{\to}u(z)
in
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
With similar arguments one can see that for
i\in\{0,1\}
also the characteristic functions
\chi_{\varepsilon}^{i}=\chi_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{i}}
defined in (
2.1
) fulfil

(2.11)

\chi_{\varepsilon}^{i}(x)\overset{2s}{\to}\chi^{i}(z)=\chi_{Q_{i}}(z)\hskip 20.00003pt\text{in }L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})

for all
p\in[1,\infty)
.

Eventually, Proposition
2.4
also implies that for two sequences
(u_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}\subset L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n}),(v_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}\subset L^{q}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
u_{\varepsilon}\overset{2s}{\rightharpoonup}u
and
v_{\varepsilon}\overset{2s}{\to}v
there holds
u_{\varepsilon}\cdot v_{\varepsilon}\overset{2s}{\rightharpoonup}u\cdot v
in
L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

We collect some further properties of two-scale convergence.
The next lemma explains why two-scale convergence is often considered as an intermediate convergence between the strong and weak one in
L^{p}(\Omega\times Q;\mathbb{R}^{n})
(cf.
[
28
, Theorem 1.3]
).

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty)
, let
(u_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}\subset L^{p}(\Omega\times Q;\mathbb{R}^{n})
be bounded and
u\in L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

(i)

If
u
is independent of the second variable and
u_{\varepsilon}\to u
strongly in
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then also
u_{\varepsilon}\overset{2s}{\to}u
in
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

(ii)

If
u_{\varepsilon}\overset{2s}{\rightharpoonup}u
in
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then

u_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup\tilde{u}(x):=\int_{Q}u(x,y)\ dx\hskip 10.00002pt\text{weakly in }L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})

and

(2.12)

\|\tilde{u}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega\times Q;\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon}\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Next, we present an augmented two-scale compactness result, which is by now well-known in the context of high-contrast homogenization, cf.
[

,
15
,

]
.
Note that, up to the weak convergence of the extensions, a similar lemma was already derived in

in Appendix

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
(\varepsilon_{k})_{k}
be an infinitesimal sequence and let

for some constant

\displaystyle\text{ for a.e. }(x,z)\in\Omega\times Q,

\displaystyle\tilde{m}_{k}\rightharpoonup\tilde{m}\hskip 20.00003pt

\displaystyle\text{ weakly in }W^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3}).

Let

2.2.
Extension operators

Extension operators providing separate bounds on the
L^{2}
-norm of the maps and of their gradients are a fundamental tool in the study of homogenization problems on perforated domains. The seminal result in this direction can be found in
[
1
, Theorem 2.1]
and reads as follows.

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and let
{\Omega^{1}_{\varepsilon}}
be as in (
1.5
).
Then, there exist a linear and continuous extension operator

T_{\varepsilon}:W^{1,p}({\Omega^{1}_{\varepsilon}};\mathbb{R}^{3})\to W^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})

and a constant
C>0
independent of
\varepsilon
and
\Omega
, such that

\displaystyle T_{\varepsilon}f=f\hskip 10.00002pt\text{a.\,e. in }{\Omega^{1}_{\varepsilon}},

\displaystyle\|T_{\varepsilon}f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}({\Omega^{1}_{\varepsilon}};\mathbb{R}^{3})},

\displaystyle\|D(T_{\varepsilon}f)\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3})}\leq C\|Df\|_{L^{p}({\Omega^{1}_{\varepsilon}};\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3})}

for every
f\in W^{1,p}({\Omega^{1}_{\varepsilon}};\mathbb{R}^{3})
.

In our problem we will need a variants of the above result in order to take care of extensions of the magnetizations. The main hurdle concerns the necessity of extending the maps while at the same time preserving the saturation constraint. This is guaranteed by
[
17
, Theorem 3.1]
,
whose simplified statement in our setting is stated in the next proposition.

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
{\Omega^{1}_{\varepsilon}}
be as in (
1.5
).
There exists an extension operator

\mathsf{T}_{\varepsilon}:W^{1,2}({\Omega^{1}_{\varepsilon}};\mathbb{S}^{2})\to W^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{2})

and a constant
C>0
, independent of
\varepsilon
, such that for
\varepsilon
sufficiently small

\displaystyle\mathsf{T}_{\varepsilon}f=f\hskip 10.00002pt\text{a.\,e. in }{\Omega^{1}_{\varepsilon}},

\displaystyle\|\mathsf{T}_{\varepsilon}f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{2})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}({\Omega^{1}_{\varepsilon}};\mathbb{S}^{2})},

\displaystyle\|D(\mathsf{T}_{\varepsilon}f)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3})}\leq C\|Df\|_{L^{2}({\Omega^{1}_{\varepsilon}};\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3})},

for every

.

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

We preliminary recall an approximation result proving that every function in the space
L^{2}(\Omega;W^{1,2}_{0}(Q_{0};\mathbb{R}^{3}))
can be attained as a strong two-scale limit of smooth functions in
C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})
. The idea of the construction goes back to
[
14
, Lemma 6.1]
, as well as
[
9
, Lemma 22 and Proposition 17]
. To make the presentation as self-contained as possible we also provide a short proof.

Lemma 3.1
.

For any infinitesimal sequence
(\varepsilon_{k})_{k}
and any
w\in L^{2}(\Omega;W_{0}^{1,2}(Q_{0};\mathbb{S}^{2}))
there exists a sequence
(\hat{w}_{k})_{k}\subset C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})
such that
\hat{w}_{k}\chi^{1}_{k}=0
for every
k
, with

(3.1)

\hat{w}_{k}\overset{2s}{\to}w(x,z)\hskip 20.00003pt\text{in }L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3}),

and

(3.2)

\varepsilon_{k}\nabla\hat{w}_{k}\overset{2s}{\to}\nabla_{z}w(x,z)\hskip 20.00003pt\text{in }L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3}).

Proof.

Let
w\in L^{2}(\Omega;W_{0}^{1,2}(Q_{0};\mathbb{S}^{2}))
. We approximate
w
by functions
(w_{j})_{j}\subset C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega\times Q_{0};\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Then, we define

(3.3)

w^{j}_{k}(x,z)\coloneqq\begin{cases}\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{k}^{3}}\int_{\varepsilon_{k}(t+Q)}w_{j}(\bar{x},z)\,{\mathrm{d}\bar{x}}\qquad&\text{if }x\in\varepsilon_{k}(t+Q)\text{ for some }t\in\hat{Z}_{k},\\
0\qquad&\text{else}\end{cases}

by averaging on cubes whose side lengths are associated to the sequence
(\varepsilon_{k})_{k}
.
In the expression above,

\hat{Z}_{k}:=\{t\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}:\,\varepsilon_{k}(t+Q)\subset\Omega\}
.

Note that since
w_{j}
has compact support in
Q
in the
z
-variable for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
, the functions

(3.4)

v_{k}^{j}(x)\coloneqq w_{k}^{j}\left(x,\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{k}}\right)

are again elements of
C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
for all
j,k\in\mathbb{N}
. Furthermore, for any fixed
j\in\mathbb{N}
the sequence
(v_{k}^{j})_{k}
strongly two-scale converges to
w_{j}
for
k\to\infty
; and at the same time
(\varepsilon_{k}\nabla v_{k}^{j})_{k}
strongly two-scale converges to
\nabla_{z}w_{j}
. This follows from the smoothness of the functions and the mean value theorem (compare
[
14
, Lemma 6.1]
). Eventually, by construction,
v^{j}_{k}\chi^{1}_{k}=0
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

In particular, owing to Proposition
2.4
, we find

\lim_{j\to+\infty}\lim_{k\to+\infty}\left(\|S_{k}(v^{j}_{k})-w\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\|\nabla_{z}S_{k}(v^{j}_{k})-\nabla_{z}w\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3})}\right)=0.

By Attouch’s diagonalization lemma
[
26
, Lemma 1.15 and Corollary 1.16]
we find a subsequence
(j(k))_{k}
such that

\lim_{k\to+\infty}\left(\|S_{k}(v^{j(k)}_{k})-w\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\|\nabla_{z}S_{k}(v^{j(k)}_{k})-\nabla_{z}w\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3})}\right)=0.

The thesis follows then by Proposition
2.4
, setting
\hat{w}_{k}:=v^{j(k)}_{k}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
∎

Let now
\tilde{m}\in W^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{2})
and
w\in L^{2}(\Omega;W^{1,2}_{0}(Q_{0};\mathbb{R}^{3}))
be such that

\tilde{m}(x)+w(x,z)\in\mathbb{S}^{2}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for a.e. }(x,z)\in\Omega\times Q_{0}.

Then, we might approximate
\tilde{m}
by functions

(3.5)

(\tilde{m}^{\prime}_{k})_{k}\subset C^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})\hskip 10.00002pt\text{such that}\hskip 10.00002pt\tilde{m}^{\prime}_{k}\to\tilde{m}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{strongly in }W^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3}),

and we apply Lemma
3.1
to find a sequence
(\hat{w}_{k})_{k}\subset C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})
that fulfils (
3.1
) and (
3.2
). In particular,

(3.6)

v_{k}(x):=\tilde{m}^{\prime}_{k}(x)+\hat{w}_{k}(x)\hskip 10.00002pt\in C^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})\cap W^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})

and

\displaystyle v_{k}

\displaystyle\overset{2s}{\to}\tilde{m}+w\chi^{0}\hskip 20.00003pt

\displaystyle\text{strongly two-scale in }L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{k}\nabla v_{k}

\displaystyle\overset{2s}{\to}(\nabla_{z}w)\chi^{0}\hskip 20.00003pt

\displaystyle\text{strongly two-scale in }L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3}).

In order to construct a recovery sequence, it would be natural to project back the functions in (

\mathbb{S}^{2}
by means of the map

(3.7)

\pi:\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\}\to\mathbb{S}^{2},\hskip 10.00002pt\pi(x)=\frac{x}{\|x\|},

[
5

. We prove now a slight generalization of Theorem

in Section

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\tilde{m}\in W^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{2})

(3.8)

\displaystyle m_{k}\overset{2s}{\to}\tilde{m}(x)+w(x,z)\chi^{0}(z)

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\text{in }L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{2}),

(3.9)

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{k}\nabla m_{k}\overset{2s}{\to}\nabla_{z}w(x,z)\chi^{0}(z)

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\text{in }L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}),

m_{k}\in C^{\infty}(\Omega\setminus\{a_{k_{1}},\dots,a_{k_{l}}\};\mathbb{S}^{2}),

.

Proof.

For
\tilde{m}\in W^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{2})
and

we define (modifying (

))

(3.11)

v_{k}(x):=\tilde{m}^{\prime}_{k}(x)+\varepsilon_{k}\psi(x,x/\varepsilon_{k})+\hat{w}_{k}(x)\hskip 10.00002pt\in C^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})\cap W^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3}).

There holds

(3.12)

\displaystyle v_{k}

\displaystyle\overset{2s}{\to}\tilde{m}+w\chi^{0}\hskip 20.00003pt

\displaystyle\text{strongly two-scale in }L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})

(3.13)

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{k}\nabla v_{k}

\displaystyle\overset{2s}{\to}(\nabla_{z}w)\chi^{0}\hskip 20.00003pt

\displaystyle\text{strongly two-scale in }L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3}).

Note that we can always assume that

converges a.e. to

Remark 3.3
.

The proof of Theorem
3.2
can be split into the following two steps: (i) first, using Lemma
3.1
, we construct a sequence approximating
\tilde{m}+w
strongly two-scale (and similarly for the gradient), where, however, we forget about the
\mathbb{S}^{2}
-constraint; (ii) we then use suitable projections in the target space to restore the constraint. In theory, one could also try to first find approximations, smooth up to finitely many points, of
\tilde{m}+w
(and its gradient) in
L^{p}(\Omega\times Q;\mathbb{S}^{2})
with the help of the ideas in
[
5
, Theorem 4]
, and then to build strong two-scale approximations in the lines of Lemma
3.1
. In fact, applying the approximation scheme from
[
5
, Theorem 4]
to a target function in
L^{2}(\Omega\times Q;\mathbb{S}^{2})
is possible in theory, but there is a catch with following Lemma
3.1
and it is two-fold: First, through the averaging in (
3.3
) we fall again out of
\mathbb{S}^{2}
in the target space; and second, introducing the fast oscillations in (
3.4
) might lead to ill-defined functions. The reason for the latter point is the following: In applying
[
5
, Theorem 4]
to a function in
L^{2}(\Omega\times Q;\mathbb{S}^{2})
with smooth approximations in
C^{\infty}(\Omega\times Q;\mathbb{S}^{2})
, we need to choose a sequence of shifts
(a_{k})_{k}
for the projections such that the analog of (
3.16
) is fulfilled. The preimages of the singularities of the shifted projections would have co-dimension
3
in
\Omega\times Q
, and nothing tells us a priori that they lie completely in
\Omega
, i.e., that, when projected onto
Q
, they have co-dimension
3
in
Q
(in particular, if this fails, they are not of Carathéodeory type). But if we mimic (
3.4
) for such functions, we cannot assure that the resulting maps are well-defined. Hence, the path chosen for Theorem
3.2
appears to the authors to be the only feasible option to construct the sought strong two-scale approximations.

Lemma 4.1

(Splitting)

For

converge to some

\displaystyle\begin{split}\limsup_{k\to+\infty}\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(m_{k})\leq\limsup_{k\to+\infty}\mathcal{F}^{0}_{\varepsilon}(w_{k})+\limsup_{k\to+\infty}\mathcal{F}^{1}_{\varepsilon}(\tilde{m}_{k}).\end{split}

Proof.

A direct computation shows that

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(m_{k})=\mathcal{F}^{0}_{\varepsilon}(m_{k})+\mathcal{F}^{1}_{\varepsilon}(m_{k})=\mathcal{F}^{0}_{\varepsilon}(w_{k})+\mathcal{F}^{1}_{\varepsilon}(\tilde{m}_{k})+\mathcal{R}_{k}

, there holds

(4.15)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\liminf_{k\to\infty}\mathcal{F}^{1}_{\varepsilon}(\tilde{m}_{k})=\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega_{k}^{1}}|\nabla\tilde{m}_{k}|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}x}\geq\mathcal{F}^{1}(\tilde{m}).\end{split}

To summarize, there holds

(4.16)

\liminf_{k\to\infty}\mathcal{F}_{k}(m_{k})\geq\mathcal{F}^{0}(w)+\mathcal{F}^{1}(\tilde{m}),

where
\mathcal{F}_{k}
is defined in (
4.1
) as the difference between
\mathcal{G}_{k}
and the magnetostatic self-energy.
In view of the superadditivity of the limes inferior, it remains to prove that

\liminf_{k\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|h_{d}[m_{k}]|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}x}\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|h_{d}[(\tilde{m}+\langle w(x,\cdot)\rangle_{Q_{0}})]|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}x}+\int_{\Omega}\int_{Q_{0}}|h_{d}^{z}[w(x,z)]|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}z}{\mathrm{d}x}.

This latter inequality is a consequence of the weak two-scale behaviour of the demagnetizing fields and of a well-known orthogonality result in two-scale convergence. From Lemma
2.8
, by the weak two-scale convergence of
(m_{k})_{k}
to
\tilde{m}+w
, then the corresponding demagnetizing fields
(h_{d}[m_{k}])_{k}
weakly two-scale converge, up to a subsequence, in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3};\mathbb{R}^{3})
to

h_{d}(x,z)\coloneqq h_{d}[(\tilde{m}(x)+\langle w(x,\cdot)\rangle_{Q_{0}})](x)+h_{d}^{z}[w](x,z)\chi_{\Omega}(x).

Note that we used that
h_{d}^{z}[m]=h_{d}^{z}[w]
in our case.
Thus, again by lower semi-continuity (see Lemma
2.6
(
ii
)) we have

(4.17)

\liminf_{k\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|h_{d}[m_{k}]|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}x}\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{Q}|h_{d}[(\tilde{m}(x)+\langle w(x,\cdot)\rangle_{Q_{0}})]+h_{d}^{z}[w]\chi_{\Omega}(x)|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}z}{\mathrm{d}x}.

We recall that the function
h_{d}^{z}[w]
is given through the identity
h_{d}^{z}[m]=\nabla_{z}r_{m}
with a uniquely defined scalar potential
r_{w}\in L^{2}(\Omega;W^{1,2}_{\text{per}}(Q;\mathbb{R})/\mathbb{R})
. In particular,
r(x,\cdot)
is periodic with respect to the
z
-variable, entailing that the
z
-gradient has vanishing mean value. Thus, we can orthogonally decompose the right-hand side above as

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}

\displaystyle\int_{Q}|h_{d}[(\tilde{m}(x)+\langle w(x,\cdot)\rangle_{Q_{0}})]+\nabla_{z}r_{w}|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}z}{\mathrm{d}x}

(4.18)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|h_{d}[(\tilde{m}(x)+\langle w(x,\cdot)\rangle_{Q_{0}})]|^{2}{\mathrm{d}x}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{Q_{0}}|h_{d}^{z}[w]|^{2}\chi_{\Omega}(x)\,{\mathrm{d}z}{\mathrm{d}x}.

The claimed lower bound follows then by combining (
4.16
)–(
4.18
).
∎

4.3.
Upper bound

The main task in showing the limsup inequality in Theorem
1.4
is the construction of a suitable approximate recovery sequence that allows to pass to the limit in all the different energy contributions. It is here that Theorem
1.1
(to be precise, its augmented version in Theorem
3.2
) becomes essential. For a given pair
(\tilde{m},w)
, we construct the recovery sequence by perturbing the base magnetization
\tilde{m}
by damped oscillations to account for the energy on the matrix component; we then add the field
w
on the inclusions to match the high-contrast scaling of the functional there. Eventually, we pass to the limit with the help of all strong two-scale convergence properties in (
3.8
)-(
3.10
) of Theorem
3.2
.

Proposition 4.5
.

For any
(\tilde{m},w)\in W^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})\times L^{2}(\Omega;W_{0}^{1,2}(Q_{0};\mathbb{R}^{3}))
there exists a sequence
(m_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}\subset W^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{2})
that converges to
(\tilde{m},w)
in the strong high-contrast sense and that satisfies

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}(m_{\varepsilon})\leq\mathcal{G}(\tilde{m},w).

Proof.

Fix
\delta>0
.
Corollary
4.3
entails that there exist
\psi^{\delta}_{m}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega;C_{\text{per}}^{\infty}(Q;\mathbb{R}^{3}))
such that

\limsup_{k\to\infty}\mathcal{F}_{k}\left(\pi\left(\tilde{m}(x)+\varepsilon\psi^{\delta}_{m}\left(x,\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\right)\right)\leq\mathcal{F}^{1}(\tilde{m})+\delta.

Let now further
(m_{k})_{k}
be the sequence of maps provided by Theorem
3.2
, with the special choice
\psi=\psi^{\delta}_{m}
. We first check the strong high contrast convergence of the sequence (see Definition
1.3
). First, from (
3.8
) in Theorem
3.2
we infer that

m_{k}\overset{2s}{\to}\tilde{m}+w\hskip 10.00002pt\text{strongly two-scale in }L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3}).

We now prove that

m_{k}\to\tilde{m}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{in the sense of extensions}.

Indeed, take an arbitrary subsequence of
(m_{k})_{k}
, say
(m_{k(j)})_{j}
, and extend the restrictions
m_{k(j)}\chi^{1}_{k(j)}
to
\Omega_{k}^{0}
with the estension operator provided by Proposition
2.10
. Denote by
\tilde{m}_{k(j)}
these extensions. From the boundedness of
(m_{k})_{k}
and the boundedness of the extension operator, we extract a further subsequence (not relabeled) that converges weakly in
W^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})
and strongly in
L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})
to a limit function
\tilde{m}^{\prime}\in W^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{2})
. At the same time, because strong
L^{2}
-convergence implies weak two-scale convergence (compare Lemma
2.6
(
ii
)), we find that
m_{k(j)}\chi^{1}_{k(j)}=\tilde{m}_{k(j)}\chi^{1}_{k(j)}
weakly two-scale converges to
\chi^{1}(z)\tilde{m}^{\prime}(x)=\chi^{1}(z)\tilde{m}(x)
in
L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})
. This yields that
\tilde{m}^{\prime}=\tilde{m}
. Since this result is independent of the subsequence that we chose in the beginning, we conclude that
(m_{k})_{k}
converges to
\tilde{m}
in the sense of extensions.

Coming back to the
\limsup
-inequality, we define

\tilde{m}_{k}\coloneqq\mathsf{T}_{k}\left(m_{k}|_{\Omega_{k}^{1}}\right)\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002ptw_{k}\coloneqq m_{k}-\tilde{m}_{k},

where
\mathsf{T}_{k}:W^{1,p}(\Omega_{k}^{1};\mathbb{S}^{2})\to W^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{2})
is the extension operator from Proposition
2.10
.
Note that
(w_{k})_{k}\subset W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega_{k}^{0};\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Then, it follows from the splitting in Proposition
4.1
that

\limsup_{k\to\infty}\mathcal{G}_{k}(m_{k})\leq\limsup_{k\to+\infty}\mathcal{F}^{0}_{k}(w_{k})+\limsup_{k\to+\infty}\mathcal{F}^{1}_{k}(\tilde{m}_{k})+\limsup_{k\to+\infty}\mathcal{W}(m_{k}).

From (
3.9
) of Theorem
3.2
we obtain

\limsup_{k\to+\infty}\mathcal{F}^{0}_{k}(w_{k})=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\int_{Q_{0}}|\nabla_{z}w(x,z)|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}z}{\mathrm{d}x}.

For the magnetostatic self-energy
\mathcal{W}
we use again the fact that by Lemma
2.8
the sequence
(h_{d}[m_{k}])_{k}
converges weakly two-scale in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3};\mathbb{R}^{3})
to

h_{d}(x,z)\coloneqq h_{d}[(\tilde{m}(x)+\langle w(x,\cdot)\rangle_{Q_{0}})]+h_{d}^{z}[w]\chi_{\Omega}(x).

By Theorem
3.2
, equation (
3.8
), we thus obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathcal{W}(m_{k})

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\to+\infty}-\int_{\Omega}h_{d}[m_{k}]\cdot m_{k}\,{\mathrm{d}x}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}\int_{Q}h_{d}(x,z)\cdot(\tilde{m}(x)+w(x,z)\chi^{0}(z))\,{\mathrm{d}z}{\mathrm{d}x}

and a direct computation shows that

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}\int_{Q}h_{d}(x,z)\cdot(\tilde{m}(x)+w(x,z)\chi^{0}(z))\,{\mathrm{d}z}{\mathrm{d}x}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|h_{d}[(\tilde{m}+\langle w(x,\cdot)\rangle_{Q_{0}})]|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}x}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{Q_{0}}|h_{d}^{z}[w]|^{2}\chi_{\Omega}(x)\,{\mathrm{d}z}{\mathrm{d}x}.

Hence, by
[
7
, Section 1.2]
it remains only to prove that

(4.19)

\limsup_{k\to\infty}\mathcal{F}^{1}_{k}(\tilde{u}_{k},\tilde{m}_{k})\leq\mathcal{F}^{1}(\tilde{u},\tilde{m})+\delta.

As already observed in the proof of the lower bound, we have to be careful in applying Proposition
4.2
because the characteristic function
\chi_{k}^{1}
is not periodic. However, for

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{R}_{k}\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega_{k}^{1}}|\nabla\tilde{m}|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}x}-\int_{(\varepsilon_{k}E)\cap\Omega}|\nabla\tilde{m}|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}x}=\int_{\Omega_{k}^{1}\setminus\overline{(\varepsilon_{k}E)\cap\Omega}}|\nabla\tilde{m}|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}x}\end{split}

we see from the
2
-equiintegrability of
(\nabla\tilde{m}_{k})_{k}
(with (
3.10
)), as well as from the fact that
|\Omega_{k}^{1}\setminus\overline{(\varepsilon_{k}E)\cap\Omega}|\to 0
as
k\to+\infty
, that
|\mathcal{R}_{k}|\to 0
as
k\to+\infty
.
Finally, from (
3.10
) and Corollary
4.3
(
ii
), we infer that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\limsup_{k\to\infty}\mathcal{F}^{1}_{k}(\tilde{m}_{k})=\limsup_{k\to\infty}\int_{(\varepsilon_{k}E)\cap\Omega}|\nabla\tilde{m}|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}x}\leq\mathcal{F}^{1}(\tilde{u},\tilde{m})+\delta.\end{split}

This yields (
4.19
) and concludes the proof of Theorem
1.4
.
∎

Appendix A
High-contrast compactness

We provide here a proof of Lemma
2.7
.

Proof of Lemma
2.7
.

We will deliberately switch to subsequences below without relabeling the sequence itself.
The assumptions together with inequality (
2.13
) entail that
(m_{k})_{k}
and
(\varepsilon_{k}\nabla m_{k})_{k}
are bounded in
L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Thus, usual two-scale compactness properties (cf.
[
2
, Proposition 1.14]
) yield the existence of
m\in L^{2}(\Omega;W^{1,2}_{\text{per}}(Q;\mathbb{R}^{3}))
such that, up to subsequences,

(A.1)

m_{k}\overset{2s}{\rightharpoonup}m,\hskip 10.00002pt\varepsilon_{k}\nabla m_{k}\overset{2s}{\rightharpoonup}\nabla_{z}m\hskip 20.00003pt\text{ weakly two-scale in }L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3}).

Additionally, there exists
m_{1}\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})
such that, up to a subsequence,

(A.2)

\chi_{k}^{1}m_{k}\rightharpoonup m_{1}\hskip 20.00003pt\text{ weakly in }L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3}).

Now, from (
2.11
) and Proposition
2.4
we infer that on the one hand

\varepsilon_{k}\chi_{k}^{1}\nabla m_{k}\overset{2s}{\rightharpoonup}\chi^{1}\nabla_{z}m\hskip 20.00003pt\text{ weakly two-scale in }L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}).

On the other hand, since
(\chi_{k}^{1}\nabla m_{k})_{k}
is bounded in
L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})
by (
2.13
), we also have

\varepsilon_{k}\chi_{k}^{1}\nabla m_{k}\to 0\hskip 10.00002pt\text{strongly in }L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Consequently, we find that
\nabla_{z}m(x,z)=0
on
\Omega\times Q_{1}
, and in particular that
m
is independent of the micro-variable
z
in
Q_{1}
, i.e., that with Lemma
2.6
(
ii
) yields

(A.3)

\chi^{1}(z)m(x,z)=\frac{1}{|Q_{1}|}\chi^{1}(z)m_{1}(x)\hskip 20.00003pt\text{ for a.e. }x\in\Omega.

Next, we will show that, up to a subsequence, there holds

(A.4)

\tilde{m}_{k}\rightharpoonup\frac{m_{1}}{|Q_{1}|}\hskip 20.00003pt\text{ weakly in }W^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3}).

This implies that
m_{1}
is actually an element of
W^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})
. By virtue of estimate (
2.13
) and the properties of the extensions, we conclude that there exists
\tilde{m}\in W^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})
and a subsequence of
(\tilde{m}_{k})_{k}
that converges weakly in
W^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})
and even strongly in
L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})
to the limit
\tilde{m}
. Because strong
L^{2}
-convergence implies weak two-scale convergence as stated in Lemma
2.6
(
ii
), it follows readily that

\chi_{k}^{1}m_{k}=\chi_{k}^{1}\tilde{m}_{k}\overset{2s}{\rightharpoonup}\chi^{1}\tilde{m}\hskip 20.00003pt\text{ weakly two-scale in }L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3}),

and then by (
A.1
), (
A.3
), and the uniqueness of the two-scale limit we find

\tilde{m}=\frac{m_{1}}{|Q_{1}|}.

Defining

(A.5)

w(x,z):=m(x,z)-\tilde{m}(x),

we have that
w\in L^{2}(\Omega;W^{1,2}_{0}(Q_{0};\mathbb{R}^{3}))
and we obtain (
2.15
). Properties (
2.14
), (
2.16
), and (
2.17
) follow by construction. This concludes the proof.
∎

Appendix B
\Gamma
-limit of the matrix related energy

We present a concise proof of the
\Gamma
-limit claimed in Proposition
4.2
. To that end we fix
m\in W^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{2})
.

Step 1: Lower bound.

Let
(m_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
be a sequence converging in
W^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{2})
weakly to
m
. We first show that

(B.1)

\mathcal{I}(m)\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}(m_{\varepsilon}).

Since the sequence
(m_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
is uniformly bounded in
W^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{2})
, we know by virtue of classical two-scale compactness properties (cf.
[
2
, Proposition 1.14]
) that there exists
m_{1}\in L^{2}(\Omega;W^{1,p}_{\text{per}}(Q;\mathbb{R}^{n})/\mathbb{R})
such that, up to a subsequence,
(\nabla m_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
weakly two-scale converges to
\nabla m(x)+\nabla_{z}m_{1}(x,z)
. Moreover, by
[
3
, Proposition 3.2]
there holds

m_{1}(x,z)\in T_{m(x)}\mathbb{S}^{2}\text{ for a.e. }(x,z)\in\Omega\times Q.

Using additionally that

S_{\varepsilon}(a_{\varepsilon})(x,z)=S_{\varepsilon}(\chi_{(\varepsilon_{k}E)\cap\Omega})(x,z)=\chi_{1}(z)

we conclude from Lemma
2.6
(
ii
) immediately that

\displaystyle\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}a_{\varepsilon}|\nabla m_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}x}

\displaystyle=\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\int_{Q_{1}}|S_{\varepsilon}(\nabla m_{\varepsilon})|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}z}{\mathrm{d}x}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\int_{Q_{1}}|\nabla m(x)+\nabla_{z}m_{1}(x,z)|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}z}{\mathrm{d}x}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\Omega}\Bigg[\inf_{\varphi\in W^{1,2}_{\text{per}}(Q;T_{m(x)}\mathbb{S}^{2})}\frac{1}{2}\int_{Q_{1}}|\nabla m+\nabla\varphi(z)|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}z}\Bigg]{\mathrm{d}x}.

Step 2: Upper bound.

For
\psi_{m}\in W^{1,2}(\Omega;C^{\infty}_{\text{per}}(Q;\mathbb{R}^{3}))
, we define the perturbation

(B.2)

m_{\varepsilon}(x)\coloneqq\pi\left(m(x)+\varepsilon\psi_{m}\left(x,\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\right),

where
\pi
is the customary projection onto
\mathbb{S}^{2}
(compare (
3.7
)). The functions
m_{\varepsilon}
are in particular well-defined for
\varepsilon
sufficiently small. There holds

(B.3)

m_{\varepsilon}\to m\hskip 20.00003pt\text{ strongly in }L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3}).

Furthermore, owing to the argument in
[
13
, Theorem 2.3, line (60)]
there also holds

(B.4)

\nabla m_{\varepsilon}\overset{2s}{\to}\nabla m+\nabla_{z}\psi_{m}\nabla\pi(m)\hskip 20.00003pt\text{ strongly in }L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}).

We claim now that for every
\delta>0
we can chose
\psi_{m}=\psi_{m}^{\delta}
such that the corresponding sequence
(m_{\varepsilon}^{\delta})_{\varepsilon}
defined in (
B.2
) fulfils

(B.5)

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}^{\delta},m_{\varepsilon}^{\delta})\leq\mathcal{I}(u,m)+\delta.

By classical
\Gamma
-convergence arguments (cf.
[
7
, Section 1.2]
), this would then establish the
\Gamma-\limsup
inequality. We first observe that using the strong two-scale convergence in (
B.4
) we have, similarly to step 1,

(B.6)

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}a_{\varepsilon}|\nabla m_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}x}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}a_{\varepsilon}|\nabla m+\nabla_{z}\psi_{m}\nabla\pi(m)|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}x}.

Now, because
C^{\infty}_{\text{per}}(Q;\mathbb{R}^{3})
is dense in
W^{1,2}_{\text{per}}(Q;\mathbb{R}^{3})
and the right-hand side of (
B.6
) is continuous with respect to the
W^{1,2}(Q;\mathbb{R}^{3})
-topology, we conclude that there exists
\psi_{m}^{\delta}\in W^{1,2}(\Omega;C^{\infty}_{\text{per}}(Q;\mathbb{R}^{3}))
such that

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}a_{\varepsilon}|\nabla m_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}x}\leq\int_{\Omega}\Bigg[\inf_{\varphi\in W^{1,2}_{\text{per}}(Q;T_{m(x)}\mathbb{S}^{2})}\frac{1}{2}\int_{Q_{1}}|\nabla m+\nabla\varphi(z)|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}z}\Bigg]{\mathrm{d}x}+\delta,

where we used additionally that
\nabla\varphi\nabla\pi(m)=\nabla\varphi
for all
\varphi\in W^{1,2}_{\text{per}}(Q;T_{m}\mathbb{S}^{2})
.
This concludes the proof.

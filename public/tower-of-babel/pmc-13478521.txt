Optimized multi-tier task offloading strategy for sustainable IoV systems in 6G networks
Authors: Abdullah Alwabli
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Sixth-generation (6G) networks are likely to support advanced Internet of Vehicles (IoV) applications that have rigid latency, reliability, and computation demands. Nevertheless, efficient task offloading is a challenging problem because vehicle environments are characterized by mobility, changing channels, varying task requirements, constrained edge resources, and growing energy demands. To address these issues, this study presents an Optimized Multi-Tier Task Offloading Strategy (OMTOS) for sustainable IoV systems. The proposed framework comprises a four-tier computing architecture comprising vehicles, roadside units (RSUs), mobile edge computing (MEC) servers, and cloud infrastructure. The generalized latency-energy optimization problem is formulated to allocate tasks across these levels, accounting for task due dates, resource capacity, communication delay, computation delay, and energy consumption. To address dynamic offloading, OMTOS employs a centralized training and decentralized execution (CTDE) based multi-agent Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) method, where the vehicle agents can make decentralized offloading decisions with centralized critics guiding the coordinated learning process during training. It is tested against rule-based and heuristic as well as deep reinforcement learning and various multi-agent reinforcement learning baselines, including LE, EO, RO, GO, DQN, DDPG, SAC, MADDPG, and MAPPO. The aforementioned results reveal that OMTOS achieves low average delay, low energy consumption, a high task success rate, and high convergence compared to the competing methods. Sensitivity analysis also indicates that the latency and energy weightings can be changed to suit various IoV service requirements, including delay-critical safety services, and energy-conscious delay-tolerant services. These results show that OMTOS offers an adaptive and sustainable task-offloading tool in 6G-enabled IoV environments.

Introduction

The development of beyond fifth-generation (B5G) and sixth-generation (6G) networks is transforming the Internet of Vehicles (IoV) through ultra-reliable and low-latency communication and scaled connectivity. Potential applications of modern vehicles, such as autonomous driving, smart traffic systems, and even safety services, generate real-time, delay-sensitive, and computationally intensive tasks1,2. Applications are sensitive to speed and decision-making, and therefore cannot be effectively handled by traditional cloud-centric architectures because transmission delays are high and the network is congested3,4. This has led to task offloading being considered as a basic process in the IoV system, where vehicles can offload computation tasks onto external resources.

Mobile Edge Computing (MEC) has significantly enhanced system responsiveness by placing computing resources closer to vehicles. The current offloading processes, however, are based on single- or two-tier architectures, where either tasks are run locally or offloaded to edge servers5–7. These methods cannot be used in highly dynamic IoV environments that involve vehicle mobility, varying network conditions, task heterogeneity, and limited edge resources. These limitations are the ones that are prone to imbalance in resource, increase the queueing time and poor use of the computing infrastructure8–10. To beat these obstacles, multi-tier task offloading has emerged as a tempting paradigm. Services can be allocated in a relaxed manner across a multi-layer architectural framework comprising multiple computation layers, such as vehicles, roadside units (RSUs), MEC servers, and cloud infrastructure11,12. This would make it possible to allocate tasks with greater flexibility and in a smarter way, with delay-sensitive tasks performed at lower levels (e.g., vehicles or RSUs) and high-processing-intensity tasks offloaded to higher levels with more processing resources. This flexibility is critical, especially in 6G-based IoV systems, where bespoke communication and computing are anticipated to enhance highly dynamic, context-based services.

Besides performance efficiency, sustainability has also been a serious issue in next-generation vehicular networks. The rapid development of IoV systems, the constant exchange of data, and the processing of data are significant factors in increasing energy consumption, both on vehicles and at the edge of the infrastructure13,14. Although most existing studies have focused on reducing latency, it often fails to elaborate on the significance of operational efficiency for energy utilization. But to achieve sustainable IoV systems, there must be concessions in latency and energy use to achieve concrete performance and environmental efficiency15,16. Thus, it is necessary to study energy-consciousness in the task redistribution during the design of green, scalable IoT systems for 6G networks. However, with the current developments, there are still some gaps in study. First, the current literature is mostly limited to simplified models and does not fully exploit the potential of multi-tier collaborative computing. Second, the heterogeneity in task size, computational demands, and delay sensitivity is not adequately accounted for in most cases17. Third, the concept of sustainability, especially the idea of offloading that is energy-efficient at various levels, remains scarce. Lastly, the changing characteristics of IoV environments require more flexible and effective policies that enable efficient decision-making across different system conditions18,19.

To address these challenges, this paper proposes an Optimized Multi-Tier Task Offloading Strategy (OMTOS) for sustainable 6G-enabled IoV systems. In contrast with traditional solutions that focus on the elements of latency-sensitive offloading, energy-sensitive optimization, MDP-based decision-making, or resource allocation based on reinforcement learning, OMTOS incorporates all the elements of latency-sensitive offloading, energy-sensitive optimization, MDP-based decision-making, or resource allocation based on reinforcement learning into the unified multi-layer framework. The suggested strategy is dynamic because it assigns heterogeneous vehicular tasks to vehicles, RSUs, MEC servers, and cloud resources, taking into account procedures for latency, energy, task deadline, resource availability, mobility, and the wireless channel. Through the exploitation of four computation tiers together with the adoption of a CTDE-informed multi-agent SAC mechanism, OMTOS allows the coordinated learning during training and the decentralized offloading decisions during execution, offering an application-aware and flexible solution to the challenge of a dynamic IoV environment. Related work

In the IoV, task offloading has attracted significant interest due to the rapid proliferation of latency-sensitive and computationally intensive vehicle applications. In20,21, MEC was introduced into the vehicular network at an early stage to consider the issues of latency reduction and responsiveness enhancement. Similarly, the report in22 explores binary offloading schemes, in which tasks are either executed locally or offloaded to local edge servers, while addressing trade-offs among delay reduction, scalability, and resource consumption. In addition, recent studies23–25 suggest partial offloading schemes that distribute tasks across multiple nodes, thereby enhancing flexibility, but these schemes introduce greater complexity in scheduling and coordination. Most such models, as explained in26,27, assume static or simplified network conditions, which makes them restrictive in the highly dynamic IoV environment with mobility and varying communication links.

In28,29, the authors present multi-tier and hierarchical computing networks to address the constraints of single-layer offloading. These architectures include numerous layers, i.e., vehicles, RSUs, edge servers, and cloud platforms to provide greater flexibility in the task distribution. Moreover, the study by30,31 shows that multi-tier systems can significantly improve system performance through heterogeneous computing resources. However, as described in32, the majority of current models have covered only two- or three-level models and do not fully utilize the entire hierarchy of the IoV. Also, the study in33,34 indicates that communication across layers is typically suboptimal, which contributes to higher overhead and inefficient task distribution. It therefore follows that the research problem of integrating decision-making on all levels is open.

Various optimization methods have been studied to address the complexity of decision-making in task offloading. In35,36, heuristics and convex optimization are used to find efficient ways to offload when resources are constrained. Similarly, game-theoretic solutions in37 equilibrate the interactions among network entities. However, these solutions are not always flexible in dynamic IoV environments. In recent years, the machine learning-based methods have become popular. Deep Q-Networks (DQN) are employed for offloading policy learners in38,39, and Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (DDPG) is applied in continuous decision space40. In addition, the experiment in41 uses Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) to improve stability and convergence in complex environments. Moreover, the methods of multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) in42 enable distributed nodes to exchange information and make coordinated decisions. Even after these developments, the majority of existing learning-based methods are primarily concerned with minimizing latency or maximizing throughput, as explored in43, and do not prioritize energy efficiency or sustainability. Besides, many approaches rely on centralized training or information at a global information, which is not scalable for large-scale IoV deployments.

The optimization of network scheduling during computing power and optimization of 6G edge computing further added to the research background of intelligent allocation of tasks. In44, computation-power netbox scheduling is explored to coordinate distributed computing resources and communication links, demonstrating how computation-aware scheduling can optimize the utilization and responsiveness of network computing resources and links in networked edge computing. Similarly45, discusses 6G-aware edge computing optimization, in which communication, computing, and service demands are comprehensively addressed to support delay-sensitive and resource-intensive applications. These researchers determine the significance of integrated computing resources in the future intelligent networks. However, they mainly focus on general computing power network scheduling or 6G edge optimization, but not specifically in multi-tier task offloading in IoV systems through the joint consideration of vehicles, RSUs, MEC servers, and cloud infrastructure. Besides, the proposed OMTOS scheme combines latency-energy optimization with a multi-agent SAC-based learning plan to support adaptive, sustainable solutions for offloading choices under dynamic vehicle conditions.

Sustainability has also become an essential demand for next-generation IoV systems. The authors in46,47 include energy consumption in task offloading by optimizing transmission power and computation resources. In the same fashion, recent studies48,49 aim at minimizing the energy footprint of a vehicular network at the expense of still ensuring satisfactory levels of performance. However, as the authors of50,51 point out, most energy-aware solutions are constrained by a one-tier or simplified architecture and do not address the complexity of multi-tier IoV systems. In addition, communication between the various layers of the network and its effect on energy efficiency are not well studied. Thus, the increasing demand is to develop a complex set of structures, which jointly optimize latency and energy consumption and exploit multi-tier structures.

The study in52 introduced present a framework in which implemented a real-world UAV delivery system by integrating MEC and blockchain-based resource management. Based on this work, it is possible to support practical computation, communication, and data management processes in aerial edge environments using UAV-edge prototypes. In53–55, the authors further examined a UAV-based MEC system that dynamically provides services, performs margin processing onboard, and provides intelligent application support. In addition, these field tests affirm the validity of UAV-assisted edge computing for future external vehicular and edge network services. However, in [3], UAV-mounted MEC nodes are not covered by the present OMTOS system model, which focuses on ground-based IoV task offloading among vehicles, RSUs, MEC servers, and cloud infrastructure. Thus, extending OMTOS to UAV-enabled MECs is a promising research direction for the future.

Recent work has tried to enhance the offloading of vehicle tasks through mobility awareness, decision-making using DRL, and coordinating tasks among agents56. examined a vehicle edge computing task-offloading strategy with the use of reinforcement learning, and vehicle position, velocity, and computing resources were among the aspects that were taken into account. They propose a V-DQN-based technique that enhances service stability but focuses more on vehicle-edge resource selection and does not fully leverage the four-tier vehicle-RSU-MEC-cloud hierarchy for joint latency-energy optimization. Similarly57,58, suggested a platform hybrid hierarchical DRL agent to complete task offloading and resource allocation in intelligent connected vehicles. Their method breaks down the decision process into an upper DDQN layer that communicates decisions and a lower DDPG layer that decides on power control, offloading ratio, and resource allocation. Despite the fact that this decomposition enhances the adaptability in dynamic environments, the framework still is centered on vehicle–road collaborative MEC and does not explicitly model cloud-tier participation or CTDE-based multi-agent SAC coordination.

Recent multi-agent schemes have gone further to enhance the coordination between the vehicles and edge nodes59. have put forward a multi-agent deep reinforcement learning policy for vehicular edge computing using NOMA, queuing theory, and a stochastic game formulation to optimize delay, energy consumption, cost, and server utilization. The approach, however, predominantly focuses on multi-agent edge offloading and is not developed as a sustainability-oriented multi-layered offloading approach between vehicles, RSUs, MEC servers, and cloud infrastructure60,61. suggested an optimization process based on cooperation between vehicle agents and RSU agents when there are resource constraints, and centralized coordination in RSU agents is necessary. This approach enhances task completion rate, delay, and energy performance; however, it remains focused on task coordination at the RSU level and does not directly address the broader multi-tier latency-energy trade-off introduced in the present work.

Emergent AI-assisted offloading systems and digital twins have also been researched62,63. proposed a VEC framework based on digital replicas to assist decisions made by multiple tasks generated by vehicles in a single time slot. This enhances monitoring and task control, but does not focus on sustainable multi-tier offloading using CTDE-based multi-agent SAC. More recently, it has been investigated to enhance adaptive, low-latency decision-making at RSU edge nodes with the assistance of LLMs. These approaches are promising to high-dimensional reasoning and real-time adaptation, although their practical implementation can raise other model-SIZE, inference-latency, and RSU computational-overhead issues.

Even though recent research on the topics of offloading of tasks to vehicular computers, scheduling of computing resources, edge-assisted vehicular computing, and reinforcement-learning-based resource allocation have made significant strides, there are still several limitations in this area of research. Most current techniques still rely on local-edge, RSU/MEC-based, single-tier, or limited two-tier offloading models and fail to fully leverage a four-tier hierarchy that includes vehicles, RSUs, MEC servers, and cloud infrastructure. Moreover, latency, energy consumption, mobility, channel variation, task heterogeneity, and resource availability are commonly studied independently, which restricts the realism and flexibility of offloading decision-making in dynamic multi-user IoV surgery applications. Certain DRL implementing approaches exploit single-agent, centralized, or weakly coordinated learning, and it is hard to obtain resource contention among multiple cars. To fill these gaps, OMTOS develops sustainable task offloading across vehicles, RSUs, MEC servers, and cloud resources, taking into account both latency and energy, as well as task due dates, mobility-aware channel variation, handover effects, and the availability of computation resources. By further extending the CTDE-based multi-agent SAC design to enable decentralized vehicle-side decisions with centralized critic-based training, OMTOS will be able to deploy a universal 6G-enabled IoV system that integrates multi-tier flexibility, latency-energy consciousness, mobility-conscious modeling, and coordinated multi-agent learning in a single 6G-enabled IoV platform.

Problem formulation

In this section, we develop the multi-tier task offloading problem as a joint optimization problem and model it as a Markov Decision Process (MDP) to facilitate intelligent, adaptive decision-making in dynamic IoV settings. Based on the elaborated system model, the problem of achieving overall agreement on the total service delay and the energy capacity deployed by all vehicles and computation levels will arise. This is developed as a constrained optimization model in which the offloading decisions are determined to achieve an efficient trade-off between latency and energy consumption.

The multi-tier task-offloading problem is expressed using a unified tier-based notation. The optimization model developed is extremely complicated and belongs to the class of mixed-integer nonlinear programs (MINLP), which are known to be NP-hard. Such binary decision variables, nonlinear objective functions, and constraints significantly complicate the process, making it difficult to find optimal solutions using conventional optimization techniques. Moreover, the problem is highly dynamic because it is affected by vehicle motion, time-varying channel conditions, and stochastic task arrival. These ambiguities further destabilize the system and worsen the decision-making process. In this case, smart, adaptable solutions to problems are needed, since conventional optimization techniques cannot address this predicament in real-time IoV.

This contribution presents the task offloading problem as a Markov Decision Process (MDP) to account for the dynamic and stochastic nature of IoV systems. These restrictions guarantee the task assignment to be feasible by assigning each task to a single computation tier, meeting the delay requirement, respecting the processing capacity of each computation tier, and preserving the binary nature of the offloading decision variables. This operation models the stochasticity of the IoV environment by accounting for changes in the system state resulting from offloading decisions. Some of the dynamics affecting state transitions are vehicle mobility, which affects network connectivity and topology; the influx of new computation tasks; changes in wireless channel conditions; and changes in queue lengths at various computation levels. The proposed framework views the IoV system as a multi-agent environment in which all vehicles are independent agents. These agents share a common environment, that is, the IoV network, and make decentralized solutions whilst contributing to a shared system goal. Both agents monitor their local state and choose an action based on their decision to offload tasks. The agents are made to work together through a collective form of rewards or a global maximization objective, which promotes joint actions to enhance overall system performance. The joint action of all agents at time t is defined as given in Eq. The task offloading problem is formulated in this framework as a joint optimization problem to minimize latency and energy consumption. As a result of the combinatorial structure of the offloading choices and interaction between the agents, a computational problem is NP-hard. To efficiently address this complexity, the problem is modeled as a Markov Decision Process (MDP), with learning-based solutions incorporated to account for the dynamic and stochastic nature of the IoV environment. The state space, action space, reward function, and policy objective are the main elements of the MDP that are established to support intelligent, adaptable decision-making.

Proposed optimized multi-tier task offloading strategy

This section introduces the Optimized Multi-Tier Task Offloading Strategy (OMTOS) that is to be proposed to achieve sustainable 6G network IoV systems. The strategy will equally distribute tasks with smartness at various levels, as well as optimizing jointly on latency and energy usage within a dynamic environment. In contrast to more traditional independent offloading models, OMTOS considers the problem of determining the tasks performed by vehicles as a coordinated multi-agent, decision-making problem whereby local vehicle behavior modulates shared RSU, MEC, and cloud resources.

The offloading task problem stated in Section “Problem Formulation” is highly dynamic and computationally intensive, and cannot be solved using traditional optimization methods. To overcome this obstacle, a proposed solution is a reinforcement-learning-based approach. This solution enables the system to learn optimal offloading choices within a continuous interaction framework with the environment. In particular, the use of a SAC-based multi-agent architecture is expected to stabilize the learning process, effectively explore, and achieve successful performance in complex IoV environments. The method will enable various agents to make task offloading decisions jointly and adapt to time-delaying network conditions. The solution proposed employs multivehicle, roadside, and MEC servers, and cloud computing, which allows flexible allocation of tasks by covering all vehicles, the RSUs, and the MEC servers, in addition to the cloud infrastructure. Moreover, it coexists with the optimization of latency and energy usage, thereby providing a performance-sustainability balance. The architecture will fit dynamic vehicular environments (in terms of mobility and communication conditions) and provide scalable, distributed learning for a large-scale IoV system.Fig. 2Flow diagram of the proposed OMTOS framework showing the main agent, environment, action, reward, and learning-update.

Flow diagram of the proposed OMTOS framework showing the main agent, environment, action, reward, and learning-update.

Under the proposed framework, a vehicle is represented as a separate agent that decides which tasks to offload based on its immediate predictions. Such a decentralized environment allows agents to work autonomously and to fail to achieve the system goal. A centralized training and decentralized execution (CTDE), paradigm is followed to coordinate the learning of numerous agents whereby agents are able to exchange information during training and independently make choices during execution. Fig. 2 shows the logical architecture and interaction flow of the proposed OMTOS framework under a centralized training and decentralized execution paradigm. The operationalization of the IoV environment at each decision step is in the form of state observations of queue conditions, channel quality, available computing resources, and task characteristics. Based on these observations, each vehicle agent decides where to take action during offloading within the actor network, either locally or offloaded to the RSU, MEC, or cloud tier. The identified actions are then implemented in the IoV environment, initiating the next state and a reward based on the joint latency-energy goal. The transitions observed during training are stored in the replay buffer and used to update the critic and actor networks during SAC learning. The architecture enables adaptive decisions about task offloading without sacrificing coordination when learning and performing lightweight tasks at the vehicle end.

The recommended OMTOS framework provides a dynamic, sustainable mechanism for task offloading among active IoV systems. It also optimizes intelligent delay and energy consumption and allocates tasks smartly across vehicles, RSUs, MEC servers, and cloud resources. The distributed multi-agent learning approach enhances scalability by enabling lightweight online vehicle-side inference, and the CTDE-based design keeps it that way. Besides that, the learning mechanism of SAC supports stable exploration and improved policy learning under changing channel conditions, task demands, mobility patterns, and resource availability. In general, this section introduces OMTOS as a coordinated multi-tier offloading framework that combines latency-energy optimization, scalable multi-agent learning, and real-time adaptability for offloading in 6G-enabled IoV systems.

Baseline methods

To determine the effectiveness of the proposed OMTOS framework, it is compared to representative rule-based, heuristic, single-agent DRL, and multi-agent DRL baselines. The Local Execution (LE) scheme performs all tasks locally at the vehicle side. The Edge-Only Offloading (EO) scheme offloads tasks only to the RSU or MEC resources. Random Offloading (RO) assigns work to tiers randomly, and Greedy Offloading (GO) assigns work to the lowest immediate estimated delay tier. The traditional SAC uses an entropy-regularized actor-critic learning model but does not directly model centralized coordination among multi-agents. To further reinforce the comparison, additional learning-based baselines are also considered. DQN is a value-based DRL approach that entails discrete offloading choices. DDPG is an actor-critic algorithm suitable for continuous control resource allocation. MADDPG extends actor-critic learning to multi-agent systems, using a single centralized critic and multiple decentralized actors. MAPPO also falls under a category of strong on-policy multi-agent policy-gradient baselines with clipped policy updates and centralized training. These extra baselines are a more stringent comparison to the proposed CTDE-based multi-agent SAC structure. Energy consumption analysis

The CTDE-based multi-agent SAC design enables the vehicle agents to learn to coordinate offloading decisions while considering channel conditions, task types, queue states, mobility information, and available computational resources. Simulation results have demonstrated that the proposed OMTOS framework is stronger in overall performance compared to the rule-based, heuristic, and single-agent DRL-based baselines. The four factors largely contribute to the increase in performance. First, OMTOS leverages the flexibility of the multi-tier architecture to distribute tasks across vehicle, RSU, MEC, and cloud resources, thereby reducing bottlenecks in any single layer of computation. Second, the formulation of the latency-energy-aware reward enables the framework to trade off service delay and energy consumption rather than maximizing a single service performance objective. Third, the multi-agent SAC design based on the CTDE design ensures centralized critic feedback during training and decentralized vehicle-side operation during deployment. This assists agents in understanding coordinated offloading policies in the event of a joint allocation of shared resources. Fourth, a mobility-aware representation of states enables OMTOS to respond to time-varying channel quality, queue conditions, task arrivals, vehicle movements, and resource availability.

In comparison with LE, EO, RO, and GO, OMTOS does not incur inefficient local-only execution, edge congestion, random assigning, and short-sighted greedy decision making. OTOS offers improved coordination among multiple vehicle agents and enhanced exploration through entropy-regularized learning, compared to DQN, DDPG, and conventional SAC. OTMOS will use off-policy replay-buffer reuse and SAC exploration, which offer advantages over MADDPG and MAPPO, which have limitations in stochastic exploitation. Thus, the generalized simulation outputs, tabular comparisons, and graphical analyses prove that OMTOS offers credible latency reduction, energy-saving, investment of higher task success rate, convergence, and scalability to adequately multi-layer deployment of IoV tasks in the 6G-enabled environment.

These results demonstrate that OMTOS is not limited to a single fixed latency–energy preference. Rather, the weight values can be varied to fit the IoV service needs of the different users by reconfiguring the same optimization structure. The latency energy relationship is of the Pareto-style trade-off: decreased delay usually leads to increased energy consumption, and decreased energy consumption usually leads to increased completion delay. Thus, the choice of α and β must be determined by the application type and the required QoS. Applications that are delay-critical with respect to safety require higher α values, while higher β values may be used for energy-sensitive and delay-tolerant applications, respectively. This affirms the adaptability of the proposed OMTOS system of heterogeneous 6G-enabling IoV services.

Conclusion and future work

This paper proposed an Optimized Multi-Tier Task Offloading Strategy (OMTOS) to execute the tasks sustainably in the 6G-enabled IoV system. The suggested four-layer framework comprises vehicles, RSUs, MEC servers, and cloud infrastructure to farm out vehicular tasks to the appropriate computational levels. The offloading problem was developed as a joint latency and energy optimization problem and modeled as an MDP to support adaptive decisions in response to a changing IoV environment. The adopted policy was a CTDE-based multi-agent SAC protocol that allowed offloading decisions to be made in a decentralized fashion and centralized critic feedback during training. The results of the simulations indicate that OMTOS is more effective in achieving lower average delay, reduced energy cost, higher task success rate, faster convergence, and higher scalability than the evaluated rule-based, heuristic, DRL, and MARL baselines.

The practical value of OMTOS is based on the capability to accommodate heterogeneous IoV services that have varying latency and energy needs. Applications that can be prioritized for fast task execution on nearby RSU/MEC resources are delay-critical applications, such as collision warning, emergency response, and less urgent data uploading. The sensitivity analysis also shows that the latency energy weighting factors can be adjusted to meet the needs of different services. The primary benefits of OMTOS include flexibility in multi-tier offloading, joint optimization of latency and energy, task-conscious decision-making, mobility-conscious modeling, and multi-agent learning. Nevertheless, the current study also has limitations, including simulation-based verification, a simplified wireless model, limited treatment of imperfect CSI, no real-world deployment testing, and no UAV-assisted MEC integration in the current system model.

Future work will address these open challenges by incorporating additional realistic communication and mobility models that account for co-channel interference, packet loss, retransmission delay, channel-estimation uncertainty, handover-induced blockage, trajectory prediction, and service migration that is aware of handover. The presented structure must also be supported by real-world traces of vehicular mobility and task-specific application profiles. Future research will involve more extensive empirical comparisons using advanced MARL baselines, such as MADDPG and MAPPO, within the same simulation settings, as well as a more detailed Pareto-frontier analysis of alternative latency-energy weighting scenarios. Beyond that, OMTOS can be scaled into UAV-assisted MEC environments, privacy-aware and federated learning systems, and renewable-energy- or carbon-aware edge computing, making it a more practical and sustainable concept for 6G-enabled IoV systems in the future.

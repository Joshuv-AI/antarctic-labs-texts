Prognostic potential of N6-methyladenosine methylation-associated genes in lung adenocarcinoma
Authors: Yuhan Lei, Jian Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundLung adenocarcinoma (LUAD), recognized as one of the most lethal variants of lung cancer, presents significant treatment challenges and is associated with a poor prognosis. In this study, we used data from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) to investigate the functional implications of N6-methyladenosine (m6A)-related genes in LUAD.MethodsThe clinical characteristics and RNA-sequencing data of patients with LUAD were obtained from the TCGA-LUAD database. Through a comprehensive analysis, we identified the genes related to m6A modifications and with prognostic value for patients with LUAD.ResultsIn this study, we found that 10 out of 13 m6A genes (including WTAP, ZC3H13, FTO, RBM15, METTL14, METTL3, HNRNPC, KIAA1429, YTHDF1, and YTHDF2) exhibited differential expression in patients with LUAD. Our objective was to determine the correlation between m6A methylation-related genes. Notably, coexpression patterns were observed between FTO and YTHDC2, while RBM15 demonstrated a significant positive correlation with the reader gene KIAA1429. In contrast, a negative correlation was identified between the methylation eraser FTO and the reader HNRNPC. We retrieved expression profiles of these genes from samples available in TCGA database. Through multivariate Cox regression analysis, we identified three m6A methylation-related genes (HNRNPC, KIAA1429, and RBM15) and included them in a prognostic model.ConclusionsIn patients diagnosed with LUAD, there is a significant correlation between m6A-related gene expression and tumor classification. Our study constructed a novel signature associated with m6A modifications, which can serve as a promising prognostic indicator for LUAD. These findings may provide valuable insights into diagnosis and therapeutic strategies for patients with LUAD.Keywords: Lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD), prognosis, N6-methyladenosine modifications (m6A modifications), biomarkers

Highlight box

Key findings

• N6-methyladenosine (m6A) methylation is a critical process in numerous types of cancer, significantly influencing both the onset and advancement of malignancy. The distinct components of m6A methylation may act as reliable indicators for the prognosis of patients with lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD).

What is known, and what is new?

• It is possible to evaluate m6A methylation in patients with LUAD.

• Distinct subtypes of LUAD and biomarkers associated with m6A methylation were identified in this study.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• Incorporating m6A methylation expression into an LUAD prediction model may facilitate the development of targeted treatment strategies.

Introduction

Lung cancer is the most prevalent and lethal type of cancer, with lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) accounting for nearly 60% of newly diagnosed cases (1-3). Standard treatment modalities for patients diagnosed with LUAD include surgical resection, chemotherapy, radiotherapy, molecular targeted therapies, and immunotherapy. Nonetheless, the aggressive characteristics of tumor growth and the emergence of resistance to existing treatment options have resulted in a 5-year survival rate of only 30% for individuals afflicted with LUAD (3). Consequently, it is imperative to clarify the underlying mechanisms that contribute to the pathogenesis of LUAD.

In addition to potentially curative surgical interventions, such as lobectomy or segmental lung resection, which may be augmented with adjuvant chemotherapy or radiotherapy, other targeted approaches, including EGFR-targeted therapies and immunotherapy, have significantly improved survival rates for patients with LUAD (4). The third-generation EGFR kinase inhibitor, osimertinib, has shown considerable efficacy in patients with advanced LUAD harboring EGFR mutations (4). Despite these therapeutic advancements, the prognosis remains poor, with an estimated overall 5-year survival rate of approximately 16%. According to the latest tumor-node-metastasis (TNM) staging criteria, the 5-year recurrence rates range from about 20% in patients with stage I disease to 50% in those with stage III disease (4-6). Thus, it is crucial to clarify the mechanisms that propel LUAD progression in order to improve prognostic outcomes.

A significant relationship between N6-methyladenosine (m6A) regulatory elements and their dual roles in tumor growth, which include both promotion and inhibition, has been established (7-9). The elements of m6A RNA methylation can be classified into three principal categories (7-10): the “writers”, including m6A methyltransferases such as METTL3, METTL14, METTL16, WTAP, KIAA1429, RBM15, and ZC3H13; the “erasers”, including m6A demethylases such as FTO and ALKBH5; and the “readers”, including m6A recognition factors such as YT521-B homology (YTH) domain family members (e.g., YTHDF1) and YTH domain-containing proteins (e.g., YTHDC1 and YTHDC2), as well as constituents of the heterogeneous nuclear ribonucleoproteins family (e.g., HNRNP). These elements are critical to essential processes such as cell proliferation, tumor development, and cancer cell dissemination, along with various specific characteristics of tumors (7-9). A study has also revealed the important role of m6A in tumor evolution and its effects on the tumor microenvironment (TME) (10). Additionally, there is strong evidence linking immune cells within the TME to m6A methylation, suggesting a connection that goes beyond simple RNA degradation (11). Ongoing research is further exploring how m6A modifications influence the interactions between cancer cells and their environment, thereby affecting tumor progression and immune responses (12). However, the specific roles of m6A in LUAD have not been extensively investigated.

Therefore, we conducted a comprehensive analysis aimed at quantifying the expression levels of m6A-related genes in LUAD, examined the correlations between these genes, and assessed their prognostic value. We present this article in accordance with the TRIPOD reporting checklist (available at https://tcr.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tcr-2025-aw-2305/rc).

Methods

Datasets from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) LUAD cohort

The analysis of RNA-sequencing data related to LUAD was conducted with information obtained from TCGA database (https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/repository). Among the 522 patients with LUAD examined, there were 280 females and 242 males. Of these patients, 279 were categorized as stage I, 124 as stage II, 85 as stage III, and 26 as stage IV. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments.

Data acquisition and processing

The m6A-related genes selected were prioritized due to their well-documented roles in m6A modification and their significant associations with tumorigenesis and cancer progression, as supported by previous literature (7-9) and preliminary analyses. A selection of potential genes implicated in m6A methylation was identified, including WTAP, YTHDC2, ZC3H13, FTO, RBM15, YTHDC1, METTL14, ALKBH5, METTL3, HNRNPC, KIAA1429, YTHDF1, and YTHDF2. The transcriptomic and clinical data for LUAD were extracted from TCGA database. Patients were excluded from the study if their survival duration, survival status, or clinicopathological characteristics were inadequately defined.

Consensus clustering analysis

Patients diagnosed with LUAD were classified into distinct subgroups based on the consensus expression profiles of m6A methylation-related genes via the “ConsensusClusterPlus” R package (The R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). The robustness of the clustering results was confirmed through principal component analysis (PCA). The Kaplan-Meier method was used to compared the overall survival (OS) between the identified clusters. Variations in age, gender, grade, and stage across the clusters were analyzed with either the Wilcoxon rank-sum test or the Kruskal-Wallis test.

Immunohistochemical analysis

Proteomic expression profiles of HNRNPC, KIAA1429, and RBM15 in LUAD and normal tissues were analyzed via the Human Protein Atlas (HPA) database (https://www.proteinatlas.org/), which complemented transcriptional-level observations.

Development and validation of the prognostic model

To identify prognostic m6A methylation-related genes, univariate Cox regression analysis was conducted. Following this, least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression analysis, a machine learning technique, was employed to reduce the risk of overfitting. Multivariate Cox regression analysis was then applied to identify the m6A methylation-related genes that were independent prognostic markers for OS. All patients with LUAD were stratified into high-risk and low-risk groups based on the median risk score. Survival curves were generated to compare the OS between the two risk categories. Additionally, the predictive accuracy of the prognostic model was evaluated via receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed with R software version 3.6.1. Univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses, LASSO regression analysis, and ROC curve analysis were conducted with the appropriate R packages. A significance level of P<0.05 was established for all comparisons.

Results

The expression pattern of m6A-related genes in LUAD

We analyzed the expression profiles of 13 m6A-related genes in patients with LUAD from TCGA and compared them with those of normal controls. The differential expression of thirteen genes (WTAP, YTHDC2, ZC3H13, FTO, RBM15, YTHDC1, METTL14, ALKBH5, METTL3, HNRNPC, KIAA1429, YTHDF1, and YTHDF2) in patients with LUAD was visualized in a heatmap (Figure 1A). Furthermore, violin plots indicated that m6A-related molecules, specifically the m6A writers (KIAA1429, METTL3, METTL14, RBM15, WTAP, and ZC3H13), m6A readers (HNRNPC, YTHDF1, and YTHDF2), and the m6A eraser (FTO), exhibited significant upregulation in the tumor tissues of patients with LUAD (Figure 1B).

To characterize the interrelationships among the various m6A genes, a correlation analysis was conducted. The strength of the interaction between each pair of genes is represented by the size and color of spheres in the visualization in Figure 1; in the figure, larger spheres indicate stronger associations, with red denoting a positive correlation, blue indicating a negative correlation, and “×” representing the absence of correlation. Notably, coexpression patterns were observed between FTO and YTHDC2, while RBM15 demonstrated a significant positive correlation with the reader gene KIAA1429 (Figure 1C). In contrast, a negative correlation was identified between the methylation eraser FTO and the reader HNRNPC.

LUAD subtypes based on the m6A-related genes

Research indicates there being a strong correlation between m6A-related genes and tumor progression (7-10); however, additional investigations are necessary to elucidate the specific mechanisms by which m6A-related genes influence LUAD. A consensus clustering analysis was performed with the aforementioned m6A-related genes (WTAP, YTHDC2, ZC3H13, FTO, RBM15, YTHDC1, METTL14, ALKBH5, METTL3, HNRNPC, KIAA1429, YTHDF1, and YTHDF2) to further categorize patients from the TCGA-LUAD cohort.

Consistency cluster analysis and PCA of TCGA-LUAD patients yielded two distinct clusters (Figure 2A-2C). As indicated in a heatmap illustrating the two patient groups within the TCGA-LUAD dataset (Figure 2D), the expression profiles of m6A-relateed genes exhibited clear differences, with OS differing significantly between clusters 1 and 2 (P=0.01; Figure 2E).

Subsequently, the relationships between the identified clusters and various clinicopathological characteristics were assessed. Clusters 1 and 2 demonstrated significant difference in terms of tumor grade (P<0.001) and metastasis stage (P<0.05) (Figure 3).

Construction of an m6A-related gene-based diagnostic signature

To identify the diagnostic biomarkers associated with m6A, we performed an analysis of TCGA-LUAD samples employing a LASSO logistic regression model. We identified three potential biomarkers, all of which exhibited nonzero coefficients in the LASSO logistic regression (Figure 4A-4C). The m6A diagnostic signature was constructed as follows: disease-mortality score (DMS) = expHNRNPC * 0.00825 + expKIAA1429 * 0.02716 + expRBM15 * 0.04948.

Significant differences in OS were observed between the high- and low-risk LUAD cohorts (P<0.05; Figure 4D). The m6A-related gene signature demonstrated moderate diagnostic performance, with an area of under the curve of 0.581 in the ROC analysis (Figure 4E). Transcriptomic profiling revealed elevated expression of HNRNPC, KIAA1429, and RBM15 in high-risk patients as compared to low-risk patients.

Independent prognostic analysis of the m6A-related gene-based prognostic model

To determine whether the three-gene signature is an independent prognostic marker for patients with LUAD, both univariable and multivariable Cox regression analyses were performed with the TCGA-LUAD dataset. The results of the univariate Cox analysis indicated that three genes—HNRNPC, KIAA1429, and RBM15—along with the tumor T stage and tumor N stage, exhibited a significant correlation with OS (Figure 5A). However, multivariate Cox analysis identified only our model as an independent prognostic factor for LUAD (P<0.001; Figure 5B).

Discussion

The process of carcinogenesis in LUAD is governed by a complex regulatory framework. Therefore, in contrast to the reliance on a singular clinicopathological parameter or gene, a prognostic model integrating multiple biomarkers can serve as a more dependable method for predicting tumor outcomes (1-3). m6A methylation figures prominently in the malignant process across various cancer types, including in their emergence and progression (11). A prognostic model grounded in selected m6A methylation-associated genes may offer enhanced accuracy and efficacy compared to a solitary clinicopathological parameter (11,12).

In our study, we observed that 10 out of 13 m6A-associated genes (including WTAP, ZC3H13, FTO, RBM15, METTL14, METTL3, HNRNPC, KIAA1429, YTHDF1, and YTHDF2) were differentially expressed in patients with LUAD. Our objective was to investigate the correlation between m6A methylation-related genes and the prognosis of individuals diagnosed with LUAD. We retrieved the expression profiles of these genes from samples available in TCGA database. Gene-based diagnostic signatures have emerged as pivotal tools in oncology, with extensive evidence supporting their association with tumorigenesis and cancer progression (13-16). These genes regulate core oncogenic processes—including proliferation, differentiation, apoptosis, and invasion—through the modulation of key signaling pathways (13-16). Notably, the integration of m6A-related biomarkers into diagnostic frameworks has gained significant traction, offering novel insights into the development of precision oncology strategies (17). Through multivariate Cox regression analysis, we identified three m6A methylation-related genes (HNRNPC, KIAA1429, and RBM15) and incorporated them to into a prognostic model. Given the constraints of our dataset, we plan to further assess the applicability of this model across a diversity of patient populations.

A growing body of evidence has indicated that m6A modification plays a crucial role in multiple stages of tumor development. Extracellular vesicles derived from serum facilitate the proliferation and metastasis of non-small cell lung cancer by transporting the m6A methylation regulator HNRNPC, which is regulated by DLGAP5 (17). Moreover, KIAA1429 facilitates the development of tumors and contributes to gefitinib resistance in LUAD through the activation of the JNK/MAPK signaling pathway, which is dependent on m6A modifications (18). Meanwhile, the downregulation of RBM15 facilitates ferroptosis through the modulation of the TGF-β/Smad2 signaling pathway in lung cancer (19). RBM15 has been shown to facilitate osimertinib resistance in LUAD by enhancing m6A modification of SPOCK1 mRNA, promoting epithelial-mesenchymal transition and resistance through a bypass activation pathway (20). This indicates that RBM15 plays a significant role in the progression of LUAD by modulating m6A-dependent pathways. Similarly, KIAA1429, an RNA methyltransferase, is implicated in LUAD tumorigenesis by regulating BTG2 expression via m6A-YTHDF2-dependent mechanisms. The gene amplification of KIAA1429 leads to its overexpression, which correlates with poor overall survival and promotes proliferation and metastasis in LUAD (21). These findings collectively suggest that both RBM15 and KIAA1429 contribute to LUAD progression through m6A-mediated pathways. Further supporting the synergistic roles of RBM15 and KIAA1429, studies have demonstrated that RBM15-mediated m6A modification stabilizes mRNA targets such as LDHA and XPR1, promoting malignant progression in LUAD (22,23).

The development of signatures based on m6A methylation-related genes may help further innovate the therapeutic approaches for individuals diagnosed with LUAD. However, certain limitations in our study should be acknowledged in this study. Unfortunately, due to the limited availability of independent LUAD datasets with complete m6A gene expression and survival data, we were unable to perform external validation in this study. Importantly, we employed a retrospective design, which might have biased the selection of variables. Additionally, the mechanisms by which m6A methylation-associated genes exert their effects in LUAD remain to be verified through both in vivo and in vitro studies, which may further confirm our findings.

This study focused on 13 core m6A regulators selected for their well-established roles in RNA modification and cancer progression, enabling a targeted investigation of functionally impactful genes. Although this approach clarified the oncological mechanisms related to m6A methylation and LUAD, we acknowledge that expanding the gene set could provide a more comprehensive understanding of m6A-mediated oncogenesis. Future studies will incorporate additional m6A-related genes to further elucidate the regulatory network.

Conclusions

We established a multigene model for OS that demonstrated value in predicting the prognosis of patients with LUAD and may enhance the treatment strategies for these patients.

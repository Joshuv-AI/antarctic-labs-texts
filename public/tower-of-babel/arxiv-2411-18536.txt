Chemical pressure tuning of competing orders in Ba 1 − x ​ Ca x ​ Ni 2 ​ As 2 \textrm{Ba}_{1-x}\textrm{Ca}_{x}\textrm{Ni}_{2}\textrm{As}_{2}
F. Henssler, K. Willa, M. Frachet, T. Lacmann, D. A. Chaney, M. Merz, A.-A. Haghighirad, M. Le Tacon

Abstract
BaNi 2 ​ As 2 \mathrm{Ba}\mathrm{Ni}_{2}\mathrm{As}_{2} , a structural-analogue to the iron-based parent compound BaFe 2 As 2 , offers a unique platform to study the interplay between superconductivity, charge density waves and, possibly, electronic nematicity. Here, we report on the growth and characterization of Ba 1 − x ​ Ca x ​ Ni 2 ​ As 2 \mathrm{Ba}_{1-x}\mathrm{Ca}_{x}\mathrm{Ni}_{2}\mathrm{As}_{2} single crystals with 0 ≤ x ≤ 0.1 0\leq x\leq 0.1 , using a combination of x-ray diffraction, diffuse x-ray scattering, heat capacity, and electronic transport measurements. Our results demonstrate that calcium substitution affects the structural, electronic and thermodynamic properties of BaNi 2 ​ As 2 \mathrm{Ba}\mathrm{Ni}_{2}\mathrm{As}_{2} in a way that is strongly reminiscent of moderate hydrostatic pressures albeit with marked differences. In particular Ca-substitution efficiently suppresses both the triclinic structural transition and the associated commensurate charge density wave formation, while increasing the superconducting transition temperature. We found that the substitution range in which the crystals remain homogeneous is limited as for concentrations x ≥ 0.04 x\geq 0.04 intense diffuse x-ray scattering indicates the formation of stacking faults, which, despite the preserved integrity of the NiAs layers, prevents investigation up to concentrations at which the chemical pressure would completely suppress the structural instability.



I
Introduction

The coexistence of magnetically or charge-ordered phases with superconductivity is a common feature in the complex phase diagrams of quantum materials. A major challenge in this field is to unravel the nature of the interplay between these electronic phases
Fernandes et al. 2019
. This is crucial for understanding unconventional superconductivity, where the emergent properties of quantum materials often depend on a delicate balance and interplay of different electronic orders. This balance can be experimentally tuned using controllable parameters such as pressure, strain, magnetic field, or charge carrier concentration
Basov et al. 2017
.
Chemical substitution is one of the most widely used and effective methods for exploring the electronic landscape of unconventional superconducting materials, including high-temperature superconducting cuprates
Keimer et al. 2015
and iron-based superconductors
Stewart 2011
. The present work focuses on the superconducting compound
\mathrm{Ba}\mathrm{Ni}_{2}\mathrm{As}_{2}

Ronning et al. 2008
, which at room temperature shares the
\mathrm{Th}\mathrm{Cr}_{2}\mathrm{Si}_{2}
tetragonal
I
4
/mmm
crystal structure with BaFe
2
As
2
, the parent compound of iron-based superconductors. However,
\mathrm{Ba}\mathrm{Ni}_{2}\mathrm{As}_{2}
exhibits a very different electronic phase diagram. Recently, it has attracted significant attention for hosting multiple charge density waves (as opposed to magnetic phases, which have not been detected so far) combined with structural distortions
Lee et al. 2019
;
Lee et al. 2021
;
Merz et al. 2021
;
Meingast et al. 2022
;
Yao et al. 2022
;
Lacmann et al. 2023
;
Souliou et al. 2022
;
Song et al. 2023
. The interplay between these features and the superconducting phase remains elusive.
Earlier studies of
\mathrm{Ba}\mathrm{Ni}_{2}\mathrm{As}_{2}
revealed a strong first-order structural transition to a triclinic (
P\bar{1}
) phase below
T_{\mathrm{S}}=$135\text{\,}\mathrm{K}$
(upon cooling), followed by a low-temperature superconducting instability at
T_{c}=$0.6\text{\,}\mathrm{K}$
, presumably of conventional BCS type

. More recently, it was discovered that a long-range commensurate charge density wave (C-CDW) with a characteristic wavevector of

1/3 0 -1/3

)

Lee et al. 2019
;
Merz et al. 2021

;
Song et al. 2023

(

The electronic phase diagram of

has been explored using hydrostatic pressure

Collini et al. 2023

;
Eckberg et al. 2020

On the one hand, a recent study on the Sr-substituted compound
\mathrm{Ba}_{1-x}\mathrm{Sr}_{x}\mathrm{Ni}_{2}\mathrm{As}_{2}
revealed the presence of a novel C-CDW with

0 1/2 1/2

Lee et al. 2021

On the other hand, elastoresistivity and thermodynamic investigations in phosphorus substituted samples revealed an overall different (but equally rich) phase diagram

;
Meingast et al. 2022
;
Yao et al. 2022

Ronning et al. 2008

Kudo et al. 2012

The available data indicate that the electronic phase of
\mathrm{Ba}\mathrm{Ni}_{2}\mathrm{As}_{2}
is particularly sensitive to the lattice parameter ratio

Meingast et al. 2022

Indeed, crystallographic data show that substituting

Furthermore, it is worth noting that whereas only

\mathrm{Ba}\mathrm{Ni}_{2}\mathrm{As}_{2}
through, e.g., the introduction of disorder, calling in turn for careful studies of the evolution of lattice and electronic degrees of freedom of
\mathrm{Ba}\mathrm{Ni}_{2}\mathrm{As}_{2}

In this work, we explore the alternative isovalent substitution of Ba with Ca rather than with Sr. In the 122 Fe-based family, the electronic properties of
\mathrm{Ca}\mathrm{Fe}_{2}\mathrm{As}_{2}
exhibit an unusually strong pressure dependence. A moderate hydrostatic pressure of

In the

\mathrm{Sr}\mathrm{Ni}_{2}\mathrm{As}_{2}

We report on the crystal structure and characterization of the basic electronic properties of
\mathrm{Ba}_{1-x}\mathrm{Ca}_{x}\mathrm{Ni}_{2}\mathrm{As}_{2}
single crystals up to
x\sim 0.1

x\sim 0.04
and characterized them using energy-dispersive x-ray spectroscopy (EDX), x-ray diffraction (XRD), diffuse x-ray scattering (DS), electrical transport and heat capacity measurements. At higher Ca concentrations, we find a strong compression of the
a
-axis parameter, however, the crystals tend to be less homogeneous and reciprocal space reconstructions reveal long streaks along the reciprocal
L

9\text{\,}\%
of
\mathrm{Ca}
substitution suppresses the triclinic phase transition temperature down to about
100\text{\,}\mathrm{K}
(which would require about
50\text{\,}\%
of
\mathrm{Sr}
) and increases the superconducting transition temperature by
\sim$50\text{\,}\%$
. In the investigated Ca-concentration range, the substitution yields a compression of lattice parameters similar to that obtained under hydrostatic pressure up to
\sim$1.5\text{\,}\mathrm{GPa}$

Lacmann et al. 2023

\mathrm{Ba}_{1-x}\mathrm{Ca}_{x}\mathrm{Ni}_{2}\mathrm{As}_{2}

IV
Discussion

Figure 7:
Phase diagram of
\mathrm{Ba}_{1-x}\mathrm{Ca}_{x}\mathrm{Ni}_{2}\mathrm{As}_{2}

We start our discussion by summarizing our experimental results in the phase diagram shown in Fig.
7
. After a small initial increase,
T_{\mathrm{S,c}}
(measured on cooling, hereafter referred to as
T_{\mathrm{S}}
) decreases continuously from about
137\text{\,}\mathrm{K}
to below
100\text{\,}\mathrm{K}
. In the entire substitution range investigated in this work, the phase transition into the triclinic phase remains hysteretic and first order. Above it, we detect an I-CDW phase similar to that of the parent compound, with a small increase of the incommensureate ordering vector
q_{\textrm{I-CDW}}
with
x
.
At low temperature, our thermodynamic and transport measurements detect a superconducting transition at a temperature that increases with
\mathrm{Ca}
content from
0.6\text{\,}\mathrm{K}
to
1\text{\,}\mathrm{K}
. No additional structural or electronic phases were detected in the investigated substitution range.

The decrease of
T_{S}
with increasing Ca concentration we report is qualitatively similar to the effects observed upon P-substitution on the As site, or Sr-substitution on the Ba site
(
Kudo et al. 2012
;
Meingast et al. 2022
;
Eckberg et al. 2020
)
. Focusing on the Ba-site substitutions, we note that quantitatively Ca substitutions are significantly more effective in suppressing
T_{S}
than Sr. Indeed
T_{\mathrm{S}}=$120\text{\,}\mathrm{K}$
is reached with only
x_{\mathrm{Ca}}\sim 0.035
, while
\mathrm{Sr}
-concentration of about
x_{\mathrm{Sr}}\sim 0.3
are needed. Similarly, we reached
T_{\mathrm{S}}=$100\text{\,}\mathrm{K}$
with
x_{\mathrm{Ca}}\sim 0.09
, which would take
x_{\mathrm{Sr}}\sim 0.5
Eckberg et al. 2020
. A simple extrapolation suggests that a concentration between 15 and
20\text{\,}\%
of Ca would be sufficient to suppress completely the triclinic/C-CDW phase.
Given that these substitutions are isovalent, this difference cannot be attributed to charge-doping effects. Furthermore, since the most efficient suppression is achieved in the system requiring the least amount of substitution, we can conclude that the rate of
T_{S}
decrease is not controlled by the associated cationic disorder.
In comparison to the Sr case, which mostly impacts the
c
-axis, while leaving
a
barely affected up to
x_{\mathrm{Sr}}\sim 0.7

Eckberg et al. 2020
, a contraction of both
a
- and
c
-axis lattice parameters can be seen already from
x_{\mathrm{Ca}}\sim 0.03
. This suggests that the modifications induced by Ca substitutions in
\mathrm{Ba}\mathrm{Ni}_{2}\mathrm{As}_{2}
are most likely rooted in chemical pressure effects.
Along this line, we note that Ca-doping of the Ba site appears unique since it preserves the NiAs layers, and both,
c
and
a
, (globally) decrease, mimicking the effect of hydrostatic pressure. A direct comparison of the relative lattice parameters changes induced by pressure
Lacmann et al. 2023
indicate that
3\text{\,}\%
of
\mathrm{Ca}
have the same effect on the sample unit cell as
\sim$0.5\text{\,}\mathrm{GPa}$
of hydrostatic pressure.
The rather weak dependence of the onset temperature of the I-CDW transition and the increase of the ordering vector
q_{\textrm{I-CDW}}
from 0.28 to 0.285 at
x=0.08
are indeed comparable to the effect of a hydrostatic compression of
\sim$1.5\text{\,}\mathrm{GPa}$

Lacmann et al. 2023
.
Nonetheless, the suppression of
T_{S}
and the small increase of the superconducting
T_{c}
upon Ca-substitution are stronger than the reported effects of pressure
Park et al. 2010
and reminiscent of the Sr and P-substituted cases.
Importantly, in this same regime Sr- and P- substituted samples show a large
B_{1g}
elastoresistance signal, whose possible electronic nematic origin is debated
Frachet et al. 2022
;
Eckberg et al. 2020
;
Meingast et al. 2022
. Although no such experiments have been performed yet on Ca-doped samples, the strong similarity of freestanding electrical resistance of Ca- and P-substituted samples strongly suggests a similar behavior. Our specific heat results (Fig.
6
(c)) further indicate that within this doping range superconductivity remains in the weak-coupling BCS limit. Finally, the limited doping range investigated did not allow us to fully suppress the coincident C-CDW and triclinic phase transition, where
T_{c}
has been reported to sharply increase to
\sim 3
K in P-
Kudo et al. 2012
;
Meingast et al. 2022
or Sr-
Eckberg et al. 2020
substituted samples. An optimization of the growth conditions appears needed to reach these optimal conditions for superconductivity while avoiding stacking faults.
This might be improved by increasing the growth temperature above 1250
∘
C, which however calls for the use of alternative growth conditions (crucible material, gas pressure, etc.). Furthermore, the increased vapour pressure of binary arsenides, CaAs and NiAs used in the growth at these elevated temperatures probably requires high-pressure synthesis similar to that used for BaNi
2
P
2

Kito et al. 2008
;
Tomioka et al. 2008
.

V
Summary

In conclusion, we report the growth and a detailed investigation of
\mathrm{Ba}_{1-x}\mathrm{Ca}_{x}\mathrm{Ni}_{2}\mathrm{As}_{2}
single crystals, up to
x\sim 0.1
. Upon Ca-substitution, akin to earlier studies on Sr- and P-substituted samples, the triclinic and associated C-CDW transition are suppressed while the superconducting one is enhanced. In contrast, and especially compared to the effect of Sr-substitution, which also occurs on the Ba site, the effect of Ca on the crystal structure appears to be much closer to that of hydrostatic pressure
Lacmann et al. 2023
. As a consequence, the amount of Ca required to suppress the triclinic transition temperature is significantly lower than that of Sr, which opens a promising avenue to study the possible nematicity of the compound at lower disorder level.
Our thermodynamic measurements strongly suggest that, within the entire range investigated, superconductivity remains of weak-coupling BCS type. However, for
x_{\textrm{Ca}}\geq 0.04
we observe the evidence of the formation of numerous stacking faults, which limits the range of our investigation and in particular of the relevance of the
c/a
ratio in the phase tuning. While this observation calls for the optimization of the growth conditions, we stress that this should not preclude investigating the possible electronic nematicity of
\mathrm{Ba}_{1-x}\mathrm{Ca}_{x}\mathrm{Ni}_{2}\mathrm{As}_{2}
.

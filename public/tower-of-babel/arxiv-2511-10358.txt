Introduction

This work can be situated within the framework of observability for the free Schrödinger equation

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u(x,t)=i\Delta u(x,t),&(x,t)\in X\times(0,\infty),\\

Our study analyzes and compares observability properties of the free Schrödinger equation in discrete (combinatorial graphs) and continuous settings. General observability criteria and quantitative inequalities for the free discrete Schrödinger flow on combinatorial graphs remain largely open. In combinatorial graphs, the lack of a smooth differential structure and geodesic flow makes standard PDE tools such as Carleman estimates

Consequently, establishing observability needs to rely on discrete spectral features and arithmetic sampling effects, rendering the analysis qualitatively different and technically more delicate. Our contribution establishes observability inequalities for the free discrete model on common combinatorial graphs and quantitatively compares them with the continuum theory.

Theorem 1.2

Let

We have the following remark to contrast the techniques employed in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with their discrete counterparts in
\mathbb{Z}^{d}

(
\mathbf{R}_{3}
)

On

In our lattice setting, we avoid such microlocal machinery: the proof is based on a
TT^{*}
operator-norm argument together with specific spectral features of the discrete Laplacian. Concretely, the projection onto the finite hole

has finite rank, so its time-averaged contribution cannot dominate the identity, which yields coercivity and the observability inequality.

Our third result addresses the finite (compact) setting and should be compared with the theory on compact Riemannian manifolds. On a finite graph, the Schrödinger flow evolves in a finite-dimensional Hilbert space, and observability reduces to a purely spectral injectivity question. This yields a sharp characterization, stated as following.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
G=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E})
be a finite graph with discrete Laplacian
\Delta_{\mathrm{disc}}
, and let
E\subseteq\mathcal{V}
. The following are equivalent:

(i)

E
is observable at any time
T>0
.

(ii)

For every nonzero eigenvector
\psi

\Delta_{\mathrm{disc}}
, its restriction
\psi|_{E}
is not identically zero.

For Theorem
1.3
, we have the following remark.

(
\mathbf{R}_{4}
)

It is classical that on a compact Riemannian manifold, any open set satisfying the geometric control condition is observable at any time for the wave and Schrödinger equations (see e.g.
[

41

The final result concerns the sets on discrete tori of large density which are not observable at any time, providing a comparison with the standard tori.

2.1.
Fourier transform on the lattice

For any
f\in l^{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}^{d}\right)
, its Fourier transform
\widehat{f}:\mathbb{T}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
is defined by

\widehat{f}\left(x\right):=\frac{1}{\left(2\pi\right)^{\frac{d}{2}}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}f\left(n\right)e^{in\cdot x}\qquad{\rm for}\;x\in\mathbb{T}^{d},

and for any
f\in L^{2}\left(\mathbb{T}^{d}\right)
, its inverse Fourier transform
f^{\vee}
is

f^{\vee}\left(n\right):=\frac{1}{\left(2\pi\right)^{\frac{d}{2}}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}f\left(x\right)e^{-in\cdot x}\mathrm{d}x\qquad{\rm for}\;n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}.

The Plancherel identity shows that for any
f\in l^{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}^{d}\right)
,

(3)

\|f\|_{l^{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}^{d}\right)}=\|\widehat{f}\|_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{T}^{d}\right)}.

The convolution
f\ast g
of
f,g\in l^{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}^{d}\right)
is defined by

f\ast g\left(n\right):=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}f\left(m\right)g\left(n-m\right),

and hence for
x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
, we have

\left(f\ast g\right)^{\wedge}\left(x\right)=\left(2\pi\right)^{\frac{d}{2}}\widehat{f}\left(x\right)\widehat{g}\left(x\right)\qquad{\rm for}\;x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}.

Here and below, we identify
\mathbb{T}^{d}
with
\left[-\pi,\pi\right)^{d}
. By basic Fourier analysis, the solution
u\left(n,t\right):\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times\left[0,\infty\right)\to\mathbb{C}
of Equation (
1
) with an initial data
u_{0}\in l^{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}^{d}\right)
is as follows:

u\left(n,t\right)=e^{it\Delta_{\rm disc}}u_{0}\left(n\right):=\left(e^{2it\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left(\cos x_{j}-1\right)}\widehat{u_{0}}\left(x\right)\right)^{\vee}\left(n\right)=u_{0}\ast K_{t}\left(n\right),

where the kernel function
K_{t}:\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
is defined by

K_{t}\left(n\right):=\frac{1}{\left(2\pi\right)^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}e^{2it\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left(\cos x_{j}-1\right)}e^{-in\cdot x}\mathrm{d}x.

We denote the Fourier multiplier of Laplacian
\Delta_{\rm disc}
by

(4)

\varphi\left(x\right):=2\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left(\cos x_{j}-1\right)

for simplicity. Let
u\in C^{1}([0,+\infty);l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d}))
be a solution of Equation (
1
), we have the

conservation law:

(5)

\|e^{it\Delta_{\rm disc}}u_{0}\|_{l^{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}^{d}\right)}=\|u_{0}\|_{l^{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}^{d}\right)}.

2.2.
Beurling density for discrete sets

In this subsection, we recall the notion of Beurling density and prove a discrete Logvinenko-Sereda uncertainty principle.

Definition 2.1
.

[Beurling density]
For a discrete set
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{Z}
, its lower Beurling density
d^{-}\left(E\right)
and upper Beurling density
d^{+}\left(E\right)
are given by

d^{-}\left(\Lambda\right):=\liminf_{R\to\infty}\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{\left|\Lambda\cap\left[x-R,x+R\right]\right|}{2R}\;{\rm and}\;d^{+}\left(\Lambda\right):=\limsup_{R\to\infty}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{\left|\Lambda\cap\left[x-R,x+R\right]\right|}{2R}.

We say a discrete set
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{Z}
has Beurling density
\gamma
if
d^{-}\left(\Lambda\right)=d^{+}\left(\Lambda\right)=\gamma
.

We remark that using integer or real-valued centers is equivalent in the definition of density.

We use the notation of Paley-Wiener space
\mathcal{PW}_{a}:=\left\{f\in L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right):{\rm spt}\mathcal{F}f\subset\left[-\pi a,\pi a\right]\right\}
, it consists of restrictions of entire functions of exponential type to the real line. Here and below,
\mathcal{F}f
is the Fourier transform of
f
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
defined by

\mathcal{F}f(y):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)e^{-ixy}\mathrm{d}x\qquad{\rm for}\;y\in\mathbb{R}.

Recall a discrete subset
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}
is a
sampling set
for
\mathcal{PW}_{a}
if for any
f\in\mathcal{PW}_{a}
,

A\sum_{x\in\Lambda}\left|f\left(x\right)\right|^{2}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|f\left(x\right)\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\leq B\sum_{x\in\Lambda}\left|f\left(x\right)\right|^{2},

where the implicit constants
A,B
are independent of function
f
.

The following theorem of Beurling gives an almost complete characterization of sampling sets for the Paley-Wiener space, which can be found in
[
6
,
22
]

Theorem 2.1

(Beurling)
.

The notations are given as above, then we have

(a).

If
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}
is discrete and
d^{-}\left(\Lambda\right)>1
, then
\Lambda
is a sampling set for
\mathcal{PW}_{1}
.

(b).

Conversely, if
\Lambda
is a sampling set for
\mathcal{PW}_{1}
, then
d^{-}\left(\Lambda\right)\geq 1
.

Remark 2.1

(On the separation hypothesis)
.

The classical Beurling sampling theorem on
\mathbb{R}
typically assumes that the sampling set is separated, i.e., there exists
q>0
such that any two distinct points are at distance at least
q
.
In our discrete setting, every
E\subset\mathbb{Z}
is automatically
1
-separated in the standard metric. After the rescaling
\sigma=\frac{a}{2\pi}
(which corresponds to the bandwidth normalization used in the proof of Theorem
2.2
), the set
\sigma E
remains
q=\sigma
separated.
Therefore, the separation hypothesis required by the theorem is automatically satisfied in our application; see
[
6
,
22
]
.

The following basic Propositions will be used in the proof of Theorem
2.2
and Theorem
1.1
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{Z}
be a
\gamma
-thick set. Then its lower density satisfies

d^{-}(E)\geq\gamma.

Moreover, whenever
\gamma\geq\frac{1}{2}
, we have the sharper bound

d^{-}(E)>\frac{1}{2}.

Proof.

Let
m:=2L+1
(so
m
is odd). By integrality of counts, the thickness condition implies

|E\cap[x-L,x+L]|\ \geq\ \big\lceil\gamma m\big\rceil\qquad\text{for all }x\in\mathbb{Z}.

For each
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, define the
m
-length integer-centered block

B_{k}:=[km-L,\,km+L]\subset\mathbb{R}.

Then
|E\cap B_{k}|\geq\lceil\gamma m\rceil
for every
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Fix
R>L
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
. A block
B_{k}
is fully contained in
[x-R,x+R]
if and only if

|km-x|\ \leq\ R-L.

Hence the set of such indices

K(x,R):=\{k\in\mathbb{Z}:\ B_{k}\subset[x-R,x+R]\}

is precisely the set of integers in an interval of length
\frac{2(R-L)}{m}
, whence

|K(x,R)|\ \geq\ \left\lfloor\frac{2(R-L)}{m}\right\rfloor.

Summing the per-block lower bounds gives

|E\cap[x-R,x+R]|\ \geq\ \sum_{k\in K(x,R)}|E\cap B_{k}|\ \geq\ |K(x,R)|\,\big\lceil\gamma m\big\rceil\ \geq\ \left\lfloor\frac{2(R-L)}{m}\right\rfloor\,\big\lceil\gamma m\big\rceil.

Therefore,

\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{|E\cap[x-R,x+R]|}{2R}\ \geq\ \frac{\big\lceil\gamma m\big\rceil}{2R}\left\lfloor\frac{2(R-L)}{m}\right\rfloor.

Letting
R\to\infty
and using
\frac{1}{2R}\left\lfloor\frac{2(R-L)}{m}\right\rfloor=\frac{1}{m}+o(1)
, we obtain

d^{-}(E)\ =\ \liminf_{R\to\infty}\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{|E\cap[x-R,x+R]|}{2R}\ \geq\ \frac{\big\lceil\gamma m\big\rceil}{m}\ \geq\ \gamma.

If
\gamma>\tfrac{1}{2}
then
d^{-}(E)\geq\gamma>\tfrac{1}{2}
. If
\gamma=\tfrac{1}{2}
, the oddness of
m
yields

\frac{\lceil\gamma m\rceil}{m}\ =\ \frac{\lceil m/2\rceil}{m}\ =\ \frac{L+1}{2L+1}\ >\ \tfrac{1}{2},

so
d^{-}(E)>\tfrac{1}{2}
also in this borderline case. This proves the proposition.
∎

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{Z}
have Beurling density
\gamma
.
Then for any
\gamma^{\prime}\in(0,\gamma)
, the set
E
is
\gamma^{\prime}
-thick on

Proof.

Since
d^{-}(E)=\gamma
, by the definition of the

for any fixed

. In particular, taking

with any integer

The next theorem states the classical Weyl equidistribution law together with its dynamical formulation for irrational rotations of the circle. If
\alpha\notin\mathbb{Q}

the Birkhoff averages

Theorem 2.4
.

[Weyl’s equidistribution law]
Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
with
\alpha\notin\mathbb{Q}
, then for any

is equidistributed. Moreover, for any

\frac{1}{M}\sum_{n=s}^{s+M-1}f\left(2\pi\left\{n\alpha\right\}\right)\rightarrow\int_{\mathbb{T}}f\left(x\right)\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{2\pi}\qquad{\rm uniformly\;in}\;s\in\mathbb{Z}\;{\rm as}\;M\to\infty.

For any fixed positive, irrational number

and irrational number

E_{\gamma}:=\left\{n\in 2\mathbb{Z}:\left\{n\alpha\right\}\in\left(\frac{1}{2}-\gamma,\frac{1}{2}+\gamma\right)\right\}.

.

E_{\gamma}
defined as (

\gamma

Proof.

We prove

So we derive

\sum_{y\in\left[x-R,x+R\right]\cap 2\mathbb{Z}}\mathbbm{1}_{\left(\pi-2\pi\gamma,\pi+2\pi\gamma\right)}\left(2\pi\left\{y\alpha\right\}\right)=\sum_{n=s}^{s+M-1}\mathbbm{1}_{\left(\pi-2\pi\gamma,\pi+2\pi\gamma\right)}\left(2\pi\left\{2n\alpha\right\}\right).

f^{-}_{\epsilon}\leq\mathbbm{1}_{\left(\pi-2\pi\gamma,\pi+2\pi\gamma\right)}\leq f^{+}_{\epsilon}\;{\rm on}\;\mathbb{T}\;{\rm and}\;\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left|f^{+}_{\epsilon}-f^{-}_{\epsilon}\right|<\epsilon.

Applying the Weyl’s Theorem, for any sufficiently large

\left|\frac{1}{M}\sum_{n=s}^{s+M-1}f^{\pm}_{\epsilon}\left(2\pi\left\{2n\alpha\right\}\right)-\int_{\mathbb{T}}f^{\pm}_{\epsilon}\left(x\right)\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{2\pi}\right|<\epsilon.

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\left|E_{\gamma}\cap\left[x-R,x+R\right]\right|}{2R}-\gamma\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left|\frac{1}{R}\sum_{n=s}^{s+M-1}\mathbbm{1}_{\left(\pi-2\pi\gamma,\pi+2\pi\gamma\right)}\left(2\pi\left\{2n\alpha\right\}\right)-\int_{\mathbb{T}}\mathbbm{1}_{\left(\pi-2\pi\gamma,\pi+2\pi\gamma\right)}\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{2\pi}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

2.5.
Observability for Bohr sets

In this subsection, we provide a complete characterization for the observability of any periodic Bohr set (see (
23
)), that is,

Theorem 2.5

(Complete arithmetic criterion)
.

Let
p\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
and
R\subset\{0,\dots,p-1\}
be not empty set. Then the following are equivalent:

(a)

E_{p,R}
is observable at any time.

(b)

The subgroup of
\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
generated by
D(R):=\big\{\,s_{1}-s_{0}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ p):\ s_{0},s_{1}\in R\,\big\}\ \subset\ \mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
equals the whole
\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
.

Denote the map
\mathcal{B}f\left(n\right):=\left(f(pn),f(pn+1),\dots,f(pn+p-1)\right)^{\top}\in\mathbb{C}^{p}
for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, then the Fourier transform
\widehat{\mathcal{B}f}:\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{C}^{p}
can be given by

\widehat{\mathcal{B}f}(x):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{B}f(n)e^{inx}\;{\rm for}\;x\in\mathbb{T}.

By the Plancherel formula (
3
), we have

\|f\|_{l^{2}(\mathbb{Z})}=\|\mathcal{B}f\|_{l^{2}(\mathbb{Z})}=\|\widehat{\mathcal{B}f}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}.

For each
p\geq 2
, for the solution
u(n,t)=e^{it\Delta_{\rm disc}}u_{0}(n)
, it satisfies that

\partial_{t}\widehat{\mathcal{B}u}(x,t)=i\Phi(x)\widehat{\mathcal{B}u}(x,t),\;\widehat{\mathcal{B}u}(x,0)=\widehat{\mathcal{B}u_{0}}(x)\;{\rm for}\;x\in\mathbb{T}.

Here the matrix-valued function
\Phi:\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{C}^{p\times p}
is defined by

\Phi(x):=2I_{p}-\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0&\cdots&0&e^{ix}\\
1&0&1&\cdots&0&0\\
0&1&0&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&1&0\\
0&\cdots&\cdots&1&0&1\\
e^{-ix}&0&0&\cdots&1&0\\
\end{pmatrix}.

Note for
p=2
,

\Phi(x)=\begin{pmatrix}2&-1-e^{ix}\\
-1-e^{-ix}&2\\
\end{pmatrix}.

Through direct computation, we derive the following lemma, characterizing the eigenvalues and eigenvectors of matrix
\Phi
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\Phi
be defined as above. For
x\in\mathbb{T}
and
s=0,1,\dots,p-1
, set

\eta_{s}(x):=\frac{-x+2\pi s}{p},\qquad U_{s}(x):=\big(e^{ir\,\eta_{s}(x)}\big)_{r=0}^{p-1}\in\mathbb{C}^{p},\qquad\mu_{s}(x):=2-2\cos\big(\eta_{s}(x)\big).

Then
\{U_{s}(x)\}_{s=0}^{p-1}
is an orthogonal basis of
\mathbb{C}^{p}
and

\Phi(x)\,U_{s}(x)=\mu_{s}(x)\,U_{s}(x),\qquad s=0,\dots,p-1.

Moreover, all eigenvalues are simple when
x\notin\{-\pi,0\}
. For
x\in\{-\pi,0\}
, each
\kappa\in(0,\pi)
yields a two-dimensional eigenspace
\mathrm{span}\{u_{\kappa},u_{-\kappa}\}
, while
\kappa\in\{-\pi,0\}
remains one-dimensional.

For any periodic Bohr set
E_{R,p}
, we define the restriction operator related to
E_{R,p}

\mathcal{R}:\mathbb{C}^{p}\to\mathbb{C}^{p},\;\mathcal{R}(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{p}):=(\mathbbm{1}_{R}(0)\cdot x_{1},\mathbbm{1}_{R}(1)\cdot x_{2},\dots,\mathbbm{1}_{R}(p-1)\cdot x_{p}).

Using this operator, one has

\sum_{n\in E_{R,p}}\left|f(n)\right|^{2}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|\mathcal{R}\mathcal{B}f(n)\right|^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left|\mathcal{R}\widehat{\mathcal{B}f}(x)\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}x,

and then

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{n\in E_{R,p}}\left|e^{it\Delta_{\rm disc}}u_{0}(n)\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left|\mathcal{R}\left(e^{it\Phi(\cdot)}\widehat{\mathcal{B}u_{0}}\right)\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{0}^{T}\left(\widehat{\mathcal{B}u_{0}}(x)\right)^{H}\left((\mathcal{R}e^{it\Phi(x)})^{H}(\mathcal{R}e^{it\Phi(x)})\right)\widehat{\mathcal{B}u_{0}}(x)\,\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left(\widehat{\mathcal{B}u_{0}}(x)\right)^{H}W_{T}(x)\widehat{\mathcal{B}u_{0}}(x)\mathrm{d}x,

Here and below
(\cdot)^{H}
denotes the conjugate transpose and we define
W_{T}(x)\in\mathbb{C}^{p\times p}
by

(29)

W_{T}(x):=\int_{0}^{T}e^{-it\Phi(x)}\mathcal{R}^{H}\mathcal{R}e^{it\Phi(x)}\,\mathrm{d}t.

The following proposition is a standard result in linear algebra; a similar discussion will arise in the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
\Phi
and
\mathcal{R}
defined as above. For
T>0
, the following are equivalent:

(i)

For every eigenvector
u
of
\Phi
,
\mathcal{R}u\neq 0
.

(ii)

W_{T}
defined in
29
is positive definite.

(iii)

There exists
C(T)>0
such that
\int_{0}^{T}\left|\mathcal{R}e^{-it\Phi}z\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}t\geq C(T)\left|z\right|^{2}
for all
z\in\mathbb{C}^{p}
.

Proof.

Diagonalize
\Phi=\sum_{\lambda}\lambda P_{\lambda}
with orthogonal spectral projectors
P_{\lambda}
. Then

W_{T}=\sum_{\lambda,\mu}\left(\int_{0}^{T}e^{-it(\lambda-\mu)}\,dt\right)P_{\lambda}\mathcal{R}^{H}\mathcal{R}P_{\mu}.

For
\lambda\neq\mu
, the oscillatory integral is bounded, while for
\lambda=\mu
it equals
T
. Moreover, restricted to each eigenspace
E_{\lambda}=P_{\lambda}\mathbb{C}^{p}
we have
P_{\mu}z=0
for
\mu\neq\lambda
and all
z\in E_{\lambda}
, hence the cross terms vanish on
E_{\lambda}
and

W_{T}|_{E_{\lambda}}=T\,(\mathcal{R}|_{E_{\lambda}})^{H}(\mathcal{R}|_{E_{\lambda}}).

Thus
W_{T}>0
if and only if
\mathcal{R}
is injective on every
E_{\lambda}
, i.e. (i). The equivalence with (iii) follows from the Rayleigh quotient characterization of the smallest eigenvalue of
W_{T}
.
∎

Now we begin to prove Theorem
2.5
.

Proof of Theorem
2.5
.

By Proposition
2.5
, (a) holds if and only if for every
x\in[-\pi,\pi)
, the pair
(\Phi(x),\mathcal{R})
satisfies that every eigenvector
u
of
\Phi(x)
obeys
\mathcal{R}u\neq 0
.

We consider nondegenerate fibers
x\notin\{0,-\pi\}
firstly. By Lemma
2.2
, each eigenspace is one-dimensional and is spanned by a vector
U_{s}(x)
whose entries are all nonzero.
\mathcal{R}U_{s}(x)\neq 0
since
R\neq\emptyset
, so every eigenvector
u
of
\Phi(x)
obeys
\mathcal{R}u\neq 0
.

Now we discuss degenerate fibers
x\in\{0,-\pi\}
. For each such
x
and
\kappa\in(0,\pi)
, the eigenspace is
E_{\kappa}=\mathrm{span}\{U_{+\kappa},U_{-\kappa}\}
where
U_{\pm\kappa}=(e^{\pm ir\kappa})_{r=0}^{p-1}
. An element
z=a_{+}U_{+\kappa}+a_{-}U_{-\kappa}
lies in
\ker\mathcal{R}
if and only if

a_{+}\,e^{ir\kappa}+a_{-}\,e^{-ir\kappa}=0,\qquad\forall\,r\in R.

Thus
\mathcal{R}|_{E_{\kappa}}
is injective if and only if there exist
r_{0},r_{1}\in R
such that

\det\begin{pmatrix}e^{ir_{0}\kappa}&e^{-ir_{0}\kappa}\\
e^{ir_{1}\kappa}&e^{-ir_{1}\kappa}\end{pmatrix}=-2i\,\sin\big((r_{1}-r_{0})\kappa\big)\neq 0,

equivalently, there exists
d\in D(R)
with
\sin(d\kappa)\neq 0
.

For
x=0
, the two-dimensional eigenspaces correspond to

\kappa=\frac{m\pi}{p},\qquad m\in\{2,4,\dots\}\cap[1,p-1],

and for
x=-\pi
, they correspond to

\kappa=\frac{m\pi}{p},\qquad m\in\{1,3,\dots\}\cap[1,p-1].

For such
\kappa
,

\sin(d\kappa)=0\ \Longleftrightarrow\ d\,\kappa\in\pi\mathbb{Z}\ \Longleftrightarrow\ p\mid m\,d.

Therefore, the injectivity of
\mathcal{R}
on every degenerate eigenspace on both fibers is equivalent to the requirement

(\ast)\qquad\forall\,m\in\{1,\dots,p-1\},\ \exists\,d\in D(R)\ \text{such that}\ p\nmid m\,d.

Let
G=\langle D(R)\rangle\leq\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
be the subgroup generated by
D(R)
. For each
m\in\{1,\dots,p-1\}
, set
\mathrm{Ker}(m):=\{x\in\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}:\ m\,x=0\}
. Then
(\ast)
is equivalent to

G\nsubseteq\mathrm{Ker}(m)\quad\text{for every }m=1,\dots,p-1.

Since
\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
is cyclic, there exists
g\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
with
g\mid p
such that
G=\{t\,g\ (\mathrm{mod}\ p):\ t\in\mathbb{Z}\}
, where

g=\gcd\big(p,\{r_{1}-r_{0}:\ r_{0},r_{1}\in R\}\big).

Moreover,
\mathrm{Ker}(m)=\{t\,(p/\gcd(p,m))\ (\mathrm{mod}\ p):\ t\in\mathbb{Z}\}
. In a cyclic group,
H_{a}\subset H_{b}
if and only if
b\mid a
. Hence

G\subset\mathrm{Ker}(m)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\frac{p}{\gcd(p,m)}\ \mid\ g.

If
g=1
, then for every
m
one has
\frac{p}{\gcd(p,m)}\geq 2
and thus does not divide
g
, so
G\nsubseteq\mathrm{Ker}(m)
and
(\ast)
holds. Conversely, if
g>1
, choose
m=p/g\in\{1,\dots,p-1\}
. Then
\frac{p}{\gcd(p,m)}=g\mid g
, so
G\subset\mathrm{Ker}(m)
, violating
(\ast)
.
Thus (a)
\Leftrightarrow
(b) follows by checking, on each fiber, that
\mathcal{R}
is injective on the eigenspaces of
\Phi(x)
.

Finally, when (b) holds,
W_{T}(x)
is positive definite for every
x\in\mathbb{T}
and
T>0
by Proposition
2.5
. The map
x\mapsto W_{T}(x)
is continuous on the compact set
\mathbb{T}
, hence its smallest eigenvalue

\lambda_{\min}(x):=\min_{\left|z\right|=1}\left\langle W_{T}(x)z,z\right\rangle

is continuous and strictly positive on
\mathbb{T}
. Therefore
m_{T}:=\min_{x\in\mathbb{T}}\lambda_{\min}(x)>0
, and

\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{n\in E_{R,p}}|u(n,t)|^{2}\,dt=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left\langle W_{T}(x)\widehat{\mathcal{B}u_{0}}(x),\widehat{\mathcal{B}u_{0}}(x)\right\rangle\,dx\geq m_{T}\,\|\widehat{\mathcal{B}u_{0}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}=m_{T}\,\|u_{0}\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})}^{2}.

Taking
C(T)=1/m_{T}
gives (a).
∎

2.6.
Observability for complements of
finite sets

We begin the proof of Theorem
1.2
with a basic spectral observation.

Fact 2.1
.

The discrete Laplacian
\Delta_{\rm disc}
does not admit any nonzero eigenfunction in
l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
.

Indeed, if not, let
f\in l^{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}^{d}\right)\setminus\{0\}
satisfies
\Delta_{\rm disc}f=\lambda f
. Applying the Fourier transform, the eigenfunction
f
satisfies

\varphi\left(x\right)\widehat{f}\left(x\right)=\lambda\widehat{f}\left(x\right),

where the Fourier multiplier
\varphi
we have defined in (
4
). This implies that

{\rm spt}\widehat{f}\subset\left\{x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}:\varphi\left(x\right)=\lambda\right\}.

Then
\widehat{f}=0\;a.e.\;{\rm on}\;\mathbb{T}^{d}
, and by the Plancherel identity (
3
) we derive
\|\widehat{f}\|_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{T}^{d}\right)}=\|f\|_{l^{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}^{d}\right)}=0
, which contradicts to the assumption.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let
E
be a subset of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
satisfying the assumption in Theorem
1.2
. By the conservation law (
5
) of the free Schrödinger equation, we have

\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{n\in E}\left|e^{it\Delta_{\rm disc}}u_{0}\left(n\right)\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}t=T\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\left|u_{0}\left(n\right)\right|^{2}-\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{n\in E^{c}}\left|e^{it\Delta_{\rm disc}}u_{0}\left(n\right)\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}t,

for any time
T>0
. We define the operator

U:l^{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}^{d}\right)\to L^{2}\left(\left(0,T\right),\mathbb{C}^{\left|E^{c}\right|}\right),\ \ Uf\left(t\right):=\left\{e^{it\Delta_{\rm disc}}f\left(n\right)\right\}_{n\in E^{c}}.

U
is well-defined via the trivial estimate

(30)

\|Uf\left(t\right)\|_{\mathbb{C}^{\left|E^{c}\right|}}\leq\|f\|_{l^{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}^{d}\right)}.

It is straightforward to calculate that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{n\in E^{c}}\left|e^{it\Delta_{\rm disc}}u_{0}\left(n\right)\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}t=\|Uu_{0}\|_{L^{2}\left(\left(0,T\right),\mathbb{C}^{\left|E^{c}\right|}\right)}^{2}=\|\mathbbm{1}_{E^{c}}e^{it\Delta_{\rm disc}}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}\left(\left(0,T\right),l^{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}^{d}\right)\right)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\langle\int_{0}^{T}e^{-it\Delta_{\rm disc}}\left(\mathbbm{1}_{E^{c}}e^{it\Delta_{\rm disc}}\right)u_{0}\mathrm{d}t,u_{0}\rangle_{l^{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}^{d}\right)}\leq\|\int_{0}^{T}e^{-it\Delta_{\rm disc}}\left(\mathbbm{1}_{E^{c}}e^{it\Delta_{\rm disc}}\right)\;\mathrm{d}t\|_{l^{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}^{d}\right)\to l^{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}^{d}\right)}\cdot\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{l^{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}^{d}\right)}.

Indeed, we apply the
TT^{\ast}
method in the last step. Below, we show that

\|S_{E^{c}}\left(T\right)\|_{l^{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}^{d}\right)\to l^{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}^{d}\right)}<T\;{\rm where}\;S_{E^{c}}\left(T\right):=\int_{0}^{T}e^{-it\Delta_{\rm disc}}\left(\mathbbm{1}_{E^{c}}e^{it\Delta_{\rm disc}}\right)\;\mathrm{d}t.

First,
\|S_{E^{c}}\left(T\right)\|_{l^{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}^{d}\right)\to l^{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}^{d}\right)}\leq T
holds since the duality argument

\displaystyle\|S_{E^{c}}\left(T\right)\|_{l^{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}^{d}\right)\to l^{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}^{d}\right)}=

\displaystyle\sup_{\|u_{0}\|_{l^{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}^{d}\right)},\|v_{0}\|_{l^{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}^{d}\right)}\leq 1}\left|\langle\int_{0}^{T}e^{-it\Delta_{\rm disc}}\left(\mathbbm{1}_{E^{c}}e^{it\Delta_{\rm disc}}\right)u_{0}\mathrm{d}t,v_{0}\rangle_{l^{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}^{d}\right)}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{\|u_{0}\|_{l^{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}^{d}\right)},\|v_{0}\|_{l^{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}^{d}\right)}\leq 1}\int_{0}^{T}\|e^{it\Delta_{\rm disc}}u_{0}\|_{l^{2}\left(E^{c}\right)}\|e^{it\Delta_{\rm disc}}v_{0}\|_{l^{2}\left(E^{c}\right)}\mathrm{d}t\leq T.

Second,
\|S_{E^{c}}(T)\|_{l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})\to l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}<T
is proved by contradiction, exploiting specific properties of
S_{E^{c}}(T)
. We define the kernel function
K_{E^{c}}\left(T\right):\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
,

\displaystyle K_{E^{c}}\left(T\right)\left(x,y\right):=\sum_{n\in E^{c}}\frac{e^{in\cdot\left(x-y\right)}}{\left(2\pi\right)^{d}}\int_{0}^{T}e^{it\left(\varphi\left(x\right)-\varphi\left(y\right)\right)}\mathrm{d}t.

Using a simple observation

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}e^{it\left(\varphi\left(x\right)-\varphi\left(y\right)\right)}\mathrm{d}t=\begin{cases}\frac{e^{iT\left(\varphi\left(x\right)-\varphi\left(y\right)\right)}-1}{i\left(\varphi\left(x\right)-\varphi\left(y\right)\right)},\;&{\rm when}\;\varphi\left(x\right)\neq\varphi\left(x\right),\\
\;\;\;\;\;T,\;&{\rm when}\;\varphi\left(x\right)=\varphi\left(y\right),\\
\end{cases}

we have

\left|K_{E^{c}}\left(T\right)\left(x,y\right)\right|\leq\frac{\left|E^{c}\right|}{\left(2\pi\right)^{d}}\cdot T\implies\|K_{E^{c}}\left(T\right)\|_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}^{d}\right)}\leq\frac{\left|E^{c}\right|}{\left(2\pi\right)^{\frac{d}{2}}}\cdot T,

that is,
K_{E^{c}}
belongs to
L^{2}\left(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}^{d}\right)
.
Hence
S_{E^{c}}\left(T\right)
is a Hilbert-Schmidt operator, which is a compact operator directly. Moreover, being positive and self-adjoint,
S_{E^{c}}(T)
attains its norm as an eigenvalue. If
\|S_{E^{c}}\left(T\right)\|_{l^{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}^{d}\right)\to l^{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}^{d}\right)}=T
, then there exists a non-zero normalized eigenfunction
u\in l^{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}^{d}\right)
such that

S_{E^{c}}\left(T\right)u=Tu.

By the definition of
S_{E^{c}}\left(T\right)
,

T=\langle S_{E^{c}}\left(T\right)u,u\rangle_{l^{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}^{d}\right)}=\int_{0}^{T}\|Uf\left(t\right)\|_{\mathbb{C}^{\left|E^{c}\right|}}^{2}\mathrm{d}t,

combining inequality (
30
), we have

\|Uu\left(t\right)\|_{\mathbb{C}^{\left|E^{c}\right|}}=1\qquad{\rm a.e.\;on}\;\left(0,T\right).

The continuity of
\|Uu\left(t\right)\|_{\mathbb{C}^{\left|E^{c}\right|}}
gives that
\|Uu\left(t\right)\|_{\mathbb{C}^{\left|E^{c}\right|}}\equiv 1
on
\left(0,T\right)
. Hence,
e^{it\Delta_{\rm disc}}u\equiv 0
on
E
for
t\in\left(0,T\right)
, that is,
e^{it\Delta_{\rm disc}}u\in{\rm Ran}U
for
t\in\left(0,T\right)
. Since
G(t):=(\mathbbm{1}_{E}\,e^{it\Delta_{\text{disc}}})u
is entire-analytic and vanishes on
(0,T)
, uniqueness of analytic property forces
e^{it\Delta_{\text{disc}}}u\equiv 0
on
E
for
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Let
W:=\mathrm{span}\{e^{it\Delta_{\rm disc}}u:\ t\in\mathbb{R}\}
. Then
W
is a finite-dimensional subspace of
l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
(since
\mathrm{Ran}(\mathbf{1}_{E^{c}})
is finite-dimensional), invariant under the unitary group
\{e^{is\Delta_{\rm disc}}\}_{s\in\mathbb{R}}
. By spectral theorem in finite dimension, the generator
\Delta_{\rm disc}|_{W}
has pure point spectrum and
u
is a finite linear combination of
l^{2}
-eigenvectors of
\Delta_{\rm disc}
. This contradicts the fact that the discrete Laplacian on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
has no nonzero
l^{2}
-eigenfunctions (Fact
2.1
). Hence
\|S_{E^{c}}(T)\|<T
.

Therefore, we derive

\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{n\in E}\big|e^{it\Delta_{\rm disc}}u_{0}(n)\big|^{2}\,dt\;\geq\;\big(T-\|S_{E^{c}}(T)\|\big)\,\|u_{0}\|_{l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}^{2},

which is the desired observability inequality at any time
T>0
, with observability constant
C_{\rm obs}(T,E)=\big(T-\|S_{E^{c}}(T)\|\big)^{-1}
. This completes the proof.
∎

3.
Observable sets for finite graph

3.1.
An equivalent criterion for observability on finite graphs

In this subsection, we study the free Schrödinger equation (
1
) on a finite graph
G=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E})
. In this setting, the discrete Laplacian
\Delta_{\mathrm{disc}}
is a finite-dimensional self-adjoint operator, and its spectral decomposition is standard.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

(i)\Rightarrow(ii)

Let
\{\psi_{k}\}_{k=1}^{n}
be an orthonormal eigenbasis of
\Delta_{\mathrm{disc}}
in
l^{2}(\mathcal{V})
with real eigenvalues
\{\lambda_{k}\}_{k=1}^{n}
, where
n=|\mathcal{V}|
. Expand the initial datum as

u_{0}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}c_{k}\psi_{k},\qquad\bm{c}=(c_{1},\dots,c_{n})^{\top},\qquad\|u_{0}\|_{l^{2}(\mathcal{V})}^{2}=\bm{c}^{H}\bm{c}.

The solution of Schrödinger equation (
1
) is

u(t)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}c_{k}e^{it\lambda_{k}}\psi_{k}.

We introduce the Gramian quadratic form as follows

\int_{0}^{T}\|u(t)\|_{l^{2}(E)}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\;=\;\bm{c}^{H}G\bm{c},\qquad G_{jk}\;=\;\big\langle\psi_{k}|_{E},\psi_{j}|_{E}\big\rangle_{l^{2}(E)}\int_{0}^{T}e^{it(\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{j})}\,\mathrm{d}t.

We claim that
G
is positive-definite. Suppose
\bm{c}^{H}G\bm{c}=0
. Then

\int_{0}^{T}\|u(t)\|_{l^{2}(E)}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t=0.

Since
t\mapsto\|u(t)\|_{l^{2}(E)}^{2}
is nonnegative and continuous, it follows that
\|u(t)\|_{l^{2}(E)}=0
for all
t\in[0,T]
, i.e.

\sum_{k=1}^{n}c_{k}e^{it\lambda_{k}}\psi_{k}(x)\;=\;0\quad\text{for all }x\in E,\;t\in[0,T].

Let
\{\Lambda_{m}\}
be the distinct eigenvalues and group terms accordingly:

\sum_{m}e^{it\Lambda_{m}}\,w_{m}(x)\;=\;0,\qquad w_{m}\;=\;\sum_{k:\lambda_{k}=\Lambda_{m}}c_{k}\psi_{k},\quad x\in E.

The exponentials
\{e^{it\Lambda_{m}}\}_{m}
with distinct frequencies are linearly independent on any interval of positive length. Hence
w_{m}(x)=0
for all
x\in E
and all
m
, i.e.
w_{m}|_{E}\equiv 0
. Each
w_{m}
lies in the eigenspace of
\Lambda_{m}
and thus is a Laplace eigenfunction; by assumption (ii), no nonzero eigenfunction can vanish on
E
. Therefore
w_{m}=0
for all
m
, and consequently
\bm{c}=\bm{0}
.

This shows
G
is positive definite. Let
\mu_{\min}>0
be its smallest eigenvalue. Then

\int_{0}^{T}\|u(t)\|_{l^{2}(E)}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\;=\;\bm{c}^{H}G\bm{c}\;\geq\;\mu_{\min}\,\bm{c}^{H}\bm{c}\;=\;\mu_{\min}\,\|u_{0}\|_{l^{2}(\mathcal{V})}^{2}.

Setting
C_{E}(T)=1/\mu_{\min}
yields (
2
). Since the linear independence of exponentials holds on any interval of positive length, the same argument gives (i) for every
T>0
.

(ii)\Rightarrow(i)

We argue by contradiction. Suppose there exists a nonzero eigenvector
\psi_{0}
with eigenvalue
\lambda_{0}
such that
\psi_{0}|_{E}\equiv 0
. Taking
u_{0}=\psi_{0}
yields
u(t)=e^{it\lambda_{0}}\psi_{0}
, hence

\|u(t)\|_{l^{2}(E)}^{2}\;=\;\sum_{x\in E}\big|e^{it\lambda_{0}}\psi_{0}(x)\big|^{2}\;=\;\sum_{x\in E}|\psi_{0}(x)|^{2}\;=\;0\quad\text{for all }t\geq 0.

Thus the right-hand side of (
2
) is zero, whereas the left-hand side equals
\|\psi_{0}\|_{l^{2}(\mathcal{V})}^{2}>0
. No positive constant
C_{E}(T)
can satisfy (
2
), so
E
is not observable. This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.1

(Equivalence for observability at some time and any time)
.

Combining the two implications shows that (i) holding for some
T>0
implies (ii), which in turn implies (i) for every
T>0
. Thus observability at some time
T>0
is equivalent to observability for any time
T>0
.

3.2.
A positive density construction for unobservable sets on discrete tori

We specialize to the discrete
d
-dimensional tori
(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{d}
with nearest-neighbor graph structure:
x,y\in(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{d}
are adjacent iff
y=x\pm e_{j}
for some standard basis vector
e_{j}
, with arithmetic taken modulo
N
.

The combinatorial Laplacian
\Delta_{\mathrm{disc}}
acts on
f:(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
by

(\Delta_{\mathrm{disc}}f)(x)\;=\;\sum_{j=1}^{d}\bigl(f(x+e_{j})+f(x-e_{j})\bigr)-2d\,f(x).

For
k:=(k_{1},\dots,k_{d})\in(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{d}
, define the character

\varphi_{k}(x)\;=\;\exp\!\Bigl(\frac{2\pi i}{N}\,\left\langle k,x\right\rangle\Bigr),\qquad x\in(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{d},

where
\left\langle k,x\right\rangle=k_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+k_{d}x_{d}
(interpreted modulo
N
). A direct computation gives that

\Delta_{\mathrm{disc}}\varphi_{k}=\left(2\sum_{j=1}^{d}\cos\!\Bigl(\frac{2\pi k_{j}}{N}\Bigr)-2d\right)\varphi_{k}:=\mu(k)\varphi_{k}.

The imaginary parts
\psi_{k}^{\sin}(x)=\sin(\tfrac{2\pi}{N}\left\langle k,x\right\rangle)
are real-valued eigenfunctions with eigenvalue
\mu(k)
. By the standard structure theory for finite abelian groups, the characters
\{\varphi_{k}\}_{k\in(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{d}}
form an orthogonal basis of the Hilbert space
L^{2}\!\bigl((\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{d}\bigr)
.

Fix
k\in(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{d}
. Consider the group homomorphism

F_{k}:(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{d}\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z},\qquad F_{k}(x)=\left\langle k,x\right\rangle\ (\mathrm{mod}\ N),

and write

d_{0}=\gcd(k_{1},\dots,k_{d},N)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}.

On the additive group
\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}
, we introduce

S_{\sin}\;:=\;\bigl\{\,r\in\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}:\ \sin\!\bigl(\frac{2\pi r}{N}\bigr)=0\,\bigr\}\;=\;\begin{cases}\{\,0\,\},&2\nmid N,\\
\{\,0,\ N/2\,\},&2\mid N,\end{cases}

Here
N/2
denotes the unique residue in
\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}
satisfying
2\cdot(N/2)\equiv 0
.

We have the following Proposition for counting the size of zero set
Z(\psi_{k}^{\sin})
.

Proposition 3.1

(Zero set size via group homomorphisms)
.

With the notation above, we have

Z\bigl(\psi_{k}^{\sin}\bigr)\;=\;F_{k}^{-1}\!\bigl(S_{\sin}\cap\mathrm{Im}\,F_{k}\bigr),\qquad\bigl|Z(\psi_{k}^{\sin})\bigr|\;=\;\bigl|S_{\sin}\cap\mathrm{Im}\,F_{k}\bigr|\cdot d_{0}\,N^{d-1},

Proof.

Consider the subgroup
H\subset\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}
generated by the residues
[k_{1}],\dots,[k_{d}]
. The preimage of
H
under the natural projection
\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}
is the ideal
\langle k_{1},\dots,k_{d},N\rangle=d_{0}\mathbb{Z}
, where
d_{0}=\gcd(k_{1},\dots,k_{d},N)
. Hence
H=\langle[d_{0}]\rangle
and therefore
|\mathrm{Im}\,F_{k}|=|H|=N/d_{0}
. Since
F_{k}
is a homomorphism between finite groups, all fibers over residues in
\mathrm{Im}\,F_{k}
have the same cardinality; explicitly,

\bigl|F_{k}^{-1}(r)\bigr|\;=\;|\ker F_{k}|\;=\;\frac{|(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{d}|}{|\mathrm{Im}\,F_{k}|}\;=\;d_{0}\,N^{d-1}\qquad\text{for every }r\in\mathrm{Im}\,F_{k}.

Next, by the elementary characterizations
\sin\theta=0
iff
\theta\in\pi\mathbb{Z}
, a residue
r\in\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}
satisfies
\sin(2\pi r/N)=0
exactly when
r\equiv 0
(and also
r\equiv N/2
if
2\mid N
). These are precisely the set
S_{\sin}
appearing in the statement.

Finally, since
\psi_{k}^{\sin}(x)=\sin\!\bigl(\tfrac{2\pi}{N}F_{k}(x)\bigr)
, an element
x\in(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{d}
is a zero of
\psi_{k}^{\sin}
if and only if
F_{k}(x)
lies in
S_{\sin}
. Thus

Z\bigl(\psi_{k}^{\sin}\bigr)\;=\;F_{k}^{-1}\!\bigl(S_{\sin}\cap\mathrm{Im}\,F_{k}\bigr).

Because the preimage of a subset is the disjoint union of the fibers over its elements, and each fiber has size
d_{0}\,N^{d-1}
, the cardinalities are

\bigl|Z(\psi_{k}^{\sin})\bigr|\;=\;\bigl|S_{\sin}\cap\mathrm{Im}\,F_{k}\bigr|\cdot d_{0}\,N^{d-1},

as claimed.
∎

Now we begin to construct high-density unobservable sets via product eigenfunctions. For
r=(r_{1},\dots,r_{d})\in(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{d}
, we consider

(31)

\Psi_{r}(x)=\prod_{j=1}^{d}\sin\!\Bigl(\frac{2\pi r_{j}x_{j}}{N}\Bigr),\qquad x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})\in(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{d}.

Expanding the product shows that

\Psi_{r}(x)\;=\;\sum_{s\in\{\pm 1\}^{d}}c_{s}\,\exp\!\Bigl(\frac{2\pi i}{N}\,\left\langle s\circ r,x\right\rangle\Bigr),

where
(s\circ r)_{j}=s_{j}r_{j}
and
c_{s}\neq 0
. Since

\mu(s\circ r)=2\sum_{j=1}^{d}\cos(\tfrac{2\pi r_{j}}{N})-2d
is independent of
s
,
\Psi_{r}
is a Laplace eigenfunction with eigenvalue
\mu(r)
.

Set
Z_{j}(r):=\{x\in(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{d}:\ \sin(\tfrac{2\pi r_{j}x_{j}}{N})=0\}
and

E_{\mathrm{prod}}(r):=\bigcup_{j=1}^{d}Z_{j}(r).

Then
\Psi_{r}
vanishes on
E_{\mathrm{prod}}(r)
, so
E_{\mathrm{prod}}(r)
is unobservable by Theorem
1.3
.

Theorem 3.1

(Product construction and its size)
.

Let
N\geq 3
,
d\geq 1
, and let
p_{\min}
denote the smallest odd prime divisor of
N

(if
N
is odd prime, then
p_{\min}=N
).
Define
r_{j}
coordinate-wise by

r_{j}\;=\;\begin{cases}N/4,&\text{if }4\mid N,\\
N/p_{\min},&\text{if }4\nmid N,\end{cases}\qquad j=1,\dots,d,

and set
r=(r_{1},\dots,r_{d})
and
E_{\mathrm{prod}}:=E_{\mathrm{prod}}(r)
.

Then
E_{\mathrm{prod}}
is unobservable and its cardinality is

|E_{\mathrm{prod}}|\;=\;\begin{cases}N^{d}-(N/2)^{d}\;=\;N^{d}\bigl(1-2^{-d}\bigr),&\text{if }4\mid N,\\[3.99994pt]
N^{d}-\bigl(N-N/p_{\min}\bigr)^{d}\;=\;N^{d}\Bigl[1-\bigl(1-\tfrac{1}{p_{\min}}\bigr)^{d}\Bigr],&\text{if }4\nmid N.\end{cases}

Proof.

We already showed
\Psi_{r}
defined in (
31
) is an eigenfunction and
\Psi_{r}|_{E_{\mathrm{prod}}}\equiv 0
.
It remains to count.

If
4\mid N
and
r_{j}=N/4
, then
\sin(\tfrac{2\pi r_{j}x_{j}}{N})=\sin(\tfrac{\pi x_{j}}{2})

vanishes iff
x_{j}
is even, i.e.
|Z_{j}(r)|=N/2
. Independence across coordinates shows the complement of
E_{\mathrm{prod}}
consists of points with all
x_{j}
odd, of size
(N/2)^{d}
,
yielding
|E_{\mathrm{prod}}|=N^{d}-(N/2)^{d}
.

If
4\nmid N
, take
r_{j}=N/p_{\min}
. Then, as discussed in Proposition
3.1
,
since
\mathrm{Im}\,F_{r_{j}}
has order
p_{\min}
and
p_{\min}
is odd, the only sine zero
in that image is
0
, so
Z_{j}(r)=\{x:\ x_{j}\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ p_{\min})\}
, of size
N/p_{\min}
.
Again independence across coordinates gives

|((\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{d})\setminus E_{\mathrm{prod}}|=\bigl(N-N/p_{\min}\bigr)^{d}
, hence the stated formula.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Let
N_{m}\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 4)
.
The nonzero function
\psi(x)=\prod_{j=1}^{d}\sin(\pi x_{j}/2)
is a Laplacian eigenfunction.
Lettling
E_{N_{m}}:=E_{\mathrm{prod}}
, Theorem
3.1
implies that
E_{N_{m}}
is not observable for any
T>0
and

|E_{N_{m}}|=N_{m}^{d}(1-2^{-d})
. This completes the proof.
∎

We remark that product construction over primes yields vanishing density. More precisely, assume
N
is odd prime and take
r_{j}=N/p_{\min}=1
for all
j
. Then
|E_{\mathrm{prod}}|=N^{d}-(N-1)^{d}\sim d\,N^{d-1}\ (N\to\infty)
,
that is,
|E_{\mathrm{prod}}|/N^{d}\to 0
as
N\to\infty
.

For
r=(r_{1},\dots,r_{d})\in(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{d}
, we introduce

\mathcal{V}_{r}\;=\;\mathrm{span}\,\bigl\{\,\varphi_{s\circ r}:\ s\in\{\pm 1\}^{d}\,\bigr\}.

Then
\dim\mathcal{V}_{r}\leq 2^{d}
(in fact
=2^{d}
if all
r_{j}\not\equiv 0
and
r_{j}\not\equiv N/2
when
2\mid N
). To conclude this paper, we show that, when
4\mid N
and
r_{j}=N/4
, the eigenfunction
\Psi_{r}
minimizes the number of nonzero entries among all nonzero vectors in
\mathcal{V}_{r}
; equivalently, it maximizes the zero set
Z(\psi)
within
\mathcal{V}_{r}
.

Proposition 3.2

(An uncertainty bound and optimality in
\mathcal{V}_{r}
)
.

Fix
r
as in Theorem
3.1
.
Then for any
0\neq\psi\in\mathcal{V}_{r}
, we have

|\mathrm{supp}\,\psi|\;\geq\;\frac{N^{d}}{2^{d}}.

If moreover
4\mid N
and
r_{j}=N/4
for all
j
, then
\Psi_{r}
satisfies

|\mathrm{supp}\,\Psi_{r}|=(N/2)^{d}=N^{d}/2^{d}
, hence it attains the uncertainty lower bound in
\mathcal{V}_{r}
.
Consequently, among all nonzero
\psi\in\mathcal{V}_{r}
, the zero set
Z(\psi)
is maximized by
\Psi_{r}
.

Proof.

Let
G=(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{d}
and let
\widehat{G}
be its dual (identified with
(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{d}
via characters).
For any nonzero
f:G\to\mathbb{C}
the Donoho–Stark uncertainty principle (see for example
[
15
,
37
]
) gives

|\mathrm{supp}\,f|\cdot|\mathrm{supp}\,\widehat{f}|\geq|G|=N^{d}
.

Every
\psi\in\mathcal{V}_{r}
has Fourier support contained in the set

\{\,s\circ r:\ s\in\{\pm 1\}^{d}\,\}
, hence
|\mathrm{supp}\,\widehat{\psi}|\leq 2^{d}
.
Applying the uncertainty inequality yields
|\mathrm{supp}\,\psi|\geq N^{d}/2^{d}
.

When
4\mid N
and
r_{j}=N/4
, we computed in Theorem
3.1
that

\Psi_{r}(x)\neq 0
iff all coordinates
x_{j}
are odd, so
|\mathrm{supp}\,\Psi_{r}|=(N/2)^{d}
.
Thus
\Psi_{r}
attains the lower bound in
\mathcal{V}_{r}
, which implies maximality of
|Z(\psi)|

within
\mathcal{V}_{r}
.
∎

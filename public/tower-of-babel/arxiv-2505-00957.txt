Classification of Principle 3D Slices of Filled-in Julia Sets in Multicomplex Spaces
Quentin Charles, Pierre Olivier Parisé

Abstract
A generalization of the filled-in Julia set is presented using the multicomplex numbers and an algorithm is presented to visualize these sets in the tridimensional space. There are many ways to visualize these higher dimensional fractals sets on a computer. We therefore introduce an equivalence relation between 3D representations and show that, for the filled-in Julia sets associated to the polynomial z p + c z^{p}+c , there are nine 3D slices when p p is an odd integer and four when p p is even. These results differs from the recent characterization obtained by Brouillette and Rochon in 2019 and the proofs require different arguments in the context of the filled-in Julia sets.

Introduction

The theory of dynamical systems is a branch of mathematics that studies the evolution of a system that follows specific rules. This discipline was initiated by H. Poincaré following his study of the three-body problem. It became popular among the public thanks to the experiments by E. Lorenz on climate models in 1963, which also gave rise to the expression “butterfly effect.” Today, this mathematical theory has become a central tool in many fields of Science and engineering

One branch of the theory of dynamical systems is holomorphic dynamics, which studies the dynamics generated by the iteration of differentiable functions with complex values. It arose from the work of two French mathematicians, Gaston Julia and Pierre Fatou, published between 1917 and 1920. The work of mathematicians such as B. Mandelbrot, A. Douady, and J. H. Hubbard revived researchers’ interest in this theory. Among other things, two sets in particular have attracted attention due to their remarkable properties: the filled-in Julia sets and the Mandelbrot set.

On one hand, given a polynomial function
f(z)=a_{n}z^{n}+a_{n-1}z^{n-1}+\cdots+a_{1}z+a_{0}
, the filled-in Julia set associated to the function
f
is the set of

under

, that is the sequence
(f^{m}(z))_{m=1}^{\infty}
, where

, is bounded. In this paper, we focus mainly on the filled-in Julia sets associated to the polynomials

, where

is an integer. These sets will be denoted by
\mathcal{K}_{1,c}^{p}
. On the other hand, the Mandelbrot set is the set of parameters

such that the orbit of

(f_{c}^{m}(0))_{m=1}^{\infty}

In recent years, more and more researchers became interested in generalizing the Mandelbrot set and the filled-in Julia sets to higher dimensions. One of the pioneers in this field was A. Norton

When

Preliminary on Multicomplex Numbers

The objective of this section is to introduce the definition of multicomplex numbers, their basic algebraic operations, and their topology.

2.1
Multicomplex Numbers

The Multicomplex number are a generalization of the complex numbers to higher dimensions. The original reference to multicomplex numbers is the book of Price
[
15
]
. For recent and succinct introductions to multicomplex numbers, the reader is also directed to the papers
[
5
,
7
]
. Our presentation will mainly follow the paper
[
7
]
.

The set of Multicomplex numbers is denoted by
\mathbb{M}\mathbb{C}(n)
for any
n\geq 1
, where
n
is an integer representing the order. For
n=0
, we set
\mathbb{M}\mathbb{C}(0):=\mathbb{R}
, for
n=1
, we set
\mathbb{M}\mathbb{C}(1):=\{\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}\mathbf{i_{1}}\,:\,\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\in\mathbb{R}\}
with
\mathbf{i_{1}}=\mathbf{i}
, the usual imaginary unit, and for any integer
n\geq 2
, the element of
\mathbb{M}\mathbb{C}(n)
are defined recursively as followed:

\mathbb{M}\mathbb{C}(n):=\{\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}\mathbf{i_{n}}\,:\,\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\in\mathbb{M}\mathbb{C}(n-1)\},

where the symbol
\mathbf{i_{n}}
is an imaginary unit such that
\mathbf{i_{n}^{2}}=-1
and
\mathbf{i_{n-1}}\neq\mathbf{i_{n}}
. Note that with this definition, the usual set of complex numbers
\mathbb{C}
corresponds to the set
\mathbb{M}\mathbb{C}(1)
.

Given two multicomplex numbers
\eta=\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}\mathbf{i_{n}}
and
\zeta=\zeta_{1}+\zeta_{2}\mathbf{i_{n}}
, the operation of addition, denoted
+
, of two multicomplex numbers is defined as followed:

\eta+\zeta=(\eta_{1}+\zeta_{1})+(\eta_{2}+\zeta_{2})\mathbf{i_{n}},

where the addition in each component is the addition defined in
\mathbb{M}\mathbb{C}(n-1)
and the operation of multiplication, denoted by
{}^{\prime}\cdot^{\prime}
, between two multicomplex numbers is defined as

\eta\cdot\zeta:=(\eta_{1}\zeta_{1}-\eta_{2}\zeta_{2})+(\eta_{1}\zeta_{2}+\eta_{2}\zeta_{1})\mathbf{i_{n}},

where the operations in each components are the addition and multiplication coming from
\mathbb{M}\mathbb{C}(n-1)
. One can show that the triplet
(\mathbb{M}\mathbb{C}(n),\cdot,+)
forms a commutative unitary ring (see, for instance,
[
15
]
).

When
n=1
, we obtain the set of complex numbers
\mathbb{M}\mathbb{C}(1)
which, compared to all other cases of multicomplex numbers, is a commutative field. When
n=2
, we obtain the set of bicomplex numbers and each bicomplex number can be written down as
\eta=\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}\mathbf{i_{2}}
, where
\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\in\mathbb{M}(1)
. Describing each component of the bicomplex number
\eta
as
\eta_{1}=\eta_{11}+\eta_{12}\mathbf{i_{1}}
and
\eta_{2}=\eta_{21}+\eta_{22}\mathbf{i_{1}}
, where
\eta_{11},\eta_{12},\eta_{21},\eta_{22}\in\mathbb{M}\mathbb{C}(0)=\mathbb{R}
, we can rewrite
\eta
as

\eta=(\eta_{11}+\eta_{12}\mathbf{i_{1}})+(\eta_{21}+\eta_{22}\mathbf{i_{1}})\mathbf{i_{2}}.

Distributing the number
\mathbf{i_{2}}
and relabeling the index of each real component, we obtain

\eta=\eta_{1}+\eta_{\mathbf{i_{1}}}\mathbf{i_{1}}+\eta_{\mathbf{i_{2}}}\mathbf{i_{2}}+\eta_{\mathbf{i_{1}}\mathbf{i_{2}}}\mathbf{i_{1}}\mathbf{i_{2}}.

We can therefore write any bicomplex number as a
\mathbb{M}\mathbb{C}(1)
-linear (complex linear) combination of the units
1
and
\mathbf{i_{2}}
or as a
\mathbb{M}\mathbb{C}(0)
-linear (real linear) combination of the units
1
,
\mathbf{i_{1}}
,
\mathbf{i_{2}}
, and
\mathbf{i_{1}}\mathbf{i_{2}}
.

The decomposition of a bicomplex number as a real-linear combination of units can be generalized to any multicomplex numbers. Let
\mathbb{I}(n)
be the set of all units of the form
\mathbf{i_{1}}^{a_{1}}\mathbf{i_{2}}^{a_{2}}\cdots\mathbf{i_{n}}^{a_{n}}
, where
a_{j}\in\{0,1\}
. For instance, we have

\mathbb{I}(2)=\{1,\mathbf{i_{1}},\mathbf{i_{2}},\mathbf{i_{1}}\mathbf{i_{2}}\}\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathbb{I}(3)=\{1,\,\mathbf{i_{1}},\,\mathbf{i_{2}},\,\mathbf{i_{1}}\mathbf{i_{2}},\,\mathbf{i_{3}},\,\mathbf{i_{1}}\mathbf{i_{3}},\,\mathbf{i_{2}}\mathbf{i_{3}},\,\mathbf{i_{1}}\mathbf{i_{2}}\mathbf{i_{3}}\}.

From combinatorial considerations, we can show that the number of elements of
\mathbb{I}(n)
is
2^{n}
.

Within the set
\mathbb{I}(n)
, there are units that square to
1
or to
-1
. An imaginary unit is a unit
\mathbf{u}
having the property that
\mathbf{u^{2}}=-1
and a hyperbolic unit is a unit
\mathbf{u}
that have the property that
\mathbf{u^{2}}=1
and
\mathbf{u}\neq 1
. Combinatorial considerations implies that the number of imaginary units in
\mathbb{I}(n)
is
2^{n-1}
and the number of hyperbolic units in
\mathbb{I}(n)
is
2^{n-1}-1
. For more details on hyperbolic units, the reader is referred to
[
7
]
, where the authors found more imaginary and hyperbolic units in the set of multicomplex numbers. For this paper, however, only the set
\mathbb{I}(n)
will be considered. Therefore, we can decompose a multicomplex number
\eta
as

\eta=\sum_{\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{I}(n)}x_{\mathbf{u}}\mathbf{u},

where
\eta_{\mathbf{u}}\in\mathbb{M}\mathbb{C}(0)=\mathbb{R}
for any
\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{I}(n)
.

We presented the example of a bicomplex numbers. Another important example is the set of tricomplex numbers that we will use later in the paper as our optimal space for the 3D slices of the Filled-in Julia sets. Any tricomplex number
\eta
can be written down as a real-linear combination of the units from the set
\mathbb{I}(3)
as followed:

\eta=x_{1}+x_{\mathbf{i_{1}}}\mathbf{i_{1}}+x_{\mathbf{i_{2}}}\mathbf{i_{2}}+x_{\mathbf{i_{1}}\mathbf{i_{2}}}\mathbf{i_{1}}\mathbf{i_{2}}+x_{\mathbf{i_{3}}}\mathbf{i_{3}}+x_{\mathbf{i_{1}}\mathbf{i_{3}}}\mathbf{i_{1}}\mathbf{i_{3}}+x_{\mathbf{i_{2}}\mathbf{i_{3}}}\mathbf{i_{2}}\mathbf{i_{3}}+x_{\mathbf{i_{1}}\mathbf{i_{2}}\mathbf{i_{3}}}\mathbf{i_{1}}\mathbf{i_{2}}\mathbf{i_{3}}

where
x_{\mathbf{u}}\in\mathbb{R}
for
\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{I}(3)
. For reference later in the paper, we set
\mathbf{j_{1}}=\mathbf{i_{1}}\mathbf{i_{2}}
,
\mathbf{j_{2}}:=\mathbf{i_{1}}\mathbf{i_{3}}
,
\mathbf{j_{3}}:=\mathbf{i_{2}}\mathbf{i_{3}}
, and
\mathbf{i_{4}}:=\mathbf{i_{1}}\mathbf{i_{2}}\mathbf{i_{3}}
.

2.2
Idempotent Representations

Define the multicomplex numbers
\mathbf{e_{n}}
and
\mathbf{\overline{e}_{n}}
, for
n\geq 2
, as followed:

\mathbf{e_{n}}=\frac{1+\mathbf{i_{n-1}}\mathbf{i_{n}}}{2}\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathbf{\overline{e}_{n}}=\frac{1-\mathbf{i_{n-1}}\mathbf{i_{n}}}{2}

Given
\eta=\eta_{1}+\eta\mathbf{i_{n}}
, it is easy to see that

\eta\mathbf{e_{n}}=(\eta_{1}-\eta_{2}\mathbf{i_{n-1}})\mathbf{e_{n}}\quad\text{ and }\quad\eta\mathbf{\overline{e}_{n}}=(\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}\mathbf{i_{n-1}})\mathbf{\overline{e}_{n}}.

Since
\mathbf{e_{n}}+\mathbf{\overline{e}_{n}}=1
, we obtain

\eta(\mathbf{e_{n}}+\mathbf{\overline{e}_{n}})=\eta\mathbf{e_{n}}+\eta\mathbf{\overline{e}_{n}}=(\eta_{1}-\eta_{2}\mathbf{i_{n-1}})\mathbf{e_{n}}+(\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}\mathbf{i_{n-1}})\mathbf{\overline{e}_{n}}.

This is called the idempotent representation of a multicomplex number. We set
\eta_{\mathbf{e}}:=\eta_{1}-\eta_{2}\mathbf{i_{n-1}}
and
\eta_{\mathbf{\overline{e}_{n}}}:=\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}\mathbf{i_{n-1}}
and write the idempotent representation of a multicomplex number
\eta
as followed

\eta=\eta_{\mathbf{e}}\mathbf{e}+\eta_{\mathbf{\overline{e}}}\mathbf{\overline{e}}.

Because
\mathbf{e_{n}}\mathbf{\overline{e}_{n}}=0
,
\mathbf{e_{n}}^{2}=\mathbf{e_{n}}
, and
\mathbf{\overline{e}_{n}}^{2}=\mathbf{\overline{e}_{n}}
, we obtain the following properties of the idempotent representation.

Theorem 2.1

Let
\eta=\eta_{\mathbf{e}}\mathbf{e}+\eta_{\mathbf{\overline{e}}}\mathbf{\overline{e}}
and
\zeta=\zeta_{\mathbf{e}}\mathbf{e}+\zeta_{\mathbf{\overline{e}}}\mathbf{\overline{e}}
be two multicomplex numbers and
n\geq 2
be an integer. Then

1.

\eta+\zeta=(\eta_{\mathbf{e}}+\zeta_{\mathbf{e}})\mathbf{e}+(\eta_{\mathbf{\overline{e}}}+\zeta_{\mathbf{\overline{e}}})\mathbf{\overline{e}}
.

2.

\eta\zeta=(\eta_{\mathbf{e}}\zeta_{\mathbf{e}})\mathbf{e}+(\eta_{\mathbf{\overline{e}}}\zeta_{\mathbf{\overline{e}}})\mathbf{\overline{e}}
.

3.

\eta^{m}=\eta_{\mathbf{e}}^{m}\mathbf{e}+\zeta_{\mathbf{\overline{e}}}^{m}\mathbf{\overline{e}}
, for any integer
m\geq 1
.

Using the idempotent representation we can defined an operation similar to the Cartesian product of two sets.

Definition 1

Let
X\subset\mathbb{M}\mathbb{C}(n)
, for
n\geq 2
. The set
X
is called an
\mathbb{M}\mathbb{C}(n)
-Cartesian product determined by two sets
X_{1}\subset\mathbb{M}\mathbb{C}(n-1)
and
X_{2}\subset\mathbb{M}\mathbb{C}(n-1)
if the following holds

X=X_{1}\times_{\mathbf{e_{n}}}X_{2}=\{\eta_{\mathbf{e}}\mathbf{e}+\eta_{\mathbf{\overline{e}}}\mathbf{\overline{e}}\,:\,\eta_{\mathbf{e}},\eta_{\mathbf{\overline{e}}}\in\mathbb{M}\mathbb{C}(n-1)\}.

2.3
Norm

The norm of a multicomplex number
\eta=\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}\mathbf{i_{n}}\in\mathbb{M}(n)
is defined recursively as the following quantity:

\|\eta\|_{n}:=\sqrt{\|\eta_{1}\|_{n-1}^{2}+\|\eta_{2}\|_{n-1}^{2}\big)}

where
\|\eta\|_{1}:=\sqrt{\eta_{1}^{2}+\eta_{2}^{2}}
. It can be shown that the expression of the norm of a multicomplex number can be written down in terms of the idempotent components in the idempotent representation of a multicomplex number:

\|\eta\|_{n}=\sqrt{\frac{\|\eta_{\mathbf{e}}\|_{n-1}^{2}+\|\eta_{\mathbf{\overline{e}}}\|_{n-1}^{2}}{2}}

where
\eta=\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}\mathbf{i_{n}}=\eta_{\mathbf{e}}\mathbf{e}+\eta_{\mathbf{\overline{e}}}\mathbf{\overline{e}}
.

The topology used on
\mathbb{M}\mathbb{C}(n)
is the one induced by the metric
d(\eta,\zeta):=\|\eta-\zeta\|_{n}
. The open balls are the set
B_{n}(\eta,r):=\{\zeta\in\mathbb{M}\mathbb{C}(n)\,:\,\|\zeta-\eta\|_{n}<r\}
. As a consequence, the
\mathbb{M}\mathbb{C}(n)
-cartesian product preserved closedness, compactness, openness, and connectedness of sets, that is

1.

If
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
are closed sets in
\mathbb{M}\mathbb{C}(n-1)
, then the set
X_{1}\times_{\mathbf{e_{n}}}X_{2}
is closed in
\mathbb{M}\mathbb{C}(n)
.

2.

If
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
are compact sets in
\mathbb{M}\mathbb{C}(n-1)
, then the set
X_{1}\times_{\mathbf{e_{n}}}X_{2}
is compact in
\mathbb{M}\mathbb{C}(n)
.

3.

If
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
are open sets in
\mathbb{M}\mathbb{C}(n-1)
, then the set
X_{1}\times_{\mathbf{e_{n}}}X_{2}
is open in
\mathbb{M}\mathbb{C}(n)
.

4.

If
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
are connected sets, then the set
X_{1}\times_{\mathbf{e_{n}}}X_{2}
is a connected set.

3
Preliminaries on the Filled-in Julia Sets in the Complex Plane

In this section, we define the Filled-in Julia sets, denoted by
\mathcal{K}_{1,c}^{p}
, over the complex plane
\mathbb{M}\mathbb{C}(1)
. We will show some basic properties of the set
\mathcal{K}_{1,c}^{p}
. These properties will be essential to draw pictures of its generalization to the bicomplex and tricomplex spaces.

3.1
Definition

For a fixed complex number
c\in\mathbb{M}\mathbb{C}(1)
, the filled-in Julia set associated to the polynomial function
f_{c}(z)=z^{p}+c
is defined as followed:

\mathcal{K}_{1,c}^{p}:=\{z\in\mathbb{M}\mathbb{C}(1)\,:\,\text{ the sequence }(f_{c}^{n}(z))_{n=1}^{\infty}\text{ is bounded}\}.

Here the notation
f_{c}^{n}(z)
stands for the
n
-th iterate of the function
f_{c}
at
z
, meaning
f_{c}^{n}(z)=f(f^{n-1}(z))
when
n\geq 2
and
f_{c}(z)=z^{p}+c
when
n=1
.

3.2
Basic Properties

The first important property of the complex Filled-in Julia sets is the fact that they are compact subsets of the complex plane. We can in fact identify exactly the radius of the disk containing a filled-in Julia set.

Theorem 3.1

Let
c\in\mathbb{M}\mathbb{C}(1)
. Then
\mathcal{K}_{1,c}^{p}
is a compact set. In particular, we have that
\mathcal{K}_{1,c}^{p}\subseteq\overline{B(0,R)}
, where
R:=\max\{|c|,2^{1/(p-1)}\}
.

Proof

The fact that
\mathcal{K}_{1,c}^{p}
is a compact subset of the complex plane is a known fact from the theory of holomorphic dynamics. A reference for this result is
(
8
, Theorem 14.2)
.

We will prove the second part of the theorem. Let
R
be as in the statement of the theorem. We will show that
\mathbb{M}\mathbb{C}(1)\backslash\overline{B(0,R)}\subset\mathbb{M}\mathbb{C}(1)\backslash\mathcal{K}_{1,c}^{p}
, which is equivalent to the statement we want to show.

Assume that
z\not\in\overline{B(0,R)}
. Then
|z|>R
and

|f_{c}(z)|=|z^{p}+c|\geq|z|^{p}-|c|>|z|^{p}-|z|=|z|(|z|^{p-1}-1).

where, in the second inequality, we used the fact that
|c|\leq R
. Since
z\not\in\overline{B(0,R)}
, then
|z|=R+\delta
for some positive real number
\delta
. This implies that

|z|^{p-1}-1=(R+\delta)^{p-1}-1=R^{p-1}(1+\delta/R)^{p-1}-1.

Recall Bernouilli’s inequality
(1+x)^{m}\geq 1+mx
for any
x\geq 0
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Set
x=\delta/R
and
m=p-1\in\mathbb{N}
, then we obtain the following inequality:

|z|^{p-1}-1\geq R^{p-1}(1+(p-1)\delta/R)-1=R^{p-1}+(p-1)\delta R^{p-2}-1.

Using the fact that
R\geq 2^{1/(p-1)}
, we have that
R^{p-1}-1\geq 1
and we obtain the following lowerbound:

|z|^{p-1}-1\geq 1+(p-1)\delta R^{p-2}.

Also, since
p-2\geq 0
and
R>1
, we have that
R^{p-2}\geq 1
and this means

|z|^{p-1}-1\geq 1+(p-1)\delta.

Therefore, we obtain

|f_{c}(z)|>|z|(1+(p-1)\delta).

Assume that
|f_{c}^{k}(z)|=|z|(1+(p-1)\delta)^{k}
for some integer
k
. Then, we have

|f_{c}^{k+1}(z)|\geq|f_{c}^{k}(z)|(1+(p-1)\delta)\geq|z|(1+(p-1)\delta)^{k}(1+(p-1)\delta)=|z|(1+(p-1)\delta)^{k+1}.

Hence, by the Principle of Induction, we just proved that if
|z|>R
, then

|f_{c}^{n}(z)|\geq|z|(1+(p-1)\delta)^{n}\quad(\forall n\in\mathbb{N}).

Since
1+(p-1)\delta>1
, then
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}|f_{c}^{n}(z)|=\infty
and therefore the sequence
(f_{c}^{n}(z))_{n=1}^{\infty}
is unbounded. This means
z\not\in\mathcal{K}_{1,c}^{p}
. This concludes the proof. ∎

From the above proof, we can extract an important lemma that will be at the core of the justification of the algorithm to draw filled-in Julia sets.

Lemma 1

Let
c\in\mathbb{M}\mathbb{C}(1)
and
R:=\max\{|c|,2^{1/(p-1)}\}
. If there exists a positive integer
m
such that
|f_{c}^{m}(z)|>R
, then there is some positive real number
\delta
such that
|f_{c}^{m+n}(z)|\geq|f_{c}^{m}(z)|(1+(p-1)\delta)^{n}
for any integer
n\geq 1
.

Proof

Assume that
|f_{c}^{m}(z)|>R
for some positive integer
m
. Let
\delta>0
be such that
|f_{c}^{m}(z)|=R+\delta
. Reproducing the proof of the preceding Theorem with
z
replaced by
f_{c}^{m}(z)
, we can show that

|f_{c}^{m+1}(z)|\geq|f_{c}^{m}(z)|(1+(p-1)\delta).

This is the base case
n=1
. Assuming that
|f_{c}^{m+k}(z)|\geq|f_{c}^{m}(z)|(1+(p-1)\delta)^{k}
, we then have

|f_{c}^{m+k+1}(z)|\geq|f_{c}^{m+k}(z)|(1+(p-1)\delta)\geq|f_{c}^{m}(z)|(1+(p-1)\delta)^{k+1}

which establishes the induction step. The Principle of Induction then concludes the proof. ∎

3.3
An Algorithm

We can now prove the following theorem which gives an algorithm to draw pictures of filled-in Julia sets in the complex plane.

Theorem 3.2

Let
z,c\in\mathbb{M}\mathbb{C}(1)
and
R:=\max\{|c|,2^{1/(p-1)}\}
. The following assertions are equivalent:

1.

z\in\mathcal{K}_{1,c}^{p}
.

2.

|f_{c}^{n}(z)|\leq R
for any positive integer
n
.

Proof

If
|f_{c}^{n}(z)|\leq R
for any positive integer
n
, then the sequence
(f_{c}^{n}(z))_{n=1}^{\infty}
is bounded and hence
z\in\mathcal{K}_{1,c}^{p}
. This proves the implication (2)
\Rightarrow
(1).

To prove the implication (1)
\Rightarrow
(2), we will prove the contrapositive. Assume that there is a positive integer
m
such that
|f_{c}^{m}(z)|>R
. By Lemma
1
, we deduce that for some
\delta>0
, we have

|f_{c}^{m+n}(z)|\geq|f_{c}^{m}(z)|(1+(p-1)\delta)^{n}\quad(\forall n\in\mathbb{N}).

Since
1+(p-1)\delta>1
, then
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}|f^{k}_{c}(z)|=\infty
and hence
z\not\in\mathcal{K}_{1,c}^{p}
. The contrapositive of the implication (1)
\Rightarrow
(2) is therefore true and hence the implication is also true. ∎

From Theorem
3.2
, we can construct an algorithm to draw a filled-in Julia set in the complex plane. Fix
c\in\mathbb{M}\mathbb{C}(1)
,
R=\max\{|c|,2^{1/(p-1)}\}
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
. For each complex number in the square
[-R,R]\times[-R,R]
,

1.

Compute the
n
-th iterate
f_{c}^{n}(z)=f_{c}(f_{c}^{n-1}(z))
, for
n=1,2,\ldots,N
.

2.

At every iteration, compute
W:=|f_{c}^{n}(z)|
and verify the following conditions:

(a)

If
W>R
for some
n<N
, then assume that
z\not\in\mathcal{K}_{1,c}^{p}
and assign a color to the number
z
.

(b)

if
W\leq R
for every
n=1,2,\ldots,N
, then assume that
z\in\mathcal{K}_{1,c}^{p}
and assign a different color than the one chosen in (a) to the number
z
.

Higher Dimensional Filled-in Julia Sets

We now generalize the filled-in Julia sets to higher dimensions. This was done previously in
[
9
]
for the polynomial
f_{c}(\zeta)=\zeta^{2}+c
. We generalize it here for the polynomial
f_{c}(\zeta)=\zeta^{p}+c
.

Throughout this section, the function
f_{c}
will be the polynomial
f_{c}(\zeta)=\zeta^{p}+c
, where
\zeta,c\in\mathbb{M}\mathbb{C}(n)
and
p,n
are positive integers greater than or equal to
2
. As we used in the previous section, the
m
-th iterate of the function
f_{c}
is defined as
f_{c}^{m}(\zeta)=f(f_{c}^{m-1}(\zeta))
when
m\geq 2
and
f_{c}^{1}(\zeta)=f_{c}(\zeta)
when

.

4.1
Multicomplex Filled-in Julia Sets

The multicomplex filled-in Julia set associated to the polynomial
f_{c}
, with
c\in\mathbb{M}\mathbb{C}(n)
is defined as followed.

Definition 2

Let
c\in\mathbb{M}\mathbb{C}(n)
. The multicomplex filled-in Julia set associated to
f_{c}
is denoted by
\mathcal{K}_{n,c}^{p}
and

\mathcal{K}_{n,c}^{p}:=\{\zeta\in\mathbb{M}\mathbb{C}(n)\,:\,\text{ the sequence }(f_{c}^{m}(\zeta))_{m=1}^{\infty}\text{ is bounded}\}.

When
n=1
, we recover the definition of the filled-in Julia sets in the complex plane. Two other cases are of importance in this paper:

1.

When
n=2
,
\mathbb{M}\mathbb{C}(2)
is the set of bicomplex numbers. We therefore obtain the bicomplex version of the filled-in Julia sets denoted by
\mathcal{K}_{2,c}^{p}
(see
[
17
]
for the case
p=2
).

2.

When
n=3
,
\mathbb{M}\mathbb{C}(3)
is the set of tricomplex numbers. We therefore obtain the tricomplex version of the filled-in Julia sets denoted by
\mathcal{K}_{3,c}^{p}
(see
[
9
]
for
p=2
).

The case of the tricomplex numbers will be particularly important for our main result.

4.2
Basic properties

Some of the basic properties stated in this section were already mentioned, without proofs, in
[
3
]
. For the sake of the reader, we provide the complete proofs of these results here.

Using the idempotent representation of a multicomplex number, we can obtain the following important decomposition of a multicomplex filled-in Julia set.

Theorem 4.1

Let
n\geq 2
be an integer and
c=c_{\mathbf{e_{n}}}\mathbf{e_{n}}+c_{\mathbf{\overline{e}_{n}}}\mathbf{\overline{e}_{n}}\in\mathbb{M}\mathbb{C}(n)
, with
c_{\mathbf{e_{n}}},c_{\mathbf{\overline{e}_{n}}}\in\mathbb{M}\mathbb{C}(n-1)
the idempotent components of
c
. Then we have

\mathcal{K}_{n,c}^{p}=\mathcal{K}_{n-1,c_{\mathbf{e_{n}}}}^{p}\times_{\mathbf{e_{n}}}\mathcal{K}_{n-1,c_{\mathbf{\overline{e}_{n}}}}^{p}.

Proof

Let
n
and
c
be as in the statement of the theorem. Using the idempotent representation, for any
m\geq 1
and
\zeta=\zeta_{\mathbf{e_{n}}}\mathbf{\overline{e}_{n}}+\zeta_{\mathbf{\overline{e}_{n}}}\mathbf{\overline{e}_{n}}
, we have

f_{c}^{m}(\zeta)=f_{c_{\mathbf{e_{n}}}}^{m}(\zeta_{\mathbf{e_{n}}})\mathbf{e_{n}}+f_{c_{\mathbf{\overline{e}_{n}}}}^{m}(\zeta_{\mathbf{\overline{e}_{n}}})\mathbf{\overline{e}_{n}}

and therefore

\|f_{c}^{m}(\zeta)\|_{n}=\sqrt{\frac{\|f_{c_{\mathbf{e_{n}}}}^{m}(\zeta_{\mathbf{e_{n}}})\|_{n-1}^{2}+\|f_{c_{\mathbf{\overline{e}_{n}}}}^{m}(\zeta_{\mathbf{\overline{e}_{n}}})\|_{n-1}^{2}}{2}}.

It immediately follows from this last equality that the sequence
(f_{c}^{m}(\zeta))_{m=1}^{\infty}
is bounded if and only if the two sequences
(f_{c_{\mathbf{e_{n}}}}^{m}(\zeta_{\mathbf{e_{n}}}))_{m=1}^{\infty}
and
(f_{c_{\mathbf{\overline{e}_{n}}}}^{m}(\zeta_{\mathbf{\overline{e}_{n}}}))_{m=1}^{\infty}
are bounded. Hence,
\zeta\in\mathcal{K}_{n,c}^{p}
if and only if
\zeta_{\mathbf{e_{n}}}\in\mathcal{K}_{n-1,c_{\mathbf{e_{n}}}}^{p}
and
\zeta_{\mathbf{\overline{e}_{n}}}\in\mathcal{K}_{n-1,c_{\mathbf{\overline{e}_{n}}}}^{p}
if and only if
\zeta\in\mathcal{K}_{n-1,c_{\mathbf{e_{n}}}}^{p}\times_{\mathbf{e_{n}}}\mathcal{K}_{n-1,c_{\mathbf{\overline{e}_{n}}}}^{p}
. This completes the proof. ∎

We collect below two subcases of the previous result.

1.

When
n=2
, we have
\mathcal{K}_{2,c}^{p}=\mathcal{K}_{1,c_{\mathbf{e_{2}}}}^{p}\times_{\gamma_{1}}\mathcal{K}_{1,c_{\mathbf{\overline{e}_{2}}}}
, where
c=c_{\mathbf{e_{2}}}\mathbf{e_{2}}+c_{\mathbf{\overline{e}_{2}}}\mathbf{\overline{e}_{2}}
.

2.

When
n=3
, we obtain
\mathcal{K}_{3,c}^{p}=\mathcal{K}_{2,c_{\mathbf{e_{3}}}}^{p}\times_{\mathbf{e_{3}}}\mathcal{K}_{2,c_{\mathbf{\overline{e}_{3}}}}^{p}
, where
c=c_{\mathbf{e_{3}}}\gamma_{3}+c_{\mathbf{\overline{e}_{3}}}\overline{\gamma}_{3}
.

The next result will be important in the next section to draw pictures of the higher dimensional filled-in Julia sets.

Theorem 4.2

Let
n\geq 1
be an integer and
c\in\mathbb{M}\mathbb{C}(n)
. Let
R:=\max\{|c|,2^{1/(p-1)}\}
. Then the following assertions are equivalent:

1.

\zeta\in\mathcal{K}_{n,c}^{p}
.

2.

\|f_{c}^{m}(\zeta)\|_{n}\leq R
for every integer
m\geq 1
.

Proof

The proof will be by induction on
n\geq 1
. The case
n=1
was proved in the previous section and is Theorem
3.2
.

Now, assume that the statement of the theorem is true for
n=k
, that is, for any
c\in\mathbb{M}\mathbb{C}(k)
, (1) is equivalent to (2). We have to prove that (1) is equivalent to (2) for
n=k+1
. Let
\zeta,c\in\mathbb{M}\mathbb{C}(k+1)
be expressed in their idempotent representation:

c=c_{\mathbf{e_{k+1}}}\mathbf{e_{k+1}}+c_{\mathbf{\overline{e}_{k+1}}}\mathbf{\overline{e}_{k+1}}\quad\text{ and }\quad\zeta=\zeta_{\mathbf{e_{k+1}}}\mathbf{e_{k+1}}+\zeta_{\mathbf{\overline{e}_{k+1}}}\mathbf{\overline{e}_{k+1}},

where
c_{\mathbf{e_{k+1}}},c_{\mathbf{\overline{e}_{k+1}}},\zeta_{\mathbf{e_{k+1}}},\zeta_{\mathbf{\overline{e}_{k+1}}}\in\mathbb{M}\mathbb{C}(k)
. To simplify the notations, we set
c_{1}:=c_{\mathbf{e_{k+1}}}
,
c_{2}:=c_{\mathbf{\overline{e}_{k+1}}}
,
\zeta_{1}:=\zeta_{\mathbf{e_{k+1}}}
, and
\zeta_{2}:=\zeta_{\mathbf{\overline{e}_{k+1}}}
.

Assume that
\|f_{c}^{m}(\zeta)\|_{n}\leq R
for any integer
m\geq 1
. Then, from the definition of the filled-in Julia set,
\zeta\in\mathcal{K}_{k+1,c}^{p}
. So, (2) implies (1).

Assume that
\zeta\in\mathcal{K}_{n,c}^{p}
. Then,
\zeta_{1}\in\mathcal{K}_{k,c_{1}}^{p}
and
\zeta_{2}\in\mathcal{K}_{k,c_{2}}^{p}
by Theorem
4.1
. By the induction hypothesis, we get

\|f_{c_{1}}^{m}(\zeta_{1})\|_{k}\leq R\quad\text{ and }\quad\|f_{c_{2}}^{m}(\zeta_{2})\|_{k}\leq R

for any integer
m\geq 1
. Using the fact that for any integer
m\geq 1
, we have

f_{c}^{m}(\zeta)=f_{c_{1}}^{m}(\zeta_{1})\mathbf{e_{k+1}}+f_{c_{2}}^{m}(\zeta_{2})\mathbf{\overline{e}_{k+1}},

and using the expression of the norm in terms of the idempotent components, we get

\|f_{c}^{m}(\zeta)\|_{k+1}=\sqrt{\frac{\|f_{c_{1}}^{m}(\zeta_{1})\|_{k}^{2}+\|f_{c_{2}}^{m}(\zeta_{2})\|_{k}^{2}}{2}}\leq\sqrt{R^{2}}=R.

Hence
\|f_{c}^{m}(\zeta)\|_{k+1}\leq R
and the statement is proved for
n=k+1
.

By the Principle of Mathematical Induction, the proof is complete. ∎

Definitions and Preliminaries for the Characterization of 3D slices

In this section, we present the main tools needed to obtain our main result on the characterization of the principal 3D slices.

6.1
An equivalent relation

The next definition introduces the space of iterates.

Definition 5

Let
c\in\mathbb{M}(n)
and
\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}}\in\mathbb{I}(n)
. The (real) vector subspace of iterates is defined as followed:

\mathrm{It}^{p}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}}):=\spn_{\mathbb{R}}\{f_{c}^{m}(\zeta)\,:\,\zeta\in\mathbb{T}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})\text{ and }m\in\mathbb{N}\}.

When we write ”characterization of the 3D slices”, what we mean is to find the equivalent classes of the following equivalent relation.

Definition 6

Let
c\in\mathbb{M}(n)
. Let
\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}}\in\mathbb{I}(n)
be distinct principal units and let
\mathbf{v_{1}},\mathbf{v_{2}},\mathbf{v_{3}}\in\mathbb{I}(n)
be three other distinct principal units
1
1

1

The units
\mathbf{v_{1}}
,
\mathbf{v_{2}}
,
\mathbf{v_{3}}
might be the same as the units
\mathbf{u_{1}}
,
\mathbf{u_{2}}
,
\mathbf{u_{3}}
. The only important fact is that you choose a set of three different units.
. Define
M_{1}:=\mathrm{It}^{p}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
and
M_{2}:=\mathrm{It}^{p}(\mathbf{v_{1}},\mathbf{v_{2}},\mathbf{v_{3}})
. The 3D slices
\mathcal{T}^{p}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
and
\mathcal{T}^{p}(\mathbf{v_{1}},\mathbf{v_{2}},\mathbf{v_{3}})
are equivalent, denoted by
\mathcal{T}^{p}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})\sim\mathcal{T}^{p}(\mathbf{v_{1}},\mathbf{v_{2}},\mathbf{v_{3}})
if and only if there exists a linear bijective application
\varphi:M_{1}\rightarrow M_{2}
such that

1.

\varphi(f_{c}(x\mathbf{u_{1}}+y\mathbf{u_{2}}+z\mathbf{u_{3}}))=f_{c}(x\mathbf{v_{1}}+y\mathbf{v_{2}}+z\mathbf{v_{3}})
, for any
x,y,z\in\mathbb{R}
.

2.

\varphi(f_{c}(\zeta))=f_{c}(\varphi(\zeta))
, for any
\zeta\in\mathrm{It}^{p}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
.

Condition (
1
) guarantees the first iterates with a starting point in
\mathbb{T}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
and in
\mathbb{T}(\mathbf{v_{1}},\mathbf{v_{2}},\mathbf{v_{3}})
respectively are the same. This is especially important if
\mathbb{T}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})\not\subseteq\mathrm{It}^{p}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
or
\mathbb{T}(\mathbf{v_{1}},\mathbf{v_{2}},\mathbf{v_{3}})\not\subseteq\mathrm{It}^{p}(\mathbf{v_{1}},\mathbf{v_{2}},\mathbf{v_{3}})
.

Condition (
2
) guarantees the subsequent iterates behave in the same way. Indeed, an induction argument shows that

\varphi(f_{c}^{m}(\zeta))=f_{c}^{m}(\varphi(\zeta)).

Since
\varphi
is a bijective linear map, the inverse is also a bijective map and
\varphi
and
\varphi^{-1}
are continuous. Therefore, for
\zeta\in\mathbb{T}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
and
\eta\in\mathbb{T}(\mathbf{v_{1}},\mathbf{v_{2}},\mathbf{v_{3}})
, the sequence
(f_{c}^{m}(\zeta))_{m=1}^{\infty}
is bounded if and only if the sequence
(f_{c}^{m}(\eta))_{m=1}^{\infty}
is bounded.

Notice that Definition
6
is similar to Definition 5 in
[
5
]
. However in general the map
\varphi
can’t be applied directly to
\mathcal{T}^{p}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
to obtain
\mathcal{T}^{p}(\mathbf{v_{1}},\mathbf{v_{2}},\mathbf{v_{3}})
because it might not be defined on the subspaces
\mathbb{T}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
and
\mathbb{T}(\mathbf{v_{1}},\mathbf{v_{2}},\mathbf{v_{3}})
. In opposition to what was just said, the map
\varphi
in the definition in
[
5
]
is always defined on the subspaces
\mathbb{T}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
and
\mathbb{T}(\mathbf{v_{1}},\mathbf{v_{2}},\mathbf{v_{3}})
. Therefore, our definition covers both cases, the multicomplex filled-in Julia sets and the multicomplex Mandelbrot sets.

Theorem 6.1

Let
c\in\mathbb{M}(n)
. The relation
\sim
is an equivalent relation on the set of 3D slices of
\mathcal{K}_{n,c}^{p}
, meaning that
\sim
is a reflexive, symmetric, and transitive binary operation on the family of principal 3D slices.

Proof

We have to show that the binary operation
\sim
is reflexive, symmetric, and transitive.

(a)

Let
\mathcal{T}^{p}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
be a principal 3D slice. Let
M_{1}=M_{2}=\mathrm{It}^{p}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
and let
\varphi:M_{1}\rightarrow M_{2}
be the identity map
\varphi(\zeta)=\zeta
. Then, all conditions in the definition of the relation
\sim
are automatically verified and
\mathcal{T}^{p}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})\sim\mathcal{T}^{p}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
.

(b)

Let
\mathcal{T}^{p}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
and
\mathcal{T}^{p}(\mathbf{v_{1}},\mathbf{v_{2}},\mathbf{v_{3}})
be two principal 3D slices such that

\mathcal{T}^{p}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})\sim\mathcal{T}^{p}(\mathbf{v_{1}},\mathbf{v_{2}},\mathbf{v_{3}}).

Then there is a bijective map
\varphi:M_{1}\rightarrow M_{2}
, where
M_{1}=\mathrm{It}^{p}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
and
M_{2}=\mathrm{It}^{p}(\mathbf{v_{1}},\mathbf{v_{2}},\mathbf{v_{3}})
, such that

(1)

\varphi(f_{c}(x\mathbf{u_{1}}+y\mathbf{u_{2}}+z\mathbf{u_{3}}))=f_{c}(x\mathbf{v_{1}}+y\mathbf{v_{2}}+z\mathbf{v_{3}})
, for any
x,y,z\in\mathbb{R}
.

(2)

\varphi(f_{c}(\zeta))=f_{c}(\varphi(\zeta))
, for any
\zeta\in\mathrm{It}^{p}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
.

Let
\psi:=\varphi^{-1}
. Therefore,
\psi:M_{2}\rightarrow M_{1}
and from the condition (1), we get

\displaystyle\psi(f_{c}(x\mathbf{v_{1}}+y\mathbf{v_{2}}+z\mathbf{v_{3}}))

\displaystyle=\varphi^{-1}\big(\varphi(f_{c}(x\mathbf{u_{1}}+y\mathbf{u_{2}}+z\mathbf{u_{3}}))\big)

\displaystyle=f_{c}(x\mathbf{u_{1}}+y\mathbf{u_{2}}+z\mathbf{u_{3}}).

Also, writing
\zeta=\varphi(\psi(\zeta))
, from condition (2), we obtain

\displaystyle f_{c}^{m}(\zeta)=f_{c}^{m}(\varphi(\psi(\zeta)))=\varphi(f_{c}^{m}(\psi(\zeta))

and taking the inverse of
\varphi
on each side, we get

\psi(f_{c}^{m}(\zeta))=f_{c}^{m}(\psi(\zeta)).

Hence,
\phi
satisfies the two conditions in Definition
6
and
\mathcal{T}^{p}(\mathbf{v_{1}},\mathbf{v_{2}},\mathbf{v_{3}})\sim\mathcal{T}^{p}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
.

(c)

To simplify the notation, we will omit the units in the 3D slices. Let
\mathcal{T}_{1}^{p}
,
\mathcal{T}_{2}^{p}
, and
\mathcal{T}_{3}^{p}
be three principal 3D slices such that
\mathcal{T}_{1}^{p}\sim\mathcal{T}_{2}^{p}
and
\mathcal{T}_{2}^{p}\sim\mathcal{T}_{3}^{p}
. Then there exist two bijective maps
\varphi_{1}:M_{1}\rightarrow M_{2}
and
\varphi_{2}:M_{2}\rightarrow M_{3}
such that conditions (1) and (2) from Definition

are satisfied. Then it is not hard to see that the map
\psi:M_{1}\rightarrow M_{3}
defined by
\psi(\zeta)=\varphi_{2}(\varphi_{1}(\zeta))
satisfies conditions (1) and (2) of Definition

. Hence,
\mathcal{T}_{1}^{p}\sim\mathcal{T}_{3}^{p}
and this ends the proof. ∎

6.2
Characterization of the space of iterates

The goal now is to find simple expressions for the space of iterates. To do so, we introduce the following (real) vector subspaces.

1.

\mathbb{M}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}}):=\spn_{\mathbb{R}}\{\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}},\mathbf{u_{1}}\mathbf{u_{2}}\mathbf{u_{3}}\}
.

2.

\mathbb{L}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}}):=\spn_{\mathbb{R}}\{1,\mathbf{u_{1}}\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{1}}\mathbf{u_{3}},\mathbf{u_{2}}\mathbf{u_{3}}\}
.

3.

\mathbb{S}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}}):=\spn_{\mathbb{R}}\{1,\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}},\mathbf{u_{1}}\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{1}}\mathbf{u_{3}},\mathbf{u_{2}}\mathbf{u_{3}},\mathbf{u_{1}}\mathbf{u_{2}}\mathbf{u_{3}}\}
.

If one of the
\mathbf{u_{1}}
,
\mathbf{u_{2}}
,
\mathbf{u_{3}}
is equal to
1
, then we will always assume, without loss of generality, that
\mathbf{u_{1}}=1
. We can now prove the following results.

Theorem 6.2

Let
\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}}\in\mathbb{I}(n)
. Then

1.

The space
\mathbb{S}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
is closed under multiplication.

2.

If
\mathbf{u_{1}}=1
or
\mathbf{u_{i}}\mathbf{u_{j}}=\pm\mathbf{u_{l}}
for some index
i,j,l\in\{1,2,3\}
, then
\mathbb{M}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})=\mathbb{L}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
.

3.

The space
\mathbb{L}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
is closed under multiplication.

4.

If
\eta\in\mathbb{M}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
and
\zeta\in\mathbb{L}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
, then
\eta\zeta\in\mathbb{M}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
.

5.

If
\eta,\zeta\in\mathbb{M}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
, then
\eta\zeta\in\mathbb{L}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
.

6.

If
u_{1}=1
or
\mathbf{u_{k}}\mathbf{u_{l}}=\pm\mathbf{u_{h}}
for some index
k,l,h\in\{1,2,3\}
, then
\mathbb{T}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})\subseteq\mathbb{L}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
.

Proof

See
(
5
, Lemma 2)
for the complete proof of part (1).

To show (2), assume first that
\mathbf{u_{1}}=1
. From the definition of both subspaces, we get the equality
\mathbb{M}(1,\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})=\mathbb{L}(1,\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
. Now, assume, without loss of generality, that
\mathbf{u_{2}}\mathbf{u_{3}}=\pm\mathbf{u_{1}}
. From that additional assumption, we see that
\mathbf{u_{1}}\mathbf{u_{2}}\mathbf{u_{3}}=\pm 1=d(1)
with
d=\pm 1
,
\mathbf{u_{1}}\mathbf{u_{2}}=\pm\mathbf{u_{2}^{2}}\mathbf{u_{3}}=a\mathbf{u_{3}}
with
a=\pm\mathbf{u_{2}^{2}}\neq 0
,
\mathbf{u_{1}}\mathbf{u_{3}}=\pm\mathbf{u_{3}^{2}}\mathbf{u_{2}}=b\mathbf{u_{2}}
with
b=\pm\mathbf{u_{3}^{2}}\neq 0
and
\mathbf{u_{2}}\mathbf{u_{3}}=\pm\mathbf{u_{1}}=c\mathbf{u_{1}}
with
c=\pm 1\neq 0
. Therefore, we get

\eta=A\mathbf{u_{1}}+B\mathbf{u_{2}}+C\mathbf{u_{3}}+D\mathbf{u_{1}}\mathbf{u_{2}}\mathbf{u_{3}}=Dd+Ac\mathbf{u_{2}}\mathbf{u_{3}}+Bb\mathbf{u_{1}}\mathbf{u_{3}}+Ca\mathbf{u_{1}}\mathbf{u_{2}}

and hence
\eta\in\mathbb{M}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
if and only if
\eta\in\mathbb{L}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
.

To show (3), it is sufficient to show that every multiplication of the basis elements generating
\mathbb{L}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
is again one of the basis elements. This can be achieved by constructing the following multiplication table:

\cdot

1

\mathbf{u_{1}}\mathbf{u_{2}}

\mathbf{u_{1}}\mathbf{u_{3}}

\mathbf{u_{2}}\mathbf{u_{3}}

1

1

\mathbf{u_{1}}\mathbf{u_{2}}

\mathbf{u_{1}}\mathbf{u_{3}}

\mathbf{u_{2}}\mathbf{u_{3}}

\mathbf{u_{1}}\mathbf{u_{2}}

\mathbf{u_{1}}\mathbf{u_{2}}

\pm 1

\pm\mathbf{u_{2}}\mathbf{u_{3}}

\pm\mathbf{u_{1}}\mathbf{u_{3}}

\mathbf{u_{1}}\mathbf{u_{3}}

\mathbf{u_{1}}\mathbf{u_{3}}

\pm\mathbf{u_{2}}\mathbf{u_{3}}

\pm 1

\pm\mathbf{u_{1}}\mathbf{u_{2}}

\mathbf{u_{2}}\mathbf{u_{3}}

\mathbf{u_{2}}\mathbf{u_{3}}

\pm\mathbf{u_{1}}\mathbf{u_{3}}

\pm\mathbf{u_{1}}\mathbf{u_{2}}

\pm 1

Hence,
\mathbb{L}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
is closed under multiplication.

To show parts (4) and (5), we use the same strategy that was used to show (3). We therefore omit their proofs and this concludes the proof of the statement. ∎

We can now prove the following result regarding the precise expression of the vector subspaces
\mathrm{It}^{p}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
.

Theorem 6.3

Let
\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}}\in\mathbb{I}(n)
and
c\in\mathbb{R}
. Then

1.

If
p
is even, then
\mathrm{It}^{p}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})=\mathbb{L}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
.

2.

If
p
is odd and if
c=0
, then
\mathrm{It}^{p}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})=\mathbb{M}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
.

3.

If
p
is odd and if
c\neq 0
, then

(a)

\mathrm{It}^{p}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})=\mathbb{M}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
if
\mathbf{u_{1}}=1
or if
\mathbf{u_{k}}\mathbf{u_{l}}=\pm\mathbf{u_{h}}
for some distinct
k,l,h\in\{1,2,3\}
.

(b)

\mathrm{It}^{p}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})=\mathbb{S}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
otherwise.

Proof

We first start by showing part (1). Assume that
p
is even, so that
p=2k
for some positive integer
k
, and that
c\in\mathbb{R}
. We have to show an equality between two sets.

We start by showing the inclusion
\mathrm{It}^{p}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})\subseteq\mathbb{L}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
. For the rest of this part, let
\zeta\in\mathbb{T}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
with
\zeta=A\mathbf{u_{1}}+B\mathbf{u_{2}}+C\mathbf{u_{3}}
where
A,B,C\in\mathbb{R}
. We will prove using induction that
f_{c}^{m}(\zeta)\in\mathbb{L}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
for any integer
m\geq 1
. The first iterate is
f^{1}_{c}(\zeta)=\zeta^{2k}+c
. We will prove with an induction on
k
that
\zeta^{2k}\in\mathbb{L}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
. Starting with
k=1
, we have

\zeta^{2}=A^{2}\mathbf{u_{1}^{2}}+B^{2}\mathbf{u_{2}^{2}}+C^{2}\mathbf{u_{3}^{2}}+2AB\mathbf{u_{1}}\mathbf{u_{2}}+2AC\mathbf{u_{1}}\mathbf{u_{3}}+2BC\mathbf{u_{2}}\mathbf{u_{3}}.

Since
\mathbf{u_{1}^{2}}=\pm 1
,
\mathbf{u_{2}^{2}}=\pm 1
, and
\mathbf{u_{3}^{2}}=\pm 1
, we deduce that
\zeta^{2}\in\mathbb{L}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
. Now assume for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we have
\zeta^{2k}\in\mathbb{L}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
. Then, we have

\zeta^{2(k+1)}=\zeta^{2}\zeta^{2k}.

From Theorem
6.2
part (
3
), the space
\mathbb{L}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
is closed under multicomplex multiplication. We know that
\zeta^{2}\in\mathbb{L}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
and, from the induction hypothesis, we have
\zeta^{2k}\in\mathbb{L}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
. Therefore
\zeta^{2}\zeta^{2k}\in\mathbb{L}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
and hence
\zeta^{2(k+1)}\in\mathbb{L}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
. Adding the number
c
, we have just shown that
f_{c}^{1}(\zeta)\in\mathbb{L}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
.

Now assume that
f_{c}^{m}(\zeta)\in\mathbb{L}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
for some integer
m\geq 1
. We want to show that
f_{c}^{m+1}(\zeta)\in\mathbb{L}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
. The expression of the
(m+1)
-th iterate is

f_{c}^{m+1}(\zeta)=(f_{c}^{m}(\zeta))^{2k}+c.

From Theorem
6.2
part (
3
), the space
\mathbb{L}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
is closed under multicomplex multiplication. Hence, we must have that
(f_{c}^{m}(\zeta))^{2k}\in\mathbb{L}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
because
f_{c}^{m}(\zeta)\in\mathbb{L}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
from the induction hypothesis. Hence,
f_{c}^{m+1}(\zeta)\in\mathbb{L}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
. This concludes the induction on
m
and the proof of
\mathrm{It}^{p}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})\subseteq\mathbb{L}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
.

We now want to prove the other direction, that is
\mathbb{L}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})\subset\mathrm{It}^{p}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
. To do that, we will show that each basis elements (
1
,
\mathbf{u_{1}}\mathbf{u_{2}}
,
\mathbf{u_{1}}\mathbf{u_{3}}
,
\mathbf{u_{2}}\mathbf{u_{3}}
) is a linear combination of some iterates. Setting
\zeta=\mathbf{u_{1}}
implies that

f_{c}^{1}(\mathbf{u_{1}})=(\mathbf{u_{1}})^{2k}+c=(\mathbf{u_{1}^{2}})^{k}+c=a\cdot 1,

where
a=(\mathbf{u_{1}^{2}})^{k}+c
. Hence,
1=f_{c}^{1}(\mathbf{u_{1}})/a
. To show that the other basis elements are a linear combination of some iterates, we will use a similar trick from Brouillette and Rochon’s paper (see the proof of
(
5
, Lemma 3)
). For
l,h\in\{1,2,3\}
with
l\leq h
and
t\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}
, we have

(t\mathbf{u_{l}}+\mathbf{u_{h}})^{2k}=\sum_{s=0}^{2k}\binom{2k}{s}t^{s}(\mathbf{u_{l}})^{s}(\mathbf{u_{h}})^{2k-s}

and splitting the sum over the
s
that are even and the
s
that are odd, we get

(t\mathbf{u_{l}}+\mathbf{u_{h}})^{2k}=\sum_{s=0}^{k}\binom{2k}{2s}t^{2s}(\mathbf{u_{l}^{2}})^{s}(\mathbf{u_{h}^{2}})^{k-s}+\Big(\sum_{s=0}^{k-1}\binom{2k}{2s+1}t^{2s+1}(\mathbf{u_{l}^{2}})^{s}(\mathbf{u_{h}^{2}})^{k-s}\Big)\mathbf{u_{l}}\mathbf{u_{h}}.

The last equation can be rewritten as
(t\mathbf{u_{l}}+\mathbf{u_{h}})^{2k}=A(t)+B(t)\mathbf{u_{l}}\mathbf{u_{h}}
, where
A(t)
and
B(t)
are real-valued polynomials of degree
2k
and
2k-1
respectively. Since a polynomial has finitely many roots, we may choose a number
t_{0}
(that depends on
l
and
h
) such that
B(t_{0})\neq 0
. We then obtain

f_{c}^{1}(t_{0}\mathbf{u_{l}}+\mathbf{u_{h}})=A(t_{0})+c+B(t_{0})\mathbf{u_{l}}\mathbf{u_{h}}

and using the fact that
1=f_{c}^{1}(\mathbf{u_{1}})/a
, we can rewrite the last expression as followed

\mathbf{u_{l}}\mathbf{u_{h}}=\frac{f_{c}^{1}(t_{0}\mathbf{u_{l}}+\mathbf{u_{h}})}{B(t_{0})}-\Big(\frac{A(t_{0})+c}{aB(t_{0})}\Big)f_{c}^{1}(\mathbf{u_{1}})

Therefore, we obtain
\mathbb{L}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})\subseteq\mathrm{It}^{p}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
, this finishes the proof of part (1).

Now we show part (2). Our goal is to show that for
p
odd and
c=0
, we have
\mathrm{It}^{p}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})=\mathbb{M}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
. We first show that
\mathrm{It}^{p}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})\subseteq\mathbb{M}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
. Consider
\zeta\in\mathbb{T}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
and
p=2k+1
for
k\in\mathbb{N}
. We will prove by induction on
m
that
f_{c}^{m}(\zeta)\in\mathbb{M}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
. For
m=1
, we have

f_{c}^{1}(\zeta)=\zeta^{2k+1}.

From part (1), we know that
\zeta^{2k}\in\mathbb{L}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
for any
k\geq 1
. Since
\zeta\in\mathbb{T}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})\subseteq\mathbb{M}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
, it follows from Theorem
6.2
part (
4
) that

\zeta^{2k+1}=\zeta\zeta^{2k}\in\mathbb{M}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}}).

Hence,
f_{c}^{1}(\zeta)\in\mathbb{M}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
. Now assume that
f_{c}^{m}(\zeta)\in\mathbb{M}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
for some positive integer
m\geq 1
. Then we have

\displaystyle f_{c}^{m+1}(\zeta)=(f_{c}^{m}(\zeta))^{2k+1}

\displaystyle=(f_{c}^{m}(\zeta))^{2k}(f_{c}^{m}(\zeta))=((f_{c}^{m}(\zeta))^{2})^{k}(f_{c}^{m}(\zeta)).

Using Theorem
6.2
part (
5
), we have
(f_{c}^{m}(\zeta))^{2}\in\mathbb{L}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
since, from the induction hypothesis,
f_{c}^{m}(\zeta)\in\mathbb{M}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
. From Theorem
6.2
part (
3
), the set
\mathbb{L}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
is closed under multicomplex multiplication and therefore
((f_{c}^{m}(\zeta))^{2})^{k}\in\mathbb{L}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
. Now, using Theorem
6.2
part (
4
), we obtain that
((f_{c}^{m}(\zeta))^{2})^{k}(f_{c}^{m}(\zeta))\in\mathbb{M}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
because
f_{c}^{m}(\zeta)\in\mathbb{M}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
. Hence, we obtain
f_{c}^{m+1}(\zeta)\in\mathbb{M}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
. Hence, by the Principle of Mathematical Induction, the proof of the inclusion
\mathrm{It}^{p}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})\subseteq\mathbb{M}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
is completed.

We now want to show that
\mathbb{M}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})\subseteq\mathrm{It}^{p}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
. We will do this by expressing each basis element
\mathbf{u_{1}}
,
\mathbf{u_{2}}
,
\mathbf{u_{3}}
, and
\mathbf{u_{1}}\mathbf{u_{2}}\mathbf{u_{3}}
as a linear combination of iterates. Let
\zeta=\mathbf{u_{l}}
with
l=1,2,3
, then we have

f_{c}^{1}(\mathbf{u_{l}})=(\mathbf{u_{l}})^{2k+1}=(\mathbf{u_{l}^{2}})^{k}\mathbf{u_{l}}.

Hence,
\mathbf{u_{l}}=f_{c}^{1}(\mathbf{u_{l}})/a_{l}
, where
a=(\mathbf{u_{l}^{2}})^{k}
. To express the other element
\mathbf{u_{1}}\mathbf{u_{2}}\mathbf{u_{3}}
as a linear combination of iterates, we will adapt the trick used earlier that comes from Brouillette and Rochon’s paper (see the proof of
(
5
, Lemma 3)
). Consider
\zeta=t\mathbf{u_{1}}+\mathbf{u_{2}}+\mathbf{u_{3}}
with
t\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}
. Then we have

f_{c}^{1}(\zeta)=\sum_{s=0}^{2k+1}\sum_{r=0}^{2k+1-s}\binom{2k+1}{s}\binom{2k+1-s}{r}t^{s}(\mathbf{u_{1}})^{s}(\mathbf{u_{2}})^{r}(\mathbf{u_{3}})^{2k+1-s-r}

Splitting the summation over the different parities of the integers
s
and
r
, we obtain

\displaystyle f_{c}^{1}(\zeta)

\displaystyle=\Big(\sum_{s=0}^{k}\sum_{r=0}^{r-s}\binom{2k+1}{2s}\binom{2k-2s+1}{2r}t^{2s}(\mathbf{u_{1}^{2}})^{s}(\mathbf{u_{2}^{2}})^{r}(\mathbf{u_{3}^{2}})^{k-s-r}\Big)\mathbf{u_{3}}

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+\Big(\sum_{s=0}^{k}\sum_{r=0}^{r-s}\binom{2k+1}{2s}\binom{2k-2s+1}{2r+1}t^{2s}(\mathbf{u_{1}^{2}})^{s}(\mathbf{u_{2}^{2}})^{r}(\mathbf{u_{3}^{2}})^{k-s-r}\Big)\mathbf{u_{2}}

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+\Big(\sum_{s=0}^{k}\sum_{r=0}^{r-s}\binom{2k+1}{2s+1}\binom{2k-2s+1}{2r}t^{2s+1}(\mathbf{u_{1}^{2}})^{s}(\mathbf{u_{2}^{2}})^{r}(\mathbf{u_{3}^{2}})^{k-s-r}\Big)\mathbf{u_{1}}

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+\Big(\sum_{s=0}^{k}\sum_{r=0}^{r-s}\binom{2k+1}{2s+1}\binom{2k-2s+1}{2r+1}t^{2s+1}(\mathbf{u_{1}^{2}})^{s}(\mathbf{u_{2}^{2}})^{r}(\mathbf{u_{3}^{2}})^{k-s-r+1}\Big)\mathbf{u_{1}}\mathbf{u_{2}}\mathbf{u_{3}}

which can be rewritten as

f_{c}^{1}(\zeta)=A(t)\mathbf{u_{1}}+B(t)\mathbf{u_{2}}+C(t)\mathbf{u_{3}}+D(t)\mathbf{u_{1}}\mathbf{u_{2}}\mathbf{u_{3}}

where
A(t)
,
B(t)
,
C(t)
, and
D(t)
are polynomials in
t
. Since polynomials have only finitely many roots, we can choose a value
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
D(t_{0})\neq 0
. Therefore, we obtain

\mathbf{u_{1}}\mathbf{u_{2}}\mathbf{u_{3}}=\frac{1}{D(t_{0})}f^{1}_{c}(\zeta)-\frac{A(t_{0})}{D(t_{0})a_{1}}f_{c}^{1}(\mathbf{u_{1}})-\frac{B(t_{0})}{D(t_{0})a_{2}}f_{c}^{1}(\mathbf{u_{2}})-\frac{C(t_{0})}{D(t_{0})a_{3}}f_{c}^{1}(\mathbf{u_{3}}).

This shows that every basis element can be rewritten as a linear combination of iterates and therefore
\mathbb{M}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})\subseteq\mathrm{It}^{p}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
.

Finally, we show part (3). Assume that
p
is odd, but
c\neq 0
. The proof of part (a) is similar to the proof of parts (1) and (2). We will therefore focus on the proof of part (b). We want to show that
\mathrm{It}^{p}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})=\mathbb{S}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
.

We start by showing that
\mathrm{It}^{p}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})\subseteq\mathbb{S}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
. Let
\zeta\in\mathbb{T}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
. We will do an induction on
m
in
f_{c}^{m}(\zeta)
. Let
m=1
so that
f_{c}^{1}(\zeta)=\zeta^{2k+1}+c
. From the proof of the base case in part (2), we know that
\zeta^{2k+1}\in\mathbb{M}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
. This implies that
f_{c}^{1}(\zeta)=\zeta^{2k+1}+c\in\mathbb{S}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
. Now assume that the claim is true for some integer
m>1
, which means that
f_{c}^{m}(\zeta)\in\mathbb{S}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
. From the definition of the iterates, we have
f_{c}^{m+1}(\zeta)=(f_{c}^{m}(\zeta))^{2k+1}+c
. From the Induction Hypothesis, we have
f_{c}^{m}(\zeta)\in\mathbb{S}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
. From Theorem
6.2
part (
1
), the subspace
\mathbb{S}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
is closed under multiplication and we then get
(f_{c}^{m}(\zeta))^{2k+1}\in\mathbb{S}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
. Hence,
f_{c}^{m+1}(\zeta)\in\mathbb{S}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
and we conclude that
\mathrm{It}^{p}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})\subseteq\mathbb{S}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
.

We now show the reverse inclusion by showing that each basis element of
\mathbb{S}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
can be written down as a linear combination of some iterates. Notice that
f_{c}^{1}(0)=c
and since
c\neq 0
, we have
1=f_{c}^{1}(0)/c
. Let
\zeta=\mathbf{u_{l}}
for
l=1,2,3
so that

f_{c}^{1}(\mathbf{u_{l}})=(\mathbf{u_{l}^{2}})^{k}\mathbf{u_{l}}+c

and hence
\mathbf{u_{l}}=\frac{f_{c}^{1}(\mathbf{u_{l}})}{a_{l}}-\frac{f_{c}^{1}(0)}{a_{l}}
with
a_{l}=(\mathbf{u_{l}^{2}})^{k}
. Let
\zeta=t_{0}\mathbf{u_{1}}+\mathbf{u_{2}}+\mathbf{u_{3}}
so that, from the calculations performed at the end of the proof of part (2), we get

\mathbf{u_{1}}\mathbf{u_{2}}\mathbf{u_{3}}=\frac{1}{D(t_{0})}f^{1}_{c}(\zeta)-f_{c}^{1}(0)-\frac{A(t_{0})}{D(t_{0})a_{1}}f_{c}^{1}(\mathbf{u_{1}})-\frac{B(t_{0})}{D(t_{0})a_{2}}f_{c}^{1}(\mathbf{u_{2}})-\frac{C(t_{0})}{D(t_{0})a_{3}}f_{c}^{1}(\mathbf{u_{3}}).

with
D_{k}\neq 0
. To find the linear combination to express the other basis elements, namely,
\mathbf{u_{1}}\mathbf{u_{2}}
,
\mathbf{u_{1}}\mathbf{u_{3}}
, and
\mathbf{u_{2}}\mathbf{u_{3}}
, we adapt for a second time the trick from Brouillette and Rochon’s paper (see the proof of
(
5
, Lemma 3)
). Let
t\in\mathbb{R}
with
t\neq 0
and define
\zeta=\mathbf{u_{1}}+t\mathbf{u_{2}}
. Then, using the binomial theorem, we get

f_{c}^{1}(\zeta)=c+\sum_{l=0}^{2k+1}\binom{2k+1}{l}(\mathbf{u_{1}})^{l}(t\mathbf{u_{2}})^{p-l}=c+A(t)\mathbf{u_{1}}+B(t)\mathbf{u_{2}}

where
A(t)=\sum_{l=0}^{k}\binom{2k+1}{2l+1}(\mathbf{u_{1}^{2}})^{l}(\mathbf{u_{2}^{2}})^{k-l}(t^{2})^{k-l}
and
B(t)=t\sum_{l=0}^{k}\binom{2k+1}{2l}(\mathbf{u_{1}^{2}})^{l}(\mathbf{u_{2}^{2}})^{k-l}(t^{2})^{k-l}
are polynomials in
t
. Using this expression for
f_{c}^{1}(\zeta)
, we can now write

\displaystyle f_{c}^{2}(\zeta)

\displaystyle=\Big(c+A(t)\mathbf{u_{1}}+B(t)\mathbf{u_{2}}\Big)^{2k+1}+c

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{2k+1}\binom{2k+1}{l}c^{l}\big(A(t)\mathbf{u_{1}}+B(t)\mathbf{u_{2}}\big)^{2k+1-l}+c

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{2k+1}\sum_{h=0}^{2k+1-l}\binom{2k+1}{l}\binom{2k+1-l}{h}c^{l}(A(t)\mathbf{u_{1}})^{h}(B(t)\mathbf{u_{2}})^{2k+1-l-h}+c.

Splitting the sums over the parity of
l
and
h
, we get

\displaystyle f_{c}^{2}(\zeta)=\Big(\sum_{l=0}^{k}\sum_{h=0}^{k-l}\binom{2k+1}{2l}\binom{2(k-l)+1}{2h}c^{2l}A(t)^{2h}B(t)^{2(k-l-h)+1}(\mathbf{u_{1}^{2}})^{h}(\mathbf{u_{2}^{2}})^{k-l-h}\Big)\mathbf{u_{2}}

\displaystyle\phantom{=}\,+\Big(\sum_{l=0}^{k}\sum_{h=0}^{k-l}\binom{2k+1}{2l}\binom{2(k-l)+1}{2h+1}c^{2l}A(t)^{2h+1}B(t)^{2(k-l-h)}(\mathbf{u_{1}^{2}})^{h}(\mathbf{u_{2}^{2}})^{k-l-h}\Big)\mathbf{u_{1}}

\displaystyle\phantom{=}\,+\Big(\sum_{l=0}^{k}\sum_{h=0}^{k-l}\binom{2k+1}{2l+1}\binom{2(k-l)}{2h}c^{2l+1}A(t)^{2h}B(t)^{2(k-l-h)}(\mathbf{u_{1}^{2}})^{h}(\mathbf{u_{2}^{2}})^{k-l-h}\Big)+c

\displaystyle\phantom{=}\,+\Big(\sum_{l=0}^{k}\sum_{h=0}^{k-l-1}\binom{2k+1}{2l+1}\binom{2(k-l)}{2h+1}c^{2l+1}A(t)^{2h+1}B(t)^{2(k-l-h)-1}(\mathbf{u_{1}^{2}})^{h}(\mathbf{u_{2}^{2}})^{k-l-h-1}\Big)\mathbf{u_{1}}\mathbf{u_{2}}

which can be rewritten as

f_{c}^{2}(\zeta)=\tilde{A}(t)+\tilde{B}(t)\mathbf{u_{1}}+\tilde{C}(t)\mathbf{u_{2}}+\tilde{D}(t)\mathbf{u_{1}}\mathbf{u_{2}}

where
\tilde{A}(t)
,
\tilde{B}(t)
,
\tilde{C}(t)
, and
\tilde{D}(t)
are polynomials in the variable
t
. Since any polynomial has finitely many roots, we can choose a value of
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
(
t_{0}\neq 0
) that avoids the roots of
\tilde{D}(t)
and therefore
\tilde{D}(t_{0})\neq 0
. Letting
t=t_{0}
, we get

f_{c}^{2}(\zeta)=\tilde{A}(t_{0})\frac{f_{c}^{1}(0)}{c}+\tilde{B}(t_{0})\Big(\frac{f_{c}^{1}(\mathbf{u_{1}})}{a_{1}}-\frac{f_{c}^{1}(0)}{a_{1}}\Big)+\tilde{C}(t_{0})\Big(\frac{f_{c}^{1}(\mathbf{u_{2}})}{a_{2}}-\frac{f_{c}^{1}(0)}{a_{2}}\Big)+\tilde{D}(t)\mathbf{u_{1}}\mathbf{u_{2}}

and after isolating
\mathbf{u_{1}}\mathbf{u_{2}}
, we get

\mathbf{u_{1}}\mathbf{u_{2}}=\frac{f_{c}^{2}(\zeta)}{\tilde{D}(t_{0})}+\Big(\frac{\frac{\tilde{B}(t_{0})}{a_{1}}+\frac{\tilde{C}(t_{0})}{a_{2}}-\frac{\tilde{A}(t_{0})}{c}}{\tilde{D}(t_{0})}\Big)f_{c}^{1}(0)-\frac{\tilde{B}(t_{0})}{a_{1}\tilde{D}(t_{0})}f_{c}^{1}(\mathbf{u_{1}})-\frac{\tilde{C}(t_{0})}{a_{2}\tilde{D}(t_{0})}f_{c}^{1}(\mathbf{u_{2}}).

Similarly, we can write
\mathbf{u_{1}}\mathbf{u_{3}}
and
\mathbf{u_{2}}\mathbf{u_{3}}
as a linear combinations of iterates. This shows the inclusion
\mathbb{S}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})\subseteq\mathrm{It}^{p}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
and completes the proof of the theorem. ∎

7
Characterization of 3D slices

The statement of Theorem
6.3
indicates that a space of iterates behave differently depending on the parity of the integer
p
and on the nature of the principal units used. We will therefore start by proving our main result on the classification of principal 3D slices when
p
is an even integer and then prove the result when
p
is an odd integer.

7.1
Main result for even powers

Before proving our first main result, we make several assumptions on the principal units. It is rather easy to see that
\mathcal{T}^{p}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})\sim\mathcal{T}^{p}(\sigma(\mathbf{u_{1}}),\sigma(\mathbf{u_{2}}),\sigma(\mathbf{u_{3}}))
where
\sigma
is a permutation of the symbols
\mathbf{u_{1}}
,
\mathbf{u_{2}}
,
\mathbf{u_{3}}
. The order of the units is therefore not important and we can choose a specific ordering.

We choose the following ordering for a triplet of principal units
\mathbf{u_{1}}
,
\mathbf{u_{2}}
,
\mathbf{u_{3}}
:
1
has priority for the first position over imaginary units and hyperbolic units; imaginary units have priority for the second position over hyperbolic units. When all of the principal units are of the same nature, we then put them in increasing index.

For instance, the 3D slices
\mathcal{T}^{p}(1,\mathbf{i_{1}},\mathbf{j_{3}})
,
\mathcal{T}^{p}(1,\mathbf{j_{3}},\mathbf{i_{1}})
,
\mathcal{T}^{p}(\mathbf{i_{1}},1,\mathbf{j_{3}})
, and
\mathcal{T}^{p}(\mathbf{i_{1}},\mathbf{j_{3}},1)
,
\mathcal{T}^{p}(\mathbf{j_{3}},1,\mathbf{i_{1}})
,
\mathcal{T}^{p}(\mathbf{j_{3}},\mathbf{i_{1}},1)
are all equivalent to
\mathcal{T}^{p}(1,\mathbf{i_{1}},\mathbf{j_{3}})
. Another example is the group of 3D slices
\mathcal{T}^{p}(\mathbf{i_{1}},\mathbf{i_{2}},\mathbf{i_{3}})
,
\mathcal{T}^{p}(\mathbf{i_{1}},\mathbf{i_{3}},\mathbf{i_{2}})
,
\mathcal{T}^{p}(\mathbf{i_{2}},\mathbf{i_{1}},\mathbf{i_{3}})
,
\mathcal{T}^{p}(\mathbf{i_{2}},\mathbf{i_{3}},\mathbf{i_{1}})
,
\mathcal{T}^{p}(\mathbf{i_{3}},\mathbf{i_{1}},\mathbf{i_{2}})
, and
\mathcal{T}^{p}(\mathbf{i_{3}},\mathbf{i_{2}},\mathbf{i_{1}})
. They are all equivalent to
\mathcal{T}^{p}(\mathbf{i_{1}},\mathbf{i_{2}},\mathbf{i_{3}})
. Without loss of generality, we will therefore assume that the principal units follow this choice of ordering.

Our first result concerning the characterization of the 3D slices of the filled-in Julia sets goes as followed. This is part 1 of Theorem
1.2

Theorem 7.1

Let
n\geq 3
be an integer and
c\in\mathbb{R}
. If
p
is an even positive integer, then there are
4
principal 3D slices.

Proof

Let
\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}}\in\mathbb{I}(n)
and choose
\mathbf{v_{1}},\mathbf{v_{2}},\mathbf{v_{3}}\in\mathbb{I}(n)
such that
\mathbf{u_{1}^{2}}=\mathbf{v_{1}^{2}}
,
\mathbf{u_{2}^{2}}=\mathbf{v_{2}^{2}}
, and
\mathbf{u_{3}^{2}}=\mathbf{v_{3}^{2}}
. Such units always exist if
n\geq 3
. We will show that
\mathcal{T}^{p}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})\sim\mathcal{T}^{p}(\mathbf{v_{1}},\mathbf{v_{2}},\mathbf{v_{3}})
.

When
p
is even, from Theorem
6.3
part (
1
), we have
\mathrm{It}^{p}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})=\mathbb{L}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
and
\mathrm{It}^{p}(\mathbf{v_{1}},\mathbf{v_{2}},\mathbf{v_{3}})=\mathbb{L}(\mathbf{v_{1}},\mathbf{v_{2}},\mathbf{v_{3}})
. So define the linear map
\varphi:\mathrm{It}^{p}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})\rightarrow\mathrm{It}^{p}(\mathbf{v_{1}},\mathbf{v_{2}},\mathbf{v_{3}})
on the basis elements and extend it linearly:
\varphi(1)=1
,
\varphi(\mathbf{u_{1}}\mathbf{u_{2}})=\mathbf{v_{1}}\mathbf{v_{2}}
,
\varphi(\mathbf{u_{1}}\mathbf{u_{3}})=\mathbf{v_{1}}\mathbf{v_{3}}
, and
\varphi(\mathbf{u_{2}}\mathbf{u_{3}})=\mathbf{v_{2}}\mathbf{v_{3}}
. Clearly
\varphi
is a bijective linear map.

A way to show that
f_{c}(\varphi(\zeta))=\varphi(f_{c}(\zeta))
is to show that
\varphi(\zeta^{p})=(\varphi(\zeta))^{p}
. In fact, we can show that
\varphi(\zeta\eta)=\varphi(\zeta)\varphi(\eta)
for any
\zeta,\eta\in\mathbb{L}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
. Let
\zeta=x+y\mathbf{u_{1}}\mathbf{u_{2}}+z\mathbf{u_{1}}\mathbf{u_{3}}+w\mathbf{u_{2}}\mathbf{u_{3}}
and
\eta=X+Y\mathbf{u_{1}}\mathbf{u_{2}}+Z\mathbf{u_{1}}\mathbf{u_{3}}+W\mathbf{u_{2}}\mathbf{u_{3}}
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\zeta\eta

\displaystyle=xX+yY\mathbf{u_{1}^{2}}\mathbf{u_{2}^{2}}+zZ\mathbf{u_{1}^{2}}\mathbf{u_{3}^{2}}+wW\mathbf{u_{2}^{2}}\mathbf{u_{3}^{2}}+(xY+yX+zW\mathbf{u_{3}^{2}}+wZ\mathbf{u_{3}^{2}})\mathbf{u_{1}}\mathbf{u_{2}}

\displaystyle\phantom{=}\,+(xZ+zX+yW\mathbf{u_{2}^{2}}+wY\mathbf{u_{2}^{2}})\mathbf{u_{1}}\mathbf{u_{3}}+(xW+wX+yZ\mathbf{u_{1}^{2}}+zY\mathbf{u_{1}^{2}})\mathbf{u_{2}}\mathbf{u_{3}}.

By linearity of
\varphi
and the assumptions on the squares of each principal units, we get

\displaystyle\varphi(\zeta\eta)

\displaystyle=xX+yY\mathbf{v_{1}^{2}}\mathbf{v_{2}^{2}}+zZ\mathbf{v_{1}^{2}}\mathbf{v_{3}^{2}}+wW\mathbf{v_{2}^{2}}\mathbf{v_{3}^{2}}+(xY+yX+zW\mathbf{v_{3}^{2}}+wZ\mathbf{v_{3}^{2}})\mathbf{v_{1}}\mathbf{v_{2}}

\displaystyle\phantom{=}\,+(xZ+zX+yW\mathbf{v_{2}^{2}}+wY\mathbf{v_{2}^{2}})\mathbf{v_{1}}\mathbf{v_{3}}+(xW+wX+yZ\mathbf{v_{1}^{2}}+zY\mathbf{v_{1}^{2}})\mathbf{v_{2}}\mathbf{v_{3}}

\displaystyle=\varphi(\zeta)\varphi(\eta).

An induction argument on
p=2k
then shows that
\varphi(\zeta^{p})=(\varphi(\zeta))^{p}
. Using this property, we get

\displaystyle\varphi(f_{c}(\zeta))=\varphi(\zeta^{p}+c)=\varphi(\zeta^{p})+\varphi(c)=(\varphi(\zeta))^{p}+c=f_{c}(\varphi(\zeta)).

Hence, condition (2) in Definition
6
is satisfied.

It remains to show that condition (1) in Definition
6
is satisfied, since
\mathbb{T}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})\not\subset\mathrm{It}^{p}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
and
\mathbb{T}(\mathbf{v_{1}},\mathbf{v_{2}},\mathbf{v_{3}})\not\subset\mathrm{It}^{p}(\mathbf{v_{1}},\mathbf{v_{2}},\mathbf{v_{3}})
. For
\eta=x\mathbf{u_{1}}+y\mathbf{u_{2}}+z\mathbf{u_{3}}
for
x,y,z\in\mathbb{R}
and
\zeta=X\mathbf{u_{1}}+Y\mathbf{u_{2}}+Z\mathbf{u_{3}}
for
X,Y,Z\in\mathbb{R}
, we calculate

\displaystyle\eta\zeta

\displaystyle=xX\mathbf{u_{1}^{2}}+yY\mathbf{u_{2}^{2}}+zZ\mathbf{u_{3}^{2}}+(xY+yX)\mathbf{u_{1}}\mathbf{u_{2}}+(xZ+zX)\mathbf{u_{1}}\mathbf{u_{3}}+(yZ+zY)\mathbf{u_{2}}\mathbf{u_{3}}.

Applying
\varphi
to the last equality and using linearity, we obtain

\varphi(\eta\zeta)=xX\mathbf{u_{1}^{2}}+yY\mathbf{u_{2}^{2}}+zZ\mathbf{u_{3}^{2}}+(xY+yX)\mathbf{v_{1}}\mathbf{v_{2}}+(xZ+zX)\mathbf{v_{1}}\mathbf{v_{3}}+(yZ+zY)\mathbf{v_{2}}\mathbf{v_{3}}

and since
\mathbf{u_{1}^{2}}=\mathbf{v_{1}^{2}}
,
\mathbf{u_{2}^{2}}=\mathbf{v_{2}^{2}}
and
\mathbf{u_{3}^{2}}=\mathbf{v_{3}^{2}}
, we conclude that

\varphi(\eta\zeta)=(x\mathbf{v_{1}}+y\mathbf{v_{2}}+z\mathbf{v_{3}})(X\mathbf{v_{1}}+Y\mathbf{v_{2}}+Z\mathbf{v_{3}}).

An induction argument on
p=2k
then implies that
\varphi(\zeta^{p})=(x\mathbf{v_{1}}+y\mathbf{v_{2}}+z\mathbf{v_{3}})^{p}
which means that

\varphi(f_{c}(x\mathbf{u_{1}}+y\mathbf{u_{2}}+z\mathbf{u_{3}}))=f_{c}(x\mathbf{v_{1}}+y\mathbf{v_{2}}+z\mathbf{v_{3}})

for arbitrary
x,y,z\in\mathbb{R}
. Hence, condition (2) of Definition
6
is satisfied and the two 3D slices are equivalent.

We therefore only need to study the sign of the squares of the principal units. There are
2
choices for each principal units, that is
+1
and
-1
. Since the order of the selection of the principal units
\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}}
is not important, this gives us
4
choices:

1.

\mathbf{u_{1}^{2}}=\mathbf{u_{2}^{2}}=\mathbf{u_{3}^{2}}=1
. In this case, all principal 3D slices are equivalent to
\mathcal{T}^{p}(1,\mathbf{j_{1}},\mathbf{j_{2}})
.

2.

\mathbf{u_{1}^{2}}=\mathbf{u_{3}^{2}}=1
and
\mathbf{u_{2}^{2}}=-1
. In this case, all principal 3D slices are equivalent to
\mathcal{T}^{p}(1,\mathbf{i_{1}},\mathbf{j_{1}})
.

3.

\mathbf{u_{1}^{2}}=1
and
\mathbf{u_{2}^{2}}=\mathbf{u_{3}^{2}}=-1
. In this case, all principal 3D slices are equivalent to
\mathcal{T}^{p}(1,\mathbf{i_{1}},\mathbf{i_{2}})
.

4.

\mathbf{u_{1}^{2}}=\mathbf{u_{2}^{2}}=\mathbf{u_{3}^{2}}=-1
. In this case, all principal 3D slices are equivalent to
\mathcal{T}^{p}(\mathbf{i_{1}},\mathbf{i_{2}},\mathbf{i_{3}})
.

This ends the proof. ∎

We notice that all principal 3D slices are obtained from units in the set of tricomplex numbers. We therefore obtain the following consequence of our result.

Corollary 1

Let
n\geq 3
et
p\geq 2
be integers and
c\in\mathbb{R}
. For any principal 3D slice
\mathcal{T}^{p}
of
\mathcal{K}_{n,c}^{p}
, there exists a principal 3D slice
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}^{p}
of
\mathcal{K}_{3,c}^{p}
such that
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}^{p}\sim\mathcal{T}^{p}
.

7.2
Main result for odd powers

We now treat the case when
p
is an odd integer and
c\neq 0
. The case
c=0
is treated very similarly as in the previous section. This comes from the fact that when
c=0
, the space of iterates is equal to
\mathbb{M}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
and this characterization of the space of iterates is valid for any choice of principal units. We therefore end up with the same characterization as in the even case and this proves part 2 of
1.2
.

Theorem 7.2

Let
p
be an odd integer and
c=0
. Then there are
4
distinct principal 3D slices.

It remains to prove part 3 of Theorem
1.2
, that is when
c\neq 0
.

Theorem 7.3

Let
p
be an odd integer and
c\neq 0
. Then there are
8
distinct principal slices when
n=3
and
9
distinct principal 3D slices when
n\geq 4

Proof

Assume that
p
is an odd integer and
c\neq 0
. Let
\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}}\in\mathbb{I}(n)
and choose
\mathbf{v_{1}},\mathbf{v_{2}},\mathbf{v_{3}}\in\mathbb{I}(n)
such that
\mathbf{u_{1}^{2}}=\mathbf{v_{1}^{2}}

\mathbf{u_{2}^{2}}=\mathbf{v_{2}^{2}}

\mathbf{u_{3}^{2}}=\mathbf{v_{3}^{2}}

Assume that

6.3

3a
) implies that

. Define
\varphi:\mathbb{M}(1,\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})\rightarrow\mathbb{M}(1,\mathbf{v_{2}},\mathbf{v_{3}})

\varphi(1)=1
,
\varphi(\mathbf{u_{2}})=\mathbf{v_{2}}
,
\varphi(\mathbf{u_{3}})=\mathbf{v_{3}}

7.1

because condition (1) in Definition
6

\mathcal{T}^{p}(1,\mathbf{j_{1}},\mathbf{j_{2}})
.

(b)

\mathbf{u_{2}^{2}}=-1
and
\mathbf{u_{3}^{2}}=1

(c)

2.

Assume that
\mathbf{u_{1}}\neq 1

. Hence, we get
\varphi(f_{c}(\zeta))=f_{c}(\varphi(\zeta))
. This implies that
\mathcal{T}^{p}(\mathbf{u_{1}},\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})\sim\mathcal{T}^{p}(\mathbf{v_{1}},\mathbf{v_{2}},\mathbf{v_{3}})
because condition (1) in Definition
6
is automatically verified from the fact that condition (2) is satisfied and
\mathbb{T}(1,\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})\subset\mathbb{M}(1,\mathbf{u_{2}},\mathbf{u_{3}})
and
\mathbb{T}(1,\mathbf{v_{2}},\mathbf{v_{3}})\subset\mathbb{M}(1,\mathbf{v_{2}},\mathbf{v_{3}})

and
\mathbf{u_{2}}

(b)

\mathbf{u_{1}^{2}}=-1
and
\mathbf{u_{2}^{2}}=\mathbf{u_{3}^{2}}=1
. In this case, choose
\mathbf{v_{1}}=\mathbf{i_{1}}
,
\mathbf{v_{2}}=\mathbf{j_{1}}
, and
\mathbf{v_{3}}=\mathbf{j_{2}}
, then all 3D slices are equivalent to
\mathcal{T}^{p}(\mathbf{i_{1}},\mathbf{i_{2}},\mathbf{j_{2}})
.

(c)

\mathbf{u_{1}^{2}}=\mathbf{u_{2}^{2}}=-1
and
\mathbf{u_{3}^{2}}=1
. In this case, choose
\mathbf{v_{1}}=\mathbf{i_{1}}
,
\mathbf{v_{2}}=\mathbf{i_{2}}
, and
\mathbf{v_{3}}=\mathbf{j_{2}}
, then all 3D slices are equivalent to
\mathcal{T}^{p}(\mathbf{i_{1}},\mathbf{i_{2}},\mathbf{j_{2}})
.

(d)

\mathbf{u_{1}^{2}}=\mathbf{u_{2}^{2}}=\mathbf{u_{3}^{2}}=-1
. In this case, choose
\mathbf{v_{1}}=\mathbf{i_{1}}
,
\mathbf{v_{2}}=\mathbf{i_{2}}
, and
\mathbf{v_{3}}=\mathbf{i_{3}}
, then all 3D slices are equivalent to
\mathcal{T}^{p}(\mathbf{i_{1}},\mathbf{i_{2}},\mathbf{i_{3}})
.

After compiling all the possible cases, we see that when
n=3
, there are
8
principal 3D slices and when
n\geq 4
, there are
9
principal 3D slices.

This concludes the proof. ∎

Conclusion

In summary, we have introduced the multicomplex numbers and their topology to investigate the filled-in Julia sets in higher dimensions. The multidimensional version of the filled-in Julia sets was introduced in Section
4
and an algorithm was presented to visualize them with a computer. In Section
6
, we introduced an equivalence relation as our basis for the analysis of the principal 3D slices. We then proved our main results on the number of 3D slices of the multicomplex filled-in Julia set in section
7
.

As a possible future direction of work, it would be interested to investigate in more details each equivalent classes obtained in our characterization. For instance, based on preliminary computer explorations, the principal 3D slice
\mathcal{T}^{p}(1,\mathbf{j_{1}},\mathbf{j_{2}})
seems to always be an octahedron for any values of
c\in\mathcal{M}^{p}\cap\mathbb{R}
. Is that always the case? Some further mathematical investigations are necessary to elucidate this question.

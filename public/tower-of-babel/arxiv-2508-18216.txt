. 1.
Introduction and statement of results

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{B},\mu,T)
be an ergodic, probability measure preserving dynamical system and let
\varphi:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
.
For
x\in\Omega
denote
S_{N}(\varphi)(x):=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\varphi(T^{k}x)
.
We are interested in the almost sure behavior of
\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{\varphi\circ T^{N}}{S_{N}(\varphi)}
. This term is also referred to as
extravagance
. If
\varphi\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mu)
, then it immediately follows that
\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{\varphi\circ T^{N}}{S_{N}(\varphi)}=0

almost everywhere (a.e.). However, from now on we will assume that
\varphi\notin\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mu)
. If
(\varphi\circ T^{n-1})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of independent, identically distributed random variables (i.i.d.), then we always have

\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{\varphi\circ T^{N}}{S_{N}(\varphi)}=\infty
a.e., see e.g.
[
9
]
. For continued fraction mixing random variables the same statement can be deduced from
[
3
, Thm. 4 and following remark]
.
However, in the ergodic (non continued fraction mixing) context this is not always the case, see
[
12
, Ex. a]
for an example of a Markov chain where
\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\varphi\circ T^{N}}{S_{N}(\varphi)}=0
a.e.
Indeed, it is possible to construct examples
for which
\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{\varphi\circ T^{N}}{S_{N}(\varphi)}=c
a.e. holds, where
c
can be any number between
0
and
\infty
, see
[
2
]
.

To see which dynamical systems may exhibit interesting extravagance behavior we note that

\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{(\varphi\circ T^{N})(x)}{S_{N}(\varphi)(x)}=c\in[0,\infty]

is equivalent to

\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{\max\{\varphi(x),\ldots,(\varphi\circ T^{N-1})(x)\}}{S_{N}^{(1)}(\varphi)(x)}=c,

where the (
1
-)trimmed sum
S_{N}^{(1)}(\varphi)
is defined as
S_{N}^{(1)}(\varphi)(x)=S_{N}(\varphi)(x)-\max\{\varphi(x),\ldots,(\varphi\circ T^{N-1})(x)\}
.

The almost sure behavior of the
1
-trimmed sum of a suitable observable
\varphi
usually having regularly varying tails with index
1
has been studied for a number of systems and for many of them a generalized strong law could be shown, i.e. the existence of a norming sequence
(d_{N})
such that

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{S_{N}^{(1)}(\varphi)}{d_{N}}=1
a.e., see e.g.
[
11
]
for i.i.d. random variables,
[
7
,
1
]
for continued fraction mixing dynamical systems and
[
5
]
for non-integrable observables over irrational rotations. By Aaronson’s theorem it is clear that such a generalized strong law implies
\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{\varphi\circ T^{N}}{S_{N}(\varphi)}=\infty
a.e.

In
[
5
]
it was shown that for the observable
\phi(x)=\frac{1}{x}-\frac{1}{1-x}
the set of irrational rotations
\alpha
for which
\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{S_{N}^{(1)}(\phi)}{d_{N}}=1
a.e. holds builds a full measure set.
1
1

1

Indeed,
[
5
]
uses as a roof function
\overline{\phi}:\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
with
\overline{\phi}(x)=\frac{1}{x}
; however, the proofs can be easily adjusted to the observable
\phi
.

However, it was also shown that for a set of exceptional
\alpha
trimming by only the largest or any finite number of maximal large entries is not enough to obtain a strong law of large numbers.
This makes irrational rotations with the observable
\phi
an interesting object to study non-trivial extravagance behavior.

In the following Subsection
1.1
we will state our results for the extravagance of
\phi
.
Moreover, in Subsection
1.2
we will see that our results have immediate consequences for the extreme historic behavior of reparametrized linear flows on the torus.

1.1.
Extravagance for non-integrable observables over an irrational rotation

Let
\Omega=\mathbb{T}
be the one-torus,
T(x)=R_{\alpha}(x)=x+\alpha\;\rm{mod}\,1
,
\alpha\in(0,1)\backslash\mathbb{Q}
, and
\lambda
be the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{T}
.
As
\alpha
is irrational, it has a unique continued fraction expansion

\displaystyle\alpha=[a_{1},a_{2},\ldots]=\cfrac{1}{a_{1}+\cfrac{1}{a_{2}+\cfrac{1}{\ddots}}}

with
a_{j}\in\mathbb{N}
for
j\geq 1
and can be well approximated by finite iterates of the expansion, i.e. by the rational numbers

\displaystyle\frac{p_{n}}{q_{n}}=\cfrac{1}{a_{1}+\cfrac{1}{a_{2}+\ldots+\cfrac{1}{a_{n}}}}

with the convention
p_{-1}=q_{0}=1
and
q_{-1}=0
.
It can be easily seen that
q_{n+1}=a_{n+1}q_{n}+q_{n-1}
.
The growth rate of the continued fraction exponents
q_{n}
will be crucial to decide if the extravagance for the system
R_{\alpha}
with the observable

\phi:\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0},\qquad\text{with}\qquad\phi(x)=\frac{1}{x}+\frac{1}{1-x}

equals
0
or
\infty
.

Theorem 1.1
.

We have

\displaystyle\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{\phi\circ R_{\alpha}^{N}}{S_{N}(\phi)}=\begin{cases}0\\
\infty\end{cases}\text{ a.e.}

according as

(1.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}W_{n}\begin{cases}<\infty\\
=\infty\end{cases}

with

\displaystyle W_{n}=W_{n}(\alpha)=\begin{cases}\frac{\log q_{n+1}-\log q_{n}}{\log q_{n}}&\text{if }q_{n+1}<2q_{n}\log q_{n}\\
\\
\tfrac{\max\left\{1,\log\log q_{n}-\log\log\frac{q_{n+1}}{q_{n}\log q_{n}}\right\}}{\log q_{n}}&\text{if }q_{n+1}\geq 2q_{n}\log q_{n}.\end{cases}

Remark 1.2
.

We note that
W_{n}\log q_{n}
can be arbitrarily small. For instance, if
a_{n}=1
, then

W_{n}\log q_{n}\leq\log q_{n+1}-\log q_{n}=\log(q_{n}+q_{n-1})-\log q_{n}=\log\left(1+\frac{q_{n-1}}{q_{n}}\right)

with
\frac{q_{n-1}}{q_{n}}
possibly arbitrarily small.
On the other hand, we generally have

(1.2)

\displaystyle W_{n}\ll\frac{\log\log q_{n}}{\log q_{n}}.

Loosely speaking,
W_{n}
has its maximum in the situation that
q_{n+1}
is close to
q_{n}\log q_{n}
in which case
W_{n}
equals (possibly up to a constant)
\frac{\log\log q_{n}}{\log q_{n}}
.

In the next theorem we will be concerned with the size of the set of
\alpha
with extravagance
0
. For this we denote by
\dim_{\mathrm{H}}(A)
the Hausdorff dimension of a set
A
.

Theorem 1.3
.

We have that

(1.3)

\displaystyle\lambda\Big(\alpha:\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}W_{n}(\alpha)<\infty\Big)=0

and

(1.4)

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\left(\alpha:\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}W_{n}(\alpha)<\infty\right)=\frac{1}{2}.

It turns out that the order of the singularity is crucial for having an extravagance of
0
as the following theorem shows.
In case we look at the observable
\widetilde{\phi}_{\gamma}(x)=x^{-\gamma}+(1-x)^{-\gamma}
, for
\gamma>1
, Theorem
1.1
simplifies to the following statement:

Theorem 1.4
.

For every
\alpha\in[0,1]\backslash\mathbb{Q}
, we have

\displaystyle\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{\widetilde{\phi}_{\gamma}\circ R_{\alpha}^{N}}{S_{N}(\widetilde{\phi}_{\gamma})}=\infty\text{ a.e.}

1.2.
Historic behavior for reparametrizations of linear flows on the torus.

Our main result or rather a special case of it has a consequence on (extreme) historic behavior for reparametrizations of linear flows on
\mathbb{T}^{2}
, strenghtening the results from
[
4
]
which we now recall. Let
(L_{t})
be a linear flow on
\mathbb{T}^{2}
generated by the constant vector field
X_{0}=(1,\alpha)
. One then considers a
reparametrization
of the flow
(L_{t})
given by a smooth function
\varphi:\mathbb{T}^{2}\to R_{\geq 0}
. More precisely let
\varphi
be a
C^{3}
non-negative function on
\mathbb{T}^{2}
which vanishes at exactly two points
{\bf p}
and
{\bf q}
. Moreover as in
[
4
]
we consider the
non-degenerate case
, i.e. we assume that the Hessians
D^{2}\varphi({\bf p})
,
D^{2}\varphi({\bf q})
are positive definite matrices. We then consider the flow
(T_{t})
which is generated by the vector field
X=X_{\varphi}=\varphi X_{0}
. Then it follows that
(T_{t})
has exactly two stopping points at
{\bf p}
and
{\bf q}
. It follows moreover (see e.g.
[
4
]
) that the only invariant probability measures for
(T_{t})
are convex combinations of
\delta_{{\bf p}}
and
\delta_{{\bf q}}
. We recall the following definition:

Definition 1.5
.

For
{\bf x}\in\mathbb{T}^{2}
consider the following
empirical measures
:

\mu_{t}({\bf x})(f)=\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}f(T_{s}{\bf x})\,\mathrm{d}s.

We say that the flow
(T_{t})
has:

(i)

physical measure
if for a.e.
{\bf x}\in\mathbb{T}^{2}
,
\lim_{t\to\infty}\mu_{t}({\bf x})=\mu_{\infty}
, for some
\mu_{\infty}
being a convex combination of
\delta_{{\bf p}}
and
\delta_{{\bf q}}
;

(ii)

historic behavior
if for a.e.
{\bf x}\in\mathbb{T}^{2}
,
\lim_{t\to\infty}\mu_{t}({\bf x})
does not exist;

(iii)

extreme historic behavior
if for a.e.
{\bf x}\in\mathbb{T}^{2}
, the accumulation points of the set
\{\mu_{t}({\bf x})\}_{t>0}
are exactly the set of
(T_{t})
-invariant probability measures.

The main result in
[
4
]
is as follows:

Theorem 1.6

(
[
4
, Theorem A]
)
.

There are subsets
\mathcal{F},\mathcal{R},\mathcal{D}\subset\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{T}^{2}\times\mathbb{T}^{2}
with the following characteristics:
\mathcal{F}
is of full Lebesgue measure,
\mathcal{R}
is a dense
G_{\delta}
set, and
\mathcal{D}
is dense (but not
G_{\delta}
), such that for any
(\alpha,{\bf p},{\bf q})\in\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{T}^{2}\times\mathbb{T}^{2}
and the corresponding reparametrized linear flow
(T_{t})
the following holds:

•

(T_{t})
has a unique physical measure if
(\alpha,{\bf p},{\bf q})\in\mathcal{D}
;

•

(T_{t})
has historic behavior if
(\alpha,{\bf p},{\bf q})\in\mathcal{F}
;

•

(T_{t})
has an extreme historic behavior if
(\alpha,{\bf p},{\bf q})\in\mathcal{R}
.

In fact the authors in
[
4
]
conjecture that it should be possible to show that there is a full measure set
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{T}^{2}\times\mathbb{T}^{2}
such that for any
(\alpha,{\bf p},{\bf q})\in\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
the flow has extreme historic behavior.
This is what we confirm:

Theorem 1.7
.

There exists a full measure set
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{T}^{2}\times\mathbb{T}^{2}
such that for every
(\alpha,{\bf p},{\bf q})\in\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
the corresponding flow
(T_{t})
has extreme historic behavior.

As shown in Section 2 and Section 3 (in particular Proposition 3.4.) in
[
4
]
,
we have the following criterion for extreme historic behavior:

Proposition 1.8
.

Let
\Theta_{N}^{\beta}(x)=\frac{S_{N}(\phi)(x)}{S_{N}(\phi)(x-\beta)}
where
\phi(x)=\frac{1}{x}+\frac{1}{1-x}
. If

(1.5)

\limsup_{N\to\infty}\Theta_{N}^{\beta}(x)=\infty\;\;\;\text{ and }\;\;\;\liminf_{N\to\infty}\Theta_{N}^{\beta}(x)=0\quad\text{ for a.e. }x\in\mathbb{T},

then the flow
(T_{t})
given by the parameters
(\alpha,{\bf p},{\bf p}+(\beta,s))
,
s\in[0,1)
, has extreme historic behavior.

Using the above proposition we will show that the set of
(\alpha,\beta)
for which the corresponding flow has extreme historic behavior is of full measure:

Proposition 1.9
.

There exists a full measure set
Z\subset\mathbb{T}^{2}
such that for every
(\alpha,\beta)\in Z
we have that (
1.5
) holds.

Before starting with the proofs of the theorems, we first introduce some additional notation. Denote

\displaystyle w_{n}=|\alpha q_{n}|\cdot q_{n+1}

which implies
w_{n}\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
. Moreover, a number of times we will use the Denjoy-Koksma inequality which states the following: for

\displaystyle\left|S_{q_{n}}(f)(x)-q_{n}\int f\mathrm{d}\lambda\right|\leq\Var(f),

where
\Var(f)
denotes the total variation of
f
.

Finally, we introduce the following two conventions:

•

u_{n}\asymp v_{n}
: There exists
C>1
such that
C^{-1}u_{n}\leq v_{n}\leq Cu_{n}
, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

•

u_{n}\lesssim v_{n}
: We have
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{u_{n}}{v_{n}}<\infty
.

The following lemma can be deduced from the Denjoy-Koksma inequality:

Lemma 2.1

For all

\displaystyle\left|S_{q_{n}}(x)-2q_{n}\log q_{n}-\phi(x_{\min,q_{n}})\right|\leq 2q_{n}.

This lemma allows us to give the following precise estimates of the ergodic sums.

Lemma 2.2

Let

and

being such that

Then we have

\displaystyle 2jq_{n}\log q_{n}-2jq_{n}\leq S_{k}(x)-\phi(x_{\min,k})\leq 2(j+1)q_{n}\log q_{n}+2(j+1)q_{n}+4q_{n+1}(2+\log j).

If we additionally assume that

(2.3)

\displaystyle x_{\min,k}\in\left(-\frac{1}{4q_{n+1}},\frac{1}{4q_{n+1}}\right),

then

(2.4)

\displaystyle S_{k}(x)-\phi(x_{\min,k})\geq 2jq_{n}\log q_{n}-2jq_{n}+\frac{4}{5}q_{n+1}\max\left\{0,\log(j-2)\right\}.

Proof.

First, we denote

x_{\min,A}=\min\{d(0,R_{\alpha}^{\ell-1}(x)),{\ell}\in A\cap\mathbb{N}\}
.

In order to estimate the first inequality, we notice that by Lemma
2.1
we have

(2.5)

\displaystyle S_{k}(x)\geq S_{jq_{n}}(x)\geq 2jq_{n}\log q_{n}-2jq_{n}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{j}\phi(x_{\min,((\ell-1)q_{n},\ell q_{n}]})

and the first inequality follows immediately.

In order to prove the upper bound,
similarly as above, we obtain by Lemma
2.1
that

\displaystyle S_{k}(x)\leq S_{(j+1)q_{n}}(x)\leq 2(j+1)q_{n}\log q_{n}+2(j+1)q_{n}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{j+1}\phi(x_{\min,((\ell-1)q_{n},\ell q_{n}]}).

First, we note that
\phi(x_{\min,k})\leq\phi(0)=\infty
.
Remembering that

,
we have that in the interval

. Moreover, as the

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{j+1}\phi(x_{\min,((\ell-1)q_{n},\ell q_{n}]})-\phi(x_{\min,k})

\displaystyle\leq\phi\Big(\frac{1}{4q_{n+1}}\Big)+2\sum_{\ell=1}^{\lceil(j-1)/2\rceil}\phi\Big(\frac{\ell}{2q_{n+1}}\Big)\leq 4q_{n+1}(2+\log j),

finishing the proof of the upper bound.

Finally, we prove (

This together with (

\bigcup_{i=0}^{q_{n}-1}\left[R_{\alpha}^{-i}(0)-\frac{1}{\epsilon q_{n}\log q_{n}}\,,\,R_{\alpha}^{-i-J_{n,\epsilon}}(0)\right]&\text{if }\alpha-\frac{p_{n}}{q_{n}}<0.\end{cases}

Furthermore, for
j\geq J_{n,\epsilon}
, we define

\displaystyle v_{n,j}=\epsilon jq_{n}\log q_{n}+\frac{\epsilon}{2}q_{n+1}\max\{0,\log(j-2)\}

and

\displaystyle\overline{A}_{n,j}^{\prime}=\bigcup_{i=0}^{q_{n}-1}\left[R_{\alpha}^{-i-jq_{n}}(0)-\frac{1}{v_{n,j}}\,,\,R_{\alpha}^{-i-jq_{n}}(0)+\frac{1}{v_{n,j}}\right]

and finally

(2.8)

\displaystyle\overline{B}_{n}=\overline{A}_{n,1}^{\prime}\cup\bigcup_{j=J_{n,\epsilon}}^{\lfloor q_{n+1}/q_{n}\rfloor}\overline{A}_{n,j}^{\prime}.

First, for
n\in\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}^{c}\cap\mathbb{N}_{\geq U_{\epsilon}}
we will show that

\bigcup_{j=1}^{J_{n,\epsilon}-1}C_{n,j}\subset\overline{A}_{n,1}^{\prime}
.

Similarly as in the argumentation above, for
k\in[q_{n},J_{n,\epsilon}q_{n})
, the occurrence of the event
\phi\circ R_{\alpha}^{k}(x)>\epsilon S_{k}(x)
implies

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle\in\bigcup_{j=1}^{J_{n,\epsilon}-1}\bigcup_{i=0}^{q_{n}-1}\left[R_{\alpha}^{-i-jq_{n}}(0)-\frac{1}{\epsilon jq_{n}\log q_{n}}\,,\,R_{\alpha}^{-i-jq_{n}}(0)+\frac{1}{\epsilon jq_{n}\log q_{n}}\right]=:\bigcup_{j=1}^{J_{n,\epsilon}-1}\bigcup_{i=0}^{q_{n}-1}\Gamma_{n,i,j},

for
n
sufficiently large.
For the moment, assume
\alpha-\frac{p_{n}}{q_{n}}>0
. The other case follows analogously with a symmetry at
0
.
We note that
\Gamma_{n,i,j}
can also be written as

\displaystyle\Gamma_{n,i,j}

\displaystyle=\left[R_{\alpha}^{-i}(0)-\frac{w_{n}j}{q_{n+1}}-\frac{1}{\epsilon jq_{n}\log q_{n}}\,,\,R_{\alpha}^{-i}(0)-\frac{w_{n}j}{q_{n+1}}+\frac{1}{\epsilon jq_{n}\log q_{n}}\right].

Moreover, we note that

\displaystyle\frac{w_{n}j}{q_{n+1}}+\frac{1}{\epsilon jq_{n}\log q_{n}}\leq\frac{w_{n}J_{n,\epsilon}}{q_{n+1}}\quad\text{ iff }\quad\frac{q_{n+1}}{\epsilon w_{n}q_{n}\log q_{n}}\leq j(J_{n,\epsilon}-j).

As the polynomial on the right hand side has its minimum at the endpoints, the inequality is true for all
j\in\{1,\ldots,J_{n,\epsilon}-1\}
if

\displaystyle\frac{q_{n+1}}{\epsilon w_{n}q_{n}\log q_{n}}\leq J_{n,\epsilon}-1

which is true by the definition of
J_{n,\epsilon}
and the fact that
w_{n}\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
.
Hence,

\displaystyle\bigcup_{j=1}^{J_{n,\epsilon}-1}\Gamma_{n,i,j}\subset\left[R_{\alpha}^{-i}(0)-\frac{w_{n}J_{n,\epsilon}}{q_{n+1}}\,,\,R_{\alpha}^{-i}(0)+\frac{1}{\epsilon q_{n}\log q_{n}}\right]

implying

\bigcup_{j=1}^{J_{n,\epsilon}-1}\bigcup_{i=0}^{q_{n}-1}\Gamma_{n,i,j}\subset\overline{A}_{n,1}^{\prime}
and thus

(2.9)

\bigcup_{j=1}^{J_{n,\epsilon}-1}C_{n,j}\subset\overline{A}_{n,1}^{\prime}.

For
n\in\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}^{c}\cap\mathbb{N}_{\geq U_{\epsilon}}
and
j\geq J_{n,\epsilon}
, we need the more precise estimate of
\overline{A}_{n,k}^{\prime}
instead of
\overline{A}_{n,k}
as in the case
n\in\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}
.
Noting that by Lemma
2.2

we have for
k\in[jq_{n},(j+1)q_{n})
and
n
sufficiently large, the statement
\phi\circ R_{\alpha}^{k}(x)>\epsilon S_{k}(x)
implies

\phi\circ R_{\alpha}^{k}(x)>\epsilon jq_{n}\log q_{n}
. In case
j\leq J_{n,\epsilon}
, this implies
x\in\overline{A}_{n,1}^{\prime}
. On the other hand, if
j>J_{n,\epsilon}
, then
\epsilon S_{k}(x)>\epsilon J_{n,\epsilon}q_{n}\log q_{n}>5q_{n+1}
. Then
\phi\circ R_{\alpha}^{k}(x)>\epsilon S_{k}(x)
implies that (
2.3
) is fulfilled and we can apply the stronger upper bound (
2.4
).
In particular, we have

\phi\circ R_{\alpha}^{k}(x)>\epsilon jq_{n}\log q_{n}+\frac{\epsilon}{2}q_{n+1}\max\{\log(j-2),0\}
for
n
sufficiently large.
This implies

R_{\alpha}^{k}(x)\in[-\frac{1}{v_{n,j}},\frac{1}{v_{n,j}}]
which is equivalent to

x\in\overline{A}_{n,j}^{\prime}
.
Thus, we obtain

(2.10)

C_{n,j}\subset\overline{A}_{n,j}^{\prime}\quad\text{for }j\geq J_{n,\epsilon}

and hence

\bigcup_{j=1}^{\lfloor q_{n+1}/q_{n}\rfloor}C_{n,j}\subset\overline{B}_{n}
.

In the next steps we will estimate
\lambda(\overline{B}_{n})
.
First assume
n\in\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}
and
a_{n+1}\neq 1
. In particular, this implies that
\lfloor\frac{q_{n+1}}{q_{n}}\rfloor\geq 2
. In this case we have

(2.11)

\displaystyle\lambda(\overline{B}_{n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{\lfloor q_{n+1}/q_{n}\rfloor}\lambda(\overline{A}_{n,j})\leq\sum_{j=1}^{\lfloor q_{n+1}/q_{n}\rfloor}\frac{2}{\epsilon j\log q_{n}}\ll\frac{\log q_{n+1}-\log q_{n}}{\log q_{n}}.

If
a_{n+1}=1
(and thus automatically
n\in\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}
), we have
q_{n+1}\leq 2q_{n}
. Thus, we can estimate

(2.12)

\displaystyle\lambda(\overline{B}_{n})=\sum_{j=1}^{\lfloor q_{n+1}/q_{n}\rfloor}\lambda(\overline{A}_{n,j})=\lambda(\overline{A}_{n,1})=\frac{q_{n+1}-q_{n}}{\epsilon q_{n}\log q_{n}}\ll\frac{q_{n+1}-q_{n}}{q_{n}}\frac{1}{\log q_{n}}\leq\frac{\log q_{n+1}-\log q_{n}}{\log q_{n}}.

Next consider
n\in\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}^{c}
.
There exists
U_{\epsilon}^{\prime}\geq U_{\epsilon}
such that for
n\in\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}^{c}\cap\mathbb{N}_{\geq U_{\epsilon}^{\prime}}
we have

(2.13)

\displaystyle\lambda(\overline{B}_{n})

\displaystyle=\lambda(\overline{A}_{n,1}^{\prime})+\sum_{j=J_{n,\epsilon}}^{\lfloor q_{n+1}/q_{n}\rfloor}\lambda(\overline{A}_{n,j}^{\prime})\leq\frac{10}{\epsilon\log q_{n}}+\sum_{j=J_{n,\epsilon}}^{\lfloor q_{n+1}/q_{n}\rfloor}\frac{2q_{n}}{v_{n,j}}.

First, set
L_{\epsilon}=2\left(\lceil\frac{2}{\epsilon}\rceil+1\right)

and consider the case
n\in(\mathcal{N}_{L_{\epsilon}}\backslash\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon})\cap\mathbb{N}_{\geq U_{\epsilon}^{\prime}}
.
Then we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j=J_{n,\epsilon}}^{\lfloor q_{n+1}/q_{n}\rfloor}\frac{2q_{n}}{v_{n,j}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=J_{n,\epsilon}}^{\lfloor L_{\epsilon}\log q_{n}\rfloor}\frac{2}{\epsilon j\log q_{n}+\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{2}\log q_{n}\max\{0,\log(j-2)\}}

(2.14)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=J_{n,\epsilon}}^{\lfloor L_{\epsilon}\log q_{n}\rfloor}\frac{2}{\epsilon j\log q_{n}}\ll\frac{\log(\lfloor L_{\epsilon}\log q_{n}\rfloor)}{\log q_{n}}\ll\frac{\log\log q_{n}}{\log q_{n}}.

Next, we consider the case
n\in\mathcal{N}_{L_{\epsilon}}^{c}\cap\mathbb{N}_{\geq U_{\epsilon}^{\prime}}
.
Denoting

\displaystyle K_{n}=\frac{q_{n+1}}{q_{n}\log q_{n}}\log\left(\frac{q_{n+1}}{q_{n}\log q_{n}}\right),

the last sum in (
2.13
) can be estimated as follows:

\displaystyle\sum_{j=J_{n,\epsilon}}^{\lfloor q_{n+1}/q_{n}\rfloor}\frac{2q_{n}}{v_{n,j}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=J_{n,\epsilon}}^{\lfloor q_{n+1}/q_{n}\rfloor}\frac{2}{\epsilon j\log q_{n}+\frac{\epsilon q_{n+1}}{2q_{n}}\max\{0,\log(j-2)\}}

(2.15)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{\epsilon J_{n,\epsilon}\log q_{n}}+\sum_{j=J_{n,\epsilon}+1}^{\min\{K_{n},\lfloor q_{n+1}/q_{n}\rfloor\}}\frac{4q_{n}}{\epsilon q_{n+1}\max\{0,\log(j-2)\}}+\sum_{j=K_{n}+1}^{\lfloor q_{n+1}/q_{n}\rfloor}\frac{2}{\epsilon j\log q_{n}}

with the convention that
\sum_{j=a}^{b}
is the empty sum if
b<a
.
Note that by assuming that
n\in\mathcal{N}_{L_{\epsilon}}^{c}
(and assuming that
\epsilon
is sufficiently small), we have that
K_{n}\geq 2J_{n,\epsilon}
and the first sum can not be empty.
Moreover, since
n\in\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}^{c}
, we have
J_{n,\epsilon}\geq 6
and thus

(2.16)

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\epsilon J_{n,\epsilon}\log q_{n}}\ll\frac{1}{\log q_{n}}.

In particular, it also implies that
J_{n,\epsilon}-2>1
.
Hence, for
n
sufficiently large, say for
n\geq U_{\epsilon}^{\prime\prime}
the first of the above sums can be estimated as

\displaystyle\sum_{j=J_{n,\epsilon}+1}^{\min\{K_{n},\lfloor q_{n+1}/q_{n}\rfloor\}}\frac{4q_{n}}{\epsilon q_{n+1}\max\{0,\log(j-2)\}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4q_{n}}{\epsilon q_{n+1}}{\int_{J_{n,\epsilon}-2}^{K_{n}}\left(\frac{1}{\log x}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{8q_{n}}{\epsilon q_{n+1}}\left(\frac{K_{n}}{\log K_{n}}-\frac{J_{n,\epsilon}}{2\log J_{n,\epsilon}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{8q_{n}}{\epsilon q_{n+1}}\left(\frac{q_{n+1}\log\left(\frac{q_{n+1}}{q_{n}\log q_{n}}\right)}{q_{n}\log q_{n}\log\left(\frac{q_{n+1}\log\left(\frac{q_{n+1}}{q_{n}\log q_{n}}\right)}{q_{n}\log q_{n}}\right)}-\frac{q_{n+1}}{q_{n}\log q_{n}\log\left(\frac{q_{n+1}}{q_{n}\log q_{n}}\right)}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{8}{\epsilon\log q_{n}}\left(\frac{\log\left(\frac{q_{n+1}}{q_{n}\log q_{n}}\right)}{\log\left(\frac{q_{n+1}\log\left(\frac{q_{n+1}}{q_{n}\log q_{n}}\right)}{q_{n}\log q_{n}}\right)}-\frac{1}{\log\left(\frac{q_{n+1}}{q_{n}\log q_{n}}\right)}\right).

Noticing that

\displaystyle\frac{\log x}{\log\left(x\log x\right)}-\frac{1}{\log x}>0\,\,\text{ for }x>0\quad\text{ and }\quad\frac{\log x}{\log\left(x\log x\right)}-\frac{1}{\log x}\sim\frac{1}{\log x}\,\,\text{ for }x\to\infty,

we can conclude

(2.17)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=J_{n,\epsilon}+1}^{\min\{K_{n},\lfloor q_{n+1}/q_{n}\rfloor\}}\frac{2q_{n}}{\epsilon q_{n+1}\max\{0,\log(j-2)\}}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{1}{\log q_{n}}.

Next, we estimate the second sum in (
2.15
).
If
\frac{q_{n+1}}{q_{n}}\leq 2K_{n}
, we have

(2.18)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=K_{n}+1}^{\lfloor q_{n+1}/q_{n}\rfloor}\frac{2}{\epsilon j\log q_{n}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{q_{n+1}}{q_{n}}-K_{n}\right)\frac{2}{\epsilon K_{n}\log q_{n}}\leq\frac{2K_{n}}{\epsilon K_{n}\log q_{n}}\ll\frac{1}{\log q_{n}}.

So, for the following, let us assume
\frac{q_{n+1}}{q_{n}}\geq 2K_{n}
. In this case we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j=K_{n}+1}^{\lfloor q_{n+1}/q_{n}\rfloor}\frac{1}{\epsilon j\log q_{n}}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{1}{\log q_{n}}\left(\log\left(\frac{q_{n+1}}{q_{n}}\right)-\log\left(\frac{q_{n+1}}{q_{n}\log q_{n}}\log\left(\frac{q_{n+1}}{q_{n}\log q_{n}}\right)\right)\right)

(2.19)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\log\log q_{n}-\log\log\left(\frac{q_{n+1}}{q_{n}\log q_{n}}\right)}{\log q_{n}}.

Combining (
2.13
), (
2.15
), (
2.16
), (
2.17
), (
2.18
), and (
2.19
),
we have for
n\in\mathcal{N}_{L_{\epsilon}}^{c}
that

(2.20)

\displaystyle\lambda\left(\overline{B}_{n}\right)

\displaystyle\ll\frac{\max\left\{1\,,\,\log\log q_{n}-\log\log\left(\frac{q_{n+1}}{q_{n}\log q_{n}}\right)\right\}}{\log q_{n}}.

Hence, combining (
2.11
), (
2.12
), (
2.14
), and (
2.20
), there exists
U_{\epsilon}^{\prime\prime}
such that

\displaystyle\sum_{n=U_{\epsilon}^{\prime\prime}}^{\infty}\lambda\left(\overline{B}_{n}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n\in\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}\cap\mathbb{N}_{U_{\epsilon}^{\prime\prime}}}\frac{\log q_{n+1}-\log q_{n}}{\log q_{n}}+\sum_{n\in(\mathcal{N}_{L_{\epsilon}}\backslash\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon})\cap\mathbb{N}_{U_{\epsilon}^{\prime\prime}}}\frac{\log\log q_{n}}{\log q_{n}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{n\in\mathcal{N}_{L_{\epsilon}}^{c}\cap\mathbb{N}_{U_{\epsilon}^{\prime\prime}}}\frac{\max\left\{1\,,\,\log\log q_{n}-\log\log\left(\frac{q_{n+1}}{q_{n}\log q_{n}}\right)\right\}}{\log q_{n}}.

Moreover, for
n\in\mathcal{N}_{2}\backslash\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}
we have

\displaystyle\log\log q_{n}

\displaystyle=\log q_{n}+\log\log q_{n}-\log q_{n}\leq\log q_{n+1}-\log q_{n}+\log\epsilon\ll\log q_{n+1}-\log q_{n}.

On the other hand, for
n\in\mathcal{N}_{L_{\epsilon}}\backslash\mathcal{N}_{2}
we have

\displaystyle\log\log q_{n}

\displaystyle\ll\log\log q_{n}-\log\log L_{\epsilon}\leq\log\log q_{n}-\log\log\left(\frac{q_{n+1}}{q_{n}\log q_{n}}\right).

Thus, there exists
U_{\epsilon}^{(iv)}
such that setting
\mathbb{N}^{(iv)}=\mathbb{N}_{\geq U_{\epsilon}^{(iv)}}
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=U_{\epsilon}^{(iv)}}^{\infty}\lambda\left(\overline{B}_{n}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n\in\mathcal{N}_{2}\cap\mathbb{N}^{(iv)}}\frac{\log q_{n+1}-\log q_{n}}{\log q_{n}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{n\in\mathcal{N}_{2}^{c}\cap\mathbb{N}^{(iv)}}\frac{\max\left\{1\,,\,\log\log q_{n}-\log\log\left(\frac{q_{n+1}}{q_{n}\log q_{n}}\right)\right\}}{\log q_{n}}.

Since we are assuming that (
1.1
) converges, we obtain by the first Borel-Cantelli lemma that

\displaystyle\lambda\left(\bigcup_{j=1}^{\lfloor q_{n+1}/q_{n}\rfloor}C_{n,j}\text{ for infinitely many }n\in\mathbb{N}\right)=0.

By (
2.6
) and (
2.7
) in case
n\in\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}
and by (
2.8
), (
2.9
) and (
2.10
) in case
n\in\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}^{c}

this implies statement (
1
).

In the next steps, we will prove statement (
2
).
The idea is to construct a subset of those points for which (
2.2
) holds infinitely often and show then that under the condition that (
1.1
) is infinite this set has full measure.

First, we define

(2.21)

\displaystyle\underline{v}_{n,j}=3c(j+1)q_{n}\log q_{n}+4cq_{n+1}(2+\log j)

and

\displaystyle\underline{A}_{n,j}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{i=0}^{q_{n}-1}\left[R_{\alpha}^{-i-jq_{n}}(0)-\frac{1}{\underline{v}_{n,j}}\,,\,R_{\alpha}^{-i-jq_{n}}(0)+\frac{1}{\underline{v}_{n,j}}\right]\quad\text{for }j\leq\Big\lfloor\frac{q_{n+1}}{q_{n}}\Big\rfloor-1,

\displaystyle\underline{A}_{n,\lfloor q_{n+1}/q_{n}\rfloor}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{i=\lfloor q_{n+1}/q_{n}\rfloor q_{n}}^{q_{n+1}-1}\left[R_{\alpha}^{-i}(0)-\frac{1}{\underline{v}_{n,\lfloor q_{n+1}/q_{n}\rfloor}}\,,\,R_{\alpha}^{-i}(0)+\frac{1}{\underline{v}_{n,\lfloor q_{n+1}/q_{n}\rfloor}}\right].

Furthermore, we define

\displaystyle\underline{B}_{n}=\bigcup_{j=1}^{\lfloor q_{n+1}/q_{n}\rfloor}\underline{A}_{n,j}.

The sets
\{\underline{B}_{n}\}
are crucial in what follows. Their properties will be summarized in Lemma
2.4
and Lemma
2.5
and used below.

In the next steps, we prove that for each
j\in\{0,\ldots,\lfloor\frac{q_{n+1}}{q_{n}}\rfloor\}
we have

(2.22)

\displaystyle\underline{C}_{n,j}:=\left\{x:\exists k\in[jq_{n},\min\{(j+1)q_{n},q_{n+1}\}):\phi\circ R_{\alpha}^{k}(x)>cS_{k}(x)\right\}\supset\underline{A}_{n,j}.

First note that the statement
x\in\underline{A}_{n,j}
is equivalent to the statement that there is a
k\in[jq_{n},\min\{(j+1)q_{n},q_{n+1}\})
such that

R_{\alpha}^{k}(x)\in[-\frac{1}{\underline{v}_{n,j}},\frac{1}{\underline{v}_{n,j}}]
. This implies that there exists
k\in[jq_{n},\min\{(j+1)q_{n},q_{n+1}\})
such that

\phi\circ R_{\alpha}^{k}(x)>\underline{v}_{n,j}
.
However, for such
k
, we can conclude by Lemma
2.2

that
S_{k+1}^{(1)}(x)\leq\frac{\underline{v}_{n,j}}{c}
,
for
n
sufficiently large.
Hence, in order to show (
2.22
) it only remains to show that in this case
S_{k}(x)\leq S_{k+1}^{(1)}(x)
which is equivalent to saying that
\phi\circ R_{\alpha}^{k}(x)=\max\{\phi(x),\ldots,\phi\circ R_{\alpha}^{k}(x)\}
. However, this has to be the case as
R_{\alpha}^{k}(x)\in[-\frac{1}{\underline{v}_{n,j}},\frac{1}{\underline{v}_{n,j}}]
and under the assumption
c>1
we have that
\frac{2}{\underline{v}_{n,j}}\leq\frac{w_{n}}{q_{n+1}}
implying that
R_{\alpha}^{k}(x)
has to be the point closest to
0
in the orbit
x,R_{\alpha}(x),\ldots,R_{\alpha}^{k}(x)
. Hence,
\phi\circ R_{\alpha}^{k}(x)
indeed has to be the maximal term.

In the following steps we will estimate
\lambda(\underline{B}_{n})
. Let us first assume
a_{n+1}>1
in which case we have
\lfloor\frac{q_{n+1}}{q_{n}}\rfloor>1
.
In this case we have

(2.23)

\displaystyle\lambda(\underline{B}_{n})

\displaystyle=\lambda\left(\bigcup_{j=1}^{\lfloor q_{n+1}/q_{n}\rfloor}\underline{A}_{n,j}\right)=\sum_{j=1}^{\lfloor q_{n+1}/q_{n}\rfloor}\lambda\left(\underline{A}_{n,j}\right)\geq\sum_{j=1}^{\lfloor q_{n+1}/q_{n}\rfloor-1}\lambda\left(\underline{A}_{n,j}\right)=\sum_{j=1}^{\lfloor q_{n+1}/q_{n}\rfloor-1}\frac{2q_{n}}{\underline{v}_{n,j}}.

Here, the last two equalities follow by the fact that the unions are disjoint. This can be seen as follows:
For fixed
j
, we have

\min_{0\leq i_{1}<i_{2}\leq q_{n}-1}d(R_{\alpha}^{-i_{1}-jq_{n}}(0)\,,\,R_{\alpha}^{-i_{2}-jq_{n}}(0))\leq\frac{1}{q_{n}}.

As
\frac{2}{\underline{v}_{n,j}}<\frac{1}{q_{n}}
, we have that the union defining
\underline{A}_{n,j}
is indeed disjoint and we obtain the last equality.
Moreover, we have

\min_{q_{n}\leq i_{1}+j_{1}q_{n}<i_{2}+j_{2}q_{n}\leq q_{n+1}}d(R_{\alpha}^{-i_{1}-j_{1}q_{n}}(0)\,,\,R_{\alpha}^{-i_{2}-j_{2}q_{n}}(0))\leq\frac{1}{q_{n+1}}.

If we assume
c>1
, then
\bigcup_{j=1}^{\lfloor q_{n+1}/q_{n}\rfloor}\underline{A}_{n,j}
is a disjoint union and we obtain the second equality in (
2.23
).

First assume
n\in\mathcal{N}_{2}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\underline{v}_{n,j}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{3c(j+1)\log q_{n}+4c\frac{q_{n+1}}{q_{n}}(2+\log j)}

(2.24)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{8c\left((j+1)\log q_{n}+\log q_{n}(2+\log j)\right)}\geq\frac{1}{16c(j+1)\log q_{n}}.

By (
2.23
) we get for
n
sufficiently large

(2.25)

\displaystyle\lambda(\underline{B}_{n})\geq\sum_{j=1}^{\lfloor q_{n+1}/q_{n}\rfloor-1}\frac{1}{16c(j+1)\log q_{n}}\gg\frac{\log q_{n+1}-\log q_{n}}{\log q_{n}}.

Next, we assume
a_{n+1}=1
. In this case we have
\lfloor\frac{q_{n+1}}{q_{n}}\rfloor=1
and thus

(2.26)

\displaystyle\lambda(\underline{B}_{n})

\displaystyle=\lambda\left(\underline{A}_{n,1}\right)=\frac{2(q_{n+1}-q_{n})}{\underline{v}_{n,j}}\gg\frac{\log q_{n+1}-\log q_{n}}{\log q_{n}}.

This follows on the one hand by the fact that the union defining
\underline{A}_{n,1}
is disjoint and on the other hand by the estimate in (
2.24
).

Next, assume
n\in\mathcal{N}_{2}^{c}
.
Then we have

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\underline{v}_{n,j}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\max\{3c(j+1)\log q_{n}\,,\,4c\frac{q_{n+1}}{q_{n}}(2+\log j)\}}.

Note that
3c(j+1)\log q_{n}\geq 4c\frac{q_{n+1}}{q_{n}}(2+\log j)
iff

\displaystyle\frac{j+1}{2+\log j}\geq\frac{4q_{n+1}}{3q_{n}\log q_{n}}.

Since
n\in\mathcal{N}_{2}^{c}
, we know that the RHS is larger or equal to
\frac{8}{3}
. Since for
j\geq\frac{8}{3}
we have

\displaystyle\frac{j+1}{20}\leq\frac{j+1}{2+\log j}\log\left(\frac{j+1}{2+\log j}\right)\leq 20(j+1),

we have

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\underline{v}_{n,j}}

\displaystyle\geq\begin{cases}\frac{q_{n}}{4cq_{n+1}(2+\log j)}&\text{if }j\leq\frac{q_{n+1}}{15q_{n}\log q_{n}}\log\left(\frac{4q_{n+1}}{3q_{n}\log q_{n}}\right)\\
\\
\frac{1}{3c(j+1)\log q_{n}}&\text{if }j\geq\frac{80q_{n+1}}{3q_{n}\log q_{n}}\log\left(\frac{4q_{n+1}}{3q_{n}\log q_{n}}\right).\end{cases}

Setting

\displaystyle\underline{K}_{n}

\displaystyle=\left\lfloor\frac{q_{n+1}}{15q_{n}\log q_{n}}\log\left(\frac{4q_{n+1}}{3q_{n}\log q_{n}}\right)\right\rfloor\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\underline{K}_{n}^{\prime}:=\left\lceil\frac{80q_{n+1}}{3q_{n}\log q_{n}}\log\left(\frac{4q_{n+1}}{3q_{n}\log q_{n}}\right)\right\rceil

we have

(2.27)

\displaystyle\lambda(\underline{B}_{n})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{q_{n}}{4cq_{n+1}}\sum_{j=1}^{\min\{\underline{K}_{n},\lfloor q_{n+1}/q_{n}\rfloor\}}\frac{1}{2+\log j}+\frac{1}{\log q_{n}}\sum_{j=\underline{K}_{n}^{\prime}}^{\lfloor q_{n+1}/q_{n}\rfloor}\frac{1}{3c(j+1)}.

For the moment let’s assume that
\underline{K}_{n}\geq 100
, otherwise we simply estimate the first sum by
0
.
Similarly as above, for
n
sufficiently large, the first of the above sums can be estimated as

\displaystyle\frac{q_{n}}{4cq_{n+1}}\sum_{j=1}^{\min\{\underline{K}_{n},\lfloor q_{n+1}/q_{n}\rfloor\}}\frac{1}{2+\log j}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{q_{n}}{4cq_{n+1}}\int_{\mathrm{e}}^{\min\{\underline{K}_{n},\lfloor q_{n+1}/q_{n}\rfloor\}}\left(\frac{1}{2+\log x}-\frac{1}{2+\log^{2}x}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\geq\frac{q_{n}}{4cq_{n+1}}\left(\frac{\min\{\underline{K}_{n},\lfloor\frac{q_{n+1}}{q_{n}}\rfloor\}}{2+\log\left(\min\{\underline{K}_{n},\lfloor\frac{q_{n+1}}{q_{n}}\rfloor\}\right)}-\frac{\mathrm{e}}{3}\right).

Let us first assume
\min\{\underline{K}_{n},\lfloor\frac{q_{n+1}}{q_{n}}\rfloor\}=\underline{K}_{n}
. In this case, setting
h_{n}=\frac{q_{n+1}}{q_{n}\log q_{n}}
and using
n\in\mathcal{N}_{2}^{c}
we have

\displaystyle\frac{q_{n}}{4cq_{n+1}}\sum_{j=1}^{\min\{\underline{K}_{n},\lfloor q_{n+1}/q_{n}\rfloor\}}\frac{1}{2+\log j}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{q_{n}}{4cq_{n+1}}\left(\frac{\underline{K}_{n}}{2+\log\underline{K}_{n}}-\frac{\mathrm{e}}{3}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{q_{n}}{8cq_{n+1}}\frac{\underline{K}_{n}}{2+\log\underline{K}_{n}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{q_{n}}{8cq_{n+1}}\frac{\frac{1}{15}h_{n}\log\left(\frac{4}{3}h_{n}\right)-1}{\log\left(\frac{1}{15}h_{n}\right)+\log\log\left(\frac{4}{3}h_{n}\right)+2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{100c\log q_{n}}\frac{\log\left(\frac{4}{3}h_{n}\right)}{\log\left(\frac{1}{15}h_{n}\right)+\log\log\left(\frac{4}{3}h_{n}\right)}

(2.28)

\displaystyle\gg\frac{1}{\log q_{n}}.

On the other hand, for
\epsilon>0
let

\mathcal{M}_{\epsilon}:=\{n\in\mathbb{N}:q_{n+1}>q_{n}^{1+\epsilon}\}
.
Note that if
n\notin\mathcal{M}_{\epsilon}
, then

\displaystyle\underline{K}_{n}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2q_{n+1}}{15q_{n}\log q_{n}}\log\left(\frac{4q_{n+1}}{3q_{n}\log q_{n}}\right)\leq\frac{2q_{n+1}}{15q_{n}\log q_{n}}\log\left(\frac{4q_{n}^{\epsilon}}{3\log q_{n}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{q_{n+1}}{q_{n}}\frac{2\left(\epsilon\log q_{n}+\log(4/3)-\log\log q_{n}\right)}{15\log q_{n}}\leq\left\lfloor\frac{q_{n+1}}{q_{n}}\right\rfloor,

if
\epsilon
is sufficiently small and
n
sufficiently large.
Let
(m_{n})
be the ordered elements of
\mathcal{M}_{\epsilon}
which may be infinitely many. In this case, as we shall see, we have

(2.29)

\displaystyle q_{m_{n}}>n^{n\log n},

for
n
sufficiently large. Let’s denote the set of
n
in
\mathcal{M}_{\epsilon}
such that (
2.29
) holds by
\mathcal{M}_{\epsilon}^{\prime}
. (
2.29
) can be seen as follows:
Assume
q_{m_{n}}=n^{n\log n}
, then

\displaystyle q_{m_{n}+1}

\displaystyle>n^{n\log n(1+\epsilon)}=(n+1)^{(n+1)\log(n+1)}\cdot\frac{n^{n\log n(1+\epsilon)}}{(n+1)^{(n+1)\log(n+1)}}

(2.30)

\displaystyle=(n+1)^{(n+1)\log(n+1)}\left(\frac{n}{n+1}\right)^{n\log n(1+\epsilon)}(n+1)^{n\log n(1+\epsilon)-(n+1)\log(n+1)}

The second factor can be estimated as

(2.31)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{n}{n+1}\right)^{n\log n(1+\epsilon)}

\displaystyle>\left(\left(\frac{n}{n+1}\right)^{n+1}\right)^{\log n(1+\epsilon)}>\left(\mathrm{e}^{-1}-\epsilon\right)^{\log n(1+\epsilon)},

for
n
sufficiently large.
The third factor in (
2.30

\displaystyle(n+1)^{n\log n(1+\epsilon)-(n+1)\log(n+1)}

\displaystyle=(n+1)^{n(\log n-\log(n+1))+\epsilon n\log n-\log(n+1)}\geq(n+1)^{\epsilon/2n\log n},

for
n
sufficiently large.
Hence, combining this with (
2.30
) and (
2.31

\displaystyle q_{m_{n}+1}>(n+1)^{(n+1)\log(n+1)},

for
n
sufficiently large. Noting that
m_{n+1}\geq m_{n}+1
and thus
q_{m_{n+1}}\geq q_{m_{n}+1}
implies (
2.29
).
Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=j}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\log q_{m_{n}+1}}\leq\sum_{n=j}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\log n^{n\log n}}=\sum_{n=j}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n\log^{2}n}<\infty,

for

implying

\sum_{n\in\mathcal{M}_{\epsilon}^{\prime}}\frac{1}{\log q_{n}}<\infty
.
Hence, since the sum over
\mathcal{M}_{\epsilon}
does at most contribute a finite value even if we use the possibly too large value
\frac{1}{\log q_{n}}
, we don’t need a more precise estimate for
n\in\mathcal{M}_{\epsilon}
.

Next, we estimate the second sum in (
2.27
).
Note that for
n\in\mathcal{M}_{\epsilon}^{c}
, we have
2\underline{K}_{n}^{\prime}<\lfloor\frac{q_{n+1}}{q_{n}}\rfloor
, for

sufficiently large.
Hence, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\log q_{n}}\sum_{j=\underline{K}_{n}^{\prime}}^{\lfloor q_{n+1}/q_{n}\rfloor}\frac{1}{3c(j+1)}

\displaystyle\gg\frac{1}{\log q_{n}}\left(\log\left(\frac{q_{n+1}}{q_{n}}\right)-\log\left(\frac{q_{n+1}}{q_{n}\log q_{n}}\log\left(\frac{q_{n+1}}{q_{n}\log q_{n}}\right)\right)\right)

(2.32)

\displaystyle=\frac{\log\log q_{n}-\log\log\left(\frac{q_{n+1}}{q_{n}\log q_{n}}\right)}{\log q_{n}}.

For

2.27

(2.33)

\displaystyle\lambda(\underline{B}_{n})\gg\frac{\max\left\{1\,,\,\log\log q_{n}-\log\log\left(\frac{q_{n+1}}{q_{n}\log q_{n}}\right)\right\}}{\log q_{n}}.

Note that in the estimate of (

if

The final steps will be devoted to prove that

\displaystyle\lambda(\underline{B}_{n}\text{ i.o.})=1

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
(D_{n})
be a sequence of events in a probability measure space
(X,\mathbb{P})
such that

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(D_{n})=\infty.

Additionally, assume that
for all
i<j
we have

(2.35)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(D_{i}\cap D_{j})-\mathbb{P}(D_{i})\mathbb{P}(D_{j})\leq\rho(|i-j|)[\mathbb{P}(D_{i})+\mathbb{P}(D_{i+1})+\mathbb{P}(D_{j})+\mathbb{P}(D_{j+1})],

with
\rho
fulfilling

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\left(\frac{\sum_{m=1}^{n-1}\rho(m)}{\sum_{m=1}^{n}\mathbb{P}(D_{m})}\right)=0.

Then
\mathbb{P}(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\chi_{D_{n}}=\infty)=1
.

In order to be able to apply this lemma, we first need some statements about the independence of the sets
\underline{B}_{n}
which will be given in the following:

Lemma 2.4
.

Let

\displaystyle J_{n}

\displaystyle:=\bigcup_{j=1}^{\lfloor q_{n+1}/q_{n}\rfloor-1}\left[R_{\alpha}^{-jq_{n}}(0)-\frac{1}{\underline{v}_{n,j}}\,,\,R_{\alpha}^{-jq_{n}}(0)+\frac{1}{\underline{v}_{n,j}}\right]\quad\text{ and }

\displaystyle J_{n}^{\prime}

\displaystyle:=\left[R_{\alpha}^{-\lfloor q_{n+1}/q_{n}\rfloor q_{n}}(0)-\frac{1}{\underline{v}_{n,\lfloor q_{n+1}/q_{n}\rfloor}}\,,\,R_{\alpha}^{-\lfloor q_{n+1}/q_{n}\rfloor q_{n}}(0)+\frac{1}{\underline{v}_{n,\lfloor q_{n+1}/q_{n}\rfloor}}\right].

Then for
c
and
n
sufficiently large

(B.1)

the set
J_{n}
is a union of disjoint intervals
\{I_{n,k}\}
,
0\leq k\leq\lfloor\frac{q_{n+1}}{q_{n}}\rfloor
, where all the
I_{n,k}

satisfy
|I_{n,k}|\in[\min\{\frac{1}{6cq_{n+1}\log q_{n}},\frac{1}{8cq_{n+1}(\log q_{n+1}-\log q_{n})}\},\frac{6}{q_{n}}]
and
\diam(J_{n})\leq\frac{6}{q_{n}}
;

(B.2)

for each
i\in\{0,\ldots,q_{n-1}-1\}
we have
|J^{\prime}_{n}|\in[\min\{\frac{1}{6cq_{n+1}\log q_{n}},\frac{1}{8cq_{n+1}(\log q_{n+1}-\log q_{n})}\},\frac{6}{q_{n}}]
;

(B.3)

we have

\underline{B}_{n}=\bigcup_{i=0}^{q_{n}-1}R^{-i}_{\alpha}(J_{n})\cup\bigcup_{i=0}^{q_{n-1}-1}R^{-i}_{\alpha}(J^{\prime}_{n});

(B.4)

the sets
\{R^{-i}_{\alpha}(J_{n})\}
and
\{R^{-i}_{\alpha}(J^{\prime}_{n})\}
are pairwise disjoint.

Proof.

Note that property
(B.2)
is immediate from the definition of
\underline{v}_{n,\lfloor q_{n+1}/q_{n}\rfloor}
(see
(
2.21
)).
Moreover property
(B.3)
is straightforward from the definition of the set
\underline{B}_{n}
- we have just exchanged the order of the involved unions and used
q_{n+1}-1-\lfloor\frac{q_{n+1}}{q_{n}}\rfloor q_{n}=q_{n-1}-1
.

We will now show
(B.1)
. Note that just from the definition of
J_{n}
it follows that
J_{n}
is a union of intervals - we will only need to show that they are disjoint.

Denote

I_{n,j}:=\left[R_{\alpha}^{-jq_{n}}(0)-\frac{1}{\underline{v}_{n,j}}\,,\,R_{\alpha}^{-jq_{n}}(0)+\frac{1}{\underline{v}_{n,j}}\right].

We have that

(2.36)

\max_{j,j^{\prime}\in\{1,\ldots,\lfloor q_{n+1}/q_{n}\rfloor-1\},j\neq j^{\prime}}d(R_{\alpha}^{-jq_{n}}(0),R_{\alpha}^{-j^{\prime}q_{n}}(0))=\frac{w_{n}}{q_{n+1}}\geq\frac{1}{2q_{n+1}}.

On the other hand, for
c\geq 2
, we have

|I_{n,j}|=\frac{2}{\underline{v}_{n,j}}\leq\frac{1}{2q_{n+1}}
, for all
j\in\{1,\ldots,\lfloor\frac{q_{n+1}}{q_{n}}\rfloor-1\}
implying disjointness.

Note that trivially

\displaystyle|I_{n,j}|\geq 2\min_{j<\lfloor q_{n+1}/q_{n}\rfloor}\frac{1}{\underline{v}_{n,j}}\geq\min\Big\{\frac{1}{6cq_{n+1}\log q_{n}},\frac{1}{8cq_{n+1}(\log q_{n+1}-\log q_{n})}\Big\},

for
n
sufficiently large.
Moreover, since
\max_{j<\lfloor q_{n+1}/q_{n}\rfloor}d(R_{\alpha}^{-jq_{n}}(0),0)\leq j|q_{n}\alpha|\leq\frac{2}{q_{n}}
it follows that
I_{n,j}\subset J_{n}\subset[-\frac{3}{q_{n}},\frac{3}{q_{n}}]
, which completes the proof of
(B.1)
.

Finally, in order to prove
(B.4)
, remember (
2.36
).
As any of the intervals defining
J_{n}
and
J_{n}^{\prime}
have a diameter of at most
\frac{1}{2q_{n+1}}
and they are all symmetric around
R_{\alpha}^{-j}(0)
, we obtain
(B.4)
.
∎

The following lemma is crucial in obtaining independence properties of the collection
\{\underline{B}_{n}\}
.

Lemma 2.5
.

We have

|\lambda(\underline{B}_{i}\cap\underline{B}_{i+m})-\lambda(\underline{B}_{i})\lambda(\underline{B}_{i+m})|={\rm O}\Big(\frac{q_{i+1}}{q_{i+m-2}}\lambda(\underline{B}_{i+m})\Big),

where
\rm O
does not depend on
i
or
m
.

Proof.

We will use Lemma
2.4
together with the Denjoy-Koksma inequality. Consider
\underline{B}_{i+m}\cap R_{\alpha}^{-\ell}(J_{i})
for

\ell<\lfloor\frac{q_{n+1}}{q_{n}}\rfloor
. To estimate the measure of this set we will estimate measures of the sets

(\bigcup_{s=0}^{q_{i+m}-1}R^{-s}_{\alpha}(J_{i+m}))\cap R_{\alpha}^{-\ell}(I_{i,k})
and
(\bigcup_{s=0}^{q_{i+m-1}-1}R^{-s}_{\alpha}(J^{\prime}_{i+m}))\cap R_{\alpha}^{-\ell}(I_{i,k})
. To do this, note that by
(B.1)
the set
R_{\alpha}^{-\ell}(I_{i,k})
is an interval of length
\frac{1}{\underline{v}_{n,k}}\leq\frac{1}{4cq_{n+1}}
;
we denote it by
[a,b]
.
Moreover,
\diam(J_{i+m})\leq\frac{6}{q_{i+m}}
. Take any
x_{i+m}\in J_{i+m}
. Then

\displaystyle\lambda\Big(\bigcup_{s=q_{i+m-1}}^{q_{i+m}-1}R^{-s}_{\alpha}(J_{i+m})\cap[a,b]\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\lambda(J_{i+m})\,\#\big\{s{\in[q_{i+m-1},q_{i+m})\cap\mathbb{N}}\;:\;R^{-s}_{\alpha}(x_{i+m})\in[a-\diam(J_{i+m}),b+\diam(J_{i+m})]\big\}

and

\displaystyle\lambda\Big(\bigcup_{s=q_{i+m-1}}^{q_{i+m}-1}R^{-s}_{\alpha}(J_{i+m})\cap[a,b]\Big)

\displaystyle\geq\lambda(J_{i+m})\,\#\big\{s{\in[q_{i+m-1},q_{i+m})\cap\mathbb{N}}\;:\;R^{-s}_{\alpha}(x_{i+m})\in[a+\diam(J_{i+m}),b-\diam(J_{i+m})]\big\}.

We will estimate both cardinalities using the Denjoy-Koksma inequality. Let

f^{+}=\chi_{[a-\diam(J_{i+m})\,,\,b+\diam(J_{i+m})]},

then

\displaystyle\#\big\{s\in[q_{i+m-1},q_{i+m})\cap\mathbb{N}\;:\;R^{-s}_{\alpha}(x_{i+m})\in[a-\diam(J_{i+m}),b+\diam(J_{i+m})]\big\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=0}^{q_{i+m}-1}f^{+}(R^{-s}_{\alpha}(x_{i+m})).

By the Denjoy-Koksma inequality we have

\displaystyle\bigg|\sum_{s=q_{i+m-1}}^{q_{i+m}-1}f^{+}(R^{-s}_{\alpha}(x_{i+m}))-\left(q_{i+m}-q_{i+m-1}\right)\lambda(f^{+})\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\bigg|\sum_{s=0}^{q_{i+m}-1}f^{+}(R^{-s}_{\alpha}(x_{i+m}))-q_{i+m}\lambda(f^{+})\bigg|+\bigg|\sum_{s=0}^{q_{i+m-1}-1}f^{+}(R^{-s}_{\alpha}(x_{i+m}))-q_{i+m-1}\lambda(f^{+})\bigg|

\displaystyle<2\Var(f^{+})\leq 8.

Moreover, by the bound on
\diam(J_{i+m})
, we have
\lambda(f^{+})\leq\lambda([a,b])+12q_{i+m}^{-1}
. Using an analogous estimate for the lower bound (and putting both estimates together) yields

\displaystyle\lambda\Big(\bigcup_{s=q_{i+m-1}}^{q_{i+m}-1}R^{-s}_{\alpha}(J_{i+m})\cap[a,b]\Big)

\displaystyle={\left(q_{i+m}-q_{i+m-1}\right)}\lambda([a,b])\lambda(J_{i+m})+{\rm O}(\lambda(J_{i+m}))

\displaystyle=\lambda\Big({\bigcup_{s=q_{i+m-1}}^{q_{i+m}-1}}R^{-s}_{\alpha}(J_{i+m})\Big)\lambda([a,b])+{\rm O}(\lambda(J_{i+m})),

where the second equality follows from
(B.4)
in Lemma
2.4
.

Summing this over all
[a,b]=R_{\alpha}^{-\ell}(I_{i,k})
and also
[a,b]=R_{\alpha}^{-\ell}(J_{i}^{\prime})
, we get

\lambda\Big({\bigcup_{s=q_{i+m-1}}^{q_{i+m}-1}}R^{-s}_{\alpha}(J_{i+m})\cap\underline{B}_{i}\Big)=\lambda\Big({\bigcup_{s=q_{i+m-1}}^{q_{i+m}-1}}R^{-s}_{\alpha}(J_{i+m})\Big)\lambda(\underline{B}_{i})+{\rm O}({\left(q_{i+1}-q_{i}\right)}\lambda(J_{i+m})).

By an analogous reasoning,

\lambda\Big({\bigcup_{s=q_{i+m-1}}^{q_{i+m-1}-1}}R^{-s}_{\alpha}(J^{\prime}_{i+m})\cap\underline{B}_{i}\Big)=\lambda\Big({\bigcup_{s=q_{i+m-1}}^{q_{i+m-1}-1}}R^{-s}_{\alpha}(J^{\prime}_{i+m})\Big)\lambda(\underline{B}_{i})+{\rm O}({\left(q_{i+1}-q_{i}\right)}\lambda(J^{\prime}_{i+m})).

Putting the two above together and using

\displaystyle\left(q_{i+1}-q_{i}\right)\lambda(J_{i+m})

\displaystyle=\frac{q_{i+1}-q_{i}}{q_{i+m}-q_{i+m-1}}\,\lambda\bigg(\bigcup_{s=q_{i+m-1}}^{q_{i+m}-1}R^{-s}_{\alpha}(J_{i+m})\bigg)

(2.37)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{q_{i+1}}{q_{i+m-2}}\,\lambda\bigg(\bigcup_{s=q_{i+m-1}}^{q_{i+m}-1}R^{-s}_{\alpha}(J_{i+m})\bigg)

and
{(q_{i+1}-q_{i})}\lambda(J^{\prime}_{i+m})=\frac{q_{i+1}-q_{i}}{q_{i+m-1}}\lambda(\bigcup_{s=0}^{q_{i+m-1}-1}R^{-s}_{\alpha}(J^{\prime}_{i+m}))
,
we get

\lambda(\underline{B}_{i}\cap\underline{B}_{i+m})=\lambda(\underline{B}_{i})\lambda(\underline{B}_{i+m})+{\rm O}\Big(\frac{q_{i+1}}{q_{i+m-2}}\lambda(\underline{B}_{i+m})\Big).

This finishes the proof.
∎

Finally, we are in the position to prove (

).
We notice that (

) together with (

Proof of Theorem

In order to prove the lower bound we use the following statement by Łuczak,
[

Lemma 2.6
.

Let

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Xi}(b,c)=\{\alpha\in[0,1]:a_{n}=c^{b^{n}}\text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{N}\}.

Then for all
b,c\in(1,\infty)
we have

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathrm{H}}(\widetilde{\Xi}(b,c))=\frac{1}{b+1}.

Let
b=1+\epsilon
and
c>1
. Moreover, we note that
W_{n}\ll\frac{\log\log q_{n}}{\log q_{n}}
and the function
\frac{\log\log(\cdot)}{\log(\cdot)}
is eventually monotonically decreasing.
Under the assumption that
\alpha\in\widetilde{\Xi}(b,c)
and noting then that
q_{n}>a_{n}=c^{(1+\epsilon)^{n}}
we have

\displaystyle W_{n}(\alpha)\ll\frac{\log\log c+n\log(1+\epsilon)}{(1+\epsilon)^{n}\log c}

and thus
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}W_{n}(\alpha)<\infty
.
Hence, for all
\epsilon>0
and all
c>1
we have that

\widetilde{\Xi}(1+\epsilon,c)\subset\{\alpha:\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}W_{n}(\alpha)<\infty\}
and
Lemma
2.6
implies that

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\Big(\alpha:\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}W_{n}(\alpha)<\infty\Big)\geq\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\big(\widetilde{\Xi}(1+\epsilon,c)\big)=\frac{1}{2+\epsilon}.

Since
\epsilon
was arbitrary, we get the lower bound for the Hausdorff dimension.

In order to prove the upper bound, we note that Fan, Liao, Wang and Wu, see
[
8
, Theorem 1.2. (1)]
, proved that

(2.38)

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\left(\alpha:\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log q_{n}}{n}=\infty\right)=\frac{1}{2}.

For the following, let
\mathcal{D}=\{n:a_{n+1}=1\text{ and }a_{n}\neq 1\}
. Note that for
n\in\mathcal{D}^{c}
we have
W_{n}\gg\frac{1}{\log q_{n}}
. This can be seen as follows: If
q_{n+1}\geq 2q_{n}\log q_{n}
, this is immediately the case; so assume
q_{n+1}<2q_{n}\log q_{n}
.
If
a_{n+1}\geq 2
, then
q_{n+1}\geq 2q_{n}
and thus
\log q_{n+1}-\log q_{n}\geq\log 2
implying that
W_{n}\geq\frac{1}{\log q_{n}}
.
On the other hand, if
a_{n+1}=a_{n}=1
, then
q_{n}=q_{n-1}+q_{n-2}
and

q_{n+1}=q_{n}+q_{n-1}=2q_{n-1}+q_{n-2}
which implies
\log q_{n+1}-\log q_{n}\leq\log(\frac{3}{2})
.

Moreover, we have that
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}W_{n}=\infty
already implies
\sum_{n\in\mathcal{D}^{c}}W_{n}=\infty
. This can be seen as follows: If
n\in\mathcal{D}
, then

(2.39)

\displaystyle\frac{\log q_{n+1}-\log q_{n}}{\log q_{n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\log\left(1+\frac{q_{n-1}}{q_{n}}\right)}{\log q_{n}}\leq\frac{\log\left(1+\frac{q_{n-1}}{a_{n}q_{n-1}+q_{n-2}}\right)}{\log q_{n}}\leq\frac{1}{\log q_{n}}.

Moreover,
q_{n}=q_{n+1}-q_{n-1}
and
q_{n+1}=(a_{n-1}+1)q_{n-1}+q_{n-2}
. This implies

q_{n}=q_{n+1}\left(1-\frac{q_{n-1}}{(a_{n-1}+1)q_{n-1}+q_{n-2}}\right)\geq\frac{2}{3}q_{n+1}

which together with (
2.39
) implies

\displaystyle\frac{\log q_{n+1}-\log q_{n}}{\log q_{n}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\log q_{n+1}+\log\big(\frac{2}{3}\big)}\leq\frac{2}{\log q_{n+1}},

for
n
sufficiently large, say for
n\geq M
. Thus, noting that

n\in\mathcal{D}
implies
n+1\in\mathcal{D}^{c}
, we have
W_{n}\leq 2W_{n+1}
, for all
n\in\mathcal{D}\cap\mathbb{N}_{\geq M}
.
Hence,
\sum_{n\in\mathcal{D}^{c}\cap\mathbb{N}_{\geq M}}W_{n}\geq\frac{1}{3}\sum_{n=M}^{\infty}W_{n}=\infty
as claimed.

We have
W_{n}\ll\frac{1}{\log q_{n}}
, for all
n\in\mathcal{D}^{c}
. As
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}W_{n}=\infty
implies
\sum_{n\in\mathcal{D}^{c}}W_{n}=\infty
, it is enough to consider
n\in\mathcal{D}^{c}
.

However,

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\log q_{n}}<\infty
implies
\frac{\log q_{n}}{n}\to\infty
and thus

\left\{\alpha:\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log q_{n}}{n}=\infty\right\}\supset\left\{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}W_{n}<\infty\right\}.

This together with (
2.38
) gives the upper bound.
∎

2.3.
Proof of Theorem
1.4

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

The proof can be done in a similar, but easier way than the proof of Theorem
1.1
. First note that

\int_{1/q_{n}}^{1-1/q_{n}}x^{-\gamma}\mathrm{d}x\asymp q_{n}^{\gamma-1}
.
Hence, by the Denjoy-Koksma inequality we have

(2.40)

\displaystyle S_{q_{n}}(\widetilde{\phi}_{\gamma})\ll q_{n}^{\gamma}+\widetilde{\phi}_{\gamma}(x_{\min,q_{n}}).

Set

\displaystyle E_{n,K}=\bigcup_{\ell=q_{n-1}}^{q_{n}-1}\Gamma_{n,\ell,K}=\bigcup_{\ell=q_{n-1}}^{q_{n}-1}\left[R_{\alpha}^{-\ell}(0)-\frac{1}{Kq_{n}}\,,\,R_{\alpha}^{-\ell}(0)+\frac{1}{Kq_{n}}\right].

Moreover, as the union defining
E_{n,K}
is disjoint, for
K
sufficiently large, we have

\displaystyle\lambda(E_{n,K})=\frac{2}{K}\frac{q_{n}-q_{n-1}}{q_{n}}=\frac{2}{K}\left(1-\frac{1}{a_{n}}+\frac{q_{n-2}}{a_{n}q_{n}}\right).

If there are infinitely many
n
such that
a_{n}>1
, it immediately follows that
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda(E_{n,K})=\infty
.
On the other hand, if
a_{n}=1
eventually, then for
n
sufficiently large we have

\displaystyle\lambda(E_{n,K})=\frac{2}{K}\frac{q_{n-2}}{2q_{n-2}+q_{n-3}}>\frac{2}{3K}

and
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda(E_{n,K})=\infty
follows as well.

In the next steps, we prove that for any given
K>0
there exists
K^{\prime}>0
such that

(2.41)

\displaystyle\widetilde{C}_{n}:=\left\{x:\exists k\in(q_{n-1},q_{n}]:\phi\circ R_{\alpha}^{k}(x)>KS_{k}(x)\right\}\supset E_{n,K^{\prime}}.

First note that the statement
x\in E_{n,K^{\prime}}
is equivalent to

R_{\alpha}^{k}(x)\in[-\frac{1}{K^{\prime}q_{n}},\frac{1}{K^{\prime}q_{n}}]
for some
k\in\{q_{n-1},\ldots,q_{n}-1\}
. This implies that there exists
k\in\{q_{n-1},\ldots,q_{n}-1\}
such that

\phi\circ R_{\alpha}^{k}(x)>(K^{\prime}q_{n})^{\gamma}
.
However, for
k\leq q_{n}
, we can conclude by (
2.40
) that there exists
K^{\prime\prime}>0
such that
S_{k}(x)-\phi(x_{\min,k})\leq K^{\prime\prime}q_{n}^{\gamma}
, for
n
sufficiently large.
Hence, (
2.41
) holds.

By an analogous calculation as in Lemma
2.5
considering the sets
\Gamma_{i,\ell,K^{\prime}}
instead of
R_{\alpha}^{-\ell}(I_{i,k})
, the sets
\Gamma_{i+m,\ell,K^{\prime}}
instead of
J_{i+m}
and noting that also the
\Gamma_{i,\ell,K^{\prime}}
are pairwise disjoint
we obtain

|\lambda(E_{i,K^{\prime}}\cap E_{i+m,K^{\prime}})-\lambda(E_{i,K^{\prime}})\lambda(E_{i+m,K^{\prime}})|={\rm O}\Big(\frac{q_{i+1}}{q_{i+m-2}}\lambda(E_{i+m,K^{\prime}})\Big).

Remember that
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda(E_{n,K})=\infty
.
Using the same construction as in the proof Theorem
1.1

we obtain by using Lemma
2.3
that
\lambda(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\chi_{E_{n,K^{\prime}}}=\infty)=1
.
∎

2.4.
Proof of Proposition
1.9

Proof of Proposition
1.9
.

Let
K
be the set of
\alpha\in\mathbb{T}
such that
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\log q_{n}}=\infty
. It follows from the Khintchine-Lévy theorem that such a set of
\alpha
is of full measure. For the following, we always assume that
\alpha\in K
. Let
\epsilon_{n}:=\frac{2}{\log\log q_{n}}
. Consider now the following sets

C_{n}:=\bigcup_{j=-q_{n}}^{q_{n}-1}R_{\alpha}^{-j}\Big[-\frac{1}{\epsilon_{n}q_{n}\log q_{n}},\frac{1}{\epsilon_{n}q_{n}\log q_{n}}\Big]

and

D_{n}:=\bigcup_{j=0}^{q_{n}-1}R_{\alpha}^{-j}\Big[-\frac{\epsilon_{n}}{q_{n}\log q_{n}},\frac{\epsilon_{n}}{q_{n}\log q_{n}}\Big].

Consider

Q_{1}:=\{(x,\beta)\in\mathbb{T}^{2}\;:\;(x,\beta)\in D_{n}\times C_{n}^{c}\text{ for infinitely many }n\in\mathbb{N}\}.

Note that if
(x,\beta)\in D_{n}\times C_{n}^{c}
, then
\{x+j\alpha\}_{j=0}^{q_{n}-1}\cap\Big[-\frac{\epsilon_{n}}{q_{n}\log q_{n}},\frac{\epsilon_{n}}{q_{n}\log q_{n}}\Big]\neq\emptyset
(in fact there is just one
j_{0}\in[0,q_{n}-1]
which is in the intersection) and
\{x-\beta+j\alpha\}_{j=0}^{q_{n}-1}\cap\Big[\frac{-1}{2\epsilon_{n}q_{n}\log q_{n}},\frac{1}{2\epsilon_{n}q_{n}\log q_{n}}\Big]=\emptyset
. Indeed, the first statement follows directly from the fact that
x\in D_{n}
(and for uniqueness we use Diophantine properties of orbits of length
q_{n}
). For the second note that if there was a
j_{1}\in[0,q_{n}-1]
such that
x-\beta+j_{1}\alpha
belonged to the set
\Big[\frac{-1}{2\epsilon_{n}q_{n}\log q_{n}},\frac{1}{2\epsilon_{n}q_{n}\log q_{n}}\Big]
, then

\beta+(j_{0}-j_{1})\alpha=(x+j_{0}\alpha)-(x-\beta+j_{1}\alpha)\in\Big[\frac{-1}{\epsilon_{n}q_{n}\log q_{n}},\frac{1}{\epsilon_{n}q_{n}\log q_{n}}\Big]
which contradicts the fact that
\beta\in C_{n}^{c}
. Therefore, for
x\in D_{n}
,

S_{j_{0}+1}(\phi)(x)\geq\phi(x+j_{0}\alpha)\geq\frac{q_{n}\log q_{n}}{\epsilon_{n}}

and by Lemma
2.1
for
(x,\beta)\in D_{n}\times C_{n}^{c}

S_{j_{0}+1}(\phi)(x-\beta)\leq S_{q_{n}}(\phi)(x-\beta)<2q_{n}\log q_{n}+q_{n}+2\epsilon_{n}q_{n}\log q_{n}.

Therefore,
\Theta_{j_{0}+1}^{\beta}(x)>\frac{1}{10\epsilon_{n}}
, for
n
sufficiently large. In particular, for
(x,\beta)\in Q_{1}
, we have
\limsup_{N\to\infty}\Theta_{N}^{\beta}(x)=\infty
. Analogously, defining the set

Q_{2}:=\{(x,\beta)\in\mathbb{T}^{2}\;:\;(x-\beta,\beta)\in D_{n}\times C_{n}^{c}\text{ for infinitely many }n\in\mathbb{N}\},

we get that for
(x,\beta)\in Q_{2}
,
\liminf_{N\to\infty}\Theta_{N}^{\beta}(x)=0
. Consider the set
Q_{1}\cap Q_{2}
. We will show that
\lambda_{2}(Q_{1}\cap Q_{2})=1
. (Here and in the following we will distinguish between
\lambda_{2}
and
\lambda_{1}
depending if we consider to two- or one-dimensional Lebesgue measure.) This by Fubini’s theorem implies that there is a full measure set
G
of
\beta
such that for each
\beta\in G
there is a full measure set
H_{\beta}
of
x
such that
(x,\beta)\in Q_{1}\cap Q_{2}
. In particular taking
Z=K\times G
finishes the proof of the proposition.

So it remains to show that
\lambda_{2}(Q_{i})=1
for
i=1,2
.
Since the proof is similar in both cases, we will only show that
\lambda_{2}(Q_{2})=1
(in fact the proof for
Q_{2}
is slightly more involved as we have a twisted condition
x-\beta
in the first coordinate).
First, for
j\in\{0,\ldots,q_{n}-1\}
define

\displaystyle\widetilde{C}_{n,j}:=R_{\alpha}^{-j}\Big[-\frac{1}{\epsilon_{n}q_{n}\log q_{n}},\frac{1}{\epsilon_{n}q_{n}\log q_{n}}\Big]\qquad\text{and}\qquad\widetilde{C}_{n}:=\bigcup_{j=0}^{q_{n}-1}\widetilde{C}_{n,j}\subset C_{n}

and let

L_{n}:=\{(x,\beta)\in\mathbb{T}^{2}\;:\;(x-\beta,\beta)\in D_{n}\times\widetilde{C}_{n}^{c}\}.

Notice first that by Fubini’s theorem and translational invariance of Lebesgue measure,
\lambda_{2}(L_{n})=\lambda_{1}(\widetilde{C}_{n}^{c})\lambda_{1}(D_{n})
. Moreover, for any
i,j
,
\lambda_{2}(L_{i}\cap L_{j})=\lambda_{1}(\widetilde{C}_{i}^{c}\cap\widetilde{C}_{j}^{c})\lambda_{1}(D_{i}\cap D_{j})
.
This implies

\displaystyle|\lambda_{2}(L_{i}\cap L_{j})-\lambda_{2}(L_{i})\lambda_{2}(L_{j})|

\displaystyle=|\lambda_{1}(\widetilde{C}_{i}^{c}\cap\widetilde{C}_{j}^{c})\lambda_{1}(D_{i}\cap D_{j})-\lambda_{1}(\widetilde{C}_{i}^{c})\lambda_{1}(\widetilde{C}_{j}^{c})\lambda_{1}(D_{i})\lambda_{1}(D_{j})|

\displaystyle\leq|\lambda_{1}(\widetilde{C}_{i}^{c}\cap\widetilde{C}_{j}^{c})\lambda_{1}(D_{i}\cap D_{j})-\lambda_{1}(\widetilde{C}_{i}^{c})\lambda_{1}(\widetilde{C}_{j}^{c})\lambda_{1}(D_{i}\cap D_{j})|

\displaystyle\qquad+|\lambda_{1}(\widetilde{C}_{i}^{c})\lambda_{1}(\widetilde{C}_{j}^{c})\lambda_{1}(D_{i}\cap D_{j})-\lambda_{1}(\widetilde{C}_{i}^{c})\lambda_{1}(\widetilde{C}_{j}^{c})\lambda_{1}(D_{i})\lambda_{1}(D_{j})|

\displaystyle\leq|\lambda_{1}(\widetilde{C}_{i}^{c}\cap\widetilde{C}_{j}^{c})-\lambda_{1}(\widetilde{C}_{i}^{c})\lambda_{1}(\widetilde{C}_{j}^{c})|\,\lambda_{1}(D_{j})

(2.42)

\displaystyle\qquad+\lambda_{1}(\widetilde{C}_{j}^{c})\,|\lambda_{1}(D_{i}\cap D_{j})-\lambda_{1}(D_{i})\lambda_{1}(D_{j})|.

Notice further that

(2.43)

\displaystyle|\lambda_{1}(\widetilde{C}_{i}^{c}\cap\widetilde{C}_{j}^{c})-\lambda_{1}(\widetilde{C}_{i}^{c})\lambda_{1}(\widetilde{C}_{j}^{c})|=|\lambda_{1}(\widetilde{C}_{i}\cap\widetilde{C}_{j})-\lambda_{1}(\widetilde{C}_{i})\lambda_{1}(\widetilde{C}_{j})|.

Notice that, for
n
sufficiently large, the intervals building
\widetilde{C}_{n}
are disjoint (indeed we have
\min_{j,j^{\prime}\in\{0,\ldots,q_{n}-1\},j\neq j^{\prime}}R_{\alpha}^{-j}(0)\geq\frac{1}{2q_{n}}
and

\diam(\widetilde{C}_{n,j})=\frac{2}{\epsilon_{n}q_{n}\log q_{n}}
, for each
j
).

Next, we follow essentially the same steps as in Lemma
2.5
looking at
\widetilde{C}_{i+m,0}
instead of
J_{i+m}
and at an interval
[a,b]=\widetilde{C}_{i,\ell}
instead of
R_{\alpha}^{-\ell}(I_{i,k})
. Moreover, the union ranges from
s\in\{0,\ldots,q_{i+m}-1\}
instead of
s\in\{q_{i+m-1},\ldots,q_{i+m}-1\}
, so in the analogous line to (
2.37
) we have a factor
\frac{q_{i+1}}{q_{i+m+1}}
instead of
\frac{q_{i+1}-q_{i}}{q_{i+m-1}}
. Thus, we obtain

(2.44)

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(\widetilde{C}_{i}\cap\widetilde{C}_{i+m})=\lambda(\widetilde{C}_{i})\lambda_{1}(\widetilde{C}_{i+m})+{\rm O}\Big(\frac{q_{i+1}}{q_{i+m+1}}\lambda_{1}(\widetilde{C}_{i+m})\Big).

Using an analogous argumentation we obtain

(2.45)

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(D_{i}\cap D_{i+m})=\lambda_{1}(D_{i})\lambda_{1}(D_{i+m})+{\rm O}\Big(\frac{q_{i+1}}{q_{i+m+1}}\lambda_{1}(D_{i+m})\Big).

We aim to use Lemma
2.3
for
L_{n}
. Combining (
2.42
), (
2.43
), (
2.44
), and (
2.45
) we obtain that there exists
\kappa>0
such that for all
i<j
we have

\displaystyle|\lambda_{2}(L_{i}\cap L_{j})-\lambda_{2}(L_{i})\lambda_{2}(L_{j})|

\displaystyle\leq\kappa{\frac{q_{i+1}}{q_{j+1}}}\lambda_{1}(\widetilde{C}_{j})\lambda_{1}(D_{j})\leq\kappa{\frac{q_{i+1}}{q_{j+1}}}\lambda_{1}(\widetilde{C}_{j}^{c})\lambda_{1}(D_{j}).

Noting that the
q_{n}
decay exponentially fast and that there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
with
\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}\lambda_{2}(L_{n})\geq\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{1}(D_{n})}{2}\geq\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\log q_{n}}=\infty
, we obtain that

\lambda_{2}(L_{n}\text{ for infinitely many }n\in\mathbb{N})=1
.
As
\{L_{n}\text{ for infinitely many }n\in\mathbb{N}\}\subset Q_{1}
this gives the statement of the proposition.
∎

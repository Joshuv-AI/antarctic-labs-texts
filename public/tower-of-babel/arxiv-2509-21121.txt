Lagrangian aspects of Yudovich theory for 2D Euler
Theodore D. Drivas and Joonhyun La

Abstract
In this note, we establish Yudovich’s existence and uniqueness result for bounded (as well as mildly unbounded) vorticity weak solution of the two-dimensional incompressible Euler equations. As a biproduct of our proof, we establish some regularity results for the Yudovich solution map as it depends of the initial conditions and the fluid domain.



Dedicated to Prof. Peter Constantin, with admiration and respect.

Let
\Omega
a bounded domain in the plane, or a two-dimensional manifold without boundary. Provided the cohomology of
\Omega
is trivial (see
[
7
]
), or else that
\Omega=\mathbb{T}^{2}
or
\mathbb{T}\times[0,1]
, the two dimensional Euler equations in vorticity form for
\omega=\omega(t,x):\mathbb{R}\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
read

\displaystyle\partial\omega+u\cdot\nabla\omega

\displaystyle=0\qquad\qquad\text{in}\ \ \mathbb{R}\times\Omega

(1)

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=K_{\Omega}[\omega]\qquad\!\text{in}\ \ \mathbb{R}\times\ \Omega

(2)

\displaystyle u\cdot n

\displaystyle=0\qquad\qquad\text{on}\ \ \mathbb{R}\times\partial\Omega

(3)

\displaystyle\omega

\displaystyle=\omega_{0}\qquad\ \ \ \ \text{on}\ \ \{0\}\times\Omega

(4)

where
n
is the normal to the boundary of
\Omega
(if non-empty), and where
K=\nabla^{\perp}\Delta^{-1}
(with trivial Dirichlet boundary conditions for
\Delta
) is the Biot Savart operator on
\Omega
.

Equations (
1
)–(
4
) show that the vorticity is simply transported within the domain by the velocity field it itself induces. Namely, if the diffeomorphism
\Phi_{t}:\Omega\to\Omega
solving

\frac{d}{dt}X_{t}=u(X_{t},t)\qquad X_{0}={\rm id}

(5)

denotes the flowmap of the velocity field
u
, then the solution to (
1
)–(
4
) ought to be

\omega(t)=\omega_{0}\circ X_{t}^{-1}.

(6)

Of course, having such a representation requires that the velocity field be sufficiently regular to at least generate a flow of homeomorphisms, and this regularity must be determined by and consistent with the corresponding vorticity smoothness. Classical results tell that of
\omega_{0}\in C^{\alpha}(\Omega)
with
\alpha>0
, then there exists a unique solution in the class
u\in C^{1,\alpha}_{x,t}
, in particular the velocity is Lipschitz, which is more than sufficient to justify the above picture. Indeed, in this classical setting the Lagrangian particle map is a flow of diffeomorphism of some finite regularity. A major improvement to this result was realized first by Yudovich
[
14
]
, who proved that if
\omega_{0}\in L_{x}^{\infty}
, then there exists a unique
weak solution
of class
\omega\in L^{\infty}_{t}L_{x}^{\infty}
. Here, the velocity is no longer Lipschitz, but bounded vorticity is enough to ensure that the velocity is
log-Lipschitz
and thus there exists a corresponding flow of Hölder continuous homeomorphisms with decaying regularity. Subsequently, Yudovich improved this theory to allow slightly unbounded vorticies which essentially have initial
L^{p}
norms allowed to grow as
p\to\infty
just slightly slower than logarithmically
[
15
]
. See (
22
) for a precise definition. The point here is that vorticity being in this space ensures that the velocity, while no longer necessarily log-Lipschitz, is still
Osgood continuous
and thus by Osgood’s theorem generates a flow of homeomorphisms. The Lagrangian picture persists to this setting, and coherent singular structures are know to exist
[
8
]
.

Although the Lagrangian picture is implied for Yudovich’s theory, it is not how Yudovich constructed his solutions of proved uniqueness. Here rather he relied on some involved quasi-Eulerian scheme and clever energy estimates. See also
[
6
]
. To the best of our knowledge, we are not aware of a fully Lagrangian proof: for example, the formulation in
[
11
]
measured the
L^{1}
-difference of flows to prove uniqueness, yet it did not regard Euler equation as the ODE in the space of volume-preserving homeomorphisms. Also, we would like to highlight Lagrangian-Eulerian formulation of
[
1
]
: here, all variables are Lagrangian, and the regularity is considered in
C^{1,\alpha}
space for fluid velocity, where Eulerian formulation faces a regularity issue. Yet, as the formulation intends to and does provide a unified framework for well-posedness of a wide range of models which involve velocity gradient in their evolution equations, it comes back to Eulerian setting to formulate velocity and velocity gradient operators. On the other hand, in Yudovich theory, velocity gradient of velocity field is not needed. Our goal in this note is to provide a simple, self-contained and fully Lagrangian proof of Yudovich’s result. As a result of this proof, we can gain additional information about the structure of the theory. In particular, we will prove that the Lagrangian solution map

X_{t}=S_{t,\omega_{0},\Omega}(X_{0})

(7)

is itself continuous at fixed times as a function of the initial data and domain, with regularity deteriorating at the same rate as that of the particle flowmap. Our main result is

Theorem 1
.

Let
\Omega
be a
C^{2}
bounded domain in the plane, or a surface without boundary,
\mathcal{M}
be the space of measure-preserving homeomorphisms on
\Omega
endowed with the
L^{\infty}
topology and
L^{1}\cap\mathbf{Y}_{\Theta}
be a Yudovich space (
22
). Then the Euler solution map
S_{t,\omega_{0},\Omega}:\mathcal{M}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}
is well defined,
C^{\infty}
smooth in time, and continuous in its dependence on the initial data and domain (with a time-dependent modulus of continuity depending on
\Theta
).

For more precise statement about existence, uniqueness and dependence on initial data see Theorem
2
. For dependence on the domain, see Theorem
3
. Smoothness in time is proved by Serfati
[
12
]
in the bounded vorticity case and generalized to the full Yudovich class by Sueur
[
13
]
. The particular modulus of continuity of the solution map depends on
\Theta
. In that case, the modulus for the solution map comes from inverting the function
\nu_{\Theta}
given in (
25
), e.g.,
\nu_{\Theta}^{-1}(e^{ct}\nu_{\Theta}(\text{difference of parameters}))
. In the special case of bounded vorticity, the modulus becomes
a time-dependent Hölder modulus. Specifically

\|S_{t,\omega_{0},\Omega}(X)-S_{t,\omega_{0}^{\prime},\Omega}(X)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\omega_{0}-\omega_{0}^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{e^{-ct}}

(8)

for appropriate constants
c,C>0
depending only on
\|X\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
and
\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
,
\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
. In terms of the domain, we consider two related by an arbitrary twice-differentiable diffeomorphism
\Phi:\Omega\to\Omega^{\prime}:=\Phi(\Omega)
. See
[
9
,
10
]
for rougher domains. The solution map obeys:

\|S_{t,\omega_{0},\Omega}(X)-\Phi^{-1}\circ S_{t,\omega_{0}\circ\Phi^{-1},\Phi(\Omega)}(X)\circ\Phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\Phi-{\rm id}\|_{C^{2}(\Omega)}^{e^{-ct}},

(9)

again for appropriate constants
c,C>0
.
Note that these estimates correlates precisely the well known deteriorating Hölder estimate for the regularity of the flowmap as a function of label. For corresponding continuity properties of the
Eulerian
solution map, which are necessarily of a somewhat weaker nature, see the work of Constantin et al.
[
3
]
. See also
[
4
]
. The reason for this estimate is that, viewed in Lagrangian coordinates, Euler becomes an ODE, parametrized by
\omega_{0}
and
\Omega
, on the space
\mathcal{M}
of measure-preserving homeomorphisms

\dot{X}_{t}=V_{\omega_{0},\Omega}[X_{t}]

(10)

where, if
\omega_{0}\in L^{1}\cap\mathbf{Y}_{\Theta}
, then the “vector field”
V_{\omega_{0},\Omega}
is Osgood continuous with Lipschitz dependence on the parameters.

Extensions to Navier-Stokes are possible due to the stochastic Lagrangian formulation of Constantin-Iyer
[
2
]
, see also
[
3
,
5
]
.

1
Preliminaries

We start with basic lemmas. Let
\Omega
be
\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
\mathbb{T}^{2}
, or a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with a smooth boundary. Recall the following estimates on the Biot-Savart kernel

Lemma 1

(
[
11
]
)
.

Let
\Delta
denote Dirichlet Laplacian, and
K=\nabla^{\perp}\Delta^{-1}
. Then

1.

|K(x,y)|\leq\frac{C}{|x-y|^{1}}
for some constant
C
,

2.

for
(x_{1},y),(x_{2},y)
satisfying

|x_{1}-x_{2}|\leq\frac{1}{2}\max(|x_{1}-y|,|x_{2}-y|),

(11)

then, for some constant
C
, we have

|K(x_{1},y)-K(x_{2},y)|\leq\frac{C|x_{1}-x_{2}|}{(|x_{1}-y|+|x_{2}-y|)^{2}}

(12)

Similarly, for
(x,y_{1}),(x,y_{2})
satisfying

|y_{1}-y_{2}|\leq\frac{1}{2}\max(|x-y_{1}|,|x-y_{2}|),

(13)

then, for some constant
C
, we have

|K(x,y_{1})-K(x,y_{2})|\leq\frac{C|y_{1}-y_{2}|}{(|x-y_{1}|+|x-y_{2}|)^{2}}.

(14)

We now introduce the notion of an Osgood modulus of continuity

Definition 1
.

For a number
a
,
0<a\leq 1
, a modulus of continuity is an increasing nonzero continuous function
\mu:[0,a]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
such that
\mu(0)=0
.
We call
\mu
an Osgood modulus if

\int_{0}^{a}\frac{dr}{\mu(r)}=\infty.

Lemma 2

(Osgood lemma)
.

Let
\rho:[t_{0},T]\rightarrow[0,a]
be a measurable function,
\gamma:[t_{0},T]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{>0}
be locally integrable, and
\mu:[0,a]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{>0}
be an Osgood modulus of continuity. If for some
c\geq 0
the following holds,

\rho(t)\leq c(1+(t-t_{0}))+\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\gamma(t^{\prime})\mu(\rho(t^{\prime}))dt^{\prime},t\in[t_{0},T],

(15)

then the following holds: if
c>0
, then for
t\in[t_{0},T]
,

-\mathcal{M}(\rho(t))+\mathcal{M}(c)\leq\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\gamma(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime},\mathcal{M}(x)=\int_{x}^{a}\frac{dr}{\mu(r)},

(16)

and if
c=0
, then
\rho=0
.

Lemma 3
.

Suppose that
(\mathcal{X},d)
is a complete metric space with some
\mathrm{Id}\in\mathcal{X}
,
\mu
an Osgood modulus of continuity, and that
S:C([0,T];\mathcal{X})\rightarrow C([0,T];X)
satisfies the following estimate: for every
t\in[0,T]
,

\begin{split}d(S[X](t),S[Y](t))&\leq d(S[X](0),S[Y](0))+\int_{0}^{t}C(\mu(d(X(t^{\prime}),Y(t^{\prime})))+\mu(d(S[x](t^{\prime}),S[Y](t^{\prime}))))dt^{\prime}.\end{split}

(17)

Then
S
has a unique fixed point in
C([0,T];\mathcal{X})\cap\{X(t):X(0)=\mathrm{id}\}
.

Proof.

We employ the Picard scheme:

X^{k+1}=S[X^{k}],\qquad X^{0}=\mathrm{id}

(18)

Then we have

d(X^{n+k}(t),X^{k}(t))\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\mu(d(X^{n+k}(t^{\prime}),X^{k}(t^{\prime})))+\mu(d(X^{n+k-1}(t^{\prime}),X^{k-1}(t^{\prime})))dt^{\prime},

(19)

and taking first supremum over
n
, then using non-decreasing property of
\mu
, and finally by defining

\rho^{k}(t):=\sup_{n}\sup_{0\leq t^{\prime}\leq t}d(X^{n+k}(t^{\prime}),X^{k}(t^{\prime})),

(20)

we have
\rho^{k}(t)\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\mu(\rho^{k}(t^{\prime}))+\mu(\rho^{k-1}(t^{\prime}))dt^{\prime},
and writing
\rho(t)=\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\rho^{k}(t)
we have
\rho(t)\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\mu(\rho(t^{\prime}))dt^{\prime}
. Here we have used Reverse Fatou’s lemma with uniform upper bounds on
\mu(\rho^{k})
using that
\mu
is bounded. This implies that
\rho=0
: in other words,
\{X^{k}\}
is a Cauchy sequence in
(M,d)
. Uniqueness immediately follows from (
17
) and Osgood lemma.
∎

Next we introduce the Yudovich spaces. Let
\mathfrak{C}_{0}
be a large constant (
\mathfrak{C}_{0}=1000
works).

Definition 2
.

Let
\Theta:(\log\mathfrak{C}_{0},\infty)\rightarrow(0,\infty)
be an increasing function satisfying

\int_{\log\mathfrak{C}_{0}}^{\infty}\frac{dp}{p\Theta(p)}=\infty.

(21)

We define the generalized Yudovich space associate with
\Theta
by

\mathbf{Y}_{\Theta}:=\left\{\omega_{0}\in L^{1}(\Omega):\|\omega_{0}\|_{\mathbf{Y}_{\Theta}}:=\sup_{p\in(\log\mathfrak{C}_{0},\infty)}\frac{\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{p}}}{\Theta(p)}<\infty\right\}.

(22)

For each
\Theta
, we define an associated modulus of continuity

\mu_{\Theta}(r):=\begin{cases}r\log(1/r)\Theta(\log(1/r)),0<r\leq\mathfrak{C}_{0}^{-1},\\
\mathfrak{C}_{0}^{-1}\log\mathfrak{C}_{0}\Theta(\log\mathfrak{C}_{0}),r\geq\mathfrak{C}_{0}^{-1}.\end{cases}

(23)

We define the associated function
\mathcal{M}_{\Theta}
by

\mathcal{M}_{\Theta}(r)=\int_{r}^{\mu_{\Theta}(\mathfrak{C}_{0}^{-1})}\frac{ds}{\mu_{\Theta}(s)}.

(24)

It is easy to check that
\mu_{\Theta}
is an Osgood modulus of continuity, thanks to (
21
). Finally, we introduce a new modulus of continuity

\nu_{\Theta}(r)=\exp(-\mathcal{M}_{\Theta}(r)).

(25)

We present the following useful lemma.

Lemma 4
.

Let
Z
be a measure-preserving homeomorphism on
\Omega
, and let
M>0
,
\ell\in[1,\mathfrak{C}_{0}]
. Also let
\omega_{0}\in L^{1}\cap\mathbf{Y}_{\Theta}
. Then for some
q>1
,

\begin{split}\left|\int_{\Omega}\omega_{0}(b)\mathbf{1}_{|Z(b)|\geq 1}(b)\frac{1}{|Z(b)|}db\right|&\leq C\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{1}},\\
\left|\int_{\Omega}\omega_{0}(b)M\mathbf{1}_{|Z(b)|\geq 1}(b)\frac{1}{|Z(b)|^{2}}db\right|&\leq C_{q}M\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{q}},\\
\left|\int_{\Omega}\omega_{0}(b)\mathbf{1}_{1\geq|Z(b)|\geq M}(b)\frac{M}{|Z(b)|^{2}}db\right|&\leq C\mu_{\Theta}(M\mathfrak{C}_{0}^{-1})\|\omega_{0}\|_{\mathbf{Y}_{\Theta}},\\
\left|\int_{\Omega}\omega_{0}(b)\mathbf{1}_{\ell M\geq|Z(b)|}(b)\frac{1}{|Z(b)|}db\right|&\leq\begin{cases}C\mu_{\Theta}(M\mathfrak{C}_{0}^{-1})\|\omega_{0}\|_{\mathbf{Y}_{\Theta}},\qquad M\in(0,1),\\
C_{q}\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{1}\cap L^{2q}}\qquad M\geq 1.\end{cases}\end{split}

(26)

Proof.

The first two inequalities are immediate, and also the case
M\geq 1
: in fact the third inequality is vacuous when
M\geq 1
. We only need to check the last two in the case
M\in(0,1)
. For the third inequality, for each
p>\log\mathfrak{C}_{0}
we have

\begin{split}&\left|\int_{\Omega}\omega_{0}(b)\mathbf{1}_{1\geq|Z(b)|\geq M}(b)\frac{M}{|Z(b)|^{2}}db\right|\leq C\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{p}}M\int_{M}^{1}r^{1-2p^{\prime}}dr\\
&\leq C\|\omega_{0}\|_{\mathbf{Y}_{\Theta}}\Theta(p)\frac{1}{2(1-p^{\prime})}M(1-M^{2(1-p^{\prime})})\leq C\|\omega_{0}\|_{\mathbf{Y}_{\Theta}}\Theta(p)(p-1)M^{1-\frac{2}{p-1}}.\end{split}

(27)

Since the constant
C
does not depend on
p
, we conclude that

\begin{split}\left|\int_{\Omega}\omega_{0}(b)\mathbf{1}_{1\geq|Z(b)|\geq M}(b)\frac{M}{|Z(b)|^{2}}db\right|\leq C\inf_{p>\log\mathfrak{C}_{0}}\|\omega_{0}\|_{\mathbf{Y}_{\Theta}}\Theta(p)(p-1)M^{1-\frac{2}{p-1}}.\end{split}

(28)

Similarly, for
M\in(0,1)
,

\begin{split}&\left|\int_{\Omega}\omega_{0}(b)\mathbf{1}_{\ell M\geq|Z(b)|}(b)\frac{1}{|Z(b)|}db\right|\leq C\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{p}}\int_{0}^{\ell M}r^{1-p^{\prime}}dr\\
&=C\|\omega_{0}\|_{\mathbf{Y}_{\Theta}}\Theta(p)\frac{1}{2-p^{\prime}}\ell^{2-p^{\prime}}M^{2-p^{\prime}}\leq C\|\omega_{0}\|_{\mathbf{Y}_{\Theta}}\Theta(p)M^{1-\frac{1}{p-1}},\end{split}

(29)

using
1/(2-p^{\prime})<C(\mathfrak{C}_{0})
for
p>\log\mathfrak{C}_{0}
and that
\ell\in[1,\mathfrak{C}_{0}]
. Again we conclude that

\begin{split}&\left|\int_{\Omega}\omega_{0}(b)\mathbf{1}_{\ell M\geq|Z(b)|}(b)\frac{1}{|Z(b)|}db\right|\leq C\|\omega_{0}\|_{\mathbf{Y}_{\Theta}}\inf_{p>\log\mathfrak{C}_{0}}\Theta(p)M^{1-\frac{1}{p-1}}.\end{split}

(30)

We obtain the conclusion by taking
p=\log\frac{\mathfrak{C}_{0}}{M}(>\log\mathfrak{C}_{0})
.
∎

2
Yudovich solution operator

Definition 3
.

We denote
\mathcal{M}
by the space of measure-preserving homeomorphisms on
\Omega
, and equip the distance
d(X,Y)=\|X-Y\|_{L^{\infty}}
.

Definition 4
.

For
T>0
and
\omega_{0}\in L^{1}\cap\mathbf{Y}_{\Theta}
, we define
S:C([0,T];\mathcal{M})\rightarrow C([0,T];\mathcal{M})

\begin{split}\tilde{X}&=S[X]=S_{\omega_{0}}[X],\qquad\frac{d}{dt}\tilde{X}(a,t)=v_{X(t)}(\tilde{X}(a,t)),\qquad\tilde{X}(\cdot,0)=\mathrm{id}\\
v_{X}(y)&=\int_{\Omega}K(y,X(b))\omega_{0}(b)db.\end{split}

(31)

In order to make sense of the definition, we need to prove three: first, the Cauchy problem in (
31
) admits the unique solution, second,
\tilde{X}(t)\in\mathcal{M}
, and last,
\tilde{X}(t)
is continuous on
t
. The first claim is immediate: we first note that if
\phi\in L^{1}
and
X\in\mathcal{M}
, then
\phi\circ X,\phi\circ X^{-1}\in L^{1}
and

\int_{\Omega}\phi(x)dx=\int_{\Omega}\phi\circ X(a)da=\int_{\Omega}\phi\circ X^{-1}(x)dx.

Then we observe that if
\omega_{0}\in L^{1}\cap\mathbf{Y}_{\Theta}
, then so is
\omega_{0}\circ X^{-1}
with its norm unchanged. Finally, we see that for each
y\in\Omega
,
x\rightarrow K(y,x)(\omega_{0}\circ X^{-1})(x)
is
L^{1}
: we have

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}\left|K(y,x)(\omega_{0}\circ X^{-1})(x)\right|dx&\leq\int_{|y-x|\geq 1}|\omega_{0}\circ X^{-1}(x)|dx+\int_{|y-x|<1}\frac{1}{|y-x|}|\omega_{0}\circ X^{-1}(x)|dx\\
&\leq\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{1}}+C_{p}\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{p}}\end{split}

for some
p>\log\mathfrak{C}_{0}
. Therefore, we see that
v_{X}(y)=\nabla^{\perp}\Delta^{-1}(\omega_{0}\circ X^{-1})(y)
and it is well-known that for
\omega_{0}\circ X^{-1}\in L^{1}\cap\mathbf{Y}_{\Theta}
,
\nabla^{\perp}\Delta^{-1}(\omega_{0}\circ X^{-1})
has an Osgood modulus of continuity
\varphi_{\Theta}
, depending only on
\Theta
, and thus admitting unique flow.

Next, we prove the last claim. It suffices to show the continuity of
S
on time at
t=0
: other cases can be shown in the same manner.

Lemma 5
.

We have the following estimate: for any
q>2
,

d(\tilde{X}(t),\mathrm{Id})\leq C_{q}\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{1}\cap L^{q}}t+C\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{1}\cap\mathbf{Y}_{\Theta}}\int_{0}^{t}\mu_{\Theta}(d(\tilde{X}(s),\mathrm{id}))ds.

(32)

Proof.

Suppose that
|\tilde{X}(a,t)-a|\leq\mathfrak{C}_{0}^{-1}
. We have

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\tilde{X}(a,t)-a\right)&=\int_{\Omega}\left(K(\tilde{X}(a,t),X(b,t))-K(a,X(b,t))\right)\omega_{0}(b)db\\
&+\int_{\Omega}K(a,X(b,t))\omega_{0}(b)db.\end{split}

(33)

First we see that
\left|\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\Omega}K(a,X(b,t))\omega_{0}(b)db\right|\leq C_{q}\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{1}\cap L^{q}}
for any
q>2
. Next, we let

F_{a}(b)=K(\tilde{X}(a,t),X(b,t))-K(a,X(b,t))

(34)

and

M=\mathfrak{C}_{0}|\tilde{X}(a,t)-a|.

(35)

Now if
|X(b,t)-\tilde{X}(a,t)|\geq M
, then
|X(b,t)-\tilde{X}(a,t)|\geq 2|\tilde{X}(a,t)-a|
(since
\mathfrak{C}_{0}
is large) and

\begin{split}|a-X(b,t)|&\geq|X(b,t)-\tilde{X}(a,t)|-\mathfrak{C}_{0}^{-1}M\geq(1-\mathfrak{C}_{0}^{-1})M\geq 2|\tilde{X}(a,t)-a|,\\
|a-X(b,t)|&\geq|X(b,t)-\tilde{X}(a,t)|-\mathfrak{C}_{0}^{-1}M\geq(1-\mathfrak{C}_{0}^{-1})|X(b,t)-\tilde{X}(a,t)|.\end{split}

On the other hand, if
|X(b,t)-\tilde{X}(a,t)|\leq M
, then

|a-X(b,t)|\leq|X(b,t)-\tilde{X}(a,t)|+|\tilde{X}(a,t)-a|\leq(1+\mathfrak{C}_{0}^{-1})M.

(36)

Therefore, we have

\begin{split}|F_{a}(b)|&\leq\frac{C\mathbf{1}_{|X(b,t)-\tilde{X}(a,t)|\geq 1}(b)}{|X(b,t)-\tilde{X}(a,t)|}+\frac{CM\mathbf{1}_{1\geq|X(b,t)-\tilde{X}(a,t)|\geq M}(b)}{|X(b,t)-\tilde{X}(a,t)|^{2}}+\frac{C\mathbf{1}_{M\geq|X(b,t)-\tilde{X}(a,t)|}(b)}{|X(b,t)-\tilde{X}(a,t)|}\\
&\qquad+\frac{C\mathbf{1}_{(1+\mathfrak{C}_{0}^{-1})M\geq|X(b,t)-a|}(b)}{|X(b,t)-a|},\end{split}

if
M\leq 1
, and if
M>1
, we have
\left|\int_{\Omega}F_{a}(b)\omega_{0}(b)db\right|\leq C_{q}\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{1}\cap L^{q}}
for any
q>2
.
Thus, by Lemma
4

\frac{d}{dt}|\tilde{X}(a,t)-a|\leq C_{q}\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{1}\cap L^{q}}+C\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{1}\cap\mathbf{Y}_{\Theta}}\mu_{\Theta}(|\tilde{X}(a,t)-a|)

(37)

for any
q>2
. Integrating over time gives the desired conclusion.
∎

Next, we show that
\tilde{X}(t)\in\mathcal{M}
. In fact, we show that
\tilde{X}(t)
is measure-preserving and has a definite modulus of continuity (which may deteriorate over time). We first show that
\tilde{X}
is measure-preserving.

Lemma 6
.

For each
t\in[0,T]
,
\tilde{X}(t)
is continuous. Also, for a continuous function
\phi
with compact support,

\int_{\Omega}\phi(\tilde{X}(a,t))da=\int_{\Omega}\phi(y)dy.

(38)

Proof.

We note that if
\omega_{0}\in H^{s}\cap L^{1}\cap\mathbf{Y}_{\Theta}
for sufficiently large
s>1
, then the statement is true: we can define
\nabla_{a}\tilde{X}
, and
\mathrm{det}\nabla_{a}\tilde{X}
can easily be shown to be 1. Now we denote
\omega_{0}^{\varepsilon}=\omega_{0}\star\phi_{\varepsilon}
, where
\phi_{\varepsilon}(x)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\phi(\varepsilon^{-1}x)
with
\phi
being a standard mollifier, and let
\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon}=S_{\omega_{0}^{\varepsilon}}[X].
Since
\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\|\omega_{0}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}\cap\mathbf{Y}_{\Theta}}\leq\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{1}\cap\mathbf{Y}_{\Theta}}<\infty
, we see that associated velocity fields
v_{X}^{(\varepsilon)}
for
S_{\omega_{0}^{\varepsilon}}
have uniform Osgood modulus
\varphi_{\Theta}
.

Next we show that
v_{X}^{(\varepsilon)}
converges to
v_{X}
in
L^{\infty}
: note that for every
y\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,

v_{X}^{(\varepsilon)}(y)-v_{X}(y)=\int_{\Omega}K(y,x)(\omega_{0}-\omega_{0}^{\varepsilon})\circ X^{-1}(x)dx,

and therefore for any
q>2
,
\|v_{X}^{(\varepsilon)}-v_{X}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C_{q}\|\omega_{0}-\omega_{0}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}\cap L^{q}}\rightarrow 0,
as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
.

Finally, we have

\frac{d}{dt}(\tilde{X}-\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon})(a,t)=\left(v_{X}(\tilde{X}(a,t))-v_{X}(\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon}(a,t))\right)+(v_{X}-v_{X}^{(\varepsilon)})(\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon}(a,t))

(39)

and thus

\frac{d}{dt}\|\tilde{X}-\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\varphi_{\Theta}(\|\tilde{X}-\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}})+\|v_{X}^{(\varepsilon)}-v_{X}\|_{L^{\infty}},

(40)

and Osgood’s lemma gives us the uniform convergence of
\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon}
to
\tilde{X}
. This shows that
\tilde{X}
is continuous. Now, since from Lemma
5
and Osgood’s lemma we see that
\sup_{t\in[0,T]}d(\tilde{X}(t),\mathrm{id})\leq C(T,\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{1}\cap\mathbf{Y}_{\Theta}})
, which implies that there exists a constant
C:=C(T,\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{1}\cap\mathbf{Y}_{\Theta}})
such that for every
\varepsilon>0

\mathrm{supp}\phi\circ\tilde{X}_{\varepsilon},\mathrm{supp}\phi\circ\tilde{X}\subset\mathrm{supp}\phi+B_{C}(0).

(41)

Then we obtain the desired result from interchangeability of uniform convergence and integration.
∎

Lemma
6
implies, by density argument, that
\tilde{X}(t)
is measure-preserving. Thus
\tilde{X}(t)\in\mathcal{M}
. Therefore,
S:C([0,T];\mathcal{M})\rightarrow C([0,T];\mathcal{M})
is well-defined.

Next, we show that
\tilde{X}
enjoys a modulus of continuity, which is uniform over
X\in\mathcal{M}
.

Lemma 7
.

We have the following estimate:

-\mathcal{M}_{\Theta}(|\tilde{X}(a,t)-\tilde{X}(b,t)|)\leq-\mathcal{M}_{\Theta}(|a-b|)+C\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{1}\cap\mathbf{Y}_{\Theta}}t.

(42)

Proof.

Let
a,b\in\Omega
, and
M=\mathfrak{C}_{0}|\tilde{X}(a,t)-\tilde{X}(b,t)|
.

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}(\tilde{X}(a,t)-\tilde{X}(b,t))&=\int_{\Omega}\left(K(\tilde{X}(a,t),X(c,t))-K(\tilde{X}(b,t),X(c,t))\right)\omega_{0}(c)dc.\end{split}

(43)

Now if
|X(c,t)-\tilde{X}(a,t)|\geq M
,

\begin{split}|\tilde{X}(b,t)-X(c,t)|&\geq|X(c,t)-\tilde{X}(a,t)|-|\tilde{X}(a,t)-\tilde{X}(b,t)|\geq(1-\mathfrak{C}_{0}^{-1})|X(c,t)-\tilde{X}(a,t)|,\\
|\tilde{X}(a,t)-X(c,t)|&\geq 2|\tilde{X}(a,t)-\tilde{X}(b,t)|,\\
|\tilde{X}(b,t)-X(c,t)|&\geq|X(c,t)-\tilde{X}(a,t)|-|\tilde{X}(a,t)-\tilde{X}(b,t)|\geq 2|\tilde{X}(a,t)-\tilde{X}(b,t)|,\end{split}

(44)

and if
|X(c,t)-\tilde{X}(a,t)|\leq M
,

|\tilde{X}(b,t)-X(c,t)|\leq|X(c,t)-\tilde{X}(a,t)|+|\tilde{X}(a,t)-\tilde{X}(b,t)|\leq(1+\mathfrak{C}_{0}^{-1})|\tilde{X}(a,t)-\tilde{X}(b,t)|.

(45)

To summarize, if
0<M<1
, we have

\begin{split}&\left|K(\tilde{X}(a,t),X(c,t))-K(\tilde{X}(b,t),X(c,t))\right|\leq\frac{CM\mathbf{1}_{|X(c,t)-\tilde{X}(a,t)|\geq 1}(c)}{|\tilde{X}(a,t)-X(c,t)|^{2}}\\
&+\frac{CM\mathbf{1}_{1\geq|X(c,t)-\tilde{X}(a,t)|\geq M}(c)}{|\tilde{X}(a,t)-X(c,t)|^{2}}+\frac{C\mathbf{1}_{|X(c,t)-\tilde{X}(a,t)|\leq M}(c)}{|\tilde{X}(a,t)-X(c,t)|}+\frac{C\mathbf{1}_{|X(c,t)-\tilde{X}(b,t)|\leq(1+\mathfrak{C}_{0}^{-1})M}(c)}{|\tilde{X}(b,t)-X(c,t)|}\end{split}

(46)

for
M\in(0,1)
. For
M\geq 1
, the first term
C\mathbf{1}_{|X(c,t)-\tilde{X}(a,t)|\geq 1}(c)\frac{M}{|\tilde{X}(a,t)-X(c,t)|^{2}}
is replaced by
C_{q}\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{1}\cap L^{q}}
for some
q>2
. Then Lemma
4
gives

\frac{d}{dt}|\tilde{X}(a,t)-\tilde{X}(b,t)|\leq C\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{1}\cap\mathbf{Y}_{\Theta}}\mu_{\Theta}(|\tilde{X}(a,t)-\tilde{X}(b,t)|)

(47)

and integration over time and application of Osgood’s lemma gives us the desired conclusion.
∎

Finally, we prove that (
17
) holds.

Lemma 8
.

Let
\tilde{X}=S_{\omega_{0}}[X]
and
\tilde{Y}=S_{\omega_{1}}[Y]
. Then for every
p>2
,

\frac{d}{dt}d(\tilde{X}(t),\tilde{Y}(t))\leq C\|\omega_{0}-\omega_{1}\|_{L^{1}\cap L^{p}}+C\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{1}\cap\mathbf{Y}_{\Theta}}(\mu_{\Theta}(d(X(t),Y(t)))+\mu_{\Theta}(d(\tilde{X}(t),\tilde{Y}(t)))).

(48)

Proof.

We write
\tilde{X}=S_{\omega_{0}}[X],\ \tilde{Y}=S_{\omega_{1}}[Y]
and

M:=\mathfrak{C}_{0}\max(\|X-Y\|_{L^{\infty}},\|\tilde{X}-\tilde{Y}\|_{L^{\infty}}).

(49)

For any
a\in\Omega
,

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}&(\tilde{X}(a,t)-\tilde{Y}(a,t))=\int_{\Omega}K(\tilde{Y}(a,t),Y(b,t))(\omega_{0}(b)-\omega_{1}(b))db\\
&+\int_{|X(b,t)-\tilde{X}(a,t)|\geq M}\big(K(\tilde{X}(a,t),X(b,t))-K(\tilde{Y}(a,t),X(b,t))\big)\omega_{0}(b)db\\
&+\int_{|X(b,t)-\tilde{X}(a,t)|\geq M}\big(K(\tilde{Y}(a,t),X(b,t))-K(\tilde{Y}(a,t),Y(b,t))\big)\omega_{0}(b)db\\
&+\int_{|X(b,t)-\tilde{X}(a,t)|\leq M}K(\tilde{X}(a,t),X(b,t))\omega_{0}(b)db-\int_{|X(b,t)-\tilde{\tilde{X}}(a,t)|\leq M}K(\tilde{Y}(a,t),Y(b,t))\omega_{0}(b)db.\end{split}

(50)

Suppose first that
M<1
. If

|X(b,t)-\tilde{X}(a,t)|\geq M,

(51)

then

\begin{split}|\tilde{Y}(a,t)-Y(b,t)|&\geq|\tilde{X}(a,t)-X(b,t)|-|\tilde{X}(a,t)-\tilde{Y}(a,t)|-|X(b,t)-Y(b,t)|\\
&\geq(1-2\mathfrak{C}_{0}^{-1})|\tilde{X}(a,t)-X(b,t)|\geq M/2,\\
|\tilde{Y}(a,t)-Y(b,t)|&\geq|\tilde{X}(a,t)-X(b,t)|-\|\tilde{X}-\tilde{Y}\|_{L^{\infty}}-\|X-Y\|_{L^{\infty}}\geq\frac{1}{2}|X(b,t)-\tilde{X}(a,t)|\geq M/2,\\
|\tilde{Y}(a,t)-X(b,t)|&\geq|\tilde{X}(a,t)-X(b,t)|-|\tilde{X}(a,t)-\tilde{Y}(a,t)|\geq\frac{1}{2}|X(b,t)-\tilde{X}(a,t)|\geq M/2\\
|\tilde{Y}(a,t)-X(b,t)|&\geq|\tilde{Y}(a,t)-Y(b,t)|-|X(b,t)-Y(b,t)|\geq\frac{1}{2}|Y(b,t)-\tilde{Y}(a,t)|,\end{split}

so by the first two inequalities, (
11
) is satisfied with
x_{1}=X(a,t)
,
x_{2}=Y(a,t)
,
y=X(b,t)
, and also (
13
) is satisfied with
x=Y(a,t)
,
y_{1}=X(b,t)
, and
y_{2}=Y(b,t)
. Thus, for
a,b
satisfying (
51
) we have

\begin{split}\left|K(\tilde{X}(a,t),X(b,t))-K(\tilde{Y}(a,t),X(b,t))\right|&\leq C\frac{|\tilde{X}(a,t)-\tilde{Y}(a,t)|}{|\tilde{X}(a,t)-X(b,t)|^{2}}\leq C\frac{M}{|\tilde{X}(a,t)-X(b,t)|^{2}},\\
\left|K(\tilde{Y}(a,t),X(b,t))-K(\tilde{Y}(a,t),Y(b,t))\right|&\leq C\frac{|X(b,t)-Y(b,t)|}{|\tilde{Y}(a,t)-Y(b,t)|^{2}}\leq C\frac{M}{|\tilde{X}(a,t)-X(b,t)|^{2}}.\end{split}

On the other hand, if

|X(b,t)-\tilde{X}(a,t)|\leq M,

(52)

then we have

\begin{split}|\tilde{Y}(a,t)-Y(b,t)|&\leq|\tilde{X}(a,t)-X(b,t)|+|\tilde{X}(a,t)-\tilde{Y}(a,t)|+|X(b,t)-Y(b,t)|\leq(1+2\mathfrak{C}_{0}^{-1})M.\end{split}

Let

F_{a}(b)=K(\tilde{X}(a,t),X(b,t))-K(\tilde{Y}(a,t),Y(b,t)).

(53)

We have

\begin{split}|F_{a}(b)|&\leq\frac{CM\mathbf{1}_{|X(b,t)-\tilde{X}(a,t)|\geq 1}(b)}{|\tilde{X}(a,t)-X(b,t)|^{2}}+\frac{CM\mathbf{1}_{1\geq|X(b,t)-\tilde{X}(a,t)|\geq M}(b)}{|\tilde{X}(a,t)-X(b,t)|^{2}}\\
&\qquad+\frac{C\mathbf{1}_{|X(b,t)-\tilde{X}(a,t)|\leq M}(b)}{|\tilde{X}(a,t)-X(b,t)|}+\frac{C\mathbf{1}_{|\tilde{Y}(a,t)-Y(b,t)|\leq(1+2\mathfrak{C}_{0}^{-1})M}(b)}{|\tilde{Y}(a,t)-Y(b,t)|},\\
\end{split}

(54)

and if
M\geq 1
, the first term is replaced by

\frac{C\mathbf{1}_{|X(b,t)-\tilde{X}(a,t)|\geq 1}(b)}{|\tilde{X}(a,t)-X(b,t)|}+\frac{C}{|\tilde{Y}(a,t)-Y(b,t)|},

(55)

and by Lemma
4
we obtain the desired estimate.
∎

Putting
\omega_{0}=\omega_{1}
, we apply Lemma
3
for each
\omega_{0}\in L^{1}\cap\mathbf{Y}_{\Theta}
we have unique fixed point
X=S_{\omega_{0}}[X]
. Now let
X=S_{\omega_{0}}[X],Y=S_{\omega_{1}}[Y]

Theorem 2
.

For every
\omega_{0}\in L^{1}\cap\mathbf{Y}_{\Theta}
there exists a unique weak solution of Euler in
\omega(t)\in L^{1}\cap\mathbf{Y}_{\Theta}
. Moreover, for every
p>2
, there exists a universal constant
C>0
such that

\nu_{\Theta}(d(X(t),Y(t)))\leq\exp(Ct\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{1}\cap\mathbf{Y}_{\Theta}})\nu_{\Theta}(C\|\omega_{0}-\omega_{1}\|_{L^{1}\cap L^{p}}).

(56)

2.1
Comparison between two domains

Let
\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2}
be two bounded, simply connected domains in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with smooth boundaries, and let
\Phi:\Omega_{1}\rightarrow\Omega_{2}
be a measure-preserving diffeomorphism between two. Also we denote

K_{1}=\nabla^{\perp}\Delta_{1}^{-1},\qquad K_{2}=\nabla^{\perp}\Delta_{2}^{-1}
, where
\Delta_{i},i=1,2
are Dirichlet Laplacian on
\Omega_{i}
. Also, we denote
G_{i}^{-1}
be Green’s function for Dirichlet Laplacian on
\Omega_{i}
. We have

\frac{d}{dt}X_{i}(a,t)=\int_{\Omega_{i}}K_{i}(X_{i}(a,t),X_{i}(b,t))\omega_{0}^{i}(b)db,i=1,2.

Now we consider
\tilde{X}_{1}=\Phi^{-1}\circ X_{2}\circ\Phi
: assuming
\omega_{0}^{1}=\omega_{0}^{2}\circ\Phi=\omega_{0}
, we have

\begin{split}&\frac{d}{dt}(\tilde{X}_{1}-X_{1})(a,t)\\
&=\int_{\Omega_{1}}\left(K_{2}(X_{2}(\Phi(a),t),X_{2}(\Phi(b),t))\cdot\nabla\Phi^{-1}(X_{2}(\Phi(a),t))-K_{1}(X_{1}(a,t),X_{1}(b,t))\right)\omega_{0}(b)db\\
&=\int_{\Omega_{1}}\left[K_{2}(\Phi\circ\tilde{X}_{1}(a),\Phi\circ\tilde{X}_{1}(b))\cdot(\nabla\Phi^{-1})\circ\Phi(\tilde{X}_{1}(a))-K_{1}(X_{1}(a,t),X_{1}(b,t))\right]\omega_{0}(b)db.\end{split}

(57)

Next, remark that
K_{1}(x,y)=\nabla_{x}^{\perp}(G_{1}(x,y))
and

K_{2}(\Phi(x),\Phi(y))\cdot(\nabla\Phi^{-1})\circ\Phi(x)=\nabla_{x}^{\perp}(G_{2}(\Phi(x),\Phi(y))).

Therefore, we have

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}(X_{1}-\tilde{X}_{1})(a,t)&=\int_{\Omega_{1}}[K_{1}(X_{1}(a,t),y)-K_{1}(\tilde{X}_{1}(a,t),y)]\omega_{0}\circ X_{1}^{-1}(y)dy\\
&+\int_{\Omega_{1}}\left(K_{1}(\tilde{X}_{1}(a,t),X_{1}(b,t))-K_{1}(\tilde{X}_{1}(a,t),\tilde{X}_{1}(b,t))\right)\omega_{0}(b)db\\
&+\left.\nabla_{x}^{\perp}\left[\int_{\Omega_{1}}\left(G_{1}(x,y)-G_{2}(\Phi(x),\Phi(y))\right)\omega_{0}\circ\tilde{X}_{1}^{-1}(y)dy\right]\right|_{x=\tilde{X}_{1}(a,t)}.\end{split}

(58)

The first and second terms are bounded by, as before,
C\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{1}\cap\mathbf{Y}_{\Theta}}\mu_{\Theta}(|(X_{1}-\tilde{X}_{1}(a,t)|)
. To investigate the last term, we first note that if we write

F(x)=\int_{\Omega_{1}}G_{2}(\Phi(x),\Phi(x^{\prime}))G(x^{\prime})dx^{\prime},

(59)

then
f=F\circ\Phi^{-1},g=G\circ\Phi^{-1}
solves

\Delta f(y)=g(y),y\in\Omega_{2},\qquad f(y)=0,y\in\partial\Omega_{2}.

By the chain rule,
\partial_{y_{i}}(F\circ\Phi^{-1})=(\partial_{y_{i}}\Phi^{-1}_{j})(\partial_{x_{j}}F)\circ\Phi^{-1}=\left[\partial_{x_{j}}^{\perp}\Phi_{i}^{\perp}(\partial_{x_{j}}F)\right]\circ\Phi^{-1},
and we have the following elliptic equation for
(F,G)
:

\left\{\begin{split}LF(x):=&\partial_{k}(\partial_{k}^{\perp}\Phi_{i}\partial_{j}^{\perp}\Phi_{i}\partial_{j}F)(x)=G(x),\qquad x\in\Omega_{1},\\
F(x)&=0,x\in\partial\Omega_{1}.\end{split}\right.

(60)

Now we denote
\psi(x)=\int_{\Omega_{1}}G_{1}(x,y)\omega_{0}\circ\tilde{X}_{1}^{-1}(y)dy
,
\tilde{\psi}(x)=\int_{\Omega_{1}}G_{2}(\Phi(x),\Phi(y))\omega_{0}\circ\tilde{X}_{1}^{-1}(y)dy
. Then the last term is
\left.\nabla_{x}^{\perp}(\psi-\tilde{\psi})(x)\right|_{x=\tilde{X}_{1}(a,t)}
, and
\psi-\tilde{\psi}
solves the following:

\left\{\begin{split}\Delta(\psi-\tilde{\psi})(x)&=(L-\Delta)\tilde{\psi}=\partial_{k}\left[(\partial_{k}^{\perp}\Phi_{i}\partial_{j}^{\perp}\Phi_{i})-\delta_{jk}\right]\partial_{j}\tilde{\psi}(x),\qquad x\in\Omega_{1}\\
(\psi-\tilde{\psi})(x)&=0,x\in\partial\Omega_{1}.\end{split}\right.

(61)

Therefore, the last term is
\nabla^{\perp}\Delta_{1}^{-1}(L-\Delta)\tilde{\psi}=\nabla^{\perp}\Delta_{1}^{-1}(L-\Delta)L^{-1}\omega_{0}\circ\tilde{X}_{1}^{-1}
, and we have, for any
q>2
,

\|\nabla\Delta_{1}^{-1}(L-\Delta)L^{-1}\omega_{0}\circ\tilde{X}_{1}^{-1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C_{q}\|(L-\Delta)L^{-1}\omega_{0}\circ\tilde{X}_{1}^{-1}\|_{L^{q}}\leq C_{q}\|\nabla\Phi-\mathbb{I}\|_{C^{1}}C(\|\nabla\Phi\|_{C^{1}})\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{q}}

by elliptic regularity theory. Thus, we have

Theorem 3
.

\frac{d}{dt}d(X_{1},\tilde{X}_{1})\leq C\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{1}\cap\mathbf{Y}_{\Theta}}\mu_{\Theta}(d(X_{1},\tilde{X}_{1}))+C_{q}\|\nabla\Phi-\mathbb{I}\|_{C^{1}}C(\|\nabla\Phi\|_{C^{1}})\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{q}}
.

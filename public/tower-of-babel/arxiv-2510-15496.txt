The Complex Dimensions of Every Sierpiński Carpet Modification of Dust Type


Abstract
Résumé We investigate modified Sierpiński Carpet fractals, constructed by dividing a square into a square n × n n\times n grid, removing a subset of the squares at each step, and then repeating that process for each square remaining in that grid. If enough squares are removed and in the proper places, we get “Dust Type” carpets, which have a path-connected complement and are themselves not path-connected. We study these fractals using the Fractal Zeta Functions, first introduced by Michel Lapidus, Goran Radunović, and Darko Ẑubrinić in their book Fractal Zeta Functions and Fractal Drums , from which we devised an analytical and combinatorial algorithm to compute the complex dimensions of every Sierpiński Carpet modification of Dust Type.



1
Introduction

1.1
The Tubular Zeta Function

We will study the geometry of subsets
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
via the Tubular Zeta Function, defined in
[
4
]
:

\tilde{\zeta}_{A}(s,\delta)=\int_{0}^{\delta}t^{s-N-1}|A_{t}|\,dt,

(1.1)

where
A_{t}=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:d(x,y)<t\}
and
|\cdot|
is Lebesgue measure in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. This function falls under a class of “Fractal Zeta Functions”, with the Tubular Zeta Function being a truncated Mellin transform of
|A_{t}|
(called the “tube function”).
[
4
]
describes how asymptotically,
|A_{t}|=t^{N-D}(G(t)\log(t^{-1})+O(t^{\alpha}))
as
t\rightarrow 0^{+}
for some
\alpha>0
, where
D
is the Minkowski dimension of
A
and
G(t)
is a nonconstant periodic function of positive amplitude. All the examples studied in this paper will be self-similar fractals or shapes, so the upper and lower Minkowski dimensions will be equal, and both will equal the Box-Counting and Hausdorff dimensions. The asymptotics of the tube function lead to considering the following modified Taylor Series :

|A_{t}|=\sum_{d\in\mathscr{P}(A)}c_{d}t^{N-d},

(1.2)

where
\mathscr{P}(A)
is a multiset, and where the largest real part of an element of
\mathscr{P}(A)
will be exactly
D
. The Mellin transform of
t^{N-d}
is
\frac{1}{s+N-d}
for sufficient conditions on
t
and
s
, so the multiset
\mathscr{P}(A)
corresponds to poles of the Tubular Zeta Function. Thus, it is valuable to find the poles of the Tubular Zeta Function, as it translates to information about the tube function. By
[
4
, Theorem 2.2.1]
, the Tubular Zeta Function is holomorphic on the half-plane
\{s\in\mathbb{C}:\re(s)>D\}
, and we will be taking meromorphic continuations in order to locate the poles. This meromorphic continuation will necessarily be unique by the principle of analytic continuation.

For computation, as a starting example, consider the unit square
A=[0,1]\times[0,1]\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
. When we apply the Tubular Zeta Function to the unit square, we get the following computation :

\displaystyle\tilde{\zeta}_{A}(s,\delta)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\delta}t^{s-3}(1+4t+\pi t^{2})\,dt

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi\delta^{s}}{s}+\frac{4\delta^{s-1}}{s-1}+\frac{\delta^{s-2}}{s-2}.

(1.3)

We can use the principle of analytic continuation to produce a meromorphic continuation of the resulting function to all of
\mathbb{C}
, thus allowing us to effectively ignore the half-plane of convergence for the integral. Using this continuation, we get that the Tubular Zeta Function on the unit square has poles at
s=0,1,2
. We will denote this as follows :

\mathscr{P}(A)=\{0,1,2\}.

(1.4)

This corresponds to the intuitive notion that the unit square is geometrically constructed out of components of Minkowski dimension 0, 1, and 2 (points, lines, and 2-dimensional faces, respectively). We now want to find the poles of the Tubular Zeta Function when we let the set
A
be a fractal.

1.2
Definitions

In this paper, we will define
\mathbb{N}
to not include 0, and we will define
0^{0}=1
for the sake of simplicity in computation.

Definition 1.1

(Sierpiński Carpet Modification).

Let
p\in\mathbb{N}
such that
p\geq 2
. We construct a Sierpiński Carpet Modification by the following algorithm :

(1)

Begin with the unit square
{}_{0}A=[0,1]\times[0,1]\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

(2)

Dissect the square into a uniform
p\times p
grid, producing nonoverlapping squares of side length
\frac{1}{p}
.

(3)

(Produce Level 1 of the fractal construction) Choose a collection of
m\in\mathbb{N}
grid squares to keep, removing all the others. We require
m\in[1,p^{2}-1]\cap\mathbb{N}
in order to avoid considering
\emptyset
and
[0,1]\times[0,1]
. We will call the result
{}_{1}A
.

(4)

(Produce Level
n
of the fractal construction from Level
n-1
) For each square that remains in
{}_{n-1}A
(Level
n-1
of the fractal construction), divide it into its own
p\times p
grid (making nonoverlapping squares of side length
\frac{1}{p^{n}}
) and remove the same collection of squares from each grid. We will call the result
{}_{n}A
.

(5)

Repeat Step
4
infinitely-many times to produce
A=\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}{}_{n}A
.

Definition 1.2

(Dust Type).

Let
A
be a Sierpiński Carpet Modification constructed from a
p\times p
grid where at least 2 squares are kept at each step.
A
is of “Dust Type” if
A^{C}
is path-connected but

itself is not.

\mathscr{P}(A)=\left\{\log_{3}(2)+\frac{2\pi iz}{\ln(3)}:z\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}.

1.4
Preview of Results

We will be studying Dust Type Sierpiński Carpet Modifications in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. The methods that we discuss in this paper are designed to circumvent the manual calculation of the area function. However, we will only be able to find a
maximal set
of poles, or a multiset of
possible
complex dimensions, leaving open the possibility for zero-pole cancellation. We find that in the case of Dust-type Sierpiński Carpet Modifications on
p\times p
grids, we get the following :

\mathscr{P}(A)\subseteq\left\{\log_{p}(m)+\frac{2\pi iz}{\ln(p)}:z\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}\cup\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}{r\in\{1,2,\dots,m-1\}}\\
{\text{(maximum of 3 unique)}}\end{subarray}}\left\{\log_{p}(r)+\frac{2\pi iz}{\ln(p)}:z\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}.

(1.11)

All of the poles we obtain will be at worst simple, except some Dust Type carpets will produce (at worst) poles of order 2 at
s=\log_{p}(1)+\frac{2\pi iz}{\ln(p)}
for all
z\in\mathbb{Z}
. We will be able to determine the values of
r
in Equation
1.11
entirely from visually counting features present in
{}_{1}A,{}_{2}A
, and
{}_{3}A
, as long as particular features are present in
A_{t}
for a positive-measure collection of
t\in[0,\delta]
.

2
Helper Theorems

Theorem 2.1

(Scaling Theorem).

If a measurable set
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
is uniformly scaled by a factor
\lambda>0
, then
\tilde{\zeta}_{\lambda A}(s,\lambda\delta)=\lambda^{s}\tilde{\zeta}_{A}(s,\delta)
.

This is discussed in
[
4
, Proposition 2.2.22]
. For completeness, we provide a proof of this result.

Démonstration.

Let
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
be Lebesgue-measurable, and let
\lambda>0
. Then by definition of the Lebesgue measure in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we have that
|(\lambda A)_{t}|=\lambda^{N}\left|A_{\frac{t}{\lambda}}\right|
. Plugging this into the Tubular Zeta Function formula, we get :

\tilde{\zeta}_{\lambda A}(s,\lambda\delta)=\int_{0}^{\lambda\delta}t^{s-N-1}|(\lambda A)_{t}|\,dt=\int_{0}^{\lambda\delta}t^{s-N-1}\lambda^{N}|A_{\frac{t}{\lambda}}|\,dt.

(2.1)

We will now substitute
\tau=\frac{t}{\lambda}
:

=\lambda\int_{0}^{\delta}\tau^{s-N-1}\lambda^{s-1}|A_{\tau}|\,d\tau=\lambda^{s}\int_{0}^{\delta}\tau^{s-N-1}|A_{\tau}|\,d\tau=\lambda^{s}\tilde{\zeta}_{A}(s,\delta).

(2.2)

∎

Theorem 2.2

(Entire Extension Theorem).

Let
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
be measurable. If
\beta>\alpha>0
, then
F(s)=\int_{\alpha}^{\beta}t^{s-N-1}|A_{t}|\,dt
can be extended to an entire function.

This theorem and the proceeding corollary are discussed in
[
4
, Proposition 2.2.13]
. For completeness, we provide proofs of the results.

Démonstration.

Let
s_{0}\in\mathbb{C}
. By definition of
A_{t}
, we have that
|A_{t}|
is monotonic, continuous, and bounded. So for any
s\in B(s_{0},R)=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z-s_{0}|<R\}
, we get :

|F(s+N+1)|=\left|\int_{\alpha}^{\beta}t^{s}|A_{t}|\,dt\right|\leq\int_{\alpha}^{\beta}|t^{s}||A_{t}|\,dt\leq|A_{\beta}|\int_{\alpha}^{\beta}t^{\re(s)}\,dt.

(2.3)

We have that on
B(s_{0},R)
,
\int_{\alpha}^{\beta}t^{\re(s)}\,dt
is uniformly bounded by
\max\{\alpha^{\re(s_{0})\pm R},\beta^{\re(s_{0})\pm R}\}
, so by the Dominated Convergence Theorem, we have the following :

\displaystyle\left|\lim_{h\rightarrow 0}\frac{F(s+h+N+1)-F(s+N+1)}{h}-\int_{\alpha}^{\beta}\ln(t)t^{s}|A_{t}|\,dt\right|

\displaystyle=\lim_{h\rightarrow 0}\left|\int_{\alpha}^{\beta}\left(\frac{t^{h}-1}{h}-\ln(t)\right)t^{s}|A_{t}|\,dt\right|

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{h\rightarrow 0}\int_{\alpha}^{\beta}\left|\frac{t^{h}-1}{h}-\ln(t)\right|t^{\re(s)}|A_{t}|\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{\alpha}^{\beta}\lim_{h\rightarrow 0}\left|\frac{t^{h}-1}{h}-\ln(t)\right|t^{\re(s)}|A_{t}|\,dt.

By limit properties,
\lim_{h\rightarrow 0}\left|\frac{t^{h}-1}{h}-\ln(t)\right|=0
, and therefore
F(s)
is differentiable at
s_{0}\in\mathbb{C}
. Therefore, since the choice of
s_{0}
was arbitrary,
F(s)
is entire by Goursat’s Theorem.
∎

Corollary 2.1
.

Given arbitrary
\delta_{1}>\delta_{2}>0
,
\tilde{\zeta}_{A}(s,\delta_{1})-\tilde{\zeta}_{A}(s,\delta_{2})
extends to an entire function.

Démonstration.

Consider two choices
\delta_{1},\delta_{2}
with
\delta_{1}>\delta_{2}>0
. Then we get the following :

\tilde{\zeta}_{A}(s,\delta_{1})-\tilde{\zeta}_{A}(s,\delta_{2})=\int_{0}^{\delta_{1}}t^{s-N-1}|A_{t}|\,dt-\int_{0}^{\delta_{2}}t^{s-N-1}|A_{t}|\,dt=\int_{\delta_{1}}^{\delta_{2}}t^{s-N-1}|A_{t}|\,dt.

(2.4)

By Theorem
2.2
, Equation
extends to an entire function. Therefore,
\tilde{\zeta}_{A}(s,\delta_{1})-\tilde{\zeta}_{A}(s,\delta_{2})
extends to an entire function.
∎

Theorem 2.3

(Threshold Theorem).

Let
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a Sierpiński Carpet Modification of Dust Type. Then there exists a maximal
T>0
where the set
A_{T}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:d(x,A)<T\}
is not path-connected.

Démonstration.

Consider the set
\mathscr{T}=\{t>0:A_{t}\text{ is not path-connected}\}
. Since
A
is not path connected, we can consider two connected components
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
of
A
for which there is no continuous path from a point in
E_{1}
to a point in
E_{2}
which goes through
A
. Since
A
is compact, so are
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
by properties of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Since there is no continuous path from
E_{1}
to
E_{2}
, they must be disjoint, and therefore the Hausdorff distance
d(E_{1},E_{2})=\inf_{x\in E_{1},y\in E_{2}}\{d(x,y)\}>0
. Setting
T=\frac{d(E_{1},E_{2})}{2}>0
will then guarantee that
A_{T}
is not path-connected. Therefore,
\mathscr{T}\neq\emptyset
since
T\in\mathscr{T}
.

Furthermore, by construction of
A
, we have that
\diam(A)=\sup_{x\in A,y\in A}\{d(x,y)\}
is finite since
A\subseteq[0,1]\times[0,1]
. Thus, by choosing
\delta>\sqrt{2}
, we get that
A\subseteq B(x,\delta)
for any
x\in A
, which gets that
A_{\delta}
is simply-connected (and thus path-connected). Therefore, we have that
\mathscr{T}
is bounded above by
\sqrt{2}
. Therefore, by completeness of
\mathbb{R}
, there exists a value
T=\sup(\mathscr{T})
which is the maximal number where
A_{T}
is not path-connected.
∎

From here onwards, we will assume that
\delta>0
is large enough that
A_{\delta}
is simply-connected (the proof of Theorem
2.3
justifies that this is always possible). Taken together with Corollary
2.1
, we no longer need to notate
\delta
in the Tubular Zeta Function formula, and we will simply write the following :

\tilde{\zeta}_{A}(s)=\int_{0}^{\delta}t^{s-N-1}|A_{t}|\,dt.

(2.5)

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
and let
\lambda>0
. Then for any
t\geq 0
:

(\lambda A)_{t}=\lambda\left(A_{\frac{1}{\lambda}t}\right).

(2.6)

Démonstration.

Consider the set
(\lambda A)_{t}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:d(x,\lambda A)<t\}
. Consider the substitution
x=\lambda y
. Then we get :

\displaystyle(\lambda A)_{t}

\displaystyle=\{\lambda y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:d(\lambda y,\lambda A)<t\}

\displaystyle=\{\lambda y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:\lambda d(y,A)<t\}

\displaystyle=\left\{\lambda y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:d(y,A)<\frac{1}{\lambda}t\right\}

\displaystyle=\lambda\left\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:d(y,A)<\frac{1}{\lambda}t\right\}

\displaystyle=\lambda\left(A_{\frac{1}{\lambda}t}\right).

Therefore,
(\lambda A)_{t}=\lambda\left(A_{\frac{1}{\lambda}t}\right)
.
∎

Theorem 2.4

(Infinite Thresholds Theorem).

Let
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a Sierpiński Carpet Modification of Dust Type constructed by keeping
m\geq 2
squares from a
p\times p
grid. Let
T>0
be the maximal number such that
A_{T}
is not path-connected, as guaranteed by Theorem
2.3
. Then for each
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
,
\frac{T}{p^{n+1}}
is the maximal number such that every connected component of
A_{\frac{T}{p^{n}}}
is not path-connected.

Démonstration.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
A
is constructed by keeping
m
squares from a
p\times p
grid, we have a self-similar fractal which can be written as follows :

A=\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}\left(\frac{1}{p}A+(x_{i},y_{i})\right),

(2.7)

where
(x_{i},y_{i})
are the corners of the subsquares where we translate
\frac{1}{p}A
in order to line up with the grid. Since this is a recursive definition, we have the following :

A=\bigcup_{j=1}^{m^{n+1}}\left(\frac{1}{p^{n+1}}A+(x_{j},y_{j})\right),

(2.8)

where
(x_{j},y_{j})
are the corners of the subsquares where we translate
\frac{1}{p^{n+1}}A
in order to line up with the grid. Since the Euclidean metric is translation-invariant in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we have the following for each
t\in[0,\delta]
:

A_{t}=\bigcup_{j=1}^{m^{n+1}}\left(\left(\frac{1}{p^{n+1}}A\right)_{t}+(x_{j},y_{j})\right).

(2.9)

Let
T>0
be the maximal number such that
A_{T}
is not path-connected, as guaranteed by Theorem
2.3
. Then by properties of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we have that
A_{\frac{T}{p^{n}}}=(E_{1})_{\frac{T}{p^{n}}}\cup(E_{2})_{\frac{T}{p^{n}}}\cup\dots\cup(E_{k})_{\frac{T}{p^{n}}}
for some
k\leq m^{n}
, where each
(E_{i})_{\frac{T}{p^{n}}}\cap(E_{j})_{\frac{T}{p^{n}}}=\emptyset
for
i\neq j
and each
(E_{j})_{\frac{T}{p^{n}}}
is path-connected. Thus, for each
j=1,\dots,k
, we have

(E_{j})_{\frac{T}{p^{n}}}=\bigcup_{s\in S_{j}}\left(\left(\frac{1}{p^{n+1}}A\right)_{\frac{T}{p^{n}}}+(x_{s},y_{s})\right),

(2.10)

for some
S_{j}\subseteq\{1,2,\dots,m^{n+1}\}
. By Lemma
2.1
, we get the following :

(E_{j})_{\frac{T}{p^{n+1}}}=\bigcup_{s\in S_{j}}\left(\left(\frac{1}{p^{n+1}}A\right)_{\frac{T}{p^{n+1}}}+(x_{s},y_{s})\right)=\bigcup_{s\in S_{j}}\left(\frac{1}{p^{n+1}}A_{T}+(x_{s},y_{s})\right).

(2.11)

Since
t=T
is the maximal number such that
A_{t}
is not path-connected, we therefore have by definition of
E_{j}
that
t=\frac{T}{p^{n+1}}
is the maximal number such that
(E_{j})_{t}
is not path-connected. This applies for each
j=1,\dots,k
, and so
\frac{T}{p^{n+1}}
is the maximal number such that the connected components of
A_{\frac{T}{p^{n}}}
are no longer path-connected.

∎

Definition 3.1

(Completely Dusty).

Let
A
be a Dust Type Sierpiński Carpet Modification constructed from a
p\times p
grid, and let
\alpha>0
be the maximal number such that
A_{\alpha}
is not path-connected (which exists by Theorem
2.3
).
A
is Completely Dusty if
A_{\alpha}
is the union of
m
disjoint copies of
\frac{1}{p}\left(A_{p\alpha}\right)
.

The Ternary Cantor Set discussed earlier in Figure

is an example of a Completely Dusty carpet. It is clear that setting
\alpha=\frac{1}{6}
gets that
A_{\frac{1}{6}}
consists of 2 copies of
A_{\frac{1}{2}}

\frac{1}{3}
.

Theorem 3.1

(Completely Dusty Theorem).

Let
A
be a Completely Dusty Sierpiński Carpet Modification constructed by keeping
m\geq 2
squares from the
p\times p
grid. Then
\tilde{\zeta}_{A}
has a meromorphic continuation to all of
\mathbb{C}
, and

\mathscr{P}(A)\subseteq\left\{\log_{p}(m)+\frac{2\pi iz}{\ln(p)}:z\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}.

(3.1)

Démonstration.

Let
\alpha>0
be the maximal number such that
A_{\alpha}
is not path-connected, as given by Theorem
2.3
. Consider the function
\tilde{A}(t):[\alpha,p\alpha]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that
\tilde{A}(t)=|A_{t}|
whenever
t\in[\alpha,p\alpha]
. Then by Definition
3.1
and Theorem
2.4
, we have that whenever
t\in\left[\frac{\alpha}{p^{k}},\frac{\alpha}{p^{k-1}}\right]
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we get the following :

|A_{t}|=m\left|\left(\frac{1}{p}A\right)_{pt}\right|=\dots=m^{k}\left|\left(\frac{1}{p^{k}}A\right)_{p^{k}t}\right|.

(3.2)

We can assume without loss of generality that
\alpha<\delta\leq p\alpha
. Plugging this into the Tubular Zeta Function formula and using Theorem
2.1
gets :

\displaystyle\tilde{\zeta}_{A}(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{\alpha}^{\delta}t^{s-3}|A_{t}|\,dt+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{\frac{\alpha}{p}}^{\frac{\alpha}{p^{k-1}}}t^{s-3}|A_{t}|\,dt

(3.3)

\displaystyle I(k)

\displaystyle=d_{H}H(1)\frac{h-h^{k-1}-m+h^{k-1}m+m^{k-1}-hm^{k-1}}{(m-1)(h-m)(h-1)}

\displaystyle+d_{V}V(1)\frac{v-v^{k-1}-m+v^{k-1}m+m^{k-1}-vm^{k-1}}{(m-1)(v-m)(h-1)}

\displaystyle+H(1)\frac{m^{k-1}-h^{k-1}}{m-h}+V(1)\frac{m^{k-1}-v^{k-1}}{m-v}+D(1)\frac{m^{k-1}-1}{m-1}+I(1)m^{k-1}.

(4.22)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{I(k)}{p^{ks}}

(4.23)

Thus, we see that the poles of the meromorphic continuation of

5.1
Non-Dust Sierpiński Carpet Modifications

The combinatorial methods described in Section
4
can still be applied to non-Dust Type Sierpiński Carpet Modifications, but the results may not be the possible sets of complex dimensions. To see the distinction, consider the following Sierpiński Carpet Modification on the
3\times 3
grid :

Figure 5.1:
The first 5 steps of the construction of a Sierpiński Carpet Modification.

Applying the combinatorial methods, we would get the following set of possible poles :

\mathscr{P}(A)\subseteq\left\{0+\frac{2\pi iz}{\ln(3)},1+\frac{2\pi iz}{\ln(3)},\log_{3}(7)+\frac{2\pi iz}{\ln(3)}:z\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}.

(5.1)

However, this does not match the results shown in
[
7
, Equation 10.190]
, which were the following :

\displaystyle\mathscr{P}(A)

\displaystyle\subseteq\left\{0+\frac{2\pi iz}{\ln(3)}^{[2]},0^{[3]},\log_{3}(3-\sqrt{2})+\frac{2\pi iz}{\ln(3)},\log_{3}(2)+\frac{2\pi iz}{\ln(3)}^{[2]},\right.

\displaystyle\left.1,\log_{3}(3+\sqrt{2})+\frac{2\pi iz}{\ln(3)},\log_{3}(7)+\frac{2\pi iz}{\ln(3)}:z\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}.

(5.2)

where the
[n]
indicates that the pole is of order
n
(all other poles listed are assumed to be simple). The fact that the combinatorial methods discussed cannot possibly produce poles of
\log_{3}(3\pm\sqrt{2})
is a strong indication that they will not be helpful in determining the set of possible complex dimensions for a non-Dust Sierpiński Carpet Modification. The author is actively researching this topic.

5.2
Menger Sponge Modifications

Definition 5.1
.

We construct a Menger Sponge Modification by the following algorithm :

(1)

Begin with the unit
N
-cube
{}_{0}A=[0,1]\times[0,1]\times\dots\times[0,1]\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
for
N\geq 3
.

(2)

Dissect the
N
-cube into a uniform
p\times\dots\times p
grid, producing nonoverlapping
N
-cubes of side length
\frac{1}{p}
.

(3)

(Produce Level 1 of the fractal construction) Choose a collection of
m
grid
N
-cubes to keep, remove all the others. We require
m\in[1,p^{N}-1]\cap\mathbb{N}
in order to avoid considering
\emptyset
and
{}_{0}A
itself. We will call the result
{}_{1}A
.

(4)

(Produce Level
n
of the fractal construction from Level
n-1
) For each
N
-cube that remains in
{}_{n-1}A
(Level
n-1
of the fractal construction), divide each of them into their own
p\times\dots\times p
grids (making nonoverlapping
N
-cubes of side length
\frac{1}{p^{n}}
) and remove that same collection of
N
-cubes from each grid. We will call the result
{}_{n}A
.

(5)

Repeat Step
4
infinitely-many times to produce
A=\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}{}_{n}A
.

The theorems we proved in Section
2
will apply to this situation as well. Furthermore, the definition of Completely Dusty (
3.1
) and resulting Theorem
3.1
extend nicely to Menger Sponge Modifications, as demonstrated below.

Theorem 5.1

(Completely Dusty Theorem For
\mathbb{R}^{N}
).

Let
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a Menger Sponge Modification constructed by keeping
m\geq 2
squares from the
p\times\dots\times p
grid. Suppose
A
has the property that there exists
\alpha>0
such that
A_{\alpha}
is a disjoint union of
m
copies of
\frac{1}{p}\left(A_{p\alpha}\right)
. Then
\tilde{\zeta}_{A}
has a meromorphic continuation to all of
\mathbb{C}
and

\mathscr{P}(A)\subseteq\left\{\log_{p}(m)+\frac{2\pi iz}{\ln(p)}:z\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}.

(5.3)

Démonstration.

First, note that since
A\subseteq[0,1]\times\dots\times[0,1]\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we have that choosing
\delta>\sqrt{N}
will get that
A\subseteq B(x,\delta)
for any
x\in A
, which makes
A_{\delta}
simply-connected. Thus, the set
\mathscr{T}=\{t>0:A_{t}\text{ is a disjoint union of $m$ copies of $\frac{1}{p}\left(A_{pt}\right)$}\}
is bounded above by
\delta
, and is non-empty in light of the assumption that
\alpha\in\mathscr{T}
. By completeness of
\mathbb{R}
, the set
\mathscr{T}
has a supremum. Without loss of generality, we will assume
\alpha=\sup(\mathscr{T})
.

Since
A
is constructed by keeping
m
squares from a
p\times\dots\times p
grid, we have a self-similar fractal which can be written as follows :

A=\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}\left(\frac{1}{p}A+(x_{1,i},x_{2,i},\dots,x_{N,i})\right),

(5.4)

where
(x_{1,i},\dots,x_{N,i})
are the corners of the subsquares where we translate
\frac{1}{p}A
in order to line up with the grid. Since this is a recursive definition, we have the following for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
:

A=\bigcup_{j=1}^{m^{n}}\left(\frac{1}{p^{n}}A+(x_{1,j},x_{2,j},\dots,x_{N,j})\right),

(5.5)

where
(x_{1,j},\dots,x_{N,j})
are the corners of the subsquares where we translate
\frac{1}{p^{n}}A
in order to line up with the grid. Since the Euclidean metric is translation invariant in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we have the following for each
t\in[0,\delta]
:

A_{t}=\bigcup_{j=1}^{m^{n}}\left(\left(\frac{1}{p^{n}}A\right)_{t}+(x_{1,j},x_{2,j},\dots,x_{N,j})\right).

(5.6)

By Lemma
2.1
, we get the following :

A_{\frac{\alpha}{p^{n}}}=\bigcup_{j=1}^{m^{n}}\left(\left(\frac{1}{p^{n}}A\right)_{\frac{\alpha}{p^{n}}}+(x_{1,s},\dots,x_{N,s})\right)=\bigcup_{j=1}^{m^{n}}\left(\frac{1}{p^{n}}A_{\alpha}+(x_{1,s},\dots,x_{N,s})\right).

(5.7)

Since
t=\alpha
is the maximal number such that
A_{t}
is a disjoint union of
m
copies of
\frac{1}{p}\left(A_{pt}\right)
, we now have that
A_{\frac{\alpha}{p^{n}}}
is a disjoint union of
m^{n}
copies of
\frac{1}{p^{n}}A_{\alpha}
.

Now consider the function
\tilde{A}(t):[\alpha,p\alpha]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that
\tilde{A}(t)=|A_{t}|
whenever
t\in[\alpha,p\alpha]
. Then we have that whenever
t\in\left[\frac{\alpha}{p^{k}},\frac{\alpha}{p^{k-1}}\right]
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we get the following :

|A_{t}|=m\left|\left(\frac{1}{p}A\right)_{pt}\right|=\dots=m^{k}\left|\left(\frac{1}{p^{k}}A\right)_{p^{k}t}\right|.

(5.8)

We can assume without loss of generality that
\alpha<\delta\leq p\alpha
. Plugging this into the Tubular Zeta Function formula and using Theorem
2.1
yields :

\displaystyle\tilde{\zeta}_{A}(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{\alpha}^{\delta}t^{s-N}|A_{t}|\,dt+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{\frac{\alpha}{p}}^{\frac{\alpha}{p^{k-1}}}t^{s-N}|A_{t}|\,dt

(5.9)

\displaystyle=\int_{\alpha}^{\delta}t^{s-N}\tilde{A}(t)\,dt+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{\frac{\alpha}{p^{k}}}^{\frac{\alpha}{p^{k-1}}}t^{s-N}m^{k}\left|\left(\frac{1}{p^{k}}A\right)_{p^{k}t}\right|\,dt

(5.10)

\displaystyle=\int_{\alpha}^{\delta}t^{s-N}\tilde{A}(t)\,dt+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left[\int_{0}^{\frac{\alpha}{p^{k-1}}}t^{s-N}m^{k}\left|\left(\frac{1}{p^{k}}A\right)_{p^{k}t}\right|\,dt\right.

\displaystyle\left.-\int_{0}^{\frac{\alpha}{p^{k}}}t^{s-N}m^{k}\left|\left(\frac{1}{p^{k}}A\right)_{p^{k}t}\right|\,dt\right]

(5.11)

\displaystyle=\int_{\alpha}^{\delta}t^{s-N}\tilde{A}(t)\,dt+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{p^{ks}}\int_{0}^{p\alpha}t^{s-N}m^{k}\left|A_{t}\right|\,dt

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{p^{ks}}\int_{0}^{\alpha}t^{s-N}m^{k}\left|A_{t}\right|\,dt

(5.12)

\displaystyle=\int_{\alpha}^{\delta}t^{s-N}\tilde{A}(t)\,dt+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{m^{k}}{p^{ks}}\int_{\alpha}^{p\alpha}t^{s-N}\tilde{A}(t)\,dt

(5.13)

\displaystyle=\int_{\alpha}^{\delta}t^{s-N}\tilde{A}(t)\,dt+\frac{m}{p^{s}-m}\int_{\alpha}^{p\alpha}t^{s-N}\tilde{A}(t)\,dt.

(5.14)

Since
\tilde{A}(t)
is monotone and
\alpha>0
, we have by Theorem
2.2
that
\int_{\alpha}^{\delta}t^{s-N}\tilde{A}(t)\,dt
and
\int_{\alpha}^{p\alpha}t^{s-N}\tilde{A}(t)\,dt
extend to entire functions. Therefore, the only possible poles of
\tilde{\zeta}_{A}(s)
will be solutions to
p^{s}-m=0
, which yields simple poles for every
s=\log_{p}(m)+\frac{2\pi iz}{\ln(p)}
for all
z\in\mathbb{Z}
. Therefore,
\mathscr{P}(A)\subseteq\left\{\log_{p}(m)+\frac{2\pi iz}{\ln(p)}:z\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}
.
∎

Note that for
\mathcal{C}\subseteq[0,1]

5.1

5.1
(and the earlier Theorem
3.1

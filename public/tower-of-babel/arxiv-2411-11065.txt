Introduction

Let
\varphi
denote Euler’s totient function
for which

\varphi(n)=n\prod_{p\mid n}\Big(1-\frac{1}{p}\Big).

In 1954, Schinzel and Sierpiński
[
7
]

proved that for positive integers
n
, the fractions
\varphi(n)/n

lie dense in the interval
]0,1]
, and in
[
6
]
, Schinzel
proved that
the fractions
\varphi(n+1)/\varphi(n)
lie dense in
]0,\infty[
.
Many research papers on related topics followed.

Recently in 2019, Garcia, Luca,
Shi and Udell proved in
[
2
]

Schinzel-type-theorems on the density of the fractions

\varphi(p+1)/\varphi(p)
and
\varphi(p)/\varphi(p-1)

when
p
runs over the set of primes.

Here we consider fractions of the form
\varphi(an+b)/(cn+d)

and
\varphi(an+b)/\varphi(cn+d)
with
appropriate constants
a,b,c,d
,
but let
n
run through
all positive integers that are at least as big as an appropriate starting value.

It can easily be observed that it suffices to restrict to the case
a,c>0
.
The maximal length
D
of the target interval depends then explicitly
on the parameters
a,b,c
, and on the greatest common divisor

(a,b)
of
a
and
b
.

The following theorem is our first main result.

Theorem 1
.

Let
a\in\mathbb{N}
,
b\in\mathbb{Z}
,
c\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
,
d\in\mathbb{R}
be constants. Put

D:=\frac{a\varphi((a,b))}{c(a,b)}

and

M:=\left\{n\in\mathbb{N};\ n>\max\Big\{-\frac{b}{a},-\frac{d}{c}\Big\}\text{ and }\frac{\varphi(an+b)}{cn+d}\leq D\right\}.

Then the set

L:=\left\{\frac{\varphi(an+b)}{cn+d};\ n\in M\right\}

is dense in the real interval
]0,D]
.
Furthermore, for arbitrary
t\in\mathbb{N}
, we can restrict
L
by any of the following
conditions on
n
such that
L
remains dense in
]0,D]
.

(1)

\frac{an+b}{(a,b)}
is squarefree,

(2)

\frac{an+b}{(a,b)}
and
t
are coprime,

(3)

there is at most one prime factor
p
of
\frac{an+b}{(a,b)}

such that
p\not\equiv 1\mod a
, and if it exists, it has
multiplicity 1 and
a>1
.

We give the proof in Section
3
.

Furthermore, we prove a generalization to
n
in an arbitrary arithmetic
progression
r
mod
m
,
Corollary
10
, which follows almost directly from
Theorem
1
. We also deduce that the
\limsup

of the fractions is
D
and
the
\liminf
is
0
, stated as Corollary
15
.

As our second main result, we prove in Section
4
the following.

Theorem 2
.

Let
a,c\in\mathbb{N}
and
b,d\in\mathbb{Z}
be constants and define

L:=\left\{\frac{\varphi(an+b)}{\varphi(cn+d)};\ n\in\mathbb{N},\ n>\max\Big\{-\frac{b}{a},-\frac{d}{c}\Big\}\right\}.

If

, then

is dense in
]0,\infty[
.
If

, then

,
where the exponent is the number of primes dividing only

, specified by Definition

.

The proof is split into Theorem
18
and
20

Notation.

\mathbb{Z}
denotes the set of all integers,

\mathbb{N}
the set of all
positive integers, and let
\mathbb{N}_{0}:=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. The symbol
\mathbb{P}
denotes the set
of all primes (in
\mathbb{N}
), and
\mathbb{R}
the set of real numbers.
By the letters
p
and

, we always denote primes,
and for
k\in\mathbb{N}
, let
p_{k}
denote the
k
-th prime, whereas

q_{k}
stands for a prime factor of some integer.

For
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}
, let
(a,b):=\gcd(a,b)
with the convention
\gcd(0,0):=0
.
For coprime
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}
,
b>0
,
let
\mathrm{ord}_{b}(a)
denote the multiplicative order
of
a
modulo
b
, i.e.
\mathrm{ord}_{b}(a)
is the smallest positive integer

such
that
a^{n}\equiv 1\mod b
.
For a prime
p
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, we write

p^{k}\mathrel{\|}n
if
p^{k}\mid n
and
p^{k+1}\nmid n
, i.e.,
p^{k}
is the exact
prime power of base
p
that divides

.

More generally, we write
m\mathrel{\|}n
for

,
if
\frac{n}{m}
and
m
are coprime.
A substack

under a sum or product sign means that the sum or
product extends over all prime divisors of
n
, and
a substack
p^{k}\mathrel{\|}n
under a sum or product sign means that the sum or
product extends over all prime powers dividing
n
exactly.
Additional restrictions may also appear.

For a nonzero integer
n
, we denote the radical or squarefree kernel of
n
as

\mathrm{rad}(n):=\prod_{p\mid n}p.

For some
s\in\mathbb{R}
and two functions
f,g:[s,\infty[\to\mathbb{R}
, we write

\lim_{x\to\infty}f(x)/g(x)=1
, and

f(x)=o(g(x))
, if
\lim_{x\to\infty}f(x)/g(x)=0
.
We write
f(x)=O(g(x))
if
there is a constant
C>0

|f(x)|\leq Cg(x)
for all

.
Finally,
\log(x)

2.
Auxiliary Lemmas

Definition 3
.

For
a\in\mathbb{Z}
,
a\neq 0
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
let

\gamma_{n}(a):=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p^{k}\mathrel{\|}a\\
p\mid n\end{subarray}}p^{k}\text{ and }\tau_{n}(a):=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p^{k}\mathrel{\|}a\\
p\nmid n\end{subarray}}p^{k},

thus
\gamma_{n}(a)
is the prime factor part of
a
that shares all
prime divisors with
n
, and
\tau_{n}(a)
is the prime factor part of

a
whose prime divisors are coprime to
n
.

We summarize some basic properties of these arithmetic functions
that can easily
be deduced from their definition and by unique prime factorization.

Lemma 4
.

For
a\in\mathbb{Z}
,
a\neq 0
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
we have

(i)

\gamma_{n}(a)\cdot\tau_{n}(a)=a
,

(ii)

\mathrm{rad}(\gamma_{n}(a))\mid n
,

(iii)

(\tau_{n}(a),n)=1
,

(iv)

\gamma_{n}(a)=\gamma_{\mathrm{rad}(n)}(a)
and
\tau_{n}(a)=\tau_{\mathrm{rad}(n)}(a)

(for
n\neq 0
),

(v)

(\gamma_{n}(a),\tau_{n}(a))=1
.

The multiplicative property of
\varphi
can be restated and
generalized in terms of
\gamma_{n},\tau_{n}
as follows.

Lemma 5
.

For all
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
we have

(i)

\varphi(nm)=\varphi(n)\varphi(m)

if and only if
n,m
are coprime,

(ii)

\varphi(nm)=n\varphi(m)

if and only if
\mathrm{rad}(n)\mid m
,

(iii)

\varphi(nm)=\gamma_{n}(m)\cdot\varphi(\tau_{n}(m))\cdot\varphi(n)
.

Proof.

The first two parts follow immediately from the
definition of
\varphi
and by unique prime factorization.
From Lemma
4
, we know that
m=\gamma_{n}(m)\cdot\tau_{n}(m)
,

\mathrm{rad}(\gamma_{n}(m))\mid n
, and
(\tau_{n}(m),n\gamma_{n}(m))=1
.
Thus, the third part follows immediately from the first parts.
∎

Remark 6

In this sense, the condition
\mathrm{rad}(n)\mid m

of coprimality
(n,m)=1

\mathrm{rad}(n)\mid m

The following version of Mertens’ theorem with primes in

Lemma 7

For coprime

\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\

A proof of Lemma

.
Trivially, it implies Dirichlet’s prime number theorem

Another handy Lemma that we use in Section

Lemma 8

For

Proof of Theorem
1
.

The inclusion
L\subseteq\ ]0,D]
is assured by the
definition of
M
. Let
x\in\ ]0,D[
be an arbitrary
interval point and put
A:=x^{\varphi(a)}
and
B:=D^{\varphi(a)}
. Then
0<A<B
.

Let
a^{\prime}:=a/(a,b)
and
b^{\prime}:=b/(a,b)
, so that
(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})=1
.
Let
(p_{k}^{*})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be the increasing sequence of all primes such
that
p_{k}^{*}\equiv b^{\prime}\mod a^{\prime}
, of which there are infinitely many by
Dirichlet’s prime number theorem, and let
(r_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be the
increasing sequence of integers such that
p_{k}^{*}=a^{\prime}r_{k}+b^{\prime}
.
Then
(a,b)p_{k}^{*}=ar_{k}+b
. (Note that if
b=0
,
then
a^{\prime}=1
and
p_{k}^{*}
is the sequence of all primes.)

Now define

m_{k}:=(a,b)p_{f(k)}^{*}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}e^{Ak}<p\leq e^{Bk}\\
p\equiv 1\mod a\end{subarray}}p

for a monotonically increasing function
f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}

such that
p_{f(k)}^{*}>e^{Bk}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
Since
(a,b)p_{f(k)}^{*}=ar_{f(k)}+b
,
we have
m_{k}\equiv b\mod a
, which means that there is an
n_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

m_{k}=an_{k}+b
. Since
p_{f(k)}^{*}\to\infty
for
k\to\infty
, also

m_{k}\to\infty
and
n_{k}\to\infty
. Hence
we have
n_{k}\in\mathbb{N}
and
n_{k}>\max\{-b/a,-d/c\}
for sufficiently large
k
.
Let
k
also be sufficiently large such that
p\leq e^{Ak}

for all
p\mid(a,b)t
. Then
(t,m_{k}/(a,b))=1

and the numbers
(a,b)
and
p_{f(k)}^{*}
and

\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}e^{Ak}<p\leq e^{Bk}\\
p\equiv 1\mod a\end{subarray}}p
are pairwise
coprime.

Since
1-\frac{1}{p_{f(k)}^{*}}\to 1
for
k\to\infty
,
Lemma
7
yields

\displaystyle\frac{\varphi(m_{k})}{m_{k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\varphi((a,b))}{(a,b)}\cdot\left(1-\frac{1}{p_{f(k)}^{*}}\right)\cdot\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}e^{Ak}<p\leq e^{Bk}\\
p\equiv 1\mod a\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)

\displaystyle\sim\frac{\varphi((a,b))}{(a,b)}\cdot\left(\frac{\log(e^{Ak})}{\log(e^{Bk})}\right)^{1/\varphi(a)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\varphi((a,b))}{(a,b)}\cdot\left(\frac{A}{B}\right)^{1/\varphi(a)}=\frac{\varphi((a,b))}{(a,b)}\cdot\frac{x}{D}.

From
n_{k}\to\infty
for
k\to\infty
and
m_{k}=an_{k}+b
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\varphi(an_{k}+b)}{cn_{k}+d}

\displaystyle=\frac{\varphi(m_{k})}{m_{k}}\cdot\frac{an_{k}+b}{cn_{k}+d}=\frac{\varphi(m_{k})}{m_{k}}\cdot\frac{a}{c}\cdot\frac{n_{k}+b/a}{n_{k}+d/c}

\displaystyle\sim\frac{\varphi((a,b))}{(a,b)}\cdot\frac{x}{D}\cdot\frac{a}{c}=D\cdot\frac{x}{D}=x.

Since
x<D
, we deduce
n_{k}\in M
for sufficiently large
k
,
thus
x\in\ ]0,D[
is the limit of a sequence from
M
. Obviously, any
of the properties (1),(2),(3) is true for

, which gives the statement.
∎

Corollary 10
.

Let
a,m\in\mathbb{N}
,
b,r\in\mathbb{Z}
,
c\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
,
d\in\mathbb{R}
be constants. Put

D:=\frac{a}{c}\cdot\frac{\varphi((am,ar+b))}{(am,ar+b)}

and

M:=\left\{n\in\mathbb{N};\ n>\max\Big\{-\frac{b}{a},-\frac{d}{c}\Big\}\text{ and }\frac{\varphi(an+b)}{cn+d}\leq D\right\}.

Then the set

L:=\left\{\frac{\varphi(an+b)}{cn+d};\ n\in M,\ n\equiv r\mod m\right\}

is dense in
]0,D]
.
Furthermore, for arbitrary
t\in\mathbb{N}
, we can restrict
L
by any of the
following conditions on
n
such that
L
remains dense in
]0,D]
.

(1)

\frac{an+b}{(am,ar+b)}
is squarefree,

(2)

\frac{an+b}{(am,ar+b)}
and
t
are coprime,

(3)

there is at most one prime factor
p\mid\frac{an+b}{(am,ar+b)}

such that
p\not\equiv 1\mod a
, and if it exists, it has
multiplicity 1 and
a>1
.

Proof.

The inclusion
L\subseteq\ ]0,D]
is assured by the definition of

M
. Let
x\in\ ]0,D[
be an interval point
and let
(s_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\mathbb{N}
be a
sequence such that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\varphi(ams_{k}+ar+b)}{cms_{k}+cr+d}=x,

which exists due to Theorem
1
.

Put
n_{k}:=ms_{k}+r
. Then
n_{k}\equiv r\mod m
and

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\varphi(an_{k}+b)}{cn_{k}+d}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\varphi(ams_{k}+ar+b)}{cms_{k}+cr+d}=x.

Since
0<x<D
, it follows that
n_{k}\in M
for all sufficiently large
k
.
Furthermore,

\frac{an_{k}+b}{(am,ar+b)}=\frac{ams_{k}+ar+b}{(am,ar+b)},

and the three additional conditions on
\frac{an_{k}+b}{(am,ar+b)}

follow directly from the choice of
s_{k}
from Theorem
1
.
∎

Remark 13
.

If
r=0
, the congruence
n\equiv r\mod m
is equivalent to
m\mid n
,
which means that an arbitrary divisor
m
of
n
is considered.
If
r=1
, the congruence

implies that

and

are coprime, which rules out a finite set of
divisors of

.
It is also possible to combine these two cases:
Let

be coprime, then
there exist integers
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
xm_{1}+ym_{2}=1

by Bézout’s identity. Now assume that

Then

and
n\equiv 1\mod m_{2}
, which means

and
(m_{2},n)=1
. More generally, since the
Chinese remainder theorem gives a tool to combine a finite set
of congruence relations into a single one, this can then be fed
into Theorem
1
to gain a generalization to systems
of congruences. We do not give the details.

Remark 14

For

Corollary 15
.

For all constants
a\in\mathbb{N}
,
b\in\mathbb{Z}
,
c\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
,
d\in\mathbb{R}
,

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\varphi(an+b)}{cn+d}=\frac{a\varphi((a,b))}{c(a,b)}

and

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\varphi(an+b)}{cn+d}=0.

Proof.

The assertion on the
\liminf
is clear by Theorem
1
,
since the fraction
\frac{\varphi(an+b)}{cn+d}
is negative
only finitely many times, namely if
n<-d/c
.

Let
g:=(a,b)
. Theorem
1
then yields

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\varphi(an+b)}{an+b}\geq\frac{\varphi(g)}{g},

since the sequence of fractions has
D=\varphi(g)/g

as a limit point.

Let
r_{n}:=na/g+b/g
. Then
r_{n}g=an+b
, and thus by Lemma

(iii),

\frac{\varphi(an+b)}{an+b}=\frac{\gamma_{g}(r_{n})\varphi(\tau_{g}(r_{n}))\varphi(g)}{r_{n}g}=\frac{\varphi(\tau_{g}(r_{n}))}{\tau_{g}(r_{n})}\cdot\frac{\varphi(g)}{g}\leq\frac{\varphi(g)}{g},

Remark 16

t_{2}u_{1}\varphi(t_{1})\varphi(u_{2})=t_{1}u_{2}\varphi(t_{2})\varphi(u_{1}).

Since
(t_{2}u_{1},t_{1}u_{2})=1
, it follows that
t_{2}u_{1}\mid\varphi(t_{2})\varphi(u_{1})
, hence

\varphi(t_{2})\varphi(u_{1})\geq t_{2}u_{1}\geq\varphi(t_{2})u_{1}

and thus
u_{1}\leq\varphi(u_{1})
, which implies
u_{1}=1
.
Similarly, we obtain
u_{2}=t_{1}=t_{2}=1
, which contradicts the
assumption that

\mathrm{rad}(t_{1})\neq\mathrm{rad}(t_{2})
or
\mathrm{rad}(u_{1})\neq\mathrm{rad}(u_{2})
.

Assertion
iii):
Let
n=q_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots q_{m}^{k_{m}}
with
m=\omega(n)
be the prime
factorization of
n
. Then any
t\mathrel{\|}n
must be of the form

t=\prod_{i=1}^{m}q_{i}^{e_{i}k_{i}}

for some
e_{1},...,e_{m}\in\{0,1\}
. We thus have 2 choices
for each of the
e_{i}
, which shows that the count of such
exact divisors
t
must be equal to
2^{m}
.
∎

Now we can complete the proof of Theorem
2
by proving
its second statement.

Theorem 20
.

Let
a,c\in\mathbb{N}
and
b,d\in\mathbb{Z}
be constants such that
ad=bc
and define

L:=\left\{\frac{\varphi(an+b)}{\varphi(cn+d)};\ n\in\mathbb{N},\ n>\max\Big\{-\frac{b}{a},-\frac{d}{c}\Big\}\right\}.

Then
\#L=2^{\omega(\tau_{c}(a)\tau_{a}(c))}
.

Proof.

Let
\Phi(n):=\varphi(n)/n
.
Since we assume
ad=bc
, Equation (22) and Lemma
4
(i) yield

\frac{\varphi(an+b)}{\varphi(cn+d)}=\frac{\gamma_{cn+d}(a)}{\gamma_{an+b}(c)}\cdot\frac{\varphi(\tau_{cn+d}(a))}{\varphi(\tau_{an+b}(c))}=\frac{a}{c}\cdot\frac{\Phi(\tau_{cn+d}(a))}{\Phi(\tau_{an+b}(c))}.

(25)

Now we show that

\displaystyle\left\{\frac{\Phi(\tau_{cn+d}(a))}{\Phi(\tau_{an+b}(c))}\,\middle|\,n\in\mathbb{N},\ n>\max\Big\{-\frac{b}{a},-\frac{d}{c}\Big\}\right\}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Phi(\tau_{d}(\gamma_{c}(a)))}{\Phi(\tau_{b}(\gamma_{a}(c)))}\cdot\left\{\frac{\Phi(t)}{\Phi(u)}\,\middle|\,t,u\in\mathbb{N},\ t\mathrel{\|}\tau_{c}(a),\ u\mathrel{\|}\tau_{a}(c)\right\}.

(26)

By Lemma
4
(i), we obtain

\tau_{cn+d}(a)=\tau_{cn+d}(\gamma_{c}(a))\cdot\tau_{cn+d}(\tau_{c}(a)),

where
\tau_{cn+d}(\gamma_{c}(a))=\tau_{d}(\gamma_{c}(a))
by
Lemma
19
(i)
and
t:=\tau_{cn+d}(\tau_{c}(a))
is an exact divisor of
\tau_{c}(a)
.
Thus
\tau_{cn+d}(a)
is of the form

\tau_{cn+d}(a)=\tau_{d}(\gamma_{c}(a))\cdot t

(27)

for a
t\mathrel{\|}\tau_{c}(a)
. Likewise, we can see that

\tau_{an+b}(c)=\tau_{an+b}(\gamma_{a}(c))\cdot\tau_{an+b}(\tau_{a}(c)),

where
\tau_{an+b}(\gamma_{a}(c))=\tau_{b}(\gamma_{a}(c))
by
Lemma
19
(i)
and
u:=\tau_{an+b}(\tau_{a}(c))\mathrel{\|}\tau_{a}(c)
.
Thus
\tau_{an+b}(c)
is of the form

\tau_{an+b}(c)=\tau_{b}(\gamma_{a}(c))\cdot u

(28)

for a
u\mathrel{\|}\tau_{a}(c)
. This shows the first inclusion
\subseteq
in
(
26
).

Now we show the second inclusion
\supseteq
, namely
that for all

t,u
with
t\mathrel{\|}\tau_{c}(a)
,
u\mathrel{\|}\tau_{a}(c)
,
there are infinitely many
n

that satisfy both (
27
)
and (
28
) simultaneously.
Let
\tau_{c}(a)=q_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots q_{m}^{k_{m}}
and

\tau_{a}(c)=r_{1}^{h_{1}}\cdots r_{\ell}^{h_{\ell}}
be their prime factorizations
and let
t,u\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
t\mathrel{\|}\tau_{c}(a)
and
u\mathrel{\|}\tau_{a}(c)
.
Then
t
and
u
must be of the form

t=\prod_{i=1}^{m}q_{i}^{e_{i}k_{i}},\quad u=\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}r_{i}^{f_{i}h_{i}}

(29)

for some
e_{1},...,e_{m},f_{1},...,f_{\ell}\in\{0,1\}
.
Let us define
Q:=q_{1}\cdots q_{m}=\mathrm{rad}(\tau_{c}(a))

and
R:=r_{1}\cdots r_{\ell}=\mathrm{rad}(\tau_{a}(c))
and observe that
Q
and
R

are coprime. By the Chinese remainder theorem, there is an
x\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} x&\equiv d&&\mod c,\\
x&\equiv e_{i}&&\mod q_{i},\text{ for }i=1,...,m\end{aligned}

(30)

and the solutions of this congruence system are exactly
those
n\in\mathbb{Z}
that satisfy
n\equiv x\mod cQ
.
Furthermore, we obtain a
y\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} y&\equiv b&&\mod a,\\
y&\equiv f_{i}&&\mod r_{i},\text{ for }i=1,...,\ell\end{aligned}

(31)

and all solutions are
n\equiv y\mod aR
.
By the first congruence in (
30
)
and (
31
),
there are
n_{0},n_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
x=cn_{0}+d
and
y=an_{1}+b
.
Applying the Chinese remainder theorem again, we obtain
infinitely many
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\displaystyle n\equiv n_{0}

\displaystyle\mod Q,

\displaystyle n\equiv n_{1}

\displaystyle\mod R.

This yields us
u,v\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
n=uQ+n_{0}=vR+n_{1}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle cn+d=ucQ+cn_{0}+d\equiv x

\displaystyle\mod cQ,

\displaystyle an+b=vaR+an_{1}+b\equiv y

\displaystyle\mod aR.

Thus
cn+d
solves the congruence system
(
30
) and
an+b

solves (
31
).
By (
30
),
we have
q_{i}\nmid cn+d
if and only if
e_{i}=1
,
thus
q_{i}\mid t
by (
29
).
Therefore, we obtain
\tau_{cn+d}(\tau_{c}(a))=t
and
thus
\tau_{cn+d}(a)=\tau_{cn+d}(\gamma_{c}(a))\cdot t
.
Using Lemma
19
i),
we obtain
\tau_{cn+d}(a)=\tau_{d}(\gamma_{c}(a))\cdot t
,
thus
n
satisfies (
27
).
Similarly, by (
31
),
we have
r_{i}\nmid an+b
if and only if
f_{i}=1
,
thus
r_{i}\mid u
by (
29
).
Therefore, we obtain
\tau_{an+b}(\tau_{a}(c))=u
and
thus
\tau_{an+b}(c)=\tau_{an+b}(\gamma_{a}(c))\cdot u=\tau_{b}(\gamma_{a}(c))\cdot u
,
which means that we have found infinitely many
n

that satisfy (
27
)
and (
28
).
Combining (
27
)
and (
28
)
with the fact that
(c,t)=(u,a)=1
, we can use the multiplicativity
of
\Phi
to see that for every
n
,

\frac{\Phi(\tau_{cn+d}(a))}{\Phi(\tau_{an+b}(c))}=\frac{\Phi(\tau_{d}(\gamma_{c}(a)))}{\Phi(\tau_{b}(\gamma_{a}(c)))}\cdot\frac{\Phi(t)}{\Phi(u)}

for some
t\mathrel{\|}\tau_{c}(a)
and
u\mathrel{\|}\tau_{a}(c)
.

We have thus proven that (
26
) holds.

Multiplying the elements on both sides of (
26
)
by
\frac{a}{c}

and using (
25
), we obtain

L=\frac{a}{c}\cdot\frac{\Phi(\tau_{d}(\gamma_{c}(a)))}{\Phi(\tau_{b}(\gamma_{a}(c)))}\cdot\left\{\frac{\Phi(t)}{\Phi(u)}\,\middle|\,t,u\in\mathbb{N},\ t\mathrel{\|}\tau_{c}(a),\ u\mathrel{\|}\tau_{a}(c)\right\},

(32)

where
\frac{\varphi(an+b)}{\varphi(cn+d)}
assumes every value on the right side for infinitely many
n
.
Using Lemma
19
(ii), we can see that two distinct pairs of
t,u
in (
32
) yield a different value of
\frac{\Phi(t)}{\Phi(u)}
, hence

\#L=\#\{t\in\mathbb{N};\ t\mathrel{\|}\tau_{c}(a)\}\cdot\#\{u\in\mathbb{N};\ u\mathrel{\|}\tau_{a}(c)\}.

By Lemma
19
(iii) and the additivity of
\omega
, we conclude

\#L=2^{\omega(\tau_{c}(a))}\cdot 2^{\omega(\tau_{a}(c))}\\
=2^{\omega(\tau_{c}(a)\tau_{a}(c))}.

∎

5.
Conditions for
M=\mathbb{N}_{>-b/a}

5.1.
A complete characterization

By Corollary
15
we know that the density interval
]0,D]

in Theorem
1
is the
largest possible, since the sequence of fractions
\varphi(an+b)/(cn+d)

has no limit point in
]-\infty,-\varepsilon[
or
]D+\varepsilon,\infty[

for arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
.
Hence the sequence cannot be dense in
]-2\varepsilon,D]
or
]0,D+2\varepsilon]
.

In this section we observe that there are cases
in which the set
M
in Theorem
1
contains
all

positive integers.
The easiest case is
a=c=1
,
b=d=0
. Then
D=1
and

0<\frac{\varphi(n)}{n}\leq 1.

Hence the arithmetic function
\varphi(n)/n

does not hit any value outside of
]0,1]
, which implies that its image
over all
n\in\mathbb{N}
is dense in
]0,1]
, i. e.
M=\mathbb{N}
.

In Theorem
23
below we provide a complete answer
when this situation happens. For this, we introduce
the following notation.

Definition 22
.

Let
a\in\mathbb{N}
,
b\in\mathbb{Z}
and put
g:=(a,b)
,
a^{\prime}:=a/g
,
b^{\prime}:=b/g
.
Then define

R(a,b):=\{n\in\mathbb{N}_{>-b/a}\ ;\ \mathrm{rad}(a^{\prime}n+b^{\prime})\mid g\},

R_{0}(a,b):=\{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>-b/a}\ ;\ \forall p^{k}\|(a^{\prime}n+b^{\prime}):p\mid g,\>k<\mathrm{ord}_{a^{\prime}}(p)\}

and

\rho(a,b):=\#R(a,b),\ \rho_{0}(a,b):=\#R_{0}(a,b),

which may be
\infty
.

Since
\mathrm{rad}(a^{\prime}n+b^{\prime})
is not defined for
a^{\prime}n+b^{\prime}=0
, we assume
n>-b/a
,
i.e.
a^{\prime}n+b^{\prime}>0
. Note that
R_{0}(a,b)\cap\mathbb{N}\subseteq R(a,b)
.
Since
(a^{\prime},a^{\prime}n+b^{\prime})=(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})=1
by Lemma
9
, we obtain that
all
p\mid(a^{\prime}n+b^{\prime})
are coprime to
a^{\prime}
, and thus
\mathrm{ord}_{a^{\prime}}(p)
occurring in
the definition of
R_{0}(a,b)
exists.

We have
\rho_{0}(a,b)<\infty
for all pairs
a,b
, since
g
determines
an upper bound to the number of distinct prime divisors of
a^{\prime}n+b^{\prime}
, and

\mathrm{ord}_{a^{\prime}}(p)
determines an upper bound to their multiplicity. Furthermore,
note that
\mathrm{rad}(a^{\prime}n+b^{\prime})\mid g
is equivalent to
\tau_{g}(a^{\prime}n+b^{\prime})=1
.

We provide a complete characterization of the condition

M=\mathbb{N}_{>-b/a}
in terms of
\rho(a,b)
with the next theorem.

Theorem 23
.

Let
a\in\mathbb{N},b\in\mathbb{Z},c\in\mathbb{R}_{\neq 0},d\in\mathbb{R}
be constants.
Put
g:=(a,b)
and
a^{\prime}:=a/g
,
b^{\prime}:=b/g
,
r_{n}:=a^{\prime}n+b^{\prime}
. Let

M:=\left\{n\in\mathbb{N};\ n>\max\Big\{-\frac{b}{a},-\frac{d}{c}\Big\}\text{ and }\frac{\varphi(an+b)}{cn+d}\leq\frac{a\varphi(g)}{cg}\right\},\\
R^{\prime}(a,b):=\{n\in\mathbb{N}_{>-b/a};\ \tau_{g}(r_{n})\geq 2\},\\
P(a,b):=\{n\in\mathbb{N};\ r_{n}\in\mathbb{P}\text{ and }r_{n}\nmid g\}.

Clearly
R(a,b)\cup R^{\prime}(a,b)=\mathbb{N}_{>-b/a}
. Then

i)

\#M=\infty
and

M=\left\{n\in\mathbb{N};\ n>-\frac{b}{a}\text{ and }n\geq\frac{\gamma_{g}(r_{n})\varphi(\tau_{g}(r_{n}))}{a^{\prime}}-\frac{d}{c}\right\}.

ii)

\#P(a,b)=\infty
and
P(a,b)\subseteq R^{\prime}(a,b)
. Furthermore,

M\cap R(a,b)\neq\emptyset\implies d/c\geq b/a\implies M=\mathbb{N}_{>-b/a}

and

M\cap P(a,b)\neq\emptyset\implies d/c\geq(b-g)/a\implies R^{\prime}(a,b)\subseteq M.

iii)

M=\mathbb{N}_{>-b/a}
if and only if

d/c\geq\begin{cases}b/a,&\text{if }\rho(a,b)\geq 1,\\
(b-g)/a,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Since
d/c\in\mathbb{R}
and

\frac{\varphi(an+b)}{cn+d}\leq\frac{a\varphi(g)}{cg}\iff\frac{\varphi(an+b)}{n+d/c}\leq\frac{a\varphi(g)}{g},

we may replace
d/c
by
d
and assume
c=1
without loss of generality.

Assertion
i):

\#M=\infty
follows immediately from Theorem
1
.
Note that by Lemma
5
,

\frac{n+d}{\varphi(an+b)}\cdot\frac{a\varphi(g)}{g}=\frac{n+d}{\varphi(r_{n}g)}\cdot a^{\prime}\varphi(g)\\
=\frac{n+d}{\gamma_{g}(r_{n})\varphi(\tau_{g}(r_{n}))\varphi(g)}\cdot a^{\prime}\varphi(g)=\frac{n+d}{\gamma_{g}(r_{n})\varphi(\tau_{g}(r_{n}))/a^{\prime}}.

If
n+d>0
, we deduce

\frac{\varphi(an+b)}{n+d}\leq\frac{a\varphi(g)}{g}\\
\iff 1\leq\frac{n+d}{\varphi(an+b)}\cdot\frac{a\varphi(g)}{g}=\frac{n+d}{\gamma_{g}(r_{n})\varphi(\tau_{g}(r_{n}))/a^{\prime}}\\
\iff\frac{\gamma_{g}(r_{n})\varphi(\tau_{g}(r_{n}))}{a^{\prime}}\leq n+d.

(33)

Now define

M^{\prime}:=\left\{n\in\mathbb{N};\ n>-\frac{b}{a}\text{ and }n\geq\frac{\gamma_{g}(r_{n})\varphi(\tau_{g}(r_{n}))}{a^{\prime}}-d\right\}.

Let
n\in M
. Then
n>\max\{-b/a,-d\}
, which implies
n+d>0
. Furthermore

\frac{\varphi(an+b)}{n+d}\leq\frac{a\varphi(g)}{g},

and (
33
) yields
n\in M^{\prime}
.
Now let
n\in M^{\prime}
. Then
n>-b/a
and

n+d\geq\frac{\gamma_{g}(r_{n})\varphi(\tau_{g}(r_{n}))}{a^{\prime}}>0,

hence
n>-d
, and from (
33
) we obtain
n\in M
.
Thus
M=M^{\prime}
.

Assertion
ii):
By Dirichlet’s prime number theorem there are infinitely many
n
such that

r_{n}=a^{\prime}n+b^{\prime}\in\mathbb{P}
(if
b=0
then
a^{\prime}=1
). Since
g
has only finitely
many prime factors, this implies that
\#P(a,b)=\infty
.

Let
n\in P(a,b)
. Then
(r_{n},g)=1
and thus
\tau_{g}(r_{n})=r_{n}\geq 2
.
Since
r_{n}=a^{\prime}n+b^{\prime}>0
, we get
n>-b^{\prime}/a^{\prime}=-b/a
, thus
n\in R^{\prime}(a,b)
,
so that
P(a,b)\subseteq R^{\prime}(a,b)
.

Assume that there is an
n\in M\cap R(a,b)
. Then
\gamma_{g}(r_{n})=r_{n}

and
\tau_{g}(r_{n})=1
, and by i) we obtain

d\geq\frac{\gamma_{g}(r_{n})\varphi(\tau_{g}(r_{n}))}{a^{\prime}}-n=\frac{r_{n}}{a^{\prime}}-n=\frac{b^{\prime}}{a^{\prime}}=\frac{b}{a}.

Now let
d\geq b/a
. Then for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{>-b/a}
we deduce

\frac{\gamma_{g}(r_{n})\varphi(\tau_{g}(r_{n}))}{a^{\prime}}-d\leq\frac{\gamma_{g}(r_{n})\tau_{g}(r_{n})}{a^{\prime}}-\frac{b}{a}=\frac{r_{n}}{a^{\prime}}-\frac{b^{\prime}}{a^{\prime}}=n.

Therefore
n\in M
and thus
M=\mathbb{N}_{>-b/a}
.

Assume that there is an
n\in M\cap P(a,b)
. Then
\gamma_{g}(r_{n})=1

and
\tau_{g}(r_{n})=r_{n}
, thus

d\geq\frac{\gamma_{g}(r_{n})\varphi(\tau_{g}(r_{n}))}{a^{\prime}}-n=\frac{\varphi(r_{n})}{a^{\prime}}-n=\frac{r_{n}-1}{a^{\prime}}-n=\frac{b^{\prime}-1}{a^{\prime}}=\frac{b-g}{a}.

Now let
d\geq(b-g)/a
and
n\in R^{\prime}(a,b)
. Since
\tau_{g}(r_{n})\geq 2

and
\gamma_{g}(r_{n})\geq 1
, we obtain

\frac{\gamma_{g}(r_{n})\varphi(\tau_{g}(r_{n}))}{a^{\prime}}-d\leq\frac{\gamma_{g}(r_{n})(\tau_{g}(r_{n})-1)}{a^{\prime}}-\frac{b-g}{a}\\
=\frac{r_{n}-\gamma_{g}(r_{n})}{a^{\prime}}-\frac{b^{\prime}-1}{a^{\prime}}=n+\frac{1-\gamma_{g}(r_{n})}{a^{\prime}}\leq n,

which shows that
n\in M
and thus
R^{\prime}(a,b)\subseteq M
.

Assertion
iii): Assume that
M=\mathbb{N}_{>-b/a}
. Since

P(a,b)\neq\emptyset
, we get
M\cap P(a,b)\neq\emptyset
, and then ii)
implies
d/c\geq(b-g)/a
. If
R(a,b)\neq\emptyset
, then

M\cap R(a,b)\neq\emptyset
and
d/c\geq b/a
.

If
d/c\geq b/a
, then ii) implies
M=\mathbb{N}_{>-b/a}
.
If
d/c\geq(b-g)/a
and
R(a,b)=\emptyset
, then
R^{\prime}(a,b)=\mathbb{N}_{>-b/a}

and thus
M=\mathbb{N}_{>-b/a}
.
∎

5.2.
Determination of
\rho(a,b)

For a fixed given pair
a,b
of integers, it is quite difficult to determine
an
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\mathrm{rad}(a^{\prime}n+b^{\prime})\mid g
, i.e.
n\in R(a,b)
,
or show its nonexistence. The following supplementary theorem
allows us to determine the
value of
\rho(a,b)
, at least for some pairs
a,b
.
Theorem
26
then allows us to calculate

\rho(a,b)
for any pair
a,b
, which we perform in Algorithm
28
.

Theorem 24
.

Let
a\in\mathbb{N}
,
b\in\mathbb{Z}
and
g:=(a,b)
,
a^{\prime}:=a/g
,
b^{\prime}:=b/g
.

i)

Assume
g\geq 2
and that at least one of the following
cases is true.

(1)

a=g
,

(2)

\mathrm{rad}(g)\nmid a^{\prime}
,
b=g
,

(3)

b>g
,
\mathrm{rad}(b)\mid g
.

Then
\rho(a,b)=\infty
. Expressed in terms of
\rho
, this is equivalent to

(1)

\rho(a,ak)=\infty
for
a\geq 2
and any
k\in\mathbb{Z}
,

(2)

\rho(kb,b)=\infty
for
b\geq 2
and any
k\in\mathbb{N}

such that
\mathrm{rad}(b)\nmid k
,

(3)

\rho(a,b)=\infty
for
b>g
and
\mathrm{rad}(b)\mid g
.

ii)

There is an
n\in\mathbb{N}_{>-b/a}
such that
a^{\prime}n+b^{\prime}=1
if and only if

b^{\prime}\leq 0
and
a^{\prime}\mid 1-b^{\prime}
. If
\mathrm{rad}(g)\mid a^{\prime}
, this is the only

n\in\mathbb{N}_{>-b/a}
such that
\mathrm{rad}(a^{\prime}n+b^{\prime})\mid g
, hence

\rho(a,b)=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if }b^{\prime}\leq 0\text{ and }a^{\prime}\mid 1-b^{\prime},\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Assertion
i):

\bullet
Case (1):
Assume
a=g\geq 2
, then
a^{\prime}=1
.

Put
n=g^{k}-b^{\prime}
for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
g^{k}>b^{\prime}
and
n>-b/a=-b^{\prime}/a^{\prime}
.
Then
a^{\prime}n+b^{\prime}=g^{k}
and thus
\mathrm{rad}(a^{\prime}n+b^{\prime})=\mathrm{rad}(g)\mid g
.

\bullet
Case (2):
Assume
\mathrm{rad}(g)\nmid a^{\prime}
and
b=g\geq 2
.

There is a
p\mid g
such that
p\nmid a^{\prime}
, which implies

(p,a^{\prime})=1
. Define
k:=\mathrm{ord}_{a^{\prime}}(p)\in\mathbb{N}
to be the multiplicative order
of
p
modulo
a^{\prime}
. Then
p^{hk}\equiv 1\mod a^{\prime}
for all
h\in\mathbb{N}
.
With this, put
n:=(p^{hk}-1)/a^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
for any
h\in\mathbb{N}
with
n>-b/a
. Then

\mathrm{rad}(a^{\prime}n+1)=\mathrm{rad}(p^{hk})=p\mid g.

\bullet
Case (3):
Assume
b>g
and
\mathrm{rad}(b)\mid g
.

Since
(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})=1
, we define
k:=h\cdot\mathrm{ord}_{a^{\prime}}(b^{\prime})+1\geq 2

for any
h\in\mathbb{N}
. Then
(b^{\prime})^{k}=b^{\prime}\mod a^{\prime}
and thus

a^{\prime}\mid(b^{\prime})^{k}-b^{\prime}.

Put
n:=((b^{\prime})^{k}-b^{\prime})/a^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
(because
b^{\prime}>1
). Then
a^{\prime}n+b^{\prime}=(b^{\prime})^{k}

and thus
\mathrm{rad}(a^{\prime}n+b^{\prime})=\mathrm{rad}(b^{\prime})\mid\mathrm{rad}(b)\mid g
.

Assertion
ii): If
n>-b/a=-b^{\prime}/a^{\prime}
, then
a^{\prime}n+b^{\prime}>0
,
and thus
a^{\prime}n+b^{\prime}\geq 1
, since this number is an integer. There is
an
n\in\mathbb{N}
with
a^{\prime}n+b^{\prime}=1
if and only if
n=(1-b^{\prime})/a^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
, which
means
1-b^{\prime}\geq 1
and
a^{\prime}\mid 1-b^{\prime}
.

By Lemma
9
, we have
(a^{\prime},a^{\prime}n+b^{\prime})=(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})=1
for all

n\in\mathbb{N}_{>-b/a}
. If
\mathrm{rad}(g)\mid a^{\prime}
, then
(g,a^{\prime}n+b^{\prime})=1
,
hence
\mathrm{rad}(a^{\prime}n+b^{\prime})\mid g
can only be true if
a^{\prime}n+b^{\prime}=1
.∎

Example 25
.

Theorem
24
does not yet cover all possible
pairs
a,b
. For example, no
statement is given when
a=14
,
b=6
, since then

g=2
,
a^{\prime}=7
,
b^{\prime}=3
.

However, by Theorem
24
ii),
we understand exactly when
n\in\mathbb{N}_{>-b/a}
with
a^{\prime}n+b^{\prime}=1

exists. Since
1
divides any
g
, it suffices
to examine the question when
n\in\mathbb{N}_{>-b/a}
with

\mathrm{rad}(a^{\prime}n+b^{\prime})\mid g
and
a^{\prime}n+b^{\prime}\geq 2
exists.
The following Theorem
26

reduces this problem to a finite question for all
a\in\mathbb{N}
,
b\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Theorem 26
.

Let
a\in\mathbb{N}
,
b\in\mathbb{Z}
and
g:=(a,b)
,
a^{\prime}:=a/g
,
b^{\prime}:=b/g
.
Assume that
\tau_{a^{\prime}}(\mathrm{rad}(g))
has the prime factorization

\tau_{a^{\prime}}(\mathrm{rad}(g))=q_{1}\cdots q_{s}
. Then the following statements
are equivalent.

(1)

\rho(a,b)=\infty
,

(2)

There is an
n\in\mathbb{N}_{>-b/a}
such that
\mathrm{rad}(a^{\prime}n+b^{\prime})\mid g
and

a^{\prime}n+b^{\prime}\geq 2
,

(3)

There are
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{s}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that

\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{s}>0
and

q_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots q_{s}^{\alpha_{s}}\equiv b^{\prime}\mod a^{\prime},

(4)

\mathrm{rad}(g)\nmid a^{\prime}
and there are
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{s}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that

q_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots q_{s}^{\alpha_{s}}\equiv b^{\prime}\mod a^{\prime}.

Proof.

If
\rho(a,b)=\infty
, there exist infinitely many

n\in\mathbb{N}_{>-b/a}
such that
\mathrm{rad}(a^{\prime}n+b^{\prime})\mid g
, hence there exists
one with
a^{\prime}n+b^{\prime}\geq 2
since
a^{\prime}\geq 1
.

Assume there exists an
n\in\mathbb{N}_{>-b/a}
such that
\mathrm{rad}(a^{\prime}n+b^{\prime})\mid g
and

a^{\prime}n+b^{\prime}\geq 2
. Due to Lemma
9
,
(a^{\prime},a^{\prime}n+b^{\prime})=(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})=1
,
thus we obtain
\mathrm{rad}(a^{\prime}n+b^{\prime})\mid\tau_{a^{\prime}}(\mathrm{rad}(g))
. Hence

a^{\prime}n+b^{\prime}=q_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots q_{s}^{\alpha_{s}}\geq 2
for some
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{s}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Since

q_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots q_{s}^{\alpha_{s}}\geq 2
, we have

\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{s}>0
and

q_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots q_{s}^{\alpha_{s}}\equiv b^{\prime}\mod a^{\prime}
.

Assume that there are
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{s}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

such that
\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{s}>0
and

q_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots q_{s}^{\alpha_{s}}\equiv b^{\prime}\mod a^{\prime}
. Then there exists
an
m\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

r:=a^{\prime}m+b^{\prime}=q_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots q_{s}^{\alpha_{s}},

hence
\mathrm{rad}(r)\mid\tau_{a^{\prime}}(\mathrm{rad}(g))
. Since
\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{s}>0
,
we deduce
r\geq 2
and
\tau_{a^{\prime}}(\mathrm{rad}(g))\geq 2
, which implies

\mathrm{rad}(g)\nmid a^{\prime}
.

Finally, assume
\mathrm{rad}(g)\nmid a^{\prime}
and that there exist

\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{s}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that

q_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots q_{s}^{\alpha_{s}}\equiv b^{\prime}\mod a^{\prime}
.
We show that there are infinitely many
n\in\mathbb{N}_{>-b/a}
such that

\mathrm{rad}(a^{\prime}n+b^{\prime})\mid g
, i.e.
\rho(a,b)=\infty
. Let
r\in\mathbb{N}
and
m\in\mathbb{Z}

be as above. First, we assume
r=1
, which implies
b^{\prime}=1-a^{\prime}m
.
By the assumption
\mathrm{rad}(g)\nmid a^{\prime}
, there exists
p\mid g
such that

p\nmid a^{\prime}
and thus
(p,a^{\prime})=1
. Put
k:=\mathrm{ord}_{a^{\prime}}(p)\in\mathbb{N}
, then

p^{hk}\equiv 1\mod a^{\prime}
for all
h\in\mathbb{N}
, thus
a^{\prime}\mid p^{hk}-1
.
Define
\ell_{h}:=(p^{hk}-1)/a^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
, with
h
being chosen sufficiently
large such that
\ell_{h}+m>0
and
\ell_{h}+m>-b/a
. Then

p^{hk}=a^{\prime}\ell_{h}+1=a^{\prime}\ell_{h}+a^{\prime}m+1-a^{\prime}m=a^{\prime}(\ell_{h}+m)+b^{\prime}.

For
n:=\ell_{h}+m\in\mathbb{N}_{>-b/a}
we deduce
\mathrm{rad}(a^{\prime}n+b^{\prime})=p\mid g
, and
since
h
can be arbitrarily large, we obtain infinitely many
n
with
this property, hence
\rho(a,b)=\infty
.

Now let
r\geq 2
. Since
(a^{\prime},r)=1
, we define
k:=\mathrm{ord}_{a^{\prime}}(r)\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then
r^{hk}\equiv 1\mod a^{\prime}
for all
h\in\mathbb{N}
, hence
a^{\prime}\mid r^{hk+1}-r
.
Using this, define
\ell_{h}:=(r^{hk+1}-r)/a^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
, where
h
is chosen
sufficiently large such that
\ell_{h}+m>0
and
\ell_{h}+m>-b/a
, which
is possible because of
r\geq 2
. Then

r^{hk+1}=a^{\prime}\ell_{h}+r=a^{\prime}\ell_{h}+a^{\prime}m+b^{\prime}=a^{\prime}(\ell_{h}+m)+b^{\prime}.

Put
n:=\ell_{h}+m\in\mathbb{N}_{>-b/a}
, then
\mathrm{rad}(a^{\prime}n+b^{\prime})=\mathrm{rad}(r)\mid g
.
Since
h
can be arbitrarily large, we obtain infinitely many
n

with this property, hence
\rho(a,b)=\infty
.∎

Let
a\in\mathbb{N},b\in\mathbb{Z}
be integers such that
\rho(a,b)\geq 2
.
Then there exist positive integers
n_{1}>n_{2}>-b/a
such that

\mathrm{rad}(a^{\prime}n_{i}+b^{\prime})\mid g
for
i=1,2
. Now
a^{\prime}n_{1}+b^{\prime}>a^{\prime}n_{2}+b^{\prime}>0
,
thus
a^{\prime}n_{1}+b^{\prime}\geq 2
, and Theorem
26

yields
\rho(a,b)=\infty
. This implies
\rho(a,b)\in\{0,1,\infty\}

for all
a\in\mathbb{N},b\in\mathbb{Z}
. From Examples
29
,
30
and
31
below,
we know that
\rho
actually hits these three values.

Note that
a^{\prime}
and
b^{\prime}
do not correlate with
g
. It is only required that

a^{\prime}
and
b^{\prime}
are coprime, where
g
can be chosen independently.
In retrospect, we define then
a:=a^{\prime}g
and
b:=b^{\prime}g
.

For every
1\leq i\leq s
, the sequence
q_{i}^{0},q_{i}^{1},q_{i}^{2},q_{i}^{3},\dots

repeats modulo
a^{\prime}
with period
\mathrm{ord}_{a^{\prime}}(q_{i})
.
The question
if there exists a tuple
(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{s})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{s}
such that

q_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots q_{s}^{\alpha_{s}}\equiv b^{\prime}\mod a^{\prime}
can therefore
be answered in finitely many steps using the following algorithm.

Algorithm 27
.

Input:

a\in\mathbb{N},b\in\mathbb{Z}

Define

g:=(a,b)
,
a^{\prime}:=a/g
,
b^{\prime}:=b/g
.

Determine prime factorization

\tau_{a^{\prime}}(\mathrm{rad}(g))=q_{1}\cdots q_{s}
.

For

0\leq\alpha_{1}<\mathrm{ord}_{a^{\prime}}(q_{1})
, …,

0\leq\alpha_{s}<\mathrm{ord}_{a^{\prime}}(q_{s})
:

If

q_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots q_{s}^{\alpha_{s}}\equiv b^{\prime}\mod a^{\prime}
:

Return true.

Return false.

Note that in the case
\mathrm{rad}(g)\mid a^{\prime}
we have
s=0

and Algorithm
27
jumps immediately to the last line
and returns false.

We intend to run this algorithm for many pairs
a,b
, and we
improve upon the running time significantly if we store every possible value
of
q_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots q_{s}^{\alpha_{s}}\mod a^{\prime}
for each pair

a^{\prime},\tau_{a^{\prime}}(\mathrm{rad}(g))
. If it happens that we stumble upon
the same pair
a^{\prime},\tau_{a^{\prime}}(\mathrm{rad}(g))
again, we simply check whether

b^{\prime}\mod a^{\prime}
is contained in the previously stored set.

The next algorithm determines
\rho(a,b)
for all
a\in\mathbb{N}
,
b\in\mathbb{Z}
,
due to Theorem
24
ii).

Algorithm 28
.

Input:

a\in\mathbb{N}
,
b\in\mathbb{Z}

Define

g:=(a,b)
,
a^{\prime}:=a/g
,
b^{\prime}:=b/g
.

Determine prime factorization

\tau_{a^{\prime}}(\mathrm{rad}(g))=q_{1}\cdots q_{s}
.

If

\mathrm{rad}(g)\nmid a^{\prime}

and there are

\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{s}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

with

q_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots q_{s}^{\alpha_{s}}\equiv b^{\prime}\mod a^{\prime}
:

Return

\rho(a,b)=\infty
.

If

b^{\prime}\leq 0
and
a^{\prime}\mid 1-b^{\prime}
:

Return

\rho(a,b)=1
.

Return

\rho(a,b)=0
.

\rho(a,b)=0
.

.

If
\ell>k
, then

and

5.3.
Growth of the counting function of
R(a,b)

We have now seen that
\rho(a,b)=\infty
for infinitely
many pairs
a,b
. Our next question is then: How frequent are
the
n\in R(a,b)
, that is, how quickly does
the counting function
\#\{n\in R(a,b);\ n\leq x\}

grow when
x\to\infty
?

We obtain the following asymptotic formula in terms of the function
\rho_{0}(a,b)\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
from Definition
22
.

Theorem 33
.

Let
a\in\mathbb{N}
,
b\in\mathbb{Z}
and put
g:=(a,b)
,
a^{\prime}:=a/g
,
b^{\prime}:=b/g
.
Let
\tau_{a^{\prime}}(\mathrm{rad}(g))=q_{1}\cdots q_{s}
be the prime factorization of

\tau_{a^{\prime}}(\mathrm{rad}(g))
. For
1\leq i\leq s
, let

k_{i}:=\mathrm{ord}_{a^{\prime}}(q_{i})
. If
\rho(a,b)=\infty
, then
s\geq 1
,

\rho_{0}(a,b)\geq 1
, and for
x\to\infty
we have

\#\{n\in R(a,b);\ n\leq x\}=\frac{\rho_{0}(a,b)}{s!\log(q_{1}^{k_{1}})\cdots\log(q_{s}^{k_{s}})}\cdot\log(x)^{s}+O(\log(x)^{s-1}).

Proof.

Since
\rho(a,b)=\infty
, Theorem
26
yields
\mathrm{rad}(g)\nmid a^{\prime}

and thus
\tau_{a^{\prime}}(\mathrm{rad}(g))\geq 2
and
s\geq 1
. Let
n\in R(a,b)
.
By Lemma
9
,
(a^{\prime},a^{\prime}n+b^{\prime})=(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})=1
, and hence

\mathrm{rad}(a^{\prime}n+b^{\prime})\mid\tau_{a^{\prime}}(\mathrm{rad}(g))
. This means that there are
integers
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{s}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that

a^{\prime}n+b^{\prime}=\prod_{i=1}^{s}q_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}.

Since
q_{i}^{k_{i}}\equiv 1\mod a^{\prime}
, we obtain

b^{\prime}\equiv\prod_{i=1}^{s}q_{i}^{\alpha_{i}+k_{i}h_{i}}\mod a^{\prime}

for all
h_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
\alpha_{i}+k_{i}h_{i}\geq 0
.

Therefore, there is a unique
m\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

a^{\prime}m+b^{\prime}=\prod_{i=1}^{s}q_{i}^{\beta_{i}}

with
\beta_{i}\equiv\alpha_{i}\mod k_{i}
and
0\leq\beta_{i}<k_{i}

for all
1\leq i\leq s
. Note that
m>-b^{\prime}/a^{\prime}
, which implies

m\in R_{0}(a,b)
. This induces a map
f:R(a,b)\to R_{0}(a,b)
that we
use to split
R(a,b)
into pairwise disjoint sets, i.e. the
inverse image
f^{-1}(m)
of each
m\in R_{0}(a,b)
, namely

R(a,b)=\dot{\bigcup_{m\in R_{0}(a,b)}}f^{-1}(m).

Since these sets are disjoint, we obtain

\#\{n\in R(a,b);\ n\leq x\}=\sum_{m\in R_{0}(a,b)}\#\{n\in f^{-1}(m);\ n\leq x\}.

(34)

Let
n\in f^{-1}(m)
. Then
n>0
and

a^{\prime}n+b^{\prime}=(a^{\prime}m+b^{\prime})\prod_{i=1}^{s}q_{i}^{k_{i}h_{i}}
for some

h_{1},\dots,h_{s}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, thus

f^{-1}(m)=\Big\{\frac{(a^{\prime}m+b^{\prime})\prod_{i=1}^{s}q_{i}^{k_{i}h_{i}}-b^{\prime}}{a^{\prime}};\ h_{1},\dots,h_{s}\in\mathbb{N}_{0},\\
\prod_{i=1}^{s}q_{i}^{k_{i}h_{i}}>\frac{b^{\prime}}{a^{\prime}m+b^{\prime}}\Big\}

for all
m\in R_{0}(a,b)
. The condition

\prod_{i=1}^{s}q_{i}^{k_{i}h_{i}}>\frac{b^{\prime}}{a^{\prime}m+b^{\prime}}
ensures
that
f^{-1}(m)
contains only positive integers,
but clearly, this condition only excludes finitely many values.
This means that for
x\to\infty
,

\#\{n\in f^{-1}(m);\ n\leq x\}=F(m,x)+O(1),

(35)

where

F(m,x):=\#\left\{\frac{(a^{\prime}m+b^{\prime})\prod_{i=1}^{s}q_{i}^{k_{i}h_{i}}-b^{\prime}}{a^{\prime}}\leq x;\ h_{1},\dots,h_{s}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\right\}\\
=\#\left\{\prod_{i=1}^{s}q_{i}^{k_{i}h_{i}}\leq\frac{a^{\prime}x+b^{\prime}}{a^{\prime}m+b^{\prime}};\ h_{1},\dots,h_{s}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\right\}.

In this situation, we apply Lemma
8

with
k=s
,
z=\log\frac{a^{\prime}x+b^{\prime}}{a^{\prime}m+b^{\prime}}
,

a_{i}=k_{i}\log q_{i}=\log(q_{i}^{k_{i}})
, therefore we obtain

F(m,x)=\frac{\log\left(\frac{a^{\prime}x+b^{\prime}}{a^{\prime}m+b^{\prime}}\right)^{s}}{s!\log(q_{1}^{k_{1}})\cdots\log(q_{s}^{k_{s}})}+O\left(\log\left(\frac{a^{\prime}x+b^{\prime}}{a^{\prime}m+b^{\prime}}\right)^{s-1}\right),

(36)

note that the implicit constant depends on the variables

q_{1},\dots,q_{s}
,
k_{1},\dots,k_{s}
which in turn only depend
on
a
and
b
and are thus independent of
x
.
Simplifying the argument of the logarithm yields

\log(a^{\prime}x+b^{\prime})^{s}=\log(x)^{s}+O(\log(x)^{s-1})

and thus

\log\left(\frac{a^{\prime}x+b^{\prime}}{a^{\prime}m+b^{\prime}}\right)^{s}=\log(x)^{s}+O(\log(x)^{s-1}).

From (
36
), we obtain

F(m,x)=\frac{\log(x)^{s}}{s!\log(q_{1}^{k_{1}})\cdots\log(q_{s}^{k_{s}})}+O(\log(x)^{s-1}).

Then (
35
) yields

\#\{n\in f^{-1}(m);\ n\leq x\}=\frac{\log(x)^{s}}{s!\log(q_{1}^{k_{1}})\cdots\log(q_{s}^{k_{s}})}+O(\log(x)^{s-1}).

Note that the terms in this asymptotic formula do not depend on
m
.
Finally, by (
34
) and
\#R_{0}(a,b)=\rho_{0}(a,b)
we deduce

\#\{n\in R(a,b);\ n\leq x\}=\frac{\rho_{0}(a,b)}{s!\log(q_{1}^{k_{1}})\cdots\log(q_{s}^{k_{s}})}\cdot\log(x)^{s}+O(\log(x)^{s-1}),

which was to be proved.∎

5.4.
The number of pairs
a,b
with
\rho(a,b)=\infty

Finally, we discuss in this last section the number of
pairs
1\leq a,b\leq x
with
\rho(a,b)=\infty
, which leads us to
an interesting open question. Thus we examine the sum

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq a,b\leq x\\
\rho(a,b)=\infty\end{subarray}}1\leq\sum_{1\leq a,b\leq x}1\leq x^{2}.

(37)

Let
g>1
be a fixed integer.
The primes
q_{i}
with
q_{1}\cdots q_{s}=\tau_{a^{\prime}}(\mathrm{rad}(g))
can be considered as
being independent of
a^{\prime}
, since if we fix the prime divisors

q_{1},\dots,q_{s}
of
g
, then the positive integers
a^{\prime}
need to be taken with

(a^{\prime},q_{1}\cdots q_{s})=1
. Hence, we obtain the lower bound

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq a,b\leq x\\
\rho(a,b)=\infty\end{subarray}}1\geq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a^{\prime}\leq x/g\\
(a^{\prime},g)=1\end{subarray}}\#\{q_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots q_{s}^{\alpha_{s}}\mod a^{\prime};\ \alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{s}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\}.

(38)

Since the terms
q_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\dots q_{s}^{\alpha_{s}}
generate a subgroup
of the multiplicative group mod
a^{\prime}

that contains all powers
q_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}
,
we continue the lower estimate with

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq a,b\leq x\\
\rho(a,b)=\infty\end{subarray}}1

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a^{\prime}\leq x/g\\
(a^{\prime},q_{1}\cdots q_{s})=1\end{subarray}}\#\{q_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\mod a^{\prime};\ \alpha_{1}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\}

(39)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a^{\prime}\leq x/g\\
(a^{\prime},q_{1})=1\end{subarray}}\#\{q_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\mod a^{\prime};\ \alpha_{1}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\}\sum_{d\mid(a^{\prime},q_{2}\cdots q_{s})}\mu(d)

\displaystyle=\sum_{d\mid q_{2}\cdots q_{s}}\mu(d)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tilde{a}\leq x/dg\\
(\tilde{a},q_{1})=1\end{subarray}}\#\{q_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\mod(d\tilde{a});\ \alpha_{1}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{d\mid q_{2}\cdots q_{s}}\mu(d)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tilde{a}\leq x/dg\\
(\tilde{a},q_{1})=1\end{subarray}}\mathrm{ord}_{d\tilde{a}}(q_{1}).

(40)

The last sum appearing in (
40
) and (
41
)
has been studied in the literature, but considered
with mean value in
q_{1}
. The growth of the average
order of elements (and similarly, of one element taken for many moduli)
is a famous question raised by Arnold in
[
1
]
,
see also
[
4
]
.

From the results of Luca and Shparlinski
in
[
5
]
, namely the asymptotic formula

\frac{1}{x}\sum_{n<x}\frac{1}{\varphi(n)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q_{1}<n\\
(n,q_{1})=1\end{subarray}}\mathrm{ord}_{n}(q_{1})=\frac{x}{\log x}\log((1+o(1))b\log\log x/\log\log\log x),

we deduce with
\varphi(n)\gg n/\log\log n
that

\frac{1}{x}\sum_{q_{1}\leq x}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q_{1}<n\leq x\\
(n,q_{1})=1\end{subarray}}\mathrm{ord}_{n}(q_{1})\geq\sum_{x/2<n<x}\varphi(n)\cdot\frac{1}{x}\cdot\frac{1}{\varphi(n)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q_{1}<n\\
(n,q_{1})=1\end{subarray}}\mathrm{ord}_{n}(q_{1})\\
\gg\frac{x}{\log\log x}\cdot\frac{x}{\log(x)}\log(b\log\log x/\log\log\log x)\\
=\frac{x^{2}}{\log x\log\log x}\log(b\log\log x/\log\log\log x)

for some constant
b>0
.
Thus, in the mean value over
q_{1}
, we get a lower bound pretty close
to
x^{2}
. A successor paper of Kim
[
3
]
shows that this lower bound
is also admissible when taking a mean value over
q_{1}
in the shorter range

q_{1}\leq y
with
x^{1-\delta}=o(y)
for some
\delta>0
. But it seems that
no good result is known if
q_{1}
is fixed.

Consider the case that
a^{\prime}
is a prime such that
q_{1}
is a primitive
root mod
a^{\prime}
. Then the powers
q_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}
run through all reduced
residues mod
a^{\prime}
, so that
\mathrm{ord}_{a^{\prime}}(q_{1})=a^{\prime}-1
. This yields
for large
x
that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq a,b\leq x\\
\rho(a,b)=\infty\end{subarray}}1\geq\sum_{1\leq a\leq x}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq b\leq a\\
\rho(a,b)=\infty\end{subarray}}1\geq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x/2g\leq a^{\prime}\leq x/g\\
a^{\prime}\text{prime}\\
q_{1}\text{ primitive root}\mod a^{\prime}\end{subarray}}(a^{\prime}-1)\\
\gg x\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x/2g<a^{\prime}\leq x/g\\
q_{1}\text{ primitive root}\mod a^{\prime}\end{subarray}}1\gg\frac{x^{2}}{\log x},

assuming in the last step Artin’s primitive root conjecture which states that
the number of primes
\leq x
for which some
q

is a primitive root, is
=(1+o(1))x/\log x
.

Question 34
.

Thus we know that the sum

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\leq x\\
(a,q_{1}\cdots q_{s})=1\end{subarray}}\#\{q_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots q_{s}^{\alpha_{s}}\mod a;\ \alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{s}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\}

is trivially bounded by
\ll x^{2}
, and heuristically by

\gg x^{2}/\log x
from unsolved conjectures. The summand is the order of the
subgroup generated by
q_{1},\dots,q_{s}
in the multiplicative group mod
a
.
It remains to be an
open question
if there exists an asymptotic
formula for this quantity, or even to decide if it is
=o(x^{2})
.
This question can be seen as a generalization of Arnold’s problem
in
[
1
]
, cp.
[
4
]
.

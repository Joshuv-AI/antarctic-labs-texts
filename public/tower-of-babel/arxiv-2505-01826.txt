Outline.
In section 2 we review preliminary definitions and introduce terminology and notation that will be used throughout. Section 3 is devoted to proving the main theorem (
1.1

We note that, because we are working with higher categories, that the diagrams that we need to commute can be quite large and so, when we find it may be helpful, we will use a color-coding for such diagrams. A

A simple example of a 2-group is the discrete 2-group

Group cohomology plays an important role in higher category theory

\displaystyle=g_{1}(f(g_{2},\cdots,g_{k+1}))

The un-normalized versions of the above are defined almost precisely the same way, except the definition of

]
, Section 6.5). The normalized version of group cohomology is certainly more convenient for applications “in nature”, e.g. in
group extensions and higher category theory. When we say cocycle or coboundary in the paper,

Group cohomology will play an essential role in our story, especially cohomology for finite
groups. Standard references for group cohomology include
[
6
,
33
]
. Given a homomorphism
\rho:G\rightarrow H
, then any

is endowed with the structure of an

The pullback is a morphism of cochain complexes, hence induce homomorpisms at the level of cocycles, coboundaries and cohomology.

Given a short exact sequence of groups

0\rightarrow L\rightarrow M\rightarrow N\rightarrow 0,

we have the

\cdots\rightarrow H^{k-1}(G,N)\rightarrow H^{k}(G,L)\rightarrow H^{k}(G,M)\rightarrow H^{k}(G,N)\rightarrow H^{k+1}(G,L)\rightarrow\cdots

where the arrows between cohomology groups of the same degree are pushforwards. The degree shifting maps

A

is a map

Note that the operation

and therefore in order for a cup product to be well-defined

must have a ring structure. Cup products will be helpful in the construction of explicit examples of anomalous actions.

\rho^{*}(\sigma_{1}^{3}\sigma_{2})=0.

Now we would like to build an action of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\rtimes_{\sigma_{2}}S_{4}
on a tensor category
\mathcal{C}
. For any symmetric group
S_{n}
, we have the standard categorical permutation action on
\mathcal{C}^{\boxtimes n}
. We also can, in general, twist an action
G\rightarrow\text{Aut}_{\otimes}(\mathcal{C})
by a 2-cocycle
c\in Z^{2}(G,\text{Aut}_{\otimes}(1_{\mathcal{C}}))
where
\text{Aut}_{\otimes}(1_{\mathcal{C}})
is the group of tensor automorphisms of the identity functor in
\mathcal{C}
. By
[
13
]
(proposition 4.14.3), we have a canonical isomorphism between
\text{Aut}_{\otimes}(1_{\mathcal{C}})
and characters of the grading group of
\mathcal{C}
. Therefore if the grading group has
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
as a subgroup, for instance
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
itself, then
\sigma_{2}
is such a 2-cocycle. The twist is given by the composition

g_{*}\circ h_{*}\cong(gh)_{*}\xrightarrow{\sigma_{2}(g,h)1_{(gh)_{*}}}(gh)_{*}

Therefore if we have an
H
-graded fusion category
\mathcal{C}
, where
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\leq H
, (for instance a Tambara-Yamagami category
[
30
]
), then we have a
\sigma_{2}
-twisted permutation action of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\rtimes_{\sigma_{2}}S_{4}
on
\mathcal{C}^{\boxtimes 4}
. Thus if we pick a trivialization
d\omega=\rho^{*}(\sigma_{1}^{3}\sigma_{2})
we can build a
\tilde{\pi}
-anomalous action of
S_{4}
on
\mathcal{C}^{\boxtimes 4}\rtimes_{\omega}\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.

Further generalizing this example we may say:

•

given any group
G=\mathbb{Z}_{n}\rtimes_{c}Q
with
\pi\in Z^{4}(Q,\mathbb{Z}_{n})
and a decomposition of
\pi=cc^{\prime}
where
c\in Z^{2}(Q,\mathbb{Z}_{n})
,

•

a tensor category
\mathcal{C}
that is
\Gamma
-graded such that
\mathbb{Z}_{n}\leq\Gamma

•

an action of
G
on the tensor category
\mathcal{C}
,
\underline{G}\rightarrow\text{Aut}_{\otimes}(\mathcal{C})
,

then we may always twist the
G
-action by
c
and therefore have a new action of
G
on
\mathcal{C}
, which then (after picking a trivialization
d\omega=\rho^{*}(\pi)
) gives a
\pi
-anomalous action of
Q
on
\mathcal{C}\rtimes_{\omega}\mathbb{Z}_{n}
.

The CML/esRAGE ratio as a potential biomarker of early diastolic impairment in asymptomatic type 1 diabetic patients without overt heart disease
Authors: Daiming Wang, Jing Lin, Shiyi Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundHeart failure is a prevalent complication of diabetic microangiopathy, and early identification of myocardial dysfuntion is crucial. Advanced glycation end products (AGEs) bind to their receptor RAGE and further promote diabetic late complications as well as heart failure. Two soluble RAGE isoforms, endogenous secretory RAGE (esRAGE) and the cleaved extracellular fragment of membrane RAGE (collectively termed sRAGE), are believed to exert cardioprotective effects by acting as RAGE decoy receptors. This pilot study aimed to investigate the associations of AGEs, sRAGE and esRAGE with early cardiac dysfunction identified via advanced echocardiography.MethodsWe enrolled 29 type 1 diabetic patients without known heart disease(EF ≥ 50%) and 31 healthy controls. Plasma levels of two major AGEs, carboxymethyllysine(CML) and methylglyoxal-derived hydroimidazolone 1(MG-H1), as well as sRAGE and esRAGE were measured using immunoassay. Conventional, tissue Doppler, and speckle-tracking echocardiography were performed.ResultsIn the diabetic group, global longitudinal strain (GLS) and peak early diastolic velocity at the lateral mitral annulus [e(lat)] were significantly lower, while E/e(lat) was higher. CML levels and CML/esRAGE ratio were increased, and esRAGE was decreased in the diabetic group. Pearson correlation analysis showed GLS was significantly associated with disease duration, total cholesterol (TC), HbA1c, esRAGE, and CML/esRAGE ratio; Univariate linear regression confirmed that higher CML/esRAGE ratio was associated with lower e(lat) (β = -0.006, P = 0.047).Variables with P < 0.1 in univariate regression together with pre-specified clinical covariates including age, gender, HbA1c, disease duration, and heart rate were incorporated into stepwise multiple regression; the model demonstrated that CML/esRAGE was independently associated with e(lat) (β = -0.006, 95%CI: [-0.012, -0.001], P = 0.049).ConclusionElevated CML/esRAGE ratio is associated with impaired left ventricular diastolic function, and may represent a candidate biomarker for early diastolic dysfunction. Our study also suggest that CML/esRAGE ratio may not associated with GLS abnormalities reflecting subclinical systolic dysfunction. All observations from this pilot study require further validation in large independent prospective cohorts.

Introduction

There is a bidirectional relationship between diabetes and heart failure [1]. Both are characterized by high incidence and mortality rates. Epidemiological and prospective studies have confirmed the rising future trend of diabetes, cardiovascular diseases and the coexistence and interaction of these diseases serve as crucial risk factors for diabetic cardiomyopathy [2–4]. The Framingham Study estimated that compared with non-diabetic individuals, men and women with diabetes have a 2-fold and 5-fold increased risk of heart failure, respectively [5]. In the 2013 ACCF/AHA guidelines for heart failure management, diabetes was classified as Stage A of heart failure, indicating that diabetes and metabolic syndrome confer a high risk of heart failure [6]. Therefore, early identification and diagnosis of diabetic cardiomyopathy can help to reverse the occurrence and progression of heart failure [7].

Early traditional echocardiography mainly evaluates ventricular cavity and wall thickness and blood flow velocity, but cannot quantitatively assess myocardial motion [8]. Newer techniques such as tissue Doppler and speckle-tracking echocardiography enable quantitative evaluation of myocardial motion and early detection of subclinical cardiac injury [8]. In clinical practice, echocardiography is not routinely used as the first-line tool for evaluating cardiac complications in diabetic patients [7]. Therefore, it is necessary to explore the value of echocardiography in the early diagnosis of diabetic cardiomyopathy.

Advanced glycation end products (AGEs) are highly active end products formed by non-enzymatic glycation between amino groups of proteins, fatty acids, or nucleic acids and aldehyde groups of reducing sugars [9]. AGEs accumulation occurs during normal aging and is accelerated in diabetes [10].One key mechanism underlying the adverse impacts of AGEs relies on their binding to the receptor for advanced glycation end products (RAGE). As a multiligand transmembrane receptor belonging to the immunoglobulin superfamily, RAGE is widely expressed on multiple cell types within atherosclerotic lesions [11–13]. Carboxymethyllysine (CML) and methylglyoxal-derived hydroimidazolone-1 (MG-H1) are two major AGEs subtypes capable of binding to RAGE. Hyperglycemia promotes AGEs production and upregulates RAGE expression. The interaction between AGEs and RAGE activates multiple signaling pathways including p38, ERK1/2, NF-κB and c-Jun N-terminal kinase (JNK), which subsequently upregulate TGF-β and growth factors, increase myocardial fibrosis and ultimately lead to cardiac dysfunction [14–16]. There are two soluble isoforms of RAGE in circulation, namely soluble RAGE (sRAGE) and endogenous secretory RAGE (esRAGE). sRAGE is generated via proteolytic cleavage of the extracellular domain of membrane-bound RAGE, whereas esRAGE arises from alternative splicing of RAGE pre-mRNA [17, 18]. Both act as decoy receptors to competitively bind AGEs and block the pro-pathological RAGE signaling. sRAGE and esRAGE which related to cardiovascular disease and inflammation in diabetic patients are detectable in the extracellular space and circulation [19, 20].

We hypothesized that AGEs CML and MG-H1, sRAGE and esRAGE are associated with early myocardial damage assessed by echocardiography. This study investigated whether high levels of CML, MG-H1, low levels of sRAGE and esRAGE, and a high AGEs/sRAGE ratio are associated with subclinical cardiac dysfunction in type 1 diabetic patients without known heart disease or autoimmune diseases.

Materials and methods

Study population

This single-center cross-sectional study prospectively enrolled consecutive participants. Patients aged 18–60 years who applied to Endocrinology Clinic of the Second Affiliated Hospital of Shantou University Medical College from December 2019 to December 2020 were enrolled. Healthy subjects were selected as the control group during the same period.

Patients were diagnosed with type 1 diabetes mellitus (T1D) according to the 1999 World Health Organization(WHO) diagnostic criteria for T1D, defined by the presence of all of the following: classic symptoms of polydipsia (excessive thirst), polyphagia (excessive hunger), polyuria (excessive urination) and unintended weight loss(the typical “three polys and one weight loss” presentation) at disease onset, presence of ketosis (ketoacidosis) at onset, dependence on insulin therapy for more than 6 months, and positivity for at least one diabetes-related autoantibody including glutamic acid decarboxylase antibody (GADA), islet cell antibody (ICA), insulinoma-associated antigen 2 antibody (IA-2 A), and zinc transporter 8 antibody (ZnT8A).In addition, patients were aged ≥ 18 years with a disease duration of diabetes mellitus ≥ 1 year.

All patients had normal LVEF (EF ≥ 50%) and no regional wall motion abnormalities on 2D echocardiography. The study protocol was approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of the Second Affiliated Hospital of Shantou University Medical College (Ethical code: 2019 − 159). Written informed consent was obtained from all participants.

Laboratory analysis

Standard biochemical tests were performed in all subjects, including total cholesterol (TC), triglycerides(TG), low-density lipoprotein-cholesterol(LDL-C), fasting blood glucose(FBG), creatinine(Cr), high-sensitivity C-reactive protein(hs-CRP), and urine albumin/creatinine ratio. HbA1c and fasting C-peptide were measured in T1D patients.

Fasting venous blood (4–5 mL) was collected after an overnight fast of at least 8 h into EDTA tubes. Samples were centrifuged at 3000 rpm for 30 min within 30 min of collection. Plasma was separated, aliquoted, and stored at − 80 ℃ to avoid repeated freeze–thaw cycles. Plasma concentrations of CML, MG-H1, esRAGE and sRAGE were quantified by commercial enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) kits. Detailed information of the assay kits is as follows: human CML ELISA kit, human esRAGE ELISA kit and human sRAGE ELISA kit were purchased from Enzyme Immunoassay Company (China, catalog numbers not provided); human MG-H1 ELISA kit was purchased from Yiyan Company (China, catalog number not provided). All detections were performed in accordance with the manufacturers’ instructions. The minimum detectable concentration (limit of detection, LOD) was 10 ng/mL for CML, MG-H1, esRAGE and 10 pg/mL for sRAGE. According to the manufacturer’s specifications, all kits exhibited high assay sensitivity and excellent specificity of human plasma samples. No obvious cross-reactivity was observed between target biomarkers and other structurally similar analogues.

Echocardiography

Echocardiography was performed using a Philips Epiq 7 C system (Philips Healthcare, Amsterdam, the Netherlands) with a 2D adult cardiac probe (frequency range: 1.5–4.0 MHz). All examinations were conducted by two professionally qualified operators who had obtained certification from the Chinese Society of Echocardiography and possessed more than 5 years of clinical experience in cardiac echocardiographic diagnosis. To reduce observation bias, the operators were single-blinded to the study groups, with no access to the participants’ grouping information during the entire examination and measurement process. Subjects were examined in the left lateral decubitus position with simultaneous ECG recording. Conventional measurements included interventricular septal thickness (IVS), left ventricular posterior wall thickness (LVPWd), left ventricular end-diastolic diameter (LVED), left atrial volume index (LAVI), and left ventricular mass index (LVMI). Mitral inflow velocities (E, A) and E/A ratio were recorded by pulsed-wave Doppler. Tissue Doppler imaging was used to measure e(sep) and e(lat). Myocardial performance index (MPI) was calculated as (ICT + IRT)/ET, where ICT denotes isovolumic contraction time, IRT denotes isovolumic relaxation time, and ET denotes ejection time.

Two-dimensional speckle tracking analysis

2D speckle-tracking analysis was performed using QLAB software (Philips Healthcare). The endocardial border was traced at end-systole. The region of interest (ROI) included the entire myocardium. The software automatically divided the left ventricle into 18 segments and calculated segmental longitudinal strain (LS) and global longitudinal strain (GLS). To verify the reproducibility of echocardiographic measurements, intra- and inter-observer variability were evaluated using the intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC). The results showed that ICC values for all measured parameters were greater than 0.85, indicating good intra- and inter-observer reproducibility of the data.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, Version 19.0 (IBM Corp., Released 2010, Armonk, NY, USA). Normality of continuous variables was tested using the Shapiro-Wilk test. Normally distributed measurement data were expressed as mean ± standard deviation; between-group comparisons were performed using independent samples Student’s t-test. Categorical variables were presented as counts (percentages) and analyzed using the chi-square test. Pearson correlation analysis was performed to explore the bivariate association between circulating biomarkers and echocardiographic parameters of myocardial function. Univariate linear regression analysis was first performed to screen potential covariates associated with the diastolic function parameter. Variables with P < 0.1 in univariate analysis, together with pre-specified clinical covariates (age, sex, HbA1c, disease duration, heart rate), were entered into stepwise multivariable linear regression analysis to identify independent predictors. To mitigate the risk of overfitting, the number of covariates included in the final model was limited following the standard 10:1 events per variable rule, which is widely accepted for exploratory clinical studies. Of note, for multiple comparisons, Bonferroni correction was applied to adjust the significance threshold for all correlation and regression analyses; uncorrected P values were also reported for full transparency, given the exploratory nature of this study. All correlation and regression analyses focused on early myocardial damage were performed exclusively in the type 1 diabetic cohort. This study was designed as an exploratory pilot study on novel biomarkers for diabetic cardiomyopathy; therefore, no a priori power calculation was performed. The sample size was determined with reference to previously published exploratory studies in the same field. For this exploratory research, a two-tailed P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant, and P values between 0.05 and 0.10 were defined as borderline significance, which was interpreted as suggestive of potential biological relevance requiring validation in larger cohorts.

Results

Clinical characteristics and standard echocardiographic features

This study included 29 patients with T1D and 31 healthy control subjects. The sample comprised 22 males (36.67%) and 38 females (63.33%), with a mean age of 32.52 ± 9.07 years. The average body mass index (BMI) was calculated to be 22.03 ± 0.96 kg/m². the duration of the T1D patients was 11.38 ± 5.25 years.

The CML level and CML/esRAGE ratio were significantly higher, and esRAGE was significantly lower in the diabetic group (P < 0.05). MG-H1 and sRAGE did not differ significantly between groups. Additional information detailed in Table 1.

Relationship between biomarkers and cardiac function

We further analyzed the correlations of CML, esRAGE, and CML/esRAGE ratio with echocardiographic parameters including IVS, LVPWd, e(lat), E/e(lat), LAVI, LVMI and GLS. No statistically significant correlations were observed between CML and all measured cardiac structural and functional indices (Table 3). however, there is a week negative correlation between esRAGE and GLS, but no correlation between esRAGE and other cardiac function parameters. Regarding the CML/esRAGE ratio, weak correlations were identified with e(lat) (r=−0.257, P = 0.047) and E/e(lat) (r = 0.255, P = 0.049); no significant relationships were found between this ratio and IVS, LVPWd, LAVI or LVMI. Multiple linear regression analysis of GLS

Univariate linear regression analysis was first performed to screen all potential covariates associated with e(lat), which revealed that the CML/esRAGE ratio was significantly negatively associated with e(lat) (β= −0.006, 95% CI: −0.011 to −0.001, P = 0.047). Variables with P < 0.1 in univariate analysis, together with pre-specified clinical covariates including age, gender, HbA1c, disease duration and heart rate, were further incorporated into stepwise multiple linear regression to identify independent predictors of e(lat). After adjustment, the CML/esRAGE ratio remained an independent predictor of reduced e(lat) (β= −0.006, 95% CI: −0.012 to −0.001, P = 0.049).

Discussion

This exploratory preliminary study enrolled young adult T1D patients aged 18–52 years, all receiving intensive insulin therapy (basal-bolus regimen). There were no significant between-group differences in BMI, WHR, HR, SBP, DBP, or statin use—factors known to influence echocardiographic measurements [21], which supports the comparability of the two groups.

In this study, CML levels were observed to be elevated in diabetic patients compared with healthy individuals (P < 0.05). AGEs are formed via non-enzymatic glycation (Maillard reaction) between reducing sugars and proteins, CML being the most abundant and well-characterized species [22]. The observed higher serum CML in the T1D group is consistent with the effects of chronic hyperglycemia and the metabolic memory phenomenon, whereby AGEs may continue to accumulate even after glycemic control is achieved [23].

Methylglyoxal (MG) is a highly reactive dicarbonyl metabolite of glycolysis that reacts with protein arginine residues to form MG-H1 [24]. In our study, MG-H1 levels did not differ significantly between groups, which is inconsistent with some previous reports. Kilhovd et al. reported increased serum MG-H1 in patients with type 2 diabetes [25], and Karachalias et al. demonstrated increased MG-H1 adducts in the retina of streptozotocin-induced diabetic rats [24]. In the present study, the mean MG-H1 level in the diabetic group was numerically higher than that in the control group, though the difference was not statistically significant, which may be attributed to the limited sample size of this pilot study.

Notably, circulating sRAGE levels were comparable between groups. Previous findings regarding sRAGE in T1D are controversial: Karachalias et al. reported decreased sRAGE at the time of seroconversion to diabetes-related autoantibodies [24], whereas Challier et al. found elevated sRAGE in patients with long-standing T1D [26]. Disease context strongly modulates sRAGE levels; sRAGE is generally decreased in cardiovascular diseases, atherosclerosis, hypertension and chronic heart failure, but increased in diabetes with renal complications [27, 28]. The Gly82Ser polymorphism of the RAGE gene may also affect measured sRAGE concentrations [29]. The lack of a significant difference in our study may be partly due to the limited sample size.

In contrast, serum esRAGE was significantly lower in the T1D group, in agreement with previous studies. Katakami et al. demonstrated markedly reduced esRAGE in patients with T1D [30]. Decreased esRAGE is not unique to diabetes, it is also observed in most inflammatory and autoimmune disorders [28].

Of the two AGEs/receptor ratios examined, CML/esRAGE was significantly increased in the T1D group, whereas MG-H1/esRAGE was not. The AGEs/esRAGE ratio has been proposed as a more robust biomarker candidate than individual AGEs or sRAGE levels in previous studies [28]. Prasad K et al. identified AGEs/esRAGE as an independent risk factor for diabetic macrovascular complications [28]. The elevated CML/esRAGE observed in our study may reflect the combined effect of increased CML and decreased cardioprotective esRAGE.

Structural parameters including IVS, LVPWd, and LVMI were significantly increased in the T1D group, which may suggest early adaptive hypertrophy or interstitial fibrosis. Diabetic patients also presented a higher LAVI, suggesting elevated left atrial filling pressure and impaired diastolic function, which is consistent with previous studies [21].

Among diastolic indices, early diastolic myocardial velocity at the lateral mitral annulus [e(lat)] was decreased and E/e(lat) was increased in the T1D group. The e′ velocity measured by tissue Doppler imaging is a reliable marker of diastolic function and is determined by myocardial relaxation, elastic recoil, and loading conditions. Reduced e(lat) may suggest impaired relaxation in diabetic patients. No significant change was observed in e(sep), which may be explained by differences in myocardial fiber architecture: longitudinal and circular fibers abundant in the lateral wall are sparsely distributed in the interventricular septum [31].

Mitral inflow velocities E, A, and E/A were not significantly different between groups, likely because E is sensitive to loading conditions and less reliable in early dysfunction. Left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) was preserved in both groups, confirming that LVEF is insensitive for detecting subclinical systolic dysfunction. The Tei index (MPI), a combined systolic-diastolic parameter, also showed no significant difference, suggesting that it may not the sensitive parameter in the early stage of left ventricular dysfunction [32, 33].

In our study, 7 of 18 segments showed reduced LS, and GLS was significantly lower in the T1D group, consistent with previous studies [34, 35]. As described by Torrent-Guasp et al., the left ventricular wall consists of endocardial, mid-myocardial, and epicardial layers with distinct fiber orientations that enable torsion and untwisting for efficient ejection and filling [36]. LS and GLS primarily reflect endocardial fiber function, which is impaired early in myocardial disease [34, 35]. These preliminary findings suggest regional and global systolic dysfunction despite normal LVEF, supporting that speckle-tracking echocardiography may be more sensitive than conventional LVEF for identifying subclinical systolic impairment [37, 38].

Notably, In univariate and multivariate analyses of this cohort, GLS—a marker of subclinical systolic dysfunction—was independently associated with HbA1c and disease duration; esRAGE and CML/esRAGE ratio were not observed to be independent predictors of GLS. For e(lat), a diastolic index, the only independent correlate identified was CML/esRAGE.

Previous studies have suggested an association between sRAGE and cardiovascular disease [27, 28, 39, 40]. This exploratory pilot study observed that in T1D patients with normal LVEF and no known overt heart disease, CML/esRAGE ratio is independently associated with early diastolic dysfunction, while subclinical systolic dysfunction (impaired GLS) is association with diabetes duration and glycemic control.

The observed association between CML/esRAGE ratio and diastolic dysfunction may be related to the potential role of the AGES-RAGE axis in myocardial firosis and reduced myocardial compliance, as proposed in previous mechanistic studies [41]. However, this study did not directly evaluate or confirm the causal or mechanistic involvement of the AGES-RAGE pathway in myocardial dysfunction. CML/esRAGE ratio reflects the balance between toxic AGEs and protective soluble receptors, and may represent a candidate biomarker of early diastolic dysfunction before structural remodeling occurs–though this requires further validation in larger independent cohorts. In contrast, GLS is a sensitive indicator of subclinical systolic dysfunction and appears to mainly related to disease duration and metabolic control [42].

Our prelimminary findings generate the hypothesis that CML/esRAGE ratio combined with advanced echocardiography may have potential value for early detection of diabetic cardiomyopathy in future research. However, no causal relationships or clinical predictive value can be inferred from this study, and all observed associations require verification in large-scale prospective cohorts and mechanistic experimental studies.

Limitations of this study

Conclusions

Elevated CML/esRAGE ratio is associated with impaired left ventricular diastolic function, and may represent a candidate biomarker for early diastolic dysfunction. Our study also suggest that CML/esRAGE ratio may not associated with GLS abnormalities reflecting subclinical systolic dysfunction. All observations from this pilot study require further validation in large independent prospective cohorts.

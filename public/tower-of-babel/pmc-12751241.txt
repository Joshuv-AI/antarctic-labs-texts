Machine learning model incorporating spectral CT quantitative parameters and clinical characteristics for differentiation of benign and malignant thyroid nodules
Authors: Jie Huang, Dan Zhang, Zuhua Song, Yuwei Chen, Qian Liu, Jiayi Yu, Xinwei Wang, Kai Su, Liang Lv, Ya Zou, Zhuoyue Tang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveThis study aims to explore the clinical value of multiple machine learning (ML) methods in predicting the benign or malignant nature of thyroid nodules based on preoperative dual-layer spectral computed tomography (DLCT) quantitative parameters and clinical characteristics.MethodsThe DLCT images and clinical data of 432 thyroid nodule patients were retrospectively analyzed. The patients were randomly divided into a training (n = 302) and validation sets (n = 130). DLCT quantitative parameters included conventional CT value, 40-keV CT value, 70-keV CT value, 100-keV CT value, spectral curve slope, iodine concentration (IC), normalized IC, effective atomic number (Zeff), and normalized Zeff in the arterial phase (AP) and venous phase, and arterial enhancement fraction (AEF). Pearson correlation analysis and feature importance ranking were employed for dimensionality reduction. Nine ML algorithms were evaluated for predicting the benign or malignant nature of thyroid nodules. The area under the curve (AUC) was obtained by plotting the receiver operating characteristic curve. Finally, SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) values were used to interpret variable contributions.ResultsSix variables remained after dimension reduction of all DLCT quantitative parameters and clinical characteristics, namely AEF, age, AP-40 keV CT value, FT4, TgAb, and TPOAb. In the validation set, the AUCs of the Extreme Gradient Boosting, Logistic Regression, Random Forest, Adaptive Boosting, Light Gradient Boosting Machine, Multilayer Perceptron Classifier, HistGradientBoostingClassifier, BaggingClassifier, and StackingClassifier models constructed based on the six reduced-dimension variables were 0.79, 0.78, 0.78, 0.82, 0.81, 0.76, 0.78, 0.76, and 0.79, respectively. The SHAP method showed the top three variables ranked by contribution degree were: AEF, age, and AP-40 keV CT value.ConclusionThe multiple ML models constructed based on dual-energy CT quantitative parameters and clinical characteristics in this study can effectively predict the benign or malignant nature of thyroid nodules, among which AEF contributes the most.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12880-025-02083-4.

Introduction

With advancements in healthcare and the widespread adoption of ultrasound (US) imaging over recent decades, thyroid nodules, now recognized as a common clinical finding, have demonstrated a significant increase in diagnostic detection, with reported rates reaching up to 60–68% in the general population [1, 2]. Most thyroid nodules are benign, while about 7–15% of thyroid nodules are malignant. For benign thyroid nodules, monitoring and medication are the main approaches, while malignant nodules typically require surgery [2]. Nevertheless, current diagnostic methods for differentiating benign from malignant thyroid nodules carry a risk of misdiagnosis, which may lead to clinical decision-making errors and unnecessary treatments, consequently increasing psychological distress, healthcare costs, and complication risks for patients, ultimately exacerbating the disease management burden on healthcare systems [1, 3]. Thus, accurate differentiation between benign and malignant thyroid nodules is critical for clinical decision-making, helping to avoid overdiagnosis and overtreatment.

US is the preferred imaging modality for evaluating benign or malignant thyroid nodules [3]. Due to the shared features between benign and malignant thyroid nodules and the differences in operation techniques and subjective judgments among doctors of different seniorities, inconsistent nodule classifications may arise [4, 5]. For preoperative diagnosis of thyroid nodules, cytological analysis from fine-needle aspiration biopsy (FNAB) is the most definitive method. Nevertheless, as an invasive procedure, it not only requires significant time investment but also faces diagnostic uncertainties in certain nodular cases [3, 6]. Conventional CT relies on morphological features for qualitative assessment of thyroid nodules, lacking quantitative evaluation metrics [7–9]. MRI has limited clinical applications in differentiating thyroid nodules due to its long scanning time and high cost [10].

Dual-layer spectral computed tomography (DLCT) enables material differentiation and quantification by utilizing differences in material attenuation properties, demonstrating significant value for tumor detection and characterization. This technique can generate virtual monoenergetic images and multiple quantitative parameters, including iodine concentration (IC), spectral attenuation curve slope (λHU), effective atomic number (Zeff), and arterial enhancement fraction (AEF). Furthermore, DLCT effectively reduces beam hardening artifacts and calcification-related artifacts compared with conventional CT, thereby enhancing image contrast and enabling more precise diagnostic assessments [11–13]. Quantitative parameters from DLCT have demonstrated significant utility in distinguishing benign from malignant breast tumors, predicting breast cancer subtypes, and differentiating thyroid nodules nature [14–18]. The AEF obtained from DLCT has been shown to play a significant role in diagnosing cervical lymph node metastasis in patients with thyroid cancer [19]. Meanwhile, clinical characteristics such as gender, Hashimoto’s thyroiditis, free thyroxine (FT4), thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH), and thyroglobulin antibody (TgAb) are independent predictors of thyroid cancer [20–22]. With the development of artificial intelligence, machine learning (ML) which is a transformative innovation in the medical field has demonstrated remarkable capabilities in processing and analyzing medical imaging data and its diagnostic predictive efficacy has been extensively validated [23]. Previous studies have demonstrated that integrating dual-energy CT quantitative parameters with ML models, as well as combining thyroid function (TF) data with ML models, both provides substantial clinical value in distinguishing benign from malignant thyroid nodules [18, 24, 25].

Therefore, This study aims to use different ML methods in conjunction with DLCT multi-quantitative parameters and clinical characteristics to distinguish between benign and malignant thyroid nodules for enhancing the accuracy of diagnosis.

Materials and methods

Patient collection and thyroid nodule selection

This retrospective study was approved by the Review Board of our hospital and the requirement of informed consent from participants was waived. Patients with thyroid nodules who underwent neck DLCT scans at our hospital were consecutively enrolled from August 2022 to July 2024. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (I) the patients underwent contrast-enhanced DLCT examinations; (II) histopathological results were obtained for all nodules. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (I) The patients had undergone received FNAB and relevant therapy before the CT examination (n = 128); (II) the pathological results were indeterminate (n = 317); (III) the imaging quality falls short of fulfilling the demands necessary for diagnosis (n = 39); (IV) complete clinical data were not available (n = 143). Ultimately, 432 patients were enrolled, including 175 benign nodules and 257 malignant nodules. The overall workflow of this study is shown in Fig. 1.

DLCT image acquisition

All patients underwent preoperative contrast-enhanced neck scans with 64-slice DLCT devices (IQon Spectral CT, Philips Healthcare, Amsterdam, the Netherlands) in the supine position from the oropharynx to the superior edge of the clavicle. The scanning protocol was as follows: tube voltage, 120 kVp; tube current, regulated through automated exposure control technology (DoseRight system, Philips Healthcare); rotation time, 0.5 s; detector collimation, 64 × 0.625 mm; matrix, 512 × 512. The noncontrast scan was acquired first, and the contrast-enhance scan was conducted following injection of nonionic contrast media (ioversol 350 mg/mL) at a dose of 1.5 mL/kg and a rate of 3–4 mL/s, which was followed by a 20-mL saline flush at the same rate. Upon reaching a threshold of 110 Hounsfield units (HU) within the descending aortic lumen at the tracheal bifurcation level, scanning of the arterial phase (AP) is initiated. Subsequently, scanning of the venous phase (VP) commences 30 s thereafter.

Collection of clinical data

Clinical characteristics, including gender, age, body mass index (BMI), TSH, free triiodothyronine (FT3), FT4, TgAb, thyroid peroxidase antibody (TPOAb), and carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA), were extracted from the hospital information system for each patient.

DLCT quantitative parameters

All images were analyzed on the spectral CT post-processing workstation (IntelliSpace Portal 10.1, Philips Healthcare) by two radiologists, each with 6 and 12 years of thyroid imaging experience, respectively. Both radiologists were blinded to the pathological results and performed the analysis independently. On the axial slices, the region of interest (ROI) with the largest possible area was delineated at the level of the solid component of the thyroid nodule. Meanwhile, the ROI of the carotid artery was also delineated at the same level. Thereby directly yielding the following quantitative parameters: the conventional CT values, monoenergetic CT values of 40-keV, 70-keV, and 100-keV, IC, Zeff in the AP and VP. All quantitative parameters were independently gauged twice by the two radiologists, and the mean values were subsequently computed. The normalized iodine concentration (NIC), normalized effective atomic number (NZeff), λHU, and AEF were ascertained through calculation. The calculation formulas are as follows: NIC = ICnodule​/ ICcarotid artery; NZeff = Zeffnodule / Zeffcarotid artery; λHU = (HU40 − keV − HU100 − keV) / (100 − 40); AEF = ICAP / ICVP.

Variable selection and building of ML models

First, the Pearson correlation coefficient test was used to screen the variables, and the threshold was set at 0.9. Subsequently, the screened variables were ranked by means of the feature importances assessment method, and the top six variables were selected as the final variables for constructing the ML model.

All patients were randomly split into a training set (n = 302, 70%) and a validation set (n = 130, 30%). In the training set, nine ML models were constructed based on the final variables, utilizing the following algorithms: Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost), Logistic Regression (LR), Random Forest (RF), Adaptive Boosting (AdaBoost), Light Gradient Boosting Machine (LightGBM), Multilayer Perceptron Classifier (MLP-C), HistGradientBoostingClassifier (HGB-C), BaggingClassifier (Bagging-C), and StackingClassifier (Stacking-C). Model building and parameter optimization were performed on the training set using 5-fold cross-validation, with independent evaluation in the validation set.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS software (version 25.0, SPSS, IBM) and Python (version 3.10.9). To evaluate data distribution, the Shapiro-Wilk test was utilized. Continuous variables were reported as mean ± standard deviation or median (interquartile range), and categorical variables were summarized as frequencies. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was performed on both the training and validation sets to evaluate the ML model performance. The area under the ROC curve (AUC), accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score were calculated. SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) values were introduced to enhance model interpretability by visually illustrating feature contributions to predictions. Decision curve analysis (DCA) was performed to assess the clinical utility of the model. A two-sided p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Variable selection

Our study initially considered 28 candidate variables, which included 19 DLCT quantitative parameters and 9 clinical characteristics. Using a correlation coefficient threshold of |r| >0.9, highly correlated variables were eliminated, resulting in 22 non-collinear variables retained for subsequent analysis. These 22 variables were then ranked by feature importance using a GradientBoostingClassifier. The top six most contributory features—AEF, age, FT4, TgAb, AP-40 keV CT value, and TPOAb—were selected as the final feature set for model construction. Detailed results of this selection process are provided in the Supplementary Material (Figure S1).

Performance evaluation of ML models

Table 2; Fig. 2 present the prediction performances of different ML models on the training and validation sets. On the training set, all nine ML models demonstrated excellent discriminative ability (AUCs ≥ 0.80), with Bagging-C, Stacking-C, and XGBoost achieving AUCs of 1.00, 1.00, and 0.99, respectively. On the validation set, all models showed good predictive performance (AUCs > 0.75), with AdaBoost and LightGBM achieving AUCs of 0.82 and 0.81, respectively. To intuitively evaluate the model performance, the precision-recall (P-R) curves and confusion matrices characterize the model performance in the validation set, with these detailed in Supplementary Material (Figure S2). Overall, the confusion matrices show the distribution of correct and incorrect classifications for benign and malignant nodules across models, while the P-R curves illustrate how precision varies with recall for each model.

Discussion

In this study, all nine ML models constructed by integrating clinical characteristics and DLCT quantitative parameters demonstrated favorable performance in discriminating benign from malignant thyroid nodules. AEF, age, FT4, TgAb, AP-40 keV CT value, and TPOAb are six crucial variables in the classification between benign and malignant thyroid nodules, among which AEF, age, and AP-40 keV CT value demonstrate more prominent discriminatory capabilities. This finding indicates a significant correlation between DLCT quantitative parameters, clinical characteristics and thyroid nodule malignancy.

Our study identifies AEF and AP-40 keV CT value as diagnostically significant DLCT-derived quantitative parameters for malignant thyroid nodules, both ranking among the top three contributing variables. Since follicular epithelial cells with normal iodine uptake function are present in benign thyroid nodules but nearly absent in malignant ones, the iodine uptake can be used to infer the nature of thyroid nodules [26, 27]. The AEF in conventional CT representing the ratio of AP attenuation increase to VP attenuation increase is equivalent to the IC ratio between AP and VP in DLCT, and it has been established as a significant quantitative parameter for diagnosing hepatocellular carcinoma, hepatic fibrosis, and papillary thyroid carcinoma lymph node metastasis, while also serving as an indicator of indirect tumor perfusion [19, 28, 29]. The evidence confirms that 40-keV monoenergetic imaging simultaneously optimizes head and neck tumor conspicuity through elevated lesion-to-background attenuation ratios, and sensitively detects iodine uptake gradient dynamics while offering dual diagnostic advantages [30, 31], which leads to a substantial increase in the standard deviation of HU in the tumor area compared to traditional CT, making it highly valuable in evaluating intratumoral heterogeneity [32]. AP-40 keV CT value showed favorable diagnostic efficacy in distinguishing benign from malignant thyroid micro-nodules [16]. Therefore, lower AEF and lower AP-40 keV CT values indirectly reflect the low iodine uptake of malignant thyroid nodules. Notably, this study identified AEF as the most critical variable for detecting malignant thyroid nodules, suggesting its potential as an imaging biomarker for thyroid cancer diagnosis.

Epidemiological evidence indicates that thyroid cancer is the most common cancer among adolescents and adults under the age of 40, especially with a relatively high incidence among people aged 15 to 39 [33, 34]. Based on the results of this study, the average age of malignant nodules is younger than that of benign nodules. TF tests constitute an essential component of the initial assessment of thyroid nodules, growing research indicates a significant association between TF and thyroid cancer [24]. FT4 and TSH are associated with an increased risk of thyroid cancer. Among them, high levels of TSH are an independent predictor of thyroid cancer, and low levels of FT4 can promote TSH release through feedback regulation of the hypothalamic-pituitary-thyroid axis [21, 22]. Based on our findings from the SHAP summary plot, the lower the FT4 level, the higher the probability of malignant transformation of thyroid nodules, and this finding is consistent with previous studies on the pathogenesis of thyroid cancer. Since the thyroglobulin epitopes produced by thyroid cancer differ from those recognized by autoantibodies associated with autoimmune thyroiditis, the development of thyroid cancer may stimulate the production of additional TgAb, consequently elevating serum TgAb levels [22]. High levels of TPOAb might be used as diagnostic markers for predicting thyroid cancer risk, as TPOAb can mediate thyroid tissue damage and concomitant cytokine production from infiltrating immune cells may synergistically drive inflammation-associated oncogenic transformation [21]. Based on our findings from the SHAP summary plot, higher levels of TgAb and TPOAb show a positive correlation with an increased risk of thyroid cancer.

In this study, ML models were constructed based on nine classifiers, namely XGBoost, LR, RF, AdaBoost, LightGBM, MLP-C, HGB-C, Bagging-C, and Stacking-C, for predicting the benign and malignant nature of thyroid nodules. All nine models had AUCs ≥ 0.8 in the training set and >0.75 in the validation set. We analyzed the performance of the nine models in the validation set from different perspectives through their respective precision-recall curves and confusion matrices. These models performed well in the number of correct predictions for benign and malignant thyroid nodules and maintained a relatively high precision rate under different recall rate levels. Due to the inherent black-box nature of ML models, it is difficult to interpret their outputs. SHAP values can be used to evaluate the importance and direction of features. Features in the positive direction diagnose malignancy, while those in the negative direction diagnose benignancy [35]. We conducted SHAP analysis based on the more stable LightGBM model, intuitively explaining the model through SHAP feature importance and summary plots to enhance the executability of the models and promote their application in clinical practice.

Previous study has utilized TF parameters combined with various machine learning algorithms to develop models for distinguishing benign from malignant thyroid nodules, validating their diagnostic value [24]. Their study identified FT4, TPOAb, and FT3 as the key features, and its best-performing model (Gradient Boosting) showed comparable predictive performance (AUC = 0.82) to our LightGBM model (AUC = 0.81). However, our model demonstrated superior recall (0.84 vs. 0.499) and F1-score (0.81 vs. 0.616), significantly reducing the risk of missed diagnosis of malignant cases without increasing misdiagnosis. By integrating DLCT quantitative parameters, this approach can provide biological characteristics such as tumor perfusion and iodine uptake that are inaccessible via TF testing, thereby improving diagnostic performance. Another study explored the utility of dual-energy CT quantitative parameters combined with ML for differentiating benign from malignant thyroid nodules, and identified higher IC, NIC, iodine concentration difference, λHU, and Zeff in malignant nodules [18]. The ML(Lasso-LDA) constructed in that study showed comparable predictive performance (AUC = 0.84, accuracy = 73.9%) to our LightGBM model (AUC = 0.81, accuracy = 76.15%). Our study identified AEF, age, and AP-40 keV CT value as key predictors of malignancy. This suggests that a multimodal strategy combining hemodynamic and clinical features may yield a more comprehensive and clinically translatable model than relying solely on iodine uptake and material composition information.

Our research has several limitations. First, this was a single-center retrospective study with a relatively small sample size. Further investigations with larger cohorts are needed to validate our findings. Second, the majority of malignant cases were papillary thyroid carcinomas, while benign cases predominantly consisted of nodular goiters. This may limit the generalizability of our ML models to other pathological types of thyroid nodules. Third, the limitation pertains to the potential simplicity and bias in feature selection. This study relied solely on the Pearson correlation coefficient (|r| >0.9) and the top six features ranked by the GradientBoostingClassifier, which may introduce bias—this method only captures linear relationships and involves subjectivity in selecting feature cutoff values. In future work, we will explore and compare a variety of advanced feature selection algorithms, including Boruta, to enhance the objectivity and comprehensiveness of feature selection. Finally, future multicenter external validation studies will be necessary to establish the broader applicability of our final models.

Conclusion

This study integrated clinical characteristics with DLCT quantitative parameters to construct nine ML models for differentiating benign and malignant thyroid nodules, all demonstrating good predictive performance. Significantly, AEF, age, and AP-40 keV CT value were identified as key discriminative indicators for thyroid nodule evaluation.

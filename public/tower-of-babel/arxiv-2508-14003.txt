Machine Learning H H -theorem
Ruben Lier

Abstract
H H -theorem provides a microscopic foundation of the Second Law of Thermodynamics and is therefore essential to establishing statistical physics, but at the same time, H H -theorem has been subject to controversy that in part persists till this day. To better understand H H -theorem and its relation to the arrow of time, we study the equilibration of randomly oriented and positioned hard disks with periodic boundary conditions. Using a model based on the DeepSets architecture, which imposes permutation invariance of the particle labels, we train a model to capture the irreversibility of the H H -functional.

Introduction

The discovery of
H
-theorem by Ludwig Boltzmann in 1872
boltzmann1872waermegleichgewicht
played a pivotal role in establishing statistical mechanics as a framework that provides thermodynamics with a microscopic origin and liberates it from its axiomatic foundations. At the same time,
H
-theorem was for a long time the topic of much debate
https://doi.org/10.1002/andp.18972960216
;

;

;

;
DiPerna1989
. One lingering issue is the
Loschmidt paradox

Cercignani2006-if
;
Cercignani1988
, which relates to the remarkable feature of
H
-theorem that there exists an
H
-functional which can only decrease in time and therefore signal the presence of an
arrow of time

eddington1929nature
despite the system being
microscopically reversible

doi:10.1073/pnas.11.7.436
, which means that the particles of which the gas is composed undergo interactions that are time-reversal invariant. This paradox is now better understood, in part because of the discovery of BBGKY hierarchy
grad1949
;
ecc82589-c02d-30e9-9891-fe9629a2fd13
, which must be truncated by assuming molecular chaos so that one is left with the Boltzmann equation. The assumption of molecular chaos introduces an arrow of time; while Lanford’s theorem
lanford1975
proves its validity in the Boltzmann–Grad limit
grad1949
for short times, no general proof exists for arbitrary times or interaction types, so its adoption remains, to some extent, a leap of faith. Nevertheless, the Boltzmann equation has been found to be a valuable tool for deriving continuum equations from first principles that has been very widely used for more than a century
Cercignani1988
;
chapman1990mathematical
in fields ranging from solid state physics
PhysRevB.97.045105
;
mueller2009graphenenearlyperfect
;
PhysRevLett.115.216806
to high energy physics
De_Groot1980-px
;
Cercignani2002-lo
;

as well as in active matter

;
PhysRevE.74.022101
;
grosvenor2025hydrodynamicsboostinvariancekinetictheory
;

.

In recent years, several works tried to exploit the emergence of an arrow of time for many-particle systems as a way to better understand sequential data. For example, in Ref.
Seif2021
, the authors show that by putting trajectories and their time-reverses into a binary classifier, it is possible to extract Crooks fluctuation theorem
PhysRevE.60.2721
;
PhysRevLett.78.2690
from the predicted probability of the binary classifier. In Ref.
kuroyanagi2025deeplearningthermodynamiclaws
, the authors use siamese neural network to compare pairs of image sets at different times for a molecular dynamics simulation which involves a moving piston, enabling the authors to construct an entropy. This equilibrium entropy is shown to be consistent with thermodynamic axioms formulated by Lieb and Yngvason
LIEB19991
and identical to the entropy of the van der Waals gas up to an affine transformation. Similarly, in Ref.
Wei_2018_CVPR
, videos played forward and reversed are classified, and the classifier is then used to identify the spatial region in the video that has the strongest arrow of time. Lastly in Ref.
rahaman2019learningarrowtime
, the authors learn a linear function that separates states early and late in time for games that have an irreversible element in it and therefore require safe exploration
moldovan2012safeexplorationmarkovdecision
;
inproceedings
. The authors show that when such a function is learned for a two-component stochastic process the function has quantitative overlap with entropy up to an affine transformation.

In this work, we learn to output a value that resembles
H

;

;

. We do this using an approach that is similar to that of Ref.
rahaman2019learningarrowtime
where a linear function is derived whose cost function is such that the learning processes pushes the model to learn to attribute higher output values to input values corresponding to later times.

H

II
Boltzmann equation

We consider the number of particles
dN
at a region in phase space to be given by

\displaystyle dN=f(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{v},t)d\mathbf{r}d\mathbf{v}~~,

(1)

where
\mathbf{r}
is the particle position,
\mathbf{v}
is its velocity and
f(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{v},t)
is the distribution function. In the presence of a force
\mathbf{F}
, the Boltzmann equation is given by

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}+\mathbf{v}\cdot\frac{\partial f}{\partial\mathbf{r}}+\frac{\mathbf{F}}{m}\cdot\frac{\partial f}{\partial\mathbf{v}}=\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}\right)_{\text{self}}~~,

(2)

where
m
is the particle mass. In this work, we set
\mathbf{F}=0
since the interactions are exclusively of the hard-sphere kind, and we also do not account for spatial dependence. The term in the right hand side of (
2
) is the collision integral for which one can find closure with the molecular chaos assumption
Cercignani1988
;
grad1958principles
. For the case of hard disks, the collision integral is given by
chapman1990mathematical
;
Cercignani1988

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}\right)_{\text{self}}=\\
&\iint d\mathbf{v}_{2}db\,u\left(f(\mathbf{v},t)f(\mathbf{v}_{2},t)-f(\mathbf{v}^{\prime}_{1},t)f(\mathbf{v}^{\prime}_{2},t)\right),\end{split}

(3)

where
b
is the impact parameter and
\mathbf{v}^{\prime}_{1}
and
\mathbf{v}^{\prime}_{2}
are velocities of particles going into the collision which are tied to
\mathbf{v}
and
\mathbf{v}_{2}
through energy and momentum balance. Galilean invariance makes it that only the relative velocities
\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{v}_{2}
and
\mathbf{u}^{\prime}=\mathbf{v}^{\prime}_{1}-\mathbf{v}^{\prime}_{2}
describe a unique collision, and the scattering angle
\chi
that gives the relative angles between these two vectors is given by (see Fig.
1
)

\displaystyle b=d\cos( \chi/ 2 )~~,

(4)

where
d
is the disk diameter.

Figure 1
:
Depiction of an elastic collision between two hard disks.

To solve (
2
) analytically for the exact collision operator is incredibly difficult. One way to do so is perturbatively, using the Chapman-Enskog expansion
chapman1990mathematical
. There exist however numerical approaches to solving (
2
)
Mouhot_2006
and recently it has been explored whether a neural network can be used to deal with the collision integral
Xiao_2021
;
Xiao_2023
. Analytical challenges notwithstanding,
H
-theorem provides us with an excellent handle for understanding how (
2
) irreversibly moves towards an equilibrium state. To wit,
H
-theorem tells us that there exists an
H
-functional given by

\displaystyle H(t)=\int d\mathbf{v}f(\mathbf{v},t)\log(f ( \mathbf{v} , t))~~,

(5)

for which it holds that upon plugging in (
2
), one obtains

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}H(t)\leq 0~~.

(6a)

Furthermore, in two dimensions the equilibrium state is characterized by

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}H_{0}(t)&=0~~,\\
f_{0}(\mathbf{v},t)&=\frac{nm}{2\pi{k_{\text{b}}}T}\exp( -\frac{m }{2 \kb T } | \mathbf{v} - \mathbf{v}_0 |^2 )~~,\end{split}

(6b)

where
n
is the particle density,
T
is temperature and
\mathbf{v}_{0}
is the average velocity.
f_{0}(\mathbf{v},t)
is called the Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution. The striking elegance of (
6
) which emerges from the chaotic nature of hard disk collisions tells us that something very simple can be learned from very chaotic data and thereby underpins our machine learning approach.

III
Hard disk simulation

Let us now specify the simulation. The simulation is based on code from
hill2020mb2d
and is initialized with 1000 hard disks of equal mass and diameter with random orientation and position in a square box with periodic boundary conditions. The initial velocity magnitude is chosen to be uniform, as a uniformly distributed velocity magnitude gives rise to an
H
-functional that undergoes only very minor changes through time and therefore it is not easy to see the downward trend dictated by
H
-theorem. For every timestep, it is checked whether two particles overlap, in which case an elastic collision takes place. This collision is constrained by energy and momentum balance and the requirement that momentum is only transferred in the direction parallel to the line of disk centers (see Fig.
1
). Since all particles have the same mass, these collision rules can be described in terms of the simple relations

\displaystyle\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{u}=-\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{u}^{\prime}~~,~~\mathbf{z}\times\mathbf{n}\times\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{z}\times\mathbf{n}\times\mathbf{u}^{\prime}~~,

(7)

where
\mathbf{n}
is the line of disk centers and
\mathbf{z}
is the out of plane direction. This collision only takes place if the relative velocity points oppositely to the line of disk centers, i.e. when it holds that

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}^{\prime}\cdot\mathbf{n}<0~~.

(8)

as otherwise the particles are moving away from each other. If this constraint is not imposed, particles will get stuck together. The radius of the disks is taken to be
d=3\cdot 10^{-3}
whereas the periodic box has a unit surface. When the frame rate is too small, it is possible that certain collisions are missed. In Fig.
2
, we show that at a frame rate of around
60
, the number of collisions starts to plateau, so this is the frame rate we take.

of the simulation.

All velocities are saved for each timestep giving rise to an amount of
N_{t}=500
time steps, and we considered
N_{\text{run}}=500
unique runs. In Fig.

IV
Machine learning
H
-theorem

Let us now describe the machine learning process. At every training step, we have as input sequential data
V^{[n]}_{t}
for all
t
and
n
, where
t
corresponds to the time step and
n
corresponds to the run. For any run
n
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}^{(i),[n]}_{t}\in V^{[n]}_{t}~~,

(9)

where
\mathbf{v}^{(i),[n]}_{t}
is the two-dimensional velocity vector of the
i
th particle. In order to effectively learn the
H
-functional, we utilize the permutation invariants of the particle labels, which is done with the DeepSets architecture
zaheer2018deepsets
. In particular, we take the model to be of the form

\displaystyle h\left(V^{[n]}_{t}\right)=\rho\left(\sum_{i}\phi\left(\mathbf{v}^{(i),[n]}_{t}\right)\right)~~,

(10a)

where
\rho
and
\phi
encode a neural network with trainable weights that treats any value of run
n
, any run
t
and any particle
i
on equal footing. Following
rahaman2019learningarrowtime
, we consider a loss function per timestep
t\leq N_{t}-1
and run
n
that is given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mathcal{L}^{(0)}\left(V^{[n]}_{t},V^{[n]}_{t+1}\right)=h\left(V^{[n]}_{t+1}\right)-h\left(V^{[n]}_{t}\right)\\
&+\lambda\,\!\left(h\left(V^{[n]}_{t+1}\right)-h\left(V^{[n]}_{t}\right)\right)^{2}\end{split}

(10b)

The loss of (
10b
) contains a term that pushes to learn a function consistent with the monotonicity property (
6a
) as well as the term with coefficient
\lambda
that serves as a regularization.
In Fig.
4
, we give a graphical representation of the learning system of (
10
) and we see that the pairwise comparative nature of (
10b
) makes it so that this learning system effectively turns into a siamese neural network
bromley1993siamese
;
burges
.

). We considered the case of 3 input vectors
\mathbf{v}^{(i)}_{t}

After experimenting with (
10
), it is found that the trained function
h(V_{t})
, unlike the real
H
-functional, displays strongly oscillatory behavior upon increasing the time step. To mitigate this, we note that (
6a
) is an inequality which only requires that the
H
-functional does not increase as a function of time, not that it decreases. Therefore, we consider a generalization of the loss function of (
10b
) which is given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}\left(V^{[n]}_{t},V^{[n]}_{t+1}\right)\\
=&-\text{leaky-ReLu}\left(h\left(V^{[n]}_{t}\right)-h\left(V^{[n]}_{t+1}\right),\alpha\right)\\
&+\lambda\,\!\left(h\left(V^{[n]}_{t+1}\right)-h\left(V^{[n]}_{t}\right)\right)^{2}~~.\end{split}

(11)

It follows that when
0\leq\alpha<1
, (
11
) makes sure that the function
h(V_{t})
is punished more for incorrectly outputting a lower value at a later time step than it is rewarded for correctly outputting a higher value at later times. This choice is not only consistent with (
6a
), which only imposes the former on the
H
-functional, but also turns out to reduce oscillation of
h(V_{t+1})
.
We optimize for a total number of epochs
N_{e}=120
with the loss of (
11
) averaged over runs
n
and time steps
t
using the Adam optimizer
kingma2017adammethodstochasticoptimization
, choosing learning rate
4\cdot 10^{-4}
and
\lambda=0.2
. All the hidden layers of the model have hidden dimension

and
ReLu
-activations (see Fig.
4
and Table

as a function of epoch

\displaystyle\alpha(e)=1-\frac{1-\alpha_{\text{final}}}{N_{e}}\cdot e~~.

(12)

We train three distinct models with hidden dimensions

\displaystyle n_{\text{hidden}}\in\{16,32,64\}~~.

(13)

Because of the choice of a gradual increase according to (

V
Affine transformation

Having fully specified the learning process of the model
h(V_{t})
, let us consider its relation to the
H
-functional provided in (
5
). The model
h(V_{t})
can only possibly resemble the
H
-functional up to an affine transformation
kuroyanagi2025deeplearningthermodynamiclaws
;
rahaman2019learningarrowtime
. This is because (
11
) is invariant under shifts constant in time. Furthermore, we have for
c\in\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\{h(V_{t}),\lambda\}\rightarrow\{ch(V_{t}),\lambda/c\}\Rightarrow\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}_{t}\rightarrow c\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}_{t}~~,

(14)

which means we can multiply the function
h(V_{t})
with a constant factor and it will only affect the loss with a total factor provided we rescale the physically meaningless
\lambda
. Therefore, in order to compare the model to the real
H
-functional we transform the function
h(V_{t})
as

\displaystyle\tilde{h}^{[n]}\left(V^{[n]}_{t}\right)=a^{[n]}h\left(V^{[n]}_{t}\right)+b^{[n]}~~.

(15)

The coefficients
a^{[n]}
and
b^{[n]}
are fitted to the
H
-functional with the least squares algorithm for each separate run
n
and for all time steps
t
, i.e. they are computed as

\displaystyle\argmin_{a^{[n]},b^{[n]}}=\sum_{t}\left(\tilde{h}^{[n]}\left(V^{[n]}_{t}\right)-H_{t}^{[n]}\right)^{2}~~.

(16)

In Fig.
5
, the result is shown for the different models and compared to the
H
-functional. It is clear that for
n_{\text{hidden}}=32
the model performs better than for
n_{\text{hidden}}=16
, whereas there is no big difference between
n_{\text{hidden}}=64
ad
n_{\text{hidden}}=32
. Although the affine parameters are fitted for each run separately, we would ideally have a model that can provide a value for the
H
-functional that is consistent throughout all runs. To find out whether the model is consistent throughout runs, we compute the average and the variance of the affine parameters in Table

VI
Discussion

In this work we used a siamese neural network to learn the
H
-functional of
H
-theorem for sequential data of a thousand hard disks colliding with each after being initialized in a state of low entropy. The loss function that was formulated in order to learn
H
-theorem was based on the requirement that the learned function should attribute higher values to input data corresponding to later points in time. This requirement forces the model to identify the arrow of time which according to
H
-theorem was predicted to exist for this simple but also infinitely chaotic model of hard disks. The resulting model only resembles the extracted
H
-functional after an appropriately fitted affine transformation. Two things that were found to be important for making the model resemble the
H
-functional are

1.

The DeepSets architecture
zaheer2018deepsets
, which implements the permutation invariance of the particle labels, thereby drastically simplifying the model compared to a model that treats every particle separately.

2.

The sign-dependent tilt of the loss which makes it so that at late epochs, the model is penalized more strongly for outputting a decrease in time than it is rewarded for outputting an increase, thereby generating a model that is less oscillatory at late times and steeper at short times.

For future work, it would be interesting to explore more complicated types of collisional interactions, such as for example polyatomic gases
Cercignani1981
or for active gas of two species of hard spheres that collide reactively and can resemble the phenomenon of bird flocking
rubenliermicroscopic
.

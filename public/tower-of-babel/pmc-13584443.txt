Characterisation of liver disease using quantitative MRI: report of the imaging arm of the ELEGANCE study

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundEvolving disease aetiology and rising prevalence of chronic liver disease are driving an increase in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC). Non-contrast quantitative MRI (qMRI) has shown promising capability for characterising liver health and risk of liver related outcomes, but has not been evaluated in HCC surveillance or specifically examined in Asian populations. We explored the characteristics of qMRI in participants with chronic liver disease undergoing HCC surveillance as part of the prospective cohort study, ELEGANCE.MethodsSeven hundred sixty-nine participants underwent qMRI using LiverMultiScan to derive information relating to liver disease activity (iron-corrected T1, cT1), liver iron (from T2*) and liver fat content (through proton density fat fraction), as well as structural features of advancing disease such as, liver and spleen volumes, and the caudate to right lobe volume ratio.ResultsThree hundred twenty-eight participants had hepatitis B (HBV); 161 metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD); and 273 had cirrhosis (any aetiology). In HBV, qMRI identified that more than 40% had concurrent SLD. In MASLD, qMRI suggested 31% had MASH. In all aetiologies, cirrhosis was associated with elevated cT1, reduced liver fat, spleen enlargement, and caudate hypertrophy. A model incorporating these features demonstrated strong discriminatory performance for cirrhosis, particularly within the MASLD cohort (AUC = 0.88). In an exploratory analysis the same score demonstrated statistical power to discriminate between those with and without a subsequent HCC diagnosis.ConclusionThis study demonstrates the capability of qMRI for comprehensive characterisation of different liver disease aetiologies and the ability to identify those at highest risk of clinical outcomes.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12876-026-05213-6.Keywords: Quantitative MRI (qMRI), Metabolic dysfunction–associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD), Compensated advanced chronic liver disease (cACLD), Cirrhosis, Hepatocellular carcinoma surveillance, Iron‑corrected T1 (cT1), Proton density fat fraction (PDFF), Non‑invasive test, Risk stratification, Metabolic–viral liver disease overlap

Introduction

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) remains a leading cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide and is closely linked to the presence of chronic liver disease and cirrhosis [1]. In East Asia, the epidemiology of HCC is undergoing a fundamental shift. While chronic hepatitis B (HBV) has historically been the dominant driver of HCC risk, widespread vaccination and effective antiviral therapy have substantially improved outcomes in HBV. Meanwhile, a rapid rise in the global burden of metabolic dysfunction-associated liver disease (MASLD) has introduced additional complexity in disease progression and risk stratification [2–4]. In Asia where HBV remains endemic, MASLD increasingly co-exists with HBV, creating a heterogeneous phenotype that is not adequately captured by current surveillance frameworks.

This epidemiological shift has important clinical implications because current non-invasive assessment strategies primarily focus on fibrosis staging using serum-based indices or elastography. Whilst these tools are widely accessible and clinically validated, they provide limited information on concomitant steatosis, fibro-inflammatory activity, and structural remodelling. Meanwhile, pharmacotherapies for MASLD have recently emerged with the potential to alter the natural history of both steatohepatitis and fibrosis for the first time. Therefore, accurate characterisation of disease activity, fibrosis severity, and progression across metabolic or viral aetiologies becomes critical to inform treatment selection and timing, and potentially help in refining HCC risk stratification for these patients [5].

Quantitative magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) enables reproducible, non-invasive quantification of features such as liver fat content measured with proton density fat fraction (PDFF), disease activity (measured with iron-corrected T1, cT1), liver iron, and structural features within a single examination [6–8]. These imaging biomarkers offer the potential to characterise liver disease across pathophysiological dimensions. However, data describing the distribution and interaction of these imaging phenotypes in real-world surveillance populations, particularly in Asian cohorts with metabolic-viral overlap, remain limited. To date, qMRI has never been systematically applied in large-scale HCC surveillance cohorts, nor used to characterise overlapping metabolic and viral liver disease phenotypes in at-risk HCC populations.

In this study, we conducted a baseline cross-sectional analysis of participants enrolled in a prospective hepatocellular carcinoma surveillance cohort undergoing qMRI. We aimed to (1) characterise quantitative imaging phenotypes across viral and metabolic liver disease, (2) determine the prevalence of lean MASLD and the burden of imaging-defined metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH) within the MASLD participants, and (3) evaluate whether integrated qMRI-derived parameters discriminate clinically defined cirrhosis within MASLD and the progression from cirrhosis to HCC.

Materials & methods

Baseline patient characteristics

The ELEGANCE study is a multi-centre prospective cohort study conducted in Singapore and approved by the Institutional Review Board (ECOS Ref. 2020/3053). Participants with clinically diagnosed HBV, HCV, MASLD or cirrhosis were recruited into the study, excluding those with previous events of HCC within the last 5 years (see the flow diagram in Fig. 1). Baseline characteristics were ascertained at enrolment and included the following variables: demographics (age, sex, ethnicity), anthropometrics (BMI), comorbidities (diabetes, hepatitis B, hepatitis C [HCV], cirrhosis, steatohepatitis), and blood biochemistry. Ethnicity was categorised as Chinese, Indian, Malay and Others. The sample size for the main study cohort was determined a priori based on power calculations for the primary study outcomes. The present analysis is of a subset of participants who elected to undergo an optional imaging visit. As participation in this sub-study was voluntary, the sample size was determined by participant uptake rather than a formal sample size calculation. Consequently, analyses of the imaging subset are descriptive in nature and may be underpowered to detect small effects.

Left: Study flowchart of the qMRI cohort in the ELEGANCE study. Right: qMRI maps derived from LiverMultiScan: [A] liver fat content measured with MRI PDFF, [B] iron-corrected T1, (cT1), [C] liver Couinaud segmentation, and [D] spleen segmentation

MRI measurements

Participants were scanned at 14 sites in Singapore. Baseline scans were performed between May 2021 and June 2025, and standard of care clinical data was collected alongside. Median follow-up for new diagnoses of HCC was 1.49 years.

A total of 769 participants underwent abdominal quantitative MRI (qMRI) using the LiverMultiScan imaging acquisition protocol. The imaging acquisition protocol was installed and calibrated across all participating MRI machines according to the manufacturer’s guidance as well as acquisition parameters. This standardised protocol was designed to remove the effect of inter-scanner variability [9]. LiverMultiScan reports metrics of liver iron through the measurements of T2*, liver fat measured with MRI PDFF, and fibro-inflammatory activity measured by iron-corrected T1 (cT1). The volume of each Couinaud segment and the whole liver were derived from additional automated post-processing [10, 11]. Two important morphological features were also derived through qMRI automatic segmentation - caudate-to-right-lobe volume ratio (C-RL ratio) and spleen volume - which are both clinically used to determine liver cirrhosis, although mostly through computed tomography (CT) examinations.

The status of chronic HBV and HCV was defined through virology tests performed in routine clinical care prior to study enrolment. MASLD was defined by ultrasound-based fat measurements. For the imaging-based analysis, only participants with MRI-confirmed steatosis (PDFF ≥ 5%) were included. Lean MASLD was defined as PDFF ≥ 5% with body mass index (BMI) < 23 kg/m² (Asian criteria). Lean MASH was defined as cT1 ≥ 800ms, PDFF ≥ 5% with BMI < 23 kg/m². Metabolic-viral overlap was defined as PDFF ≥ 5% in participants with HBV or HCV infection [6].

The severity of hepatic steatosis was classified into low, intermediate and high based on PDFF cut-offs of < 5%, ≥ 5% to < 10%, and ≥ 10% respectively [12]. In the absence of biopsy, elevated fibro-inflammatory activities consistent with MASH and at-risk MASH phenotypes were defined by cT1 ≥ 800ms and ≥ 875ms respectively, as previously determined [6]. cT1 between 800-875ms indicates elevated levels of disease activity, presenting an increased risk of cardiovascular outcomes [13, 14]. Whilst cT1 ≥ 875ms indicates likely active MASH with significant fibrosis in a steatotic liver disease cohort [6] and higher risk of liver related outcomes [14].

Statistical analysis

Participants with complete data for cT1 and PDFF were included. Descriptive statistics were used to report qMRI measurements for the different aetiologies, mean and standard deviation for continuous variables, and proportions for categorical variables. Between-group differences were assessed using independent t-tests or Mann-Whitney U tests for continuous variables (depending on whether data was normally distributed) and chi-square tests for categorical variables.

Predictive models to identify liver cirrhosis from the imaging data were constructed using logistic regression with imaging-derived parameters (cT1, PDFF, C-RL ratio, spleen volume) in the whole cohort and a subset with only MASLD and MASLD-derived cirrhosis. Only participants with a full set of cT1, PDFF, caudate-to-right-lobe ratio and spleen volume were used as inputs to the model. Diagnostic accuracy was assessed using areas under the receiver operating curves (AUROCs). The coefficients of the model were used to compute the model’s linear predictor (logit score), defined as the weighted sum of the input features. This linear score was used directly as a composite score, and compared between non-HCC cirrhotic group versus those who went on to develop HCC. New-onset HCC cases were considered as a whole group and the subset with cirrhosis-derived HCC only. Survival analysis using a Cox proportional hazards model was performed to account for time-dependent estimation for HCC risk and results were presented in age and sex adjusted hazard ratios.

All statistical analyses were performed using R (version 4.5.1; R foundation for statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). A two-sided p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Where appropriate, p-values were adjusted for multiple corrections using the False discovery rate (FDR) correction. A summary of missing data is provided in supplementary Table 3.

Results

Study population

At the time of analysis, 769 participants had undergone baseline qMRI. The mean age was 62 (standard deviation [SD] ± 7.4), 39% were females, and the mean BMI was 25 kg/m2. By primary aetiology, 161 participants had MASLD, 328 had HBV, 7 had HCV, and 273 had clinically defined cirrhosis. 15 participants went on to develop HCC – 12 with a background diagnosis of cirrhosis, 3 with HBV.

Across aetiologies, qMRI metrics demonstrated distinct phenotypic patterns. Compared with HBV, participants with MASLD exhibited higher liver fat content and fibro-inflammatory activity (mean PDFF 11% vs. 6%, p<0.001; cT1: 752ms vs. cT1: 717ms, p<0.001). Participants with cirrhosis demonstrated the highest cT1 values (mean 773ms), increased spleen volume (mean 220mL) and a higher caudate-to-right-lobe ratio, consistent with advanced structural remodelling (Table 1).

Prevalence of HBV-SLD overlap

Among participants with chronic HBV infection (mean age 61 years, 44% female, mean BMI 24), 41% (136/328) had co-existing steatotic liver disease (SLD).

When stratified by liver fat content, those with high hepatic steatosis (PDFF ≥ 10%) exhibited a distinct metabolic and imaging phenotype compared to those with low hepatic steatosis (PDFF < 5%). Specifically, HBV participants with high hepatic steatosis had a higher cT1, greater liver volume, increased caudate-to-right-lobe ratio, and slightly increased liver iron content (Table 2). This group also demonstrated less favourable metabolic and biochemical profiles, as evidenced by higher Alanine Aminotransferase (ALT), Gamma‑Glutamyl Transferase (GGT), Hemoglobin A1c (Hba1c) and Triglycerides (TG) (Table 2), which indicates an increased fibro-inflammatory, and metabolic burden in HBV patients with high hepatic steatosis. Comparing participants with HBV with HBV-derived cirrhosis, PDFF was lower and cT1 was higher in those with HBV-cirrhosis (supplementary Fig. 1).

Lean MASLD and qMRI-defined MASH

Among the MRI-confirmed MASLD participants (PDFF ≥ 5%, n = 124), 30% (n = 38/124) met the imaging threshold indicating MASH (cT1 ≥ 800 ms) and 5% (n = 7/124) passed the threshold for at-risk MASH (cT1 ≥ 875 ms). Using the recommended Asian BMI threshold < 23 kg/m2 [15], 17% (n = 21/124) of MASLD participants were classified as lean MASLD, of which n = 2/21 had lean MASH. Compared to non-lean MASLD, lean MASLD participants had lower cT1, PDFF and liver volume than those with non-lean MASLD (see supplementary Table 2).

When MASLD participants were stratified by cT1 category, 25% (n = 31/124) had cT1 ≥ 800ms and 6% (n = 7/124) had cT1 ≥ 875ms. Increased cT1 was associated with progressive increases in BMI, Aspartate Aminotransferase (AST), HbA1c, PDFF and liver volume. Increased fibro-inflammatory activity across cT1 categories was associated with slightly decreased liver iron content although this remained within normal range (supplementary Table 3).

Among the MASLD subgroup, participants with clinically defined cirrhosis demonstrated higher liver cT1 values and caudate-to-right-lobe ratio, as well as a larger spleen volume, but they had a lower PDFF value, compared to those without cirrhosis (Fig. 2).

The results of the model to identify cirrhosis held in both the MASLD subset and the whole population. A logistic regression model incorporating cT1, PDFF, caudate-to-right-lobe ratio, spleen volume demonstrated strong discriminatory performance for cirrhosis in MASLD (AUROC: 0.88, 95%CI: 0.84–0.93) (Fig. 3). A statistically meaningful difference of the risk score can also be observed across all considered disease aetiologies (i.e. MASLD, viral-hepatitis, liver cirrhosis and HCC). To factor in time-dependent risk, Cox proportional hazards analysis demonstrated that the baseline composite imaging score was significantly associated with subsequent HCC development in the whole cohort (age and sex adjusted hazard ratio (HR) per standard deviation increase in risk score 1.78, 95% CI 1.44–2.20, p < 0.001) and in the subset with cirrhosis (age and sex adjusted HR 1.55, 95% CI 1.21–1.99, p < 0.001). The model showed good discriminative performance (C-index 0.86 and 0.81 for total cohort and cirrhosis sub-cohort), supporting the prognostic value of the baseline composite score for short-term HCC risk stratification.

Discussion

The ELEGANCE study represents the first large-scale application of quantitative MRI to characterise chronic liver disease phenotypes within an Asian HCC surveillance cohort. Importantly, it is also amongst the first surveillance studies to include MASLD participants in an HCC surveillance programme irrespective of clinically defined cirrhosis, reflecting the evolving epidemiology of liver disease and HCC risk in Asia.

There are three key findings from this baseline imaging analysis study. Firstly, MASLD frequently co-exists with chronic HBV infection, highlighting a growing prevalence of overlapping metabolic-viral liver disease. Secondly, a substantial proportion of MASLD occurs in lean individuals, with a significant burden of imaging-defined MASH. Finally, cirrhosis was associated with a reproducible qMRI phenotype characterised by elevated fibro-inflammatory activity (cT1), reduced hepatic fat, spleen enlargement, and caudate hypertrophy. Notably, a novel model incorporating these features demonstrated strong discriminatory performance for cirrhosis within MASLD, and HCC within cirrhosis.

Our understanding of the interplay between steatosis, metabolic syndrome, HBV viral activity, and liver disease progression is still developing. It has been reported that concomitant hepatic steatosis in HBV patients is associated with better clinical outcomes, a lower risk of cirrhosis progression, HCC, mortality, and a higher probability in achieving a HBsAg seroclearance rate [3, 16]. Meanwhile, metabolic burden has been shown to adversely affect clinical outcomes in patients with HBV [17].

We observed that over 40% of participants with HBV had concomitant steatotic liver disease, consistent with prior reports, indicating a rising prevalence of metabolic liver disease in Asian patients. While HBV participants with higher hepatic fat content exhibited increased cT1 values and adverse metabolic profiles, suggesting a greater fibro-inflammatory burden, participants with HBV-cirrhosis showed lower fat, and higher cT1 compared to non-cirrhotic HBV. In line with these results and literature, we hypothesise that high liver fat in HBV in the absence of elevated cT1 and metabolic risk factors may not be a risk-enhancing factor in HCC surveillance. However, high cT1, particularly when fat is low, could warrant more intensive surveillance strategies. Further investigation of these phenotypes within surveillance cohorts is increasingly relevant, especially as disease-modifying therapies become available.

Our findings support the concept that following viral suppression, metabolic injury emerges as a dominant driver of liver disease progression. In this context, quantitative imaging biomarkers such as cT1 and PDFF may offer a practical means to monitor disease progression among patients with overlapping disease.

A predictive model derived from qMRI metrics consisting of cT1, hepatic steatosis (measured as PDFF), liver morphology and spleen volume demonstrated good discriminatory performance for advanced disease. Increases in cT1 have been linked to liver related endpoints [14], and decreases in PDFF to liver related mortality in chronic liver disease [18]. Additionally, the composite score derived from these qMRI metrics continued to increase in participants who went on to develop HCC. Importantly, these features align closely with the contemporary concept of compensated advanced chronic liver disease (cACLD), emphasising the continual spectrum of chronic liver disease rather than focusing on the traditional definition of cirrhosis and a binary histological outcome [19]. This distinction is clinically relevant as current MASLD guidelines largely restrict HCC surveillance to established cirrhotic patients, despite accumulating evidence that clinically significant risk may arise earlier within the cACLD spectrum [20–22]. Current non-invasive risk stratification in Asian populations largely focuses on fibrosis markers such as the FIB-4 fibrosis score and transient elastography, which have been shown to correlate well with major adverse liver outcomes [23]. In particular, serial changes provide incremental prognostic value for hepatic decompensation and liver-related outcomes, and are increasingly used for disease monitoring [24, 25]. Within this framework, qMRI may serve as a complementary phenotyping tool to improve baseline risk-stratification across the cACLD spectrum. qMRI may also be successfully leveraged for longitudinal monitoring: it has a repeatability coefficient lower than the level required to detect a clinically meaningful change, and has been shown to be more repeatable than both MR elastography and more widely accessible modalities such as vibration-controlled transient elastography (VCTE). As such it may also be more sensitive to detect subtle changes when used for disease monitoring [26]. Such strategies may improve precision in surveillance allocation, optimise resource utilisation, and bridge baseline disease characterisation with dynamic risk stratification in MASLD.

Several limitations of this study merit consideration. The analysis is cross-sectional and descriptive; longitudinal outcome data are required to validate prognostic implications – follow-up scans are being performed as part of the ongoing ELEGANCE study and will enable the assessment of how these imaging biomarkers can be used in a monitoring context of use. While the standardised qMRI metrics used in this study mean that results are scanner independent and generalisable, future work is ongoing to confirm these results in larger datasets with a different mix of Asian and European ethnicities. Future work in a larger confirmed HCC cohort could also assess whether quantitative imaging of the lesion itself or surrounding structure of the liver parenchyma, hepatic or portal vein could provide useful information about likely recurrence, response to treatment, or metastatic potential such as microvascular invasion [27]. Sensitivity analysis was not performed, and replication is needed in additional data sources. Cirrhosis was clinically defined, and some degree of misclassification is possible. Imaging-based definitions of MASH represent phenotypic stratification rather than diagnostic substitution. Finally, the small HCV subgroup precludes meaningful inference.

Conclusion

These results from the imaging arm of the ELEGANCE study have established comprehensive characterisation of different liver disease aetiologies in Singapore, demonstrating the importance of phenotyping at-risk populations undergoing HCC surveillance. qMRI enabled the identification of participants with differing severities of steatosis and steatohepatitis, the prevalence of lean MASLD, and overlapping disease between hepatic steatosis and viral hepatitis. qMRI demonstrated good diagnostic accuracy for distinguishing participants with cirrhosis, and in an exploratory analysis identified those at risk of new-onset HCC. By combining functional and morphological parameters, qMRI showed promising capability of risk-stratification in a diverse cohort of disease aetiologies.

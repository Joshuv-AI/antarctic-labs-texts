ASYMPTOTIC ISSUES FOR FRACTIONAL LAPLACIAN ON LONG CYLINDERS
Tahir Boudjeriou and Prosenjit Roy

Abstract
. In this paper, we are concerned with the asymptotic behavior of weak solutions to certain elliptic and parabolic problems involving the fractional p p -Laplacian in cylindrical domains that become unbounded in one direction. The nonlocal nature of the operator describing the equations creates several technical difficulties in treating problems of this type. The main results, obtained within a nonlocal abstract framework, extend and complement related properties established in the local setting. Key words : fractional p p -Laplacian, stationary fractional problem, parabolic fractional problem, asymptotic behavior of solutions, expanding cylindrical domains. 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification : 35B40, 35K55, 35K59, 35R11, 45K05, 47G20.

Introduction and the main results

Recently, the study of the fractional Laplacian and related problems has received increasing attention. The interest in such problems is driven by their applications in continuum mechanics, minimal surfaces, finance, optimization, and game theory; see, for example,
[
4

8

In this paper, we are inspired by some established results concerning the asymptotic behavior of weak solutions as
\ell\rightarrow+\infty
to the following problem

(P)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{llc}(-\Delta)^{s}u_{\ell}=f&\text{in}&D_{\ell},\\
u_{\ell}=0&\text{in}&\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash D_{\ell},\end{array}\right.

where
s\in(0,1)
,
D_{\ell}=(-\ell,\ell)^{m}\times\omega
,
1\leq m<N
,

, and
\omega
is an open set in
\mathbb{R}^{N-m}
.

To the best of our knowledge, the pioneering work on the asymptotic behavior of weak solutions to (
P
) as
\ell\rightarrow+\infty
was carried out by Yeressian
[
25
]
, who established the following result.

Theorem
.

Let
u_{\ell}
be the unique weak solution of (
P
) for
s=\frac{1}{2}
, and assume the following conditions are satisfied :

(1.1)

\text{support}\,(f)\subset D_{\ell}\backslash D_{\ell-1}\quad\text{and}\quad\|f\|_{L^{2}(D_{\ell})}\leq 1.

Then the following estimate holds

\int_{D_{1}}u_{\ell}^{2}(x)\,dx\leq\frac{C}{\ell^{2}}\;\;\;\;\text{for all}\;\;\ell>0,

where
C>0
is a constant independent of
\ell
.

Problems of this type arise in scale analysis, which provides valuable insight into the behaviour of complex fluid systems in the regime where some of the characteristic dimensionless parameters become small or infinitely large.

Later on, Chowdhury and Roy
[
13
]
extended Yeressian’s result to the case where
s\in(0,1)
. Furthermore, when
m=1
and the force term

is assumed to be defined only on
\omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N-1}
, that is,
f=f(x_{2},x_{3},\ldots,x_{N})
, the authors in
[
13
]
also proved the following result.

Theorem
.

Suppose that
s\in\left(\frac{1}{2},1\right)
and
f(x_{2},x_{3},\ldots,x_{N})\in L^{2}(\omega)
. Let
u_{\ell}
be the unique weak solution of (
P
) for each
\ell
, and let
u_{\infty}
be the unique weak solution to the following equation on the cross-section
\omega
of the cylinder
D_{\ell}

(1.2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{llc}(-\Delta^{\prime})^{s}u_{\infty}=f(x_{2},x_{3},\ldots,x_{N})&\text{in}&\omega,\\
u_{\infty}=0&\text{in}&\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\omega,\end{array}\right.

where
(-\Delta^{\prime})^{s}
denotes the
N-1
dimensional fractional Laplace operator. Then, for each
\alpha\in(0,1)
, the following estimate holds

\int_{D_{\alpha\ell}}|u_{\ell}-u_{\infty}|^{2}\,dx\leq\frac{1}{\ell^{2s-1}}\;\;\;\;\text{for all }\;\;\ell>0.

The above-mentioned results have been extended in
[
5

to the case of parabolic equations involving the fractional Laplacian. In

\left\{\begin{array}[]{llc}(-\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{N+k}})^{s}u_{\ell}=f_{\infty}&\text{in}&D^{N+k}_{\ell},\\

where

,

,

either the rectangle

denotes the
(N+k)

•

\lim\limits_{\ell\rightarrow+\infty}\inf\limits_{u\in H_{0}^{s}(D^{N+k}_{\ell})\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\left\langle(-\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{N+k}})^{s}u,u\right\rangle}{\|u\|_{L^{2}(D^{N+k}_{\ell})}^{2}}=\inf\limits_{u\in H_{0}^{s}(\omega^{N})\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\left\langle(-\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{N}})^{s}u,u\right\rangle}{\|u\|_{L^{2}(\omega^{N})}^{2}}=\lambda^{s}(\omega^{N}).

Let

be the unique weak solution of (

\frac{1}{\ell^{k}B^{k}_{1}}\int_{B^{k}_{\ell}}u_{\ell}(x,t)\,dt\rightarrow u_{\infty}\;\;\text{strongly in}\;H_{0}^{s}(\omega)\;\;\text{as}\;\ell\rightarrow+\infty,

\left\{\begin{array}[]{llc}(-\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{N}})^{s}u_{\infty}=f_{\infty}&\text{in}&\omega^{N},\\
u_{\infty}=0&\text{in}&\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\omega^{N}.\end{array}\right.

We emphasize that the asymptotic behavior of weak solutions to elliptic and parabolic equations involving the fractional Laplacian in domains becoming unbounded in one or several directions remains largely unaddressed in the literature and deserves further investigation. In fact, the analysis of such problems involving the fractional Laplacian is far from straightforward and significantly more involved than that of the corresponding local case, which was studied in the seminal paper by Chipot and Rougirel
[
10

\left\{\begin{array}[]{llc}\partial_{t}u_{\ell}-\text{div}(A(x,t)\nabla u_{\ell})=f(X_{2},t)&\text{in}&D_{\ell}\times(0,T),\\
u_{\ell}=0&\text{on}&\partial D_{\ell}\times(0,T),\\
u_{\ell}(x,0)=u_{0}(X_{2})&\text{in}&D_{\ell},\end{array}\right.

\left\{\begin{array}[]{llc}\partial_{t}u_{\infty}-\text{div}(A_{2,2}(x,t)\nabla_{X_{2}}u_{\infty})=f(X_{2},t)&\text{in}&\omega\times(0,T),\\
u_{\infty}=0&\text{on}&\partial\omega\times(0,T),\\

Next, we recall some known facts about fractional Sobolev spaces. Let
\Omega
be an open set in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. For any
s\in(0,1)
and
p\geq 2
, the space
W^{s,p}(\Omega)
is defined by

W^{s,p}(\Omega)=\left\{u\in L^{p}(\Omega):\,[u]_{s,p,\mathbb{R}^{N}}<+\infty\right\},

where
[u]_{s,p,\mathbb{R}^{N}}
is the so-called Gagliardo seminorm, given by

[u]^{p}_{s,p,\mathbb{R}^{N}}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\,dxdy.

We define the space
W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)
by

W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)=\left\{u\in W^{s,p}(\Omega):\ u=0\;\;\text{a.e. in}\;\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega\right\}

equipped with the norm

\|u\|:=\left(\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+[u]^{p}_{s,p,\mathbb{R}^{N}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Functions in
W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)
can be regarded as elements of
W_{0}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
by extending them by zero outside
\Omega
. For
1\leq p<\frac{N}{s}
, the fractional critical exponent is defined by
p^{*}_{s}=\frac{Np}{N-sp}
. If
1\leq r\leq p^{*}_{s}
, we have the fractional Sobolev embedding
W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{r}(\Omega)
, which is compact when
1\leq r<p^{*}_{s}
.
For more details on fractional Sobolev spaces and nonlocal fractional equations, we refer to the monograph by Molica Bisci, Rădulescu, and Servadei
[
20
]
.

For each
T>0
, we consider the parabolic space

W(0,T;W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega))=\left\{u\in L^{\infty}(0,T;W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)),\;u_{t}\;\text{exists and}\;u_{t}\in L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega)\right\}.

1.1.
The elliptic problem (
1.6
)

We now present our first main result, starting with the definition of weak solutions to the elliptic problem (
1.6
).

Definition 1.1
.

Let
f\in L^{2}(\Omega_{\ell})
. We say that
u_{\ell}\in W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega_{\ell})
is a weak solution of (
1.6
) if, for every
v\in W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega_{\ell})
, the following identity holds

(1.11)

\frac{C_{N,s,p}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\varphi_{p}(u_{\ell}(x)-u_{\ell}(y))(v(x)-v(y))}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\,dxdy=\int_{\Omega_{\ell}}fv\,dx.

The existence and uniqueness of a weak solution to (
1.6
) can be established by following the same approach as in
[
18
]
, applying the direct method in the calculus of variations to the functional
E:W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
given by

E(v)=\frac{C_{N,s,p}}{2p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|v(x)-v(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\,dxdy-\int_{\Omega_{\ell}}fv\,dx.

Assume that the function
f
depends only on
X_{2}
. We aim to study the asymptotic behavior of
u_{\ell}
as
\ell\rightarrow+\infty
on a fixed domain
\Omega_{\ell_{0}}
. More precisely, we intend to show that
u_{\ell}
converges to
u_{\infty}
in the
L^{p}
-norm where
u_{\infty}
is the unique weak solution of

(1.12)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{llc}(-\Delta^{\prime})_{p}^{s}u_{\infty}(X_{2})=f(X_{2})&\text{in}&\omega,\\
u_{\infty}(X_{2})=0&\text{in}&\mathbb{R}^{N-1}\backslash\omega,\end{array}\right.

where
(-\Delta^{\prime})^{s}_{p}
denotes the
N-1
dimensional fractional
p
-Laplace operator.

For
f\in L^{2}(\omega)
, we say that
u_{\infty}\in W_{0}^{s,p}(\omega)
is a weak solution of (
1.12
) if, for any
v\in W_{0}^{s,p}(\omega)
there holds

(1.13)

\frac{C_{N-1,s,p}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N-1}\times\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}\frac{\varphi_{p}(u_{\infty}(X_{2})-u_{\infty}(Y_{2}))(v(X_{2})-v(Y_{2}))}{|X_{2}-Y_{2}|^{N-1+sp}}\,dX_{2}dY_{2}=\int_{\omega}fv\,dX_{2}.

We note that the existence and uniqueness of a weak solution to (
1.12
) can be established in the same manner as above, by applying the direct method in the calculus of variations.

The first main result is stated as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
u_{\ell}
be the unique weak solution of (
1.6
) and
u_{\infty}
the unique weak solution of (
1.12
). Assume that
f\in L^{2}(\omega)
is independent of
x_{1}
, that is,
f(x)=f(X_{2})
.
Moreover, if
p>2
and
s\in\left(\frac{1}{p^{\prime}},1\right)
, then for any fixed

there holds

\|u_{\ell}-u_{\infty}\|_{p,\Omega_{\ell_{0}}}\precsim\left[\frac{1}{\ell^{s(p-1)-\frac{p-1}{p}}}+\frac{1}{\ell^{s-\frac{p-1}{p}}}\right]^{\frac{1}{p-1}},

for all sufficiently large
\ell>0
.

Remark 1
.

It is not known whether the result stated in Theorem
1.1
can be extended to more general domains of the form
\Omega_{\ell}=(-\ell,\ell)^{m}\times\omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{N-m}
for any

also remains an open question.

We recall that, for the classical

-Laplace operator with Dirichlet boundary conditions, Chipot and Xie
[

proved the estimate

\|\nabla\left(u_{\ell}-u_{\infty}\right)\|_{p,\Omega_{\ell_{0}}}\precsim\frac{\|\partial_{x_{2}}u_{\infty}\|_{p,\omega}}{\ell^{\frac{2}{p(p-2)}}},

where
p>2
and
u_{\infty}
denotes the unique solution of

\left\{\begin{array}[]{llc}-\Delta_{p}u_{\infty}(x_{2})=f(x_{2})&\text{in}&(-1,1),\\
u_{\infty}\in W_{0}^{1,p}(-1,1).\end{array}\right.

.

The second main result of this paper is presented in the following theorem.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
u_{\ell}
and
u_{\infty}
be the unique weak solutions of (
1.7
) and (
1.16
), respectively. Assume that the functions
f\in L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\omega))
and
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\omega)
are independent of

, that is,

f(x,t)=f(X_{2},t),\;\;\;\;u_{0}(x)=u_{0}(X_{2}).

If
p>2
and
s\in\left(\frac{1}{p^{\prime}},1\right)
, then for any fixed
\ell_{0}>0
we have

\|u_{\ell}-u_{\infty}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega_{\ell_{0}}))}+\|u_{\ell}-u_{\infty}\|^{p}_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{p}(\Omega_{\ell_{0}}))}\precsim\frac{1}{\ell^{sp-1}}+\frac{1}{\ell^{\frac{sp}{p-1}-1}},

for all sufficiently large

\ell>0
.

The proofs of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
rely on energy estimates combined with a fractional
p

As noted by Yeressian
[
25

, the estimates established in Theorems
1.1

1.2
play an important role in numerical computations of solutions in large domains, particularly when the interest lies in computing the solution within a smaller subdomain. Integrating this over
(0,t)
and applying Proposition
2.1
together with Hölder’s inequality, we find

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|u_{\infty}\sqrt{\rho^{p}_{\ell}}\|^{2}_{2,\Omega_{\ell}}+\frac{C_{N,s}}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u_{\infty}(X_{2},\sigma)-u_{\infty}(Y_{2},\sigma)|^{p}\rho^{p}_{\ell}(x_{1})}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\,dxdyd\sigma

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ell\|f\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\omega))}\|u_{\infty}\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{p}(\omega))}+\ell\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{2,\omega}

\displaystyle+2^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\epsilon\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u_{\infty}(X_{2},\sigma)-u_{\infty}(Y_{2},\sigma)|^{p}\rho^{p}_{\ell}(x_{1})}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\,dxdyd\sigma

\displaystyle+C(\epsilon)\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u_{\infty}(Y_{2},\sigma)|^{p}|\rho_{\ell}(x_{1})-\rho_{\ell}(y_{1})|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\,dxdyd\sigma,\;\;\;\text{for all}\;\epsilon>0.

Finally, choosing

sufficiently small and absorbing the corresponding term on the left-hand side yields the desired estimate.
∎

Recall the definitions

\left[\Lambda^{1}_{\ell}(t)\right]^{\frac{p}{p-1}}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}|v_{\ell}(x,y,t)|^{p}\rho^{p}_{\ell}(x_{1})\,d\mu(x,y)\right)^{\frac{p-2}{p-1}}=\left[\Sigma_{\ell}^{1}(t)\right]^{\frac{p-2}{p-1}},

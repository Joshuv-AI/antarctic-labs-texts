Counting 𝔽 q {\mathbb{F}}_{q} -points of orbital varieties in ad-nilpotent ideals of type A n A_{n}
Mohammad Bardestani, Keivan Mallahi-Karai, Samrith Ram, Hadi Salmasian

Abstract
. Let 𝔟 n ​ ( 𝔽 q ) \mathfrak{b}_{n}({\mathbb{F}}_{q}) denote the Lie algebra of upper triangular n × n n\times n matrices with entries in the finite field 𝔽 q {\mathbb{F}}_{q} . For every 𝔟 n ​ ( 𝔽 q ) \mathfrak{b}_{n}({\mathbb{F}}_{q}) -stable ideal 𝔞 \mathfrak{a} of the Lie algebra 𝔲 n ​ ( 𝔽 q ) \mathfrak{u}_{n}({\mathbb{F}}_{q}) of strictly upper triangular matrices, and every partition μ \mu of n n , we prove two explicit formulas for the number of elements of 𝔞 \mathfrak{a} of Jordan type μ \mu : the first one is given by the Hall scalar product of a modified Hall-Littlewood function indexed by μ \mu and a chromatic quasisymmetric function associated to 𝔞 \mathfrak{a} . The second one is given in terms of a sum of products of q q -integers over certain standard tableaux that are compatible with a partial order associated to 𝔞 \mathfrak{a} . In the special case that 𝔞 = 𝔲 Λ ​ ( 𝔽 q ) \mathfrak{a}=\mathfrak{u}_{\Lambda}({\mathbb{F}}_{q}) , where 𝔲 Λ ​ ( 𝔽 q ) \mathfrak{u}_{\Lambda}({\mathbb{F}}_{q}) is the nilradical of the standard parabolic subalgebra of 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 n ​ ( 𝔽 q ) \mathfrak{gl}_{n}({\mathbb{F}}_{q}) associated to a composition Λ \Lambda of n n , our formulas reduce to the statement (recently proved in [ 20 , Theorem 5.13] ) that up to an explicit polynomial factor in q q , the number of elements in 𝔲 Λ ​ ( 𝔽 q ) \mathfrak{u}_{\Lambda}({\mathbb{F}}_{q}) of Jordan type μ \mu is equal to the coefficient of the monomial 𝐱 Λ {\bf x}^{\Lambda} in the specialization of the dual Macdonald symmetric function Q μ ′ ​ ( 𝐱 , q − 1 , t ) \mathrm{Q}_{\mu^{\prime}}({\bf x};q^{-1},t) at t = 0 t=0 . We obtain a new and shorter proof of the latter assertion using a parabolic variation of Borodin’s division algorithm [ 4 , 23 ] . We give three applications of our main result stated above. First, we obtain an analogous formula for the number of points of a nilpotent Hessenberg variety. Second, we give an explicit formula for the number of X ∈ 𝔲 Λ ​ ( 𝔽 q ) X\in\mathfrak{u}_{\Lambda}({\mathbb{F}}_{q}) that satisfy X 2 = 0 X^{2}=0 . In the special case Λ = ( 1 n ) \Lambda=(1^{n}) , our formula is different from the one conjectured by Kirillov and Melnikov [ 21 ] (and proved by Ekhad and Zeilberger [ 9 ] ). However, we obtain a new proof of the Kirillov-Melnikov-Ekhad-Zeilberger formula using our latter result and a formula for two-rowed Macdonald polynomials by Jing and Józefiak [ 18 ] . As a byproduct, we demonstrate that a recurrence relation of Kirillov in [ 22 ] for the number of strictly upper triangular matrices of Jordan type μ \mu is a straightforward consequence of our main results combined with the Cauchy identity for Macdonald polynomials. Third, we give an explicit formula for the number of double cosets 𝖴 1 \ GL n ​ ( 𝔽 q ) / 𝖴 2 \mathsf{U}_{1}\backslash\mathrm{GL}_{n}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})/\mathsf{U}_{2} where 𝖴 1 \mathsf{U}_{1} and 𝖴 2 \mathsf{U}_{2} denote unipotent subgroups of GL n ​ ( 𝔽 q ) \mathrm{GL}_{n}({\mathbb{F}}_{q}) that correspond to two 𝔟 n ​ ( 𝔽 q ) \mathfrak{b}_{n}({\mathbb{F}}_{q}) -stable ideals of 𝔲 n ​ ( 𝔽 q ) \mathfrak{u}_{n}({\mathbb{F}}_{q}) .



§ 1. Introduction

Let
\mathsf{U}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
be the group of
n\times n
upper-triangular matrices over the finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with all the diagonal entries equal to 1, and let
\mathfrak{u}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
denote the Lie algebra of
n\times n
upper-triangular nilpotent matrices over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. In an influential and elegant paper

[
23
]
, Kirillov initiated a comprehensive study of the orbit method for
\mathsf{U}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, establishing various connections between the adjoint and coadjoint orbit structures of

\mathsf{U}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
and

\mathfrak{u}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})

and representation theory.
In connection to these ideas, Kirillov posed the following question:

Given a partition
\mu
of
n
, count the number of
X\in\mathfrak{u}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
of Jordan type
\mu
.

This problem was investigated by Kirillov himself and revisited later on by Borodin
[
4
]
, who used a technique called “the division algorithm” to provide a recursive formula for the number of these matrices. Borodin applied this formula to study the asymptotic behavior of the Jordan type of strictly upper triangular
n\times n
matrices as
n\to\infty
.

One can think of
\mathfrak{u}_{n}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
as the nilradical of the standard Borel subalgebra of
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
. Then Kirillov’s question is about the number of points of the (not necessarily irreducible) variety obtained as the intersection of
\mathfrak{u}_{n}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
and a nilpotent orbit of
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
. At least over
\mathbb{C}
, such varieties are sometimes called
orbital varieties
; see for example
[
19
]
.
From this viewpoint it is natural to ask Kirillov’s question for nilradicals of other parabolic subalgebras,
or even more generally, for any ideal of
\mathfrak{u}_{n}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
that is stable under the action of the standard Borel subalgebra

\mathfrak{b}_{n}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})

of
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
. The latter ideals are sometimes simply called
ad-nilpotent ideals
of
\mathfrak{u}_{n}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
. Their interaction with nilpotent orbits has attracted attention (for example see
[
8
,
7
,
10
]
). Our primary goal in this paper is to answer this more general form of Kirillov’s question (see
Theorems

1.12
,
1.15
and
1.5
). Subsequently, we give various applications of the latter theorems.

Recall that a composition
\Lambda
of
n
is a finite tuple of positive integers whose sum equals
n
.
When the parts of the composition form a weakly decreasing sequence, we call it a partition.
The standard parabolic subgroup of
{\mathrm{G}}=\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
associated to
\Lambda
is denoted by
\mathrm{P}_{\Lambda}=\mathrm{P}_{\Lambda}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, and the unipotent radical
of
\mathrm{P}_{\Lambda}

is denoted by
\mathrm{U}_{\Lambda}=\mathrm{U}_{\Lambda}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. The
nilradical of the parabolic subalgebra of
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
associated to
\Lambda
will be denoted by
\mathfrak{u}_{\Lambda}=\mathfrak{u}_{\Lambda}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
.
For instance when
\Lambda=(2,3,1)
we have:

\mathrm{P}_{\Lambda}=\scalebox{0.8}{$\begin{pNiceMatrix}\Block[fill=gray]{2-2}{}\ast&\ast&\ast&\ast&\ast&\ast\\
\ast&\ast&\ast&\ast&\ast&\ast\\
0&0&\Block[fill=gray]{3-3}{}\ast&\ast&\ast&\ast\\
0&0&\ast&\ast&\ast&\ast\\
0&0&\ast&\ast&\ast&\ast\\
0&0&0&0&0&\Block[fill=gray]{1-1}{}\ast\end{pNiceMatrix}$},\,\,\mathrm{U}_{\Lambda}=\scalebox{0.8}{$\begin{pNiceMatrix}\Block[fill=gray]{2-2}{}1&0&\ast&\ast&\ast&\ast\\
0&1&\ast&\ast&\ast&\ast\\
0&0&\Block[fill=gray]{3-3}{}1&0&0&\ast\\
0&0&0&1&0&\ast\\
0&0&0&0&1&\ast\\
0&0&0&0&0&\Block[fill=gray]{1-1}{}1\end{pNiceMatrix}$},\,\,\mathfrak{u}_{\Lambda}=\scalebox{0.8}{$\begin{pNiceMatrix}\Block[fill=gray]{2-2}{}0&0&\ast&\ast&\ast&\ast\\
0&0&\ast&\ast&\ast&\ast\\
0&0&\Block[fill=gray]{3-3}{}0&0&0&\ast\\
0&0&0&0&0&\ast\\
0&0&0&0&0&\ast\\
0&0&0&0&0&\Block[fill=gray]{1-1}{}0\end{pNiceMatrix}$},

where the
*
’s represent arbitrary elements of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
.
For
\Lambda=(1^{n})
, we obtain
\mathfrak{u}_{\Lambda}=\mathfrak{u}_{n}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
.

There is a simple yet quite useful characterization of the ad-nilpotent ideals of
\mathfrak{u}_{n}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
, connecting them to Dyck paths.
To give this characterization, we need the notion of a Hessenberg function.

Definition 1.1

(Hessenberg function)
.

A Hessenberg function is a non-decreasing function
\mathsf{h}\colon[n]\to[n]
such that
\mathsf{h}(i)\geq i
for every
i\in[n]
, where
[n]:=\{1,\dots,n\}
. The set of all Hessenberg functions on
[n]
will be denoted by
\mathcal{H}_{n}
. We sometimes identify a Hessenberg function by the
n
-tuple
(\mathsf{h}(1),\ldots,\mathsf{h}(n))
.

One can naturally associate a graph to
\mathsf{h}
with vertex set
[n]
and set of edges
E=\{\{i,j\}:i<j\leq\mathsf{h}(i)\}
. This graph, known as the
indifference graph
of
\mathsf{h}
, will be denoted by
\mathsf{G}(\mathsf{h})
. A Hessenberg function
\mathsf{h}\in\mathcal{H}_{n}
also produces a partial order on
[n]
by declaring
i\prec_{\mathsf{h}}j
if and only if
\mathsf{h}(i)<j
. We denote the poset
([n],\preceq_{\mathsf{h}})
by
\mathcal{P}_{\mathsf{h}}
. Associated to
\mathsf{h}
, we also define the following Lie subalgebra of
\mathfrak{u}_{n}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
:

(1)

\mathfrak{u}_{\mathsf{h}}=\mathfrak{u}_{\mathsf{h}}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=}\{(a_{ij})\in\mathfrak{u}_{n}({\mathbb{F}}_{q}):a_{ij}=0\mbox{ for }j\leq\mathsf{h}(i)\}.

The following assertion is well-known and straightforward to prove.

Proposition 1.3
.

The assignment
\mathsf{h}\mapsto\mathfrak{u}_{\mathsf{h}}
is a bijective correspondence between
\mathcal{H}_{n}
and ad-nilpotent ideals of
\mathfrak{u}_{n}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
.

Throughout the paper, we denote
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathsf{h}(i)
by
|\mathsf{h}|
.
The (non-commutative) product of
\mathsf{h}_{1}\in\mathcal{H}_{n}
and
\mathsf{h}_{2}\in\mathcal{H}_{m}
is defined by

\mathsf{h}=\mathsf{h}_{1}\sqcup\mathsf{h}_{2}\in\mathcal{H}_{n+m}\qquad\mathsf{h}(i)=\begin{cases}\mathsf{h}_{1}(i)&1\leq i\leq n;\\
n+\mathsf{h}_{2}(i-n)&n+1\leq i\leq n+m.\end{cases}

The Hessenberg function
k_{n}=(n,\dots,n)\in\mathcal{H}_{n}
is called the complete Hessenberg function of size
n
. To each composition
\Lambda=(\Lambda_{1},\dots,\Lambda_{s})
of
n
we assign the Hessenberg function

(2)

k_{\Lambda}\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=}k_{\Lambda_{1}}\sqcup k_{\Lambda_{2}}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup k_{\Lambda_{s}},

and we note that
\mathfrak{u}_{k_{\Lambda}}=\mathfrak{u}_{\Lambda}
.

For any
k\geq 1
let
J_{k}
be the
k\times k
nilpotent (upper triangular) Jordan block.
Given a partition
\mu=(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{k})
of
n
, we use
J_{\mu}
to denote the square block-diagonal matrix of size
|\mu|=\mu_{1}+\cdots+\mu_{k}
with diagonal blocks
J_{\mu_{1}},\ldots,J_{\mu_{k}}
. We say that the Jordan type of a matrix is
\mu
when its Jordan form is
J_{\mu}
.

Definition 1.4

(Jordan type counting function)
.

Let
\mu
be a partition of
n
.
For a given composition
\Lambda
of
n
we set

(3)

\mathrm{F}_{\mu\Lambda}(q)\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=}|\mathcal{C}_{\mu}\cap\mathfrak{u}_{\Lambda}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})|,

where
\mathcal{C}_{\mu}=\mathcal{C}_{\mu}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
is the
\mathrm{GL}_{n}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
-conjugacy class of
J_{\mu}
.
Similarly, for a Hessenberg function
h\in\mathcal{H}_{n}
we set

(4)

\mathrm{F}_{\mu\mathsf{h}}(q)\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=}|\mathcal{C}_{\mu}\cap\mathfrak{u}_{\mathsf{h}}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})|.

1.1. Main results

In order to state our first main result (see
Theorem

1.5
), we start by reviewing the definition of the chromatic quasisymmetric functions introduced in an influential paper by Shareshian and Wachs
[
29
]
.
Let
\mathsf{G}=(V,E)
be a graph with
V
a finite subset of
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
. Given a subset
S
of
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, a proper
S
-coloring of
\mathsf{G}
is a function
\kappa\colon V\to S
such that
\kappa(i)\neq\kappa(j)
whenever
\{i,j\}\in E
. Let
\mathcal{C}(\mathsf{G})
be the set of proper
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
-colorings of
\mathsf{G}
. Stanley
[
30
]
defined the chromatic symmetric function of
\mathsf{G}
as

X_{\mathsf{G}}({\bf x}):=\sum_{\kappa\in\mathcal{C}(\mathsf{G})}\prod_{v\in V}x_{\kappa(v)},

where
{\bf x}=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots)
. The
chromatic quasisymmetric function
of
\mathsf{G}=(V,E)
is a
q
-deformation of Stanley’s chromatic symmetric function defined in
[
29
]
by

\displaystyle X_{\mathsf{G}}({\bf x};q):=\sum_{\kappa\in\mathcal{C}(\mathsf{G})}q^{\mathrm{asc}({\kappa})}\prod_{v\in V}x_{\kappa(v)},

where
\mathrm{asc}(\kappa):=\#\{\{i,j\}\in E(\mathsf{G}):i<j\text{ and }\kappa(i)<\kappa(j)\}
,
is the number of ascents of the coloring
\kappa
. The chromatic quasisymmetric function is not symmetric in general. However, for the graphs
\mathsf{G}(\mathsf{h})
, where
\mathsf{h}\in\mathcal{H}_{n}
, Shareshian and Wachs
[
29
, Theorem 4.5]
show that
X_{\mathsf{G}(\mathsf{h})}({\bf x},q)
is a symmetric function with coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}[q]
. Therefore,
X_{\mathsf{G}(\mathsf{h})}({\bf x},q)
can be expressed in terms of the elementary symmetric functions:

X_{\mathsf{G}(\mathsf{h})}({\bf x};q)=\sum_{\lambda\,\vdash n}c_{\lambda\mathsf{h}}(q)e_{\lambda}({\bf x}).

An explicit formula for the coefficients
c_{\lambda\mathsf{h}}(q)
was recently given by Hikita
[
15
, Theorem 1.7]
.

Recall that the length of a partition

\mu
, denoted
by
\ell(\mu)
, is the number of its parts.
It is convenient to define
\mu_{i}=0
for all
i>\ell(\mu)
. The conjugate of
\mu
, denoted by
\mu^{\prime}=(\mu_{1}^{\prime},\dots,\mu_{l}^{\prime})
, is defined by

\mu^{\prime}_{j}=\#\{1\leq i\leq k:\mu_{i}\geq j\}.

Given partitions
\mu
and
\nu
of
n
, we say that
\nu
“dominates”
\mu
, and we write
\mu\unlhd\nu
, if and only if

\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}+\dots+\mu_{i}\leq\nu_{1}+\nu_{2}+\dots+\nu_{i},\quad\text{for }i\geq 1.

Assigned to each partition
\mu
we define

(5)

\mathsf{n}(\mu)\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=}\sum_{i\geq 1}(i-1)\mu_{i}=\sum_{i\geq 1}\binom{\mu_{i}^{\prime}}{2}.

Let
\tilde{\mathrm{H}}_{\mu}({\bf x};t)
denote the
modified Hall-Littlewood function
indexed by
\mu
, obtained from the modified Macdonald polynomials of
[
14
]
by specialization at
q=0
. More concretely, if
J_{\mu}({\bf x};q,t)
is the integral form of the Macdonald polynomial
[
25
, p. 352, Eq. (8.3)]
then

\tilde{\mathrm{H}}_{\mu}({\bf x};t)=t^{\mathsf{n}(\mu)}J_{\mu}\left[\frac{{\bf x}}{1-t^{-1}};0,t^{-1}\right].

For a non-negative integer
n
we adopt the following conventions:

[0]_{q}\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=}0,\quad[n]_{q}\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=}\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1},\ \ [0]_{q}!\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=}1,\ \ [n]_{q}!\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=}[1]_{q}\cdots[n]_{q},\ \ {n\brack k}_{q}\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=}\frac{[n]_{q}!}{[k]_{q}![n-k]_{q}!}.

Let
\mathrm{Z}_{\mathrm{G}}(I+J_{\mu})
denote the centralizer of
I+J_{\mu}
in
{\mathrm{G}}
. By
[
25
, p. 181, Eq. (1.6)]
we have

(6)

\left|\mathrm{Z}_{\mathrm{G}}(I+J_{\mu})\right|=q^{n+2\mathsf{n}(\mu)}\prod_{i\geq 1}\varphi_{m_{i}(\mu)}\left(1/q\right),

where
\varphi_{m}(t)=(1-t)(1-t^{2})\cdots(1-t^{m})
and
m_{i}(\mu)=\mu_{i}^{\prime}-\mu^{\prime}_{i+1}
. Indeed

(7)

\varphi_{m}\left(1/q\right)=[m]_{q}!(q-1)^{m}q^{-\binom{m}{2}-m}.

We are now able to state the first main result of this paper. Henceforth we denote the Hall scalar product
[
25
, Sec. I.4]
of symmetric functions by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\mathsf{h}\in\mathcal{H}_{n}
be a Hessenberg function and let
\mu
be a partition of
n
. Then
\mathrm{F}_{\mu\mathsf{h}}(q)\in{\mathbb{Z}}[q]
is a polynomial in
q
and

(8)

\mathrm{F}_{\mu\mathsf{h}}(q)=|\mathfrak{u}_{\mathsf{h}}|\sum_{\lambda\,\vdash n}\frac{c_{\lambda\mathsf{h}}(q)}{[\lambda]_{q}!}\frac{\mathrm{F}_{\mu\lambda}(q)}{|\mathfrak{u}_{\lambda}|}=\frac{q^{n^{2}-|\mathsf{h}|}(q-1)^{n}}{|\mathrm{Z}_{\mathrm{G}}(I+J_{\mu})|}\langle\tilde{\mathrm{H}}_{\mu}({\bf x};q),X_{\mathsf{G}(\mathsf{h})}({\bf x};q)\rangle.

The strategy of the proof of
Theorem

1.5
is to reduce the computation of
\mathrm{F}_{\mu\mathsf{h}}(q)
to the special case
\mathrm{F}_{\mu\lambda}(q)
using the
modular law
of Abreu and Nigro
[
2
]
(see
), and then use the fact that
\mathrm{F}_{\mu\lambda}(q)
can be expressed in terms of monomial coefficients of the
q
-Whittaker functions
\mathrm{P}_{\mu^{\prime}}({\bf x};1/q,0)
(see
Theorem

1.15
).
We remark that
Theorem

1.15
has also been proved recently by Karp and Thomas
[
20
, Theorem 5.13]
.
In
[
20
]
,
the authors extract
Theorem

1.15
from a combination of results, including a probabilistic bijection between non-negative integer matrices and pairs of semistandard Young tableaux of the same shape, which they call the
q
-Burge correspondence.
Our proof of
Theorem

1.15

is more direct: first we obtain a recursive formula for
\mathrm{F}_{\mu\lambda}(q)
with explicit coefficients (see
).
Then we solve this recursion by a simple iterative substitution. This leads in a natural way to a solution for the recursion in terms of the well-known tableau product formulas that occur in the coefficients of Macdonald polynomials!

As usual, to any
\mu=(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{k})
we associate a Young diagram of shape
\mu
: a left-justified shape of
k
rows of boxes, where the
i
-th row from the top has
\mu_{i}
boxes for
1\leq i\leq k
. A
standard
Young tableau is a filling of the Young diagram
of
\mu

by
\{1,\ldots,|\mu|\}

with strictly increasing rows and columns. A
semi-standard
Young tableau of shape
\mu
is
a filling of this Young diagram by positive integers
with
weakly increasing rows and strictly increasing columns.
A semi-standard Young tableau with shape
\mu
has
content

\Lambda
if there are
\Lambda_{i}
occurrences of
i
for every
i\geq 1
. The number of semi-standard Young tableaux with shape
\mu
and content
\Lambda
is called a
Kostka number
and it is denoted by
{\mathrm{K}}_{\mu\Lambda}
.

Our second main result is a tableau formula for
\mathrm{F}_{\mu\mathsf{h}}(q)
using a statistic introduced by
[
6
,
3
]
. We use the same notation as
[
3
]
. Let
\mathrm{SYT}^{\mathsf{h}}_{\mu}
be the set of standard Young tableaux of shape
\mu
such that if
i
is above
j
in the same column, then
i\prec_{\mathsf{h}}j
. For
T\in\mathrm{SYT}^{\mathsf{h}}_{\mu}
and a box
b\in T
, let
\mathrm{coleg}(b)
(resp.
\mathrm{arm}(b)
) be the number of boxes strictly above
b
in its column (resp. strictly to its right in its row). The entry in
b
is denoted by
T(b)
.
For
T\in\mathrm{SYT}^{\mathsf{h}}_{\mu}
and a box
b\in T
, let
\gamma(T,b)
be the number of boxes
c
in any row above
b
such that
T(c)<T(b)
but
T(c)\nprec_{\mathsf{h}}T(b)
. Set
\gamma(T,b)=0
if
\mathrm{coleg}(b)=0
. Equivalently,
\gamma(T,b)
counts entries in any row above
b
that are smaller in the usual order on
{\mathbb{Z}}
but not in the partial order generated by
\mathsf{h}
. Define

\gamma(T):=\sum_{\mathrm{coleg}(b)\geq 1}\gamma(T,b)
.
If
\mathrm{coleg}(b)\geq 1
, let
\mathrm{up}(b)
denote the box directly above
b
. We now consider the following polynomial in
q
:

\underline{\mathrm{F}}_{\mu\mathsf{h}}(q)\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=}\sum_{T\in\mathrm{SYT}^{\mathsf{h}}_{\mu}}q^{\gamma(T)}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}b\in T\\
\mathrm{coleg}(b)\geq 1\end{subarray}}[\mathrm{arm}_{\mathsf{h}}(\mathrm{up}(b),T(b))+1]_{q}

where
\mathrm{arm}_{\mathsf{h}}(\mathrm{up}(b),T(b))
is the number of boxes
c
on the right side of
\mathrm{up}(b)
in the same row of
\mathrm{up}(b)
such that
T(c)\prec_{\mathsf{h}}T(b)
. Note that the coefficients of
\underline{\mathrm{F}}_{\mu\mathsf{h}}(q)
are non-negative integers.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
\mathsf{h}\in\mathcal{H}_{n}
be a Hessenberg function and let
\mu
be a partition of
n
. Then

\mathrm{F}_{\mu\mathsf{h}}(q)=q^{\binom{n}{2}-\mathsf{n}(\mu)-(n-\ell(\mu))}(q-1)^{n-\ell(\mu)}\underline{\mathrm{F}}_{\mu^{\prime}\mathsf{h}}(1/q).

\mu
and
\mathsf{h}
we have
\mathrm{F}_{\mu\mathsf{h}}(q)\neq 0
. In
Theorem

1.7
, we address this problem and indeed prove a stronger result over an arbitrary field. Let
\mathcal{P}
be any poset of cardinality
n
and let

denote the maximum possible cardinality of a union
\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}C_{i}
where each

is a chain in
\mathcal{P}
. For each

, define integers
\lambda_{i}
by

for

. Then it can be shown that the sequence

is weakly decreasing and forms a partition of
n
(see Britz and Fomin
[
5

.
Denote by
\lambda_{\mathcal{P}}
the Greene-Kleitman shape of
\mathcal{P}
and write
\lambda_{\mathsf{h}}
for
\lambda_{\mathcal{P}_{\mathsf{h}}}
when
\mathsf{h}\in\mathcal{H}_{n}
is a Hessenberg function.
Our third main result is the following.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
{\mathbb{F}}
be an arbitrary field, and let
\mathsf{h}\in\mathcal{H}_{n}
be a Hessenberg function with Greene–Kleitman shape
\lambda_{\mathsf{h}}
.
Let
\mathcal{C}_{\mu}=\mathcal{C}_{\mu}({\mathbb{F}})
denote the

-conjugacy class of

.

\mu
of

, we have

if and only if
\mu\unlhd\lambda_{\mathsf{h}}
.

1.2. The intermediate special case of
\mathrm{F}_{\mu\lambda}(q)

Recall from
Section

1.1
that the computation of
\mathrm{F}_{\mu\mathsf{h}}(q)
is reduced to the computation of
\mathrm{F}_{\mu\lambda}(q)
, which is achieved in
Theorem

1.12
and
Theorem

1.15
. In this subsection, we describe the latter theorem and its corollaries.

For partitions
\nu
and
\mu
, we write
\nu\subseteq\mu
to mean that the Young diagram of
\mu
contains the Young diagram of
\nu
, i.e.,
\nu_{i}\leq\mu_{i}
for all
i\geq 1
. The set-theoretic difference of the Young diagrams of
\mu
and
\nu
, denoted by
\mu/\nu
, is called a
skew diagram
.
A
horizontal
k
-strip
is a skew diagram with
k
boxes with no two squares in the same column. For instance the skew diagram

(5,4,4,1)/(4,4,2)
is a
4
-horizontal strip:

For a composition
\Lambda
of
n
we use

\mathrm{sort}(\Lambda)
to denote the partition obtained by sorting the parts of
\Lambda
in decreasing order.

Definition 1.11
.

Let
\lambda
and
\mu
be partitions of
n
with
\ell(\lambda)=s
. We define

b_{\mu\lambda}(q)\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=}\sum_{\mathcal{F}}\prod_{j=1}^{s}\theta_{\mu^{j}/\mu^{j-1}}(q),

with

(9)

\quad\theta_{\eta/\rho}(q)\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=}\frac{\left[|\eta|-|\rho|\right]_{q}!}{\left[\eta_{1}-\rho_{1}\right]_{q}!}\,\prod_{i\geq 1}{\rho_{i}-\rho_{i+1}\brack\eta_{i+1}-\rho_{i+1}}_{q},

where
\eta/\rho
is a horizontal strip and
\mathcal{F}:=\mathcal{F}(\mu,\lambda)

is the set of all chains of partitions

\emptyset=\mu^{0}\subseteq\mu^{1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\mu^{s}=\mu
,
such that
\mu^{j}/\mu^{j-1}
is a horizontal
\lambda_{j}
-strip.
By convention,

b_{\mu\lambda}(q)=0
when
\mathcal{F}=\emptyset
.
Note that in
Eq.

9
we have
\rho_{i}-\rho_{i+1}\geq\eta_{i+1}-\rho_{i+1}
because
\eta/\rho
is a horizontal strip.

Theorem 1.12
.

Let
\Lambda
be a composition of
n
and set
\lambda=\mathrm{sort}(\Lambda)
. Let
\mu
be a partition of
n
.
Then

\mathrm{F}_{\mu\Lambda}(q)\neq 0

if and only if
\lambda\unlhd\mu^{\prime}
.
When
\lambda\unlhd\mu^{\prime}
, the following hold:

(1)

\mathrm{F}_{\mu\Lambda}(q)=\mathrm{F}_{\mu\lambda}(q)=(q-1)^{n-\ell(\mu)}q^{\binom{n}{2}-\mathsf{n}(\mu)-(n-\ell(\mu))}\,\,b_{\mu^{\prime}\lambda}(1/q)
.

(2)

\mathrm{F}_{\mu\lambda}(q)\in{\mathbb{Z}}[q]
, the degree of

\mathrm{F}_{\mu\lambda}(q)

is
\binom{n}{2}-\mathsf{n}(\mu)
, and its leading coefficient is
{\mathrm{K}}_{\mu^{\prime}\lambda}
, the Kostka number of shape
\mu^{\prime}
and content
\lambda
.

(3)

There exists a polynomial

\mathrm{R}_{\mu\lambda}(q)\in\mathbb{Z}[q]
such that
\mathrm{R}_{\mu\lambda}(0)=1
,
\mathrm{R}_{\mu\lambda}(1)>0
, and

(10)

\mathrm{F}_{\mu\lambda}(q)=(q-1)^{n-\ell(\mu)}q^{\binom{n}{2}-\binom{\ell(\mu)}{2}-\sum_{j\geq 1}\mu_{j}^{\prime}\mu_{j+1}^{\prime}}\,\mathrm{R}_{\mu\lambda}(q).

Remark 1.13
.

When the composition
\Lambda
has only one part i.e.,
\Lambda=(n)
, we have
\mathfrak{u}_{\Lambda}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})=\{0\}
. Thus
\mathcal{C}_{\mu}\cap\mathfrak{u}_{\Lambda}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})\neq\emptyset
if and only if
\mu=(1^{n})
. In this case the zero matrix is the only element in
\mathcal{C}_{\mu}\cap\mathfrak{u}_{\Lambda}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
and so
\mathrm{F}_{\mu\Lambda}(q)=1
.

Parts (2) and (3) of
Theorem

1.12
extend some of the results of

[
4
]
and
[
11
, Theorem 4.1]
. The main tool in the proof of
Theorem

1.12
is a parabolic variant of Borodin’s division algorithm to reduce the counting problem from
\mathfrak{u}_{\Lambda}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
to a smaller nilradical.

For
\lambda=(1^{n})
, Fulman
[
12
]

\mathrm{F}_{\mu\lambda}(q)
can be expressed in terms of the Hall-Littlewood
polynomials. In the special case
\lambda=(1^{n})
,
the formula given for
\mathrm{F}_{\mu\lambda}(q)
in

1.12
is different from Fulman’s result. The

are related to the coefficients of Macdonald polynomials up to an explicit normalization factor, as we explain below.

Definition 1.14
.

Let
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{s})
be a partition of
n
. For any partition
\mu\vdash n
, we define

(11)

a_{\mu\lambda}(q)\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=}\sum_{\mathcal{F}}\prod_{j=1}^{s}\psi_{\mu^{j}/\mu^{j-1}}(q)\quad\text{with}\quad\psi_{\eta/\rho}(q)\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=}\prod_{i\geq 1}{\eta_{i}-\eta_{i+1}\brack\eta_{i}-\rho_{i}}_{q},

where
\eta/\rho
is a horizontal strip and
\mathcal{F}
represents
\mathcal{F}(\mu,\lambda)
as in

1.11
. By convention,
a_{\mu\lambda}(q)=0
when
\mathcal{F}=\emptyset
.

Let
\mathrm{P}_{\mu}({\bf x};q,t)
denote the Macdonald symmetric function in
{\bf x}=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots)
indexed by
\mu
.
Also, let the
\mathrm{Q}_{\mu}({\bf x};q,t)
denote the duals of the
\mathrm{P}_{\mu}({\bf x};q,t)
with respect to the
q,t
-scalar product
[
25
, p. 323, Eq. (4.12)]
.
From
[
25
, p. 346, Eq. (7.13’)]
, one obtains

(12)

\mathrm{P}_{\mu}({\bf x};q,0)=\sum_{\nu}a_{\mu\nu}(q)m_{\nu}({\bf x}),

where
m_{\nu}({\bf x})
is the monomial symmetric function associated to

. Then by a result of Ram and Schlosser
[

, Theorem 5.3]
, we have

(13)

b_{\mu\lambda}(q)=\left(\prod_{i\geq 1}\frac{[\lambda_{i}]_{q}!}{[\mu_{i}-\mu_{i+1}]_{q}!}\right)\,a_{\mu\lambda}(q).

We denote the coefficient of
{\bf x}^{\lambda}=x_{1}^{\lambda_{1}}x_{2}^{\lambda_{2}}\cdots
in
\Phi({\bf x})
by
[{\bf x}^{\lambda}]\Phi({\bf x})
.
Then using
Eq.

, we can state the formula for

in

1.12
as follows.

Theorem 1.15
.

Let
\mu
and
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{s})
be partitions of

\displaystyle\mathrm{F}_{\mu\lambda}(q)

\displaystyle=(q-1)^{n}q^{\binom{n}{2}-\mathsf{n}(\mu)-\mathsf{n}(\lambda^{\prime})-n}[\lambda]_{q}!\,[{\bf x}^{\lambda}]\mathrm{Q}_{\mu^{\prime}}({\bf x};1/q,0),

Example 1.16
.

Let
\lambda=(2^{k})
and
\mu=(k,k)
be two partitions of
2k
. From
Theorem

1.15
we have:

(14)

\mathrm{F}_{\mu\lambda}(q)=(q-1)^{2k-2}q^{\binom{2k-2}{2}}(q+1)^{k-1}.

To see this, we first observe that
\mu^{\prime}=\lambda
. Indeed, by
[
25
, p. 325, Eq. (4.17)]

we have
\mathrm{P}_{\mu^{\prime}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{k};q,t)=(x_{1}\cdots x_{k})^{2}
.
Thus from
Theorem

1.15
we obtain:

\begin{split}\mathrm{F}_{\mu\lambda}(q)&=(q-1)^{2k-\ell(\mu)}q^{\sum_{i\geq 1}\binom{\mu^{\prime}_{i}-\mu^{\prime}_{i+1}}{2}+\binom{2k}{2}+\ell(\mu)-\mathsf{n}(\mu)-\mathsf{n}(\lambda^{\prime})-n}\left(\prod_{i\geq 1}\frac{[\lambda_{i}]_{q}!}{[\mu_{i}^{\prime}-\mu_{i+1}^{\prime}]_{q}!}\right)\\
&=(q-1)^{2k-2}q^{\binom{2k-2}{2}}\frac{([2]_{q}!)^{k}}{[2]_{q}!}=(q-1)^{2k-2}q^{\binom{2k-2}{2}}(q+1)^{k-1}.\end{split}

We remark that
Theorem

1.15
is a special case of
Theorem

1.5
in a direct way.
Recall that
{\mathrm{G}}=\mathrm{GL}_{n}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
and
\mathsf{U}_{\lambda}=\{I+X:X\in\mathfrak{u}_{\lambda}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})\}
.
Now
|\mathsf{U}_{\lambda}|=q^{\binom{n}{2}-\mathsf{n}(\lambda^{\prime})}
. Furthermore,
if
\langle\,,\,\rangle

denotes the Hall scalar product
[
25
, Sec. I.4]
, then

\left\langle h_{\mu}({\bf x}),m_{\lambda}({\bf x})\right\rangle=\delta_{\mu\lambda}
, where the
h_{\mu}
are the complete symmetric functions.
It follows that

\displaystyle[{\bf x}^{\lambda}]\,q^{\mathsf{n}(\mu)}

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}_{\mu^{\prime}}({\bf x};1/q,0)=\left\langle h_{\lambda}({\bf x}),q^{\mathsf{n}(\mu)}\mathrm{P}_{\mu^{\prime}}({\bf x};1/q,0)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle h_{\lambda}({\bf x}),\omega\tilde{\mathrm{H}}_{\mu}({\bf x};q)\right\rangle=\left\langle\omega h_{\lambda}({\bf x}),\tilde{\mathrm{H}}_{\mu}({\bf x};q)\right\rangle=\left\langle e_{\lambda}({\bf x}),\tilde{\mathrm{H}}_{\mu}({\bf x};q)\right\rangle,

where the
e_{\lambda}({\bf x})
are the elementary symmetric functions.
Thus,
Theorem

1.15
is equivalent to the following assertion.

Theorem 1.17
.

Let
\mu
and
\lambda
be partitions of
n
with
\lambda\unlhd\mu^{\prime}
. Then

(15)

\displaystyle\mathrm{F}_{\mu\lambda}(q)

\displaystyle=\frac{(q-1)^{n}|\mathsf{U}_{\lambda}|}{|\mathrm{Z}_{G}(I+J_{\mu})|}[\lambda]_{q}!\,[{\bf x}^{\lambda}]q^{\mathsf{n}(\mu)}\mathrm{P}_{\mu^{\prime}}({\bf x};1/q,0)=\frac{(q-1)^{n}|\mathsf{U}_{\lambda}|}{|\mathrm{Z}_{G}(I+J_{\mu})|}\left\langle[\lambda]_{q}!e_{\lambda}({\bf x}),\tilde{\mathrm{H}}_{\mu}({\bf x};q)\right\rangle.

Now the key observation is that
[\lambda]_{q}!e_{\lambda}({\bf x})
is the chromatic quasisymmetric function of a disjoint union of complete graphs.

Theorem

1.5
establishes that this interpretation of the formula for
\mathrm{F}_{\mu\lambda}(q)
extends verbatim to
\mathrm{F}_{\mu\mathsf{h}}(q)
!

We conclude this section by noting another perspective on
\mathrm{F}_{\mu\mathsf{h}}(q)
that comes from representation theory. Let
{\mathrm{G}}
be a finite group. For
u\in{\mathrm{G}}
the conjugacy class of
u
and the centralizer of
u
are denoted by
\mathrm{Cl}_{\mathrm{G}}(u)
and
\mathrm{Z}_{\mathrm{G}}(u)
, respectively.
Then for any subgroup
\mathrm{H}
of
{\mathrm{G}}
we have

(16)

\#\{g\in{\mathrm{G}}:g^{-1}ug\in\mathrm{H}\}=|\mathrm{Cl}_{\mathrm{G}}(u)\cap\mathrm{H}|\cdot|\mathrm{Z}_{\mathrm{G}}(u)|.

Now suppose
\mathrm{ch}(\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathrm{H}}^{{\mathrm{G}}}1)(u)
denotes the value of the character of the induced representation
\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathrm{H}}^{{\mathrm{G}}}1
at
u\in{\mathrm{G}}
. Then

(17)

\displaystyle\mathrm{ch}(\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathrm{H}}^{{\mathrm{G}}}1)(u)

\displaystyle=\#\left\{g\mathrm{H}:g^{-1}ug\in\mathrm{H}\right\}=\frac{\#\left\{g\in{\mathrm{G}}:g^{-1}ug\in\mathrm{H}\right\}}{|\mathrm{H}|}=\frac{|\mathrm{Z}_{\mathrm{G}}(u)|}{|\mathrm{H}|}|\mathrm{Cl}_{\mathrm{G}}(u)\cap\mathrm{H}|.

Let

(18)

\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{h}}=\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{h}}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=}\{I+A:A\in\mathfrak{u}_{\mathsf{h}}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})\}

denote the unipotent subgroup of
{\mathrm{G}}=\mathrm{GL}_{n}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
associated with
\mathsf{h}
. Let
\chi_{\mathsf{h}}
be the character of the induced representation
\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{h}}}^{\mathrm{G}}\mathbf{1}
. Then from
Eqs.

16
and
17
we have:

(19)

\chi_{\mathsf{h}}(I+J_{\mu})=\frac{|\mathrm{Z}_{\mathrm{G}}(I+J_{\mu})|}{|\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{h}}|}\mathrm{F}_{\mu\mathsf{h}}(q).

Therefore, understanding
\mathrm{F}_{\mu\mathsf{h}}(q)
reduces to analyzing the induced representation of the trivial representation from
\mathsf{U}_{\mathsf{h}}
to
{\mathrm{G}}
. This approach was explored by Gagnon, among other things, in his recent and inspiring paper
[
13
]
. To describe the connection with Gagnon’s work, we need to recall the work of Zelevinsky
[
33
]
which introduces a family of Hopf algebras, known as PSH-algebras, that unify the representation theories of
\mathrm{S}_{n}
and
\mathrm{GL}_{n}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
. In Gagnon’s notation, Zelevinsky’s construction produces a graded Hopf algebra isomorphism

\mathbf{p}_{\{1\}}\colon\mathsf{cf}^{\mathrm{uni}}_{\mathrm{supp}}(\mathrm{GL}_{\bullet})\to\mathrm{Sym},\quad\delta_{\lambda}\mapsto\tilde{\mathrm{P}}_{\lambda}({\bf x};q):=q^{-\mathsf{n}(\lambda)}\mathrm{P}_{\lambda}({\bf x};q^{-1}),

where
\mathrm{Sym}
is the ring of symmetric functions with coefficients in
\mathbb{Q}(q)
and

\mathrm{P}_{\lambda}({\bf x};t)\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=}\mathrm{P}_{\lambda}({\bf x};0,t)

denotes a Hall-Littlewood function. Using this, Gagnon proves
[
13
, Theorem 3.1]
that

\chi_{\mathsf{h}}=(q-1)^{n}\,\mathbf{p}_{\{1\}}^{-1}(X_{\mathsf{G}(\mathsf{h})}({\bf x};q)).

Gagnon then applies this identity to derive a formula for
\chi_{\mathsf{h}}
. As

\tilde{\mathrm{P}}_{\lambda}({\bf x};q)
is dual to the modified Hall-Littlewood basis
\tilde{\mathrm{H}}_{\lambda}({\bf x};q)
with respect to the Hall scalar product, this formula is in principle equivalent to
Theorem

1.5
above. Our proof of
Theorem

1.5
is entirely combinatorial and thus in some sense more elementary.

Let

Let
\chi_{3}

Theorem 2.7
.

(Ekhad–Zeilberger) For

we have

\begin{split}\mathrm{C}_{2n+1}&=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{2n+1}{n-j}\left[\chi_{3}(j+1)q^{\frac{1}{3}\binom{2j+1}{2}}-\chi_{3}(j)q^{\frac{1}{3}\binom{2j+2}{2}}\right]q^{n^{2}+n-j^{2}-j}\\

and

\begin{split}\mathrm{C}_{2n}&=\binom{2n}{n}q^{n^{2}}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\binom{2n}{n-j}\left[\chi_{3}(2j+1)q^{\frac{1}{3}\binom{2j}{2}}-\chi_{3}(2j-1)q^{\frac{1}{3}\binom{2j+1}{2}}\right]q^{n^{2}-j^{2}}\\
&=\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\left[\binom{2n}{n-3j}-\binom{2n}{n-3j-1}\right]q^{n^{2}-3j^{2}-2j}.\end{split}

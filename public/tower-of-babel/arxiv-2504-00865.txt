The Zilber-Pink conjecture for varieties not defined over ℚ ¯ \overline{\mathbb{Q}}
Bruno Klingler, Salim Tayou

Abstract
. In this note, we prove the Zilber–Pink conjecture for subvarieties of mixed Shimura varieties, which are not defined over ℚ ¯ \overline{\mathbb{Q}} in a strong sense. We prove similar results for general variations of mixed Hodge structure of geometric origin, assuming furthermore that they are absolute.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
The Zilber-Pink conjecture for variations of mixed Hodge structures

Let
X
be a smooth quasi-projective algebraic variety over
\mathbb{C}
and let
{\mathbb{V}}=\{{\mathbb{V}}_{\mathbb{Z}},W_{\bullet},F^{\bullet}\}
be a variation of mixed Hodge structures
1
1

1

In this paper, all
\mathbb{Z}
VMHS are assumed to be admissible graded-polarizable
(
\mathbb{Z}
VMHS) over
X
. The Hodge locus
\mathrm{HL}(X,{\mathbb{V}}^{\otimes})
of the variation
{\mathbb{V}}
is the subset of points
x\in X(\mathbb{C})
where the Hodge structure
{\mathbb{V}}_{x}
admits ”more Hodge tensors” than the very general fiber of
{\mathbb{V}}
.
When
{\mathbb{V}}
is of the form
{\mathbb{V}}=R^{i}g_{*}\mathbb{Z}
for some flat surjective equisingular morphism
g:Z\rightarrow X
, the Hodge conjecture predicts that the locus
\mathrm{HL}(X,{\mathbb{V}}^{\otimes})
is a countable union of irreducible algebraic subvarieties of
X
:
the special subvarieties of
X
for
{\mathbb{V}}
. A famous result of Cattani-Deligne-Kaplan
[
CDK95
]
when
{\mathbb{V}}
is pure (see also
[
BKT20
]
), generalized to the mixed case in
[
BP09
]
,
[
BPS10
]
,
[
BBKT24
]
, states that this holds true unconditionally for a general
\mathbb{Z}
VMHS
{\mathbb{V}}
.

Let
({\mathbf{G}},{\mathcal{D}})
be the generic mixed Hodge datum of
{\mathbb{V}}
(thus
{\mathbf{G}}
is the generic Mumford-Tate group of
{\mathbb{V}}
and
{\mathcal{D}}
the associated mixed Mumford-Tate domain, see
[
Kli17
]
for instance). The
\mathbb{Z}
VMHS
{\mathbb{V}}
is completely described by the complex analytic period map

\varphi:X^{\textnormal{an}}\rightarrow\Gamma\backslash{\mathcal{D}}~,

where
\Gamma\subset{\mathbf{G}}(\mathbb{Q})
is an arithmetic subgroup and
X^{\textnormal{an}}
is the complex manifold analytification of
X
. In general, the complex locally homogeneous space
\Gamma\backslash{\mathcal{D}}
does not admit any algebraic structure. The special subvarieties
Z\subset X
for
{\mathbb{V}}
are precisely the analytic irreducible components of
\varphi^{-1}(\Gamma_{Z}\backslash{\mathcal{D}}_{Z})
, where
({\mathbf{G}}_{Z},{\mathcal{D}}_{Z})\subsetneq({\mathbf{G}},{\mathcal{D}})
is the generic Hodge datum of
{\mathbb{V}}_{|Z}
, see
[
Kli17
]
,
[
KO21
]
. The content of the Cattani-Deligne-Kaplan theorem is that, although
\Gamma\backslash{\mathcal{D}}
and the period map
\varphi
are not algebraic objects in general, these analytic irreducible components of
\varphi^{-1}(\Gamma_{Z}\backslash{\mathcal{D}}_{Z})
are algebraic subvarieties of
S
. We will also write
\mathrm{HL}(X,\varphi)
for the Hodge locus
\mathrm{HL}(X,{\mathbb{V}}^{\otimes})
.

Remark 1.1
.

When
\Gamma\backslash{\mathcal{D}}
admits an algebraic structure, the
\mathbb{Z}
VMHS
{\mathbb{V}}
is said of mixed Shimura type. In that case
\Gamma\backslash{\mathcal{D}}
is the analytification of a (connected) mixed Shimura variety
{\mathcal{S}}
and the period map
\varphi:X\rightarrow{\mathcal{S}}
is algebraic.

The distribution of the special subvarieties of
X
for
{\mathbb{V}}
has been the focus of much work in the last ten years. Using the period map associated to
{\mathbb{V}}
, Klingler
[
Kli17
, Def.4.3]
, generalized by Baldi-Klingler-Ullmo
[
BKU24
, Def.5.1 and 5.2]
, introduced a fundamental dichotomy: a special subvariety is said
typical
if its image under the period map has the dimension predicted by intersection theory:

\mathrm{codim}_{\Phi(X^{\textnormal{an}})}\,\Phi(Z^{\textnormal{an}})=\mathrm{codim}_{\Gamma\backslash{\mathcal{D}}}\,\Gamma_{Z}\backslash{\mathcal{D}}_{Z},

and is said
atypical
otherwise. This gives rise to a decomposition:

\mathrm{HL}(X,{\mathbb{V}}^{\otimes})=\mathrm{HL}(X,{\mathbb{V}}^{\otimes})_{\mathrm{typ}}\cup\mathrm{HL}(X,{\mathbb{V}}^{\otimes})_{\mathrm{atyp}},

where the typical Hodge locus
\mathrm{HL}(X,{\mathbb{V}}^{\otimes})_{\mathrm{typ}}
is the countable union of the typical special subvarieties of
X
for
{\mathbb{V}}
and the atypical Hodge locus
\mathrm{HL}(X,{\mathbb{V}}^{\otimes})_{\mathrm{atyp}}
is the countable union of the atypical ones. In
[
Kli17
, 1.5]
, generalized by
[
BKU24
, Conjecture 2.5]
, the first author proposed the following conjecture, which extends to any
\mathbb{Z}
VMHS the classical Zilber-Pink conjecture for mixed Shimura varieties.

Conjecture 1.2

(Zilber–Pink for
\mathbb{Z}
VMHS)
.

For any smooth irreducible quasi-projective algebraic variety
X
over
\mathbb{C}
endowed with an admissible graded-polarizable variation of mixed Hodge structures
{\mathbb{V}}
, the atypical Hodge locus
\mathrm{HL}(X,{\mathbb{V}}^{\otimes})_{\mathrm{atyp}}
is not Zariski-dense in
X
.
Equivalently:
\mathrm{HL}(X,{\mathbb{V}}^{\otimes})_{\mathrm{atyp}}
is a union of finitely many maximal atypical special subvarieties of
X
for
{\mathbb{V}}
.

Remark 1.3
.

The definition of atypical subvarieties in
[
BKU24
]
is more general than the one in
[
Kli17
]
, see
[
BKU24
, Section 5.3]
for the comparison. The equivalence mentioned in
Conjecture

1.2
is proven in
[
Kli17
, Prop. 5.1]
using the restricted definition of atypicality. The proof extends unchanged to the more general definition.

When
{\mathbb{V}}
is of mixed Shimura type, the algebraic period map

\varphi:X\rightarrow{\mathcal{S}}

decomposes as
\varphi=\iota\circ f
, where
Y\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\iota}}{{\hookrightarrow}}{\mathcal{S}}
denotes the Zariski closure of the constructible set
\varphi(X)
in the mixed Shimura variety
{\mathcal{S}}
and
f:X\rightarrow Y
the natural dominant projection. Although

is usually singular, we still call
\iota:Y\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{S}}
a period map and define
\mathrm{HL}(Y,\iota)
and
\mathrm{HL}(Y,\iota)_{\mathrm{atyp}}
as before. In particular
\mathrm{HL}(X,\varphi)_{\mathrm{atyp}}=f^{-1}(\mathrm{HL}(Y,\iota)_{\mathrm{atyp}})
. Hence
Conjecture

1.2
for
\mathbb{Z}
VMHS of Shimura type reduces to the classical Zilber-Pink conjecture for mixed Shimura varieties
[
Zil02
]
,
[

]
.

Conjecture 1.4
.

(Zilber-Pink)
Let
X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\iota}}{{\hookrightarrow}}{\mathcal{S}}
be a closed irreducible algebraic subvariety of a mixed Shimura variety
{\mathcal{S}}
. Then
\mathrm{HL}(X,\iota)_{\mathrm{atyp}}
is not Zariski-dense in
X
.

For a general period map
\varphi:X^{\textnormal{an}}\rightarrow\Gamma\backslash{\mathcal{D}}
with a non-algebraic target, the picture above remains partially true. According to
[
BBT23a
]
,
[
BBT23b
]
, the image of the period map
\varphi
factorizes (uniquely up to unique isomorphism) as:

\varphi:X^{\textnormal{an}}\rightarrow W^{\textnormal{an}}\hookrightarrow\Gamma\backslash D~,

where
W
(the
image
of the period map) is a (usually singular) irreducible quasi-projective variety, the algebraic morphism
X\rightarrow W
is dominant and
W^{\textnormal{an}}\hookrightarrow\Gamma\backslash{\mathcal{D}}
is a closed analytic immersion. To take care of fields of definition it will be convenient for us to consider the normalization
Y
of

W
itself.

Definition 1.5
.

We call

\varphi:X^{\textnormal{an}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f^{\textnormal{an}}}}{{\rightarrow}}Y^{\textnormal{an}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\iota}}{{\rightarrow}}\Gamma\backslash D

the factorization of the period map
\varphi
, and
Y
the
normalized image
of
\varphi
.

Again,
\mathrm{HL}(X,\varphi)_{\mathrm{atyp}}=f^{-1}(\mathrm{HL}(Y,\iota)_{\mathrm{atyp}})
, and the study of

1.2
for
X
and

Conjecture

1.2
for
Y
and
\iota
.

1.2.
Results

[
BKU24
]
in the pure case, generalized by
[

]
in the mixed case, proved the

, see

Our first result deals with the mixed Shimura case. Any mixed Shimura variety
{\mathcal{S}}
admits a canonical model
{\mathcal{S}}_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}
over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
. Any morphism of mixed Shimura variety coming from a morphism of mixed Shimura data is defined over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
. In particular, any quotient
{\mathcal{S}}\twoheadrightarrow{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}
coming from a quotient mixed Shimura datum
({\mathbf{G}},{\mathcal{D}})\twoheadrightarrow({\mathbf{G}}^{\prime},{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime})
is defined over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
; and any special subvariety of
{\mathcal{S}}
is defined over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\iota}}{{\hookrightarrow}}{\mathcal{S}}
be a Hodge-generic closed irreducible algebraic subvariety of a mixed Shimura variety
{\mathcal{S}}
. If no non-trivial image
X^{\prime}
of
X
in a quotient Shimura variety
{\mathcal{S}}\twoheadrightarrow{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}
is defined over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
(as a subvariety of
{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}
), then
Conjecture

1.4
holds true for
X
.

Let us illustrate this result in the pure case. Let
{\mathcal{S}}
be a pure Shimura variety, with Shimura datum
({\mathbf{G}},{\mathcal{D}})
. The decomposition of the adjoint group
{\mathbf{G}}^{\mathrm{ad}}
into a product of simple factors
{\mathbf{G}}_{1}\times\ldots\times{\mathbf{G}}_{k}
induces a decomposition of (a finite quotient of)
{\mathcal{S}}
into
{\mathcal{S}}_{1}\times\ldots\times{\mathcal{S}}_{k}
.

Corollary 1.7
.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}
be a pure Shimura variety.
Let
X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varphi}}{{\hookrightarrow}}{\mathcal{S}}
be a Hodge generic closed irreducible complex subvariety. If its non-constant projections
X_{i}\subset{\mathcal{S}}_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq k
, are not defined over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
(as subvarieties of the
{\mathcal{S}}_{i}
), then
Conjecture

1.4
holds true for
X
. In particular if
{\mathcal{S}}
is indecomposable (i.e.
{\mathbf{G}}^{\mathrm{ad}}
is simple) and

is not defined over

, then

1.2
holds true for
X
.

Remark 1.8
.

The special case of

1.7
where

the moduli space of principally polarized abelian varieties of dimension

was recently also proven by Barroero-Dill
[

2.
Geometric finiteness

In this section, we recall the finiteness theorem for atypical Hodge loci
of positive period dimension
recently established in the pure case
[
BKU24
]

and in the mixed case
[
BU24
]
.

Let
X
,
{\mathbb{V}}
,
({\mathbf{G}},{\mathcal{D}})
be as in
Section

1
, with period map

\varphi:X^{\textnormal{an}}\rightarrow\Gamma\backslash{\mathcal{D}}
.
The notion of special subvariety can be generalized, using the algebraic monodromy group instead of the generic Mumford-Tate group. Let
({\mathbf{H}},{\mathcal{D}}_{{\mathbf{H}}})
be the monodromy datum of the variation
{\mathbb{V}}
. Thus
{\mathbf{H}}
is the algebraic monodromy group of the local system underlying
{\mathbb{V}}
, and
{\mathcal{D}}_{{\mathbf{H}}}\subset{\mathcal{D}}
is the corresponding monodromy domain. According to a well-known result of Deligne and André
[
And92
, Theorem 1]
, the semi-simple group
{\mathbf{H}}
is normal in the derived group
{\mathbf{G}}^{\rm{der}}
of
{\mathbf{G}}
. Up to a finite cover of
X
, the period map
\varphi
factors through the monodromy special subvariety
{\mathit{\Gamma}}_{{\mathbf{H}}}\backslash{\mathcal{D}}_{{\mathbf{H}}}
of
{\mathit{\Gamma}}\backslash{\mathcal{D}}_{{\mathbf{G}}}
:

\varphi:X^{\textnormal{an}}\rightarrow{\mathit{\Gamma}}_{{\mathbf{H}}}\backslash{\mathcal{D}}_{{\mathbf{H}}}\subset{\mathit{\Gamma}}\backslash{\mathcal{D}}_{{\mathbf{G}}}.

We refer to
[
Kli17
]
,
[
KO21
]
,
[
BBKT24
]
[
GK24
]
for more details.

The following result is proven in
[
KO21
]
in the pure case, the proof generalizes easily to the mixed case:

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
Z\subset X
be an irreducible algebraic subvariety. Let
({\mathbf{H}}_{Z},{\mathcal{D}}_{{\mathbf{H}}_{Z}})
be the monodromy datum of
{\mathbb{V}}_{|Z}
. The following conditions are equivalent.

(1)

Z
is maximal among the irreducible subvarieties of
X
with monodromy datum
({\mathbf{H}}_{Z},{\mathcal{D}}_{{\mathbf{H}}_{Z}})
.

(2)

Z^{\textnormal{an}}
is an irreducible component of the preimage under
\varphi
of a monodromy special variety
{\mathit{\Gamma}}_{{\mathbf{H}}_{Z}}\backslash{\mathcal{D}}_{{\mathbf{H}}_{Z}}
.

If
Z
satisfies one of the conditions above, then we say that
Z
is weakly (or monodromy) special.

One easily checks that any special subvariety of
X
is monodromy special, see
[
Kli17
, 7.1]
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
Z\subset X
be a monodromy special subvariety for
{\mathbb{V}}
, with monodromy data
({\mathbf{H}}_{Z},{\mathcal{D}}_{{\mathbf{H}}_{Z}})
.
We say that
Z
is monodromy atypical if:

\mathrm{codim}_{\varphi(X^{\textnormal{an}})}(\varphi(Z^{\textnormal{an}}))<\mathrm{codim}_{{\mathcal{D}}_{{\mathbf{H}}}}({\mathcal{D}}_{{\mathbf{H}}_{Z}})~.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
Z\subset X
be an atypical (resp. a maximal atypical) special subvariety for
{\mathbb{V}}
. Then
Z
is a monodromy atypical (resp. a maximal monodromy atypical) subvariety of
S
for
{\mathbb{V}}
.

Proof.

When
{\mathbb{V}}
is pure this is
[
BKU24
, Lemma 5.6]
, and
[
BKU24
, Rem. 4.1]
for the maximality. The proof there extends verbatim to the mixed case.

The following theorem solves the geometric part of Zilber–Pink conjecture. It was established in the pure case by Baldi-Klingler-Ullmo
[
BKU24
, Theorem 6.1]
and in the mixed case by Baldi-Urbanik
[
BU24
, Theorem 7.1]
. Recall that by a
family of subvarieties of
X
we mean a pair
(f:Z\rightarrow Y,\pi:Z\rightarrow X)
where
f
is a flat surjective morphism of irreducible varieties, and
\pi:Z\rightarrow X
is an algebraic morphism whose restriction to each fiber of
f
is a closed embedding.

Theorem 2.4
.

There are only finitely many families
(f_{i}:Z_{i}\rightarrow Y_{i},\,\pi_{i}:Z_{i}\rightarrow X)
,
i\in\Sigma
, of maximal monodromy atypical subvarieties of
X
for
{\mathbb{V}}
.

More precisely: there exists a finite set
\Sigma
of triples
({\mathbf{G}}_{i},{\mathcal{D}}_{i},{\mathbf{N}}_{i})_{i\in\Sigma}
, where
({\mathbf{G}}_{i},{\mathcal{D}}_{i})
is a sub-Hodge datum of the generic Hodge datum
({\mathbf{G}},{\mathcal{D}}_{{\mathbf{G}}})
,
{\mathbf{N}}_{i}
is a normal subgroup of
{\mathbf{G}}_{i}
whose reductive part is semisimple, and the following property holds: for each maximal monodromy atypical subvariety
Y\subset X
, there exist
i\in\Sigma
and
y\in{\mathcal{D}}_{i}
for which
{\mathcal{D}}_{{\mathbf{H}}_{Y}}={\mathbf{N}}_{i}(\mathbb{R})^{+}{\mathbf{N}}_{i}(\mathbb{C})^{u}\cdot y

(
up to the action of
{\mathit{\Gamma}}
)
and
Y
is isomorphic to a fiber of
f_{i}
.

3.
Reduction to the atypical Hodge locus of zero period dimension

Let
X
be a smooth quasi-projective algebraic variety defined over
\mathbb{C}
and
{\mathbb{V}}
a
\mathbb{Z}
VMHS on
X
. Let
({\mathbf{G}},{\mathcal{D}})
be the generic Hodge datum of
{\mathbb{V}}
and
\varphi:X^{\textnormal{an}}\rightarrow\Gamma\backslash{\mathcal{D}}
the associated period map.

Definition 3.1
.

(see
[
KO21
]
)
Let
Z
be a special subvariety of
X
for
{\mathbb{V}}
. We say that
Z
has zero period dimension if
\varphi(Z^{\textnormal{an}})
is a point; otherwise we say that
Z
has positive period dimension.

We denote by
\mathrm{HL}(X,\varphi)_{\mathrm{atyp},\mathrm{pos}}
the countable union of atypical special subvarieties of positive period dimension, and by
\mathrm{HL}(X,\varphi)_{\mathrm{atyp},\,\mathrm{zero}}
the union of atypical special subvarieties of zero period dimension, thus obtaining a decomposition:

\mathrm{HL}(X,\varphi)_{\mathrm{atyp}}=\mathrm{HL}(X,\varphi)_{\mathrm{atyp},\mathrm{pos}}\cup\mathrm{HL}(X,\varphi)_{\mathrm{atyp},\,\mathrm{zero}}~.

Definition 3.2
.

We define

\mathrm{HL}(X,\varphi)_{\mathrm{max.atyp},\,\mathrm{zero}}:=\mathrm{HL}(X,\varphi)_{\mathrm{atyp},\,\mathrm{zero}}\setminus\mathrm{HL}(X,\varphi)_{\mathrm{atyp},\mathrm{pos}}

the union of maximal atypical special subvarieties of zero period dimension (i.e. those not contained in an atypical special subvariety of positive period dimension).

Proposition 3.3
.

Conjecture

1.2
holds true for
X
and
{\mathbb{V}}
if and only if for any quotient Hodge datum
({\mathbf{G}},{\mathcal{D}})\twoheadrightarrow({\mathbf{G}}^{\prime},{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime})
,
\mathrm{HL}(X,\varphi^{\prime})_{\mathrm{max.atyp},\,\mathrm{zero}}
is not Zariski-dense in
X
.

Proof.

As
\mathrm{HL}(X,\varphi^{\prime})_{\mathrm{max.atyp},\,\mathrm{zero}}\subset\mathrm{HL}(X,\varphi)_{\mathrm{atyp}}
,
Conjecture

1.2
certainly implies the right hand side condition. Conversely, let us suppose that for any quotient Hodge datum
({\mathbf{G}},{\mathcal{D}})\twoheadrightarrow({\mathbf{G}}^{\prime},{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime})
,
\mathrm{HL}(X,\varphi^{\prime})_{\mathrm{max.atyp},\,\mathrm{zero}}
is not Zariski-dense in
X
.

Let
Z\subset X
be a maximal atypical special subvariety of
X
for
{\mathbb{V}}
. By
Lemma

2.3
,
Z\subset X
is a maximal monodromy atypical weakly special subvariety of
X
for
{\mathbb{V}}
. Thus
Z
is a fiber of one of the finitely many families
f_{i}:Z_{i}\rightarrow Y_{i}
,
i\in\Sigma
, appearing in
Theorem

2.4
. For simplicity we will call such a fiber an atypical special fiber of
f_{i}
. We are reduced to showing that the union of the atypical special fibers of
f_{i}
,
i\in\Sigma
, is not Zariski-dense in
X
.

Let us say that a family
(f,\pi)
of subvarieties of
X
is dominant if
\pi
is.
If
f_{i}:Z_{i}\rightarrow Y_{i}
is not dominant, then a fortiori the union of all atypical special fibers of
f_{i}
, which is contained in the image of
\pi_{i}:Z_{i}\rightarrow X
, is not Zariski-dense in
X
. Let
\Sigma^{\mathrm{dom}}\subset\Sigma
denote the finite set of dominant families. We are thus reduced to showing that the union of the atypical special fibers of
f_{i}:Z_{i}\rightarrow Y_{i}
,
i\in\Sigma^{\mathrm{dom}}
, is not Zariski-dense in
X
.

Let
i\in\Sigma^{\mathrm{dom}}
. Then necessarily
({\mathbf{G}}_{i},{\mathcal{D}}_{i})=({\mathbf{G}},{\mathcal{D}})
(otherwise the image of
\pi_{i}
would be contained in the Hodge locus of
X
for
{\mathbb{V}}
with Hodge subdatum
({\mathbf{G}}_{i},{\mathcal{D}}_{i})
, a contradiction to the fact that
\pi_{i}
is dominant). Let
({\mathbf{G}}^{\prime}_{i},{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}_{i})
be the quotient Hodge datum of
({\mathbf{G}},{\mathcal{D}})
by
{\mathbf{N}}_{i}
and let

\varphi^{\prime}_{i}:X^{\textnormal{an}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varphi}}{{\rightarrow}}\Gamma\backslash{\mathcal{D}}\twoheadrightarrow{\Gamma^{\prime}_{i}}\backslash{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}_{i}

be the associated quotient period map. As
{\mathbf{N}}_{i}
is the algebraic monodromy group of the fibers of
f_{i}
, the atypical special fibers of
f_{i}
are in bijection with the maximal atypical special subvarieties of zero period dimension of
X
for
\varphi^{\prime}_{i}
. Hence the union of all atypical special fibers of the morphism
f_{i}
coincides with
\mathrm{HL}(X,\varphi^{\prime}_{i})_{\mathrm{max.atyp},\,\mathrm{zero}}
. It follows from our hypothesis that this is not Zariski-dense in
X
. Thus
Conjecture

1.2
holds true.

4.
Proof of
Theorem

1.6

In view of

Theorem 4.1

Let

Choose

has transcendence degree

over

Let

is defined over

(1)

(2)

the base

is irreducible, smooth over

(3)

In particular the normalized image

For each

for
\Phi

5.
Proof of
Theorem

1.11

5.1.
Preliminaries

As for the proof of
Theorem

1.6
, spreading is the main tool in the proof of
Theorem

1.11
. This requires the
\mathbb{Z}
VMHSs we consider to be geometric. In this section, we discuss some of the consequences of
Definition

1.9
that we will need.
First, we need to extend a bit
Definition

1.9
.

Definition 5.1
.

Given a non-necessarily geometric
\mathbb{Z}
VMHS
{\mathbb{V}}
on
X
, we say that it is defined over a field
K\subset\mathbb{C}
if (possibly after replacing
X
by a Zariski-dense open subset) the variety
X
, the filtered module with integrable connection
({\mathcal{V}},F^{\bullet},\nabla)
on
X
corresponding to
{\mathbb{V}}
under the Deligne-Riemann-Hilbert correspondence, its weight filtration
W_{\bullet}
and the graded-polarisations
Q_{i}:\textnormal{Gr}^{W}_{i}\times\textnormal{Gr}^{W}_{i}\rightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
are all defined over
K
.

We say that a period map
\varphi:X^{\textnormal{an}}\rightarrow\Gamma\backslash{\mathcal{D}}
is defined over a field
K\subset\mathbb{C}
if it is the period map of a
\mathbb{Z}
VMHS defined over
K
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
X
be a smooth irreducible quasi-projective algebraic variety over
\mathbb{C}
, and
{\mathbb{V}}
a
\mathbb{Z}
VMHS on
X
defined over
K
. Then the period map of any subquotient of
{\mathbb{V}}
is defined over
K
.

Proof.

Let
\varphi
be the period map of a sub-
\mathbb{Z}
VMHS

. In general
{\mathbb{W}}
is defined only over an extension
M
of
K
. Notice however that the isotypic component

\sum_{\tau\in\mathrm{Hom}({\mathbb{W}},{\mathbb{V}})}\tau({\mathbb{W}})\subset{\mathbb{V}}

of
{\mathbb{W}}
in
{\mathbb{V}}
is defined over
K
, as the corresponding filtered module with integrable connection, weight filtration and polarization are stable under
\textnormal{Aut}(M/K)
. Moreover, this isotypic component has the same period map
\varphi
as
{\mathbb{V}}
. It follows that
\varphi
is defined over
K
.

Arguing with duals, we deduce in the same way that the period map of a quotient of a
\mathbb{Z}
VMHS defined over
K
is also defined over
K
. The result follows.
∎

Corollary 5.3
.

Let
X
be a smooth irreducible quasi-projective algebraic variety over

, and
\varphi:X^{\textnormal{an}}\rightarrow\Gamma\backslash{\mathcal{D}}

is defined over

Proof.

By definition

is the period map of a subquotient

of some
\mathbb{Z}

It is classical that

. See
[
Urb22

The fact that

∎

Proposition 5.4

(1)

Lemma 5.5

Let

be a smooth irreducible quasi-projective variety over
\mathbb{C}
,
\varphi:X^{\textnormal{an}}\rightarrow\Gamma\backslash{\mathcal{D}}
a period map on

, and

is also geometric. If moreover

Proof.

Suppose that
\varphi
is geometric. After replacing

by a Zariski-dense open subset if necessary, there exists

a flat surjective equisingular map such that

is the period map of a subquotient

of

. Thus

.

Let

, and

Let

, and

, it is stable under the representation

If

is defined over an algebraically closed subfield

, it follows from the previous construction and

too.

∎

.

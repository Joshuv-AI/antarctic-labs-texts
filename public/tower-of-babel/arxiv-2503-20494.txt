Moderate deviations of many–server queues, idempotent processes and quasipotentials
Anatolii A. Puhalskii

Abstract
A Large Deviation Principle (LDP) is established for the stationary distribution of the number of customers in a many–server queue in heavy traffic for a moderate deviation scaling akin to the Halfin–Whitt regime. The interarrival and service times are assumed generally distributed. The deviation function is given by a quasipotential. It is related to the longterm idempotent distribution of the large deviation limit of the number-in-the-system process. New results on the trajectorial LDP for that process are also obtained. Proofs rely on the characterisation of large deviation relatively compact sequences as exponentially tight ones and use methods of weak convergence and idempotent processes. Another contribution of the paper concerns bounds on the higher–order moments of counting renewal processes.

.

, as

Appendix B
A nonlinear renewal equation

This section is concerned with the properties of the equation

g(t)=f(t)+\int_{0}^{t}g(t-s)^{+}\,dF(s)\,,t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\,.

(B.1)

It is assumed that

is a locally bounded measurable
function,
that

is a non–lattice distribution function on

(B.2)

The existence and uniqueness of an essentially locally bounded

to (

[

Lemma B.1
.

Suppose that

satisfies (
B.1
).

Proof.

Since

),

\liminf_{t\to\infty}g(t)\geq-\alpha\,.

It needs to be proved that

\limsup_{t\to\infty}g(t)\leq-\alpha\,.

(B.3)

By (
B.1
),

g(t)^{+}\leq f(t)^{+}+\int_{0}^{t}g(t-s)^{+}\,dF(s)\,.

(B.4)

Let

represent the unique locally bounded measurable
function that solves the renewal equation

(B.5)

(For the existence and uniqueness of

[
2
]

[
43

Hence,

u(t)\leq\sup_{s\leq t}u(s)\,F(t)\,.

(B.7)

Similarly, for
s\leq t
,

(B.8)

the latter inequality holding because

is nondecreasing. It follows that

implying that

\sup_{s\leq t}u(s)\leq 0\,,

provided

By (
B.6
) and (
B.2
),

u(t^{\ast})\leq 0
so that (

[

Since

[

By the key renewal theorem, see, e.g., Theorem 3.10.1 on p.237

–exponentially tight of order

.

.

Sequence

of real–valued processes
is
\mathbb{C}_{0}
–exponentially tight of order
r_{n}

if and only if it is

\mathbb{C}
–exponentially tight of order

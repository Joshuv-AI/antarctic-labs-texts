Conjugacy in fibre products, distortion, and the geometry of cyclic subgroups
Martin R. Bridson

Abstract
. We investigate the complexity of the conjugacy problem for fibre products in torsion-free hyperbolic groups. Let G G be a torsion-free hyperbolic group and let P < G × G P<G\times G be the fibre product associated to an epimorphism G → → Q G{\kern 3.0pt\to\kern-8.0pt\to\kern 3.0pt}Q . We establish inequalities that relate the conjugator length function of P P to the geometry of cyclic subgroups in Q Q , the Dehn function of Q Q , the rel-cyclics Dehn function of Q Q , and the distortion of P P in G × G G\times G . These estimates provide tools for extending the library of (large) functions that are known to arise as the conjugator length functions of finitely generated and finitely presented groups.

1.
Introduction

If a subdirect product of torsion-free hyperbolic groups

P<G_{1}\times\dots\times G_{m}

projects to a subgroup
of finite index in each pair of factors
G_{i}\times G_{j}
, then
P
is finitely
presented and its conjugacy problem can be solved using algorithms in an
associated nilpotent group
[
12
,
11
]
.
If
P
does not virtually surject each
G_{i}\times G_{j}
, then the situation is
much wilder. It is this wildness that we seek to quantify in this article,
concentrating on the case
m=2
.

In the case
m=2
, there
is a correspondence between subdirect products and
fibre products
: if
P<G_{1}\times G_{2}
is subdirect
then there is an isomorphism

G_{1}/(P\cap G_{1})\cong G_{2}/(P\cap G_{2})
and
P
is the
fibre product of the maps
p_{i}:G_{i}{\kern 3.0pt\to\kern-8.0pt\to\kern 3.0pt}G_{i}/(P\cap G_{i})
;
see
[
12
]
.
The quotient
Q:=G_{1}/(P\cap G_{1})
will be finitely presented if and only if
P
is finitely generated.
We will restrict our attention to the case where
G_{1}=G_{2}
and
p_{1}=p_{2}
.

It has been known for a long time that if the word problem in
Q
in unsolvable, then the conjugacy problem in
P

is unsolvable
[
18
,
19
,
2
]
, but the following characterisation of exactly when the conjugacy
problem in
P
is solvable was only proved recently
[
9
]
.

Theorem A

(
[
9
]
)
.

Let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}

be torsion-free hyperbolic groups, let
P<G_{1}\times G_{2}
be a finitely generated
subdirect product, and let
Q
be the finitely presented group
G_{1}/(G_{1}\cap P)
. Then,
the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

the conjugacy problem in
P
is solvable;

(2)

there is a uniform algorithm to decide membership of cyclic subgroups in
Q
;

(3)

the rel-cyclics Dehn function
\delta_{Q}^{c}(n)
is recursive.

The main purpose of this article is to provide quantitative companions to this theorem. These are intended as tools
that can be used to extend the library of functions that are
known to arise as conjugator length functions of finitely generated and finitely presented groups
[
14
]
,
a project that is being pursued with Tim Riley
[
13
]
.
By definition, the
conjugator length function
of a group
G
is

\hbox{\rm CL}_{G}(n):=\max_{u,v}\min_{\gamma}\{|\gamma|_{G}\colon u,v,\gamma\in G,\ \gamma^{-1}u\gamma=v,\ |u|_{G}+|v|_{G}\leq n\},

where
|\gamma|_{G}
denotes distance from the identity in the
word metric associated to a fixed finite generating set for
G
.
Given a pair of finitely generated groups
G<\Gamma
,
we also consider the function
\hbox{\rm CL}_{G}^{\Gamma}(n)
obtained by replacing
the condition
|u|_{G}+|v|_{G}\leq n
with
|u|_{\Gamma}+|v|_{\Gamma}\leq n
, still measuring
|\gamma|_{G}
in
G
.
We refer to
[
14
]
for an account of what is known about conjugator length functions.

An important theme running throughout this article is that the complexity of the
conjugacy problem in a fibre product
P
depends not only on the nature of the word problem in
the quotient
Q
,
but also on the geometry of cyclic subgroups in
Q
. This is why our
analysis involves not only the
Dehn function
\delta_{Q}(n)
of
Q
but also the
rel-cyclics Dehn function

\delta_{Q}^{c}(n)
.
Whereas

\delta_{Q}(n)
bounds the number of relators of
Q
that one
must apply to reduce null-homotopic words
w
with
|w|\leq n
to the empty word,

\delta_{Q}^{c}(n)
bounds the number of relators that one must apply to reduce null-homotopic words
of the form
wu^{-p}
to the empty word, where
|w|+|u|\leq n
and
|p|
is minimal;
see Definition
2.2
.

By design,

\delta_{Q}^{c}(n)
controls both the Dehn function
\delta_{Q}(n)
and the distortion of cyclic subgroups in
Q
.
We will be particularly interested in groups that have

uniformly quasigeodesic cyclics
, i.e. there is a constant
\lambda>0

such that
|q^{n}|_{Q}\geq\lambda n
for all
q\in Q\smallsetminus\{1\}
. Torsion-free
hyperbolic and CAT
(0)
groups have this property, as do their finitely
generated subgroups and many other groups (see Section
4.1
).
In the
hyperbolic case, infinite cyclic subgroups enjoy an additional monotonicity property
that will be crucial for us (Proposition
4.7
): there is a constant
k>0
such that
|h^{i}|_{H}\leq k|h^{p}|_{H}
for all
h\in H
and all
0<i<p
.

It is standard practice in geometric group theory to write
f\preceq g
for functions
f,g:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}

if there exists a
positive integer
C
such that

f(n)\leq Cg(Cl+C)+Cn+C

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. One writes
f\simeq g
if
f\preceq g
and
g\preceq f
.

Theorem B
.

Let
Q
be a finitely presented group, let
G{\kern 3.0pt\to\kern-8.0pt\to\kern 3.0pt}Q
be an epimorphism from a
torsion-free hyperbolic group, and let
P<G\times G
be the associated fibre
product. Then,

\delta_{Q}(n)\preceq\hbox{\rm CL}_{P}(n)\preceq\hbox{\rm CL}_{P}^{G\times G}(n)\preceq\delta_{Q}^{c}(n).

If
Q
is torsion-free, then

\delta_{Q}^{c}(n)\preceq n\,\hbox{\rm CL}_{P}^{G\times G}(n),

and if
Q
has uniformly quasigeodesic cyclics, then

\delta_{Q}^{c}(n)\simeq\hbox{\rm CL}_{P}^{G\times G}(n),

provided
\hbox{\rm CL}_{P}^{G\times G}(n)\succeq n^{2}
.

Corollary C
.

If
Q
has uniformly quasigeodesic cyclics, then

\delta_{Q}(n)\preceq\hbox{\rm CL}_{P}(n)\preceq\delta_{Q}(n^{2}).

Theorem
B
is proved in Section
8
by bringing together estimates established
in Sections
3
to
7
. Some of these estimates contain finer information.

Finitely presented groups are the main objects of interest in geometric and
combinatorial group theory and, correspondingly,
the preceding results are of most interest when

P
is finitely presented. According to the
1-2-3 Theorem from
[
2
]
, this will be the case
when the kernel of
G{\kern 3.0pt\to\kern-8.0pt\to\kern 3.0pt}Q
is finitely generated
and
Q
is of type
{\rm{F}}_{3}
(i.e. there
is a classifying space
K(Q,1)
with a finite 3-skeleton).
For the moment, it is unclear whether one can arrange for the Dehn function of
Q
to be
as unconstrained as one would like if one has to ensure both
that
Q
is of type
{\rm{F}}_{3}
and that its cyclic subgroups
are controlled in the manner needed for our results. To circumvent this
problem, we describe a construction that establishes the following.

Proposition D
.

There is an algorithm that, given a finite presentation for a
group
Q
, will produce a finite presentation for a group
Q^{\dagger}
, with

Q^{\dagger}{\kern 3.0pt\to\kern-8.0pt\to\kern 3.0pt}Q
, so
that
Q^{\dagger}
has uniformly quasigeodesic cyclics and

n\ \delta_{Q}(n)\preceq\delta_{Q^{\dagger}}(n)\leq n\ (\delta_{Q}(n))^{2}.

If
Q
is of type
{\rm{F}}_{3}
, then so is
Q^{\dagger}
.

Theorem
B
and Proposition
D

rely on the following calculation of
distortion
for fibre products, variations of which
appear elsewhere in the literature. By definition,

{\rm{Dist}}_{P}^{G\times G}(n):=\max\{|\gamma|_{P}\colon|\gamma|_{G\times G}\leq n\}
.

Theorem E
.

Let
Q
be a finitely presented group. If
G
is hyperbolic and
P<G\times G
is the fibre product of an epimorphism

G\twoheadrightarrow Q
, then

{\rm{Dist}}_{P}^{G\times G}(n)\simeq\delta_{Q}(n).

In Section
9
we combine the dagger construction of Proposition
D
with
Corollary
C
and a template for
designer groups described in
[
7
]
.
This enables us to provide the following tool for investigations into the range of behaviour
that conjugator length functions of finitely presented groups can exhibit.
A Dehn function
\delta(n)
is
standard

if
\delta(n)\simeq\delta_{Q}(n)
for some group
Q
of type
{\rm{F}}_{3}
. All known
Dehn functions are standard; see Remark
2.4
.

Theorem F
.

For every standard Dehn function
\delta(n)
, there exists a finitely presented
group
P
with

n\ \delta(n)\preceq\hbox{\rm CL}_{P}(n)\preceq n^{2}(\delta(n^{2}))^{2}.

The group
P
in Theorem
F
will be a
subgroup of
G\times G
for some torsion-free hyperbolic group
G
. It will
also be
virtually special in the
sense of Haglund and Wise
[
17
]
. In particular, it is residually finite
and can be embedded

{\rm{SL}}(d,\mathbb{Z})
and in the mapping class group of
a closed surface of some genus
[
8
]
, where
d
and the
genus depend on
\delta(n)
.

If
\delta(n)\succeq s^{n}
, then the difference between
the upper and lower bounds in Theorem
F

lies entirely with the fact that the argument of

\delta
in the upper bound is
n^{2}
rather than
n
. This gap originates in
Corollary
C
, so to close the gap one would have to identify reasonable
hypotheses on
Q
that allow the upper bound in that result to be improved.

2.
The rel-cyclics Dehn function

We are interested in fibre products
P<G\times G
of maps
G\twoheadrightarrow Q
,
particularly features of
P
that are related to aspects of the word problem in
Q
.
In order to quantify these connections, we need to recall some
standard facts and terminology concerning the geometry of the word problem
in finitely presented groups. The reader unfamiliar with this material may wish to consult

[
6
]
. or
[
5
]

Let
\mathcal{P}\equiv\langle X\mid R\rangle
be a finite presentation of a group
\Gamma
. By definition,
a word
w
in the free group
F(X)
represents the identity in
\Gamma
if and only if
there is
an equality in
F(X)

(2.1)

w=\prod_{i=1}^{M}\theta_{i}^{-1}r_{i}\theta_{i}

with
r_{i}\in R\cup R^{-1}
and
\theta_{i}\in F(X)
.
The number
M
of factors in the product on the righthand side is defined to be
the
area
of the product.
This terminology is motivated by the correspondence with diagrams
that comes from van Kampen’s Lemma
[
14
,
6
]
.

One defines

{\rm{Area}}(w)
to be the least area among all products of this form for
w
.
The
Dehn function
of
\mathcal{P}
is the function
\delta:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
defined by

\delta(n)=\max\{{\rm{Area}}(w)\mid w=_{\Gamma}1\ {\rm{and}}\ |w|\leq n\}.

The
noise
of the product on the right of (
2.1
) is defined to be

\sum_{i=0}^{M}|\theta_{i}\theta_{i+1}^{-1}|
, with the convention that

\theta_{0}
and
\theta_{M+1}
are the empty word.
An analysis of the standard proof of van Kampen’s Lemma
yields the
Bounded Noise Lemma
, an observation (and terminology) that is
due to the authors of
[
2
]
; see
[
15
]
, p.18
for an explicit proof.

Lemma 2.1

(Bounded Noise Lemma)
.

If
{\rm{Area}}(w)=M
, then there is a product of the form (
2.1
)
with area
M
and noise at most
ML+|w|
, where
L
is the length of the longest relator in
R
.

2.1.
The rel-cyclics Dehn function

The following functions will arise naturally in our study of
fibre products.

Definition 2.2
.

The
rel-cyclics
Dehn function of a finitely presented group
\Gamma=\langle X\mid R\rangle
is

\delta^{c}(n):=\max_{w,u}\{{\rm{Area}}(w\,u^{p})+|pn|\colon|w|+|u|\leq n,\ w=_{\Gamma}u^{-p},\ |p|\leq o(u)/2\},

where
o(u)\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
is the order of
u
in
\Gamma
. (The condition
|p|\leq o(u)/2

is equivalent to requiring that
|p|
is the least non-negative integer such that
w=u^{\pm p}
in
\Gamma
.)
The
rel
\infty
-cyclics
Dehn function will also enter our discussion,

\delta^{z}(n):=\max_{w,u}\{{\rm{Area}}(w\,u^{p})+|pn|\colon|w|+|u|\leq n,\ w=_{\Gamma}u^{-p},\ o(u)=\infty\},

as will the following variant of
\delta^{c}(n)
,

\delta^{o}(n):=\max_{w,u}\{{\rm{Area}}(w\,u^{p})+|pn|\colon|w|+|u|\leq n,\ w=_{\Gamma}u^{-p},\ |p|\leq o(u)\}.

Note the dependence of these functions on the
return of cyclics
function of
\Gamma
,
which is defined as

{\mathfrak{m}}(n):=\max\{p\colon\exists u\in\Gamma,\ o(u)=\infty,\ |u|_{\Gamma}\leq n,\,|u^{p}|_{\Gamma}\leq n\}.

\delta^{o}(n)
is also related to the
torsion-evolution
function of
\Gamma
, which is

{\mathfrak{t}}(n):=\max\{o(u)\colon|u|_{\Gamma}\leq n,\,o(u)<\infty\}.

The Dehn functions associated to different finite
presentations of isomorphic groups are
\simeq
equivalent;
see e.g.
[
6
,
5
]
. A similar argument shows that
\delta_{\Gamma}^{o}(n)
and its variants are
well-defined up to
\simeq
equivalence. This justifies the notation

\delta_{\Gamma}(n)
and
\delta_{\Gamma}^{o}(n)
in the statements of our theorems,
where only the
\simeq
equivalence class is of concern. Likewise, it is
easy to verify that, up to
\simeq
equivalence,
{\mathfrak{m}}(n)
and
{\mathfrak{t}}(n)
are independent of
the chosen finite generating set. The
\simeq
equivalence of
\delta_{\Gamma}^{c}(n)
is also indifferent
to whether one quantifies over words
w,u
with
\max\{|w|,\,|u|\}\leq n
rather than
|w|+|u|\leq n
.

We will often use a subscript on each of the functions discussed above
to emphasize which group the function is attached to, suppressing mention of
the finite presentation chosen.

Remark 2.3

(
On the definitions of
\delta^{c}(n)
and
\delta^{o}(n)
)
.

In the definition of
\delta^{o}(n)
, by taking
w
to be the empty word we see that

\delta^{o}(n)\geq{\rm{Area}}(u^{o(u)})+{\mathfrak{t}}(n)
for each torsion element
u
with
|u|\leq n
.
The more stringent condition
|p|\leq o(u)/2
in
\delta^{c}(n)

decouples
\delta^{c}(n)
from
{\rm{Area}}(u^{o(u)})
and removes the direct comparison with
{\mathfrak{t}}(n)
,
treating finite and infinite cyclic subgroups in a more equitable manner. The fact that
\delta^{c}(n)

does not control
{\mathfrak{t}}(n)
is an essential feature of Theorem
A
, because there exist fibre products

P<G_{1}\times G_{2}
that satisfy the conditions of that theorem but are such that
there is no algorithm to decide the orders of elements in
Q=G_{1}/(G_{1}\cap P)
: this
means that
\delta_{Q}^{c}(n)
is recursive but
{\mathfrak{t}}_{Q}(n)
is not – see
[
9
, Corollary B]
.

Remark 2.4

(
Standard Dehn functions)
.

We say that a Dehn function is
standard
if it is the Dehn function of a
group of type
{\rm{F}}_{3}
. It is unknown whether all Dehn functions
are standard, up to
\simeq
equivalence, but all of those in the literature
are.

In
[
3
]
, Birget, Rips and Sapir went a long way towards
characterising the functions
which are Dehn functions of finitely presented groups. Their
criterion for being a Dehn function
is expressed in terms of
time functions of Turing machine, with a super-additivity condition
and a technicality
related to the
{\bf{P}}
versus
{\bf{NP}}
problem that only affects sub-exponential
Dehn functions.
This criterion covers all of the known Dehn functions

\delta(n)\succeq n^{4}
.
In addition, the groups constructed in
[
14
]
are all fundamental
groups of finite aspherical complexes, as are the groups in
[
4
]
, so
their Dehn functions
\delta(n)
are standard.

3.
Distortion of Fibre Products

We fix an exact sequence

1\to N\to G\overset{f}{\to}Q\to 1

and consider the fibre product
P<G\times G
. Although we shall not mention it explicitly,
on several occasions we will exploit the observation that

P=(N\times 1)\rtimes\Delta
, where
\Delta\cong G
is the diagonal in
G\times G
.

If
G
and
Q

are finitely presented, then there is
a finite subset
A\subset N
that generates
N
as a normal subgroup.
We choose a finite presentation
G=\langle X\mid R\rangle
with

A\subset X
, and we
present
Q
as
Q=\langle X\mid R\cup A\rangle
, so
the identity map on
X
induces
f:G\to Q
.
The following well-known observation is easily verified.

Lemma 3.1
.

P
is generated by
\{(a,1),(x,x)\mid a\in A,\,x\in X\}
.

The bound
{\rm{Dist}}_{P}^{G\times G}(n)\preceq\delta_{Q}(n)
in the following theorem remains valid
when
G
is not hyperbolic, and Remark
3.2
explains what one can say about the reverse inequality.

See
E

Proof.

Working with the generators
\{(1,x),(x,1)\mid x\in X\}
for
G\times G
and

\{(a,1),(x,x)\mid a\in A,\,x\in X\}
for
P
,
we have
|(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})|_{G\times G}=|\gamma_{1}|_{G}+|\gamma_{2}|_{G}
and
|(\gamma,\gamma)|_{P}=|\gamma|_{G}
.
(To see this last equality, note that if one deletes the generators
(a,1)
from a word
w

representing
(\gamma,\gamma)\in P
, then the projection to
1\times G
still represents
\gamma
,
and therefore the redacted word still represents
(\gamma,\gamma)
. Thus only the diagonal
generators
(x,x)
occur in geodesic words.)

Suppose
(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\in P
and let
\gamma=\gamma_{2}^{-1}\gamma_{1}\in N
.
From the triangle inequality in
G
,

|\gamma|_{G}\leq|\gamma_{1}|_{G}+|\gamma_{2}|_{G}=|(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})|_{G\times G}.

And from the triangle inequality in
P
, noting that
(\gamma,1)=(\gamma_{2},\gamma_{2})^{-1}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})
,

\big||(\gamma,1)|_{P}-|(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})|_{P}\big|\leq|(\gamma_{2},\gamma_{2})|_{P}=|\gamma_{2}|_{G}\leq|\gamma_{1}|_{G}+|\gamma_{2}|_{G}=|(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})|_{G\times G}.

Thus, if
|(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})|_{G\times G}\leq n
, then
|\gamma|_{G}\leq n
and the difference between
|(\gamma,1)|_{P}

and
|(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})|_{P}
is at most
n
. Therefore it is enough to establish upper and lower bounds on

|(\gamma,1)|_{P}
for
\gamma\in N
with
|\gamma|_{G}\leq n
.

Let
w
be a word of length at most
n
in the generators
X^{\pm 1}
with
w=\gamma\in N
.
As
w=1
in
Q=\langle X\mid R\cup A\rangle
, there is an equality
in the free group
F(X)
,

(3.1)

w=\prod_{i=1}^{M}\theta_{i}^{-1}r_{i}\theta_{i},

with
r_{i}\in(R\cup A)^{-1}
and
M\leq\delta_{Q}(n)
. By Lemma
2.1
,
we may assume that the noise

\sum_{i}|\theta_{i}\theta_{i+1}^{-1}|

is at most
n+\delta_{Q}(n)L
, where
L
is the length of the longest relator in
R
.
If
r_{i}\in R^{\pm 1}
, then
r_{i}=1
in
G
. So
by deleting each factor with
r_{i}\in R^{\pm 1}
from the product
and reindexing we get an
equality in
G
of the form

(3.2)

w=\prod_{j=1}^{M^{\prime}}\theta_{j}^{-1}a_{j}\theta_{j},

with
a_{j}\in A^{\pm 1}
and
M^{\prime}\leq M
. The noise of the product in (
3.2
), which we still regard as an element
of the free group, is no greater
than that of (
3.1
) because if the
factor
\theta_{i}^{-1}r_{i}\theta_{i}
is deleted, then
|\theta_{i-1}\theta_{i}^{-1}|+|\theta_{i}\theta_{i+1}^{-1}|
is subtracted from the noise while

|\theta_{i-1}\theta_{i+1}^{-1}|
is added, and by the triangle
inequality

|\theta_{i-1}\theta_{i}^{-1}|+|\theta_{i}\theta_{i+1}^{-1}|\geq|\theta_{i-1}\theta_{i+1}^{-1}|.

We can regard (
3.2
) as an equality
in
G\times 1
by replacing each symbol
x
with
(x,1)
.
And since
(x,x)^{-1}(a,1)(x,x)=(x,1)^{-1}(a,1)(x,1)
for all
x\in X
and

a\in A
, the equality remains valid in
G\times G
if we subsequently replace
each symbol
(x,1)
in each of the
\theta_{i}
by
(x,x)
while replacing
a_{j}
with
(a_{j},1)
.
Writing
\Theta_{i}
for the word derived from
\theta_{i}
in this way, we
obtain an equality in
P<G\times G
where the righthand side is a product of conjugates of generators:

(\gamma,1)=(w,1)=\prod_{j=1}^{M^{\prime}}\Theta_{j}^{-1}(a_{j},1)\Theta_{j},

where
M^{\prime}\leq M\leq\delta_{Q}(n)
. Moreover, since each
|\Theta_{j}\Theta_{j+1}^{-1}|=|\theta_{j}\theta_{j+1}^{-1}|
, the noise
\sum_{j}|\Theta_{j}\Theta_{j+1}^{-1}|

is no greater than the noise of (
3.1
), which is at most
L\delta_{Q}(n)+n
.
Thus, recalling the convention that
\theta_{0}
and
\theta_{M^{\prime}+1}

are empty words, we get

(3.3)

|(\gamma,1)|_{P}\leq\sum_{j=0}^{M^{\prime}}(|\theta_{j}\theta_{j+1}^{-1}|+1)\leq(L+1)\delta_{Q}(n)+n.

This finishes the proof that
{\rm{Dist}}_{P}^{G\times G}(n)\preceq\delta_{Q}(n)
.

Towards proving that
\delta_{Q}(n)\preceq{\rm{Dist}}_{P}^{G\times G}(n)
,
consider a word
\omega_{n}\in F(X)
of length at most
n

such that
\omega_{n}=1
in
Q
and

\hbox{\rm Area}(\omega_{n})=\delta_{Q}(n)
. Note that
\omega_{n}\in N<G
.
Let
W
be a word of length
\ell=|(\omega_{n},1)|_{P}

in the generators
A_{0}=\{(a,1)\mid a\in A\}
and
X_{\Delta}=\{(x,x)\mid x\in X\}
that equals
(\omega_{n},1)
in
P
.
We move all occurrences of each
(x,x)
to the left in
W
to obtain
an equality
in the free group

F(A_{0}\cup X_{\Delta})

(3.4)

4.
The geometry of cyclic subgroups

In this section we explore three geometric properties of cyclic subgroups of
finitely generated groups. The first property, having
uniformly quasigeodesic
cyclics
, is enjoyed by a wide range of groups, while the second property,

uniform monotonicity of cyclic subgroups
, is something that we establish only
for direct products of torsion-free hyperbolic groups. The third property,
having
uniformly proper cyclics
, is less stringent; it is of interest to us largely
because its relationship to the computability of the strengthened rel-cyclics
Dehn function
\delta^{o}(n)
and the membership problem for cyclic sub-semigroups
parallels the relationship between

\delta^{c}(n)
and the membership problem for cyclic subgroups – see Proposition
4.10
.

4.1.
Uniformly quasigeodesic cyclics

In the following definition, the value of the constant
\lambda
depends
on the choice of finite generating set for
G
but its existence does not.

Definition 4.1
.

We say that a finitely generated group
G
has
uniformly quasigeodesic cyclics
(UQC)
if there is a constant
\lambda>0
such that
|g^{n}|_{G}\geq\lambda|n|
for all
integers
n
and all
g\in G\smallsetminus\{1\}
.

In any finitely generated
group, the sequence
|g^{n}|_{G}/n
is sub-additive, so the limit as
n\to\infty

exists; it is called the
algebraic translation number
of
g\in G
,

\tau(g):=\lim_{n}\frac{1}{n}|g^{n}|_{G}.

By the triangle inequality,

\frac{|g^{2n}|_{G}}{2n}\leq\frac{|g^{n}|_{G}}{2n}+\frac{|g^{n}|_{G}}{2n}=\frac{|g^{n}|_{G}}{n}.

Hence
\frac{|g^{n}|_{G}}{n}\geq\frac{|g^{n2^{k}}|_{G}}{n2^{k}}
and letting
k\to\infty

we have
\tau(g)\leq\frac{|g^{n}|_{G}}{n}
for all
g
and
n
. The following
lemma follows easily from this observation.

Lemma 4.2
.

A finitely generated group

G
has uniformly quasigeodesic cyclics (UQG) if and only if
\{\tau(g)\mid g\in G\smallsetminus\{1\}\}
is bounded away from
0
.

Torsion-free hyperbolic groups have uniformly quasigeodesic cyclics
– see
[
10
]
, pp. 464–466. So too do
groups that act
freely, properly and cocompactly by isometries on CAT
(0)
spaces:
this follows from the Švarc-Milnor Lemma and Proposition II.6.10(3) of
[
10
]
.
Other groups with UQC include torsion-free subgroups
of automorphism groups of free groups
[
1
]
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
G
and
H
be finitely generated groups and let

M<G
be a finitely generated subgroup.
If
G
and
H
have uniformly quasigeodesic cyclics, then so do
G\times H,\ M
and
G\dot{\ast}_{M}=(G,t\mid[t,m]=1\ \forall m\in M)
.

Proof.

For suitable choices of word metrics,
|\mu|_{G}\leq|\mu|_{M}
and
|(g,h)|_{G\times H}=|g|_{G}+|h|_{H}
for all
g\in G,h\in H
and
\mu\in M
, so the only non-trivial assertion is
that
G\dot{\ast}_{M}
has UQC if
G
does.

We fix a finite generating set for
G
and add the stable letter
t
to
get a generating set for
G\dot{\ast}_{M}
. Note that the inclusion
G\hookrightarrow G\dot{\ast}_{M}
is an isometric embedding for the corresponding
word metrics.

If a non-trivial element
\gamma\in G\dot{\ast}_{M}

is not conjugate into
G
,
then
it is conjugate to an element
\gamma_{0}
represented by a word in cyclically reduced HNN form

g_{0}t^{e_{0}}\cdots g_{l}t^{e_{l}}
, with each exponent
e_{i}
non-trivial and

g_{i}\in G\smallsetminus M
or else
l=0
and
g_{0}\in M
.
If
l=0
then
(g_{0}t^{e_{0}})^{n}=g_{0}^{n}t^{ne_{0}}
.
In the case
l>0
, for every integer
n>0
, the expression

(g_{0}t^{e_{0}}\cdots g_{l}t^{e_{l}})^{n}

is still in reduced HNN form. Regardless, by Britton’s Lemma, any reduced word representing

\gamma_{0}^{n}
will contain
n
occurrences of
t^{\pm 1}
for each occurrence in the reduced form of
\gamma_{0}
.
Thus
|\gamma_{0}^{n}|_{G\dot{\ast}_{M}}\geq n
, whence
\tau(\gamma)=\tau(\gamma_{0})\geq 1
.

If
\gamma
is conjugate to
g\in G
, then
\tau(\gamma)=\tau(g)

can be calculated in
G
as well as
G\dot{\ast}_{M}
.
Thus

\inf\{\tau(\gamma)\mid\gamma\in G\dot{\ast}_{M})\}=\inf\{\tau(g)\mid g\in G\}

and
G\dot{\ast}_{M}
has UQC if and only if
G
does.
∎

The following simple observation will be crucial in the proof of our main results.

Lemma 4.4
.

If a finitely presented group
G
has UQG, then
\delta_{G}^{Z}(n)=\delta_{G}^{c}(n)=\delta_{G}^{o}(n)\preceq\delta_{G}(n^{2})
.

Proof.

If
G
has UQG then it is torsion-free and hence
\delta_{G}^{Z}(n)=\delta_{G}^{c}(n)=\delta_{G}^{o}(n)
.
In the torsion-free case, the definition of
\delta_{G}^{c}(n)
involves the maximum of
{\rm{Area}}(wu^{p})

with
w=u^{-p}
, where
p\in\mathbb{Z}
and
|w|+|u|\leq n
.
If
G
has UQG, there is a constant
\lambda>0
such that
|u^{p}|_{G}\geq\lambda|p|
,
so
w=u^{-p}
and
|w|\leq n
implies
|p|\leq n/\lambda
. Therefore,
|wu^{p}|\leq n(1+n/\lambda)

and
\delta_{G}^{c}(n)\leq\delta_{G}(n(1+n/\lambda))
.
∎

4.2.
Uniformly monotone cyclics

In the following definition, the value of the constant
k
depends
on the choice of finite generating set for
G
but its existence does not.

Definition 4.5
.

A finitely generated group
G
is said to have
uniformly monotone
cyclics
if
there exists a constant
k
such that
|g^{i}|_{G}\leq k|g^{p}|_{G}

for all
g\in G
and all integers
0<i<|p|
.

One might be tempted to think that this property would follow if one knew
that
G
has uniformly quasigeodesic cyclics, but the following example
shows that it does not.

Example 4.6
.

Let
G=\langle x,y\mid[x^{2},y]\rangle
be the group obtained from
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
by freely
attaching a square root to a primitive element. It is
easy to see, for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, that
y^{n}xy^{-n}
is a geodesic word in
G
.
But
(y^{n}xy^{-n})^{2}=x^{2}
. Thus
G
does not have uniformly monotone cyclics.
On the other hand,
G
does have UQG, because
the standard 2-complex of the given presentation can be endowed with a metric
of non-positive curvature by metrizing the 2-cell as a Euclidean rectangle
with sides labelled
x^{2}
and
y
. (Alternatively, one can observe that
G=\mathbb{Z}\dot{\ast}_{2\mathbb{Z}}
.)

We will need the following result in the proof of
our main results. This observation does not appear to be in the literature.
The proof is less direct than one would like, but
uses only standard techniques.

Torsion-free hyperbolic groups have

4.3.
Uniformly proper cyclics and cyclic semigroup membership

It is easy to verify that the following property is independent of the choice of finite generating set for
\Gamma
, although
the exact function
\rho(n)
is not.

Definition 4.9
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated group. We say that the cyclic subgroups of
\Gamma

are
uniformly proper

if there is a recursive function
\rho:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
such that, for all

\gamma\in\Gamma
with
|\gamma|_{\Gamma}\leq n
, either
\gamma
is a torsion element of order less that
\rho(n)
, or
else
|\gamma^{p}|_{G}>n
for all
p\geq\rho(n)
.
Equivalently,
both the torsion-evolution function
{\mathfrak{t}}_{\Gamma}(n)
and
the cyclic return function
{\mathfrak{m}}_{\Gamma}(n)
, as defined in (
2.2
, are bounded above by a recursive function of
n
.

Proposition 4.10
.

For a finitely generated group
\Gamma
, the
following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

\Gamma
has solvable word problem and uniformly proper cyclics;

(ii)

in
\Gamma
, there is uniform algorithm
to solve the membership problem for the cyclic sub-semigroups.

If
\Gamma
is finitely presented, then each of these conditions is equivalent to

(iii)

\delta_{\Gamma}^{o}(n)
is a recursive function.

Proof.

First we prove
(ii)\implies(i)
.
Suppose that there is an algorithm that, given words
x
and
y
in the generators, will decide whether or not
there is an integer
p>0
such that
x=y^{p}
in
\Gamma
.
Taking
y
to be the empty word solves the word problem in
\Gamma
. Taking
y=x^{-1}

enables us to determine if
x
has finite order in
\Gamma
or not. If
x
does have finite
order, then we can use the solution to the word problem to determine its order;
by definition,
{\mathfrak{t}}_{\Gamma}(n)
is the maximum of the orders among words of length
n
.
If
x
has infinite order in
\Gamma
, then for each word
w
with
|w|\leq n
the algorithm
identifies whether or not
w
equals a positive power of
x
; if it does,
the solution to the word problem finds this unique power

.
Let
\rho_{0}(n)
be the maximum of these

with
|x|\leq n
and
|w|\leq n
. Then
\rho(n)=\rho_{0}(n)+{\mathfrak{t}}_{\Gamma}(n)+1

is a recursive function with the properties required in Definition
4.9
.

Conversely, if
\Gamma
has a solvable word problem and
\rho(n)
is as in definition

, then given words
x,y
with
\max\{|x|,|y|\}\leq n

the word problem to check whether or not

in
\Gamma

,
and conclude that

is not in the semigroup generated by

We prove
(iii)\implies(i)

\Gamma
is finitely presented. Assume

is recursive (i.e. computable).
By definition,
\delta_{\Gamma}(n)\leq\delta_{\Gamma}^{o}(n)

and a finitely presented group has a solvable word problem if and only if
its Dehn function is (equivalently, is bounded above by) a recursive function. Thus

\Gamma
has a solvable word problem. The presence of the summand
|pn|

in the definition of

ensures that if
\gamma
is a torsion

then the order of

is less than
\delta_{\Gamma}^{o}(n)
, and
so
{\mathfrak{t}}_{\Gamma}(n)
is bounded above by this recursive

2.3
). It follows that
{\mathfrak{t}}_{\Gamma}(n)
can be calculated exactly by using the

for each word of length at most
n
and each positive integer

.
The inclusion of the
|pn|
term in
\delta_{\Gamma}^{o}(n)
also ensures that

{\mathfrak{m}}_{\Gamma}(n)\leq\delta_{\Gamma}^{o}(n)
, from which it follows that
{\mathfrak{m}}_{\Gamma}(n)
is also recursive.

Finally, we prove
(i)\implies(iii)
if
\Gamma
is finitely presented. Given

, we list pairs of

with

and for each positive integer

we use the solution to the word problem to determine if

in
\Gamma
.
Thus we determine the order of each

. If
u
has order

then for each positive

we use the solution to the problem to decide if

. If it does, then we calculate

this can be done by naively testing all free equalities of the form (

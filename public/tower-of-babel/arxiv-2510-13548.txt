Strange Pentaquark Molecules in QCD Sum Rules
Di Ben, Sheng-Qi Zhang

Abstract
In this article, we systematically construct hidden- and open-strange pentaquarks in the hadronic molecular picture to search for possible partners of hadronic molecular state multiplets in the strange quark sector using QCD sum rules. Within this picture, 21 distinct currents are constructed with J P = 1 / 2 − J^{P}=1/2^{-} and 3 / 2 − 3/2^{-} . There is no bound state found from J P = 1 / 2 − J^{P}=1/2^{-} currents, while three of them from J P = 3 / 2 − J^{P}=3/2^{-} currents are bound-state solutions consistent with the Λ ⁡ ( 2050 ) ​ 3 / 2 − \Lambda(2050)3/2^{-} , Ξ ⁡ ( 2250 ) \Xi(2250) , and N ⁡ ( 2120 ) ​ 3 / 2 − N(2120)3/2^{-} states, which can be identified as Λ ​ ϕ \Lambda\phi , Ξ ​ ϕ \Xi\phi , and Σ ​ K ∗ \Sigma K^{\ast} hadronic molecular states, respectively. Other constructions are interpreted as hadronic molecular states composed of excited states with the same quantum numbers, and some of these exhibit mass values that align with known resonances listed in the RPP(PDG). Our numerical results indicate that Λ ⁡ ( 2050 ) ​ 3 / 2 − \Lambda(2050)3/2^{-} and Ξ ⁡ ( 2250 ) \Xi(2250) should be the multiplet partner of each other. Additionally, although Σ ​ K ∗ \Sigma K^{\ast} bound state was identified under this framework, no corresponding partner states were found within the context of the currents we constructed. Alternative constructions may offer new possibilities for identifying such states.

The correlation functions can be described at both the quark-gluon level and the hadron level, where the former is called QCD representation and the latter is called phenomenological representation. At the quark-gluon level, the correlation functions can be represented by the operator product expansion (OPE) and dispersion relations in the following form:

\displaystyle\Pi^{\text{QCD}}_{i}(q^{2})=\int_{s_{min}}^{\infty}\frac{\rho_{i}^{\text{QCD}}(s)}{s-q^{2}}ds\,,

\displaystyle\rho^{\text{QCD}}_{i}(s)=\frac{1}{\pi}\,\text{Im}\,\Pi^{\text{QCD}}_{i}(s)\,.

Moreover, the masses of the

QCD sum rules involve two crucial parameters, which are the Borel parameter
M_{B}^{2}
and the threshold parameter
s_{0}
. The selection of appropriate ranges for these two parameters is essential to ensure the reliability of the QCD sum rule analysis. The determination of these two parameters is typically guided by two main criteria. First, the convergence of the OPE series must be ensured, which requires that the contributions from the highest dimensional condensates are sufficiently suppressed. In this work, to guarantee convergence of the OPE we demand that the contribution from the highest dimension term remain below 20% of the total OPE contribution, i.e.

R^{\text{OPE}}=\frac{\int_{s_{min}}^{s_{0}}ds\,\rho_{1,D_{max}}^{\text{QCD}}}{\int_{s_{min}}^{s_{0}}ds\,\rho_{1}^{\text{QCD}}}<20\%\,,

(27)

where
\rho_{1,D_{max}}^{\text{QCD}}
represents the contribution from the highest-dimensional condensate considered in the OPE series.

Second, the pole contribution (PC) from the ground state should be substantial compared to the total contribution. As noted in Refs.
[
69
,
70
,
71
]
, the high power of
s
in the spectral density will suppress the PC value; accordingly the pole contribution will be required larger than 10%, i.e.

\displaystyle R^{\text{PC}}=\frac{\int_{s_{min}}^{s_{0}}ds\,\rho_{1}^{\text{QCD}}(s)e^{-s/M_{B}^{2}}}{\int_{s_{min}}^{\infty}ds\,\rho_{1}^{\text{QCD}}(s)e^{-s/M_{B}^{2}}}>10\%\,.

(28)

Furthermore, since the hadron mass is a physical observable, it should be independent of auxiliary parameters. Consequently, a trustworthy prediction is obtained in an optimal window where the extracted mass is minimally sensitive to the Borel parameter
M_{B}^{2}
.

In practice, we gradually adjust

4
Summary and Conclusions

In this article, we systematically construct hidden- and open-strange hadronic molecules from the baryon octet combining with a pair of
s\bar{s}
quarks and discuss the spectrum obtained by QCD sum rules. The aim is to explore, whether existing proposed hadronic molecular states possess corresponding partners within a multiplet framework.

Within this picture, we have constructed a total of 21 distinct currents, covering both spin-parity
J^{P}=1/2^{-}
and
3/2^{-}
. There is no bound state found in
J^{P}=1/2^{-}
sector, while three of them in
J^{P}=3/2^{-}
sector are bound-state solutions consistent with the hadronic molecular picture, named,
\Lambda(2050)3/2^{-}
,
\Xi(2250)
, and
N(2120)3/2^{-}
, as
\Lambda\phi
,
\Xi\phi
, and
\Sigma K^{\ast}
molecular states, respectively. This further suggests that if state
\Xi(2250)
is indeed the anticipated hadronic molecular state, then the QCD sum rule calculation predicts its spin-parity to be
J^{P}=3/2^{-}
. Other states are interpreted as hadronic molecular states composed of excited states with the same quantum numbers, noted as
N\eta(1295)
,
\Lambda\eta(1295)
,
\Lambda K(1460)
,
N(1710)\phi
,
\Lambda\phi(1680)
,
\Sigma(1660)\phi
,
\Lambda K^{*}(1680)
, and
N\phi(1680)
, and some of these exhibit mass values that align with known resonances listed in the RPP(PDG), which are
\Xi(2500)
,
\Sigma(2455)
, and
\Sigma(2620)
.

In addition, a special case of
\Sigma\eta_{s}
system, which appears near the threshold of
\Sigma\eta^{\prime}
, and also can be linked with
\Sigma(2120)1/2^{-}
resonance may indicating that while the
\Sigma(2120)1/2^{-}
state can be interpreted as a hadronic molecular state of
\Sigma\eta^{\prime}
, the
s\bar{s}
component plays a critical role in forming the bound molecular structure.

Our numerical results indicate that

\Lambda(2050)3/2^{-}
and
\Xi(2250)
should be the multiplet partner of each other, while both
N\eta_{s}
and
\Lambda\eta_{s}
systems were generated a resonance state near the
N\eta(1295)
and
\Lambda\eta(1295)
two-particle thresholds, respectively, indicating that they should also belong to the same multiplet and be the partner of each other. Unfortunately, although
\Sigma K^{\ast}
bound state was identified under this framework, no corresponding partner states were found within the context of the currents we constructed.

Alternative constructions may offer new possibilities for identifying such states. Future investigations into a broader range of current structures may provide further insights into the open questions addressed in this work, while also improving the interpretation of the light-flavor hadron spectrum from the perspective of QCD sum rules.

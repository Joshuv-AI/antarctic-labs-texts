FAO veterinary vaccine registration: enhancing access to safe and high-quality livestock vaccines for disease control programs and during emergency in countries
Authors: Gian Mario Cosseddu, Yu Qiu, Danny Goovaerts, Artem Metlin, Davide Blancato, Sergei Maliski, Thanawat Tiensin, Samia Metwally
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractFAO recently, developed and adopted a Vaccine Registration system for vaccines against priority diseases. It consists of a transparent and scientifically sound assessment process to ensure that selected vaccines meet global standards of quality, safety and efficacy, coupled with a procurement mechanism limiting the open public tender to the selected and approved suppliers. This streamlined approach accelerates the procurement of quality vaccines for disease control programs and during emergencies in FAO member nations.

Vaccination against transboundary animal diseases (TADs) is one of the most effective interventions for controlling infectious diseases in livestock [1]. It is cornerstone of the One Health approach which links animal, human and environmental health. Livestock vaccination limits the circulation of pathogens, including zoonotic agents, reducing the burden of diseases [2] and therefore the use of antimicrobials and the emergence and the spread of resistant bacteria [3]. Protecting livestock health is key for improving global health, economic development, food security, food quality, and poverty reduction, especially in low- and middle-income countries (LIMCs) [4, 5], while mitigating against climate change and biodiversity loss [6, 7].

In many LMICs the quality assessment of veterinary vaccines often faces challenges due to inconsistent regulatory standards and the limited resources and expertise available within national regulatory authorities [8–10]. The vaccines composition need to be adapted to reflect locally circulating strains and ensure optimal vaccine-strain matching [11]. The variable quality of the products available on the market raise concern [12, 13] as vaccine failures and disease outbreaks among vaccinated animals are frequently reported [14, 15]. This undermines confidence among veterinarians, farmers, and the broader public [16, 17] and warn on the critical importance to ensure proper quality control of vaccines for efficient and safe use on field [18]. The Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations (FAO) has played a pivotal role in supporting countries by providing vaccines during emergencies and for global control programs. Over the past three years, FAO has supplied approximately 212 million doses of emergency vaccines for priority TADs, valued at around 20 million USD. Key diseases addressed include: Foot and Mouth Disease (FMD), Peste des Petits Ruminants (PPR), Sheep and Goat pox (SGP), Lumpy Skin Disease (LSD), among others [4]. Throughout these activities, the FAO maintains the critical responsibility to ensure the quality of the procured vaccines by rigorous evaluation of submitted data on quality, safety, and efficacy for all products intended for acquisition.

Vaccine procurement at FAO used to be through open Invitations to Bid (ITB) following a technical review and clearance process. This ITB-by-ITB approach is time consuming and may challenge the urgent need for timely procurement of quality vaccines during emergency, particularly in remote areas [4]. To address this issue the FAO recently, developed and adopted a Vaccine Registration initiative for vaccines against priority diseases. It consists of a transparent and scientifically sound assessment process to ensure that selected vaccines meet global standards of quality, safety and efficacy, including the disease specific standards developed by the World Organisation for Animal Health (WOAH) [19], coupled with a procurement mechanism limiting the open public tender to the selected and approved suppliers. This streamlined approach accelerates vaccine procurement and deployment optimizing use of resources and improving health outcomes in the field [1, 4, 17].

The vaccine registration process begins with an invitation to manufacturers to submit complete dossiers in response to a Request for Expression of Interest (REOI) call published on the UN Global Marketplace (UNGM). To facilitate a better understanding of the process, interested bidders may participate in an online Bidder’s Conference conducted by FAO technical teams involved in the procedure. Submitted dossiers are evaluated by independent international experts, recruited by FAO for quality, safety and efficacy in accordance with the FAO Registration Procedure for Veterinary Vaccines. Appointed experts are required to sign confidentiality agreements and declare the absence of conflict of interest as specified in the Guidelines. Dossier also includes information on manufacturing capacity and expected timeline for delivery. Manufacturing site compliance with Good Manufacturing Practices (GMP) is verified through site visits, when necessary.

Manufacturers that meet all requirements are invited to sign an agreement with the FAO enabling facilitated procurement for three years with the possibility of a two-year extension. Those not selected receive technical feedback and, upon request, support to improve the production standards.

Independent testing of final vaccine products by FAO reference laboratories ensure that the final products consistently meet quality, safety, and efficacy standards. Post-registration monitoring of vaccine performance in recipient countries allow to rapidly react in cases of non-compliance. This registration system reduces the timeline for vaccine procurement from about 16 weeks to 5–7 weeks.

Since 2024, REOIs have been launched for vaccines against LSD, PPR, SGP and FMD. A total of 53 submissions were received, of which 18 met the procedure’s requirements, resulting in the selection of 4 vaccines for LSD, 10 for PPR, and 4 for SGP. The assessment of the FMD dossiers is currently ongoing. Deficiencies identified during dossier review highlight key areas for improvement in vaccine manufacture and quality, enabling tailored support to manufacturers in enhancing their capacity to produce quality vaccines.

Registration enhances confidence in quality and safety and represents a mechanism for expanding both the number of vaccines and the pool of assessed manufacturers, ultimately increasing quality assured vaccine availability. Furthermore, this process is free of charge for vaccine manufacturers, as the associated costs are covered by the UN agency.

The FAO Veterinary Vaccine Registration process shares several features with the World Health Organization (WHO) Vaccine Prequalification (PQv) system [20]. Both systems are based on international standards, utilize independent international expertise, conduct manufacturing site visits, and combine dossier-based evaluation with post-registration monitoring of vaccine quality [20]. Additionally, the FAO procedure provides a framework for supporting vaccine manufacturers in meeting international requirements.

Unlike the human vaccines sector, there are currently no internationally recognized mechanisms to independently assure the quality of veterinary vaccines supplied by vendors. The FAO Veterinary Vaccine Registration is the first system, established by an international organization to evaluate vaccine performance. While it currently operates as an internal mechanism it holds significant potential for scaling up or being adopted by external partners, which could foster broader global dialogue and provide a standardized benchmark for vaccine quality.

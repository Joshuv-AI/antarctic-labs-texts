Characterization of P-divisibility in two-level open quantum systems
G. Théret, C. Lombard-Latune, D. Sugny

Abstract
We study different characterizations of P-divisibility in two-level open quantum systems whose dynamics are governed by a time-local master equation with time-dependent relaxation rates. Necessary and sufficient conditions for the P-divisibility of the dynamical map are given in terms of inequalities on such relaxation rates. The equivalence between several P-divisibility characterizations existing in the literature is explicitly proven. The connection to the Breuer-Laine-Piilo measure of non-Markovianity is also established. As an application of such characterizations, we study the open dynamics of a qubit interacting with a bosonic mode. More precisely, we characterize the properties of the local map on the qubit generated by its interaction with the bosonic mode, playing the role of an extremely reduced bath. Interesting observations are made, opening perspectives for a deeper physical understanding of CP-divisibility, P-divisibility, and BLP measure.

The model system

We consider an open two-level quantum system interacting with a bath. The state of the system at time
\displaystyle t
is described by a density operator
\displaystyle\rho(t)
. The two-dimensional Hilbert space of the system
\displaystyle\mathcal{H}=\mathbb{C}^{2}
is spanned by the canonical basis
\displaystyle\{|1\rangle,|2\rangle\}
associated with the ground and excited states, respectively. We introduce
\displaystyle\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{H})
the set of density operators, i.e. the set of positive semi-definite Hermitian operators of unit trace and
\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
the set of Hermitian operators (not necessarily of trace one). The dynamical map
\displaystyle\Phi
describing the dynamics of the system maps the initial state
\displaystyle\rho(0)
to
\displaystyle\rho(t)=\Phi(t)\rho(0)
. This map is assumed to be a completely positive trace preserving map (CPTP) to ensure that the properties of the density operator are preserved throughout its time evolution. In the following, we consider a map described by the following GKLS-like master equation

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{d\rho(t)}{dt}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle-i\left[H(t),\rho(t)\right]

(1)

\displaystyle\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{3}\gamma_{j}(t)\!\left(L_{j}\rho(t)L_{j}^{\dagger}-\frac{1}{2}\left\{L_{j}^{\dagger}L_{j},\rho(t)\right\}\right),

in units where
\displaystyle\hbar=1
with

\displaystyle L_{1}=\sigma_{+}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}
,

\displaystyle L_{2}=\sigma_{-}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}
,

\displaystyle L_{3}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\sigma_{z}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix}
,

\displaystyle H(t)=-\frac{\omega(t)}{2}\sigma_{z}=\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}-\omega(t)&0\\
0&\omega(t)\end{pmatrix}
,
and
\displaystyle\sigma_{\pm}\equiv\frac{1}{2}(\sigma_{x}\pm i\sigma_{y})
, with
\displaystyle\sigma_{x,y,z}
the Pauli matrices. The diagonal relaxation rates
\displaystyle\gamma_{j}
(
\displaystyle j=1,2,3
) can be expressed in terms of the population relaxation rates
\displaystyle\gamma_{12},\gamma_{21}
and decoherence rate
\displaystyle\Gamma
as
\displaystyle\gamma_{1}=\gamma_{12}
,
\displaystyle\gamma_{2}=\gamma_{21}
and
\displaystyle\gamma_{3}=\Gamma-\frac{1}{2}(\gamma_{12}+\gamma_{21})

[
16
]
. We introduce the parameters
\displaystyle\gamma_{\pm}
defined by
\displaystyle\gamma_{\pm}\equiv\gamma_{12}\pm\gamma_{21}
. The necessary and sufficient conditions of CP-divisibility at time
\displaystyle t
,
\displaystyle\gamma_{j}(t)\geq 0
(
\displaystyle j=1,2,3
)
[
19
,
20
,
21
]
, are then given by

\displaystyle\displaystyle|\gamma_{-}|\leq\gamma_{+},

and

\displaystyle\displaystyle 2\Gamma\geq\gamma_{+}.

(2)

The density matrix of the system can be written in terms of the Bloch coordinates
\displaystyle(x,y,z)=(\textrm{Tr}[\rho\sigma_{x}],\textrm{Tr}[\rho\sigma_{y}],\textrm{Tr}[\rho\sigma_{z}])
as

\rho=\frac{1}{2}(\mathbb{I}+x\sigma_{x}+y\sigma_{y}+z\sigma_{z})=\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}1+z&x-iy\\
x+iy&1-z\end{pmatrix},

(3)

where
\displaystyle\mathbb{I}
is the identity matrix. We recall that the Bloch ball
\displaystyle\mathfrak{B}
is the ball in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}
such that
\displaystyle x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}\leq 1
and a one-to-one mapping to
\displaystyle\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{H})
can be defined from (
3
). It is then straightforward to show that the GKLS-like equation can be transformed into the Bloch equations

\frac{d}{dt}\begin{pmatrix}x\\
y\\
z\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}-\Gamma&\omega&0\\
-\omega&-\Gamma&0\\
0&0&-\gamma_{+}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}x\\
y\\
z\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}0\\
0\\
\gamma_{-}\end{pmatrix},

whose solutions are given by

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}x(t)&=e^{-\tilde{\Gamma}}(x_{0}\cos\tilde{\omega}+y_{0}\sin\tilde{\omega})\\
y(t)&=e^{-\tilde{\Gamma}}(-x_{0}\sin\tilde{\omega}+y_{0}\cos\tilde{\omega})\\
z(t)&=s+z_{0}e^{-\tilde{\gamma}_{+}}\end{array}\right.,

where
\displaystyle(x_{0},y_{0},z_{0})\equiv(x(0),y(0),z(0))
,
\displaystyle\tilde{\Gamma}(t)\equiv\int_{0}^{t}\Gamma(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}
,
\displaystyle\tilde{\gamma}_{\pm}(t)\equiv\int_{0}^{t}\gamma_{\pm}(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}
,
\displaystyle\tilde{\omega}(t)\equiv\int_{0}^{t}\omega(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}
, and
\displaystyle s
is the solution of
\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}s(t)=-\gamma_{+}s(t)+\gamma_{-}
such that
\displaystyle s(0)=0
. Note that
\displaystyle s(t)=e^{-\tilde{\gamma}_{+}(t)}\int_{0}^{t}e^{\tilde{\gamma}_{+}(u)}\gamma_{-}(u)du
, which depends on
\displaystyle\tilde{\gamma}_{+}
and
\displaystyle\gamma_{-}
.
Integrating by parts yields
\displaystyle s(t)=\tilde{\gamma}_{-}(t)-e^{-\tilde{\gamma}_{+}(t)}\int_{0}^{t}\tilde{\gamma}_{-}(u)e^{\tilde{\gamma}_{+}(u)}\gamma_{+}(u)du
, which now depends on
\displaystyle\tilde{\gamma}_{-}
and
\displaystyle\gamma_{+}
.
We deduce (by equating both expressions and varying independently
\displaystyle\gamma_{\pm}
without changing
\displaystyle\tilde{\gamma}_{\pm}
) that the evolution depends only upon the averaged coefficients
\displaystyle\tilde{\omega},\tilde{\gamma}_{\pm},\tilde{\Gamma}
.

The associated dynamical map
\displaystyle t\mapsto\Phi(t)
is given in matrix form by

\Phi(t)=\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}1+s+e^{-\tilde{\gamma}_{+}}&0&0&1+s-e^{-\tilde{\gamma}_{+}}\\
0&2e^{-(\tilde{\Gamma}-i\tilde{\omega})}&0&0\\
0&0&2e^{-(\tilde{\Gamma}+i\tilde{\omega})}&0\\
1-s-e^{-\tilde{\gamma}_{+}}&0&0&1-s+e^{-\tilde{\gamma}_{+}}\\
\end{pmatrix},

(4)

for a density matrix
\displaystyle\rho
written as a column vector,
\displaystyle(\rho_{11},\rho_{12},\rho_{21},\rho_{22})^{\intercal}
, with
\displaystyle\rho_{ij}
the matrix elements of
\displaystyle\rho
in the canonical basis.
It will be useful in the following to extend the dynamical map (

) to
\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
as a map
\displaystyle t\mapsto\Phi(t)\,:\,\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\to\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
. For
\displaystyle q\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
, the expression (
3
) can be generalized as
\displaystyle q=\frac{1}{2}({\rm Tr}(q)\mathbb{I}+x\sigma_{x}+y\sigma_{y}+z\sigma_{z})
where the Bloch coordinates are defined as for
\displaystyle\rho
. This gives in matrix form

\Phi(t)q=\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}{\rm Tr}(q)+z(t)&x(t)-iy(t)\\
x(t)+iy(t)&{\rm Tr}(q)-z(t)\end{pmatrix},

(5)

where

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}x(t)=&2e^{-(\tilde{\Gamma}-i\tilde{\omega})}q_{12}\\
y(t)=&2e^{-(\tilde{\Gamma}+i\tilde{\omega})}q_{21}\\
z(t)=&sq_{+}+e^{-\tilde{\gamma}_{+}}q_{-}\end{array}\right.

and
\displaystyle q_{\pm}=q_{11}\pm q_{22}
, the
\displaystyle q_{ij}
being the coefficients of
\displaystyle q
in the canonical basis.

The density operator
\displaystyle\rho

is said to be an
instantaneous fixed point
of the dynamics at time
\displaystyle t
if
\displaystyle\dot{\rho}(t)=0
, that is,

\begin{pmatrix}-\gamma_{21}&0&0&\gamma_{12}\\
0&i\omega-\Gamma&0&0\\
0&0&-i\omega-\Gamma&0\\
\gamma_{21}&0&0&-\gamma_{12}\\
\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\rho_{11}\\
\rho_{12}\\
\rho_{21}\\
\rho_{22}\end{pmatrix}=0.

Using
\displaystyle\rho_{11}+\rho_{22}=1
, we have
\displaystyle\rho_{11}=\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\frac{\gamma_{-}}{\gamma_{+}}\right)
and
\displaystyle\rho_{22}=\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{\gamma_{-}}{\gamma_{+}}\right)
. We deduce that there is a unique instantaneous fixed point for the dynamics at time
\displaystyle t
which is the state

\rho_{\rm fp}=\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}1+z_{\rm fp}&0\\
0&1-z_{\rm fp}\end{pmatrix},

where
\displaystyle z_{\rm fp}=\frac{\gamma_{-}}{\gamma_{+}}
. When the ratio
\displaystyle\gamma_{-}/\gamma_{+}
is constant, the time evolution of the coordinate
\displaystyle z(t)
reads

z(t)=z_{\rm fp}-(z_{\rm fp}-z_{0})e^{-\tilde{\gamma}_{+}(t)}.

3
P-divisibility

3.1
P-divisibility with trace norm

A first characterization of P-divisibility in terms of trace norm is given by the following theorem
[
25
]
.

Theorem 1
.

A dynamical map
\displaystyle t\mapsto\Phi(t)\,:\,\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\to\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
is P-divisible
at time
\displaystyle t
if and only if, for all non-zero operators
\displaystyle q\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
,

\frac{d}{dt}{||\Phi(t)q||_{1}}\leq 0,

(6)

where
\displaystyle||A||_{1}=\rm{Tr}[\sqrt{AA^{\dagger}}]
denotes the trace-norm.

The trace norm is easily calculated for two-level quantum systems. We have

\displaystyle\displaystyle||q||_{1}^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=(|\varphi_{+}|+|\varphi_{-}|)^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\rm Tr}(q)^{2}&\textrm{if}\ {\rm det}(q)\geq 0\\
{\rm Tr}(q)^{2}-4{\rm det}(q)&\textrm{if}\ {\rm det}(q)\leq 0\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\rm Tr}(q)^{2}&\textrm{if}\ r^{2}\leq{\rm Tr}(q)^{2}\\
r^{2}&\textrm{if}\ r^{2}\geq{\rm Tr}(q)^{2}\end{array}\right.,

where
\displaystyle\varphi_{\pm}
denote the (real) eigenvalues of
\displaystyle q
and
\displaystyle r^{2}=x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}
. Using
\displaystyle\textrm{Tr}(q)=\textrm{Tr}(\Phi(t)q)
, we obtain for all
\displaystyle t\geq 0
and all
\displaystyle q\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
that

||\Phi(t)q||_{1}^{2}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\rm Tr}(q)^{2}&\textrm{if}\ r(t)^{2}\leq{\rm Tr}(q)^{2}\\
r(t)^{2}&\textrm{if}\ r(t)^{2}\geq{\rm Tr}(q)^{2}\end{array}.\right.

The derivative of the norm
\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}||\Phi(t)q||_{1}
has the same sign as
\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}||\Phi(t)q||_{1}^{2}
.
Hence, condition (
6
) is equivalent to

\forall q\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}),\ r(t)^{2}\geq{\rm Tr}(q)^{2}\Rightarrow\frac{dr}{dt}(t)\leq 0.

(7)

This condition can be reformulated in terms of the rates
\displaystyle\Gamma,\gamma_{\pm}
. We get,
\displaystyle\forall q\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
,

r(t)^{2}\geq{\rm Tr}(q)^{2}\Rightarrow(\Gamma-\gamma_{+})z^{2}+\gamma_{-}{\rm Tr}(q)z-\Gamma r^{2}\leq 0.

(8)

A drawback of the characterization of P-divisibility by the trace norm (
6
) is that it is given for
\displaystyle q\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
, which may not represent a physical state, i.e. such a
\displaystyle q
may not belong to
\displaystyle\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{H})
or lie outside the Bloch ball
\displaystyle\mathfrak{B}
. It turns out that the trace-norm condition (
6
) can be refined by considering states
\displaystyle q
such that, at time
\displaystyle t
,
\displaystyle\Phi(t)q\in\partial\mathfrak{B}
, where
\displaystyle\partial\mathfrak{B}
denotes the Bloch sphere of pure states, for which
\displaystyle r(t)=1
. Note that we can interpret this condition as if the dynamics were restarted over at time
\displaystyle t
from the Bloch sphere.

The following result can then be established.

Theorem 2
.

A dynamical map
\displaystyle t\mapsto\Phi(t)
is P-divisible
at time
\displaystyle t
if and only if for any state
\displaystyle q
such that

\displaystyle\Phi(t)q\in\partial\mathfrak{B}
,
we have

\frac{dr}{dt}(t)\leq 0.

(9)

This means that, if we consider the dynamical map at time
\displaystyle t\geq 0
and if we apply it to trace-one states
\displaystyle q
such that
\displaystyle r(t)
= 1, the radius should not increase locally, i.e.
\displaystyle r(t+dt)\leq 1
.

Proof

First note that condition (
6
) is homogeneous, so we can assume that
\displaystyle{\rm Tr}(q)=0
or
\displaystyle{\rm Tr}(q)=1
.
Hence condition (
8
) is equivalent to

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}r(t)\geq 1,{\rm Tr}(q)=1&\Rightarrow&(\Gamma-\gamma_{+})z^{2}+\gamma_{-}z-\Gamma r^{2}\leq 0\\
{\rm Tr}(q)=0&\Rightarrow&\Gamma(z^{2}-r^{2})-\gamma_{+}z^{2}\leq 0\end{array}\right.

(10)

Clearly, if condition (
10
) (or condition (
7
)) holds, then (
9
) holds as well.

Let us show the converse and assume that condition (
9
) holds, that is, by taking Eq. (
8
) with
\displaystyle r(t)=1
and
\displaystyle{\rm Tr}(q)=1
, for all
\displaystyle q
with
\displaystyle\Phi(t)q\in\partial\mathfrak{B}
,

(\Gamma-\gamma_{+})z^{2}+\gamma_{-}z-\Gamma\leq 0.

The coordinates
\displaystyle x,y,z
can be chosen arbitrarily provided that
\displaystyle r=1
and the state is pure.
Taking
\displaystyle z=0
we get
\displaystyle\Gamma\geq 0
.
Taking
\displaystyle z=\pm 1
we get
\displaystyle-\gamma_{+}+\gamma_{-}\leq 0
and
\displaystyle-\gamma_{+}-\gamma_{-}\leq 0
.
By summing these two relations we obtain
\displaystyle\gamma_{+}\geq 0
and
\displaystyle|\gamma_{-}|\leq\gamma_{+}
. The inequalities
\displaystyle\Gamma(t)\geq 0
and
\displaystyle\gamma_{+}(t)\geq 0
imply that the last condition in (
10
) holds using
\displaystyle z^{2}\leq r^{2}
.

Consider now
\displaystyle q\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
with
\displaystyle{\rm Tr}(q)=1
and
\displaystyle r^{2}\geq 1
.
Since
\displaystyle\Gamma\geq 0
, we have

(\Gamma-\gamma_{+})z^{2}+\gamma_{-}z-\Gamma r^{2}\leq(\Gamma-\gamma_{+})z^{2}+\gamma_{-}z-\Gamma

and we know from condition (
9
) that
\displaystyle(\Gamma-\gamma_{+})z^{2}+\gamma_{-}z-\Gamma\leq 0
for all
\displaystyle z^{2}\leq 1
.

It remains to study the operators
\displaystyle q
such that
\displaystyle z^{2}\geq 1
and
\displaystyle r^{2}\geq 1
.
Since
\displaystyle\Gamma\geq 0
and
\displaystyle z^{2}\leq r^{2}
, we have

(\Gamma-\gamma_{+})z^{2}+\gamma_{-}z-\Gamma r^{2}\leq-\gamma_{+}z^{2}+\gamma_{-}z,

with equality if and only if
\displaystyle x=y=0
.

If
\displaystyle\gamma_{+}=0
, so is
\displaystyle\gamma_{-}
and the inequality is satisfied.
Suppose that
\displaystyle\gamma_{+}\neq 0
.
The right-hand side is a polynomial which is not positive for all
\displaystyle|z|\geq|z_{0}|=\left|\frac{\gamma_{-}}{\gamma_{+}}\right|
.
We know that
\displaystyle|\gamma_{-}|\leq\gamma_{+}
, so
\displaystyle|z_{0}|\leq 1
.
Therefore, for any
\displaystyle|z|\geq 1
,
\displaystyle-\gamma_{+}z^{2}+\gamma_{-}z\leq 0

and condition (
10
) is satisfied.
This concludes the proof. ∎

3.2
P-divisibility in terms of relaxation rates

Let us now give a characterization of P-divisibility in terms of the rates
\displaystyle\gamma_{\pm},\Gamma
.

Theorem 3
.

The dynamical map is P-divisible at time
\displaystyle t
if and only if

\displaystyle\displaystyle|\gamma_{-}(t)|\leq\gamma_{+}(t)

and

\displaystyle\displaystyle\gamma_{-}(t)^{2}\leq 4\Gamma(t)(\gamma_{+}(t)-\Gamma(t))~\textrm{if}\ 2\Gamma(t)\leq\gamma_{+}(t).

For the proof see Appendix
A
. Some comments can be made about Theorem
3
. As expected, the conditions for CP-divisibility, i.e.
\displaystyle|\gamma_{-}|\leq\gamma_{+}
and
\displaystyle\gamma_{+}\leq 2\Gamma
are stronger than those for P-divisibility. These inequalities also imply that
\displaystyle\gamma_{+}\geq 0
and
\displaystyle\Gamma\geq 0
. Using
\displaystyle\gamma_{-}/\gamma_{+}=z_{\rm fp}
, the above conditions can be written as

\displaystyle\displaystyle|\gamma_{-}(t)|\leq\gamma_{+}(t)

and

\displaystyle\displaystyle 2\Gamma(t)\geq\gamma_{+}(t)(1-\sqrt{1-z_{\rm fp}^{2}})~\textrm{if}\ 2\Gamma(t)\leq\gamma_{+}(t),

i.e. they are very similar to the CP-divisible case (
2
) where
\displaystyle 2\Gamma(t)\geq\gamma_{+}(t)
. We observe that we obtain the same condition as CP-divisibility when
\displaystyle z_{\rm fp}^{2}=1
or
\displaystyle\gamma_{-}=\pm\gamma_{+}
, which corresponds to the situation where the instantaneous fixed point is located on the Bloch sphere.

3.3
Kossakowski’s Condition for P-Divisibility

Another condition is due to Kossakowski
[
39
,
19
]
and can be expressed as follows for a two-level system.

Theorem 4
.

The dynamical map
\displaystyle t\mapsto\Phi(t)
of a two-level quantum system is P-divisible
at time
\displaystyle t
if and only if

\displaystyle\displaystyle(\gamma_{+}(t)+\gamma_{-}(t))|\langle n|\sigma_{+}|m\rangle|^{2}+(\gamma_{+}(t)-\gamma_{-}(t))|\langle n|\sigma_{-}|m\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle+(\Gamma(t)-\frac{\gamma_{+}(t)}{2})|\langle n|\sigma_{z}|m\rangle|^{2}\geq 0,

(11)

for any orthonormal basis
\displaystyle(|m\rangle,|n\rangle)
of the Hilbert space
\displaystyle\mathcal{H}=\mathbb{C}^{2}
.

An explicit proof of this theorem for two-level quantum systems is given in Appendix
B
.

3.4
P-divisibility from local positivity

We show here that the conditions for P-divisibility can be derived from a local analysis of the positivity of the dynamical map. We recall the following theorem established in
[
38
]
.

Theorem 5
.

The dynamical map
\displaystyle\Phi
is P if and only if

\displaystyle\displaystyle\tilde{\gamma}_{+}(t)\geq 0,\tilde{\Gamma}(t)\geq 0,~\forall t\geq 0,

and

\displaystyle\displaystyle s^{2}\leq(1-e^{-\tilde{\gamma}_{+}})^{2}~\textrm{if}\ \tilde{\gamma}_{+}\leq 2\tilde{\Gamma},

and

\displaystyle\displaystyle s^{2}\leq(1-e^{-2\tilde{\Gamma}})(1-e^{-2(\tilde{\gamma}_{+}-\tilde{\Gamma})})~\textrm{if}\ \tilde{\gamma}_{+}\geq 2\tilde{\Gamma}.

We apply this theorem for a small time
\displaystyle t
. At first order in
\displaystyle t
, we have
\displaystyle\tilde{\Gamma}\simeq\Gamma t
and
\displaystyle\tilde{\gamma}_{+}\simeq\gamma_{+}t
, where
\displaystyle\Gamma=\Gamma(0)
and
\displaystyle\gamma_{+}=\gamma_{+}(0)
to simplify the notations. It can also be shown that
\displaystyle s(t)\simeq\gamma_{-}t
. The first condition gives
\displaystyle\gamma_{-}^{2}t^{2}\leq\gamma_{+}^{2}t^{2}
, which leads to
\displaystyle|\gamma_{-}|\leq\gamma_{+}
. The second condition is always verified, while the third condition can be transformed into

\gamma_{-}^{2}t^{2}\leq 2\Gamma t(2(\gamma_{+}-\Gamma)t)

i.e.

\gamma_{-}^{2}\leq 4\Gamma(\gamma_{+}-\Gamma)

when
\displaystyle 2\Gamma\leq\gamma_{+}
. As might be expected, we find the criteria for P-divisibility from a local study of P, which is a global property in time of the dynamical map.

3.5
Summary

In summary, we found the following results.

Theorem 6

(Necessary and Sufficient Conditions for P-Divisibility)
.

Consider an open two-level quantum system.
The dynamics of this system is P-divisible at time
\displaystyle t\geq 0
if and only if one of the following equivalent conditions is satisfied at time
\displaystyle t
.

1.

(Trace-Norm Condition) For all Hermitian non-zero operators
\displaystyle q\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
,

\frac{d}{dt}{||\Phi(t)q||_{1}}\leq 0,

where
\displaystyle||A||_{1}=\rm{Tr}(\sqrt{AA^{\dagger}}]
denotes the trace-norm.

2.

(Radius Rate Condition)

r(t)=1\Rightarrow\frac{dr}{dt}(t)\leq 0

3.

(Kossakowski Condition) For all orthonormal basis

Another interesting byproduct of this study is the connection between P-divisibility and the BLP criterion
[

which states that there is no back-flow of information from the bath to the system if

Applications

4.1
Standard examples

We first consider two examples used in the literature to study non-Markovian behaviors.

The first example corresponds to a qubit interacting with multiple decoherence channels
[
19
,
40
,
25
]
. The Master equation can be written as

\dot{\rho}=\sum_{i}\frac{\gamma_{i}}{2}(\sigma_{i}\rho\sigma_{i}-\rho)

where
\displaystyle i=x,y,z
and the
\displaystyle\sigma_{i}
are the Pauli matrices. The dynamics can be written in terms of the Bloch coordinates as follows

\displaystyle\displaystyle\dot{x}=-(\gamma_{y}+\gamma_{z})x

\displaystyle\displaystyle\dot{y}=-(\gamma_{x}+\gamma_{z})y

\displaystyle\displaystyle\dot{z}=-(\gamma_{x}+\gamma_{y})z.

We consider the case of eternal non-Markovianity for which
\displaystyle\gamma_{x}=\gamma_{y}=1
and
\displaystyle\gamma_{z}=-\tanh(t)

[
24
,
41
]
. We deduce that
\displaystyle\gamma_{+}=2
,
\displaystyle\gamma_{-}=0
and
\displaystyle\Gamma=1-\tanh(t)
. Since
\displaystyle 2\Gamma<\gamma_{+}
for
\displaystyle t>0
, the map is never CP-divisible. Using
\displaystyle z_{\rm{fp}}=0
, the condition of P-divisibility is
\displaystyle 2\Gamma\geq 0
and the map is always P-divisible. It also verifies the BLP criterion.

The second example is a qubit coupled to a lossy cavity
[
19
,
42
]
. In this case, the dynamics of the Bloch vector are governed by the following equations

\displaystyle\displaystyle\dot{x}=-\frac{\gamma}{2}x+\frac{S}{2}y,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\dot{y}=-\frac{\gamma}{2}y-\frac{S}{2}y,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\dot{z}=\gamma-\gamma z.

We have
\displaystyle\gamma_{-}=\gamma_{+}=\gamma
and
\displaystyle\Gamma=\frac{\gamma}{2}
. We deduce that
\displaystyle z_{\rm{fp}}=1
. The condition of CP-divisibility and P-divisibility is the same,
\displaystyle 2\Gamma\geq\gamma_{+}
and is verified at any time
\displaystyle t
.

4.2
A qubit interacting with a bosonic mode

In this section, we present an application of the above criteria to a qubit interacting with a single bosonic mode, a rather unusual situation in the open quantum systems community. Traditionally, one uses an explicit microscopic model of a qubit interacting with a bath, and from it one can derive a large variety of master equations depending on the properties of the bath (essentially spectral density and coupling strength with the qubit) and on the approximations used during the derivation of the master equation
[
1
]
. Here we study a very unusual regime in which the ”bath” is reduced to a single bosonic mode. Although a single bosonic mode is definitely not a bath as defined in the standard theory of open quantum system
[
1
]
, the dynamics induced by the interaction with the bosonic mode is still an open dynamics from the qubit point of view, and moreover can be described by an exact master equation of the same form as (
1
). Thus, even in this unusual configuration, it is legitimate to analyze the properties of the resulting map in terms of CP-divisibility, P-divisibility, and information backflow as defined by the BLP criterion.

The model system can be defined as follows. We consider a two-level system
\displaystyle A
interacting with a harmonic oscillator
\displaystyle B
via the Jaynes-Cumming coupling described by the density operator
\displaystyle\rho_{AB}
. The total Hamiltonian can be written as

H_{AB}=\omega_{A}\sigma_{+}\sigma_{-}+g(\sigma_{+}a+\sigma_{-}a)+\omega_{B}a^{\dagger}a.

We assume that at time
\displaystyle t=0
there is no correlation between
\displaystyle A
and
\displaystyle B
and that the initial state of
\displaystyle B
is in a thermal state at temperature
\displaystyle T_{B}
, namely
\displaystyle\rho_{B}(0)=Z^{-1}e^{-\beta_{B}H_{B}}
with
\displaystyle\beta_{B}=\frac{1}{k_{B}T_{B}}
,
\displaystyle H_{B}=\omega_{B}a^{\dagger}a
and
\displaystyle Z={\rm Tr}[e^{-\beta_{B}H_{B}}]=(1-e^{-\beta_{B}\omega_{B}})^{-1}
. The reduced dynamics of
\displaystyle A
given by

\dot{\rho}_{A}={\rm Tr}_{B}(\dot{\rho}_{AB})={\rm Tr}_{B}(-i[H_{AB},\rho_{AB}]),

can be written as an exact master equation
[
43
]
of the form (
1

\omega(t)=-\Im[\frac{\dot{\gamma}(t)}{\gamma(t)}],

5
Conclusion

In this paper, we provide different characterizations of P-divisibility for two-level quantum systems and show their equivalence. Interestingly, one of the signatures is given in terms of inequalities on the relaxation rates and can be easily verified when the dynamics is governed by a time-local master equation. The connection with other dynamical properties such as CP-divisibility and the BLP criterion is also discussed. A hierarchy in the P-divisibility at a given time
\displaystyle t
is related to the position of the instantaneous fixed point of the dynamics (see Fig.
1
). If the latter is on the Bloch sphere, then P-divisibility is equivalent to CP-divisibility, while for a fixed point at the center of the Bloch sphere, i.e. for unital dynamics, P-divisibility corresponds to the BLP criterion. In all other cases, the different concepts are not equivalent. Such correspondences are not fixed in time and evolve with the relaxation rates of the dynamical map.

This study paves the way to other interesting problems. A natural question is to generalize the relaxation rate criterion established in this paper to more complicated systems with three or more levels
[
16
,
46
]
. In this case it would also be valuable to characterize the different notions of
\displaystyle n
-divisible maps introduced in
[
25
]
. Another related question is based on the relative entropy between arbitrary pair of states, which is decreasing in time if the map is P-divisible
[
47
]
. The violation of this condition indicates that the map is not P-divisible. In this direction, one can wonder if another concept of relative entropy could be defined with properties that are satisfied for any P-divisible map. A final question concerns the optimal control of open quantum systems
[
8
,
48
]
and its connection with the CP- of P-divisibility of the dynamical map. For example, one can ask whether such concepts play a role in the set of states that can be reached by the system and in the structure of the associated control protocol
[
49
,
50
,
51
,
52
,
53
,
54
]
.

Finally, the application of such criteria to ”extremely small bath” situations reveals some unexpected features, and raises the question of a deeper physical understanding of such central concepts as CP-divisibility and P-divisibility.

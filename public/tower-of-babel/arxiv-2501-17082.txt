Abstract
. We derive equivariant localization formulas of Atiyah–Bott and cohomological field theory types in the Batalin-Vilkovisky formalism and discuss their applications in Poisson geometry and quantum field theory.



1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a compact Lie group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
.
Let
M
be a manifold endowed with a left
G
-action.
Let
\Omega_{G}(M)=(\Omega(M)\otimes\mathrm{Sym}(\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}))^{G}
denote the graded commutative algebra of
G
-equivariant differential forms on
M
, i.e., differential forms
\alpha
on
M
with values in the ring of polynomials over
\mathfrak{g}
, such that

\alpha(\mathrm{Ad}_{g}\xi)=L_{g^{-1}}^{*}\alpha(\xi),\quad\forall g\in G,~\forall\xi\in\mathfrak{g},

where
\mathrm{Ad}
is the adjoint action of
G
on
\mathfrak{g}
,
L_{g}\colon M\rightarrow M
is the left multiplication by
g\in G
. The grading on
\Omega_{G}(M)
is given by

\Omega_{G}^{p}(M)=\bigoplus_{r+2q=p}(\Omega^{r}(M)\otimes\mathrm{Sym}^{q}(\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}))^{G}.

The equivariant differential
\mathrm{d}_{\mathfrak{g}}\colon\Omega_{G}^{p}(M)\rightarrow\Omega_{G}^{p+1}(M)
is defined as

\mathrm{d}_{\mathfrak{g}}\alpha(\xi)=(\mathrm{d}-\iota_{X_{\xi}})\alpha(\xi),

where
X_{\xi}(p)=\frac{d}{dt}(p\cdot\exp(t\xi))|_{t=0}
is the fundamental vector field generated by
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}
. Fixing a basis
\{\xi_{a}\}
of
\mathfrak{g}
, the differential
\mathrm{d}_{\mathfrak{g}}
can be written as

\mathrm{d}_{\mathfrak{g}}=\mathrm{d}-\phi^{a}\iota_{X_{a}},

where
X_{a}=X_{\xi_{a}}
and
\{\phi^{a}\}
is the dual basis of
\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}
.

If
M
is compact and oriented, we can intergrate equivariant differential forms on
M
to obtain a map:

\int_{M}\colon\Omega_{G}(M)\rightarrow\mathrm{Sym}(\mathfrak{g}^{\vee})^{G}.

Stokes’ theorem can be easily generalized to this equivariant setting: Let
\alpha\in\Omega_{G}(M)
. If
\mathrm{d}_{\mathfrak{g}}\alpha=0
, then the integral

\int_{M}\alpha

depends only on
[\alpha]\in H_{G}(M)
, where
H_{G}(M)
is the cohomology group of
(\Omega_{G}(M),\mathrm{d}_{\mathfrak{g}})
.

The Equivariant Localization Principle is the following simple observation:

Observation 1.1
.

Let
\gamma
be a
G
-equivariant
1
-form on
M
. Let
\alpha\in\Omega_{G}(M)
. If
\mathrm{d}_{\mathfrak{g}}\alpha=0
, then the integral

(1.1)

Z_{\gamma}[t](\alpha)\coloneqq\int_{M}\alpha e^{\mathrm{i}t\mathrm{d}_{\mathfrak{g}}\gamma}=\int_{M}\alpha e^{\mathrm{i}t\left(\mathrm{d}\gamma-\phi^{a}\gamma(X_{a})\right)}

is independent of
t\in\mathbb{R}
. In particular,
Z_{\gamma}[t](\alpha)=Z_{\gamma}[0](\alpha)=\int_{M}\alpha
.

Remark 1.1
.

Note that
\alpha e^{\mathrm{i}t\mathrm{d}_{\mathfrak{g}}\gamma}=e^{\mathrm{i}t[\mathrm{d}_{\mathfrak{g}},\gamma\wedge]}\alpha
.

The basic idea of equivariant localization is, instead of computing the integral
\int_{M}\alpha
directly, one should compute the superficially more complicated integral
Z_{\gamma}[t](\alpha)
in the limit
t\to\infty
, which will “localize” to a integral over the zero locus of
\gamma(\eta)(X_{\eta})
,
\eta\in\mathfrak{g}
.
Different choices of
\gamma
then lead to different localization formulas. Let us consider the following two strategies for selecting
\gamma
.

Remark 1.2
.

For simplicity, we will take
G
to be
\mathrm{U}(1)
.
\Omega_{\mathrm{U}(1)}(M)
consists of
U(1)
-invariant differential forms on
M
with values in
\mathbb{R}[\phi]
, where
\phi\in\mathfrak{u}(1)^{\vee}
is the dual of
\mathrm{i}\in\mathfrak{u}(1)\cong\mathrm{i}\mathbb{R}
. The corresponding equivariant differential can be expressed as

\mathrm{d}_{\mathfrak{u}(1)}=\mathrm{d}-\phi\iota_{X},

where
X(p)\coloneqq\frac{d}{dt}(p\cdot\exp(\mathrm{i}t))|_{t=0}
.

•

Equipping
M
with a
\mathrm{U}(1)
-invariant Riemannian metric
g
, we choose
\gamma
to be

(1.2)

\gamma=X^{\flat}\coloneqq g(X,\cdot).

It follows that

\mathrm{d}_{\mathfrak{u}(1)}\gamma=g(\nabla X,\cdot)+g(X,\nabla(\cdot))-\phi g(X,X),

where
\nabla
is the Levi-Civita connection of
g
. The zero locus
M_{X}
of
\gamma(X)=g(X,X)
consists of the fixed points of the
\mathrm{U}(1)
-action.
Computing
Z_{\gamma}[t](\alpha)
in the limit
t\to\infty
yields the Berline–Vergne localization theorem
[
4
]
when
M_{X}
is discrete. More generally, it leads to the Atiyah–Bott localization theorem
[
2
]
when the connected components of
M_{X}
have positive dimensions. For more details, we refer the reader to
[
1
]
and
[
3
, Chapter 7]
.

•

Let
W
be a vector space endowed with a left linear action
\rho
of
\mathrm{U}(1)
and a
\mathrm{U}(1)
-invariant inner product
h(\cdot,\cdot)
. Let
\mathcal{F}\colon M\rightarrow W
be a
\mathrm{U}(1)
-equivariant smooth map. We choose
\gamma
to be

(1.3)

\gamma=h(\dot{\rho}(\mathrm{i})\mathcal{F},\mathrm{d}\mathcal{F}),

where
\dot{\rho}
is the induced
\mathfrak{u}(1)
-action on
W
. It follows that

\mathrm{d}_{\mathfrak{u}(1)}\gamma=h(\dot{\rho}(\mathrm{i})\mathrm{d}\mathcal{F},\mathrm{d}\mathcal{F})-\phi h(\dot{\rho}(\mathrm{i})\mathcal{F},\dot{\rho}(\mathrm{i})\mathcal{F}),

because

(1.4)

\displaystyle\iota_{X}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{F}=\mathrm{Lie}_{X}\mathcal{F}=\dot{\rho}(\mathrm{i})\mathcal{F}.

If
\mathrm{U}(1)
acts freely on
W-\{0\}
, then the zero locus
M_{\mathcal{F}}
of
\gamma(X)=h(\dot{\rho}(\mathrm{i})\mathcal{F},\dot{\rho}(\mathrm{i})\mathcal{F})
is exactly the zero locus of
\mathcal{F}
. (
1.4
) also implies that

M_{X}\subset M_{\mathcal{F}}.

If
M_{\mathcal{F}}
is discrete, we also have

M_{\mathcal{F}}\subset M_{X},

since
gM_{\mathcal{F}}=M_{\mathcal{F}}
for all
g\in\mathrm{U}(1)
.

In particular, let us consider
W=\mathbb{C}^{k}
, equipped with the standard inner product and the following
\mathrm{U}(1)
-action:

e^{\mathrm{i}\theta}(f_{1},\dots,f_{k})=(e^{\mathrm{i}w_{1}\theta}f_{1},\dots e^{\mathrm{i}w_{k}\theta}f_{k}),\quad e^{\mathrm{i}\theta}\in\mathrm{U}(1),~(f_{1},\dots,f_{k})\in\mathbb{C}^{k},

where
w_{1},\dots,w_{k}
are non-zero integers. Denote
\mathcal{F}=(\mathcal{F}^{1},\cdots,\mathcal{F}^{k})
, where
\mathcal{F}^{1},\dots,\mathcal{F}^{k}
are complex functions on
M
. If
\mathcal{F}
has isolated zeros, then
M_{\mathcal{F}}=M_{X}
and
\dim M
is even. Computing
Z_{\gamma}[t](\alpha)
in the limit
t\to\infty
leads to the following theorem:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\alpha\in\Omega_{\mathrm{U}(1)}(M)
. If
d_{\mathfrak{u}(1)}\alpha=0
, then

(1.5)

\int_{M}\alpha=(-2\pi)^{\dim(M)/2}\sum_{p\in M_{X}}\frac{\mathrm{pf}\,\mathrm{Im}(\sum_{l=1}^{k}w_{l}\partial_{i}\overline{\mathcal{F}^{l}}\partial_{j}\mathcal{F}^{l})(p)}{\sqrt{\det\mathrm{Re}(\sum_{l=1}^{k}w_{l}^{2}\partial_{i}\overline{\mathcal{F}^{l}}\partial_{j}\mathcal{F}^{l})(p)}}\alpha_{[0]}(p),

where
\partial_{i}\mathcal{F}
are the partial derivatives
\mathcal{F}
computed in some local coordinates of
M
,
\alpha_{[0]}
is the
0
-form component of
\alpha
.

Remark 1.3
.

We refer to the localization induced by (
1.2
) as the Atiyah–Bott type localization and refer to the localization induced by (
1.3
) as the cohomological field theory (CohFT) type localization. The terminology is due to the fact that, for CohFT type localization,
Z_{\gamma}[t]
can be viewed as the partition function of a zero dimensional CohFT.

In this work, we prove the Berline–Vergne localization theorem and (
1.5
) using the standard stationary phase approximation for Morse functions, applied in the setting of equivariant multivector fields rather than equivariant differential forms. We then extend the equivariant localization principle (
1.1
) to the Batalin-Vilkovisky formalism and apply the stationary phase approximation for Morse-Bott functions to establish a BV analog of the Atiyah–Bott localization theorem and to generalize (
1.5
) (cf. Theorems
3.1
and
3.2
). In the final section, we discuss applications of our localization theorems to Poisson geometry and quantum field theory. Specifically, we derive a Poisson-geometric counterpart of the Duistermaat-Heckman formula
[
12
]
for unimodular Poisson manifolds.

2.
Equivariant localization principle in BV formalism

Let
M
be an orientable manifold. Let
\mathcal{V}(M)=\Gamma(\Lambda TM)
denote the space of multivector fields over
M
.
\mathcal{V}(M)
is a graded commutative algebra over
\mathbb{R}
. The grading of
\mathcal{V}(M)
is given by

\mathcal{V}^{p}(M)\coloneqq\Gamma(\Lambda^{-p}TM)

for
-\dim M\leq p\leq 0
and
\mathcal{V}^{p}(M)=0
for all other values of
p
. The graded commutative product on
\mathcal{V}(M)
is the wedge product
\wedge
between multivector fields.
There exists a natural graded Lie superbracket of degree
1
on
\mathcal{V}(M)
, called the Schouten–Nijenhuis bracket, which is determined by the following properties:

(1)

\{f,g\}=0
for all
f,g\in\mathcal{V}^{0}(M)=C^{\infty}(M)
;

(2)

\{X,\cdot\}=\mathrm{Lie}_{X}
for all
X\in\mathcal{V}^{-1}(M)=\mathfrak{X}(M)
;

(3)

\{P_{1},P_{2}\wedge P_{3}\}=\{P_{1},P_{2}\}\wedge P_{3}+(-1)^{|P_{2}|(|P_{1}|-1)}P_{2}\wedge\{P_{1},P_{3}\}
for all
P_{1},P_{2},P_{3}\in\mathcal{V}(M)
.

\mathcal{V}(M)
equipped with the Schouten-Nijenhuis bracket is a Gerstenhaber algebra.

Let
\Omega(M)=\Gamma(\Lambda T^{*}M)
denote the space of differential forms on
M
. There exists a canonical paring
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\colon\Omega(M)\times\mathcal{V}(M)\rightarrow C^{\infty}(M)
, given by

\langle\alpha,P\rangle=\alpha(x)(P(x)),\quad\alpha\in\Omega(M),~P\in\mathcal{V}(M).

The right contraction of a
p
-differential form
\alpha
by a
q
-multivector field
P
is a
(p-q)
-differential form
\alpha\lrcorner P
is defined by the relations

\langle\alpha\lrcorner P,Q\rangle=\langle\alpha,P\wedge Q\rangle,\quad\forall Q\in\mathcal{V}^{p-q}(M).

For
X\in\mathcal{V}^{-1}(M)
,
\lrcorner X
is the usual right contraction by the vector field
X
. In general,
\lrcorner P
is not a superderivation, and we have

\lrcorner(P\wedge Q)=\lrcorner Q\lrcorner P.

The left contraction by
P
, denoted as
P\llcorner
(or
\iota_{P}
), is defined in a similar manner. Likewise, we also have

(P\wedge Q)\llcorner=P\llcorner Q\llcorner.

\mathrm{vol}\in\Omega^{top}(M)
be a volume form on
M

1

From a graded geometric point of view,
\mathcal{V}(M)

\mathcal{M}
such that locally,

\mu=d^{n}xd^{n}\xi.

(We allow the coordinate functions
x^{i}
to have nonzero degree
d(x^{i})
. The corresponding degree of the anti-coordinates
\xi_{i}
are
-1-d(x^{i})
.) The BV Laplacian
\Delta
is defined locally by the following formula
[
18
]
:

\Delta=\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi_{i}}.

Alternatively,
\Delta
can be defined as

\Delta(f)\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{div}_{\mu}(X_{f}),\quad f\in C^{\infty}(M),

where
X_{f}
is the Hamiltonian vector field of
f
, defined via

\iota_{X_{f}}\omega=\mathrm{d}f,

or equivalently,
X_{f}=(-1)^{\deg f}\{f,\cdot\}
,
\mathrm{div}_{\mu}(X)
is the divergence of a vector field
X
over
M
, defined via

\int_{\mathcal{M}}\mu X(f)=-\int_{\mathcal{M}}\mu\mathrm{div}_{\mu}(X)f.

C^{\infty}(\mathcal{M})
endowed with
\{\cdot,\cdot\}
and
\Delta
is a BV algebra.

Let us reformulate the equivariant localization principle within the BV formalism. This will be done in two steps: first for
(T^{*}[-1]M,\omega_{st})
, and then for a general odd symplectic manifold
(\mathcal{M},\omega)
of degree
-1
.

Suppose that we have an action of a Lie group
G
on
M
. Let

\mathcal{V}_{G}(M)=(\mathcal{V}(M)\otimes\mathrm{Sym}(\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}))^{G}

denote the graded commutative algebra of equivariant multivector fields over
M
. We define the degree of a
q
-homogeneous polynomial valued
r
-multivector field to be
2q-r
. Let
\mathrm{vol}
be a
G
-invariant volume form on
M
. Under the identification

\mathcal{V}(M)\overset{\llcorner\mathrm{vol}}{\cong}\Omega(M),

the left contraction
X\llcorner
on
\Omega(M)
becomes a left multiplication

X\wedge\cdot

on
\mathcal{V}(M)
.
We define the equivariant differential
\Delta_{\mathfrak{g}}\colon\mathcal{V}^{p}_{G}(M)\rightarrow\mathcal{V}^{p+1}_{G}(M)
as

\Delta_{\mathfrak{g}}\coloneqq\Delta-\phi^{a}X_{a}\wedge\cdot.

Note that neither
\Delta
nor
X_{a}\wedge\cdot
are derivations of
\mathcal{V}(M)
. However, their commutator

[\Delta,X_{a}\wedge\cdot](P)=\Delta(X_{a}\wedge P)+X_{a}\wedge\Delta(P)=\mathrm{div}_{\mathrm{vol}}(X_{a})\wedge P-\{X_{a},P\}=-\mathrm{Lie}_{X}(P)

is a derivation since
\mathrm{div}_{\mathrm{vol}}(X_{a})=0
by our choice of
\mathrm{vol}
.
(Another way to see this is to observe that
\mathrm{d}_{\mathfrak{g}}(P\llcorner\mathrm{vol})=(\Delta_{\mathfrak{g}}P)\llcorner\mathrm{vol}
.)
We have the desired formula:

\Delta_{\mathfrak{g}}^{2}=\phi^{a}\mathrm{Lie}_{X_{a}}.

If
M
is compact, we can integrate equivariant multivector fields over
M
to obtain a map

\int_{M}\colon\mathcal{V}_{G}(M)\rightarrow\mathrm{Sym}(\mathfrak{g}^{\vee})^{G},

where
\int_{M}P
is understood as

\int_{M}P\coloneqq\int\mathrm{vol}\lrcorner P=\int_{M}\mathrm{vol}P_{[0]},

where the function
P_{[0]}
is the
0
-multivector field component of
P
. The divergence theorem can be easily generalized to this equivariant setting: Let
P\in\mathcal{V}_{G}(M)
. If
\Delta_{\mathfrak{g}}P=0
, then the integral

\int_{M}P

depends only on
[P]\in H_{G}(M)
, where
H_{G}(M)
is the cohomology group of
(\mathcal{V}_{G}(M),\Delta_{\mathfrak{g}})
.

The Equivariant Localization Principle
1.1
can be reformulated as follows:

Observation 2.1
.

Let
\gamma
be a
G
-equivariant
1
-form on
M
. Let
P\in\mathcal{V}_{G}(M)
. If
\Delta_{\mathfrak{g}}P=0
, then the integral

(2.1)

Z_{\gamma}[t](P)\coloneqq\int_{M}e^{\mathrm{i}t[\Delta_{\mathfrak{g}},\gamma\llcorner]}(P)=\int_{M}e^{-\mathrm{i}t(\phi^{a}\gamma(X_{a}))}e^{\mathrm{i}t[\Delta,\gamma\llcorner]}(P),

is independent of
t\in\mathbb{R}
, where
\gamma\llcorner
is the left contraction by
\gamma
. In particular,
Z_{\gamma}[t](P)=\int_{M}P
.

Let us now consider a general odd symplectic manifold
(\mathcal{M},\omega)
of degree
-1
endowed with a left Hamiltonian
G
-action. (Here
G
is just an ordinary Lie group.) Let
\mu
be a Berezinian on
\mathcal{M}
that is compatible with
\omega
and the
G
-action. Let

C^{\infty}_{G}(\mathcal{M})=(C^{\infty}(\mathcal{M})\otimes\mathrm{Sym}(\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}))^{G}.

As usual, we assign degree
2
to an element of
\mathrm{Sym}^{1}(\mathfrak{g}^{\vee})
. The degree of an element of
C^{\infty}_{G}(\mathcal{M})
is then defined as the corresponding total degree.
We define the equivariant differential
\Delta_{\mathfrak{g}}\colon C^{\infty}(\mathcal{M})^{p}_{G}(M)\rightarrow C^{\infty}(\mathcal{M})^{p+1}_{G}(M)
as

\Delta_{\mathfrak{g}}\coloneqq\Delta-\phi^{a}f_{X_{a}}\cdot,

where
f_{X_{a}}
is the Hamiltonian of the fundamental vector fields
X_{a}
. (Such
f_{X_{a}}
is unique because it is of odd degree.) One can easily check that

\Delta_{\mathfrak{g}}^{2}=\phi^{a}X_{a}.

If the body
\mathcal{M}_{red}
of
\mathcal{M}
is compact, we can perform the following BV integral:

\int_{\mathcal{L}\subset\mathcal{M}}\sqrt{\mu|_{\mathcal{L}}}f|_{\mathcal{L}},\quad f\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{M}),

where
\mathcal{L}

\mathcal{M}
,

is the density on
\mathcal{L}
induced by

. The BV integral can be extended to an integral of equivariant super functions on
\mathcal{M}
:

\int_{\mathcal{L}\subset\mathcal{M}}\sqrt{\mu|_{\mathcal{L}}}\colon C^{\infty}_{G}(\mathcal{M})\rightarrow\mathrm{Sym}(\mathfrak{g}^{\vee})^{G},

The BV Stokes’ theorem can also be easily generalized to the equivariant BV setting: Let
f\in C^{\infty}_{G}(\mathcal{M})
. Let
\mathcal{L}_{s}
be a smooth family of Lagrangian sub-supermanifold of
\mathcal{M}
,
s\in[0,1]
. Assume that
\mathcal{L}_{s}
is
G
-invariant, i.e., the restriction
f_{X_{\xi}}|_{\mathcal{L}_{s}}
vanishes for all
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}
. If

, then the equivariant BV integral

\int_{\mathcal{L}_{s}\subset\mathcal{M}}\sqrt{\mu|_{\mathcal{L}_{s}}}f|_{\mathcal{L}_{s}}

depends only on
[f]\in H_{\Delta_{\mathfrak{g}}}(\mathcal{M})
and does not depend on

.

The Equivariant Localization Principle
2.1
can be generalized as follows:

Observation 2.2
.

Let
Y\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{M})\otimes\mathrm{Sym}(\mathfrak{g}^{\vee})
be a
G
-equivariant odd vector field over
\mathcal{M}
, i.e.,
[X_{\xi},Y]=[\xi,Y]
for all
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}
. We have

[\Delta_{\mathfrak{g}},Y]=[\Delta,Y]-\phi^{a}Y(f_{X_{a}})=[\Delta,Y]-\phi^{a}\omega(X_{a},Y),

where we use

. Assume that

(1)

[\Delta,Y]
is nilpotent;

(2)

commutes with
\phi^{a}\omega(X_{a},Y)

Let

. If

, then the integral

(2.2)

Z_{Y,\mathcal{L}}[t](f):=\int_{\mathcal{L}\subset\mathcal{M}}\sqrt{\mu|_{\mathcal{L}}}\left(e^{\mathrm{i}t[\Delta_{\mathfrak{g}},Y]}(f)\right)|_{\mathcal{L}}=\int_{\mathcal{L}\subset\mathcal{M}}\sqrt{\mu|_{\mathcal{L}}}\left(e^{-\mathrm{i}t(\phi^{a}\omega(Y,X_{a}))}e^{\mathrm{i}t[\Delta,Y]}(f)\right)|_{\mathcal{L}},

is independent of

, where
\mathcal{L}
be a

-invariant Lagrangian sub-supermanifold of

. In particular,
Z_{Y,\mathcal{L}}[t](f)=\int_{\mathcal{L}\subset\mathcal{M}}\sqrt{\mu|_{\mathcal{L}}}f_{\mathcal{L}}
.

Combined with the stationary phase method for supermanifolds

can be used to derive localization formulas for equivariant BV integrals; however, this will not be discussed in the present work.

3.
Equivariant localization theorems

For simplicity, let us consider
G=\mathrm{U}(1)
.

Let
M
be an orientable compact manifold of dimension
n
. Let us equip
M
with a
\mathrm{U}(1)
-invariant Riemannian metric
g
and choose
\mathrm{vol}
to be the volume form of
g
. Let
S
be a Morse–Bott function on
M
, i.e., the critical locus
\mathrm{crit}\,S
of
S
is a closed submanifold of
M
and the Hessian
\mathrm{Hess}\,S
of
S
is non-degenerate along the normal bundle
N_{\mathrm{crit}\,S}
of
\mathrm{crit}\,S
. Recall the stationary phase approximation:

\int_{M}\mathrm{vol}fe^{\mathrm{i}tS}\sim_{t\to\infty}\left(\frac{2\pi}{t}\right)^{k/2}e^{\mathrm{i}\frac{\pi}{4}\mathrm{sgn}\,\mathrm{Hess}\,S|_{N_{\mathrm{crit}\,S}}}\left(\int_{\mathrm{crit}\,S}fe^{\mathrm{i}tS}\frac{\mathrm{vol}}{|\det\mathrm{Hess}\,S|_{N_{\mathrm{crit}\,S}}|^{1/2}}+O(t^{-1})\right),

where
k
is the codimension of
\mathrm{crit}\,S
,
\mathrm{sgn}\,\mathrm{Hess}\,S|_{N_{\mathrm{crit}\,S}}
is the signature of the Hessian matrix, and
\mathrm{vol}/|\det\mathrm{Hess}\,S|_{N_{\mathrm{crit}\,S}}|^{1/2}
defines a density over
\mathrm{crit}\,S
.

3.1.
Atiyah–Bott type localization

Let
\gamma=X^{\flat}\coloneqq g(X,\cdot)
be the metric dual of
X
. Taking the limit
t\to\infty
localizes the integral

Z_{\gamma}[t](P)=\int_{M}e^{-\mathrm{i}tg(X,X)}e^{\mathrm{i}t[\Delta,X^{\flat}\llcorner]}(P)

to the fixed point set
M_{X}
of the
\mathrm{U}(1)
-action.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
P\in\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{U}(1)}(M)
. If
\Delta_{\mathfrak{u}(1)}P=0
and
M_{X}
is of codimension
2m
, then

(3.1)

\int_{M}P=\frac{(-2\pi)^{m}}{m!}\int_{M_{X}}\langle(\nabla X^{\flat})^{m},P_{[2m]}\rangle\frac{\mathrm{vol}}{\sqrt{\det\mathrm{Hess}\,g(X,X)|_{N_{X}}}},

where
\nabla
is the Levi-Civita connection of
g
and
N_{X}
is the normal bundle of
M_{X}
. In particular, if
n=2m
, i.e., if
M_{X}
is discrete, then

(3.2)

\int_{M}P=(-2\pi)^{m}\sum_{p\in M_{X}}\frac{\langle\mathrm{vol},P_{[2m]}\rangle(p)}{\lambda_{1}(p)\cdots\lambda_{m}(p)},

where
\lambda_{1}(p),\dots,\lambda_{m}(p)
are the weights of the induced
\mathrm{U}(1)
-action on
T_{p}M
.

Remark 3.1
.

(
3.2
) is essentially the Berline–Vergne localization formula.

Proof.

Since
[\Delta_{\mathfrak{u}(1)},\iota_{X}]
is invertible outside of
M_{X}
, the integrand
e^{-\mathrm{i}tg(X,X)}e^{\mathrm{i}t[\Delta,X^{\flat}\llcorner]}(P)
is
\Delta_{\mathfrak{u}(1)}
-exact outside of
M_{X}
. Applying the stationary phase approximation, we obtain

\displaystyle Z_{\gamma}[t](P)\sim_{t\to\infty}\left(\frac{2\pi}{t}\right)^{m}e^{\mathrm{i}m\pi/2}\int_{M_{X}}f\frac{\mathrm{vol}}{\sqrt{\det\mathrm{Hess}\,g(X,X)|_{N_{X}}}}+O(t^{-1}),

where

f=\frac{(\mathrm{i}t)^{m}}{m!}[\Delta,X^{\flat}\llcorner]^{m}(P_{[2m]}).

Since
Z_{\gamma}[t](P)
is independent of
t
, we have

Z_{\gamma}[t](P)=\frac{(-2\pi)^{m}}{m!}\int_{M_{X}}[\Delta,X^{\flat}\llcorner]^{m}(P_{[2m]})\frac{\mathrm{vol}}{\sqrt{\det\mathrm{Hess}\,g(X,X)|_{N_{X}}}}.

Let us work in normal coordinates
x^{1},\ldots,x^{n}
around a fixed point
p
of the
\mathrm{U}(1)
-action. In such coordinates, the BV Laplacian
\Delta
has the following expression:

\Delta=dx^{\mu}\llcorner\nabla_{\partial_{\mu}}.

It follows that

[\Delta,X^{\flat}\llcorner]=dx^{\mu}\llcorner\nabla_{\partial_{\mu}}(X^{\flat}\llcorner)+X^{\flat}\llcorner dx^{\mu}\llcorner\nabla_{\partial_{\mu}}=\nabla_{\partial_{\mu}}X^{\flat}_{\nu}dx^{\mu}\llcorner dx^{\nu}\llcorner=\nabla X^{\flat}\llcorner,

thus proving the first part of the theorem.
If
M_{X}
is discrete, then
X
can be linearized around
p\in M_{X}
:

X=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}(p)(x^{2i}\partial_{2i-1}-x^{2i-1}\partial_{2i}),

where
\lambda_{1}(p),\dots,\lambda_{m}(p)
are non-zero integers. The Hessian of
g(X,X)
at
p
is the following diagonal matrix:

\mathrm{Hess}\,g(X,X)(p)=2\mathrm{diag}(\lambda_{1}^{2},\lambda_{1}^{2},\cdots,\lambda_{m}^{2},\lambda_{m}^{2}).

Therefore,
\sqrt{\det\mathrm{Hess}\,g(X,X)(p)}=2^{m}\lambda_{1}^{2}\cdots\lambda_{m}^{2}
. We also have

\displaystyle\nabla X^{\flat}(p)=2\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}(p)dx^{2i}\wedge dx^{2i-1}.

It follows that

(\nabla X^{\flat})^{m}(p)=2^{m}m!\lambda_{1}(p)\cdots\lambda_{m}(p)dx^{1}\wedge\cdots dx^{2m},

thus proving the second part of the theorem.
∎

Recall the Atiyah–Bott localization theorem:

\int_{M}\alpha=\int_{M_{X}}\frac{\alpha}{e(N_{X})},

where
\alpha
is an equivariantly closed form on
M
, and
e(N_{X})
is a representative of the equivariant Euler class of
N_{X}
. Letting
\alpha=\mathrm{vol}\lrcorner P
, we obtain

(3.3)

\int_{M}P=\int_{M_{X}}\frac{\mathrm{vol}\lrcorner P}{e(N_{X})}.

(
3.3
) is essentially different from our localization formula (
3.1
) when
\dim M_{X}>0
. This is because the right-hand side of (
3.3
) involves all
P_{[2k]}
with
2k-\mathrm{codim}M_{X}\geq 0
, whereas the right-hand side of (
3.1
) only involves
P_{[\mathrm{codim}M_{X}]}
.

3.2.
CohFT type localization

Let
\mathbb{C}^{k}
be equipped with the standard inner product and a diagonal
\mathrm{U}(1)
-action
\rho
with non-zero weights
w_{1},\dots,w_{k}
. Let
\mathcal{F}=(\mathcal{F}^{1},\dots,\mathcal{F}^{k})
be a
\mathrm{U}(1)
-equivariant map from
M
to
\mathbb{C}^{k}
. Let
\gamma=\mathrm{Re}(\overline{\dot{\rho}(\mathrm{i})\mathcal{F}}d\mathcal{F})
. Taking the limit
t\to\infty
localizes the integral

(3.4)

\displaystyle Z_{\gamma}[t](P)=\int_{M}\mathrm{vol}e^{-\mathrm{i}t|\dot{\rho}(\mathrm{i})\mathcal{F}|^{2}}e^{\mathrm{i}t[\Delta,\iota_{\gamma}]}(P)

to the zero locus
M_{\mathcal{F}}
of
\mathcal{F}
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
P\in\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{U}(1)}(M)
. If
\Delta_{\mathfrak{u}(1)}P=0
and
M_{\mathcal{F}}
has codimension
2m
, then

(3.5)

\int_{M}P=\frac{(-2\pi)^{m}}{m!}\int_{M_{\mathcal{F}}}\langle(\sum_{l=1}^{k}w_{l}\mathrm{Im}(d\overline{\mathcal{F}^{l}}\wedge\mathrm{d}\mathcal{F}^{l}))^{m},P_{[2m]}\rangle\frac{\mathrm{vol}}{\sqrt{\det\mathrm{Hess}\,(\sum_{l=1}^{k}w_{l}^{2}|\mathcal{F}^{l}|^{2})|_{N_{\mathcal{F}}}}}

If
M_{\mathcal{F}}
is discrete, then
M_{X}=M_{\mathcal{F}}
and
n=2m
, we have

(3.6)

\int_{M}P=(-2\pi)^{m}\sum_{p\in M_{X}}\frac{\mathrm{pf}\,\mathrm{Im}(\sum_{l=1}^{k}w_{l}\partial_{i}\overline{\mathcal{F}^{l}}\partial_{j}\mathcal{F}^{l})(p)}{\sqrt{\det\mathrm{Re}(\sum_{l=1}^{k}w_{l}^{2}\partial_{i}\overline{\mathcal{F}^{l}}\partial_{j}\mathcal{F}^{l})(p)}}\langle\mathrm{vol},P_{[2m]}\rangle(p).

Proof.

Applying the stationary phase approximation, we obtain

\displaystyle Z_{\gamma}[t](P)=\frac{(-2\pi)^{m}}{m!}\int_{M_{X}}\langle(\nabla\gamma)^{m},P_{[2m]}\rangle\frac{\mathrm{vol}}{\sqrt{\det\mathrm{Hess}\,|\dot{\rho}(\mathrm{i})\mathcal{F}|^{2}|_{N_{X}}}},

where
\nabla
is the Levi-Civita connection of
g
. A direct computation shows that

\gamma=\sum_{l=1}^{k}w_{l}(\mathcal{F}^{l}_{1}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{F}^{l}_{2}-\mathcal{F}^{l}_{2}d\mathcal{F}^{l}_{1}),

where
\mathcal{F}^{l}=\mathcal{F}^{l}_{1}+\mathrm{i}\mathcal{F}^{l}_{2}
,
\mathcal{F}^{l}_{1}
and
\mathcal{F}^{l}_{2}
are real functions on
M
. It follows that

\nabla\gamma(p)=2\sum_{l=1}^{k}w_{l}(\mathrm{d}\mathcal{F}^{l}_{1}\wedge\mathrm{d}\mathcal{F}^{l}_{2})(p)=\sum_{l=1}^{k}w_{l}\mathrm{Im}(\mathrm{d}\overline{\mathcal{F}^{l}}\wedge\mathrm{d}\mathcal{F}^{l})(p),

since
\mathcal{F}(p)=0
and
\nabla\mathcal{F}=\mathrm{d}\mathcal{F}
. The proof for the first part of the theorem is completed by noting that
|\dot{\rho}(\mathrm{i})\mathcal{F}|^{2}=\sum_{l=1}^{k}w_{l}^{2}|\mathcal{F}^{l}|^{2}
.
In normal coordinates
x^{1},\dots,x^{2m}
around
p\in M_{\mathcal{F}}
, we have
\nabla\mathrm{d}|\mathcal{F}^{l}|^{2}(p)=2\mathrm{Re}(\partial_{i}\overline{\mathcal{F}^{l}}\partial_{j}\mathcal{F}^{l})(p)dx^{i}\otimes dx^{j}
, and

[\Delta,\gamma\llcorner]=2\sum_{l=1}^{k}w_{l}\mathrm{Im}(\partial_{i}\overline{\mathcal{F}}^{l}\partial_{j}\mathcal{F}^{l})\iota_{dx^{i}}\iota_{dx^{j}}.

It follows that

[\Delta,\gamma\llcorner]^{m}=2^{m}m!\mathrm{pf}\,\mathrm{Im}(\partial_{i}\overline{\mathcal{F}}\partial_{j}\mathcal{F}),

which completes the proof for the second part of the theorem.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

If
M_{\mathcal{F}}
is discrete, then both
X
and
\mathcal{F}
can be linearized in the normal coordinates
x^{1},\cdots,x^{2m}
around any point
p\in M_{\mathcal{F}}
. We can write

X(p)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lambda_{j}(p)(x^{2j}\partial_{2j-1}-x^{2j-1}\partial_{2j}),\quad\mathcal{F}^{l}(p)=\sum_{j=1}^{2m}c^{l}_{j}(p)x^{j},

where
c^{l}_{j}(p)
are complex numbers. Since
\mathcal{F}
is
\mathrm{U}(1)
-equivariant, we must have
X(\mathcal{F})(p)=w_{l}\mathrm{i}\mathcal{F}
, which implies that

\displaystyle\begin{cases}c_{2j-1}^{l}=\pm\mathrm{i}c^{l}_{2j},&\text{if}~\lambda_{j}=\pm w_{l};\\
c_{2j-1}^{l}=c^{l}_{2j}=0,&\text{if}~\lambda_{j}\neq\pm w_{l}.\end{cases}

Let
\Lambda_{X}\coloneqq\cup_{p\in M_{X}}\{\lambda_{1}(p),\dots,\lambda_{m}(p)\}
. Since
\mathrm{Hess}\,|\dot{\rho}(\mathrm{i})\mathcal{F}|^{2}
is non-degenerate at each
p\in M_{X}
, we conclude that

\Lambda_{X}\subset\{w_{1},\dots,w_{k}\}.

In particular, we must have
k\geq|\Lambda_{X}|
.

4.
Applications and discussions

To apply Theorems
3.1
and
3.2
, we need to find a way to extend a
\Delta
-closed multivector field to a equivariantly closed multivector field. Let us consider a function of the form
e^{S}\in C^{\infty}(M)
and assume that we can find a bivector field
I_{X}\in\mathcal{V}^{-2}(M)
such that

\Delta_{\mathfrak{u}(1)}e^{S_{eq}}=0,\quad S_{eq}\coloneqq S+\phi I_{X}.

Such
I_{X}
exists if and only if

\Delta(S_{eq})+\frac{1}{2}\{S_{eq},S_{eq}\}-\phi X=0.

This equation can be broken into three independent equations:

(4.1)

\displaystyle\Delta(S)+\frac{1}{2}\{S,S\}=0,

(4.2)

\displaystyle\Delta(I_{X})+\{S,I_{X}\}=X,

(4.3)

\displaystyle\{I_{X},I_{X}\}=0.

4.1.
Poisson geometry

For the BV algebra
\mathcal{V}(M)
, the quantum master equation (
4.1
) is satisfied since the restrictions of
\Delta
and
\{\cdot,\cdot\}
to
C^{\infty}(M)
vanish. Equation (
4.3
) tells us that
I_{X}
defines a Poisson structure
\pi
on
M
. Equation (
4.2
) is the most interesting one. In particular, it implies that

\mathrm{Lie}_{X}(\pi)=\{X,\pi\}=\{\Delta(\pi),\pi\}+\{\{S,\pi\},\pi\}=[\Delta,\{\pi,\pi\}]+\frac{1}{2}\{S,\{\pi,\pi\}\}=0.

Therefore, the
\mathrm{U}(1)
-action on
(M,\pi)
is Poisson.
It follows from Theorem
3.1
that

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
(M,\pi)
be a
2m
-dimensional orientable Poisson compact manifold endowed with a Poisson
\mathrm{U(1)}
-action. Let
\mathrm{vol}
be a
\mathrm{U}(1)
-invariant volume form on
M
. Supposing that

(4.4)

\displaystyle[X]=[X_{\mathrm{vol}}]\in H^{1}_{\pi}(M),

where
H^{\bullet}_{\pi}(M)
are the Poisson cohomology groups of
M
,
X
is the fundamental vector field of the
\mathrm{U}(1)
-action, and
X_{\mathrm{vol}}=\Delta(\pi)
is the divergence of
\pi
with respect to
\mathrm{vol}
, then we can find a function
h
on
M
satisfying

\displaystyle X=X_{\mathrm{vol}}+X_{h},

where
X_{h}=\{h,\pi\}
is the Hamiltonian vector field of
h
. If the
\mathrm{U}(1)
-action has isolated fixed points, then

(4.5)

\int_{M}e^{h}\mathrm{vol}=\frac{(-2\pi)^{m}}{m!}\sum_{p\in M_{X}}e^{h(p)}\frac{\langle\mathrm{vol},\pi^{\wedge m}\rangle(p)}{\lambda_{1}(p)\cdots\lambda_{m}(p)}.

Remark 4.1
.

If
\pi=\omega^{-1}
and
\mathrm{vol}=\omega^{\wedge m}/m!
, where
\omega
is a symplectic structure on
M
, (
4.5
) recovers the Duistermaat-Heckman localization formula in symplectic geometry.

In particular, if the Poisson structure
\pi
is unimodular, i.e.,
[X_{\mathrm{vol}}]=0
, then (
4.4
) implies that the
\mathrm{U}(1)
-action on
(M,\pi)
is Hamiltonian.

Corollary 4.1
.

Let
(M,\pi)
be a
2m
-dimensional compact unimodular Poisson manifold. If
(M,\pi)
has a Hamiltonian
\mathrm{U}(1)
-action with a set
M_{X}
of isolated fixed points, then one can find
p\in M_{X}
such that
\mathrm{rank}(\pi_{p})=2m
.

Proof.

By assumption, one can find a
\mathrm{U}(1)
-invariant volume form
\mathrm{vol}
on
M
such that
X_{\mathrm{vol}}=0
. Theorem
4.1
then implies that

\sum_{p\in M_{X}}\exp(h(p))\frac{\langle\mathrm{vol},\pi^{\wedge m}\rangle(p)}{\lambda_{1}(p)\cdots\lambda_{m}(p)}\neq 0,

where
h
is any Hamiltonian function of the
\mathrm{U}(1)
-action. This is possible only if
\pi
has rank
2m
at one of the fixed points of the
\mathrm{U}(1)

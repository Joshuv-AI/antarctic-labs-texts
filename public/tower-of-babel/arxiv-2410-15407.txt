Spin One Matter Fields
Daniel O.R. Azevedo, Oswaldo M. Del Cima, Thadeu D.S. Dias, Daniel H.T. Franco, Emílio D. Pereira, Olivier Piguet

Abstract
It is shown how spin one vector matter fields can be coupled to a Yang-Mills theory. Such matter fields are defined as belonging to a representation R R of the Yang-Mills gauge algebra 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} . It is also demanded that these fields together with the original gauge fields be the gauge fields of an embedding total gauge algebra 𝔤 tot \mathfrak{g}_{\rm tot} . The existence of a physically consistent Yang-Mills action for the total algebra is finally required. These conditions are rather restrictive, as shown in some examples: non-trivial solutions may or may not exist depending on the choice of the original algebra 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} and of the representation R R . The case of the initial algebra 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} = 𝔲 ⁡ ( 1 ) ⊕ 𝔰 ​ 𝔲 ​ ( 2 ) \mathfrak{u}(1)\oplus\mathfrak{su}(2) is treated in more detail.



Keywords: Spin one matter; Quantum field theory; Yang-Mill theories; Lie algebra extensions
.

Three years after the proposal by Lee and Yang

fields of electric charge
\pm e
with the electromagnetic field.
They showed that the only consistent way to achieve this is
within a SU(2) non-Abelian Yang-Mills theory
[
9
]
,
the gauge field multiplet of the latter
being composed by the electromagnetic field together with the
spin one matter fields.

A short time before the publication or Ref.
[
3
]

appeared a quite general proposal by Ogievetsky and
Polubarinov
[
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
]
for the definition of
higher spin tensorial fields. For the special case of spin 1

eliminating its spin 0 part. For massless vector fields, which is

Yang-Mills theory having these vector fields as its gauge fields.
This was confirmed in various ways, among them the BRST

[
10
]

In the present paper, we propose a more restricted definition of
“spin-one matter fields”
{\phi}^{i}_{\mu}

\mathfrak{g}

and gauge fields

, must transform in some

R

\mathfrak{g}
.
Of course, in view of the general results quoted above,
the fields

\mathfrak{g}_{\rm tot}
containing the original
gauge algebra
\mathfrak{g}
as a subalgebra.
We will look for the whole set of necessary and sufficient
conditions on the couplings for the existence of a solution
obeying to our requirements in a general situation,
and apply them to some concrete examples.
The field content of the present paper is restricted to
the Yang-Mills fields associated to the initial gauge algebra

\mathfrak{g}
and the spin one matter fields, all taken without mass.

We begin in Section
2

reviewing a simple example, in the BRST framework,
as a preparation for our further discussion.
The general scheme is exposed in
Section
3

Section
4

some prospective in the Conclusion Section.
Some useful facts about Lie Algebra theory are presented in an

As it is well known

s{\cal A}_{\mu}^{\alpha}=\partial_{\mu}{\cal G}^{\alpha}+{\cal X}_{\beta\gamma}{}^{\alpha}{\cal A}_{\mu}^{\beta}{\cal G}^{\gamma},\quad s{\cal G}^{\alpha}=-\dfrac{1}{2}{\cal C}_{\beta\gamma}{}^{\alpha}{\cal G}^{\beta}{\cal G}^{\gamma}.

We have taken into account the ghost number conservation, the ghost fields carrying

(2.6)

From these structure constants we can write the Killing form,

(2.7)

The terms in

Spin one matter in a Yang-Mills theory

3.1
Gauge invariances and algebraic structure of the
theory

Let us begin with a Yang-Mills gauge theory
associated to a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, in dimension
four space-time
3
3

3

Everything in the present paper,

. The algebra may contain an invariant Abelian subalgebra

Our aim is to couple to the gauge fields

Let us summarize here the conditions we have imposed in order
to achieve a consistent coupling of the spin one matter fields:

1.

The original
\mathfrak{g}
gauge fields together with
the spin one vector fields are the gauge fields of a total algebra

\mathfrak{g}_{\rm tot}
containing

as a subalgebra.

2.

The spin one vector fields belong to a representation of the
initial gauge algebra
\mathfrak{g}

(

3.

It exists a physically relevant Yang-Mills action

By this we mean in particular that the action must involve
the spin one matter fields in a non-trivial way, and also that
its kinetical term must obey the positivity conditions necessary for
the unitarity of the corresponding quantum theory.

As we will see in some of the examples discussed below,
this set of conditions is rather restrictive.

3.2
Invariant action

An important quantity we will need is the invariant Killing form of the total

\hskip 5.69054ptK_{ij}=-(S^{a}T_{a})_{ij}-(S^{a}T_{a})_{ji}+i(Y_{j}-Y_{i})S_{ij}{}^{0}+t_{il}{}^{k}t_{jk}{}^{l}.\end{array}

We see that the Killing form may be
non-degenerate

3.3
Some remarks on the renormalization of the theory

A general result
[
17
,
18
,
12
,
13
]

is that the Yang-Mill theories in four dimensions
are renormalizable and thus exist perturbatively as quantum theories whose
\hbar=0
limit is a given classical theory.
(The Standard Model of particle interactions is the most well-known example.)
In particular all independent parameters of the theory are
multiplicatively renormalized. Among them we have the part of
the structure constants

{\cal C}_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\gamma}
which remain independent after all Jacobi identities
are satisfied. But, in our construction, we have imposed the
extra conditions (
3.11
), which in general are expected to be
broken by the quantum corrections. A way to preserve them is to work in
the Landau gauge
\partial^{\mu}{\cal A}_{\mu}^{\alpha}=0
and make profit of the antighost
equation
[
19
,
15
]
which holds in this gauge,
as we will show now.

Let us first recall the functional formalism used in the
BRS algebraic renormalization program
[
15
]
.
The implementation of the Laudau gauge is done adding to the
gauge invariant action (
3.21
) a trivially
BRST-invariant gauge fixing action

S_{\rm gf}=s\int d^{4}x\bar{{\cal G}}_{\alpha}\partial^{\mu}{\cal A}_{\mu}^{\alpha}=\int d^{4}x\left(\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}\partial^{\mu}{\cal A}_{\mu}^{\alpha}-\bar{{\cal G}}_{\alpha}\partial^{\mu}(\partial_{\mu}{\cal G}^{\alpha}+{\cal C}_{\gamma\beta}{}^{\alpha}{\cal A}_{\mu}^{\gamma}{\cal G}^{\beta})\right),

(3.24)

where one has
introduced a new set of fields, namely the antighosts

\bar{{\cal G}}_{\alpha}
and the Lagrange multipliers
{\cal B}_{\alpha}
, which
transform as BRST-doublets:

s\bar{{\cal G}}_{\alpha}={\cal B}_{\alpha},\quad s{\cal B}_{\alpha}=0.

(3.25)

One recovers the Landau gauge condition through the functional identity

\frac{\delta S}{\delta\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}}=\partial^{\mu}{\cal A}_{\mu}^{\alpha},

(3.26)

where

S=S_{\rm YM}+S_{\rm gf}+S_{\rm ext},

(3.27)

is the total action. The last term,

S_{\rm ext}=\int d^{4}x\left(\rho^{\mu}_{\alpha}s{\cal A}_{\mu}^{\alpha}+\sigma_{\alpha}s{\cal G}^{\alpha}\right),

(3.28)

defines the coupling of external sources
\rho
and
\sigma

to the
{\cal A}
and
{\cal G}
BRST transforms
(
3.9
) or (
3.13
),
introduced in order to control their renormalization.

The BRST invariance of the theory is expressed functionally,
at the classical level, by the Slavnov-Taylor identity

\mathcal{S}(S)=\int d^{4}x\left(\frac{\delta S}{\delta\rho_{\alpha}^{\mu}}\frac{\delta S}{\delta{\cal A}^{\alpha}_{\mu}}+\frac{\delta S}{\delta\sigma_{\alpha}}\frac{\delta S}{\delta{\cal G}^{\alpha}}+\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}\frac{\delta S}{\delta\bar{{\cal G}}_{\alpha}}\right)=0.

(3.29)

Another functional identity, the ghost equation

\frac{\delta S}{\delta\bar{{\cal G}}_{\alpha}}+\partial_{\mu}\frac{\delta S}{\delta\rho_{\mu\alpha}}=0,

(3.30)

is a consequence of (
3.26
) and (
3.29
).

The restriction to the Landau gauge
as defined by (
3.26
), instead of a general
linear covariant gauge, gives us an additional functional
identity: the antighost equation

\int d^{4}x\left(\frac{\delta S}{\delta{\cal G}^{\gamma}}-{\cal C}_{\beta\gamma}{}^{\alpha}\bar{{\cal G}}_{\alpha}\frac{\delta S}{\delta\mathcal{B}_{\beta}}\right)=\Delta^{cl}_{\gamma}

(3.31)

where

\Delta^{cl}_{\gamma}=\int d^{4}x\,{\cal C}_{\beta\gamma}^{\alpha}\left(-\rho_{\alpha}^{\mu}{\cal A}_{\mu}^{\beta}+\sigma_{\alpha}{\cal G}^{\beta}\right)

(3.32)

Renormalizibility amounts to show that there is a solution of
the Slavnov identity (
3.29
) together with the gauge condition (
3.26
), with the action
S
substituted by the
generating functional
\Gamma[{\cal A},{\cal G},{\cal B},\bar{{\cal G}},\rho,\sigma]
of the amputated
1-particle irreducible Green functions. The solution is obtained
as a (formal) power series in
\hbar
(equivalently: in the number of
loops in the Feynman graphs). At zero
th
order,
\Gamma=S
.
Renormalizability was proved in the general case of a Yang-Mills
theory with scalar and spinor fields, in the absence of the
Adler–Bardeen non-Abelian gauge
anomaly
[
17
,
18
,
13
,
15
]
.

The antighost equation (
3.31
), with
S
substituted
by
\Gamma
, was proven to hold at all orders,
too
[
19
,
15
]
.
It is a peculiarity of the Landau gauge choice, and its usefulness
resides in its right-hand side being linear in the dynamical
fields
{\cal A}
and
{\cal G}
, which implies the non-renormalization of its coefficients, the structure constants
{\cal C}_{\gamma\beta}^{\alpha}
. As a consequence, the S1PR conditions (
3.11
) we have imposed on these coefficients remain stable in the renormalization process.

In the case where the matter vector field representation is the

Remarks

1.

In the case of isospin 1/2 and 1, a completion
of the solution with
\kappa=\lambda=0

to a fully invariant action is indeed possible, but with the price of
modifying the gauge transformation laws. And this will reproduce the
two cases studied above, with total gauge algebras being
\mathfrak{su}(3)

and
\mathfrak{so}(5)
, respectively!

2.

Such a completion, for the higher isospins, is doomed to fail
as we may conclude from what we just have shown.
This turns out to be the case even if there is a simple Lie
algebra of the appropriate dimension and having the original algebra

\mathfrak{u}(1)\oplus\mathfrak{su}(2)
as a subalgebra –
for instance for isospin 9/2,
where the total dimension, 24, is that of

\mathfrak{su}(5)
.

5
Conclusion

Given a gauge theory based on some given gauge algebra

\mathfrak{g}
,
we have shown how to implement the conditions we have proposed in order to define spin-one matter fields as belonging to
a representation of this gauge algebra.
This lead to the construction of an extension
\mathfrak{g}_{\rm tot}
of the initial gauge algebra submitted to a set of conditions summarized at the end of Subsection
3.1
and to look for a physically relevant corresponding action.

We have then applied these conditions
to two examples. The first one is that of the
initial gauge algebra
\mathfrak{g}
being
\mathfrak{so}(N)
.
We have restricted ourselves to the
fundamental representation for the matter field
{\phi}^{i}_{\mu}
,
and made a special Ansatz
for the choice of the structure constants
(see last two of Eqs. (
4.3
)). The result is the
total algebra being
\mathfrak{so}(N+1)
– a rather obvious embedding
of the initial algebra.

The second example, where the initial algebra is
\mathfrak{u}(1)\oplus\mathfrak{su}(2)
, is far less trivial and potentially more
interesting for physics. The representations of
\mathfrak{su}(2)

we have taken into account are
the direct sums of a (generally complex) irreducible representation
of isospin
I
and its conjugate, carrying
\mathfrak{u}(1)

hypercharges
Y
and
-Y
, respectively. Of all the isospin values

I=1/2,\,1,\,\cdots,\,7
considered in the numerical
evaluations, only
the values
I=1/2
and
1
have yielded non-degenerate, compact
total algebras
\mathfrak{g}_{\rm tot}
, which turned out to be
\mathfrak{su}(3)
and

\mathfrak{so}(5)
, respectively.
For the other values of
I
, the total algebra is not semisimple,
more precisely, it is the semidirect sum of the initial
algebra
\mathfrak{su}(2)
by a non-Abelian radical

\mathfrak{h}
, the basis of which is given by the

\mathfrak{u}(1)
generator
\tau_{0}
and the generators

w_{i}
and
w^{*}_{i}
associated
to the matter fields
{\phi}_{\mu}^{i}
and their conjugates.
As the latter solutions do not provide an action which
involves the spin one matter fields, they must be discarded.
The physically relevant solutions are those which produce a simple or semi-simple algebra.

We can conclude, in particular from the latter example, that
our conditions are very
selective. Solutions with a simple or semisimple total algebra
may be present or not, depending on the choice of the initial algebra
and its representations
R
for the matter multiplet

{\phi}^{i}_{\mu}
. Moreover nothing guaranties that the resulting
total algebra is compact, hence having a (positive or negative)
definite Killing form allowing the construction of an action
compatible with unitarity,
i.e.
, such that the matrix
of the propagator residues at each physical pole is positive definite.

We have restricted ourselves to the original Yang-Mills fields

A^{a}_{\mu}
and the matter fields
{\phi}^{i}_{\mu}
, all taken as massless.
An important generalization, not considered in the present paper
but deserving further investigation,
would be the introduction of spin one half
matter fields, as well as of suitable spin zero Higgs fields
in order to produce mass for some of (or all) the matter and
Yang-Mills fields.

Semidirect sums of Lie algebras:

Let us give two Lie algebras
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{h}
with no common element else than
the element zero, and an homomorphism

\sigma:\mathfrak{g}\to\mbox{Der}\,\mathfrak{h}
, where

\mbox{Der}\,\mathfrak{h}
is the set of

derivative operators
acting on
\mathfrak{h}
.
The latter means that such an operator,
\sigma
, acts as

\begin{array}[]{l}\sigma(g_{1}g_{2})=\sigma(g_{1})g_{2}+g_{1}\sigma(g_{2}),\quad\quad\forall g_{1},g_{2}\in\mathfrak{g},\\[8.53581pt]
\sigma(g)[h_{1},h_{2}]=[\sigma(g)h_{1},h_{2}]+[h_{1},\sigma(g)h_{2}],\quad\forall g\in\mathfrak{g}\mbox{ and }h_{1},h_{2}\in\mathfrak{h}.\end{array}

We denote by
(g,h)
the pair of one element of
\mathfrak{g}
and
one element of
\mathfrak{h}
.
Then, the
semidirect sum
of
\mathfrak{g}
by
\mathfrak{h}
,
noted as
\mathfrak{g}\ltimes\mathfrak{h}
, is
defined
[
21
]
by the bracket

[(g_{1},h_{1}),(g_{2},h_{2})]=\left([g_{1},g_{2}]\,,\,[h_{1},h_{2}]+\sigma(g_{1})h_{2}-\sigma(g_{2})h_{1}\right).

(A.1)

Identifying the derivative operator
\sigma(g)
as
the adjoint action of
\mathfrak{g}
on
\mathfrak{h}
,

\sigma(g)h=[g,h],

yields the algebra (
3.14
) in the case of vanishing tensors

S_{ij}{}^{0}
and
S_{ij}{}^{a}
, if one identifies
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{h}
with
the algebras of basis

\{\tau_{0},\tau_{a}\}
and
\{w_{i}\}
, respectively.

Solvable algebra:

A Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is called solvable if the sequence of commutator and commutators of commutators

[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]\supseteq[[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}],[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]],\supseteq[[[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}],[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]],[[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}],[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]]]\supseteq\cdots,

where
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
means the commutator of any element
of
\mathfrak{g}
with any other element of
\mathfrak{g}
,
is finite and terminates with the 0 subalgebra.

Levi’s decomposition:

A theorem proved by Levi
[
22
,
23
]
states that any Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
on the real or complex numbers can be decomposed as the direct product of a semisimple
\mathfrak{g}_{\rm S}
by the maximal solvable ideal of
\mathfrak{g}
, its radical rad(
\mathfrak{g}
):

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{\rm S}\ltimes{\rm rad}(\mathfrak{g}).

(A.2)

Uniform asymptotics for a multidimensional renewal risk model with multivariate subexponential claims
Dimitrios G. Konstantinides, JiaJun Liu, Charalampos D. Passalidis

Abstract
. In this paper, we study a multidimensional risk model with a common renewal process and in the presence of a constant interest force. The claim sizes are independent and identically distributed random vectors, with the distribution of dependent components belonging to the class of multivariate subexponential distributions. We establish locally uniform asymptotic estimations for the entrance probability of the discounted aggregate claims into some ‘rare sets’, and further derive asymptotic estimations uniformly over all the time horizons. Furthermore, we present some distribution examples that belong to these multivariate heavy-tailed distribution classes, which are not restricted only to the case of multivariate regular variation.



Keywords: multidimensional risk model; renewal process; multivariate subexponentiality; multivariate positively decreasing distributions; uniformity

Mathematics Subject Classification
: Primary 62P05 ; Secondary 60G70.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we consider an insurer operating
d
-lines of business
(d\in{\mathbb{N}})

that share a common counting process for claim vectors
\{{\bf X}^{(i)},\,i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}.
These claim vectors form a sequence of independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) non-negative random vectors with a common distribution
F.

Note that each claim vector
\mathbf{X}^{(i)}=\left(X_{1}^{(i)},\ldots,X_{d}^{(i)}\right)
, for
i\in\mathbb{N}
, may contain zero components (but not all zero), and arrives at time
\tau_{i}
, with
\tau_{0}=0
by convention. The sequence
\{\tau_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
forms a renewal counting process

\displaystyle N(t):=\sup\{n\in{\mathbb{N}}\;:\;\tau_{n}\leq t\}\,,

(1.1)

where the inter-arrival times
\{\theta_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
, defined by
\theta_{i}=\tau_{i}-\tau_{i-1}
for
i\geq 1
, constitute a sequence of i.i.d. non-negative random variables. The renewal process
\{N(t),\,t\geq 0\}
is assumed to have a finite renewal function:

\displaystyle\lambda(t)=\mathbf{E}[N(t)]=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}[\tau_{i}\leq t]\,,

For applicability to risk theory, we restrict our analysis to the interval

\Lambda:=\{t\;:\lambda(t)>0\}=\{t\;:\;\mathbf{P}[\tau_{1}\leq t]>0\}
.
This restriction is natural in univariate and multivariate risk models: if there is no positive probability of claim occurrence, the ruin probability trivially equals zero.

Assuming the insurer invests surplus at a constant interest force
r\geq 0
(with
r=0
representing no investment), the discounted aggregate claims at time
t\geq 0
are given by

\displaystyle{\bf D}_{r}(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{N(t)}{\bf X}^{(i)}\,e^{-r\,\tau_{i}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\sum_{i=1}^{N(t)}X_{1}^{(i)}\,e^{-r\,\tau_{i}}\\
\vdots\\
\sum_{i=1}^{N(t)}X_{d}^{(i)}\,e^{-r\,\tau_{i}}\end{array}\right)\,.

Furthermore, we consider that the initial capital of the insurer is equal to
x>0
and is allocated through the deterministic weights
l_{1},\,\ldots,\,l_{d}>0
, with

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{d}l_{i}=1\,,

into
d
-lines of business. The insurer receives the premium, whose density is given by the vector
{\bf p}(t)=(p_{1}(t),\,\ldots,\,p_{d}(t))
, for any
t\geq 0
, and we assume that the premium density of each line is bounded above by some positive constant, namely
0\leq p_{i}(t)\leq C_{i}
, for some
C_{i}\in(0,\,\infty)
and for any
i=1,\,\ldots,\,d
,
t\geq 0
. So, the discounted surplus process at the moment
t\geq 0
, is given by the relation

\displaystyle{\bf U}(t):=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}U_{1}(t)\\
\vdots\\
U_{d}(t)\end{array}\right)=x\,\left(\begin{array}[]{c}l_{1}\\
\vdots\\
l_{d}\end{array}\right)+\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\int_{0-}^{t}e^{-r\,s}\,p_{1}(s)\,ds\\
\vdots\\
\int_{0-}^{t}e^{-r\,s}\,p_{d}(s)\,ds\end{array}\right)-{\bf D}_{r}(t)\,.

Due to modern insurance requirements, insurers are compelled to maintain multiple risky portfolios to remain competitive, which has driven significant research interest in the risk model (
1
). For the special case
d=2,
numerous studies have analyzed this framework under varying distributional assumptions and dependence structures for the claims. Examples include
[
8
]
,
[
14
]
,
[
21
]
,
[
52
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
6
]
and
[
49
]
, among others.

For higher-dimensional settings, several studies have examined the risk model in (
1
) under the assumption that claim vectors exhibit multivariate regular variation (MRV). Key examples include
[
19
]
,
[
24
]
,
[
35
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
50
]
, and
[
11
]
. Most of these works further assume asymptotically dependent components within each claim vector, implying that their marginal distributions necessarily belong to a regularly varying class. This focus on asymptotic dependence and MRV, restricts the framework to scenarios with sufficiently extreme events, thereby excluding many moderately heavy-tailed distributions (e.g., lognormal and Weibull) from consideration.

Based on this gap in the literature, following the pioneering paper
[
42
]
, we examine the model in (
1
) under some multivariate distribution classes, greater than MRV and under arbitrarily dependent components of each claim vector. See for some related models in
[
42
]
,
[
26
]
,
[
27
]
,
[
40
]
. In this paper, we follow the claim-vectors model, with distribution classes bigger than MRV, and with arbitrarily dependent components. We also establish uniform asymptotic estimations for the behavior of the entrance probability of the discounted aggregate claims, into some ‘rare sets’, and further for the ruin probability of the insurer.

Namely, we establish the asymptotic relation

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf D}_{r}(t)\in x\,A\right]\sim\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{P}[{\bf X}\in x\,e^{r\,s}\,A]\,\lambda(ds)\,,

(1.16)

as
x\to\infty
, uniformly for any
t\in\Lambda_{T}:=\Lambda\cap(0,\,T]
, for some fixed constant
T\in\Lambda
, when the distribution of the claim-vectors belongs to the class of multivariate subexponential distributions, see Section 2. The rare set
x\,A
can represent various actuarially relevant scenarios. For example, in the univariate case where
A=(1,\infty)
, relation (
1.16
) reduces to the classical asymptotic framework for ruin probabilities in one-dimensional risk models, as seen in
[
45
]
,
[
18
]
,
[
36
]
, among others.

Under a slightly narrower distributional assumption-specifically, the class of multivariate positively-decreasing subexponential distributions (see Section 2, for the definition)—we strengthen the local uniformity condition in (
1.16
) to a global uniformity condition, ensuring that (
1.16
) holds uniformly for all
t\in\Lambda.
A direct consequence of this result is a uniform asymptotic estimate for the finite-time ruin probability, where ruin is defined as the surplus entering a predefined ‘ruin set’.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we give the preliminaries about the multivariate distribution classes with heavy tails. In Section 3, we present our main results. In Section 4, we construct some distribution examples that belong to the classes of multivariate subexponential and multivariate positively decreasing, subexponential distributions, based mainly on a concrete ‘rare-set’. In Sections 5 and 6 we provide the proofs of the main results together with some necessary lemmas.

\displaystyle 0<\liminf\dfrac{{\bf f}(x\,{\mathbb{B}})}{{\bf g}(x\,{\mathbb{B}})}\leq\limsup\dfrac{{\bf f}(x\,{\mathbb{B}})}{{\bf g}(x\,{\mathbb{B}})}<\infty\,.

For the

-variate, positive functions

,

, if

\displaystyle\limsup_{x\to\infty}\sup_{t\in E}\dfrac{{\bf f}^{*}(x\,{\mathbb{B}},\,t)}{{\bf g}^{*}(x\,{\mathbb{B}},\,t)}\leq 1\,.

Finally, for a distribution
V
, we denote its tail by
\overline{V}(x)=1-V(x)
, for any
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
. In what follows, the random vectors have as support the non-negative quadrant

. In this section, all the univariate distributions

are such that
\overline{V}(x)>0
, for any

.

2.2.
Multivariate distribution classes

To present the definition of multivariate distribution classes, we need the following family of sets

\displaystyle\mathscr{R}:=\{A\subsetneq{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\;:\;A\,{\text{o}pen,\;increasing},\;A^{c}\;{\text{c}onvex},\;{\bf 0}\notin\overline{A}\}\,,

(2.1)

where the set
A
is called increasing if for any
{\bf x}\in A
and
{\bf y}\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{d}
, it holds
{\bf x}+{\bf y}\in A
. If
{\bf X}
is a random vector and
A\subsetneq\mathscr{R}
is a fixed set, then the random variable
Y_{A}
defined by

\displaystyle Y_{A}:=\sup\{u\;:\;{\bf X}\in u\,A\}\,,

(2.2)

has a proper distribution, let say
F_{A}
, whose tail is defined as follows

\displaystyle\overline{F}_{A}(x):=\mathbf{P}[{\bf X}\in x\,A]=\mathbf{P}\left[\sup_{{\bf p}\in I_{A}}{\bf p}^{T}\,{\bf X}>x\right]\,,

(2.3)

for any
x>0
, see in
[
42
, Lem. 4.3(c)]
for the existence of
I_{A}\subsetneq{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, the index set for any
A\subsetneq\mathscr{R}
, and in
[
42
, Lem. 4.5]
for the fact that
F_{A}
is a proper distribution.

Hence, for any fixed set
A\subsetneq\mathscr{R}
, by
[
42
]
we define the multivariate subexponentiality as follows. We say that the distribution
F
belongs in the class
\mathcal{S}_{A}
, that means the class of multivariate subexponential distributions on set
A
, symbolically
F\in\mathcal{S}_{A}
, if
F_{A}\in\mathcal{S}
, namely if for any (or equivalently, for some) integer
n\geq 2
, it holds

\displaystyle\lim\dfrac{\overline{F_{A}^{n*}}(x)}{\overline{F}_{A}(x)}=n\,,

(2.4)

where by
F_{A}^{n*}
we denote the
n
-th order convolution power of
F_{A}
. Additionally, as multivariate subexponentiality over the whole set
\mathscr{R}
was defined as class
\mathcal{S}_{\mathscr{R}}:=\bigcap_{A\subsetneq\mathscr{R}}\mathcal{S}_{A}
.

On similar lines in
[
25
]
, we can find the class of multivariate long-tailed distributions. We say that the distribution
F
follows a multivariate long tailed distribution on
A
, symbolically
F\in\mathcal{L}_{A}
, if
F_{A}\in\mathcal{L}
, namely if for any (or equivalently, for some)

it holds

\displaystyle\lim\dfrac{\overline{F}_{A}(x-a)}{\overline{F}_{A}(x)}=1\,.

(2.5)

For more details about uni-variate classes
\mathcal{S}
,
\mathcal{L}
, we refer to
[
13
]
and
[
32
]
. In
[
27
]
was introduced the class of multivariate positively decreasing distribution on
A
, symbolically
F\in\mathcal{P_{D}}_{A}
, which is characterized by distributions, that satisfy
F_{A}\in\mathcal{P_{D}}
, namely for any (or equivalently, for some)
v>1
, it holds

\displaystyle\limsup\dfrac{\overline{F}_{A}(v\,x)}{\overline{F}_{A}(x)}<1\,,

(2.6)

as also the class of multivariate subexponential, positively decreasing distributions on
A
, symbolically
F\in\mathcal{A}_{A}
, when it holds
F_{A}\in\mathcal{A}
, where
\mathcal{A}:=\mathcal{S}\cap\mathcal{P_{D}}
. For all these classes above, we keep a general notation
\mathcal{B}_{\mathscr{R}}:=\bigcap_{A\subsetneq\mathscr{R}}\mathcal{B}_{A}
, where
\mathcal{B}\in\{\mathcal{L},\,\mathcal{P_{D}},\,\mathcal{A},\,\mathcal{S}\}
. From
[
44
]
and from
[

]
, for the univariate case, we find that class

is a wide enough one, that covers distributions with light and heavy tails. In fact, for all distributions with infinite right endpoint, the only distributions that do not belong in class

The restriction in class

fails. These problems are related to infinite sums and ruin probability estimations with infinite time horizon, or in global uniformity versions, see for example in
[

]
,
[

]
,
[
18
]
, among others. We remark that the class

\displaystyle J_{F_{A}}^{-}=-\lim_{v\to\infty}\dfrac{\log\overline{F}_{A}^{*}(v)}{\log v}\,,

(2.7)

with

\displaystyle\overline{F}_{A}^{*}(v)=\limsup\dfrac{\overline{F}_{A}(v\,x)}{\overline{F}_{A}(x)}\,,

for

. For more results on Matuszewska indexes, see in
[

]

[

[

]

]

We should recall that a distribution

\displaystyle\lim\dfrac{\overline{V}(t\,x)}{\overline{V}(x)}=t^{-\alpha}\,,

Therefore, a distribution

, that means

. Symbolically we write

In

where we denote the union of

.

3.
Main results

Here we provide the main results of the paper. At first, we need the first assumption for the risk model.

Assumption 3.1
.

The sequences
\{{\bf X}^{(i)}\,,\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
,
\{N(t)\,,\;t\geq 0\}

\{{\bf p}(t)\,,\;t\geq 0\}
, are mutually independent.

Assumption
3.1

[
38
]
,
[
35
]
, and
[
10
]
, among others. Our first result establishes locally uniform asymptotic expressions for the probability that the discounted aggregate claims enter a ‘rare-set’

, where

. This holds under the condition that the joint claim distribution

belongs to the class of multivariate subexponential distributions on

Theorem 3.1
.

Let

be a fixed proper subset. For the discounted aggregate claims in (
1
), if Assumption

holds and

, then (

) holds uniformly for any

The next theorem extends (

and imposing a technical condition on inter-arrival times.

Theorem 3.2

Let
A\subsetneq\mathscr{R}
be a fixed proper subset. Consider the discounted aggregate claims in (
1
) under Assumption
3.1
. If
F\in\mathcal{A}_{A}
, and there exists

Remark 3.1
.

Two important forms for the insurance practice, that can take the set

\displaystyle A_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{{\bf x}\;:\;x_{i}>u_{i}\,,\;\exists\;i=1,\,\ldots,\,d\}\,,

\displaystyle A_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{{\bf x}\;:\;\sum_{i=1}^{d}l_{i}\,x_{i}>u\}\,,

with

.

Initially, we notice that in univariate case

Analogous to the univariate case, the asymptotic behavior of discounted aggregate claims directly governs the ruin probability in multivariate risk models. While multiple definitions of ruin probabilities exist in the multivariate framework, we focus on ruin probability defined as the first entrance of the surplus process into a ruin set
L
, which satisfies the following key assumption.

Assumption 3.2
.

We assume that the ruin set
L
is open, decreasing (that means the

is increasing), its complement,

, is convex, and it holds

Remark 3.4
.

Assumption

, introduced in
[
19
]
and later employed in
[

\displaystyle L_{1}=\{{\bf x}\;:\;x_{i}<0\,,\;\exists\;i=1,\,\ldots,\,d\}\,,

(3.3)

\displaystyle L_{2}=\{{\bf x}\;:\;\sum_{i=1}^{d}x_{i}<0\}\,.

(3.4)

represents the probability that at least one of the

\displaystyle A=\{{\bf x}\;:\;x_{i}>u_{i}\,,\;\forall\;i=1,\,\ldots,\,d\}\,,

as it comes from
[

. For more discussions about

,

,

]
,
[

]

Hence, for the ruin sets

, that satisfy Assumption

\displaystyle\psi_{{\bf l},\,L}(x,\,t)=\mathbf{P}[{\bf U}(s)\in L\;:\;\exists\;s\in[0,\,t]]\,,

\displaystyle\psi_{{\bf l},\,L}(x,\,t)=\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf D}_{r}(s)-\int_{0}^{s}e^{-rz}\,{\bf p}(z)dz\in x\,({\bf l}-L)\;:\;\exists\;s\in[0,\,t]\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf D}_{r}(s)-\int_{0}^{s}e^{-rz}\,{\bf p}(z)dz\in x\,A\;:\;\exists\;s\in[0,\,t]\right]\,,

where

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}e^{-rz}\,{\bf p}(z)dz=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-rz}\,p_{1}(z)dz\\

4.
Examples of distribution from
\mathcal{S}_{A}
and
\mathcal{A}_{A}

Now, we proceed in the construction of some distribution examples belonging to family
\mathcal{B}_{A}
, with
\mathcal{B}\in\{\mathcal{S},\,\mathcal{A}\}
for some set
A
of the form (
3.2
) with
u=1
. In these examples, firstly we leave the class
MRV
, see in relation (
2.9
), and secondly, we use only conditions for the marginal distributions and dependence structures among the components, making these examples easily verifiable in practice. Let us note that only through subexponential marginals, it does NOT imply the distribution class
\mathcal{S}_{A}
, as we find out from
[
40
, Exam. 2.1]
. For sufficient conditions, in order to belong to a distribution in the class
\mathcal{S}_{\mathscr{R}}
, we refer to
[
42
, Sec. 4]
.

Now we focus our attention on the set of the form

\displaystyle A=\left\{{\bf x}\,:\,\sum_{i=1}^{d}l_{i}\,x_{i}>1\right\}\,,

(4.1)

where it holds
l_{1},\,\ldots,\,l_{d}>0
, such that
\sum_{i=1}^{d}l_{i}=1
. We note that all the examples can be easily generalized, taking instead the sets from (
4.1
), the sets from (
3.2
), but for the sake of convenience, we choose the first case. Additionally, we restrict ourselves to the case
d=2
; however, the extension for any
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
, for all the examples, is straightforward.

Hence, we assume that the random vector
{\bf X}=(X_{1},\,X_{2})
has non-negative components, with marginals
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
. For the set
A
from relation (
4.1
), we can choose some
I_{A}=\{(l_{1},\,l_{2})\}
and then it holds

\displaystyle\overline{F}_{A}(x)=\mathbf{P}[{\bf X}\in x\,A]=\mathbf{P}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{2}l_{i}\,x_{i}>x\right]\,.

(4.2)

From relations (
4.1
) and (
4.2
), we can see that the multivariate distribution of
{\bf X}
is directly connected with the distribution of the (deterministic) weighted sums. Therefore, the first example has value by its own merit, since it provides a closure property of classes
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{A}
, with respect to the sum of dependent random variables, under the regression dependence, which introduced by
[
30
]
.

Example 4.1
.

Let a set
A
as relation (
4.1
), with
(l_{1},\,l_{2})\in[a,\,b]^{2}
, with
0<a\leq b<1
. We assume that the random vector
{\bf X}=(X_{1},\,X_{2})
has marginal distributions
F_{1},\,F_{2}\in\mathcal{B}\in\{\mathcal{S},\,\mathcal{A}\}
, and the components
X_{1},\,X_{2}
have regression dependence, namely there exist constants
x_{0}>0
and
C>0
such that it holds

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}[X_{i}>x_{i}\;|\;X_{j}=x_{j}]\leq C\,\mathbf{P}\left[X_{i}>x_{i}\right]\,,

(4.3)

for any
1\leq i\neq j\leq 2
and for any
\min\{x_{i},\,x_{j}\}\geq x_{0}
. Furthermore, we assume that
F_{1}*F_{2}\in\mathcal{S}
. Then, by
[
48
, Th. 2]
we obtain that it holds

\displaystyle\overline{F}_{A}(x)=\mathbf{P}[l_{1}\,X_{1}+l_{2}\,X_{2}>x]\sim\mathbf{P}[l_{1}\,X_{1}>x_{i}]+\mathbf{P}[l_{2}\,X_{2}>x]=:\overline{F}_{1}^{\prime}(x)+\overline{F}_{2}^{\prime}(x)\,,

(4.4)

where
F_{i}^{\prime}
represents the distribution of the product
l_{i}\,X_{i}
, for
i=1,\,2
. Again by
[
48
, Th. 2]
, since the independence is a special subcase of (
4.3
), it is implied that

\displaystyle\overline{F_{1}^{\prime}*F_{2}^{\prime}}(x)\sim\overline{F}_{1}^{\prime}(x)+\overline{F}_{2}^{\prime}(x)\,.

(4.5)

However, from the inclusion
F_{1}^{\prime},\,F_{2}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}\in\{\mathcal{S},\,\mathcal{A}\}
, and from relation (
4.5
), taking into account
[
31
, Cor. 1.1]
in case of class
\mathcal{S}
and
[
28
, Cor. 3.1]
, we find that it holds
F_{1}^{\prime}*F_{2}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}\in\{\mathcal{S},\,\mathcal{A}\}
, and furthermore from relations (
4.4
) and (
4.5
), we obtain that it holds
\overline{F}_{A}(x)\sim\overline{F_{1}^{\prime}*F_{2}^{\prime}}(x)
. Hence, by the closure property of class
\mathcal{B}
with respect to strong tail equivalence, see in
[
47
, Th. 3]
in case of
\mathcal{S}
and in
[
28
, Rem. 2.1]
in case of
\mathcal{A}
, we conclude that it holds
F_{A}\in\mathcal{B}
, that means
F\in\mathcal{B}_{A}
.

Remark 4.1
.

The closure problem of the distribution class
\mathcal{S}
, with respect to convolution, is a well-studied issue, and several papers are devoted to it. We refer to
[
33
]
, for a famous counterexample, though which it was shown that the class
\mathcal{S}
is not closed with respect to convolution, and to
[
31
]
, for necessary and sufficient conditions for the closedness of
\mathcal{S}
with respect to convolution. However, the closure property of
\mathcal{S}
with respect to sum of dependent random variables, is NOT well-studied issue, in spite of the papers about the asymptotic behavior of the distribution tail of sum of finite number summands, see in
[
23
]
,
[
15
]
,
[
20
]
, among others. In previous example we see that if
F_{1},\,F_{2}\in\mathcal{B}\in\{\mathcal{S},\,\mathcal{A}\}
and
F_{1}*F_{2}\in\mathcal{S}
, then there exists some kind of insensitivity of the closure of the sum, with respect to dependence from relation (
4.3
), namely it holds
F_{X_{1}+X_{2}}\in\mathcal{B}\in\{\mathcal{S},\,\mathcal{A}\}
. This can be verified by following the same methodology with Example
4.1
, putting
l_{1}=l_{2}=1/2
.

For the next example, we need the introduction of the distribution class
\mathcal{D}
, called the class of dominatedly varying distributions. We say that a distribution
V\in\mathcal{D}
, if for any (or equivalently, for some )
b\in(0,\,1)
it holds

\displaystyle\limsup\dfrac{\overline{V}(b\,x)}{\overline{V}(x)}<\infty\,.

(4.6)

From
[
16
]
, it is known that
\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{L}\equiv\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{S}
. Further, for a smaller subclass of
\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{L}
we obtain the class
\mathcal{C}
, of consistently varying distributions, that characterize all the distributions satisfying the following relation

\displaystyle\lim_{v\downarrow 1}\liminf\dfrac{\overline{V}(v\,x)}{\overline{V}(x)}=1\,.

(4.7)

Let us remind that the following inclusion is well-known, see for example in
[
32
, Ch. 2]

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}:=\bigcup_{\alpha>0}\mathcal{R}_{-\alpha}\subsetneq\mathcal{C}\cap\mathcal{P_{D}}\subsetneq\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{A}\subsetneq\mathcal{A}\,.

(4.8)

In the next example, we consider smaller classes for the marginal distributions, in comparison with the classes
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{A}
, used in Example
4.1
, but now we employ a larger dependence structure among the components, in comparison with the dependence in relation (
4.3
). This dependence, called tail asymptotic independence, is introduced in
[
15
]
.

Example 4.2
.

Let
A
be a set as relation (
4.1
). We assume that the vector
{\bf X}=(X_{1},\,X_{2})
, has marginal distributions
F_{1},\,F_{2}\in\mathcal{B}\in\{\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{S},\,\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{A}\}
and its components
X_{1},\,X_{2}
, satisfy the tail asymptotic independence dependence structure, namely it holds

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}[X_{i}>x_{i}\>|\>X_{j}>x_{j}]\to 0\,,

(4.9)

as
\min\{x_{i},\,x_{j}\}\to\infty
, for any
1\leq i\neq j\leq 2
. Then via
[
34
, Th. 2.3]
, we find that it holds

\displaystyle\overline{F}_{A}(x)=\mathbf{P}[l_{1}\,X_{1}+l_{2}\,X_{2}>x]\sim\mathbf{P}[l_{1}\,X_{1}>x]+\mathbf{P}[l_{2}\,X_{2}>x]\,,

from where we obtain easily that it holds
F_{A}\in\mathcal{B}\in\{\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{S},\,\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{A}\}
. Hence, we conclude
F_{A}\in\mathcal{S}
, (or respectively,
F_{A}\in\mathcal{A}
) and thus we have
F\in\mathcal{S}_{A}
, (or respectively,
F\in\mathcal{A}_{A}
).

In the next example, we restrict ourselves to the marginals of the classes
\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{L}
,
\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{A}
, to the marginals of the classes
\mathcal{C}
,
\mathcal{C}\cap\mathcal{P_{D}}
, respectively. But for this purpose, we use the quasi asymptotic independence, firstly appearing in
[
4
]
, which is more general than dependence from relation (
4.9
).

Example 4.3
.

Let
A
be a set as relation (
4.1
). Let assume that the vector
{\bf X}=(X_{1},\,X_{2})
, has marginal distributions
F_{1},\,F_{2}\in\mathcal{B}\in\{\mathcal{C},\,\mathcal{C}\cap\mathcal{P_{D}}\}
and its components
X_{1},\,X_{2}
, satisfy the quasi asymptotic independence dependence structure, namely it holds

\displaystyle\lim\dfrac{\mathbf{P}[X_{1}>x\,,\>X_{2}>x]}{\overline{F}_{1}(x)+\overline{F}_{2}(x)}=0\,.

(4.10)

Then via
[
4
, Th. 3.2]
, we find that it holds

\displaystyle\overline{F}_{A}(x)=\mathbf{P}[l_{1}\,X_{1}+l_{2}\,X_{2}>x]\sim\mathbf{P}[l_{1}\,X_{1}>x]+\mathbf{P}[l_{2}\,X_{2}>x]\,,

from where we obtain easily that it holds
F_{A}\in\mathcal{B}\in\{\mathcal{C},\,\mathcal{C}\cap\mathcal{P_{D}}\}
. Hence,
F_{A}\in\mathcal{S}
(or respectively,
F_{A}\in\mathcal{A}
), so we conclude that
F\in\mathcal{S}_{A}
(or respectively,
F\in\mathcal{A}_{A}
).

In the previous three examples, we used several forms of ’asymptotic independence’ among the marginal distributions, in order to construct the classes
\mathcal{S}_{A}
and
\mathcal{A}_{A}
.

In the next example we consider arbitrary dependence among the components of the marginals, with the one to belong to
\mathcal{C}
, while the other can belong to
\mathcal{D}
, but simultaneously the tail distribution of the second is asymptotically negligible with respect to the tail distribution of the first.

Example 4.4
.

Let
A
be a set as relation (
4.1
). Let assume that the vector
{\bf X}=(X_{1},\,X_{2})

\displaystyle\lim\dfrac{\mathbf{P}\left[\left(\dfrac{X_{1}-x}{a(x)}\,,\;\dfrac{X_{2}-x}{a(x)}\right)\in{\mathbb{B}}\right]}{\overline{F}_{1}(x)}=\mu({\mathbb{B}})\,,

\displaystyle\lim\dfrac{\overline{F}_{2}(x)}{\overline{F}_{1}(x)}=\mu([-\infty,\,\infty]\times(0,\,\infty])>0\,,

, inspired by

using rotationally invariant random variables.

In what follows, we consider a non-negative random vector
{\bf X}=(X_{1},\,\ldots,\,X_{d})
, following distribution
F
, which satisfies the relation
\mathbf{P}[{\bf X}={\bf 0}]=0
. Furthermore, we also consider the unit sphere
\Delta_{d}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

Example 4.6
.

Let
A\subsetneq\mathscr{R}
, be some fixed set. Following the
[
42
, Exam. 4.17]
, let us define
Z
as uni-dimensional random variable, with distribution
V
, with distribution tail

\displaystyle\overline{V}(x)=\mathbf{P}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{d}X_{i}>x\>\Big|\>\dfrac{{\bf X}}{\sum_{i=1}^{d}X_{i}}\in(\theta_{1},\,\ldots,\,\theta_{d})\right]\,,

(4.18)

for any
(\theta_{1},\,\ldots,\,\theta_{d})\in\Delta_{d}
. Let us also consider a random variable

\displaystyle H=\inf\left\{u>0\>:\>u\,\dfrac{{\bf X}}{\sum_{i=1}^{d}X_{i}}\in A\right\}\,,

(4.19)

for any
A\subsetneq\mathscr{R}
. If the random variables
Z
and
H
are independent, then we define a rotational invariant distribution
F_{A}
, of the random variable
Y_{A}
, where
Y_{A}
is given by

\displaystyle Y_{A}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}Z\,H^{-1}\,,

(4.20)

where by ‘
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}
’ we denote the equality in distribution.

Then, by relation (
4.19
), the random variable
H
is bounded away from zero, hence the inverse
H^{-1}
is bounded from above. Hence, if
V\in\mathcal{A}
, by relation (
4.20
) and the independence between

and

, we can use
[

, Th. 2.1]
to find that it holds

. Thus for any

, that implies

5.
Proof of Theorem
3.1

Now we present the proof of Theorem
3.1
with the help of a preliminary lemma. The following lemma is a generalization from
[
40
, Lem. 6.1]
and requires more general conditions, than these of Theorem
3.1
. In what follows, we use the notation

\displaystyle F_{i}(x\,A)=\mathbf{P}[{\bf X}^{(i)}\in x\,A]\,,\qquad\overline{F}_{A}^{(i)}(x)=\mathbf{P}[Y^{(i)}_{A}>x]\,,

with
Y^{(i)}_{A}=\sup\{u\;:\;{\bf X}^{(i)}\in u\,A\}
, for any
i=1,\,\ldots,\,n
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
A
be some fixed set from
\mathscr{R}
. Consider
n
independent, non-negative, random vectors
{\bf X}^{(1)},\,\ldots,\,{\bf X}^{(n)}
with corresponding distribution
F_{1},\,\ldots,\,F_{n}\in\mathcal{L}_{A}
. We assume that there exists a distribution
F\in\mathcal{S}_{A}
, such that it holds
F_{i}(x\,A)\asymp F(x\,A)
, for any
i=1,\,\ldots,\,n
. Then, for any fixed
0<a\leq b<\infty
, it holds

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}\,{\bf X}^{(i)}\in x\,A\right]\sim\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{P}\left[c_{i}\,{\bf X}^{(i)}\in x\,A\right]\,,

(5.1)

uniformly for any
{\bf c}_{n}:=(c_{1},\,\ldots,\,c_{n})\in[a,\,b]^{n}
.

Proof.

We start with estimation of the upper bound of relation (
5.1
). Using at first the
[
25
, Prop. 2.4]
and continuing with
[
46
, Lem. 1]
, since
F_{A}^{(1)},\,\ldots,\,F_{A}^{(n)}\in\mathcal{L}
,
F_{A}\in\mathcal{S}
and
\overline{F}_{A}^{(i)}(x)\asymp\overline{F}_{A}(x)
for any
i=1,\,\ldots,\,n
, we obtain that it holds

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}{\bf X}^{(i)}\in xA\right]\leq\mathbf{P}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}Y_{A}^{(i)}>x\right]\sim\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{P}\left[c_{i}Y_{A}^{(i)}>x\right]=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{P}\left[c_{i}{\bf X}^{(i)}\in xA\right],

uniformly, for any
{\bf c}_{n}\in[a,\,b]^{n}
.

For the lower bound, because of the fact that
A
is an increasing set, and the random vectors
{\bf X}^{(i)}
are non-negative and independent, by the Bonferroni inequality, we obtain that it holds

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}\,{\bf X}^{(i)}\in x\,A\right]\geq\mathbf{P}\left[\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\{c_{i}\,\,{\bf X}^{(i)}\in x\,A\}\right]\geq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{P}\left[c_{i}\,{\bf X}^{(i)}\in x\,A\right]

\displaystyle-\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\mathbf{P}\left[c_{i}\,{\bf X}^{(i)}\in x\,A\,,\;c_{j}\,{\bf X}^{(j)}\in x\,A\right]=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{P}\left[c_{i}\,Y_{A}^{(i)}>x\right]

(5.3)

\displaystyle-\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\mathbf{P}\left[c_{i}\,Y_{A}^{(i)}>x\,,\;c_{j}\,Y_{A}^{(j)}>x\right]\sim\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{P}\left[c_{i}\,Y_{A}^{(i)}>x\right]=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{P}\left[c_{i}\,{\bf X}^{(i)}\in x\,A\right]\,,

uniformly for any
{\bf c}_{n}\in[a,\,b]^{n}
. Hence, by relations (
5
) and (
5
) we get the (
5.1
).
∎

So, we can prove now Theorem
3.1
.

Proof of Theorem
3.1

Let
M\in{\mathbb{N}}
be some integer and consider some
t\in\Lambda_{T}
. Then, by the total probability theorem, we obtain that

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf D}_{r}(t)\in x\,A\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{N(t)}{\bf X}^{(i)}\,e^{-r\tau_{i}}\in x\,A\right]

(5.4)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{n=1}^{M}+\sum_{n=M+1}^{\infty}\right)\mathbf{P}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\bf X}^{(i)}\,e^{-r\tau_{i}}\in x\,A\,,\;N(t)=n\right]

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle I_{1}(x,\,t,\,M)+I_{2}(x,\,t,\,M)\,,

Let’s start with the estimation of
I_{2}(x,\,t,\,M)
. Because A is increasing, it holds that

\displaystyle I_{2}(x,\,t,\,M)\leq\sum_{n=M+1}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\bf X}^{(i)}\,e^{-r\tau_{1}}\in x\,A\,,\;\tau_{n}\leq t\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=M+1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{P}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\bf X}^{(i)}\,e^{-r\,s}\in x\,A\right]\,\mathbf{P}[\tau_{n-1}\leq t-s]\,\mathbf{P}[\tau_{1}\in ds]

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=M+1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{P}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\bf X}^{(i)}\,e^{-r\,s}\in x\,A\right]\,\mathbf{P}[N(t-s)\geq n-1]\,\mathbf{P}[\tau_{1}\in ds]

\displaystyle\leq k\sum_{n=M+1}^{\infty}\,\mathbf{P}[N(T)\geq n-1](1+\delta)^{n}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}\,e^{-rs}\in x\,A\right]\,\mathbf{P}[\tau_{1}\in ds]

\displaystyle\leq k\left(\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}\,e^{-rs}\in x\,A\right]\,\lambda(ds)\right)\sum_{n=M+1}^{\infty}\,\mathbf{P}[N(T)\geq n-1](1+\delta)^{n}\,,

uniformly for any
t\in\Lambda_{T}
, where for any
\delta>0
there exists some constant
k>0
, from
[
42
, Prop. 4.12(c)]
, since
F\in\mathcal{S}_{A}
. Hence, taking into account that
\{N(t)\,,\;t\geq 0\}
is a renewal process and
T<\infty
, then it has an analytic renewal function near zero, see in
[
43
]
. Therefore, for any
\delta^{\prime}\in(0,\,1)
we can find some large enough
M
, such that it holds

\displaystyle I_{2}(x,\,t,\,M)\leq k\,\delta^{\prime}\,\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}\in x\,e^{rs}\,A\right]\,\lambda(ds)\,,

(5.5)

uniformly, for any
t\in\Lambda_{T}
.

Let’s consider now the first term
I_{1}(x,\,t,\,M)
. In this case, we obtain that

\displaystyle I_{1}(x,\,t,\,M)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{M}\iint_{\{0\leq\tau_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\tau_{n}\leq t<\tau_{n+1}\}}\mathbf{P}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\bf X}^{(i)}\,e^{-rs_{i}}\in x\,A\right]\,\mathbf{P}[\tau_{1}\in ds_{1},\,\ldots,\,\tau_{n+1}\in ds_{n+1}]

\displaystyle\sim\sum_{n=1}^{M}\iint_{\{0\leq\tau_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\tau_{n}\leq t<\tau_{n+1}\}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}^{(i)}\,e^{-rs_{i}}\in x\,A\right]\,\mathbf{P}[\tau_{1}\in ds_{1},\,\ldots,\,\tau_{n+1}\in ds_{n+1}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{M}\,\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}^{(i)}\,e^{-r\tau_{i}}\in x\,A\,,\;N(t)=n\right]

(5.6)

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}-\sum_{n=M+1}^{\infty}\right)\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}^{(i)}\,e^{-r\tau_{i}}\in x\,A\,,\;N(t)=n\right]=:I_{11}(x,\,t,\,M)-I_{12}(x,\,t,\,M)\,,

holds uniformly for any
t\in\Lambda_{T}
, where in the second step we applied Lemma
5.1
, through the dominated convergence theorem.

For the estimation of

, we interchange the order of summation to find that

\displaystyle I_{11}(x,\,t,\,M)\sim\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}^{(i)}\,e^{-r\tau_{i}}\in x\,A\,,\;\tau_{i}\leq t\right]=\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}\in x\,e^{rs}\,A\right]\,\lambda(ds)\,,

(5.7)

holds uniformly for any

. For

\displaystyle I_{12}(x,\,t,\,M)\leq\sum_{n=M+1}^{\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}^{(i)}\,e^{-r\tau_{1}}\in x\,A\,,\;\tau_{n}\leq t\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=M+1}^{\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}\,e^{-rs}\in x\,A\right]\,\mathbf{P}[\tau_{n-1}\leq t-s]\,\mathbf{P}[\tau_{1}\in ds]

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{n=M+1}^{\infty}n\mathbf{P}[N(T)\geq n-1]\right)\,\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}\in x\,e^{rs}\,A\right]\,\lambda(ds)\,,

holds uniformly for any
t\in\Lambda_{T}
. Because
\{N(t)\,,\;t\geq 0\}
is a renewal counting process and

, for any
\delta^{\prime}\in(0,\,1)
there exists some large enough

such that

\displaystyle I_{12}(x,\,t,\,M)\leq\delta^{\prime}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}\in x\,e^{rs}\,A\right]\,\lambda(ds)\,,

(5.8)

holds uniformly for any
t\in\Lambda_{T}
. Thus, by relations (

5.7
), and (

\displaystyle(1-\delta^{\prime})\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}\in x\,e^{rs}\,A\right]\,\lambda(ds)\lesssim I_{1}(x,\,t,\,M)\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}\in x\,e^{rs}\,A\right]\,\lambda(ds)\,,

(5.9)

Lemma 6.1

Let

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf D}_{r}(\infty)\in x\,A\right]\lesssim\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}\in x\,e^{rs}\,A\right]\,\lambda(ds)\,.

Proof.

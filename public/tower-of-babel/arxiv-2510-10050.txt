Quantum many-body analysis of a spin-2 bosons with two-body inelastic decay
Takeshi Takahashi and Hiroki Saito

Abstract
Bose-Einstein condensates (BECs) of Rb 87 {}^{87}\textrm{Rb} atoms with a hyperfine spin of 2 are open quantum systems, where the atoms are lost through two-body inelastic collisions. In this dissipation process, a collision channel with total spin of 4 is forbidden by angular momentum conservation, which results in magnetization of the atoms remaining in the condensate. Here, we investigate the quantum many-body properties of spin-2 bosons that undergo two-body atomic loss. We show that the system finally reaches a steady state, which is a mixture of the states with maximum total spins. In addition, we find that a non-classical steady state can be obtained by quenching the quadratic Zeeman coefficient.



I
Introduction

Atomic Bose-Einstein condensates (BECs) with spin degrees of freedom
Kawaguchi

have been produced experimentally by far-off-resonant optical traps
Stamper-Kurn
.
The spin degrees of freedom enable a rich variety of ground-state phases
Stenger
;
Zhang
;
Murata
and topological excitations
Leanhardt
;
Kumakura
.
For example, skyrmions, monopoles, and knots have been experimentally demonstrated
Leslie
;
Ray1
;
Ray2
;
Hall
;
Lee
.

Spin-2 BECs have attracted much interest
because they exhibit more diverse magnetic phases than spin-1 BECs
Ciobanu
;
Klausen
;
Widera
.
However, for the atomic species used in experiments (e.g.,
{}^{87}\textrm{Rb}
),
the spin-2 hyperfine state has a larger energy than the spin-1 state
and is unstable against transition to the spin-1 state through binary collisions
Kuwamoto
;
Schmaljohann
;
Saito
.
In such inelastic collisions, the hyperfine energy (e.g.,
\simeq 6.8
GHz for
{}^{87}\textrm{Rb}
) transforms to
kinetic energy and the colliding atoms escape from the trap.
Such a system with particle dissipation is an example of an open quantum system
Tojo1
;
Tojo2
;
Eto
.

In the experiment reported in Ref.
Eto
, magnetization was
observed to
dynamically develop in a spin-2 BEC of
{}^{87}\textrm{Rb}
atoms,
even though the spin-dependent contact interaction was not ferromagnetic.
The mechanism involved in such magnetization is spin-dependent atomic loss,
in which two-body inelastic collisions of particles with a total spin
of 4 (
=2+2
) is prohibited
because of angular momentum conservation (i.e., only atomic pairs having different spin directions are lost).
Consequently, the remaining atoms tend to become magnetized in the same direction.

In Ref.
Eto
, the experimental results have been confirmed by numerical calculations using the mean-field approximation.
However, the mean-field picture is inappropriate for systems with a small number of particles.
In fact, when the trap confinement is tight and all atoms occupy the same spatial wave function,
the true ground state of a spin-1 BEC with antiferromagnetic interaction is
a symmetry-preserving state referred to as a fragmented state
Mueller
;
Koashi
;
Ueda
.
Recently, such a quantum many-body state was experimentally observed in a BEC with
\sim 100
atoms
Evrard1
;
Evrard2
.

In the present study, we perform quantum many-body analysis of spin-2
bosons that undergo two-body inelastic loss.
We prepare an initial state in which all atoms occupy the
m=0
magnetic sublevel, as in the experiment
Eto

and investigate the time evolution using the Lindblad master equation
Gardiner
.
We show that the system eventually reaches a steady state, which is a statistical mixture of the states with
maximum total spins (i.e., the final state is fully magnetized).
We perform numerical simulations of the time evolution of the density matrix and
investigate physical quantities in the final state.
In the presence of the quadratic Zeeman effect,
the transverse magnetization is found to be enhanced during the early
stage of evolution of the system.
When the quadratic Zeeman coefficient is quenched,
the final steady state acquires a large transverse magnetization.
This state exhibits a nonclassical magnetization distribution, indicating a Schrödinger-cat-like state.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows.
First, we formulate the open quantum system in Sec.
II

and describe the dynamics of the quantum state using the Lindblad master equation.
In Sec.
III
, we focus on the steady states of this equation
and discuss their general properties.
In Sec.
IV
, we present the results of a numerical analysis
of the dynamics of the system.
The conclusions to this study are provided in Sec.
V
.

II
Formulation of problem

II.1
Master equation with atomic loss

We consider spin-2 bosonic atoms confined in a trap potential
V(\bm{r})
.
The field operators
\hat{\psi}_{m}(\bm{r})
annihilate atoms in the magnetic sublevels

m=-2,-1,0,1,2
, which satisfy the bosonic commutation relations
[\hat{\psi}_{m}(\bm{r}),\hat{\psi}_{m^{\prime}}^{\dagger}(\bm{r}^{\prime})]=\delta(\bm{r}-\bm{r}^{\prime})\delta_{m,m^{\prime}}
.
The non-intaracting component of the Hamiltonian is given by

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{0}=\int\sum_{m=-2}^{2}\left\{\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}_{m}(\bm{r})\left[-\frac{\hbar^{2}\nabla^{2}}{2M}+V(\bm{r})\right]\hat{\psi}_{m}(\bm{r})\right.

(1)

\displaystyle+qm^{2}\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}_{m}(\bm{r})\hat{\psi}_{m}(\bm{r})\bigg\}d\bm{r},

where
M
is the mass of an atom and
q
is the quadratic Zeeman coefficient.
Here, we ignore the linear Zeeman effect because it can be eliminated by the unitary transformation.

The interaction term
\hat{H}_{\textrm{int}}
that represents the elastic collisions is written as

\hat{H}_{\textrm{int}}=\int\sum_{\mathcal{F}=0,2,4}\frac{g_{\mathcal{F}}}{2}\sum_{\mathcal{M}=-\mathcal{F}}^{\mathcal{F}}\hat{A}^{\dagger}_{\mathcal{FM}}(\bm{r})\hat{A}_{\mathcal{FM}}(\bm{r})d\bm{r},

(2)

where
\mathcal{F}
is the total spin of the colliding atoms
and
g_{\mathcal{F}}=4\pi\hbar^{2}a_{\mathcal{F}}/M
with
a_{\mathcal{F}}

being the
s
-wave scattering length of the collision channel with
\mathcal{F}
.
The operator
\mathcal{\hat{A}}_{\mathcal{FM}}
is defined as

\hat{A}_{\mathcal{FM}}(\bm{r})=\sum_{m,m^{\prime}=-2}^{2}C_{m,m^{\prime}}^{\mathcal{FM}}\hat{\psi}_{m}(\bm{r})\hat{\psi}_{m^{\prime}}(\bm{r}),

(3)

where
C_{m,m^{\prime}}^{\mathcal{FM}}
is the Clebsch-Gordan coefficient.
The Hamiltonian for the total system is given by
\hat{H}=\hat{H}_{0}+\hat{H}_{\textrm{int}}
.

Next, we consider two-body inelastic collisions, in which a transition
from a hyperfine spin-2 state to a hyperfine spin-1 state occurs.
The energy difference between hyperfine spin-2 and spin-1 states for
{}^{87}\textrm{Rb}
is
\simeq 6.8
GHz,
and this energy is transferred to the kinetic energy of the colliding
atoms, causing them to escape from the trap.
The dynamics of such an open quantum system with atomic loss can be described by the density matrix
\hat{\rho}
as

\frac{\partial\hat{\rho}}{\partial t}=\frac{1}{i\hbar}[\hat{H},\hat{\rho}]+\left(\frac{\partial\hat{\rho}}{\partial t}\right)_{\textrm{loss}},

(4)

where
(\partial\hat{\rho}/\partial t)_{\textrm{loss}}

represents the atomic loss due to inelastic collisions.
Assuming that the atoms escaping from the trap do not interact with other atoms,
the atomic loss is described by a Markov process.
In this case,
(\partial\hat{\rho}/\partial t)_{\textrm{loss}}

in Eq. (
4
) is given in the Lindblad form as
Tojo2

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial\hat{\rho}}{\partial t}\right)_{\textrm{loss}}=\int\sum_{\mathcal{F}=0,2}\frac{b_{\mathcal{F}}}{4}\sum_{\mathcal{M}=-\mathcal{F}}^{\mathcal{F}}\left[2\hat{A}_{\mathcal{FM}}(\bm{r})\hat{\rho}\hat{A}_{\mathcal{FM}}^{\dagger}(\bm{r})\right.

(5)

\displaystyle\left.-\hat{A}_{\mathcal{FM}}^{\dagger}(\bm{r})\hat{A}_{\mathcal{FM}}(\bm{r})\hat{\rho}-\hat{\rho}\hat{A}_{\mathcal{FM}}^{\dagger}(\bm{r})\hat{A}_{\mathcal{FM}}(\bm{r})\right]d\bm{r},

where
b_{\mathcal{F}}
is the loss coefficient for a collision channel with total spin
\mathcal{F}
.
Notably, for
\mathcal{F}=4
, inelastic collisions are forbidden
(i.e.,
b_{4}=0
) because of the conservation of spin angular momentum.
The first term in the square bracket in Eq. (
5
) is called
a jump term, which eliminates two particles from the system.

II.2
Previous results

Here, we briefly review the results of a previous study on a spin-2
{}^{87}{\rm Rb}
BEC with particle dissipation
Eto
.
In this study, all atoms were initially in the
m=0
state and the subsequent dynamics was investigated.
It was observed that the system eventually became fully magnetized in the transverse direction
despite the ferromagnetic state being energetically unfavorable
Widera
.
This is due to the spin-dependent inelastic collisions (
b_{4}=0
);
that is, atoms with different spin directions are selectively lost.

A numerical analysis was also performed in Ref.
Eto
, in which Eq. (
4
) was
reduced to the dissipative Gross-Pitaevskii equation.
In the mean-field approximation,
the macroscopic wave function
\psi_{m}(\bm{r})\equiv\textrm{Tr}[\hat{\psi}_{m}(\bm{r})\hat{\rho}]
obeys
Tojo2

\displaystyle i\hbar\frac{\partial\psi_{m}}{\partial t}=\left[-\frac{\hbar^{2}\nabla^{2}}{2M}+V(\bm{r})+qm^{2}\right]\psi_{m}(\bm{r})

(6)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{F}=0,2,4\\
\mathcal{M}=-\mathcal{F},...,\mathcal{F}\end{subarray}}\tilde{g}_{\mathcal{F}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j,k=-2\end{subarray}}^{2}C_{m,i}^{\mathcal{FM}}C_{j,k}^{\mathcal{FM}}\psi_{i}^{\star}(\bm{r})\psi_{j}(\bm{r})\psi_{k}(\bm{r}),

where
\tilde{g}_{\mathcal{F}}=g_{\mathcal{F}}-i\hbar b_{\mathcal{F}}/2
for
\mathcal{F}=0,2
, and
\tilde{g}_{4}=g_{4}
.
In Ref.
Eto
, the numerical solutions of Eq. (
6
) were
found to agree well with the experimental results.
However, the mean-field approximation is only valid for a large number of atoms and we do not use Eq. (
6
) in the present study.

II.3
Single-mode approximation

We consider a situation in which the trap is tight and the size of the system is much smaller than the spin healing length.
In this case, all particles occupy the same spatial wave function with a uniform spin state and we can use the single-mode approximation.
The field operators can be rewritten as

\hat{\psi}_{m}(\bm{r})\simeq\hat{a}_{m}\psi_{\textrm{SMA}}(\bm{r}),

(7)

where
\hat{a}_{m}
are bosonic operators and the wave function
\psi_{\textrm{SMA}}(\bm{r})

is the ground state of
[-\hbar^{2}\nabla^{2}/(2M)+V]\psi_{\rm SMA}=\varepsilon_{0}\psi_{\rm SMA}
satisfying
the normalization condition
\int|\psi_{\textrm{SMA}}(\bm{r})|^{2}d\bm{r}=1
.
Substituting Eq. (
7
) into Eqs. (
1
) and (
2
), we obtain

\displaystyle\hat{H}=

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{0}\hat{N}+q\sum_{m=-2}^{2}m^{2}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{m}\hat{a}_{m}

(8)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\mathcal{F}=0,2,4}\frac{g_{\mathcal{F}}}{2V^{\textrm{eff}}}\sum_{\mathcal{M}=-\mathcal{F}}^{\mathcal{F}}\hat{A}^{\dagger}_{\mathcal{FM}}\hat{A}_{\mathcal{FM}},

where
\hat{N}=\sum_{m}\hat{a}_{m}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{m}
,
V^{\rm eff}=\left[\int|\psi_{\textrm{SMA}}(\bm{r}))|^{4}d\bm{r}\right]^{-1}
, and

\hat{A}_{\mathcal{FM}}=\sum_{m,m^{\prime}=-2}^{2}C_{m,m^{\prime}}^{\mathcal{FM}}\hat{a}_{m}\hat{a}_{m^{\prime}}.

(9)

Similarly, substituting Eq. (
7
) into Eq. (
5
) gives

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial\hat{\rho}}{\partial t}\right)_{\textrm{loss}}=\sum_{\mathcal{F}=0,2}\frac{b_{\mathcal{F}}}{4V^{\textrm{eff}}}\sum_{\mathcal{M}=-\mathcal{F}}^{\mathcal{F}}\left(2\hat{A}_{\mathcal{FM}}\hat{\rho}\hat{A}_{\mathcal{FM}}^{\dagger}\right.

(10)

\displaystyle\left.-\hat{A}_{\mathcal{FM}}^{\dagger}\hat{A}_{\mathcal{FM}}\hat{\rho}-\hat{\rho}\hat{A}_{\mathcal{FM}}^{\dagger}\hat{A}_{\mathcal{FM}}\right).

III
GENERAL FORMS OF STEADY STATES

This section provides a general discussion of the steady state for the Lindblad equation (
4
) with a single-mode approximation.
The steady states satisfy
\partial\hat{\rho}/\partial t=0
.
In the present case, the unitary and dissipation terms on the
right-hand side of Eq. (
4
) must each be zero in a steady state.

First, let us consider the condition for

\left(\frac{\partial\hat{\rho}}{\partial t}\right)_{\textrm{unitary}}=\frac{1}{i\hbar}[\hat{H},\hat{\rho}]=0.

(11)

Denoting the energy eigenstates of
\hat{H}
by
|n\rangle
and their corresponding energy eigenvalues by
E_{n}
,
we can generally write the density matrix as

\hat{\rho}=\sum_{n,m}c_{nm}|n\rangle\langle m|,

(12)

where
c_{nm}
is a positive semidefinite Hermitian matrix with unit trace.
Equation (
11
) can be written as

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial\hat{\rho}}{\partial t}\right)_{\textrm{unitary}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{i\hbar}\sum_{n,m}(E_{n}-E_{m})c_{nm}|n\rangle\langle m|

(13)

\displaystyle=0.

Therefore, the density matrix that satisfies Eq. (
11
) is given by Eq. (
12
) with
E_{n}=E_{m}
for all
n
and
m
.

The energy eigenstates of
\hat{H}
are expressed in the form
|N_{0},N_{S},F,F_{z}\rangle
for
q=0
,
where
N_{S}
is the number of particles forming singlet pairs,
F
is the total spin, and
F_{z}
is the magnetic quantum number.
The total number of particles is
N=N_{0}+2N_{S}
.
The energy eigenvalue is given by
Koashi

\displaystyle E=

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{0}N+\frac{c_{0}}{2}N(N-1)

(14)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{c_{1}}{2}[F(F+1)-6N]+\frac{c_{2}}{5}N_{S}(N+N_{0}+3),

where
c_{0},c_{1}
, and
c_{2}
are defined as

\displaystyle c_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{4g_{2}+3g_{4}}{7},

(15)

\displaystyle c_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{g_{4}-g_{2}}{7},

\displaystyle c_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{7g_{0}-10g_{2}+3g_{4}}{7}.

We note that the energy eigenstates
|N_{0},N_{S},F,F_{z}\rangle
are independent of the values of
g_{0},g_{2}
and
g_{4}
.

Second, let us consider the condition for

\left(\frac{\partial\hat{\rho}}{\partial t}\right)_{\textrm{loss}}=0,

(16)

where the left-hand side is given by Eq. (
10
).
We will show that such states are mixtures of energy eigenstates with the maximum total spin
F
,

|F=2N,F_{z}\rangle\equiv|N_{0}=N,N_{S}=0,F=2N,F_{z}\rangle,

(17)

where
-2N\leq F_{z}\leq 2N
.
In the following, we write the density matrix in the
N
-particle subspace as
\hat{\rho}_{N}

and express the density matrix for the total Hilbert space as

\hat{\rho}=\oplus_{N=0}^{N_{\textrm{ini}}}\hat{\rho}_{N},

(18)

where
N_{\textrm{ini}}
is the maximum number of atoms contained in the initial state.
We will prove that
\hat{\rho}_{N}
has the form

\hat{\rho}_{N}=\sum_{F_{z},F_{z}^{\prime}}c_{F_{z}F_{z}^{\prime}}|F=2N,F_{z}\rangle\langle F=2N,F_{z}^{\prime}|,

(19)

where
c_{F_{z}F_{z}^{\prime}}
is a positive semidefinite Hermitian matrix.

To begin with, we prove that Eq. (
19
) is a sufficient condition for Eq. (
16
).
The states in Eq. (
17
) are expressed as
Koashi

\displaystyle|F=2N,F_{z}\rangle=Z^{-1/2}(\hat{F}_{-})^{\Delta F}(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{2})^{N}|vac\rangle,

(20)

where
\hat{F}_{-}=2(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{1}\hat{a}_{2}+\hat{a}_{-2}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{-1})+\sqrt{6}(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{0}\hat{a}_{1}+\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{-1}\hat{a}_{0})
,

\Delta F=2N-F_{z}
,
Z=N!(4N)!\Delta F!/(4N-\Delta F)!
is a normalization constant,
and
|vac\rangle
is the vacuum state.
From the form of
\hat{A}_{00}=(\hat{a}_{0}\hat{a}_{0}-2\hat{a}_{1}\hat{a}_{-1}+2\hat{a}_{2}\hat{a}_{-2})/\sqrt{5}
, we find
\hat{A}_{00}(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{2})^{N}|vac\rangle=0
and
[\hat{A}_{00},\hat{F}_{-}]=0
,
which gives
\hat{A}_{00}(\hat{F}_{-})^{\Delta F}(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{2})^{N}|vac\rangle=0
.
Similarly,
\hat{A}_{2\mathcal{M}}
satisfies
\hat{A}_{2\mathcal{M}}(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{2})^{N}|vac\rangle=0
.
From the commutation relations between
\hat{A}_{2\mathcal{M}}
and
\hat{F}_{-}
,

\displaystyle\left[\hat{A}_{2,2},\hat{F}_{-}\right]=0,

(21)

\displaystyle\left[\hat{A}_{2,1},\hat{F}_{-}\right]=2\hat{A}_{2,2},

\displaystyle\left[\hat{A}_{2,0},\hat{F}_{-}\right]=\sqrt{6}\hat{A}_{2,1},

\displaystyle\left[\hat{A}_{2,-1},\hat{F}_{-}\right]=2\sqrt{6}\hat{A}_{2,0},

\displaystyle\left[\hat{A}_{2,-2},\hat{F}_{-}\right]=4\hat{A}_{2,-1},

we obtain
\hat{A}_{2\mathcal{M}}(\hat{F}_{-})^{\Delta F}(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{2})^{N}|vac\rangle=0
.
Thus, we have shown that

\hat{A}_{\mathcal{FM}}|F=2N,F_{z}\rangle=0,

(22)

for
\mathcal{F}=0
and 2, and therefore, Eq. (
19
) satisfies Eq. (
16
).

Next, we show that Eq. (
19
) is a necessary condition for Eq. (
16
).
First, we prove the lemma that Eq. (
16
) leads to

\hat{A}_{\mathcal{FM}}\hat{\rho}_{N}\hat{A}_{\mathcal{FM}}^{\dagger}=0

(23)

for
\mathcal{F}=0,2
and for all possible
N
.
We start from the dissipation from the
N=2
space to the
N=0
space.
From
\hat{A}_{\mathcal{FM}}^{\dagger}\hat{A}_{\mathcal{FM}}\rho_{0}=\rho_{0}\hat{A}_{\mathcal{FM}}^{\dagger}\hat{A}_{\mathcal{FM}}=0
, Eq. (
16
) gives

\sum_{\mathcal{F}=0,2}\frac{b_{\mathcal{F}}}{4}\sum_{\mathcal{M}=-\mathcal{F}}^{\mathcal{F}}2\hat{A}_{\mathcal{FM}}\hat{\rho}_{2}\hat{A}_{\mathcal{FM}}^{\dagger}=0.

(24)

Taking the trace of both sides of Eq. (
24
), we obtain

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\textrm{Tr}\left[\sum_{\mathcal{F}=0,2}\frac{b_{\mathcal{F}}}{4}\sum_{\mathcal{M}=-\mathcal{F}}^{\mathcal{F}}2\hat{A}_{\mathcal{FM}}\hat{\rho}_{2}\hat{A}_{\mathcal{FM}}^{\dagger}\right]

(25)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathcal{F}=0,2}\frac{b_{\mathcal{F}}}{4}\sum_{\mathcal{M}=-\mathcal{F}}^{\mathcal{F}}2\textrm{Tr}\left[\hat{A}_{\mathcal{FM}}^{\dagger}\hat{A}_{\mathcal{FM}}\hat{\rho}_{2}\right]

\displaystyle=0.

From the positive definiteness of
\hat{A}_{\mathcal{FM}}^{\dagger}\hat{A}_{\mathcal{FM}}
, each term of the sum in Eq (
25
) must vanish;
therefore, Eq. (
23
) is satisfied for
N=2
.
For the dissipation from
N=4
to
N=2
,

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathcal{F}=0,2}\frac{b_{\mathcal{F}}}{4}

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathcal{M}=-\mathcal{F}}^{\mathcal{F}}\left(2\hat{A}_{\mathcal{FM}}\hat{\rho}_{4}\hat{A}_{\mathcal{FM}}^{\dagger}\right.

(26)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\left.\hat{A}_{\mathcal{FM}}^{\dagger}\hat{A}_{\mathcal{FM}}\hat{\rho}_{2}-\hat{\rho}_{2}\hat{A}_{\mathcal{FM}}^{\dagger}\hat{A}_{\mathcal{FM}}\right)=0

holds from Eq. (
16
), where the second and third terms in the bracket vanish according to Eq. (
25
).
Therefore, by a similar procedure, we can show that Eq. (
23
) also holds for
N=4
.
By mathematical induction, Eq. (
23
) holds for
N=2,4,...,N_{\textrm{ini}}
.
Also, for odd values of
N
, we can similarly show that Eq. (
23
) holds.
Thus, when the density matrix
\hat{\rho}_{N}
satisfies Eq. (
16
),
Eq. (
23
) holds for all possible
N
.

In general, the density matrix
\hat{\rho}_{N}
is written as a mixture of pure states
\{|\psi_{N}^{(k)}\rangle\}_{k=1,2,...,K}
as

\hat{\rho}_{N}=\sum_{k=1}^{K}P_{N}^{(k)}|\psi_{N}^{(k)}\rangle\langle\psi_{N}^{(k)}|,

(27)

where
P_{N}^{(k)}
is positive.
When the density matrix satisfying Eq. (
23
) is expressed by Eq. (
27
),

\displaystyle\hat{A}_{\mathcal{FM}}|\psi_{N}^{(k)}\rangle

\displaystyle=0~~~~(\mathcal{F}=0,2),

(28)

must hold for each pure state
|\psi_{N}^{(k)}\rangle
.
The states in Eq. (
17
) satisfy Eq. (
28
) as shown in Eq. (
22
).
We can show that no other states satisfy Eq. (
28
) as follows.
Equation (
28
) leads to

\sum_{\mathcal{F}=0,2}\frac{g_{\mathcal{F}}}{2V^{\textrm{eff}}}\sum_{\mathcal{M}=-\mathcal{F}}^{\mathcal{F}}\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{\dagger}_{\mathcal{FM}}\hat{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{FM}}|\psi_{N}^{(k)}\rangle=0.

Since the operator on the left-hand side has the same form as the Hamiltonian in Eq. (

) with

, the eigenvalue

is given by

\displaystyle\lambda=

\displaystyle\frac{g_{2}}{7}[2N(2N+1)-F(F+1)]

(30)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{7g_{0}-10g_{2}}{7}N_{S}(N+N_{0}+3)

from Eq. (

The quantum numbers

and

that give

are only

and

; that is, Eq. (

Therefore, Eq. (

Thus, Eq. (

In conclusion, the steady state that satisfies Eqs. (

Finally, we consider the steady state for

Since Eq. (

\displaystyle=-2N\rangle

, which requires
\sim 10^{9}
matrix elements to represent the density matrix.
We consider an atomic gas of
{}^{87}\mathrm{Rb}
confined in an isotropic harmonic potential with a trap frequency
\omega/(2\pi)=2~\mathrm{kHz}
.
We use the single-particle ground-state wave function for the single-mode approximation, and the effective volume
is given by
V^{\textrm{eff}}\simeq 2.2\times 10^{-13}~\mathrm{cm}^{3}
.
The interaction and dissipation parameters are set to the experimentally measured values given in Table
1
.
For these interaction parameters, the ground state of the Hamiltonian for
q=0
is the state where all atoms form singlet pairs.

As the system evolves over time,
the total density matrix for the system has the form
\hat{\rho}=\oplus_{N=0}^{20}\hat{\rho}_{N}
, where
N
are even integers.
The expectation value of an operator
\hat{A}
is given by
\langle\hat{A}\rangle=\textrm{Tr}[\hat{A}\hat{\rho}]/\textrm{Tr}[\hat{\rho}]
,
and that of
\hat{A}
within the
N
-particle subspace is defined by
\langle\hat{A}\rangle_{N}=\textrm{Tr}[\hat{A}\hat{\rho}_{N}]/\textrm{Tr}[\hat{\rho}_{N}]
.
The spin operator in the
j
-direction (
j=x,y,z
) is expressed as

\hat{F}_{j}=\sum_{m,m^{\prime}=-2}^{2}[f_{j}]_{m,m^{\prime}}\hat{a}_{m}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{m^{\prime}}
,
where
f_{j}
are spin-2 matrices.
For the present initial state,
\langle\hat{F}_{j}\rangle
and
\langle\hat{F}_{j}\rangle_{N}
always vanish as the system evolves.
We define the transverse magnetization as
S_{\perp}=\sqrt{\langle\hat{F}_{x}^{2}+\hat{F}_{y}^{2}\rangle}
.
To examine whether the state converges to the fully magnetized steady state in Eq. (
18
) with Eq. (
17
),
we introduce the normalized spin-squared operator as

\hat{\textbf{F}}^{2}_{\textrm{norm}}=\frac{\hat{F}_{x}^{2}+\hat{F}_{y}^{2}+\hat{F}_{z}^{2}}{2\hat{N}(2\hat{N}+1)}.

(34)

To avoid divergence arising from
N=0
,
we define the density matrix restricted to the
N>0
subspace as
\hat{\rho}_{N>0}=\oplus_{N=2}^{20}\hat{\rho}_{N}
.
The expectation value of Eq. (
34
) is taken with respect to this restricted state as

F^{2}_{\textrm{norm}}=\frac{\textrm{Tr}[\hat{\textbf{F}}^{2}_{\textrm{norm}}\hat{\rho}_{N>0}]}{\textrm{Tr}[\hat{\rho}_{N>0}]}.

(35)

This quantity becomes unity for the state in Eq. (
18
) with Eq. (
17
).
Let
P_{N}
be the probability of finding the system in a state with
N
particles,

P_{N}=\frac{\textrm{Tr}[\hat{\rho}_{N}]}{\textrm{Tr}[\hat{\rho}]}.

(36)

We define the probability of measuring a magnetization of
m
within the
N
-particle subspace as

Q_{m}^{(N)}=\frac{\textrm{Tr}[\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{m}\hat{\rho}_{N}]}{\textrm{Tr}[\hat{\rho}_{N}]},

(37)

where
\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{m}
is an operator describing the projection onto the eigenstates of
\hat{F}_{z}
with an eigenvalue
m
.

V
Conclusions

We have investigated the dynamics of spin-2 bosons with atomic dissipation.
In the previous study
Eto
,
it was shown experimentally and numerically that the dissipation can enhance the transverse magnetization in the time evolution,
and the results of the mean-field calculations were found to agree
well with the experiments.
By contrast, the present work focused on the quantum many-body nature of a system with a small number of particles.
We proved that the steady states for this system are restricted to statistical mixtures of states with maximum total spins, given by Eq. (18) with Eq. (17).
We performed numerical simulations using the master equation with and without the quadratic Zeeman term.
Without the quadratic Zeeman term, we found that the system indeed
reached a steady state with the maximum total spins.
When the quadratic Zeeman term was removed in the early stages of time
evolution, a relatively large number of particles were found to remain in the steady state.
We concluded that this state resembles a Schrödinger-cat-like state,
formed by the superposition of mean-field-like states.
This suggests that particle dissipation not only destroys the quantum properties but also creates nontrivial quantum many-body states.
Although in the present study we considered
{}^{87}\textrm{Rb}
atoms, other atomic species, such as
{}^{23}\textrm{Na}
atoms,
may be experimentally suitable, since their inelastic decay rates are much larger
Gorlitz
;
Julienne
;
Burke
and steady states can be reached much faster.

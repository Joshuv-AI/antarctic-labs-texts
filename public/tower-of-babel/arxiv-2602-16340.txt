The Implicit Bias of Adam and Muon on Smooth Homogeneous Neural Networks
Eitan Gronich Gal Vardi Weizmann Institute of Science {eitan.gronich, gal.vardi}@weizmann.ac.il

Abstract
We study the implicit bias of momentum-based optimizers on homogeneous models. We first extend existing results on the implicit bias of steepest descent in homogeneous models to normalized steepest descent with an optional learning rate schedule. We then show that for smooth homogeneous models, momentum steepest descent algorithms like Muon (spectral norm), MomentumGD ( ℓ 2 \ell_{2} norm), and Signum ( ℓ ∞ \ell_{\infty} norm) are approximate steepest descent trajectories under a decaying learning rate schedule, proving that these algorithms too have a bias towards KKT points of the corresponding margin maximization problem. We extend the analysis to Adam (without the stability constant), which maximizes the ℓ ∞ \ell_{\infty} margin, and to Muon-Signum and Muon-Adam, which maximize a hybrid norm. Our experiments corroborate the theory and show that the identity of the margin maximized depends on the choice of optimizer. Overall, our results extend earlier lines of work on steepest descent in homogeneous models and momentum-based optimizers in linear models.



1
Introduction

Deep neural networks show remarkable generalization performance despite often being overparameterized, and even when trained with no explicit regularization. A well-established line of work attempts to explain this phenomenon with the notion of the
implicit
bias (tendency) of gradient-based optimization algorithms to converge to well-generalizing solutions. This bias is often realized in the form of maximizing a certain
margin
for the training points (cf.
46
).

While earlier works studied mostly gradient descent and showed its implicit bias towards maximizing the
\ell_{2}
margin in increasingly complex models, recent years have witnessed an interest in the study of the implicit bias of other optimizers, such as Adam
(
23
)
, AdamW
(
27
)
, and recently Muon
(
22
)
, hand-in-hand with their rising popularity. Indeed, as these algorithms are used near-universally for training large language models (LLMs) and vision transformers, there is a growing imperative to understand their inner workings.

In this work, we study smooth homogeneous models and show a margin-maximization bias of Adam and Muon. Previous work analyzed the implicit bias of Adam and Muon on linear predictors
(
50
;
13
)
, and we extend these results to the substantially broader class of smooth homogeneous models.
Moreover, our analysis of Muon is a special case of a more general framework that we develop, which is applicable to all momentum-based optimizers built on top of steepest descent algorithms. All of our results hold for a family of exponentially tailed losses that includes the logistic and exponential losses.

Our main contributions are as follows:

1.

We show that any limit point of
\frac{\bm{\theta}_{t}}{\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert}
in a
normalized
steepest descent trajectory with a
learning rate schedule

\eta(t)
is a KKT point of the
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert
-max-margin problem, as long as
\int_{0}^{\infty}\eta(t)dt=\infty
. This result holds for any locally Lipschitz,
C^{1}
-stratifiable homogeneous model, including ReLU networks. It extends a result by
44
that considered (unnormalized) steepest descent with a constant learning rate.

2.

We show that when
\frac{\bm{\theta}_{t}}{\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert}
converges, it converges to the direction of a KKT point of the
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert
-max-margin problem even for trajectories which
approximate
steepest descent. This allows us to focus on momentum-based optimizers on smooth homogeneous models and show:

(a)

Muon has an implicit bias towards margin maximization with respect to a norm defined using spectral norms of the weight matrices, under a decaying learning rate regime. In fact, the bias towards margin maximization holds for any normalized Momentum Steepest Descent (MSD) algorithm, for the appropriate norm. We show this includes composite MSD algorithms such as Muon-Signum. In addition, we prove an implicit bias of Muon-Adam.

(b)

Adam (without the stability constant) has an implicit bias towards
\ell_{\infty}
margin maximization under a decaying learning rate regime.

Related Work

43

\ell_{2}
margin. This result
was followed by several works on margin maximization in linear fully-connected, convolutional and diagonal networks (e.g.,
20
;
16
;
49
;

\frac{\bm{\theta}_{t}}{\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert}
is the direction of a KKT point of the max-margin problem. In a complementary result,
21
showed that directional convergence of the parameters is indeed guaranteed when optimizing homogeneous models definable in an o-minimal structure with gradient descent.
The implicit bias of gradient descent in certain non-homogeneous neural networks was studied in
35
;
24
;
6
.
For a more comprehensive survey on the implicit bias of gradient descent, see
46
.

A general treatment of the implicit bias of the family of steepest descent algorithms was given for linear models by
17
, who proved maximization of the appropriate norm-dependent margin.
44
generalized that result and the result by
28
and proved for homogeneous models under steepest descent that any limit point of
\frac{\bm{\theta}_{t}}{\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert}
is a KKT point of the max-margin problem.

Adam in the context of homogeneous models was studied by
47
, who showed a bias towards
\ell_{2}
-margin maximization. Notably, this work studied Adam without
momentum in the numerator and with a stability constant
\varepsilon
which asymptotically dominates the denominator, driving behavior to be similar to gradient descent. Follow-up works have argued that the analysis of the implicit bias with the stability constant is less faithful to the characteristics of Adam in practice, as the stability constant is typically negligible throughout the trajectory.
Such works on Adam without the stability constant have so far focused on linear models and include
50
and
13

\ell_{\infty}

2

50

48
, who showed that limit points of the trajectory are KKT points of the loss under the constraint that the
\ell_{\infty}
norm of the parameters is bounded.

13

2.1
Setting and Notations

(1)

) and logistic (

) losses.
We denote for brevity
z_{i}(\bm{\theta})=y_{i}f(\mathbf{x}_{i};\bm{\theta})
and
q_{\mathrm{min}}(\bm{\theta})=\min_{i\in[m]}z_{i}(\bm{\theta})
. For a trajectory
\bm{\theta}_{t}
we often write
z_{i}^{t}=z_{i}(\bm{\theta}_{t}),q_{\mathrm{min}}^{t}=q_{\mathrm{min}}(\bm{\theta}_{t})
or

is clear from context.

For a vector
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
we denote by
\mathbf{v}[j]
the
j
’th coordinate of
\mathbf{v}
.
For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we denote
[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}
.
We denote by
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{p}
the
\ell_{p}
norm for
p\in[1,\infty]
. For an arbitrary norm
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert
we denote by
{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}_{\star}
the dual norm, defined by
{\left\lVert\mathbf{x}\right\rVert}_{\star}=\max_{\left\lVert\mathbf{u}\right\rVert=1}\left\langle\mathbf{u},\mathbf{x}\right\rangle

{\left\lVert W\right\rVert}_{\mathrm{sp}}
the standard spectral norm of a matrix
W
, and
{\left\lVert(W_{1},...,W_{K})\right\rVert}_{\mathrm{msp}}:=\max_{k\in[K]}{\left\lVert W_{k}\right\rVert}_{\mathrm{sp}}
(short for max-spectral). We use the standard asymptotic notations
\mathcal{O},\Omega,\Theta,\mathrm{o},\mathrm{\omega}
. We denote by
C^{k}(X)
for
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n},n\in\mathbb{N}
the class of
k
-times continuously differentiable functions from
X
to
\mathbb{R}
. By
\log u
we refer to the natural logarithm. By
\esslim,\essliminf,\esslimsup
we refer to

limits holding up to sets of measure
0
.

2.2
Optimizers

The optimization algorithms we study are derivatives of the
steepest descent
family, a generalization of gradient descent defined with respect to a norm
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert
(and its dual norm
{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}_{\star}
). We study the infinitesimal step size (flow) limit of the optimization trajectories. We define steepest descent and its normalized variant in the general case of a subdifferentiable model
f
, allowing for a learning rate schedule
\eta(t)>0
, as follows:

Steepest Descent:

\frac{d\bm{\theta}_{t}}{dt}\in\left\{\eta(t)\cdot\arg\min_{\left\lVert\mathbf{u}\right\rVert={\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}}\left\langle\mathbf{u},\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rangle\mid\mathbf{g}_{t}\in\partial\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta}_{t})\right\}~,

(2)

Normalized Steepest Descent:

\frac{d\bm{\theta}_{t}}{dt}\in\left\{\eta(t)\cdot\arg\min_{\left\lVert\mathbf{u}\right\rVert=1}\left\langle\mathbf{u},\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rangle\mid\mathbf{g}_{t}\in\partial\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta}_{t})\right\}~,

(3)

for almost every
t\geq 0
, where
\partial\mathcal{L}
is the Clarke subdifferential of
\mathcal{L}
(see Appendix
A
), which reduces to
\nabla\mathcal{L}
wherever
f
is differentiable. Notably,
gradient descent
and
coordinate descent
are recovered with
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert=\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{2},\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{1}
respectively from Equation
2
, and
sign gradient descent
is recovered with
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert=\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\infty}
from Equation
3
.

Introducing momentum-based optimizers, we consider a choice of subgradients
\mathbf{g}_{t}\in\partial\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta}_{t})
along the trajectory, and denote the momentum estimate by the following ODE with the given explicit solution:

\begin{gathered}\frac{d\mathbf{m}_{t}}{dt}=c_{1}(\mathbf{g}_{t}-\mathbf{m}_{t}),\quad\mathbf{m}_{0}=\mathbf{0}\\
\left(\text{Explicitly:}\quad\mathbf{m}_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}c_{1}e^{-c_{1}(t-s)}\mathbf{g}_{s}ds\right)~.\end{gathered}

(4)

For the full derivation of the above continuous analogue of momentum, see Appendix
B
. Here,
c_{1}>0
is the
momentum smoothing parameter
, and
\frac{1}{c_{1}}
is the characteristic time frame in which past gradients are accumulated (
c_{1}
is analogous to
-\log(\beta_{1})
for a discrete momentum parameter
\beta_{1}\in(0,1)
, and is roughly
1-\beta_{1}
when
\beta_{1}
is close to 1). We now define
momentum steepest descent
and its normalized counterpart:

Momentum Steepest Descent:

\frac{d\bm{\theta}_{t}}{dt}\in\left\{\eta(t)\cdot\arg\min_{\left\lVert\mathbf{u}\right\rVert={\left\lVert\mathbf{m}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}}\left\langle\mathbf{u},\mathbf{m}_{t}\right\rangle\right\}~,

(5)

Normalized Momentum Steepest Descent:

\frac{d\bm{\theta}_{t}}{dt}\in\left\{\eta(t)\cdot\arg\min_{\left\lVert\mathbf{u}\right\rVert=1}\left\langle\mathbf{u},\mathbf{m}_{t}\right\rangle\right\}~.

(6)

Classical gradient descent with momentum, for example, is obtained from Equation (
5
) with
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert=\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{2}
.
Muon
, the recently proposed weight-matrix optimizer
(
22
)
, applies Newton-Schulz orthogonalization iterations on a momentum estimate of the matrix. In our work (as in
13
), Muon refers to the exact orthogonalization setting rather than the Netwon-Schulz approximation (i.e.,
U\Sigma V^{T}\mapsto UV^{T}
where
U\Sigma V^{T}
is the SVD of the weight matrix
W
). Muon with exact orthogonalization is recovered from Equation
6
with
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert={\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}_{\mathrm{sp}}
for a single-layer network. When running Muon simultaneously on each weight matrix of a multi-layer network, the resulting trajectory follows Equation
6
with
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert={\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}_{\mathrm{msp}}
(see notations in Subsection
2.1
).
3
noted that Shampoo with accumulation disabled is, too, spectral descent, although accumulation in Shampoo is not identical to momentum. Another algorithm adhering exactly to Equation (
6
) is
Signum

(
4
)
, i.e. momentum sign gradient descent (
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert=\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\infty}
).

The final optimizer we discuss is
Adam
, for which we define similarly to the above

\begin{gathered}\frac{d\mathbf{v}_{t}}{dt}=c_{2}(\mathbf{g}_{t}^{2}-\mathbf{v}_{t}),\quad\mathbf{v}_{0}=\mathbf{0}\\
\left(\text{Explicitly:}\quad\mathbf{v}_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}c_{2}e^{-c_{2}(t-s)}\mathbf{g}_{s}^{2}ds\right)~,\end{gathered}

(7)

where the square is taken element-wise.
Following
50
;
13
;
2
;
48
, we consider Adam without the stability constant, as this setting more accurately reflects the behavior of Adam in practice, where the stability constant is typically negligible throughout the trajectory. Therefore we define Adam as the following:

\text{{Adam:}}\quad\frac{d\bm{\theta}_{t}}{dt}=-\eta(t)\cdot\frac{\hat{\mathbf{m}}_{t}}{\sqrt{\hat{\mathbf{v}}_{t}}}~,

(8)

where
\hat{\mathbf{m}}_{t}=(1-e^{-c_{1}t})^{-1}\mathbf{m}_{t}
and
\hat{\mathbf{v}}_{t}=(1-e^{-c_{2}t})^{-1}\mathbf{v}_{t}
are bias-corrected terms, and division and square root are taken element-wise. Adam in the discrete case (
23
) is defined using parameters
\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\in[0,1)
, where
\beta_{1}\leq\beta_{2}
(see definition for the discrete case in Appendix
B
).
1
1

1

Pytorch
(
39
)
defaults are
\beta_{1}=0.9,\beta_{2}=0.999

Due to the inverse relation between

2.3
Assumptions

We now introduce the assumptions made in this work. Note that some assumptions overlap, and not all assumptions are used in all sections of the work.

Model Assumptions.

Our main contributions include the following assumptions on
f
:

(M1)

f
is smooth in
\bm{\theta}
, i.e.
\forall\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:f(\mathbf{x};\cdot)\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{p})

(M2)

\forall\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\bm{\theta}\in\mathbb{R}^{p},\alpha>0:f(\mathbf{x};\alpha\bm{\theta})=\alpha^{L}f(\mathbf{x};\bm{\theta})

This includes (deep) linear networks, for which implicit bias has been extensively studied (e.g.,

;

One example for an activation function that induces non-linear smooth homogeneous networks is
\text{ReLU}^{q}(z):=\max\{0,z\}^{q}
, for any constant

(networks with this activation function have been studied in, e.g.,
7
;
31
;
32
;
8
). Another example for a smooth homogeneous activation is the quadratic activation

, which has been studied in many prior works (e.g.,
42
;
12
;
15
;
41
;
33
;
29
;

;

The results for normalized steepest descent have weaker model assumptions:

(M1-Weak)

-stratifiable (thereby admitting a chain rule), see Appendix

(M2-Weak)

In fact, all of our results allow Assumption

Learning Rate Assumptions.

We detail the different sets of assumptions on the learning rate schedule
\eta(t)
, according to the setting (normalized steepest descent, momentum steepest descent and Adam).

(LR-NSD)

\eta(t)
satisfies
\int_{0}^{\infty}\eta(t)dt=\infty
.

(LR-MSD)

\eta(t)
satisfies
\int_{0}^{\infty}\eta(t)dt=\infty

\eta(t)\leq\mathrm{o}\left(t^{\frac{1}{L}-1}\right)
, where
L\geq 1
is from
(M2)
.

(LR-Adam)

\eta(t)
satisfies
\int_{0}^{\infty}\eta(t)dt=\infty
and
\eta(t)\leq\mathrm{o}\left(t^{\frac{1}{L}-1}\right)
, where
L\geq 1
is from
(M2)
, and is non-increasing.

We note that existing works on Adam and momentum steepest descent in linear models
(
50
;
13
;

)
assumed a non-increasing learning rate
\eta_{t}
with
\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}\eta_{t}=\infty
and
\eta_{t}=\mathrm{o}\left(1\right)
, as well as additional technical assumptions; our Assumption
(LR-Adam)
in the linear predictor case (
L=1
) is somewhat weaker than theirs.

Realizability and Trajectory Assumptions.

For normalized steepest descent, we assume as in
28
;
44
and others,

(R1)

There exists
t_{0}\geq 0
with
\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta}_{t_{0}})<\ell(0)
.

Assumption
(R1)
is not assumed in the momentum steepest descent and Adam results; instead we assume the following:

(T1)

Nontrivial trajectory:
\exists N_{\text{min}}>0,t_{0}\geq 0:\forall t\geq t_{0}:\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert\geq N_{\text{min}}
.

(T2)

Directional Convergence:
\frac{\bm{\theta}_{t}}{\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert}
converges to some
\bar{\bm{\theta}}
with a positive margin
\gamma(\bar{\bm{\theta}})>0
.

Assumption
(T1)
guarantees only that
\bm{\theta}_{t}
is eventually bounded away from the origin. In particular this assumption holds if eventually
\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta}_{t})<m\cdot\ell(0)-\delta
for some
\delta>0
. This is therefore a very mild assumption.
(T1)
follows from
(R1)
when analyzing (normalized) steepest descent, since the loss is proved to decay once
\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta}_{t})<\ell(0)
; momentum steepest descent and Adam, however, only asymptotically approximate steepest descent, and such a decay is only proved for them in this work under Assumptions
(T1)
and
(T2)
.

Regarding
(T2)
, we note that the directional convergence assumption can be found in the implicit bias literature of linear and homogeneous networks (see, for instance,
17
;
16
;
8
;
36
). The assumption of a strictly positive margin, which also implies realizability, can also be found in
16
;
36
. We remark that

(T2)
to be crucial, as opposed to arguing about arbitrary limit points of
\frac{\bm{\theta}_{t}}{\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert}
(as in

Adam Well-Definability Assumption.

To discuss Adam without the stability constant and guarantee that
\mathbf{v}_{t}[j]>0
for all

, which is required to prevent division by zero, we introduce the following technical assumption regarding the initialization. This assumption appears in similar form also in

, while

(A1)

There exist
\tau>0,\rho>0
such that for all

and for almost any

\mathbf{g}_{t}[j]^{2}>\rho
. These

may be arbitrarily small.

2.4
Margin Maximization and KKT Conditions

An important notion when discussing implicit bias is that of the
(hard) margin
, defined for homogeneous models with respect to a norm
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert
by

\gamma(\bm{\theta})=\min_{i\in[m]}y_{i}f\left(\mathbf{x}_{i};\frac{\bm{\theta}}{\left\lVert\bm{\theta}\right\rVert}\right)~.

(9)

Under Assumption
(T2)
,
\gamma(\bm{\theta}_{t})
converges to
\gamma(\bar{\bm{\theta}})
.
We also consider the following
soft margin

\widetilde{\gamma}(\bm{\theta})=\frac{\varphi^{-1}\left(\log\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta})}\right)}{\left\lVert\bm{\theta}\right\rVert^{L}}~,

(10)

as a convenient substitute for
\gamma(\bm{\theta})
. The soft margin approximates the hard margin with a
\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\log m}{\left\lVert\bm{\theta}\right\rVert^{L}}\right)
error that vanishes whenever
\left\lVert\bm{\theta}\right\rVert\to\infty
(as is indeed proved in all of our results). The examination of quantities relating to
\widetilde{\gamma}
prove important in the analysis.

Our results pertain to the following objective, known as margin maximization, which is not directly optimized by any of the aforementioned algorithms:

\min_{\bm{\theta}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}}\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\bm{\theta}\right\rVert^{2}\quad\text{s.t.}\quad\forall i\in[m]:y_{i}f(\mathbf{x}_{i};\bm{\theta})\geq 1~.

(11)

Minimizing the norm
\left\lVert\bm{\theta}\right\rVert
while preserving feasibility (
\forall i\in[m]:y_{i}f(\mathbf{x}_{i};\bm{\theta})\geq 1
) is known to be equivalent to maximizing the margin
\gamma(\bm{\theta})
. For general homogeneous models, Problem (
11
) is non-convex, and the implicit bias of algorithms towards minimizing it is shown in light of the
KKT (Karush-Kuhn-Tucker) conditions
, which are local stationarity conditions:

Definition 2.1
.

A point
\bm{\theta}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
with
y_{i}f(\mathbf{x}_{i};\bm{\theta})\geq 1
for all
i\in[m]
is said to satisfy the KKT conditions of Problem (
11
) if there exist
\mathbf{k}\in\partial\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\bm{\theta}\right\rVert^{2}
, coefficients
\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{m}\geq 0
and subgradients
\mathbf{h}_{i}\in\partial f(\mathbf{x}_{i};\bm{\theta})
with:

1.

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}y_{i}\mathbf{h}_{i}-\mathbf{k}=\mathbf{0}
;

2.

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}(y_{i}f(\mathbf{x}_{i};\bm{\theta})-1)=0
.

3.1
Normalized Steepest Descent with a LR Schedule

Our first result, assuming only

,

,
(R1)
,

,

,

,

is non-decreasing on

Next, we prove that any limit point of
\frac{\bm{\theta}_{t}}{\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert}
is a KKT-point of the appropriate max-margin problem.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\bm{\theta}_{t}
be a trajectory of normalized steepest descent with respect to a norm
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert
(Equation (
3
)). Under Assumptions
(M1-Weak)
,
(M2-Weak)
,
(R1)
,

, any limit point

3.2

4
Non-Smooth Models

Our results for momentum steepest descent and Adam are stated under the assumption of smooth models
(M1)
. However, as our proofs (Appendix
C.5
,
C.7
) show, this may be weakened to
(M1-Weak)
, if the normalized model subgradients converge. More specifically, denote for all
t
subgradients
\mathbf{h}(\mathbf{x}_{i};\bm{\theta}_{t})\in\partial f(\mathbf{x}_{i};\bm{\theta}_{t})
for which
\mathbf{g}_{t}=-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\ell\left(z_{i}^{t}\right)\varphi^{\prime}(z_{i}^{t})y_{i}\mathbf{h}(\mathbf{x}_{i};\bm{\theta}_{t})
. The condition is:

(T3)

\forall i\in[m]:\frac{\mathbf{h}(\mathbf{x}_{i};\bm{\theta}_{t})}{\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert^{L-1}}
converges.

Note first that this condition is trivially satisfied for smooth models under
(T2)
: by Theorem B.2(a) in
28
, it holds that
\frac{\mathbf{h}(\mathbf{x}_{i};\bm{\theta}_{t})}{\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert^{L-1}}\in\partial f(\mathbf{x}_{i};\frac{\bm{\theta}_{t}}{\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert})
. Therefore, for smooth models, i.e.,
f\in C^{1}
, convergence of
\frac{\mathbf{h}(\mathbf{x}_{i};\bm{\theta}_{t})}{\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert^{L-1}}
is guaranteed from convergence of
\frac{\bm{\theta}_{t}}{\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert}
by continuity of
\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{i};\bm{\theta}_{t})
.

For non-smooth models under
(M1-Weak)
,
\frac{\mathbf{h}(\mathbf{x}_{i};\bm{\theta}_{t})}{\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert^{L-1}}
converges whenever
\frac{\bm{\theta}_{t}}{\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert}
eventually stays in the same
C^{1}
stratum of
f(\mathbf{x}_{i};\cdot)
(if
\frac{\bm{\theta}_{t}}{\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert}
is exactly on a stratum boundary,
\mathbf{h}
can be chosen to conform to any of the bordering strata; the choice of
\mathbf{h}
should be continuous to ensure convergence).
In particular, under
(T2)
, this holds whenever the limiting direction
\bar{\bm{\theta}}
is an inner point of a stratum.
In homogeneous ReLU networks, stratum boundaries are the parameters
\bm{\theta}_{t}
for which a neuron preactivation is exactly 0. Therefore, when using a consistent choice of ReLU subgradient at 0 (which is always the case in practice),
(T3)
follows from
(T2)
for every trajectory in which signs of neuron preactivations eventually stabilize. It is unclear whether this is satisfied in practice or under what conditions; it appears to be violated in our experiments on two-layer ReLU networks with the MNIST dataset, but we leave open the possibility that some settings comply with this condition.

The proofs of Theorems

\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert
and

as
\int_{0}^{t}\eta
and

respectively (where
\gamma_{\infty}

for details). Also, as noted by
44

21

\frac{\bm{\theta}_{t}}{\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert}
is closely related to alignment of parameters and gradients
\left\langle\frac{\bm{\theta}_{t}}{\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert},-\frac{\mathbf{g}_{t}}{{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}}\right\rangle
. We extract this insight into a general blueprint that serves us to prove implicit bias results on homogeneous models; namely, Theorem
C.8
states that regardless of the optimization algorithm, any limit point
\bar{\bm{\theta}}
of
\frac{\bm{\theta}_{t}}{\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert}
with
\gamma(\bar{\bm{\theta}})>0
is guaranteed to be a KKT point of Problem (
11
), if
\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta}_{t_{n}})\overset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}0
and
\left\langle\frac{\bm{\theta}_{t_{n}}}{\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t_{n}}\right\rVert},-\frac{\mathbf{g}_{t_{n}}}{{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t_{n}}\right\rVert}_{\star}}\right\rangle\overset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}1
on a subsequence
t_{n}
for which
\frac{\bm{\theta}_{t_{n}}}{\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t_{n}}\right\rVert}\overset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\bar{\bm{\theta}}
.

5.1
Approximate Steepest Descent

Our main technical contribution is the extension of the KKT stationarity results to
approximate steepest descent algorithms
and specifically momentum-based algorithms, which we describe here. Steepest descent (normalized or unnormalized) with respect to a norm
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert
may be described succinctly with the following equation for almost any
t\geq 0
:

\left\langle\frac{\frac{d\bm{\theta}_{t}}{dt}}{\left\lVert\frac{d\bm{\theta}_{t}}{dt}\right\rVert},-\frac{\mathbf{g}_{t}}{{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}}\right\rangle=1~.

(12)

Equation (
12
) is the linchpin of analyses of steepest descent, as it allows to prove eventual alignment of the (negative) gradients with the parameters themselves.
When analyzing momentum-based algorithms, Equation (
12
) will not be exactly satisfied, but the hope is that a similar relation will hold asymptotically. Hence we define:

Definition 5.1

(Approximate Steepest Descent)
.

We say that an arc
\bm{\theta}_{t}
is a trajectory of Approximate Steepest Descent with respect to
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert
if there exist
\nu(t)>0,R_{\text{max}}>0
with:

1.

\lim_{t\to\infty}N(t):=\lim_{t\to\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\nu=\infty
;

2.

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert}{N(t)}\leq R_{\text{max}}
;

3.

\essliminf_{t\to\infty}r(t)\geq 1
, where

r(t)\overset{a.e.}{=}\sup_{\mathbf{g}_{t}\in\partial\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta}_{t})}\left\langle\frac{1}{\nu(t)}\frac{d\bm{\theta}_{t}}{dt},-\frac{\mathbf{g}_{t}}{{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}}\right\rangle~.

The quantity
\nu(t)
can be chosen in a flexible manner; for momentum steepest descent (and indeed exact steepest descent)
\nu(t)=\left\lVert\frac{d\bm{\theta}_{t}}{dt}\right\rVert
is chosen, but for Adam we choose
\nu(t)=\eta(t)
(the learning rate). Lemma
C.15
shows that the properties in Definition
5.1
, taken together with a positive lower bound on the margin, suffice to prove that
\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta}_{t})\overset{t\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}0,\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert\overset{t\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\infty
. Building on this result and on Theorem
C.8
, in Theorem
C.17
we prove that under
(T2)
and provided that
R_{\text{max}}\leq 1
, the limiting direction
\bar{\bm{\theta}}
is a KKT point of Problem (
11
).

5.2
Asymptotic Momentum-Gradient Relations

Our results for momentum steepest descent (MSD) and Adam rely on the analysis of Approximate Steepest Descent. To show that MSD and Adam indeed satisfy Definition
5.1
, we analyze the properties of the momentum operator in Appendix
B
.

In particular, Corollary
B.8
offers a key insight, namely that the ratio
\frac{m(t)}{g(t)}
for a real-valued function
g(t)
and its momentum estimator
m(t)
tends to a well-defined limit whenever
\frac{d\log g}{dt}
converges. This is applied in Lemma
C.19
, which demonstrates, that in our setting of optimization trajectories, when
\left\lVert\frac{d\bm{\theta}_{t}}{dt}\right\rVert\leq\mathrm{o}\left(t^{\frac{1}{L}-1}\right)
, it holds that
\mathbf{m}_{t}[j]=\mathbf{g}_{t}[j]\left(1\pm\mathrm{o}\left(1\right)\right)
for any coordinate
j\in[p]
which is momentarily of
“significant”
magnitude at time
t
. This is formalized by observing the set
J_{\varepsilon}(t)=\left\{j\in[p]\mid\frac{\left|\mathbf{g}_{t}[j]\right|}{{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}}>\varepsilon\right\}
for an arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
. Lemma
C.19
also shows that
\frac{\mathbf{m}_{t}}{{\left\lVert\mathbf{m}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}}-\frac{\mathbf{g}_{t}}{{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}}\overset{t\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}0
, which allows proving that MSD is indeed an Approximate Steepest Descent algorithm.

In the analysis of Adam, Lemma
C.19
is again vital, as it implies that
\frac{\hat{\mathbf{m}}_{t}[j]}{\sqrt{\hat{\mathbf{v}}_{t}}[j]}=\mathrm{sign}\left(\mathbf{g}_{t}[j]\right)(1\pm\mathrm{o}\left(1\right))
whenever
\mathbf{g}_{t}[j]
is momentarily significant (as above). The approximation of Adam to sign gradient descent is in fact the essence of showing
\ell_{\infty}
margin maximization, as sign gradient descent is normalized steepest descent with
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert=\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\infty}
. However, it is not necessarily true for Adam that
\left\langle\frac{\frac{d\bm{\theta}_{t}}{dt}}{\left\lVert\frac{d\bm{\theta}_{t}}{dt}\right\rVert_{\infty}},-\frac{\mathbf{g}_{t}}{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert_{1}}\right\rangle\overset{t\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}1
. Instead, we rely on the flexibility of Definition
5.1
, choosing
\nu(t)=\eta(t)
. We adapt to our setting an important result proved by
50
in the discrete case for linear models, which shows that even if momentarily
\frac{\hat{\mathbf{m}}_{t}[j]}{\sqrt{\hat{\mathbf{v}}_{t}[j]}}>1
, the opposite holds on average. Namely, we prove in Lemma
B.10
that for any
j\in[p]

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\left|\bm{\theta}_{t}[j]\right|}{\int_{0}^{t}\eta}=\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\left|\int_{0}^{t}\eta(s)\frac{\hat{\mathbf{m}}_{s}[j]}{\sqrt{\hat{\mathbf{v}}_{s}[j]}}ds\right|}{\int_{0}^{t}\eta}\leq 1~,

allowing
us
to choose
R_{\text{max}}\leq 1
for
Definition
5.1

and finish the proof.

7
Conclusion

In this work we examined the properties of normalized steepest descent in homogeneous models, as well as those of the popular momentum-based optimizers Adam and Muon on smooth homogeneous models. The technical bridge between the two is the unifying perspective of approximate steepest descent, a framework we believe to be general and widely applicable to first-order optimization methods related to the steepest descent family. In the case of momentum-based optimizers we crucially show that the momentum mechanism is asymptotically faithful to the significant gradient coordinates, when the learning rate decays. Our treatment of Muon and Muon-Signum is only a special case of compositions of normalized momentum steepest descent algorithms, while results for Adam and Muon-Adam rely directly on the framework of approximate steepest descent.

Several important questions remain open. First, our results for momentum-based optimizers hold also for
non-smooth
models under a strong trajectory Assumption
(T3)
, as discussed in Section
4
. It is unclear whether these algorithms have a provable margin-maximization bias for non-smooth models, notably ReLU networks, with no such assumptions, or whether Assumption
(T3)
can be formally proved in certain settings.
Second, our results assume directional convergence of the parameters. For gradient descent, the implicit bias in homogeneous models was analyzed by
28
before
21
formally proved directional convergence; a natural question is whether a directional-convergence guarantee can also be proved for Adam and Muon.
Third, the implicit bias of gradient descent was analyzed also
for certain non-homogeneous models
(
35
;
24
;
6
)
, and it would be interesting to show such results for other optimizers.

Finally, exploring the theoretical and practical implications of our results is an intriguing research direction.
In which settings can generalization of models be deliberately improved with an informed choice of optimizer?
Are training-data reconstruction attacks based on satisfaction of KKT conditions, as shown in
18
;
5
;
38
for gradient descent, also feasible for Adam and Muon?
What are the implications of the implicit bias in these optimizers for adversarial robustness
(
45
;
14
)
?
We hope that our results will help advance understanding of the above questions.

Appendix A
Clarke Subgradients and Chain Rule

Our notion of the subgradient of a function
f=f(\bm{\theta}):\mathbb{R}^{p}\to\mathbb{R}
is that of
9
:

\partial f(\bm{\theta}):=\mathrm{conv}\left\{\lim_{k\to\infty}\nabla f(\bm{\theta}_{k})\mid\lim_{k\to\infty}\bm{\theta}_{k}=\bm{\theta},f\text{ is differentiable at }\bm{\theta}_{k}\right\},

(13)

where
\mathrm{conv}(\cdot)
is the convex hull of a set (the set of finite convex combinations of points from that set).

The following basic chain rule holds for all locally Lipschitz functions.

Theorem A.1

(Theorem 2.3.9 and 2.3.10 in
10
)
.

Let
f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}:\mathbb{R}^{p}\to\mathbb{R}
,
\mathcal{L}:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
be locally Lipschitz functions and define
\mathbf{f}=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})
. Let
(\mathcal{L}\circ\mathbf{f})(\bm{\theta})=\mathcal{L}(f_{1}(\bm{\theta}),\ldots,f_{m}(\bm{\theta})):\mathbb{R}^{p}\to\mathbb{R}
be the composition of
\mathcal{L}
with
f
. Then, it holds:

\partial(\mathcal{L}\circ\mathbf{f})(\bm{\theta})\subseteq\mathrm{conv}\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}\mathbf{h}_{i}:\bm{\alpha}\in\partial\mathcal{L}(f_{1}(\bm{\theta}),\ldots,f_{m}(\bm{\theta})),\mathbf{h}_{i}\in\partial f_{i}(\bm{\theta})\right\}~.

(14)

The following corollary holds for a smooth
\mathcal{L}
, and implies in our setting for the loss
\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta})=\mathcal{L}(f(\mathbf{x}_{1};\bm{\theta}),\dots,f(\mathbf{x}_{m};\bm{\theta}))
that any
\mathbf{g}\in\partial\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta})
is given as a sum
\mathbf{g}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\ell^{\prime}(y_{i}f(\mathbf{x}_{i};\bm{\theta}))y_{i}\mathbf{h}_{i}
where
\ell^{\prime}
is the derivative of the per-sample loss, and
\mathbf{h}_{i}\in\partial f(\mathbf{x}_{i};\bm{\theta})
.

Corollary A.2

(Chain Rule with an Outer Smooth Function)
.

Let
f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}:\mathbb{R}^{p}\to\mathbb{R}
be locally Lipschitz and
\mathcal{L}:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
be
C^{1}
. Then

\partial(\mathcal{L}\circ\mathbf{f})(\bm{\theta})\subseteq\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}\mathbf{h}_{i}:\bm{\alpha}\in\partial\mathcal{L}(f_{1}(\bm{\theta}),\ldots,f_{m}(\bm{\theta})),\mathbf{h}_{i}\in\partial f_{i}(\bm{\theta})\right\}~.

(15)

Proof.

Denote

A=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}\mathbf{h}_{i}:\bm{\alpha}\in\partial\mathcal{L}(f_{1}(\bm{\theta}),\ldots,f_{m}(\bm{\theta})),\mathbf{h}_{i}\in\partial f_{i}(\bm{\theta})\right\}~.

Using Theorem
A.1
, it suffices to show that
\text{conv}A\subseteq A
. Indeed, let
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\bm{\alpha}^{(j)}\in\partial\mathcal{L}(f_{1}(\bm{\theta}),\ldots,f_{m}(\bm{\theta})),\mathbf{h}_{i}^{(j)}\in\partial f_{i}(\bm{\theta})
for
i\in[m],j\in[n]
and let
\lambda_{j}>0,j\in[n]
with
\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}=1
. Since
\mathcal{L}\in C^{1}
,
\partial\mathcal{L}=\{\nabla\mathcal{L}\}
is unique at any point, so in fact
\forall j\in[n]:\bm{\alpha}^{(j)}=\nabla\mathcal{L}(f_{1}(\bm{\theta}),...,f_{m}(\bm{\theta}))=:\bm{\alpha}
, and

\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}^{(j)}\mathbf{h}_{i}^{(j)}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}\mathbf{h}_{i}^{(j)}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}\mathbf{h}_{i}^{(j)}~.

Since
\partial f_{i}(\bm{\theta})
is convex by definition,
\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}\mathbf{h}_{i}^{(j)}\in\partial f_{i}(\bm{\theta})
, so we are finished.
∎

We also consider the notion of Whitney-
C^{1}
stratifiability. We refer the reader to Section 5.2 in
11
for a technical introduction to the topic. For our purposes, it suffices to know that if
f:\mathbb{R}^{p}\to\mathbb{R}
is Whitney-
C^{1}
stratifiable, its graph is a finite union of
C^{1}
manifolds (implying that
(T3)
follows from
(T2)
for some trajectories), with conditions along the boundaries allowing us to assume that
f
admits a chain rule.

Definition A.3
.

\bm{\theta}_{t}:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}^{p}
is an
arc
if it is absolutely continuous on every compact interval, or equivalently if there exists
\bm{\theta}^{\prime}_{t}:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}^{p}
which is Lebesgue integrable on every interval
[0,t]
so that

\forall t\geq 0:\bm{\theta}_{t}=\bm{\theta}_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\bm{\theta}^{\prime}_{s}ds~.

In all the trajectories we discuss,
\bm{\theta}_{t}
is defined as an integral over
\frac{d\bm{\theta}_{t}}{dt}
, so
\bm{\theta}_{t}
is an arc.

Theorem A.4

(Chain Rule for Arcs and Stratifiable Functions - Theorem 5.8 in
11
)
.

If
f:\mathbb{R}^{p}\to\mathbb{R}
is locally Lipschitz and Whitney
C^{1}
-stratifiable, then for any arc
\bm{\theta}_{t}:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}^{p}
, almost all
t\geq 0
, and all
\mathbf{g}\in\partial f(\bm{\theta}_{t})
, it holds:

\frac{df(\bm{\theta}_{t})}{dt}=\left\langle\mathbf{g},\frac{d\bm{\theta}_{t}}{dt}\right\rangle~.

(16)

Finally, we also need a chain rule for the norm
\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert
. We may circumvent the requirement that
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert
be
C^{1}
-stratifiable with the following definition and theorem:

Definition A.5

(Subdifferentially Regular Functions - Definition 5.3 in
11
)
.

f:\mathbb{R}^{p}\to\mathbb{R}
is said to be subdifferentially regular if
\forall\bm{\theta}\in\mathbb{R}^{p},\mathbf{g}\in\partial f(\bm{\theta})
,

f(\bm{\theta}^{\prime})\geq f(\bm{\theta})+\left\langle\mathbf{g},\bm{\theta}^{\prime}-\bm{\theta}\right\rangle+\mathrm{o}\left(\left\lVert\bm{\theta}^{\prime}-\bm{\theta}\right\rVert\right)\quad\text{as $\bm{\theta}^{\prime}\to\bm{\theta}$}~.

In particular, any convex function, including any norm, satisfies the above inequality without an error term
\mathrm{o}\left(\left\lVert\bm{\theta}^{\prime}-\bm{\theta}\right\rVert\right)
and thus is subdifferentially regular.

Theorem A.6

(Chain Rule for Arcs and Subdifferentially Regular Functions - Lemma 5.4 in
11
)
.

If
f:\mathbb{R}^{p}\to\mathbb{R}
is locally Lipschitz and subdifferentially regular, then for any arc
\bm{\theta}_{t}:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}^{p}
, almost all
t\geq 0
, and all
\mathbf{g}\in\partial f(\bm{\theta}_{t})
, it holds:

\frac{df(\bm{\theta}_{t})}{dt}=\left\langle\mathbf{g},\frac{d\bm{\theta}_{t}}{dt}\right\rangle~.

(17)

For reference, the following is a standard characterization of the subdifferential of a norm:

\partial\left\lVert\bm{\theta}\right\rVert=\left\{\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}\mid\left\langle\mathbf{v},\bm{\theta}\right\rangle=\left\lVert\bm{\theta}\right\rVert,{\left\lVert\mathbf{v}\right\rVert}_{\star}\leq 1\right\}~.

(18)

Note that by definition of the dual norm it holds that
\left\langle\mathbf{v},\bm{\theta}\right\rangle\leq\left\lVert\bm{\theta}\right\rVert{\left\lVert\mathbf{v}\right\rVert}_{\star}
, so in fact when
\bm{\theta}\neq{\mathbf{0}}
,
{\left\lVert\mathbf{v}\right\rVert}_{\star}=1
for any
\mathbf{v}\in\partial\left\lVert\bm{\theta}\right\rVert
.

Appendix B
Momentum (EMA)

B.1
Discrete Momentum

For a parameter
\beta\in(0,1)
and a real-valued sequence
g_{n},n\geq 1
, discrete momentum
m_{n}
is defined as follows:

m_{n}=\beta m_{n-1}+(1-\beta)g_{n},\quad m_{0}=0~,

(19)

with the explicit solution:

m_{n}=(1-\beta)\sum_{k=1}^{n}\beta^{n-k}g_{k}~.

We give for reference the definition of Adam in the discrete case for a sequence of subgradients
\mathbf{g}_{n}
, parameters
\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\in(0,1)
, a learning rate
\eta_{n}
and a constant
\varepsilon\geq 0
(in our analysis
\varepsilon=0
). Square, division and square root are taken elementwise.

Adam (Discrete):

\displaystyle\mathbf{m}_{n}

\displaystyle=\beta_{1}\mathbf{m}_{n-1}+(1-\beta_{1})\mathbf{g}_{n},\quad\mathbf{m}_{0}={\mathbf{0}}

(20)

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}_{n}

\displaystyle=\beta_{2}\mathbf{v}_{n-1}+(1-\beta_{2})\mathbf{g}_{n}^{2},\quad\mathbf{v}_{0}={\mathbf{0}}

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbf{m}}_{n}

\displaystyle=(1-\beta_{1}^{n})^{-1}\mathbf{m}_{n},\quad\hat{\mathbf{v}}_{n}=(1-\beta_{2}^{n})^{-1}\mathbf{v}_{n}

\displaystyle\Delta\bm{\theta}_{n}

\displaystyle=-\eta_{n}\frac{\hat{\mathbf{m}}_{n}}{\sqrt{\hat{\mathbf{v}}_{n}}+\varepsilon}~.

B.2
Continuous Momentum

Notice that Equation (
19
) shows that the direction of update of
m_{n}
is
g_{n}-m_{n-1}
:

m_{n}-m_{n-1}=(1-\beta)(g_{n}-m_{n-1})~.

The natural analogue for a continuous time variable
t
and a parameter
c>0
is therefore:

\frac{dm_{t}}{dt}=c(g_{t}-m_{t}),\quad m_{0}=0~.

This equation has the following explicit solution, clearly of a similar form to the discrete version:

m_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}ce^{-c(t-s)}g_{s}ds~.

This form is identical to
(
47
)
, with the caveat that they assumed
c=1-\beta
, a form restricting
c
to small values (note that taking
c\to\infty
induces the regime
m_{t}=g_{t}
). To clearly uncover the connection between
\beta
and
c
, assume that a discrete momentum iteration takes a unit time
\Delta t=1
, and that steps are small enough so that
g_{s}
is roughly constant on
[t,t+1]
. Then

\displaystyle m_{t+1}-m_{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t+1}ce^{-c(t+1-s)}g_{s}ds-\int_{0}^{t}ce^{-c(t-s)}g_{s}ds=(e^{-c}-1)m_{t}+\int_{t}^{t+1}ce^{-c(t+1-s)}g_{s}ds

\displaystyle\approx(e^{-c}-1)m_{t}+(1-e^{-c})g_{t}=(1-e^{-c})(g_{t}-m_{t})~.

So
c
is analogous to
-\log\beta
, hence spanning the entire range
(0,\infty)
. When
\beta
is close to
1
we obtain
c\approx(1-\beta)
.

In the definition of discrete Adam, a multiplicative factor of
(1-\beta^{n})^{-1}
is used to correct the initial bias accrued when initializing
m_{0}=0
. The continuous analogue for the bias correction is
(1-e^{-ct})^{-1}
, since for small
t
,
m_{t}\approx g_{0}\int_{0}^{t}ce^{-c(t-s)}ds=g_{0}(1-e^{-ct})
.

Definition B.1
.

We denote

\mathcal{F}=L^{\infty}_{\text{loc}}([0,\infty)),\quad\mathcal{G}=\left\{g:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}\mid\exists\tau>0,\rho>0:g^{2}(t)>\rho\text{ a.e. on }[0,\tau]\right\}~,

where
L^{\infty}_{\text{loc}}([0,\infty))
is the space of Lebesgue measurable functions
g(t):[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
so that
g(t)
is essentially bounded (bounded except on a measure zero set) on every compact interval
[a,b]
. We denote also for convenience

M_{[a,b]}(g):=\text{ess}\sup_{[a,b]}\left|g\right|~.

It is a standard fact that
L^{\infty}_{\text{loc}}([0,\infty))\subseteq L^{p}_{\text{loc}}([0,\infty))
for any
p\geq 1
, the latter being the space of functions
g
with
\int_{[a,b]}|g|^{p}<\infty
on every compact interval
[a,b]
. This allows us to define the momentum expression for
g
. The class
\mathcal{G}
encapsulates Assumption
(A1)
, facilitating a discussion of the Adam ratio.

Definition B.2

(Momentum/EMA)
.

Let
g(t)\in\mathcal{F}
. For a parameter
c>0
denote the following by
\mathrm{EMA}\left(g,c\right)(t)
or
A(g,c)(t)
for short:

\mathrm{EMA}\left(g,c\right)(t)=A(g,c)(t):=\int_{0}^{t}ce^{-c(t-s)}g(s)ds~.

Also denote for convenience:

I(g,c)(t)=\int_{0}^{t}e^{cs}g(s)ds,\quad I_{\infty}(g,c)=\lim_{t\to\infty}I(g,c)(t)~,

so
A(g,c)(t)=ce^{-ct}I(g,c)(t)
.

We prove a series of useful lemmas about the properties of the EMA.

Lemma B.3

(Momentum ODE)
.

Let
g(t)\in\mathcal{F}
and
c>0
. Then for almost any
t\geq 0
,

\frac{dA(g,c)}{dt}=c\left(g(t)-A(g,c)(t)\right)~.

Proof.

Since
g
is Lebesgue integrable, by differentiation rules and the fundamental theorem of calculus we have

\frac{dA(g,c)}{dt}=-c^{2}e^{-ct}I(g,c)(t)+ce^{-ct}\frac{dI(g,c)}{dt}=-cA(g,c)(t)+ce^{-ct}e^{ct}g(t)=c(g(t)-A(g,c)(t))

∎

Lemma B.4

(Uniform Bound on Adam Ratio)
.

Let
g(t)\in\mathcal{F}
and
c_{1}>\frac{c_{2}}{2}>0
. Then,

\left|A(g,c_{1})(t)\right|\leq A(\left|g\right|,c_{1})(t)\leq\frac{c_{1}}{\sqrt{c_{2}(2c_{1}-c_{2})}}\sqrt{A(g^{2},c_{2})(t)}~.

In particular,

A(\left|g\right|,c_{1})(t)\leq\mathcal{O}\left(\sqrt{A(g^{2},c_{2})(t)}\right)~,

and

A(\left|g\right|,c_{1})(t)\leq\sqrt{A(g^{2},c_{1})(t)}~.

Proof.

By the triangle inequality and Cauchy-Schwarz on the inner product space of
L^{2}
-integrable functions on
[0,t]
, we get

\displaystyle\left|A(g,c_{1})(t)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}c_{1}e^{-c_{1}(t-s)}\left|g(s)\right|ds=\int_{0}^{t}c_{1}e^{-(c_{1}-\frac{c_{2}}{2})(t-s)}e^{-\frac{c_{2}}{2}(t-s)}\left|g(s)\right|ds

\displaystyle\leq c_{1}\left(\int_{0}^{t}e^{-c_{2}(t-s)}g^{2}(s)ds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(2c_{1}-c_{2})(t-s)}ds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c_{1}}{\sqrt{c_{2}}}\sqrt{A\left(g^{2},c_{2}\right)(t)}\left(\frac{1-e^{-(2c_{1}-c_{2})t}}{2c_{1}-c_{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c_{1}}{\sqrt{c_{2}(2c_{1}-c_{2})}}\sqrt{A\left(g^{2},c_{2}\right)(t)}

∎

Lemma B.5

(Asymptotic Relations)
.

Let
c>0
and
0\leq g(t)\in\mathcal{F}
. Assume
g
is not a.e. 0.

1.

If
I_{\infty}(g,c)<\infty
then
\frac{A(g,c)(t)}{e^{-ct}}\overset{t\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}cI_{\infty}(g,c)>0
and

\forall F\in\mathcal{F},I_{\infty}(F,c)<\infty:\frac{A(F,c)}{A(g,c)}\longrightarrow\frac{I_{\infty}(F,c)}{I_{\infty}(g,c)}~.

2.

If
I_{\infty}(g,c)=\infty
then
\frac{A(g,c)(t)}{e^{-ct}}\to\infty
and

(a)

\forall t_{0}\geq 0:\left|\frac{\int_{t_{0}}^{t}ce^{-c(t-s)}g(s)ds}{A(g,c)(t)}-1\right|\leq\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{e^{-ct}}{A(g,c)(t)}\right)\overset{t\to\infty}{\rightarrow}0~.

(b)

For any
F(t)\in\mathcal{F}
and
C>0,t_{0}\geq 0
:

\left(\forall t\geq t_{0}:F(t)\leq Cg(t)\right)\Rightarrow A(F,c)(t)\leq CA(g,c)(t)(1+\mathrm{o}\left(1\right))~,

\left(\forall t\geq t_{0}:F(t)\geq Cg(t)\right)\Rightarrow A(F,c)(t)\geq CA(g,c)(t)(1-\mathrm{o}\left(1\right))~.

(c)

If eventually
g(t)>0
, for any
F(t)\in\mathcal{F}
with
\frac{F(t)}{g(t)}\overset{t\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}C\in[-\infty,\infty]
it holds that

\frac{A(F,c)}{A(g,c)}\overset{t\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}C~.

3.

In both cases (
I_{\infty}(g,c)=\infty,I_{\infty}(g,c)<\infty
), for any
0\leq F(t)\in\mathcal{F}
:

F(t)\leq\mathcal{O}\left(g(t)\right)\Rightarrow A(F,c)(t)\leq\mathcal{O}\left(A(g,c)(t)\right)~,

F(t)\geq\Omega{(g(t))}\Rightarrow A(F,c)(t)\geq\Omega{(A(g,c)(t))}~.

Proof.

1.

By definition
A(g,c)(t)=ce^{-ct}\int_{0}^{t}e^{cs}g(s)ds
, so

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{A(g,c)(t)}{e^{-ct}}=\lim_{t\to\infty}cI(g,c)(t)=cI_{\infty}(g,c)~.

And for any
g,F
with
I_{\infty}(F,c)<\infty
,

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{A(F,c)(t)}{A(g,c)(t)}=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{A(F,c)(t)}{ce^{-ct}}\frac{ce^{-ct}}{A(g,c)(t)}=\frac{I_{\infty}(F,c)}{I_{\infty}(g,c)}~.

2.

(a)

Note that since
g\geq 0
,

\int_{t_{0}}^{t}ce^{-c(t-s)}g(s)ds\leq A(g,c)(t)\leq M_{[0,t_{0}]}(g)\cdot(e^{-c(t-t_{0})}-e^{-ct})+\int_{t_{0}}^{t}ce^{-c(t-s)}g(s)ds~.

Since
I_{\infty}(g,c)=\infty
, in particular
A(g,c)(t)>0
for large enough
t
. Dividing by
A(g,c)(t)
we get

1-\frac{M_{[0,t_{0}]}(g)\cdot(e^{-c(t-t_{0})}-e^{-ct})}{A(g,c)(t)}\leq\frac{\int_{t_{0}}^{t}ce^{-c(t-s)}g(s)ds}{A(g,c)(t)}\leq 1~,

\left|\frac{\int_{t_{0}}^{t}ce^{-c(t-s)}g(s)ds}{A(g,c)(t)}-1\right|\leq\frac{M_{[0,t_{0}]}(g)\cdot(e^{-c(t-t_{0})}-e^{-ct})}{A(g,c)(t)}=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{e^{-ct}}{A(g,c)(t)}\right)=\mathrm{o}\left(1\right)~.

(b)

Let
C>0,t_{0}\geq 0
with
\forall t\geq t_{0}:F(t)\leq Cg(t)
. Then,

A(F,c)(t)\leq M_{[0,t_{0}]}(F)\cdot(e^{-c(t-t_{0})}-e^{-ct})+C\int_{t_{0}}^{t}ce^{-c(t-s)}g(s)ds~.

Therefore by item 2(a),

A(F,c)(t)\leq\mathcal{O}\left(e^{-ct}\right)+CA(g,c)(t)(1+\mathrm{o}\left(1\right))\leq\mathrm{o}\left(A(g,c)\right)+CA(g,c)(t)(1+\mathrm{o}\left(1\right))=CA(g,c)(t)(1+\mathrm{o}\left(1\right))~.

The other direction is completely symmetrical.

(c)

First address the case
C\in(0,\infty)
. Since
C>0
, by the previous item,

\frac{A(F,c)(t)}{A(g,c)(t)}\leq C(1+\mathrm{o}\left(1\right))\overset{t\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}C~,

so

\limsup_{t\to\infty}{\frac{A(F,c)(t)}{A(g,c)(t)}}\leq C~.

In the same fashion

\liminf_{t\to\infty}{\frac{A(F,c)(t)}{A(g,c)(t)}}\geq C~,

showing the limit. If
C=0
, then fixing
\varepsilon>0
,
\frac{\left|F\right|+\varepsilon g}{g}\overset{t\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\varepsilon
, implying by the previous case

\frac{A(\left|F\right|,c)}{A(g,c)}+\varepsilon=\frac{A(\left|F\right|,c)+\varepsilon A(g,c)}{A(g,c)}=\frac{A(\left|F\right|+\varepsilon g,c)}{A(g,c)}\overset{t\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\varepsilon~,

and so

\left|\frac{A(F,c)}{A(g,c)}\right|\leq\frac{A(\left|F\right|,c)}{A(g,c)}\overset{t\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}0~.

For
C\in(-\infty,0)
, applying the case
C>0
with
-F
suffices, since
\frac{-F}{g}\to-C>0
and
A(-F,c)=-A(F,c)
. This finishes for a finite
C
. If
C=\infty
then the above shows
\liminf_{t\to\infty}{\frac{A(F,c)(t)}{A(g,c)(t)}}\geq C^{\prime}
for any
C^{\prime}>0
, showing
\liminf_{t\to\infty}{\frac{A(F,c)(t)}{A(g,c)(t)}}=\infty
, and symmetrically for
C=-\infty
.

3.

If
I_{\infty}(g,c)=\infty
then the result follows from item 2(b). Assume
I_{\infty}(g,c)<\infty
. If
F(t)\leq\mathcal{O}\left(g(t)\right)
then
I_{\infty}(F,c)<\infty
, so the result follows from item 1. If
F(t)\geq\Omega(g(t))
, consider two cases: if
I_{\infty}(F,c)<\infty
then the result again follows from item 1, and if
I_{\infty}(F,c)=\infty
then
\frac{A(F,c)}{A(g,c)}=\frac{I(F,c)(t)}{I(g,c)(t)}\to\infty
and in particular
A(F,c)\geq\Omega(A(g,c))
.

∎

Corollary B.6
.

Let
c>0
and
g\in\mathcal{F}
.

1.

If eventually
g(t)\leq M
for some
M\in\mathbb{R}
then
A(g,c)(t)\leq M+\mathrm{o}\left(1\right)
, and if eventually
g(t)\geq M
then
A(g,c)(t)\geq M-\mathrm{o}\left(1\right)
.

2.

If
\lim_{t\to\infty}g(t)=C\in[-\infty,\infty]
then
\lim_{t\to\infty}A(g,c)(t)=C
.

Proof.

Let
M\in\mathbb{R}
, and
t_{0}
with
\forall t\geq t_{0}:g(t)\leq M
. Then

A(g,c)(t)\leq M_{[0,t_{0}]}(g)(e^{-c(t-t_{0})}-e^{-ct})+M(1-e^{-c(t-t_{0})})\leq M+\mathrm{o}\left(1\right)~.

The lower bound is symmetrical.

For the limit, choose
h\equiv 1\in\mathcal{F}
. Clearly
I_{\infty}(h,c)=\infty
, and
A(h,c)=1-e^{-ct}
. If
\lim_{t\to\infty}g(t)=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{g(t)}{h(t)}=C
then
\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{A(g,c)}{A(h,c)}=C
by Lemma
B.5
, implying
\lim_{t\to\infty}A(g,c)(t)=C
.
∎

The following lemma gives sufficient conditions for
\frac{A(g,c)}{g}
converging to a constant ratio; see the following corollary for a simpler condition for differentiable functions.

Lemma B.7
.

Let
c>0
and
0<g(t)\in\mathcal{F}
. Assume that

1.

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{e^{-ct}}{g(t)}=0
.

2.

There exists
k\in[0,c)
so that for every fixed
u>0
,
\frac{g(t-u)}{g(t)}\overset{t\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}e^{ku}
.

3.

There exists
M(u)\geq 0
with
\int_{0}^{\infty}ce^{-cu}M(u)du<\infty
, and
t_{0}\geq 0
so that
\forall t\geq t_{0},0<u<t-t_{0}:\frac{g(t-u)}{g(t)}\leq M(u)
.

Then
\frac{A(g,c)(t)}{g(t)}\to\frac{c}{c-k}
.

Proof.

Denote
H_{t}(u)=\mathbf{1}_{\{u\leq t-t_{0}\}}\cdot ce^{-cu}\frac{g(t-u)}{g(t)}
. For any
t\geq t_{0}
,

\displaystyle\frac{A(g,c)(t)}{g(t)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{g(t)}\int_{0}^{t_{0}}ce^{-c(t-s)}g(s)ds+\int_{t_{0}}^{t}ce^{-c(t-s)}\frac{g(s)}{g(t)}ds=

\displaystyle\leq\frac{M_{[0,t_{0}]}(g)}{g(t)}\left(e^{-c(t-t_{0})}-e^{-ct}\right)+\int_{0}^{t-t_{0}}ce^{-cu}\frac{g(t-u)}{g(t)}du=

\displaystyle=\frac{M_{[0,t_{0}]}(g)}{g(t)}\left(e^{-c(t-t_{0})}-e^{-ct}\right)+\int_{0}^{\infty}H_{t}(u)du~.

And since
g(t)>0,A(g,c)(t)>0
,

\frac{A(g,c)(t)}{g(t)}\geq\int_{0}^{t-t_{0}}ce^{-cu}\frac{g(t-u)}{g(t)}du=\int_{0}^{\infty}H_{t}(u)du~.

Recall that by hypothesis
\frac{e^{-ct}}{g(t)}\overset{t\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}0
. Also, by hypothesis,
H_{t}(u)\leq ce^{-cu}M(u)
with
\int_{0}^{\infty}ce^{-cu}M(u)du<\infty
. Finally note
H_{t}(u)\overset{t\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}ce^{-(c-k)u}
pointwise. Therefore by the dominated convergence theorem on
[0,\infty)
:

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{A(g,c)(t)}{g(t)}=\lim_{t\to\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}H_{t}(u)du=\int_{0}^{\infty}\lim_{t\to\infty}H_{t}(u)du=\int_{0}^{\infty}ce^{-(c-k)u}du=\frac{c}{c-k}

∎

The following useful corollary is stated for
g(t)>0
, but if
g(t)<0
it applies to
-g
and
A(-g,c)=-A(g,c)
. Therefore for
g(t)\neq 0
with a constant sign, the condition
-\frac{g^{\prime}}{g}=-\frac{d\log\left|g\right|}{dt}\to k
implies items 1-3 as written.

Corollary B.8

(Asymptotic Momentum-Function Ratio )
.

Let
c>0
and
0<g(t)\in\mathcal{F}
.
Assume
g(t)
is differentiable almost everywhere, locally absolutely continuous and
\esslim_{t\to\infty}-\frac{g^{\prime}(t)}{g(t)}=\esslim_{t\to\infty}-\frac{d\log g(t)}{dt}=k\in[0,\infty)
. Then:

1.

If
k>c
then
\frac{A(g,c)(t)}{e^{-ct}}\overset{t\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}cI_{\infty}(g,c)<\infty
and in particular
\frac{A(g,c)(t)}{g(t)}\to\infty
.

2.

If
k=c
then
\frac{A(g,c)(t)}{g(t)}=c\cdot\frac{\int_{0}^{t}G}{G(t)}\overset{t\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\infty
for
G(t)=e^{ct}g(t)
.

3.

If
k<c
then
\frac{A(g,c)(t)}{g(t)}\overset{t\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\frac{c}{c-k}
.

Proof.

For any
k\in[0,\infty)
, if
-\frac{d\log g}{dt}\overset{t\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}k
then, since
g
is locally absolutely continuous, we have by integration on
[0,t]

\log g(t)=-kt+o(t)\quad\Rightarrow\quad g(t)=e^{-kt+o(t)}

This implies
\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{ct}g(t)dt<\infty
, thus the case
k>c
is simply a reiteration of Lemma
B.5
item 1.

For the case
k=c
, denote
G(t)=e^{ct}g(t)
, so it holds that
\frac{d\log G}{dt}\overset{t\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}c-c=0
and:

A(g,c)(t)=ce^{-ct}\int_{0}^{t}G(s)ds

\frac{A(g,c)(t)}{g(t)}=c\cdot\frac{\int_{0}^{t}G(s)ds}{G(t)}

We claim this expression tends to
\infty
. Indeed, for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists
t_{0}\geq 0
with for almost all
t\geq t_{0}:\left|\frac{d\log G}{dt}\right|\leq\varepsilon
, hence it holds by integration on any interval
[s,t],t\geq s\geq t_{0}
that
\log\frac{G(t)}{G(s)}\leq\varepsilon(t-s)
, hence
G(t)\leq G(s)e^{\varepsilon(t-s)}
and

\int_{0}^{t}\frac{G(s)}{G(t)}ds\geq\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\frac{G(s)}{G(t)}ds\geq\int_{t_{0}}^{t}e^{-\varepsilon(t-s)}ds=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\left(1-e^{-\varepsilon(t-t_{0})}\right)\overset{t\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}~.

Since
\varepsilon
was arbitrary this proves
\int_{0}^{t}\frac{G(s)}{G(t)}ds\overset{t\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\infty
.

For the case
k<c
we use Lemma
B.7
. First note that
\frac{e^{-ct}}{g(t)}=e^{-(c-k)t+o(t)}\to 0
. Also, for any fixed
u
,

\log\frac{g(t-u)}{g(t)}=-\int_{t-u}^{t}\frac{d\log g(s)}{ds}ds\overset{t\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}ku~,

\frac{g(t-u)}{g(t)}\overset{t\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}e^{ku}~.

And there exists
t_{0}
and
k<c^{\prime}<c
for which
\forall t\geq t_{0}:\frac{-d\log g}{dt}<c^{\prime}
, so
\forall t\geq t_{0},0<u<t-t_{0}
:

\log\frac{g(t-u)}{g(t)}\leq c^{\prime}u~,

\frac{g(t-u)}{g(t)}\leq e^{c^{\prime}u}=:M(u)~.

With
\int_{0}^{\infty}ce^{-cu}M(u)du<\infty
. Thus the conditions of Lemma
B.7
hold, implying
\frac{A(g,c)(t)}{g(t)}\overset{t\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\frac{c}{c-k}
.

∎

Lemma B.9

(Adam Ratio is Bounded at Initialization)
.

Let
g(t)\in\mathcal{F}\cap\mathcal{G}
, and let
\rho,\tau>0
for which
g^{2}(t)>\rho
a.e. on
[0,\tau]
. Denote
m_{t}=A(g,c_{1})(t),v_{t}=A(g^{2},c_{2})(t)
for
c_{1}\geq c_{2}>0
, and
\hat{m}_{t}=(1-e^{-c_{1}t})^{-1}m_{t},\hat{v}_{t}=(1-e^{-c_{2}t})^{-1}v_{t}
. Then
\frac{\hat{m}_{t}}{\sqrt{\hat{v}_{t}}}
is bounded on
(0,\tau]
.

Proof.

For any
s\in(0,\tau]
it holds that

\left|\hat{m}_{s}\right|=\frac{\left|m_{s}\right|}{1-e^{-c_{1}s}}\leq\frac{\int_{0}^{s}c_{1}e^{-c_{1}(s-r)}\left|g_{r}\right|dr}{1-e^{-c_{1}s}}\leq\frac{M_{[0,\tau]}(g)\int_{0}^{s}c_{1}e^{-c_{1}(s-r)}dr}{1-e^{-c_{1}s}}=M_{[0,\tau]}(g)<\infty~.

And that

\hat{v}_{s}=\frac{v_{s}}{1-e^{-c_{2}s}}=\frac{\int_{0}^{s}c_{2}e^{-c_{2}(s-r)}g^{2}(r)dr}{1-e^{-c_{2}s}}\geq\frac{\rho\int_{0}^{s}c_{2}e^{-c_{2}(s-r)}dr}{1-e^{-c_{2}s}}=\rho>0~.

Therefore

\forall s\in(0,\tau]:\left|\frac{\hat{m}_{s}}{\sqrt{\hat{v}_{s}}}\right|\leq\frac{M_{[0,\tau]}(g)}{\sqrt{\rho}}<\infty~.

∎

Lemma B.10

(Adaptation of Lemma A.4 in

.

Let

and

non-increasing with

\frac{\left|\int_{0}^{t}\eta(s)\frac{\hat{m}_{s}}{\sqrt{\hat{v}_{s}}}ds\right|}{\int_{0}^{t}\eta(s)ds}\leq 1+o(1)~.

From the hypothesis

\left|\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\eta(s)\frac{\hat{m}_{s}}{\sqrt{\hat{v}_{s}}}ds\right|<\infty~.

Thus we focus now on
[t_{0},t]

q(s)=\frac{\sqrt{1-e^{-c_{2}s}}}{1-e^{-c_{1}s}}
. By Cauchy-Schwarz,

\displaystyle\left|\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\eta(s)q(s)\frac{m_{s}}{\sqrt{v_{s}}}ds\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\underbrace{\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\eta(s)q^{2}(s)ds}_{\star}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\underbrace{\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\eta(s)\frac{m_{s}^{2}}{v_{s}}ds}_{\star\star}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}~.

It suffices to show that
\star,\star\star
are each at most
\int_{0}^{t}\eta(s)ds+\mathcal{O}\left(1\right)
.
Since
\int_{0}^{\infty}\eta(s)ds=\infty

\int_{t_{a}}^{t}\eta(s)ds+\mathcal{O}\left(1\right)

.

For

for all

\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\eta(s)q^{2}(s)ds\leq\int_{t_{0}}^{\frac{\log 2}{c_{1}}}\eta(s)q^{2}(s)ds+\int_{\frac{\log 2}{c_{1}}}^{t}\eta(s)ds+6\int_{\frac{\log 2}{c_{1}}}^{t}\eta(s)e^{-c_{1}s}ds~.

The first term is fixed and finite since

is bounded on the fixed interval
[t_{0},\frac{\log 2}{c_{1}}]
. The third term is
\mathcal{O}\left(1\right)
since
\eta(t)
is bounded, so
\int_{\frac{\log 2}{c_{1}}}^{\infty}\eta(s)e^{-c_{1}s}ds
converges. This finishes for
\star
.

For
\star\star
, by Lemma
B.4
,

m_{t}^{2}=A({g,c_{1}})^{2}\leq A({g^{2},c_{1}})~,

so

\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\eta(s)\frac{m_{s}^{2}}{v_{s}}ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\frac{\eta(s)}{v_{s}}\int_{0}^{s}c_{1}e^{-c_{1}(s-r)}g^{2}_{r}drds~.

(21)

It suffices to show that (
21
) is at most
\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\eta(s)ds+\mathcal{O}\left(1\right)
.

By Lemma
B.3
,
g_{r}^{2}=\frac{1}{c_{2}}\frac{dv_{r}}{dr}+v_{r}
, and integration by parts gives

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{s}\frac{c_{1}}{c_{2}}e^{-c_{1}(s-r)}\frac{dv_{r}}{dr}dr

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{1}}{c_{2}}\left(v_{r}e^{-c_{1}(s-r)}\Big|_{0}^{s}-\int_{0}^{s}c_{1}e^{-c_{1}(s-r)}v_{r}dr\right)

\displaystyle\overset{v_{0}=0}{=}\frac{c_{1}}{c_{2}}\left(v_{s}-\int_{0}^{s}c_{1}e^{-c_{1}(s-r)}v_{r}dr\right)~.

So,

\int_{0}^{s}c_{1}e^{-c_{1}(s-r)}g^{2}_{r}dr=\int_{0}^{s}\left(\frac{c_{1}}{c_{2}}e^{-c_{1}(s-r)}\frac{dv_{r}}{dr}+c_{1}e^{-c_{1}(s-r)}v_{r}\right)dr=\frac{c_{1}}{c_{2}}v_{s}-\left(\frac{c_{1}}{c_{2}}-1\right)\int_{0}^{s}c_{1}e^{-c_{1}(s-r)}v_{r}dr~,

and

RHS of (

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{1}}{c_{2}}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\eta(s)ds-\left(\frac{c_{1}}{c_{2}}-1\right)\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\eta(s)\int_{0}^{s}c_{1}e^{-c_{1}(s-r)}\frac{v_{r}}{v_{s}}drds

\displaystyle=\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\eta(s)ds+\left(\frac{c_{1}}{c_{2}}-1\right)\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\eta(s)ds-\left(\frac{c_{1}}{c_{2}}-1\right)\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\eta(s)\int_{0}^{s}c_{1}e^{-c_{1}(s-r)}\frac{v_{r}}{v_{s}}drds

\displaystyle=\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\eta(s)ds+\frac{c_{1}-c_{2}}{c_{2}}\left(\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\eta(s)ds-\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\eta(s)\int_{0}^{s}c_{1}e^{-c_{1}(s-r)}\frac{v_{r}}{v_{s}}drds\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\eta(s)ds+\frac{c_{1}-c_{2}}{c_{2}}\left(\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\eta(s)\left(1-\int_{0}^{s}c_{1}e^{-c_{1}(s-r)}\frac{v_{r}}{v_{s}}dr\right)ds\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\eta(s)ds+\frac{c_{1}-c_{2}}{c_{2}}\left(\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\eta(s)\left(e^{-c_{1}s}+\int_{0}^{s}c_{1}e^{-c_{1}(s-r)}dr-\int_{0}^{s}c_{1}e^{-c_{1}(s-r)}\frac{v_{r}}{v_{s}}dr\right)ds\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\eta(s)ds+\frac{c_{1}-c_{2}}{c_{2}}\left(\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\eta(s)e^{-c_{1}s}ds+\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\eta(s)\int_{0}^{s}c_{1}e^{-c_{1}(s-r)}\left(1-\frac{v_{r}}{v_{s}}\right)drds\right)~.

Since

is bounded,
\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\eta(s)e^{-c_{1}s}ds\leq\mathcal{O}\left(1\right)
.
Therefore it suffices to show

\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\eta(s)\int_{0}^{s}c_{1}e^{-c_{1}(s-r)}\left(1-\frac{v_{r}}{v_{s}}\right)drds\leq\mathcal{O}\left(1\right)

\displaystyle\text{LHS of \eqref{eq:term_1_minus_frac}}\leq

Since

\displaystyle\eta(s)(1-e^{-c_{1}s})-\int_{s}^{t}\eta(r)c_{1}e^{-c_{1}(r-s)}dr

\displaystyle=\eta(s)(1-e^{-c_{1}s})-\eta(s)(1-e^{-c_{1}(t-s)})

\displaystyle=\eta(s)\left(e^{-c_{1}(t-s)}-e^{-c_{1}s}\right)

Since eventually

), hence

\displaystyle\int_{t_{1}}^{t}\log v_{s}\left(\eta(s)(1-e^{-c_{1}s})-\int_{s}^{t}\eta(r)c_{1}e^{-c_{1}(r-s)}dr\right)ds

Since

v_{t}\geq e^{-c_{2}t}\int_{0}^{t}c_{2}e^{c_{2}s}g_{s}^{2}ds\geq\Omega(e^{-c_{2}t})

\int_{t_{1}}^{t}-\log v_{s}\eta(s)e^{-c_{1}s}ds\leq\int_{t_{1}}^{\infty}(c_{2}s+C)\eta(s)e^{-c_{1}s}ds<\infty~.

And since on
[t_{0},t_{1}]

(

Appendix C
Proof Details

C.1
Losses

In this work we consider
log-concave, exponentially-tailed losses
. Namely, we consider a loss of the form

\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta})=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\ell(y_{i}f(\mathbf{x}_{i};\bm{\theta}))~,

where
\ell(u)=e^{-\varphi(u)}
, and
\varphi\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R})
with
\exists\Phi_{M}^{\prime},\Phi_{M}^{\prime\prime}>0:\forall u\in\mathbb{R}:0<\varphi^{\prime}(u)\leq\Phi_{M}^{\prime},0\leq\varphi^{\prime\prime}(u)\leq\Phi_{M}^{\prime\prime}
.

Note that
\ell\in(\ell_{\mathrm{exp}},\ell_{\mathrm{log}})
satisfy this:

\ell_{\mathrm{exp}}(u)=e^{-u}=e^{-\varphi_{\exp}(u)},\quad\ell_{\mathrm{log}}(u)=\log(1+e^{-u})=e^{-\varphi_{\log}(u)}~,

for

\varphi_{\exp}(u)=u,\quad\varphi_{\log}(u)=-\log\log(1+e^{-u})~.

The conditions on
\varphi
imply the following:

1.

\varphi
is strictly monotone increasing and
\ell
is strictly monotone decreasing. Also
u\mapsto\varphi^{\prime}(u)u
is strictly monotone increasing on
[0,\infty)
:

\frac{d\varphi^{\prime}(u)u}{du}=\varphi^{\prime\prime}(u)u+\varphi^{\prime}(u)\geq\varphi^{\prime}(u)>0~.

2.

For any
u_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
,
\varphi
has at least a linear growth rate on
[u_{0},\infty)
:

\varphi(u)-\varphi(u_{0})=\int_{u_{0}}^{u}\varphi^{\prime}(w)dw\geq\varphi^{\prime}(u_{0})(u-u_{0})~.

(23)

In particular
\varphi(u)\overset{u\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\infty
.

3.

\varphi
has a strictly monotone increasing inverse
\varphi^{-1}
defined on
\varphi(\mathbb{R})
. In particular
\varphi^{-1}
is defined on
[\varphi(0),\infty)
,
\varphi^{-1}(u)\overset{u\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\infty
, and
(\varphi^{-1})^{\prime}
is
nonincreasing
since
\left(\varphi^{-1}\right)^{\prime}(u)=\frac{1}{\varphi^{\prime}(\varphi^{-1}(u))}
and
\varphi^{-1},\varphi^{\prime}
are increasing. This implies also that
(\varphi^{-1})^{\prime}
is bounded on
[\varphi(0),\infty)
.

4.

For any
a\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{\pm\infty\}
,
\lim_{u\to a}\ell(u)=0
implies
\lim_{u\to a}\varphi(u)=\infty
, implying
a=\infty
, since
\varphi
is bounded on any compact interval and monotone increasing. Note that since the same is true of
\varphi^{-1}
, this implies also that if
\lim_{u\to a}\varphi^{-1}(u)=\infty
then
a=\infty
.

C.2
Algorithm-Independent KKT Stationarity

In this section we provide general insight into properties of trajectories of homogeneous models, culminating in Theorem
C.8
which shows that KKT stationarity of a limit point of
\frac{\bm{\theta}_{t}}{\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert}
with respect to Problem (
11
) follows from decay of the loss and alignment between gradients and parameters.

Definition C.1

(Hard and Soft Margins)
.

Let
f(\mathbf{x};\bm{\theta})
be a model satisfying
(M1-Weak)
,
(M2-Weak)
. We denote the
“hard”
and
“soft”
margins
\gamma(\bm{\theta}),\widetilde{\gamma}(\bm{\theta})

Lemma C.2

(Properties of Any Trajectory )

(24)

There exists

Proof.

1.

This follows directly from the definition of the loss,

\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta}_{t})=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\ell\left(y_{i}f(\mathbf{x}_{i};\bm{\theta}_{t})\right)~,

noting that

From item 1, when
\bm{\theta}_{t}\neq{\mathbf{0}}

\frac{1}{m}e^{\varphi(q_{\mathrm{min}}^{t})}\leq\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta}_{t})}\leq e^{\varphi(q_{\mathrm{min}}^{t})}~,

(28)

\varphi^{-1}
to the right inequality (recall

is increasing) and divide by

\widetilde{\gamma}(\bm{\theta}_{t})\leq\gamma(\bm{\theta}_{t})~.

Whenever

-\log m+\varphi\left(q_{\mathrm{min}}^{t}\right)>\varphi(0)

\frac{\varphi^{-1}\left(\varphi\left(q_{\mathrm{min}}^{t}\right)-\log m\right)}{\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert^{L}}\leq\widetilde{\gamma}(\bm{\theta}_{t})~.

Applying the mean value theorem, for some

it holds that

q_{\mathrm{min}}^{t}-\varphi^{-1}\left(\varphi\left(q_{\mathrm{min}}^{t}\right)-\log m\right)=\varphi^{-1}(\varphi(q_{\mathrm{min}}^{t}))-\varphi^{-1}\left(\varphi\left(q_{\mathrm{min}}^{t}\right)-\log m\right)=(\varphi^{-1})^{\prime}(u)\cdot(\log m)~.

And since
(\varphi^{-1})^{\prime}
is monotone decreasing,

q_{\mathrm{min}}^{t}-\varphi^{-1}\left(\varphi\left(q_{\mathrm{min}}^{t}\right)-\log m\right)\leq(\varphi^{-1})^{\prime}(\varphi(q_{\mathrm{min}}^{t})-\log m)\cdot\log m~.

Altogether

\gamma(\bm{\theta}_{t})-\frac{(\varphi^{-1})^{\prime}(\varphi(q_{\mathrm{min}}^{t})-\log m)\cdot\log m}{\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert^{L}}\leq\widetilde{\gamma}(\bm{\theta}_{t})~.

(29)

Now assume
\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta}_{t})\overset{t\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}0
. In particular
\ell(q_{\mathrm{min}}^{t})=\ell(\gamma(\bm{\theta}_{t})\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert^{L})\overset{t\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}0
, implying
\varphi(q_{\mathrm{min}}^{t})=\varphi(\gamma(\bm{\theta}_{t})\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert^{L})\overset{t\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\infty
and therefore
\gamma(\bm{\theta}_{t})\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert^{L}\overset{t\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\infty
. In particular this means
\gamma(\bm{\theta}_{t})>0
for all large enough
t
, and since
\gamma(\bm{\theta}_{t})
is bounded, also
\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert\overset{t\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\infty
. Since
\varphi(q_{\mathrm{min}}^{t})-\log m\overset{t\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\infty
we have Equation (
29
) for all large enough
t
.
Finally, since
\varphi(q_{\mathrm{min}}^{t})\overset{t\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\infty,\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert\overset{t\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\infty
and
(\varphi^{-1})^{\prime}
is bounded on
[\varphi(0),\infty)
it holds that

\left|\gamma(\bm{\theta}_{t})-\widetilde{\gamma}(\bm{\theta}_{t})\right|\overset{t\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}0~.

3.

Let
\mathbf{n}_{t}\in\partial\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert
, so by the chain rule for arcs (Theorem
A.6
), and using
{\left\lVert\mathbf{n}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}\leq 1
from Equation (
18
):

\left|\frac{d\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert}{dt}\right|\leq\left|\left\langle\mathbf{n}_{t},\frac{d\bm{\theta}_{t}}{dt}\right\rangle\right|\leq\left\lVert\frac{d\bm{\theta}_{t}}{dt}\right\rVert{\left\lVert\mathbf{n}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}\leq\left\lVert\frac{d\bm{\theta}_{t}}{dt}\right\rVert~.

Therefore

\displaystyle\left\lVert\frac{d\frac{\bm{\theta}_{t}}{\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert}}{dt}\right\rVert=\left\lVert\frac{1}{\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert}\frac{d\bm{\theta}_{t}}{dt}+\bm{\theta}_{t}\left(-\frac{1}{\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}}\frac{d\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert}{dt}\right)\right\rVert\leq\frac{\left\lVert\frac{d\bm{\theta}_{t}}{dt}\right\rVert}{\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert}+\frac{1}{\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert}\left|\frac{d\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert}{dt}\right|\leq 2\cdot\frac{\left\lVert\frac{d\bm{\theta}_{t}}{dt}\right\rVert}{\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert}~.

4.

By the chain rule (Corollary
A.2
), let
\mathbf{h}_{i}^{t}\in\partial f(\mathbf{x}_{i};\bm{\theta}_{t})
with

\mathbf{g}_{t}=-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\ell(z_{i}^{t})\varphi^{\prime}(z_{i}^{t})y_{i}\mathbf{h}_{i}^{t}~.

For the upper bound, using Theorem B.2(a) of
(
28
)
,

\mathbf{g}_{t}=-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\ell\left(z_{i}^{t}\right)\varphi^{\prime}(z_{i}^{t})y_{i}\mathbf{h}_{i}^{t}=-\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert^{L-1}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\ell\left(z_{i}^{t}\right)\varphi^{\prime}(z_{i}^{t})y_{i}\bar{\mathbf{h}}_{i}^{t}~,

where
\bar{\mathbf{h}}_{i}^{t}\in\partial f(\mathbf{x}_{i};\frac{\bm{\theta}_{t}}{\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert})
. On the unit sphere, which is a compact set,
f
is Lipschitz, hence
\exists M^{\prime}>0:{\left\lVert\bar{\mathbf{h}}_{i}^{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}\leq M^{\prime}
for any
t
. So, using
\left|\varphi^{\prime}(z_{i}^{t})\right|\leq\Phi_{M}^{\prime}
,

{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}\leq M^{\prime}\Phi_{M}^{\prime}\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert^{L-1}\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta}_{t})=:M\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert^{L-1}\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta}_{t})~.

For the lower bounds,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}\geq\left\langle\bm{\theta}_{t},-\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\bm{\theta}_{t},\sum_{i=1}^{m}\ell\left(z_{i}^{t}\right)\varphi^{\prime}(z_{i}^{t})y_{i}\mathbf{h}_{i}^{t}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\ell\left(z_{i}^{t}\right)\varphi^{\prime}(z_{i}^{t})y_{i}\left\langle\bm{\theta}_{t},\mathbf{h}_{i}^{t}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=L\sum_{i=1}^{m}\ell\left(z_{i}^{t}\right)\varphi^{\prime}(z_{i}^{t})z_{i}^{t}~.

Where the last equality uses Theorem B.2 from
(
28
)
(Euler’s Theorem for homogeneous functions). Continuing, we use that fact that
u\mapsto\varphi^{\prime}(u)u
is increasing on
[0,\infty)
, that
\log\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}}>\varphi(0)
, that
z_{i}^{t}\geq q_{\mathrm{min}}^{t}\geq\varphi^{-1}(\log\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}})
by previous items, and finally that
\varphi^{\prime}(\varphi^{-1}(u))=\frac{1}{(\varphi^{-1})^{\prime}(u)}
. Altogether, this gives
\varphi^{\prime}(z_{i}^{t})z_{i}^{t}\geq\varphi^{\prime}(\varphi^{-1}(\log\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}}))\cdot\varphi^{-1}(\log\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}})=\frac{\varphi^{-1}(\log\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}})}{(\varphi^{-1})^{\prime}(\log\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}})}
, so we continue with

\displaystyle\geq L\mathcal{L}\min_{i\in[m]}\varphi^{\prime}(z_{i}^{t})z_{i}^{t}

\displaystyle\geq L\mathcal{L}\frac{\varphi^{-1}(\log\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}})}{(\varphi^{-1})^{\prime}(\log\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}})}~.

Also note that if
q_{\mathrm{min}}(\bm{\theta}_{t})>q>0
then
\forall i\in[m]:\varphi^{\prime}(z_{i}^{t})\geq\varphi^{\prime}(q)>0
, and recall
z_{i}^{t}\geq q_{\mathrm{min}}^{t}=\gamma(\bm{\theta}_{t})\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert^{L}
, so

\varphi^{\prime}(q)L\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta}_{t})\gamma(\bm{\theta}_{t})\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert^{L}\leq L\mathcal{L}\min_{i\in[m]}\varphi^{\prime}(z_{i}^{t})z_{i}^{t}\leq\left\langle-\mathbf{g}_{t},\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rangle~,

finishing the lower bounds.

5.

Note that
q_{\mathrm{min}}^{t}\geq N_{min}^{L}\gamma_{min}=:q>0
, so we use Equations (
25
), (
27
) to conclude

\varphi^{\prime}(q)\gamma_{min}L\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta}_{t})\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert^{L-1}\leq{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}\leq M\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert^{L-1}\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta}_{t})~.

We claim the RHS is bounded. Since it is strictly positive, so we show that its logarithm is bounded from above. Indeed,
\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta}_{t})\leq me^{-\varphi(q_{\mathrm{min}}(\bm{\theta}_{t}))}\leq me^{-\varphi(\gamma_{min}\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert^{L})}
, so

\log\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert^{L-1}\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta}_{t})\leq(L-1)\log\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert+\log m-\varphi(\gamma_{min}\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert^{L})~.

On any compact interval
\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert\in[N_{min},N]
this expression is bounded from above by continuity, and by Equation (
23
), choosing
0<u<q
,
\varphi(\gamma_{min}\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert^{L})\geq\varphi(u)+\varphi^{\prime}(u)(\gamma_{min}\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert^{L}-u)
, so the expression tends to
-\infty
as
\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert\to\infty
.

∎

Proof.

First, note that since

\gamma(\bm{\theta}_{t_{n}})>\frac{1}{2}\gamma(\bar{\bm{\theta}})>0
. Therefore it holds by Lemma
C.2

{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t_{n}}\right\rVert}_{\star}\geq C\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta}_{t_{n}})\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t_{n}}\right\rVert^{L-1}~.

Now, any
\bm{\theta}
with
\gamma(\bm{\theta})>0
has for all
i\in[m]
that

and therefore there exists a feasible point

of Equation (

Denote
\hat{\bm{\theta}}:=\widetilde{\bar{\bm{\theta}}}=\frac{\bar{\bm{\theta}}}{\gamma(\bar{\bm{\theta}})^{1/L}}
. We claim
\hat{\bm{\theta}}
is the desired KKT point. We show that in fact
\hat{\bm{\theta}}
is itself a
(\varepsilon_{s},\delta_{s})
-approximate KKT point for arbitrarily small
(\varepsilon_{s},\delta_{s})

C.7
with the constant sequence
\hat{\bm{\theta}}
.

First note that by continuity and scale-invariance of
\gamma(\bm{\theta})

\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}_{t_{n}}=\frac{1}{\gamma(\bm{\theta}_{t_{n}})^{1/L}}\frac{\bm{\theta}_{t_{n}}}{\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t_{n}}\right\rVert}\overset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\frac{1}{\gamma(\bar{\bm{\theta}})^{1/L}}\bar{\bm{\theta}}=\hat{\bm{\theta}}~.

Take subgradients
\mathbf{h}_{i}^{t}\in\partial f(\mathbf{x}_{i},\bm{\theta}_{t})
defining
\mathbf{g}_{t}
by the chain rule as in Lemma
C.2
. Since
\left(q_{\mathrm{min}}(\bm{\theta}_{t})^{-\frac{1}{L}}\right)^{L-1}=q_{\mathrm{min}}(\bm{\theta}_{t})^{\frac{1}{L}-1}

28

\hat{\bm{\theta}}

there exists a subsequence

for some
\hat{\mathbf{h}}_{i}\in\partial f(\mathbf{x}_{i};\hat{\bm{\theta}})
. By iteratively choosing subsequences for
i=1,...,m
we may assume that
\widetilde{\mathbf{h}}_{i}^{(t_{s})}\rightarrow\hat{\mathbf{h}}_{i}
for all
i\in[m]
. Take a Bolzano-Weierstrass limit
\mathbf{u}^{\star}
of
-\frac{\mathbf{g}_{t_{s}}}{{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t_{s}}\right\rVert}_{\star}}
, and again abuse notation by assuming w.l.o.g

. It therefore holds that
{\left\lVert\mathbf{u}^{\star}\right\rVert}_{\star}=1
and

Denote

Note that

and
{\left\lVert\mathbf{u}^{\star}\right\rVert}_{\star}=1
. Choosing
\hat{\mathbf{h}}_{i},i\in[m]

C.4

\varepsilon_{s},\delta_{s}\to 0

(31)

The second term goes to 0 since
-\frac{\mathbf{g}_{t_{s}}}{{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t_{s}}\right\rVert}_{\star}}\rightarrow\mathbf{u}^{\star}
. To show the first term goes to 0, we use

are bounded. We use
{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t_{s}}\right\rVert}_{\star}\geq C\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta}_{t_{s}})\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t_{s}}\right\rVert^{L-1}

\displaystyle\lambda_{s,i}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left\lVert\hat{\bm{\theta}}\right\rVert q_{\mathrm{min}}(\bm{\theta}_{t_{s}})^{1-\frac{1}{L}}\ell\left(z_{i}^{t}\right)\varphi^{\prime}(z_{i}^{t})}{{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t_{s}}\right\rVert}_{\star}}\leq\frac{\Phi_{M}^{\prime}\left\lVert\hat{\bm{\theta}}\right\rVert\gamma(\bm{\theta}_{t_{s}})^{1-\frac{1}{L}}\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t_{s}}\right\rVert^{L-1}\mathcal{L}}{{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t_{s}}\right\rVert}_{\star}}

since
\gamma
is bounded. For
\delta_{s}

\delta_{s}=\left|\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{s,i}(y_{i}f(\mathbf{x}_{i};\hat{\bm{\theta}})-1)\right|\leq\left|\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{s,i}(y_{i}f(\mathbf{x}_{i};\hat{\bm{\theta}})-y_{i}f(\mathbf{x}_{i};\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}_{t_{s}}))\right|+\left|\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{s,i}(y_{i}f(\mathbf{x}_{i};\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}_{t_{s}})-1)\right|

(32)

The first term goes to 0 from continuity of

\lambda_{s,i}
. As for the second term, note that each summand is non-negative. Plugging in
\lambda_{s,i}
(here
z_{i}=z_{i}^{t_{s}}
, and similarly for
q_{\mathrm{min}},\mathcal{L}
),

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{s,i}\big(y_{i}f(\mathbf{x}_{i};\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}_{t_{s}})-1\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{\left\lVert\hat{\bm{\theta}}\right\rVert}{{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t_{s}}\right\rVert}_{\star}}\sum_{i=1}^{m}q_{\mathrm{min}}^{1-\frac{1}{L}}\ell\left(z_{i}\right)\varphi^{\prime}(z_{i})\left(\frac{z_{i}}{q_{\mathrm{min}}}-1\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\left\lVert\hat{\bm{\theta}}\right\rVert}{q_{\mathrm{min}}^{1/L}{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t_{s}}\right\rVert}_{\star}}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\varphi^{\prime}(z_{i})\ell\left(z_{i}\right)\left(z_{i}-q_{\mathrm{min}}\right)

\displaystyle{\leq}\frac{\left\lVert\hat{\bm{\theta}}\right\rVert}{\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t_{s}}\right\rVert\gamma(\bm{\theta}_{t_{s}})^{1/L}C\mathcal{L}\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t_{s}}\right\rVert^{L-1}}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\varphi^{\prime}(z_{i})e^{-\varphi(z_{i})}\left(z_{i}-q_{\mathrm{min}}\right)

\displaystyle\overset{s\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}0

The first inequality is again by
{{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}\geq C\mathcal{L}\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert^{L-1}}

,

, so by Equation (

\forall i\in[m],z_{i}^{t_{s}}>q_{\mathrm{min}}^{t_{s}}:\frac{z_{i}^{t_{s}}-q_{\mathrm{min}}^{t_{s}}}{\varphi(z_{i}^{t_{s}})-\varphi(q_{\mathrm{min}}^{t_{s}})}\leq\frac{z_{i}^{t_{s}}-q_{\mathrm{min}}^{t_{s}}}{\varphi^{\prime}(1)(z_{i}^{t_{s}}-q_{\mathrm{min}}^{t_{s}})}=\frac{1}{\varphi^{\prime}(1)}<\infty

∎

C.4
Approximate Steepest Descent

In this section we prove a KKT result for trajectories
\bm{\theta}_{t}
approximating steepest descent. We provide the following definition:

Definition C.12

(Approximate Steepest Descent)
.

We say an arc
\bm{\theta}_{t}
is a trajectory of Approximate Steepest Descent with respect to a norm
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert
if eventually
\exists\mathbf{g}_{t}\neq{\mathbf{0}}\in\partial\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta}_{t})
for almost any
t
, and there exist
\nu(t)>0,R_{\text{max}}>0
with:

1.

\lim_{t\to\infty}N(t):=\lim_{t\to\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\nu=\infty

2.

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert}{N(t)}\leq R_{\text{max}}

3.

\essliminf_{t\to\infty}r(t)\geq 1
, where

r(t)\overset{a.e.}{=}\sup_{\mathbf{g}_{t}\in\partial\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta}_{t})\setminus\{{\mathbf{0}}\}}\left\langle\frac{1}{\nu(t)}\frac{d\bm{\theta}_{t}}{dt},-\frac{\mathbf{g}_{t}}{{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}}\right\rangle~.

Note that as
\partial\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta}_{t})
is a compact set for any locally Lipschitz
\mathcal{L}
, the supremum in the definition of
r(t)
is attained by some
\mathbf{g}_{t}\in\partial\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta}_{t})
.

Some motivation is in order. A possible and natural choice of
\nu(t)
is
\nu(t)=\left\lVert\frac{d\bm{\theta}_{t}}{dt}\right\rVert
. This choice is motivated by the fact that for exact Steepest Flow, we have by definition for almost any
t

r(t)=\left\langle\frac{1}{\nu(t)}\frac{d\bm{\theta}_{t}}{dt},-\frac{\mathbf{g}_{t}}{{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}}\right\rangle=1~,

and

\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert\leq\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{0}\right\rVert+\int_{0}^{t}\nu~.

Also, the following type of trajectory, which is a simpler and more
“natural”
definition of Approximate Steepest Descent, is also covered by our definition when choosing
\nu(t)=\left\lVert\frac{d\bm{\theta}_{t}}{dt}\right\rVert
:

\left\langle\frac{\frac{d\bm{\theta}_{t}}{dt}}{\left\lVert\frac{d\bm{\theta}_{t}}{dt}\right\rVert},-\frac{\mathbf{g}_{t}}{{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}}\right\rangle\overset{t\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}1~.

It is crucial in our analysis for an inner product of this type to tend to 1. However, we can allow
\nu(t)
to be momentarily larger than
\left\lVert\frac{d\bm{\theta}_{t}}{dt}\right\rVert
, as long as on average it is not so (condition 2, when
R_{\text{max}}\leq 1
). Thus the definition allows more flexibility for adaptive algorithms such as Adam, since we can choose
\nu(t)
as an external learning rate, with
\frac{d\bm{\theta}_{t}}{dt}
affected both by
\nu(t)
and by the dynamics. In particular this flexibility will be invaluable for the main result of KKT convergence for Adam.

\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta}_{t})=\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta}_{t_{1}})+\int_{t_{1}}^{t}\frac{d\mathcal{L}}{ds}ds<\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta}_{t_{1}})\leq m\cdot\ell\left(\gamma_{min}\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t_{1}}\right\rVert^{L}\right)<m\cdot\ell(0)~.

\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert\geq\Omega(1)
, because if towards a contradiction there existed a sequence
t_{n}\overset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\infty
with
\bm{\theta}_{t_{n}}\overset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}{\mathbf{0}}
, this would imply by continuity of
f
that
\forall i\in[m]:f(\mathbf{x}_{i};\bm{\theta}_{t_{n}})\to f(\mathbf{x}_{i};\mathbf{0})=0
and therefore
\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta}_{t_{n}})\overset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}m\cdot\ell(0)
, a contradiction. Therefore denote
N_{min}=\inf_{t\geq t_{1}}\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert>0
. By Lemma
C.2
, there exists
C>0
with
{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}\geq C\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta}_{t})\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert^{L-1}
.

So, by Lemma
C.14
, for almost any
t\geq t_{1}
:

\displaystyle-\frac{d\mathcal{L}}{dt}

\displaystyle=r(t)\nu(t){\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}\geq r(t)\nu(t)C\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert^{L-1}\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{N_{min}^{L-1}C}{2}\nu(t)\mathcal{L}~.

Dividing by
\mathcal{L}
and integrating both sides on
[t_{1},t]
we get

-\log\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta}_{t})-(-\log\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta}_{t_{1}}))\geq\frac{N_{min}^{L-1}C}{2}(N(t)-N(t_{1}))\overset{t\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\infty~,

implying
\mathcal{L}\rightarrow 0
and therefore
\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert\rightarrow\infty
.
∎

Lemma C.16
.

Assume
\bm{\theta}_{t}
is a trajectory of Approximate Steepest Descent with
R_{\text{max}}\leq 1
, and assume that eventually
\bm{\theta}_{t}\neq 0
and
\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\bm{\theta}_{t}}{\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert}=\bar{\bm{\theta}}
with
\gamma(\bar{\bm{\theta}})>0
. Then there exists a sequence
t_{n}\to\infty
with
\left\langle\frac{\bm{\theta}_{t_{n}}}{\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t_{n}}\right\rVert},-\frac{\mathbf{g}_{t_{n}}}{{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t_{n}}\right\rVert}_{\star}}\right\rangle\to 1
.

Proof.

Since
\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\bm{\theta}_{t}}{\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert}=\bar{\bm{\theta}}
with
\gamma(\bar{\bm{\theta}})>0
, eventually
\gamma(\bm{\theta}_{t})>\frac{1}{2}\gamma(\bar{\bm{\theta}})>0
, so from Lemma
C.15
,
\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta}_{t})\to 0,\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert\to\infty
. By Lemma
C.2
we conclude
\left|\gamma(\bm{\theta}_{t})-\widetilde{\gamma}(\bm{\theta}_{t})\right|\to 0
, so
\gamma(\bm{\theta}_{t}),\widetilde{\gamma}(\bm{\theta}_{t})
both converge to
\gamma(\bar{\bm{\theta}})>0
, hence also
\log\widetilde{\gamma}(\bm{\theta}_{t})
converges. We build a sequence
t_{n}
by induction. Choose
t_{0}
with
\forall t\geq t_{0}:\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta}_{t})<\ell(0)
, and suppose
t_{0}<...<t_{n-1}
have been chosen. Choose
\tau_{1}>t_{n-1}+1
with

\sup_{\tau_{2}\geq\tau_{1}}\log\left(\frac{\widetilde{\gamma}(\bm{\theta}_{\tau_{2}})}{\widetilde{\gamma}(\bm{\theta}_{\tau_{1}})}\right)\leq\frac{L}{n}

\forall t>\tau_{1}:r(t)>1-\frac{1}{n}\ a.e.\

For almost any
t\geq t_{0}
,
\exists\mathbf{g}_{t}\neq{\mathbf{0}}\in\partial\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta}_{t})
with:

\displaystyle\frac{d\log\widetilde{\gamma}}{dt}

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dt}\log\varphi^{-1}\left(\log\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta}_{t})}\right)-L\frac{d}{dt}\log\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert

\displaystyle=-\frac{d\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta}_{t})}{dt}\frac{(\varphi^{-1})^{\prime}(\log\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta}_{t})})}{\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta}_{t})\varphi^{-1}(\log\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta}_{t})})}-L\frac{d}{dt}\log\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert

\displaystyle=\nu(t){\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}r(t)\frac{(\varphi^{-1})^{\prime}(\log\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta}_{t})})}{\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta}_{t})\varphi^{-1}(\log\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta}_{t})})}-L\frac{d}{dt}\log\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert

\displaystyle\geq\frac{L\nu(t)}{\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert}\frac{r(t)}{\left\langle\frac{\bm{\theta}_{t}}{\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert},-\frac{\mathbf{g}_{t}}{{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}}\right\rangle}-L\frac{d}{dt}\log\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert~.

In the last inequality we used Equation (
26
), namely
\left\langle\bm{\theta}_{t},-\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rangle\geq L\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta}_{t})\frac{\varphi^{-1}(\log\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta}_{t})})}{(\varphi^{-1})^{\prime}(\log\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta}_{t})})}
, implying
\frac{(\varphi^{-1})^{\prime}(\log\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta}_{t})})}{\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta}_{t})\varphi^{-1}(\log\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta}_{t})})}\geq\frac{L}{\left\langle\bm{\theta}_{t},-\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rangle}
.

So, rearranging terms and integrating on
[\tau_{1},\tau_{2}]
for any
\tau_{2}>\tau_{1}
,

\frac{1}{L}\log\left(\frac{\widetilde{\gamma}(\bm{\theta}_{\tau_{2}})}{\widetilde{\gamma}(\bm{\theta}_{\tau_{1}})}\right)+\log\frac{\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{\tau_{2}}\right\rVert}{\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{\tau_{1}}\right\rVert}\geq\int_{\tau_{1}}^{\tau_{2}}\frac{\nu(t)}{\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert}\frac{r(t)}{\left\langle\frac{\bm{\theta}_{t}}{\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert},-\frac{\mathbf{g}_{t}}{{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}}\right\rangle}dt~.

In particular, by a standard proof-by-contradiction argument (see Theorem
C.11
), for every
\tau_{2}
there exists a non-zero measure set of points
t_{n}\in(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})
with

\frac{\frac{1}{L}\log\left(\frac{\widetilde{\gamma}(\bm{\theta}_{\tau_{2}})}{\widetilde{\gamma}(\bm{\theta}_{\tau_{1}})}\right)+\log\frac{\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{\tau_{2}}\right\rVert}{\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{\tau_{1}}\right\rVert}}{\int_{\tau_{1}}^{\tau_{2}}\frac{\nu(t)}{\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert}dt}\geq\frac{r(t_{n})}{\left\langle\frac{\bm{\theta}_{t_{n}}}{\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t_{n}}\right\rVert},-\frac{\mathbf{g}_{t_{n}}}{{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t_{n}}\right\rVert}_{\star}}\right\rangle}~.

In particular
t_{n}
can be chosen with
r(t_{n})>1-\frac{1}{n}
.

Note that for any
F(t),G(t)
with
\limsup{\frac{F}{G}}\leq 1
and
\int_{0}^{\infty}G=\infty
it holds that
\limsup\frac{\int_{0}^{t}F}{\int_{0}^{t}G}\leq 1
. Indeed, for any
\varepsilon>0
take
t_{\varepsilon}
with
\forall t\geq t_{\varepsilon}:F(t)\leq(1+\varepsilon)G(t)
, so
\frac{\int_{0}^{t}F}{\int_{0}^{t}G}=\frac{\int_{0}^{t_{\varepsilon}}F+\int_{t_{\varepsilon}}^{t}F}{\int_{0}^{t_{\varepsilon}}G+\int_{t_{\varepsilon}}^{t}G}\leq\frac{\int_{0}^{t_{\varepsilon}}F+(1+\varepsilon)\int_{t_{\varepsilon}}^{t}G}{\int_{0}^{t_{\varepsilon}}G+\int_{t_{\varepsilon}}^{t}G}\overset{t\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}1+\varepsilon
. Also, note that if
\limsup\frac{F}{G}\leq 1
and
\int_{0}^{\infty}F=\infty
then in particular
\int_{0}^{\infty}G=\infty
.

In our case, since
\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\nu(t)}{N(t)}\big/\frac{\nu(t)}{\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert}=\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert}{N(t)}\leq 1
, and
\int_{\tau_{1}}^{\infty}\frac{\nu(t)}{N(t)}dt=\lim_{t\to\infty}\log\frac{N(t)}{N(\tau_{1})}=\infty
(implying also
\int_{\tau_{1}}^{\infty}\frac{\nu(t)}{\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert}dt=\infty
), there exists
q(\tau_{2})
with
q(\tau_{2})\overset{\tau_{2}\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}1
and

\int_{\tau_{1}}^{\tau_{2}}\frac{\nu(t)}{\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert}dt\geq q(\tau_{2})\int_{\tau_{1}}^{\tau_{2}}\frac{\nu(t)}{N(t)}dt=q(\tau_{2})\log\left(\frac{N(\tau_{2})}{N(\tau_{1})}\right)~.

Since
\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert,N(t)\to\infty
and again since
\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert}{N(t)}\leq 1
there exists large enough
\tau_{2}
with
\int_{\tau_{1}}^{\tau_{2}}\frac{\nu(t)}{\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert}dt\geq 1
and

1+\frac{1}{n}\geq\frac{\log\frac{\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{\tau_{2}}\right\rVert}{\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{\tau_{1}}\right\rVert}}{q(\tau_{2})\log\frac{N(\tau_{2})}{N(\tau_{1})}}~.

Altogether for such
\tau_{2}
and
t_{n}\in(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})
as above,

\frac{\frac{1}{L}\log\left(\frac{\widetilde{\gamma}(\bm{\theta}_{\tau_{2}})}{\widetilde{\gamma}(\bm{\theta}_{\tau_{1}})}\right)+\log\frac{\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{\tau_{2}}\right\rVert}{\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{\tau_{1}}\right\rVert}}{\int_{\tau_{1}}^{\tau_{2}}\frac{\nu(t)}{\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert}dt}\leq\frac{1}{n}+\left(1+\frac{1}{n}\right)=1+\frac{2}{n}~.

So,

\left\langle\frac{\bm{\theta}_{t_{n}}}{\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t_{n}}\right\rVert},-\frac{\mathbf{g}_{t_{n}}}{{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t_{n}}\right\rVert}_{\star}}\right\rangle\geq\frac{1-\frac{1}{n}}{1+\frac{2}{n}}\overset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}1~.

And the other direction is immediate by the definition of the dual norm, implying
\left\langle\frac{\bm{\theta}_{t_{n}}}{\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t_{n}}\right\rVert},-\frac{\mathbf{g}_{t_{n}}}{{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t_{n}}\right\rVert}_{\star}}\right\rangle\overset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}1
.
∎

Lemma C.20
.

Let

{\left\lVert\mathbf{w}\right\rVert}_{\star}-{\left\lVert\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{w}\right\rVert}_{\star}\leq{\left\lVert\mathbf{u}\right\rVert}_{\star}\leq{\left\lVert\mathbf{w}\right\rVert}_{\star}+{\left\lVert\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{w}\right\rVert}_{\star}

This gives

\quad\left|\frac{{\left\lVert\mathbf{u}\right\rVert}_{\star}}{{\left\lVert\mathbf{w}\right\rVert}_{\star}}-1\right|\leq\frac{{\left\lVert\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{w}\right\rVert}_{\star}}{{\left\lVert\mathbf{w}\right\rVert}_{\star}}\leq\xi

\displaystyle{\left\lVert\frac{\mathbf{u}}{{\left\lVert\mathbf{u}\right\rVert}_{\star}}-\frac{\mathbf{w}}{{\left\lVert\mathbf{w}\right\rVert}_{\star}}\right\rVert}_{\star}

\displaystyle\leq{\left\lVert\frac{\mathbf{u}}{{\left\lVert\mathbf{u}\right\rVert}_{\star}}-\frac{\mathbf{u}}{{\left\lVert\mathbf{w}\right\rVert}_{\star}}\right\rVert}_{\star}+{\left\lVert\frac{\mathbf{u}}{{\left\lVert\mathbf{w}\right\rVert}_{\star}}-\frac{\mathbf{w}}{{\left\lVert\mathbf{w}\right\rVert}_{\star}}\right\rVert}_{\star}

B.8
,

\frac{\mathbf{m}_{t}^{\infty}[i,j]}{\mathbf{g}_{t}^{\infty}[i,j]}\to 1,\quad\frac{\mathbf{v}_{t}^{\infty}[i,i^{\prime},j]}{\mathbf{g}_{t}^{\infty}[i,j]\mathbf{g}_{t}^{\infty}[i^{\prime},j]}\to 1~.

(41)

Also, the fact that
\frac{d\log\left|\mathbf{g}_{t}^{\infty}[i,j]\right|}{dt}\to 0
implies that
\forall c>0
and
\forall i,i^{\prime}\in I_{j}
,

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{cs}\left|g_{s}^{\infty}[i,j]\right|ds=\infty,\quad\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{cs}\left|g_{s}^{\infty}[i^{\prime},j]g_{s}^{\infty}[i^{\prime},j]\right|ds=\infty~.

So by Lemma
B.5
2(c) and Equation (
39
),

\frac{\mathbf{m}_{t}[i,j]}{\mathbf{m}_{t}^{\infty}[i,j]}\to 1,\quad\quad\frac{\mathbf{v}_{t}[i,i,^{\prime}j]}{\mathbf{v}_{t}^{\infty}[i,i^{\prime},j]}\to 1~.

(42)

Putting together (
39
), (
41
), (
42
), we get
\forall i,i^{\prime}\in I_{j}
:

\frac{\mathbf{m}_{t}[i,j]}{\mathbf{g}_{t}[i,j]}\overset{t\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}1,\quad\frac{\mathbf{v}_{t}[i,i^{\prime},j]}{\mathbf{g}_{t}[i,j]\mathbf{g}_{t}[i^{\prime},j]}\overset{t\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}1~.

(43)

Denote
G(t)=\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert^{L-1}\ell(q_{\mathrm{min}}^{t})>0
, and notice that since
\forall t\geq 0,\exists i\in[m]:q_{\mathrm{min}}^{t}=\gamma(\bm{\theta}_{t})\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert^{L}=y_{i}f(\mathbf{x}_{i};\bm{\theta}_{t})
it holds as in Equation (
40
) that
G
is differentiable almost everywhere, locally absolutely continuous and
\esslim_{t\to\infty}\frac{d\log G}{dt}=0
, implying by Corollary
B.8
that
\frac{A(G,c)}{G}\to 1
for any
c>0
.

Since
\gamma(\bar{\bm{\theta}})>0
and by Assumption
(T1)
, by Lemma
C.2
we have
{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}=\Theta(\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert^{L-1}\mathcal{L})=\Theta(G(t))
. Also note that for every
j\in[p]
and
\bar{i}\notin I_{j}
it holds that
\bar{\mathbf{h}}_{\infty}[\bar{i},j]=0
, so
\left|\mathbf{g}_{t}[\bar{i},j]\right|\leq\mathrm{o}\left(\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert^{L-1}\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta}_{t})\right)\leq\mathrm{o}\left(G(t)\right)
. By Lemma
B.5
, item 2(c) (applied with
g=G,F=\mathbf{g}_{t}[\bar{i},j]
), it follows also that
\left|\mathbf{m}_{t}[\bar{i},j]\right|\leq\mathrm{o}\left(A(G,c_{1})(t)\right)\leq\mathrm{o}\left(G(t)\right)\leq\mathrm{o}\left({\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}\right)
.

Therefore, for any
j\in[p]
,

\forall\bar{i}\notin I_{j}:\left|\mathbf{m}_{t}[\bar{i},j]-\mathbf{g}_{t}[\bar{i},j]\right|\leq\left|\mathbf{m}_{t}[\bar{i},j]\right|+\left|\mathbf{g}_{t}[\bar{i},j]\right|=\mathrm{o}\left({\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}\right)~.

Also, for any
i\in[m],j\in[p]:\left|\mathbf{g}_{t}[i,j]\right|\leq\mathcal{O}\left(\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert^{L-1}\mathcal{L}\right)=\mathcal{O}\left({\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}\right)
. Therefore by Equation (
43
),

\forall i\in I_{j}:\left|\mathbf{m}_{t}[i,j]-\mathbf{g}_{t}[i,j]\right|=\mathrm{o}\left(\mathbf{g}_{t}[i,j]\right)=\mathrm{o}\left({\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}\right)~.

So altogether, for any
j\in[p]

\left|\mathbf{m}_{t}[j]-\mathbf{g}_{t}[j]\right|\leq\sum_{i\in[m]}\left|\mathbf{m}_{t}[i,j]-\mathbf{g}_{t}[i,j]\right|=\mathrm{o}\left({\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}\right)~.

(44)

Therefore, denoting
J_{\varepsilon}(t)=\{j\in[p]:\frac{\left|\mathbf{g}_{t}[j]\right|}{{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}}>\varepsilon\}
we have:

\forall j\in J_{\varepsilon}(t):\left|\frac{\mathbf{m}_{t}[j]}{\mathbf{g}_{t}[j]}-1\right|=\frac{\left|\mathbf{m}_{t}[j]-\mathbf{g}_{t}[j]\right|}{\left|\mathbf{g}_{t}[j]\right|}\leq\frac{\mathrm{o}\left({\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}\right)}{\varepsilon{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}}\overset{t\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}0~,

which gives item 1 for
\mathbf{m}_{t}
. We repeat the argument with
\mathbf{v}_{t}
and
\mathbf{g}_{t}^{2}
. Whenever
i\in[m],\bar{i}\notin I_{j}
we have
\mathbf{g}_{t}[i,j]\mathbf{g}_{t}[\bar{i},j]=\mathrm{o}\left({\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}^{2}\right)
and
\mathbf{v}_{t}[i,\bar{i},j]=\mathrm{o}\left({\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}^{2}\right)
by applying Lemma
B.5
2(c) (with
g=G^{2},F=\mathbf{g}_{t}[i,j]\mathbf{g}_{t}[\bar{i},j]
), so

\forall i\in[m],\bar{i}\notin I_{j}:\left|\mathbf{v}_{t}[i,\bar{i},j]-\mathbf{g}_{t}[i,j]\mathbf{g}_{t}[\bar{i},j]\right|\leq\mathrm{o}\left({\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}^{2}\right)+\mathrm{o}\left({\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}^{2}\right)~,

and by Equation (
43
)

\forall i,i^{\prime}\in I_{j}:\left|\mathbf{v}_{t}[i,i^{\prime},j]-\mathbf{g}_{t}[i,j]\mathbf{g}_{t}[i^{\prime},j]\right|=\mathrm{o}\left(\mathbf{g}_{t}[i,j]\mathbf{g}_{t}[i^{\prime},j]\right)=\mathrm{o}\left({\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}^{2}\right)~,

so

\forall j\in[p]:\left|\mathbf{v}_{t}[j]-\mathbf{g}_{t}^{2}[j]\right|\leq\mathrm{o}\left({\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}^{2}\right)~.

(45)

Hence,

\forall j\in J_{\varepsilon}(t):\left|\frac{\mathbf{v}_{t}[j]}{\mathbf{g}_{t}^{2}[j]}-1\right|=\frac{\left|\mathbf{v}_{t}[j]-\mathbf{g}_{t}^{2}[j]\right|}{\left|\mathbf{g}_{t}^{2}[j]\right|}\leq\frac{\mathrm{o}\left({\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}^{2}\right)}{\varepsilon^{2}{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}^{2}}\overset{t\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}0

This gives the result of item 1 for
\mathbf{v}_{t}
.

Notice that from norm equivalence,
{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}^{2}\leq\mathcal{O}\left(\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert_{\infty}^{2}\right)=\mathcal{O}\left(\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}^{2}\right\rVert_{\infty}\right)\leq\mathcal{O}\left({\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}^{2}\right\rVert}_{\star}\right)
. Therefore Equations (
44
), (
45
) imply that there exists a vanishing
e(t)\overset{t\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}0
with

{\left\lVert\mathbf{m}_{t}-\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}\leq e(t){\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star},\quad{\left\lVert\mathbf{v}_{t}-\mathbf{g}_{t}^{2}\right\rVert}_{\star}\leq e(t){\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}^{2}\right\rVert}_{\star}~.

Thus item 2 follows from Lemma
C.20
(note that
\mathbf{g}_{t}\neq 0
since
{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}\geq\Omega(\mathcal{L}\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert^{L-1})>0
by Assumptions
(T1)
,
(T2)
and Lemma
C.2
, and therefore
\mathbf{m}_{t},\mathbf{v}_{t}\neq 0
by Equations (
44
), (
45
)).

∎

Theorem C.21

(KKT Stationarity for Momentum SD - Implies Theorem
3.3
)
.

Assume
\bm{\theta}_{t}
follows a trajectory of normalized or unnormalized momentum steepest descent under Assumptions
(M1-Weak)
,
(M2)
,
(LR-MSD)
,
(T1)
,
(T2)
,
(T3)
.
Then
\bar{\bm{\theta}}=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\bm{\theta}_{t}}{\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert}
is along the direction of a KKT point of the
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert
-max-margin problem (Equation (
11
)).

Proof.

We claim that in both the normalized and unnormalized cases it holds that
\left\lVert\frac{d\bm{\theta}_{t}}{dt}\right\rVert\leq\mathrm{o}\left(t^{\frac{1}{L}-1}\right)
. In the normalized case this is straightforward since
\left\lVert\frac{d\theta_{t}}{dt}\right\rVert=\eta(t)\leq\mathrm{o}\left(t^{\frac{1}{L}-1}\right)
. In the unnormalized case, by
(T1)
,
(T2)
, it holds that
{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}=\Theta(\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert^{L-1}\mathcal{L})
is bounded (Lemma
C.2
), so
{\left\lVert\mathbf{m}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}
is bounded (Corollary
B.6
), implying that also in this case
\left\lVert\frac{d\bm{\theta}_{t}}{dt}\right\rVert=\eta(t){\left\lVert\mathbf{m}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}\leq\mathcal{O}\left(\eta(t)\right)\leq\mathrm{o}\left(t^{\frac{1}{L}-1}\right)
.

Note that for any
\alpha>0
it holds that

\arg\min_{\left\lVert\mathbf{u}\right\rVert=\alpha}\left\langle\mathbf{u},\mathbf{m}_{t}\right\rangle=\arg\min_{\left\lVert\mathbf{u}\right\rVert=1}\left\langle\alpha\mathbf{u},\mathbf{m}_{t}\right\rangle=\alpha\arg\min_{\left\lVert\mathbf{u}\right\rVert=1}\left\langle\mathbf{u},\mathbf{m}_{t}\right\rangle~,

so in both the normalized and unnormalized cases, it holds that

\frac{\frac{d\bm{\theta}_{t}}{dt}}{\left\lVert\frac{d\bm{\theta}_{t}}{dt}\right\rVert}=\arg\min_{\left\lVert\mathbf{u}\right\rVert=1}\left\langle\mathbf{u},\mathbf{m}_{t}\right\rangle~.

Thus

\displaystyle\left\langle\frac{\frac{d\bm{\theta}_{t}}{dt}}{\left\lVert\frac{d\bm{\theta}_{t}}{dt}\right\rVert},-\frac{\mathbf{g}_{t}}{{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\arg\min_{\left\lVert\mathbf{u}\right\rVert=1}\left\langle\mathbf{u},\mathbf{m}_{t}\right\rangle,-\frac{\mathbf{g}_{t}}{{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}}\right\rangle=\left\langle\arg\min_{\left\lVert\mathbf{u}\right\rVert=1}\left\langle\mathbf{u},\mathbf{m}_{t}\right\rangle,-\frac{\mathbf{m}_{t}}{{\left\lVert\mathbf{m}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}}+\frac{\mathbf{m}_{t}}{{\left\lVert\mathbf{m}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}}-\frac{\mathbf{g}_{t}}{{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=1+\left\langle\arg\min_{\left\lVert\mathbf{u}\right\rVert=1}\left\langle\mathbf{u},\mathbf{m}_{t}\right\rangle,\frac{\mathbf{m}_{t}}{{\left\lVert\mathbf{m}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}}-\frac{\mathbf{g}_{t}}{{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}}\right\rangle~.

We claim that
\bm{\theta}_{t}
is an approximate steepest descent trajectory (Definition
C.12
) with
\nu(t)=\left\lVert\frac{d\bm{\theta}_{t}}{dt}\right\rVert
and
R_{max}\leq 1
. We have already seen
{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}\neq 0
. By Lemma
C.13
, it suffices to show that
\esslim_{t\to\infty}\left\langle\frac{\frac{d\bm{\theta}_{t}}{dt}}{\left\lVert\frac{d\bm{\theta}_{t}}{dt}\right\rVert},-\frac{\mathbf{g}_{t}}{{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}}\right\rangle=1
. It therefore suffices to show that

\esslim_{t\to\infty}\left\langle\arg\min_{\left\lVert\mathbf{u}\right\rVert=1}\left\langle\mathbf{u},\mathbf{m}_{t}\right\rangle,\frac{\mathbf{m}_{t}}{{\left\lVert\mathbf{m}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}}-\frac{\mathbf{g}_{t}}{{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}}\right\rangle=0~.

And since
\left\lVert\arg\min_{\left\lVert\mathbf{u}\right\rVert=1}\left\langle\mathbf{u},\mathbf{m}_{t}\right\rangle\right\rVert=1
, it suffices to show that

\frac{\mathbf{m}_{t}}{{\left\lVert\mathbf{m}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}}-\frac{\mathbf{g}_{t}}{{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}}\overset{t\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}0~.

And this is a result of Lemma
C.19
. Now the KKT result follows from Theorem
C.17
.
∎

C.6
Composite MSD Algorithms, Muon and Muon-Signum

In this section we prove a simple lemma showing that by partitioning the parameters of a model and training each part with a different normalized SD/MSD algorithm, the resulting algorithm is itself normalized SD/MSD with respect to the maximum among the norms. This allows formulating Muon (on multi-layer networks) and composite algorithms (such as running Muon on matrices and Signum on non-matrix parameters) as normalized momentum steepest descent algorithms.

Lemma C.22

(Dual of Max Norm)
.

Let
\bm{\theta}=\left(\mathbf{u}_{1},...,\mathbf{u}_{K}\right)
be a product representation of
\mathbb{R}^{p}
, with
\mathbf{u}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{p_{k}},\sum_{k}p_{k}=p
. Let
\{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{(k)}\}_{k=1}^{K}
be norms on
\mathbb{R}^{p_{k}}
with dual norms
\{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{(k),\star}\}_{k=1}^{K}
. Denote
\left\lVert\bm{\theta}\right\rVert=\max_{k}\left\lVert\mathbf{u}_{k}\right\rVert
. Then
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert
is a norm, and its dual norm is
{\left\lVert\bm{\theta}\right\rVert}_{\star}=\sum_{k}\left\lVert\mathbf{u}_{k}\right\rVert_{(k),\star}
.

Proof.

First, it is a standard result that
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert
is a norm (one can easily verify that
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert
satisfies positive definiteness, homogeneity and the triangle inequality). Note that

\displaystyle{\left\lVert\bm{\theta}\right\rVert}_{\star}

\displaystyle:=\max\left\{\left\langle\mathbf{x},\bm{\theta}\right\rangle\mid\left\lVert\mathbf{x}\right\rVert=1\right\}

\displaystyle=\max\left\{\sum_{k}\left\langle\mathbf{x}_{k},\mathbf{u}_{k}\right\rangle\mid\max_{k}\left\lVert\mathbf{x}_{k}\right\rVert_{(k)}=1\right\}

\displaystyle=\max\left\{\sum_{k}\left\lVert\mathbf{x}_{k}\right\rVert_{(k)}\left\lVert\mathbf{u}_{k}\right\rVert_{(k),\star}\mid\max_{k}\left\lVert\mathbf{x}_{k}\right\rVert_{(k)}=1\right\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\left\lVert\mathbf{u}_{k}\right\rVert_{(k),\star}~.

The third equality holds since for each
k
, by definition of the dual norm
\left\langle\mathbf{x}_{k},\mathbf{u}_{k}\right\rangle
is upper bounded by
\left\lVert\mathbf{x}_{k}\right\rVert_{(k)}\left\lVert\mathbf{u}_{k}\right\rVert_{(k),\star}
and this bound is attained by choosing
\mathbf{x}_{k}
in the direction that defines
\left\lVert\mathbf{u}_{k}\right\rVert_{(k),\star}
. The last equality holds by choosing
\forall k:\left\lVert\mathbf{x}_{k}\right\rVert_{(k)}=1
.
∎

Lemma C.23

(Composing SD and MSD Algorithms)
.

Let
\bm{\theta}_{t}=\left(\mathbf{u}_{1}(t),...,\mathbf{u}_{K}(t)\right)
be an arc of parameters of a model
f(\mathbf{x}_{i};\bm{\theta}_{t})
with
\mathbf{u}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{p_{k}},\sum_{k}p_{k}=p
. Denote a choice of subgradients
\mathbf{g}_{t}=(\mathbf{g}_{1}(t),...,\mathbf{g}_{K}(t))\in\partial\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta}_{t})
, and momentum estimates
\mathbf{m}_{t}=(\mathbf{m}_{1}(t),...,\mathbf{m}_{K}(t))
. Let
\{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{(k)}\}_{k=1}^{K}
be norms on
\mathbb{R}^{p_{k}}
with dual norms
\{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{(k),\star}\}_{k=1}^{K}
. Assume there exists
\eta(t)>0
so that for all
k\in[K]
one of the following hold:

1.

(Normalized SD) For almost any
t
,

\frac{d\mathbf{u}_{k}(t)}{dt}\in\eta(t)\arg\min_{\left\lVert\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{(k)}=1}\left\langle\mathbf{u},\mathbf{g}_{k}(t)\right\rangle~.

2.

(Normalized MSD) For almost any
t
,

\frac{d\mathbf{u}_{k}(t)}{dt}\in\eta(t)\arg\min_{\left\lVert\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{(k)}=1}\left\langle\mathbf{u},\mathbf{m}_{k}(t)\right\rangle~.

Then,
\bm{\theta}_{t}
is a trajectory of normalized SD / normalized MSD respectively, with respect to the norm
\left\lVert\bm{\theta}\right\rVert=\max_{k}\left\lVert\mathbf{u}_{k}\right\rVert
.

Proof.

By definition of the dual norm it holds that

\min_{\left\lVert\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{(k)}=1}\left\langle\mathbf{u},\mathbf{g}_{k}(t)\right\rangle=-\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{k}(t)\right\rVert_{(k),\star},\quad\min_{\left\lVert\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{(k)}=1}\left\langle\mathbf{u},\mathbf{m}_{k}(t)\right\rangle=-\left\lVert\mathbf{m}_{k}(t)\right\rVert_{(k),\star}~.

Therefore in the case of SD, for almost any
t
,

\left\langle\frac{1}{\eta(t)}\frac{d\bm{\theta}_{t}}{dt},\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rangle=\sum_{k}\left\langle\frac{1}{\eta(t)}\frac{d\mathbf{u}_{k}(t)}{dt},\mathbf{g}_{k}(t)\right\rangle=-\sum_{k}\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{k}(t)\right\rVert_{(k),\star}\overset{\text{Lemma~\ref{lemma:dual_norm_of_max}}}{=}-{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}~.

Similarly in the case of MSD,

\left\langle\frac{1}{\eta(t)}\frac{d\bm{\theta}_{t}}{dt},\mathbf{m}_{t}\right\rangle=\sum_{k}\left\langle\frac{1}{\eta(t)}\frac{d\mathbf{u}_{k}(t)}{dt},\mathbf{m}_{k}(t)\right\rangle=-\sum_{k}\left\lVert\mathbf{m}_{k}(t)\right\rVert_{(k),\star}\overset{\text{Lemma~\ref{lemma:dual_norm_of_max}}}{=}-{\left\lVert\mathbf{m}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}~.

∎

The following is a more general lemma pertaining to approximate SD algorithms.

Lemma C.24

(Composing Approximate SD Algorithms)
.

Let
\bm{\theta}_{t}=\left(\mathbf{u}_{1}(t),...,\mathbf{u}_{K}(t)\right)
be an arc of parameters of a model
f(\mathbf{x}_{i};\bm{\theta}_{t})
with
\mathbf{u}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{p_{k}},\sum_{k}p_{k}=p
. Denote a choice of subgradients
\mathbf{g}_{t}=(\mathbf{g}_{1}(t),...,\mathbf{g}_{K}(t))\in\partial\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta}_{t})
, with eventually
\mathbf{g}_{i}(t)\neq{\mathbf{0}}
for almost any
t
. Assume there exists
\nu(t)>0,R_{1},..,R_{K}>0
and norms
\{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{(k)}\}_{k=1}^{K}
(with dual norms
\{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{(k),\star}\}_{k=1}^{K}
) so that:

1.

\int_{0}^{t}\nu\overset{t\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\infty

2.

\forall k\in[K]:\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\left\lVert\mathbf{u}_{k}(t)\right\rVert_{(k)}}{\int_{0}^{t}\nu}\leq R_{k}

3.

\forall k\in[K]:\essliminf_{t\to\infty}r_{k}(t)\geq 1
where
r_{k}(t)\overset{a.e.}{=}\left\langle\frac{1}{\nu(t)}\frac{d\mathbf{u}_{k}(t)}{dt},-\frac{\mathbf{g}_{k}(t)}{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{k}(t)\right\rVert_{(k),\star}}\right\rangle

Then
\bm{\theta}_{t}
is a trajectory of Approximate Steepest Descent with respect to the norm
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert=\max_{k}\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{(k)}
,
\nu(t)
and
R_{\text{max}}\leq\max_{k}R_{k}
.

Proof.

It clearly holds by definition that
\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert}{\int_{0}^{t}\nu}\leq\max_{k}R_{k}=R_{\text{max}}
. Therefore it remains to show
\essliminf_{t\to\infty}r(t)\geq 1
. Indeed, with the same choice of subgradients
\mathbf{g}_{t}
we have

r(t)=\left\langle\frac{1}{\nu(t)}\frac{d\bm{\theta}_{t}}{dt},-\frac{\mathbf{g}_{t}}{{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}}\right\rangle=\frac{\sum_{k}\left\langle\frac{1}{\nu(t)}\frac{d\mathbf{u}_{k}(t)}{dt},-\mathbf{g}_{k}(t)\right\rangle}{\sum_{k}\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{k}(t)\right\rVert_{(k),\star}}~.

Now note the following general claim: for any set of functions
q_{1}(t),...,q_{K}(t)
and
\widetilde{q}_{1}(t),...,\widetilde{q}_{K}(t)>0
with
\forall k\in[K]:\essliminf_{t\to\infty}\frac{q_{k}}{\widetilde{q}_{k}}\geq 1
it holds that
\essliminf_{t\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{k}q_{k}}{\sum_{k}\widetilde{q}_{k}}\geq 1
. Indeed, for any
\varepsilon>0
it holds eventually for almost any
t
that
q_{k}(t)\geq\widetilde{q}_{k}(t)(1-\varepsilon)
for all
k
, so
\sum_{k}q_{k}(t)\geq\sum_{k}\widetilde{q}_{k}(t)(1-\varepsilon)=(1-\varepsilon)\sum_{k}\widetilde{q}_{k}(t)
.

Applying the claim with
q_{k}(t)=\left\langle\frac{1}{\nu(t)}\frac{d\mathbf{u}_{k}(t)}{dt},-\mathbf{g}_{k}(t)\right\rangle
and
\widetilde{q}_{k}(t)=\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{k}(t)\right\rVert_{(k),\star}
finishes.
∎

Below is our definition of Muon in the exact orthogonalization setting:

Definition C.25

(Spectral and Nuclear Norms)
.

For a real-valued matrix
W
let
W=U\Sigma V^{T}
the SVD of
W
, for
\Sigma=\text{diag}(\sigma_{1},...,\sigma_{r})
,
\sigma_{i}>0
. We denote the spectral and nuclear norms of
W
respectively as

{\left\lVert W\right\rVert}_{\mathrm{sp}}=\max_{i\in[r]}\sigma_{i},\quad{\left\lVert W\right\rVert}_{\mathrm{nuc}}=\sum_{i\in[r]}\sigma_{i}~.

For a collection of matrices
\mathbf{W}=(W_{1},...,W_{K})
we denote (msp short for max-sp, snuc short for sum-nuc)

{\left\lVert\mathbf{W}\right\rVert}_{\mathrm{msp}}=\max_{k\in[K]}{\left\lVert W_{k}\right\rVert}_{\mathrm{sp}},\quad{\left\lVert\mathbf{W}\right\rVert}_{\mathrm{snuc}}=\sum_{k\in[K]}{\left\lVert W_{k}\right\rVert}_{\mathrm{nuc}}~.

It is a standard fact that
{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}_{\mathrm{nuc}}
is the dual norm of
{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}_{\mathrm{sp}}
. By Lemma
C.22
this implies that
{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}_{\mathrm{snuc}}
is the dual norm of
{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}_{\mathrm{msp}}
.

Definition C.26

(Muon)
.

Let
\bm{\theta}_{t}=(W_{1}(t),...,W_{K}(t))\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
be a trajectory representing a collection of matrices,
\mathbf{g}_{t}=(G_{1}(t),...,G_{K}(t))\in\partial\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta}_{t})
a choice of subgradients and
\mathbf{m}_{t}=(M_{1}(t),...,M_{K}(t))
a momentum estimate of
\mathbf{g}_{t}
with parameter
c_{1}>0
. The Muon update is defined as
\frac{d\bm{\theta}_{t}}{dt}=(\frac{dW_{1}}{dt},...,\frac{dW_{K}}{dt})
for:

\forall k\in[K]:\frac{dW_{k}}{dt}\in\left\{-\eta(t)\cdot U_{k}(t)V_{k}^{T}(t)\mid M_{k}(t)\overset{\text{SVD}}{=}U_{k}(t)\Sigma_{k}(t)V_{k}^{T}(t)\right\}~,

where SVD is the reduced SVD operator, i.e.
\Sigma_{k}(t)
is a square diagonal matrix with strictly positive diagonal entries and
U_{k}(t),V_{k}(t)
have orthonormal columns.

Lemma C.27
.

Muon is a normalized momentum steepest descent algorithm with
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert={\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}_{\mathrm{msp}}
.

Proof.

By the definition of normalized momentum steepest descent, we need to prove that

{\left\lVert\frac{1}{\eta(t)}\frac{d\bm{\theta}_{t}}{dt}\right\rVert}_{\mathrm{msp}}=1,\quad\left\langle\frac{1}{\eta(t)}\frac{d\bm{\theta}_{t}}{dt},\mathbf{m}_{t}\right\rangle=-{\left\lVert\mathbf{m}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\mathrm{snuc}}~.

By definition of these norms, it suffices to show for every
k
that (omitting
t
for brevity)
{\left\lVert U_{k}V_{k}^{\top}\right\rVert}_{\mathrm{sp}}=1
and
\left\langle U_{k}V_{k}^{\top},M_{k}\right\rangle={\left\lVert M_{k}\right\rVert}_{\mathrm{nuc}}
. Indeed,
{\left\lVert U_{k}V_{k}^{\top}\right\rVert}_{\mathrm{sp}}=1
by definition (
U_{k}V_{k}^{\top}
is an SVD of itself with singular values in
\{1,0\}
). Denote
\Sigma_{k}=\text{diag}(\sigma_{1},...,\sigma_{r})
then we must show

\left\langle U_{k}V_{k}^{\top},M_{k}\right\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sigma_{i}~.

And indeed, since the elementwise dot product between two matrices
X,Y
is equal to
\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}[X^{\top}Y]
, using
U_{k}^{\top}U_{k}=I,V_{k}^{\top}V_{k}=I
we get

\left\langle U_{k}V_{k}^{\top},M_{k}\right\rangle=\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}[M_{k}^{\top}U_{k}V_{k}^{\top}]=\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}[V_{k}\Sigma_{k}^{\top}U_{k}^{\top}U_{k}V_{k}^{\top}]=\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}[V_{k}\Sigma_{k}^{\top}V_{k}^{\top}]=\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}[V_{k}^{\top}V_{k}\Sigma_{k}^{\top}]=\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}[\Sigma_{k}^{\top}]=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sigma_{i}

∎

Corollary C.28

(KKT Stationarity for Muon - Implies Corollary
3.4
)
.

Assume

\eta(t)

\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert
-max-margin problem (Equation (

Proof.

C.21

C.7
Adam

In this section we show that Adam is an approximate steepest descent algorithm with
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert=\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\infty}
in the regime of a non-increasing learning rate
\eta(t)\leq\mathrm{o}\left(t^{\frac{1}{L}-1}\right),\int_{0}^{\infty}\eta=\infty
(i.e.
(LR-Adam)
). This allows us to infer using Theorem
C.17
that the assumed directional limit point
\bar{\bm{\theta}}=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\bm{\theta}_{t}}{\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert}
of the trajectory is the direction of a KKT point of the
\ell_{\infty}
-max-margin problem. Recall again that
(T3)
is implied by
(T2)
if strengthening
(M1-Weak)
to
(M1)
.

Theorem C.30

(KKT Stationarity for Adam - Implies Theorem
3.6
)
.

Let
\bm{\theta}_{t}
be a trajectory of Adam with
c_{1}\geq c_{2}
(Equation (
8
)), under Assumptions
(M1-Weak)
,
(M2)
,
(LR-Adam)
,
(T1)
,
(T2)
,
(T3)
,
(A1)
. Then the limit point
\bar{\bm{\theta}}
of
\frac{\bm{\theta}_{t}}{\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert}
is the direction of a KKT point of Problem (
11
) with
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert=\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\infty}
.

Proof.

First, by assumptions
(T1)
,
(T2)
, it holds (Lemma
C.2
) that
\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert_{1}=\Theta(\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert^{L-1}\mathcal{L})
and in particular eventually
\mathbf{g}_{t}\neq{\mathbf{0}}
. By Lemma
B.4
, denote
C=C(c_{1},c_{2})>0
with
\forall t:\left\lVert\frac{\mathbf{m}_{t}}{\sqrt{\mathbf{v}_{t}}}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq C
. This and
(LR-Adam)
imply that
\left\lVert\frac{d\bm{\theta}_{t}}{dt}\right\rVert\leq\mathrm{o}\left(t^{\frac{1}{L}-1}\right)
. Therefore by Lemma
C.19
with
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert=\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\infty}
, fixing
\varepsilon>0
, there exist
e_{j}(t),e_{j}^{\prime}(t)\overset{t\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}0
with:

\forall j\in J_{\varepsilon}(t):\quad\quad\mathbf{m}_{t}[j]=\mathbf{g}_{t}[j](1+e_{j}(t)),\quad\sqrt{\mathbf{v}_{t}[j]}=\left|\mathbf{g}_{t}[j]\right|(1+e_{j}^{\prime}(t))~.

Recalling
\hat{\mathbf{m}}_{t}=(1-e^{-c_{1}t})^{-1}\mathbf{m}_{t},\hat{\mathbf{v}}_{t}=(1-e^{-c_{2}t})^{-1}\mathbf{v}_{t}
, since the bias correction terms themselves tend to 1 there exist
e_{j}(t)\overset{t\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}0
with (reusing the notation
e_{j}
)

\forall j\in J_{\varepsilon}(t):\frac{\hat{\mathbf{m}}_{t}[j]}{\sqrt{\hat{\mathbf{v}}_{t}}[j]}=\mathrm{sign}\left(\mathbf{g}_{t}[j]\right)\cdot(1+e_{j}(t))~.

Again since
\frac{\sqrt{1-e^{-c_{2}t}}}{1-e^{-c_{1}t}}\to 1
there exists
t_{1}\geq 0
with
\forall t\geq t_{1}:2C\geq\left\lVert\frac{\hat{\mathbf{m}}_{t}}{\sqrt{\hat{\mathbf{v}}_{t}}}\right\rVert_{\infty}
. Denote
\hat{C}:=\max\left\{2C,\sup_{t\in(0,t_{1}]}\left\lVert\frac{\hat{\mathbf{m}}_{t}}{\sqrt{\hat{\mathbf{v}}_{t}}}\right\rVert_{\infty}\right\}
an upper bound on
\left\lVert\frac{\hat{\mathbf{m}}_{t}}{\sqrt{\hat{\mathbf{v}}_{t}}}\right\rVert_{\infty}
for all
t>0
(the supremum is finite by Lemma
B.9
). For any
j\notin J_{\varepsilon}(t)
it holds that
\left|\frac{\hat{\mathbf{m}}_{t}[j]}{\sqrt{\hat{\mathbf{v}}_{t}[j]}}\frac{\mathbf{g}_{t}[j]}{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert_{1}}\right|\leq\varepsilon\hat{C}
. Also,
\sum_{j\in J_{\varepsilon}(t)}\left|\mathbf{g}_{t}[j]\right|\geq\sum_{j\in[p]}\left|\mathbf{g}_{t}[j]\right|-\varepsilon p\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert_{1}=\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert_{1}(1-\varepsilon p)
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\left\langle\frac{\hat{\mathbf{m}}_{t}}{\sqrt{\hat{\mathbf{v}}_{t}}},\frac{\mathbf{g}_{t}}{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert_{1}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{j\in J_{\varepsilon}(t)}\frac{\hat{\mathbf{m}}_{t}[j]}{\sqrt{\hat{\mathbf{v}}_{t}}[j]}\frac{\mathbf{g}_{t}[j]}{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert_{1}}-\varepsilon\hat{C}p=\sum_{j\in J_{\varepsilon}(t)}(1+e_{j}(t))\frac{\mathrm{sign}\left(\mathbf{g}_{t}[j]\right)\mathbf{g}_{t}[j]}{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert_{1}}-\varepsilon\hat{C}p

\displaystyle\geq\min_{j}(1+e_{j}(t))\cdot\frac{\sum_{j\in J_{\varepsilon}(t)}\left|\mathbf{g}_{t}[j]\right|}{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert_{1}}-\varepsilon\hat{C}p

\displaystyle\geq\min_{j}(1+e_{j}(t))\cdot\frac{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert_{1}(1-\varepsilon p)}{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert_{1}}-\varepsilon\hat{C}p

\displaystyle\overset{t\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}1-\varepsilon p-\varepsilon\hat{C}p~.

Since this holds for any
\varepsilon>0
, this implies

\liminf_{t\to\infty}\left\langle\frac{\hat{\mathbf{m}}_{t}}{\sqrt{\hat{\mathbf{v}}_{t}}},\frac{\mathbf{g}_{t}}{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert_{1}}\right\rangle\geq 1~.

Therefore,

\lim\inf_{t\to\infty}\left\langle\frac{1}{\eta(t)}\frac{d\bm{\theta}_{t}}{dt},-\frac{\mathbf{g}_{t}}{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert_{1}}\right\rangle\geq 1~.

Also, since
\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq\int_{0}^{t}\eta(s)\left\lVert\frac{\hat{\mathbf{m}}_{s}}{\sqrt{\hat{\mathbf{v}}_{s}}}\right\rVert_{\infty}ds\leq\hat{C}\int_{0}^{t}\eta(s)ds
it holds that

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert_{\infty}}{\int_{0}^{t}\eta}\leq\hat{C}~.

Therefore, by Lemma
C.15
,
\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta}_{t})\to 0,\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert\to\infty
. This in turn implies that
\forall j\in[p]:\left|\mathbf{g}_{t}[j]\right|\overset{t\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}0
(by Equation (
25
), noting that
\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert^{L-1}=\mathrm{o}\left(\mathcal{L}^{-1}\right)
), so by Lemma
B.10
it follows that in fact

\lim\sup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert_{\infty}}{\int_{0}^{t}\eta}\leq 1~.

Therefore by Theorem
C.17
with
\nu(t)=\eta(t)
we get the result.

∎

C.8
Muon-Adam

Muon-Adam differs from Muon-Signum in that it does not adhere to the definition of normalized momentum steepest descent (due to the presence of Adam). Therefore the treatment of Muon-Adam relies directly on the framework of Approximate Steepest Descent, drawing main techniques from both Theorem
C.30
and Theorem
C.21
. In addition to those, the key technical step is dividing
[0,\infty)
into times
t
when the dual norm has a significant contribution from the
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{1}
norm of the gradient w.r.t
\mathbf{u}
, and times when there is a significant contribution from the
{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}_{\mathrm{snuc}}
norm of the gradient w.r.t
\mathbf{W}
(these subsets may overlap).

Theorem C.31

(KKT Stationarity for Muon-Adam - Implies Theorem
3.7
)
.

Assume
\bm{\theta}=(W_{1},...,W_{K},\mathbf{u})=(\mathbf{W},\mathbf{u})\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
is a parameter vector representing a collection of matrices and additional parameters
\mathbf{u}
. Assume
W_{1},...,W_{K}
follow a trajectory of Muon and
\mathbf{u}
follows a trajectory of Adam, with respective learning rates of the form
\eta_{0}^{M}\eta(t),\eta_{0}^{A}\eta(t)
for
\eta_{0}^{M},\eta_{0}^{A}>0
and momentum parameters
c_{M}
for Muon and
c_{1}\geq c_{2}
for Adam. Assume
(M1-Weak)
,
(M2)
,
(LR-Adam)
,
(T1)
,
(T2)
,
(T3)
,
(A1)
.
Then
\bar{\bm{\theta}}=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\bm{\theta}_{t}}{\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert}
is along the direction of a KKT point of the
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert
-max-margin problem (Equation (
11
)), with

\left\lVert\bm{\theta}\right\rVert=\max\left\{\frac{\eta_{0}^{A}}{\eta_{0}^{M}}{\left\lVert\mathbf{W}\right\rVert}_{\mathrm{msp}},\left\lVert\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{\infty}\right\}~.

Proof.

Denote
\mathbf{g}_{t}=(\mathbf{G}_{t},\mathbf{g}_{t}^{(u)}),\mathbf{G}_{t}=(G_{1}(t),...,G_{k}(t))
the choice of subgradients along the trajectory. Denote
\mathbf{M}_{t},\mu_{t},\omega_{t}
the momentum estimates of
\mathbf{G}_{t},\mathbf{g}_{t}^{(u)},{\mathbf{g}_{t}^{(u)}}^{2}
with parameters
c_{M},c_{1},c_{2}
respectively, and
\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}_{t}
the momentum estimate for the whole gradient vector
\mathbf{g}_{t}
with
c_{M}
. We retain the notations
\mathbf{m}_{t},\mathbf{v}_{t}
for the momentum estimates of
\mathbf{g}_{t},\mathbf{g}_{t}^{2}
with
c_{1},c_{2}
. Denote
J_{M}\cup J_{A}=[p]
a partition of
[p]
into the indices optimized by Muon and by Adam respectively. Define
\alpha=\frac{\eta_{0}^{A}}{\eta_{0}^{M}}
,
{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}_{\alpha\cdot\mathrm{msp}}=\alpha{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}_{\mathrm{msp}}
, and its dual norm by
{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}_{\frac{1}{\alpha}\cdot\mathrm{snuc}}=\frac{1}{\alpha}{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}_{\mathrm{snuc}}
. We aim to show that
\bm{\theta}_{t}
is a trajectory of Approximate Steepest Descent with respect to the norm
\left\lVert\bm{\theta}\right\rVert=\max\{{\left\lVert\mathbf{W}\right\rVert}_{\alpha\cdot\mathrm{msp}},\left\lVert\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{\infty}\}
,
\nu(t)=\eta_{0}^{A}\eta(t)
and
R_{\text{max}}\leq 1
. First, by assumptions
(T1)
,
(T2)
, it holds (Lemma
C.2
) that
{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}=\Theta(\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert^{L-1}\mathcal{L})
and in particular eventually
\mathbf{g}_{t}\neq{\mathbf{0}}
.

First note that we can write the update for Muon and Adam separately as

\frac{d\mathbf{W}_{t}}{dt}\in\eta_{0}^{M}\eta(t)\cdot\arg\min_{{\left\lVert\mathbf{W}\right\rVert}_{\mathrm{msp}}=1}\left\langle\mathbf{W},\mathbf{M}_{t}\right\rangle,\quad\frac{d\mathbf{u}_{t}}{dt}=-\eta_{0}^{A}\eta(t)\frac{\hat{\mu}_{t}}{\sqrt{\hat{\omega}_{t}}}~.

But since by definition
\forall\mathbf{W},{\left\lVert\mathbf{W}\right\rVert}_{\alpha\cdot\mathrm{msp}}=\alpha{\left\lVert\mathbf{W}\right\rVert}_{\mathrm{msp}}
, we can also write

\frac{d\mathbf{W}_{t}}{dt}\in\eta_{0}^{A}\eta(t)\cdot\arg\min_{{\left\lVert\mathbf{W}\right\rVert}_{\mathrm{msp}}=\frac{\eta_{0}^{M}}{\eta_{0}^{A}}}\left\langle\mathbf{W},\mathbf{M}_{t}\right\rangle=\eta_{0}^{A}\eta(t)\cdot\arg\min_{{\left\lVert\mathbf{W}\right\rVert}_{\alpha\cdot\mathrm{msp}}=1}\left\langle\mathbf{W},\mathbf{M}_{t}\right\rangle~.

So we consider the optimization of
\mathbf{W}_{t}
as normalized momentum steepest descent with respect to
{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}_{\alpha\cdot\mathrm{msp}}
and learning rate
\nu(t)=\eta_{0}^{A}\eta(t)
. In particular this implies
{\left\lVert\frac{1}{\nu(t)}\frac{d\mathbf{W}_{t}}{dt}\right\rVert}_{\alpha\cdot\mathrm{msp}}=1
.

We observe the quantity of interest for Definition
C.12
, with the choice of subgradients fixed to be the subgradients chosen by the momentum:

\displaystyle r(t)=\left\langle\frac{1}{\nu(t)}\frac{d\bm{\theta}_{t}}{dt},-\frac{\mathbf{g}_{t}}{{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\frac{1}{\nu(t)}\frac{d\mathbf{u}_{t}}{dt},\frac{-\mathbf{g}_{t}^{(u)}}{{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}}\right\rangle+\left\langle\frac{1}{\nu(t)}\frac{d\mathbf{W}_{t}}{dt},-\frac{\mathbf{G}_{t}}{{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}}\right\rangle

(46)

\displaystyle=\frac{\left\langle\frac{\hat{\mu}_{t}}{\sqrt{\hat{\omega}_{t}}},\mathbf{g}_{t}^{(u)}\right\rangle+\left\langle\frac{1}{\nu(t)}\frac{d\mathbf{W}_{t}}{dt},-\mathbf{G}_{t}\right\rangle}{{\left\lVert\mathbf{G}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\frac{1}{\alpha}\cdot\mathrm{snuc}}+\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}^{(u)}\right\rVert_{1}}~.

Our main goal is showing that
\essliminf_{t\to\infty}r(t)\geq 1
.

Fix
\frac{1}{2p}>\varepsilon>0
and denote
J_{\varepsilon^{2}}(t)=\left\{j\in[p]\mid\frac{\left|\mathbf{g}_{t}[j]\right|}{{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}}>\varepsilon^{2}\right\}
. Also denote

T_{\varepsilon,M}=\left\{t\geq 0\mid\frac{{\left\lVert\mathbf{G}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\frac{1}{\alpha}\cdot\mathrm{snuc}}}{{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}}\geq\varepsilon p\right\},\quad T_{\varepsilon,A}=\left\{t\geq 0\mid\frac{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}^{(u)}\right\rVert_{1}}{{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}}\geq\varepsilon p\right\}~.

Since
{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}={\left\lVert\mathbf{G}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\frac{1}{\alpha}\cdot\mathrm{snuc}}+\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}^{(u)}\right\rVert_{1}
(Lemma
C.22
) and
\varepsilon p<\frac{1}{2}
it holds that
T_{\varepsilon,M}\cup T_{\varepsilon,A}=[0,\infty)
. By Lemma
C.19
(applied separately with
c_{M}
and with
c_{1},c_{2}
) it holds that there exist vanishing error terms
e_{j}(t)\to 0
with

\forall j\in J_{\varepsilon^{2}}(t):\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}_{t}[j]=\mathbf{g}_{t}[j](1+e_{j}(t)),\quad\frac{\hat{\mathbf{m}}_{t}[j]}{\sqrt{\hat{\mathbf{v}}_{t}[j]}}=\mathrm{sign}\left(\mathbf{g}_{t}[j]\right)(1+e_{j}(t))~.

We first observe the Adam expression. Notice that by definition
\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}^{(u)}\right\rVert_{1}=\sum_{j\in J_{A}}\left|\mathbf{g}_{t}[j]\right|
, so it holds for
t\in T_{\varepsilon,A}
that

\sum_{j\in J_{\varepsilon^{2}}(t)\cap J_{A}}\left|\mathbf{g}_{t}[j]\right|=\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}^{(u)}\right\rVert_{1}-\sum_{j\in J_{A}\setminus J_{\varepsilon^{2}}(t)}\left|\mathbf{g}_{t}[j]\right|\geq\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}^{(u)}\right\rVert_{1}-\varepsilon^{2}p{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}\geq\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}^{(u)}\right\rVert_{1}-\varepsilon\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}^{(u)}\right\rVert_{1}=(1-\varepsilon)\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}^{(u)}\right\rVert_{1}~.

The first inequality from the definition of
J_{\varepsilon^{2}}(t)
and the second from the definition of
T_{\varepsilon,A}
.
Denoting
\hat{C}=\sup_{t\in[0,\infty)}\left\lVert\frac{\hat{\mu}_{t}}{\sqrt{\hat{\omega}_{t}}}\right\rVert_{\infty}
as in the proof of Theorem
C.30
,

\displaystyle\forall t\in T_{\varepsilon,A}:\left\langle\frac{1}{\nu(t)}\frac{d\mathbf{u}_{t}}{dt},\frac{-\mathbf{g}_{t}^{(u)}}{{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\frac{\hat{\mu}_{t}}{\sqrt{\hat{\omega}_{t}}},\frac{\mathbf{g}_{t}^{(u)}}{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}^{(u)}\right\rVert}_{1}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}^{(u)}\right\rVert_{1}}\sum_{j\in J_{A}}\frac{\hat{\mathbf{m}}_{t}[j]}{\sqrt{\hat{\mathbf{v}}_{t}[j]}}\mathbf{g}_{t}[j]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}^{(u)}\right\rVert_{1}}\sum_{j\in J_{A}\cap J_{\varepsilon^{2}}(t)}\frac{\hat{\mathbf{m}}_{t}[j]}{\sqrt{\hat{\mathbf{v}}_{t}[j]}}\mathbf{g}_{t}[j]-\varepsilon^{2}\hat{C}p

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}^{(u)}\right\rVert}_{1}}\sum_{j\in J_{A}\cap J_{\varepsilon^{2}}(t)}(1+e_{j}(t))\left|\mathbf{g}_{t}[j]\right|-\varepsilon^{2}\hat{C}p

\displaystyle\geq\min_{j}(1+e_{j}(t))\frac{1}{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}^{(u)}\right\rVert_{1}}\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}^{(u)}\right\rVert_{1}\left(1-\varepsilon\right)-\varepsilon^{2}\hat{C}p

\displaystyle\overset{t\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}1-\mathcal{O}\left(\varepsilon\right)~.

Where
\mathcal{O}\left(\cdot\right)
hides uniform constants independent of
t
and
\varepsilon
.
Also

\forall t\notin T_{\varepsilon,A}:\left|\left\langle\frac{\hat{\mu}_{t}}{\sqrt{\hat{\omega}_{t}}},\frac{\mathbf{g}_{t}^{(u)}}{{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}}\right\rangle\right|\leq\hat{C}\frac{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}^{(u)}\right\rVert_{1}}{{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}}\leq\hat{C}\varepsilon p=\mathcal{O}\left(\varepsilon\right)~.

Considering the Muon expression, by norm equivalence and by Lemma
C.19
(and since
\mathbf{M}_{t}-\mathbf{G}_{t}
is a subvector of
\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}_{t}-\mathbf{g}_{t}
), it holds that

{\left\lVert\mathbf{M}_{t}-\mathbf{G}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\frac{1}{\alpha}\cdot\mathrm{snuc}}\leq\mathcal{O}\left(\left\lVert\mathbf{M}_{t}-\mathbf{G}_{t}\right\rVert_{\infty}\right)\leq\mathcal{O}\left(\left\lVert\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}_{t}-\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert_{\infty}\right)\leq\mathcal{O}\left({\left\lVert\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}_{t}-\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}\right)\leq\mathrm{o}\left({\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}\right)~.

For

{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon p}{\left\lVert\mathbf{G}(t)\right\rVert}_{\frac{1}{\alpha}\cdot\mathrm{snuc}}
so there exists a vanishing

\forall t\in T_{\varepsilon,M}:{\left\lVert\mathbf{M}_{t}-\mathbf{G}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\frac{1}{\alpha}\cdot\mathrm{snuc}}\leq e(t){\left\lVert\mathbf{G}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\frac{1}{\alpha}\cdot\mathrm{snuc}}~.

Note that in particular
{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon p}{\left\lVert\mathbf{G}(t)\right\rVert}_{\frac{1}{\alpha}\cdot\mathrm{snuc}}
implies
\mathbf{G}_{t}\neq 0
. Since
e(t)
is vanishing this implies that
\mathbf{M}_{t}\neq 0
for all large enough
t\in T_{\varepsilon,M}
. Thus by Lemma
C.20
, for all large enough
t
,

\forall t\in T_{\varepsilon,M}:{\left\lVert\frac{\mathbf{M}_{t}}{{\left\lVert\mathbf{M}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\frac{1}{\alpha}\cdot\mathrm{snuc}}}-\frac{\mathbf{G}_{t}}{{\left\lVert\mathbf{G}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\frac{1}{\alpha}\cdot\mathrm{snuc}}}\right\rVert}_{\frac{1}{\alpha}\cdot\mathrm{snuc}}\leq 2e(t)~.

For all
t
large enough with
2e(t)\leq\varepsilon
, since
{\left\lVert\frac{1}{\nu(t)}\frac{d\mathbf{W}_{t}}{dt}\right\rVert}_{\alpha\cdot\mathrm{msp}}=1
,

\forall t\in T_{\varepsilon,M}:\left\langle\frac{1}{\nu(t)}\frac{d\mathbf{W}_{t}}{dt},-\frac{\mathbf{G}_{t}}{{\left\lVert\mathbf{G}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\frac{1}{\alpha}\cdot\mathrm{snuc}}}\right\rangle=1-\left\langle\frac{1}{\nu(t)}\frac{d\mathbf{W}_{t}}{dt},\frac{\mathbf{M}_{t}}{{\left\lVert\mathbf{M}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\frac{1}{\alpha}\cdot\mathrm{snuc}}}-\frac{\mathbf{G}_{t}}{{\left\lVert\mathbf{G}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\frac{1}{\alpha}\cdot\mathrm{snuc}}}\right\rangle\geq 1-\varepsilon~,

\forall t\notin T_{\varepsilon,M}:\left|\left\langle\frac{1}{\nu(t)}\frac{d\mathbf{W}_{t}}{dt},-\frac{\mathbf{G}_{t}}{{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}}\right\rangle\right|\leq\frac{{\left\lVert\mathbf{G}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\frac{1}{\alpha}\cdot\mathrm{snuc}}}{{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}}<\varepsilon p~.

Now observe any such large enough
t
. From Equation (
46
),

\displaystyle r(t)=\left\langle\frac{1}{\nu(t)}\frac{d\bm{\theta}_{t}}{dt},-\frac{\mathbf{g}_{t}}{{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{\left\langle\frac{\hat{\mu}_{t}}{\sqrt{\hat{\omega}_{t}}},\mathbf{g}_{t}^{(u)}\right\rangle+\left\langle\frac{1}{\nu(t)}\frac{d\mathbf{W}_{t}}{dt},-\mathbf{G}_{t}\right\rangle}{{\left\lVert\mathbf{G}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\frac{1}{\alpha}\cdot\mathrm{snuc}}+\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}^{(u)}\right\rVert_{1}}~.

If
t\in T_{\varepsilon,M}\setminus T_{\varepsilon,A}
then
{\left\lVert\mathbf{G}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\frac{1}{\alpha}\cdot\mathrm{snuc}}\geq(1-\varepsilon p){\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}=(1-\varepsilon p)\left({\left\lVert\mathbf{G}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\frac{1}{\alpha}\cdot\mathrm{snuc}}+\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}^{(u)}\right\rVert_{1}\right)
, so

r(t)\geq\frac{(1-\varepsilon){\left\lVert\mathbf{G}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\frac{1}{\alpha}\cdot\mathrm{snuc}}-\mathcal{O}\left(\varepsilon\right){\left\lVert\mathbf{G}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\frac{1}{\alpha}\cdot\mathrm{snuc}}}{\frac{1}{1-\varepsilon p}{\left\lVert\mathbf{G}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\frac{1}{\alpha}\cdot\mathrm{snuc}}}=1-\mathcal{O}\left(\varepsilon\right)~.

And similarly if
t\in T_{\varepsilon,A}\setminus T_{\varepsilon,M}
. Also, if
t\in T_{\varepsilon,M}\cap T_{\varepsilon,A}
then

r(t)\geq\frac{(1-\varepsilon){\left\lVert\mathbf{G}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\frac{1}{\alpha}\cdot\mathrm{snuc}}+(1-\mathcal{O}\left(\varepsilon\right))\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}^{(u)}\right\rVert_{1}}{{\left\lVert\mathbf{G}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\frac{1}{\alpha}\cdot\mathrm{snuc}}+\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}^{(u)}\right\rVert_{1}}\geq 1-\mathcal{O}\left(\varepsilon\right)~.

Altogether we have
r(t)\geq 1-\mathcal{O}\left(\varepsilon\right)
for almost all large enough
t
. Since
\varepsilon>0
was arbitrarily small, this implies

\essliminf_{t\to\infty}r(t)\geq 1~.

Denote
R_{\text{max}}=\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert}{\int_{0}^{t}\widetilde{\eta}}
. As in the proof of Theorem
C.30
, we first show
R_{\text{max}}
is finite and then that
R_{\text{max}}\leq 1
. Since
\left\lVert\bm{\theta}_{t}\right\rVert=\max\{{\left\lVert\mathbf{W}\right\rVert}_{\alpha\cdot\mathrm{msp}},\left\lVert\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{\infty}\}
and

\limsup\frac{{\left\lVert\mathbf{W}\right\rVert}_{\alpha\cdot\mathrm{msp}}}{\int_{0}^{t}\nu}\leq 1,\quad\limsup\frac{\left\lVert\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{\infty}}{\int_{0}^{t}\nu}\leq\hat{C}~,

It holds that
R_{\text{max}}\leq\hat{C}
, so by Lemma
C.15
it holds that
\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta}_{t})\overset{t\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}0
, implying that
{\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rVert}_{\star}\to 0
, so by Lemma
B.10
, it holds in fact that
\limsup\frac{\left\lVert\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{\infty}}{\int_{0}^{t}\nu}\leq 1
, so
R_{\text{max}}\leq 1
as required. Therefore we get the result by Theorem
C.17
.
∎

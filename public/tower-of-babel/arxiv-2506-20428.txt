Thermodynamic criteria for signaling in quantum channels Preprint: APS/123-QED
Yutong Luo, Simon Milz, Felix C. Binder

Abstract
Signaling quantum channels are fundamental to quantum communication, enabling the transfer of information from input to output states. In contrast, thermalisation erases information about the initial state. This raises a crucial question: How does the thermalising tendency of a quantum channel constrain its signaling power and vice versa? In this work, we address this question by considering three thermodynamic tasks associated with a quantum channel: the generation, preservation, and transmission of athermality. We provide faithful measures for athermality generation and athermality preservation of quantum channels, and prove that their difference quantifies athermality transmission. Analysing these thermodynamic tasks, we find that the signaling ability of a quantum channel is upper-bounded by its athermality preservation and lower-bounded by its athermality transmission, thereby establishing a fundamental relationship between signaling and thermodynamic properties of channels for quantum communication. We demonstrate this interplay for the example of the quantum switch, revealing an explicit trade-off between the signaling ability and athermality of the quantum channels it can implement.



Quantum communication fundamentally relies on the ability of quantum channels to transmit information. It requires channels that are
signaling
, such that changes in the input state lead to distinguishable changes at the output. In practice, quantum communication devices are subject to energetic and thermodynamic constraints
Jarzyna 2017
;
Wilde and Qi 2018
;
Ding et al. 2019
;
Narasimhachar et al. 2019
;
Korzekwa et al. 2022
;
Biswas et al. 2022
and unavoidably exposed to environmental interactions that induce thermal noise
Breuer and Petruccione 2007
;
Schlosshauer 2007
, driving systems toward thermal equilibrium and erasing information about the initial state. This introduces a fundamental tension between two competing effects: the signaling power of a channel and its thermalising tendency. While the thermodynamic cost of
implementing
a quantum channel is well understood
Faist et al. 2015a
;
Faist and Renner 2018
;
Faist et al. 2021
, the
interplay
between signaling and thermodynamic resources of a given quantum channel is physically distinct and remains to be fully characterised. Understanding this trade-off is essential for assessing the limitations of quantum communication under realistic, thermal noise. For incoherent states and dynamics, it was recently shown that a quantum channel can transmit
n
bits of classical information if and only if it can transmit
n
units of energy
Hsieh 2025
.

In this work, we consider the fully quantum case and reveal the interrelation between a quantum channel’s signaling and thermodynamic capabilities by analysing three essential thermodynamic tasks: the generation, preservation, and transmission of athermality. Relating these thermodynamic tasks to resource-theoretic quantities

;
Brandão and Gour 2015
;
Coecke et al. 2016
;
Chitambar and Gour 2019
;
Gour 2025
, we prove that the ability of a quantum channel to signal is thermodynamically constrained from below (above) by how much athermality it can transmit (preserve). We then derive an operational interpretation of these resources via a channel simulation problem, yielding the resource cost of implementing a channel and extending the work-cost framework discussed in Refs.
Faist and Renner 2018
;
Faist et al. 2021
. An area where thermodynamic, signaling, and causality considerations concurrently come to the fore is the quantum switch
Chiribella et al. 2013
, which has attracted attention in both quantum communication and thermodynamics
Ebler et al. 2018
;
Liu et al. 2022
;
Xi et al. 2024
;
Capela et al. 2023
;
Wu et al. 2025
. Using the thermodynamic resourcefulness of coherence

;
Streltsov et al. 2017
;
Francica et al. 2020
to control the causal order of two quantum channels, it is known to enhance their signaling
Ebler et al. 2018
;
Goswami et al. 2020
;
Chiribella et al. 2021
and athermal properties
Felce and Vedral 2020
;
Liu et al. 2022
;
Zhu et al. 2023
, at a thermodynamic cost. Our bounds extend these results by specifying the explicit trade-off between signaling power and athermality that the induced channel of a quantum switch exhibits in terms of invested resources. Together, our results allow us to gauge the thermodynamic and signaling abilities of quantum channels in a unified way.

Robustness of athermality and signaling.—
To quantify the amount of resource in a general state (operation), we adopt the robustness measure of resources. It gauges how well a state (operation) can remain resourceful under mixing. Robustness-based measures are widely used in the resource theories of entanglement
Vidal and Tarrach 1999
;
Steiner 2003
;
Lami and Regula 2023
and coherence
Piani et al. 2016
;
Díaz et al. 2018
, and in general resource theories
Liu et al. 2019
;
Regula and Takagi 2021
;
Takagi and Regula 2019
. Here, we adopt the robustness of athermality
R_{\mathrm{T}}
to measure the thermodynamic resource inherent to quantum states and channels. For a general state
\rho_{A}
, we have

1+R_{\mathrm{T}}(\rho_{A}):=\min\left\{\lambda|\rho_{A}\leq\lambda\gamma_{A}\right\},

(1)

where “
X\leq Y
” means that
Y-X
is positive semidefinite.
Throughout, we consider full-rank thermal states
\gamma
, thus avoiding divergence of
R_{\mathrm{T}}
. When
\rho_{A}
is incoherent,
R_{\mathrm{T}}(\rho_{A})
relates to the necessary work for preparing
\rho_{A}
from
\gamma_{A}
(the
work of formation
)
Horodecki and Oppenheim 2013b
.
The set of athermality non-generating operations from
A
to
B
are the
Gibbs-preserving operations

\mathrm{GPO}(A\rightarrow B):=\left\{G|G(\gamma_{A})=\gamma_{B},\,G\in\mathcal{O}(A\rightarrow B)\right\}
.
For a general channel
\Lambda\in\mathcal{O}(A\rightarrow B)
,
R_{\mathrm{T}}(\Lambda)
is defined as

1+R_{\mathrm{T}}(\Lambda):=\min\left\{\lambda|\Lambda\leq\lambda G,\,G\in\mathrm{GPO}(A\rightarrow{B})\right\},

(2)

where “
\mathcal{M}\leq\mathcal{N}
” means that
\mathcal{N}-\mathcal{M}
is a completely positive map. We use
R_{\mathrm{T}}
for both static and dynamical athermality because dynamical athermality can be reduced to static athermality (Theorem
1
). The type of athermality
R_{\mathrm{T}}
will be unambiguous from its argument.

Turning to information transmission of a general quantum channel
\Lambda
, its signaling ability can be quantified by the robustness of signaling
R_{\mathrm{S}}
, where the free operations are non-signaling channels
Fang et al. 2019
;
Takagi et al. 2020
;
Milz et al. 2022
.
The set of
non-signaling operations
(NSOs) from
A
to
B
is given by trace-and-replace channels
\Phi_{\sigma}(\cdot)\equiv\mathrm{Tr}\{\cdot\}\sigma
as
\mathrm{NSO(A\rightarrow B)}:=\left\{\Phi_{\sigma}|\sigma\in\mathcal{S}(B)\right\}
.
For a general channel
\Lambda\in\mathcal{O}(A\rightarrow B)
,
R_{\mathrm{S}}(\Lambda)
is defined as

\displaystyle 1+R_{\mathrm{S}}(\Lambda):=\min\left\{\lambda|\Lambda\leq\lambda\Phi,\,\Phi\in\mathrm{NSO}(A\rightarrow B)\right\}.

(3)

In Ref.
Milz et al. 2022
,
R_{\mathrm{S}}(\Lambda)
has been interpreted as a measure of the “strength” of causal loops that can be closed by contracting
\Lambda
with a general channel
\Omega\in\mathcal{O}(B\rightarrow A)
, and it has been shown that the logarithm of
R_{\mathrm{S}}
asymptotically converges to the channel mutual information
Fang et al. 2019
.

Joint measure of athermality and signaling of quantum channels.—

R_{\mathrm{T}}
and
R_{\mathrm{S}}
measure athermality and signaling of a quantum channel, respectively, due to the two different free sets in their definitions, GPO and NSO.
Given the input system
A
and the output system
B
, the intersection between
\mathrm{GPO}(A\rightarrow B)
and
\mathrm{NSO}(A\rightarrow B)
is the completely-thermalising channel
\Gamma(\cdot)\equiv\mathrm{Tr}\{\cdot\}\gamma_{B}
.

We thus define a joint measure that collectively gauges signaling and athermality of a general channel
\Lambda\in\mathcal{O}(A\rightarrow B)
as

\displaystyle 1+R(\Lambda||\Gamma):=\min\{\lambda|\Lambda\leq\lambda\Gamma\}.

(4)

Operationally,
R(\Lambda||\Gamma)
quantifies athermality preservability of
\Lambda
as we discuss just below. It follows from its definition that

\displaystyle R(\Lambda||\Gamma)

\displaystyle\geq\max\left\{R_{\mathrm{T}}(\Lambda),\,R_{\mathrm{S}}(\Lambda)\right\}.

(5)

We note that equality holds when
\Phi\in\mathrm{NSO}(A\rightarrow B)
, such that
R_{\mathrm{S}}(\Phi)=0
and
R(\Phi||\Gamma)=R_{\mathrm{T}}(\Phi)
. However, when
G\in\mathrm{GPO}(A\rightarrow B)
,
R(G||\Gamma)\geq R_{\mathrm{S}}(G)
in general.

Generation, preservation and transmission of athermality.—
We now introduce three thermodynamic tasks associated with a general quantum channel
\Lambda
and show that they are characterised by the resource measures defined above. Considering a purification of
\gamma_{A}
as input, for simplicity, we start with the thermofield double state
Takahashi and Umezawa 1996

\tilde{\gamma}_{AA^{\prime}}\equiv\mathinner{|{\tilde{\gamma}}\rangle}\!\mathinner{\langle{\tilde{\gamma}}|}_{AA^{\prime}}
with
\mathinner{|{\tilde{\gamma}}\rangle}_{AA^{\prime}}\equiv\sum_{i}\sqrt{g_{i}}\mathinner{|{i}\rangle}_{A}\otimes\mathinner{|{i}\rangle}_{A^{\prime}}
. Here,
g_{i}
is the
i
th population of
\gamma_{A}
, corresponding to energy eigenstate
\mathinner{|{i}\rangle}_{A}
. We have
\mathrm{Tr}_{\{A,A^{\prime}\}\text{\textbackslash}X}\{\tilde{\gamma}_{AA^{\prime}}\}=\gamma_{X}
for
X\in\{A,A^{\prime}\}
. A generalisation to all purified thermal states on
AA^{\prime}
is given in the SM.

We consider the following three thermodynamic tasks and related resources (see Fig.
1
):

I.

Athermality generation (
R_{\mathrm{T}}
):
The amount of athermality a quantum channel
\Lambda\in\mathcal{O}(A\rightarrow B)
generates from the thermal state
\gamma_{A}
.

II.

Athermality preservation (
R
):
The amount of athermality a local quantum channel
\Lambda\in\mathcal{O}(A^{\prime}\rightarrow B)
can preserve when acting on
\tilde{\gamma}_{AA^{\prime}}
.

III.

Athermality transmission (
R-R_{\mathrm{T}}
):
The amount of athermality a local quantum channel
\Lambda\in\mathcal{O}(A^{\prime}\rightarrow B)
can transmit when acting on
\tilde{\gamma}_{AA^{\prime}}
.

The athermality generating ability of a quantum channel
\Lambda
is equivalent to its dynamical athermality
R_{\mathrm{T}}(\Lambda)
:

Theorem 1
.

For a general channel
\Lambda\in\mathcal{O}(A\rightarrow B)
, the following equality holds:

\displaystyle R_{\mathrm{T}}(\Lambda)=R_{\mathrm{T}}[\Lambda(\gamma_{A})].

(6)

Hence, Task I is quantified by
R_{\mathrm{T}}(\Lambda)
.
We remark that similar relations between static and dynamic resources have been found for coherence
Díaz et al. 2018
and entanglement
Lami and Regula 2023
.
Analogously, the joint dynamical resource measure
R
can also be reduced to a static resource:

Theorem 2
.

For a general channel
\Lambda\in\mathcal{O}(A^{\prime}\rightarrow B)
, the following equality holds:

\displaystyle R(\Lambda||\Gamma)=R_{\mathrm{T}}[\mathcal{I}\otimes\Lambda(\tilde{\gamma}_{AA^{\prime}})],

(7)

where
\mathcal{I}
is the identity channel.

Therefore,
R(\Lambda||\Gamma)
is equivalent to the output athermality when
\Lambda
is locally applied on one part of the thermofield double state
\tilde{\gamma}_{AA^{\prime}}
. To see that
R
quantifies athermality
preservation
(Task II) – and not athermality generation, like
R_{\mathrm{T}}
– consider the following Theorem:

Theorem 3
.

For a general channel
\Lambda\in\mathcal{O}(A\rightarrow B)
with
\gamma_{A}=\gamma_{B}\equiv\gamma
, the following inequality holds:

\displaystyle R(\Lambda||\Gamma)\leq R_{\mathrm{T}}(\tilde{\gamma}_{AA^{\prime}})=\mathrm{Tr}\{\gamma^{-1}\}-1,

(8)

where equality holds when
\Lambda
is unitary.

Combined with Theorem
2
, this yields a no-go relation:

\displaystyle R_{\mathrm{T}}[\mathcal{I}\otimes\Lambda(\tilde{\gamma}_{AA^{\prime}})]\leq R_{\mathrm{T}}(\tilde{\gamma}_{AA^{\prime}}),\,\forall\,\Lambda\in\mathcal{O}(A^{\prime}\rightarrow B).

(9)

That is, no local channel can increase the overall athermality of the thermofield double state
\tilde{\gamma}_{AA^{\prime}}
, rendering
R(\Lambda||\Gamma)
a quantifier of athermality
preservation
.

An important property of
\tilde{\gamma}_{AA^{\prime}}
is that it is locally thermal in both subsystems with all athermality residing solely in the correlations between
A
and
A^{\prime}
. The transmission of this type of athermality is encapsulated in Task III.

By Theorems
1
and
2
,
R(\Lambda||\Gamma)
quantifies the total athermality in
\tilde{\gamma}_{AA^{\prime}}
that is preserved under the action of
\Lambda
, while
R_{\mathrm{T}}(\Lambda)
quantifies the athermality that
\Lambda
generates in the local system
A^{\prime}
. The difference
R(\Lambda||\Gamma)-R_{\mathrm{T}}(\Lambda)
thus quantifies the athermality contained in
\tilde{\gamma}_{AA^{\prime}}
transmitted via the local channel
\Lambda
. We conclude that Task III is measured by
R-R_{\mathrm{T}}
.

Athermality preservability and transmitability bound signaling.—
We now show how the signaling capability of a general quantum channel is limited by its thermodynamic properties.

Theorem 4
.

Given a general channel
\Lambda\in\mathcal{O}(A\rightarrow B)
,

\displaystyle R(\Lambda||\Gamma)\geq R_{\mathrm{S}}(\Lambda)\geq 2\left(g_{\min}^{(AB)}\right)^{2}[R(\Lambda||\Gamma)-R_{\mathrm{T}}(\Lambda)]^{2},

(10)

where
g_{\min}^{(AB)}
is the smallest population in the energy eigenbasis of the thermal state
\gamma_{AB}\equiv\gamma_{A}\otimes\gamma_{B}
.

The upper and lower bounds on
R_{\mathrm{S}}(\Lambda)
can be viewed as necessary and sufficient conditions for signaling channels, respectively:

\displaystyle\text{Athermality preservation }(R>0)\,\Leftarrow\,R_{\mathrm{S}}>0,

\displaystyle\text{Athermality transmission }(R-R_{\mathrm{T}}>0)\,\Rightarrow\,R_{\mathrm{S}}>0.

For GPOs, athermality transmitability is equivalent to athermality preservability. Following Ref.
Hsieh 2020
, we set

\displaystyle P_{\mathrm{T}}(G):=R(G||\Gamma)\text{ for }G\in\mathrm{GPO}(A\rightarrow B).

(11)

With this, for GPOs, Eq. (
10
) becomes

\displaystyle P_{\mathrm{T}}(G)\geq R_{\mathrm{S}}(G)\geq 2\left(g_{\min}^{(AB)}\right)^{2}P_{\mathrm{T}}(G)^{2}.

(12)

That is, non-zero athermality preservability guarantees non-zero signaling ability, and vice versa. According to the bounds, the completely-thermalising channel
\Gamma
is the only GPO can never signal, as expected.

Rearranging the lower bound in Eq. (
10
), we obtain

\displaystyle R(\Lambda||\Gamma)\leq R_{\mathrm{T}}(\Lambda)+\left(\sqrt{2}\,g_{\min}^{(AB)}\right)^{-1}\!\sqrt{R_{\mathrm{S}}(\Lambda)}.

(13)

Eq. (
13
) together with Eq. (
5
) bounds the joint measure
R(\Lambda||\Gamma)
from both sides in terms of the two resources
R_{\mathrm{T}}(\Lambda)
and
R_{\mathrm{S}}(\Lambda)
. In addition,
R
and
R_{\mathrm{T}}
are operationally directly connected through a channel simulation task, as we now detail.

GPO dilation of a quantum channel.—

We now show that the joint measure
R
is not only a generalisation of
P_{\mathrm{T}}
to general quantum channels, but also admits an operational interpretation in terms of
P_{\mathrm{T}}
. In particular,
R(\Lambda||\Gamma)
bounds the minimal athermality preservability of the
GPO dilation
of
\Lambda
:

A general channel
\Lambda

Theorem 5
.

Given a general quantum channel
\Lambda\in\mathcal{O}(A\rightarrow B)
, we define the set of GPOs which can simulate this channel with some state
\rho_{C}
(see Fig.
2
) as
\mathcal{O}^{\,\mathrm{sim}}_{\mathrm{GPO}}(\Lambda)
. The following inequalities hold:

\displaystyle R(\Lambda||\Gamma)\leq\min_{G\in\mathcal{O}^{\,\mathrm{sim}}_{\mathrm{GPO}}(\Lambda)}P_{\mathrm{T}}(G)\leq\max\left\{1/R_{\mathrm{T}}(\Lambda),\,R(\Lambda||\Gamma)\right\}.

(14)

Especially, whenever
R_{\mathrm{T}}(\Lambda)R(\Lambda||\Gamma)\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle R(\Lambda||\Gamma)=\min_{G\in\mathcal{O}^{\,\mathrm{sim}}_{\mathrm{GPO}}(\Lambda)}P_{\mathrm{T}}(G).

(15)

We remark that when
\Lambda\equiv G\in\mathrm{GPO}(A\rightarrow B)
, although the upper bound diverges, the lower bound becomes tight. Besides, since
R(\Lambda||\Gamma)\geq R_{\mathrm{T}}(\Lambda)
,
R_{\mathrm{T}}(\Lambda)R(\Lambda||\Gamma)\geq 1,\,\forall\,R_{\mathrm{T}}(\Lambda)\geq 1
. Thus, Eq. (
15
) holds when
R_{\mathrm{T}}(\Lambda)\notin(0,1)
.

Additionally, we note that
R_{\mathrm{T}}(\Lambda)
can also be interpreted via the GPO dilation of
\Lambda
. In the SM, we show that

\displaystyle R_{\mathrm{T}}(\Lambda)=\min_{\rho_{C}\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{GPO}}^{\,\mathrm{sim}}(\Lambda)}R_{\mathrm{T}}(\rho_{C}),

(16)

where
\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{GPO}}^{\,\mathrm{sim}}(\Lambda)
is the set of states that can simulate
\Lambda
with a GPO using the protocol in Fig.
2
. As a consequence,
R_{\mathrm{T}}(\Lambda)
and
R(\Lambda||\Gamma)
are connected via the GPO dilation of
\Lambda
, but stem from distinct types of resources in the dilation. We now demonstrate the interplay between the three resources identified above and showcase the application of the derived bounds via a channel simulation scenario involving the quantum switch
Chiribella et al. 2013
.

Signaling ability activation by quantum switch.—
The quantum switch
\mathdutchcal{S}
realises the superposition of causal orders between quantum operations
Chiribella et al. 2013
. Intuitively, it transforms two quantum channels and the state of a control qubit into a channel
\Lambda{S}
, where the input quantum channels are implemented in a superposition of their possible orderings if the control is in a superposition of
\mathinner{|{0}\rangle}
and
\mathinner{|{1}\rangle}
(see Fig.
3
). It is known that, with coherent control and two GPOs, the induced channel
\Lambda{S}
is both signaling and athermal
Liu et al. 2022
. However, a comprehensive analysis of how the thermodynamic and signaling properties of
\Lambda{S}
depend on input control and channels remains lacking.
Considering the switch as a channel simulation task in Fig.
3
, the framework developed above is ideally suited to address this challenge.

Taking two identical signaling GPOs as input channels, we demonstrate that the joint resource
R
of the induced channel
\Lambda{S}
is constrained by the supplied resources, while
R_{\mathrm{T}}
and
R_{\mathrm{S}}
exhibit a trade-off relation.

\displaystyle G\equiv s\Gamma+(1-s)\mathcal{I},

Theorem 6
.

Given two input channels

\displaystyle R(\Lambda{S}||\Gamma)

\displaystyle\leq R_{\mathrm{T}}(\rho_{C})+2P_{\mathrm{T}}^{2}(G)/P_{\mathrm{T}}(\mathcal{I}),

\displaystyle R(\Lambda{S}||\Gamma)

\displaystyle\geq R_{\mathrm{S}}(\Lambda{S})\geq(g_{\min}^{4}/2)[R(\Lambda{S}||\Gamma)-R_{\mathrm{T}}(\Lambda{S})]^{2},

where

\displaystyle R_{\mathrm{T}}(\Lambda{S})=\sqrt{1-4(1-g_{\max}^{2})[2-(1-g_{\max}^{2})s^{2}]s^{2}\alpha(1-\alpha)},

(20)

)] is only relatively tighter for more signaling input GPOs (smaller
s
). Despite the potential looseness of the lower bound, it provides an interpretation of the phenomenon that increased
R_{\mathrm{S}}(\Lambda{S})
comes at the expense of
R_{\mathrm{T}}(\Lambda{S})
, aligning with their trends in both plots.

Finally, we remark that the above resource analysis is not specific to quantum switches. In the SM, we provide a similar analysis to coherent control of quantum channels
Abbott et al. 2020
, where
R_{\mathrm{T}}
and
R_{\mathrm{S}}
are found to exhibit a similar trade-off.

Discussion and Conclusion.—

In this work, we have provided a full-fledged characterisation of the signaling and thermodynamic properties of quantum channels and demonstrated how they mutually bound and condition each other. Our main results are the precise identification of the thermodynamic resources for the generation,
preservation, and transmission of athermality (Theorems
1
,
2
, and
3
), the characterisation of a quantum channel’s signaling ability in terms of its capacity for athermality preservation and transmission (Theorem
4
), and an operational interpretation of the resulting resource measures in terms of channel-dilatability by a GPO (Theorem
5
). We finally used our results for an encompassing characerisation of resources in the quantum switch (Theorem
6
).

We note the relation of our findings to those of Ref.
Hsieh 2025
, which demonstrates that, in the asymptotic limit, information and energy transmission in specific incoherent setups are equivalent. Here, in contrast, we do not restrict ourselves to the classical case – in particular, the considered states
\widetilde{\gamma}_{AA^{\prime}}
are entangled. On the other hand, the thermodynamic resource we analysed is athermality, a joint measure of work and coherence, while Ref.
Hsieh 2025
investigated extractable work. Finally, according to our measure of athermality transmitability, a signaling channel (
R_{\mathrm{S}}>0)
may have zero athermality transmitability (
R-R_{\mathrm{T}}=0)
(See SM for example).

Using the trade-off relations of athermality and signaling, we demonstrated the interplay of these properties for the paradigmatic example of the quantum switch. This resolves the open question posed in Ref.
Liu et al. 2022
, showing that only limited activation of information capacity is possible when the switch is composed with GPOs, as the output resources are bounded by those at the input.

A natural next step is to analyse how the bounds we provide behave when thermal operations or related subsets of GPOs
Janzing et al. 2000
;
Brandao et al. 2013
;
Horodecki and Oppenheim 2013b
;
Ćwikliński et al. 2015
;
Lostaglio et al. 2018
;
Faist et al. 2015b
;
Ding et al. 2021
;
Tajima and Takagi 2025
are considered as thermodynamically resourceless channels instead of GPOs. Since these channels are all contained within GPOs, our core bounds Eq. (
10
) still hold – albeit possibly in a looser sense. Another promising research direction is the study of general quantum supermaps as a complete framework for manipulating dynamical resources and their conversion of different types of resources
Theurer et al. 2019
;
Liu and Winter 2019
;
Liu and Yuan 2020
;
Gour and Scandolo 2020a
;
Gour and Scandolo 2020b
;
Gour and Scandolo 2021
;
Gour et al. 2025
. Concretely, one could ask: What kind of supermaps do not increase the total resources
R_{\mathrm{T}}+R_{\mathrm{S}}
but can exchange them? Noticing that
2R(\Lambda||\Gamma)\geq R_{\mathrm{T}}(\Lambda)+R_{\mathrm{S}}(\Lambda)
, one may think
\Gamma
-preserving supermaps are the direct answer, but we find that there exist counterexamples where
\Gamma
-preserving supermaps increase the sum
R_{\mathrm{T}}+R_{\mathrm{S}}
.

Having combined signaling and athermality, another possible direction is to study other resource theories where two distinct free sets have an intersection. In this case, the results from Ref.
Hoffmann 1992
may be applied to resource measures defined via distance metrics
Li et al. 2020
.

1
Basic properties

The dual SDP of
R_{\mathrm{T}}
for states is given as
Watrous 2020

\displaystyle 1+R_{\mathrm{T}}(\rho_{A})=\max\left\{\mathrm{Tr}\{S\rho_{A}\}\Big|S\geq 0,\,\mathrm{Tr}\{S\gamma_{A}\}=1\right\},

(A.8)

for which strong duality holds, i.e., primal and dual SDPs yield the same optimal values
Skrzypczyk and Cavalcanti 2023
.
As a resource monotone,
R_{\mathrm{T}}
satisfies desired properties. Here, we show them explicitly, by following similar proofs as Ref.
Díaz et al. 2018
, where they are for the robustness of coherence.

1.

(Faithfulness)
R_{\mathrm{T}}(\rho)=0\Leftrightarrow\rho=\gamma
.

Proof.
Direct result from Eq. (
A.3
). ∎

2.

(Monotonicity)
R_{\mathrm{T}}(\rho_{A})\geq R_{\mathrm{T}}[G(\rho_{A})],\,\forall\,G\in\mathrm{GPO}(A\rightarrow B)
.

Proof.
Let
\lambda^{*}\equiv 1+R_{\mathrm{T}}(\rho_{A})
be the optimal value that achieves the minimum. Since
G\in\mathrm{GPO}(A\rightarrow B)
is completely positive,
\lambda^{*}\gamma_{A}-\rho_{A}\geq 0\Rightarrow G(\lambda^{*}\gamma_{A}-\rho_{A})=\lambda^{*}\gamma_{B}-G(\rho_{A})\geq 0
. Consequently,
\lambda^{*}
is in the feasible set of the minimisation defining
R_{\mathrm{T}}[G(\rho_{A})]
, such that
\lambda^{*}\geq 1+R_{\mathrm{T}}[G(\rho_{A})]
. Hence,
R_{\mathrm{T}}(\rho_{A})\geq R_{\mathrm{T}}[G(\rho_{A})]
. ∎

3.

(Convexity)
R_{\mathrm{T}}(\sum_{i}p_{i}\rho_{i})\leq\sum_{i}p_{i}R_{\mathrm{T}}(\rho_{i})
, for
\sum_{i}p_{i}=1
and
p_{i}\in[0,1],\forall\,i
.

Proof.
Let
\lambda^{*}_{i}\equiv 1+R_{\mathrm{T}}(\rho_{i})
be the optimal value for each
i
.
\lambda^{*}_{i}\gamma-\rho_{i}\geq 0,\forall\,i\Rightarrow\sum_{i}p_{i}\lambda^{*}_{i}\gamma-\sum_{i}p_{i}\rho_{i}\geq 0
. Consequently,
\sum_{i}p_{i}\lambda^{*}_{i}
is a feasible for the minimisation defining
R_{\mathrm{T}}(\sum_{i}p_{i}\rho_{i})
, such that
\sum_{i}p_{i}\lambda^{*}_{i}\geq 1+R_{\mathrm{T}}(\sum_{i}p_{i}\rho_{i})
. Hence,
R_{\mathrm{T}}(\sum_{i}p_{i}\rho_{i})\leq\sum_{i}p_{i}R_{\mathrm{T}}(\rho_{i})
. ∎

4.

(Multiplicity)
1+R_{\mathrm{T}}(\rho_{A}\otimes\rho_{B})=[1+R_{\mathrm{T}}(\rho_{A})][1+R_{\mathrm{T}}(\rho_{B})]
.

Proof.
Let
\lambda_{X}^{*}
be the optimal value such that
1+R_{\mathrm{T}}(\rho_{X})\equiv\lambda_{X}
, where
X=A,B
.
\lambda^{*}_{X}\gamma_{X}-\rho_{X}\geq 0,\forall\,X\Rightarrow\lambda_{A}^{*}\lambda_{B}^{*}\gamma_{A}\otimes\gamma_{B}\geq\rho_{A}\otimes\rho_{B}
.
\lambda_{A}^{*}\lambda_{B}^{*}
is feasible for the minimisation defining
R_{\mathrm{T}}(\rho_{A}\otimes\rho_{B})
, such that
\lambda_{A}^{*}\lambda_{B}^{*}=[1+R_{\mathrm{T}}(\rho_{A})][1+R_{\mathrm{T}}(\rho_{B})]\geq 1+R_{\mathrm{T}}(\rho_{A}\otimes\rho_{B})
. Let
S_{X}^{*}
be the optimal operator of the dual SDP [Eq. (
A.8
)] for
X=A,B
, such that
1+R_{\mathrm{T}}(\rho_{X})=\mathrm{Tr}\{S^{*}_{X}\rho_{X}\}
. Now
S\equiv S^{*}_{A}\otimes S^{*}_{B}
is feasible for the dual SDP of
R_{\mathrm{T}}(\rho_{A}\otimes\rho_{B})
, so that
\mathrm{Tr}\{S\rho_{A}\otimes\rho_{B}\}=\mathrm{Tr}\{S^{*}_{A}\rho_{A}\}\mathrm{Tr}\{S^{*}_{B}\rho_{B}\}=[1+R_{\mathrm{T}}(\rho_{A})][1+R_{\mathrm{T}}(\rho_{B})]\leq 1+R_{\mathrm{T}}(\rho_{A}\otimes\rho_{B})
. Hence,
1+R_{\mathrm{T}}(\rho_{A}\otimes\rho_{B})=[1+R_{\mathrm{T}}(\rho_{A})][1+R_{\mathrm{T}}(\rho_{B})]
. ∎

By Theorem
A.3
(See below),
R_{\mathrm{T}}(\Lambda)=R_{\mathrm{T}}[\Lambda(\gamma_{A})],\,\forall\,\Lambda\in\mathcal{O}(A\rightarrow B)
. Thus,
R_{\mathrm{T}}

1.

(Faithfulness)
R_{\mathrm{T}}(\Lambda)=0\Leftrightarrow\Lambda\text{ is a GPO}
.

2.

(Monotonicity)
R_{\mathrm{T}}(\Lambda)\geq R_{\mathrm{T}}(G\circ\Lambda\circ G^{\prime}),\,\forall\,G\text{ and }G^{\prime}\text{ are GPOs}
.

3.

(Convexity)
R_{\mathrm{T}}(\sum_{i}p_{i}\Lambda_{i})\leq\sum_{i}p_{i}R_{\mathrm{T}}(\Lambda_{i})
, for
\sum_{i}p_{i}=1
and
p_{i}\in[0,1],\forall\,i
.

4.

(Multiplicity)
1+R_{\mathrm{T}}(\Lambda\otimes\Omega)=[1+R_{\mathrm{T}}(\Lambda)][1+R_{\mathrm{T}}(\Omega)]
.

Lemma A.1
.

The optimal
S^{*}
that maximises the dual SDP of the

of states [Eq. (

)] can always be chosen to be rank-1.

Proof.

Since the Gibbs state

is full-rank by assumption, we can define a bijective map,
X\equiv\gamma^{1/2}S\gamma^{1/2}
, such that Eq. (
A.8
) can be rewritten as

1+R_{\mathrm{T}}(\rho)=\max_{X}\left\{\text{Tr}\{\tilde{\rho}X\}\Big|X\geq 0,\,\text{Tr}\{X\}=1\right\},

(A.10)

where we denote
\tilde{\rho}\equiv\gamma^{-1/2}\rho\gamma^{-1/2}
.
The feasible set
\{X|X\geq 0,\,\text{Tr}\{X\}=1\}
is the set of quantum states, meaning that any
X
in the set can be written as a convex combination of rank-1 states. Let
X^{*}=\sum_{i}p_{i}\mathinner{|{\phi_{i}}\rangle}\!\mathinner{\langle{\phi_{i}}|}
for
\sum_{i}p_{i}=1
with
p_{i}\in[0,1],\forall\,i
, be the optimal operator, such that

\displaystyle 1+R_{\mathrm{T}}(\rho)=\text{Tr}\{\tilde{\rho}X^{*}\}=\sum_{i}p_{i}\text{Tr}\{\tilde{\rho}\mathinner{|{\phi_{i}}\rangle}\!\mathinner{\langle{\phi_{i}}|}\}\leq\max_{i}\text{Tr}\{\tilde{\rho}\mathinner{|{\phi_{i}}\rangle}\!\mathinner{\langle{\phi_{i}}|}\}.

(A.11)

However, by Eq. (
A.10
),
1+R_{\mathrm{T}}(\rho)\geq\max_{i}\text{Tr}\{\tilde{\rho}\mathinner{|{\phi_{i}}\rangle}\!\mathinner{\langle{\phi_{i}}|}\}
. We thus have

1+R_{\mathrm{T}}(\rho)=\max_{i}\text{Tr}\{\tilde{\rho}\mathinner{|{\phi_{i}}\rangle}\!\mathinner{\langle{\phi_{i}}|}\}
and
X^{*}
can be chosen to be rank-1. Note that
X^{*}
does not have to be rank-1 since for some
\tilde{\rho}
,
\text{Tr}\{\tilde{\rho}\mathinner{|{\phi_{i}}\rangle}\!\mathinner{\langle{\phi_{i}}|}\}
can be independent of

The optimal

in Eq. (

Since

can always be chosen to be rank-1, such that

S^{*}\equiv\gamma^{-1/2}X^{*}\gamma^{-1/2}=\gamma^{-1/2}\mathinner{|{\phi}\rangle}\!\mathinner{\langle{\phi}|}\gamma^{-1/2}
is also rank-1.

We now find the state with highest
R_{\mathrm{T}}
, i.e., the most athermal state.

Theorem A.1

(The most athermal state)
.

Given a non-degenerate, full-rank Gibbs state
\gamma\equiv\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}g_{i}\mathinner{|{i}\rangle}\!\mathinner{\langle{i}|}
with the populations
g_{d-1}<g_{d-2}<\dots<g_{0}
, the unique state with the maximal
R_{\mathrm{T}}
is the most energetic state:

\rho^{*}\equiv\mathinner{|{d-1}\rangle}\!\mathinner{\langle{d-1}|}
, with
1+R_{\mathrm{T}}(\rho^{*})=g_{d-1}^{-1}.

Proof.

We define
\lambda(\rho)\equiv 1+R_{\mathrm{T}}(\rho)
. From the dual form of
1+R_{\mathrm{T}}(\rho)
[Eq. (
A.8
)] and Lemma
A.1
, we have

\lambda(\rho)=\max_{\mathinner{|{\phi}\rangle}}\left\{\text{Tr}\{\rho S\}\Big|S=\frac{\mathinner{|{\phi}\rangle}\!\mathinner{\langle{\phi}|}}{\text{Tr}\{\gamma\mathinner{|{\phi}\rangle}\!\mathinner{\langle{\phi}|}\}}\right\}=\max_{\mathinner{|{\phi}\rangle}}\frac{\mathinner{\langle{\phi}|}\rho\mathinner{|{\phi}\rangle}}{\mathinner{\langle{\phi}|}\gamma\mathinner{|{\phi}\rangle}}.

(A.12)

We denote the value of
\lambda
corresponding to the maximal
1+R_{\mathrm{T}}
as
\lambda_{\max}
, such that

\lambda_{\max}\equiv\max_{\rho}\lambda(\rho)=\max_{\rho}\max_{\mathinner{|{\phi}\rangle}}\frac{\mathinner{\langle{\phi}|}\rho\mathinner{|{\phi}\rangle}}{\mathinner{\langle{\phi}|}\gamma\mathinner{|{\phi}\rangle}}\leq\frac{\max_{\rho}\max_{\mathinner{|{\phi}\rangle}}\mathinner{\langle{\phi}|}\rho\mathinner{|{\phi}\rangle}}{\min_{\mathinner{|{\phi}\rangle}}\mathinner{\langle{\phi}|}\gamma\mathinner{|{\phi}\rangle}}.

(A.13)

It is clear that
\max_{\rho}\max_{\mathinner{|{\phi}\rangle}}\mathinner{\langle{\phi}|}\rho\mathinner{|{\phi}\rangle}=1
. For the denominator, since
\mathinner{\langle{\phi}|}\gamma-g_{d-1}\mathbbm{1}\mathinner{|{\phi}\rangle}\geq 0,\forall\mathinner{|{\phi}\rangle}
and the equality holds as
\mathinner{|{\phi}\rangle}=\mathinner{|{d-1}\rangle}
, we have
\min_{\mathinner{|{\phi}\rangle}}\mathinner{\langle{\phi}|}\gamma\mathinner{|{\phi}\rangle}=g_{d-1}
. Therefore,

\lambda_{\max}=\max_{\rho}\max_{\mathinner{|{\phi}\rangle}}\frac{\mathinner{\langle{\phi}|}\rho\mathinner{|{\phi}\rangle}}{\mathinner{\langle{\phi}|}\gamma\mathinner{|{\phi}\rangle}}\leq\frac{1}{g_{d-1}}.

(A.14)

Equality holds for
\rho=\rho^{*}\equiv\mathinner{|{d-1}\rangle}\!\mathinner{\langle{d-1}|}
and
\mathinner{|{\phi}\rangle}=\mathinner{|{d-1}\rangle}
, i.e.,
\rho^{*}
is the most athermal state with
1+R_{\mathrm{T}}(\rho^{*})=\lambda_{\max}=g_{d-1}^{-1}
.

Now we prove
\rho=\mathinner{|{d-1}\rangle}\!\mathinner{\langle{d-1}|}
is the only most athermal state.
Due to the convexity of
R_{\mathrm{T}}
, the most athermal state can only be mixed if it is a convex combination of pure states with the same
R_{\mathrm{T}}
. Therefore, to prove that
\rho
is unique, it is enough to assume that there is another pure state
\sigma\equiv\mathinner{|{\psi}\rangle}\!\mathinner{\langle{\psi}|}
with
\lambda(\sigma)=g_{d-1}^{-1}
and to show that
\sigma
does not exist. Since
\sigma\neq\rho
,
\mathinner{|{\psi}\rangle}\neq\mathinner{|{d-1}\rangle}
.
Let
\mathinner{|{\phi^{\prime}}\rangle}
be the optimal state that gives

\frac{\mathinner{\langle{\phi^{\prime}}|}\sigma\mathinner{|{\phi^{\prime}}\rangle}}{\mathinner{\langle{\phi^{\prime}}|}\gamma\mathinner{|{\phi^{\prime}}\rangle}}=\frac{|\langle\phi^{\prime}|\psi\rangle|^{2}}{\mathinner{\langle{\phi^{\prime}}|}\gamma\mathinner{|{\phi^{\prime}}\rangle}}=\frac{1}{g_{d-1}}.

(A.15)

We note that
\mathinner{|{\phi^{\prime}}\rangle}\neq\mathinner{|{d-1}\rangle}
, since
\dfrac{|\langle d-1|\psi\rangle|^{2}}{\mathinner{\langle{d-1}|}\gamma\mathinner{|{d-1}\rangle}}=\dfrac{|\langle d-1|\psi\rangle|^{2}}{g_{d-1}}<\dfrac{1}{g_{d-1}}
. However, since
\mathinner{|{\phi^{\prime}}\rangle}\neq\mathinner{|{d-1}\rangle}
, we have
\mathinner{\langle{\phi^{\prime}}|}\gamma\mathinner{|{\phi^{\prime}}\rangle}>g_{d-1}
(
\mathinner{|{d-1}\rangle}
is the only state satisfying
\mathinner{\langle{d-1}|}\gamma\mathinner{|{d-1}\rangle}=g_{d-1}

because
\gamma
is non-degenerate). Eq. (
A.15
) therefore requires
|\langle\phi^{\prime}|\psi\rangle|>1
. Because such a state
\sigma
cannot exist,
\rho=\mathinner{|{d-1}\rangle}\!\mathinner{\langle{d-1}|}
is the only state with the maximal
R_{\mathrm{T}}
, for which
1+R_{\mathrm{T}}(\rho)=g_{d-1}^{-1}
.
∎

We further show that
R_{\mathrm{T}}
can be related with commonly used distances, which is given by a continuity property. In the context of resource theories, the continuity of resource monotone refers to the property of the monotone guaranteeing that any two states that are close in some chosen distance are close in terms of their resources according to this monotone.
The continuity of robustness measures has been studied in Ref.
Schluck et al. 2023
and the continuity of
R_{\mathrm{T}}
has been explicitly proven in Lemma F.3 in Ref.
Hsieh 2020
. Here, we provide a self-contained proof for the continuity of
R_{\mathrm{T}}
, which improves the bound of Ref.
Hsieh 2020
by a factor
1/2
.

Theorem A.2

(Continuity of
R_{\mathrm{T}}
)
.

For any two states
\rho
,
\sigma
and a given Gibbs state
\gamma
, the following inequality holds:

\big|R_{\mathrm{T}}(\rho)-R_{\mathrm{T}}(\sigma)\big|\leq\frac{1}{2g_{\min}}||\rho-\sigma||_{1},

(A.16)

where
||A||_{1}:=\mathrm{Tr}\{\sqrt{A^{\dagger}A}\}
is the trace norm and
g_{\min}
is the minimal population of energy eigenbasis of
\gamma
.

Proof.

Consider the dual SDP of
R_{\mathrm{T}}

4

In this section, we find the most resourceful channels according to the measure

Lemma A.2
.

Given two operators
X
and
Y
where
Y
is positive, the following statements are equivalent:

1.

\min\{f|X\leq fY\}=1
.

2.

\exists\mathinner{|{\psi}\rangle},\,\mathinner{\langle{\psi}|}Y-X\mathinner{|{\psi}\rangle}=0
and
Y-X\geq 0
.

Proof.

For the direction (
1\Rightarrow 2
), it is straightforward that
\min\{f|X\leq fY\}=1\Rightarrow Y-X\geq 0
. To show the existence of such a state
\mathinner{|{\psi}\rangle}
, we assume that
\forall\mathinner{|{\psi}\rangle},\mathinner{\langle{\psi}|}Y-X\mathinner{|{\psi}\rangle}>0
. Define
\mathinner{|{\psi_{\min}}\rangle}\equiv\arg\min_{\mathinner{|{\psi}\rangle}}\mathinner{\langle{\psi}|}Y-X\mathinner{|{\psi}\rangle}
and
\mathinner{|{\psi_{\max}}\rangle}\equiv\arg\max_{\mathinner{|{\psi}\rangle}}\mathinner{\langle{\psi}|}Y\mathinner{|{\psi}\rangle}
. Then,
\forall\mathinner{|{\psi}\rangle}
,

\displaystyle\mathinner{\langle{\psi}|}\left[\left(1-\frac{\mathinner{\langle{\psi_{\min}}|}Y-X\mathinner{|{\psi_{\min}}\rangle}}{\mathinner{\langle{\psi_{\max}}|}Y\mathinner{|{\psi_{\max}}\rangle}}\right)Y-X\right]\mathinner{|{\psi}\rangle}

\displaystyle=\mathinner{\langle{\psi}|}Y-X\mathinner{|{\psi}\rangle}-\frac{\mathinner{\langle{\psi}|}Y\mathinner{|{\psi}\rangle}}{\mathinner{\langle{\psi_{\max}}|}Y\mathinner{|{\psi_{\max}}\rangle}}\mathinner{\langle{\psi_{\min}}|}Y-X\mathinner{|{\psi_{\min}}\rangle}

(A.31)

\displaystyle\geq\mathinner{\langle{\psi}|}Y-X\mathinner{|{\psi}\rangle}-\mathinner{\langle{\psi_{\min}}|}Y-X\mathinner{|{\psi_{\min}}\rangle}\geq 0.

(A.32)

Therefore,

\displaystyle\min\{f|X\leq fY\}\leq 1-\frac{\mathinner{\langle{\psi_{\min}}|}Y-X\mathinner{|{\psi_{\min}}\rangle}}{\mathinner{\langle{\psi_{\max}}|}Y\mathinner{|{\psi_{\max}}\rangle}}<1,

(A.33)

which contradicts
\min\{f|X\leq fY\}=1
. The direction (

) is proven.

For the direction (

), since

, we have
\min\{f|X\leq fY\}\leq 1
. Assume that

. For the state
\mathinner{|{\psi}\rangle}
satisfying
\mathinner{\langle{\psi}|}Y-X\mathinner{|{\psi}\rangle}=0
, we have

\displaystyle\mathinner{\langle{\psi}|}f^{*}Y-X\mathinner{|{\psi}\rangle}

\displaystyle=\mathinner{\langle{\psi}|}[(f^{*}-1)Y+Y-X]\mathinner{|{\psi}\rangle}=(f^{*}-1)\mathinner{\langle{\psi}|}Y\mathinner{|{\psi}\rangle}<0,

(A.34)

because

. This contradicts the assumption that
\mathinner{\langle{\psi}|}f^{*}Y-X\mathinner{|{\psi}\rangle}\geq 0,\,\forall\mathinner{|{\psi}\rangle}
. Therefore,

must be 1. The direction (

Theorem B.1

(Lower bound on channel mutual information by
R
and

)
.

Given a general channel
\Lambda\in\mathcal{O}(A^{\prime}\rightarrow B)
, the following bounds hold

\displaystyle I(\Lambda)\geq I(A:B)_{\mathcal{I}\otimes\Lambda(\tilde{\gamma}_{AA^{\prime}})}\geq 2\left(g_{\min}^{(AB)}\right)^{2}\left[R(\Lambda||\Gamma)-R_{\mathrm{T}}(\Lambda)\right]^{2}.

(B.2)

Here,
\tilde{\gamma}_{AA^{\prime}}\equiv\mathinner{|{\tilde{\gamma}}\rangle}\!\mathinner{\langle{\tilde{\gamma}}|}_{AA^{\prime}}
is the thermofield double state with

\mathinner{|{\tilde{\gamma}}\rangle}_{AA^{\prime}}\equiv\sum_{i}\sqrt{g_{i}}\mathinner{|{i}\rangle}_{A}\otimes\mathinner{|{i}\rangle}_{A^{\prime}},

where
g_{i}
is the population of
\gamma_{A}
of the

\mathinner{|{i}\rangle}_{A}
, and

is the smallest population for the thermal state
\gamma_{AB}\equiv\gamma_{A}\otimes\gamma_{B}
.

Proof.

The first inequality directly follows from the definition of the channel mutual information [Eq. (
B.1
)]. To prove the second inequality, we denote
\rho_{AB}\equiv\mathcal{I}\otimes\Lambda(\tilde{\gamma}_{AA^{\prime}})

\displaystyle I(A:B)_{\rho_{AB}}

\displaystyle=D(\rho_{AB}||\rho_{A}\otimes\rho_{B})

(B.3)

\displaystyle=D(\rho_{AB}||\gamma_{A}\otimes\rho_{B})

(B.4)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}||\rho_{AB}-\gamma_{A}\otimes\rho_{B}||_{1}^{2}

(B.5)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}||\mathcal{I}\otimes\Lambda(\tilde{\gamma}_{AA^{\prime}})-\gamma_{A}\otimes\rho_{B}||_{1}^{2}

(B.6)

\displaystyle\geq 2\left(g_{\min}^{(AB)}\right)^{2}\Big|R_{\mathrm{T}}[\mathcal{I}\otimes\Lambda(\tilde{\gamma}_{AA^{\prime}})]-R_{\mathrm{T}}[\gamma_{A}\otimes\Lambda(\gamma_{A^{\prime}})]\Big|^{2},

(B.7)

(Theorem
A.2

A.3

A.4

Theorem B.2

\displaystyle R_{\mathrm{S}}(\Lambda)\geq I(A:B)_{\mathcal{I}\otimes\Lambda(\tilde{\gamma}_{AA^{\prime}})}.

(B.9)

Proof.

We consider the max-relative entropy

\displaystyle D_{\max}(\rho||\sigma):=\ln\min\{\lambda|\rho\leq\lambda\sigma\}.

(B.10)

Define

\displaystyle I(A:B)_{\mathcal{I}\otimes\Lambda(\tilde{\gamma}_{AA^{\prime}})}

\displaystyle=D(\rho_{AB}||\rho_{A}\otimes\rho_{B})

(B.11)

\displaystyle=\min_{\sigma_{B}}D(\rho_{AB}||\rho_{A}\otimes\sigma_{B})

(B.12)

\displaystyle=\min_{\sigma_{B}}D(\rho_{AB}||\gamma_{A}\otimes\sigma_{B})

(B.13)

\displaystyle\leq\min_{\sigma_{B}}D_{\max}(\rho_{AB}||\gamma_{A}\otimes\sigma_{B})

(B.14)

\displaystyle=\min_{\sigma_{B}}D_{\max}(J_{\Lambda}||\mathbbm{1}_{A}\otimes\sigma_{B})

(B.15)

\displaystyle=\ln[1+R_{\mathrm{S}}(\Lambda)],

(B.16)

where the second line follows from

\displaystyle D(\rho_{AB}||\rho_{A}\otimes\rho_{B})

\displaystyle=\min_{\sigma_{B}}\left[D(\rho_{AB}||\rho_{A}\otimes\rho_{B})+D(\rho_{B}||\sigma_{B})\right]=\min_{\sigma_{B}}D(\rho_{AB}||\rho_{A}\otimes\sigma_{B}),

(B.17)

the inequality is due to
D_{\max}(\rho||\sigma)\geq D(\rho||\sigma),\,\forall\rho,\sigma

Datta 2009
, the fifth line is from the fact that

\displaystyle J_{\Lambda}\leq\lambda\mathbbm{1}_{A}\otimes\sigma_{B}\Leftrightarrow(\gamma_{A}^{1/2}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{B})J_{\Lambda}(\gamma_{A}^{1/2}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{B})\leq\lambda(\gamma_{A}^{1/2}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{B})\mathbbm{1}_{A}\otimes\sigma_{B}(\gamma_{A}^{1/2}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{B})\Leftrightarrow\rho_{AB}\leq\lambda\gamma_{A}\otimes\sigma_{B},

(B.18)

and the last line follows from the definition of

[Eq. (
A.5
)] with noticing that
J_{\Phi_{\sigma}}\equiv\mathbbm{1}\otimes\sigma
. Because

R_{\mathrm{S}}(\Lambda)\geq\ln[1+R_{\mathrm{S}}(\Lambda)]\geq I(A:B)_{\mathcal{I}\otimes\Lambda(\tilde{\gamma}_{AA^{\prime}})}

Here we exemplify that a signaling channel
\Lambda\in\mathcal{O}(A^{\prime}\rightarrow B)

1+R_{\mathrm{T}}(\rho)=||\gamma^{-1/2}\rho\gamma^{-1/2}||_{\infty}
. Therefore,

R_{\mathrm{T}}(\rho_{AB})=R_{\mathrm{T}}(\rho_{B})
is equivalent to

\displaystyle||(\gamma_{A}\otimes\gamma_{B})^{-1/2}\rho_{AB}(\gamma_{A}\otimes\gamma_{B})^{-1/2}||_{\infty}=||\gamma_{B}^{-1/2}\rho_{B}\gamma_{B}^{-1/2}||_{\infty}.

(B.20)

We consider the infinite temperature case, where
\gamma_{A(B)}=\mathbbm{1}/d_{A(B)}

\Lambda
is entanglement-breaking

\rho_{AB}
is separable. We consider a special
\rho_{AB}
which can be written as

\displaystyle\rho_{AB}=\sum_{i=0}^{d_{A}-1}\sum_{j=0}^{d_{B}-1}p_{ij}\mathinner{|{i}\rangle}\!\mathinner{\langle{i}|}_{A}\otimes\mathinner{|{\psi_{j}}\rangle}\!\mathinner{\langle{\psi_{j}}|}_{B},

(B.21)

\displaystyle||(\gamma_{A}\otimes\gamma_{B})^{-1/2}\rho_{AB}(\gamma_{A}\otimes\gamma_{B})^{-1/2}||_{\infty}

C
GPO dilation of a general quantum channel (Proof of Theorem
5
)

In this section, we provide operational meanings of the measures
R_{\mathrm{T}}(\Lambda)
and
R(\Lambda||\Gamma)
by considering the GPO dilation of
\Lambda
.
A GPO dilation of a general channel
\Lambda\in\mathcal{O}(A\rightarrow B)
can be viewed as the combination of a GPO
G\in\mathrm{GPO}(CA\rightarrow B)
and an athermal state
\rho_{C}\in\mathcal{S}(C)
, such that
\Lambda(\cdot)=G(\rho_{C}\otimes\cdot)
. We emphasise that in a GPO dilation, the GPO
G
needs not to be unitary, which is different from the Stinespring’s representation of quantum channels
Stinespring 1955
.

The following theorem relates this simulation task to
R_{\mathrm{T}}(\Lambda)
:

Theorem C.1

(Operational meaning of
R_{\mathrm{T}}(\Lambda)
)
.

Given a general quantum channel
\Lambda\in\mathcal{O}(A\rightarrow B)
, we define the set of states which can simulate this channel with a GPO as
\mathcal{S}^{\,\mathrm{sim}}_{\mathrm{GPO}}(\Lambda)
. The states in
\mathcal{S}^{\,\mathrm{sim}}_{\mathrm{GPO}}(\Lambda)
can use different GPOs for simulation. The following equality holds:

\displaystyle R_{\mathrm{T}}(\Lambda)=\min_{\rho_{C}\in\mathcal{S}^{\,\mathrm{sim}}_{\mathrm{GPO}}(\Lambda)}R_{\mathrm{T}}(\rho_{C}).

(C.1)

Proof.

First, we prove that
R_{\mathrm{T}}(\Lambda)\leq\min_{\rho_{C}\in\mathcal{S}^{\,\mathrm{sim}}_{\mathrm{GPO}}(\Lambda)}R_{\mathrm{T}}(\rho_{C})
. Assume that
\exists\,\rho_{C}\in\mathcal{S}(C),\,G\in\mathrm{GPO}(CA\rightarrow B)
such that
G(\rho_{C}\otimes\cdot)=\Lambda(\cdot)
. We have

\displaystyle R_{\mathrm{T}}(\Lambda)=R_{\mathrm{T}}[\Lambda(\gamma_{A})]=R_{\mathrm{T}}[G(\rho_{C}\otimes\gamma_{A})]\leq R_{\mathrm{T}}(\rho_{C}\otimes\gamma_{A})=R_{\mathrm{T}}(\rho_{C}),

(C.2)

where the first equality follows from Theorem
A.3
, the inequality is due to the monotonicity of
R_{\mathrm{T}}
and in the final equality we used the multiplicity of
R_{\mathrm{T}}
. Thus,
R_{\mathrm{T}}(\Lambda)\leq R_{\mathrm{T}}(\rho_{C})
holds for all
\rho_{C}
and
G
that simulate
\Lambda
. We conclude that
R_{\mathrm{T}}(\Lambda)\leq\min_{\rho_{C}\in\mathcal{S}^{\,\mathrm{sim}}_{\mathrm{GPO}}(\Lambda)}R_{\mathrm{T}}(\rho_{C})
.

To prove that
R_{\mathrm{T}}(\Lambda)\geq\min_{\rho_{C}\in\mathcal{S}^{\,\mathrm{sim}}_{\mathrm{GPO}}(\Lambda)}R_{\mathrm{T}}(\rho_{C})
. we consider a specific channel simulation protocol using a qubit system
C
, with the thermal state
\gamma_{C}
given by

\displaystyle\gamma_{C}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1+R_{\mathrm{T}}(\Lambda)}\mathinner{|{0}\rangle}\!\mathinner{\langle{0}|}_{C}+\frac{R_{\mathrm{T}}(\Lambda)}{1+R_{\mathrm{T}}(\Lambda)}\mathinner{|{1}\rangle}\!\mathinner{\langle{1}|}_{C}.

(C.3)

The joint GPO for simulation is constructed in the following:

\displaystyle\tilde{G}(\tau_{C}\otimes\rho_{A}):=\mathrm{Tr}\{\mathinner{|{0}\rangle}\!\mathinner{\langle{0}|}_{C}\tau_{C}\}\Lambda(\rho_{A})+\mathrm{Tr}\{\mathinner{|{1}\rangle}\!\mathinner{\langle{1}|}_{C}\tau_{C}\}\sigma_{B},

(C.4)

where
\sigma_{B}
is determined from

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1+R_{\mathrm{T}}(\Lambda)}\Lambda(\gamma_{A})+\frac{R_{\mathrm{T}}(\Lambda)}{1+R_{\mathrm{T}}(\Lambda)}\sigma_{B}=\gamma_{B}.

(C.5)

According to the equivalence relation between the static and dynamical
R_{\mathrm{T}}
(Theorem
A.3
), i.e.,
R_{\mathrm{T}}(\Lambda)=R_{\mathrm{T}}[\Lambda(\gamma_{A})]
, we note that the state
\sigma_{B}
exists and is the optimal state in the standard form of
R_{\mathrm{T}}[\Lambda(\gamma_{A})]
[Eq. (
A.1
)]. It is straightforward to check that using the state
\rho_{C}\equiv\mathinner{|{0}\rangle}\!\mathinner{\langle{0}|}_{C}
, the channel

\tilde{G}
in Eq. (
C.4
) is a valid GPO that simulates
\Lambda
:

\displaystyle\tilde{G}(\mathinner{|{0}\rangle}\!\mathinner{\langle{0}|}_{C}\otimes\cdot)

\displaystyle=\Lambda(\cdot),

(C.6)

\displaystyle\tilde{G}(\gamma_{C}\otimes\gamma_{A})

\displaystyle=\gamma_{B}.

(C.7)

Since
R_{\mathrm{T}}(\rho_{C})=R_{\mathrm{T}}(\mathinner{|{0}\rangle}\!\mathinner{\langle{0}|}_{C})=R_{\mathrm{T}}(\Lambda)
, we have
R_{\mathrm{T}}(\Lambda)=R_{\mathrm{T}}(\rho_{C})\geq\min_{\rho_{C}\in\mathcal{S}^{\,\mathrm{sim}}_{\mathrm{GPO}}(\Lambda)}R_{\mathrm{T}}(\rho_{C})
. Hence, we have

\displaystyle R_{\mathrm{T}}(\Lambda)=\min_{\rho_{C}\in\mathcal{S}^{\,\mathrm{sim}}_{\mathrm{GPO}}(\Lambda)}R_{\mathrm{T}}(\rho_{C}).

(C.8)

∎

Lemma D.2
.

Given a GPO

Proof.

It is straightforward that the equality holds when

or

,

To find

recall Eq. (

\displaystyle\Lambda{S}^{(G)}(\gamma_{A})

\displaystyle=s^{2}\Lambda{S}^{(\Gamma)}(\gamma_{A})+2s(1-s)\rho_{C}\otimes\Gamma(\gamma_{A})+(1-s)^{2}\rho_{C}\otimes\gamma_{B}

(D.59)

where

A^{\prime}

in Eq. (

[\lambda\gamma_{C^{\prime}}\otimes\gamma_{B}-\Lambda{S}^{(G)}(\gamma_{A})]
in the energy eigenbasis of

as a block matrix

(from now we ignore the labels of systems):

\displaystyle N

\displaystyle=\lambda\begin{pmatrix}\gamma/2&0\\
0&\gamma/2\end{pmatrix}-s^{2}\begin{pmatrix}\alpha\gamma&\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\varphi}\sqrt{\alpha(1-\alpha)}\gamma^{3}\\
\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\varphi}\sqrt{\alpha(1-\alpha)}\gamma^{3}&(1-\alpha)\gamma\end{pmatrix}-(1-s^{2})\begin{pmatrix}\alpha\gamma&\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\varphi}\sqrt{\alpha(1-\alpha)}\gamma\\

\displaystyle N\geq 0\Leftrightarrow(\lambda/2+\alpha-1)\gamma-\frac{\alpha(1-\alpha)}{\lambda/2-\alpha}[s^{2}\gamma^{3}+(1-s^{2})\gamma]\gamma^{-1}[s^{2}\gamma^{3}+(1-s^{2})\gamma]\geq 0.

\displaystyle\gamma\geq\frac{\alpha(1-\alpha)}{(\lambda/2+\alpha-1)(\lambda/2-\alpha)}[s^{2}\gamma^{3}+(1-s^{2})\gamma]\gamma^{-1}[s^{2}\gamma^{3}+(1-s^{2})\gamma].

(D.63)

\displaystyle g_{i}\geq\frac{\alpha(1-\alpha)}{(\lambda/2+\alpha-1)(\lambda/2-\alpha)}[s^{2}g_{i}^{3}+(1-s^{2})g_{i}]g_{i}^{-1}[s^{2}g_{i}^{3}+(1-s^{2})g_{i}],\quad\forall\,i=0,1,\dots,d-1,

\displaystyle\lambda\geq 1+\sqrt{1-4(1-g_{i}^{2})[2-(1-g_{i}^{2})s^{2}]s^{2}\alpha(1-\alpha)},\quad\forall\,i=0,1,\dots,d-1,

(D.66)

where

\gamma

\lambda\geq 1
and
\lambda/2>\max\{\alpha,1-\alpha\}
to neglect other ranges of
\lambda
and the last line follows from that the right-hand side of the second line is a monotonically increasing function of
g_{i}

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle R_{\mathrm{T}}(\Lambda{S}^{(G)})=R_{\mathrm{T}}[\Lambda{S}^{(G)}(\gamma_{A})]=\lambda_{\min}-1=\sqrt{1-4(1-g_{\max}^{2})[2-(1-g_{\max}^{2})s^{2}]s^{2}\alpha(1-\alpha)},

(D.67)

\forall\,\alpha\in[0,1],\,\varphi\in[0,2\pi],\,s\in[0,1]

E
Resource analysis for coherent control

An alternative way to activate the signaling ability of a quantum channel by multiple using it is the coherent control of quantum channels
Abbott et al. 2020
. In this setup, the implementation of one of two quantum channels is conditioned on the state of a control qubit, either
\mathinner{|{0}\rangle}
or
\mathinner{|{1}\rangle}
. By preparing the control qubit in the superposition state
\mathinner{|{+}\rangle}
, the two channel implementations can be coherently superposed. This differs from the case of the quantum switch, where it is the causal order of the two channels—not their implementations—that is placed in a quantum superposition.
Due to the similar structures, the performances of coherent control and quantum switch have been compared in the literature
Loizeau and Grinbaum 2020
;
Abbott et al. 2020
. In this section, we perform a similar resource analysis as we did for the quantum switch and show how the two objects are similar.

A coherent control of two GPOs with a control qubit
\rho_{C}
can be considered as a channel simulation of
\Lambda{C}
.

For a fair comparison, we choose the same control qubit
\rho_{C}
[Eq. (
D.25
)], systems
A
and
B
, and the signaling GPO [Eq. (
D.5
)] as those for the quantum switch. The channel induced by the coherent control is denoted as
\Lambda{C}\equiv\mathdutchcal{C}[G,G](\rho_{C}\otimes\cdot)\in\mathcal{O}(A\rightarrow BC^{\prime})
(See Fig.
E.1
). We have the following theorem:

Theorem E.1
.

Given the coherent control with the two input channels as
G
in Eq. (
D.5
), the following bounds hold

\displaystyle R(\Lambda{C}||\Gamma)

\displaystyle\leq R_{\mathrm{T}}(\rho_{C})+2P_{\mathrm{T}}(G)+\sqrt{\alpha(1-\alpha)s(1-s)}f(\gamma),

(E.1)

\displaystyle R(\Lambda{C}||\Gamma)

\displaystyle\geq R_{\mathrm{S}}(\Lambda{C})\geq(g_{\min}^{4}/2)\left[R(\Lambda{C}||\Gamma)-R_{\mathrm{T}}(\Lambda{C})\right]^{2},

(E.2)

where
R_{\mathrm{T}}(\rho_{C})=1,\,\forall\,\alpha\in[0,1],\,\varphi\in[0,2\pi]
and

\Omega\in\mathcal{O}(A\rightarrow B)

\Omega(\cdot)\equiv\sum_{n=0}^{N_{\mathrm{Kraus}}-1}K_{n}(\cdot)K_{n}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=\lambda\begin{pmatrix}\gamma/2&0\\
0&\gamma/2\end{pmatrix}-\begin{pmatrix}\alpha\gamma&d^{-2}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\varphi}\sqrt{\alpha(1-\alpha)}\hat{\gamma}\\
d^{-2}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\varphi}\sqrt{\alpha(1-\alpha)}\hat{\gamma}&(1-\alpha)\gamma\end{pmatrix}

(E.66)

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}(\lambda/2-\alpha)\gamma&-d^{-2}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\varphi}\sqrt{\alpha(1-\alpha)}\hat{\gamma}\\
-d^{-2}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\varphi}\sqrt{\alpha(1-\alpha)}\hat{\gamma}&(\lambda/2+\alpha-1)\gamma\end{pmatrix}.

(E.67)

When
\alpha=0
or
1
, it is easy to find that the minimal
\lambda
ensuring
N\geq 0
is
2
, and thus
R_{\mathrm{T}}(\Lambda{C}^{(\Gamma)})=R_{\mathrm{T}}[\Lambda{C}^{(\Gamma)}(\gamma_{A})]=2-1=1
. For
\alpha\in(0,1)
, to let
N\geq 0
, we need
\lambda/2>\max\{\alpha,1-\alpha\}
, which renders the top-left and bottom-right blocks in
N
[Eq. (
E.67
)] be positive definite. By Schur complement (Theorem 1.12 in Ref.
Zhang 2006
), we have

\displaystyle N\geq 0\Leftrightarrow(\lambda/2+\alpha-1)\gamma-\frac{\alpha(1-\alpha)}{d^{4}(\lambda/2-\alpha)}\,\hat{\gamma}\gamma^{-1}\hat{\gamma}\geq 0.

(E.68)

Using the equality
\hat{\gamma}\gamma^{-1}\hat{\gamma}=d\hat{\gamma}
[Eq. (
E.42
)], the condition can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\gamma\geq\frac{\alpha(1-\alpha)}{d^{3}(\lambda/2+\alpha-1)(\lambda/2-\alpha)}\hat{\gamma}.

(E.69)

Now we show that
\min\{f|\hat{\gamma}\leq f\cdot d\gamma\}=1
. By Lemma
A.2
, it is equivalent to show that
\exists\,\mathinner{|{\phi}\rangle},\,\mathinner{\langle{\phi}|}d\gamma-\hat{\gamma}\mathinner{|{\phi}\rangle}=0
and
d\gamma-\hat{\gamma}\geq 0
. Such a state
\mathinner{|{\phi}\rangle}
can be chosen as
\mathinner{|{\phi}\rangle}\equiv\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}g_{i}^{-1/2}\mathinner{|{i}\rangle}
so that

\displaystyle\mathinner{\langle{\phi}|}d\gamma\mathinner{|{\phi}\rangle}

\displaystyle=d\left(\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}g_{i}^{-1/2}\mathinner{\langle{i}|}\right)\gamma\left(\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}g_{j}^{-1/2}\mathinner{|{j}\rangle}\right)=d\left(\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}g_{i}^{-1/2}\mathinner{\langle{i}|}\right)\left(\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}g_{j}^{1/2}\mathinner{|{j}\rangle}\right)=d\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}1=d^{2}.

(E.70)

\displaystyle\mathinner{\langle{\phi}|}\hat{\gamma}\mathinner{|{\phi}\rangle}

\displaystyle=\mathinner{\langle{\phi}|}\sum_{i,j=0}^{d-1}\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}g_{i}^{1/2}g_{j}^{1/2}g_{k}^{-1/2}\mathinner{|{i}\rangle}\!\mathinner{\langle{j}|}\mathinner{|{k}\rangle}=d\mathinner{\langle{\phi}|}\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}g_{i}^{1/2}\mathinner{|{i}\rangle}=d\left(\sum_{\ell=0}^{d-1}g_{\ell}^{-1/2}\mathinner{\langle{\ell}|}\right)\left(\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}g_{i}^{1/2}\mathinner{|{i}\rangle}\right)=d\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}1=d^{2},

(E.71)

Thus,
\mathinner{\langle{\phi}|}d\gamma-\hat{\gamma}\mathinner{|{\phi}\rangle}=d^{2}-d^{2}=0
. Besides, we note that
d\gamma-\hat{\gamma}=\gamma^{1/2}(d\mathbbm{1}-O_{d})\gamma^{1/2}
where
O_{d}\equiv\sum_{i,j=0}^{d-1}\mathinner{|{i}\rangle}\!\mathinner{\langle{j}|}
. Since
d\mathbbm{1}-O_{d}\geq 0
and
\gamma
is full-rank, we have
d\gamma-\hat{\gamma}\geq 0
. Therefore, for Eq. (
E.69
) to holds, we have

\displaystyle d\leq\frac{d^{3}(\lambda/2+\alpha-1)(\lambda/2-\alpha)}{\alpha(1-\alpha)},

(E.72)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\quad

\displaystyle\lambda\geq 1+\sqrt{(1-2\alpha)^{2}+4d^{-2}\alpha(1-\alpha)}

(E.73)

where in the second line we used
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
\lambda\geq 1
and
\lambda/2>\max\{\alpha,1-\alpha\}
to neglect other ranges of
\lambda
. We thus have

\displaystyle R_{\mathrm{T}}(\Lambda{C}^{(\Gamma)})=R_{\mathrm{T}}[\Lambda{C}^{(\Gamma)}(\gamma_{A})]=\lambda_{\min}-1=\sqrt{(1-2\alpha)^{2}+4d^{-2}\alpha(1-\alpha)},\quad\forall\,\alpha\in[0,1],\,\varphi\in[0,2\pi],

(E.74)

where we have included the case of
\alpha=0
or
1
because the expression correctly gives
R_{\mathrm{T}}(\Lambda{C}^{(\Gamma)})=1
as
\alpha=0
or
1
. Eq. (
E.3
) is hence derived.

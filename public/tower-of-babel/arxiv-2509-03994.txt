Generalized hypergeometric equations and 2 2 d TQFT for dormant opers in characteristic ≤ 7 \leq 7
Keita Mori, Yasuhiro Wakabayashi

Abstract
. This note studies PGL n \mathrm{PGL}_{n} -opers arising from generalized hypergeometric differential equations in prime characteristic p p . We prove that these opers are rigid within the class of dormant opers. By combining this rigidity result with previous work in the enumerative geometry of dormant opers, we obtain a complete and explicit description of the 2 2 d TQFTs that compute the number of dormant PGL n \mathrm{PGL}_{n} -opers for primes p ≤ 7 p\leq 7 .

1.
Introduction

A
G
-oper
for a reductive group
G
is a particular type of flat
G
-bundle on an algebraic curve.
This notion was introduced
in the context of the geometric Langlands correspondence, serving as an element for constructing Hecke eigensheaves on the moduli space of bundles via quantization of Hitchin’s integrable system (cf.
[
BeDr
]
).
When
G=\mathrm{GL}_{n}
or
\mathrm{PGL}_{n}
(with
n\geq 2
),
such opers correspond to certain
flat vector bundles of rank
n
equipped with complete flags, and are associated with ordinary linear differential operators whose principal symbols are unit.
For instance, each
\mathrm{PGL}_{2}
-oper on the projective line with at most regular singularities at the three points
0
,
1
, and
\infty
arises from a
Gauss hypergeometric differential operator

\displaystyle D_{a,b,c}:=\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}+\left(\frac{c}{x}+\frac{1-c+a+b}{x-1}\right)\cdot\frac{d}{dx}+\frac{ab}{x(x-1)},

(1.1)

determined by a triple
(a,b,c)
of parameters, where
x
denotes the standard coordinate on the projective line.

In the complex analytic setting,

\mathrm{PGL}_{2}
-opers on a closed Riemann surface
can be identified, via the Riemann-Hilbert correspondence, with certain refinements of its complex structure known as

projective structures
, i.e., atlases of coordinate charts whose transition functions are Möbius transformations.
A canonical example of a projective structure is constructed by the system of local inverse maps of the universal covering arising from uniformization.

G
-opers
in prime characteristic
p>0
have been studied as a part of the characteristic-
p
analogue of the geometric Langlands correspondence (cf.
[
BeTr
]
), as well as in connection with various topics, including

p
-adic Teichmüller theory (cf., e.g.,
[
Moc1
]
,
[
Moc2
]
,
[
JRXY
]
,
[
JoPa
]
,
[
LaPa
]
, and
[
LiOs
]
).
A central concept in these developments is the
p
-curvature
of a flat
G
-bundle, which serves as an
invariant measuring the obstruction to compatibility between
p
-power operations on certain spaces of infinitesimal symmetries.
This invariant also involves
the Grothendieck-Katz conjecture, which provides a conjectural criterion for
the algebraicity of solutions to linear differential equations
(cf.
[
NKa3
]
,
[
And
]
).

A
G
-oper is said to be
dormant
if its
p
-curvature vanishes.
In the context of
p
-adic Teichmuller theory, dormant
\mathrm{PGL}_{2}
-opers (or more generally,
\mathrm{PGL}_{2}
-opers with nilpotent
p
-curvature) may be viewed as analogues of “well-behaved” projective structures on Riemann surfaces such as those arising from uniformization.
The theory of dormant
G
-opers for general
G
has been developed extensively in the author’s works (cf., e.g.,
[
Wak1
]
,
[
Wak2
]
,
[
Wak3
]
,
[
Wak4
]
,
[
Wak6
]
,
[
Wak7
]
,
[
Wak8
]
, and
[
Wak9
]
).

Now, let us consider the case where
G=\mathrm{PGL}_{n}
with
2\leq n\leq p
, and fix a pair of nonnegative integers
(g,r)
satisfying
2g-2+r>0
.
A central object in aforementioned works is the moduli stack

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}p^{{}^{\mathrm{Zzz...}}}_{n,\rho,g,r}

(1.2)

(cf. (
3.1
)), which classifies pairs
(\mathscr{X},\mathscr{E}^{\spadesuit})

consisting of a pointed curve
\mathscr{X}
in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,r}
(:= the moduli stack of
r
-pointed stable curves of genus
g
in characteristic
p
) and
a dormant
\mathrm{PGL}_{n}
-oper
\mathscr{E}^{\spadesuit}
on
\mathscr{X}
of prescribed radii
\rho
(cf.
[
Wak4
, Definition 2.32]
for the definition of radius).
As shown in
[
Wak2
]
and
[
Wak4
]
,
the stack
\mathcal{O}p^{{}^{\mathrm{Zzz...}}}_{n,\rho,g,r}
is finite and generically étale over
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,r}
, so it is meaningful to consider its generic degree

\displaystyle N_{p,n,\rho,g,r}:=\mathrm{deg}(\mathcal{O}p^{{}^{\mathrm{Zzz...}}}_{n,\rho,g,r}/\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,r})

(1.3)

(cf. (
3.3
)),
which counts the number of dormant
\mathrm{PGL}_{n}
-opers of radii
\rho
on a general curve in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,r}
.

Recall from
[
Wak10
]
(or
[
Wak5
]
) that
the values
N_{p,n,\rho,g,r}

satisfy a factorization rule governed by various
gluing procedures of underlying stable curves,
and this structure endows them with the properties of a
2
-dimensional topological quantum field theory (
2
d TQFT).
A major goal of our study is to understand this
2
d TQFT, as it provides a bridge between
the theory of dormant opers and other enumerative geometries, such as the Gromov-Witten theory of Grassmannians and the conformal field theory associated to the affine Lie algebras (cf.
[
Wak4
]
).

Concerning this,
it is known that a
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
-oper or a
\mathrm{PGL}_{n}
-oper is dormant if and only if the corresponding differential operator
admits a full set of solutions
.
When a given
\mathrm{PGL}_{2}
-oper arises from the operator
D_{a,b,c}
as defined above,
this condition translates into the requirement that

(a,b,c)
is the mod
p
reduction of a triple of integers
(\widetilde{a},\widetilde{b},\widetilde{c})
in
\{1,\cdots,p\}
satisfying either
\widetilde{a}<\widetilde{c}\leq\widetilde{b}
or
\widetilde{b}<\widetilde{c}\leq\widetilde{a}
(cf.
[
Iha
]
,
[
NKa2
]
).
This characterization enables an explicit description of the
2
d TQFT governing dormant
\mathrm{PGL}_{2}
-opers.

However, a comprehensive understanding of this TQFT remains out of reach for general
n
,
as little seems to be known beyond the special cases
n=p-2,p-1,p
.
Thus, in this note, we take a step toward a broader understanding by investigating the number of dormant
\mathrm{PGL}_{n}
-opers for
n>2
.

Thanks to the factorization property of the values
N_{p,n,\rho,g,r}
,
it suffices to consider the case
(g,r)=(0,3)
.
A key insight in our discussion is that, in this case, most dormant opers arise from
generalized hypergeometric differential operators
, expressed as

\displaystyle D_{\alpha,\beta}:=x\frac{d}{dx}\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(x\frac{d}{dx}+\beta_{j}-1\right)-x\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{n}\left(x\frac{d}{dx}+\alpha_{j}\right)

(1.4)

for suitable parameters
\alpha:=(\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{n})
,
\beta:=(\beta_{1},\cdots,\beta_{n-1})
.
We then apply a result of Katz (cf.
[
NKa4
]
), which generalizes the case
n=2
, i.e, Gauss’ hypergeometric operators, to determine the conditions under which such an operator
D_{\alpha,\beta}
has a full set of root functions (i.e., functions annihilated by
D_{\alpha,\beta}
), or equivalently, when the associated
\mathrm{PGL}_{n}
-oper is dormant.
As a consequence, we obtain a complete and explicit description of the
2
d TQFT for dormant
\mathrm{PGL}_{n}
-opers in characteristic
p\leq 7
(cf. Section
3.3
for details).
This provides the first effective method for computing the values
N_{p,n,\rho,g,r}
in the previously unexplored range
2<n<p-2
and
r>0
.

2.
\mathrm{PGL}_{n}
-opers induced from generalized hypergeometric operators

In this section, we study generalized hypergeometric differential operators in characteristic
p
, as well as the

\mathrm{PGL}_{n}
-opers
induced from them.
These opers are described in terms of logarithmic connections on vector bundles, following the approach of
[
Wak4
]
).
We emphasize that, on the
3
-pointed projective line, the dormant opers, i.e., those with vanishing
p
-curvature, are
classified by certain configurations of radii.
This classification follows from
Katz’s result characterizing when a generalized hypergeometric operator admits a full set of root functions (cf. Proposition
2.12
).
The main result of this section asserts that such dormant
\mathrm{PGL}_{n}
-opers are uniquely determined by their radii (cf. Theorem
2.13
).

2.1.
Generalized hypergeometric operators in characteristic
p

Let
k(x)
denote the field of rational functions in the variable
x
over
k
, and endow it with the structure of a differential field over
k
via the derivation

\delta_{x}:=x\frac{d}{dx}
.

Let
n
,
m
be positive integers and consider tuples
\alpha:=(\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{n})\in k^{n}
,
\beta:=(\beta_{1},\cdots,\beta_{m})\in k^{m}
.
To this pair
(\alpha,\beta)
, we associate
the
generalized hypergeometric differential operator

\displaystyle D_{\alpha,\beta}:=\delta_{x}\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{m}\left(\delta_{x}+\beta_{j}-1\right)-x\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{n}\left(\delta_{x}+\alpha_{j}\right),

(2.1)

defined as a linear differential operator on
k(x)
.
When we regard
k(x)
as a
k(x^{p})
-vector space with basis
1,x,\cdots,x^{p-1}
,
the operator
D_{\alpha,\beta}
defines a
k(x^{p})
-linear endomorphism of
k(x)
.
In particular, the kernel
\mathrm{Ker}(D_{\alpha,\beta})
forms a
k(x^{p})
-vector subspace of
k(x)
, and its dimension satisfies
\mathrm{dim}_{k(x^{p})}(\mathrm{Ker}(D_{\alpha,\beta}))\leq p
.
In what follows, we investigate how the dimension of
\mathrm{Ker}(D_{\alpha,\beta})
can be described in terms of the data
(\alpha,\beta)
.

Note that the operator
D_{\alpha,\beta}
is invariant under reordering of the entries in
\alpha
and
\beta
.
Thus, without loss of generality, we may assume the following conditions after possibly reordering the elements:

•

\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{n^{\prime}},\beta_{1},\cdots,\beta_{m^{\prime}}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
and
\alpha_{n^{\prime}+1},\cdots,\alpha_{n},\beta_{m^{\prime}+1},\cdots,\beta_{m}\in k\setminus\mathbb{F}_{p}
, for some integers
0\leq n^{\prime}\leq n
and
0\leq m^{\prime}\leq m
;

•

The following inequalities are fulfilled:

\displaystyle p\geq\widetilde{\alpha}_{1}\geq\widetilde{\alpha}_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\widetilde{\alpha}_{n^{\prime}}\geq 1\ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ p\geq\widetilde{\beta}_{1}\geq\widetilde{\beta}_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\widetilde{\beta}_{m^{\prime}}\geq 1,

(2.2)

where, for each
\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
, we denote by
\widetilde{\gamma}
the unique integer in
\{1,\cdots,p\}
congruent to
\gamma
modulo
p
.

We then define

\displaystyle T_{\alpha,\beta}

(2.3)

to be the subset of
\{1,\cdots,m^{\prime}\}
consisting of those indices
j
for which there exists some
j^{\prime}
satisfying
\widetilde{\beta}_{j}>\widetilde{\alpha}_{j^{\prime}}\geq\widetilde{\beta}_{j+1}
, where we set
\widetilde{\beta}_{m^{\prime}+1}:=1
by convention.

Now, observe that, for each
s\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, the following equality holds:

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{x}\cdot D_{\alpha,\beta}\right)(x^{s})=\left(s\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{m}(s-1+\beta_{j})\right)x^{s-1}+\left(-\prod_{j=1}^{n}(s+\alpha_{j})\right)x^{s}.

(2.4)

Define the polynomials

\displaystyle P(X):=-\prod_{j=1}^{n}(X+\alpha_{j}),\hskip 28.45274ptQ(X):=(X+1)\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{m}(X+\beta_{j}).

(2.5)

Then, the matrix representation of the operator
\frac{1}{x}\cdot D_{\alpha,\beta}
with respect to the basis
1,x,\cdots,x^{p-1}
of the
k(x^{p})
-vector space
k(x)
is given by the upper bidiagonal matrix

\displaystyle R_{\alpha,\beta}:=\begin{pmatrix}P(0)&Q(0)&0&0&0&\cdots&0\\
0&P(1)&Q(1)&0&0&\cdots&0\\
0&0&P(2)&Q(2)&0&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\vdots&\vdots&&&P(p-3)&Q(p-3)&0\\
0&0&\cdots&\cdots&0&P(p-2)&Q(p-2)\\
0&0&\cdots&\cdots&0&0&P(p-1)\end{pmatrix}.

(2.6)

In particular, we have the identity

\displaystyle p-\mathrm{rank}\left(R_{\alpha,\beta}\right)=\mathrm{dim}_{k(x^{p})}\left(\mathrm{Ker}\left(\frac{1}{x}\cdot D_{\alpha,\beta}\right)\right)=\mathrm{dim}_{k(x^{p})}(\mathrm{Ker}(D_{\alpha,\beta})).

(2.7)

Moreover, note that the diagonal entry
P(\ell)
(for
0\leq\ell\leq p-1
) is congruent to zero modulo
p
precisely when

\ell=p-\widetilde{\alpha}_{j}
for some
j\in\{1,\cdots,n^{\prime}\}
.

Proposition 2.1
.

For
(\alpha,\beta)\in k^{n}\times k^{m}
as above, the following equality holds:

\displaystyle\mathrm{rank}(\mathrm{Ker}(D_{\alpha,\beta}))=\sharp(T_{\alpha,\beta}).

(2.8)

Proof.

For convenience,
we set
b_{0}:=-1
,
b_{m^{\prime}+1}:=p-1
, and
b_{j}:=p-\widetilde{\beta}_{j}
for
j=1,\cdots,m^{\prime}
.
For each
j=1,\cdots,m^{\prime}+1
, we define a
k(x^{p})
-vector subspace of
k(x)
by

L_{j}:=\bigoplus_{s=b_{j-1}+1}^{b_{j}}k(x^{p})x^{s}\left(\subseteq L\right)
.
Then,
k(x)
decomposes as
L=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{m^{\prime}+1}L_{j}
, which induces a decomposition
\frac{1}{x}\cdot D_{\alpha,\beta}=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{m^{\prime}+1}D^{\prime}_{j}
,
where each
D^{\prime}_{j}
is a
k(x^{p})
-linear endomorphism of
L_{j}
.
The matrix representing
D_{j}
with respect to the basis
x^{b_{j-1}+1},\cdots,x^{b_{j}}
is given by the
(b_{j}-b_{j-1})\times(b_{j}-b_{j-1})
bidiagonal matrix

\displaystyle R^{\prime}_{j}:=\begin{pmatrix}P(b_{j-1}+1)&Q(b_{j-1}+1)&0&\cdots&0&0&0\\
0&P(b_{j-1}+2)&Q(b_{j-1}+2)&\cdots&0&0&0\\
0&0&P(b_{j-1}+3)&\ddots&0&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\cdots&P(b_{j}-2)&Q(b_{j}-2)&0\\
0&0&0&\cdots&0&P(b_{j}-1)&Q(b_{j}-1)\\
0&0&0&\cdots&0&0&P(b_{j})\end{pmatrix}.

(2.9)

Since all off-diagonal entries
Q(b_{j-1}+1),\cdots,Q(b_{j}-1)
are nonzero, we have
\mathrm{rank}(R^{\prime}_{j})=b_{j}-b_{j-1}-1
if
j-1\in T_{\alpha,\beta}
(
\Leftrightarrow b_{j-1}<p-\widetilde{\alpha}_{j^{\prime}}\leq b_{j}
for some
j^{\prime}
), and
\mathrm{rank}(R^{\prime}_{j})=b_{j}-b_{j-1}
if otherwise.
Hence, the following equalities hold:

\displaystyle\mathrm{dim}_{k(x^{p})}\left(\mathrm{Ker}\left(\frac{1}{x}\cdot D_{\alpha,\beta}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=p-\mathrm{rank}R_{a,b}

(2.10)

\displaystyle=p-\sum_{j=1}^{m^{\prime}+1}\mathrm{rank}(R^{\prime}_{j})

\displaystyle=p-\left(\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m^{\prime}+1}(b_{j}-b_{j-1})\right)-\sharp(T_{\alpha,\beta})\right)

\displaystyle=p-\left(b_{m^{\prime}}-b_{0}-\sharp(T_{\alpha,\beta})\right)

\displaystyle=\sharp(T_{\alpha,\beta}).

The assertion then follows from (
2.7
).
∎

The following assertion is a direct consequence of the proposition above.
It was already established in

[
NKa4
, Sublemma 5.5.2.1]
(and
[
Iha
, Section 1.6]
for the case of Gauss’ hypergeometric operators) to provide a complete classification of hypergeometric differential operators with finite monodromy; see also
[
BeHe
, Remark 4.9]
.

Corollary 2.2
.

(Recall that
(\alpha,\beta)
has assumed to satisfy the inequalities in (
2.2
).)
The kernel
\mathrm{Ker}(D_{\alpha,\beta})
has rank
n
(as a
k(x^{p})
-vector space) if and only if the following two conditions are fulfilled:

(1)

n^{\prime}=n
and
m^{\prime}=m
, i.e., the two sets
\{\alpha_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n},\{\beta_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}
are contained in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
;

(2)

m=n-1
and the following chain of inequalities holds:

\displaystyle\widetilde{\alpha}_{1}\geq\widetilde{\beta}_{1}>\widetilde{\alpha}_{2}\geq\widetilde{\beta}_{2}>\cdots\geq\widetilde{\beta}_{n-1}>\widetilde{\alpha}_{n}.

(2.11)

Proof.

By the definition of
T_{\alpha,\beta}
,
the equality
\sharp(T_{\alpha,\beta})=n
holds precisely when
both conditions (1) and (2) are fulfilled.
Thus, the assertion follows from Proposition
2.1
.
∎

2.2.
Dormant
\mathrm{PGL}_{n}
-opers

We now begin our discussion of
dormant
\mathrm{PGL}_{n}
-opers (= dormant
\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}_{n}
-opers) of prescribed radii on pointed curves.
To simplify the exposition, we will work with equivalent objects described in terms of log connections on vector bundles (without using the formulation by log structures).
For a comprehensive treatment of
\mathrm{PGL}_{n}
-opers on log curves and their various properties, we refer the reader to
[
Wak4
]

Let
(g,r)
be a pair of nonnegative integers with
2g-2+r>0
, and

\mathscr{X}:=(X,\{\sigma_{i}\}_{i=1}^{r})
an
r
-pointed proper smooth curve of genus
g
over
k
, where
X
denotes the underlying curve and
\sigma_{1},\cdots,\sigma_{r}
are ordered marked points on
X

Denote by
\Omega\left(:=\Omega_{X/k}(\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sigma_{i})\right)
the sheaf of logarithmic

-forms on
X/k
with poles along the marked points
\sigma_{i}
.
Also, denote by
\mathcal{T}
its dual, i.e.,
\mathcal{T}:=\Omega^{\vee}
.
For each
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, we have
the sheaf of crystalline logarithmic differential operators
\mathcal{D}_{\leq j}
on
X/k
(with poles along
\sigma_{i}

\leq j
(cf.
[

[
Wak4

We set
\mathcal{D}:=\bigcup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}\mathcal{D}_{\leq j}

Recall that a

\mathcal{O}_{X}

\mathcal{F}
is a

\nabla:\mathcal{F}\rightarrow\Omega\otimes\mathcal{F}
satisfying the usual Leibnitz rule

\displaystyle\nabla_{\partial}(a\cdot v)=\partial(a)\cdot v+a\cdot\nabla_{\partial}(v)

(2.12)

for any local sections
\partial\in\mathcal{T}
,
a\in\mathcal{O}_{X}
, and
v\in\mathcal{F}
, where
\nabla_{\partial}:=(\partial\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{F}})\circ\nabla
(cf.
[

, Definition 4.1]
).

The

-curvature
of such a log connection

\mathcal{O}_{X}
-linear morphism

\displaystyle\psi(\nabla):\mathcal{T}^{\otimes p}\rightarrow\mathcal{E}nd_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{F})

(2.13)

determined uniquely by the condition that
\psi(\nabla)(\partial^{\otimes p})=\nabla_{\partial}^{p}-\nabla_{\partial^{p}}
for any local section
\partial\in\mathcal{T}
, where
\partial^{p}
denotes
the local section of
\mathcal{T}
corresponding to the

-th iterate of the (locally defined) derivation on

associated to

.

We now fix
an

\mathscr{X}
in the sense of

\displaystyle\vartheta:=(\varTheta,\nabla_{\vartheta})

consisting of a line bundle
\varTheta
on

and a log connection
\nabla_{\vartheta}
on the line bundle
\mathcal{T}^{\otimes\frac{n(n-1)}{2}}\otimes\varTheta^{\otimes n}
.
Moreover, we assume that
\nabla_{\vartheta}
has vanishing
p
-curvature.
(Such an
n
-theta characteristic always exists, according to the discussion in
[
Wak4
, Section 4.6.4]
.)
Then,
the residue
\mathrm{Res}_{\sigma_{i}}(\nabla_{\vartheta})
of
\nabla_{\vartheta}
at each marked point
\sigma_{i}
(

) is given by an element of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
(cf., e.g.,
[
Wak10
, Proposition-Definition 4.8]
).

We define

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\varTheta}:=\mathcal{D}_{\leq n-1}\otimes\varTheta\ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ \mathcal{F}_{\varTheta}^{j}:=\mathcal{D}_{\leq n-j-1}\otimes\varTheta\ (j=0,\cdots,n).

(2.15)

In particular,
\{\mathcal{F}_{\varTheta}^{j}\}_{j=0}^{n}
forms an
n
-step decreasing filtration on
\mathcal{F}_{\varTheta}
whose graded pieces are line bundles.
The determinant
\mathrm{det}(\mathcal{F}_{\varTheta})
of
\mathcal{F}_{\varTheta}
admits a sequence of canonical isomorphisms

\displaystyle\mathrm{det}(\mathcal{F}_{\varTheta})\xrightarrow{\sim}\bigotimes_{j=0}^{n-1}\mathcal{F}^{j}_{\varTheta}/\mathcal{F}^{j+1}_{\varTheta}\xrightarrow{\sim}\bigotimes_{j=0}^{n-1}\left(\mathcal{T}^{\otimes n-j-1}\otimes\varTheta\right)\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{T}^{\otimes\frac{n(n-1)}{2}}\otimes\varTheta^{\otimes n}.

(2.16)

Definition 2.3

(cf.
[
Wak4
]
, Definition 4.36)
.

(i)

A
(\mathrm{GL}_{n},\vartheta)
-oper
on
\mathscr{X}
is a log connection
\nabla^{\diamondsuit}
on
\mathcal{F}_{\varTheta}
satisfying the following three conditions:

–

For each

3.2.
2
d TQFT for dormant
\mathrm{PGL}_{n}
-opers

To formulate a factorization property for
N_{n,\rho,g,r}

we recall the definition of a
2
d TQFT.
For a precise and detailed account, we refer to
[
Koc
]
, as well as
[
Ati
]
,
[
DuMu1
]
,
[
DuMu2
]
.

Let
\Sigma
and
\Sigma^{\prime}
be
closed oriented
1
-dimensional manifolds.
An
oriented cobordism
from
\Sigma
to
\Sigma^{\prime}
is defined as a compact oriented
2
-dimensional manifold
M
together with smooth maps
\Sigma\rightarrow M
,
\Sigma^{\prime}\rightarrow M

such that
\Sigma
maps diffeomorphically (preserving orientation) onto the in-boundary of
M
, and
\Sigma^{\prime}
maps diffeomorphically (preserving orientation) onto the out-boundary of
M
.
We denote such a cobordism by
M:\Sigma\Rightarrow\Sigma^{\prime}
.
Two
oriented cobordisms
M,M^{\prime}:\Sigma\Rightarrow\Sigma^{\prime}
are
equivalent
if there exists an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism
\psi:M\xrightarrow{\sim}M^{\prime}
inducing the identity morphisms of
\Sigma
and
\Sigma^{\prime}
.
In this way, one obtains the category

2\text{-}\mathcal{C}ob
whose objects are

1
-dimensional closed oriented manifolds and whose morphisms
from
\Sigma
to
\Sigma^{\prime}
are
equivalence classes
of oriented cobordisms
M:\Sigma\Rightarrow\Sigma^{\prime}
.
The composition of morphisms is given by gluing cobordism classes, and
this category carries a structure of symmetric monoidal category under disjoint union.
On the other hand,
let
\mathcal{V}ect_{\mathbb{Q}}
denote the symmetric monoidal category
of finite-dimensional
\mathbb{Q}
-vector spaces, also with monoidal structure given by the tensor product.
Following
[
Koc
, Section 1.3.32]
,
a
2
-dimensional topological quantum field theory
(over
\mathbb{Q}
), or
2
d TQFT
for short,
is defined to be
a symmetric monoidal functor of the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}:2\text{-}\mathcal{C}ob\rightarrow\mathcal{V}ect_{\mathbb{Q}}.

(3.7)

Note that
each isomorphism class of objects in
2\text{-}\mathcal{C}ob
can be classified by an integer
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
indicating the number of connected components, i.e., the number of disjoint circles
\mathbb{S}:=\left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\,|\,x^{2}+y^{2}=1\right\}
.
In other words, the full subcategory consisting of objects
\{\mathbb{S}^{r}\,|\,r\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\}
, where
\mathbb{S}^{0}:=\emptyset
and
\mathbb{S}^{r}
denotes the disjoint union of
r
copies of
\mathbb{S}
, forms
a skeleton of
2\text{-}\mathcal{C}ob
.
Moreover,
each connected oriented cobordism in
2\text{-}\mathcal{C}ob
may be represented by

\mathbb{M}^{r\Rightarrow s}_{g}
for some triple of nonnegative integers
(g,r,s)
,
where
\mathbb{M}^{r\Rightarrow s}_{g}
denotes a connected, compact oriented surface with in-boundary
\mathbb{S}^{r}
and out-boundary
\mathbb{S}^{s}
.
According to
[
Koc
, Lemma 1.4.19]
, every oriented cobordism in
2\text{-}\mathcal{C}ob
factors as a permutation cobordism, followed by a disjoint union of
\mathbb{M}^{r\Rightarrow s}_{g}
’s (for various triples
(g,r,s)
), followed by a permutation cobordisms.
It follows that a
2
d TQFT
\mathcal{Z}:\mathcal{V}ect_{K}\rightarrow 2\text{-}\mathcal{C}ob
is uniquely determined by the
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space
A:=\mathcal{Z}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
together with a collection of
\mathbb{Q}
-linear maps

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}(\mathbb{M}^{r\Rightarrow s}_{g}):A^{\otimes r}\left(=\mathcal{Z}(\mathbb{S}^{r})\right)\rightarrow A^{\otimes s}\left(=\mathcal{Z}(\mathbb{S}^{s})\right)

(3.8)

for
(g,r,s)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{3}
(where
A^{\otimes 0}:=\mathbb{Q}
).
Using this formalism of
2
d TQFT, we have arrived at the following result.

Theorem 3.3

(cf.
[
Wak10
]
, Theorem C, (ii))
.

There exists a unique
2
d TQFT

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{n}:2\text{-}\mathcal{C}ob\rightarrow\mathcal{V}ect_{\mathbb{Q}}

(3.9)

determined by the following properties:

•

\mathcal{Z}_{n}(\mathbb{S}^{r})=(\mathbb{Q}^{\Xi_{p,n}})^{\otimes r}

r

\mathbb{Q}
-vector space with basis
\Xi_{p,n}

\mathcal{Z}_{n}(\mathbb{M}_{0}^{0\Rightarrow 0})=\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{Q}}
, and

\mathcal{Z}_{n}(\mathbb{M}_{1}^{0\Rightarrow 0})=\frac{(p-1)!}{n!\cdot(p-n)!}\cdot\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{Q}}

•

\mathcal{Z}_{n}(\mathbb{M}_{0}^{0\Rightarrow 1}):\mathbb{Q}\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}^{\Xi_{p,n}}

\mathcal{Z}_{n}(\mathbb{M}_{0}^{0\Rightarrow 2}):\mathbb{Q}\rightarrow(\mathbb{Q}^{\Xi_{p,n}})^{\otimes 2}

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{n}(\mathbb{M}_{0}^{0\Rightarrow 1})(1)=\varepsilon\hskip 14.22636pt\text{and}\hskip 14.22636pt\mathcal{Z}_{n}(\mathbb{M}_{0}^{0\Rightarrow 2})(1)=\sum_{\lambda\in\Xi_{p,n}}\lambda\otimes\lambda^{\veebar},

(3.10)

respectively (cf. Theorem
3.1

[\![a_{1},\cdots,a_{n}]\!]\mapsto[\![-a_{1},\cdots,-a_{n}]\!]

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{n}(\mathbb{M}_{0}^{1\Rightarrow 0})(\lambda)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $\lambda=\varepsilon$};\\

(3.11)

respectively.

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{n}(\mathbb{M}_{g}^{r\Rightarrow s})(\bigotimes_{i=1}^{r}\rho_{i})=\sum_{(\lambda_{j})_{j}\in\Xi_{p,n}^{s}}N_{p,n,((\rho_{i})_{i},(\lambda_{j}^{\veebar})_{j}),g,r+s}\bigotimes_{j=1}^{s}\lambda_{j}.

\displaystyle N_{p,n,(\rho_{1},\rho_{2}),g_{1}+g_{2},r_{1}+r_{2}}=\sum_{\rho_{0}\in\Xi_{p,n}}N_{p,n,(\rho_{1},\rho_{0}),g_{1},r_{1}+1}\cdot N_{p,n,(\rho_{2},\rho_{0}^{\veebar}),g_{2},r_{2}+1}.

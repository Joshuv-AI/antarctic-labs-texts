Simultaneous Sequential Compactness
Sirio Resteghini, Cesare Straffelini

Abstract
. A set of sequences is said to converge simultaneously if there exists an infinite subset H H of the index set ω \omega such that all sequences converge when restricted to H H . We discuss simultaneous convergence of sequences in the same or in different sequentially compact spaces; we link the results for different spaces to ones for the same space; we show that simultaneous convergence happens for less than 𝔰 \mathfrak{s} sequences in spaces with weight bounded by 𝔰 \mathfrak{s} and for less than 𝔥 \mathfrak{h} sequences in general; we show a slight generalisation of these results in the context of Hausdorff spaces; and finally we investigate their optimality.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
03E17, 54A25 (Primary) 54D10, 54D30, 54D55 (Secondary)

1.
Introduction and Preliminary Results

Given a topological space
X
and a sequence
x=\langle x_{n}:n<\omega\rangle
of points in
X
, where the index set
\omega
is the least infinite ordinal, we say that
x
converges to some point
x_{\infty}\in X
if, for every open neighbourhood
U
of
x_{\infty}
in
X
, there exists a natural number
n_{U}<\omega
such that
x_{n}\in U
for all
n>n_{U}
. If
H
is an infinite subset of
\omega
, we say that
x
converges along
H
if the subsequence
x\upharpoonright H=\langle x_{n}:n\in H\rangle
converges.

Our aim is to study the following problem: given several sequences (which may take values in different topological spaces), can we find an infinite subset
H
of
\omega
such that all these sequences converge along
H
? We seek an answer that may depend both on the number of sequences and on the topological properties of spaces under consideration.

Let
[\omega]^{\omega}
denote the set of all infinite subsets of
\omega
. For two elements
H_{0}
and
H_{1}
in
[\omega]^{\omega}
, we write
H_{0}=^{\ast}H_{1}
if their symmetric difference is finite (i.e., they differ only on a finite set), and
H_{0}\subseteq^{\ast}H_{1}
if there exists
H^{\prime}\subseteq H_{1}
with
H_{0}=^{\ast}H^{\prime}
.

If
H_{0}=^{\ast}H_{1}
, then
x\upharpoonright H_{0}
converges if and only if
x\upharpoonright H_{1}
does. On the other hand, if
H_{0}\subseteq^{\ast}H_{1}
, then
x\upharpoonright H_{0}
converges if (but not necessarily only if)
x\upharpoonright H_{1}
converges.

Definition 1.1
.

If
\lambda
is a cardinal, a space
X
is
\lambda
-
simultaneously sequentially compact
(
\lambda
-ssc for short) if, whenever we are given
\lambda
-many sequences having values in
X
, we can find
H\in[\omega]^{\omega}
such that each of those sequences converges along
H
.

Note that if
\lambda\leqslant\kappa
and a space
X
is
\kappa
-ssc, then
X
is
\lambda
-ssc. Moreover, the definition of “
1
-ssc” coincides with the standard definition of sequential compactness.

Sequential compactness already implies
\aleph_{0}
-simultaneous sequential compactness, a fact that can be proved via a diagonalisation argument. For sake of completeness, we provide a proof below.

Lemma 1.2
.

A topological space is sequentially compact if and only if it is
\aleph_{0}
-ssc.

Proof.

First, assume
X
is
\aleph_{0}
-ssc. Then it is
1
-ssc, and thus sequentially compact.

Now, assume
X
is sequentially compact. Let
x^{k}
be sequences with values in
X
, for
k<\omega
. By sequential compactness, there exists
H_{0}\in[\omega]^{\omega}
such that
x^{0}\upharpoonright H_{0}
converges. Next, consider the sequence
x^{1}\upharpoonright H_{1}
. Since
X
is sequentially compact, there is
H_{1}\subseteq H_{0}
in
[\omega]^{\omega}
such that
x^{1}\upharpoonright H_{1}
converges. By a previous remark, we can add finitely many elements to
H_{1}
without losing convergence, so we assume that
H_{0}
and
H_{1}
have the same least element.

Proceed by induction. Suppose we have sets
H_{i}
for
i<k
such that
H_{i+1}\subseteq H_{i}
, and that
H_{i}
and
H_{i+1}
agree on the first
i+1
elements, with each subsequence
x^{i}\upharpoonright H_{i}
converging.
Consider
x^{k}\upharpoonright H_{k-1}
. Since
X
is sequentially compact, there exists
H_{k}\subseteq H_{k-1}
such that
x^{k}\upharpoonright H_{k}
converges. We may assume that
H_{k-1}
and
H_{k}
agree on their first
k
elements.

Let
H:=\bigcap_{k<\omega}H_{k}
. This set is infinite because for all
k<\omega
the first
k
elements of
H_{k}
belong to
H
. Since
H\subseteq H_{k}
for all
k<\omega
, every sequence
x^{k}\upharpoonright H
converges, as required.
∎

This work is then structured as follows. In Section
2
, we generalise a result by Andreas Blass from
[
Bla10
]
, showing the first of our main results:

Theorem
2.7
.

If
\lambda<\mathfrak{s}
, any sequentially compact space
X
with
w(X)<\mathfrak{s}
is
\lambda
-ssc.

Here,
\mathfrak{s}
denotes the splitting number.

In Section
3
, we consider the case of Hausdorff spaces. We define a cardinal invariant, called
countable Hausdorff weight
(
\mathrm{cHw}
), which we use in place of the weight to improve on Theorem
2.7
:

Theorem
3.5
.

If
\lambda<\mathfrak{s}
, any sequentially compact Hausdorff space
X
with
\mathrm{cHw}(X)<\mathfrak{s}
is
\lambda
-ssc.

In Section
4
, we address the problem of sequences in different topological spaces. There, both the general results from Section
2
and the specific results for Hausdorff spaces from Section
3
are generalised to this new context, showing that they essentially continue to hold, provided that all sequences come from spaces with comparable properties.

Finally, in Section
5
we prove the following result, which does not rely on any restriction on the cardinal invariants of the space:

Theorem
5.2
.

If
\lambda<\mathfrak{h}
, any sequentially compact space
X
is
\lambda
-ssc.

Here,
\mathfrak{h}
denotes the distributivity number. At the end, we propose some further questions regarding the optimality of some of our results.

2.
Results based on the Splitting Number

Lemma
1.2
provides a lower bound for the extent of simultaneous sequential compactness exhibited by a sequentially compact space. We now turn to the question of finding an upper bound, which, unlike the lower bound, will depend on additional topological properties of the space. Indeed, in the extremely trivial case where a space only has one element, it is easy to show that it is
\lambda
-ssc for all cardinals
\lambda
.

Definition 2.1
.

We say that a topological space
X
is “nontrivial enough to distinguish two points” (in the following, we just write nontrivial) if in
X
there are two elements
a
and
b
such that the closure of
\{a\}
and the closure of
\{b\}
are disjoint.

For a nontrivial space
X
, there is indeed an upper bound for the possible values of
\lambda
for which
X
could be
\lambda
-ssc. This bound is given by the splitting number
\mathfrak{s}
.

Definition 2.2
.

A family
\mathcal{S}\subseteq[\omega]^{\omega}
is said to be a
splitting family
if for every
A\in[\omega]^{\omega}
there is some
S\in\mathcal{S}
such that both
A\cap S
and
A-S
are infinite sets. The
splitting number

\mathfrak{s}
is then defined to be the least cardinality of a splitting family.

It holds
\aleph_{1}\leqslant\mathfrak{s}\leqslant 2^{\aleph_{0}}
, and it is consistent relative to
ZFC
that
\aleph_{1}<\mathfrak{s}<2^{\aleph_{0}}
. For more informations about the splitting number, we refer the interested reader to Andreas Blass’s handbook chapter
[
Bla10
]
. There, one may also find the proof of the statement “the discrete space
\{0,1\}
is not
\mathfrak{s}
-ssc”, which, as can be easily seen, can be readapted to prove also the apparently stronger Lemma
2.3
.

Lemma 2.3
.

If
X
is a nontrivial topological space,
X
is not
\mathfrak{s}
-ssc.

Proof.

Let
a
and
b
be in
X
such that the closures of
\{a\}
and
\{b\}
are disjoint.

Let
\mathcal{S}=\langle S_{\alpha}:\alpha<\mathfrak{s}\rangle
be a splitting family of size
\mathfrak{s}
. For
\alpha<\mathfrak{s}
, let
x^{\alpha}
be

x^{\alpha}_{n}:=\begin{cases}a\ \text{if}\ n\in S_{\alpha};\\
b\ \text{if}\ n\not\in S_{\alpha}.\end{cases}

Since
\mathcal{S}
is a splitting family, for every
H\in[\omega]^{\omega}
there is
\alpha<\mathfrak{s}
such that
H\cap S_{\alpha}
and
H-S_{\alpha}
are both infinite. Therefore, the sequence
x^{\alpha}\upharpoonright H
takes both values
a
and
b
infinitely often, hence it cannot converge, because it is frequently outside a neighbourhood of
a
(the complementary of the closure of
\{b\}
) and outside a neighbourhood of
b
(the complementary of the closure of
\{a\}
).

Hence there is a family
\langle x^{\alpha}:\alpha<\mathfrak{s}\rangle
of size
\mathfrak{s}
of sequences with values in
X
with no
H\in[\omega]^{\omega}
along which all sequences converge, so
X
is not
\mathfrak{s}
-ssc, as desired.∎

Therefore, in the case of nontrivial spaces, the best result we can aim for is them to be
\lambda
-ssc for
\lambda<\mathfrak{s}
. As proved in
[
Bla10
]
, for the discrete space
\{0,1\}
this holds. We show now the proof of this fact using our notations and definitions.

Lemma 2.4
.

If
\lambda<\mathfrak{s}
, the discrete space
\{0,1\}
is
\lambda
-ssc.

Proof.

Let
\lambda<\mathfrak{s}
and let
x^{\alpha}
be a sequence with values in
\{0,1\}
, for
\alpha<\lambda
.

For ordinals
\alpha<\lambda
, let
A_{\alpha}:=\{n\in\omega:x^{\alpha}_{n}=1\}
and
B_{\alpha}:=\{n\in\omega:x^{\alpha}_{n}=0\}
. If
A_{\alpha}
is infinite, let
S_{\alpha}:=A_{\alpha}
; otherwise let
S_{\alpha}:=B_{\alpha}
. Now consider the family
\mathcal{F}:=\{S_{\alpha}:\alpha<\lambda\}\subseteq[\omega]^{\omega}
. Since
\lambda<\mathfrak{s}
,
\mathcal{F}
cannot be a splitting family, hence there is some
H\in[\omega]^{\omega}
such that, for all
\alpha<\lambda
, either
H\cap S_{\alpha}
or
H-S_{\alpha}
is finite.

This means that, for all
\alpha<\lambda
, either
H\cap A_{\alpha}
or
H\cap B_{\alpha}
is finite. If
H\cap A_{\alpha}
is finite, then
x^{\alpha}\upharpoonright H
converges to
0
; if
H\cap B_{\alpha}
is finite,
x^{\alpha}\upharpoonright H
converges to
1
.
∎

This proof is using some particular properties of the space
\{0,1\}
, hence it can not be as easily generalised to all nontrivial sequentially compact spaces.

In the already cited chapter
[
Bla10
]
, Blass proves also the following Lemma
2.5
.

Lemma 2.5
.

If
\lambda<\mathfrak{s}
, the compact unit interval
[0,1]\subseteq\mathbb{R}
is
\lambda
-ssc.

We are not going to prove Lemma
2.5
because it will be a corollary to Theorem
2.7
. Though, we can notice that Blass’s proof is indeed showing something stronger than Lemma
2.5
: the same exact argument works for any compact subset of any separable metric space.

Theorem
2.7
is even stronger than this, since it implies that any compact subset of any metric space is
\lambda
-ssc for
\lambda<\mathfrak{s}
. Let us recall an important topological notion.

Definition 2.6
.

Given a topological space
X
, its weight is defined as

w(X):=\aleph_{0}+\min\{\left|\mathcal{B}\right|:\mathcal{B}\ \text{is a basis for the topology on}\ X\}.

Theorem 2.7
.

If
\lambda<\mathfrak{s}
, any sequentially compact space
X
with
w(X)<\mathfrak{s}
is
\lambda
-ssc.

Proof.

Let
\lambda<\mathfrak{s}
and consider
\lambda
-many sequences
x^{\alpha}
for
\alpha<\lambda
with values in
X
.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a basis for the topology on
X
with
\left|\mathcal{B}\right|<\mathfrak{s}
. Let
\kappa:=\left|\mathcal{B}\right|
. Consider an enumeration
\langle B^{\beta}:\beta<\kappa\rangle
of
\mathcal{B}
. For all
\alpha<\lambda
and
\beta<\kappa
let us define

y^{\alpha,\beta}_{n}:=\begin{cases}1&\text{if}\ x^{\alpha}_{n}\in B^{\beta};\\
0&\text{if}\ x^{\alpha}_{n}\not\in B^{\beta}.\end{cases}

The set
\{y^{\alpha,\beta}:\alpha<\lambda,\beta<\kappa\}
is a set of
\lambda\kappa
-many sequences with values in
\{0,1\}
, so since
\lambda\kappa<\mathfrak{s}
there is by Lemma
2.4
some
H\in[\omega]^{\omega}
such that
y^{\alpha,\beta}\upharpoonright H
converges for all
\alpha<\lambda
and all
\beta<\kappa
. We want to prove that
x^{\alpha}\upharpoonright H
converges, for all
\alpha<\lambda
.

Fix
\alpha<\lambda
. Since
X
is sequentially compact, there is
H^{\prime}\in[\omega]^{\omega}
such that
H^{\prime}\subseteq H
and the sequence
x^{\alpha}\upharpoonright H^{\prime}
converges to some
x^{\alpha}_{\infty}
. For all
\beta<\kappa
, if
x^{\alpha}_{\infty}\in B^{\beta}
, then the sequence
x^{\alpha}\upharpoonright H^{\prime}
from some point onwards is always inside
B^{\beta}
, hence
y^{\alpha,\beta}\upharpoonright H^{\prime}
converges to
1
, so since we know that
y^{\alpha,\beta}\upharpoonright H
converges, and
H^{\prime}\subseteq H
, the sequence
y^{\alpha,\beta}\upharpoonright H
also converges to
1
. This implies that
X^{\alpha}\upharpoonright H
lies definitively inside every open neighbourhood of
x^{\alpha}_{\infty}
, and thus it converges to
x^{\alpha}_{\infty}
, as desired.
∎

A proof similar to the one of Theorem
2.7
implies the same result of
\lambda
-ssc for
\lambda<\mathfrak{s}
for compact spaces of weight less than
\mathfrak{s}
. Recall that, in general, there is no direct correlation between topological compactness and sequential compactness.

Theorem 2.8
.

Every compact space
X
with
w(X)<\mathfrak{s}
is
\lambda
-ssc for all
\lambda<\mathfrak{s}
.

Proof.

Let
\lambda
,
x^{\alpha}
,
\mathcal{B}
,
\kappa
,
y^{\alpha,\beta}
and
H
be as in the proof of Theorem
2.7
. Our goal is to prove, as in the proof of Theorem
2.7
, that
x^{\alpha}\upharpoonright H
converges, for all
\alpha<\lambda
.

Assume by contradiction that
x^{\alpha}\upharpoonright H
does not converge, for some
\alpha<\lambda
. This means that for all
x\in X
there is a
\beta(x)<\kappa
such that
x\in B^{\beta(x)}
but the sequence
x^{\alpha}\upharpoonright H
is infinitely many times outside of
B^{\beta(x)}
, so the sequence
y^{\alpha,\beta(x)}\upharpoonright H
is infinitely many times
0
, hence since it converges, it has to converge to
0
.

But now
\bigcup_{x\in X}B^{\beta(x)}
is an open cover of
X
, hence we can extract a finite subcover given by
X=B^{\beta(x_{1})}\cup\ldots\cup B^{\beta(x_{k})}
for some
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\}\subseteq X
. Since
y^{\alpha,\beta(x)}\upharpoonright H
converges to
0
for all
x\in X
, the sequence
x^{\alpha}\upharpoonright H
is definitively outside each
B^{\beta(x)}
, so it is definitively outside
B^{\beta(x_{1})}\cup\ldots\cup B^{\beta(x_{k})}
, that is
X
, a contradiction.
∎

Since
1<\mathfrak{s}
, as a corollary of Theorem
2.8
we get back the following Corollary
2.9
, which is a well-known result that can be found for example in Eric Van Douwen’s handbook chapter
[
Van84
]
. Notice how Theorem
2.8
is a direct generalisation of it.

Corollary 2.9
.

Every compact space
X
with
w(X)<\mathfrak{s}
is sequentially compact.

Theorem
2.7
generalises several classical results in analysis, thus it has the potential to be employed in practical applications. For instance, the following Corollary
2.10
extends the Banach–Bourbaki–Alaoglu theorem, showing that simultaneous weak-
\star
sequential compactness holds for small families of functionals.

Corollary 2.10
.

Let
\mathbb{E}
be a separable normed vector space. If
\lambda<\mathfrak{s}
, then the unit ball of its topological dual
\mathbb{E}^{\prime}
is
\lambda
-simultaneously weakly-
\star
sequentially compact.

3.
Hausdorff Spaces

Now we turn our attention to Hausdorff spaces, in order to show an improvement to Theorem
2.7
using a different kind of cardinal invariant of the topological space.

If
X
is a topological space and
Y\subseteq X
is a subset, we denote by
\mathcal{L}(Y)
the set of limit points of
Y
in the topology of
X
, i.e., the set of all points
x_{\infty}\in X
such that there exists a sequence in
Y
that converges to
x_{\infty}
. This may differ from the sequential closure of
Y
,
\mathrm{SeqCl}(Y)
, by which we mean the smallest sequentially closed superset of
Y
, because
\mathcal{L}(Y)
might not be sequentially closed.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
X
be a topological space,
Y\subseteq X
. A
sequentially separating limit cover
for
Y
is a family
\mathcal{C}_{Y}
of open subsets of
\mathcal{L}(Y)
such that for each
p\in\mathcal{L}(Y)
and each
q\in X
with
q\neq p
there is
U\in\mathcal{C}_{Y}
with
p\in U
and
q\not\in\mathrm{SeqCl}(U)
.

Definition 3.2
.

The
countable Hausdorff weight
of an Hausdorff space
X
, denoted by
\mathrm{cHw}(X)
, is the least infinite cardinal
\kappa
such that each countable subset
Y\subseteq X
admits a sequentially separating limit cover
\mathcal{C}_{Y}
such that
|\mathcal{C}_{Y}|\leqslant\kappa
.

If
X
is not an Hausdorff space, there may not exist sequentially separating limit covers for some countable
Y\subseteq X
, and thus
\text{cHw}(X)
may not be defined. Instead, for
X
an Hausdorff space, its countable Hausdorff weight is always defined and is at most
w(X)
, as the following Lemma
3.3
shows.

Lemma 3.3
.

For each Hausdorff space
X
we have
\mathrm{cHw}(X)\leqslant w(X)
.

Proof.

If
\mathcal{B}
is a basis for
X
with
|\mathcal{B}|=w(X)
and
Y\subseteq X
is countable, the family

\{U\cap\mathcal{L}(Y):U\in\mathcal{B}\}

is a sequentially separating limit cover for
Y
of cardinality at most
w(X)
.
∎

One interesting remark, shown to us by Angelo Bella, is that while the weight of a topological space is not bounded by the cardinality of the space (not even when the space is Hausdorff), we can prove an inequality for the countable Hausdorff weight:

Lemma 3.4
.

For
X
a topological Hausdorff space, it holds
\mathrm{cHw}(X)\leqslant|X|
.

Proof.

Let
Y\subseteq X
be countable. In order to prove the lemma, it is enough to show that
Y
has a sequentially separating limit cover of size at most
|X|
. For each
p\neq q
in
X
, let us fix, by the Hausdorff property, open subsets
U_{p}^{q}\subseteq X
and
U_{q}^{p}\subseteq X
such that
p\in U_{p}^{q}
,
q\in U_{q}^{p}
and
U_{p}^{q}\cap U_{q}^{p}=\emptyset
. Let now

\mathcal{C}:=\{U_{p}^{q}\cap\mathcal{L}(Y):p\in\mathcal{L}(Y),q\in X,p\neq q\}.

Since
\mathrm{SeqCl}(U_{p}^{q}\cap\mathcal{L}(Y))\subseteq\mathcal{L}(Y)-U_{q}^{p}
, this is a sequentially separating limit cover for
Y
. Moreover, it holds
|\mathcal{C}|\leqslant|X|^{2}=|X|
, as desired.
∎

We now state our improvement to Theorem
2.7
for Hausdorff spaces.

Theorem 3.5
.

If
\lambda<\mathfrak{s}
, any sequentially compact Hausdorff space
X
with
\mathrm{cHw}(X)<\mathfrak{s}
is
\lambda
-ssc.

Proof.

For each
\alpha<\lambda
, let
x^{\alpha}=\langle x^{\alpha}_{n}:n<\omega\rangle
be a sequence of values in
X
, and let
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}
be a sequentially separating limit cover for the countable set
\{x^{\alpha}_{n}:n<\omega\}
such that
|\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}|\leqslant\mathrm{cHw}(X)
. For each
\alpha<\lambda
and
U\in\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}
, we define a sequence
s^{\alpha,U}
with values in
\{0,1\}
as follows:

s^{\alpha,U}_{n}=\begin{cases}1&\mathrm{if}\ x^{\alpha}_{n}\in U;\\
0&\mathrm{if}\ x^{\alpha}_{n}\notin U.\end{cases}

Notice that, since
\lambda<\mathfrak{s}
and
|\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}|\leqslant\mathrm{cHw}(X)<\mathfrak{s}
for each
\alpha<\lambda
, the set of pairs
\langle\alpha,U\rangle
with
\alpha<\lambda
and
U\in\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}
has size less than
\mathfrak{s}
. Therefore, by Lemma
2.4
, there is
H\in[\omega]^{\omega}
such that, for each
\alpha<\lambda
and
U\in\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}
, the sequence
s^{\alpha,U}\upharpoonright H
converges. We now aim to prove that the sequence
x^{\alpha}\upharpoonright H
converges for each
\alpha<\lambda
.

Fix
\alpha<\lambda
. Since
X
is sequentially compact, there are subsets
B,B^{\prime}\subseteq H
such that
x^{\alpha}\upharpoonright B
and
x^{\alpha}\upharpoonright B^{\prime}
both converge, to some points
x^{\alpha}_{B}
and
x^{\alpha}_{B^{\prime}}
, respectively. Suppose by contradiction that
x^{\alpha}_{B}\neq x^{\alpha}_{B^{\prime}}
. Then, by definition of sequentially separating limit cover, there exists
V\in\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}
with
x^{\alpha}_{B}\in V
and
x^{\alpha}_{B^{\prime}}\notin\mathrm{SeqCl}(V)
.

We know that
s^{\alpha,V}\upharpoonright H
converges, and since
x^{\alpha}_{B}\in V
we know that
s^{\alpha,V}\upharpoonright H
must converge to 1. Therefore,
s^{\alpha,V}\upharpoonright B^{\prime}
also converges to 1, so
x^{\alpha}_{n}\in V
for infinitely many
n\in B^{\prime}
, which contradicts
x^{\alpha}_{B^{\prime}}\notin\mathrm{SeqCl}(V)
. This yields that
x^{\alpha}_{B}=x^{\alpha}_{B^{\prime}}
for any choice of
B
and
B^{\prime}
as above, so we can denote
x^{\alpha}_{B}
simply by
x^{\alpha}_{\infty}
.

Notice that, for each
H^{\prime}\subseteq H
, there exists
H^{\prime\prime}\subseteq H^{\prime}
such that
x^{\alpha}\upharpoonright H^{\prime\prime}
converges, and we have proven that it must converge to
x^{\alpha}_{\infty}
. Next we employ a result that is true in any topological space, and is sometimes called
Urysohn’s property
: a sequence
x
converges to a point
p
if and only if every subsequence of
x
has a further subsequence converging to
p
. This, applied to our context, yields that
x^{\alpha}\upharpoonright H
converges to
x^{\alpha}_{\infty}
, which proves the statement.
∎

We can further refine Theorem
3.5
, introducing the following:

Definition 3.6
.

We say that a Hausdorff space
X

reaches
its countable Hausdorff weight if there exists a countable subset
Y\subseteq X
such that each sequentially separating limit cover for
Y
has cardinality at least
\mathrm{cHw}(X)
.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
X
be a sequentially compact Hausdorff space with
\mathrm{cHw}(X)=\mathfrak{s}
, not reaching its countable Hausdorff weight. Then,
X
is
\lambda
-ssc for all
\lambda<\mathrm{cof}(\mathfrak{s})
.

Proof.

The proof is identical to the proof of Theorem
3.5
, except that now the set of pairs
\langle\alpha,U\rangle
with
\alpha<\lambda
and
U\in\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}
has size less than
\mathfrak{s}
simply because
\lambda<\mathrm{cof}(\mathfrak{s})
and
|\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}|<\mathrm{cHw}(X)=\mathfrak{s}
for all
\alpha<\lambda
.
∎

Notice that Lemma
3.3
guarantees that the assumption of Theorem
3.5
is indeed a relaxation of the assumption of Theorem
2.7
, for Hausdorff spaces. To prove that this is a meaningful improvement, we show an example where
\text{cHw}(X)<\mathfrak{s}\leqslant w(X)
, using some results from Section
4
.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
X^{\prime}
be a discrete topological space with
|X^{\prime}|=\kappa\geqslant\aleph_{0}
, and let
X=X^{\prime}\cup\{\Omega\}
be the Aleksandroff compactification of
X^{\prime}
. Then,
\mathrm{cHw}(X)=\aleph_{0}
and
w(X)=\kappa
. In particular, if
\kappa\geqslant\mathfrak{s}
, we have
\text{cHw}(X)<\mathfrak{s}\leqslant w(X)
.

Proof.

Since every point of
X^{\prime}
is open, it is obvious that
w(X^{\prime})=\kappa
, and Lemma
4.2
yields
w(X)=\kappa
. Since
X^{\prime}
is the disjoint union of
\kappa
-many points, Theorem
4.13
yields
\mathrm{cHw}(X)\leqslant\aleph_{0}
. Definition
3.2
implies
\mathrm{cHw}(X)\geqslant\aleph_{0}
, hence the statement.
∎

4.
Sequences in Different Spaces

In this section, we examine the –seemingly more general– case in which the sequences may belong to different topological spaces. Indeed, in Blass’s proof of Lemma
2.5
, he does not assume that all the sequences lie in the same interval
[0,1]
; rather, he only requires that each sequence belongs to some compact subset of
\mathbb{R}
. Here we show that the two situations are, in a certain regard, equivalent, hence all results for sequences in different spaces follows as corollaries of the theorems for sequences in the same space.

Definition 4.1
.

If
\lambda
is a cardinal, a family
\mathcal{F}
of topological spaces is
\lambda
-ssc if given
\lambda
-many sequences, each of which with values in one of the topological spaces in
\mathcal{F}
, there is
H\in[\omega]^{\omega}
such that each considered sequence converges along
H
.

Notice that a family
\mathcal{F}
is
\lambda
-ssc if and only if every subfamily
\mathcal{G}\subseteq\mathcal{F}
with
|\mathcal{G}|\leqslant\lambda
is
\lambda
-ssc. This allows us, in cases where the family
\mathcal{F}
is a proper class, to restrict to families that are sets. This justifies some abuses of notation throughout the rest of this section, for example the implication from Theorem
4.4
(which requires
\mathcal{F}
to be a set) to Corollary
4.5
(which deals with a proper class).

Let us recall a famous topological construction, that we will use more than once. Given a topological space
X^{\prime}
which is not compact, let
\Omega
be not in
X^{\prime}
and define

X:=X^{\prime}\cup\{\Omega\}.

Consider the topology on
X
where the open sets are the sets which are either equal to
A
for some
A\subseteq X^{\prime}
open, or to
X-K
for some
K\subseteq X^{\prime}
compact. Then the space
X
, endowed with this topology, is compact and satisfies the following:

Lemma 4.2
.

w(X)=w(X^{\prime})
.

This space
X
is called the
Aleksandroff compactification
of
X^{\prime}
. For further information about this standard topological construction, and for proofs of the results just mentioned, we refer the reader to Ryszard Engelking’s seminal book on general topology,
[
Eng89
]
. The next Lemma
4.3
is of fundamental importance for the presentation of our results.

We note that here, and later on as well, we speak of the disjoint union of certain spaces. Formally, if
\mathcal{F}=\langle Y_{\alpha}:\alpha<\mu\rangle
is a sequence (or even merely a family, which we may well-order thanks to the axiom of choice) of spaces, not necessarily distinct, the disjoint union of all spaces in
\mathcal{F}
is defined as the set

\textstyle\bigcup_{\alpha<\mu}(\{\alpha\}\times Y_{\alpha}),

where the topology is the one generated by taking, for each
\alpha<\mu
, the topology on
\{\alpha\}\times Y_{\alpha}
that makes it homeomorphic to
Y_{\alpha}
via the natural bijection
\langle\alpha,y\rangle\mapsto y
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be an infinite family of sequentially compact topological spaces, let
X^{\prime}
be the disjoint union of all spaces in
\mathcal{F}
, and let
X=X^{\prime}\cup\{\Omega\}
be the Aleksandroff compactification of
X^{\prime}
. Then,
X
is sequentially compact.

Proof.

Order
\mathcal{F}
as
\langle Y_{\alpha}:\alpha<\mu\rangle
, with the axiom of choice. Let
x
be a sequence with values in
X
. We need to show that there is
H\in[\omega]^{\omega}
such that
x\upharpoonright H
converges.

For each
\alpha<\mu
, let
H_{\alpha}:=\{n<\omega:x_{n}\in\{\alpha\}\times Y_{\alpha}\}
. In addition to this, let also
H_{\Omega}:=\{n<\omega:x_{n}=\Omega\}
. Suppose there is some
\alpha_{0}<\mu
such that
H_{\alpha_{0}}
is infinite. Then,
x\upharpoonright H_{\alpha_{0}}
is a sequence with values in
\{\alpha_{0}\}\times Y_{\alpha_{0}}
, that is isomorphic to
Y_{\alpha_{0}}
hence sequentially compact. Thus,
x\upharpoonright H_{\alpha_{0}}
admits a converging subsequence, so
x
as well admits a converging subsequence. Similarly, if
H_{\Omega}
is infinite, then
x
has a subsequence constantly equal to
\Omega
, hence a converging one.

To finish the proof, assume that
H_{\alpha}
is finite for all
\alpha<\mu
, and also
H_{\Omega}
is finite. Let us prove that
x
converges to
\Omega
. Fix any compact subset
K\subseteq X^{\prime}
, and let

\mathcal{U}:=\{K\cap(\{\alpha\}\times Y_{\alpha}):\alpha<\mu\}.

Notice that
\mathcal{U}
is an open cover of the compact set
K
, and that the elements of
\mathcal{U}
are pairwise disjoint. Therefore,
\mathcal{U}
is finite, thus the set

\mathcal{F}_{K}:=\{\alpha<\mu:K\cap(\{\alpha\}\times Y_{\alpha})\neq\emptyset\}

is finite, and
H_{K}:=\bigcup_{\alpha\in\mathcal{F}_{K}}H_{\alpha}
is finite. Therefore, for each
n>\max H_{K}
, we have
x_{n}\in X-K
. Since the choice of
K
was arbitrary, and all open neighbourhoods of
\Omega
are of the form
X-K
for
K
compact, this proves that
x
converges to
\Omega
.
∎

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be an infinite family of sequentially compact topological spaces,
X^{\prime}
the disjoint union of all spaces in
\mathcal{F}
, and
X=X^{\prime}\cup\{\Omega\}
the Aleksandroff compactification of
X^{\prime}
. Suppose
X
is
\lambda
-ssc. Then, the family
\mathcal{F}
is
\lambda
-ssc.

Proof.

For
\alpha<\lambda
, let
X^{\alpha}\in\mathcal{F}
and let
x^{\alpha}
be a sequence with values in
X^{\alpha}
. Consider the sequences
y^{\alpha}
for
\alpha<\lambda
, defined simply as
y^{\alpha}_{n}:=\langle\alpha,x^{\alpha}_{n}\rangle\in X
. These are
\lambda
-many sequences in
X
, hence there is
H\in[\omega]^{\omega}
such that every
y^{\alpha}\upharpoonright H
converges. We are going to prove that
x^{\alpha}\upharpoonright H
converges, for
\alpha<\lambda
.

The sequence
y^{\alpha}\upharpoonright H
never takes value
\Omega
, hence if it converges in
X
it means it already converged in the topology on
X^{\prime}
. Since
y^{\alpha}
takes values in
\{\alpha\}\times X^{\alpha}
, the sequence
y^{\alpha}\upharpoonright H
converges there, hence the sequence
x^{\alpha}\upharpoonright H
converges in
X^{\alpha}
.
∎

As corollaries to Lemma
4.3
and Theorem
4.4
, we get some simple generalisations of Lemmata
1.2
and
2.3
.

Corollary 4.5
.

The family of all sequentially compact spaces is
\aleph_{0}
-ssc.

Corollary 4.6
.

The family of all sequentially compact spaces is not
\mathfrak{s}
-ssc.

As a generalisation for Theorem
2.7
, one could expect to be possible to prove “if
\lambda<\mathfrak{s}
, the family of all sequentially compact spaces of weight less than
\mathfrak{s}
is
\lambda
-ssc”.

Anyway, this can not be inferred from Theorems
2.7
and
4.4
, and –at the time of writing this sentence– the authors still do not know if it is provable in
ZFC
alone. The corollary we can get straight away from Theorems
2.7
and
4.4
is Lemma
4.7
.

Lemma 4.7
.

Fix some cardinal
\kappa<\mathfrak{s}
. Then, if
\lambda<\mathfrak{s}
, the family of all sequentially compact topological spaces having weight not greater than
\kappa
is
\lambda
-ssc.

Proof.

For
\alpha<\lambda
, let
X^{\alpha}
be a sequentially compact set with
w(X^{\alpha})<\kappa
, and let
x^{\alpha}
be a sequence with values in
X^{\alpha}
. Let
\mathcal{F}:=\{X^{\alpha}:\alpha<\lambda\}
, and let also
X
be the Aleksandroff compactification of the disjoint union of all spaces in
\mathcal{F}
.

Lemmata
4.2
and
4.3
, together with Theorem
2.7
, yield that

is
\lambda
-ssc, which by Theorem
4.4
implies that the family

is
\lambda
-ssc.
∎

The stronger desired result can be obtained by assuming the regularity of
\mathfrak{s}
.

Lemma 4.8
.

Assume that
\mathfrak{s}
is regular. If
\lambda<\mathfrak{s}
, then the family of all sequentially compact topological spaces having weight strictly less than
\mathfrak{s}
is
\lambda
-ssc.

Proof.

For
\alpha<\lambda
, let

be a sequentially compact space with

. Then

is a set of cardinals below

with size less than

.

Given a topological space
X
, its
sequential refinement
, which we denote by
\mathrm{seq}(X)
, is the topological space that has the same underlying set as
X
and whose closed sets are precisely the sequentially closed sets of
X
. It is straightforward to verify that a sequence in
X
converges to a certain value with respect to the topology of
X
if and only if it converges to the same value in the topology of
\mathrm{seq}(X)
. This entails that a space
X
is sequentially compact (or, more generally,
\lambda
-ssc) if and only if
\mathrm{seq}(X)
is sequentially compact (respectively,
\lambda
-ssc).

Furthermore, since the topology of
\mathrm{seq}(X)
is a refinement of the topology of
X
, it follows that if
X
is Hausdorff, then
\mathrm{seq}(X)
is also Hausdorff. The fact that
\mathrm{seq}(X)
refines the topology of
X
also yields:

Lemma 4.9
.

If
X
is a sequentially compact Hausdorff space,

\mathrm{cHw}(\mathrm{seq}(X))\leqslant\mathrm{cHw}(X).

We now introduce a notion similar to the Aleksandroff compactification.

Definition 4.10
.

Let
X^{\prime}
be a topological space that is not sequentially compact, and let
\Omega\notin X^{\prime}
. Define
X:=X^{\prime}\cup\{\Omega\}
. Consider the topology on
X
where the open sets are the sets which are either equal to
A
for some
A\subseteq\mathrm{seq}(X^{\prime})
open, or to
X-K
for some
K\subseteq X^{\prime}
sequentially compact. We say that the space
X
, endowed with this topology, is the
sequential Aleksandroff compactification
of
X^{\prime}
.

Lemma 4.11
.

Let
X^{\prime}
be a topological space that is not sequentially compact. Then, the sequential Aleksandroff compactification
X
of
X^{\prime}
is sequentially compact.

Proof.

Let
x
be a sequence with values in
X
, and assume it has no subsequence with values in
X^{\prime}
that converges in
X^{\prime}
.
Let
K\subseteq X^{\prime}
be sequentially compact. If there exists
H\in[\omega]^{\omega}
such that
x\upharpoonright H
has values in
K
, then
x\upharpoonright H
admits a converging subsequence, which contradicts our assumption on
x
. Therefore,
x
lies definitively in
X-K
. Since this holds for each such
K
, we have that
x
lies definitively in each open neighbourhood of
\Omega
in
X
, and thus it converges to
\Omega
.
∎

Thanks to Lemma
4.11
, we can prove the exact analogue of Theorem
4.4
for the setting of the sequential Aleksandroff compactification:

Theorem 4.12
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be an infinite family of sequentially compact spaces,
X^{\prime}
the disjoint union of all spaces in
\mathcal{F}
, and
X=X^{\prime}\cup\{\Omega\}
the sequential Aleksandroff compactification of
X^{\prime}
. Suppose that
X
is
\lambda
-ssc. Then, the family
\mathcal{F}
is
\lambda
-ssc.

Proof.

The proof is exactly the same as for Theorem
4.4
. Notice, in particular, that

\textstyle\mathrm{seq}(\bigcup_{\alpha<\mu}\{\alpha\}\times Y_{\alpha})=\bigcup_{\alpha<\mu}\{\alpha\}\times\mathrm{seq}(Y_{\alpha}).\qed

Next, we want to apply Theorem
4.12
to the setting of Hausdorff spaces with bounded countable Hausdorff weight, to show some variations of Lemmata
4.7
and
4.8
, that will be our Lemmata
4.14
and
4.15
. In order to do so, we have to prove a bound for the countable Hausdorff weight of the sequential Aleksandroff compactification of a disjoint union of sequential refinement of Hausdorff spaces. Notice that, if we simply were to use the Aleksandroff compactification, we would not have any guarantee that the compactification would have been Hausdorff.

Theorem 4.13
.

Let
\mathcal{F}=\{Y_{\alpha}:\alpha<\mu\}
be an infinite family of sequentially compact Hausdorff spaces,
X^{\prime}
the disjoint union of all spaces in
\mathcal{F}
, and
X=X^{\prime}\cup\{\Omega\}
the sequential Aleksandroff compactification of
X^{\prime}
. Then,
X
is a sequentially compact Hausdorff space, and

\mathrm{cHw}(X)\leqslant\textstyle\sup_{\alpha<\mu}\mathrm{cHw}(Y_{\alpha}).

Proof.

First, we note that
X
is sequentially compact by Lemma
4.11
.
Then, we shall prove that
X
is Hausdorff. Let
p,q\in X
. If
\Omega\notin\{p,q\}
, it is easy to show they get separated: if they belong to the same
\{\alpha\}\times Y_{\alpha}
, this follows from the Hausdorff property of
\{\alpha\}\times Y_{\alpha}
; if they do not, hence
p\in\{\alpha\}\times Y_{\alpha}
and
q\in\{\beta\}\times Y_{\beta}
, then
\{\alpha\}\times Y_{\alpha}
and
\{\beta\}\times Y_{\beta}
are disjoint open neighbourhoods of
p
and
q
. Otherwise, suppose
p\in\{\alpha\}\times Y_{\alpha}
for some
\alpha<\mu
and
q=\Omega
. Since
\mathrm{seq}(Y_{\alpha})
is sequentially compact, we have that
X-\{\alpha\}\times Y_{\alpha}
is an open neighbourhood of
\Omega
in
X
, that is disjoint from
\{\alpha\}\times Y_{\alpha}
, which is an open neighbourhood of
p
in
X
.

Next, we prove that
\mathrm{cHw}(X)\leqslant\sup_{\alpha<\mu}\mathrm{cHw}(Y_{\alpha})
. Let
Z\subseteq X
be countable, and for each
\alpha<\mu
we let
Z_{\alpha}\subseteq Y_{\alpha}
be such that
\{\alpha\}\times Z_{\alpha}=Z\cap(\{\alpha\}\times Y_{\alpha})
. For each
\alpha<\mu
, we now define
\mathcal{C}_{Z_{\alpha}}
as:

•

the empty set, if
Z_{\alpha}=\emptyset
;

•

a sequentially separating limit cover for
Z_{\alpha}\subseteq\mathrm{seq}(Y_{\alpha})
, if
Z_{\alpha}\neq\emptyset
.

In addition, we are now in the position to define

\mathcal{C}_{Z}^{0}:=\{\mathcal{L}(Z)-(\{\alpha\}\times Y_{\alpha}):\alpha<\mu\};

\mathcal{C}_{Z}:=\mathcal{C}_{Z}^{0}\cup\textstyle\bigcup_{\alpha<\mu}\big\{\{\alpha\}\times V:V\in\mathcal{C}_{Z_{\alpha}}\big\}.

It is clear that
|\mathcal{C}_{Z}|\leqslant|\mathcal{C}_{Z}^{0}|+\sup_{\alpha<\mu}\mathrm{cHw}(\mathrm{seq}(Y_{\alpha}))
. Moreover, since
Z
is countable,
\mathcal{L}(Z)-(\{\alpha\}\times Y_{\alpha})
is equal to
L(Z)
for each
\alpha<\mu
except countably many, hence
|\mathcal{C}_{Z}^{0}|\leqslant\aleph_{0}
. This proves
|\mathcal{C}_{Z}|\leqslant\aleph_{0}+\sup_{\alpha<\mu}\mathrm{cHw}(\mathrm{seq}(Y_{\alpha}))=\sup_{\alpha<\mu}\mathrm{cHw}(\mathrm{seq}(Y_{\alpha}))
.

Given Lemma
4.9
, to prove the statement we only need to prove that
\mathcal{C}_{Z}
is a sequentially separating limit cover for
Z\subseteq X
. Let
p\in\mathcal{L}(Z)
and let
q\in X-\{p\}
. We need to show there exists
U\in\mathcal{C}_{Z}
with
p\in U
and
q\notin\mathrm{SeqCl}(U)
. If there exists
\alpha<\mu
such that
q\in\{\alpha\}\times Y_{\alpha}
and
p\notin\{\alpha\}\times Y_{\alpha}
, then we can choose
U=X-\{\alpha\}\times Y_{\alpha}
. If instead we have
q=\Omega
, then we fix
\alpha
such that
p\in\{\alpha\}\times Y_{\alpha}
, and we can choose any
U=\{\alpha\}\times V
such that
V\in\mathcal{C}_{Z_{\alpha}}
and
p\in U
. Finally, we have the case where there exists
\alpha<\mu
with
p,q\in\{\alpha\}\times Y_{\alpha}
, and in that case we use the fact that
\mathcal{C}_{Z_{\alpha}}
is a sequentially separating limit cover for
Z_{\alpha}\subseteq\mathrm{seq}(Y_{\alpha})
to choose a
U=\{\alpha\}\times V
with
V\in\mathcal{C}_{Z_{\alpha}}
,
p\in U
and
q\notin\mathrm{SeqCl}(U)
.
∎

Lemma 4.14
.

Fix some cardinal
\kappa<\mathfrak{s}
. Then, if

, the family of all sequentially compact Hausdorff spaces

For

, let

be a sequence with values in

Theorem 4.16
.

Let
\mathcal{F}=\{Y_{\alpha}:\alpha<\mu\}

the disjoint union of all spaces in

\mathrm{cHw}(Y_{\alpha})<\mathrm{cHw}(X)

Theorems

5.
Results based on the Distributivity Number

The answer given by Theorem
2.7
and its analogues is not solving completely our question, since we would like to have simultaneous sequential compactness for all topological spaces, and not only for those having some bounded weight-like characteristic. We do so in this section, but we need to lower the bound from the splitting number
\mathfrak{s}
to the distributivity number
\mathfrak{h}
.

Definition 5.1
.

A set
\mathcal{A}\subseteq[\omega]^{\omega}
is said to be
open
if it is downwards closed, i.e., for all
H\in\mathcal{A}
and all
H^{\prime}\in[\omega]^{\omega}
with
H^{\prime}\subseteq H
it holds
H^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}
. Similarly, a set
\mathcal{D}\subseteq[\omega]^{\omega}
is
dense
if it meets every open set, so if for all
H\in[\omega]^{\omega}
there is some
H^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}
such that
H^{\prime}\subseteq H
. The
distributivity number

\mathfrak{h}
is then defined as the least cardinality of a family of dense open subsets of
[\omega]^{\omega}
having empty intersection.

It holds
\aleph_{1}\leqslant\mathfrak{h}\leqslant 2^{\aleph_{0}}
, and it is consistent relative to
ZFC
that
\aleph_{1}<\mathfrak{h}<2^{\aleph_{0}}
. For more informations about the distributivity number, we refer again to
[
Bla10
]
.

Theorem 5.2
.

If
\lambda<\mathfrak{h}
, any sequentially compact space
X
is
\lambda
-ssc.

Proof.

Consider sequences
x^{\alpha}
with values in
X
, for
\alpha<\lambda
, and define

\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}:=\{H\in[\omega]^{\omega}:\ x^{\alpha}\upharpoonright H\ \text{converges}\}.

If
x^{\alpha}\upharpoonright H
converges and
H^{\prime}\in[\omega]^{\omega}
is such that
H^{\prime}\subseteq H
, then
x^{\alpha}\upharpoonright H^{\prime}
converges; and for all
H\in[\omega]^{\omega}
there is some
H^{\prime}\subseteq H
with
H^{\prime}\in[\omega]^{\omega}
and such that
x^{\alpha}\upharpoonright H^{\prime}
converges, since
X
is sequentially compact.
\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}
is dense open, so since
\lambda<\mathfrak{h}
, there is some
H\in[\omega]^{\omega}
with
H\in\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}
for all
\alpha<\lambda
, hence
x^{\alpha}\upharpoonright H
converges for
\alpha<\lambda
.
∎

As a corollary of Theorems
5.2
and
4.4
, we also get the following:

Corollary 5.3
.

If
\lambda<\mathfrak{h}
, the family of all sequentially compact spaces is
\lambda
-ssc.

From Theorem
5.2
and Lemma
2.3
, it follows that
\mathfrak{h}\leqslant\mathfrak{s}
, a known inequality (see
[
Bla10
]
). This also gives a complete answer to the problem when assuming
\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{s}
.

Corollary 5.4
.

If
\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{s}
, a nontrivial sequentially compact space is
\lambda
-ssc iff
\lambda<\mathfrak{s}
.

Thanks to Theorem
5.2
and Lemma
2.3
we can also see, as shown in the following Lemma
5.5
, that
\mathfrak{h}\leqslant\mathrm{cof}(\mathfrak{s})
, which is another well-known inequality, a strengthening of the previously mentioned one (and indeed, a proper strengthening, as shown by Alan Dow and Saharon Shelah in
[
DS18
]
).

Lemma 5.5
.

It holds
\mathfrak{h}\leqslant\mathrm{cof}(\mathfrak{s})
.

Proof.

For
\alpha<\mathfrak{s}
, let
x^{\alpha}
be a sequence with values in
\{0,1\}
such that there is no
H\in[\omega]^{\omega}
along which all sequences
x^{\alpha}
converge. These sequences
x^{\alpha}
exist thanks to Lemma
2.3
. Now let
\lambda_{\beta}
for
\beta<\mathrm{cof}(\mathfrak{s})
be cardinals with
\sup\{\lambda_{\beta}:\beta<\mathrm{cof}(\mathfrak{s})\}=\mathfrak{s}
.

Thanks to Lemma
2.4
, we know that for
\beta<\mathrm{cof}(\mathfrak{s})
the sequences
x^{\alpha}
for
\alpha<\lambda_{\beta}
converge along a common set. Let
\mathcal{D}_{\beta}
be the set of all
H\in[\omega]^{\omega}
such that
x^{\alpha}\upharpoonright H
converges for all
\alpha<\lambda_{\beta}
: this is a dense open subset of
[\omega]^{\omega}
. Now, if
\mathrm{cof}(\mathfrak{s})<\mathfrak{h}
, then the intersection of all
\mathcal{D}_{\beta}
for
\beta<\mathrm{cof}(\mathfrak{s})
would be not empty (containing
H
), hence the sequences
x^{\alpha}
for all
\alpha<\mathfrak{s}
converge along a common
H
, a contradiction.
∎

Our next goal is to prove that there is a sequentially compact space which is not
\mathfrak{h}
-ssc. This trivially follows from
\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{s}
, but we are looking for a
ZFC
proof.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
\mathcal{D}\subseteq[\omega]^{\omega}
be dense open. There is a sequentially compact space
X
and a sequence
x
with values in
X
such that
\mathcal{D}=\{H\in[\omega]^{\omega}:x\upharpoonright H\ \text{converges}\}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be the family of all open subsets of
\mathcal{D}
, and let
X:=\omega\cup\mathcal{L}
.

We now endow
X^{\prime}
with a topology, defined as follows. A subset
C\subseteq X^{\prime}
is closed if and only if for all
H\in[\omega]^{\omega}
and
\ell\in\mathcal{L}
with
H\in\ell
and
H\subseteq C
, it holds
\ell\in C
.

Let us check that it is a topology. Clearly
\emptyset
and
X^{\prime}
are closed. Let
C_{\alpha}
be closed for all
\alpha<\kappa
, any
\kappa
, and prove that
C:=\bigcap_{\alpha<\kappa}C_{\alpha}
is closed. If
H\in[\omega]^{\omega}
and
\ell\in\mathcal{L}
are such that
H\in\ell
, then assume
H\subseteq C
. This implies
H\subseteq C_{\alpha}
for all
\alpha<\kappa
, so since
C_{\alpha}
is closed we get
\ell\in C_{\alpha}
for all
\alpha<\kappa
, then
\ell\in C
, as desired.

To finish, let
C_{0}
and
C_{1}
be closed and let us prove that
C:=C_{0}\cup C_{1}
is closed. Assume that
H\in[\omega]^{\omega}
,
\ell\in\mathcal{L}
,
H\in\ell
, and
H\subseteq C
. Define now
H_{0}:=H\cap C_{0}
and
H_{1}:=H\cap C_{1}
. At least one of
H_{0}
and
H_{1}
is an infinite subset of
\omega
; without loss of generality, we may assume
H_{0}\in[\omega]^{\omega}
. Then since
H\in\ell
,
H_{0}\subseteq H
is infinite and
\mathcal{D}
is open, we get
H_{0}\in\ell
. Since
C_{0}
is closed and
H_{0}\subseteq C_{0}
, we have
\ell\in C_{0}\subseteq C
.

Consider now the sequence
x
with values in
X^{\prime}
defined as
x_{n}:=n\in\omega
. Thanks to how we defined the topology on
X^{\prime}
, we get that, for every
H\in[\omega]^{\omega}
, the subsequence
x\upharpoonright H
converges to
\ell\in\mathcal{L}
if and only if
H\in\ell
. In particular, we get that

\{H\in[\omega]^{\omega}:x\upharpoonright H\ \text{converges}\}=\textstyle\bigcup_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}}\ell=\mathcal{D}.

Let now
X:=X^{\prime}\cup\{\Omega\}
be the Aleksandroff compactification of
X^{\prime}
. We want to show that
X
is sequentially compact. Let
y
be a sequence with values in
X
and let us distinguish between some possible cases. Clearly if
y
takes infinitely many times the same value, there is a subsequence of
y
that converges. Hence, by restricting to a subsequence if necessary, we assume
y
takes each value at most once, and never takes value
\Omega
. By restricting furthermore to a subsequence if necessary, we might also assume that if
n<m<\omega
are such that
y_{n}\in\omega
and
y_{m}\in\omega
, then
y_{n}<y_{m}
.

If
y
takes value in
\omega
infinitely many times, then
H:=\{n<\omega:y_{n}\in\omega\}\in[\omega]^{\omega}
. If we let
Z:=\{y_{n}\in\omega:n\in H\}
, then
y\upharpoonright H=x\upharpoonright Z
. Since
\mathcal{D}
is dense, there is
Z^{\prime}\in[\omega]^{\omega}
with
Z^{\prime}\subseteq Z
such that
x\upharpoonright Z^{\prime}
converges, and if
H^{\prime}:=\{n<\omega:y_{n}\in Z^{\prime}\}
we get that
y\upharpoonright H^{\prime}=x\upharpoonright Z^{\prime}
converges, so
y
has a convergent subsequence.

To finish the proof of the sequential compactness of
X
, assume
y
takes values in
\omega
only finitely many times. By eventually restricting to a subsequence, we may assume that
y
only takes values in
\mathcal{L}
and never takes the same value twice. But then, since the relative topology on
\mathcal{L}
is the discrete one, we know that a subset of
\mathcal{L}
is compact if and only if it is finite. Hence, if
U
is an open neighbourhood of
\Omega
, the sequence
y
is eventually contained in
U
, hence
y
converges to
\Omega
, as desired.
∎

Theorem 5.7
.

There is a sequentially compact space
X
which is not
\mathfrak{h}
-ssc.

Proof.

Let
\langle\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}:\alpha<\mathfrak{h}\rangle
be a family of dense open subsets of
[\omega]^{\omega}
that have empty intersection. For all
\alpha<\mathfrak{h}
, thanks to Lemma
5.6
we can find a sequentially compact
X^{\alpha}
and a sequence
x^{\alpha}
with values in
X^{\alpha}
and
\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}=\{H\in[\omega]^{\omega}:x^{\alpha}\upharpoonright H\ \text{converges}\}
.

Since the intersection
\bigcap_{\alpha<\mathfrak{h}}\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}
is empty, there is no
H\in[\omega]^{\omega}
along which all the sequences
x^{\alpha}
converge. Hence the family of all sequentially compact spaces is not
\mathfrak{h}
-ssc, so thanks to Theorem
4.4
there is a sequentially compact topological space
X
which is not
\mathfrak{h}
-ssc, as we wanted.
∎

After a first draft of this article was completed, we learned –through a personal communication by Michael Hrušák, whom we thank deeply– that our Theorems
5.2
and
5.7
are easily seen to be equivalent to results whose proofs can be found in Petr Simon’s article
[
Sim93
]
. Since our proofs are shorter, in another context, and use different techniques, we believe it is still worthwhile to present them here.

In the proof of Lemma
5.6
, we could take
\mathcal{L}
to be the family of all open subsets of
\mathcal{D}
which are closed under finite unions and maximal with these properties. If we do so, the sequentially compact space
X
not
\mathfrak{h}
-ssc we get from Theorem
5.7
is such that
w(X)\leqslant 2^{\aleph_{0}}
. We can now ask if it is possible to find a sequentially compact space
X
not
\mathfrak{h}
-ssc and such that
w(X)=\mathfrak{s}
. We find interesting to ask both if this is provable in
ZFC
, and if this is consistent together with the hypothesis
\mathfrak{h}<\mathfrak{s}<2^{\aleph_{0}}
.

An even more interesting question is: is it consistent with
ZFC
that the family of all sequentially compact spaces of weight less than
\mathfrak{s}
is not
\lambda
-ssc, for some
\lambda<\mathfrak{s}
? A positive answer can hold only in a model where
\mathfrak{s}
is singular (hence, where
\mathfrak{h}<\mathfrak{s}
).

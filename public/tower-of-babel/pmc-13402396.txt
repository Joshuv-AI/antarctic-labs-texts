Parameter-uniform numerical method for a coupled system of singularly perturbed turning point problems with Robin boundary conditions
Authors: Chaturya Karanam, Prashanth Maroju
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis paper investigates a system of singularly perturbed turning point problems subject to Robin-type boundary conditions, that exhibit sharp boundary and interior layers that pose significant challenges for standard numerical methods. To effectively handle these difficulties, a trigonometric cubic B-spline (TCBS) collocation approach is proposed for obtaining accurate numerical approximations. The smoothness and flexibility of the TCBS basis functions enable the method to capture the rapid variations associated with turning point behavior. A rigorous theoretical framework is developed to analyze the stability and convergence of the scheme, and it is shown that the method achieves parameter-uniform convergence of order , which corresponds to second-order accuracy up to a logarithmic factor. The performance of the method is demonstrated through a numerical example, where the computed results are compared with existing results available in the literature. In addition, the computational efficiency of the proposed method is evaluated through the reported CPU time. The numerical results confirm the reliability, accuracy, and effectiveness of the present approach.Keywords: Singularly perturbed systems, Turning point, Shishkin mesh, TCBS, Uniform convergence, Boundary layerSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

A significant class of differential equations known as singularly perturbed problems (SPPs) is distinguished by the existence of a small positive parameter that multiplies the highest-order derivative. The presence of this small parameter introduces pronounced multiscale behaviour in the solution, typically manifested through sharp boundary or interior layers, where rapid variations occur, while the solution remains relatively smooth in the remainder of the domain. Such problems arise naturally in a wide range of scientific and engineering applications, including high Reynolds number fluid flows, convection-diffusion processes, heat and mass transfer with large Péclet numbers, semiconductor device modelling, chemical kinetics, control systems, and quantum mechanics. The presence of thin layers poses significant challenges for classical numerical methods, particularly those based on uniform meshes, as they often fail to resolve steep gradients accurately and may produce parameter-dependent errors or spurious oscillations. Consequently, the development of reliable and parameter-uniform numerical techniques capable of accurately capturing these layer phenomena has become an active and important area of research in numerical analysis. Several advanced approaches have therefore been developed to address these difficulties, including fitted operator methods, layer-adapted meshes, and parameter-uniform discretization techniques designed to maintain stability and accuracy independent of the perturbation parameter. For further discussion and detailed analysis, see1–3.

Turning point problems (TPPs) form a particularly challenging subclass of singularly perturbed differential equations. These problems occur when the coefficient of the convective term vanishes at an interior point or changes sign within the computational domain, giving rise to a turning point. Around this point, the solution typically develops a pronounced interior layer that may coexist with boundary layers, resulting in sharp variations in localized regions of the domain. The presence and interaction of these layers significantly increase both the analytical and numerical complexity of the problem. Standard discretization techniques, especially those based on uniform meshes, often fail to accurately resolve these steep gradients and may produce oscillatory or inaccurate solutions unless extremely fine meshes are employed near the turning point and layer regions. Moreover, as the perturbation parameter becomes smaller, the accuracy of classical numerical methods frequently deteriorates, leading to solutions whose quality strongly depends on the perturbation parameter. When the problem involves a coupled system of differential equations, the complexity increases further because the interaction between the solution components can influence the formation and location of the layers. To address these challenges, robust parameter numerical methods have been developed that maintain stability and accuracy independent of the perturbation parameter. Such methods typically incorporate specially designed layer-adapted meshes, including piecewise-uniform Shishkin meshes4, Bakhvalov meshes5, or exponentially fitted meshes6, which effectively capture the steep gradients present in the solution. Because of their important role in modeling transport-dominated processes and fluid flow phenomena, singularly perturbed turning point problems (SPTPPs) continue to be an active and important area of research.

In recent years, significant progress has been made in the development of numerical methods for solving SPPs. Several approaches have been proposed in the literature, including finite difference methods (FDM), finite element methods, fitted operator techniques, and spline-based schemes. Early investigations mainly focused on finite difference and fitted operator methods, which were designed to maintain numerical stability in the presence of strong convection effects. With further advancements, more sophisticated discretization techniques such as finite element formulations, spline-based methods, and hybrid numerical approaches were introduced to enhance both accuracy and computational efficiency. In particular, parameter-uniform FDMs implemented on layer-adapted meshes have been widely used to approximate solutions of singularly perturbed boundary value problems (SP-BVPs). Among the available techniques, spline-based approximation methods have gained considerable attention due to their smoothness properties, local support, and ability to provide highly accurate approximations using relatively few grid points. In this context, B-spline functions have proven to be especially effective for constructing stable and efficient numerical schemes. Consequently, several researchers have developed cubic spline, exponential spline, and B-spline based methods for SPPs exhibiting boundary or interior layers. When combined with appropriately designed layer-adapted meshes, these spline-based approaches have demonstrated strong stability characteristics and reliable convergence behaviour.

Despite these advances, the numerical treatment of coupled systems of SPTPPs with Robin boundary conditions remains relatively less explored in the literature. The simultaneous presence of coupling, turning point behaviour, and Robin boundary conditions introduces additional mathematical and computational challenges. In particular, accurately resolving boundary layers in coupled systems requires numerical schemes that are not only stable but also capable of maintaining parameter-uniform accuracy across the entire domain. This motivates the development of more robust and efficient numerical techniques specifically designed for such complex problems.

In recent years, significant attention has been devoted to the development of parameter-uniform numerical methods for SPTPPs, with a focus on achieving high accuracy, improved stability, and efficient resolution of the boundary and interior layers. For a class of singularly perturbed interior TPPs with two outflow exponential boundary layers, Gupta et al.7 devised a parameter-uniform fitted mesh FDM on a piecewise-uniform mesh. The method employs a hybrid midpoint-upwind and central difference approach, and achieves almost second-order uniform convergence, up to a logarithmic factor. Raja8 provided an FDM constructed on a Shishkin mesh, along with the trapezoidal discretization of the integral boundary condition, to approximate a second-order TPP exhibiting boundary layers at both endpoints, yielding nearly first-order convergence. Wang and Wang9 rigorously analyzed SP-BVPs with multiple turning points using the method of matched asymptotic expansions. They derived a composite expansion that accurately captures both boundary and interior layer behaviors and established its validity through differential inequality theory. For two-dimensional singularly perturbed convection-diffusion problems with interior turning points, symmetric and non-symmetric interior penalty discontinuous Galerkin schemes on a Shishkin mesh were suggested by Ranjan and Gowrishankar10. They established parameter-uniform error estimates of order for the non-symmetric interior penalty discontinuous Galerkin method and for symmetric interior penalty discontinuous Galerkin method. Tesfaye et al.11 presented a uniformly convergent scheme for a singularly perturbed parabolic TPP by combining the Crank-Nicolson method in time with an exponentially fitted FDM in space, achieving first-order accuracy in space and second-order accuracy in time. An accelerated fitted mesh scheme for SP-BVPs exhibiting an interior layer near a turning point was introduced by Bullo12, utilizing a Shishkin-type mesh and Richardson extrapolation to improve the convergence from almost second-order to nearly sixth-order. A parameter-uniform numerical method for a singularly perturbed parabolic convection-diffusion TPP with Robin boundary conditions was formulated by Gelu and Durressa13, combining an implicit trapezoidal scheme in time with a second-order central difference method on a Shishkin mesh in space to achieve uniform convergence independent of the diffusion parameter. A trigonometric quintic B-spline method for singularly perturbed turning point boundary value problems (BVPs) was developed by Alam et al.14, employing a piecewise-uniform mesh to resolve boundary and interior layers and achieving almost fourth-order convergence independent of the perturbation parameter. A parameter-uniform numerical method for one-dimensional singularly perturbed parabolic convection-diffusion problems with multiple turning points was implemented by Yadav and Rai15, combining the implicit Euler time discretization with a hybrid spatial scheme on a generalized Shishkin mesh to achieve first-order convergence in time and almost second-order convergence in space up to a logarithmic factor. Kumar16 investigated a quintic B-spline collocation scheme constructed on a piecewise-uniform mesh for SP-BVPs with an interior turning point, establishing parameter-uniform convergence with fourth-order accuracy while effectively resolving interior and boundary layers.

The existing literature on systems of SPTPPs remains relatively limited and underdeveloped, particularly when compared with studies of single-equation or non-turning point problems. Although a few contributions have addressed this challenging class of problems, the available numerical methods are often restricted in scope or accuracy. Geetha and Tamilselvan17,18 proposed a Shishkin mesh-based parameter-uniform FDM for systems of SPTPPs, with Dirichlet and Robin boundary conditions in their respective works. Their methods yield error estimates of order and in the supremum norm, ensuring uniform convergence even in the presence of boundary layers. A quadratic spline-based numerical scheme on an exponentially graded mesh for fourth-order SPCD problems were analyzed by Satpal et al.19, after reformulating the problem as a coupled system and applying quasilinearization to the nonlinear case, achieving second-order uniform convergence in the discrete maximum norm. In more recent studies, Geetha et al.20 investigated a system of SPTPPs with integral boundary conditions and introduced FDM and cubic B-spline collocation technique, demonstrating almost first-order and second-order accuracy through theoretical and numerical analysis. Satpal et al.21 proposed a parameter-robust collocation scheme combined with the Crank-Nicolson time discretization on an exponentially graded mesh for convection-diffusion problems with a boundary turning point, attaining second-order accuracy. An upwind FDM on a piecewise-uniform mesh was presented by Raja et al.22 for a third-order SPTPP with an integral boundary condition, after reformulating the problem into an equivalent system. The method achieves almost first-order convergence while effectively resolving boundary layers at both endpoints. Karanam and Singh23 developed a numerical scheme for a system of second-order SPCD TPPs by combining the Crank-Nicolson method in time with a TCBS collocation approach on a Shishkin mesh in space. The method achieves nearly second-order convergence while effectively resolving the boundary layer near the left end of the domain.

The present study focuses on the numerical solution of a system of SPCD problems with an interior turning point and Robin boundary conditions, a class of problems that remains relatively unexplored in the literature. Existing numerical approaches, particularly FDMs, often achieve only first-order accuracy and may require very fine meshes to resolve sharp boundary and interior layers. Moreover, classical polynomial spline methods have limited effectiveness in capturing rapidly varying solutions associated with such problems in a parameter-uniform framework. To address these limitations, a TCBS collocation method is developed on a Shishkin mesh. The proposed TCBS collocation method offers several advantages over existing finite difference and classical spline-based approaches. In contrast to FDMs, which typically rely on piecewise linear approximations and may require very fine meshes to resolve steep gradients, the TCBS approach employs smooth trigonometric basis functions that provide higher-order approximation with fewer mesh points. Compared to conventional polynomial spline methods, the trigonometric basis functions are better suited for approximating rapidly varying solutions, particularly in the presence of boundary and interior layers. Furthermore, when combined with a Shishkin mesh, the proposed method achieves parameter-uniform convergence of order , improving upon the first-order convergence commonly observed in standard FDMs. The resulting discrete system is sparse and well-structured due to the local support of the spline basis, leading to improved computational efficiency and numerical stability. These features collectively demonstrate the effectiveness of the TCBS method in handling singularly perturbed problems more accurately and efficiently than existing approaches.

Conclusions and future work

In this work, a numerical scheme based on the TCBS collocation method on a Shishkin mesh has been proposed for solving a second-order coupled SPTPPs with Robin boundary conditions. The presence of a small perturbation parameter together with the turning point lead to the formation of boundary layers near both ends of the spatial domain, which makes the problem challenging for conventional numerical techniques. The use of the Shishkin mesh enables appropriate mesh refinement in the layer regions, allowing the proposed spline-based approach to accurately capture the rapid variations in the solution. The theoretical analysis confirms that the method achieves second-order convergence with respect to the mesh parameter . To support the theoretical findings, numerical experiments were carried out for several values of the perturbation parameter , ranging from to . The computed maximum nodal errors and convergence rates are presented in tabular form and clearly verify the predicted error estimates. In particular, the observed convergence rates are close to two for all tested cases, which is consistent with the theoretical prediction up to logarithmic terms. In addition, the CPU time required for the computations is reported, which demonstrates the computational efficiency of the proposed scheme. The numerical results were also compared with those reported by Geetha and Tamilselvan18, who employed a FDM, and the comparison indicates that the proposed method provides improved numerical accuracy. The graphical illustrations further demonstrate that the method effectively resolves the boundary layer behavior near both ends of the domain. In future work, the proposed approach can be extended to nonlinear singularly perturbed systems, higher-dimensional problems, or higher-order spline-based numerical schemes, which may further enhance the applicability and efficiency of the method for solving more complex SPPs.

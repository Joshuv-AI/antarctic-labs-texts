Abstract
. We show that if μ \mu is a self-affine measure on the plane defined by an iterated function system of contractions with diagonal linear parts, then under an irrationality assumption on the entries of the linear parts, dim H μ ∘ π − 1 = min ⁡ { 1 , dim H μ } \dimh\mu\circ\pi^{-1}=\min\{1,\dimh\mu\} for any non-principal orthogonal projection π \pi .

Primary 28A80; Secondary 37A10

1.
Introduction

The study of the size of orthogonal projections of sets and measures is an active and classical research topic in geometric measure theory and fractal geometry. Denoting by
\pi_{\theta}
the orthogonal projection from
\mathbb{R}^{2}
to the line spanned by
(1,2^{\theta})
, the expected phenomenon is that for any Borel measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, the set of
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
for which

(1.1)

\dimh\mu\circ\pi_{\theta}^{-1}<\min\{1,\dimh\mu\}

should be small, where
\dimh\mu=\inf\{\dimh E:\ \mu(E)>0\}
. While a classical result of Marstrand asserts that for any Borel measure
\mu
this is indeed the case in the sense that the set of
\theta
’s for which (
1.1
) can happen (the “exceptional set”) has zero length, in this generality it is difficult to go any further; For example, the exceptional set may easily have large Hausdorff dimension. On the other hand, if we impose any additional structural assumptions on
\mu
, it is reasonable to expect that using this structure we should be able to say more about the exceptional set of
\theta
’s, perhaps regarding its dimension or, in the extreme case, even determine the set exactly.

According to folklore, for many sets or measures arising from
dynamical systems
the set of
\theta
’s satisfying (
1.1
) should be explicitly determinable. A breakthrough in this line of research was achieved by Hochman and Shmerkin
[
14
]
who proved that for a large class of dynamically defined measures on the plane, including planar self-similar measures with strong separation, (
1.1
) cannot hold for
any

\theta\in\mathbb{R}
if the associated iterated function system satisfies an
irrationality condition
, that is, it involves a rotation which generates a dense subgroup of
SO_{2}(\mathbb{R})
. The role of such a condition is to prevent exact aligntment of cylinders in any direction and is necessary for the exceptional set to be empty, in general. The analogous result has since then been verified for numerous other measures as well, including self-similar
[
7
]
and self-conformal measures
[
3
]
with no separation assumptions at all, and many self-affine measures under various separation assumptions
[
6
,
4
,
2
,
11
,
1
]
.

Recall that an affine iterated function system
\{\varphi_{i}(x)=A_{i}x+a_{i}\}_{i\in\Gamma}
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
is called diagonal if for each
i\in\Gamma
,

A_{i}=\begin{bmatrix}\lambda_{1}(i)&0\\
0&\lambda_{2}(i)\end{bmatrix}

for some
\lambda_{1}(i),\lambda_{2}(i)\in(-1,1)\setminus\{0\}
, and that a measure
\mu
is called self-affine if

\mu=\sum_{i\in\Gamma}p_{i}\mu\circ\varphi_{i}^{-1}

for some
p_{i}>0
with
\sum_{i\in\Gamma}p_{i}=1
. In this paper we obtain a full resolution to the problem of Hausdorff dimension of projections of planar diagonal self-affine measures with an irrationality condition on the elements of
\{A_{i}\}_{i\in\Gamma}
. Previous partial results for such measures include the projection theorems of Ferguson et. al.
[
11
, Theorem 1.4]
for self-affine measures on Bedford-McMullen carpets, and of Bárány et. al.
[
2
, Theorem 1.6]
for planar diagonal self-affine measures with the strong separation condition. In this paper we write
\pi_{\theta}\mu:=\mu\circ\pi_{\theta}^{-1}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Phi=\{\varphi_{i}(x)=A_{i}x+a_{i}\}_{i\in\Gamma}
be a diagonal affine iterated function system on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Suppose that

(1)

there exists
l\in\Gamma
such that
|\lambda_{1}(l)|\neq|\lambda_{2}(l)|
, and

(2)

there exist
(s,t)\in\{1,2\}^{2}
and
(i,j)\in\Gamma^{2}
such that
\frac{\log|\lambda_{s}(i)|}{\log|\lambda_{t}(j)|}\not\in\mathbb{Q}
.

Then for any self-affine measure
\mu
associated to
\Phi
and for any
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
,

\dimh\pi_{\theta}\mu=\min\{1,\dimh\mu\}.

Condition (1) of Theorem
1.1
ensures that
\mu
is not self-similar, and is necessary for the statement to hold. We remark that while Theorem
1.1
relaxes the separation assumption from
[
2
, Theorem 1.6]
, there the authors also prove something stronger for the measures in question, namely that they have so-called
uniform scaling sceneries
. Proving this property for self-affine measures in the absence of any separation conditions remains a challenging open problem. Finally, we remark that the conclusion of Theorem
1.1
is not true for orthogonal projections to the coordinate axes. Counter-examples are found, for example, by choosing the contractions in
\Phi
in such a way that their fixed points lie on a vertical or horizontal line.

1.1.
On the proof

For simplicity, suppose that
\mu
has simple Lyapunov spectrum, and that
y
-axis is the major asymptotic contracting direction. As the majority of the previous works on the topic, also the present paper relies on the method of local entropy averages that Hochman and Shmerkin introduced in
[
14
]
. This method makes it possible to bound
\dimh\pi_{\theta}\mu
by bounding the
finite-scale entropies
of the measures
\pi_{\theta}\mu_{k}
, where
(\mu_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
are
magnifications
of
\mu
along a suitable filtration. As in the work of Falconer and Jin
[
7
]
on projections of measures on self-similar sets with no separation conditions, the first step towards bypassing the requirement for separation conditions in our setting is taken by magnifying the measure
\mu
(or rather, the corresponding Bernoulli measure
\bar{\mu}
) in the symbolic space
\Gamma^{\mathbb{N}}
instead of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, along a properly chosen filtration of
\Gamma^{\mathbb{N}}
. The elements of the filtration we choose produce approximate squares when projected onto the plane through the canonical projection
\Pi
, and relying on the non-conformal structure of
\mu
, we show that the canonical projections of the magnifications
\mu_{k}
of
\bar{\mu}
along this filtration have a product structure which is essentially of the form

(1.2)

\Pi\mu_{k}=A_{k}(\pi_{\mathtt{x}}\mu)_{I_{k}}\times\nu_{k}

where
A_{k}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}
is affine,
\pi_{\mathtt{x}}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
denotes the orthogonal projection to the
x
-axis,
I_{k}\subset\mathbb{R}
is a short interval and
\nu_{k}
is a conditional measure of
\mu
supported on a vertical line segment. Crucially,

\dimh((\pi_{\mathtt{x}}\mu)_{I_{k}}\times\nu_{k})=\dimh\pi_{\mathtt{x}}\mu+\dimh\nu_{k}=\dimh\mu

by a dimension conservation result of Feng and Hu
[
10
]
. Similar product structures were also observed in
[
11
,
2
]
for the measures in question. The representation (
1.2
) is made precise in Proposition
4.4
.

Given this product structure of magnifications, our goal is now to bound the finite-scale entropies of the measures

(1.3)

\pi_{\theta}A_{k}(\pi_{\mathtt{x}}\mu)_{I_{k}}\times\nu_{k},\qquad\theta\in\mathbb{R},\ k\in\mathbb{N}.

Here
\pi_{\mathtt{x}}\mu
is a self-similar measure on
\mathbb{R}
, whence the problem of bounding the size of (
1.3
) is related to the problems studied in
[
4
,
2
]
: Were the conditional measure
\nu_{k}
in (
1.3
) also self-similar (which it nearly is, in a certain dynamical sense), then we would know that the Hausdorff dimension of (
1.3
) would equal
\dimh\mu
for every
\theta
. However, even knowing the dimension of (
1.3
) is of no immediate use, since we need a bound for the finite-scale entropies of (
1.3
) which is
uniform over most

k
. In
[
2
]
and
[
11
]
, by relying on additional separation conditions the authors managed to show that the sequence

(1.4)

(A_{k}(\pi_{\mathtt{x}}\mu)_{I_{k}}\times\nu_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}

equidistributes for a well-structured distribution supported on measures whose projections have large entropy, which then led to the desired uniform bound for the entropies of (
1.3
). In our setting, (
1.4
) does not seem to equidistribute for any obvious distribution since the length of
I_{k}
decreases to
0
and its location is difficult to control in
k
. This forces us to find a uniform lower bound for the entropies of
\pi_{\theta}A(\pi_{\mathtt{x}}\mu)_{I}\times\nu
directly, where
\nu
is a conditional measure of
\mu
and the parameters
A:\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
range over all affine maps and intervals such that
{\rm diam}\,A(I\times[-1,1])\approx 1
. This bound is achieved with the help of Proposition
4.5
, the key technical tool of the paper, which yields a uniform lower bound for the small-scale entropies of
\pi_{\theta}(\pi_{\mathtt{x}}\mu\times\nu)
as
\theta
ranges over a large interval.

2.
Preliminaries

For
t\in\mathbb{R}
, let
S_{t}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
denote the map
x\mapsto 2^{t}x
. For
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
, we let
\pi_{\theta}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
denote the map
(x,y)\mapsto x+2^{\theta}y
. Up to an affine change of coordinates,
\pi_{\theta}
is the orthogonal projection to the line spanned by
(1,2^{\theta})
. For a measure
\mu
and a measurable set
B
, let
\mu|_{B}(\cdot):=\mu(\cdot\cap B)
denote the restriction of
\mu
on
B
and if
\mu(B)>0
, let
\mu_{B}:=\mu(B)^{-1}\mu|_{B}
.

2.1.
Entropy

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let

\mathcal{D}_{n}=\mathcal{D}_{n}(\mathbb{R})=\{[k2^{-n},(k+1)2^{-n}):\ k\in\mathbb{Z}\}

denote the partition of
\mathbb{R}
into dyadic intervals of length
2^{-n}
. For a Borel measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}
and a partition
\mathcal{E}
of
\mathbb{R}
, let

H(\nu,\mathcal{E}):=-\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}}\mu(E)\log\mu(E)

denote the entropy of
\mu
with respect to the partition
\mathcal{E}
. For entropy with respect to the dyadic partition, we write
H_{n}(\mu):=H(\mu,\mathcal{D}_{n})
. It is well-known (see
[
8
, Theorem 1.3]
) that for any Borel probability measure
\mu
,

\dimh\mu\leq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}H_{n}(\mu).

For two partitions
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{F}
, we denote by
H(\nu,\mathcal{E}|\mathcal{F}):=\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}}\nu(F)H(\nu_{F},\mathcal{E})
the conditional entropy of
\mu
with respect to
\mathcal{E}
given
\mathcal{F}
. Let also
\mathcal{E}\vee\mathcal{F}:=\{E\cap F:\ E\in\mathcal{E},F\in\mathcal{F}\}
denote the join of
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{F}
. Below, we record some elementary properties of entropy, cf.
[
5
, Section 2]
.

Lemma 2.1

(Concavity)
.

If
\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{m}
are Borel probability measures on
\mathbb{R}
,
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{F}
are partitions of
\mathbb{R}
and
p_{1},\ldots,p_{m}
are non-negative reals with
\sum_{i=1}^{m}p_{i}=1
, then

H\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}p_{i}\mu_{i},\mathcal{E}|\mathcal{F}\right)\geq\sum_{i=1}^{m}p_{i}H(\mu_{i},\mathcal{E}|\mathcal{F}).

Lemma 2.2

(Continuity)
.

If
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{F}
are partitions of
\mathbb{R}
such that each
E\in\mathcal{E}
intersects at most
k
elements of
\mathcal{F}
and vice versa, then

|H(\mu,\mathcal{E})-H(\mu,\mathcal{F})|\leq\log k.

Lemma 2.3

(Chain rule)
.

If
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{F}
are partitions of
\mathbb{R}
, then for any Borel probability measure
\mu
,

H(\mu,\mathcal{E}\vee\mathcal{F})=H(\mu,\mathcal{F})+H(\mu,\mathcal{E}|\mathcal{F}).

2.2.
Self-affine measures

Let
\Gamma
be a finite set with
\#\Gamma\geq 2
, and let
\Phi=\{\varphi_{i}(x)=A_{i}x+a_{i}\}_{i\in\Gamma}
be a collection of affine invertible contractions of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, where

A_{i}=\begin{bmatrix}\lambda_{1}(i)&0\\
0&\lambda_{2}(i)\end{bmatrix}

with
\lambda_{1}(i),\lambda_{2}(i)\in\mathbb{R}
for each
i\in\Gamma
. We let
\Gamma^{*}=\bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty}\Gamma^{n}
denote the collection of finite words composed of characters of
\Gamma
, where
\Gamma^{0}:=\{\emptyset\}
. For
\mathtt{i}=(i_{0},i_{1},\ldots)\in\Gamma^{\mathbb{N}}
and
n=1,2,\ldots
, let
\mathtt{i}|_{n}\in\Gamma^{n}
denote the projection to the first
n
coordinates. We equip
\Gamma^{\mathbb{N}}
with the topology generated by the cylinder sets

[\mathtt{i}]=\{\mathtt{j}\in\Gamma^{\mathbb{N}}:\ \mathtt{j}|_{|\mathtt{i}|}=\mathtt{i}\},

\mathtt{i}\in\Gamma^{*}
, where
|\mathtt{i}|
denotes the unique integer for which
\mathtt{i}\in\Gamma^{|\mathtt{i}|}
.
For
\mathtt{j}=(j_{0},\ldots,j_{n})\in\Gamma^{*}
, we write
\varphi_{\mathtt{j}}:=\varphi_{j_{0}}\circ\cdots\circ\varphi_{j_{n}}
and for
j\in\{1,2\}
,
\lambda_{j}(\mathtt{j})=\lambda_{j}(j_{0})\cdots\lambda_{j}(j_{n})
. We let
\sigma:\Gamma^{\mathbb{N}}\rightarrow\Gamma^{\mathbb{N}}
denote the left-shift,
\sigma(i_{0},i_{1},\ldots)=(i_{1},i_{2},\ldots)
.

Let
\Pi:\Gamma^{\mathbb{N}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}
denote the canonical projection,

\Pi(\mathtt{i})=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\varphi_{\mathtt{i}|_{n}}(0).

If
\bar{\mu}
is a Bernoulli probability measure on
\Gamma^{\mathbb{N}}
, then the measure
\mu:=\Pi\bar{\mu}
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
is called self-affine and can easily be seen to satisfy the equation

\mu=\sum_{i\in\Gamma}\bar{\mu}([i])\varphi_{i}\mu.

It follows from Kingman’s ergodic theorem that for
\bar{\mu}
-almost every
\mathtt{i}\in\Gamma^{\mathbb{N}}
, the limits

0>\lambda_{j}:=\lambda_{j}^{\mu}:=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log|\lambda_{j}(\mathtt{i}|_{n})|}{n}

for
j=1,2
exist. The numbers
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
are called the
Lyapunov exponents
of
\mu
. We say that
\bar{\mu}
(or
\mu
) has
simple Lyapunov spectrum
if
\lambda_{1}\neq\lambda_{2}
. If
\lambda_{i}>\lambda_{j}
, then it is easy to see that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}|\lambda_{j}(\mathtt{i}|_{n})||\lambda_{i}(\mathtt{i}|_{n})|^{-1}=0

for
\bar{\mu}
-almost every
\mathtt{i}\in\Gamma^{\mathbb{N}}
. We recall the following result regarding the dimension conservation for diagonal self-affine measures, which follows from the Ledrappier-Young formula due to Feng and Hu
[
10
, Theorem 2.11]
; see also
[
9
, Proof of Theorem 1.7 and Remark 6.3]
for more discussion on how
[
10
, Theorem 2.11]
implies Theorem
2.4
.

Theorem 2.4

(Corollary of Theorem 2.11 of
[
10
]
)
.

Let
\mu
be a diagonal self-affine measure on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, let
\pi_{\mathtt{x}}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
denote the orthogonal projection to the
x
-axis, and let
\mu=\int\mu_{x}\,d\pi_{\mathtt{x}}\mu(x)
be the disintegration of
\mu
with respect to
\pi_{\mathtt{x}}
. Then for
\pi_{\mathtt{x}}\mu
-almost every
x
,

\dimh\mu=\dimh\pi_{\mathtt{x}}\mu+\dimh\mu_{x}.

2.3.
Suspension flows

Let
f:\Gamma^{\mathbb{N}}\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
be a locally Hölder continuous function. The set

Z=\{(\mathtt{i},t):\ \mathtt{i}\in\Gamma^{\mathbb{N}},\ 0\leq t\leq f(\mathtt{i})\}

equipped with the identification
(\mathtt{i},f(\mathtt{i}))=(\sigma\mathtt{i},0)
is called the
suspension of

\Gamma

under

f
. The suspension
Z
together with the action
(\mathcal{T}_{s})_{s\geq 0}
induced on
Z
by the non-negative reals, given by
\mathcal{T}_{s}(\mathtt{i},t)=(\mathtt{i},t+s)
, is called the
suspension semi-flow
of
\Gamma^{\mathbb{N}}
over
f
. If
\mu
is a
\sigma
-invariant and ergodic measure on
\Gamma^{\mathbb{N}}
(that is,
\sigma\mu=\mu
and the only invariant functions are the almost everywhere constant functions), then it is easy to see that the measure
(\mu\times\mathcal{L})_{Z}
is invariant and ergodic under the action
(\mathcal{T}_{s})_{s\geq 0}
.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
(Z,(\mathcal{T}_{s})_{s\geq 0},(\mu\times\mathcal{L})_{Z})
be an ergodic suspension semi-flow. Suppose that
(\mathtt{i},0)
is a periodic point of
(\mathcal{T}_{s})_{s\geq 0}
with period
\alpha>0
. For any
\beta>0
which is not a rational multiple of
\alpha^{-1}
, the discrete-time dynamical system
(Z,\mathcal{T}_{\beta},(\mu\times\mathcal{L})_{Z})
is ergodic.

Proof.

It is proven for example in
[
12
, Lemma 3.11]
that the system
(Z,\mathcal{T}_{\beta},(\mu\times\mathcal{L})_{Z})
is ergodic if and only if no integer multiple of
\beta
is an eigenvalue of
(Z,(\mathcal{T}_{s})_{s\geq 0},(\mu\times\mathcal{L})_{Z})
. Recall that
\beta
is an eigenvalue of
(Z,(\mathcal{T}_{s})_{s\geq 0},(\mu\times\mathcal{L})_{Z})
if there exists a measurable function
\psi
, called an eigenfunction, such that
\psi(\mathtt{i},t)=e^{-2\pi i\beta t}\psi(\mathtt{i},0)
for
\bar{\mu}
-almost every
\mathtt{i}\in\Gamma^{\mathbb{N}}
and every
t\geq 0
.

For a contradiction, suppose that for some
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,
n\beta
is an eigenvalue with eigenfunction
\psi
. By
[
16
, Proposition 6.2]
we may take
\psi
to be continuous, in particular,
\psi
is defined at the periodic point
(\mathtt{i},0)
and

\psi(\mathtt{i},0)=\psi(\mathtt{i},\alpha)=e^{-2\pi in\beta\alpha}\psi(\mathtt{i},0).

Because
|\psi|
is invariant and therefore almost surely constant by ergodicity of the system
(Z,(\mathcal{T}_{s})_{s\geq 0},(\mu\times\mathcal{L})_{Z})
, it follows that
2n\beta\alpha
must be an integer. This is a contradiction.
∎

3.
Local entropy averages

3.1.
The filtration

In
[
14
]
, Hochman and Shmerkin introduced a method which makes it possible to bound the Hausdorff dimension of a projection of a measure by bounding finite-scale entropies of magnifications of the measure along a suitably chosen filtration. We will next define the filtration along which we magnify.

Let
\Phi=\{\varphi_{i}\}_{i\in\Gamma}
be a system of affine contractions as in the statement of Theorem
1.1
, and let
\mu
be a self-affine measure associated to
\Phi
. Let
\bar{\mu}
denote the associated Bernoulli measure on
\Gamma^{\mathbb{N}}
. Define the sets

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{n}^{1}

\displaystyle=\{[\mathtt{i}]:\ \mathtt{i}\in\Gamma^{*},\ |\lambda_{2}(\mathtt{i})|>|\lambda_{1}(\mathtt{i})|,\ 2^{-n}\geq|\lambda_{1}(\mathtt{i})|\geq 2^{-n}\min_{i\in\Gamma}|\lambda_{1}(i)|\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{n}^{2}

\displaystyle=\{[\mathtt{i}]:\ \mathtt{i}\in\Gamma^{*},\ |\lambda_{1}(\mathtt{i})|\geq|\lambda_{2}(\mathtt{i})|,\ 2^{-n}\geq|\lambda_{2}(\mathtt{i})|\geq 2^{-n}\min_{i\in\Gamma}|\lambda_{2}(i)|\}

for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then clearly, for every
n
,
\mathcal{E}_{n}^{1}\cap\mathcal{E}_{n}^{2}=\emptyset
and
\Gamma^{\mathbb{N}}=\bigcup_{[\mathtt{i}]\in\mathcal{E}_{n}^{1}\cup\mathcal{E}_{n}^{2}}[\mathtt{i}]
, where the union is disjoint. Note that for
[\mathtt{i}]\in\mathcal{E}_{n}^{j}
, the set
\Pi([\mathtt{i}])\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a rectangle with shorter side of length
|\lambda_{j}(\mathtt{i})|\approx 2^{-n}
. Here and in the following, we write
A\approx B
to indicate that
A\leq O(B)
and
B\leq O(A)
where the implicit constants do not depend on
A
or
B
. We proceed to refine the partitions
\mathcal{E}_{n}^{1}\cup\mathcal{E}_{n}^{2}
further by joining them with either vertical or horizontal “dyadic tubes” of width
2^{-n}
, so that the canonical projections of elements of this join are “approximate squares”, that is, rectangles of dimensions roughly
2^{-n}

Proposition 4.4

(Product structure)

Remark 4.6
.

It is the estimate (
4.19
) in which it is crucial that Proposition
4.5
gives the desired lower bound
uniformly
for every
\theta
in an arbitrarily large fixed interval. Indeed, the exact value of
\theta(\mathtt{i},\mathtt{j},n)
is something we do not know how to control as
\mathtt{j}
varies in
E_{nN}^{\delta}(\mathtt{i})
.

4.2.
The case
\lambda_{1}^{\mu}=\lambda_{2}^{\mu}

In the case
\lambda_{1}^{\mu}>\lambda_{2}^{\mu}
, the cylinders
\Pi([\mathtt{i}|_{n}])
were “vertically flat” for almost all
\mathtt{i}
and all large enough
n
, which allowed us to deduce the product structure of the measures
\Pi\bar{\mu}_{E_{n}(\mathtt{i})}
in Proposition
4.4
. In the case
\lambda_{1}^{\mu}=\lambda_{2}^{\mu}
this “uniform vertical flatness” is no longer the case; However,
[
17
, Theorem 1]
asserts that the sequence

\left(\log\frac{|\lambda_{1}(\mathtt{i}|_{k})|}{|\lambda_{2}(\mathtt{i}|_{k})|}\right)_{k}=\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\log\frac{|\lambda_{1}(\sigma^{\ell}\mathtt{i}|_{1})|}{|\lambda_{2}(\sigma^{\ell}\mathtt{i}|_{1})|}\right)_{k}

equidistributes (in a sense) for the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}
, in particular, for any
M>0
,

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\#\left\{1\leq k\leq n:\ \left|\log\frac{|\lambda_{1}(\mathtt{i}|_{k})|}{|\lambda_{2}(\mathtt{i}|_{k})|}\right|\leq M\right\}=0.

In other words, the number
|\lambda_{1}(\mathtt{i}|_{k})||\lambda_{2}(\mathtt{i}|_{k})|^{-1}
(the “eccentricity” of the rectangle
\Pi([\mathtt{i}|_{k}])
) is usually far from one in the sense that for any
M>0
, with full frequency, either

(4.21)

|\lambda_{1}(\mathtt{i}|_{n})|>2^{M}|\lambda_{2}(\mathtt{i}|_{n})|\ \text{or}\ |\lambda_{2}(\mathtt{i}|_{n})|>2^{M}|\lambda_{1}(\mathtt{i}|_{n})|.

In particular, with full frequency, the rectangle
\Pi([\mathtt{i}|_{n}])
is either vertically or horizontally flat, and so we are essentially reduced to a combination of the cases
\lambda_{1}^{\mu}>\lambda_{2}^{\mu}
and
\lambda_{2}^{\mu}>\lambda_{1}^{\mu}
. Indeed, in Proposition
4.4
, for every
n
we have to switch between
\pi_{\mathtt{x}}
and
\pi_{\mathtt{y}}
depending on which one of the numbers
|\lambda_{1}(\mathtt{i}|_{n})|
and
|\lambda_{2}(\mathtt{i}|_{n})|
dominates in (
4.21
). Proposition
4.5
does not assume anything regarding the Lyapunov exponents of
\mu
and so works also if
\pi_{\mathtt{x}}
is replaced by
\pi_{\mathtt{y}}
and
\mu_{\mathtt{i}}
by a conditional measure supported on a horizontal line segment. Regarding the proof of Theorem
1.1
, one first duplicates it for the case
\lambda_{2}^{\mu}>\lambda_{1}^{\mu}
, and then in the case
\lambda_{1}^{\mu}=\lambda_{2}^{\mu}
, the average
\frac{1}{m}\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\frac{1}{N}H_{N}(\pi_{\theta}\Pi\bar{\mu}_{E_{nN}(\mathtt{i})})
is split into two parts: The average of those terms in which
\lambda_{1}(\mathtt{i}|_{nN})\gg\lambda_{2}(\mathtt{i}|_{nN})
is handled as in the case
\lambda_{1}^{\mu}>\lambda_{2}^{\mu}
, and the average of those terms in which
\lambda_{2}(\mathtt{i}|_{nN})\gg\lambda_{1}(\mathtt{i}|_{nN})
is handled as in the case
\lambda_{2}^{\mu}>\lambda_{1}^{\mu}
. We leave the details to the interested reader.

Proof of Claim.

Define the function

Proof of Proposition

,

Singular vectors, characters, and composition series for the N=1 BMS superalgebra
Wei Jiang, Dong Liu, Yufeng Pei, Kaiming Zhao

Abstract
. This paper investigates the structure of Verma modules over the N=1 BMS superalgebra. We provide a detailed classification of singular vectors, establish necessary and sufficient conditions for the existence of subsingular vectors, uncover the structure of maximal submodules, present the composition series of Verma modules, and derive character formulas for irreducible highest weight modules.

1.
Introduction

Infinite-dimensional symmetries play a significant role in physics. Specifically, Virasoro-type symmetries have significant applications in two-dimensional field theory, string theory, gravity, and other areas. The representation theory of the Virasoro algebra has also been widely and deeply studied
[
20
,
25
,
30
,
36
]
. In recent years, two-dimensional non-relativistic conformal symmetries have gained importance in establishing holographic dualities beyond the AdS/CFT correspondence
[
9
,
12
,
35
]
. The Bondi-Metzner-Sachs algebra, commonly known as BMS algebra, generates the asymptotic symmetry group of three-dimensional Einstein gravity
[
16
,
17
,
34
]
. Although BMS algebra extends the Virasoro algebra, its representation theory differs fundamentally. Studies on special highest weight modules for the BMS algebra have explored various aspects: determinant formulas
[
8
]
, character formulas
[
32
]
, free field realizations
[
14
]
, and modular invariance
[
10
,
13
]
. However, a complete understanding of highest weight modules is still lacking.

In mathematical literature, the BMS algebra is known as the Lie algebra
W(2,2)
, an infinite-dimensional Lie algebra first introduced in
[
38
]
to study the classification of moonshine-type vertex operator algebras generated by two weight-2 vectors. They examined the vacuum modules of the
W(2,2)
algebra (with a VOA structure) and established necessary and sufficient conditions for these modules to be irreducible. Their key insight was creating a total ordering on the PBW bases, which facilitated computations of determinant formulas (see also
[
28
]
). It is worth mentioning that the
W(2,2)
algebra has also been discovered and studied in several different mathematical fields, such as
[
21
,
22
,
37
]
. The irreducibility conditions for Verma modules over the
W(2,2)
algebra are also given in
[
37
]
. In
[
26
]
, it was proposed that maximal submodules of reducible Verma modules are generated by singular vectors. However, Radobolja
[
33
]
pointed out that this is true only for typical highest weights. For atypical weights, the maximal submodules are generated by both a singular vector and a subsingular vector. He also derived a character formula for irreducible highest weight modules and established necessary conditions for subsingular vector existence. The study further conjectured that these necessary conditions are also sufficient. Later,
[
27
]
provided additional support for this conjecture.
Adamovic et al. used the free field realization of the twisted Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra at level zero
[
4
,
15
]
, along with constructing screening operators in lattice vertex algebras, to derive an expression for singular vectors of Verma modules for the
W(2,2)
algebra under certain conditions in
[
2
,
3
]
. To our knowledge, explicit formulas for singular and subsingular vectors, as well as the composition series for general Verma modules over the
W(2,2)
algebra, remain unresolved prior to the present paper.

The N=1 BMS superalgebra, introduced in
[
7
]
, is the minimal supersymmetric extension of the BMS
3
algebra with central extensions. It incorporates a set of spin-
\frac{3}{2}
generators
Q_{n}
within the BMS
3
algebra framework. Although this superalgebra is a subalgebra of the truncated Neveu-Schwarz superalgebra, its representation theory differs significantly from that of the N=1 Neveu-Schwarz superalgebra
[
11
,
23
,
24
,
31
]
.
In recent paper
[
29
,
19
]
, the authors classified simple smooth modules including Whittaker modules over the N=1 BMS superalgebra under mild conditions and provided necessary and sufficient conditions for the irreducibility of Verma modules and Fock modules. Further detailed analysis on the structure of reducible Verma modules over the N=1 BMS superalgebra will be carried out in the present paper.

As established in
[
29
]
, the Verma module
V(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M})
over the N=1 BMS superalgebra
\mathfrak{g}
is irreducible if and only if
2h_{M}+\frac{p^{2}-1}{12}c_{M}\neq 0
for any positive integer
p
. If further
c_{M}=0
, then
h_{M}=0
, resulting in the degeneration of the irreducible highest weight module into an irreducible highest weight module over the Virasoro algebra (refer to Lemma
2.4
). In this paper, we study the structure of the Verma module
V(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M})
over
\mathfrak{g}
under the obvious conditions that

c_{M}\neq 0\ \text{and}\ \ 2h_{M}+\frac{p^{2}-1}{12}c_{M}=0\ \text{for some}\ p\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}.

We classify all singular vectors of the Verma module
V(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M})
and provide explicit formulas. We also identify the necessary and sufficient conditions for the existence of subsingular vector and list them all. Our first main result is as follows:

Main Theorem 1.
(Theorems
3.8
,
3.16
,
4.17
below)
Let
(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{4}
such that
2h_{M}+\frac{p^{2}-1}{12}c_{M}=0
for some
p\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
with
c_{M}\neq 0
.

(1)
All singular vectors in
V(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M})
are of the form
{\rm S}^{i}{\bf 1}
(when
p
even) or
{\rm R}^{i}{\bf 1}
(when
p
odd) for
i\in\mathbb{N}
, where
{\rm S}
and
{\rm R}
are given in Proposition
3.1
and Proposition
3.11
, respectively.

(2)
There exists a subsingular vector of
V(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M})
if and only if
h_{L}=h_{p,r}
for some
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, where

\displaystyle h_{p,r}=-\frac{p^{2}-1}{24}c_{L}+\frac{(41p+5)(p-1)}{48}+\frac{(1-r)p}{2}-\frac{1+(-1)^{p}}{8}p.

In this case,
{\rm T}_{p,r}{\bf 1}
is the unique subsingular vector, up to a scalar multiple, where
{\rm T}_{p,r}
are given in Theorem
4.17
.

By utilizing the information provided in Main Theorem 1 regarding singular and subsingular vectors, we can derive the character formulas for irreducible highest weight modules over
\mathfrak{g}
as follows:

Main Theorem 2.
(Theorems
5.3
,
5.4
below)
Let
(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{4}
such that
2h_{M}+\frac{p^{2}-1}{12}c_{M}=0
for some
p\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
with
c_{M}\neq 0
.

(1)
If
V(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M})
is typical (i.e.,
h_{L}\neq h_{p,r}
for any
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
), then the maximal submodule of
V(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M})
is generated by
{\rm S}{\bf 1}
(when
p
is even), or by
{\rm R}{\bf 1}
(when
p
is odd). Additionally, the character formula of the irreducible highest weight module
L(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M})
can be expressed as follows:

{\rm char}\,L(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M})=q^{h_{L}}(1-q^{\frac{p}{2}})\left(1+\frac{1}{2}(1+(-1)^{p})q^{\frac{p}{2}}\right)\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1+q^{k-\frac{1}{2}}}{(1-q^{k})^{2}}.

(2)
If
V(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M})
is atypical (i.e.,
h_{L}=h_{p,r}
for some
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
), then the maximal submodule of
V(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M})
is generated by the subsingular vector
{\rm T}_{p,r}{\bf 1}
. Additionally, the character formula of the irreducible highest weight module
L(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M})
can be expressed as follows:

{\rm char}\,L(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M})=q^{h_{p,r}}(1-q^{\frac{p}{2}})\left(1+\frac{1}{2}(1+(-1)^{p})q^{\frac{p}{2}}\right)(1-q^{rp})\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1+q^{k-\frac{1}{2}}}{(1-q^{k})^{2}}.

Following Main Theorems 1 and 2, we derive the composition series of the Verma modules as follows:

is atypical, then the Verma module

has the following infie nit composition series of submodules:

\displaystyle V(c_{L},

As a byproduct, we also explicitly determine all singular vectors, subsingular vectors, and the composition series of Verma modules over the algebra

(see Corollary

It is worth noting that subsingular vectors have been observed in studies of Verma modules for the N=1 Ramond algebra
[
23
]
, the N=2 superconformal algebra
[
18
]
, and the N=1 Heisenberg-Virasoro superalgebra at level zero
[

]
.
Our main theorems reveal significant differences between the structure of Verma modules for the N=1 BMS superalgebra and those for the Virasoro algebra and N=1 Neveu-Schwarz algebra. For Verma modules over the Virasoro algebra, the maximal submodule is usually generated by two distinct weight vectors
[
5
,
6
]
. In contrast, the maximal submodule of a Verma module
V(c_{L},c_{M},h_{p,r},h_{M})
can always be generated by a single weight vector. Additionally, some submodules of
V(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M})
cannot be generated by singular vectors.

The methods used to prove the main theorems differ from those in
[
15
,
33
]
and also from the one in
[
1
,
2
]
.
Motivated by
[
27
]
, in the present paper we introduce key operators
{\rm S}
,
{\rm R}
, and
{\rm T}
, derive their crucial properties, and reveal significant relationships among them.
Our method of classifying singular and subsingular vectors differs from those used for the Virasoro, super-Virasoro, and
W(2,2)
algebras
[
5
,
27
,
33
]
.
A significant advancement is the application of the total ordering on PBW bases, as defined in
[
29
]
, to analyze the coefficient of the highest order terms of the vectors
{\rm S}{\bf 1},{\rm R}{\bf 1}
or
{\rm T}{\bf 1}
with respect to
L_{-p}
. This approach facilitates the recursive identification of all singular and subsingular vectors. Our future research will focus on the Fock modules of the N=1 BMS superalgebra as introduced in
[
29
]
, with the aim of deepening our understanding of Verma modules.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we briefly review the relevant results on representations of the N=1 BMS superalgebra. In Section 3, in order to determine the maximal submodule of the Verma module

for both

and

are actually determined by one element

(or

) in

, see Theorems

,
3.16
. In Section 4, We study the quotient module of the Verma module by the submodule generated by all singular vectors determined in Section 3. In particular, we find the necessary and sufficient conditions for the existence of a subsingular vector in

, and determine all subsingular vectors. See Theorems
4.3
,
4.6
.
In Section 5, we give the maximal submodules of

(which is always generated by one weight vector) and the character formula for irreducible highest weight modules in both typical and atypical cases, see Theorems
5.3
,
5.4
.
We obtain the composition series (of infinite length) of Verma modules

5.7

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall some notations and results related to the N=1 BMS superalgebra.

2.1.
The N=1 BMS superalgebra

Definition 2.1
.

[
7
]

The
N=1 BMS superalgebra

\mathfrak{g}=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}L_{n}\oplus\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}M_{n}\oplus\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}}\mathbb{C}Q_{n}\oplus\mathbb{C}{\bf c}_{L}\oplus\mathbb{C}{\bf c}_{M}

is a Lie superalgebra, where

\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}L_{n}\oplus\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}M_{n}\oplus\mathbb{C}{\bf c}_{L}\oplus\mathbb{C}{\bf c}_{M},\quad\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}=\bigoplus_{r\in\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}}\mathbb{C}Q_{r},

with the following commutation relations:

\displaystyle{[L_{m},L_{n}]}=(m-n)L_{m+n}+{1\over 12}\delta_{m+n,0}(m^{3}-m){\bf c}_{L},

\displaystyle{[L_{m},M_{n}]}=(m-n)M_{m+n}+{1\over 12}\delta_{m+n,0}(m^{3}-m){\bf c}_{M},

\displaystyle{[Q_{r},Q_{s}]}=2M_{r+s}+{1\over 3}\delta_{r+s,0}\left(r^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\right){\bf c}_{M},

\displaystyle{[L_{m},Q_{r}]}=\left(\frac{m}{2}-r\right)Q_{m+r},

\displaystyle{[M_{m},M_{n}]}=[M_{n},Q_{r}]=0,

\displaystyle[{\bf c}_{L},\mathfrak{g}]=[{\bf c}_{M},\mathfrak{g}]=0,

for any
m,n\in\mathbb{Z},r,s\in\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}
.

Note that the even part
\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}
corresponds to the BMS algebra
W(2,2)
. Additionally, the subalgebra
{{\mathfrak{vir}}}=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}L_{n}\oplus\mathbb{C}\mathbf{c}_{L}
represents the Virasoro algebra.

The N=1 BMS superalgebra
\mathfrak{g}
has a
\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}
-grading by the eigenvalues of the adjoint action of
L_{0}
. It is clear that
\mathfrak{g}
has the following triangular decomposition:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}={\mathfrak{g}}_{-}\oplus{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}\oplus{\mathfrak{g}}_{+},

where

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{g}}_{\pm}=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}{\mathbb{C}}L_{\pm n}\oplus\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}{\mathbb{C}}M_{\pm n}\oplus\bigoplus_{r\in\frac{1}{2}+\mathbb{N}}{\mathbb{C}}Q_{\pm r},

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}={\mathbb{C}}L_{0}\oplus{\mathbb{C}}M_{0}\oplus{\mathbb{C}}{\bf c}_{L}\oplus{\mathbb{C}}{\bf c}_{M}.

2.2.
Verma modules

For
(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{4}
, let
{\mathbb{C}}
be the module over

{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}\oplus{\mathfrak{g}}_{+}
defined by

\displaystyle{\bf c}_{L}{1}=c_{L}{1},\quad{\bf c}_{M}{1}=c_{M}{1},\quad L_{0}{1}=h_{L}{1},\quad M_{0}{1}=h_{M}{1},\quad{\mathfrak{g}}_{+}1=0.

The Verma module over
{\mathfrak{g}}
is defined as follows

\displaystyle V(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M})=U({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes_{U({\mathfrak{g}}_{0}\oplus{\mathfrak{g}}_{+})}{\mathbb{C}}\simeq U({\mathfrak{g}}_{-}){\bf 1},

where
{\bf 1}=1\otimes 1
.
It follows that

V(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M})=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{N}}V(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M})_{n}
and
U({\mathfrak{g}_{-}})=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}U(\mathfrak{g}_{-})_{n},

where

V(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M})_{n}=\{v\in V(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M})\,|\,L_{0}v=(h_{L}+n)v\}

and

U(\mathfrak{g}_{-})_{n}=\{x\in U(\mathfrak{g}_{-})\,|\,[L_{0},x]=nx\}.

Moreover,

V(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M})=V(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M})_{\bar{0}}\oplus V(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M})_{\bar{1}}

with

\displaystyle V(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M})_{\bar{0}}=

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{N}}V(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M})_{n},

\displaystyle V(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M})_{\bar{1}}=

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{N}}V(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M})_{\frac{1}{2}+n}.

It is clear that
V(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M})
has
a unique maximal submodule
J(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M})
and the factor module

L(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M})=V(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M})/J(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M})

is an irreducible highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-module. Define

{\rm char}\,V(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M})=q^{h_{L}}\sum_{i\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{N}}{\rm dim}\,V(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M})_{i}q^{i}.

An eigenvector
u
in
V(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M})
with respect to
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
is called a
singular vector
if
\mathfrak{g}_{+}u=0
.
Let
J^{\prime}(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M})
be the submodule of
V(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M})
generated by all singular vectors.
A weight vector
u^{\prime}
in
V(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M})
is called a
subsingular vector
if
u^{\prime}+J^{\prime}(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M})
is a singular vector in
V(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M})/J^{\prime}(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M})
.

Recall that a partition of a positive integer
n
is a finite non-increasing sequence of
positive integers
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots,\lambda_{r})
such that
n=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\lambda_{i}
. The positive integer
\lambda_{i}
is called the
i
-th entry of the partition
\lambda
. We call
r
the length of
\lambda
, denoted by
\ell(\lambda)
, and call the sum of
\lambda_{i}
’s the weight of
\lambda
, denoted
by
|\lambda|
. Denote
\lambda-\frac{1}{2}=\left(\lambda_{1}-\frac{1}{2},\lambda_{2}-\frac{1}{2},\dots,\lambda_{r}-\frac{1}{2}\right)
and
-\lambda=(-\lambda_{1},-\lambda_{2},\dots,-\lambda_{r})
.
The number of partitions of
n
is given by the partition function
{\tt p}(n)
. Denote by
\mathcal{P}
the set of all partitions (including the empty partition) and

\mathcal{P}(n)
the set of all partitions with weight
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
.
A partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots,\lambda_{r})
is called strict if
\lambda_{1}>\lambda_{2}>\dots>\lambda_{r}>0
. The set
\mathcal{SP}
consists of all strict partitions (including the empty partition). Recall that the natural ordering on
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{SP}
is defined as follows:

\displaystyle\lambda>\mu\iff|\lambda|>|\mu|,\text{ or }|\lambda|=|\mu|,\lambda_{1}=\mu_{1},\dots,\lambda_{k}=\mu_{k},\text{ and }\lambda_{k+1}>\mu_{k+1}\text{ for some }\ k\geq 0;

\displaystyle\lambda=\mu\iff\lambda_{i}=\mu_{i}\quad\text{for all }\ i.

According to the Poincaré-Birkhoff-Witt (
\mathrm{PBW}
) theorem, every vector
v
of
V(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M})
can be uniquely written in
the following form

v=\sum_{\lambda,\nu\in\mathcal{P},\mu\in\mathcal{SP}}a_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}M_{-\lambda}Q_{-\mu+\frac{1}{2}}L_{-\nu}{\bf 1},

(2.1)

where
a_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}\in\mathbb{C}
and only finitely many of them are non-zero, and

M_{-\lambda}:=M_{-\lambda_{1}}\cdots M_{-\lambda_{r}},\ Q_{-\mu+\frac{1}{2}}:=Q_{-\mu_{1}+\frac{1}{2}}\cdots Q_{-\mu_{s}+\frac{1}{2}},\ L_{-\nu}:=L_{-\nu_{1}}\cdots L_{-\nu_{t}}.

For any
v\in V(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M})
as in (
2.1
), we denote by
\mathrm{supp}(v)
the set of all
(\lambda,\mu,\nu)\in\mathcal{P}\times\mathcal{SP}\times\mathcal{P}
such that
a_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}\neq 0
.
Next, we define

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}={\rm span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{M_{i}\mid i\in\mathbb{Z}\},\quad\mathcal{M}_{-}={\rm span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{M_{-i}\mid i\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}={\rm span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{Q_{-i+\frac{1}{2}}\mid i\in\mathbb{Z}\},\ \mathcal{Q}_{-}={\rm span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{Q_{-i+\frac{1}{2}}\mid i\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\}.

Note that
\mathcal{M}+\mathbb{C}{\bf c}_{M}
and
\mathcal{M}+\mathcal{Q}+\mathbb{C}{\bf c}_{M}
are ideals of
\mathfrak{g}
.

For
y=M_{-\lambda}Q_{-\mu+\frac{1}{2}}L_{-\nu}
or
y{\bf 1}
, we define

\ell(y)=\ell(y{\bf 1})=\ell(\lambda)+\ell(\mu)+\ell(\nu),\ {\rm deg}(y)=|\lambda|+|\mu-\frac{1}{2}|+|\nu|.

For (
2.1

\displaystyle B_{n}=\{b_{i}\mid b_{i}\succ b_{j}\ \text{for}\ i>j\},\quad\text{where}\quad b_{i}=M_{-\lambda^{(i)}}G_{-\mu^{(i)}}L_{-\nu^{(i)}}{\bf 1},

\displaystyle f_{1}(M)=c_{1}M_{-1},f_{i}(M)=c_{i}M_{-i}+\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}c_{i}f_{j}(M)M_{-(i-j)},

and
c_{i}=\frac{6}{i(p-i)c_{M}}
for
i=1,\cdots,\frac{p-1}{2}
.

Proof.

Let
{\rm R}{\bf 1}
be as (
3.7
), a singular vector in
V(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M})_{\frac{p}{2}}
,
where
f_{i}(M)\in U(\mathcal{M}_{-})
with degree
i
,
i=1,\cdots,\frac{p-1}{2}
.

For
i=1,2,\cdots,\frac{p-1}{2}
, using the action of
Q_{\frac{p}{2}-i}
on (
3.7
), we deduce that

\displaystyle 0=Q_{\frac{p}{2}-i}{\rm R}{\bf 1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[Q_{\frac{p}{2}-i},Q_{-\frac{p}{2}}]{\bf 1}+\sum_{j=1}^{i}f_{j}(M)[Q_{\frac{p}{2}-i},Q_{-\frac{p}{2}+j}]{\bf 1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2M_{-i}{\bf 1}+2f_{1}(M)M_{-i+1}{\bf 1}+\cdots+f_{i}(M)\left(2M_{0}+\frac{(p-2i)^{2}-1}{12}{\bf c}_{M}\right){\bf 1}.

Applying
h_{M}=-\frac{p^{2}-1}{24}c_{M}
we deduce that

2M_{-i}+2f_{1}(M)M_{-i+1}+\cdots-f_{i}(M)\frac{i(p-i)}{3}c_{M}=0,

\displaystyle f_{1}(M)=c_{1}M_{-1},f_{i}(M)=c_{i}M_{-i}+\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}c_{i}f_{j}(M)M_{-(i-j)},

and
c_{i}=\frac{6}{i(p-i)c_{M}}
for
i=1,\cdots,\frac{p-1}{2}
.
∎

Example 3.12
.

\displaystyle(1)

\displaystyle p=1,h_{M}=0:{\rm R}=Q_{-\frac{1}{2}};

\displaystyle(2)

\displaystyle p=3,h_{M}=-\frac{1}{3}c_{M}:{\rm R}=Q_{-\frac{3}{2}}+\frac{3}{c_{M}}M_{-1}Q_{-\frac{1}{2}};

\displaystyle(3)

\displaystyle p=5,h_{M}=-c_{M}:{\rm R}=Q_{-\frac{5}{2}}+\frac{3}{2c_{M}}M_{-1}Q_{-\frac{3}{2}}+\frac{1}{c_{M}}M_{-2}Q_{-\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{3}{2c_{M}^{2}}M_{-1}^{2}Q_{-\frac{1}{2}}.

By direct calculation, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 3.13
.

For any
x\in\mathfrak{g}_{+}
, we have

\displaystyle[x,{\rm R}]\subset U(\mathcal{M}_{-}+\mathcal{Q}_{-})\left(2M_{0}+\frac{p^{2}-1}{12}{\bf c}_{M}\right)+U(\mathcal{M}_{-}+\mathcal{Q}_{-})\mathfrak{g}_{+}.

Proof.

It follows from the fact that
[x,{\rm R}]{\bf 1}=0
in

and
[x,{\rm R}]\in U(\mathcal{M}+\mathcal{Q}+\mathbb{C}{\bf c}_{M})
for any

.
∎

Proposition 3.14
.

Let
p\in 2\mathbb{Z}_{+}-1
. Then

{\rm R}^{2}={\rm S}
,
{\rm R}^{n}{\bf 1}
is also a singular vector for any

.

Proof.

It follows from Lemma
3.13
that
{\rm R}^{2}{\bf 1}
is a singular vector in

. By Theorem
3.8
,
{\rm R}^{2}={\rm S}
.
Moreover, for any

,
{\rm R}^{n}{\bf 1}
is also a singular vector by Lemma
3.13
.
∎

Lemma 3.15
.

If
u
is a singular vector in
V(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M})_{n-\frac{1}{2}}
for
n\geq 1
, then
\ell_{L}(u)=0
.

Proof.

Write
u=y{\bf 1}\in V(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M})_{n-\frac{1}{2}}
, where

. Then
M_{0}u=M_{0}y{\bf 1}=[M_{0},y]{\bf 1}+h_{M}u
. By a similar argument in the proof of Lemma

, we have
M_{0}y{\bf 1}=h_{M}y{\bf 1}
. For any

x{\rm R}y{\bf 1}={\rm R}xy{\bf 1}+[x,{\rm R}]y{\bf 1}=0

.
Then
{\rm R}y{\bf 1}
is a singular vector in

{\ell}_{L}({\rm R}y)=0

3.7
and then

.
∎

Now we get the following result.

Theorem 3.16
.

Let
(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{4}
such that
\phi(p)=0
with
c_{M}\neq 0
. Then
V(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M})_{n-\frac{1}{2}}
for

has a singular vector
u
if and only if
p\in 2\mathbb{Z}_{+}-1
and there exists
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
such that
n-\frac{1}{2}=\frac{p}{2}(2k-1)
. Moreover,
all singular vectors of

, up to a scalar multiple, are
{\rm R}^{2k-1}{\bf 1}
for
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
.

Proof.

By Lemmas
3.9
,
3.15
and Propositions
3.10
and
3.14
, we can suppose that

u=(a_{0}{\rm R}^{2k-1}+a_{1}{\rm R}^{2k-3}+\cdots a_{k-1}{\rm R}+a_{k})\mathbf{1},

(3.9)

where
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},a_{i}\in U(\mathcal{M}_{-})
not involving
M_{-p}
for any

, and

not involving

Assume that
a_{k}\neq 0

\ell_{Q}(a_{k})=1

{\rm hm}(a_{k})=M_{-\mu}Q_{-\frac{q}{2}}
, where
\mu\in\mathcal{P},q\neq p

on

.

Set
{\rm max}\{{\rm hm}(a_{0}),\cdots,{\rm hm}(a_{k-1})\}=M_{-\lambda}

a_{i}\in U(\mathcal{M}_{-})

.

for any

∎

Corollary 3.18
.

(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M})\neq(c_{L}^{\prime},c_{M}^{\prime},h_{L}^{\prime},h_{M}^{\prime})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{4}
.

{\rm Hom}_{\mathfrak{g}}(V(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M}),V(c_{L}^{\prime},c_{M}^{\prime},h_{L}^{\prime},h_{M}^{\prime}))\neq 0

c_{M}=c_{M}^{\prime},c_{L}=c_{L}^{\prime},h_{M}=h_{M}^{\prime}
,
2h^{\prime}_{M}+\frac{p^{2}-1}{12}c^{\prime}_{M}=0
for some
p\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, and
h_{L}=h_{L}^{\prime}+ip
for some
i\in\mathbb{N}
(when
p
even) or
i\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{N}
(when

odd).

We know that
{\rm Hom}_{\mathfrak{g}}(V(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M}),V(c_{L}^{\prime},c_{M}^{\prime},h_{L}^{\prime},h_{M}^{\prime}))\neq 0
if and only if there is a non-zero
\mathfrak{g}
-module homomorphism

\varphi:V(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M})=\langle{\bf 1}\rangle\to V(c_{L}^{\prime},c_{M}^{\prime},h_{L}^{\prime},h_{M}^{\prime})=\langle{\bf 1^{\prime}}\rangle,

\varphi({\bf 1})=u{\bf 1^{\prime}}
is a singular vector of

for some
u\in U(\mathfrak{g}_{-})
, by Theorem
3.17
, if and only if
u={\rm S}^{k}
(
p
even) or
{\rm R}^{k}
(
p
odd) for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
. So
c_{M}=c_{M}^{\prime},c_{L}=c_{L}^{\prime},h_{M}=h_{M}^{\prime}
and
h_{L}=h_{L}^{\prime}+ip
for some
i\in\mathbb{N}
(when
p
even) or

(when

.

4.
Classification of subsingular vectors of Verma modules

In this section, we continue considering reducible Verma modules
V(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M})
over
\mathfrak{g}
for fixed

(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{4}
.
So we always assume that
\phi(p)=2h_{M}+\frac{p^{2}-1}{12}c_{M}=0
for some
p\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
with
c_{M}\neq 0
. We will determine all subsingular vectors in the Verma module
V(h_{L},h_{M},c_{L},c_{M})
.

Let
J^{\prime}(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M})
be the submodule of
V(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M})
generated by all singular vectors.
Set

L^{\prime}(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M})=V(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M})/J^{\prime}(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M}).

By Theorem
3.17
,
J^{\prime}(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M})
is generated by
u={\rm S}{\bf 1}
if
p\in 2\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, by
u={\rm R}{\bf 1}
if
p\in 2\mathbb{Z}_{+}-1
, defined in Section 3.

For convenience, for
x\in V(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M})
we will abuse the notation that
x\in L^{\prime}(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M})
means
x+J^{\prime}(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M})\in L^{\prime}(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M})
.

4.1.
Necessary condition for the existence of subsingular vectors

From the construction of
{\rm R}
and
{\rm S}
we have the following results.

Lemma 4.1
.

(1) If
p\in 2\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, then the image of

\displaystyle{\mathcal{B}}=\{M_{-\lambda}Q_{-\mu}L_{-\nu}{\bf 1}\mid\lambda,\nu\in\mathcal{P},\mu\in\mathcal{SP},\ \mbox{and}\ M_{-\lambda}\ \mbox{does't involve }\ M_{-p}\}

(4.1)

under the natural projection

\pi:V(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M})\rightarrow L^{\prime}(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M})=V(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M})/J^{\prime}(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M})

forms a PBW basis of
L^{\prime}(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M})
.

(2) If
p\in 2\mathbb{Z}_{+}-1
, then the image of

{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}=\{M_{-\lambda}Q_{-\mu}L_{-\nu}{\bf 1}\mid\lambda,\nu\in\mathcal{P},\mu\in\mathcal{SP},\ \mbox{and}\ \ Q_{-\mu},M_{-\lambda}\ \mbox{does't involve }\ Q_{-\frac{p}{2}},M_{-p}\ \mbox{respectively}\}

(4.2)

under the natural projection
\pi
forms a PBW basis of
L^{\prime}(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M})
.

Lemma 4.2
.

If
L^{\prime}(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M})
is reducible and
u^{\prime}
is a singular vector not in
\mathbb{C}{\bf 1}
, then
{\rm hm}(u^{\prime})=L_{-p}^{r}{\bf 1}
for some
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, and
\ell_{L}(u^{\prime})=r
.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.1
, we may assume that any term of
u^{\prime}
does not involve
M_{-p}
or
Q_{-\frac{p}{2}}
(this factor does not appear if
p
is even).

If
\ell_{L}(u^{\prime})=0
, using similar discussions in Section 3 (see the beginning part of the proof of Lemma
3.5
, and Theorem
3.6
), we can get
u^{\prime}\in J^{\prime}(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M})
, a contradiction.

So
\ell_{L}(u^{\prime})\neq 0
, and suppose that

u^{\prime}=(g_{0}L_{-p}^{r}+g_{1}L_{-p}^{r-1}+g_{2}L_{-p}^{r-2}+\cdots+g_{r}){\bf 1},

where
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},g_{i}\in U(\mathfrak{g}_{-}),i=0,1,\cdots,r,g_{0}\neq 0
and any
g_{i}
does not involve
L_{-p},M_{-p},Q_{-\frac{p}{2}}
.

By the proof of Lemma
3.7
(ignoring the eigenvalue of
u^{\prime}
), we can get
{\ell}_{L}(g_{0})=0
.
Using the proof of Lemma
3.5
, we have
{\ell}_{Q}(g_{0})=0
. So
g_{0}\in U(\mathcal{M}_{-})
. Now we need to show that
g_{0}\in\mathbb{C}
.

(1) First we consider the case of
p=1
. Note that
h_{M}=0
, hence
[L_{-1},M_{1}]=0
. If
\ell_{L}(g_{1})\neq 0
. Set
{\rm hm}(g_{1})=b(M,Q)L_{-\nu}
for some
b(M,Q)\in U(\mathcal{M}_{-}+\mathcal{Q}_{-})
. Then
\nu_{1}>1
.
By the action of
M_{\nu_{1}}
on
u^{\prime}
, we can get a contradiction by comparing the coefficient of
L_{-1}^{r-1}
. So
\ell_{L}(g_{1})=0
. Similarly, we have
\ell_{L}(g_{2})=\cdots=\ell_{L}(g_{r})=0
since
M_{0}L_{-1}^{j}{\bf 1}=0,M_{k}L_{-1}^{j}{\bf 1}=0
for any
k,j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
(Theorem
2.3
).

If
g_{0}\notin\mathbb{C}
, set
{\rm hm}\,(g_{0})=M_{-\mu}
not involving
M_{-1}
, then

L_{\mu_{1}}u^{\prime}=[L_{\mu_{1}},g_{0}]L_{-1}^{r}+B,

where the degree of
L_{-1}
in
B
is no more than
r-1
and
[L_{\mu_{1}},M_{-\mu}]{\bf 1}\neq 0
. It gets a contradiction.
So
g_{0}\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
. Consequently,
{\rm hm}(u^{\prime})=L_{-1}^{r}{\bf 1}
. In this case,
g_{1}=0
since
g_{1}
is not involving
M_{-1},Q_{-\frac{1}{2}}
.

(2) Now we consider the case of
p>1
.

As in Lemma
3.7
and Lemma
3.5
(using
M_{1}
instead of
M_{0}
in the arguments), we get

\ell_{L}(g_{1})=1\ {\rm and}\ g_{1}=\sum_{i=1}^{s}b_{i}L_{-i}+b_{0},

where
b_{i}\in,i=1,\cdots,s
,
b_{s}\neq 0
and
s<p
,
b_{0}\in\mathcal{MQ}
.

Moreover, we can get

\displaystyle\ell_{L}(g_{i})=i

for
i=1,\cdots,r
by induction, and all
L_{-\nu}
in
g_{i},i\geq 1
must be satisfied the condition that
\nu_{1}<p
(see the proof of Lemma
3.7
using
M_{1}
instead
M_{0}
in the arguments).
In this case
\ell_{L}(u^{\prime})=r
.

Now we shall prove that
g_{0}\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
. Otherwise, set
{\rm hm}\,(g_{0})=M_{-\mu}
not involving
M_{-p}
, then

L_{\mu_{1}}u^{\prime}=[L_{\mu_{1}},g_{0}]L_{-p}^{r}+B,

where the degree of
L_{-p}
in
B
is no more than
r-1
and
[L_{\mu_{1}},M_{-\mu}]{\bf 1}\neq 0
. It gets a contradiction.
The lemma follows.
∎

Lemma
4.2
tells us that, if there exists a singular vector
u^{\prime}\in L^{\prime}(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M})
of weight
pr
, then it is unique up to a scalar multiple.
In the following, we provide the necessary conditions for the existence of subsingular vectors in the Verma module
V(h_{L},h_{M},c_{L},c_{M})
over the N=1 BMS superalgebra
\mathfrak{g}
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{4}
such that
\phi(p)=2h_{M}+\frac{p^{2}-1}{12}c_{M}=0
for some
p\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and
c_{M}\neq 0
. Assume that there exists a singular vector
u^{\prime}\in L^{\prime}(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M})

such that
{\rm hm}(u^{\prime})=L_{-p}^{r}{\bf 1}
for some
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
.
Then
h_{L}=h_{p,r}
where

\displaystyle h_{p,r}=-\frac{p^{2}-1}{24}c_{L}+\frac{(41p+5)(p-1)}{48}+\frac{(1-r)p}{2}-\frac{1+(-1)^{p}}{8}p.

(4.3)

Proof.

Case 1
:
p=1
. From the proof of Lemma
4.2
, we can suppose that

u^{\prime}=(L_{-1}^{r}+g_{2}L_{-1}^{r-2}+\cdots+g_{r-1}L_{-1}+g_{r}){\bf 1},

where
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, each
g_{i}\in U(\mathcal{M}_{-}+\mathcal{Q}_{-})
does not involve
M_{-1},Q_{-\frac{1}{2}}
for
i=1,2,\cdots,r
.
Considering the coefficient of
L_{-1}^{r-1}
in
L_{1}u^{\prime}

and using the formula

L_{1}L_{-1}^{r}{\bf 1}=L_{-1}^{r-1}\left(rL_{0}+\frac{r(r-1)}{2}\right){\bf 1}

we can get
h_{L}=\frac{1-r}{2}
by comparing the coefficient of
L_{-1}^{r-1}
.

Case 2
:
p>1
.
From Lemma
4.2
, we can suppose that

u^{\prime}=(L_{-p}^{r}+g_{1}L_{-p}^{r-1}+\cdots+g_{r-1}L_{-p}+g_{r}){\bf 1},

where
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
,
g_{i}\in U(\mathfrak{g}_{-}),i=1,2,\cdots,r
do not involve
L_{-p},M_{-p},Q_{-\frac{p}{2}}
.
By Lemma
4.2
, we can further assume that

g_{1}=\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}l_{i}M_{-i}L_{-(p-i)}+\sum_{j=1}^{\lfloor\frac{p}{2}\rfloor}n_{i}Q_{-p+i-\frac{1}{2}}Q_{-i+\frac{1}{2}}+C,

(4.4)

where
l_{i},n_{j}\in\mathbb{C}
and

C=\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i=1,2,\cdots,p-1}}{{\ell(\lambda)\geq 2}}}a_{\lambda,i}M_{-\lambda}L_{-i}+\sum_{\ell(\lambda)+\ell(\mu)\geq 3}b_{\lambda,\mu}M_{-\lambda}Q_{-\mu+\frac{1}{2}}

(4.5)

for some
a_{\mu,i},b_{\lambda,\mu}\in\mathbb{C}
.

For any
k=1,2,\cdots,p-1
,

\displaystyle L_{k}u^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[L_{k},L_{-p}^{r}+g_{1}L_{-p}^{r-1}+\cdots+g_{r-1}L_{-p}+g_{r}]\mathbf{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle([L_{k},L_{-p}^{r}]+[L_{k},g_{1}]L_{-p}^{r-1}+B){\bf 1},

where the degree of
L_{-p}
in
B
is less than
r-2
.
The coefficient with
L_{-p}^{r-1}{\bf 1}
in
L_{k}u^{\prime}
should be zero.

Comparing the coefficients of
L_{-p+k}L_{-p}^{r-1}
in
L_{k}u^{\prime}
, we can get

r(k+p)+l_{k}(2kh_{M}+\frac{k^{3}-k}{12}c_{M})=0
, yielding that

\displaystyle l_{k}=-r\frac{p^{2}-1}{2h_{M}k(p-k)}

for
k=1,\ldots,p-1
. Note that here the degree of
L_{-p}
of
[L_{k},C]L_{-p}^{r-1}{\bf 1}
is
r-1
, or
r-2
. For the former, the length of any non-zero summand
in
[L_{k},C]
is not less than
2
with respect to
M
(see (
4.5
)).

For any
k=1,2,\cdots,\lfloor\frac{p}{2}\rfloor
, comparing the coefficients of
Q_{-p+k-\frac{1}{2}}L_{-p}^{r-1}
in
Q_{k-\frac{1}{2}}u^{\prime}
, we obtain that

\frac{p+2k-1}{2}+\left(2h_{M}-\frac{8(k^{2}-k)h_{M}}{p^{2}-1}\right)n_{k}=0
, yielding that

\displaystyle n_{k}=r\frac{p^{2}-1}{4h_{M}(p-2k+1)}.

Note that

\displaystyle[L_{p},L_{-p}^{r}]=rpL_{-p}^{r-1}\Big((r-1)p+2L_{0}+\frac{p^{2}-1}{12}c_{L}\Big).

The coefficient with
L_{-p}^{r-1}{\bf 1}
in

{\rm T}_{p,1}={\rm T}
and
[M_{0},{\rm T}]=p{\rm S}
. Now we assume that
r>1
.
By (
4.19
), we have
M_{(r-1)p}{\rm T}_{p,r}{\bf 1}=M_{(r-1)p}{\rm T}^{r}{\bf 1}+v_{1}M_{(r-1)p}{\rm T}^{r-1}{\bf 1}
.

By Lemma
4.11
(b) and by induction on
k\geq 1
we see that

M_{kp}{\rm T}^{k}{\bf 1}=U(\mathcal{M})\left(M_{0}+\frac{1}{24}(p^{2}-1)c_{M}\right){\bf 1}=0.

By induction on
k\geq 1
and using Lemma
4.11
(b), we can prove that
M_{(k-1)p+j}{\rm T}^{k}{\bf 1}=0
for any
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
.

Now by induction on
k\geq 2
we will prove that
M_{(k-1)p}{\rm T}^{k}{\bf 1}=k!p^{k}{\rm S}{\bf 1}
. This is clear for
k=2
by direct computations. We compute that

\displaystyle M_{(k-1)p}{\rm T}^{k}{\bf 1}=

\displaystyle[M_{(k-1)p},{\rm T}]{\rm T}^{k-1}{\bf 1}+{\rm T}M_{(k-1)p}{\rm T}^{k-1}{\bf 1}=[M_{(k-1)p},{\rm T}]{\rm T}^{k-1}{\bf 1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[M_{(k-1)p},L_{-p}]{\rm T}^{k-1}{\bf 1}+\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}g_{i}(M)[M_{(k-1)p},L_{-p+i}]{\rm T}^{k-1}{\bf 1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(kp)M_{(k-2)p}{\rm T}^{k-1}{\bf 1}+\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}g_{i}(M)M_{(k-2)p+i}{\rm T}^{k-1}{\bf 1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(kp)M_{(k-2)p}{\rm T}^{k-1}{\bf 1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k!p^{k}{\rm S}{\bf 1},\,\,\,(\text{induction used}).

So (
5.1
) holds.

Now we prove (
5.2
). By induction on
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and using Lemma
4.11
(c), we can prove that

Q_{(k-1)p+j+\frac{p}{2}}{\rm T}^{k}{\bf 1}=0
for any
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
.

Now by induction on
k\geq 1
we will prove that
Q_{(k-1)p+\frac{p}{2}}{\rm T}^{k}{\bf 1}=k!p^{k}{\rm R}{\bf 1}
. This is clear for
k=1
by direct computations. We compute that

\displaystyle Q_{(k-1)p+\frac{p}{2}}{\rm T}^{k}{\bf 1}=

\displaystyle[Q_{(k-1)p+\frac{p}{2}},{\rm T}]{\rm T}^{k-1}{\bf 1}+{\rm T}Q_{(k-1)p+\frac{p}{2}}{\rm T}^{k-1}{\bf 1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[Q_{(k-1)p+\frac{p}{2}},{\rm T}]{\rm T}^{k-1}{\bf 1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle kpQ_{(k-2)p+\frac{p}{2}}{\rm T}^{k-1}{\bf 1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k!p^{k}{\rm R}{\bf 1},\,\,\,(\text{induction used}).

Then
Q_{(r-1)p+\frac{p}{2}}{\rm T}_{p,r}{\bf 1}=Q_{(r-1)p+\frac{p}{2}}{\rm T}^{r}{\bf 1}=r!p^{r}{\rm R}{\bf 1}.

∎

Theorem 5.4
.

(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L},h_{M})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{4}

We first consider the case that

5.2

\displaystyle{\rm char}\,V(c_{L},c_{M},h_{p,r},h_{M})/\langle{\rm T}_{p,r}{\bf 1}\rangle=q^{h_{p,r}}(1-q^{p})(1-q^{rp})\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1+q^{k-\frac{1}{2}}}{(1-q^{k})^{2}}.

Remark 5.5
.

From the above two theorems, unlike the Virasoro algebra, any maximal submodule of the Verma module

5.2.

In this subsection, we investigate the composition series of submodules of the Verma module

We have seen that the chain length of the submodules is infinite in the reducible Verma module

Theorem 5.6

(1) We know that
\langle{\rm S}^{n}{\bf 1}\rangle

and (
5.5

. Note that

,

. Clearly, the submodule
\langle{\rm S}^{n}{\bf 1}\rangle
is isomorphic to

with the highest weight
(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L}+np,h_{M})
.
Note that
{\rm S}^{n}{\bf 1}+\langle{\rm S}^{n+1}{\bf 1}\rangle
generates

and

. Then
\langle{\rm S}^{n}{\bf 1}\rangle/\langle{\rm S}^{n+1}{\bf 1}\rangle
is isomorphic to a highest weight module with weight

. By the calculation of character, we have

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm char}\,L(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L}+np,h_{M}).

By Theorem
5.3
,
\langle{\rm S}^{n+1}{\bf 1}\rangle
is the maximal submodule of
\langle{\rm S}^{n}{\bf 1}\rangle
and the quotient module

for
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

For Statement (2),
{\rm R}^{n}{\bf 1}
is a singular vectors in

3.16

. Note that
{\rm R}^{2n}{\bf 1}+\langle{\rm R}^{2n+1}{\bf 1}\rangle

\displaystyle{\rm char}\,L(c_{L},c_{M},h_{L}+np,h_{M}).

(2) Let

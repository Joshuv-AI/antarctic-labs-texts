Does physical exercise enhance physical appearance? An empirical study based on CFPS
Authors: Long Cui, Yumei Xing, Jia Qian, Nan Jiang, Yifeng Bu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundIn contemporary society, physical appearance significantly influences individuals' social interactions and career achievements. Although some studies suggest that physical exercise may positively impact physical appearance, most rely on subjective self-assessment data. In contrast, third-party evaluations are widely regarded as more objective and reliable. Thus, this study aims to explore the effects of physical exercise on physical appearance, as assessed by third-party evaluations, and to examine its heterogeneity.MethodsThis study utilized third-party physical appearance evaluation data from 25,460 respondents in the 2018 China Family Panel Studies (CFPS) and assessed the impact of physical exercise on physical appearance using multiple linear regression and instrumental variable analysis. Furthermore, the heterogeneity in the effects of physical exercise was investigated using subgroup regression and quantile regression analyses.ResultsThe findings indicate that physical exercise substantially enhances physical appearance, with its effects varying significantly across different populations. Specifically, women, urban residents, and those with lower appearance scores experience more pronounced enhancement from physical exercise compared to men, rural residents, and those with higher scores. Additionally, the effect of age on the impact of physical exercise on appearance exhibits an inverted U-shaped relationship. Middle-aged adults (40–59 years) experience the most significant improvements, while benefits are lower for adolescents (10–20 years), young adults (20–39 years), and older adults (60–80 years).ConclusionsThis study systematically reveals the positive impact of physical exercise on physical appearance, demonstrating that regular exercise can significantly enhance individual appearance scores. This finding contributes to a broader understanding of the multifaceted benefits of physical exercise.Keywords: Physical appearance, Physical exercise, Instrumental variable method

Introduction

In modern society, an individual's physical appearance significantly influences their social interactions and professional success. Numerous studies have shown that good appearance provides significant social advantages at different life stages. In adolescence, individuals who have a better physical appearance are more likely to build extensive social networks and gain peer recognition [1]; in youth and adulthood, these advantages translate into greater workplace competitiveness, facilitating access to career opportunities and promotions [2–4]; in middle and old age, good physical appearance contributes to self-esteem and self-confidence, and it also promotes social interaction [5].

Physical appearance is shaped by various factors, including genetics, lifestyle habits, and socio-cultural influences [6–8], with physical exercise being an important and controllable factor. Regular physical exercise boosts metabolism and enhances blood circulation, thereby aiding skin cell renewal and nutrient supply, which helps maintain skin health and slows the aging process [9, 10]. Furthermore, physical exercise helps maintain an ideal body shape by burning fat and building muscle. These physical changes directly improve an individual's appearance [11–13]. Moreover, physical activity boosts self-confidence by enhancing self-esteem and self-efficacy. This enhancement is not only evident in improved physical posture, but also in the individual's social charisma through non-verbal communication [14–17].

However, although studies have been conducted supporting the idea that physical exercise improves physical appearance, these studies have limitations. Firstly, these studies have generally relied on participants' self-assessments to judge their level of physical appearance [18–20]. This self-appraisal approach may be biased as self-assessments are influenced by personal emotions, confidence levels, and social expectations. Conversely, evaluations by others may be more objective and reliable. Therefore, this study opted to use other-rated physical appearance as an assessment criterion. Secondly, the issue of model endogeneity has rarely been considered in previous studies [19, 21]. Endogeneity issues may involve omitted variables, sample selection bias, reverse causality, and measurement errors, potentially leading to biased OLS estimates. This study aims to overcome these endogeneity problems by employing the Instrumental Variable (IV) method to achieve unbiased estimation of the effects of physical exercise on physical appearance.

In addition, this study aims to explore the heterogeneity in the impact of physical exercise across different populations. Specifically, subgroup regression analyses will be conducted based on gender, Residence (urban vs. rural), and various age groups, including adolescents (10–19 years), young adults (20–39 years), middle-aged adults (40–59 years), and older adults (60–80 years). Meanwhile, quantile regression will be employed to assess the specific effects of physical exercise on individuals with varying levels of physical appearance, aiming to reveal the potential impact of exercise on enhancing appearance and its magnitude of change. In summary, this study aims to comprehensively assess the effects of physical exercise on physical appearance and explore the potential factors affecting its effectiveness. This study seeks to provide more in-depth and detailed insights into the relationship between physical exercise and physical appearance through scientifically rigorous methodologies and comprehensive data analysis.

Data and methods

Data sources

The data were sourced from the 2018 China Family Panel Studies (CFPS), conducted by the Institute of Social Science Survey of Peking University. The CFPS is a significant survey project organized by the Institute of Social Sciences at Peking University, which aims to comprehensively collect various types of information from participants through multiple survey instruments (including village, household, and individual questionnaires), thus ensuring that the dataset is high-quality and broadly representative. The CFPS survey encompasses 25 provincial-level administrative regions, representing 95% of China's total population, thereby making the CFPS sample nationally representative [22]. The CFPS project has received approval from the Biomedical Ethics Committee of Peking University.

Considering that advanced age may significantly affect appearance, this study limited participants' ages to between 10 and 80 years to ensure sample homogeneity. After applying this age range criterion for screening, a final valid sample of 25,460 was obtained. This screening strategy enhanced the comparability of the samples and provided a solid foundation for accurately assessing the effects of physical exercise on physical appearance.

Variables

Physical appearance

In the existing literature, three main methods are typically used to obtain appearance evaluation data. The first method involves experimental approaches, which collect photos of study subjects for professional assessment across multiple dimensions, such as biological structure, social psychology, and aesthetic culture, or use multi-round anonymous evaluations to obtain more objective appearance data [23, 24]. This method offers the highest level of professionalism and objectivity but has significant drawbacks, including high survey costs and limited sample sizes. Additionally, such data, typically collected by research institutions, are not easily accessible to others. The second method involves respondents' self-assessment of their appearance, which is the least costly and allows for larger sample sizes, but the accuracy and objectivity of the evaluations are difficult to ensure. The third method involves interviewers assessing respondents' appearance as part of large-scale social surveys. For instance, the U.S. Quality of Employment Survey (QES) [3], the U.S. National Longitudinal Study of Adolescent Health (Add Health) [25], and the German General Social Survey (ALLBUS) [26] all included physical attractiveness evaluations. Compared to the first two methods, this approach allows for sufficient sample sizes, and trained interviewers can provide relatively objective evaluations. As a result, respondent appearance data from large-scale social surveys are widely used [25, 27].

The CFPS data used in this study also include appearance scores evaluated during the survey. Specifically, during the household survey, interviewers were required to use a 1 to 7 scale to score respondents' appearance, with 1 indicating very poor appearance, 7 indicating excellent appearance, and each higher score reflecting a progressively better evaluation.

Physical exercise

CFPS collected data on respondents' physical exercise behaviors using two questions: "Frequency of exercise last week" and "Total exercise duration last week." Drawing on methods from existing literature [28, 29], this study uses the average daily exercise time as an indicator of an individual's physical exercise status. In calculating the average daily exercise time, we first excluded outlier samples reporting "more than 10 h per exercise session." The average daily exercise time was then calculated using the formula "Average daily exercise time = Weekly exercise duration / 7." To normalize the distribution of the independent variable and include samples with an average daily exercise time of 0, we added 0.01 to the average daily exercise time and then applied the natural logarithm, creating a continuous variable to measure physical exercise [28]. A higher value of this variable indicates a greater level of an individual's participation in physical exercise.

Control variables

According to prior research [30], this study leverages the rich information contained in the survey data and systematically selects control variables across four levels: demographic, socio-economic, health, and regional characteristics, to mitigate potential confounders and accurately estimate the net effect of physical exercise. Demographic control variables comprise age (10–80 years), binary gender (Men = 1, Women = 0), residence (Urban = 1, Rural = 0), and marital status (Married = 1, Unmarried = 0) [31]. Socio-economic control variables encompass educational level (Illiterate = 1, Elementary = 2, Junior High = 3, High School = 4, Associate Degree = 5, Bachelor's Degree = 6, Master's Degree = 7, PhD = 8) and economic status (measured by per capita family income, in CNY). Health control variables consist of BMI index (weight divided by height squared), health status (1–5, from unhealthy to very healthy, based on the respondent's self-assessment), and smoking habits (smoker = 1, non-smoker = 0). Regional characteristic control variables utilize provincial codes (treated as dummy variables in subsequent analyses). Results

Effects of physical exercise on physical appearance

Based on the full sample estimation results, there is a significant positive correlation between physical exercise and physical appearance scores (see Table 2). Specifically, for every 1% increase in exercise duration, the physical appearance score increases by 0.030 points, which equals 0.555% of the sample’s average physical appearance score (5.406). This finding suggests that physical exercise plays a substantial role in enhancing personal physical appearance. In addition to physical exercise, other control variables also have significant effects on individual physical appearance. Age is negatively correlated with physical appearance scores, with an average decrease of 0.013 points for each additional year of age. Although the effect for a single year is small, its negative impact can accumulate over time. There is also a significant gender difference, with men's physical appearance scores 0.065 points lower than those of women (p < 0.01). Although the effect size is small, it may reflect that women generally have higher expectations and concerns about physical appearance, while men face less pressure regarding appearance, reducing their motivation to improve it.

Improvement in health status has a significant positive impact on physical appearance scores, with a coefficient of 0.119 (p < 0.01). Individuals with better health typically exhibit healthier skin tone and physique, contributing positively to their physical appearance. In contrast, smoking habits have a significant negative effect on physical appearance scores, with a coefficient of -0.062 (p < 0.01), likely due to the adverse effects of smoking on skin and overall health. Heterogeneity in the effects of physical exercise on physical appearance

Robustness tests for the role of physical exercise: based on IV-2SLS estimation

In the field of social and behavioral sciences, numerous factors such as omitted variables, sample selection bias, reverse causality, and measurement errors can affect the reliability of regression analysis. For instance, physical appearance might inversely influence an individual's participation in sports, leading to the emergence of reverse causality. Moreover, if certain unobservable variables, such as family background, affect both physical exercise and physical appearance, conventional regression models may overlook these factors, leading to biased results.

To effectively address potential endogeneity issues between physical exercise and physical appearance, this study employs the Instrumental Variable (IV) method. A valid instrumental variable must meet two fundamental criteria: relevance and exogeneity. First, the relevance criterion requires that there be a substantial correlation between the instrumental variable and the main explanatory variable—physical exercise. Second, the exogeneity criterion stipulates that the instrumental variable must not be correlated with the error term of the model, nor should it directly affect the dependent variable—physical appearance. In this study, the chosen instrumental variable is the family-level rate of participation in physical exercise (excluding the individual).1 The rationale for selecting this variable as an instrument is based on the following: The family-level rate of participation in physical activities reflects the overall attitude and involvement of the family in sports, which is positively correlated with the exercise behaviors of its members. Furthermore, there is currently no evidence to suggest that the family-level rate of participation in physical exercise (excluding the individual) directly influences an individual's physical appearance. Therefore, this instrumental variable effectively meets the criteria of relevance and exogeneity and is suitable for the analytical framework of this study.

Table 3 reports in detail the results of the two-stage least squares (2SLS) analysis. The report is divided into two parts: Panel A displays the results of the first stage, while Panel B reports the results of the second stage. In the first stage, the F-value significantly exceeds the empirical threshold of 10 [35], indicating that the chosen instrumental variable does not suffer from the weak instrument problem. Further analysis shows that, after controlling for other relevant variables, the family-level rate of participation in physical exercise (excluding the individual) significantly and positively affects individual physical exercise at the 1% statistical level, further confirming the appropriateness of the chosen instrumental variable. Moreover, the results of the Durbin-Wu-Hausman test for endogeneity indicate that the hypothesis that "all variables are exogenous" can be rejected at the 1% statistical level, showing that physical exercise is an endogenous explanatory variable and requires estimation using the Instrumental Variable (IV) method. From the estimates of the second stage, physical exercise has a significant positive effect on physical appearance at the 1% statistical level. Overall, the estimates obtained using the Instrumental Variable (IV) method are consistent with the results of the benchmark regression analysis, indicating that, after controlling for potential endogeneity issues, the positive effect of physical exercise on physical appearance is empirically supported, thereby validating the reliability of the OLS regression results.

Robustness tests for the role of physical exercise: replacing the dependent variable

Although other-rated physical appearance is more objective than self-rated appearance, and regional effects were controlled in the analysis to reduce differences among interviewers (in the CFPS project, respondents from the same region are assessed by the same group of interviewers, leading to more consistent scoring standards), appearance scores may still be influenced by interviewers' subjective factors. To address this, this study replaces the dependent variable using two methods to perform robustness tests on the benchmark regression.

First, due to potential subtle differences in scoring standards among interviewers, the original appearance scores are reclassified into three categories: "ordinary appearance," "better appearance," and "best appearance." This reclassification aims to reduce differences in scoring standards among interviewers, thereby alleviating rating discrepancies to some extent. The original CFPS appearance scores range from 1 to 7; based on related studies, scores of 1–5 are defined as "ordinary appearance," a score of 6 as "better appearance," and a score of 7 as "best appearance" [32].

Second, since the interviewers for the CFPS projects in 2016 and 2018 were different individuals, this study uses the average appearance scores from both periods to replace the dependent variable, mitigating the subjectivity of individual interviewers' scores. The results of Model (2) and Model (3) in Table 4 indicate that both the reclassified results and the average score results support the benchmark regression, showing that physical exercise significantly contributes to the enhancement of appearance.

Specifically, although the enhancement effect of physical exercise on women (β = 0.031, p < 0.01) is slightly higher than that on men (β = 0.030, p < 0.01), the difference is minimal (see Table 5). Additionally, from the perspective of residence, physical exercise significantly improves the physical appearance of both urban and rural residents (p < 0.01). However, urban residents (β = 0.036, p < 0.01) experience a more significant improvement in physical appearance compared to rural residents (β = 0.025, p < 0.01).

Effects of physical exercise on physical appearance: the impact on individual appearance levels

OLS regression provides only the average effect of physical exercise on appearance, while quantile regression more precisely reveals the impact of explanatory variables on the dependent variable at different distribution levels, aiding our understanding of the heterogeneity of physical exercise's effects across different levels of appearance. In this study, we selected the 0.25, 0.5, and 0.75 quantiles of physical appearance scores, representing individuals at low-medium, medium, and medium–high levels of physical appearance, respectively. The results show that at the 0.25, 0.5, and 0.75 quantiles, the impact parameter estimates of physical exercise on physical appearance are 0.041 (p < 0.01), 0.030 (p < 0.01), and 0.013 (p < 0.1), respectively (see Table 6). Overall, as physical appearance scores increase, the enhancing effect of physical exercise on physical appearance shows a declining trend.

Discussion

As far as we know, this is the first study to explore the impact of physical exercise on others' evaluations of physical appearance. The findings suggest that regular physical exercise can significantly enhance an individual's physical appearance. For every 1% increase in time spent on physical exercise, physical appearance scores increase by 0.030 points, equating to 0.555% of the average score (5.406) in the sample. Notably, the positive impact of physical exercise on physical appearance varies significantly among different demographic groups. Women, urban residents, middle-aged adults (40–59 years old), and individuals with lower initial physical appearance scores experience more significant improvements through physical exercise compared to men, rural residents, adolescents (10–20 years old), young adults (20–39 years old), older adults (60–80 years old), and those with higher initial appearance scores. This study broadens the public's understanding of the benefits of physical exercise, not only in terms of enhancing health and physical fitness, but also in terms of improving physical appearance. These findings offer a new perspective for a more comprehensive understanding of the multiple benefits of physical exercise.

This study effectively isolated potential confounders between physical exercise and physical appearance scores, such as genetic predispositions and early life experiences, by using the Instrumental Variable (IV) method, thereby ensuring the reliability of the results. The Instrumental Variable (IV) method revealed that physical exercise significantly enhances individuals' physical appearance scores. Although previous studies did not directly explore the connection between physical exercise and evaluations of physical appearance by others, several mechanisms could help explain this outcome. Psychologically, physical exercise can increase self-confidence and reduce anxiety, which can be expressed through more positive and open body language, potentially enhancing an individual's outward charm [14, 36, 37]. Furthermore, physiologically, regular physical exercise can decrease body fat, increase muscle mass and improve skin hydration, all of which may affect evaluations of physical appearance [38–40]. However, it should be recognized that while most people can significantly improve their body shape through physical exercise, some individuals may experience minimal changes in weight or body shape despite regular exercise. This outcome arises from the fact that alterations in weight and body shape are not only influenced by exercise and diet but are also constrained by uncontrollable factors such as genetics and metabolism [41]. Therefore, when using physical exercise as a means to enhance physical appearance, individual differences should be carefully considered, with the understanding that improvements in physical appearance are determined by multiple factors.

Heterogeneity analysis results indicate that women experience slightly greater improvements in physical appearance scores through physical exercise compared to men. This gender difference may be closely related to women's heightened concerns about appearance and weight [42]. Studies suggest that men's motivation to participate in physical exercise is primarily intrinsic, such as increasing strength, pursuing competition, and facing challenges, whereas women tend to be more influenced by external factors, such as weight control and appearance improvement [43, 44]. This could lead women to focus more on improving weight and body shape during exercise, thereby achieving more significant improvements in appearance. This finding aligns with existing research, which indicates that women generally reap more benefits in self-perceived improvements in appearance after engaging in physical exercise [19, 45]. Additionally, the research finds that urban residents gain significantly greater improvements in physical appearance scores from physical exercise compared to rural residents. This difference may stem from greater accessibility to fitness facilities, more varied forms of exercise, and better fitness services in urban environments. Urban residents usually have more opportunities to engage in organized sports activities and receive professional health guidance, which helps them more effectively improve their physique and appearance [46, 47]. Simultaneously, the urban culture's emphasis on appearance and body aesthetics further encourages residents to focus on these aspects during exercise routines [48]. The advantage of urban residents in physical exercise may stem from China's unique urban–rural dual structure. In contrast, the level of urban development far exceeds that of rural areas, and from 2000 to 2020, the urban–rural income gap ratio in China has consistently remained above 2.5 [49], which may affect urban and rural residents' access to and utilization of health and fitness resources.

Existing studies have demonstrated that physical exercise has beneficial effects on the skin, physique, and mental state across various age groups, all of which may influence evaluations of physical appearance [50–54]. This study is the first to systematically compare and evaluate the variations in the impact of physical exercise on physical appearance across age groups, revealing an inverted U-shaped relationship with age. Specifically, in children and adolescents under 20, the effect of physical exercise on physical appearance is relatively minor. This phenomenon may be related to the rapid physiological development and hormonal changes occurring in children and adolescents of this age group [55], which could mask the external effects of physical exercise. However, as age increases to the young adult stage of 20 to 39 years, due to slowed metabolism and changes in body shape [56], physical exercise begins to significantly enhance the positive impact on physical appearance by promoting healthy weight management and maintaining muscle tone. Entering the middle-aged adult stage of 40 to 59 years, the beneficial impacts of physical exercise on physical appearance reach their peak, mainly due to continuous and regular exercise helping to maintain a stable weight and healthy skin condition [10, 57]. However, in the older adult stage of 60 to 80 years, although physical exercise continues to have a positive impact on physical appearance, its effects are relatively diminished. This may be due to the reduced daily activity of older adults and the natural decline in physiological functions [58], which limit the benefits of exercise. Although physical exercise can still improve the quality of life and delay some aging characteristics, it cannot fully offset the physiological and physical appearance changes that occur in the natural aging process. Therefore, the findings of this study not only deepen our understanding of the impact of physical exercise on physical appearance at different life stages but also highlight the importance of designing personalized exercise programs for specific age groups to maximize the cosmetic and health benefits of physical exercise.

Additionally, the quantile regression method used in this study reveals a significant phenomenon: as physical appearance scores increase, the beneficial impacts of physical exercise on physical appearance gradually diminish. This trend may be because individuals with already high physical appearance scores have less potential for improvement in their appearance; thus, even with increased exercise, the improvement in their appearance is not as noticeable as for individuals starting from a lower point. This finding aligns with the theory of diminishing marginal returns, which states that as investment increases, the additional benefits derived gradually decrease [59]. This phenomenon suggests that the baseline of physical appearance is an important factor in the impact of physical exercise on physical appearance.

Despite the valuable insights provided by this study regarding the relationship between physical exercise and physical appearance, several limitations exist. First, the appearance data were evaluated by interviewers. While regional effects were controlled to reduce biases arising from differences in scoring standards, and robustness tests were conducted using instrumental variable (IV) methods and alternative dependent variables, respondents' appearance scores may still have been influenced by interviewers' subjective attitudes. Second, since the questionnaire did not distinguish between the duration of different types of physical exercise, the distinct effects of various activities could not be identified. Future research should introduce more rigorous experimental controls to assess the impact of physical exercise on appearance more accurately. Additionally, future studies should explore the specific effects of different types of exercise to gain a comprehensive understanding of their impact on appearance.

Conclusion

This study demonstrates that physical exercise significantly enhances physical appearance, with noticeable differences among various demographic groups. Specifically, women, urban residents, and individuals with initially lower physical appearance scores benefit more significantly from physical exercise compared to men, rural residents, and those with initially higher scores. Moreover, the study reveals an inverted U-shaped correlation between age and the effectiveness of physical exercise in improving physical appearance. Specifically, through exercise, middle-aged adults (40–59 years old) see the most significant improvement in appearance, while the improvement is relatively lower in adolescents (10–20 years old), young adults (20–39 years old), and older adults (60–80 years old). These findings offer critical insights into the extent to which physical exercise impacts individual physical appearance, underscoring the necessity of personalized physical activity plans to maximize benefits across different populations.

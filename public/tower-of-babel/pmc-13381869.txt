ABSTRACT
AbstractNear-limit emergency maneuvers are dominated by nonlinear tire saturation and stability constraints, calling for a short-horizon handling envelope that can be queried online. We formulate a stability-constrained finite-time reachability tube for a planar single-track vehicle with Magic-Formula tires. For a discretized primitive library and an environment channel , where denotes tire–road friction, s road grade, and w a crosswind surrogate, the tube aggregates all states that remain dynamically admissible and reachable for . We prove compactness and, under a local robustness-of-admissibility condition, a Hausdorff-continuity result, and derive sampling-complexity scaling with time resolution, geometric tolerance, and environment dimension. Offline, we construct tube slices using analytic stability screening and nonconvex -shape reconstruction in a normalized feature space, enabling fast membership queries for online feasibility screening. The main high-fidelity benchmark is carried out on the nominal flat-road/no-wind slice with friction-only variation; the Supplementary Information extends the study to fixed grade-only, wind-only, and combined off-nominal slices, and includes a stratified CarSim spot-check on the separated grade-only and wind-only cases. On an extreme-scenario benchmark, the tube screen provides a conservative triage gate and deployment-oriented diagnostics, including conflict-time statistics, risk–coverage frontiers, baseline comparisons, and guard-band sensitivity. Introduction

Near-limit emergency maneuvers (hard braking with steering, rapid lane changes on low friction, abrupt friction drops) are governed by strongly nonlinear vehicle dynamics, tire saturation and stability constraints. For active safety and motion planning, it is therefore useful to characterize, over a short horizon, the set of states that are physically reachable while remaining dynamically admissible. Classical handling envelopes are often steady-state or terminal-time constructs and may certify reachability at without enforcing path-wise admissibility over .

We address this gap with a finite-time reachability tube that acts as a dynamic handling envelope. Instead of enumerating external traffic configurations, we take a vehicle-centric view: given an initial state and bounded control actions, what states can the nonlinear vehicle model reach within the next T seconds while satisfying interpretable stability limits (sideslip, yaw-rate margin, load transfer ratio and adhesion)? Stacking reachable sets across time yields a state–time object that (i) rules out trajectories that temporarily leave the admissible region and (ii) supports online membership queries for downstream decision-making.

Reliability-engineering viewpoint

We interpret tube membership as a risk-managed feasibility screen (triage gate) for near-horizon emergency maneuvers. An accept decision means that the requested state/time requirement is not ruled out by the tube under the assumed model and environment, and therefore the maneuver is admitted into a candidate set for downstream handling (e.g., higher-fidelity re-simulation, closed-loop controller synthesis, or a runtime-assurance layer). A reject decision means “cannot support feasibility under the tube model” and is treated as fail–closed at the screening stage. Because the screen is allowed to trade coverage for a quantified unsafe-accept rate, we report not only conventional confusion-matrix scores but also risk (unsafe-accept rate within the accepted set) and coverage (fraction accepted), and we select operating points from risk–coverage sweep frontiers (Fig. 6, Table 1). We further quantify sensitivity to friction mis-estimation and model mismatch to support reliability and safety-case discussions (Supplementary Sections S11–S14). The proposed tube is built for a planar single–track model with Magic–Formula tires and an environment channel collecting tire–road friction, road grade and a cross-wind surrogate. In the main empirical study we instantiate the nominal flat-road, no-wind slice and vary friction only (i.e., ). Supplementary Information reports additional fixed grade-only, wind-only, and combined off-nominal slice studies, together with a small CarSim spot-check on the separated grade-only and wind-only cases (Supplementary Section S19, Supplementary Section S18). We compute the tube offline by enumerating a curated library of short-horizon two-segment open-loop inputs, rejecting unstable trajectories with an analytic stability filter, and reconstructing nonconvex time-slice envelopes via -shapes in a normalized four-dimensional feature space . On the analysis side, we establish compactness and a local-robustness-qualified Hausdorff continuity result for the reachable sets and the tube map in (t, e) and derive a sampling-complexity bound that quantifies how time resolution, geometric tolerance and the dimension of the environment grid affect the offline coverage burden.

A practical design choice is the short horizon T. We set to target the time window in which emergency avoidance is dominated by instantaneous vehicle physics: within a couple of seconds the vehicle must initiate a feasible brake–steer response or the opportunity to avoid a conflict may already be lost. To avoid relying on region-of-interest (ROI) entry-time statistics (which are degenerate at in our benchmark due to scenario alignment), we instead characterize a critical/conflict time (first predicted collision/minimum-distance crossing/TTC, depending on scenario type) and report its empirical CDF; in our benchmark, and (Table 2; full CDF in Supplementary Section S11). Extending the horizon substantially (e.g., ) moves the problem toward conventional prediction–planning over traffic interactions, and the corresponding reachable sets quickly inflate, reducing their usefulness as tight handling envelopes. Our tube is therefore intended as a near-horizon feasibility object that can be queried at high rate and, if desired, used in a receding-horizon manner by updating the initial state and environment estimate online.

Beyond single-vehicle feasibility, tubes provide a compact interface between nonlinear dynamics and interaction-aware planning. If an ego vehicle can estimate (or communicate) other agents’ -tubes, a planner can treat them as conservative dynamic occupancy regions over [0, T] and generate trajectories that maintain separation from these reachable sets, rather than relying solely on point predictions. This shifts emergency motion planning toward geometric clearance with dynamics-consistent constraints, and it is compatible with runtime assurance and safety-filter architectures1,2.

A motivating application is rare, safety-critical (“long-tail”) situations: statistically validating the tail is limited by the “curse of rarity”3 and by the scale of mileage required for field testing4,5. Rare-event simulation can reduce this burden by targeted sampling, but it still relies on modelling assumptions and careful distribution design6. In our setting, once a situation is mapped to a required terminal feature and a critical time, feasibility reduces to a set-membership query in the tube. This converts a high-dimensional combinatorial scene space into a compact state–time feasibility object rooted in nonlinear dynamics, and it provides a principled way to separate the “vehicle-physics” part of feasibility from the surrounding traffic combinatorics.

Contributions

Our main contributions, oriented toward nonlinear vehicle dynamics and set-valued analysis, are:

Dynamic handling envelope via stability-constrained reachability tubes. We formalise a finite-time reachability tube for planar vehicle models under an environment channel and stability constraints, and interpret it as a dynamic handling envelope over [0, T]. The main numerical study instantiates the nominal slice and varies friction only; Supplementary Information reports additional off-nominal slice studies and a small separated-slice CarSim spot-check (Supplementary Section S19, Supplementary Section S18).

Regularity and coverage. We show that per-slice reachable sets are compact and that, under a local robustness-of-admissibility condition made explicit in the Supplementary Information, (and ) are Hausdorff-continuous over compact environment domains, enabling finite tube libraries with explicit coverage guarantees.

Complexity scaling. We derive a sampling-complexity bound for approximating within Hausdorff distance : polynomial in time resolution and state/geometry resolution, and scaling as for an m-dimensional environment grid (hence exponential in m, as expected for -nets). We instantiate (friction only) in the main numerical study and contrast this structured coverage with combinatorial growth in scenario enumeration.

Computable construction with nonconvex geometry. We design an analytic stability filter built from sideslip, yaw-rate, load-transfer, and adhesion limits, together with a lightweight frozen-time Jacobian pre-filter, and a sample-based reconstruction pipeline using per-slice -shapes in a normalized 4D feature space together with compression for fast membership queries.

Deployment-oriented reliability diagnostics. We cast tube membership as a risk-managed feasibility screen (triage gate) and provide a deployment-oriented diagnostic stack: (i) conflict-time statistics to justify the short horizon T; (ii) a risk–coverage Pareto frontier for operating-point selection; (iii) matched-recall +speed heuristic baselines and screening-aligned baselines (online random shooting and -cover membership); (iv) g(s) sensitivity and a non-degenerate clearance guard-band knob; and (v) friction mis-estimation sensitivity and error-mode audit to motivate conservative down-binning/fail-closed fallback (summarized in Table 2 and detailed in the Supplementary Information).

The resulting tube provides a geometric handling envelope (“safety ruler”): online screening can be performed in sub-millisecond time via a lightweight 2D xy proxy as a first stage, and candidates can be confirmed in the full 4D representation when needed. All guarantees and reported metrics are defined in the full four-dimensional feature space.

Related work

Safety validation, scenario engineering, and risk quantification for automated driving

A recurring theme in the safety validation of automated driving systems is that direct mileage accumulation is an inefficient (often infeasible) path to statistically meaningful safety evidence, because severe outcomes are rare and the confidence requirements are stringent. This limitation has been quantified both from a transportation-safety perspective4 and from a system-safety viewpoint emphasizing the mismatch between traditional V-model validation and autonomy-enabled functionality5. More recently, the “curse of rarity” has been highlighted as a fundamental obstacle to conventional validation, further motivating structured, risk-aware test design that deliberately targets safety-critical regions of the operating space3.

To cope with this validation bottleneck, the literature has increasingly adopted scenario engineering and accelerated evaluation, including rare-event simulation and importance-sampling strategies6, as well as learning-driven approaches that search for adversarial or safety-critical scenarios with substantially improved sample efficiency7. Complementary contributions frame the test-generation problem through causal reasoning and structured search, with evidence that causality can improve both effectiveness and efficiency of ADS testing. In parallel, scenario generation and calibration/verification workflows have been studied in the intelligent-vehicle community8,9, reinforcing the view that practical validation pipelines must unify data-driven scenario discovery with physics- and system-level constraints.

The above trends are often connected to explicit risk modeling, structured hazard analysis, and human/system interaction concerns. Recent papers quantify explicit/implicit risks in mixed traffic10, develop quantitative risk assessment pipelines for connected and automated vehicles using STPA variants and Bayesian networks11, and propose integrated safety-risk assessment methods combining fault-tree analysis with multi-criteria decision support12. Human-in-the-loop aspects, such as takeover reliability in conditional automation, are also analyzed with system-theoretic process analysis and probabilistic human reliability models13. Moreover, under conditions of uncertainty and adversarial interference, verification may become overly conservative, motivating hybrid verification–falsification paradigms for ADAS/ADS14. These works collectively motivate safety artifacts that can (i) support scenario targeting, (ii) quantify feasibility and risk under operational constraints, and (iii) remain tractable for integration into engineering workflows.

Runtime safety assurance via safety filters, barrier certificates, and tube-based methods

At run time, safety assurance is frequently formulated as a minimally invasive modification layer that monitors and, when necessary, projects nominal control onto a certified-safe set of actions. Safety filters provide a unifying view for this paradigm and clarify relationships across model predictive safety, shielding, and set-invariance approaches1. Data-driven safety filters extend this perspective to settings where dynamics or uncertainty models are partially learned, while still aiming for enforceable safety constraints and measurable performance impacts15.

Control barrier functions (CBFs) have become a central mechanism for encoding safety as forward invariance, with recent tutorial and survey work addressing practical issues such as disturbances, sampled-data effects, and input/time constraints16. Reduced-order CBF formulations further target computational tractability for autonomous systems17. In parallel, tube-based approaches provide robust constraint satisfaction by propagating uncertainty sets and tightening constraints. This line includes homothetic tube MPC as a foundational framework18 and, more recently, vehicle-domain instantiations for highly dynamic regimes such as autonomous racing with varying friction conditions19. Related work also develops robust control invariant tubes specifically for constraint handling in autonomous vehicles2.

A key commonality across these runtime assurance methods is their reliance on computable safe sets and fast online checks. For handling-limited vehicle dynamics, constructing such safe sets remains challenging, because feasibility is governed by coupled state–input limits, transient evolution, and stability/controllability considerations. This motivates offline-computed safety artifacts that can be queried online at high rates, while retaining physical interpretability and alignment with risk-oriented validation workflows.

Reachability-based envelopes and set representations

Reachability analysis provides a principled route to safety certification by characterizing the set of states that can be reached (or avoided) under system dynamics and admissible controls. The Hamilton–Jacobi formulation has long been a cornerstone for such analyses in differential games and safety problems20, with more recent surveys emphasizing applications and computational trends in safety-critical autonomy21,22. However, exact reachability is often computationally prohibitive for nonlinear, high-dimensional vehicle models, driving sustained interest in approximation methods and representations that balance conservatism, fidelity, and query efficiency.

Recent work has explored scalable reachability for planning and verification. For example, real-time polytope propagation provides a tractable mechanism for safe trajectory planning via set propagation. Reachability-based trajectory design has been advanced through controller-parameterized forward reachable sets (FRS) using zonotopic methods, enabling online collision avoidance constraints with offline reachability computation23. Online reachability has also been integrated with reinforcement learning to provide safety guarantees for automated driving policies24. In terms of set representations, nonconvex reachable sets can be represented compactly through constructs such as hybrid zonotopes25, while boundary-focused geometric reconstructions (e.g., -shapes) offer practical approximations when sample-based reachable sets are available26.

In the vehicle domain, the broader notion of a safe operating envelope aims to delimit physically admissible states and maneuvers under stability and constraint considerations27, and surveys of global stability analysis highlight how stability boundaries can inform feasible regions and control design28. Against this landscape, stability-constrained finite-time reachability tubes can be viewed as an offline-computed, physically grounded envelope artifact that supports risk-aware feasibility screening, scenario targeting, and deployment-time diagnostics while maintaining a clear linkage to vehicle constraints and safety margins. Several ingredients of our pipeline are established in the literature—single-track vehicle modelling, Magic-Formula tires, motion-primitive simulation, -shape reconstruction, and runtime safety filtering. Our contribution is therefore not to claim any one ingredient in isolation, but to organize them into a specific object and use case that, to our knowledge, are not treated together in prior work: a finite-time, stability-constrained reachability tube indexed by an explicit environment channel , equipped with regularity/coverage results, an offline-to-online compression pipeline, and a deployment-oriented screening evaluation based on risk–coverage operating points under matched screening semantics. Relative to HJ reachability, our construction is approximate but computationally lightweight; relative to CBF/tube-MPC safety filters, it is a precomputed feasibility prior rather than an online controller-synthesis layer.

Friction monotonicity and worst-case tubes Under standard tire models, the admissible acceleration bounds that enter (S1)–(S4) scale monotonically with road friction. If in addition the admissible input set is held fixed across and the stability margins are chosen uniformly, one expects a nested family of reachable envelopes: for , the feasible region on low friction cannot exceed that on high friction, so that is contained in (or very close to) and likewise for the tube . In practice, competing constraints (e.g., rollover margin) and discretization/reconstruction effects can introduce small non-monotone artifacts; we therefore treat monotonicity as a physically motivated design principle and quantify empirical slice-wise violation rates on our dataset (Supplementary Section S7; Supplementary Table S14 and Supplementary Fig. S5).

This monotonic structure motivates two practical deployment modes: (i) a single worst-case tube used as a robust under-approximation for any time-varying , and (ii) scheduled switching between tubes at known change times, which follows from the semigroup property of forward reachable sets.

Stability constraints and offline admissibility screening

Rationale

To ensure that every sample admitted to the reachability tube arises from a physically stable trajectory, we screen all simulated trajectories offline by a set of sufficient inequalities. The chosen indicators mirror those used in electronic stability control (ESC) and other active safety functions: sideslip and yaw rate for lateral stability, load transfer ratio for rollover risk, and longitudinal acceleration for braking and traction margins. The filter is designed to be (i) conservative—violating any constraint flags the whole trajectory as unstable; (ii) algebraic—each test is a closed–form inequality evaluable at each time sample; and (iii) parameterised by the environment channel : the explicit limits scale with friction , while non-zero grade/wind would enter through the simulated dynamics f(x, u, e). (Screening sufficiency of the filter). Suppose a trajectory over [0, T] satisfies (16)–(22) for all t. Then (i) no wheel lift–off occurs; (ii) longitudinal/lateral accelerations remain within the imposed adhesion-related bounds; and (iii) the frozen-time Jacobian screen (22) excludes operating points whose linearized lateral–yaw dynamics have positive-real-part eigenvalues. Extreme-scenario evaluation and rule ablation

We evaluate the geometric decision (tube membership) against high-fidelity outcomes from a CarSim full-vehicle reference model on extreme scenarios. High-fidelity protocol CarSim is configured as in Sec. Nonlinear vehicle model and reachability-tube formulation: ABS enabled, ESC and AEB disabled, and constant road friction (with ). For each scenario we replay the two-segment primitives (3) as open-loop steering/throttle/brake commands at 100 Hz over s. A scenario is marked successful if there exists at least one primitive that completes without collision or lane departure and respects the stability constraints (5)–(8) throughout [0, T]; otherwise it is marked failed. This existential definition matches the open-loop existence quantifier underlying forward reachability. Note that the tube construction additionally enforces the sufficient local-linear stability screen (9); therefore tube membership is expected to be conservative relative to these high-fidelity labels. In the language of Theorem 1, the benchmark’s positive-support subset consists of scenarios for which this existential high-fidelity label is positive.

When is acceptable? In this paper the tube predicate is used as a feasibility screen: an accept means the case is not ruled out by the tube and should enter a downstream evaluation/mitigation stage, rather than being executed without further checks. Consequently, false positives (unsafe acceptances relative to CarSim) translate to additional workload or intervention (e.g., higher-fidelity re-simulation, closed-loop rollout, or an independent runtime-assurance layer), not an immediate safety claim. When unsafe acceptances must be minimized (e.g., direct actuation gating), one should choose a more risk-averse operating point (e.g., Rule A with conservative discounting/downward binning) and/or apply an explicit clearance guard-band in the scenario query (Supplementary Section S12.1), as also highlighted by the cost-sensitive trade-off analysis below.

Discussion and limitations

Our finite-time reachability tube is intended to complement, not replace, classical HJ reachability, tube MPC and CBF-based safety filters. HJ methods provide exact level-set solutions in low dimensions but scale poorly with state and environment parameters; tube MPC and CBFs offer robust tracking or invariance around a nominal plan. In contrast, we precompute a vehicle-level handling envelope parameterised by an environment channel (evaluated here on the friction-only slice ) and tailored to near-horizon emergency maneuvers, so that online queries reduce to set-membership tests.

For deployment, friction monotonicity suggests two practical modes: (i) a single worst-case tube that is safe for any at the price of conservatism on high friction, and (ii) scheduled switching between tubes when friction changes can be localised in time (e.g., detected patches), implemented by composing per-slice reachable sets. Uncertainty in state or environment estimates can be handled by using a clearance guard-band and discounting the effective friction; details are given in Supplementary Section S4.

The tube integrates naturally with existing planning and AEB stacks as a physics-grounded feasibility prior or screening module: planners can add soft penalties based on signed distance to the per-slice envelope in an MPC cost, or use the lightweight xy proxy as a fast guard inside a broader runtime-assurance pipeline. In line with the risk-managed screen viewpoint in Sec. Introduction, a positive membership should be interpreted as “candidate feasible” rather than a standalone safety guarantee; when used online it should be paired with an independent downstream safeguard (e.g., collision checking, closed-loop rollout, MPC/CBF-style filtering, or a fail-safe brake fallback). These hooks are implementation choices and do not affect the construction or regularity results of the tube itself; examples appear in Supplementary Section S10.

Limitations include that the primary high-fidelity benchmark and most quantitative results are reported on the friction-only, flat-road slice with constant over the horizon. Supplementary Section S19 and Supplementary Section S18 extend the analysis to fixed grade-only, wind-only, and combined off-nominal slices, and Supplementary Section S19 further adds a small stratified CarSim spot-check on the separated grade-only and wind-only slices; however, we do not provide a second full 216-scenario high-fidelity benchmark over nonzero grade/wind, nor a high-fidelity check of the combined slice. Other limitations include dependence on the chosen feature map and two-segment input parameterisation; the absence of explicit map and obstacle constraints (reach–avoid tubes are a natural extension); purely numerical evaluation to date; and a deliberately simple mapping g from scenarios to tube queries (Sec. Extreme-scenario evaluation and rule ablation). Reproducibility-related assumptions and parameter tables are collected in Supplementary Section S1 and Supplementary Section S4.

Conclusions

We studied stability-constrained finite-time reachability tubes as dynamic handling envelopes for nonlinear vehicle dynamics under an environment channel . While the regularity and complexity results hold for general compact , the main empirical study instantiates the nominal slice and varies friction only; Supplementary Information reports additional off-nominal slice studies and a small separated-slice CarSim spot-check (Supplementary Section S19, Supplementary Section S18). On the analysis side, we established compactness and a local-robustness-qualified Hausdorff continuity result for the per-slice reachable sets and the tube map over compact environment domains, derived a tube-compression statement that links feasibility queries (after scenario-to-requirement mapping) to tube membership under an explicit error budget, and provided a sampling-complexity bound that quantifies the offline burden of tube-library construction in terms of time resolution, geometric tolerance and the dimension of the environment grid.

On the computational side, we introduced a practical offline construction pipeline based on (i) a curated library of short-horizon two-segment inputs, (ii) an analytic stability filter built from interpretable vehicle-dynamics limits (sideslip, yaw-rate margin, load transfer ratio, adhesion and a frozen-time Jacobian pre-filter), and (iii) nonconvex per-slice reconstruction using -shapes in a normalized 4D feature space with key-frame compression. In a representative mid-size passenger-car study (2 s horizon, 100 Hz sampling, 2 142 input primitives and ), the resulting tubes exhibit strong cross-resolution consistency under shared rollouts, physically consistent friction scaling and sub-millisecond stage-1 membership screening via a lightweight 2D proxy (with optional 4D confirmation). These properties support the use of the tube as a reusable handling envelope for near-limit motion planning and conservative feasibility screening in rare safety-critical situations, within the nominal benchmark and the supplementary slice-specific off-nominal diagnostics reported here.

In addition, the Supplementary Information reports deployment-oriented diagnostics (horizon critical-time statistics, risk–coverage operating curves, mis-estimation sensitivity, and error-mode localization with ROM mismatch proxies) to align the study with reliability engineering and system-safety practice.

Future work includes reach–avoid tubes that incorporate map/obstacle constraints, sparse tube libraries over with interpolation and worst-case selection, independent native re-integration across time steps, closed-loop integration with MPC/CBF-style safety layers, and validation on hardware-in-the-loop and proving-ground platforms.

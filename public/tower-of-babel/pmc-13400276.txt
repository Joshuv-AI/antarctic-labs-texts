Commentary on “Which spine surgery techniques are most appealing to the public? A survey examining public perception of spine surgery techniques and factors associated with procedure preference”
Authors: Thomas Bieganowski, Zorica Buser
Published: 2026

Perez-Alba et al. [1], conducted a cross-sectional survey study to evaluate the general public’s understanding as well as preference regarding spine surgery techniques which included both traditional open procedures as well as newer methods of surgical intervention. The evolution of spine surgery over the past 2 decades has seen the advancement of technology such as robotics and minimally invasive surgery and the increased ubiquity of endoscopic techniques. Given the favorable outcomes demonstrated with these less invasive techniques, there has been a surge in marketing utilizing several different phrases focused on introducing patients to these surgeries [2]. Despite this, the study of patient understanding on how these new tools are perceived is lacking. Therefore, the current study sought to assess public knowledge and preference for spine surgery, with the hope of identifying areas where educational efforts could be improved to help enhance patient understanding.

The reviewed study used a Qualtrics digital survey distributed on the online research platform Prolific to assess 493 respondents on their spine surgery technique preferences. Median response time for the survey was 5 minutes and the respondent population was predominantly female (58.62%) under the age of 40 (60%). Minimally invasive surgery (72%) and laser spine surgery (61%) were the most chosen techniques when respondents were asked which surgery they would choose for themselves. This is in keeping with previous research demonstrating that minimally invasive spine surgery is often preferred over open surgery due to perceptions of reduced postoperative pain, fewer complications, shorter recovery times, and lower costs [3]. With this in mind, public perception may correlate with patient preference and influence surgical discussion, mandating uniform marketing strategies to avoid confusion amongst patients and providers.

One area that is particularly novel in current spine literature is the identification of social media influence of patient understanding. Per the results of the present analyses, social media use was primarily YouTube (70.6%), Instagram (56.6%), and Facebook (51.9%). However, the most important factors when deciding on a surgeon were the recommendation of former patients (8.6), hospital reputation (8.4), and recommendation of other physicians (8.4). Therefore, the present study presents an interesting comparison. Although contemporary patients are utilizing social media to an increasing degree when researching their preferences, the choice of surgeon was most substantially influenced by word of mouth from previous patients. While not specifically elucidated in their research, an interesting future analysis might consider the percentage of patients utilizing social media when looking for physician recommendations and social media’s influence of perceived surgeon experience, abilities, and breadth of operative techniques offered.

The reviewed study also touched upon the influence of region within the United States on surgical preference. The authors noted that endoscopic surgery preferences were negatively associated with living in the Midwest and Northeast. Geographic factors are a quintessential consideration when determining patient understanding and potential preference for surgery. Previous research has found that urban setting significantly influences spine surgery preference and utilization through multiple interacting mechanisms, including surgeon density, hospital resources, socioeconomic factors, and referral patterns [4]. Patients living in more urban settings likely have a greater specialist density and therefore may be exposed to newer technology and greater options compared to patients in rural settings who may have limited access to surgeons that offer less variation.

The cross-sectional nature of the study and lack of comparison group limit the generalization of the results. Nonetheless, the query of the general population regarding their preference for spine surgery provides data for further research aimed at increasing awareness and limiting misunderstanding in the ever changing landscape of contemporary spine surgery.

In summary, the currently reviewed study offers insight into the general public’s understanding and preference of spine surgery. As technology and techniques in spine surgery continue to grow and evolve, improving patient outreach with appropriate marketing tools will influence preoperative discussion and limit misunderstanding between physicians and patients.

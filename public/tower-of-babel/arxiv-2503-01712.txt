Unconditionally stable time discretization of Lindblad master equations in infinite dimension using quantum channels
Rémi Robin, Pierre Rouchon, Lev-Arcady Sellem

Abstract
We examine the time discretization of Lindblad master equations in infinite-dimensional Hilbert spaces. Our study is motivated by the fact that, with unbounded Lindbladian, projecting the evolution onto a finite-dimensional subspace using a Galerkin approximation inherently introduces stiffness, leading to a Courant–Friedrichs–Lewy type condition for explicit integration schemes. We propose and establish the convergence of a family of explicit numerical schemes for time discretization adapted to infinite dimension. These schemes correspond to quantum channels and thus preserve the physical properties of quantum evolutions on the set of density operators: linearity, complete positivity and trace. Numerical experiments inspired by bosonic quantum codes illustrate the practical interest of this approach when approximating the solution of infinite dimensional problems by that of finite dimensional problems of increasing dimension.

Introduction

The Lindblad equation, also known as Gorini-Kossakowski-Sudarshan-Lindblad equation
[
Lin76
,
GKS76
,
CP17
]
,
describes open quantum systems
within the regime of weak coupling to a Markovian environment

[
BP06
]
.
In this setting, the state of an open quantum system is given by
a density operator
{\boldsymbol{\rho}}
, that is a positive trace-class operator of unit trace, on a separable Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
.
The GKSL equation describes the evolution of the density operator
{\boldsymbol{\rho}}
as follows:

\displaystyle\frac{d{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}{dt}=\mathcal{L}({\boldsymbol{\rho}})\coloneqq-i[{\boldsymbol{H}},{\boldsymbol{\rho}}]+\sum_{j}\left({\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}^{\dagger}-\frac{1}{2}\left({\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}^{\dagger}{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}+{\boldsymbol{\rho}}{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}^{\dagger}{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}\right)\right),

(1)

where
{\boldsymbol{H}}
is a self-adjoint operator called the Hamiltonian of the system,
and the so-called jump operators

({\boldsymbol{L}}_{j})_{j}

characterize the interaction of the system with its environment.
\mathcal{L}
is a super-operator, namely acting on the set of operators on
\mathcal{H}
, and is called a Lindbladian.

In numerous applications, including quantum optics, quantum chemistry or quantum electrodynamics, the underlying Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
is infinite-dimensional, and both the Hamiltonian
{\boldsymbol{H}}
and the operators
({\boldsymbol{L}}_{j})_{j}
are unbounded.
The numerical simulation of
Eq.

1
thus requires discretizing
\mathcal{H}

(referred to as space discretization), which means selecting a finite-dimensional subspace,
as well as discretizing time to solve the evolution equation.

This paper focuses on structure-preserving time discretization schemes, inspired by the principles outlined in
[
HWL06
]
. The semigroup
(e^{t\mathcal{L}})_{t\geq 0}
is for every
t\geq 0
a quantum channel, see e.g.
[
NC10
, Chapter 8]
. A quantum channel is a linear map that is both Completely Positive and Trace-Preserving (CPTP). We are interested in numerical schemes that share this property.

Recently, in the setting of finite-dimensional Lindblad equations,
Lu and Cao introduced time discretization schemes that preserve the set of density operators
[
CL24
]
. However, these schemes are non-linear, unlike the continuous solution of the Lindblad equation. Similarly,
[
AC24
]
proposes Completely Positive (CP) schemes that can be renormalized to preserve the trace, but this also comes at the cost of non-linearity, again resulting in non-CPTP maps.
To address this issue, we extend a technique introduced in
[
Jor+16
]
[Appendix B], enabling the transformation of any CP scheme from the aforementioned references into a CPTP, and thus linear, scheme. Notably, this construction can be applied even to unbounded Lindbladians, resulting in a CPTP scheme that is inherently bounded.

The first contribution of this article is to theoretically investigate these new explicit quantum channel/CPTP schemes. Our main result, presented in
Theorem

2
, establishes that, under reasonable assumptions, the first order variant of these schemes is consistent and stable for any positive time-step, even for the infinite-dimensional Lindblad equation.

In a second time, we study the interest of these schemes for numerical simulations. To this aim, we adhere to a classical approach by employing a Galerkin method. Specifically, we project the Hamiltonian
{\boldsymbol{H}}
and the operators
({\boldsymbol{L}}_{j})_{1\leq j\leq N_{d}}
to a finite-dimensional Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{N}
. One advantage of this approach is that it leads to a Lindblad equation
on the finite dimensional Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{N}
, thus preserving the structure of the evolution.
Limitation and convergence of this method for the Schrödinger equation can be found in the recent preprint
[
FBL25
]
; while
a posteriori
error estimate for the Lindblad equation are developed in
[
ERR25
]
. Note also that several efforts have been undertaken to go beyond the linear space discretization, with techniques including low rank approximation
[
LR13
,
GS24
]
or model reduction
[
ASR16
,
LR24
]
.
Once we have set a finite-dimensional Hilbert space, the task reduces to solving a Lindblad equation on this space, taking the form of a set of ordinary differential equations (ODE).
As one should expect, the fact that the operators
{\boldsymbol{H}}
and
({\boldsymbol{L}}_{j})_{1\leq j\leq N_{d}}
are unbounded
implies that stiffness naturally occurs in this set of ODEs,
as illustrated in

4.2.1
. Hence, the use of explicit solvers

leads to unstable schemes if the dimension of the discretization space is increased without reducing the time-step. We show that this restriction does not apply to our quantum channel schemes.

2.2
Time discretization in finite dimension

2.2.1
Non-linear positivity-preserving schemes

In this section, we recall the main ideas of the method developed in
[
CL24
]
to obtain
(non-linear) numerical schemes of
arbitrarily high order
preserving the positivity and the trace when the underlying Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
is finite dimensional. In particular, all the linear operators are bounded.

We recall that with our notation

{\boldsymbol{G}}=-i{\boldsymbol{H}}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{d}}{\boldsymbol{L}}^{\dagger}_{j}{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}
, and we can thus write the Lindbladian as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}({\boldsymbol{\rho}})={\boldsymbol{G}}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}+{\boldsymbol{\rho}}{\boldsymbol{G}}^{\dagger}+\sum_{j=1}^{N_{d}}{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}^{\dagger}.

(8)

Introducing the two superoperators

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{1}({\boldsymbol{\rho}})={\boldsymbol{G}}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}+{\boldsymbol{\rho}}{\boldsymbol{G}}^{\dagger},\quad\mathcal{L}_{2}({\boldsymbol{\rho}})=\sum_{j=1}^{N_{d}}{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}^{\dagger},

(9)

we have the splitting
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{1}+\mathcal{L}_{2}
.
Crucially, both
\mathcal{L}_{1}
and
\mathcal{L}_{2}
generate positivity-preserving semigroups.
The semigroup generated by
\mathcal{L}_{1}
has the simple expression

\displaystyle e^{t\mathcal{L}_{1}}({\boldsymbol{\rho}})=e^{t{\boldsymbol{G}}}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}e^{t{\boldsymbol{G}}^{\dagger}}.

Note that this formula allows to straightforwardly transform any numerical approximation of the semigroup generated by
{\boldsymbol{G}}

into a positivity-preserving numerical approximation of the semigroup generated by
\mathcal{L}_{1}
.
Indeed, for any approximation of the semigroup
e^{\Delta t{\boldsymbol{G}}}
by
{\boldsymbol{M}}_{0,\Delta t}
such that
\left\|e^{\Delta t{\boldsymbol{G}}}-{\boldsymbol{M}}_{0,\Delta t}\right\|_{\infty}\leq C\Delta t^{N+1}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|e^{\Delta t\mathcal{L}_{1}}({\boldsymbol{\rho}})-{\boldsymbol{M}}_{0,\Delta t}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}{\boldsymbol{M}}_{0,\Delta t}^{\dagger}\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle=\left\|e^{\Delta t{\boldsymbol{G}}}\,{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\,e^{\Delta t{\boldsymbol{G}}^{\dagger}}-{\boldsymbol{M}}_{0,\Delta t}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}{\boldsymbol{M}}_{0,\Delta t}^{\dagger}\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|e^{\Delta t{\boldsymbol{G}}}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}(e^{\Delta t{\boldsymbol{G}}^{\dagger}}-{\boldsymbol{M}}_{0,\Delta t}^{\dagger})\right\|_{1}+\left\|(e^{\Delta t{\boldsymbol{G}}}-{\boldsymbol{M}}_{0,\Delta t}){\boldsymbol{\rho}}{\boldsymbol{M}}_{0,\Delta t}^{\dagger}\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|e^{\Delta t{\boldsymbol{G}}}\right\|_{\infty}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right\|_{1}\left\|e^{\Delta t{\boldsymbol{G}}}-{\boldsymbol{M}}_{0,\Delta t}\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle+\left\|e^{\Delta t{\boldsymbol{G}}}-{\boldsymbol{M}}_{0,\Delta t}\right\|_{\infty}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right\|_{1}\left\|{\boldsymbol{M}}_{0,\Delta t}\right\|_{\infty}.

Since
(e^{\Delta t{\boldsymbol{G}}})_{t\geq 0}
is a contraction semigroup, we have
\left\|e^{\Delta t{\boldsymbol{G}}}\right\|_{\infty}\leq 1
, and thus
\left\|{\boldsymbol{M}}_{0,\Delta t}\right\|_{\infty}\leq 1+C\Delta t^{N+1}
.
As a consequence, for
\Delta t
small enough, the previous equation leads to

\displaystyle\left\|e^{\Delta t\mathcal{L}_{1}}({\boldsymbol{\rho}})-{\boldsymbol{M}}_{0,\Delta t}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}{\boldsymbol{M}}_{0,\Delta t}^{\dagger}\right\|_{1}\leq\tilde{C}\Delta t^{N+1}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right\|_{1},

(10)

for some constant
\tilde{C}
.
Finally,
{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\mapsto{\boldsymbol{M}}_{0,\Delta t}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}{\boldsymbol{M}}_{0,\Delta t}^{\dagger}
is a completely positive map.
To obtain a first order scheme, one can choose the approximation

{\boldsymbol{M}}_{0,\Delta t}=\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}+\Delta t{\boldsymbol{G}}
.
Indeed,
\left\|e^{\Delta t{\boldsymbol{G}}}-{\boldsymbol{M}}_{0,\Delta t}\right\|_{\infty}\leq C\Delta t^{2}
as

\left\|e^{\Delta t{\boldsymbol{G}}}-(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}+\Delta t{\boldsymbol{G}})\right\|_{\infty}=\left\|\int_{0}^{\Delta t}(\Delta t-s)e^{s{\boldsymbol{G}}}{\boldsymbol{G}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\right\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{\Delta t^{2}}{2}\left\|{\boldsymbol{G}}\right\|_{\infty}^{2},

were we used again
\left\|e^{s{\boldsymbol{G}}}\right\|\leq 1
.
Later in the paper, for technical reasons explained in
Remark

3
,
we also use the approximation

{\boldsymbol{M}}_{0,\Delta t}=\left(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}-i\frac{\Delta t}{2}{\boldsymbol{H}}\right)\left(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}+i\frac{\Delta t}{2}{\boldsymbol{H}}\right)^{-1}\left(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}-\frac{\Delta t}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{d}}{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}^{\dagger}{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}\right)

(11)

which still satisfies

{\boldsymbol{M}}_{0,\Delta t}=\Big(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}-i\Delta t{\boldsymbol{H}}\Big)\left(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}-\frac{\Delta t}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{d}}{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}^{\dagger}{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}\right)+O\left(\Delta t^{2}\right)=\left(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}+\Delta t{\boldsymbol{G}}\right)+O\left(\Delta t^{2}\right).

(12)

This choice amounts to treating separately the Hamiltonian and non-Hamiltonian parts of
{\boldsymbol{G}}

when approximating the semigroup
e^{t{\boldsymbol{G}}}
.
This splitting strategy takes advantage of the fact that
the propagator
e^{-it{\boldsymbol{H}}}
of the Hamiltonian part can be approximated by a unitary operator
by the symmetric (1,1)-Padé approximant

(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}-i\tfrac{t}{2}{\boldsymbol{H}})(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}+i\tfrac{t}{2}{\boldsymbol{H}})^{-1}
.

Going back to the approximation of
e^{t\mathcal{L}}=e^{t(\mathcal{L}_{1}+\mathcal{L}_{2})}
, Duhamel’s formula gives

\displaystyle e^{\Delta t\mathcal{L}}({\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{0})

\displaystyle=e^{\Delta t\mathcal{L}_{1}}({\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{0})+\int_{0}^{\Delta t}e^{(\Delta t-s)\mathcal{L}_{1}}(\mathcal{L}_{2}({\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{s}))\,ds

(13)

\displaystyle=e^{\Delta t\mathcal{L}_{1}}({\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{0})+\Delta t\,\mathcal{L}_{2}({\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{0})+\int_{0}^{\Delta t}\left(e^{(\Delta t-s)\mathcal{L}_{1}}(\mathcal{L}_{2}({\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{s}))-\mathcal{L}_{2}({\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{0})\right)\,ds.

(14)

For
\Delta t
sufficiently small and
0\leq s\leq\Delta t
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|e^{(\Delta t-s)\mathcal{L}_{1}}(\mathcal{L}_{2}({\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{s}))-\mathcal{L}_{2}({\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{0})\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|e^{(\Delta t-s)\mathcal{L}_{1}}(\mathcal{L}_{2}({\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{s})-\mathcal{L}_{2}({\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{0}))\right\|_{1}+\left\|\left(e^{(\Delta t-s)\mathcal{L}_{1}}-\operatorname{Id}\right)(\mathcal{L}_{2}({\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{0}))\right\|_{1}

(15)

\displaystyle\leq\left\|e^{(\Delta t-s)\mathcal{L}_{1}}\right\|_{\mathfrak{L}(\mathcal{K}^{1})}\left\|\mathcal{L}_{2}\right\|_{\mathfrak{L}(\mathcal{K}^{1})}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{s}-{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{0}\right\|_{1}+\left\|e^{(\Delta t-s)\mathcal{L}_{1}}-\operatorname{Id}\right\|_{\mathfrak{L}(\mathcal{K}^{1})}\left\|\mathcal{L}_{2}\right\|_{\mathfrak{L}(\mathcal{K}^{1})}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{0}\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{0}\right\|_{1}\Delta t,

where
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathfrak{L}(\mathcal{K}^{1})}
stand for the operator norm from
\mathcal{K}^{1}(\mathcal{H})
to itself and
C
is a constant independent of
\Delta t
,
s
, and
{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{0}
.
Thus, the integral on the right-hand side of
Eq.

14
can be neglected within an error of order
\Delta t^{2}
.
Consequently, for each dissipator
{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}
,
we introduce
{\boldsymbol{M}}_{j,\Delta t}=\sqrt{\Delta t}{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}
, so that
\Delta t\mathcal{L}_{2}({\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{0})=\sum_{j=1}^{N_{d}}{\boldsymbol{M}}_{j,\Delta t}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{0}{\boldsymbol{M}}_{j,\Delta t}^{\dagger}
,
and define a first-order scheme

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{n+1}=\mathcal{A}_{\Delta t}^{1}({\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{n})\coloneq{\boldsymbol{M}}_{0,\Delta t}\,{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{n}\,{\boldsymbol{M}}_{0,\Delta t}^{\dagger}+\sum_{j=1}^{N_{d}}{\boldsymbol{M}}_{j,\Delta t}\,{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{n}\,{\boldsymbol{M}}_{j,\Delta t}^{\dagger}=e^{\Delta t\mathcal{L}}({\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{n})+\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{n}\right\|_{1}O(\Delta t^{2}).

(16)

Higher-order schemes can be obtained by iteratively applying Duhamel’s formula in
Eq.

13
and choosing a quadrature rule for the resulting integral on
time
simplexes
0\leq t_{1}\leq\ldots\leq t_{p}\leq\Delta t
– see
[
CL24
]
for details. All these schemes share the generic structure

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{\Delta t}({\boldsymbol{\rho}})=\sum_{j=0}^{m}{\boldsymbol{M}}_{j,\Delta t}\,{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\,{\boldsymbol{M}}_{j,\Delta t}^{\dagger}=e^{\Delta t\mathcal{L}}({\boldsymbol{\rho}})+\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right\|_{1}O(\Delta t^{p+1}),

(17)

where
p
is the order of the scheme
and the integer
m
and operators
{\boldsymbol{M}}_{j,\Delta t}
depend on
p
, the number of jump operators
N_{d}
and the choice of quadrature rule.

These schemes are completely positive, yet they do not preserve the trace, which deviates by an error of order
\Delta t^{p+1}
per time-step.
To address this issue, Cao and Lu introduced in
[
CL24
]
the associated normalized non-linear schemes

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\Delta t}({\boldsymbol{\rho}})=\frac{\mathcal{A}_{\Delta t}({\boldsymbol{\rho}})}{\operatorname{Tr}\left(\mathcal{A}_{\Delta t}({\boldsymbol{\rho}})\right)}.

Note that, despite the fact that these normalized schemes preserve both the positivity and the trace, the lack of linearity means that they are not proper quantum dynamical maps.
In particular, they can lose important properties of quantum dynamical maps, such as contractivity of the nuclear norm of the solution over time.

2.2.2
Quantum channel schemes

We now present an alternative procedure to transform a linear CP scheme of the form given in
Eq.

17
into a CPTP scheme; this procedures ensures the preservation of the trace without sacrificing the linearity of the scheme. To achieve this, we employ a method introduced in
[
Jor+16
, Appendix B]

for the first-order approximation of stochastic Lindblad equations.
We observe that their technique also trivially applies to all orders for deterministic equations.
Let us consider the evolution of the identity operator through the dual evolution of the
non trace-preserving scheme in
Eq.

17
.
For any
{\boldsymbol{O}}\in B(\mathcal{H})
, we have

\operatorname{Tr}\left({\boldsymbol{O}}\mathcal{A}_{\Delta t}({\boldsymbol{\rho}})\right)=\operatorname{Tr}\left(\mathcal{A}^{*}_{\Delta t}({\boldsymbol{O}}){\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right),

(18)

where

\mathcal{A}^{*}_{\Delta t}({\boldsymbol{O}})=\sum_{j=1}^{k}{\boldsymbol{M}}_{j,\Delta t}^{\dagger}{\boldsymbol{O}}{\boldsymbol{M}}_{j,\Delta t},

(19)

and in particular

\mathcal{A}^{*}_{\Delta t}(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}})=\sum_{j}{\boldsymbol{M}}_{j,\Delta t}^{\dagger}{\boldsymbol{M}}_{j,\Delta t}.

(20)

It is important to emphasize that for the CP scheme
\mathcal{A}_{\Delta t}
to be trace-preserving, it is both necessary and sufficient that
\mathcal{A}^{*}_{\Delta t}(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}})=\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}
.
If
\mathcal{A}
is a scheme of order
p
, we also have the estimate

e^{\Delta t\mathcal{L}^{*}}(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}})=\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}=\mathcal{A}^{*}_{\Delta t}(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}})+O(\Delta t^{p+1}).

(21)

Define
{\boldsymbol{S}}_{\Delta t}=\mathcal{A}^{*}_{\Delta t}(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}})=\sum_{j}{\boldsymbol{M}}_{j,\Delta t}^{\dagger}{\boldsymbol{M}}_{j,\Delta t}
,
which is a positive operator.
For
\Delta t
small enough,
Eq.

21
implies that
{\boldsymbol{S}}_{\Delta t}

is positive definite, and we can define the positive matrix
{\boldsymbol{S}}_{\Delta t}^{-1/2}

which also satisfies

{\boldsymbol{S}}_{\Delta t}^{-1/2}=\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}+O(\Delta t^{p+1}).

(22)

Substituting
{\boldsymbol{M}}_{j,\Delta t}
with
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{M}}_{j,\Delta t}={\boldsymbol{M}}_{j,\Delta t}{\boldsymbol{S}}_{\Delta t}^{-1/2}

in
Eq.

17
leads to a Completely Positive Trace-Preserving (CPTP) scheme
\mathcal{F}_{\Delta t}

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\Delta t}({\boldsymbol{\rho}})=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{M}}_{j,\Delta t}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{M}}_{j,\Delta t}^{\dagger}=e^{\Delta t\mathcal{L}}({\boldsymbol{\rho}})+\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right\|_{1}O(\Delta t^{p+1}),

(23)

with now

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\Delta t}^{*}(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}})=\sum_{j}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{M}}_{j,\Delta t}^{\dagger}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{M}}_{j,\Delta t}

\displaystyle={\boldsymbol{S}}_{\Delta t}^{-1/2}\,\left(\sum_{i}{\boldsymbol{M}}_{j,\Delta t}^{\dagger}{\boldsymbol{M}}_{j,\Delta t}\right)\,{\boldsymbol{S}}_{\Delta t}^{-1/2}=\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}.

(24)

Eq.

23
shows that
\mathcal{F}_{\Delta t}
has the same order
p
as the original scheme, while
Eq.

24
shows that it is a CPTP map. Thus, introducing the perturbation of the identity
{\boldsymbol{S}}_{\Delta t}
, allows us to enforce trace preservation in any CP scheme without compromising linearity.

Remark 1
.

The fact that
\mathcal{F}_{\Delta t}
is a CPTP map implies that the time discretized evolution, like the continuous evolution, contracts the nuclear norm.
Another important feature is that every
0\leq j\leq N_{d}
,
{\boldsymbol{M}}_{j,\Delta t}
satisfy
{\boldsymbol{M}}_{j,\Delta t}^{\dagger}{\boldsymbol{M}}_{j,\Delta t}\leq\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}
which implies that
\left\|{\boldsymbol{M}}_{j,\Delta t}\right\|_{\infty}\leq 1
.
Later, we will consider unbounded operators
{\boldsymbol{H}}
and/or
{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}
in an infinite dimensional Hilbert space.
Formally extending the previous notations, all the
{\boldsymbol{M}}_{j,\Delta t}
might then be unbounded operators, whereas
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{M}}_{j,\Delta t}
are now ensured to be bounded operators with
\left\|\widetilde{\boldsymbol{M}}_{j,\Delta t}\right\|_{\infty}\leq 1
.

2.3
Quantum Markov semigroups with unbounded generators

Let us now focus on the rigorous definition of the solution of Lindblad equations
in the infinite-dimensional setting.
One can opt for either of two equivalent definitions for the solution to a Lindblad equation: one based on the Von Neumann algebra
B(\mathcal{H})
, as pursued in Chebotarev and Fagnola’s works
[
CF93
,
CF98
]
, or directly on the Banach space of trace class operators, as initially introduced by Davies
[
Dav79
,
Dav77
]
. We follow the former approach.
Let us recall the definition of a Quantum Dynamical Semigroup:

Definition 1
.

A quantum dynamical semigroup
(\mathcal{T}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a family of operators acting on
B(\mathcal{H})
that satisfies the following properties:

•

\mathcal{T}_{0}({\boldsymbol{X}})={\boldsymbol{X}}
for all
{\boldsymbol{X}}\in B(\mathcal{H})
.

•

\mathcal{T}_{t+s}({\boldsymbol{X}})=\mathcal{T}_{t}(\mathcal{T}_{s}({\boldsymbol{X}}))
for all
t,s\geq 0
and
{\boldsymbol{X}}\in B(\mathcal{H})
.

•

\mathcal{T}_{t}(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}})\leq\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}
for all
t\geq 0
.

•

\mathcal{T}_{t}
is a completely positive map for all
t\geq 0
, meaning that for any finite sequences
({\boldsymbol{X}}_{j})_{1\leq j\leq n}
and
({\boldsymbol{Y}}_{j})_{1\leq j\leq n}
of elements of
B(\mathcal{H})
, we have
\sum_{1\leq j,l\leq n}{\boldsymbol{Y}}_{l}^{\dagger}\,\mathcal{T}_{t}({\boldsymbol{X}}_{l}^{\dagger}{\boldsymbol{X}}_{j})\,{\boldsymbol{Y}}_{j}\geq 0
.

•

(normality) for every weakly converging sequence
({\boldsymbol{X}}_{n})_{n}\rightharpoonup X
in
B(\mathcal{H})
, the sequence
(\mathcal{T}_{t}({\boldsymbol{X}}_{n}))_{n}
converges weakly towards
\mathcal{T}_{t}({\boldsymbol{X}})
.

•

(ultraweak continuity) for all
{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\in\mathcal{K}^{1}
and
{\boldsymbol{X}}\in B(\mathcal{H})
, we have
\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\operatorname{Tr}\left({\boldsymbol{\rho}}\mathcal{T}_{t}({\boldsymbol{X}})\right)=\operatorname{Tr}\left({\boldsymbol{\rho}}{\boldsymbol{X}}\right)
.

Furthermore, if
\mathcal{T}_{t}(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}})=\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}
for all
t\geq 0
, the semigroup is called conservative.

Now, let us establish the connection between the Lindblad equation and the concept of quantum dynamical semigroup. We will make the following assumptions:

•

{\boldsymbol{G}}=-i{\boldsymbol{H}}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j}{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}^{\dagger}{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}
is the generator of a strongly continuous semigroup of contractions on
\mathcal{H}
,

•

D({\boldsymbol{G}})\subset\cap_{j}\mathcal{D}({\boldsymbol{L}}_{j})
and for all
u\in\mathcal{D}({\boldsymbol{G}})

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N_{d}}\braket{{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}u|{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}u}+2\operatorname{Re}\braket{{\boldsymbol{G}}u|u}=0.

(25)

Definition 2
.

The quantum dynamical semigroup
(\mathcal{T}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a solution of the Lindblad equation defined in (
6
) if the following weak formulation holds:

\displaystyle\Braket{v|\mathcal{T}_{t}({\boldsymbol{X}})u}=\Braket{v|{\boldsymbol{X}}u}+\int_{0}^{t}\left(\Braket{{\boldsymbol{G}}v|\mathcal{T}_{s}({\boldsymbol{X}})u}+\Braket{v|\mathcal{T}_{s}({\boldsymbol{X}}){\boldsymbol{G}}u}+\sum_{j=1}^{N_{d}}\Braket{{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}v|\mathcal{T}_{s}({\boldsymbol{X}})\,{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}u}\right)\mathrm{d}s,

(26)

for all
u,v\in\mathcal{D}({\boldsymbol{G}})
,
{\boldsymbol{X}}\in B(\mathcal{H})
, and
t\geq 0
.

One can show (see
[
CF93
, Proposition 2.6]
) that a given quantum dynamical semigroup
(\mathcal{T}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
satisfies
Eq.

26
if and only if it satisfies the following Duhamel formulation

\displaystyle\Braket{v|\mathcal{T}_{t}({\boldsymbol{X}})u}=\Braket{{e^{t{\boldsymbol{G}}}v}|{\boldsymbol{X}}{e^{tG}}u}+\sum_{j=1}^{N_{d}}\int_{0}^{t}\Braket{{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}e^{(t-s)G}v|\mathcal{T}_{s}({\boldsymbol{X}})\,{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}e^{(t-s){\boldsymbol{G}}}u}ds,

(27)

for all
u,v\in\mathcal{D}({\boldsymbol{G}})
,
{\boldsymbol{X}}\in B(\mathcal{H})
, and
t\geq 0
.

Iterating a fixed point procedure on
Eq.

27
leads to a quantum dynamical semigroup
(\mathcal{T}_{t}^{min})_{t\geq 0}
:

Theorem 1

(Theorem 3.22 of
[
Fag99
]
)
.

Assume that
{\boldsymbol{G}}
is the generator of a contraction semigroup,
D({\boldsymbol{G}})\subset\cap_{j}\mathcal{D}({\boldsymbol{L}}_{j})
and for all
u\in\mathcal{D}({\boldsymbol{G}})
,
Eq.

25
holds. Then, there exists a quantum dynamical semigroup
(\mathcal{T}_{t}^{\min})_{t\geq 0}
solution of
Eq.

26
. Besides, for
every quantum dynamical semigroup
(\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
solving
Eq.

26
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{t}^{\min}(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}})\leq\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{t}(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}})\leq\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}.

If the minimal semigroup is conservative, that is for every
t\geq 0
,
\mathcal{T}_{t}^{\min}(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}})=\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}
, then
\mathcal{T}_{t}^{\min}=\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{t}
for all
t
. In this case, there exists a unique quantum dynamical semigroup solution to the Lindblad equation. Note that, although
\mathcal{L}^{*}(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}})=0
formally, conservativity of the minimal semigroup cannot be deduced from our assumptions because
\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}
is not necessary in the domain of
\mathcal{L}^{*}
. We also have the following counter-example:

Example 1

(
[
Dav77
]
, Example 3.3)
.

The minimal semigroup
(\mathcal{T}_{t}^{min})_{t\geq 0}

of the Lindblad equation
\dot{\boldsymbol{\rho}}=D[{\boldsymbol{a}}^{{\dagger}2}]({\boldsymbol{\rho}})

is not conservative.

An important tool to study the conservativity of the minimal semigroup is the following representation of its resolvent, as established in
[
Che90
]
.
For each
\lambda>0
, we define the completely positive maps

P_{\lambda}\in\mathfrak{L}\left(B(\mathcal{H})\right)

and

Q_{\lambda}\in\mathfrak{L}\left(B(\mathcal{H})\right)

as follows:

\displaystyle\braket{u|P_{\lambda}({\boldsymbol{X}})v}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda s}\braket{e^{s{\boldsymbol{G}}}u|{\boldsymbol{X}}e^{s{\boldsymbol{G}}}v}ds

(28)

\displaystyle\braket{u|Q_{\lambda}({\boldsymbol{X}})v}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{N_{d}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda s}\braket{{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}e^{s{\boldsymbol{G}}}u|{\boldsymbol{X}}{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}e^{s{\boldsymbol{G}}}v}ds

(29)

for
u,v\in\mathcal{D}({\boldsymbol{G}})
and
{\boldsymbol{X}}\in B(\mathcal{H})
.
Note that
\left\|P_{\lambda}\right\|_{\mathfrak{L}\left(B(\mathcal{H})\right)}\leq\frac{1}{\lambda}
;
using
Eq.

25
with an integration by parts also yields
\left\|Q_{\lambda}\right\|_{\mathfrak{L}\left(B(\mathcal{H})\right)}\leq 1
.
Then, using

[
CF98
, Theorem 3.1]
,
the resolvent of the minimal quantum dynamical semigroup
(R_{\lambda}^{min})_{\lambda>0}
, characterized by

\displaystyle\braket{u|R_{\lambda}^{min}({\boldsymbol{X}})v}=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda s}\braket{u|\mathcal{T}_{s}^{min}({\boldsymbol{X}})v}ds,

(30)

satisfies, for every
\lambda>0
and
{\boldsymbol{X}}\in B(\mathcal{H})
:

\displaystyle R_{\lambda}^{min}({\boldsymbol{X}})=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}Q_{\lambda}^{n}(P_{\lambda}({\boldsymbol{X}})),

(31)

with the series converging in the strong topology. Note also that the truncated series

R_{\lambda}^{(m)}({\boldsymbol{X}})=\sum_{n=0}^{m}Q_{\lambda}^{n}(P_{\lambda}({\boldsymbol{X}}))

obeys the recurrence relation

\displaystyle R_{\lambda}^{(0)}({\boldsymbol{X}})=P_{\lambda}({\boldsymbol{X}}),\quad R_{\lambda}^{(m+1)}({\boldsymbol{X}})=P_{\lambda}({\boldsymbol{X}})+Q_{\lambda}(R_{\lambda}^{(m)}({\boldsymbol{X}})).

(32)

The conservativity of the minimal semigroup is equivalent to
R_{\lambda}^{min}(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}})=\frac{1}{\lambda}\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}
for every
\lambda>0
. Chebotarev and Fagnola derived several necessary and sufficient conditions for this to hold in
[
CF93
,
CF98
,
Fag99
]
. In this context, the Lindbladian
\mathcal{L}
is called unital whenever the associated minimal semigroup is conservative.
Finally, the pre-dual semigroup
(\mathcal{S}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
on
\mathcal{K}^{1}
is defined from
(\mathcal{T}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
through
Eq.

7
.
Given an initial condition
{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{0}\in\mathcal{K}^{1}
,
we denote
{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{t}=\mathcal{S}_{t}({\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{0})
the solution of the Lindblad equation.
The conservativity of the semigroup
(\mathcal{T}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is equivalent to the trace-preserving property of
(\mathcal{S}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
on
\mathcal{K}^{1}
.

Davies provided another sufficient and necessary condition
[
Dav77
, Theorem 3.2]
linking conservativity with the domain of the generator of the pre-dual semigroup, i.e., the Lindbladian
\mathcal{L}
:

Proposition 1
.

[
Fag99
, Proposition 3.32]

The linear manifold
\mathcal{U}
generated by rank one operators

\displaystyle\ket{u}\bra{v},\qquad u,v\in\mathcal{D}({\boldsymbol{G}})

(33)

is contained in the generator of predual semigroup
(\mathcal{S}_{t}^{min})
of
(\mathcal{T}_{t}^{min})
and

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\ket{u}\bra{v})=\ket{{\boldsymbol{G}}u}\bra{v}+\ket{u}\bra{{\boldsymbol{G}}v}+\sum_{j=1}^{N_{d}}\ket{{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}u}\bra{{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}v}.

(34)

Besides, the following conditions are equivalent:

1.

the linear manifold
\mathcal{U}
is a core for
\mathcal{L}
,

2.

the minimal semigroup
(\mathcal{T}_{t}^{min})
is conservative.

2.4
Regularity results

Inspired by
[
CGQ97
]
and the recent paper
[
GMR24
]
, we introduce some tools to obtain
a priori
estimates. Let us consider a given self-adjoint operator
({\boldsymbol{\Lambda}},D({\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}))
on
\mathcal{H}
with
{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}\geq\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}
.
We define the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}^{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}}=\left(\mathcal{D}({\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{1/2}),\left\|\cdot\right\|_{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}}\right)
where
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}}
is defined from the inner product

\displaystyle\braket{\varphi|\psi}_{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}=\braket{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{1/2}\varphi|{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{1/2}\psi}.

When the context ensures unambiguity, we denote
\mathcal{H}^{0}=\mathcal{H}\supset\mathcal{H}^{1}=\mathcal{H}^{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}\supset\mathcal{H}^{2}=\mathcal{H}^{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{2}}\ldots

We also introduce
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}=\cap_{k\geq 0}\mathcal{H}^{k}
, which is dense in all the
\mathcal{H}^{k}
equipped with their norms.

Associated to these spaces, we introduce a hierarchy of Banach spaces
of trace-class operators

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{k}}=\{{\boldsymbol{\rho}}={\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{-k/2}{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{-k/2}\mid{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}\in\mathcal{K}^{1}(\mathcal{H}),\,\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right\|_{\mathcal{K}_{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{k}}}=\left\|{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}\right\|_{1}\}

(35)

Note that the mapping
{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}\to{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{-k/2}{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{-k/2}
is an isometry from
\mathcal{K}^{1}
to
\mathcal{K}_{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{k}}
.
To prove that an element of
\mathcal{K}^{1}
belongs to
\mathcal{K}_{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{k}}
, we have the following characterization:

Lemma 1
.

Let
{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\in\mathcal{K}^{1}_{+}
, and
k,r\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
. The following properties are equivalent:

1.

{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\in\mathcal{K}_{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{k}}
and
\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right\|_{\mathcal{K}_{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{k}}}=r
, i.e., there exists
{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}\in\mathcal{K}^{1}_{+}
with
\left\|{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}\right\|_{1}=r
such that
{\boldsymbol{\rho}}={\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{-k/2}{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{-k/2}
,

2.

\sup_{n>0}\operatorname{Tr}\left(n{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{k}R(n,-{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{k}){\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right)=r<\infty
.

Proof.

Assume
{\boldsymbol{\rho}}={\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{-k/2}{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{-k/2}
with
{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}\in\mathcal{K}^{1}_{+}
. We have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}\left(n{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{k}R(n,-{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{k}){\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Tr}\left({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}^{1/2}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{-k/2}n{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{k}R(n,-{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{k}){\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{-k/2}{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}^{1/2}\right)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Tr}\left({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}^{1/2}nR(n,-{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{k}){\boldsymbol{\sigma}}^{1/2}\right)\leq\operatorname{Tr}\left({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}\right)

where we used
nR(n,-{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{k})=(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}+n^{-1}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{k})^{-1}\leq\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}
. Besides,
nR(n,-{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{k})\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}
in the strong operator topology by well-known properties of the resolvent. Thus, it converges in the weak operator topology. Besides, as weak and
\sigma
-weak topologies coincide on bounded sets,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\operatorname{Tr}\left(nR(n,-{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{k}){\boldsymbol{\sigma}}\right)=\operatorname{Tr}\left({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}\right).

Conversely, assume
Item

2
. Then, for any
\psi\in\mathcal{H}
with
\left\|\psi\right\|=1
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\left(n{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{k}R(n,-{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{k})\right)^{1/2}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}^{1/2}\ket{\psi}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{Tr}\left(n{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{k}R(n,-{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{k}){\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{n>0}\operatorname{Tr}\left(n{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{k}R(n,-{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{k}){\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right)<\infty.

The sequence
\left(nR(n,-{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{k})\right)^{1/2}
converges strongly towards
\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}
while keeping a norm smaller than
1
.
The sequence
\left(nR(n,-{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{k})\right)^{1/2}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}^{1/2}\ket{\psi}
is thus bounded in
\mathcal{D}({\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{k/2})
. As a consequence,
{\boldsymbol{\rho}}^{1/2}\ket{\psi}
belongs to the domain of
{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{k/2}
. Hence,
\xi={\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{k/2}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}^{1/2}
is a well-defined bounded operator. Using again the
\sigma
-weak convergence of
nR(n,-{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{k})
, we get

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}\left(n{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{k}R(n,-{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{k}){\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right)=\operatorname{Tr}\left(\xi^{\dagger}R(n,-{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{k})\xi\right)\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}\operatorname{Tr}\left(\xi^{\dagger}\xi\right)

we deduce that
\xi
is an Hilbert-Schmidt operator,
{\boldsymbol{\rho}}={\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{-1/2}\xi^{\dagger}\xi{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{-1/2}
and
\left\|\xi^{\dagger}\xi\right\|_{1}=r
.

∎

The forthcoming proposition will serve to establish a connection between the regularity of the state
{\boldsymbol{\rho}}
and bounds on
\mathcal{L}({\boldsymbol{\rho}})
.

Proposition 2
.

Let
k\geq 0
. For every
{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\in\mathcal{K}_{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{k}}
and
{\boldsymbol{M}}_{1},{\boldsymbol{M}}_{2}\in B(\mathcal{H}^{k},\mathcal{H})
,
we have
{\boldsymbol{M}}_{1}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}{\boldsymbol{M}}_{2}^{\dagger}\in\mathcal{K}^{1}
and

\displaystyle\left\|{\boldsymbol{M}}_{1}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}{\boldsymbol{M}}_{2}^{\dagger}\right\|_{1}\leq\left\|{\boldsymbol{M}}_{1}\right\|_{B(\mathcal{H}^{k},\mathcal{H})}\left\|{\boldsymbol{M}}_{2}\right\|_{B(\mathcal{H}^{k},\mathcal{H})}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right\|_{\mathcal{K}_{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{k}}}

(36)

Proof.

Since
{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\in{\mathcal{K}_{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{k}}}
, there exists
{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}\in\mathcal{K}^{1}
such that
{\boldsymbol{\rho}}={\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{-k/2}{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{-k/2}
and
\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right\|_{\mathcal{K}_{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{k}}}=\left\|{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}\right\|_{1}
.
Moreover,
\left\|{\boldsymbol{M}}_{1}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{-k/2}\right\|_{\infty}=\left\|{\boldsymbol{M}}_{1}\right\|_{B(\mathcal{H}^{k},\mathcal{H})}
and
\left\|{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{-k/2}{\boldsymbol{M}}_{2}^{\dagger}\right\|_{\infty}=\left\|{\boldsymbol{M}}_{2}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{-k/2}\right\|_{\infty}=\left\|{\boldsymbol{M}}_{2}\right\|_{B(\mathcal{H}^{k},\mathcal{H})}
,
which concludes the proof.
∎

Remark 2
.

In this paper, we consider
{\boldsymbol{M}}_{2}
both as an unbounded operator on
\mathcal{H}
and as an element of
B(\mathcal{H}^{k},\mathcal{H})
. The notation
{\boldsymbol{M}}_{2}^{\dagger}
may lead to ambiguity since it refers to both the unbounded operator from
\mathcal{D}({\boldsymbol{M}}_{2}^{\dagger})
to
\mathcal{H}
and the continuous operator in
B(\mathcal{H}^{*},\mathcal{H}^{k*})
. However, they coincide on
\mathcal{D}({\boldsymbol{M}}_{2}^{\dagger})
under the duality induced by the pivot space
\mathcal{H}
. Henceforth, we adopt the convention that the operator on the right of
{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\in\mathcal{K}_{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{k}}
represents the continuous operator from
\mathcal{H}
to
(\mathcal{H}^{k})^{*}
. Note also that, following the classical literature on Sobolev spaces, we could define the negative Sobolev space
\mathcal{H}^{-k}\subset\mathcal{H}
as
\mathcal{H}^{k*}
.

3
Time discretization in infinite dimension

3.1
Infinite dimensional schemes

In this section,
{\boldsymbol{G}}
and
{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}
denote the unbounded operators defining our Lindblad equation.
Transposing
Section

2.2.2
, we define a time discretization scheme
\mathcal{F}_{\Delta t}
by

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{Q}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{N_{d}}{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}^{\dagger}{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j},

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{M}}_{0,\Delta t}

\displaystyle=(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}-i\frac{\Delta t}{2}{\boldsymbol{H}})(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}+i\frac{\Delta t}{2}{\boldsymbol{H}})^{-1}(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}-\frac{\Delta t}{2}{\boldsymbol{Q}})

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{M}}_{j,\Delta t}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\Delta t}{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j},

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{S}}_{\Delta t}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{k}{\boldsymbol{M}}_{j,\Delta t}^{\dagger}{\boldsymbol{M}}_{j,\Delta t}=\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}+\frac{\Delta t^{2}}{4}{\boldsymbol{Q}}^{2},

\displaystyle\widetilde{\boldsymbol{M}}_{j,\Delta t}

\displaystyle={\boldsymbol{M}}_{j,\Delta t}{\boldsymbol{S}}_{\Delta t}^{-1/2},

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\Delta t}({\boldsymbol{\rho}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{M}}_{j,\Delta t}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{M}}_{j,\Delta t}^{\dagger}.

(37)

Our main task is now to prove that, in this infinite-dimensional setting and under suitable assumptions, this scheme is still well-defined
and provides a first-order approximation of the continous solution to the Lindblad equation, which is
the object of our main
Theorem

2
.
Let us start with some basic remarks about the well-posedness of
\mathcal{F}
. Assuming that the positive operator
{\boldsymbol{Q}}
defined on
\cap_{j=1}^{N_{d}}\mathcal{D}({\boldsymbol{L}}_{j})
is essentially self-adjoint,
{\boldsymbol{S}}_{\Delta t}^{-1/2}
is a well-defined bounded operator on
\mathcal{H}
. In addition, note that

(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}-i\frac{\Delta t}{2}{\boldsymbol{H}})(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}+i\frac{\Delta t}{2}{\boldsymbol{H}})^{-1}
is a unitary operator and an approximation of
e^{-i\Delta t{\boldsymbol{H}}}
. In particular,

\displaystyle\widetilde{\boldsymbol{M}}_{0,\Delta t}^{\dagger}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{M}}_{0,\Delta t}={\boldsymbol{S}}_{\Delta t}^{-1/2}(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}-\frac{\Delta t}{2}{\boldsymbol{Q}})^{2}{\boldsymbol{S}}_{\Delta t}^{-1/2}\leq\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}},\qquad

\displaystyle\widetilde{\boldsymbol{M}}_{j,\Delta t}^{\dagger}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{M}}_{j,\Delta t}\leq{\boldsymbol{S}}_{\Delta t}^{-1/2}{\boldsymbol{Q}}{\boldsymbol{S}}_{\Delta t}^{-1/2}\leq\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}.

We deduce that
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{M}}_{0,\Delta t}
and
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{M}}_{j,\Delta t}
are well-defined and bounded. In fact, we again have

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{k}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{M}}_{j,\Delta t}^{\dagger}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{M}}_{j,\Delta t}=\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}.

Thus
\mathcal{F}_{\Delta t}
is a CPTP map on
B(\mathcal{H})
.

Remark 3
.

The results of this section (including
Theorem

2
) can be straightforwardly adapted
to accommodate the
following variations of the scheme
\mathcal{F}_{\Delta t}
:

•

Replace
{\boldsymbol{M}}_{j,\Delta t}
for
1\leq j\leq N_{d}
by
(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}-i\frac{\Delta t}{2}{\boldsymbol{H}})(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}+i\frac{\Delta t}{2}{\boldsymbol{H}})^{-1}{\boldsymbol{M}}_{j,\Delta t}
, that is performing the splitting consisting of first computing the dynamics corresponding to the non-Hamiltonian part, then applying the Hamiltonian evolution.

•

Remove
(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}-i\frac{\Delta t}{2}{\boldsymbol{H}})(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}+i\frac{\Delta t}{2}{\boldsymbol{H}})^{-1}
from
{\boldsymbol{M}}_{0,\Delta t}
but applies it to all
{\boldsymbol{M}}_{j,\Delta t}
for
0\leq j\leq N
on the right. This corresponds to the first order splitting starting with the Hamiltonian part.

Crucially, in all these variations,
{\boldsymbol{S}}_{\Delta t}
does not depend on
{\boldsymbol{H}}
.
In fact, without extra assumptions, we are not able to replace
{\boldsymbol{M}}_{0,\Delta t}
by the
(perhaps more natural) choice
(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}+\Delta t{\boldsymbol{G}})
as this would imply that
{\boldsymbol{S}}_{\Delta t}
depends on
{\boldsymbol{G}}^{\dagger}{\boldsymbol{G}}
instead of
{\boldsymbol{Q}}^{2}
, and
{\boldsymbol{Q}}^{2}
is not controlled by
{\boldsymbol{G}}^{\dagger}{\boldsymbol{G}}
which causes issues to adapt step 3 of
Section

a priori

3.2.1
Simplified assumption when there is no Hamiltonian

We start with the case
{\boldsymbol{H}}=0
.

Assumption 1
.

We consider a finite set of closed operators
({\boldsymbol{L}}_{j})_{1\leq j\leq N_{d}}
such that

1.

\cap_{j}\mathcal{D}({\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}^{\dagger}{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j})
is dense in
\mathcal{H}
and the operator
\sum_{j=1}^{N_{d}}{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}^{\dagger}{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}
defined on
\cap_{j}\mathcal{D}({\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}^{\dagger}{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j})
is essentially self-adjoint. We denote
({\boldsymbol{Q}},\mathcal{D}({\boldsymbol{Q}}))
its closure. Additionally, we define
{\boldsymbol{G}}=-2{\boldsymbol{Q}}
with domain
\mathcal{D}({\boldsymbol{G}})=\mathcal{D}({\boldsymbol{Q}})
.

2.

We consider the positive operator
{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}={\boldsymbol{Q}}+\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}>0
, and introduce, as in
Section

2.4
, the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}^{n}
, that is
\mathcal{D}({\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{n/2})\cong\mathcal{D}({\boldsymbol{G}}^{n/2})
with inner product
\braket{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{n/2}\cdot|{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{n/2}\cdot}
. We also assume that
{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}
belong to
B(\mathcal{H}^{5},\mathcal{H}^{4})
and there exists a constant
\gamma\geq 0
such that for all
u\in\mathcal{H}^{6}

\displaystyle 2\operatorname*{Re}\braket{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{2}{\boldsymbol{G}}u|{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{2}u}+\sum_{j=1}^{N_{d}}\|{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{2}{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}u\|^{2}\leq\gamma\|{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{2}u\|^{2}

(38)

Formally,
Eq.

38
means

\mathcal{L}^{*}({\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{4})\leq\gamma{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{4}
. This assumption is the key element to prove
a priori
estimates.

Let us check that
Assumption

1
implies that the associated Lindblad equation admits a minimal semigroup. Indeed, for every
u\in\cap_{j}\mathcal{D}({\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}^{\dagger}{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j})
, we have from the definition of
{\boldsymbol{G}}

\displaystyle 2\operatorname*{Re}\braket{{\boldsymbol{G}}u|u}=-\sum_{j=1}^{N_{d}}\|{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}u\|^{2}.

As the
{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}
are closed, this implies that
\mathcal{D}({\boldsymbol{G}})\subset\cap_{j=1}^{N_{d}}\mathcal{D}({\boldsymbol{L}}_{j})
. Besides,
{\boldsymbol{G}}=-2{\boldsymbol{Q}}
is self-adjoint and dissipative, thus the generator of a semigroup of contractions. Hence, there exists a minimal quantum dynamical semigroup solution of the Lindblad equation by
Theorem

1
.

Note also that, by definition of the spaces
\mathcal{H}^{n}
,
{\boldsymbol{Q}}\in B(\mathcal{H}^{n+2},\mathcal{H}^{n})
for all even number
n
. On the other hand,
{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}^{\dagger}{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}\leq{\boldsymbol{Q}}
implies that
{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}\in B(\mathcal{H}^{1},\mathcal{H})
. Together with the assumption that
{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}\in B(\mathcal{H}^{5},\mathcal{H}^{4})
, we get by interpolation (see
Appendix

B
) that
{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}\in B(\mathcal{H}^{n+1},\mathcal{H}^{n})
for
0\leq n\leq 5
.

{\boldsymbol{H}}\neq 0

{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}

{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}\geq\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}

.

The operator
{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}\geq\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}

The operators

{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}

{\boldsymbol{H}}\in B(\mathcal{H}^{6},\mathcal{H}^{4})

{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}\in B(\mathcal{H}^{5},\mathcal{H}^{4})

{\boldsymbol{Q}}\in B(\mathcal{H}^{6},\mathcal{H}^{4})

3.

There exists

\displaystyle 2\operatorname*{Re}\braket{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{2}{\boldsymbol{G}}u|{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{2}u}+\sum_{j=1}^{N_{d}}\|{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{2}{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}u\|^{2}\leq\gamma\|{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{2}u\|^{2}.

4.

The closure of the operator

is the generator of a semigroup of contractions on

We have the following
a priori

Lemma 2
.

Under
Assumption

1
or
Assumption

2
, the minimal semigroup
(\mathcal{T}_{t}^{min})_{t\geq 0}
is conservative and the following a priori estimate holds for every
t\geq 0
:

\displaystyle\sup_{n}\braket{u|\mathcal{T}_{t}^{min}(n{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{4}R(n,-{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{4}))u}\leq e^{\gamma t}\|{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{2}u\|^{2},\quad\forall u\in\mathcal{H}^{4},

(39)

where
\gamma\geq 0
is the constant defined in
Assumption

1
or
Assumption

2
.

The proof primarily involves establishing estimates by utilizing the representation of the resolvent of the minimal semigroup, as provided in
Eq.

31
. We defer this proof to
Appendix

A
.

Proposition 3
.

Under
Assumption

1
or
Assumption

2
, the restriction of the Lindblad semigroup
\mathcal{S}_{t}
to
\mathcal{K}_{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{4}}
is a
\gamma
-quasi contraction semigroup on
\mathcal{K}_{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{4}}

Proof.

As
{\boldsymbol{S}}_{t}
is positivity preserving, without loss of generality we can restrict our attention to
{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{0}\in\mathcal{K}^{1}_{+}\cap\mathcal{K}_{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{4}}
. Let
{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0}\in\mathcal{K}_{+}^{1}
such that
{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{0}={\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{-2}{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{-2}
. Diagonalization of
{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0}
gives a sequence of orthonormal vector
(\mu_{k})_{k}\in\mathcal{H}^{\mathbb{N}}
and a sequence of positive reals
(\mu_{k})\in L^{1}(\mathbb{N})
such that
{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mu_{k}\ket{u_{k}}\bra{u_{k}}
, where the series converges in trace norm. Defining the truncated sequences
{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0,m}=\sum_{k=0}^{m}\mu_{k}\ket{u_{k}}\bra{u_{k}}
and using
Lemma

2
, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}\left(\mathcal{T}_{t}^{min}(n{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{4}R(n,-{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{4})){\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{-2}{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0,m}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{-2}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{m}\braket{u_{k}|n{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{4}R(n,-{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{4})u_{k}}

(40)

\displaystyle\leq e^{\gamma t}\sum_{k=0}^{m}\|u_{k}\|^{2}

(41)

\displaystyle\leq e^{\gamma t}\|{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0}\|_{1}

(42)

Besides, as
{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0,m}\xrightarrow[m\to\infty]{\mathcal{K}^{1}}{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0}
, the increasing sequence
{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{-2}{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0,m}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{-2}
converges towards
{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{0}
in
\mathcal{K}_{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{4}}
thus in
\mathcal{K}^{1}
, leading to

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}\left(n{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{4}R(n,-{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{4}){\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{t}\right)=\operatorname{Tr}\left(\mathcal{T}_{t}^{min}(n{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{4}R(n,-{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{4})){\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{0}\right)\leq e^{\gamma t}\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{0}\|_{\mathcal{K}_{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{4}}}.

(43)

Finally,
Lemma

1
implies that
\forall t\geq 0
,
{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{t}\in\mathcal{K}_{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{4}}
and
\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{t}\|_{\mathcal{K}_{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{4}}}\leq e^{\gamma t}\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{0}\|_{\mathcal{K}_{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{4}}}
.
∎

3.3
Main Theorem

Let us now state our first main theorem

Theorem 2
.

Assume that
Assumption

1
or
Assumption

2
is satisfied.
Then, there exists
C>0
such that for every final time
T\geq 0
, every
(\Delta t,N)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{N}
such that
\Delta tN=T
, we have for every
{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{0}\in\mathcal{K}^{1}_{+}\cap\mathcal{K}_{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{4}}
,

\displaystyle\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{T}-\mathcal{F}_{\Delta t}^{N}({\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{0})\|_{1}\leq CT\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathcal{K}_{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{4}})}\Delta t.

(44)

Namely, the time-discretized scheme given by
\mathcal{F}_{\Delta t}
is a first order approximation scheme even in the infinite dimensional setting.

The proof relies on the following Lemma whose proof is the object of
Section

3.4
.

Lemma 3

(Consistency)
.

Under
Assumption

1
or
Assumption

2
. There exists a constant
C>0
, such that for every final time
T>0
, initial state
{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{0}\in\mathcal{K}^{1}_{+}\cap\mathcal{K}_{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{4}}
,
\Delta t
and
0\leq t\leq T-\Delta t
, we have

\displaystyle\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{t+\Delta t}-\mathcal{F}_{\Delta t}({\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{t})\|\leq CT\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathcal{K}_{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{4}})}\Delta t^{2}.

(45)

To prove the convergence of the scheme stated in
Theorem

2
we need both consistency of the scheme (
Lemma

3
) and a stability property. For the latter, we use that
\mathcal{F}_{\Delta t}
, being a CPTP map, induces contraction of the trace norm:

\displaystyle\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{T}-\mathcal{F}_{\Delta t}^{N}({\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{0})\|_{1}

\displaystyle=\|\sum_{k=1}^{N}\mathcal{F}_{\Delta t}^{N-k}({\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{k\Delta t}-\mathcal{F}_{\Delta t}({\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{(k-1)\Delta t}))\|_{1}

(46)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{N}\|({\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{k\Delta t}-\mathcal{F}_{\Delta t}({\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{(k-1)\Delta t}))\|_{1}

(47)

\displaystyle\leq CT\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathcal{K}_{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{4}})}\Delta t,

(48)

which concludes the proof of
Theorem

2
.

Under
Assumption

1
or
Assumption

2
, the bound we proved on the accuracy of the scheme increases exponentially with the final time, namely in
Te^{\gamma T}
as the upper bound we have on
\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathcal{K}_{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{4}})}
increases exponentially. Under a stronger version than
Eq.

38
, we can obtain only a linear dependency in
T
.

Corollary 1
.

Assume
Assumption

1
or
Assumption

2
holds, and that there exists
\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1}>0
such that
\forall u\in\mathcal{H}^{6}
,

\displaystyle 2\operatorname*{Re}\braket{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{2}{\boldsymbol{G}}u|{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{2}u}+\sum_{j=1}^{N_{d}}\|{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{2}{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}u\|^{2}\leq\gamma_{0}\|u\|^{2}-\gamma_{1}\|{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{2}u\|^{2}

(49)

then
Theorem

2
holds and one has

\displaystyle\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{T}-\mathcal{F}_{\Delta t}^{N}({\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{0})\|_{1}\leq CT\Delta t\max\left(\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{0}\|_{\mathcal{K}_{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{4}}},\frac{\gamma_{0}}{\gamma_{1}}\right).

(50)

Sketch of proof.

We only have to show that
Eq.

49
implies that for every
{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{0}\in\mathcal{K}_{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{4}}
and
t\geq 0
,
\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{t}\|_{\mathcal{K}_{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{4}}}\leq\max\left(\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{0}\|_{\mathcal{K}_{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{4}}},\frac{\gamma_{0}}{\gamma_{1}}\right)
. Formally
Eq.

49
means that
\mathcal{L}^{*}({\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{4})\leq\gamma_{0}-\gamma_{1}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{4}
, thus
t\mapsto\operatorname{Tr}\left({\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{t}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{4}\right)
is decreasing if
\operatorname{Tr}\left({\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{t}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{4}\right)>\frac{\gamma_{0}}{\gamma_{1}}
. We refer to
[
RRS24
, Section 4.4.3]
for the proof of this statement.
∎

We also have a non-quantitative convergence for a non-regular initial state:

Corollary 2
.

Under
Assumption

1
or
Assumption

2
, for any final time
T>0
, for every
{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{0}\in\mathcal{K}^{1}_{+}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{T/N}^{N}({\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{0})\xrightarrow[N\to\infty]{\mathcal{K}^{1}}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{T}

(51)

Proof.

For every
\epsilon>0
, by density of
\mathcal{K}_{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{4}}
in
\mathcal{K}^{1}
, there exists
\tilde{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{0}\in\mathcal{K}^{1}_{+}\cap\mathcal{K}_{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{4}}
such that
\|\tilde{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{0}-{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{0}\|_{1}\leq\epsilon/3
. By
Theorem

2
, there exists
N
such that
\|\mathcal{S}_{t}(\tilde{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{0})-\mathcal{F}^{N}_{T/N}(\tilde{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{0})\|_{1}\leq\epsilon/3
. Moreover, as both
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{S}_{t}
are CPTP, they contract the trace norm so

\|\mathcal{S}_{t}(\tilde{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{0})-\mathcal{S}_{t}({\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{0})\|_{1}\leq\epsilon/3
and

\|\mathcal{F}^{N}_{T/N}(\tilde{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{0})-\mathcal{F}^{N}_{T/N}({\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{0})\|_{1}\leq\epsilon/3
.
∎

3.4.1
Preliminaries and a Duhamel formula

Lemma 4
.

{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\in\mathcal{K}^{1}_{+}\cap\mathcal{K}_{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{2}}
, then
{\boldsymbol{\rho}}
is in the domain of the Lindbladian and

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}({\boldsymbol{\rho}})={\boldsymbol{G}}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}+{\boldsymbol{\rho}}{\boldsymbol{G}}^{\dagger}+\sum_{j=1}^{N_{d}}{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}^{\dagger}

(52)

Before proving this Lemma, let us recall that in line with
Remark

2
,
{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{s}{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}^{\dagger}\in\mathcal{K}^{1}
is a slight abuse of notation. Indeed, one should consider
{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}^{\dagger}
as an element of
B(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H}^{1,*})
(the adjoint of the continuous operator
{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}:\mathcal{H}^{1}\to\mathcal{H}
) and not as
{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}^{\dagger}:\mathcal{D}({\boldsymbol{L}}^{\dagger}_{j})\to\mathcal{H}
(the adjoint of the unbounded operator
{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}:\mathcal{D}({\boldsymbol{L}}_{j})\to\mathcal{H}
). Hence, with our convention
{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{s}{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}^{\dagger}
is indeed a bounded operator on
\mathcal{H}
and an element of
\mathcal{K}^{1}
. The same applies to
{\boldsymbol{\rho}}{\boldsymbol{G}}^{\dagger}
.

Proof.

We use that the linear span
\mathcal{U}
generated by

\ket{u}\bra{v}
for
u,v\in\mathcal{H}^{2}\subset\mathcal{D}({\boldsymbol{G}})
is in the domain of
\mathcal{L}
by
Proposition

1
(and is even a core in our case). We decompose

{\boldsymbol{\rho}}={\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{-1}{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{-1}
and
{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mu_{k}\ket{u_{k}}\bra{u_{k}}
with
u_{k}
an orthonormal basis and
\mu_{k}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{N})
. The sequence

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{m}\mu_{k}\ket{u_{k}}\bra{u_{k}}

(53)

converges towards
{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}
. Besides
{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{m}={\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{-1}{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{m}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{-1}

\mathcal{U}
and converges in

towards
{\boldsymbol{\rho}}
. Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}({\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{m})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{m}\mu_{k}\left(\ket{{\boldsymbol{G}}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{-1}u_{k}}\bra{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{-1}u_{k}}+\ket{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{-1}u_{k}}\bra{{\boldsymbol{G}}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{-1}u_{k}}+\sum_{j=1}^{N_{d}}\ket{{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{-1}u_{k}}\bra{{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{-1}u_{k}}\right)

We have

Lemma 5
.

Let
({\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{s})_{0\leq s\leq t}\in L^{\infty}(0,t;\mathcal{K}^{1}_{+}\cap\mathcal{K}_{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{2}})
be the solution of the Lindblad equation, then for all

\displaystyle=e^{t{\boldsymbol{G}}}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{0}e^{t{\boldsymbol{G}}}+\sum_{j=1}^{N_{d}}\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s){\boldsymbol{G}}}{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{s}{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}^{\dagger}e^{(t-s){\boldsymbol{G}}^{\dagger}}ds.

The application

\displaystyle\frac{d}{ds}(e^{(t-s){\boldsymbol{G}}}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{s}e^{(t-s){\boldsymbol{G}}^{\dagger}})

\displaystyle=e^{(t-s){\boldsymbol{G}}}{\boldsymbol{G}}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{s}e^{(t-s){\boldsymbol{G}}^{\dagger}}+e^{(t-s){\boldsymbol{G}}}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{s}{\boldsymbol{G}}e^{(t-s){\boldsymbol{G}}^{\dagger}}

(55)

\displaystyle+e^{(t-s){\boldsymbol{G}}}\mathcal{L}({\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{s})e^{(t-s){\boldsymbol{G}}^{\dagger}}

(56)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{N_{d}}e^{(t-s){\boldsymbol{G}}}{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{s}{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}^{\dagger}e^{(t-s){\boldsymbol{G}}^{\dagger}}

(57)

Then, we integrate over
[0,t]
to concludes.
∎

Let
{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{0}\in\mathcal{K}_{+}^{1}\cap\mathcal{K}_{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{4}}
, by
Proposition

3
, for every
t\geq 0
,
{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{t}\in\mathcal{K}_{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{4}}
, thus it belongs to
\mathcal{K}_{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{2}}
. Consequently, we ascertain the applicability of the Duhamel formula established in
Lemma

5
. Thus, the proof of

3
is reduced to demonstrating the existence of positive constants
C_{0}
and
C_{j}
such that

\displaystyle\|e^{\Delta t{\boldsymbol{G}}}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{t}e^{\Delta t{\boldsymbol{G}}^{\dagger}}-\widetilde{\boldsymbol{M}}_{0,\Delta t}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{t}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{M}}_{0,\Delta t}^{\dagger}\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathcal{K}_{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{4}})}\Delta t^{2},

3.4.2
Proof of estimate (
58
)

First, we have that
(e^{t{\boldsymbol{G}}})_{t\geq 0}
is a semigroup of contraction, thus
\|e^{t{\boldsymbol{G}}}\|_{\infty}\leq 1
. Besides,
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{M}}_{0,\Delta t}^{\dagger}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{M}}_{0,\Delta t}\leq\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}
, hence
\|\widetilde{\boldsymbol{M}}_{0,\Delta t}\|_{\infty}\leq 1
.
Therefore, by introducing the cross-terms

\pm(e^{\Delta t{\boldsymbol{G}}}-\widetilde{\boldsymbol{M}}_{0,\Delta t}){\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{t}e^{\Delta t{\boldsymbol{G}}^{\dagger}},

we compute

\displaystyle\|e^{\Delta t{\boldsymbol{G}}}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{t}e^{\Delta t{\boldsymbol{G}}}-\widetilde{\boldsymbol{M}}_{0,\Delta t}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{t}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{M}}_{0,\Delta t}^{\dagger}\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\|(e^{\Delta t{\boldsymbol{G}}}-\widetilde{\boldsymbol{M}}_{0,\Delta t}){\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{t}e^{\Delta t{\boldsymbol{G}}}\|_{1}+\|\widetilde{\boldsymbol{M}}_{0,\Delta t}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{t}(e^{\Delta t{\boldsymbol{G}}}-\widetilde{\boldsymbol{M}}_{0,\Delta t}^{\dagger})\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|(e^{\Delta t{\boldsymbol{G}}}-\widetilde{\boldsymbol{M}}_{0,\Delta t}){\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{t}\|_{1}.

All that remains to do is prove that
\|(e^{\Delta t{\boldsymbol{G}}}-\widetilde{\boldsymbol{M}}_{0,\Delta t})\|_{B(\mathcal{H}^{4},\mathcal{H})}\leq C(\Delta t)^{2}
.

Under
Assumption

1
(
{\boldsymbol{H}}=0
)

We have

\widetilde{\boldsymbol{M}}_{0,\Delta t}=(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}-\Delta t\frac{{\boldsymbol{Q}}}{2})(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}+(\Delta t\frac{{\boldsymbol{Q}}}{2})^{2})^{-1/2}.

Besides, for any positive real number
x\geq 0
,

0\leq e^{-x}-\frac{1-x}{\sqrt{1+x^{2}}}\leq x^{2}.

This implies that

\displaystyle 0\leq e^{-\Delta t\frac{{\boldsymbol{Q}}}{2}}-\widetilde{\boldsymbol{M}}_{0,\Delta t}\leq\frac{(\Delta t{\boldsymbol{Q}})^{2}}{4}.

(60)

As a consequence,

\displaystyle\|e^{-\Delta t\frac{{\boldsymbol{Q}}}{2}}-\widetilde{\boldsymbol{M}}_{0,\Delta t}\|_{B(\mathcal{H}^{4},\mathcal{H})}\leq C\Delta t^{2},

(61)

leading to

\displaystyle\|(e^{\Delta t{\boldsymbol{G}}}-\widetilde{\boldsymbol{M}}_{0,\Delta t}){\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{t}\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq C\Delta t^{2}\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{t}\|_{\mathcal{K}_{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{4}}}\leq C\Delta t^{2}\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathcal{K}_{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{4}})}

(62)

Which concludes the proof of estimate (

).

\displaystyle(e^{\Delta t{\boldsymbol{G}}}-(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}+\Delta t{\boldsymbol{G}}))\ket{\psi}=\int_{0}^{\Delta t}(\Delta t-s)e^{s{\boldsymbol{G}}}{\boldsymbol{G}}^{2}\ket{\psi},\quad\ket{\psi}\in\mathcal{D}({\boldsymbol{G}}^{2})

for every
\ket{\psi}\in\mathcal{H}^{4}\subset\mathcal{D}({\boldsymbol{G}}^{2})
. In the next equations, we consider both side of the equation as elements of
B(\mathcal{H}^{4},\mathcal{H})
. We can then compute

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{M}}_{0,\Delta t}-(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}+\Delta t{\boldsymbol{G}})

\displaystyle=(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}-i\frac{\Delta t}{2}{\boldsymbol{H}})(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}+i\frac{\Delta t}{2}{\boldsymbol{H}})^{-1}(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}-\frac{1}{2}\Delta t{\boldsymbol{Q}})-(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}-i\Delta t{\boldsymbol{H}}-\frac{\Delta t}{2}{\boldsymbol{Q}})

\displaystyle=\left((\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}-i\frac{\Delta t}{2}{\boldsymbol{H}})(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}+i\frac{\Delta t}{2}{\boldsymbol{H}})^{-1}-(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}-\Delta t{\boldsymbol{H}})\right)(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}-\frac{1}{2}\Delta t{\boldsymbol{Q}})

\displaystyle+(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}-i\Delta t{\boldsymbol{H}})(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}-\frac{1}{2}\Delta t{\boldsymbol{Q}})-(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}-i\Delta t{\boldsymbol{H}}-\frac{\Delta t}{2}{\boldsymbol{Q}})

\displaystyle=\left((\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}-i\frac{\Delta t}{2}{\boldsymbol{H}})(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}+i\frac{\Delta t}{2}{\boldsymbol{H}})^{-1}-(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}-i\Delta t{\boldsymbol{H}})\right)(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}-\frac{1}{2}\Delta t{\boldsymbol{Q}})+i(\Delta t)^{2}{\boldsymbol{H}}{\boldsymbol{Q}}.

As
{\boldsymbol{H}}\in B(\mathcal{H}^{2},\mathcal{H})
and
{\boldsymbol{Q}}\in B(\mathcal{H}^{4},\mathcal{H}^{2})
, we have
{\boldsymbol{H}}{\boldsymbol{Q}}\in B(\mathcal{H}^{4},\mathcal{H})
. Thus, we only have to tackle the first term of the last expression. We denote

{\boldsymbol{A}}_{\Delta t}=\left((\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}-i\frac{\Delta t}{2}{\boldsymbol{H}})(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}+i\frac{\Delta t}{2}{\boldsymbol{H}})^{-1}-(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}-i\Delta t{\boldsymbol{H}})\right).

For every
x\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1-\frac{i}{2}x}{1+\frac{i}{2}x}-(1-ix)\right|^{2}\leq\min(x^{2},x^{4}).

(64)

Thus,

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{A}}_{\Delta t}^{\dagger}{\boldsymbol{A}}_{\Delta t}\leq(\Delta t)^{2}{\boldsymbol{H}}^{2},\quad{\boldsymbol{A}}_{\Delta t}^{\dagger}{\boldsymbol{A}}_{\Delta t}\leq(\Delta t)^{4}{\boldsymbol{H}}^{4}

(65)

Multiplying by
{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{-1}
on both side of the first equations shows that
\|{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{-1}{\boldsymbol{A}}_{\Delta t}^{\dagger}{\boldsymbol{A}}_{\Delta t}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{-1}\|_{\infty}\leq C(\Delta t)^{2}
, implying
\|{\boldsymbol{A}}_{\Delta t}\|_{B(\mathcal{H}^{2},\mathcal{H})}\leq C\Delta t
. Similarly we get
\|{\boldsymbol{A}}_{\Delta t}\|_{B(\mathcal{H}^{4},\mathcal{H})}\leq C(\Delta t)^{2}
. Together with the fact that
{\boldsymbol{Q}}\in B(\mathcal{H}^{4},\mathcal{H}^{2})
, we get

\displaystyle\|{\boldsymbol{M}}_{0,\Delta t}-(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}+\Delta t{\boldsymbol{G}})\|_{B(\mathcal{H}^{4},\mathcal{H})}\leq C(\Delta t)^{2}.

(66)

Thus,

\displaystyle\|(e^{\Delta t{\boldsymbol{G}}}-{\boldsymbol{M}}_{0,\Delta t}){\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{t}\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq C\Delta t^{2}\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{t}\|_{\mathcal{K}_{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{4}}}.

(67)

Next, we have

\displaystyle({\boldsymbol{M}}_{0,\Delta t}-\widetilde{\boldsymbol{M}}_{0,\Delta t})

\displaystyle=(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}-i\frac{\Delta t}{2}{\boldsymbol{H}})(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}+i\frac{\Delta t}{2}{\boldsymbol{H}})^{-1}(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}-\frac{1}{2}\Delta t{\boldsymbol{Q}})(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}-{\boldsymbol{S}}_{\Delta t}^{-1/2}).

(68)

As
(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}-i\frac{\Delta t}{2}{\boldsymbol{H}})(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}+i\frac{\Delta t}{2}{\boldsymbol{H}})^{-1}
is unitary, we get

\displaystyle\|({\boldsymbol{M}}_{0,\Delta t}-\widetilde{\boldsymbol{M}}_{0,\Delta t})\|_{B(\mathcal{H}^{4},\mathcal{H})}=\|(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}-\frac{1}{2}\Delta t{\boldsymbol{Q}})(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}-{\boldsymbol{S}}_{\Delta t}^{-1/2})\|_{B(\mathcal{H}^{4},\mathcal{H})}.

Then, using that for every positive real number
x\geq 0
,

\displaystyle-x^{2}\leq(1-x)\left(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+x^{2}}}\right)\leq x^{2},

(69)

we obtain

\displaystyle-(\Delta t)^{2}\frac{{\boldsymbol{Q}}^{2}}{4}\leq(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}-\frac{1}{2}\Delta t{\boldsymbol{Q}})(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}-{\boldsymbol{S}}_{\Delta t}^{-1/2})\leq(\Delta t)^{2}\frac{{\boldsymbol{Q}}^{2}}{4}.

(70)

Hence,
\|(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}-\frac{1}{2}\Delta t{\boldsymbol{Q}})(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}-{\boldsymbol{S}}_{\Delta t}^{-1/2})\|_{B(\mathcal{H}^{4},\mathcal{H})}\leq C(\Delta t)^{2}
, implying

\displaystyle\|({\boldsymbol{M}}_{0,\Delta t}-\widetilde{\boldsymbol{M}}_{0,\Delta t}){\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{t}\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq C\Delta t^{2}\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{t}\|_{\mathcal{K}_{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{4}}}

(71)

3.4.3
Proof of estimate (
59
)

The sketch of proof is the following:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\Delta t}e^{(\Delta t-s){\boldsymbol{G}}}{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{t+s}{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}^{\dagger}e^{(\Delta t-s){\boldsymbol{G}}^{\dagger}}ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\Delta t}e^{(\Delta t-s){\boldsymbol{G}}}{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{t}{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}^{\dagger}e^{(\Delta t-s){\boldsymbol{G}}^{\dagger}}ds+O(\Delta t^{2})

(Step 1)

\displaystyle={\boldsymbol{M}}_{j,\Delta t}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{t}{\boldsymbol{M}}_{j,\Delta t}^{\dagger}+O(\Delta t^{2})

(Step 2)

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\boldsymbol{M}}_{j,\Delta t}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{t}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{M}}_{j,\Delta t}^{\dagger}+O(\Delta t^{2}),

(Step 3)

where
O(\Delta t^{2})
means an element of
\mathcal{K}^{1}
with trace norm bounded by
C\Delta t^{2}\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathcal{K}_{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{4}})}
for
\Delta t
small enough.

Step 1

\displaystyle\|e^{(\Delta t-s){\boldsymbol{G}}}{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}({\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{t+s}-{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{t}){\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}^{\dagger}e^{(\Delta t-s){\boldsymbol{G}}^{\dagger}}\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\|{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}({\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{t+s}-{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{t}){\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}^{\dagger}\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{s}\|{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}\mathcal{L}({\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{t+s^{\prime}}){\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}^{\dagger}\|_{1}ds^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{s}\|{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}({\boldsymbol{G}}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{t+s^{\prime}}-{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{t+s^{\prime}}{\boldsymbol{G}}^{\dagger}+\sum_{i=1}^{N_{d}}{\boldsymbol{L}}_{i}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{t+s^{\prime}}{\boldsymbol{L}}_{i}^{\dagger}){\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}^{\dagger}\|_{1}ds^{\prime}.

(72)

We recall that we assumed that
{\boldsymbol{L}}_{i}\in B(\mathcal{H}^{5},\mathcal{H}^{4})
, besides we have already shown that
{\boldsymbol{L}}_{i}\in B(\mathcal{H}^{1},\mathcal{H}^{0})
thus by classical result of interpolation (see
Appendix

B
),
{\boldsymbol{L}}_{i}\in B(\mathcal{H}^{l+1},\mathcal{H}^{l})
for
0\leq l\leq 4
.
As a consequence, we have
{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}{\boldsymbol{L}}_{i}\in B(\mathcal{H}^{2},\mathcal{H})
, implying

\displaystyle\|{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}{\boldsymbol{L}}_{i}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{t+s^{\prime}}{\boldsymbol{L}}_{i}^{\dagger}{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}^{\dagger}\|_{1}\leq\|{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}{\boldsymbol{L}}_{i}\|^{2}_{B(\mathcal{H}^{2},\mathcal{H})}\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{t+s^{\prime}}\|_{\mathcal{K}_{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{2}}}.

Similarly,
{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}{\boldsymbol{G}}
belongs to
B(\mathcal{H}^{3},\mathcal{H})
, thus

\displaystyle\|{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}{\boldsymbol{G}}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{t+s^{\prime}}{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}^{\dagger}\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\|{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}{\boldsymbol{G}}\|_{B(\mathcal{H}^{3},\mathcal{H})}\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{t+s^{\prime}}\|_{\mathcal{K}_{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{3}}}\|{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}\|_{B(\mathcal{H}^{1},\mathcal{H})}.

(73)

Leading to

\displaystyle\|e^{(\Delta t-s){\boldsymbol{G}}}{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}({\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{t+s}-{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{t}){\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}^{\dagger}e^{(\Delta t-s){\boldsymbol{G}}^{\dagger}}\|_{1}\leq sC\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{t+s^{\prime}}\|_{\mathcal{K}_{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{3}}}.

(74)

We integrate for
s\in[0,\Delta t]
and this concludes step 1

Step 2

We start with

\displaystyle\|e^{(\Delta t-s){\boldsymbol{G}}}{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{t}{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}^{\dagger}e^{(\Delta t-s){\boldsymbol{G}}^{\dagger}}-{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{t}{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}^{\dagger}\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|(e^{(\Delta t-s){\boldsymbol{G}}}-\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}){\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{t}{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}^{\dagger}\|_{1}

(75)

As
\|(e^{(\Delta t-s){\boldsymbol{G}}}-\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}})\|_{B(\mathcal{H}^{2},\mathcal{H})}\leq C(\Delta t-s)
, we get

\displaystyle\|(e^{(\Delta t-s){\boldsymbol{G}}}-\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}){\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{t}{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}^{\dagger}\|_{1}\leq C(\Delta t-s)\|{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}\|_{B(\mathcal{H}^{3},\mathcal{H}^{2})}\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{t}\|_{\mathcal{K}_{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{3}}}\|{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}\|_{B(\mathcal{H}^{1},\mathcal{H})}

Integration for
s\in[0,\Delta t]
leads to the end of step 2.

Step 3

We have

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{M}}_{j,\Delta t}-\widetilde{\boldsymbol{M}}_{j,\Delta t}=\sqrt{\Delta t}{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}-{\boldsymbol{S}}_{\Delta t}^{-1/2})

(76)

Thus

\displaystyle 0\leq({\boldsymbol{M}}_{j,\Delta t}-\widetilde{\boldsymbol{M}}_{j,\Delta t})^{\dagger}({\boldsymbol{M}}_{j,\Delta t}-\widetilde{\boldsymbol{M}}_{j,\Delta t})

\displaystyle=\Delta t(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}-{\boldsymbol{S}}_{\Delta t}^{-1/2}){\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}^{\dagger}{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}-{\boldsymbol{S}}_{\Delta t}^{-1/2})

(77)

\displaystyle\leq\Delta t(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}-{\boldsymbol{S}}_{\Delta t}^{-1/2}){\boldsymbol{Q}}(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}-{\boldsymbol{S}}_{\Delta t}^{-1/2})

(78)

Besides, for every
x\geq 0
, a standard computation shows

\displaystyle 0\leq 2x(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+x^{2}}})^{2}\leq x^{4}.

(79)

Thus,

\displaystyle\Delta t(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}-{\boldsymbol{S}}_{\Delta t}^{-1/2}){\boldsymbol{Q}}(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}-{\boldsymbol{S}}_{\Delta t}^{-1/2})\leq\Delta t^{4}\frac{{\boldsymbol{Q}}^{4}}{2^{4}}.

(80)

As
{\boldsymbol{Q}}^{2}\in B(\mathcal{H}^{4},\mathcal{H})
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|{\boldsymbol{M}}_{j,\Delta t}-\widetilde{\boldsymbol{M}}_{j,\Delta t}\|_{B(\mathcal{H}^{4},\mathcal{H})}\leq C\Delta t^{2},

(81)

which concludes the proof of estimate (
59
), thus of
Lemma

3
and
Theorem

2
.

4
Galerkin approximation, stability of the schemes and numerical results

Now that we have established the convergence of the quantum channel schemes defined in
Eq.

37
for unbounded Lindblad equations, let us dive into the implications for the time discretization of a Galerkin approximation of the Lindblad equation.

4.1
Galerkin approximation

We consider an increasing sequence
(\mathcal{H}_{n})_{n}
of Hilbert space included in
\mathcal{H}
. We raise the attention of the reader to the fact that
\mathcal{H}_{n}
is not related to the space
\mathcal{H}^{n}
introduced in
Section

2.4
.
We denote by
{\boldsymbol{P}}_{n}
the orthogonal projectors onto
\mathcal{H}_{n}
. We assume that
{\boldsymbol{P}}_{n}
converges strongly towards
\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}
and that for all
n
,
\mathcal{H}_{n}\subset\mathcal{D}({\boldsymbol{G}})
. Then, one can define the truncated operators

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{H}}_{n}={\boldsymbol{P}}_{n}{\boldsymbol{H}}{\boldsymbol{P}}_{n},\quad{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j,n}={\boldsymbol{P}}_{n}{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j}{\boldsymbol{P}}_{n}

(82)

and consider the Lindbladian
\mathcal{L}_{n}
defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{n}({\boldsymbol{\rho}})=-i[{\boldsymbol{H}}_{n},{\boldsymbol{\rho}}]+\sum_{j=1}^{N_{d}}D_{{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j,n}}({\boldsymbol{\rho}}).

(83)

As the operators
{\boldsymbol{H}}_{n}
and
{\boldsymbol{L}}_{j,n}
are bounded,
\mathcal{L}_{n}
is the generator of a uniformly continuous quantum Markov semigroup as studied initially by Lindblad in
[
Lin76
]
.

Example 2
.

In the case
\mathcal{H}=L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C})
, we will consider the typical choice
\mathcal{H}_{n}=\{\ket{k}\mid 0\leq k\leq n\}
, where
(\ket{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
denotes the so-called Fock basis (that is the eigenbasis of a quantum harmonic oscillator).

4.2
Explicit analysis of time discretization of the multi-photons loss channel

We consider the following Lindblad equation on
\mathcal{H}=L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C})
:

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}=D[{\boldsymbol{a}}^{l}]({\boldsymbol{\rho}}),

(84)

where
{\boldsymbol{a}}
is the usual annihilation operator on
\mathcal{H}
and
l\geq 1
is a fixed parameter;
this equation models, for instance, a dissipative process where a quantum harmonic oscillator loses
l
-uplets of photons. For
l=1
we obtain the so-called photon loss channel which is the dominant error channel in many quantum experiments, for example in quantum optics or superconducting circuits. On the other hand, for
l\geq 2
, the coherent loss of
l
-uplets of photons can be used as a powerful resource for bosonic error correction–see e.g.
[
Mir+14
,
Leg+15
]
.

It is known that the solutions of
Eq.

84
converge, as
t
goes to infinity, to a density operator that depends on the initial condition and has support on
\operatorname*{Span}\left\{\ket{j}\mid 0\leq j\leq l-1\right\}
.
This is, for instance, a direct application of the results in
[
ASR16a
]
.

The aim of this section is to show that an explicit Euler scheme is subject to a Courant–Friedrichs–Lewy (CFL) type condition: the dimension of the discretization space
\mathcal{H}_{n}
imposes a constraint on the smallness of the time-step
\Delta t
to ensure the stability of the scheme. On the other hand, the quantum channel schemes introduced in this article do not suffer from this weakness and exhibit a robustness in the time discretization independent of the Galerkin truncation.

Remark 5
.

Note that, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the fact that
\mathcal{H}_{n}
is stable by
{\boldsymbol{a}}
entails that the convex cone
\mathcal{K}_{+}^{1}\cap B(\mathcal{H}_{n})
is stable under
Eq.

84
:
in fact, we even have that if
{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{0}\in\mathcal{K}_{+}^{1}\cap B(\mathcal{H}_{n})
, then
\mathcal{S}_{t}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{0}=e^{t\mathcal{L}_{n}}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{0}
.

4.2.1
A CFL condition for the first-order explicit Euler scheme

The first-order explicit Euler scheme applied to the Galerkin truncation
n
yields:

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{1}^{\Delta t,n}({\boldsymbol{\rho}})={\boldsymbol{\rho}}+\Delta t\left({\boldsymbol{a}}^{l}_{n}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}{\boldsymbol{a}}^{l\,{\dagger}}_{n}-\frac{1}{2}({\boldsymbol{a}}^{l\,{\dagger}}_{n}{\boldsymbol{a}}^{l}_{n}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}+{\boldsymbol{\rho}}{\boldsymbol{a}}^{l\,{\dagger}}_{n}{\boldsymbol{a}}^{l}_{n})\right).

(85)

A simple computation shows that for any
0\leq k\leq n
, we have

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{a}}^{l\,{\dagger}}_{n}{\boldsymbol{a}}^{l}_{n}\ket{k}=c_{k}\ket{k},\quad{\boldsymbol{a}}^{l}_{n}\ket{k}=\sqrt{c_{k}}\ket{k-l},\quad c_{k}=\Pi_{j=0}^{l-1}(k-j).

(86)

Note that we don’t make the dependence of
c_{k}
on the parameter
l
explicit to alleviate the notations.

Lemma 6

(CFL condition on
l
-photon loss.)
.

If
\Delta tc_{n}>2
, the scheme
\mathcal{E}_{1}^{\Delta t,n}
is unstable.

Proof.

Focusing on the
(n,n)
term of
{\boldsymbol{\rho}}
, we have the decoupled equation

\displaystyle\bra{n}\mathcal{E}_{1}^{\Delta t,n}({\boldsymbol{\rho}})\ket{n}=(1-\Delta tc_{n})\bra{n}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\ket{n}

(87)

This shows the unstability of the scheme if
\Delta tc_{n}>2
.
∎

Conversely, we have the following stability property for
\Delta tc_{n}<2
:

Lemma 7
.

For a given
n\in\mathbb{N}
, if
\Delta tc_{n}<2
, then for any initial condition
{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{0}\in\mathcal{H}_{n}
, there exists
{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{\infty}\in B(\mathcal{H}_{n})
such that
P_{l}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{\infty}P_{l}={\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{\infty}
and

(\mathcal{E}_{1}^{\Delta t,n})^{p}({\boldsymbol{\rho}})\xrightarrow[p\to\infty]{}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{\infty}
at a geometric rate.

Proof.

Let us assume
\Delta tc_{n}<2
, we have the system of coupled equations

\displaystyle\bra{k}\mathcal{E}_{1}^{\Delta t,n}({\boldsymbol{\rho}})\ket{m}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cr}\left(1-\Delta t\frac{c_{k}+c_{m}}{2}\right)\bra{k}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\ket{m}&\text{ if }\max\{k+l,m+l\}>n\\
\\
\left(1-\Delta t\frac{c_{k}+c_{m}}{2}\right)\bra{k}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\ket{m}+\Delta t\sqrt{c_{k+l}c_{m+l}}\bra{k+l}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\ket{m+l}&\text{ otherwise}\end{array}\right.

Note that this system is triangular,
i.e.

\bra{k}\mathcal{E}_{1}^{\Delta t,n}({\boldsymbol{\rho}})\ket{m}

depends only on values
\bra{k^{\prime}}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\ket{m^{\prime}}
with
k^{\prime}+m^{\prime}\geq k+m
–
hence, we readily deduce that the eigenvalues of
\mathcal{E}_{1}^{\Delta t,n}
are given
by the diagonal coefficients
\left(1-\Delta t\frac{c_{k}+c_{m}}{2}\right)
,
all of magnitude less than
1
since
0\leq c_{k}\leq c_{n}
for
k\leq n
.

∎

4.2.2
Proof of convergence of the quantum channel schemes

Let us now show that the
l
-photon loss channel, modeled by
Eq.

84
, satisfies
Assumption

1
with
{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}=\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}+{\boldsymbol{a}}^{l{\dagger}}{\boldsymbol{a}}^{l}
. Indeed, as
{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{k}
and
\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}+({\boldsymbol{a}}^{{\dagger}}{\boldsymbol{a}})^{kl}
are relatively bounded with respect to one another, we have

\mathcal{H}^{k}=\{\ket{\psi}=\sum_{n}\psi_{n}\ket{n}\mid\sum_{n}(1+n^{kl})|\psi_{n}|^{2}<\infty\}.

(91)

We easily see that
\mathcal{H}^{2}
is dense in
\mathcal{H}
, and
{\boldsymbol{a}}^{l{\dagger}}{\boldsymbol{a}}^{l}
with domain
\mathcal{H}^{2}
is self-adjoint. Besides
{\boldsymbol{L}}={\boldsymbol{a}}^{l}
is bounded from
\mathcal{H}^{n+1}
to
\mathcal{H}^{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. It remains to establish the
a priori
estimate of
Eq.

38
. Formally, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{*}((\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}+{\boldsymbol{L}}^{\dagger}{\boldsymbol{L}})^{4})

\displaystyle={\boldsymbol{L}}^{\dagger}(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}+{\boldsymbol{L}}^{\dagger}{\boldsymbol{L}})^{4}{\boldsymbol{L}}-{\boldsymbol{L}}^{\dagger}{\boldsymbol{L}}(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}+{\boldsymbol{L}}^{\dagger}{\boldsymbol{L}})^{4}

\displaystyle={\boldsymbol{L}}^{\dagger}[(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}+{\boldsymbol{L}}^{\dagger}{\boldsymbol{L}})^{4},{\boldsymbol{L}}].

Introducing
f(k)=\prod_{j=0}^{l-1}(k-j)
, one can check that
{\boldsymbol{L}}^{\dagger}{\boldsymbol{L}}=f({\boldsymbol{a}}^{\dagger}{\boldsymbol{a}})
. Introducing now
g=(1+f)^{4}
and noting that both
f
and
g
are increasing, we get

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{L}}^{\dagger}[(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}+{\boldsymbol{L}}^{\dagger}{\boldsymbol{L}})^{4},{\boldsymbol{L}}]={\boldsymbol{a}}^{{\dagger}l}[g({\boldsymbol{a}}^{\dagger}{\boldsymbol{a}}),{\boldsymbol{a}}^{l}]={\boldsymbol{a}}^{{\dagger}l}{\boldsymbol{a}}^{l}(g({\boldsymbol{a}}^{\dagger}{\boldsymbol{a}}-l\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}})-g({\boldsymbol{a}}^{\dagger}{\boldsymbol{a}}))=f({\boldsymbol{a}}^{\dagger}{\boldsymbol{a}})(g({\boldsymbol{a}}^{\dagger}{\boldsymbol{a}}-l\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}})-g({\boldsymbol{a}}^{\dagger}{\boldsymbol{a}}))\leq 0

(92)

As a consequence,
Eq.

38
holds with
\gamma=0
. We conclude that
Theorem

2
holds; that is, for any
T>0
, there exists
C>0
such that the scheme

\displaystyle\widetilde{\boldsymbol{M}}_{0,\Delta t}

\displaystyle=(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}-\frac{\Delta t}{2}{\boldsymbol{L}}^{\dagger}{\boldsymbol{L}})(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}+\frac{(\Delta t{\boldsymbol{L}}^{\dagger}{\boldsymbol{L}})^{2}}{4})^{-1/2},

(93)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\boldsymbol{M}}_{1,\Delta t}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\Delta t}{\boldsymbol{L}}(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}+\frac{(\Delta t{\boldsymbol{L}}^{\dagger}{\boldsymbol{L}})^{2}}{4})^{-1/2},

(94)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\Delta t}({\boldsymbol{\rho}})

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\boldsymbol{M}}_{0,\Delta t}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{M}}_{0,\Delta t}^{\dagger}+\widetilde{\boldsymbol{M}}_{1,\Delta t}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{M}}_{1,\Delta t}^{\dagger},

(95)

satisfies, for every
(\Delta t,N)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{N}
such that
\Delta tN=T
, and for every
{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{0}\in\mathcal{K}^{1}_{+}\cap\mathcal{K}_{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{4}}
,

\displaystyle\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{T}-\mathcal{F}_{\Delta t}^{N}({\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{0})\|_{1}\leq C\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{0}\|_{\mathcal{K}_{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{4}}}\Delta t.

(96)

Introducing the same quantum channel scheme
\mathcal{F}_{\Delta t,n}
for the Lindblad equation
\mathcal{L}_{n}
on the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{n}
, we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{\boldsymbol{M}}_{0,\Delta t,n}

\displaystyle=(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}-\frac{\Delta t}{2}{\boldsymbol{L}}_{n}^{\dagger}{\boldsymbol{L}}_{n})(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}+\frac{(\Delta t{\boldsymbol{L}}_{n}^{\dagger}{\boldsymbol{L}}_{n})^{2}}{4})^{-1/2},

(97)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\boldsymbol{M}}_{1,\Delta t,n}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\Delta t}{\boldsymbol{L}}_{n}(\operatorname{\mathbf{Id}}+\frac{(\Delta t{\boldsymbol{L}}_{n}^{\dagger}{\boldsymbol{L}}_{n})^{2}}{4})^{-1/2},

(98)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\Delta t,n}({\boldsymbol{\rho}})

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\boldsymbol{M}}_{0,\Delta t,n}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{M}}_{0,\Delta t,n}^{\dagger}+\widetilde{\boldsymbol{M}}_{1,\Delta t,n}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{M}}_{1,\Delta t,n}^{\dagger}.

(99)

Note that the restriction of
\mathcal{F}_{\Delta t}
to element of
B(\mathcal{H}_{n})
coincide with
\mathcal{F}_{\Delta t,n}
,
since
\mathcal{H}_{n}
is stable by both
{\boldsymbol{L}}
and
{\boldsymbol{L}}^{\dagger}{\boldsymbol{L}}
. This leads to:

Proposition 4
.

Let
l\in\mathbb{N}
and
T>0
. There exists a positive constant
C>0
such that, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, every
{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{0}\in\mathcal{K}^{1}_{+}\cap B(\mathcal{H}_{n})
and every
(\Delta t,N)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{N}
such that
\Delta tN=T
, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{S}_{t}({\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{0})-\mathcal{F}_{\Delta t,n}^{N}({\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{0})\|_{1}\leq C\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{0}\|_{\mathcal{K}_{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{4}}}\Delta t.

(100)

As mentionned above in
Remark

5
, we have
\mathcal{S}_{t}({\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{0})=e^{t\mathcal{L}_{n}}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{0}
when
{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{0}\in\mathcal{H}_{n}
.
Besides,

{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{0}\in\mathcal{H}_{n}
, i.e.,
{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{0}
is supported only on the first
n+1
Fock states
and in particular

{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{0}\in\mathcal{K}_{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{4}}
.

We emphasize once again that the fact that
\mathcal{S}_{t}
and
e^{t\mathcal{L}_{n}}
coincide on
B(\mathcal{H}_{n})
is a very specific feature of the multi-photon loss channel, and is not to be generically expected in other contexts.
Thus, the generalization of
Proposition

4
to other dynamics would require in particular errors estimates on
\|(\mathcal{S}_{t}-e^{t\mathcal{L}_{n}}){\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{0}\|_{1}
and is left to future work. Nevertheless, in the next section, we numerically illustrate how the quantum channel schemes perform in some other examples.

5
Conclusion and perspectives

We have introduced and investigated a new class of Completely Positive Trace-Preserving schemes for the time discretization of Lindblad master equations. We established that these schemes approximate the exact solution even when the Lindbladian is unbounded. Furthermore, through simple examples and numerical simulations, we showed that these CPTP schemes are robust and not subject to a CFL condition. This advantageous property is not shared by existing non-linear positive trace-preserving schemes.

We believe that the following avenues are worth exploring for future research:

1.

Higher-order schemes.
Higher-order quantum channel schemes can be obtained using the method developed in
[
CL24
]
. However, we have not yet performed an analysis of these schemes in the infinite-dimensional setting. Another interesting question would be to determine the minimum number of matrix operations required per time-step as a function of the order of the scheme. Following
[
CL24
]
, the scaling
of the positivity-preserving schemes presented here is
\Theta(N_{d}^{p})
, where
p
is the desired order and
N_{d}
the number of dissipators entering the Lindblad equation, compared to
\Theta(dN_{d})
for Euler or Runge-Kutta methods. For
p=2
, it is possible to achieve a scaling of
\Theta(N_{d})
; we are still investigating whether this observation could be generalized. This optimization is important for implementing fast, high-order schemes, especially when many jump operators are involved.

2.

Time-dependent Lindbladian.
While we believe that most of our results can be extended to reasonably time-dependent Lindbladians with some technical assumptions, we also think that investigating possible optimizations of the numerical implementation would be valuable. Since
{\boldsymbol{S}}=\sum_{j}{\boldsymbol{M}}_{j}^{\dagger}{\boldsymbol{M}}_{j}
is now time-dependent, the naive generalization of our algorithm would involve computing the inverse of its square root at each time-step. In the noteworthy case where the Hamiltonian is time-dependent but the dissipators are constant, the aforementionned difficulty is enterily circumvented since the matrix
{\boldsymbol{S}}
does not depend on
{\boldsymbol{H}}
(this fundamentally stems from the splitting operated in the definition of
{\boldsymbol{M}}_{0,\Delta t}
).

3.

Convergence of Galerkin approximations and link with time discretization.
This article does not investigate the convergence of the Galerkin approximation, that is the solution of
Eq.

83
, to the solution of the original Lindblad equation. A recent preprint
[
ERR25
]
provides
a posteriori
error estimates, but does not include a proof of convergence. We hypothesise that the
a priori
estimates presented in this article could be leveraged to establish convergence with an explicit rate. Once this is achieved, an interesting next step would be to generalize the results of
Section

4.2.2
to dynamics that lack the stability property described in
Remark

5
. Specifically, this would involve demonstrating that for the CPTP schemes introduced in this paper, the error between the exact solution and the time discretized solution of the Galerkin approximation can be bounded by the sum of two terms: one controlling the time discretization error and the other controlling the Galerkin error.

Appendix B
Interpolation

Lemma 8
.

Let
n_{0}\geq l\geq 0
and
{\boldsymbol{T}}
be a continuous operator from
\mathcal{H}^{l}\to\mathcal{H}^{0}
and from
\mathcal{H}^{n_{0}}\to\mathcal{H}^{n_{0}-l}
.
Then the restriction of
{\boldsymbol{T}}
to
\mathcal{H}^{n_{0}}
can be extended to a continuous operator
\mathcal{H}^{n}\to\mathcal{H}^{n-l}
for all
n\in[n_{0},l]
.

Proof.

This is a standard result of interpolation theory. For every
\ket{\psi}\in\mathcal{H}^{n_{0}},\,\ket{\varphi}\in\mathcal{H}
, we introduce the function
g
defined on the strip
S=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\mid 0\leq\operatorname*{Re}(z)\leq 1\}

\displaystyle g(z)=\bra{\varphi}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{z(n_{0}-l)/2}{\boldsymbol{T}}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{-z(n_{0}-l)/2-l/2}\ket{\psi}

Let us show that
g
is continuous on
S
and analytic on
S^{\circ}
. We rewrite the previous equation as

\displaystyle g(z)=\bra{\varphi}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{(z-1)(n_{0}-l)/2}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{(n_{0}-l)/2}{\boldsymbol{T}}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{-n_{0}/2}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{-z(n_{0}-l)/2-l/2}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{n_{0}/2}\ket{\psi}

(118)

We recall that
{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{(z-1)(n_{0}-l)/2}
,
{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{(n_{0}-l)/2}{\boldsymbol{T}}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{-n_{0}/2}
and
{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{-z(n_{0}-l)/2-l/2}
are bounded on
\mathcal{H}
. Besides

\displaystyle z\mapsto\bra{\varphi}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{(z-1)(n_{0}-l)/2},\quad z\mapsto{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{-z(n_{0}-l)/2-l/2}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{n_{0}/2}\ket{\psi}

(119)

are continuous on
S
and holomorphic (As
{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}
is the generator of an analytic semigroup) on
S^{\circ}
; thus so is
g
.

Let us now compute

\displaystyle|g(it)|

\displaystyle=|\bra{\varphi}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{itn_{0}/2}{\boldsymbol{T}}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{-itn_{0}/2-l/2}\ket{\psi}|

(120)

\displaystyle\leq\|{\boldsymbol{T}}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{l}\to\mathcal{H}}\|\ket{\varphi}\|\|\ket{\psi}\|

(121)

and

\displaystyle|g(1+it)|

\displaystyle=|\bra{\varphi}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{itn_{0}/2+(n_{0}-l)/2}{\boldsymbol{T}}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{-itn_{0}/2-n_{0}/2}\ket{\psi}|

(122)

\displaystyle\leq\|{\boldsymbol{T}}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{n_{0}}\to\mathcal{H}^{n_{0}-l}}\|\ket{\varphi}\|\|\ket{\psi}\|

(123)

We deduce using Hadamard’s three-lines theorem that for
0\leq\theta\leq 1

\displaystyle|g(\theta)|\leq\|\ket{\varphi}\|\|\ket{\psi}\|\|{\boldsymbol{T}}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{n_{0}}\to\mathcal{H}^{n_{0}-l}}^{1-\theta}\|{\boldsymbol{T}}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{l}\to\mathcal{H}}^{\theta}

(124)

Taking the supremum over
\ket{\varphi}
such that
\|\ket{\varphi}\|=1
leads to

\displaystyle\|{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{(\theta n_{0}-l)/2}{\boldsymbol{T}}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}^{-\theta}\ket{\psi}\|\leq\|\ket{\psi}\|\|{\boldsymbol{T}}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{n_{0}}\to\mathcal{H}^{n_{0}-l}}^{1-\theta}\|{\boldsymbol{T}}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{l}\to\mathcal{H}}^{\theta}

(125)

Thus
{\boldsymbol{T}}
can be extended to a bounded linear map
\mathcal{H}^{\theta n_{0}}\to\mathcal{H}^{\theta n_{0}-l}
.
∎

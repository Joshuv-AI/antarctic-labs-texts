Irreducibility of Polynomials with Square Coefficients over Finite Fields
Lior Bary-Soroker, Roy Shmueli

Abstract
. We study a random polynomial of degree n n over the finite field 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} , where the coefficients are independent and identically distributed and uniformly chosen from the squares in 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} . Our main result demonstrates that the likelihood of such a polynomial being irreducible approaches 1 / n + O ( q − 1 / 2 ) 1/n+O(q^{-1/2}) as the field size q q grows infinitely large. The analysis we employ also applies to polynomials with coefficients selected from other specific sets.



1.
Introduction

Consider the random polynomial

(1)

P_{{\underline{\xi}}}\left\lparen x\right\rparen=x^{n}+\xi_{1}x^{n-1}+\xi_{2}x^{n-2}+\dots+\xi_{n},

where the coefficients
\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n}
are independent random variables taking values in a ring
R
.
The main question of interest is what is the probability that
P_{{\underline{\xi}}}\left\lparen x\right\rparen
is irreducible over
R
.

When
R={\mathbb{Z}}
, one expects the probability to be close to
1
. To clarify this, we focus on two central models. The first, often called the large box model, assumes that the degree
n
is fixed and the coefficients
\xi_{i}
are uniformly distributed within the interval
\{-H,\dots,H\}
as
H\to\infty
. In this case, not only is
P_{{\underline{\xi}}}(x)
asymptotically almost surely (a.a.s.) irreducible, but the Galois group is also the full symmetric group a.a.s. The main challenge in this model is to bound the error term and demonstrate that it arises from reducible polynomials (see
[
vdW36
,
Kno57
,
Che63
,
Gal73
,
Kub09
,
Zyw10
,
Die13
,
CD20
,
AGO+21
,
CD23a
,
CD23b
]
). This problem remains open despite a recent major breakthrough
[
Bha21
]
.

In the second model, while the coefficients do not grow, the degree does—for example, when
\xi_{i}
are Rademacher variables. Here, we always condition on
\xi_{n}\neq 0
. In this context,
[
BV19
]
proved that under the General Riemann Hypothesis (GRH), the polynomial is irreducible a.a.s. Unconditional results
[
BSK20
,
BSKK23
]
show that the probability is bounded away from zero in full generality and under mild restrictions tend to
1
(e.g.,
\xi_{i}
are uniformly distributed on an interval of length
\geq 35
). See
[
BSG24
]
for a treatment of a hybrid model in which
\xi_{i}
are as in the large box model, but
n
may grow arbitrarily fast as a function of
H
.

In this work we assume that
R
is a finite field. Let
q
be a prime power and
R={\mathbb{F}}_{q}
the finite field with
q
elements. In this setting, the ring of polynomials
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]
is closely analogous to the ring of integers
\mathbb{Z}
, and therefore the irreducible polynomials are analogous to prime numbers. And indeed, the first result is the prime polynomial theorem
[
Ros02
, Theorem 2.2]
which may be interpreted as the statement that if the
\xi_{i}
are uniform on
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, then

(2)

{\mathbb{P}}\left[P_{{\underline{\xi}}}\left\lparen x\right\rparen\text{ irreducible over }{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\right]=\frac{1}{n}+O\left\lparen q^{-n/2}\right\rparen,

as
q^{n}\to\infty
. There are variants when we fix some of the coefficients, see
[
Pol13
,
Ha16
]
.
Motivated by Maynard’s theorem
[
May19
]
on primes without the digit 7,
Porritt
[
Por19
]

considered polynomials with coefficients that are uniformly distributed in a subset
S\subsetneq{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
.
He proved that if
\#S\geq q-\sqrt{q}/2
then

(3)

{\mathbb{P}}\left[P_{{\underline{\xi}}}\left\lparen x\right\rparen\text{ irreducible over }{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\right]\sim\frac{q}{n\left\lparen q-1\right\rparen},

as
n\to\infty
. See
[
Mos17
]
for generalizations.

In the case that
n
is fixed, there are much stronger results. In
[
Coh72
,
BBSR15
]
, one can fix all coefficients but three, under very mild assumptions. A general criterion by Entin
[
Ent21
]
deals with uniform coefficients in a subset
S\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n}
that is “regular” in some precise sense:

(4)

{\mathbb{P}}\left[P_{{\underline{\xi}}}\left\lparen x\right\rparen\text{ irreducible over }{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\right]=\frac{1}{n}+O_{n}\left\lparen q^{-1/2}\cdot\operatorname{irreg}\left\lparen S\right\rparen\right\rparen,

where
\irreg\left\lparen S\right\rparen
is an explicit constant that depends on
S
, see (
40
).
This asymptotic formula is useful in several classical settings, such as when
S
is a product of arithmetic progressions.

However, when
q
is odd and
S=\left\{\alpha^{2}\;\middle|\;\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\right\}^{n}
is the set of vector with square entries, the error term in (
4
) is bigger than the main term:
\irreg\left\lparen S\right\rparen\geq\left\lparen q^{1/2}-1\right\rparen^{n}
, see (
41

Our main result says that the probability for irreducibility is still asymptotically

:

Theorem 1
.

Let

be a random polynomial as in (

uniformly distributed in

{\mathbb{P}}\left[P_{{\underline{\xi}}}\left\lparen x\right\rparen\text{ irreducible over }{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\right]=\frac{1}{n}+O_{n}\left\lparen q^{-1/2}\right\rparen,

If

are uniform in

, hence it follows from (

is odd.

2.1.
Splitting types

Let
\mathcal{S}_{n}
denote the set of
n
-tuples
{\underline{s}}=\left\lparen s_{1},\dots,s_{n}\right\rparen
of non-negative integers
s_{i}\geq 0
with the constraint
s_{1}+2s_{2}+\dots+ns_{n}=n
. The
splitting type
of a permutation
\sigma
in the symmetric group
\operatorname{Sym}\left\lparen n\right\rparen
on
n
letters is defined as
\SplitType\left\lparen\sigma\right\rparen:={\underline{s}}\in\mathcal{S}_{n}
such that
s_{i}
represents the number of cycles of length
i
in the disjoint cycle decomposition of
\sigma
. Therefore, two permutations in
\operatorname{Sym}\left\lparen n\right\rparen
are conjugate if and only if they share the same splitting type. For
{\underline{s}}\in\mathcal{S}_{n}
, we denote by
C_{{\underline{s}}}
the conjugacy class in
\operatorname{Sym}\left\lparen n\right\rparen
comprising all permutations with splitting type
{\underline{s}}
. The size of
C_{{\underline{s}}}
is determined by Cauchy’s formula:

(10)

\#C_{{\underline{s}}}=n!\left\lparen\prod_{i=1}^{n}i^{s_{i}}\cdot s_{i}!\right\rparen^{-1}.

Let
k
be a field, and let
P\in k\left[x\right]
be a polynomial of degree
n
. The
splitting type
of
P
is
\SplitType\left\lparen P\right\rparen:={\underline{s}}\in\mathcal{S}_{n}
where
s_{i}
is the number of irreducible factors of
P
of degree
i
, counted with multiplicity.

2.2.
Discriminant

Let
k
be a field and let
P\in k\left[x\right]
be a separable polynomial.
Denote by
k_{P}
the splitting field of
P
over
k
and set
G=\Gal\left\lparen k_{P}/k\right\rparen
.
Let
z_{1},\dots,z_{n}
be the roots of
P
in
k_{P}
, and identify
G
with a subgroup of
\operatorname{Sym}\left\lparen n\right\rparen
by the action of
G
on the roots of
P
.

The discriminant of
P
is defined as

(11)

\operatorname{Disc}_{x}P=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\left\lparen z_{i}-z_{j}\right\rparen^{2}\in k^{\times}.

For
\sigma\in G
, we have that
\sigma\left\lparen\sqrt{\operatorname{Disc}_{x}P}\right\rparen=\sign\left\lparen\sigma\right\rparen\sqrt{\operatorname{Disc}_{x}P}
where
\sign\left\lparen\sigma\right\rparen
is the sign of the permutation
\sigma
.
Thus, the following statement is immediate from the fundamental theorem of Galois theory:

Proposition 3
.

Let
k
be a field of characteristic
\neq 2
.
Then
G\not\leq\operatorname{Alt}\left\lparen n\right\rparen
if and only if
\operatorname{Disc}_{x}P
is not a square in
k
.

is of complexity
\leq M
. This means that there exists
m_{i}\leq M
and there exist
S_{i1},\ldots,S_{im_{i}}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
of
\pi
-complexity
\leq M
such that
U_{i}=\bigcup_{j=1}^{m_{i}}S_{ij}
.

If we set

(36)

\mathcal{F}=\left\{\hat{S}=S_{1j_{1}}\times\dots\times S_{nj_{n}}\;\middle|\;1\leq j_{i}\leq m_{i}\right\},

then

\hat{U}:=U_{1}\times\cdots\times U_{n}=\bigcup_{\hat{S}\in\mathcal{F}}\hat{S}.

Let
E
be the event that
\SplitType(P_{{\underline{\xi}}})={\underline{s}}
.
Also, for simplicity we identify subsets
\hat{S}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n}
with the event that
{\underline{\xi}}\in\hat{S}
.
By the inclusion-exclusion principle we get

(37)

\begin{split}{\mathbb{P}}\left[E\right]&=\sum_{i=1}^{\#\mathcal{F}}(-1)^{i-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\hat{S}_{1},\ldots,\hat{S}_{i}\in\mathcal{F}\\
\text{distinct}\end{subarray}}{\mathbb{P}}\left[E\cap\bigcap_{k=1}^{i}\hat{S}_{k}\right]\end{split}

To ease notation, we fix for a moment one tuple
(\hat{S}_{1},\ldots,\hat{S}_{i})
of distinct elements of
\mathcal{F}
, and write
\hat{S}=\bigcap_{k=1}^{i}\hat{S}_{k}
. Since
\hat{S}\subseteq\hat{U}
and
{\underline{\xi}}
is uniform on
\hat{U}
, we have that

(38)

{\mathbb{P}}\left[\hat{S}\right]=\frac{\#\hat{S}}{\#\hat{U}}.

Also, if we condition on
{\underline{\xi}}
to be in
\hat{S}
, then it distributes uniformly on
\hat{S}
. Hence, we may apply
section 5
, noting that by (
32
), the
\pi
-complexity of each factor of
\hat{S}
is
\leq\#\mathcal{F}\cdot M=O_{M}(1)
and using (
38
):

(39)

{\mathbb{P}}\left[E\cap\hat{S}\right]={\mathbb{P}}\left[E\;\middle|\;\hat{S}\right]{\mathbb{P}}\left[\hat{S}\right]=C\cdot{\mathbb{P}}\left[\hat{S}\right]+O_{n,M}(q^{-1/2}\#\hat{U}^{-1})).

where
C=\left\lparen\prod_{i=1}^{n}i^{s_{i}}\cdot s_{i}!\right\rparen^{-1}
.
Plugging (
39
) into (
37
) and noting that the sum in the latter has
O_{n,M}(1)
terms, we deduce that

{\mathbb{P}}\left[E\right]=C\sum_{i=1}^{\#\mathcal{F}}(-1)^{i-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\hat{S}_{1},\ldots,\hat{S}_{i}\in\mathcal{F}\\
\text{distinct}\end{subarray}}{\mathbb{P}}\left[\bigcap_{k=1}^{i}\hat{S}_{k}\right]+O_{n,M}\left\lparen q^{-1/2}\#\hat{U}^{-1}\right\rparen=C+O_{n,M}\left\lparen q^{-1/2}\#\hat{U}^{-1}\right\rparen,

where in the last equation we used the inclusion-exclusion principle for the event
\hat{U}
.
∎

6.
Examples of large size sets of bounded complexity

In
section 1
and
section 3
, the error term may be larger than the main term. This happens if
\#U_{i}\ll_{M}q^{1/2}
for at least one
i
. One the other hand, if
\#U_{i}\gg_{M}q
for all
i
, then the error term is of size
O_{M}(q^{-1/2})
and it tends to zero as
q\to\infty
.

Let
U
be a set of complexity
\leq M
.
The goal of this section is to show that if
\#U\not\gg_{M}q
, then
\#U\ll_{M}1
, and to give a sufficient condition for
\#U\gg_{M}q
.

Since
U
is a union of sets
S
of
\pi
-complexity
\leq M
, we restrict the discussion to the latter sets.

Proposition 19
.

Let
S\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
be a set of
\pi
-complexity
\leq M
. Then either
\#S\ll_{M}1
or
\#S\gg_{M}q
.

Proof.

We use
subsection 2.4
and its notation, taking
n=1
,
t=t_{1}={\underline{t}}
,
\pi_{j}=\pi_{ij}
. Moreover, we identify
\phi\colon{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[t]\to{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
with
\phi(t)\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. For an unramified
\phi\colon{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[t]\to{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
we have that
\phi(t)\in S
if and only if
\mathchoice{\left\lparen\frac{\mathcal{O}_{N}/\mathcal{O}_{F}}{\phi}\right\rparen}{\left\lparen\tfrac{\mathcal{O}_{N}/\mathcal{O}_{F}}{\phi}\right\rparen}{Artinsymbolshouldnotbeinasubscript/superscript}{Artinsymbolshouldnotbeinasubscript/superscript}\cap\pi_{j}^{-1}(H_{j})\neq\varnothing
.

Hence if
\Omega\cap G_{N,1}\neq\varnothing
, then the assertion follows from (
25
). Otherwise,
\phi(t)\not\in S
. In the latter case, only ramified
\phi
may satisfy
\phi(t)\in S
, and hence
\#S=O_{M}(1)
.
∎

Remark 20
.

The proof of
section 6
gives that for large
q
we have

\#S\gg_{M}q
if and only if
\Omega\cap G_{N,1}\neq\varnothing
.

We give a geometric sufficient condition for
\#S
to be large.

Proposition 21
.

Let
M>0
, let
k\leq M
, let

f_{j}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]
be polynomials of positive degree
\leq M
,
j=1,\ldots,k
. Let
C_{j}=\{f_{j}(x)-t\}
and
\pi_{j}\colon C_{j}\to\mathbb{A}^{1}
the projection
\pi_{j}(x,t)=t
. Assume that the fiber product
D=C_{1}\times_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}\dots\times_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}C_{k}
is absolutely irreducible. Then,

\#\bigcap_{j=1}^{k}f_{j}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})\gg_{M}q.

Proof.

Let
\pi\colon D\to\mathbb{A}^{1}
be the natural projection map. Then,
\deg\pi=\prod\deg\pi_{j}=\prod\deg f_{j}\ll_{M}1
. Since
\pi
factors through
\pi_{j}
, for each
j
, we have
\pi(D({\mathbb{F}}_{q}))\subseteq\bigcap_{j}\pi_{j}(C_{j}({\mathbb{F}}_{q}))=\bigcap_{j}f_{j}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
.
It remains to prove that
\pi(\#D({\mathbb{F}}_{q}))\gg_{M}q
. And indeed,
by the Lang-Weil estimates,
\#D({\mathbb{F}}_{q})=q+O_{M}(q^{-1/2})
.
Hence,
\#\pi(D({\mathbb{F}}_{q}))\geq\frac{1}{\deg\pi}q+O_{M}(q^{-1/2})\gg_{M}q
.
∎

7.
Comparison with Entin’s theorem

For a set
\hat{S}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n}
, the irregularity of
\hat{S}
as defined in
[
Ent21
, eq. (4)]
is:

(40)

\irreg\left\lparen\hat{S}\right\rparen=\frac{q^{n}}{\#\hat{S}}\sum_{{\underline{\beta}}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n}}\left\lvert\widehat{{\mathbbm{1}}_{\hat{S}}}\left\lparen{\underline{\beta}}\right\rparen\right\rvert.

Let
S=\{\alpha^{2}|\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\}
.
We compute
\irreg\left\lparen S^{n}\right\rparen

and show that

(41)

\irreg\left\lparen S^{n}\right\rparen\geq\left\lparen\sqrt{q}-1\right\rparen^{n}.

This implies that the error term in Entin’s theorem (
4
) is
\gg q^{(n-1)/2}
, hence it does not imply
section 1
.

Computation of (
41
).

By
[
Ent21
, Lemma 5.1]
,
\irreg\left\lparen S^{n}\right\rparen=\irreg\left\lparen S\right\rparen^{n}
. Hence, its suffice to show that
\irreg\left\lparen S\right\rparen\geq\sqrt{q}-1
.

Consider the quadratic multiplicative character
\chi_{2}\colon{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\to{\mathbb{C}}
defined by

(42)

\chi_{2}\left\lparen\alpha\right\rparen=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }\exists\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times}\text{ such that }\beta^{2}=\alpha,\\
0&\text{if }\alpha=0,\\
-1&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

So for
\alpha\neq 0
we have that
{\mathbbm{1}}_{S}\left\lparen\alpha\right\rparen=\left\lparen 1+\chi_{2}\left\lparen\alpha\right\rparen\right\rparen/2
.
Thus, for
\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times}
,

(43)

\widehat{{\mathbbm{1}}_{S}}\left\lparen\beta\right\rparen=\frac{1}{q}\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}{\mathbbm{1}}_{S}\left\lparen\alpha\right\rparen e_{q}\left\lparen\alpha\beta\right\rparen=\frac{1}{q}\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times}}\frac{1+\chi_{2}\left\lparen\alpha\right\rparen}{2}\cdot e_{q}\left\lparen\alpha\beta\right\rparen+\frac{1}{q}\\
=\frac{1}{2q}\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times}}e_{q}\left\lparen\alpha\beta\right\rparen+\frac{1}{2q}\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times}}\chi_{2}\left\lparen\alpha\right\rparen e_{q}\left\lparen\alpha\beta\right\rparen+\frac{1}{q}.

By orthogonality of characters,
\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times}}e_{q}\left\lparen\alpha\beta\right\rparen=-1
.
By the classical theory of Gauss sums (see e.g.
[
IR90
, Propostion 8.2.2]
),

(44)

\left\lvert\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times}}\chi_{2}\left\lparen\alpha\right\rparen e_{q}\left\lparen\alpha\beta\right\rparen\right\rvert=\sqrt{q}.

Thus by the reverse triangle inequality, we have that

(45)

\left\lvert\widehat{{\mathbbm{1}}_{S}}\left\lparen\beta\right\rparen\right\rvert\geq\frac{1}{2\sqrt{q}}-\frac{1}{q}=\frac{\sqrt{q}-1}{2q}.

Finally, since
\#S=\left\lparen q+1\right\rparen/2
, we substitute (
45
) in (
40
) to get that

(46)

\irreg\left\lparen S\right\rparen=\frac{2q}{q+1}\sum_{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\left\lvert\widehat{{\mathbbm{1}}_{S}}\left\lparen\beta\right\rparen\right\rvert\\
=\frac{2q}{q+1}\sum_{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times}}\left\lvert\widehat{{\mathbbm{1}}_{S}}\left\lparen\beta\right\rparen\right\rvert+\frac{2q}{q+1}\geq\frac{2q}{q+1}\frac{(q-1)(\sqrt{q}-1)}{2q}+\frac{2q}{q+1}\\
=\sqrt{q}-1+\frac{2q-2\sqrt{q}+2}{q+1}\geq\sqrt{q}-1.\qed

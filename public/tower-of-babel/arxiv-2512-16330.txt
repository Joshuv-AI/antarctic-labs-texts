Projective Determinacy and Generic Absoluteness
Cesare Straffelini

Abstract
Generic absoluteness refers to the stability of the set-theoretic universe under forcing expansions. A result by Kripke ( [ Kri67 ] ) asserts that every complete Boolean algebra can be completely embedded into a countably generated one. In terms of forcing, this means that every forcing expansion of the universe is contained in one derived by a collapse forcing. The central question of this article is whether some level of generic absoluteness for collapse forcings is equivalent to the same kind of absoluteness for any forcing notion. We solve this question affirmatively under natural determinacy hypotheses, which follow from the existence of large cardinals. The long proof, which combines many set-theoretic techniques, estabilishing new connections within the field. For instance, we generalise the famous proof of Projective Determinacy by Martin and Steel ( [ MS89 ] ) to the context of homogeneity relative to an inner model, as well as giving a new proof of Neeman’s optimal Projective Determinacy result ( [ Nee02 ] ).



Keywords: generic absoluteness, determinacy, inner models, homogeneity

MSC: 03E57, 03E60, 03E45

Introduction

Forcing, introduced by Paul Joseph Cohen in
[
Coh63
]
and
[
Coh64
]
, is a set-theoretic technique used to prove results of independence in mathematics, with many applications outside set theory. After its introduction, where it was used to prove the independence of the Continuum Hypothesis, it has since been applied to abstract algebra (e.g., the Whitehead problem, proved independent by Saharon Shelah in
[
She74
]
), to geometry (e.g., the problem of additivity of strong homology, solved by Jeffrey Bergfalk in
[
Ber17
]
), and even to quantum mechanics (Ilijas Farah, in
[
Far11
]
, proved that the statement that all automorphisms of the Calkin algebra are inner is consistent with ZFC). Nevertheless, for the usual mathematical practice is better to have results ensuring that some statements cannot be changed by forcing, which means that some problems are solvable within the usual techniques of mathematics: the way to measure how much a set-theoretic universe is immune to forcing is called
generic absoluteness
.

The concept of generic absoluteness is strongly entwined with the one of universally Baire sets of reals, which is a strong generalisation of the usual Baire property. The universally Baire property was introduced by Qi Feng, Menachem Magidor and Hugh Woodin (
[
FMW92
]
), and it has been shown that the existence of sets of reals with this powerful regularity property implies some level of generic absoluteness.

Another important property that has many connections with the regularity of sets of reals is
determinacy
. Indeed, under determinacy hypotheses all sets of reals that are “simple enough” have the Baire property, the perfect set property, are Lebesgue-measurable, and satisfy many other regularity features. Determinacy was proved to be true for Borel sets by Tony Martin (
[
Mar75
]
), and later to be equiconsistent with the existence of Woodin cardinals for projective sets (the sets that are obtained by closing the class of Borel sets under the operations of continuous image and complementation) in the seminal paper
[
MS89
]
by Martin together with John Steel. In their article, they also explore the connection between determinacy and the more abstract set-theoretic concept of homogeneity measures, which also connects back to universally Baire sets, and to the existence of inner models of ZFC.

In this article, we begin by asking a simple question about generic absoluteness. Then, we take on a journey passing through all the aforementioned concepts, ultimately providing a general positive answer to our question. Along the way, we are able to shed some more light on the relations between these properties, offering modified proofs of some known theorems and introducing a slightly more general definition of homogeneity, relative to an inner model.

2
Preliminaries and the Main Question

2.1
Generic Absoluteness

Generic absoluteness is a property of some models of set theory, and it measures how the truth of certain statements cannot be changed by going to some forcing extension. The ultimate form of generic absoluteness would be that the universe
\mathsf{V}
is elementarily equivalent to any of its forcing extensions, but this is clearly inconsistent, since we are able to force incompatible statements, like the Continuum Hypothesis and its negation. In this article, we are interested in restricting generic absoluteness to a specific class of statements, namely some level of second-order arithmetic.

Definition 2.1
.

A formula in the language of set theory is said to be

•

\Sigma^{1}_{0}
or
\Pi^{1}_{0}
if it is arithmetic, i.e., if all quantifiers are restricted to
\omega
;

•

\Sigma^{1}_{n+1}
if it has the form
\exists x\subseteq\omega\ \psi(x,y_{1},\ldots,y_{k})
and
\psi(x,y_{1},\ldots,y_{k})
is
\Pi^{1}_{n}
;

•

\Pi^{1}_{n+1}
if it has the form
\forall x\subseteq\omega\ \psi(x,y_{1},\ldots,y_{k})
and
\psi(x,y_{1},\ldots,y_{k})
is
\Sigma^{1}_{n}
.

A
\Sigma^{1}_{n}
- (or
\Pi^{1}_{n}
-) formula where the free variables are interpreted as parameters that are arbitrary subsets of
\omega
is said to be a
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{1}_{n}
- (or
\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{1}_{n}
-) sentence.

Now we are ready to formalise the concept of generic absoluteness.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\mathbb{P}
be a forcing notion. We say that
\Gamma
-
\mathbb{P}
-Absoluteness holds, where
\Gamma
is
\Sigma^{1}_{n}
or
\Pi^{1}_{n}
for some

, if for every
\Gamma
-sentence

it holds

\varphi\leftrightarrow\mathbb{P}\Vdash\varphi.

Similarly,
\boldsymbol{\Gamma}
-
\mathbb{P}
-Absoluteness holds, where
\boldsymbol{\Gamma}
is
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{1}_{n}
or
\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{1}_{n}
for
n<\omega
, if for every
\Gamma
-formula
\varphi(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})
and any choice of
a_{1}\subseteq\omega
,
\ldots
,

we have

\varphi(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k})\leftrightarrow\mathbb{P}\Vdash\varphi(\check{a}_{1},\ldots,\check{a}_{k}).

By “projective absoluteness” we refer informally to absoluteness for all projective statements, i.e., statements that are
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{1}_{n}
or
\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{1}_{n}
for some
n<\omega
.

The most fundamental result about absoluteness is Shoenfield’s theorem. It was proved by Joseph Shoenfield in
[
Sho62
]
, shortly before the discovery of forcing by Paul Cohen. Indeed, the theorem is not about

Theorem 2.3

(Shoenfield)
.

\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{1}_{2}
-
\mathbb{P}
-Absoluteness holds, for any
\mathbb{P}
.

Notice that, since the negation of any

Lemma 2.4
.

Assume
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{1}_{n}
-
\mathbb{P}
-Absoluteness holds. Then, for any
\Sigma^{1}_{n+1}

,
\ldots

Since

\exists y\subseteq\omega\ \psi(y,x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})

2.2
Universally Baire Sets

There is a strong link between generic absoluteness and universally Baire sets of reals, which were introduced in
[
FMW92
]
. There are two equivalent definitions of this property, but here we focus only on the one that has stronger connections with generic absoluteness. Recall that, as is customary in set theory, when we talk about the set of real numbers we typically mean either the powerset
\wp(\omega)
of
\omega
or the set
{}^{\omega}\omega
of sequences of length
\omega
with values in
\omega
: we do not concern ourselves with whether a real is coded by a set or by a sequence of naturals.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
A\subseteq{}^{\omega}\omega
and let
\mathbb{P}
be any forcing notion. We say that
A
is
\mathbb{P}
-universally Baire (
\mathbb{P}
-uB for short) if there are two trees
S\subseteq{}^{<\omega}\omega\times{}^{<\omega}\zeta
and
T\subseteq{}^{<\omega}\omega\times{}^{<\omega}\zeta
, for
\zeta
an ordinal, with the following properties:

•

A=\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}[S]
;

•

{}^{\omega}\omega-A=\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}[T]
;

•

\mathbb{P}\Vdash\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}[S]\cup\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}[T]={}^{\omega}\omega
.

To clarify the notation: if
Q
is any tree by
[Q]
we mean the set of branches of
Q
. From the definition, it follows that
[S]\subseteq{}^{\omega}\omega\times{}^{\omega}\zeta
and
[T]\subseteq{}^{\omega}\omega\times{}^{\omega}\zeta
. Moreover, the map
\pi_{1}:{}^{\omega}\omega\times{}^{\omega}\zeta\to{}^{\omega}\omega
is the first projection, and also, if
f
is a map and
a

denotes the image of
a
under

, i.e.,
f^{\prime\prime}a=\{f(x):x\in a\}
. The idea behind this definition is to have a pair
\langle S,T\rangle
of trees projecting to
A
and to its complement, and with the property that even in a generic extension by
\mathbb{P}
the pair
\langle S,T\rangle
still projects to complements. Indeed, in the definition we ask
\mathbb{P}
only to force that
\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}[S]
and
\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}[T]
together cover the whole set of reals (so, a priori, we do not require them to be complements), but it is not hard to show that the property of empty intersection for projections of trees is preserved:

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
S\subseteq{}^{<\omega}\omega\times{}^{<\omega}\zeta
and
T\subseteq{}^{<\omega}\omega\times{}^{<\omega}\zeta
be trees and assume that
\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}[S]\cap\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}[T]=\varnothing
. Then for any forcing notion
\mathbb{P}
it holds

\mathbb{P}\Vdash\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}[S]\cap\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}[T]=\varnothing.

Proof.

Let
G\subseteq\mathbb{P}
be
\mathsf{V}
-generic and assume, towards a contradiction, that

\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}[S]^{\mathsf{V}[G]}\cap\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}[T]^{\mathsf{V}[G]}\neq\varnothing.

Then let
Z:=\{\langle s,t_{1},t_{2}\rangle:\langle s,t_{1}\rangle\in S,\ \langle s,t_{2}\rangle\in T\}
. The hypothesis tells us that, in
\mathsf{V}[G]
, we have
[Z]\neq\varnothing
, hence
\langle Z,\supseteq\rangle
is ill-founded. By absoluteness of well-foundedness,
\langle Z,\supseteq\rangle
is ill-founded also in
\mathsf{V}
, so
[Z]\neq\varnothing
in
\mathsf{V}
, meaning that there is some
\langle x,y_{1},y_{2}\rangle\in[Z]\cap\mathsf{V}
, but then

, a contradiction.
∎

.

Let

be a forcing notion and assume that every

\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{1}_{n}
-

2.3
Collapse Forcing

The collapse forcing, for an infinite cardinal
\lambda
, adds a bijection between
\lambda
and
\omega
, thus making
\lambda
countable in the forcing extension. Formally, the collapse forcing is defined as follows.

Definition 2.10
.

Let
\lambda
be an ordinal. We let
\mathrm{Coll}(\omega,\lambda)
be the poset consisting of all finite partial functions
p:\omega\to\lambda
, ordered by reverse inclusion.

We refer to
[
Jec03
, Section 15]
for more information and facts about the collapse forcing. An important remark, used several times below, is that in a certain sense we can think of the collapse forcing as the terminal object in the category of forcing notions of size at most
\lambda
, where
\lambda
is a cardinal:

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
\mathbb{P}
be any forcing notion,
|\mathbb{P}|\leq\lambda
. Then

\mathbb{P}\times\mathrm{Coll}(\omega,\lambda)\simeq\mathbb{P}\ast\mathrm{Coll}(\omega,\lambda)\simeq\mathrm{Coll}(\omega,\lambda).

This is a corollary of a well-known result of Saul Kripke (see
[
Kri67
]
). It is noted also in
[
FMW92
]
, where the authors exploit it to show the following:

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
A
be a set of reals that is
\mathrm{Coll}(\omega,\lambda)
-universally Baire, for
\lambda
a cardinal. Then
A
is
\mathbb{P}
-universally Baire for any forcing notion
\mathbb{P}
with
|\mathbb{P}|\leq\lambda
.

At this point, having seen the relation between universally Baire sets and generic absoluteness, and the fact that collapse forcing suffices to determine the universally Baire property for all forcing posets of no greater size, it is natural to ask the following question, which is the main problem discussed in this article:

Question 2.13
.

Assume
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{1}_{n}
-
\mathrm{Coll}(\omega,\lambda)
-Absoluteness holds, for
\lambda
a cardinal and
n<\omega
. Does
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{1}_{n}
-
\mathbb{P}
-Absoluteness hold for every
\mathbb{P}
with
|\mathbb{P}|\leq\lambda
?

Clearly, Question
2.13
has a positive answer for
n=2
and any cardinal
\lambda
, by Shoenfield’s theorem
2.3
. We also notice that the answer is positive in the case
n=3
, a fact already mentioned in the article

.

Lemma 2.14
.

Assume
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{1}_{3}
-
\mathrm{Coll}(\omega,\lambda)
-Absoluteness holds, for
\lambda
a cardinal. Then
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{1}_{3}
-
\mathbb{P}
-Absoluteness holds for every
\mathbb{P}
with
|\mathbb{P}|\leq\lambda
.

Proof.

Let
G\subseteq\mathrm{Coll}(\omega,\lambda)
be
\mathsf{V}
-generic and let
g\subseteq\mathbb{P}
be also
\mathsf{V}
-generic, for some fixed forcing notion
\mathbb{P}
such that
|\mathbb{P}|\leq\lambda
. By Lemma
2.11
, there is, in
\mathsf{V}[G]
, a
\mathsf{V}[g]
-generic filter
H\subseteq\mathrm{Coll}(\omega,\lambda)
with
\mathsf{V}[g][H]=\mathsf{V}[G]
. By Shoenfield’s theorem
2.3
and Lemma
2.4
, we just need to show downwards absoluteness from
\mathsf{V}[g]
to
\mathsf{V}
. Hence, assume
\varphi
is a
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{1}_{3}
-sentence true in
\mathsf{V}[g]
, with parameters in
\mathsf{V}
. Again thanks to Shoenfield’s theorem
2.3
and Lemma
2.4
, we know that
\varphi
holds also in
\mathsf{V}[g][H]=\mathsf{V}[G]
. But now, by our hypothesis that
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{1}_{3}
-
\mathrm{Coll}(\omega,\lambda)
-Absoluteness holds in
\mathsf{V}
, since
\varphi
has parameters in
\mathsf{V}
we get that it is true in
\mathsf{V}
, as desired.
∎

Looking at the proof of Lemma

, it would be tempting to try and give a general positive answer to Question
2.13
by induction on

, by following the same proof strategy. But this already fails in the

\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{1}_{4}
-sentences from
\mathsf{V}[g]
to
\mathsf{V}[g][H]

\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{1}_{3}

-Absoluteness to hold in
\mathsf{V}[g]
, but a priori there is no reason why this should hold. If working with
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{1}_{3}
-sentences having parameters in
\mathsf{V}
, we could exploit the absoluteness between
\mathsf{V}
and
\mathsf{V}[g]
and between
\mathsf{V}
and
\mathsf{V}[G]
to show they are also absolute between
\mathsf{V}[g]
and
\mathsf{V}[G]=\mathsf{V}[g][H]
, when we instead allow the parameters to range over the whole set of real numbers of
\mathsf{V}[g]
there is no immediate way to let them “pass through” the model
\mathsf{V}
, since they may be represented by a
\mathbb{P}
-name that is too big to be coded by a real. This can be solved easily by the following lemma from
[

]
:

Lemma 2.15
.

Assume
a^{\sharp}
exists for every set of ordinals
a\subseteq\mathsf{Ord}
. Then, in any generic extension,
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{1}_{3}
-
\mathbb{P}
-Absoluteness holds, for any forcing notion
\mathbb{P}
.

We will define

in the next section, but for now, it is clear that Lemma
2.15
provides a positive answer to Question
2.13
in the case
n=4
, assuming the existence of
a^{\sharp}
for every set of ordinals

. However, it is not optimal. Indeed, the next section will show that the hypothesis can be reduced to the existence of
a^{\sharp}
for every

\mathrm{Coll}(\omega,\lambda)
-Absoluteness, thus this assumption does not trivialise the implication given by the positive answer to Question

. The proof in the next section could be done also more concisely, but it is exposed in detail in order to already introduce concepts and definitions that will be used later to prove the result for

3.1
Ultrafilters with respect to Inner Models

Before introducing the meaning of
a^{\sharp}
and the concept of analytic determinacy, we need to give a couple of definitions that will turn out to be useful later. The concept of ultrafilter on a set is very common in set theory, and we just need to tweak it slightly. Fix a transitive inner model
\mathsf{W}
of ZFC; that is,
\mathsf{W}
is a transitive model of ZFC that contains all ordinals and is definable. We introduce the notion of a
\mathsf{W}
-ultrafilter from the point of view of our universe
\mathsf{V}
. In particular, such objects need not to lie in
\mathsf{W}
, which may differ from other definitions of
\mathsf{W}
-ultrafilters found in the literature. Our definition of a
\mathsf{W}
-ultrafilter is therefore the following:

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mathsf{W}
be a transitive inner model of
\mathrm{ZFC}
and
\varnothing\neq Z\in\mathsf{W}
. A
\mathsf{W}
-ultrafilter over
Z
is some
\mathcal{U}\subseteq\wp(Z)
such that:

•

Z\in\mathcal{U}
and
\varnothing\not\in\mathcal{U}
;

•

if
X\in\mathcal{U}
and
Y\in\mathcal{U}
then
(X\cap Y)\in\mathcal{U}
;

•

if
X\in\mathcal{U}
and
X\subseteq Y\subseteq Z
with
Y\in\mathsf{W}
, then
Y\in\mathcal{U}
;

•

for all
X\subseteq Z
with
X\in\mathsf{W}
, either
X\in\mathcal{U}
or
(Z-X)\in\mathcal{U}
.

Note that in Definition
3.1
we do not require that
\mathcal{U}\subseteq\mathsf{W}
. Consequently, if
\mathsf{W}_{1}\subseteq\mathsf{W}_{2}
are transitive inner models of ZFC, then every
\mathsf{W}_{2}
-ultrafilter on a set
Z\in\mathsf{W}_{1}
is also a
\mathsf{W}_{1}
-ultrafilter. It is also worth noting that if
\mathsf{W}=\mathsf{V}
, then our notion of a
\mathsf{W}
-ultrafilter coincides with the usual notion of an ultrafilter.

Typically, when working with (classical) ultrafilters, one performs the ultrapower construction in order to obtain an elementary embedding

j:\langle\mathsf{M},\in\rangle\hookrightarrow\langle\mathsf{Ult}(\mathsf{M},\mathcal{U}),E_{\mathcal{U}}\rangle

when given a standard structure
\langle\mathsf{M},\in\rangle
. How can we produce the same kind of embeddings using
\mathsf{W}
-ultrafilters? We are going to require that
\mathsf{M}\subseteq\mathsf{W}
(either as a set or as a class). Notice that if
\mathsf{M}
is a class, it might not be a definable subclass of
\mathsf{W}
, but rather a definable subclass of
\mathsf{V}
contained in
\mathsf{W}
. Then we define, as usual:

Definition 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{U}
be a
\mathsf{W}
-ultrafilter over
Z
, and let
\langle\mathsf{M},\in\rangle
be such that
\mathsf{M}\subseteq\mathsf{W}
. For every
f\in{}^{Z}\mathsf{M}\cap\mathsf{W}
, consider the class, definable in
\mathsf{V}
(with parameters
\mathcal{U}
and the parameters of the definitions of
\mathsf{W}
and
\mathsf{M}
),

[f]^{0}_{\mathcal{U}}:=\{g\in{}^{Z}\mathsf{M}\cap\mathsf{W}:\{p\in Z:f(p)=g(p)\}\in\mathcal{U}\}.

If
\mathsf{M}
is a proper class, this is also a proper class of
\mathsf{V}
, but we can define
[f]_{\mathcal{U}}
to consist of those members of
[f]^{0}_{\mathcal{U}}
with minimal rank in the von Neumann hierarchy of
\mathsf{W}
, by the usual trick by Dana Scott, and deduce that
[f]_{\mathcal{U}}
is a set.

Hence, we are now able to define the
\mathsf{W}
-ultrapower of
\langle\mathsf{M},\in\rangle
with respect to
\mathcal{U}
as the class, again definable in
\mathsf{V}
having as parameters
\mathcal{U}
and the parameters of the definition of
\mathsf{W}
:

\mathsf{Ult}(\mathsf{M},\mathcal{U}):=\{[f]_{\mathcal{U}}:f\in{{}^{Z}}\mathsf{M}\cap\mathsf{W}\}

together with the relation
E_{\mathcal{U}}
on
\mathsf{Ult}(\mathsf{M},\mathcal{U})
given by
[f]_{\mathcal{U}}E_{\mathcal{U}}[g]_{\mathcal{U}}
if and only if

\{p\in Z:f(p)\in g(p)\}\in\mathcal{U}.

Notice that, in general,
\mathrm{Ult}(\mathsf{M},\mathcal{U})
and
E_{\mathcal{U}}
are subclasses of
\mathsf{W}
that are definable in
\mathsf{V}
but not necessarily in
\mathsf{W}
.

Note that in Definition
3.2
we did not explicitely write the dependency on
\mathsf{W}
in the definition of the various sets and classes
[f]^{0}_{\mathcal{U}}
,
[f]_{\mathcal{U}}
,
\mathsf{Ult}(\mathsf{M},\mathcal{U})
and
E_{\mathcal{U}}
. However, these definitions may yield different sets and classes if we apply them with the same
\mathcal{U}
considered as a
\mathsf{W}_{1}
-ultrafilter or as a
\mathsf{W}_{2}
-ultrafilter, for
\mathsf{W}_{1}\subseteq\mathsf{W}_{2}
different transitive inner models of
\mathrm{ZFC}
, assuming that
\langle\mathsf{M},\in\rangle
is such that
\mathsf{M}\subseteq\mathsf{W}_{1}
. Our reason for omitting this dependency from
\mathsf{W}
to avoid unnecessary complexity in the notation, since the model with respect to which we consider the ultrapower will always be clear from the context. Now, following the usual proof (for
\mathsf{V}
-ultrapowers) we obtain

Lemma 3.3
.

E_{\mathcal{U}}
is well-defined, and there is an elementary embedding

j_{\mathcal{U}}:\langle\mathsf{M},\in\rangle\hookrightarrow\langle\mathsf{Ult}(\mathsf{M},\mathcal{U}),E_{\mathcal{U}}\rangle

given by
j_{\mathcal{U}}(x):=[c_{x}]_{\mathcal{U}}
, where
c_{x}\in{}^{Z}\{x\}
is constant for each
x\in\mathsf{M}
.

We also care about whether the ultrapower
\mathsf{Ult}(\mathsf{M},\mathcal{U})
is well-founded by
E_{\mathcal{U}}
. To prove it, we need to introduce the concept of completeness with respect to an inner model.

Definition 3.4
.

The
\mathsf{W}
-ultrafilter
\mathcal{U}
is
\alpha
-
\mathsf{W}
-complete, for some ordinal
\alpha
, if, for all sequences
\langle X_{\eta}:\eta<\beta\rangle\in\mathsf{W}
of length
\beta<\alpha
with
X_{\eta}\in\mathcal{U}
for all
\eta<\beta
,

\textstyle\bigcap_{\eta<\beta}X_{\eta}\in\mathcal{U}.

Note that this definition depends more heavily on the choice of the model. If
\mathsf{W}_{1}\subseteq\mathsf{W}_{2}\subseteq\mathsf{V}
are two transitive inner models of
\mathrm{ZFC}
and
\mathcal{U}
is a
\mathsf{W}_{2}
-ultrafilter over
Z\in\mathsf{W}_{1}
, then it may happen that
\mathcal{U}
is
\alpha
-
\mathsf{W}_{1}
-complete for some
\alpha
but not
\alpha
-
\mathsf{W}_{2}
-complete. This is because we are not only requiring that each
X_{\eta}
belongs to the inner model
\mathsf{W}
, but also that the whole sequence
\langle X_{\eta}:\eta<\beta\rangle
lies in
\mathsf{W}
. Once again, the notion of
\alpha
-
\mathsf{V}
-completeness for
\mathsf{V}
-ultrafilters is just the notion of
\alpha
-completeness for ultrafilters. Following the proof of the classical statement of equivalence between
\omega_{1}
-completeness for ultrafilters and well-foundedness of their ultrapowers, we would like to get an equivalent characterisation of when the
\mathsf{W}
-ultrapower is well-founded. The relation
E_{\mathcal{U}}
may not be definable in the inner model
\mathsf{W}
, though, hence this well-foundedness can only be witnessed from outside the model. This is once again solved by introducing a new notion:

Definition 3.5
.

A set-like relation
E
(not necessarily definable in
\mathsf{W}
) is said to be
\mathsf{W}
-well-founded if there is no sequence in
\mathsf{W}
of length
\omega
that is
E
-descending.

Notice that the relation
E_{\mathcal{U}}
is always set-like, for each
\mathsf{W}
-ultrafilter
\mathcal{U}
. Hence, the aforementioned classical proof, with the only change of imposing that some sequences belong to the inner model
\mathsf{W}
, produces the following:

Lemma 3.6
.

The
\mathsf{W}
-ultrafilter
\mathcal{U}
is
\omega_{1}
-
\mathsf{W}
-complete if and only if the relation
E_{\mathcal{U}}
is
\mathsf{W}
-well-founded.

One important point to remark is that the
\omega_{1}
in Lemma
3.6
is
\omega_{1}^{\mathsf{V}}
, which in general may differ from
\omega_{1}^{\mathsf{W}}
. In the situation where
E_{\mathcal{U}}
is
\mathsf{W}
-well-founded, we would like to take the Mostowski collapse of the structure
\langle\mathsf{Ult}(\mathsf{M},\mathcal{U}),E_{\mathcal{U}}\rangle
, obtaining a transitive standard structure
\langle\mathsf{N},\in\rangle
such that
\mathsf{N}\subseteq\mathsf{W}
. This is not so simple, since the structure
\langle\mathrm{Ult}(\mathsf{M},\mathcal{U}),E_{\mathcal{U}}\rangle
might not be definable in
\mathsf{W}
. So we need to prove an analogous version of Mostowski’s theorem in this setting. To do so, we have to show some properties of
\mathsf{W}
-well-founded relations.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
E
be a
\mathsf{W}
-well-founded relation on
\mathsf{X}
, where
\mathsf{X}\subseteq\mathsf{W}
is a set or class that is definable in
\mathsf{V}
. Then we can define

•

\mathsf{X}_{0}:=\varnothing
;

•

\mathsf{X}_{\alpha+1}:=\mathsf{X}_{\alpha}\cup\{x\in\mathsf{X}:\forall y(yEx\to y\in\mathsf{X}_{\alpha})\};

•

\mathsf{X}_{\lambda}:=\bigcup_{\alpha<\lambda}\mathsf{X}_{\alpha}
if
\lambda
is limit.

Then the
\mathsf{X}_{\alpha}
s form an increasing chain, and
\bigcup_{\alpha\in\mathsf{Ord}}\mathsf{X}_{\alpha}=\mathsf{X}
. In particular, for every
x\in\mathsf{X}
, we can define
\varrho(x)
as the least
\alpha
such that
x\in\mathsf{X}_{\alpha+1}
. Notice that it holds
\varrho(x)=\sup\{\varrho(y)+1:yEx\}
and, if
xEy
, then
\varrho(x)<\varrho(y)
.

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
\mathsf{W}
be a transitive inner model of ZFC. Let
\mathsf{X}\subseteq\mathsf{W}
be a set or a class that is definable in
\mathsf{V}
. Let
E
be a
\mathsf{W}
-well-founded relation on
\mathsf{X}
. Let
G:\mathsf{W}\to\mathsf{W}
be a class function, definable in
\mathsf{V}
. Then there is a unique (class) function
F:\mathsf{X}\to\mathsf{W}
, definable in
\mathsf{V}
, such that for all
x\in\mathsf{X}
:

F(x)=G(x,F\upharpoonright\{z:zEx\}).

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
\mathsf{W}
be an inner model of
\mathsf{V}
; let
\langle\mathsf{M},E\rangle
be an extensional structure definable in
\mathsf{V}
with
\mathsf{M}\subseteq\mathsf{W}
and assume
E
is
\mathsf{W}
-well-founded. Then there is a unique transitive standard structure
\langle\mathsf{N},\in\rangle
definable in
\mathsf{V}
such that
\mathsf{N}\subseteq\mathsf{W}
and a unique isomorphism
\pi:\langle\mathsf{M},E\rangle\to\langle\mathsf{N},\in\rangle
definable in
\mathsf{V}
.

Theorem
3.9
is a version of Mostowski’s theorem that we can apply in our setting, hence obtaining that if the relation
E_{\mathcal{U}}
is
\mathsf{W}
-well-founded, we can find an extensional standard structure
\langle\mathsf{N},\in\rangle
isomorphic to
\langle\mathsf{Ult}(\mathsf{M},\mathcal{U}),E_{\mathcal{U}}\rangle
such that
\mathsf{N}\subseteq\mathsf{W}
, even though
\mathsf{N}
may not be definable in
\mathsf{W}
but surely it is in
\mathsf{V}
.

3.2
Determinacy and Sharps

The axiom of determinacy was introduced by Jan Mycielski and Hugo Steinhaus in
[
MS62
]
, as an alternative to the axiom of choice. Later, some fragments of it were proved to be consistent together with the axiom of choice, relative to large cardinals. For example, determinacy for all subsets of the real line belonging to
\mathsf{L}(\mathbb{R})
is equiconsistent with the existence of
\omega
-many Woodin cardinals. Before diving deep into the consequences and the proofs of these determinacy results, it is better to state formally the meaning of this concept.

Definition 3.10
.

The game
\mathcal{G}(A,T)
consists of a tree
T\subseteq{}^{\omega}Y
, where
Y\neq\varnothing
is a non-empty set, and a subset
A\subseteq[T]
called the payoff set. We call the two players Alberto (the first to move) and Barbara. The game then goes as follows:

•

Alberto starts by choosing an element
a_{0}\in X
, such that
\langle a_{0}\rangle\in T
.

•

then Barbara chooses
b_{0}\in X
, such that
\langle a_{0},b_{0}\rangle\in T
.

•

afterwards, Alberto plays
a_{1}\in X
such that
\langle a_{0},b_{0},a_{1}\rangle\in T
.

•

they keep on playing elements of
X
, knowing all previous moves, in a way that, at each step, the finite sequence built so far belongs to
T
.

This way they build an infinite sequence
x=\langle a_{0},b_{0},a_{1},\ldots\rangle\in[T]
, a branch on
T
. Alberto wins if
x\in A
. If he does not win (
x\not\in A
), Barbara wins.

Definition 3.11
.

A strategy for Alberto in
\mathcal{G}(A,T)
is a map
\sigma
from the set

E:=\textstyle\bigcup_{n<\omega}{}^{2n}X

of all even length sequences in
T
to
X
, with
\langle s,\sigma(s)\rangle\in T
for all
s\in E
. Given a final position
x=\langle a_{0},b_{0},\ldots\rangle\in[T]
, we say that Alberto has played according to
\sigma
if
a_{k}=\sigma(\langle a_{0},\ldots,b_{k-1}\rangle)
for all
k<\omega
. If
x\in A
for every possible final position
x
played according to
\sigma
, then
\sigma
is a winning strategy for Alberto.

Similarly, a strategy for Barbara is a map
\sigma
from the set

O:=\textstyle\bigcup_{n<\omega}{}^{2n+1}X

of all odd length sequences in
T
to
X
, with
\langle s,\sigma(s)\rangle\in T
for all
s\in O
. Given
x=\langle a_{0},b_{0},\ldots\rangle\in[T]
, Barbara has played according to
\sigma
if
b_{k}=\sigma(\langle a_{0},\ldots,a_{k}\rangle)
for all
k<\omega
. If
x\not\in A
for all final positions
x
that were played according to
\sigma
, we say that
\sigma
is a winning strategy for Barbara.

Definition 3.12
.

A game
\mathcal{G}(A,T)
is determined if (exactly) one of the two players has a winning strategy. If
\Gamma
is a class of sets of reals, by
\Gamma
-
\mathrm{Determinacy}
we denote the statement: “for every
A\in\Gamma
, the game
\mathcal{G}(A,{}^{\omega}\omega)
is determined”.

The first determinacy results appear in a paper
[
GS53
]
by David Gale and Frank Stewart, where the authors prove –assuming the axiom of choice– that there is always a game played on the reals that is not determined (hence, ZFC disproves
\wp(\omega)
-Determinacy, often denoted AD). At the same time, they show that every game with an open or closed payoff set is determined, so ZFC proves both
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{1}_{0}
- and
\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{1}_{0}
-Determinacy. Indeed, ZFC even proves
\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{1}_{1}
-Determinacy, as shown by Martin in
[
Mar75
]
. However,
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{1}_{1}
-Determinacy, commonly known as analytic determinacy, already requires hypotheses stronger than ZFC. Its first proof, published by Martin in
[
Mar70
]
, assumed the existence of a measurable cardinal. Later, he was able to relax this requirement to just the existence of
a^{\sharp}
for all
a\subseteq\omega
, a result that Leo Harrington showed to be optimal in his article
[
Har78
]
, as demonstrated by the following theorem:

Theorem 3.13

(Harrington)
.

If
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{1}_{1}
-Determinacy holds,
a^{\sharp}
exists for all
a\subseteq\omega
.

We are not going to give a proof of Harrington’s theorem, though, we are instead giving the proof of Martin’s analytic determinacy theorem from sharps, since parts of the intermediate results of that proof will be needed later. Before doing so, we need to introduce the concept of
a^{\sharp}
:

Definition 3.14
.

A subset of an ordinal
\kappa
is a club if it is closed and unbounded. A class of ordinals is a club class if it is closed and unbounded in the ordinals.

Definition 3.15
.

If
a\subseteq\lambda
, for
\lambda
an infinite cardinal, is a set of ordinals,
a^{\sharp}
exists if there is a non-trivial elementary embedding
j:\mathsf{L}[a]\hookrightarrow\mathsf{L}[a]
or, equivalently, if there is a club class
\mathcal{I}_{a}
of indiscernibles for the model
\mathsf{L}[a]
such that
\mathcal{I}_{a}\cap\lambda^{+}
is a club subset of
\lambda^{+}
.

For more information about sharps, see
[
Kan03
, Section 9]
or
[
Sol67
]
.

Definition 3.16
.

If
\kappa
is a cardinal, the upwards closure of the family of all club subsets of
\kappa
is a filter, called the club filter and denoted by
\mathcal{C}_{\kappa}
.

The club filter
\mathcal{C}_{\kappa}
on any cardinal
\kappa
is normal and
\kappa
-complete. We are going to exploit the normality hypothesis in a later proof, hence we need a definition that is the relativisation of normality to the context of
\mathsf{W}
-ultrafilters:

Definition 3.17
.

A
\mathsf{W}
-ultrafilter
\mathcal{U}
on
\kappa
is said to be
\mathsf{W}
-normal if, whenever we have a sequence
\langle X_{\eta}:\eta<\kappa\rangle\in\mathsf{W}
such that
X_{\eta}\in\mathcal{U}
for each
\eta<\kappa
, then

\Delta_{\eta<\kappa}X_{\eta}:=\{\beta<\kappa:\textstyle\beta\in\bigcap_{\eta<\beta}X_{\eta}\}\in\mathcal{U}.

The following result can be found, for
\lambda=\omega
, in
[
Kan03
, Lemma 15.8]
, due to Richard Mansfield in
[
Man71
]
. It is straightforward to see that the proof also generalises to any
\lambda
, by substituting
\lambda^{+}
in place of
\omega_{1}
.

Theorem 3.18
.

Let
\lambda
be a cardinal,
a\subseteq\lambda
, and assume
a^{\sharp}
exists. For
n<\omega
and all subsets
Y\subseteq[\lambda^{+}]^{n}
such that
Y\in\mathsf{L}[a]
, there is some element of the club filter
H\in\mathcal{C}_{\lambda^{+}}
such that either
[H]^{n}\subseteq Y
or
[H]^{n}\cap Y=\varnothing
.

If we apply Theorem
3.18
with
n=1
, we obtain that
\mathcal{C}_{\lambda^{+}}
is a
\mathsf{L}[a]
-ultrafilter, since we already know it is a filter. Additionally, it is
\lambda^{+}
-complete and normal, so we can conclude that it is
\lambda^{+}
-
\mathsf{L}[a]
-complete and
\mathsf{L}[a]
-normal. Now we introduce a definition that unifies all these properties, in order to state a corollary:

Definition 3.19
.

Let
\mathsf{W}
be a transitive inner model of
\mathrm{ZFC}
. A cardinal
\kappa
is
\mathsf{W}
-measurable if there is a
\kappa
-
\mathsf{W}
-complete
\mathsf{W}
-normal
\mathsf{W}
-ultrafilter over
\kappa
.

As always, a
\mathsf{V}
-measurable cardinal is just a measurable cardinal.

Corollary 3.20
.

If
a^{\sharp}
exists, for some
a\subseteq\lambda
, then
\lambda^{+}
is
\mathsf{L}[a]
-measurable.

In particular, Theorem
3.18
also tells us that the
\mathsf{L}[a]
-ultrafilter witnessing the
\mathsf{L}[a]
-measurability of
\lambda^{+}
is the club filter
\mathcal{C}_{\lambda^{+}}
. Note also that here
\lambda^{+}
always denotes the successor of
\lambda
in
\mathsf{V}
, and not in the inner model
\mathsf{L}[a]
, where it could be different. The reverse implication of Corollary
3.20
is also true:

Lemma 3.21
.

If
\lambda
is a cardinal and
\lambda^{+}
is
\mathsf{L}[a]
-measurable for
a\subseteq\lambda
,
a^{\sharp}
exists.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{U}
denote a witness to the
\mathsf{L}[a]
-measurability of
\lambda^{+}
. Consider now

j:\langle\mathsf{L}[a],\in\rangle\hookrightarrow\langle\mathsf{Ult}(\mathsf{L}[a],\mathcal{U}),E_{\mathcal{U}}\rangle

as in Lemma
3.3
. It is easy to see that, in fact, the image of
j
is contained inside
\mathsf{L}[a]
; hence,
j
is a non-trivial elementary embedding from
\mathsf{L}[a]
into itself.
∎

An alternative proof of Martin’s original theorem from
[
Mar70
]
is as follows: first, we can prove that if
\kappa
is a measurable cardinal then every co-analytic set is
\kappa
-homogeneous; second, we show that a game having as payoff the complement of a
\kappa
-homogeneous set is determined. This is, basically, the proof strategy we want to follow but, in order to do so, we have again to generalise the concept of homogeneity, as we did for ultrafilters and for measurable cardinals.

3.3
Homogeneity with respect to Inner Models

In order to introduce homogeneity, we first have to state some other definitions about
\mathsf{W}
-ultrafilters, that are all generalisations of the classical concepts.

Definition 3.22
.

Let
\varnothing\neq Z\in\mathsf{W}
and let
a\subseteq b
be sets in
\mathsf{W}
. Assume
\mathcal{U}
is a
\mathsf{W}
-ultrafilter over
{}^{a}Z
and
\mathcal{V}
is a
\mathsf{W}
-ultrafilter over
{}^{b}Z
. We say that
\mathcal{V}
projects to
\mathcal{U}
if for all
X\in{}^{a}Z
it holds that
X\in\mathcal{U}
if and only if

\{p\in{}^{b}Z:(p\upharpoonright a)\in X\}\in\mathcal{V}.

The following lemma is easy to show, adapting the classical proof:

Lemma 3.23
.

If
\mathcal{V}
is a
\mathsf{W}
-ultrafilter over
{}^{b}Z
that projects to the
\mathsf{W}
-ultrafilter
\mathcal{U}
over
{}^{a}Z
, and
\langle\mathsf{M},\in\rangle
is an extensional structure with
\mathsf{M}\subseteq\mathsf{W}
, then there is an elementary embedding

e_{\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V}}:\langle\mathsf{Ult}(\mathsf{M},\mathcal{U}),E_{\mathcal{U}}\rangle\hookrightarrow\langle\mathsf{Ult}(\mathsf{M},\mathcal{V}),E_{\mathcal{V}}\rangle

given by
e_{\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V}}([f]_{\mathcal{U}}):=[f^{\ast}]_{\mathcal{V}}
, where
f^{\ast}(p):=f(p\upharpoonright a)
for all
p\in{}^{b}Z
.

Recall that a directed set of sets is a set
D
such that for all
a
and
b
in
D
there is some element
c\in D
such that
a\cup b\subseteq c
. This concept can be equivalently interpreted as the one of an upwards directed posetal category.

Definition 3.24
.

Let
\varnothing\neq Z\in\mathsf{W}
, and let
D\in\mathsf{W}
be a directed set of sets. A sequence
\mathcal{E}\in\mathsf{V}
of
\mathsf{W}
-ultrafilters
\mathcal{E}:=\langle\mathcal{U}_{a}:a\in D\rangle
is called a
\mathsf{W}
-tower over
Z
if

•

\mathcal{U}_{a}
is a
\omega_{1}
-
\mathsf{W}
-complete
\mathsf{W}
-ultrafilter over
{}^{a}Z
, for all
a\in D
;

•

\mathcal{U}_{b}
projects to
\mathcal{U}_{a}
, whenever
a\subseteq b
.

Given a
\mathsf{W}
-tower
\langle\mathcal{U}_{a}:a\in D\rangle
and an extensional structure
\langle\mathsf{M},\in\rangle
such that
\mathsf{M}\subseteq\mathsf{W}
, we can consider the functor from the upwards directed posetal category
D
to the category of structures that are elementarily equivalent to
\langle\mathsf{M},\in\rangle
. For
a\in D
, consider the structure
\langle\mathsf{Ult}(\mathsf{M},\mathcal{U}_{a}),E_{\mathcal{U}_{a}}\rangle
, which is elementarily equivalent to
\langle\mathsf{M},\in\rangle
by Lemma
3.3
, and for
a\subseteq b
consider the embedding
e_{\mathcal{U}_{a},\mathcal{U}_{b}}
. Since the category of models of a first-order theory (where morphisms are elementary embeddings) has all directed limit cocones, we can consider the direct limit

\langle\mathsf{Ult}(\mathsf{M};\mathcal{E}),E_{\mathcal{E}}\rangle

of the system given by our
\mathsf{W}
-tower, and the various elementary embeddings

\imath_{a,\infty}:\langle\mathsf{Ult}(\mathsf{M},\mathcal{U}_{a}),E_{\mathcal{U}_{a}}\rangle\hookrightarrow\langle\mathsf{Ult}(\mathsf{M};\mathcal{E}),E_{\mathcal{E}}\rangle

for
a\in D
. This direct limit
\langle\mathsf{Ult}(\mathsf{M};\mathcal{E}),E_{\mathcal{E}}\rangle
is not necessarily
\mathsf{W}
-well-founded: we want now to show an equivalent characterisation for its
\mathsf{W}
-well-foundedness.

Definition 3.25
.

A
\mathsf{W}
-tower
\langle\mathcal{U}_{a}:a\in D\rangle
over
Z
is said to be
\omega_{1}
-
\mathsf{W}
-complete if, whenever we pick a subset
D^{\prime}\subseteq D
countable in
\mathsf{W}
and
\langle X_{a}:a\in D^{\prime}\rangle\in\mathsf{W}
with
X_{a}\in\mathcal{U}_{a}
for
a\in D^{\prime}
, there is
h\in{}^{\bigcup D^{\prime}}Z\cap\mathsf{W}
with
(h\upharpoonright a)\in X_{a}
for
a\in D^{\prime}
.

Note that, if the set
D
is countable in
\mathsf{W}
, a
\mathsf{W}
-tower is
\omega_{1}
-
\mathsf{W}
-complete if and only if the above condition holds when
D^{\prime}=D
. One implication is obvious; to see the reverse one, just let
X_{a}:={}^{a}Z
for all
a\in D-D^{\prime}
.

Lemma 3.26
.

A
\mathsf{W}
-tower
\mathcal{E}:=\langle\mathcal{U}_{a}:a\in D\rangle
over a set
Z
is
\omega_{1}
-
\mathsf{W}
-complete if and only if its direct limit
\langle\mathsf{Ult}(\mathsf{M};\mathcal{E}),E_{\mathcal{E}}\rangle
is such that
E_{\mathcal{E}}
is
\mathsf{W}
-well-founded.

Proof.

The proof is analogous to the one of
[
MS89
, Lemma 1.1]
, for (
\mathsf{V}
-)towers. The main difference is that we must ensure that some of the sequences are in
\mathsf{W}
. If
\mathcal{E}
is not
\omega_{1}
-
\mathsf{W}
-complete, this is witnessed by a sequence
\langle X_{a}:a\in D^{\prime}\rangle\in\mathsf{W}
; if
E_{\mathcal{E}}
is not
\mathsf{W}
-well-founded, this is also witnessed by a sequence in
\mathsf{W}
. From one of these two sequences, we can construct the other, following
[
MS89
, Lemma 1.1]
, but with care to ensure that every step is definable and hence feasible within
\mathsf{W}
. As a consequence, the sequence produced this way is in
\mathsf{W}
.
∎

Definition 3.27
.

Let
\alpha
be an ordinal and
Y\in\mathsf{W}
a set. A subset
A\subseteq{}^{\omega}Y
is said to be
\alpha
-
\mathsf{W}
-homogeneous if there is a sequence
\langle\mathcal{U}_{s}:s\in{}^{<\omega}Y\rangle\in\mathsf{V}
where

•

\mathcal{U}_{s}
is an
\alpha
-
\mathsf{W}
-complete
\mathsf{W}
-ultrafilter on
{}^{\ell(s)}Z
, for
Z
a set;

•

if
s_{1}\subseteq s_{2}
then
\mathcal{U}_{s_{2}}
projects to
\mathcal{U}_{s_{1}}
;

•

x\in A
if and only if the
\mathsf{W}
-tower
\langle\mathcal{U}_{x\upharpoonright n}:n<\omega\rangle
is
\omega_{1}
-
\mathsf{W}
-complete.

The main difference with the classical notion of homogeneity is that here the sequence
\langle\mathcal{U}_{s}:s\in{}^{<\omega}Y\rangle
of
\mathsf{W}
-ultrafilters witnessing the homogeneity does not need to belong to the inner model
\mathsf{W}
. Anyways, we can now prove that:

Theorem 3.28
.

Let
\lambda
be a cardinal where
\lambda^{+}
is
\mathsf{W}
-measurable, for some transitive inner model
\mathsf{W}
. Let
Y\in\mathsf{W}
be non-empty and such that
|Y|^{\mathsf{W}}\leq\lambda
. Let
a\subseteq\lambda
and
a\in\mathsf{W}
. Every
\Pi^{1}_{1}(a)
-definable subset
A\subseteq{}^{\omega}Y
is
\lambda^{+}
-
\mathsf{W}
-homogeneous.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{U}
be a
\lambda^{+}
-
\mathsf{W}
-complete
\mathsf{W}
-normal
\mathsf{W}
-ultrafilter on
\lambda^{+}
. Since
A
is a
\Pi^{1}_{1}(a)
-definable set, there is a map
p\mapsto(<_{p})
with domain
{}^{<\omega}Y
such that

•

for all
p\neq\varnothing
,
(<_{p})
is a linear order on
\ell(p)
with greatest element
0
;

•

if
p\subseteq q
, then
(<_{p})
is the restriction of
(<_{q})
to
\ell(p)
;

•

for all
x\in{}^{\omega}Y
, we can define
(<_{x}):=\bigcup_{n<\omega}(<_{x\upharpoonright n})
, and it holds

x\in A\ \text{if and only if}\ (<_{x})\ \text{is a well-ordering of}\ \omega

(cfr
[
Kec95
, Theorem 36.12]
). Also, the map
p\mapsto(<_{p})
is in
\mathsf{L}[a]
. Since
a\in\mathsf{W}
and
\mathsf{L}[a]
is the least inner model of
\mathrm{ZFC}
containing
a
, we have that
\mathsf{L}[a]\subseteq\mathsf{W}
.

For
p\in{}^{<\omega}Y
, we define the set
T_{p}
as follows:

T_{p}:=\{u\in{}^{\ell(p)}\lambda^{+}:\forall m<n<\ell(p),(m<_{p}n\Leftrightarrow u(m)<u(n))\}.

Basically,
T_{p}
is the set of sequences of ordinals less than
\lambda^{+}
of length
\ell(p)
ordered in the same way as
(<_{p})
. Now we define
\mathcal{U}_{p}
, for
p\in{}^{<\omega}Y
, as

\mathcal{U}_{p}:=\{X\subseteq{}^{\ell(p)}\lambda^{+}:\exists H\in\mathcal{U}\cap\mathsf{W},{}^{\ell(p)}H\cap T_{p}\subseteq X\}.

First of all, we check that each
\mathcal{U}_{p}
for
p\in{}^{<\omega}Y
is a
\lambda^{+}
-
\mathsf{W}
-complete
\mathsf{W}
-ultrafilter. The fact it is a filter is easily seen to be true. For the
\lambda^{+}
-
\mathsf{W}
-completeness, let
\langle X_{\eta}:\eta<\beta\rangle\in\mathsf{W}
be a sequence such that
\beta<\lambda^{+}
and
X_{\eta}\in\mathcal{U}_{p}
for all
\eta<\beta
. Then for all
\eta<\beta
there is
H_{\eta}\in\mathcal{U}\cap\mathsf{W}
such that

{}^{\ell(p)}H_{\eta}\cap T_{p}\subseteq X_{\eta}.

In addition, the sequence
\langle H_{\eta}:\eta<\beta\rangle
is definable from
\langle X_{\eta}:\eta<\beta\rangle
thanks to the axiom of choice (that holds in
\mathsf{W}
), hence
\langle H_{\eta}:\eta<\beta\rangle\in\mathsf{W}
. Since
\mathcal{U}
is
\lambda^{+}
-
\mathsf{W}
-complete, it holds that
H:=\bigcap_{\eta<\beta}H_{\eta}\in\mathcal{U}\cap\mathsf{W}
. But then

{}^{\ell(p)}H\cap T_{p}\subseteq\textstyle\bigcap_{\eta<\beta}X_{\eta},

hence implying
\bigcap_{\eta<\beta}X_{\eta}\in\mathcal{U}_{p}
, as desired. To show that
\mathcal{U}_{p}
is a
\mathsf{W}
-ultrafilter, we need something more. This complication is due to the ordering
(<_{p})
, that does not let itself work well with a proof by induction. Hence, for
p\in{}^{<\omega}Y
, let

\sigma_{p}:\langle\ell(p),<_{p}\rangle\to\langle\ell(p),<\rangle

be the unique order isomorphism, that is,
\sigma_{p}
“rearranges” the set
\ell(p)
in the natural order. Let
[\lambda^{+}]^{\ell(p)}
denote the subset of
{}^{\ell(p)}\lambda^{+}
consisting of strictly increasing sequences. Then
\sigma_{p}
induces a bijection
\sigma_{p}^{\ast}:T_{p}\to[\lambda^{+}]^{\ell(p)}
given by

\sigma_{p}^{\ast}(\langle\alpha_{0},\ldots,\alpha_{\ell(p)-1}\rangle):=\langle\alpha_{\sigma_{p}^{-1}(0)},\ldots,\alpha_{\sigma_{p}^{-1}(\ell(p)-1)}\rangle.

Define also, for each
m<\omega
,

\mathcal{U}^{(m)}:=\{X\subseteq{}^{m}(\lambda^{+})^{\mathsf{V}}:\exists H\in\mathcal{U}\cap\mathsf{W},{}^{m}H\cap[\lambda^{+}]^{m}\subseteq X\}.

It is easy to see that
X\in\mathcal{U}_{p}
if and only if
(\sigma_{p}^{\ast})^{\prime\prime}X\in\mathcal{U}^{(\ell(p))}
. Since
\sigma_{p}\in\mathsf{W}
, the map
p\mapsto(<_{p})
is in
\mathsf{W}
and the definition of
T_{p}
is absolute (so
T_{p}\in\mathsf{W}
) then also
\sigma_{p}^{\ast}\in\mathsf{W}
hence it is enough to prove that
\mathcal{U}^{(m)}
is a
\mathsf{W}
-ultrafilter for all
m<\omega
.

Hence, we need to show that for all
X\subseteq{}^{m}\lambda^{+}
if
X\in\mathsf{W}
then either
X\in\mathcal{U}^{(m)}
or
({}^{m}\lambda^{+}-X)\in\mathcal{U}^{(m)}
. Let us do it by induction on
m<\omega
. For
m=0
, the claim is that either
\{\varnothing\}\in X
or
\{\varnothing\}\in\{\varnothing\}-X
, that is true for every
X\subseteq\{\varnothing\}={}^{0}\lambda^{+}
. Let us now do the inductive step.

Let
X\in\mathsf{W}
be a subset of
{}^{m+1}\lambda^{+}
. For each
\beta<\lambda^{+}
we define

K_{\beta}:=\{u\in{}^{m}\lambda^{+}:\langle\beta\rangle^{\smallfrown}u\in X\cap[\lambda^{+}]^{m+1}\}.

The requirement about
\langle\beta\rangle^{\smallfrown}u
is equivalent to asking
u\in[\lambda^{+}]^{m}
,
\langle\beta\rangle^{\smallfrown}u\in X
and
\beta
is smaller than every element of the
u
. In particular, notice
K_{\beta}\subseteq[\lambda^{+}]^{m}
, and
K_{\beta}\in\mathsf{W}
, so by inductive hypothesis either
K_{\beta}\in\mathcal{U}^{(m)}
or
([\lambda^{+}]^{m}-K_{\beta})\in\mathcal{U}^{(m)}
. Now, let

Z:=\{\beta\in\lambda^{+}:K_{\beta}\in\mathcal{U}^{(m)}\}.

Since
Z\in\mathsf{W}
, there are two possibilities: either
Z\in\mathcal{U}
or
(\lambda^{+}-Z)\in\mathcal{U}
, since
\mathcal{U}
is a
\mathsf{W}
-ultrafilter. We distinguish the two cases.

If
Z\in\mathcal{U}
, then for all
\beta\in Z
we know there is some
H_{\beta}\in\mathcal{U}\cap\mathsf{W}
such that
{}^{m}H_{\beta}\cap[\lambda^{+}]^{m}\subseteq K_{\beta}
. Let
X_{\beta}:=H_{\beta}
for
\beta\in Z
and
X_{\beta}:=\lambda^{+}
for
\beta\not\in Z
. The sequence
\langle X_{\beta}:\beta<\lambda^{+}\rangle
belongs to
\mathsf{W}
because it is defined uniformly, hence we can apply the
\mathsf{W}
-normality of
\mathcal{U}
to get that

\Delta_{\beta<\lambda^{+}}X_{\beta}\in\mathcal{U}.

Let
H:=Z\cap\Delta_{\beta<\lambda^{+}}X_{\beta}
. This is in
\mathcal{U}
and in
\mathsf{W}
. We want to show that

{}^{m+1}H\cap[\lambda^{+}]^{m+1}\subseteq X,

to finish the proof. So, consider any sequence in
{}^{m+1}H\cap[\lambda^{+}]^{m+1}
. We denote this sequence by
\langle\beta\rangle^{\smallfrown}u
, simply by writing explicitly the value of the last element of the sequence. So,
u\in{}^{m}H
,
\beta\in H
, and
\langle\beta\rangle^{\smallfrown}u\in[\lambda^{+}]^{m+1}
. In particular, write
u=\langle\alpha_{0},\ldots,\alpha_{m-1}\rangle
. Since each
\alpha_{i}\in H
, and also
\beta<\alpha_{i}
for each
i
, then by definition of the diagonal intersection it holds
\alpha_{i}\in X_{\beta}
for all
i
. But also
\beta\in Z
, hence
X_{\beta}=H_{\beta}
, so we got that
u\in{}^{m}H_{\beta}\cap[\lambda^{+}]^{m}
, hence
u\in K_{\beta}
. This means that
\langle\beta\rangle^{\smallfrown}u\in X
, as desired.

In the other case, if
Z\not\in\mathcal{U}
, then everything is reversed. So, for
\beta\in(\lambda^{+}-Z)
we find some
H_{\beta}\in\mathcal{U}\cap\mathsf{W}
such that
{}^{m}H_{\beta}\cap[\lambda^{+}]^{m}\subseteq[\lambda^{+}]^{m}-K_{\beta}
. We then let
X_{\beta}:=H_{\beta}
for
\beta\not\in Z
and
X_{\beta}:=\lambda^{+}
for
\beta\in Z
. Again as before, the sequence
\langle X_{\beta}:\beta<\lambda^{+}\rangle
belongs to
\mathsf{W}
because it is defined uniformly, hence by
\mathsf{W}
-normality we get
\Delta_{\beta<\lambda^{+}}X_{\beta}\in\mathcal{U}
and we can let

H:=(\lambda^{+}-Z)\cap\Delta_{\beta<\lambda^{+}}X_{\beta}\in\mathcal{U}\cap\mathsf{W}.

Our claim is
{}^{m+1}H\cap[\lambda^{+}]^{m+1}\subseteq([\lambda^{+}]^{m+1}-X)
. Let
\langle\beta\rangle^{\smallfrown}u
be in
{}^{m+1}H\cap[\lambda^{+}]^{m+1}
. If
u=\langle\alpha_{0},\ldots,\alpha_{m-1}\rangle
then each
\alpha_{i}\in H
and also
\beta<\alpha_{i}
, hence
\alpha_{i}\in X_{\beta}
and since
\beta\in(\lambda^{+}-Z)
we get that
X_{\beta}=H_{\beta}
, so we deduce
u\in{}^{m}H_{\beta}\cap[\lambda^{+}]^{m}
, implying that
u\in[\lambda^{+}]^{m}-K_{\beta}
. So this means that
\langle\beta\rangle^{\smallfrown}u\not\in X
, as desired.

This proves that each
\mathcal{U}_{p}
is a
\lambda^{+}
-
\mathsf{W}
-complete
\mathsf{W}
-ultrafilter on
{}^{\ell(p)}\lambda^{+}
. Checking that this sequence witnesses the
\lambda^{+}
-
\mathsf{W}
-homogeneity of
A
is not too difficult. First, if
s_{1}\subseteq s_{2}
, we have to show that
\mathcal{U}_{s_{2}}
projects to
\mathcal{U}_{s_{1}}
. This means that for all
X\in{}^{\ell(s_{1})}\lambda^{+}
we have
X\in\mathcal{U}_{s_{1}}
if and only if

\{p\in{}^{\ell(s_{2})}\lambda^{+}:(p\upharpoonright\ell(s_{1}))\in X\}\in\mathcal{U}_{s_{2}}.

So, let
X\in\mathcal{U}_{s_{1}}
. Then there is
H\in\mathcal{U}\cap\mathsf{W}
such that
{}^{\ell(s_{1})}H\cap T_{s_{1}}\subseteq X
. Now,

{}^{\ell(s_{2})}H\cap T_{s_{2}}\subseteq\{p\in{}^{\ell(s_{2})}\lambda^{+}:(p\upharpoonright\ell(s_{1}))\in X\}

because if
p\in{}^{\ell(s_{2})}H\cap T_{s_{2}}
then clearly
(p\upharpoonright\ell(s_{1}))\in{}^{\ell(s_{1})}H
but it also holds
(p\upharpoonright\ell(s_{1}))\in T_{s_{1}}
because
(<_{s_{1}})=(<_{s_{2}})\upharpoonright\ell(s_{1})
. Viceversa, assume that

{}^{\ell(s_{2})}H\cap T_{s_{2}}\subseteq\{p\in{}^{\ell(s_{2})}\lambda^{+}:(p\upharpoonright\ell(s_{1}))\in X\}.

Then if we take any sequence in
{}^{\ell(s_{1})}H\cap T_{s_{1}}
and we extend it to a sequence in
{}^{\ell(s_{2})}H\cap T_{s_{2}}
, its restriction to
\ell(s_{1})
is in
X
, hence
{}^{\ell(s_{1})}H\cap T_{s_{1}}\subseteq X
so
X\in\mathcal{U}_{s_{1}}
.

The last thing left to prove is that
x\in A
if and only if
\langle\mathcal{U}_{x\upharpoonright n}:n<\omega\rangle
is
\omega_{1}
-
\mathsf{W}
-complete. We already know, by construction, that
x\in A
if and only if
(<_{x})
is a well-ordering of
\omega
. So it is enough to prove that
(<_{x})
is ill-founded if and only if the
\mathsf{W}
-tower
\langle\mathcal{U}_{x\upharpoonright n}:n<\omega\rangle
is not
\omega_{1}
-
\mathsf{W}
-complete.

Assume
(<_{x})
is ill-founded. So, there is a sequence
\langle n_{k}:k<\omega\rangle
such that
n_{k+1}<_{x}n_{k}
for all
k<\omega
. Since being ill-founded is absolute, we can assume that
\langle n_{k}:k<\omega\rangle\in\mathsf{W}
. Now for all
k<\omega
let us define

a_{k}:=x\upharpoonright(\max\{n_{0},\ldots,n_{k}\}+1).

By definition,
a_{k}\leq a_{k+1}
for all
k<\omega
, and for all
N<\omega
there is
k<\omega
such that
N<a_{k}
. Now let us define

D:=\{a_{k}:\forall h>k:a_{k}\neq a_{h}\}.

This set
D
is infinite and for all
a\in D
it holds
a\subseteq x
. For each
a\in D
, let us pick
X_{a}:=T_{a}
. Since
\langle n_{k}:k<\omega\rangle\in\mathsf{W}
, we also have that
D\in\mathsf{W}
hence in particular
\{X_{a}:a\in D\}\in\mathsf{W}
(and that can be ordered by the lengths of
a
s). So, assume by contradiction that the
\mathsf{W}
-tower
\langle\mathcal{U}_{x\upharpoonright n}:n<\omega\rangle
is
\omega_{1}
-
\mathsf{W}
-complete. Then there is a map
h:\omega\to\lambda^{+}
in
\mathsf{W}
such that
(h\upharpoonright a)\in X_{a}
for all
a\in D
. But since
X_{a}=T_{a}
, this implies that for all
k<\omega
it holds

h(n_{k})<\ldots<h(n_{0})

so necessarily
\langle h(n_{k}):k<\omega\rangle\in\mathsf{W}
is an infinite descending sequence in
\lambda^{+}
, that is a contradiction.

Viceversa, assume the
\mathsf{W}
-tower
\langle\mathcal{U}_{x\upharpoonright n}:n<\omega\rangle
is not
\omega_{1}
-
\mathsf{W}
-complete. By Lemma
3.26
, we know the ultrapower along this tower is ill-founded. This means we can find a sequence of increasing lengths
\langle\ell_{i}:i<\omega\rangle
and elements
p_{i}\in\mathsf{Ult}(\mathsf{W},\mathcal{U}_{x\upharpoonright\ell_{i}})
such that
[\langle p_{i},x\upharpoonright\ell_{i}\rangle]_{\sim}E_{\infty}[\langle p_{j},x\upharpoonright\ell_{j}\rangle]_{\sim}
for
j<i
. For all
n<\omega
, there is a map
f_{n}:{}^{\ell_{n}}\lambda^{+}\to\mathsf{W}
in
\mathsf{W}
such that
p_{n}=[f_{n}]_{\mathcal{U}_{x\upharpoonright\ell_{n}}}
.

Now, like in the proof of Lemma

X_{\ell_{n+1}}:=\{p\in{}^{\ell_{n+1}}\lambda^{+}:f_{n+1}(p)\in f_{n}(p\upharpoonright\ell_{n})\}\in\mathcal{U}_{x\upharpoonright\ell_{n+1}}.

So, there is

such that

\langle\ell_{i}:i<\omega\rangle
and
\langle p_{i}:i<\omega\rangle
belong to
\mathsf{W}
, then also the sequence
\langle H_{n+1}:n<\omega\rangle\in\mathsf{W}
. Since
\mathcal{U}
is
\lambda^{+}
-
\mathsf{W}
-complete, surely
\omega_{1}\leq\lambda^{+}
hence we know that there is
H\in\mathcal{U}
such that

for all

.

Since
(<_{x})
is a well-ordering of
\omega
, and

\alpha_{n}\in H
for all

3.4
Consequences of Homogeneity

One of the original lemmata in the proof of analytic determinacy by Martin is that if a set of reals is
\omega_{1}
-homogeneous, then the game having its complement as payoff set is determined. This still holds true if we weaken the hypothesis to homogeneity with respect to some inner model, as the following lemma shows.

Lemma 3.30
.

Let
A\subseteq{}^{\omega}Y
be
(\lambda^{+})^{\mathsf{W}}
-
\mathsf{W}
-homogeneous, for some
\mathsf{W}
with
Y\in\mathsf{W}
and such that
|Y|^{\mathsf{W}}\leq\lambda
. Then the game
\mathcal{G}({}^{\omega}Y-A,{}^{<\omega}Y)
is determined.

Proof.

Let
\langle\mathcal{U}_{s}:s\in{}^{<\omega}Y\rangle
witness the fact that
A
is
(\lambda^{+})^{\mathsf{W}}
-
\mathsf{W}
-homogeneous. Let
Z
be the set such that each
\mathcal{U}_{s}
is over
{}^{\ell(s)}Z
. For each
s\not\in A
, pick a sequence
\langle X_{n}^{s}:n<\omega\rangle\in\mathsf{W}
such that
X_{n}^{s}\in\mathcal{U}_{s}
for each
n<\omega
but there is no
h:\omega\to Z
in
\mathsf{W}
such that
(h\upharpoonright n)\in X_{n}^{s}
for all
n<\omega
. Consider now the set
T_{A}\subseteq{}^{<\omega}Y\times{}^{<\omega}Z
given by all pairs
\langle u,v\rangle
such that
\ell(u)=\ell(v)
and such that for all
s\not\in A
with
u\subseteq s
it holds that
v\in X_{\ell(u)}^{s}
. This defines
T_{A}
uniquely.

The construction of this tree
T_{A}
is done inside
\mathsf{W}
, so in particular
T_{A}\in\mathsf{W}
. Let us define an auxiliary game
\mathcal{G}^{\ast}
, played on
Y\cup(Y\times Z)\cup\{\bot\}
, as follows:

•

at his
(k+1)
-th move, Alberto plays just
a_{k}\in Y
;

•

at her
(k+1)
-th move, Barbara plays
\langle b_{k},\eta_{k}\rangle\in Y\times Z
in a way that

\langle\langle a_{0},b_{0},\ldots,a_{k},b_{k}\rangle\upharpoonright(k+1),\langle\eta_{0},\ldots,\eta_{k}\rangle\rangle\in T_{A},

if she is able to. If she is not, then she has to play
\bot
, and the moves from either player do not matter anymore, since Barbara loses.

Alberto wins if and only if Barbara plays
\bot
at some point. Hence the payoff set for
\mathcal{G}^{\ast}
is open, so by Gale-Stewart’s theorem,
\mathcal{G}^{\ast}
is determined. If Barbara has a winning strategy
\sigma^{\ast}
for
\mathcal{G}^{\ast}
, she has one for
\mathcal{G}(A,{}^{<\omega}Y)
too: if the strategy
\sigma^{\ast}
tells her to play
\langle b_{k},\eta_{k}\rangle
, she plays
b_{k}
and wins, since if
x:=\langle a_{0},b_{0},\ldots\rangle
and
\eta:=\langle\eta_{0},\eta_{1},\ldots\rangle
, and
\sigma^{\ast}
is winning,
\langle x,\eta\rangle\in[T_{A}]
hence
x\in\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}[T_{A}]=A
.

Suppose now Alberto has a winning strategy
\sigma^{\ast}
for
\mathcal{G}^{\ast}
. If he does, he also has such a
\sigma^{\ast}
that belongs to
\mathsf{W}
, since the statement declaring that he has a winning strategy for a fixed open game is a
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{1}_{2}
-sentence, hence is absolute. We want to prove that this implies Alberto has a winning strategy for
\mathcal{G}(A,{}^{<\omega}Y)
, as well. We can consider
\sigma^{\ast}
as being a map having as variable not a sequence of the form

\langle a_{0},\langle b_{0},\eta_{0}\rangle,a_{1},\ldots,\langle b_{k},\eta_{k}\rangle\rangle
, but rather a pair of finite sequences, as

\langle\langle a_{0},b_{0},a_{1},\ldots,b_{k}\rangle,\langle\eta_{0},\ldots,\eta_{k}\rangle\rangle.

We want to define a strategy
\sigma
for Alberto in
\mathcal{G}(A,X^{<\omega})
, as follows. Let
s:=\langle a_{0},\ldots,b_{k}\rangle
be a position in the game, with Alberto to move. Since
\mathcal{U}_{s\upharpoonright(k+1)}
is
(\lambda^{+})^{\mathsf{W}}
-
\mathsf{W}
-complete (and
|Y|^{\mathsf{W}}\leq\lambda<(\lambda^{+})^{\mathsf{W}}
), there is
a\in Y
such that

\{t\in{}^{k+1}Z:\sigma^{\ast}(\langle s,t\rangle)=a\}\in\mathcal{U}_{s\upharpoonright(k+1)},

and this
a
is unique. Hence we can define
\sigma(s):=a
. We want to prove that this is a winning strategy, so suppose that
x=\langle a_{0},b_{0},\ldots\rangle\in{}^{\omega}Y
is a play of the game
\mathcal{G}(A,{}^{<\omega}Y)
consistent with the strategy
\sigma
. Then, for all
k<\omega
, let

X_{k+1}:=\{t\in{}^{k+1}Z:\sigma^{\ast}(\langle\langle a_{0},b_{0},\ldots,a_{k},b_{k}\rangle,t\rangle)=a_{k+1}\}.

By the definition of
\sigma
, it holds
X_{k+1}\in\mathcal{U}_{x\upharpoonright(k+1)}
for all
k<\omega
. If by contradiction Alberto does not win, we would have
x\in A=\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}[T_{A}]
, so by definition there would be some
f\in{}^{\omega}Z\cap\mathsf{W}
such that
(f\upharpoonright(k+1))\in X_{k+1}
for all
k<\omega
. Then if Barbara plays
b_{k}
according to the sequence
x
, and
\eta_{k}:=f(k)
, we would have a play of the game
\mathcal{G}^{\ast}
consistent with the strategy
\sigma^{\ast}
but won by Barbara.∎

As a corollary of Corollary
3.20
, Theorem
3.28
and Lemma
3.30
, we get

Corollary 3.31
.

If
a^{\sharp}
exists for all
a\subseteq\lambda
, any game
\mathcal{G}(A,{}^{\omega}\lambda)
with
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{1}_{1}
-definable payoff set is determined. So, if
a^{\sharp}
exists for all

,
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{1}_{1}
-Determinacy holds.

For our results about generic absoluteness, the corollaries about determinacy are not needed, but still interesting to state and prove as they show the strength of the homogeneity property for subsets of the reals. But, we are more interested in another consequence of homogeneity: that is, the universally Baire property.

Theorem 3.32
.

Let
A\subseteq{}^{\omega}\omega\times{}^{\omega}\omega
be
(\lambda^{+})^{\mathsf{W}}
-
\mathsf{W}
-homogeneous, for
\lambda

\mathsf{W}
with

Proof.

As in the beginning of the proof of Lemma

for each

but there is no map

in
\mathsf{W}
such that
(h\upharpoonright n)\in X_{n}^{\langle x,y\rangle}
for all
n<\omega
. Consider now the set

T_{A}\subseteq{}^{<\omega}\omega\times{}^{<\omega}\omega\times{}^{<\omega}\lambda^{+}

given by all triples

such that
\ell(u)=\ell(v)=\ell(w)
, and for all
\langle x,y\rangle\not\in A
with

and

it holds that

. The construction of this set

is done entirely inside
\mathsf{W}
, so in particular
T_{A}\in\mathsf{W}
. Also,
\pi_{1,2}^{\prime\prime}[T_{A}]=A
.

Now we want to build a pair of trees
S
and
T
on
{}^{<\omega}\omega\times{}^{<\omega}\mathsf{Ord}
such that
\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}[S]=\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}A

\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}[Z]={}^{\omega}\omega-\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}A

\mathbb{P}
with
|\mathbb{P}|\leq\lambda

\mathbb{P}\Vdash\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}[S]\cup\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}[T]={}^{\omega}\omega.

First of all, let us build
S
. Define
S:=\pi_{13}^{\prime\prime}T_{A}\subseteq{}^{<\omega}\omega\times{}^{<\omega}\lambda^{+}
. We have

\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}[S]=\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}[\pi_{13}^{\prime\prime}T_{A}]=\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}[T_{A}]=\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}(\pi_{12}^{\prime\prime}[T_{A}])=\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}A.

Now, for
p_{1}\subseteq p_{2}
and
q_{1}\subseteq q_{2}
such that
\ell(p_{1})=\ell(q_{1})
and
\ell(p_{2})=\ell(q_{2})
, we can define the elementary embedding

j_{\langle p_{1},q_{1}\rangle,\langle p_{2},q_{2}\rangle}:\mathsf{Ult}(\mathsf{W},\mathcal{U}_{\langle p_{1},q_{1}\rangle})\hookrightarrow\mathsf{Ult}(\mathsf{W},\mathcal{U}_{\langle p_{2},q_{2}\rangle}).

Let us now fix a bijection
\psi
from
\omega
to
\omega^{<\omega}
such that if
\psi(n)\subseteq\psi(m)
then
n\leq m
. We define the tree
T\subseteq{}^{<\omega}\omega\times{}^{<\omega}\mathsf{Ord}
as

T:=\{\langle p,u\rangle:\forall n<m<\ell(q):\psi(n)\subseteq\psi(m)\Rightarrow u(m)<j(u(n))\}

where
j=j_{\langle p\upharpoonright\ell(\psi(n)),\psi(n)\rangle,\langle p\upharpoonright\ell(\psi(m)),\psi(m)\rangle}
. Let us show
\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}[T]={}^{\omega}\omega-\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}A
.

Pick
x\in\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}[T]
. Then
\langle x,f\rangle\in[T]
for some
f\in{}^{\omega}\mathsf{Ord}
, and let

be any. We want to prove
\langle x,y\rangle\not\in A
, to show
x\in{}^{\omega}\omega-\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}A
. This is equivalent to

not being a well-order on
\omega
. Define

for

\psi(n_{k})=y\upharpoonright k

\psi(n_{k})\subseteq\psi(n_{k+1})

.

Now we have

and

with
\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}[S]=\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}A
,

. We just need to check that if

is a forcing notion with

, then

forces that

Notice that we have a family of

\mathsf{W}

Since

, we have that

hence the various
\mathcal{U}_{p}^{\ast}
are
(\lambda^{+})^{\mathsf{W}}
-
\mathsf{W}[G]
-complete

-ultrafilters in
\mathsf{V}[G]
. Now, we can follow the entire construction of

and

in
\mathsf{V}[G]
instead that in

, and we get precisely the same trees

and

. But now,

is precisely

∎

3.20
and Theorem

:

Corollary 3.33
.

Assume

To conclude this section, we want to give a positive answer to Question
2.13
in the case

for

. Before doing so, we need to recall two lemmata about the absoluteness of sharps.

.

Let

Lemma 3.35
.

Assume

exists for all

\mathsf{V}

-generic, we can lift this

.

Assume
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{1}_{1}
-Determinacy and
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{1}_{4}
-

\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{1}_{4}
-

First of all, it is easy to check that

\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{1}_{3}
-
\mathrm{Coll}(\omega,\lambda)
-Absoluteness holds.

The Answer for

4.1
Martin-Steel Generalised

Our goal now is to replicate the same results but for
n\geq 5
. In order to do so, we need to generalise a result from the paper
[

]
by Martin and Steel. The result in question is a way to propagate the homogeneity for subsets of the reals: if a set
A\subseteq{}^{\omega}\omega\times{}^{\omega}\omega
is
\delta^{+}
-homogeneous, where
\delta
is a Woodin cardinal, then
{}^{\omega}\omega-\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}A
is
\alpha
-homogeneous for all
\alpha<\delta
. Hence, starting with homogeneity for
\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{1}_{1}
-sets and having

\mathsf{W}

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\langle\mathsf{M},\in\rangle
and
\langle\mathsf{N},\in\rangle
be transitive models of
\mathrm{ZFC}
, with
\mathsf{M}\subseteq\mathsf{W}
and
\mathsf{N}\subseteq\mathsf{W}
. Suppose
{}^{\omega}\mathsf{M}\cap\mathsf{W}\subseteq\mathsf{M}
. Assume
\mathsf{W}_{\kappa+1}\cap\mathsf{M}=\mathsf{W}_{\kappa+1}\cap\mathsf{N}
for some

, and let

be a
\mathsf{M}
-extender with

-well-founded.

Proof.

This proof is very similar to the one of
[
MS89
, Lemma 1.6]
. The main change is that the sequence witnessing the

-ill-foundedness of

. Also, where in the original theorem they use the hypothesis

, here we need to apply the weaker hypothesis

, that is enough exactly because we picked the sequence to belong to
\mathsf{W}
. At the end, we reach a contradiction by showing that it is impossible for

to be a
\omega_{1}
-
\mathsf{M}

\mathsf{M}
-extender.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

In the same hypotheses of Lemma

, assume
{}^{\omega}\mathsf{N}\cap\mathsf{W}\subseteq\mathsf{N}
and that the support

of

is countably closed in
\mathsf{W}
(meaning that every countable subset of

belonging to the model

is still inside

This proof is essentially identical to the one of
[
MS89
, Lemma 1.8]
. The only changes are that all countable closures are assumed with sequences belonging to
\mathsf{W}
: indeed, the hypotheses are
{}^{\omega}\mathsf{M}\cap\mathsf{W}\subseteq\mathsf{M}
,
{}^{\omega}\mathsf{N}\cap\mathsf{W}\subseteq\mathsf{N}
and that every countable subset of
S
belonging to
\mathsf{W}
is still inside

. By using these properties, we are able to follow the classical proof, starting with a sequences of elements in
\mathsf{Ult}(\mathsf{N};\mathcal{E})
belonging to
\mathsf{W}
and checking that every set-theoretic operation we apply is doable inside
\mathsf{W}
, hence producing sequences in
\mathsf{W}
to which we can apply our weaker hypotheses of countable closure with respect to
\mathsf{W}
.
∎

We gave this particular definition of
\mathsf{W}
-extender because it fits well with the concept of
\mathsf{W}
-towers introduced in the previous section, but this version has one particular defect: each
\mathcal{U}_{a}
is a
\mathsf{W}
-ultrafilter on
{}^{a}\mathsf{W}_{\kappa}
, but we would like it to be a
\mathsf{W}
-ultrafilter on
{}^{|a|}\mathsf{W}_{\kappa}
, in order to avoid notational problems. For this reason, we shall use an alternative definition of
\mathsf{W}
-extender. This is the same approach followed by Martin and Steel in
[
MS89
]
. Hence, we are going to replace any of our
\mathsf{W}
-extenders
\mathcal{E}=\langle\mathcal{U}_{a}:a\in\wp_{\omega}(S)\rangle
with a sequence

\langle\mathcal{V}_{s}:s\in{}^{<\omega}S\rangle,

where
{}^{<\omega}S
is the collection of all finite (ordered) sequences of elements of
S
. Each
\mathcal{V}_{s}
is a
\mathsf{W}
-ultrafilter on
{}^{\ell(s)}\mathsf{W}_{\kappa}
, where
\ell(s)
is the length of
s
, defined by the property that
X\in\mathcal{V}_{s}
if and only if

\{f\in{}^{s^{\prime\prime}\ell(s)}\mathsf{W}_{\kappa}:(n\mapsto f(s(n)))\in X\}\in\mathcal{U}_{s^{\prime\prime}\ell(s)},

where
s^{\prime\prime}\ell(s)
is the image of the sequence
s
, and
(n\mapsto f(s(n)))
denotes the map that sends every
n<\ell(s)
to
f(s(n))
. The sequence
\langle\mathcal{V}_{s}:s\in{}^{<\omega}S\rangle
is not a
\mathsf{W}
-extender, because in particular it is not a
\mathsf{W}
-tower. From now onwards, we may say “let
\langle\mathcal{V}_{s}:s\in{}^{<\omega}S\rangle
be a
\mathsf{W}
-extender”, but what we mean really is that we consider a
\mathsf{W}
-extender
\mathcal{E}
as defined in Definition
4.1
and then we follow this construction to build
\langle\mathcal{V}_{s}:s\in{}^{<\omega}S\rangle
. We want now to use
\mathsf{W}
-extenders to introduce the concept of
\mathsf{W}
-iteration tree of finite length. Martin and Steel studied iteration trees in their article
[
MS94
]
, and they had used them previously in the paper
[
MS89
]
. We mainly follow the presentation given in the latter.

Definition 4.4
.

Fix

. A
\mathsf{W}
-iteration tree of length

consists of

where

with

as the
(\triangleleft)
-least element, such that

implies

(
\triangleleft
respects the natural order on
n+1
), and

•

\mathsf{M}_{0}=\mathsf{W}
;

•

for all

is a transitive inner model of
\mathrm{ZFC}
;

•

\varrho_{0}\leq\varrho_{1}\leq\ldots\leq\varrho_{n}
are ordinals;

•

if

it holds
\mathsf{W}_{\varrho_{k}+1}\cap\mathsf{M}_{k}=\mathsf{W}_{\varrho_{k}+1}\cap\mathsf{M}_{h}
;

•

for all
k<n
,
\mathcal{E}_{k}\in\mathsf{M}_{k}
is an
\mathsf{M}_{k}
-extender;

•

for all
k<n
, the support of
\mathcal{E}_{k}
contains
\mathsf{W}_{\varrho_{k}+1}\cap\mathsf{M}_{k}
;

•

for all

, the support of
\mathcal{E}_{k}
is countably closed in
\mathsf{W}
;

•

if
k^{\ast}
is the immediate
(\triangleleft)
-predecessor of
k+1
, for some
k<n
, then
\varrho_{k^{\ast}}\geq\mathrm{crit}(\mathcal{E}_{k})
and
\mathsf{M}_{k+1}
is the Mostowski collapse of
\mathsf{Ult}(\mathsf{M}_{k^{\ast}};\mathcal{E}_{k})
.

It seems like we are implicitely assuming that
\mathsf{Ult}(\mathsf{M}_{k^{\ast}};\mathcal{E}_{k})
is well-founded. Indeed we are not, because the other hypotheses suffice to prove it. Indeed, let us prove by induction on
k<\omega
that
{}^{\omega}\mathsf{M}_{k}\cap\mathsf{W}\subseteq\mathsf{M}_{k}
and
\mathsf{M}_{k}
is well-founded. The base step is trivial. For the inductive step, since we know that
\mathsf{M}_{k^{\ast}}
is well-founded and

, we can apply Lemma
4.2
to get that

is well-founded, and then we use Lemma
4.3
and the fact that the support of

is countably closed in

to get also that
{}^{\omega}\mathsf{M}_{k+1}\cap\mathsf{W}\subseteq\mathsf{M}_{k+1}
. In general, it could be possible to define
\mathsf{W}
-iteration trees also in the case where the support of
\mathcal{E}_{k}
is not countably closed in
\mathsf{W}
, but in that case the proof of the well-foundedness of
\mathsf{Ult}(\mathsf{M}_{k^{\ast}};\mathcal{E}_{k})
would become longer, but not useful for us. Indeed, since we are working only with these
\mathsf{W}
-iteration trees having the property that the supports of the extenders are countably closed in
\mathsf{W}
, we decided to put it directly into the definition, thus calling “
\mathsf{W}
-iteration tree” what elsewhere could be a “countably closed
\mathsf{W}
-iteration tree”. A similar hypothesis is assumed in
[
MS89
]
.

If we are given a

Definition 4.7

A branch of a

-iteration tree of length

If

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
\mathcal{T}=\langle\triangleleft,\langle\mathsf{M}_{k}:k<\omega\rangle,\langle\varrho_{k}:k<\omega\rangle,\langle\mathcal{E}_{k}:k<\omega\rangle\rangle
be a
\mathsf{W}
-iteration tree of length

and let

be an infinite branch of

\langle\xi_{n}:n\in\omega-b\rangle\in\mathsf{W}
such that, if

\xi_{n+1}<\imath_{n^{\ast},n+1}(\xi_{n^{\ast}}).

Proof.

The proof is identical to the one of
[
MS89
, Lemma 3.2]
. The only changes are that we have to use Lemmata
4.2
and
4.3
in place of
[
MS89
, Lemma 1.6]
and
[

∎

s\mapsto\mathcal{T}_{s}
that is definable in the inner model
\mathsf{W}
. This is easily formalised better in the language for set theory with an additional parameter to describe the inner model
\mathsf{W}
, but we think this slightly informal presentation is good enough to present these result. Granted this, we are finally ready to state and prove our generalised version of the main theorem by Martin and Steel from
[
MS89
]
:

Theorem 4.18
.

Let
\mathsf{W}
be a transitive inner model of
\mathrm{ZFC}
, let
\lambda
be a cardinal in
\mathsf{W}
, and
Y
a non-empty set with
|Y|^{\mathsf{W}}\leq\lambda
. Assume that some
A\subseteq{}^{\omega}Y\times{}^{\omega}Y
belonging to
\mathsf{W}
is
(\delta^{+})^{\mathsf{W}}
-
\mathsf{W}
-homogeneous, for
\delta
such that

\mathsf{W}\models``\delta\ \text{is a Woodin cardinal''}.

Then the set
{}^{\omega}Y-\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}A
is
\alpha
-
\mathsf{W}
-homogeneous for all
\alpha<\delta
.

Proof.

Since this result is arguably the most important of the section, we give a full proof, even though it follows thoroughly the one of
[
MS89
, Theorem 5.10]
. Let
\langle\mathcal{U}_{\langle s,r\rangle}:\langle s,r\rangle\in{}^{<\omega}Y\times{}^{<\omega}Y,\ \ell(s)=\ell(r)\rangle
witness the fact that the set
A
is
(\delta^{+})^{\mathsf{W}}
-
\mathsf{W}
-homogeneous. Let
\kappa_{0}<\delta
be such that Barbara has a winning strategy for
\mathcal{G}_{\kappa_{0}}^{\circ}(A)
. Thanks to Lemma
4.16
, pick
\kappa_{0}
such that
\alpha<\kappa_{0}
. Lemma
4.17
gives us
\mathsf{W}
-iteration trees
\langle\mathcal{T}_{s}:s\in{}^{<\omega}Y\rangle
where the functional
s\mapsto\mathcal{T}_{s}
is definable in the model
\mathsf{W}
(as discussed in a comment above), a family

\langle X_{\langle s,r\rangle}:\langle s,r\rangle\in{}^{<\omega}Y\times{}^{<\omega}Y,\ell(s)=\ell(r)\rangle\in\mathsf{W}

and some positions
\langle P_{\langle s,r,t\rangle}:\langle s,r\rangle\in{}^{<\omega}Y\times{}^{<\omega}Y,\ell(s)=\ell(r),t\in X_{\langle s,r\rangle}\rangle\in\mathsf{W}
.

Thanks to the
(\delta^{+})^{\mathsf{W}}
-
\mathsf{W}
-completeness of
\mathcal{U}_{\langle s,r\rangle}
, there is a set of
t\in X_{\langle s,r\rangle}
belonging to the ultrafilter
\mathcal{U}_{\langle s,r\rangle}
on which
u_{k+1}(s,r,t)
is constant, where

u_{k+1}(s,r,t):=\langle\imath_{2n+1,2k+1}(\eta_{n}^{\prime}(s,r,t)):n<k+1\rangle.

Indeed, the number of possible values for
u_{k+1}(s,r,t)
given
s
and
r
is below
(\delta^{+})^{\mathsf{W}}
.
We assume that
X_{\langle s,r\rangle}
is a subset of this set, hence we will write
u_{k+1}(s,r)
instead of
u_{k+1}(s,r,t)
for any
t\in X_{\langle s,r\rangle}
. Recall that
\mathcal{T}_{s}
consists of

\langle\langle\mathsf{M}_{k}(s):k<2\ell(s)+1\rangle,\langle\mathcal{E}_{k}(s):k<2\ell(s)+1\rangle,\langle\varrho_{k}(s):k<2\ell(s)+1\rangle\rangle.

Let in addition
\imath_{m,n}(s)
be the canonical embedding from
\mathsf{M}_{m}(s)
to
\mathsf{M}_{n}(s)
, defined clearly only in the case that
m\ll n
, in the iteration tree order.

For
k<\ell(s)
,
\psi(k)\subseteq r
and
t\in X_{\langle s,r\rangle}
, let us consider the ordinal

\beta:=\beta_{\ell(\psi(k))}(s,r,t).

Thanks to the conditions in Lemma
4.17
, the value of
\beta
only depends on
s
and
t\upharpoonright\ell(\psi(k))
for
t\in X_{\langle s,r\rangle}
for some
r
. Hence, for
s\in{}^{<\omega}Y
and
k<\ell(s)
, we can define a function
f_{s,k}:\lambda_{s,k}\to\mathsf{Ord}
defined as

f_{s,k}(t):=\beta_{\ell(\psi(k))}(s,r,t^{\prime})

for all
r\supseteq\psi(k)
with
\ell(r)=\ell(s)
and all
t^{\prime}\supseteq t
with
t^{\prime}\in X_{\langle s,r\rangle}
. Since this is done uniformly in
s
and
k
, both the sequence
\langle\lambda_{s,k}:s\in{}^{<\omega}Y,k<\ell(s)\rangle
and the sequence
\langle f_{s,k}:s\in{}^{<\omega}Y,k<\ell(s)\rangle
belong to the model
\mathsf{W}
.

The domain
\lambda_{s,k}
is an element of the ultrafilter
\mathcal{U}_{\langle s\upharpoonright\ell(\psi(k)),\psi(k)\rangle}
. Now we can consider the Mostowski collapse of the equivalence class of the function
f_{s,k}
in the ultrapower
\mathsf{Ult}(\mathsf{W},\mathcal{U}_{\langle s\upharpoonright\ell(\psi(k)),\psi(k)\rangle})
and call it
e_{k}(s)
.

Let now fix again some
s\in{}^{<\omega}Y
, and define

\mathcal{V}_{s}:=\{X:\langle(\imath_{2k,2\ell(s)}(s))(e_{k}(s)):k<\ell(s)\rangle\in(\imath_{0,2\ell(s)}(s))(X)\}.

By hypothesis, the critical point of
\mathcal{E}_{k}(s)
is greater than or equal to
\kappa_{0}
, hence the
\mathsf{W}
-ultrafilters
\mathcal{V}_{s}
are
\kappa_{0}
-
\mathsf{W}
-complete, thus also
\alpha
-
\mathsf{W}
-complete.

If
s_{1}\subseteq s_{2}
, then
X\in\mathcal{V}_{s_{1}}
if and only if (by definition)

\langle(\imath_{2k,2\ell(s_{1})}(s_{1}))(e_{k}(s_{1})):k<\ell(s_{1})\rangle\in(\imath_{0,2\ell(s_{1})}(s_{1}))(X)

but since
s_{1}\subseteq s_{2}
, the first
\ell(s_{1})
elements of
s_{2}
are the same of
s_{1}
, hence

\langle(\imath_{2k,2\ell(s_{1})}(s_{2}))(e_{k}(s_{2})):k<\ell(s_{1})\rangle\in(\imath_{0,2\ell(s_{1})}(s_{2}))(X)

and now, composing with the embedding
\imath_{2\ell(s_{1}),2\ell(s_{2})}
, we get

\langle(\imath_{2k,2\ell(s_{2})}(s_{2}))(e_{k}(s_{2})):k<\ell(s_{1})\rangle\in(\imath_{0,2\ell(s_{2})}(s_{2}))(X)

that is equivalent to
\{z:z\upharpoonright\ell(s_{1})\in X\}\in\mathcal{V}_{s_{2}}
. We have compatibility.

We want to use the family
\langle\mathcal{V}_{s}:s\in{}^{<\omega}Y\rangle
to witness the
\alpha
-
\mathsf{W}
-homogeneity of
{}^{\omega}Y-\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}A
. Since we already checked the
\alpha
-
\mathsf{W}
-completeness and the compatibility of the family of
\mathsf{W}
-ultrafilters, it is enough to show that
x\in\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}A
if and only if the
\mathsf{W}
-tower
\langle\mathcal{U}_{x\upharpoonright n}:n<\omega\rangle
is not
\omega_{1}
-
\mathsf{W}
-complete.

First, fix
x\not\in\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}A
. Then, for all
y\in{}^{\omega}Y
, we know
\langle x,y\rangle\not\in A
. Let us set, for
k<\omega
,
\mathsf{M}_{k}(x):=\mathsf{M}_{k}(x\upharpoonright N_{k})
,
\mathcal{E}_{k}(x):=\mathcal{E}_{k}(x\upharpoonright N_{k})
and
\varrho_{k}(x):=\varrho_{k}(x\upharpoonright N_{k})
, where
N_{k}
is large enough such that these are defined (the precise value does not matter, since eventually they are constant). In the direct limit of the
\mathsf{W}
-iteration trees
\mathcal{T}_{x\upharpoonright N_{k}}
for
N_{k}<\omega
, there is an infinite branch given by the even-indexed structures
\mathsf{M}_{2k}(x)
,
\langle\langle\mathsf{M}_{2k}(x):k<\omega\rangle,\langle\imath_{2m,2n}(x):m<n<\omega\rangle\rangle
, since there is a branch given by
0\ll 2\ll 4\ll\ldots
in the ordering
\ll
on
\omega
. The direct limit
\mathsf{M}(x)
of this branch is well-founded thanks to Lemma
4.8
. Hence, the limit of the
\mathsf{W}
-ultrapowers
\mathsf{Ult}(\mathsf{W},\langle\mathcal{V}_{x\upharpoonright k}:k<\omega\rangle)
is well-founded. By Lemma
3.26
, this is equivalent to the
\mathsf{W}
-tower being
\omega_{1}
-
\mathsf{W}
-complete, as desired.

Now, let
x\in\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}A
. Then, we have
\langle x,y\rangle\in A
for some
y\in{}^{\omega}Y
. If we do the same construction as in the previous case, we end up in a situation where the even branch is ill-founded, because
y
lets us build an infinite descending chain. This implies that the limit of the
\mathsf{W}
-ultrapowers
\mathsf{Ult}(\mathsf{W},\langle\mathcal{V}_{x\upharpoonright k}:k<\omega\rangle)
is also ill-founded, hence by Lemma
3.26
the
\mathsf{W}
-tower is not
\omega_{1}
-
\mathsf{W}
-complete.
∎

4.2
Mice with Woodin Cardinals

Our idea now is to use Theorem
4.18
as a way to bootstrap homogeneity: if we start having
\mathsf{W}
-homogeneity for all
\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{1}_{1}
sets, every Woodin cardinal in
\mathsf{W}
lets us prove
\mathsf{W}
-homogeneity for an higher level in the projective hierarchy of complexity. Though, we need a starting point, analogous to Corollary
3.29
, but having in place of
\mathsf{L}[a]
a model containing some Woodin cardinals.

Definition 4.19
.

Let
a\subseteq\lambda
and
n>0
. Consider the smallest inner model
\mathsf{W}
of
\mathrm{ZFC}
such that
a\in\mathsf{W}
and
\mathsf{W}\models``\text{there are }n\text{-many Woodin cardinals}^{\prime\prime}
, and being
(\omega_{1}+1)
-iterable. If such a model exist, we call it
\mathsf{M}_{n}(a)
. Notice that

\mathsf{M}_{n}(a)=\mathsf{L}[\mathsf{V}_{\delta_{n}(a)}],

where
\delta_{n}(a)
is the biggest Woodin cardinal of
\mathsf{M}_{n}(a)
.

This model
\mathsf{M}_{n}(a)
takes the place of
\mathsf{L}[a]
in Corollary
3.29
, but we also need to introduce something that can substitute
a^{\sharp}
. This is called the
mouse

M_{n}^{\sharp}(a)
, and we do not define it directly but instead we characterise its existence by an equivalent property. The original definition and proof of the equivalence can be found in
[
Sch10
, Section 5.1]
. Here we are not going to use any other property of
M_{n}^{\sharp}(a)
except this one, so for us it is cleaner to just introduce it this way.

Definition 4.20
.

Given
a\subseteq\lambda
, we say
M_{n}^{\sharp}(a)
exists if and only if
\mathsf{M}_{n}(a)
exists and there is a club class
\mathcal{I}^{n}_{a}
of indiscernibles for the model
\mathsf{M}_{n}(a)
.

Mansfield’s result that gave us Theorem
3.18
also generalises to this setting. The proof is similar, but we include it for sake of completeness.

Theorem 4.21
.

Let
\lambda
be a cardinal,
n>0
, and
a\subseteq\lambda
. If
M_{n}^{\sharp}(a)
exists, then for all
k<\omega
and all
Y\subseteq[\lambda^{+}]^{k}
, if
Y\in\mathsf{M}_{n}(a)
, there is some
H\in\mathcal{C}_{\lambda^{+}}
such that either
[H]^{k}\subseteq Y
or
[H]^{k}\cap Y=\varnothing
.

Proof.

Since
Y\in\mathsf{M}_{n}(a)=\mathsf{L}[\mathsf{V}_{\delta_{n}(a)}]
, there is some formula

\psi(s_{1},\ldots,s_{n},t_{1},\ldots,t_{k})

and indiscernibles
\alpha_{1}<\ldots<\alpha_{k}
in
\mathcal{I}_{x}^{a}
such that
\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}\}\in Y
if and only if

\mathsf{M}_{n}(a)\models\psi(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k},\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{h}).

Now, let
\alpha_{i}
be the largest of those indiscernibles with
\alpha_{i}<\lambda^{+}
, and let

H:=\mathcal{I}_{a}^{n}\cap(\lambda^{+}-(\alpha_{i}+1).

Since
\mathcal{I}_{a}^{n}\cap\lambda^{+}
is a club, then also
H\in\mathcal{C}_{\lambda^{+}}
. And whenever we pick two sequences
a_{1}<\ldots<a_{k}
or
b_{1}<\ldots<b_{k}
in
H
, the sequences given by

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}<\ldots<\alpha_{i}<a_{1}<\ldots<a_{k}<\alpha_{i+1}<\ldots<\alpha_{h}

and

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}<\ldots<\alpha_{i}<b_{1}<\ldots<b_{k}<\alpha_{i+1}<\ldots<\alpha_{h}

are two sequences of indiscernibles for
\mathsf{M}_{n}(a)
, so in particular

\mathsf{M}_{n}(a)\models\psi(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k},\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{h})

holds if and only if

\mathsf{M}_{n}(a)\models\psi(b_{1},\ldots,b_{k},\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{h})

so
\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}\}\in Y
if and only if
\{b_{1},\ldots,b_{k}\}\in Y
, that means that either
[H]^{k}\subseteq Y
or
[H]^{k}\cap Y=\varnothing
, the desired conclusion.
∎

If we apply Theorem
4.21
with
n=1
, we get that
\mathcal{C}_{\lambda^{+}}
is a
\mathsf{M}_{n}(a)
-ultrafilter. Additionally, we know it is
\lambda^{+}
-complete and normal, so we can conclude that:

Corollary 4.22
.

If
M_{n}^{\sharp}(a)
exists, for
a\subseteq\lambda
and
n>0
,
\lambda^{+}
is
\mathsf{M}_{n}(a)
-measurable.

Now, as a consequence of Theorem
3.28
and Corollary
4.22
, we get

Corollary 4.23
.

Assume
M_{n}^{\sharp}(a)
exists for
a\subseteq\lambda
,
n>0
. If
|Y|^{\mathsf{M}_{n}(a)}\leq\lambda
, every subset
A\subseteq{}^{\omega}Y
that is
\Pi^{1}_{1}(a)
-definable is
(\lambda^{+})^{\mathsf{V}}
-
\mathsf{M}_{n}(a)
-homogeneous.

Now, we are finally able to apply Theorem
4.18
to this setting.

Lemma 4.24
.

Assume
M_{n}^{\sharp}(a)
exists for
a\subseteq\lambda
,
n>0
. If
|Y|^{\mathsf{M}_{n}(a)}\leq\lambda
, every subset
A\subseteq{}^{\omega}Y
that is
\Pi^{1}_{n+1}(a)
-definable is
(\lambda^{+})^{\mathsf{M}_{n}(a)}
-
\mathsf{M}_{n}(a)
-homogeneous.

Proof.

Let
\delta_{1}<\cdots<\delta_{n}
denote the
n
Woodin cardinals of
\mathsf{M}_{n}(a)
. Notice that
\lambda<\delta_{1}
and
\delta_{n}<(\lambda^{+})^{\mathsf{V}}
. We proceed by induction, showing that every
A\subseteq{}^{\omega}Y
that is
\Pi^{1}_{k+1}(a)
-definable is
(\delta_{n-k}^{+})^{\mathsf{M}_{n}(a)}
-
{\mathsf{M}_{n}(a)}
-homogeneous, for
k<n
.

The base step,
k=0
, is by Corollary
4.23
. Indeed, since
\lambda<\delta_{n}<(\lambda^{+})^{\mathsf{V}}
, it follows that
(\delta_{n}^{+})^{\mathsf{M}_{n}(a)}<(\lambda^{+})^{\mathsf{V}}
. For the inductive step, apply Theorem
4.18
. Since
\delta_{i}
is a Woodin cardinal in
\mathsf{M}_{n}(a)
,
(\delta_{i}^{+})^{\mathsf{M}_{n}(a)}<\delta_{i+1}
.

At the end of the induction, we know that every
\Pi^{1}_{n}(a)
-definable
A\subseteq{}^{\omega}Y
is also
(\delta^{+}_{1})^{\mathsf{M}_{n}(a)}
-
{\mathsf{M}_{n}(a)}
-homogeneous. Applying for one last time Theorem
4.18
, exploiting the fact that
(\lambda^{+})^{\mathsf{M}_{n}(a)}<\delta_{1}
, we finally are able to obtain that if
A\subseteq{}^{\omega}Y
is
\Pi^{1}_{n+1}(a)
-definable then it is
(\lambda^{+})^{{\mathsf{M}_{n}(a)}}
-
{\mathsf{M}_{n}(a)}
-homogeneous.
∎

4.3
Consequences and Conclusions

To wrap up the article, we are going to see some consequences of the results proved so far, and give a final answer to Question
2.13
for
n\geq 5
. First, notice that our results give a “new” proof of Neeman’s optimal projective determinacy theorem from
[
Nee02
]
, using the homogeneity with respect to inner models with Woodin cardinals. This is just a corollary of Lemma
4.24
and Lemma
3.30
.

Corollary 4.25

(Neeman)
.

Fix
n>0
. If
M_{n}^{\sharp}(a)
exists for all
a\subseteq\lambda
, any game
\mathcal{G}(A,{}^{\omega}\lambda)
having a
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{1}_{n+1}
-definable payoff set is determined. Hence, in particular, if
M_{n}^{\sharp}(a)
exists for all
a\subseteq\omega
, then
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{1}_{n+1}
-Determinacy holds.

This result is optimal in the sense given by the following theorem by Woodin, first published in
[
MSW20
]
, with Sandra Müller and Ralf Schindler:

Theorem 4.26

(Woodin)
.

If
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{1}_{n+1}
-Determinacy holds,
M_{n}^{\sharp}(a)
exists for
a\subseteq\omega
.

Similarly, we can get the universally Baire property for sets of reals, putting together in a very intuitive way Lemma
4.24
and Theorem
3.32
:

Corollary 4.27
.

Let
n>0
, and assume that
M_{n}^{\sharp}(a)
exists for all
a\subseteq\lambda
. Then, every
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{1}_{n+2}
-definable subset of
{}^{\omega}\omega
is
\mathbb{P}
-universally Baire, for all
|\mathbb{P}|\leq\lambda
.

Now, we want to finally give an answer to Question
2.13
. To do so, we first need a couple lemmata about the absoluteness of mice. The proofs are analogue to the ones for sharps (Lemma
3.34
and Lemma
3.35
, respectively).

Lemma 4.28
.

Let
a
be a set of ordinals. Assume there is a forcing notion
\mathbb{P}
forcing the existence of
M_{n}^{\sharp}(a)
, for some
n>0
. Then
M_{n}^{\sharp}(a)
exists also in
\mathsf{V}
.

Lemma 4.29
.

Assume
M_{n}^{\sharp}(a)
exists for all
a\subseteq\lambda
, for a fixed
n>0
. Let
\mathbb{P}
be a forcing notion with
|\mathbb{P}|\leq\lambda
. Then
\mathbb{P}
forces that
M_{n}^{\sharp}(a)
exists for all
a\subseteq\lambda
.

Theorem 4.30
.

Let
\lambda
be a cardinal and
n>0
. Assume
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{1}_{n+1}
-Determinacy and
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{1}_{n+4}
-
\mathrm{Coll}(\omega,\lambda)
-Absoluteness. Then, if
|\mathbb{P}|\leq\lambda
,
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{1}_{n+4}
-
\mathbb{P}
-Absoluteness holds.

Proof.

First of all,
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{1}_{n+1}
-Determinacy is expressed by a
\Pi^{1}_{n+4}
-sentence, hence
\mathrm{Coll}(\omega,\lambda)
forces it true, by absoluteness. Thanks to Woodin’s theorem
4.26
,
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{1}_{n+1}
-Determinacy implies that
M_{n}^{\sharp}(a)
exists for all
a\subseteq\omega
. Now, let
a\subseteq\lambda
be a subset of
\lambda
in
\mathsf{V}
. Since
\lambda
is collapsed to be in bijection with
\omega
by
\mathrm{Coll}(\omega,\lambda)
, this implies that
\mathrm{Coll}(\omega,\lambda)
forces the existence of a real coding
a
, hence –thanks to the existence of sharps for reals in any
\mathrm{Coll}(\omega,\lambda)
-extension– the forcing notion
\mathrm{Coll}(\omega,\lambda)
forces
M_{n}^{\sharp}(a)
to exist. But now, by Lemma
4.28
,
M_{n}^{\sharp}(a)
is also in
\mathsf{V}
. Hence we proved that in
\mathsf{V}
the mouse
M_{n}^{\sharp}(a)
exists for all
a\subseteq\lambda
.

Next, let
G\subseteq\mathrm{Coll}(\omega,\lambda)
and
g\subseteq\mathbb{P}
be
\mathsf{V}
-generic, for
|\mathbb{P}|\leq\lambda
. By Lemma
2.11
, there is, in
\mathsf{V}[G]
, a
\mathsf{V}[g]
-generic filter
H\subseteq\mathrm{Coll}(\omega,\lambda)
with
\mathsf{V}[g][H]=\mathsf{V}[G]
. We prove our statement by induction, hence assume it is already proven for all
0<n^{\prime}<n
. By Theorem
3.36
and Lemma
2.4
, we just need to show downwards absoluteness from
\mathsf{V}[g]
to
\mathsf{V}
for
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{1}_{n+4}
-sentences. By Lemma
4.29
,
\mathbb{P}
forces the statement “
M_{n}^{\sharp}(a)
exists for all
a\subseteq\lambda
”. By Corollary
4.27
, this implies that, in
\mathsf{V}[g]
, every
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{1}_{n+2}
-set of reals is
\mathrm{Coll}(\omega,\lambda)
-universally Baire, hence by Lemma
2.9
we know that, in
\mathsf{V}[g]
,
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{1}_{n+3}
-
\mathrm{Coll}(\omega,\lambda)
-Absoluteness holds.

Let
\varphi
be a
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{1}_{n+4}
-sentence true in
\mathsf{V}[g]
, with parameters in
\mathsf{V}
. By Lemma
2.4
and the fact that
\mathsf{V}[g]
satisfies
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{1}_{n+3}
-
\mathrm{Coll}(\omega,\lambda)
-Absoluteness, we get that
\varphi
is true in
\mathsf{V}[g][H]=\mathsf{V}[G]
. But by our original hypothesis we know that
\mathsf{V}
satisfies
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{1}_{n+4}
-
\mathrm{Coll}(\omega,\lambda)
-Absoluteness, hence
\varphi
is true also in
\mathsf{V}
.
∎

Similarly to the remark after Theorem
3.36
, notice that
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{1}_{n+1}
-Determinacy does not imply
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{1}_{n+4}
-
\mathrm{Coll}(\omega,\lambda)
-Absoluteness for
\lambda>\omega
, so the result of Theorem
4.30
is not trivially true. Indeed, not even the full schema of projective determinacy (i.e., the schema consisting of
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{1}_{n}
-Determinacy for every
n<\omega
) is enough to imply the existence of any sharp of an uncountable set, hence it does not even imply
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{1}_{4}
-
\mathrm{Coll}(\omega,\lambda)
-Absoluteness for
\lambda>\omega
. Next, it may be interesting to ask: is it consistent with ZFC that
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{1}_{n}
-
\mathrm{Coll}(\omega,\lambda)
-Absoluteness holds but
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{1}_{n}
-
\mathbb{P}
-Absoluteness does not, for some
n<\omega
,
\lambda
a cardinal and
|\mathbb{P}|\leq\lambda
?

Abstract
. The mean exit time function defined on the δ \delta -tube around any equator 𝕊 n − 1 ⊆ 𝕊 n \mathbb{S}^{n-1}\subseteq\mathbb{S}^{n} of the sphere 𝕊 n \mathbb{S}^{n} , ( 0 < δ < π / 2 0<\delta<\pi/2 ), goes to infinity with the dimension, so that when we consider a Brownian particle that begins its motion at one equator of the sphere, this particle will remain near this equator for an almost infinite amount of time when the dimension of the sphere goes to infinity. On the other hand, if the Brownian particle begins its motion at the North pole, then this particle will leave quickly, when the dimension of the sphere goes to infinity, any geodesic ball with radius δ < π / 2 \delta<\pi/2 , centered at this point. Namely, the mean exit time function defined on the equatorial tubes presents a kind of concentration phenomenon or fat equator effect, as it has been described in the book [ 27 ] . Moreover, the same concentration phenomenon occurs when we consider this mean exit time function defined on tubes around closed and minimal hypersurfaces of a compact Riemannian n n -manifold M M with Ricci curvature bounded from below, Ric M ≥ ( n − 1 ) {\rm Ric}_{M}\geq(n-1) . Namely, a Brownian particle that begins its random movement around a closed embedded minimal hypersurface of a compact n n -manifold M M with Ric M ≥ ( n − 1 ) {\rm Ric}_{M}\geq(n-1) will wanders arbitrarily close to the hypersurface for a time that approaches infinity as the dimension of the ambient manifold does so as well.

Our first result confirms the suggestion that the mean exit time defined on the

T_{\delta}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})

When we consider a Brownian particle that begins its motion at one equator of the sphere, this particle will remain near this equator for an almost infinite amount of time when the dimension of the sphere goes to infinity, which in its turn comes from the fact that the mean exit time function
u_{\delta,n}
will be bounded from below by a one-variable function
F_{\delta,n}
which goes to infinity in each value of its dominion when
n

On the other hand, if the Brownian particle begins its motion at the North pole, then this particle will leave quickly, when the dimension of the sphere goes to infinity, any geodesic ball centered at the North pole, with radius
\delta<\pi/2
, because the mean exit time function
v_{\delta,n}
will be uniformly bounded from above by a constant depending on
\delta
and
n
which goes to zero when
n
goes to infinity.

All these considerations are formalized in the following statement.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let us consider the tube
T_{\delta}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})\subseteq\mathbb{S}^{n}
, of radius
\delta\in(0,\pi/2)
, around the totally geodesic submanifold
\varphi:\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{n}
, and the geodesic ball
B_{\pi/2-\delta}(p_{N})\subseteq\mathbb{S}^{n}
centered at the north pole
p_{N}
. Then, we have the following statements:

i)

The mean exit time function
u_{\delta,n}
defined on the tube
T_{\delta}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
, is the radial solution of (
2
), and it is bounded from below by the function
F_{\delta,n}\circ s
as

\displaystyle u_{\delta,n}(x)=u_{\delta,n}(s(x))

\displaystyle\geq(F_{\delta,n}\circ s)(x)\,\,\,\forall x\in T_{\delta}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})\,\mathpunct{\raisebox{2.15277pt}{.}}

where
s(x)=\mathrm{dist}_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}(x,\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
and
F_{\delta,n}:(0,\delta)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is the function defined as

F_{\delta,n}(r)=\frac{(\delta-r)}{\sin(\delta)}\cdot\frac{1-\cos^{n}(r)}{n\cos^{n-1}(r)}\,\mathpunct{\raisebox{2.15277pt}{.}}

which satisfies that

\lim_{n\to\infty}F_{\delta,n}(r)=\infty\,\,\,\forall r\in(0,\delta)

ii)

The mean exit time function
v_{\delta,n}
from the ball
B_{\pi/2-\delta}(p_{N})\subseteq\mathbb{S}^{n}
is the radial solution of (
3
), and it is uniformly bounded from above as

v_{\delta,n}(y)=v_{\delta,n}(t(y))\leq G_{\delta,n}:=\frac{1-\cos(\pi/2-\delta)}{n\cos(\pi/2-\delta)}\cdot

where
t(y)=\mathrm{dist}_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}(y,p_{N})
, and we have that the uniform bound
G_{\delta,n}
satisfies

\lim_{n\to\infty}G_{\delta,n}=0\,.

The mean exit time function defined in a domain
\Omega\subseteq M
is the first in a sequence of functions
\{u_{0}=1,u_{1,\Omega,n}=E_{\Omega}\;,u_{2,\Omega,n}\;,....\}
defined inductively as follows

(4)

\begin{split}\Delta^{M}u_{1,\Omega,n}+1&=0,\,\text{ on }\Omega,\\
u_{1,\Omega,n}\lvert_{{}_{\partial\Omega}}&=0,\end{split}

and, for
k\geq 2
,

(5)

\begin{split}\Delta^{M}u_{k,\Omega,n}+ku_{k-1,\Omega,n}&=0,\,\,\text{on }\,\,\Omega,\\
u_{k,\Omega,n}\lvert_{{}_{\partial\Omega}}&=0.\end{split}

This sequence is the so-called
Poisson hierarchy for

\Omega
, see
[
10
]
,
[
Mc2
]
.

As occurs with the mean exit time function
u_{1,\Omega,n}=E_{\Omega}
, (which is the second element of the Poisson hierarchy, or, in fact, its first non-trivial element), it can be proved, (see
[

]

[

\Omega\subseteq M
, the

-element of the Poisson hierarchy for
\Omega
agrees with the
k^{\rm th}
-exit moment of
\Omega
,
k\geq 1
, which is the expected value of the
k^{\rm th}
-power of variable
\tau_{\Omega}
given in (
1
), i.e.,

u_{k,\Omega,n}(x)=\mathbb{E}[(\tau_{\Omega}^{x})^{k}]

When we restrict ourselves to our setting, in which we have
\Omega=T_{\delta}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
or
\Omega=B_{\pi/2-\delta}(p_{N})
, we will adopt the following notation for the elements of the Poisson hierarchy: in case
\Omega=T_{\delta}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})

u_{k,\Omega,n}=u_{\delta,k,n}(x)
and in case
\Omega=B_{\pi/2-\delta}(p_{N})

v_{k,\Omega,n}=v_{\delta,k,n}(x)

In view of Theorem (
1.1
), it is natural to expect that the
k^{\rm th}
-exit moments,

Indeed, this is the content of the following result.

Corollary 1.1
.

Let us consider the tube
T_{\delta}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})\subseteq\mathbb{S}^{n}
, of radius
\delta\in(0,\pi/2)
, around the totally geodesic submanifold
\varphi:\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{n}
, and the geodesic ball
B_{\pi/2-\delta}(p_{N})\subseteq\mathbb{S}^{n}
centered at the north pole
p_{N}
.

i)

The
k^{\rm th}
-exit moment
u_{\delta,k,n}
defined on the tube
T_{\delta}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
is bounded from below by the a function
F_{\delta,k,n}\circ s
as

\displaystyle u_{\delta,k,n}(x)

\displaystyle=u_{\delta,k,n}(s(x))\geq(F_{\delta,k,n}\circ s)(x)\,\,\,\,\forall x\in T_{\delta}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})\,\mathpunct{\raisebox{2.15277pt}{.}}

where
s(x)=\mathrm{dist}_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}(x,\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
and
F_{\delta,k,n}:(0,\delta)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a function which satisfies that

\lim_{n\to\infty}F_{\delta,k,n}(r)=\infty\,\,\,\forall r\in(0,\delta),\,\,\,\forall k\in\mathbb{N}

ii)

The
k^{\rm th}
-exit moment
v_{\delta,k,n}
defined on the ball
B_{\pi/2-\delta}(p_{N})\subseteq\mathbb{S}^{n}
is uniformly bounded from above as

v_{\delta,k,n}(s(x))\leq k!\left(v_{\delta,1,n}(0)\right)^{k}

and we have that the unifom bound
k!\left(v_{\delta,1,n}(0)\right)^{k}
satisfies

\lim_{n\to\infty}k!\left(v_{\delta,1,n}(0)\right)^{k}=0\,.

\tilde{u}_{\delta,n}(x):=u_{\delta,n}(s(x))\,\,\forall x\in T_{\delta}(\Sigma)

In this setting, the tube radius

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
M
be a compact Riemannian

-manifold with Ricci curvature
{\rm Ric}_{M}\geq(n-1)

\varphi\colon\Sigma\to M^{n}

be the
\delta

\Sigma

\partial B_{\pi/2}(p_{N})

0<\delta<{\rm min}\{\pi/2,C(\Sigma)\}
, (see Section
2

C(\Sigma)
). The mean exit time
w_{\delta,n}(x)
of
T_{\delta}(\Sigma)
is bounded below as

(6)

\displaystyle w_{\delta,n}(x)\geq\tilde{u}_{\delta,n}(s(x))

\displaystyle\geq(F_{\delta,n}\circ s)(x)\,\,\,\,\forall x\in T_{\delta}(\Sigma)\,\mathpunct{\raisebox{2.15277pt}{.}}

where
s(x)={\rm dist}_{M}(x,\Sigma)
, and
F_{\delta,n}
is the function defined in Theorem
1.1
, which satisfies

\lim_{n\to\infty}F_{\delta,n}(r)=\infty,\,\,\,\forall r\in(0,\delta).

Moreover, if
w_{\delta,n}(x_{0})=\tilde{u}_{\delta,n}(s(x_{0}))
for some
x_{0}\in T_{\delta}(\Sigma)
, and some
n\in\mathbb{N}
then

(1)

Every tubular hypersurface
\partial T_{r}(\Sigma)
has constant mean curvature,
r\in(0,\delta)
.

(2)

The volume of
\Sigma
is bounded from above

{\rm vol}(\Sigma)\leq\frac{1}{1-2e^{-(n-1)\delta^{2}/2}}\cdot{\rm vol}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}).

Remark 1.2
.

As we have said before, Theorem
1.2
tell us that the mean exit time function
w_{\delta,n}(x)
becomes larger and larger as the dimension
n
of the manifold
M
increases.
On the other hand, and with regard to the bound on the Ricci curvature, which is established in terms of the dimension

, we must take into account, when interpreting the behavior of the curvatures of the manifold when the dimension increases, that although the Ricci curvature increases with the dimension, the sectional curvatures can remain constant in any plane (as occurs with spheres or projective spaces), or in any case, bounded in any plane, from above and from below, by finite quantities.

An analogous statement to that of Theorem
1.2
is true for the
k^{\rm th}
-exit moments defined on
T_{\delta}(\Sigma)
, just as it was stablished in Corollary
1.1
for the spherical model, as we can see in the following result. We remark here that, in order to get the comparison, we have transplanted the radial
k^{\rm th}
-exit moments defined on
T_{\delta}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
to the tube
T_{\delta}(\Sigma)
in the same way as we did in Theorem
1.2
.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
M
be a compact Riemannian
n
-manifold with Ricci curvature
{\rm Ric}_{M}\geq(n-1)
and
\varphi\colon\Sigma\to M^{n}
be a closed embedded minimal hypersurface. The

-exit moments

In particular, by Corollary (

\lim_{n\to\infty}F_{\delta,k,n}(r)=\infty,\,\,\,\forall r\in(0,\delta),\,\,\,\forall k\in\mathbb{N}

2.
Tubes around submanifolds

In this section we will recall the geometry of tubes around submanifolds, following the book
[
11
]
. Let
\varphi\colon\!\Sigma\to M
be an isometric embedding of a complete Riemannian
m
-manifold
\Sigma
into a Riemannian
n
-manifold
M
. Let us identify
\Sigma
with the zero section
s\colon\Sigma\to T\Sigma^{\perp}
of the normal bundle. The exponential map
\exp^{\perp}\colon T\Sigma^{\perp}\to M
is a diffeomorphism when restricted to an open neighborhood
\Omega_{\Sigma}
of
T\Sigma^{\perp}
containing
\Sigma
. To describe this set
\Omega_{\Sigma}
observe that for every
q\in\exp^{\perp}(\Omega_{\Sigma})
there exists a unique minimal geodesic
\gamma_{\xi}\colon[0,\ell]\to M
joining
p=\gamma_{\xi}(0)\in\Sigma
to
\gamma_{\xi}(\ell)=q
with
\gamma_{\xi}^{\prime}(0)=\xi\in\big(T_{\gamma_{\xi}(0)}\Sigma\big)^{\perp}
. The geodesic
\gamma_{\xi}
realize the distance
\ell={\rm dist}_{{}_{M}}(\Sigma,q)
between
q
and
\Sigma
.

Letting
c\colon ST\Sigma^{\perp}=\{(p,\xi)\in T\Sigma^{\perp}\colon\|\xi\|=1\}\to\mathbb{R}
defined as

c(p,\xi)=\sup_{t>0}\{t\colon{\rm dist}_{{}_{M}}(p,\gamma_{\xi}(t))=t\}

we have that

\exp^{\perp}\big(\Omega_{\Sigma}\big)=\{\exp^{\perp}(p,s\xi)=\gamma_{\xi}(s),\,\,\,(p,\xi)\in ST\Sigma^{\perp}\,,\,0\leq s\leq c(p,\xi)\}.

The minimal focal distance
C(\Sigma)
of
\Sigma
in
M
is defined as

C(\Sigma)=\inf\{c(p,\xi),(p,\xi)\in ST\Sigma^{\perp}\}.

Definition 2.1
.

Let us consider
\varphi:\Sigma^{m}\longrightarrow M^{n}
a submanifold of a Riemannian manifold
M
, and a number
r\leq C(\Sigma)
. The tube of radius
r
about
\Sigma
in
M
is the set

T_{r}(\Sigma):=\left\{x\in M\,:\,\text{dist}_{M}(x,\Sigma)\leq r\right\}.

and the tubular hypersurface of radius
r
about
\Sigma
in
M
is the set

\partial T_{r}(\Sigma):=\left\{x\in M\,:\,\text{dist}_{M}(x,\Sigma)=r\right\}.

Remark 2.2
.

If
M
is complete and
\Sigma
is closed then
C(\Sigma)>0
. Thus we have for all
0\leq r\leq C(\Sigma)
that

T_{r}(\Sigma)=\exp^{\perp}\Big(\left\{(p,\xi)\in T\Sigma^{\bot},\text{ with }\,\|\xi\|\leq r\right\}\Big)\subseteq\exp^{\perp}\left(\Omega_{\Sigma}\right).

so, as
\Sigma
is closed, the points in the tube of radius
r
are those whose distance to the submanifold is less than or equal to
r
, that is, those that can be reached by a minimizing geodesic that starts from the submanifold orthogonally.

On the other hand, given
(p,\xi)\in ST\Sigma^{\bot}
, and given
r\in(0,C(\Sigma))
, let
\gamma_{\xi}
be the unique geodesic, unit speed and perpendicular to
\Sigma
from
p\in\Sigma
to
\gamma_{\xi}(r)
which realizes the distance

. Then, the tangent space in
\gamma_{\xi}(r)
to the tubular hypersurface
\partial T_{r}(\Sigma)
is given by

T_{\gamma_{\xi}(r)}\partial T_{r}(\Sigma)=\{\gamma_{\xi}^{\prime}(r)\}^{\bot}

where
\gamma_{\xi}^{\prime}(r)=\nabla\,^{M}{\rm dist_{M}}(\cdot,\Sigma)

Given
(p,\xi)\in ST\Sigma^{\perp}
, let

S_{\xi}(t)\colon\{\gamma_{\xi}^{\prime}(r)\}^{\perp}\to\{\gamma_{\xi}^{\prime}(t)\}^{\perp}\,\,\,{\rm and}\,\,\,\,R_{\xi}(r)\colon\{\gamma_{\xi}^{\prime}(r)\}^{\perp}\to\{\gamma_{\xi}^{\prime}(r)\}^{\perp}

be respectively the Shape and the Curvature operators of the hypersurface

at

as defined in
[
11
]

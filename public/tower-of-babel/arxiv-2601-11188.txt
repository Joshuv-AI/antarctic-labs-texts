From three-body resonances to bound states in a continuum: pole trajectories
Lucas Happ

Abstract
We investigate the formation of three-body bound states in the continuum by tracing pole trajectories in the complex energy plane under variation of system parameters. Using a one-dimensional model of two identical bosons and a distinguishable particle interacting via Gaussian potentials, we systematically vary the interaction strength, interaction range, and mass ratio. Our results confirm the parametric nature of few-body bound states in a continuum (BIC) and extend this characterization to a broader set of system parameters. Specifically, we find that variations of both interaction parameters and the mass ratio can lead to the formation of at least one three-body BIC. However, the exact shape of trajectories differs, and for the mass ratio variation we find a more regular pattern with multiple BIC locations. These results suggest that the mechanism of few-body BIC formation is more sensitive to the kinematic structure of the problem than to the specific details of the two-body interaction.

This approach allows us to systematically compare the influence of different parameters on the resonance properties and to trace the continuous transformation from resonances to bound states in the continuum.

2
Model and Method

In this work we consider a three-body system of two identical bosons of masses
m_{B}
, and a third, distinguishable particle of mass
m_{X}
, all confined to one spatial dimension. The parameter
\beta=m_{B}/m_{X}
denotes the mass ratio. We assume the interaction between different particles to be of Gaussian shape

V(r)=v_{0}\exp\left[-\mu_{g}(r/r_{0})^{2}\right]

(1)

and no interaction between the two identical bosons. This reduces the number of system parameters to a minimum and allows to study the remaining ones (
v_{0},\mu_{g},\beta
) in a more isolated manner.

We work in dimensionless units throughout this paper, where masses are given in units of
m_{X}
, distances in units of a characteristic length scale
r_{0}
, and energies in units of
\hbar^{2}/(m_{X}r_{0}^{2})
. Transforming to the center-of-mass frame of the three-body system and separating the relative dynamics from the center-of-mass motion, the three-body system is governed by the Schrödinger equation

\left[-\frac{1}{2\mu_{12}}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial r_{3}^{2}}-\frac{1}{2\mu_{12,3}}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial R_{3}^{2}}+V\left(r_{2}\right)+V\left(r_{3}\right)-E^{(3)}\right]\psi(r_{3},R_{3})=0.

(2)

We use the index
1
for the different particle, and
2,3
for the two identical Bosons. Then,
r_{3}
(
r_{2}
) denotes the distance between the pair of particles
\{1,2\}
(
\{3,1\}
), and
R_{3}
the distance between particle
3
relative to the center-of-mass of the two-particle subsystem of particles
\{1,2\}
. The corresponding reduced masses are defined as

\mu_{12}=\frac{m_{1}m_{2}}{m_{1}+m_{2}}=\frac{\beta}{\beta+1},\qquad\mu_{12,3}=\frac{m_{3}(m_{1}+m_{2})}{m_{1}+m_{2}+m_{3}}=\frac{\beta(1+\beta)}{1+2\beta}.

(3)

For the parameter values considered in this work, the two-body subsystem consisting of the distinguishable particle and one boson supports two bound states: a deeply-bound “deep dimer” state with binding energy
E_{0}^{(2)}
, and an excited dimer state with binding energy
E_{1}^{(2)}
. The three-body resonances of interest lie energetically between these two dimer states and can decay into the
2+1
continuum of the deep dimer plus a free boson.

Since in this work we are interested in three-body resonances, we employ the complex scaling method (CSM)
28
. This method transforms resonances, which appear as poles in the complex energy plane
36
, into discrete eigenvalues that can be computed using standard bound-state techniques. We then solve the three-body Schrödinger equation, Eq. (
2
), with complex scaling via the Gaussian expansion method (GEM)
20
. This method relies on expanding the total three-body state into a superposition of centered Gaussian functions with different widths for each of the Jacobi sets
k=1,2,3
(for our system we only need
k=2
and
k=3
) and for each of the relative coordinates
r_{k}
,
R_{k}
in each set. This coherent superposition of Gaussians allows to represent also the more general class of non-Gaussian states
13
;
40
. We use the open source julia package FewBodyToolkit.jl
19
which provides a general implementation of the GEM for few-body systems, with options to enable the CSM. With this, we obtain the complex scaled spectrum from which we can extract the complex energies of the three-body resonances.

3
Pole Trajectories

Pole trajectories trace a resonance location in the complex energy plane for various values of the system parameters. In this work, we focus on three parameters: (i) the interaction strength
v_{0}
, (ii) the interaction range parameter
\mu_{g}
, and (iii) the mass ratio
\beta

Both parameter variations exhibit similar behavior: the trajectories start near the origin, which marks the threshold of binding (right side), then shortly move into the complex plane, before re-approaching and touching the real axis once again. The range of parameters is limited in our model: for more attractive interactions (more negative

Conversely, for weaker interactions (“moving to the right”), the three-body resonance eventually reaches the threshold of binding and becomes unbound. In both cases, these points mark the limits beyond which we cannot trace the poles within our analysis.

At the location where the pole touches the real axis, the width of the three-body resonance is decreased by several orders of magnitude (theoretically down to zero), and we can identify it as a bound state in a continuum. Notably, for both interaction parameters the pole approaches the real axis at almost the same real energy. This similarity suggests that the formation of the BIC is a robust feature.

5
Conclusion and Outlook

In summary, we have traced the formation of three-body bound states in the continuum (BICs) from resonances by following pole trajectories in the complex energy plane as system parameters are varied. Using a simplified one-dimensional model of two identical bosons and a distinguishable particle, we confirmed the parametric nature of such BICs, which was initially reported in our previous work
16
, and extended it to a broader selection of system parameters.

Our present investigation reveals both similarities and differences between the effects of interaction parameters and kinematic parameters on the pole trajectories. Variations of the interaction strength
v_{0}
and range parameter
\mu_{g}
produced only a single BIC in the available parameter interval with an overall rather irregular pattern. In contrast, variation of the mass ratio
\beta
yields a pole trajectory with faster oscillations and therefore multiple BIC locations. This distinction suggests that the mechanism of few-body resonance stabilization to BICs is affected differently by kinematic and interaction parameters. Nevertheless, all parameter variations led to the formation of at least one BIC, demonstrating the robustness of its general existence. An interesting question for future work is whether the difference between the parameters is specific to the Gaussian interaction potential used in the present study, or whether it represents a more universal feature of few-body BICs.

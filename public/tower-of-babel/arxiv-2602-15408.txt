Constant curvature rotational nets and periodic Bäcklund transforms
T. Raujouan, W. Rossman, N. Suda

Abstract
. After giving explicit parametrizations of discrete constant negative Gaussian curvature surfaces (negative CGC, i.e. discrete pseudospherical surfaces) of revolution, we construct Bäcklund transformations that again will have explicit parametrizations and are new examples of non-rotational discrete pseudospherical surfaces. In the process of doing this, for discrete CGC circular nets, we can provide rotationally invariant families of flat connections and give conditions on them so that the Bäcklund transformations preserve periodicity, that is, have annular topology.



Introduction

Bobenko and Pinkall
[
8
]
investigated Lax pairs
corresponding to the discrete constant
negative Gaussian curvature surfaces introduced by Wunderlich
[
40
]
and
Sauer
[
31
]
, establishing a framework based on the
discretization of asymptotic coordinates.
Furthermore, Bobenko and Pinkall
[
9
]
, as well as Pedit and Wu
[
27
]
, investigated
Lax pairs for discrete
constant positive Gaussian curvature surfaces and their parallel surfaces,
which includes discrete constant mean curvature surfaces.
In addition, Bobenko and Pinkall
[
7
]
also addressed isothermic nets as a broader class which contains discrete constant mean curvature surfaces,
where the isothermic nets — discretizations of conformal curvature line coordinates — are defined in terms of cross-ratios.
Bobenko, Pottmann, and Wallner
[
10
]
demonstrated that
for circular nets, which constitute a discretization of curvature line coordinates,
the principal curvatures, Gaussian curvature, and mean curvature can be defined via discrete analog of the Steiner formula.
Subsequently, Hoffmann and Sageman-Furnas
[
24
]
constructed a Lax pair for circular nets of constant negative Gaussian curvature (hereafter abbreviated as cK-nets),
building on the Lax pair introduced in
[
8
]
.
Furthermore, they computed the Gaussian curvature of the associated family arising from the Lax pair with the spectral parameter
\lambda=e^{t}
,
employing edge-constraint nets and their curvature as introduced by Hoffmann, Sageman-Furnas, and Wardetzky
[
23
]
.
Hoffmann and Sageman-Furnas
[
24
]
elucidated the relationship between asymptotic coordinates and curvature line coordinates for discrete surfaces, and further revisited the Bäcklund transformation for cK-nets introduced by Schief
[
32
]

as gauge transformations of Lax pairs, and the Bäcklund transformation of
[
32
]
is interpreted in
[
24
]
as a double Bäcklund transformation.
Moreover, explicit parametrizations of the Kuen surface and the breather surface as circular nets are presented in
[
24
]
.

On the other hand, the third author
[
35
]
provided parametrizations of rotationally symmetric circular nets (hereafter abbreviated as rc-nets) with constant Gaussian curvature
derived from the definition of the Gaussian curvature, using the Steiner formula.
In the case of positive constant Gaussian curvature, this is highly related to work of Bobenko and Hoffmann, because such surfaces are parallel to discrete constant mean curvature
surfaces of revolution as studied in
[
6
]
, where they employed a unified approach which includes the notion of s-isothermic found in
[
9
,
5
]
.
These parametrizations of constant Gaussian curvature rc-nets have made the following questions more accessible:

•

For smooth surfaces, it is well known that obtaining a surface of revolution
with constant Gaussian curvature leads to a second-order linear differential equation
whose solutions are written by trigonometric, hyperbolic or exponential functions.
Does an analogous difference equation arise in the case of circular nets?

•

How is the rotational symmetry reflected in the Lax pair associated with rc-nets of constant Gaussian curvature?

Periodicity has been well studied in this context.
Periodic breather surfaces can be obtained from
the straight line by applying a double Bäcklund transformation
[
2
,
29
]
.
In the case of constant mean curvature surfaces, periodic examples such
as bubbletons and Delaunay bubbletons have been extensively studied
through Darboux transformations
[
21
,
3
,
4
,
34
,
19
]
.
In addition, doubly periodic tori with constant curvature have also been
investigated from the viewpoint of integrable systems
[
33
,
38
,
39
,
1
,
28
,
12
,
13
,
18
,
26
,
37
]
.
Thus, whether or not a surface exhibits periodicity constitutes an
important theme in the study of surfaces with integrable systems.
This leads us to the following query:

•

For rc-nets with constant negative Gaussian curvature, under what conditions do the cK-nets
obtained via the Bäcklund transformation defined in
[
24
]
preserve periodicity, and thus remain annular?

This paper concentrates mainly on the above three questions.

More specifically, in Section 1 we reconsider the
Lax pair for circular nets of constant Gaussian curvature,
interpreting it as a flat connection within the framework of
discrete gauge theory
[
17
,
14
,
15
,
20
]
. In Section 2, we first demonstrate that,
for rc-nets with constant Gaussian curvature, trigonometric, hyperbolic, and exponential
functions arise as solutions to the associated difference equations obtained from the definition of curvature.
As one consequence, we clarify how the singular vertices defined by Yasumoto and the second author
[
30
]
appear in circular nets of constant
Gaussian curvature while employing Jacobi elliptic functions. In Section 3,
for rc-nets, we present several results on flat connections that are invariant in one of the two lattice directions, which leads to their explicit description.
In Section 4, after
showing that the transformation of a certain related unitary function
can be expressed in terms of a rational difference equation, we establish results on the periodicity
of rc-nets with constant negative Gaussian curvature and their single and double Bäcklund transformations.
Finally, we also discuss a method of linearization for the rational difference equations associated with
rc-nets.

1  Surfaces from integrable systems

1.1  Quaternionic model of
\mathbb{R}^{3}

We denote by
\mathbb{H}
the field of quaternions, which we realize as the real 4-dimensional vector space generated by the matrices
(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0},-i\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1},-i\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{2},-i\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{3})
where

\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix},\quad\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-i\\
i&0\end{pmatrix},\quad\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{3}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix}.

(1.1)

We identify the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{3}
with
\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\mathbb{H}
endowed with the norm
\lVert x\rVert^{2}=\det x
and the orientation that agrees with
x\times y=\frac{1}{2}[x,y]
. Note that for all
x,y\in\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\mathbb{H}
,

x\cdot y=\frac{-\mathrm{tr}(xy)}{2},\quad x\times y=(xy)^{\mathrm{tr}=0}.

(1.2)

where
(x)^{\mathrm{tr}=0}:=x-\frac{\mathrm{tr}x}{2}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}
for
x\in\mathbb{H}
is the projection onto
\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\mathbb{H}
.

Definition 1.1

\eta_{\mathfrak{ip}}=\Phi(\mathfrak{i})\Phi(\mathfrak{p})^{-1}\quad\forall\mathfrak{ip}\in E.

\sigma_{\mathfrak{i}}=\eta_{\mathfrak{ip}}\sigma_{\mathfrak{p}}

A

A discrete connection

\eta_{\mathfrak{rq}}\eta_{\mathfrak{qp}}=\eta_{\mathfrak{rs}}\eta_{\mathfrak{sp}}

(1.4)

on any face

It is immediately seen that the following statements are equivalent:

(1)

\eta

(2)

\eta

Definition 1.2
.

The collection of frames

.
It acts on sections

(1.5)

for all edges
\mathfrak{ip}\in E
.

Note that
G\sigma

\eta

Also, if

Conversely if
\eta

1.3  Circular nets and their flat connections

1.3.1 Sym-type formulas

For
\mathbb{S}^{2}
the unit sphere centered at the origin in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, we call a pair of maps
(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{n}):V\to\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}
a
contact element net
.
Let
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{\times}
and
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
. For any
t
-differentiable family
\Phi=(\Phi_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
of frames on
\underline{\mathbb{H}}
, consider the
Sym-type
formulas (which we will abbreviate to “Sym formula”)

\mathbf{x}:=\xi\left[\Phi^{-1}\partial_{t}\Phi\right]^{\mathrm{tr}=0}+\tau\mathbf{n},\quad\mathbf{n}:=\Phi^{-1}(-i\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{3})\Phi.

(1.8)

Then for each
t
,
(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{n})
is a contact element net.

1.3.2 Ridid motions

For contact element nets,
a transformation
(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{n})\mapsto(\tilde{\mathbf{x}},\tilde{\mathbf{n}})
defined by

\tilde{\mathbf{x}}=R^{-1}\mathbf{x}R+T,\;\;\tilde{\mathbf{n}}=R^{-1}\mathbf{n}R

for constants
R,T\in\mathbb{H}
is a
rigid motion
.
Let
\Phi\colon V\to\mathbb{H}^{\times}
be a frame with connection
\eta
and let
H(t)\in\mathbb{H}^{\times}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
(
H
is constant with respect to
V
).
Then
\tilde{\Phi}:=\Phi H
is another frame with the same associated connection
\eta
. Let
(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{n})
be the contact element net induced by
\Phi
via the Sym formula (
1.8
).
Then the new frame
\tilde{\Phi}
also induces a contact element net
(\tilde{\mathbf{x}},\tilde{\mathbf{n}})
given by

\tilde{\mathbf{x}}=H^{-1}\mathbf{x}H+\xi\left[H^{-1}\partial_{t}H\right]^{\mathrm{tr}=0},\;\;\tilde{\mathbf{n}}=H^{-1}\mathbf{n}H.

(1.9)

Therefore, changing the initial condition chosen for the parallel frame
\Phi
induces
a rigid motion for
(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{n})
.

1.3.3 Gauging

Definition 1.4
.

A gauge
G
is
admissible
if there exists a family
x_{t}\colon V\to\mathbb{R}
and a section
y\colon V\to\mathbb{R}
such that

G=e^{x_{t}}\begin{pmatrix}e^{iy}&0\\
0&e^{-iy}\end{pmatrix}=\exp(x_{t}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{0}+iy\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{3}).

(1.10)

For an admissible gauge
G
, we compute the gauged net:

\hat{\mathbf{x}}=\mathbf{x}+\xi\Phi_{t}^{-1}\left[G^{-1}\partial_{t}G\right]^{\mathrm{tr}=0}\Phi_{t}=\mathbf{x},

\hat{\mathbf{n}}=\Phi^{-1}G^{-1}(-i\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{3})G\Phi=\mathbf{n},

and we have the following lemma.

Lemma 1.5

(Gauge invariance)
.

Let
\Phi_{t}
be a family of frames and let
(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{n})
be the contact element
net given by the Sym formula. Let
G
be an admissible gauge, let
\hat{\Phi}_{t}:=G\Phi_{t}
, and let
(\hat{\mathbf{x}},\hat{\mathbf{n}})
be the net induced by
\hat{\Phi}_{t}
.
Then
(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{n})=(\hat{\mathbf{x}},\hat{\mathbf{n}})
.

1.3.4 Edge-constraint nets and circular nets

We give notations for the difference and summation operators acting on
functions of two discrete variables. For a function
F:\mathbb{Z}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
or
\mathbb{C}^{n}
,

\delta_{j}F(j,k):=F(j+1,k)-F(j,k),\;\;\delta_{k}F(j,k):=F(j,k+1)-F(j,k),

\sigma_{j}F(j,k):=F(j+1,k)+F(j,k),\;\;\sigma_{k}F(j,k):=F(j,k+1)+F(j,k).

In the case of a function of one discrete variable, we can abbreviate these to
\delta
and
\sigma
.

We will define edge-constraint nets and their curvatures as in
[

23
]
.

Definition 1.6
.

A contact element net
(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{n})
is an
edge-constraint net
(abbr.

)
if, for
\star=j,k
,

\left\langle\delta_{\star}\mathbf{x}(j,k),\sigma_{\star}\mathbf{n}(j,k)\right\rangle=0,

(1.11a)

and

\delta_{\star}\mathbf{x}(j,k)\neq 0,\;\;\sigma_{\star}\mathbf{n}(j,k)\neq 0

(1.11b)

for all edges
(j+1,k)(j,k)
and
(j,k+1)(j,k)
, and

\det(\mathbf{x}(j+1,k+1)-\mathbf{x}(j,k),\mathbf{x}(j+1,k)-\mathbf{x}(j,k+1),N)\neq 0

(1.11c)

for all faces of
\mathbf{x}
, where
N
is a unit normal vector such that

N\perp\textnormal{span}\{\mathbf{n}(j+1,k+1)-\mathbf{n}(j,k),\mathbf{n}(j+1,k)-\mathbf{n}(j,k+1)\}.

Definition 1.7
.

For every face of an ec-net
(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{n})
, the
Gaussian curvature

K
is
defined as

K:=\frac{\det(\mathbf{n}(j+1,k+1)-\mathbf{n}(j,k),\mathbf{n}(j+1,k)-\mathbf{n}(j,k+1),N)}{\det(\mathbf{x}(j+1,k+1)-\mathbf{x}(j,k),\mathbf{x}(j+1,k)-\mathbf{x}(j,k+1),N)}

(1.12)

and
mean curvature

H
is defined as

H:=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\det(\mathbf{x}(j+1,k+1)-\mathbf{x}(j,k),\mathbf{n}(j+1,k)-\mathbf{n}(j,k+1),N)}{\det(\mathbf{x}(j+1,k+1)-\mathbf{x}(j,k),\mathbf{x}(j+1,k)-\mathbf{x}(j,k+1),N)}\\
+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\det(\mathbf{n}(j+1,k+1)-\mathbf{n}(j,k),\mathbf{x}(j+1,k)-\mathbf{x}(j,k+1),N)}{\det(\mathbf{x}(j+1,k+1)-\mathbf{x}(j,k),\mathbf{x}(j+1,k)-\mathbf{x}(j,k+1),N)},

(1.13)

where
N
is a unit normal vector as in Definition
1.6
.

We will abbreviate the cases of constant Gaussian curvature

and constant mean curvature

1.3.5 CGC and CMC circular nets

We will now compare choices for flat connections for constant Gaussian curvature circular nets (both with
K>0
and
K<0
), which involves looking
at functions defined on vertices or edges, and at spectral parameters and compatibility conditions.
In the case of constant negative Gaussian curvature, we will have two distinct choices, each with its own advantages, as we will see later.

Positive CGC circular nets and CMC isothermic nets
:

In
[
9
,
27
]
, positive CGC circular nets with
K=1
are made with
\xi=-2
and
\tau=0

and their corresponding CMC isothermic nets are made with
\xi=-2
and
\tau=\pm 1
in (
1.8
).
For two different edges

,
e_{2}\in E_{2}^{\pm}
,
we will use a connection that takes the form

\eta_{e_{1}}=\frac{1}{\alpha}\begin{pmatrix}\mathfrak{v}&-e^{it}u-e^{-it}u^{-1}\\
{e^{-it}}{u}+e^{it}{u}^{-1}&\bar{\mathfrak{v}}\end{pmatrix},\;\;\mathfrak{v}\colon E_{1}^{\pm}\to\mathbb{C},\;\;u\colon E_{1}^{\pm}\to\mathbb{R}^{\times},

(1.15a)

\eta_{e_{2}}=\frac{1}{\beta}\begin{pmatrix}\mathfrak{h}&-ie^{it}v+ie^{-it}v^{-1}\\

(1.15b)

where

\alpha^{2}=|\mathfrak{v}|^{2}+u^{2}+u^{-2}+e^{2it}+e^{-2it},\;\;\beta^{2}=|\mathfrak{h}|^{2}+v^{2}+v^{-2}-e^{2it}-e^{-2it}

Note that the equations for

With regard to the orientation, we have

\alpha_{\mathfrak{pq}}=-\alpha_{\mathfrak{qp}},\;\;u_{\mathfrak{pq}}=u_{\mathfrak{qp}},\;\;\mathfrak{v}_{\mathfrak{pq}}=-\overline{\mathfrak{v}_{\mathfrak{qp}}}

(1.17)

for all edges
\mathfrak{qp}\in E_{1}^{+}
,
\mathfrak{pq}\in E_{1}^{-}
, and

\beta_{\mathfrak{ps}}=-\beta_{\mathfrak{sp}},\;\;v_{\mathfrak{ps}}=v_{\mathfrak{sp}},\;\;\mathfrak{h}_{\mathfrak{ps}}=-\overline{\mathfrak{h}_{\mathfrak{sp}}}

(1.18)

for all edges
\mathfrak{sp}\in E_{2}^{+}
,
\mathfrak{ps}\in E_{2}^{-}
.
We note that the cross ratio for each face of
\mathbf{x}
for a CMC isothermic net at

(see
[

2  Circular nets of revolution with constant Gaussian curvature

In
[
35
]
, a way of finding explicit parametrizations of rotational
symmetric circular nets with constant Gaussian curvature was
introduced.
In this section, we will review those parametrizations,
which use discrete trigonometric functions and
discrete hyperbolic functions satisfying particular difference equations.
Then, invoking Jacobi elliptic functions, we discuss the singularities of
those nets. This section lays groundwork for investigating the corresponding flat connections in Section 3.

2.1  Circular nets of revolution

Definition 2.1
.

A map
\mathbf{x}:V\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
is called a

if
the motion
\mathbf{x}(j,k)\mapsto\mathbf{x}(j,k+1)
(or
\mathbf{x}(j,k)\mapsto\mathbf{x}(j+1,k)

L

\theta\in(-\pi,0)\cup(0,\pi)
for all
(j,k)
.
Furthermore, a contact element net
(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{n})
is called a
contact element net of revolution (r-net)
if
additionally
\mathbf{n}(j,k)\mapsto\mathbf{n}(j,k+1)
(or
\mathbf{n}(j,k)\mapsto\mathbf{n}(j+1,k)
, respectively)
represents the rotation of the same angle
\theta
about an axis
through the origin parallel to
L
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Assume that
\mathbf{x}
is a cc-net and a net of revolution in the
k
direction.
Then
\mathbf{x}
is locally embedded
if and only if the image of the map

\mathbf{x}(\cdot,k):j\mapsto\mathbf{x}(j,k)

is a planar curve whose edges neither vanish nor intersect the rotational axis.

Proof.

Recalling Definition
1.8
of local embeddedness in Equation (
1.14
), all of its edges must be non-zero.
Up to rigid motion, we may assume that the axis of rotation is the
-i\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{3}
-axis.
Without loss of generality, for one arbitrary quadrilateral we may assume

\mathbf{x}(j,k)=(f,0,0),\;\;\mathbf{x}(j,k+1)=(f\cos\theta,f\sin\theta,0),

\mathbf{x}(j+1,k+1)=(f_{1}\cos(\theta+\tau),f_{1}\sin(\theta+\tau),h),\;\;\mathbf{x}(j+1,k)=(f_{1}\cos\tau,f_{1}\sin\tau,h)

for some
f,f_{1},h,\theta,\tau\in\mathbb{R}
.
Then

\text{cr}(\mathbf{x}(j,k),\mathbf{x}(j+1,k),\mathbf{x}(j+1,k+1),\mathbf{x}(j,k+1))<0

is equivalent to
f\neq 0
and
f_{1}\neq 0
, and

h\sin\tau=(h^{2}+f_{1}^{2}-f^{2})\sin\tau=0,\;\;ff_{1}((f^{2}+h^{2}+f_{1}^{2})\cos\tau-2ff_{1})>0.

These relations cannot hold if
\sin\tau\neq 0
,
because then

and with

we have a contradiction.
Therefore we may assume

and the relations simplify to

\cos\tau=\pm 1,\;\;\text{sign}(f_{1})=\text{sign}(f\cos\tau),\;\;\text{and}\;\;f_{1}\neq f\cos\tau\;\;\text{or}\;\;h\neq 0.

Therefore,
\mathbf{x}(j,k)
and
\mathbf{x}(j+1,k)
lie on the plane
\text{span}\{-i\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1},-i\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{3}\}
and the edge
connecting them is nonvanishing and does not intersect the
-i\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{3}
-axis.
Continuing this argument inductively to other values of

,
we find that
\mathbf{x}(\cdot,k)
satisfies the conclusion of the lemma.

A segregating PTK2B variant in a primary biliary cholangitis (PBC) family induces PBC-like autoimmune features in knock-in mice
Authors: Sainan Bian, Yanlei Yang, Zhilei Chen, Li Wang, Hua Chen, Suying Liu, Chengmei He, Yongzhe Li, Xue Zhang, Fengchun Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPrimary biliary cholangitis (PBC) is a chronic autoimmune cholestatic liver disease with an incompletely understood genetic basis. This study aimed to identify disease-causing genetic variants in a PBC family and to evaluate their functional relevance in vivo.ResultsA total of 27 family members were enrolled in the analysis, among whom three were affected. After filtering of the whole-exome sequencing results of the PBC family, 17 candidate variants were identified, among which a protein tyrosine kinase 2β (PTK2B) c.1679C > G variant exclusively co-segregated with PBC in affected family members and was absent in unaffected relatives, healthy controls, and public databases. Homozygous knock-in mice exhibited significantly elevated serum alkaline phosphatase (236 ± 55 vs. 142 ± 43 U/L, P = 0.01) and antimitochondrial antibody levels (3924 ± 769 vs. 1972 ± 632 U/mL, P = 0.001) compared with heterozygous mice, along with portal lymphocytic infiltration, intrahepatic bile duct proliferation, and liver fibrosis. Female homozygous knock-in mice showed increased hepatic infiltration of CD3+ T cells (50.8% ± 1.7% vs. 38.9% ± 3.2%, P = 0.005), CD8+ T cells (14.3% ± 2.8% vs. 7.5% ± 1.3%, P = 0.005), CD19+ B cells (44.7% ± 1.7% vs. 32.6% ± 2.2%, P = 0.002), and CCR2+ cells (31.7% ± 6.2% vs. 20.4% ± 2.2%, P = 0.03), accompanied by reduced CCR2+ cells in peripheral blood (4.8% ± 6.5% vs. 33.8% ± 12.8%, P = 0.001) and elevated serum MCP-1 levels (2209 ± 412 vs. 1187 ± 89 pg/mL, P < 0.0001) than wildtype mice.ConclusionsThis study identifies a segregating PTK2B variant in a family with PBC and demonstrates that the corresponding knock-in mutation induces PBC-like autoimmune features in mice. These findings provide human genetic and in vivo evidence supporting PTK2B as a candidate susceptibility gene for PBC.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13578-026-01589-w.

Introduction

Primary biliary cholangitis (PBC), previously termed primary biliary cirrhosis, is a chronic autoimmune disease that predominantly affects middle-aged women [1]. It is characterized histologically by chronic nonsuppurative destruction of the intrahepatic small bile ducts and clinically by cholestasis, and progressive liver dysfunction. Serologically, PBC is associated with elevated alkaline phosphatase (ALP) levels, γ-glutamyl transpeptidase (GGT) levels, and the presence of antimitochondrial antibodies (AMA) [2].

Accumulating evidence supports a substantial genetic contribution to PBC susceptibility. Familial aggregation, increased risk among first-degree relatives, and high concordance rates in monozygotic twins indicate a heritable component underlying disease development [3–5]. Genome-wide association studies (GWAS) have identified multiple risk loci involved in PBC [6–9]; however, these variants generally confer modest effect sizes and do not fully explain disease heritability.

In contrast, rare germline variants with larger functional effects may play a critical role in familial PBC, yet such variants remain poorly characterized. Whole-exome sequencing provides an effective approach for identifying rare, potentially pathogenic variants in multiplex families [10], but its application in familial PBC has been limited, and functional validation of candidate variants in vivo is scarce.

Therefore, in this study, we performed whole-exome sequencing in a Chinese Han family with PBC to identify a segregating susceptibility variant and assessed its pathogenic relevance using a knock-in mouse model. By integrating human genetic evidence with in vivo phenotypic characterization, we aimed to establish a causal link between the identified variant and PBC-related autoimmune features.

Material and methods

Participants

A Chinese Han family with PBC was investigated. Peripheral blood samples and genomic DNA were collected from 27 family members spanning three generations. Family members were clinically evaluated based on liver biochemical abnormalities, including elevated ALP and GGT levels, as well as the presence of AMA.

Secondary causes of liver dysfunction, such as viral hepatitis, and other concomitant autoimmune diseases were excluded. Three individuals in the first generation (I.2, I.3, and I.4) met the diagnostic criteria for PBC.

Exome sequencing and Sanger sequencing

Peripheral blood samples were collected from family members into potassium ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA) tubes. Genomic DNA was extracted using the QIAamp DNA Blood Kit (Qiagen, Hilden, Germany) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. DNA concentration and purity were assessed using a NanoDrop ND-2000 spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA), and DNA integrity was evaluated by agarose gel electrophoresis.

Whole-exome sequencing was performed on genomic DNA from three affected individuals (I.2, I.3, and I.4) and one unaffected sibling (I.6). Targeted exome enrichment and sequencing were conducted at Chigene Translational Medical Research Center Co., Ltd. (Beijing, China; formerly JOY ORIENT) using the Illumina HiSeq 2500 platform. Sequencing reads were aligned to the human reference genome (GRCh37/hg19).

Variant calling was performed using SAMtools (version 1.0), followed by annotation with ANNOVAR. Variants shared by affected individuals (I.2, I.3, and I.4) but absent in the unaffected sibling (I.6) were initially selected. Further filtering was applied using the following criteria: (1) previously reported single-nucleotide variants were excluded; (2) variants with a minor allele frequency greater than 0.1% in the Exome Aggregation Consortium (ExAC) database or in population databases from the 1000 Genomes Project (Chinese populations, including Southern and Northern Han Chinese) were excluded; and (3) synonymous and noncoding variants were excluded, retaining nonsense, missense, splice-site, and frameshift variants.

The pathogenic potential of retained variants was evaluated using SIFT and PolyPhen-2. Potential effects on RNA splicing were assessed using Human Splicing Finder version 3.1 and NetGene2. All candidate variants identified by exome sequencing were validated by Sanger sequencing in additional family members and in 48 unrelated healthy Chinese controls, and were further cross-referenced with the National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI) database. This stringent filtering strategy was designed to prioritize rare, potentially pathogenic variants with high penetrance in a multiplex family.

Sanger sequencing was performed by direct sequencing of purified PCR products using an ABI 3730XL DNA Analyzer (Thermo Fisher Scientific). PCR primers used for sequencing were identical to those used for amplification (Table S1). Sequence alignment and variant analysis were conducted using CodonCode Aligner software (CodonCode Aligner Corporation, Centerville, MA, USA).

Construction of a knock-in mouse model carrying a Ptk2b variant

The knock-in mouse model was generated at the Nanjing Biomedical Research Institute of Nanjing University (Nanjing, China). A CRISPR/Cas9-mediated genome-editing strategy was used to introduce the corresponding protein tyrosine kinase 2β (Ptk2b) variant into the mouse genome. Guide RNAs (gRNAs) targeting the Ptk2b locus and donor templates carrying the desired variant were designed and synthesized.

Cas9 mRNA, gRNAs, and donor templates were microinjected into fertilized C57BL/6 J mouse zygotes to generate founder (F0) mice. Genomic DNA extracted from tail biopsies was screened by polymerase chain reaction (PCR) and confirmed by Sanger sequencing to identify mice carrying the targeted mutation. Positive F0 founders were backcrossed with C57BL/6 J mice to generate F1 offspring. Male and female F1 mice were then intercrossed to produce F2 progeny containing homozygous mutant, heterozygous mutant, and wild-type mice for subsequent experiments. Homozygous knock-in mice carrying the Ptk2b p.P560R variant are referred to as KI/KI; heterozygous knock-in mice as KI/WT; wild-type mice as WT/WT. The strategy for genome editing and genotyping is shown in Fig. S1, and primer sequences are provided in Table S2.

Mouse analyses

Serum biochemistry and autoantibody detection

Serum ALP levels were measured using a Beckman AU series fully automated biochemical analyzer (Beckman Coulter, Brea, CA, USA) in the clinical laboratory of Peking Union Medical College Hospital (Beijing, China). Mouse AMAs were quantified using a mouse AMA ELISA kit (MyBiosource, Inc., San Diego, CA, USA) according to the manufacturer’s instructions.

Flow cytometry

Hepatic lymphocyte subsets were analyzed by flow cytometry. Single-cell suspensions were prepared from mouse livers and stained with fluorochrome-conjugated antibodies against CD3, CD8, CD19, CD45, CCR2, and CD49b (Biolegend, USA). CCR2 + cell proportions were assessed by flow cytometry. Data acquisition and analysis were performed using standard flow cytometry protocols.

Cytokine measurement

Serum monocyte chemoattractant protein-1 (MCP-1, also known as CCL2) was measured by ELISA using a mouse MCP-1 ELISA kit (Abcam, Boston, MA, USA) according to the manufacturer’s protocol.

Histological analysis

Fresh mouse liver tissues were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for 48 h, embedded in paraffin, and sectioned at 4 µm. Sections were deparaffinized, rehydrated, and stained with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) for general histology. Collagen deposition was evaluated by Sirius Red staining (Abcam, USA). Histological scoring was performed in a blinded manner as previously described, combining severity (0–4) and frequency (0–4) scores [11]. The validated histological scoring method for the mouse model is described in the Supplementary Methods [11].

Intrahepatic small bile ducts were visualized using immunohistochemical staining for cytokeratin-19. Liver sections were subjected to deparaffinization, rehydration, antigen retrieval, and blocking, then incubated with anti-mouse cytokeratin-19 antibody (Abcam). Sections were subsequently incubated with HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies (Servicebio) at room temperature for 50 min, developed with diaminobenzidine (DAB), counterstained with hematoxylin, dehydrated, and mounted. Immunohistochemical staining for PDC-E2, CD3, CD4, CD8, and CD20 (Abcam) was performed on mouse liver sections. All sections were evaluated by pathologists blinded to the experimental groups at Peking Union Medical College Hospital.

Statistical analysis

Data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD) for normally distributed variables. Comparisons between two groups were performed using the unpaired two-tailed Student’s t-test. For non-normally distributed data, nonparametric tests were applied. Multiple group comparisons were conducted using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by appropriate post hoc tests. All statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism version 8.0 (GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA, USA). A two-sided p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Exome sequencing and identification of PTK2B variant

Exome sequencing identified 36,022, 36,604, 36,265, and 37,737 reliable variants in participants I.2, I.3, I.4, and unaffected sibling I.6, respectively (minimum depth 2 × , variant allele frequency ≥ 10%). After strict filtering, 17 candidate variants were selected (Fig. 1, Table S4). Sanger sequencing of additional family members confirmed that only a PTK2B variant (c.1679C > G, p.P560R) co-segregated with PBC, being present in I.2, I.3, I.4, and their offspring but absent in other relatives, 48 healthy controls, NCBI dbSNP (containing > 60,000 healthy controls at the time of analysis) and the public PBC GWAS dataset in NCBI (6,480 PBC cases and 14,736 controls) (Fig. 1B). The P560 residue is highly conserved across species. This missense variant was absent from the 1000 Genomes and ExAC databases (PM2, moderate pathogenic evidence), located in a highly conserved functional domain (PM1), and caused a marked physicochemical change via proline-to-arginine substitution (PP2, supporting pathogenic evidence). Multiple in silico tools predicted damaging effects on protein function (PP3). Combining PM1, PM2, PP2 and PP3, the variant was classified as likely pathogenic according to American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics (ACMG) guidelines (Fig. S2). Detailed screening results for other candidate variants are provided in Fig. S3A–Q.

Discussion

This study focused on establishing the genetic causality and in vivo phenotypic relevance of PTK2B in PBC. Using exome sequencing of a Chinese PBC pedigree, we identified a novel pathogenic PTK2B variant (c.1679C > G; p.Pro560Arg). Its pathogenicity was validated in a knock-in mouse model that recapitulated key PBC phenotypes. Co-segregation in the family and in vivo evidence support a causal role for the variant, consistent with Mendelian principles.

There have been some previous reports on susceptibility genes for PBC [6, 12]. In the Chinese Han population, loci that were most significant in European and Japanese patients with PBC have also been examined [13]. GWAS in the Han Chinese population identified variants in IL21, IL21R, CD28/CTLA4/ICOS, CD58, ARID3A, and IL16 as PBC risk loci [14]. However, a comprehensive review of existing GWAS and post-GWAS studies of PBC revealed no prior evidence linking the gene identified in the present study to PBC [6–9, 14, 15], highlighting its novel mechanistic implications. In addition, previous studies have reported single-nucleotide polymorphisms within related signaling pathways, such as variants at 19p13.2 near TYK2, which encodes a non-receptor tyrosine kinase involved in immune regulation [8]. Notably, these GWAS findings generally lack functional validation, whereas family-based studies integrated with animal model validation, as performed here, provide more direct evidence of pathogenicity while reducing the impact of population heterogeneity.

We further searched the Human Gene Mutation Database (HGMD, http://www.hgmd.cf.ac.uk/ac/index.php) for reported variants in PTK2B and identified some deletion or nonsense variants. The c.637A > G (p.Lys225Glu) and rs2741342 variant has been reported in cohorts with Alzheimer’s disease, whereas the c.1241G > T (p.Gly414Val) and c.2591C > T (p.Ala864Val) variants have been identified in patients with Parkinson’s disease [16–18]. Notably, Parkinson’s disease has also been linked to mitochondrial dysfunction and immune dysregulation, suggesting that PTK2B variants may contribute to disease susceptibility across multiple immune-related conditions.

The identified PTK2B variant (c.1679C > G; p.Pro560Arg) affects a highly conserved residue within the tyrosine kinase catalytic domain, suggesting a potential impact on kinase function. Mice carrying the knock-in variant developed serological and hepatic pathological features consistent with PBC, supporting a role for PTK2B in disease pathogenesis. PTK2B (also known as Pyk2) encodes protein tyrosine kinase 2β, a cytoplasmic tyrosine kinase involved in immune cell activation, adhesion, migration, and innate immune signaling [19, 20]. Previous studies have implicated PTK2B in the regulation of inflammatory responses via the IRF5-mediated innate immune pathway and in controlling neutrophil immune responses in ulcerative colitis [21, 22]. In addition, Pyk2 activation has been shown to play an important role in Toll-like receptor–mediated inflammation and autoimmunity [23]. Consistent with these findings, PTK2B has also been associated with other autoimmune diseases, including systemic lupus erythematosus and Takayasu arteritis [24, 25].

In this study, Ptk2b knock-in mice exhibited increased hepatic infiltration of T cells, B cells, and CCR2+ cells, accompanied by elevated serum MCP-1 levels, suggesting dysregulated immune cell trafficking. These immune cell populations have been mechanistically implicated in the pathogenesis of PBC [1]. The reduced proportion of CCR2+ cells in peripheral blood, together with their accumulation in the liver, suggesting enhanced recruitment of inflammatory cells to hepatic tissue and a possible involvement of the MCP-1/CCR2 axis. Previous studies have shown that the MCP-1/CCR2 axis is involved in the regulation of γδ T cells in systemic lupus erythematosus and promotes macrophage infiltration, activation, and fibroblast migration [26–28]. Collectively, these findings suggest that the PTK2B variant may contribute to PBC pathogenesis through immune dysregulation and alteration of the hepatic immune microenvironment, although the precise molecular mechanisms require further investigation.

The knock-in mutation induced PBC-like autoimmune features in mice. Several genetically modified mouse models of PBC have been established, including dominant-negative form of TGF-beta receptor type II (dnTGF-βRII) mice, IL-2Rα−/− mice, NOD.c3c4 mice, AE2a,b−/− mice, and ARE-Del−/− mice [29]. Each of these models has distinct strengths and limitations [29]. In this context, mice carrying the Ptk2b variant (c.1679C > G) may serve as a novel genetic mouse model for studying PBC.

We have retrieved several reports on familial PBC cases. Among these patients, one presented with PBC-AIH overlap syndrome [30], one was complicated with Sjögren’s syndrome and rheumatoid arthritis [3], and another had Sjögren’s syndrome [31]. No other immune-mediated liver diseases were documented in the remaining cases.

Interestingly, the proband’s generation (I.1–I.9) included nine sisters and no brothers. Because both parents had died before sample collection, it was not possible to determine whether the father carried Y-chromosomal abnormalities. Previous studies have reported an increased frequency of Y chromosome loss in peripheral blood cells of male patients with PBC compared with healthy controls [32]. Whether sex chromosome abnormalities contribute to disease susceptibility in familial PBC warrants further investigation in additional families.

Limitations

In this study, the heterozygous variant was detected not only in the three affected participants but also in their currently asymptomatic offspring. Given that PBC predominantly affects middle-aged and older women, it is possible that these offspring have not yet reached the typical age of disease onset. The study is limited by the small sample size and inclusion of a single family. Moreover, the knock-in mouse model provides only preliminary validation, without detailed investigation of underlying molecular mechanisms. Future studies will focus on elucidating the PTK2B signaling pathways involved in PBC.

Conclusions

In summary, we identified a novel PTK2B variant (c.1679C > G) in a Chinese PBC pedigree and demonstrated its pathogenicity through a knock-in mouse model recapitulating key disease phenotypes. These findings establish PTK2B as a candidate pathogenic variant of PBC and provide a new in vivo model for studying disease mechanisms and potential therapeutic targets.

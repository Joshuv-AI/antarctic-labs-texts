Real-World Application of Evolocumab Among Patients with Hyperlipidemia in Korea: A Multicenter Prospective Study
Authors: Min Chul Kim, Seung Hun Lee, Joon Ho Ahn, Yongwhan Lim, Kyung-il Park, Choongki Kim, Jong-Hwa Ahn, JinSeon Jeong, Hojoon Lee, Woong Chol Kang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionElevated low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) is a major residual risk factor among patients with acute coronary syndrome (ACS). In the absence of sufficient real-world evidence, this observational (noninterventional) study investigated the effectiveness and safety of evolocumab in patients with hyperlipidemia treated with evolocumab for ACS in a real-world clinical setting in Korea.MethodsBetween January 2022 and February 2023, patients from 10 hospitals in Korea who initiated evolocumab within 24 weeks of an ACS event were enrolled. Data collected at visit 1 (evolocumab initiation) included patients’ characteristics, comorbidities, and lipid-lowering therapies. LDL-C reduction from visit 1 (week 0) to visit 2 (week 8) was assessed. The primary outcome was the proportion of patients who achieved LDL-C < 1.4 mmol/L (55 mg/dL) at follow-up; the secondary outcome was the proportion who achieved LDL-C < 1.8 mmol/L (70 mg/dL) at follow-up.ResultsIn this study, 89 out of 142 enrolled patients were included in the effectiveness analysis. The mean (SD) age of the included patients was 59.3 (12.3) years, with the majority being male (87.6%). Sixty-one patients received statin–ezetimibe combination therapy (68.5%). The median [Q1, Q3] LDL-C level at the start of the study was 2.5 [2.0, 3.0] mmol/L (98 [77, 115] mg/dL), which decreased to 1.3 [0.7, 1.7] mmol/L (49 [29, 67] mg/dL) after 8 weeks of evolocumab treatment, resulting in an mean (SD) 50.9 (28.6) % reduction and 1.4 (1.0) mmol/L (55.1 (37.9) mg/dL) absolute reduction. At follow-up, 55.1% and 78.7% of patients achieved LDL-C goals of < 1.4 mmol/L (55 mg/dL) and < 1.8 mmol/L (70 mg/dL), respectively. No adverse or serious adverse drug reactions were reported.ConclusionEvolocumab treatment was associated with significant LDL-C lowering and favorable safety and guideline-recommended LDL-C goal achievement rates among patients with ACS in the real-world clinical practice setting in South Korea.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s40119-024-00389-y.Keywords: Atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease, Acute coronary syndrome, Low-density lipoprotein cholesterol, Evolocumab, Real-world evidence

Key Summary Points

Why carry out this study?Patients with acute coronary syndrome (ACS) and elevated low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) remain at increased risk of further cardiovascular events; however, there is insufficient real-world evidence of LDL-C target goal achievement rates following intensification of lipid-lowering therapy, particularly in Asian patients.The aim of this prospective, observational (noninterventional) study was to investigate LDL-C target goal achievement rates in recent patients with ACS treated with evolocumab in the clinical practice setting in Korea.What was learned from the study?The study showed that evolocumab initiated within 24 weeks of an ACS event in Korea significantly lowered LDL-C levels by 50.9%, and 55.1% and 78.7% of patients achieved LDL-C goals of < 1.4 mmol/L (55 mg/dL) and < 1.8 mmol/L (70 mg/dL), respectively, at 8 weeks in the absence of any treatment-related adverse events.Study findings fill the gap in the evidence for the real-world effectiveness and safety of evolocumab in patients with ACS and add to the totality of the evidence from clinical trial results in very high-risk patients with ASCVD.

Introduction

Cardiovascular disease (CVD) remains the leading cause of mortality worldwide [1]. In 2019, CVD deaths in Asia alone accounted for 58% of the global rate (18.6 million CVD) [2, 3]. Elevated cholesterol, in particular low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C), is a well-established, modifiable risk factor for patients with atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD) [4, 5]. The 2018 American College of Cardiology/American Heart Association (ACC/AHA) and 2019 European Society of Cardiology/European Atherosclerosis Society (ESC/EAS) guidelines recommend LDL-C lowering with high-intensity lipid-lowering therapy (LLT) as first-line treatment for very high-risk patients with ASCVD and specify risk-based LDL-C goals [6, 7]. LLTs reduce the risk of major vascular events by 22% per 1 mmol/L (38.7 mg/dL) reduction in LDL-C, consistently across baseline LDL-C levels, and with more versus less intensive LLTs providing greater absolute LDL-C reduction and CV benefit [8, 9]; consequently the ESC/EAS [7] and South Korean [10] guidelines recommend a lower goal for very high-risk patients, < 1.4 mmol/L (55 mg/dL) and a reduction of at least 50%.

Although statins are effective for many patients, optimal reduction in lipids may not be achieved by all patients, including those who have or are at very high risk for CV events. The randomized, placebo-controlled EVOlocumab for Early Reduction of LDL-cholesterol Levels in Patients with Acute Coronary Syndromes (EVOPACS) study showed that evolocumab added to high-intensity statin therapy was well tolerated with substantially greater reductions (41%) in LDL-C levels at week 8 than high-intensity statin alone [11]. However, observational studies have generally shown low rates of guideline-recommended LLT use and LDL-C goal achievement; hence the majority of patients remain at residual risk of recurrent CV events [12, 13]. A European consensus statement recommends that, in addition to the “the lower, the better” approach, “strike early and strike strong” is required in the early post-ACS phase with initiation of high-intensity statins and ezetimibe, and a PCSK9 (proprotein convertase subtilisin/kexin type 9) inhibitor [12].

Real-world evidence from several studies across various countries has demonstrated the effectiveness and safety of evolocumab, consistent with results from a large randomized pivotal trial of evolocumab [14]. The real-world studies provided evidence of guideline-recommended initiation of evolocumab in various healthcare settings and patient populations, including in Spain [15], the Czech Republic [16], the USA [17], and Canada [18]. However, there is a demand for further real-world clinical evidence of evolocumab, to add to the totality of the evidence, especially in Asian populations for whom there is a paucity of such data. Therefore, we conducted a real-world study in Korea to investigate the effectiveness, primarily focusing on LDL-C reduction and goal achievement rates, as well as safety of evolocumab in patients with hyperlipidemia initially treated with evolocumab following a recent ACS event in a real-world clinical practice setting.

Methods

Study Design

This was a prospective, single-arm, multicenter, observational (noninterventional) study over an 8 (± 4)-week period following initial evolocumab treatment in adults with ACS in Korean real-world clinical practice. The study was conducted between January 2022 and February 2023 at 10 hospitals in South Korea, in accordance with recommendations of the 18th World Health Congress (Helsinki 1964) and all applicable amendments, and the International Conference on Harmonization guidelines for Good Clinical Practice (GCP). The protocol was approved by relevant independent ethics committees at each hospital (the complete list is available in online Supplementary Material). All participants had to sign an informed consent form to be included in the study.

Eligible patients (aged ≥ 19 years) must have experienced an ACS event within 24 weeks before starting evolocumab. Only patients unable to reach an LDL-C level < 1.8 mmol/L (70 mg/dL) with the maximum tolerated dose of a statin, or for whom the decision to initiate treatment with evolocumab had already been made on the basis of the investigator’s assessment during routine practice and in accordance with the local label, were eligible for study inclusion. Excluded from enrolment were patients with a contraindication for evolocumab, according to the current Korean marketing authorization and Summary of Product Characteristics [19], and those who were participating in another interventional study of a PCSK9 inhibitor within 12 weeks prior to initiation of evolocumab treatment. All patients provided written informed consent before study enrolment.

Index Visit and Follow-up Visit Assessments

The index visit (visit 1) was defined as when the patient received their first dose of evolocumab (week 0) and the follow-up visit (visit 2) was defined as at 8 weeks post treatment (± 4 weeks which provided a window for data collection of 4–12 weeks), or at death or lost to follow-up, whichever was earliest.

At visit 1 (week 0), informed consent was collected, study eligibility was confirmed, and baseline data were assessed, including patient demographics, medical history, and medications history. ACS details collected were the date of diagnosis, the ACS type, and history of previous percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) and coronary artery bypass graft (CABG) surgery status. Details regarding LLTs (ingredient, dosage, duration, and intensity) other than PCSK9 inhibitors were collected. Detailed evolocumab treatment information was reported with dosage, duration of treatment, ongoing status of evolocumab at the end of the study, dosage adjustments and suspension, and associated reasons. Lipid level data (total cholesterol, LDL-C, high-density lipoprotein cholesterol [HDL-C], triglycerides, and non-HDL-C) were measured at week 0 (visit 1) and at week 8 (± 4) (visit 2).

Study Outcomes

The outcomes of interest were LDL-C reduction, described as absolute and percentage reduction after initiation of evolocumab, and goal achievement rates based on different guideline standards. The primary outcome was the proportion of patients who achieved LDL-C < 1.4 mmol/L (55 mg/dL) at follow-up and the secondary outcome was the proportion of patients who achieved LDL-C < 1.8 mmol/L (70 mg/dL) at follow-up. Exploratory endpoints were LDL-C < 1.4 mmol/L (55 mg/dL) at follow-up and ≥ 50% reduction from projected baseline (untreated) and LDL-C < 1.8 mmol/L (70 mg/dL) at follow-up or ≥ 50% reduction from projected baseline (untreated). The projected baseline (untreated) LDL-C levels were estimated on the basis of the average LDL-C lowering effectiveness of the given LLT medications, as outlined in the ESC/EAS guidelines [7]. Subgroup analyses were performed by patient’s initial ACS type and type of LLT at visit 1.

Safety Assessments

Safety was assessed as the number of patients and number of events of reportable drug-related adverse events and non-drug related adverse events during the period from patient enrolment to final contact. Reportable events included adverse drug reactions and serious adverse drug reactions, both related and non-related to the drug. Investigators assessed the causal relationship between evolocumab administration and adverse events.

Statistical Analysis

All statistical analyses were descriptive and no imputations of missing data were planned. Summary statistics for categorical variables included the frequency or percentage, with a 95% confidence interval (CI). Summary statistics for continuous variables included the number of patients, mean with standard deviation or standard error, median with 25th percentile (Q1) and 75th percentile (Q3), minimum, and maximum.

The LDL-C level was summarized for the two time points, index visit and follow-up. The percentage and absolute reduction were calculated from LDL-C level at index visit and follow-up visit. To assess the proportion of patients who achieved LDL-C target goals, LDL-C data at the follow-up visit were used for the absolute cutoff value.

Results

Demographic and Clinical Characteristics

Of the 151 patients enrolled in the study at 10 hospitals in South Korea, nine patients were excluded, primarily for not fulfilling inclusion/exclusion criteria (Fig. 1). The remaining 142 patients were included in the safety analysis set, among whom 89 were included in the effectiveness analysis set; reasons for exclusion of 53 patients from the effectiveness analysis included no or insufficient LDL-C data at 8 (± 4) weeks.

Flow diagram of patient disposition

Demographics and clinical characteristics of the 89 patients in the effectiveness analysis set are summarized in Table 1. Patients’ mean age was 59.3 years, most (87.6%) were male, mean BMI was 25.4 kg/m2, more than half of the patients (51.7%) were current smokers, and the most common comorbidities were hypertension (51.7%) and diabetes mellitus (29.2%). ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI) (39.3%) was the most frequent ACS type, followed by non-STEMI (32.6%) and unstable angina (UA) (28.1%). Overall, a third of patients (33.7%) had received a previous PCI and 1.1% had undergone a previous CABG. At the index visit, most patients (68.5%) were using ezetimibe combination therapy while almost a third were using statin only (30.3%).

LDL-C Reduction

Median [Q1, Q3] LDL-C of the effectiveness analysis set at the index visit was 2.5 [2.0, 3.0] mmol/L (98 [77, 115] mg/dL) and at the follow-up visit was 1.3 [0.7, 1.7] mmol/L (49 [29, 67] mg/dL). Following initiation of evolocumab treatment at the index visit, the mean (SD) percentage reduction was 50.9% (28.6) and the absolute reduction was 1.4 (1.0) mmol/L (55.1 (37.9) mg/dL) (Fig. 2). LDL-C Goal Achievement Rates

The proportion of patients that achieved LDL-C < 1.4 mmol/L (55 mg/dL) at the follow-up visit was 55.1% (49/89). The proportion of patients that achieved LDL-C < 1.8 mmol/L (70 mg/dL) was 78.7% (70/89). In exploratory analysis, the proportions of patients that achieved LDL-C < 1.4 mmol/L (55 mg/dL) and > 50% reduction from projected baseline (untreated) and LDL-C < 1.8 mmol/L (70 mg/dL) or ≥ 50% reduction from projected baseline (untreated) were 55.1% (49/89) and 94.4% (84/89), respectively (Fig. 4).

Goal achievement rate by primary, secondary, and exploratory endpoints

LDL-C Goal Achievement by Subgroups

Subgroup analyses by ACS subtype showed no difference in achievement of the primary LDL-C goal, and further subgroup analyses by LLT intensity also showed no difference in the rate of achievement of the primary goal (Supplement Tables 1 and 2). In a detailed regression analysis with other variables, no factors were identified as statistically significantly associated with achievement of the primary LDL-C goal (Supplement Table 3).

Persistence Rate of Evolocumab

Over the study period, overall, 32.6% of patients (29/89) continued the use of evolocumab until the end of the study (Table 2), 60 patients discontinued evolocumab treatment. The most common reason of discontinuation was investigators’ judgement (43 patients), followed by patients’ refusal (16 patients).

Discussion

In this real-world effectiveness and safety study of evolocumab in Korea, evolocumab was initiated within 24 weeks of an ACS event in a hospital setting. LDL-C levels were significantly reduced by 50.9% (1.4 mmol/L (55.1 mg/dL) absolute reduction) from evolocumab initiation and 55.1% and 78.7% of patients achieved LDL-C goals of < 1.4 mmol/L (55 mg/dL) and < 1.8 mmol/L (70 mg/dL), respectively, at 8 weeks in the absence of any treatment-related adverse events. These findings fill the gap in the real-world effectiveness of evolocumab among the Asian population, while consolidating the real-world effectiveness of evolocumab in global perspectives and supporting the totality of the evidence combined with the clinical trial results in very high-risk patients with ASCVD.

This study demonstrated the LDL-C-lowering effect of evolocumab among very high-risk patients with ACS in Asia. At the index visit, the median lipid level for the study population was 98 mg/dL, despite that the majority (68.5%) were receiving moderate or high-intensity statin plus ezetimibe. The percentage (50.9%) and absolute (1.4 mmol/L (55.1 mg/dL)) reduction in LDL-C observed in this study at 8 weeks after initiation of evolocumab in patients with post-ACS is comparable with that observed in the large evolocumab outcomes trial, Further Cardiovascular Outcomes Research with PCSK9 Inhibition in Subjects with Elevated Risk (FOURIER) [14]. In patients with established ASCVD in FOURIER, LDL-C was reduced by 59% (absolute reduction 1.6 mmol/L (62 mg/dL)) at 48 weeks. Similar levels of LDL-C reduction have been shown in other real-world studies of evolocumab. In the Canadian cohort (n = 131) of the observational chart review study of evolocumab initiation in patients with ASCVD and/or familial hypercholesterolemia (ZERBINI), after 12 months there was a 59% reduction in LDL-C [18]. In retrospective, observational studies of hospitalized patients with refractory hyperlipidemia and established CVD in Spain (n = 62) [15] and the Czech Republic (n = 108) [16], who received PCSK9 inhibitors (evolocumab or alirocumab) in addition to statins and/or ezetimibe for secondary prevention, LDL-C reductions of 48% (absolute 2.4 mmol/L (92 mg/dL)) and 64% at 6-month and 12-week follow-up, respectively, were shown. Additionally, a retrospective analysis of healthcare claims data in the USA for 152 patients with a recent myocardial infarction (MI) within 12 months prior to evolocumab initiation showed LDL-C levels were lowered by 65% at 12-month follow-up [17]. Notably, in a subgroup analysis of the FOURIER trial, the efficacy and safety of evolocumab was compared between Asian (n = 2723) and other (n = 24,841) patients [20]. Baseline LDL-C levels were marginally lower among Asian than among the other patients (2.3 mmol/L (89 mg/dL) vs 2.4 mmol/L (92 mg/dL)); however, at 48 weeks, compared with placebo, mean percentage (66% vs 58%) and absolute (1.6 mmol/L (61 mg/dL) vs 1.4 mmol/L (55 mg/dL)) reductions in LDL-C with evolocumab were greater in the Asian than in the other patients. The authors of the analysis showed that the differences between Asians and others in LDL-C reductions were despite adjustment for baseline body weight and high-intensity statin use (P interaction < 0.001) [20].

Our study also provides evidence of the real-world effectiveness of evolocumab in meeting guideline-recommended LDL-C goal for very high-risk patients with ACS [7]. Importantly, the stringent LDL-C goal of < 1.4 mmol/L (55 mg/dL) was achieved by 55.1% of patients following evolocumab treatment at index visit, while 78.7% of patients achieved the less stringent 2018 ACC/AHA LDL-C goal, < 1.8 mmol/L (70 mg/dL) [6]. The most recent ESC/EAS LDL-C goal for very high-risk patients [7], < 1.4 mmol/L (55 mg/dL) and ≥ 50% reduction from projected baseline (untreated), was achieved by 55.1% of patients, while 94.4% achieved the LDL-C goal recommended by previous 2016 ESC/EAS cholesterol guidelines [21], < 1.8 mmol/L (70 mg/dL) or ≥ 50% reduction from projected baseline (untreated). Previous clinical evidence demonstrating the benefit of early evolocumab initiation comes from EVOPACS in which evolocumab initiated in-hospital with LLTs in patients with ACS resulted in > 95% of patients achieving LDL-C < 1.8 mmol/L (70 mg/dL) [11]. In the placebo-controlled Evolocumab in Acute Coronary Syndrome (EVACS) study, 81% and 65% of patients treated with evolocumab within 24 h of an ACS event achieved the recommended AHA/ACC (< 1.8 mmol/L (70 mg/dL)) and ESC (< 1.4 mmol/L (55 mg/dL)) goals [22]. By contrast, observational studies show that real-world compliance with guideline-recommended LDL-C goals is low in the USA [23] and across Europe, where < 50% of high/very high-risk primary and secondary prevention patients achieve 2016 LDL-C goals and about 20% achieve the lower 2019 goals [24]. In the real-world study of Desai et al. in the USA that included patients with a recent MI, for this subgroup, 69.7% achieved LDL-C < 1.8 mmol/L (70 mg/dL) at 12-month follow-up [17]. Our real-world study shows that evolocumab treatment in patients with very high-risk ACS in a clinical practice setting in Korea results in a higher rate of patients achieving LDL-C goals than that seen in other real-world studies, and similar to that seen in clinical trials.

In the current real-world study in Korean clinical practice, no drug-related adverse or serious adverse events were reported. The favorable safety profile of evolocumab in this study is generally consistent with that shown in randomized controlled trials [14, 25] and other real-world studies in different regions, including Europe [15, 16], the USA [17], Canada [18], and China [26]. This is an important finding as Korean patients were limited in representation in these trials; hence, this study provides relevant data for Korean clinical practice. The finding that no additional safety concerns were identified in our study is not surprising. Consistent with other real-world studies in which no [18] or a few [27] injection-site reactions were reported, in our study there were none; in FOURIER the incidence was 2.1% [14]. In FOURIER, the incidence of AEs (77.4% and 77.4%) and serious AEs (24.8% and 24.7%) with evolocumab was the same/similar as that seen with placebo [14]. In the subanalysis of data from FOURIER in Asians compared with other patients, there were no significant between-group differences in the incidence of AEs including rhabdomyolysis, hemorrhagic stroke, new-onset diabetes mellitus, or cognitive decline [20].

This is the first real-world study of evolocumab conducted exclusively in Korea to provide a perspective of the effectiveness and safety of evolocumab in the treatment of a very high-risk population with ACS in a real-world clinical setting. Moreover, it used projected baseline (untreated) LDL-C levels according to the extrapolation that was suggested in the most recent 2019 ESC/EAS guidelines to estimate baseline (untreated) LDL-C levels [7]. Finally, this study was a multicenter (10 hospitals), prospective study to mitigate the potential bias which can originate from single-center or retrospective settings. Despite its strengths, several important limitations should be addressed. First, the single LDL-C measurement over 8 weeks limits our ability to assess the long-term efficacy of evolocumab in real-world practice. Second, the observational design, with patients’ self-selection and lack of blinding, introduces the potential for confounding and bias. Third, the study cohort focused on a single country, Korea, and its reliance on reimbursement criteria may limit the generalizability to other populations with different age groups, geographical areas, and risk factors.

Conclusion

This study provided real-world evidence showing that early administration of evolocumab in recent patients with ACS in routine clinical practice in Korea effectively reduces LDL-C, facilitating goal attainment. The study also suggested that adding evolocumab to statin and ezetimibe therapy is safe and effective and may reduce the potential CV risk in this very high-risk population by lowering the residual LDL-C risk burden. Further studies are warranted to determine the long-term effectiveness and CV benefits for patients who initiate evolocumab early after a recent ACS.
